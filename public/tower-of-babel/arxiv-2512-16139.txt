Consensus tracking of perturbed open multi-agent systems with repelling antagonistic interactions footnoteinfo
Mengqi Xue, Yuchao Xiong, Yue Song

Abstract
An open multi-agent system (OMAS) comprises migrating agents which produce a flexible network structure that is naturally switching and size-varying. Meanwhile, agent migrations also make an OMAS more prone to environmental adversities. In this work, we deal with the consensus tracking problem of OMASs suffering these migration-induced adversities, including non-vanishing perturbations in the agent dynamics/state and the repelling antagonistic interactions among agents, over an intermittently disconnected signed digraph. The OMAS is interpreted into a perturbed M 3 ​ D M^{3}D system in which unstable subsystems are created when repelling interactions dominate the normal cooperative ones in the OMAS network regardless of its connectivity. To handle the destabilizing effects brought by the repelling interaction as well as the non-vanishing perturbations, we extend the stability theory for M 3 ​ D M^{3}D systems and apply it to the OMAS to show that practical consensus tracking can be achieved if the migration-induced switching satisfies the piecewise average dwell time and activation time ratio constraints. Particularly, we indicate that for vanishing perturbations and repelling interactions, asymptotic tracking can be expected under weaker switching constraints.



keywords

Open multi-agent systems, switched systems, repelling interactions, perturbations.

1
Introduction

Open multi-agent systems (OMASs)
Hendrickx and Martin 2016
;
Franceschelli and Frasca 2021
extend traditional multi-agent systems by allowing agents to dynamically join or leave the group. Such migrations of agents constantly change the composition of an OMAS, rendering it a naturally switching and size-varying network structure. Owing to this feature, the OMAS has shown superiority over its traditional counterparts in covering a wide range of practical networks that have variable structures, such as mobile ad-hoc networks
Conti and Giordano 2014
, microgrids with plug-and-play control
Xu et al. 2025
, biomes with migrating species
Levine et al. 2017
. Despite giving rise to a flexible network structure, the agent migration also makes an OMAS more prone to environmental perturbations owing to its intermediation role between the OMAS and the external environment. For example, any vehicle joining a platoon by a lane changing maneuver can bring in abrupt disturbances against both the platoon network dynamics as well as the platoon state
Song and Li 2021
. On the other hand, agent migrations can also affect the normal cooperative interactions among agents, turning them into or directly bringing in antagonistic interactions. For instance, a cyber-physical network can be vulnerable to trojan attacks which connect disguised malicious devices to the network to convert normal connections into or create sabotage connections
Yu et al. 2023
. Typically, OMASs suffering these migration-induced adversities can be constantly perturbed or exhibit instability both of which usually impede desired coordination goals from being achieved. Given these facts, it is thus of both theoretical and practical significance to pay attention to the migration-induced adversities against the OMAS.

OMASs have become one of the emerging topics in control community over the last few years sparked by pioneering works of
Hendrickx and Martin 2016
;
Hendrickx and Martin 2017
;
Abdelrahim et al. 2017
;
Franceschelli and Frasca 2018
. Two notable methods featuring the concepts of “scale-independent quantities”
Hendrickx and Martin 2016
;
Hendrickx and Martin 2017
;
Abdelrahim et al. 2017
and “open distance”
Franceschelli and Frasca 2018
;
Franceschelli and Frasca 2021
have been developed to deal with the size varying feature of the OMAS network. Some subsequent works can also be found in
Monnoyer de Galland and Hendrickx 2023
;
Sawamura et al. 2025
;
Oliva et al. 2024
;
Deplano et al. 2025
;
Monnoyer de Galland et al. 2024
. Further,
Xue et al. 2022
provided an alternative line based on
M^{3}D
systems
Verriest 2012
for analyzing OMASs with non-vanishing impulsive state perturbations. Note that only the leaderless consensus problem of OMASs under cooperative interactions and state perturbations was considered in
Xue et al. 2022
, which may not well reflect some practical scenarios such as disaster rescues. More recently, a first exploration on OMASs with antagonistic interactions has also been reported by

Sekercioglu et al. 2025
, where the bipartite consensus problem was studied for OMASs under opposing interactions
Shi et al. 2019
. However, the result on OMASs with the more practical repelling interactions
Shi et al. 2019
, which can lead to Laplacians comprising eigenvalues with negative real parts, is still limited. Besides, most existing results only apply to OMASs without non-vanishing dynamics/state perturbations
Sawamura et al. 2025
;
Liu et al. 2024
;
Monnoyer de Galland and Hendrickx 2023
, or those with undirected or connected graphs
Oliva et al. 2024
;
Monnoyer de Galland et al. 2024
, which also restrict the application scenarios.

Motivated by the above, this work will focus on the consensus tracking problem of OMASs with nonvanishing perturbations and repelling antagonistic interactions. The main contributions of this work are highlighted as follows:
1) The consensus tracking problem of OMASs with migration-induced non-vanishing dynamics/state perturbations and antagonistic interactions-induced instability is studied, which extends the results of
Xue et al. 2022
which considered the leaderless consensus problem for OMASs under only non-vanishing state perturbations and cooperative interactions.
2) The consideration of repelling interactions compensates the results for OMASs with purely cooperative interactions
Monnoyer de Galland and Hendrickx 2023
;
Sawamura et al. 2025
;
Monnoyer de Galland et al. 2024
or opposing antagonistic interactions
Sekercioglu et al. 2025
. We also show that the OMAS must exhibit instability regardless of the network connectivity if the repelling interactions dominate the normal cooperative ones.
3) A new
M^{3}D
system-based method featuring a combination of piecewise average dwell time (ADT) and activation time ratio is proposed to accommodate the non-vanishing perturbations and the destabilizing repelling interactions, which no longer entails the “alternating switching” condition as in
Xue et al. 2022
, and the topology graph is allowed to be directed and intermittently disconnected compared with
Oliva et al. 2024
;
Monnoyer de Galland et al. 2024
.

5
Conclusion

The consensus tracking problem of the OMAS under migration-induced non-vanishing perturbations as well as repelling antagonistic interactions has been investigated. We show that the OMAS under repelling Laplacians must be unstable regardless of the network connectivity condition if the repelling interactions outnumber the normal ones. In the presence of the destabilizing effects brought by both the repelling interactions and the non-vanishing perturbations, we have further extended the stability theory for
M^{3}D
systems and applied it to the OMAS to show that practical consensus tracking can be reached if a combination condition of piecewise ADT and activation time ratio is satisfied. In particular, we indicate that asymptotic consensus tracking can be reached for vanishing perturbations under weaker switching conditions featuring the combination of traditional ADT and activation time ratio.

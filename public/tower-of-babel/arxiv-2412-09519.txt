On polynomial automorphisms commuting with a simple derivation


Abstract
. Let D D be a simple derivation of the polynomial ring 𝕜 ⁡ [ x 1 , … , x n ] {\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]} , where 𝕜 {\mathbb{k}} is an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, and denote by Aut ⁡ ( D ) ⊂ Aut ⁡ ( 𝕜 ⁡ [ x 1 , … , x n ] ) {\rm Aut}(D)\subset{\rm Aut}\bigl({\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}\bigr) the subgroup of 𝕜 {\mathbb{k}} -automorphisms commuting with D D . We show that the connected component of Aut ⁡ ( D ) {\rm Aut}(D) passing through the identity is a unipotent algebraic group of dimension at most n − 2 n-2 , this bound being sharp. Moreover, Aut ⁡ ( D ) {\rm Aut}(D) is an algebraic group if and only if it is a connected ind-group. Given a simple derivation D D , we characterize when Aut ⁡ ( D ) {\rm Aut}(D) contains a normal subgroup of translations. As an application of our techniques we show that if n = 3 n=3 , then either Aut ⁡ ( D ) {\rm Aut}(D) is a discrete group or it is isomorphic to the additive group acting by translations, and give some insight on the case n = 4 n=4 .

1.
Introduction

Let
{\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}
be the polynomial ring over an algebraically closed field

{\Bbbk}
of characteristic 0. Recall that a

{\Bbbk}
-derivation of
{\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}
is a linear endomorphism
D\in\operatorname{End}\bigl({\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}\bigr)

such that
D(fg)=D(f)g+fD(g)
for all
f,g\in{\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}
; we denote
D\in\operatorname{Der}\bigl({\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}\bigr)
.

A central and, of course, very difficult problem is to classify
derivations up conjugation by an automorphism of

{\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}
. If
n=2
there is a partial
classification (
[
BaPa2019
]
), but essentially nothing is known in
higher dimension. In order to provide contributions to the solution of this
problem, one may try to classify derivations whose interest has
been proven in relation to various areas of mathematics. This is the
case, for example, of the
locally nilpotent
derivations
and the

simple derivations
(see Definition
2.1
below). In the first case, significant progress has
been made in recent decades (see
[
Fr2017
]
and references
therein), while little is known about the second one.

Recall that if
\Delta
is a locally nilpotent
derivation then the formal series

e^{t\Delta}:=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{i}\Delta^{i}}{i!}

acts as a finite sum on every polynomial and defines an element in

{\rm Aut}\bigl({\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}\bigr)
(the group of
{\Bbbk}
-automorphisms of
{\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}
) for all

t\in{\Bbbk}
; in this way,
\Delta
induces an action of the
additive group
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}=({\Bbbk},+)
on
{\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}
.
Conversely, every action of

{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
on
{\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}
is of this form (see for example
[
Fr2017
, §1.5]
). When

{\Bbbk}={\mathbb{C}}
, the field of complex numbers, if one associates to a
derivation
D
the polynomial vector field
\bigl(D(x_{1}),\ldots,D(x_{n})\bigr)
, then
D
is a locally
nilpotent derivation if and only if the general solution of the differential
equation of the corresponding vector field is polynomial (
[
FiWa1997
]
).

On the other hand, given a simple derivation
D\in\operatorname{Der}\bigl({\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}\bigr)
— that is the only
D
-stable ideals are the trivial ones
—, one may extend the polynomial ring
{\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}
to a “skew”
polynomial ring
R
by adjoining a new
indeterminate
t
with the rule:
t\cdot f-f\cdot t=D(f)
for any

f\in{\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}
. It is well known that
D
is simple if and only if

R
has no non trivial bilateral ideals
(see for example
[
GoWa1989
, Chap. 2]
). Finally, if
{\Bbbk}={\mathbb{C}}
and we consider the singular
foliation
{\mathcal{F}}
associated with the vector field
\bigl(D(x_{1}),\ldots,D(x_{n})\bigr)
on
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
, then
D
being simple is equivalent to saying that

{\mathcal{F}}
is not singular and any of its leaves is Zariski dense. For a more
general field, the previous equivalence may be
described in terms of algebraic
independence of power series in one indeterminate (
[
Le2008
]
).

Thus, if we compare the two kind of derivations previously
mentioned by focusing on
their corresponding flows (when
{\Bbbk}={\mathbb{C}}
), then locally
nilpotent and simple derivations appear as opposite in a certain
sense. More generally, if
{\Bbbk}
is an arbitrary field and
\Delta
is a
locally nilpotent derivation of
{\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}
, then
\ker\Delta=\bigl\{f\in{\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]};\Delta(f)=0\bigr\}
is a
{\Bbbk}
-subalgebra of
{\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}

with transcendence degree over
{\Bbbk}
equal to
n-1

(see for example
[
vdE
, Pro.1.3.32(i)]
), whereas clearly
\ker D={\Bbbk}
when
D
is a
simple derivation. One finds further evidence for this intuition on the opposite behavior of simple and locally nilpotent derivations in
dimension
2
(
[
MePa2016
]
,
[
Pa2022
]
), looking at the automorphisms
group of the derivations:

Definition 1.1
.

Let
D\in\operatorname{Der}\bigl({\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}\bigr)
. The
automorphisms group of
D

is the isotropy group of
D
for the action of
{\rm Aut}\bigl({\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}\bigr)

on
\operatorname{Der}\bigl({\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}\bigr)

by conjugations:

{\rm Aut}(D)={\rm Aut}\bigl({\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}\bigr)_{D}\subset{\rm Aut}\bigl({\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}\bigr).

Theorem 1.2
.

If
D\in\operatorname{Der}\bigl({\Bbbk}[x_{1},x_{2}]\bigr)
, then

(a)

D
is locally nilpotent if and only if
{\rm Aut}(D)
is not an algebraic group.

(b)
If
D
is simple, then
{\rm Aut}(D)=1
. ∎

Recall that if
n\geq 2
, then
{\rm Aut}\bigl({\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}\bigr)
does not admit a compatible
structure of group scheme, but it is an ind-group with filtration
induced by total degree (see for example
[
Sha1981
]
and
[
Ku2002
, Chapter 4]
). More precisely, if
we consider the set theoretical inclusion

{\rm Aut}\bigl({\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}\bigr)\hookrightarrow{\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}^{n}
, given by
\varphi\mapsto\bigl(\varphi(x_{1}),\dots,\varphi(x_{n})\bigr)
, then the
degree of

\varphi
induces a filtration

{\rm Aut}\bigl({\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}\bigr)=\bigcup_{p}{\rm Aut}\bigl({\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}\bigr)_{p}
, where

{\rm Aut}\bigl({\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}\bigr)_{p}=\bigl\{\varphi=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})\in{\rm Aut}\bigl({\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}\bigr)\mathrel{:}\forall i\ \operatorname{deg}(f_{i})\leq p\ \bigr\}.

It follows that a closed group
G\subset{\rm Aut}\bigl({\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}\bigr)
is algebraic if and only if there exists
p

such that
G\subset{\rm Aut}\bigl({\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}\bigr)_{p}
.
Clearly,
{\rm Aut}\bigl({\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}\bigr)
identifies in a canonical way
with
{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{n})
, the automorphisms group of the
n
-dimensional affine
space; under this identification, if
G\subset{\rm Aut}\bigl({\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}\bigr)
is algebraic, then the
induced action
G\times{\mathbb{A}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
is regular.

If
n>2
, it is not difficult to show that
{\rm Aut}(D)
is never
algebraic when
D
is locally nilpotent; however, very few is known about
the simple case. In fact,
{\rm Aut}(D)
is expected to be
algebraic: in
[
Ya2022
]
, after exhibiting examples of
simple derivations whose isotropy is
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
acting by translations,
the author conjectures that in general, up to conjugation, if
D
is
simple then
{\rm Aut}(D)
acts by translations.

The main objective of
this work is to give evidence in the direction
of proving that
{\rm Aut}(D)
is in fact an algebraic group.
The structure of this work is as follows:

In Section
2
we collect some basic definitions and
results on
\operatorname{Der}\bigl({\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}\bigr)
and the ind-group structure of
{\rm Aut}\bigl({\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}\bigr)
.

In Section
3
we prove that if
D
is a simple
derivation, then
{\rm Aut}(D)^{0}
, the connected component of
{\rm Aut}(D)

passing through the identity, is a unipotent algebraic group (Theorem

3.2
), and that any algebraic element of
{\rm Aut}(D)
is unipotent
(Theorem
3.4
). In
particular,
{\rm Aut}(D)
is algebraic if and only if it is a connected group.

In Section
4
we show that if

D\in\operatorname{Der}\bigl({\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}\bigr)
is a simple
derivation, then
\dim{\rm Aut}(D)^{0}\leq n-2
(Theorem
4.7
), and that this
bound is sharp (Corollary
4.13
).

When a simple derivation is such that
{\rm Aut}(D)
contains a non trivial
translation, then one can give more insight on the structure of

{\rm Aut}(D)^{0}
; this is done in Section
5
.
More precisely, if
D
is a simple derivation, we characterize in terms of a coordinate system when
{\rm Aut}(D)
contains a non trivial normal subgroup of translations and we use this characterization to somewhat describe
{\rm Aut}(D)^{0}
(Theorem
5.4
). We apply our point
of view in order to prove that the isotropy group of a simple
derivation of
{\Bbbk}[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]
is either trivial, either the
additive group acting by translations (upon conjugation) or it is
an infinite countable discrete
group —
however, we remark that, up to our knowledge, there is
no known example of this last possibility.

Most examples of explicit simple derivations of
{\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}
are
produced by extending, recursively, a simple derivation in
{\Bbbk}[x_{1}]
,
using Shamsuddin’s criterion (see Proposition
4.8
), or some of
its variants, and are therefore of the form
D=\partial/\partial x_{1}+\sum_{i=2}^{n}a_{i}\partial/\partial x_{i}
,
a_{i}\in{\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}
. In this case — we say that
D
admits
x_{1}
as a
linear coordinate
—, one can describe quite well the action of an element of
{\rm Aut}(D)^{0}
on the coordinate
x_{1}
(see Lemma
5.8
). If
n=4
, we use this description in order to give more insight on the algebraicity of
{\rm Aut}(D)
(Theorem
5.10
).

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we recall some basic definitions and results on simple
and locally nilpotent derivations.

Let
V
be a
\Bbbk
-vector space. A linear endomorphism

\varphi:V\to V
is
locally finite
if for all
v\in V
, there exists a
finite dimensional
\varphi
-stable vector space
W
containing

v
. If moreover
\varphi|_{{}_{W}}
is nilpotent (resp. semisimple) for all finite
dimensional
\varphi
-stable vector space
W
, then
\varphi
is said
to be
locally nilpotent
(resp.
locally semisimple
). We say that

\varphi
is
locally unipotent
if
\varphi-{\rm Id}

is locally nilpotent.

If
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}\bigl({\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}\bigr)
, we denote by
\langle\varphi\rangle
the
group generated by
\varphi
; we say that
\varphi
is
algebraic
if
the closure
\overline{\langle\varphi\rangle}\subset{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{n})
is an algebraic group — along this work,
closures are taken with respect to the inductive topology of the
ind-variety structure. It is well known that
\varphi
is algebraic
if and only if the total degree of the positive powers of

\varphi
is bounded:
\max\bigl\{\deg(\varphi^{\ell}\bigr)\mathrel{:}\ell\geq 0\bigr\}<\infty
(see for example
[
FuKr2018
, Lemma 9.1.4]
).

Finally, it is easy to prove that
\varphi
is algebraic if and only
if
\varphi
is locally
finite (see
[
FuKr2018
, Lemma 9.1.4]
);
in particular,
\varphi
is an algebraic unipotent automorphism if and
only if
\varphi
is locally unipotent.

Definition 2.1
.

A
locally nilpotent derivation

is a derivation
D
that is locally nilpotent as a linear endomorphism of

{\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}
.

Let
D
be a derivation of
{\mathbb{k}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
. An ideal
I
of

{\mathbb{k}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
is
D
-stable
if
D(I)\subset I
. If the
only
D
-stable ideals are the trivial ones, we say that
D
is a

simple derivation
.

Remark 2.2
.

(1) It is clear that if
D
is a derivation of
{\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}
, then
D=\sum_{i}a_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}
, with
a_{i}\in{\mathbb{k}}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
.

(2) Let
D=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}

be a derivation of
{\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}
. Then the ideal
\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\rangle_{{\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}}\subset{\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}
is
D
-stable.

If
D
is a simple derivation then
\langle a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\rangle={\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}
.

(3) If
D=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\in\operatorname{Der}\bigl({\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}\bigr)

and
s
is such that

a_{i}\in{\mathbb{k}}[x_{s+1},\dots,x_{n}]
for
i>s
, then
the restriction
\overline{D}=D|_{{}_{{\mathbb{k}}[x_{s+1},\dots,x_{n}]}}:{\mathbb{k}}[x_{s+1},\dots,x_{n}]\to{\mathbb{k}}[x_{s+1},\dots,x_{n}]
is also a derivation. If moreover

D
is simple, then
\overline{D}
is also a simple derivation.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
D\in\operatorname{Der}\bigl({\mathbb{k}}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\bigr)
. We say that
f\in{\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}\setminus\{0\}
is a
Darboux polynomial for
D
of
eigenvalue
\lambda\in{\mathbb{k}}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
if
D(f)=\lambda f
. In the literature
f
is also called an
eigenvector of
D
.

Remark 2.4
.

(1)

If
f\in{\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}
is a Darboux polynomial for
D
, then the ideal
\langle f\rangle\subset{\mathbb{k}}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
is
D
-stable.

(2)

The kernel of a derivation
D
, denoted as
\ker(D)
, is the subspace of Darboux
polynomials of eigenvalue equal to
0
.

(3)

In particular, if

D
is a simple derivation, the only Darboux polynomials are
the constant polynomials, and therefore
\ker(D)=\Bbbk
.

Remark 2.5
.

Let
\Delta\in\operatorname{Der}\bigl({\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}\bigr)
be a locally nilpotent
derivation and consider
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}=\bigl\{e^{t\Delta}\mathrel{:}t\in{\Bbbk}\bigr\}
. Recall that the linear span

\langle\Delta\rangle_{\Bbbk}\subset\operatorname{\operatorname{Der}}\bigl({\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}\bigr)
is
naturally identified with the Lie algebra of
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
and

\ker(\Delta)={\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}^{{\mathbb{G}}_{a}}
, the subalgebra of

{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
-invariants.

Definition 2.6
.

If
\Delta\in\operatorname{Der}\bigl({\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}\bigr)
is a locally nilpotent
derivation, we say that
s\in{\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}
is a
slice
for
\Delta
if
\Delta(s)=1
.

Let
D\in\operatorname{Der}\bigl({\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}\bigr)
be an arbitrary derivation. We say that
s\in{\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}
is a
linear coordinate
for
D
if

D(s)=1
and there
exist polynomials
s_{2},\ldots,s_{n}
such that

{\Bbbk}[s,s_{2},\ldots,s_{n}]={\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}
:

D=\frac{\partial}{\partial s}+\sum_{i=2}^{n}a_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial s_{i}}\ ,\ a_{i}\in{\Bbbk}[s,s_{2},\dots,s_{n}].

3.
Automorphisms of simple derivations: first results

As noted in the introduction, the main object of study of this work is
the isotropy subgroup of a simple derivation (see Definition
1.1
).

3.1.
On the ind-group structure of the isotropy group of a simple derivation

Recall that
{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{n})
is an ind-group, therefore,
{\rm Aut}(D)
being
an isotropy group, it is
also an ind-group. We begin this section by showing that if
D
is
simple, then
{\rm Aut}(D)^{0}\subset{\rm Aut}(D)
,
the unique connected component containing the identity, is an
algebraic group.

First, we recall the following result of Baltazar (see
[
B2016
, Proposition 7]
):

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
D
be a simple derivation of
{\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}
. Then every non trivial element of

{\rm Aut}(D)
admits no fixed point. ∎

As a consequence of Proposition
3.1
above and some general results about ind-groups, we obtain the following description of

{\rm Aut}(D)
as an ind-group.

Theorem 3.2
.

If
D\in\operatorname{Der}\bigl({\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}\bigr)
is simple, then
{\rm Aut}(D)^{0}
is an
affine algebraic group of dimension at most
n
which acts freely on
{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
, and there exists a
family of automorphisms
(\varphi_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
, with
\varphi_{i}\in{\rm Aut}(D)
, such that
\varphi_{0}={\rm Id}
and

{\rm Aut}(D)=\bigcup_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\varphi_{i}{\rm Aut}(D)^{0}.

(1)

In particular
X_{n}=\bigcup_{i=0}^{n}\varphi_{i}{\rm Aut}(D)^{0}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, is a
filtration of
{\rm Aut}(D)
by (affine) algebraic varieties.

Proof.

The first assertion follows from Proposition
3.1

above and
[
FuKr2018
, Propositions 1.8.3 and 7.1.2]
. By

[
FuKr2018
, proposition 1.7.1 and 2.2.1]
it follows that
{\rm Aut}(D)

is a countable disjoint union of connected algebraic varieties:

{\rm Aut}(D)=\bigcup_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}Y_{i}
, such that
Y_{0}={\rm Aut}(D)^{0}
, where

{\rm Aut}(D)^{0}
is a connected and normal algebraic subgroup of
{\rm Aut}(D)
.

If we choose
\varphi_{i}\in Y_{i}
,
i\neq{0}
, then
\varphi_{i}{\rm Aut}(D)^{0}=Y_{i}
, since left
multiplication by
\varphi_{i}
is an isomorphism of ind-varieties. It
follows that
{\rm Aut}(D)=\bigcup_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\varphi_{i}{\rm Aut}(D)^{0}
, where
\varphi_{0}={\rm Id}
.
∎

Question 3.3
.

To our knowledge, there are no known examples of a simple
derivation of
{\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}
with non-connected automorphisms group.

Is
it true that if
D
is a simple derivation, then
{\rm Aut}(D)
is
connected?

In view of Theorem
3.2
, this would
imply that any simple derivation has algebraic automorphisms group.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
D\in\operatorname{Der}\bigl({\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}\bigr)
be a simple derivation and
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(D)
. If
\varphi
is algebraic, then
\varphi
is unipotent.
In particular, if
G\subset{\rm Aut}(D)
is an algebraic subgroup, then
G
is a unipotent subgroup and therefore
G
is a closed connected subgroup of
{\rm Aut}(D)^{0}
.

Proof.

If
\varphi
is algebraic, then there exists a Jordan decomposition

\varphi=\varphi_{s}\varphi_{u}
, with
\varphi_{s}
semi-simple and
\varphi_{u}
unipotent and such that
\varphi_{s},\varphi_{u}\in\overline{\langle\varphi\rangle}\subset{\rm Aut}(D)
. By a result of Furter and Kraft (
[
FuKr2018
, Proposition
15.9.3.]
),

\varphi_{s}
acts in
{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
with a fixed point, and it follows from Proposition
3.1

that
\varphi_{s}
is trivial. Hence,
\varphi=\varphi_{u}
— that is,

\varphi
is unipotent.

If
G\subset{\rm Aut}(D)
is algebraic, then we deduce that

G
is unipotent, so it is connected, proving the second assertion.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

If
D
is a simple derivation and
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(D)
is a locally
finite automorphism, then applying Theorem
3.4
to

G=\overline{\langle\varphi\rangle}\subset{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{n})
we deduce that
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(D)^{0}
.

As a direct follow up of theorems
3.4
and

3.2
, we have the
following characterization of the algebraicity of the automorphisms
group of a simple derivation.

Corollary 3.6
.

If
D\in\operatorname{Der}\bigl({\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}\bigr)
is simple then the following are equivalent:

(1)

The ind-group
{\rm Aut}(D)
is an algebraic group.

(2)

The ind-group
{\rm Aut}(D)
is connected.

(3)

Every element
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(D)
is algebraic.

(4)

Every element
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(D)
is a locally finite
automorphism.

(5)

Every element
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(D)
is a unipotent locally finite
automorphism. ∎

3.2.
Additive subgroups of
{\rm Aut}(D)

Definition 3.7
.

Let
X
be an affine algebraic variety and
\varphi:{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{s}\times X\to X
be a regular action. We say
that
\varphi
is
globally (equivariantly) trivial
, if there
exists an affine algebraic variety
V
and an action
\phi:{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{s}\times({\mathbb{A}}^{s}\times V)
,
\phi\bigl(a,(t,v)\bigr)=(t+a,v)
for all
a\in{\mathbb{G}}^{s}_{a}
,
t\in{\mathbb{A}}^{s}
,
v\in V
, together with an equivariant isomorphism
X\cong{\mathbb{A}}^{s}\times V
.

We say that
\varphi
is
locally trivial
if there exists a cover

X=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\ell}U_{i}
by affine open
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{s}
-stable subsets
such that the restrictions
\varphi|_{{}_{{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{s}\times U_{i}}}:{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{s}\times U_{i}\to U_{i}
are
globally trivial actions.

Let
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{s}\hookrightarrow{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{n})
be a closed immersion. We say
that (the image of)
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{s}

acts by translations
or that

{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
is
a
group of translations
if the
induced action
\varphi:{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{s}\times{\mathbb{A}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
is globally
trivial where
{\mathbb{A}}^{n}\cong{\mathbb{A}}^{s}\times{\mathbb{A}}^{n-s}
and
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{s}
acts by

Remark 3.8

Proposition 4.1

Let

Remark 4.9

(1)

has the form

N_{{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{n})}(H)=\bigl\{(x,v)\mapsto\bigl(Ax+g_{1}(v),g_{2}(v)\bigr)\mathrel{:}A\in\operatorname{GL}_{s}(\Bbbk),g_{1}:V\to{\mathbb{A}}^{s},g_{2}\in{\rm Aut}(V)\bigr\}.

Proof.

We describe an automorphism

pair of

, with

f_{2}:{\mathbb{A}}^{s}\times V\to V

H=\bigl\{(t_{a},\operatorname{Id}_{V})\mathrel{:}a\in\Bbbk^{s}\bigr\}
, and if
f=(f_{1},f_{2})\in N_{{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{n})}(H)
,
we have that
\sigma_{f}:H\to H
,

\sigma_{f}(t_{a},\operatorname{Id}_{V})=f(t_{a},\operatorname{Id}_{V})f^{-1}
is a
morphism of algebraic groups. It follows that there exists

A_{f}\in GL_{s}({\Bbbk})
such that
\sigma_{f}(t_{a},\operatorname{Id}_{V})=(t_{A_{f}a},\operatorname{Id}_{V})
for all

\operatorname{char}(\Bbbk)=0
.

From the equality

f(t_{a},\operatorname{Id}_{V})=(t_{A_{f}a},\operatorname{Id}_{V})f:{\mathbb{A}}^{s}\times V\to{\mathbb{A}}^{s}\times V

we deduce that

f_{1}\bigl(x+a,v)=f_{1}\bigl(x,v\bigr)+A_{f}a\quad,\quad f_{2}(x+a,v)=f_{2}\bigl(x,v\bigr)\text{ for all }a\in\Bbbk^{s}\,,v\in V.

Let
f_{1}(x,v)=\bigl(f_{11}(x,v),\dots f_{1s}(x,v)\bigr)
and consider the

\Bbbk[V][x]

and an easy

f_{1}(x,v)=A_{f}x+g_{1}(v)\,,g_{1}:V\to\Bbbk^{s}

On the other hand, since
f_{2}:{\mathbb{A}}^{s}\times V\to V
is a

{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{s}

.

Finally, applying the same reasoning to the

, we deduce that
g_{2}\in{\rm Aut}(V)
.
∎

Proposition 5.2
.

Let

By Lemma

\begin{split}\overline{D}g_{2}(p)=&D\bigl(p\bigl(g_{2}(v)\bigr)\bigr)=D\bigl(p\bigl(f_{2}(x,v)\bigr)\bigr)=D\bigl(p\bigl(f(x,v)\bigr)\bigr)=Df(p)\\

Theorem 5.4

Let

5.2.
Simple derivations of
{\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}

In this section we study the automorphisms group of a simple derivation

{\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}

(1)

A derivation

(2)

Any simple derivation of

{\Bbbk}[x,y]

Simple derivations of

Let

{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
or possible a countable discrete group:
More precisely,

is unipotent
and of dimension

or

, its action being by translations.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let

be a simple derivation such that

. Then

{\rm Aut}(D)={\rm Aut}(D)^{0}
. Moreover, there exist coordinates such that

\begin{split}{\rm Aut}(D)&=\bigl\{(x_{1}+a,x_{2},x_{3})\mathrel{:}a\in\Bbbk\}\\
D&=\sum a_{i}(x_{2},x_{3})\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\end{split}

Proof.

If
u\in{\rm Aut}(D)^{0}
is a non trivial automorphism, then by
Proposition
3.1
,

has no fixed point but, by a
result of Kaliman (see
[
Kal2004
]
), a non trivial unipotent
automorphism

without fixed point is conjugated to
a translation. It follows from Proposition

that there exist coordinates as in Equation
(

A family of simple derivations of
\Bbbk[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}]

Next, we consider the four dimensional affine space and
describe the automorphisms group of a simple
derivation
D\in\operatorname{Der}\bigl(\Bbbk[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}]\bigr)
that admits
a linear coordinate.

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
D\in\operatorname{Der}\bigl({\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}\bigr)
be a
simple derivation that admits a linear coordinate
s
. Let

\phi\in{\rm Aut}(D)^{0}
and
\Delta
be a locally nilpotent derivation such
that
\phi=e^{\Delta}
(see Remark
2.5
). Then
\Delta(s)\in{\Bbbk}
. In
particular, then either
\phi(s)=s
or
\phi
is a translation along the
direction of
s
.

Proof.

Since
\phi\in{\rm Aut}(D)
, then
[D,\Delta]=0
and therefore

D\bigl(\Delta(s)\bigr)=0
, so
\Delta(s)\in{\Bbbk}
.

If
\Delta(s)=0
, then
\phi(s)=s
.
On the other hand, if
\Delta(s)=c\neq 0
, then

\Delta(c^{-1}s)=1
, i.e.
c^{-1}s
is a slice for

\Delta
. Hence,

{\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}=\ker(\Delta)[c^{-1}s]
. Since
c^{-1}s
is a
coordinate we may, up to conjugation, assume
x_{1}=c^{-1}s
. Thus

\ker(\Delta)={\Bbbk}[x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}]
,
i.e.
\phi
is
translation along the
s
coordinate.
∎

Now we specialize Lemma
5.8
to the case where

n=4
. We begin by a useful result.

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
D\in\operatorname{Der}\bigl({\Bbbk}[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}]\bigr)
be a simple

nontrivial element of
{\rm Aut}(D)^{0}
is
conjugate to a translation.

Proof.

Let
\phi\in{\rm Aut}(D)^{0}
and let
\Delta
be a locally nilpotent
derivation such that
\phi=e^{\Delta}
. By Lemma

5.8
, it suffices to prove that if
s
is a
linear coordinate for
D
such that
\Delta(s)=0
, then

\phi
is a translation. By Proposition
3.11
we know that

{\mathbb{G}}_{a}=\{e^{t\Delta};t\in{\Bbbk}\}
acts over
{\mathbb{A}}^{4}
in a locally trivial, and
in particular a proper,
way. Then, the result follows from

[
Kal2018
, Thm. 01]
.
∎

Theorem 5.10
.

Let
D\in\operatorname{Der}\bigl({\Bbbk}[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}]\bigr)
be a simple
derivation that admits a
linear coordinate. If
\dim{\rm Aut}(D)^{0}>0
, then
{\rm Aut}(D)^{0}

acts by translations. Moreover, if
\dim{\rm Aut}(D)^{0}=2
, then

{\rm Aut}(D)
is algebraic.

Proof.

Recall that, by Theorem
4.7
,
{\rm Aut}(D)^{0}
is a unipotent
group of dimension
1
or
2
. In the case of dimension 1 the
theorem is a direct consequence of Lemma
5.9
.

Now, we suppose that
\dim{\rm Aut}(D)^{0}=2
. It is well known that in this
case
{\rm Aut}(D)^{0}=U_{1}\times U_{2}
,
U_{i}\cong{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
, — recall that

\operatorname{char}{\Bbbk}=0
. Consider two generators
u_{1},u_{2}
of

U_{1}
and
U_{2}
respectively. Again by Lemma
5.9
we may suppose that

u_{1}
is a translation with respect to
x_{1}
; therefore,
we deduce from Lemma
5.1
that
u_{2}=\bigl(ax_{1}+g_{1}(x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}),g_{2}(x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}),g_{3}(x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}),g_{4}(x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})\bigr)
;
moreover,
since
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
commute, we deduce that

. Then the
geometric quotient
q_{1}:{\mathbb{A}}^{4}\to X_{1}:={\mathbb{A}}^{4}/\overline{\langle U_{1}\rangle}
exists and
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{A}}^{3}
, and
D
induces a simple derivation

\overline{D}\in\operatorname{Der}\bigl({\Bbbk}[X_{1}]\bigr)=\operatorname{Der}\bigl({\Bbbk}[x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}]\bigr)

u_{1}
and
u_{2}

commute, we deduce that

acts on
X_{1}
, in such a way that

5.7
that either the action of

over

is trivial

If

acts trivially, it follows that
u_{2}=\bigl(x_{1}+g_{1}(x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}),x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}\bigr)

acts by translations over

.
Changing coordinates in

assume that
u_{2}=\bigl(x_{1}+w(x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}),x_{2}+1,x_{3},x_{4})

\Delta

, then

x_{2}
, then

\displaystyle w

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u_{2}(x_{1})-x_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta(x_{1})+\frac{1}{2}\Delta^{2}(x_{1})+\cdots+\frac{1}{\ell!}\Delta^{\ell}(x_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}(a)+\cdots+\frac{1}{\ell!}\frac{\partial^{\ell-1}}{\partial x_{2}^{\ell-1}}(a).

Let

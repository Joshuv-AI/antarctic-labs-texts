First achievement of high-confinement mode in proton-boron plasmas
Authors: Yumin Wang, Yuejiang Shi, Dong Guo, Yihang Zhao, Guang Yang, Ao Wang, Hongyue Li, Shaodong Song, Ying Yuan, Xuxu Zhang, Xiang Gu, Cong Zhang, Jia Li, Songjian Li, Renyi Tao, Xianli Huang, Yupeng Zhang, Zhaokun Wang, Qifeng Xie, Lei Li, Lei Han, Xin Zhao, Huasheng Xie, Xianming Song, Jiaqi Dong, Y-K Martin Peng, Yunfeng Liang, Minsheng Liu, the EXL-50U Team
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

High-confinement mode (H-mode) discharges have been achieved for the first time in proton-boron (p-11B) plasmas on the EXL-50U spherical torus (ST). A working gas mixture consisting of 30% diborane (B2H6) and 70% hydrogen (H2) was employed, along with boron powder injection for real-time boronization, during the discharges. The boron concentration in the plasma is estimated to be approximately 10%. H-mode operation was obtained using neutral beam injection (NBI) with injected power ranging from 0.5 to 2.5 MW at an on-axis magnetic field of 0.8 T. Edge-localized modes (ELMs) with a characteristic frequency of about 100 Hz were observed. A clear edge pedestal structure in the electron density profile was formed during the H-mode phase. These results represent the first demonstration of H-mode operation in p-11B plasmas in magnetic confinement devices.

The H-mode was first discovered in the ASDEX tokamak and was subsequently reproduced in a wide range of conventional tokamaks.1 In STs, H-mode operation has also been achieved, such as MAST,2 MAST Upgrade, and NSTX.3 Owing to its ability to significantly enhance plasma pressure and energy confinement time, H-mode has become the standard operational regime envisioned for future fusion devices, such as ITER.4

Previously, H-mode operation has been achieved exclusively in hydrogen, deuterium, and tritium plasmas, since the D-T reaction possesses the largest fusion cross-section at achievable plasma temperatures. H-mode in helium plasma was first achieved in ASDEX Upgrade5 and then in other devices. In recent years, p-11B fusion has attracted increasing theoretical interest due to its potential for aneutronic operation.6 Experimentally, alpha particles generated from p-11B fusion reactions have been successfully detected in a magnetically confined plasma.7 Building on these theoretical and experimental advances, a roadmap for p-11B fusion development has been proposed by ENN Science and Technology Development Co., Ltd..8 EHL-2, an ST designed for the demonstration of thermal p-11B fusion reactions, has been designed and is currently under construction.9 The device is equipped with a total auxiliary heating power of 31 MW, and H-mode operation has been predicted based on empirical scaling laws derived from hydrogen and its isotope plasmas. However, despite extensive studies of H-mode in hydrogenic and helium plasmas, the compatibility of H-mode operation with p-11B plasmas has not yet been experimentally demonstrated using the existing EXL-50U device.

The experimental setup

EXL-50U is an upgraded version of EXL-50, featuring the installation of central solenoid (CS) coils to enable ohmic heating and assist plasma current ramp-up.10 A photograph and model of EXL-50U are shown in Figures 1A and 1B, respectively. An electron cyclotron resonance heating (ECRH) system with a source power of 0.8 MW has been installed for plasma heating and current drive. The NBI system consists of two injectors: the first beam delivers up to 1 MW at a beam energy of 25–30 keV, while the second beam delivers 1 MW at 25 keV. Both beams are injected in the co-current direction and are primarily used for ion heating.

The major radius of EXL-50U varies from 0.6 to 0.9 m, corresponding to an aspect ratio in the range of 1.4–1.8. The magnetic field at the magnetic axis can be adjusted between 0.5 and 1.2 T. Divertor plates are installed inside EXL-50U to protect the vacuum vessel.

The lines of sight of the diagnostics and divertor plates are shown schematically in Figure 1C. The HCN interferometer measures the vertically line-averaged electron density at a major radius of R = 0.7 m. The Thomson scattering system provides electron density and temperature profiles at R = 0.8 m, with a total of 15 measurement points and a vertical spatial resolution of 4 cm. Edge electron density profiles are measured horizontally by reflectometry systems installed on the midplane of the low-field side (LFS). In addition, Balmer-α emission from hydrogen at the plasma edge is detected along a horizontal line of sight from the LFS toward the center post at Z = 0.147 m. The boron powder injectors are installed on the top of EXL-50U at R = 0.8 m.

A mixed gas consisting of 30% diborane (B2H6) and 70% hydrogen (H2) is used for wall conditioning, and the same gas mixture is employed for routine hydrogen-boron discharges. Boron powder with a characteristic particle diameter of approximately 150 μm m is injected from the top of the device at a major radius of R = 0.7 m for real-time wall conditioning, which could also increase the boron concentration in the plasma.

The vacuum vessel can be baked up to 160°C at the beginning of each experimental campaign to desorb trapped gases. The armor tiles of the center post are made of carbon fiber composite (CFC), while tungsten is used for the divertor plates.

Characteristics of H-mode discharge for p-11B plasma in EXL-50U

Typical ELMy H-mode waveforms obtained on EXL-50U are shown in Figure 1D. The plasma is operated in a double-null (DN) configuration. During the flat-top phase of this discharge, the plasma elongation, κ, and triangularity, δ, are 1.38 and 0.27, respectively. The total NBI heating power is 1 MW. The edge safety factor, q95, evaluated using EFIT reconstruction, is 7.79. The magnetic field at R = 0.7 m is 0.8 T.

The L-H transition occurs at t = 0.42 s, characterized by a simultaneous increase in electron density, electron and ion temperatures, and the diamagnetic stored energy. A reduction in the Hα emission is observed immediately after the L-H transition, followed by the onset of ELMs with a characteristic frequency of approximately 100 Hz. An H-L back transition is observed after the NBI heating is turned off.

Multiple L-H-L transitions are also observed, as shown in Figure 1E. The edge electron density profiles in both L-mode and H-mode are presented in Figure 1F. A clear pedestal structure is formed in the edge density profile during the H-mode phase. A reduction in the Hα emission is observed during H-mode intervals; however, no clear ELMs are detected in this discharge. Boron powder is injected in this shot to increase plasma density and real-time boronization.

On EXL-50U, the measured Hα emission predominantly originates from the center post rather than the divertor region, which may explain why a sharp drop in the Hα signal at the L-H transition is not clearly observed. The estimated energy confinement time, τE, increases from 16 ms in L-mode to 40 ms in H-mode for this discharge, indicating a substantial improvement in energy confinement.

Conclusion

ELMy H-mode discharges with a characteristic ELM frequency of approximately 100 Hz have been successfully obtained on the EXL-50U ST. In these discharges, a fueling gas mixture consisting of 30% diborane (B2H6) and 70% hydrogen (H2) is employed, and the boron concentration in the plasma is estimated to be around 10%. These experimental results demonstrate that p-11B plasmas are compatible with H-mode operation. Since diborane boronization will be adopted in ITER, these results provide a useful experimental basis for its operation. The transport behavior in boron-rich plasmas supports scenario development and model validation. In addition, boron deposition on plasma-facing components can alter heat transfer and reflectivity; thus, understanding these effects is important for the design of divertor and blanket cooling systems, as well as diagnostic first mirrors in ITER.

Future work will focus on the qualitative investigation of the boron concentration effects on confinement, determination of the L-H transition power, and energy confinement scaling law. The control of ELMs will also be investigated in the future.

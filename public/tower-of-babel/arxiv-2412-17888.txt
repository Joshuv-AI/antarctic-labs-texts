Stability Bounds for the Unfolded Forward-Backward Algorithm
Emilie Chouzenoux, Cécile Della Valle, Jean-Christophe Pesquet

Abstract
We consider a neural network architecture designed to solve inverse problems where the degradation operator is linear and known. This architecture is constructed by unrolling a forward-backward algorithm derived from the minimization of an objective function that combines a data-fidelity term, a Tikhonov-type regularization term, and a potentially nonsmooth convex penalty. The robustness of this inversion method to input perturbations is analyzed theoretically. Ensuring robustness complies with the principles of inverse problem theory, as it ensures both the continuity of the inversion method and the resilience to small noise - a critical property given the known vulnerability of deep neural networks to adversarial perturbations. A key novelty of our work lies in examining the robustness of the proposed network to perturbations in its bias, which represents the observed data in the inverse problem. Additionally, we provide numerical illustrations of the analytical Lipschitz bounds derived in our analysis.

Stability analysis, neural networks, deep unrolling, forward-backward, proximal-gradient, inverse problems

Related works and contributions.

There has been a plethora of techniques developed to invert models of the form
(
1
).
Among these methods, Tikhonov-type methods are attractive from a theoretical viewpoint, especially because they provide good convergence rate as the noise level decreases, as shown in
Hegland (1995)
or
Hofmann and Yamamoto (2005)
.
Optimization techniques
Combettes and
Pesquet (2011)
are then classically used to solve Problem (
4
).
However, limitations of such methods may be encountered in their implementation.
Indeed, certain parameters such as gradient descent stepsizes or the regularization coefficient
need to be set, as discussed in
Åkesson and Daun (2008)
or
Daun et al. (2006)
.
The latter parameter depends on the noise level, as shown in
Engl (1996)
, which is not always easy to estimate.
In practical scenarios, methods like the L-curve method (see
Hansen (1999)
) can be used to set the regularization parameter.
However, when dealing with more than one parameter, their application becomes computationally expensive.

Moreover, incorporating constraints on the solution may be difficult in such approaches, and often reduces to projecting the resulting solution onto the desired set.
These reasons justify the use of a neural network structure to avoid laborious calibration of the parameters and to easily incorporate constraints on the solution.

The use of neural networks for solving inverse problems has become increasingly popular,
especially in the image processing community.
A rich panel of approaches have been proposed,
either adapted to the sparsity of the data
Antholzer et al. (2019)
;
Kofler et al. (2018)
,
or mimicking variational models
Hammernik et al. (2018)
;
Adler and Öktem (2017)
, or iterating learned operators
Aggarwal et al. (2018)
;
Meinhardt et al. (2017)
;
Pesquet et al. (2021)
;
Hasannasab et al. (2020)
;
Galinier et al. (2020)
,
or
adapting
Tikhonov method
Li et al. (2020)
.
The successful numerical results of the aforementioned works raise two theoretical questions: in cases when these methods are based on the iterations of an optimization algorithm, do they converge (in the algorithmic sense)? Are these inversion methods stable or robust?

In iterative approaches,
a regularization operator is learned, either in the form of a proximity (or denoiser) operator as
Meinhardt et al. (2017)
;
Aggarwal et al. (2018)
;
Galinier et al. (2020)
, of a regularization term
Li et al. (2020)
,
of a pseudodifferential operator
Bubba et al. (2021)
,
or of its gradient
Gilton et al. (2019)
;
Wu et al. (2020)
.
Strong connections also exist with Plug-and-Play methods
Ryu et al. (2019)
;
Sun et al. (2019)
;
Pesquet et al. (2021)
, where the regularization operator is a pre-trained neural network.
Such strategies have in particular enabled high-quality imaging restoration or tomography inversion
Wu et al. (2020)
. Here, the nonexpansiveness of the neural network is a core property to establish convergence of the algorithm
Wu et al. (2020)
;
Pesquet et al. (2021)
,
although recent works have provided convergence guarantees using alternative tools
Hurault et al. (2022)
.

Other recent works solve linear inverse problems by unrolling the optimization iterative process in the form of a network architecture as in
Gregor and LeCun (2010)
;
Borgerding and
Schniter (2016)
;
Jin et al. (2017)
.
Here the number of iterations is fixed, instead of iterating until convergence, and the network is often trained in an end-to-end fashion.
Neural network frameworks offer powerful differential programming capabilities for learning hyper-parameters in an unrolled optimization algorithm as in
Bertocchi et al. (2020)
;
Natarajan and
Caro (2020)
;
Monga et al. (2021)
;
Gharbi et al. (2024)
;
Le et al. (2024)
.

With respect to a traditional optimization method applied to (
4
)-(
3
),
the use of these data-driven techniques provides an increased flexibility leading to a better solution of the inverse problem.

All of the above strategies have shown very good numerical results.
However, few studies have been conducted on their theoretical properties, especially on their stability.
The study of the robustness of such a structure is often empirical, based on a series of numerical tests, as performed in
Genzel et al. (2023)
;
Le et al. (2024)
.
In
Li et al. (2020)
, stability properties are established at low noise regime when using neural networks regularization.
A fine characterization of the convergence conditions of recurrent neural network and of their stability via the estimation of a Lipschitz constant is done in
Combettes and Pesquet (2020a)
;
Combettes and Pesquet (2020b)
.
In particular, the Lipschitz constant estimated in
Combettes and Pesquet (2020b)
is more accurate than in basic approaches
which often rely in computing the product of the norms of the linear weight operators
of each layer as in
Serrurier
et al. (2021)
;
Cisse et al. (2017)
.
Thanks to the aforementioned works, proofs of stability/convergence have been demonstrated on specific neural networks applied to classification (e.g.,
Neacşu et al. (2024a)
;
Neacşu et al. (2024b)
), or inverse problems (e.g.,
Pesquet et al. (2021)
;
Hasannasab et al. (2020)
;
Bertocchi et al. (2020)
). The analysis carried out in this article is in the line of these references.

Our contributions in this paper are the following.

1.

We propose a neural network architecture to solve the inverse problem (
1
), incorporating the proximity operator of a potentially nonsmooth convex function as its activation function. This design enables the imposition of constraints on the sought solution. A key advantage of this architecture lies in its foundation on the unrolling of a forward-backward algorithm, which ensures the network structure is both interpretable and frugal in terms of the number of parameters to be learned.

2.

We study theoretically and numerically the stability of the so-built neural network. The sensitivity analysis is conducted with respect to the observed data
y
, which corresponds to a bias term in each layer of Model (
6
).
This analysis is more general than the one performed in
Bertocchi et al. (2020)
, in which only the impact of the initialization was considered.

Outline.

The outline of the paper is as follows. In Section
2
, we recall the theoretical background of our work and specify the notation, which follows the framework in
Bauschke and Combettes (2017)
.
Section
3
introduces a class of dynamical systems with a leakage factor, providing a more general framework than the neural network defined by (

).
In Section
4

\mathscr{X}\times\mathscr{X}
, building upon the results from

and

By stability, we mean that the network output remains controlled with respect to both its initial input

and the bias term

We also provide sufficient conditions for the averagedness of the network. Finally, we show how stability properties for network (

Section

We introduce the elements of convex analysis

Let us consider the Hilbert space

An operator

(\forall(x,y)\,\in\,\mathscr{X}\times\mathscr{X})\qquad\|Sx-Sy\|\leq\|x-y\|\;.

Moreover,

1.

firmly nonexpansive if

(\forall(x,y)\,\in\,\mathscr{X}\times\mathscr{X})\qquad\|Sx-Sy\|^{2}+\|({\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}-S)x-({\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}-S)y\|^{2}\leq\|x-y\|^{2}\;;

2.

a Banach contraction if there exists
\kappa\in]0,1[

(\forall(x,y)\,\in\,\mathscr{X}\times\mathscr{X})\qquad\|Sx-Sy\|\leq\kappa\|x-y\|\;.

(11)

If

On the other hand, when

A way of recovering the convergence of the iterates

If

has a fixed point and it is averaged,
then the iterates
(S^{n}x)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converge weakly to a fixed point.
Note that

is firmly nonexpansive if and only

4.1
Assumptions

We will make the following assumption on the degradation operator
T
and the regularization operators
(D_{n})_{1\leq n\leq m}
:

Operators
T^{*}T
and
(D_{n}^{*}D_{n})_{1\leq n\leq m}

can be diagonalized
in an orthonormal basis

(v_{p})_{p}
of
\mathscr{X}
.

The existence of such an eigenbasis is satisfied in the following cases:

•

T
and
(D_{n})_{1\leq n\leq m}
are compact operators (which guarantees the existence of their singular value decompositions),
and
(D_{n}^{*}D_{n})_{0\leq n\leq m}
commute pairwise if we set

D_{0}=T

•

\mathscr{X}=\mathscr{Y}
is the space

of square summable complex-valued functions defined on
[0,\mathcal{T}]

is a filter (i.e., circular convolutive operator) and, for every
n\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
,

is a single-input
c
-output filter that maps every signal
x\in\mathscr{X}
to a vector
(D_{n,1}x,\ldots,D_{n,c}x)\in\mathscr{X}^{c}
. In this case,
the diagonalization is performed in the Fourier basis defined as

(\forall p\in\mathbb{Z})(\forall t\in[0,\mathcal{T}])\quad v_{p}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathcal{T}}}\exp\left(2\pi\imath\frac{pt}{\mathcal{T}}\right).

•

\mathscr{X}=\mathscr{Y}
is the space
\ell^{2}(N)

of complex-valued sequences
x=(x_{n})_{0\leq n\leq N-1}
equipped with the standard Hermitian norm,

is a discrete periodic filter
and, for every
n\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
,
D_{n}
is a single-input
c
-output periodic filter that maps every signal
x\in\mathscr{X}
to a vector
(D_{n,1}x,\ldots,D_{n,c}x)\in\mathscr{X}^{c}

(\forall(p,n)\in\{0,\ldots,N-1\}^{2})\quad v_{p}(n)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\exp\left(2\pi\imath\frac{pn}{N}\right).

•

The two previous examples extend to

d

for images),
defined on
[0,\mathcal{T}_{1}]\times\cdots\times[0,\mathcal{T}_{d}]

or
\{0,\ldots,N_{1}-1\}\times\cdots\times\{0,\ldots,N_{d}-1\}
.

Based on the above assumption,
we define the respective nonnegative real-valued eigenvalues

(\beta_{T,p})_{p}
and
(\beta_{D_{n},p})_{p}
of

and

with
n\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
, as well as
the following quantities,
for every eigenspace index

and

,

\displaystyle\beta_{p}^{(n)}=\frac{1}{1+\lambda_{n}\chi_{n}}\Big(1-\lambda_{n}(\beta_{T,p}+\beta_{D_{n},p})\Big)\;,

(21)

\displaystyle\beta_{i,n,p}=\prod_{j=i}^{n}\beta_{p}^{(j)}\;,

(22)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\beta}_{i,n,p}=\sum_{j=i}^{n-1}\beta_{p}^{(n)}\cdots\beta_{p}^{(j+1)}\frac{\lambda_{j}\eta_{i,j-1}}{1+\lambda_{j}\chi_{j}}+\frac{\lambda_{n}\eta_{i,n-1}}{1+\lambda_{n}\chi_{n}}\;,

with the convention

.
As limit cases, we have

\beta_{n,n,p}=\beta_{p}^{(n)},\quad\widetilde{\beta}_{n,n,p}=\frac{\lambda_{n}}{1+\lambda_{n}\chi_{n}}.

(24)

Note that
(\beta_{p}^{(n)},v_{p})_{p}
,

(\beta_{i,n,p},v_{p})_{p}
,
and

are the eigensystems of

4.2
Stability results for the virtual network

We first recall some existing results on the stability of neural networks
(
Combettes and Pesquet, 2020a
, Proposition 3.6(iii))

(
Combettes and Pesquet, 2020b
, Theorem 4.2)
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
m>1
be an integer, let
(\mathcal{H}_{i})_{0\leq i\leq m}
be non null Hilbert spaces.
For every
n\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
, let
U_{n}
be a bounded linear operator from
\mathcal{H}_{n-1}
to
\mathcal{H}_{n}

and let
Q_{n}\colon\mathcal{H}_{n}\to\mathcal{H}_{n}
be a firmly nonexpansive operator.
Set
U=U_{m}\circ\cdots\circ U_{1}
and

\theta_{m}=\|U\|\\
+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\sum_{1\leq j_{1}<\ldots<j_{k}\leq m-1}\|U_{m}\circ\cdots\circ U_{j_{k}+1}\|\,\|U_{j_{k}}\circ\cdots\circ U_{j_{k-1}+1}\|\cdots\|U_{j_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ U_{1}\|.

(25)

Let
S=Q_{m}\circ U_{m}\circ\cdots\circ Q_{1}\circ U_{1}
.
Then the following hold:

1.

\theta_{m}/2^{m-1}
is a Lipschitz constant of
S
.

2.

Let
\alpha\in[1/2,1]
.
If
\mathcal{H}_{m}=\mathcal{H}_{0}
and

\|U-2^{m}(1-\alpha){\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}\|-\|U\|+2\theta_{m}\leq 2^{m}\alpha\;,

(26)

then
S
is
\alpha
-averaged.

In light of these results,
we will now analyze the properties of the virtual network (
12
)
based on the spectral quantities
defined at the end of Section
4.1
,
and the parameters
(\lambda_{n})_{1\leq n\leq m}
,
\overline{\chi}
(and possibly
(\tau_{n})_{1\leq n\leq m}
).
One of the main difficulties with respect to the case already studied by
Bertocchi et al. (2020)

is that, in our context, the involved operators
(U_{n})_{1\leq n\leq m}
are no longer self-adjoint.

A preliminary result will be needed:

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
be
the total number of layers.
For every layer indices
n\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
and

i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
,
the norm of
U_{n}\circ\cdots\circ U_{i}
is
equal to
\sqrt{a_{i,n}}
with

a_{i,n}=\frac{1}{2}\sup_{p}\left(\beta_{i,n,p}^{2}+\widetilde{\beta}_{i,n,p}^{2}+\eta_{i,n}^{2}+\sqrt{(\beta_{i,n,p}^{2}+\widetilde{\beta}_{i,n,p}^{2}+\eta_{i,n}^{2})^{2}-4\beta_{i,n,p}^{2}\eta_{i,n}^{2}}\right)\;,

(27)

where
p
indexes the eigenspaces of
T^{*}T
.

Proof.

Thanks to expressions (
12
), (
18
), (
19
), and (
20
),
we can calculate the norm of
U_{n}\circ\cdots\circ U_{i}
.
For every
z=(x,b)
,
U_{n}\circ\cdots\circ U_{i}z=(W_{i,n}x+\widetilde{W}_{i,n}b,\eta_{i,n}b)
and

\begin{split}\|U_{n}\circ\cdots\circ U_{i}z\|^{2}=&\;\|W_{i,n}x+\widetilde{W}_{i,n}b\|^{2}+\eta_{i,n}^{2}\|b\|^{2}\\
=&\;\|W_{i,n}x\|^{2}+2\langle W_{i,n}x,\widetilde{W}_{i,n}b\rangle+\|\widetilde{W}_{i,n}b\|^{2}+\eta_{i,n}^{2}\|b\|^{2}\;.\end{split}

Let
(\beta_{i,n,p},v_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}

defined by (
22
)
and
(\widetilde{\beta}_{i,n,p},v_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}

defined by (
23
)
be the respective eigensystems of
W_{i,n}
and
\widetilde{W}_{i,n}
.
Let us decompose
(x,b)
on the basis of eigenvectors
(v_{p})_{p}
of
T^{*}T
, as

\begin{cases}\displaystyle x=\sum_{p}\xi_{p}\,v_{p}\;,\\
\displaystyle b=\sum_{p}\zeta_{p}\,v_{p}\;.\end{cases}

(28)

In the following, we will assume that
\mathscr{X}
is a real Hilbert space. A similar proof can be followed for complex Hilbert spaces.
We have then

\|U_{n}\circ\cdots\circ U_{i}z\|^{2}=\;\sum_{p}\beta_{i,n,p}^{2}\xi_{p}^{2}+2\sum_{p}\beta_{i,n,p}\widetilde{\beta}_{i,n,p}\xi_{p}\zeta_{p}+\sum_{p}(\widetilde{\beta}_{i,n,p}^{2}+\eta_{i,n}^{2})\zeta_{p}^{2}\;.

By definition of the operator norm,

\|U_{n}\circ\cdots\circ U_{i}\|^{2}=\;\underset{\|x\|^{2}+\|b\|^{2}=1}{\sup}\left(\sum_{p}\beta_{i,n,p}^{2}\,\xi_{p}^{2}+(\eta_{i,n}^{2}+\widetilde{\beta}_{i,n,p}^{2})\,\zeta_{p}^{2}+2\beta_{i,n,p}\widetilde{\beta}_{i,n,p}\,\xi_{p}\,\zeta_{p}\right)\;.

Note that, for every integer
p
and
\omega_{p}=(\xi_{p},\zeta_{p})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

\beta_{i,n,p}^{2}\,\xi_{p}^{2}+(\eta_{i,n}^{2}+\widetilde{\beta}_{i,n,p}^{2})\,\zeta_{p}^{2}+2\beta_{i,n,p}\widetilde{\beta}_{i,n,p}\,\xi_{p}\,\zeta_{p}=\omega_{p}^{\top}A_{i,n,p}\,\omega_{p}

(29)

where

A_{i,n,p}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\beta_{i,n,p}^{2}&{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\beta_{i,n,p}\widetilde{\beta}_{i,n,p}}\\
\widetilde{\beta}_{i,n,p}\beta_{i,n,p}&\eta_{i,n}^{2}+\widetilde{\beta}_{i,n,p}^{2}\end{array}\right)\;.

Hence,

\|U_{n}\circ\cdots\circ U_{i}\|^{2}=\;\underset{z=(\omega_{p})_{p},\|z\|=1}{\sup}\sum_{p}\omega_{p}^{\top}A_{i,n,p}\,\omega_{p}\;.

(30)

For every index
p
, let
\nu_{i,n,p}
be the maximum eigenvalue of the symmetric positive semidefinite matrix
A_{i,n,p}
.
We have

\sum_{p}\omega_{p}^{\top}A_{i,n,p}\,\omega_{p}\leq\sum_{p}\nu_{i,n,p}\|\omega_{p}\|^{2}\leq\left(\sup_{p}\,\nu_{i,n,p}\right)\sum_{p}\|\omega_{p}\|^{2}=\left(\sup_{p}\,\nu_{i,n,p}\right)\|z\|^{2}\;.

Altogether with (
30
),
this shows that

\|U_{n}\circ\cdots\circ U_{i}\|^{2}\leq\sup_{p}\,\nu_{i,n,p}\;.

In addition, from the definition of
the supremum, for every
\epsilon>0
,
there exists
p^{*}
such that

\sup_{p}\,\nu_{i,n,p}-\epsilon<\nu_{i,n,p^{*}}.

By setting
z^{*}=(\omega^{*}_{p})_{p}
where

\omega^{*}_{p}=0
if

p\neq p^{*}
, and

\omega^{*}_{p^{*}}
is a unit norm eigenvector
associated with eigenvalue
\nu_{i,n,p^{*}}
,

\sum_{p}\omega_{p}^{\top}A_{i,n,p}\,\omega_{p}=\omega_{p^{*}}^{\top}A_{i,n,p^{*}}\,\omega_{p^{*}}=\nu_{i,n,p^{*}}>\sup_{p}\,\nu_{i,n,p}-\epsilon\;.

Since
\epsilon
can be chosen arbitrarily small, we deduce that

\|U_{n}\circ\cdots\circ U_{i}\|^{2}=\sup_{p}\,\nu_{i,n,p}\;.

(31)

We finally note that
the two eigenvalues of matrix
A_{i,n,p}
are the roots of the characteristic polynomial

\begin{split}(\forall\nu\in\mathbb{R})\quad\text{det}(A_{i,n,p}-\nu{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}_{2})=&\;(\beta_{i,n,p}^{2}-\nu)(\widetilde{\beta}_{i,n,p}^{2}+\eta_{i,n}^{2}-\nu)-\beta_{i,n,p}^{2}\widetilde{\beta}_{i,n,p}^{2}\\
=&\;\nu^{2}-(\beta_{i,n,p}^{2}+\widetilde{\beta}_{i,n,p}^{2}+\eta_{i,n}^{2})\nu+\beta_{i,n,p}^{2}\eta_{i,n}^{2}\;.\end{split}

The discriminant of this second-order polynomial reads

\begin{split}\Delta_{i,n,p}=&\;(\beta_{i,n,p}^{2}+\widetilde{\beta}_{i,n,p}^{2}+\eta_{i,n}^{2})^{2}-4\beta_{i,n,p}^{2}\eta_{i,n}^{2}\\
=&\;(\beta_{i,n,p}^{2}-\widetilde{\beta}_{i,n,p}^{2}-\eta_{i,n}^{2})^{2}+4\beta_{i,n,p}^{2}\widetilde{\beta}_{i,n,p}^{2}\;\geq 0\;.\end{split}

Therefore, for every integer
p
,

\nu_{i,n,p}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\beta_{i,n,p}^{2}+\widetilde{\beta}_{i,n,p}^{2}+\eta_{i,n}^{2}+\sqrt{(\beta_{i,n,p}^{2}+\widetilde{\beta}_{i,n,p}^{2}+\eta_{i,n}^{2})^{2}-4\beta_{i,n,p}^{2}\eta_{i,n}^{2}}\right)\;.

(32)

It then follows from (
31
) that

\|U_{n}\circ\cdots\circ U_{i}\|^{2}=a_{i,n}\;.

∎

Remark 4.1
.

The previous result assumes that the product space
\mathscr{X}\times\mathscr{X}
is equipped with the standard norm. Since we may be interested in the stability w.r.t. variations of the observed data
y
, and
b_{0}=T^{*}y
, it might be more insightful to consider instead the following weighted norm:

(\forall z=(x,b)\in\mathscr{X}\times\mathscr{X})\quad\|z\|=\sqrt{\|x\|^{2}+\|b\|_{\varpi}^{2}}\,,

(33)

where
b
is decomposed as in (
28

\|b\|_{\varpi}^{2}=\sum_{p}\frac{|\zeta_{p}|^{2}}{\varpi_{p}}\,,

(34)

and

is such that
\inf_{p}\varpi_{p}>0

and
\sup_{p}\varpi_{p}<+\infty
. The latter weights aim to compensate the effect of
T^{*}
on the observed data.
This space renormalization is possible since
the operator
Q_{n}
remains firmly nonexpansive after this norm change, because of its specific structure.

As shown in Appendix
7
,

the expression of
a_{i,n}

for
n\in\{1,\ldots,m\}

and
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}

in Lemma
4.1
is then modified as follows:

a_{i,n}=\frac{1}{2}\sup_{p}\left(\beta_{i,n,p}^{2}+\varpi_{p}\widetilde{\beta}_{i,n,p}^{2}+\eta_{i,n}^{2}+\sqrt{(\beta_{i,n,p}^{2}+\varpi_{p}\widetilde{\beta}_{i,n,p}^{2}+\eta_{i,n}^{2})^{2}-4\beta_{i,n,p}^{2}\eta_{i,n}^{2}}\right)\;.

(35)

Proposition 4.4
.

Let

be the virtual Model (

)
without leakage factor, i.e., for every

\eta_{n}=1
.
Assume that

of the unfolded algorihm associated with

is distinct from its output

associated with
(x,b^{\prime})

Then the Lipschitz constant of

is greater than 1.

Proof.

If
\eta_{1,m}=1
then,
for every
n\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
,

z_{n}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}x_{n}\\
b_{n}\end{array}\right),\quad z_{n}=Q_{n}(U_{n}z_{n-1})\;,\quad\text{with}\quad\begin{cases}\displaystyle Q_{n}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}R_{n}\\
{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}\end{array}\right)\;,\\
\\
\displaystyle U_{n}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}W_{n}&V_{n}\\
0&{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}\end{array}\right)\;.\end{cases}

(44)

Suppose that the
virtual network
S
is nonexpansive.
Thus, for every
(x,b)\in\mathscr{X}^{2}
and
(x^{\prime},b^{\prime})\in\mathscr{X}^{2}

\|S(x,b)-S(x^{\prime},b^{\prime})\|\leq\|x-x^{\prime}\|^{2}+\|b-b^{\prime}\|_{\varpi}^{2}

Let

(resp.

) denote the first

\mathscr{X}
of

(resp.
S(x^{\prime},b^{\prime})
).

Since

\begin{split}\|S(x,b)-S(x,b^{\prime})\|^{2}=&\;\Big\|\left(\begin{array}[]{c}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}x_{m}-x^{\prime}_{m}}\\
b-b^{\prime}\end{array}\right)\Big\|^{2}\\

Consequently, when

whatever the choice of

.
This contradicts our standing assumption.
∎

Note that the assumption made on

is relevant from the applicative viewpoint. It is typically satisfied if we have two different ground truth data

The above result
shows
that the virtual network without leakage factors cannot be averaged since any averaged operator is nonexpansive.

6
Conclusion

Unrolling optimization algorithms into neural architectures offers significant advantages, including a reduced number of parameters to learn, efficient GPU implementation, and the ability to optimize the quality of the results using relevant loss functions. However, there is currently a lack of theoretical analysis for these structures.

In this work, by leveraging tools from fixed-point theory, we conducted an analysis of the robustness of an unrolled version of the forward-backward algorithm, extending the work in
Bertocchi et al. (2020)
, where sensitivity to observed data was overlooked. Our analysis relies on Lipschitz bounds that are computationally efficient (with quadratic complexity) and tighter than the separable bounds commonly used in the neural network community
Serrurier
et al. (2021)
.

We acknowledge that,
although these bounds are rigorous
, they are not optimal. Future work could focus on deriving tighter bounds to further refine the analysis. Additionally, exploring more general proximal algorithms beyond the one considered in this study would be an interesting direction.
For example, we could consider the presence of a heavy ball/inertial term in the forward-backward algorithm.

Finally, we believe the

\alpha
-averaging properties we established could inspire the design of recurrent networks in the spirit of
Combettes and Pesquet (2020a)
.

A TRAC-478-adjuvanted recombinant N1 neuraminidase influenza virus vaccine induces balanced and broadly protective immune responses
Authors: Irene Hoxie, Kirill Vasilev, Jordan Clark, Hardik Amin, Garazi Peña Alzua, Disha Bhavsar, Eduard Puente-Massaguer, Karthik Siram, Kristopher Short, Rebekah Tee, David Burkhart, Jay T. Evans, Florian Krammer
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Licensed influenza vaccines primarily target the variable hemagglutinin protein and provide inadequate cross-protection against mismatched or drifted viral strains. One promising approach to enhance the breadth of protection is to target the viral neuraminidase by incorporating a stable, recombinant neuraminidase protein with an effective adjuvant. We evaluated an intramuscular recombinant neuraminidase vaccine (N1-MPP) adjuvanted with a lipidated toll-like receptor (TLR)7/8 agonist (INI-4001), a synthetic TLR4 agonist (INI-2002), or both (TRAC-478) delivered as aqueous, liposomal, or squalene oil-in-water emulsions in mice. TLR agonists offset the Th2 bias of squalene emulsions and boosted antibody responses. Combining ligands synergistically amplified CD4⁺ immunity—increasing polyfunctional cytokine-producing T cells in lungs and spleen—while promoting Th1 cytokine production and antibody class switching. Aqueous and emulsion TRAC-478 N1-MPP induced high titers of cross-reactive, functional antibodies that exhibited strong ADCC activity and conferred protection in passive-transfer experiments. TRAC-478 N1-MPP vaccination protected against both H5N1 clade 1 and clade 2.3.4.4b viruses and recent H1N1 isolates, supporting the further development of N1-MPP adjuvanted with TRAC-478 emulsion as a stand-alone vaccine or potentially as a supplement for current vaccination regimens to improve protection against both seasonal influenza virus and strains with pandemic potential.

Introduction

Neuraminidase (NA) is targeted during natural influenza virus infection and is critical during multiple stages of the influenza virus life cycle and pathogenesis, particularly egress from infected cells and transport through the mucosa1–5. Human NA antibodies induced by infection or vaccination are highly functional and cross-protective6–9 and serve as an independent correlate of protection against transmission and severe disease10–15. However, licensed seasonal vaccines are standardized to hemagglutinin (HA) and often elicit variable or weak NA responses8. NA undergoes slower, discordant antigenic drift relative to HA16,17. Therefore, strengthening vaccine-induced NA immunity would provide a safety net for HA mismatch and for emerging strains with pandemic potential (e.g., swine H1N1, avian H5N1)18–21.

The generation of durable and protective immune responses against NA requires optimization of antigen, adjuvant, and delivery system. Recent studies in various animal models demonstrate the progress in the expression and production of stable, tetrameric recombinant NA (rNA), which when administered with an appropriate adjuvant, can induce robust and broadly-protective anti-NA immune responses22–33.

Among the adjuvant systems undergoing different stages of preclinical and clinical evaluation, toll-like receptor (TLR)7 and TLR8 agonists are particularly compelling34. These receptors are expressed within endosomes and primarily recognize single-stranded RNA, triggering a myeloid differentiation primary response 88 (MyD88)-dependent signaling cascade that activates transcription factors nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB) and interferon regulatory factor (IRF)7 with the subsequent expression of various proinflammatory cytokines, such as tumor necrosis factor (TNF), interleukin (IL) 6, IL-12, and type I interferons (IFN)35. The immunomodulatory effects of TLR7/8 agonists, including Imiquimod, Resiquimod (R-848), CL075, 3M-052, Vesikur (VTX-1463), GS-9620, VTX-2337, and INI-4001, have been extensively studied in both preclinical and clinical research36–42.

Incorporation of TLR4 agonists in the TLR7/8-activating adjuvant system can provide additional or synergistic immunostimulatory effects to boost both humoral and cellular immunity and balance T-helper type (Th)1/Th2-polarization. TLR4 initiates signaling from both the cell surface and endosomes through the MyD88 and TIR-domain-containing adapter-inducing interferon-β (TRIF)-dependent pathways, respectively43,44. Co-encapsulation of TLR4 with TLR7/8 in liposomes synergistically increases IL-12p70 and Th1-biased IgG2a in mice45. A combination of TLR4/7/8 agonists helps to overcome neonatal Th2 bias46. TLR4 combined with TLR8 optimize dendritic cell (DC) maturation and IFN-γ for Tc1-driven cellular immunity47. Additionally, recombinant HA adjuvanted with TLR4 and TLR7 agonists induces rapid, durable immunity and protection across homologous, heterologous, and heterosubtypic challenges48. However, adjuvant properties and combinatorial effects can differ depending on inoculation route. McKay et al. showed that a TLR4 agonist, GLA-AF, additively enhanced the adjuvant effect of R848 (TLR7/8 agonist) when injected intradermally but abrogated its adjuvant activity after intranasal inoculation49.

INI-4001 contains oxoadenine conjugated to a lipid tail to raise molecular weight to reduce systemic exposure and reactogenicity, which also allows the compound to be incorporated into various lipid-based carriers50. INI-4001 has a balanced TLR7/TLR8 signaling in humans41, however in mice, signaling is expected to be exclusively through TLR7, as murine TLR8 has impaired functionality51,52. INI-4001 has previously been demonstrated to promote strong humoral and Th1-biased cellular immune responses when used as an adjuvant with recombinant H7 protein50. INI-2002 is a synthetic monophosphoryl lipid A (MPL) mimetic and TLR4 agonist53. Intramuscular (IM) vaccination with ancestral severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) spike protein adjuvanted with emulsion formulations of INI-4001 and INI-2002 conferred cross-protection against lethal challenge with recent variants42. Co-delivery of SARS-CoV-2 receptor binding domain (RBD) with INI-4001 and/or INI-2002 on aluminum salts efficiently skewed the immune response towards Th1 and generated strong neutralizing antibodies54. Both compounds alone or in combination (TRAC-478), have also been evaluated in other contexts in multiple animal models41,50,54–58, and INI-4001 is undergoing a phase I in-human clinical trial (NCT06302426).

Formulation59,60 and immunostimulant design61 significantly affect the immunomodulatory properties of combinatory adjuvant systems, such as TLR4 and TLR7/8. Co-delivery of both molecules to the same cells is optimal for ensuring both MyD88 and TRIF activation resulting in a synergistic effect45,60,62. The lipid structures allow the adjuvants to incorporate into the oil-water interface or liposome bilayer, effectively enhancing the co-delivery of recombinant protein antigens63 with both the TLR agonists, which can enhance both humoral and cell—mediated immune responses63.

The physicochemical properties of adjuvant formulations also significantly affect vaccine immunogenicity64–67. Emulsion formulations can trigger the release of damage-associated molecular patterns and promote cytokine/chemokines and recruitment of immune cells at the injection site, increasing antigen uptake and migration of mature antigen presenting cells (APCs) to draining lymph nodes and interaction with antigen-specific T cells68–72.

In this study, we compared the effects of incorporating a TLR7/8 agonist (INI-4001), a TLR4 agonist (INI-2002), or TRAC-478- a combination of both agonists, with an influenza virus recombinant neuraminidase antigen (N1-MPP, N1 head domain stabilized by a measles virus phosphoprotein tetramerization domain), in either an aqueous, liposomal, or squalene-based oil in water emulsion formulation. Although each formulation has been described individually in previous studies45,54,73, this is the first study to provide a head-to-head evaluation of the impact of these three formulations on the immunogenicity and protective efficacy of the N1-MPP vaccine. We found a squalene-based oil-in-water emulsion of TRAC-478 adjuvant best enhanced humoral and cellular responses to intramuscularly-administered recombinant N1. The N1-MPP-TRAC-478 vaccine induced broadly cross-reactive responses to N1, which were protective against H5N1 and H1N1 influenza virus challenge.

Results

Incorporation of TLR4 and TLR7/8 agonists in emulsion formulation boosts antibody responses and provides superior protection from lethal virus challenge in BALB/c mice

The highest antibody responses were achieved with emulsion formulations of N1-MPP adjuvanted with INI-4001, INI-2002, or TRAC-478. TRAC-478 in emulsion formulation outperformed INI-4001 in aqueous and liposomal formulation and INI-2002 in liposomal formulation. In aqueous formulation, TRAC-478 induced a significantly higher increase in IgG titers relative to INI-2002 in liposomal and INI-4001 in aqueous and liposomal formulations (Fig. 1A and Supplementary Fig. 2A).

Immunization with N1-MPP in the absence of any TLR agonists, including N1-MPP adjuvanted with Addavax, resulted in the development of IgG1-dominant immune response, indicating a Th2-polarization (Fig. 1B). For all three formulations INI-4001 (TLR7/8 agonist) promoted a stronger Th1-associated IgG2a-dominated response than the TLR4 agonist, INI-2002. The highest Th1 polarization was observed in the aqueous formulation of TRAC-478 (Fig. 1B and Supplementary Fig. 2B). Amongst the emulsion groups, the addition of either TLR agonist substantially boosted Th1 responses and offset the strong Th2 polarization observed in the groups lacking a TLR adjuvant component. INI-4001 promoted stronger Th1-polarization, whereas the INI-2002 and TRAC-478 emulsion groups induced more mixed or balanced responses, however we note that only IgG2a, and not IgG2b, was measured. Aqueous and emulsion formulations of TRAC-478 stimulated the most prominent increase in the level of IgG3 antibodies. Similarly, the emulsion formulations of INI-4001 and INI-2002 induced a more prominent increase in the IgG3 level, compared to aqueous and liposomal formulations of these adjuvants (Fig. 1B and Supplementary Fig. 2C). Functional NA-inhibiting antibody titers against A/Michigan/45/2015 virus with the NA homologous to the vaccine antigen, closely mirrored the ELISA results: the highest response was generated in the groups immunized with INI-4001 and INI-2002 in the emulsion formulation and TRAC-478 in aqueous and emulsion formulations (Fig. 1D and Supplementary Fig. 3).

When administered in emulsion formulation in combination with either of the tested adjuvants, N1-MPP induced protection against the homologous challenge with 25 × the 50% lethal dose (LD50) of A/Singapore/GP1908/2015 (H1N1) influenza virus. Mice that received the N1-MPP + TRAC-478 emulsion vaccine displayed the least weight loss following viral challenge (Fig.1E; detailed in Supplementary Figs. 4 and 5A). TLR-mediated differences in protection were most clearly observed in the aqueous formulation groups. While the non-adjuvanted N1-MPP in aqueous formulation did not confer robust protection, the aqueous INI-2002 and TRAC-478 formulations were protective. The liposomal vaccine formulations of 1 μg N1-MPP were not protective in combination with INI-2002 and INI-4001 adjuvants. All mice vaccinated with liposomal TRAC-478 survived but demonstrated the highest weight loss among the groups vaccinated with N1-MPP + TRAC-478.

Viral titers were evaluated in the mouse lungs on days 3 and 6 after the challenge with 0.1 × LD50 of A/Singapore/GP1908/2015 (H1N1) influenza virus. The N1-MPP + TRAC-478-vaccinated group demonstrated the most significant reduction in viral loads on day 3, as well as the INI-4001 group that received the vaccine in emulsion formulation (Fig. 1F, G and Supplementary Fig. 5B, C). No detectable virus replication in the lung was observed in the TRAC-478 emulsion group 6 days post-infection, and 2 out of 3 mice had cleared the virus in the TRAC-478 aqueous group. Since the liposomal formulations of the studied vaccine demonstrated the lowest protective efficiency and decreased ability to induce the NA-targeting antibody response, we next examined TLR-mediated differences and formulation effects only in the aqueous and emulsion groups.

INI-4001 and TRAC-478 adjuvants promote Th1-polarization of antigen-specific cytokine responses

Antigen-specific cytokine production was assessed in BALB/c mice immunized with 1 μg of N1-MPP adjuvanted with INI-2002, INI-4001, or TRAC-478 in emulsion or aqueous formulation. On day 10 after the second vaccination, splenocytes were stimulated for 24 h with a peptide library covering the entire sequence of the N1 protein, and the concentrations of Th1/Th2/Th17-associated cytokines were determined in the supernatants using a bead-based multiplex assay. The overall level of N1-specific T-cell cytokines was higher in the emulsion groups compared with the aqueous groups. Immunization in the absence of TLR agonist resulted in the release of more Th2-associated cytokines (IL-5, IL-4, IL-10, IL-13) from splenocytes upon peptide stimulation. The addition of the TLR7/8 agonist suppressed the Th2 response. Both INI-4001 and TRAC-478 promoted higher levels of N1-specific Th1-associated cytokines (IFNγ, IL-2, TNFα) relative to INI-2002. The INI-2002 emulsion–adjuvanted vaccine induced higher IL-5 and lower IFN-γ and TNF-α, compared with the INI-4001 emulsion group (Fig. 1H and Supplementary Fig. 6). Principal component analysis of the cytokine data (Fig. 1I) revealed group-level differences, most pronounced for the emulsion formulations. Mice receiving N1-MPP with INI-4001 or TRAC-478 clustered separately from animals given non-adjuvanted N1-MPP or the INI-2002-adjuvanted vaccine. Conversely, groups vaccinated with aqueous formulations containing the same adjuvants displayed overlapping clusters, distinct from the nonadjuvanted aqueous vaccine group.

While the TRAC-478 emulsion vaccine reliably induced potent and balanced Th1/Th2 responses, IgG subclass and cytokine comparisons indicate Th1-polarized response in the aqueous TRAC-478 and INI-4001 emulsion groups. Because squalene emulsions alone often skew toward Th2 and may not yield a balanced response without additional adjuvants74–76, we compared TLR–emulsion vaccines for their ability to enhance antigen-specific T-cell responses. Initial findings in BALB/c mice supported combining TLR agonists; we therefore assessed whether co-administration of the two TLR ligands yielded additive or synergistic effects on T-cellular and humoral immunity in C57BL/6 mice.

INI-2002, INI-4001, and TRAC-478 adjuvants promote Th-1 polarization of the humoral immune response and the production of cross-reactive anti-NA antibodies in C57BL/6 mice

As previous studies showed the C57BL/6 strain generates a more robust T-cellular immune response to N1-MPP, compared to BALB/c mice33, which are known to favor Th-2 responses77,78, we chose this model to further evaluate cellular immune responses. Additionally, we compared two different ratios (10 μg:1 μg (10:1) and 10 μg:5 μg (10:5)) of TLR7/8 and TLR4 agonists, respectively.

All vaccines significantly enhanced antibody titers relative to the unadjuvanted N1-MPP vaccine and the negative control mice (Fig. 2). Both TRAC-478-emulsion vaccines promoted higher titers than INI-2002 or INI-4001 alone, however, only the 10:5 group was significantly higher (Fig. 2A). Both TRAC-478 groups displayed higher serum IgG3 titers, with the 10:5 being the highest, however, the differences were not statistically significant between the adjuvanted vaccines (Fig. 2B). The addition of TLR agonist was sufficient to promote class-switching to IgG2, whereas the unadjuvanted vaccine induced a heavily IgG1-dominated response (Fig. 2C). TRAC-478 10:1 showed the highest IgG2c titers and IgG2c/IgG1 ratio, suggesting an enhanced Th1-polarization in this group. To measure enzymatic NA-inhibiting antibodies and assess their cross-reactivity, enzyme-linked lectin assays (ELLA) were performed against different influenza virus strains within the N1 subtype (Fig. 2D–J). Serum from all adjuvanted N1-MPP-immunized mice contained high NAI antibody titers against a virus with a homologous NA and significantly increased antigen-specific NAI titers relative to the PBS-vaccinated mice (Fig. 2D). All the emulsion-TLR adjuvants promoted cross-reactive NAI antibodies against all heterologous strains tested (Fig. 2E–J), including a recent human H1N1 strain, A/New York City/PV63249/2022 (Fig. 2E), pandemic A/Netherlands/602/2009 H1N1 (Fig. 2F), a recent avian H5N1 strain, A/bald eagle/FL/W22-134-OP/2022 (Fig. 2G) and the A/Vietnam/1203/2004 H5N1 strain (Fig. 2H), as well as classical seasonal H1N1 strain, A/New Caledonia/20/1999 (Fig. 2I). The lowest NAI activity was observed against A/Puerto Rico/8/1934 H1N1 (Fig. 2J) with only two mice from both TRAC-478 groups generating a response above the limit of detection (LOD). Both TRAC-478 emulsion groups displayed consistently high NAI titers, with the TRAC-478 10:5 group showing slightly higher titers without a statistically significant difference from TRAC-478 10:1.

INI-2002, INI-4001 and TRAC-478 adjuvants stimulate CD4 + T-cellular cytokine response in C57BL/6 mice

CD8+ T-cells did not exhibit a significant increase in cytokine production upon restimulation (Supplementary Fig. 7). On average, the most pronounced response was shown in the group that received N1-MPP adjuvanted with TRAC-478-10:5 adjuvant, though the TRAC-478 10:1 group had an individual mouse with the highest T-cell immune response of all the experimental animals. All tested formulations significantly reduced viral replication in BALFs. Sterilizing immunity was observed in the TRAC-478 10:5 group. In the INI-2002, INI-4001, and TRAC-478 10:1 group, some mice showed marginal viral replication on 5 DPI, while the majority had cleared the virus (Fig. 4C).

As expected, higher viral titers were associated with higher inflammatory cytokine levels. Adjuvanted N1-MPP vaccine formulations decreased the levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines and chemokines, including C-C motif chemokine ligand 2 (CCL2), C-X-C motif chemokine ligand 1 (CXCL1), IFNα, and TNFα in the BALFs of the C57BL/6 mice 5 DPI, compared to the unadjuvanted and PBS-vaccinated groups (Fig. 4A). One mouse vaccinated with TRAC-478 10:1 produced IFNγ and Th17 cytokines (IL-17 and IL-22) in response to viral replication but notably suppressed the production of Th2 cytokines and IFNα, suggesting the TRAC-478 adjuvant would provide superior protection against severe disease. In contrast, the unadjuvanted N1-MPP group showed enhanced concentrations of Th2-associated cytokines IL-4, IL-5, and IL-13 in response to the challenge.

Immunization prevented the accumulation of immunopathology-associated innate immune populations (exudative macrophages, inflammatory monocytes, neutrophils, eosinophils) and increased the level of alveolar macrophages in the lungs (Fig. 4B). Eosinophil accumulation, indicative of a Th2-immune response, was shown in the lungs of mice immunized with non-adjuvanted N1-MPP, matching the Th2-associated cytokine profile in this group. On the other hand, the TRAC-478 groups had the lowest eosinophil percentages (Fig. 4B).

TRAC-478 10:5 adjuvant induces the highest CD4+ T-cellular cytokine response in C57BL/6 mice 5 days after the challenge with A/Singapore/GP1908/2015 H1N1 influenza virus

On day 5 post-challenge with 0.5 × LD50 of the A/Singapore/GP1908/2015 H1N1 influenza virus, all experimental animals developed an antigen-specific CD4+ T-cell-mediated immune response characterized by polyfunctional T cells (IFNγ+IL-2+TNFα+, IFNγ+IL-2−TNFα+). The strongest lung response occurred in the TRAC-478 10:5 adjuvanted group. The INI-4001 and TRAC-478 10:5 adjuvanted groups exhibited the highest systemic T-cell responses in the spleen (Fig. 5). Both TRAC-478 formulations induced higher levels of polyfunctional CD4+ T cells, predominantly producing IFNγ and TNFα with or without IL-2. INI-4001 notably increased the proportion of IL-2 single-producing cells in the spleen. Interestingly, although the inclusion of TLR agonists significantly enhanced IgG2c production (Fig. 2C), mice vaccinated with INI-2002 emulsion did not show a corresponding increase in IFNγ production. The CD8+ T-cells did not exhibit a significant increase in measured cytokine production in response to N1 peptide stimulation (Supplementary Fig. 8).

Given that co-administration of TLR4 and TLR7/8 agonists produced the most robust immune responses and protection, we conducted a follow-up study in BALB/c mice focusing on immunogenicity and cross-protective efficacy of TRAC-478 in aqueous, liposomal, and emulsion formulations; for the emulsion, we additionally examined two TLR4:TLR7/8 ratios (10:1 and 10:5). Quadrivalent inactivated influenza vaccine (QIV) was included as a standard-of-care control.

To assess the adjuvants’ ability to enhance IgG subclass diversity and functionality, we also compared relative IgG subclass titers. TRAC-478 was able to significantly increase IgG3 (Fig. 6D) and IgG2a (Fig. 6E) antibody titers, whereas unadjuvanted N1-MPP induced almost exclusively IgG1, matching the previous results (Figs. 1B and 2C). While IgG1 titers also remained high in most TRAC-478-vaccinated BALB/c mice, the aqueous TRAC-478 formulation in particular displayed a relative decrease in IgG1 and a significant increase in IgG2a, indicating a partial shift towards a Th1 phenotype. The emulsion TRAC-478 vaccines promoted a more balanced Th1/Th2 immune profile, with high IgG2a and IgG1 titers, while the liposomal vaccine induced both the lowest IgG2a titers and IgG2a/IgG1 ratios of the TRAC-478 vaccines.

While IgG1 NA-antibodies in mice are assumed to predominantly protect via NA enzymatic inhibition, IgG2a/c, IgG2b, and IgG3 antibodies can also protect against virus infection via FcγR-mediated activation of effector cells, so we assessed this using an antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity (ADCC) reporter assay (Fig. 6F). We observed the highest ADCC activity against A/Singapore/GP1908/2015 H1N1 influenza virus-infected cells in the groups that received the TRAC478-adjuvanted vaccine in emulsion and aqueous formulations. The area under the curve (AUC) of the ADCC activity positively correlated with IgG2a antibody titers (Spearman’s R = 0.94, p < 0.001).

To measure enzymatic NA-inhibiting antibodies and assess their cross-reactivity, NAIs were performed against six different IAV strains within the N1 subtype (Table 1). Serum from N1-MPP + TRAC-478-immunized mice contained high NAI antibody titers against the antigenically homologous virus, A/Michigan/45/2015. All N1-MPP + TRAC-478 vaccines significantly increased antigen-specific NAI titers relative to the control as well as the QIV group (Fig. 6G). Of the heterologous viruses tested, NAI titers were the highest against A/New York City/PV63249/2022 (H1N1). Notably, sera from the TRAC-478 vaccines displayed high NAI activity against A/New Caledonia/20/1999 (Fig. 6H), which is antigenically distant (Table 1). The emulsion and aqueous TRAC-478 groups also displayed high NAI activity against a more recent H5N1 strain, A/bald eagle/FL/W22-134-OP/2022, as well as historical clade 1 A/Vietnam/1203/2004 H5N1 (Fig. 6J). Most mouse sera in liposomal group displayed low NAI activity against A/Puerto Rico/8/1934 H1N1 (Fig. 6I). To assess the role of NA-specific antibodies in serum as a contributing mechanism of protection, we performed a passive transfer challenge experiment. Naïve BALB/c mice were intraperitoneally injected with the sera from vaccinated mice and were challenged with 5 × LD50 homologous virus (A/Singapore/GP1908/2015 H1N1) 2 h later (Fig. 7C, D). Groups that received sera from mice vaccinated with N1-MPP adjuvanted with either TRAC-478 (10:1 or 10:5) emulsion, or the TRAC-478 aqueous formulation, were completely protected against lethal viral challenge, though the aqueous TRAC-478 serum-treated mice exhibited higher weight loss (Fig. 7C) than the emulsion groups. Mice that received serum from liposomal TRAC-478-vaccinated animals lost significantly more weight, with two mice succumbing to infection by 7 DPI. Passive transfer of serum from either QIV-vaccinated or BSA-vaccinated mice failed to protect the animals from the challenge; all mice succumbed to infection by day 9 (Fig. 7D).

N1-MPP adjuvanted with TRAC-478 in aqueous and emulsion formulation induces protection against heterologous H5N1 and novel H1N1 influenza virus

Both emulsion and aqueous formulations of the N1-MPP + TRAC-478 vaccine provided cross-protection against recent avian influenza virus strains A/bald eagle/Florida/W22-134-OP/2022 H5N1 (clade 2.3.4.4b) and the older A/Vietnam/1203/2004 H5N1 (A/Puerto Rico/8/1934 reassortants), as well as against a 2022 pdm09-like H1N1 strain A/New York City/PV63249/2022 (Fig. 7F–K). Although mice from all vaccinated groups experienced substantial weight loss, complete protection from mortality was observed in both TRAC-478 emulsion groups. In the aqueous TRAC-478 group, only one mouse succumbed after the A/bald eagle/Florida/W22-134-OP/2022 H5N1 challenge (Fig. 7F, G). In contrast, high mortality rates were recorded in liposomal groups challenged with either of the H5N1 viruses (Fig. 7G, I). Notably, all vaccinated groups, including liposomal TRAC-478, demonstrated a similar level of protection against the A/New York City/PV63249/2022 H1N1 (recent clinical isolate) infection. A moderate weight loss and the absence of mortality was shown in the vaccinated groups, whereas control mice succumbed to infection (Fig. 7J, K). Additionally, all QIV-vaccinated mice succumbed to infection in the 2022 H5N1 challenge experiment, unlike the mice vaccinated with adjuvanted N1-MPP (Fig. 7F, G).

Discussion

A growing body of experimental evidence indicates that rNA-based constructs represent a promising strategy for development of broadly protective influenza virus vaccines23,27,31,32,79–81. Optimizing delivery strategies and selecting appropriate adjuvants substantially enhances the immunogenicity and protective efficacy of recombinant protein-based vaccines, enabling fine-tuning of immune mechanisms specifically tailored for effective antiviral protection. In this study, we compared N1-MPP formulated with synthetic TLR7/8 and/or TLR4 agonists in aqueous, liposomal, and squalene oil-in-water emulsions. Co-formulating TLR7/8 + TLR4 in an emulsion markedly broadened and improved NA-specific immunity, driving Th1-biased cellular responses, broad IgG subclass switching, and robust cross-reactive antibody and T-cell responses protective against diverse N1 strains.

In a head-to-head comparative study of N1-MPP adjuvanted with INI-4001 (TLR7/8 agonist), INI-2002 (TLR4 agonist), or TRAC-478 (a combination of TLR7/8 and TLR4 agonists) formulated in aqueous, liposomal, or emulsion systems, we demonstrated that the emulsion formulation alone substantially enhanced the overall anti-NA antibody response, even without additional TLR agonists. However, inclusion of TLR4 and TLR7/8 agonists, particularly in combination, enhanced the breadth of the cross-reactive NA-inhibiting antibody response, as well as IgG subclass diversity and Fc polyfunctionality, indicated by the significant increase in IFNγ production, subsequent IgG3 and IgG2 class switching, and high ADCC activity.

While Th2-associated IgG1 antibodies mainly protect through neutralization of extracellular virus (enzyme inhibition in the case of NA), Fc-functional antibodies can target more conserved alternative epitopes of NA82,83. NAI does not capture the full cross-protective potential of anti-NA IgG2 and IgG3, which can suppress viral replication by activating FcγR-dependent effector functions such as ADCC, antibody-dependent cellular phagocytosis (ADCP), or complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC)83–86 without a direct NAI activity. While NA-inhibiting antibodies tend to display broader cross-reactivity compared to the neutralizing antibodies targeting the variable HA head, production of IFNγ/Th1-associated IgG subclasses (IgG2a/c, IgG2b, and IgG3 in mice) is critical for cross-protection87,88. Shibuya et al. found that influenza vaccines that induced cross-reactive IgG1-dominated responses were not cross-protective in vivo, while vaccines that favored cross-reactive IgG2 were cross-protective. Non-neutralizing IgG1 was further shown to interfere with the cross-protective effects of IgG2 by inhibiting binding of non-neutralizing IgG2 to virus89.

A consistent, significant increase in IgG3 with TRAC-478–adjuvanted N1-MPP (emulsion or aqueous), even without higher total antigen-specific IgG, indicates enhanced antibody functionality and broader protection. Despite IgG3 being the least abundant IgG subclass in mouse or human sera, vaccine-induced IgG3 mediates enhanced protection against many viral pathogens, while its deficiency is associated with recurrent respiratory infections90,91. IgG3 antibodies exhibit more potent Fc-mediated cellular phagocytosis and complement activation. The long, flexible hinge region of IgG3 makes it superior to the other subclasses at inducing intracellular antiviral immunity, via TRIM-21-dependent effector functions and complement C1/C4-dependent neutralization92. Many broadly neutralizing IgG3 antibodies recognize epitopes poorly accessible to other subclasses. Thus, IgG3 induction contributes to antibody reactivity breadth, as its Fc/hinge architecture supports superior bivalent binding and, consequently, greater neutralization potency than other IgG subclasses, which was shown in the neutralization experiments with influenza viruses and SARS-CoV-293,94.

While both INI-2002 and INI-4001 individually showed robust performance within emulsion formulations, the aqueous formulations provided variable results. Specifically, INI-4001 alone performed sub-optimally in aqueous form, whereas INI-2002 did not exhibit a comparable loss in immunogenicity when delivered in the aqueous formulation. This variation may be attributed to a decrease in the association between N1-MPP and INI-4001 or differences in charge or particle size, contributing to insufficient cellular uptake or activation by INI-4001 without an appropriate formulation vehicle. When delivered as an emulsion, the lipid modification of INI-4001 should facilitate incorporation of the TLR ligand into the emulsion interface. Electrostatic attraction between N1-MPP and the emulsion-INI-4001 adjuvant system may then enhance N1-MPP co-delivery with INI-4001 to promote innate immune activation. This substantial variation in performance observed in different formulation-TLR ligand combinations underscores the critical role of formulation in maximizing the efficacy of vaccine adjuvants, especially those engaging intracellular receptors such as TLR7/8 agonists. Previous studies support this explanation: Russo et al. and Evans et al. showed that endosomal localization is crucial for TLR7/8 ligands; without a vehicle that promotes internalization, signaling—and thus immunogenicity—collapses95,96. Gadd et al. demonstrated that the TLR7 agonist UC-1V150 was virtually inactive in simple aqueous buffer but became more potent once assembled into nanoparticles or liposomes97. Imidazopyrimidine TLR7/8 adjuvants have also been shown to synergize with oil-in-water emulsions to enhance SARS-CoV-2 vaccine immunogenicity98. Notably, INI‑4001 in aqueous buffer still elicited significantly higher antibody responses than unadjuvanted N1‑MPP, indicating that the ligand can reach endosomal receptors without a particulate vehicle; however, its potency was enhanced in emulsions, consistent with improved cellular uptake and antigen co‑localization. Collectively, these observations emphasize that formulation–ligand matching is critical for maximizing adjuvant efficacy, but the precise mechanisms underlying performance differences among formulations are beyond the scope of this study and require further investigation.

Both aqueous and emulsion formulations outperformed the liposomal formulation of the studied adjuvants in terms of immunogenicity and protective efficacy for the TRAC-478 adjuvant. The liposomal formulation induced lower antibody responses and class-switching and failed to confer full cross-protection against H5N1 influenza virus strains. The observed effect may be associated with the level of availability of TLR7/8 agonists for the interaction with the corresponding receptor within the endosome. For example, Van Hoeven et al. demonstrated that R848 (TLR7/8 agonist) was far more potent in a squalene nano-emulsion than in matched liposomes because the liposomal form stayed embedded and could not reach endosomal TLR7/899. The substantially smaller particle size of the liposomal vaccines along with their lower particle homogeneity compared with the emulsion vaccines (Table 2) may help explain the weaker Th1 responses and higher variation in performance, observed in the liposomal vaccines100,101. Neutrally charged particles have been shown to have lower APC uptake compared to charged, particularly cationic, particles102, which may have also contributed to the neutral liposomes’ lower efficacy. Incorporating TLR4 and TLR7/8 agonists into the N1-MPP emulsion shifted immunity away from Th2. INI-4001 (TLR7/8) drove a stronger Th1 response than INI-2002 (TLR4), consistent with prior reports103. The superior Th1-polarizing capacity of INI-4001 may be explained by the localization of the corresponding receptors and differences in the signaling along with potential differences in uptake from cell populations. INI-4001 activates the MyD88-IRF7 and NF-κB-dependent pathways, inducing strong type I IFN and IL-12p70 responses—classic drivers of Th1 differentiation. In contrast, INI-2002 activates surface-confined TLR4, favoring TNF and IL-6 production and only modest TRIF-dependent type I IFN expression, leading to weaker Th1 polarization41,104,105. Additionally, plasmacytoid dendritic cells (pDCs), the primary producers of IFNα/β, express very high levels of TLR7 and are likely to be efficiently activated by INI-4001, but not by TLR4-agonist INI-2002106. IFNα produced by pDCs conditions conventional DCs to secrete IL-12 and upregulate T-bet in naïve CD4 T cells, reinforcing Th1 lineage choice107,108. TLR7/8 agonists can also directly stimulate B cells: TLR7 ligation promotes T-bet and class switching to IgG2a/c independent of T-cell help, whereas lower TLR4 expression and signaling on B cells limit this effect109, aligning with the higher IgG2a/c in INI-4001 versus INI-2002 groups.

The co-administration of INI-4001 and INI-2002 synergistically elevated the magnitude, breadth, and quality of humoral and cellular immunity over single-adjuvant formulations. While emulsion-formulated INI-4001 was a potent Th1-polarizing adjuvant, co-administration with INI-2002 synergistically boosted the generation of polyfunctional antigen-specific memory CD4 T cells secreting IFNγ, IL-2, and TNFα—an important correlate of protection against influenza virus infection110–112. The simultaneous engagement of endosomal TLR7/8 and surface TLR4 has been previously shown to provide a broader pattern-recognition signal inside the same dendritic cells, yielding an additional burst of type I IFN and IL-12 that imprints a durable, polyfunctional Th1 response109. Short et al. clearly demonstrated that co-encapsulation of a TLR4 agonist (CRX-601) and a lipidated TLR7/8 agonist (UM-3004) in the same liposome bilayer induces higher IgG2a responses, compared to either agonist alone and to the same two agonists delivered in separate, admixed liposomes45. Dual-agonist platforms built on MPL (TLR4) and small-molecule TLR7 ligands have likewise generated larger pools of antigen-specific CD4⁺ T cells and broader cross-clade protection after influenza virus challenge48,113. In another study, soluble synthetic 1Z105 (TLR4) + 1V270 (TLR7) admixed with recombinant HA induced a CD4⁺ T-cell response that contributed to the protection against heterologous challenge in T-cell depletion experiments62. Our data extend these findings to an NA-focused vaccine: the 10:5 ratio of INI-4001 to INI-2002 (TRAC-478) delivered in a squalene oil-in-water emulsion elicited the highest frequencies of lung-resident and splenic polyfunctional CD4⁺ T cells after both vaccination and heterologous H1N1 challenge, confirming that balanced, complementary TLR4 + TLR7/8 signaling is a powerful strategy for activating the T-cellular response that correlates with robust, cross-protective antiviral immunity. Notably, only NA-specific T cell responses were evaluated, however in humans, most T-cell responses associated with protection are directed against conserved internal proteins. While the contribution of NA-specific T-cell responses to protection is poorly understood, the immunity afforded by the N1-MPP-TRAC-478 vaccine could promote back-boosting to other proteins by permitting low levels of infection whilst still enhancing breadth of protection against disease114.

Previous studies have also reported an expanded diversity of IgG variable region sequences following vaccination with TLR agonist-adjuvanted antigens, which may help explain the enhanced protection observed. Sato-Kaneko et al. showed that a liposomal formulation of a TLR4 ligand (2B182C) with TLR7 ligand 1V270 increased Ig heavy-chain clonotype diversity and led to elevated IgG1 and IgG2a responses against HA and NA, protecting mice from lethal H1N1 challenge113. Similarly, Goff et al. found that co-delivery of TLR4 agonist 1Z105 with TLR7 agonist 1V270 induced balanced Th1/Th2 immunity and conferred broad protection when administered with recombinant or split influenza vaccines48. Additionally, Kasturi et al. showed that poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid)) (PLGA) nanoparticles bearing MPL (TLR4) and R837 (TLR7) induced durable germinal centers and robust antibody responses that protected both mice and rhesus macaques from diverse influenza virus strains109. Combined TLR4 and TLR7/8 adjuvants have also proven useful for SARS-CoV-2: addition of these ligands to an alum-adjuvanted RBD vaccine shifted responses toward a Th1 profile and greatly increased neutralizing antibody production54. Together, these data support the idea that the observed synergy between INI-4001 and INI-2002 when administered in combination (TRAC-478) generates a qualitatively richer cytokine and co-stimulatory environment. This, in turn, promotes sustained germinal center activity, broader antibody repertoires, and highly polyfunctional Th1 memory CD4+ T cells. Importantly, a previous study evaluating INI-4001 and INI-2002 in an emulsion formulation did not report a synergistic interaction between the two agonists73. Considered alongside our data, this suggests that the effect may depend on the experimental context, including the adjuvant concentrations, antigen, antigen dose, and immunization schedule. Incorporating higher concentrations of INI-4001 in the emulsion reduced particle size and lowered the polydispersity index (PDI) compared to the emulsion alone, whereas INI-2002 incorporation did not significantly impact droplet diameter or PDI73. Conversely, the zeta potential of the emulsion droplets is decreased by the addition of either agonist, but more significantly by INI-2002, due to its formal charge of −2, whereas INI-4001 has a formal charge −1. Higher absolute values of zeta potential enhance colloidal stability and affect APC uptake, however this value would vary depending on the specific combination of antigen and adjuvant. In our study, the clearest synergistic enhancement was observed at the 10:5 INI-4001 to INI-2002 ratio, which has not been examined previously, whereas the responses at 10:1 more closely resembled the published findings from similar experiments.

In vivo challenge studies confirmed the superior cross-protective capacity of the N1-MPP + TRAC-478 vaccine. It reduced morbidity and conferred complete protection from mortality in mice challenged with H5N1 strains representing two highly divergent NA phenotypes from 2004 and 2022. A/Vietnam/1203/2004 H5N1 NA has a 20 amino-acid deletion (positions 49-68) in its stalk associated with avian host adaptation and increased pathogenicity115–117. The A/bald eagle/Florida/W22-134-OP/2022 H5N1 challenge strain belongs to a widespread, highly pathogenic clade 2.3.4.4b, possessing a reverted “long-stalk” NA, which is linked to improved respiratory transmission in mammals118.

Despite their ability to cause severe disease in humans, avian influenza viruses have historically exhibited poor human-to-human transmission119. However, a dramatic rise in zoonotic H5N1 cases in humans and growing evidence of mammal-to-mammal H5N1 transmission since 2021 (reviewed in ref. 120) underscores the urgency for development of broadly-protective influenza virus vaccines. The expression of both α2-3- and α2-6-linked sialic acid receptors in many animal hosts facilitates infection by viruses with either human- or avian-type specificity121, reducing selective pressure on HA/NA balance122, thereby increasing opportunities for viral reassortment events and adaptation to humans123–125. N1 immunity from H1N1 infection or vaccination has been shown to provide cross-protection against H5N1 and drifted H1N1 strains13,19,79,126–131, and cross-reactive, protective epitopes within the N1 subtype have been well characterized79,132–136. Unfortunately, N1 immunity is generally less durable and robust compared with N2 immunity 137, potentially due to greater surface instability and lower antigenicity of N129,138. Therefore, stable N1 antigen delivered with an optimal adjuvant system may significantly enhance the durability of protective N1 immunity.

Despite a relatively high NA sequence variability, the magnitude of genetic drift does not necessarily correlate with antigenic drift16,139. Major antigenic changes are disproportionately driven by a limited set of solvent‑exposed residues on the NA head or by gaining or losing glycosylation sites, such as the one partially responsible for recent H1N1 zoonotic transmission from swine140,141. The NAI assays used in this study to analyze the cross-reactivity of the sera only measure the inhibition of fetuin cleavage by NA, meaning that certain mutations may disproportionately affect the assay outcome, limiting its ability to reflect cross-binding and protection. For example, despite being phylogenetically close, the A/Michigan/45/2015 antigen possesses several mutations compared to the older A/Netherlands/602/2009, known to significantly impact NAI activity and antigenicity, such as 321 V/386 K (loss of glycosylation site), 432E, and 270 K/314 M, which may have contributed to the ~1–2 log reduction in serum NAI activity against A/Netherlands/602/2009, compared to the matched virus.

We further demonstrated that passive transfer of sera from N1-MPP + TRAC-478-vaccinated mice conferred protection in naïve mice against homologous viral challenge. This indicates that vaccine-induced humoral responses alone are sufficient for homologous protection, though cellular immunity likely contributes to protection against more divergent strains.

Importantly, the incorporation of both INI-2002 and INI-4001 into the oil-in-water emulsion consistently enhanced both humoral and cellular immune responses and did not reduce functional antibody responses compared to the emulsion alone. The N1-MPP-TRAC-478 emulsion vaccine was stable and reliable at both concentrations (10:1 and 10:5) tested.

The recombinant N1-MPP-TRAC-478 emulsion vaccine has several advantages over other vaccine platforms, particularly with concern to expanding breadth of protection. The TRAC-478-emulsion adjuvant provides excellent adaptive immune stimulation, but with low reactogenicity and higher thermostability, so it can be stored at easily maintainable temperatures for extended periods of time73. The recombinant NA antigen can also be rapidly produced from a genetic sequence with no live pathogen required and no risk of mutations from cell or egg passaging. The incorporation of effective, stable adjuvants induced desirable Th1 responses, while also providing a significant dose-sparing effect, which is critical for maximizing the safety profile and cost-effectiveness of the vaccine.

As this study was performed in inbred naïve female mice, future studies with pre-immune animals or alternative animal models would be useful to address factors such as pre-existing immunity and antigenic imprinting, which are of particular relevance to influenza viruses and may affect vaccine translation to humans142,143. Further testing in other models is particularly important given species differences in TLR expression.

Together, our findings demonstrate that combining the rNA-based vaccine with synthetic TLR4 and TLR7/8 agonists markedly improves both the breadth and quality of the immune response, offering a promising path toward cross-protective influenza vaccination. The emulsion-based TRAC-478 adjuvant, representing a combination of INI-2002 and INI-4001, was particularly effective in inducing broad-spectrum antibody immunity and durable, polyfunctional Th1-type CD4⁺ T-cell responses. This dual-adjuvant strategy not only enhanced homologous protection but also conferred cross-protection against highly virulent and antigenically distinct H5N1 strains, a critical feature for pandemic preparedness. The superior performance of emulsion over liposomal or aqueous formulations further emphasizes the importance of adjuvant delivery systems in maximizing innate immune activation and downstream adaptive responses. Overall, these results support continued development of recombinant NA-based vaccines incorporating synergistic TLR agonist combinations to achieve robust, long-lasting, and broadly protective influenza immunity.

Methods

Cells and viruses

Madin-Darby canine kidney (MDCK) cells (ATCC, #CCL-34) were maintained in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM; ThermoFisher, #11995065) containing 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; Thermo Scientific, #10082147) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin (100 U/ml penicillin, 100 μg/ml streptomycin; Gibco). BTN-TN-5B1-4 (Trichoplusia ni, High Five) cells were maintained in Express Five media (Gibco, #10486025) containing 1% L-glutamine (Gibco, #25030081) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin mix. Sf9 (Spodoptera frugiperda) cells were maintained in Trichoplusia ni medium – formulation Hink (TNM-FH; Gemini Bioproducts, #50-753-3107) containing 10% FBS and 1% penicillin/streptomycin mix (Thermo Scientific, #15140122).

Viruses were grown in 8–10-day-old specific pathogen-free (SPF) embryonated chicken eggs (Charles River Laboratories) at 37 °C for 48 h. For the mouse challenges and serology, A/Singapore/GP1908/2015 (H1N1) (NIBSC, IVR-180, #16/292), A/Michigan/45/2015 (H1N1)pdm09, A/New York City/PV63249/2022 (H1N1), A/Puerto Rico/8/1934 (PR8, H1N1), A/New Caledonia/20/1999 (H1N1), A/Netherlands/602/2009 (H1N1), A/Vietnam/1203/2004 (H5N1) and A/bald eagle/FL/W22-134-OP/2022 (H5N1) were used. Both H5N1 strains were 6:2 PR8 reassortant viruses with their HA polybasic cleavage site removed. The A/Michigan/45/2015 H7N1 virus used in the NA inhibition assay (NAI) is a 6:2 PR8 H1N1 reassortant with an H7 HA from A/mallard/Alberta/24/2001 (without a polybasic cleavage site). Table 1 contains a comparative analysis of the NA amino acid sequence similarity across the influenza virus strains used in the study.

Vaccine antigens

Recombinant N1 constructs used in this study were expressed in insect cells via the baculovirus expression system, as previously described30,144. The recombinant rNA-MPP constructs were engineered using a modified pFastBac vector containing an N-terminal signal peptide, a hexahistidine purification tag (His-tag), a measles virus phosphoprotein (MPP) tetramerization domain, a thrombin cleavage site, and the globular head domain of N1 derived from the A/Michigan/45/2015 (H1N1) influenza virus strain. These recombinant vectors were transformed into DH10Bac bacteria (Thermo Scientific, #10361012). Selected transformed colonies were cultured, and bacmid DNA was extracted using the PureLink Plasmid Filter Midiprep Kit (Thermo Scientific, #K210004). Subsequently, bacmid DNA was transfected into Sf9 insect cells with Cellfectin II reagent (Thermo Scientific, #10362100) to generate recombinant baculovirus, and recombinant protein expression was confirmed by Western blot analysis. The recombinant baculovirus was then amplified in Sf9 cells and used for infection of High Five cells to produce the target proteins. Protein harvesting from culture supernatants was followed by purification using Ni²⁺–nitrilotriacetic acid resin (Qiagen, #30210) chromatography as described elsewhere145. Protein concentration was measured using Quick Start™ Bradford 1 × Dye Reagent (Bio-Rad, #5000205), and the purified proteins were stored at −80 °C in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). Protein integrity and tetramerization were assessed by sodium dodecyl-sulfate polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) using 4–20% Mini-PROTEAN TGX gels (Bio-Rad, #4561093) after crosslinking with bis-[sulfosuccinimidyl]suberate (BS3, Fisher Scientific). The NA-Star influenza virus neuraminidase inhibitor resistance detection kit (ThermoFisher) was used to assess enzymatic activity. Luminescence was measured using a Synergy H1 hybrid multimode microplate reader (BioTek). An enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) against a broadly reactive NA-specific monoclonal antibody, 1G01132, was also used to validate protein integrity (Supplementary Fig. 1). For serum ELISAs, N1 proteins were produced following a similar methodology, substituting the MPP tetramerization domain with a vasodilator-stimulated phosphoprotein (VASP) domain to minimize cross-reactivity from antibodies targeting the tetramerization domain.

The 2018/2019 FluLaval (GSK), quadrivalent inactivated influenza vaccine (QIV), was included as a standard of care control, and included four virus strains; an A/Michigan/45/2015 (H1N1)pdm09-like virus, an A/Singapore/INFIMH-16–0019/2016 (H3N2)-like virus, a B/Colorado/06/2017-like virus, and a B/Phuket/3073/2013-like virus.

Adjuvant synthesis and formulation

Synthetic TLR adjuvants INI-2002 and INI-4001 were prepared as previously described41,53. Aqueous formulations of INI-4001 and INI-2002 were prepared as previously described55. Briefly, the aqueous formulation of INI-4001 was prepared using a high-shear homogenization method. INI-4001 was accurately weighed into a glass beaker, followed by the addition of a suitable volume of aqueous buffer composed of 50 mM tris-hydroxymethylaminomethane (Tris) and 0.1% Tween 80 pH 7.4. The resulting mixture was then subjected to homogenization using a Silverson L5MA high-shear device operating at 10,000 rpm for a duration of 20 min until the particle size was below 200 nm. To prepare the aqueous formulation of INI-2002, the compound was dissolved in 2% glycerol with the aid of a bath sonicator (FB11201, Fisherbrand, Thermo Fisher Scientific) operated for 3 h at a temperature maintained below 35 °C. The formulation was sonicated until the particle size was below 200 nm. Liposomes of the adjuvants (INI-4001 and INI-2002) were prepared using a modified thin-film hydration method146. Stock solutions of the 1,2-dioleoyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphocholine (DOPC) and cholesterol were prepared in chloroform at a concentration of 125 mM. A 2:1 molar ratio of phospholipid to cholesterol was combined in a 4 mL Covaris vial. INI-4001 and INI-2002, pre-dissolved in chloroform, were introduced into the lipid. The mixture was vortexed for a minute before solvent removal using a rotary evaporator (Hei-VAP Expert HL, Heidolph Instruments GmbH & Co. KG, Schwabach, Germany). The evaporation process was conducted at 250 mBar for 4 min, followed by 50 mBar for 20 min at 40 °C to form a thin lipid film. The films were were subsequently vacuum-dried at RT overnight to eliminate residual solvent. The dried films were then hydrated using a buffer containing 50 mM Tris and 75 mM NaCl at pH 7.4, and processed with a Covaris S2 focused-ultrasonicator (Woburn, MA, USA) for 45–60 min to achieve particle sizes under 80 nm.

Emulsions containing the adjuvants (INI-4001 or INI-2002) were formulated using a two-phase method, followed by size reduction by sonication and high-pressure homogenization techniques42. Initially, a uniform oil phase was prepared by dispersing squalene, DL-α-tocopherol, polysorbate-80, and the adjuvants in isopropanol. Organic solvent removal was carried out using a Buchi rotary evaporator (New Castle, DE, USA) to generate a homogenous oil phase. Both the oil and aqueous phases (Dulbecco's PBS (DPBS), pH 6.8) were preheated to 50 °C. The aqueous phase was then introduced to the oil phase under bath sonication at 50 °C, followed by acoustic processing using a Covaris S2 Ultrasonicator (Woburn, MA, USA) for 15 min with 15 cycles of focused ultrasonication. The crude emulsion obtained was then subjected to high-pressure homogenization through a Microfluidics LV1 homogenizer (Westwood, MA, USA) at 25,000 PSI for four successive passes.

All the formulations were sterilized by filtration using 0.22 μm pore size, 13 mm Millex GV polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) filters (MilliporeSigma). The physicochemical properties were assessed using a Zetasizer Nano-ZS instrument (Malvern Panalytical, Malvern, UK). The concentrations of INI-4001 and INI-2002 were quantified via reverse-phase high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC), following a previously established method42. The final concentrations of the adjuvants in the formulations were determined using a Thermo Scientific Vanquish Flex ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography (UHPLC, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) equipped with a UV-visible and charged aerosol detector. A Waters (Millford, MA, USA) XSelect® CSH™ C18 2.1 × 150 mm, 2.5 μm particle size column at 65 °C was used to elute the compounds using an acetonitrile and isopropyl alcohol gradient with 10 mM ammonium formate and 0.1% formic acid, at 0.3 ml/min.

The physicochemical characterization data of the formulations are shown in Table 2. Based on the concentrations of INI-4001 and INI-2002 in the formulations, the appropriate amount to provide the required amount of INI-4001 and/or INI-2002 per dose was mixed with the antigen to make the vaccine within an hour of injection.

Immunization and virus challenge of mice

All experiments with mice were performed following the protocols approved by the Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (protocol #s IACUC-2019-0080, IACUC-2014-0254). In the first experiment, to determine the most protective formulation/TLR agonist adjuvant combination, naïve female 6–8-week-old BALB/c mice were primed intramuscularly with 1 µg of rNA-N1-MPP vaccine (50 μL) alone or in combination with INI-4001 (10 μg), INI-2002 (1 μg), or TRAC-478 (10 μg INI-4001 + 1 μg INI-2002) in aqueous, liposomal, or emulsion formulations. One group of mice received 25 μL AddaVax (InvivoGen, #vac-adx-10) diluted in 25 μL PBS to serve as a positive-control. Negative control group mice were vaccinated with 1 μg bovine serum albumin (BSA) combined with INI-2002, INI-4001, or TRAC-478. Twenty-one days post-prime, mice were administered a booster dose with the same formulations. Blood samples were collected 21 days after the prime and 28 days after the boost and used for serology assays. To evaluate cellular immune responses, female 6–8-week-old C57/B6 mice (The Jackson Laboratory) were immunized twice with 3 µg of rNA-N1-MPP vaccine in PBS, or with the emulsion formulation of INI-2002, INI-4001, TRAC-478 (10 μg + 5 μg), or TRAC-478 (10 μg + 1 μg).

For viral challenges (28 days post-boost), mice were anesthetized with 100 µL of ketamine-xylazine cocktail (87.5 mg/kg, 12.5 mg/kg) and then intranasally administered 50 µL of virus. Twenty-five times the 50% lethal dose (25 × LD50) was used for survival analysis and body weight monitoring in the homologous challenge experiments with A/Singapore/GP1908/2015 (H1N1) influenza virus; 5 × LD50 of A/bald eagle/Florida/W22-134-OP/2022 H5N1, A/Vietnam/1203/2004 H5N1 and A/New York City/PV63249/2022 H1N1 was used in the heterologous challenge experiments. 0.1 × LD50 (for mice vaccinated with 1 μg N1-MPP) or 0.5 × LD50 (for mice vaccinated with 3 μg N1-MPP) was used for viral titers analysis to ensure survival of all groups until the terminal time point. Survival and body weight change in mice were monitored for 14 days following the challenge. Mice that lost 25% or more of their starting body weight were euthanized. CO2 inhalation followed by cervical dislocation was used for euthanasia in all experiments on mice.

Serum passive transfer

To assess whether the various N1-MPP + TRAC-478 vaccine-induced NA-reactive antibodies in sera were sufficient to protect against homologous viral challenge, we performed a passive transfer experiment. Naive mice were intraperitoneally administered 200 μL of serum collected from vaccinated BALB/c mice four weeks post-boost and pooled by group. After 2 h, the mice were intranasally challenged with 5 × LD50 of A/Singapore/GP1908/15 H1N1. Weight loss and survival were monitored over 14 days.

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

N1-specific IgG antibody titers in sera were measured 21 days post-prime and 28 days post-boost by ELISA. 96-well plates (Immulon 4 HBX plates; ThermoFisher, #3855) were coated overnight at 4 °C, with 50 μL/well of the recombinant N1-VASP (A/Michigan/45/2015) at a concentration of 2 μg/mL in PBS. The coating was removed, and the plates were blocked with 200 μL/well of 3% milk in PBS with 0.1% Tween 20 (PBST) for 1 h at room temperature (RT). The blocking solution was removed, the serum samples were serially diluted 1:3 in 1% milk/PBST across the plate starting from a 1:50 dilution, and incubated for 2 h at RT. The plates were then washed three times with PBST and incubated for 1 h at RT with 100 μL/well of anti-mouse H&L peroxidase-conjugated (Rockland, #610-4302) IgG diluted 1:3000 in 1% milk/PBST. The plates were washed three times with PBST, incubated for 10 min in 100 μL/well of SigmaFast o-phenylenediamine dihydrochloride (OPD) developing solution (Sigma-Aldrich, #P9187-5SET), followed by the addition of 50 μL/well 3 M hydrochloric acid (HCl, Fisher Scientific, #S25856) to stop the reaction. Then the optical density (OD) at 490 nm was measured using a Synergy H1 hybrid multimode microplate reader (BioTek).

To compare the adjuvants’ ability to promote a broad IgG subclass response, IgG1 (IL-4/Th2-associated), IgG2a/c (IFNγ/Th1-associated), and IgG3 (IFNγ-induced) in sera were compared. Antigen-specific IgG2a/c and IgG1 endpoint antibody titers ratios calculated from the sera dilution at OD (0.3) were used to approximate the Th1/Th2 polarization of the humoral immune response. Anti-mouse IgG1 (Thermofisher, #PA1-74421) and anti-mouse IgG2a (Thermofisher, #A-10685) for BALB/c mice, or IgG2c for C57/B6 mice (Thermofisher, #PA1-29288), were used to measure subclass titers. Total antigen-specific IgG3 (Thermofisher, #M32607) was also measured as a marker for polyfunctionality and IFNγ-induced (Th1-associated) class switching, using a starting serum dilution of 1:15 and serially diluting twofold across the plate. IgG3 and total IgG responses for each mouse were represented as the area under the curve (AUC), which was calculated in Prism 10. The average OD values of blank wells plus 3 standard deviations (SD) for each plate were used as a cut-off.

Neuraminidase inhibition assay (NAI)

To compare NA-inhibiting antibody titers in sera, an NAI assay was conducted, as described previously147. Briefly, 96-well plates (Immulon 4 HBX plates, ThermoFisher, #3855) were coated overnight at 4 °C with 100 μl/well of fetuin (50 μg/ml; Sigma Aldrich, #F3004). Serum samples were heat-inactivated for 1 h at 56 °C and then diluted in Dulbecco’s phosphate-buffered saline with 0.05% Tween 20 and 3% BSA, to a starting concentration of 1:25 or 1:50, then serially diluted twofold across a 96-well plate. The serum dilutions were incubated with an equal volume of 2 × the 50% effective concentration (EC50) of each virus for 2 h with shaking at RT. The fetuin-coated plates were washed three times with PBST, and 100 μL of the serum-virus mixture was transferred to the fetuin-coated plates and incubated overnight at 37 °C. Plates were washed three times with PBST and then incubated with 100 μL/well horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated peanut agglutinin (PNA, Bioworld, #21510927-1) for 2 h at RT. Plates were washed four times with shaking and developed with 100 μL/well SigmaFast OPD, followed by 50 μL/well of 3 M HCl to stop the reaction. Absorbance was read at 490 nm on a microplate reader, and the individual 50% inhibitory dilution (ID50) for each sample was calculated using GraphPad Prism 10.

Plaque assay

A set of vaccinated mice was challenged with a sublethal challenge dose of 0.1 or 0.5 × LD50 of A/Singapore/GP1908/2015. To obtain the viral titers in BALB/c mice shown in Fig. 1, lungs were harvested 3 and 6 days post-infection (DPI), and homogenized. The viral titers in the bronchoalveolar fluid samples (BALF) were measured following the same protocol 5 DPI. Virus titers were measured by performing plaque assays on MDCK cells seeded at 4 × 105 cells/ml in 12-well plates. The plates were incubated overnight at 37 °C with 5% CO2. Lung homogenate samples were serially diluted 1:10 and added to confluent MDCK monolayers (200 μL/well) and incubated at 37 °C for 1 h with shaking every 15 min before the inoculum in each well was replaced with an overlay consisting of a final concentration of 0.64% agar (Oxoid, #LP0028), 1 × minimal essential media (MEM) (Thermo Scientific, #11430030) supplemented with 2 mM L-glutamine, 0.1% (w/v) sodium bicarbonate, 10 mM 4-(2-hydroxyethyl)-1-piperazineethanesulfonic acid (HEPES, Corning, #7365-45-9), penicillin (100 U/ml), streptomycin (100 μg/ml), 0.2% (w/v) BSA, N-tosyl-L-phenylalanine chloromethyl ketone (TPCK)-treated trypsin (1 μg/ml, #9002-07-7), and 0.1% (w/v) diethylaminoethyl-dextran. The cells were then incubated for 72 h at 37 °C with 5% CO2 and then fixed with 10% formalin. Plaques were visualized and counted through immunostaining. Mouse antisera and sheep anti-mouse IgG (H&L) peroxidase conjugate (Rockland, #610-603-002) were used for the plaque staining.

ADCC assay

Antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) activity in mouse sera was evaluated using an FcγRIV cell-based reporter assay according to the manufacturer’s protocol (Promega, #M1201). Briefly, white 96-well plates (Corning) were seeded with 2 × 10⁴ MDCK cells per well and incubated overnight at 37 °C with 5% CO2. After 24 h, cells were washed with PBS and infected with the A/Singapore/GP1908/2015 influenza virus at a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 5 for a single replication cycle. Cells were incubated overnight at 37 °C and 5% CO2. Next, cell culture medium was removed, and 25 μl assay buffer (Roswell Park Memorial Institute (RPMI) 1640 medium supplemented with 4% v/v low IgG FBS, Gibco, #11875101) was added to each well. Mouse sera, previously heat-inactivated at 56 °C for 1 h, were serially diluted twofold in RPMI 1640 medium and added to infected MDCK cells at 25 μl per well. After a 30-min incubation at 37 °C, 7.5 × 10⁴ Jurkat cells expressing mouse FcγRIV with a luciferase reporter driven by the nuclear factor–activated T cell promoter (Promega) were added (25 μl per well) and incubated for 6 h at 37 °C. Post-incubation, 75 μl of Bio-Glo luciferase assay reagent (Promega) was added per well and incubated for 10 min at room temperature in the dark. Luminescence was measured using a Synergy H1 microplate reader and reported in relative luminescence units for each dilution.

Tissue collection for flow cytometry experiments

Mice were humanely euthanized, and their chest cavities were opened. To collect the BALFs for viral titer and cytokine analysis following infection, a 1 ml syringe with an 18 G needle was inserted into the trachea, and the lungs were instilled with 1 ml of cold PBS and retrieved. Subsequently, the right ventricle was perfused with 10 mL of cold PBS. Lungs were harvested and processed using a gentleMACS™ Octo Dissociator for tissue homogenization and a Lung Dissociation Kit (Miltenyi Biotec, #130-095-927) to obtain a single-cell suspension. The cell suspension was then filtered through a 70-μm cell strainer (CELLTREAT, #229484 s). Spleens were harvested and mechanically disrupted using pestle homogenizers and then filtered. Cells were incubated with red blood cell (RBC) lysis buffer (BioLegend, #420302), followed by the addition of 2% FBS in PBS. The resulting cell suspension was centrifuged, and the pellet was resuspended in complete RPMI 1640 media.

Cellular immune response in lungs and spleens

For T-cell immune response analysis, 2 × 106 cells from lung and spleen samples were seeded in 96-well plates for antigen-specific stimulation. Immune cell populations in the lung and spleen were stained with fluorescently labeled antibodies right after the tissue processing. Cells were stimulated with a peptide pool library containing 15 amino acid peptides with an 11 amino acid overlap, at a concentration of 5 μg/ml. These peptides covered the entire sequence of the NA protein of the A/California/04/2009 (H1N1) influenza virus strain. Cells were incubated at 37 °C with 5% CO2 for 6 h in the presence of Brefeldin A (5 μg/ml; BioLegend), 2 μM Monensin (BioLegend), and costimulatory anti-CD28 antibodies (25 μg/ml; BioLegend). Negative control samples were incubated with all the components except the specific antigens. Following stimulation, cells were stained using fluorescently labeled antibodies (0.5 μl per sample of CD3-BV711 (Biolegend, #100241), 0.125 μl per sample of CD4-PerCP/Cy5.5 (Tonbo Biosciences, #65-0041-U100), 0.25 μl per sample of CD8-BV785 (Biolegend,#100750), 0.125 μl per sample of CD62L-APC/Cy7 (Biolegend, #104427), and 0.25 μl per sample of CD44-PE/Cy7 (Tonbo Biosciences, #600441U100). Intracellular staining of cytokines was performed using the Cytofix/Cytoperm Fixation/Permeabilization Solution Kit (BD Biosciences, #554714) following the manufacturer’s instructions. Antibodies for TNFα-AF488 (BioLegend, #506313), 0.25 μl per sample, IFNγ-BV421 (BioLegend, #502532), 0.5 μl per sample, and IL-2-PE (BioLegend, #503808), 0.5 μl per sample, were used to identify the corresponding cytokines. Statistical differences between groups were calculated by subtracting the percentage of cytokine-producing cells in non-stimulated samples from the corresponding values for peptide-stimulated samples. Data were collected using an Attune flow cytometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific) and analyzed using FlowJo_v10.8.1 software. For innate immune cell analysis, the following antibody cocktail was used for the analysis of cell populations in lungs: CD117-AF488 (0.25 μl, BioLegend, Cat# 105815), Ly6C-PerCP/Cy5.5, (0.25 μl, BioLegend, Cat# 128011), CD103-APC (1 μl, BioLegend, Cat# 121413), Ly6G-AF700 (0.25 μl, BioLegend, Cat# 127621), CD11b-APC/Cy7 (0.25 μl, BioLegend, Cat# 101225), CD64-BV421 (1.5 μl, BioLegend, Cat# 139309), CD11c-BV605 (1 μl, BioLegend, Cat# 117333), MHC-II (IA/IE)-BV650 (0.25 μl, BioLegend, Cat# 107641), CD24-BV711 (0.25 μl, BioLegend, Cat# 101851), CD45-BV785 (0.125 μl, BioLegend, Cat# 103149), FCeRI-PE (0.5 μl, BioLegend, Cat# 134307) SiglecF-PE/CF594, (0.5 μl, BD Biosciences, Cat# 562757). Data were collected using an Attune flow cytometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific). FlowJo_v10.10.0 software was used for the flow cytometry data analysis. The cell populations were identified as previously described33.

Cytokine analysis in BALF

BALF samples from mice challenged with 0.1 × LD50 virus were collected as described above. Mouse LEGENDplex kits (Biolegend, #741043, #740621) were used to measure the concentration of pro-inflammatory and Th1/Th2/Th17-associated cytokines in BALFs in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions. Data were collected using an Attune flow cytometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific) and processed with LEGENDplex™ data analysis software (https://legendplex.qognit.com).

Statistical analysis

Group comparisons were performed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with Tukey’s correction for multiple comparisons in Prism 9 Software or RStudio software (R version 4.5.1, RStudio Inc). Survival analysis results were visualized using Kaplan-Meier curves in Prism 9 Software. Principal component analysis (PCA) was performed in RStudio software. Comparison with a single control group was performed using Dunnett’s test. Differences with p-values equal to or less than 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

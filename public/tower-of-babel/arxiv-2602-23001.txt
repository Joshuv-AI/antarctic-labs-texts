Abstract
. We establish local boundedness for solutions to fractional porous medium-type equations in the fast diffusion regime, under optimal tail assumptions.

1.
Introduction

In this paper we investigate boundedness properties of weak solutions to the fractional porous medium-type equation

(1.1)

\partial_{t}u^{q}-\mathcal{L}_{K}^{s}u=0\qquad\text{in }\Omega_{T},

where
s\in(0,1)
,
q>1
, and
\Omega_{T}:=\Omega\times(0,T]
is a space-time cylinder over an open and bounded set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 2
. The nonlocal operator
\mathcal{L}_{K}^{s}
is defined as

-\mathcal{L}^{s}_{K}u(x,t)=2\mathrm{P.V.}\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}{(u(x,t)-u(y,t))}K(x,y,t)\,{\rm d}y,

where P.V. stands for the principal value and the kernel
K:\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T]\to[0,\infty)
is a measurable function such that

\frac{\textnormal{{L}}^{-1}}{{|x-y|^{N+2s}}}\leq K(x,y,t)=K(y,x,t)\leq\frac{\textnormal{{L}}}{{|x-y|^{N+2s}}},

for any
t\in(0,T],(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
and for some constant
\textnormal{{L}}\geq 1
. A wide range of physical models is described by equations of porous medium-type, and more generally by doubly nonlinear parabolic equations. In the local setting, their regularity theory has been extensively developed; we refer the reader to
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
7
,
8
,
25
,
26
,
27
]
and the references therein for a comprehensive overview. Boundedness of solutions is a significant starting point for the study of higher regularity, such as Harnack inequalities,
C^{0,\alpha}
- or
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity. In this direction, the first results on boundess of solution and gradient regularity for equations of the form

(1.2)

\partial_{t}u^{q}-\mathrm{div}(|Du|^{p-2}Du)=0

go back to the seminal works of Ivanov
[
19
,
22
,
21
,
20
]
. More recently, Bögelein & Duzaar & Giannazza & Liao & Scheven
[
6
]
established gradient Hölder continuity of weak solutions to (
1.2
) in the fast diffusion regime, and prove their boundedness in the range

0<p-1<q<\frac{N(p-1)+p}{(N-p)_{+}}=:q_{c}^{p}.

The exponent
q_{c}^{p}
plays the role of a critical threshold for boundedness. Indeed, when
p<N
and
q>q_{c}^{p}
, there exist nonnegative local weak solutions to (
1.2
) in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(-\infty,T)
which fail to be locally bounded. Thus,
q=q_{c}^{p}
represents the borderline case separating boundedness from blow-up phenomena. In particular, it is shown in
[
6
]
that if
q<q_{c}^{p}
, then weak solutions to (
1.2
) belong to
L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}
and satisfy quantitative estimates. When
q\geq q_{c}^{p}
, quantitative bounds can only be derived under the additional qualitative assumption that the solution is locally bounded.

New insights come from
[
12
]
, where the authors study local weak sub-solutions to doubly nonlinear equations with double-phase, Orlicz-type, and fully anisotropic operators. Specializing their results to the porous medium equation, corresponding to the case of (
1.2
) with
p=2
, one finds that at the critical exponent a qualitative
L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}
-regularity result holds without any additional a priori assumptions, improving upon earlier results where such regularity was available only under the extra assumption

u\in L^{A}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega_{T})
for sufficiently large
A
, see for instance
[
18
]
and the references therein.

In the supercritical (and critical) regime, instead, quantitative estimates are obtained provided the solution belongs locally to
L^{A}
, with

A>\frac{N}{p}(q-p+1),\qquad\text{when }q>p-1.

This condition can be viewed as the parabolic counterpart of the assumptions introduced in
[
34
]
to establish boundedness for solutions to elliptic equations with
(p,q)
-growth, namely

(1.3)

q>\frac{Np}{N-p},\qquad p<N,\qquad u\in L^{A}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega),\qquad A>\frac{N}{p}(q-p),

which are known to be essentially sharp, in the sense that if the bound on
q
in (
1.3
) holds and no further regularity is imposed on the solution, counterexamples to boundedness arise; see
[
30
,
31
,
16
]
.

The regularity theory for nonlocal parabolic equations has been intensively developed over the last decade. Recent advances have extended the analysis of doubly nonlinear parabolic equations to nonlocal operators, see for example
[
9
,
10
,
11
,
1
,
2
]
and references therein. In particular, equations like

\partial_{t}u^{q}+(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}u=0,

where
(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}
is the fractional
p
-Laplace operator, arises in connection with the global existence of the gradient flow associated with the fractional Sobolev inequality, see
[
33
]
. Throughout the paper, we work in the fast diffusion regime
q>1
, where solutions exhibit infinite speed of propagation, similarly to the heat equation.

In this context, a central issue is the boundedness of weak solutions to (
1.1
), and in particular the delicate interplay with the exponent
q
in critical regimes. Our analysis relies on a Caccioppoli-type estimate combined with a De Giorgi-type iteration. While energy estimates of this kind are known for porous medium-type equations of the form (
1.1
) (see
[
32
]
), the main challenge here is executing the iteration to obtain the
L^{\infty}
-bound. This requires a precise control of the nonlocal contribution, which introduces a tail condition distinct from the one in
[
32
]
, where the focus was on the expansion of positivity. To address this, we employ a time-dependent truncation method based on the so-called tail, following the approach introduced in
[
24
]
, see also
[
29
,
23
]
. This technique allows us to effectively remove the tail contribution from the Caccioppoli estimate. As a consequence, we obtain the first boundedness result under optimal tail assumptions in the setting of fractional porous medium-type equations.

For a local weak sub-solution to (
1.1
) (see Definition
2.1
), Theorem
1.1
below shows that, for
q
less than or equal to the critical threshold, i.e.

(1.4)

q_{c}:=\frac{N+2s}{N-2s},

local boundedness can be obtained without any further integrability requirements, confirming the scenario described in
[
12
, Theorem 1.1]
for the local case.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
u
be a local, nonnegative, weak sub-solution to (
1.1
) in
\Omega_{T}
. If

(1.5)

q\leq\frac{N+2s}{N-2s},

then
u
is locally bounded.

Let us now turn to the quantitative estimate. To this purpose, we introduce the exponent

(1.6)

\textnormal{{m}}:=\frac{2}{N}(N+(q+1)s),

which arises naturally from the parabolic embedding in Lemma
2.5
. For a given
\textnormal{{x}}>0
, we also define

(1.7)

\Lambda_{\textnormal{{x}}}:=2s\textnormal{{x}}+N(1-q),

a quantity that, when evaluated at the solution’s natural integrability exponent
q+1
, distinguishes the admissible range for
q
. Our first theorem provides a quantitative
L^{\infty}
-estimate for local weak sub-solutions, distinguishing between two regimes.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
q>1
and
\textnormal{{r}}\geq 1
. Assume that
u
is a local, nonnegative, weak sub-solution to (
1.1
) in
\Omega_{T}
. If
\textnormal{{r}}\leq\textnormal{{m}}
and
\Lambda_{\textnormal{{r}}}>0
, with
m
as in (
1.6
), then on any parabolic cylinder
Q_{\varrho,\sigma}(x_{0},t_{0})\Subset\Omega_{T}
, it holds

(1.8)

\displaystyle\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{Q_{\varrho/2,\sigma/2}(x_{0},t_{0})}u\leq c\left(\frac{{\varrho}^{2s}}{\sigma}\right)^{\frac{N}{\Lambda_{\textnormal{{r}}}}}\left(\mathop{\int\hskip-10.50005pt-\,\!\!\!}\nolimits\mathop{\int\hskip-10.50005pt-\,\!\!\!}\nolimits_{Q_{{\varrho},\sigma}}u^{\textnormal{{r}}}(x,t)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t\right)^{\frac{2s}{\Lambda_{\textnormal{{r}}}}}+c\left(\frac{\sigma}{\varrho^{2s}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q-1}}+c\textnormal{Tail}^{\frac{1}{q}}(u;Q_{\varrho/2,\sigma}),

for a constant
c\equiv c(s,q,N,\textnormal{{L}})
.
If
\textnormal{{r}}>\textnormal{{m}}
and
\Lambda_{\textnormal{{r}}}\leq 0
, assume moreover that

(1.9)

u\in L^{A}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\Omega_{T}),\ \text{ for some }A>\frac{N}{2s}(q-1).

Then,

(1.10)

\displaystyle\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{Q_{\varrho/2,\sigma/2}(x_{0},t_{0})}u\leq c\left(\frac{{\varrho}^{2s}}{\sigma}\right)^{\frac{N}{\Lambda_{A}}}\left(\mathop{\int\hskip-10.50005pt-\,\!\!\!}\nolimits\mathop{\int\hskip-10.50005pt-\,\!\!\!}\nolimits_{Q_{{\varrho},\sigma}}u^{A}(x,t)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t\right)^{\frac{2s}{\Lambda_{A}}}+c\left(\frac{\sigma}{\varrho^{2s}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q-1}}+c\textnormal{Tail}^{\frac{1}{q}}(u;Q_{\varrho/2,\sigma}),

on any parabolic cylinder
Q_{\varrho,\sigma}(x_{0},t_{0})\Subset\Omega_{T}
, for a constant
c\equiv c(s,q,N,\textnormal{{L}},A)
.

Remark
1.1
below identifies the critical threshold (
1.4
) and clarifies the role of the exponent
m
, as well as the higher integrability assumed in Theorem
1.2
. In particular, we show how the condition
\textnormal{{r}}\leq\textnormal{{m}},\Lambda_{\textnormal{{r}}}>0
corresponds to the regime in which
q
ensures local boundedness without further assumptions, whereas
\textnormal{{r}}>\textnormal{{m}},\Lambda_{\textnormal{{r}}}\leq 0
requires the additional hypothesis (
1.9
) and necessarily places
q
in the supercritical and critical range.

Remark 1.1
.

Since the exponent
m
in (
1.6
) coincides with the exponent appearing in the parabolic embedding Lemma
2.5
, the solution satisfies
u\in L^{\textnormal{{m}}}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\Omega_{T})
. Therefore, if
\textnormal{{r}}\leq\textnormal{{m}}
, the right-hand side of (
1.8
) is finite. Moreover, requiring
\textnormal{{r}}\in[1,\textnormal{{m}}]
together with
\Lambda_{\textnormal{{r}}}>0
is equivalent to
\Lambda_{\textnormal{{m}}}>0
. Now, observing that

\Lambda_{\textnormal{{m}}}=\frac{N+2s}{N}\,\Lambda_{q+1}

we deduce that
\Lambda_{\textnormal{{m}}}>0
if and only if
q+1<\textnormal{{m}}
and
\Lambda_{q+1}>0
, which is equivalent to

q<\frac{N+2s}{N-2s},

namely the critical exponent. If
\textnormal{{r}}>\textnormal{{m}}
and
\Lambda_{\textnormal{{r}}}\leq 0
, one necessarily has

(1.11)

q\geq\frac{N+2s}{N-2s}.

In order to obtain a quantitative estimate in this regime, the additional higher integrability assumption (
1.9
) is required.

As observed in Remark
1.1
, the behavior of the solution depends on whether the exponent
q
is subcritical, critical, or supercritical. In the subcritical case, Theorem
1.2
adapts the approach of Theorem 1.5 in
[
6
]
to the nonlocal case. In the complementary case, quantitative estimates require assuming higher integrability of the solution a priori, in line with what we have seen before in
[
12
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we collect the necessary preliminaries and several useful lemmas. In particular, we present the Caccioppoli-type energy estimate, where we already employ a time-dependent truncation level that will serve to balance the nonlocal tail in the De Giorgi iteration. This is precisely the content of Proposition
2.6
. Section 3 is then devoted to the derivation of the qualitative and quantitative estimates stated in Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.

2.
Preliminaries

This section introduces the basic notation and collects several useful results and technical lemmas that will be employed throughout the paper. Moreover, we prove a Caccioppoli-type estimate.

Notation

The symbol
c
will
denote a generic positive constant, not necessarily the same at each occurrence, that can be determined by the data from the problem under consideration. We use symbols "
\lesssim,\gtrsim
" with subscripts, to indicate that a certain inequality holds up to constants whose relevant dependencies are marked in the suffix. Let
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}.
With
B_{R}(x_{0})
we denote the open ball of center
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and radius
R>0
. When
x_{0}=0
we simply write
B_{R}
. Moreover, we define a general backward parabolic cylinder as

Q_{R,\theta}(x_{0},t_{0}):=B_{R}(x_{0})\times(t_{0}-\theta,t_{0}),\qquad(x_{0},t_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}.

For
v\in L^{1}(\Omega_{T},\mathbb{R}^{k})
and a measurable subset
U\subset\Omega
with positive
\mathcal{L}^{N}
-measure, we define the slice-wise mean
(v)_{U}:(0,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
of
v
on
U
as

(v)_{U}(t):=\mathop{\int\hskip-10.50005pt-\,\!\!\!}\nolimits_{U}v(\cdot,t)\,{\rm d}x=\frac{1}{|U|}\int_{U}v(\cdot,t)\,{\rm d}x,\qquad\text{for a.e. }t\in(0,T).

Similarly, for a measurable set
Q\subset\Omega_{T}
of positive
\mathcal{L}^{N+1}
-measure, we define the mean value
(v)_{Q}
of
v
on
Q
as

(v)_{Q}:=\mathop{\int\hskip-10.50005pt-\,\!\!\!}\nolimits\mathop{\int\hskip-10.50005pt-\,\!\!\!}\nolimits_{Q}v\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t=\frac{1}{|Q|}\iint_{Q}v\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t.

An essential element in our analysis is the tail
[
14
,
15
]
of a function
u(\cdot,t)\in L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{R})
with respect to
Q_{R,\theta}(x_{0},t_{0})
defined as:

(2.1)

\textnormal{Tail}(u;Q_{R,\theta}(x_{0},t_{0})):=\int_{t_{0}-\theta}^{t_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}(x_{0})}\frac{|u(x,t)|}{|x-x_{0}|^{N+2s}}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t.

For
s\in(0,1)
and a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we introduce the fractional Sobolev space

W^{s,2}(\Omega)
defined by

W^{s,2}(\Omega):=\left\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega):\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y<\infty\right\},

which is endowed with the norm

\|v\|_{W^{s,2}(\Omega)}:=\left(\int_{\Omega}|v|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We conclude with the notion of weak super(sub)-solution.

Definition 2.1
.

A measurable function
u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying

u\in C_{\operatorname{loc}}(0,T;L^{q+1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}_{\operatorname{loc}}(0,T;W^{s,2}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\Omega)),

is a local, weak super(sub)-solution to the equation (
1.1
) in
\Omega_{T}
, if for every sub-interval
[t_{1},t_{2}]\subset(0,T]
, every bounded open set
\tilde{\Omega}\subset\Omega
, it holds

(2.2)

\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x,t)|}{1+|x|^{N+2s}}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t<\infty

and

\displaystyle\int_{{\tilde{\Omega}}}|u|^{q-1}u\phi\,{\rm d}x\big|_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}-\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{{\tilde{\Omega}}}|u|^{q-1}u\,\partial_{t}\phi\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t

(2.3)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}(u(x,t)-u(y,t))(\phi(x,t)-\phi(y,t))K(x,y,t)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t\geq(\leq)\,0

is satisfied for all nonnegative test functions

\phi\in L^{2}_{\operatorname{loc}}(0,T;W^{s,2}_{0}({\tilde{\Omega}}))\cap W^{1,q+1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(0,T;L^{q+1}({\tilde{\Omega}})).

A function that is both a weak super- and sub-solution is called a weak solution.

Note that
(\ref{st})
makes the tail (
2.1
) of the super(sub)-solution finite.

Useful lemmas

For
u,k\in\mathbb{R}
and
q>0
, we introduce

(2.4)

\mathfrak{g}_{\pm}(u,k):=\pm q\int_{k}^{u}|t|^{q-1}(t-k)_{\pm}\,{\rm d}t,

where

(t-k)_{+}:=\max\{t-k,0\},\qquad(t-k)_{-}:=\max\{-(t-k),0\}.

The following lemma holds, see
[
3
, Lemma 2.2]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
q>0
and consider the function
\mathfrak{g}
defined in (
2.4
). Then there exists a constant
c\equiv c(q)>0
such that for all
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
it holds

\frac{1}{c}\big(|a|+|b|\big)^{q-1}(a-b)_{\pm}^{2}\leq\mathfrak{g}_{\pm}(a,b)\leq c\big(|a|+|b|\big)^{q-1}(a-b)_{\pm}^{2}.

Let us now state the fast geometric convergence Lemma, see
[
13
, Chapter I, Lemma 4.1]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(Y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be a sequence of positive real numbers satisfying the recursive inequalities

Y_{n+1}\leq c\,b^{n}\,Y_{n}^{1+\alpha}

where
c,b>1
and
\alpha>1
are given numbers.
If

Y_{0}\leq c^{-1/\alpha}\,b^{-1/\alpha^{2}},

then
Y_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.

Next, the basic iteration Lemma
[
17
, Lemma 6.1]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

For
r<R
, let
h\colon[r,R]\to\mathbb{R}
be a nonnegative bounded function such that

h(r_{1})\leq\vartheta\,h(r_{2})+\frac{A}{(r_{2}-r_{1})^{\alpha}}+\frac{B}{(r_{2}-r_{1})^{\beta}}+C

for all
r<r_{1}<r_{2}<R
, where
A,B,C\geq 0
,
\alpha>\beta\geq 0
and
\vartheta\in(0,1)
.
Then there exists a constant
c\equiv c(\alpha,\vartheta)>0
such that

h(r)\leq c(\alpha,\vartheta)\left(\frac{A}{(R-r)^{\alpha}}+\frac{B}{(R-r)^{\beta}}+C\right).

We conclude this section with the parabolic Sobolev embedding lemma, see
[
28
, Proposition A.3]
.

Lemma 2.5

(Parabolic embedding)
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
and
m
be as in (
1.6
). For any function

u\in L^{2}\big(t_{1},t_{2};W^{s,2}(B_{R})\big)\cap L^{\infty}\big(t_{1},t_{2};L^{q+1}(B_{R})\big),

which is compactly supported in
B_{(1-d)R}
for some
d\in(0,1)
and for almost every
t\in(t_{1},t_{2})
, there holds

\displaystyle\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{B_{R}}|u(x,t)|^{\textnormal{{m}}}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq c\Bigg(R^{2s}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\iint_{B_{R}\times B_{R}}\frac{|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t+\frac{1}{d^{N+2s}}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{B_{R}}|u(x,t)|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t\Bigg)

\displaystyle\times\Bigg(\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{t_{1}<t<t_{2}}\int_{B_{R}}|u(x,t)|^{q+1}\,{\rm d}x\Bigg)^{\frac{2s}{N}},

for a positive constant
c\equiv c(s,q,N)
.

2.1.
Caccioppoli estimate

Weak solutions to parabolic equations generally lack a time derivative in the Sobolev sense, which prevents their direct use in testing functions. To overcome this difficulty, one commonly employs suitable mollifications in the time variable. Then, we introduce for any
v\in L^{1}(Q_{R,\theta})
and
h>0
, the mollified functions

(v)_{h}(x,t):=\frac{1}{h}\int_{-\theta}^{t}e^{\frac{\tau-t}{h}}v(x,\tau)\,\,{\rm d}\tau,\qquad[v]_{h}(x,t):=\frac{1}{h}\int_{t}^{0}e^{\frac{t-\tau}{h}}v(x,\tau)\,\,{\rm d}\tau.

We are ready to prove the Caccioppoli inequality for weak sub-solutions to (
1.1
) in
\Omega_{T}
.

Proposition 2.6

(Caccioppoli estimate)
.

Let
q>1
. Assume that
u
is a local, nonnegative, weak sub-solution to (
1.1
) in
\Omega_{T}
. Let
0<\sigma_{1}<\sigma_{2}<\theta
and
0<r<\varrho<\tilde{\varrho}<R
be such that
Q_{R,\theta}(x_{0},t_{0})\Subset\Omega_{T}
. For any
k>0
, define
k(t):=k+\ell(t)
, where

(2.5)

\ell(t):=\left[2\textnormal{{L}}\left(\frac{R}{R-\tilde{\varrho}}\right)^{N+2s}\int_{t_{0}-\theta}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}(x_{0})}\frac{u(x,\tau)}{|x-x_{0}|^{N+2s}}\,{\rm d}x\,\mathrm{d}\tau\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Then,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in(t_{0}-\sigma_{1},t_{0})}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\mathfrak{g}_{+}(u(x,t),k(t))\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{t_{0}-\sigma_{1}}^{t_{0}}\!\!\iint_{B_{r}(x_{0})\times B_{r}(x_{0})}\frac{|(u(x,t)-k(t))_{+}-(u(y,t)-k(t))_{+}|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\qquad+\iint_{Q_{r,\sigma_{1}}(x_{0},t_{0})}(u(x,t)-k(t))_{+}\Big(\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\frac{(u(y,t)-k(t))_{-}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,{\rm d}y\Big)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{c}{\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1}}\iint_{Q_{\varrho,\sigma_{2}}(x_{0},t_{0})}\mathfrak{g}_{+}(u(x,t),k(t))\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t+c\frac{\varrho^{2(1-s)}}{(\varrho-r)^{2}}\iint_{Q_{\varrho,\sigma_{2}}(x_{0},t_{0})}(u(x,t)-k(t))_{+}^{2}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t

(2.6)

\displaystyle\qquad+cR^{N}\left(\frac{1}{\varrho-r}\right)^{N+2s}\sup_{t\in[t_{0}-\theta,t_{0}]}\mathop{\int\hskip-10.50005pt-\,\!\!\!}\nolimits_{B_{R}(x_{0})}(u(y,t)-k(t))_{+}\,{\rm d}y\iint_{Q_{\varrho,\sigma_{2}}(x_{0},t_{0})}(u(x,t)-k(t))_{+}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t,

for a positive constant
c\equiv c(s,N,\textnormal{{L}})
.

The proof follows the general scheme of
[
32
, Lemma 4.3]
.
The main difference concerns the truncation level: instead of a fixed constant
k>0
,
we introduce a time-dependent truncation
k=k(t)
, which is explicitly chosen and is
absolutely continuous in time. This choice produces an additional term in the energy
estimate coming from the time derivative of
k(\cdot)
. Such a term is arranged to exactly
compensate the nonlocal contribution arising from the tail.

Proof of Proposition
2.6
.

Let us highlight the main differences with respect to
[
32
, Proof of Lemma 4.3]
. Let
u
be as in the statement of the theorem. Take for simplicity
(x_{0},t_{0})=(0,0)
. We divide the proof into four steps.

Step 1: test function

Let
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},r,\varrho,\tilde{\varrho}
be as in the statement and choose
any
\tau_{0}\in(-\sigma_{1},0]
. Consider the time dependent cut-off functions
\zeta,\psi_{\varepsilon}
,

0<\varepsilon<\tau_{0}+\sigma_{1}
, defined as follows

\zeta(t):=\begin{cases}0&t\in(-\infty,-\sigma_{2}],\\
\displaystyle\frac{t+\sigma_{2}}{\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1}}&t\in[-\sigma_{2},-\sigma_{1}],\\
1&t\in[-\sigma_{1},\infty),\end{cases}\qquad\psi_{\varepsilon}(t):=\begin{cases}1&t\in(-\infty,\tau_{0}-\varepsilon],\\
\displaystyle\frac{\tau_{0}-t}{\varepsilon}&t\in[\tau_{0}-\varepsilon,\tau_{0}],\\
0&t\in[\tau_{0},\infty),\end{cases}

and the space dependent cut-off function
\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{R})
defined by

\eta(y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&y\in B_{r},\\
0&y\in B_{R}\backslash B_{\frac{r+\varrho}{2}},\end{array}\right.

with
0\leq\eta\leq 1
,

Reintroducing the Second Player in EPR
Leroy Chew, Mikoláš Janota, Miroslav Olšák, Martin Suda

Abstract
In this work we investigate the computational complexity of the satisfiability problem of sub-fragments of the Bernays-Schoenfinkel class of first-order logic, also known as EPR (Effectively Propositional). While Bernays-Schoenfinkel is NEXPTIME-complete, we already can obtain fragments that are PSPACE-complete by restricting our clauses to DET-HORN or KROM. However such restrictions yield very different formulas to the canonical PSPACE-complete language of Quantified Boolean Formulas (QBF). This is despite Bernays-Schoenfinkel having a natural connection to an extension of QBF known as Dependency QBF. Our main contribution is the definition of a PSPACE-complete sub-fragment of Bernays-Schoenfinkel that extends from a translation of QBF, retains a similar two-player game evaluation for its semantics and can be restricted in various ways to obtain other complete problems, particularly those at different levels in the polynomial hierarchy. We use this definition to identify problems in the TPTP library that fall into this fragment and their level in the polynomial hierarchy.

Now we describe an observation about FOL that we could not find in the literature and will be very important for this paper.
Notice that any satisfiability problem effectively adds an outermost existential question. In FOL this existential question is implicitly second-order. Given the Herbrand universe

Proof

If we have an unsatisfiable first-order problem
\phi

\pi

\pi

Notice that in each axiom clause

Therefore, all ancestors of the empty clause must have the same term in the first argument for every clause. 2.2
Quantified Boolean Formulas (QBF) and Dependency Quantified Boolean Formulas (DQBF)

The Boolean quantifiers
\forall_{B}
and
\exists_{B}
can be used to extend propositional logic. They are not to be used in the same way as quantifiers in first-order logic. Instead we quantify a propositional atom symbol, that appears without Boolean quantification in a formula (free), we forbid any other kind of atom. These formulas are called QBFs (quantified Boolean formulas) and have the following meaning:

\forall_{B}x\phi:=\phi[\bot/x]\wedge\phi[\top/x]\quad\exists_{B}x\phi:=\phi[\bot/x]\vee\phi[\top/x]

Like in other languages, algorithms exist to prenex a QBF. Additionally QBF satisfiability can be understood as the truth of the QBF sentence where all free propositional atoms are placed into an outer existential quantifier.
The satisfiability of a QBF is a
\mathsf{PSPACE}
-complete problem. In fact, it is understood as canonically
\mathsf{PSPACE}
-complete.
\mathsf{PSPACE}
is a subset of
\mathsf{NEXPTIME}
, therefore
more
solving techniques and reductions are available for QBF satisfiability than with Bernays-Schönfinkel.

One observation about QBFs is that they can be thought of as semantic games between a universal and existential player. In such a game, the turns are given by the prefix from left to right, whoever’s quantifier appears next gets to set a Boolean value (
\{\bot,\top\}
) to replace the quantified atom. At the end of the game, the universal player wins if the resulting expression evaluates to
\bot
and the existential player wins if the resulting expression evaluates to
\top
. A QBF is true if and only if the existential player has a winning strategy and false if and only if the universal player has winning strategy. These strategies can be thought of as the Skolem and Herbrand functions, respectively, which are now Boolean functions. A QBF is true (false) if and only if there are Skolem (Herbrand) functions for the existentially (universally) quantified atoms and the formula becomes a tautology (contradiction) after substituting in the Skolem (Herbrand) functions.
The quantifier order matters as it affects the size of the domain of the Skolem (Herbrand) functions.

One extension of prenex QBF that is relevant to this paper is (S-form) DQBFs (Dependency QBFs). Once again we have a propositional problem with a quantifier prefix, however there is no intrinsic order of the prefix. Instead, S-form DQBFs use Henkin quantifiers for
\exists_{B}
that explicitly specify the universal atom symbols that the quantified existential atom depends on. A DQBF is still true if and only if it is a tautology after Skolemisation of the existential variables and every QBF can be written as a DQBF. But the linear two player game can no longer apply in general, so it is difficult to make a simple branching procedure. DQBF turns out to be
\mathsf{NEXPTIME}
-complete and equivalent to Bernays-Schönfinkel fragment. The existence of the Boolean Skolem functions for a universally quantified set of atoms (with a Boolean range), is similar to the existence of an interpretation of the predicate symbols over the universally quantified set of variables (with a finite Herbrand universe range).

\displaystyle\ \forall u_{3},u_{4}\quad\big(\lnot x(u_{1},u_{1},u_{3},c_{3})\vee\lnot y(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},u_{4})\vee x(u_{1},u_{2},c_{2},u_{1})\big)\big)

Now for a universal assignment. Here the first argument
u_{1}
is assigned to
c_{1}
and the second argument (for the first and the third clause) is assigned
c_{1}
. But eventually if the universal player cannot win with this move, all
\{c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}\}^{2}
combinations would be attempted here, before concluding there is a no winning universal strategy.
Each application of a universal assignment yields another problem still in the fragment.

\displaystyle\forall u_{3},u_{4}\quad\big(y(c_{1},c_{1},u_{3},u_{4})\big)\wedge

\displaystyle\forall u_{3},u^{\prime}_{3},u_{4}\quad\big(x(c_{1},c_{1},u_{3},u_{3})\vee\lnot y(c_{1},c_{1},u_{3},u_{4})\vee\lnot x(c_{1},c_{1},u^{\prime}_{3},c_{1})\big)\wedge

\displaystyle\ \forall u_{3},u_{4}\quad\big(\lnot x(c_{1},c_{1},u_{3},c_{3})\vee\lnot y(c_{1},c_{1},u_{3},u_{4})\vee x(c_{1},c_{1},c_{2},c_{1})\big)

At this point some clauses are separable into components, this means we can rewrite the problem using miniscoping.

\displaystyle\big(\forall u_{3},u_{4}\kern 5.0pty(c_{1},c_{1},u_{3},u_{4})\big)\wedge

\displaystyle\big(\forall u_{3},u_{4}\kern 5.0ptx(c_{1},c_{1},u_{3},u_{3})\vee\lnot y(c_{1},c_{1},u_{3},u_{4})\big){\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\vee}\big(\forall u^{\prime}_{3}\kern 5.0pt\lnot x(c_{1},c_{1},u^{\prime}_{3},c_{1})\big)\wedge

\displaystyle\ \big(\forall u_{3},u_{4}\kern 5.0pt\lnot x(c_{1},c_{1},u_{3},c_{3})\vee\lnot y(c_{1},c_{1},u_{3},u_{4})\big){\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\vee}\big(x(c_{1},c_{1},c_{2},c_{1})\big)

Now it is fine for the existential player to choose the disjuncts he will use to gain a satisfiable ground problem. There are
1\times 2\times 2
options this time.
After choosing the disjunct we remain in the fragment, and now return to every clause being inseparable.
However unfortunately for the existential player no matter the choice here, if we continue to play this game the universal player can force a contradiction.

One alternative formulation is to understand components as abstracted to propositional literals and we loop through assignments to those variables during the existential query. This formulation may lead to more interesting solving approaches (i.e. clause learning or CEGAR: counter-example guided abstraction and refinement), but we do not need it here to show correctness and constraint learning complicates our incoming argument about space complexity.

Lemma 3
(Correctness)

If
\phi
is a QEALM-fragment problem that does not contain the empty clause then

\texttt{{SolveByAlternation}}(\phi)
(Algorithm
1
) returns SAT if and only if there is a satisfying interpretation for
\phi

Proof

We can perform induction on the number of quantified variables + predicate symbols appearing in the formula

Base Case: Fully Ground.

If
\phi
is ground and
SolveByAlternation
(
\phi

If
\phi
is ground and
SolveByAlternation
(
\phi
) returns UNSAT then there is no satisfying Boolean assignment to the ground values, hence there is no truth table interpretation of the predicates which does not falsify
\phi

Inductive Step: Universal Query.

If
\phi
has a satisfying interpretation then the same satisfying interpretation satisfies all clauses under instantiation, therefore
\texttt{{SolveByAlternation}}(\phi)
returns SAT after it exhausts all assignments to the variables at
\opos(\phi)
.
If
\phi
has no satisfying assignment then it has a Ground Resolution refutation, however said refutation cannot mix variables from different instantiations of the fully unified variables (there are no resolution paths). Therefore, there is some instantiation of the

variables where
\phi
has no satisfying interpretation and hence
SolveByAlternation
(
\phi
) returns UNSAT.

Inductive Step: Existential Query.
If
\phi
is unsatisfiable then any problem composed of subclauses of
\phi
is also unsatisfiable, therefore
\texttt{{SolveByAlternation}}(\phi)
returns UNSAT.
Now suppose
\phi
has a satisfying interpretation.
We can take
\phi
out of its prefix form.
Since
\forall
and
\wedge
commute, each clause could have its own quantification, then we push all universal quantifiers to the level of the components.
Lemma
2
is important because components are disjoint and quantified variables are not shared between them.
So we have a disjunction where each disjunct is universally quantified. The satisfying interpretation must satisfy at least one disjunct in each conjunct, and every assignment of disjuncts gets selected in the loop.

Lemma 4
(
\mathsf{PSPACE}
inclusion)

Given a suitable SAT solver black box, the amount of memory of Algorithm
1
is bounded from above by a polynomial in the size of the input CNF
\phi
, if
\phi
is a QEALM-fragment problem.

Proof

The algorithm requires a stack of recursive calls. The stack size of recursive calls from a universal search is bounded by the largest arity of a predicate or the number of universal quantifiers, since each instantiation sets a constant value to at least one variable that is shared in all literals.
The stack of the recursive calls from an existential search is bounded by the number of times we can split at least one clause, so an upper bound is the total number of literals.
Therefore there are at most a linear number of recursive calls at any one time. Moreover, each recursive call only uses a polynomial amount of memory for the loops, propositional logic and the SAT call. Hence the algorithm uses
\mathsf{PSPACE}
.

In fact, with a bit more detail we can do better than just
\mathsf{PSPACE}
and identify the fragments that belong in classes of the polynomial hierarchy. The idea is to identify which argument positions may force an existential query in Algorithm
1
. If we bound the number of times an existential query happens between universal queries we bound the number of alternations by roughly twice of that.

By induction on the maximum number of recursive calls of Algorithm

Base Case:

Lemma 6
(
\mathsf{PSPACE}
-hardness)

Any closed (totally quantified) QBF problem can be transformed into a QEALM-fragment problem with polynomial time computable function

,
where

is satisfiable if and only the QBF
\Psi
is true.

This transformation is similar to that in the tool
qbf2epr

[
22
]
, but removes first-order literals that represent the universal literals.

Proof

Start with a closed QBF where the propositional matrix is a CNF. We will use two constants symbols
0
and
1
, which will also together form our Herbrand universe.
For each universal Boolean variable
u
in the QBF, we introduce a universal first-order variable which we can again call
u
.
For each existential Boolean variable

we introduce a predicate
p_{e}
the arity
\mathfrak{a}(p_{e})
, which is the number of universal variables that occur left of
e
in the QBF prefix.

We convert a non-tautological QBF clause
C
to a Bernays-Schönfinkel clause
C^{\prime}
. The clause
C^{\prime}
consists of literals
l
using predicate symbol
p_{e}(\dots)
for every occurrence of a positive existential literal

in
C
and
\lnot p_{e}(\dots)
for every occurrence of a negative existential literal
\lnot e
, the arguments depend on the universal literals in
C
. Let

be the
i
-th universal variable appearing from left to right in the QBF prefix. The
i
-th argument of any literal

(if

is
0
if

is a literal in

is a literal in

otherwise.
The resulting Bernays-Schönfinkel problem is the conjunction of these clauses and we can see that this problem is in QEALM-fragment as the arguments are defined globally over all literals in the clause.

To see the correctness of the conversion, we can use the fact that a QBF is true if and only if there exists some set of Boolean Skolem functions for each existential variable that define the existential response, so that every clause is always satisfied under every possible universal assignment. These Boolean Skolem functions will correspond to the satisfying Boolean interpretations of the predicate symbols over the Herbrand universe in first-order logic.

In one direction, if a QBF is true then the Skolem functions satisfy the clause

even when all universal literals are false, which is exactly what the clause

represents; the arguments substitute in when the literals are false. Likewise if

is always true that means the interpretation as a Boolean function will always satisfy the clause

However when any of them are true, the clause is also satisfied.

Theorem 3.2
(
\mathsf{\Sigma_{2k-1}^{\mathsf{p}}}

•

•

The set of all QEALM-fragment problems which have

\mathsf{\Pi_{2k}^{\mathsf{p}}}
-complete.

Proof

Using Lemma
5
we get inclusion.
Hardness can be shown by transforming a Prenex QBF with a bounded number of alternations to QEALM-fragment and while staying within the boundaries of side conditions in the Theorem statement.

Suppose we have a Prenex QBF which starts with an existential quantifier and then has
2k-1

\exists_{B}X_{1}\forall_{B}U_{2}\exists_{B}X_{2}...\exists_{B}X_{k}

(this is

-complete).
In either case let it end with an existential block. After the prefix, there is the propositional part known as the matrix. We can use a Tseitin transformation to change it into conjunctive normal form.
The transformation introduces new existentially quantified variables that are to be placed in the final existential block after all other variables. This preserves satisfiability, as any winning strategies can be extended to play the new variables according to their definitions. Therefore, after the Tseitin transformation, we retain the same number of quantifier blocks.

We can then use the conversion to the QEALM-fragment as given in Lemma

,
after which we observe the following:

is a fork index if and only if the QBF begins with an existential quantifier and

.
Hence there are going to be

5
Conclusion

This work widens our understanding of first-order logic by finding a new
\mathsf{PSPACE}
-complete fragment, that shares many similarities to QBF.
Remarkably,
despite not being defined on propositional connectives, the QEALM-fragment has multiple properties shared by
Krom
and
Horn
. QEALM instances can be detected per-clause, the fragment is closed under Robinson’s Resolution, and even retains hardness when intersected with
Krom
,
Horn
and
Affine
(unlike QBF
[
5
,
19
]
).

Part of our motivation is to utilise the
\mathsf{PSPACE}
-completeness and connection to QBF for benefits in solving, certification and theory.
At present our recursive solving algorithms shows
\mathsf{PSPACE}
inclusion, but lacks
optimisations we would need to use it practically.

One area of future interest that can also take advantage of is QBF proof complexity, as our understanding of it has grown substantially over the last decade.
For example, some QBF proof systems are known to have super-polynomial lower bounds only if either proof complexity or circuit complexity open problems are solved
[
4
]
. In theory, a proof system with this property exists in every
\mathsf{PSPACE}
language through a QBF translation, but finding one that is natural and suitable for applications is more difficult. Unfortunately, Robinson’s Resolution is known to have exponential lower bounds even for the QEALM-fragment. In fact, it runs foul of something similar to a well known QBF proof complexity pitfall known as “semantic hardness”
[
3
]
, which we aim to demonstrate in future work.

, and its second value as
z_{k+1}
. By induction hypothesis,
both

are false, so as well

finishing the induction step, and the proof as well.
∎

Definition 7
(Robinson’s Resolution)

Robinson’s Resolution works on prenex conjunctive normal forms.
In a Robinson’s Resolution proof we have a prenex sentence
\Pi\phi
, where
\Pi
is a quantifier prefix and
\phi
is quantifier-free formula. The lines of the proof are clauses and we aim to show
\Pi\phi
is unsatisfiable by deriving the empty clause.
Since the rules work with clauses, we need an axiom rule that initialises a clause from the matrix as an axiom.

(Axiom)

C

Where

\Pi\phi
.

The two derivation rules are Resolution and Factoring which are as follows.

C_{1}\vee\neg p(t_{1}\dots t_{n})

C_{2}\vee p(s_{1}\dots s_{n})

(Resolution)

C_{1}\sigma\vee C_{2}\sigma

Where
\sigma=\mgu(p(t_{1}\dots t_{n}),p(s_{1}\dots s_{n}))
. If no unifier exists, the inference step is disallowed. Technically,

and

on the fly if they do.

C\vee p(t_{1}\dots t_{n})\vee p(s_{1}\dots s_{n})

(Factoring)

C\vee p(t_{1}\dots t_{n})\sigma

Where
\sigma=\mgu(p(t_{1}\dots t_{n}),p(s_{1}\dots s_{n}))
.

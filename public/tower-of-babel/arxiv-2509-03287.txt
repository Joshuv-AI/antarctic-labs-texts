Fractional Bessel-Sobolev and Bessel B-potential spaces
Mouna Chegaar and Á. P. Horváth

Abstract
. After proving the equivalence of the Bessel K K -functional and the corresponding spherical modulus of smoothness we define fractional Bessel-Sobolev spaces. As an analog of the classical one the imbedding relation of fractional Bessel-Sobolev and Bessel B-potential spaces is pointed out. Applying the defined notions a potential theoretic characterization of removable sets with respect to certain fractional partial differential operator is derived.

1.
Introduction

Bessel and fractional Bessel differential operators occur frequently in physics and in practice, see e.g.
[
24
]
or
[
29
]
. The generalized (or Bessel) translation defined by Delsarte,
[
12
]
and developed by Levitan,
[
22
]
has proven to be a fairly effective tool for examining these operators. Application of Bessel translation rather than the standard one modifies the geometry of the space appropriately. This is why it is worth to introduce B-Hardy, B-Morrey or the different generalized Bessel B-potential spaces etc., see
[
18
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
28
]
and
[
30
]
.

For instance, to study the convergence properties of a numerical solution, to characterize removable sets or to develop the suitable spectral theory the investigation of the underlying space is essential.

First we focus on the equivalence of modulus of smoothness and the corresponding
K
-functional. It is a crucial inequality in approximation theory and also in the theory of interpolation of spaces. In
[
14
]
several results on
K
-functional are summarized in classical cases. In the following we extend the one-dimensional results of
[
27
]
to higher dimensions. The main tool of this section is the weighted spherical modulus of smoothness introduced in
[
11
]
.

This equivalence allows to define the appropriate spherical Gagliardo-type seminorm (c.f.
[
25
]
) and fractional Bessel-Sobolev spaces, i.e. to extend the results of
[
27
]
and
[
28
]
to fractional exponents.

As usual, Bessel translation defines a convolution and so Bessel B-potential spaces, i.e. the spaces of functions derived by Bessel convolution with the Bessel kernel. Classical Bessel potential spaces were introduced systematically in a series of papers by Aronszajn and Smith, see e.g.
[
4
]
and by Calderón, see
[
9
]
for the one-dimensional case. An analog of Calderón’s theorem is derived in the Bessel case, see
[
28
]
or
[
17
]
. The fractional case is a little different, c.f.
[
1
]
. By examining the defined norms, a suitable imbedding theorem can be proven.

As an application, we characterize removable sets with respect to certain fractional partial differential operator. The potential theoretic characterization of removable sets is due to Carleson, see
[
10
]
. For Laplace-Bessel type operators similar results can be found in
[
17
]
. For fractional parabolic operators the results of
[
23
]
are extended in
[
11
]
. In the last section we give a characterization of removable sets with respect to fractional elliptic operators via capacity defined by fractional Bessel B-potencial spaces.

2.
Notation, preliminaries

2.1.
Norms, spaces

On
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}:=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}):x_{i}>0\hskip 4.0pti=1,\dots,n\}
we introduce the
L^{p}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
space with norm

\|f\|_{p,a}^{p}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|f(x)|^{p}x^{a}dx,

where
a=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})
,
a_{i}>0
,
i=1,\dots,n
;
x^{a}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{a_{i}}
.

The appropriate weighted inner product is as follows.

\langle f,g\rangle_{a}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}f(x)g(x)x^{a}dx.

\mathcal{S}_{(e)}
stands for the even functions from the Schwartz class, i.e.

\mathcal{S}_{(e)}:=\left\{f\in\mathcal{S}:\frac{\partial^{2m+1}}{\partial x_{i}^{2m+1}}f\large{|}_{x_{i}=0}=0,\hskip 4.0pt\forall m\in\mathbb{N},\hskip 4.0pti=1,\dots,n\right\}.

If
h\in\mathcal{S}_{(e)}^{\prime}
,
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}_{(e)}
we denote the effect of
h
on
\varphi
by
\langle h,\varphi\rangle_{a}
as well.

2.2.
Bessel differential operators

The Bessel-Laplace operator on
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
is defined as follows.

(1)

\Delta_{a}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}B_{\alpha_{i}},\hskip 4.0pt\mbox{where}\hskip 4.0ptB_{\alpha_{i}}:=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{i}}+\frac{2\alpha_{i}+1}{x_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}},

and

a_{i}=2\alpha_{i}+1,

(i.e.
\alpha_{i}>-\frac{1}{2}
,
i=1,\dots,n
).
Hereafter we deal with partial differential operators which contain integer or fractional powers of
\Delta_{a}
or
I-\Delta_{a}
.

2.3.
Bessel functions, Bessel translation

The entire Bessel functions are

(2)

j_{\alpha}(z)=\Gamma(\alpha+1)\left(\frac{2}{z}\right)^{\alpha}J_{\alpha}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}\Gamma(\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(k+1)\Gamma(k+\alpha+1)}\left(\frac{z}{2}\right)^{2k},

where
\alpha>-\frac{1}{2}
. We note that
j_{-\frac{1}{2}}(x)=\cos x

B_{\alpha}

(3)

\|j_{\alpha}\|_{\infty,\mathbb{R}_{+}}=j_{\alpha}(0)=1.

(4)

j_{\alpha}^{\prime}(z)=-\frac{1}{2(\alpha+1)}zj_{\alpha+1}(z),

[

We introduce the following abbreviation, cf.
[

\mathbb{j}_{a}(x,\xi):=\prod_{i=1}^{n}j_{\alpha_{i}}(x_{i}\xi_{i}).

Subsequently we also need the modified Bessel function of the second kind

(5)

K_{\nu}(x)=\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{i^{\nu}J_{-\nu}(ix)-i^{-\nu}J_{\nu}(ix)}{\sin\nu\pi},

[

Around zero:

(6)

K_{\nu}(r)\sim\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\ln\frac{r}{2}-c,\hskip 4.0pt\mbox{if}\hskip 4.0pt\nu=0\\
C(\nu)r^{-\nu},\hskip 4.0pt\mbox{if}\hskip 4.0pt\nu>0,\end{array}\right.

Around infinity:

(7)

K_{\nu}(r)\sim\frac{c}{\sqrt{r}}e^{-r},

see e.g.
[

Moreover around zero for

(8)

|(r^{\nu}K_{\nu}(r))^{\prime}|\leq c\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}r^{2\nu-1}\ln\frac{1}{r},\hskip 4.0pt\mbox{if}\hskip 4.0pt1>2\nu,\hskip 4.0pt\nu\in\mathbb{N}\\
r^{2\nu-1},\hskip 4.0pt\mbox{if}\hskip 4.0pt1>2\nu,\hskip 4.0pt\nu\not\in\mathbb{N}\\
1,\hskip 4.0pt\mbox{if}\hskip 4.0pt1<2\nu,\hskip 4.0pt\nu\not\in\mathbb{N}\\
r,\hskip 4.0pt\mbox{if}\hskip 4.0pt1<2\nu,\hskip 4.0pt\nu\not\in\mathbb{N},\end{array}\right.,

[
5
, (40),(41)]

With the above notation the Bessel translation of a function,

where

(9)

T_{a}^{t}f(x)=T_{a}^{x}f(t),

(10)

2.4.

f*_{a}g=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}T_{a,x}^{t}f(x)g(x)x^{a}dx.

We have

(12)

f*_{a}g=g*_{a}f,\hskip 4.0pt\hskip 4.0ptf*_{a}(g*_{a}h)=(f*_{a}g)*_{a}h.

Furthermore Young’s inequality fulfils i. e. if

\mathcal{H}_{a}(f,\xi)=\hat{f}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}f(x)\mathbb{j}_{a}(x,\xi)x^{a}dx.

(14)

The relationship of Hankel transform to translation and convolution is as follows.

(15)

\mathcal{H}_{a}(T^{y}_{a}f)(\xi)=\mathbb{j}_{a}(u,y)(\xi,y)\mathcal{H}_{a}(f)(\xi);\hskip 4.0pt\hskip 4.0pt\hskip 4.0ptT^{y}_{a}(\mathcal{H}_{a}f)(\xi)=\mathcal{H}_{a}(\mathbb{j}_{a}(u,y)f(x)),

[

for

Subsequently we need that the Hankel transform of a radial function. We begin with the following formula.

(17)

\frac{1}{|S_{1+}^{n}|_{a}}\int_{S_{1+}^{n}}\mathbb{j}_{a}(r\Theta,\xi))\Theta^{a}dS(\Theta)=j_{\frac{n+|a|}{2}-1}(r|\xi|),

Definition 1

The differences are

Subsequently the following property of

Lemma 1

[

(20)

\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}^{-1}\left(\frac{\mathcal{J}_{\alpha,k}(\xi)}{\xi^{2k}}\right)\in L^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}_{+}).

With this notation we introduce the following spherical differences.

\Delta_{sph,r,a}f(x):=\frac{1}{|S^{n}_{1+}|_{a}}\int_{S^{n}_{1+}}(f(x)-T^{r\Theta}_{a}f(x))\Theta^{a}dS(\Theta);

\omega_{sph,m}(f,t)_{a,p}:=\sup_{0<h<t}\|\Delta_{sph,h,a}^{m}f\|_{p,a}.

Properties.

In view of (

(21)

\mathcal{H}_{a}(\Delta_{sph,r,a}f)(\xi)=(1-j_{\frac{n+|a|}{2}-1}(r|\xi|))(\mathcal{H}_{a}f)(\xi).

From the definition and from (

3.
K-functional

We give the definition of the Bessel-Sobolev space and the corresponding
K
-functional, see
[
28
]
,
[
17
]
and in 1-dimension see
[
27
, (1.9)]
. The following theorem describes the connection beetween the defined
K
-functional and spherical modulus of smoothness.

Definition 3
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
;
m\in\mathbb{N}
.

W^{m,p}_{\Delta_{a}}:=\left\{f:\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}:\Delta_{a}^{k}f\in L^{p}_{a},\hskip 4.0ptk=0,\dots,m\right\},

\|f\|_{W^{m,p}_{\Delta_{a}}}=\left(\sum_{k=0}^{m}\|\Delta_{a}^{k}f\|_{p,a}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Definition 4
.

K\left(f,t,L_{a}^{p},W^{k,p}_{\Delta_{a}}\right)=\inf\{\|f-g\|_{p,a}+t\|\Delta_{a}^{k}g\|_{p,a}:g\in W^{k,p}_{\Delta_{a}}\},

where
f\in L_{a}^{p}
,
t>0
.

Theorem 1
.

There is a positive constant
c=c(m,n,a)
, such that

\frac{1}{c}\omega_{sph,m}(f,t)_{a,p}\leq K\left(f,t^{2m},L_{a}^{p},W^{m,p}_{\Delta_{a}}\right)\leq c\omega_{sph,m}(f,t)_{a,p},

where
f\in L_{a}^{p}
,
t>0
.

This theorem is an
n
-dimensional version of
[
27
, Theorem 1.2]
. The proof follows the chain of ideas of the original one. The main tool for the
n
-dimensional case is the spherical modulus of smoothness defined above. For sake of self-containedness we give the detailed proof.

For the proof of the inequality on the left-hand side we need the next lemma.

Lemma 2
.

If
g\in W^{k,p}_{\Delta_{a}}
, then for all
t>0
and
1\leq p\leq\infty

\omega_{sph,m}(g,t)_{a,p}\leq ct^{2m}\|\Delta_{a}^{m}g\|_{p,a},

where
c=c(a,n,m)
.

Proof.

(of the left inequality of Theorem
1
) Let
f\in L_{a}^{p}
and
g\in W^{m,p}_{\Delta_{a}}
. According to (
22
) and (
23
) we have

\omega_{sph,m}(f,t)_{a,p}\leq\omega_{sph,m}(f-g,t)_{a,p}+\omega_{sph,m}(g,t)_{a,p}\leq 2^{m}\|f-g\|_{p,a}+ct^{2m}\|\Delta_{a}^{m}g\|_{p,a}

\leq c\left(\|f-g\|_{p,a}+t^{2m}\|\Delta_{a}^{m}g\|_{p,a}\right),

where Lemma
2
is applied. Taking infimum over
g\in W^{k,p}_{\Delta_{a}}
, the left-hand side of Theorem
1

To prove Lemma
2

Notation.

\|P_{\nu}(f)\|_{p,a}\leq c\|f\|_{p,a};\hskip 4.0pt\hskip 4.0ptc\neq c(\nu).

=c\sup_{\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{e},\|\tau\|_{p,a}\leq 1}\langle G_{a,s-s_{k}}*_{a}g,\tau\rangle_{a}\leq c\|g\|_{p^{\prime},a}\leq c\varepsilon.

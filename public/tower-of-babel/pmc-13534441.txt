miRNAs as cell-intrinsic epigenetic regulators in muscle in type 2 diabetes
Authors: Allah Nawaz, Marsel Lino, Nida Haider, Antonio S Gomes, Anindya Ghosh, Eiichiro Satake, Arijeet K Gattu, C Ronald Kahn
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractmiRNAs are important metabolic regulators and are altered at both the cellular and secreted levels in diseases, including type 2 diabetes (T2D). However, to what extent these alterations are in response to factors in the in vivo milieu or are cell-intrinsic remains unclear. Here we used a disease-in-a-dish model in which iPSCs from T2D patients and controls were differentiated into myoblasts (iMyos), and their cellular and secreted miRNAs were profiled. We found that iMyos from T2D donors exhibit cell-intrinsic alterations in miRNA expression and secretion in small extracellular vesicles (sEVs)/exosomes. Integrating miRNA-predicted targets with transcriptomic and proteomic data revealed that miRNAs altered in T2D iMyos were associated with coordinated changes in their predicted targets, but with a much greater impact on protein than on mRNA levels. This effect was validated by miRNA overexpression in control iMyos. The upregulated miRNAs targeted pathways related to aerobic respiration, membrane trafficking, and RNA metabolism. Even more marked changes were observed in sEV-associated miRNAs secreted by T2D iMyos, indicative of T2D-associated effects on miRNA sorting and release. Target genes of secreted miRNAs altered in T2D iMyos were enriched in metabolic pathways including insulin signaling and mitochondrial metabolism. Consistent with this, sEVs derived from control iMyos increased glucose uptake and mitochondrial function in recipient human white adipocytes, whereas sEVs from T2D iMyos did not. Thus, in T2D, muscle exhibits cell-intrinsic alterations in expression and secretion of miRNAs, which function as epigenetic regulators of protein expression locally, as well as potentially in distal tissues. Subject terms: Non-coding RNAs, Pluripotent stem cells, Stem-cell differentiation

Introduction

Skeletal muscle is one of the largest organs in the body and is a primary site for insulin action to control blood glucose.1,2 In skeletal muscle, insulin facilitates glucose uptake, promotes glycogen and protein synthesis, and inhibits protein degradation. Dysfunction of skeletal muscle metabolism in type 2 diabetes (T2D) is a major contributor to whole body insulin resistance and precedes and predicts T2D in offspring of diabetic parents.1,3,4 Our group recently demonstrated that induced pluripotent stem (iPS) cells from patients with T2D or insulin resistance without T2D differentiated into myoblasts in vitro (iMyos) exhibit multiple defects found in skeletal muscle in vivo, including altered insulin signaling, decreased insulin-stimulated glucose uptake, and reduced mitochondrial respiration.5,6 Thus, the insulin resistance observed in T2D iMyos in vitro mirrors many features of muscle insulin resistance in vivo but in the absence of potentially confounding circulating factors.

Cell-intrinsic drivers of insulin resistance can include both genetic and epigenetic factors, such as changes in DNA sequence, DNA methylation, histone modification, and altered levels of noncoding RNAs, including miRNAs.6–10 Indeed, miRNAs are major regulators of mRNA and protein levels and have been implicated in regulating various aspects of T2D, including insulin resistance, glucose metabolism, and diabetic complications.11,12 Thus, studies have shown that miR-133, miR-206, and miR-1, which are capable of regulating myoblast differentiation and insulin sensitivity, are all decreased in the muscle of T2D patients.13,14 Likewise, a recent meta-analysis identified miR-144, miR-29a, and miR-133 as regulators of insulin signaling, glucose metabolism, and insulin resistance in muscle, thus contributing to the pathogenesis of T2D.15

In addition, previous reports have shown that circulating miRNAs, such as miR-122, miR-192, miR-194, and miR-193, are increased in the serum of T2D patients.16–19 miR-122 can target the regulatory subunit of AMP-activated protein kinase (PRKAB1), interleukin 1 receptor type 1, the REL proto-oncogene, and the NF-kB subunit, potentially contributing to the pathogenesis of insulin resistance.20 Likewise, miR-192 is known to target the transcription factor RFX6, which regulates islet formation and insulin production, whereas miR-194 can target RUNX1, FOXA1, AKT, and GSK3, all of which regulate metabolic genes and phosphorylation in muscle. Similarly, elevated circulating miR-193b levels have been shown to negatively correlate with muscle mass in T2D patients, suggesting its involvement in muscle dysfunction in T2D. This is thought to occur via effects of miR-193b to suppress Pdk1 expression, leading to the deactivation of the Akt/mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR)/p70S6 kinase (S6K) signaling pathway, which in turn reduces protein synthesis.19

In the present study, we sought to determine whether changes in miRNA abundance and secretion are part of a cell-intrinsic program using a disease-in-a-dish approach with iPS cells from control and T2D patients differentiated into myoblasts (iMyos) in vitro and performed a sensitive nuclease protection assay to assess the expression profile of cellular and secreted miRNAs. We found that 22 miRNAs were significantly upregulated and 49 were downregulated in iMyos in T2D. Overlapping the predicted targets of these miRNAs with gene and protein expression data indicated that these miRNAs may contribute to altered expression of over 200 proteins. Interestingly, this occurs in most cases with little to no change in mRNA levels. This can be reproduced in vitro using miRNA mimics. We also show cell-intrinsic alterations in miRNA secretion in sEVs in T2D iMyos, most of which occur independent of changes in cellular miRNA content. Together, these data demonstrate that miRNAs may serve as important epigenetic regulators of protein expression in skeletal muscle in T2D and may mediate additional systemic effects through independent changes in secreted miRNAs.

Results

Cell-intrinsic alterations in expression of cellular miRNAs in T2D

To identify cell-intrinsic drivers of skeletal muscle insulin resistance in T2D without the interference of systemic factors, we derived iPS cell lines from people with T2D or healthy controls and differentiated these cells into myoblasts (iMyos) in vitro (Fig. 1a). Patient characteristics of donors are provided in Supplementary Fig. 1a. Cellular and secreted miRNAs were then assessed using the HTG EdgeSeq nuclease protection assay, which detects over 2,000 known human miRNAs. In total, 71 miRNAs were significantly altered by T2D, with 22 miRNAs being increased and 49 being decreased in T2D iMyos (Fig. 1b). In general, there were not only more downregulated than upregulated miRNAs, but the magnitude of change was also greater. The upregulated miRNAs in T2D iMyos included miR-7161-3p, miR-5004-3p, and miR-1272, which were increased by 1.8-3.0-fold, while the downregulated miRNAs, including miR-222-5p, miR-155-5p, and miR-142-5p, were decreased by 1.9-2.3-fold (Fig. 1c, d). In T2D iMyos, upregulation was observed for both mature miRNAs and their precursors (Fig. 1e), indicating that upregulation was likely due to enhanced transcription rather than changes in half-life. In contrast, for the miRNAs that were decreased in T2D iMyos, including miR-222-5p, miR-155-5p, and miR-142-5p, there was no change in precursor levels, suggesting that these decreases reflect altered processing, increased degradation or secretion of these miRNAs from the cell, rather than changes at the transcriptional level (Fig. 1f). Interestingly, of the 22 miRNAs increased in T2D iMyos, 2 miRNAs were significantly increased only in males (Supplementary Fig. 1b). Likewise, of the 49 miRNAs decreased in T2D iMyos, 24 were significantly decreased in males only, while 1 was decreased in females only, indicating some level of sexual dimorphism. Representative examples of miRNAs that were decreased only in males with T2D (miR-335-5p), only in females with T2D (miR-23a-5p), or in both males and females (miR-142-5p, miR-193a-3p, and miR-193a-5p) are shown in Supplementary Fig. 1c, d. Importantly, two of the most abundant miRNAs downregulated in T2D iMyos, miR-206 and miR-133 (Supplementary Fig. 2a), have also been found to be decreased in the muscle of patients with T2D.13 Similarly, miR-194-5p was decreased in T2D iMyos (Supplementary Fig. 2b) and has also been found to be decreased in the muscle of patients with T2D.18 Likewise, we observed increased expression of miR-135a-5p in T2D iMyos (Supplementary Fig. 2b), consistent with previous studies of skeletal muscle biopsies of humans with T2D and db/db mice.21,22 As summarized in Table 1, several of the downregulated miRNAs have been previously linked to insulin resistance, impaired glucose metabolism, and inflammatory signaling in metabolic tissues.13,23–34

To determine whether altered miRNA expression is only a consequence of impaired insulin signaling, we induced insulin resistance in control iMyos by expressing a kinase-dead insulin receptor mutant (InsR K1030R), as confirmed by immunoblot analysis (Supplementary Fig. 3a). Assessment of 2-NBDG uptake in these cells revealed a marked reduction in insulin-stimulated glucose uptake (Supplementary Fig. 3b), confirming the establishment of an insulin-resistant phenotype. This resulted in partial recapitulation of the miRNA signature seen in T2D iMyos (Supplementary Fig. 3c) with a trend toward increased expression of miR-135a-5p and a significant decrease in its predicted target mRNA BZW1 and a decrease in miR-142-5p associated with a trend toward an increase in its predicted target RHOA (Supplementary Fig. 3d). Thus, altered miRNA levels in T2D iMyos in vitro retain the phenotype observed in vivo, and some of these changes may be secondary to insulin resistance, while others may contribute to insulin resistance.

The changes in miRNA expression in T2D iMyos were unlikely due to changes in miRNA biogenesis machinery, since changes involved only a subset of all miRNAs, and multiple miRNA synthesis enzymes, including DICER and DROSHA, were unchanged at either the mRNA or protein level (Supplementary Fig. 4a–c). However, some of the downregulated miRNAs were in clusters (defined as ≥2 miRNAs within a 10 kb region) located on chromosomes 7, 9, 19 and X.35,36 Thus, of the 49 downregulated miRNAs, seven were located in specific genomic regions on chromosome 7, and six were located on chromosome 9 (Fig. 1g), suggesting that these miRNAs may share common transcriptional control mechanisms. Likewise, three of the upregulated miRNAs were encoded on chromosome 9 and two miRNAs each on chromosomes 6, 12, 16 and 19 (Fig. 1g), although these are not part of any recognized miRNA clusters. We previously showed that miRNA imprinting may serve as an epigenetic control mechanism,37 and the substantial alterations in miRNA expression in T2D could occur through disruption of these pathways. Future studies incorporating allele-specific and epigenetic analyses in larger cohorts will be required to determine whether imprinting contributes to the observed differences in miRNA expression in T2D.

Metabolic genes targeted by miRNAs regulated by T2D

To determine which cellular processes might be affected by altered miRNA expression in T2D iMyos, we performed target analysis using microT-CDS and TargetScanHuman (v8.0).38,39 We then cross-referenced all candidate target genes with RNA-seq and MS-based proteomics data from control and T2D iMyos. Analysis of the predicted targets of the 22 upregulated miRNAs revealed 127 that were significantly decreased at the protein level in T2D iMyos (Fig. 2a, left panel). Interestingly, overlapping these data with RNA-seq data from the same cells revealed that only 12 of the predicted targets decreased at the protein and mRNA levels, and three were actually increased at the mRNA level (Fig. 2a, right panel), suggesting regulation of mRNA translation rather than effects on mRNA degradation. Pathway analysis of these downregulated proteins showed that they were enriched in pathways related to aerobic respiration, electron transport chain, membrane trafficking, and metabolism of RNA (Fig. 2b). Representative examples of these downregulated proteins and their quantitation, including NDUFS8 (a core subunit of mitochondrial complex I), IDH2 (a mitochondrial enzyme in the TCA cycle), TOMM20 (a key component of the mitochondrial protein import machinery), COPB2 and COG4 (Golgi-associated proteins essential for vesicular trafficking), and METTL14 and YTHDF1 (writers and readers of m6A RNA modifications, respectively), are shown in Fig. 2c.

In a similar fashion, target prediction analysis for the 49 downregulated miRNAs revealed that 190 of their predicted targets were significantly increased in T2D iMyos at the protein level (Fig. 2d, left panel), but only 2 were also increased at the mRNA level, and 11 were actually decreased at the mRNA level (Fig. 2d, right panel). Pathway analysis of these upregulated proteins showed that the most enriched pathways were those for endosomal sorting complex required for transport (ESCRT), signaling by VEGF, and metabolism of RNA (Fig. 2e). Representative examples of the upregulated proteins and their quantification, including STAM2 (involved in endosomal sorting and signal transduction), VPS25 (a core component of the ESCRT-II complex essential for endosomal trafficking), RBMX (RNA-binding protein implicated in splicing regulation), TNPO1 (nuclear transport receptor mediating protein import into the nucleus), ANKRD28 (scaffolding protein associated with phosphatase complexes and signaling), and TUBA1C (structural component of microtubules critical for cytoskeletal organization), are shown in Fig. 2f. These proteins represent key nodes in pathways related to intracellular trafficking, RNA processing, and cytoskeletal dynamics, all of which were among the top enriched pathways identified in our analysis.

Effects of miRNA mimics on expression of their predicted target genes at the mRNA and protein levels

To more directly assess whether these miRNAs mediated the alterations in target gene/protein expression, we transfected control iMyos with miRNA mimics of the top two upregulated miRNAs (miR-7161-3p and miR-1272) and two downregulated miRNAs (miR-222-5p and miR-30b-3p) and assessed some of their predicted targets involved in signaling or metabolism (Fig. 3a, f). Of the upregulated miRNAs, two (miR-7161-3p, miR-1272) were predicted to target the enzyme METTL14, which is responsible for adenosine N6-methylation, as well as PIK3CA, the p110α catalytic subunit of phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3K). METTL14 has been reported to be a major driver of RNA methylation in T2D,40 and PIK3CA is the dominant isoform of PI3K in muscle and plays a central role in insulin action.41 Indeed, both of these targets were detected in iMyos by MS-based proteomics, and both were decreased in T2D by 20-25% (Fig. 3b). While overexpression of miR-7161-3p in control iMyos resulted in only small, non-significant changes in METTL14 and P110α at the mRNA and protein levels, overexpression of miR-1272 resulted in significant 37-45% reductions in these two targets at the mRNA level and 39-48% at the protein level (Fig. 3c, d), demonstrating the posttranscriptional regulatory role of miRNAs. Indeed, inhibition of miR-7161-3p and miR-1272 rescued the inhibition of METTL14 in iMyos (Fig. 3e). These results were confirmed by luciferase reporter assays, which showed that the 3’UTR reporter expression of METTL14 and PIK3CA was decreased by overexpression of miR-1272 in control iMyos (Supplementary Fig. 5).

Among miRNAs decreased in T2D iMyos, miR-222-5p and miR-30b-3p were predicted to target FOXO1, ERK1/2 and p38 MAPK (Fig. 3f). Transfection of miR-222-5p and miR-30b-3p mimics resulted in significant reductions in the expression of their target proteins, and in this case, as ex vivo, this occurred with no significant changes at the mRNA level (Fig. 3g, h). Conversely, inhibition of miR-222-5p and miR-30b-3p rescued the suppression of FOXO1 in iMyos (Fig. 3i). Taken together, these data indicate that the miRNAs that are upregulated and downregulated in T2D iMyos have a significant impact on the levels of their target proteins acting at the 3’UTR of their mRNAs but that, in most cases, this occurs with little or no change at the mRNA level. This highlights the potential role of these changing miRNAs in the posttranscriptional regulation of their target proteins independent of the regulation of mRNA abundance.

Alterations in secreted miRNAs in sEVs from T2D iMyos

Most tissues secrete miRNAs into the circulation either bound to proteins, such as Argonaute or HDL, or in small extracellular vesicles (sEVs) called exosomes. Alterations in circulating miRNAs have been identified as disease markers15,42 and have also been suggested to play roles as disease mediators, since these secreted miRNAs can be taken up by other tissues and alter their function.42–44 To determine whether T2D altered muscle secretion of miRNAs in this ex vivo setting, we collected secreted sEVs from the culture media of iMyos and purified them using size exclusion chromatography (SEC) (Fig. 4a), as previously described.45,46 Assessment of sEV number and size using nanoparticle tracking analysis (NTA) revealed particles ranging from 50-200 nm in diameter. There was no significant difference in particle concentrations in media from T2D iMyos compared to control cells, but there was a trend toward a ~ 10% reduction in size (Fig. 4b). Immunoelectron microscopic analysis and immunostaining confirmed enrichment of sEVs using classical sEVs/exosomal markers, e.g., CD81, CD9 and CD63 (Fig. 4c).

To determine whether T2D affects the miRNA content of secreted sEVs/exosomes, we performed miRNA profiling on isolated sEVs using the HTG EdgeSeq system. While there was no effect of T2D on sEV number, 2,081 miRNAs detected in sEVs, 366 miRNAs were significantly altered, with 296 decreased and 70 miRNAs increased in sEVs from T2D iMyos compared to controls (Fig. 4d). Thus, compared to cellular content, there were many more miRNAs regulated by T2D in sEVs, pointing to an effect of T2D on either sEV packaging and sorting, indicating the important effects of T2D on miRNA secretion, independent of its effects on cellular miRNAs. Indeed, among the sEV miRNAs altered in T2D, only two miRNAs (miR-7162-5p and miR-4654) were increased in both cells and sEVs, and only three miRNAs (miR-210-3p, miR-193a-3p and miR-152-3p) were decreased in both cells and sEVs in T2D (Fig. 4e, f).

To identify biological processes potentially targeted by sEV-secreted miRNAs from T2D iMyos compared to healthy iMyos, we performed pathway enrichment analysis of the target genes of up- or down-regulated miRNAs (Fig. 4g, h). Interestingly, for the sEV-miRNAs upregulated in T2D, the most enriched target pathways were membrane trafficking, neuronal system, RNA polymerase II transcription, and signaling by receptor tyrosine kinases, including many mRNAs encoding proteins involved in insulin signaling, such as AKT1, AKT2, ATF1, and AP2B1. Other predicted target pathways of the miRNAs increased in sEVs included diseases of signal transduction by growth factor receptors and second messengers and posttranslational protein modification, with specific targets for the first group including CAMK2B, AKT3 and AKAP9, and for the latter including ADAM10, APC, FOXK1, FOXK2, FOXO4, and FSTL1. Pathway enrichment analysis of genes targeted by sEV-miRNAs that were decreased in T2D included pathways involved in transmission across chemical synapses (with target genes including PRKAA1, TUBA3C, MAPK1), neuronal systems (with target genes including NTRK3, ARHGEF7, and ABCC8), and membrane trafficking pathways (with target genes including PUM1, RAB7B, RHOQ, VAMP2, and KIF2C). Since these miRNAs are decreased in sEVs from T2D iMyos, these pathways would be predicted to be upregulated in distal tissues. Interestingly, many of the miRNAs downregulated in secretion from T2D iMyos, including miR-122-5p, miR-148a-3p, miR-192-3p, miR-192-5p, miR-193a-3p, miR-210-3p, and miR-23a-3p (Supplementary Fig. 6a), have been suggested to be involved in the development and/or progression of obesity and T2D,17,20,47,48 and some are decreased in circulating sEVs/exosomes of patients with obesity and T2D.49–51 In addition, many of the decreased exosomal miRNAs in T2D iMyos have been previously reported to be increased in circulation in response to acute exercise, a condition associated with increased insulin sensitivity,52 including miR-126-3p, miR-222-3p, miR-30a-3p, miR-339-3p, and miR-99a-5p (Supplementary Fig. 6b). sEV-miRNAs altered in T2D iMyos are likely to modulate metabolic functions in distal tissues, such as fat, liver and β-cells. To test this hypothesis, we treated human white adipocytes in culture with sEVs derived from healthy and T2D iMyos and performed glucose uptake and mitochondrial function assays. Interestingly, we found that while treatment with sEVs from healthy donor iMyos enhanced glucose uptake and mitochondrial respiration in recipient fat, treatment with sEVs from T2D iMyos donors blunted glucose uptake and decreased mitochondrial respiration in recipient fat (Fig. 4I, j), indicating that T2D-derived sEVs and their miRNAs can modulate metabolic functions in recipient cells.

Identification of nucleotide motifs enriched in sEV-miRNAs dysregulated in T2D

miRNA secretion in exosomes/sEVs is a highly regulated process and differs from cell type to cell type.53–55 Secretion of miRNAs in sEVs has also been shown to be regulated by insulin, at least in adipose tissue and muscle.46,56 To assess potential differences in miRNA secretion patterns in T2D iMyos, we performed an enrichment analysis comparing levels of secreted miRNAs to their cellular content. Using a threshold of a 4-fold higher concentration in sEVs than in cells for secretion preference or vice versa for preference to retention, we found that 23% of miRNAs in control iMyos showed either sEV or cellular enrichment, with 118 miRNAs enriched in sEVs compared to cells and 371 miRNAs enriched in cells compared to sEVs (Fig. 5a–c). Similarly, with T2D iMyos, 176 miRNAs were enriched in sEVs, and 339 miRNAs were enriched in cells (Fig. 5b). This difference in enrichment was accompanied by a modest decrease in the overall abundance of miRNAs in sEVs, while the overall abundance of cell-enriched miRNAs remained unchanged (Fig. 5d).

In previous studies, we46,54 and others57–59 have shown that miRNA loading and secretion in sEVs is not a random process but is highly regulated by factors including miRNA modifications, miRNA interaction with RNA binding proteins, and factors involved in exosome biogenesis. Analysis of the miRNAs selectively secreted or retained in control vs. T2D iMyos revealed five motifs associated with increased secretion of miRNAs in sEVs and seven motifs associated with increased cell retention at a false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.1 (Supplementary Fig. 7a). All of the former were AU-rich and overrepresented in miRNAs whose secretion was increased in T2D. The top AU-rich motif was UAUAAA (with some degeneracy), which was found in 64.3% of miRNAs whose secretion was increased in T2D versus 31.6% of miRNAs in controls, representing ~2-fold enrichment (FDR = 0.001). Another AU-rich motif (FDR = 0.001) was AUAAU (with some degeneracy), which was found in 41.2% of miRNAs whose secretion was increased in T2D versus only 15.4% of miRNAs in the control group. In contrast, seven motifs were identified as being enriched in miRNAs whose retention in cells was increased in T2D (Supplementary Fig. 7a). These varied in length from 5 to 8 nt, and all were G + C or A + C enriched. The most enriched motif among miRNAs retained in cells in T2D was the AGUGCAC motif found in 22% of retained miRNAs versus 6.1% of background miRNAs. Another motif enriched in G + C was GCUGCGU (with some degeneracy), which was found in 12.5% of miRNAs retained in cells in T2D versus 2.3% of background miRNAs.

Pathway analysis of predicted targets of sEV-enriched miRNAs (motifs 1-5) revealed significant overrepresentation of interconnected signaling pathways, including MAPK signaling, PI3K–AKT signaling, receptor tyrosine kinase signaling, neurotrophin signaling, and growth factor receptor-mediated cascades (Supplementary Fig. 7b). In addition, pathways related to membrane trafficking, RAB GTPase exchange factors, clathrin-mediated endocytosis, and posttranslational protein modification were significantly enriched. Thus, these miRNAs sharing common sequence motifs may coordinately target signal transduction and vesicle-associated pathways. While the exact mechanism governing the process of sorting miRNAs for secretion vs. retention needs further study, analysis of mass-spec-based global proteomics and phosphoproteomics in T2D and control iMyos5 revealed several RNA binding proteins (RBPs) that might contribute to this process whose phosphorylation and/or expression was altered in T2D (Fig. 6 and Table 2). Indeed, we observed significant changes in the expression levels of HNRNPA0, HNRNPA3, and RBM3 in iMyos in T2D, and analysis of phosphoproteomic data from control and T2D iMyos5 identified several RBPs whose phosphorylation was altered in T2D iMyos (Table 2). The latter include hnRNPA1, an RBP known to bind to an AU-rich motif (UAAUU), which showed decreased phosphorylation at Ser4 and Ser6 in T2D (p = 0.00558); KHSRP, which showed decreased phosphorylation at Ser181 in T2D (p = 0.0386); and PUM2, which showed decreased phosphorylation at Ser136 in T2D (p = 0.0169). Other RBPs that showed a decrease in phosphorylation in T2D are given in Table 2. Finally, reanalysis of phosphoproteomic data5 revealed the effects of insulin on the phosphorylation of RBPs. Thus, T2D iMyos exhibited reduced phosphorylation of RBMX at S149, HNRNPA1 at S4, and PUM2 at S136, while there was increased phosphorylation of HNRNPAB at S241 and HNRNPU at S59, both under basal conditions and following insulin stimulation (Fig. 6a, b and Table 2). Thus, altered miRNA secretion in T2D may be influenced by both altered expression and altered phosphorylation of RBPs involved in miRNA sorting in sEVs.

Discussion

Muscle is the first site of insulin resistance in T2D pathogenesis.1,2 We have previously shown that myoblasts derived from iPS cells (iMyos) from people with T2D, as well as from people with insulin resistance but without diabetes, exhibit defects in insulin signaling, insulin-stimulated glucose uptake, mitochondrial function, and protein expression in vitro.5,6 While primary myotube cultures from individuals with obesity and T2D also show similar alterations,60–63 iMyos have undergone genetic reprogramming to iPS cells and then redifferentiation to muscle, conditions associated with a loss of most epigenetic marks, such as those created by DNA methylation and histone methylation.8,64,65 Differences in miRNA expression can also serve as epigenetic regulators, both of the myoblast itself or of other cells by miRNA secretion.

In the present study, we investigated how miRNA expression and secretion are altered in iMyos from people with T2D. Using a sensitive nuclease protection assay capable of measuring over 2,000 human miRNAs,45,66 we found significant alterations in 71 miRNAs in T2D at the cellular level, with 22 upregulated and 49 downregulated in T2D iMyos. While T2D and other insulin-resistant states have been shown to be associated with changes in cellular miRNAs in muscle and other tissues in vivo,20,43,46,67–70 to our knowledge, this is the first study to explore whether such changes can persist in vitro. Indeed, miR-206 and miR-133, which are significantly reduced in T2D iMyos in vitro, are known to serve as regulators of insulin resistance and glucose metabolism11,70 and have been reported to be downregulated in skeletal muscle of individuals with T2D or insulin resistance,13,70 indicating that they not only reflect the disease effect but are also cell-intrinsic alterations. Likewise, we also found a significant reduction in miR-194-5p and an increase in miR-135a-5p levels in iMyos in vitro, and similar changes have been observed in skeletal muscle of insulin-resistant rodents and humans with T2D or prediabetes.17,18,21

Meta-analysis of the miRNAs downregulated in T2D iMyos reveals many linked to insulin resistance or metabolic dysregulation. For example, miR-133a and miR-210-3p can target FGFR1, SRF, BDNF, ChREBP and GLUT4, altering insulin sensitivity and glucose uptake.13,32 Likewise, miR-29b-3p can participate in the regulation of insulin-stimulated glucose metabolism and lipid oxidation by targeting GLUT4 and HK2, as well as extracellular matrix and fibrosis (targeting COL4A5 and LAMA2), inflammatory/NF-κB-associated signaling (targeting REL), and lipid metabolism (targeting HMGCR), and miR-200c-3p targets genes involved in the regulation of cell identity/stress signaling (ZEB1), inflammatory signaling (IKBKB), and translational/mTOR-related control (RPS6KB1), all pathways altered in insulin-resistant muscle.23–25,29–31,71 In addition, miR-138-5p targets SIRT1 and contributes to TNFα-induced insulin resistance,28 and SOX4 regulates β islet cell function.27 One unexpected finding in our study was that several miRNAs identified in our study, including miR-5004-3p, miR-196a-3p, miR-335-5p, and miR-23a-5p, exhibit sex-dependent expression patterns, particularly in the setting of T2D. Sex-specific differences in miRNA expression have been reported in other biological contexts and may contribute to sex-dependent differences in disease susceptibility or disease pathogenesis.72–76 In iMyos, miRNAs implicated in the regulation of insulin signaling pathways, e.g., miR-29b-1-5p, miR-138-5p, miR-155-5p, and miR-335-5p, were reduced in males with T2D but not in females with T2D. These miRNAs have been shown to target key components of insulin signaling, including the insulin receptor, IRS proteins, PI3K regulatory subunits, PTEN and PPARγ.23,72,77 Further studies are warranted to confirm these differences, as well as any functional consequences of these sex-specific changes in miRNAs.

Although most of the differentially expressed miRNAs are encoded as isolated transcriptional units, a subset of the miRNAs decreased in T2D iMyos are organized into clusters on chromosomes 7 and 9. Notably, seven of the downregulated miRNAs mapped to two distinct clusters on chromosome 7. Cluster 1, located on chromosome 7q32.3, encodes miR-29a-5p, miR-29a-3p, miR-29b-1-5p and miR-29b-3p. Cluster 2 on chromosome 7q32.2 gives rise to miR-182, miR-96 and miR-183. All seven of these miRNAs are downregulated in T2D iMyos, suggesting that some chromatin-level or epigenetic mechanisms may underlie their coordinated suppression in T2D. At chromosome 9q22.32, there is a cluster that gives rise to miRNAs, including let-7a-5p, let-7f-5p and let-7d-5p, all of which are downregulated in T2D, again suggesting the possibility of transcriptional or epigenetic regulation, potentially contributing to disrupted metabolic and inflammatory signaling characteristics of insulin-resistant muscle. In contrast, other miRNAs on chromosomes 7 and 9, such as miR-7-1-3p, miR-455-5p, and miR-876-3p, arise from distinct genomic loci and are therefore likely subject to independent regulatory control. In addition to alterations at the cellular level, our study reveals even greater differences in miRNAs secreted from iMyos in sEVs in T2D, with 70 sEV-secreted miRNAs upregulated and almost 300 downregulated. This occurs with no differences in DICER or DGCR8 at either the mRNA or protein level, indicating that it is not due to some general alteration in miRNA biogenesis or processing but rather a change in miRNA sorting and/or secretion. Several studies have reported alterations in circulating sEVs/exosomal miRNAs in obesity and T2D and have suggested that this dysregulation contributes to the pathophysiology of obesity/diabetes.43,67,68 Pathway enrichment analysis of target genes of miRNAs that are upregulated in T2D sEVs reveals potential targeting of several metabolic processes in distal tissues, including fatty acid metabolism and signal transduction. Consistent with this, we found that in vitro treatment of recipient fat cells with sEVs from control iMyos enhances glucose uptake and mitochondrial metabolism, while treatment with sEVs from T2D iMyos results in decreased insulin-stimulated glucose uptake and impaired mitochondrial function in recipient adipocytes, supporting the concept that sEVs exert direct functional effects on recipient cells.45 Conversely, pathway analysis of genes targeted by miRNAs whose sEV secretion is decreased in T2D shows that the most enriched target pathways are related to transmission across chemical synapses and membrane trafficking pathways. While further studies are needed, this pathway enrichment analysis of sEV-miRNAs supports the concept that sEV-mediated miRNA transfer may orchestrate a coordinated program of effects, integrating and amplifying the metabolic and functional effects of these miRNAs in distant tissues such as adipocytes.

miRNAs are known to alter their targets at the protein level through two major mechanisms: 1) enhancing degradation of the target mRNA or 2) interfering with translation of the target mRNA. Interestingly, when we examined the mRNA levels of the target genes of the miRNAs altered in iMyos, we found that the overwhelming majority were regulated at the protein level but not at the mRNA level. Thus, 127 of the predicted targets of the miRNAs that were upregulated in T2D were significantly decreased in T2D iMyos at the protein level, and 190 of the predicted targets of the 49 downregulated miRNAs were increased in T2D iMyos, but only two of these were changed coordinately at the mRNA level. This striking finding suggests that the T2D-associated alterations in miRNA expression contribute to changes in their targets at the protein level by regulation of translation rather than by promoting mRNA decay or degradation. Indeed, this could be validated for several of the miRNAs by transfection into control iMyos. Thus, transfection of control cells with miR-7161-3p and miR-1272, which were increased in T2D, and miR-222-5p and 30b-3p, which were decreased in T2D, altered several of their target genes, including METTL14, PIK3CA, FOXO1, ERK1/2 and p38 MAPK, at the protein level, but in most cases, not at the mRNA level. This finding is consistent with regulation at the level of translation efficiency and/or protein stability,78–80 mechanisms known to be governed by miRNAs and RNA-binding proteins. This is also supported by our luciferase reporter assays showing that miR-1272 targets the 3’UTRs of METTL14 and PIK3CA mRNA and regulates METTL14 and PIK3CA posttranscriptionally. Interestingly, there was very limited overlap between the miRNAs that were altered in expression in iMyos and those altered or enriched in the sEVs secreted from the iMyos, indicating that miRNA export is not simply a mirror cellular change. This is consistent with previous studies by us and others showing that secretion involves selective miRNA sorting,46,54,58,81,82 such that some miRNAs are preferentially retained within cells to modulate intracellular pathways, while others are preferentially packaged into sEVs for secretion, where they may play a role in intercellular communication. Mechanisms contributing to preferential release or retention include the presence of sequence motifs within the miRNA and their interaction with RNA-binding proteins and AGO.54,58,82–84 In the context of T2D, this mechanism may also contribute to the redistribution of miRNAs, potentially altering both cellular homeostasis and signaling to recipient tissues. For instance, alterations in the phosphorylation of hnRNPA1 have been shown to affect miRNA retention versus secretion in response to insulin stimulation in adipocytes and muscle cells.46,54 Multiple RBPs, including hnRNP family members, are known to regulate miRNA biogenesis and function. For example, hnRNPA1 regulates gene expression at multiple levels, binding to the terminal loop (near the 3′ side of the stem–loop structure) of pri-miR-18a to enhance Drosha processing and modulating pri-let-7a-1.85–87 Through dynamic changes in expression, posttranslational modification, and subcellular localization, particularly in response to insulin or in the T2D state, these RBPs act in a combinatorial manner to control selective miRNA retention or secretion, thereby fine-tuning intercellular communication and metabolic adaptation. Our research expands this framework to human iMyos, suggesting that signaling changes associated with T2D might influence RBP activity and, consequently, miRNA trafficking within the cell, leading to dysregulation of miRNA secretion in sEVs in T2D.

In summary, using iPS-derived myoblasts to model human muscle, we found that T2D influences both miRNA expression and, independently, miRNA secretion in sEVs. These alterations occur in a cell-intrinsic manner and serve as important epigenetic regulators of protein expression both in muscle and potentially other tissues involved in T2D-related pathogenesis, such as adipose, liver and beta cells. This interaction between miRNA, protein expression, and cellular function adds an additional dimension to the pathogenesis of this disease.

While our study demonstrates that T2D affects miRNA expression and secretion in T2D and suggests that these might contribute to disease pathogenesis, proof of this point will require further in vivo studies to determine how this mechanism interfaces with other factors leading to insulin resistance and T2D. Additionally, direct studies of how phosphorylation and other posttranslational modifications can influence miRNA expression or sEV loading in in vivo models will be important. We also acknowledge the absence of in vivo validation of sEV targets and downstream functional effects, as well as the possibility that patient-derived iPS cells may retain donor-specific epigenetic variation beyond disease-related changes that could contribute to interindividual variability in the observed miRNA and functional phenotypes. Future work should also explore whether interactions between miRNA-processing proteins (e.g., Dicer, AGO2) and RNA-binding proteins contribute to selective and altered miRNA sorting into sEVs in T2D. Addressing these points, as well as studies using genetic ablation and overexpression of these altered miRNAs in vivo and in vitro, will further define how this contributes to T2D pathophysiology.

Materials and methods

Study subjects

All procedures were approved by the ethics committee at the Karolinska Institute. Participants in the study gave their informed consent. Muscle samples from the vastus lateralis were collected from individuals both with and without a history of T2D, as outlined in our earlier research.5,6,8 The biopsy was conducted using local anesthesia (5 mg/mL lidocaine hydrochloride) in chilled PBS, which contained a mix of penicillin (100 U/mL) and streptomycin (100 μg/mL).

Electron microscopy of purified sEVs

Purified sEVs were adsorbed (5 µL/sample) onto glow-discharged carbon-coated grids. Grids were blocked in 1% BSA for 10 min and incubated with primary antibodies against CD63, CD81, or CD9 diluted in 1% BSA. For CD63, anti-human CD63 antibody (BD Pharmingen, 556019; 1:20), CD9, anti-human CD9 antibody (BD Pharmingen, 555370; 1:20), and CD81, anti-human CD81 antibody (BD Pharmingen, 555675; 1:20) were applied for 30 min, followed by PBS washes. Grids were then incubated with rabbit anti-mouse bridging antibody (Abcam, ab6709; 1:50) for 20 min, washed, and labeled with 10-nm protein A-gold (1:50) for 20 min. After PBS and water washes, grids were negatively stained with 0.75% uranyl formate for 30 s. Samples were examined using a JEOL 1200EX transmission electron microscope, and images were recorded with an AMT 2k CCD camera.

miRNA profiling

To assess miRNAs in cells and sEVs, we applied HTG EdgeSeq technology (HTG Molecular Diagnostics, Inc., Tucson, AZ), a next-generation sequencing-based miRNA profiling platform using nuclease protection,88,89 as previously described.45 Briefly, the HTG EdgeSeq miRNA Whole Transcriptome Assay (WTA), which included 2102 total probes, was designed to detect 2083 human miRNAs. To filter out miRNAs with low expression, we kept miRNAs that had counts per million (CPM) greater than 1.19 in at least 4 samples. We then normalized counts by weighted trimmed mean of M-values (TMM).90 The normalization factors were between 0.19 and 21.76.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis and preparation of graphs were performed using GraphPad Prism v8 Software. Data are presented as the means ± SEMs. Bar graphs depict the mean (n = 8 per group), and error bars depict the SEM. Statistical analysis was performed by unpaired, two-tailed t test (∗p < 0.05, ∗∗p < 0.01, ∗∗∗p < 0.001). A detailed description of the data, statistical methodology used, and sample size can be found at the end of the figure legend corresponding to each figure.

Graphene oxide nanoparticles oscillation in drilling fluid for heat transfer with entropy generation over radiative cylinder in fluctuating and laminar regimes
Authors: Nidhal Ben Khedher, Zia Ullah, Wubale Demis Alamirew, Saleh Al Arni, Isam Elbadawi, Ilyas Khan, Ioannis E. Sarris, Mouldi Ben Amara, Mohamed Boujelbene
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The oscillatory motion of graphene oxide-Go nanoparticles in water lubricating Maxwell nanofluid flow for heat and mass transfer enhancement in steady and fluctuating regime is important significance of this study. The aim of this work indicates the temperature distribution, nanoparticle concentration rate and velocity field around stretching radiating-cylinder in drilling systems. The nonlinear radiating energy, entropy generation, mixed convection, buoyancy ratio and oscillatory effects are assumed for heat and mass performance. The partial differential based mathematical expressions are developed to estimate the values of current analysis. The oscillatory stokes conditions, complex variables, and primitive transformations are applied to develop steady and fluctuating results. The computational outputs are secured using very efficient methods like finite difference and Gaussian elimination. The graphical outputs are displayed through TecPlot-360 and FORTRAN. The fluid velocity field, energy, and concentration outputs are executed with the help of various physical factors. The steady friction and steady thermal rate are depicted and are used in transient algorithm to depict the fluctuating friction rate and oscillatory thermal-mass transport. The magnitude of fluid velocity, fluid temperature and fluid concentration enhances as Maxwell parameter is enhanced. The variation in fluid velocity amplitude and fluid temperature increases as radiating energy is enhanced. For high parametric range of Maxwell number, the steady skin friction and mass transfer increases but heat transfer decreases. At smaller Eckert number, the oscillations in transient skin friction, transient heat and mass transfer are enhanced. At higher Maxwell parameter, buoyancy and Schmidt number, the large amplitude in heat and mass oscillations is observed.

Introduction

In previous years, multiple simulations for graphene-oxide (Go) nanoliquid have been developed in order to comprehend the rheological feature and function. The Maxwell approach, which is a rate-type hypothesis, was examined together with the properties of relaxation time. The graphene-oxide (Go) water nanofluid is a topic of discussion among investigators due to its unique use and qualities of thermal conductivity. Non-Newtonian substances are found in the nutritional, biological, radioactive sectors, polymerized fluids, fabricated materials, and biotechnology. When the temperature of an object varies, a forced phenomenon called thermal transfer takes place. The heat transmission has numerous applications in industry and biomedicine and experts have recently been motivated by the phenomena of heat transmission as a waveform. Heat transport is prominent in connective tissues, nuclear plant temperature, refrigeration of electrical equipment and power generators. Under the impact of the reactive chemical conditions, Wang et al. [1] concentrated on multidimensional Maxwell bio-convective nanotechnology fluid moving over an exponentially expanding surface. The diminishing behavior of Nusselt number was examined at higher Deborah number. Friction impacts and stationary region of upper-convected Maxwell liquid through an elastic sheet were examined by Ibrahim and Negera [2]. At higher magnetic parameter, the enhancing temperature and lower velocity field were observed. Ahmed et al. [3] used Cattaneo-Christov model to analyze the thermal properties of a Maxwell nanofluids in a multidimensional buoyancy-driven forces. It was observed that the thermophoretic diffusion enhances the thermal flow rate in assisting flow regime. Using Ellis fluid model, the significance of hybrid nanoparticles in water liquid for thermal conductivity and viscosity enhancement was studied by Mishra and Pathak [4]. It was noted that higher velocity slip and porosity decreases the temperature rate. In reference [5], it was investigated how an upper-convected Maxwell (UCM) liquid above a stretched sheet was affected by MHD movement and transfer of heat inside the flow separation movement. The decreasing wall skin friction rate and thin boundary layer was depicted at higher elastic number. A MHD nanofluid model with Xue method of hybrid nanoparticles and water fluid for enhancement of heat across a stretching surface under chemical effects was predicted by Mishra [6]. At higher Brownian motion and chemical reaction, a remarkable drop in concentration levels was observed. Patil et al. [7] described impermeable stretched plate contained in an impermeable substance with heat radiation and reacting magneto-hydrodynamic Maxwell nanofluid movement. The role of graphene oxide (Go) nanoparticles in water-based fluid for heat and mass transportation was discussed numerically and experimentally by several scientists [8–11].

The graphene-oxide (Go)-water nanofluid has better temperature conductivity than a fundamental liquid with accelerated rate of heat transmission. The thermodynamic properties of basic liquid, particle suspension, movement sequence, circulation order and volumetric portion of suspending nanoparticles affect the convection heat transmission of nanofluids. Due to remarkable increase in thermal efficiency, Go-water nanofluids are now acknowledged and shown to be beneficial in a variety of manufacturing and technological uses. In recent years, researchers have seen significant interests in Go-water nanofluids along stretching cylinder, including power-law liquids, Maxwell and Jeffrey liquids. It has wide range of technological and industrial uses, including the manufacturing of paper, polymeric materials, optical fibers, and metal-based pipes for cooling baths. The temperature and concentrating slips on heat and mass transport properties of a Maxwell nanofluid mixture across a stretching/shrinking interface with chemical interactions, Cattaneo-Christov heat and radiant heat were solved in [12]. The comprehensive simulation of graphene oxide nanoparticles with water fluid for heat transfer performance using radiation, MHD and joule energy effects was explored by Chari et al. [13]. Cao et al. [14] examined the oscillation of Maxwell liquid across a stretched perforated material with heat sink and source with nanomaterial. The thermal and mass transport of a Maxwell nanofluid moving across a heating stretched surface was studied by Arif [15]. The effects of oscillatory MHD on non-Newtonian fluids for heating efficiency rate across wavy channel with MHD and chemical reaction were inspected by Jalili et al. [16]. In the context of solar radioactivity and kinematic dissolution, a constant flow, temperature and mass movement of Maxwell nanofluid under slipping boundaries was examined in [17]. The influence of grephene-oxide Go-water nanofluid over different structures for prominent efficiency and performance of heat transfer was elaborated by various authors [18–20].

The transient movement of heat across an expanding material can be found in the metallurgical, glass-fiber and polymeric sectors. Go-water nanofluids have been produced recently for a variety of applications in industry, medicinal purposes and refrigeration procedures. Glass production, radiators, solar-powered water heat, draining operations, steam collectors, geological energy processing, hydraulically breaks, thermal exchangers, and refrigeration of chip technology are remarkable applications of heat transfer. The liquid movement across an elastic cylinder has multiple uses in metallurgical and thermoplastic technology. In drilling industries, glass container production, metallic wire illustration, metalworking operations, fabric weaving, plastic and rubber fabrication, film finishes, and development of crystals, the stretching surface movements are crucial. The temperature study of a bio-convective movement of Maxwell nanomaterial across expanding and rotating cylindrical shape was reported by Khan et al. [21]. Santhi et al. [22] showed a statistical research on Buongiorno Maxwell nanofluid model for radiation and mass transmission features across an elastic sheet. Using Cattaneo-Christov approach, the transmission of heat in a Maxwell nanoliquid circulation across an elastic sheet was conducted in [23]. Alharbi et al. [24] inspected the hybrid type nanomaterial by taking thermal radiation. Abbas et al. [25] discussed the effect of second-order slip for Maxwell liquid at horizontally hyperbolic stretched plate. Dayo et al. [26] investigated thermal energy, chemical processes, and volume properties of Maxwell nanofluid flow over extending surfaces. Narender et al. [27] focused on the investigation of heat transmission of a convected Maxwell liquid with slipping boundaries, viscoelastic dispersion, and chemical interactions. The bio-convection, slip influence and heat surface condition were applied on Maxwell nanomaterial over stretched sheet and compared the outputs with published data in [25, 28]. The significance of heat and mass transportation in water-based fluid containing graphene-oxide (Go) nanoparticles was explored by several investigators [29, 30].

Conclusions

The present study focused on the periodic, amplitude, wave oscillations and steady form of graphene-oxide (Go) based water nanofluid over stretching radiative-cylinder. The heating efficiency, skinfriction and mass flow level of mixed convection Maxwell Go-water nanoliquid movement is prominent in drilling and hydraulic press cylinder systems. The thermal buoyancy, energy dissipation and nonlinear thermal radiation effects are operated for the enhancement of heating energy and mass flow level. The present mixed convection Maxwell Go-water nanofluid is represented with mathematical expressions under periodical modeling and simulation. The physical parameters are generated through this model using dimensionless quantities. The flow dynamics, thermal properties and mass flow level are depicted using physical parameters in FORTRAN tool. By using these values, the behavior of skinfriction, heating energy and mass flow level is inspected using implicit FDM and Gaussian elimination schemes. The oscillations, periodicity, amplitude and phase angle of skinfriction, heating energy and mass flow rate are elaborated using steady results.

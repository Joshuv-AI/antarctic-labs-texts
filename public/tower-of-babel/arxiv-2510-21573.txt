Integrals of stable envelopes for cotangent bundles to Grassmannians
Matthew Crawford, Pavan Kartik, Reese Lance

Abstract
We consider cohomological stable envelopes for a natural torus action 𝖳 \mathsf{T} on X = T ∗ ​ G ​ r ​ ( k , n ) X=T^{*}Gr(k,n) , introduced by Maulik-Okounkov. We define the ℂ ℏ ∗ \C^{*}_{\hbar} -equivariant integral of the stable envelope using equivariant localization over the subtorus ℂ ℏ ∗ ⊂ 𝖳 \C^{*}_{\hbar}\subset\mathsf{T} , and compute the integral as a non-equivariant limit of the localization over the full torus, 𝖳 \mathsf{T} . The integral of such a class is an integer times a power of ℏ \hbar , and the main result of this paper is a combinatorial formula for these integers. In 3d mirror symmetry, these non-equivariant limits are expected to reflect some curve counting phenomena on the 3d mirror dual, X ∨ X^{\vee} . When k = 1 k=1 , we obtain the binomial coefficients, and we study some of the combinatorics of the integers for higher k k , which haven’t appeared in the literature before. We give some conjectures and interpretations on extending this phenomena to type A quiver and bow varieties.



1
Overview

1.1
Stable Envelopes

Cohomological stable envelopes were introduced by Maulik-Okounkov
[
MO12
]
in order to construct geometric actions of the Yangian algebra on the cohomology of Nakajima quiver varieties. These quiver varieties provide a rich class of symplectic resolutions of singularities, and play a key role in the 3D mirror symmetry conjectures. In this paper we restrict our attention to the most fundamental Nakajima quiver variety, the cotangent bundle to the Grassmannian.

If a torus
\mathsf{T}
acts on a smooth variety,
X
, cohomological stable envelopes are maps of
H_{\mathsf{T}}^{\bullet}(\text{pt})
-modules

\stab_{\mathfrak{C}}:H_{\mathsf{T}}^{\bullet}(X^{\mathsf{T}})\to H_{\mathsf{T}}^{\bullet}(X)

depending on a choice of chamber,
\mathfrak{C}
, which is a connected component of the complement of all root hyperplanes in an associated root system. The stable envelope of a torus fixed point,
p
, is the image of its equivariant cohomology class under this map,
\stab_{\mathfrak{C}}(p)
. According to the theory of equivariant localization via
[
AB84
]
, each
\stab_{\mathfrak{C}}(p)
class is determined by its restrictions to fixed points. Thus we may think of the map
\stab_{\mathfrak{C}}
as being encoded in the matrix of restrictions

(\stab_{\mathfrak{C}})_{ij}=\stab_{\mathfrak{C}}(p_{i})\big|_{p_{j}}

In the enumerative formulation of 3D mirror symmetry, the elliptic stable envelopes conjecturally relate certain enumerative counts on
X
to enumerative counts on its mirror dual,
X^{!}
, see
[
AO21
]
. When setup appropriately this “relation” is via matrix multiplication and simple algebraic changes of variables. Mathematically these counts are given by vertex functions, equivariant curve counts, which are also of physical interest for the corresponding quiver gauge theories.

In this case, the integral is expressed as a sum of rational functions in the parameter

Our study is motivated by the role of stable envelopes in 3d mirror symmetry, and its interaction with vertex functions. Originally, the integrals of stable envelopes as we defined appear as coefficients of expansion of the integral representation of vertex functions for

)

We give an explicit, combinatorial formula

1.4
Combinatorics of integrals

The
n
integers
\int_{X}\Stab(p)
(ignoring the
\hbar
factor) obtained from
T^{*}Gr(1,n)=T^{*}\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
are simply the binomial coefficients, and thus can be arranged into Pascal’s triangle. This is an infinite triangle (2-simplex) where each number in a given layer is the sum of the two numbers in the layer directly above it. We prove that a similar phenomenon occurs for the
{n\choose 2}
integers arising from integrals over
T^{*}Gr(2,n)
if we arrange them in a 3-simplex, where the integers in one layer are determined solely by the “nearby” integers in the layer above. This nice recurrence relation, which we name 4-neighbor addition, does have exceptions, which we can fix in an ad-hoc way. We demonstrate two methods to modify the combinatorial problem so that 4-neighbor addition holds without exception. These combinatorial structures may be shadows of nearby geometric problems.

These results we present for
k=2
demonstrate the complexity of this family of integers. This is especially interesting when they are viewed as a generalization of the binomial coefficients.

We also provide an outline of a new conjectural method to find the
\mathsf{T}
-equivariant integrals of stable envelopes due to A. Varchenko. We introduce the quantities
\mathbb{V}({\lambda})
, for each partition
\lambda
in an
(n-k)\times k
rectangle, which are some sums of weighted paths to the Young diagram
Y_{\lambda}
. The weights are in formal variables
\textbf{a},\hbar
, and we conjecture that
\mathbb{V}(\lambda)
is exactly the
\mathsf{T}
-equivariant integral of the corresponding fixed point.

1.5
Future Directions

Cohomological stable envelopes may be defined naturally for all type A quiver varieties. Though our definition via
\C^{*}_{\hbar}
-equivariant localization no longer makes sense, their integrals can nevertheless be computed by
\mathsf{T}
-equivariant localization. We conjecture their nonequivariant limits also exist and are rational numbers times some power of
\hbar
, supported by computer experiments.

However for bow varieties this limit does not exist, though we do not have a conceptual reason for why that is the case. Such an explanation would be interesting to learn.

One interesting property we observe is the following, (see section
5
for precise statement):

Conjecture

(
5.2
)
.

A bow variety
\mathcal{C}
is Hanany-Witten equivalent to a quiver variety
if and only if the non-equivariant limit of the integral of the stable envelope
\displaystyle\lim_{\textbf{a}\to 0}\int_{X}\Stab(p)
exists for all torus fixed points
p
.

We look at only cohomological stable envelopes here. In the same settings there are also K-theoretic and elliptic stable envelopes and weight functions and vertex functions, and we plan to extend these results to those cases.

The combinatorial interpretations we mention for
k=2

do
have obvious generalizations to higher
k
, but they do not match the integers arising from the integrals. We still seek natural interpretations for higher
k
. These interpretations are of interest since these integers correspond to some “nonabelian” generalizations of the binomial coefficients, and we do not believe these integers have appeared in previous literature.

2.1
Stable Envelopes

We summarize the axiomatic treatment of stable envelopes as in
[
MO12
]
, in our restricted setting. For a more general and detailed discussion, see the cited source.

Let
X:=T^{*}Gr(k,\C^{n})
be the cotangent bundle to the Grassmannian parameterizing
k
-dimensional linear subspaces of
\C^{n}
. Let
\mathsf{A}:=(\C^{*})^{n}
, which has an induced action on
X
by the action on the underlying
\C^{n}
that preserves the symplectic form. The larger torus
\mathsf{T}:=\mathsf{A}\times\C^{*}_{\hbar}
acts on
X
by letting
\hbar
scale the cotangent fibers. The fixed points of the
\mathsf{T}

and are labeled by increasing subsets
I\subset[n]
of size
k
. The fixed point corresponding to the subset
I
is denoted
p_{I}
. We will not distinguish between
p_{I}
as an element of
T^{*}Gr(k,n)
versus
Gr(k,n)
.

The inclusion
i:X^{\mathsf{T}}\to X

\mathsf{T}
-equivariant cohomology:

i^{*}:H_{\mathsf{T}}^{\bullet}(X)\to H_{\mathsf{T}}^{\bullet}(X^{\mathsf{T}})

Stable envelopes are maps in the other direction

\stab_{\mathfrak{C}}:H^{\bullet}_{\mathsf{T}}(X^{\mathsf{T}})\to H^{\bullet}_{\mathsf{T}}(X)

satisfying a collection of axioms, which we must fix some notation to state: We fix the cocharacter
\sigma:\C^{*}\to\mathsf{A}

z\mapsto(z,z^{2},\dots,z^{n})

and the corresponding chamber as
\mathfrak{C}=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
, meaning
a_{1}\leq a_{2}\leq\dots\leq a_{n}
. These choices are constant throughout, so we omit the usual subscripts which indicate a dependence on the chamber. We only consider the standard chamber because any other choice of chamber will simply permute which integral is associated to which fixed point, along with appropriate substitutions in the
a
-variables: We state this proposition here, which is not from the Maulik-Okounkov source, but reserve the proof for Section §
2.4
. Any other chamber,
\mathfrak{C}^{\tau}
, is related to
\mathfrak{C}
by a permutation
\tau\in S_{n}
. A choice of chamber is denoted by a permutation
[\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{n}]
of
1,\ 2,\ \dots,n
, and
[\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{n}]
denotes the chamber in which
a_{\nu_{1}}\leq a_{\nu_{2}}\leq\dots a_{\nu_{n}}
. In this case
[\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{n}]^{\tau}
denotes the chamber
[\tau(\nu_{1}),\dots,\tau(\nu_{n})]
.

Proposition

(Proposition (
2.2.1
))
.

Let
\tau\in S_{n}
. Then

\int_{X}\Stab_{\mathfrak{C}^{\tau}}(p)=\tau\left(\int_{X}\Stab_{\mathfrak{C}}(\tau^{-1}(p))\right)

where
\tau(p)=\{\tau(i)\ |\ i\in p\}
, and the action of
\tau
on the left hand side sends
a_{i}\mapsto a_{\tau(i)}
.

Due to this proposition, we assume the standard chamber for the rest of our calculations. The choice of chamber determines a
\mathsf{T}
-invariant decomposition of the tangent space of any fixed point into attracting and repelling directions:

T_{I}X\cong N_{I}^{+}\oplus N_{I}^{-}

Denote the attracting set of a fixed point
p_{I}\in X^{\mathsf{T}}

\text{Attr}(p_{I})=\big\{x\ \big|\ \lim_{z\to 0}(z,z^{2},\dots,z^{n})\cdot x=p_{I}\big\}

and the full attracting set

\text{Attr}^{f}(p_{I})=\bigsqcup_{p_{J}\prec p_{I}}\text{Attr}(p_{J})

and say that
p_{J}\prec p_{I}
if
p_{J}\in\text{Attr}_{\mathfrak{C}}^{f}(p_{I})
. The transitive closure of this relation defines a partial order on the set of torus fixed points.

Theorem 2.1
.

There exists a unique map of

-modules

,  for any

2.2
Equivariant Localization and Integration

Over

Similarly well-known is the so-called GKM description of the equivariant cohomology due to

2.4
Weight functions

Weight functions are rational functions introduced in
[
TV97
]
to describe certain solutions to quantum Knizhnik-Zamolodchikov equations. Later,
[
RTV12
]
showed that these weight functions may be interpreted as representatives for the stable envelope classes. The rest of this section summarizes the results we need from this paper, and gives the delayed proof from subsection §
2.1
.

The weight functions depend on a cocharacter or chamber, encoded by a permutation
\Sigma
, and we use
\sigma
as the symmetrizing variable (our convention differs slightly from the source).

Definition 2.1
.

The
weight function
for the standard chamber,
W(p_{I})
, for
p_{I}\in X^{\mathsf{T}}
is

W(p_{I}):=\sum\limits_{\sigma\in S_{k}}\dfrac{\prod\limits_{r=1}^{k}\prod\limits_{\alpha=1}^{i_{r}-1}(a_{\alpha}-t_{\sigma(r)})\prod\limits_{\beta=i_{r}+1}^{n}(t_{\sigma(r)}-a_{\beta}+\hbar)}{\prod\limits_{1\leq\ell<m\leq k}(t_{\sigma(\ell)}-t_{\sigma(m)})(t_{\sigma(\ell)}-t_{\sigma(m)}+\hbar)}

and for another chamber
\mathfrak{C}^{\tau}
(in which
\{a_{\tau(1)}\leq a_{\tau(2)}\dots\leq a_{\tau(n)}\}
) is defined as

W_{\mathfrak{C}^{\tau}}(p_{I}):=\tau\left(W\left(p_{\tau^{-1}(I)}\right)\right)

where
\tau:a_{i}\mapsto a_{\tau(i)},\ 1\leq i\leq n
.

These are rational functions in variables
a,t,\hbar
, satisfying the property that, when the
t
variables are evaluated at subsets of the
a
variables corresponding to a fixed point
p_{J}
,
W_{\mathfrak{C}^{\tau}}(p_{I})|_{t_{i}=a_{J_{i}}}
, becomes a polynomial, and further that the collection

\big(W_{\mathfrak{C}^{\tau}}(p_{I})|_{t_{i}=a_{J_{i}}}\big)_{p_{J}\in X^{\mathsf{T}}}

defines a class in
H_{\mathsf{T}}^{*}(X)
, i.e. satisfies the GKM relations corresponding to the moment graph, denoted
[W_{\mathfrak{C}^{\tau}}(p_{I})]
.

That weight functions are representatives of stable envelope classes is captured by the theorem

Theorem 2.2
.

For all fixed points
I\subset[n]
,

[W_{\mathfrak{C}^{\tau}}(p_{I})]=\Stab_{\mathfrak{C}^{\tau}}(p_{I})

where
[W_{\mathfrak{C}^{\tau}}(p_{I})]
denotes the equivariant cohomology class of
W_{\mathfrak{C}^{\tau}}(p_{I})
.

In the source, they have an additional factor
c_{\lambda}
which accounts for different normalization.

In particular, the stable envelope restriction to a fixed point can be computed by evaluating the weight function at the same fixed point,

W_{\Sigma}(p_{I})|_{p_{J}}=\Stab_{\Sigma}(p_{I})|_{p_{J}}

With this theorem, we can prove the Proposition stated in Subsection §
2.1
regarding the effect of choosing a different chamber, and the result is that a different chamber will simply permute which integral is associated to which fixed point, with
\mathsf{A}
-parameter substitutions. In fact this proof shows that these integrals agree at the level of
\mathsf{T}
-equivariant localization, before taking limits.

Proposition 2.2.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{C}
be the standard chamber, and
\tau\in S_{n}
be a permutation representing another chamber,
\mathfrak{C}^{\tau}
. Then

\int_{X}\Stab_{\mathfrak{C}^{\tau}}(p)=\tau\left(\int_{X}\Stab_{\mathfrak{C}}(\tau^{-1}(p))\right)

where
\tau(p)=\{\tau(i)\ |\ i\in p\}
, and the action of
\tau
on the left hand side sends
a_{i}\mapsto a_{\tau(i)}
.

Proof.

For convenience, we swap
p\leftrightarrow\tau(p)
. Recall that

W_{\tau}(\tau(p)):=\tau(W(\tau^{-1}(\tau(p))))=\tau(W(p))

Computing the RHS by
\mathsf{T}
-equivariant localization,

\int_{X}\Stab_{\mathfrak{C}^{\tau}}(\tau(p))=\sum_{p_{J}\in X^{\mathsf{T}}}\frac{W_{\tau}(\tau(p))|_{p_{J}}}{e(T_{p_{J}}X)}

We can write any
k
-subset
\{\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{k}\}
as
\{\tau(\tau^{-1}(\theta_{1})),\dots,\tau(\tau^{-1}(\theta_{k}))\}

So we can index the sum by
\tau(p_{J})
) instead, and the integral can be written as

\sum_{p_{J}\in X^{\mathsf{T}}}\frac{(\tau(W(p)))|_{\tau(p_{J})}}{e(T_{\tau(p_{J})}X)}

The LHS can be written similarly as

\tau\left(\int_{X}\Stab_{\mathfrak{C}}(p)\right)=\tau\left(\sum_{p_{J}\in X^{\mathsf{T}}}\frac{W(p)|_{p_{J}}}{e(T_{p_{J}}X)}\right)

Applying
\tau
to the denominator

\displaystyle\tau(e(T_{p_{J}}X))

\displaystyle=\tau\left(\prod\limits_{m=1}^{k}\prod\limits_{v\notin J}(a_{v}-a_{j_{m}})(a_{j_{m}}-a_{v}+\hbar)\right)

\displaystyle=\prod\limits_{m=1}^{k}\prod\limits_{v\notin J}(a_{\tau(v)}-a_{\tau(j_{m})})(a_{\tau(j_{m})}-a_{\tau(v)}+\hbar)

\displaystyle=e(T_{\tau(p_{J})}X)

Hence the LHS can be written as

\displaystyle\tau\left(\int_{X}\Stab_{\mathfrak{C}}(p)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p_{J}\in X^{\mathsf{T}}}\frac{\tau(W(p)|_{p_{J}})}{e(T_{\tau(p_{J})}X)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p_{J}\in X^{\mathsf{T}}}\frac{(\tau(W(p)))|_{\tau(p_{J})}}{e(T_{\tau(p_{J})}X)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p_{J}\in X^{\mathsf{T}}}\frac{(\tau(W(p)))|_{p_{J}}}{e(T_{p_{J}}X)}

∎

5
Generalizations in Type A

For this section we discuss only type A bow and quiver varieties. The main content of this section is to conjecture about how far this phenomenon can be carried, and will contain no proofs. We find that it does not extend to all bow varieties, but we conjecture that it does extend to all quiver varieties. For this section we assume all stability conditions are generic.

5.1
Bow variety limits do not exist

Because we don’t offer any proofs at this level, we refrain from providing a full background and definition, and instead refer the reader to a source for stable envelopes and weight functions on bow varieties,
[
RS24
]
.

Briefly, (type A) bow varieties are holomorphic symplectic varieties generalizing type A quiver varieties, in the sense that quiver varieties can be identified with a proper subset of bow varieties. Bow varieties are assembled from combinatorial data called brane diagrams in a manner similar to the assembly of quiver varieties from quiver diagrams. They admit a torus action by the torus
\mathsf{T}=(\C^{*})^{|\text{D5-branes}|}\times\C^{*}_{\hbar}\equiv\mathsf{A}\times\C^{*}_{\hbar}
. In the source, they develop the theory of cohomological stable envelopes for an appropriate
\mathsf{T}
-action on these bow varieties.

Our definition of integral of stable envelope as localization over
\C^{*}_{\hbar}
no longer holds, as there is no guarantee that

is compact. However, we can still compute the localization over
\mathsf{T}

(the same formula as always).

We are naturally led to ask if the limits

\hat{J}:=(J\backslash\{y\})\cup\{x\}\in Attr^{f}(I)

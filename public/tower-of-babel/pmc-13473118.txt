Alcohol-related health information on Chinese short-video platforms: a cross-sectional content analysis of Douyin and Bilibili
Authors: Liping Xu, Zeping Cheng, Jianxiang Ma, Binxun Jiang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractShort-video platforms have become important sources of online health information, yet the quality and reliability of alcohol-related health content on these platforms remain unclear. This study aimed to compare the characteristics, informational quality, and audience engagement of alcohol-related health videos on Douyin and Bilibili. This study aimed to evaluate the characteristics and informational quality of alcohol-related health videos on Douyin and Bilibili and to identify factors associated with video quality. A cross-sectional content analysis was conducted on alcohol-related health videos retrieved from Douyin and Bilibili using four Chinese alcohol-related keywords. Videos were evaluated using the Global Quality Scale (GQS), modified DISCERN instrument, JAMA benchmark criteria, PEMAT-A/V, and an alcohol-related content coverage score. Video characteristics, uploader types, and audience engagement metrics were also analyzed. Ordinal logistic regression was performed to identify factors associated with GQS scores. A total of 254 videos were included, including 127 videos from each platform. Compared with Douyin videos, Bilibili videos demonstrated significantly higher informational quality across multiple evaluation tools, including content coverage, DISCERN, PEMAT-U, GQS, and JAMA benchmark scores (all P < 0.05). In contrast, Douyin videos showed significantly higher audience engagement metrics, including likes, shares, comments, and engagement index (all P < 0.001). Science communicators, physicians, and media organizations generally produced videos with higher quality scores than individual users. Engagement indicators were negatively associated with several quality metrics. In ordinal logistic regression analysis, platform type, physician uploader status, and engagement index were independently associated with GQS scores. The informational quality of alcohol-related health videos on Chinese short-video platforms remains variable. Bilibili videos generally demonstrated higher informational quality and reliability, whereas Douyin videos showed stronger audience engagement. Engagement indicators were negatively associated with several quality metrics, suggesting that video popularity does not necessarily correspond to informational quality. Introduction

Alcohol consumption is a major global public health concern and is associated with a wide range of health risks, including liver disease, cardiovascular disorders, mental health problems, and increased mortality1,2. Harmful alcohol use contributes substantially to the global burden of disease and remains a significant challenge for public health systems worldwide3. In addition to medical consequences, alcohol misuse can lead to various social and behavioral problems, such as impaired productivity, family conflicts, and increased risk of accidents4,5. Consequently, improving public awareness and providing accurate information about alcohol-related health risks and treatment options are essential for effective prevention and intervention.

In recent years, social media platforms have become an increasingly important source of health information for the general public6. Short-video platforms in particular have experienced rapid growth due to their accessibility, visual format, and ability to disseminate information quickly. Platforms such as Douyin and Bilibili attract millions of users and have become influential channels for sharing health-related knowledge7. Compared with traditional health communication approaches, short videos can present complex medical information in a more engaging and easily understandable manner. However, the open nature of social media also raises concerns regarding the accuracy and reliability of health information shared on these platforms8.

Several studies have examined the quality of health-related videos on social media platforms and have reported considerable variability in informational quality across different medical topics9,10. Previous research has evaluated health-related videos on social media platforms across various medical and public health topics and has shown that although such videos attract substantial public attention, the scientific quality and reliability of the information presented remain inconsistent11–13. In addition, differences between platforms have been observed in terms of content characteristics, user engagement, and educational value14,15. Despite the growing popularity of short-video platforms, research focusing specifically on alcohol-related health information remains limited.

Therefore, the present study focused on alcohol-related health information videos on Douyin and Bilibili, two of the most widely used short-video platforms in China and major channels through which the public accesses health information online. This study aimed to systematically evaluate alcohol-related health information videos on these platforms. Specifically, this study sought to (1) analyze the basic characteristics of alcohol-related videos, (2) assess the informational quality and reliability of the videos using multiple standardized evaluation tools, and (3) explore factors associated with video quality, including uploader characteristics and audience engagement metrics. By examining these aspects, this study aimed to provide insights into the current landscape of alcohol-related health information on short-video platforms and to inform strategies for improving the quality of online health communication.

Methods

Study design

This study was designed as a cross-sectional content analysis of alcohol-related health information videos on two major Chinese short-video platforms, Douyin and Bilibili. The study aimed to compare video characteristics, audience engagement, and informational quality between the two platforms.

The study protocol was developed based on methodologies commonly used in previous evaluations of health-related information on social media platforms. Only publicly accessible videos were analyzed, and no interaction with uploaders or viewers was involved.

Video search strategy and data collection

Alcohol-related health information videos were retrieved from Douyin and Bilibili using four Chinese keywords related to alcohol use and alcohol cessation: “酒精成瘾” (alcohol addiction), “酒精使用障碍” (alcohol use disorder), “戒酒” (quitting drinking), and “自己怎么戒酒” (how to quit drinking by oneself). The four Chinese keywords were selected to reflect commonly used alcohol-related search expressions on Chinese social media platforms and to capture both alcohol dependence–related and alcohol cessation–related content. The keyword strategy incorporated clinically relevant terminology as well as colloquial expressions commonly used by the general public when seeking alcohol-related health information online.

The video search was conducted on February 7, 2026. To minimize the influence of personalized recommendation algorithms, all searches were performed using newly registered accounts with no browsing history, watch history, or prior interactions. Searches were conducted under identical device and network conditions, and no search filters or sorting modifications were applied. The default ranking order generated by each platform search engine was retained to simulate typical user search behavior. Retrieved videos and corresponding metadata were archived at the time of screening for subsequent evaluation and verification.

Each keyword was entered separately into the search bar of each platform. For every keyword, the first 100 videos returned by the platform search algorithm were collected from Douyin and Bilibili, respectively. Similar sampling strategies have been widely adopted in previous studies evaluating health-related information on social media platforms because users generally focus on top-ranked search results when seeking online health information. Previous social media content analyses commonly adopted the first 50–100 search results to reflect realistic user viewing behaviors and ensure feasibility of manual evaluation16,17.

In total, 800 videos were initially retrieved, including 400 videos from Douyin and 400 videos from Bilibili.

For each included video, the following information was extracted: video title; video URL; uploader identity; verification status; upload date; video duration; number of likes; number of comments; number of favorites; number of shares; number of followers of the uploader.

Classification of uploaders and video formats

Uploader identities were categorized into five groups: physicians; hospitals or medical institutions; science communicators; individual users; media organizations. Physicians referred to licensed medical professionals or healthcare practitioners. Hospitals or medical institutions included official healthcare organizations and hospital-affiliated accounts. Science communicators referred to creators primarily engaged in public science education. Individual users referred to uploaders without identifiable professional medical backgrounds. Media organizations included officially certified news or media accounts.

Uploader verification status was categorized as verified or non-verified according to platform certification labels.

Video presentation formats were classified into three categories: live explanation; slides or animation; mixed-format videos. Mixed-format videos referred to videos combining multiple presentation styles, such as live explanation with slides, subtitles, animation, or screen recordings.

Video quality assessment

Prior to formal evaluation, both reviewers received training on the scoring criteria. A randomly selected pilot sample consisting of 20% of included videos was independently evaluated by both reviewers to assess inter-rater reliability. After satisfactory agreement was achieved, all included videos were independently evaluated by the two reviewers. Discrepancies were resolved through discussion, and a third reviewer was consulted when necessary.

Inter-rater reliability was evaluated using intraclass correlation coefficients (ICC; two-way random-effects model with absolute agreement).

Each item was scored on a 5-point Likert scale ranging from 1 to 5, resulting in a total score ranging from 5 to 25. Higher scores indicated greater informational reliability and quality. Detailed scoring criteria are provided in Supplementary Table S1.

JAMA benchmark criteria

The Journal of the American Medical Association (JAMA) benchmark criteria were used to evaluate transparency and credibility. Four domains were assessed: authorship; attribution; disclosure; currency. Each domain received 1 point if adequately reported, resulting in a total score ranging from 0 to 4.

PEMAT-A/V

The Patient Education Materials Assessment Tool for Audiovisual Materials (PEMAT-A/V) was used to evaluate understandability and actionability of the included videos. Because several original PEMAT-A/V items were not applicable to short-video content, an adapted version of the PEMAT-A/V was used in this study. The adapted instrument included 12 understandability items and 4 actionability items that were applicable to audiovisual short-video materials.

PEMAT-Understandability (PEMAT-U) assessed whether the material was easy to understand and process for the general public, whereas PEMAT-Actionability (PEMAT-A) evaluated whether viewers could identify clear actionable behaviors based on the presented information.

Each item was scored as 1 (agree) or 0 (disagree). Summed PEMAT-U scores ranged from 0 to 12, and summed PEMAT-A scores ranged from 0 to 4, with higher scores indicating better understandability and actionability.

Complications or adverse social, psychological, or medical consequences of alcohol misuse.

Each adequately addressed domain received 1 point, resulting in a total content coverage score ranging from 0 to 8. Higher scores indicated more comprehensive alcohol-related educational content. A domain was considered “adequately addressed” when the video provided at least one meaningful and medically relevant explanation, description, or educational statement related to that domain with sufficient informational context for viewer understanding. Brief mentions without explanatory content were not considered sufficient for scoring.

For example, videos describing alcohol withdrawal manifestations such as tremor, anxiety, insomnia, or seizures received 1 point for the withdrawal-related domain, whereas videos discussing alcohol-related liver injury, cognitive impairment, or cardiovascular complications received 1 point for the health risk domain (see Supplementary Table S3).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics version 27.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Continuous variables were expressed as median and interquartile range (IQR), whereas categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages. Because engagement-related variables demonstrated non-normal distributions, non-parametric statistical tests were used. Comparisons between Douyin and Bilibili were performed using the Mann–Whitney U test for continuous variables and the chi-square test for categorical variables. For chi-square tests, effect sizes were estimated using Cramér’s V. Differences among uploader categories were evaluated using the Kruskal–Wallis test. Because the analyses were primarily exploratory, post hoc pairwise comparisons were not routinely performed. Effect sizes were additionally reported where appropriate. For Mann–Whitney U tests, the effect size r was calculated. Effect size r values of 0.1, 0.3, and 0.5 were interpreted as small, medium, and large effects, respectively. For Kruskal–Wallis analyses, eta-squared (η2) values were reported where appropriate. Spearman correlation analysis was conducted to evaluate associations between video characteristics and informational quality scores.

Ordinal logistic regression analysis was performed to identify factors independently associated with higher GQS scores because GQS is an ordered 5-point categorical variable. Variables were selected based on theoretical relevance and findings from previous social media quality studies rather than solely on univariable statistical significance. Variables included in the multivariable model were platform type, video duration, log-transformed engagement index, physician uploader status, and verification status. Continuous variables were entered into the model as continuous predictors. Binary variables were coded as follows: platform: Douyin = 0, Bilibili = 1; physician uploader: no = 0, yes = 1; verified account: no = 0, yes = 1. Odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were reported. Variance inflation factors (VIFs) were used to assess multicollinearity, and VIF values below 5 were considered indicative of acceptable collinearity. The proportional odds assumption was assessed using the test of parallel lines. Engagement-related variables with markedly skewed distributions were log-transformed using log10(x + 1) prior to regression analysis. No data points were excluded as outliers because the observed distributions were considered representative of real-world social media engagement patterns.

No missing data were observed for the analyzed variables. All statistical tests were two-tailed, and P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Video selection

A total of 800 videos were initially retrieved from Douyin and Bilibili using four alcohol-related keywords (400 from each platform). After removing duplicate records identified by video IDs, titles, and links, 385 videos remained for screening (206 from Douyin and 179 from Bilibili).

During the relevance screening stage, 52 videos were excluded because they were unrelated to alcohol-related health information, classified as entertainment content, advertisements, or presented in a non-Chinese language. This resulted in 333 videos being assessed for eligibility (183 from Douyin and 150 from Bilibili).

During the eligibility assessment, 79 videos were further excluded, including 11 videos lacking substantial health information, 45 videos shorter than 30 s, and 23 videos without audio or subtitles necessary for evaluation. Videos were considered to contain substantial alcohol-related health information if they addressed at least one predefined educational domain included in the content coverage scoring system with sufficient explanatory information for viewer understanding.

Finally, 254 videos were included in the analysis, consisting of 135 videos from Douyin and 119 videos from Bilibili (Fig. 1).

Inter-rater reliability

Inter-rater reliability analysis demonstrated acceptable-to-excellent agreement between the two reviewers across all assessment instruments. The intraclass correlation coefficients (ICCs) were 0.663 for GQS, 0.976 for DISCERN, 0.663 for JAMA, 0.845 for PEMAT-U, 0.789 for PEMAT-A, and 0.878 for the content coverage score (Supplementary Table S2).

Discussion

This study systematically evaluated the characteristics and informational quality of alcohol-related health videos on Douyin and Bilibili. Overall, Bilibili videos demonstrated significantly higher informational quality across multiple evaluation tools, including content coverage, DISCERN, PEMAT-U, GQS, and JAMA benchmark scores, whereas Douyin videos generated significantly higher audience engagement. In addition, engagement indicators were negatively associated with several quality metrics. Taken together, these findings may reflect differences in platform-specific communication environments, with Bilibili videos tending to provide more structured health information and Douyin videos showing stronger audience engagement and dissemination patterns within the sampled videos.

The findings of this study are broadly consistent with previous research evaluating health-related information on social media platforms17,19–21. Previous studies have reported substantial variability in the quality and reliability of medical information on short-video platforms, as well as platform-specific differences in informational depth and educational value22–25. Similar to prior studies, our results also demonstrated that higher audience engagement does not necessarily correspond to higher informational quality12,21. These findings suggest that while short-video platforms have considerable potential for health communication, content popularity may not reliably reflect scientific quality15,26.

Influence of uploader type

Another important finding concerns the influence of uploader type on video quality. Science communicators, physicians, and media organizations generally achieved higher DISCERN, GQS, and JAMA scores than individual users, consistent with previous studies showing that professionally produced content is often associated with greater informational reliability27. In the multivariable model, physician-uploaded videos were associated with slightly higher GQS scores. One possible explanation is that physician-generated content may emphasize medical accuracy and technical detail, whereas science communicators and media creators may place greater emphasis on accessibility, visual presentation, and simplified explanations28. These findings highlight the importance of balancing informational accuracy with effective communication strategies in online health education.

Engagement–quality paradox

Another notable finding was the negative association between audience engagement and informational quality. Engagement indicators such as engagement index and daily engagement were negatively correlated with several quality metrics, suggesting that videos receiving higher interaction are not necessarily those providing the most comprehensive or reliable health information. Similar findings have been reported in previous studies of health communication on social media platforms29–31.

One possible explanation may relate to platform recommendation mechanisms and audience consumption behaviors32,33. Short-video platforms often prioritize content that maximizes watch time, emotional engagement, and interaction frequency. As a result, concise, emotionally appealing, or entertainment-oriented videos may receive greater visibility and engagement regardless of informational quality. In contrast, videos containing more comprehensive medical explanations may be longer and more cognitively demanding for viewers.

In addition, differences in platform ecology may further contribute to the observed platform-level variations. Douyin emphasizes rapid content dissemination and short-form entertainment-oriented consumption, whereas Bilibili is more commonly associated with longer-form educational and knowledge-sharing content. These ecosystem differences may partly explain why Douyin videos achieved higher engagement metrics while Bilibili videos demonstrated higher informational quality scores.

Nevertheless, the present study did not directly evaluate algorithmic recommendation processes, linguistic characteristics, emotional framing, or audiovisual presentation features. Future studies incorporating computational content analysis, natural language processing, or longitudinal recommendation tracking may help clarify the mechanisms underlying the relationship between engagement and informational quality. Therefore, these interpretations should be considered hypothesis-generating rather than causal conclusions.

In addition to quantitative engagement metrics, user comments may provide valuable insights into audience perceptions, emotional responses, and trust toward alcohol-related health information on short-video platforms34–36. Future studies could incorporate topic modeling, sentiment analysis, stance analysis, or large language model (LLM)-based approaches to further examine audience discourse and platform-specific communication patterns.

Alcohol-related health communication may present unique challenges compared with many other medical topics commonly evaluated on social media platforms. Alcohol-related information frequently involves behavioral addiction, relapse prevention, self-management, stigma, and misinformation regarding alcohol withdrawal or rapid “cure” strategies37,38. In addition, individuals with alcohol-related problems may preferentially seek anonymous online information rather than professional medical consultation39. Therefore, evaluating alcohol-related health information on short-video platforms may provide broader insights into addiction-related communication and online health behaviors40.

Implications for health communication

The findings of this study have several implications for online health communication. First, the observed variability in informational quality suggests that the reliability of alcohol-related health information on short-video platforms remains inconsistent41. Second, healthcare professionals may benefit from adopting more accessible and audience-oriented communication strategies, such as visual explanations, storytelling approaches, or collaboration with science communicators42. Finally, improving digital health literacy may help users critically evaluate the credibility of online health information rather than relying primarily on popularity metrics.

Strengths and limitations

This study has several strengths. It provides a comprehensive evaluation of alcohol-related health videos on two major Chinese short-video platforms using multiple standardized assessment tools. In addition, both video characteristics and audience engagement indicators were analyzed, allowing a multidimensional exploration of factors associated with informational quality.

However, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the study was cross-sectional and reflects the status of videos available at the time of data collection. Given the dynamic nature of social media platforms, search rankings and video availability may change over time. Second, although several commonly used keywords were employed to identify relevant videos, it is possible that some relevant content was not captured. Furthermore, overlap among search results across different keywords may have reduced the diversity of included videos despite duplicate removal procedures. In addition, the study relied on the top-ranked search results generated by platform search algorithms, which may introduce algorithmic ranking bias and limit the representativeness of the included videos. Although this approach was intended to simulate typical user search behavior and reduce ecological bias, the retrieved content may still have been influenced by platform-specific recommendation mechanisms. In addition, the exclusion of videos shorter than 30 s may have reduced the representation of brief “quick tip” or awareness-oriented content, particularly on Douyin, where ultra-short videos are common. As a result, the findings may be more representative of videos containing sufficient informational content for formal quality evaluation rather than the full spectrum of short-form platform content. Future studies could improve external validity by incorporating random sampling, time-stratified sampling, or repeated longitudinal sampling strategies. Third, video quality assessment relied on standardized scoring tools and reviewer judgment, which may introduce a degree of subjectivity despite the use of independent evaluations. Finally, this study focused only on two Chinese short-video platforms, and therefore the findings may not be directly generalizable to other social media environments.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the informational quality of alcohol-related health videos on Chinese short-video platforms remains variable. Bilibili videos generally demonstrated higher informational quality and reliability, whereas Douyin videos showed stronger audience engagement. Engagement indicators were negatively associated with several quality metrics, suggesting that video popularity does not necessarily correspond to informational quality. These findings may help inform future research and health communication strategies aimed at improving the quality and credibility of alcohol-related health information on short-video platforms.

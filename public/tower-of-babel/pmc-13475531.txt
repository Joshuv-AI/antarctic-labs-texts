Development and Validation of a Novel Composite Indicator SIRI_HDL_ALB for Prognostic Prediction in Patients with Distal Cholangiocarcinoma After Radical Resection
Authors: Xiaoyong Ye, Yang Qian, Hanxuan Wang, Qiang He, Ren Lang, Zhili Ji
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundDistal cholangiocarcinoma (dCCA) carries a poor prognosis even after radical resection. Systemic inflammation, lipid metabolism, and nutritional status are all involved in tumor progression, but a biomarker integrating these three dimensions is lacking. This study aimed to develop and validate a novel composite indicator integrating the systemic inflammatory response index, high-density lipoprotein cholesterol, and albumin (SIRI_HDL_ALB), and evaluate its prognostic value in dCCA patients after pancreaticoduodenectomy.Materials and Methods230 dCCA patients who underwent radical resection (2011–2024) were retrospectively enrolled. SIRI_HDL_ALB was calculated as (neutrophils × monocytes/lymphocytes)/(HDL cholesterol × albumin). Time‑dependent ROC, Kaplan–Meier, Cox regression, restricted cubic spline (RCS), competing risk analysis, and subgroup analyses were performed. A nomogram incorporating SIRI_HDL_ALB was constructed and internally validated by bootstrap (1000 iterations).ResultsFor descriptive baseline comparisons and Kaplan–Meier visualization only, an exploratory 36-month OS-derived cut-off value of 0.143 was applied, resulting in a low-score group (n=28) and a high-score group (n=202). The high-score group had significantly worse OS and RFS than the low-score group (both log-rank P<0.01). In all subsequent Cox regression, restricted cubic spline, nomogram construction, and model-performance analyses, SIRI_HDL_ALB was analyzed as a continuous variable. Higher SIRI_HDL_ALB remained independently associated with OS (HR=1.858, 95% CI: 1.095–3.155, P=0.022) and RFS (HR=1.700, 95% CI: 1.014–2.849, P=0.044). Adding SIRI_HDL_ALB to the clinicopathological model provided modest incremental discrimination, with corrected C-index values of 0.720 for OS and 0.737 for RFS.ConclusionSIRI_HDL_ALB is an independent prognostic factor for OS and RFS in dCCA patients after radical resection. By integrating inflammation, lipid metabolism, and nutrition, it adds incremental value to traditional clinicopathological factors.

Distal cholangiocarcinoma (dCCA) is a rare but aggressive biliary tract malignancy arising from the distal common bile duct, proximal to the ampulla of Vater, and represents approximately 20–30% of all cholangiocarcinomas.1 Because of its deep anatomical location and subtle early manifestations, most patients are diagnosed at a locally advanced stage or with lymph node involvement.2 Radical pancreaticoduodenectomy remains the only potentially curative treatment for dCCA. Nevertheless, even after R0 resection, the 5-year overall survival rate ranges from 20% to 40%, and recurrence rates reach as high as 50%–70%.3,4 Accordingly, accurately identifying high-risk patients and refining individualized therapeutic strategies are essential for improving outcomes in dCCA.

Previous studies have confirmed that the prognosis of dCCA is influenced by various clinical and pathological factors, including TNM stage, lymph node metastasis, nerve invasion, margin status, tumor differentiation, and postoperative adjuvant chemotherapy.5 These traditional indicators, however, primarily reflect the local burden of tumor invasion and fail to fully capture how the host’s overall condition modulates tumor progression and treatment response. In recent years, multiple composite scores derived from peripheral blood markers—such as the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR),6 platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR),7 systemic immune inflammation index (SII),8 and systemic inflammatory response index (SIRI)9—have been established as independent prognostic factors for various solid tumors. High-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL) not only participates in reverse cholesterol transport but also exerts anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and immunomodulatory effects; reduced HDL levels are associated with poor prognosis in multiple cancers.10 Similarly, low albumin (ALB) levels, a classic marker of nutrition and inflammation, predict poorer tumor outcomes.11

There is a complex interplay among inflammation, lipid metabolism, and nutritional status in tumor progression. However, most existing blood-based prognostic indicators evaluate these domains separately. In the present study, we constructed a new composite indicator integrating the systemic inflammatory response index, high-density lipoprotein cholesterol, and albumin, termed SIRI_HDL_ALB. SIRI reflects systemic inflammatory burden and immune imbalance, HDL cholesterol reflects lipid metabolic and anti-inflammatory status, and albumin reflects nutritional status and hepatic synthetic reserve. Therefore, SIRI_HDL_ALB was designed as the ratio of systemic inflammatory burden to lipid-nutritional reserve, aiming to capture an integrated host-state phenotype in patients with dCCA.

Our research group has previously developed prognostic models for dCCA using the same institutional database, including a FAR-SII/CA19-9 combined model12 and a study evaluating preoperative SIRI alone.13 The present study differs from these reports because the previous models mainly focused on coagulation-inflammation, tumor-marker burden, or systemic inflammation alone, whereas SIRI_HDL_ALB integrates systemic inflammation, lipid metabolism, and nutritional/hepatic synthetic reserve into a single interpretable index. Thus, this study aimed to evaluate whether SIRI_HDL_ALB could provide complementary prognostic information beyond established clinicopathological predictors and conventional blood-based indicators, and to construct a nomogram for postoperative risk stratification in patients with dCCA after radical resection.

Method

Patient Selection

This study retrospectively included patients with distal cholangiocarcinoma (dCCA) who underwent radical surgery at Beijing Chaoyang Hospital, Capital Medical University between January 2011 and December 2024. Inclusion criteria were: (1) postoperative pathology confirmed dCCA; (2) radical pancreaticoduodenectomy was performed; (3) complete clinical and follow-up data were available. Exclusion criteria were: (1) neoadjuvant therapy before surgery; (2) presence of other malignancies; (3) death during the perioperative period. A total of 230 patients were ultimately included. Approval was obtained from the hospital ethics committee (approval number: 2024-D.-511), and informed consent was waived given the retrospective nature of the study. The study flow diagram is shown in Figure S5.

Data Collection and Follow-Up

The following baseline data were extracted from the electronic medical record system: age, sex, smoking history, history of diabetes, preoperative laboratory indicators (albumin, total bilirubin, CA19-9, CEA, HDL cholesterol, neutrophils, lymphocytes, monocytes, platelets, etc), surgery-related data (operation time, blood loss), postoperative pathological report (T stage, lymph node metastasis, nerve invasion, portal vein invasion, degree of differentiation, margin status), postoperative complications, and adjuvant chemotherapy. The preoperative laboratory test results are defined as the data obtained within a week prior to the surgery date. In patients who underwent preoperative biliary drainage, the closest available preoperative values before surgery were used; therefore, the calculated SIRI_HDL_ALB reflected the immediate preoperative host status rather than the initial status at diagnosis. Based on available preoperative medication records, no included patient had documented use of lipid-lowering medications before surgery; therefore, lipid-lowering therapy was not included as a covariate in the multivariable models. The composite indicator SIRI_HDL_ALB was defined as the systemic inflammatory response index (SIRI = neutrophils x monocytes/lymphocytes) divided by the product of high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL) and albumin (ALB), the following formula was constructed: SIRI_HDL_ALB = Neutrophils (x109/L) x Monocytes (x109/L)/Lymphocytes (x109/L)/HDL cholesterol (mmol/L)/Albumin (g/L). No data-driven weighting was assigned to the individual components in order to preserve simplicity and clinical interpretability and to reduce the risk of overfitting in this single-center retrospective cohort. SIRI_HDL_ALB was primarily analyzed as a continuous variable. The OS-derived cut-off value of 0.143 was obtained from the 36-month time-dependent ROC analysis using the Youden index and was used only for Kaplan–Meier visualization and descriptive risk stratification.

The primary outcome of this study was overall survival (OS), defined as the interval from surgery to death from any cause or the last follow-up. The secondary outcome was recurrence-free survival (RFS), defined as the time from surgery to the first imaging-confirmed recurrence (local or distant); patients who died without recurrence were censored. In the competing risk analysis, recurrence served as the primary event, while non-recurrence-related deaths were treated as competing events. All included patients underwent regular follow-up via telephone and outpatient visits. Follow-up began immediately after discharge, with visits scheduled at 1 month and 3 months post-surgery. Thereafter, follow-up occurred every 3 months until 2 years after surgery. After 2 years, the follow-up interval was extended to every 6 months. The primary endpoint was postoperative death, and the secondary endpoint was tumor recurrence. At each visit, data were collected on blood tests, imaging findings, subsequent treatments, tumor recurrence, and overall survival.

Statistical Analysis

Continuous variables are expressed as mean ± standard deviation or median (interquartile range); categorical variables are presented as frequencies (percentages). For baseline comparisons and Kaplan–Meier visualization, patients were grouped according to the exploratory SIRI_HDL_ALB cut-off value. For Cox regression, restricted cubic spline analysis, nomogram construction, and model-performance evaluation, SIRI_HDL_ALB was analyzed as a continuous variable. Group comparisons were conducted using independent sample t-tests, Mann–Whitney U-tests, or χ2-tests. Survival analysis was performed with the Kaplan–Meier method to generate survival curves, and Log rank tests were used to compare overall survival (OS) and recurrence-free survival (RFS) between groups. Univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards models assessed the impact of SIRI_HDL_ALB and other potential prognostic factors on OS and RFS. The multivariate model incorporated variables with P < 0.05 from the univariate analysis along with clinically recognized prognostic factors. Results are reported as hazard ratios (HR) with 95% confidence intervals (CI). Restricted cubic splines (RCS) were applied in the Cox model to examine the dose–response relationship between SIRI_HDL_ALB as a continuous variable and OS risk, with non-linearity tested (P for non-linearity). A nomogram integrating SIRI_HDL_ALB and other independent risk factors was constructed to predict 1-, 3-, and 5-year OS and RFS probabilities; model performance was evaluated as follows: discrimination was assessed using time-dependent ROC area under the curve (AUC) and Harrell’s C-index, calibration was examined via 1000 bootstrap resamples to generate calibration curves, and clinical utility was quantified using decision curve analysis (DCA) for net benefit across risk thresholds, with the clinical impact curve (CIC) displaying the number of patients classified as high risk and the corresponding number of actual events per 1000 individuals. All internal validations employed bootstrap resampling (1000 iterations) for correction, and the corrected C-index with its 95% percentile confidence interval is reported.

The clinical model was defined as the reference clinicopathological model without SIRI_HDL_ALB. The full model was constructed by adding SIRI_HDL_ALB to the clinical model. For OS, the clinical model included lymph node metastasis, portal vein invasion, tumor infiltration, CA19-9, perineural invasion, and tumor differentiation. For RFS, the clinical model included lymph node metastasis, portal vein invasion, tumor infiltration, perineural invasion, and tumor differentiation.

Model stability was illustrated with a bootstrap C-index distribution histogram. In key clinical subgroups (age, sex, CA19-9, CEA, lymph node metastasis, nerve invasion, T stage, portal vein invasion, margin status, postoperative chemotherapy, etc), the prognostic effect of SIRI_HDL_ALB was evaluated using the Cox model, with interaction P values (P for interaction) calculated and results displayed in a forest plot. Given that non-recurrence death may confound recurrence observation, the cumulative incidence function (CIF) was used to estimate recurrence risk, and Gray’s test compared the recurrence cumulative incidence between high- and low-score groups, with non-recurrence death defined as the competing event.

All statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.3.1), with key packages including survival, timeROC, rms, rmda, cmprsk, jstable and forestploter. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. This retrospective cohort study using routinely collected electronic medical record data was reported in accordance with the Reporting of Studies Conducted Using Observational Routinely Collected Health Data (RECORD) statement.

Result

The Baseline Characteristics of the Patient

A total of 230 patients who underwent surgery for distal cholangiocarcinoma were included in this study. Based on the SIRI_HDL_ALB cutoff value (Table 1), they were divided into the low group (n=28) and the high group (n=202). No significant differences in clinical or pathological characteristics—such as gender, age, smoking, diabetes, preoperative jaundice treatment, operation time, intraoperative blood loss, tumor invasion depth, lymph node metastasis, portal vein invasion, resection margin status, or postoperative complications—were observed between the two groups (P > 0.05). However, patients in the low SIRI_HDL_ALB group had higher preoperative albumin (38.6 vs 35.6 g/L, P=0.001) and HDL levels (1.15 vs 0.6 mmol/L, P<0.001), lower neutrophil and monocyte counts, but higher lymphocyte counts (all P<0.001), and a lower proportion of elevated CA19-9 (39.3% vs 69.8%, P=0.003). The incidence of nerve invasion (75.0% vs 92.1%, P=0.012) and poorly differentiated tumors (7.1% vs 37.1%, P=0.003) was also significantly lower in the low SIRI_HDL_ALB group. The Prognostic Discrimination Ability of SIRI_HDL_ALB

The time-dependent ROC curve shows that the AUCs of SIRI_HDL_ALB for predicting 1, 3, and 5-year OS were 0.631, 0.586, and 0.59 (Figure 1A), respectively; and the AUCs for predicting 1, 3, and 5-year RFS were 0.614, 0.564, and 0.578 (Figure 1B). Kaplan–Meier survival analysis indicated that the OS (P < 0.001) and RFS (P = 0.002) of the high SIRI_HDL_ALB group were significantly worse than those of the low-value group (Figure 1C and D). In the single-marker comparison, SIRI_HDL_ALB showed consistent prognostic associations with both OS and RFS. Each 1-SD increase in SIRI_HDL_ALB was associated with increased risks of death (HR=1.282, 95% CI: 1.119–1.468, P<0.001) and recurrence (HR=1.234, 95% CI: 1.071–1.422, P=0.004). Restricted Cubic Spline Analysis

To explore the dose-response relationship between SIRI_HDL_ALB as a continuous variable and the risk of death, restricted cubic spline (RCS) analysis was applied in the Cox model (Figure 1E and F). The results indicated that SIRI_HDL_ALB was not non-linearly associated with OS risk (P for non-linearity = 0.092) or with RFS risk (P for non-linearity = 0.163).

In the univariate analysis, smoking, SIRI_HDL_ALB, elevated CA19-9, decreased CEA, tumor infiltration depth > 12 mm, low differentiation, nerve invasion, portal vein invasion, lymph node metastasis, and postoperative chemotherapy were significantly associated with RFS (all P < 0.05, Table 3). Construction and Evaluation of Nomogram Charts

Based on the results of multivariate Cox regression, a nomogram was constructed to predict 1-, 3-, and 5-year overall survival (OS) and recurrence-free survival (RFS) (Figure 2A and B). Time-dependent ROC analysis revealed that, for both OS and RFS, the area under the curve (AUC) of the full model (including SIRI_HDL_ALB) was marginally higher than that of the clinical model (defined as the reference clinicopathological model without SIRI_HDL_ALB) at all evaluated time points. For OS, the AUC of the full model at 1, 2, 3, 4, and 5 years was 0.745, 0.786, 0.773, 0.814, and 0.779, respectively, compared to 0.734, 0.782, 0.772, 0.813, and 0.771 for the clinical model. For RFS, the corresponding AUC values were 0.785, 0.795, 0.759, 0.822, and 0.797 for the full model, versus 0.777, 0.788, 0.755, 0.812, and 0.784 for the clinical model. The AUC for both models remained between 0.75 and 0.82 at each time point, indicating good discriminative performance; the full model demonstrated a slight but consistent improvement across all time points, suggesting that SIRI_HDL_ALB may provide modest additional predictive information (Figure 2C and D). In the incremental model comparison, SIRI_HDL_ALB provided the largest improvement in C-index among the evaluated blood-based indicators, with Delta C-index values of 0.012 (95% CI: 0.003–0.028) for OS and 0.016 (95% CI: 0.004–0.026) for RFS. The C-index increased from 0.708 to 0.720 for OS and from 0.721 to 0.737 for RFS after adding SIRI_HDL_ALB (Table S1). The calibration curves indicated good agreement between the predicted and observed outcomes (Figure 3). Decision curve analysis (DCA) suggested that the model incorporating SIRI_HDL_ALB provided higher or comparable net benefit across selected threshold probabilities (Figure 4). Competing Risk Analysis

The cumulative incidence curve for competing risks revealed that the cumulative incidence of the primary endpoint was significantly higher in the High group than in the Low group (Gray’s test p = 0.0034), whereas the incidence of competing events did not differ significantly between the two groups (p = 0.451). These findings suggest that the SIRI_HDL_ALB classification may serve as a useful tool for prognostic stratification (Figure S2A).

Model Stability

The histogram of the C-index distribution from 1000 bootstrap replications shows that the C-index for the OS model is concentrated in the range of 0.65–0.80, with an original value of 0.715, a bootstrap mean of 0.72, and a 95% confidence interval of 0.674–0.765. For the RFS model, the C-index falls within 0.652–0.84, with an original value of 0.737, a bootstrap mean of 0.737, and a 95% confidence interval of 0.684–0.788. These findings suggested acceptable internal stability of the model, although external validation remains necessary.(Figure S2B and C).

Subgroup Analysis

To evaluate whether the prognostic stratification effect of SIRI_HDL_ALB remains stable across different clinical subgroups, we conducted subgroup analyses based on key variables such as CA19-9, CEA, lymph node metastasis, nerve invasion, T stage, degree of differentiation, smoking, diabetes, portal vein invasion, margin status, gender, postoperative complications, and postoperative adjuvant chemotherapy, and calculated the HR and interaction P-values within each subgroup.

Overall, high SIRI_HDL_ALB was significantly associated with a worse OS (HR = 2.46, 95% CI: 1.50–4.02, P < 0.001). In all subgroups, high SIRI_HDL_ALB consistently represented a risk factor (HR > 1), and the effects in most subgroups were statistically significant (Figure S3). The interaction P-values between subgroups did not reach statistical significance (all P > 0.05), including: CA19-9 group (P for interaction = 0.179), CEA group (0.945), lymph node metastasis (0.099), nerve invasion (0.316), T stage (0.825), degree of differentiation (0.946), smoking (0.670), diabetes (0.324), portal vein invasion (0.571), margin status (0.801), gender (0.865), postoperative complications (0.721), and postoperative adjuvant chemotherapy (0.897). These results suggest that no statistically significant effect modification was observed across the evaluated clinical subgroups.

For RFS, high SIRI_HDL_ALB also significantly increased the risk of recurrence (HR = 2.14, 95% CI: 1.28–3.58, P = 0.004) (Figure S4). In each subgroup, high SIRI_HDL_ALB exhibited a consistent adverse prognostic trend with the overall trend (HR > 1), and the interaction P-values were not significant: CA19-9 (0.289), CEA (0.209), lymph node metastasis (0.437), nerve invasion (0.599), T stage (0.767), degree of differentiation (0.681), smoking (0.915), diabetes (0.799), portal vein invasion (0.615), margin status (0.530), gender (0.882), postoperative complications (0.949), and postoperative adjuvant chemotherapy (0.773).

Discussion

This study constructed and internally validated a novel composite indicator, SIRI_HDL_ALB, integrating systemic inflammation, lipid metabolism, and nutritional status in a cohort of 230 patients with resected distal cholangiocarcinoma. SIRI_HDL_ALB was independently associated with both OS and RFS when modeled as a continuous variable. Multivariate Cox regression revealed that the predictive effect magnitude of a high SIRI_HDL_ALB was comparable to that of established strong risk factors, such as lymph node metastasis,14 portal vein invasion,15 nerve invasion,16 and low differentiation.17 In addition, comparative analyses showed that SIRI_HDL_ALB achieved the highest single-marker C-index for both OS and RFS among the evaluated inflammatory, nutritional, and lipid-related indicators. When added to the clinicopathological model, SIRI_HDL_ALB provided the largest C-index increment among the evaluated blood-based indicators, suggesting that it captures complementary host-state information beyond conventional pathological risk factors.18

Models incorporating SIRI_HDL_ALB showed modest but measurable incremental predictive value. The absolute AUC increase of the full model over the clinicopathological model was small, generally around 0.01, and should therefore be interpreted cautiously. However, this modest improvement should be considered in the context of a strong reference clinicopathological model that already included established prognostic factors, including lymph node metastasis, portal vein invasion, tumor infiltration, perineural invasion, tumor differentiation, and CA19-9 for OS.19 In such a setting, a large increase in discrimination would not be expected from a single preoperative blood-based indicator. Nevertheless, after adding SIRI_HDL_ALB, the ΔC-index was 0.012 for OS and 0.016 for RFS, with bootstrap-derived confidence intervals not crossing zero. These findings suggest that SIRI_HDL_ALB should not be regarded as a replacement for established clinicopathological predictors, but rather as a simple preoperative blood-based adjunct that may refine postoperative risk stratification and follow-up planning. Decision curve analysis20 further indicated that the full model provided higher or comparable net benefit across clinically relevant thresholds, supporting its potential supplementary clinical utility. The clinical impact curve (CIC)21 also showed that the number of patients classified as high risk by the model was generally consistent with the number of patients who actually experienced events, suggesting reasonable clinical applicability of the model.

The stability of the prognostic association between SIRI_HDL_ALB and postoperative outcomes was further evaluated from three complementary perspectives. First, subgroup analyses showed that high SIRI_HDL_ALB was consistently associated with an increased risk of poor outcomes across key clinical subgroups, including CA19-9, CEA, lymph node metastasis, perineural invasion, T stage, tumor differentiation, smoking, diabetes, portal vein invasion, margin status, sex, postoperative complications, and adjuvant chemotherapy. No statistically significant interactions were observed across these subgroups, suggesting that the prognostic association of SIRI_HDL_ALB was broadly consistent in different clinical contexts. Even among traditional low-risk patients (eg, R0 resection, no lymph node metastasis),22 it could effectively identify those with poorer prognosis. Second, competing risk analysis23 using the cumulative incidence function and Gray’s test showed that patients in the high SIRI_HDL_ALB group had a significantly higher cumulative incidence of recurrence than those in the low-score group (P=0.0034), whereas the incidence of non-recurrence death was not significantly different between groups (P=0.451). This finding suggests that the association between SIRI_HDL_ALB and recurrence was not mainly driven by differences in competing mortality. Finally, bootstrap internal validation with 1000 resamples24 showed corrected C-index values of 0.720 for OS and 0.737 for RFS, with bootstrap-derived 95% confidence intervals of 0.674–0.765 and 0.684–0.788, respectively. The calibration curves also showed acceptable agreement between predicted and observed probabilities. Together, these analyses support the internal consistency of the model, although external validation in independent cohorts remains necessary.

The SIRI_HDL_ALB indicator constructed in this study, with SIRI (neutrophils × monocytes/lymphocytes) as the numerator and the product of HDL and ALB as the denominator, mathematically reflects the ratio of “inflammatory load” to “anti-inflammatory/nutritional reserve”. In tumor-associated inflammatory responses, neutrophils can release reactive oxygen species, proteases, and neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs), directly damaging tissues and promoting tumor cell invasion and epithelial-mesenchymal transition.25 Monocytes differentiate into tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) within the tumor microenvironment and secrete cytokines such as interleukin-6 (IL-6), TGF-β, and VEGF, thereby inhibiting the function of CD8⁺ T cells and inducing immunosuppression.26 Lymphocytes, particularly T cells, serve as the core effector cells in anti-tumor immunity.27 In this study, the high SIRI_HDL_ALB group exhibited significantly increased neutrophils and monocytes, alongside markedly decreased lymphocytes, a profile consistent with a typical “pro-inflammatory-immunosuppressive” phenotype.

High-density lipoprotein (HDL) exerts anti-cancer effects mainly through reverse cholesterol transport, anti-inflammatory and antioxidant activities, and immune regulation: it removes excess cholesterol from tumor cells, thereby inhibiting proliferation—particularly in hormone-dependent tumors;28 it reduces chronic inflammation by binding endotoxins and blocking the TLR4/NF-κB pathway;29 and it enhances the anti-tumor immune response by promoting tumor-associated macrophage polarization toward the M1 phenotype and preserving T cell membrane lipid raft function.30 In cholangiocarcinoma, low HDL levels are closely associated with tumor progression, lymph node metastasis, and poor prognosis, and the underlying mechanisms include cholesterol imbalance-driven malignant transformation and enhanced tumor stem cell self-renewal through reprogrammed fatty acid metabolism.31 Albumin (ALB), the most abundant serum protein, is tightly regulated by liver function and inflammatory status, and low ALB levels therefore indicate insufficient nutrient intake or increased consumption.32 In this study, the ALB level in the high-value group was significantly lower than that in the low-value group, suggesting a more pronounced inflammatory and nutritional deficiency state in these patients.

This study has several limitations. First, it is a single-center retrospective cohort study, with all data derived from the same medical center between 2011 and 2024. Temporal heterogeneity in perioperative management, imaging assessment, and adjuvant treatment may have introduced selection bias and time-effect bias. Second, although SIRI_HDL_ALB showed incremental prognostic value beyond the clinicopathological model, the absolute improvement in model discrimination was modest; therefore, it should be interpreted as a complementary biomarker rather than a standalone prediction tool. Third, the OS-derived cut-off value of 0.143 was used only for Kaplan–Meier visualization and descriptive stratification, and its generalizability to other cohorts remains uncertain, particularly because the resulting groups were highly unbalanced. Fourth, preoperative laboratory values were defined as the results closest to surgery, and paired laboratory data before and after biliary drainage were not consistently available. Therefore, the dynamic influence of biliary decompression on SIRI_HDL_ALB could not be fully assessed. Fifth, residual confounding related to cholestasis, hepatic functional reserve, and perioperative host status cannot be excluded. Sixth, subgroup analyses involving small strata, such as R1 margins and portal vein invasion, had limited statistical power and wide confidence intervals. Seventh, although no documented use of lipid-lowering medications was identified in the available preoperative medication records, medication history was retrospectively extracted, and undocumented medication use could not be completely excluded. Finally, this study was a prognostic association analysis and cannot establish causality. The mechanistic explanations proposed in the discussion are hypothesis-generating and require further experimental validation. External validation in multicenter prospective cohorts is necessary before routine clinical application.

Conclusion

SIRI_HDL_ALB is an independent prognostic factor for overall survival and recurrence-free survival in patients undergoing radical resection of distal cholangiocarcinoma. By integrating information on inflammation, lipid metabolism, and nutrition, it complements prognostic assessments based on traditional pathological classification. The nomogram incorporating this indicator showed acceptable internal performance and may provide supplementary information for postoperative risk stratification. These findings require further validation in multi-center prospective studies.

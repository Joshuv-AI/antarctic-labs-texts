On the existence of solutions to the multi-species Landau equation
Jonathan JunnéRaphael Winter, Havva Yoldaş

Abstract
We consider the spatially homogeneous Landau equation for multiple species with different masses. As in the single-species case, the singularity of the collision operator is determined by a parameter γ ∈ [ − 3 , 1 ] \gamma\in[-3,1] , where γ = − 3 \gamma=-3 corresponds to Coulomb interactions. We prove that if γ ≥ − 8 \gamma\geq-\sqrt{8} in the cross-interaction operators, then there exists a natural multi-species generalization of the Fisher information which is a Lyapunov functional for the multi-species Landau system. On the other hand, we give a counterexample showing that the Fisher information is in general no longer a Lyapunov functional below the threshold ( γ < − 8 ) (\gamma<-\sqrt{8}) for the two-species system if one species has infinite mass. However, we are able to provide a new method to show global well-posedness, by constructing a different Lyapunov functional based on the spherical Fisher information. † † Keywords and phrases. Kinetic theory, Collisional plasma, Multi-species Landau system, Fisher information, Coulomb potential. † † 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification. 35Q70, 82C40, 58J35.

Introduction

We study the spatially homogeneous multi-species Landau equation, a kinetic model for the time evolution of a plasma consisting of several distinct particle species, such as ions and electrons. The homogeneous multi-species Landau system, satisfied by the probability densities
f_{i}=f_{i}(t,v)
with
(t,v)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
for
N
species, is given by

\partial_{t}f_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}Q_{ij}(f_{i},f_{j}),\qquad 1\leq i\leq N,

(1.1)

where
Q_{ij}(f_{i},f_{j})
is the Landau collision operator, derived by Landau in 1936
[
30
]
,

\displaystyle Q_{ij}(f_{i},f_{j})(v_{i})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m_{i}}\,\nabla_{v_{i}}\cdot\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}c_{ij}A(v_{i}-v_{j})\,\left(\frac{\nabla_{v_{i}}}{m_{i}}-\frac{\nabla_{v_{j}}}{m_{j}}\right)\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)(v_{i},v_{j})\,\,\mathrm{d}v_{j}\right),

with

\displaystyle A(z)\coloneqq\,\left\lvert z\right\rvert^{2+\gamma}\Pi^{\perp}_{z},\quad\Pi^{\perp}_{z}\coloneqq\operatorname{Id}-\frac{z\otimes z}{\left\lvert z\right\rvert^{2}},\quad c_{ij}\coloneqq\frac{\left\lvert\log{\Lambda_{c}}\right\rvert q_{i}^{2}q_{j}^{2}n_{i}n_{j}}{8\pi\varepsilon_{0}^{2}},

(1.2)

where
|\log\Lambda_{c}|
is the Coulomb logarithm, and
\varepsilon_{0}
is the vacuum permittivity. The constants
n_{i}
are the number densities of each species per unit volume, so we can assume
f_{i}
to be probability densities. The constant
\gamma\in[-3,1]
determines the singularity of the interaction kernel, the physically most relevant case describing the interaction of charged particles via Coulomb potentials corresponds to
\gamma=-3
. The Landau equation can be obtained in the
grazing collisions limit
from the Boltzmann equation, see e.g.,
[
2
,
13
,
40
,
31
]
.

The breakthrough result of Guillen and Silvestre
[
24
]
establishes the global existence of solutions for the Landau-Coulomb equation, when
f
solves (
1.1
) for only one species. They achieve this by showing that the Fisher information

\displaystyle I\!\left(f\right)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left\lvert\nabla\log f\right\rvert^{2}f

(1.3)

is non-increasing along the solution of the Landau equation. On the other hand, for the multi-species Landau equation only a couple of mathematically rigorous results are available.

As the multi-species Landau equation arises from (grazing) elastic collisions it conserves, at least formally, the number of particles for all species, as well as the total momentum
M
and total energy
E
. Therefore, we have the following conserved quantities

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}m_{i}v\,f_{i}(v)\,\mathrm{d}v,\quad E:=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}m_{i}\left\lvert v\right\rvert^{2}f_{i}(v)\,\mathrm{d}v.

(1.4)

Moreover, the Boltzmann entropy
\mathcal{H}
, given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{N})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{i}\log f_{i},

(1.5)

is formally non-increasing in time.

In this paper, we tackle the problem of global well-posedness for the spatially homogeneous multi-species Landau equation. To this end, we first derive a natural generalization of the Fisher information in the multi-species setting. Since the global equilibrium of the multi-species system is given by the Gibbs state

\displaystyle F_{\mathrm{eq}}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{N})=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal{M}_{T,m_{i}}(v_{i}),

(1.6)

where
T>0
is the temperature and
\mathcal{M}_{T,m_{i}}
is the Maxwellian velocity distribution for particles of mass
m_{i}
, i.e.

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{T,m_{i}}(v)=\left(\frac{m_{i}}{2\pi T}\right)^{3/2}\exp\left(-\frac{m_{i}}{2T}\left\lvert v\right\rvert^{2}\right),

(1.7)

it is natural to seek a multi-species Fisher information
\mathcal{I}
of the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}\!\left(F_{N};{\bf a}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left\lvert\nabla\log f_{i}\right\rvert^{2}f_{i},

where
F_{N}=\left(f_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{N}
solution to (
1.1
), and
{\bf a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{N})
positive constants
a_{i}>0
to be chosen. We can only expect monotonicity of this functional if the equilibrium (
1.6
) is a minimizer among all configurations with fixed total kinetic energy
E
defined in (
1.4
). Testing this condition on factorized functions with the same kinetic energy shows that, up to a multiplicative constant, the unique choice is
a_{i}=m_{i}^{-1}
. We therefore define

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}\!\left(F_{N}\right)\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{m_{i}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left\lvert\nabla\log f_{i}\right\rvert^{2}f_{i}.

(1.8)

This functional is indeed a Lyapunov functional for the homogeneous multi-species Landau equation provided that
\gamma\in[-\sqrt{8},-2]
which is our first main result
Theorem

1.1
. We recast the cross-interaction operators in a new set of variables (
2.6
) and differential operators (
2.3
). After this transformation, the operators can be treated with similar to
[
24
]
, and the constant in the log-Sobolev inequality without additional symmetry is responsible for the threshold value
-\sqrt{8}
. We can even prove that for
\gamma\in[-3,-\sqrt{8})
the Fisher information
\mathcal{I}(F_{N})
is in general no longer a Lyapunov functional, by giving an explicit counterexample in
Theorem

1.2
.

The first two theorems pose the question whether the multi-species Landau system is indeed globally well-posed below the threshold value
-\sqrt{8}
. This is the main objective of our analysis. We are able to answer this question positively in the case of two-species Landau system where one species has infinite mass, i.e. the collisional dynamics of electrons and ions. While our counterexample shows that the time derivative of the Fisher information along this dynamics is no longer under control, we construct a new Lyapunov functional
\Lambda(f)
, defined in (
1.12
), by combining a weighted spherical Fisher information, the classical Fisher information and the Boltzmann entropy. With this new functional we establish global well-posedness for
\gamma\in[-3,-\sqrt{8})
in
Theorem

1.3
.

1.1
Summary of previous results

In this section, we summarize the theory of the Landau equation with a particular focus on the results after
[
24
]
. We refer the reader to
[
24
]
for a detailed review and historical remarks. The global well-posedness theory for the spatially homogeneous Landau equation is well-established for hard potentials and Maxwellian molecules, i.e.,
\gamma\geq 0

[
41
]
for
\gamma=0

[
11
,
12
]
for
\gamma>0

[

36

43

imply global smooth solutions. For the very soft potentials, i.e.,

including the case of Coulomb interactions, this problem was open until the recent breakthrough result by Guillen and Silveste
[
24
]
. They prove that the Fisher information is monotone decreasing for the spatially homogeneous Landau equation with Coulomb interactions, which gives the existence of global smooth solutions. Since then, this new information-theoretical approach has been used to address other open problems in kinetic theory. We refer the reader to the recent notes by Villani
[
42
]
on this topic. In
[
27
]
, Imbert, Silvestre and Villani generalized this method to show the existence of global smooth solutions to the space-homogeneous Boltzmann equation in the regime of very soft potentials. The Fisher information approach has been generalized to initial data without finite Fisher information, see
[
4
,
10
,
19
,
29
]
. Let us briefly discuss the Lenard-Balescu equation which is considered as a more accurate model than Landau equation for weakly coupled systems with long-range forces (especially Coulomb plasmas). The presence of collective effects leads to an additional non-local nonlinearity in the collision operator and currently no Fisher information based theory is known. The state of the art regarding the spatially homogeneous Lenard-Balescu equation is due to
[
14
,
15
,
38
]
.

The spatially in-homogenous Landau equation with Coulomb interactions still remains open for general initial data. However, it is possible to obtain rigorous results when the collision operator is mollified in space. In
[
21
]
, the authors study a fuzzy version of the spatially in-homogeneous Landau equation where the collisions are delocalized via a spatially dependent kernel
\kappa(x-x_{*})
. They prove the global well-posedness of the fuzzy Landau equation for moderately soft interactions where
\gamma\in(-2,0]
and a positive spatial kernel
\kappa(x)\sim\langle x\rangle^{-\lambda},\lambda>0
when the initial data is sufficiently smooth. For
\kappa\equiv 1
, and
\gamma\in[-3,1]
, they also prove that the spatial Fisher information decays monotonically and the full Fisher information remains uniformly bounded in finite time intervals. We also refer to recent article series
[
16
,
17
,
18
]
for more details on the fuzzy Landau equation.

When it comes to the multi-species Landau equation, mathematical results are rather scarce. Classical references for the derivation of (
1.1
) are
[
32
, Chapter 4]
and
[
35
, Chapter 6]
. This problem is closely related to the study of multi-species Boltzmann equation; thus, the existing results use similar approaches. Let us start by summarizing some landmark results in this case. In
[
37
]
, the authors study the Boltzmann equation for a mixture of two gases in one-spatial dimension, and prove sharp pointwise-in-
(t,x)
asymptotics where one species is near vacuum and the other one is near Maxwellian equilibrium. One of the first spectral gap results for the spatially in-homogeneous multi-species Boltzmann system on
(x,v)\in\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
covering hard potentials and Maxwellian molecules, i.e.,
\gamma\in[0,1]
with Grad’s cutoff assumption is
[
9
]
. This work uses a multi-species adaptation of the quantitative hypocoercivity techniques developed in
[
34
]
. However,
[
9
]
does not allow species to have different masses in the mixture. In
[
3
]
, the authors develop a perturbative Cauchy theory for the multi-species Boltzmann equation posed on
(x,v)\in\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
in the weighted space
L_{v}^{1}L_{x}^{\infty}
with a polynomial weight. They prove the existence of a spectral gap for the linear multi-species Boltzmann operator allowing different masses which induce a loss of symmetry and the standard methods developed for the mono-species case are not immediately available. They study the perturbed linear equation by means of
L^{1}-L^{\infty}
theory due to
[
25
]
.

To the best of our knowledge, there are only two works on the Cauchy problem of the multi-species Landau system. In
[
23
]
, the authors study the system of the spatially in-homogeneous Landau system defined in the phase space
(x,p)\in\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
with moderately soft potentials, i.e.,
\gamma\in[-2,1]
. Using a similar strategy as in
[
9
]
, they provide explicit spectral gap and hypocoercivity estimates for a linearized multi-species Landau system where all species are assumed to be close to equilibrium. In
[
33
]
, the authors study the Landau system on
(x,p)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
for
\gamma\in[-2,1]
for
2
species where one species starts near vacuum and the other one starts near a Maxwellian equilibrium state. Using a similar approach in the case of the multi-species Boltzmann system
[
37
]
, they show that the solutions to the linearized system become instantaneously smooth both in
x
and
p
without requiring any smoothness assumption on the initial datum. On the numerics side,
[
7

develops a deterministic, structure-preserving particle method for the spatially homogeneous, multi-species Landau equation by regularizing the collision operator so that each species distribution can be approximated as a sum of Dirac masses whose locations evolve according to an ODE system.

1.2
Main results

Theorem 1.1

(Global well-posedness)
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
be the number of species and
m_{i}>0
,
1\leq i\leq N
their respective particle masses. Assume that the interaction is soft with
-2>\gamma\geq-\sqrt{8}
. Further assume that the initial distributions satisfy
f^{\circ}_{i}\in L^{1}_{2\ell}\cap L^{2}_{\ell}\cap L\log L
for some
\ell\geq 7
and are probability densities. Then there exists a global-in-time strong solution
F_{N}=(f_{i})_{i=1}^{N}
which solves (
1.1
), and

\displaystyle f_{i}\in C^{\infty}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{3}),\quad\text{for }1\leq i\leq N.

Moreover, for any
t>0
, the generalized Fisher information
\mathcal{I}\!\left(F_{N}\right)
defined in (
1.8
) is finite and non-increasing in time on
(0,\infty)
.

The proof of this theorem is the content of
Section

2.4
.

The theorem covers a large range of soft potentials, down to the threshold
\gamma_{*}=-\sqrt{8}
. This naturally raises the question whether the monotonicity of the Fisher information persists for
\gamma<-\sqrt{8}
. We answer this question negatively in the limiting case where one species has infinite mass. This equation models, in particular, the collisional dynamics of electrons interacting with ions, since the mass ratio is about 1836 already between protons and electrons. The resulting equation can be found in
[
35
, Equation 6.4.11]
and reads

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f=Q_{\gamma}(f,f)+c_{ei}Lf,

(1.9)

with the choice
\gamma=-3
and
c_{ei}>0
in the general formula

\displaystyle Q_{\gamma}(f,f)(v)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\nabla_{v}\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}A(v-v^{*})\left(\nabla_{v}-\nabla_{v^{*}}\right)\left(f\otimes f\right)(v,v^{*})\,\mathrm{d}{v^{*}},

\displaystyle Lf(v)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\nabla_{v}\cdot\left(|v|^{2+\gamma}\Pi^{\perp}_{v}\nabla_{v}f(v)\right).

In other words, the interaction with ions induces a diffusion on spheres in velocity space. We prove that the Fisher information is a Lyapunov function for this equation if the initial datum
f^{\circ}
is
even
, but is
not
monotone decreasing in general.

Theorem 1.2

(Non-monotonicity of the Fisher information below the critical threshold)
.

Let
-3\leq\gamma<-\sqrt{8}
. Then there exists a probability density
f^{\circ}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
such that the local-in-time solution
f
with initial data
f^{\circ}
to (
1.9
) satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}I\!\left(f\right)\big|_{t=0}>0.

On the other hand, if
f^{\circ}
is
even
, i.e.
f^{\circ}(v)=f^{\circ}(-v)
, then the Fisher information
I(f)
is a Lyapunov functional for (
1.9
) for any
-3\leq\gamma\leq-2
.

The proof of this theorem is the content of
Section

3.2
.

This theorem shows that the Fisher information is no longer a Lyapunov functional. Moreover, the proof reveals that the time derivative of the Fisher information cannot be controlled in terms of
I\!\left(f\right)
alone, which makes any direct growth estimate for
I\!\left(f\right)
inaccessible. This prompts the question whether (
1.9
) is in fact globally well-posed. We answer this positively even for Coulomb interactions between electrons and ions. We consider the equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f=Q_{\gamma}(f,f)+c_{ei}\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{\Pi^{\perp}_{v}}{\left\lvert v\right\rvert}\,\nabla f\right),

(1.10)

where
Q_{\gamma}(f,f)
is given by the single-species Landau operator with coefficient
\gamma\in[-3,2]
in (
1.2
).

To achieve this, we introduce, in addition to the standard Fisher information, a weighted spherical Fisher information
J\!\left(f\right)
defined by

\displaystyle J\!\left(f\right)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\left\lvert\Pi^{\perp}_{v}\nabla\log f(v)\right\rvert^{2}}{\left\lvert v\right\rvert}\,f(v)\,\,\mathrm{d}v,

together with the Boltzmann relative entropy
H\!\left(f|\mathcal{M}_{{T^{\circ}},1}\right)
with respect to the Maxwellian
\mathcal{M}_{{T^{\circ}},1}
(
1.7
) with temperature
T^{\circ}
corresponding to the conserved free energy, given by

\displaystyle H\!\left(f|\mathcal{M}_{{T^{\circ}},1}\right)\coloneqq H(f)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f\log\mathcal{M}_{{T^{\circ}},1}\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f\log f-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f\log\mathcal{M}_{{T^{\circ}},1}.

Here,
T^{\circ}
is chosen such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{2}|v|^{2}\mathcal{M}_{{T^{\circ}},1}(v)\,\,\mathrm{d}v=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{2}|v|^{2}f^{\circ}(v)\,\,\mathrm{d}v=E.

We are now in the position to state the main result.

Theorem 1.3

(New Lyapunov functional for the infinite-mass case)
.

Let
-3\leq\gamma<-\sqrt{8}
and
c_{ei}>0
and
f
be the (local) strong solution to (
1.10
) with initial datum given by a probability density
f^{\circ}
satisfying
f^{\circ}\in L^{1}_{2\ell}\cap L^{2}_{\ell}\cap L\log L
for some
\ell\geq 7
and
J\!\left(f^{\circ}\right)<\infty
.
In the case of
\gamma=-3
, further assume that
c_{ei}>0
and the initial datum
f^{\circ}
satisfy

\displaystyle\left(2+\frac{J(f^{\circ})}{I^{\frac{3}{2}}(f^{\circ})}+H\!\left(f^{\circ}|\mathcal{M}_{{T^{\circ}},1}\right)\right)\leq\frac{c_{ei}}{K},

(1.11)

where
K>0
is a sufficiently large constant.
Then there exist constants
a,R>0
such that the non-negative functional

\displaystyle\Lambda(f)=I\!\left(f\right)+a\,J\!\left(f\right)+R\,H\!\left(f|\mathcal{M}_{{T^{\circ}},1}\right)

(1.12)

is non-increasing in time. In particular, the Fisher information
I\!\left(f\right)
remains globally bounded by a constant depending only on the initial datum
f^{\circ}
, and the solution exists globally-in-time.

The proof of this theorem is the content of
Section

3.3
.

Let us emphasize that this result shows that the global-in-time boundedness of the Fisher information persists, even though the Fisher information itself is no longer monotone for
\gamma<-\sqrt{8}

For general finite mass-ratio of two species, the question of well-posedness of the two-species system remains open when
\gamma<-\sqrt{8}
. In the linearized setting and large mass ratio, the problem is related to the two-scale problem considered in
[

2
The multi-species Landau system with finite masses

2.1
Lifting argument for the multi-species system

We perform a lifting procedure by doubling the number of variables as in
[
24
]
. To this end, we define

m_{N+i}\coloneqq m_{i},\qquad 1\leq i\leq N,

so that particles
i
and
N+i
belong to the same species. We introduce the lifted linear operators acting on functions

\widetilde{F}=\widetilde{F}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{2N}):(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{2N}\to\mathbb{R}

by

\widetilde{Q}_{ij}(\widetilde{F})\coloneqq\left(\frac{\nabla_{v_{i}}}{m_{i}}-\frac{\nabla_{v_{j}}}{m_{j}}\right)\cdot\left(c_{ij}A(v_{i}-v_{j})\,\left(\frac{\nabla_{v_{i}}}{m_{i}}-\frac{\nabla_{v_{j}}}{m_{j}}\right)\widetilde{F}\right),

and consider the linear evolution of the Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\widetilde{F}=\widetilde{Q}(\widetilde{F})=\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq N\\
N+1\leq j\leq 2N\end{subarray}}\widetilde{Q}_{ij}(\widetilde{F}),\\[5.0pt]
\widetilde{F}^{\circ}(v_{1},\dots,v_{2N})=\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{N}f^{\circ}_{i}(v_{i})\,f^{\circ}_{i}(v_{N+i}).\end{cases}

(2.1)

The evolution (
2.1
) is useful because the equations for the marginals can be related back to the multi-species system (
1.1
). To see this, we consider the marginals

\Pi_{k}\widetilde{F}=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{2N-1}}\widetilde{F}\,\,\mathrm{d}\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{k},\qquad 1\leq k\leq 2N,

where
\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{k}
denotes the vector
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{2N})
with
v_{k}
omitted.
For
1\leq i\leq N
, we have

\partial_{t}\Pi_{i}\widetilde{F}=\sum_{j=N+1}^{2N}\frac{\nabla_{v_{i}}}{m_{i}}\cdot\left(\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{2N-1}}c_{ij}A(v_{i}-v_{j})\,\left(\frac{\nabla_{v_{i}}}{m_{i}}-\frac{\nabla_{v_{j}}}{m_{j}}\right)\widetilde{F}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{2N})\,\,\mathrm{d}{\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{i}}\right),

and thus, at time
t=0
, we recover the multi-species equation (
1.1
);

\partial_{t}\Pi_{i}\widetilde{F}\Big|_{t=0}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}Q_{ij}(f_{i}^{\circ},f_{j}^{\circ}).

For a single density, we define the Fisher information by

I\!\left(f\right)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left\lvert\nabla\log f\right\rvert^{2}f\,\mathrm{d}v.

For the lifted multi-species system we introduce the weighted Fisher information

\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}(\widetilde{F})\coloneqq\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{2N}}\sum_{i=1}^{2N}\frac{1}{m_{i}}\left\lvert\nabla_{v_{i}}\log\widetilde{F}\right\rvert^{2}\widetilde{F}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{v}_{2N},

where
\mathbf{v}_{2N}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{2N})
. Although this definition is asymmetric, the weighted Fisher information retains the key property that we need: it controls a suitably scaled sum of the marginals’ Fisher information.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\widetilde{F}:(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{2N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a probability density with finite
weighted Fisher information
\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}(\widetilde{F})
and marginals

\Pi_{i}\widetilde{F}=\Pi_{N+i}\widetilde{F}=f_{i},\qquad 1\leq i\leq N.

Then

\frac{1}{2}\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}(\widetilde{F})\;\geq\;\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{m_{i}}\,I\!\left(f_{i}\right),

(2.2)

with equality if and only if
\widetilde{F}=\left(f_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{N}\right)^{\otimes 2}
.

Proof.

Consider the change of variables
\Phi:(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{2N}\to(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{2N}
defined by

\Phi:\ (v_{1},\dots,v_{2N})\mapsto\left(\sqrt{m_{1}}\,v_{1},\dots,\sqrt{m_{2N}}\,v_{2N}\right).

Then the push-forward density
G=|\det D\Phi|^{-1}\,\widetilde{F}\circ\Phi^{-1}
satisfies

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\left\lvert\nabla\log G\right\rvert^{2}G\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{v}_{2N}=\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}(\widetilde{F}).

The claim is then a direct consequence of Carlen’s inequality, i.e.
[
5
, Theorem 3]
. The factor
\frac{1}{2}
stems from the fact that
f_{i}=f_{N+i}
, so each probability distribution appears twice as a marginal.
∎

2.2
Dissipation of the weighted Fisher information

This section is devoted to the proof of the dissipation of the weighted Fisher information, given local well-posedness. This is the fundamental tool to prove
Theorem

1.1
. One key ingredient is to introduce the following differential operators,

\,\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla_{i,j}\coloneqq\left(\frac{\nabla_{v_{i}}}{\sqrt{m_{i}}},\frac{\nabla_{v_{j}}}{\sqrt{m_{j}}}\right),\qquad\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla$\cr\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{$/$}\crcr}}}_{i,j}\mkern-2.5mu\>\>\coloneqq\frac{\nabla_{v_{i}}}{m_{i}}-\frac{\nabla_{v_{j}}}{m_{j}}.

(2.3)

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\widetilde{F}(v_{1},\cdots,v_{2N})=\otimes_{k=1}^{2N}f_{k}
be a probability density with marginals
f_{k}
and finite weighted Fisher information
\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}(\widetilde{F})
. Then for any pair
(i,j)\,:\,1\leq i\leq N,\,N+1\leq j\leq 2N
,

\big\langle\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}^{\prime}(\widetilde{F}),\widetilde{Q}_{ij}(\widetilde{F})\big\rangle=\big\langle\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}_{ij}^{\prime}\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right),\widetilde{Q}_{ij}\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)\big\rangle,

where

\widetilde{Q}_{ij}(f_{i}\otimes f_{j})\coloneqq\>\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla$\cr\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{$/$}\crcr}}}_{i,j}\mkern-2.5mu\cdot\left(c_{ij}A(v_{i}-v_{j})\,\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla$\cr\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{$/$}\crcr}}}_{i,j}\mkern-2.5mu\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)\right)

and

\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}_{ij}\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)\coloneqq\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{2}}\left\lvert\,\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla_{i,j}\log\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)\right\rvert^{2}\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right).

As a consequence, we have the comparison principle

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{I}(F)\big|_{t=0}\leq\frac{1}{2}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}^{\prime}(\widetilde{F})\big|_{t=0}.

(2.4)

Proof.

By linearity, it suffices to show that for any index
k\neq i,j
and

\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}_{k}(\widetilde{F})\coloneqq\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{2N}}\frac{1}{m_{k}}\left\lvert\nabla_{v_{k}}\log\widetilde{F}\right\rvert^{2}\widetilde{F},

we have

\big\langle\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}_{k}^{\prime}(\widetilde{F}),\widetilde{Q}_{ij}(\widetilde{F})\big\rangle=0.

A direct computation gives

\displaystyle\big\langle\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}_{k}^{\prime}(\widetilde{F}),\widetilde{Q}_{ij}(\widetilde{F})\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{2N}}\frac{2}{m_{k}}\left(\nabla_{v_{k}}\left(\frac{\widetilde{Q}_{ij}(\widetilde{F})}{\widetilde{F}}\right)\cdot\nabla_{v_{k}}\log\widetilde{F}\right)\widetilde{F}+\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{2N}}\frac{1}{m_{k}}\widetilde{Q}_{ij}(\widetilde{F})\,\left\lvert\nabla_{v_{k}}\log\widetilde{F}\right\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m_{k}}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{2N}}2\,\nabla_{v_{k}}\left(\frac{\widetilde{Q}_{ij}(\widetilde{F})}{\widetilde{F}}\right)\cdot\nabla_{v_{k}}\widetilde{F}+\widetilde{Q}_{ij}(\widetilde{F})\left\lvert\nabla_{v_{k}}\log\widetilde{F}\right\rvert^{2}.

Since
\widetilde{F}
is factorized,
\widetilde{Q}_{ij}(\widetilde{F})/\widetilde{F}
does not depend on
v_{k}
while
\nabla_{v_{k}}\log\widetilde{F}
only depends on
v_{k}
. As a consequence, the above integrals vanish, which proves the claim.

Finally, the comparison principle (
2.4
) follows from the same argument as in the single-species case:

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{I}(F)\big|_{t=0}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\big\langle\frac{1}{m_{i}}I^{\prime}(f_{i}),Q_{ij}(f_{i},f_{j})\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\big\langle\frac{1}{m_{i}}I^{\prime}(f_{i}),\partial_{t}\Pi_{i}\widetilde{F}\big|_{t=0}\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{m_{i}}I(\Pi_{i}\widetilde{F})-\frac{1}{2}\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}(\widetilde{F})\right)\Big|_{t=0}+\partial_{t}\frac{1}{2}\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}(\widetilde{F})\big|_{t=0}

\displaystyle\leq\partial_{t}\frac{1}{2}\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}(\widetilde{F})\big|_{t=0},

where the first term in the second line is non-positive due to
Lemma

2.1
.
∎

Let
\widetilde{F}
be a solution to (
1.1
). At time
t=0
, the initial data is factorized as
\widetilde{F}^{\circ}=(f_{1}^{\circ}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{N}^{\circ})^{\otimes 2}
, and
Lemma

2.2
allows us to rewrite each contribution to the dissipation as

\partial_{t}\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}(\widetilde{F})\big|_{t=0}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq N\\
N+1\leq j\leq 2N\end{subarray}}\big\langle\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}^{\prime}(\widetilde{F}^{\circ}),\widetilde{Q}_{ij}(\widetilde{F}^{\circ})\big\rangle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq N\\
N+1\leq j\leq 2N\end{subarray}}\big\langle\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}^{\prime}_{ij}\left(f_{i}^{\circ}\otimes f_{j}^{\circ}\right),\widetilde{Q}_{ij}\left(f_{i}^{\circ}\otimes f_{j}^{\circ}\right)\big\rangle.

Lemma 2.3

(Two-particle dissipation identity)
.

Let
1\leq i\leq N
and
N+1\leq j\leq 2N
, and let
f_{i},f_{j-N}\,(\coloneqq f_{j})
be solutions of (
1.1
) given by
Lemma

2.7
. The Gâteaux derivative of the two-particles weighted Fisher information
\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}_{ij}(f_{i}\otimes f_{j})
in the direction
\widetilde{Q}_{ij}(f_{i}\otimes f_{j})
is given by

\big\langle\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}_{ij}^{\prime}\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right),\widetilde{Q}_{ij}\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)\big\rangle=-2c_{ij}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{2}}\!\!\!\,\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla_{i,j}\left(A(v_{i}-v_{j})\,\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla$\cr\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{$/$}\crcr}}}_{i,j}\mkern-2.5mu\log\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)\right):\,\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla_{i,j}\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla$\cr\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{$/$}\crcr}}}_{i,j}\mkern-2.5mu\log\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right).

(2.5)

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\big\langle\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}_{ij}^{\prime}\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right),\widetilde{Q}_{ij}\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{2}}2\left(\,\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla_{i,j}\left(\frac{\widetilde{Q}_{ij}\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)}{f_{i}\otimes f_{j}}\right)\cdot\,\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla_{i,j}\,\log\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)\right)\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{2}}\widetilde{Q}_{ij}\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)\left\lvert\,\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla_{i,j}\,\log\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)\right\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle\eqqcolon\text{T}_{1}+\text{T}_{2}.

Throughout the proof, all integrations by parts are justified the regularity provided by
Lemma

2.7
; we will not comment on this in the sequel.

We begin with the term
\text{T}_{2}
, integrating by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle\text{T}_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{2}}\widetilde{Q}_{ij}\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)\,\left\lvert\,\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla_{i,j}\,\log\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)\right\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{2}}\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla$\cr\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{$/$}\crcr}}}_{i,j}\mkern-2.5mu\cdot\left(c_{ij}A(v_{i}-v_{j})\,\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla$\cr\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{$/$}\crcr}}}_{i,j}\mkern-2.5mu\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)\right)\left\lvert\,\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla_{i,j}\,\log\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)\right\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle=-2c_{ij}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{2}}\,\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla_{i,j}\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla$\cr\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{$/$}\crcr}}}_{i,j}\mkern-2.5mu\log\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right):\left(\,\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla_{i,j}\,\log\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)\otimes A(v_{i}-v_{j})\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla$\cr\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{$/$}\crcr}}}_{i,j}\mkern-2.5mu\log\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)\right)\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right).

Next we treat the term
\text{T}_{1}
. Writing
A=A(v_{i}-v_{j})
we have the identity

\displaystyle\frac{\widetilde{Q}_{ij}\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)}{f_{i}\otimes f_{j}}=c_{ij}\left(\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla$\cr\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{$/$}\crcr}}}_{i,j}\mkern-2.5mu\cdot\left(A\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla$\cr\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{$/$}\crcr}}}_{i,j}\mkern-2.5mu\log\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)\right)+\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla$\cr\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{$/$}\crcr}}}_{i,j}\mkern-2.5mu\log\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)\cdot A\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla$\cr\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{$/$}\crcr}}}_{i,j}\mkern-2.5mu\log\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)\right),

which allows us to decompose
\text{T}_{1}
as

\displaystyle\text{T}_{1}

\displaystyle=2c_{ij}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{2}}\left(\,\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla_{i,j}\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla$\cr\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{$/$}\crcr}}}_{i,j}\mkern-2.5mu\cdot\left(A\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla$\cr\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{$/$}\crcr}}}_{i,j}\mkern-2.5mu\log\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)\right)\cdot\,\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla_{i,j}\,\log\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)\right)\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+2c_{ij}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{2}}\left(\,\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla_{i,j}(\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla$\cr\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{$/$}\crcr}}}_{i,j}\mkern-2.5mu\log\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)\cdot A\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla$\cr\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{$/$}\crcr}}}_{i,j}\mkern-2.5mu\log\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right))\cdot\,\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla_{i,j}\,\log\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)\right)\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)

\displaystyle\eqqcolon T_{1,1}+T_{1,2}.

Integrating by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle\text{T}_{1,1}

\displaystyle=2c_{ij}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{2}}\,\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla_{i,j}\left(\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla$\cr\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{$/$}\crcr}}}_{i,j}\mkern-2.5mu\cdot\left(A\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla$\cr\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{$/$}\crcr}}}_{i,j}\mkern-2.5mu\log\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)\right)\right)\cdot\,\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla_{i,j}\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=-2c_{ij}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{2}}\,\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla_{i,j}\left(A\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla$\cr\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{$/$}\crcr}}}_{i,j}\mkern-2.5mu\log\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)\right):\,\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla_{i,j}\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla$\cr\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{$/$}\crcr}}}_{i,j}\mkern-2.5mu\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=-2c_{ij}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{2}}\,\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla_{i,j}\left(A\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla$\cr\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{$/$}\crcr}}}_{i,j}\mkern-2.5mu\log\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)\right):\,\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla_{i,j}\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla$\cr\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{$/$}\crcr}}}_{i,j}\mkern-2.5mu\log\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-2c_{ij}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{2}}\,\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla_{i,j}\left(A\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla$\cr\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{$/$}\crcr}}}_{i,j}\mkern-2.5mu\log\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)\right):\left(\,\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla_{i,j}\,\log\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)\,\otimes\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla$\cr\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{$/$}\crcr}}}_{i,j}\mkern-2.5mu\log\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)\right)\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right),

where we used

\frac{\,\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla_{i,j}\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla$\cr\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{$/$}\crcr}}}_{i,j}\mkern-2.5mu\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)}{f_{i}\otimes f_{j}}=\,\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla_{i,j}\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla$\cr\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{$/$}\crcr}}}_{i,j}\mkern-2.5mu\log\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)+\,\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla_{i,j}\,\log\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)\,\otimes\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla$\cr\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{$/$}\crcr}}}_{i,j}\mkern-2.5mu\log\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right).

By the product rule,

\displaystyle\text{T}_{1,2}

\displaystyle=2c_{ij}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{2}}\,\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla_{i,j}\left(A\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla$\cr\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{$/$}\crcr}}}_{i,j}\mkern-2.5mu\log\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)\right):\left(\,\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla_{i,j}\,\log\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)\otimes\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla$\cr\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{$/$}\crcr}}}_{i,j}\mkern-2.5mu\log\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)\right)\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+2c_{ij}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{2}}\,\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla_{i,j}\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla$\cr\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{$/$}\crcr}}}_{i,j}\mkern-2.5mu\log\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right):\left(\,\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla_{i,j}\,\log\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)\otimes A\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla$\cr\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{$/$}\crcr}}}_{i,j}\mkern-2.5mu\log\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)\right)\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right).

Collecting the identities for
\text{T}_{2}
,
\text{T}_{1,1}
and
\text{T}_{1,2}
yields (
2.5
).
∎

Now we introduce two new physical variables, namely the relative velocity and the center-of-momentum frame; for a fixed pair
(i,j)
we set

z\coloneqq v_{i}-v_{j},\quad z^{*}\coloneqq\frac{m_{i}v_{i}+m_{j}v_{j}}{M_{ij}},\quad M_{ij}\coloneqq m_{i}+m_{j},\quad\alpha_{ij}\coloneqq\frac{1}{m_{i}}+\frac{1}{m_{j}}.

(2.6)

Then the transformation
(v_{i},v_{j})\mapsto(z,z^{*})
is volume-preserving and we obtain

\,\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla_{i,j}\mapsto\left(\frac{\nabla_{z}}{\sqrt{m_{i}}}+\frac{\sqrt{m_{i}}}{M_{ij}}\nabla_{z^{*}},-\frac{\nabla_{z}}{\sqrt{m_{j}}}+\frac{\sqrt{m_{j}}}{M_{ij}}\nabla_{z^{*}}\right),\quad\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla$\cr\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{$/$}\crcr}}}_{i,j}\mkern-2.5mu\>\,\mapsto\alpha_{ij}\nabla_{z}.

Although the function
\smash[b]{\underline{f}}_{ij}(z,z_{*})\coloneq\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)(v_{i},v_{j})
is no longer a tensor product in these variables, we obtain convenient formulas such as

\displaystyle\,\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla_{i,j}\left(A(v_{i}-v_{j})\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla$\cr\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{$/$}\crcr}}}_{i,j}\mkern-2.5mu\log\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)\right)=\alpha_{ij}

\displaystyle\Bigg(\frac{\nabla_{z}}{\sqrt{m_{i}}}\left(A(z)\nabla_{z}\log\smash[b]{\underline{f}}_{ij}\right)+\frac{\sqrt{m_{i}}}{M_{ij}}\nabla_{z^{*}}\nabla_{z}\log\smash[b]{\underline{f}}_{ij}A(z),

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\nabla_{z}}{\sqrt{m_{j}}}\left(A(z)\nabla_{z}\log\smash[b]{\underline{f}}_{ij}\right)+\frac{\sqrt{m_{j}}}{M_{ij}}\nabla_{z^{*}}\nabla_{z}\log\smash[b]{\underline{f}}_{ij}A(z)\Bigg)

and

\,\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla_{i,j}\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla$\cr\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{$/$}\crcr}}}_{i,j}\mkern-2.5mu\log\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)\\
=\alpha_{ij}\left(\frac{\nabla_{z}^{2}}{\sqrt{m_{i}}}\log\smash[b]{\underline{f}}_{ij}+\frac{\sqrt{m_{i}}}{M_{ij}}\nabla_{z^{*}}\nabla_{z}\log\smash[b]{\underline{f}}_{ij}A(z),-\frac{\nabla_{z}^{2}}{\sqrt{m_{j}}}\log\smash[b]{\underline{f}}_{ij}+\frac{\sqrt{m_{j}}}{M_{ij}}\nabla_{z^{*}}\nabla_{z}\log\smash[b]{\underline{f}}_{ij}A(z)\right).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\big\langle\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}_{ij}^{\prime}\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right),\widetilde{Q}_{ij}\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)\big\rangle

\displaystyle=-2c_{ij}\alpha_{ij}^{3}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{2}}\nabla_{z}^{2}\log\smash[b]{\underline{f}}_{ij}:\left(\nabla_{z}\left(A(z)\nabla_{z}\log\smash[b]{\underline{f}}_{ij}\right)\right)\smash[b]{\underline{f}}_{ij}

(2.7)

\displaystyle\quad-2c_{ij}\frac{\alpha_{ij}^{2}}{M_{ij}}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{2}}\nabla_{z^{*}}\nabla_{z}\log\smash[b]{\underline{f}}_{ij}:\left(\nabla_{z^{*}}\nabla_{z}\log\smash[b]{\underline{f}}_{ij}A(z)\right)\smash[b]{\underline{f}}_{ij}.

(2.8)

\displaystyle\eqqcolon\text{I}_{ij}+\text{II}_{ij}.

Note that the second term (
2.8
) is non-positive since the symmetric matrix
A
is positive semi-definite;

\big\langle\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}_{ij}^{\prime}\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right),\widetilde{Q}_{ij}\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)\big\rangle\leq\text{I}_{ij}.

In the regime
-2\geq\gamma\geq-\sqrt{8}
, we also show that
\text{I}_{ij}
is non-positive.

Definition 2.4
.

We define the optimal constant
\Lambda_{3}
in the log-Sobolev inequality, for any function
\psi
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\Gamma_{2}(\log\psi,\log\psi)\psi\,\,\mathrm{d}{\omega}\geq\Lambda_{3}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\left\lvert\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\psi\right\rvert^{2}\psi\,\mathrm{d}{\omega},

(2.9)

and
\Lambda_{3}^{\mathrm{sym}}
as the optimal constant under the additional symmetry constraint
\psi(z)=\psi(-z)
. Note that
\Lambda_{3}=2
and
\Lambda_{3}^{\mathrm{sym}}\geq 5.5
, see
[
28
, Remark 1.4 and Theorem 1.1]
).

Proposition 2.5

(Coercivity of the two-species dissipation)
.

In the setting of
Lemma

2.3
,

D_{\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}_{ij},\widetilde{Q}_{ij}}(f_{i}\otimes f_{j})\coloneqq-\big\langle\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}^{\prime}_{ij}\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right),\widetilde{Q}_{ij}\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)\big\rangle

whenever
-2\geq\gamma\geq-\sqrt{8}
. Moreover, setting
C_{ij}\coloneqq\Lambda_{3}\mathds{1}_{j\neq N+i}+\Lambda_{3}^{\mathrm{sym}}\mathds{1}_{j=N+i}
, we have the lower bound

\displaystyle D_{\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}_{ij},\widetilde{Q}_{ij}}\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)

\displaystyle\geq 2c_{ij}\alpha_{ij}^{3}\int_{({\mathbb{R}^{3}})^{2}}|z|^{2+\gamma}\left\lvert\Pi^{\perp}_{z}\nabla_{z}\left(\hat{z}\cdot\nabla_{z}\log\smash[b]{\underline{f}}_{ij}\right)+\frac{\gamma}{2|z|}\Pi^{\perp}_{z}\nabla_{z}\log\smash[b]{\underline{f}}_{ij}\right\rvert^{2}\smash[b]{\underline{f}}_{ij}\,\,\mathrm{d}{z}\,\mathrm{d}{z^{*}}

\displaystyle\quad+2c_{ij}\alpha_{ij}^{3}\left(C_{ij}-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4}\right)\int_{({\mathbb{R}^{3}})^{2}}|z|^{\gamma}\left\lvert\Pi^{\perp}_{z}\nabla_{z}\log\smash[b]{\underline{f}}_{ij}(z,z^{*})\right\rvert^{2}\smash[b]{\underline{f}}_{ij}(z,z^{*})\,\,\mathrm{d}{z}\,\mathrm{d}{z^{*}}

\displaystyle\quad+2c_{ij}\frac{\alpha_{ij}^{2}}{M_{ij}}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{2}}\nabla_{z^{*}}\nabla_{z}\log\smash[b]{\underline{f}}_{ij}(z,z^{*}):\left(\nabla_{z^{*}}\nabla_{z}\log\smash[b]{\underline{f}}_{ij}(z,z^{*})A(z)\right)\smash[b]{\underline{f}}_{ij}(z,z^{*})\,\,\mathrm{d}{z}\,\mathrm{d}{z^{*}}.

Proof.

To compute the contraction in (
2.7
), namely
\text{I}_{ij}
, we use the notation of Appendix
A
;

\nabla_{z}\coloneqq\hat{z}\partial_{|z|}+\frac{1}{|z|}\nabla_{\hat{z}},\quad\hat{z}\coloneq\frac{z}{|z|},\quad\partial_{|z|}\coloneqq\hat{z}\cdot\nabla_{z},\quad\nabla_{\hat{z}}\coloneqq|z|\Pi^{\perp}_{z}\nabla_{z}.

We denote
\overline{g}(r,\omega,z^{*})\coloneqq\overline{f}_{ij}(r,\omega,z^{*})\coloneqq\smash[b]{\underline{f}}_{ij}(z,z^{*})\eqqcolon g(z,z^{*})
in spherical variables
(z,z^{*})=(r\omega,z^{*})
for readability. Combining
Lemma

A.2
with
Lemma

A.3
, recalling that
\hat{z}\cdot\nabla_{\hat{z}}=0
and
\Pi^{\perp}_{z}\hat{z}=0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\nabla_{z}^{2}\log g

\displaystyle:\nabla_{z}\left(|z|^{2+\gamma}\Pi^{\perp}_{z}\nabla_{z}\log g\right)

courtesy of

(2.12)

2.3
Local well-posedness

To derive global-in-time well-posedness of the multi-species Landau equation from the monotonicity of the Fisher information, we first establish local well-posedness. The proof is similar to the single-species case, and therefore we are brief on some of the details.

Lemma 2.7

(Local well-posedness)
.

Let
l\geq 5
and
f^{\circ}_{i}\in L^{2}_{l}\cap L\log L
be probability densities for
1\leq i\leq N
. Then for some
T>0
depending only on
\|f_{i}^{\circ}\|_{L^{2}_{l}}
,
\mathcal{H}(F_{N}^{\circ})
, there exists a unique strong solution
f_{i}\in L^{\infty}([0,T],L^{2}_{l})\cap C^{\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{3})
to the system

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N}Q_{ij}(f_{i},f_{j}),\quad f_{i}(0,v)=f_{i}^{\circ}(v),

(2.16)

which satisfies

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|f_{i}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{l}}+c\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{T}\|f_{i}\|^{2}_{H^{1}_{l+\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|f_{i}^{\circ}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{l}}.

(2.17)

Moreover, the entropy is non-increasing,

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{H}(F_{N})

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{D}(F_{N})=-\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}D_{ij}(f_{i},f_{j}),

(2.18)

where

D_{ij}(f_{i},f_{j})\coloneqq-\frac{1}{2}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{2}}\left(\left(\Pi^{\perp}_{v_{i}-v_{j}}|v_{i}-v_{j}|^{2+\gamma}\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla$\cr\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{$/$}\crcr}}}_{i,j}\mkern-2.5mu\log\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)\right)\cdot\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla$\cr\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{$/$}\crcr}}}_{i,j}\mkern-2.5mu\log\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)\right)\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)\,\mathrm{d}{v_{i}}\,\mathrm{d}{v_{j}}.

Proof.

The existence of the solution follows by a standard regularization argument, along with the conservation of mass, total momentum and energy. For the regularity estimate, we consider the time derivative

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{i}^{2}(v)\langle v\rangle^{l}\,\,\mathrm{d}{v}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{i}(v)\,Q_{ij}(f_{i},f_{j})\,\langle v\rangle^{l}\,\,\mathrm{d}{v}.

We then estimate each term in the sum separately. By definition of
Q_{ij}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{i}(v)

\displaystyle Q_{ij}(f_{i},f_{j})\langle v\rangle^{l}\,\,\mathrm{d}{v}

\displaystyle\,=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{i}(v_{i})\,\frac{\nabla_{v_{i}}}{m_{i}}\,\cdot\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}c_{ij}\,A(v_{i}-v_{j})\,\left(\frac{\nabla_{v_{i}}}{m_{i}}-\frac{\nabla_{v_{j}}}{m_{j}}\right)\left(f_{i}\otimes f_{j}\right)(v_{i},v_{j})\,\,\mathrm{d}v_{j}\right)\langle v_{i}\rangle^{l}\,\,\mathrm{d}{v_{i}}

\displaystyle\,=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathcal{A}_{ij}[f_{j}]:\left(\frac{{\nabla f_{i}}}{m_{i}}\right)^{\otimes 2}\langle v\rangle^{l}\,\,\mathrm{d}{v}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathcal{A}_{ij}[f_{j}]:\left(\frac{\nabla f_{i}}{m_{i}}\otimes\frac{\nabla\left(\langle v\rangle^{l}\right)}{m_{i}}\right)f_{i}\,\,\mathrm{d}{v}

\displaystyle\quad\,+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(\frac{\nabla}{m_{j}}\cdot\mathcal{A}_{ij}[f_{j}]\right)\cdot\frac{\nabla}{m_{i}}\left(f_{i}\,\langle v\rangle^{l}\right)f_{i}\,\,\mathrm{d}{v}

\displaystyle\,\eqqcolon-\text{I}_{ij}+\text{II}_{ij}+\text{III}_{ij}.

where the operator
\mathcal{A}_{ij}
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{ij}[g](v)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}c_{ij}\Pi_{v-v^{*}}^{\perp}|v-v^{*}|^{2+\gamma}g(v^{*})\,\mathrm{d}v^{*}.

Since
f_{j}
are probability densities whose energy and entropy are bounded by the total energy and entropy of the system, we have the classical estimate, see
[
11
]
,

\displaystyle\text{I}_{ij}\geq c\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla f_{i}(v)|^{2}\langle v\rangle^{l+\gamma}\,\,\mathrm{d}{v},

where
c>0
depends on the total energy and entropy of the initial data. Using standard interpolation estimates, making use of
l\geq 5
, we bound

\displaystyle|\text{II}_{ij}|+|\text{III}_{ij}|\leq\frac{1}{4}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\text{I}_{ij}+C\big(1+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{2}_{l}}\big)^{\beta}\|f_{i}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{l}},

where
\beta>0
is a constant depending on
\gamma
. For the quantity

\displaystyle y(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{i}^{2}(v)\langle v\rangle^{l}\,\mathrm{d}{v},

this yields the differential inequality

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}y(t)+\frac{3}{4}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\text{I}_{ij}\leq C\langle y\rangle^{1+\beta}(t).

Hence, for some
T>0
we obtain the claimed estimate.
∎

Corollary 2.8

(Production of Fisher information)
.

Let
l\geq 7
and the initial data
f^{\circ}_{i}\in L^{2}_{l}\cap L\log L
be probability densities for
1\leq i\leq N
, and
f_{i}\in L^{\infty}([0,T],L^{2}_{l})\cap C^{\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{3})
be the solution from
Lemma

2.7
. Then
t\in(0,T)
a.e. we have for all
1\leq i\leq N
,

\displaystyle\|f_{i}\|_{H^{2}_{k}}\leq C_{T}<\infty,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{I}(f_{i})\leq C_{T}<\infty,

in particular the Fisher information
\mathcal{I}(F)
is finite.

Proof.

Lemma

2.7
shows that almost everywhere in
(0,T)
we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla f_{i}(t^{*})\|_{L^{2}_{l+\gamma}}<\infty,\quad\text{for all }1\leq i\leq N.

We now derive energy estimates for
\partial_{k}f_{i}
for any
k\in\{1,2,3\}
. The equation for the partial derivative reads

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\partial_{k}f_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}Q_{ij}(\partial_{k}f_{i},f_{j})+Q_{ij}(f_{i},\partial_{k}f_{j}).

We apply the same argument as in
Lemma

2.7
, which yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\partial_{k}f_{i})^{2}(v)\langle v\rangle^{l}\,\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\partial_{k}f_{i}(v)\left(Q_{ij}(\partial_{k}f_{i},f_{j})+Q_{ji}(f_{i},\partial_{k}f_{j})\right)\langle v\rangle^{l}\,\mathrm{d}v.

As before, we can separate the contributions from the individual species
1\leq j\leq N
. Using the notation from above, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\partial_{k}f_{i}(v)\left(Q_{ij}(\partial_{k}f_{i},f_{j})\right.

\displaystyle+\left.Q_{ji}(f_{i},\partial_{k}f_{j})\right)\langle v\rangle^{l}\,\mathrm{d}v=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\nabla\partial_{k}f_{i}}{m_{i}}\mathcal{A}[f_{j}]\frac{\nabla\partial_{k}f_{i}}{m_{i}}\langle v_{i}\rangle^{l}\,\mathrm{d}v_{i}

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\nabla\partial_{k}f_{i}}{m_{i}}\mathcal{A}[f_{j}]\partial_{k}f_{i}\frac{\nabla}{m_{i}}\langle v_{i}\rangle^{l}\,\mathrm{d}v_{i}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\partial_{k}f_{i}\frac{\nabla_{v_{j}}}{m_{j}}\cdot\mathcal{A}[f_{j}]\frac{\nabla}{m_{i}}\big(\partial_{k}f_{i}\langle v_{i}\rangle^{l}\big)\,\mathrm{d}v_{i}

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\nabla f_{i}}{m_{i}}\mathcal{A}[\partial_{k}f_{j}]\frac{\nabla}{m_{i}}\big(\partial_{k}f_{i}\langle v_{i}\rangle^{l}\big)\,\mathrm{d}v_{i}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{i}\frac{\nabla_{v_{j}}}{m_{j}}\cdot\mathcal{A}[\partial_{k}f_{j}]\frac{\nabla}{m_{i}}\big(\partial_{k}f_{i}\langle v_{i}\rangle^{l}\big)\,\mathrm{d}v_{i}

\displaystyle\eqqcolon-\text{I}_{ij}+\text{II}_{ij}+\text{III}_{ij}+\text{IV}_{ij}+\text{V}_{ij}.

We again use the lower bound for the matrix
A
in terms of total energy and entropy, and obtain the bound

\displaystyle\text{I}_{ij}\geq c\|\partial_{k}\nabla f_{i}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{l+\gamma}}.

The remaining terms can be bounded by straightforward interpolation arguments to get the total bound

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\nabla f_{i}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{l}}+c\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\nabla^{2}f_{i}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{l+\gamma}}\leq C\big(1+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\nabla f_{i}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{l}}\big)^{1+\beta},

for some constant
\beta>0
. The upper bound for the solution of the corresponding ODE yields, after possibly choosing a smaller
T>0
, the bound

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla^{2}f_{i}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{l+\gamma}}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq C_{T}<\infty.

To conclude the finiteness of the Fisher information, we recall that for any
k>3
we can estimate

\displaystyle I(f)=4\|\sqrt{f}\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{1}}\leq C_{k}\|f\|_{H^{2}_{k}},

see
[
39
, Lemma 1]
(observe the slightly different convention for
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}_{l}}
therein). This concludes the proof.
∎

This local result is complemented by a control of the growth of the moments of the solution.

Lemma 2.9

(Growth bound for the moments)
.

Let
l\in\mathbb{N}
and the initial data
f^{\circ}_{i}\in L^{2}_{l}\cap L^{1}_{2l}\cap L\log L
be probability densities and let
F_{N}=\left(f_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{N}
be the unique local strong solution from
Lemma

2.7
. Then
f_{i}(t)
are probability densities and the total momentum and total energy are conserved,

\displaystyle M(t)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}m_{i}v\,f_{i}(t,v)\,\,\mathrm{d}{v}=M(0),

\displaystyle E(t)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{2}m_{i}|v|^{2}\,f_{i}(t,v)\,\,\mathrm{d}{v}=E(0).

The higher-order moments satisfy the growth estimate

\displaystyle\|f_{i}\|_{L^{1}_{l}}\leq C_{l}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|f_{i}^{\circ}\|_{L^{1}_{l}}+C_{l}t^{1+\lceil{\frac{l-4}{2}\rceil}}.

(2.19)

Remark 2.10
.

For
l\leq 4
, the estimate (
2.19
) coincides with the bound for the single species case, see
[
6
]
. On the other hand, for
l>4
the estimates are likely not sharp.

Proof of
Lemma

2.9
.

Conservation of total mass and momentum follows from the fact that these quantities are preserved by each collision operator
Q_{ij}
. The growth bound for higher-order moments requires some care due to the asymmetry of the multi-species equation. Nevertheless, we can largely follow the strategy of
[
6
]
. Let
l\geq 0
, and consider the
total

l
-th moment

\displaystyle M_{l}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}m_{i}\langle v\rangle^{l}f_{i}(v)\,\,\mathrm{d}{v},

(2.20)

and its time derivative

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}M_{l}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}m_{i}\langle v\rangle^{l}Q_{ij}(f_{i},f_{j})\,\,\mathrm{d}{v}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}m_{i}\langle v\rangle^{l}\,Q_{ii}(f_{i},f_{i})\,\,\mathrm{d}{v}+\sum_{i<j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}m_{i}\langle v\rangle^{l}Q_{ij}(f_{i},f_{j})+m_{j}\langle v\rangle^{l}Q_{ji}(f_{j},f_{i})\,\,\mathrm{d}{v}

\displaystyle\eqqcolon\sum_{i=1}^{N}K_{i}+\sum_{i<j}K_{ij}.

The terms
K_{i}
can be treated as in
[
6
, Lemma 8]
, yielding for some
C>0

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}K_{i}\leq\mathcal{D}(F_{N})M_{l}+CM_{l-4}+C,

where
\mathcal{D}
is the dissipation functional introduced in (
2.18
).
It remains to estimate the cross-interaction terms
K_{ij}
. Due to the
m_{i}
-weights in (
2.20
), the terms
K_{ij}
,
i\neq j
, take the form

\displaystyle K_{ij}=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}(f_{i}\otimes f_{j})|v_{i}-v_{j}|^{2+\gamma}\left(\Pi^{\perp}_{v_{i}-v_{j}}\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla$\cr\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{$/$}\crcr}}}_{i,j}\mkern-2.5mu\log(f_{i}\otimes f_{j})\right)\cdot\left(\nabla\langle v_{i}\rangle^{l}-\nabla\langle v_{j}\rangle^{l}\right)\,\mathrm{d}v_{i}\,\mathrm{d}v_{j}.

As in the single-species case, we introduce a smooth, radially symmetric cutoff function
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}
satisfying
\mathds{1}_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}}\leq\chi\leq\mathds{1}_{B_{1}}
.

We then decompose
K_{ij}
into a contribution near the singularity and a far-field part:

\displaystyle K_{ij}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}(f_{i}\otimes f_{j})\chi(v_{i}-v_{j})|v_{i}-v_{j}|^{2+\gamma}\Pi^{\perp}_{v_{i}-v_{j}}\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla$\cr\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{$/$}\crcr}}}_{i,j}\mkern-2.5mu\log(f_{i}\otimes f_{j})\cdot\left(\nabla\langle v_{i}\rangle^{l}-\nabla\langle v_{j}\rangle^{l}\right)\,\mathrm{d}v_{i}\,\mathrm{d}v_{j}

\displaystyle\quad\,-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}(f_{i}\otimes f_{j})(1-\chi)(v_{i}-v_{j})|v_{i}-v_{j}|^{2+\gamma}\Pi^{\perp}_{v_{i}-v_{j}}\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla$\cr\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{$/$}\crcr}}}_{i,j}\mkern-2.5mu\log(f_{i}\otimes f_{j})\cdot\left(\nabla\langle v_{i}\rangle^{l}-\nabla\langle v_{j}\rangle^{l}\right)\,\mathrm{d}v_{i}\,\mathrm{d}v_{j}

\displaystyle=:K_{ij,1}+K_{ij,2}.

The first term can be estimated exactly as in the single-species case, giving

\displaystyle|K_{ij,1}|\leq 2\mathcal{D}_{ij}M_{l}+Cl^{-\gamma}M_{l-4}.

To treat the term
K_{ij,2}
, we integrate by parts and obtain

\displaystyle K_{ij,2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}(f_{i}\otimes f_{j})(1-\chi)|v_{i}-v_{j}|^{2+\gamma}\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla$\cr\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{$/$}\crcr}}}_{i,j}\mkern-2.5mu\big(\Pi^{\perp}_{v_{i}-v_{j}}(\nabla\langle v_{i}\rangle^{l}-\nabla\langle v_{j}\rangle^{l}\big)\,\mathrm{d}v_{i}\,\mathrm{d}v_{j}

\displaystyle=-\alpha_{ij}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}(f_{i}\otimes f_{j})(1-\chi)|v_{i}-v_{j}|^{\gamma}(v_{i}-v_{j})\cdot(\nabla\langle v_{i}\rangle^{l}-\nabla\langle v_{j}\rangle^{l})\,\,\mathrm{d}v_{i}\,\mathrm{d}v_{j}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}(f_{i}\otimes f_{j})(1-\chi)|v_{i}-v_{j}|^{2+\gamma}\Pi_{v_{i}-v_{j}}^{\perp}\colon\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla$\cr\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{$/$}\crcr}}}_{i,j}\mkern-2.5mu(\nabla\langle v_{i}\rangle^{l}-\nabla\langle v_{j}\rangle^{l})\,\,\mathrm{d}v_{i}\,\mathrm{d}v_{j}.

Now the first term in
K_{ij,2}
is non-positive since

\displaystyle(v_{i}-v_{j})(\nabla\langle v_{i}\rangle^{l}-\nabla\langle v_{j}\rangle^{l})

\displaystyle\geq 0,

while the second term can be bounded by

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}(f_{i}\otimes f_{j})(1-\chi)|v_{i}-v_{j}|^{2+\gamma}\Pi_{v_{i}-v_{j}}^{\perp}\colon\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\nabla\mkern-12.0mu\nabla$\cr\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{$/$}\crcr}}}_{i,j}\mkern-2.5mu(\nabla\langle v_{i}\rangle^{l}-\nabla\langle v_{j}\rangle^{l})\,\mathrm{d}v_{i}\,\mathrm{d}v_{j}\leq CM_{l-2}

Collecting terms, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}M_{l}(t)\leq\mathcal{D}(F_{N})M_{l}+CM_{l-4}+CM_{l-2}\leq\mathcal{D}(F_{N})M_{l}+CM_{l-2}.

(2.21)

For
l\leq 4
, the moment
M_{l-2}
is uniformly bounded, and therefore

\displaystyle M_{l}(t)\leq CM_{l}(F_{N}^{\circ})+Ct.

(2.22)

For the case
l>4
, it suffices to iteratively insert the estimates obtained from (
2.22
) back into (
2.21
).
∎

2.4

Proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

The global well-posedness follows by combining the results of the previous sections. Let
\ell\geq 7
and
F_{N}^{\circ}
be the initial datum. The local well-posedness result in

yields a strong solution that can be continued as long as

and

remain bounded. Since

is monotone decreasing in time, it remains to provide a bound for the weighted

To this end, we observe that

yields finiteness of the Fisher information for small

establishing the uniform bound and finishing the proof.

3
The two-species Landau system with one species having infinite mass

3.1
Dissipation identities and spherical Fisher information

As advertised in
Theorem

1.2
, the standard Fisher information is not a Lyapunov functional for the multi-species Landau system in the limiting case of infinite mass, due to its lack of dissipation under the spherical diffusion operator

Lf(v)\coloneqq\nabla\cdot\left(|v|^{2+\gamma}\Pi^{\perp}_{v}\nabla f\right)

whenever
\gamma<-\sqrt{8}
. We will prove this in the next
Section

3.2
. However, its (weighted) spherical part

J_{\beta}(f)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\left\lvert\Pi^{\perp}_{v}\nabla\log f\right\rvert^{2}}{|v|^{\beta}}f\,\,\mathrm{d}{v},\quad\beta\geq 0,

does dissipate along the flow generated by
Lf
.

Lemma 3.1

(Dissipation of the spherical Fisher information along
L
)
.

Let
f
be a smooth probability density. The dissipation

D_{J_{\beta},L}(f)\coloneqq-\big\langle J_{\beta}^{\prime}(f),Lf\big\rangle

is given by

D_{J_{\beta},L}(f)=2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v|^{2+\gamma-\beta}\left\lVert\left(\Pi^{\perp}_{v}\nabla\right)^{2}\log f\right\rVert^{2}_{\mathrm{HS}}f\,\,\mathrm{d}{v}.

(3.1)

Proof.

We first note, in view of (
A.3
) and (
A.4
), using spherical variables
v=r\omega
with
(r,\omega)\coloneqq(|v|,\hat{v})
and
\hat{v}\coloneqq v/|v|
, and denoting
\overline{f}(r,\omega)\coloneqq f(v)
, that

Lf=r^{\gamma}\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\overline{f},\quad\frac{Lf}{f}=r^{\gamma}\left(\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{f}+\left\lvert\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{f}\right\rvert^{2}\right),\quad\overline{f}(r,\omega)\coloneq f(v).

Hence,

\displaystyle\big\langle J_{\beta}^{\prime}(f),Lf\big\rangle

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}r^{\gamma-\beta}\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{f}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\left(\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{f}+\left\lvert\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{f}\right\rvert^{2}\right)\overline{f}\,\mathrm{d}{\omega}\,\mathrm{d}{r}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}r^{\gamma-\beta}\left\lvert\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{f}\right\rvert^{2}\left(\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{f}+\left\lvert\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{f}\right\rvert^{2}\right)\overline{f}\,\mathrm{d}{\omega}\,\mathrm{d}{r}.

Integrating by parts yields

\big\langle J_{\beta}^{\prime}(f),Lf\big\rangle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}r^{2+\gamma-\beta}\left(2\left\lvert\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{f}\right\rvert^{4}+3\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\left(\left\lvert\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{f}\right\rvert^{2}\right)\cdot\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{f}-2\left(\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{f}\right)^{2}\right)\overline{f}\,\mathrm{d}{\omega}\,\mathrm{d}{r}.

(3.2)

Recall Bochner’s formula on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
;

\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\left(\left\lvert\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{f}\right\rvert^{2}\right)=\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}^{2}\log\overline{f}\right\rVert^{2}_{\mathrm{HS}}+\left\lvert\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{f}\right\rvert^{2}+\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\left(\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{f}\right)\cdot\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{f}.

Multiplying this identity by
\overline{f}
and integrating by parts, we obtain

-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\left(\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\left(\left\lvert\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{f}\right\rvert^{2}\right)\cdot\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{f}\right)\overline{f}\\
=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\left(\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}^{2}\log\overline{f}\right\rVert^{2}_{\mathrm{HS}}+\left\lvert\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{f}\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{f}\right\rvert^{4}+\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\left(\left\lvert\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{f}\right\rvert^{2}\right)\cdot\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{f}-\left(\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{f}\right)^{2}\right)\overline{f},

whose convenient form is

\frac{3}{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\left(\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\left(\left\lvert\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{f}\right\rvert^{2}\right)\cdot\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{f}\right)\overline{f}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\left(\left(\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{f}\right)^{2}-\Gamma_{2}(\log\overline{f},\log\overline{f})-\left\lvert\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{f}\right\rvert^{4}\right)\overline{f},

(3.3)

and inserting (
3.3
) into (
3.2
) yields

\big\langle J_{\beta}^{\prime}(f),Lf\big\rangle=-2\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}r^{\gamma-\beta}\Gamma_{2}\left(\log\overline{f},\log\overline{f}\right)\overline{f}\,\,\mathrm{d}{\omega}\,\mathrm{d}{r}.

(3.4)

The result follows recalling (
A.6
).
∎

Proposition 3.3

(Evolution of the Fisher information along
L
)
.

Let
f
be a smooth probability density. Then

\displaystyle\big\langle I^{\prime}(f),Lf\big\rangle=

\displaystyle-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v|^{2+\gamma}\left\lvert\Pi^{\perp}_{v}\nabla\left(\hat{v}\cdot\nabla\log f\right)+\frac{\gamma}{2|v|}\Pi^{\perp}_{v}\nabla\log f\right\rvert^{2}f\,\,\mathrm{d}{v}

Proof.

We decompose

\displaystyle\big\langle I^{\prime}(f),Lf\big\rangle

\displaystyle=2\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\partial_{r}\log\overline{f}\,\partial_{r}\left(r^{\gamma}\left(\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{f}+\left\lvert\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{f}\right\rvert^{2}\right)\right)\overline{f}\,r^{2}\,\mathrm{d}{r}\,\mathrm{d}{\omega}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{\gamma}\left(\partial_{r}\log\overline{f}\right)^{2}\left(\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{f}+\left\lvert\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{f}\right\rvert^{2}\right)\overline{f}\,r^{2}\,\mathrm{d}{r}\,\mathrm{d}{\omega}

\displaystyle\quad+\big\langle J_{0}^{\prime}(f),Lf\big\rangle.

Integrating by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle 2\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\partial_{r}\log\overline{f}\,

\displaystyle\partial_{r}\left(r^{\gamma}\left(\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{f}+\left\lvert\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{f}\right\rvert^{2}\right)\right)\overline{f}\,\mathrm{d}{\omega}

\displaystyle=2\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\partial_{r}\log\overline{f}\left(\gamma r^{\gamma-1}+\partial_{r}\right)\left(\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{f}+\left\lvert\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{f}\right\rvert^{2}\right)\overline{f}\,\,\mathrm{d}{\omega}

\displaystyle=2\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}r^{\gamma}\left(\frac{1}{2}\partial_{r}\log\overline{f}\,\partial_{r}\left(\left\lvert\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{f}\right\rvert^{2}\right)-\left\lvert\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\partial_{r}\log\overline{f}\right\rvert^{2}-\frac{\gamma}{r}\partial_{r}\left(\left\lvert\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{f}\right\rvert^{2}\right)\right)\overline{f}\,\mathrm{d}{\omega}

and

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}r^{\gamma}\left(\partial_{r}\log\overline{f}\right)^{2}\left(\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{f}+\left\lvert\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{f}\right\rvert^{2}\right)\overline{f}\,\mathrm{d}{\omega}\\
=-\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}r^{\gamma}\partial_{r}\log\overline{f}\,\partial_{r}\left(\left\lvert\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{f}\right\rvert^{2}\right)\overline{f}\,\mathrm{d}{\omega}.

Completing the square

\left\lvert\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\partial_{r}\log\overline{f}\right\rvert^{2}+\frac{\gamma}{r}\partial_{r}\left(\left\lvert\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{f}\right\rvert^{2}\right)=\left\lvert\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\partial_{r}\log\overline{f}+\frac{\gamma}{2r}\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{f}\right\rvert^{2}-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4r^{2}}\left\lvert\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{f}\right\rvert^{2},

courtesy of (
3.4
), we are left with

\displaystyle\big\langle I^{\prime}(f),Lf\big\rangle

\displaystyle=-2\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{\gamma}\left\lvert r\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\partial_{r}\log\overline{f}+\frac{\gamma}{2}\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{f}\right\rvert^{2}\overline{f}\,\,\mathrm{d}{r}\,\mathrm{d}{\omega}

\displaystyle\quad-2\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{\gamma}\left(\Gamma_{2}\left(\log\overline{f},\log\overline{f}\right)-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4}\left\lvert\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{f}\right\rvert^{2}\right)\overline{f}\,\,\mathrm{d}{r}\,\mathrm{d}{\omega}.

∎

Lemma 3.4

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\left\lvert\Pi^{\perp}_{v}\nabla\log f\right\rvert^{2}}{\left\lvert v\right\rvert}f\right)^{1/3}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\left\lvert\Pi^{\perp}_{v}\nabla\log f\right\rvert^{2}}{\left\lvert v\right\rvert^{4}}f\right)^{2/3}.

Hence, recalling
Lemma

3.2
and (
3.5
), we get by Young’s inequality for any
\eta>0
,

\big\langle I^{\prime}(f),L_{S}f\big\rangle\leq 2^{-5/3}\left(\eta^{-2}D_{H,L_{S}}(f)\right)^{1/3}\left(\eta\,D_{J_{1},L_{S}}(f)\right)^{2/3}\leq 2^{-5/3}\left(\frac{1}{3\eta^{2}}D_{H,L_{S}}(f)+\frac{2\eta}{3}D_{J_{1},L_{S}}(f)\right).

(3.18)

Now, we can control the evolution of the new functional
\Lambda
. For this, we assume that

1+\Lambda(f)\leq C^{\circ}R

(3.19)

for some constant
C^{\circ}>0
large enough; note that this holds initially at time
t=0
with

C^{\circ}\coloneqq 1+I(f^{\circ})+J(f^{\circ})+H\!\left(f^{\circ}|\mathcal{M}_{T^{\circ},1}\right)

if we furthermore assume that
a\leq 1
and
R\geq 1
. Then, its time derivative is given by

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle\Lambda(f)

\displaystyle=-D_{I,Q_{\gamma}}(f)+c_{ei}\big\langle I^{\prime},L_{S}(f)\big\rangle+a\big\langle J^{\prime}_{1},Q_{\gamma}(f,f)\big\rangle-ac_{ei}D_{J_{1},L_{S}}(f)-RD_{H,Q_{\gamma}}(f,f)-Rc_{ei}D_{H,L_{S}}(f)

\displaystyle\leq-D_{I,Q_{\gamma}}(f)+\frac{c_{ei}}{2^{5/3}}\left(\frac{1}{3\eta^{2}}D_{H,L_{S}}(f)+\frac{2\eta}{3}D_{J_{1},L_{S}}(f)\right)+\frac{aC_{S,\gamma,\Lambda_{3}^{\mathrm{sym}},\delta}\left(C^{\circ}R\right)^{\theta_{\gamma,\delta}}\kappa}{2}D_{J_{1},L_{S}}(f)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{a\,C_{S,\gamma,\Lambda_{3}^{\mathrm{sym}},\delta}\left(C^{\circ}R\right)^{\theta_{\gamma,\delta}}}{2\kappa}D_{I,Q_{\gamma}}(f,f)-ac_{ei}D_{J_{1},L_{S}}(f)-RD_{H,Q_{\gamma}}(f,f)-Rc_{ei}D_{H,L_{S}}(f)

\displaystyle=\,\left(\frac{a\,C_{S,\gamma,\Lambda_{3}^{\mathrm{sym}},\delta}\left(C^{\circ}R\right)^{\theta_{\gamma,\delta}}}{2\kappa}-1\right)D_{I,Q_{\gamma}}(f,f)

(3.20)

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\frac{c_{ei}}{2^{2/3}\,3}\eta+\frac{a\,C_{S,\gamma,\Lambda_{3}^{\mathrm{sym}},\delta}\left(C^{\circ}R\right)^{\theta_{\gamma,\delta}}\,\kappa}{2}-ac_{ei}\right)D_{J_{1},L_{S}}(f)

(3.21)

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\frac{c_{ei}}{2^{5/3}\,3}\,\eta^{-2}-Rc_{ei}\right)D_{H,L_{S}}(f)-RD_{H,Q_{\gamma}}(f,f),

(3.22)

where in the inequality we used the upper bounds (
3.17
) and (
3.18
).

Case 1:
-3<\gamma<-2
.
We choose the parameters
\kappa
and
\eta
such that all prefactors are non-positive. For this, we first set

\kappa\coloneqq a\,C_{S,\gamma,\Lambda_{3}^{\mathrm{sym}},\delta}\left(C^{\circ}R\right)^{\theta_{\gamma,\delta}},\quad\text{hence}\quad\frac{1}{2}=\frac{a\,C_{S,\gamma,\Lambda_{3}^{\mathrm{sym}},\delta}\left(C^{\circ}R\right)^{\theta_{\gamma,\delta}}}{2\kappa}

so that the coefficient (
3.20
) of
D_{I,Q_{\gamma}}(f,f)
becomes non-positive. Second, we set

\eta\coloneqq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{2/3}3R}},\quad\text{hence}\quad\frac{c_{ei}}{2^{5/3}\,3}\,\eta^{-2}=\frac{Rc_{ei}}{2}

so that the coefficient (
3.22
) of
D_{H,Q_{\gamma}}(f,f)
becomes
-R/2
. Third, we need to ensure that the coefficient of
D_{J_{1},L_{S}}(f)
is non-positive; substituting
\kappa
and
\eta
in (
3.21
), this amounts to

\frac{c_{ei}}{6\sqrt{3}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{R}}+\frac{a^{2}\,C^{2}_{S,\gamma,\Lambda_{3}^{\mathrm{sym}},\delta}\left(C^{\circ}R\right)^{2\theta_{\gamma,\delta}}}{2}-ac_{ei}\leq 0.

We can absorb the first term setting
\frac{a}{4}\coloneqq\tfrac{1}{6\sqrt{3}}\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{R}}
so that it remains to verify

a\,C^{2}_{S,\gamma,\Lambda_{3}^{\mathrm{sym}},\delta}\left(C^{\circ}R\right)^{2\theta_{\gamma,\delta}}\leq\frac{c_{ei}}{2}.

(3.23)

In the case where
-3<\gamma<-2
, we can choose
0<\delta<3(3+\gamma)
for which
\theta_{\gamma,\delta}<1/4
, hence (
3.23
) holds for
R>0
large enough. This choice satisfies in particular
\delta<5+\gamma
and the
L^{\infty}
-estimate (
3.16
) is thus valid. Then

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\Lambda(f)\leq-\frac{1}{2}D_{I,Q_{\gamma}}(f,f)-\frac{ac_{ei}}{2}D_{I,L_{S}}(f)-\frac{R}{2}D_{H,Q_{\gamma}}(f,f)-RD_{H,L_{S}}(f)\leq 0.

Recall that we assumed a priori (
3.19
), namely that
1+\Lambda(f)\leq C^{\circ}R
, which holds at time
t=0
. This holds a posteriori for all time since
\Lambda
is non-decreasing given

, which concludes the proof in that case.

Case 2:
\gamma=-3
.
For
\lambda>0
, consider the rescaled function

given by

\displaystyle f_{\lambda}(v)=\lambda^{-3}f(v/\lambda).

Pick
\lambda>0
such that

. By scale-invariance of the equation, it suffices to prove the claim for

, and we consider

C^{\circ}_{\lambda}\coloneqq 1+I(f^{\circ}_{\lambda})+J(f^{\circ}_{\lambda})+H\!\left(f^{\circ}_{\lambda}|\mathcal{M}_{T^{\circ}/\lambda^{2},1}\right).

(3.24)

Further, we remark that for
\gamma=-3

and it remains to determine constants such that

Hence, we obtain monotonicity of the functional

∎

Appendix A
Spherical calculus

In this appendix, we gather some spherical calculus identities that will be used throughout the manuscript.

We decompose the gradient as

\nabla_{z}=\left(\hat{z}\otimes\hat{z}\right)\nabla_{z}+\Pi^{\perp}_{z}\nabla_{z}=\hat{z}\partial_{|z|}+\frac{1}{|z|}\nabla_{\hat{z}},\quad\hat{z}\coloneqq\frac{z}{|z|},\quad\partial_{|z|}\coloneq\hat{z}\cdot\nabla_{z},\quad\nabla_{\hat{z}}\coloneq|z|\Pi^{\perp}_{z}\nabla_{z}.

If we further perform the change of variables
z\mapsto r\omega
with
(r,\omega)\coloneqq(|z|,\hat{z})
, then for scalar functions we have

\nabla_{\hat{z}}\mapsto\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}},\quad\partial_{|z|}\mapsto\partial_{r},\quad\nabla_{z}\mapsto\omega\partial_{r}+\frac{1}{r}\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}},

(A.1)

We denote by
\overline{g}(r,\omega)\coloneqq g(z)
a smooth function on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
in spherical coordinates. The following handy relations hold;

\partial_{|z|}\hat{z}=0,\quad\nabla_{\hat{z}}|z|=0,\quad\nabla_{\hat{z}}\hat{z}=\Pi^{\perp}_{z},\quad\partial_{|z|}\nabla_{\hat{z}}\,g=\nabla_{\hat{z}}\partial_{|z|}\,g.

Most of the relations in the manuscript contain
\log
; we write the sequel of the appendix that way, but these relations are of course general.

Lemma A.1

(Spherical Hessian and Laplacian identities)
.

\frac{1}{r^{2}}\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}^{2}\log\overline{g}=\Pi^{\perp}_{z}\nabla^{2}_{z}\log g\,\Pi^{\perp}_{z}-\frac{1}{|z|}\partial_{|z|}\log g\,\Pi^{\perp}_{z}.

(A.2)

In particular,

\frac{1}{r^{2}}\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{g}=\Pi^{\perp}_{z}:\left(\Pi^{\perp}_{z}\nabla^{2}_{z}\log g\,\Pi^{\perp}_{z}-\frac{1}{|z|}\partial_{|z|}\log g\,\Pi^{\perp}_{z}\right).

(A.3)

Proof.

We have (see e.g.
[
1
]
) that

\left(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}^{2}\log\overline{g}\right)\sigma=\left(\Pi^{\perp}_{z}\nabla^{2}_{z}\log g\right)\sigma+\Pi^{\perp}_{z}D_{\sigma}\Pi^{\perp}_{z}\nabla_{z}\log g,\quad\sigma\in T_{\hat{z}}(\mathbb{S}^{2})\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Now, since
\Pi^{\perp}_{z}\sigma=\sigma
,

\left(\Pi^{\perp}_{z}\nabla^{2}_{z}\log g\right)\sigma=\left(\Pi^{\perp}_{z}\nabla^{2}_{z}\log g\,\Pi^{\perp}_{z}\right)\sigma,

and, using
D_{\sigma}\hat{z}=(\nabla_{z}\hat{z})\sigma=\tfrac{1}{r}\Pi^{\perp}_{z}\sigma=\tfrac{1}{r}\sigma
, we obtain

\Pi^{\perp}_{z}D_{\sigma}\Pi^{\perp}_{z}\nabla_{z}\log g=-\frac{1}{|z|}\Pi^{\perp}_{z}\left(\sigma\otimes\hat{z}+\hat{z}\otimes\sigma\right)\nabla_{z}\log g=-\frac{1}{|z|}\left(\sigma\otimes\hat{z}\right)\nabla_{z}\log g=-\frac{1}{|z|}\partial_{|z|}\log g\,\Pi^{\perp}_{z}\sigma.

(
A.3
) follows noticing that
\Pi^{\perp}_{z}=\sigma_{1}\otimes\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}\otimes\sigma_{2}
for any orthonormal basis
\{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\}
of
T_{\hat{z}}\mathbb{S}^{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
∎

Lemma A.2

(Decomposition of the Euclidean Hessian)
.

\displaystyle\nabla_{z}^{2}\log g

\displaystyle=\partial_{|z|}^{2}\log g\,\hat{z}\otimes\hat{z}+\frac{2}{|z|}\left(\hat{z}\otimes_{\mathrm{sym}}\!\nabla_{\hat{z}}\partial_{|z|}\log g\right)-\frac{2}{|z|^{2}}\left(\hat{z}\otimes_{\mathrm{sym}}\!\nabla_{\hat{z}}\log g\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\Pi^{\perp}_{z}\nabla^{2}_{z}\log g\,\Pi^{\perp}_{z}-\frac{1}{|z|}\partial_{|z|}\log g\,\Pi^{\perp}_{z}\right)+\frac{1}{|z|}\partial_{|z|}\log g\,\Pi^{\perp}_{z}.

Proof.

We decompose the Euclidean Hessian as

\nabla^{2}_{z}\log g=\nabla^{2}_{z}\log g\left(\hat{z}\otimes\hat{z}\right)+\nabla^{2}_{z}\log g\,\Pi^{\perp}_{z}.

One checks that

\nabla^{2}_{z}\log g\left(\hat{z}\otimes\hat{z}\right)=\partial_{|z|}^{2}\log g\,\hat{z}\otimes\hat{z}+\frac{1}{|z|}\nabla_{\hat{z}}\partial_{|z|}\log g\otimes\hat{z}-\frac{1}{r^{2}}\nabla_{\hat{z}}\log g\otimes\hat{z}.

Similarly,

\nabla^{2}_{z}\log g\,\Pi^{\perp}_{z}=\left(\hat{z}\otimes\hat{z}\right)\nabla^{2}_{z}\log g\,\Pi^{\perp}_{z}+\Pi^{\perp}_{z}\nabla^{2}_{z}\log g\,\Pi^{\perp}_{z},

where

\left(\hat{z}\otimes\hat{z}\right)\nabla^{2}_{z}\log g\,\Pi^{\perp}_{z}=\frac{1}{|z|}\hat{z}\otimes\nabla_{\hat{z}}\partial_{|z|}\log g-\frac{1}{r^{2}}\hat{z}\otimes\nabla_{\hat{z}}\log g.

We conclude recalling (
A.2
).
∎

Lemma A.3
.

\displaystyle\nabla_{z}\left(|z|^{2+\gamma}\Pi^{\perp}_{z}\nabla_{z}\log g\right)

\displaystyle=-2|z|^{2+\gamma}\left(\hat{z}\otimes_{\mathrm{sym}}\!\nabla_{\hat{z}}\log g\right)+\left(2+\gamma\right)|z|^{\gamma}\hat{z}\otimes\nabla_{\hat{z}}\log g

\displaystyle\quad+|z|^{1+\gamma}\hat{z}\otimes\nabla_{\hat{z}}\,\partial_{|z|}\log g+|z|^{2+\gamma}\left(\Pi^{\perp}_{z}\nabla_{z}^{2}\log g\,\Pi^{\perp}_{z}-\frac{1}{|z|}\partial_{|z|}\log g\,\Pi^{\perp}_{z}\right).

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\nabla_{z}\left(|z|^{2+\gamma}\Pi^{\perp}_{z}\nabla_{z}\log g\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\hat{z}\partial_{|z|}+\frac{1}{|z|}\nabla_{\hat{z}}\right)\left(|z|^{1+\gamma}\nabla_{\hat{z}}\log g\right)

\displaystyle=\left(1+\gamma\right)|z|^{\gamma}\hat{z}\otimes\nabla_{\hat{z}}\log g+|z|^{1+\gamma}\hat{z}\otimes\nabla_{\hat{z}}\partial_{|z|}\log g+|z|^{\gamma}\nabla_{\hat{z}}^{2}\log g

with

\nabla_{\hat{z}}^{2}\log g=|z|^{2}\left(\Pi^{\perp}_{z}\nabla_{z}^{2}\log g\Pi^{\perp}_{z}-\frac{1}{|z|}\partial_{|z|}\log g\Pi^{\perp}_{z}\right)-\nabla_{\hat{z}}\log g\otimes\hat{z},

(A.4)

were we used that

\nabla_{\hat{z}}\Pi^{\perp}_{z}=-3\left(\Pi^{\perp}_{z}\!\otimes\hat{z}\right)_{\mathrm{sym}}+2\hat{z}\otimes\Pi^{\perp}_{z}.

∎

Remark A.4
.

We emphasize that
\nabla_{\hat{z}}^{2}
, as a matrix, is not (a multiple of) the extrinsic spherical Hessian as shown by (
A.2
) and (
A.4
). They differ by the spherical gradient tensorized with the associated unit vector. Nevertheless, in view of (
A.3
),

\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{g}=\Pi^{\perp}_{z}:\nabla_{\hat{z}}^{2}\log g.

(A.5)

Moreover, a direct computation yields

\left\lVert\nabla_{\hat{z}}^{2}\log g\right\rVert^{2}_{\mathrm{HS}}=|z|^{4}\left\lVert\left(\Pi^{\perp}_{z}\nabla_{z}\right)^{2}\log g\right\rVert^{2}_{\mathrm{HS}}=\Gamma_{2}\left(\log\overline{g},\log\overline{g}\right),

(A.6)

where the iterated
carré du champs

\Gamma_{2}
of the spherical Laplacian on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
is given by

\Gamma_{2}\left(\log\overline{g},\log\overline{g}\right)=\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}^{2}\log\overline{g}\right\rVert^{2}_{\mathrm{HS}}+\left\lvert\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\log\overline{g}\right\rvert^{2}.

(A.7)

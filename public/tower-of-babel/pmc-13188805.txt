Association between supraphysiological estradiol levels and propofol injection pain: a prospective observational study
Authors: Mahmut Sami Tutar, Ahmet Erarkadas, Serife Nur Yildirim, Dilay GOK Korucu, Oguzhan Gunenc, Munise Yildiz, Dilek Tutar, Betul Kozanhan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPain on propofol injection (POPI) remains a distressing problem despite preventive strategies. Estradiol, a major estrogen, has been implicated in nociceptive and inflammatory pathways. This study aimed to determine whether supraphysiological estradiol levelsare associated with the incidence of POPI and with pain severity among patients experiencing pain.MethodsNinety female patients (ASA I–II, 18–45 years) undergoing elective surgery under general anesthesia were enrolled in this prospective observational study. Patients were divided into two groups based on preoperative estradiol concentration: supraphysiological (IVF, > 350 pg/mL) and normal (non-IVF, 15–350 pg/mL). Propofol (2–2.5 mg/kg) was injected without lidocaine pretreatment. POPI severity was assessed using a four-point behavioral scale (0–3). Correlation analyses and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analyses were performed to evaluate the association and discriminative performance of estradiol concentration with propofol injection pain.ResultsPOPI occurred more frequently in the IVF group (95.6%) than in the non-IVF group (68.9%, p = 0.001). Median estradiol levels were significantly higher in patients with pain (1096.5 vs. 88 pg/mL, p < 0.001). Among patients experiencing pain, estradiol levels showed a modest positive correlation with pain severity (r = 0.34, p = 0.003). ROC analysis yielded an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.847 (95% CI: 0.755–0.939) with an optimal cut-off of 122 pg/mL (sensitivity 87.8%, specificity 75%). This cut-off should be considered exploratory within this single-center cohort.ConclusionsSupraphysiological estradiol levels were associated with a higher incidence of POPI. In addition, estradiol concentration showed a modest correlation with pain severity among patients who experienced pain. These findings demonstrate an association between estradiol concentration and propofol injection pain within the studied population.Trial registrationNCT06929416, date: April 8, 2025, first patient registration date: May 2, 2025.

Background

Propofol is a widely used intravenous anesthetic agent valued for its rapid onset and short duration of action. Despite these advantages, pain on propofol injection (POPI) remains a frequent and distressing complication, reported in 28–90% of patients [1]. The mechanism of POPI is multifactorial, involving the physicochemical properties of propofol—such as its lipid solvent and low pH—as well as direct irritation of the venous endothelium and factors related to injection technique, including vein size and injection site [2–5]. Preventive strategies have mainly focused on local measures such as lidocaine admixture or pretreatment, slower injection rates, and the use of larger veins [6]. However, the persistence of POPI despite these measures suggests that systemic or patient-specific modulators of vascular nociception may also contribute to this phenomenon.

Estradiol, the predominant circulating estrogen, is a key hormonal modulator of pain processing. Experimental and clinical studies have demonstrated that estradiol enhances pain perception through multiple mechanisms, including activation of inflammatory pathways, modulation of nociceptor excitability, and increased neuroimmune crosstalk [7–9]. Clinically, elevated preoperative estradiol levels have been correlated with higher postoperative pain scores, supporting a potential role for sex hormones in perioperative nociceptive modulation [10]. Moreover, estradiol exerts important effects on vascular physiology: it enhances endothelial nitric oxide synthase (eNOS) activity, increases vascular permeability, and amplifies local inflammatory responses [11]. These mechanisms may intensify propofol’s irritant effect on venous endothelium, thereby lowering the threshold for vascular pain activation.

In vitro fertilization (IVF) provides a unique clinical model for examining the effects of markedly elevated estradiol concentrations. Controlled ovarian hyperstimulation during IVF induces supraphysiological estradiol levels—often several-fold higher than in natural cycles—without introducing other confounding systemic diseases [12, 13]. Comparing IVF patients with normal-cycling women under standardized anesthetic conditions may help clarify the association between elevated estradiol levels and pain on injection, although other procedure-related factors may also contribute.

Despite the biological plausibility, the relationship between supraphysiological estradiol levels and POPI has not been clinically investigated. We hypothesized that elevated estradiol concentrations would increase both the incidence and severity of POPI. The primary aim of this study was to evaluate the association between serum estradiol levels and POPI, while secondary objectives included analyzing correlations between estradiol concentration, pain severity, and local inflammatory reactions at the injection site.

Methods

Study design and ethical approval

This prospective observational study was approved by the Hamidiye Non-Interventional Clinical Research Ethics Committee (Approval No: 25/69; Date: February 6, 2025) and was registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (Identifier: NCT06929416; Registration date: April 8, 2025) prior to patient enrollment. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants. The research adhered to the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki and the standards of Good Clinical Practice [14].

Study population

Female patients aged 18–45 years with ASA physical status I–II who were scheduled for elective surgery under general anesthesia were eligible for enrollment. Two groups were defined [15]:

IVF group (n = 45): patients undergoing transvaginal oocyte retrieval as part of IVF treatment, characterized by supraphysiological serum estradiol concentrations (> 350 pg/mL).Non-IVF group (n = 45): patients with preoperatively measured normal estradiol concentrations (15–350 pg/mL) undergoing other elective surgical procedures under general anesthesia.

Exclusion criteria included ASA status III–IV, pregnancy, perimenopausal status, male sex, major psychiatric disorders impairing communication, age < 18 or > 45 years, emergency surgery, and comorbidities that could interfere with pain assessment, including chronic pain syndromes, neuropathic disorders, chronic opioid or regular analgesic use, and cognitive impairment.

Anesthetic protocol and pain assessment

Standard monitoring was applied to all patients. General anesthesia was induced with intravenous propofol (2–2.5 mg/kg) and remifentanil, followed by rocuronium (0.6 mg/kg) for neuromuscular blockade. To assess the natural pain response without confounding interventions, no lidocaine was mixed with propofol or administered as a pretreatment. Anesthesia was maintained with propofol and remifentanil infusions titrated to a Bispectral Index (BIS) of 40–60.

A 20-gauge catheter was inserted into a dorsal hand vein of the non-dominant hand. Propofol was injected manually at a uniform speed of approximately 0.5 mL/s to maintain a consistent injection rate. Pain assessment was conducted immediately upon propofol injection and before the administration of remifentanil could induce loss of consciousness. Patients were observed for pain-related responses (vocalization, grimacing, arm withdrawal, tearing). In the absence of obvious signs, they were questioned every 5–10 s until they became unconscious. Pain severity was graded using a predefined 4-point behavioral scale: 0 = no response; 1 = verbal complaint only; 2 = verbal complaint accompanied by facial grimacing or arm withdrawal; and 3 = pronounced vocal response, marked withdrawal, or distress behavior [16]. For statistical analysis, the highest observed pain score before loss of consciousness was recorded. All assessments were performed by the same attending anesthesiologist. The assessor was not blinded to group allocation due to the clinical context, which may have introduced observer bias.

Postoperatively, an investigator blinded to the study groups assessed the injection site at 24 h for local reactions (redness, edema, swelling). Pain at the propofol injection site was reassessed at 24 h postoperatively. This evaluation was distinct from surgical pain assessment. Local inflammatory signs at the injection site (redness, edema, swelling) were also recorded separately.

Outcomes

The primary outcome was the discriminative performance of serum estradiol concentration for identifying patients who experienced propofol injection pain (yes/no), assessed by receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis. Secondary outcomes included the correlation between estradiol concentration and pain severity, as well as the incidence of postoperative pain and local reactions.

Sample size

A prospective pilot study conducted at the same institution under identical clinical and anesthetic conditions included 20 patients (10 IVF and 10 non-IVF). The pilot data yielded an effect size of w = 0.408 for the difference in POPI incidence between groups. Using G*Power version 3.1.9.4, an a priori power analysis for a chi-square test (goodness-of-fit, df = 1) was performed. Based on this effect size, with α = 0.05 and power = 0.95, a total of 79 patients were required. We aimed to enroll 90 patients (45 in each group) to account for potential dropouts or missing data.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed utilizing IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, version 26.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). The Kolmogorov–Smirnov test was used to determine the normality of data distribution. Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) for normally distributed data, and median (25th–75th percentiles) for non-normally distributed data. Categorical variables are shown as counts and percentages. An Independent samples t-test or Mann–Whitney U test was used for group comparisons. Categorical variables were analyzed using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test.

Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient was used to assess the association between pain intensity and demographic, anthropometric, and hormonal characteristics. ROC curve analysis evaluated the discriminative ability of serum estradiol concentration for distinguishing patients with and without propofol injection pain. The area under the ROC curve (AUC) with a 95% confidence interval (CI) was computed, and the optimal cut-off value was established using the Youden index. A p value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

A total of 101 patients were screened for eligibility. Seven patients declined participation, and four were excluded due to insufficient data, leaving 90 patients for the final analysis. Participants were divided into two groups: the IVF group (patients with supraphysiological estradiol levels, n = 45) and the non-IVF group (patients with normal estradiol levels, n = 45) [Figure 1]. Baseline characteristics, including age, height, weight, body mass index (BMI), smoking status, comorbidities, and ASA physical status, did not differ significantly between groups (p > 0.05 for all). Serum progesterone and estradiol concentrations were significantly higher in the IVF group compared to the non-IVF group (median (IQR): 1.11 (0.5–1.61) vs. 0.35 (0.18–0.78) ng/mL for progesterone, and 2787 (1488–3743) vs. 131.5 (78.8–165) pg/mL for estradiol; both p < 0.001).

Postoperative pain at 24 h occurred more frequently in the IVF group, although the difference did not reach statistical significance (28.9% vs. 13.3%, p = 0.071). Redness at the injection site was observed more often in the IVF group (26.7% vs. 6.7%, p = 0.011), whereas rates of edema and swelling were similar between groups (p > 0.05) [Table 1].

Of the 90 patients analyzed, 74 (82.2%) reported POPI. Baseline demographic and clinical variables—including age, BMI, smoking status, comorbidities, and ASA status—were comparable between patients with and without pain (p > 0.05 for all). Correlation analyses were performed to explore the relationship between pain severity and patient characteristics, BMI, and hormonal parameters, restricted to patients who experienced injection pain (n = 74). No significant correlations were found with age, height, weight, BMI, or progesterone concentration (all p > 0.05). In contrast, serum estradiol concentration showed a modest but significant positive correlation with pain severity (r = 0.340, p = 0.003) [Table 3].

ROC curve analysis was used to evaluate the ability of serum estradiol concentration to discriminate between patients with and without POPI. Estradiol demonstrated good discriminative performance, with an AUC of 0.847 (95% CI: 0.755–0.939, p < 0.001). The optimal cut-off value was 122 pg/mL, yielding a sensitivity of 87.8% and a specificity of 75% [Table 4; Fig. 2].

Discussion

This prospective observational study demonstrated that supraphysiological estradiol levels were significantly associated with a higher incidence of POPI. Patients undergoing IVF experienced more frequent POPI than those with normal estradiol levels. Although the categorical distribution of pain severity grades did not differ significantly between groups, serum estradiol concentration showed a modest positive correlation with pain severity among patients reporting pain. ROC analysis demonstrated good discriminative performance of estradiol concentration for identifying patients with propofol injection pain.

Overall, estradiol concentration was associated with the likelihood of experiencing propofol injection pain. The observed correlation with pain severity was limited to patients who reported pain and therefore reflects within-subgroup variation rather than an effect on overall pain susceptibility.

Progesterone levels were also elevated in the IVF group; however, no statistically significant association was observed between progesterone concentration and pain outcomes in this cohort. Although progesterone has been described as exerting vasodilatory and potentially antinociceptive effects, the interaction between sex steroids is complex, and our findings should not be interpreted as evidence against a possible modulatory role.

These results align with experimental and clinical evidence demonstrating that estrogen influences nociceptive processing. Estradiol enhances pain sensitivity through both central and peripheral mechanisms, including upregulation of inflammatory mediators and modulation of neuronal excitability [7–9]. Elevated estradiol levels have been linked to heightened postoperative pain, increased sensitivity to mechanical or thermal stimuli, and augmented neuroinflammatory responses [10]. Our findings extend this evidence by showing that estradiol may also amplify acute vascular pain responses such, as those caused by propofol injection. Although causality cannot be inferred in this observational design, the observed discriminative performance reflects a statistically significant association. Further studies are required to determine whether this association has underlying biological mechanisms.

Mechanistically, estradiol facilitates neuroimmune interactions that may contribute to this phenomenon. Preclinical studies have shown that estradiol enhances macrophage–nerve communication, upregulates neurotrophic factors such as BDNF and NT-3, and augments local inflammatory cascades [7, 8]. In addition, estradiol increases eNOS activity and vascular permeability, potentially intensifying the irritant effect of propofol on venous endothelium [11]. These mechanisms collectively lower the activation threshold of peripheral nociceptors. In our cohort, the higher rate of 24-hour injection-site redness observed in the IVF group may be compatible with hormonal modulation of vascular responses. However, given the absence of inflammatory biomarker measurements and direct correlation analyses, this finding should be interpreted cautiously. The absence of a statistically significant association between progesterone and pain severity in this cohort should not be interpreted as definitive evidence of hormone-specific effects, as the interplay between sex steroids is complex.

From a clinical perspective, the identification of a serum estradiol cut-off with strong discriminative performance suggests potential utility for risk stratification, particularly in IVF or hormonally stimulated patients. When elevated estradiol levels are anticipated, anesthesiologists might consider preventive strategies such as lidocaine admixture or pretreatment, slower injection rates, or antecubital venous access [6, 16]. Although routine preoperative estradiol measurement is not warranted for the general surgical population, incorporating hormonal context into perioperative pain assessment could refine personalized anesthesia care—especially for patients in hormonally altered states such as assisted reproduction or estrogen therapy cycles.

Limitations

Several limitations warrant consideration. The study population was limited to women aged 18–45 years, restricting generalizability to men and postmenopausal patients. Estradiol levels were measured only once preoperatively, without accounting for perioperative fluctuations. Preoperative anxiety levels and baseline discomfort related to ovarian stimulation were not formally assessed using validated instruments. Although major psychiatric disorders were excluded, transient procedural anxiety or pre-existing discomfort may have influenced subjective pain perception and represent potential confounding factors. Although the correlation between estradiol and pain severity was statistically significant, it was moderate in magnitude, and stratified analysis across mild, moderate, and severe categories did not reach significance. In addition, the correlation analysis was restricted to patients who experienced pain, which limits the generalizability of severity-related conclusions to the entire study population. Moreover, inflammatory and neurotrophic biomarkers were not measured, which could have provided deeper insight into estrogen-mediated nociception.

Conclusions

In conclusion, supraphysiological estradiol levels were associated with a higher incidence of POPI. Among the hormonal parameters evaluated, estradiol showed a statistically significant correlation with pain severity in patients reporting pain and demonstrated discriminative performance in ROC analysis, although the identified 122 pg/mL cut-off should be considered exploratory. These findings suggest an association between estradiol concentration and variability in propofol injection pain. External validation in larger and independent cohorts is required before this threshold can be considered for clinical application.

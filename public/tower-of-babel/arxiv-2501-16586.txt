Isomorphism Spectra and Computably Composite Structures
Joey Lakerdas-Gayle

Abstract
Adapting a result of Bazhenov, Kalimullin, and Yamaleev, we show that if a Turing degree d is the degree of categoricity of a computable structure ℳ \mathcal{M} and is not the strong degree of categoricity of any computable structure, then ℳ \mathcal{M} has a pair of computable copies whose isomorphism spectrum is not finitely generated. Motivated by this result, we introduce a class of computable structures called computably composite structures with the property that the isomorphisms between arbitrary computable copies of these structures are exactly the unions of isomorphisms between the computable copies of their components. We use this to show that any computable union of isomorphism spectra is also an isomorphism spectrum. In particular, this gives examples of isomorphism spectra that are not finitely generated.

Introduction

Mathematical structures are usually considered up to isomorphism. However, isomorphic structures may have different computability-theoretic properties. In particular, a pair of isomorphic computable structures need not have any computable isomorphisms. A major topic in computable structure theory is the complexity of isomorphisms between isomorphic computable structures. See
[

If
\mathcal{A}
and

\mathcal{A}
to
\mathcal{B}

\textrm{IsoSpec}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})=\{\textbf{d}:\exists f:\mathcal{A}\cong\mathcal{B}[f\leq_{T}\textbf{d}]\}.

\mathcal{A}

\textbf{d}\in\textrm{IsoSpec}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
for every computable copy

\mathcal{A}

\mathcal{A}

\mathbf{0}

We write

If a structure has degree of categoricity
d

Fokina, Kalimullin, and Miller
[

[

]

[

[

[

2
Computably composite structures

We begin by introducing the notion of
computably composite structures
. Informally, given a computable structure
\mathcal{S}
, we wish to “attach” a computable structure
\mathcal{A}_{x}
to each point
x\in S
. This new structure
\mathcal{S}[\mathcal{A}_{x}:x\in S]
will be computable. Its computable copies and the isomorphisms between those copies will have desirable properties.

Throughout this paper, we use calligraphic capital letters for copies of structures, and boldface capital letters for collections of copies of structures.

Definition 3
.

Let
I
be any computable set and let
\textbf{A}:=\{\mathcal{A}_{x}:x\in I\}
be a collection of computable copies of structures. We say that
A
is
uniformly computable
if each
\mathcal{A}_{x}
is a particular computable copy of a computable
\mathcal{L}_{x}
-structure, the languages
\{\mathcal{L}_{x}:x\in I\}
are uniformly computable, and there is a computable function
f:I\to\omega
such that
\varphi_{f(x)}=D(\mathcal{A}_{x})
for each
x\in I
. That is, the Turing machine with index
f(x)
computes the atomic diagram of the structure
\mathcal{A}_{x}
.

Remark 4
.

In Definition
3
, if
x\neq y
, then
\mathcal{A}_{x}
and
\mathcal{A}_{y}
may not be copies of the same computable structure, and may not even have the same language or universe. In particular, the universes of these copies may not be subsets of
\omega
. However, since they are uniformly computable, there are standard uniformly computable encodings of the universes into computable subsets of
\omega
.

Definition 5

(Computably composite structures)
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a computable
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{S}}
-structure with universe
S
, and let
\textbf{A}:=\{\mathcal{A}_{x}:x\in S\}
be a uniformly computable collection of copies such that the universes
\{S\}\cup\{A_{x}:x\in S\}
are pairwise disjoint. We define the
computable composition of
\mathcal{S}
with
A
to be the structure
\mathcal{S}[\textbf{A}]
with universe
S\cup\bigcup_{x\in S}A_{x}
, relational language
\mathcal{L}:=\{\mu\}\cup\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{S}}\cup\bigcup_{x\in S}\mathcal{L}_{x}
, and satisfying:

1.

\mu^{\mathcal{S}[\textbf{A}]}=\left\{(a,x):x\in S,a\in A_{x}\right\}\cup\{(x,x):x\in S\}
.

2.

If
R
is an
n
-ary relation in
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{S}}
and
x_{0},\dots,x_{n-1}\in S
, then
R^{\mathcal{S}[\textbf{A}]}(x_{0},\dots,x_{n-1})
iff
R^{\mathcal{S}}(x_{0},\dots,x_{n-1})
.

3.

If
Q
is an
n
-ary relation in
\mathcal{L}_{x}
and
a_{0},\dots,a_{n-1}\in A_{x}
, then
Q^{\mathcal{S}[\textbf{A}]}(a_{0},\dots,a_{n-1})
iff
Q^{\mathcal{A}_{x}}(a_{0},\dots,a_{n-1})
.

4.

No other relations hold.

A structure of the form
\mathcal{S}[\textbf{A}]
is called a
computably composite structure
. The structure
\mathcal{S}
is the
base structure
of
\mathcal{S}[\textbf{A}]
, and the structures
\{\mathcal{A}_{x}:x\in S\}
are the
component structures
.

Remark 6
.

In a computably composite structure,
\mu
is a directed edge. Every element has exactly one outward
\mu
-edge, so we may use the notation
\mu(z)
to denote the unique element for which
(z,\mu(z))\in\mu
. This is computable since the element is unique and the universe of the structure is computable. Also notice that
\mu(z)\in S
for all elements
z
of
\mathcal{S}[\textbf{A}]
. We may think of
\mu
as connecting members of
\mathcal{A}_{x}
to the point
x\in\mathcal{S}
.

Remark 7
.

In
\mathcal{S}
and in each component structure, the relations are uniformly computable. By the uniform computability of
A
, the relations of
\mathcal{S}[\textbf{A}]
are also uniformly computable. Since
\{S\}\cup\{A_{x}:x\in S\}
are pairwise disjoint and uniformly computable, the relation
\mu^{\mathcal{S}[\textbf{A}]}
is computable. Thus,
\mathcal{S}[\textbf{A}]
is a computable structure.

Remark 8
.

\mathcal{S}
is a reduct of the substructure of
\mathcal{S}[\textbf{A}]
with universe
S
and for each
x\in S
,
\mathcal{A}_{x}
is a reduct of the substructure of
\mathcal{S}[\textbf{A}]
with universe
A_{x}
. As these reducts remove only trivial relations, we will identify them with the actual substructures.

2.1
Isomorphisms between computably composite structures

First, we will show that every computable copy of a computably composite structure is also computably composite.

Proposition 10
.

Suppose
\mathcal{M}
is a computable copy of
\mathcal{S}[\textbf{A}]
via isomorphism
\rho:\mathcal{S}[\textbf{A}]\cong\mathcal{M}
. Let
\mathcal{G}
be the substructure of
\mathcal{M}
generated by
\rho(S)
. For each
g\in G
let
\mathcal{B}_{g}
be the substructure of
\mathcal{M}
generated by
\rho(A_{\rho^{-1}(g)})
and let
\textbf{B}:=\{\mathcal{B}_{g}:g\in G\}
. Then
\mathcal{G}[\textbf{B}]=\mathcal{M}
is a computably composite structure.

Proof.

Since
\{S\}\cup\{A_{x}:x\in S\}
are pairwise disjoint and
\rho
is a bijection, it follows that
\{G\}\cup\{B_{g}:g\in G\}
are also pairwise disjoint. It is clear that the construction of
\mathcal{G}[\textbf{B}]
yields exactly the structure
\mathcal{M}
.
\mathcal{G}
is the substructure of
\mathcal{M}
generated by the set

G=\{x\in M:\mu^{\mathcal{M}}(x,x)\}
which is computable, so
\mathcal{G}
is computable. It remains to verify that
B
is uniformly computable.

For each
g\in G
,
\mathcal{B}_{g}
is the substructure of
\mathcal{M}
generated by the set
B_{g}=\{b\in M:\mu^{\mathcal{M}}(b,g)\land b\neq g\}

which is uniformly computable in
g
. Then
\textbf{B}=\{\mathcal{B}_{g}\}_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
is uniformly computable since a relation holds over elements of
B_{g}
if and only if the relation holds over the same elements of
\mathcal{M}
.
∎

Next, we characterize the isomorphisms between two computably composite structures
\mathcal{S}[\textbf{A}]
and
\mathcal{G}[\textbf{B}]
. Informally, these are exactly the maps that induce an isomorphism from
\mathcal{S}
to
\mathcal{G}
and induce isomorphisms between the corresponding component structures.

Proposition 11
.

Suppose
\mathcal{S}[\textbf{A}]
and
\mathcal{G}[\textbf{B}]
are isomorphic computably composite structures. Then the isomorphisms from
\mathcal{S}[\textbf{A}]
to
\mathcal{G}[\textbf{B}]
are exactly the maps of the form

\rho=\theta\cup\bigcup_{x\in S}\psi_{x}

where
\theta:\mathcal{S}\cong\mathcal{G}
and
\psi_{x}:\mathcal{A}_{x}\cong\mathcal{B}_{\theta(x)}
for each
x\in S
.

Proof.

For any such
\theta
and
\{\psi_{x}\}_{x\in S}
, since
\textrm{dom}(\theta)\cup\bigcup_{x\in S}\textrm{dom}(\psi_{x})=S\cup\bigcup_{x\in S}A_{x}
,
\rho
is actually a function from the universe of
\mathcal{S}[\textbf{A}]
to the universe of
\mathcal{G}[\textbf{B}]
. Since
\theta(S)\cup\bigcup_{x\in S}\psi_{x}(A_{x})=G\cup\bigcup_{x\in S}B_{\theta(x)}
and
\theta
is a bijection,
\rho
is a bijection. It is easily checked that all relations are preserved by
\rho
, so every such
\rho
is an isomorphism.

Let
\rho:\mathcal{S}[\textbf{A}]\to\mathcal{G}[\textbf{B}]
be any isomorphism. We have
x\in S\Leftrightarrow\mu^{\mathcal{S}[\textbf{A}]}(x,x)
and
g\in G\Leftrightarrow\mu^{\mathcal{G}[\textbf{B}]}(g,g)
, so since
\rho
is an isomorphism, we must have that
\theta:=\rho{\upharpoonright}_{S}
is an isomorphism from
\mathcal{S}
to
\mathcal{G}
. For any
x\in S
,
a\in A_{x}\Leftrightarrow\left(\mu^{\mathcal{S}[\textbf{A}]}(a,x)\land\neg\mu^{\mathcal{S}[\textbf{A}]}(a,a)\right)
and

g\in B_{\theta(x)}\Leftrightarrow\left(\mu^{\mathcal{G}[\textbf{B}]}(g,\theta(x))\land\neg\mu^{\mathcal{G}[\textbf{B}]}(g,g)\right)\Leftrightarrow\left(\mu^{\mathcal{G}[\textbf{B}]}(g,\rho(x))\land\neg\mu^{\mathcal{G}[\textbf{B}]}(g,g)\right),

so since
\rho
is an isomorphism, we must have that
\psi_{x}:=\rho{\upharpoonright}_{A_{x}}
is an isomorphism from
\mathcal{A}_{x}
to
\mathcal{B}_{\theta(x)}
. As this covers the domain of
\mathcal{S}[\textbf{A}]
, we must have
\rho=\theta\cup\bigcup_{x\in S}\psi_{x}
.
∎

Remark 12
.

An important case is when the base structure
\mathcal{S}
is
computably stable
: for every computable copy
\mathcal{G}
of
\mathcal{S}
, every isomorphism from
\mathcal{S}
to
\mathcal{G}
is computable. When
\mathcal{S}
is computably stable and
\mathcal{S}[\textbf{A}]
is computably composite, Proposition
10
implies that up to computable isomorphism, the computable copies of
\mathcal{S}[\textbf{A}]
are exactly the computably composite structures
\mathcal{S}[\textbf{B}]
where
\mathcal{A}_{x}\cong\mathcal{B}_{x}
for all

. Proposition

implies that the complexity of the isomorphisms between
\mathcal{S}[\textbf{A}]
and

depend only on the complexity of the isomorphisms between the corresponding component structures. This will be an important property of the structure defined in Section

2.2
Degrees of categoricity that are never strong

It is not known whether there exists a degree of categoricity that is not the strong degree of categoricity of any computable structure. We adapt a result of Bazhenov, Kalimullin, and Yamaleev which serves as motivation for the main result of this paper. For this, we define a particularly simple class of computably composite structures:

Example 13
.

For each
n<\omega
, define the directed graph
\mathcal{P}_{n}=(\{0,\dots,n-1\},E_{n})
where
E_{n}=\{(k,k+1):k<n-1\}
. If
\{\mathcal{A}_{i}:i<n\}
is any collection of computable structures, it is uniformly computable since it is finite, so
\mathcal{P}_{n}[\{i\}\times\mathcal{A}_{i}:i<n]
is a computably composite structure (we relabel the universe
A_{i}
as
\{i\}\times A_{i}
to ensure disjointness). We may think of
\mathcal{P}_{n}[\{i\}\times\mathcal{A}_{i}:i<n]
as a directed path
of the structures
:

\mathcal{A}_{0}\to\mathcal{A}_{1}\to\dots\to\mathcal{A}_{n-1}.

Since
\mathcal{P}_{n}
is computably stable, it follows from Proposition
10
and Proposition
11
that the computable copies of
\mathcal{P}_{n}[\{i\}\times\mathcal{A}_{i}:i<n]
are (up to computable isomorphism) of the form
\mathcal{P}_{n}[\{i\}\times\mathcal{B}_{i}:i<n]
where
\mathcal{B}_{i}
is a computable copy of
\mathcal{A}_{i}
for each
i<n
. Each isomorphism between these paths is Turing-equivalent to a union
\bigcup_{i<n}\psi_{i}
where
\psi_{i}:\mathcal{A}_{i}\cong\mathcal{B}_{i}
for each
i<n
. Thus, their isomorphism spectrum is exactly
\bigcap_{i<n}\textrm{IsoSpec}(\mathcal{A}_{i},\mathcal{B}_{i})
.

Theorem 14

(follows from Bazhenov, Kalimullin, Yamaleev
2020
[
5
]
, Proposition 1b)
.

If
\mathcal{M}
is a computable structure with spectral dimension
n<\omega
and degree of categoricity
d
, then
\mathcal{P}_{n}[\{i\}\times\mathcal{M}:i<n]
has strong degree of categoricity
d
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{M}
has spectral dimension
n
, there are pairs
(\mathcal{A}_{i},\mathcal{B}_{i})_{i<n}
of computable copies of
\mathcal{M}
such that

\bigcap_{i<n}\textrm{IsoSpec}(\mathcal{A}_{i},\mathcal{B}_{i})=\textrm{CatSpec}(\mathcal{M})=\mathcal{D}_{\geq}(\textbf{d}).

Now
\mathcal{P}_{n}[\mathcal{A}_{i}:i<n]
and
\mathcal{P}_{n}[\mathcal{B}_{i}:i<n]
are computably composite structures as in the previous example, and are both isomorphic to
\mathcal{P}_{n}[\{i\}\times\mathcal{M}:i<n]
. Thus,

\textrm{IsoSpec}(\mathcal{P}_{n}[\mathcal{A}_{i}:i<n],\mathcal{P}_{n}[\mathcal{B}_{i}:i<n])=\bigcap_{i<n}\textrm{IsoSpec}(\mathcal{A}_{i},\mathcal{B}_{i})=\mathcal{D}_{\geq}(\textbf{d}).

Since every computable copy of
\mathcal{P}_{n}[\{i\}\times\mathcal{M}:i<n]
is a path
\mathcal{N}_{0}\to\dots\to\mathcal{N}_{n-1}
with each
\mathcal{N}_{i}
a computable copy of
\mathcal{M}
, and
\mathcal{M}
is
d
-computably categorical, every isomorphism between these paths is computable in
d
. So
\mathcal{P}_{n}[\mathcal{M}]
has degree of categoricity
d
and spectral dimension 1 witnessed by copies
\mathcal{P}_{n}[\mathcal{A}_{i}:i<n]
and
\mathcal{P}_{n}[\mathcal{B}_{i}:i<n]
.
∎

Definition 15
.

An upward-closed set of Turing degrees
\mathcal{Z}\subseteq\mathcal{D}
is
generated
by a subset
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{Z}
if
\mathcal{Z}=\bigcup_{\textbf{d}\in\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{D}_{\geq}(\textbf{d})
. We say that
\mathcal{Z}
is
finitely generated
if it is generated by a finite set of Turing degrees. That is, if it is equal to a union of finitely-many cones.

Bazhenov, Kalimullin, and Yamaleev
[
3
]
show that any computable structure with a degree of categoricity and infinite spectral dimension must have a pair of copies whose isomorphism spectrum is not finitely generated.

Theorem 16

(Bazhenov, Kalimullin, Yamaleev
[
3
]
, Theorem 3.1)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a computable structure with degree of categoricity
d
, and infinite spectral dimension. Then there are computable copies
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
of
\mathcal{M}
such that
\textrm{IsoSpec}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
is not finitely generated.

By combining these results, we obtain the following necessary conditions for any never-strong degree of categoricity:

Theorem 17
.

If
d
is the degree of categoricity of a computable structure
\mathcal{M}
and is not the strong degree of categoricity of any computable structure, then
\mathcal{M}
has infinite spectral dimension, an infinite automorphism group, and a pair of computable copies
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
such that
\textrm{IsoSpec}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
is not finitely generated.

Motivated by this result, we now work toward constructing pairs of computably composite structures whose isomorphism spectra are not finitely generated.

3
An infinite base structure

In the proof of Theorem
14

\mathcal{P}_{n}
to construct new computable structures whose isomorphism spectra are the intersection of the original
n
isomorphism spectra. Similar constructions yield finite unions of isomorphism spectra (for example, if
\mathcal{A}_{1}\cong\mathcal{B}_{1}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}\cong\mathcal{B}_{2}

\mathcal{A}_{1}-\mathcal{A}_{2}-\mathcal{B}_{2}-\mathcal{B}_{1}

3.1
Automorphisms of the base structure

By Proposition
11
, isomorphisms between copies of a computably composite structure on
\mathcal{H}
depend partly on the isomorphisms between computable copies of
\mathcal{H}
. We will see that it is sufficient to consider the automorphisms of
\mathcal{H}
.

Lemma 21
.

Let
\theta
be an automorphism of
\mathcal{H}
. If
X\in[\omega]^{<\omega}
, then
\theta(X)\in[\omega]^{<\omega}
. If
(i,a)\in\omega\times\{0,1\}
, then either
\theta(i,a)=(i,0)
or
\theta(i,a)=(i,1)
.

Proof.

We consider the number of inward and outward
D_{i}
edges at each point in
H
:

1.

If
X\in[\omega]^{<\omega}
, then
X
has exactly one outward directed
D_{i}
-edge for each
i<\omega
.

2.

If
(i,a)\in\omega\times\{0,1\}
, then
(i,a)
has no outward directed
D_{j}
-edges for any
j<\omega
.

3.

If
(i,a)\in\omega\times\{0,1\}
, then
(i,a)
has infinitely-many incoming
D_{i}
-edges.

4.

If
(i,a)\in\omega\times\{0,1\}
, then
(i,a)
has no incoming
D_{j}
-edges when
j\neq i
.

As these properties must be preserved by any automorphism, the result follows.
∎

Lemma 22
.

For
X,Y\in[\omega]^{<\omega}
,
h_{X}\circ h_{Y}=h_{X\triangle Y}
. In particular,
h_{X}\circ h_{X}=h_{\emptyset}=id_{H}
and
h_{X}\circ h_{\{i\}}=h_{X\cup\{i\}}
when
i\notin X
.

Proof.

For
Z\in[\omega]^{<\omega}
,

h_{X}\circ h_{Y}(Z)=X\triangle(Y\triangle Z)=(X\triangle Y)\triangle Z=h_{X\triangle Y}(Z).

For
(i,a)\in\omega\times\{0,1\}
,

h_{X}\circ h_{Y}(i,a)=h_{X}(i,a+Y(i))=(i,a+Y(i)+X(i))=(i,a+(X\triangle Y)(i))=h_{X\triangle Y}(i,a).

∎

Proposition 23
.

For each
X\in[\omega]^{<\omega}
, the map
h_{X}
is an automorphism of
\mathcal{H}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
22
,
h_{X}\circ h_{X}=id_{H}
, so
h_{X}
is bijective. If
i\notin X
, then
i\in Y\Leftrightarrow i\in(X\triangle Y)
. If
i\in X
, then
i\in Y\Leftrightarrow i\notin(X\triangle Y)
. So in general, we have

Y(i)=a\Leftrightarrow(X\triangle Y)(i)=a+X(i).

The following equivalences show that
h_{X}
preserves all relations
E_{i}
and
D_{i}
in
\mathcal{H}
and so is an automorphism:

\displaystyle D_{i}(Y,(i,a))

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow Y(i)=a

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow(X\triangle Y)(i)=a+X(i)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow h_{X}(Y)(i)=a+X(i)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow D_{i}(h_{X}(Y),(i,a+X(i)))

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow D_{i}(h_{X}(Y),h_{X}(i,a)).

\displaystyle E_{i}(Y,Z)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow Y\triangle Z=\{i\}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow(Y\triangle X)\triangle(Z\triangle X)=\{i\}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow E_{i}(Y\triangle X,Z\triangle X)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow E_{i}(h_{X}(Y),h_{X}(Z)).

∎

Proposition 24
.

For each
X\in[\omega]^{<\omega}
, the map
h_{X}
is the unique automorphism sending
\emptyset
to
X
.

Proof.

Every
h_{X}
is an automorphism by Proposition
23
. Suppose
\theta:\mathcal{H}\cong\mathcal{H}
is an automorphism and that
\theta(\emptyset)=X
. By Lemma
21
, for each
i<\omega
there is an
a_{i}\in\{0,1\}
such that
\theta(i,0)=(i,a_{i})
and
\theta(i,1)=(i,1+a_{i})
. Since
(\emptyset,(i,0))\in D_{i}
and
\theta
is an automorphism, we have
(\theta(\emptyset),\theta(i,0))=(X,(i,a_{i}))\in D_{i}
for each
i<\omega
. But then by the definition of
D_{i}
,
a_{i}=X(i)
for all
i
, so

\theta(i,0)=(i,a_{i})=(i,X(i))=h_{X}(i,0)

and

\theta(i,1)=(i,1+a_{i})=(i,1+X(i))=h_{X}(i,1).

Thus,
\theta(i,a)=h_{X}(i,a)
for all
(i,a)\in\omega\times\{0,1\}
.

Let
Y\in[\omega]^{<\omega}
, so by Lemma
21
,
Z:=\theta(Y)\in[\omega]^{<\omega}
. Since
\theta(i,a)=h_{X}(i,a)=(i,a+X(i))
, we have

Y(i)=a\Leftrightarrow D_{i}(Y,(i,a))\Leftrightarrow D_{i}(Z,(i,a+X(i)))\Leftrightarrow Z(i)=a+X(i),

so
Z(i)=Y(i)+X(i)
for all
i<\omega
, and so
\theta(Y)=Z=X\triangle Y=h_{X}(Y)
. Since
Y\in[\omega]^{<\omega}
is arbitrary, we have
\theta=h_{X}
.
∎

Corollary 25
.

The set of automorphisms of
\mathcal{H}
is
\left\{h_{X}:X\in[\omega]^{<\omega}\right\}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
21
, every automorphism of
\mathcal{H}
sends
\emptyset
to some
X\in[\omega]^{<\omega}
, so by Proposition
24
is equal to
h_{X}
. The result then follows from Proposition
23
.
∎

Remark 26
.

By Lemma
22
, we have that if
X=\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{k}\}\in[\omega]^{<\omega}
, then

h_{X}=h_{\{x_{1}\}}\circ h_{\{x_{2}\}}\circ\dots\circ h_{\{x_{k}\}}.

Since
h_{\{i\}}
is the reflection of the hypercube along the
i^{\text{th}}
dimension, each
h_{X}
is a finite composition of reflections along the dimensions corresponding to the elements of
X
.

From Remark
12
, the following proposition means that if
\mathcal{H}[\textbf{A}]
is a computably composite structure on
\mathcal{H}
, the computable copies of
\mathcal{H}[\textbf{A}]
are up to computable isomorphism exactly the computably composite structures of the form
\mathcal{H}[\textbf{B}]
where
\mathcal{B}_{z}\cong\mathcal{A}_{z}
for all
z\in H
. Hence, the complexity of an isomorphism between two computably composite structures on
\mathcal{H}
depends only on the isomorphisms between the corresponding component structures.

Proposition 27
.

\mathcal{H}
is computably stable. That is, if
\mathcal{G}
is a computable copy of
\mathcal{H}
, then every isomorphism from
\mathcal{H}
to
\mathcal{G}
is computable.

Proof.

Let
f
be any isomorphism from
\mathcal{H}
to
\mathcal{G}
. We may assume that
\mathcal{G}
has universe
\omega
. Define
n_{\emptyset}:=f(\emptyset)
. By Proposition
24
, the automorphisms of
\mathcal{H}
are determined by where
\emptyset
is mapped to. Thus,
f
is determined by
f(\emptyset)
. We now computably build
f
out from
\emptyset
.

Suppose we know
f(X)\in\omega
for some
X\in[\omega]^{<\omega}
and suppose
n\notin X
. Then
X\cup\{n\}
is the unique element of
[\omega]^{<\omega}
with the property
E^{\mathcal{H}}_{n}(X,X\cup\{n\})
. Thus,
f(X\cup\{n\})
is the unique element of
\omega
with the property
E^{\mathcal{G}}_{n}(f(X),f(X\cup\{n\}))
and can be found by iterating through
\omega
in finitely-many steps since
E^{\mathcal{G}}_{n}
is computable. Since we know
f(\emptyset)
, we can find
f(Z)
for any
Z\in[\omega]^{<\omega}
by iterating this process once for each of the (finitely-many) elements of
Z
.

Fix
i\in\omega
. Then
(i,0)
is the unique element of
H
with the property
D_{i}^{\mathcal{H}}(\emptyset,(i,0))
. Thus,
f(i,0)
is the unique element of
\omega
with the property
D_{i}^{\mathcal{G}}(f(\emptyset),f(i,0))
. Since we know
f(\emptyset)
,
f(i,0)
can be found by iterating through
\omega
in finitely-many steps. Similarly,
f(i,1)
is the unique element of
\omega
with the property
D_{i}^{\mathcal{G}}(\{i\},(i,1))
. Since we can computably find
f(\{i\})
, we can find
f(i,1)
by iterating through
\omega
in finitely-many steps.
∎

Corollary 28
.

\mathcal{H}
is computably categorical.

Lemma 33

i<\omega
and let
\theta:\mathcal{A}_{i+1}\cong\mathcal{B}_{i+1}
. Then there is an isomorphism
\rho:\mathcal{M}\cong\mathcal{N}
such that
\rho\equiv_{T}\theta
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{M}_{i,0}=\{(i,0)\}\times\mathcal{A}_{i+1}
and
\mathcal{M}_{i,1}=\{(i,1)\}\times\mathcal{B}_{i+1}
, the function
\widehat{\theta}((i,0),a):=((i,1),\theta(a))
is an isomorphism from
\mathcal{M}_{(i,0)}
to
\mathcal{M}_{(i,1)}
and
\widehat{\theta}\equiv_{T}\theta
. Define the isomorphism
\rho=h_{X}\cup\bigcup_{z\in H}\psi_{z}
as follows:

Take
X=\{i\}
, so
h_{X}:=h_{\{i\}}
which permutes
(i,0)
with
(i,1)
, permutes each
Y\in[\omega]^{<\omega}
with
Y\triangle\{i\}
, and fixes all
(j,a)
where
j\neq i
. We need to define each
\psi_{z}:\mathcal{M}_{z}\cong\mathcal{N}_{h_{\{i\}}(z)}
.

1.

We have
\mathcal{N}_{h_{\{i\}}(i,0)}=\mathcal{N}_{(i,1)}=\mathcal{M}_{(i,1)}
and
\mathcal{N}_{h_{\{i\}}(i,1)}=\mathcal{N}_{(i,0)}=\mathcal{M}_{(i,0)}
, so we may take
\psi_{(i,0)}:=\widehat{\theta}
and
\psi_{(i,1)}:=\widehat{\theta}^{-1}
.

2.

For
j\neq i
and
a\in\{0,1\}
,
\mathcal{N}_{h_{\{i\}}(j,a)}=\mathcal{N}_{(j,a)}=\mathcal{M}_{(j,a)}
, so let
\psi_{(j,a)}:=id_{\mathcal{M}_{(j,a)}}
.

3.

For
|Y|
even, take
\psi_{Y}:=\alpha_{Y,Y\triangle\{i\}}:\mathcal{M}_{Y}\cong\mathcal{N}_{Y\triangle\{i\}}=\mathcal{N}_{h_{\{i\}}(Y)}
.

4.

For
|Y|
odd, take
\psi_{Y}:=\beta_{Y,Y\triangle\{i\}}:\mathcal{M}_{Y}\cong\mathcal{N}_{Y\triangle\{i\}}=\mathcal{N}_{h_{\{i\}}(Y)}
.

Then all
\psi_{z}
are uniformly computable in
z
except for
\psi_{(i,0)}=\widehat{\theta}
and
\psi_{(i,1)}=\widehat{\theta}^{-1}
. Since
h_{i}
is computable,
\rho=h_{\{i\}}\cup\bigcup_{z\in H}\psi_{z}\equiv_{T}\widehat{\theta}\equiv_{T}\theta
.
∎

Lemma 34
.

Let
\rho:\mathcal{M}\cong\mathcal{N}
be any isomorphism. Then there is an
n<\omega
and
\theta:\mathcal{A}_{n}\cong\mathcal{B}_{n}
such that
\rho\geq_{T}\theta
.

Proof.

By Proposition
31
,
\rho=h_{X}\cup\bigcup_{z\in H}\psi_{z}
for some
X\in[\omega]^{<\omega}
and
\psi_{z}:\mathcal{M}_{z}\cong\mathcal{N}_{h_{X}(z)}
for each
z\in H
. If
X=\emptyset
, then
h_{X}=id_{H}
, so
\rho\geq_{T}\rho{\upharpoonright}_{M_{\emptyset}}=\psi_{\emptyset}:\mathcal{M}_{\emptyset}\cong\mathcal{N}_{\emptyset}
. Since
\mathcal{M}_{\emptyset}
and
\mathcal{N}_{\emptyset}
are computably isomorphic to
\mathcal{A}_{0}
and
\mathcal{B}_{0}
respectively,
\rho
computes an
\mathcal{A}_{0}\cong\mathcal{B}_{0}
isomorphism.

Now suppose
X\neq\emptyset
and pick any
i\in X
. Then
h_{X}
permutes
(i,0)
and
(i,1)
, so

\rho\geq_{T}\rho{\upharpoonright}_{M_{(i,0)}}=\psi_{(i,0)}:\mathcal{M}_{(i,0)}\cong\mathcal{N}_{h_{X}(i,0)}=\mathcal{N}_{(i,1)}.

Since
\mathcal{M}_{(i,0)}
and
\mathcal{N}_{(i,1)}
are computably isomorphic to
\mathcal{A}_{i+1}
and
\mathcal{B}_{i+1}
respectively,
\rho
computes an
\mathcal{A}_{i+1}\cong\mathcal{B}_{i+1}
isomorphism.
∎

We can now describe the isomorphism spectrum of
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{N}
in terms of the isomorphism spectra of their components:

Proposition 35
.

\textrm{IsoSpec}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})=\bigcup_{n<\omega}\textrm{IsoSpec}(\mathcal{A}_{i},\mathcal{B}_{i})
.

Proof.

It follows from Lemma
32
and Lemma
33
that
\bigcup_{i<\omega}\textrm{IsoSpec}(\mathcal{A}_{i},\mathcal{B}_{i})\subseteq\textrm{IsoSpec}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})
. It follows from Lemma
34
that
\textrm{IsoSpec}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})\subseteq\bigcup_{n<\omega}\textrm{IsoSpec}(\mathcal{A}_{i},\mathcal{B}_{i})
.
∎

As
\{\mathcal{A}_{i}:i<\omega\}
and
\{\mathcal{B}_{i}:i<\omega\}
were taken to be arbitrary, Theorem
29
follows.

3.3
An isomorphism spectrum that is not finitely generated

Recall that we say that an isomorphism spectrum is
finitely generated
if it is equal to the upward-closure of a finite set of Turing degrees. We apply Theorem
29
to results of Thomason
[
17
]
to construct an isomorphism spectrum that is not finitely generated.

We will consider the computable copies
(\omega,<_{X})
of
(\omega,<)
from Example
1
that encode c.e. sets
X\subseteq\omega
. Recall that for each c.e. set
X
, there is a unique isomorphism
f:(\omega,<)\cong(\omega,<_{X})
and
f\equiv_{T}X
.

We use a result of Thomason
[
17
]
to construct a uniformly computable family of copies of
(\omega,<)
so that the collection of isomorphisms between these structures and the usual copy,
(\omega,<)
, have pairwise disjoint Turing degrees.

Theorem 36

(Thomason, 1971
[
17
]
)
.

There is a sublattice of the c.e. degrees isomorphic to the lattice of all finite sets of natural numbers.

To prove this, Thomason constructs a uniformly c.e. collection of sets
\{X_{n}:n<\omega\}
, each with a fixed computable enumeration. For each
n<\omega
and finite
F\subseteq\omega
, they define

X_{F}:=\bigoplus_{m\in F}X_{m}\text{, }\textbf{d}_{n}:=\deg_{T}(X_{n})\text{, and }\textbf{d}_{F}:=\deg_{T}(X_{F})=\bigoplus_{m\in F}\textbf{d}_{m}

and show that for all
F,G\in[\omega]^{<\omega}
,

1.

\textbf{d}_{F}\leq\textbf{d}_{G}\Leftrightarrow F\subseteq G
,

2.

\textbf{d}_{F}\oplus\textbf{d}_{G}=\textbf{d}_{F\cup G}
,

3.

\textbf{d}_{F}\wedge\textbf{d}_{G}
exists and
\textbf{d}_{F}\wedge\textbf{d}_{G}=\textbf{d}_{F\cap G}
.

Remark 37
.

The collection
\{(\omega,<_{X_{n}}):n<\omega\}
is uniformly computable, and the set of degrees
\{\textbf{d}_{n}:n<\omega\}
is pairwise incomparable. Moreover
\textbf{d}_{n}=\deg_{T}(X_{n})
is the Turing degree of the unique isomorphism from
(\omega,<)
to
(\omega,<_{X_{n}})
.

Theorem 38
.

Define the uniformly computable collections
\textbf{A}:=\{\mathcal{A}_{n}:n<\omega\}
and
\textbf{B}:=\{\mathcal{B}_{n}:n<\omega\}
where for all
n<\omega
,
\mathcal{A}_{n}=(\omega,<)
, and
\mathcal{B}_{n}=(\omega,<_{X_{n}})
. Let
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{N}
be the two computable copies from Theorem
29
. Then
\textrm{IsoSpec}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})
is not finitely generated.

Proof.

By Theorem
29
,

\textrm{IsoSpec}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})=\bigcup_{n<\omega}\textrm{IsoSpec}\big((\omega,<),(\omega,<_{X_{n}})\big)=\bigcup_{n<\omega}\mathcal{D}_{\geq}(\textbf{d}_{n})

which is not finitely generated since the degrees
\{\textbf{d}_{n}:n<\omega\}
are pairwise incomparable.
∎

4
Automorphism spectra of computably composite structures

Harizanov, Morozov, and Miller
[
13
]
have considered similar questions about
automorphism spectra
which have some interesting connections to our results. We would like to thank an anonymous referee for bringing this work to our attention.

Definition 39

(Harizanov, Morozov, Miller
[
13
]
Definition 1.1)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a computable structure. The
automorphism spectrum
of
\mathcal{M}
is the set

\text{AutSp}^{*}(\mathcal{M})=\{\deg_{T}(f):f\in\textrm{Aut}(\mathcal{M}){\smallsetminus}\{1_{\mathcal{M}}\}\}

where
1_{\mathcal{M}}
is the identity automorphism on
\mathcal{M}
.

They also consider the upward-closures of automorphism spectra under
\leq_{T}
for which we will write

\text{AutSp}^{*}_{\geq}(\mathcal{M})=\{\textbf{d}:\exists f\in\textrm{Aut}(\mathcal{M}){\smallsetminus}\{1_{\mathcal{M}}\}[f\leq_{T}\textbf{d}]\}.

Thus, AutSp
{}^{*}_{\geq}(\mathcal{M})
is equal to
\textrm{IsoSpec}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{M})
with the omission of the degrees that only compute the trivial isomorphism
1_{\mathcal{M}}:\mathcal{M}\cong\mathcal{M}
. It is known that the upward-closure of an automorphism spectrum is itself an automorphism spectrum:

Proposition 40

(Harizanov, Morozov, Miller
[
13
]
Proposition 7.1)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be any computable nonrigid structure. Then there exists a computable structure
\mathcal{C}
such that

\text{AutSp}^{*}(\mathcal{C})=\text{AutSp}^{*}_{\geq}(\mathcal{A}).

No automorphism spectrum has the form
\{\textbf{d}_{1},\textbf{d}_{2}\}
where
\textbf{d}_{1}
and
\textbf{d}_{2}
are incomparable (
[
13
]
Proposition 6.1). Since there are incomparable degrees
\textbf{d}_{1}
and
\textbf{d}_{2}
such that
\{\textbf{d}_{1}\}
and
\{\textbf{d}_{2}\}
are automorphism spectra (see
[
13
]
Proposition 2.1), the class of automorphism spectra is not closed under even finite unions. However, the following observation uses a uniform infinitary version of the proof of Lemma 4.1 in
[
13
]
to show that the class of
upward-closures
of automorphism spectra is closed under computable union, satisfying the analog of our Theorem
29
:

Observation 41
.

If
\textbf{A}=\{\mathcal{A}_{i}:i<\omega\}
is a uniformly computable collection of copies and
\mathcal{O}
is the structure with domain
\omega
and unary relations
U_{n}^{\mathcal{O}}=\{n\}
for each
n<\omega
, then
\text{AutSp}^{*}_{\geq}(\mathcal{O}[\textbf{A}])=\bigcup_{i<\omega}\text{AutSp}^{*}_{\geq}(\mathcal{A}_{i})
.

5
Categoricity spectra of some computably composite structures

We conclude by considering some basic properties of the categoricity spectra of computably composite structures on
\mathcal{H}
. We will consider structures of the form
\mathcal{H}[\{z\}\times\mathcal{A}:z\in H]
, which we will denote by
\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{A}]
, and their computable copies up to computable isomorphism. That is, we are concerned with the case where all components are isomorphic to a particular computable structure
\mathcal{A}
. For example, the structures in Theorem
38
are computable copies of
{\mathcal{H}[(\omega,<)]}
.

We may assume that computable copies of
\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{A}]
have the form
\mathcal{H}[\textbf{B}]
where
\textbf{B}=\{\mathcal{B}_{z}:z\in H\}
is a uniformly computable collection of copies of
\mathcal{A}
. The isomorphisms from
\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{A}]
to
\mathcal{H}[\textbf{B}]
are the functions
\rho=h_{X}\cup\bigcup_{z\in H}\psi_{z}
where
X\in[\omega]^{<\omega}
and
\psi_{z}:\{z\}\times\mathcal{A}\cong\mathcal{B}_{h_{X}(z)}
for all
z\in H
.
\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{A}]
is
d
-computably categorical if and only if
d
computes some such
\rho
for every uniformly computable collection of copies
\{\mathcal{B}_{z}:z\in H\}
. That is,
\mathcal{A}
must be
d
-computably categorical in a uniform way.

Definition 42
.

We will say that
\mathcal{A}
is
uniformly
d
-computably categorical
if there is a partial
d
-computable binary function
g
such that whenever
\varphi_{e}
is the atomic diagram of a copy
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}
of
\mathcal{A}
,
g(e,\cdot)
is defined and is an isomorphism from
\mathcal{A}
to
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}
. We define the
uniform categoricity spectrum
of
\mathcal{A}
and
degree of uniform categoricity
of
\mathcal{A}
in the analogous ways.

It is not hard to see that if
\mathcal{A}
is uniformly
d
-computably categorical, then
\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{A}]
is
d
-computably categorical. In some examples, the opposite implication holds as well:

Example 43
.

The simplest case is when
\mathcal{A}
is uniformly computably categorical, so
\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{A}]
is computably categorical. Then the categoricity spectrum of
\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{A}]
and the uniform categoricity spectrum of
\mathcal{A}
are both
\mathcal{D}
, the set of all Turing degrees.

Example 44
.

The structure
(\omega,<)
has strong degree of categoricity
\mathbf{0}^{\prime}
and also uniform degree of categoricity
\mathbf{0}^{\prime}
. Every isomorphism between the copies
\mathcal{H}[(\omega,<)]
and
\mathcal{H}[(\omega,<_{\emptyset^{\prime}})]
as defined in Example
1
computes
\mathbf{0}^{\prime}
, so
\mathcal{H}[(\omega,<)]
has strong degree of categoricity
\mathbf{0}^{\prime}
, as do the structures in Theorem
38
.

By the same argument, if
\mathcal{A}
has strong degree of categoricity
d
and uniform degree of categoricity
d
, then
\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{A}]
has strong degree of categoricity
d
.

Example 45
.

The structure
(\omega,S)
where
S(n)=n+1
is computably categorical, but has uniform degree of categoricity
\mathbf{0}^{\prime}
. We can construct a uniformly computable collection of copies
\{\mathcal{A}_{n}:n<\omega\}
each with universe
\omega
, such that the
S
-least element of
\mathcal{A}_{n}
is
0
if and only if
n\notin\emptyset^{\prime}
. Then defining
\alpha:H\to\omega
so that
\alpha(X)=0
for
X\in[\omega]^{<\omega}
and
\alpha(i,a)=i+1
for
i<\omega
and
a<2
, the collection
\{\{z\}\times\mathcal{B}_{\alpha(z)}:z\in H\}
is uniformly computable. So
\mathcal{H}[\{z\}\times\mathcal{B}_{\alpha(z)}:z\in H]
is a computable copy of
\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{A}]
, but every isomorphism between these two copies computes
\emptyset^{\prime}
. So
\mathcal{H}[(\omega,S)]
also has strong degree of categoricity
\mathbf{0}^{\prime}
.

However, it appears that the full power of uniform
d
-computable categoricity of
\mathcal{A}
may not be required for
\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{A}]
to be
d
-computably categorical since the following weakening is sufficient:

Definition 46
.

If
\textbf{C}=\{\mathcal{C}_{i}:i<\omega\}
is a uniformly computable collection of copies of
\mathcal{A}
, we say that
\mathcal{A}
is
uniformly
d
-computably categorical within
C
if there is a total
d
-computable binary function
h
such that for all
i<\omega
,
h(i,\cdot)
is an isomorphism from
\mathcal{A}
to
\mathcal{C}_{i}
.

Proposition 47
.

\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{A}]
is
d
-computably categorical if and only if
\mathcal{A}
is uniformly
d
-computably categorical within
C
for every uniformly computable collection
C
of copies of
\mathcal{A}
.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{A}]
is
d
-computably categorical and let
\textbf{C}=\{\mathcal{C}_{i}:i<\omega\}
be a uniformly computable collection of copies of
\mathcal{A}
. Define
\alpha:H\to\omega
so that
\alpha(X)=0
for all
X\in[\omega]^{<\omega}
, and
\alpha(i,a)=i+1
for
i<\omega
and
a<2
. Then
\{\{z\}\times\mathcal{C}_{\alpha(z)}:z\in H\}
is a pairwise disjoint uniformly computable collection of copies of
\mathcal{A}
. Since
\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{A}]
is
d
-computably categorical, there is a
d
-computable isomorphism
\rho:\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{A}]\cong\mathcal{H}[\{z\}\times\mathcal{C}_{\alpha(z)}:z\in H]
and there is an
X\in[\omega]^{<\omega}
such that
\rho{\upharpoonright}_{\{\emptyset\}\times A}:\{\emptyset\}\times\mathcal{A}\cong\{X\}\times\mathcal{C}_{0}
and
\rho{\upharpoonright}_{\{(i,0)\}\times A}:\{(i,0)\}\times\mathcal{A}\cong\{(i,X(i))\}\times\mathcal{C}_{i+1}
for each
i<\omega
. Now define a function
h
so that
h(0):=\pi_{2}(\rho{\upharpoonright}_{\{\emptyset\}\times A}(X))
and
h(i+1):=\pi_{2}(\rho{\upharpoonright}_{\{(i,0)\}\times A}(i,X(i)))
where
\pi_{2}
is the projection onto the second coordinate. Then
h
is total
d
-computable and
h(i)
is an isomorphism from
\mathcal{A}
to
\mathcal{C}_{i}
for all
i<\omega
.

Now suppose that
\mathcal{A}
is uniformly
d
-computably categorical within
C
for every uniformly computable collection
C
of copies of
\mathcal{A}
and let
\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{B}_{z}:z\in H]
be an arbitrary computable copy of
\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{A}]
. Fix a computable bijection
\eta:\omega\to H
. Then
\{\mathcal{B}_{\eta(i)}:i<\omega\}
is uniformly computable, so there is a total
d
-computable function
h
such that
h(i)
is an isomorphism from
\mathcal{A}
to
\mathcal{B}_{\eta(i)}
. Define a function
\rho:\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{A}]\to\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{B}_{z}:z\in H]
by so that for
z\in H
and
a\in A
,
\rho(z):=z
and
\rho(z,a):=h(\eta^{-1}(z),a)
. Then
\rho{\upharpoonright}_{\{z\}\times A}
is an isomorphism from
\{z\}\times\mathcal{A}
to
\mathcal{B}_{\eta(\eta^{-1}(z))}=\mathcal{B}_{z}
. Thus,
\rho
is a
d
-computable isomorphism from
\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{A}]
to
\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{B}_{z}:z\in H]
.
∎

In the previous examples,
\mathcal{A}
was uniformly
d
-computably categorical exactly when
\mathcal{A}
was uniformly
d
-computably categorical within every uniformly computable collection of copies. It is not clear whether this is true in general:

Question 48
.

Is there a computable structure
\mathcal{A}
and a Turing degree
d
such that
\mathcal{A}
is not uniformly
d
-computably categorical, but is uniformly
d
-computably categorical within every uniformly computable collection of copies of
\mathcal{A}
?

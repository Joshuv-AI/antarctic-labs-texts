Actor-Critic based Online Data Mixing For Language Model Pre-Training
Jing Ma, Chenhao Dang, Mingjie Liao

Abstract
The coverage and composition of pretraining data significantly impacts the generalization ability of Large Language Models (LLMs). To reduce the carbon footprint and financial costs of training, some data mixing methods, which applied the optimized domain weights of a small proxy model to train a larger one, were proposed. However, these methods did not evolute with the training dynamics. The existing online data mixing (ODM) method addressed this limitation by applying the multi-armed bandit algorithm as data sampling strategy. Yet, it did not consider the intra-domain interactions. In this paper, we develop an actor-critic based online data mixing (AC-ODM) method, which captures the varying domain weights by auxiliary actor-critic networks and consider the intra-domain interactions with the reward function. While constructing the dataset to pretrain a large target LLM, we directly apply the actor, which is trained with a small proxy LLM as the environment, as the sampling strategy. The transfer of sampling strategy can not only ensure the efficiency of dynamical data mixing, but also expedite the convergence of pretraining the target LLM. Numerical results demonstrate that AC-ODM-410M, which invokes the sampling strategy obtained by a proxy LLM with 410M parameters, reaching the optimal validation perplexity of ODM 71 % 71\% faster, and improves performance on the zero-shot MMLU benchmark by 27.5 % 27.5\% of accuracy, about 2.23 ​ × 2.23× better on pass@1 of HumanEval benchmark.

Introduction

The dataset, which contains data from various domains, is crucial for pretraining large language models (LLMs), due to its significant impact on effectiveness
John and Draper 1975
;
Du et al. 2022
. In particular, when the pretraining corpus is fixed, the quantity and composition of the selected data primarily determine the amount of information absorbed, as well as the model’s convergence speed
Lee et al. 2022
;
Sorscher et al. 2023
;
Xie et al. 2023b
;
Albalak et al. 2024
. However, the domain weights of almost all current state-of-the-art LLMs are determined by heuristics
Gao et al. 2020
, raising the question of whether a more performant set of weights could be found.

Data mixing methods try to optimize the weights of the data domains to improve training efficiency and model performance
Xie et al. 2023a
;
Fan et al. 2024
;
Albalak et al. 2023
;
Xia et al. 2024
. There are two main categories: methods that determine the weights
apart from model training (offline) and those that determine the weights during model training (online)
Albalak et al. 2024
. The representative offline method DOREMI
Xie et al. 2023a
started the two-stage training mode. Firstly, a well-trained small-scale “reference” model was obtained using uniform domain weights, then the weights were adjusted by maximizing the information gained of a “proxy” model over the “reference” model. Secondly, it transferred the domain weights as sampling probability to pretrain the target large-scale base model. However, apart from training two auxiliary models, the goal of minimizing the worst-case loss gap is slightly different from obtaining a well-trained model.
Following this mode, the domain reweighting with generalization estimation (DoGE)
Fan et al. 2024
optimized the gradient alignment between each domain and all other domains, instead of optimizing excess loss in the first stage. However,
Xie et al. 2023a
showed that in this two-stage mode, the domain weights did not transfer well across models due to the inability to adapt to changing dynamics during pretraining. It required training a new model to determine the best weights for each new architecture or tokenizer, which reduced the effectiveness and efficiency of the two-stage offline framework.

Follow the principle that the best domain weights to train on are those that adapt to changing dynamics of training the ultimate target model. Motivated by the goal of maximizing information gain during model training, the online data mixing (ODM)
Albalak et al. 2023
optimized the distribution of data from various sources using an online mode. To minimize computation, it viewed each data domain as an arm of a multi-armed bandit (MAB) based on EXP3
Auer et al. 2002
and formulated the reward for each domain as its loss. The design of rewards and strategies would steer the MAB algorithm to prioritize learning from data domains with larger losses. Hence, data domains whose semantic space distributions differ more from that of current LLM’s predictions are prioritized for training. On the other hand, this reward implicitly assumed that data qualities across domains were consistent and ignored complex intra-domain interactions. From a data perspective, the data not only assist in the knowledge construction of the corresponding domain, but also contribute to accelerate the learning of other domains due to lexical, syntactic, or semantic overlap. By focusing on the domains with stronger intra-domain interactions in the early stage of pretraining, we could accelerate the pretraining process.

In summary, this paper proposes an online data mixing method based on the actor-critic (AC) algorithm, which models the impacts of data domain weights on the LLM pretraining process with a reinforcement learning (RL) model. Inspired by DoGE, we define the reward as the measure of the gradient alignment
W
, which reflects both the contribution to the loss and the intra-domain interactions. More precisely, by maximizing the expected reward, we prioritize learning datasets with the strongest commonalities and tasks with the greatest generality, thereby accelerating the convergence of pretraining. In terms of the solution, we adopt the deep deterministic policy gradient (DDPG) method. By invoking the AC algorithm, this method is applicable when the state space and action space are continuous, and allows for independent execution of the actor for data mixing inference on datasets composed of the same data domains (e.g., The Pile, which consists of 22 data domains). To pretraining a large target LLM, we can first optimize an online data mixing policy with the environment provided by a small proxy LLM, then apply the policy to sampling the batch data dynamically during training the target LLM.

The main contributions of this work are two-fold.

1.

We improve ODM by considering the intra-domain interactions. Following the idea of DoGE, we design the reward to emphasize the intra-domain interactions. Furthermore, to implement the AC networks, we design the state representing the training status of the LLM.

2.

We improve the efficiency of the online data mixing by transferring the sampling strategy optimized by training a proxy LLM (410M) to the target LLM (1B). This two-stage method can also reduce the computational cost. More specifically, the proposed method converges to the validation perplexity of ODM 71% faster, improves performance on the zero-shot MMLU benchmark by 27.5% of accuracy, about 2.23 better on pass@1 of HumanEval benchmark, with about 0.4% overhead wall-clock time on training the small proxy model.

2.1
Problem formulation

Let
D=\{D_{1},...,D_{k}\}
be a large corpus composed of
k
domains for language model pretraining. We aim to find the optimal weights of the domains on the probability simplex
\alpha\in\Delta^{k}\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
. The final data used to train the language model is constructed by first sampling a domain according to the domain-wise distribution
\alpha
, followed by uniformly sampling a batch
B
from the selected domain, namely
B\sim UNIF(D_{i})
. In general, this leads to the instance-wise distribution
P_{\alpha}\triangleq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}\cdot UNIF(D_{i})
.
In offline data mixing methods,
P_{\alpha}
is determined before training. In online data mixing,
P_{\alpha}
is computed at each iteration. This work addresses the challenge of efficiently updating
P_{\alpha}
with minimum overhead wall-clock time.

2.2
Adapting AC to online data mixing

We adopt the deep deterministic policy gradient (DDPG) framework to attack the problem of online data mixing by formulating it as a Markov decision process (MDP).
As illustrated in Figure
2
, the LLM provides the environment represented by the status
s^{t}
. The agent takes action
a^{t}
given by the policy
\mu(s^{t})
to adjust the domain weights
\alpha
, therefore, formulate the sampling strategy
P_{\alpha}
.
More precisely, for each iteration at step
t\in\{1,2,...,T\}
, the agent obtains the state
s^{t}
according to observations of the training status of the LLM, which contains the number of samples for each domain
n=\{n_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
, iteration step
t
, the loss of each domain
\ell(\theta_{M},B)=\{\ell_{i}(\theta_{M},B_{i})\}_{i=1}^{k}
and its difference with the domain loss of last step
\Delta\ell(\theta_{M},B)=\{\Delta\ell_{i}(\theta_{M},B_{i})\}_{i=1}^{k}
, as well as the
L_{2}
-norm of weights of specified LLM layers
||\omega||_{2}
and the norm of their difference with those of last step
||\Delta\omega||_{2}
. In order to maximize the expected cumulative rewards, which is denoted as
\mathbb{E}[\sum_{t=0}^{T}\gamma^{t}r_{i}^{t}]
, the agent takes the action of adjusting the weight of the data domain from
\alpha_{i}^{t}
to
\alpha_{i}^{t+1}
and provides the sampling strategy
P_{\alpha_{i}^{t+1}}
. We feedback to the environment by training the LLM on the newly sampled data, resulting in the status
s^{t+1}
. Considering that both the action space and the state space are continuous, we select the DDPG as the optimizer.

In summary, the four-tuple
(s^{t},a^{t},r^{t},\mu^{t})
for an agent environment is defined as:

s^{t}=(n,t,\ell(\theta_{M},B),\Delta\ell(\theta_{M},B),||\omega||_{2},\|\Delta\omega\|_{2})
is the state of the agent.

a^{t}=[a^{t}_{1},a^{t}_{2},...,a^{t}_{k}]
is the action of the agent,
k
is the number of data domains.

r^{t}=[r^{t}_{1},r^{t}_{2},...,r^{t}_{k}]
is the rewards from the environment.

\mu_{\theta_{A}}:s^{t}\to a_{i}^{t+1}

is the deterministic policy that the agent will take action
a^{t+1}
at the next iteration according to the current state
s^{t}
.

2.3
Designing the reward function

To ensure efficient and stable convergence of the LLM pretraining, there are two main concerns related to the reward function. One is to ensure that the sampling strategy favors data with the highest contribution to learning other domains. The other is to avoid overfocusing on a fixed set of domains. Inspired by DoGE
Fan et al. 2024
, we define the reward of each domain to be its gradient alignment score:

W_{i}\triangleq<\nabla\ell_{i}(\theta_{M}),\sum_{j\in[k]}\nabla\ell_{j}(\theta_{M})>
, where
\nabla\ell_{i}(\theta_{M})
is the stochastic gradient for
B_{i}
, and
B_{i}
is the set containing samples from data domain
i
in the batch.
This quantity measures the alignment of learning tasks across domains. A high
W_{i}
means that learning
B_{i}
contributes more to learning other domains. We write
W=[W_{1},...,W_{k}]\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
for the vectorized alignment scores of all domains and assign the agent reward at step
t
as
r_{i}^{t}=W_{i}^{t}
.
Moreover, we use the moving average of the weighted importance to ensure a smooth evolution of the reward,
\hat{r}_{i}^{t}=\xi\hat{r}_{i}^{t-1}+(1-\xi)\frac{r_{i}^{t}}{P_{\alpha_{i}}^{t-1}}
.
Here, the denominator
P_{\alpha_{i}}^{t-1}
is applied to avoid overfocusing on certain domains having high sampling rates.

The primary overhead wall-clock time incurred from the computation of
W
. Although the storage pressure of
W
can be alleviated to CPU, the storage burden of
\nabla\ell(\theta_{M})
grows exponentially with increasing model size and the number of data domains, leading to a heightened Memory Access Cost (MAC). Fortunately, the AC-ODM algorithm takes advantage of enabling individually executable data mixing strategies trained on the same dataset, effectively bypassing the time-consuming issue posed by the MAC. More precisely, we first train the actor with the environment provided by a proxy LLM. Then, we conduct pretraining for the target model on the mixing data dynamically sampled by the actor at each iteration. In this way, the reward calculation in updating the AC networks occurs only during the training of the small proxy LLM, and the pressure due to the computation of
W
is minimized.

2.4
Model update

Each training iteration requires updating the parameters of three models:
\theta_{M}
of LLM,
\theta_{C}
of critic and
\theta_{A}
of actor.

Firstly, updating
\theta_{M}
. The current state
s^{t}
is produced by the LLM environment, and an action is determined by the current policy, i.e., sampling a batch
B_{i}
of data according to the current data domain weights. Then the loss
\ell_{i}(\theta_{M}^{t})
is obtained by training
B_{i}
with the proxy model. Subsequently, the proxy model
\theta_{M}(\alpha)
undergoes an update process that incorporates the rescaling factor
\alpha
:

\theta_{M}^{t+1}\triangleq\theta_{M}^{t}-\eta^{t}\sum_{i\in[k]}\alpha_{i}^{t}\nabla\ell_{i}(\theta_{M}^{t})

where
\alpha\in\Delta^{k}
is used to re-weight the loss from each domain at step
t
,
\eta^{t}
is the step size, and
\nabla\ell_{i}(\theta_{M}^{t})
is the stochastic gradient for samples of
B_{i}
.

Secondly, updating
\theta_{C}
and
\theta_{A}
. We denote the critic as
Q_{\theta_{C}}(s,a)
, and the actor as
\mu_{\theta_{A}}(s)
. The agent integrates the reward
r^{t}=W^{t}
and the state
s^{t+1}=(n,t,\ell(\theta_{M},B),\Delta\ell(\theta_{M},B),||\omega||_{2},\|\Delta\omega\|_{2})
, then stores the tuple
(s^{t},a^{t},r^{t},s^{t+1})
in the replay buffer. Based on N tuples
\{(s^{j},a^{j},r^{j},s^{j+1})\}_{j}^{N}
sampled from the replay buffer, the actor-critic (AC) network is used to compute the temporal difference (TD) target,

y_{j}=r^{j}+\gamma Q_{\theta_{C}}(s^{j+1},\mu_{\theta_{A}}(s^{j+1})).

Update the critic by minimizing the target loss.

L=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(y_{j}-Q_{\theta_{C}}(s^{j},a^{j}))^{2}.

Update the actor by computing the gradient of the sampling policy.

\nabla_{\theta_{A}}J\approx\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\nabla_{\theta_{A}}\mu_{\theta_{A}}(s^{j})\nabla_{a}Q_{\theta_{C}}(s^{j},a)|_{a=\mu_{\theta_{A}}(s^{j})}.

Furthermore, to avoid the instability in neural network training caused by updates of both the actor and the critic, we adhere to the original strategy of DDPG by retaining the target networks.

3
Experimental Setup

The experimental setup considers dataset selection, model training protocols, baseline configurations and evaluation criteria. For the AC networks, we include a detailed elaboration on state setting and reward setting. All experiments were completed under a unified hardware environment, namely: an Intel(R) Xeon(R) Platinum 8468 CPU and 8 NVIDIA H800 GPUs, each with 80 GB of memory.

LLM training

We utilize The Pile
Gao et al. 2020
, an open-source language modeling dataset comprising 825GB of text from 22 diverse sources, including YouTube Subtitles, GitHub, Wikipedia, and so on. To train our decoder-only Transformer model, we adopt a modified version of the GPT-NeoX library
Black et al. 2022
. Across all experiments, we configure our model following Pythia
Biderman et al. 2023
and train a 1-billion-parameter model. Each GPU processes a micro-batch of 8 sequences, and we employ gradient accumulation across 8 GPUs with the accumulation step being 18, resulting in an effective batch size of 1152 samples. Specifically, for each batch, we initially conduct random sampling of
10\%
of the samples from each domain to capture intra-domain relationships and to uphold the exploration and exploitation policy of AC network.
The sequence length is set to 1024, and we apply the sequence packing
Roberts et al. 2022
. The model is trained in 41,667 steps, corresponding to 50 billion tokens. During the first 833-step warmup phase, instead of using domain weights provided by AC, we apply domain weights from The Pile with an added Gaussian noise sampled from
N(0,0.02)
.

AC training

AC networks and LLM undergo the same number of warmup and main training steps, with a cosine decay learning rate schedule that starts at 0.01 and decays to 0.001 during the course of training. During the warmup phase, AC networks are trained using the replaybuffer
\mathcal{B}
. To initialize the actor, we use the domain weights of the LLM during warmup as labels (those of The Pile with an added Gaussian noise) and optimize using MSE Loss. For the critic, we initialize the labels as
(1+\gamma)r^{t}
and optimize with MSE Loss. In the main training phase, we sample 256 tuples from
\mathcal{B}
per iteration as training samples, and dispatch 32 tuples as a micro-batch to each GPU. We employ gradient accumulation across 8 GPUs with an accumulation step of 1, resulting in an effective batch size of 256 samples. The Pythia-1B and AC networks can be found in the Appendix
A
.

State Setting

We design the representation of states from six perspectives: 1. Number of samples in each domain in the current step. 2. The current training step. 3. Losses of all domains in the current training step. 4. Variations of the losses of the current domain from the last step. 5. The
L^{2}
norm of weights of the selected layers of the LLM. 6. The norm of the variation of weights of the selected layers. The selected layers of Pythia-1B model are the Transformer layers with even indexes along with the first one.

Reward Setting

To balance the efficiency and capability of pretraining the LLM, we carefully select partial parameters of the LLM to participate in the calculation of rewards. More precisely, for the Pythia-1B framework, we choose the last full connected layers of the transformers indexed with 12, 14 and 16 (50,331,648 parameters) as the model representation to calculate the rewards. In this way, we dramatically drop the computational overhead while keeping the representation effective.

Baselines

The original domain weights suggested by The Pile
Gao et al. 2020
refer to as The Pile Weights (TPW) serve as a baseline. Furthermore, the domain weights proposed by ODM and DoReMi computed with a 50k vocabulary tokenizer
Albalak et al. 2023
are also considered.

Evaluation

We perform a comprehensive evaluation to compare the proposed method with baselines. First, we measure the perplexity on both validation and test sets across all domains of the Pile dataset. To assess the performance of downstream tasks, we evaluate on 57 diverse multiple-choice tasks from the MMLU
Hendrycks et al. 2021
benchmark, employing 0-shot and 5-shot in-context learning for each task. Furthermore, we extend our evaluation to code completion using HumanEval
Chen et al. 2021
dataset, thereby verifying model performance in programming tasks.

Improvement of perplexity to baselines

The reward of AC-ODM selects data from domains bringing forward gain to other domains by emphasizing intra-domain interactions. Therefore, exploit the data carrying shared information across domains to accelerate the training process. Figure
3
shows the test perplexity of each method on held-out data as well as the average perplexity.
AC-ODM-410M outperforms all other methods across all 22 domains, and achieves a minimum
20\%
relative improvement over the original ODM baseline in 17 out of 22 domains. AC-ODM outperforms the others in 14 domains, and performs close to the best method in the remaining 8 domains. We investigate in the 12 domains with data proportion below
3\%
, AC-ODM-410M and AC-ODM leads in 11 domains with apparent lower perplexities. This observation illustrates that our algorithm prefers to optimize domains with small or medium size.
In the 4 domains where the data proportion exceeds
10\%
, AC-ODM-410M consistently outperforms other methods by a significant margin. These results depict that well-trained AC networks can adequately capture intra-domain interactions, and the sampling strategy can dynamically leverage the data strengths of all domains by prioritizing samples that maximize cross-domain knowledge (i.e., data with the highest contribution to learning other domains). This further demonstrates the effectiveness of the reward mechanism across different data distributions. Consequently, AC-ODM achieves the lowest global perplexity throughout the training process, validating its robustness in multi-domain pretraining scenarios.

Effect of proxy model size

Figure

The well-trained sampling strategy is able to select training data according to the training dynamics, while adjusting the sampling strategy during training (AC-ODM) cannot fully utilize the sampling strategy since the actor is not converged, especially at the early steps.
AC-ODM-70M performs the worst, which indicates that the model with less parameters may not gain the generalization ability required, yet make it to serve as a pool environment.
AC-ODM-160M performs very similar to AC-ODM-410M, especially at the early steps. This may due to the limit size of the target model. We believe the gap between 410M and 160M would enlarge if the size of the target model increased.
We will study if there would be extrapolation relationship between the sizes of the proxy and target LLMs in the future.

Conclusion

In this work, we propose the actor-critic based online data mixing. By regarding the gradient alignment score of each domain as its reward, we take into account the intra-domain interactions.
With regard to perplexity in numerical results, AC-ODM-410M obtained a lower perplexity to TPW, ODM and AC-ODM by
20.7\%
,
16.4\%
and
13.1\%
with 41667 steps. Moreover, it outperforms all other methods across all 22 domains. Consequently, AC-ODM-410M demonstrates superior performance in evaluations of downstream tasks. In 0-shot and 5-shot accuracy evaluations of MMLU, it outperforms ODM by
27.5\%
and
23.9\%
, respectively. In the HumanEval benchmark, it achieves a
2.23×
relative improvement over ODM. It shows that well-trained AC networks can adequately capture intra-domain interactions, and the sampling strategy can dynamically leverage the data strengths of all domains by prioritizing samples that maximize cross-domain knowledge.
The efficiency of AC-ODM is ensured by directly applying the well-trained sampling strategy of a proxy LLM to select data for the target LLM during pretraining. The numerical results demonstrate that the efficacy of AC-ODM, as the target model can achieve lower perplexity in fewer iterations on held-out data. Specifically, with the introduction of
0.4\%
overhead wall-clock time AC-ODM-410M spared
80\%
,
71\%
and
64\%
steps of TPW, ODM and AC-ODM to reach the same validation perplexity.
However, in this work, we do not investigate the optimal size of proxy LLM, which is not the main concern of current work. We believe that the optimal size of the proxy LLM referring to the size of the target model. In the future, we will investigate the relationship between the sizes of proxy and target LLMs to further improve the performance of online data mixing.

Appendix A
A Model Configuration

A.1
LLM Model Configuration

We adopt the sequence length of 1024 and employ a 16-layer Transformers architecture with a hidden size of 2048 and 16 attention heads. Rotary positional embedding
Su et al. 2023
is incorporated. We leverage FlashAttention
Dao et al. 2022
, which optimizes memory access and reduces computation overhead, to improve training efficiency. The model is trained using Adam optimizer
Kingma and Ba 2017
. The learning rate undergoes a linear warm-up for 833 iterations, gradually increasing from a minimum of 2.5e-5 to a peak of 2.5e-4, followed by a cosine decay back to 2.5e-5. We utilize the GPT-NeoX-20B tokenizer
Black et al. 2022
for text processing.

A.2
AC Networks Configuration

For both the actor and the critic, we employ a fully connected 6-layer neural network with 1024 neurons per hidden layer. Except for the output layer, each layer is followed by layer normalization and a ReLU activation. In the actor, the output layer is further processed by the softmax activation function, while in the critic, the output layer is post-processed by the identity activation function.

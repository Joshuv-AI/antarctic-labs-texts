Introduction

Gliomas are the most common and aggressive primary tumors of the central nervous system, accounting for approximately 80% of all malignant brain tumors [1]. Despite advances in surgical techniques, radiotherapy, and chemotherapy, the prognosis for glioma patients—especially those with high-grade gliomas such as glioblastoma multiforme (GBM)—remains dismal, with a median survival of less than 15 months [1–3]. The high degree of intratumoral heterogeneity, diffuse infiltration, and resistance to therapy contribute to treatment failure and recurrence [4, 5]. Therefore, identifying novel molecular biomarkers and therapeutic targets is critical for improving glioma diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment.

Vesicle-associated membrane proteins (VAMPs) are members of the SNARE (soluble N-ethylmaleimide-sensitive factor attachment protein receptor) family that play essential roles in vesicular transport, membrane fusion, and intracellular trafficking [6–8]. Recent pan-cancer analyses have suggested that VAMP5 may be upregulated in several cancer types, raising the possibility that it could be involved in tumor progression. However, no comprehensive study to date has explored the expression profile, functional implications, and clinical significance of VAMP5 in glioma. Understanding whether VAMP5 contributes to glioma pathophysiology could yield valuable insights into glioma biology and uncover new prognostic biomarkers or therapeutic targets.

In this study, we comprehensively analyzed the expression patterns and clinical relevance of VAMP5 in glioma using data from TCGA and GEO databases, as well as clinical specimens from our institution. We further investigated the molecular interactions and signaling pathways associated with VAMP5 using protein-protein interaction (PPI) networks and gene enrichment analyses. In addition, we explored the relationship between VAMP5 expression and immune cell infiltration in the glioma microenvironment, and evaluated its prognostic value using survival and Cox regression analyses. Finally, we performed functional experiments to assess the impact of VAMP5 knockdown on glioma cell proliferation, migration, and invasion. Our results suggest that VAMP5 promotes glioma progression and may serve as a potential prognostic marker and therapeutic target.

Materials and methods

Data collection and preprocessing

The mRNA expression profile of VAMP5 across various cancer types and corresponding normal tissues was obtained from the TCGA and GTEx databases [9]. In addition, relevant datasets—GSE4290, GSE50161, and GSE108474—were identified through a search of the published literature and the GEO database, and were incorporated into the study [10–12]. Data normalization, standardization, and visualization were conducted using the ‘limma’ package and other tools within the R software environment (version 4.2.2).

Single-cell RNA sequencing data processing

Single cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) datasets were retrieved from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) repository (GSE 138794). The raw expression matrix was processed using the Seurat package following a standardized workflow: Initial quality control (QC) was performed to retain genes expressed in ≥ 5 cells and cells with ≥ 40 detected genes; low-quality cells were further excluded during secondary screening, specifically those with a mitochondrial gene proportion > 20% or fewer than 300 detected genes. After normalization via the LogNormalize method (scale factor = 10,000), the FindVariableFeatures function was utilized to identify the top 2,500 highly variable genes for downstream analyses. Cell clustering was conducted by constructing a K-nearest neighbor network based on the first 24 principal components (resolution = 0.6), and the clustering results were visualized using Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP). The FeaturePlot function was applied to depict the expression distribution of core genes in the UMAP embedding, and single-cell expression levels were exported for subsequent validation.

Differential expression analysis of VAMP5

The Wilcoxon rank-sum test was applied to evaluate differential expression of VAMP5 across pan-cancer, with statistical significance defined as p < 0.05. Samples were divided into high- and low-expression groups based on the median expression level of VAMP5, followed by identification of differentially expressed genes (DEGs). DEGs were filtered using adjusted p < 0.05 and |log₂ fold change (FC)| > 2. Volcano plots were generated to visualize DEGs. A PPI network was constructed using the STRING database, and hub genes were identified using Cytoscape with the cytoHubba plugin [13]. Heatmaps of the top 30 VAMP5-correlated genes and hub genes were generated to assess co-expression patterns.

Functional enrichment analysis

Functional enrichment analyses, including Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analysis, were carried out on VAMP5-related differentially expressed genes (DEGs) utilizing the ‘clusterProfiler’ package in R. Additionally, Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) was performed with the curated gene set collection ‘c2.cp.all.v2022.1.Hs.symbols.gmt [All Canonical Pathways] (3050)’ to explore signaling pathways significantly associated with elevated VAMP5 expression [14]. Significance thresholds were set at a normalized enrichment score (NES) > 1 and a false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.05.

Immune infiltration analysis of VAMP5

The extent of immune cell infiltration within glioma specimens was assessed using the single-sample Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (ssGSEA) algorithm, as implemented in the GSVA package. Comparative analysis of immune infiltration between high and low VAMP5 expression cohorts was conducted via the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. To investigate potential associations between VAMP5 expression levels and specific immune cell populations, correlation analyses were performed. A statistically significant correlation was defined by a p-value < 0.001 and an absolute correlation coefficient exceeding 0.3.

Survival and prognostic analysis

Kaplan-Meier survival analysis was used to compare overall survival (OS), disease-specific survival (DSS), and progression-free interval (PFI) between high and low VAMP5 expression groups. The SurvivorROC package was utilized to construct receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves for evaluating the prognostic value of VAMP5 in glioma. Univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards models were used to evaluate the prognostic significance of VAMP5 and other clinical covariates. A nomogram and calibration curves were constructed to assess the predictive performance of a VAMP5-based survival model.

Clinical tissue specimens

Tumor tissue samples were obtained from patients diagnosed with GBM who underwent surgical resection at Jiangnan University Medical Center, Wuxi, China. Normal brain tissue samples were collected from patients undergoing surgery for traumatic brain contusions, where non-pathological pericontusional tissue was safely resected for clinical reasons. All specimens were immediately snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at − 80 °C or fixed in formalin for further analysis. None of the patients had received radiotherapy or chemotherapy prior to surgery. All patients provided written informed consent, and the study protocol was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee. Samples were used for qRT-PCR, western blotting, and immunohistochemical analysis to validate VAMP5 expression.

Immunohistochemical (IHC) analysis

Formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE) glioma and normal brain tissue Sect. (4 μm thick) were subjected to deparaffinization in xylene followed by rehydration through a graded ethanol series. Antigen retrieval was conducted by heating the sections in citrate buffer (pH 6.0) for 15 min. To quench endogenous peroxidase activity, slides were treated with 3% hydrogen peroxide for 10 min. After blocking with non-immune serum for 30 min at room temperature, the sections were incubated overnight at 4 °C with a primary anti-VAMP5 antibody (1:200 dilution; UpingBio, YP-Ab-09580). Subsequently, HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies were applied, and signal development was achieved using DAB substrate (Dako, USA). Nuclei were counterstained with hematoxylin, followed by dehydration and coverslip mounting. Immunoreactivity was assessed under a light microscope, and staining intensity and distribution were semi-quantitatively analyzed using the H-score method by two independent pathologists blinded to clinical information.

Cell culture and culture of stably transfected cell lines

Human glioma cell lines U87MG and LN229 were purchased from the Cell Resource Center of the Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences. Cells were cultured in DMEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) at 37°C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO₂. U87MG and LN229 cells were maintained in DMEM with 10% FBS and 1% penicillin-streptomycin. Lentiviral transfection was used to establish VAMP5-knockdown cell models. The human VAMP5-shRNA lentiviral vector was designed and produced by Wuxi Nash Biotech Co., Ltd., with the shRNA sequence: 5′-GACGGAAATTATGCGTAACAA-3′ (sh1-VAMP5), 5′-CCTTCAACAAGACTACACAGA-3′ (sh2-VAMP5), 5′-CGAACGAGGTGACGGAAATTA-3′ (sh3-VAMP5). Control and shRNA lentiviruses were labeled with GFP-Luc-Puro. Transfected cells were selected with puromycin-containing medium 48 h post-transfection, passaged 2–3 times, and cryopreserved at -80 °C. Stable cell lines were confirmed by green fluorescence visualization under a microscope.

Real-time PCR

Total RNA was extracted from tissues or cells using Trizol reagent (Thermo Fisher) according to the manufacturer’s protocol, followed by immediate reverse transcription. Gene-specific primers were as follows: VAMP5 (Forward 5′-ACCTTCAACAAGACTACACAGAACC-3′, Reverse 5′-GACGACCAGCAGCACAATCAG-3′), GAPDH (Forward 5′-GCGACACCCACTCCTCCAC-3′, Reverse 5′-ACCACCCTGTTGCTGTAGCC-3′). The relative expression of VAMP5 was calculated using the 2^–ΔΔCt method, with GAPDH as the internal control. All reactions were performed in triplicate.

Western blotting

Total proteins were extracted from tissue samples or cultured cells using RIPA lysis buffer (Beyotime, China) supplemented with protease and phosphatase inhibitors. Protein concentrations were quantified using the BCA assay kit (Thermo Scientific, USA). Equivalent amounts of protein (30–50 µg) were resolved on 10–12% SDS-PAGE gels and transferred onto PVDF membranes (Millipore, USA). Membranes were blocked with 5% non-fat milk in TBST for 1 h at room temperature, followed by overnight incubation at 4 °C with primary antibodies, including anti-VAMP5 (1:1000, UpingBio, YP-Ab-09580) and anti-GAPDH (1:1000, Proteintech, 10491-1-AP). After thorough washing, membranes were incubated with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies for 1 h at room temperature. Protein signals were developed using an enhanced chemiluminescence substrate (Millipore, USA) and visualized with a Bio-Rad Gel Doc EZ imaging system (Bio-Rad Laboratories, Hercules, CA, USA). All experiments were independently repeated at least three times to ensure reproducibility. As the blots had been cut prior to antibody hybridization, we have provided cropped images of all replicate experiments. The images and their corresponding legends have been uploaded as supplementary files.

Cell counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) assay

Logarithmically growing cells were trypsinized and seeded into 96-well plates at 2000 cells/well in 100 µL medium. Cell viability was measured at 0, 24, 48, 72, and 96 h post-transfection by adding 10 µL CCK-8 reagent (Nanjing Novizan Biotech Co., Ltd.) and incubating at 37 °C for 2 h. Absorbance at 450 nm was measured using an XT-96DJ ELISA reader.

Colony formation assay

Cells were seeded into 6-well plates at 1500 cells/well in 2 mL DMEM with 10% FBS. After two weeks of incubation at 37 °C, cells were fixed with ice-cold methanol and stained with crystal violet (Solarbio, China) for 20 min. Colonies were visualized and counted under a light microscope.

Wound healing assay

Cells (1 × 10⁶ cells/well) were cultured in 6-well plates until confluent. Wounds were created using a sterile 200-µL pipette tip, and medium was replaced with DMEM containing 1% FBS. Images were captured at 0 and 24 h using an inverted microscope, and relative migration rates were calculated by normalizing to the initial wound width.

Transwell assay

Cell migration was evaluated using Transwell chambers with 8-µm pore polycarbonate membranes (Corning, USA). A total of 1 × 10⁵ cells suspended in 200 µL of serum-free medium were seeded into the upper chambers, while the lower chambers were filled with 700 µL of medium containing 20% fetal bovine serum (FBS) as a chemoattractant. After 24 h of incubation at 37 °C, non-migrated cells on the upper surface of the membrane were gently removed with a cotton swab. Migrated cells adhering to the lower surface were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, stained with crystal violet, and visualized under a light microscope for quantification.

Xenograft assay

All animal experiments were conducted with approval from the Animal Ethics Committee of Jiangnan University. SPF-grade male nude mice aged 5–6 weeks (approximately 20 g) were used in the experiments. All mice were maintained under a 12-hour light–dark cycle with free access to food and water. Tumor cells were injected subcutaneously into the right inguinal region of the mice. Tumor volume and body weight were monitored every five days, and tumor volume was calculated using the formula: length × width² / 2.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software (version 22.0) and GraphPad Prism (version 10). Group comparisons were conducted using either Student’s t-test or the Wilcoxon rank-sum test, depending on data distribution. Kaplan–Meier survival curves were analyzed using the log-rank test. A p-value less than 0.05 was considered indicative of statistical significance.

Results

VAMP5 is highly expressed in glioma tissues

Pan-cancer analysis using TCGA datasets revealed that VAMP5 expression is significantly upregulated across multiple cancer types, including glioblastoma multiforme (GBM) and low-grade glioma (LGG) (P < 0.05, Fig. 1A). This observation was further validated using independent GEO datasets (GSE4290, GSE50161 and GSE108474), which consistently showed significantly higher VAMP5 expression in glioma samples (Fig. 1B–D). To corroborate these findings, we performed qRT-PCR, western blotting, and IHC on clinical specimens from our hospital, confirming that VAMP5 expression is markedly elevated in glioma tissues compared to normal brain tissues (Fig. 1E–G).

scRNA-seq confirmed distinct distribution heterogeneity of VAMP5 within glioma cells

When analyzing the scRNA-seq data of the glioma dataset, this study established stringent QC criteria. To eliminate interference from low-quality samples in subsequent analyses, each sample was required to meet two concurrent conditions: containing at least 5 cells and having more than 200 detected genes, thus completing the initial data filtering. A scatter plot was utilized to visualize the distribution correlation between nCount (UMI count) and nFeature (number of detected genes) across cells from different samples, with results demonstrating a significant positive correlation (correlation coefficient = 0.92; Fig. 2A). Following data optimization, the clustering algorithm implemented in the Seurat package (resolution set to < 0.6) successfully identified multiple cell subpopulations with distinct molecular profiles, and their spatial distribution characteristics are illustrated in Fig. 2B. Through manual annotation, the clustered subpopulations were accurately classified into 9 cell types, including TPC (Tumor Progenitor Cell), NPC (Neural Progenitor Cell), OPC (Oligodendrocyte Precursor Cell), Astrocyte, Endothelia, Macrophages, Malignant, Oligo, and Neutrophils, with detailed classification results presented in Fig. 2C. Further analysis of the expression pattern of the VAMP5 gene in this scRNA-seq dataset revealed substantial variations in VAMP5 expression across different cell types, with prominent high expression observed in Astrocytes and Neutrophils (Fig. 2D).

PPI network analysis reveals functional interactions between VAMP5 and key hub genes

Differential expression analysis identified 715 DEGs between high and low VAMP5 expression groups (Fig. 3B). Among these, hub genes were identified, including members of the SNAP and STX gene families, which are known to be involved in intracellular trafficking, autophagy, and cell signaling (Fig. 3A). The interactions between these hub genes and VAMP5 were examined, and a co-expression heatmap was generated (Fig. 3C). Moreover, the co-expression pattern of the top 30 genes most closely related to VAMP5 further corroborated its potential role in glioma progression (Fig. 3D).

Functional enrichment analysis of VAMP5-associated genes

GO and KEGG enrichment analyses of VAMP5-associated DEGs demonstrated significant enrichment in terms related to glycosaminoglycan binding, peptidase regulator activity, and peptide receptor activity (Fig. 4A–C). Regarding the KEGG pathway enrichment assessment, the results indicated that VAMP5-associated DEGs were predominantly associated with PI3K-AKT pathway, JAK-STAT pathway, ECM-receptor interaction (Fig. 4D). GSEA further revealed that high VAMP5 expression is significantly associated with activation of several oncogenic signaling pathways, including the PI3K-AKT, SMAD2, JAK-STAT, and Notch pathways (Fig. 4E, F).

VAMP5 expression correlates with immune cell infiltration in the tumor microenvironment

Significant differences in immune infiltration scores were observed between the high and low VAMP5 expression groups across various immune cells, including cytotoxic cells, dendritic cells (DC), activated DCs (aDC), macrophages, T cells, immature DCs (iDC), NK CD56dim cells, and neutrophils (p < 0.05, Fig. 5A). Furthermore, using the ssGSEA algorithm, we assessed immune cell infiltration across glioma samples stratified by VAMP5 expression levels (Fig. 5B). Tumors with high VAMP5 expression showed significantly higher infiltration of cytotoxic cells, macrophages, and T cells, while infiltration of central memory T cells (Tcm) and gamma delta T cells (Tgd) cells was negatively correlated with VAMP5 levels. Subsequently, scatter plots were generated to illustrate the correlations between VAMP5 expression and the infiltration scores of individual immune cell types (Fig. 5C–I). In addition, a correlation chord plot was constructed to visualize the relationships between VAMP5 expression and the infiltration levels of various immune cells (Fig. 5J). These results further support a strong association between elevated VAMP5 expression and an immunologically active tumor microenvironment.

Association between VAMP5 expression and clinical features and prognosis

Clinical baseline data from 699 glioma samples in the TCGA database were compiled to investigate the association between VAMP5 expression and clinicopathological characteristics, as presented in Table 1. Clinical correlation analysis demonstrated that high VAMP5 expression is significantly associated with older age, higher WHO grade, IDH wild-type status, and absence of 1p/19q codeletion (Fig. 6A–E). ROC analysis revealed that VAMP5 expression could effectively differentiate tumors from normal tissues, with AUC of 0.833, indicating its potential diagnostic value (Fig. 6F). Kaplan-Meier analysis demonstrated that patients with high VAMP5 expression had significantly shorter OS, DSS, and PFS compared to those with low expression (p < 0.001, Fig. 6G–I), strongly indicating a close association between high VAMP5 expression and adverse clinical outcomes.

Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were conducted to evaluate the prognostic significance of common clinicopathological variables. In the univariate analysis, age, WHO grade, IDH mutation status, 1p/19q codeletion status, and VAMP5 expression were all significantly associated with overall survival (Fig. 7A). Variables demonstrating statistical significance were subsequently included in the multivariate Cox regression analysis. The results confirmed that these factors, including elevated VAMP5 expression, remained independently and significantly associated with overall survival (p < 0.05, Fig. 7B), indicating that high VAMP5 expression is an independent prognostic risk factor in glioma patients. Based on the multivariate Cox regression analysis, a prognostic nomogram was constructed incorporating age, WHO grade, IDH mutation status, and VAMP5 expression. To evaluate the predictive accuracy of the model, a calibration plot was generated, as shown in Fig. 7C. This model demonstrated a moderate predictive accuracy with a C-index of 0.781 (95% CI: 0.758–0.804). The bias-corrected line closely aligned with the ideal 45-degree reference line, indicating strong concordance between the predicted and observed survival outcomes (Fig. 7D).

VAMP5 knockdown suppresses glioma cell proliferation and migration

To functionally validate the role of VAMP5 in glioma, we established glioma cell lines with stable VAMP5 knockdown using shRNA (Fig. 8A, B). qRT-PCR confirmed a significant reduction in VAMP5 expression. Functional assays revealed that VAMP5 knockdown led to significant decreases in glioma cell proliferation, migration, and colony formation ability, indicating that VAMP5 promotes the malignant phenotype of glioma cells (Fig. 8C-M). Tumorigenicity assays further demonstrated that stable knockdown of VAMP5 in glioma cells suppressed their ability to induce tumor growth in nude mice (Fig. 8N–P).

Discussion

GBM represents the most prevalent and aggressive form of primary malignant brain tumor in adults, characterized by rapid proliferation, extensive infiltration, and remarkable molecular heterogeneity [15]. Its highly invasive nature and complex genetic landscape contribute to a dismal prognosis and pose significant therapeutic challenges [16]. GBM harbors an exceptionally intricate immune microenvironment, characterized by an abundance of immunosuppressive cells and a profound dysregulation of the anti-tumor immune response [17]. Current standard treatments, including surgical resection, radiotherapy, and chemotherapy, are often hampered by high recurrence rates and therapeutic resistance [18]. Consequently, the identification of novel molecular biomarkers for improved diagnosis, prognosis, and targeted therapy is of critical importance.

In this study, we investigate the expression of VAMP5 as a potential biomarker in gliomas. Through pan-cancer analysis, we found that VAMP5 is highly expressed in various types of tumors, including gliomas, as well as cholangiocarcinoma (CHOL), kidney renal clear cell carcinoma (KIRC), acute myeloid leukemia (LAML), liver hepatocellular carcinoma (LIHC), and pancreatic adenocarcinoma (PAAD). However, its role and underlying mechanisms in tumor initiation and progression remain unclear. A comprehensive proteome-wide Mendelian randomization (MR) analysis identified VAMP5 as one of eight novel circulating biomarkers for prostate cancer [6]. We demonstrate that VAMP5 is significantly upregulated at both mRNA and protein levels across glioma of different grades. This consistent overexpression suggests a pivotal role for VAMP5 in glioma progression. Moreover, elevated VAMP5 expression correlates with poorer patient survival, indicating its potential as a valuable prognostic biomarker for glioma.

This study offers novel insights into the molecular mechanisms underpinning tumor progression and their potential clinical implications, with particular emphasis on the roles of VAMP5 in cancer biology. Our integrative analyses identified 21 key hub genes associated with VAMP5 expression, notably members of the SNAP and STX families, which are central components of the SNARE complex—a critical mediator of vesicle fusion and membrane trafficking [19–21]. These proteins are known to regulate various cellular processes including signal transduction, immune responses, and extracellular matrix remodeling, all of which are fundamental to tumor invasion, metastasis, and modulation of the tumor microenvironment [22–25].

Functionally, GO enrichment analyses revealed that VAMP5-related DEGs are predominantly involved in glycosaminoglycan binding, protease regulation, and peptide receptor activity, suggesting that VAMP5 may promote tumor invasion by remodeling the extracellular matrix (ECM) microenvironment. The enrichment of glycosaminoglycan binding underscores the importance of extracellular matrix interactions in tumor invasion, and metastasis formation [26]. Meanwhile, the regulation of protease activity highlights the role of matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) and inhibitors such as TIMPs in facilitating tumor cell infiltration and dissemination—mechanisms that are well documented in cancer progression [27]. The involvement of peptide receptor activity suggests potential crosstalk with growth factor signaling pathways that influence proliferation and angiogenesis [28]. Furthermore, our KEGG results demonstrated that VAMP5-associated DEGs are significantly enriched in key oncogenic pathways such as PI3K- AKT pathway, JAK-STAT pathway, ECM-receptor interaction, which was cross-validated by GSEA. The PI3K-AKT pathway, in particular, is a well-established driver of cell survival, proliferation, and therapy resistance across multiple tumor types [29–31]. Abnormal JAK-STAT activation drives tumor immune evasion through PD-L1 upregulation, thereby impairing cytotoxic T cell-mediated antitumor responses [32]. The integrin-mediated extracellular matrix-receptor interaction signaling promotes the adhesion of tumor cells to the basement membrane and their subsequent migration [33].

Our findings reveal a complex immunomodulatory role of VAMP5 within the tumor microenvironment, which correlates positively with the infiltration of cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs), macrophages, and general T cells, yet negatively with the presence of Tcm and Tgd cells. In solid tumors, macrophage polarization toward the pro-tumor M2 phenotype impairs immune surveillance [34]. Although the infiltration of cytotoxic T cells and T cells is increased, VAMP5 may disrupt their functional regulation, leading to reduced tumor-killing activity and rendering their infiltration insufficient to effectively curb tumor progression [35]. Tcm and Tgd cells play pivotal roles in anti-tumor immunity: the former mediates rapid recall responses to tumor antigens, while the latter bridges innate and adaptive immune signaling [36, 37]. The negative correlation between VAMP5 and the infiltration of these cells indicates that VAMP5 drives the tumor microenvironment toward immune suppression, consistent with the poor prognosis observed in patients with high VAMP5 expression. Enhancing the proportion of Tcm and Tgd cells in the tumor microenvironment may improve clinical outcomes [38]. However, the inverse relationship between VAMP5 and these cells, combined with its impairment of other infiltrating immune cells, hinders tumor control—highlighting VAMP5 as a promising therapeutic target for cancer intervention. Targeting VAMP5 might restore the balance of effector and memory immune responses, enhancing the efficacy of immunotherapies. Overall, VAMP5 as a key modulatory node that not only influences tumor cell behavior but also shapes the immune landscape, presenting it as a promising candidate for therapeutic intervention aimed at overcoming immune resistance and improving prognosis.

Meanwhile, clinical analyses have established the prognostic significance of VAMP5 as a biomarker in glioma. Statistical analyses revealed that high VAMP5 expression is significantly associated with higher WHO pathological grades and shortened overall survival in patients. These findings were not only validated through rigorous multivariate Cox regression modeling but also further corroborated by a prognostic nomogram with robust predictive efficacy.

Our in vitro experiments demonstrated that stable shRNA-mediated knockdown of VAMP5 in human tumor cell lines leads to a profound impairment of tumor cell proliferation, and migration, indicating that VAMP5 is a pivotal regulator of tumor cell malignancy. The expression level of VAMP5 is closely associated with tumor progression and patient prognosis, positioning it as a promising biomarker for predicting tumor development. These discoveries lay a solid foundation for the development of VAMP5 targeted therapeutic strategies. However, the specific molecular mechanisms underlying its regulation of tumor cell behavior and modulation of the immune microenvironment remain elusive. Further thoroughly investigations, such as proteomic and transcriptomic analyses, are warranted to fully elucidate the complex regulatory networks associated with VAMP5, which may open new frontiers for cancer treatment and improve patient outcomes.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that VAMP5 is significantly overexpressed in glioma and is closely associated with tumor progression, immune cell infiltration, and poor clinical outcomes. These findings suggest that VAMP5 is not only a potential biomarker for glioma prognosis but also a promising target for therapeutic intervention. Further in vivo studies and mechanistic investigations are warranted to explore the translational potential of targeting VAMP5 in glioma treatment.

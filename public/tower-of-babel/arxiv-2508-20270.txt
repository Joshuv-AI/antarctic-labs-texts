p p -curvature operators and Satake-type phenomenon for 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 2 \mathfrak{sl}_{2} KZ equations with κ = ± 2 \kappa=\pm 2
P. Belkale ∗ , E. Mukhin ∘ , A. Varchenko ⋆

Abstract
. The 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 2 \mathfrak{sl}_{2} KZ differential equations with values in the tensor power of the fundamental representation with parameter κ = ± 2 \kappa=\pm 2 are considered. A Satake-type correspondence is established over complex numbers and subsequently reduced to finite characteristic. This correspondence enables the study of the KZ equations on the lower weight subspaces of the tensor power in terms of the wedge powers of the weight subspace of the weight just below the highest weight. We apply this approach to analyze the p p -curvature operators associated with our KZ equations, evaluate the dimension of the solution space in characteristic p p , and determine whether all solutions are generated by the so-called p p -hypergeometric solutions. In particular, we show that not all solutions of the KZ equations with κ = 2 \kappa=2 in characteristic p p are generated by p p -hypergeometric solutions. Previously, no such examples were known.

Introduction

The
KZ
equations form a system of linear partial differential equations
that exhibit profound connections with theoretical physics, representation theory, algebraic geometry,
and integrable systems. In 1984, Vladimir Knizhnik and Alexander Zamolodchikov first introduced
these equations within the context of conformal field theory to depict the behavior of correlation
functions of vertex operators in two-dimensional quantum field theories,
[
KZ
]
. Since then, the
equations have emerged
as a central object in both mathematical physics and pure mathematics, bridging seemingly disparate
fields such as Lie algebras, braid groups, hypergeometric integrals, congruences in number theory,
and geometric topology.

In this paper we consider the example of the
KZ
differential equations associated with the tensor power

L^{\otimes(2g+1)}
of the two-dimensional irreducible representation
L
of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}

with standard generators
e,f,h
.
The
KZ
equations are the system of differential equations on an
L^{\otimes(2g+1)}
-valued function

N(z)
,
z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{2g+1})
,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\qquad\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}-\frac{1}{\kappa}\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{P^{(i,j)}-1}{z_{i}-z_{j}}\right)\!N(z)=0,\qquad i=1,\dots,2g+1,

where
P^{(i,j)}
is the permutation of the
i
-th and
j
-th factors of
L^{\otimes(2g+1)}

and
\kappa
is a scalar parameter of the equations.

The differential operators
\nabla^{\operatorname{KZ},\kappa}_{i}=\partial_{i}-\frac{1}{\kappa}\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{P^{(i,j)}-1}{z_{i}-z_{j}}
define the
KZ
connection on the trivial
bundle
L^{\otimes(2g+1)}\times\mathbb{A}^{2g+1}\to\mathbb{A}^{2g+1}
, where

\mathbb{A}^{2g+1}
is the affine space with coordinates
z_{1},\dots,z_{2g+1}
. The connection is flat
for any value of
\kappa
and has singularities over the union of all diagonal hyperplanes in
\mathbb{A}^{2g+1}
.

As a rule one considers the
KZ
equations over the field of complex numbers.
Then the
KZ
differential equations have multidimensional hypergeometric
solutions,
[
SV1
]
.

More precisely, the
differential operators
\nabla^{\operatorname{KZ},\kappa}_{i}
commute with the
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-action on

L^{\otimes(2g+1)}
. Hence the
KZ
connection
has invariant subbundles

{\operatorname{Sing}}L^{\otimes(2g+1)}[2g+1-2r]\times\mathbb{A}^{2g+1}\to\mathbb{A}^{2g+1}
where

{\operatorname{Sing}}L^{\otimes(2g+1)}[2g+1-2r]\subset L^{\otimes(2g+1)}
is the subspace of vectors
v
such that

ev=0
and
hv=(2g+1-2r)v
. Here
r=0,\dots,g
. All
L^{\otimes(2g+1)}
-valued solutions of
the
KZ
equations are generated by solutions with values in these subbundles.

We have

\dim{\operatorname{Sing}}L^{\otimes(2g+1)}[2g+1-2r]=\binom{2g}{r}-\binom{2g}{r-2}
.
For
r=0
,
\dim{\operatorname{Sing}}L^{\otimes(2g+1)}[2g+1]=1
, and the
KZ
equations on a

{\operatorname{Sing}}L^{\otimes(2g+1)}[2g+1]
-valued (scalar) function
N(z)
take the form

\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}N(z)=0
,
i=1,\dots,2g+1
, with obvious solutions.
(In this case
P^{(i,j)}-1=0
.)

We have
\dim{\operatorname{Sing}}L^{\otimes(2g+1)}[2g-1]=2g
if
r=1
.
In a natural basis of
L^{\otimes(2g+1)}
, the
vectors of
{\operatorname{Sing}}L^{\otimes(2g+1)}[2g-1]
are identified with

2g+1
-vectors
(N_{1},\dots,N_{2g+1})
with
\sum_{i}N_{i}
=0, and the
{\operatorname{Sing}}L^{\otimes(2g+1)}[2g-1]
-valued
hypergeometric solutions are given by the vectors of one-dimensional hyperelliptic
integrals,

(1.2)

\displaystyle N^{\gamma}(z)=\int_{\gamma(z)}\prod_{i=a}^{2g+1}(t_{1}-z_{a})^{-1/\kappa}\left(\frac{1}{t_{1}-z_{1}},\dots,\frac{1}{t_{1}-z_{2g+1}}\right)dt_{1}

where
\gamma(z)
is a flat family of Pochhammer cycles. The solutions depend on the choice of Pochhammer cycles. All

{\operatorname{Sing}}L^{\otimes(2g+1)}[2g-1]
-valued solutions are linear combinations of such vectors of integrals.

For
r>1
, the
{\operatorname{Sing}}L^{\otimes(2g+1)}[2g+1-2r]
-valued hypergeometric solutions solutions are given by analogous

r
-dimensional hypergeometric integrals,
[
CF
,
DJMM
,
SV1
,
V2
,
V3
]
, but considerably
more complicated.

Notice that solutions of
KZ
equations over complex numbers have remarkable properties.
For example, it was shown in
[
L
,
BBM
,
BF
,
BFM
]
recently,
that when
\kappa
is an arbitrary nonzero rational number, the spaces of solutions of
KZ
equations
carry the structure of variations of mixed Hodge structures with coefficients in a cyclotomic field.

In this paper we study the
KZ
differential equations with parameters
\kappa=2
and
-2
.

Our first result, Theorem
5.2
and formula (
), describes
the
{\operatorname{Sing}}L^{\otimes(2g+1)}[2g+1-2r]
-valued solutions of the
KZ
equations with
\kappa=2

in terms of
the
r
-fold wedge products of the

{\operatorname{Sing}}L^{\otimes(2g+1)}[2g-1]
-valued solutions with
\kappa=2
.
The transformation of solutions is given by a linear map

T(z):\wedge^{r}{\operatorname{Sing}}L^{\otimes(2g+1)}[2g-1]\to{\operatorname{Sing}}L^{\otimes(2g+1)}[2g+1-2r]

which is a homogeneous polynomial in
z_{1},\dots,z_{2g+1}
of degree
\binom{r}{2}
.

We
construct a natural direct decomposition of the space

\wedge^{r}{\operatorname{Sing}}L^{\otimes(2g+1)}[2g-1]
with the first direct summand denoted by

\mathcal{P}_{r}^{\operatorname{KZ}}(z)
and called the primitive part. We show that

T(z)|_{\mathcal{P}_{r}^{\operatorname{KZ}}(z)}:\mathcal{P}_{r}^{\operatorname{KZ}}(z)\to{\operatorname{Sing}}L^{\otimes(2g+1)}[2g+1-2r]
is an isomorphism of the corresponding
KZ
connections with
\kappa=2
and show that

T(z)
restricted to other direct summands equals zero.

Theorem
5.6
describes an analogous statement for

\kappa=-2
. In that case, we construct
a map
\bar{T}(z):{\operatorname{Sing}}L^{\otimes(2g+1)}[2g+1-2r]\to\wedge^{r}{\operatorname{Sing}}L^{\otimes(2g+1)}[2g-1]
which defines an embedding
of the corresponding
KZ
connections with
\kappa=-2
.
The map
\bar{T}(z)
is a homogeneous polynomial in
z_{1},\dots,z_{2g+1}
of degree
\binom{r}{2}
.

Formula (
) presents a useful map

\tilde{T}(z):{\operatorname{Sing}}L^{\otimes(2g+1)}[2g+1-2r]\to\wedge^{r}{\operatorname{Sing}}L^{\otimes(2g+1)}[2g-1]
.
We show that
\tilde{T}(z)
equals
\bar{T}(z)

up to a nonzero multiplicative constant, Theorem
5.14
.

Remark
.

The
quantum Satake principle in
[
GM
,
GGI
]
says that
the quantum connection on the Grassmannian
\operatorname{Gr}(r,n)
is
the
r
-th wedge of the quantum connection on the projective space

\operatorname{P}^{n-1}
.
The quantum connection on
\operatorname{Gr}(r,n)
is a version of the KZ connection on
the weight space
L^{\otimes n}[n-2r]
, while the
quantum connection on
\operatorname{P}^{n-1}
is a version of the KZ connection on
the weight space
L^{\otimes n}[n-2]
, see, for example,

[
MO
,
GRTV
,
CDG
,
CV
]
. Therefore our Theorems
5.2
and
5.6

exhibit a flavor of the quantum Satake phenomenon.

Remark
.

The development of the Satake-type operators
T(z)
and
\bar{T}(z)
was inspired by

For any positive integer

\displaystyle\prod_{i=a}^{2g+1}(t_{1}-z_{a})^{(p-1)/2}\left(\frac{1}{t_{1}-z_{1}},\dots,\frac{1}{t_{1}-z_{2g+1}}\right)

.
For any positive integer
\ell_{1}
, the vector of polynomials

\kappa=2
,
[

]
.
The solutions

the solutions

equal zero,
[

The

For any positive integer

The

2.1.
Definition of KZ equations

Consider the complex Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
with generators
e,f,h
and relations
[e,f]=h,\,[h,e]=2e,\,[h,f]=-2f
.
Consider the complex vector space
L
with basis
w_{1},w_{2}
and the
{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
-action,

\displaystyle e=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
0&0&\end{pmatrix},\qquad f=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
1&0&\end{pmatrix},\qquad h=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1&\end{pmatrix}.

The
{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
-module
L^{\otimes n}
has a basis labeled by subsets
J\subset\{1,\dots,n\}
,

\displaystyle w_{J}=w_{j_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes w_{j_{n}},

where
j_{i}=1
if
j_{i}\notin J
and

j_{i}=2
if
j_{i}\in J
.

Consider the weight decomposition of
L^{\otimes n}

into eigenspaces of
h
,

L^{\otimes n}=\sum_{r=0}^{n}L^{\otimes n}[n-2r]
.
The vectors
w_{J}
with
|J|=r
form a basis of
L^{\otimes n}[n-2r]
.
Denote by
{\mathcal{I}_{r}}
the set of all
r
-element subsets of
\{1,\dots,n\}
.

Define the space of singular vectors of weight
n-2r
,

\displaystyle{\operatorname{Sing}}L^{\otimes n}[n-2r]=\{w\in L^{\otimes n}[n-2r]\mid ew=0\}.

This space is nonempty if and only if
n\geqslant 2r
. We have

\displaystyle\dim{\operatorname{Sing}}L^{\otimes n}[n-2r]=\dim L^{\otimes n}[n-2r]-\dim L^{\otimes n}[n-2r+2]=\binom{n}{r}-\binom{n}{r-1}.

We shall also use the formula

(2.1)

\displaystyle\dim{\operatorname{Sing}}L^{\otimes n}[n-2r]=\binom{n-1}{r}-\binom{n-1}{r-2}.

Define the Casimir element

\Omega=\frac{1}{2}h\otimes h+e\otimes f+f\otimes e\,\in\,{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}\otimes{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
. It acts on

L\otimes L
as
P-\frac{1}{2}
where
P
is the permutation of factors.

Define the linear operators on
L^{\otimes n}
depending on parameters
z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
,

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{i}(z)=\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{\Omega^{(i,j)}}{z_{i}-z_{j}}\,,\qquad i=1,\dots,n,

where

\Omega^{(i,j)}:L^{\otimes n}\to L^{\otimes n}
is the Casimir operator acting in the

Proposition 5.10

Let

\displaystyle d\alpha_{q,a,J}=\frac{2g+5+2a_{1}-2r}{2}\beta_{q,a,J}+\sum_{(q^{\prime},a^{\prime},J^{\prime})}f_{q^{\prime},a^{\prime},J^{\prime}}(z)\beta_{q^{\prime},a^{\prime},J^{\prime}}

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle d\alpha_{q,a,J}=(a_{1}+1)\beta_{q,a,J}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{d\Phi}{\Phi}\wedge\alpha_{q,a,J}

\displaystyle\frac{d\Phi}{\Phi}=2\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant r}\frac{dt_{i}-dt_{j}}{t_{i}-t_{j}}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{a=1}^{2g+1}\frac{dt_{i}}{t_{i}-z_{a}}.

Calculating the product

we obtain the proposition. Here are the main elements of the calculation:

\displaystyle\frac{t_{1}^{a_{1}+1}t_{j}^{a_{j}}}{t_{1}-t_{j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t_{1}^{a_{1}}t_{j}^{a_{j}}+t_{1}^{a_{1}-1}t_{j}^{a_{j}+1}+\dots+t_{1}^{a_{j}}t_{j}^{a_{1}}+\frac{t_{j}^{a_{1}+1}t_{i}^{a_{j}}}{t_{1}-t_{j}}\,,

\displaystyle\frac{t_{1}^{a_{1}+1}}{(t_{1}-t_{j})(t_{j}-z_{j_{i}})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{t_{1}^{a_{1}}+t_{1}^{a_{1}-1}t_{j}+\dots+t_{j}^{a_{1}}}{t_{j}-z_{j_{i}}}+

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{t_{j}^{a_{1}}+t_{j}^{a_{1}-1}z_{j_{i}}+\dots+z_{j_{i}}^{a_{1}}}{t_{1}-t_{j}}+\frac{z_{j_{i}}^{a_{1}+1}}{(t_{1}-t_{j})(t_{j}-z_{j_{i}})},

\displaystyle\frac{t_{1}^{a_{1}+1}}{t_{1}-z_{b}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t_{1}^{a_{1}}+z_{b}t_{1}^{a_{1}-1}+\dots+z_{b}^{a_{1}}+\frac{z_{b}^{a_{1}+1}}{t_{1}-z_{b}}\,.

Lemma 8.5

Proof of lemma.

Let

Then

\displaystyle z_{a}^{p}(c_{1,i}+z_{a}^{p}c_{2,i}+\dots+z_{a}^{p(g-1)}c_{g,i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{1,i+1}+z_{a}^{p}c_{2,i+1}+\dots+z_{a}^{p(g-1)}c_{g,i+1}.

\displaystyle-c_{1,i+1}+z_{a}^{p}(c_{1,i}-c_{2,i+1})+\dots+z_{a}^{p(g-1)}(c_{g-1,i}-c_{g,i+1})+z_{a}^{pg}c_{g,i}=0.

\displaystyle-c_{1,i+1}+z_{a}^{p}(c_{1,i}-c_{2,i+1})+\dots+z_{a}^{p(g-1)}(c_{g-1,i}-c_{g,i+1})+z_{a}^{pg}c_{g,i}=0,\quad a=1,\dots,g+1,

Let

\displaystyle-z_{a}^{p}c_{1,2}-z_{a}^{2p}c_{1,3}-\dots-z_{a}^{p(g-1)}c_{1,g}

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle c_{1,2}-z_{a}^{2p}c_{2,3}-\dots-z_{a}^{p(g-1)}c_{2,g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle\dots

\displaystyle c_{1,g}+z_{a}^{p}c_{2,g}+\dots+z_{a}^{p(g-2)}c_{g-1,g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

\displaystyle K_{a}\wedge\sum_{1\leqslant i_{1}<\dots<i_{r}\leqslant g}c_{i_{1},\dots,i_{g}}\sum_{j=1}^{r}z_{a}^{p(i_{j}-1)}(-1)^{j-1}v_{i_{1}}\wedge\dots\wedge\widehat{v_{i_{j}}}\wedge\dots\wedge v_{i_{r}}\,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K_{a}\wedge i_{L_{a}}\omega.

We have
\tilde{C}_{a}(\omega)=0
for all
a
if and only if
i_{L_{a}}\omega=0
for all
a
.
By Lemma
9.3
. this implies that
\omega=0
since the vectors

L_{a}
,
a=1,\dots,g
, are linearly independent.
∎

9.2.
Intersection of kernels in
V_{2,1}

Lemma 9.4
.

For
q\geqslant 3
, the intersection of kernels
of the reduced
p
-curvature operators
\tilde{C}_{m}:V_{2,1}\to V_{1,2}
,

Proof.

A vector
\alpha\in V_{2,1}
has the form

\displaystyle\alpha=\sum_{1\leqslant a<b\leqslant g}\sum_{c=1}^{g}d_{a,b;c}\,v_{a}\wedge v_{b}\wedge w_{c}\,

We set
d_{a,b;c}=0
if
b\leqslant a
or if any of the indices

is not in the set

\{1,\dots,g\}
.
Similarly to previous reasonings, we observe that

\alpha
lies in the intersection of kernels if its coefficients

d_{a,b:c}
satisfy the system of equations

(9.1)

\displaystyle d_{s-k,i;j}-d_{i,s-k;j}+d_{i,s-j;k}-d_{s-j,i;k}=0

where the equations are labeled by the parameters
s,k,i,j

with the only condition
j<k
. Equation (
9.1
)
comes as the coefficient of
z_{m}^{p(s-2)}v_{i}\wedge w_{j}\wedge w_{k}

in the vector
\tilde{C}_{m}\alpha
.

This system of equations implies that all coefficients

Indeed, if
c>a
and

and

we choose

.
∎

9.3.
Intersection of kernels in
V_{1,2}

Introduce the following

2g-2
elements of
V_{1,2}
:

(9.2)

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}=\sum_{a=1}^{k}\sum_{b=k+1}^{g}v_{a+b-k-1}\wedge w_{a}\wedge w_{b}\,,\qquad\beta_{k}=\sum_{a=1}^{k}\sum_{b=k+1}^{g}v_{a+b-k}\wedge w_{a}\wedge w_{b}\,,

where
k=1,\dots,g-1
. Denote by
X_{1,2}
the span of these elements.

Let
Y_{1,2}
be the intersection of kernels
of the reduced
p
-curvature operators
\tilde{C}_{m}:V_{1,2}\to V_{0,3}
,

m=1,\dots,2g+1
.

Lemma 9.5
.

The elements
\alpha_{k},\beta_{k}
,
k=1,\dots,g-1
,
are linearly independent and
X_{1,2}\subset Y_{1,2}
.

Proof.

Consider the lexicographical ordering on the elements
v_{a}\wedge w_{b}\wedge w_{c}
. Then
the lexicographically minimal summand of
\alpha_{k}
is

v_{1}\wedge w_{1}\wedge w_{k}
and
the lexicographically minimal summand of
\beta_{k}
is

v_{2}\wedge w_{1}\wedge w_{k}
. These minimal summands are distinct. Hence

\alpha_{k},\beta_{k}
,
k=1,\dots,g-1
, are linearly independent.

We have

\displaystyle\tilde{C}_{m}\alpha_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{g}z_{i}^{p(i-1)}w_{i}\right)\wedge\sum_{a=1}^{k}\sum_{b=k+1}^{g}z_{m}^{p(a+b-2)}w_{a}\wedge w_{b}\,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{a=1}^{k}z_{m}^{p(a-1)}w_{a}+\sum_{b=k+1}^{g}z_{m}^{p(b-1)}w_{b}\right)\wedge\left(\sum_{a=1}^{k}z_{m}^{p(a-1)}w_{a}\right)\wedge\left(\sum_{b=k+1}^{g}z_{m}^{p(b-1)}w_{b}\right)=0.

Similarly we show that
\tilde{C}_{m}\beta_{k}=0
.

∎

Lemma 9.6
.

The intersection
X_{1,2}\cap\mathcal{P}_{1,2}
has dimension
g-2
.

Proof.

Recall that
\mathcal{P}_{1,2}
is the kernel of the map

f:V_{1,2}\to V_{g-1,g}
of multiplication by the
(g-2)
-th wedge-power of
the element
\sum_{i=1}^{g}v_{i}\wedge w_{i}
.
The elements

\displaystyle\gamma_{i}=w_{i}\wedge\left(\wedge_{j\neq i}(v_{j}\wedge w_{j})\right),\qquad i=1,\dots,g,

form a basis of
V_{g-1,g}
.

We have
f(X_{1,2})=V_{g-1,g}
because of

f(\beta_{i})=-(g-i)!\gamma_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,g-1
, and
f(\beta_{g-1})=(g-1)!\gamma_{g}
.
Since
f(X_{1,2})=V_{g-1,g}
, we obtain that
\dim X_{1,2}\cap\mathcal{P}_{1,2}=2g-2-g=g-2
.
∎

Theorem 9.7
.

We have
X_{1,2}=Y_{1,2}
.

Proof.

Define the set

\displaystyle S=\{v_{1}\wedge w_{1}\wedge w_{k+1},\,v_{2}\wedge w_{1}\wedge w_{k+1}\mid k=1,\dots,g-1\}.

The elements of
S
are
the lexicographically minimal summands of
\alpha_{k}
and
\beta_{k}
with
k=1,\dots,g-1
.
The theorem clearly follows from the following lemma.

Lemma 9.8
.

If
\alpha
is a nonzero element of
Y_{1,2}
, then the lexicographically minimal summand of

\alpha
belongs to the set
S
.

Proof of lemma.

Let

\displaystyle\alpha=\sum_{1\leqslant b<c\leqslant g}\sum_{a=1}^{g}d_{a;b,c}\,v_{a}\wedge w_{b}\wedge w_{c}.

We set
d_{a;b,c}=0
if
c\leqslant b
or if any of the indices
a,b,c
is not in the set

\{1,\dots,g\}
.
Similarly to previous reasonings, we observe that

\alpha
lies in the intersection of kernels if its coefficients

d_{a;b,c}
satisfy the system of equations

(9.3)

\displaystyle d_{s-i;j,k}-d_{s-j;i,k}+d_{s-k;i,j}=0

where the equations are labeled by the parameters
s,i,j,k

with the only condition
i<j<k
. Equation (
9.3
) comes as
the coefficient of
z_{m}^{p(s-2)}w_{i}\wedge w_{j}\wedge w_{k}
in

\tilde{C}_{m}\alpha
.

Claim 1.

If
a>c>b
, then
d_{a;b,c}=0
.

Indeed, choose

i=b,\,j=c,\,s=c+g+1,\,k=c+g-a+1
. Then (
9.3
)
implies that
d_{a;b,c}=0
.

Claim 2.

If
1<b<c
, then
v_{a}\wedge w_{b}\wedge w_{c}
cannot be the lexicographically minimal summand
of
\alpha

Indeed, choose

. Then equation
(

\displaystyle d_{a;b,c}-d_{a+1-b;1,c}+d_{a+1-c;1,b}=0.

Claim 3.

If
b=1

then

of

\displaystyle d_{c+1;c+2-a,c}-d_{a;1,c}+d_{2;1,c+2-a}=0.

In this equation, the first coefficient is zero by Claim 1, and the element

Appendix A

A.1.
Cohomology classes on
C(z)

Fix
z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{2g+1})
with distinct coordinates.
Recall
\Psi(t,z)=\prod_{a=1}^{2g+1}(t-z_{a})
.
Consider the following differential 1-forms on the hyperelliptic
curve
C(z)
:

\displaystyle\omega_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Psi(t,z)^{-1/2}}{t-z_{i}}dt,\qquad i=1,\dots,2g+1,

\displaystyle\mu_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi(t,z)^{-1/2}t^{k-1}dt,\qquad k=1,\dots,g.

The forms
\omega_{i}
have zero residues at all points of
C(z)
and define elements
\left[\omega_{i}\right]\in H^{1}(C(z))
. The forms

\left[\omega_{i}\right]
,
i=1,\dots,2g+1
, span
H^{1}(C(z))
and satisfy the relation

\sum_{i=1}^{2g+1}\left[\omega_{i}\right]=0
, see
[
VV1
, Theorem 4.2]
.
The forms
\mu_{k}
,
k=1,\dots,g,
are regular on
C(z)
and give a basis of the Hodge subspace
F^{1}\subset H^{1}(C(z))
,
[
VV1
, Lemma 4.4]
.

The derivatives of the elements
\left[\mu_{k}\right]
with respect to the Gauss-Manin connection are given by the formula

(A.1)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\!\!\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}}[\mu_{k}]=\frac{1}{2}\big([\mu_{k-1}]+z_{i}[\mu_{k-2}]+\dots+z_{i}^{k-2}[\mu_{1}]+z_{i}^{k-1}[\omega_{i}]\big),

[
VV1
, Lemma 4.5]
.

Consider the linear functions
(\cdot,\mu_{k})
,
k=1,\dots,g
, on
H^{1}(C(z))
where
(\cdot,\cdot)
is the Poincaré
pairing. These linear functions are zero on
F^{1}
and define a coordinate system on
H^{1}(C(z))/F^{1}
.
Let
\nu_{k}
,
k=1,\dots,g
, be the dual basis of
H^{1}(C(z))/F^{1}
,
(\nu_{i},\omega_{j})=\delta_{ij}
.
For any element
\gamma\in H^{1}(C(z))
, its projection
\bar{\gamma}
to
H^{1}(C(z))/F^{1}
equals the linear combination

(A.2)

\displaystyle(\gamma,\mu_{1})\nu_{1}+(\gamma,\mu_{2})\nu_{2}+\dots+(\gamma,\mu_{g})\nu_{g}\,.

Consider the direct sum
W=F^{1}\oplus H^{1}(C(z))/F^{1}
with basis

\mu_{k},\nu_{k}
,
k=1,\dots,g
. The element

\sum_{k=1}^{g}\nu_{k}\wedge\mu_{k}
in
\wedge^{2}W
corresponds
to the Poincare pairing element.

The Poincaré pairing of
[\omega_{i}]
and
[\mu_{k}]
is given by the formula

(A.3)

\displaystyle([\omega_{i}],[\mu_{k}])\,=\,-\,\frac{2z_{i}^{k-1}}{D_{i}(z)}\,,

where
D_{i}(z)=(z_{i}-z_{1})\dots(z_{i}-z_{i-1})(z_{i}-z_{i+1})\dots(z_{i}-z_{n})
,
[
VV1
, Theorem 4.7]
.

A.2.
Kodaira-Spencer maps

Recall the Kodaira-Spencer maps

(A.4)

\displaystyle\text{KS}_{a}(z):F^{1}\to H^{1}(C(z))/F^{1},\qquad a=1,\dots,z_{2g+1}.

By definition, the map
\operatorname{KS}_{a}(z)
sends the class
[\mu_{k}]
of a regular 1-form to the
projection
\overline{\nabla_{\!\!\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{a}}}[\mu_{k}]}
in

\nabla_{\!\!\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{a}}}[\mu_{k}]

We extend the Kodaira-Spencer map to a map

Lemma A.1
.

For

, the Kodaira-Spencer map

is defined by the formula:

(A.5)

\displaystyle\operatorname{KS}_{a}(z)\ :\ \sum_{k=1}^{g}(b_{k}\mu_{k}+c_{k}\nu_{k})\ \mapsto\ \frac{-1}{D_{a}(z)}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{g}b_{k}z_{a}^{k-1}\right)\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{g}z_{a}^{\ell-1}\nu_{k}\right).

Proof.

This formula follows from (
A.1
), (
A.2
), and (
A.3
).
∎

Define the reduced Kodaira-Spencer map by the formula

(A.6)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\operatorname{KS}}_{a}(z)\ :\ \sum_{k=1}^{g}(b_{k}\mu_{k}+c_{k}\nu_{k})\ \to\ \left(\sum_{k=1}^{g}b_{k}z_{a}^{k-1}\right)\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{g}z_{a}^{\ell-1}\nu_{k}\right).

The reduced map has the same kernel and image as the map
\operatorname{KS}_{a}(z)

.

Formula (
A.6

Notice that the
p
-curvature operators are defined in characteristic

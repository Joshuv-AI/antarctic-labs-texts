Abstract
We investigate the blow-up behavior and Liouville-type theorems of solutions to a class of generalized Camassa-Holm-Kadomtsev-Petviashvili (CH-KP) equations with a generally smooth nonlinear term g ⁡ ( u ) g(u) . First, using the continuation method, we establish a blow-up criterion that is independent of the regularity index of initial data. Under the assumption that g ′ ​ ( u ) g^{\prime}(u) is uniformly bounded, we prove the blow-up theorem and a weighted blow-up result by means of characteristic lines, a priori estimates and the Riccati inequality. Moreover, we extend these blow-up results to the setting where g ′ ​ ( u ) g^{\prime}(u) is polynomially controlled, which includes typical nonlinearities such as g ⁡ ( u ) = κ ​ u + 3 ​ u 2 g(u)=\kappa u+3u^{2} for the classical CH-KP equations. Furthermore, a Liouville-type uniqueness theorem is established under the condition g ⁡ ( u ) ≥ γ ​ u 2 g(u)\geq\gamma u^{2} with u ≠ 0 u\neq 0 , g ⁡ ( u ) > γ ​ u 2 g(u)>\gamma u^{2} . Introduction

The study of water waves has been a popular and fascinating subject in recent years. One of the most celebrated models is the Korteweg-de Vries (KdV) equation
[
14
]
, which describes unidirectional, shallow-water waves and is given by

\displaystyle u_{t}+u_{x}+\frac{3}{2}uu_{x}+\frac{1}{6}u_{xxx}=0.

(1.1)

In two dimensions, the KdV equation naturally extends to the Kadomtsev-Petviashvili (KP) equation
[
13
]
, which incorporates transverse effects:

\displaystyle(u_{t}+u_{xxx}+u_{x}+uu_{x})_{x}+u_{yy}=0,t>0,(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

(1.2)

with initial condition
u|_{t=0}=u_{0}(x,y),\>(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The well-posedness of equation (
1.2
), which generalizes the KdV equation by considering weak diffraction, has been extensively studied over the past three decades. While the KP equation captures the propagation of weakly dispersive waves in two dimensions
[
10
,
19
]
, other models are required to describe wave breaking. A prominent example is the Camassa-Holm (CH) equation
[
3
,
5
,
6
,
11
]
, that is

\displaystyle u_{t}-u_{xxt}+2\kappa u_{x}+3uu_{x}=2u_{x}u_{xx}+uu_{xxx},

(1.3)

where
\kappa
is a parameter. The CH equation has garnered significant attention due to its integrable structure and its admission of peakon solutions
[
6
,
11
]
.

A natural two-dimensional counterpart of the CH equation, analogous to the relationship between the KdV and KP equations, is the Camassa-Holm-Kadomtsev-Petviashvili (CH-KP) equation. As pointed in
[
9
]
, it takes the form

\displaystyle{{\left({{u}_{t}}-\frac{5}{12}\mu{{u}_{xxt}}+u_{x}+\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon u{{u}_{x}}-\frac{1}{4}\mu u_{xxx}-\frac{5}{24}\mu\varepsilon\left(2{{u}_{x}}{{u}_{xx}}+u{{u}_{xxx}}\right)\right)}_{x}}+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{3}{{u}_{yy}}=0.

(1.4)

where
u
represents the horizontal velocity field at height
h_{0}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
in flow domain,
\varepsilon=a/h_{0}\ll 1
and
\mu=h_{0}^{2}/\lambda^{2}\ll 1
are amplitude and shallowness parameters, respectively, where
a
denotes the small amplitude and
\lambda
the long wavelength.

Through suitable scaling and Galilean transformations with a parameter
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
, eqation(
1.4
) can be simplified to the standard form
[
9
]

\displaystyle{{\left({{u}_{t}}-u_{xxt}+\kappa u_{x}+3u{{u}_{x}}-\left(2{{u}_{x}}{{u}_{xx}}+u{{u}_{xxx}}\right)\right)}_{x}}+{{u}_{yy}}=0.

(1.5)

This equation was formally derived by Gui
et al.
[
9
]
from the governing equations for three-dimensional incompressible and irrotational water waves in the long-wave limit.

While significant progress has been made on the local well-posedness, blow-up phenomena, and solitary wave solutions of equation (
1.5
)
[
4
,
9
,
20
,
22
,
23
]
, the question of global existence for arbitrary initial data remains open due to its nonlocal structure and weak linear dispersion.

In this paper, we consider a further generalization of (
1.5
) with a general smooth nonlinear term. By replacing the quadratic nonlinearity
3uu_{x}
with a general function
g(u)
, we study the following initial value problem:

\displaystyle\left(u_{t}-u_{xxt}+\left(\frac{g(u)}{2}\right)_{x}-\gamma\left(2u_{x}u_{xx}+uu_{xxx}\right)\right)_{x}+u_{yy}=0.

(1.6)

Here,
u(t,x,y)
represents the fluid velocity,
g\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, with
g(0)=0
, and
\gamma>0
is a constant. This formulation allows us to explore the impact of a broader class of nonlinearities on the dynamic behavior of the solution. Note that (
1.5
) is recovered by setting

g(u)=2\kappa u+3u^{2}
and
\gamma=1
. Preliminary investigations into various forms of the CH-KP and generalized CH-KP equations have been reported in
[
2
,
7
,
12
,
15
,
16
,
21
,
24
]
and references therein.

Defining
G(x)=\frac{1}{2}e^{-|x|},x\in\mathbb{R}
, we have the relation
\left(1-\partial_{x}^{2}\right)^{-1}f=G*f
for all
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
(see
[
9
]
). The Cauchy problem for equation (
1.6
) can then be reformulated as the following system

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{t}+\gamma uu_{x}+G*\partial_{x}\left(\frac{1}{2}\>g(u)+\frac{\gamma}{2}\>u_{x}^{2}-\frac{\gamma}{2}u^{2}\right)+G*v_{y}=0,\>(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\\
u_{y}=v_{x},\\
u|_{t=0}=u_{0}(x,y),\>(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.\end{cases}

(1.7)

This paper focuses on the well-posedness of system (
1.7
) within the following Hilbert space.

Definition 1.1
.

For
s>0
, the Hilbert space
X^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is defined by

X^{s}=X^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2}):=\left\{u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})|\partial_{x}^{-1}u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\,\partial_{x}u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\right\},

equipped with the inner product

(u,v)_{X^{s}}:=(u,v)_{H^{s}}+(\partial_{x}^{-1}u,\partial_{x}^{-1}v)_{H^{s}}+(\partial_{x}u,\partial_{x}v)_{H^{s}},\ \forall\ u,\,v\ \in X^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),

and the norm

\|u\|_{X^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}:=(\|u\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}+\|\partial_{x}^{-1}u\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}+\|\partial_{x}u\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\penalty\ \mbox{ for}\penalty\ u\in X^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),

where
\partial_{x}^{-1}u(x,y):=\mathcal{F}^{-1}((i\xi)^{-1}\mathcal{F}(u)(\xi,\eta))
, and

H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})=\left\{u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(1+|\xi|^{2}+|\eta|^{2})^{s}|\mathcal{F}(u)(\xi,\eta)|^{2}{\rm d}\xi{\rm d}\eta<+\infty\right.\right\}

with the norm
\|u\|^{2}_{H^{s}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(1+|\xi|^{2}+|\eta|^{2})^{s}|\mathcal{F}(u)(\xi,\eta)|^{2}{\rm d}\xi{\rm d}\eta,

\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{-1}
denote the Fourier transform and inverse Fourier transform with respect to the variables
x,\ y
, respectively.

In the previous work
[
24
]
, we established the blow-up theory for system (
1.7
) in
X^{\mathrm{s}}
for specific nonlinear terms such as
g(u)=u^{3}
or
u^{4}
. That analysis,however, relied heavily on the specific structure of these nonlinearities. It is a subtle challenge for a direct generalization to arbitrary smooth functions
g(u)
. The primary goal of this paper is to extend these results to a much wider class of nonlinearities. We begin to establish a general blow-up criterion under suitable initial conditions. Subsequently, using energy methods, we obtain the corresponding blow-up theorems for the nonlinearities satisfying a polynomial growth condition

\displaystyle|g^{\prime}(\xi)|\leq C_{1}|\xi|^{\alpha}+C_{2},\ \xi\in\mathbb{R},\ \alpha\geq 0.

(1.8)

This generalization is significant as it encompasses many physically relevant models. In particular, it includes the CH-KP equation (
1.5
) (with
g(u)=2\kappa u+3u^{2}
and
\gamma=1
) as well as the power-law cases
g(u)=u^{3}\mbox{or}\penalty\ u^{4}
studied in
[
24
]
.

In whole paper, we assume
g\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\ \text{and}\ g(0)=0
and establish the following blow-up results.

.

Let

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T_{u_{0}}^{*}}\left\|\nabla u(\tau)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau=\infty.

(1.9)

The blow-up criterion (

of the initial value

.

Assume that the smooth function
g
satisfies (

) and

, where

. Define

Preliminary Lemmas

Lemma 2.1
.

(a)
The convolution operator
G*:H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\to H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is bounded for any real number
s
. That is, for any
f\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, we have

\displaystyle\|G*f\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq\|f\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

(2.1)

(b)
The operator
(\partial_{x}G)*:L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\to L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is bounded and and the identity
\|\partial_{x}(G*f)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}=\|(\partial_{x}G)*f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}
holds. That is, for any
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, it follows

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}(G*f)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}=\|(\partial_{x}G)*f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

(2.2)

(c)
The operator
\partial_{x}\circ(G*):H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\to H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is bounded for any real number
s
. Namely, for any
f\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
,

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}(G*f)\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq\|f\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

(2.3)

Proof.

(a)
For brevity, denote
\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi,\eta)
by
\widehat{f}(\xi,\eta)
. Combining Fourier transform formula for the convolution
\widehat{G*f}(\xi,\eta)=\widehat{G}(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi,\eta)
and
\widehat{G}(\xi)=\frac{1}{1+\xi^{2}}
yields

\displaystyle\|G*f\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\widehat{(G*f)}(\xi,\eta)\right|^{2}(1+\xi^{2}+\eta^{2})^{s}{\rm d}\xi{\rm d}\eta

(2.4)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\frac{1}{1+\xi^{2}}\hat{f}(\xi,\eta)\right|^{2}(1+\xi^{2}+\eta^{2})^{s}{\rm d}\xi{\rm d}\eta

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\hat{f}(\xi,\eta)\right|^{2}(1+\xi^{2}+\eta^{2})^{s}{\rm d}\xi{\rm d}\eta=\|f\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}.

Therefore, it holds that

\displaystyle\|G*f\|_{H^{s}}\leq\|f\|_{H^{s}}.

(2.5)

(b)
From
G(x)=\frac{1}{2}e^{-|x|}
, we can compute that
\partial_{x}G(x)=-\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{sgn}(x)e^{-|x|}
for
x\in\mathbb{R}
. It then follows that
\|\partial_{x}G\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|-\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{sgn}(x)e^{-|x|}\right|{\rm d}x=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-|x|}{\rm d}x=1
. The two-dimensional convolution is defined as

\displaystyle(\partial_{x}G*f)(x,y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}G(x-\xi)f(\xi,y){\rm d}\xi.

(2.6)

According to the Young inequality for convolutions, we obtain, for any
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

\displaystyle|(\partial_{x}G*f)(x,y)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\partial_{x}G(x-\xi)|\cdot|f(\xi,y)|{\rm d}\xi

(2.7)

\displaystyle\leq\|f(\cdot,y)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{x}G\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}=\|f(\cdot,y)\|_{L^{\infty}}.

One yields

\displaystyle\sup_{x,y}|(\partial_{x}G*f)(x,y)|\leq\sup_{y}\|f(\cdot,y)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\leq\sup_{x,y}|f(x,y)|=\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

(2.8)

Moreover, since

\displaystyle(G*f)(x,y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}G(x-\xi)f(\xi,y){\rm d}\xi,

(2.9)

it follows that

\displaystyle\partial_{x}(G*f)(x,y)=\partial_{x}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}G(x-\xi)f(\xi,y){\rm d}\xi\right)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}G(x-\xi)f(\xi,y){\rm d}\xi=\partial_{x}G*f.

(2.10)

Therefore, we have

\|\partial_{x}(G*f)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}=\|\partial_{x}G*f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

(c)
From the properties of the Fourier transform
[
8
]
, it follows that

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}(G*f)\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(1+\xi^{2}+\eta^{2})^{s}\left|\widehat{\partial_{x}(G*f)}(\xi,\eta)\right|^{2}{\rm d}\xi{\rm d}\eta

(2.11)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(1+\xi^{2}+\eta^{2})^{s}\frac{\xi^{2}}{(1+\xi^{2})^{2}}\left|\widehat{f}(\xi,\eta)\right|^{2}{\rm d}\xi{\rm d}\eta

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(1+\xi^{2}+\eta^{2})^{s}\left|\widehat{f}(\xi,\eta)\right|^{2}{\rm d}\xi{\rm d}\eta=\|f\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}.

It follows that
\|\partial_{x}(G*f)\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq\|f\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}
. ∎

3
Blow-up necessary condition of solution

The literature
[
24
]
establishes blow-up criteria for the cases
g(u)=u^{3}
or
u^{4}
, demonstrating that a necessary condition for solutions of system (
1.7
) to blow up in finite time is the divergence of the gradient integral. However, Theorem
1.1
shows that, for general infinitely differentiable functions
g(u)
, a necessary condition for finite-time blow-up of solutions to system (
1.7
) is the divergence of the integral of the squared gradient.

The proof of Theorem
1.1

Let the global maximal existence time of the solution
u
be
T_{u_{0}}^{*}<\infty
. Prove the conclusion by contradiction. Suppose the integral
\int_{0}^{T_{u_{0}}^{*}}\|\nabla u(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau
is bounded, then
\int_{0}^{T_{u_{0}}^{*}}\|\nabla u(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\mathrm{d}\tau
is also bounded. Let
M>0
large enough and determined later such that
\sup\limits_{\tau\in[0,T^{*}_{u_{0}}]}\|u(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}<M.

First, we derive the energy estimate for
X^{s}
: for
s\geq 2
, there exists
T>0
such that for any
t\in[0,T]
, we have

\sup_{\tau\in[0,t]}\|u(\tau)\|_{X^{s}}\leq 2\|u_{0}\|_{X^{s}}.

(3.1)

Step 1
:
L^{2}-
estimate. From
u_{y}=v_{x}
, we get
\partial_{x}^{-1}u_{y}=v
. Applying
\partial_{x}^{-1}
to (
1.6
), taking the
L^{2}
inner product with

4
Blow-up sufficient conditions of solution

4.1
The case of
g^{\prime}\in L^{\infty}

Since
g^{\prime}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, there exists a constant
L>0
such that
\|g^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq L.

The proof of Theorem
1.2

The proof Theorem
1.2
proceeds in four steps: first, defining characteristic lines and associated functions; second, deriving the corresponding equations along these lines; third, estimating the
L^{\infty}
norm of the residual term; and finally, establishing the blow-up conclusion by the Riccati inequality.

Proof.

Step 1: Defining the trajectories and the functions along trajectories

Choose initial values
(x_{0},y_{0})
such that
\partial_{x}u_{0}(x_{0},y_{0})=m_{0}
. Fix
y_{0}
and define the characteristic curve
x=q(t,x_{0},y_{0})
by

\frac{\mathrm{d}q(t,x_{0},y_{0})}{\mathrm{d}t}=\gamma u(t,q(t,x_{0},y_{0}),y_{0}),\ q(0,x_{0},y_{0})=x_{0}.

Along this characteristic line, we define

w(t)=\partial_{x}u(t,x(t),y_{0}).

Clearly,
w(0)=m_{0}
.

Step 2: Deriving the evolution equation for the derivative along the characteristics

We commence by rewriting the first equation of system (
1.7
) in the form:

\begin{split}u_{t}+\gamma uu_{x}+G\ast\partial_{x}F+G\ast v_{y}=0,\ F=\frac{1}{2}g(u)+\frac{\gamma}{2}u_{x}^{2}-\frac{\gamma}{2}u^{2}.\end{split}

(4.1)

Differentiating (
4.1
) with respect to
x
yields

\partial_{x}u_{t}+\gamma\left(u_{x}\partial_{x}u+uu_{xx}\right)+\partial_{x}\left(G\ast\partial_{x}F\right)+\partial_{x}\left(G\ast v_{y}\right)=0.

(4.2)

Substituting
w
into equation (
4.2
), this gives

\partial_{t}w+\gamma\left(w^{2}+uw_{x}\right)+\partial_{x}\left(G\ast\partial_{x}F\right)+\partial_{x}\left(G\ast v_{y}\right)=0.

(4.3)

Employing the material derivative
\frac{\mathrm{D}}{\mathrm{D}t}=\partial_{t}+\gamma u\cdot\partial_{x}
along the characteristic lines, we derive the evolution equation

\frac{\mathrm{D}w}{\mathrm{D}t}=-\gamma w^{2}-R(t),\quad w(0)=m_{0},

(4.4)

where the residual term is defined as

R(t)=\partial_{x}(G*\partial_{x}F)+\partial_{x}(G*v_{y}).

Step 3: Estimating the residual term
R(t)

We decompose the residual term as
R(t)=R_{1}(t)+R_{2}(t)
,
where
R_{1}(t)=\partial_{x}\left(G\ast\partial_{x}F\right),\ R_{2}(t)=\partial_{x}\left(G\ast v_{y}\right)
.

Estimate of
R_{1}
.

From the condition
\|g^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq L
and Lemma
2.5
, it follows that

\|g(u)\|_{H^{s}}\leq C\left(L,\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)\|u\|_{H^{s}}\leq C\left(L,\|u\|_{X^{s}}\right)\|u\|_{X^{s}}.

(4.5)

Combining this with Lemma
2.2
, we obtain

\|F\|_{H^{s}}=\left\|\frac{1}{2}g(u)+\frac{\gamma}{2}u_{x}^{2}-\frac{\gamma}{2}u^{2}\right\|_{H^{s}}\leq C\left(L,\|u\|_{X^{s}}\right)\|u\|_{X^{s}}+C(\gamma)\|u\|_{X^{s}}^{2}.

(4.6)

By Lemma
2.1
(c), we have
\|\partial_{x}(G\ast f)\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq\|f\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}
. Combining this with (
4.6
), we get

\displaystyle\|R_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\|\partial_{x}(G\ast\partial_{x}F)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|\partial_{x}(G\ast\partial_{x}F)\|_{H^{s-1}}

(4.7)

\displaystyle\leq\|F\|_{H^{s}}\leq C\left(L,\|u\|_{X^{s}}\right)\|u\|_{X^{s}}+C(\gamma)\|u\|_{X^{s}}^{2}.

The estimate of
R_{2}
. From the relation
u_{y}=v_{x}
, we deduce
v_{y}=\partial^{-1}_{x}u_{yy}
. Applying first inequality of Lemma
2.4

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{x}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{y}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{xy}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}},

we obtain, for
s\geq 3
,

\displaystyle\|v_{y}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\partial_{x}^{-1}u_{yy}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\partial_{x}^{-1}u_{yy}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{x}^{-1}u_{xyy}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{x}^{-1}u_{yyy}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{x}^{-1}u_{yyyx}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\partial_{x}^{-1}u_{yy}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{x}^{-1}u_{yyy}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{yy}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{yyy}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\partial_{x}^{-1}u\|_{H^{3}}\|u\|_{H^{3}}\leq C\|u\|^{2}_{X^{3}}\leq C\|u\|_{X^{s}}^{2}.

Combining the inequality above with Lemma
2.1
(a), we conclude that

\displaystyle\|R_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}=\|\partial_{x}(G*v_{y})\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|v_{y}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|u\|_{X^{s}}.

(4.8)

From (
3.1
), there exists
T>0
such that for any
t\in[0,T]
, we have
\sup\limits_{\tau\in[0,t]}\|u(\tau)\|_{X^{s}}\leq 2\|u_{0}\|_{X^{s}}
. Combining this with (
4.7
) and (
4.8
), we see that there exists a constant
K=C\left(\gamma,L,\|u_{0}\|_{X^{s}}\right)>0
such that for
t\in[0,T]
, we have

|R(t)|\leq\|R_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|R_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq K.

Step 4: Deriving the blow-up conclusion from the Riccati inequality

Along the characteristic line, it holds that

\frac{\mathrm{D}w}{\mathrm{D}t}=-\gamma w^{2}-R(t),\ |R(t)|\leq K,\ w(0)=m_{0}.

(4.9)

Thus

\frac{\mathrm{D}w}{\mathrm{D}t}\leq-\gamma w^{2}+K.

(4.10)

Let
\psi(t)=-w(t)
, so that
\psi(0)=-m_{0}
, and

\frac{\mathrm{D}\psi}{\mathrm{D}t}=-\frac{\mathrm{D}w}{\mathrm{D}t}\geq\gamma w^{2}-K=\gamma\psi^{2}-K.

(4.11)

If
w_{0}=m_{0}<-\sqrt{\frac{K}{\gamma}}
, then
\psi(t)
is monotonically increasing and
\psi(t)>\sqrt{\frac{K}{\gamma}}
. Thus

\frac{{\rm d}\psi}{\gamma\psi^{2}-K}\geq{\rm d}t.

(4.12)

Simultaneously integrating both sides and simplifying yields

\psi(t)\geq\frac{2\sqrt{K/\gamma}}{1-\frac{\psi_{0}-\sqrt{K/\gamma}}{\psi_{0}+\sqrt{K/\gamma}}\cdot e^{2\sqrt{K\gamma}\,t}}-\sqrt{K/\gamma}.

(4.13)

Thus, as
t\to\left(\frac{1}{2\sqrt{K\gamma}}\ln\left(\frac{\psi_{0}+\sqrt{K/\gamma}}{\psi_{0}-\sqrt{K/\gamma}}\right)\right)^{-}
, we have

\left(\frac{2\sqrt{K/\gamma}}{1-\frac{\psi_{0}-\sqrt{K/\gamma}}{\psi_{0}+\sqrt{K/\gamma}}\cdot e^{2\sqrt{K\gamma}\,t}}-\sqrt{K/\gamma}\right)\to+\infty.

(4.14)

Thus
\psi(t)\to+\infty
, and consequently
\omega(t)=\partial_{x}u(t)\to-\infty
, meaning the solution blows up. Let

\displaystyle T^{*}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{K\gamma}}\ln\left(\frac{\psi_{0}+\sqrt{K/\gamma}}{\psi_{0}-\sqrt{K/\gamma}}\right)=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{K\gamma}}\ln\left(\frac{\sqrt{\gamma}m_{0}-\sqrt{K}}{\sqrt{\gamma}m_{0}+\sqrt{K}}\right).

(4.15)

Thus, as
t\to(T^{*})^{-}
,
\|\partial_{x}u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\to\infty
. The theorem is proved.

The proof of Theorem
1.3

In

]

of this paper shows that for a general smooth function

Proof.

As demonstrated in the proof of Theorem 4 in
[

]
, after differentiating both sides of equation (

) with respect to
x
, then multiplying by
\varphi(y)
, integrating the resulting expression with respect to
y
over
\mathbb{R}
along the trajectory, we have already obtained

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u_{x}\big(t,q(t,x,y),y\big)\varphi(y)\mathrm{d}y=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(u_{tx}+\gamma u_{xx}u\big)\varphi\mathrm{d}y=-\frac{\gamma}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u_{x}^{2}\varphi\mathrm{d}y\\
+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[\frac{1}{2}g(u)-\frac{\gamma}{2}u^{2}-G*\left(\frac{1}{2}g(u)+\frac{\gamma}{2}u_{x}^{2}-\frac{\gamma}{2}u^{2}\right)\right]\varphi\mathrm{d}y-\int_{\mathbb{R}}(G*u)\varphi^{\prime\prime}\mathrm{d}y.

(4.16)

Let
M_{1}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}u_{x}\big(t,q(t,x,y),y\big)\varphi(y)\mathrm{d}y
, then we have

\begin{split}\frac{{\rm d}M_{1}(t)}{{\rm d}t}&=-\frac{\gamma}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u_{x}^{2}\varphi\,dy-\int_{\mathbb{R}}G*u\varphi^{\prime\prime}\,{\rm d}y\\
&\quad+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[\frac{1}{2}g(u)-\frac{\gamma}{2}u^{2}-G*\left(\frac{1}{2}g(u)+\frac{\gamma}{2}u_{x}^{2}-\frac{\gamma}{2}u^{2}\right)\right]\varphi\,{\rm d}y.\end{split}

(4.17)

The literature
[
24
]
has obtained the following estimation formulas:

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\gamma}{2}u^{2}\varphi\,{\rm d}y\leq\frac{\gamma}{2}\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}L_{y}^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{\gamma}{2}\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|u_{x}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{\gamma}{2}E(u_{0})\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}},

(4.18)

\displaystyle-\frac{\gamma}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u_{x}^{2}\varphi\,{\rm d}y\leq-\frac{\gamma}{2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}u_{x}\varphi\,dy\right)^{2}=-\frac{\gamma}{2}M_{1}^{2}(t),

(4.19)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}G*\left(\frac{\gamma}{2}u_{x}^{2}-\frac{\gamma}{2}u^{2}\right)\varphi\,{\rm d}y\leq\|G*\left(\frac{\gamma}{2}u_{x}^{2}-\frac{\gamma}{2}u^{2}\right)\|_{L^{1}}\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\gamma E(u_{0})\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}},

(4.20)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}G*u\varphi^{\prime\prime}\,{\rm d}y\leq\|G*u\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}L_{y}^{2}}\|\varphi^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{x}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\varphi^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{2}}\leq E^{\frac{1}{2}}(u_{0})\|\varphi^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{2}},

(4.21)

where
E(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(u^{2}+u_{x}^{2})\,{\rm d}y.

In what follows, we present an alternative estimation procedure for the key term, which differs from that in
[
24
]
. Applying Hölder’s inequality, we find

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{2}g(u)\varphi\,{\rm d}y&\leq\frac{1}{2}\|g(u)\|_{L^{2}}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\|g(u)\|_{H^{s}}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq C(L,\|u_{0}\|_{X^{s}})\|u_{0}\|_{X^{s}}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

(4.22)

Then, by the Hölder’s inequality and the Young inequality for convolution, we obtain

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G*\left(\frac{1}{2}g(u)\right)\varphi\,{\rm d}y&\leq\left\|G*\left(\frac{1}{2}g(u)\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\|G\|_{L^{1}}\|g(u)\|_{L^{2}}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq C(L,\|u_{0}\|_{X^{s}})\|u_{0}\|_{X^{s}}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

(4.23)

Substituting the inequalities (
4.18
) through (
4.23
) into equation (
4.17
), one observes

\begin{split}\frac{{\rm d}M_{1}(t)}{{\rm d}t}&=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[\frac{1}{2}g(u)-\frac{\gamma}{2}u^{2}-G*\left(\frac{1}{2}g(u)+\frac{\gamma}{2}u_{x}^{2}-\frac{\gamma}{2}u^{2}\right)\right]\varphi{\rm d}y\\
&\quad-\frac{\gamma}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u_{x}^{2}\varphi{\rm d}y-\int_{\mathbb{R}}G*u\varphi^{\prime\prime}{\rm d}y\\
&\leq-\frac{\gamma}{2}M_{1}^{2}(t)+C(L,\|u_{0}\|_{X^{s}})\|u_{0}\|_{X^{s}}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}}+\frac{\gamma}{2}E(u_{0})\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}}\\
&\quad+\gamma E(u_{0})\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}}+E^{1/2}(u_{0})\|\varphi^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq-\frac{\gamma}{2}M_{1}^{2}(t)+C_{3}^{2},\end{split}

(4.24)

where

C_{3}^{2}=C(L,\|u_{0}\|_{X^{s}})\|u_{0}\|_{X^{s}}+\frac{3}{2}\gamma E(u_{0})\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}}+E^{1/2}(u_{0})\|\varphi^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{2}}.

(4.25)

By the assumption
M_{1}(0)<-\sqrt{\frac{2}{\gamma}}C_{3}
. By continuity, it follows that for all
t<T_{0}
, we have

M_{1}(t)<-\sqrt{\frac{2}{\gamma}}C_{3}
. Solving inequality (
4.24
), we know that

\frac{M_{1}(0)+\sqrt{\frac{2}{\gamma}}C_{3}}{M_{1}(0)-\sqrt{\frac{2}{\gamma}}C_{3}}e^{\sqrt{2\gamma}C_{3}\,t}-1\leq\frac{2\sqrt{\frac{2}{\gamma}}C_{3}}{M_{1}(t)-\sqrt{\frac{2}{\gamma}}C_{3}}\leq 0.

(4.26)

Therefore, there exists a time
T_{0}
such that

\begin{split}T_{0}&<\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\gamma}\,C_{3}}\ln\frac{M_{1}(0)-\sqrt{\frac{2}{\gamma}}C_{3}}{M_{1}(0)+\sqrt{\frac{2}{\gamma}}C_{3}}\\
&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\gamma}C_{3}}\ln\frac{\sqrt{\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u_{0x}(x_{0},y)\varphi(y)\mathrm{d}y-\sqrt{2}C_{3}}{\sqrt{\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u_{0x}(x_{0},y)\varphi(y)\mathrm{d}y+\sqrt{2}C_{3}}<\infty.\end{split}

(4.27)

and it follows that
\lim_{t\to T_{0}}M_{1}(t)=-\infty
. Thus Theorem
1.3
is proven. ∎

4.2
The case of
g
satisfies (
1.8
)

If the function
g
satisfies the polynomial growth condition (
1.8
), these arguments for Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.3
are still valid.

In fact, as demonstrated in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, when
s\geq 2
, for any
t\in[0,T]
, we have

\|u\|_{X^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq 2\|u_{0}\|_{X^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

When
s\geq 2
, the embedding
X^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
implies

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\|u\|_{X^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{X^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

then

|g^{\prime}(u)|\leq C_{1}|u|^{\alpha}+C_{2}\leq C_{1}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{\alpha}+C_{2}\leq C_{1}^{\prime}\|u_{0}\|_{X^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{\alpha}+C_{2}\leq C,

(4.28)

where the constant
C
is independent of
u
. This shows that
g^{\prime}\in L^{\infty},
therefore, both Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.3
hold.

5
Liouville-type Theorem

The literature
[
9
]
proves the Liouville-type property for a specific quadratic nonlinear term
g(u)=3u^{2}
. In this section, we generalize this result to arbitrary smooth nonlinear terms satisfying the conditions
g(u)\geq\gamma u^{2}
, and
g(u)>\gamma u^{2}
, for
u\neq 0
as follows.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
u=u(t,x,y)
be a non-trivial solution to the system (
1.7
) in the space
C([0,T];X^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))\cap C^{1}([0,T];X^{s-2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})),s\geq 2
, and assume
g
satisfies

\displaystyle g(u)\geq\gamma u^{2},

(5.1)

and that
g(u)>\gamma u^{2}
for all
u\neq 0
. Then there exists no open set
W\subseteq[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}
such that

u(t,x,y)=0,\forall(t,x)\in W,\ y\in\mathbb{R}.

Proof.

Using the framework established in
[
9
]
, we prove Theorem
5.1
by contradiction. Suppose there exists an open set
W\subseteq[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}
such that for any
(t,x)\in W
and
y\in\mathbb{R}
, we have
u(t,x,y)=0
.

First, integrating the system (
1.7
) with respect to
y
over
\mathbb{R}
yields

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(u_{t}+\gamma uu_{x}\right){\rm d}y=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}G*\partial_{x}\left(\frac{1}{2}g(u)+\frac{\gamma}{2}u_{x}^{2}-\frac{\gamma}{2}u^{2}\right){\rm d}y.

(5.2)

It follows from the assumption that

u_{t}+\gamma uu_{x}=0,\quad\forall\ (t,x)\in W,\ y\in\mathbb{R},

(5.3)

\frac{1}{2}g(u)+\frac{\gamma}{2}u_{x}^{2}-\frac{\gamma}{2}u^{2}=0,\quad\forall\ (t,x)\in W,\ y\in\mathbb{R}.

(5.4)

Since
W
is an open set, there exists
t^{*}\in[0,T],\ I\subseteq[c,d],\ c<d
such that
\{t^{*}\}\times I\subseteq W
. Define

\begin{split}p(x,y)&=G_{x}*\left(\frac{1}{2}g(u)+\frac{\gamma}{2}u_{x}^{2}-\frac{\gamma}{2}u^{2}\right)(t^{*},x,y)\\
&=-\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{sgn}(\cdot)e^{-|\cdot|}\ast\left(\frac{1}{2}g(u)+\frac{\gamma}{2}u_{x}^{2}-\frac{\gamma}{2}u^{2}\right)(t^{*},\cdot,y),\end{split}

(5.5)

q(x,y)=\left(\frac{1}{2}g(u)+\frac{\gamma}{2}u_{x}^{2}-\frac{\gamma}{2}u^{2}\right)(t^{*},x,y).

(5.6)

The regularity of functions
u,g
guarantees the continuity of functions
p,q
, then

\int_{\mathbb{R}}p(x,y){\rm d}y=0,\quad q(x,y)=0,\quad\forall\ x\in[c,d].

(5.7)

From the condition
g(u)\geq\gamma u^{2}
, we obtain
q(x,y)\geq 0
. By the condition, for all
u\neq 0
, we have
g(u)>\gamma u^{2}
, implying
q(x,y)=0
if and only if
u=0
. Thus, when
g(u)\geq\gamma u^{2}
, the equation
\frac{1}{2}g(u)+\frac{\gamma}{2}u_{x}^{2}-\frac{\gamma}{2}u^{2}=0
is equivalent to
u=0
(i.e., it admits only the trivial solution).

For any
z\notin[c,d]
, we have

-\operatorname{sgn}(d-z)e^{-|d-z|}>-\operatorname{sgn}(c-z)e^{-|c-z|},

(5.8)

Then given
y\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\begin{split}p(d,y)&=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\operatorname{sgn}(d-z)e^{-|d-z|}q(z,y){\rm d}z\\
&\geq-\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\operatorname{sgn}(c-z)e^{-|c-z|}q(z,y){\rm d}z=p(c,y),\end{split}

(5.9)

with equality holds if and only if
\forall\ z\in\mathbb{R},\ q(z,y)=0
. By the first equality of (
5.7
), it follows

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(p(d,y)-p(c,y)\right)dy=0.

Furthermore, since
p(d,y)-p(c,y)\geq 0
, we conclude that

p(d,y)-p(c,y)=0,\forall y\in\mathbb{R},

hence
\forall\ (y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\ q(z,y)=0
, i.e.

\left(\frac{1}{2}g(u)+\frac{\gamma}{2}u_{x}^{2}-\frac{\gamma}{2}u^{2}\right)(t^{*},z,y)=0,\quad\forall\ (y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

(5.10)

This implies that
\ u(t^{*},z,y)=0,\forall\ (y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

By the local well-posedness and uniqueness (see
[
24
]
), solutions that agree at some time must coincide for all times. Hence
u(t,x,y)\equiv 0
for all
t\in[0,T]
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
, contradicting the assumption that
u
is non-trivial. This completes the proof.
∎

In short, under the conditions
g(u)\geq\gamma u^{2}
and
g(u)>\gamma u^{2}
, for
u\neq 0
, no non-trivial solution can vanish identically on any nonempty open space-time cylinder. In other words, the solutions enjoy Liouville-type property.

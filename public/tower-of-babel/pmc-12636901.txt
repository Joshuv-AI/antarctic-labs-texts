Efficacy and safety of hyaluronic acid-enriched transfer medium in women undergoing single blastocyst transfer: a study protocol for a multicentre randomised controlled trial
Authors: He Cai, Dingfei Xu, Zhiqiang Wang, Bo Huang, Xia Xue, Haiyan Bai, Yuanyuan Man, Dan Lei, Qiongfang Wu, Yali Ni, Jin Lei, Juanzi Shi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionEmbryoGlue, a commercially available hyaluronic acid (HA)-enhanced culture medium, is commonly recommended as an adjunct to support embryo implantation. Given the current lack of conclusive evidence regarding its efficacy and safety, this study aims to evaluate the effectiveness and safety of HA-enriched medium in women undergoing in vitro fertilisation (IVF) treatment.Methods and analysisThis multicentre, patient-blinded, randomised controlled trial involves couples planning a single fresh or frozen blastocyst-stage embryo transfer. On the morning of embryo transfer, individual blastocysts are randomly allocated to either a high-concentration HA medium (EmbryoGlue, 0.5 mg/mL) or a conventional low-concentration HA medium (G-2 PLUS, 0.125 mg/mL). The primary outcome is live birth rate per embryo transfer, with secondary outcomes including biochemical pregnancy rate, clinical pregnancy rate, miscarriage rate, ectopic pregnancy rate, ongoing pregnancy rate, multiple pregnancy rate, as well as obstetric and perinatal outcomes and related complications. The trial plans to enrol 858 female patients from four IVF centres in China, with 429 patients in each group.Ethics and disseminationThe study has been approved by the Ethics Committee of Northwest Women’s and Children’s Hospital (approval no.: 2024–127), and all participating centres have obtained approvals from their respective institutional review boards or ethics committees. Results will be disseminated through publication in a peer-reviewed open-access journal and oral presentations at relevant domestic and international academic conferences.Trial registration numberNCT06492785.Keywords: Reproductive medicine, Research Design, Randomized Controlled Trial

Introduction

Embryo implantation is one of the crucial determinants of success in in vitro fertilisation (IVF) cycles.1 Consequently, exploring novel strategies to enhance implantation efficiency has been a focus of extensive research, including the addition of adhesive compounds such as hyaluronic acid (HA) to embryo transfer medium.

HA is a naturally occurring molecule belonging to the glycosaminoglycan family within the extracellular matrix and is widely distributed in the female reproductive tract across multiple species, including humans.2,5 In the human endometrium, HA expression exhibits cyclical fluctuations: HA levels increase in the endometrial stroma during the secretory phase of the menstrual cycle and decline to very low levels during menstruation, suggesting a potential role in the process of embryo implantation.6 The physiological functions of HA include preventing embryo expulsion; regulating cell proliferation, differentiation and migration; modulating gene expression; as well as facilitating endometrial decidualisation and trophoblast invasion.7 8

Historically, conventional culture medium containing low-concentration HA at 0.125 mg/mL, known as G-2 PLUS, has been widely used to assist embryo transfer (ET). However, recent studies suggest that high-concentration HA medium (EmbryoGlue, 0.5 mg/mL, manufactured by Vitrolife, Gothenburg, Sweden) may improve clinical pregnancy rates and live birth outcomes in assisted reproductive technology (ART) cycles. A previous Cochrane meta-analysis of 26 randomised controlled trials (RCTs), involving a total of 6704 participants, indicated that the addition of HA to the transfer medium could increase live birth rates and reduce the risk of miscarriage compared with medium with low HA concentration (0.125 mg/mL) or without HA.9 However, considerable heterogeneity across studies limited the definitive conclusions. Notably, subgroup analyses suggested that the potential benefits of HA may be more pronounced in fresh cycles, with less evident effect in frozen-thawed ETs.9 Furthermore, moderate-quality evidence suggested that HA supplementation may slightly increase the rate of multiple pregnancy (relative risk (RR) 1.45, 95% CI 1.24 to 1.70; n=3337; I2=36%), though this effect was likely due to the transfer of more than one embryo per cycle rather than a direct biological effect of HA.9

However, high-quality evidence regarding the use of HA in single embryo transfer (SET) cycles remains extremely limited. The only prospective randomised trial evaluating high-concentration HA transfer medium in SET cycles showed no significant impact on live birth rates with HA-enriched medium (RR 1.28; 95% CI 0.65 to 2.50).10 However, the live birth data in this study were derived solely from a small subgroup of 82 participants who underwent fresh ET, and the results were presented as a percentage of the total number of clinical pregnancies, with limitations in both sample size and statistical methodology. Therefore, further research on the use of HA in SET is warranted.

In addition to the effectiveness of IVF treatment, the culture medium used for the in vitro embryo cultivation is also postulated to influence newborn birthweight.11 However, the impact of HA-enriched medium on perinatal outcomes has not yet been clearly established through large randomised trials.

EmbryoGlue, a commercially available culture medium enriched with HA, currently has been proposed as an adjunctive strategy to support ET.12 13 However, before widespread adoption into routine clinical practice, its effectiveness and safety warrant further evaluation in large-scale trials. Therefore, this study aims to evaluate whether the use of HA-enriched transfer medium (EmbryoGlue) in single fresh or frozen blastocyst transfer influences live birth rates and other pregnancy and perinatal outcomes.

Methods and analysis

Trial design and setting

The purpose of this study is to compare live birth rates between the use of a high-concentration HA medium (EmbryoGlue, 0.5 mg/mL) and a routine medium with low-concentration HA medium (G-2 PLUS, 0.125 mg/mL) in women undergoing single fresh or frozen blastocyst transfer. Based on clinical observations and research needs, we have designed a multicentre, two-arm RCT with a 1:1 treatment allocation ratio. Except for the randomised intervention, all clinical procedures of IVF treatment and luteal phase support measures following transfer will be performed in accordance with local institutional protocols. This trial has been approved by the ethics committees of all participating centres (including the Ethics Committee of Northwest Women’s and Children’s Hospital, the Ethics Committee of Jiangxi Maternal and Child Health Hospital, the Ethics Committee of Gansu Provincial Maternity and Child-Care Hospital and the Ethics Committee of Tongji Hospital, Tongji Medical College, Huazhong University of Science and Technology) and will be conducted under the supervision of an independent Data Safety Monitoring Committee (DSMC), in compliance with Good Clinical Practice guidelines and the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki (2002). All participants are required to provide written informed consent before enrolment. The trial has been registered on ClinicalTrials.gov (https://classic.clinicaltrials.gov/; NCT06492785).

Patient recruitment

During the initial phase or before the start of the treatment cycle, the attending physician will provide a detailed explanation of the study to both partners. Eligible female participants must undergo screening by an embryologist 1 day prior to the ET procedure. If the couple expresses willingness to participate, a research nurse will provide a comprehensive explanation of the study protocol. On the day of ET, the treatment process will commence only after both partners have jointly signed the written informed consent form.

Withdrawal procedures

Couples will be able to withdraw their consent to take part in the trial at any time without giving a reason. If withdrawal occurs prior to randomisation, the participant will be excluded from the trial and no data will be analysed. After randomisation, participants may discontinue intervention or follow-up, but their data will be included in the intention-to-treat (ITT) analysis to the extent permitted by ethical guidelines and data protection policies. A per-protocol analysis, restricted to participants who completed the intervention as assigned, will be conducted as a secondary analysis.

Sample size calculation

We based our sample size calculation on the live birth rate per ET (48%) at the centre of the Northwest Women’s and Children’s Hospital in 2022. A minimal increase of 10% in the live birth rate is considered to be clinically relevant. In order to detect or refute a 10% increase (power 80%, two-sided significance level of 0.05), each group should have at least 390 cases. Considering a 10% drop-out rate, we plan to recruit 429 participants in each group (858 participants in total).

Randomisation, concealment of allocation and blinding

Eligible women will be randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to either the high-concentration HA group (EmbryoGlue) or the low-concentration HA group (G-2 PLUS). Randomisation will take place on the morning of the ET day through an online trial system using a computer-generated randomisation list. The randomisation process will include a sequence of permuted blocks of different sizes stratified by centre. The allocation sequence and designated treatment will be completely concealed from participating couples, clinicians including those conducting the transfer procedures and outcome assessors. Given the intrinsic viscous nature of EmbryoGlue, embryologists responsible for laboratory procedures will not be subjected to blinding. Only at the end of the study, when data collection has been completed, will the allocation sequence be revealed to the primary investigators.

Treatment allocation

Following fertilisation, embryos will be initially cultured in G-1 PLUS medium until day 3, then subsequently will be cultured in G-2 PLUS medium from day 3 onwards. On the morning of embryo transfer (ET), blastocysts designated for transfer will be randomly assigned to either the high-concentration HA group (EmbryoGlue) or the low-concentration HA group (G-2 PLUS). ET will be performed on either the fifth or sixth day of blastocyst development (exclusively on day six for frozen-thawed ET cycles).

All culture procedures will be conducted in strict accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions. Both EmbryoGlue and G-2 PLUS medium will be equilibrated overnight at 37°C in a 6% carbon dioxide, 5% oxygen incubator. The G-2 PLUS medium, routinely used across four laboratories, will serve as the control in this investigation. The primary difference between the two media lies in HA concentration: EmbryoGlue contains 0.5 mg/mL HA, which will be substantially higher than the 0.125 mg/mL HA present in G-2 PLUS. Prior to transfer, blastocysts in the control group will be incubated in G-2 PLUS medium for 30 min. In the experimental group, a single blastocyst will be placed in a double-well dish containing 1 mL of EmbryoGlue in the central well and 2 mL of G-2 PLUS medium in the peripheral well. During the embryo transfer procedure, a single blastocyst will be loaded into a Wallace/Cooke transfer catheter, which has been flushed with 0.5 mL of pre-equilibrated EmbryoGlue or G-2 PLUS medium, in accordance with the group assignment.

All culture and transfer medium will be manufactured by the same company (Vitrolife, Gothenburg, Sweden). ET will be performed under transabdominal ultrasound guidance using a soft catheter. Women will receive vaginal or intramuscular progesterone for luteal phase support in accordance with the centre’s standard operating procedures.

Outcome measures

Reproductive outcomes

The primary outcome is the live birth rate per ET. Live birth is defined as the delivery of at least one live infant after 24 weeks of gestation.14 Pre-specified secondary reproductive outcomes include biochemical pregnancy, clinical pregnancy, miscarriage, ectopic pregnancy, ongoing pregnancy and multiple pregnancy.

Biochemical pregnancy: defined as serum beta-human chorionic gonadotropin (β-hCG) >20 IU/L 12 days after ET.

Pregnancy complications and perinatal outcomes

For women who achieve ongoing pregnancy, all maternal and perinatal outcomes will be recorded, including hypertensive disorders in pregnancy (HDP, encompassing essential hypertension, pregnancy-induced hypertension, pre-eclampsia and eclampsia), placental implantation disorders (placenta previa, placenta accreta spectrum, placental abruption and/or abnormal cord insertion), preterm birth, preterm premature rupture of membranes, mode of delivery, gestational age, birth weight, low birth weight, very low birth weight, mean birth weight, macrosomia, large for gestational age, small for gestational age, neonatal intensive care unit admission, stillbirth or late fetal death, and early neonatal death (up to 28 days after birth), as well as congenital anomaly or birth defect. Core outcomes will be reported in accordance with the international consensus on Developing a core outcome set for future infertility research.15

Data collection and management

All baseline characteristics, clinical outcomes and laboratory results will be recorded in the couples’ electronic medical records as part of routine clinical care. Data from enrolled participants will be compiled using customised electronic case report forms and entered into the electronic database by research staff at the IVF centre. The data will undergo multiple validation checks to ensure accuracy and completeness of the captured information.

Statistical analysis

All analyses will be performed on an ITT basis using R software. Per-protocol analyses may be conducted but will be considered exploratory only. Baseline data will be presented using descriptive statistics (mean±SD for normally distributed variables or median and IQR for skewed variables). Categorical data will be expressed as frequency (percentage). The live birth rate and corresponding 95% CI will be estimated and compared between groups using the exact binomial method. Differences between groups in secondary outcome variables will be analysed using Student’s t-test (for normally distributed data) or Wilcoxon signed-rank test (for skewed data) for continuous variables, and Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables, with results reported as RR with 95% CI.

For missing data related to endpoint measures (β-hCG, clinical pregnancy, ongoing pregnancy), imputation will be performed based on results from subsequent pregnancy assessments. For example, if β-hCG results are missing but clinical pregnancy is positive, β-hCG will be imputed as ‘positive’. Endpoints that cannot be imputed using subsequent assessments (eg, live birth) will be treated as missing data. For adverse events, missing values will be handled as missing, except for causality, severity and intensity of adverse events, where a worst-case approach will be applied. Detailed analytical procedures will be described in a separate statistical analysis plan, which will be developed during the study and finalised before database lock.

Blastocysts cultured in vitro until day 5 or 6 will be graded according to the Gardner grading system.16 Based on the scores of the inner cell mass and trophectoderm, blastocysts will be classified into high quality (≥BB) and fair quality (BC, CB and CC).

DSMC

An independent DSMC will conduct quarterly reviews of the trial’s progress and provide guidance on its management. A single interim analysis will be conducted when 50% of the planned participants have been enrolled. This analysis will be carried out by an independent data analyst. The DSMC holds the ultimate authority to decide whether the trial should be terminated due to efficacy or safety concerns. In addition, the DSMC will oversee the monitoring of any serious adverse events (SAEs) that may occur. The committee will consist of an independent chair and at least two other independent members with expertise in relevant fields such as embryology, statistics or IVF clinical practice.

Follow-up procedure

Women with a clinical pregnancy will be followed up by their research nurses to document pregnancy events and outcomes at 12 and 24 weeks of gestation, at the completion of pregnancy and approximately 6 weeks postpartum. Participants with a negative pregnancy test will not receive further follow-up in this trial. Those who cannot be reached after multiple contact attempts at different time points will be considered lost to follow-up. In practice, all women conceiving through ART will be continuously monitored by their respective IVF centres, in accordance with mandatory requirements for reporting early pregnancy and delivery outcomes to the Chinese Society of Reproductive Medicine. These data will typically be collected by clinical staff through telephone communication with the couples.

Safety reporting

The investigator will notify the participants and the accredited medical research ethics committee if any event occurs that indicates the disadvantages of participation may be significantly greater than anticipated in the research proposal. The study will be suspended pending further review by the accredited medical research ethics committee, unless such suspension would jeopardise the participants’ health. The investigator will ensure that all participants are kept informed of relevant developments.

Adverse and SAEs

All observed or voluntarily reported adverse events will be recorded, regardless of the treatment group or suspected causal relationship to the intervention. An adverse event is defined as any undesirable experienc/e occurring to a participant during the trial, whether or not considered related to the intervention. All adverse events, whether observed by the investigator or spontaneously reported by the participant, will be recorded, including details such as timing, effects, dosage, severity or discontinuation of the intervention. A SAE refers to an event resulting in death (maternal, fetal or neonatal); life-threatening consequences (at the time of the event); hospitalisation or prolongation of existing hospitalisation; permanent or severe disability/incapacity; congenital anomaly or birth defect; or other significant medical events that may compromise participant safety or health (eg, ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome, ectopic pregnancy, miscarriage, pre-eclampsia). All SAEs will be continuously monitored and reported to the Medical Education and Technology Committee that approved the study protocol, in accordance with ethical and regulatory requirements, to ensure participant safety and trial integrity.

Symmetry and localisation in causally constrained quantum operator dynamics
Marcell D. Kovács, Christopher J. Turner, Lluís Masanes

Abstract
This paper explores the connection between causality and many-body dynamics by studying the algebraic structure of tri-partite unitaries ( ‘walls’ ) which permanently arrest local operator spreading in their time-periodic evolution. We show that the resulting causally independent subsystems arise from the invariance of an embedded sub-algebra in the system (ie. a generalised symmetry) that leads to the splitting of operator space into commuting sub-algebras. The commutant structure of the invariant algebra is then used to construct local conserved quantities. Using representation theory of finite matrix algebras, the general form of wall gates is derived as unitary automorphisms. Taking causal independence as a minimal model for non-ergodic dynamics, we study its effect on probes of many-body quantum chaos. We prove an entanglement area-law due to local constraints and we study its stability against projective measurements. In a random ensemble exhibiting causal independence, we compare spectral correlations with the universal (chaotic) ensemble using the spectral form factor. Our results offer a rigorous understanding of locally constrained quantum dynamics from a quantum information perspective.

This phenomenon can be understood as a strong form of localisation, where the non-trivial support of time-evolved local operators remains strictly finite in the thermodynamic limit and at arbitrarily late times. As we show, such a stringent constraint naturally lends itself to an algebraic analysis of the Floquet time-evolution operator’s invariants in operator space, connecting our work to the notion of causal independence in quantum information theory and field-theory. Our main generalisation from the works of

The use of operator algebras enables a mathematically rigorous understanding of local integrals of motions that can exist in operator dynamics and how they influence information propagation. The generality of our approach also gateways into finding the structure of a random unitary ensemble exhibiting the associated phenomenology without the need to rely on a specific model system (e.g. Clifford circuits). Adopting the operator algebra framework also facilitates the comparison with a more traditional notion of symmetries in many-body systems and fragmentation. Localisation in these circuits – by construction – is robust against perturbations within localised regions and coupling them to an environment. Analysis of the invariant subspaces of the unitary then enables understanding the stability of localisation against perturbations in the form of local projective measurement or gates. These can restore unbounded operator spreading and ergodicity in the circuit. Our starting point will be to construct algebras from the time-evolution of local operators
and derive the constraints on the unitary from the irreducible structure of their automorphisms. We will discuss in detail the local conservation laws associated with the bounded light cone, employing the structure of commutant algebras. Notably, the bounded light-cone can exist without local conserved charges which highlights that this form of ergodicity-breaking is more general than, for example, quantum circuits with (conventional) local symmetries or soliton dynamics

The paper is structured as follows. In

We conclude and comment on further generalisations of our circuit to infinite-dimensional systems as well as the hardness of verifying causally decoupled dynamics on quantum computers.

II
Bounded light cones in tri-partite systems

II.1
General setup

To begin, we consider the bounded light-cone problem generically defined over a tri-partite quantum system composed of a left system
L
, central system
C
and right system
R

\mathcal{M}_{LCR}

\mathcal{H}_{LCR}

\mathcal{M}_{LCR}=\mathcal{M}_{L}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{C}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{R}
(see
Appendix

A

1
, we are concerned with the adjoint evolution of a local traceless operator
O

O(t)=\mathrm{Ad}^{t}_{U}(O)=U^{t}OU^{-t}.

(1)

We will refer to

The focus of this paper is on

‘wall’
unitary on a tri-partite quantum system.
Such an obstruction prevents the operator to explore the available phase space, breaking ergodicity.
For convenience and generality, we first derive the localisation conditions on unstructured unitaries, and apply this result to brickwork circuits in later sections. We define a
‘wall’
as a Floquet unitary which generates arrested local operator spreading. Since adjoint unitary evolution is an algebraic isomorphism (ie.
\Ad_{U}
is linear operator preserving product, structure, adjoint structure and dimension), we can recast the localisation condition in terms of the evolution of operator algebras, too.

Definition II.1

(Walls)
.

Consider a unitary
U
jointly acting on the systems
L
(left),
C
(centre) and
R
(right). We say that

is a left-wall, if the evolution of any operator

\mathrm{Ad}_{U}^{t}\left(m_{L}\otimes\mathds{1}_{CR}\right)=m^{\prime}(t)_{LC}\otimes\mathds{1}_{R}\ .

(2)

Equivalently, and in terms of operator algebras,

\mathrm{Ad}_{U}^{t}\left(\mathcal{M}_{L}\otimes\mathds{1}_{CR}\right)\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{LC}\otimes\mathds{1}_{R}\ ,

(3)

for all

. Analogously,

\mathrm{Ad}_{U}^{t}\left(\mathds{1}_{LC}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{R}\right)\subseteq\mathds{1}_{L}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{CR}\ ,

.

Note that the above definition is a generalisation of causal independence in the works
52
;
4

In particular, a Haar-random tri-partite unitary would only lead to localisation with probability zero. Our goal in this section is to understand the invariant subspaces of a wall unitary. The left/right wall property of

II.1
is robust to perturbations in the following sense. By interleaving the Floquet unitary

with arbitrary, possibly non-unitary and time-dependent, local transformations in

and in

3
). Walls permit generic local evolution within subsystems

which can arise in coupling these to an environment.

Mathematically, consider a sequence of (local) quantum channels
\Phi^{(\tau)}
acting on

Then, the localisation conditions holds for all

\displaystyle\left(\Phi^{(t)}_{L}\circ\Ad_{U}\right)\circ\cdots\circ\left(\Phi^{(1)}_{L}\circ\Ad_{U}\right)(\mathcal{M}_{L}\otimes\mathds{1}_{CR})

\displaystyle\ \subseteq\mathcal{M}_{LC}\otimes\mathds{1}_{R}\ .

From a many-body physics perspective, wall unitaries lead to non-ergodic dynamics which is inherently robust under arbitrarily strong local perturbations in

II.2
Equivalence of left and right localisation

Stable embedding of a tri-partite wall unitary into an arbitrary circuit environment under

Our first result shows that left-wall condition implies the right-wall condition and vice versa.

Theorem II.1
.

U
is a left-wall if and only if it is a right-wall too.

Proof.

We start by recasting the wall condition as a commutator between the decoupled subsystem algebras and the time-evolved operators under
Definition

II.1
:

\displaystyle\text{Left wall: }[\mathrm{Ad}_{U}^{t}(\mathcal{M}_{L}),\mathcal{M}_{R}]=0\ ,

(6)

\displaystyle\text{Right wall: }[\mathcal{M}_{L},\mathrm{Ad}_{U}^{t}(\mathcal{M}_{R})]=0\ ,

(7)

for all
t\geq 0
. This is an equivalent formulation of the wall, since
\mathcal{M}_{LC}
(
\mathcal{M}_{RC}
) is the largest set of commuting operators with
\mathcal{M}_{R}
(
\mathcal{M}_{L}
) within
\mathcal{M}_{LCR}
under the double-commutant theorem for finite matrix algebras (see
Appendix

B
).
We denote by
\mathrm{spec}(\Ad_{U})
the set of eigenvalues of the map
\Ad_{U}
and by
\Pi_{\lambda}
the corresponding eigen-projectors, so that

\displaystyle\Ad_{U^{t}}=\!\!\sum_{\lambda\in\mathrm{spec}(\Ad_{U})}\!\!\!\!\lambda^{t}\,\Pi_{\lambda}\ .

(8)

It is proven in
80
that these eigen-projectors can be written as

\Pi_{\lambda}=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=0}^{N-1}\lambda^{-t}\mathrm{Ad}_{U}^{t}\ .

(9)

Using this identity we obtain (
7
),

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=0}^{N-1}[\mathcal{M}_{L},\lambda^{-t}\mathrm{Ad}_{U}^{t}(\mathcal{M}_{R})]=[\mathcal{M}_{L},\Pi_{\lambda}(\mathcal{M}_{R})]

which implies

[\mathcal{M}_{L},\Pi_{\lambda}(\mathcal{M}_{R})]=0\ ,

(10)

for every
\lambda\in\mathrm{spec}(\Ad_{U})
.
Analogously, by using (
8
) we can prove that (

) implies (
7
). So both identities constitute equivalent formulations of the right-wall condition.

Next, we recall that

\displaystyle\Ad_{U^{-1}}=\!\!\sum_{\lambda\in\mathrm{spec}(\Ad_{U})}\!\!\!\!\lambda^{-1}\,\Pi_{\lambda}\ .

(11)

Thus, a linear combination of
(
10

\lambda^{-t}
yields

[\mathcal{M}_{L},\mathrm{Ad}_{U^{-1}}^{t}(\mathcal{M}_{R})]=0\ .

(12)

Finally, using the identity

\Ad_{U}^{t}[\mathcal{M}_{L},\Ad_{U^{-1}}^{t}(\mathcal{M}_{R})]=[\Ad_{U}^{t}(\mathcal{M}_{L}),\mathcal{M}_{R}],

we arrive at
(
6

7

6

∎

The theorem implies that under a wall unitary’s evolution, left-local and right-local operators both remain localised. In the next section, we study how these operators interact through the centre by constructing the operator subspaces that encloses them.

Localised operator sub-algebras

We now turn to the edge cases where the embedded sub-algebra is not proper and show that this is trivial localisation due to the periodic repetition of a product gate.

Theorem II.5
.

The wall unitary is of bi-partite product form:

Definition II.3

(Local conserved charge)
.

Let
x\in\mathcal{M}_{C}
.

generates a local conserved charge if
\mathrm{Ad}^{t}_{U}(x)\in\mathcal{M}_{C}
for all

.

The above definition includes operators which commute with the unitary but local conserved charges are more generic, i.e. they include central operators which evolve up to an arbitrary phase, ie.

for some real scalar

Proposition II.1
.

Conserved charges on

\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{C}
.

Proof.

Follows straightforwardly from the closure
\ \mathrm{Alg}\{\mathrm{Ad}^{t}_{U}(x)\}_{t=0}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{C}
.
∎

Lemma II.6
.

A projector to the sub-algebra spanned by the co-domain of the infinite-time evolution of a conserved chage is a commuting operator.

Proof.

Define projector superoperator
P_{\mathcal{C}}:\mathcal{M}_{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
so that
P_{\mathcal{C}}\circ P_{\mathcal{C}}=P_{\mathcal{C}}
. We have
P_{\mathcal{C}}[\Ad_{U}^{t}(\mathcal{M}_{C})]=\Ad_{U}^{t}P_{\mathcal{C}}[\mathcal{C}]
from the closure of
\mathcal{C}
under time evolution. Then,
[P_{\mathcal{C}},\Ad_{U}^{t}]=0
and therefore

is conserved by the evolution.
∎

In the following, we can treat conserved charge and

\mathcal{C}
can be thought of as a symmetry of the adjoint super-operator which has been discussed in the literature as a model for non-universal dynamics

Theorem II.7
.

The algebra of local conserved charges on

is the intersection of commutants:

\mathcal{C}=\mathrm{Comm}\left({\overline{\mathcal{M}_{L}}}\right)\cap\mathrm{Comm}\left({\overline{\mathcal{M}_{R}}}\right).

(19)

Proof.

For one side of the implication, we note that
\mathrm{Comm}(\mathcal{M}_{L})=\mathcal{M}_{CR}
and
\mathrm{Comm}(\mathcal{M}_{R})=\mathcal{M}_{LC}
since finite matrix algebras are von-Neumann algebras satisfying the double-commutant theorem (see

\mathcal{M}_{C}=\mathrm{Comm}(\mathcal{M}_{R})\cap\mathrm{Comm}(\mathcal{M}_{L})
. Then,
\mathcal{M}_{L}\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{M}_{L}}
and we have the same construction for
\mathcal{M}_{R}
. It follows that
\mathrm{Comm}\left(\overline{\mathcal{M}_{L}}\right)\subseteq\mathrm{Comm}(\mathcal{M}_{L}).
This sub-algebra is invariant under the

map under

\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathrm{Comm}\left(\overline{\mathcal{M}_{L}}\right)\cap\mathrm{Comm}\left(\overline{\mathcal{M}_{R}}\right)

For the other side of the implication, the condition implies
[\mathrm{Ad}^{t}_{U}(\mathcal{C}),\mathcal{M}_{L}]=0\text{ for all }t\geq 0
. Since unitary evolution preserves the commutator, it follows that
[\mathcal{C},\mathrm{Ad}_{U^{-1}}^{t}(\mathcal{M}_{L})]=0\text{ for all }t\geq 0
. However, using Theorem

\mathrm{Ad}_{U^{-1}}^{t}(\mathcal{M}_{L})\in\overline{\mathcal{M}_{L}}
and thus
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathrm{Comm}\left(\overline{\mathcal{M}_{L}}\right)
. By the same construction on

\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathrm{Comm}\left(\overline{\mathcal{M}_{L}}\right)\cap\mathrm{Comm}\left(\overline{\mathcal{M}_{R}}\right)
for all

.

\langle\mathds{1}\rangle\subseteq\mathcal{C}
. Non-trivial intersection imply non-trivial conserved quantities under

. From this splitting of operator space, there can be conserved quantities which don’t participate in the dynamics of the
L-R
coupling and therefore are conserved (these are independent subsystems in the circuit). For operators which do participate in the coupling, the conserved quantities are determined from the intersection of Abelian sub-algebras of
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}
with the commutants.

For non-Abelian embedded algebras, the wall can be constructed so that not every element of the algebra is conserved. Under
Theorem

II.4
, if one chooses the right-localised algebra
\mathcal{B}_{C}
to be equal to
\mathrm{Comm}(\mathcal{A}_{C})
, then the conserved charges are the centre of the embedded algebra
\mathcal{C}=\mathrm{Comm}(\mathcal{A}_{C})\cap\mathrm{Comm}(\mathcal{B}_{C})=Z(\mathcal{A}_{C})
(see
Appendix

B
). However, a finite non-Abelian algebra is always the direct sum of full matrix blocks (see
Appendix

E
) which have trivial centre spanning only the identity
7
. In these cases, the conserved operators are the Abelian algebra generated by the projectors to the irreducible matrix blocks which is strictly lower dimensional than the full matrix block.

Local operators translated in time (but not in space) up-to a phase are sometimes referred to as still-
‘solitons’
in the literature which lead to non-ergodic evolution in dual-unitary circuit dynamics
10
;
11
;
40
. Some wall instances will feature these solitons (up to the gauge freedom discussed in the previous section) with an Abelian embedded algebra. However, the bounded light cone constraint is more general in that there can be instances where there is central-local operator space fragment which is decoupled from the left/right subsystems and therefore do not have a local charge. In these instances, the operator space is split fragments that commute at the central subsystem but are not separable both at
L-C
and
R-C
boundary. We will see an example of this in the following section.

II.5
Gauge freedom and time-dependent sequences

Figure 5:

Extending the wall condition to time-dependent unitary sequences. On

, the sequence consistes of elements of the normaliser (see

) group of
\overline{\mathcal{M}_{L}}
. On

Not only time-periodic evolution can exhibit causal independence in the sense of
Definition

II.1
. This will lead to an important restriction of our results due to gauge freedom, namely that the wall is only defined up to a global unitary isomorphism class.

Lemma II.10

(Wall sequence)
.

Let
(U_{\tau})_{\tau=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of unitaries where the algebra
\overline{\mathcal{M}_{L}}
is left invariant at all times:

\Ad_{U_{\tau}}\left(\overline{\mathcal{M}_{L}}\right)=\overline{\mathcal{M}_{L}}\text{ for all }\tau\geq 1.

(20)

Then, the wall conditions hold for all
\tau\geq 1
under the evolution induced by the sequence. We then refer to
(U_{\tau})_{\tau=1}^{\infty}
as a wall sequence.

Proof.

Follows from
Corollary

II.2
. Note that that the invariance of
\overline{\mathcal{M}_{R}}
follows automatically since commutation relations are preserved under any unitary isomorphism.
∎

The wall condition does not prevent a restricted notion of ergodicity within the localised subspaces. We may have wall sequences which have generic dynamics such as uniform mixing of operators. In this sense, it is an example of Hilbert-subspace ergodicity recently discussed in literature
51
. Under wall dynamics the subspaces are spatially localised subsystems.

Definition II.4

(Gauged wall sequence)
.

Let
(U_{\tau})_{\tau=1}^{\infty}
be a wall sequence under
Lemma

II.10
. We define a gauged wall sequence as
(\tilde{U}_{\tau})_{\tau=1}^{\infty}
under suitably chosen gauge transformations:

\tilde{U}_{\tau}=\prod_{t=1}^{\tau}{G}^{-1}_{t}U_{t}{G_{t-1}},

(21)

where
\Ad_{G_{0}}
acts invariantly on
\overline{\mathcal{M}_{L}}
and
G_{t}
are arbitrary unitaries for
t>1
.

From the previous definition, a gauged wall sequence violates the wall conditions at timesteps
\tau=1,2,...
since the gauge transformations are not required to preserve localisation. However, due to the underlying wall-sequence, the evolution merely induces a sequence of isomorphic transformations:

\mathcal{L}_{0}=\overline{\mathcal{M}_{L}}\overset{\Ad_{\tilde{U}_{1}}}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{L}_{1}\overset{\Ad_{\tilde{U}_{2}}}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{L}_{2}\overset{\Ad_{\tilde{U}_{3}}}{\longrightarrow}...,

(22)

where
\mathcal{L}_{i}\cong\mathcal{L}_{j}
for all
i,j\geq 0
, therefore the phenomenology of localisation still holds in a non-local operator basis. Another intepretation is that the localised operator evolution is
‘obfuscated’
, so that the observed operator evolution appears random to mask the invariance of a localised subspace.

The gauged construction highlights that the wall condition is only well-defined up to the class of isomorphisms of the embedded sub-algebras due to the arbitrary choice of global operator basis in the circuit. The sequence above need not terminate as there are infinitely many isomorphic sub-algebras. If the sequence recurs, then the gauged sequence reduces to a a time-periodic wall sequence under coarse-graining time. In the following theorem, we formalise this intuition.

Theorem II.11
.

Consider the sequence of isomorphic algebras
(\mathcal{L}_{\tau})_{\tau=1}^{\infty}
under the evolution of a gauged wall sequence
(\tilde{U}_{\tau})_{\tau=1}^{\infty}
. If the sequence recurs, the evolution is generated by a time-periodic wall sequence acting invariantly on a static sub-algebra. That is, there exists gauge transformations
G,H
so that:

\tilde{V}_{\tau}=\left(G\left(\prod_{t=1}^{T-1}\tilde{U}_{t}\right)H\right)^{\tau}.

(23)

(\tilde{V}_{\tau})_{\tau=1}^{\infty}
is then a time-periodic wall sequence which leaves the static algebra
\mathcal{L}
invariant at every step.
T
is the finite recurrence time (or equivalently the size) of the sequence of algebras.

Proof.

If sequence returns to itself in finite
T
, then there exists
P\leq T
, so that:

\Ad_{\tilde{U}_{T}}(\mathcal{L}_{T})=\mathcal{L}_{P}.

(24)

The unitary evolution is reversible, so we have:

\displaystyle\Ad_{\tilde{U}^{-1}_{P}}(\mathcal{L}_{P})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{P-1}=\mathcal{L}_{T}.

(25)

By iteration of the reverse evolution, the algebras up to
P-1
included already between indices
P
to
T
. Therefore, the sequence reduces to a single loop and we take
\mathcal{L}_{T+1}=\mathcal{L}_{1}
without loss of generality.

In this case, we choose the gauge transformation according to:

\displaystyle\Ad_{H}\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{1},

(26)

\displaystyle\Ad_{G}\mathcal{L}_{T}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L},

(27)

where we have introduced an arbitrarily chosen isomorphic sub-algebra
\mathcal{L}\cong\mathcal{L}_{i}
. Then, by substitution, the transformed, time-periodic, sequence
\tilde{V}_{\tau}=\left(G\left(\prod_{t=1}^{T-1}\tilde{U}_{t}\right)H\right)^{\tau}
leaves the sub-algebra
\mathcal{L}
invariant at every step.
∎

We expect that the converse of the above statement does not hold, since periodic repetitions can give rise to aperiodic sequences in the algebras which will not lead to a strict recurrence. One should view the above statement as an analogue of Floquet’s theorem showing that static invariant sub-algebras are always associated with time-periodic evolution
33
;
70
. An interesting outstanding question is how to construct continuous time-evolution (either periodic or non-periodic) to preserve the wall structure. This would involve understanding the relation between Lie algebras in Hamiltonian evolution and the desired
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebraic invariant structure that we leave for future work
41
;
53
;
16
.

III
Structure of brickwork walls

III.1
Representation theory

In this section, we utilise the representation theory of finite
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras in order to construct the detailed structure of wall unitaries in brickwork circuits and that of local conserved charges. We will focus on representations of a left-wall for convenience. From
Corollary

II.2
, a wall unitary leaves an embedded sub-algebra invariant. This implies that the wall is a representation of a unitary automorphism of the sub-algebra. We refer to formal literature on representations of finite algebras in
Appendix

E
while this section focuses on their application.

The invariance of the embedded wall algebra induces a splitting of Hilbert space under a global unitary isomorphism (a choice of operator basis):

\mathcal{H}_{LCR}\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n_{i}}\mathcal{H}_{L}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{D}_{i}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{E}_{i}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{R}.

(28)

where
\mathcal{D}_{i}
are irreducible matrix blocks and
\mathcal{E}_{i}
are the corresponding degeneracy space.
n_{i}
denotes the number of irreducible blocks.

Then, we can represent the algebra as full matrix algebras within irreducible blocks according to:

\mathcal{A}_{C}\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n_{i}}\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{D}_{i}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{E}_{i}}.

(29)

The commutant algebra follows the same block structure:

\mathrm{Comm}(\mathcal{A}_{C})\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n_{i}}\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{D}_{i}}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{E}_{i}}.

(30)

As we show in
Appendix

D
, unitary operators that leave the wall algebra invariant form an automorphism group that we call the
normaliser
well-known in error-correction literature as the set of logical operations implementable on a codespace
45
. This group is spanned by the unitary elements of the invariant algebra, the elements of the commutant and any unitary whose action is to permute equivalent matrix blocks in the decomposition of
Equation

29
. The structure of the group elements we derive in the following theorems.

Theorem III.1

(Wall structure)
.

Let
U
be a left-wall with an invariant algebra
\overline{\mathcal{M}_{L}}=\mathcal{M}_{L}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{C}\otimes\mathds{1}_{R}
. Then,
U
decomposes as follows (up to a permutation of equivalent matrix blocks):

U=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n_{i}}T^{i}_{L\mathcal{D}_{i}}\otimes R^{i}_{\mathcal{E}_{i}R},

(31)

where
T^{i}
and
R^{i}
are arbitrary unitaries in the irreducible blocks of
\mathcal{H}_{LCR}
under the Hilbert-space decomposition.

Proof.

We make use of the representation theory of finite
C^{*}
-algebras detailed in
Appendix

E
. The unitary automorphisms which leave a full matrix algebra
\mathcal{M}_{S}
of a subsystem
S
invariant are the unitary elements of
\mathcal{M}_{S}
(all automorphisms are
inner
). This means that the wall unitary is an arbitrary unitary in each irreducible block. Unitary elements of the commutant algebra are trivial automorphisms (they act as the identity on the algebra) since their adjoint action leaves each element invariant. Additionally, we note that one can construct permutations of the equivalent matrix blocks (that also have a unitary representation) that act non-trivially on both the elements of the algebra and its commutant.
∎

Corollary III.2
.

Consier a brickwork wall unitary
U=V_{LC}W_{CR}
composed of bi-partite gates. Then, there is a
C
-local gauge transformation
G
, so that
U=\tilde{V}\tilde{W}=(VG^{-1})(GW)
with the following decompositions:

\displaystyle\tilde{V}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{i}\tilde{T}^{\Pi(i)}_{L\mathcal{D}_{i}}\otimes r^{\Pi(i)}_{\mathcal{E}_{i}},

(32)

\displaystyle\tilde{W}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{i}t^{\pi(i)}_{\mathcal{D}_{i}}\otimes\tilde{R}^{\pi(i)}_{\mathcal{E}_{i}R},

(33)

where
t^{i},r^{i}
are arbitrary local unitaries satisfying
T^{i}=\tilde{T}^{i}t^{i}
and
R^{i}=\tilde{R}^{i}r^{i}
.
\Pi,\pi
are arbitrary permutations of equivalent block labels.

Proof.

Since the wall is only well-defined up to global isomorphisms under
Definition

II.4
, one can carry out an arbitrary gauge transformation in between brickwork layers whilst preserving the splitting of the space (which is a change of local operator basis in between layers). The structure of the constituent bi-partite gates then follows from the more general structure under
Theorem

III.1
. The irreducible block permutations of the two gates need not commute.
∎

In the next section, the explicit construction of wall unitaries will highlight that unitaries acting as block permutations on the algebra don’t follow the same block-diagonal form as the inner automorphism which implies that gates in brickwork walls
need not commute. Note that the above two results do not require the minimality of the region
C
containing the wall. If
C
is composed of multiple subsystems, we define minimality of a wall so that one can not enlarge the left and right subsystems by factors of
C
without breaking the localisation condition. We conclude the section by characterising the algebra of operators that commute with the wall-unitary in terms of the Schmidt vectors of the unitary.

Lemma III.3
.

The set of operators contained in
C
that commute with
F
is

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{C}=\bigoplus_{i}\mathrm{Comm}\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{D}_{i}}\otimes\mathrm{Comm}\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{E}_{i}}

where
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{D}_{i}}
is the
C^{*}
-algebra generated by the Schmidt vectors of

on
\mathcal{D}_{i}

\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{E}_{i}}

R^{i}_{\mathcal{E}_{i}R}

\mathcal{E}_{i}

Proof.

If

In other words,

\bigoplus_{i}\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{D}_{i}}\otimes\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{E}_{i}}

\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{D}_{i}}

IV
Locally constrained many-body dynamics

IV.1
Operator space fragmentation

In this section, we link the observed splitting of operator space to Hilbert-space fragmentation studied in many-body physics
57
in the context of random circuit dynamics.
Due to bounded light-cones, operator space splits into spatially localised invariant sectors
\mathcal{L},\mathcal{R}
which induces a splitting of the central operator space to the intersection
\mathcal{I}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}

\displaystyle=\overline{\mathcal{M}_{L}}\cap\overline{\mathcal{M}_{R}}=\mathcal{A}_{C}\cap\mathcal{B}_{C},

(44)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=\overline{\mathcal{M}_{L}}\setminus\mathcal{I}\supseteq\mathcal{M}_{L},

(45)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}

\displaystyle=\overline{\mathcal{M}_{R}}\setminus\mathcal{I}\supseteq\mathcal{M}_{R}.

(46)

This implies the following decomposition of operator space:

\mathcal{M}_{LCR}=\mathcal{F}\oplus\mathcal{F}^{\perp}=\mathcal{L}\oplus\mathcal{R}\oplus\left(\mathcal{L}\times\mathcal{R}\right)\oplus\mathcal{I}\oplus\mathcal{F}^{\perp}.

(47)

Fragmented circuits are defined to have of an exponentially large sector of invariant subspace in the dynamics. The wall induces fragmentation of the many-body system as individual left and right localised subsystems can be multiplied to create larger invariant spaces. In random circuits, this can lead to fragmentation whenever the circuit hosts an extensive number of bounded light cones.

A specific instance of fragmented circuit ensemble was investigated in detail in our previous work
44
where the unitaries are chosen uniformly randomly of entangling Clifford gates with a finite probability of non-Clifford perturbations. In the large system limit, randomly gates will act invariantly of, e.g., diagonal algebras that lead to fragmentation. We stipulate that any gateset which has a finite probability of satisfying the wall constraint (e.g. containing conditional unitaries as defined in
Appendix

F
) will lead to phenomenologically equivalent non-ergodic evolution, with an extensive set of walls and consequently exponentially sized fragment space. We note, however, that due to the dimensional reduction required for sub-algebra embedding to satisfy the wall constraint, we expect that fragmented circuit ensembles always form a zero-measure set of all unitaries, independent of circuit connectivity.

IV.2
Entanglement area law

A wall consisting of entangling gates requires the embedding of a sub-maximal dimensional algebra in the circuit. This is manifest in the restricted entanglement growth across the central region due to sub-maximal operator (and state) Schmidt rank.

Theorem IV.1

(Bounded entanglement)
.

Consider the product state
\ket{\alpha}_{L}\ket{\beta}_{CR}
evolving under a left-wall unitary
U
with invariant algebra
\mathcal{M}_{L}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{C}\otimes\mathds{1}_{R}
. The Schmidt rank of the evolved state with respect to the partition
L-CR
is bounded by

\displaystyle\mathrm{Sr}(U^{t}\ket{\alpha}_{L}\ket{\beta}_{CR})\leq\mathrm{dim}(\mathcal{A}_{C})\text{ for all }t.

(48)

Proof.

Denote the irreducible subspaces of the invariant algebra
\mathcal{A}_{C}
as
\mathcal{D}_{i},\mathcal{E}_{i}
under
Theorem

III.1
.
Let
\ket{\beta_{i}}_{\mathcal{D}_{i}\mathcal{E}_{i}R}
be the projection of
\ket{\beta}_{CR}
onto the subspace
\mathcal{D}_{i}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{i}\otimes R
, and let

\displaystyle\ket{\beta_{i}}_{\mathcal{D}_{i}\mathcal{E}_{i}R}=\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}\ket{\mu_{i,j}}_{\mathcal{D}_{i}}\ket{\nu_{i,j}}_{\mathcal{E}_{i}R}

(49)

be its Schmidt decomposition, whose rank is at most
\mathrm{dim}\mathcal{D}_{i}
.
After the action of
U^{t}
, the state
\ket{\alpha}_{L}\ket{\mu_{i,j}}_{\mathcal{D}_{i}}\ket{\nu_{i,j}}_{\mathcal{E}_{i}R}
evolves into
\ket{\tilde{\mu}_{i,j}}_{L\mathcal{D}_{i}}\ket{\tilde{\nu}_{i,j}}_{\mathcal{E}_{i}R}
, whose Schmidt rank with respect to the partition
L-\mathcal{D}_{i}\mathcal{E}_{i}R
is the same as the Schmidt rank of
\ket{\tilde{\mu}_{i,j}}_{L\mathcal{D}_{i}}
, which is at most
\mathrm{dim}\mathcal{D}_{i}
. This implies that the Schmidt rank of

\displaystyle U^{t}\ket{\alpha}_{L}\ket{\beta_{i}}_{\mathcal{D}_{i}\mathcal{E}_{i}R}=\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}\ket{\tilde{\mu}_{i,j}}_{L\mathcal{D}_{i}}\ket{\tilde{\nu}_{i,j}}_{\mathcal{E}_{i}R}

(50)

is at most
\mathrm{dim}^{2}\mathcal{D}_{i}
. Which implies that the Schmidt rank of

\displaystyle U^{t}\ket{\alpha}_{L}\ket{\beta}_{CR}=\sum_{i,j}\ket{\tilde{\mu}_{i,j}}_{L\mathcal{D}_{i}}\ket{\tilde{\nu}_{i,j}}_{\mathcal{E}_{i}R}

(51)

is bounded by
\sum_{i}\dim^{2}\mathcal{D}_{i}=\dim\mathcal{A}_{C}
.
∎

There are several remarks in order. We have showed the entanglement bound under a left wall evolution while an entirely similar calculation establishes a bound for the right wall evolution using
Theorem

II.4
. For a maximal Abelian sub-algebra, the above bound saturates to
\dim C
which restricts the attainable entanglement across the
L-CR
and
LC-R
by the central
‘bottleneck’
subsystem dimension. For wall unitaries which have random blocks (drawn under the Haar-measure for each invariant subspace) we expect the bound to be saturated up to the Page correction of random state entanglement
62
.

For non-abelian embedded algebras, we expect the bound to be looser, and the Schmidt rank bounded by the dimension of the maximal Abelian sub-algebra of
\mathcal{A}_{C}
. The operator localisation implies that
L,R
subsystems share no mutual information while each can be entangled with a third-party highlighting the subtle differences between operator spreading and entanglement spreading in unitary dynamics. Operator dynamics under a wall unitary is therefore an instance of an area-law phase
27
, since the scaling the dimensions of
L,R
cannot increase the state entanglement.

IV.3
Measurement-induced dynamics

In this section, we consider the effect of central projective measurement on the entanglement generation under wall dynamics. As a setup, we consider evolving a
L-CR
product state under the wall dynamics (as in

) and measuring afterwards. Due to the splitting of operator space, the effect of measurement is determined from the compatibility of the measured operator with the invariant algebra of the wall.

If

, the state is projected to one of the invariant blocks of the embedded algebra, therefore the Schmidt rank of the state satisfies:
\mathrm{Sr(\Ad_{M}\Ad_{U}(\ket{\alpha}_{L}\ket{\beta}_{CR}})\leq\dim^{2}\mathcal{D}_{i}
where

is determined from the measurement outcome. If the wall unitary is Haar-random within irreducible blocks, this is in accordance with the conventional notion of measurement being detrimental to entanglement generation in the circuit

;
49
;

. If

, the state is undisturbed by the measurement and its entanglement is unchanged.

Now consider

. These measurements break the splitting of operator space and force the evolved state into the commutant space of

. Analogous to entanglement swapping protocols

, leading to the entangling of
L,R
subsystems without the bottleneck system. In this case, the state can exhibit

entanglement as a result of the local measurement by iterating a non-unitary unitary interleaved with a local measurement (see

).

Concrete examples of random circuit dynamics interleaved with measurement in the presence of bounded constraints thus promises rich measurement-induced dynamics that we leave for future work. Recent work investigated this behaviour using controlled-
Z
gates with random
X
or
Z
measurements to model the stability of localised circuit evolution against local measurements

. The analytical theory developed in this paper provides the roadmap to generalisations of these results with the algebraic characterisation determining which measurements are stable and which are not. Finally, understanding the effect of generalised measurements in the presence of operator space fragmentation is an intriguing direction for future analysis.

IV.4
Spectral correlations

This section considers the spectral form factor (SFF) in the presence of wall constraints for a tri-partite random wall ensembles. This quantity is a well-established probe of quantum chaos and the exact operator space decomposition facilitates its analytical calculation for bounded-light cone dynamics
17
;
36
;
37
;
21
;
23
;
12
. Consider drawing gates from a random unitary ensemble
U\sim{\mathbf{U}}
. The SFF is defined as the ensemble-average trace powers:

K(t)=\mathds{E}_{U\sim\mathbf{U}}\left[|\tr(U^{t})|^{2}\right].

(52)

From random-matrix theory, it is well-known that taking
\mathbf{U}
as
d
-dimensional Haar-random unitaries leads to the SFF
K_{\mathrm{Haar}}(t)=\min(t,d)

54
;
37
.

Consider a random ensemble of walls with randomised invariant blocks
Theorem

III.1
with
T^{i}\sim\mathrm{Haar}(U(L\mathcal{D}_{i})),R^{i}\sim\mathrm{Haar}(U(L\mathcal{E}_{i}))
. Since we are interested in trace powers, any permutation of the invariant blocks leave SFF invariant. It is easily shown the form factor is multiplicative under ensembles of the form
U_{1}\otimes U_{2}
where
U_{1},U_{2}
are i.i.d. drawn random unitaries and additive over irreducible blocks.
This implies that for the wall ensemble,

K(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{i}}\min(t,\dim L\mathcal{D}_{i})\min(t,\dim\mathcal{E}_{i}R),

(53)

where we note the dimensional constraint
\sum_{i=1}^{n_{i}}\dim\mathcal{D}_{i}\dim\mathcal{E}_{i}=\dim C
. In the presence of a single bounded light cone, early time spectral correlations scale as
K(t)\sim t^{2}
indicating the effect of symmetry. As an example, consider Abelian wall with a maximal diagonal algebra (
Section

III.2.1
) with
\dim L=\dim R=d
and
\dim C=d_{C}
. The spectral form factor is
K(t)=d_{C}\min(t,d)^{2}
indicating that the saturation (Heisenberg) time scales as
\tau\sim\sqrt{d}
separating the fragmented ensemble for a uniform random ensemble with
K_{\mathrm{Haar}}(t)=\min(t,d_{C}d^{2})
. For a non-Abelian embedded algebra with full matrix invariant blocks, we expect that
K(t)\sim t^{2}
for early times and crosses over to
K(t)\sim t
at a timescale set out by the size of the largest invariant block dimension.

Consider now an ensemble with extensively many-walls, where the operator space is fragmented. In this case, we expect the spectral form factor to be super-polynomial
K(t)\sim\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}t^{n}
in the thermodynamic limit which highlights the difference between an integrable form factor
K(t)=d
and a putative many-body localised one
K(t)\sim\exp(t)

23
;
22
;
23
. We leave for future numerical investigations whether operator space fragmented circuits in this sense are sufficient to create many-body integrable spectral correlations. Finally, we note that perturbing a bounded light cone by a non-wall unitary can lead to slow spectral relaxation e.g. in Clifford circuits with Haar-random perturbations
44
. By breaking up invariant subspaces, we expect that at late times,
K(t)\approx t
and that the signature localisation (fast growth of SFF) is only visible for early times. It would be intriguing to compare our work with cross-over behaviour in systems with approximately broken symmetries exhibiting
‘Hilbert-space diffusion’

6
and to calculate analytical bounds on the crossover timescale.

V
Conclusion

This paper investigated the general algebraic structure of tri-partite unitary evolution that bounds the causal light cone in time-periodic operator dynamics. We understood this phenomenon through the splitting of operator space into commuting sectors from the invariance of an embedded sub-algebra on a subsystem. The wall unitaries which exhibit causal independence were then constructed as the unitary representations of the invariant algebra’s automorphism. The algebraic framework naturally lended itself to characterise the local conservation laws of these unitaries based on commutant structure of the invariant algebra. The causal decoupling studied here is – by construction – leads to a robust localised operator evolution against local perturbations on
L,R
including dephasing which naturally motivates its applications for many-body dynamics.

Our construction can be understood as localised many-body dynamics with a strictly finite non-trivial operator support in the evolution. This work generalises the author’s previous results on this topic in time-periodic random Clifford circuits by constructing the most general unitaries exhibiting the associated phenomenology of operator space fragmentation
44
. We have constructed wall unitaries without non-trivial central conservation laws using non-commutative central algebras. These highlight that locally constrained non-ergodic quantum dynamics is possible without solitons or integrability since the central subsystem may evolve ergodically within the commuting operator subspaces. Our model is thus a simple etalon for Hilbert-subspace ergodicity with the invariant subspaces being spatially localised
51
.

Some observable probes in many-body ergodicity were then characterised. We have proven an entanglement area-law due to the bounded light-cone due to the reduction of maximal dimension of the invariant algebra without relying on the stabiliser formalism used in the Clifford-case. Local projective measurements outside of the invariant algebra and its commutant can break the local constraint needed for fragmentation and counter-intuitively restore volume-law entanglement in the unitary evolution interleaved with measurements. In previous work, a similar instability was observed for generic central unitary perturbations, restoring the coupling between left/right subsystems. Finally, we showed that spectral correlations in a randomised wall-ensemble are directly related to the invariant sub-spaces which is manifest in the polynomial scaling of the spectral form factor which was conjectured in
44
. Our mathematically salient results are a rigorous study of locally constrained dynamics and we hope that the formalism here will enable the analytic study of circuit toy models to observe novel measurement-induced phase transitions in the presence of non-ergodic evolution.

There are several theoretical directions for generalising the theory presented. All our results focused on finite-dimensional algebras which immediately raises the question how much of the phenomenology persists in infinite dimensions. For example, we can conceive that the symmetry of restricted operator spreading from left-to-right and right-to-left in
Theorem

II.1
may not hold in infinite systems which could lead to operator dynamics with
one-sided
causality: ie. left subsystem being causally independent of right subsystem but not vice versa. In this work, we focused on tri-partite systems which is the simplest setting in which non-trivial causal decoupling can occur while it remains as an open problem how such causal decompositions can be constructed in multi-partite systems.

We have pre-dominantly focused on discrete and time-periodic operator evolution with simple generalisations to time-dependent dynamics through gauge-transformations of an underlying wall unitary evolution. Understanding the conditions under which continuous time (e.g. Hamiltonian) evolution exhibits causal independence remains an interesting open problem. Deriving the structure of a Hamiltonian’s Lie algebra required to span the automorphism group of invariant matrix algebras could enable the systematic construction of many-body Hamiltonians exhibiting causal independence.

Finally, we conjecture that given a classical description of a unitary circuit, deciding whether it exhibits a bounded light cone can be efficiently done on a quantum computer for finite times. Encoding a left-local operator as a Choi-state, the arrested operator evolution would be manifest as the separability of the time-evolved state. Efficient schemes exist for testing separability of multi-partite systems
15
which could be utilised for this purpose. Given that a bounded light-cone leads to an area-law, we hypothesise that efficient tensor network representations also exist for the unitaries studied. Our model may be utilised as a simple benchmarking scheme for complex dynamics in quantum simulators which could lift the impact of our analysis to practical applications.

. Up to an equivalence under permutations of irreducible blocks, all automorphisms are inner or elementwise outer.

Proof.

Let
U\in\mathcal{M}_{S}
be a potentially outer automorphism. The centre is left invariant under this automorphism from
Corollary

D.3
.
The centre of an algebra is spanned by unique (one-dimensional), ortogonal projection operators (as it is an Abelian algebra):

Z(\mathcal{A}_{C})=\langle\Pi_{1},...,\Pi_{m}\rangle,

(81)

satisfying
\Pi_{i}\Pi_{j}=\delta_{ij}\mathds{1}
. Because the projectors are unique up to re-ordering, the automorphism map acts:

\Ad_{U}(\mathcal{A}_{C})=\langle\Pi_{\pi(i)}\rangle_{i=1}^{m},

(82)

where
\pi:\mathcal{S}_{m}\rightarrow\mathcal{S}_{m}
is a permutation of
m
labels. Therefore, there exist another permutation
\chi
, so that
\chi\circ\pi=1
since permutations form a group. Let
U_{\chi}
be a unitary representation of
\chi
on
\mathcal{M}_{S}
. Thus, we have:

\Ad_{U_{\chi}}\Ad_{U}(\langle\Pi_{i}\rangle_{i=1}^{m})=\langle\Pi_{i}\rangle_{{i=1}}^{m}.

(83)

This shows that up to permutations, the center algebra is left element-wise invariant which fixes the irreducible subspaces of the Hilbert-space decomposition. Then the automorphism must follow the block-decomposition which implies the desired statement.
∎

Theorem E.7
.

The normaliser group of
\mathcal{A}_{S}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{S}
is generated by the group of inner automorphisms
\mathcal{U}_{i}
, elementwise outer automorphisms
\mathcal{U}_{o}
, and block permutation unitary group
\mathcal{U}_{\pi}
:

\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{A}_{S})=\langle\mathcal{U}_{i},\mathcal{U}_{o},\mathcal{U}_{\pi}\rangle_{g},

(84)

where
\langle\cdot\rangle_{g}
denotes the group closure.

Proof.

Follows from the previous lemmas straightforwardly.
∎

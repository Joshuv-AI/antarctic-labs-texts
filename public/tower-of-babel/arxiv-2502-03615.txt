Combinatorics of three-Catalan numbers and some positivities
Boualam Rezig, Moussa Ahmia

Abstract
. We introduce the three-Catalan triangle, highlighting the three-Catalan numbers along with their recurrence relation and combinatorial interpretation, which allows us to establish their log-convexity. Additionally, we prove that the rows of the three-Catalan triangle form a log-concave sequence.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
05A05, 05A15, 05A19, 05A30.

1.
Introduction

The Catalan numbers
C_{n}
have a deep historical lineage, tracing back to Euler and Catalan, with broad generalizations that enhance their combinatorial significance. They play a fundamental role in modern combinatorics, algebra, and computer science, counting numerous combinatorial structures. Defined by the formula

C_{n}:=\binom{2n}{n}-\binom{2n}{n+1}=\frac{1}{n+1}\binom{2n}{n}=\frac{(2n)!}{(n+1)!n!},\ \ \ n\geq 0,

these numbers were formally introduced in
1838
by the Belgian mathematician Eugène Charles Catalan in connection with counting nested parenthetical sequences and polygon triangulations, securing his name in their legacy. However, Segner had already identified them in
1758
in his study of binary trees and convex polygon triangulations. Earlier still, in
1751
, Euler investigated the number of ways to divide an
n
-sided convex polygon into triangles using non-crossing diagonals, deriving a recurrence relation that corresponds to the Catalan numbers, though he did not name them. Later, other mathematicians, including Bertrand and Kirkman, rediscovered them in various combinatorial contexts.

The Catalan numbers have several combinatorial interpretations
[
16
,
17
,
23
]
. Notably, they are associated with binary trees and Dyck paths:

•

The Catalan number represents the number of rooted plane trees with
n
edges (see
[
17
]
for further details).

•

The Catalan number also counts the number of Dyck paths from
(0,0)
to
(2n,0)
using steps
(1,1)
and
(1,-1)
, while staying above the
x
-axis. Equivalently, it represents the number of lattice paths from
(0,0)
to
(n,n)
with steps
(0,1)
and
(1,0)
, remaining below or on the line
y=x
.

Over time, several generalizations of Catalan numbers have been developed.

(1)

Super-Catalan numbers
(or Schröder numbers): These numbers count paths that are less restricted than those counted by the standard Catalan numbers. They follow the recurrence:

S_{n}=S_{n-1}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}S_{k}S_{n-k}.

They generalize the number of ways to divide a polygon into parts using non-intersecting diagonals.

(2)

m
-Catalan numbers
(or Higher-order Catalan numbers): are a generalization that count the number of ways to divide an
(mn+1)
-gon into
n
regions using non-crossing diagonals:

C_{n}^{(m)}=\frac{1}{(m-1)n+1}\binom{mn}{n}.

(3)

Fuss–Catalan numbers
(or Raney numbers)
[
22
]
: These are a
p
-ary generalization:

C_{n}^{(p)}=\frac{1}{pn+1}\binom{(p+1)n}{n}.

They count
p
-ary trees and appear in higher-dimensional lattice paths.

(4)

q
-Catalan numbers

[
11
]
: These incorporate a parameter
q
to keep track of extra structure (e.g., weighted combinatorial objects like lattice paths with area constraints):

C_{q}(n)=\frac{1}{[n+1]_{q}}{2n\brack n}_{q}=\frac{[2n]_{q}!}{[n+1]_{q}![n]_{q}!},

where
[k]_{q}!=[1]_{q}\cdots[k]_{q}
is
q
-factorial number with
[k]_{q}=1+q+\cdots+q^{k-1}
.

(5)

Higher-dimensional Catalan numbers
(or
d
-dimensional Catalan numbers)
[
10
]
: Appearing in algebraic topology and category theory, these numbers generalize Catalan numbers to higher dimensions, such as the Tamari lattice:

C_{d}(n)=(dn)!\prod_{i=0}^{d-1}\frac{i!}{(n+i)!},\ \ \ d=2,3,\ldots

which for
d=2
clearly reduce the conventional Catalan numbers.

(6)

s
-Catalan number

[
8
]
: By the way, there is another generalization of Catalan numbers using bi
s
nomial coefficients
\binom{n}{k}_{s}

[
6
,
7
]
which are defined as the
k
-th coefficient in the expansion

(1+x+\cdots+x^{s})^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{sn}\binom{n}{k}_{s}x^{k}.

This generalization, introduced by Belbachir and Igueroufa
[
8
]
for odd
s
, is denoted by
C_{n}^{(s)}
and referred to as the
s
-Catalan number
, given by

(1)

C_{n}^{(s)}:=\binom{2n}{sn}_{s}-\binom{2n}{sn+1}_{s},

where
\binom{2n}{sn}_{s}
is the central bi
s
nomial coefficients. Linz
[
20
]
later extended the definition of
s
-Catalan numbers to all positive integers
s
and provided a combinatorial interpretation of these numbers in terms of Littlewood–Richardson coefficients. Ghemit and Ahmia
[
18
]
further studied the two-Catalan numbers
C_{n}^{(2)}
, establishing a combinatorial interpretation based on a subset of vertically constrained Motzkin-like paths introduced by Irvine et al
[
19
]
. This approach ultimately led to proving the log-convexity of these numbers.

In this paper, we focus on the specific case of
s=3
for the
s
-Catalan numbers, as defined by relation (
1
), since the general case is too complex to analyze using the techniques employed here. In Section
2
, we introduce the coefficients of the three-Catalan triangle, referring to the first column of this triangle as the three-Catalan numbers. We then derive a recurrence relation and provide a combinatorial interpretation of these coefficients through a set of vertically constrained paths. Leveraging this interpretation, we establish the log-convexity of two-Catalan numbers in Section
3
. Finally, in Section
4
, we demonstrate that the rows of the three-Catalan triangle form a log-concave sequence.

2.
Definition and path interpretation of three-Catalan numbers

This section focuses on defining three-Catalan numbers and presenting combinatorial formulas, including a recurrence relation and interpretations through lattice paths.

Since the
s
-Catalan number is defined as the difference between two consecutive terms of the central bi
s

[

s\geq 1
and
n\geq 0

k=0,1,\ldots,sn

\binom{n}{k}_{s}
represents the
k

(2)

(1+x+\cdots+x^{s})^{n}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\binom{n}{k}_{s}x^{k},

where
\binom{n}{k}_{s}=0
for
k>sn
or
k<0

•

Symmetry:

(3)

\binom{n}{k}_{s}=\binom{n}{sn-k}_{s}.

•

Longitudinal recurrence:

(4)

\binom{n}{k}_{s}=\sum_{j=0}^{s}\binom{n-1}{k-j}_{s}.

•

Absorption identity:

(5)

k\binom{n}{k}_{s}=n\sum_{j=1}^{s}j\binom{n-1}{k-j}_{s}.

These coefficients, similar to standard binomial coefficients, follow the construction of Pascal’s triangle, referred to as the ”

Definition 2.1
.

Let

C_{n,k}^{(3)}:=\binom{2n}{3n+k}_{3}-\binom{2n}{3n+k+1}_{3},

Specifically, for

C_{n}^{(3)}:=C_{n,0}^{(3)}=\binom{2n}{3n}_{3}-\binom{3n}{3n+1}_{3},

where
\binom{2n}{3n}_{3}
represents the central quadrinomial coefficient.

This definition leads to the following key proposition.

Proposition 2.2
.

The coefficients of the three-Catalan triangle satisfy the recurrence relations:

(6)

\displaystyle C_{n+1,0}^{(3)}=C_{n,0}^{(3)}+C_{n,1}^{(3)}+C_{n,2}^{(3)}+C_{n,3}^{(3)},

(7)

\displaystyle C_{n+1,1}^{(3)}=C_{n,0}^{(3)}+3C_{n,1}^{(3)}+3C_{n,2}^{(3)}+2C_{n,3}^{(3)}+C_{n,4}^{(3)},

(8)

\displaystyle C_{n+1,2}^{(3)}=C_{n,0}^{(3)}+3C_{n,1}^{(3)}+4C_{n,2}^{(3)}+3C_{n,3}^{(3)}+2C_{n,4}^{(3)}+C_{n,5}^{(3)},

(9)

\displaystyle C_{n+1,k}^{(2)}=C_{n,k-3}^{(3)}+2C_{n,k-2}^{(3)}+3C_{n,k-1}^{(3)}+4C_{n,k}^{(3)}+3C_{n,k+1}^{(3)}+2C_{n,k+2}^{(3)}+C_{n,k+3}^{(3)},\text{\ for\ }k\geq 3.

3.
Log-convexity of three-Catalan numbers

A sequence of nonnegative numbers
(a_{n})_{n}

a_{i}a_{i+2}\geq a_{i+1}^{2}
holds. For further details, we refer the reader to
[

27
]

4.
Log-concavity of rows of two-Catalan triangle

A sequence of nonnegative numbers
(a_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is said to be log-concave if it satisfies
a_{i-1}a_{i+1}\leq a_{i}^{2}
for all
i>0
, which is equivalent to the condition
a_{i-1}a_{j+1}\leq a_{i}a_{j}
for all
1\leq i\leq j
. Log-concavity arises in combinatorics and various other fields of mathematics, attracting significant research interest. For a comprehensive overview, see Stanley’s survey article
[
25
]
and Brenti’s supplement
[
13
]
.

In this section, we prove the log-concavity of the rows of three-Catalan triangle.

Let us denote by
\mathfrak{S}
the set of sequences
(u_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
of nonnegative numbers. Given
7
nonnegative numbers
\lambda_{-3},\lambda_{-2},\lambda_{-1},\lambda_{0}
,
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}
, define the linear operator
L=L[\lambda_{-3},\lambda_{-2},\lambda_{-1},\lambda_{0}
,
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}]
, on
\mathfrak{S}
by

L(u_{k})=\sum_{j=-3}^{3}\lambda_{j}u_{k+j}\ \ (k\in\mathbb{Z}).

For
n\geq 2,
define
L^{n}:=L(L^{n-1})
by induction. It is convenient to view
L^{0}
as the identity operator. Let
(u_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a log-concave sequence.

Lemma 4.1
.

If the sequence
\left(\lambda_{-3},\lambda_{-2},\lambda_{-1},\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}\right)
is symmetric and log-concave, then so is the sequence
\left(L^{n}(u_{k})\right)_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
.

Proof.

In fact

\displaystyle\left(L(u_{k})\right)^{2}-L(u_{k-1})L(u_{k+1})=\left(\sum_{j=-3}^{3}\lambda_{j}u_{k+j}\right)^{2}-\sum_{j=-3}^{3}\lambda_{j}u_{k+j-1}\sum_{j=-3}^{3}\lambda_{j}u_{k+j+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=-3}^{3}\lambda_{j}^{2}(u_{k+j}^{2}-u_{k+j-1}u_{k+j+1})+\sum_{-3\leq j<l\leq 3}\lambda_{j}\lambda_{l}(u_{k+j}u_{k+l}-u_{k+j-1}u_{k+l+1})

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\sum_{-3\leq j<l\leq 3}\lambda_{j}\lambda_{l}u_{k+j}u_{k+l}-\sum_{-3\leq j<l\leq 3}\lambda_{j}\lambda_{l}u_{k+j+1}u_{k+l-1}

\displaystyle=T_{1}+T_{2}+T_{3}

with

\displaystyle T_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=-3}^{3}\lambda_{j}^{2}(u_{k+j}^{2}-u_{k+j-1}u_{k+j+1}),

\displaystyle T_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{-3\leq j<l\leq 3}\lambda_{j}\lambda_{l}(u_{k+j}u_{k+l}-u_{k+j-1}u_{k+l+1})

and

\displaystyle T_{3}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{-2\leq j+1<l\leq 3}\lambda_{j}\lambda_{l}(u_{k+j+1}u_{k+l-1}-u_{k+j}u_{k+l}).

It follows that

\displaystyle\left(L(u_{k})\right)^{2}-L(u_{k-1})L(u_{k+1})=\sum_{j=-2}^{2}\left(\lambda_{j}^{2}-\lambda_{j-1}\lambda_{j+1}\right)\times\left(u_{k+j}^{2}-u_{k+j-1}u_{k+j+1}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\lambda_{-3}^{2}\left(u_{k-3}^{2}-u_{k-4}u_{k-2}\right)+\lambda_{3}^{2}\left(u_{k+3}^{2}-u_{k+2}u_{k+4}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\sum_{-1\leq j+2<l\leq 2}\left(\lambda_{j}\lambda_{l}-\lambda_{j-1}\lambda_{l+1}\right)\times\left(u_{k+j}u_{k+l}-u_{k+j-1}u_{k+l+1}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\sum_{j=-3}^{2}\lambda_{j}\lambda_{j+1}\left(u_{k+j}u_{k+j+1}-u_{k+j-1}u_{k+j+2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\sum_{j=-3}^{1}\lambda_{j}\lambda_{j+2}\left(u_{k+j}u_{k+j+2}-u_{k+j-1}u_{k+j+3}\right)\geq 0.

By induction, the sequence
\left(L^{n}(u_{k})\right)_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is also log-concave for
n\geq 0
.
∎

By setting
\left(\lambda_{-3},\lambda_{-2},\lambda_{-1},\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}\right)=(1,2,3,4,3,2,1)
and
u_{k}=C_{1,k}^{(3)}
in Lemma
4.1
, we obtain the following result.

Proposition 4.2
.

The rows sequence of three-Catalan triangle
\left(C^{(3)}_{n,k}\right)_{0\leq k\leq 3n}
are log-concave.

-Catalan numbers for any positive integer

-Catalan triangle for any positive integer
s
?

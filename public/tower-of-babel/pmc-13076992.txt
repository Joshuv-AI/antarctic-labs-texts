Addendum: Versatile phosphonium-catechol dispersant for inorganic nanoparticle stability across diverse media and manufacturing processes
Authors: Shuta Hara, Keiya Kawamura, Atsushi Furukawa, Yuumi Takeoka, Yuichiro Koide, Keisuke Fukasawa, Genza Sanae, Hiroyuki Hirata, Takao Gunji, Hiroki Ikake, Takayuki Ikehara, Shigeru Shimizu

In the originally published version of this Article, tensile tests were experimentally performed at a crosshead speed of 10 mm/min, as described in the Methods section. However, during data export, the crosshead speed was incorrectly set to 30 mm/min. As a result, the displacement and strain values were systematically overestimated by a factor of three. During a subsequent post-publication review of the exported tensile datasets and associated data processing procedures, we also identified that inconsistent datasets were inadvertently used for Fig. 6b and Supplementary Fig. S10. Upon verification, the dataset corresponding to Supplementary Fig. S10c was confirmed to be correct. A revised version of Fig. 6b is presented below.

In addition, the fracture energy was originally calculated by integrating stress with respect to strain expressed in percentage units, rather than dimensionless strain, leading to an additional scaling error. Accordingly, the strain-dependent mechanical parameters derived from the tensile tests have been recalculated and are shown below. Specifically, the strain axes in Fig. 6a, 6c and 6e, as well as Fig. S10, and the numerical values of Young’s modulus, elongation at break, and fracture energy reported in Table S7 in the Supporting Information have been corrected in the following figures and tables.

The stress values and maximum tensile strength, which were calculated from the measured load and cross-sectional area, are not affected by these corrections. The corresponding numerical values described in the main text should be as follows.

The addition of nanoparticles (NPs) increased the Young’s modulus of the SBS-based nanocomposites to 11.0 MPa (10 wt%) and 15.1 MPa (20 wt%), representing approximately twofold and threefold enhancements compared with pristine SBS (4.7 MPa).

In the tensile tests of the PDLLA-based samples, the addition of NPs significantly increased the Young’s modulus from 27.2 MPa for unmodified PDLLA to 45.8 MPa (5 wt%) and 80.3 MPa (10 wt%), indicating effective stiffening of the matrix. However, the elongation at break decreased from 503.9% to 320.3% (5 wt%) and 162.6% (10 wt%), demonstrating a pronounced loss of ductility with increasing NP content.

For the pHEMA-based samples, the Young’s modulus increased from 154.8 MPa for the unmodified polymer to 208.5 MPa (0.1 wt%) and 292.0 MPa (1 wt%).

Furthermore, descriptions in the main text concerning the tensile behavior of the PDLLA-based samples, particularly those related to fracture energy and toughness, should read as follows: Consequently, the fracture energy decreased from 24.0 MJ m⁻³ for unmodified PDLLA to 19.1 MJ m⁻³ (5 wt%) and 13.1 MJ m⁻³ (10 wt%), reflecting a clear stiffness–ductility trade-off. Notably, the sample containing 5 wt% NPs exhibited a more balanced mechanical response than the 10 wt% sample, retaining higher elongation and fracture energy. This behavior is consistent with the enlarged semicircular arc observed in the Cole–Cole plot, indicating more balanced viscoelastic behavior in the small-strain regime. In contrast, excessive NP loading (10 wt%) led to stronger restriction of polymer chain mobility, resulting in severe ductility loss despite further stiffening, as supported by the DMA results and TEM observations.

In addition, the systematic names of the phosphonium–catechol dispersants need to be corrected to accurately reflect their chemical structures. The compound names originally described as “tributyl(3-((3,4-dihydroxyphenylamino)-3-oxopropyl)phosphonium acetate (TPA)” and “tributyl(3-((3,4-dihydroxyphenylamino)-3-oxopropyl)phosphonium chloride (TPC)” should instead be “Tributyl[2-(N-(3,4-dihydroxyphenethyl)carbamoyl)ethyl]phosphonium acetate (TPA)” and “Tributyl[2-(N-(3,4-dihydroxyphenethyl)carbamoyl)ethyl]phosphonium chloride (TPC)”, respectively.

In the originally published version of this Article, the sentence

“Tensile tests were performed to investigate the effect of the interaction between TPC and each polymer matrix on the mechanical properties (Fig. 5a–c and Table 1).”

contained incorrect figure and table citations.

This should instead read:

“Tensile tests were performed to investigate the effect of the interaction between TPC and each polymer matrix on the mechanical properties (Fig. 6a–c, Fig. S10 and Table S7).”

We sincerely apologize for these errors and appreciate the opportunity to correct the published record. We believe that these corrections improve the accuracy and transparency of the reported data while preserving the scientific integrity of the work.

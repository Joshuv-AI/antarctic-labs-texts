Weak bosons as partons below 10 TeV partonic center-of-momentum
Innes Bigaran, Richard Ruiz

Abstract
We investigate the modeling of weak boson number densities for leptons and hadrons in practical calculations in the Standard Model. In the framework of the Effective W W Approximation (EWA) and in the R ξ R_{\xi} and axial gauges, we derive the unrenormalized, tree-level parton number densities for weak bosons from massless fermions at next-to-leading power in the collinear expansion. Corrections exhibit various pathologies and properties, including those conjectured but not proven, and parallel heavy quark factorization. We suppress pathologies through a new set of kinematical consistency conditions. When satisfied, shapes and normalizations of full matrix elements for many-leg processes can be well approximated by the EWA and fragmentation contributions at leading power, suggesting the onset of tree-level factorization. Findings also suggest that the EWA is testable at the LHC with ℒ = 450 \mathcal{L}=450 fb -1 of same-sign W ​ W WW scattering data at s = 13.6 \sqrt{s}=13.6 TeV.

Introduction

Despite confinement in quantum chromodynamics (QCD),
the structure of hadrons can be reliably described
with the QCD-improved parton model.
A core tenet of this framework is the existence
of a “parton sea” within a hadron,
i.e., a near continuum of low-energy gluons and
light quark-antiquark pairs.
Importantly, sea partons originate
from higher energy “valance” quarks
that radiate gluons,
which subsequently split
and
populate lower-energy modes.

In multi-TeV
pp

like those at the Large Hadron Collider (LHC),

can be

Under such conditions,

VI
Conclusion

We report the first NLP-accurate calculation of

W/Z
PDFs for high-energy fermions [Eq. (
6
)].
From these PDFs,
we have derived kinematical criteria
[Eq. (
10
)]
that lead to good agreement between
full and approximated MEs,
including at the differential level,
and suggest the onset of EW factorization
at tree level.
Partonic behavior emerges when
weak bosons carry at least

E_{V}=10\times M_{V}
of energy
(pCM frame) and
p_{T}\sim\mu_{f}\ll E_{V}

is near threshold.
This corresponds to

\mathcal{O}(M_{V}^{2}/E_{V}^{2})\sim 1\%

power corrections.

Given this energy scale,
it may be possible to observe the emergence of

W
bosons as partons
of a proton at the LHC.
At
\sqrt{s}=13.6{\rm\ TeV}
, the same-sign
WW
scattering
process
pp\to W^{\pm}W^{\pm}jj

has a LO cross section
of
\sigma_{\rm LHC13.6}\sim 5.5\ (1.0){\rm\ fb}

for
M(WW)>1\ (1.6){\rm\ TeV}
and
M(jj)>500{\rm\ GeV}
.
Taking the
W\to e/\mu
branching rate BR
{}^{2}\approx 4/81
,
and assuming
selection and acceptance rates of

\epsilon\times\mathcal{A}\approx 25\%

[
70
,
1
,
2
,
71
]
,
then about
N=\mathcal{L}\sigma{\rm BR}^{2}\epsilon\mathcal{A}\approx 30\ (6)
events are anticipated
with
\mathcal{L}=450{\rm~fb^{-1}}
,
or
N=300\ (60)
with
\mathcal{L}=4500{\rm~fb^{-1}}
.
This is large enough to be interesting
and motivates further study.
Of particular importance is an
EWA-compatible parton shower, which is now
the last missing ingredient for
simulating
fully exclusive events at colliders.

Appendix C
Scale variation at LP and NLP

Assuming a collection of events,
each with cross section weight
\Delta\sigma_{k}

at a baseline scale
\mu=\mu_{0}
, then the scale variation
of the cross section weight
(\Delta\sigma_{k}^{\prime})
at a scale

\mu=\zeta\mu_{0}
is given by the reweighting scheme

\displaystyle\Delta\sigma_{k}^{\prime}(\zeta\mu_{0})\ =\ \Delta\sigma_{k}(\mu_{0})\ \times\ \frac{w_{k}(\zeta\mu_{0})}{w_{k}(\mu_{0})}\ .

(28)

Here,
w_{k}(\mu)
is the scale weight. The scale weight varies according to PDF species and accuracy.

For transverse PDFs at LLA, LP [Eqs. (
5a
)-(
5b
)], and NLP [Eqs. (
6a
)-(
6b
)], the scale weights are

\displaystyle w_{k}^{\rm LLA}(V_{\lambda=\pm},\mu)

\displaystyle=\log\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{M_{V}^{2}}\right)\ ,

(29)

\displaystyle w_{k}^{\rm LP}(V_{\lambda=\pm},\mu)

\displaystyle=\log\left(\frac{M_{V}^{2}+\mu^{2}}{M_{V}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{M_{V}^{2}+\mu^{2}}\right)\ ,

(30)

\displaystyle w_{k}^{\rm NLP}(V_{\lambda=\pm},\mu)

\displaystyle=(a_{\lambda}+b_{T})\log\left(\frac{M_{V}^{2}+\mu^{2}}{M_{V}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{\mu^{2}+M_{V}^{2}}\right)(a_{\lambda}+c_{T})\ ,

(31)

with coefficients

\displaystyle a_{\lambda=+}

\displaystyle=(1-z),\quad a_{\lambda=-}=1,

\displaystyle b_{T}

\displaystyle=(2-z)\frac{M_{V}^{2}}{E_{V}^{2}},

(32)

\displaystyle c_{T}

\displaystyle=b_{T}+(2-z)\frac{\mu^{2}}{2E_{V}^{2}}\ .

(33)

For longitudinal PDFs at LLA, LP [Eq. (
5c
)], and NLP [Eq. (
6c
)], the scale weights are respectively,

\displaystyle w_{k}^{\rm LLA}(V_{\lambda=0},\mu)

\displaystyle=1\ ,

(34)

\displaystyle w_{k}^{\rm LP}(V_{\lambda=0},\mu)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{M_{V}^{2}+\mu^{2}}\right)\ ,

(35)

\displaystyle w_{k}^{\rm NLP}(V_{\lambda=0},\mu)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{M_{V}^{2}+\mu^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle-\Bigg\{\left[\frac{1-(1-z)^{2}}{(1-z)}\right]\left(\frac{M_{V}^{2}}{4E_{V}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\times\Big[\log\left(\frac{M_{V}^{2}+\mu^{2}}{M_{V}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{M_{V}^{2}+\mu^{2}}\right)\Big]\Bigg\}\ .

(36)

Bruxism, Sleep Quality, Anxiety Disorders, and Tension-Type Headache in Temporomandibular Joint Disorders: A Systematic Review
Authors: Aditya S. Dupare, Mukta Motwani, Aarati Panchbhai, Vidyarjan Sukhadeve, Charwak Tayade, Snehal V. Bhalerao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTTemporomandibular joint disorders (TMD) involve pain and dysfunction of the temporomandibular joint and associated muscles. Emerging evidence suggests associations with bruxism, poor sleep quality, anxiety disorders, and tension-type headache (TTH). To systematically review the associations between bruxism, sleep quality, anxiety disorders, and TTH in patients with TMD. Systematic searches were conducted in PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, and Cochrane Library up to May 2025. Studies included adults with TMD and reported associations with bruxism, sleep quality, anxiety, or TTH. Risk of bias was assessed using the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale. Data were narratively synthesized. Twenty-six studies were included. Bruxism prevalence ranged from 45% to 87% in TMD patients, with significant associations with myofascial pain (P < 0.05). Poor sleep quality was reported in 40–75% of TMD patients and correlated with pain severity. Anxiety disorders were present in 30–60% of TMD patients, often exacerbating pain perception. TTH coexisted in 25–65% of TMD patients, sharing central sensitization as a mechanism. Study heterogeneity prevented meta-analysis. Bruxism, poor sleep quality, anxiety disorders, and TTH frequently coexist with TMD. Multidisciplinary assessment is crucial. Future longitudinal studies are warranted.

INTRODUCTION

Temporomandibular joint disorders (TMD) encompass a spectrum of musculoskeletal and neuromuscular conditions characterized by pain, joint sounds, and functional limitation of the jaw.[1] Increasing evidence supports the involvement of biopsychosocial factors in TMD etiology.[23] Bruxism, sleep disturbances, anxiety, and TTH have been linked to TMD, yet their interactions remain incompletely understood.[456789]

Bruxism can exert excessive forces on the temporomandibular joint, possibly leading to structural damage or pain.[6] Sleep disturbances may modulate pain sensitivity and contribute to chronic pain.[7] Anxiety is a known risk factor and exacerbating factor in chronic pain syndromes, including TMD.[8] TTH frequently co-occurs with TMD, possibly reflecting shared central sensitization processes.[9]

This review aims to systematically evaluate evidence linking bruxism, sleep quality, anxiety disorders, and TTH in patients with TMD.

Sleep quality

Poor sleep quality was prevalent in 40–75% of TMD patients.[15161718] Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI) scores were significantly worse in TMD groups. Poor sleep correlated with higher pain intensity and disability [Table 1].[17]

Anxiety disorders

Anxiety prevalence ranged from 30% to 60%.[19202122] Higher anxiety levels were associated with greater pain catastrophizing and TMD symptom severity [Table 1].

Tension-type headache

TTH was reported in 25–65% of TMD patients.[23242526] Studies suggested overlapping mechanisms, including central sensitization and muscle hyperactivity [Table 1].

DISCUSSION

This review underscores significant overlap between TMD and bruxism, poor sleep quality, anxiety disorders, and TTH. Bruxism appears both a risk factor and a consequence of TMD-related pain. Sleep disturbances further exacerbate pain perception, possibly creating a vicious cycle. Anxiety plays a key role in symptom chronicity and treatment outcomes. TTH frequently coexists with TMD, reflecting shared pathophysiology.

Limitations include heterogeneity of diagnostic criteria, sample sizes, and reliance on self-reported data. Future longitudinal and interventional studies are needed to clarify causation and guide treatments.

CONCLUSION

Bruxism, poor sleep quality, anxiety disorders, and TTH are common comorbidities in TMD. Comprehensive biopsychosocial assessment and multidisciplinary treatment are recommended.

Unified Magnetoelectric Mechanism for Spin Splitting in Magnets
Carlos Mera Acosta

Abstract
We identify a relativistic magnetoelectric correction that unifies the understanding of spin splitting (SS) in ferromagnets, antiferromagnets, and altermagnets. Based on Dirac theory, we show that local electric multipoles coupled to magnetic moments govern SS across compensated magnets, leading to quadrupole-, dipole-, or monopole-driven regimes. Our model predicts momentum-dependent and momentum-independent SS, reconciling seemingly distinct phenomena such as altermagnetism and the spin Zeeman effect. This work establishes a general physical mechanism for SS beyond conventional Zeeman and Rashba paradigms.

In this Letter, we identify a relativistic correction that couples the local magnetization
\bm{m}_{n}
to the electric potential
\mathcal{V}n(\bm{r})
determined by the local symmetry of magnetic motifs.
In compensated magnets, this coupling scales with the difference in electric multipole distributions,
\mu_{\text{B}}(\mathcal{V}_{1}-\mathcal{V}_{2}),\bm{\sigma}\cdot\bm{m}

The emergence of SS is thus governed by the interplay between magnetic moment orientations, motif symmetries defining the local electric potential, and crystal-imposed motif connectivity.
We find that distinct types of SS—altermagnetic, spin Zeeman, and

\mathcal{V}_{n}

Moreover, the magnetoelectric coupling reveals a

Altogether, the magnetoelectric coupling provides a unified framework for understanding SS across compensated magnets, showing that these effects are manifestations of a single physical mechanism.

The magnetoelectric coupling:

The Dirac equation, in contrast to the non-relativistic Schrödinger framework, encodes spin from first principles, unveiling Zeeman- and SOC-induced SS as intrinsic relativistic phenomena
192 1928
.
Decomposing the four-component spinor into large (
\chi_{+}
) and small (
\chi_{-}
) components, the Dirac formalism yields the coupled equations
c\bm{\sigma}\cdot\bm{\pi}\chi_{\mp}=(E-\mathcal{V}(\bm{r})\mp mc^{2})\chi_{\pm}
, equivalently, for the large component (with
\mathcal{E}=E-mc^{2}
),

c^{2}\bm{\sigma}\cdot\bm{\pi}(2mc^{2}+\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{V}(\bm{r}))^{-1}\bm{\sigma}\cdot\bm{\pi}\chi_{+}=(\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{V}(\bm{r}))\chi_{+}.

(1)

The covariant momentum operator
\bm{\pi}=\bm{p}-e\bm{A}
accounts for minimal coupling to the magnetic vector potential
\bm{A}
associated with local magnetization, while
\mathcal{P}
-breaking is encoded in the electric potential
\mathcal{V}(\bm{r})=e\phi
.
the Dirac formalism can be simplified by expanding
(2mc^{2}+\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{V}(\bm{r}))^{-1}=\eta_{0}+\eta_{1}+\dots
, with
\eta_{0}=1/2mc^{2}
and
\eta_{1}=-\eta_{0}^{2}(\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{V}(\bm{r}))
.
The zero-order relativistic correction,
c^{2}(\bm{\sigma}\cdot\bm{\pi})\eta_{0}(\bm{\sigma}\cdot\bm{\pi})
, yields the Pauli equation
(\bm{\pi}^{2}/2m-\mu_{\text{B}}\bm{\sigma}\cdot\bm{m})\chi_{+}=(\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{V}(\bm{r}))\chi_{+}
, where the Zeeman term arises from the non-commutativity of the covariant momenta,
[\pi_{i},\pi_{j}]=-ie\hbar\epsilon_{ijk}m_{k}
.
The first-order correction,
c^{2}(\bm{\sigma}\cdot\bm{\pi})\eta_{1}(\bm{\sigma}\cdot\bm{\pi})
, includes:

i
) the conventional SOC,
c^{2}\eta_{0}^{2}\hbar\bm{\sigma}\cdot(\nabla\mathcal{V}(\bm{r})\times\bm{\pi})
;

ii
) a kinetic energy correction,
c^{2}\eta_{0}^{2}(\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{V}(\bm{r}))\bm{\pi}^{2}
;

iii
) the Darwin term,
ic^{2}\eta_{0}^{2}\hbar\nabla\mathcal{V}(\bm{r})\cdot\bm{\pi}
;
and
iv
) a term we identify as a magnetoelectrical relativistic correction,
\mathcal{H}_{\text{ME}}=\mu_{\text{B}}\eta_{0}(\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{V}(\bm{r}))\bm{\sigma}\cdot\bm{m}
.
Magnetic and electric relativistic corrections thus emerge at different orders (Zeeman at zeroth, SOC at first) often justifying independent treatments, yet overlooking the intrinsic magnetoelectric coupling unveiled here.

The magnetoelectric correction can be interpreted as an on-site term, where at each motif

;
Hayami and Kusunose 2018
;
Hayami et al. 2018

\mathcal{V}_{n}(\bm{r})=\lambda_{0}Q_{n}+\lambda_{1}\sum_{i}d_{ni}r_{i}+\lambda_{2}\sum_{ij}\mathcal{Q}_{nij}r_{i}r_{j}+\dots,

(2)

where
\lambda_{l}=(1/4\pi\varepsilon_{0})\langle r^{-(l+1)}\rangle

l

(

\bm{d}_{n}
(

Once the local motif symmetry is fixed, a crystal symmetry operation
\mathcal{U}
determines the magnetic motifs connectivity, i.e., in a unit cell with two motifs, their electric dipoles and quadrupoles relate via
\bm{d}_{2}=\mathcal{U}\bm{d}_{1}
and
\mathcal{Q}_{2}=\mathcal{U}\mathcal{Q}_{1}\mathcal{U}^{\dagger}

The resulting SS arises from the interplay between magnetic ordering, site symmetry, and motif connectivity.
For example, for antiparallel magnetic moments (
\bm{m}_{n}=(-1)^{n-1}\bm{m}

\mathcal{H}_{\text{ME}}=-\mu_{\text{B}}\eta_{0}(\mathcal{V}_{1}-\mathcal{V}_{2})\bm{\sigma}\cdot\bm{m}.

(3)

This spatial modulation of the electric potential provides a microscopic mechanism that substantiates the phenomenological models proposed by Rashba and Pekar

et al.

In the trivial case of a compensated CS magnet composed of two isotropic CS motifs, both dipole and quadrupole contributions vanish, resulting in no SS. For non-isotropic motifs in CS or NCS nonpolar environments, where electric dipoles are symmetry-forbidden, SS arises solely from differences in the quadrupole tensor. In contrast, when polar magnetic motifs are present in NCS or CS crystals and are connected by a crystal symmetry operation
\mathcal{U}
, the magnetoelectric coupling becomes

\mathcal{H}_{\text{ME}}=-\mu_{\text{B}}\eta_{0}\lambda_{1}\bm{\sigma}\cdot\bm{m}\sum_{i}\left(d_{1,i}-[\mathcal{U}d_{1}]_{i}\right)r_{i}.

(7)

Here, the operation
\mathcal{U}
may preserve, invert, or reorient the dipole direction, as classified in Table II.

In ferroelectric altermagnets, where local motifs are connected by crystal rotational symmetries aligned with their local polar axis (see Table II), the electric dipole is preserved across sites, causing the magnetoelectric contribution from dipoles to vanish.
Consequently, SS in these systems arises exclusively from electric quadrupoles.
This behavior contrasts sharply with ferroelectric Rashba systems
Acosta et al. 2020
;
Picozzi 2014
;
Rinaldi et al. 2018
, where spin polarization (SP) couples directly to electric dipoles and reverses upon polarization switching.
In ferroelectric altermagnets, by contrast, reversing the electric polarization does not necessarily invert the SP.
Indeed, as reported by Gu et. al.
Gu et al. 2025
, most such systems exhibit robust SP direction even under dipole reversal, highlighting the quadrupolar (not dipolar) origin of the SS.

In contrast, when motif connectivity enforces antiparallel alignment of local dipoles, as in antiferroelectric altermagnets
Duan et al. 2025
, the dipolar contributions add constructively in the magnetoelectric term, leading to a linear-in-
\bm{k}
SS consistent with the
spin Zeeman effect
proposed by Zhao
et. al.

Zhao et al. 2022

Here, motif connectivity can be mediated by inversion
\mathcal{P}
, a twofold rotation
R_{2}\parallel\bm{c}
, a mirror plane

S_{n}\parallel\bm{c}

For instance, when motifs are related by
\mathcal{P}
, the magnetoelectric is

\mathcal{H}_{\text{ME}}(\bm{k})=-2\mu_{\text{B}}\eta_{0}\lambda_{1}(\bm{\sigma}\cdot\bm{m})\,(\bm{d}\cdot\bm{k}),

(8)

while quadrupolar contributions vanish, placing such systems outside the altermagnetism category.
Notably, as discussed by Duan et. al.
Duan et al. 2025
, achieving controllable SS requires motif connectivity mediated by
R_{2}

In reorienting symmetry classes (see Table II), electric dipoles at different sites are canted relative to each other, generating a net electric polarization (third line in Figure 1).
This mechanism is only symmetry-allowed in NCS polar crystals.
In the magnetoelectric coupling, antiparallel (parallel) dipole components across magnetic motifs add constructively (destructively).
For instance, for dipoles
\bm{d}_{\pm}=(\pm d_{x},\pm d_{y},d_{z})

and

Thus, a net polarization, e.g., generated by an external electric field, can induce SS, enabling SP reversal as observed in a few reported cases

Physical mechanism leading to SS at the
\Gamma
point:

Having addressed quadrupolar and dipolar contributions, we now examine the SS arising from monopolar magnetoelectric term (fourth line in Figure 1).
Unlike dipole- or quadrupole-induced SS, which inherently depend on
\bm{k}
, the monopolar contribution yields a
k
-independent SS, allowing SS even at the high-symmetry
\Gamma
point.
In an electrically neutral unit cell, monopolar contributions from distinct motifs must globally cancel, i.e.,
Q_{n}=(-1)^{n-1}Q_{0}
(with
n=1,2
), leading to

\mathcal{H}_{\text{ME}}=-2\mu_{\text{B}}\eta_{0}\lambda_{0}Q_{0}\,\bm{\sigma}\cdot\bm{m},

(9)

a condition achievable by doping within motifs without altering the magnetic moments, e.g., MnSiSnN
4

Yuan et al. 2024
.

Remarkably, even in systems with zero net magnetization,
\mathcal{H}_{\text{ME}}
mirrors the functional form of the Zeeman SS, however, similar to the Zeeman effect, it fails to determine the energetic scale of the SS.
The magnetization generates a Zeeman splitting of only few meV, two orders of magnitude smaller than the observed exchange splitting in ferromagnets
Paul et al. 2023
;
Steeneken et al. 2002
;
Norden et al. 2019
.
Thus, while both conventional Zeeman effect and magnetoelectric terms correctly describe the SP and its symmetry properties, they substantially underestimate the splitting magnitude, requiring an effective exchange field far exceeding the magnetization itself. This holds not only for monopolar contributions, but also for higher-order multipoles in
\mathcal{V}_{n}
, which may coexist with charge disproportionation. As a result, spin splitting at
\Gamma
is typically accompanied by a
k
-dependent component
Yuan et al. 2024
. These conclusions naturally extend to ferromagnets, where all magnetic motifs align and multipolar contributions add constructively, i.e., the relevant term in ferromagnets scales with their
sum
.

For motifs with identical electric potentials
\mathcal{V}_{n}
and magnetic moments
\bm{m}_{n}=\bm{m}
, the magnetoelectric correction reads
\mathcal{H}_{\text{ME}}=-\mu_{\text{B}}\eta_{0}(\mathcal{V}_{1}+\mathcal{V}_{2})\bm{\sigma}\cdot\bm{m}
.
The physical implications differ strikingly from the compensated case:

i
) A nonzero monopolar term (
Q_{n}\neq 0
) enhances the
k
-independent SS at
\Gamma
, augmenting the conventional Zeeman term,
ii
) dipolar terms, aligned across motifs, induce a linear-in-
\bm{k}
SS even in the absence of
\mathcal{P}
-breaking, which add up constructively (destructively) for parallel (antiparallel) dipole components, and
iii
) quadrupolar terms, now additive, generate
k
-quadratic SS with enhanced anisotropy compared to the compensated case, particularly when motifs share lower symmetries with a single mirror, the SS is proportional to
(\bm{\sigma}\cdot\bm{m})\left[\mathcal{Q}_{x}(k^{2}_{x}+k^{2}_{y})-2\mathcal{Q}_{x}k_{z}^{2}\right]
.
The manipulation of the magnetic or dipole moments through external fields can thus potentially provide a SS control mechanism.

Conclusion:
This work uncovers a fundamental magnetoelectric mechanism governing SS across magnetic materials. We show that local electric multipoles coupled to magnetic moments drive distinct SS behaviors—quadratic, linear, and
k
-independent—across ferromagnets, compensated magnets, and altermagnets, even without spin-orbit coupling. Our findings not only reconcile previous symmetry-based approaches but also predict characteristic
k
-textures in ferroelectric and antiferroelectric magnets, opening new directions for engineering spin phenomena via local symmetry and electric ordering. This unified framework establishes magnetoelectric coupling as a key principle in spintronics beyond the traditional Zeeman and Rashba paradigms.

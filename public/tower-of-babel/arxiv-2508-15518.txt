Existential completions and Herbrand’s theorem This work has its origin in a question I asked Marco Abbadini at his talk at the Logic Colloquium 2025. I thank Marco for his stimulating talk as well as the conference organisers for the wonderful event. I acknowledge the support of Agence Nationale de la Recherche project ANR-23-CE48-0012-01.
Joshua L. Wrigley

Abstract
Recently, Abbadini and Guffanti gave an algebraic proof of Herbrand’s theorem using a completion for Lawvere doctrines that freely adds existential and universal quantifiers. A more direct argument can be given by only completing with respect to existential quantifiers. We construct the free existential completion on a presheaf of distributive lattices, and deduce Herbrand’s theorem for coherent logic from the explicit description. We also discuss the cases involving presheaves of meet-semilattices, due to Trotta, and presheaves of frames.

Introduction

Background

A classical result in proof theory, Herbrand’s theorem
[
14
]
, asserts that a
universal
first-order theory, i.e. one whose axioms avoid any use of existential quantification, can only admit ‘constructive proofs’ of existential statements. That is to say, if a universal (classical) theory
\mathbb{T}
proves that
\top\vdash\exists x.\,\varphi(x)
, where
\varphi(x)
is a quantifier-free formula, then there are a finite number of closed terms
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
that witness the formula
\varphi(x)
, i.e.
\mathbb{T}
proves
\top\vdash\varphi(t_{1})\lor\dots\lor\varphi(t_{n})
(in
[
4
, §2]
, this is called the
weak
form of Herbrand’s theorem, but it is the only version we will consider in this paper). Most textbook accounts of Herbrand’s theorem employ cut elimination (see
[
5
, §2.5.1]
for instance), a modern technique that was not available when Herbrand first proved the result.

In a recent work
[
1
]
, Abbadini and Guffanti give an algebraic proof of Herbrand’s theorem phrased in the language of
Lawvere doctrines
. Doctrines
à la
Lawvere, introduced in
[
21
,
22
]
, are a predicate generalisation of the familiar Lindenbaum-Tarski algebras from propositional logic, an alternative to the polyadic algebras of Halmos
[
12
]
and the cylindric algebras of Tarski
[
13
]
. A recurring topic within doctrine theory is the study of
completions
: universally adding richer structure to the doctrine. For example, the
quotient completion
of a doctrine has been extensively studied
[
24
,
25
,
26
,
27
,
10
]
, paralleling the category-theoretic exact completion
[
7
,
9
,
8
]
and the ‘tripos-to-topos’ construction found in
[
15
,
28
,
29
]
. Recently, more focus has been given to completions that leave the base category of a doctrine unchanged and instead add structure to the fibres: Trotta builds the
existential completion
in
[
31
]
while the
geometric completion
is considered in
[
6
,
32
,
33
]
.

Across a pair of papers
[
2
,
1
]
, Abbadini and Guffanti study the completion of a presheaf of Boolean algebras
P\colon\mathcal{C}^{\rm op}\to\mathbf{BA}
with respect to both universal and existential quantifiers. They were motivated to develop a doctrinal language in which to phrase problems of quantifier complexity, as sketched in a talk of Gehrke
[
11
]
. The quantifier completion
P^{\forall\exists}
of
P
, to arbitrary quantifier depth, is proven to exist via an abstract argument in
[
2
, Theorem 5.2]
. In their follow-up paper
[
1
]
, Abbadini and Guffanti study the completion up to quantifier depth 1, denoted by
P^{\forall\exists}_{1}
, i.e. the free addition of existential and universal formulae to the quantifier-free ones. To understand the structure of
P^{\forall\exists}_{1}
, they identify the formulae of quantifier depth 1 as a subdoctrine of
P^{\forall\exists}
via a semantic condition (their notion of a
universal ultrafilter

[
1
, Definition 3.3]
).

A careful analysis of the order relation in
P^{\forall\exists}_{1}
recovers Herbrand’s theorem
[
1
, Theorem 4.9]
. The idea is the following: the datum of a universal theory
\mathbb{T}
is essentially contained in a presheaf of Boolean algebras – the doctrine of quantifier-free formulae – and so its free completion is the doctrine of formulae of quantifier depth 1 modulo
\mathbb{T}
-provability. The order relation in the completed doctrine thus describes, algebraically, the statement of Herbrand’s theorem.

Our contribution

The purpose of this paper is to point out that a more direct doctrinal proof of Herbrand’s theorem is possible by only completing with respect to existential quantifiers, which in comparison is less involved than the completion with respect to both quantifiers constructed in
[
1
]
. The first observation to make is that a proof of Herbrand’s theorem for
Horn logic
is implicit in the construction of Trotta’s existential completion of a primary doctrine
[
31
]
. In
[
33
, Lemma IV.38]
, it is shown how Trotta’s existential completion can be carved out of the topos-theoretic
geometric completion
found in
[
32
,
6
,
33
]
, similar to how the exact completion of a regular category is constructed in
[
20
]
. In a similar fashion (Remark
2
), the geometric completion instructs us on how to modify Trotta’s construction in order to obtain the two novel completions presented in this paper:

(i)

the free existential completion on a doctrine interpreting
\{\land,\lor,=\}
,

(ii)

and the free existential completion on a doctrine interpreting
\{\land,\bigvee,=\}
.

As a result of the explicit description of these completions we can deduce versions of Herbrand’s theorem for coherent logic and geometric logic, with the former subsuming the classical version of Herbrand’s theorem (see Remark
6
). In contrast, it is not clear how the methods of
[
1
]
generalise beyond the classical setting.

Overview

We proceed as follows:

(1)

In
Section

1
, we recall the theory of Lawvere doctrines and how this acts as an algebraic framework for studying first-order theories, including how existential quantification and the equality predicate are interpreted algebraically.

(2)

In
Section

2
, we first recall the construction of Trotta’s existential completion for presheaves of meet-semilattices, i.e. doctrines interpreting only (finite) conjunction, and that this completion respects the interpretation of equality. Next, we construct the existential completion for presheaves of distributive lattices, and then for presheaves of frames. For the sake of narrative clarity, and because the proofs closely mirror those in
[
31
]
, we initially state the universal properties of our existential completions as fact. We include explicit proofs of the necessary properties in
Section

4
.

(3)

In
Section

3
, we explain how the explicit descriptions of the existential completions described in the previous section yield versions of Herbrand’s theorem for Horn, coherent, classical and geometric logic.

(4)

Finally, in
Section

4
, we give explicit proofs that our construction does in fact yield the existential completion on a presheaf of distributive lattices. Explicit proofs that our construction describes the existential completion on a presheaf of frames can be obtained by easily modifying our argument.

1
Doctrines

Lawvere doctrines, as introduced in
[
21
,
22
]
, come in many different flavours depending on the fragment of logic they are intended to interpret. We will discuss exclusively fragments of
geometric
logic, which include
coherent
logic (also called
positive
logic, for instance, in
[
3
]
) and therefore subsumes classical logic (
[
16
, Lemma D1.5.13]
). Recall that in geometric logic and its fragments, we use a Gentzen-style sequent calculus, where a sequent
\varphi(\vec{x})\vdash_{\vec{x}}\psi(\vec{x})
expresses “for all
\vec{x}
, if
\varphi(\vec{x})
then
\psi(\vec{x})
”. For an introduction to the syntax of geometric logic, the reader is directed to
[
16
, §D1]
.

Definition 1
.

By a
doctrine
unqualified, we mean a presheaf of posets
P\colon\mathcal{C}^{\rm op}\to\mathbf{Pos}
. Given an arrow
f\colon c^{\prime}\to c
in
\mathcal{C}
, we will write
f^{\ast}\colon P(c)\to P(c^{\prime})
rather than
P(f)
.

(i)

A
\{\land\}
-
doctrine
is a functor
P\colon\mathcal{C}^{\rm op}\to\mathbf{MLat}
, where
\mathcal{C}
is a category with finite products and
\mathbf{MLat}
denotes the category of meet-semilattices (i.e. posets with a top element and binary joins). In
[
24
,
31
]
, these are called
primary doctrines
. The elements
x\in P(c)
should be thought of as “predicates in context”, and so in a
\{\land\}
-doctrine we can interpret conjunctions of predicates.

(ii)

A
\{\land,\lor\}
-
doctrine
is a
\{\land\}
-doctrine that factors through the subcategory of distributive lattices
\mathbf{DLat}\subseteq\mathbf{MLat}
, allowing for disjunctions of predicates.

(iii)

A
\{\land,\bigvee\}
-
doctrine
is a
\{\land\}
-doctrine that factors through the subcategory of frames
\mathbf{Frm}\subseteq\mathbf{MLat}
.

Remark 1
.

Note that, in some other places (e.g.
[
32
,
33
]
), the term
primary doctrine
assumes all finite limits on the
base
category
\mathcal{C}
. This is often a convenient assumption, especially when working with the Grothendieck construction of a doctrine (cf. Remark
2
), but it won’t be needed for the constructions in this paper. (It is possible to avoid assuming any limits in the base category, even products, by employing
relative
definitions, see
[
6
, §5]
or
[
34
, §3]
).

So far, the doctrines described above only interpret the logical connectives that were already present in propositional logic. This is why, for example, a
\{\land,\lor\}
-doctrine is merely a functor that takes values in the category giving algebraic semantics for coherent propositional logic, i.e.
\mathbf{DLat}
. The key insight of Lawvere in
[
21
]
was that first-order logical connectives can be captured by structure
between
the fibres.

Example 1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a lex category (i.e. a category with all finite limits), then taking the subobject functor yields a
\{\land,=\}
-doctrine

\mathrm{Sub}_{\mathcal{C}}\colon\mathcal{C}^{\rm op}\to\mathbf{MLat}

where the equality predicate at an object

is given by the diagonal subobject

is a

\mathcal{C}

Example 2

(The syntactic doctrine of a first-order theory)
.

Let
\mathbb{T}
be first-order theory over a signature
\Sigma
. By
\mathbf{Term}_{\Sigma}
we denote the category of
terms
over
\Sigma
, i.e. the category where:

(i)

the objects of
\mathbf{Term}_{\Sigma}
are finite contexts
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
of variables,

(ii)

and the morphisms
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\to(y_{1},\dots,y_{m})
are tuples of terms with free variables
(t_{1}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}),\dots,t_{m}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}))
, where the term
t_{i}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
is of the same sort as
y_{i}
. The composition of morphisms is given by substitution of terms.

Note that the category
\mathbf{Term}_{\Sigma}
has finite products: the terminal object is given by the empty context
\emptyset
, while the product is
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\times(y_{1},\dots,y_{m})=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},\dots,y_{m})
.

We define a
\{\land,=\}
-doctrine
F_{\{{\land}\}}({\mathbb{T}})\colon\mathbf{Term}_{\Sigma}\to\mathbf{MLat}
by taking
F_{\{{\land,=}\}}({\mathbb{T}})(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
to be the poset whose elements are
\mathbb{T}
-provable equivalence classes of
\{\land,=\}
-
formulae
, or
Horn formulae
, in context
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
, i.e. the formulae of the form
\chi_{1}\land\dots\land\chi_{n}
where each
\chi_{i}
is either:

(i)

simply
truth

\top
,

(ii)

an instance of equality
s=t
, where
s
and
t
are both terms over
\Sigma
of the same sort with free variables
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
,

(iii)

or an instance of a relation
R(t_{1},\dots,t_{k})
, where
R
is a relation symbol in
\Sigma
and each
t_{\ell}
is a term (of the appropriate sort) with free variables
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
.

We order these formulae by provability modulo the theory
\mathbb{T}
, i.e. if
\mathbb{T}
proves the sequent
\psi\vdash_{\vec{x}}\varphi
then
\psi\leqslant\varphi
. For a morphism
(t_{1},\dots,t_{m})\colon(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\to(y_{1},\dots,y_{m})
in
\mathbf{Term}_{\Sigma}
, the transition map

F_{\{{\land,=}\}}({\mathbb{T}})(t_{1},\dots,t_{m})\colon F_{\{{\land,=}\}}({\mathbb{T}})(y_{1},\dots,y_{m})\to F_{\{{\land,=}\}}({\mathbb{T}})(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})

acts by sending a
\{\land,=\}
-formula
\varphi(\vec{y})
to the result
\varphi(\vec{t}/\vec{y})
of substituting
t_{i}(\vec{x})
for
y_{i}
(since contexts are assumed to be disjoint, there are no problems with simultaneous substitution). The equality predicate, at a context
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
, is unsurprisingly given by

(x_{1}=x^{\prime}_{1})\land\dots\land(x_{n}=x^{\prime}_{n})\in F_{\{{\land,=}\}}({\mathbb{T}})(x_{1},\dots,x_{n},x^{\prime}_{1},\dots,x^{\prime}_{n}).

If we also include formulae constructed using finitary disjunction
\lor
or existential quantification
\exists
, we obtain a
\{\land,\lor,=\}
-doctrine that we denote
F_{\{{\land,\lor,=}\}}({\mathbb{T}})
(respectively, a
\{\exists,\land,=\}
-doctrine
F_{\{{\exists,\land,=}\}}({\mathbb{T}})
), and similarly for infinitary disjunction. More details can be found in
[
30
]
.

If
\mathbb{T}
is a propositional coherent theory, i.e. the signature
\Sigma
has no sorts, then
\mathbf{Term}_{\Sigma}
is the one-point category
\ast
and
F_{\{{\land,\lor}\}}({\mathbb{T}})(\ast)
is the usual Lindenbaum-Tarski algebra of coherent formulae for
\mathbb{T}
.

3
Herbrand-style theorems

Thus, we arrive at the culmination of our narrative: versions of Herbrand’s theorem for Horn, coherent, classical and geometric logic. As a particular case of Example
4
, given a
universal
theory
\mathbb{T}
(as defined below), the free existential completion on the syntactic doctrine of quantifier-free formulae associated with
\mathbb{T}
is the syntactic doctrine of
existential
formulae associated with
\mathbb{T}
. Herbrand-style theorems can therefore be deduced by simply reading off the definition of the order relation in the appropriate existential completion.

Definition 4
.

We distinguish between the following kinds of theories:

(i)

A theory
\mathbb{T}
is a
universal Horn
theory if, for each axiom
\varphi\vdash_{\vec{x}}\psi
of
\mathbb{T}
, both
\varphi
and
\psi
are quantifier-free
Horn formulae
, i.e. they are constructed using the connectives
\{\land,=\}
.

(ii)

A theory
\mathbb{T}
is a
universal coherent
theory if, for each axiom
\varphi\vdash_{\vec{x}}\psi
of
\mathbb{T}
, both
\varphi
and
\psi
are quantifier-free
coherent formulae
, i.e. they are constructed using the connectives
\{\land,\lor,=\}
.

(iii)

A theory
\mathbb{T}
is a
universal geometric
theory if, for each axiom
\varphi\vdash_{\vec{x}}\psi
of
\mathbb{T}
, both
\varphi
and
\psi
are quantifier-free
geometric formulae
, i.e. they are constructed using the connectives
\{\land,\bigvee,=\}
.

Corollary 1

(Herbrand
[
14
]
)
.

(i)

If
\mathbb{T}
is a universal Horn theory that proves
\top\vdash\exists x.\,\varphi(x)
where
\varphi(x)
is a quantifier-free Horn formula, then there is a closed term
t
such that
\mathbb{T}
proves
\top\vdash\varphi(t)
.

(ii)

If
\mathbb{T}
is a universal coherent theory that proves
\top\vdash\exists x.\,\varphi(x)
where
\varphi(x)
is a quantifier-free coherent formula, then there is a finite set of closed terms
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
such that
\mathbb{T}
proves
\top\vdash\varphi(t_{1})\lor\dots\lor\varphi(t_{n})
.

(iii)

If
\mathbb{T}
is a universal geometric theory that proves
\top\vdash\exists x.\,\varphi(x)
where
\varphi(x)
is a quantifier-free geometric formula, then there is a set of closed terms
\{{t_{i}}\,|\,{i\in I}\}
such that
\mathbb{T}
proves
\top\vdash\bigvee_{i\in I}\varphi(t_{i})
.

Proof.

We spell out the details for a universal coherent theory
\mathbb{T}
, the other cases being analogous. As
\mathbb{T}
is a universal theory, it is equivalent to its theory of universal consequences. Thus, by Example
4
, we have that
F_{\{{\land,\lor,=}\}}({\mathbb{T}})^{\exists}\cong F_{\{{\exists,\land,\lor,=}\}}({\mathbb{T}})
. Under this isomorphism, the quantifier free formula
\varphi(x)\in F_{\{{\exists,\land,\lor}\}}({\mathbb{T}})
is identified with
\eta_{x}(\varphi(x))=\{(\emptyset,\varphi(x))\}\in F_{\{{\land,\lor,=}\}}({\mathbb{T}})^{\exists}
(recall that
\emptyset
describes the terminal object of
\mathbf{Term}_{\Sigma}
). Therefore,
\mathbb{T}
proves the sequent
\top\vdash\exists x.\,\varphi(x)
if and only if there is an inequality

\eta_{\emptyset}(\top)=\{(\emptyset,\top)\}\leqslant\Sigma_{x}\{(\emptyset,\varphi(x))\}=\{(x,\varphi(x))\}

(note that
\{(\emptyset,\top)\}
describes the top element of
F_{\{{\land,\lor,=}\}}({\mathbb{T}})^{\exists}(\emptyset)
, see Lemma
1
). Recall from
Section

2.2
that this inequality holds if and only if there is a finite family of arrows
\{{t_{i}\colon\emptyset\to x}\,|\,{i\leqslant n}\}
, i.e. a finite set of closed terms, such that

\top\vdash t_{1}^{\ast}\varphi(x)\lor\dots\lor t_{n}^{\ast}\varphi(x)=\varphi(t_{1})\lor\dots\lor\varphi(t_{n}).

∎

Remark 5
.

Of course, Corollary
1
only describes the simplest instance of Herbrand’s theorem where we consider a sequent of the form
\top\vdash\exists x.\,\varphi(x)
. As it is sufficiently clear how to calculate the statement from the description of the existential completion, we leave it as an exercise to the reader to write out explicitly what it means for a coherent theory to prove a more complicated sequent such as
\varphi(\vec{x})\land\exists\vec{y}.\,\psi(\vec{x},\vec{y})\vdash_{\vec{x}}\chi(\vec{x})\land\exists\vec{z}.\,\xi(\vec{x},\vec{z})
, for
\varphi,\psi,\chi,\xi
quantifier-free coherent formulae.

Remark 6

(Herbrand’s theorem for classical logic)
.

Not much must be changed if we wish to incorporate classical logic into our doctrinal account of Herbrand’s theorem. First note that in classical first-order logic the sequent
\varphi\vdash_{\vec{x}}\psi
is equivalent to the sequent
\top\vdash_{\vec{x}}\neg\varphi\lor\psi
, and so we can use a Hilbert-style calculus if we prefer. By a
universal classical
theory
\mathbb{T}
we mean a theory of classical first-order logic whose axioms are all of the form
\top\vdash_{\vec{x}}\varphi
where
\varphi
is a quantifier-free classical formula, i.e. constructed using the connectives
\{\land,\lor,=,\neg\}
. Let
F_{\{{\land,\lor,=,\neg}\}}({\mathbb{T}})\colon\mathbf{Term}_{\Sigma}\to\mathbf{BA}\subseteq\mathbf{DLat}
denote the doctrine of quantifier-free classical formulae modulo
\mathbb{T}
-provability, taking values in the subcategory
\mathbf{BA}
of Boolean algebras and their homomorphisms. By an argument identical to that in Example
4
, the existential completion
F_{\{{\land,\lor,=,\neg}\}}({\mathbb{T}})^{\exists}
is the doctrine of
existential formulae
, i.e. formulae of the form
\exists\vec{x}.\,\varphi(\vec{x},\vec{y})
where
\varphi(\vec{x},\vec{y})
is a quantifier-free classical formula, ordered by
\mathbb{T}
-provability. Thus, just as in Corollary
1
, a universal classical theory
\mathbb{T}
proves
\top\vdash\exists x.\,\varphi(x)
, where
\varphi(x)
is a quantifier-free classical formula, if and only if there is a finite set of closed terms
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
such that
\mathbb{T}
proves
\top\vdash\varphi(t_{1})\lor\dots\lor\varphi(t_{n})
.

Alternatively, we could substitute the universal classical theory
\mathbb{T}
with an equivalent universal coherent theory
\mathbb{T}^{\prime}
by replacing each instance of a negated (quantifier-free) coherent formula
\varphi(\vec{x})
in the axiomatisation of
\mathbb{T}
with a new relation symbol
(\neg\varphi)(\vec{x})
satisfying the sequents
\varphi(\vec{x})\land(\neg\varphi)(\vec{x})\vdash_{\vec{x}}\bot
and
\top\vdash_{\vec{x}}\varphi(\vec{x})\lor(\neg\varphi)(\vec{x})
(cf. the
Morleyisation
of a classical first-order theory
[
16
, Lemma D1.5.13]
), to which we can directly apply the statement of Corollary
1
.

By manipulating our sequents using classical first-order logic, we are not restricted to statements involving only existential quantification. For example, we might ask (as the opening of
[
1
]
does) when a universal classical theory
\mathbb{T}
proves
\forall x.\,\varphi(x)\vdash\forall y.\,\psi(y)
where
\varphi
and
\psi
are quantifier-free classical formulae. Using classical logic, we can mutate the sequent
\forall x.\,\varphi(x)\vdash\forall y.\,\psi(y)
into a form which only mentions existential quantification and to which we can apply the existential completion of
Section

2.2
, namely we have that the following sequents are provably equivalent:

\displaystyle\forall x.\,\varphi(x)

\displaystyle\vdash\forall y.\,\psi(y)

\displaystyle\iff\top

\displaystyle\vdash\neg\forall x.\,\varphi(x)\lor\forall y.\,\psi(y),

\displaystyle\iff\top

\displaystyle\vdash\exists x.\,\neg\varphi(x)\lor\forall y.\,\psi(y),

\displaystyle\iff\top

\displaystyle\vdash\forall y.\,(\exists x.\,\neg\varphi(x)\lor\psi(y)),

\displaystyle\iff\top

\displaystyle\vdash_{y}\exists x.\,\neg\varphi(x)\lor\psi(y),

where we have used that
\forall x.\,\varphi(x)\equiv\neg\exists x.\,\neg\varphi(x)
and the ‘dual’ of the Frobenius condition for universal quantification, which is valid in classical logic (see
[
16
, Remark D1.3.9]
). Therefore, just as in Corollary
1
, the universal theory
\mathbb{T}
proves the sequent
\top\vdash_{y}\exists x.\,\neg\varphi(x)\lor\psi(y)
if and only if there is an inequality

\displaystyle\eta_{y}(\top)=\{(\emptyset,\top)\}

\displaystyle\leqslant\Sigma_{x}\pi_{y}^{\ast}\eta_{x}(\neg\varphi(x))\lor\eta_{y}(\psi(y))=\Sigma_{x}\eta_{x,y}(\neg\varphi(x))\lor\eta_{y}(\psi(y))

\displaystyle=\{(x,\neg\varphi(x)),(\emptyset,\psi(y))\}

in the poset
F_{\{{\land,\lor,=,\neg}\}}({\mathbb{T}})^{\exists}(y)
, which we recall occurs if and only if there is a finite set of arrows
t_{1}\colon y\to x\times y,\dots,t_{n}\colon y\to x\times y
in
\mathbf{Term}_{\Sigma}
satisfying the commutativity condition
\pi_{x}\circ t_{i}=1_{y}
, in other words a finite set of terms
t_{1}(y),\dots,t_{n}(y)
of type
x
with free variable
y
, for which
\mathbb{T}
proves the sequent
\top\vdash_{y}\neg\varphi(t_{1}(y))\lor\dots\lor\neg\varphi(t_{n}(y))\lor\psi(y)
. Once again, we can use classical logic to manipulate the former sequent and deduce that the following are provably equivalent:

\displaystyle\top

\displaystyle\vdash_{y}\neg\varphi(t_{1}(y))\lor\dots\lor\neg\varphi(t_{n}(y))\lor\psi(y)

\displaystyle\iff\neg(\neg\varphi(t_{1}(y))\lor\dots\lor\neg\varphi(t_{n}(y)))

\displaystyle\vdash_{y}\psi(y),

\displaystyle\iff\quad\quad\ \ \,\varphi(t_{1}(y))\land\dots\land\varphi(t_{n}(y))

\displaystyle\vdash_{y}\psi(y).

Hence, we conclude that
\mathbb{T}
proves
\forall x.\,\varphi(x)\vdash\forall y.\,\psi(y)
if and only if there are a finite set of terms
t_{1}(y),\dots,t_{n}(y)
such that
\mathbb{T}
proves
\varphi(t_{1}(y))\land\dots\land\varphi(t_{n}(y))\vdash_{y}\psi(y)
(cf. Theorem 4.7
[
1
]
).

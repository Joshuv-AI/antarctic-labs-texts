Caputo fractional stochastic differential equations: Lipschitz continuity in the fractional order
Ta Cong Son, Nguyen Tien Dung ∗, Pham Thi Phuong Thuy, Tran Manh Cuong ∗, Hoang Thi Phuong Thao ∗, Pham Dinh Tung ∗

Abstract
In this paper, we consider a class of the Caputo fractional stochastic differential equations of fractional order α ∈ ( 1 2 , 1 ] \alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},1] . Our aim is to analyze of the continuous dependence of solutions on the fractional order α . \alpha. We first provide explicit estimates for the rate of weak convergence the solutions. We then describe the exact asymptotic behavior of this convergence to show that the rate is optimal.



Keywords:
Caputo fractional derivative, Stochastic differential equation, Malliavin calculus.

2020 Mathematics Subject Classification:
26A33, 60J70, 60H07.

1
Introduction

In recent decades, the fractional calculus and fractional stochastic differential equations have attracted great attention of researchers due to their coverage for a great variety of applications in the real world such as dynamics of complex systems in engineering, fluid mechanics as well as financial and biological models, and so on. By using different definitions of fractional operators (such as Caputo
derivative, Caputo-Fabrizio derivative, Riemann-Liouville derivative and integral, Atangana-Baleanu derivative, etc), many types of fractional stochastic differential equations have been introduced. We refer the reader to monographs
[
6
,
9
,
12
]
for more details. In this paper, we consider Caputo fractional stochastic differential equations of the form

\displaystyle D_{0^{+}}^{\alpha}X_{\alpha,t}=b(t,X_{\alpha,t})+\sigma(t,X_{\alpha,t})\frac{dB_{t}}{dt},\,\,0\leq t\leq T,

(1.1)

where the fractional order
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},1],

B=(B_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
is a standard Brownian motion and
b,\sigma:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
are measurable functions. Here we write
X_{\alpha,t}
to stress the dependence of solutions on
\alpha.
Note that Caputo fractional operator
D_{0^{+}}^{\alpha}
is non-local, which strongly makes the equation (
1.1
) suitable and
efficient to describe the long memory or non-local effects characterizing most physical phenomena, see
[
7
,
15
,
17
]
and the references therein.

By its definition, a stochastic process
X_{\alpha}=(X_{\alpha,t})_{t\in[0,T]}
is called a solution of the equation (
1.1
) with the initial condition
X_{\alpha,0}=x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
if it satisfies the following equation

X_{\alpha,t}=x_{0}+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\left(\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}b(s,X_{\alpha,s})ds+\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\sigma(s,X_{\alpha,s})dB_{s}\right),\,0\leq t\leq T,

(1.2)

where the second integral is interpreted as an Itô stochastic integral and
\Gamma(\alpha)=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha-1}e^{-x}dx
is the Gamma function.

In the last years, several fundamental properties of the solutions to the equation (
1.2
) have been studied by various authors, see e.g.
[
10
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
,
22
]
. We note that the equation (
1.1
) belongs to the class of stochastic Volterra equations with singular kernels. For this class, the convergence of solutions with respect to the kernels plays an important role in many applications. We refer the reader to
[
3
,
2
,
1
,
4
]
and references therein for several fruitful results. In this research theme, the results obtained for the equation (
1.1
) can be summarized as follows: Given
\beta\in(\frac{1}{2},1],
let
(X_{\beta,t})_{t\in[0,T]}
solve the following equation

X_{\beta,t}=x_{0}+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\left(\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\beta-1}b(s,X_{\beta,s})ds+\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\beta-1}\sigma(s,X_{\beta,s})dB_{s}\right),\,0\leq t\leq T.

(1.3)

In
[
11
,
23
]
, authors proved that

E|X_{\alpha,t}-X_{\beta,t}|^{p}\to 0\ \ \mbox{ as}\ \alpha\to\beta

(1.4)

for every
0\leq t\leq T
and
p\geq 2.
Furthermore, by using Theorem 3.1 in recent paper
[
4
]
, one can get the following estimate for the rate of convergence

E|X_{\alpha,t}-X_{\beta,t}|^{2}\leq Ct^{2(\alpha\wedge\beta)-1}(|\ln t|^{2}+1)|\alpha-\beta|^{2}\,\,\,\forall\,\alpha,\beta\in(\frac{1}{2},1],

(1.5)

where
C
is a positive constant.

Let us recall that, in the literature,
L^{p}
-results of the forms (
1.4
) and (
1.5
) are the so-called the strong convergence of
X_{\alpha,t}
to
X_{\beta,t}
as
\alpha\to\beta.
On the other hand, from practical point of view, weak convergence results are very useful and have been widely investigated, see e.g.
[
16
]
. The main task is to obtain an explicit estimate for the quantity
|Eg(X_{\alpha,t})-Eg(X_{\beta,t})|,
where
g
belongs to a suitable class of test functions. When
g
is a Lipschitz continuous function, the problem is trivial. Indeed, it follows from (
1.5
) that

|Eg(X_{\alpha,t})-Eg(X_{\beta,t})|\leq CE|X_{\alpha,t}-X_{\beta,t}|\leq C|\alpha-\beta|\,\,\,\forall\,\alpha,\beta\in(\frac{1}{2},1].

The non-trivial case is when the test function
g
is only measurable and bounded. In the present paper, our aim is to handle this non-trivial case. Our tactics is to use a general result established in our recent paper
[
8
]
by means of Malliavin calculus, see Lemma
2.5
below. We obtain the following estimate

|Eg(X_{\alpha,t})-Eg(X_{\beta,t})|\leq C\|g\|_{\infty}t^{\alpha\wedge\beta-\beta}(|\ln t|+1)|\alpha-\beta|\,\,\,\forall\,\alpha\in\big(\frac{1}{2},1],\beta\in[\frac{7}{8},1],

(1.6)

where
\|.\|_{\infty}
denotes the supremum norm. We also prove the optimality of the estimate (
1.6
) as
\alpha\to\beta
by showing that the limit
\lim\limits_{\alpha\to\beta}\frac{Eg(X_{\alpha,t})-Eg(X_{\beta,t})}{\alpha-\beta}
exists and can be computed explicitly.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we provide some useful estimates and recall some concepts of Malliavin calculus. In Section
3
, we revisit the strong convergence results. We point out, in Theorem
3.1
below, that the limit
Y_{\beta,t}:=\lim\limits_{\alpha\to\beta}\frac{X_{\alpha,t}-X_{\beta,t}}{\alpha-\beta}
exists and can be computed explicitly. Our main results are then stated and proved in Section
4
. We provide optimal estimates for the rate of weak convergence in Theorem
4.1
.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Some useful estimates

In this subsection, we collect some fundamental integral estimates and provide a Gronwall-type lemma for singular kernels.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\Gamma(\alpha)=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha-1}e^{-x}dx,\alpha>0
be the Gamma function. Then

(i)
there exits a positive constant
C
such that
|\Gamma^{\prime}(\alpha)|+|\Gamma^{\prime\prime}(\alpha)|\leq C
for all
\frac{1}{2}<\alpha\leq 1,

(ii)
there exists
\alpha_{\ast}\in(1,2)
such that
\Gamma(\alpha)
is decreasing in
(0,\alpha_{\ast})
and increasing in
(\alpha_{\ast},\infty).

Proof.

(i)
It is easy to get

\Gamma^{\prime}(\alpha)=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha-1}e^{-x}\ln{x}dx\ \ \mbox{and }\ \ \Gamma^{\prime\prime}(\alpha)=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha-1}e^{-x}\ln^{2}{x}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle|\Gamma^{\prime}(\alpha)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha-1}e^{-x}|\ln{x}|dx=\int_{0}^{1}x^{\alpha-1}e^{-x}|\ln{x}|dx+\int_{1}^{\infty}x^{\alpha-1}e^{-x}|\ln{x}|dx

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{0}^{1}x^{-1/2}\ln{x}dx+\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-x}\ln{x}dx=4+\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-x}\ln{x}dx.

and

\displaystyle|\Gamma^{\prime\prime}(\alpha)|

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}x^{\alpha-1}e^{-x}\ln^{2}{x}dx+\int_{1}^{\infty}x^{\alpha-1}e^{-x}\ln^{2}{x}dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}x^{-1/2}\ln^{2}{x}dx+\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-x}\ln^{2}{x}dx

\displaystyle=16+\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-x}\ln^{2}{x}dx.

So it holds that

|\Gamma^{\prime}(\alpha)|+|\Gamma^{\prime\prime}(\alpha)|\leq 20+\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-x}\ln{x}dx+\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-x}\ln^{2}{x}dx=:C<\infty.

(ii)
We observe that
\Gamma(1)=1=\Gamma(2).
Then there exists
\alpha_{\ast}\in(1,2)
such that
\Gamma^{\prime}(\alpha_{\ast})=0.
On the other hand,
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}(\alpha)>0\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \alpha\in(0,\infty).

So
\Gamma(\alpha)
is decreasing in
(0,\alpha_{\ast})
and increasing in
(\alpha_{\ast},\infty).

∎

Lemma 2.2
.

For all
\alpha,\beta\in\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right]
. There exists a constant
C>0
such that

\left|\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\right|\leq C|\alpha-\beta|.

(2.1)

Proof.

Since
\Gamma(\beta),\Gamma(\alpha)\geq\Gamma(1)=1
for
\alpha,\beta\in\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right],
we have

\bigg|\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\bigg|\leq|\Gamma(\beta)-\Gamma(\alpha)|.

So, using the Lagrange theorem, there exists
\xi
lying between
\alpha
and
\beta
such that

\bigg|\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\bigg|\leq|\Gamma^{\prime}(\xi)(\alpha-\beta)|,

and hence, by the part
(i)
of Lemma
2.1
, we get

\bigg|\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\bigg|\leq C|\alpha-\beta|.

The proof of the lemma is complete.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\frac{1}{2}<\alpha_{0}<1.
For all
\alpha,\beta\in[\alpha_{0},1]
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left((t-s)^{\alpha-1}-(t-s)^{\beta-1}\right)^{2}ds\leq C(t^{2\alpha-1}+t^{2\beta-1})(\ln^{2}t+1)(\alpha-\beta)^{2}

(2.2)

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left((t-s)^{\alpha-1}-(t-s)^{\beta-1}\right)^{2}\ln^{2}(t-s)ds\leq C(t^{2\alpha-1}+t^{2\beta-1})(\ln^{4}t+1)(\alpha-\beta)^{2},

(2.3)

where
C
is a positive constant depending only on
\alpha_{0}.

Proof.

Put
H_{z}(t,s):=(t-s)^{z-1}
. We have

\frac{\partial H_{z}(t,s)}{\partial z}=(t-s)^{z-1}\ln(t-s).

By using the Lagrange theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle H_{\beta}(t,s)-H_{\alpha}(t,s)

\displaystyle=(\beta-\alpha)(t-s)^{\gamma-1}\ln(t-s)

where
\gamma
is between
\alpha
and
\beta
. Hence,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\big(

\displaystyle(t-s)^{\alpha-1}-(t-s)^{\beta-1}\big)^{2}ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\left(H_{\beta}(t,s)-H_{\alpha}(t,s)\right)^{2}ds=(\alpha-\beta)^{2}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{2\gamma-2}\ln^{2}(t-s)ds

\displaystyle\leq(\alpha-\beta)^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left((t-s)^{2\alpha-2}+(t-s)^{2\beta-2}\right)\ln^{2}(t-s)ds

\displaystyle=(\alpha-\beta)^{2}\left(t^{2\alpha-1}\int_{0}^{1}v^{2\alpha-2}(\ln t+\ln v)^{2}dv+t^{2\beta-1}\int_{0}^{1}v^{2\beta-2}(\ln t+\ln v)^{2}dv\right)

\displaystyle\leq(\alpha-\beta)^{2}\left(t^{2\alpha-1}+t^{2\beta-1}\right)\int_{0}^{1}v^{2\alpha_{0}-2}(\ln t+\ln v)^{2}dv,

which give us the required estimate (
2.2
). The proof of (
2.3
) can be done similarly. So we omit it.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
v:[0,T]\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be a real function and
\omega(.)
is a non-negative continuous function on
[0,T]
and there are constants
a>0
and
0<\eta<1
such that

v(t)\leq\omega(t)+a\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\eta-1}v(s)ds,\,\,0\leq t\leq T.

Then, we have

v(t)\leq 2\omega(t)+\frac{8at^{\frac{\eta}{2}}}{\eta}\exp\left(\frac{4^{\frac{2}{\eta}}a^{\frac{2}{\eta}}t^{2}}{\eta^{\frac{2}{\eta}}}\right)\left(\int_{0}^{t}\omega^{\frac{2}{\eta}}(s)ds\right)^{\frac{\eta}{2}},\,\,0\leq t\leq T.

(2.4)

If, in addition, the function
w
is non-decreasing, then

v(t)\leq 2\omega(t)\exp\left(\frac{4^{\frac{2}{\eta}}a^{\frac{2}{\eta}}t^{2}}{\eta^{\frac{2}{\eta}}}\right),\,\,0\leq t\leq T.

(2.5)

Proof.

For any
q>\frac{1}{\eta},
by using Hölder’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle v^{q}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\omega(t)+a\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\eta-1}v(s)ds\right)^{q}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{q-1}\omega^{q}(t)+2^{p-1}a^{q}\left(\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\eta-1}v(s)ds\right)^{q}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{q-1}\omega^{q}(t)+2^{p-1}a^{q}\left(\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\frac{q(\eta-1)}{q-1}}ds\right)^{q-1}\int_{0}^{t}v^{q}(s)ds

\displaystyle=2^{q-1}\omega^{q}(t)+2^{q-1}a^{q}\left(\frac{q-1}{q\eta-1}\right)^{q-1}t^{q\eta-1}\int_{0}^{t}v^{q}(s)ds,\,\,0\leq t\leq T.

Fixed
t\in[0,T],
we have

v^{q}(x)\leq 2^{q-1}\omega^{q}(x)+2^{q-1}a^{q}\left(\frac{q-1}{q\eta-1}\right)^{q-1}t^{q\eta-1}\int_{0}^{x}v^{q}(s)ds,\,\,0\leq x\leq t.

So, by using Gronwall’s lemma, we obtain

v^{q}(x)\leq 2^{q-1}\omega^{q}(x)+4^{q-1}a^{q}\left(\frac{q-1}{q\eta-1}\right)^{q-1}t^{q\eta-1}\\
\times\int_{0}^{x}\omega^{q}(s)\exp\left(2^{q-1}a^{q}\left(\frac{q-1}{q\eta-1}\right)^{q-1}t^{q\eta-1}(x-s)\right)ds,\,\,0\leq x\leq t.

(2.6)

This, together with the fundamental inequality
(a+b)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq a^{\frac{1}{q}}+b^{\frac{1}{q}}\,\forall\,a,b\geq 0,
gives us

v(x)\leq 2\omega(x)\\
+4a\left(\frac{q-1}{q\eta-1}\right)^{\frac{q-1}{q}}t^{\eta-\frac{1}{q}}\exp\left(\frac{2^{q-1}a^{q}}{q}\left(\frac{q-1}{q\eta-1}\right)^{q-1}t^{q\eta}\right)\left(\int_{0}^{x}\omega^{q}(s)ds\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},\,\,0\leq x\leq t.

Choosing
x=t
and
q=\frac{2}{\eta}
yields

v(t)\leq 2\omega(t)+\frac{8at^{\frac{\eta}{2}}}{\eta}\exp\left(\frac{4^{\frac{2}{\eta}}a^{\frac{2}{\eta}}t^{2}}{\eta^{\frac{2}{\eta}}}\right)\left(\int_{0}^{t}\omega^{\frac{2}{\eta}}(s)ds\right)^{\frac{\eta}{2}},\,\,0\leq t\leq T.

So the inequality (
2.4
) is verified. When the function
w
is non-decreasing, we have
\omega^{q}(s)\leq\omega^{q}(x)
for
s\leq x,
and hence, (
2.6
) reduces to the following

v^{q}(x)\leq 2^{q-1}\omega^{q}(t)\exp\left(2^{q-1}a^{q}\left(\frac{q-1}{q\eta-1}\right)^{q-1}t^{q\eta-1}x\right),\,\,0\leq x\leq t.

Choosing
x=t
and
q=\frac{2}{\eta}
gives us (
2.5
). The proof of the lemma is complete.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

The Gronwall-type lemma for singular kernels plays an important role in the theory of fractional differential equations. Under the assumption of Lemma
2.4
, it was proved in
[
14
]
that

v(t)\leq\omega(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\Big[\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(a\Gamma(\beta))^{n}}{\Gamma(n\beta)}(t-s)^{n\beta-1}\omega(s)\Big]ds,\,\,0\leq t\leq T.

The above estimate has been widely used in the literature. Here, we need to use estimates (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) for establishing the estimates bounded uniformly in
\alpha.

2.2
Malliavin calculus

Let us recall some elements of Malliavin calculus. We suppose that
(B_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
is defined on a complete probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{F},P)
, where
\mathbb{F}=(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
is a natural filtration generated by the Brownian motion
B.
For
h\in L^{2}[0,T]
, we denote by
B(h)
the Wiener integral

B(h)=\int_{0}^{T}h(t)dB_{t}.

Let
\mathcal{S}
denote a dense subset of
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
that consists of smooth random variables of the form

\displaystyle F=f(B(h_{1}),B(h_{2}),...,B(h_{n})),

(2.7)

where
n\in\mathbb{N},f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),h_{1},h_{2},...,h_{n}\in L^{2}[0,T]
. If
F
has the form (
2.7
), we define its Malliavin derivative as the process
DF:={D_{t}F,t\in[0,T]}
given by

D_{t}F=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{k}}(B(h_{1}),B(h_{2}),...,B(h_{n}))h_{k}(t).

More generally, for each
k\geq 1,
we can define the iterated derivative operator on a cylindrical random variable by setting

D_{t_{1},...,t_{k}}^{k}F=D_{t_{1}}...D_{t_{k}}F.

For any
1\leq p,k<\infty
, we denote by
\mathbb{D}^{k,p}
the closure of
\mathcal{S}
with respect to the norm

||F||_{k,p}^{p}:=E|F|^{p}+E\left[\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}|D_{u}F|^{2}du\bigg)^{\frac{p}{2}}\right]+...+E\left[\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}...\int_{0}^{T}|D^{k}_{t_{1},...,t_{k}}F|^{2}dt_{1}...dt_{k}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{2}}\right].

A random variable
F
is said to be Malliavin differentiable if it belongs to
\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
.
For the convenience of the reader, we recall that a derivative operator
D
satisfies the chain rule, i.e,
D\phi(F)=\phi^{\prime}(F)DF
. Furthermore, we have the following relations between Malliavin derivative and the integrals

D_{r}\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}u_{s}ds\bigg)=\int_{r}^{T}D_{r}u_{s}ds

and

D_{r}\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}u_{s}dB_{s}\bigg)=u_{r}+\int_{r}^{T}D_{r}u_{s}dB_{s},0\leq r\leq T,

where
(u_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
is an
\mathbb{F}
-adapted and Malliavin differentiable stochastic process.

An important operator in the Malliavin’s calculus theory is the divergence operator
\delta
, it is the adjoint of the derivative operator
D
. The domain of
\delta
is the set of all functions
u\in L^{2}(\Omega,L^{2}[0,T])
such that

E|\langle DF,u\rangle_{L^{2}[0,T]}|\leq C(u)\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

where
C(u)
is some positive constant depending on
u
. Let
F\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
and
u\in Dom\delta
such that
Fu\in L^{2}(\Omega\times[0,T])
Then
\delta(u)
is characterized by the following relationship

\delta(Fu)=F\delta(u)-\langle DF,u\rangle_{L^{2}[0,T]}.

(2.8)

We have the following general result.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
F_{1}\in\mathbb{D}^{2,4}
be such that
\|DF_{1}\|_{L^{2}[0,T]}>0\,\,a.s.
Then, for any random variable
F_{2}\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
and any measurable function
g
with
\|g\|_{\infty}:=\sup\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|g(x)|\leq 1,
we have

\displaystyle|Eg(F_{1})-Eg(F_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(E\|DF_{1}\|^{-8}_{L^{2}[0,T]}E\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}|D_{\theta}D_{r}F_{1}|^{2}d\theta dr\right)^{2}+(E\|DF_{1}\|^{-2}_{L^{2}[0,T]})^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\|F_{1}-F_{2}\|_{1,2},

(2.9)

provided that the expectations exist, where
C
is an absolute constant.

Proof.

This lemma is Theorem 3.1 in our recent paper
[
8
]
. Here we note that the inequality (
2.9
) follows from the relation

Eg(F_{1})-Eg(F_{2})\\
=E\left[\int_{F_{2}}^{F_{1}}g(z)dz\delta\left(\frac{DF_{1}}{\|DF_{1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}[0,T]}}\right)\right]-E\left[\frac{g(F_{2})\langle DF_{1}-DF_{2},DF_{1}\rangle_{L^{2}[0,T]}}{\|DF_{1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}[0,T]}}\right].

(2.10)

We also have
E\left[\left(\delta\bigg(\frac{DF_{1}}{\|DF_{1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}[0,T]}}\bigg)\right)^{2}\right]<\infty.

∎

3
Strong convergence

As discussed in the introduction, the strong convergence of the solutions to the equation (
1.2
) have been well studied. In this section, our aim is to prove the optimality of the estimate (
1.5
) as
\alpha\to\beta.
More specifically, in Theorem
3.1
below, we show that the limit

Y_{\beta,t}:=\lim\limits_{\alpha\to\beta}\frac{X_{\alpha,t}-X_{\beta,t}}{\alpha-\beta}
exists and can be computed explicitly.

Let us introduce some notations: We denote by
C
(with or without an index) a generic constant which may vary at each appearance. Given a function
h(t,x),
we denote

h^{\prime}(t,x):=\frac{\partial h}{\partial x}(t,x),\quad h^{\prime\prime}(t,x):=\frac{\partial h}{\partial x^{2}}(t,x).

For any
a,b\in\mathbb{R},
we denote
a\vee b=\max\{a,b\},\ a\wedge b=\min\{a,b\}.
In the proofs, we frequently use the fundamental inequality

(a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n})^{p}\leq n^{p-1}(a_{1}^{p}+\cdots+a_{n}^{p})\ \ \mbox{for all}\ a_{1},...,a_{n}\geq 0\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ p\geq 2.

(3.1)

We also frequently use a trick of the Hölder inequality as follows: For all
p>1,q>1
with
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1,
we have

\left(\int\limits_{a}^{b}|f(t)g(t)|dt\right)^{p}=\left(\int\limits_{a}^{b}|f(t)|^{\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p}}|g(t)|dt\right)^{p}\leq\left(\int\limits_{a}^{b}|f(t)|dt\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\int\limits_{a}^{b}|f(t)||g(t)|^{p}dt.

(3.2)

For the existence and uniqueness of the solutions, we make the use of the following assumption, see e.g.
[
18
,
21
]
.

Assumption 3.1
.

The coefficients
b,\sigma:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
are Lipschitz and have linear growth, that is, there exists
L>0
such that

|b(t,x)-b(t,y)|+|\sigma(t,x)-\sigma(t,y)|\leq L|x-y|\,\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R},t\in[0,T],

and

|b(t,x)|+|\sigma(t,x)|\leq L(1+|x|)\,\forall x\in\mathbb{R},t\in[0,T].

We first need the following technical results.

Proposition 3.1
.

Suppose Assumption
3.1
. Let
(X_{\alpha,t})_{t\in[0,T]}
be the solution to the equation (
1.2
). Then, for every
\frac{1}{2}<\alpha_{0}<1
and
p\geq 2,
we have

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{\alpha\in\left[\alpha_{0},1\right]}\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}E|X_{\alpha,t}|^{p}\leq C,

(3.3)

where
C
is a positive constant depending only on
L,T,p,

\alpha_{0}
and
x_{0}.

Proof.

This proposition is not new. We give a proof to show that the moments are bounded uniformly in
\alpha.
By using the inequality (
3.1
), the fact
\Gamma(\alpha)\geq 1
and Burkhölder-Davis-Gundy inequality, it follows from the equation (
1.2
) that

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle\left|X_{\alpha,t}\right|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq 3^{p-1}\left(|x_{0}|^{p}+E\left|\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}b(s,X_{\alpha,s})ds\right|^{p}+E\left|\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\sigma(s,X_{\alpha,s})dB_{s}\right|^{p}\right)

where
C
is a positive constant depending only on
p
and
x_{0}.

Next, by using the Hölder inequality (
3.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle\left|X_{\alpha,t}\right|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C+Ct^{\frac{p}{2}}E\left|\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}b^{2}(s,X_{\alpha,s})ds\right|^{\frac{p}{2}}+CE\left|\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}\sigma^{2}(s,X_{\alpha,s})ds\right|^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C+Ct^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}E|b(s,X_{\alpha,s})|^{p}ds

\displaystyle\qquad+C\left(\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}E|\sigma(s,X_{\alpha,s})|^{p}ds,\,\,0\leq t\leq T.

Then, under Assumption
3.1
, we deduce

\displaystyle E\left|X_{\alpha,t}\right|^{p}\leq C+2^{p}L^{p}Ct^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(\frac{t^{2\alpha-1}}{2\alpha-1}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}(1+E|X_{\alpha,s}|^{p})ds

\displaystyle\leq C+C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}E|X_{\alpha,s}|^{p}ds,\ \ \ 0\leq t\leq T,

where
C
is a positive constant depending only on
L,T,p,x_{0}
and
\alpha_{0}.
Applying the inequality (
2.5
) to
\eta=2\alpha-1,
we get

E\left|X_{\alpha,t}\right|^{p}\leq 2C\exp\left(\frac{4^{\frac{2}{\eta}}C^{\frac{2}{\eta}}t^{2}}{\eta^{\frac{2}{\eta}}}\right),\,\,0\leq t\leq T,

which give us the required conclusion (
3.3
). The proof of the proposition is complete.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Suppose Assumption
3.1
. Let
(X_{\alpha,t})_{t\in[0,T]}

) and (

\displaystyle E|X_{\alpha,t}-X_{\beta,t}|^{p}\leq Ct^{p(\alpha\wedge\beta)-\frac{p}{2}}(|\ln t|^{p}+1)|\alpha-\beta|^{p}\,\,\forall\,t\in[0,T],\alpha,\beta\in[\alpha_{0},1],

(3.4)

where

L,T,p,

\alpha_{0}

Proof.

We recall that, for

\displaystyle X_{\alpha,t}-X_{\beta,t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}b(s,X_{\alpha,s})ds-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\beta-1}b(s,X_{\beta,s})ds

\displaystyle I_{1}:=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}b(s,X_{\alpha,s})ds-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\beta-1}b(s,X_{\beta,s})ds,

\displaystyle I_{2}:=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\sigma(s,X_{\alpha,s})dB_{s}-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\beta-1}\sigma(s,X_{\beta,s})dB_{s}.

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\big(b(s,X_{\alpha,s})-b(s,X_{\beta,s})\big)ds

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\big(\sigma(s,X_{\alpha,s})-\sigma(s,X_{\beta,s})\big)dB_{s}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}((t-s)^{\alpha-1}-(t-s)^{\beta-1})\sigma(s,X_{\beta,s})dB_{s}

), the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Assumption

, we deduce

\displaystyle E|I_{1}|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq 3^{p-1}L^{p}t^{\frac{p}{2}}E\left(\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}|X_{\alpha,s}-X_{\beta,s}|^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle+6^{p-1}L^{p}t^{\frac{p}{2}}E\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left((t-s)^{\alpha-1}-(t-s)^{\beta-1}\right)^{2}(1+|X_{\beta,s}|)^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle+6^{p-1}L^{p}t^{\frac{p}{2}}\bigg|\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\bigg|^{p}E\left(\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{2\beta-2}(1+|X_{\beta,s}|^{2})ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}.

3.2
), we get

\displaystyle E|I_{1}|^{p}\leq 3^{p-1}L^{p}t^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}E|X_{\alpha,s}-X_{\beta,s}|^{p}ds

\displaystyle+6^{p-1}L^{p}(2t)^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left((t-s)^{\alpha-1}-(t-s)^{\beta-1}\right)^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{t}\left((t-s)^{\alpha-1}-(t-s)^{\beta-1}\right)^{2}(1+E|X_{\beta,s}|^{p})ds

\displaystyle+6^{p-1}L^{p}t^{\frac{p}{2}}\bigg|\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\bigg|^{p}\left(\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{2\beta-2}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{2\beta-2}(1+E|X_{\beta,s}|^{p})ds.

We now use the estimate (
3.3
) to get

\displaystyle E|I_{1}|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}E|X_{\alpha,s}-X_{\beta,s}|^{p}ds

\displaystyle+Ct^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left((t-s)^{\alpha-1}-(t-s)^{\beta-1}\right)^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}+Ct^{p\beta}\bigg|\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\bigg|^{p},

where

is a positive constant depending only on

and
\alpha_{0}.
Recalling Lemmas

and

E|I_{1}|^{p}\leq C(t^{p\alpha}+t^{p\beta})(|\ln t|^{p}+1)|\alpha-\beta|^{p}+C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}E|X_{\alpha,s}-X_{\beta,s}|^{p}ds,

(3.6)

where
C
is a positive constant depending on
L,T,p,

\alpha_{0}
and
x_{0}
.

For

, we have

\displaystyle E|I_{2}|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq 3^{p-1}L^{p}c_{p}E\left(\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}|X_{\alpha,s}-X_{\beta,s}|^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle+6^{p-1}L^{p}c_{p}E\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left((t-s)^{\alpha-1}-(t-s)^{\beta-1}\right)^{2}(1+|X_{\beta,s}|)^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle+3^{p-1}L^{p}c_{p}\bigg|\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\bigg|^{p}E\left(\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{2\beta-2}(1+|X_{\beta,s}|)^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}},

Theorem 3.1
.

) and (

), respectively. Then, for every

(3.9)

is a positive constant depending only on

\alpha_{0}
,
x_{0},\nu,\delta
and
(Y_{\beta,t})_{0\leq t\leq T}

\displaystyle Y_{\beta,t}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}(\beta)}{\Gamma^{2}(\beta)}\left(\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\beta-1}b(s,X_{\beta,s})ds+\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\beta-1}\sigma(s,X_{\beta,s})dB_{s}\right)

\displaystyle\ \ +\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\beta-1}\left(\ln(t-s)b(s,X_{\beta,s})+b^{\prime}(s,X_{\beta,s})Y_{\beta,t}\right)ds

\displaystyle\ \ +\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\beta-1}\left(\ln(t-s)\sigma(s,X_{\beta,s})+\sigma^{\prime}(s,X_{\beta,s})Y_{\beta,t}\right)dB_{s},\,\,0\leq t\leq T.

Proof.

For
\alpha\neq\beta,
we obtain from the relation (

\displaystyle\frac{X_{\alpha,t}-X_{\beta,t}}{\alpha-\beta}=\frac{1}{\alpha-\beta}\left(\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}b(s,X_{\alpha,s})ds-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\beta-1}b(s,X_{\beta,s})ds\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\alpha-\beta}\left(\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\sigma(s,X_{\alpha,s})dB_{s}-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\beta-1}\sigma(s,X_{\beta,s})dB_{s}\right),\,0\leq t\leq T.

We decompose the addends in the right hand side as follows

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha-\beta}\left(\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}b(s,X_{\alpha,s})ds-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\beta-1}b(s,X_{\beta,s})ds\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha-\beta}\left(\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\right)\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}b(s,X_{\alpha,s})ds+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(t-s)^{\alpha-1}-(t-s)^{\beta-1}}{\alpha-\beta}b(s,X_{\beta,s})ds

(3.11)

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha-\beta}\left(\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\sigma(s,X_{\alpha,s})dB_{s}-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\beta-1}\sigma(s,X_{\beta,s})dB_{s}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha-\beta}\left(\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\right)\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\sigma(s,X_{\alpha,s})dB_{s}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(t-s)^{\alpha-1}-(t-s)^{\beta-1}}{\alpha-\beta}\sigma(s,X_{\beta,s})dB_{s}

\displaystyle\ \ +\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{0}^{t}\left((t-s)^{\alpha-1}-(t-s)^{\beta-1}\right)\frac{\sigma(s,X_{\alpha,s})-\sigma(s,X_{\beta,s})}{\alpha-\beta}dB_{s}

\displaystyle\ \ +\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\beta-1}\frac{\sigma(s,X_{\alpha,s})-\sigma(s,X_{\beta,s})}{\alpha-\beta}dB_{s}.

(3.12)

By Taylor’s expansion, we have

\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}=-(\alpha-\beta)\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}(\beta)}{\Gamma^{2}(\beta)}+(\alpha-\beta)R_{1}(\alpha,\beta),

(3.13)

where

\alpha_{0}.

\displaystyle b(s,X_{\alpha,s})-b(s,X_{\beta,s})

\displaystyle=(X_{\alpha,s}-X_{\beta,s})\int_{0}^{1}b^{\prime}(s,X_{\beta,s}+\theta(X_{\alpha,s}-X_{\beta,s}))d\theta

(3.18)

\sigma(s,X_{\alpha,s})-\sigma(s,X_{\beta,s})=\sigma^{\prime}(s,X_{\beta,s})(X_{\alpha,s}-X_{\beta,s})+R_{\sigma}(\alpha,\beta,t,s),

(3.19)

where the remainder term

(3.20)

We insert (
3.13
), (
3.15
), (
3.17
) and (
3.19
) into the decompositions (
3.11
) and (
3.12

\displaystyle\frac{X_{\alpha,t}-X_{\beta,t}}{\alpha-\beta}

\displaystyle=\left(-\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}(\beta)}{\Gamma^{2}(\beta)}+R_{1}(\alpha,\beta)\right)\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}b(s,X_{\alpha,s})ds

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{0}^{t}\left((t-s)^{\beta}\ln(t-s)+R_{2}(\alpha,\beta,t,s)\right)b(s,X_{\beta,s})ds

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{0}^{t}\left((t-s)^{\alpha-1}-(t-s)^{\beta-1}\right)\frac{b(s,X_{\alpha,s})-b(s,X_{\beta,s})}{\alpha-\beta}ds

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\beta-1}\left(b^{\prime}(s,X_{\beta,s})\frac{X_{\alpha,s}-X_{\beta,s}}{\alpha-\beta}+\frac{R_{b}(\alpha,\beta,t,s)}{\alpha-\beta}\right)ds

\displaystyle+\left(-\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}(\beta)}{\Gamma^{2}(\beta)}+R_{1}(\alpha,\beta)\right)\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\sigma(s,X_{\alpha,s})dB_{s}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{0}^{t}\left((t-s)^{\beta}\ln(t-s)+R_{2}(\alpha,\beta,t,s)\right)\sigma(s,X_{\beta,s})dB_{s}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{0}^{t}\left((t-s)^{\alpha-1}-(t-s)^{\beta-1}\right)\frac{\sigma(s,X_{\alpha,s})-\sigma(s,X_{\beta,s})}{\alpha-\beta}dB_{s}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\beta-1}\left(\sigma^{\prime}(s,X_{\beta,s})\frac{X_{\alpha,s}-X_{\beta,s}}{\alpha-\beta}+\frac{R_{\sigma}(\alpha,\beta,t,s)}{\alpha-\beta}\right)dB_{s},\,\,0\leq t\leq T.

We recall that

\displaystyle Y_{\beta,t}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}(\beta)}{\Gamma^{2}(\beta)}\left(\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\beta-1}b(s,X_{\beta,s})ds+\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\beta-1}\sigma(s,X_{\beta,s})dB_{s}\right)

\displaystyle\ \ +\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\beta-1}\left(\ln(t-s)b(s,X_{\beta,s})+b^{\prime}(s,X_{\beta,s})Y_{\beta,t}\right)ds

\displaystyle\ \ +\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\beta-1}\left(\ln(t-s)\sigma(s,X_{\beta,s})+\sigma^{\prime}(s,X_{\beta,s})Y_{\beta,t}\right)dB_{s},\,\,0\leq t\leq T.

Then, for
Z_{\alpha,\beta,t}:=\frac{X_{\alpha,t}-X_{\beta,t}}{\alpha-\beta}-Y_{\beta,t},
we obtain

\displaystyle Z_{\alpha,\beta,t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\left(\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\beta-1}b^{\prime}(s,X_{\beta,s})Z_{\alpha,\beta,s}ds+\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\beta-1}\sigma^{\prime}(s,X_{\beta,s})Z_{\alpha,\beta,s}dB_{s}\right)

\displaystyle+R(\alpha,\beta,t)+\bar{R}(\alpha,\beta,t),\,\,0\leq t\leq T,

(3.21)

where the terms
R(\alpha,\beta,t)
and
\bar{R}(\alpha,\beta,t)
are defined by

R(\alpha,\beta,t):=R_{1}(\alpha,\beta)\left(\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}b(s,X_{\alpha,s})ds+\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\sigma(s,X_{\alpha,s})dB_{s}\right)\\
+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{0}^{t}R_{2}(\alpha,\beta,t,s)b(s,X_{\beta,s})ds+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{0}^{t}R_{2}(\alpha,\beta,t,s)\sigma(s,X_{\beta,s})dB_{s}\\
+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\beta-1}\frac{R_{b}(\alpha,\beta,t,s)}{\alpha-\beta}ds+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\beta-1}\frac{R_{\sigma}(\alpha,\beta,t,s)}{\alpha-\beta}dB_{s}

(3.22)

and

\bar{R}(\alpha,\beta,t):=-\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}(\beta)}{\Gamma^{2}(\beta)}\int_{0}^{t}((t-s)^{\alpha-1}b(s,X_{\alpha,s})+(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\sigma(s,X_{\alpha,s}))ds\\
+\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}(\beta)}{\Gamma^{2}(\beta)}\int_{0}^{t}((t-s)^{\beta-1}b(s,X_{\beta,s})+(t-s)^{\beta-1}\sigma(s,X_{\beta,s}))dB_{s}\\
+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{0}^{t}\left((t-s)^{\alpha-1}-(t-s)^{\beta-1}\right)\frac{b(s,X_{\alpha,s})-b(s,X_{\beta,s})}{\alpha-\beta}ds\\
+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{0}^{t}\left((t-s)^{\alpha-1}-(t-s)^{\beta-1}\right)\frac{\sigma(s,X_{\alpha,s})-\sigma(s,X_{\beta,s})}{\alpha-\beta}dB_{s}.

(3.23)

For every
p\geq 2,
by using the Cauchy-Schwarz and Burkhölder-Davis-Gundy inequalities and the fact
\Gamma(\beta)\geq 1,
we obtain

E|R(\alpha,\beta,t)|^{p}\leq C|R_{1}(\alpha,\beta)|^{p}E|\Gamma(\alpha)(X_{\alpha,t}-x_{0})|^{p}\\
+Ct^{\frac{p}{2}}E\left(\int_{0}^{t}|R_{2}(\alpha,\beta,t,s)b(s,X_{\beta,s})|^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}+CE\left(\int_{0}^{t}|R_{2}(\alpha,\beta,t,s)\sigma(s,X_{\beta,s})|^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\\
+Ct^{\frac{p}{2}}E\left(\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{2\beta-2}\frac{|R_{b}(\alpha,\beta,t,s)|^{2}}{(\alpha-\beta)^{2}}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}+CE\left(\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{2\beta-2}\frac{|R_{\sigma}(\alpha,\beta,t,s)|^{2}}{(\alpha-\beta)^{2}}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}},

where
C
is a positive constant depending only on
p.
Then, by the inequality (
3.2
), we deduce

E|R(\alpha,\beta,t)|^{p}\leq C|R_{1}(\alpha,\beta)|^{p}E|\Gamma(1/2)(X_{\alpha,t}-x_{0})|^{p}\\
+C\left(\int_{0}^{t}|R_{2}(\alpha,\beta,t,s)|^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\int_{0}^{t}|R_{2}(\alpha,\beta,t,s)|^{2}\left(E|b(s,X_{\beta,s})|^{p}+E|\sigma(s,X_{\beta,s})|^{p}\right)ds\\
+CE\left(\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{2\beta-2}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{2\beta-2}\frac{E|R_{b}(\alpha,\beta,t,s)|^{p}+E|R_{\sigma}(\alpha,\beta,t,s)|^{p}}{|\alpha-\beta|^{p}}ds,

where
C
is a positive constant depending only on
T
and
p.
Consequently, by using the estimate (
3.3
), the linear growth property of
b
and
\sigma
and the estimates (
3.14
), (
3.16
), (
3.18
) and (
3.20
), we get

E|R(\alpha,\beta,t)|^{p}\leq C|\alpha-\beta|^{p\delta},

(3.24)

where
C
is a positive constant depending only on
L,T,p,

\alpha_{0}
,
x_{0},\nu,\delta.

Similarly, we have the following estimates for the term
\bar{R}(\alpha,\beta,t)

E|\bar{R}(\alpha,\beta,t)|^{p}\leq CE|\Gamma(\alpha)(X_{\alpha,t}-x_{0})-\Gamma(\beta)(X_{\beta,t}-x_{0})|^{p}\\
+Ct^{\frac{p}{2}}E\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left((t-s)^{\alpha-1}-(t-s)^{\beta-1}\right)^{2}\frac{|b(s,X_{\alpha,s})-b(s,X_{\beta,s})|^{2}}{(\alpha-\beta)^{2}}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\\
+CE\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left((t-s)^{\alpha-1}-(t-s)^{\beta-1}\right)^{2}\frac{|\sigma(s,X_{\alpha,s})-\sigma(s,X_{\beta,s})|^{2}}{(\alpha-\beta)^{2}}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}},

and hence,

E|\bar{R}(\alpha,\beta,t)|^{p}\leq C\Gamma^{p}(\alpha)\Gamma^{p}(\beta)\left|\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\right|^{p}E|X_{\alpha,t}-x_{0}|^{p}+\Gamma^{p}(\beta)E|X_{\alpha,t}-X_{\beta,t}|^{p}\\
+CE\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left((t-s)^{\alpha-1}-(t-s)^{\beta-1}\right)^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\\
\times\int_{0}^{t}\left((t-s)^{\alpha-1}-(t-s)^{\beta-1}\right)^{2}\frac{E|b(s,X_{\alpha,s})-b(s,X_{\beta,s})|^{p}+E|\sigma(s,X_{\alpha,s})-\sigma(s,X_{\beta,s})|^{p}}{|\alpha-\beta|^{p}}ds.

Note that
\Gamma(\alpha),\Gamma(\beta)\leq\Gamma(1/2).
So it follows from Lipschitz property of
b
and
\sigma
and the estimates (
2.1
), (
2.2
), (
3.4
) and (
3.3
) that

E|\bar{R}(\alpha,\beta,t)|^{p}\leq C|\alpha-\beta|^{p}\leq C|\alpha-\beta|^{p\delta},

(3.25)

where
C
is a positive constant depending only on
L,T,p,

\alpha_{0}
,
x_{0}.

To finish the proof, we use the same arguments as in the proof of Proposition
3.1
and we obtain from (
3.21
) that, for
p\geq 2,

\displaystyle E|Z_{\alpha,\beta,t}|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{2\beta-2}E|Z_{\alpha,\beta,s}|^{p}ds+CE|R(\alpha,\beta,t)|^{p}+E|\bar{R}(\alpha,\beta,t)|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C|\alpha-\beta|^{p\delta}+C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{2\beta-2}E|Z_{\alpha,\beta,s}|^{p}ds,\,\,0\leq t\leq T,

where
C
is a positive constant depending only on
L,T,p,\alpha_{0},x_{0},\nu
and
\delta.
Then, by applying the inequality (
2.5
) to
\eta=2\beta-1,
we conclude that

E|Z_{\alpha,\beta,t}|^{p}\leq C|\alpha-\beta|^{p\delta},\ \ \ 0\leq t\leq T.

This completes the proof of the theorem because
Z_{\alpha,\beta,t}=\frac{X_{\alpha,t}-X_{\beta,t}}{\alpha-\beta}-Y_{\beta,t}.

∎

4
Weak convergence

In this section, our aim is to bound the quantity
|Eg(X_{\alpha,t})-Eg(X_{\beta,t})|
when
g
is a bounded and measurable function. We are going to impose the following assumption.

Assumption 4.1
.

For each
t\in[0,T],
the coefficients
b(t,.),\sigma(t,.)
are twice differentiable functions and the derivatives are bounded uniformly in
t
by some constant
L>0
.

Note that we can use the results established in
[
5
]
and (
3.4
) to derive the following bound

|Eg(X_{\alpha,t})-Eg(X_{\beta,t})|\leq C|\alpha-\beta|^{1-\varepsilon}\,\,\forall\,\alpha,\beta\in(\frac{1}{2},1],

where
\varepsilon
is some positive constant. However, this estimate is sub-optimal when
\alpha\to\beta.
In the next theorem, we provide an optimal bound for
|Eg(X_{\alpha,t})-Eg(X_{\beta,t})|.
The price to pay is that we require
\alpha\vee\beta\in[\frac{7}{8},1].

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose Assumptions
3.1
and
4.1
. Let
(X_{\alpha,t})_{t\in[0,T]}
and
(X_{\beta,t})_{t\in[0,T]}
be solution to the equations (
1.2
) and (
1.3
), respectively. We assume additionally that

|\sigma(t,x)|\geq\sigma_{0}>0\mbox{ }\forall\,t\in[0,T],x\in\mathbb{R}.

Let
g
be a measurable function with
\|g\|_{\infty}<\infty
and
\alpha_{0}\in(\frac{1}{2},1].
Then, for any
\alpha\in[\alpha_{0},1]
and
\beta\in[\frac{7}{8},1],
we have

\displaystyle|Eg(X_{\alpha,t})-Eg(X_{\beta,t})|\leq\|g\|_{\infty}t^{\alpha\wedge\beta-\beta}(|\ln t|+1)|\alpha-\beta|\,\,\forall\,t\in(0,T],

(4.1)

where
C
is a positive constant depending only on
L,T,p,

\alpha_{0}
and
x_{0}.
Furthermore, when
g
is continuous, it holds that

\lim\limits_{\alpha\to\beta}\frac{Eg(X_{\alpha,t})-Eg(X_{\beta,t})}{\alpha-\beta}=E\left[g(X_{\beta,t})\delta\left(\frac{Y_{\beta,t}DX_{\beta,t}}{\|DX_{\beta,t}\|_{L^{2}[0,T]}}\right)\right],

(4.2)

where
(Y_{\beta,t})_{t\in[0,T]}
is defined by (
3.10
).

4.1
Malliavin derivative of the solutions

The proof of Theorem
4.1
is based on Lemma
2.5
. Hence, in this subsection, we investigate Malliavin derivatives of the solution to the equation (
1.2
).

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose Assumptions
3.1
and
4.1
. Let
(X_{\alpha,t})_{t\in[0,T]}
be solution to the equation (
1.2
). Then, for each
t\in[0,T],

X_{\alpha,t}
is a twice Malliavin differentiable random variable. Moreover, the Malliavin derivative
D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,t}
satisfies
D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,t}=0
for
\theta>t
and for
0\leq r,\theta<t,

\displaystyle D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,t}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\sigma(\theta,X_{\alpha,\theta})(t-\theta)^{\alpha-1}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}b^{\prime}(s,X_{\alpha,s})D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}ds

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\sigma^{\prime}(s,X_{\alpha,s})D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}dB_{s},

(4.3)

and

D_{r}D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,t}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\big((t-r)^{\alpha-1}\sigma^{\prime}(r,X_{\alpha,r})D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,r}+(t-\theta)^{\alpha-1}\sigma^{\prime}(\theta,X_{\alpha,\theta})D_{r}X_{\alpha,\theta}\big)\\
+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{r\vee\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\left[b^{\prime\prime}(s,X_{\alpha,s})D_{r}X_{\alpha,s}D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}+b^{\prime}(s,X_{\alpha,s})D_{r}D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}\right]ds\\
+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{r\vee\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\left[\sigma^{\prime\prime}(s,X_{\alpha,s})D_{r}X_{\alpha,s}D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}+\sigma^{\prime}(s,X_{\alpha,s})D_{r}D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}\right]dB_{s}.

(4.4)

Proof.

We consider the Picard approximation sequence
{X_{\alpha,t}^{n}}
defined by
X^{0}_{\alpha,t}=x_{0}
and for
n\geq 0,

X^{n+1}_{\alpha,t}=x_{0}+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\bigg[\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}b(s,X^{n}_{\alpha,s})ds+\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\sigma(s,X^{n}_{\alpha,s})dB_{s}\bigg],\,\,0\leq t\leq T.

It is well known that
X^{n}_{\alpha,t}\rightarrow X_{\alpha,t}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
as
n\to\infty
(this is an immediate consequence of the contraction mapping argument presented in the proof of Theorem 3.3 in
[
3
]
). We next prove that

\sup\limits_{n\geq 0}E\|D_{\theta}X^{n}_{\alpha,t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}[0,T]}<\infty,\,\,0\leq t\leq T.

It is easy to see that
X^{n}_{\alpha,t}
is Malliavin differentiable for all
n\geq 0
and
t\in[0,T].
Moreover, we have

\displaystyle D_{\theta}X^{n+1}_{\alpha,t}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\bigg[\sigma(\theta,X^{n}_{\alpha,\theta})(t-\theta)^{\alpha-1}

\displaystyle+\int_{\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}b^{\prime}(s,X^{n}_{\alpha,s})D_{\theta}X^{n}_{\alpha,s}ds

\displaystyle+\int_{\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\sigma^{\prime}(s,X^{n}_{\alpha,s})D_{\theta}X^{n}_{\alpha,s}dB_{s}\bigg],\,\,0\leq t\leq T.

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle E\|D_{\theta}

\displaystyle X^{n+1}_{\alpha,t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}[0,T]}=\int_{0}^{t}E|D_{\theta}X^{n+1}_{\alpha,t}|^{2}d\theta

\displaystyle\leq 3\int_{0}^{t}E\sigma^{2}(\theta,X^{n}_{\alpha,\theta})(t-\theta)^{2\alpha-2}d\theta+3t\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}E|b^{\prime}(s,X^{n}_{\alpha,s})D_{\theta}X^{n}_{\alpha,s}|^{2}dsd\theta

\displaystyle+3\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}E|\sigma^{\prime}(s,X^{n}_{\alpha,s})D_{\theta}X^{n}_{\alpha,s}|^{2}dsd\theta,\,\,0\leq\theta<t\leq T.

By Assumption
3.1
and the estimate (
3.3
)

E\|D_{\theta}X^{n+1}_{\alpha,t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}[0,T]}\leq C+C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}\int_{0}^{s}E|D_{\theta}X^{n}_{\alpha,s}|^{2}d\theta ds,\,\,0\leq t\leq T,

(4.5)

where
C
is a positive constant not depending on
t
and
n.
Put
u_{t}:=\sup\limits_{n\geq 0}(E\|D_{\theta}X^{n}_{\alpha,t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}[0,T]})^{p}
. Taking supremum the two sides of (
4.5
) we get

\displaystyle u_{t}\leq C+C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}u_{s}ds,\,\,0\leq t\leq T.

So, by the inequality (
2.5
) with
\eta=2\alpha-1,
we get

u(t)\leq 2C\exp\left(\frac{4^{\frac{2}{\eta}}C^{\frac{2}{\eta}}t^{2}}{\eta^{\frac{2}{\eta}}}\right),\,\,0\leq t\leq T.

This implies that

\sup\limits_{n\geq 0}E\|D_{\theta}X^{n}_{\alpha,t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}[0,T]}<\infty,\,\,0\leq t\leq T.

We now use Lemma 1.5.3 in
[
13
]
to conclude that, for any
t\in[0,T],
the random variable
X_{\alpha,t}
is Malliavin differentiable and its Malliavin derivative of
X_{\alpha,t}
is given by (
4.3
).

By using the same arguments as above, we can deduce for each
t\in[0,T]
,
X_{\alpha,t}
is a twice Malliavin differentiable random variable. The proof of the proposition is complete.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Assume that Assumptions
3.1
and
4.1
hold. Let
(X_{\alpha,t})_{t\in[0,T]}
be solution to the equation (
1.2
). Let
\alpha_{0}\in(\frac{1}{2},1).
Then, for every

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}E|D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,t}|^{p}d\theta\leq Ct^{(\alpha-1)p+1}\,\,\forall\,0\leq t\leq T,

(4.6)

where
C
is a positive constant depending only on
L,T,p,

x_{0}
and
\alpha_{0}.

Proof.

For any
p\geq 2,
by the inequality (
3.1
), it follows from (
4.3

\displaystyle E|D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,t}|^{p}\leq 3^{p-1}\bigg[E|\sigma(\theta,X_{\alpha,\theta})|^{p}

\displaystyle(t-\theta)^{(\alpha-1)p}+E\bigg|\int_{\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}b^{\prime}(s,X_{\alpha,s})D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}ds\bigg|^{p}

\displaystyle+E\bigg|\int_{\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\sigma^{\prime}(s,X_{\alpha,s})D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}dB_{s}\bigg|^{p}\bigg],\,\,0\leq\theta<t\leq T.

By the estimate (
3.3
) and linear growth property of
\sigma,
one can derive that

E[|\sigma(\theta,X_{\alpha,\theta})|^{p}(t-\theta)^{(\alpha-1)p}]\leq C(t-\theta)^{(\alpha-1)p},\,\,0\leq\theta<t\leq T,

where
C
is a positive constant depending only on
L,T,p,

\alpha_{0}
and
x_{0}.
In addition, by Assumption
4.1
, the Hölder inequality and Burkhölder-Davis-Gundy inequality, we have

\displaystyle E\bigg|\int_{\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}b^{\prime}(s,X_{\alpha,s})

\displaystyle D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}ds\bigg|^{p}\leq L(t-\theta)^{\frac{p}{2}}\bigg(\int_{\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}E|D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}|^{2}ds\bigg)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq Lt^{\frac{p}{2}}\bigg(\int_{\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}ds\bigg)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\int_{\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}E|D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}|^{p}ds

\displaystyle\leq Lt^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(\frac{t^{2\alpha-1}}{2\alpha-1}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\int_{\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}E|D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}|^{p}ds

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}E|D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}|^{p}ds,\,\,0\leq\theta<t\leq T.

and, for some
c_{p}>0,

\displaystyle E\bigg|\int_{\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\sigma^{\prime}(s,X_{\alpha,s})

\displaystyle D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}dB_{s}\bigg|^{p}\leq Lc_{p}E\bigg(\int_{\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}|D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}|^{2}ds\bigg)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq Lc_{p}\bigg(\int_{\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}ds\bigg)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\int_{\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}E|D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}|^{p}ds

\displaystyle\leq Lc_{p}\left(\frac{t^{2\alpha-1}}{2\alpha-1}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\int_{\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}E|D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}|^{p}ds

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}E|D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}|^{p}ds,\,\,0\leq\theta<t\leq T,

where
C
is a positive constant depending only on
L,T,p,

\alpha_{0}
and
x_{0}.
We deduce

\displaystyle E|D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,t}|^{p}\leq C(t-\theta)^{(\alpha-1)p}+C\int_{\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}E|D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}|^{p}ds,\,\,0\leq\theta<t\leq T.

Fixed

we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}E|D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,t}|^{p}d\theta\leq Ct^{(\alpha-1)p+1}+C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}\int_{0}^{s}E|D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}|^{p}d\theta ds,\,\,0\leq\theta<t\leq T.

Since
w(t)=t^{(\alpha-1)p+1},0\leq t\leq T
is an increasing function, this allows us to use the inequality (
2.5
) with
\eta=2\alpha-1
and we obtain the desired estimate (
4.6
).

The proof of the proposition is complete.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Suppose that Assumptions
3.1
and
4.1

(X_{\alpha,t})_{t\in[0,T]}

p\in(1,\frac{1}{4-4\alpha_{0}}]

E\left[\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}|D_{r}D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,t}|^{2}drd\theta\right]^{p}\leq Ct^{2p(2\alpha-1)},\,\,0\leq t\leq T,

\displaystyle E|D_{r}D_{\theta}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}E|D_{r}D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,t}|^{2p}drd\theta

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}(t-r)^{2p(\alpha-1)}\int_{0}^{r}E|D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,r}|^{2p}d\theta dr+C\int_{0}^{t}(t-\theta)^{2p(\alpha-1)}\int_{0}^{\theta}E|D_{r}X_{\alpha,\theta}|^{2p}drd\theta

Fixed
p\in(1,\frac{1}{4-4\alpha_{0}}].
Recalling the estimate (
4.6
) gives us

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}E|D_{r}D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,t}|^{2p}drd\theta

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}(t-r)^{2p(\alpha-1)}r^{2p(\alpha-1)+1}dr+C\int_{0}^{t}(t-\theta)^{2p(\alpha-1)}\theta^{2p(\alpha-1)+1}d\theta

\displaystyle+C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}s^{4p(\alpha-1)+2}ds+C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}\left(\int_{0}^{s}\int_{0}^{s}E|D_{r}D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}|^{2p}drd\theta\right)ds,

(4.7)

where
C
is a positive constant depending on
L,T,p,

\alpha_{0}
and
x_{0}
. Next, we observe that

\int_{0}^{t}(t-r)^{2p(\alpha-1)}r^{2p(\alpha-1)+1}dr=t^{4p(\alpha-1)+2}\int_{0}^{1}(1-u)^{2p(\alpha-1)}u^{2p(\alpha-1)+1}du

and

\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}s^{4p(\alpha-1)+2}ds=t^{(4p+2)(\alpha-1)+3}\int_{0}^{t}(1-u)^{2\alpha-2}u^{4p(\alpha-1)+2}du

Furthermore,
\int_{0}^{1}(1-u)^{2p(\alpha-1)}u^{2p(\alpha-1)+1}du=\frac{\Gamma(2p(\alpha-1)+1)\Gamma(2p(\alpha-1)+2)}{\Gamma(4p(\alpha-1)+3)}
and
\int_{0}^{1}(1-u)^{2\alpha-2}u^{4p(\alpha-1)+2}du=\frac{\Gamma(2\alpha-1)\Gamma(4p(\alpha-1)+3)}{\Gamma((4p+2)(\alpha-1)+4)}.
Hence, for every
p\in(1,\frac{1}{4-4\alpha_{0}}],
we can use the part
(ii)
of Lemma
2.1
to verify that the integrals
\int_{0}^{1}(1-u)^{2p(\alpha-1)}u^{2p(\alpha-1)+1}du
and
\int_{0}^{1}(1-u)^{2\alpha-2}u^{4p(\alpha-1)+2}du
are bounded uniformly in
\alpha\in[\alpha_{0},1].
So we obtain from (
4.7
) that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}E|D_{r}D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,t}|^{2p}drd\theta\leq Ct^{4p(\alpha-1)+2}+C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}\left(\int_{0}^{s}\int_{0}^{s}E|D_{r}D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}|^{2p}drd\theta\right)ds,

where
C
is a positive constant depending on
L,T,p,

\alpha_{0}
and
x_{0}
. Therefore, by using the inequality (
2.5
) with
\eta=2\alpha-1,
we obtain

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}E|D_{r}D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,t}|^{2p}drd\theta\leq Ct^{4p(\alpha-1)+2},\,\,0\leq t\leq T,

where
p\in(1,\frac{1}{4-4\alpha_{0}}]
and
C
is a positive constant depending only on
L,T,p,

\alpha_{0}
and
x_{0}
. Using the Hölder inequality, we deduce

\displaystyle E\left[\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}|D_{r}D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,t}|^{2}drd\theta\right]^{p}\leq Ct^{2(p-1)}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}E|D_{r}D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,t}|^{2p}drd\theta\leq Ct^{2p(2\alpha-1)}.

The proof of the proposition is complete.
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

Assume that Assumptions
3.1
and
4.1
hold. Let
(X_{\alpha,t})_{t\in[0,T]}
and
(X_{\beta,t})_{t\in[0,T]}

) and (

and
\alpha_{0}\in(\frac{1}{2},1],

where

is a positive constant depending on
L,T,\alpha_{0}
and
x_{0}.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,t}-D_{\theta}X_{\beta,t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\bigg[\sigma(\theta,X_{\alpha,\theta})(t-\theta)^{\alpha-1}+\int_{\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}b^{\prime}(s,X_{\alpha,s})D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}ds\bigg]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\sigma^{\prime}(s,X_{\alpha,s})D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}dB_{s}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\bigg[\sigma(\theta,X_{\beta,\theta})(t-\theta)^{\beta-1}+\int_{\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{\beta-1}b^{\prime}(s,X_{\beta,s})D_{\theta}X_{\beta,s}ds\bigg]

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{\beta-1}\sigma^{\prime}(s,X_{\beta,s})D_{\theta}X_{\beta,s}dB_{s}\bigg]

\displaystyle=J_{1}(\theta)+J_{2}(\theta)+J_{3}(\theta),\,\,0\leq t\leq T,

where

\displaystyle J_{1}(\theta)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\sigma(\theta,X_{\alpha,\theta})(t-\theta)^{\alpha-1}-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\sigma(\theta,X_{\beta,\theta})(t-\theta)^{\beta-1},

\displaystyle J_{2}(\theta)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}b^{\prime}(s,X_{\alpha,s})D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}ds-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{\beta-1}b^{\prime}(s,X_{\beta,s})D_{\theta}X_{\beta,s}ds,

\displaystyle J_{3}(\theta)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\sigma^{\prime}(s,X_{\alpha,s})D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}dB_{s}-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{\beta-1}\sigma^{\prime}(s,X_{\beta,s})D_{\theta}X_{\beta,s}dB_{s}.

3.1
), we have

\displaystyle E\|DX_{\alpha,t}-DX_{\beta,t}\|_{L^{2}[0,T]}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 3\int_{0}^{t}(E|J_{1}(\theta)|^{2}+E|J_{2}(\theta)|^{2}+E|J_{3}(\theta)|^{2})d\theta,\,\,0\leq t\leq T.

We next observe that

\displaystyle J_{1}(\theta)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\sigma(\theta,X_{\alpha,\theta})\Big((t-\theta)^{\alpha-1}-(t-\theta)^{\beta-1}\Big)+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}(t-\theta)^{\beta-1}\Big(\sigma(\theta,X_{\alpha,\theta})-\sigma(\theta,X_{\beta,\theta})\Big)

\displaystyle+\bigg(\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\bigg)\sigma(\theta,X_{\beta,\theta})(t-\theta)^{\beta-1}.

Then, using the inequality (
3.1
) and Assumption
3.1
, we deduce

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}E|J_{1}(\theta)|^{2}d\theta

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3}{\Gamma^{2}(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}\Big((t-\theta)^{\alpha-1}-(t-\theta)^{\beta-1}\Big)^{2}E|\sigma(\theta,X_{\alpha,\theta})|^{2}d\theta

\displaystyle\ \ +\frac{3}{\Gamma^{2}(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-\theta)^{2\beta-2}E\Big(\sigma(\theta,X_{\alpha,\theta})-\sigma(\theta,X_{\beta,\theta})\Big)^{2}d\theta

\displaystyle\ \ +3\bigg(\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\bigg)^{2}\int_{0}^{t}(t-\theta)^{2\beta-2}E|\sigma(\theta,X_{\beta,\theta})|^{2}d\theta

\displaystyle\leq 6L^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\Big((t-\theta)^{\alpha-1}-(t-\theta)^{\beta-1}\Big)^{2}(1+E|X_{\alpha,\theta}|^{2})d\theta

\displaystyle\ \ +3L^{2}\int_{0}^{t}(t-\theta)^{2\beta-2}E|X_{\alpha,\theta}-X_{\beta,\theta}|^{2}d\theta

\displaystyle\ \ +3L^{2}\bigg(\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\bigg)^{2}\int_{0}^{t}(t-\theta)^{2\beta-2}(1+E|X_{\alpha,\theta}|^{2})d\theta.

This, together with the estimates obtained in Lemmas
2.2
and
2.3
and Proposition
3.1
, yields

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}E|J_{1}(\theta)|^{2}d\theta

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{2(\alpha\wedge\beta)-1}(|\ln t|^{2}+1)|\alpha-\beta|^{2}+C|\alpha-\beta|^{2}\int_{0}^{t}(t-\theta)^{2\beta-2}d\theta

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{2(\alpha\wedge\beta)-1}(|\ln t|^{2}+1)|\alpha-\beta|^{2}+C|\alpha-\beta|^{2}\frac{t^{2\beta-1}}{2\beta-1}

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{2(\alpha\wedge\beta)-1}(|\ln t|^{2}+1)|\alpha-\beta|^{2},

where
C
is a positive constant depending only on
L,T,\alpha_{0}
and
x_{0}.

For
J_{2}(\theta)
, we have the decomposition

\displaystyle J_{2}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{\theta}^{t}\bigg((t-s)^{\alpha-1}b^{\prime}(s,X_{\alpha,s})D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}-(t-s)^{\beta-1}b^{\prime}(s,X_{\beta,s})D_{\theta}X_{\beta,s}\bigg)ds

\displaystyle+\bigg(\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\bigg)\int_{\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{\beta-1}b^{\prime}(s,X_{\beta,s})D_{\theta}X_{\beta,s}ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}b^{\prime}(s,X_{\alpha,s})(D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}-D_{\theta}X_{\beta,s})ds

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}(b^{\prime}(s,X_{\alpha,s})-b^{\prime}(s,X_{\beta,s}))D_{\theta}X_{\beta,s}ds

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{\theta}^{t}\left((t-s)^{\alpha-1}-(t-s)^{\beta-1}\right)b^{\prime}(s,X_{\beta,s})D_{\theta}X_{\beta,s}ds

\displaystyle+\bigg(\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\bigg)\int_{\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{\beta-1}b^{\prime}(s,X_{\beta,s})D_{\theta}X_{\beta,s}ds.

By the Hölder inequality and Assumption
3.1
we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}E|J_{2}(\theta)|^{2}d\theta

\displaystyle\leq 4\int_{0}^{t}(t-\theta)\left(\int_{\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}E|b^{\prime}(s,X_{\alpha,s})(D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}-D_{\theta}X_{\beta,s})|^{2}ds\right)d\theta

\displaystyle+4\int_{0}^{t}(t-\theta)\left(\int_{\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}E|(b^{\prime}(s,X_{\alpha,s})-b^{\prime}(s,X_{\beta,s}))D_{\theta}X_{\beta,s}|^{2}ds\right)d\theta

\displaystyle+4\int_{0}^{t}(t-\theta)\left(\int_{\theta}^{t}\left((t-s)^{\alpha-1}-(t-s)^{\beta-1}\right)^{2}E|b^{\prime}(s,X_{\beta,s})D_{\theta}X_{\beta,s}|^{2}ds\right)d\theta

\displaystyle+4\bigg(\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\bigg)^{2}\int_{0}^{t}(t-\theta)\left(\int_{\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{2\beta-2}E|b^{\prime}(s,X_{\beta,s})D_{\theta}X_{\beta,s}|^{2}ds\right)d\theta

\displaystyle+4L^{2}T\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}E|(X_{\alpha,s}-X_{\beta,s})D_{\theta}X_{\beta,s}|^{2}ds\right)d\theta

\displaystyle+4L^{2}T\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{\theta}^{t}\left((t-s)^{\alpha-1}-(t-s)^{\beta-1}\right)^{2}E|D_{\theta}X_{\beta,s}|^{2}ds\right)d\theta

\displaystyle+4T\bigg(\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\bigg)^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{2\beta-2}E|D_{\theta}X_{\beta,s}|^{2}ds\right)d\theta,

and hence,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}E|J_{2}(\theta)|^{2}d\theta

\displaystyle\leq 4L^{2}T\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}\bigg(\int_{0}^{s}E|D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}-D_{\theta}X_{\beta,s}|^{2}d\theta\bigg)ds

\displaystyle+4L^{2}T\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}\bigg(\int_{0}^{s}\sqrt{E|X_{\alpha,s}-X_{\beta,s}|^{4}}\sqrt{E|D_{\theta}X_{\beta,s}|^{4}}d\theta\bigg)ds

\displaystyle+4L^{2}T\int_{0}^{t}\left((t-s)^{\alpha-1}-(t-s)^{\beta-1}\right)^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{s}E|D_{\theta}X_{\beta,s}|^{2}d\theta\right)ds

\displaystyle+4T\bigg(\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\bigg)^{2}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{2\beta-2}\left(\int_{0}^{s}E|D_{\theta}X_{\beta,s}|^{2}d\theta\right)ds.

3.4

is a positive constant depending on
L,T,x_{0}
and
\alpha_{0}.
As a consequence, recalling the estimates (

) and (

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}E|J_{2}(\theta)|^{2}d\theta

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}\int_{0}^{s}E|D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}-D_{\theta}X_{\beta,s}|^{2}d\theta ds+Ct^{2(\alpha\wedge\beta)-1}(|\ln t|^{2}+1)|\alpha-\beta|^{2}.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let Assumptions
3.1
and
4.1
hold. In addition, we assume that

|\sigma(t,x)|\geq\sigma_{0}>0\mbox{ }\forall\,t\in[0,T],x\in\mathbb{R}.

Let
\alpha_{0}\in(\frac{1}{2},1).
Then, for every
\alpha\in\left[\alpha_{0},1\right]
and
\gamma\in(1,\frac{1}{2-2\alpha_{0}}],
we have

E\|DX_{\alpha,t}\|^{-2\gamma}_{L^{2}[0,T]}\leq Ct^{(1-2\alpha)\gamma},\,\,0<t\leq T,

where
C
is a positive constant depending only on
L,T,x_{0},\sigma_{0}
and
\alpha_{0}.

Proof.

By using the fundamental inequality
(a+b+c)^{2}\geq\frac{a^{2}}{2}-2(b^{2}+c^{2}),
we obtain from (
4.3
) that

\displaystyle|D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,t}|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(t-\theta)^{2\alpha-2}\sigma^{2}({\theta},X_{\alpha,{\theta}})}{2\Gamma^{2}(\alpha)}

\displaystyle-2\bigg(\int_{\theta}^{t}\frac{(t-s)^{\alpha-1}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}b^{\prime}(s,X_{\alpha,s})D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}ds\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle-2\bigg(\int_{\theta}^{t}\frac{(t-s)^{\alpha-1}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\sigma^{\prime}(s,X_{\alpha,s})D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}dB_{s}\bigg)^{2}.

Fixed
t\in(0,T].
For each
y\geq y_{0}:=\frac{4(2\alpha-1)\Gamma^{2}(\alpha)}{\sigma_{0}^{2}t^{2\alpha-1}}
, the real number
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{t}\left(\frac{4(2\alpha-1)\Gamma^{2}(\alpha)}{y\sigma_{0}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2\alpha-1}}
belongs to
(0,1]
. Hence,

\displaystyle\|DX_{\alpha,t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}[0,T]}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}|D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,t}|^{2}d{\theta}\geq\int_{t(1-\varepsilon)}^{t}|D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,t}|^{2}d{\theta}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\sigma^{2}_{0}}{2\Gamma^{2}(\alpha)}\int_{t(1-\varepsilon)}^{t}(t-{\theta})^{2\alpha-2}d\theta-2\int_{t(1-\varepsilon)}^{t}\bigg(\int_{\theta}^{t}\frac{(t-s)^{\alpha-1}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}b^{\prime}(s,X_{\alpha,s})D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}ds\bigg)^{2}d\theta

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad-2\int_{t(1-\varepsilon)}^{t}\bigg(\int_{\theta}^{t}\frac{(t-s)^{\alpha-1}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\sigma^{\prime}(s,X_{\alpha,s})D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}dB_{s}\bigg)^{2}d\theta

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\sigma^{2}_{0}(t\varepsilon)^{2\alpha-1}}{2(2\alpha-1)\Gamma^{2}(\alpha)}-I_{y}(t)=\frac{2}{y}-I_{y}(t),

where
I_{y}(t)
is given by

\displaystyle I_{y}(t):=2\int_{t(1-\varepsilon)}^{t}\bigg(\int_{\theta}^{t}\frac{(t-s)^{\alpha-1}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}b^{\prime}(s,X_{\alpha,s})D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}ds\bigg)^{2}d\theta

\displaystyle+2\int_{t(1-\varepsilon)}^{t}\bigg(\int_{\theta}^{t}\frac{(t-s)^{\alpha-1}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\sigma^{\prime}(s,X_{\alpha,s})D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}dB_{s}\bigg)^{2}d\theta.

Put

A:=\int_{t(1-\varepsilon)}^{t}\bigg(\int_{\theta}^{t}\frac{(t-s)^{\alpha-1}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}b^{\prime}(s,X_{\alpha,s})D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}ds\bigg)^{2}d\theta

and

B:=\int_{t(1-\varepsilon)}^{t}\bigg(\int_{\theta}^{t}\frac{(t-s)^{\alpha-1}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\sigma^{\prime}(s,X_{\alpha,s})D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}dB_{s}\bigg)^{2}d\theta.

Fixed
q\in[2,\frac{1}{1-\alpha_{0}}].
By the Hölder inequality, we have

\displaystyle E|A|^{q/2}

\displaystyle\leq L^{q}E\bigg(\int_{t(1-\varepsilon)}^{t}\bigg(\int_{\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}|D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}|ds\bigg)^{2}d\theta\bigg)^{\frac{q}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq L^{q}(t\varepsilon)^{\frac{q}{2}-1}\int_{t(1-\varepsilon)}^{t}E\bigg(\int_{\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}|D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}|ds\bigg)^{q}d\theta

\displaystyle\leq L^{q}(t\varepsilon)^{\frac{q}{2}-1}\int_{t(1-\varepsilon)}^{t}\bigg(\int_{\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}ds\bigg)^{q-1}\int_{\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}E|D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}|^{q}dsd\theta

\displaystyle\leq C(t\varepsilon)^{\frac{q}{2}-1}(t\varepsilon)^{\alpha(q-1)}\int_{t(1-\varepsilon)}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\int_{t(1-\varepsilon)}^{s}E|D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}|^{q}d\theta ds.

By using the same arguments as in the proof of (
4.6
), we have

\int_{t(1-\varepsilon)}^{s}E|D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}|^{q}d\theta\leq C(s-t(1-\varepsilon))^{(\alpha-1)q+1}\leq(t\varepsilon)^{(\alpha-1)q+1},\,\,t(1-\varepsilon)\leq s\leq t.

So we obtain

E|A|^{q/2}\leq C(t\varepsilon)^{\frac{q}{2}-1}(t\varepsilon)^{\alpha(q-1)}\int_{t(1-\varepsilon)}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}(t\varepsilon)^{(\alpha-1)q+1}ds\leq C(t\varepsilon)^{\frac{q(4\alpha-1)}{2}}.

Similarly, we also have

\displaystyle E|B|^{q/2}

\displaystyle\leq(t\varepsilon)^{\frac{q}{2}-1}\int_{t(1-\varepsilon)}^{t}E\bigg(\int_{\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\sigma^{\prime}(s,X_{\alpha,s})D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}dB_{s}\bigg)^{q}d\theta

\displaystyle\leq C(t\varepsilon)^{\frac{q}{2}-1}\int_{t(1-\varepsilon)}^{t}E\bigg(\int_{\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}|D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}|^{2}ds\bigg)^{\frac{q}{2}}d\theta

\displaystyle\leq C(t\varepsilon)^{\frac{q}{2}-1}\int_{t(1-\varepsilon)}^{t}\bigg(\int_{\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}ds\bigg)^{\frac{q}{2}-1}\int_{\theta}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}E|D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}|^{q}dsd\theta

\displaystyle\leq C(t\varepsilon)^{\frac{q}{2}-1}(t\varepsilon)^{(2\alpha-1)(\frac{q}{2}-1)}\int_{t(1-\varepsilon)}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}\int_{t(1-\varepsilon)}^{s}E|D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,s}|^{q}d\theta ds

\displaystyle\leq C(t\varepsilon)^{q(2\alpha-1)}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle E|I_{y}(t)|^{\frac{q}{2}}\leq 2^{\frac{q}{2}-1}(E|A|^{\frac{q}{2}}+E|B|^{\frac{q}{2}})\leq C(t\varepsilon)^{q(2\alpha-1)},

where
C
is a positive constant depending only on
L,T,x_{0}
and
\alpha_{0}.
By the Markov inequality, we get

\displaystyle P\bigg(\|DX_{\alpha,t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}[0,T]}\leq\frac{1}{y}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq P\bigg(\frac{2}{y}-I_{y}(t)\leq\frac{1}{y}\bigg)

\displaystyle=P\bigg(I_{y}(t)\geq\frac{1}{y}\bigg)\leq y^{q/2}E|I_{y}(t)|^{q/2}

\displaystyle\leq Cy^{q/2}(t\varepsilon)^{q(2\alpha-1)}

\displaystyle=Cy^{q/2}t^{q(2\alpha-1)}\frac{1}{t^{q(2\alpha-1)}}\left(\frac{2(2\alpha-1)\Gamma^{2}(\alpha)}{y\sigma_{0}^{2}}\right)^{q}

\displaystyle\leq Cy^{-\frac{q}{2}}\,\,\forall\,y\geq y_{0},

where
C
is a positive constant depending only on
L,T,x_{0},\sigma_{0}
and
\alpha_{0}.

For any
\gamma\in(1,\frac{1}{2-2\alpha_{0}}],
we put
2q:=\gamma+\frac{1}{2-2\alpha_{0}}.
Then,
q\in[2,\frac{1}{1-\alpha_{0}}],
and we obtain

\displaystyle E\|DX_{\alpha,t}\|^{-2\gamma}_{L^{2}[0,T]}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\gamma y^{\gamma-1}P(\|DX_{\alpha,t}\|^{-2}_{L^{2}[0,T]}\geq y)dy

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{y_{0}}\gamma y^{\gamma-1}dy+\int_{y_{0}}^{\infty}\gamma y^{\gamma-1}P\big(\|DX_{\alpha,t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}[0,T]}\leq\frac{1}{y}\big)dy

\displaystyle\leq y_{0}^{\gamma}+\int_{y_{0}}^{\infty}\gamma y^{\gamma-1}P\big(\|DX_{\alpha,t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}[0,T]}\leq\frac{1}{y}\big)dy

\displaystyle\leq y_{0}^{\gamma}+C\gamma\int_{y_{0}}^{\infty}y^{\gamma-1}y^{-q/2}dy

\displaystyle\leq y_{0}^{\gamma}+C\gamma y_{0}^{\gamma-\frac{q}{2}}.

Since
y_{0}=\frac{4(2\alpha-1)\Gamma^{2}(\alpha)}{\sigma_{0}^{2}t^{2\alpha-1}},
we conclude that

E\|DX_{\alpha,t}\|^{-2\gamma}_{L^{2}[0,T]}\leq Ct^{(1-2\alpha)\gamma},

where
C
is a positive constant depending only on
L,T,x_{0},\sigma_{0}
and
\alpha_{0}.
The proof of the proposition is complete.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.
We will carry out the proof in two parts.

Part 1.
In this part, we prove (
4.1
). Fixed
t\in(0,T],
an application of Lemma
2.5
to
F_{1}=X_{\beta,t}
and
F_{2}=X_{\alpha,t}
gives us

\displaystyle|Eg(X_{\alpha,t})

\displaystyle-Eg(X_{\beta,t})|\leq C\|g\|_{\infty}\|X_{\alpha,t}-X_{\beta,t}\|_{1,2}

\displaystyle\times\left(E\|DX_{\beta,t}\|^{-8}_{L^{2}[0,T]}E\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}|D_{\theta}D_{r}X_{\beta,t}|^{2}d\theta dr\right)^{2}+(E\|DX_{\beta,t}\|^{-2}_{L^{2}[0,T]})^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}.

For any
\beta\in[\frac{7}{8},1],
by using Proposition
4.3
with
\alpha_{0}=\frac{7}{8}
and
p=2,
we obtain

E\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}|D_{\theta}D_{r}X_{\beta,t}|^{2}d\theta dr\right)^{2}\leq Ct^{4(2\beta-1)}.

On the other hand, by using Proposition
4.5
with
\alpha_{0}=\frac{7}{8}
and
\gamma=4,
we have

E\|DX_{\beta,t}\|^{-8}_{L^{2}[0,T]}\leq Ct^{4(1-2\beta)},

(E\|DX_{\beta,t}\|^{-2}_{L^{2}[0,T]})^{2}\leq Ct^{2(1-2\beta)}.

Thanks to Proposition
4.4
we have

\displaystyle\|X_{\alpha,t}-X_{\beta,t}\|_{1,2}

\displaystyle=\left(E|X_{\alpha,t}-X_{\beta,t}|^{2}+E\|D_{\theta}X_{\alpha,t}-D_{\theta}X_{\beta,t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}[0,T]}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(Ct^{2(\alpha\wedge\beta)-1}(|\ln t|^{2}+1)|\alpha-\beta|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq Ct^{\alpha\wedge\beta-\frac{1}{2}}(|\ln t|+1)|\alpha-\beta|.

Combining the above computations yields

\displaystyle\big|Eg(X_{\alpha,t})-Eg(X_{\beta,t})\big|

\displaystyle\leq C\|g\|_{\infty}\left(t^{4(1-2\beta)}t^{4(2\beta-1)}+t^{2(1-2\beta)}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}t^{\alpha\wedge\beta-\frac{1}{2}}(|\ln t|+1)|\alpha-\beta|

\displaystyle\leq C\|g\|_{\infty}t^{\alpha\wedge\beta-\beta}(|\ln t|+1)|\alpha-\beta|,

which is the required estimate (
4.1
).

Part 2
. In this part, we prove (
4.2
). We note that, by the estimates in Theorem
3.1
and Proposition
4.4
, we have
\frac{X_{\alpha,t}-X_{\beta,t}}{\alpha-\beta}\to Y_{\beta,t}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
as
\alpha\to\beta

and
\max\limits_{\alpha\neq\beta}\frac{E\|DX_{\alpha,t}-DX_{\beta,t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}[0,T]}}{|\alpha-\beta|^{2}}<\infty.

Thus, it follows from Lemma
1.2.3
in
[
13
]
that
Y_{\beta,t}\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
for every
t\in[0,T],\beta\in[\alpha_{0},1]
and

\frac{DX_{\alpha,t}-DX_{\beta,t}}{\alpha-\beta}
weakly converges to
DY_{\beta,t}
in
L^{2}(\Omega\times[0,T])
as
\alpha\to\beta.
By the relationship (
2.8
), we have

\delta\left(\frac{Y_{\beta,t}DX_{\beta,t}}{\|DX_{\beta,t}\|_{L^{2}[0,T]}}\right)=Y_{\beta,t}\delta\left(\frac{DX_{\beta,t}}{\|DX_{\beta,t}\|_{L^{2}[0,T]}}\right)-\frac{\langle DY_{\beta,t},DX_{\beta,t}\rangle_{L^{2}[0,T]}}{\|DX_{\beta,t}\|_{L^{2}[0,T]}}.

Let
g
be a bounded and continuous function. By the relation (
2.10
) we have

Eg(X_{\alpha,t})-Eg(X_{\beta,t})\\
=E\left[\int_{X_{\beta,t}}^{X_{\alpha,t}}g(z)dz\delta\left(\frac{DX_{\beta,t}}{\|DX_{\beta,t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}[0,T]}}\right)\right]-E\left[\frac{g(X_{\alpha,t})\langle DX_{\alpha,t}-DX_{\beta,t},DX_{\beta,t}\rangle_{L^{2}[0,T]}}{\|DX_{\beta,t}\|_{L^{2}[0,T]}^{2}}\right].

(4.8)

Then, for
\alpha\not=\beta,
we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{Eg(X_{\alpha,t})-Eg(X_{\beta,t})}{\alpha-\beta}-E\left[g(X_{\beta,t})\delta\left(\frac{Y_{\beta,t}DX_{\beta,t}}{\|DX_{\beta,t}\|_{L^{2}[0,T]}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha-\beta}E\left[\int_{X_{\beta,t}}^{X_{\alpha,t}}g(z)dz\delta\left(\frac{DX_{\beta,t}}{\|DX_{\beta,t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}[0,T]}}\right)\right]-\frac{1}{\alpha-\beta}E\left[\frac{g(X_{\alpha,t})\langle DX_{\alpha,t}-DX_{\beta,t},DX_{\beta,t}\rangle_{L^{2}[0,T]}}{\|DX_{\beta,t}\|_{L^{2}[0,T]}^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle-E\left[g(X_{\beta,t})Y_{\beta,t}\delta\left(\frac{DX_{\beta,t}}{\|DX_{\beta,t}\|_{L^{2}[0,T]}}\right)\right]+E\left[\frac{g(X_{\beta,t})\langle DY_{\beta,t},DX_{\beta,t}\rangle_{L^{2}[0,T]}}{\|DX_{\beta,t}\|_{L^{2}[0,T]}^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=E\left[\left(\frac{1}{\alpha-\beta}\int_{X_{\beta,t}}^{X_{\alpha,t}}g(z)dz-g(X_{\beta,t})Y_{\beta,t}\right)\delta\left(\frac{DX_{\alpha,t}}{\|DX_{\beta,t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}[0,T]}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle+E\left[\frac{(g(X_{\alpha,t})-g(X_{\beta,t}))\langle DX_{\alpha,t}-DX_{\beta,t},DX_{\alpha,t}\rangle_{L^{2}[0,T]}}{(\alpha-\beta)\|DX_{\beta,t}\|_{L^{2}[0,T]}^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle-E\left[g(X_{\beta,t})\left\langle\frac{DX_{\alpha,t}-DX_{\beta,t}}{\alpha-\beta}-DY_{\beta,t},\frac{DX_{\beta,t}}{\|DX_{\beta,t}\|_{L^{2}[0,T]}^{2}}\right\rangle_{L^{2}[0,T]}\right].

(4.9)

We now observe that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha-\beta}\int_{X_{\beta,t}}^{X_{\alpha,t}}g(z)dz-g(X_{\beta,t})Y_{\beta,t}

\displaystyle=\frac{X_{\alpha,t}-X_{\beta,t}}{\alpha-\beta}\int_{0}^{1}g(X_{\beta,t}-z(X_{\alpha,t}-X_{\beta,t}))dz-g(X_{\beta,t})Y_{\beta,t}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{X_{\alpha,t}-X_{\beta,t}}{\alpha-\beta}-Y_{\beta,t}\right)\int_{0}^{1}g(X_{\beta,t}-z(X_{\alpha,t}-X_{\beta,t}))dz

\displaystyle+Y_{\beta,t}\int_{0}^{1}(g(X_{\beta,t}-z(X_{\alpha,t}-X_{\beta,t}))-g(X_{\beta,t}))dz.

This implies

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle\left|\left(\frac{1}{\alpha-\beta}\int_{X_{\beta,t}}^{X_{\alpha,t}}g(z)dz-g(X_{\beta,t})Y_{\beta,t}\right)\delta\left(\frac{DX_{\alpha,t}}{\|DX_{\beta,t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}[0,T]}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|g\|_{\infty}E\left|\left(\frac{X_{\alpha,t}-X_{\beta,t}}{\alpha-\beta}\right)\delta\left(\frac{DX_{\alpha,t}}{\|DX_{\beta,t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}[0,T]}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle+E\left|\delta\left(\frac{DX_{\alpha,t}}{\|DX_{\beta,t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}[0,T]}}\right)Y_{\beta,t}\int_{0}^{1}(g(X_{\beta,t}-z(X_{\alpha,t}-X_{\beta,t}))-g(X_{\beta,t}))dz\right|.

On the other hand, by the Hölder inequality and Theorem
3.1
we get

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle\left|\left(\frac{X_{\alpha,t}-X_{\beta,t}}{\alpha-\beta}-Y_{\beta,t}\right)\delta\left(\frac{DX_{\alpha,t}}{\|DX_{\beta,t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}[0,T]}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(E\left|\frac{X_{\alpha,t}-X_{\beta,t}}{\alpha-\beta}-Y_{\beta,t}\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(E\left|\delta\left(\frac{DX_{\alpha,t}}{\|DX_{\beta,t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}[0,T]}}\right)\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\to 0\ \mbox{as}\ \alpha\to\beta.

Furthermore, noting that
g(X_{\beta,t}-z(X_{\alpha,t}-X_{\beta,t}))-g(X_{\beta,t})\to 0\ \mbox{a.s. \ as}\ \alpha\to\beta.
Using the dominated convergence theorem we can get

E\left|\delta\left(\frac{DX_{\alpha,t}}{\|DX_{\beta,t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}[0,T]}}\right)Y_{\beta,t}\int_{0}^{1}(g(X_{\beta,t}-z(X_{\alpha,t}-X_{\beta,t}))-g(X_{\beta,t}))dz\right|\to 0\ \mbox{as}\ \alpha\to\beta.

So it holds that

E\left|\left(\frac{1}{\alpha-\beta}\int_{X_{\beta,t}}^{X_{\alpha,t}}g(z)dz-g(X_{\beta,t})Y_{\beta,t}\right)\delta\left(\frac{DX_{\alpha,t}}{\|DX_{\beta,t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}[0,T]}}\right)\right|\to 0\ \mbox{as}\ \alpha\to\beta.

(4.10)

Next, using the Hölder inequality and Propositions
4.4
and
4.5
, we have

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle\left[\frac{(g(X_{\alpha,t})-g(X_{\beta,t}))\langle DX_{\alpha,t}-DX_{\beta,t},DX_{\alpha,t}\rangle_{L^{2}[0,T]}}{(\alpha-\beta)\|DX_{\beta,t}\|_{L^{2}[0,T]}^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq E\left[\frac{|g(X_{\alpha,t})-g(X_{\beta,t})|\|DX_{\alpha,t}-DX_{\beta,t}\|_{L^{2}[0,T]}}{|\alpha-\beta|\|DX_{\beta,t}\|_{L^{2}[0,T]}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq(E|g(X_{\alpha,t})-g(X_{\beta,t})|^{4})^{1/4}\left(\frac{E\|DX_{\alpha,t}-DX_{\beta,t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}[0,T]}}{|\alpha-\beta|^{2}}\right)^{1/2}(E\|DX_{\beta,t}\|_{L^{2}[0,T]}^{-4})^{1/4}

\displaystyle\leq C(E|g(X_{\alpha,t})-g(X_{\beta,t})|^{4})^{1/4}.

Once again, by the dominated convergence theorem, we have

E\left[\frac{(g(X_{\alpha,t})-g(X_{\beta,t}))\langle DX_{\alpha,t}-DX_{\beta,t},DX_{\alpha,t}\rangle_{L^{2}[0,T]}}{(\alpha-\beta)\|DX_{\beta,t}\|_{L^{2}[0,T]}^{2}}\right]\\
\leq C(E|g(X_{\alpha,t})-g(X_{\beta,t})|^{4})^{1/4}\to 0\ \mbox{as}\ \alpha\to\beta.

(4.11)

It is easy to verify that the function
r\mapsto\frac{g(X_{\beta,t})D_{r}X_{\beta,t}}{\|DX_{\beta,t}\|_{L^{2}[0,T]}^{2}}
is continuous. Hence, since
\frac{DX_{\alpha,t}-DX_{\beta,t}}{\alpha-\beta}
weakly converges to
DY_{\beta,t}
in
L^{2}(\Omega\times[0,T]),
we deduce

E\left[g(X_{\beta,t})\left\langle\frac{DX_{\alpha,t}-DX_{\beta,t}}{\alpha-\beta}-DY_{\beta,t},\frac{DX_{\beta,t}}{\|DX_{\beta,t}\|_{L^{2}[0,T]}^{2}}\right\rangle_{L^{2}[0,T]}\right]\to 0\ \mbox{as}\ \alpha\to\beta.

(4.12)

Combining (
4.9
)-(
4.12
), one can obtain

\frac{Eg(X_{\alpha,t})-Eg(X_{\beta,t})}{\alpha-\beta}\to E\left[g(X_{\beta,t})\delta\left(\frac{Y_{\beta,t}DX_{\beta,t}}{\|DX_{\beta,t}\|_{L^{2}[0,T]}}\right)\right]\ \mbox{as}\ \alpha\to\beta.

This completes the proof of Theorem
4.1
.

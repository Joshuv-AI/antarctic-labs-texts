On cliques in hypergraphs
Jun Gao

Abstract
We prove that for any k ≥ 3 k\geq 3 , every k k -uniform hypergraph on n n vertices contains at most n − ω ⁡ ( 1 ) n-\omega(1) different sizes of cliques (maximal complete subgraphs). In particular, the 3-uniform case answers a question of Erdős.



1
Introduction

Given an integer
k\geq 2
and a
k
-uniform hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
, we say a vertex set
S
of
\mathcal{H}
forms a complete graph if every
k
-vertex subset of
S
is an edge in
\mathcal{H}
. A complete subgraph of
\mathcal{H}
is called a clique if it is not contained in any other complete subgraph of
\mathcal{H}
.

In this paper we study how many distinct clique sizes that can occur in a
k
-uniform hypergraph on
n
vertices. We denote by

g(n,k)
the largest possible number of distinct clique sizes in a
k
-uniform hypergraph of
n
vertices.
For graphs (i.e., when
k=2
), such problems were first studied by Moon and Moser
[
4
]
in 1965. They proved (throughout this paper
\log
will denote logarithm to the base 2) that

n-\log n-2\log\log n<g(n,2)\leq n-\lfloor\log n\rfloor.

Subsequently, Erdős
[
2
]
improved the lower bound to

g(n,2)>n-\log n-\log_{*}n-O(1),

Where
\log_{*}n
is the number of iterated logarithms such that
\log\log\dots\log n<1
.
Erdős conjectured that this represented the correct order of magnitude. Spencer
[
5
]
later disproved this by proving that

g(n,2)>n-\log n-O(1).

For hypergraphs, Erdős (see
[
3
]
, or Erdős Problem
\#
775
[
1
]
) showed that there is a 3-uniform hypergraph on
n
vertices which contains
n-\log_{*}n
cliques of distinct size and asked whether it can be shown that there is no 3-uniform hypergraph with
n-C
cliques of distinct sizes, for some constant
C
and large
n
. In this paper, we prove that such a hypergraph does not exist for any
uniformity
k
, thereby answering this question.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any integers
k\geq 3
and
C\geq 0
, there exists a constant
N=N(k,C)
such that for all
n\geq N
, every
n
-vertex
k
-uniform hypergraph contains no more than
n-C
different sizes of cliques.

Definition 2.1

We say a tree

.

(3)

.

The next lemma shows that any

Lemma 2.2
.

There exists a constant

Proof.

.

Assume Lemma

in

Set

.
For each

Let

Claim 2.3

If

is a
(k-1,2^{C}+C+\sum^{i}_{j=1}C_{j})

Next, we prove that

Note that

\deg_{T^{\prime}_{i}}(w_{0})=\sum_{j=1}^{i}\left(\deg_{T_{i}}(v_{x_{j}})-1\right)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{i}\left(\deg_{T}(v_{x_{j}})-1\right)<\sum^{i}_{j=1}C_{j}\leq 2^{\sum^{i}_{j=1}C_{j}},

For each

So

is a
(k-1,2^{C}+C+\sum^{i}_{j=1}C_{j})
-layered tree.

Claim 2.4
.

For each

, there exists a constant
C_{j}=C_{j}(C,k)

\deg_{T}(v_{x_{j}})\leq C_{j}
.

Proof of the claim.

Let

be the maximum integer such that there exists a constant

\deg_{T}(v_{x_{m^{\prime}}})\leq C_{m^{\prime}}

Suppose to the contrary that

Take

By Claim

3
Concluding remarks

If we let
f(n,k)=n-g(n,k)
, then in this paper, we prove that for every
k\geq 3
,
f(n,k)\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
. A natural question arising from this result is: what growth rate can the function
f(n,k)
achieve based on this proof? Erdős showed that
f(n,3)\leq\log_{*}n
. Using our approach, we can obtain

f(n,3)\geq\log\!\bigl(\log_{*}(n+1)\bigr)-1
by applying the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
T
be a
(k,C)
-layered tree with a maximum number of vertices and vertex order
v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{t}
. Then
v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{t}
corresponds to a depth-first search (DFS) of
T
.

Proof.

Suppose, to the contrary, that
v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{t}
is not a DFS of
T
.
Let
j
be the smallest integer that does not satisfy the DFS process.
Since
T
is
(k,C)
-layered, there exists
j^{\prime}<j
such that
v_{j^{\prime}}v_{j}\in T
.
Then there exists an index
i<j
with
\operatorname{dist}(v_{0},v_{i})>\operatorname{dist}(v_{0},v_{j^{\prime}})

and some
i^{\prime}>j
such that
v_{i}v_{i^{\prime}}\in T
.
Let
T^{\prime}
be the tree obtained from
T
by adding a new vertex
w

and a new edge
wv_{i}
, deleting the edge
v_{i}v_{i^{\prime}}
,
and adding the edge
v_{i^{\prime}}v_{j}
.
It is straightforward to verify that
T^{\prime}
is also a
(k,C)
-layered tree
with vertex order

v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{j-1},w,v_{j},\dots,v_{t},

which leads to a contradiction.
∎

We define a sequence
a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{2^{C}}
by setting
a_{0}=1
and, for
i\geq 1
,

a_{i}=2^{a_{i-1}+C}+a_{i-1,}\text{ which implies that $a_{i}+C+1\leq 2^{a_{i-1}+C+1}.$}

By Lemma
3.1
and defination of layerd tree, we know that the maximum number of vertices in a
(2,C)
-layered tree is
a_{2^{C}}
.
Let
\mathcal{H}
be a
3
-uniform hypergraph on
n
vertices that contains
n-C
distinct clique sizes. Then
C\geq 2
.
Define
\log^{(1)}n=\log n
and
\log^{(i+1)}n=\log\bigl(\log^{(i)}n\bigr)
for
i\geq 1
.
It follows from the proof of Theorem
1.1
that
n-C\leq a_{2^{C}}
, which implies that

\log^{(2^{C})}(n+1)\leq C+2.

If
C<\log\!\bigl(\log_{*}(n+1)\bigr)-1
, then

2^{C}\cdot 2<\log_{*}(n+1),

which yields

\log^{(2^{C})}(n+1)>2^{C}\geq C+2,

a contradiction. Therefore,

C\geq\log\!\bigl(\log_{*}(n+1)\bigr)-1.

Nonequilibrium fluctuation relations for non-Gaussian processes
Arthur M. Faria 1,2, Marcus V. S. Bonança 1, Eric Lutz 2

Abstract
Non-Gaussian noise is omnipresent in systems where the central-limit theorem is inapplicable. We here investigate the stochastic thermodynamics of small systems that are described by a general Kramers-Moyal equation that includes both Gaussian and non-Gaussian white noise contributions. We obtain detailed and integral fluctuation relations for the nonequilibrium entropy production of these Markov processes in the regime of weak noise. As an application, we analyze the properties of driven objects that are locally coupled to a heat bath via a finite-range interaction, by considering an overdamped particle that is pulled by a moving harmonic potential. We find that reducing the bath interaction range increases non-Gaussian features, and strongly suppresses the average nonequilibrium entropy production. We further discuss a generalized detailed-balance condition.

Fluctuation relations for general Markov processes.

Let us consider a stochastic process without memory described by a time-homogeneous Markov function. Its sample paths
x_{t}
are generically composed of a deterministic drift plus continuous (Gaussian) diffusion and discrete (Poisson-like) jump contributions
[
6
,
7
]
. For small jumps, the probability density function
p(x,t)
obeys the Kramers-Moyal equation
[
6
,
7
]
,

\partial_{t}p(x,t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-\partial_{x})^{n}}{n!}\left[a_{n}(x)p(x,t)\right],

(1)

with coefficients
a_{n}(x)
. Equation (
1
) involves an infinite number of higher derivatives. The usual Fokker-Planck equation
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
, which is generally studied in stochastic thermodynamics
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
]
, is obtained by keeping the first two terms of the expansion. For a particle confined in a potential
V(x)
, globally coupled to a bath at temperature
T
with overdamped friction
\gamma
, the drift and diffusion coefficients are respectively given by
a_{1}(x)=-V^{\prime}(x)/\gamma
and
a_{2}(x)=2kT/\gamma
, where
k
is the Boltzmann constant. This situation corresponds to Gaussian white noise
[
6
,
7
]
; (Poisson-like) jump contributions are here absent. Keeping higher-order terms in the Kramers-Moyal equation (
1
) leads to discrete jumps and to non-Gaussian white noise. It is useful to remember that, according to Pawula’s theorem, the series may be stopped at the first or second order, or must contain an infinite number of terms, to guarantee a positive probability distribution
[
6
,
7
]
. As commonly done
[
40
,
41
,
42
,
43
]
, we consider the noise to be weak, compared to the drift, to ensure that the problem is mathematically well-behaved. To that end, we introduce a small parameter
\epsilon
, and rescale the Kramers-Moyal coefficients according to
a_{n}(x)\rightarrow\epsilon^{n}a_{n}(x)
.

In order to study the nonequilibrium stochastic thermodynamics of an object described by Eq. (
1
), we drive the potential
V(x,\alpha_{t})
with an arbitrary time-dependent parameter
\alpha_{t}
. For a constant value,
\alpha_{t}=\alpha
, we assume that the probability distribution relaxes to a unique steady state,
p_{s}(x)=N\exp[-\phi(x,\alpha)]
, where
N
is a normalization factor. In the limit
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
, we explicitly find (Supplemental Material
[
44
]
)

\displaystyle\!\phi(x,\alpha)=-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{2^{n}}{\epsilon n!}\int_{0}^{x}\!\!dy\frac{a_{n}(y)}{a_{2}^{n}(y)}[V^{\prime}(y,\alpha)/\gamma]^{n-1}.

(2)

It is advantageous to distinguish Gaussian (
n=2
) and non-Gaussian (
n\geq 3
) contributions to
p_{s}(x)
, and write
\phi(x,\alpha)=\phi^{G}(x,\alpha)+\phi^{NG}(x,\alpha)
.
The Gaussian part
\phi^{G}(x,\alpha)
only depends on the diffusion coefficient
a_{2}(x)
, whereas the non-Gaussian element
\phi^{NG}(x,\alpha)
contains all the higher-order coefficients
a_{n}(x)
(
n\geq 3
).

We next derive detailed and integral fluctuation theorems for the nonequilibrium entropy production by using a path integral representation of the solution of Eq. (
1
)
[
38
,
39
]
. We denote by
X=\{x\}^{\tau}_{-\tau}
a trajectory of the driven system that starts at
t=-\tau
and ends at
t=+\tau
. We furthermore consider a forward process
\alpha_{t}^{F}
for which the parameter
\alpha_{t}
is varied from an initial value
\alpha_{-\tau}^{F}=\alpha_{i}
to a final value
\alpha_{+\tau}^{F}=\alpha_{f}
. By extending the analysis of Refs.
[
45
,
46
]
from a Fokker-Planck dynamics to the full Kramers-Moyal expansion (
1
), the conditional probability
P^{F}[X|x_{-\tau}]
of observing a trajectory
X
given the initial microstate
x_{-\tau}
may be expressed as

\displaystyle P^{F}[X|x_{-\tau}]=\mathcal{N}\exp\left[-\int_{-\tau}^{\tau}dt^{\prime}\,S(x_{t},\dot{x}_{t};\alpha_{t}^{F})\right],

with a constant
\mathcal{N}
and the Onsager-Machlup functional
S

[
47
]
. We concretely obtain (Supplemental Material
[
44
]
)

\displaystyle\!\!\!S(x_{t},\dot{x}_{t};\alpha_{t}^{F})=\!\sum_{n=3}^{\infty}\frac{a_{n}(x_{t})}{n!}\left[\frac{\dot{x}_{t}+V^{\prime}(x_{t},\alpha_{t}^{F})/\gamma}{\epsilon a_{2}(x_{t})}\right]^{n}\hskip-1.42262pt.\hskip-5.69054pt

(3)

It is again convenient to write
S
as a sum of a Gaussian (
n=2
) and a non-Gaussian (
n\geq 3
) term,
S=S^{G}+S^{NG}
.
We may likewise consider the time-reversed process with driving function
\alpha_{t}^{R}=-\alpha_{-t}^{F}
. The associated conditional probability,
P^{R}[X^{\dagger}|x^{\dagger}_{-\tau}]
, for the time-reversed trajectory
X^{\dagger}=\{x^{\dagger}\}^{\tau}_{-\tau}
, with the variable
x^{\dagger}_{t}=x_{-t}
, reads

\displaystyle P^{R}[X^{\dagger}|x^{\dagger}_{-\tau}]=

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}\exp\left[-\int_{-\tau}^{\tau}dt^{\prime}\,S^{\dagger}(x_{t},\dot{x}_{t};\alpha_{t}^{F})\right],

(4)

where we have defined the conjugate Onsager-Machlup functional
S^{\dagger}(x_{t},\dot{x}_{t};\alpha_{t})=S(x_{t},-\dot{x}_{t};\alpha_{t})
. The ratio of the two conditional probabilities is accordingly given by

\displaystyle\frac{P^{F}[X|x_{-\tau}]}{P^{R}[X^{\dagger}|x^{\dagger}_{-\tau}]}

\displaystyle\underset{\epsilon\to 0}{=}\exp\left[-\frac{2}{\epsilon}\int_{-\tau}^{\tau}dt\,\dot{x}_{t}\frac{V^{\prime}/\gamma}{a_{2}}\right]

(5)

\displaystyle\hskip-17.07164pt\times{\exp\left[-\frac{2}{\epsilon}\sum_{n=3}^{\infty}\frac{n}{n!}\int_{-\tau}^{\tau}dt\,\dot{x}_{t}\frac{a_{n}}{a_{2}^{n}}(V^{\prime}/\gamma)^{n-1}\right]}\hskip-1.42262pt,\hskip-2.84526pt

where higher orders in
\epsilon
have been neglected (Supplemental Material
[
44
]
). Assuming that the system is initially in the steady state
p_{s}(x_{-\tau},\alpha_{i})
, the net probability of observing the trajectory
X
for the forward process is
P^{F}(X)=p_{s}(x_{-\tau},\alpha_{i})P^{F}[X|x_{-\tau}]
. For the reversed process, we similarly have
P^{R}(X^{\dagger})=p_{s}(x_{\tau},\alpha_{f})P^{R}[X^{\dagger}|x^{\dagger}_{-\tau}]
.
Further noting that
p_{s}(x_{-\tau},\alpha_{i})/p_{s}(x_{\tau},\alpha_{f})=\exp\left(\Delta\phi\right)
, with
\Delta\phi=\int_{-\tau}^{\tau}dt\,\dot{\alpha}_{t}^{F}\partial_{\alpha}\phi+\dot{x}_{t}\partial_{x}\phi
, we arrive at the ratio

\displaystyle\frac{P^{F}(X)}{P^{R}(X^{\dagger})}

\displaystyle=\frac{p_{s}(x_{-\tau},\alpha_{i})P^{F}[X|x_{-\tau}]}{p_{s}(x_{\tau},\alpha_{f})P^{R}[X^{\dagger}|x^{\dagger}_{-\tau}]}=e^{\Sigma},

(6)

with the random nonequilibrium entropy production (Supplemental Material
[
44
]
)

\displaystyle\Sigma=-\int_{-\tau}^{\tau}dt\,\dot{\alpha}^{F}_{t}\frac{\partial_{\alpha}p_{s}}{p_{s}}+\int_{-\tau}^{\tau}dt\,\dot{x}_{t}\partial_{x}\phi^{NG}.

(7)

We may eventually obtain a detailed fluctuation relation by introducing the distribution
\rho^{F}(\Sigma)=\int{\cal D}XP^{F}(X)\delta(\Sigma-\Sigma^{F}(X))
for an ensemble of forward process realizations. Using Eq. (
6
), we find

\displaystyle\rho^{F}(\Sigma)=e^{\Sigma}\rho^{R}(-\Sigma),

(8)

with
\rho^{R}(-\Sigma)=\int{\cal D}X^{\dagger}P^{R}(X^{\dagger})\delta(\Sigma+\Sigma^{R}(X^{\dagger}))
. An integral fluctuation relation follows, as usual, after integration over the forward distribution,
\langle\exp(-\Sigma)\rangle=1
, which directly implies
\langle\Sigma\rangle\geq 0
via Jensen’s inequality. The above expressions are generalizations of the standard nonequilibrium equalities by Crooks
[
48
]
and by Jarzynski
[
49
]
for general driven Markov processes described by the full Kramers-Moyal equation (
1
) in the low-noise limit; they include contributions of both Gaussian and non-Gaussian (white noise) fluctuations.

Modified detailed-balance condition.

Since the probability current vanishes in a stationary state, individual transition probabilities (from and to a given state) must balance themselves. This key property of a stationary system is known as detailed-balance condition
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
. The presence of non-Gaussian noise will affect that condition. To see this, we write the evolution of the density probability
p(x,t)
of a general Markov process in the form of a differential Chapman-Kolmogorov equation
[
6
,
7
]

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\partial_{t}p(x,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!-\partial_{x}[a_{1}(x)p(x,t)]+\frac{1}{2}\partial^{2}_{x}[a_{2}(x)p(x,t)]

(12)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\!\!\int dx^{\prime}[W(x|x^{\prime})p(x^{\prime},t)-W(x^{\prime}|x)p(x,t)],

with transition probabilities
W(x|x^{\prime})
and
W(x^{\prime}|x)
. Stationarity,
\partial_{t}p_{s}(x)=0
, then implies the detailed balance equation
W(x|x^{\prime})p_{s}(x^{\prime})=W(x^{\prime}|x)p_{s}(x)
for the transition probabilities
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
. This leads to

\frac{W(x|x^{\prime})}{W(x^{\prime}|x)}=e^{-[\phi^{G}(x)-\phi^{G}(x^{\prime})]}e^{-[\phi^{NG}(x)-\phi^{NG}(x^{\prime})]},

(13)

with the stationary probability distribution
p_{s}(x)=N\exp[-\phi^{G}(x)-\phi^{NG}(x)]
. For a general Markov process, the detailed-balance condition hence includes a contribution from the non-Gaussian potential
\phi^{NG}(x)
. We note that
W(x|x^{\prime})
is related to the dynamic structure factor of the environment
[
15
]
, that characterizes its density fluctuations. It is thus directly accessible in a suitable scattering experiment
[
57
,
58
]
, and would allow one to determine the characteristics of the non-Gaussian reservoir. Figure 4 shows the function
\ln[{W(x|x^{\prime})}/{W(x^{\prime}|x)}]
for the example of the dragged harmonic particle (
9
), with
x^{\prime}=x+1/l^{2}
, a stochastic jump from
x
away, as a function of
x
for several values of the length scale
l
. We note that non-Gaussian noise increases the ratio of the transition probabilities, compared to the Gaussian case.

Conclusions.

The coupling to a Gaussian heat bath is in many cases an idealized approximation that breaks down when the central-limit theorem does not hold. Using a path integral (Onsager-Machlup) method, we have derived nonequilibrium fluctuation relations for general non-Gaussian Markov processes that are described by a full Kramers-Moyal equation in the weak noise regime. We have applied these findings to examine the thermodynamic consequences of a finite-range bath interaction. We have found that a reduced reservoir coupling range enhances non-Gaussianity and, at the same time, suppresses the average entropy production below its Gaussian value. It additionally affects the detailed-balance condition. Our results extend stochastic thermodynamics to non-Gaussian Markov processes, and signal that the assumption of global bath coupling overestimates irreversible dissipation for small bath correlation lengths.

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dyP_{1}(y)=0
. We may set the constant
C_{2}=0
as it does not affect the derivation of the fluctuation theorem. We therefore obtain

\displaystyle p_{s}(x)=Ne^{\int_{0}^{x}dy\frac{2\epsilon a_{1}(y)-\epsilon^{2}\partial_{y}a_{2}(y)}{\epsilon^{2}a_{2}(y)}}\left(1-\sum_{n=3}^{\infty}\frac{(-\epsilon)^{n}}{n!}\int_{0}^{x}dy\frac{2}{\epsilon^{2}a_{2}(y)}\frac{\partial_{y}^{n-1}\left[a_{n}(y)P_{0}\right]}{P_{0}}\right),

(S8)

with
N=e^{C_{1}}
a normalization factor. We additionally note that in the low-noise limit, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{2}\left[a_{3}P_{0}\right]=a_{3}\partial_{x}^{2}\left[P_{0}\right]+2\partial_{x}\left[a_{3}\right]\partial_{x}\left[P_{0}\right]+P_{0}\partial_{x}^{2}\left[a_{3}\right]\ \underset{\epsilon\to 0}{\sim}\ a_{3}\partial_{x}^{2}\left[P_{0}\right],

(S9)

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{3}\left[a_{4}P_{0}\right]=a_{4}\partial_{x}^{3}\left[P_{0}\right]+3\partial_{x}\left[a_{4}\right]\partial_{x}^{2}\left[P_{0}\right]+3\partial_{x}^{2}\left[a_{4}\right]\partial_{x}\left[P_{0}\right]+P_{0}\partial_{x}^{3}\left[a_{4}\right]\ \underset{\epsilon\to 0}{\sim}\ a_{4}\partial_{x}^{3}\left[P_{0}\right],

(S10)

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt\vdots

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{n}\left[a_{n}P_{0}\right]=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\begin{pmatrix}n\\
k\end{pmatrix}a_{n}^{(n-k)}(x)P_{0}^{(k)}\ \underset{\epsilon\to 0}{\sim}\ a_{n}\partial_{x}^{n}\left[P_{0}\right],

(S11)

since

\displaystyle\partial_{x}\left[P_{0}\right]=\frac{2\epsilon a_{1}(x)-\epsilon^{2}\partial_{x}a_{2}(x)}{\epsilon^{2}a_{2}(x)}P_{0}\ \underset{\epsilon\to 0}{\sim}\ \frac{2a_{1}(x)}{\epsilon a_{2}(x)}P_{0},

(S12)

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{2}\left[P_{0}\right]=\left(\partial_{x}\left[\frac{2\epsilon a_{1}(x)-\epsilon^{2}\partial_{x}a_{2}(x)}{\epsilon^{2}a_{2}(x)}\right]+\left(\frac{2\epsilon a_{1}(x)-\epsilon^{2}\partial_{x}a_{2}(x)}{\epsilon^{2}a_{2}(x)}\right)^{2}\right)P_{0}\ \underset{\epsilon\to 0}{\sim}\ \left(\frac{2a_{1}(x)}{\epsilon a_{2}(x)}\right)^{2}P_{0},

(S13)

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt\vdots

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{n}\left[P_{0}\right]\underset{\epsilon\to 0}{\sim}\ \left(\frac{2a_{1}(x)}{\epsilon a_{2}(x)}\right)^{n}P_{0},

(S14)

or, in other words,
\ \epsilon^{n}\partial_{x}^{n}\left[P_{0}\right]\,\sim\,\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{0})
. Equation (
S1
) may then by expressed as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\left[p_{s}\right]=0

\displaystyle=-\epsilon a_{1}(x)p_{s}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}\partial_{x}\left[a_{2}(x)p_{s}\right]+\sum_{n=3}^{\infty}\frac{(-\epsilon)^{n}}{n!}\partial_{x}^{n-1}\left[a_{n}(x)p_{s}\right],

(S15)

\displaystyle\underset{\epsilon\to 0}{\sim}-\epsilon a_{1}(x)p_{s}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}a_{2}(x)\partial_{x}\left[p_{s}\right]+\sum_{n=3}\frac{(-\epsilon)^{n}}{n!}a_{n}(x)\partial_{x}^{n-1}\left[p_{s}\right].

(S16)

Equation (
S8
) may accordingly be simplified to

\displaystyle p_{s}(x)

\displaystyle\underset{\epsilon\to 0}{\sim}Ne^{\int_{0}^{x}dy\frac{2a_{1}(y)}{\epsilon a_{2}(y)}}\left(1-\sum_{n=3}^{\infty}\frac{(-\epsilon)^{n}}{n!}\int_{0}^{x}dy\frac{2}{\epsilon^{2}a_{2}(y)}\frac{\partial_{y}^{n-1}\left[a_{n}(y)P_{0}\right]}{P_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle=N\exp(\frac{2}{\epsilon}\int_0^x dy \frac{ a_1(y)}{ a_2(y)} + \frac{2}{\epsilon}\sum_{n=3}^{\infty}(-1)^{n-1}\frac{2^{n-1}}{ n!}\int_0^x dy \frac{ a_n(y)}{ a_2^n(y)} a_1^{n-1}(y)).

(S17)

Introducing now the decomposition,
\phi(x)=\phi^{G}(x)+\phi^{NG}(x)
, where
\phi^{G}(x)
and
\phi^{NG}(x)
represent the respective Gaussian and non-Gaussian steady-state contributions, we find

\displaystyle p_{s}(x)

\displaystyle\underset{\epsilon\to 0}{\sim}N\exp[-\phi(x)]={N}\exp[-\phi^{G}(x)]\exp[-\phi^{NG}(x)].

(S18)

with

\displaystyle\phi^{G}(x,\alpha)=\frac{2}{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{x}dy\frac{1}{a_{2}(y)}V^{\prime}(y,\alpha)/\gamma,

(S19)

\displaystyle\phi^{NG}(x,\alpha)=-\frac{2}{\epsilon}\sum_{n=3}^{\infty}\frac{2^{n-1}}{n!}\int_{0}^{x}dy\frac{a_{n}(y)}{a_{2}^{n}(y)}\left(V^{\prime}(y,\alpha)/\gamma\right)^{n-1},

(S20)

where we have taken
a_{1}(x)=-V^{\prime}(x,\alpha)/\gamma
, with the confining potential
V(x,\alpha)
(driven by the external, time-dependent parameter
\alpha
) and the damping coefficient
\gamma
.

II
II. Ratio of Forward and Reversed Probabilities in the Low-Noise regime

We here derive the path propagator for the Kramers-Moyal expansion in the weak-noise limit. We begin with the propagator corresponding to Eq. (
S1
) for small time jumps (
\delta t\to 0
) which is given by
[
4
]

\displaystyle P[X|x_{-\tau}]=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dk\,e^{\Gamma(k)/\epsilon},

(S21)

with

\displaystyle\Gamma(k)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\tau}^{\tau}dt\,\epsilon\left(\dot{x}_{t}-\epsilon a_{1}(x_{t})\right)ik+\epsilon\left(\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}a_{2}(x_{t})(ik)^{2}+\sum_{n=3}^{\infty}\frac{(-\epsilon)^{n}}{n!}a_{n}(x_{t})(ik)^{n}\right),

(S22)

where we denote by
X=\{x\}^{\tau}_{-\tau}
a trajectory of the system that starts at
t=-\tau
and ends at
t=+\tau
, and
\alpha_{t}
is the external driving parameter. To obtain the conditional probability
P[X|x_{-\tau}]
given the initial microstate
x_{-\tau}
, we apply the saddle-point method considering the low noise limit
\epsilon\to 0

[
8
]
. It is important to notice that the dependence on
x_{t}
comes from the solution
k=k(x_{t})
obtained from
\partial_{k}\Gamma(k)=0
. Hence, from Eq. (
S22
), we have

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{t}-\epsilon a_{1}=-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-\epsilon)^{n}}{(n-1)!}a_{n}(ik)^{n-1}.

(S23)

Pawula’s theorem says that higher-order terms in the Kramers-Moyal expansion may violate the non-negativity of the probability distribution
[
4
]
. However, treating these higher orders as small perturbations, with
\epsilon^{2}a_{2}\gg\epsilon^{n}a_{n}
for
n>3
allows for approximations that preserve the calculation of the moments
[
4
]
. To leading order, Eq. (
S23
) can be approximated as
\dot{x}_{t}-\epsilon a_{1}\approx-\epsilon^{2}a_{2}ik
, resulting in

\displaystyle P[X|x_{-\tau}]

\displaystyle\underset{\epsilon\to 0}{\sim}\mathcal{N}\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\tau}^{\tau}dt\,a_{2}\left(\frac{\dot{x}_{t}-\epsilon a_{1}}{\epsilon a_{2}}\right)^{2}+\sum_{n=3}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\int_{-\tau}^{\tau}dt\ a_{n}\left(\frac{\dot{x}_{t}-\epsilon a_{1}}{\epsilon a_{2}}\right)^{n}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 5.69054pt=\mathcal{N}\exp\left[-\int_{-\tau}^{\tau}dt\,S(x_{t},\dot{x}_{t};\alpha_{t})\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 5.69054pt=\mathcal{N}\exp\left[-\int_{-\tau}^{\tau}dt\,S^{G}(x_{t},\dot{x}_{t};\alpha_{t})\right]\exp\left[\int_{-\tau}^{\tau}dt\,S^{NG}(x_{t},\dot{x}_{t};\alpha_{t})\right],

(S24)

where
\mathcal{N}
is again a normalization factor. The generalized Onsager-Machlup functionals are here given by

\displaystyle S^{G}(x_{t},\dot{x}_{t};\alpha_{t})=\frac{a_{2}}{2}\left(\frac{\dot{x}_{t}-\epsilon a_{1}}{\epsilon a_{2}}\right)^{2},

(S25)

\displaystyle S^{NG}(x_{t},\dot{x}_{t};\alpha_{t})=\sum_{n=3}^{\infty}\frac{a_{n}}{n!}\left(\frac{\dot{x}_{t}-\epsilon a_{1}}{\epsilon a_{2}}\right)^{n},

(S26)

and
S=S^{G}+S^{NG}
. In order to proceed, we introduce a forward process denoted as
\alpha_{t}^{F}
, where the driving parameter is varied from an initial value
\alpha_{-\tau}^{F}=\alpha_{i}
to a final value
\alpha^{F}_{+\tau}=\alpha_{f}
. We additionally consider the time-reversed process, denoted as
\alpha_{t}^{R}=-\alpha_{-t}^{F}
. Based on the expression (
S24
), the conditional probability of observing a trajectory starting at
x_{-\tau}
for the forward process reads

\displaystyle P^{F}[X|x_{-\tau}]\underset{\epsilon\to 0}{\sim}

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}\exp\left[-\int_{-\tau}^{\tau}dt^{\prime}\,S(x_{t},\dot{x}_{t};\alpha_{t}^{F})\right].

(S27)

Analogously, the propagator for the reversed process
P^{R}[X^{\dagger}|x^{\dagger}_{-\tau}]
, being the time-reversed trajectory
X^{\dagger}=\{x^{\dagger}\}^{\tau}_{-\tau}
with
x^{\dagger}_{t}=x_{-t}
, is

\displaystyle P^{R}[X^{\dagger}|x^{\dagger}_{-\tau}]\underset{\epsilon\to 0}{\sim}

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}\exp\left[-\int_{-\tau}^{\tau}dt^{\prime}\,S(x_{t}^{\dagger},\dot{x}_{t}^{\dagger};\alpha_{t}^{R})\right]=\mathcal{N}\exp\left[-\int_{-\tau}^{\tau}dt^{\prime}\,S^{\dagger}(x_{t},\dot{x}_{t};\alpha_{t}^{F})\right],

with
S^{\dagger}(x_{t},\dot{x}_{t};\alpha_{t})=S(x_{t},-\dot{x}_{t};\alpha_{t})
. We may now proceed to derive the fluctuation theorem for the generalized entropy production. We first evaluate the ratio of path propagators (
S24
) as

\displaystyle\frac{P^{F}[X|x_{-\tau}]}{P^{R}[X^{\dagger}|x^{\dagger}_{\tau}]}

\displaystyle=\exp\left({-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\int_{-\tau}^{\tau}dt\,a_{n}\,\mathcal{F}_{n}(\dot{x}_{t},x_{t},\alpha_{t})}\right),

(S28)

where
\mathcal{F}_{n}(\dot{x}_{t},x_{t},\alpha_{t})=\left(\left(\dot{x}_{t}-\epsilon a_{1}\right)^{n}-(-1)^{n}\left(\dot{x}_{t}+\epsilon a_{1}\right)^{n}\right)/(\epsilon a_{2})^{n}
. In the low-noise limit
\epsilon\to 0
, we have
\mathcal{F}_{n}\sim 2n(-1)^{n-1}\dot{x}_{t}a_{1}^{n-1}/(\epsilon(a_{2})^{n})
, since

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}_{2}=-\frac{2}{\epsilon}\frac{2}{a_{2}^{2}}\dot{x}_{t}a_{1}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{3}=\frac{\dot{x}_{t}}{\epsilon a_{2}}\left(6\left(\frac{\epsilon a_{1}}{\epsilon a_{2}}\right)^{2}+2\left(\frac{\dot{x}_{t}}{\epsilon a_{2}}\right)^{2}\right)\underset{\epsilon\to 0}{\sim}\frac{2}{\epsilon}\frac{3}{a_{2}^{3}}\dot{x}_{t}a_{1}^{2},

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{4}=-\frac{8}{\epsilon a_{2}}\left(\dot{x}_{t}\left(\frac{\epsilon a_{1}}{\epsilon a_{2}}\right)^{3}+\epsilon a_{1}\left(\frac{\dot{x}_{t}}{\epsilon a_{2}}\right)^{3}\right)\underset{\epsilon\to 0}{\sim}-\frac{2}{\epsilon}\frac{4}{a_{2}^{4}}\dot{x}_{t}a_{1}^{3},

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\vdots

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{n}\underset{\epsilon\to 0}{\sim}(-1)^{n-1}\frac{2n}{\epsilon a_{2}^{n}}\dot{x}_{t}a_{1}^{n-1},

(S29)

where
\left|\epsilon a_{1}/\epsilon a_{2}\right|>\left|1/\epsilon a_{2}\right|
, which also holds in the low-noise limit.Using again
a_{1}(x_{t})=-V^{\prime}(x_{t},\alpha_{t})/\gamma
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{P^{F}[X|x_{-\tau}]}{P^{R}[X^{\dagger}|x^{\dagger}_{\tau}]}

\displaystyle\underset{\epsilon\to 0}{\sim}\exp\left[-\frac{2}{\epsilon}\int_{-\tau}^{\tau}dt\,\dot{x}_{t}\frac{V^{\prime}/\gamma}{a_{2}}+\frac{2}{\epsilon}\sum_{n=3}^{\infty}\frac{n}{n!}\int_{-\tau}^{\tau}dt\,\dot{x}_{t}\frac{a_{n}}{a_{2}^{n}}\left(V^{\prime}/\gamma\right)^{n-1}\right].

(S30)

On the other hand, the ratio between the forward and reversed steady states (
S17
) gives

\displaystyle\frac{p_{s}(x_{-\tau},\alpha_{-\tau})}{p_{s}(x_{\tau},\alpha_{\tau})}=\exp\left(\Delta\phi\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\int_{-\tau}^{\tau}dt\,\left[\dot{\alpha}_{t}^{F}\partial_{\alpha}\phi+\dot{x}_{t}\partial_{x}\phi\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\int_{-\tau}^{\tau}dt\,\dot{\alpha}_{t}^{F}\partial_{a}\phi+\frac{2}{\epsilon}\int_{-\tau}^{\tau}dt\,\dot{x}_{t}\frac{V^{\prime}/\gamma}{a_{2}}-\frac{2}{\epsilon}\sum_{n=3}^{\infty}\frac{2^{n-1}}{n!}\int_{-\tau}^{\tau}dt\,\dot{x}_{t}\frac{a_{n}}{a_{2}^{n}}\left(V^{\prime}/\gamma\right)^{n-1}\right).

(S31)

Combining Eqs. (
S30
) and (
S31
), the ratio between the unconditional probabilities further reads

\displaystyle\frac{P^{F}(X)}{P^{R}(X^{\dagger})}

\displaystyle=\frac{p_{s}(x_{-\tau},\alpha_{-\tau})P^{F}[X|x_{-\tau}]}{p_{s}(x_{\tau},\alpha_{\tau})P^{R}[X^{\dagger}|x^{\dagger}_{\tau}]}

\displaystyle\underset{\epsilon\to 0}{\sim}\exp(\int_{-\tau}^{\tau} dt\, \dot{ a}_t^F \partial_{ a}\phi){\exp(-\frac{2}{\epsilon}\sum_{n=3}^{\infty}\frac{(2^{n-1} + n)}{ n!}\int_{-\tau}^{\tau} dt\, \dot{x}_t\frac{ a_n(x_t)}{a_2^n(x_t)} (V'(x_t, \alpha_{t})/\gamma)^{n-1})}.

(S32)

We finally obtain the general fluctuation relation

\displaystyle\frac{P^{F}(X)}{P^{R}(X^{\dagger})}

\displaystyle=\exp(-\int_{-\tau}^{\tau} dt\, \dot{ a}_t^F \frac{\partial_{\alpha}p_{s}}{p_{s}})\exp(\int_{-\tau}^{\tau} dt\, \dot{x}_t \partial_x \phi^{NG})

(S33)

\displaystyle{=\frac{2}{\epsilon\gamma}\int_{-\tau}^{\tau}dt\,\dot{\alpha}_{t}^{F}\frac{1}{a_{2}(x_{t})}V^{\prime}(x_{t},\alpha)-\frac{2}{\epsilon}\sum_{n=3}^{\infty}\frac{\left(2/\gamma\right)^{n-1}}{n!}\int_{-\tau}^{\tau}dt\frac{a_{n}(x_{t})}{a_{2}(x_{t})^{n}}\left(\dot{\alpha}_{t}^{F}+\dot{x}_{t}\right)V^{\prime}(x_{t},\alpha)^{n-1}}.

(S34)

since for
n\geq 3
and
\gamma\gg 1
,
{e^{-(2^{n-1}+n)/(n!\gamma^{n-1})}\sim e^{-2^{n-1}/(n!\gamma^{n-1})}}
. Equation (
S33
) is valid for a generic Kramers-Moyal equation and any external potential
V(x)
(in the weak-noise regime).

III
III. Numerical simulation of the non-Gaussian process

In order to simulate non-Gaussian processes, it is convenient to map the Kramers-Moyal equation onto a Langevin equation that is easily solved numerically. We here introduce a Langevin equation whose moments are identical to those of the Kramers-Moyal equation (11) of the main text. It has the following form
[
11
]

\displaystyle\gamma dx=-V^{\prime}(x)dt+\sqrt{\nu}d\xi^{G}(t)+\eta(d\xi_{\gamma}^{P}(t)+d\xi_{\nu l^{2}}^{*P}(t)),

(S35)

where the white noise term
\xi^{G}(t)
describes a Gaussian diffusion process with unit variance, while
\xi_{z}^{P}(t)
and
\xi_{z^{\prime}}^{*P}(t)
respectively represent a Poisson and a modified Poisson process with jump rates
z
and
z^{\prime}
. The modified Poisson process is taken to have zero mean and variance,
\langle d\xi_{\nu l^{2}}^{*P}(t)\rangle=\langle(d\xi_{\nu l^{2}}^{*P}(t))^{2}\rangle=0
, and
\langle(d\xi_{\nu l^{2}}^{*P}(t))^{2n}\rangle=\nu l^{2}dt
, for
n\geq 2
.
The jump amplitude, denoted as
\eta
, is independent of the system’s state and follows a normal distribution with zero average and variance
l^{-2}
. The constant diffusion rate is moreover given by
\nu=2\gamma kT
, with the damping coefficient
\gamma
.

The Kramers-Moyal coefficients are directly related to the jump parameters
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
(see also Refs.
[
10
,
11
]
) via

a_{n}(x)=\lim_{{dt\to 0}}\frac{1}{dt}\langle(x(t+dt)-x(t))^{n}|_{x(t)=x}\rangle\sim\frac{1}{dt}\langle(dx(t))^{n}|_{x(t)=x}\rangle.

(S36)

We explicitly find for the first moment

\displaystyle\left<dx(t)|_{x(t)=x}\right>

\displaystyle=\left<\left(-\frac{V^{\prime}(x)}{\gamma}dt+\frac{\sqrt{\nu}}{\gamma}d\xi^{G}+\frac{\eta}{\gamma}\left(d\xi_{\gamma}^{P}+d\xi_{\nu l^{2}}^{*P}(t)\right)\right)\right>

(S37)

\displaystyle=-\frac{V^{\prime}(x)}{\gamma}dt+\frac{\sqrt{\nu}}{\gamma}\left<d\xi^{G}\right>+\frac{1}{\gamma}\left<\eta\right>\left<(d\xi_{\gamma}^{P}+d\xi_{\nu l^{2}}^{*P}(t))\right>\underset{dt\to 0}{\sim}-\frac{V^{\prime}(x)}{\gamma}dt,

(S38)

while the second moment reads

\displaystyle\left<dx(t)^{2}|_{x(t)=x}\right>

\displaystyle=\left<\left(-\frac{V^{\prime}(x)}{\gamma}dt+\frac{\sqrt{\nu}}{\gamma}d\xi^{G}+\frac{\eta}{\gamma}\left(d\xi_{\gamma}^{P}+d\xi_{\nu l^{2}}^{*P}(t)\right)\right)^{2}\right>

(S39)

\displaystyle=\frac{(V^{\prime}(x))^{2}}{\gamma^{2}}dt^{2}+\frac{\nu}{\gamma^{2}}\left<(d\xi^{G})^{2}\right>+\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}}\left<\eta^{2}\right>\left<(d\xi_{\gamma}^{P}+d\xi_{\nu l^{2}}^{*P}(t))^{2}\right>

(S40)

\displaystyle-2\frac{V^{\prime}(x)}{\gamma}\frac{\sqrt{\nu}}{\gamma}\left<d\xi^{G}\right>dt-2\frac{V^{\prime}(x)}{\gamma^{2}}\left<\eta\right>\left<(d\xi_{\gamma}^{P}+d\xi_{\nu l^{2}}^{*P}(t))\right>dt+2\frac{\sqrt{\nu}}{\gamma^{2}}\left<\eta\right>\left<d\xi^{G}\right>\left<(d\xi_{\gamma}^{P}+d\xi_{\nu l^{2}}^{*P}(t))\right>

(S41)

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69054pt\underset{dt\to 0}{\sim}\frac{\nu}{\gamma^{2}}\left<(d\xi^{G})^{2}\right>+\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}}\left<\eta^{2}\right>\left<(d\xi_{\gamma}^{P})^{2}\right>=\left(\frac{\nu}{\gamma^{2}}+\frac{1}{\gamma l^{2}}\right)dt,

(S42)

where we have used
\langle\eta\rangle=0
and
\langle\eta^{2n}\rangle=(2n)!/(2^{n}l^{2n}n!)

[
10
,
11
]
. We moreover have (i) for Gaussian noise,
\langle d\xi^{G}\rangle=0
,
\langle(d\xi^{G})^{2}\rangle=dt
, and
\langle(d\xi^{G})^{n}\rangle=0
, for
n\geq 3
, whereas (ii) for Poisson noise
\langle(d\xi_{z}^{P})^{n}\rangle=zdt
as
dt\to 0

[
10
,
11
]
. We further assume the independence of Wiener, Poisson, and
\eta
processes, that is,
\langle d\xi^{G}\eta d\xi_{\gamma}^{P}\rangle=\langle d\xi^{G}\rangle\langle\eta\rangle\langle d\xi_{\gamma}^{P}\rangle
.

The third moment is additionally given by

\displaystyle\left<dx(t)^{3}|_{x(t)=x}\right>

\displaystyle=\left<\left(-\frac{V^{\prime}(x)}{\gamma}dt+\frac{\sqrt{\nu}}{\gamma}d\xi^{G}+\frac{\eta}{\gamma}\left(d\xi_{\gamma}^{P}+d\xi_{\nu l^{2}}^{*P}\right)\right)^{3}\right>

(S43)

\displaystyle=-\frac{(V^{\prime}(x))^{3}}{\gamma^{3}}dt^{3}+\frac{\nu^{3/2}}{\gamma^{3}}\left<(d\xi^{G})^{3}\right>+\frac{1}{\gamma^{3}}\left<\eta^{3}\right>\left<(d\xi_{\gamma}^{P}+d\xi_{\nu l^{2}}^{*P})^{3}\right>

(S44)

\displaystyle\hskip 11.66563pt+3\left(\frac{(V^{\prime}(x))^{2}}{\gamma^{2}}\frac{\sqrt{\nu}}{\gamma}\left<d\xi^{G}\right>dt^{2}-\frac{V^{\prime}(x)}{\gamma}\frac{\nu}{\gamma^{2}}\left<(d\xi^{G})^{2}\right>dt\right)

(S45)

\displaystyle\hskip 11.66563pt+3\left(\frac{(V^{\prime}(x))^{2}}{\gamma^{2}}\frac{1}{\gamma}\left<\eta\right>\left<(d\xi_{\gamma}^{P}+d\xi_{\nu l^{2}}^{*P})\right>dt^{2}-\frac{V^{\prime}(x)}{\gamma}\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}}\left<\eta^{2}\right>\left<(d\xi_{\gamma}^{P}+d\xi_{\nu l^{2}}^{*P})^{2}\right>dt\right)

(S46)

\displaystyle\hskip 11.66563pt+3\left(\frac{\nu}{\gamma^{2}}\left<(d\xi^{G})^{2}\right>\frac{1}{\gamma}\left<\eta\right>\left<(d\xi_{\gamma}^{P}+d\xi_{\nu l^{2}}^{*P})\right>+\frac{\sqrt{\nu}}{\gamma}\left<d\xi^{G}\right>\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}}\left<\eta^{2}\right>\left<(d\xi_{\gamma}^{P}+d\xi_{\nu l^{2}}^{*P})^{2}\right>\right)

(S47)

\displaystyle\hskip 11.66563pt-6\frac{V^{\prime}(x)}{\gamma}\frac{\sqrt{\nu}}{\gamma}\left<d\xi^{G}\right>\frac{1}{\gamma}\left<\eta\right>\left<\left(d\xi_{\gamma}^{P}+d\xi_{\nu l^{2}}^{*P}\right)\right>dt\underset{dt\to 0}{\sim}0.

(S48)

Finally, for
n\geq 4
, we find

\displaystyle\left<dx(t)^{2n-1}|_{x(t)=x}\right>=0

(S49)

\displaystyle\left<dx(t)^{2n}|_{x(t)=x}\right>=\left<\eta^{2n}\right>\left<(d\xi_{\nu l^{2}}^{P})^{2n}\right>+\frac{1}{\gamma^{2n}}\left<\eta^{2n}\right>\left<(d\xi_{\lambda}^{P})^{2n}\right>=\frac{(2n)!}{2^{n}n!}\left(\frac{\nu}{\gamma^{2}}\frac{1}{(\gamma l)^{2n-2}}+\frac{\gamma}{(\gamma l)^{2n}}\right)dt.

(S50)

We may hence conclude that all the odd Kramers-Moyal coefficients,
a_{2n-1}
with
n\geq 2
, vanish. We therefore have

\displaystyle a_{1}(x)=-\frac{V^{\prime}(x)}{\gamma},\qquad{a_{2n}=\frac{(2n)!}{2^{n}n!}\frac{\gamma}{(\gamma l)^{2n}}\left(1+2kTl^{2}\right)}.

(S51)

which are those of the Kramers-Moyal equation (11) of the main text.

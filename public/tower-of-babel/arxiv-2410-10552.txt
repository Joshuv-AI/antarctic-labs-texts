Combinatorial flats and Schubert varieties of subspace arrangements
Colin Crowley, Connor Simpson, Botong Wang

Abstract
. The lattice of flats ℒ M \mathcal{L}_{M} of a matroid M M is combinatorially well-behaved and, when M M is realizable, admits a geometric model in the form of a “Schubert variety of hyperplane arrangement”. In contrast, the lattice of flats of a polymatroid exhibits many combinatorial pathologies and admits no similar geometric model. We address this situation by defining the lattice ℒ P \mathcal{L}_{P} of “combinatorial flats” of a polymatroid P P . Combinatorially, ℒ P \mathcal{L}_{P} exhibits good behavior analogous to that of ℒ M \mathcal{L}_{M} : it is graded, determines P P when P P is simple, and is top-heavy. When P P is realizable over a field of characteristic 0, we show that ℒ P \mathcal{L}_{P} is modeled by “the Schubert variety of a subspace arrangement”. Our work generalizes a number of results of Ardila-Boocher and Huh-Wang on Schubert varieties of hyperplane arrangements; however, the geometry of Schubert varieties of subspace arrangements is noticeably more complicated than that of Schubert varieties of hyperplane arrangements. Many natural questions remain open.

1.3.

Unlike the usual poset of flats of

Work of Hassett-Tschinkel

with certain coordinate subspaces, we define

to be the

.

If
V\subset\mathbb{K}^{\mathbf{n}}
is a polymatroid general subspace realizing

, then

is nonempty if and only if

is a combinatorial flat.

(i)

\overline{Y_{V}\cap O_{\mathbf{s}}}\supset Y_{V}\cap O_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}

Definition 1.4
.

Let

be a polymatroid with ground set

Equivalently, in terms of Definition

2.
Polymatroids

We review polymatroids, the nearly-equivalent notion of multisymmetric matroids, and some operations on them.

2.1.

A
polymatroid

P
on a finite set
E=\{1,\ldots,N\}
is the data of a
rank
function
\rk:2^{E}\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
that is

normalized
:

\rk(\emptyset)=0
,

increasing
:

if
A\subset B
, then
\rk(A)\leq\rk(B)
, and

submodular
:

if
A,B\subset E
, then
\rk(A\cup B)+\rk(A\cap B)\leq\rk(A)+\rk(B)
.

The
rank
of
P
is
\rk(P):=\rk(E)
, and
the
corank
of a set is
\crk(A):=\rk(P)-\rk(A)
.
If
\rk(A)\leq|A|
for every
A\subset E
, then
P
is a
matroid
.
A
flat
is a subset
F\subset E
such that
\rk(F\cup i)>\rk(F)
for all
i\in E\setminus F
.
The intersection of two flats is a flat.

The
closure
of
A\subset E
is
\overline{A}:=\cap_{F\supset A}F
, where
F
runs over all flats of
P
containing
A
.
The closure of
A
is the smallest flat that contains
A
, so
\rk(A)=\rk(\overline{A})
.
Call
P

loopless
if each element of
E
has positive rank, and
simple
if each one-element subset of
E
is a flat of positive rank.

A
basis
of
P
is a multiset
\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{E}
satisfying
|\mathbf{b}|=\rk(P)
and
|\pi_{A}(\mathbf{b})|\leq\rk(A)
for all
A\subsetneq E
.
A polymatroid is determined by its bases because
\rk(A)=\max\{|\pi_{A}(\mathbf{b})|:\mathbf{b}\text{ is a basis of $P$}\}
.
A multiset
\mathbf{s}
is
independent
if there is a basis
\mathbf{b}
such that
\mathbf{s}\leq\mathbf{b}
, equivalently, if
|\pi_{A}(\mathbf{s})|\leq\rk(A)
for all
A\subset E
.

When necessary, we will use subscripts to distinguish the rank functions of different polymatroids, e.g.
\rk_{P}
is the rank function of
P
.

Example 2.1

(Free polymatroids)
.

Let
E=\{1,\ldots,N\}
and let
\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{E}
be a multiset.
The
free polymatroid

B_{\mathbf{s}}
has rank function

\rk_{B_{\mathbf{s}}}(A)=\sum_{i\in A}s_{i}
.

\diamondsuit

Example 2.2

(Realizable polymatroids)
.

Let
W_{1},\ldots,W_{N}
be vector spaces.
A linear subspace
V\subset\prod_{i=1}^{N}W_{i}
defines a polymatroid
P
on
E=\{1,\ldots,N\}
with rank function

\rk(A):=\dim\pi_{A}(V)=\codim_{V}V\cap\ker(\pi_{A}).

In this case, we say
V

realizes

P
.
The flats
F
of
P
are in inclusion-reversing bijection with subspaces
V\cap\ker(\pi_{F})
.
The closure of
A\subset E
is the largest
A^{\prime}\subset E
such that
V\cap\ker(\pi_{A})=V\cap\ker(\pi_{A^{\prime}})
.

Likewise, a subspace arrangement
V_{1},\ldots,V_{N}
in a vector space
V

realizes

P
if
\rk(A)=\codim_{V}\cap_{i\in A}V_{i}
.
These two notions of realizability are equivalent. Given a subspace
V\subset\prod_{i}W_{i}
, take
V_{i}=\ker\pi_{i}
. Given a subspace arrangement, choose vector spaces
W_{1},\ldots,W_{N}
and a map
f:V\to\prod_{i}W_{i}
with
\ker(\pi_{i}\circ f)=V_{i}
.
The image of
f
is a subspace realizing
P
.

\diamondsuit

Example 2.3

(Lifts)
.

If
M

2.2.

Remark 2.10

.

The multisymmetric lift of

is isomorphic to

, where

.

Proof.

We may think of both matroids as being multisymmetric on

.
By

\rk_{\widetilde{P}\setminus j}((\cup_{k\in A}E_{k})\setminus j)=\rk_{R_{i}P}(A)

If

Suppose that

\widetilde{P}
.
Hence,

\rk_{\widetilde{P}\setminus j}((\cup_{k\in A}E_{k})\setminus j)=\rk_{\widetilde{P}}((\cup_{k\in A}E_{k})\setminus j)=\rk_{\widetilde{P}}((\cup_{k\in A}E_{k}))=\rk_{P}(A)=\rk_{R_{i}P}(A).\qed

3.

We introduce the poset of combinatorial flats of a polymatroid

We construct the poset using a chosen lift

In Sections

Finally, in

•

3.1.

We construct the poset of combinatorial flats and note some properties of its rank function.

Definition 3.1
.

A
combinatorial flat
of
(P,\mathbf{n})
is a multiset
\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{E}
such that there exists a flat
F
of
\widetilde{P}
satisfying
\mathbf{s}=(|F\cap E_{1}|,\ldots,|F\cap E_{N}|)
.
(Rephrased in the language of
Section

1.5
,
\mathbf{s}
is a combinatorial flat if it is represented by a flat of
\widetilde{P}
.)

Ordered by inclusion, the combinatorial flats of
(P,\mathbf{n})
form the
poset of combinatorial flats
, denoted
\mathcal{L}_{P,\mathbf{n}}
.
Observe that
\mathcal{L}_{P,\mathbf{n}}
is isomorphic to the quotient poset
\mathcal{L}_{\widetilde{P}}/\mathfrak{S}
, in which
\mathfrak{S}\cdot F\geq\mathfrak{S}\cdot F^{\prime}
if and only if there is
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}
such that
\sigma\cdot F\supset F^{\prime}
.
Since the action of
\mathfrak{S}
on
\mathcal{L}_{\widetilde{P}}
is rank-preserving,
\mathcal{L}_{P,\mathbf{n}}
is a graded poset,
and the rank of
\mathbf{s}\in\mathcal{L}_{P,\mathbf{n}}
is equal to
\rk_{\widetilde{P}}(S)
, for any
S\in\mathcal{L}_{\widetilde{P}}
representing
\mathbf{s}
.

More generally, if
\mathbf{s}\leq\mathbf{n}
is a multiset represented by
S\subset\widetilde{E}
, then we may define its rank by
\rk_{P,\mathbf{n}}(\mathbf{s}):=\rk_{\widetilde{P}}(S)
.
The
closure
of
\mathbf{s}
is the combinatorial flat
\overline{\mathbf{s}}=(\overline{s}_{1},\ldots,\overline{s}_{N})
represented by
\overline{S}
, and satisfies
\rk_{P,\mathbf{n}}(\mathbf{s})=\rk_{P,\mathbf{n}}(\overline{\mathbf{s}})
. The
geometric part
of
\mathbf{s}
is
\mathbf{s}^{\mathrm{geo}}
, the multiset represented by
S^{\mathrm{geo}}
.
Multisymmetry of
\widetilde{P}
guarantees these definitions do not depend on choice of
S
.

Viewing
\mathcal{L}_{P,\mathbf{n}}
as a quotient and applying
Lemma

2.4
, we learn that rank and closure of multisets have the following properties:

Lemma 3.2
.

•

If
\mathbf{s}
is a combinatorial flat of
(P,\mathbf{n})
, then
\rk_{P,\mathbf{n}}(\mathbf{s})=\rk_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathbf{s}^{\mathrm{geo}})+|\mathbf{s}-\mathbf{s}^{\mathrm{geo}}|
.

•

If
\mathbf{a}\leq\mathbf{n}
is a multiset, then for each
1\leq i\leq N
, either
\overline{a}_{i}=a_{i}
or
\overline{a}_{i}=n_{i}
.

•

If
\mathbf{s}
is a combinatorial flat of
(P,\mathbf{n})
and
0<s_{i}<n_{i}
, then
\mathbf{s}-\mathbf{e}_{i}
is also a combinatorial flat.

From submodularity of
\rk_{\widetilde{P}}
, we also obtain:

Lemma 3.3
.

If
\mathbf{s}\leq\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{e}_{i}
is a multiset, then

\rk_{P,n}(\mathbf{s}+\mathbf{e}_{i})\leq\rk_{P,\mathbf{n}}(\mathbf{s})+1
.

A
basis
of a multiset
\mathbf{s}
is an independent multiset of
P
maximal among those contained in
\mathbf{s}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

If
\mathbf{b}
is a basis of a multiset
\mathbf{s}
, then
\rk_{P,\mathbf{n}}(\mathbf{s})=|\mathbf{b}|
.
If
\mathbf{s}
is a combinatorial flat, then the bases of
\mathbf{s}
are precisely multisets of the form
\mathbf{b}^{\prime}+\mathbf{s}-\mathbf{s}^{\mathrm{geo}}
, where
\mathbf{b}^{\prime}
is a basis of
\mathbf{s}^{\mathrm{geo}}
.

Proof.

Choose
S\subset\widetilde{E}
representing
\mathbf{s}
.
If
\mathbf{b}
is a basis of
\mathbf{s}
,
then
\mathbf{b}
is represented by a basis

in
\widetilde{P}
by
Proposition

.
The first assertion holds because
\rk_{P,\mathbf{n}}(\mathbf{s})=\rk_{\widetilde{P}}(S)=|B|=|\mathbf{b}|

If
\mathbf{s}
is a combinatorial flat, then

consists of coloops of the flat

.
Hence, bases of

Remark 3.6

(Equivalence of definitions)

Lemma

explains the equivalence of the different definitions of “rank” and “combinatorial flat” given in

The first statement of

Suppose that the polymatroid

Lemma 3.9

Let

Proof.

The if direction follows from
Lemma

and
Lemma

.
For the “only if”, suppose that

is a combinatorial flat of

. We will show that
\mathbf{s}
must be a flat by proving
\mathbf{s}=\overline{\mathbf{s}}
.
By

and the hypothesis that

s_{i}=\overline{s}_{i}
.
Hence, it suffices to show that
s_{j}=\overline{s}_{j}
for

By

2.12
and

, there is a combinatorial flat
\mathbf{s}^{\prime}
of
(P,\mathbf{n})

\pi_{E\setminus i}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})=\pi_{E\setminus i}(\mathbf{s})

Otherwise,

By

\overline{\mathbf{s}}-s_{i}\mathbf{e}_{i}

Since

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
(P,\mathbf{n})
be a caged polymatroid on
E
.
Let
F\subset E
be a flat, and
\mathbf{f}=\sum_{i\in F}n_{i}\mathbf{e}_{i}
.
The combinatorial flats of
(T_{F}P,\mathbf{n})
are the combinatorial flats
\mathbf{s}
of
P
that either contain
\mathbf{f}
or satisfy
\overline{\mathbf{s}+\mathbf{e}_{i}}\not\geq\mathbf{f}
for every
i\in E
such that
s_{i}<n_{i}
.
In the former case,
\rk_{T_{F}P}(\mathbf{s})=\rk_{P}(\mathbf{s})-1
, and in the latter,
\rk_{T_{F}P}(\mathbf{s})=\rk_{P}(\mathbf{s})
.

Proof.

For matroids and ordinary flats, this lemma is proved in
[

, Proposition 7.4.9]
. Explicitly, if

is a matroid, then the flats of

are the flats

of

that either contain

By the matroid case and

, a set

is a flat of
\widetilde{T_{F}P}
if and only if

is a flat of
\widetilde{P}
that either contains

We prove that

Lemma 3.11
.

If
\mathbf{s}
and
\mathbf{s}^{\prime}
are multisets contained in
\mathbf{n}
, then

\rk_{P}(\mathbf{s}\wedge\mathbf{s}^{\prime})+\rk_{P}(\mathbf{s}\vee\mathbf{s}^{\prime})\leq\rk_{P}(\mathbf{s})+\rk_{P}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}).

Proof.

Pick sets
S,S^{\prime}\subset\widetilde{E}
representing
\mathbf{s}
and
\mathbf{s}^{\prime}
, respectively, such that
S\cap S^{\prime}
represents
\mathbf{s}\wedge\mathbf{s}^{\prime}
and
S\cup S^{\prime}
represents
(\max(s_{i},s_{i}^{\prime}))_{i}
.
By submodularity of

,

\displaystyle\rk_{P}(\mathbf{s}\wedge\mathbf{s}^{\prime})+\rk_{P}(\mathbf{s}\vee\mathbf{s}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\rk_{\widetilde{P}}(S\cap S^{\prime})+\rk_{\widetilde{P}}(\overline{S\cup S^{\prime}})=\rk_{\widetilde{P}}(S\cap S^{\prime})+\rk_{\widetilde{P}}(S\cup S^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\rk_{\widetilde{P}}(S)+\rk_{\widetilde{P}}(S^{\prime})=\rk_{P}(\mathbf{s})+\rk_{P}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}).\qed

Lemma 3.12
.

\mathcal{L}_{P,\mathbf{n}}
is a semimodular lattice, with join and meet given by
\vee
and
\wedge
, respectively. Moreover,
\mathcal{L}_{P,\mathbf{n}}
is top heavy and
|\mathcal{L}_{P,\mathbf{n}}^{k}|\leq|\mathcal{L}_{P,\mathbf{n}}^{k+1}|
when
k\leq d/2
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{s},\mathbf{s}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}_{P,\mathbf{n}}
.
The join of
\mathbf{s}
and
\mathbf{s}^{\prime}
is equal to
\mathbf{s}\vee\mathbf{s}^{\prime}
because any combinatorial flat containing
\mathbf{s}
and
\mathbf{s}^{\prime}
must contain
(\max(s_{i},s_{i}^{\prime}))_{i}
, hence must also contain
\mathbf{s}\vee\mathbf{s}^{\prime}
.

For meets: any combinatorial flat contained in both
\mathbf{s}
and
\mathbf{s}^{\prime}
is contained in
\mathbf{s}\wedge\mathbf{s}^{\prime}
, so we need only show that
\mathbf{s}\wedge\mathbf{s}^{\prime}
is a combinatorial flat.
To see this, pick sets
S,S^{\prime}\subset\widetilde{E}
representing
\mathbf{s}
and
\mathbf{s}^{\prime}
, and such that
S\cap S^{\prime}
represents
\mathbf{s}\wedge\mathbf{s}^{\prime}
. Since
\mathbf{s}
and
\mathbf{s}^{\prime}
are combinatorial flats,
S
and
S^{\prime}
are flats of
\widetilde{P}
.
Since flats are closed under intersection,
\mathbf{s}\wedge\mathbf{s}^{\prime}
is also a combinatorial flat.
Hence,
\mathbf{s}\wedge\mathbf{s}^{\prime}
is the meet of
\mathbf{s}
and
\mathbf{s}^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{L}_{P,\mathbf{n}}
.

Semimodularity is
Lemma

3.11
.

For top-heaviness:
let
H^{k}(\widetilde{P})
be the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space basis
\{v_{F}:F\in\mathcal{L}_{\widetilde{P}}^{k}\}

be the

-vector space with basis

From the quotient construction of

, there are isomorphisms

H^{k}(P,\mathbf{n})\overset{\cong}{\to}H^{k}(\widetilde{P})^{\mathfrak{S}}\subset H^{k}(\widetilde{P}),\quad v_{\mathbf{s}}\mapsto\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}}v_{\sigma\cdot S},

\mathbf{s}

By

gives a linear injection

H^{k}(P,\mathbf{n})\cong H^{k}(\widetilde{P})^{\mathfrak{S}}\lhook\joinrel\xrightarrow{\;L\;}H^{d-k}(\widetilde{P})^{\mathfrak{S}}\cong H^{d-k}(P,\mathbf{n}),

Lemma 3.13

If

3.4.

We show that reduction and loops do not change the lattice of combinatorial flats.
From these facts, we derive
Theorem

1.1
.

Lemma 3.15
.

Suppose
P
is loopless.
If
\{i\}\subset E
is not a flat of
P
with rank
n_{i}
, then
\mathcal{L}_{R_{i}(P,\mathbf{n})}\cong\mathcal{L}_{P,\mathbf{n}}
.

Proof.

We will show the map
\psi:\mathcal{L}_{P,\mathbf{n}}\to\mathcal{L}_{R_{i}(P,\mathbf{n})}
defined by

\psi(\mathbf{s})=\begin{cases}\mathbf{s},&s_{i}<n_{i}\\
\mathbf{s}-\mathbf{e}_{i},&s_{i}=n_{i}\end{cases}

is an isomorphism.
Multisets in the image of
\psi
are combinatorial flats of
R_{i}(P,\mathbf{n})
by Lemmas
2.17
and
2.12
.

For surjectivity, suppose that
\mathbf{s}\in\mathcal{L}_{R_{i}(P,\mathbf{n})}
is represented by a flat
S
of
\widetilde{R_{i}(P,\mathbf{n})}
.
We will show there is
\mathbf{s}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}_{P,\mathbf{n}}
with
\psi(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})=\mathbf{s}
.
By Lemmas
2.17
and
2.12
, there is
e\in E_{i}\setminus S
such that
S\cup e
or
S
is a flat of
\widetilde{P}
.
If
S
is a flat of
\widetilde{P}
, then
s_{i}<n_{i}
, so we may take
\mathbf{s}^{\prime}=\mathbf{s}
.
Otherwise, suppose
S\cup e
is a flat of
\widetilde{P}
.
If
s_{i}+1<n_{i}
, then by
Lemma

3.2
,
\mathbf{s}
is a combinatorial flat of
\widetilde{P}
, so we may again take
\mathbf{s}^{\prime}=\mathbf{s}
.
Otherwise, if
S\cup e
is a flat of
\widetilde{P}
and
s_{i}+1=n_{i}
, then take
\mathbf{s}^{\prime}=\mathbf{s}+\mathbf{e}_{i}
.
This completes the proof of surjectivity.

We now prove injectivity.
Let
\mathbf{s}\in\mathcal{L}_{R_{i}(P,\mathbf{n})}
.
It is only possible for
\mathbf{s}
to have more than one preimage if
s_{i}=n_{i}-1
.
In this case, the two possible preimages of
\mathbf{s}
are
\mathbf{s}
and
\mathbf{s}+\mathbf{e}_{i}
.

If
\rk_{P}(i)<n_{i}
, then
\mathbf{s}
cannot be a combinatorial flat of
(P,\mathbf{n})
.
This is because, assuming the contrary, we obtain the false inequality

\displaystyle\rk(\mathbf{s})<\rk(\mathbf{s}+\mathbf{e}_{i})

\displaystyle=\rk((\mathbf{s}-(n_{i}-1)\mathbf{e}_{i})\vee n_{i}\mathbf{e}_{i})

\displaystyle\leq\rk(\mathbf{s}-(n_{i}-1)\mathbf{e}_{i})+\rk(n_{i}\mathbf{e}_{i})\quad\text{by \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{submodularity}}

\displaystyle=\rk(\mathbf{s}-(n_{i}-1)\mathbf{e}_{i})+\rk_{P}(i)

\displaystyle\leq\rk(\mathbf{s}-(n_{i}-1)\mathbf{e}_{i})+n_{i}-1

\displaystyle=\rk(\mathbf{s})\quad\text{by \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{combrankformula} and the contrarian assumption.}

Since
\mathbf{s}
is not a combinatorial flat of
(P,\mathbf{n})
,
\mathbf{s}
has only one preimage when
\rk_{P}(i)<n_{i}
.

Otherwise, we are in the case where
\rk_{P}(i)=n_{i}
and
\{i\}
is not a flat of
P
.
Choose sets
S\subset S^{\prime}\subset\widetilde{E}
representing
\mathbf{s}
and
\mathbf{s}+\mathbf{e}_{i}
, respectively, and
assume towards a contradiction that
S
and
S^{\prime}
are both flats of
\widetilde{P}
.
Let
e^{\prime}
be an element of
\overline{E_{i}}\setminus E_{i}
, which is nonempty because
\{i\}
is not a flat of
P
.
If
e
is the unique element of
S^{\prime}\setminus S
, then
S
contains
\overline{(E_{i}\setminus e)\cup e^{\prime}}
.

We now show that
\overline{(E_{i}\setminus e)\cup e^{\prime}}=\overline{E_{i}}
, which implies that
S=S^{\prime}
, a contradiction.
By the definition of
\widetilde{P}
, there is
A\subset E
such that

\rk_{\widetilde{P}}((E_{i}\setminus e)\cup e^{\prime})=\rk_{P}(A)+|((E_{i}\setminus e)\cup e^{\prime})\setminus\cup_{k\in A}E_{k}|.

If
i\in A
, then
\rk_{\widetilde{P}}((E_{i}\setminus e)\cup e^{\prime})\geq\rk_{P}(i)
, so
\overline{(E_{i}\setminus e)\cup e^{\prime}}=\overline{E_{i}}
.
Otherwise, if
i\not\in A
, then

\rk_{\widetilde{P}}((E_{i}\setminus e)\cup e^{\prime})=\rk_{P}(A)+|E_{i}\setminus e|+|\{e^{\prime}\}\setminus\cup_{k\in A}E_{k}|=\rk_{P}(A)+\rk_{P}(i)-1+|\{e^{\prime}\}\setminus\cup_{k\in A}E_{k}|.

This quantity is always at least
\rk_{P}(i)
because
P
is loopless; therefore,
\overline{(E_{i}\setminus e)\cup e^{\prime}}=\overline{E_{i}}
.
∎

.

Remark 3.19
.

If

is a flat of

.

Claim.

If
\{j\}
is a flat of a loopless polymatroid
P
and

, then either
\{i\}
and
\{j\}
are both flats of
R_{i}P
, or

is a loop of
R_{i}P
and

is a flat of
R_{i}P

Proof of claim.

Suppose

If

•

the join-irreducibles of
\mathcal{L}\setminus\hat{0}
form a downward-closed set,

•

each join-irreducible of
\mathcal{L}\setminus\hat{0}
is below a unique maximal join-irreducible, and

•

for any
e\in\mathcal{L}
,
\nul(e)=\nul(e^{\mathrm{geo}})
.

Proof.

If
P
is a polymatroid (which we may assume simple by
Theorem

1.1
ii
), then the prescribed properties hold for
\mathcal{L}_{P}
by
Theorem

1.1
i
,
Lemma

3.13
, and
Lemma

3.2
.

Conversely, suppose that
\mathcal{L}
is a poset with the prescribed properties.
Let
E
be the set of maximal join-irreducibles of
L
and define a simple polymatroid
P
by
\rk_{P}(A):=\rk_{\mathcal{L}}(\vee_{i\in A}i)
.
Let
\mathbf{n}=(\rk_{\mathcal{L}}(i))_{i\in E}
.

Since the irreducibles of
\mathcal{L}
are downward closed, for each
i\in E
,

[\hat{0},i]=\{\hat{0}<x_{i,1}<\cdots<x_{i,n_{i}}=i\}
.
By construction of
P
, the following are equivalent:

•

F\subset E
is a flat of rank
r

•

\{i:x_{i,n_{i}}\leq\vee_{j\in F}x_{j,n_{j}}\}=F
and
\rk_{\mathcal{L}}(\vee_{j\in F}x_{j,n_{j}})=r
,

•

and
\sum_{j\in F}n_{j}\mathbf{e}_{j}
is a combinatorial flat of rank
r
.

Define maps

\displaystyle\varphi:\mathcal{L}_{P}\cong\mathcal{L}_{P,\mathbf{n}}\to\mathcal{L},

\displaystyle\quad\varphi(\mathbf{s})=\vee_{i\in E}\,x_{i,s_{i}}

\displaystyle\varphi^{\prime}:\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{L}_{P,\mathbf{n}}\cong\mathcal{L}_{P},

\displaystyle\quad x\mapsto\vee_{x_{i,j}\leq x}\,j\mathbf{e}_{i}.

Plainly, the compositions
\varphi\circ\varphi^{\prime}
and
\varphi^{\prime}\circ\varphi
are increasing maps (i.e.
(\varphi\circ\varphi^{\prime})(x)\geq x
and
(\varphi^{\prime}\circ\varphi)(\mathbf{s})\geq\mathbf{s}
).
To finish, we show that both
\varphi
and
\varphi^{\prime}
decrease rank.
Let
\mathbf{s}
be a combinatorial flat and let
F=\{i:s_{i}=n_{i}\}
, so that
\mathbf{s}^{\mathrm{geo}}=\sum_{i\in F}n_{i}\mathbf{e}_{i}
.
Using submodularity of
\rk_{\mathcal{L}}
, our discussion of flats of
P
, and
Lemma

3.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\rk_{\mathcal{L}}(\varphi(\mathbf{s}))

\displaystyle\leq\rk_{\mathcal{L}}(\vee_{i\in F}x_{i,n_{i}})+\sum_{i\in E\setminus F}s_{j}=\rk_{P}(F)+\sum_{i\in E\setminus F}s_{j}=\rk_{P}(\mathbf{s}^{\mathrm{geo}})+|\mathbf{s}-\mathbf{s}^{\mathrm{geo}}|=\rk_{P}(\mathbf{s}).

This shows that
\varphi
decreases rank.
On the other hand, if
x\in\mathcal{L}
and
F=\{i:x_{i,n_{i}}\leq x\}
, then

\sum_{i\in E}\max\{j:x_{ij}\leq x\}-\rk_{\mathcal{L}}(x)=\nul(x)=\nul(x^{\mathrm{geo}})=\sum_{i:x_{i,n_{i}}\leq x}n_{i}-\rk_{\mathcal{L}}(x^{\mathrm{geo}}),

so

\displaystyle\rk_{\mathcal{L}}(x)

\displaystyle=\rk_{\mathcal{L}}(x^{\mathrm{geo}})+\sum_{i:x_{i,n_{i}}\not\leq x}\max\{j:x_{i,j}\leq x\}

\displaystyle=\rk_{P}\left(\sum_{i\in F}n_{i}\mathbf{e}_{i}\right)+\sum_{i:x_{i,n_{i}}\not\leq x}\max\{j:x_{i,j}\leq x\}\geq\rk_{P}(\vee_{x_{i,j}\leq x}j\mathbf{e}_{j})=\rk_{P}(\varphi^{\prime}(x)).

This shows that
\varphi^{\prime}
also decreases rank.
Hence, both
\varphi\circ\varphi^{\prime}
and
\varphi^{\prime}\circ\varphi
are increasing maps that decrease rank, which means they must be the identity.
∎

4.1.

We explain a construction of Hassett and Tschinkel
[
17
]
, which we will eventually think of as the variety associated to a polymatroid on one element.
Loosely speaking, Hassett and Tschinkel show that there is only one action of the
n
-dimensional additive group
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{n}
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
with finitely many orbits. More precisely:

Theorem 4.1
.

[
17
, Proposition 3.7]

Over an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0, there is a left action of
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{n}
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
with finitely many orbits.The action is unique up to isomorphism of
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{n}
-variety structures.

To make matters concrete, choose coordinates
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
and
b_{0},\ldots,b_{n}
on
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{n}
and
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, respectively.
The action on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
is given by the faithful representation
\rho_{n}:\mathbb{G}_{a}^{n}\to\Aut(\mathbb{P}^{n})=\PGL_{n+1}
,

\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\mapsto\exp\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0&\cdots&0\\
a_{1}&0&0&\ddots&\vdots\\
a_{2}&a_{1}&0&\ddots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&0\\
a_{n}&\cdots&a_{2}&a_{1}&0\end{pmatrix}.

The entries in each northwest-to-southeast diagonal of
\rho_{n}(\mathbf{a})
are all equal to one another.
Explicitly, the
(i,j)
entry is

\big(\rho_{n}(\mathbf{a})\big)_{i,j}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{(i-j)!}B_{i-j}(1!a_{1},2!a_{2},\ldots,(i-j)!a_{i-j}),&i\geq j\\
0,&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

where
B_{k}
is the (exponential)
Bell polynomial
of degree
k
, defined by
B_{0}=1
and

B_{i}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i})=i!\sum_{j_{1}+2j_{2}+\cdots+ij_{i}=i}\frac{1}{j_{1}!j_{2}!\cdots j_{i}!}\left(\frac{x_{1}}{1!}\right)^{j_{1}}\left(\frac{x_{2}}{2!}\right)^{j_{2}}\cdots\left(\frac{x_{i}}{i!}\right)^{j_{i}}\quad\text{for $i\geq 1$.}

This description implies the following facts.

Proposition 4.2
.

The action of
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{n}
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
partitions
\mathbb{P}^{n}
into orbits

O_{k}\coloneqq\{b_{0}=b_{1}=\cdots=b_{n-k-1}=0,\,b_{n-k}\neq 0\},\quad 0\leq k\leq n.

The stabilizer of any point of
O_{k}
is

\St_{k}\coloneqq\{a_{1}=a_{2}=\cdots=a_{k}=0\}\subset\mathbb{G}_{a}^{n},

and the closure of
O_{k}
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
is
\overline{O_{k}}=\cup_{i\leq k}\,O_{i}
.

Example 4.3
.

The action of
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{2}
on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
is

(a_{1},a_{2})\cdot[b_{0}:b_{1}:b_{2}]=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
a_{1}&1&0\\
\frac{1}{2}a_{1}^{2}+a_{2}&a_{1}&1\end{pmatrix}\cdot[b_{0}:b_{1}:b_{2}]=\left[b_{0}:a_{1}b_{0}+b_{1}:\tfrac{1}{2}a_{1}^{2}b_{0}+a_{2}b_{0}+a_{1}b_{1}+b_{2}\right].

\diamondsuit

4.2.

Consider now the semidirect product
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{n}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}
(with
n\geq 1
), in which
\mathbb{G}_{m}
acts on
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{n}
by
t\cdot\mathbf{a}=(ta_{1},t^{2}a_{2},\ldots,t^{n}a_{n})
.
If we define

\lambda_{n}:\mathbb{G}_{m}\to\Aut(\mathbb{P}^{n}),\quad t\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}1&&&&\\
&t&&&\\
&&t^{2}&&\\
&&&\ddots&\\
&&&&t^{n}\end{pmatrix}\in\Aut(\mathbb{P}^{n}),

then

extends to an injective homomorphism

\mathbb{G}_{a}^{n}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}\hookrightarrow\Aut(\mathbb{P}^{n}),\quad(\mathbf{a},t)\mapsto\rho_{n}(\mathbf{a})\lambda_{n}(t),

in which the image of

normalizes the image of

.
The orbits of

on

are the same as those of

, but each

-orbit

contains a unique

-fixed point. In coordinates, the fixed point is

with

via the map

.

4.4.

We construct Schubert varieties of subspace arrangements, bootstrapping from the Hassett-Tschinkel action of

4.5.

We compute the support of the Chow class of

.
Given a nonsingular variety

be the Chow group of

If

Lemma 4.5
.

The Chow class of
Y_{V}
in
A_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{\mathbf{n}})
is of the form

[Y_{V}]=\sum_{\mathbf{b}\text{ basis of $P$}}c_{\mathbf{b}}\,[\mathbb{P}^{\mathbf{b}}].

Proof.

Write
[Y_{V}]=\sum_{|\mathbf{t}|=d}c_{\mathbf{t}}[\mathbb{P}^{\mathbf{t}}]
.
Let
\mathbf{s}
be a multiset with
|\mathbf{s}|=d
that is not a basis of
P
. We will show that
[\mathbb{P}^{\mathbf{s}}]
has coefficient zero in
[Y_{V}]
.

Let
T=\{i:\overline{s}_{i}=n_{i}\}
. This set is nonempty: if we suppose falsely that
T=\emptyset
, then by
Lemma

3.2
,
\mathbf{s}=\overline{\mathbf{s}}
. This means that
\mathbf{s}
is a combinatorial flat satisfying
\mathbf{s}^{\mathrm{geo}}=\mathbf{0}
, so by
Lemma

3.2
once more,

\rk_{P}(\mathbf{s})=\rk_{P}(\mathbf{s}^{\mathrm{geo}})+|\mathbf{s}-\mathbf{s}^{\mathrm{geo}}|=0+|\mathbf{s}|=d,

contrary to the fact that
\mathbf{s}
is not a basis.

Let
\mathbf{s}^{\prime}=\mathbf{s}-\sum_{i\in E\setminus T}s_{i}\mathbf{e}_{i}
.
The pushforward
\pi_{T*}:A_{0}(\mathbb{P}^{\mathbf{n}})\to A_{0}(\mathbb{P}^{\pi_{T}(\mathbf{n})})
is an isomorphism, so

if and only if
\pi_{T*}(c_{\mathbf{s}}[\mathrm{pt}])
is zero in

By the projection formula,

\pi_{T*}(c_{\mathbf{s}}[\mathrm{pt}])=\pi_{T*}([\mathbb{P}^{\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{s}}]\cdot[Y_{V}])=\pi_{T*}([\mathbb{P}^{\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}]\cdot[\mathbb{P}^{\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{s}+\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}]\cdot[Y_{V}])=[\mathbb{P}^{\pi_{T}(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{s}^{\prime})}]\cdot\pi_{T*}([\mathbb{P}^{\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{s}+\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}]\cdot[Y_{V}]).

The class

is represented by a subvariety
Y^{\prime}
of
Y_{V}
, obtained by intersecting
Y_{V}
with appropriately general hyperplanes.
To show that
[\mathbb{P}^{\pi_{T}(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{s}^{\prime})}]\cdot\pi_{T*}([\mathbb{P}^{\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{s}+\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}]\cdot[Y_{V}])=[\mathbb{P}^{\pi_{T}(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{s}^{\prime})}]\cdot\pi_{T*}([Y^{\prime}])
is zero, it suffices to show that
\dim\pi_{T}(Y^{\prime})<|\pi_{T}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})|
.
In fact, this is the case: by

,

\displaystyle\dim\pi_{T}(Y^{\prime})\leq\dim\pi_{T}(Y_{V})

\displaystyle=\rk_{P}(T)=\rk_{P,\mathbf{n}}(\overline{\mathbf{s}}^{\mathrm{geo}})

\displaystyle=\rk_{P,\mathbf{n}}(\overline{\mathbf{s}})-\sum_{i\in E\setminus T}s_{i}<d-\sum_{i\in E\setminus T}s_{i}=\sum_{i\in T}s_{i}=|\pi_{T}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})|.

We conclude that
c_{\mathbf{s}}=0
.
∎

Remark 4.7

The Schubert variety

The next two results allow us to make inductive arguments; we will use them often without mention.

Proposition 4.8

Problem

Determine when Schubert varieties of subspace arrangements are normal.

5.3.

The ideals defining Schubert varieties of hyperplane arrangements in
(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{N}
are studied in
[
2
]
, and found to be highly tractable: multidegrees, multigraded Betti numbers, and initial ideals can all be understood explicitly in terms of the combinatorics of the relevant matroid.
We believe these objects will also admit combinatorial descriptions for Schubert varieties of subspace arrangements.
A major obstacle to verifying this is that we do not know their defining equations.

Problem
.

Find defining equations for Schubert varieties of subspace arrangements.

Unlike those of Schubert varieties of hyperplane arrangements, equations for Schubert varieties of subspace arrangements cannot be obtained by mere homogenization of circuits, even in relatively simple cases.

Example 5.2
.

Let
V
be the line in
\mathbb{C}^{3}
spanned by
(1,1,1)
, which represents the polymatroid
P
of rank 1 on one element.
A minimal set of polynomials defining
Y_{V}\subset\mathbb{P}^{3}
is:

\displaystyle 6\,x_{0}b_{12}+4\,x_{1}b_{12}+2\,b_{12}^{2}-6\,x_{0}x_{3}-3\,x_{1}x_{3}

\displaystyle x_{1}^{2}-2\,x_{1}b_{12}-2\,b_{12}^{2}+3\,x_{1}x_{3}

\displaystyle 3\,x_{0}x_{1}+5\,x_{1}b_{12}+4\,b_{12}^{2}-3\,x_{0}x_{3}-6\,x_{1}x_{3}.

The ideal generated by these polynomials is not the ideal of homogenized circuits of
V
.
However, observe that there are three minimal equations of degree 2, and that the matroid lift
\widetilde{P}
with respect to
\mathbf{n}=(3)
is a uniform matroid of rank 1 on three elements, which has three circuits of size 2.

In general, we observe in examples that if
Y_{V}\subset\mathbb{P}^{\mathbf{n}}
, then the number of
minimal equations of a given multidegree
\mathbf{d}
is equal to the number of circuits of the lift
\widetilde{P}
with respect to
\mathbf{n}
that represent
\mathbf{d}
.

\diamondsuit

Homotopy properties of regular mappings into real retract rational varieties
Juliusz Banecki

Abstract
In the paper, we study homotopy properties of regular mappings from spheres into a real retract rational variety Y Y . We show that the homotopy classes which are represented by such mappings form subgroups of the homotopy groups of Y Y , and that the groups are independent on the choice of the basepoint on Y Y as long as Y Y is connected. We also construct regular representatives of all the Whitehead products in all the homotopy groups of Y Y .



1
Introduction

In this paper we deal with
real affine varieties
, meaning Zariski closed subsets of the affine space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for some
n
. Note that, unlike in the complex case, the projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{R})
is isomorphic with an affine variety. Morphisms of such varieties are called
regular maps
. The ring of real valued regular functions of an affine variety
X
is denoted by
\mathcal{R}(X)
, while the field of rational functions of an irreducible affine variety is denoted by
\mathbb{R}(X)
. A point
x\in X
is said to be non-singular if the local ring of germs of regular functions
\mathcal{R}(X)_{x}
is a regular local ring. These definitions are compatible with the ones given in
[
6
,
13
]
.

Our main object of study is the following class of
retract rational varieties
, introduced in the 1980s by Saltman:

Definition 1.1
.

An affine variety
Y
is said to be retract rational, if there exists a Zariski open and dense subset
U\subset Y
, a Zariski open subset
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
for some
n
and two regular mappings
i:U\rightarrow V,r:V\rightarrow U
such that their composition

U\xrightarrow{i}V\xrightarrow{r}U

is equal to the identity on
U
.

It has recently been realised, through the results of
[
1
,
2
]
, that this class of varieties is of great use when studying approximation properties of regular mappings between real affine varieties. The current paper aims to extend these results even further, using tools developed in the aforementioned papers and bringing new ideas into the field.

We begin by summarising the recent results regarding real retract rational varieties, which will be of some use in the current paper and are interesting on their own. The starting point of the summary is the following theorem:

Theorem 1.2

(
[
2
, Theorem 1.8]
)
.

Let
Y
be a non-singular retract rational affine variety. Then it is
uniformly retract rational
, meaning that for each point
y_{0}\in Y
there exists a Zariski open neighbourhood
U
of
y_{0}
in
Y
, a Zariski open subset
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
for some
n
and two regular mappings
i:U\rightarrow V,r:V\rightarrow U
such that their composition

U\xrightarrow{i}V\xrightarrow{r}U

is equal to the identity on
U
.

Theorem
1.2
allows us to reformulate older results of
[
1
]
, which regard uniformly retract rational varieties, so that now we can talk about non-singular retract rational varieties instead. Without getting into too much detail, the main result of
[
1
]
can be formulated as follows:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
Y
be a nonsingular retract rational affine variety, let
X
be an affine variety, let
Z\subset X
be a compact Zariski closed subvariety of
X
, and let
f:X\rightarrow Y
be a continuous mapping satisfying the following two conditions:

1.

f
is homotopic to a regular mapping
g:X\rightarrow Y
,

2.

f|_{Z}:Z\rightarrow Y
is regular.

Then
f
can be approximated by regular mappings
\widetilde{f}:X\rightarrow Y
in the compact-open topology, agreeing with
f
on
Z
.

The result is an important tool used in
[
11
]
, where Kucharz was able to characterise the weak homotopy type of the connected components of the space
\mathcal{R}(X,Y)
of regular mappings from an affine variety
X
to a nonsingular retract rational variety
Y
equipped with the compact-open topology. It also plays a major role in
[
5
]
, where approximation of continuous mapping by piecewise regular ones is considered.

Having such satisfying results regarding approximation properties of regular mappings, in this paper we come to study their homotopy properties. Our main results deal with
algebraic homotopy classes
, studied before in
[
8
,
9
,
14
]
and most recently in
[
3
]
. To introduce them, we need to fix some notation. Let
\mathbb{S}^{n}
denote the
n
-dimensional unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, and let
e:=(1,0,\dots,0)\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
be the north pole of
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Given an affine variety
Y
with a fixed point
y_{0}\in Y
, we denote by
\pi_{n}(Y,y_{0})
the set of homotopy classes of continuous mappings of pointed topological spaces
f:(\mathbb{S}^{n},e)\rightarrow(Y,y_{0})
. For
n\geq 1
, the set admits a natural group structure and is called the
n
-th homotopy group of
(Y,y_{0})
. By
\pi_{n}^{\textnormal{alg}}(Y,y_{0})
we denote the set of
algebraic homotopy classes
, i.e. these elements of
\pi_{n}(Y,y_{0})
which admit representatives
\tilde{f}:(\mathbb{S}^{n},e)\rightarrow(Y,y_{0})
being regular mappings. The main result can now be stated as follows:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
Y
be a nonsingular retract rational affine variety with a highlighted point
y_{0}\in Y
. Then the set
\pi_{n}^{\textnormal{alg}}(Y,y_{0})
comprises a subgroup of the homotopy group
\pi_{n}(Y,y_{0})
for every
n\geq 1
.

The theorem was earlier known to hold in the special case when
Y
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{k}
for some
k
, and it is also known to hold for
algebraic cohomotopy classes
which we will not be defining here; see
[
3
]
for the definition and some other results regarding it. The current result is nonetheless significantly more general than the previous ones.

It is worth noting here, that Theorem
1.3
implies that the groups
\pi_{n}(Y,y_{0})
are independent on the choice of the basepoint
y_{0}
as long as
Y
is connected:

Observation 1.5
.

Let
Y
be a nonsingular retract rational variety which is connected (note that according to
[
1
, Observation 6.1]
this is the case whenever
Y
is compact). Then, for
n\geq 1
, for any two points
y_{0},y_{1}\in Y
the groups
\pi_{n}(Y,y_{0})
and
\pi_{n}(Y,y_{1})
are isomorphic.

Proof.

Let
\varphi:[0,1]\rightarrow Y
be a path connecting
y_{0}
and
y_{1}
. It induces a canonical isomorphism
\varphi_{\ast}:\pi_{n}(Y,y_{0})\xrightarrow{\cong}\pi_{n}(Y,y_{1})
. Let
\alpha\in\pi_{n}^{\textnormal{alg}}(Y,y_{0})
be represented by a regular mapping
f:(\mathbb{S}^{n},e)\rightarrow(Y,y_{0})
and let
g:(\mathbb{S}^{n},e)\rightarrow(Y,y_{1})
be a continuous mapping representing
\varphi_{\ast}(\alpha)
. By construction
f
and
g
are homotopic. Hence, according Theorem
1.3
, the mapping
g
can be approximated by a regular one
\tilde{g}:(\mathbb{S}^{n},e)\rightarrow(Y,y_{1})
. If the approximation is close enough, it represents the same homotopy class as
g
. This shows that
\varphi_{\ast}\big(\pi_{n}^{\textnormal{alg}}(Y,y_{0})\big)\subset\pi_{n}^{\textnormal{alg}}(Y,y_{1})
. The other inclusion follows by an analogous argument.
∎

Our next result, analogous to
[
3
, Observation 3.7]
, deals with
Whitehead products
, defined in
[
15
, Chapter X, Section 7]
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
Y
be a nonsingular retract rational affine variety with a highlighted point
y_{0}\in Y
. Let
n,m\geq 1
be natural numbers. Then, for any two classes
\alpha\in\pi_{n}(Y,y_{0}),\;\beta\in\pi_{m}(Y,y_{0})
, their Whitehead product
[\alpha,\beta]\in\pi_{n+m-1}(Y,y_{0})
belongs to
\pi_{n+m-1}^{\textnormal{alg}}(Y,y_{0})
.

Our last result deals with mappings from nonsingular curves into nonsingular retract rational varieties:

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
X
be a nonsingular compact affine curve and let
Y
be a nonsingular retract rational affine variety. Let
f:X\rightarrow Y
be continuous. Then
f
can be approximated by regular mappings
\tilde{f}:X\rightarrow Y
.

Such a theorem was earlier known to hold in few other cases: when
Y
is rational, when
Y
is birational to a smooth cubic hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{n}
for
n\geq 3
, and when
Y
is birational to a homogenous space of a real algebraic group, see
[
4
,
7
]
. In particular, this includes examples which are not retract rational, see for example
[
1
, Example 6.3]
. It is expected that the the theorem holds in general for
rationally connected
varieties,
see
[
12
, Question 4]
. If true, this statement would imply all the results mentioned above. Here a variety
Y
is said to be rationally connected if every two points of
Y
lying in the same connected component of
Y
can be connected by a rational curve in
Y
.

As a consequence we obtain a statement regarding algebraic classes in the fundamental group of
Y
:

Corollary 1.8
.

Let
Y
be a nonsingular retract rational affine variety and let
y_{0}\in Y
. Then
\pi_{1}^{\textnormal{alg}}(Y,y_{0})=\pi_{1}(Y,y_{0})
.

Proof.

Let
f:(\mathbb{S}^{1},e)\rightarrow(Y,y_{0})
be a representative of a given homotopy class
\alpha\in\pi_{1}(Y,y_{0})
. According to Theorem
1.7
,
f
can be approximated by a regular mapping
\tilde{f}:\mathbb{S}^{1}\rightarrow Y
. If the approximation is close enough, then
f
is homotopic to
\tilde{f}
. Hence we can apply Theorem
1.3
to find a regular mapping
\hat{f}:\mathbb{S}^{1}\rightarrow Y
which agrees with
f
at the point
e
and approximates
f
. If the approximation is close enough then
\hat{f}
represents
\alpha
.
∎

The author believes that the result presented here, which most importantly include Theorem
1.4
, suggest that nonsingular retract rational varieties may comprise just the right class of varieties to study homotopy properties of regular mappings in real algebraic geometry.

2
Preliminaries

Recall the following technical lemma playing a role in
[
1
]
:

Lemma 2.1

(
[
1
, Lemma 3.2]
)
.

Let
Y
be an irreducible variety and let
n
be a natural number. Let
U\subset Y\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a Zariski open set such that
U\cap(Y\times\{0\})\neq\emptyset
. Let
f\in\mathcal{R}(U)
and let
P,Q\in\mathbb{R}(Y\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
be two rational functions, regular on a neighbourhood of
Y\times\{0\}
satisfying

Qf=P\textnormal{ on a neighbourhood of }U\cap(Y\times\{0\}).

Suppose further that
f|_{U\cap(Y\times\{0\})}
admits a regular extension onto the entire variety
Y\times\{0\}
. Then, the function

g(y,v):=f(y,Q(y,0)v)

defined on a neighbourhood of
U\cap(Y\times\{0\})
admits a regular extension to a neighbourhood of
Y\times\{0\}
.

We will need the following corollary of the result:

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
Y
be an irreducible variety, let
y_{0}\in Y
and let
n
be a natural number. Let
Z
be another affine variety and let
F:Y\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\dashrightarrow Z
be a germ of a regular mapping at the point
(y_{0},0)
. Then, there exists
Q\in\mathcal{R}(Y)
such that
q(y_{0})=1
and the germ of a regular mapping at
(y_{0},0)

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle:Y\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\dashrightarrow Y,

\displaystyle G(x,v)

\displaystyle:=F(y,q(y)v),

extends to a neighbourhood of
Y\times\{0\}
.

Proof.

Embed
Z
as a Zariski closed subset of the affine space
\mathbb{R}^{m}
for some
m
. Since
F
is regular in a neighbourhood of the point
(y_{0},0)
, there exists a common denominator of all the coordinates of
F
, i.e. a regular function
q\in\mathcal{R}(Y\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
, which can be assumed to attain value
1
at
(y_{0},0)
such that

qF=P\text{ in a neighbourhood of }(y_{0},0),

for some regular mapping
P:Y\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow Z
. It suffices to take
q(y):=Q(y,0)
and apply Lemma
2.1
.
∎

We also need the following simple observation:

Observation 2.3
.

Let
Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a nonsingular retract rational variety, embedded as a Zariski closed subset in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Fix a point
y_{0}\in Y
. Then, there exists a Zariski open set
W\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
containing
y_{0}
, and a regular mapping
R:W\rightarrow Y
such that
R(y)=y
for
y\in Y\cap W
.

Proof.

According to Theorem
1.2
, there exists a neighbourhood
U
of
y_{0}
in
Y
, and a Zariski open subset
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
for some
m
, such that there exist two regular mappings
i:U\rightarrow V,r:V\rightarrow U
satisfying
r\circ i=\textnormal{id}_{U}
. The mapping
i
admits a regular extension
\widetilde{i}
to a Zariski open set
W\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
containing
U
as a subset. It suffices to define
R:=r\circ\widetilde{i}
.
∎

2.1
Rational H-spaces

To prove Theorem
1.4
we introduce the following notion:

Definition 2.4
.

Let
Y
be an irreducible affine variety and let
y_{0}\in Y
. We say that the pair
(Y,y_{0})
is a
rational H-space
if there exist a Zariski open set
A\subset Y\times Y
containing
Y\times\{y_{0}\}\cup\{y_{0}\}\times Y
and a regular mapping
\mu:A\rightarrow Y
such that

1.

Y\ni y\mapsto\mu(y,y_{0})\in Y
is homotopic to the identity relatively to
\{y_{0}\}
,

2.

Y\ni y\mapsto\mu(y_{0},y)\in Y
is homotopic to the identity relatively to
\{y_{0}\}
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
Y
be a nonsingular retract rational variety, and let
y_{0}\in Y
be a point. Then
(Y,y_{0})
is a rational H-space.

Proof.

Fix an embedding of
Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
as a Zariski closed subset in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for some
n
. Let
W
be the Zariski open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
containing
y_{0}
, and let
R:W\rightarrow Y
be the regular mapping from Lemma
2.3
. After a shift of coordinates we might assume that
y_{0}
is the origin in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Applying Corollary
2.2
to the germ of a regular mapping at
(0,0)

\displaystyle F:Y\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\dashrightarrow Y,

\displaystyle F(x,v):=R(x+v),

we can find a Zariski open neighbourhood
U
of
Y\times\{0\}
in
Y\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a regular function
q\in\mathcal{R}(Y)
such that
q(0)=1
and

\displaystyle G:Y\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\dashrightarrow Y,

\displaystyle G(x,v):=R(x+q(x)v),

extends to a regular mapping on
U
.

Using Łojasiewicz’s inequality
[
6
, Proposition 2.6.2]
we can find
N>0
such that

\{(x,v)\in Y\times\mathbb{R}^{m}:||v||<||x||(1+||x||^{2})^{-N}|q(x)|\}\subset U.

Define
s(x):=q^{2}(x)(1+||x||^{2})^{-N}
. Now consider the rational mapping

\displaystyle\mu

\displaystyle:Y\times Y\dashrightarrow Y,

\displaystyle\mu(x,y)

\displaystyle:=R\left(\frac{s(y)}{s(x)+s(y)}x+\frac{s(x)}{s(x)+s(y)}y\right).

First of all we claim that the mapping extends to a regular one on a neighbourhood of
Y\times\{0\}
. Near
Y\times\{0\}
it can be written as

\mu(x,y)=G\left(x,(y-x)(1+||x||^{2})^{-N}\frac{q(x)}{s(x)+s(y)}\right).

(1)

For
(x,y)\in Y\times\{0\}
we have that

\left\|(y-x)(1+||x||^{2})^{-N}\frac{q(x)}{s(x)+s(y)}\right\|\leq||x||(1+||x||^{2})^{-N}|q(x)|,

so the point
\left(x,(y-x)(1+||x||^{2})^{-N}\frac{q(x)}{s(x)+s(y)}\right)
belongs to
U
. This shows that (
1
) gives a representation of
\mu
as a regular mapping on a neighbourhood of
Y\times\{0\}
.

Notice also that
\mu(x,0)=G(x,-x(1+||x||^{2})^{-N}\frac{1}{s(x)+1})
is homotopic to the identity through the following homotopy relative to
0
:

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle:Y\times[0,1]\rightarrow Y,

\displaystyle H(x,t)

\displaystyle:=G\left(x,-tx(1+||x||^{2})^{-N}\frac{1}{s(x)+1}\right).

One can now analogously show that
\mu
extends to a regular mapping on a neighbourhood of
\{0\}\times Y
and that the mapping
\mu(0,y)
considered as a function of
y
is homotopic to the identity relatively to
\{0\}
as well. This finishes the proof.
∎

3
Proofs

Theorems
1.4
and
1.6
now follow easily:

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Given a regular mapping
f:(\mathbb{S}^{n},e)\rightarrow(Y,a)
, the class
-[f]\in\pi_{n}(Y,y_{0})
is represented by
f\circ\textnormal{sym}
, where
\textnormal{sym}:\mathbb{S}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{n}
is any symmetry through a vector hyperplane passing through
e
. Hence
\pi_{n}^{\textnormal{alg}}(Y,y_{0})
is closed under taking inverse elements.

Let now
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\pi_{n}^{\textnormal{alg}}(Y,y_{0})
be two algebraic homotopy classes. Choose
f_{1},f_{2}
to be their representatives such that
f_{1}
is constantly equal to
a
on the hemisphere
\{x_{2}\leq 0\}
, while
f_{2}
is constantly equal to
y_{0}
on the hemisphere
\{x_{2}\geq 0\}
. Then
\mu(f_{1},f_{2})
is well defined and by definition represents the homotopy class
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}
. Now applying Theorem
1.3
, we can find regular approximations
\widetilde{f_{1}},\widetilde{f_{2}}
of
f_{1},f_{2}
respectively satisfying
\widetilde{f_{1}}(e)=\widetilde{f_{2}}(e)=y_{0}
. If the approximations are close enough then
\mu(\widetilde{f_{1}},\widetilde{f_{2}})
is well defined and close to
\mu(f_{1},f_{2})
, hence it represents the same homotopy class.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

By the definition, given two continuous mappings
f:(\mathbb{S}^{n},e)\rightarrow(Y,y_{0}),g:(\mathbb{S}^{m},e)\rightarrow(Y,y_{0})
the Whitehead product of their classes is represented by the following composition
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h:\mathbb{S}^{n+m-1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{S}^{n}\vee\mathbb{S}^{m}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f\vee g}

where
\phi
is a certain attaching map. Let
\text{const}_{n}:\mathbb{S}^{n}\rightarrow Y,\;\text{const}_{m}:\mathbb{S}^{m}\rightarrow Y
denote the mappings constantly equal to
y_{0}
, and define:

\displaystyle\psi:=\phi(f\vee\text{const}_{m}),

\displaystyle\xi:=\phi(\text{const}_{n}\vee g).

It is then clear that
\psi
and
\xi
are homotopic to the constant mapping, as they represent the homotopy classes of the products
[f,\text{const}_{m}]
and
[\text{const}_{n},g]
respectively. Moreover it follows from the construction that for
x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
we either have
\psi(x)=h(x)
and
\xi(x)=y_{0}
or
\psi(x)=y_{0}
and
\xi(x)=h(x)
, so
h
is homotopic to
\mu(\psi,\xi)
relatively to
\{e\}
.

Applying Theorem
1.3
we can find regular mappings

\displaystyle\tilde{\psi}:\mathbb{S}^{n}\rightarrow Y,

\displaystyle\tilde{\xi}:\mathbb{S}^{n}\rightarrow Y,

which satisfy
\psi(e)=\xi(e)=y_{0}
and approximate
\psi
(respectively
\xi
) closely. If the approximation is close enough then
\tilde{h}:=\mu(\tilde{\psi},\tilde{\xi})
is a close regular approximation of
\mu(\psi,\xi)
. In particular it represents the same homotopy class.
∎

Remark 3.0.1
.

A close analysis of the proof shows, that if instead of
\mathbb{S}^{n+m-1}
one considers a different real affine variety
\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}^{n+m-1}
homeomorphic with a sphere, then it still holds that
[\alpha,\beta]
is represented by a regular mapping
f:(\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}^{n+m-1},e)\rightarrow(Y,y_{0})
. This shows that certain homotopy classes of
(Y,y_{0})
are algebraic irrespective of what algebraic structure we equip the sphere with. We plan to study this interesting phenomenon closer in the future in another paper.

Lastly, we prove Theorem
1.7
. The proof is independent of the rest of the theory presented here in the paper, and it relies only on an old result of Bochnak and Kucharz.

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

Let
Y
be a nonsingular retract rational variety. Thanks to the resolution of singularities, without loss of generality we can substitute for
Y
its nonsingular compactification, so we assume that
Y
is compact.

Let
U
be an open subset of
Y
, let
V
be an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for some
n
, so that there exist the two regular mappings
i:U\rightarrow V
and
r:V\rightarrow U
such that
r\circ i=\textnormal{id}_{U}
. Again thanks to the resolution of singularities, there exists a nonsingular compactification
Z
of
V
, such that
r
extends to a regular mapping
R:Z\rightarrow Y
.

Let
X
be a nonsingular compact curve and let
f:X\rightarrow Y
be continuous. After a small perturbation we can assume that the image of
f
is not contained in
Y\backslash U
. Also, thanks to
[
10
, Chapter VII, Theorem 2.25]
,
f
can be uniformly approximated by analytic mappings, so we can assume that it is analytic.

Now the composition
i\circ f
is a meromorphic mapping from
X
into the compact variety
Z
. As
X
is nonsingular and one-dimensional it follows that it admits an extension to an analytic mapping
g:X\rightarrow Z
, which then necessarily satisfies
R\circ g=f
. As the variety
Z
is rational it follows from
[
7
]
that
g
can be approximated by a regular mapping
\tilde{g}:X\rightarrow Z
. Then,
R\circ\tilde{g}
is a regular approximation of
f
.
∎

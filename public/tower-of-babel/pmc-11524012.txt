Digital impression (intraoral scanners) and factors affecting its accuracy – an insight into knowledge and awareness amongst graduates, and clinical practitioners
Authors: Saeed M Alqahtani, Saurabh Chaturvedi, Maram A Alahmari, Abdulaziz M Alaleyani, Abdullah A Alqahtani, Abdulrahim A Sahal, Mohammed Salem
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis study aimed to evaluate the knowledge, awareness, and practice of intraoral scanners (IOS) and the factors affecting their accuracy amongst dental interns and practitioners.MethodsThis was an observational cross-sectional study in which data was collected from a representative population in Saudi Arabia, with the help of online questionnaires. The forms were distributed to 500 dental interns and practitioners and the questionnaire’s validity and reliability were evaluated for vetting and remarks. The questionnaire consisted of three parts: the participants’ demographic details, questions on the awareness and knowledge of participants regarding IOS, and the factors affecting the accuracy in clinical use of IOS. Questions were to be answered with ‘yes’, ‘no’ and ‘I don’t know’. Descriptive statistical analysis was performed using the control chart technique and the chi-squared test, with statistical significance set at p < 0.05.ResultsMost of the participants (58%) knew about the IOS and its applications (47.5%), but the majority of the participants did not use IOS in their clinics (43.5%). The majority of males (57.39%) and females (50.86%) strongly agreed that cost was a barrier to purchasing an IOS. To better get more knowledge on IOS, the specialists 66 (16.5%) and interns 54 (13.5%) chose continuing dental education programs and workshops. There was a significant association (p < 0.05) of knowledge and awareness, perception, and factors affecting parameters with different categories and years of experience.ConclusionWithin the limitations of this study, most participants had a positive stance towards IOS but due to inadequate knowledge and understanding, its use and applications were minimal. IOS was well-received regardless of operator training and experience. There is a significant need to increase awareness of this concept, as it may increase treatment efficiency and effectiveness.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12903-024-05075-y.Keywords: Digital dentistry, Survey, Patient comfort, Digital workflow

Introduction

Impression making in dentistry is one of the most important steps. A precise impression provides exact replica of the patient’s oral tissues, it helps in diagnosis, treatment planning, treatment, follow-up and helps in fabrication of comfortable prosthesis thus, reducing the risk of discomfort and further complications in the prosthesis [1–3].

Along with conventional method of making impressions, the digital impression techniques are gaining popularity among clinicians. Several studies compared the conventional and digital impression making and ascertain that limitations of conventional impressions can be overcome by digital impressions and proved that digital impression techniques are more effective [3, 4].

Conventional techniques use impression materials to make physical impressions, while digital impressions are recorded with intra oral scanners (IOS). IOS uses artificial intelligence (AI) to join the different scans segments together to produce complete picture of the scanned tissues directly [5, 6]. Compared to digital impressions various disadvantages have been reported with conventional impressions such as decreased accuracy and precision, increased gag reflex, discomfort for patients, laboratory work, slow restoration processing time because digital impressions can be sent immediately to dental laboratories. Tabesh [7] and Hasanzade [8] reported higher marginal fit accuracy values of restorations produced with digital workflow than those produced with conventional impressions. The digital impression improve communication between dentists and dental technicians through the sharing of digital files and remote collaboration, and improved patient education and treatment planning thanks to the ability to view and manipulate digital models [5, 9]. Also, digital impressions provide the capability to rescan missing or improperly recorded portions and preview sections, allowing for real-time feedback [10].

Understanding the dentists’/students’ perspective on IOS training is essential for institutions to select the optimal IOS to meet the needs of both pre-clinical and clinical programs [11, 12]. The established dental prospectus comprises very limited information about IOS. However, the actual prevalence of such training and the level of IOS knowledge amongst dental students, dental school graduates and clinicians remains unclear, despite previous efforts to measure dental students’ knowledge and perceptions of the IOS technology [13–15]. Thus, it is important to identify the knowledge, and awareness of dentists towards IOS. However, to the best of the authors’ knowledge, no survey has been conducted until now in the field of oral health and preventive dentistry concentrating on IOS usage and knowledge among dental interns and practitioners, especially in Saudi Arabia. Thus, the present study was aimed to evaluate the knowledge, awareness, and practice of intraoral scanners and the factors affecting their accuracy during clinical use among dental interns and practitioners [16–20].

Materials and methods

Study setting

The current research was an observational cross-sectional type of study where the data was collected from the representative population in Saudi Arabia (SA). It was a web-based (online) questionnaire study and was conducted amongst interns or graduated dentists, dental residents practicing in government set-ups, private dental centers, and dental colleges from SA. [dentists graduate/interns group involving participants who completed the dental school studies and practising dentistry independently, and/or (interns) who completed their final year in dental school and required a compulsory internship to become a licensed dentist. Dental residents group involving participants who were perusing postgraduate and speciality training] and dental practitioners of SA and of different nationalities (dentists graduated from other countries), through an e-survey using Google Forms.

Ethical considerations

This study was conducted in compliance with the protocol; ethical approval (IRB/KKUCOD/ETH/2023-24/012) was taken from the ethical committee of the College of Dentistry. The subjects participating in the study provided their informed consent after accepting to fill out the survey form. Participation was voluntary and there were no incentives. Data protection and anonymity were guaranteed.

Study population and sampling technique

A total of 500 individuals participated in the study. Data for this study were gathered from a representative sample of the dental practitioners and interns of Saudi Arabia, who finished their studies and practiced dentistry in an institution, and dental practitioners, who finished their education and became licensed dentists. To calculate the sample size the following formula was used - sample Size=(Z-score) ^2 X Standard Deviation X (1-Standard Deviation) / (confidence interval) ^2 (Andrew Fisher’s Formula- where standard deviation 0.5, Confidence level 95%, Z-score 1.96, confidence interval 6.) [21], Considering a total of 43000 dentists in 2023 [22], Saudi Arabia, the sample size calculated was 265, in our study total 500 individual participated. Out these total of 487 forms were sent online, 13 forms sent by email remained unsent may be due to the wrong email address.

Study tools and data collection

Validity of questionnaires

The questionnaire form was developed for the present study and lawshe’s methods were used for content validity using judgments from a panel of 10 subject matter experts. Content validity is most often measured by relying on the knowledge of people who are familiar with the idea being measured. In our study the idea being measured was IOS and digital impression making. 10 Prosthodontists [selected based on - minimum 5 years of clinical and research experience after master’s degree, have been trained in using IOS, having IOS incorporation in his clinical practice, attached to academic institute] were asked to review the questionnaires, and their feedback was taken on how well each question. In the Lawshe’s method, the content validity ratio (CVR) was calculated using Lawshe’s formula CVR = (ne -N/2)/N/2 where “ne” is the number of experts identifying an item as “essential” whereas N is the total number of experts. CVR values range from − 1 to + 1. Later CVI was calculated which presents the average of the CVR that constitutes the final questionnaire form. Values can range from 0 to 1. The calculated I-CVI of 0.79 or more indicates the items are relevant, and 0.70 to 0.79 indicates the item needs revisions. In contrast, less than 0.70 suggests that items need to be eliminated. With more than five experts, the acceptable value for I-CVI is 0.78 The criteria used in selecting the items in questions were mainly the points related to digital impressions and techniques involved. Any question asserting points other than IOS or impressions were rejected by experts [23–25].

Reliability of questionnaires

Twenty volunteers from a similar population participated in test retests to confirm reliability. A high level of reliability was suggested by the kappa score of 0.9. The questionnaire then underwent pilot testing with 10 volunteers who were requested to complete it and offer feedback on its content, clarity, and length. Checkboxes were provided, and participants had to click on one option for each question. Care was taken that one person could answer the questionnaire only once, and all questions were mandatory.

The responses were directly recorded through Google Forms. Since this was an e-survey, the informed consent was included in the Google form, and they were sent through WhatsApp/email and other online methods. The communication data of participants was coded, and provision was made to discuss any point related to the study, if any participant needs. At an interval every 1 week two times, all participants were reminded regarding filling of the questionnaire forms with the response. Wherever essential detailed conversation was provided to the respondents’ clarification was provided regarding the study and its use through phone or email.

Questionnaire parts

The English version of the questionnaire, divided into three parts, was distributed. The questionnaire consisted of a brief explanation of the purpose of the study, a consent form to be signed/ticked by the participant and close-ended questions. The first part consisted of the demographic details of the participants. The second part consisted of questions based on the awareness and knowledge of the participants regarding IOS and its applications. The third part consisted of questions on knowledge about the factors affecting the accuracy in clinical use of IOS and its perception.

Statistical analysis

In order to achieve the outlined objectives, the Scores were calculated based on the responses given by participants. A single investigator analyzed all filled questionnaires forms. Data were analyzed using statistical software R, version 4.2.2 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing) and Microsoft Excel. Categorical variables were represented by frequency and percentages. The chi-square test was used to check the association between categorical variables. A p-value less than or equal to 0.05 indicates statistical significance. A data wrapper was used to generate descriptive graphs.

Results

Demographic characteristics

Out of 487 participants, 400 completed the forms and submitted them. Thus, a response rate of 82% was recorded. Amongst them, 29% of the participants were females, and 71% were males. Out of which 36% of the participants were interns, 8% were residents, 23.5% were general practitioners, and 32.5% were specialists.

Most dentists have under 1–5 years of experience, and only 8.5% have more than 15 years of experience. Of 130 specialists, 58.46% were prosthodontists, 13.85% of them were orthodontists, and 7.69% were endodontists (Figs. 1 and 2)

Perception about IOS

According to most participants, IOS was better than conventional impressions (48.5%). Discussion

IOSs have become one of the most valuable dental treatment devices for patients, dentists, dental technicians, and dental hygienists [10, 26]. Budding dentists now must incorporate newer technologies and practice digital dentistry to stay ahead, and dental institutions play a significant role in their awareness of these trends. The results of the study showed that IOS will have a profound future impact on dental practice. This was also acknowledged by most respondents in our cohort, with 58% knowing about the IOS and its applications (47.5%) and agreeing that IOS is useful for oral health and preventive dentistry, and it would play an essential task in dental care management and training. The key goal of this survey was to gauge the accessibility and knowledge of digital impression technology among recent graduates and experienced clinicians. A new set of questionnaire tool was developed for the study as the previous study questionnaire were not fulling our objectives of assessment of knowledge of digital impression (intraoral scanners) and factors affecting its accuracy. This questionnaire tool was validated using the lawshe’s method. In the development of the question bank, extensive literature review was thoroughly searched, the instrument’s layout design guided by the survey purpose, and key questions were identified from the domain.

In the present study total 400 participants returned online filled form. Most of them were males and interns and specialists. Acharya and colleagues discovered in 2023 that almost all dentistry practitioners (98.9%) and a majority of technicians (92.7%) were aware of the use of digital technology in their field. However, a small percentage of practitioners (9.28%) and technicians (17.7%) were unfamiliar with 3D printing, which was found to be statistically significant [12]. In the same year, Al-Hassiny conducted a study on the user experience of IOS technology and the perceived quality of various IOS used by dental clinicians worldwide. The study included 75.8% general dentists, 16.2% dental specialists, 4.7% dental technicians, 2.3% dental students, 0.7% dental assistants, and 0.3% dental hygienists [27].

Most participants agreed that IOS was better than conventional impressions, but there are still hindrances such as cost, acceptance by dental laboratories, maintenance, digital workflow, and compatibility with CAD systems. Dentists should not overlook the importance of Intraoral Scanners (IOS) and there is a need for enhanced understanding and education in this area. Although participants were knowledgeable about IOS, they admitted to not using it in their clinical practices. This contradicts Al-Hassiny et al., where over three-quarters of the survey group used IOS daily [27]. This difference could be due to a significant portion of our participants being dental interns and general practitioners with less than 1–5 years of experience. Cost was a barrier to acquiring an IOS for both men and women. Disadvantages of IOS include difficulties in detecting deep margin lines and bleeding, the learning curve, and purchasing and managing costs. A study found that vertical preparation design allows for deep margin recording [28, 29]. Conventional impression technique is reported to be better than digital techniques in laboratory studies, but this may be due to differences in clinical settings setting [10, 30]. Inaccuracies in impressions can be controlled with better understanding and technique of use.

In the present study, participants strongly agreed that cost, limited acceptance by dental laboratories, maintenance workflow, and compatibility with CAD systems made it difficult for usage in clinics, this result was in association with the study by Ahmed et al. in 2021, where 85% of participants stated that cost would influence their decision to adopt IOS in their practice. Scanning performance and training needs depend on the operator’s background [31]. Lam et al., in 2021 [32], assessed dental students’ preference and perception of IOS and impression-making and reported that the pro-scanning group was in favor of scanning and believed that IOS was easier to use without direct assistance, simpler for beginners to learn, more efficient when used by experienced practitioners, and had a good understanding of the scanner software. Similarly, most participants (58%) in our study were familiar with IOS and its uses (47.5%). In addition, nearly half of the participants in the study considered IOS to be superior to traditional impressions (48.5%) [32].

With all technological advancements, a high level of user education and preparation is essential for successfully integrating CAD/CAM technology into routine medical practice [33]. Most study participants believed that IOS could enhance treatment but had limited knowledge and wanted to learn more. Specialists were the most knowledgeable about using IOS in dentistry, followed by interns. Specialists believed that using IOS improved diagnosis and treatment planning. Only specialists knew various scanning techniques with IOS. An online survey by Hegedus T et al. found that dentists, dental technicians, and CAD/CAM experts used 3D printers for various dental purposes [34]. in another study, HR Park and colleagues found that the attitudes of dental hygienists towards the use of IOS and digital impression technology improved after receiving training [35].

The primary characteristic of IOS is precision. However, there are factors that affect accuracy, such as saliva, scanning sequence and method. Poor scanning can lead to inaccuracies in the prosthesis [36–38]. A study found that CS 3600 and TRIOS 3 scanners are highly functional and accurate for routine clinical use. They perform well in capturing the upper dental arch, particularly the front region [39]. Additional factors affecting intraoral scanner accuracy include ambient light, distance, preparatory plan, scanning technique, experience, light colors in scanners, and lighting used during scanning [37, 38, 40]. Survey participants acknowledge the importance of considering these aspects, particularly younger participants with less experience (Tables 3 and 4).

Aman et al. and previous studies assessed the awareness of intraoral scanners and lighting effects on their accuracy among dental practitioners and students [26]. Clinicians should be aware that lighting conditions significantly impact IOS scan accuracy and should use a light meter with digital equipment.

The use of technology and AI in dentistry is changing thinking and working. Dentists need to know how to deal with real objects. In our opinion, the problem lies in the dentistry syllabus, which doesn’t emphasize enough to the technological advancements and thus the field remains unfamiliar.

In this current research, most individuals chose CDE programs and expressed concerns about the expensive nature of training courses acting as a hindrance to learning. When considering the most effective approach to acquiring additional knowledge about IOS, females believed that adding to the teaching curriculum would be beneficial, while males suggested continued dental education programs and workshops. Specialists and interns also favored continued dental education programs and workshops to gain knowledge about IOS.

The issue is adopting new digital technologies in dental treatment without disregarding manual skills training. Students need access to computer-assisted methods for learning digital dentistry, but this is a barrier in educational institutions. The foundational curriculum in dentistry lacks exposure to cutting-edge technology advancements. Incorporating computerized approaches into the undergraduate curriculum would benefit from involving many centers. Students have a favorable attitude towards integrating digital dentistry into the preclinical curriculum. After training sessions, students feel confident in using digital preparation analysis and CAD/CAM workflow. Prosthodontic students, dentists, and postgraduates desire additional training [41–46]. The findings of the study conducted by schlenz et al. [11] demonstrated that students have a favourable attitude towards integrating digital dentistry into the preclinical curriculum [11].

Limitations

The limitations of the study included its limited sample size and low response rate equated to the area covered, thus findings should not be generalized. Inherent limitations of cross-sectional study type (non-response from the participants), close-ended questionnaire (suggestions or ideas to participants for questions, simplistic responses to complex questions, misinterpretation of question may go unnoticed) should also be taken into considerations. The dental interns would have more access to learning in a college but cannot compete with the specialists in the field. A more uniform number of participants, according to their expertise, would give us more significant findings about the critical knowledge gaps and learning curve.

Recommendations

Based on this analysis, it is suggested that IOS be included in the BDS curriculum for students to learn about advanced technologies. Dental education programs should be conducted to enhance dentists’ knowledge of IOS. Authorities should promote and motivate the use of IOS in dental treatment. Future studies should collaborate with government and private agencies to determine the use of IOS among dentists in Saudi Arabia. A study segregating nationality is recommended to develop country-wise policies.

Conclusion

Within this study’s limitations, most dental practitioners were found to be familiar with IOS. Practitioner qualifications are associated significantly with IOS awareness. Specialists knew more about IOS than residents and general dentists. IOS was well-received regardless of operator training and experience. Operator background affected scanning performance, training needs, and benefits. The expense of purchasing IOS continues to be a significant obstacle on the path of digital dentistry. There is an urgent need to increase awareness of IOS concept at the undergraduate level as it may increase the knowledge and awareness among the new dentists thus the treatment efficiency and effectiveness.

Sheaves of A ​ V AV -modules on quasi-projective varieties
Yuly Billig, Emile Bouaziz

Abstract
. We study sheaves of modules for the Lie algebra of vector fields with the action of the algebra of functions, compatible via the Leibniz rule. A crucial role in this theory is played by the virtual jets of vector fields – jets that evaluate to a zero vector field under the anchor map. Virtual jets of vector fields form a vector bundle ℒ + {\mathcal{L}}_{+} whose fiber is Lie algebra L ^ + \widehat{L}_{+} of vanishing at zero derivations of power series. We show that a sheaf of A ​ V AV -modules is characterized by two ingredients – it is a module for ℒ + {\mathcal{L}}_{+} and an ℒ + {\mathcal{L}}_{+} -charged D D -module. For each rational finite-dimensional representation of L ^ + \widehat{L}_{+} , we construct a bundle of jet A ​ V AV -modules. We also show that Rudakov modules may be realized as tensor products of jet modules with a D D -module of delta functions.

1.
Introduction

The theory of
D
-modules (modules over the algebra of differential operators) has been very successful. The technique of
D
-modules was used in the proof of Kazhdan-Luzstig conjecture
[
1
,
8
]
and in the formulation of the geometric Langlands correspondence
[
11
,
12
]
.
D
-modules may be viewed as modules over the Lie algebra of vector fields, together with the action of the algebra of functions, satisfying the Leibniz rule and subject to the condition that the composition
f\circ\eta
of the actions by function
f
and vector field
\eta
coincides with the action by vector field
f\eta
.

There are many examples from geometry where we have the actions of both vector fields and functions, where the above composition property fails. It does not hold, for example, for the adjoint action of vector fields. An
AV
-module is a generalization of a
D
-module, where we drop the composition axiom. We say that
M
is an
AV
-module if
M
is a module for Lie algebra
V
of vector fields and a module for the commutative algebra
A
of functions, with two actions compatible via the Leibniz rule.

A general theory of
AV
-modules on a smooth affine algebraic variety was developed in
[
3
,
4
,
7
,
6
,
10
]
. The machinery of
AV
-modules was indispensable in establishing classifications of simple weight modules for the Lie algebras of vector fields on a torus
[
3
]
, and an affine space
[
18
,
13
]
.

The category of
AV
-modules may be presented as modules over an associative algebra
A\#U(V)
, which is the weak enveloping algebra of Lie-Rinehart pair
(A,V)
.

The goal of the present paper is to extend the theory of
AV
-modules to the case of a smooth quasiprojective variety
X
of dimension
n
, which requires working in a sheaf-theoretic setting. It was seen in
[
5
]
that sheafification requires taking a completion
A\widehat{\#}U(V)
of the associative algebra
A\#U(V)
. This completion is the strong enveloping algebra of the Lie-Rinehart pair
(A,A\widehat{\#}V)
,
where
A\widehat{\#}V
is the Lie algebra of
\infty
-jets of vector fields. This yields a quasicoherent sheaf
\mathcal{AV}
of associative algebras.

Locally, in an étale chart, we have an isomorphism
[
18
,
6
,
5
]
:

A\widehat{\#}U(V)\cong D\otimes U(\widehat{L}_{+}),

where
D
is the algebra of differential operators and
\widehat{L}_{+}
is the Lie algebra of derivations of the algebra of power series
K[[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]]
which vanish at
0
.
In fact,
\mathcal{AV}
contains as a subsheaf a bundle
{\mathcal{L}}_{+}
of Lie algebras with
\widehat{L}_{+}
as its fiber. This is a bundle of virtual jets of vector fields, that is, jets that evaluate to a zero vector field under the anchor map. In contrast to this, locally defined algebras of differential operators do not glue into a subsheaf in
\mathcal{AV}
.

Our main result, Theorem
15
, states that a sheaf of
AV
-modules is defined by two ingredients – it is a sheaf of modules for
{\mathcal{L}}_{+}
, and also an
{\mathcal{L}}_{+}
-charged
D
-module (see Definition
13
).

In Section
6
we construct an important class of sheaves of jet modules. For this, we generalize the notion of the jacobian of a change of coordinates. First, we show that
\infty
-jets of functions is a bundle of commutative algebras with fiber
K[[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]]
. The group of automorphisms of
K[[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]]
is an infinite-dimensional algebraic group scheme
with Lie algebra
\widehat{L}_{+}
. We show that a change of coordinates transformation on an intersection
U_{1}\cap U_{2}
of two étale charts gives rise to an element of
\text{Aut\,}K[[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]]
with coefficients in
\mathcal{O}(U_{1}\cap U_{2})
. If we factor out the terms in
K[[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]]
of degrees greater than 1, this will reduce to the jacobian of the change of coordinates.

We use this generalization of the jacobian to construct a functor from the category of rational finite-dimensional
\widehat{L}_{+}
-modules to the category of sheaves of
AV
-modules. The corresponding jet module is a vector bundle with the given
\widehat{L}_{+}
-module as its fiber.

In 1974, Rudakov introduced a class of modules for the Lie algebra of vector fields on an affine space. Generalizations of Rudakov modules supported at a non-singular point of an arbitrary affine variety were constructed in
[
4
]
. In Section
7
, we give a realization of Rudakov modules as tensor products of jet modules with the
D
-module of delta functions supported at a given point.

In Section
8
, we define holonomic sheaves of
AV
-modules. We state a conjecture that every holonomic
AV
-module is differentiable, that is, there exists
N\geq 1
for which the module is annihilated by all elements of
A\#V
of the form

\sum_{k=0}^{N}(-1)^{k}{N\choose k}f^{k}\#f^{N-k}\eta

with
f\in A
,
\eta\in V
. In
[
10
]
, the authors proved that
AV
-modules that are finitely generated over
A
, are differentiable. This result is a special case of this conjecture. Rudakov modules provide another class of holonomic modules for which the conjecture holds (Lemma
22
).

As an illustration of our methods, in the final section of the paper, we construct two families of rank 2 bundles of
AV
-modules on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

2.
AV
-modules

Let
K
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0.
Let
A
be the commutative algebra of functions on a smooth irreducible affine algebraic variety
X
, and let
V=\text{Der\,}(A)
be the Lie algebra of derivations on
A
. Finally, let
D
be the associative algebra of differential operators, defined as the subalgebra in
\text{End\,}_{K}(A)
generated by
A
(acting on itself by multiplication) and
V
(acting on
A
by derivations).

An
AV
-module
M
is a module for both Lie algebra
V
and for commutative unital algebra
A
, with the two actions compatible via the Leibniz rule:

\eta(fm)=\eta(f)m+f(\eta m),\ \ \text{for \ }\eta\in V,\,f\in A,\,m\in M.

The commutative algebra
A
and the Lie algebra
V
themselves are naturally
AV
-modules, with the former even a
D
-module.

The category of
AV
-modules has a tensor product
M\otimes_{A}N
and internal mapping spaces
\operatorname{Map}(M,N)
adjoint to the tensor product in the usual sense that

\operatorname{Hom}_{AV}(M\otimes_{A}N,L)=\operatorname{Hom}_{AV}(M,\operatorname{Map}(N,L)).

\operatorname{Map}(M,N)
is constructed as the module
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N)
of all
A
-linear homomorphisms from
M
to
N
, with an evident action of
V
. The unit of the tensor structure is the
AV
module
A
, and so in particular the above adjunction induces an isomorphism

\operatorname{Hom}_{AV}(A,\operatorname{Map}(M,N))=\operatorname{Hom}_{AV}(M,N).

We record in particular the
dual

AV
-module
\operatorname{Map}(M,A)
, which we recall has underlying
A
-module
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,A)
.

Taking the dual module of
V
, we construct the module of differential 1-forms:
\Omega^{1}=\text{Hom}_{A}(V,A)
. By taking a tensor product of
m
copies of
V
with
k
copies of
\Omega^{1}
, we construct
AV
-modules of
(m,k)
-tensors.

There exists an associative algebra that controls the category of
AV
-modules. This algebra is the smash product
A\#U(V)
of the universal enveloping algebra
U(V)
, viewed as a Hopf algebra, with its module
A
. As a vector space, it is the space

A\otimes_{K}U(V)
, where the commutation relations between the elements of
A
and
V
are given by the Leibniz rule:

\eta\cdot f=\eta(f)+f\cdot\eta
.

There is a natural surjective homomorphism of associative algebras
A\#U(V)\rightarrow D
. This implies that every
D
-module is automatically an
AV
-module.
D
-modules are precisely
AV
-modules with an additional axiom

f\,(\eta\,m)=(f\eta)\,m.

While
A
is a
D
-module, the
AV
-module
V
does not have a natural
D
-module structure, since the Lie bracket in
V
is not
A
-linear and so the above axiom does not hold in
V
.

The pair of algebras
(A,V)
is a Lie-Rinehart pair. Let us recall the definition:

Definition 1
.

A pair
(\widetilde{A},\widetilde{V})
is a Lie-Rinehart pair if
\widetilde{A}
is a unital commutative associative algebra,
\widetilde{V}
is a Lie algebra,
\widetilde{V}
is an
\widetilde{A}
-module,
\widetilde{V}
acts on
\widetilde{A}
by derivations, and the Lie bracket in
\widetilde{V}
satisfies

[\mu,f\eta]=\mu(f)\eta+f[\mu,\eta],\ \ \ \text{for \ }f\in\widetilde{A},\ \mu,\eta\in\widetilde{V}.

A Lie-Rinehart pair
(\widetilde{A},\widetilde{V})
has two enveloping algebras:
the weak enveloping algebra

U_{\text{weak}}(\widetilde{A},\widetilde{V})=\widetilde{A}\#U(\widetilde{V}),

and
the strong enveloping algebra

U_{\text{strong}}(\widetilde{A},\widetilde{V})=U_{\text{weak}}(\widetilde{A},\widetilde{V})/\left<f\#\eta-1\#f\eta\right>.

When
A
is the algebra of functions on a smooth irreducible affine variety, and
V=\text{Der\,}(A)
,
U_{\text{strong}}(A,V)
is isomorphic to the algebra
D
of differential operators on
X
.

It is an important fact that subspace
A\#V
is a Lie subalgebra in
A\#U(V)
with Lie bracket

[f\#\eta,g\#\mu]=f\eta(g)\#\mu-g\mu(f)\#\eta+fg\#[\eta,\mu].

Note that
(A,A\#V)
is also a Lie-Rinehart pair, and

U_{\text{strong}}(A,A\#V)\cong U_{\text{weak}}(A,V).

Definition 2
.

An
AV
-module
M
is called differentiable if there exists
N\geq 1
such that the following elements of
A\#V

\sum_{k=0}^{N}(-1)^{k}{N\choose k}f^{k}\#f^{N-k}\eta

annihilate
M
for all
f\in A
and
\eta\in V
.

Note that an
AV
-module is differentiable with
N=1
precisely when it is a
D
-module.

For an
AV
-module to be
N
-differentiable is equivalent to the action of
V
being a differential operator of order at most
N
, in the sense of Grothendieck
[
14
]
.
It is easy to see that the subcategory of
N
-differentiable
AV
-modules is closed under tensor products and duality.

3.
Group of automorphisms of the algebra of power series and its representations

In this section, we discuss the group of automorphisms of the algebra
K[[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]]
of power series, and its finite-dimensional representations.

First of all, we point out that every automorphism of
K[[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]]
is continuous in the power series topology. To see this, let
\mathfrak{m}=\left<X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\right>
be the (unique) maximal ideal in
K[[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]]
. Since this is the unique maximal ideal, every automorphism preserves
\mathfrak{m}
, and hence also preserves all powers of
\mathfrak{m}
. This implies continuity.

Continuity of automorphisms implies that every automorphism
F
of
K[[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]]
is determined by the images of
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
, and may be written as
X_{i}\mapsto F_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
, where

F_{i}=\sum_{s\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{1}{s!}\,A_{i,s}X^{s},{\hskip 28.45274pt}{\text{w}ith\ }A_{i,s}\in K,

where the matrix
(A_{i,\epsilon_{j}})
is invertible. Here
\epsilon_{j}
is an element of
\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
with a single 1 in position
j
.

This yields an infinite-dimensional group scheme with the algebra of functions
K[A_{i,s}\,|\,i=1,\ldots,n,\,s\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}\backslash\{0\}]_{(\det)}
, localized at
\det=\det(A_{i,\epsilon_{j}})
.

The algebra of functions on a group has a Hopf algebra structure, and for
\text{Aut\,}K[[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]]
the coproduct is given on the generators as follows:

\Delta(A_{i,s})=\sum_{r=1}^{|s|}\sum_{1\leq j_{1},\ldots,j_{r}\leq n}\ \sum_{p\in P_{r}(s)}A_{i,\epsilon_{j_{1}}+\ldots+\epsilon_{j_{r}}}\otimes(A_{j_{1},p_{1}}\cdot\ldots\cdot A_{j_{r},p_{r}}),

\Delta(\det{}^{-1})=\det{}^{-1}\otimes\det{}^{-1}.

Here for
s\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{+}
we use notiations
|s|=s_{1}+\ldots+s_{n}
,
s!=s_{1}!\ldots s_{n}!
, etc,
and
P_{r}(s)
is a set of partitions of
s
into
r
parts,
s=p_{1}+\ldots+p_{r}
.

We point out the peculiar property of this coproduct that it is linear in the first tensor factor, and non-linear in the second.

For example,

\Delta(A_{i,\epsilon_{a}+\epsilon_{b}})=A_{i,\epsilon_{k}}\otimes A_{k,\epsilon_{a}+\epsilon_{b}}+A_{i,\epsilon_{k}+\epsilon_{\ell}}\otimes A_{k,\epsilon_{a}}A_{\ell,\epsilon_{b}}.

Here, and throughout the paper we use Einstein’s notations, with summation over repeated indices.

The above example of the coproduct is a reflection of the chain rule:

\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{a}\partial x_{b}}F_{i}(G(x))=\frac{\partial F_{i}}{\partial x_{k}}(G(x))\frac{\partial^{2}G_{k}}{\partial x_{a}\partial x_{b}}+\frac{\partial^{2}F_{i}}{\partial x_{k}\partial x_{\ell}}(G(x))\frac{\partial G_{k}}{\partial x_{a}}\frac{\partial G_{\ell}}{\partial x_{b}}.

The Lie algebra of the group
\text{Aut\,}K[[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]]
is a (proper) subalgebra in the Lie algebra of derivations of

K[[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]]
. An argument, similar to one given above, shows that every derivation of the algebra of power series is continuous. Thus

\text{Der\,}K[[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]]=\mathop{\oplus}\limits_{i=1}^{n}K[[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]]\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{i}}.

The Lie algebra of the group
\text{Aut\,}K[[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]]
is

\widehat{L}_{+}=\mathop{\oplus}\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathfrak{m}\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{i}}.

Derivations
\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{i}}
do not belong to the Lie algebra of the group of automorphisms, since they correspond to the shifts
X_{i}\mapsto X_{i}+a
, which are not automorphisms of the algebra of power series.

Let us discuss Lie correspondence between the group of automorphisms and
\widehat{L}_{+}
. The group

\text{Aut\,}K[[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]]
is a semidirect product of
GL_{n}
(linear automorphisms) and the normal subgroup
\mathcal{N}
of
automorphisms
X_{i}\mapsto X_{i}+\text{\it higher order terms}
, for all
i=1,\ldots,n
. Subgroup
\mathcal{N}
is pronilpotent – it has a descending chain of normal subgroups with finite-dimensional nilpotent quotients.

Using coordinates
\{A_{i,s}\}
, we obtain the following realizations:

\\
AutK[[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]]=GL_{n}\ltimes\prod\limits_{r=1}^{\infty}V\otimes S^{r}(V^{*}),

\mathcal{N}=\{I\}\times\prod\limits_{r=1}^{\infty}V\otimes S^{r}(V^{*}),

where
V=K^{n}
.

Likewise, Lie algebra
\widehat{L}_{+}
is the direct sum of its subalgebra
L_{0}\cong gl_{n}
, spanned by
\left\{X_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{i}}\right\}
, and a pronilpotent ideal
\mathfrak{m}\widehat{L}_{+}
.

The exponential map
\exp:\ \mathfrak{m}\widehat{L}_{+}\rightarrow\mathcal{N}
associates to a derivation
\eta
an automorphism, sending
X_{i}
to

\exp(\eta)X_{i}
. It is easy to see that the exponential map between
\mathfrak{m}\widehat{L}_{+}
and
\mathcal{N}
is bijective.

Example 3
.

\exp\left(\alpha X^{2}\frac{d}{dX}\right)g(X)=g\left(\frac{X}{1-\alpha X}\right).

Finally, let us consider finite-dimensional representations of
\widehat{L}_{+}
and of the group of automorphisms of the algebra of power series.

Lemma 4

(cf.
[
2
]
)
.

Let
W
be a finite-dimensional module for
\widehat{L}_{+}
. Then there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
, depending on
\dim W
, such that

\mathfrak{m}^{N}\widehat{L}_{+}
annihilates
W
.

Proof.

Let us outline the sketch of the proof. A version of this Lemma for the algebra of polynomials was given in
[
2
]
.

Consider the action on
W
by derivation
X_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{1}}+\ldots+X_{n}\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{n}}
. The space
W
may be decomposed into a direct sum of the generalized eigenspaces with respect to this operator. Denote by
S_{k}
the span of monomials in
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
of total degree
k
. Then
S_{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{i}}
increases the eigenvalue by
k-1
.
Since the total number of the generalized eigenspaces is finite, we conclude that for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
,

S_{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{i}}
annihilates
W
for all
k\geq N
and for all
i=1,\ldots,n
.

It is easy to check that for
N>1

\left[S_{N}\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{i}}\oplus S_{N+1}\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{i}},\,\mathfrak{m}\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{i}}\right]=\mathfrak{m}^{N}\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{i}}.

This implies the claim of the Lemma.
∎

Let
W
be a finite-dimensional
\widehat{L}_{+}
-module. We will call
W
rational if the action of subalgebra
L_{0}
integrates to a rational
GL_{n}
-module.

Lemma 5
.

Let
(W,\rho)
be a finite-dimensional rational
\widehat{L}_{+}
-module.
Then
W
admits the action of the group
\text{Aut\,}K[[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]]
, compatible with the exponential map.

Proof.

We have seen in the previous Lemma that there exists
N
such that
\mathfrak{m}^{N}\widehat{L}_{+}
annihilates
W
. Thus
W
is a module for a finite-dimensional Lie algebra
\widehat{L}_{+}/\mathfrak{m}^{N}\widehat{L}_{+}
, which is a semidirect product of
gl_{n}
with a nilpotent ideal

\mathfrak{m}\widehat{L}_{+}/\mathfrak{m}^{N}\widehat{L}_{+}
.
It follows from the proof of Lemma
4
that
\mathfrak{m}\widehat{L}_{+}/\mathfrak{m}^{N}\widehat{L}_{+}
acts on
W
by nilpotent operators.
Using the exponential map, we can define the action on
W
of the nilpotent quotient group

\overline{\mathcal{N}}=\{I\}\times\prod\limits_{r=1}^{N}V\otimes S^{r}(V^{*}).

Now we have two algebraic groups,
GL_{n}
and
\overline{\mathcal{N}}
, acting rationally on
W
.
Their Lie algebras span the Lie algebra
\widehat{L}_{+}/\mathfrak{m}^{N}\widehat{L}_{+}
, which also acts on
W
. By Theorem 7.6 in
[
9
]
,
these actions extend to the action on
W
of an algebraic group with Lie algebra
\widehat{L}_{+}/\mathfrak{m}^{N}\widehat{L}_{+}
.
Let us show that this algebraic group is

GL_{n}\ltimes\prod\limits_{r=1}^{N}V\otimes S^{r}(V^{*}).

Group structure in this group is determined by the group structures of
GL_{n}
,
\overline{\mathcal{N}}
, and by the conjugation action of
GL_{n}
on
\overline{\mathcal{N}}
. Since we know that
GL_{n}
and
\overline{\mathcal{N}}
act on
W
, we only need to show that

(1)

\rho(g)\rho(\exp(\eta))\rho(g^{-1})=\rho(g\exp(\eta)g^{-1}),

for all
g\in GL_{n}
and
\eta\in\mathfrak{m}\widehat{L}_{+}/\mathfrak{m}^{N}\widehat{L}_{+}
.
We have

\rho(g)\rho(\exp(\eta))\rho(g^{-1})=\rho(g)\exp(\rho(\eta))\rho(g^{-1})=\exp\left(\rho(g)\rho(\eta)\rho(g^{-1})\right).

Let
g=\exp(u)
, where
u
is a nilpotent element in
sl_{n}
. Then

\displaystyle\rho(g)\rho(\eta)\rho(g^{-1})

\displaystyle=\exp(\rho(u))\rho(\eta)\exp(-\rho(u))=\exp\left(\text{ad}\rho(u)\right)\rho(\eta)

\displaystyle=\rho(\exp(\text{ad}u)\eta)=\rho(g\exp(\eta)g^{-1}).

In a similar way, we can see that this equality is also valid for the scalar matrices in
GL_{n}
. Since the desired equality holds for the exponentials of the nilpotent elements, which, together with scalar matrices, generate
GL_{n}
, relation (
1
) holds for all
g\in GL_{n}
. This shows that we have an action of
GL_{n}\ltimes\overline{\mathcal{N}}
on
W
. Finally, if we postulate that the subgroup corresponding to
\mathfrak{m}^{N}\widehat{L}_{+}
acts on
W
trivially, we get the action of the group
\text{Aut\,}K[[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]]
.
∎

4.
Jets

Passing from
AV
-module theory on an affine variety to a sheaf-based theory on non-affine varieties will require taking completions of our algebras. This is done using the notion of jets.

From now on, let
X
be a smooth quasiprojective variety of dimension
n
with the sheaves
\mathcal{O}
of functions,

\Theta
of vector fields, and
\mathcal{D}
of differential operators.

In order to perform a local analysis of the sheaves we are interested in, we will use
étale charts
.

Definition 6
.

An affine open subset
U\subset X
is called an étale chart if there exist functions

x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in A=\mathcal{O}(U)
such that

(1) the set
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
is algebraically independent, that is
K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]\subset A
,

(2) every
f\in A
is algebraic over
K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
,

(3) derivations
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},\ldots,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}
of
K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
extend to derivations of
A
.

We will call such
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})

uniformizing parameters
on
U
. Since
A
is algebraic over

K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, an extension of
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}
to
A
is unique. Vector fields
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},\ldots,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}
commute.

Lemma 7
.

(
[
15
, Theorem III.6.1]
,
[
3
]
)

Let
U
be an étale chart of
X
with uniformizing parameters
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
.
Let
A=\mathcal{O}(U)
,
V=\Theta(U)
,
D=\mathcal{D}(U)
. Then

(1)

V=\mathop{\bigoplus}\limits_{i=1}^{n}A\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}},

(2)

D=\mathop{\bigoplus}\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}A\partial^{k},

where for
k=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})
we set
\partial^{k}=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}\right)^{k_{1}}\ldots\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}\right)^{k_{n}}
.

(3)

\Omega^{1}(U)=\mathop{\bigoplus}\limits_{i=1}^{n}Adx_{i}

with the differential given by

df=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}dx_{i}\text{ \ for \ }f\in A.

(4) The map
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}):\ U\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{n}
is étale.

Any smooth irreducible quasi-projective variety
X
has an atlas
\{U_{i}\}
of
étale charts
,
X=\mathop{\bigcup}\limits_{i}U_{i}
(see e.g.
[
15
]
,
[
3
]
).

Let
U
be an affine open set, and let
A=\mathcal{O}(U)
,
V=\Theta(U)
,
D=\mathcal{D}(U)
.

Let
\Delta
be the kernel of the multiplication map

be an étale chart of
X
with uniformizing parameters
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
, containing point
P
.

Let
\mathfrak{m}_{P}
be the maximal ideal in
A=\mathcal{O}(U)
, corresponding to point
P
. Let
V=\Theta(U)
.

Lemma 19

(
[
4
]
)
.

There is an isomorphism of Lie algebras:

\mathfrak{m}_{P}V/\mathfrak{m}_{P}^{N+1}V\,\cong\,\widehat{L}_{+}/\mathfrak{m}^{N}\widehat{L}_{+}.

The isomorphism is given by the expansion in local parameters:

f\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\mapsto\sum_{0<|s|<N}\frac{1}{s!}\,\frac{\partial^{s}f}{\partial x^{s}}(P)\,X^{s}\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{i}}.

By Lemma
4
, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
W
is annihilated by
\mathfrak{m}^{N}\widehat{L}_{+}
. The isomorphism of the previous Lemma allows us to view
W
as a module for the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{m}_{P}V
with
\mathfrak{m}_{P}^{N+1}V
acting trivially on
W
.
We also view
W
as an
A
-module, with
fw=f(P)w
for
f\in A
,
w\in W
.

Let us also state the following Lemma. Its proof may be given using the methods of
[
3
]
.

Lemma 20
.

Every non-zero ideal of
\mathfrak{m}_{P}V
contains
\mathfrak{m}_{P}^{N+1}V
for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
.

Corollary 21
.

Let
W
be a finite-dimensional representation of
\mathfrak{m}_{P}V
. Then there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\mathfrak{m}_{P}^{N+1}V
annihilates
W
.

Proof.

Annihilator of a module is an ideal, and it must be non-zero since
\mathfrak{m}_{P}V
is infinite-dimensional and
W
is finite-dimensional.
∎

Rudakov module
R^{W}_{P}
is defined as the induced module

R^{W}_{P}=\text{Ind\,}_{A\#U(\mathfrak{m}_{P}V)}^{A\#U(V)}W\cong K\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},\ldots,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}\right]\otimes W.

Lemma 22
.

Rudakov module
R^{W}_{P}
is differentiable, it is annihilated by
\Delta^{N+1}\otimes_{A}V
.

Proof.

This proof is due to Henrique Rocha
[
17
]
.

By Lemma 12 in
[
5
]
, the space
\Delta^{N+1}\otimes_{A}V
is spanned by

\left\{(g\otimes 1)\delta(f_{1})\delta(f_{2})\ldots\delta(f_{N+1})\eta\,|\,g,f_{1},\ldots,f_{N+1}\in A,\eta\in V\right\}.

Since
A=K\cdot 1\oplus\mathfrak{m}_{P}
, we may assume that each
f_{j}
is either 1, or belongs to
\mathfrak{m}_{P}
. However,
\delta(1)=0
.
Thus we may assume that all
f_{j}\in\mathfrak{m}_{P}
.

Let us show that
\Delta^{N+1}\otimes_{A}V
annihilates
1\otimes W
.

\displaystyle(g\otimes 1)\delta(f_{1})\delta(f_{2})\ldots

\displaystyle\delta(f_{N+1})\,\eta\,w

\displaystyle=g\sum_{I\dot{\cup}J=\{1,\ldots,N+1\}}(-1)^{|I|}\left(\prod_{i\in I}f_{i}\right)\left(\prod_{j\in J}f_{j}\cdot\eta\right)w.

The terms with
I\neq\varnothing
,
J\neq\varnothing
vanish since in this case
\prod\limits_{j\in J}f_{j}\cdot\eta\in\mathfrak{m}_{P}V
and
\big(\prod\limits_{j\in J}f_{j}\cdot\eta\big)w\in W
, while
\prod\limits_{i\in I}f_{i}
annihilates
W
.
For the two remaining terms,
(f_{1}\ldots f_{N+1}\eta)w=0
since
f_{1}\ldots f_{N+1}\eta\in\mathfrak{m}_{P}^{N+1}V
, while

(f_{1}\ldots f_{N+1})\,\eta\,w=(f_{1}\ldots f_{N})\,\eta\,f_{N+1}\,w+(f_{1}\ldots f_{N})\,\eta(f_{N+1})\,w=0.

The general case is then proved by induction on the degree of the monomial in
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},\ldots,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}
in front of
w
.
To carry out the induction step, we will need the commutation relation:

\displaystyle\big[\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}},\,(g\otimes 1)\delta(f_{1})\delta(f_{2})\ldots

\displaystyle\delta(f_{N+1})\eta\big]=(\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{i}}\otimes 1)\delta(f_{1})\delta(f_{2})\ldots\delta(f_{N+1})\eta

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{N+1}(g\otimes 1)\delta(f_{1})\ldots\delta\left(\frac{\partial f_{k}}{\partial x_{i}}\right)\ldots\delta(f_{N+1})\eta

\displaystyle+(g\otimes 1)\delta(f_{1})\delta(f_{2})\ldots\delta(f_{N+1})\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}},\eta\right].

Decomposing
\frac{\partial f_{k}}{\partial x_{i}}
into
K\cdot 1+\mathfrak{m}_{P}
, we will be able to carry out the step of induction.
∎

Corollary 23
.

The Rudakov module
R^{W}_{P}
admits the action of the completed algebra
A\widehat{\#}U(V)
.

Note that the Rudakov module is supported at the point
P
.

Consider now the
D
-module
\mathcal{F}_{P}
of delta functions supported at a point
P\in X
. Let
U
be an étale chart containing point
P
. Let
\delta_{P}
be the generator of the evaluation
A
-module:
f\delta_{P}=f(P)\delta_{P}
. This induces a
D
-module

\mathcal{F}_{P}(U)=K\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},\ldots,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}\right]\otimes\delta_{P}.

As a sheaf,
\mathcal{F}_{P}
is also supported at
P
.

Let us construct a realization of Rudakov modules using delta functions. Let
K_{\text{tr}}
be a 1-dimensional
gl_{n}
-module, with the action given by the trace of a matrix. If we view it as a
GL_{n}
-module, the action is given by the determinant, and the corresponding module of tensor fields is the module
\Omega^{n}
of top differential forms.

Theorem 24
.

Let
W
be a rational finite-dimensional module for
\widehat{L}_{+}
, and let
P\in X
.
We have an isomorphism of sheaves of
AV
-modules:

\mathcal{R}_{P}^{W}\cong\mathcal{F}_{P}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{J}^{W\otimes K_{\text{tr}}}.

Proof.

Since both sheaves are supported at
P
, it is sufficient to verify the isomorphism locally, in an étale chart
U
, containing point
P
. We have

\mathcal{R}_{P}^{W}(U)\cong\mathcal{F}_{P}(U)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{J}^{W\otimes K_{\text{tr}}}(U)

as vector spaces, since each of them is isomorphic to
K\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},\ldots,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}\right]\otimes W
.
Let us show that this identification is an isomorphism of
AV
-modules. Since the commutation relations of the elements of
A
and
V
with

\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}
are the same in both modules, it is sufficient to show that the actions of
A
and
V
on
W
agree.

In both modules,
A
acts on
W
by evaluation at
P
. Since in a jet module
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}
annihilates the space
1\otimes W
,
we see that in both modules
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}
acts on
W
freely. The final case to consider is the action of
f\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}
with
f\in\mathfrak{m}_{P}
on
W
and
W\otimes K_{\text{tr}}
respectively.

In Rudakov module
f\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}
acts on
W
via the action (see Lemma
19
):

\sum_{0<|s|\leq N}\frac{1}{s!}\,\frac{\partial^{s}f}{\partial x^{s}}(P)\,\rho\left(X^{s}\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{i}}\right).

In
\mathcal{F}_{P}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{J}^{W\otimes K_{\text{tr}}}
the action is

\displaystyle f\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\delta_{P}\otimes w\otimes 1_{\text{tr}}

\displaystyle=\left(-\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}(P)\delta_{P}\right)\otimes w\otimes 1_{\text{tr}}

\displaystyle+\delta_{P}\otimes\sum_{0<|s|\leq N}\frac{1}{s!}\,\frac{\partial^{s}f}{\partial x^{s}}(P)\,\rho\left(X^{s}\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{i}}\right)w\otimes 1_{\text{tr}}

\displaystyle+\delta_{P}\otimes w\otimes\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{k}}(P)\text{tr}(E_{ki})1_{\text{tr}}.

The first and the last terms in the right-hand side cancel out, and we get that the two actions on
\mathcal{R}_{P}^{W}
and
on
\mathcal{F}_{P}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{J}^{W\otimes K_{\text{tr}}}
agree.
∎

Remark 25
.

We note that this gives another proof that Rudakov modules are differentiable, as both of the tensor factors in the decomposition of the above theorem are themselves differentiable.

In
[
4
]
the authors constructed a contravariant pairing

R_{P}^{W}\times J^{W*}\rightarrow K,

where
W^{*}
is the dual
\widehat{L}_{+}
-module. Let us interpret this result in light of Theorem
24
.
The above invariant pairing is equivalent to the existence of a homomorphism of
V
-modules (but not as
A
-modules!)

R_{P}^{W}\otimes_{A}J^{W*}\rightarrow K.

By Theorem
24
,

R_{P}^{W}\otimes_{A}J^{W*}\cong\mathcal{F}_{P}\otimes_{A}\Omega^{n}\otimes_{A}J^{W}\otimes_{A}J^{W^{*}}.

Since
J^{W^{*}}=\text{Hom}_{A}(J^{W},A)
as
AV
-modules, there exists a homomorphism of
AV
-modules

J^{W^{*}}\otimes_{A}J^{W}\rightarrow A.

Also, by Theorem
24
, we can realize the Rudakov module corresponding to the trivial one-dimensional
\widehat{L}_{+}
-module
K\cdot 1
as a tensor product:

R_{P}^{K\cdot 1}\cong\mathcal{F}_{P}\otimes_{A}\Omega^{n}.

It is easy to see that for this Rudakov module, the space
VR_{P}^{K(1)}
is a
V
-submodule of codimension 1, which gives us a
homomorphism of
V
-modules:

\mathcal{F}_{P}\otimes_{A}\Omega^{n}\rightarrow K.

Combining, we get a chain of homomorphisms of
V
-modules:

R_{P}^{W}\otimes_{A}J^{W*}\cong\mathcal{F}_{P}\otimes_{A}\Omega^{n}\otimes_{A}J^{W}\otimes_{A}J^{W^{*}}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}_{P}\otimes_{A}\Omega^{n}\otimes_{A}A\rightarrow K.

Remark 26
.

Recall that by Kashiwara’s lemma,
D
-modules supported on a subvariety
Z\subset X
are precisely those pushed forward from
Z
. In particular, we have the
D
-module of delta functions along
Z
, here denoted
\mathcal{F}_{Z}
. Taking the tensor product of these with jet-type
AV

AV
modules generalizing Rudakov’s original construction. It remains an interesting problem to investigate functoriality of
AV
-modules under general maps, or even closed embeddings.

8.
Holonomic
\mathcal{AV}
-modules

Let
C
be an associative algebra and
M
be a
C
-module with a finite set
S
of generators.
For a finite subset
B\subset C
, we define a filtration in
M
:

F_{0}^{B}\subset F_{1}^{B}\subset F_{2}^{B}\subset\ldots,

where
F_{0}^{B}=\text{Span\,}(S)
and
F_{k+1}^{B}=\text{Span\,}\left\{F_{k}^{B},\,BF_{k}^{B}\right\}
.

Definition 27
.

Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of
M
is

\text{GK}\dim(M)=\sup\limits_{B}\varlimsup\limits_{k\to\infty}\root k \of{\dim F_{k}^{B}}.

Definition 28
.

A sheaf
\mathcal{M}
of
\mathcal{AV}
-modules on
X
is called holonomic if for every étale chart
U
, an
\mathcal{AV}(U)
-module
\mathcal{M}(U)

is finitely generated and has Gelfand-Kirillov dimension
n=\dim X
.

It is easy to see that both jet modules and Rudakov modules are holonomic.

Conjecture.
Every holonomic sheaf of
AV
-modules is differentiable.

It was proved in
[
10
]
that for a smooth affine variety every
AV
-module, which is finitely generated over
A
, is differentiable. This is a special case of the above conjecture. In Lemma
22
, we proved that this conjecture also holds for Rudakov modules.

Let us finish this section with one more conjecture.

Conjecture.
The Lie algebra of vector fields on a smooth affine variety is finitely generated as a Lie algebra.

9.
Examples of rank 2 bundles of
AV
-modules on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

As an illustration of our methods, we will construct in this section two families of rank 2 bundles of
AV
-modules on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

In our first example, we begin with a family of 2-dimensional representations
W
of
\widehat{L}_{+}
:

\displaystyle\rho_{m}\left(X\frac{d}{dX}\right)=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}m+1&0\\
0&m\end{pmatrix},\ \ \rho_{m}\left(X^{2}\frac{d}{dX}\right)=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\rho_{m}\left(X^{k}\frac{d}{dX}\right)=0\text{ \ for \ }k\geq 3.

This representation is rational whenever
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, and integrates to the following representation of
\text{Aut\,}K[[X]]
:
for
\varphi(X)=aX+bX^{2}+

higher order terms
,

\rho_{m}(\varphi)=a^{m}\begin{pmatrix}a&ba^{-1}\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.

As usual, we cover
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with two copies of
\mathbb{A}^{1}
with the coordinate transformation between the charts given by

y=-x^{-1}
(the negative sign will be more convenient for our computations). In our earlier notations,
H(x)=-x^{-1}
,
G(y)=-y^{-1}
,
\frac{dH}{dx}(G(y))=y^{2}
.

The automorphism of
K[[X]]
, corresponding to this change of variables is

\varphi_{G}(X)=G(y+Y)-G(y)=y^{-2}Y-y^{-3}Y^{2}+y^{-4}Y^{3}+\ldots

and

\rho_{m}(\varphi_{G})=y^{-2m}\begin{pmatrix}y^{-2}&-y^{-1}\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.

Let us denote the bases of the trivializations of the vector bundle
\mathcal{J}^{W}
in
x
-chart by
\{e_{1}^{x},e_{2}^{x}\}
, and in
y
-chart by

\{e_{1}^{y},e_{2}^{y}\}
. Then by (
7
), we have the following coordinate transformation in the bundle:

\displaystyle e_{1}^{x}=y^{-2m-2}e_{1}^{y},

\displaystyle e_{2}^{x}=y^{-2m}e_{2}^{y}-y^{-2m-1}e_{1}^{y}.

Using (
4
), we have the following transformations in the bundle
{\mathcal{L}}_{+}
of virtual jets of vector fields on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
:

\displaystyle X\frac{d}{dX}

\displaystyle=Y\frac{d}{dY}+y^{-1}\,Y^{2}\frac{d}{dY},

\displaystyle X^{2}\frac{d}{dX}

\displaystyle=y^{-2}\,Y^{2}\frac{d}{dY},

\displaystyle X^{3}\frac{d}{dX}

\displaystyle=y^{-4}\,Y^{3}\frac{d}{dY}\,+\text{\it higher order terms}.

It follows from Theorem
17
(and could be easily verified directly), that coordinate transformation laws in
{\mathcal{L}}_{+}
and
\mathcal{J}^{W}
are compatible, and that
\mathcal{J}^{W}
is, in fact, an
{\mathcal{L}}_{+}
-bundle.

Bundle
\mathcal{J}^{W}
is also an
{\mathcal{L}}_{+}
-charged sheaf of
D
-modules. In each chart,
D
-module structure is given by the action on functions, and the transformation law for
\frac{\partial}{\partial x}
is given by (
6
):

\frac{\partial}{\partial x}=y^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+2y\,Y\frac{d}{dY}+Y^{2}\frac{d}{dY},

and we could easily verify that this relation holds in
\mathcal{J}^{W}
.

The
AV
-module structure on
\mathcal{J}^{W}
can be written explicitly as follows:

\displaystyle f(x)\frac{d}{dx}\cdot g(x)e_{1}^{x}=f\frac{dg}{dx}\,e_{1}^{x}+(m+1)\,g\frac{df}{dx}\,e_{1}^{x},

\displaystyle f(x)\frac{d}{dx}\cdot g(x)e_{2}^{x}=f\frac{dg}{dx}\,e_{2}^{x}+m\,g\frac{df}{dx}\,e_{2}^{x}+\frac{1}{2}\,g\frac{d^{2}f}{dx^{2}}\,e_{1}^{x},

and analogously in
y
-chart.

Even though the representation we used here is rational only when
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, the above formulas yield a well-defined bundle for all
m\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
.

Our second example is based on another 2-dimensional representation of
\widehat{L}_{+}
:

\displaystyle\sigma_{m}\left(X\frac{d}{dX}\right)=\begin{pmatrix}m+2&0\\
0&m\end{pmatrix},\ \ \sigma_{m}\left(X^{2}\frac{d}{dX}\right)=0

\displaystyle\sigma_{m}\left(X^{3}\frac{d}{dX}\right)=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \sigma_{m}\left(X^{k}\frac{d}{dX}\right)=0\text{ \ for \ }k\geq 4.

When
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, this representation integrates to a rational representation of
\text{Aut\,}K[[X]]
. For
\varphi(X)=aX+bX^{2}+cX^{3}+

higher order terms
,

\sigma_{m}(\varphi)=a^{m}\begin{pmatrix}a^{2}&ca^{-1}-b^{2}a^{-2}\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.

This gives us the coordinate transformation law in the bundle
\mathcal{J}^{W}
:

\displaystyle e_{1}^{x}=y^{-2m-4}e_{1}^{y},

\displaystyle e_{2}^{x}=y^{-2m}e_{2}^{y}.

By Theorem
17
,
\mathcal{J}^{W}
is an
{\mathcal{L}}_{+}
-charged sheaf of
D
-modules, and is a bundle of
AV
-modules, with the following
explicit formulas for the action:

\displaystyle f(x)\frac{d}{dx}\cdot g(x)e_{1}^{x}=f\frac{dg}{dx}\,e_{1}^{x}+(m+2)\,g\frac{df}{dx}\,e_{1}^{x},

\displaystyle f(x)\frac{d}{dx}\cdot g(x)e_{2}^{x}=f\frac{dg}{dx}\,e_{2}^{x}+m\,g\frac{df}{dx}\,e_{2}^{x}+\frac{1}{6}\,g\frac{d^{3}f}{dx^{3}}\,e_{1}^{x}.

Again, this bundle is well-defined for all

.

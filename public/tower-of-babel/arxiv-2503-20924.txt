Split Interpolation Refining Craig’s Theorem via Three-Valued Logics
Quentin Blomet a,b

Abstract
Which choices of truth tables and consequence relations for two logics 𝖫 1 \mathsf{L}_{1} and 𝖫 2 \mathsf{L}_{2} ensure the satisfaction of the following split interpolation property: If two formulas ϕ \phi and ψ \psi share at least one propositional atom and ϕ \phi classically entails ψ \psi , then there is a formula χ \chi that shares all its propositional atoms with both ϕ \phi and ψ \psi , such that ϕ \phi entails χ \chi in 𝖫 1 \mathsf{L}_{1} and χ \chi entails ψ \psi in 𝖫 2 \mathsf{L}_{2} ? We identify the cases in which this property holds for any pair of propositional logics based on the same three-valued Boolean normal monotonic scheme for connectives and two monotonic consequence relations. Since the resulting logics are subclassical, every instance of this property constitutes a particular refinement of Craig’s deductive interpolation theorem, as it entails the latter and further restricts the range of possible interpolants.

Note the difference in the antecedent of the conditional in Craig’s interpolation theorem compared to Milne’s. While Craig’s theorem requires that

The refinement of Craig’s deductive interpolation theorem is evident from the fact that classical logic extends both
\mathsf{K}_{3}
and
\mathsf{LP}

\phi

The operations of both
\mathsf{K}_{3}
and
\mathsf{LP}
are defined over the set
\{0,\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}},1\}
using the
Strong Kleene

\mathit{max}
and
\mathit{min}
over the total order
0<\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}<1
. The distinction between
\mathsf{K}_{3}
and
\mathsf{LP}

\mathsf{LP}

\mathbf{t}=\{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}},1\}
, then the conclusion does as well. In contrast,
\mathsf{K}_{3}

\mathbf{s}=\{1\}
. The Strong Kleene truth tables belong to a class of truth tables referred to as
Boolean normal monotonic schemes
by
da2023three
. Informally, a scheme for the connectives is
Boolean normal
if all its operations coincide with the classical two-valued ones when applied to classical values. A scheme is
monotonic
if all its operations are monotonic with respect to the information order
\leq_{I}
, where
\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}<_{I}1
and
\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}<_{I}0
. Alongside the two standards of evaluation
\mathbf{ss}
(which ensures the preservation of
1
from premises to conclusion) and
\mathbf{tt}

1
and
\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}

\mathbf{st}
,
\mathbf{ts}

\mathbf{ss}\cap\mathbf{tt}

\mathbf{st}

\mathbf{s}

\mathbf{t}
, and conversely for
\mathbf{ts}

\mathbf{ss}\cap\mathbf{tt}

\mathbf{ss}
and
\mathbf{tt}

Preliminary definitions

In the following section, we define the terminology used throughout the paper and present the semantics of each logic discussed. The semantics will be presented through two key components: a (mixed) consequence relation that establishes the criteria for evaluating premises and conclusions, and a set of truth tables for the connectives, referred to as a
scheme
for the logic
(
da2023three
)
.

Consider a language composed of an enumerable set
\mathit{Var}
of atomic formulas
p_{1},\dots,p_{n}
and a collection
\{\neg,\wedge,\lor\}
of logical constants. The set of formulas
\mathcal{L}
is recursively defined from the language in the usual way. Elements of
\mathcal{L}
will be denoted by lower case Greek letters. Subsets of
\mathcal{L}
will be denoted by uppercase Greek letters. An inference on
\mathcal{L}
is an ordered pair
\langle\Gamma,\Delta\rangle
(hereafter symbolized by
\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta
). Given a formula
\phi
, the set of atoms from which it is constructed is denoted by
\mathit{At}(\phi)
.

As mentioned above, the semantics of the logics is presented through two key components: a consequence relation and a scheme for the connectives. Both notions rely on the definition of a truth value assignment, that is, a function
w:\textit{Var}\longrightarrow\mathcal{V}=\{0,\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}},1\}
. A truth value assignment is extended to a valuation
v:\mathcal{L}\longrightarrow\mathcal{V}
through a three-valued
scheme
for the connectives. A scheme is a triple
\langle f^{\neg},f^{\wedge},f^{\lor}\rangle
of operations defined over
\mathcal{V}
. Given a scheme
\mathbf{X}
, a truth value assignment
w
is extended to an
\mathbf{X}
-valuation
v
by letting:

\displaystyle v(p)

\displaystyle=w(p),\ {\text{if}\ p\in\textit{Var},}

\displaystyle v(\neg\phi)

\displaystyle=f^{\neg}v(\phi),

\displaystyle v(\phi\wedge\chi)

\displaystyle=f^{\wedge}(v(\phi),v(\chi)),

\displaystyle v(\phi\lor\chi)

\displaystyle=f^{\lor}(v(\phi),v(\chi)).

In this paper, we will consider the three-valued schemes satisfying the properties of
Boolean normality
and
monotonicity
.

Definition 2.1

(
da2023three
)
.

An
n
-ary operation
f^{\star}
is
Boolean normal
if for
\{a_{1},...,a_{n}\}\subseteq\{0,1\}
,
f^{\star}(a_{1},...,a_{n})=f^{\star}_{\mathsf{CL}}(a_{1},...,a_{n})
, where
f^{\star}_{\mathsf{CL}}
is the corresponding operation over the usual two-element Boolean algebra.
A scheme is
Boolean normal
if and only if each of its operations is.

In other words, a scheme is Boolean normal if each of its operations agrees on the classical values with the classical two-valued operations.

The notion of monotonicity is defined from the information order over
\mathcal{V}
, where it is assumed that the value
\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}
is less informative than
1
and
0
. If a scheme is monotonic, each of its operations is order-preserving with respect to the informational order.

Definition 2.2

(
da2023three
)
.

Given the order
\leq_{I}
defined over
\mathcal{V}
so that
\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}<_{I}0
and
\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}<_{I}1
, a scheme is said to be
monotonic
if the following holds for each of its operations
f^{\star}
of arity
n
:

\textup{If}\ x_{i}\leq_{I}y_{i}\ \textup{for all}\ 1\leq i\leq n,\ \textup{then}\ f^{\star}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\leq_{I}f^{\star}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}).

As a consequence, for any formula
\phi
, and two valuations
v
and
v^{\prime}
, if
v^{\prime}
is more informative than
v
with respect to the atoms of
\phi
, then
v^{\prime}
is more informative than
v
regarding
\phi
. This is illustrated in the next corollary.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
\mathbf{X}
be a monotonic scheme with operations
f^{\star}_{1}\ldots f^{\star}_{n}
and
v,v^{\prime}
be two
\mathbf{X}
-valuations. For all
\phi\in\mathcal{L}
, it holds that if
v(p)\leq_{I}v^{\prime}(p)
for all

, then
v(\phi)\leq_{I}v^{\prime}(\phi)
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on the complexity of
\phi

Base case:
Straightforward.

Inductive step:
Assume
v(p)\leq_{I}v^{\prime}(p)
for all

\phi=\star(\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{n})

\star

f^{\star}_{i}

v(\psi_{i})\leq_{I}v^{\prime}(\psi_{i})

\mathit{At}(\psi_{i})\subseteq\mathit{At}(\phi)

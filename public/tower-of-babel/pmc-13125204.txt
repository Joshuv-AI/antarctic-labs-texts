Dynamical behavior of analytic solutions of the generalized derivative nonlinear Schrödinger equation under stochastic perturbations in fiber communication systems
Authors: Muhammad Amin S Murad, Aljethi Reem Abdullah, Mohammed A Mustafa, Ejaz Hussain
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn this study, a range of novel optical soliton solutions to the nonlinear conformable Schrödinger equation influenced by multiplicative white noise is presented. By employing the unified method, several types of solutions are derived, including solitary kink-type, wave, and singular solitons. The dynamic characteristics and physical behaviors of these solutions are examined, with emphasis on the effects of the conformable derivative order, multiplicative white noise, and temporal parameters, as illustrated through various plots. The incorporation of stochastic noises introduces a new analytical dimension, which enhances the understanding of non-linear optical processes. Moreover, the developed framework for managing noises is an effective representation of real-world scenarios in optical fibers, which provide useful insights into the stability of solitons, error minimization, and reliability in communications.Keywords: Nonlinear Schrödinger equation, conformable derivative, multiplicative white noise, uniform method, optical fibersSubject terms: Mathematics and computing, Optics and photonics, Physics

Introduction

In recent years, the study of nonlinear partial differential equations (NLPDEs) has garnered significant attention from researchers and scientists across various fields. This growing interest stems from the critical role NLPDEs play in modeling complex physical phenomena, including fluid dynamics, optical fiber communications, plasma physics, and quantum mechanics1–5. The pursuit of exact solutions to these equations is particularly important, as they provide deeper insights into the underlying dynamics of such systems, enabling precise predictions and more effective control strategies6–9. Moreover, advancements in analytical and numerical methods have further fueled this interest, offering new approaches to tackle the inherent challenges associated with solving NLPDEs10–13.

Among the various nonlinear evolution equations, the nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLSE) emerges as one of the most thoroughly investigated due to its profound applications in nonlinear optics and optical fiber communication systems14–16. Optical fibers have become indispensable in modern telecommunication infrastructure, enabling high-speed data transmission over vast distances with remarkably low attenuation and minimal signal distortion. However, ensuring the stability and fidelity of transmitted signals necessitates a comprehensive understanding of several critical parameters. In particular, chromatic dispersion, nonlinear effects such as self-phase modulation, and signal attenuation must be carefully analyzed and managed, as they significantly affect signal quality and impose constraints on the maximum achievable data rates and transmission distance17–19. Advancing the theoretical understanding of the NLSE and its variants including those incorporating fractional derivatives and power-law nonlinearities offers valuable insights for optimizing fiber-optic systems, improving signal integrity, and supporting the development of next-generation communication technologies. The nonlinear Schrödinger equation stands as a cornerstone in the study of fiber optics, offering a robust framework for modeling wave propagation in nonlinear dispersive media. It effectively captures the intricate interplay between dispersion and nonlinearity, making it instrumental in the analysis and design of optical communication systems. The NLSE has been extensively employed by researchers to explore a wide array of physical phenomena, such as the formation and propagation of optical solitons, nonlinear pulse dynamics, microtubule motion in biological systems, and various localized structures including peakons and cuspons. Additionally, it has provided insight into modified versions of the Schrödinger equation adapted for complex media and non-standard conditions20–22.

Further investigations have extended the classical NLSE to incorporate a variety of nonlinear effects, including parabolic law nonlinearities, quadratic-cubic, dual-power law, and Kerr law23. These extensions allow for a more accurate representation of the physical processes occurring in advanced optical fibers, particularly in systems where standard Kerr-type nonlinearity is insufficient. By analyzing the influence of these nonlinearities on wave propagation, researchers have been able to develop innovative fiber designs, optimize pulse shaping techniques, and enhance modulation formats. Such advancements are vital for increasing transmission capacity, improving signal integrity, and enabling more efficient data processing in modern high-speed optical networks.

Stochastic partial differential equations are essential for modeling a broad spectrum of physical, biological, and chemical systems that involve inherent uncertainty or random fluctuations24–27. Their broad applicability spans disciplines such as financial mathematics, engineering, climate modeling, and biophysics, underscoring the importance of the stochastic influences into the analysis of complex systems28–30. In particular, SPDEs are instrumental in the study of optical solitons self-reinforcing wave packets that maintain their shape during propagation through nonlinear media especially within the context of fiber optics governed by the nonlinear Schrödinger equation. When stochastic components, such as multiplicative white noise, are introduced into the system, they profoundly influence soliton dynamics. These random perturbations can lead to the stabilization of solutions near zero, modify long-term behaviors, and affect the robustness and propagation characteristics of solitons. Consequently, understanding how noise interacts with nonlinearity has become a focus of significant research, with the goal of improving signal integrity and performance in optical communication systems.

The unified method is an effective analytical tool for solving various types of nonlinear PDEs, which can offer exact solutions such as solitons, periodic waves, and singular waves. The unified method can transform PDEs into ODEs, which can make the solution process easier by reducing the complexity of PDEs. Moreover, the unified method can offer flexibility in solving PDEs by using adjustable parameters. Its limitations include the ansatz requirement, algebraic complexity for highly nonlinear PDEs, and the requirement for integrability or partial integrability of PDEs. In this work, the unified method is used to construct kink-type soliton, wave soliton, and singular soliton solutions to the present model. Various optical soliton solutions, e.g., (kink type soliton, dark soliton, bright soliton, wave soliton, etc.), can be constructed using analytic algorithms. Kink type, wave, singular solitons have significant importance in nonlinear science and applied physics. Kink type solitons, which connect two distinct stable states, have been used to describe transition phenomena in condensed matter physics, phase transition in field theory, switching phenomena in optical systems, etc. Wave solitons, which include bright solitons and dark solitons, represent localized wave patterns that retain their form during propagation. Wave solitons have significant importance in fiber optic communication systems and plasma physics, where they represent robust carriers of energy or information.

These models not only enhance our understanding of optical communication systems but also extend their relevance to other domains such as plasma physics, fluid dynamics, and nonlinear wave theory. The insights gained from these studies have significantly contributed to advancements in both theoretical research and practical technologies across these interdisciplinary fields45,46. In this study, we focus on exploring the dynamical behavior of several new optical soliton solutions within the framework of a nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLSE). This extended model incorporates perturbative terms along with multiplicative white noise, providing a more realistic and comprehensive representation of complex wave dynamics in optical fibers. The inclusion of stochastic elements aims to capture the influence of environmental randomness and system fluctuations, offering deeper insight into the stability and evolution of soliton structures under real-world conditions. Conclusion

In this study, we successfully developed a broad class of novel analytic solutions and investigated their physical behavior under the combined effect of stochastic parameter and conformable derivatives. Utilizing the unified method, we derived various types of solutions, encompassing kink-type solitons, wave soliton solutions, and singular solitons. The influence of the white noise parameter, conformable fractional-order derivative, and temporal parameter on the obtained solutions was examined through a series of three-dimensional, two-dimensional, and contour plots. These results provide valuable insights into the nonlinear Schrödinger equation, offering a deeper understanding of its stochastic dynamics. Such knowledge can guide the development of advanced optical signal processing technologies, enhancing noise suppression and improving signal clarity in telecommunication systems. For future work, further exploration of the bifurcation and chaotic behavior of the present model is suggested in order to understand the transition mechanisms among the stable, periodic, and chaotic solutions.

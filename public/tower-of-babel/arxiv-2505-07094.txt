A gluing formula for the ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} -valued index of odd symmetric operators
Maxim Braverman, Ahmad Reza Haj Saeedi Sadegh, Junrong Yan

Abstract
. We investigate Dirac-type operator D D on involutive manifolds with boundary with symmetry, which forces the index of D D to vanish. We study the secondary ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} -valued index of elliptic boundary value problems for such operators. We prove a ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} -valued analog of the splitting theorem: the ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} -valued index of an operator on a closed manifold M M equals the ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} -valued index of a boundary value problem on a manifold obtained by cutting M M along a hypersurface N N . When N N divides M M into two disjoint submanifolds M 1 M_{1} and M 2 M_{2} , the ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} -valued index on M M is equal to the mod 2 reduction of the usual ℤ \mathbb{Z} -valued index of the Atiyah-Patodi-Singer boundary value problem on M 1 M_{1} . This leads to a cohomological formula for the ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} -valued index.

1.
Introduction

Atiyah and Singer,
[
3
]
, initiated the study of the index theory of real operators, which commute with an action of the Clifford algebra
Cl_{k}
. The special case of operators commuting with
Cl_{2}
is equivalent to the study of so-called
odd symmetric
operators. These are the operators on a complex Hilbert space
H
with an anti-unitary anti-involution, i.e., an anti-linear map
\tau
with
\tau^{-1}=\tau^{*}=-\tau
. A Fredholm operator
D
is called
odd symmetric
if
\tau D\tau^{-1}=D^{*}
. This case attracted particular attention because of its connection to physical systems with time-reversal symmetry
[
26
,
14
,
15
,
20
,
22
]
. The usual index of an odd symmetric operator vanishes, but Atiyah and Singer defined a secondary
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued index, called the
\tau
-index, as the dimension of the kernel of
D
modulo 2:

(1.1)

\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D\ :=\ \dim\ker D\qquad\text{mod}\quad 2.

In
[
7
,
10
]
, the first and the second authors extended several classical index-theoretical results to the
\tau
-index of odd symmetric differential operators on a manifold
M
with an involution
\theta^{M}:M\to M
. They also gave an extension and a new interpretation of the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued bulk index for topological insulators of type AII,
[
20
]
, and a generalization of bulk-edge correspondence for
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued invariants (this should be compared with
[
12
]
, where similar results were obtained for usual
\mathbb{Z}
-valued invariants).

In the present paper, we discuss boundary value problems for odd symmetric operators. We obtain a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued analogue of the classical splitting theorem,
[
4
, Theorem 8.17]
, which relates the index of
D
with the index of the Atiyah-Patodi-Singer (APS) boundary value problem on a manifold obtained by cutting
M
along a hypersurface. If a manifold
M
with involution
\theta^{M}
is divided into two disjoint manifolds
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
by a
\theta^{M}
-invariant hypersurface, then we show that the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-index of an odd symmetric Dirac-type operator
D
on
M
is equal modulo 2 to the usual
\mathbb{Z}
-valued index of the APS boundary value problem of the restriction of
D
to
M_{1}
. This allows us to express
\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D
as an integral of a differential form over
M_{1}
.

Let us give more details about our results.

An
involutive manifold

(M,\theta^{M})
is a manifold
M
together with a smooth involution
\theta^{M}:M\to M
. A
quaternionic bundle
over
M
is a complex Hermitian vector bundle
E
together with an anti-unitary anti-involution
\theta^{E}:E\to E
, which covers
\theta^{M}
,
[
16
,
13
,
22
]
. We define an anti-unitary anti-involution
\tau:\Gamma(M,E)\to\Gamma(M,E)
by

\tau:\,f(x)\ \mapsto\ \theta^{E}f(\theta^{M}x),\qquad f\in\Gamma(M,E),

and consider a Dirac-type operator
D
which is odd symmetric with respect to
\tau
. If
M
is compact, this operator is Fredholm. If
M
is a compact manifold with boundary we consider an elliptic boundary condition
B\subset H^{-1/2}(\partial M,E_{\partial M})
for
D
and denote by
D_{B}
the restriction of
D
to the sections of
E
, whose restrictions to
\partial M
lie in
B
. Then
D_{B}
is Fredholm. If
\tau
interchange
B
with the
adjoint boundary conditions

B^{\rm ad}
, then
D_{B}
is odd symmetric, and we are interested in its
\tau
-index (
1.1
).

Suppose
N\subset M
is a
\theta^{M}
-invariant hypersurface such that a small neighborhood
U
of
N
decomposes as
U=U_{1}\sqcup_{N}U_{2}
with
\theta^{M}(U_{1})=U_{2}
. We cut
M
along
N
and obtain a new manifold
\hat{M}
, whose boundary consists of two copies of
N
, denoted by
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
.

Let
D
be an odd symmetric Dirac-type operator acting on sections of a quaternionic Dirac bundle
E
over
M
.
Let
\hat{D}
denote the induced Dirac-type operator on
\hat{M}
. Let
\hat{B}
be the boundary condition for
\hat{D}
, whose restriction to
N_{1}
is the APS boundary conditions and whose restriction to
N_{2}
is the dual APS boundary conditions. Then
\hat{D}_{\hat{B}}
is an odd symmetric operator. Our first result, Theorem
4.8
, is the following analog of the classical Splitting Theorem for the index

(1.2)

\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D\ =\ \operatorname{ind}_{\tau}\hat{D}_{\hat{B}}.

The proof is similar to the proof for the
\mathbb{Z}
-valued index but has the following difficulty. As in the classical case, the main part of the proof is a construction of a deformation
T_{s}
(
s\in[0,1]
) between
D
and
\hat{D}_{\hat{B}}
(these are the operators denoted by
\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}K_{s}
in Section
4.14
), cf., for example,
[
4
, Lemma 8.11, Theorem 8.12]
). However, the operators
T_{s}
are not odd symmetric in general. In Lemma
4.15
, we show that despite this, the dimension of the kernel of these operators is independent of
s
modulo 2, which is enough to prove the Splitting Theorem.

In case when
N
separates
M
into two disjoint components
M=M_{1}\sqcup_{N}M_{2}
our assumptions imply that
\theta^{M}(M_{1})=M_{2}
. In this case, the index of
\hat{D}_{\hat{B}}
is equal to the sum of the indices of boundary value problems on
M_{1}
and on
M_{2}
. One checks that the involution
\tau
sends the kernel of the boundary value problem on
M_{2}
to the cokernel of the boundary value problem on
M_{1}
. This implies, cf. Theorem
5.3
, that in this case

(1.3)

\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D^{+}\ \equiv\ \operatorname{ind}(D_{1})^{+}_{APS}\ \equiv\ \operatorname{ind}(D_{2})^{+}_{APS},

where
\equiv
stands for equality modulo 2,
D_{j}
is the restriction of
D
to
M_{j}
(
j=1,2
), and
\operatorname{ind}(D_{j})^{+}_{APS}
is the usual
\mathbb{Z}
-valued index of the Atiyah-Patodi-Singer boundary value problem for
D_{j}
.

We can now apply the usual Atiyah-Patodi-Singer index theorem to compute the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued index of
D
. Suppose all the structures are products near
N\subset M
and let
A
denote the restriction of
D
to
N
. Then
\tau A\tau^{-1}=-A
. It follows that the eta-invariant
\eta(A)
vanishes and (
1.3
) and the standard Atiyah-Patodi-Singer index theorem implies

(1.4)

\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D^{+}\ \equiv\ \int_{M_{j}}\,\hat{A}(TM)\,\operatorname{ch}(E/S)\ -\frac{\dim\ker A^{+}}{2},\qquad j=1,2.

By Kramer’s degeneracy, cf.
[
23
]
, the dimension of
\ker A^{+}
is even. We conclude that in this situation

(1.5)

\int_{M_{j}}\,\hat{A}(TM)\,\operatorname{ch}(E/S)\ \in\ \mathbb{Z},\qquad j=1,2.

2.
Boundary value problems for Dirac-type operators

In this section, we recall the notion of an elliptic boundary value problem for a Dirac-type operator. The Atiyah-Patody-Singer, the dual Atiyah-Patodi-Singer, and the transmission boundary conditions are elliptic. Our notation and terminology are mostly borrowed from
[
4
]
.

2.1.
A Dirac-type operator

Let
M
be a compact oriented Riemannian manifold (possibly with boundary) and let
E=E^{+}\oplus E^{-}
be a Dirac bundle over
M
, cf.
[
24
, Definition II.5.2]
. In particular,
E
is a Hermitian vector bundle endowed with a Clifford multiplication
c:T^{*}M\to\operatorname{End}(E)
and a compatible Hermitian connection
\nabla^{E}
.

A
Dirac-type operator
on
E
is the differential operator

(2.1)

D:=\ \sum_{1\leq j\leq n}\,c(e_{i})\nabla^{E}_{e_{j}}+V:\,C^{\infty}(M,E)\to C^{\infty}(M,E),

where
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})
is an orthonormal frame of
TM
and
V:E\to E
is a self-adjoint bundle map. Then
D
is a formally self-adjoint differential operator, which is independent of the choice of the frame
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})
, cf.
[
5
, §3.3]
.

If
E=E^{+}\oplus E^{-}
is a graded bundle,
E^{+}
is orthogonal to
E^{-}
, the connection preserves the grading, and
c(\xi),V:E^{\pm}\to E^{\mp}
for all
\xi\in T^{*}M
, we say that
E
is a graded Dirac bundle. In this case,
D
takes the form

(2.2)

D\ =\ \begin{pmatrix}0&D^{-}\\
D^{+}&0\end{pmatrix},

where
D^{\pm}
is the restriction of
D
to
C^{\infty}(M,E^{\pm})
.

We denote by
E_{\partial M}=E_{\partial M}^{+}\oplus E_{\partial M}^{-}
the restriction of
E
to the boundary.

2.2.
A boundary-defining function

If the boundary
\partial M
of
M
is non-empty, we fix a function
t:M\to[0,\infty)
such that
t|_{\partial M}\equiv 0
and
dt
does not vanish on
\partial M
. Such a function is called a
boundary-defining function
.

Given a boundary-defining function
t
, we identify a neighborhood
U\subset M
of the boundary with a cylinder

(2.3)

Z_{r}\ :=\ [0,r)\times{\partial M}.

in such a way that
t
becomes a projection onto the first factor. Note that, in general, this identification is not an isometry.

We use the Riemannian metric on
M
to identify the cotangent bundle to the boundary with a subset of
T^{*}M
.

2.3.
An adapted operator on the boundary

Any Dirac-type operator satisfies the conditions of Lemma 4.1 of
[
4
]
. Hence, by this lemma, there exists a self-adjoint first-order elliptic differential operator
A:C^{\infty}({\partial M},E_{\partial M})\to C^{\infty}({\partial M},E_{\partial M})
such that the restriction of
D
to the neighborhood
U
, defined in the previous subsection, is given by

(2.4)

D\ =\ c(dt)\,\left(\,\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+A+R_{t}\,\right),

where
R_{t}
is a differential operator on
{\partial M}
of order at most one, depends smoothly on
t
, and such that
R_{0}
is an operator of order zero. In this situation, we refer to
A
as an
adapted operator of
D
at the boundary. Note that the notion of an adapted operator depends on the choice of the boundary-defining function
t
. One readily sees that the adapted operator is a Dirac-type operator on
E_{\partial M}
. Note, however, that it behaves differently with respect to the grading on
E_{\partial M}
, namely
A:E_{\partial M}^{\pm}\to E_{\partial M}^{\pm}
, i.e., with respect to the grading on
E_{\partial M}
it has the form

A=\begin{pmatrix}A^{+}&0\\
0&A^{-}\end{pmatrix}:\,C^{\infty}({\partial M},E_{{\partial M}})\;\to\;C^{\infty}({\partial M},E_{{\partial M}})

Remark 2.4
.

The adapted operator
A
is not unique. Different choices of
A
lead to various boundary value problems. See the discussion of which choice is convenient for which operator in Sections 7 and 8 of
[
8
]
.

2.5.
The product case

We say that the manifold
M
and the Dirac bundle
E
are
product near the boundary
if the identification between the neighborhood
U\subset M
of the boundary and the cylinder (
2.3
) is an isometry and the Clifford multiplication
c:T^{*}M\to{\rm End}(E)
and the connection
\nabla^{E}
have product structure on
Z_{r}
. Then the restriction (
2.4
) of
D
to
Z_{r}
takes the form

(2.5)

D\;=\;c(dt)\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+A\right).

Since both
D
and
A
are formally self-adjoint operators and
c(dt)
is skew-adjoint, one sees that
A
anti-commutes with
c(dt)
:

(2.6)

c(dt)\circ A\ =\ -\,A\circ c(dt).

If (
2.5
) holds, we say that
D
is
a product near the boundary
.

2.6.
Sobolev spaces on the boundary

The
Sobolev space

H^{s}({\partial M},E_{{\partial M}})
is defined to be the completion of
C^{\infty}({\partial M},E_{{\partial M}})
with respect the norm

(2.7)

\|\mathbf{u}\|_{H^{s}({\partial M},E_{\partial M})}^{2}\;:=\;\big\|(\operatorname{id}+A^{2})^{s/2}\mathbf{u}\big\|_{L^{2}({\partial M},E_{\partial M})}^{2}.

Though the norm depends on the operator
A
, the Sobolev spaces
H^{s}({\partial M},E_{{\partial M}})
are independent of
A
,
[
27
, §I.7]
.

2.7.
The hybrid Soblev spaces

The eigensections of
A
belong to
H^{s}({\partial M},E_{\partial M})
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
. For
I\subset\mathbb{R}
we denote by

H_{I}^{s}(A)\ \subset\ H^{s}({\partial M},E_{{\partial M}})

the span of the eigensections of
A
whose eigenvalues belong to
I
and by
P_{I}^{A}:H^{s}({\partial M},E_{{\partial M}})\to H_{I}^{s}(A)
the orthogonal projection. We set
H_{I}^{s}(A^{\pm}):=H_{I}^{s}(A)\cap H^{s}({\partial M},E_{\partial M}^{\pm})
.

Definition 2.8
.

Fix
a\in\mathbb{R}
and define the
hybrid
Sobolev space

(2.8)

\check{H}(A)\;:=\;H_{(-\infty,a]}^{1/2}(A)\;\oplus\;H_{(a,\infty)}^{-1/2}(A)\ \subset\ H^{-1/2}({\partial M},E_{{\partial M}})

with
\check{H}
-norm

\|\mathbf{u}\|_{\check{H}(A)}^{2}\;:=\;\big\|P_{(-\infty,a]}^{A}\mathbf{u}\big\|_{H^{1/2}({\partial M},E_{\partial M})}^{2}\;+\;\big\|P_{(a,\infty)}^{A}\mathbf{u}\big\|_{H^{-1/2}({\partial M},E_{\partial M})}^{2}.

We set
\check{H}(A^{\pm})
the restriction of
\check{H}(A)
to
E^{\pm}_{\partial M}
.

The space
\check{H}(A)
is independent of the choice of
a
,
[
4
, p. 5]
. By Theorem 6.7 of
[
4
]
the hybrid space

\check{H}(A)
coincides with the space of restrictions to the boundary of sections of
E
which lie in the maximal domain of
D
.

Similarly, we define

(2.9)

\hat{H}(A)\;:=\;H_{(-\infty,a]}^{-1/2}(A)\;\oplus\;H_{(a,\infty)}^{1/2}(A)

with
\hat{H}
-norm

\|\mathbf{u}\|_{\hat{H}(A)}^{2}\;:=\;\|P_{(-\infty,a]}^{A}\mathbf{u}\|_{H^{-1/2}({\partial M},E_{\partial M})}^{2}\;+\;\|P_{(a,\infty)}^{A}\mathbf{u}\|_{H^{1/2}({\partial M},E_{\partial M})}^{2},

and the restrictions
\hat{H}(A^{\pm})
of this space to
E^{\pm}_{\partial M}
.
Then

\hat{H}(A)\;=\;\check{H}(-A).

The
L_{2}
-scalar product on sections of
E
extends naturally to a perfect pairing

\check{H}(A)\;\times\;\hat{H}(A)\;\to\;\mathbb{C}.

By (
2.6
),
c(dt)
induces an isometry between Sobolev spaces
H_{(-\infty,a]}^{s}(A^{+})
and
H_{[-a,\infty)}^{s}(A^{-})
. Therefore, we conclude that

Lemma 2.9
.

Over
{\partial M}
, the isomorphism
c(dt):E_{{\partial M}}\to E_{{\partial M}}
induces an isomorphism
\hat{H}(A^{+})\to\check{H}(A^{-})
. In particular, the sesquilinear form

\beta:\check{H}(A^{+})\times\check{H}(A^{-})\ \to\ \mathbb{C},\qquad\ \beta(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})\ :=\ -\,\big(\mathbf{u},-c(dt)\mathbf{v}\big)\ =\ -\,\big(c(dt)\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\big),

is a perfect pairing of topological vector spaces.

2.10.
Boundary value problems

It is well known, cf., for example, Theorem 5.22 of
[
1
]
, that the natural restriction map
{R}:C^{\infty}(M,E)\to C^{\infty}({\partial M},E_{{\partial M}})
extends to a bounded map
{R}:H^{s}(M,E)\to H^{s-1/2}({\partial M},E_{{\partial M}})
. Let
\operatorname{dom}D_{\max}
denote the maximal domain of
D
. Theorem 1.7 of
[
4
]
states that
{R}:\operatorname{dom}D_{\max}\to\check{H}(A)
. In other words,
\check{H}(A)
can be identified with boundary values of sections in the maximal domain of
D
.

Definition 2.11
.

A closed subspace
B\subset\check{H}(A^{\pm})
is called a
boundary condition
for
D^{\pm}
. We will use the notation
D^{+}_{B}
for the operator
D^{+}
with the following domains

\operatorname{dom}(D^{\pm}_{B})\;=\;\{u\in\operatorname{dom}D^{\pm}_{\max}:{R}u\in B\}.

Thus,
D^{\pm}_{B}
is the operator

D^{\pm}_{B}:\,\operatorname{dom}(D^{\pm}_{B})\ \to\ L^{2}(M,E^{\mp}).

Remark 2.12
.

In
[
4
]
, the operator
D^{\pm}_{B}
is denoted by
D^{\pm}_{B,\max}
.

The space

(2.10)

B^{\rm ad}\;:=\;\big\{\,\mathbf{v}\in\check{H}(A^{\mp}):\,\big(c(dt)\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\big)=0\mbox{ for all }\mathbf{u}\in B\,\big\}

is called the adjoint boundary condition to
B
. It is a closed subspace of
\check{H}(A^{\mp})
. By equation (62) of
[
4
]
, the operator
D^{\mp}_{B^{\rm ad}}
is the adjoint of the operator
D^{\pm}_{B}
:

(2.11)

\big(D_{B}^{\pm}\big)^{*}\ =\ D^{\mp}_{B^{\rm ad}},

where
D^{\mp}_{B^{\rm ad}}
is the operator

D^{\mp}:\,\operatorname{dom}(D^{\mp}_{B^{\rm ad}})\ \to\ L^{2}(M,E^{\pm}).

Definition 2.13
.

A boundary condition
B
is said to be
elliptic
if
B\subset H^{1/2}(A^{\pm})
and
B^{\rm ad}\subset H^{1/2}(A^{\mp})
.

By Corollary 8.6 of
[
4
]
, if
B
is an elliptic boundary condition then the operators
D^{\pm}_{B}
and
D^{\mp}_{B^{\rm ad}}
are Fredholm. We define the index of
D^{+}_{B}
by

(2.12)

\operatorname{ind}D^{\pm}_{B}\ :=\ \dim\ker D^{\pm}_{B}\ -\ \dim\operatorname{coker}D^{\pm}_{B}\ =\ \dim\ker D^{\pm}_{B}\ -\ \dim\ker D^{\mp}_{B^{\rm ad}}.

2.14.
Atiyah-Patodi-Singer boundary conditions

The boundary conditions

B(A^{\pm})\ :=\ H^{1/2}_{(-\infty,0)}({\partial M},E_{\partial M}^{\pm})

are called the
the Atiyah–Patodi–Singer (APS) boundary conditions
for
D^{\pm}
. We set

(2.13)

D^{\pm}_{APS}\ :=\ D^{\pm}_{B(A^{\pm})}.

We define the
dual APS boundary conditions

\bar{B}(A^{\pm})\ :=\ H^{1/2}_{(-\infty,0]}({\partial M},E_{\partial M}^{\pm}).

One readily checks that
B(A^{\pm})
and
\bar{B}(A^{\pm})

are elliptic boundary conditions (cf.
[
4
, Example 1.16]
) and that

(2.14)

\operatorname{ind}D^{\pm}_{\bar{B}(A^{\pm})}\ =\ \operatorname{ind}D^{\pm}_{APS}\ +\ \dim\ker A^{\pm},

(cf.
[
4
, Corollary 8.8]
).

From (
2.10
) we get

(2.15)

\big(B(A^{\pm})\big)^{{\rm ad}}\ =\ \bar{B}(A^{\mp}).

Therefore, by (
2.12
), we now obtain

(2.16)

\operatorname{ind}D^{\pm}_{APS}\ =\ \dim\ker D^{\pm}_{APS}\ -\ \dim\ker D^{\mp}_{\bar{B}(A^{\mp})}.

2.15.
The Atiyah-Patodi-Singer index theorem

Suppose that
D
is a product near the boundary, cf. Section
2.5
. Let
R
denote the Riemannian curvature of the metric
g^{M}
and let

\hat{A}(TM)\ :=\operatorname{det}{}^{1/2}\left(\frac{R/2}{\sinh(R/2)}\right)

be the
\hat{A}
-genus differential form. Let
\operatorname{ch}(E/S)
denote the relative Chern character form of
E
, cf.
[
5
, Page 146]
. Let
\eta(A)
denote the eta-invariant of the operator
A
. The celebrated Atiyah-Patodi-Singer theorem,
[
2
]
(see also
[
6
, Theorem 22.18]
), states that

(2.17)

\operatorname{ind}D^{+}_{APS}\ =\ \int_{M}\,\hat{A}(TM)\,\operatorname{ch}(E/S)\ -\ \frac{\eta(A)+\dim\ker A}{2}.

Remark 2.16
.

Grubb
[
21
]
suggested extending the Atiyah-Patodi-Singer construction to the case where
D
is not a product near the boundary. She approached it by deforming all the data near the boundary to those with a product structure. As a result, the contribution of the boundary to the index consists of two terms – the
\eta
-invariant and an integral of a certain differential form defined by
D
over the boundary. Gilkey
[
17
,
19
,
18
]
used invariance theory to compute this integral for different geometric Dirac operators, including the twisted spin-Dirac and the twisted signature operator. A slightly more unified approach to getting an index theorem for general Dirac-type operators in a non-product case was suggested in
[
8
]
. We don’t discuss these results in this paper. But an interested reader might easily extend our index theorem
5.5
to the non-product case using these ideas.

2.17.
The splitting theorem

Let
M
be a compact manifold without boundary. Let
N\subset M
be a hypersurface. Then a neighborhood
U
of
N
can be identified with
(-r,r)\times N
. As in Section
2.2
we denote by
t
the coordinate along the axis of
(-r,r)
.

Cutting
M
along
N
we obtain a manifold
\hat{M}
(connected or not) with two copies of
N
as the boundary. So we can write
\hat{M}=(M\setminus N)\sqcup N_{1}\sqcup N_{2}
, where a neighborhood of
N_{1}
is naturally identified with
[0,r)\times N_{1}
and a neighborhood of
N_{2}
is naturally identified with
(-r,0]\times N_{2}
. We set
t_{1}:=t
in the neighborhood of
N_{1}
and
t_{2}:=-t
in the neighborhood of
N_{2}
.

The bundle
E=E^{+}\oplus E^{-}\to M
and the operators
D^{\pm}
induce a graded Dirac bundle
\hat{E}=\hat{E}^{+}\oplus\hat{E}^{-}\to\hat{M}
and Dirac-type operators

\hat{D}^{\pm}:C^{\infty}(\hat{M},\hat{E}^{\pm})\ \to\ C^{\infty}(\hat{M},\hat{E}^{\mp})

on
\hat{M}
.

As in Section
2.3
we can choose an
adapted operator
A:C^{\infty}(N,E_{N})\to C^{\infty}(N,E_{N})
for the hypersurface
N
, such that on
U\simeq(-r,r)\times N

(2.18)

D\ =\ c(dt)\,\left(\,\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+A+R_{t}\,\right),

where
R_{t}
is a differential operator on
N
of order at most one, depending smoothly on
t
and such that
R_{0}
is an operator of order zero.
Then
A_{1}=A
is an adapted operator for
N_{1}
and
A_{2}=-A
is an adapted operator for
N_{2}
and in neighborhoods of
N_{j}
in
\hat{M}
, we have

(2.19)

D\;=\;c(dt_{j})\,\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{j}}+A_{j}+R_{t,j}\,\right),\qquad\text{where}\quad A_{1}=A,\ A_{2}=-A,\ R_{t,1}=R_{t},\ R_{t,2}=-R_{-t}.

Thus
\hat{A}=A\oplus(-A)
is an adapted operator of
\hat{D}
to
\partial\hat{M}
. Hence,

(2.20)

B(\hat{A})\ =\ B(A)\oplus B(-A).

The following
Splitting Theorem
is proven in
[
4
, Theorem 8.17]
:

Theorem 2.18
.

Suppose
M,D^{+},\hat{M},(\hat{D})^{+}
are as above. Let
B_{1}=H_{(-\infty,0)}^{1/2}(A)
and
B_{2}=H_{(-\infty,0]}^{1/2}(-A)=H_{[0,\infty)}^{1/2}(A)
be the APS and the dual APS boundary conditions for
(\hat{D})^{+}
along
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
, respectively. Then
(\hat{D})^{+}_{B_{1}\oplus B_{2}}
is a Fredholm operator and

(2.21)

\operatorname{ind}D^{+}\;=\;\operatorname{ind}(\hat{D})^{+}_{B_{1}\oplus B_{2}}.

Using (
2.20
) and (
2.14
), we can rewrite (
2.21
) as

(2.22)

\operatorname{ind}D^{+}\;=\;\operatorname{ind}(\hat{D})^{+}_{APS}\ +\ \dim\ker A^{+}.

3.
The
\tau
-index

In this section, we recall the construction of the secondary
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued index of odd symmetric operators studied in
[
26
,
14
,
15
]
for bounded operators and in
[
9
]
for differential operators on a non-compact involutive manifold.

The exposition in this section follows closely Section 2 of
[
10
]
.

3.1.
An anti-linear involution

Let
H
be a complex Hilbert space and let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H}
denote the scalar product on
H
. For an
anti-linear
map
\tau:H\to H
we denote by
\tau^{*}
the unique anti–linear operator satisfying

\langle\tau x,y\rangle_{H}\ =\ \overline{\langle x,\tau^{*}y\rangle_{H}}\qquad\text{for all}\quad x,y\in H.

Definition 3.2
.

An anti-linear map
\tau:H\to H
is called an
anti-involution
if
\tau^{*}=-\tau=\tau^{-1}
.

3.3.
Odd symmetric operators

Let
D:H\to H
be a closed unbounded linear operator on
H
whose domain is
W
, which is dense in
H
. We view
W
as a Hilbert space with the scalar product

\langle x,y\rangle_{W}\ =\ \langle x,y\rangle_{H}\ +\ \langle Dx,Dy\rangle_{H}.

Then
D:W\to H
is a bounded operator.

Definition 3.4
.

A closed operator
D:H\to H
with a dense domain
W
is called
odd symmetric
(or
\tau
-symmetric) if

(3.1)

\tau\,D\,\tau^{-1}\ =\ D^{*},

where
D^{*}
is the adjoint of the unbounded operator
D
. Equation (
3.1
) means, in particular, that
\tau(W)
is equal to the domain of the operator
D^{*}
.

We denote by
{B}_{\tau}(W,H)
the space of
\tau
-symmetric operators on
H
whose domain is
W
. Each
T\in{B}_{\tau}(W,H)
is a bounded operator from the Hilbert space
W
to the Hilbert space
H
. The space
{B}_{\tau}(W,H)
with the operator norm of these operators is a Banach space.

3.5.
Graded odd symmetric operators

Let
H=H^{+}\oplus H^{-}
be a graded Hilbert space and let

D\ =\ \begin{pmatrix}0&D^{-}\\
D^{+}&0\end{pmatrix}:\,H\to H

be a closed operator with dense domain
W=W^{+}\oplus W^{-}
. Then
D:H^{\pm}\to H^{\mp}
. If
D
is self-adjoint, then
(D^{\pm})^{*}=D^{\mp}
.

Assume that the anti-unitary anti-involution
\tau
satisfies
\tau:H^{\pm}\to H^{\mp}
. If
D
is odd symmetric, then (
3.1
) implies that
\tau D^{\pm}\tau^{-1}=(D^{\pm})^{*}
. In this situation, we say that
D
is a
graded odd symmetric operator
.

We denote the space of graded odd symmetric self-adjoint operators with domain
W
by
\widehat{{B}}_{\tau}(W,H)
.
If
D\in\widehat{{B}}_{\tau}(W,H)
, then
\tau D^{\pm}\tau^{-1}=D^{\mp}=(D^{\pm})^{*}
. Then
the operators
D^{\pm}
are odd symmetric
.

3.6.
The
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued index

If
D
is an odd symmetric operator, then
\operatorname{ind}D=0
by (
3.1
). We define the
secondary

\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued invariant – the
\tau
-index of
D
:

Definition 3.7
.

If
D
is an odd symmetric operator on
H
its
\tau
-index
is

(3.2)

\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D\ :=\ \dim\ker D\qquad\mod 2.

If
D\in\widehat{{B}}_{\tau}(W,H)
is a graded odd symmetric self-adjoint operator on a graded Hilbert space
H
, we define

(3.3)

\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D^{+}\ :=\ \dim\ker D^{+}\qquad\mod 2.

Theorem 2.5 of
[
9
]
states that the
\tau
-index is constant on connected components of the space of odd symmetric Fredholm operators:

Theorem 3.8
.

The
\tau
-index
\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D
is constant on the connected components of
{B}_{\tau}(W,H)
. The
\tau
-index
\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D^{+}
is constant on the connected components of
\widehat{{B}}_{\tau}(W,H)
.

We now discuss some geometric settings leading to many interesting of odd symmetric operators,
[
9
]
.

3.9.
Quaternionic vector bundles

An
involutive manifold

(M,\theta^{M})
is a Riemannian manifold
M
together with a metric preserving involution
\theta^{M}:M\to M
. A
quaternionic vector bundle
over an involutive manifold
M
is Hermitian vector bundle
E
endowed with an anti-linear anti-unitary bundle map
\theta^{E}:E\to\theta^{*}E
, such that
(\theta^{E})^{2}=-1
, cf.
[
16
,
13
,
22
]
. This means that for every
x\in M
there exists an anti-linear map
\theta^{E}_{x}:E_{x}\to E_{\theta(x)}
, which depends smoothly on
x
and satisfies
\theta^{E}_{\theta^{M}(x)}\,\theta^{E}_{x}=-1
,

(\theta^{E}_{x})^{*}\theta^{E}_{x}=1
.

If
E=E^{+}\oplus E^{-}
is a graded vector bundle and
\theta^{E}
is
odd with respect to the grading
(i.e.
\theta^{E}(E^{\pm})=E^{\mp}
), we say that
(E,\theta^{E})
is a
graded quaternionic bundle
.

Given a quaternionic vector bundle
(E,\theta^{E})
over an involutive manifold
(M,\theta^{M})
we define an anti-unitary anti-involution
\tau
on the space
\Gamma(M,E)
of sections of
E
by

(3.4)

\tau:\,f(x)\ \mapsto\ \theta^{E}f(\theta^{M}x),\qquad f\in\Gamma(M,E).

3.10.
Odd symmetric elliptic operators

Let
(E=E^{+}\oplus E^{-},\theta^{E})
be a graded quaternionic vector bundle over an involutive manifold
(M,\theta^{M})
. Let
D:\Gamma(M,E)\to\Gamma(M,E)
be a self-adjoint odd symmetric elliptic differential operator on
M
, which is odd with respect to the grading, i.e.
D:\Gamma(M,E^{\pm})\to\Gamma(M,E^{\mp})
. We set
D^{\pm}:=D|_{\Gamma(M,E^{\pm})}
. Then
\tau\,D^{\pm}\,\tau^{-1}=(D^{\pm})^{*}=D^{\mp}
and

D\ =\ \begin{pmatrix}0&D^{-}\\
D^{+}&0\end{pmatrix}

with respect to the decomposition
E=E^{+}\oplus E^{-}
.

Suppose now that
D
is Fredholm, then the
\tau
-index
\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D^{+}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is defined by (
3.2
). In
[
9
]
, the first and the second authors computed this index in several examples and proved the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued analogs of the Relative Index Theorem, the Callias index theorem, and Boutet de Monvel’s index theorem for Toeplitz operators.

4.
The Splitting Theorem for the
\tau
-index

In this section, we prove an analog of the Splitting theorem
2.18
for the
\tau
-index.

4.1.
An odd symmetric Dirac operator

Let
(E,\theta^{E})
be a graded quaternionic bundle over a closed connected oriented involutive manifold
(M,\theta)
, which is also a graded Dirac bundle (no connections between the two structures on
E
yet). As before, we assume that
\theta^{E}
is odd with respect to the grading:
\theta^{E}:E^{\pm}\to E^{\mp}
.

Let
\tau
be as in (
3.4
) and let
D
be a Dirac-type operator on
E
, cf. (
2.1
). Suppose
D
is odd symmetric,
\tau\,D\,\tau^{-1}=D
. Then
D^{\pm}=\tau\,D^{\mp}\,\tau^{-1}
.

4.2.
A
\theta^{M}
-invariant hypersurface

Suppose there exists a
\theta^{M}
-invariant oriented hypersurface
N\subset M
. Then there is a small equivariant neighborhood
U
of
N
such that
N
divides it into two disjoint components
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
. We assume that

(4.1)

\theta^{M}(U_{1})=U_{2}.

Then there exists a function
t:U\to\mathbb{R}
such that
N=t^{-1}(0)
,
dt
never vanishes on
N
, and

t\big(\theta^{M}(x)\big)\ =\ -t(x).

A possibly smaller neighborhood of
N
, which by a slight abuse of notations we still denote by
U
, can be identified with the cylinder
(-r,r)\times{\partial M}
in such a way that

\theta^{M}(t,y)\ =\ \big(-t,\theta^{M}(y)\big),\qquad t\in(-r,r),\ \ y\in N.

In
U
we have
\tau\partial_{t}\tau^{-1}=-\partial_{t}
. Using (
2.18
) and
\tau\,D\,\tau^{-1}=D
we obtain

(4.2)

\tau\,c(dt)\,\tau^{-1}\ =\ -\,c(dt).

Lemma 4.3
.

There exists an adapted operator
A
for
D
on
N
such that

(4.3)

\tau\,A\,\tau^{-1}\ =\ -\,A.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{A}
be any adapted operator for
D
, so that

(4.4)

D\ =\ c(dt)\,\left(\,\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\tilde{A}+\tilde{R}_{t}\,\right).

From (
2.18
) and (
4.2
) we see that

(4.5)

\tau\,(\tilde{A}+\tilde{R}_{t})\,\tau^{-1}\ =\ -\,\tilde{A}-\tilde{R}_{-t}.

Set

A\ :=\ \frac{\tilde{A}-\tau\tilde{A}\tau^{-1}}{2},\qquad R_{t}\ :=\ \frac{\tilde{R}_{t}-\tau\tilde{R}_{-t}\tau^{-1}}{2}.

Then
R_{t}
is a differential operator of order at most one,
R_{0}
is an operator of order zero, and

(4.6)

D\ =\ c(dt)\,\left(\,\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+A+R_{t}\,\right).

Hence,
A
is an adapted operator for
D
which clearly satisfies (
4.3
).
∎

Remark 4.4
.

In the product case, when
R_{t}=0
, any adapted operator satisfies (
4.3
) by (
4.5
).

4.5.
Cutting along the invariant hypersurface

Fix an adapted operator
A
for
D
which satisfies (
4.3
). Let
\hat{M}
,
\hat{E}
,
\hat{D}
,
N_{1}
,
N_{2}
,
t
,
t_{1}
,
t_{2}
,
A_{1}
, and
A_{2}
be as in Section
2.17
. Then
\theta^{M}
induces a natural involution
\theta^{\hat{M}}
on
\hat{M}
such that
\theta^{\hat{M}}:N_{1}\to N_{2}
. Similarly,
\theta^{E}
induces an anti-unitary anti-involution
\theta^{\hat{E}}
of
\hat{E}
.

By a slight abuse of notation we denote by
\tau:\Gamma(\hat{M},\hat{E})\to\Gamma(\hat{M},\hat{E})
the operator induced by
\theta^{\hat{M}}
and
\theta^{\hat{E}}
as in (
3.4
). Then

(4.7)

\tau\hat{D}\tau^{-1}\ =\ \hat{D}^{*},

where
\hat{D}^{*}
denotes the operator formally adjoint to
\hat{D}
. We will say that
\hat{D}
is
formally odd symmetric
.

From (
4.3
) and (
4.7
) we conclude that

(4.8)

\tau\,A_{1}\,\tau^{-1}\ =\ A_{2}.

Recall that the boundary conditions
B(A)
and
\bar{B}(A)
were defined in Section
2.14
. It follows from (
4.8
) that
\tau
maps
B(A_{1})
to
B(A_{2})
and
\bar{B}(A_{1})
to
\bar{B}(A_{2})
. Recall from Section
2.17
that
\hat{A}=A_{1}\oplus A_{2}
is an adapted operator for
\hat{D}
and set

\hat{B}(\hat{A})\ :=\ B(A_{1})\oplus\bar{B}(A_{2}).

Lemma 4.6
.

The operator
\hat{D}_{\hat{B}(\hat{A})}
is odd symmetric.

Proof.

Equation (
2.15
) implies that

\hat{B}(\hat{A})^{{\rm ad}}\ =\ \bar{B}(A_{1})\oplus B(A_{2}).

Thus
\tau
sends
\hat{B}(\hat{A})
to
\hat{B}(\hat{A})^{\rm ad}
. Hence, the lemma follows from (
2.11
) and (
4.7
).
∎

4.7.
The Splitting theorem

We are now ready to formulate the main result of this section:

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
D
be an odd symmetric Dirac-type operator on a graded quaternionic Dirac bundle
(E=E^{+}\oplus E^{-},\theta^{E})
over a closed involutive manifold
(M,\theta^{M})
. Suppose
N\subset M
is a
\theta^{M}
-invariant hypersurface such that (
4.1
) is satisfied. Let
\hat{A}
,
A_{1}
, and
A_{2}
be as above. Then

(4.9)

\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D^{+}\ =\ \operatorname{ind}_{\tau}\hat{D}^{+}_{\hat{B}(\hat{A})}.

The rest of this section is occupied with the proof of this theorem, which is modeled on the proof of Theorem 5.11 of
[
11
]
.

4.9.
The transmission boundary conditions

First, we reformulate the Splitting theorem
4.8
solely in terms of the manifold
\hat{M}
. Specifically, we introduce a boundary condition
B
on
\hat{M}
such that
\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}\hat{D}^{+}_{B}=\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D^{+}
by construction.

Notice that

H^{1/2}(\partial\hat{M},E_{\partial\hat{M}})\ =\ H^{1/2}(N_{1},E_{N_{1}})\oplus H^{1/2}(N_{2},E_{N_{2}}).

We define the
transmission boundary conditions
on
\hat{M}

(4.10)

B_{\operatorname{tr}}\;:=\;\big\{(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u})\in H^{1/2}(N_{1},E_{N_{1}}^{+})\oplus H^{1/2}(N_{2},E_{N_{2}}^{+})\big\}.

It is shown in Example 1.16(d) of
[
4
]
that this is an elliptic boundary condition with
B^{\rm ad}_{\operatorname{tr}}=B_{\operatorname{tr}}
. Clearly,
\tau:B_{\operatorname{tr}}\to B_{\operatorname{tr}}
.

The canonical pull-back of sections from
E
to
\hat{E}
identifies the domains of
D^{+}
and
\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{\operatorname{tr}}}
. It follows that

\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{\operatorname{tr}}}\ =\ \operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D^{+}.

Hence, the Splitting theorem is equivalent to the equality

(4.11)

\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{\operatorname{tr}}}\ =\ \operatorname{ind}_{\tau}(\hat{D})^{+}_{\hat{B}(\hat{A})}.

Below we prove this equality by constructing a homotopy between the operators
\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{\operatorname{tr}}}
and
(\hat{D})^{+}_{\hat{B}(\hat{A})}
.

4.10.
A deformation of boundary conditions

We construct a continuous family of elliptic boundary conditions connecting
\hat{B}(\hat{A})
and the transmission boundary condition
B
.

Any
\mathbf{u}\in H^{1/2}(N,E_{N}^{+})
can be decomposed as
\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{u}^{+}+\mathbf{u}^{-}
, where
\mathbf{u}^{+}\in H^{1/2}_{[0,\infty)}(N,E_{N}^{+})
and
\mathbf{u}^{-}\in H^{1/2}_{(-\infty,0)}(N,E_{N}^{+})
. Then
\mathbf{u}^{+}\in\bar{B}(A_{2})
and
\mathbf{u}^{-}\in B(A_{1})
. Define two families of boundary conditions on
\hat{N}:=N_{1}\sqcup N_{2}
:

B_{s}\ :=\ \Big\{\,\big(\mathbf{u}^{-}+(1-s)\mathbf{u}^{+},\mathbf{u}^{+}+(1-s)\mathbf{u}^{-}\big):\,\mathbf{u}\in H^{1/2}(N,E_{N}^{+})\,\Big\}.

Then
B_{s}\subset H^{1/2}(N_{1},E_{N_{1}}^{+})\oplus H^{1/2}(N_{2},E_{N_{2}}^{+})
and the adjoint of
B_{s}
is the boundary condition

B_{s}^{\rm ad}\\
=\;\Big\{(\mathbf{v}^{-}+(1-s)\mathbf{v}^{+},\mathbf{v}^{+}+(1-s)\mathbf{v}^{-})\Big\}\subset H^{1/2}(\partial\hat{M},E_{\partial\hat{M}}^{-}),

where
\mathbf{v}^{-}\in H_{(-\infty,0]}^{1/2}(N,E_{N}^{-})
and
\mathbf{v}^{+}\in H_{(0,\infty)}^{1/2}(N,E_{N}^{-})
. Thus
B_{s}
is an elliptic boundary condition for all
s\in[0,1]
and we get a family of Fredholm operators
\{\hat{D}_{B_{s}}\}_{0\leq s\leq 1}
.

By (
4.8
),

(4.12)

\tau:\,B_{s}\ \to\ B_{s}^{\rm ad}.

Hence,

(4.13)

\tau\,\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}\,\tau^{-1}\ =\ \hat{D}^{-}_{B_{s}^{\rm ad}}.

By (
2.11
), the right-hand side of this equality is equal to the adjoint of
\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}^{\rm ad}}
. Hence, the operators
\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}
are odd symmetric, for all
s\in[0,1]
.

4.11.
A family of operators with the same boundary conditions

Clearly,
\hat{B}(\hat{A})=B_{1}
and the transmission boundary conditions
B=B_{0}
. Thus the family
\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}
interpolates between
\hat{D}^{+}_{\hat{B}(\hat{A})}
and
\hat{D}^{+}_{B}
. However, the operators
D^{+}_{B_{s}}
have different domains, so Theorem
3.8
, can no be applied directly. We finish the proof of Theorem
4.8
by a slight modification of the method of
[
4
, Lemma 8.11, Theorem 8.12]
: we construct a continuous family of operators

K_{s}:\,L^{2}(\hat{M},\hat{E}^{+})\to L^{2}(\hat{M},\hat{E}^{+})

such that the restriction

K_{s}\;:\;\operatorname{dom}\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{\operatorname{tr}}}\;\to\;\operatorname{dom}\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}

is an isomorphism. Then

(4.14)

\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}\circ K_{s}:\,\operatorname{dom}\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{\operatorname{tr}}}\ \to\ L^{2}\big(\hat{M},\hat{E}^{-}\big)

is a continuous family of operators. These operators are not odd symmetric. But in Lemma
4.15
we show that
\dim\ker\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}\circ K_{s}
is independent of
s
modulo 2. Hence,

(4.15)

\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{\operatorname{tr}}}\ \equiv\ \operatorname{ind}_{\tau}\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{1}}\ \equiv\ \dim\ker\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{1}}\circ K_{1}\\
\equiv\ \dim\ker\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{0}}\circ K_{0}\ \equiv\ \operatorname{ind}_{\tau}\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{0}}\ \equiv\ \operatorname{ind}_{\tau}\hat{D}^{+}_{\hat{B}(\hat{A})},

where, as usual, “
\equiv
” denotes equality modulo 2.
This proves (
4.11
) and, hence, Theorem
3.8
.

We finish the proof by constructing the family
K_{s}
and showing that the dimension of the kernel of
\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}\circ K_{s}
is independent of
s
modulo 2.

4.12.
Family of isomorphisms of boundary conditions

Recall that we denote by
B_{\operatorname{tr}}
the transmission boundary conditions (
4.10
) and that
B_{0}=B_{\operatorname{tr}}
. First, consider a family of isomorphisms

k_{s}:B_{\operatorname{tr}}\to B_{s},\qquad k_{s}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u}):=(\mathbf{u}^{-}+(1-s)\mathbf{u}^{+},\mathbf{u}^{+}+(1-s)\mathbf{u}^{-}).

Then
k_{0}=\operatorname{id}
and
k_{s}
are isomorphisms from
B_{\operatorname{tr}}
to
B_{s}
.

By definition,

(k_{s_{1}}-k_{s_{2}})(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u})\;=\;(s_{2}-s_{1})(\mathbf{u}^{+},\mathbf{u}^{-}).

Notice that
\|(\mathbf{u}^{+},\mathbf{u}^{-})\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial\hat{M},\hat{E}_{\partial\hat{M}})}\leq\|(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u})\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial\hat{M},\hat{E}_{\partial\hat{M}})}
.
Hence, for
s_{1},s_{2}\in[0,1]
with
|s_{1}-s_{2}|<\varepsilon
, the operator

k_{s_{1}}-k_{s_{2}}:\,B_{\operatorname{tr}}\to\ H^{1/2}(\partial\hat{M},\hat{E}_{\partial\hat{M}})

has a norm not greater than
\varepsilon
. This implies that
\{k_{s}\}
is a continuous family of maps from
B_{\operatorname{tr}}
to
H^{1/2}(\partial\hat{M},\hat{E}_{\partial\hat{M}})
.

4.13.
The extension map

Fix a
\theta^{M}
-invariant smooth cut-off function
\chi:M\to[0,1]
such that
\chi(t)=1
in a small neighborhood of
N
and
\chi(t)=0
outside of
U\simeq(-r,r)\times N
. By slight abuse of notation, we denote by
\chi
also the induced function on
\hat{M}
.

Let
B\in H^{1/2}(\partial\hat{M},\hat{E}^{+}_{\partial\hat{M}})
be an elliptic boundary condition.
By Lemma 7.3 of
[
4
]
the domain
\operatorname{dom}\hat{D}_{B}
coincides with the space which is denoted by
H^{1}_{D}(M,E^{+};B)
in
[
4
]
. Hence, it follows from Theorem 6.7(iii) of
[
4
]
that the formula

({E}\phi)(t,y)\ :=\ \chi(t)\cdot\exp\big(-|t||A|\big)\,\phi,\qquad t\in(-r,r),\ y\in N,

defines a continuous linear map,

{E}:B\ \to\ \operatorname{dom}\hat{D}^{+}_{B},

where

\operatorname{dom}\hat{D}^{+}_{B}
is viewed as a Hilbert space with the scalar product

(4.16)

\langle\phi,\psi\rangle_{\operatorname{dom}\hat{D}^{+}_{B}}\ :=\ \langle\phi,\psi\rangle_{L^{2}(M,E^{+})}\ +\ \langle\hat{D}_{B}\phi,\hat{D}_{B}\psi\rangle_{L^{2}(M,E^{-})}.

The map
{E}
is called the
extension map
.

4.14.
A family of operators with the same domain

Let
B_{s}
be the family of boundary conditions defined in Section
4.10
and
k_{s}
be as in Section
4.12
. Define a family of maps

(4.17)

K_{s}:\,\operatorname{dom}(\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{0}})\ \to\ \operatorname{dom}(\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}),\qquad K_{s}(\phi)\ :=\ \phi\ +\ {E}\big(k_{s}({R}(\phi))-{R}(\phi)\big)

It is shown in the proof of Theorem 8.12 of
[
4
]
that
K_{s}
is a continuous family of isomorphisms of Hilbert spaces and that the compositions

\operatorname{dom}(\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{0}})\ \overset{K_{s}}{\longrightarrow}\ \operatorname{dom}(\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}})\ \overset{\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}}{\longrightarrow}\ L^{2}(M,E^{-})

is a continuous family of bounded operators from the Hilbert space
\operatorname{dom}(\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{0}})
to
L^{2}(M,E^{-})
.

The composition
\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}K_{s}
is not odd symmetric. However, the following lemma is true

Lemma 4.15
.

The dimension of the kernel of

(4.18)

\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}K_{s}:\,\operatorname{dom}(\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{0}})\ \to\ L^{2}(M,E^{-})

is independent of
s
modulo 2.

Proof.

Let
K_{s}^{\dagger}:\operatorname{dom}(\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}})\to\operatorname{dom}(\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{0}})
denote the adjoint of
K_{s}
. Here, for an unbounded operator
A
on
L^{2}(M,E)
we reserve the notation
A^{*}
for its adjoint with respect to the
L^{2}
-scalar products, and denote by
A^{\dagger}
the adjoint with respect to some other scalar products, which will be specified in each case. In particular,
K_{s}^{\dagger}
is defined with respect to the graph scalar products on
\operatorname{dom}(\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}})
and
\operatorname{dom}(\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{0}})

Set

U_{s}\ :=\ K_{s}(K_{s}^{\dagger}K_{s})^{-1/2}:\,\operatorname{dom}(D_{B_{0}})\,\ \to\ \operatorname{dom}(D_{B_{s}})

is a family of unitary operators,
U_{s}^{\dagger}=U_{s}^{-1}
, and the composition

(4.19)

\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}U_{s}:\,\,\operatorname{dom}(\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{0}})\ \to\ L^{2}(M,E^{-})

is a continuous family of bounded maps from the Hilbert space
\operatorname{dom}(\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{0}})
to
L^{2}(M,E^{-})
. It is enough to show that the dimension of the kernel of these maps is constant modulo 2.

Recall that the adjoint
(\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}})^{*}
of the unbounded operator
\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}
is equal to
\hat{D}^{-}_{B_{s}^{\rm ad}}
. Let

(\hat{D}_{B_{s}}^{+})^{\dagger}:\,L^{2}(M,E^{-})\ \to\ \operatorname{dom}(\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}})

denote the adjoint of the bounded operator

\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}:\,\operatorname{dom}(\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}})\to\ L^{2}(M,E^{-})

between the Hilbert spaces
\operatorname{dom}(\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}})
and
L^{2}(M,E^{-})
.
Then for
\phi\in\operatorname{dom}(\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}})
and
\psi\in\operatorname{dom}(\hat{D}^{-}_{B_{s}^{\rm ad}})
we have

(4.20)

\Big\langle\,\phi,\hat{D}^{-}_{B_{s}^{\rm ad}}\psi\,\Big\rangle_{L^{2}(M,E^{+})}\ =\ \Big\langle\,\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}\phi,\psi\,\Big\rangle_{L^{2}(M,E^{-})}\ =\ \Big\langle\,\,\phi,(\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}})^{\dagger}\psi\,\Big\rangle_{\operatorname{dom}(\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}})}\\
=\ \Big\langle\,\,\phi,(\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}})^{\dagger}\psi\,\Big\rangle_{L^{2}(M,E^{+})}\ +\ \Big\langle\,\,\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}\phi,\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}(\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}})^{\dagger}\psi\,\Big\rangle_{L^{2}(M,E^{-})}\\
=\ \Big\langle\,\phi,(1+\hat{D}^{-}_{B_{s}^{\rm ad}}\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}})(\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}})^{\dagger}\psi\,\Big\rangle_{L^{2}(M,E^{+})}.

Since
\operatorname{dom}(\hat{D}^{-}_{B_{s}^{\rm ad}})
is dense in
L^{2}(M,E^{-})
, comparing the left and right-hand sides of this equality gives

(4.21)

(\hat{D}_{B_{s}}^{+})^{\dagger}\ =\ \Big(1+\hat{D}^{-}_{B_{s}^{\rm ad}}\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}\Big)^{-1}\hat{D}^{-}_{B_{s}^{\rm ad}}.

Let
\big(\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}U_{s}\big)^{\dagger}:L^{2}(M,E^{-})\to\operatorname{dom}(\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{0}})
denote the adjoint of the operator (
4.19
). Using
U_{s}^{\dagger}=U_{s}^{-1}
and (
4.21
), we obtain

\big(\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}U_{s}\big)^{\dagger}\ =\ U_{s}^{-1}\Big(1+\hat{D}^{-}_{B_{s}^{\rm ad}}\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}\Big)^{-1}\hat{D}^{-}_{B_{s}^{\rm ad}}.

We now compute the dimension of the kernel of
\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}K_{s}
as

(4.22)

\dim\ker\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}K_{s}\ =\ \dim\ker\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}U_{s}\ =\ \dim\ker\big(\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}U_{s}\big)^{\dagger}\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}U_{s}\\
=\ \dim\ker U_{s}^{-1}\Big(1+\hat{D}^{-}_{B_{s}^{\rm ad}}\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}\Big)^{-1}\hat{D}^{-}_{B_{s}^{\rm ad}}\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}U_{s}.

Here, the last two operators are positive self-adjoint endomorphisms of the Hilbert space
\operatorname{dom}(D^{+}_{B_{0}})
.

We are now ready to prove the lemma. Set

P_{s}\ :=\ \big(\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}U_{s}\big)^{\dagger}\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}U_{s}\ =\ U_{s}^{-1}\Big(1+\hat{D}^{-}_{B_{s}^{\rm ad}}\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}\Big)^{-1}\hat{D}^{-}_{B_{s}^{\rm ad}}\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}U_{s}.

It follows from (
4.22
) that it suffices to show that for every
s_{0}\in[0,1]
there exists
\epsilon>0
such that
\dim\ker P_{s}
is independent of
s
modulo 2 for
s\in(s_{0}-\epsilon,s_{0}+\epsilon)
.

Since
D_{s}^{*}D_{s}
is an elliptic operator of positive order and the boundary conditions
B_{s}
and
B_{s}^{\rm ad}
are elliptic, the spectrum of the operator
\hat{D}^{-}_{B_{s}^{\rm ad}}\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}
is discrete. Therefore, the intersection of the spectrum of the operator
\Big(1+\hat{D}^{-}_{B_{s}^{\rm ad}}\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}\Big)^{-1}\hat{D}^{-}_{B_{s}^{\rm ad}}\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}
and, hence, of
P_{s}
,
with the interval
[0,1)
is discrete. Hence,
for
s_{0}\in[0,1]
, there exists
\epsilon,\delta>0
such that
\delta<1
is not in the spectrum of
P_{s}
for all
s\in(s_{0}-\epsilon,s_{0}+\epsilon)
and the intersection of the spectrum of
P_{s_{0}}
with the interval
[0,\delta]
is equal to
\{0\}
. Then
for all
s\in(s_{0}-\epsilon,s_{0}+\epsilon)

\dim\ker P_{s_{0}}\ =\ \sum_{0\leq\lambda<\delta}\dim E_{s,\lambda},

where
E_{s,\lambda}
is the eigenspace of
P_{s}
with eigenvalue
\lambda
.

Hence, to prove the lemma, it is enough to show that the dimension of
E_{s,\lambda}
is even for all
0<\lambda<\delta
.

Set
\tilde{E}_{s,\lambda}:=U_{s}E_{s,\lambda}
. Then, for all
\phi\in\tilde{E}_{s,\lambda}
,

\Big(1+\hat{D}^{-}_{B_{s}^{\rm ad}}\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}\Big)^{-1}\hat{D}^{-}_{B_{s}^{\rm ad}}\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}\,\phi\ =\ \lambda\phi.

It follows that

\hat{D}^{-}_{B_{s}^{\rm ad}}\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}\,\phi\ =\ \frac{\lambda}{1-\lambda}\,\phi.

Hence,
\tilde{E}_{s,\lambda}
is an eigenspace of
\hat{D}^{-}_{B_{s}^{\rm ad}}\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}
. To finish the proof of the lemma we need to show that

\dim\tilde{E}_{s,\lambda}\ =\ \dim E_{s,\lambda}

is even. We proceed as in the proof of Theorem 2.5 of
[
9
]
. Set
\mu=\frac{\lambda}{1-\lambda}
.

Recall that
\tau\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}\tau^{-1}=\hat{D}^{-}_{B_{s}^{\rm ad}}
and
\tau^{-1}=-\tau
. Hence,

\hat{D}^{-}_{B_{s}^{\rm ad}}\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}\,\tau\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}\ =\ \tau\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}\,\hat{D}^{-}_{B_{s}^{\rm ad}}\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}.

It follows that
\tau\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}(\tilde{E}_{s,\lambda})\subset\tilde{E}_{s,\lambda}
. For
\lambda>0
consider the operator

{E}_{s,\lambda}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu}}\tau\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}:\,\tilde{E}_{s,\lambda}\ \to\tilde{E}_{s,\lambda}.

Then

{E}_{s,\lambda}^{2}\ =\ \frac{1}{\mu}\,\tau\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}\tau\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}\ =\ -\frac{1}{\mu}\tau\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}\tau^{-1}\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}\ =\ -\frac{1}{\mu}\hat{D}^{-}_{B_{s}^{\rm ad}}\hat{D}^{+}_{B_{s}}\ =\ -\operatorname{Id}:\,\tilde{E}_{s,\lambda}\ \to\tilde{E}_{s,\lambda}.

Thus
{E}_{s,\lambda}:\,\tilde{E}_{s,\lambda}\ \to\tilde{E}_{s,\lambda}
is an anti-unitary anti-involution. From Kramers’ degeneracy (cf., for example, Lemma 2.2 of
[
9
]
) we conclude now that the dimension of
\tilde{E}_{s,\lambda}
is even for all
\lambda\in(0,\delta)
.
∎

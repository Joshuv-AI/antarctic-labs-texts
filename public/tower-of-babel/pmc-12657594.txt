Response to: “Reconsidering Patellofemoral Outcomes in Medially Stabilized Total Knee Arthroplasty: The Role of Implant Design and Rotational Strategy in Kinematic Alignment”
Authors: Samuel W. King, Nicolas Silvestrini, Anne Lübbeke, Hemant Pandit, Hermes H. Miozzari
Published: 2025

Dear Editor,

We thank Dr Hsu for their thoughtful comments on our article and for the opportunity to clarify several points.

Our study evaluated kinematic vs mechanical alignment using a single medially stabilized total knee arthroplasty system [1]. To our knowledge this is the first clinical study of its kind. We agree that patellofemoral features of this implant such as a lateralized trochlear groove and a medialized dome patella may influence patellar tracking. Our article must be viewed in the context of being the study of a specific implant. We list this as a limitation in our discussion section. We would welcome further comparative studies across multiple implant platforms.

We agree that our study is especially pertinent to surgeons interested in the restoration of native knee kinematics and who may consider combining medially stabilized total knee arthroplasty and kinematic alignment (KA) in this pursuit. In our cohort, KA was associated with greater patellar tilt without worse 1-year WOMAC pain or function, which suggests that any increase in tilt did not translate into early patient-reported detriment within this implant context. Whether similar findings hold for less anatomical trochlear geometries warrants investigation.

As described, we used a calipered KA technique that aims to reproduce the prearthritic joint lines as described by Howell et al. rather than prescribe a fixed degree of femoral external rotation [2]. We agree that rotation may interact with patellofemoral mechanics and implant geometry. We would welcome future studies where femoral component rotation angles are presented; regretfully these data were not available for our analysis.

We thank the Editor for the opportunity to respond to this letter and hope that this enhances our original article.

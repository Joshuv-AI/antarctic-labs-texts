Acceleration of convergence in approximate solutions of Urysohn integral equations with Green’s kernels
Shashank K. Shukla, Gobinda Rakshit

Abstract
Consider a non-linear operator equation x − K ⁡ ( x ) = f x-K(x)=f , where f f is a given function and K K is a Urysohn integral operator with Green’s function type kernel defined on L ∞ ​ [ 0 , 1 ] L^{\infty}[0,1] . We apply approximation methods based on interpolatory projections onto the approximating space 𝒳 n \mathcal{X}_{n} , which is the space of piecewise polynomials of even degree with respect to a uniform partition of [ 0 , 1 ] [0,1] . The approximate solutions obtained from these methods demonstrate enhanced accuracy compared to the classical collocation solution for the same equation. Numerical examples are given to support our theoretical results.

Introduction

Let
\mathcal{X}=L^{\infty}[0,1]
and
\Omega=[0,1]\times[0,1]\times\mathbb{R}
. Let
\kappa:\Omega\mapsto\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function. Consider the non-linear integral operator

K:L^{\infty}[0,1]\mapsto C[0,1]
as

K(x)(s)=\int_{0}^{1}\kappa(s,t,x(t))~dt,\quad x\in\mathcal{X},\quad s\in[0,1].

(1)

Such an operator is called as Urysohn integral operator. Since the kernel
\kappa
is continuous,
K
is compact operator on
\mathcal{X}
. For given
f
and
\kappa
, we consider the following Urysohn integral equation

x-K(x)=f.

(2)

The Fréchet derivative of
K
at
x\in\mathcal{X}
is given by

(K^{\prime}(x)y)(s)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\partial\kappa}{\partial u}(s,t,x(t))y(t)~dt,\quad y\in\mathcal{X},\quad s\in[0,1],

where
\frac{\partial\kappa}{\partial u}(s,t,x(t))
represents the partial derivative of
\kappa
with respect to its third component
u
at
x(t)
. Assuming that the equation (
2
) possesses a solution
\varphi
and,
1
is not an eigenvalue of the compact linear operator
K^{\prime}(\varphi)
. Then,
\varphi
is an isolated solution of (
2
). See
[
1
,
2
]
. Note that,
\varphi\in C^{\alpha}[0,1]
whenever
f\in C^{\alpha}[0,1]
for any positive integer
\alpha
. (See
[
3
, Corollory 3.2]
). Solving the equation (
2
) exactly, is often challenging, so we focus on finding approximate solutions. In collocation method, we employ a sequence of finite-rank interplatory projections that converge pointwise to the identity operator to approximate the operator
K
. This method has been extensively studied in
[
1
,
2
,
4
]
.

Let
\mathcal{X}_{n}
denotes a sequence of finite-dimensional subspaces approximating
\mathcal{X}
, and let
\left\{Q_{n}\right\}
represents a sequence of interpolory projections from
\mathcal{X}
to
\mathcal{X}_{n}
. In the collocation method, equation (
2
) is approximated by

\varphi_{n}^{C}-Q_{n}K(\varphi_{n}^{C})=Q_{n}f.

For linear integral equations, Sloan
[
5
]
introduced the iterated collocation solution, obtained through a single iteration of the collocation solution. This approach was further opted by Atkinson-Potra
[
3
]
for Urysohn integral equations. The iterated collocation solution is defined by

\varphi_{n}^{S}=K(\varphi_{n}^{C})+f.

Additionally, there exists an enhanced approach known as the modified collocation method, proposed by Grammont-Kulkarni
[
6
]
as

\varphi_{n}^{M}-K_{n}^{M}(\varphi_{n}^{M})=f,\quad\mbox{ with }~K_{n}^{M}(x)=Q_{n}K(x)+K(Q_{n}x)-Q_{n}K(Q_{n}x).

This approach generalizes the modified collocation method for the linear case, as proposed by Kulkarni
[
7
]
. The solution obtained by applying a single iteration in
\varphi_{n}^{M}
is termed the iterated modified collocation solution which is defined by

\widetilde{\varphi}_{n}^{M}=K(\varphi_{n}^{M})+f.

For positive integer
r\geq 0
, let
\mathcal{X}_{n}
be the space of piecewise polynomials of degree
\leq 2r
associated with the uniform partition of
[0,1]
defined as
\{0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{n}=1\}
, where
t_{j}=\frac{j}{n}
for
j=1,2,\ldots,n
with subinterval length
\displaystyle{h=\frac{1}{n}}
. In each subinterval
[t_{j-1},t_{j}]
, choose
2r+1
collocation points as Gauss points (i.e. zeros of the Legendre polynomials), and let
Q_{n}:\mathcal{X}\mapsto\mathcal{X}_{n}
be the interpolatory projection at the collocation points. Then, the following orders of convergence are proved in Atkinson-Potra
[
3
]
for sufficiently smooth kernel of
K
:

\norm{\varphi- \varphi_{n}^C}_{\infty}=O\left(h^{2r+1}\right),\quad\norm{\varphi- \varphi_{n}^S}_{\infty}=O\left(h^{4r+2}\right).

The following convergence rates are established in Grammont et al.
[
8
]
:

\norm{\varphi- \varphi_n^M }_{\infty}=O(h^{6r+3}),\quad\norm{\varphi- \widetilde{\varphi}_n^M }_{\infty}=O(h^{8r+4}).

In the case of interpolatory projection with collocation points which are not necessarily Gauss points, but
2r+1
distinct special points in each subinterval as collocation points, and sufficiently smooth kernels, Grammont et al
[
8
]
proved that the above modified projection solutions (both) are of the order
h^{4r+2}
. We observe that selecting Gauss points as collocation points improves the convergence orders through iterative refinement.

In Rakshit et al
[
9
]
, we analyzed the orders of convergence for the approximate solutions of Fredholm integral equations with Green’s kernel obtained by the collocation method, iterated collocation method and its modified variants. In the same paper, we considered
\mathcal{X}_{n}
as the piecewise polynomial space of degree
\leq 2r
(
r\geq 1
) with respect to the same uniform partition, and the projections as interpolatory operator at
2r+1
midpoints of each subinterval
[t_{j-1},t_{j}]
for
j=1,2,\ldots,n
. Then, we obtain the orders of convergence of collocation, iterated collocation, modified collocation and iterated modified collocation solutions as
h^{2r+1},h^{2r+2},h^{2r+2}
and
h^{2r+3}
respectively.

In this paper, we consider Urysohn integral equations with smooth and non-smooth kernels, and the projection
Q_{n}:\mathcal{X}\mapsto\mathcal{X}_{n}
defined as interpolation at
2r+1
equidistant points (including boundary points) in each subinterval
[t_{j-1},t_{j}]
for
j=1,2,\ldots,n
. Next, the approximate solutions of (
2
) using collocation methods and their variants are derived, and their orders of convergence are analyzed.

In many recent studies , such as
[
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
, improved convergence is typically achieved by using collocation points that are zeros of special functions. These choices are known to enhance the accuracy of numerical integration and are commonly used in classical iterated collocation methods.
In contrast, our method achieves higher-order convergence by using even-degree piecewise polynomial spaces with equidistant collocation points, which are not necessarily tied with zeros of special functions. This simpler setup still delivers strong performance, both theoretically and numerically, demonstrating the flexibility and effectiveness of our approach.

This article is organized as follows. Subsection
2.1
presents the assumptions concerning the kernel of the Urysohn integral operator, preliminary results, and notations. Subsections
2.2
and
2.3
focus on interpolatory projection and few important lemmas using divided difference. Section
3
discusses the methods of approximation including significant proofs and main theorems. Subsections
3.1
,
3.2
, and
3.3
establish the orders of convergence for collocation and iterated collocation, modified collocation, and iterated modified collocation methods, respectively. Section
4
provides numerical results, and Section
5
draws a conclusion of this paper.

Preliminaries

For an integer
\alpha\geq 0
, let
C^{\alpha}[0,1]
represent the space of all real-valued functions on
[0,1]
which are
\alpha
-times continuously differentiable, equipped with the norm

\norm{x}_{\alpha,\infty}=\max_{0\leq i\leq\alpha}\norm{x^{(i)}}_{\infty}=\max_{0\leq i\leq\alpha}\left\{\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}|x^{(i)}(t)|\right\}.

where
x^{(i)}
is the
i
-th derivative of the function
x
.

2.1
Green’s function type kernel

Consider the Urysohn integral operator (
1
) as

K(x)(s)=\int_{0}^{1}\kappa(s,t,x(t))\,dt,\quad s\in[0,1],\quad x\in\mathcal{X},

where the kernel
\kappa\in C(\Omega)
.
Let
\alpha\geq 1
be an integer. We assume that the kernel
\kappa
of
K
defined in equation (
1
) satisfies the following properties:

1.

For
i=1,2,3,4
, the functions
\kappa,~\frac{\partial^{i}\kappa}{\partial u^{i}}\in C(\Omega).

2.

Divide
\Omega
into two parts as
\Omega_{1}=\{(s,t,u):0\leq t\leq s\leq 1,u\in\mathbb{R}\}

and
\Omega_{2}=\{(s,t,u):0\leq s\leq t\leq 1,u\in\mathbb{R}\}
. There are two functions
\kappa_{i}\in C^{\alpha}(\Omega_{i})
,
i=1,2
, such that

\kappa(s,t,u)=\begin{cases}\kappa_{1}(s,t,u)&\text{if }(s,t,u)\in\Omega_{1},\\
\kappa_{2}(s,t,u)&\text{if }(s,t,u)\in\Omega_{2}.\end{cases}

3.

Let

\ell(s,t,u)=\frac{\partial\kappa}{\partial u}(s,t,u),\quad m(s,t,u)=\frac{\partial^{2}\kappa}{\partial u^{2}}(s,t,u),\quad\text{for all}\;(s,t,u)\in\Omega.

Clearly,
\ell
,
m\in C(\Omega)
. The partial derivatives of
\ell(s,t,u)
and
m(s,t,u)
with respect to
s
and
t
exhibit jump discontinuities along the line
s=t
.

4.

There exist functions
\ell_{i}
,
m_{i}\in C^{\beta}(\Omega_{i})
,
i=1,2
, such that

\ell(s,t,u)=\begin{cases}\ell_{1}(s,t,u)&\text{if }(s,t,u)\in\Omega_{1},\\
\ell_{2}(s,t,u)&\text{if }(s,t,u)\in\Omega_{2}\end{cases}

and

m(s,t,u)=\begin{cases}m_{1}(s,t,u)&\text{if }(s,t,u)\in\Omega_{1},\\
m_{2}(s,t,u)&\text{if }(s,t,u)\in\Omega_{2}.\end{cases}

Since
\frac{\partial^{i}\kappa}{\partial u^{i}}\in C(\Omega)
for
i=1,2,3,4
, the operator
K
being four times Fréchet differentiable at
x\in\mathcal{X}
is expressed as

K^{(i)}(x)(y_{1},\ldots,y_{i})(s)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\partial^{i}\kappa}{\partial u^{i}}(s,t,x(t))y_{1}(t)\cdots y_{i}(t)~dt,\quad s\in[0,1],

where

\frac{\partial^{i}\kappa}{\partial u^{i}}(s,t,x(t))=\left.\frac{\partial^{i}\kappa}{\partial u^{i}}(s,t,u)\right|_{u=x(t)},\quad i=1,2,3,4,

and
y_{1},y_{2},y_{3},y_{4}\in\mathcal{X}
. Note that
K^{\prime}(x):\mathcal{X}\mapsto\mathcal{X}
is a compact linear operator, while
K^{(i)}(x):\mathcal{X}^{i}\mapsto\mathcal{X}
are multilinear operators, where
\mathcal{X}^{i}
represents the Cartesian product of
i
copies of
\mathcal{X}
(see Rall
[
14
]
). The norms of these operators are given by

\norm{K^{(i)}(x)}=\sup\left\{\norm{K^{(i)}(x)(y_1, \dots, y_i)}_{\infty}:\norm{y_j}_{\infty}\leq 1,\,j=1,\dots,i\right\}

for
i=1,2,3,4
. It follows that

\norm{K^{(i)}(x)}\leq\sup_{s,t\in[0,1]}\left|\frac{\partial^{i}\kappa}{\partial u^{i}}(s,t,x(t))\right|,\quad i=1,2,3,4.

We use the following notations throughout the article:

Define
S_{1}=\{(s,t):0\leq t\leq s\leq 1\}
,
S_{2}=\{(s,t):0\leq s\leq t\leq 1\}
and

\displaystyle\ell_{*}(s,t):=\ell(s,t,\varphi(t))=\begin{cases}\ell_{1,*}(s,t)=\ell_{1}(s,t,\varphi(t)),~~~&(s,t)\in S_{1},\\
\ell_{2,*}(s,t)=\ell_{2}(s,t,\varphi(t)),~~~&(s,t)\in S_{2}.\end{cases}

Assume that
\ell_{*}\in C([0,1]\times[0,1])
,
\ell_{1,*}\in C^{2r+1}(S_{1})
and
\ell_{2,*}\in C^{2r+1}(S_{2})
.

For
j,k
non-negative integers, we set

D^{(j,k)}\ell_{i,*}(s,t)=\frac{\partial^{j+k}\ell_{i,*}}{\partial s^{j}\partial t^{k}}(s,t),\quad\text{for}\;i=1,2.

Define

C_{1}=\max_{0\leq i\leq 4}\left(\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}s,t\in[0,1]\\
|u|\leq\norm{\varphi}_{\infty}+\delta_{0}\end{subarray}}\left|\dfrac{\partial^{i}\kappa}{\partial u^{i}}(s,t,u)\right|\right),\quad C_{2}=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq n\\
j=1,2\end{subarray}}\bigg\{\sup_{t_{k-1}\leq t\leq s\leq t_{k}}\left|D^{(j,0)}\ell_{1,*}(s,t)\right|,\sup_{t_{k-1}\leq s\leq t\leq t_{k}}\left|D^{(j,0)}\ell_{2,*}(s,t)\right|\bigg\}.

From the Fréchet derivatives of
K
, it is easy to see that

\norm{(K'(\varphi)y)^{(i)}}_{\infty}\leq C_{1}\norm{y}_{\infty},\;i=0,1,2,

(3)

\norm{(K''(\varphi)(y_1,y_2))^{(i)}}_{\infty}\leq C_{1}\norm{y_1}_{\infty}\norm{y_2}_{\infty},\;i=0,1,2.

(4)

We have

(K^{\prime}(\varphi)y)(s)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\partial\kappa}{\partial u}(s,t,\varphi(t))y(t)~dt=\int_{0}^{1}\ell(s,t,\varphi(t))y(t)~dt,\quad s\in[0,1].

For
\delta_{0}>0
, let
\mathcal{B}(\varphi,\delta_{0})=\{x\in\mathcal{X}:\norm{x - \varphi}_{\infty}<\delta_{0}\}
be a closed ball with centre
\varphi
and radius
\delta_{0}
.
If
x,y\in\mathcal{B}(\varphi,\delta_{0})
, then it can be easily shown that

\norm{K'(x) - K'(y)}\leq\gamma\norm{x - y }_{\infty},

(5)

for some constant
\gamma
.

2.2
Interpolatory projection

Let the uniform partition of
[0,1]
be

\left\{0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{n}=1\right\},

where
t_{j}=\frac{j}{n}=jh,\quad j=1,2,\ldots,n.
Denote
\Delta_{j}=[t_{j-1},t_{j}]
. For
r\geq 0
, define

\mathcal{X}_{n}=\{x\in L^{\infty}[0,1]:x|_{\Delta_{j}}\text{ is a polynomial of even degree }\leq 2r\}.

In each sub-interval
[t_{j-1},t_{j}]
, consider
2r+1
interpolation points as

\tau_{j}^{i}=t_{j-1}+\frac{ih}{2r},\quad i=0,1,\ldots,2r\;\text{ for }\;r\geq 1,\quad\text{and}\quad\tau_{j}=\frac{t_{j-1}+t_{j}}{2}\;\text{ for }\;r=0.

Define
Q_{n}:\mathcal{X}\mapsto\mathcal{X}_{n}
by

Q_{n}x(\tau_{j}^{i})=x(\tau_{j}^{i}),\quad i=0,1,2,\ldots,2r;~j=1,2,\ldots,n.

(6)

Let
x\in C[0,1]
, then the
(2r+1)^{\text{th}}
divided difference of
x
at
\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\dots,\tau_{j}^{2r}
is defined by

\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\dots,\tau_{j}^{2r}\right]x=\frac{\left[\tau_{j}^{1},\tau_{j}^{2},\dots,\tau_{j}^{2r}\right]x-\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\dots,\tau_{j}^{2r-1}\right]x}{\tau_{j}^{2r}-\tau_{j}^{0}}.

Let

\Psi_{j}(t)=\left(t-\tau_{j}^{0}\right)\left(t-\tau_{j}^{1}\right)\cdots\left(t-\tau_{j}^{2r}\right),\quad j=1,2,\ldots,n.

As
Q_{n,j}x
(where
Q_{n,j}
is
Q_{n}
restricted on
{\Delta_{j}}
) interpolates
x
at the points
\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\ldots,\tau_{j}^{2r},
therefore

x(t)-Q_{n,j}x(t)=\Psi_{j}(t)\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\dots,\tau_{j}^{2r},t\right]x,\quad t\in\Delta_{j}.

If
x\in C^{2r+1}(\Delta_{j})
, then we have

\norm{(I - Q_{n,j})x}_{\infty}\leq C_{3}\norm{x^{(2r+1)}}_{\infty}h^{2r+1},

(7)

where
x^{(2r+1)}
denotes the
(2r+1)^{\text{th}}
derivative of
x
and
C_{3}
is a constant independent of
h
. See
[
15
]
.

2.3
Even degree polynomial interpolation

The scenario of interpolatory projection at equidistant collocation points (not necessarily the Gauss points) for a Green’s type kernel of the Urysohn integral operator onto a space of piecewise polynomials of degree
\leq 2r
over a uniform partition of
[0,1]
has not been explored in the research literature. This article addresses that gap by studying this case. We first prove two important lemmas based on the divided difference of
(K^{\prime}(\varphi)x)
at
\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\dots,\tau_{j}^{2r}
and
s
denoted by
\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\dots,\tau_{j}^{2r},s\right](K^{\prime}(\varphi)x)
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
r\geq 0
and
\left\{\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\dots,\tau_{j}^{2r}\right\}
be the set of collocation points in
[t_{j-1},t_{j}]
for
j=1,2,\dots,n
. Then

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}]}\left|\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\dots,\tau_{j}^{2r},s\right](K^{\prime}(\varphi)x)\right|\leq C_{6}\norm{x}_{\infty}h^{-2r},

for some constant
C_{6}
.

Proof.

Recall that

\tau_{j}^{i}=t_{j-1}+\frac{ih}{2r}=t_{j-1}+h\zeta_{i},~\text{ for }i=0,1,2,\dots,2r;~j=1,2,\dots,n,

where
\displaystyle{\zeta_{i}=\frac{i}{2r}\in[0,1]}
.
Clearly
\tau_{j}^{i}\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}]
, we have

(K^{\prime}(\varphi)x)(s)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\partial\kappa}{\partial u}(s,t,\varphi(t))x(t)~dt=\int_{0}^{1}\ell_{*}(s,t)x(t)~dt,\quad s\in[0,1],

where

\ell_{*}(s,t)~=~\begin{cases}\ell_{1,*}(s,t),~~~&0\leq t\leq s\leq 1,\\
\ell_{2,*}(s,t),~~~&0\leq s\leq t\leq 1.\\
\end{cases}
Let
s\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}]
, then

\displaystyle[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},s](K^{\prime}(\varphi)x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},s]\ell_{*}(.,t)x(t)~dt

\displaystyle=\underset{k\neq j}{\sum_{k=1}^{n}}\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},s]\ell_{*}(.,t)x(t)~dt+\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},s]\ell_{*}(.,t)x(t)~dt.

(8)

Since
\kappa
is sufficiently differentiable in the interval
[t_{k-1},t_{k}]
for
k\neq j
,

\left|\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},s]\ell_{*}(.,t)x(t)dt\right|\leq C_{2}\norm{x}_{\infty}h.

(9)

For
\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},s\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}]
, we need to find bound for
[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},s]\ell_{*}(.,t)
. Fix
s\in[t_{j-1},{t_{j}}].

Case (I)

When
t_{j-1}\leq s<\tau_{j}^{0}
. Note that

[\tau_{j}^{0},s]\ell_{*}(.,t)=\dfrac{\ell_{*}(s,t)-\ell_{*}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}~=~\begin{cases}\dfrac{\ell_{1,*}(s,t)-\ell_{1,*}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}~&\text{if}\;t_{j-1}\leq t\leq s,\\
\dfrac{\ell_{2,*}(s,t)-\ell_{1,*}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}~&\text{if}\;s<t<\tau_{j}^{0},\\
\dfrac{\ell_{2,*}(s,t)-\ell_{2,*}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}~&\text{if}\;\tau_{j}^{0}\leq t\leq t_{j}.\end{cases}

Thus, for a fixed
s\in[t_{j-1},\tau_{j}^{0}]
, the function
[\tau_{j}^{0},s]\ell_{*}(.,t)
is continuous on
[t_{j-1},t_{j}].
Since
\ell_{1,*}(.,t)
and
\ell_{2,*}(.,t)
are continuous on
[s,\tau_{j}^{0}]
and differentiable on
(s,\tau_{j}^{0})
, by mean value theorem, we have

\frac{\ell_{1,*}(s,t)-\ell_{1,*}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}=D^{(1,0)}\ell_{1,*}(\eta_{1},t),~~\text{and}~~\frac{\ell_{2,*}(s,t)-\ell_{2,*}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}=D^{(1,0)}\ell_{2,*}(\eta_{2},t);

for some
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\in(s,\tau_{j}^{0}).

Now, for
t_{j-1}\leq t\leq s
,

\bigg|\frac{\ell_{1,*}(s,t)-\ell_{1,*}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}\bigg|\leq\bigg(\sup_{t_{j-1}\leq t\leq s\leq t_{j}}\left|D^{(1,0)}\ell_{1,*}(s,t)\right|\bigg)=C_{2}.

Similarly, for
\tau_{j}^{0}\leq t\leq t_{j}
,

\bigg|\frac{\ell_{2,*}(s,t)-\ell_{2,*}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}\bigg|\leq\bigg(\sup_{t_{j-1}\leq s\leq t\leq t_{j}}\left|D^{(1,0)}\ell_{2,*}(s,t)\right|\bigg)=C_{2}.

Hence,

\left|[\tau_{j}^{0},s]\ell_{*}(.,t)\right|\leq C_{2},\quad\text{if}\;t_{j-1}\leq t\leq s~\text{and}~\tau_{j}^{0}\leq t\leq t_{j}.

(10)

Now, for
s<t<\tau_{j}^{0}
, first we find

\displaystyle\frac{\ell_{2,*}(s,t)-\ell_{1,*}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\ell_{2,*}(s,t)-\ell_{2,*}(t,t)+\ell_{1,*}(t,t)-\ell_{1,*}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}

\displaystyle=\frac{D^{(1,0)}\ell_{2,*}(\eta_{3},t)(s-t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}+\frac{D^{(1,0)}\ell_{1,*}(\eta_{4},t)(t-\tau_{j}^{0})}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}},

for some
\eta_{3}\in(s,t)
and
\eta_{4}\in(t,\tau_{j}^{0}).
Hence,

\bigg|\frac{\ell_{2,*}(s,t)-\ell_{1,*}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}\bigg|\leq 2C_{2}.

Therefore,

\left|[\tau_{j}^{0},s]\ell_{*}(.,t)\right|\leq 2C_{2},\quad\text{if}\;s<t<\tau_{j}^{0}.

Thus, by (
10
) and above inequality, we obtain

\sup_{t\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}]}\left|[\tau_{j}^{0},s]\ell_{*}(.,t)\right|\leq 2C_{2}.

(11)

Case (II)

When
s=\tau_{j}^{0}
. In this case

\displaystyle[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{0}]\ell_{*}(.,t)=\begin{cases}D^{(1,0)}\ell_{1,*}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)\quad&\text{if}\;t_{j-1}\leq t<\tau_{j}^{0}<1,\\
D^{(1,0)}\ell_{2,*}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)\quad&\text{if}\;t_{j-1}\leq\tau_{j}^{0}<t<1.\\
\end{cases}

Hence,

\sup_{t\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}]}\left|[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{0}]\ell_{*}(.,t)\right|\leq C_{2}.

(12)

Case (III)

When
\tau_{j}^{0}<s\leq t_{j}
. Proceeding the same way as in Case (I), we have

\sup_{t\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}]}\left|[\tau_{j}^{0},s]\ell_{*}(.,t)\right|\leq 2C_{2}.

Hence, by (
11
), (
12
) and above inequality, we obtain

\sup_{s,t\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}]}\left|[\tau_{j}^{0},s]\ell_{*}(.,t)\right|\leq 2C_{2}.

(13)

Note that

[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},s]\ell_{*}(.,t)=\dfrac{[\tau_{j}^{0},s]\ell_{*}(.,t)-[\tau_{j}^{1},s]\ell_{*}(.,t)}{\tau_{j}^{0}-\tau_{j}^{1}}=\dfrac{1}{h(\zeta_{0}-\zeta_{1})}\bigg[[\tau_{j}^{0},s]\ell_{*}(.,t)-[\tau_{j}^{1},s]\ell_{*}(.,t)\bigg],

which gives

\displaystyle\bigg|[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},s]\ell_{*}(.,t)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{1}{h|\zeta_{0}-\zeta_{1}|}\bigg[\left|[\tau_{j}^{0},s]\ell_{*}(.,t)\right|+\left|[\tau_{j}^{1},s]\ell_{*}(.,t)\right|\bigg].

It follows from (
13
) that

\sup_{s,t\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}]}\left|[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},s]\ell_{*}(.,t)\right|\leq\dfrac{4C_{2}}{h|\zeta_{0}-\zeta_{1}|}.

Therefore,

\bigg|\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},s]\ell_{*}(.,t)x(t)~dt\bigg|\leq\dfrac{4C_{2}}{h|\zeta_{0}-\zeta_{1}|}\norm{x}_{\infty}=\dfrac{C_{4}}{h}\norm{x}_{\infty},

where
C_{4}=\dfrac{4C_{2}}{|\zeta_{0}-\zeta_{1}|}
is some constant. Hence, using (
9
) and above inequality in (
8
), we obtain

\left|[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},s](K^{\prime}(\varphi)x)\right|\leq\bigg(C_{2}h+\dfrac{C_{4}}{h}\bigg)\norm{x}_{\infty}\leq\dfrac{C_{5}}{h}\norm{x}_{\infty},

where
C_{5}
is some constant. Using mathematical induction, this gives the required estimate

\left|[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\dots,\tau_{j}^{2r},s](K^{\prime}(\varphi)x)\right|\leq C_{6}\norm{x}_{\infty}\dfrac{1}{h^{2r}},

for some constant
C_{6}.
Since
s\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}]
is arbitrary, the proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 2
.

Let
r\geq 0
and
\left\{\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\dots,\tau_{j}^{2r}\right\}
be the set of collocation points in
[t_{j-1},t_{j}]
for
j=1,2,\dots,n
. Then

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}]}\left|\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\dots,\tau_{j}^{2r},s,s\right]\left(K^{\prime}(\varphi)x\right)\right|\leq C_{8}\norm{x}_{\infty}h^{-2r},

for some constant
C_{8}.

Proof.

Let
s\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}]
, then

\displaystyle[\tau_{j}^{0},s,s](K^{\prime}(\varphi)x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}[\tau_{j}^{0},s,s]\ell_{*}(.,t)x(t)~dt

\displaystyle=\underset{k\neq j}{\sum_{k=1}^{n}}\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}[\tau_{j}^{0},s,s]\ell_{*}(.,t)x(t)~dt+\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}[\tau_{j}^{0},s,s]\ell_{*}(.,t)x(t)~dt.

(14)

Since
\kappa
is sufficiently differentiable in the interval
[t_{k-1},t_{k}]
for
k\neq j
,

\left|\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}[\tau_{j}^{0},s,s]\ell_{*}(.,t)x(t)dt\right|\leq C_{2}\norm{x}_{\infty}h.

(15)

For
\tau_{j}^{0},s\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}]
, we need to find bound for
[\tau_{j}^{0},s,s]\ell_{*}(.,t)
. Fix
s\in[t_{j-1},{t_{j}}].

Case (I)

When
t_{j-1}\leq s<\tau_{j}^{0}
. Note that

[\tau_{j}^{0},s,s]\ell_{*}(.,t)=\dfrac{[s,s]\ell_{*}(.,t)-[\tau_{j}^{0},s]\ell_{*}(.,t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}.

Thus,

[\tau_{j}^{0},s,s]\ell_{*}(.,t)~=~\begin{cases}\dfrac{\frac{\partial\ell_{1,*}}{\partial s}(s,t)-\left(\frac{\ell_{1,*}(s,t)-\ell_{1,*}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}\right)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}\quad&\text{if}\;t_{j-1}\leq t\leq s,\\
\dfrac{\frac{\partial\ell_{2,*}}{\partial s}(s,t)-\left(\frac{\ell_{2,*}(s,t)-\ell_{1,*}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}\right)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}\quad&\text{if}\;s<t<\tau_{j}^{0},\\
\dfrac{\frac{\partial\ell_{2,*}}{\partial s}(s,t)-\left(\frac{\ell_{2,*}(s,t)-\ell_{2,*}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}\right)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}\quad&\text{if}\;\tau_{j}^{0}\leq t\leq t_{j}.\end{cases}

This function
[\tau_{j}^{0},s,s]\ell_{*}(.,t)
is possibly discontinuous at
t=s.
Since
\ell_{1,*}(.,t)
and
\ell_{2,*}(.,t)
are continuous on
[s,\tau_{j}^{0}]
and differentiable on
(s,\tau_{j}^{0})
, by mean value theorem, we get

\frac{\ell_{1,*}(s,t)-\ell_{1,*}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}=D^{(1,0)}\ell_{1,*}(\nu_{1},t);~~\text{and}~~\frac{\ell_{2,*}(s,t)-\ell_{2,*}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}=D^{(1,0)}\ell_{2,*}(\nu_{2},t),

for some
\nu_{1},\nu_{2}\in(s,\tau_{j}^{0})
. Now, for
t_{j-1}\leq t\leq s,

\displaystyle\left|\dfrac{\frac{\partial\ell_{1,*}}{\partial s}(s,t)-\left(\frac{\ell_{1,*}(s,t)-\ell_{1,*}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}\right)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}\right|

\displaystyle=\Bigg|\dfrac{D^{(1,0)}\ell_{1,*}(s,t)-D^{(1,0)}\ell_{1,*}(\nu_{1},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}\Bigg|

\displaystyle=\Bigg|\dfrac{D^{(2,0)}\ell_{1,*}(\nu_{3},t)(s-\nu_{1})}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}\Bigg|,~\text{for}\;\nu_{3}\in(s,\nu_{1})

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\sup_{t_{j-1}\leq t\leq s\leq t_{j}}\left|D^{(2,0)}\ell_{1,*}(s,t)\right|\bigg)=C_{2}.

Similarly, it can be shown that for
\tau_{j}^{0}\leq t\leq t_{j}
,

\displaystyle\left|\dfrac{\frac{\partial\ell_{2,*}}{\partial s}(s,t)-\left(\frac{\ell_{2,*}(s,t)-\ell_{2,*}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}\right)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}\right|\leq\bigg(\sup_{t_{j-1}\leq s\leq t\leq t_{j}}\left|D^{(2,0)}\ell_{2,*}(s,t)\right|\bigg)=C_{2}.

Hence,

\left|[\tau_{j}^{0},s,s]\ell_{*}(.,t)\right|\leq C_{2},\quad\text{if}\;t_{j-1}\leq t\leq s~\text{and}~\tau_{j}^{0}\leq t\leq t_{j}.

(16)

Now, for
s<t<\tau_{j}^{0}
, we find

\displaystyle\frac{\ell_{2,*}(s,t)-\ell_{1,*}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\ell_{2,*}(s,t)-\ell_{2,*}(t,t)+\ell_{1,*}(t,t)-\ell_{1,*}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}

\displaystyle=\frac{D^{(1,0)}\ell_{2,*}(\nu_{5},t)(s-t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}+\frac{D^{(1,0)}\ell_{1,*}(\nu_{6},t)(t-\tau_{j}^{0})}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}},

for some
\nu_{5}\in(s,t)
and
\nu_{6}\in(t,\tau_{j}^{0})
. Then

\Bigg|\frac{\partial\ell_{2,*}}{\partial s}(s,t)-\left(\frac{\ell_{2,*}(s,t)-\ell_{1,*}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}\right)\Bigg|\\
=\Bigg|D^{(1,0)}\ell_{2,*}(s,t)-\frac{D^{(1,0)}\ell_{2,*}(\nu_{5},t)(s-t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}-\frac{D^{(1,0)}\ell_{1,*}(\nu_{6},t)(t-\tau_{j}^{0})}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}\Bigg|,

which gives

\Bigg|\frac{\partial\ell_{2,*}}{\partial s}(s,t)-\left(\frac{\ell_{2,*}(s,t)-\ell_{1,*}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}\right)\Bigg|\leq 3C_{2}.

Hence,

\mathop{\text{\Large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.63885pts$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\tau_{j}^{0}$}}$}}\nolimits\Bigg|\dfrac{\frac{\partial\ell_{2,*}}{\partial s}(s,t)-\left(\frac{\ell_{2,*}(s,t)-\ell_{1,*}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}\right)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}\Bigg|~dt\leq 3C_{2}.

Using (
16
) and above inequality, we obtain

\bigg|\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}[\tau_{j}^{0},s,s]\ell_{*}(.,t)x(t)~dt\bigg|\leq\left(C_{2}+3C_{2}+C_{2}\right)\norm{x}_{\infty}=5C_{2}\norm{x}_{\infty}.

Therefore, using (
15
) and above estimate in (
14
) we have

\left|[\tau_{j}^{0},s,s](K^{\prime}(\varphi)x)\right|\leq\left(C_{2}h+5C_{2}\right)\norm{x}_{\infty}\leq 6C_{2}\norm{x}_{\infty}.

(17)

Case (II)

Whenever
s=\tau_{j}^{0}
.

\displaystyle[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{0}](K^{\prime}(\varphi)x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(K^{\prime}(\varphi)x)^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\tau_{j}^{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial\ell_{1,*}}{\partial s}(\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{0})-\frac{\partial\ell_{2,*}}{\partial s}(\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{0})\right)x(\tau_{j}^{0})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\left[\int_{t_{j}-1}^{\tau_{j}^{0}}\frac{\partial^{2}\ell_{1,*}}{\partial s^{2}}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)x(t)~dt+\int_{\tau_{j}^{0}}^{t_{j}}\frac{\partial^{2}\ell_{2,*}}{\partial s^{2}}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)x(t)~dt\right].

Hence,

\left|[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{0}]K^{\prime}(\varphi)x\right|\leq 2C_{2}\norm{x}_{\infty}.

(18)

Case (III)

When
\tau_{j}^{0}<s\leq t_{j}
. Similarly as Case (I), we have

\left|[\tau_{j}^{0},s,s](K^{\prime}(\varphi)x)\right|\leq\left(C_{2}h+5C_{2}\right)\norm{x}_{\infty}\leq 6C_{2}\norm{x}_{\infty}.

From (
17
), (
18
) and above inequality, it follows that

\sup_{s\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}]}\left|[\tau_{j}^{0},s,s](K^{\prime}(\varphi)x)\right|\leq 6C_{2}\norm{x}_{\infty},

(19)

which proves the required estimate for the case
r=0
. Now for
r=1
,

\displaystyle[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},s,s](K^{\prime}(\varphi)x)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{[\tau_{j}^{0},s,s](K^{\prime}(\varphi)x)-[\tau_{j}^{1},s,s](K^{\prime}(\varphi)x)}{\tau_{j}^{0}-\tau_{j}^{1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h(\zeta_{0}-\zeta_{1})}\bigg[[\tau_{j}^{0},s,s](K^{\prime}(\varphi)x)-[\tau_{j}^{1},s,s](K^{\prime}(\varphi)x)\bigg],

which gives

\left|[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},s,s](K^{\prime}(\varphi)x)\right|\leq\frac{1}{h|\zeta_{0}-\zeta_{1}|}\bigg[\left|[\tau_{j}^{0},s,s](K^{\prime}(\varphi)x)\right|+\left|[\tau_{j}^{1},s,s](K^{\prime}(\varphi)x)\right|\bigg].

Hence, it follows from (
19
) that

\sup_{s\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}]}\left|[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},s,s](K^{\prime}(\varphi)x)\right|\leq\frac{12C_{2}}{h|\zeta_{0}-\zeta_{1}|}\norm{x}_{\infty}=C_{7}\norm{x}_{\infty}\frac{1}{h},

where
C_{7}=\frac{12C_{2}}{|\zeta_{0}-\zeta_{1}|}
is some constant. By mathematical induction, it follows that

\sup_{s\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}]}\left|[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\dots,\tau_{j}^{2r},s,s](K^{\prime}(\varphi)x)\right|\leq C_{8}\norm{x}_{\infty}\frac{1}{h^{2r}},

where
C_{8}
is some constant.
∎

3
Iteration methods of approximation

We see that
K
is a Urysohn integral operator with Green’s function type kernel and
Q_{n}
is the interpolatory operator at
2r+1
interpolating points defined by (
6
). In the collocation method, the main equation (
2
) is approximated by

\varphi_{n}^{C}-Q_{n}K(\varphi_{n}^{C})=Q_{n}f.

(20)

The iterated collocation solution is defined by

\varphi_{n}^{S}=K(\varphi_{n}^{S})+f.

(21)

In Grammont-Kulkarni
[
6
]
, the following modified collocation method is proposed:

\varphi_{n}^{M}-K_{n}^{M}(\varphi_{n}^{M})=f,

(22)

where

K_{n}^{M}(x)=Q_{n}K(x)+K(Q_{n}x)-Q_{n}K(Q_{n}x).

The iterated modified collocation solution is defined as

\widetilde{\varphi}_{n}^{M}=K(\varphi_{n}^{M})+f.

(23)

In this section, we present some preliminary results using conventional methodologies and the previously mentioned lemmas. These results significantly contribute to our main result.

Proposition 3
.

If
x\in C^{2r+2}[0,1]
, then

\displaystyle\norm{K'(\varphi)(I-Q_n)x }_{\infty}\leq 2C_{9}\norm{x}_{2r+2,\infty}h^{2r+2},

where
C_{9}
is a constant independent of
h
.

Proof.

Let
s\in[0,1]
. Then we have

\displaystyle K^{\prime}(\varphi)(I-Q_{n})x(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\ell_{*}(s,t)(I-Q_{n})x(t)~dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\ell_{*}(s,t)(I-Q_{n,j})x(t)~dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\ell_{*}(s,t)\Psi_{j}(t)\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\dots,\tau_{j}^{2r},t\right]x~dt,

where

\Psi_{j}(t)=\prod_{i=0}^{2r}(t-\tau_{j}^{i}),\quad\text{for}~j=1,2,\dots,n.

Let
s\in[t_{i-1},t_{i}]\subset[0,1]
, for some
1\leq i\leq n
. Then we write

K^{\prime}(\varphi)(I-Q_{n})x(s)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\ell_{*}(s,t)\Psi_{j}(t)\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\dots,\tau_{j}^{2r},t\right]x~dt+\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}\ell_{*}(s,t)\Psi_{i}(t)\left[\tau_{i}^{0},\tau_{i}^{1},\dots,\tau_{i}^{2r},t\right]x~dt.

(24)

For fix
s
, let

\displaystyle f_{j}^{s}(t)=\ell_{*}(s,t)\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\dots,\tau_{j}^{2r},t\right]x,\quad t\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}],~\text{for}~j=1,2,\dots,n.

That is

f_{j}^{s}(t)~=~\begin{cases}\ell_{1,*}(s,t)\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\dots,\tau_{j}^{2r},t\right]x,~~~&j\leq i,\\
\ell_{2,*}(s,t)\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\dots,\tau_{j}^{2r},t\right]x,~~~&j\geq i.\\
\end{cases}

Note that the function
f_{j}^{s}
is continuous on
[t_{j-1},t_{j}]
and differentiable on
(t_{j-1},t_{j})
for
j\neq i
. Therefore by Taylor series expansion of
f_{j}^{s}
about the point
s_{j}=\frac{t_{j-1}+t_{j}}{2}
, there exist a point
\zeta_{j1}\in(t_{j-1},t_{j})
such that

(f_{j}^{s})(t)=f_{j}^{s}\left(s_{j}\right)+\left(t-s_{j}\right)(f_{j}^{s})^{\prime}(\zeta_{j1}),

where

(f_{j}^{s})^{\prime}(\zeta_{j1})~=~\begin{cases}\ell_{1,*}(s,\zeta_{j1})\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\dots,\tau_{j}^{2r},\zeta_{j1},\zeta_{j1}\right]x+D^{(0,1)}\ell_{1,*}(s,\zeta_{j1})\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\dots,\tau_{j}^{2r},\zeta_{j1}\right]x,~~~&j\leq i,\\
\ell_{2,*}(s,\zeta_{j1})\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\dots,\tau_{j}^{2r},\zeta_{j1},\zeta_{j1}\right]x+D^{(0,1)}\ell_{2,*}(s,\zeta_{j1})\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\dots,\tau_{j}^{2r},\zeta_{j1}\right]x,~~~&j\geq i.\\
\end{cases}

Let

C_{9}=\max_{k=0,1,2}\bigg\{\sup_{0\leq t\leq s\leq 1}\left|D^{(0,k)}\ell_{1,*}(s,t)\right|,\sup_{0\leq s\leq t\leq 1}\left|D^{(0,k)}\ell_{2,*}(s,t)\right|\bigg\}.

Then

\left|(f_{j}^{s})^{\prime}(\zeta_{j1})\right|\leq 2C_{9}\norm{x}_{2r+2,\infty}.

Now, we write

\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\ell_{*}(s,t)\Psi_{j}(t)\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\dots,\tau_{j}^{2r},t\right]x~dt=\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\left(t-s_{j}\right)(f_{j}^{s})^{\prime}(\zeta_{j1})\Psi_{j}(t)~dt.

It follows that

\left|\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\ell_{*}(s,t)\Psi_{j}(t)\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\dots,\tau_{j}^{2r},t\right]x~dt\right|\leq 2C_{10}\norm{x}_{2r+2,\infty}h^{2r+3}.

Since
\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}\Psi_{i}(t)~dt=0
,

\displaystyle\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}\ell_{*}(s,t)\Psi_{i}(t)\left[\tau_{i}^{0},\tau_{i}^{1},\dots,\tau_{i}^{2r},t\right]x~dt

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}(f_{i}^{s})(t)\Psi_{i}(t)~dt,~s\in[t_{i-1},t_{i}]

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}[(f_{i}^{s})(t)-(f_{i}^{s})(s)]\Psi_{i}(t)~dt.

Since the function
f_{i}^{s}
is continuous on
[t_{i-1},t_{i}]
and differentiable on
(t_{i-1},t_{i})
, by mean value theorem, we have

(f_{i}^{s})(t)-(f_{i}^{s})(s)=(t-s)(f_{i}^{s})^{\prime}(\zeta_{i2}),

for some
\zeta_{i2}\in(t_{i-1},t_{i})
. Then

\left|\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}\ell_{*}(s,t)\Psi_{i}(t)\left[\tau_{i}^{0},\tau_{i}^{1},\dots,\tau_{i}^{2r},t\right]x~dt\right|\leq 2C_{10}\norm{x}_{2r+2,\infty}h^{2r+3}.

By (
24
),

\displaystyle\left|K^{\prime}(\varphi)(I-Q_{n})x(s)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}\left|\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\ell_{*}(s,t)\left([\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\dots,\tau_{j}^{2r},t]x\right)\Psi_{j}(t)~dt\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\left|\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}\ell_{*}(s,t)\left([\tau_{i}^{0},\tau_{i}^{1},\dots,\tau_{i}^{2r},t]x\right)\Psi_{i}(t)~dt\right|.

Hence

\norm{K'(\varphi)(I-Q_n)x(s)}_{\infty}\leq 2C_{9}\norm{x}_{2r+2,\infty}h^{2r+2},

which proves the result.
∎

Proposition 4
.

If
x\in C^{2r+1}[0,1]
, then

\displaystyle\norm{K'(\varphi)(I-Q_n)K'(\varphi)(I-Q_n)x }_{\infty}\leq C_{11}\norm{x}_{2r+1,\infty}h^{2r+3},

where
C_{11}
is a constant independent of
h
.

Proof.

For any
s\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle K^{\prime}(\varphi)(I-Q_{n})K^{\prime}(\varphi)(I-Q_{n})x(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\ell_{*}(s,t)(I-Q_{n})K^{\prime}(\varphi)(I-Q_{n})x(t)~dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\ell_{*}(s,t)(I-Q_{n,j})K^{\prime}(\varphi)(I-Q_{n})x(t)~dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}g_{j}^{s}(t)\Psi_{j}(t)~dt,

(25)

where

\displaystyle g_{j}^{s}(t)=\ell_{*}(s,t)\left(\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\dots,\tau_{j}^{2r},t\right]K^{\prime}(\varphi)(I-Q_{n})x\right),~t\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}],~\text{for}~j=1,2,\dots,n.

Let
s\in[t_{i-1},t_{i}]\subset[0,1]
, for some
1\leq i\leq n
.
Note that

g_{j}^{s}(t)~=~\begin{cases}\ell_{1,*}(s,t)\left(\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\dots,\tau_{j}^{2r},t\right]K^{\prime}(\varphi)(I-Q_{n})x\right),~~~&j\leq i,\\
\ell_{2,*}(s,t)\left(\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\dots,\tau_{j}^{2r},t\right]K^{\prime}(\varphi)(I-Q_{n})x\right),~~~&j\geq i.\\
\end{cases}

Denote

\left(\delta_{j}^{2r}K^{\prime}(\varphi)(I-Q_{n})x\right)[t]=\left(\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\dots,\tau_{j}^{2r},t\right]K^{\prime}(\varphi)(I-Q_{n})x\right),

and

\left(\delta_{j}^{2r}K^{\prime}(\varphi)(I-Q_{n})x\right)[t,t]=\left(\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\dots,\tau_{j}^{2r},t,t\right]K^{\prime}(\varphi)(I-Q_{n})x\right).

Note that the function
g_{j}^{s}
is continuous on
[t_{j-1},t_{j}]
and differentiable on
(t_{j-1},t_{j})
for
j\neq i
. Then it follows that

(g_{j}^{s})^{\prime}(t)~=~\begin{cases}\ell_{1,*}(s,t)\left(\delta_{j}^{2r}K^{\prime}(\varphi)(I-Q_{n})x\right)[t,t]+\left(\delta_{j}^{2r}K^{\prime}(\varphi)(I-Q_{n})x\right)[t]~D^{(0,1)}\ell_{1,*}(s,t),~~~&j\leq i,\\
\ell_{2,*}(s,t)\left(\delta_{j}^{2r}K^{\prime}(\varphi)(I-Q_{n})x\right)[t,t]+\left(\delta_{j}^{2r}K^{\prime}(\varphi)(I-Q_{n})x\right)[t]~D^{(0,1)}\ell_{2,*}(s,t),~~~&j\geq i.\\
\end{cases}

Therefore, by Lemma
1
, Lemma
2
and estimate (
7
), we have

\left|(g_{j}^{s})^{\prime}(t)\right|\leq C_{9}C_{10}\norm{(I-Q_n)x}_{\infty}h^{-2r}\leq C_{3}C_{9}C_{10}\norm{x^{(2r + 1)}}_{\infty}h.

From (
25
), we write

\displaystyle K^{\prime}(\varphi)(I-Q_{n})K^{\prime}(\varphi)(I-Q_{n})x(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}g_{j}^{s}(t)\Psi_{j}(t)~dt+\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}g_{i}^{s}(t)\Psi_{i}(t)~dt

By employing similar argument as presented in the proof of Proposition
3
, we acquire

\left|K^{\prime}(\varphi)(I-Q_{n})K^{\prime}(\varphi)(I-Q_{n})x(s)\right|\leq 2C_{3}C_{9}C_{10}\norm{x^{(2r + 1)}}_{\infty}h^{2r+3}.

This proves the proposition.
∎

Proposition 5
.

If
x\in C[0,1]
, then

\displaystyle\norm{K'(\varphi)(I-Q_n)K'(\varphi)x }_{\infty}\leq C_{12}\norm{x}_{\infty}h^{2},

where
C_{12}
is a constant independent of
h
.
Further,

\displaystyle\norm{K'(\varphi)(I-Q_n)K'(\varphi) }=O(h^{2}).

Proof.

Let
s\in[0,1]
. Then

\displaystyle K^{\prime}(\varphi)(I-Q_{n})K^{\prime}(\varphi)x(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\ell_{*}(s,t)(I-Q_{n})K^{\prime}(\varphi)x(t)~dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\ell_{*}(s,t)(I-Q_{n,j})K^{\prime}(\varphi)x(t)~dt.

Since
x\in C[0,1]
, then
K^{\prime}(\varphi)x\in C^{2}[0,1]
, and therefore

\displaystyle|K^{\prime}(\varphi)(I-Q_{n})K^{\prime}(\varphi)x(s)|

\displaystyle\leq\norm{\ell_*}_{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\norm{(I-Q_{n,j})K'(\varphi)x}_{\infty}~dt

\displaystyle\leq\norm{\ell_*}_{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}C_{3}\norm{(K'(\varphi)x)^{(2)}}_{\infty}h^{2}~dt.

From the estimate (
3
), we have

\displaystyle|K^{\prime}(\varphi)(I-Q_{n})K^{\prime}(\varphi)x(s)|

\displaystyle\leq\norm{\ell_*}_{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}C_{1}C_{3}\norm{x}_{\infty}h^{2}~dt=C_{1}C_{3}\norm{\ell_*}_{\infty}\norm{x}_{\infty}h^{2}.

This proves the required result, where
C_{12}=C_{1}C_{3}\norm{\ell_*}_{\infty}<\infty
. Thus,

\norm{K'(\varphi)(I-Q_n)K'(\varphi)}=\sup_{\norm{x}_{\infty}\leq 1}\left\{\sup_{s\in[0,1]}|K^{\prime}(\varphi)(I-Q_{n})K^{\prime}(\varphi)x(s)|\right\}=O\left(h^{2}\right),

which completes the proof.
∎

3.2
Modified collocation method

We begin by establishing the following key result, which plays a crucial role in proving Theorem
9
, the main theorem of this section. Henceforth, we assume that
n_{0}
is a positive integer such that for all
n\geq n_{0}
, it follows that
Q_{n}\varphi\in\mathcal{B}(\varphi,\delta_{0})
.

Lemma 8
.

Let
r\geq 1
and
f\in C^{2r+1}[0,1]
. Then

\displaystyle\norm{(I-Q_n) \big[K(Q_n \varphi)-K(\varphi)-K'(\varphi)(Q_n \varphi-\varphi)\big]}_{\infty}=O\left(h^{4r+3}\right).

Proof.

The generalized Taylor’s theorem gives

\displaystyle K(Q_{n}\varphi)-K(\varphi)-K^{\prime}(\varphi)(Q_{n}\varphi-\varphi)=\frac{1}{2}K^{\prime\prime}(\varphi)(Q_{n}\varphi-\varphi)^{2}+R(Q_{n}\varphi-\varphi),

with

R(Q_{n}\varphi-\varphi)(s)=\int_{0}^{1}\left[K^{(3)}(\varphi+\theta(Q_{n}\varphi-\varphi))(Q_{n}\varphi-\varphi)^{3}\right](s)\dfrac{(1-\theta)^{2}}{2}~d\theta,\;\;s\in[0,1].

(26)

Recall that
\dfrac{\partial^{3}\kappa}{\partial u^{3}}
is continuous on
\Omega
. Then the operator
K
is three times Fréchet differentiable and the third Fréchet derivative of
K
is given by,

\left[K^{(3)}(\varphi+\theta(Q_{n}\varphi-\varphi))(Q_{n}\varphi-\varphi)^{3}\right](s)=\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\partial^{3}\kappa}{\partial u^{3}}(s,t,\varphi(t)+\theta(Q_{n}\varphi-\varphi)(t))(Q_{n}\varphi-\varphi)^{3}(t)~dt.

Then

\left|\left[K^{(3)}(\varphi+\theta(Q_{n}\varphi-\varphi))(Q_{n}\varphi-\varphi)^{3}\right](s)\right|\leq\left(\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}s,t\in[0,1]\\
|u|\leq\norm{\varphi}_{\infty}+\delta_{0}\end{subarray}}\left|\dfrac{\partial^{3}\kappa}{\partial u^{3}}(s,t,u)\right|\right)\norm{Q_n \varphi-\varphi}_{\infty}^{3}.

Thus,

\norm{\left[K^{(3)}(\varphi+ \theta(Q_n \varphi-\varphi))(Q_n \varphi-\varphi)^3\right]}_{\infty}\leq C_{1}\norm{Q_n \varphi-\varphi}_{\infty}^{3}.

Therefore, equation (
26
) gives

\norm{ R(Q_n \varphi-\varphi)}_{\infty}\leq\dfrac{C_{1}}{6}\norm{Q_n \varphi-\varphi}_{\infty}^{3}.

(27)

Note that

(I-Q_{n})[K(Q_{n}\varphi)-K(\varphi)-K^{\prime}(\varphi)(Q_{n}\varphi-\varphi)]=\frac{1}{2}(I-Q_{n})K^{\prime\prime}(\varphi)(Q_{n}\varphi-\varphi)^{2}+(I-Q_{n})R(Q_{n}\varphi-\varphi).

(28)

Since
f\in C^{2r+1}[0,1]
,
\varphi\in C^{2r+1}[0,1]
. Thus
\norm{Q_n \varphi-\varphi}_{\infty}=O\left(h^{2r+1}\right)
. As
Q_{n}\varphi-\varphi\in C[0,1]
, therefore
K^{\prime\prime}(\varphi)(Q_{n}\varphi-\varphi)^{2}\in C^{2}[0,1]
. Hence,

\displaystyle\norm{(I-Q_n) K''(\varphi)(Q_n \varphi-\varphi)^2}_{\infty}\leq C_{3}\norm{( K''(\varphi) \left(Q_n \varphi-\varphi)^2\right)'}_{\infty}h.

From (
4
), we have

\displaystyle\norm{( K''(\varphi)\left(Q_n \varphi-\varphi)^2\right)'}_{\infty}\leq C_{1}\norm{Q_n \varphi-\varphi}_{\infty}^{2}.

Therefore,

\norm{(I-Q_n) K''(\varphi)(Q_n \varphi-\varphi)^2}_{\infty}=O\left(h^{4r+3}\right).

On the other hand, since
\left\{Q_{n}\right\}
is uniformly bounded and using (
27
), we obtain

\norm{(I-Q_n)R(Q_n \varphi-\varphi)}_{\infty}=O\left(h^{6r+3}\right).

Hence, by employing the above two inequalities in (
28
), the required estimate as

\norm{(I-Q_n) \big[K(Q_n \varphi)-K(\varphi)-K'(\varphi)(Q_n \varphi-\varphi)\big]}_{\infty}=O\left(h^{4r+3}\right).

∎

Theorem 9
.

Let
K
be a Urysohn integral operator defined as (
1
) with Green’s function type kernel and
f\in C^{2r+2}[0,1]
be a given function. Consider the equation (
2
) with
\varphi
as the unique solution in
\mathcal{B}(\varphi,\delta_{0})
. Let
Q_{n}
be the interpolatory operator onto the approximating space
\mathcal{X}_{n}
defined by (
6
). Let
\varphi_{n}^{M}\in\mathcal{B}(\varphi,\delta_{0})
be the modified collocation solution of (

). Then

\displaystyle\norm{\varphi- \varphi_n^{M}}_{\infty}=\begin{cases}O\left(h^{3}\right),~~&r=0,\\
O\left(h^{2r+2}\right),~~&r\geq 1.\end{cases}

Proof.

Case I: When
r=0
. See
[
10
]
.

Case II: When
r\geq 1
.

We write the following estimate from Grammont et al
[
8
]
:

\displaystyle\norm{\varphi- \varphi_n^{M}}_{\infty}\leq 4\norm{(I-K'(\varphi))^{-1}}\norm{[K(\varphi) - K_n^{M}(\varphi)]}_{\infty}.

(29)

Further consider,

\displaystyle K(\varphi)-K_{n}^{M}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=(I-Q_{n})(K(\varphi)-K(Q_{n}\varphi))

\displaystyle=-(I-Q_{n})[K(Q_{n}\varphi)-K(\varphi)-K^{\prime}(\varphi)(Q_{n}\varphi-\varphi)]+(I-Q_{n})K^{\prime}(\varphi)(I-Q_{n})\varphi.

Now,

\displaystyle\norm{(I-Q_n)K'(\varphi)(I-Q_n)\varphi}_{\infty}\leq\norm{I-Q_n}\norm{K'(\varphi)(I-Q_n)\varphi}_{\infty}.

Uniformly boundedness

of and Proposition

implies

\displaystyle\norm{(I-Q_n)K'(\varphi)(I-Q_n)\varphi}_{\infty}=O\left(h^{2r+2}\right).

Applying above estimate and Lemma
8
in equation (

), we obtain

\displaystyle\norm{ K(\varphi)-K_n^{M}(\varphi)}_{\infty}=O\left(h^{2r+2}\right).

Using the above estimate in (

), we obtained the desired result as

\displaystyle\norm{\varphi- \varphi_n^{M}}_{\infty}=O\left(h^{2r+2}\right).

This proves the theorem. ∎

3.3
Iterated modified collocation method

In this subsection, we establish our primary result. We demonstrate that the convergence rate in the iterated modified collocation method is higher than the modified collocation methods. We proceed to prove a series of intermediate results essential for the proof of our main theorem.

Lemma 11
.

Let
r\geq 1
and
f\in C^{2r+1}[0,1]
, then

\displaystyle\norm{K'(\varphi)(I-Q_n) \big[K(Q_n \varphi)-K(\varphi)-K'(\varphi)(Q_n \varphi-\varphi)\big]}_{\infty}=O\left(h^{4r+4}\right).

Proof.

Let
n_{0}
be a positive integer such that
n\geq n_{0}
implies that

Q_{n}\varphi\in\mathcal{B}(\varphi,\delta_{0}).
Then by generalized Taylor’s theorem,

K(Q_{n}\varphi)-K(\varphi)-K^{\prime}(\varphi)(Q_{n}\varphi-\varphi)=\frac{1}{2}K^{\prime\prime}(\varphi)(Q_{n}\varphi-\varphi)^{2}+\frac{1}{3!}K^{(3)}(\varphi)(Q_{n}\varphi-\varphi)^{3}+R(Q_{n}\varphi-\varphi),

where

R(Q_{n}\varphi-\varphi)(s)=\int_{0}^{1}\left[K^{(4)}(\varphi+\theta(Q_{n}\varphi-\varphi))(Q_{n}\varphi-\varphi)^{4}\right](s)\dfrac{(1-\theta)^{3}}{6}~d\theta,~s\in[0,1].

(31)

Now

\left[K^{(4)}(\varphi+\theta(Q_{n}\varphi-\varphi))(Q_{n}\varphi-\varphi)^{4}\right](s)=\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\partial^{4}\kappa}{\partial u^{4}}\left(s,t,\varphi(t)+\theta(Q_{n}\varphi-\varphi)(t)\right)(Q_{n}\varphi-\varphi)^{4}(t)~dt.

It follows that

\left|\left[K^{(4)}(\varphi+\theta(Q_{n}\varphi-\varphi))(Q_{n}\varphi-\varphi)^{4}\right](s)\right|\leq\left(\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}s,t\in[0,1]\\
|u|\leq\norm{\varphi}_{\infty}+\delta_{0}\end{subarray}}\left|\dfrac{\partial^{4}\kappa}{\partial u^{4}}(s,t,u)\right|\right)\norm{Q_n \varphi-\varphi}_{\infty}^{4}.

Thus,
\displaystyle{\norm{\left[K^{(4)}(\varphi+ \theta(Q_n \varphi-\varphi))(Q_n \varphi-\varphi)^4\right]}_{\infty}\leq C_{1}\norm{Q_n \varphi-\varphi}_{\infty}^{4}}
and, therefore the equation (
31
) gives

\norm{ R(Q_n \varphi-\varphi)}_{\infty}\leq\dfrac{C_{1}}{24}\norm{Q_n \varphi-\varphi}_{\infty}^{4}.

Note that

\displaystyle K^{\prime}(\varphi)(I-Q_{n})[K(Q_{n}\varphi)-K(\varphi)-K^{\prime}(\varphi)(Q_{n}\varphi-\varphi)]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}K^{\prime}(\varphi)(I-Q_{n})K^{\prime\prime}(\varphi)(Q_{n}\varphi-\varphi)^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{6}K^{\prime}(\varphi)(I-Q_{n})K^{(3)}(\varphi)(Q_{n}\varphi-\varphi)^{3}

\displaystyle+K^{\prime}(\varphi)(I-Q_{n})R(Q_{n}\varphi-\varphi).

(32)

Now, from the Remark
6
and the equation (
7
), we obtain

\norm{K'(\varphi)(I-Q_n) K''(\varphi)(Q_n \varphi-\varphi)^2}_{\infty}\leq\norm{K'(\varphi)(I-Q_n) K''(\varphi)}\norm{Q_n \varphi-\varphi}_{\infty}^{2}=O\left(h^{4r+4}\right),

(33)

\displaystyle\norm{K'(\varphi)(I-Q_n) K^{(3)}(\varphi)(Q_n \varphi-\varphi)^3}_{\infty}\leq\norm{K'(\varphi)(I-Q_n)K^{(3)}(\varphi)}\norm{Q_n \varphi-\varphi}_{\infty}^{3}=O\left(h^{6r+5}\right).

(34)

Also,

\norm{K'(\varphi)(I-Q_n)R(Q_n \varphi-\varphi)}_{\infty}\leq\norm{K'(\varphi)}\norm{I-Q_n}\norm{R(Q_n \varphi-\varphi)}_{\infty}\leq C_{14}\norm{R(Q_n \varphi-\varphi)}_{\infty}=O\left(h^{8r+4}\right),

for some constant
C_{14}
.
Hence, using (
33
), (
34
) and the above estimate in (
32
), it follows that

\norm{K'(\varphi)(I-Q_n) \big[K(Q_n \varphi)-K(\varphi)-K'(\varphi)(Q_n \varphi-\varphi)\big]}_{\infty}=O\left(h^{4r+4}\right).

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 12
.

Let

and

\displaystyle K(\varphi)-K_{n}^{M}(\varphi)

\displaystyle\norm{K'(\varphi) \big[K(\varphi)-K_n^{M}(\varphi)\big]}_{\infty}

5
Conclusion

We study the collocation method and its variants for the approximate solutions of Urysohn integral equations with smooth and Green’s function-type kernels. The projection used is an interpolatory projection at
2r+1
collocation points defined on each subinterval with respect to a partition of
[0,1]
. Previous studies have shown that selecting Gauss points (i.e., the zeros of Legendre polynomials) as collocation points enhances the convergence rates of iterative methods. Usually, if the collocation points are not Gauss points (or not zeros of special functions), the solutions
\varphi_{n}^{C}
(collocation solution),
\varphi_{n}^{S}
(the iterated collocation solution),
\varphi_{n}^{m}
(modified collocation solution) and
\widetilde{\varphi}_{n}^{M}
(iterated modified collocation solution) exhibit the same orders of convergence, regardless of whether the kernel is smooth or of Green’s function type.

For example, consider
\mathcal{X}=L^{\infty}[0,1]
and
\mathcal{X}_{n}
be the space of piecewise constant functions on the uniform partition of
[0,1]
, given by
\{0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{n}=1\}
, where
t_{j}=\frac{j}{n}
for
j=0,1,\ldots,n
. Let
Q_{n}:C[0,1]\mapsto\mathcal{X}_{n}
be the interpolatory map at collocation the points
\tau_{j}=\frac{3j-2}{3n}
, for
j=1,\ldots,n
. Using the Hahn-Banach extension theorem, the domain of definition of
Q_{n}
can be extended to
L^{\infty}[0,1]
and so
Q_{n}
is a projection. Consider a nonlinear integral equation as

x-K(x)=f
, where
f(s)=s
,
s\in[0,1]
, and

K(x)(s)=\left(\int_{0}^{1}x(t)~dt+1\right)s,\quad s\in[0,1].

We have
\varphi(s)=4s
for
s\in[0,1]
as the exact solution of this equation. It is evident that the operator
K
is compact and Fréchet differentiable, with

\left(K^{\prime}(\varphi)v\right)(s)=\left(\int_{0}^{1}v(t)~dt\right)s,\quad\text{ for all }v\in\mathcal{X},\;s\in[0,1].

Note that
1
is not an eigenvalue of
K^{\prime}(\varphi)
.
Observe that

(I-Q_{n})\varphi(s)=4s-\frac{4(3j-2)}{3n},\quad s\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}],

and

\int_{0}^{1}(I-Q_{n})\varphi(t)~dt=\sum_{j=1}^{n}~\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\left(4t-\frac{4(3j-2)}{3n}\right)~dt=\frac{2}{3n}.

We get

\norm{K'(\varphi)(I -Q_n) \varphi}_{\infty}=\max_{s\in[0,1]}\left|\left(\int_{0}^{1}(I-Q_{n})\varphi(t)~dt\right)s\right|=\frac{2}{3n},

and

\norm{(I - Q_n ) \varphi}_{\infty}=\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\left\{\max_{s\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}]}|(I-Q_{n})\varphi(s)|\right\}\leq\frac{8}{3n}.

In general, the iterated collocation solution ia superconvergent (see
[
15
]
) if and only if

\frac{\norm{K'(\varphi)(I -Q_n) \varphi}_{\infty}}{\norm{(I - Q_n ) \varphi}_{\infty}}\to 0\;\text{as}\;n\to\infty.

In this case, the above condition is not satisfied, because

\frac{\norm{K'(\varphi)(I -Q_n) \varphi}_{\infty}}{\norm{(I - Q_n ) \varphi}_{\infty}}\geq\frac{1}{4}.

In this paper, we consider a sequence of interpolatory projections at
2r+1
equidistant collocation points (not necessarily Gauss points) onto the space of piecewise polynomials of even degree
\leq 2r
with respect to a uniform partition
\{0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{n}=1\}
, where
t_{j}=\frac{j}{n}=jh
. The key findings are as follows:

For smooth kernels, the orders of convergence for the collocation, iterated collocation, modified collocation, and iterated modified collocation methods are
h^{2r+1}
,
h^{2r+2}
,
h^{4r+3}
and
h^{4r+4}
, respectively. In contrast, for Green’s function-type kernels, the respective convergence orders are
h^{2r+1}
,
h^{2r+2}
,
h^{2r+2}
and
h^{2r+3}
, respectively. These findings clearly demonstrate that the iterated modified collocation method achieves the fastest convergence, as the sequence
\{\widetilde{\varphi}_{n}^{M}\}
approaches the exact solution
\varphi
more rapidly than the other approximations, verified by the numerical results.
In future, we want to employ these methods to integral equations with singular or weakly singular kernels, developing asymptotic series expansions for iterated solutions, and generalizing the theoretical framework and numerical schemes to higher-dimensional problems such as boundary integral equations.

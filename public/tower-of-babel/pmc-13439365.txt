A time-delayed mathematical framework for analyzing pertussis transmission dynamics
Authors: Ali Raza, Marek Lampart, Eugenio M. Rocha, Wojciech Sumelka, Emad Fadhal, Wafa F. Alfwzan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Pertussis remains one of the most important public health challenges worldwide, with 5,436 cases reported in the United States in 2023, representing an 8% increase from the previous year, and up to 18,000 cases reported globally in recent outbreaks. This study is important because delayed epidemiological processes, including waning immunity and delayed behavioral responses, may help explain recurrent pertussis persistence despite vaccination, but their combined role is not fully captured in many classical instantaneous models. This paper examines the delayed transmission dynamics of pertussis and analyzes the epidemiological impact of time delays associated with vaccination, waning immunity, and delayed behavioral responses within a deterministic modeling framework. We present a deterministic, time-delayed compartmental model that divides the population into susceptible, infected, vaccinated, recovered, waned immunity, and secondary susceptible classes. Parameters representing temporary mitigation effects and behavioral responses are incorporated into the model. We establish basic mathematical properties, including positivity and boundedness of solutions. The disease-free and endemic equilibria are derived and analyzed using the Routh–Hurwitz criterion and Lyapunov stability techniques to assess their local and global stability with respect to the basic reproduction number. Numerical simulations are performed to confirm the analytical results and to illustrate the dynamics of each subpopulation under different scenarios. The model shows that the basic reproduction number determines the transition from pertussis elimination to persistence. Time delays significantly affect system stability at certain thresholds, leading to oscillatory or damped epidemic patterns. Simulation results indicate that delay, in combination with vaccination, reduces infection prevalence and can shift the system toward disease eradication when parameters are appropriately chosen. The proposed time-delayed mathematical framework provides insight into how vaccination, waning immunity, and delayed behavioral responses interact in shaping pertussis dynamics. The results highlight the influence of delay mechanisms on transmission dynamics and epidemic persistence, and provide a mathematical basis for improving public health strategies targeting pertussis transmission.

Introduction

Bordetella pertussis causes Pertussis (whooping cough), which is a major public health problem in the world even though there are effective vaccines against it. The illness is mainly seen in children at a young age; however, more cases are also being reported in adolescents and adults due to decreased immunity. Clinically and in terms of general health, Pertussis is a significant threat, especially among infants who are too young to be vaccinated. Severe complications might also include pneumonia, neurological complications, as well as, in uncommon instances death. Also, asymptomatic or mildly symptomatic infections in older age groups can continue the chain of transmission, which is almost impossible to detect during routine surveillance. These aspects render Pa long-standing challenge for global population health systems and underscore the need to enhance our knowledge of its modes of transmission. Mathematical modeling has entered the repertoire of techniques used to study the dynamics of infectious diseases and to assess epidemiological processes that are hard to observe directly. The impact of vaccination, decreased immunity, and reinfection on disease spread is widely analyzed using compartmental models. Mathematical models have provided useful insights into the long-term dynamics of epidemic cycles, vaccination, and immunity in the context of Pertussis. Nevertheless, most classical epidemic models assume instantaneous epidemiological processes. As a matter of fact, there are always delays in the dynamics of infectious diseases. In the case of Pertussis, time delays occur naturally through biology and epidemiology, such as incubation periods, slow diagnosis and reporting, responding to an outbreak, and slow waning or enhancing immunity after infection or vaccination. By introducing these delays into mathematical modeling, a more realistic way to describe epidemic processes is achievable and may account for oscillatory outbreak behavior in pertussis epidemiology.

In this study, an established pertussis disease transmission model is extended and refined through the incorporation of a discrete epidemiological time delay in the transmission model and the study of its effects on positivity, boundedness, basic reproduction number, equilibrium, and dynamics.

Driven by the desire to learn more about the importance of delayed epidemiology in the spread of Pertussis, this paper develops a time-retarded compartmental model that considers vaccination, immunity waning, and secondary susceptibility. It is intended to examine the effects of delays on disease stability, persistence, and the efficacy of possible control measures.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 1 provides a brief introduction to the study. Section 2 presents the formulation of the proposed model. Sections 3 and 4 are devoted to the analysis of the local and global stability of the model equilibria, respectively. Section 5 presents the numerical simulations and their discussion, while Sect. 6 concludes the paper with a summary of the main findings.

Biological interpretation of delay and transmission mechanisms

The time delay \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\tau\:$$\end{document} which appears in the transmission term delivers epidemiologically significant lag effects which do not represent an actual intervention. Pertussis shows multiple mechanisms that create delays between actual events and their recognition through case detection and reporting systems, between actual behavior changes and the rise of new cases, and between vaccination and exposure. The delayed transmission term demonstrates how current infection rates depend on past infection rates because these processes exist. The transmission period becomes more dangerous because physicians need extra time for both diagnosis and reporting, resulting in longer periods during which infected individuals can transmit the disease to others. People exhibit temporary increases in case numbers because they delay their behavioral changes, leading to temporary increases in case numbers and subsequent decreases in infected individuals. The population experiences susceptibility renewal at specific times, which are delayed by immune system effects during waning and boosting periods. The model uses delay mechanisms to demonstrate two patterns that occur during multi-year epidemic cycles in pertussis outbreaks. The delay parameter serves as an epidemiological measure of surveillance efficiency, which is affected by two factors: stronger disease control systems, which decrease detection time, and faster response systems, which deliver immediate results.

However, the delay paramter is a combination of all the lagging phenomena that exist within an epidemiological model. This delay includes those related to incubation, identification, immunity, and response processes. In this regard, the aggregation of delay variables makes it possible to examine the effect of delay variables on the dynamics, but it does not differentiate between them.

Local stability

The roubstness of the stability analysis to the delay parameter is influenced by the type of equilibrium being considered. Specifically, for the disease-free equilibrium, the stability criterion involves the basic reproduction number, which is independent of the delay parameter. However, for the endemic equilibrium, the delay term enters the characteristic equation, and changes in the delay parameter may affect the stability of the system, leading to stability switching and oscillations in specific intervals of the delay parameter. Numerical results have been conducted over various delay parameter ranges, and the findings are consistent with theoretical predictions. Given the epidemiological interpretation of the delay parameter described above, we now analyze how delayed transmission processes influence the local and global stability of pertussis equilibria.

Results and discussion

The numerical simulations serve as an illustration of the analytical findings that have been derived from the stability analysis. More specifically, it is possible to observe that the simulations reflect threshold-type dynamics depending on the basic reproduction number, and show how changes in the value of the delay impact system stability.

This section presents the numerical simulations of the pertussis epidemic model (Eqs. 1–6) using the parameter values listed in Table 1. We examine the system’s behavior both with and without delay. The results show that introducing a delay significantly reduces the number of infected individuals without altering any of the transmission parameters given in Table 1. Over time, the number of infected individuals approaches zero, and the system converges to the pertussis-free equilibrium, indicating that the disease can be effectively controlled. Although complete eradication of a disease in a population is generally unrealistic, achieving a disease-free equilibrium reflects successful control.

Furthermore, the simulations demonstrate that employing an effective delay strategy reduces the basic reproduction number without directly modifying any infection-related parameters of the model. All simulations were performed using MATLAB. In all simulations, variations in the delay parameter were associated with the timing of the epidemiological response rather than with the actual control measures. In all numerical simulations, the initial conditions were chosen as.

Basic reproduction number of Pertussis is extensively reviewed in the literature on epidemiology. In fully susceptible populations, the estimates are usually between 12 and 17, indicating the very contagiousness of the disease. Nevertheless, partial immunity and persistent changes in contact structure have been observed to reduce effective reproduction numbers in the vaccinated populace. As an illustration, the simulation of studies with serological and contact data approximated around \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{R}_{0}\approx\:5.5$$\end{document}. We emphasize that the model does not evaluate specific real-world interventions or optimal control strategies; instead, it provides qualitative and threshold-level insights into the epidemiological consequences of delayed processes. The current model does not include age structure, pathogen evolution, and heterogeneous contact patterns, all of which are known factors that affect pertussis epidemiology. The findings should be understood as qualitative dynamical insights that do not represent the actual observed incidence of the disease. The delay parameter \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\tau\:$$\end{document} is a sum of epidemiological lag effects rather than an individual biological process. PIn practice, surveillance weaknesses for Pertussis can be caused by delayed diagnosis, laboratory confirmation, reporting to surveillance systems, and delayed behavioural response during outbreaks. Delays of several weeks to months in reporting have been reported in previous epidemiological studies between the time of infection and the reporting of cases, especially during outbreak investigations. Hence, the simulation accounts for a range of delays. 0.1–0.5 years (about 1–6 months) to cover likely delays in surveillance and response in behavioral reactions. The alternating process created by the delay mechanism Paligns with the multi-year epidemiological cycles observed in Pertussis. The recurrent outbreaks of Pertussis have been reported even in the most highly vaccinated population, based on several epidemiological studies, and it is possible that this can be explained by waning immunity, delayed behavioral change, and heterogeneous patterns of its spread. Such cyclic outbreak behaviour can be described by a proposed delay framework that provides a theoretical explanation.

Conclusion

This paper introduces a delayed epidemic model of Pertussis, with saturated incidence and several epidemiological compartments, including susceptible, vaccinated, infected, recovered, waned immunity, and secondary susceptible persons. The fundamental reprduction number \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{R}_{0}$$\end{document}was obtained to describe the disease transmission potential, and basic properties of the model, including positivity and boundedness of solutions, were established. The stability analysis indicated that the disease-free equilibrium is stable as long as \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{R}_{0}<1$$\end{document}, with the endemic equilibrium being realized and reaching stability when \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{R}_{0}>1$$\end{document}. The outcomes show that the delay parameter \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\tau\:$$\end{document} is that \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\tau\:\:$$\end{document}is significant in the transmission dynamics, as it affects the effective process of infection. \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{e}^{-\mu\:\tau\:}$$\end{document}. Different values of delay can influence the system’s stability and contribute to the persistence of the disease or an epidemic cycle. The impact of key epidemiological variables, such as transmission, vaccination coverage, and immunity loss, on the progression and maintenance of Pertussis is further demonstrated through numerical simulations. Epidemiologically, the results emphasize the need to curb the spread and ensure efficient vaccination mechanisms to prevent Pertussis. But the current model assumes a homogeneous mixture and uses parameter values based on reasonable assumptions, given the scarcity of empirical data. The model may be further developed to include age structure, booster vaccination policy, asymptomatic transmission, and combination with actual epidemiological data or optimal control strategies to increase its practicality as an instrument for public health decision-making. The present study focuses on the qualitative analysis of pertussis transmission dynamics. Future research could involve calibration of the proposed model using real epidemiological data to investigate specific outbreak scenarios and improve predictive capabilities. Moreover, future work can also investigate other methods of analysis or approximation, such as operational and orthogonal polynomials, which have been effectively used in similar studies in mathematics. These methods can be used to gain further insight into the structure of the analysis and the behavior of the solution in complex delay epidemic models.

SUPPLEMENTARY INFORMATION:

Background

    The MMPA prohibits the ``take'' of marine mammals, with certain 
exceptions. Sections 101(a)(5)(A) and (D) of the MMPA (16 U.S.C. 1361 
et seq.) direct the Secretary of Commerce (as delegated to NMFS) to 
allow, upon request, the incidental, but not intentional, taking of 
small numbers of marine mammals by U.S. citizens who engage in a 
specified activity (other than commercial fishing) within a specified 
geographical region if certain findings are made and either regulations 
are promulgated or, if the taking is limited to harassment, an 
incidental harassment authorization is issued.
    Authorization for incidental takings shall be granted if NMFS finds 
that the taking will have a negligible impact on the species or 
stock(s) and will not have an unmitigable adverse impact on the 
availability of the species or stock(s) for taking for subsistence uses 
(where relevant). Further, NMFS must prescribe the permissible methods 
of taking and other ``means of effecting the least practicable adverse 
impact'' on the affected species or stocks and their habitat, paying 
particular attention to rookeries, mating grounds, and areas of similar 
significance, and on the availability of such species or stocks for 
taking for certain subsistence uses (referred to here as ``mitigation 
measures''). NMFS must also prescribe requirements pertaining to 
monitoring and reporting of such takings. The definition of key terms 
such as ``take,'' ``harassment,'' and ``negligible impact'' can be 
found in the MMPA and NMFS's implementing regulations (see 16 U.S.C. 
1362; 50 CFR 216.103).
    NMFS' regulations implementing the MMPA at 50 CFR 216.107(e) 
indicate that IHAs may be renewed for additional periods of time not to 
exceed 1 year for each reauthorization. In the notice of proposed IHA 
for the initial IHA, NMFS described the circumstances under which we 
would consider issuing a renewal for this activity and requested public 
comment on a potential renewal under those circumstances. Specifically, 
on a case-by-case basis, NMFS may issue a one-time 1-year renewal IHA 
following notice to the public providing an additional 15 days for 
public comments when (1) up to another year of identical, or nearly 
identical, activities as described in the Detailed Description of 
Specified Activities section of the initial IHA issuance notice is 
planned or (2) the activities as described in the Description of the 
Specified Activities and Anticipated Impacts section of the initial IHA 
issuance notice would not be completed by the time the initial IHA 
expires and a renewal would allow for completion of the activities 
beyond that described in the DATES section of the notice of issuance of 
the initial IHA, provided all of the following conditions are met:
     A request for renewal is received no later than 60 days 
prior to the needed renewal IHA effective date (recognizing that the 
renewal IHA expiration date cannot extend beyond 1 year from expiration 
of the initial IHA).
     The request for renewal must include the following:
     An explanation that the activities to be conducted under 
the requested renewal IHA are identical to the activities analyzed 
under the initial IHA, are a subset of the activities, or include 
changes so minor (e.g., reduction in pile size) that the changes do not 
affect the previous analyses, mitigation and monitoring requirements, 
or take estimates (with the exception of reducing the type or amount of 
take).
     A preliminary monitoring report showing the results of the 
required monitoring to date and an explanation showing that the 
monitoring results do not indicate impacts of a scale or nature not 
previously analyzed or authorized.
     Upon review of the request for renewal, the status of the 
affected species or stocks, and any other pertinent information, NMFS 
determines that there are no more than minor changes in the activities, 
the mitigation and monitoring measures will remain the same and 
appropriate, and the findings in the initial IHA remain valid.
    An additional public comment period of 15 days (for a total of 45 
days), with direct notice by email, phone, or postal service to 
commenters on the initial IHA, is provided to allow for any additional 
comments on the proposed renewal. A description of the renewal process 
may be found on our website at: https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/national/marine-mammal-protection/incidental-harassment-authorization-renewals.

Description of the Specified Activities and Anticipated Impacts

    The purpose of this construction project is to remove an existing 
passenger dock and replace it with a new passenger dock to provide safe 
harbor for cruise ships and passengers during the visitor season and 
limited freight and utilities in the off-season at the head of 
Resurrection Bay in Seward, Alaska. As described in detail in the 
notices for the initial IHA (90 FR 34463, July 22, 2025; 90 FR 44047, 
September 11, 2025) and proposed renewal IHA (91 FR 51737, September 8, 
2026), the in-water construction activities include vibratory removal 
of existing H and steel pipe piles, vibratory installation and removal 
of temporary steel pipe piles, vibratory and impact installation of 
permanent steel pipe piles, and down-the-hole drilling (DTH) if 
required for installation of steel pipe piles deep into the bedrock. 
Under the initial IHA, TMC completed the majority of planned 
activities. However, a 1-year renewal of the initial IHA is needed to 
complete installation of a small number of remaining piles. Takes of 
marine mammals by Level A harassment and Level B harassment are 
expected to occur as a result of noise produced by impact and vibratory 
pile driving, vibratory removal, and DTH.

Comments and Responses

    A notice of NMFS' proposal to issue a renewal IHA to TMC was 
published in the Federal Register on September 8, 2026 (91 FR 51737). 
That notice either described, or referenced descriptions of TMC's 
activities, the marine mammal species that may be affected by the 
activity, the anticipated effects on marine mammals and their habitat, 
estimated amount and manner of take, and proposed mitigation, 
monitoring and reporting measures.
    During the 15-day public comment period, NMFS received comments 
from one member of the public. Our responses to relevant substantive 
comments are below. We have not responded to comments that failed to 
raise a significant point for us to consider (e.g., comments that are 
out of scope of the proposed renewal IHA or that call for mitigation, 
monitoring, or reporting measures already included in the proposed 
renewal IHA). Furthermore, if a comment received was unclear, NMFS does 
not include it here as it could not determine whether it raised a 
significant point for NMFS to consider. The comments and 
recommendations are available online at: https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/action/incidental-take-authorization-turnagain-marine-constructions-seward-cruise-ship-passenger. Please see the 
comment submission for full details regarding the recommendations and 
supporting rationale.
    Comment 1: The commenter requests that NMFS publish TMC's 
preliminary monitoring report and extend the public comment period to 
allow meaningful public review,
    Response: TMC's request met NMFS' requirements for a renewal IHA, 
in part, because it included preliminary monitoring data describing all 
observations of species within the areas estimated to be ensonified at 
or above the MMPA harassment thresholds. Therefore, the data referred 
to by the commenter was made available to the public during the comment 
period, and no additional public review is warranted.
    For each species and harassment type, the number of observed 
animals was well below the number of authorized takes, supporting the 
conclusion that no impacts of a scale or nature not previously analyzed 
occurred. Further, the low number of animals observed entering the 
Level A harassment zone indicates successful implementation of required 
mitigation through soft starts and shutdowns that eliminate the 
potential for exposure to noise at or above the MMPA Level A harassment 
thresholds (i.e., no more than the authorized number of takes by Level 
A harassment occurred). TMC is required to submit a full monitoring 
report including all details outlined in IHA measure 6(c) for each 
sighting, which NMFS will make available to the public once finalized 
at: https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/action/incidental-take-authorization-turnagain-marine-constructions-seward-cruise-ship-passenger.
    Comment 2: The commenter requests that NMFS resolve contradictory 
language regarding proposed requirements for use of a bubble-curtain 
and require attenuation for the remaining 48-inch impact driving and 
drilling, unless an equally protective alternative is supported by 
site-specific evidence,
    Response: NMFS agrees with the commenter's recommendations to 
reconcile the bubble-curtain measures identified in the proposed 
renewal IHA FRN and IHA. TMC's planned specified activities included 
installation of 48-in piles for construction of mooring dolphins and a 
trestle. The initial IHA did not require the use of a bubble curtain 
for the installation of 48-inch trestle piles because they would be 
spaced too closely together for use of a bubble curtain to be 
practicable. TMC installed all mooring dolphin piles, for which a 
bubble curtain was required, under the original IHA. Thus, to address 
the commenter's recommendation, NMFS removed the bubble curtain 
requirement in the issued renewal IHA to align with the statement in 
the proposed renewal IHA FRN indicating no bubble curtain is required 
under the renewal IHA.
    Comment 3: The commenter suggests that the renewal IHA should 
include an explicit 48-inch drilling row stating the source level, 
attenuation assumption, shutdown zones and Level B monitoring zone.
    In addition, the commenter requests that NMFS explain why the 
proposed operational shutdown distances adequately address the 
substantially larger modelled auditory-injury distances.
    Response: NMFS agrees with the commenter's suggestion regarding 
mitigation for marine mammals during DTH drilling of 48-in piles and, 
accordingly, included shutdown zones and the Level B harassment zone in 
the bottom row of table 2 of the proposed renewal IHA. However, table 3 
of the proposed renewal IHA FRN mistakenly excluded this row. Thus, to 
address the commenter's suggestion, NMFS added a row to table 3 
specifying the shutdown zones and Level B harassment zone for DTH 
installation of 48-inch piles. The associated source level for this 
activity was included in table 5 of the proposed initial IHA FRN (90 FR 
34463, July 22, 2025).
    For all hearing groups other than HFC, the shutdown zones for this 
activity are smaller than the unattenuated modeled distances to the 
Level A harassment zones, thus TMC and NMFS acknowledge there is some 
potential for take by Level A harassment for certain species during DTH

[[Page 66688]]

installation of 48-in piles. Shutdown zone sizes are limited by the 
cryptic nature and assumed lower detectability of some species (i.e., 
harbor and Dall's porpoise and pinnipeds) or limits to the distances to 
which PSOs can practicably observe. To account for these limitations, 
TMC requested and NMFS is authorizing a small number of takes by Level 
A harassment for each species.
    Comment 4: The commenter requested that NMFS demonstrate that PSOs 
can adequately monitor the shutdown and Level B harassment zones.
    Response: Figure 6 of TMC's Protected Species Monitoring and 
Mitigation Plan, included as an appendix to initial IHA application, 
provides a map depicting PSO deployment locations, all selected by TMC 
to optimize monitoring of the required zones. The initial and renewal 
IHAs include a requirement for monitoring for marine mammals for 30 
minutes before, during, and after pile driving and removal and DTH 
activities and stating that TMC may not begin or resume these 
activities if visibility limits monitoring of these zones in their 
entirety. In addition, PSOs are required to document environmental 
conditions (e.g., cloud cover, estimated observable distance) 
throughout monitoring periods and include these data in the final 
report submitted to NMFS following completion of work under the IHA. 
The commenter recommended that NMFS require TMC to use other monitoring 
methods (e.g., passive acoustic monitoring, vessel-based monitoring) to 
supplement the required visual monitoring. TMC did not propose and NMFS 
did not require additional monitoring methods given practicability 
concerns. Finally, the commenter requested that NMFS offer a 
transparent method for estimating the number of animals present in 
unobserved portions of the Level B harassment zone. The MMPA does not 
require that IHA Holders monitor the entire Level B harassment zone or 
extrapolate the observed number of marine mammals to estimate those 
potentially unobserved. Take estimation for the initial and renewal 
IHAs incorporated the expected occurrence of each species based on the 
best available science within the entire modeled Level B harassment 
zones (truncated by land interference), thus accounting for the likely 
presence of each species. For each species, the number of individuals 
TMC observed within the Level B harassment is substantially less than 
the number of authorized takes.
    Comment 5: The commenter requests that NMFS clarify whether sound 
measurements were collected during the work completed under the initial 
IHA and, if so, replace proxy source levels used for modeling with 
those values collected during acoustic monitoring or, if not, explain 
why and require hydroacoustic verification during the remaining 48-inch 
pile installations.
    Response: NMFS agrees that site-specific hydroacoustic monitoring 
data valuably inform analyses of potential acoustic impacts to marine 
mammals. However, TMC did not propose and NMFS did not require this 
type of monitoring for the initial or renewal IHAs given practicably 
concerns (i.e., budget constraints).
    Comment 6: The commenter suggested that NMFS require specific 
protections for the AT1 stock of transient killer whales and that the 
final IHA specify how stock-level take limits will be enforced.
    Response: TMC sighted only ten killer whales in total throughout 
approximately 200 in-water construction days under the initial IHA. 
Acoustic monitoring of stocks in Resurrection Bay predicts that 1.6 
percent of all killer whales that occur in the project area are part of 
the AT1 transient stock (Yurk et al., 2010). Given the small stock size 
(n=7), low likelihood of encounters, and the limited number of 
construction days under the renewal IHA (n=12), NMFS does not 
anticipate that AT1 transient killer whales would occur in the area 
impacted by TMC's construction activities under the renewal IHA.
    Comment 7: The commenter suggested that NMFS assess overlapping 
Seward construction and other disturbances.
    Response: Under the MMPA, NMFS may authorize the incidental, but 
not intentional, taking of small numbers of marine mammals by U.S. 
citizens who engage in a specified activity (other than commercial 
fishing) within a specified geographical region if certain findings are 
made and either regulations are proposed or, if the taking is limited 
to harassment. In this case, the specified activity is limited to the 
in-water construction activities TMC described in their initial IHA 
application and renewal request.

Determinations

    TMC's action requires the completion of a subset of pile driving 
activities that were not completed under the initial IHA (i.e., 
installation of eight permanent piles using eight temporary template 
piles). The method of taking and effects of the work were analyzed in 
the initial IHA. Work planned under the initial IHA was expected to 
take 203 in-water construction days. TMC estimates that only 12 days of 
work remain under the renewal IHA. The take estimates for the renewal 
IHA are based on the activities proposed to occur during these 
remaining days. The renewal IHA take numbers represent a subset of the 
total take authorized under the initial IHA. In analyzing the effects 
of the activities for the initial IHA, NMFS determined that TMC's 
activities would have a negligible impact on the affected species or 
stocks and that authorized take numbers of each species or stock were 
small relative to the relevant stocks (e.g., less than one-third the 
abundance of all stocks). The mitigation measures and monitoring and 
reporting requirements as described above are identical to the initial 
IHA. NMFS has concluded that there is no new information suggesting 
that our analysis or findings should change from those reached for the 
initial IHA. Based on the information and analysis contained here and 
in the referenced documents, NMFS has determined the following: (1) the 
required mitigation measures will effect the least practicable impact 
on marine mammal species or stocks and their habitat; (2) the 
authorized takes will have a negligible impact on the affected marine 
mammal species or stocks; (3) the authorized takes represent small 
numbers of marine mammals relative to the affected stock abundances; 
(4) TMC's activities will not have an unmitigable adverse impact on 
taking for subsistence purposes as no subsistence uses of marine 
mammals would be affected by this action, and; (5) appropriate 
monitoring and reporting requirements are included.

National Environmental Policy Act

    To comply with the National Environmental Policy Act of 1969 (NEPA; 
42 U.S.C. 4321 et seq.) and NOAA Administrative Order (NAO) 216-6A, 
NMFS must review our action (i.e., the issuance of a renewal IHA) with 
respect to potential impacts on the human environment.
    This action is consistent with categories of activities identified 
in Categorical Exclusion B4 (incidental take authorizations with no 
anticipated serious injury or mortality) of the Companion Manual for 
NAO 216-6A, which do not individually or cumulatively have the 
potential for significant impacts on the quality of the human 
environment and for which we have not identified any extraordinary 
circumstances that would preclude this categorical exclusion. 
Accordingly, NMFS determined that the issuance of the initial IHA 
qualified to be categorically excluded from further

NEPA review. NMFS has determined that the application of this 
categorical exclusion remains appropriate for this renewal IHA.

Endangered Species Act

    Section 7(a)(2) of the Endangered Species Act of 1973 (ESA, 16 
U.S.C. 1531 et seq.) requires that each Federal agency ensure that any 
action it authorizes, funds, or carries out is not likely to jeopardize 
the continued existence of any endangered or threatened species or 
result in the destruction or adverse modification of designated 
critical habitat. To ensure ESA compliance for the issuance of IHAs, 
NMFS consults internally whenever we propose to authorize take for 
endangered or threatened species.
    The NMFS Alaska Regional Office issued a Biological Opinion under 
section 7 of the ESA (16 U.S.C. 1531 et seq.) on the issuance of an IHA 
and potential renewal IHA to TMC under section 101(a)(5)(D) of the MMPA 
by the NMFS Office of Protected Resources. NMFS is authorizing take of 
the following distinct population segments: Western United States 
Steller sea lion, Western North Pacific humpback whale, Mexico-North 
Pacific humpback whale, and fin whale, which are listed under the ESA. 
The Alaska Regional Office's biological opinion states that the action 
is not likely to jeopardize the continued existence of the listed 
species.

Renewal

    NMFS has issued a renewal IHA to TMC for authorization to take 
marine mammals incidental to the Seward Cruise Ship Passenger Dock and 
Terminal Facility project in Seward, Alaska from September 25, 2026, 
through September 20, 2027.

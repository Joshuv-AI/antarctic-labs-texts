The Hecke-Baxter operators via Heisenberg group extensions
A.A. Gerasimov, D.R. Lebedev, S.V. Oblezin

Abstract
. The G ​ L ℓ + 1 ​ ( ℝ ) GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R}) Hecke-Baxter operator was introduced as an element of the O ℓ + 1 O_{\ell+1} -spherical Hecke algebra associated with the Gelfand pair O ℓ + 1 ⊂ G ​ L ℓ + 1 ​ ( ℝ ) O_{\ell+1}\subset GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R}) . It was specified by the property to act on an O ℓ + 1 O_{\ell+1} -fixed vector in a G ​ L ℓ + 1 ​ ( ℝ ) GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R}) -principal series representation via multiplication by the local Archimedean L L -factor canonically attached to the representation. In this note we propose another way to define the Hecke-Baxter operator, identifying it with a generalized Whittaker function for an extension of the Lie group G ​ L ℓ + 1 ​ ( ℝ ) × G ​ L ℓ + 1 ​ ( ℝ ) GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})\times GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R}) by a Heisenberg Lie group. We also show how this Whittaker function can be lifted to a matrix element of an extension of the Lie group S ​ p 2 ​ ℓ + 2 ​ ( ℝ ) × S ​ p 2 ​ ℓ + 2 ​ ( ℝ ) Sp_{2\ell+2}(\mathbb{R})\times Sp_{2\ell+2}(\mathbb{R}) by a Heisenberg Lie group.



1
Introduction

Introducing the
GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
Hecke-Baxter operator in

[
GLO08
]
is a result of combining two different lines of
research. Along the one direction, to each admissible representation
of the split real Lie group
GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
one might
canonically attach a local Archimedean
L
-factor, a function in one
complex variable capturing data of the considered representation
(see e.g.
[
ILP
]
for pedagogical introduction). Consideration of
this particular function is motivated by deep connections with the
theory of zeta-functions associated with global number fields. Also
Archimedean
L
-factors attached to representations of non-compact
real Lie groups play an essential role in formulation of the
Langlands correspondence for such groups. However the standard
construction of
L
-factor for a given representation followed a
very round-about strategy pioneered in
[
JL
]
.

Meanwhile, along quite different line of research R. Baxter
introduced a special type of operators acting in the space of states
of lattice quantum integrable systems
[
Ba
]
. This type of
operators turns out to be a particular instance of a universal
construction instrumental in finding exact solutions of various
quantum integrable systems. For example, in
[
PG
]
the Baxter
operator is proposed for the closed Toda chain. In spite of large
amount of work, the underlying reason for considering these
particular operators is not manifested (which is quite usual case
with
ad hoc
constructions in the theory of quantum integrable
systems).

Fortunately, these two lines of development intersect in the theory
of
GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
-Toda chains, one of the simplest but still
interesting exactly solvable quantum mechanical systems having
numerous ties with various branches of mathematics and mathematical
physics. Solutions of
GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
-Toda chains (common
eigenfunctions of the mutually commuting quantum Hamiltonians)
may be expressed in terms of a particular set of matrix elements in principal
series spherical representations of
GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
. These matrix
elements appear to be generalizations of a particular class of the
Whittaker functions
[
Ja
]
. This quantum integrable system also
allows explicit construction of the Baxter operator as an integral
operator. In this regard in
[
GLO08
]
(see also
[
G
]
), a
particular element of the spherical Hecke algebra

\mathcal{H}(GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R}),O_{\ell+1})
associated with the Gelfand pair

O_{\ell+1}\subset GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
is introduced. It is
demonstrated that its action on the
GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
-Whittaker
functions descends to the action of the Baxter integral operator for
the
GL_{\ell+1}
-Toda chain. Moreover the Whittaker function is an
eigenfunction of this integral operator with the eigenvalue equal to
the local Archimedean
L
-factor attached to the considered
principal series
GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
-representation. This result is
not restricted to the Whittaker functions, since the action of this
particular element of the spherical Hecke algebra on the spherical
vector in principal series representation is reduced to
multiplication by the corresponding
L
-factor. We coined the term
Hecke-Baxter operator for this element of spherical Hecke algebra

\mathcal{H}(GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R}),O_{\ell+1})
.

Although general framework for constructing the Baxter operators in
representation theory started to emerge, the question of why we
should consider this particular element of the spherical Hecke
algebra is yet to be clarified. Its relation with local Archimedian

L
-factor is intriguing but does not substantiate the appearance of
this particular element of spherical Hecke algebra as the
Hecke-Baxter operator (the theory of
L
-factors itself is a kind of
mystery and requires further clarifications).

In this note we demonstrate that the
O_{\ell+1}
-biinvariant
spherical function on
GL_{\ell+1}
entering the description of the
Hecke-Baxter operator may be identified with a (generalized)
Whittaker function on a non-reductive Lie group obtained as an
extension
\mathcal{H}GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
of
GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})\times GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
by a
Heisenberg Lie group. Precisely, generalizing the definition of Whittaker function to the
case of non-reductive Lie groups, we construct the Whittaker
function as a matrix element of an appropriate irreducible
representation of the Lie group extension
\mathcal{H}GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
of

GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})\times GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
by a Heisenberg Lie group. The corresponding
Heisenberg Lie algebra
\mathcal{H}^{(\ell+1)}
is the standard Heisenberg
algebra associated with the vector space
{\rm Mat}_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
of
real
(\ell+1)\times(\ell+1)
matrices. The right and left actions of
two copies of
GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
on
{\rm Mat}_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
provides the
extension
\mathcal{H}GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
of
GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})\times GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
by the
Heisenberg Lie group. The resulting extension may be called the
matrix hyperbolic/inverted harmonic oscillator group, since in the
case
\ell=0
this group reduces to the hyperbolic version of the
harmonic oscillator group (see e.g.
[
K
]
,
[
Ho
]
).

Our construction of Whittaker functions for
\mathcal{H}GL_{\ell+1}
has
some obvious hidden symmetries. This suggests that the
considered Whittaker
functions might be naturally lifted to the Whittaker functions
given by the matrix elements
of larger Lie groups. Indeed at the end of this note we construct Whittaker
functions for a Heisenberg group extension of
Sp_{2\ell+2}\times Sp_{2\ell+2}
and demonstrate that a special instance of these
functions reproduce the Whittaker functions for
\mathcal{H}GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
constructed earlier.
Thus representation theory of symplectic Lie groups and their
Heisenberg extensions looks like a natural habitat for the

GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
Hecke Baxter operator.

The appearance of matrix version of the hyperbolic harmonic
oscillator Lie group is significant for several reasons.
First of all, the hyperbolic harmonic oscillator algebra plays
an important role in attempts of spectral interpretation of the
Riemann zeta-function (see
[
BK
]
and
[
GLO09
]
). This
relation is to be the subject of our forthcoming publications. Also
note that classical interpretation of the Gamma-function (a basic
building block of the local Archimedean
L
-factors) as a matrix
element of the affine Lie group
{\rm Aff}(\mathbb{R})=\mathbb{R}\rtimes\mathbb{R}^{*}
(see
e.g.
[
V
]
) corroborates our interpretation of the Hecke-Baxter
operator as a matrix element, since
{\rm Aff}(\mathbb{R})
is a subgroup
of the hyperbolic harmonic oscillator group. In particular, spectral
theory of
{\rm Aff}(\mathbb{R})
naturally fits into
framework of the inverted oscillator group. (see e.g
[
Ho
]
).
Next, it is worth noting that the hyperbolic harmonic
oscillator group is also instrumental in the description of the
Gelfand-Kapranov-Zelevinsky hypergeometric functions as matrix
elements
[
GLO23
]
,
[
GLO22
]
. The proposed representation of
the Hecke-Baxter operator by Whittaker function for extensions of
reductive Lie groups might lead to interesting applications. Thus it
would be fruitful to consider matrix generalization of the
Gelfand-Kapranov-Zelevinsky construction
[
GKZ
]
by using matrix
hyperbolic harmonic oscillator group we encounter in this note. This
would provide a kind of general construction for the matrix analogs
of various special functions (compare e.g. with
[
Ka
]
). Finally
let us note that the Hecke-Baxter operators are also constructed for
other series of classical Lie groups in
[
GL
]
. Their
interpretation along the line of this note would be an interesting
and useful task.

The paper organized as follows. Section 2 contains basic facts on
the
GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
Hecke-Baxter operator from
[
GLO08
]
. In
Section 3 a simple example of the basic construction for

GL_{1}(\mathbb{R})
is considered. In Section 4 the general construction
for
GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
is presented. Finally in Section 5 we lift
the functions constructed in Section 4 to a matrix element of the
Heisenberg group extension of
Sp_{2\ell+2}\times Sp_{2\ell+2}
.

2
The Hecke-Baxter operator for
GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})

In this Section we recall the construction
[
GLO08
]
of the
Hecke-Baxter operator for the real group
GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
. For
pedagogical survey of the spherical Hecke algebra basics see e.g.

[
ILP
]
. In the following, we write

GL_{\ell+1}:=GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
for more concise notation. Let

O_{\ell+1}
be the orthogonal subgroup in
GL_{\ell+1}
. The
convolution operation defines an action of compactly supported
smooth functions on
GL_{\ell+1}
on the function space

L^{1}(GL_{\ell+1})
. This results in the commutative convolution
algebra structure on the space of compactly supported smooth
functions given by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}(f_{1}*f_{2})(g)=\int_{G}\,d\mu^{H}(\tilde{g})\,\,f_{1}(g\tilde{g}^{-1})\,f_{2}(\tilde{g})\,,\end{array}

Equivalently, (
2
) provides the action of the function
f_{1}

on
f_{2}\in L^{1}(GL_{\ell+1})
via the
associated integral operator with kernel

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}K_{f_{1}}(g,\tilde{g})=f_{1}(g\tilde{g}^{-1})\,d\mu^{H}(\tilde{g})\,.\end{array}

Here
d\mu^{H}
is a Haar measure on
GL_{\ell+1}
, which is unique
up to multiplication by an element of
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Thus
the multiplication structure (
2
) depends on the
choice of the Haar measure. To get rid of this dependence
one might consider the space of compactly supported measures on

GL_{\ell+1}
with its natural convolution action on the space

L^{1}(GL_{\ell+1})
. To recover (
2
), consider the measure on

GL_{\ell+1}
of the form

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}d\mu^{f}(g)=f(g)\,d\mu^{H}(g)\,,\end{array}

where
f
is a positive smooth compactly supported function.
In the following we use less invariant formulation
(
2
) with a fixed choice of the Haar measure, but we comment
on the relevance of a more invariant formulation in terms of
measures at the end of this Section.

Consider the space of functions on
GL_{\ell+1}
invariant under the
left and right action of
O_{\ell+1}
. The algebra structure
(
2
) descends to the subspace of the

O_{\ell+1}
-biinvariant functions and defines the structure of
spherical Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}(GL_{\ell+1},O_{\ell+1})
. We
extend the standard definition slightly by allowing

O_{\ell+1}
-biinvaraint functions to be in the Schwartz space of

GL_{\ell+1}
.

Let
B_{-}\subset GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
be the Borel subgroup of lower
triangular matrices, let
N\subset B_{-}
be the maximal
unipotent subgroup and let us identify the Cartan torus
A\subset B_{-}
with the subgroup of diagonal matrices, so that
B_{-}=N\rtimes A
. Then for
{\gamma}\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell+1}
, let
\chi_{{\gamma}}:\,B_{-}\to\mathbb{C}^{*}
be
the
B_{-}
-character, for
b=na\in B_{-}
, given by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\chi_{{\gamma}}(na)=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell+1}|a_{j}|^{\imath{\gamma}_{j}-\rho_{j}},\qquad\rho_{j}=\frac{\ell}{2}+1-j\,.\end{array}

Let
(\pi_{{\gamma}},\mathcal{V}_{{\gamma}})
be the spherical principal series
irreducible
GL_{\ell+1}
-representation induced from (
2
)
realized in the space
\mathcal{V}_{{\gamma}}={\rm Ind}_{B_{-}}^{GL_{\ell+1}}(\chi_{{\gamma}})
of
B_{-}
-equivariant functions:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}f(bg)=\chi_{{\gamma}}(b)\,f(g)\,,\quad b\in B_{-}\,.\end{array}

Then for
g\in G
, the group action in
\mathcal{V}_{{\gamma}}
is given by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\bigl(\pi_{{\gamma}}(g)f\bigl)(h)\,=\,f(hg)\,,\quad f\in\mathcal{V}_{{\gamma}}\,.\end{array}

The spherical principal series representation
(\pi_{{\gamma}},\mathcal{V}_{{\gamma}})

is unitarizable, and there exists an invariant skew-Hermitian
pairing
\langle\,,\,\rangle
on
\mathcal{V}_{{\gamma}}
.

By the multiplicity one theorem
[
Sha
]
, there is a unique (up to
multiplication by an element of
\mathbb{C}^{*}
)
O_{\ell+1}
-invariant
vector
\varphi
in
\mathcal{V}_{{\gamma}}
. In the following
we impose the normalization
condition
\langle\varphi,\varphi\rangle=1
. Then there is a linear continuous
map
F:\,\mathcal{V}_{{\gamma}}\to L^{2}(G)
sending every non-zero
v\in\mathcal{V}_{{\gamma}}

to the matrix element of
(\pi_{{\gamma}},\mathcal{V}_{{\gamma}})
:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}F_{v}(g)=\langle\varphi,\pi_{\gamma}(g)\,v\rangle\,,\quad g\in G\,.\end{array}

Every
O_{\ell+1}
-biinvariant function
\Phi\in\mathcal{H}(GL_{\ell+1},O_{\ell+1})
provides convolution action on

F_{v}(g)
, so every matrix element of the form (
2
) is a

\Phi
-eigenfunction (by uniqueness of
\varphi
) ,

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}(\Phi*F_{v})(g)=\Lambda_{\Phi}\,F_{v}(g)\,,\quad g\in GL_{\ell+1}\,.\end{array}

Therefore, taking the eigenvalues defines a character of the Hecke
algebra

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\Lambda\,:\quad\mathcal{H}(GL_{\ell+1},O_{\ell+1})\longrightarrow\mathbb{C},\,\qquad\Phi\mapsto\Lambda_{\Phi}\,.\end{array}

In the following we consider two specific matrix elements of the
given principal series representation
(\pi_{{\gamma}},\mathcal{V}_{{\gamma}})
. The
first one is the zonal spherical function:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\Phi_{{\gamma}}(g)=\langle\varphi\,,\pi_{{\gamma}}(g)\,\varphi\rangle\,,\quad\Phi_{{\gamma}}({\rm Id})=1\,,\end{array}

where
{\rm Id}\in GL_{\ell+1}
is the identity element. To define the
other matrix element, let us pick a principal character
\psi_{N_{+}}

of the maximal unipotent subgroup
N_{+}\subset GL_{\ell+1}
; the
latter can be identified with the subgroup of upper triangular
unipotent matrices. Then the Jacquet-Whittaker function is given by
the matrix element
[
Ja
]
,

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\Psi_{{\gamma}}(g)=\langle\varphi\,,\pi_{{\gamma}}(g)\,\psi\rangle\,,\end{array}

where the Whittaker
vector
\psi\in\mathcal{V}_{{\gamma}}
is singled out by the
following
N_{+}
-equivariance condition:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\Psi_{{\gamma}}(gn)=\psi_{N_{+}}(n)\,\Psi_{{\gamma}}(g)\,,\quad n\in N_{+}\,.\end{array}

As a consequence of (
2
), for any
\Phi\in\mathcal{H}(GL_{\ell+1},O_{\ell+1})
, both
matrix elements (
2
) and (
2
) are

\Phi
-eigenfunctions with the same eigenvalue:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\bigl(\Phi*\Phi_{{\gamma}}\bigr)(g)=\Lambda_{\Phi}({\gamma})\,\Phi_{{\gamma}}(g),\quad\bigl(\Phi*\Psi_{{\gamma}}\bigr)(g)=\Lambda_{\Phi}({\gamma})\,\Psi_{{\gamma}}(g)\,.\end{array}

Following the Langlands philosophy (see e.g.
[
ILP
]
,
[
Kna
]
)
to the principal series
representation
(\pi_{{\gamma}}\,,\mathcal{V}_{\gamma})
of
GL_{\ell+1}
one attaches
the corresponding local Archimedean
L
-factor,

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}L(s)=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell+1}\,\pi^{-\frac{s-\imath\gamma_{j}}{2}}\,\Gamma\Big(\frac{s-\imath\gamma_{j}}{2}\Big)\,.\end{array}

One should stress that the particular expression (
2
)
for the local Archimedean
L
-factor implicitly includes
normalization associated with the global structures of number fields
(captured by the functional equation of the corresponding global
zeta-function). Purely local characteristic of representations over
the field of real numbers gives rise to a more general form of

L
-factor,

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}L(s;c)=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell+1}\,(2c)^{\frac{s-\imath\gamma_{j}}{2}}\,\Gamma\Big(\frac{s-\imath\gamma_{j}}{2}\Big)\,,\end{array}

depending on a parameter
c\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Thus local Archimedean

L
-factor is better understood as an
\mathbb{R}_{+}
-torsor. The expression
(
2
) arises upon fixing the section
2c=\pi^{-1}

of the
\mathbb{R}_{+}
-torsor.

One of the main results of
[
GLO08
]
may be formulated as follows:

Let
Q_{s,c}(g)
be a two-parameter family of

O_{\ell+1}
-biinvariant functions on
GL_{\ell+1}
given by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}Q_{s,c}(g)=\bigl|\det(g)\bigr|^{s+\frac{l}{2}}\,e^{-\frac{1}{2c}{\rm Tr}\,(g^{\top}g)}\,,\quad s\in\mathbb{C}\,,\quad c\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\,,\end{array}

where
g^{\top}
is the standard matrix transposition of
g
. Then
with an appropriate normalization of the Haar measure, the function

Q_{s,c}\in\mathcal{H}(GL_{\ell+1},O_{\ell+1})
acts
on the spherical vector
\varphi\in\mathcal{V}_{{\gamma}}
via multiplication by
the corresponding
L
-factor (
2
).

One should notice that here we imply a bit
different normalization conventions for the Haar measure, so that
(
2
) differs from that in
[
GLO08
]
by the factor

2^{\ell+1}
, the size of the intersection
O_{\ell+1}\cap A=\{\pm 1\}^{\ell+1}
with the Cartan torus
A\subset GL_{\ell+1}
.
We also adapt the expressions for a more
general form of
L
-factor (
2
). This more general
form actually appears quite naturally in the related Quantum Field
Theory considerations
[
GLO11
]
.

The structure of
Q_{s,c}
in (
2
) is rather simple: it is a
product of the multiplicative
GL_{\ell+1}
-character
|\det(g)|^{s}

and the
O_{\ell+1}
-biinvariant Gaussian function. This suggests
considering the
O_{\ell+1}
-biinvariant Gaussian function as a
part of the measure on the Lie group,

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}d\mu^{\text{Gauss}}(g)\,=\,e^{-\frac{1}{2c}{\rm Tr}\,(g^{\top}g)}\,d\mu^{H}(g)\,,\end{array}

in concordance with (
2
). Appearance of the Gaussian
measure is tied up with the definition of local Archimedean

L
-factors and ultimately is imposed by functional equations for
the corresponding global zeta-functions
[
W
]
.
The underlying meaning of (
2
) is that it is the Archimedean counterpart of
the characteristic function of the subset
G(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\subset G(\mathbb{Q}_{p})

arising in the non-Archimedean case. Nevertheless its appearance
still remains rather mysterious.

One approach to understanding the underlying structure of the
Hecke-Baxter operator (
2
) would be to look into the theory
of quantum integrable systems. Recall that the
operator acting on the Whittaker functions (
2
)
by convolution with the kernel function (
2
) have already
appeared in the theory of quantum integrable theories of Toda type
as an instance of the Baxter operator
[
GLO08
]
. Although this
relation with the theory of quantum integrable systems is very
interesting and fruitful, it does not explain specific choice of
the
O_{\ell+1}
-biinvariant function (
2
). Therefore,
further clarifications of the appearance of the Gaussian function
in (
2
) are still required.

Below we make a modest step in this direction by proposing a
description of the kernel (
2
) in terms of representation
theory of a non-reductive extension of the Lie group
GL_{\ell+1}
.

3
The
GL_{1}(\mathbb{R})
Hecke-Baxter operator via an extension

Our objective is to describe the kernel (
2
) of the
Hecke-Baxter operator as a matrix element of some extension of the
Lie group
GL_{\ell+1}
. In this Section we treat the case of the
group
G=GL_{1}
. Although it looks like a trivial exercise it allows
for rather straightforward generalization to higher ranks.

We start with the Gelfand pair
K\subset G
with

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}G=GL_{1}=\mathbb{R}^{*}=\mathbb{R}-\{0\}\,,\quad K=O_{1}=\{\pm 1\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{*}\,.\end{array}

The maximal unipotent subgroup of
GL_{1}
is trivial, hence the
Iwasawa decomposition reduces to

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}G=AK=\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\{\pm 1\}\,,\quad\mathbb{R}_{+}=\{t\in\mathbb{R}^{*}|t>0\}\,.\end{array}

Spherical principal series representation

(\pi_{{\gamma}},\,\mathcal{V}_{{\gamma}}),\,{\gamma}\in\mathbb{R}
is realized in a one-dimensional

\mathbb{C}
-vector space with trivial action of
O_{1}=\{\pm 1\}
and the
action of the Lie algebra generator
H\in\mathfrak{gl}_{1}={\rm Lie}(GL_{1})
is given
by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\pi_{{\gamma}}(H)\cdot v=\imath{\gamma}\,v\,,\quad v\in\mathcal{V}_{{\gamma}}\,.\end{array}

We define a skew-Hermitian
GL_{1}
-invariant pairing on
\mathcal{V}_{{\gamma}}

by fixing a vector
v\in\mathcal{V}_{{\gamma}}
, and requiring

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\langle v,\,\pi_{{\gamma}}(H)\,v\rangle\,=\,-\langle\pi_{{\gamma}}(H)\,v,\,v\rangle\,,\qquad\langle v,v\rangle=1\,.\end{array}

Vector
v
is both spherical vector and the Whittaker vector, hence
in the case of
GL_{1}
the matrix elements (
2
) and
(
2
) coincide and are equal to

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\Phi_{{\gamma}}(x)=\langle v,\,\pi_{{\gamma}}(|x|^{H})v\rangle=|x|^{\imath{\gamma}}\,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{*}\,.\end{array}

The function (
3
) also gives the character of

(\pi_{{\gamma}},\,\mathcal{V}_{{\gamma}})
.

The
GL_{1}
Hecke-Baxter operator (
2
) is given by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}Q_{s,c}(x)\,=\,\,|x|^{s}\,e^{-\frac{1}{2c}|x|^{2}},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{*}\,,\quad s\in\mathbb{C}\,,\quad c\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\,.\end{array}

Note that the function (
3
) is obviously invariant under
the subgroup
O_{1}=\{\pm 1\}
acting via
t\to\pm t
, and thus
indeed belongs to the spherical Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}(GL_{1},O_{1})
. We
fix the Haar measure as follows

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}d\mu^{H}(y)=\frac{dy}{y}\,,\qquad y\in\mathbb{R}^{*}\,.\end{array}

The convolution action of
Q_{s,c}
on the matrix element (
3
)
is easy to calculate with the following result, for
{\rm Re}(s)>0

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}(Q_{s,c}*\Phi_{{\gamma}})(x)\,=\!\!\int\limits_{GL_{1}}\!\!d\mu^{H}(y)\,\,Q_{s,c}(y)\,\Phi_{{\gamma}}(xy^{-1})\,=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{*}}\frac{dy}{y}\,|y|^{s}\,e^{-\frac{1}{2c}y^{2}}\,|xy^{-1}|^{\imath{\gamma}}\\
=\,2|x|^{\imath{\gamma}}\!\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dy}{y}\,y^{s-\imath{\gamma}}\,e^{-\frac{1}{2c}y^{2}}\,=\,L(s,c)\,\Phi_{{\gamma}}(x)\,,\end{array}

where

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}L(s,c)\,=\,(2c)^{\frac{s-\imath{\gamma}}{2}}\,\Gamma\Big(\frac{s-\imath{\gamma}}{2}\Big)\,.\end{array}

Thus the matrix element (
3
) is indeed a

Q_{s,c}
-eigenfunction with the eigenvalue equal to the
Archimedean
L
-factor (
2
) with
\ell=0
attached to
the one-dimensional principal series representation

(\pi_{{\gamma}},\,\mathcal{V}_{{\gamma}})
of
GL_{1}
.

Now we construct an extension of the group
GL_{1}
allowing to
describe the Hecke-Baxter kernel (
3
) in representation
theory terms. Let us start with the Heisenberg algebra
\mathcal{H}^{(1)}

generated by
X,Y,C
satisfying the following defining relations

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}[X,Y]=C,\qquad[X,C]=0,\qquad[Y,C]=0\,.\end{array}

Consider the
GL_{1}
-action of on
\mathcal{H}^{(1)}
by automorphisms, so
that the orthogonal subgroup
O_{1}\subset GL_{1}
acts trivially and
the action of the connected component
\mathbb{R}_{+}\subset GL_{1}
on

\mathcal{H}^{(1)}
is defined by the action of the generator
H
of

\mathfrak{gl}_{1}={\rm Lie}(GL_{1})
:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}[H,X]=X,\qquad[H,Y]=-Y,\qquad[H,C]=0\,.\end{array}

Introduce the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{gl}_{1}=\mathfrak{gl}_{1}\ltimes\mathcal{H}^{(1)}

generated by
H,X,Y,Z
and with the defining relations (
3
)
and (
3
). We denote by
\mathcal{H}GL_{1}
a Lie group such that

\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{gl}_{1}={\rm Lie}(\mathcal{H}GL_{1})
. The Lie algebra

\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{gl}_{1}
may be called the Lie algebra of hyperbolic
harmonic oscillator by analogy with a more standard case of the Lie
algebra of harmonic oscillator (see e.g.
[
K
]
).

Representation theory of the Heisenberg Lie algebra is very simple.
For a fixed nontrivial action of the central element, the Heisenberg
Lie algebra
\mathcal{H}^{(1)}
has a unique irreducible unitary
representation. In turn, irreducible representations of

\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{gl}_{1}
(for invertible image of
C
) are directly
related with irreducible representations of
\mathcal{H}^{(1)}
and of

\mathfrak{gl}_{1}
. To infer this, note that the universal enveloping
algebra
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{gl}_{1})
of
\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{gl}_{1}
with the
invertible generator
C
allows the following factorization:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{gl}_{1})\otimes_{\mathbb{C}[C]}\mathbb{C}[C,C^{-1}]\simeq\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{gl}_{1})\otimes\left(\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H}^{(1)})\otimes_{\mathbb{C}[C]}\mathbb{C}[C,C^{-1}]\right)\,.\end{array}

Indeed, let the algebra in the r.h.s. be generated by
\tilde{H}\in\mathfrak{gl}_{1}
and
X,Y,C\in\mathcal{H}^{(1)}
satisfying the relations

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}[X,Y]=C,\qquad[X,C]=0,\qquad[Y,C]=0\,,\\
\,[\tilde{H},X]=0,\qquad[\tilde{H},Y]=0,\qquad[\tilde{H},C]=0\,.\end{array}

Then one can notice that these relations become equivalent to the
defining relations (
3
) and (
3
) for the algebra in
l.h.s. of (
3
) via taking

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\tilde{H}=H-\frac{1}{2}C^{-1}(XY+YX).\end{array}

Therefore, for every irreducible
\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{gl}_{1}
-representation

(\pi,\mathcal{V})
with invertible action of the central element
C
,
vector space
\mathcal{V}
can be identified with a tensor product of an
irreducible
\mathfrak{gl}_{1}
-representation and an irreducible representation
of the Heisenberg algebra
\mathcal{H}^{(1)}
(this tensor product evidently
does not respect the structure of a

\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{gl}_{1}
-representation).

Let
(\pi_{\kappa,\,c}\,,\mathcal{V}_{\kappa,\,c})
be irreducible

\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{gl}_{1}
-representation realized in the space
\mathcal{S}
of
Schwartz functions in variable
x\in\mathbb{R}
:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\pi_{\kappa,\,c}(Y)=-c\partial_{x}\,,\quad\pi_{\kappa,\,c}(X)=\imath x\,,\quad\pi_{\kappa,\,c}(H)=\frac{1}{2}+\imath\kappa+x\partial_{x}\,,\\
\pi_{\kappa,\,c}(C)=\imath c\,,\end{array}

where
c\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
is implied. Consider the contragredient
representation
(\pi^{\vee}_{\kappa,c},\mathcal{V}^{\vee}_{\kappa,c})

realized in the space of tempered distributions
\mathcal{D}
on
\mathbb{R}
. We
write down a natural Hermitian pairing in the following explicit
form

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\langle\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\rangle=\!\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\!dx\,\,\,\overline{\chi_{1}(x)}\,\,\,\chi_{2}(x)\,,\end{array}

implying that
\overline{\chi_{1}(x)}\,dx
represents a tempered
distribution. Then we have

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\langle\chi_{1},\pi_{\kappa,c}(U)\chi_{2}\rangle=-\langle\pi^{\vee}_{\kappa,c}(U)\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\rangle\,,\quad U\in\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{gl}_{1}\,.\end{array}

Assume that the
\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{gl}_{1}
-representation

\pi_{\kappa,\,c}
integrates to the Lie group action of
\mathcal{H}GL_{1}
.

Define the spherical and Whittaker vectors,
\phi\in\mathcal{V}_{\kappa,c}
and
\psi\in\mathcal{V}_{\kappa,c}^{\vee}
, by the
following relations:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\pi_{\kappa,c}(X-\imath Y)\cdot\phi=0,\qquad\pi^{\vee}_{\kappa,c}(X)\cdot\psi=\imath\psi\,.\end{array}

Explicitly, we have

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\phi(x)=e^{-\frac{1}{2c}\,x^{2}},\quad\psi(x)=\delta(x-1)\,,\end{array}

where
\delta(x)
is the standard delta-function
considered as a (tempered) distribution.
Now for the pairing (
3
), introduce
the following matrix element

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}W_{\kappa,c}(y)=\langle\psi\,,\pi_{\kappa,c}(|y|^{H})\phi\rangle\,,\qquad y\in GL_{1}=\mathbb{R}^{*}\,,\end{array}

which may be called the
\mathcal{H}GL_{1}
-Whittaker function (recall that

O_{1}\subset GL_{1}
acts trivially on vectors (
3
)). Then
substitution of (
3
) into (
3
) results in

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}W_{\kappa,c}(y)\,=\,|y|^{\frac{1}{2}+\imath\kappa}\!\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\!dx\,\delta(x-1)\,|y|^{\,x\partial_{x}}e^{-\frac{1}{2c}\,x^{2}}=|y|^{\frac{1}{2}+\imath\kappa}\,e^{-\frac{1}{2c}\,t^{2}}\,.\end{array}

Therefore, for
s=1/2+\imath\kappa
we arrive at the expression
(
3
) for the kernel of the
GL_{1}
Hecke-Baxter operator:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}Q_{s,c}(y)\,=\,|y|^{s}\,e^{-\frac{1}{2c}\,y^{2}}\,.\end{array}

To realize the general case of a complex
s
one should consider
non-unitary
\mathfrak{hgl}
-representations

(\pi_{{\gamma},c},\mathcal{V}_{{\gamma},c}),\,{\gamma}\in\mathbb{C}
(we skip these simple
modifications here). Thus we represent the
GL_{1}
Hecke-Baxter

\mathcal{H}GL_{1}
-Whittaker function.

4
The
GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
Hecke-Baxter operator via an extension

In this Section we generalize considerations of the previous

group
GL_{\ell+1}

analogous to the

introduced in the previous

Let
\mathcal{H}^{(\ell+1)}
be the standard Heisenberg Lie algebra
associated with the vector space
{\rm Mat}_{\ell+1}
of real

(\ell+1)\times(\ell+1)
-matrices. Precisely, we consider the

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbb{R}^{2(\ell+1)^{2}}\,=\,{\rm Mat}_{\ell+1}\oplus{\rm Mat}_{\ell+1},\quad\Big(\begin{smallmatrix}Y\\
&\\
X\end{smallmatrix}\Big)\,\in\mathbb{R}^{2(\ell+1)^{2}},\\
\Omega(X,Y)={\rm Tr}\,\bigl(dX\wedge dY^{\top}\bigr)\,=\,\sum_{i,j=1}^{\ell+1}dX_{ij}\wedge dY_{ij}\,.\end{array}

The corresponding Lie algebra
\mathcal{H}^{(\ell+1)}
is generated by

X_{ij},\,Y_{ij},\,1\leq i,j\leq\ell+1
and the central element
C

satisfying the relations

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}[X_{ij},X_{kl}]=0,\qquad[Y_{ij},Y_{kl}]=0\,,\qquad[X_{ij},Y_{kl}]=C\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}\,.\end{array}

Introduce the semi-direct sum
\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{gl}_{\ell+1}
of the Lie
algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{\ell+1}\oplus\mathfrak{gl}_{\ell+1}
and the Heisenberg Lie
algebra
\mathcal{H}^{(\ell+1)}

\{e_{ij}^{L}\,,1\leq i,j\leq(\ell+1)\}
be the standard set of generators of the one copy
of
\mathfrak{gl}_{\ell+1}

be
the standard set of generators of the other copy of
\mathfrak{gl}_{\ell+1}
.
Then
\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{gl}_{\ell+1}
is defined by the relations
(
4

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}[e^{L,R}_{ij},e^{L,R}_{kl}]=\delta_{jk}e^{L,R}_{il}-\delta_{il}e^{L,R}_{kj},\quad\,[e^{L}_{ij},e^{R}_{kl}]=0,\quad[e^{L,R}_{ij},C]=0\,,\end{array}

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}[e^{L}_{ij},X_{kl}]=-\delta_{ik}X_{jl},\qquad[e^{L}_{ij},Y_{kl}]=\delta_{jk}Y_{il}\,.\end{array}

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}[e^{R}_{ij},X_{kl}]=\delta_{jl}X_{ki},\qquad[e^{R}_{ij},Y_{kl}]=-\delta_{il}Y_{kj}\,.\end{array}

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{gl}_{\ell+1}
might be considered as a
special kind of matrix generalization of the hyperbolic harmonic

\mathcal{H}GL_{\ell+1}
a Lie group such

Irreducible representations of the extension

\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{gl}_{\ell+1}
of

[

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{gl}_{\ell+1})\otimes_{\mathbb{C}[C]}\mathbb{C}[C,C^{-1}]\\
\simeq\,\mathcal{U}\bigl(\mathfrak{gl}_{\ell+1}\oplus\mathfrak{gl}_{\ell+1}\bigr)\otimes\left(\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H}^{(\ell+1)})\otimes_{\mathbb{C}[C]}\mathbb{C}[C,C^{-1}]\right)\,.\end{array}

5
Lifting to symplectic Lie group matrix elements

In this Section we construct a lifting of the
\mathcal{H}GL_{\ell+1}
-Whittaker function considered in the previous Section
to a matrix element of the larger Lie group
\mathcal{H}Sp_{2\ell+2}
given

and the Heisenberg group accosiated with the matrix space

{\rm Mat}_{\ell+1}

\mathcal{H}\mathfrak{sp}_{2\ell+2}={\rm Lie}(\mathcal{H}Sp_{2\ell+2})

Recall that the symplectic Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sp}_{2\ell+2}

allows the following embedding:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathfrak{sp}_{2\ell+2}\,=\,\Big\{\Big(\begin{smallmatrix}A&&B\\
&&\\
C&&-A^{\top}\end{smallmatrix}\Big)\,:\quad B^{\top}=B,\quad C^{\top}=C\Big\}\,\subset\,\mathfrak{gl}_{2\ell+2}\,,\end{array}

with the block-diagonal subalgebra

\mathfrak{gl}_{\ell+1}={\rm diag}\{A,\,-A^{\top}\}\subset\mathfrak{sp}_{2\ell+2}
. Denote
by
a_{ij},\,1\leq i,j\leq(\ell+1)
and
b_{ij},c_{ij},\,1\leq i\leq j\leq(\ell+1)
the corresponding generators of

\mathfrak{sp}_{2\ell+2}
. In the following we consider two copies
of
\mathfrak{sp}_{2\ell+2}
with the two sets of generators:

a^{L}_{ij},b^{L}_{ij},c^{L}_{ij}
and
a^{R}_{ij},b^{R}_{ij},c^{R}_{ij}
.
The generators
a^{L,R}_{ij}\,,b^{L}_{ij},c^{L}_{ij}\,,1\leq i\leq j\leq\ell+1
of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sp}_{2\ell+2}\oplus\mathfrak{sp}_{2\ell+2}

satisfy the following commutation relations:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}[b^{L,R}_{ij},b^{L,R}_{kl}]=[c^{L,R}_{ij},c^{L,R}_{kl}]=0,\quad[a^{L,R}_{ij},a^{L,R}_{kl}]=\delta_{jk}a^{L,R}_{il}-\delta_{il}a^{L,R}_{kj},\\
\,[b^{L,R}_{ij},c^{L,R}_{kl}]=\delta_{ik}a^{L,R}_{jl}+\delta_{jl}a^{L,R}_{ik}+\delta_{jk}a^{L,R}_{il}+\delta_{il}a^{L,R}_{jk},\\
\,[a^{L,R}_{ij},b^{L,R}_{kl}]=\delta_{jk}b^{L,R}_{il}+\delta_{jl}b^{L,R}_{ik},\quad[a^{L,R}_{ij},c^{L,R}_{kl}]=-\delta_{ik}c^{L,R}_{jl}-\delta_{il}c^{L,R}_{jk}\,.\end{array}

Now we introduce the semidirect sum

{\mathfrak{h}}\mathfrak{sp}_{2\ell+2}=\bigl(\mathfrak{sp}_{2\ell+2}\oplus\mathfrak{sp}_{2\ell+2}\bigr)\ltimes\mathcal{H}^{(\ell+1)}
as follows. The Lie algebra

{\mathfrak{h}}\mathfrak{sp}_{2\ell+2}
is generated by

a_{ij}^{L,R},X_{ij},Y_{ij}\,,1\leq i,j\leq\ell+1
, and

b_{ij}^{L,R},c_{ij}^{L,R},\,1\leq j
and the central element
C

satisfying the commutation relations (
5
) and
(
4
),(
4
),(
4

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}[b^{L}_{ij},Y_{kl}]=0,\quad[b^{L}_{ij},X_{kl}]=\delta_{jk}Y_{il}+\delta_{ik}Y_{jl},\\
\,[c^{L}_{ij},X_{kl}]=0,\quad[c^{L}_{ij},Y_{kl}]=\delta_{jk}X_{il}+\delta_{ik}X_{jl}\,,\end{array}

and

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}[b^{R}_{ij},X_{kl}]=0,\quad[b^{R}_{ij},Y_{kl}]=-\delta_{il}X_{kj}-\delta_{jl}X_{ki},\\
\,[c^{R}_{ij},Y_{kl}]=0,\quad[c^{R}_{ij},X_{kl}]=-\delta_{il}Y_{kj}-\delta_{jl}Y_{ki}\,.\end{array}

It is easy to check directly that thus defined action of the Lie
algebra of
\mathfrak{sp}_{2\ell+2}\oplus\mathfrak{sp}_{2\ell+2}
is compatible with
the structure of the Heisenberg Lie algebra

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}[X_{ij},X_{kl}]=0,\qquad[Y_{ij},Y_{kl}]=0\,,\qquad[X_{ij},Y_{kl}]=C\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}\,.\end{array}

Using the injective homomorphism,

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathfrak{gl}_{\ell+1}\oplus\mathfrak{gl}_{\ell+1}\,\longrightarrow\,\mathfrak{sp}_{2\ell+2}\oplus\mathfrak{sp}_{2\ell+2}\,,\\

we obtain the embedding of the Lie algebra (

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{gl}_{\ell+1}\,=\,\bigl(\mathfrak{gl}_{\ell+1}\oplus\mathfrak{gl}_{\ell+1}\bigr)\ltimes\mathcal{H}^{(\ell+1)}\,\subset\,{\mathfrak{h}}\mathfrak{sp}_{2\ell+2}\,.\end{array}

In the following, we consider representations of the Lie algebra

\mathfrak{hsp}_{2\ell+2}

\mathfrak{hgl}_{\ell+1}
-representations

Lemma 5.2

Abstract
In this article, we present and analyze a fully discrete second-order scheme for a general class of non-local system of conservation laws in multiple spatial dimensions. The method employs a MUSCL-type spatial reconstruction coupled with Runge-Kutta time integration. We analytically prove that the proposed scheme preserves the positivity in all the unknowns, a critical property for ensuring the physical validity of quantities like density, which must remain non-negative. Additionally, the scheme is proven to exhibit L ∞ \mathrm{L}^{\infty} -stability. Numerical experiments conducted on both the non-local scalar and system cases illustrate the importance of the second-order scheme when compared to its first-order counterpart and verify the theoretical results.

Although first-order

This has led to an increasing emphasis on research aimed at developing high-order methods. In the context of

To the best of our knowledge, so far, no results are available on second- or high-order schemes for the multi-dimensional case.

It is the purpose of this work to propose a fully-discrete second-order scheme for systems of multi-dimensional non-local conservation laws
and present numerical simulations together with desirable theoretical results.

To derive a second-order scheme, we combine a MUSCL-type spatial reconstruction

with a second-order strong stability preserving Runge-Kutta (RK) time-stepping method

;
gottliebsigalshu2001

This property is particularly important in models such as those of crowd dynamics, where the unknowns represent densities of different species and must remain non-negative.
Additionally, we establish that the numerical solutions obtained from the proposed second-order scheme are
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}

Furthermore, the asymptotic compatibility of the proposed scheme is numerically investigated in the context of the singular limit problem (see

colombocrippa2019

We are also interested in the theoretical convergence of the second-order scheme, for which the main ingredient is the bounded variation (BV) estimates. We note that, for the case of local conservation laws as well, no BV estimates are available; instead, convergence is established in

through weak BV estimates for measure-valued solutions. Given the difficulties associated with obtaining BV estimates, we aim to further investigate in this direction in a forthcoming paper.

\mathbb{R}^{2}=\bigcup_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}}[x_{i-\frac{1}{2}},x_{i+\frac{1}{2}})\\

4
Second-order scheme

To develop a second-order scheme, we adhere to the fundamental principle of utilizing a spatial linear reconstruction and a Runge-Kutta time-stepping method. Specifically, we employ a two-stage Runge-Kutta method, where in each step, a piecewise linear polynomial is reconstructed within each cell using slopes obtained from the minmod limiter in each direction. Additionally, the reconstructed piecewise polynomial is formulated to preserve the cell average in each cell.
To begin with, we describe the reconstruction procedure at the time level
t^{n},
where we write the piecewise linear polynomial in each cell as

\tilde{\rho}^{k,n}_{\Delta}(x,y):=ax+by+c,\quad\mbox{for}\quad(x,y)\in[x_{i-\frac{1}{2}},x_{i+\frac{1}{2}})\times[y_{{j-\frac{1}{2}}},y_{{j+\frac{1}{2}}}),

where
a,b
and
c
are constants.
Given that
\tilde{\rho}^{k,n}_{\Delta}
preserves the cell averages, we obtain

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}^{k,n}_{\Delta}(x,y)=\rho_{ij}^{k,n}+a(x-x_{i})+b(y-y_{j}),

(4.1)

where
a=\partial_{x}\tilde{\rho}^{k,n}_{\Delta}(x_{i},y_{j})
and
b=\partial_{y}\tilde{\rho}^{k,n}_{\Delta}(x_{i},y_{j}).
The slopes are determined using the minmod slope-limiter as
\Delta x\partial_{x}\tilde{\rho}^{k,n}_{\Delta}(x_{i},y_{j})=\sigma^{x,k,n}_{ij},

\Delta y\partial_{y}\tilde{\rho}^{k,n}_{\Delta}(x_{i},y_{j})=\sigma^{y,k,n}_{ij},
where

\displaystyle\sigma^{x,k,n}_{ij}

\displaystyle:=2\theta\textrm{minmod}\left((\rho^{k,n}_{i,j}-\rho^{k,n}_{i-1,j}),\ \frac{1}{2}(\rho^{k,n}_{i+1,j}-\rho^{k,n}_{i-1,j}),\ (\rho^{k,n}_{i+1,j}-\rho^{k,n}_{i,j})\right),

(4.2)

\displaystyle\sigma^{y,k,n}_{ij}

\displaystyle:=2\theta\textrm{minmod}\left((\rho^{k,n}_{i,j}-\rho^{k,n}_{i,j-1}),\ \frac{1}{2}(\rho^{k,n}_{i,j+1}-\rho^{k,n}_{i,j-1}),\ (\rho^{k,n}_{i,j+1}-\rho^{k,n}_{i,j})\right),

\mbox{for}\ \theta\in[0,1],
where the minmod function is defined by

\displaystyle\textrm{minmod}(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})\coloneqq\begin{cases}\mathrm{sgn}(a_{1})\min\limits_{1\leq k\leq m}\{\lvert a_{k}\rvert\}\hskip 15.6491pt\mbox{if}\ \mathrm{sgn}(a_{1})=\cdots=\mathrm{sgn}(a_{m}),\\
0\hskip 98.16191pt\mbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}

The face values of the reconstructed polynomial in the
x
-direction are given by

\displaystyle\rho_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}^{k,n,-}=\rho^{k,n}_{i,j}+\frac{\sigma_{i,j}^{x,k,n}}{2},\ \ \ \rho_{i-\frac{1}{2},j}^{k,n,+}=\rho^{k,n}_{i,j}-\frac{\sigma_{i,j}^{x,k,n}}{2}.

(4.3)

Similarly, the face values in the
y
-direction are given by

\displaystyle\rho_{i,j+\frac{1}{2}}^{k,n,-}=\rho^{k,n}_{i,j}+\frac{\sigma_{i,j}^{y,k,n}}{2},\ \ \ \rho_{i,j-\frac{1}{2}}^{k,n,+}=\rho^{k,n}_{i,j}-\frac{\sigma_{i,j}^{y,k,n}}{2},

(4.4)

where, within each cell, the superscripts
+
and
-
indicate the left (bottom) and right (top) interfaces, respectively.

Given the cell-averaged solutions
\rho_{\Delta}^{k,n},\,k\in\{1,2,\dots,N\}
at the time stage
t^{n}
, the fully discrete scheme involves two stages of the Runge-Kutta method
gottlieb1998otalVD
;
shu1988
to compute the solution at the time level
t^{n+1}.
This is described as follows.

Step 1:
Define

\displaystyle\rho^{k,(1)}_{ij}

\displaystyle=\rho^{k,n}_{ij}-\lambda_{x}\left[F^{k,n}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}(\rho_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}^{k,n,-},\rho_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}^{k,n,+})-F^{k,n}_{i-\frac{1}{2},j}(\rho_{i-\frac{1}{2},j}^{k,n,-},\rho_{i-\frac{1}{2},j}^{k,n,+})\right]

(4.5)

\displaystyle}{\displaystyle-\lambda_{y}\left[G^{k,n}_{i,j+\frac{1}{2}}(\rho_{i,j+\frac{1}{2}}^{k,n,-}\rho_{i,j+\frac{1}{2}}^{n,+})-G^{k,n}_{i,j-\frac{1}{2}}(\rho_{i,j-\frac{1}{2}}^{k,n,-},\rho_{i,j-\frac{1}{2}}^{k,n,+})\right],

\mbox{for each }\;i,\;j\in\mathbb{Z},

where the numerical fluxes
F
and
G
are as defined in (

5.1
, we obtain that the FO scheme is positivity preserving under the CFL condition (
5.13
).

Remark 5
.

For the first-order scheme (
3.1
), the CFL condition (
5.13
) implies that the coefficients
\alpha,\beta
in the numerical flux (
3.2
) should satisfy
\alpha,\beta\in\left(0,\frac{1}{3}\right)
. Similarly, for the second-order scheme (
4.7
), the CFL condition (
5.3
) requires that
\alpha,\beta\in\left(0,\frac{2}{9}\right)
.

We now present a corollary to Theorem
5.1
, which will aid in proving the
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
-stability.

Corollary 1

(
\mathrm{L}^{1}
- stability) Under the assumptions of Theorem
5.1
, for a non-negative initial data
\bm{\rho}_{0}\in\mathrm{L}^{1}\cap\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}),
the approximate solution
\bm{\rho}_{\Delta}
obtained from the scheme (
4.7
) satisfies

\displaystyle\lVert\rho^{k}_{\Delta}(t)\rVert_{\mathrm{L}^{1}}=\lVert\rho^{k}_{\Delta}(0)\rVert_{\mathrm{L}^{1}},

(5.14)

for all
k\in\{1,2,\dots,N\}

and
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

Proof.

By Theorem (
5.1
), the assumptions
\rho^{k,0}_{i,j}\geq 0,
imply that
\rho^{k,n}_{i,j}\geq 0
for all
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}.
Additionally, each stage in the Runge-Kutta time stepping satisfies
\rho^{k,(1)}_{i,j},\rho^{k,(2)}_{i,j}\geq 0
for all
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}.

Therefore, we have the following

\displaystyle\lVert\rho^{k,(1)}\rVert_{\mathrm{L}^{1}}

\displaystyle=\Delta x\Delta y\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}}\rho^{k,(1)}_{i,j}=\Delta x\Delta y\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}}\rho^{k,n}_{i,j}

and

\displaystyle\lVert\rho^{k,(2)}\rVert_{\mathrm{L}^{1}}

\displaystyle=\Delta x\Delta y\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}}\rho^{k,(2)}_{i,j}=\Delta x\Delta y\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}}\rho^{k,(1)}_{i,j}.

Consequently, we obtain the result

\displaystyle\lVert\rho^{k,n+1}\rVert_{\mathrm{L}^{1}}=\Delta x\Delta y\sum_{i,j}\rho_{i,j}^{k,n+1}

\displaystyle=\Delta x\Delta y\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{\rho_{i,j}^{k,(2)}+\rho_{i,j}^{k,n}}{2}

\displaystyle=\Delta x\Delta y\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}}\rho^{k,n}_{i,j}=\lVert\rho^{k,n}\rVert_{\mathrm{L}^{1}}.

The equality (
5.14
) now follows immediately.
∎

6
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
stability

In this section, we establish that the second-order scheme given by (
4.7
) exhibits
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
- stability.

Theorem 6.1

(
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
-stability) Let
\bm{\rho}_{0}\in\mathrm{L}^{1}\cap\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N})
. If the hypotheses
(H0)
,
(H1)
and
(H2)
and the CFL condition (
5.3
) hold along with the mesh-size restriction
\displaystyle\Delta x,\Delta y\leq\frac{1}{3M}
, then there exists a constant
C\geq 0
depending only on
\bm{\rho}_{0},\bm{\eta},\bm{\nu},\{f^{k}\}_{k=1}^{N}
and
\{g^{k}\}_{k=1}^{N}
such that the approximate solution
\bm{\rho}_{\Delta}
obtained from the second-order scheme (
4.7
) satisfies

\displaystyle\lVert\bm{\rho}_{\Delta}(t)\rVert\leq\lVert\bm{\rho}_{\Delta}(0)\rVert e^{Ct},

for any
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

Proof.

By Corollary
1
and applying the mean value theorem, we observe that the discrete convolutions (
3.3
) satisfy the following estimate

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\lVert\bm{A}^{n}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}-\bm{A}^{n}_{i-\frac{1}{2},j}\rVert

(6.1)

\displaystyle}{\displaystyle=\Bigg\lVert\left(\Delta x\Delta y\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sum_{p,l\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\eta_{i+\frac{1}{2}-l,j-p}^{q,k}-\eta_{i-\frac{1}{2}-l,j-p}^{q,k}\right)\ \rho^{k,n}_{l,p}\right)_{q=1}^{m}\Bigg\rVert

\displaystyle}{\displaystyle\leq\Delta x\left(\lVert\partial_{x}\bm{\eta}\rVert\lVert\bm{\rho}_{\Delta}(t^{n})\rVert_{\mathrm{L}^{1}}\right)

\displaystyle}{\displaystyle\leq\Delta x\left(\lVert\partial_{x}\bm{\eta}\rVert\lVert\bm{\rho}_{\Delta}(0)\rVert_{\mathrm{L}^{1}}\right).

Further, invoking the estimate (
6.1
) and using the hypotheses
(H0)
and
(H1)
, we arrive at the following estimate

\displaystyle\Big\lvert F^{k,n}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}(\rho_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}^{k,n,-},\rho_{i-\frac{1}{2},j}^{k,n,+})-F^{k,n}_{i-\frac{1}{2},j}(\rho_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}^{k,n,-},\rho_{i-\frac{1}{2},j}^{k,n,+})\Big\rvert

(6.2)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\Big\lvert\left(f^{k}(t^{n},x_{i+\frac{1}{2}},y_{j},\rho_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}^{k,n,-},\bm{A}^{n}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j})-f^{k}(t^{n},x_{i-\frac{1}{2}},y_{j},\rho_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}^{k,n,-},\bm{A}^{n}_{i-\frac{1}{2},j})\right)\Big\rvert

\displaystyle}{\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\Big\lvert\left(f^{k}(t^{n},x_{i+\frac{1}{2}},y_{j},\rho_{i-\frac{1}{2},j}^{k,n,+},\bm{A}^{n}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j})-f^{k}(t^{n},x_{i-\frac{1}{2}},y_{j},\rho_{i-\frac{1}{2},j}^{k,n,+},\bm{A}^{n}_{i-\frac{1}{2},j})\right)\Big\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}|\partial_{x}f^{k}(t^{n},\bar{x_{i}},y_{j},\rho_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}^{k,n,-},\bar{\bm{A}}^{n}_{i,j})\Delta x|

\displaystyle}{\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\left(\lVert\nabla_{A}f^{k}(t^{n},\bar{x_{i}},y_{j},\rho_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}^{k,n,-},\bar{\bm{A}}_{i,j}^{n})\rVert\lVert\bm{A}^{n}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}-\bm{A}^{n}_{i-\frac{1}{2},j}\rVert\right)

\displaystyle}{\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}|\partial_{x}f^{k}(t^{n},\tilde{x}_{i},y_{j},\rho_{i-\frac{1}{2},j}^{k,n,+},\tilde{\bm{A}}^{n}_{i,j})\Delta x|

\displaystyle}{\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\left(\lVert\nabla_{A}f^{k}(t^{n},\tilde{x_{i}},y_{j},\rho_{i-\frac{1}{2},j}^{k,n,+},\tilde{\bm{A}}^{n}_{i,j})\rVert\lVert\bm{A}^{n}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}-\bm{A}^{n}_{i-\frac{1}{2},j}\rVert\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(M\rho_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}^{k,n,-}\Delta x\Bigl(\lVert\partial_{x}\bm{\eta}\rVert\lVert\bm{\rho}_{\Delta}(0)\rVert_{\mathrm{L}^{1}}+1\Bigr)\right)

\displaystyle}{\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\left(M\rho_{i-\frac{1}{2},j}^{k,n,+}\Delta x\Bigl(\lVert\partial_{x}\bm{\eta}\rVert\lVert\bm{\rho}_{\Delta}(0)\rVert_{\mathrm{L}^{1}}+1\Bigr)\right)

\displaystyle=M\rho_{i,j}^{k,n}\Delta x\left(\lVert\partial_{x}\bm{\eta}\rVert\lVert\bm{\rho}_{\Delta}(0)\rVert_{\mathrm{L}^{1}}+1\right),

where
\bar{x}_{i},\tilde{x}_{i}\in(x_{i-\frac{1}{2}},x_{i+\frac{1}{2}})
and
\bar{\bm{A}}^{n}_{i,j},\tilde{\bm{A}}^{n}_{i,j}\in\mathcal{I}(\bm{A}^{n}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j},\bm{A}^{n}_{i-\frac{1}{2},j}).

Now, in view of the estimates (
5.5
), (
5.6
) and (
6.2
), the terms
V_{ij}^{k,(1)}
in (
5.4
) can be bounded as

\displaystyle|V_{ij}^{k,(1)}|

\displaystyle\leq\Bigl(1-a^{k,n}_{i-\frac{1}{2},j}-b^{k,n}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}\Big)\lvert\rho_{i,j}^{k,n}\rvert+a^{k,n}_{i-\frac{1}{2},j}\lvert\rho_{i-1,j}^{k,n}\rvert+b^{k,n}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}\lvert\rho_{i+1,j}^{k,n}\rvert

(6.3)

\displaystyle+\bar{\lambda}_{x}\Big\lvert F^{k,n}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}(\rho_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}^{k,n,-},\rho_{i-\frac{1}{2},j}^{k,n,+})-F^{k,n}_{i-\frac{1}{2},j}(\rho_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}^{k,n,-},\rho_{i-\frac{1}{2},j}^{k,n,+})\Big\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\rho^{k}_{\Delta}(t^{n})\rVert\Bigl(1+2M\Delta t\Bigl(\lVert\partial_{x}\bm{\eta}\rVert\lVert\bm{\rho}_{\Delta}(0)\rVert_{\mathrm{L}^{1}}+1\Bigr)\Bigr).

An analogous argument for
W_{ij}^{k,(1)}
yields

\displaystyle|W_{ij}^{k,(1)}|\leq\lVert\rho^{k}_{\Delta}(t^{n})\rVert\Bigl(1+2M\Delta t\Bigl(\lVert\partial_{y}\bm{\nu}\rVert\lVert\bm{\rho}_{\Delta}(0)\rVert_{\mathrm{L}^{1}}+1\Bigr)\Bigr).

(6.4)

Therefore, using the bounds (
6.3
) and (
6.4
) in (
5.1
), it follows that

\displaystyle|\rho^{k,(1)}_{ij}|

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\rho^{k}_{\Delta}(t^{n})\rVert\Bigl(1+2M\Delta t\Bigl(\max\{\lVert\partial_{x}\bm{\eta}\rVert,\lVert\partial_{y}\bm{\nu}\rVert\}\lVert\bm{\rho}_{\Delta}(0)\rVert_{\mathrm{L}^{1}}+1\Bigr)\Bigr).

Similar arguments for the second forward Euler step (
4.6
) give us the estimate

\displaystyle|\rho^{k,(2)}_{ij}|

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\rho^{k,(1)}_{\Delta}\rVert\Bigl(1+2M\Delta t\Bigl(\max\{\lVert\partial_{x}\bm{\eta}\rVert,\lVert\partial_{y}\bm{\nu}\rVert\}\lVert\bm{\rho}_{\Delta}(0)\rVert_{\mathrm{L}^{1}}+1\Bigr)\Bigr)

(6.5)

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\rho^{k}_{\Delta}(t^{n})\rVert\Bigl(1+2M\Delta t\Bigl(\max\{\lVert\partial_{x}\bm{\eta}\rVert,\lVert\partial_{y}\bm{\nu}\rVert\}\lVert\bm{\rho}_{\Delta}(0)\rVert_{\mathrm{L}^{1}}+1\Bigr)\Bigr)^{2}.

Finally, in light of the estimate (
6.5
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\lvert\rho_{ij}^{k,n+1}\rvert

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\lvert\rho_{ij}^{k,n}\rvert+\lvert\rho_{ij}^{k,(2)}\rvert)

(6.6)

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\rho^{k}_{\Delta}(t^{n})\rVert\Bigl(1+2M\Delta t\Bigl(\max\{\lVert\partial_{x}\bm{\eta}\rVert,\lVert\partial_{y}\bm{\nu}\rVert\}\lVert\bm{\rho}_{\Delta}(0)\rVert_{\mathrm{L}^{1}}+1\Bigr)\Bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\rho^{k}_{\Delta}(t^{n-1})\rVert\Bigl(1+2M\Delta t\Bigl(\max\{\lVert\partial_{x}\bm{\eta}\rVert,\lVert\partial_{y}\bm{\nu}\rVert\}\lVert\bm{\rho}_{\Delta}(0)\rVert_{\mathrm{L}^{1}}+1\Bigr)\Bigr)^{4}

\displaystyle\vdots

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\rho^{k}_{\Delta}(0)\rVert\Bigl(1+2M\Delta t\Bigl(\max\{\lVert\partial_{x}\bm{\eta}\rVert,\lVert\partial_{y}\bm{\nu}\rVert\}\lVert\bm{\rho}_{\Delta}(0)\rVert_{\mathrm{L}^{1}}+1\Bigr)\Bigr)^{2(n+1)}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\bm{\rho}_{\Delta}(0)\rVert e^{Ct},

for
t=(n+1)\Delta t,
where

\displaystyle C:=4M\Bigl(1+\max\{\lVert\partial_{x}\bm{\eta}\rVert,\lVert\partial_{y}\bm{\nu}\rVert\}\lVert\bm{\rho}_{\Delta}(0)\rVert_{\mathrm{L}^{1}}\Bigr).
The estimate (
6.6
) completes the proof.
∎

the
first- and second-order schemes for two-dimensional non-local conservation laws.
We primarily consider two types of test cases: one involving a scalar equation and the other a system, both adhering to the framework of (
2.1
).
In all the numerical results, the time-step
\Delta t
is computed using the CFL condition (
5.3
) corresponding to the second-order scheme and the computational domain
[x_{1},x_{2}]\times[y_{1},y_{2}]
is discretized into
(n_{x}\times n_{y})
number of Cartesian cells, where the grid sizes
\displaystyle\Delta x=({x_{2}-x_{1}})/n_{x}
and
\displaystyle\Delta y=({y_{2}-y_{1}})/n_{y}.

The coefficient in (
4.2
) is set to be
\theta=0.5,
and the parameters in the numerical fluxes (

\alpha=\beta=1/6,
in all the examples.
The initial and boundary conditions are prescribed in the description of each example. Hereafter, the first-order scheme (

Example 1

In this example, we consider the two-dimensional macroscopic crowd dynamics problem studied in
aggarwal2015
, where the density
\rho
of pedestrians is modeled to evolve according to the scalar non-local conservation law:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho+\nabla\cdot(\rho(1-\rho)(1-\rho*\mu)\vec{\bm{v}})=0.

(7.1)

Here, the convolution is given by

\displaystyle\rho*\mu(t,x,y)=\int\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mu(x-x^{\prime},y-y^{\prime})\rho(t,x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}.

The smooth kernel function
\mu
quantifies the weight assigned by pedestrians to their surrounding crowd density

\vec{\bm{v}}(x,y)=(v^{1}(x,y),v^{2}(x,y))^{T}
describes the path they follow. It is evident that (
7.1
) aligns with the framework of (
2.1
) (see Lemma 3.1 in
aggarwal2015
).

We examine a scenario where two groups of individuals start from two different locations within the domain
[0,10]\times[-1,1],
move in the same direction and eventually stop at the spot
\{9.5\}\times[-1,1].
To account for this dynamics, the velocity vector field is chosen as

\displaystyle\vec{\bm{v}}(x,y)=\begin{bmatrix}(1-y^{2})^{3}\textrm{exp}(-1/(x-9.5)^{2})\chi_{(-\infty,9.5]\times[-1,1]}(x,y)\\
-2y\textrm{exp}(1-1/y^{2})\\
\end{bmatrix},

where for
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2},

\chi_{\Omega}
denotes the indicator function of
\Omega.
Further, the kernel function is defined to be of compact support in a disk of radius
r=0.4
centered at the origin:

\displaystyle\mu(x,y)=\frac{1}{\int\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\tilde{\mu}}\tilde{\mu}(x,y),

(7.2)

where

\displaystyle\tilde{\mu}(x,y)=(0.16-x^{2}-y^{2})^{3}\chi_{\{(x,y):x^{2}+y^{2}\leq 0.16\}}(x,y).

Note that the kernel function
\mu
in (
7.2
) attains a global maximum at the origin
(0,0)
and decreases radially, reflecting the idea that pedestrians prioritize nearby crowd density over distant ones.
We solve the problem (
7.1
) with the initial datum:

\displaystyle\rho^{0}(x,y)=\chi_{[1,4]\times[0.1,0.8]}(x,y)+\chi_{[2,5]\times[-0.8,-0.1]}(x,y),

given in Fig.

,
along with ‘no flow’  boundary conditions
on all the boundaries of the domain.
Throughout this example,
based on the CFL condition (
5.3
) for the SO scheme, we set a common time-step for both the FO and SO schemes,
\Delta t=0.026\Delta x.

This time-step is computed from (
5.3

\lVert\partial_{\rho}f\rVert,\lVert\partial_{\rho}g\rVert\leq 2,
in this example.
The numerical solutions are computed in the domain
[0,10]\times[-1,1].

In Fig.

computed using both the FO and SO schemes with the same initial data as in (

\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
-stability, thus confirming the theoretical results.

\displaystyle=0,

(7.4)

Example 4

In this example, we consider the behavior of solutions of the non-local Keyfitz-Kranzer model (
7.4
) as the radius of the convolution kernels approaches zero, which is equivalent to the convolution kernels converging to the Dirac delta distribution.
This problem, known as the ‘singular limit problem’  has been investigated numerically in
amorim2015
;
aggarwal2015
, and theoretical results have been established for specific cases in
coclite2020
;
colombocrippa2023
;
colombocrippa2019
. However, analytical convergence results for the general case remain an open problem.

It is desirable that numerical schemes that approximate non-local models retain their robustness under variations in model parameters. A recent study in this direction is available in
kuang2024
. In view of this, we investigate the behavior of both the FO and SO schemes for the singular limit problem, where the local version of the Keyfitz-Kranzer system is given by:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho^{1}+\partial_{x}(\rho^{1}\varphi^{1}(\rho^{1},\rho^{2}))+\partial_{y}(\rho^{1}\varphi^{2}(\rho^{1},\rho^{2}))

\displaystyle=0,

(7.6)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho^{2}+\partial_{x}(\rho^{2}\varphi^{1}(\rho^{1},\rho^{2}))+\partial_{y}(\rho^{2}\varphi^{2}(\rho^{1},\rho^{2}))

\displaystyle=0.

r\in\{0.04,0.02,\\
0.01,0.005,0.0025\}
across different time levels
t\in\{0.03,0.07,0.1\}.

\mathrm{L}^{1}
distance between the solutions corresponding to the non-local (

) and local (

(1600\times 1600)

Conclusion

In this work, we propose a fully discrete second-order scheme for a general system of non-local conservation laws in multiple dimensions. The resulting scheme is theoretically shown to satisfy the positivity-preserving property and proven to be
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
-stable. Numerical experiments clearly indicate the superiority of the SO scheme over its first-order counterpart, as illustrated in Figs.
3
and
5
for both scalar and system cases. We have also shown the numerical convergence of the SO scheme in the scalar case and compared
it
to
that of
the FO scheme, see Table
1
. The robustness of the SO scheme is further evaluated in the context of the ‘singular limit problem’  and the results show that the SO scheme solutions approach the local problem as the parameter
r
tends to zero, with a higher convergence rate compared to that of FO scheme, as is evident from Table
2
.
Additionally, we wish to note that a key challenge in analyzing the theoretical convergence of the second-order scheme lies in deriving a bounded variation estimate. To the best of our knowledge, such estimates are unavailable in the literature for the case of local conservation laws as well, except
for the weak-BV estimates
in
Coquel1991
. We plan to address these theoretical aspects in a future work.

Flourescent Based Camera (QSCAN Plus) for Dental Plaque Quantification: Validation and Reliability
Authors: Antarmayee Panigrahi, Snigdha Pattanaik, Noorjahan Mohammad, Swati Patnaik, Subhrajeet N. Sahoo, Debarchita Sarangi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTIntroduction:Detection and quantification of dental plaque is crucial in both clinical dentistry and research. Established clinical indices used to measure dental plaque have limitations. The aim of the study was to evaluate the reliability and validity of plaque quantification using a fluorescent camera (QScan Plus).Materials and Methods:Double doubleblind study was carried out on 20 children of age ranging from 9 to 14 yrs. Plaque measurement was performed by two examiners on a total of 160 incisors at the base, 24, 36, 48, and 96 hrs. Images were captured by the fluorescent camera, digitally analyzed, and scored. Another scoring was done using Addy Plaque Index area after plaque disclosure. The average scores were used for statistical analysis.Results:The results were within the excellent reliability category for interoperator and intraoperator repeatability.Conclusion:This new system provides an improved objective method of quantifying dental plaque areas with increased accuracy.

INTRODUCTION

Dental caries still remain as challenge for modern advanced practice, as the most prevalent disease affecting all age groups resulting from demineralisation of tooth structure by the production of acid by microorganisms present in plaque. Dental plaque acts as a double-edged sword that harbors the bacteria causing the damage and also can aid in maintaining a high concentration of caries preventive agents. With decades of research, it has been well established that dental caries is plaque mediated disease and thus every preventive approach is now targeted at early identification, quantification, and reduction of dental plaque.[1]

Most widely using clinically scoring methods of plaque gives no area coverage and are subjective. Addy et al. 1983 introduced a method for quantification by drawing outlines and related grids. Planimetric methods by Clayden and Addy 1995 and the Navy index by Elliot et al. 1972, Rustogi et al. 1992, were based mainly on the ability of clinicians to demarcate the dye disclosed area thus lacking reliability and inter-operators differences. These methods can therefore not be used for research purposes.[2345]

Methods based on image analysis systems like CMOS (complementary metal oxide semiconductor) digital cameras and CCD (charge-coupled device) based cameras were also introduced in the past but resulted in images lacking details and resolution.[6]

The recent emergence of digital imaging techniques has given us direction to explore and improvise early detection as well as intercept dental plaque. Quantifying plaque is important for patients, clinicians, as well as researchers. Going beyond mere detection of incipient caries, QLF (Quantitative Light Fluorescence) is a promising new technique for plaque measurements and quantification. Recent studies have demonstrated that plaque fluoresces red when it is excited by light with a wavelength of 350–420 nm emitted from the QLF device.

A novel fluorescence camera (FC) using a similar wavelength (QScan Plus) has been recently introduced into the market. However, there is sparse literature available on its clinical applicability.[7] Thus the present study was taken to evaluate the sensitivity of fluorescent cameras for plaque quantification and to validate in comparison to established plaque scoring methods.

METHODOLOGY

This present double-blinded study was conducted among 20 children of a residential school, in Bhubaneswar aged 9–14 years of age. Institutional ethical clearance and informed consent were taken before the study and were conducted under the supervision of two examiners.

Anterior teeth were disclosed using Erythrosin FDC Red 3 dye and assessed at the same appointment. Buccal and lingual surface plaque scores were recorded using Quighley and Hein Modification plaque Index 1962. The plaque area was delineated using Addy et al.[3] plaque area index 1983 after plaque disclosure.

Fluorescent images of plaque were recorded using Qscan Plus, with and without disclosure. The obtained images were processed using Image ProPlus software (ver 4.0, Datacell Ltd, UK) using the ‘Spatial Calibration’ to calculate several pixels and teeth pixels in the image to derive plaque percentage area, along with tooth/plaque perimeter.

The digitally processed images were then scored and tabulated. The assessment of plaque was done at the baseline, 24 h, 36 h, 48 h, and 96 h. To evaluate the inter- and intraoperator reliability, two operators were assessed on two separate occasions on each incisor and subjected to Fliess’ Coefficient of Reliability (1986) test.

RESULTS

Reliability

The present method under study, the Fluorescent image analysis method could identify both single tooth and quadrant teeth plaque areas as well as specific tooth surface areas. Table 1 shows the scoring of the Fliess Coefficient of Reliability for two operators. The coefficients ranged from 0.82-0.98, indicating a very minor difference in reproducibility between examiners. Also, the intra-examiner repeatability for the plaque area between the labial and lingual tooth surface was significantly greater. The scores obtained indicate excellent reliability and objectivity [Table 1].

DISCUSSION

Various methods of assessment of dental plaque have been used extensively by clinicians as well as researchers of periodontal disease, cariology, and evaluation of preventive therapies and the efficacy of oral hygiene products. Newer methods and techniques are widely explored for better assessment and quantification of plaque with good discriminatory power and reliability; however, there is scanty literature available.[8]

Image analysis methods using fluorescent-based images have proven to be advantageous for plaque quantification. Results obtained from the present study show that fluorescent image analysis is a reliable and reproducible plaque method. The inter- and intra-examiner reliability results showed an excellent Fleiss coefficient. This proves to be a more predictable method for plaque research databases. The results showed higher sensitivity at each interval enabling recording minor changes in plaque development.[9]

In 2010, Raggio et al.[10] conducted an in-situ study to evaluate the reliability and discriminatory power of dental plaque quantification methods and concluded Turesky Index and plaque percentage using images from fluorescent cameras have good reliability and could discriminate plaque development precisely.

Han et al. 2015[11] conducted a study on 600 sound anterior teeth of 50 subjects and the examination included the Turesky index and Sillness and Loe index, to evaluate the reliability of autofluorescence-based plaque quantification. The results obtained showed excellent reliability and fair validity.

Thus within the limitations of the study, it can be concluded that combining planimetric methods with fluorescent image analysis can provide reliable and reproducible plaque quantification data. Also, it can be performed quickly and thus can be widely used in clinical trials, and field studies as well as for patient education and motivation. The images obtained can also be stored for future reference thereby creating a resource database.

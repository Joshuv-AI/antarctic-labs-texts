Rigidity of the gradient estimate for Einstein manifolds
Sanghoon Lee, Jiewon Park

Abstract
. We study the rigidity of Ricci-flat manifolds with quadratic curvature decay under conditions on the Green function. We show that if the gradient of the Green function is uniformly bounded from below, then the manifold is flat. Furthermore, we prove that for a Ricci-flat manifold with quadratic curvature decay and Euclidean volume growth, the curvature is in L p L^{p} for any p ≥ 2 p\geq 2 . Combining with Cheeger-Tian [ CT ] and Kröncke-Szabó [ KS ] , we obtain that the manifold must be ALE of optimal order.



1.
Introduction

We consider a complete nonparabolic Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
,
n\geq 3
, and fix a point
p\in M
. Let
G(p,\cdot)
denote the minimal positive Green function with pole at
p
. Following Colding
[
C
]
, we introduce the renormalized function

b(x):=G(p,x)^{\frac{1}{2-n}}.

A direct computation shows that
b
satisfies the identity

\Delta b^{2}=2n|\nabla b|^{2}.

Since
b
behaves like the distance function from
p
(on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
one has
b(x)=|x-p|
), it plays an important role in the study of rigidity phenomena and geometric inequalities on manifolds with Ricci curvature bounds.

In
[
C
, Theorem 3.1]
, Colding established the following sharp gradient estimate for
b
.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
C
, Theorem 3.1]
)
.

If moreover
\mathrm{Ric}\geq 0
on
M
, then

(1.1)

|\nabla b|\leq 1,

with equality at one point implying that M is isometric to the flat Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

A natural question is whether one can obtain rigidity under a complementary lower bound on
|\nabla b|
. In this paper we address this question under additional structural assumptions on
(M,g)
. Our first result shows that, for Ricci-flat manifolds with quadratic curvature decay, a uniform positive lower bound for
|\nabla b|
forces flatness. Throughout we write
d(x,y)
for the Riemannian distance between
x,y\in M
.

Theorem 1.2
.

For each
\delta\in(0,1)
and dimension
n\geq 3
, there exists a positive constant
C(n,\delta)
with the following significance.

Let
(M,g)
be an
n
-dimensional nonparabolic Ricci-flat Riemannian manifold and
p\in M
a fixed point. Suppose that

|Rm|(x)\leq\frac{C(n,\delta)}{d(p,x)^{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad|\nabla b|(x)\geq\delta

for all
x\in M
. Then
M
is flat.

Remark 1.3
.

The constant
C(n,\delta)
can be explicitly computed, as our proofs do not rely on any compactness argument.

When
M
has Euclidean volume growth, i.e. when
v_{M}:=\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{\mathrm{vol}(B_{p}(r))}{\omega_{n}r^{n}}>0

where
\omega_{n}
denotes the volume of the unit
n
-ball in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, we obtain the following rigidity result by combining with works of Colding-Minicozzi
[
CM1
,
CM2
]
.

Theorem 1.4
.

For each dimension
n\geq 3
, there exists a positive constant
C(n)
with the following significance.

Let
(M,g)
be an
n
-dimensional nonparabolic Ricci-flat Riemannian manifold with Euclidean volume growth, and
p\in M
a fixed point. Suppose that

|Rm|(x)\leq\frac{C(n)}{d(p,x)^{2}}

for all
x\in M
. Then
\int_{M}|Rm|^{p}<\infty
for any
p\geq 2
.

Combining Theorem
1.4
with the results of Bando–Kasue–Nakajima
[
BKN
]
, Cheeger–Tian
[
CT
]
, and Kröncke–Szabó
[
KS
]
, we immediately obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 1.5
.

Under the same assumptions as in Theorem
1.4
,
M\setminus K
is diffeomorphic to
\left({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash B_{0}(R)\right)/\Gamma
for some compact set
K\subset M
, where
B_{0}(R)
is the ball of radius
R
centered at
0
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Moreover,
M
is an ALE manifold of order
n
.

The key ingredients in the proof of Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.4
are monotonicity formulas tailored to Ricci-flat manifolds, given in (
2.4
) and (
3.5
). Our methods are inspired by similar techniques used by Lee-Wang
[
LW
]
in their study of Poincaré-Einstein manifolds with the flat Euclidean space as their conformal infinity.

Under the assumption of Euclidean volume growth, it is known that the pointwise decay
\lim_{d(p,x)\to\infty}|Rm|(x)=0
already implies a quadratic curvature decay
|Rm|\leq C/d^{2}
for some
C>0
; see Cheng–Zhu
[
CZ
, Theorem 1.3]
. On the other hand, the lower bound assumption on
|\nabla b|
in Theorem
1.2
essentially cannot be omitted. The Eguchi-Hanson metric provides a complete non-flat Ricci-flat manifold with Euclidean volume growth and quadratic curvature decay. There is now an abundance of ALE and AC spaces, constructed for instance in
[
K1
,
K2
,
St
,
TY
]
, which suggests that additional conditions beyond quadratic curvature decay are necessary to conclude flatness.

We conclude this introduction by mentioning that rigidity theorems for complete Einstein manifolds have been extensively studied. Regarding
L^{n/2}
curvature bounds, Anderson
[
A1
, Theorem 3.5]
proved that if
M
is a complete Ricci-flat
n
-manifold of Euclidean volume growth and

\int_{M}|Rm|^{n/2}<\infty,

then
M^{n}\setminus B_{p}(R_{0})
is diffeomorphic to
(R_{0},\infty)\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}/\Gamma
for some large
R_{0}>0
, where
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}/\Gamma
is a space form. Shen
[
Sh
]
showed that there is a constant
c(n)
so that if moreover

\int_{M}|Rm|^{n/2}\leq c(n)v_{M}^{n+1},

then
M
is isometric to
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. As for gap theorems on the volume, Anderson
[
A2
]
showed that there exists
\epsilon(n)>0
for which if the Ricci-flat manifold
M
has
v_{M}>1-\epsilon(n)
, then
M
is isometric to
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Honda-Mondino
[
HM
]
confirmed that the optimal
\epsilon(4)
is
1/2
; we refer the reader to
[
HM
]
for the proof and several interesting open questions. We also remark that Yokota
[
Y
]
proved an analogous gap theorem on gradient Ricci solitons for the Gaussian density. Our results are complementary to these works, focusing on bounds on the Green function.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we prove a model ODE lemma which captures the mechanism behind our main theorems. In Section
3
we apply the same ideas to the curvature tensor on Ricci-flat manifolds and prove Theorems
1.2
and
1.4
.

2.
An ODE lemma

The following lemma on the rigidity of quadratically decaying solutions of a linear elliptic equation serves as a model problem for our main results.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
n\geq 3
and
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a smooth function with quadratic decay, so that
\underset{B_{0}(r)}{\sup}|f|\leq\frac{C_{1}}{r^{2}}
for any
r>0
where
C_{1}\leq(n-2)^{2}/8
is a positive constant. Let
u:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a smooth solution to the equation

\Delta u=-fu.

Then
g(r)
defined by
\textstyle g(r)=\int_{0}^{r}s^{n-3}\fint_{\partial B_{s}}u^{2}\,ds
enjoys the growth estimate

(2.1)

g(s)\leq\frac{s^{x}g(r)}{r^{x}}

for any
r\geq s>0
, where
x=\left(n-2+\sqrt{(n-2)^{2}-8C_{1}}\right)/2
.
Moreover, if
n<10
or
C_{1}<2n-12
, then any quadratically decaying solution
u
so that
\underset{B_{0}(r)}{\sup}|u|\leq\frac{C_{0}}{r^{2}}
for some constant
C_{0}>0
must be identically zero.

Proof.

Let
B_{r}
denote a ball of radius
r
centered at a fixed point. Integration by parts yields

(2.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}0\leq\frac{1}{r^{n-1}}\int_{B_{r}}|Du|^{2}&=-\frac{1}{r^{n-1}}\int_{B_{r}}u\Delta u+\frac{1}{r^{n-1}}\int_{\partial B_{r}}u\partial_{r}u\\
&=\frac{1}{r^{n-1}}\int_{B_{r}}fu^{2}+\frac{1}{r^{n-1}}\int_{\partial B_{r}}u\partial_{r}u.\end{split}

The first term can be estimated by

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}fu^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{r}\underset{\partial B_{s}}{\sup}|f|\cdot|\partial B_{s}|\fint_{\partial B_{s}}u^{2}\,ds=(n+1)\omega_{n+1}\int_{0}^{r}\underset{\partial B_{s}}{\sup}|f|\cdot s^{n-1}\fint_{\partial B_{s}}u^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq(n+1)\omega_{n+1}C_{1}\int_{0}^{r}s^{n-3}\fint_{\partial B_{s}}u^{2}\,ds,

where we used that
|\partial B_{s}|=(n+1)\omega_{n+1}s^{n-1}
,
\omega_{n+1}
denoting the volume of the unit
(n+1)
-ball. Denote
g(r)
by

g(r)=\int_{0}^{r}s^{n-3}\fint_{\partial B_{s}}u^{2}\,ds.

Then
\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}fu^{2}\leq(n+1)\omega_{n+1}C_{1}g(r)
, and

g^{\prime}(r)=r^{n-3}\fint_{\partial B_{r}}u^{2}.

Differentiating gives

\displaystyle g^{\prime\prime}(r)=\frac{n-3}{r}g^{\prime}(r)+2r^{n-3}\fint_{\partial B_{r}}u\partial_{r}u.

We may now rewrite (
2.2
) to be

\displaystyle\begin{split}0&\leq\frac{1}{r^{n-1}}\int_{B_{r}}fu^{2}+\frac{|\partial B_{r}|}{r^{n-1}}\fint_{\partial B_{r}}u\partial_{r}u\\
&\leq\frac{(n+1)\omega_{n+1}C_{1}}{r^{n-1}}g(r)+\frac{(n+1)\omega_{n+1}}{2r^{n-3}}\left(g^{\prime\prime}(r)-\frac{n-3}{r}g^{\prime}(r)\right).\end{split}

which is equivalent to the following inequality,

(2.3)

\frac{(n-3)}{r}g^{\prime}(r)\leq g^{\prime\prime}(r)+\frac{2C_{1}}{r^{2}}g(r).

Let
x\geq y
be the indicial roots of the above homogeneous ODE, i.e. they solve the quadratic equation

t^{2}-(n-2)t+2C_{1}=0.

Such
x,y
exist if the inequality
(n-2)^{2}\geq 8C_{1}
holds. They are both positive and less than
(n-2)
.
Letting
h(r)=g(r)/r^{x}
, (
2.3
) is equivalent to

(2.4)

0\leq\left(\frac{h^{\prime}(r)}{r^{y-x-1}}\right)^{\prime}.

The definition of
g(r)
gives that
g(r)=O(r^{n-2})
and
g^{\prime}(r)=O(r^{n-3})
for
r\ll 1
. Therefore

\frac{h^{\prime}(r)}{r^{y-x-1}}=\frac{rg^{\prime}(r)-xg(r)}{r^{y}}\to 0\text{ as }r\to 0.

Thus
\frac{h^{\prime}(r)}{r^{y-x-1}}\geq 0
and
h^{\prime}(r)\geq 0
for all
r\geq 0
. This implies for
r\geq s>0,

g(s)\leq\frac{s^{x}g(r)}{r^{x}}.

Now if
u
also decays quadratically by
\underset{B_{0}(r)}{\sup}|u|\leq C_{0}/r^{2}
, then
g(r)=O(r^{n-6})
as
r\to\infty
for
n\neq 6
, and
g(r)=O(\log r)
as
r\to\infty
for
n=6
. Thus, we can conclude that
g\equiv 0
whenever
x=\frac{n-2}{2}+\sqrt{\left(\frac{n-2}{2}\right)^{2}-2C_{1}}>n-6
, which in turn implies that
u\equiv 0
.
∎

3.
Application to Ricci-flat manifolds

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a complete noncompact nonparabolic Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
with
\mathrm{Ric}\equiv 0
and quadratic curvature decay, i.e. there exists a constant
C_{0}>0
such that

|Rm|\leq\frac{C_{0}}{d^{2}},

where
d
denotes the distance from a fixed point
p\in M
.

Using the same notation as in the Introduction, let
G
be the minimal positive Green function on
M
, and denote by
b:M\to{\mathbb{R}}
the function

b(x)=G(p,x)^{1/(2-n)}.

Then the harmonicity of
G
is equivalent to

\Delta b^{2}=2n|\nabla b|^{2}.

Arguing along the same lines as in Lemma
2.1
, we now prove our main results.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We recall the integral identity

(3.1)

\big(r^{1-n}\int_{\{b=r\}}f|\nabla b|\big)^{\prime}=r^{1-n}\int_{\{b\leq r\}}\Delta f

which can be found, for instance, in
[
C
]
and
[
CM1
]
. Define
f:M\to{\mathbb{R}}
by

f=b^{2}|Rm|^{2}.

Applying the above identity to
f
, we obtain

(3.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\big(&r^{3-n}\int_{\{b=r\}}|Rm|^{2}|\nabla b|\big)^{\prime}=\big(r^{1-n}\int_{\{b=r\}}b^{2}|Rm|^{2}|\nabla b|\big)^{\prime}=r^{1-n}\int_{\{b\leq r\}}\Delta(b^{2}|Rm|^{2})\\
&=r^{1-n}\int_{\{b\leq r\}}\big[b^{2}\Delta|Rm|^{2}+2\nabla b^{2}\cdot\nabla|Rm|^{2}+|Rm|^{2}\Delta b^{2}\big]\\
&=r^{1-n}\int_{\{b\leq r\}}\big[b^{2}\Delta|Rm|^{2}-|Rm|^{2}\Delta b^{2}\big]+2r^{1-n}\int_{\{b\leq r\}}\big[|Rm|^{2}\Delta b^{2}+\nabla b^{2}\cdot\nabla|Rm|^{2}\big]\\
&=r^{1-n}\int_{\{b\leq r\}}\big[b^{2}\Delta|Rm|^{2}-|Rm|^{2}\Delta b^{2}\big]+2r^{1-n}\int_{\{b=r\}}|Rm|^{2}\frac{\nabla b^{2}\cdot\nabla b}{|\nabla b|}\\
&=r^{1-n}\int_{\{b\leq r\}}\big[b^{2}\Delta|Rm|^{2}-|Rm|^{2}\Delta b^{2}\big]+4r^{2-n}\int_{\{b=r\}}|Rm|^{2}|\nabla b|\\
&=r^{1-n}\int_{\{b\leq r\}}\big[b^{2}\Delta|Rm|^{2}-2n|Rm|^{2}|\nabla b|^{2}\big]+4r^{2-n}\int_{\{b=r\}}|Rm|^{2}|\nabla b|\\
&\geq r^{1-n}\int_{\{b\leq r\}}\big[-C(n)b^{2}|Rm|^{3}-2n|Rm|^{2}|\nabla b|^{2}\big]+4r^{2-n}\int_{\{b=r\}}|Rm|^{2}|\nabla b|,\end{split}

where
C(n)>0
is a dimensional constant; in the last line, we used the identity on Ricci-flat manifolds that
\Delta|Rm|^{2}=2|\nabla Rm|^{2}+Rm*Rm*Rm
, hence
\Delta|Rm|^{2}\geq-C(n)|Rm|^{3}.

Next we observe that
b\leq d
since
\lim_{d\to 0}b=0
(see for example
[
CM1
, (2.19)]
) and
|\nabla b|\leq 1
by Theorem
1.1
. Hence, from the assumption that
|Rm|\leq\frac{C_{0}}{d^{2}}
for some
C_{0}>0
, we have
|Rm|\leq\frac{C_{0}}{b^{2}}
. Together with the assumption that
|\nabla b|\geq\delta
, (
3.2
) implies

(3.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\big(r^{3-n}\int_{\{b=r\}}|Rm|^{2}|\nabla b|\big)^{\prime}\\
&\geq-r^{1-n}\int_{\{b\leq r\}}\big[\frac{C_{0}C(n)}{\delta^{2}}|Rm|^{2}|\nabla b|^{2}+2n|Rm|^{2}|\nabla b|^{2}\big]+4r^{2-n}\int_{\{b=r\}}|Rm|^{2}|\nabla b|.\end{split}

Define
F:[0,\infty)\to{\mathbb{R}}
by

F(r)=\int_{\{b\leq r\}}|Rm|^{2}|\nabla b|^{2}.

The coarea formula implies

F^{\prime}(r)=\int_{\{b=r\}}|Rm|^{2}|\nabla b|.

Thus, (
3.3
) can be rewritten in terms of
F
to be

\left(r^{3-n}F^{\prime}(r)\right)^{\prime}\geq-r^{1-n}\left(2n+\frac{C_{0}C(n)}{\delta^{2}}\right)F(r)+4r^{2-n}F^{\prime}(r),

or equivalently

(3.4)

F^{\prime\prime}(r)-\frac{(n+1)}{r}F^{\prime}(r)+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\left(2n+\frac{C_{0}C(n)}{\delta^{2}}\right)F(r)\geq 0.

Let
x\geq y
be the indicial roots of the homogeneous ODE, i.e. they solve the equation

t^{2}-(n+2)t+\left(2n+\frac{C_{0}C(n)}{\delta^{2}}\right)=0.

If
C_{0}<\frac{(n-2)^{2}\delta^{2}}{4C(n)}
, there are two positive roots; for small
C_{0}
,
x
is close to
n
and
y
is close to
2
. In particular,
y\leq(n+2)/2<n
.

We observe that defining
h(r)=F(r)/r^{x}
, (
3.4
) is equivalent to

\left(\frac{h^{\prime}(r)}{r^{y-x-1}}\right)^{\prime}\geq 0.

It follows from the definition of
F
that
F(r)=O(r^{n})
and
F^{\prime}(r)=O(r^{n-1})
for
r\ll 1
. Therefore

(3.5)

\frac{h^{\prime}(r)}{r^{y-x-1}}=\frac{rF^{\prime}(r)-xF(r)}{r^{y}}\to 0\text{ as }r\to 0.

Thus
h^{\prime}(r)\geq 0
for
r>0
, and

F(s)\leq s^{x}\cdot\frac{F(r)}{r^{x}}

whenever
r\geq s>0
. On the other hand, in fact since
|\nabla b|\leq 1
,

F(r)=\int_{\{b\leq r\}}|Rm|^{2}|\nabla b|^{2}\leq C_{0}^{2}r^{-4}\cdot\text{vol}(B_{p}(r))\leq Cr^{n-4}

by the Bishop-Gromov inequality, where
C=C(C_{0},n)
is a constant. Altogether we have

F(s)\leq Cs^{x}\cdot r^{n-4-x}.

When
C_{0}
is so small that
x=\frac{n+2}{2}+\sqrt{\left(\frac{n-2}{2}\right)^{2}-\frac{C_{0}C(n)}{\delta^{2}}}>n-4
, we can take the limit
r\rightarrow\infty
to see that
F(s)=0
for any
s>0
, thus
|Rm|\equiv 0
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

When the Ricci-flat manifold
M
has Euclidean volume growth, by
[
CM2
, Section 2.3]
the limit
\lim_{b\rightarrow\infty}|\nabla b|=\lim_{b\rightarrow\infty}b/d=:\theta
exists, where
\theta\in(0,1)
is determined by the volume growth of
M
. Thus we can choose
r_{0}
large so that
|\nabla b|\geq\theta/2
and
b/d\leq 2\theta
whenever
b\geq r_{0}
.

Since
|Rm|
fails to be smooth where
Rm=0
, we take the standard regularization
u_{\delta}:=(|Rm|^{2}+\delta^{2})^{1/2}
of
|Rm|
where
0<\delta<1
. By the improved Kato inequality for Ricci-flat manifolds (
[
BKN
, Corollary 4.10]
), there exist constants
C=C(n)
and
\epsilon=\epsilon(n)
so that
\Delta|Rm|^{1-\epsilon}\geq-C|Rm|^{2-\epsilon}
at points where
|Rm|\neq 0
, from which it follows that
\Delta(u_{\delta})^{1-\epsilon}\geq-C|Rm|(u_{\delta})^{1-\epsilon}
. On the other hand, the integral identity (
3.1
) for
u_{\delta}
yields

\big(r^{1-n}\int_{\{b=r\}}(u_{\delta})^{1-\epsilon}|\nabla b|\big)^{\prime}=r^{1-n}\int_{\{b\leq r\}}\Delta(u_{\delta})^{1-\epsilon}.

Using these facts we compute for
r\geq r_{0}
,

\displaystyle\big[r^{n-1}\big(r^{3-n}\int_{\{b=r\}}(u_{\delta})^{1-\epsilon}|\nabla b|\big)^{\prime}\big]^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\{b=r\}}\big[b^{2}\Delta(u_{\delta})^{1-\epsilon}/|\nabla b|-2n(u_{\delta})^{1-\epsilon}|\nabla b|\big]+4\big(r\int_{\{b=r\}}(u_{\delta})^{1-\epsilon}|\nabla b|\big)^{\prime}

\displaystyle\geq-\int_{\{b=r\}}\big[Cb^{2}|Rm|(u_{\delta})^{1-\epsilon}/|\nabla b|+2n(u_{\delta})^{1-\epsilon}|\nabla b|\big]+4\big(r\int_{\{b=r\}}(u_{\delta})^{1-\epsilon}|\nabla b|\big)^{\prime}

\displaystyle\geq-\int_{\{b=r\}}\big[16CC_{0}(u_{\delta})^{1-\epsilon}|\nabla b|+2n(u_{\delta})^{1-\epsilon}|\nabla b|\big]+4\big(r\int_{\{b=r\}}(u_{\delta})^{1-\epsilon}|\nabla b|\big)^{\prime}.

Define

F_{\delta}(r)=\int_{\{b=r\}}(u_{\delta})^{1-\epsilon}|\nabla b|.

Then the differential inequality above can be written as

[r^{n-1}(r^{3-n}F_{\delta}(r))^{\prime}]^{\prime}\geq(-2n-16CC_{0})F_{\delta}(r)+4(rF_{\delta}(r))^{\prime}

which is equivalent to

(3.6)

r^{2}F_{\delta}^{\prime\prime}(r)-(n-1)rF_{\delta}^{\prime}(r)+(n-1+16CC_{0})F_{\delta}(r)\geq 0.

If
C_{0}
is small (precisely,
C_{0}<(n-2)^{2}/64C
), then the indicial roots
x>y
exist and are positive, with
1<y<x<n-1
. Arguing as in the proof of Theorem
1.2
, we see that (
3.6
) implies
0\leq\big(g_{\delta}^{\prime}(r)/r^{y-x-1}\big)^{\prime}
where
g_{\delta}(r):=F_{\delta}(r)/r^{x}
. Hence, for
r\geq r_{0}
we have

\frac{g_{\delta}^{\prime}(r_{0})}{r_{0}^{y-x-1}r^{x-y+1}}\leq g_{\delta}^{\prime}(r).

Integrating on
(r,\delta^{-1/2})
, we get

g_{\delta}(r)\leq g_{\delta}(\delta^{-1/2})+\frac{g_{\delta}^{\prime}(r_{0})}{(x-y)r_{0}^{y-x-1}}\left(\delta^{\frac{x-y}{2}}-\frac{1}{r^{x-y}}\right).

Using (
3.1
) again, it is straightforward to calculate that

g_{\delta}^{\prime}(r_{0})=(n-1-x)r_{0}^{-x-1}\int_{\{b=r_{0}\}}u_{\delta}^{1-\epsilon}|\nabla b|+r_{0}^{-x}\int_{\{b\leq r_{0}\}}\Delta(u_{\delta})^{1-\epsilon}|\nabla b|,

from which it follows there exists
C(n,r_{0})
so that
|g_{\delta}^{\prime}(r_{0})|<C(n,r_{0})
for
\delta>0
small. Moreover,
\lim_{\delta\to 0}g_{\delta}(\delta^{-1/2})=0
if
C_{0}
is so small that
x>n-1-2(1-\epsilon)
. Thus taking
\delta\to 0
we obtain

(3.7)

\lim_{\delta\to 0}g_{\delta}(r)=r^{-x}\int_{\{b=r\}}|Rm|^{1-\epsilon}|\nabla b|\leq\frac{C(n,r_{0})}{(x-y)r_{0}^{y-x-1}}r^{-x+y}.

Finally we observe that

\displaystyle\int_{b\geq r_{0}}|Rm|^{2}|\nabla b|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}\int_{b=s}|Rm|^{2}|\nabla b|\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}\int_{b=s}|Rm|^{1+\epsilon}|Rm|^{1-\epsilon}|\nabla b|\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}^{1+\epsilon}\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}s^{-2-2\epsilon}\int_{b=s}|Rm|^{1-\epsilon}|\nabla b|\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{0}^{1+\epsilon}C(n,r_{0})}{(x-y)r_{0}^{y-x-1}}\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}s^{y-2-2\epsilon}\,ds,

where (
3.7
) was used in the last step. If
C_{0}
is so small that
y=\frac{n}{2}-\sqrt{\left(\frac{n-2}{2}\right)^{2}-CC_{0}}<1+2\epsilon
, then the integral above is finite. Thus we conclude that
\int_{M}|Rm|^{p}<\infty
for any
p\geq 2
by Hölder’s inequality. This proves the theorem.

∎

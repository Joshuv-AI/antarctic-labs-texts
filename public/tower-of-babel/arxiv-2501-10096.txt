A stochastic process defined via the random permutation divisors
Eugenijus Manstavičius

Abstract
The normalised partial sums of values of a nonnegative multiplicative function over divisors with appropriately restricted sizes of a random permutation from the symmetric group define trajectories of a stochastic process. We prove a functional limit theorem in the Skorokhod space when the permutations are drawn uniformly at random. Furthermore, we show that the paths of the limit process almost surely belong to the space of continuous functions on the unit interval and, exploiting the results from number-theoretical papers, we obtain rather complex formulas for the limits of joint power moments of the process.

The very idea of the problem statement goes back to the number-theoretical paper by the author and N. M. Timofeev
[

]
. The recent result by G. Bareikis and the author
[
7
]

Recall that
\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}
is a one-to-one (bijective) mapping
\sigma:\mathbb{N}_{n}\to\mathbb{N}_{n}:=\{1,\dots,n\}
. It can be represented by the table

\sigma=\binom{1\;2\;\dots\;n\,}{i_{1}\,i_{2}\,\dots\,i_{n}},

where
\sigma(r)=i_{r}
for
1\leq r\leq n
, or by the digraph
G_{\sigma}
with vertex set
V(\sigma)=\mathbb{N}_{n}
. Its components are oriented cycles. A typical cycle has a vertex set
V(\varkappa)=\{k_{1},\dots,k_{j}\}\subset\mathbb{N}_{n}
defined by

k_{1}\xrightarrow{\sigma}k_{2}\xrightarrow{\sigma}\cdots\xrightarrow{\sigma}k_{j}\xrightarrow{\sigma}k_{1}.

Using mapping multiplication,
one obtains the unique (up to the order of factors)
decomposition of
\sigma
into cycles
\varkappa_{i}
on pairwise disjoint subsets
V(\varkappa_{i})
, namely,

\sigma=\varkappa_{1}\cdots\varkappa_{w}.

(1)

Here and in what follows

w=w(\sigma)
denotes the number of cycles and
\mathbb{N}_{n}=V(\varkappa_{1})\cup\cdots\cup V(\varkappa_{w})
. Let us also introduce the empty permutation
\emptyset
and
\mathbb{S}_{0}=\{\emptyset\}
.
A subset
\delta\subset\{\varkappa_{1},\dots,\varkappa_{w}\}
, including the empty one,
is a
divisor
of
\sigma
. Being used to the product expression
(
1
), we use the notation
\delta|\sigma
rather than
\delta\subset\sigma
.
Let
k_{j}(\delta)
be the number of cycles in
\delta
of length
j
if
1\leq j\leq n
.
The vector

\bar{k}(\delta):=\big(k_{1}(\delta),\dots,k_{n}(\delta)\big)

will be called the
cycle vector
of
\delta
. Observe that
0\leq k_{j}(\delta)\leq k_{j}(\sigma)
for each
j\leq n
if
\delta|\sigma
.
If
\ell(\bar{s}):=1s_{1}+\cdots+ns_{n}
for a vector
\bar{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
, then

\ell\big(\bar{k}(\delta)\big)=\#V(\delta)=:|\delta|
is the
size
of
\delta
.

A construction of the processes we are interested in is based on the
multiplicative functions

q:\mathbb{S}_{n}\to\mathbb{R}
. For our present purpose, the definition has to be consistently extended for the divisors of
\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}
as well. Thus, we begin with a family of functions
q_{j}:\mathbb{Z}_{+}\to\mathbb{C}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
, such that

q_{j}(0)=1
for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
, and put

q(\delta)=\prod_{j\leq n}q_{j}(k_{j}(\delta)),\quad\delta|\sigma,\;\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}.

(2)

We can say that
q_{j}(s)
,
0\leq s\leq n
, is the function value prescribed to an
s
-subset of cycles of length
j
from a permutation divisor
\delta
. If
q_{j}(k)=q_{j}(1)^{k}
for each
j\leq n
and
k\geq 0
, the function
q
will be called
completely multiplicative
.
Note that the multiplicative functions are
structure dependent
, that is, their values
q(\delta)
depend only on the vector
\bar{k}(\delta)
irrespective of the vertex labels.
In the sequel, the multiplicative functions
f,h
, and
g
will have expressions as in (
2
) with

f_{j}(\cdot),h_{j}(\cdot)
, and
g_{j}(\cdot)
, respectively.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}
and
{\mathcal{M}}_{c}
denote the classes of multiplicative and completely multiplicative functions defined as in (
2
) for
\delta|\sigma
, where
\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}
.
Given
g,h\in{\mathcal{M}}
, one can introduce the convolution

q(\sigma)=\sum_{\sigma=\delta\tau}g(\delta)h(\tau)=\sum_{\delta|\sigma}g(\delta)h(\sigma/\delta).

Here the first summation is over the ordered decompositions of
\sigma
into the product of divisors. Observe also that the relation

q_{j}(k)=\sum_{s=0}^{k}\binom{k}{s}g_{j}(s)h_{j}(k-s),\quad j\leq n,

(3)

holds. Indeed, for each
j\leq n
, any
\delta|\sigma
has an
s
-subset,
0\leq s\leq k_{j}(\sigma)=:k
,
of the cycles of length
j
from
\sigma
. The subset can be chosen
in
\binom{k}{s}
ways and, moreover, the values of
g
on these subsets coincide, as do the values of
h
on the
(k-s)
-subsets of the remaining cycles of the same length. Note also that the classes
{\mathcal{M}}
and
{\mathcal{M}}_{c}
are closed under convolution. The recalled toolkit will be used in what follows.

Given a nonnegative
g\in{\mathcal{M}}
, we define the multiplicative function

f(\sigma):=\sum_{\delta|\sigma}g(\delta),

(4)

and the family of cumulative distribution functions supported by
[0,1]
:

X_{n}(\sigma,t):=X_{n}(\sigma,t;g)=\frac{1}{f(\sigma)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\delta|\sigma\\
|\delta|\leq tn\end{subarray}}g(\delta),\quad\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n},\;0\leq t\leq 1.

(5)

In particular,

X_{n}(\sigma,t;1)=2^{-w(\sigma)}\#\big\{M\subset\mathbb{N}_{n}:\;\#M\leq tn,\;\sigma(M)=M\big\},\quad\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n},\;0\leq t\leq 1,

is the relative density of the
\sigma
-invariant subsets
M\subset\mathbb{N}_{n}
with cardinality not exceeding
tn
. Indeed,
each divisor
\delta|\sigma
uniquely determines the vertex set
V(\delta)
which is
\sigma
-invariant.

Let
\nu_{n}
denote the uniform probability measure (Haar) on
\mathbb{S}_{n}
and
{\mathbf{E}}_{n}
be the expectation with respect to it. The henceforth used facts about uniformly sampled permutations can be found in the book
[
1
]
. We recall just relation (1.3) from page 11; namely,

\nu_{n}\big(\bar{k}(\sigma)=\bar{s}\big)={\mathbf{1}}\{\ell(\bar{s})=n\}\prod_{j\leq n}\frac{1}{j^{s_{j}}s_{j}!},

showing the dependence type of the coordinates of cycle vector. With respect to
\nu_{n}
, the process
X_{n}:=X_{n}(\sigma,t)
is fairly mysterious. On the other hand, the monotonicity of its trajectories makes the problem a bit easier to handle. The paper
[
7
]
witnesses asymptotic regularity of its mean value as
n\to\infty
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\vartheta
be a positive constant and
g\in{\mathcal{M}}_{c}
be defined by
g_{j}(1)=\vartheta
, where
j\leq n
. Then, uniformly in
0\leq t\leq 1
,

{\mathbf{E}}_{n}X_{n}(t)=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}}X_{n}(\sigma,t;g)=B\big(t;\theta,1-\theta\big)+O(n^{-\min\{\theta,1-\theta\}}),

where
\theta:=\vartheta/(1+\vartheta)
and

B(t;a,b)=\frac{\Gamma(a+b)}{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{{\rm d}v}{v^{a}(1-v)^{b}},

for
0\leq t\leq 1
and
0<a,b<1
, is the two-parameter beta distribution function.

In the present paper, we focus on the distribution of
X_{n}
defined by

P_{n}(A):=\nu_{n}\cdot X_{n}^{-1}(A)=\nu_{n}\big(\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}:\;X_{n}(\sigma,\cdot)\in A\big),

(6)

where
A\in\mathcal{D}
. Formulating the result, we take into account the Addendum
[
22
]
to the paper
[
18
]
by G. Tenenbaum.

Theorem 2
.

Let
g\in{\mathcal{M}}_{c}
be defined by
g_{j}(1)=\vartheta
, where
\vartheta
is a positive constant and
j\geq 1
. The sequence of distributions
P_{n}
converges weakly as
n\to\infty
to a limiting measure
P
supported by a subset of
\mathbb{C}[0,1]
.

Since
0\leq X_{n}(t)\leq 1
, by the dominated convergence theorem, the weak convergence
of measures
P_{n}\Rightarrow P
implies convergence of the moments.

Corollary 1
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
2
, for a fixed
l\in\mathbb{N}
and uniformly in
\bar{t}:=(t_{1},\dots,t_{l})\in[0,1]^{l}
, the following relation for the mixed moments holds:

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathbf{E}}_{n}\Big(\prod_{i\leq l}X_{n}(t_{i})\Big)=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\prod_{i\leq l}\varphi(t_{i}){\rm d}P(\varphi)=:E(l,\bar{t}).

(7)

If

without the remainder term estimate.
As Proposition

Lemmata

From the general asymptotic theory on the mean values of multiplicative functions, nowadays having a vast literature, we will use the following result.

Lemma 1
.

If
q\in{\mathcal{M}}_{c}
satisfies
0\leq q_{j}(1)\leq 1
for
j\leq n
, then

{\mathbf{E}}_{n}q\leq\big({\rm e}^{\gamma}+O(n^{-1})\big)\exp\bigg\{\sum_{j\leq n}\frac{q_{j}(1)-1}{j}\bigg\}.

Here
\gamma
denotes the Euler–Mascheroni constant.

Proof.

See
[
16
]
. This paper also exposes a possibility to substitute
{\rm e}^{\gamma}
by a smaller quantity.∎

If
1\leq r\leq m
and
m\geq 2
are arbitrary integers, then we can uniquely decompose
\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{m}
into the product
\sigma=\sigma^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime\prime}
, where
\sigma^{\prime}
is the so-called

-

(smooth) and
\sigma^{\prime\prime}
is

-

divisor. Namely,
\sigma^{\prime}
comprises all cycles in
\sigma
whose lengths do not exceed
r
, while
\sigma^{\prime\prime}
contains the remaining ones. Counting such divisors, we will reduce the task to enumerate the
r
-friable and the
r
-free permutations in
\mathbb{S}_{r}
where

. Now we present a few known results on that.

As usual, let
\rho\colon[0,\infty[\to]0,1]
denote the Dickman–de Bruijn function defined as
\rho(u)=1
for
[0,1]
and, for the rest of its range, by the delay differential equation
u\rho^{\prime}(u)+\rho(u-1)=0
. Recall (see, e.g. Section 5.4 in
[
21
]
) that
\rho(u)=u^{-u+o(u)}
as

.

Lemma 2
.

If
1\leq r\leq m
,
m\geq 2
, and
u:=m/r
, then

\nu_{m}\big(\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{m}:\;\sigma\,\,\text{is}\,\,\text{r-friable}\big)=\rho(u)\Big(1+O\Big(\frac{u\log(u+1)}{r}\Big)\Big).

Proof.

This is Proposition 1.8 in
[
13
]
extending the asymptotic formula valid for
\sqrt{m\log(m+1)}\leq r\leq m
established in the paper
[
17
]
written jointly with R. Petuchovas.
∎

Let
\omega\colon[0,\infty[\to]0,1]
denote the Buchstab’s function defined as
\omega(u)=1/u
for
[1,2]
and by the delay differential equation
(u\omega(u))^{\prime}=\omega(u-1)
for

. Theorem 4 on page 402 in
[

gives that

.

Lemma 3

If
1\leq r\leq m
and

\nu_{m}\big(\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{m}:\;\sigma\,\,\text{is}\,\,\text{r-free}\big)=\exp\Big\{-\sum_{j\leq r}\frac{1}{j}\Big\}\Big({\rm e}^{\gamma}\omega(u)+O(r^{-1})\Big).

Proof.

This is Theorem 3 from the author’s paper
[
15
]
. See
[

]
and

for a state-of-the-art survey on enumeration of the

3
Proof of Theorem
2

We split the
proof
into three parts.

3.1
Long cycles are essential

Let us discover the role of the divisors having long cycles.
If
0<\varepsilon<1
, we let

\sigma^{\prime}(\varepsilon)=\prod_{\varkappa|\sigma\atop|\varkappa|\leq\varepsilon n}\varkappa,\qquad\sigma(\varepsilon)=\prod_{\varkappa|\sigma\atop\varepsilon n<|\varkappa|\leq n}\varkappa

denote the
(\varepsilon n)
-friable and
(\varepsilon n)
-free divisors, respectively. Introduce the process

X_{n}(\sigma(\varepsilon),t)=\frac{1}{f(\sigma(\varepsilon))}\sum_{\delta|\sigma(\varepsilon)\atop|\delta|\leq tn}g(\delta),\quad 0\leq t\leq 1.

For brevity, let
\alpha=(\log(1/\varepsilon))^{1/2}
.

Proposition 1
.

There exist absolute positive constants
c_{0}
,
\varepsilon_{0}
and
C
such that

\nu_{n}\big(X_{n}(\sigma,t)\not=X_{n}(\sigma(\varepsilon),t)\big)\leq C\alpha^{-1}

uniformly in
\varepsilon^{c}\leq t\leq 1-\varepsilon^{c}
, provided that
0<c<c_{0}
,
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
and
n\geq n_{0}(\varepsilon)
.

Proof.

We adopt the original arguments used in the number-theoretical paper
[
18
]
. We may start with
\varepsilon_{0}\leq{\rm e}^{-1}
and
n_{0}(\varepsilon)>\varepsilon^{-1}\geq{\rm e}
and refine the choice in the proof process.

Each
\delta|\sigma^{\prime}(\varepsilon)\sigma(\varepsilon)
splits into a product of two divisors
\delta=\delta_{1}\delta_{2}
such that

\delta_{1}|\sigma^{\prime}(\varepsilon)
and
\delta_{2}|\sigma(\varepsilon)
. Hence

\displaystyle X_{n}(\sigma,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{f(\sigma^{\prime}(\varepsilon))}\sum_{\delta_{1}|\sigma^{\prime}(\varepsilon)}g(\delta_{1})\cdot\frac{1}{f(\sigma(\varepsilon))}\sum_{\delta_{2}|\sigma(\varepsilon)\atop|\delta_{2}|\leq nt}g(\delta_{2})

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{f(\sigma)}\sum_{\delta_{1}|\sigma^{\prime}(\varepsilon)}g(\delta_{1})\sum_{\delta_{2}|\sigma(\varepsilon)\atop tn-|\delta_{1}|<|\delta_{2}|\leq nt}g(\delta_{2})

\displaystyle=:X_{n}(\sigma(\varepsilon),t)-Y_{n}(\sigma,t).

The largest
(\varepsilon n)
-friable divisor of
\sigma
is
\sigma^{\prime}(\varepsilon)
.
Observe that the subset of
\sigma
having comparatively large
\sigma^{\prime}(\varepsilon)
is sparse. Indeed,

\displaystyle\nu_{n}\big(\sigma:\;|\sigma^{\prime}(\varepsilon)|>\varepsilon\alpha n\big)

\displaystyle=\nu_{n}\bigg(\sum_{j\leq\varepsilon n}jk_{j}(\sigma)>\varepsilon\alpha n\bigg)\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon\alpha n}\sum_{j\leq\varepsilon n}j{\mathbf{E}}_{n}k_{j}(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon\alpha n}\lfloor{\varepsilon}n\rfloor\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}.

Hence

\begin{split}\nu_{n}\big(Y_{n}(\sigma,t)\not=0\big)&\leq\alpha^{-1}+\nu_{n}\bigg(\sigma:\;|\sigma^{\prime}(\varepsilon)|\leq\varepsilon\alpha n,\,\exists\delta_{2}|\sigma(\varepsilon),\,tn-|\sigma^{\prime}(\varepsilon)|<|\delta_{2}|\leq tn\bigg)\\
&=:\alpha^{-1}+\mu_{n}(t).\end{split}

(8)

Let us focus on the
\sigma
’s counted in
\mu_{n}(t)
. If
\sigma(\varepsilon)=\delta_{2}\delta_{2}^{\prime}
, then solving the inequalities between the parentheses in
\mu_{n}(t)
, by virtue of

|\delta_{2}|+|\delta^{\prime}_{2}|=n-|\sigma^{\prime}(\varepsilon)|,

we have

(t-\varepsilon\alpha)n\leq|\delta_{2}|\leq tn,\qquad(1-t-\varepsilon\alpha)n\leq|\delta^{\prime}_{2}|\leq(1-t)n

(9)

provided that
\varepsilon\alpha\leq t\leq 1-\varepsilon\alpha
.
Now, it is essential to verify that at least one of such
\delta_{2}
or
\delta^{\prime}_{2}
has a comparatively small number of cycles. By the definition of the number-of-cycles function,

w(\sigma(\varepsilon))=\sum_{\varepsilon n<j\leq n}k_{j}(\sigma).

The effective inequality for the second moment of an arbitrary completely additive function has been established by J. Klimavičius and the author in
[
14
]
. For the particular case, we have

\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}}\big(w(\sigma(\varepsilon))-h(\varepsilon n,n)\big)^{2}=h(\varepsilon n,n)-\sum_{\varepsilon n<i,j\leq n\atop i+j>n}\frac{1}{ij}\leq h(\varepsilon n,n),\quad n\geq 2,

(10)

where

h(y,x):=\sum_{y<j\leq x}1/j
satisfying the inequality
\big|h(y,x)-\log(x/y)\big|\leq 1/y
.
Applying Chebyshev’s inequality and (
10
), we obtain

\nu_{n}\big(w(\sigma(\varepsilon))>(3/2)\alpha^{2}\big)\leq\nu_{n}\big(w(\sigma(\varepsilon))-h(\varepsilon n,n)>\alpha^{2}/4\big)\leq 32\alpha^{-2}

(11)

if
n\geq\varepsilon^{-1}\alpha^{-2}
. Thus, for all but
O(n!\alpha^{-2})
permutations
\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}
, one of the above
\delta_{2}
or
\delta^{\prime}_{2}
has no more than
(3/4)\alpha^{2}
cycles.
In either case, for the most of
\sigma
counted in the frequency
\mu_{n}(t)
, we obtain a decomposition
\sigma=\delta\tau(\varepsilon)
, where
\tau(\varepsilon)
is
(\varepsilon n)
-free and, with
\delta(\varepsilon)=\delta_{2}
or
\delta(\varepsilon)=\delta^{\prime}_{2}
, the divisor
\delta:=\delta(\varepsilon)\sigma^{\prime}(\varepsilon)
belongs to the set

\Delta_{t}:=\big\{\delta|\sigma:\;w(\delta(\varepsilon))\leq(3/4)\alpha^{2},\,(t-\varepsilon\alpha)n\leq|\delta|\leq(t+\varepsilon\alpha)n\big\}

for
t
(or
(1-t)
) from
[\varepsilon\alpha,\,1-\varepsilon\alpha]
, as indicated in (
9
). In the shorter interval for

, we have to establish the uniform estimate. Therefore, we proceed with

\mu_{n}:=\max_{\varepsilon^{c}\leq t\leq 1-\varepsilon^{c}}\mu_{n}(t)\ll\alpha^{-2}+\max_{\varepsilon^{c}\leq t\leq 1-\varepsilon^{c}}\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma=\delta\tau(\varepsilon)\in\mathbb{S}_{n}\atop\delta\in\Delta_{t}}1.

(12)

where
0<c<1/2
.

As described in
[
7
]
or
[
11
]
, Chapter II, the sum over decompositions in (
12
) can be reduced to summation over permutations belonging to respective symmetric groups of lower order. For that, the vertex labels in
\tau(\varepsilon)
and simultaneously the labels in
\delta
belonging to
\mathbb{N}_{n}
can be substituted by the numbers from
\mathbb{N}_{|\tau(\varepsilon)|}
and
\mathbb{N}_{|\delta|}
so that the former orders of the labels in either of the divisors is preserved. If
|\delta|=k
, then exactly
\binom{n}{k}
of the pairs
(\delta,\tau(\varepsilon))
are reduced to one pair
(\delta,\tau(\varepsilon))
, with
\delta\in\mathbb{S}_{k}
and
\tau(\varepsilon)\in\mathbb{S}_{n-k}
. Here we are leaving the same notation after the relabelling since the reduction does not change the cycle structure of divisors; in particular, neither the sizes and nor
w(\delta(\varepsilon))
. Consequently, (
12
) attains the form

\mu_{n}\ll\alpha^{-2}+\max_{\varepsilon^{c}\leq t\leq 1-\varepsilon^{c}}\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{(t-\varepsilon\alpha)n\leq k\leq(t+\varepsilon\alpha)n}\binom{n}{k}\sum_{\delta\in\mathbb{S}_{k}\atop w(\delta(\varepsilon))\leq(3/4)\alpha^{2}}\sum_{\tau(\varepsilon)\in\mathbb{S}_{n-k}}1.

(13)

The innermost sum counts the
(\varepsilon n)
-free permutations in the symmetric group
\mathbb{S}_{n-k}
.
Since

(n-k)/(\varepsilon n)\geq(1-t-\varepsilon\alpha)/\varepsilon\geq\varepsilon^{c-1}/2\geq 1,

for
0<c\leq 1/2
assuring
\varepsilon\alpha\leq\varepsilon^{c}/2
if
\varepsilon\leq{\rm e}^{-2}
,
we can apply Lemma
3
to get

\frac{1}{(n-k)!}\sum_{\tau(\varepsilon)\in\mathbb{S}_{n-k}}1\ll\frac{1}{\varepsilon n}.

Inserting this into estimate (
13
), we obtain

\mu_{n}\ll\alpha^{-2}+\max_{\varepsilon^{c}\leq t\leq 1-\varepsilon^{c}}\frac{1}{\varepsilon n}\sum_{(t-\varepsilon\alpha)n\leq k\leq(t+\varepsilon\alpha)n}\nu_{k}\bigg(\delta\in\mathbb{S}_{k}:\;w(\delta(\varepsilon))\leq(3/4)\alpha^{2}\bigg).

(14)

The summation is over large
k
, namely,
k\geq(t-\varepsilon\alpha)n\geq\varepsilon^{c}n/2
. Therefore, we can again use inequality (
10
) with
k
instead of
n
, centralizing
w(\delta(\varepsilon))
by
h(\varepsilon n,k)
.
The frequency under the sum does not exceed

\nu_{k}:=\nu_{k}\big(\delta\in\mathbb{S}_{k}:\;w(\delta(\varepsilon))-h(\varepsilon n,k)\leq-(1/4)\alpha^{2}+R\big),

where

R=\alpha^{2}-\log\frac{k}{\varepsilon n}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon n}=\log\frac{n}{k}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon n}\leq c\alpha^{2}+\log 2+\frac{1}{\varepsilon n}.

If
c<1/24=:c_{0}
,
\varepsilon<2^{-24}=:\varepsilon_{0}
, and
n\geq n_{0}(\varepsilon):=24\varepsilon^{-1}\alpha^{-2}
, then
R\leq\alpha^{2}/8
. Hence

\nu_{k}\leq\nu_{k}\big(\delta\in\mathbb{S}_{k}:\;w(\delta(\varepsilon))-h(\varepsilon n,k)\leq-(1/8)\alpha^{2}\big)\ll\alpha^{-4}h(\varepsilon n,k)\ll\alpha^{-2}.

Combining the last estimate with (
14
), we obtain

\mu_{n}\ll\alpha^{-2}+(\varepsilon n)^{-1}\cdot\alpha^{-2}\cdot\varepsilon\alpha n\ll\alpha^{-1}.

Recalling inequalities (
12
) and (
8
), we complete the proof of Proposition
1
.
∎

3.2
Convergence of finite-dimensional distributions

This step is devoted to the finite-dimensional distributions of the process
X_{n}(t)
.
Let
l\in\mathbb{N}
and
0\leq t_{1}<\cdots<t_{l}\leq 1
be arbitrary fixed numbers. Define the vectors
T:=(t_{1},\dots,t_{l})
,
U:=(u_{1},\dots,u_{l})\in[0,1]^{l}
, and the distribution function

F_{n}(U,T):=\nu_{n}\big(X_{n}(t_{1})\leq u_{1},\,\dots,X_{n}(t_{l})\leq u_{l}\big).

Proposition 2
.

For all vectors
T
and
U
, the distribution function

F_{n}(U,T)
converges as
n\to\infty
to an
l
-dimensional distribution function.

Proof.

Since
0<X_{n}(\sigma,t)\leq X_{n}(\sigma,1)=1
,
\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}
and
0\leq t\leq 1
, without loss of generality, we can assume that
t_{l}<1
and
x_{1}>0
.
To settle the case
t_{1}=0
, we check that, according to (
3
), the function
1/f\in{\mathcal{M}}_{c}
is defined by
f_{j}(1)=1+g_{j}(1)=1+\vartheta
for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Using this, we evaluate the difference

\displaystyle 0\leq

\displaystyle\nu_{n}\big(X_{n}(t_{2})\leq u_{2},\dots,X_{n}(t_{l})\leq u_{l}\big)

\displaystyle\quad-\nu_{n}\big(X_{n}(0)\leq u_{1},X_{n}(t_{2})\leq u_{2},\dots,X_{n}(t_{l})\leq u_{l}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\nu_{n}\big(X_{n}(0)>u_{1}\big)\leq\frac{1}{u_{1}}{\mathbf{E}}_{n}(1/f)\ll n^{-c(\vartheta)}.

In the last step, we applied Lemma
1
. Here
c(\vartheta)
is a positive constant depending on
\vartheta
only.
Thus, for
u_{1}=0
, our task would reduce to the
(l-1)
-dimensional problem.

Henceforth let
0<\varepsilon<\min\big\{t_{1}^{1/c_{0}},\,(1-t_{l})^{1/c_{0}}\big\}
, where
c_{0}
has been found in Proposition
1
. Introduce the distribution functions

\displaystyle G_{n}(U,T)

\displaystyle:=\nu_{n}\big(\sigma:\;X_{n}(\sigma(\varepsilon),t_{1})\leq u_{1},\,\dots,X_{n}(\sigma(\varepsilon),t_{l})\leq u_{l}\big)

\displaystyle=:\nu_{n}\big(\sigma:\;\overline{X}_{n}(\sigma(\varepsilon),T)\in A\big),

where the process
X_{n}(\sigma(\varepsilon),t)
has been defined in Part 1,

\overline{X}_{n}(\sigma(\varepsilon),T):=\big(X_{n}(\sigma(\varepsilon),t_{1}),\dots,X_{n}(\sigma(\varepsilon),t_{l})\big),\quad A:=\prod_{i\leq l}]0,u_{i}[\subset]0,1[^{l}.

By Proposition
1
,

\displaystyle F_{n}(U,T)

\displaystyle=G_{n}(U,T)+O(\alpha^{-1})=

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma=\sigma(\varepsilon)\delta\in\mathbb{S}_{n}\atop\delta\,\text{is}\,(\varepsilon n)-\text{friable}}{\mathbf{1}}\big\{\overline{X}_{n}(\sigma(\varepsilon),T)\in A\big\}+O(\alpha^{-1}).

As in derivation of (
13
), we can apply reduction of labels (then
\binom{n}{k}
of the
(\varepsilon n)
-free permutations
\sigma(\varepsilon)
reduce to one
\tau\in\mathbb{S}_{k}
) and rewrite

\displaystyle F_{n}(U,T)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\varepsilon n\leq k\leq n}\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{\tau\in\mathbb{S}_{k}}{\mathbf{1}}\big\{\overline{X}_{n}(\tau,T)\in A\big\}

\displaystyle\quad\times\nu_{n-k}\big(\delta\in\mathbb{S}_{n-k}:\;\delta\,\text{is}\,(\varepsilon n)-\text{friable}\big)+O(\alpha^{-1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\varepsilon n\leq k\leq n}\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{\tau\in\mathbb{S}_{k}}{\mathbf{1}}\big\{\overline{X}_{n}(\tau,T)\in A\big\}\rho\Big(\frac{n-k}{\varepsilon n}\Big)+O(\alpha^{-1}),

by Lemma
2
with
m=n-k\geq n(1-\varepsilon)
and
r=\lfloor\varepsilon n\rfloor
, taking into account that
\rho(u)\ll u^{-u/2}
for
u=(n-k)/(\varepsilon n)\geq\varepsilon^{-1}/2
. Similarly, here we can get rid of
\tau
having cycles with repeated lengths. For that, we can use the estimate

\displaystyle\sum_{\varepsilon n\leq k\leq n}\nu_{k}\big(\tau\in\mathbb{S}_{k},\;\exists j\,\text{such\, that}\,k_{j}(\tau)\geq 2\big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\varepsilon n\leq k\leq n}\sum_{\varepsilon n\leq j\leq k}{\mathbf{E}}_{k}\Big(k_{j}(\tau)\big(k_{j}(\tau)-1\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\varepsilon n\leq k\leq n}\sum_{\varepsilon n\leq j\leq k}\frac{1}{j^{2}}\ll\frac{1}{\varepsilon}.

Multiplied by
\rho(\varepsilon^{-1}/2)
, this quantity gives also the remainder
O(\alpha^{-1})
.
So after simplifications, we arrive at

\displaystyle F_{n}(U,T)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\varepsilon n\leq k\leq n}\rho\Big(\frac{n-k}{\varepsilon n}\Big)\nu_{k}\Big(\tau\in\mathbb{S}_{k}:\;k_{j}(\tau)\leq 1,\,\varepsilon n\leq j\leq k,\;\overline{X}_{n}(\tau,T)\in A\Big)+O(\alpha^{-1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\leq\varepsilon^{-1}}\sum_{\varepsilon n\leq k\leq n}\rho\Big(\frac{n-k}{\varepsilon n}\Big)\nu_{k}\Big(\tau\in\mathbb{S}_{k}(m):\;\overline{X}_{n}(\tau,T)\in A\Big)+O(\alpha^{-1}).

(15)

Here
\mathbb{S}_{k}(m)
denotes the subset of
(\varepsilon n)
-free permutations
\tau
in
\mathbb{S}_{k}
which have exactly
m
,
1\leq m\leq\varepsilon^{-1}
, cycles of different lengths. We further intend to change the summation over
k
by a summation over
m
-tuples of the cycle lengths appearing in all accounted
\tau
.

Let
\tau\in\mathbb{S}_{k}(m)
and its cycle lengths in the fixed order be
(j_{1},\dots,j_{m})\in\rfloor\varepsilon n,n]^{m}
provided that
j_{1}+\cdots+j_{m}=k
.
The number of such
\tau
equals
k!/(j_{1}\cdots j_{m})
.
What is the meaning of the condition
\overline{X}_{n}(\tau,T)\in A
in terms of the cycle lengths
(j_{1},\dots,j_{m})
for the given
\tau
?

The following observations hold for any such
\tau
. Firstly,
f(\tau)=(1+\vartheta)^{m}
. Secondly, the divisors
\delta|\tau
are enumerated by the vector of indicators
(i_{1},\dots,i_{m})
, where
i_{r}=1
if the cycle of length
j_{r}
appears in
\delta
. Hence

|\delta|=i_{1}j_{1}+\cdots+i_{m}j_{m}
. Thirdly, the vector
(j_{1}/n,\dots,j_{m}/n)
necessarily belongs to the intersection, denoted by
D_{m}(\varepsilon)
, of the following two sets of the vectors
\bar{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})
:

\big\{\bar{x}\in\rfloor\varepsilon,1]^{m}:\;x_{1}+\cdots+x_{m}\leq 1\big\}

and

\bigcap_{j\leq l}\bigg\{\bar{x}:\;\sum_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{m})\in\{0,1\}^{m}}\vartheta^{i_{1}+\dots+i_{m}}{\mathbf{1}}\big\{i_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+i_{m}x_{m}\leq t_{j}\big\}\leq u_{j}(1+\vartheta)^{m}\bigg\}.

Having all this in mind, we obtain from (
15
)

\displaystyle F_{n}(U,T)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\leq\varepsilon^{-1}}\sum_{(j_{1}/n,\dots,j_{m}/n)\in D_{m}(\varepsilon)}\rho\bigg(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big(1-\sum_{r\leq m}\frac{j_{r}}{n}\Big)\bigg)\frac{1}{j_{1}\cdots j_{m}}+O(\alpha^{-1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\leq\varepsilon^{-1}}\int_{D_{m}(\varepsilon)}\rho\Big(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big(1-\sum_{r\leq m}x_{r}\Big)\Big)\frac{{\rm d}x_{1}\cdots{\rm d}x_{m}}{x_{1}\cdots x_{m}}+O(\alpha^{-1})+o_{\varepsilon}(1)

(16)

as
n\to\infty
, by the definition of the
m
-dimensional Riemann integral. We have arrived at the situation described on page 6 of the paper
[
18
]
. Letting successively
n\to\infty
and
\varepsilon\to 0
, by virtue of the notation
\alpha=(\log(1/\varepsilon))^{-1/2}
, we verify that

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}I(\varepsilon)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}F_{n}(U,T)\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}F_{n}(U,T)\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}I(\varepsilon),

where
I(\varepsilon)
denotes the sum of integrals in the above relation. This shows that, as claimed in Proposition
2
, the limits

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\,I(\varepsilon)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\,F_{n}(U,T)

exist for all vectors
T
and
U
.
∎

3.3
Tightness

This part is devoted to showing the tightness of the sequence of distributions
\{P_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
.
We take advantage of the G. Tenenbaum’s idea
[
22
]
to verify a stronger tightness criterion than that used in the Skorokhod space
\mathbb{D}[0,1]
. According to Theorem 15.5 of
[
8
]
, the following assertion also assures that the weak limit
P
of a subsequence
\{P_{n^{\prime}}\}
as
n^{\prime}\to\infty
is supported by a subset of the space
\mathbb{C}[0,1]
.

Proposition 3
.

For every
0<a,\epsilon<1
,

\nu_{n}\Big(\sup_{|s-t|\leq a}\big|X_{n}(t)-X_{n}(s)\big|\geq\epsilon\Big)\ll\epsilon^{-1}a^{\vartheta/(1+\vartheta)}.

Proof.

By virtue of the monotonicity of the process
X_{n}(t)
,
0\leq t\leq 1
, the modulus of continuity is

\sup_{|s-t|\leq a}\big|X_{n}(t)-X_{n}(s)\big|=\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1-a}\big|X_{n}(t+a)-X_{n}(t)\big|=:Q_{n}(\sigma,a),

where
0\leq s\leq t\leq 1
and
0<a<1
.
Thus, the estimate of Proposition
3
will follow from

{\mathbf{E}}_{n}Q_{n}(\sigma,a)\ll a^{\vartheta/(1+\vartheta)}.

(17)

For a given
\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}
, the introduced
Q_{n}(\sigma,a)
is just the concentration function of the random variable taking value
|\delta|/n
with probability
g(\delta)/f(\sigma)
when
\delta|\sigma
.
By Lemma 2.6.1 in Chapter III of the book
[
21
]
,

Q_{n}(\sigma,a)\leq 3an\int_{0}^{1/(an)}\big|G(\sigma,v)\big|{\rm d}v,

where

G(\sigma,v)=\frac{1}{f(\sigma)}\sum_{\delta|\sigma}g(\delta){\rm e}^{iv|\delta|},\quad v\in\mathbb{R}.

Consequently,

{\mathbf{E}}_{n}Q_{n}(\sigma,a)\ll an\int_{0}^{1/(an)}{\mathbf{E}}_{n}\big|G(\sigma,v)\big|{\rm d}v.

(18)

Since
|G(\sigma,v)|
is a bounded completely multiplicative function on
\mathbb{S}_{n}
defined by
G_{j}(1,v)=|1+\vartheta{\rm e}^{ijv}|/(1+\vartheta)
, where
j\in\mathbb{N}
and
v\in\mathbb{R}
is a parameter,
Lemma
1
yields

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}_{n}\big|G(\sigma,v)|

\displaystyle\ll\exp\bigg\{\sum_{j\leq n}\Big(\frac{|1+\vartheta{\rm e}^{ivj}|}{1+\vartheta}-1\Big)\frac{1}{j}\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq\exp\bigg\{-\frac{\vartheta}{1+\vartheta}\sum_{j\leq n}\frac{1-\cos vj}{j}\bigg\}.

Here we have applied the inequality

|1+r{\rm e}^{ix}|/(1+r)\leq 1-r(1-\cos x)/(1+r)^{2},\quad 0\leq r\leq 1,\,x\in\mathbb{R},

presented on page 17 of the paper
[
22
]
with
r=\vartheta
and
x=vj
. Using the equality

\sum_{j\leq n}\frac{1-\cos vj}{j}=\log\frac{|1-{\rm e}^{-1/n+iv}|}{1-{\rm e}^{-1/n}}+O(1),

we obtain

{\mathbf{E}}_{n}\big|G(\sigma,v)|\ll\Big(\frac{1-{\rm e}^{-1/n}}{|1-{\rm e}^{-1/n+iv}|}\Big)^{\vartheta/(1+\vartheta)}\leq\Big(\frac{1}{n|1-{\rm e}^{-1/n+iv}|}\Big)^{\vartheta/(1+\vartheta)}

with an absolute constant in the symbol
\ll
. Returning to (
18
), we proceed
as follows:

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}_{n}Q_{n}(\sigma,a)

\displaystyle\ll an^{1/(1+\vartheta)}\int_{0}^{1/(an)}\frac{{\rm d}v}{|1-{\rm e}^{-1/n+iv}|^{\vartheta/(1+\vartheta)}}

\displaystyle\ll a+an^{1/(1+\vartheta)}\int_{1/n}^{1/(an)}v^{-\vartheta/(1+\vartheta)}{\rm d}v\ll a^{\vartheta/(1+\vartheta)}.

This is the estimate (
17
).
∎

Concluding remark.
Doing more cumbersome work, one can extend Theorem
2
in two ways. Firstly, the function
g
can be taken from
{\mathcal{M}}
, not necessarily from
{\mathcal{M}}_{c}
. It can be proved that the values
g_{j}(k)
for
k\geq 2
and
j\leq n
are negligible. Adopting Tenenbaum’s
[
22
]
argument, one can replace the condition on
g_{j}(1)=\vartheta>0
so that their weighted sums over
\varepsilon n\leq j\leq n
behave in the needed manner. An analytic technique in the style of Bareikis and Mačiulis
[
6
]
works here as well.
Secondly, as seen from Theorem 3 of the paper
[
7
]
, analogous results could be established for permutations drawn according to the Ewens probability measure on
\mathbb{S}_{n}
.

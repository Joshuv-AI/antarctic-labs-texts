Introduction

Reno-enteric fistulas represent a rare and complex medical anomaly, characterized by an abnormal communication between the renal system and the gastrointestinal tract. While cases involving reno-colic and reno-duodenal fistulas are more commonly reported in the literature, reno-jejunal fistulas remain exceedingly rare, with few documented instances [2]. The etiology of these fistulas often encompasses a spectrum of pathological conditions, including infectious diseases such as tuberculosis (TB) and complications from nephrolithiasis or staghorn calculi [9]. Notably, in developing countries, TB is a significant contributor to renal pathology leading to fistula formation, underscoring the importance of considering infectious etiologies in differential diagnoses [11].

This case report presents a unique instance of a renal jejunal fistula in a pediatric patient, further complicated by concurrent TB and parasitic infestation. Such a constellation of conditions presents both diagnostic and therapeutic challenges, emphasizing the need for a multidisciplinary approach to management. Through a detailed examination of this case, we aim to shed light on the diagnostic processes, therapeutic interventions, and the critical lessons learned, contributing to the scarce body of knowledge on such rare medical phenomena.

Case Presentation

A 6-year-old Afghan boy, residing in Pakistan, was brought to our outpatient department by his father for a 5-month persisting condition. The child exhibited a discharging sinus on his left flank with a history of a worm emergence from the site, accompanied by a recent cough, indicating a complex medical issue. Upon examination, he showed pain and a purulent, foul-smelling discharge from the sinus, suggesting an infection. These rare and concerning symptoms posed significant diagnostic and treatment challenges, with the boy's geographical history adding context to the potential epidemiological implications.

During the examination, the patient showed key symptoms, including tenderness and pain in the left flank where the sinus discharged a purulent, foul-smelling fluid, suggesting a severe infection or abscess with potential implications for the renal system or adjacent structures (refer to Fig. 1 for a visual representation). Additionally, a recent cough, beginning five days before the visit, hinted at respiratory involvement or systemic spread of the infection. The absence of a significant medical history, with no chronic conditions or prior surgeries, made this case exceptionally unusual, emphasizing the need for a detailed exploration of possible underlying causes.Fig. 1Clinical photograph of the discharging sinus located on the patient's left flank, demonstrating purulent discharge indicative of an underlying infection.Fig. 1

A critical finding from the initial assessments was the patient's anemia, with a hemoglobin level of 9.6, below the normal range for his age, potentially multifactorial, related to nutritional deficiencies, chronic infection, or underlying diseases such as renal pathology or chronic inflammatory conditions. Baseline investigations confirmed the severity, with hemoglobin dropping to 8.3, necessitating blood transfusions. Elevated platelet counts initially recorded at 802, later normalized to 534, suggest inflammation or infection, while a high white blood cell count, initially 11.82, later returned to the normal range of 6.7, reflecting the body's response. Renal function tests showed impaired kidney function, with creatinine levels moving from 0.54 to 0.4, indicating the extent of impairment.

Diagnostic tests played a pivotal role in delineating the case's complexities. An initial DPTA scan revealed a stark difference in renal function: the right kidney exhibited 84 % efficiency, while the left kidney's function was severely reduced to 16 %, indicating significant left kidney pathology. Subsequent CT intravenous pyelogram (IVP) imaging provided deeper insight, showing multiple stones with a large staghorn calculus in the left kidney's renal pelvis, alongside mild pelvicalyceal dilatation and diminished corticomedullary differentiation. These signs pointed to advanced nephrolithiasis. Additionally, there was evidence of focal renal and perinephric thickening and loculations, suggesting subacute severe pyelonephritis. The radiological indications raised the likelihood of xanthogranulomatous pyelonephritis, pending histopathological confirmation, as illustrated in Fig. 2.Fig. 2CT intravenous pyelogram (IVP) showing the non-functioning left kidney with multiple calculi and signs of significant pyelonephritis.Fig. 2

CT intravenous pyelogram (IVP) showing the non-functioning left kidney with multiple calculi and signs of significant pyelonephritis.

Diagnostic evaluations, including CT IVP imaging, identified a severely compromised left kidney with stones and infection, leading to an elective left nephrectomy. During the surgery, an unexpected renal jejunal fistula and worm infestation were discovered, featuring a small hole in the jejunum and a worm's presence. This required removing the adherent kidney, and immediate repair of the fistula and worm removal. The operation highlighted the case's complexities, involving meticulous repair of a bowel perforation by an Ascaris worm and submitting the kidney for histopathology. Postoperatively, the patient was managed with a nasogastric tube and nil per os for 4 days, receiving cefoperazone/sulbactam and metronidazole. The biopsy of the removed kidney revealed chronic granulomatous inflammation, suggestive of tuberculosis (TB). The patient's postoperative recovery included managing ileus, a typical yet controllable complication that persisted for 2 to 3 days, through nasogastric decompression and careful reintroduction of oral intake based on tolerance. This approach effectively mitigated the ileus without additional issues. Simultaneously, the patient's postoperative anemia, indicated by a drop in hemoglobin levels from 9.6 to 8.3, necessitated the transfusion of two blood units. This intervention successfully stabilized his hemoglobin levels and enhanced his recovery.

Following surgery, the patient was discharged with a detailed post-operative plan to ensure comprehensive recovery, including essential postoperative deworming due to a worm found during surgery. Despite clear recommendations for follow-up visits to track recovery and evaluate health, the patient did not return, raising concerns about potential long-term health effects and the need for further interventions. This highlights the challenge of ensuring ongoing care, particularly in pediatric cases. The prescribed medication regimen included a single dose of Syrup Albendazole; Syrup Metronidazole, one teaspoonful three times daily for 7 days; Syrup Norethindrone 125 mg, one tablet twice daily for 7 days; Syrup Paracetamol, two teaspoonful three times daily for 7 days; and Syrup Multivitamin, one teaspoonful daily for a month. This regimen aimed at addressing immediate post-surgery needs and supporting the patient's overall long-term health, underlining the importance of medication adherence and follow-up visits in the recovery trajectory.

Discussion

Symptoms associated with reno-alimentary fistulas typically include flank pain, upper gastrointestinal symptoms, urinary frequency, urgency, general malaise, and weight loss. These presentations may be accompanied by fever and localized tenderness, which were evident in our case, emphasizing the clinical complexity these fistulas introduce. This symptom profile aligns with findings reported by ([6,7], emphasizing the diagnostic challenges these symptoms pose due to their non-specific nature.

Excluding the esophagus, all divisions of the gastrointestinal tract can be involved in nephro-intestinal fistulas, with reno-colic and reno-duodenal being the most common. In contrast, reno-gastric, reno-jejunal, and reno-ileal fistulas are exceedingly rare, with fewer than a dozen cases reported in the literature [3] [11]. Our case adds to this scarce data, presenting a unique instance of a reno-jejunal fistula with a cutaneous extension in the left flank region, unlike the lumbar and gluteal extensions reported by [14].

The origins of reno-enteric fistulas are often rooted in kidney complications such as nephrolithiasis and staghorn calculi, with developing countries frequently seeing tuberculosis (TB) as a contributing factor, as both histopathological findings in this case and literature suggest [5]. Traumatic fistulas, on the other hand, may stem from iatrogenic interventions, external injuries like gunshot wounds, or internally from ingested foreign bodies. The development of these fistulous tracts is typically driven by repeated infections following calculus obstruction, leading to inflammatory adhesions between the kidney and adjacent intestinal loops. This process can culminate in pyonephrosis evolving into a pyonephric abscess that, upon rupture, forms fistulas which compromise renal function and ultimately necessitate radical nephrectomy, as observed in our patient [11].

Diagnosis of fistulas is predominantly confirmed through radiographic evidence, with CT scans serving as a highly reliable imaging modality for such assessments [4]. Laboratory investigations in our case mirrored typical findings, such as elevated white blood cell counts and anemia, corroborating the diagnosis. An intravenous pyelogram further confirmed the lack of function in the affected kidney, highlighting the necessity for surgical intervention [8].

The co-occurrence of tuberculosis (TB) and parasitic infection played a pivotal role in our patient's renal jejunal fistula. TB, causing genitourinary infections, weakens renal parenchyma, facilitating fistula formation [12]. The parasitic infestation exacerbated inflammation and tissue damage, likely expediting fistula development, showcasing a synergistic effect. This case stresses the need to consider infectious diseases in renal fistula diagnoses, especially in endemic areas. Effective management of TB and parasitic infections is crucial in preventing complications. Additionally, comprehensive diagnostics and vigilant post-operative care are vital to mitigate long-term impacts on patient health [10].

The management of this case relied on a robust diagnostic strategy, utilizing CT scans and intravenous pyelograms to diagnose the renal jejunal fistula. The subsequent elective nephrectomy addressed the condition effectively, demonstrating the surgical team's adaptability to intraoperative findings. Interdisciplinary collaboration ensured comprehensive care, managing both surgical and infectious complications efficiently. However, challenges surfaced, with the fistula evading preoperative detection, possibly due to imaging approach limitations. Inadequate post-discharge follow-up hindered long-term management evaluation, emphasizing the need for improved patient engagement. Proactive management of the patient's anemia could have benefitted from earlier and more integrated treatment strategies.

This case of renal jejunal fistula with concurrent tuberculosis (TB) and parasitic infestation offers vital medical insights. It stresses the need for vigilance regarding infectious causes in unusual renal or gastrointestinal symptoms, especially in endemic areas. The synergistic effect of TB and parasitic infections in fistula formation highlights the importance of comprehensive diagnostic evaluations, including infectious disease screenings. Unexpected intraoperative findings emphasize surgical teams' need for flexibility, impacting outcomes significantly. Lastly, interdisciplinary collaboration among specialists— infectious disease experts, surgeons, radiologists, and pediatricians— improves patient outcomes [10].

Conclusion

This case of a renal jejunal fistula, alongside tuberculosis (TB) and parasitic infection, highlights three main lessons: the importance of considering infectious causes in unusual renal presentations, especially in endemic regions; the need for surgical teams to anticipate and adapt to unexpected findings; and the crucial role of interdisciplinary collaboration in managing complex cases. It demonstrates the necessity for a thorough diagnostic approach, readiness for intraoperative surprises, and the integration of specialized knowledge for successful patient outcomes. This case contributes valuable insights into the complexities of diagnosing and treating rare medical conditions, emphasizing a holistic and prepared approach to patient care.

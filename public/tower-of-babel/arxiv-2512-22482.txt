Spectral supersaturation for color-critical graphs
Longfei Fang, Yongtao Li, Huiqiu Lin, Jie Ma

Abstract
A graph is color-critical if it contains an edge whose deletion reduces its chromatic number. This class of graphs, including cliques and odd cycles, plays a central role in extremal graph theory. In this paper, following an influential line of research initiated by Bollobás–Nikiforov, we study the spectral supersaturation problem for color-critical graphs. Let T n , r T_{n,r} be the r r -partite Turán graph, let 𝒯 n , r , q \mathcal{T}_{n,r,q} denote the family of graphs obtained from T n , r T_{n,r} by adding q q edges, and let λ ⁡ ( G ) \lambda(G) be the spectral radius of a graph G G . We first prove that for any color-critical graph F F with chromatic number r + 1 r+1 , there exists δ F > 0 \delta_{F}>0 such that for sufficiently large n n and all 1 ≤ q ≤ δ F ​ n 1\leq q\leq\delta_{F}\sqrt{n} , any n n -vertex graph G G with λ ⁡ ( G ) ≥ min T ∈ 𝒯 n , r , q ⁡ λ ⁡ ( T ) \lambda(G)\geq\min_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{n,r,q}}\lambda(T) contains at least q ⋅ c ⁡ ( n , F ) q\cdot c(n,F) copies of F F , where c ⁡ ( n , F ) c(n,F) denotes the minimum number of copies of F F created by adding a single edge to T n , r T_{n,r} ; moreover, any extremal graph G G must belong to 𝒯 n , r , q \mathcal{T}_{n,r,q} . Next, we prove a spectral supersaturation result for the analogous condition λ ⁡ ( G ) ≥ max T ∈ 𝒯 n , r , q ⁡ λ ⁡ ( T ) \lambda(G)\geq\max_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{n,r,q}}\lambda(T) , valid for all 1 ≤ q ≤ δ F ​ n 1\leq q\leq\delta_{F}n . Together, these results provide a complete resolution to a problem proposed by Ning–Zhai, and establish a spectral counterpart to the well-known results of Mubayi and Pikhurko–Yilma in the extremal supersaturation setting. A notable feature of our first result is that the restriction q = O ⁡ ( n ) q=O(\sqrt{n}) is tight up to a constant factor, in contrast to the linear bounds provided by other settings discussed above. As applications, we extend a result of Liu–Mubayi, and solve a related conjecture by Li–Lu–Peng. Our proof is based on a novel spectral incremental technique for graphs close to the Turán graph T n , r T_{n,r} , which may be of independent interest.

Introduction

The supersaturation problem for a fixed graph
F
asks for the minimum number of copies of
F
in a host graph with a given number of edges, and is a fundamental topic in extremal graph theory.
This line of research dates back to a classical theorem of Rademacher (see
[

n

, then

contains at least
\lfloor n/2\rfloor
triangles. Subsequently, Erdős
[
5
]
and Lovász and Simonovits
[
21
]
obtained further refinements in counting triangles. Supersaturation for cliques was later studied by Lovász and Simonovits
[
22
]
and eventually resolved by Reiher in his breakthrough work
[
33
]
.
We refer to
[

,

,
1
,

,
33
]
and references therein for further background and subsequent developments.

A graph is called
color-critical
if it contains an edge whose deletion reduces its chromatic number. This rich family of graphs, which includes cliques and odd cycles, plays a central role in the development of extremal graph theory. Let
T_{n,r}
denote the

-vertex complete balanced

-partite graph.
A classical theorem of Simonovits
[

states that for every color-critical graph

Extending Simonovits’ theorem, Mubayi
[

]
established the corresponding supersaturation result for every color-critical graph

[

We summarize their findings as follows.
Let

Over the past two decades, many classical problems in extremal graph theory have been revisited using spectral methods, giving rise to the vibrant field known as spectral extremal graph theory.
The adjacency matrix of an

\chi(F)=r+1

Nikiforov
[

\lambda(G)\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{r}+o(1)\right)n
.
By invoking the property
\lambda(G)\geq\frac{2}{n}e(G)

[
26

extended this and the aforementioned Simonovits’ theorem by proving
\lambda(G)\leq\lambda(T_{n,r})
for sufficiently large

G=T_{n,r}
.
For more progress, we refer to the survey
[
29
]
, as well as to
[

,
25
,
37
,
38
,
14
,
15
]
(by no means a comprehensive list) and the references therein.

The study of counting substructures in a host graph

in terms of spectral radius condition was initialed by

[
2
]

where they showed that every graph

Inspired by Rademacher’s theorem, Ning and Zhai

For example, the condition

1.1
Main results

We now introduce the first main result of this paper as follows.
Throughout the rest, let
N_{F}(G)
be the number of copies of

in a graph

Theorem 1.2
.

Let

be a color-critical graph

\chi(F)=r+1

if

1\leq q\leq\delta_{F}\sqrt{n}

Note that the bound

.
This presents a phenomenon significantly different from that of Theorem
1.1

As the second result of this paper, we provide the following companion of Theorem

.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
F
be a color-critical graph with
\chi(F)=r+1
.
There exists
\delta_{F}>0
such that
if
n
is sufficiently large,

0\leq q\leq\delta_{F}n
, and

is an
n
-vertex graph with

\lambda(G)\geq\max_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{n,r,q}}\lambda(T),

Theorem

This range is broader than the (optimal) square root range of Theorem

In particular, in the case of

[

]

We will illustrate how the proof of Theorem

Combining Theorems

Although not essential to the proofs of Theorems

1.2
Proof ingredients

In addition to utilizing the graph removal lemma and spectral supersaturation-stability,
our proof introduces novel spectral techniques to capture the variation in the spectral radius in relation to the structural differences between the host graph and the Turán graph
T_{n,r}
, as well as the number of copies of
F
.
In this subsection, we present and discuss two key components of our proof, which may have potential applications in other spectral extremal problems.

The first result provides an optimal quantitative estimate for the spectral radius
\lambda(G)
of any graph
G
that differs from a complete multipartite graph by only a few edges.
Given a partition
V(G)=V_{1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{r}
,
we say that an edge
e\in E(G)
is a
class-edge
if
e\in E(G[V_{i}])
for some
i\in[r]
, and a
cross-edge
otherwise.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
n
be sufficiently large and
G
be a graph obtained from an
n
-vertex complete
r
-partite graph
K=K_{r}(n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{r})
by
adding
\alpha_{1}
class-edges and deleting
\alpha_{2}
cross-edges,
where
n_{1}\geq n_{2}\geq\cdots\geq n_{r}
and
\max\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\}\leq\frac{n}{(10r)^{3}}
.

(i)

If
n_{1}-n_{r}\leq\frac{n}{100}
, then by denoting
\phi:=\max\{n_{1}-n_{r},2(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})\}
, we have

\left|\lambda(G)-\lambda(K)-\frac{2(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})}{n}\right|\leq\frac{56(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})\phi}{n^{2}}.

(ii)

If
n_{1}-n_{r}\geq 2k
for an integer
k\leq\frac{n}{(10r)^{3}}
, then by denoting
\psi:=\max\{3k,2(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})\}
,

\displaystyle\lambda(G)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda(T_{n,r})+\frac{2(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})}{n}-\frac{2(r-1)k^{2}}{rn}\cdot\Big(1-\frac{28r\psi}{n}\Big)^{4}+\frac{56(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})\cdot 7r\psi}{n^{2}}.

The second result provides a structural characterization of a graph
G
with a small number of copies of a given color-critical graph
F
, under the condition
\lambda(G)\geq\lambda(T_{n,r})
. Specifically, it states that if
G
satisfies this condition and contains
O(n^{|F|-2})
copies of
F
, then
G
can be transformed into an almost balanced
r
-partite graph with only a few edge additions or deletions.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
0<\varepsilon_{1}<\frac{1}{2}
and
F
be color-critical with
\chi(F)=r+1
.
There exist
\delta_{F},\eta_{F}>0
such that if
n
is sufficiently large,
1\leq q\leq\delta_{F}{n}
, and
G
is an
n
-vertex graph with
\lambda(G)\geq\lambda(T_{n,r})
and
N_{F}(G)\leq(q+\varepsilon_{1})c(n,F)
,
then
G
can be obtained from
K_{r}(n_{1},\dots,n_{r})
by adding
\alpha_{1}
class-edges and deleting
\alpha_{2}
cross-edges, where
\alpha_{2}\leq 2\alpha_{1}\leq\frac{4q}{\eta_{F}}
and

|n_{i}-n_{j}|\leq 4\sqrt{\alpha_{1}}
for any
i\neq j
.

In Section

In Section

Prior to establishing our main results, we prove two key ingredients, Theorems

n_{0}^{f-2}\geq(n-3r\phi)^{f-2}\geq n^{f-2}-3rf\phi n^{f-3}
.
By Lemma
2.3
,
there exist constants
\alpha_{F},\beta_{F}>0
such that

c(n_{0},F)\geq\alpha_{F}n_{0}^{f-2}-\beta_{F}n_{0}^{f-3}\geq\alpha_{F}n^{f-2}-3\alpha_{F}rf\phi n^{f-3}-\beta_{F}n^{f-3}.

By Lemma
2.3
again, we have
c(n,F)\leq\alpha_{F}n^{f-2}+\beta_{F}n^{f-3}
.
Thus, we get

\displaystyle N_{F}(G_{uv})=c(n_{0},F)\geq c(n,F)-(3\alpha_{F}rf\phi+2\beta_{F})n^{f-3}.

For each edge
uv\in E(G_{in})
,
any copy of
F
in
G_{uv}
contains the only edge

. Thus,

N_{F}(G)\geq\sum_{uv\in E(G_{in})}N_{F}(G_{uv})\geq\alpha_{1}c(n,F)-O_{F}(\alpha_{1}\phi n^{f-3}).

Next, we prove the desired lower bound on

.
For two edges
e_{i}
and
e_{j}
,
let

be the number of copies of
F
in
G
containing both
e_{i}
and
e_{j}
.
A copy of
F
containing
e_{i}
and
e_{j}
needs at most
f-3
extra vertices;
these extra vertices can be chosen in at most
n^{f-3}
ways.
Once the
f
vertices are fixed,
there are at most

possible choices to select edges
so that the resulting graph is isomorphic to
F
.
Hence,
N_{F}(G,e_{i},e_{j})\leq 2^{f^{2}}n^{f-3}
.
By Lemma
2.3
,
there are constants
\alpha_{F},\beta_{F}>0
such that

c(n+r\phi,F)\leq\alpha_{F}(n+r\phi)^{f-2}+\beta_{F}(n+r\phi)^{f-3}
.
For any edge
e\in E(G_{in})
, we have

\displaystyle N_{F}(G,e)\leq c(n+r\phi,F)\leq\alpha_{F}n^{f-2}+(\alpha_{F}fr\phi+2\beta_{F})n^{f-3}\leq c(n,F)+(\alpha_{F}fr\phi+3\beta_{F})n^{f-3},

where the last inequality holds by Lemma
2.3
.
Consequently, we get

\displaystyle N_{F}(G)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{e\in E(G_{in})}N_{F}(G,e)+\sum_{\{e_{i},e_{j}\}\subseteq E(G_{in})}N_{F}(G,e_{i},e_{j})

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{1}\Big(c(n,F)+(\alpha_{F}fr\phi+3\beta_{F})n^{f-3}\Big)+\binom{\alpha_{1}}{2}2^{f^{2}}n^{f-3}

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{1}c(n,F)+O_{F}\big((\alpha_{1}\phi)n^{f-3}\big),

where the last inequality holds as
\binom{\alpha_{1}}{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{1}\phi
.
The proof of Lemma
2.5
is complete.

The following lemma has its roots in

Lemma 2.6
.

If

is an

-vertex graph with
e(G)\geq(1-\frac{1}{r})\frac{n^{2}}{2}-t
,
and

is a vertex partition such that

, then for each

\frac{n}{r}-\sqrt{2(s+t)}\leq|V_{i}|\leq\frac{n}{r}+\sqrt{2(s+t)}.

Proof.

Let

be the

-partite subgraph of

by deleting all edges of

. Then

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}\Bigl(|V_{i}|-\frac{n}{r}\Bigr)^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}|V_{i}|^{2}-\frac{n^{2}}{r}=\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}|V_{i}|\right)^{2}-2\left(\sum\limits_{i<j}|V_{i}||V_{j}|\right)-\frac{n^{2}}{r}.

Since
\sum_{i<j}|V_{i}||V_{j}|\geq e(H)\geq(1-\frac{1}{r})\frac{n^{2}}{2}-t-s

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}\Bigl(|V_{i}|-\frac{n}{r}\Bigr)^{2}\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)n^{2}-2e(H)\leq 2(s+t).

Thus, it follows that

.

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{w_{\ell+1}(H_{i})}{x^{\ell+1}}}{\big(1+\frac{n_{i}}{x}\big)\Big(1+\frac{n_{i}}{x}+\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{w_{\ell+1}(H_{i})}{x^{\ell+1}}\Big)}-\frac{\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{w_{\ell+1}(H)}{x^{\ell+1}}-\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{w_{\ell+1}(H_{j})}{x^{\ell+1}}}{\Big(1+\frac{n_{j}}{x}+\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{w_{\ell+1}(H_{j})}{x^{\ell+1}}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{n_{j}}{x}+\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{w_{\ell+1}(H)}{x^{\ell+1}}\Big)}

(4)

Our goal is to show that

for every

.
By direct computation, for each

Since

\displaystyle\frac{2a}{x^{2}}+\frac{a^{2}+a}{x^{3}}<\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{w_{\ell+1}(S_{a+1})}{x^{\ell+1}}=\frac{2a}{x^{2}}+\frac{a^{2}+a}{x^{3}}+\frac{2a^{2}}{x^{4}}+\frac{a^{3}+a^{2}}{x^{5}}+\cdots<\frac{2a}{x^{2}}+\frac{a^{2}+a}{x^{3}}+\frac{3a^{2}}{x^{4}}.

(5)

Meanwhile, we have

for each

\displaystyle\frac{6}{x^{2}}+\frac{12}{x^{3}}<\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{w_{\ell+1}(K_{3})}{x^{\ell+1}}=\frac{6}{x^{2}}+\frac{12}{x^{3}}+\frac{24}{x^{4}}+\frac{48}{x^{5}}+\cdots<\frac{6}{x^{2}}+\frac{12}{x^{3}}+\frac{27}{x^{4}}.

(6)

.

Combining (

\displaystyle\frac{\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{w_{\ell+1}(H)}{x^{\ell+1}}-\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{w_{\ell+1}(H_{j})}{x^{\ell+1}}}{\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{w_{\ell+1}(H_{i})}{x^{\ell+1}}}>\frac{\frac{2a}{x^{2}}+\frac{a^{2}+2ab+a}{x^{3}}-\frac{3b^{2}}{x^{4}}}{\frac{2a}{x^{2}}+\frac{a^{2}+a}{x^{3}}+\frac{3a^{2}}{x^{4}}}>1+\frac{b}{2x},

(7)

.

Note that

\displaystyle\frac{\Big(1+\frac{n_{j}}{x}+\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{w_{\ell+1}(H_{j})}{x^{\ell+1}}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{n_{j}}{x}+\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{w_{\ell+1}(H)}{x^{\ell+1}}\Big)}{\big(1+\frac{n_{i}}{x}\big)\Big(1+\frac{n_{i}}{x}+\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{w_{\ell+1}(H_{i})}{x^{\ell+1}}\Big)}

\displaystyle<\frac{\big(1+\frac{n_{i}}{x}+\frac{3b}{x^{2}}\big)\big(1+\frac{n_{i}}{x}+\frac{3q}{x^{2}}\big)}{\big(1+\frac{n_{i}}{x}\big)\big(1+\frac{n_{i}}{x}\big)}<\Big(1+\frac{3q}{x^{2}}\Big)^{2}<1+\frac{1}{2x},

.

Definition
.

-vertex graph obtained from an

4
Estimation on spectral radius: Proof of Theorem
1.5

Before showing Theorem
1.5
, we introduce the following technical lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
K=K_{r}(n_{1},\dots,n_{r})
be an
n
-vertex complete
r
-partite graph with
n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{r}
.
We denote
K^{\prime}=K_{r}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{i}-1,\ldots,n_{j}+1,\ldots,n_{r})
.
If
2\leq n_{i}-n_{j}\leq\phi
for some
i<j
, then

\lambda(K^{\prime})-\lambda(K)\geq\frac{2(r-1)(n_{i}-n_{j}-1)}{rn}\cdot\Big(1-\frac{4\phi}{n}\Big)^{4}.

Under the above condition, if
\phi\leq\frac{n}{20}
, then
\lambda(K^{\prime})-\lambda(K)\leq\frac{2(r-1)(n_{i}-n_{j}-1)}{rn}\big(1+\frac{8\phi}{n}\big)^{4}+\frac{5\phi}{n^{2}}
.

Proof.

We denote
V(K)=V_{1}\cup V_{2}\cup\cdots\cup V_{r}
, where
|V_{i}|=n_{i}
for every
i\in[r]
.
For notational convenience, we write
\lambda=\lambda(K)
and
\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda(K^{\prime})
.
Since
K
is connected,
by the Perron–Frobenius theorem,
there exists a positive unit eigenvector

\bm{x}
corresponding to
\lambda(K)
.
For each
k\in[r]
, we see that
x_{u}=x_{v}
for any
u,v\in V_{k}
.
Then we write
x_{v}=x_{k}
for every
v\in V_{k}
.
Note that

\lambda x_{k}=\sum_{u\in N(k)}x_{u}=\sum_{v\in V(K)}x_{v}-n_{k}x_{k},

which yields

(\lambda+n_{k})x_{k}=\sum_{v\in V(K)}x_{v}.

Then
x_{k}=\frac{1}{\lambda+n_{k}}\sum_{v\in V(K)}x_{v}
.
It follows that

1=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{r}\frac{n_{k}}{\lambda+n_{k}}=\sum_{k\in[r]\setminus\{i,j\}}\frac{n_{k}}{\lambda+n_{k}}+\frac{n_{i}}{\lambda+n_{i}}+\frac{n_{j}}{\lambda+n_{j}}.

(9)

A similar argument on
K^{\prime}
yields

1=\sum_{k\in[r]\setminus\{i,j\}}\frac{n_{k}}{\lambda^{\prime}+n_{k}}+\frac{n_{i}-1}{\lambda^{\prime}+n_{i}-1}+\frac{n_{j}+1}{\lambda^{\prime}+n_{j}+1}.

(10)

Note that

\displaystyle\frac{n_{i}}{\lambda+n_{i}}-\frac{n_{i}-1}{\lambda^{\prime}+n_{i}-1}=\frac{(n_{i}-1)(\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda)+\lambda^{\prime}}{(\lambda+n_{i})(\lambda^{\prime}+n_{i}-1)},

and

\displaystyle\frac{n_{j}}{\lambda+n_{j}}-\frac{n_{j}+1}{\lambda^{\prime}+n_{j}+1}=\frac{(n_{j}+1)(\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda)-\lambda^{\prime}}{(\lambda+n_{j})(\lambda^{\prime}+n_{j}+1)}.

Subtracting (
10
) from (
9
), we get

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle:=\left(\sum\limits_{k\in[r]\setminus\{i,j\}}\frac{n_{k}(\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda)}{(\lambda+n_{k})(\lambda^{\prime}+n_{k})}\right)+\frac{(n_{i}-1)(\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda)}{(\lambda+n_{i})(\lambda^{\prime}+n_{i}-1)}+\frac{(n_{j}+1)(\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda)}{(\lambda+n_{j})(\lambda^{\prime}+n_{j}+1)}

(11)

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{(\lambda+n_{j})(\lambda^{\prime}+n_{j}+1)}-\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{(\lambda+n_{i})(\lambda^{\prime}+n_{i}-1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(n_{i}-n_{j}-2)\lambda+(n_{i}-n_{j})\lambda^{\prime}+(n_{i}+n_{j})(n_{i}-n_{j}-1)}{(\lambda+n_{i}-1)(\lambda+n_{i})(\lambda+n_{j}+1)(\lambda+n_{j})}\lambda^{\prime}.

(12)

Since
n_{i}-n_{j}\geq 2
,
we obtain from (
12
) that
L>0
, which implies
\lambda^{\prime}>\lambda
by using (
11
).
Since
n_{1}-n_{r}\leq\phi
, we have

\frac{n}{r}-\phi\leq n_{r}\leq n_{1}\leq\frac{n}{r}+\phi
.
Consequently, we get

\displaystyle\frac{r-1}{r}n-\phi\leq\delta(K)\leq\lambda<\lambda^{\prime}\leq\Delta(K^{\prime})\leq\frac{r-1}{r}n+\phi.

(13)

Since
n_{1}\geq n_{2}\geq\cdots\geq n_{r}
, we obtain from (
11
) and (
10
) that

\displaystyle L\leq\frac{\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda}{\lambda+n_{r}}\left(\sum\limits_{k\in[r]\setminus\{i,j\}}\frac{n_{k}}{\lambda^{\prime}+n_{k}}+\frac{n_{i}-1}{\lambda^{\prime}+n_{i}-1}+\frac{n_{j}+1}{\lambda^{\prime}+n_{j}+1}\right)=\frac{\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda}{\lambda+n_{r}}\leq\frac{\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda}{n-2\phi}.

(14)

In view of (
13
), we obtain

\displaystyle(n_{i}-n_{j}-2)\lambda+(n_{i}-n_{j})\lambda^{\prime}+(n_{i}+n_{j})(n_{i}-n_{j}-1)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(n_{i}-n_{j}-1)(\lambda+\lambda^{\prime}+n_{i}+n_{j})\geq 2(n_{i}-n_{j}-1)(n-2\phi).

Using (
13
) again, we get

(\lambda+n_{i}-1)(\lambda+n_{i})(\lambda+n_{j}+1)(\lambda+n_{j})\leq(n+2\phi)^{4}.

Using the second formula of
L
in (
12
), we get

L\geq\frac{2(n_{i}-n_{j}-1)(n-2\phi)}{(n+2\phi)^{4}}\left(\frac{r-1}{r}n-\phi\right).

(15)

Combining (
14
) with (
15
), we obtain

\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda\geq\frac{2(n_{i}-n_{j}-1)(n-2\phi)^{2}}{(n+2\phi)^{4}}\left(\frac{r-1}{r}n-\phi\right)\geq\frac{2(r-1)(n_{i}-n_{j}-1)}{rn}\cdot\frac{(1-\frac{2\phi}{n})^{3}}{(1+\frac{2\phi}{n})^{4}},

which yields the desired bound since
(1-\frac{2\phi}{n})^{3}/(1+\frac{2\phi}{n})^{4}>(1-\frac{4\phi}{n})^{4}
.

Next, we assume that
\phi\leq\frac{n}{20}
.
Similar to (
14
), we have

L\geq\frac{\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda}{\lambda+n_{1}}\geq\frac{\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda}{n+2\phi}
. Note that

\displaystyle(n_{i}-n_{j}-2)\lambda+(n_{i}-n_{j})\lambda^{\prime}+(n_{i}+n_{j})(n_{i}-n_{j}-1)

\displaystyle=(n_{i}-n_{j}-1)(\lambda+\lambda^{\prime}+n_{i}+n_{j})-\lambda+\lambda^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq 2(n_{i}-n_{j}-1)(n+2\phi)+2\phi,

and
(\lambda+n_{i}-1)(\lambda+n_{i})(\lambda+n_{j}+1)(\lambda+n_{j})\geq((n-2\phi)^{2}-1)(n-2\phi)^{2}
. Therefore, we get

\frac{\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda}{n+2\phi}\leq L\leq\frac{2(n_{i}-n_{j}-1)(n+2\phi)+2\phi}{(n-3\phi)^{4}}\left(\frac{r-1}{r}n+\phi\right).

Simplifying the above inequality, and using
\phi\leq\frac{n}{20}
, we have

\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda\leq\frac{2(r-1)(n_{i}-n_{j}-1)}{rn}\left(1+\frac{8\phi}{n}\right)^{4}+\frac{5\phi}{n^{2}}.\qed

In the above, we get by (
11
) and (
12
) that
\lambda(K^{\prime})>\lambda(K)
when
n_{i}-n_{j}\geq 2
.
Iteratively, we obtain that the Turán graph
T_{n,r}
achieves the maximal spectral radius over all
n
-vertex complete
r
-partite graphs. This recovers a result of Feng, Li and Zhang
[
7
, Theorem 2.1]
by a different method.

Now, we are in the position to prove Theorem
1.5
, which provides the asymptotic spectral radius
for a graph sufficiently close to a Turán graph.
This estimate will serve as a crucial tool in determining the increment on spectral radius
and in proving the subsequent main results.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Let
V_{1},\ldots,V_{r}
be the vertex parts of
K
with
|V_{i}|=n_{i}
for every
i\in[r]
.
Note that
|E(G)\setminus E(K)|=\alpha_{1}
and

|E(K)\setminus E(G)|=\alpha_{2}
.
Let
G_{d}
be the graph induced by all edges of
(E(G)\setminus E(K))\cup(E(K)\setminus E(G))
.
Then
e(G_{d})=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}
and

|G_{d}|\leq 2(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})
.

Proof of part (i).
Recall that
\phi=\max\{n_{1}-n_{r},2(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})\}
.
Then
\frac{n}{r}-\phi\leq n_{r}\leq n_{1}\leq\frac{n}{r}+\phi
and

\delta(G)\geq(n-n_{1})-\alpha_{2}\geq\frac{r-1}{r}n-2\phi

and

\Delta(G)\leq(n-n_{r})+\alpha_{1}\leq\frac{r-1}{r}n+2\phi
.
It follows that

\frac{r-1}{r}n-2\phi\leq\delta(G)\leq\lambda(G)\leq\Delta(G)\leq\frac{r-1}{r}n+2\phi.

(16)

For each
i\in[r]
, we denote
V_{i}^{\prime}=V_{i}\setminus V(G_{d})
and
V_{i}^{\prime\prime}=V_{i}\cap V(G_{d})
.
Let
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the unit Perron–Frobenius eigenvector corresponding to
\lambda(G)
.

Claim 4.2
.

Let
u^{*}\in V(G)
be such that
x_{u^{*}}=\max_{v\in V(G)}x_{v}
.
Then for any
u\in V(G)
,

x_{u}\geq\Big(1-\frac{12\phi}{n}\Big)x_{u^{*}}.

Proof of claim.

For each vertex
v\in V_{i}^{\prime}
, by definition, we know that
v
is adjacent to all vertices of
V_{j}
for any
j\neq i
. So we write
x_{i}
for the coordinate of
\bm{x}
corresponding to the vertices of
V_{i}^{\prime}
.
Fix an integer
i\in[r]
.
If
V_{i}^{\prime\prime}\neq\varnothing
,
then we choose a vertex
u_{i}\in V_{i}^{\prime\prime}
such that
x_{u_{i}}=\max_{v\in V_{i}^{\prime\prime}}x_{v}
.
Clearly, we have

\lambda(G)x_{u_{i}}-\lambda(G)x_{i}\leq\sum_{u\in V_{i}^{\prime\prime}}x_{u}\leq\phi x_{u_{i}},

which yields
x_{u_{i}}\leq\frac{\lambda(G)}{\lambda(G)-\phi}x_{i}<2x_{i}
.
Consequently, we have

0<\sum_{v\in V^{\prime\prime}_{i}}x_{v}\leq|V_{i}^{\prime\prime}|x_{u_{i}}<2|V_{i}^{\prime\prime}|x_{i}.

Thus, there exists a constant
a_{i}\in(-|V^{\prime\prime}_{i}|,|V^{\prime\prime}_{i}|)
such that

\sum_{v\in V^{\prime\prime}_{i}}x_{v}=(|V^{\prime\prime}_{i}|+a_{i})x_{i}.

Note that
\sum_{v\in V_{i}^{\prime}}x_{v}=|V_{i}^{\prime}|x_{i}
.
Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V_{i}}x_{v}=(|V^{\prime\prime}_{i}|+a_{i})x_{i}+|V_{i}^{\prime}|x_{i}=(n_{i}+a_{i})x_{i}.

If
V^{\prime\prime}_{i}=\varnothing
, then we can simply set
a_{i}=0
.
In both cases, we always have
\sum_{v\in V_{i}}x_{v}=(n_{i}+a_{i})x_{i}
for some constant
a_{i}
with
|a_{i}|\leq|V_{i}^{\prime\prime}|
.
Combining with
\sum_{v\in V(G)\setminus V_{i}}x_{v}=\lambda(G)x_{i}
,
we obtain

\displaystyle(\lambda(G)+n_{i}+a_{i})x_{i}=\sum_{v\in V(G)}x_{v}.

(17)

In what follows, we show that
x_{u}\geq\big(1-\frac{12\phi}{n}\big)x_{u^{*}}
for any
u\in V(G)
.
Assume that
u\in V_{i}
and
u^{*}\in V_{i^{*}}
for some indices
i,i^{*}\in[r]
, respectively.
Similarly, let
x_{i^{*}}
be the coordinate of
\bm{x}
corresponding to vertices of
V_{i^{*}}^{\prime}
.
Using (
17
) to
V_{i^{*}}
, we get

\frac{x_{i}}{x_{i^{*}}}=\frac{\lambda(G)+n_{i^{*}}+a_{i^{*}}}{\lambda(G)+n_{i}+a_{i}}=1+\frac{n_{i^{*}}-n_{i}+a_{i^{*}}-a_{i}}{\lambda(G)+n_{i}+a_{i}}.

Since
|n_{i^{*}}-n_{i}|\leq\phi
and
|a_{i}|\leq|V_{i}^{\prime\prime}|\leq\phi
,
we get
|a_{i^{*}}-a_{i}|\leq|V_{i}^{\prime\prime}|+|V_{i^{*}}^{\prime\prime}|\leq|G_{d}|\leq\phi
for
i\neq i^{*}
and
|a_{i^{*}}-a_{i}|=0
otherwise.
Using (
16
), we see that
|\lambda(G)+n_{i}+a_{i}|\geq n-4\phi
.
It follows that

1-\frac{3\phi}{n}\leq\frac{x_{i}}{x_{i^{*}}}\leq 1+\frac{3\phi}{n}.

(18)

Recall that
i^{*}
is the index where
u^{*}\in V_{i^{*}}
. Then we have

-\phi x_{u^{*}}\leq-\sum_{v\in V(G_{d})}x_{v}\leq\lambda(G)x_{u^{*}}-\lambda(G)x_{i^{*}}\leq\sum_{v\in V(G_{d})}x_{v}\leq\phi x_{u^{*}},

which together with (

) implies

1-\frac{3\phi}{n}\leq\frac{\lambda(G)-\phi}{\lambda(G)}\leq\frac{x_{i^{*}}}{x_{u^{*}}}\leq\frac{\lambda(G)+\phi}{\lambda(G)}\leq 1+\frac{3\phi}{n}.

(19)

Combining (
18
) with (
19
), we obtain

\displaystyle 1-\frac{7\phi}{n}\leq\frac{x_{i}}{x_{u^{*}}}\leq 1+\frac{7\phi}{n}.

(20)

Since

is sufficiently large, we obtain
x_{u^{*}}\leq 2x_{i}
. Hence, for any

, we have

-2\phi x_{i}\leq-\phi x_{u^{*}}\leq-\sum_{v\in V(G_{d})}x_{v}\leq\lambda(G)x_{u}-\lambda(G)x_{i}\leq\sum_{v\in V(G_{d})}x_{v}\leq\phi x_{u^{*}}\leq 2\phi x_{i}.

Combining with (

, we get
\lambda(G)\geq\frac{2n}{5}
and

\displaystyle 1-\frac{5\phi}{n}\leq\frac{\lambda(G)-2\phi}{\lambda(G)}\leq\frac{x_{u}}{x_{i}}\leq\frac{\lambda(G)+2\phi}{\lambda(G)}\leq 1+\frac{5\phi}{n}.

Invoking (
20
), we complete the proof of Claim
4.2
.
∎

Claim 4.3
.

For every

, we have
\big(1-\frac{13\phi}{n}\big)\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\leq x_{u}\leq\big(1+\frac{13\phi}{n}\big)\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}

Proof of claim.

Claim

\big(1-\frac{12\phi}{n}\big)x_{u^{*}}\leq x_{u}

. Hence,

\displaystyle(n-24\phi)x_{u^{*}}^{2}<n\Big(1-\frac{12\phi}{n}\Big)^{2}x_{u^{*}}^{2}\leq\sum_{u\in V(G)}x_{u}^{2}.

Note that

.
Then

\frac{1}{n}\leq x_{u^{*}}^{2}<\frac{1}{n-24\phi}<\frac{1}{n}+\frac{25\phi}{n^{2}}.

By Claim

\Big(1-\frac{12\phi}{n}\Big)\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\leq\Big(1-\frac{12\phi}{n}\Big)x_{u^{*}}\leq x_{u}\leq x_{u^{*}}\leq\sqrt{\frac{1}{n}+\frac{25\phi}{n^{2}}}\leq\Big(1+\frac{13\phi}{n}\Big)\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}.\qed

Let
G_{in}
and
G_{cr}
be
two graphs induced by the edges
of

and

, respectively.
Note that

.
By Claim

, we have

\displaystyle\lambda(G)-\lambda(K)

\displaystyle\leq\bm{x}^{\mathrm{T}}\big(A(G)-A(K)\big)\bm{x}\leq\!\!\!\sum_{uv\in E(G_{in})}\!\!\!2x_{u}x_{v}-\!\!\!\sum_{uv\in E(G_{cr})}\!\!\!2x_{u}x_{v}

\displaystyle\leq 2\alpha_{1}\Big(1+\frac{13\phi}{n}\Big)^{2}\frac{1}{n}-2\alpha_{2}\Big(1-\frac{13\phi}{n}\Big)^{2}\frac{1}{n}\leq\frac{2(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})}{n}+\frac{56(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})\phi}{n^{2}},

where the last inequality holds since
\phi\leq\frac{n}{100}
.

Let
\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the unit Perron–Frobenius eigenvector of
K
.
If
\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}=0
, then
G=K
.
So Claim
4.3
can also be applicable for
K
. Then for every
u\in V(K)
, we have

\Big(1-\frac{13\phi}{n}\Big)\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\leq y_{u}\leq\Big(1+\frac{13\phi}{n}\Big)\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\lambda(G)-\lambda(K)

\displaystyle\geq\bm{y}^{\mathrm{T}}\big(A(G)-A(K)\big)\bm{y}\geq\!\!\!\sum_{uv\in E(G_{in})}\!\!\!2y_{u}y_{v}-\!\!\!\sum_{uv\in E(G_{cr})}\!\!\!2y_{u}y_{v}

\displaystyle\geq 2\alpha_{1}\Big(1-\frac{13\phi}{n}\Big)^{2}\frac{1}{n}-2\alpha_{2}\Big(1+\frac{13\phi}{n}\Big)^{2}\frac{1}{n}\geq\frac{2(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})}{n}-\frac{56(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})\phi}{n^{2}}.

Thus, we finished the proof of part (i) of Theorem
1.5
.

Proof of part (ii).

We may assume that
G
achieves the maximum spectral radius
over all
n
-vertex graphs that are obtained from
K=K_{r}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{r})
by
adding
\alpha_{1}
class-edges and deleting
\alpha_{2}
cross-edges, where
n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{r}
and
n_{1}-n_{r}\geq 2k
for an integer
k\geq 0
.
Let
G_{1}
be such a graph obtained from
K_{t_{1}+k,t_{2},\ldots,t_{r}-k}
by adding
\alpha_{1}
class-edges and deleting
\alpha_{2}
cross-edges, where
t_{1},t_{2},\ldots,t_{r}
are as equal as possible and
t_{1}\geq t_{k}
. Since
e(T_{n,r})\geq(1-\frac{1}{r})\frac{n^{2}}{2}-\frac{r}{8}
, the maximality of
G
yields

\lambda(G)\geq\lambda(G_{1})\geq\frac{2e(G_{1})}{n}=\frac{2(e(T_{n,r})-k^{2}+\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})}{n}>\frac{r-1}{r}n-\psi,

(21)

where the last inequality holds since
\psi:=\max\{3k,2(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})\}
and
k\leq\frac{n}{(10r)^{3}}
.
Let
V_{1},\dots,V_{r}
be the
r
partite sets of
K
,
where
|V_{i}|=n_{i}
for each
i\in[r]
.
Recall that
G_{d}
is the graph induced by all edges of
(E(G)\setminus E(K))\cup(E(K)\setminus E(G))
.
Note that
|G_{d}|\leq 2\alpha_{1}+2\alpha_{2}\leq\psi
.
Recall that
V^{\prime}_{i}=V_{i}\setminus V(G_{d})
and
V^{\prime\prime}_{i}=V_{i}\cap V(G_{d})
.
Let
\bm{x}
be the non-negative unit eigenvector of
G
.
By definition, every vertex of
V_{i}^{\prime}
is adjacent to all vertices of
V_{j}
for any
j\neq i
. So we may write
x_{i}
for the coordinate of
\bm{x}
corresponding to the vertices of
V_{i}^{\prime}
.
Let
u^{*}\in V(G)
be a vertex such that
x_{u^{*}}=\max_{v\in V(G)}x_{v}
.
Let
w^{*}\in V(G)\setminus V(G_{d})
be a vertex such that
x_{w^{*}}=\max_{v\in V(G)\setminus V(G_{d})}x_{v}
. Then

\lambda(G)x_{u^{*}}\leq\sum_{v\in V(G_{d})}x_{v}+d_{G}(u^{*})x_{w^{*}}\leq\psi x_{u^{*}}+nx_{w^{*}},

which together with (
21
) gives that
x_{w^{*}}\geq\frac{\lambda(G)-\psi}{n}x_{u^{*}}\geq\frac{1}{3}x_{u^{*}}
.
We assume that
w^{*}\in V_{i^{*}}^{\prime}
for some index
i^{*}\in[r]
, and denote

W:=\cup_{i\in[r]\setminus\{i^{*}\}}V_{i}
.
Since
|G_{d}|\leq 2\alpha_{1}+2\alpha_{2}\leq\psi
, we get

\displaystyle\lambda(G)x_{w^{*}}=\sum_{v\in N_{G_{d}}(w^{*})}x_{v}+\sum_{v\in W}x_{v}<\psi\cdot 3x_{w^{*}}+\sum_{v\in W}x_{v}.

Combining with (
21
) yields

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in W}x_{v}>\big(\lambda(G)-3\psi\big)x_{w^{*}}>\Big(\frac{r-1}{r}n-4\psi\Big)x_{w^{*}}.

(22)

Claim 4.4
.

We have
n_{1}\leq\frac{n}{r}+7\psi
and
n_{1}-n_{r}\leq 7r\psi
.

Proof of claim.

Suppose on the contrary that
n_{1}>\frac{n}{r}+7\psi
. Then
n-n_{1}<\frac{r-1}{r}n-7\psi
.
Fix a vertex
u_{0}\in V_{1}^{\prime}
, and
let
G^{\prime}
be the graph obtained from
G
by deleting all edges incident to
u_{0}
,
and adding all edges between
u_{0}
and the vertices of
W
.
So
G^{\prime}
can be obtained from
K_{r}(n_{1}-1,\dots,n_{i^{*}}+1,\dots,n_{r})

by adding
\alpha_{1}
class-edges and deleting
\alpha_{2}
cross-edges.
Observe that
n_{r}\leq\frac{n}{r}
and

(n_{1}-1)-(n_{r}+1)\geq 7\psi-2>2k
.
For any vertex
v\in V(G_{d})
, we have
x_{v}\leq x_{u^{*}}\leq 3x_{w^{*}}
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in N_{G}(u_{0})}x_{v}\leq\psi\cdot 3x_{w^{*}}+(n-n_{1})x_{w^{*}}<\Big(\frac{r-1}{r}n-4\psi\Big)x_{w^{*}}.

Combining with (
22
) gives

\displaystyle\lambda(G^{\prime})-\lambda(G)\geq\bm{x}^{\mathrm{T}}\big(A(G^{\prime})-A(G)\big)\bm{x}\geq 2x_{u_{0}}\left(\sum\limits_{v\in W}x_{v}-\sum\limits_{v\in N_{G}(u_{0})}x_{v}\right)>0.

Hence, we get
\lambda(G^{\prime})>\lambda(G^{*})
, contradicting with the maximality of
\lambda(G)
.
We conclude that
n_{1}\leq\frac{n}{r}+7\psi
,
which implies
n_{r}\geq n-(r-1)n_{1}\geq\frac{n}{r}-7(r-1)\psi
.
It follows that
n_{1}-n_{r}\leq 7r\psi
.
∎

We denote
\phi^{\prime}:=\max\{n_{1}-n_{r},2(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})\}
. Since
\psi=\max\{3k,2(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})\}
,
by Claim
4.4
, we have
\phi^{\prime}\leq 7r\psi
.
Applying Theorem
1.5
(i), we get

\lambda(G)\leq\lambda(K)+\frac{2(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})}{n}+\frac{56(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})\cdot 7r\psi}{n^{2}}.

(23)

To finish the proof of Theorem
1.5
(ii), it suffices to prove the following claim.

Claim 4.5
.

We have
\lambda(K)\leq\lambda(T_{n,r})-\frac{2(r-1)k^{2}}{rn}\big(1-\frac{28r\psi}{n}\big)^{4}
.

Proof of claim.

Recall that
K=K_{r}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{r})
is a complete
r
-partite graph of order
n
, where
n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{r}
and
n_{1}-n_{r}\geq 2k
for an integer
k\geq 0
.
We define a sequence of graphs as follows:
K(0)=K
and
K(i)=K_{r}(n_{1}-i,n_{2},\dots,n_{r-1},n_{r}+i)
for each
i\in[k]
.
By Lemma
4.1
, we have
\lambda(K(i))>\lambda(K(i-1))
.
Since
n_{1}-n_{r}\leq 7r\psi
,
by Lemma
4.1
again, for each

,
we obtain that
(n_{1}-i+1)-(n_{r}+i-1)\geq 2k-2i+2\geq 2
and

\lambda(K(i))-\lambda(K(i-1))\geq\frac{2(r-1)(2k-2i+1)}{rn}\Big(1-\frac{28r\psi}{n}\Big)^{4}.

Summing the inequalities over all

, we get

\lambda(K(k))-\lambda(K(0))\geq\frac{2(r-1)k^{2}}{rn}\Big(1-\frac{28r\psi}{n}\Big)^{4}.

Note that
K(0)=K
and
\lambda(K(k))\leq\lambda(T_{n,r})
.
This completes the proof of Claim
4.5
.
∎

Combining (
23
) with
Claim
4.5
,
we complete the proof of Theorem
1.5
(ii).
∎

Corollary 4.6
.

Let

∎

5
Structure of graphs with few
F
-copies: Proof of Theorem
1.6

In this section, we present the proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Let
t
be an absolute constant such that
t:=\max\{6r,2e(F),|F|\}
.
Assume that
q\leq\delta_{F}{n}
, where
\delta_{F}>0
is a small number depending only on
F
, and it will be determined later.
It is well-known that
e(T_{n,r})\geq\frac{r-1}{r}\frac{n^{2}}{2}-\frac{r}{8}
. Then

\displaystyle\lambda(G)\geq\lambda(T_{n,r})\geq\frac{2e(T_{n,r})}{n}\geq\frac{r-1}{r}n-\frac{r}{4n}.

(24)

Note that
N_{F}(G)\leq(q+\varepsilon_{1})c(n,F)=o(n^{f})
.
Let
\varepsilon=(10t)^{-15}
be a fixed constant.
For this fixed
\varepsilon
, let
\delta_{\ref{thm-sss}}
and
\eta_{\ref{thm-sss}}
be determined in Theorem
2.8
.
Then we can choose
n
to be sufficiently large such that

\lambda(G)\geq(1-\frac{1}{r}-\delta_{\ref{thm-sss}})n
and
N_{F}(G)\leq\eta_{\ref{thm-sss}}n^{f}
. Thus, Theorem
2.8
implies that

G
can be obtained from
T_{n,r}
by adding and deleting at most
\varepsilon n^{2}
edges.

Now, we proceed with the proof through a sequence of claims.

Claim 5.1
.

Let
V(G)=\cup_{i=1}^{r}V_{i}
be a partition
such that
\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq r}e(V_{i},V_{j})
is maximized. Then

\sum_{i=1}^{r}e(V_{i})\leq\varepsilon n^{2}
and
\big||V_{i}|-\frac{n}{r}\big|\leq\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}n

for each
i\in[r]
.

Proof of claim.

Since
G
differs from
T_{n,r}
in at most
\varepsilon n^{2}
edges, we have

e(G)\geq e(T_{n,r})-\varepsilon n^{2}\geq\frac{r-1}{2r}n^{2}-\frac{r}{8}-\varepsilon n^{2}>\frac{r-1}{2r}n^{2}-2\varepsilon n^{2}.

(25)

Moreover, there exists a partition
V(G)=\cup_{i=1}^{r}U_{i}
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{r}e(U_{i})\leq\varepsilon n^{2}

and
\big\lfloor\frac{n}{r}\big\rfloor\leq|U_{i}|\leq\big\lceil\frac{n}{r}\big\rceil
for each
i\in[r]
.
We select a new partition
V(G)=\cup_{i=1}^{r}V_{i}

that maximizes
\sum_{i<j}e(V_{i},V_{j})
.
Consequently, we get
\sum_{i=1}^{r}e(V_{i})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{r}e(U_{i})\leq\varepsilon n^{2}.

Lemma
2.6
implies that for each
i\in[r]
, we have

\big||V_{i}|-\frac{n}{r}\big|\leq\sqrt{6\varepsilon n^{2}}<\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}n
, where the last inequality holds since
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small.
∎

Claim 5.2
.

Let
S:=\{v\in V(G):d_{G}(v)\leq\big(\frac{r-1}{r}-10\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\big)n\}.

Then
|S|\leq\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}n
.

Proof of claim.

Suppose on the contrary that
|S|>\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}n
.
Then there exists a subset
S_{0}\subseteq S

with
|S_{0}|=\lfloor\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}n\rfloor
.
Setting
n_{0}=|G-S_{0}|=n-\lfloor\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}n\rfloor
and using (
25
), we obtain

\displaystyle e(G-S_{0})

\displaystyle\geq e(G)-\sum_{v\in S_{0}}d_{G}(v)\geq\frac{r-1}{2r}n^{2}-2\varepsilon n^{2}-\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}n\Big(\frac{r-1}{r}-10\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\Big)n

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{r-1}{2r}-\frac{r-1}{r}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}+10\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}-2\varepsilon\Big)n^{2}>\Big(\frac{r-1}{2r}+2\varepsilon\Big)n_{0}^{2},

where the last inequality follows since
\varepsilon=(10t)^{-9}
. For this real number
\varepsilon>0
,
let
\delta_{\ref{lem-ES-super}}
be the parameter determined in Lemma
2.2
.
Then for sufficiently large
n
, we have

N_{F}(G-S_{0})\geq\delta_{\ref{lem-ES-super}}n_{0}^{f}>(q+\varepsilon_{1})c(n,F),

where the last inequality holds since
q<\delta_{F}{n}
and
c(n,F)\leq 2\alpha_{F}n^{f-2}
by Lemma
2.3
.
This leads to a contradiction with
N_{F}(G)\leq(q+\varepsilon_{1})c(n,F)
.
Thus, we get
|S|\leq\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}n
.
∎

Claim 5.3
.

Let
R=\cup_{i=1}^{r}R_{i}
, where

R_{i}:=\{v\in V_{i}:d_{V_{i}}(v)\geq 2\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}n\}
.
Then
|R|\leq\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}n
.

Proof of claim.

For each
i\in[r]
, we see that

e(V_{i})=\sum\limits_{v\in V_{i}}\frac{1}{2}d_{V_{i}}(v)\geq\sum\limits_{v\in R_{i}}\frac{1}{2}d_{V_{i}}(v)\geq|R_{i}|\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}n.

Using Claim
5.1
gives

2\varepsilon n^{2}\geq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}e(V_{i})\geq|R|\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}n,

and thus
|R|\leq 2\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}n\leq\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}n
.
∎

We denote
V^{*}_{i}:=V_{i}\setminus(R\cup S)
for each
i\in[r]
.

Claim 5.4
.

If
u_{0}\in\cup_{i\in[r]\setminus\{k\}}(R_{i}\setminus S)
and

\{u_{1},\dots,u_{t^{2}}\}\subseteq\cup_{i\in[r]\setminus\{k\}}V^{*}_{i}
for some
k\in[r]
,
then
u_{0},u_{1},\dots,u_{t^{2}}
have at least
\frac{n}{2r^{2}}
common vertices in
V^{*}_{k}
.

Proof of claim.

Suppose that
u_{0}\in R_{i_{0}}\setminus S

for some
{i_{0}}\in[r]\setminus\{k\}
.
Then
d_{G}(u_{0})>(\frac{r-1}{r}-10\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}})n

as
u_{0}\notin S
. Claim
5.1
implies

d_{V_{i}}(u_{0})\leq|V_{i}|\leq(\frac{1}{r}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}})n
for each
i\in[r]
.
Since
\cup_{i=1}^{r}V_{i}

is a partition of
V(G)
such that
\sum_{i<j}e(V_{i},V_{j})

is maximized, we have

d_{V_{{i_{0}}}}(u_{0})\leq\frac{1}{r}d_{G}(u_{0})
.
Thus,

\displaystyle d_{V_{k}}(u_{0})

\displaystyle=d_{G}(u_{0})-d_{V_{i_{0}}}(u_{0})-\sum_{i\in[r]\setminus\{k,i_{0}\}}d_{V_{i}}(u_{0})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{r-1}{r}d_{G}(u_{0})-\big(r-2\big)\Big(\frac{1}{r}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\Big)n>\Big(\frac{1}{r^{2}}-(r+8)\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\Big)n>\Big(\frac{1}{r^{2}}-2t\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\Big)n,

(26)

where the last inequality holds since
r+8<2t
.

For every
j\in[t^{2}]
, we may
assume that
u_{j}\in V^{*}_{i_{j}}

for some
i_{j}\in[r]\setminus\{k\}
.
Then
u_{j}\notin R_{i_{j}}\cup S
by the definition of
V^{*}_{i_{j}}
.
Hence, we have
d_{V_{i_{j}}}(u_{j})<2\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}n
and

.
Clearly, we get
d_{V_{i}}(u_{j})\leq|V_{i}|\leq(\frac{1}{r}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}})n

for any
i\in[r]
.
Thus,

\displaystyle d_{V_{k}}(u_{j})

\displaystyle=d_{G}(u_{j})-d_{V_{i_{j}}}(u_{j})-\sum_{i\in[r]\setminus\{k,i_{j}\}}d_{V_{i}}(u_{j})

\displaystyle>\Big(\frac{r-1}{r}-12\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\Big)n-\big(r-2\big)\Big(\frac{1}{r}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\Big)n=\Big(\frac{1}{r}-(r+10)\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\Big)n\geq\Big(\frac{1}{r}-2t\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\Big)n,

(27)

where the last inequality holds as
r+10\leq 2t
.
Combining (
5
) with (
5
),
we have

\displaystyle\Big|\bigcap_{j=0}^{t^{2}}N_{V_{k}}(u_{j})\Big|

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j=0}^{t^{2}}d_{V_{k}}(u_{j})-t^{2}|V_{k}|

\displaystyle>\Big(\frac{1}{r^{2}}-2t\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\Big)n+t^{2}\Big(\frac{1}{r}-2t\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\Big)n-t^{2}\Big(\frac{1}{r}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\Big)n\geq\Big(\frac{1}{r^{2}}-4t^{3}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\Big)n.

By Claims
5.2
and
5.3
, we know that

|R\cup S|<2\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}n
.
So
(\frac{1}{r^{2}}-4t^{3}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}})n-2\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}n\geq\frac{n}{2r^{2}}
for sufficiently large

.
Thus, there exist at least
\frac{n}{2r^{2}}
vertices
in
V_{k}^{*}
adjacent to
u_{0},u_{1},\dots,u_{t^{2}}
.
∎

Since
F
is a color-critical graph with
\chi(F)=r+1
, there exists an edge
uv\in E(F)
such that

is
r
-partite. Thus,
we may assume that
U_{1},U_{2},\ldots,U_{r}
are pairwise disjoint vertex sets of
F
, where
U_{1}
contains the edge
uv
, and
U_{2},\ldots,U_{r}
are independent sets of
F
.
Recall that
N_{F}(G,uv)
denotes the number of copies of
F
in
G
that contain the edge

.

Claim 5.5
.

If
u\in V^{*}_{i}\cup R_{i}
and
v\in V^{*}_{i}
, and
uv\in E(G[V_{i}])
for some
i\in[r]
,
then there exists an absolute constant
\eta_{F}>0
such that

N_{F}(G,uv)\geq\eta_{F}\cdot c(n,F)
.

Proof of claim.

Without loss of generality,
we may assume that
i=1
.
First of all, we can arbitrarily find

|U_{1}|-2
vertices in
V_{1}^{*}
.
These vertices together with
\{u,v\}
from a set, say
U_{1}^{*}
.
Note that there are
{|V_{1}^{*}|\choose|U_{1}|-2}
ways to choose such a set
U_{1}^{*}
, and then we embed
U_{1}
to
U_{1}^{*}
.

Using Claim
5.4
,
we know that the vertices of
U_{1}^{*}
have at least
\frac{n}{2r^{2}}
common neighbors in
V_{2}^{*}
.
So there are at least
{n/(2r^{2})\choose|U_{2}|}
ways to choose a subset
U_{2}^{*}
for adding vertices of
U_{2}
. Applying Claim
5.4
iteratively, for each

,
we can find a subset
U_{k}^{*}
such that

with size

, and

lies in the common neighbors of vertices of
\cup_{i=1}^{k-1}U_{i}^{*}
.
From Claim
5.4
, we see that there are at least
{n/(2r^{2})\choose|U_{k}|}
ways to choose
U_{k}^{*}
for each
k\in\{3,\ldots,r\}

It is clear that

7
Applications

7.1
Graphs with given
F
-covering number

Given a graph
F
,
the
F
-covering number

\tau_{F}(G)
of a graph
G
is defined to be the minimum size of a subset
S\subseteq V(G)
such that every copy of
F
in
G
hits at least one vertex of
S
. In 2021, Xiao and Katona
[
36
]
studied the stability of Erdős–Rademacher’s result and proved that if
G
is an
n
-vertex graph with

e(G)>\lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor
and
\tau_{K_{3}}(G)\geq 2
, then
G
contains at least
n-2
triangles;
see
[
19
,
1
]
for related extensions.
Recently, Li, Feng and Peng
[
13
]
studied
the spectral stability for triangles and proved that
if
n\geq 28s^{2}
and
G
is an
n
-vertex graph with
\lambda(G)\geq\lambda(T_{n,2})
and
\tau_{K_{3}}(G)\geq s
, then
G
has at least
\frac{1}{2}sn-5s^{2}
triangles.
The bound is tight up to an
O(1)
term.

In 2022,
Liu and Mubayi
[
19
]
provided a stability variant of Theorem
1.1
:
Let
s>t\geq 1
be fixed integers and
F
be a color-critical graph on
f
vertices with
\chi(F)=r+1\geq 3
.
Then there exists
C>0
such that if
n
is sufficiently large and
G
is an
n
-vertex graph with
e(G)\geq e(T_{n,r})+t
and
\tau_{F}(G)\geq s
, then
N_{F}(G)\geq s\cdot c(n,F)-Cn^{f-3}
.
We prove a spectral extension of this result.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
s\geq 1
and
r\geq 2
be fixed integers.
Let
F
be color-critical and
\chi(F)=r+1
.
Then there exists a constant
C=C(F,s)>0
such that if
n
is sufficiently large and

G
is a graph on
n
vertices with
\lambda(G)\geq\lambda(T_{n,r})
and
\tau_{F}(G)\geq s
, then
N_{F}(G)\geq s\cdot c(n,F)-Cn^{f-3}
.

Theorem
7.1
extends the above result of Liu and Mubayi, since every graph
G
with
e(G)>e(T_{n,r})
must satisfy the spectral condition
\lambda(G)>\lambda(T_{n,r})
. The case
s=1
in Theorem
7.1
implies that if
\lambda(G)\geq\lambda(T_{n,r})
and
G\neq T_{n,r}
, then
N_{F}(G)\geq c(n,F)-O(n^{f-3})
. This extends the result of Ning–Zhai
[
30
]
on the triangle case to general color-critical graphs.
In addition,
the above bound on
N_{F}(G)
in Theorem
7.1
is tight up to an error term. For example, taking
G=Y_{n,r,s}
or
L_{n,r,s}
, we see that
N_{F}(G)\leq s\cdot c(n,F)+C^{\prime}n^{f-3}
for some constant
C^{\prime}=C^{\prime}(F,s)>0
using Lemma
2.5
.

Proof of Theorem
7.1
.

Suppose that
G
is an
n
-veretx graph with
\lambda(G)\geq\lambda(T_{n,r})
and
\tau_{F}(G)\geq s\geq 1
.
Trivially, we have
G\neq T_{n,r}
since
\tau_{F}(T_{n,r})=0
.
Among all such graphs
G
, we may further assume that
G
achieves the minimum number of copies of
F
.
Since
\lambda(Y_{n,r,s})>\lambda(T_{n,r})
and
\tau_{F}(Y_{n,r,s})=s
, by the choice of
G
, and applying Lemma
2.5
, we have

N_{F}(G)\leq N_{F}(Y_{n,r,s})=s\cdot c(n,F)+O(n^{f-3}).

(31)

By Lemma
2.3
, we see that
c(n,F)>\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{F}n^{f-2}
. Then for any fixed
\varepsilon_{1}\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, we have
N_{F}(G)\leq(s+\varepsilon_{1})c(n,F)
as long as
n
is sufficiently large.
Setting
q=s
in Theorem
1.6
, we see that
G
is obtained from
K=K_{r}(n_{1},\dots,n_{r})
by adding
\alpha_{1}
class-edges and deleting
\alpha_{2}
cross-edges, where
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
are nonnegative integers such that
\alpha_{1}\leq\frac{2s}{\eta_{F}}
for some

,
and
\alpha_{2}\leq 2\alpha_{1}

n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{r}
are positive integers such that
n_{1}-n_{r}<4\sqrt{\alpha_{1}}
.

We claim that
\alpha_{2}\leq\alpha_{1}\leq s
.
Indeed, setting
k=0
and
\psi=2(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})
in Theorem
1.5
(ii), we have

and

\lambda(G)\leq\lambda(T_{n,r})+\frac{2(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})}{n}+O(\frac{1}{n^{2}})
,
which together with

From the above argument, we see that

∎

7.2
A related variant on Theorem
1.2

Recall that
T_{n,r,q}
is the graph obtained by
adding
a star with

q

into a
largest
partite set of

. In the case where

divides

, and

, we see by definition that

Incidentally, we have

. In this section, we prove that the range of

in Theorem

can be extended to be linear under the condition
\lambda(G)\geq\lambda(T_{n,r,q})
, instead of the stronger condition
\lambda(G)\geq\lambda(L_{n,r,q})
in Theorem
1.3
. This result confirms a conjecture of Li, Lu and Peng
[
10
]
.

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
F
be a color-critical graph with
\chi(F)=r+1
. There exists an absolute constant
\delta_{F}>0
such that
if
n
is sufficiently large,
1\leq q\leq\delta_{F}n
, and

is an
n

\lambda(G)\geq\lambda(T_{n,r,q})
,

G
contains at least
q\cdot c(n,F)
copies of
F
.

We point out that when

, the graph

is the unique graph that satisfies the condition of Theorem

and contains the minimum number of copies of

.
Before showing the proof of Theorem
7.2
, we prove the following lemmas. Recall that
L_{n,r,q}
is obtained from
T_{n,r}
by adding a graph
H
into a smallest partite set, where

if

; or

if

.

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
r\geq 2
and
1\leq q\leq\frac{n}{100r}
. Then
\lambda(L_{n,r,q-1})<\lambda(T_{n,r,q})
.

Proof.

We denote by
n_{1}\geq n_{2}\geq\cdots\geq n_{r}
the sizes of

. Let

be a variable with
x>\delta(T_{n,r})=\lfloor\frac{r-1}{r}n\rfloor

By Lemma

, we know that
\lambda(L_{n,r,q-1})
is the largest root of

f(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{r-1}\frac{1}{1+\frac{n_{k}}{x}}+\frac{1}{1+\frac{n_{r}}{x}+\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{w_{\ell+1}(H)}{x^{\ell+1}}}.

Similarly,
\lambda(T_{n,r,q})
is the largest root of

f^{*}(x)=\frac{1}{1+\frac{n_{1}}{x}+\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{w_{\ell+1}(S_{q+1})}{x^{\ell+1}}}+\sum_{k=2}^{r}\frac{1}{1+\frac{n_{k}}{x}}.

To prove

, it suffices to show

for every
x>\delta(T_{n,r})
.
Note that

\displaystyle f(x)-f^{*}(x)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{1+\frac{n_{r}}{x}+\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{w_{\ell+1}(H)}{x^{\ell+1}}}-\frac{1}{1+\frac{n_{1}}{x}+\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{w_{\ell+1}(S_{q+1})}{x^{\ell+1}}}\right)-\left(\frac{1}{1+\frac{n_{r}}{x}}-\frac{1}{1+\frac{n_{1}}{x}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\frac{n_{1}-n_{r}}{x}+\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{w_{\ell+1}(S_{q+1})}{x^{\ell+1}}-\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{w_{\ell+1}(H)}{x^{\ell+1}}}{\Big(1+\frac{n_{r}}{x}+\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{w_{\ell+1}(H)}{x^{\ell+1}}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{n_{1}}{x}+\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{w_{\ell+1}(S_{q+1})}{x^{\ell+1}}\Big)}-\frac{\frac{n_{1}-n_{r}}{x}}{\Big(1+\frac{n_{r}}{x}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{n_{1}}{x}\Big)}

(32)

Using (
5
) and (
6
), we obtain that

\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{w_{\ell+1}(S_{q+1})}{x^{\ell+1}}-\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{w_{\ell+1}(H)}{x^{\ell+1}}>\frac{2}{x^{2}}+\frac{2q}{x^{3}}-\frac{3(q-1)^{2}}{x^{4}}>0,

where the last inequality holds for
q\leq\frac{n}{100r}
and
x>\delta(T_{n,r})
. In the case
n_{1}=n_{r}
, we can see from (
32
) that
f(x)-f^{*}(x)>0
. Next, we consider the case

Exceptional Krall polynomials
A. Kasman, R. Milson

Abstract




1.
Introduction

The purpose of this paper is to describe a novel class of exceptional
Krall orthogonal polynomials of Hermite type. This means that the
polynomials in question are (i) orthogonal with respect to a
Hermite-type weight; (ii) are the eigenfunctions of a higher-order
differential operator; (iii) the degree sequence of the polynomial
family in question is missing a finite number of degrees. Regarding
the second point, unlike the known class of exceptional Hermite
polynomials that satisfy a second-order eigenvalue equation, the
polynomials we introduce here are not eigenfunctions of any 2nd order
differential operator, but are for one of 4th order. Regarding the
third point, our family does not include a polynomial of degree zero
and consequently satisfies a 5th order recurrence relation instead of
the classical 3-term relation. It is for these reasons, that we refer
to the polynomials under discussion as exceptional Krall polynomials.

Classical orthogonal polynomials of the Hermite, Laguerre, Jacobi and
Bessel type are precisely those families of orthogonal polynomials
that arise as eigenfunctions of a second-order differential
operator

The question of classification followed quickly and was often cast

This way of framing the classification question has a natural

At present, the latter problem remains unsolved, but in a series of

A connection between exceptional polynomials and bispectral Darboux
transformations was discovered by studying the recurrence relations
enjoyed by exceptional Hermite polynomials
[

This procedure works as long as we take

where

is a
choice of distinct natural numbers. The corresponding recurrence
relations can then be recovered by applying the bispectral involution
to the corresponding point in the adelic Grassmannian and translating
the differential operators in the spectral variable into difference

The construction that we exhibit here is structurally similar in that
we exhibit the factorization
A^{\dagger}A=p_{2}(T_{2};b)
, where

(1)

p_{2}(T;b):=(T+b^{2})(T+b^{2}-2)

is the indicated 2nd degree polynomial that depends on a continuous
parameter

transformation as
\hat{T}_{4}=AA^{\dagger}
. In this construction, it is not
possible to express the 4th-order
\hat{T}_{4}
as a polynomial of a second
order operator, and so the resulting eigenpolynomials may be
reasonably construed as exceptional analogues of the usual Krall
polynomials. By contrast with orthogonal polynomials where every
degree is present, the exceptional Krall polynomials do not satisfy a
3-term recurrence relation, but rather a fifth order recurrence
relation.

Since the above construction belongs to the class of bispectral
Darboux transformations, there is a dual construction of the
recurrence relations based on a factorization of the

difference operator, where

These polynomials cannot be orthogonal, but as we remarked

Remarkably, orthogonality can be recovered by considering the special

2.
Key definitions and main results.

2.1.
Classical Hermite polynomials

We begin by recalling the definition and essential properties of the
classical Hermite polynomials. We will utilize the monic form of these
polynomials:

(2)

\displaystyle h_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\frac{n!}{(n-2j)!j!}x^{n-2j}(-4)^{-j}

These can also be defined by means of the 3-term recurrence relation

(3)

xh_{n}=h_{n+1}+\frac{n}{2}h_{n-1},\quad n=0,1,2,\ldots

subject to initial condition
h_{0}=1
. The
h_{n}(x),\;n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

constitute a family of orthogonal polynomials because they obey
orthogonality relations

(4)

\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h_{m}(x)h_{n}(x)e^{-x^{2}}dx=\delta_{mn}\nu_{n},\quad m,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

where

(5)

\nu_{n}=2^{-n}n!,\quad n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

In addition to being orthogonal, the
h_{n}(x),\;n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
are
considered to be classical polynomials because they are polynomial
solutions of a second-order eigenvalue equation:

(6)

T_{2}h_{n}=-2nh_{n},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

where

(7)

T_{2}(x,\partial_{x})=\partial_{x}^{2}-2x\partial_{x}.

is the classical Hermite differential operator.

Classical Hermite polynomials obey a lowering and raising relations

(8)

\displaystyle\partial_{x}h_{n}=nh_{n-1},\quad n=1,2,\ldots.

(9)

\displaystyle\left(-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{x}+x\right)h_{n}=h_{n+1},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

These relations are, essentially, a restatement of the 3-term
recurrence relation (
3
). The raising relation, in
particular, directly implies the Rodriguez formula

(10)

h_{n}(x)=(-2)^{-n}e^{x^{2}}\partial_{x}^{n}e^{-x^{2}},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Hermite polynomials may also be recovered as the coefficients of the
generating function

(11)

\Psi(x,z):=\exp(xz-z^{2}/4)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}h_{n}(x)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}

This can be demonstrated by observing that

(12)

(z-2x+2\partial_{z})\Psi_{0}=0.

Expanding in powers of

z
, we see that the latter is equivalent to the 3-term relation
(
3
).

Properties (
4
), (
6
),
(
8
), and (
9
) can all be established
directly by straightforward manipulations of the above
\Psi_{0}
. For
example, the evident relations

(13)

\displaystyle T_{2}(x,\partial_{x})\Psi(x,z)

\displaystyle=-2z\partial_{z}\Psi(x,z)=(z^{2}-2xz)\Psi(x,z)

(14)

\displaystyle x\Psi(x,z)

\displaystyle=\left(\partial_{z}+\frac{z}{2}\right)\Psi(x,z)

directly imply (
3
) and (
6
).
Orthogonality (
4
) can be recovered by observing that

\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int\Psi(x,z)\Psi(x,\bar{z})e^{-x^{2}}dx=\frac{1}{2}\exp(z\bar{z}/2)\Erf(x-\Re z)+C(z),

where

\Erf(x)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{x}e^{-u^{2}}du

is the usual
error function. It follows that

\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Psi(x,z)\Psi(x,\bar{z})e^{-x^{2}}dx=\exp(z\bar{z}/2).

The orthogonality relation for the
Hermite polynomials (
4

expanding both sides of the above equation as power series in
z
and

\bar{z}

2.2.
The exceptional Krall polynomials

We now define some novel analogues of the classical Hermite
polynomials. An important note is that, unlike classical and
exceptional Hermite polynomials, the polynomials we consider here
depend on a continuous parameter, which we take to be
b
. Our
primary definition is the following.

(15)

\displaystyle\hat{h}_{n}(x;b)

\displaystyle:=(b^{2}-2n)h_{n}(x)-\frac{bh_{n}(x)+nh_{n-1}(x)}{x+b},\quad n=0,1,\ldots

Setting

(16)

\displaystyle U(x,\partial_{x};b)

\displaystyle=\partial_{x}+b,

(17)

\displaystyle\hat{A}(x,\partial_{x};b)

\displaystyle=T_{2}+b^{2}-(x+b)^{-1}U

we can restate (
15
) simply as

(18)

\hat{h}_{n}=\hat{A}h_{n},\quad n=0,1,2,\ldots.

We will refer to the resulting family of rational functions as being

quasi-polynomial.
This means that this family consists of
polynomials divided by a common denominator; in this case the
denominator is
x+b
. The exceptional Krall polynomials

\tilde{h}_{n+1}(x),\;n=0,1,\ldots,
can then be given as the numerators of
this family, namely as

(19)

\displaystyle\tilde{h}_{n+1}(x;b)

\displaystyle=(x+b)\hat{h}_{n}(x;b),\quad n=0,1,2,\ldots

\displaystyle=(b^{2}-2n)(x+b)h_{n}(x)-bh_{n}(x)-nh_{n-1}(x)

The resulting expressions are polynomials in
x
having the indicated
degree with a quadratic dependence on
b
.

By inspection, if
b^{2}\neq 2n
, then
\deg\tilde{h}_{n+1}(x)=n+1
. Thus,
generically,
\tilde{h}_{1},\tilde{h}_{2},\tilde{h}_{3},\ldots
span a codimension-1
polynomial subspace, and as we demonstrate below, may be regarded as a
new class of orthogonal polynomial that are somehow related to the
classical Hermite polynomials.

2.3.
Key properties

Next, we exhibit a suite of properties of the above defined polynomial
sequence. These properties are all analogues of the properties
enjoyed by the classical Hermite polynomials: an exponential
generating function, an eigenvalue equation, orthogonality, and a
recurrence relation. The proofs of these assertions are given in the
sequel. The eigenvalue equation involves a fourth order operator,
justifying the “Krall” designation. Since there are missing
degrees, the recurrence relation is fifth order. This is another
consequence of the “exceptionality” of this family.

\displaystyle=\left((x+b)(T_{2}+b)+U-V+3V-2U-2V)\right)\circ\hat{A}^{\dagger}

as was to be shown.

3.1.
Proof of Orthogonality

In this section, we prove the quasi-polynomial orthogonality relations
(
29
). From that, the orthogonality of the
exceptional polynomials shown in (
28
) follows
immediately. The first step is to make sense of the quadratic form
defined by the seemingly singular integral in (
29
).
We do this by introducing a bilinear form on a certain vector space of
quasi-polynomials, and then showing that this quadratic form can be
realized by the integrals in question.

Let
\mathcal{P}
denote the vector space of complex-valued polynomials. We
define a bilinear form on
\mathcal{P}
using the following
definition:

(46)

\eta(p_{1},p_{2})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}p_{1}(x)p_{2}(x)e^{-x^{2}}dx,\quad p_{1},p_{2}\in\mathcal{P}.

The above integral is path-independent and
gives the same value for every contour from
x=-\infty
to

x=+\infty
. This bilinear form underlies the orthogonality of the
classical Hermite polynomials and has the following characterization
in terms of formal anti-derivatives.

Going forward, we will say that an expression
W(x)
is

quasi-rational
provided the log derivative
W^{\prime}(x)/W(x)
is a
rational function. Let

\hat{\mathcal{P}}(x)=\mathcal{P}(x)e^{-x^{2}}=\{p(x)e^{-x^{2}}:p\in\mathcal{P}\}

denote the vector space of the indicated quasi-rational functions.

Proposition 3.3
.

For every
p_{1},p_{2}\in\mathcal{P}
the value
\eta(p_{1},p_{2})
defined by
(
46
) is the unique constant such that

\int\left(p_{1}(x)p_{2}(x)-\eta(p_{1},p_{2})\right)e^{-x^{2}}dx

defines a quasi-rational anti-derivative. That anti-derivative is
unique and belongs to
\hat{\mathcal{P}}(x)
.

The next step is to extend this bilinear form to the quasi-polynomials
defined in (
15
). For a rational function
f(x)
, let

f(x)\hat{\mathcal{P}}(x)=\{p(x)f(x)e^{-x^{2}}\colon p\in\mathcal{P}\},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

be the vector space of consisting of quasi-rational expressions of
the indicated form. Observe that the derivatives of elements of

(x+b)^{-1}\hat{\mathcal{P}}(x)
(i) belong to
(x+b)^{-2}\hat{\mathcal{P}}(x)
, and (ii) have
a vanishing residue at
x=-b
. Conversely, the following holds.

Proposition 3.4
.

A quasi-rational
\phi(x)\in(x+b)^{-2}\hat{\mathcal{P}}(x)
has a single-valued
anti-derivative if and only if

\operatorname*{Res}\limits_{x=-b}\phi(x)=0.

In such a case, there exists a unique
anti-derivative that has the form of a quasi-rational
expression in
(x+b)^{-1}\hat{\mathcal{P}}(x)
plus a constant times
\Erf(x)
.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
U(x,\partial_{x})=\partial_{x}+b
be the differential operator
defined in (
16
). For
b\in\mathbb{C}
, let

\gamma_{b}=\ev_{-b}\circ U
be the functional on
\mathcal{P}
defined by

(47)

\gamma_{b}(p)=(Up)(-b)=p^{\prime}(-b)+bp(-b),\quad p\in\mathcal{P}.

Let

(48)

\mathcal{U}_{b}(x)=(x+b)^{-1}\ker\gamma_{b}=\{(x+b)^{-1}p(x):p\in\mathcal{P},\;p^{\prime}(-b)+bp(-b)=0\},\quad b\in\mathbb{C}

be the subspace of quasi-polynomials in
(x+b)^{-1}\mathcal{P}(x)
whose
numerator
p(x)
satisfies the 1-point condition
\gamma_{b}(p)=0
.

The following characterization of
\mathcal{U}_{b}
is crucial to all that
follows.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
q(x)\in(x+b)^{-1}\mathcal{P}(x)
. Then,
q\in\mathcal{U}_{b}
if and only if

\operatorname*{Res}\limits_{x=-b}\left(q(x)^{2}e^{-x^{2}}\right)=0.

Proof.

Write
q(x)=(x+b)^{-1}p(x)
and let
a_{0}=p(-b)
and

a_{1}=p^{\prime}(-b)
so that

q(x)=\frac{a_{0}}{x+b}+a_{1}+O(x+b).

Hence,

\displaystyle q(x)^{2}e^{-x^{2}}

\displaystyle\equiv\left(\frac{a_{0}}{x+b}+a_{1}\right)^{2}(1+2b(b+x))e^{-b^{2}}+O(1)

\displaystyle\equiv\left(\frac{a_{0}^{2}}{(x+b)^{2}}+\frac{2a_{0}(a_{0}b+a_{1})}{x+b}\right)e^{-b^{2}}+O(1)

Hence, the residue in question vanishes if and only if
a_{1}+ba_{0}=0
.
∎

Proposition 3.7
.

For every
q_{1},q_{2}\in\mathcal{U}_{b}
there exists a unique constant

\tilde{\eta}_{b}(q_{1},q_{2})
such that

\int\left(q_{1}(x)q_{2}(x)-\tilde{\eta}_{b}(q_{1},q_{2})\right)e^{-x^{2}}dx\in(x+b)^{-1}\hat{\mathcal{P}}(x).

The
correspondence
(q_{1},q_{2})\mapsto\tilde{\eta}_{b}(q_{1},q_{2})
defines a bilinear
form on
\mathcal{U}_{b}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume that
q_{1}=q_{2}
. Once we have
established the existence of the quadratic form
\tilde{\eta}_{b}(q,q)
, the
case of the bilinear form follows by a polarization argument that
expands
\tilde{\eta}_{b}(q_{1}+q_{2},q_{1}+q_{2})
.

Suppose that
q\in\mathcal{U}_{b}
. By the argument from the proof of
Proposition
3.6
, if
q\in\mathcal{U}_{b}
, then

q(x)=\frac{a_{0}}{x+b}-ba_{0}+(x+b)p(x),\quad p\in\mathcal{P}.

Hence,

\displaystyle q(x)^{2}e^{-x^{2}}

\displaystyle=a_{0}^{2}\left(\frac{1}{(x+b)^{2}}-\frac{2b}{x+b}\right)e^{-x^{2}}+r(x)e^{-x^{2}}

\displaystyle=-a_{0}^{2}\,\partial_{x}\left(\frac{e^{-x^{2}}}{x+b}\right)+r(x)e^{-x^{2}},

where
r\in\mathcal{P}
is a polynomial. Expand
r(x)
in Hermite
polynomials. The desired constant is then a multiple of the
coefficient of
h_{0}(x)
.
∎

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
q_{1},q_{2}\in\mathcal{U}_{b}
. Then, for every contour from
x=-\infty
to

x=\infty
that avoids the singularity, we have

\tilde{\eta}_{b}(q_{1},q_{2})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}q_{1}(x)q_{2}(x)e^{-x^{2}}dx.

In this sense, we have a generalization of (
46

extends the integral inner product to quasi-polynomials in
\mathcal{U}_{b}

If there is no risk of ambiguity, going forward we will simply write

\eta
instead of

since the formally defined bilinear form
and the bilinear form defined by an integral are equivalent.

The operator

3.2.

4.

By Lemma

The construction of the exceptional Krall polynomials should not be

5.1.
Review of basic concepts.

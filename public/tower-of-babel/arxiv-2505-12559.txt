Semigroups on generalized Sobolev spaces associated with Laplacians with applications Stochastic PDEs with singular boundary conditions
Sergio Albeverio, Zdzisław Brzeźniak, Szymon Peszat

Abstract
. Laplacians associated with domains with singular boundary conditions and are considered together with semigroups on generalized Sobolev spaces, they generate. Applications are given to stochastic PDEs with singular boundary conditions.



1.
Introduction

The description of many dynamical systems in the material world, both by deterministic and stochastic methods, often has to take into account the natural presence of boundary conditions.

Boundary conditions may depend on the geometry of the underlying spaces and are often characterized by specifying values for the solutions and their derivatives on the boundary, such as Dirichlet-type, Neumann-type, or mixed conditions.

Sturm-Liouville problems provide examples of ordinary deterministic differential equations. Probabilistic methods offer new insights into these problems. See the example

[
43

and
[

and
[

for elliptic and parabolic systems and
[

for hyperbolic systems.

In many areas of the applications of mathematics, it has turned out to be useful to consider other types of boundary conditions, namely singular ones, in the sense that they must be defined either by generalized functions supported on the boundary or, more generally, by suitable limits of regularized systems and boundary conditions. A simple example was encountered first in the modeling of very short-range strong interactions in physical systems, see

1.1.
Notation

The set of all natural numbers, including the zero number, will be denoted by
\mathbb{N}

we will denote the subset of

consisting of all non-zero natural numbers.
By

,
i=1,\ldots,n
, we will denote the weak, that is the distributional, derivatives in open subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Some authors prefer to use the notation
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}
. When
n=1

D_{1}
will be denoted by
D
. Next, for a multiindex
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{n})

|\alpha|=\alpha_{1}+\ldots+\alpha_{n}

2.
Preliminaries

Given an open set
\mathscr{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
k=0,1,\ldots
and
p\in(1,+\infty)
,
W^{k,p}(\mathscr{O})
is defined, see
[
2
, Section 3.2]
to be the space of all functions
f\in L^{p}(\mathscr{O})
such that the weak derivatives
D^{\alpha}f
, for
|\alpha|\leq k
, belong to
L^{p}(\mathscr{O})
as well. It is known that
W^{k,p}(\mathscr{O})
is a Banach space with the norm

(2.1)

|f|_{k,p}:=\left\{\sumop\displaylimits_{|\alpha|\leq k}\intop\nolimits_{\mathscr{O}}\left|D^{\alpha}f(x)\right|^{p}{\rm d}x\right\}^{1\over p},

where as usual we put
D^{0}f:=f
. Let us also recall, see
[
2
, Section 3.2]
, that
W^{k,p}(\mathscr{O})
can be defined equivalently as the completion of the space
\mathscr{C}^{k,p}:=\{f\in\mathscr{C}^{k}(\mathscr{O})\colon|f|_{k,p}<+\infty\}
, see
[
77
]
and/or
[
2
, Theorem 3.17]
. Thus in particular,
\mathscr{C}^{k,p}
is dense in
W^{k,p}(\mathscr{O})
.

Remark 2.1
.

Assume that the boundary
\partial\mathscr{O}
is of a class
\mathscr{C}^{k}
and
|\alpha|<k-\frac{1}{p}
. Let
B^{r,p}_{q}(\partial\mathscr{O})
be the Besov space, see
[
101
, Definition 3.6.1]
. It is well known, see e.g.
[
101
, Theorem 4.7.1]
or
[
3
]
, that there exists a unique linear bounded mapping

(2.2)

\gamma_{\alpha}\colon W^{k,p}(\mathscr{O})\mapsto B^{k-{1\over p}-|\alpha|,p}_{p}(\partial\mathscr{O}),

such that

(2.3)

\gamma_{\alpha}(u)=D^{\alpha}u|_{\partial\mathscr{O}},\quad u\in\mathscr{C}^{k}(\overline{\mathscr{O}}).

One first defines a map
\mbox{$\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 2.58865pt\hskip-2.58865pt\hbox{$\SavedStyle\gamma$}\hskip-2.58865pt\hskip-3.94444pt\raisebox{4.20554pt}{\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\SavedStyle\mkern 0.2mu\AC}}}}\hskip-3.94444pt\hskip 2.58865pt}}\,$\par}_{\alpha}\colon\mathscr{C}^{k}(\overline{\mathscr{O}})\cap\mathscr{C}^{k,p}\ni u\mapsto D^{\alpha}u|_{\partial\mathscr{O}}\in\mathscr{C}^{k}(\partial\mathscr{O})
and proves that it is continuous in the norms (
2.1
). Making use of the density of
\mathscr{C}^{k}(\overline{\mathscr{O}})\cap\mathscr{C}^{k,p}
in the space
W^{k,p}(\mathscr{O})
, see
[
101
, Theorem 3.2.5]
, one proves that
\mbox{$\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 2.58865pt\hskip-2.58865pt\hbox{$\SavedStyle\gamma$}\hskip-2.58865pt\hskip-3.94444pt\raisebox{4.20554pt}{\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\SavedStyle\mkern 0.2mu\AC}}}}\hskip-3.94444pt\hskip 2.58865pt}}\,$\par}_{\alpha}
has exactly one continuous extension which is the map
\gamma_{\alpha}
from (
2.2
). For this and related results we suggest to see
[
101
, Sections 2.9.1 and 4.7.1 ]
or
[
3
]
. However, if the boundary
\partial\mathscr{O}
is not regular enough, the situation becomes more complicated. It is our aim to consider here an example of a domain
\mathscr{O}
with irregular boundary, in a sense made precise below.

Let
\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{O})
be the space of test functions, i.e.
\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{O})=\mathscr{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathscr{O})
endowed with the locally convex topology as defined in
[
90
, Definition 6.3]
. By
\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\mathscr{O})
we denote the space of distributions on
\mathscr{O}
in the sense of
[
90
, Chapter 6]
. Let
B
be a differential operator of order
\leq m
with
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\mathscr{O})
coefficients, i.e.

(2.4)

Bu=\sumop\displaylimits_{\alpha\colon|\alpha|\leq m}a_{\alpha}(\cdot)D^{\alpha}u\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\mathscr{O}),\quad u\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\mathscr{O}).

In other words,
B
is a linear operator in the space
\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\mathscr{O})
of distributions on
\mathscr{O}
, defined by

(2.5)

\bigl(Bu,\varphi\bigr):=\bigl(u,B^{\ast}\varphi\bigr),\qquad\varphi\in\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{O}),

where
B^{\ast}
is the formally adjoint operator of
B
defined by

(2.6)

\begin{split}B^{\ast}\varphi&=\sumop\displaylimits_{\alpha\colon|\alpha|\leq m}(-1)^{|\alpha|}D^{\alpha}\bigl(a_{\alpha}(\cdot)\varphi\bigr)\\
&=\sumop\displaylimits_{\alpha\colon|\alpha|\leq m}\sumop\displaylimits_{\beta\leq\alpha}(-1)^{|\alpha|}\,\binom{\alpha}{\beta}\,D^{\alpha-\beta}a_{\alpha}(\cdot)D^{\beta}\varphi\in\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{O}),\quad\varphi\in\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{O}).\end{split}

We will say that
Bu\in L^{p}(\mathscr{O})
if and only if there exists
v\in L^{p}(\mathscr{O})
such that

(2.7)

\bigl(v,\varphi\bigr):=\bigl(u,B^{\ast}\varphi\bigr),\qquad\varphi\in\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{O}).

The next definition is a basis for our whole paper.

Definition 2.2
.

Assume that
\mathcal{B}=\{B_{1},\ldots,B_{N}\}
is a finite set of differential operators of the form (
2.4
). Let us define
W^{\mathcal{B},p}(\mathscr{O})
to be the space of all
u\in L^{p}(\mathscr{O})
such that
B_{i}u\in L^{p}(\mathscr{O})
,
i=1,\cdots,N
, i.e.

(2.8)

W^{\mathcal{B},p}(\mathscr{O}):=\bigl\{u\in L^{p}(\mathscr{O})\colon B_{i}u\in L^{p}(\mathscr{O}),\;i=1,\cdots,N\bigr\}.

The space
W^{\mathcal{B},p}(\mathscr{O})
is endowed with the following norm

(2.9)

\|u\|_{\mathcal{B},p}:=\left\{|u|_{L^{p}(\mathscr{O})}^{p}+\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{N}|B_{i}u|_{L^{p}(\mathscr{O})}^{p}\right\}^{1\over p}.

When the family
\mathcal{B}=\{B\}
is a singleton, we will simply use a simpler notation
W^{B,p}(\mathscr{O})
.

Example 2.3
.

The space
W^{k,p}(\mathscr{O})
, where
k\in\mathbb{N}
, is an example of the space
W^{\mathcal{B},p}(\mathscr{O})
, with
\mathcal{B}=\{D^{\alpha}\colon\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\colon|\alpha|\leq k\}
. The space
W^{\Delta,p}(\mathscr{O}):=W^{\{\Delta\},p}(\mathscr{O})
, is another example of the space
W^{\mathcal{B},p}(\mathscr{O})
.

It is standard to prove that
W^{\mathcal{B},p}(\mathscr{O})
is a Banach space. Moreover, if
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
, then

W^{\mathcal{B}^{\prime},p}(\mathscr{O})\hookrightarrow W^{\mathcal{B},p}(\mathscr{O})

continuously. It may happen that
W^{\mathcal{B}^{\prime},p}(\mathscr{O})=W^{\mathcal{B},p}(\mathscr{O})
with equivalent norms.

In many cases, for example if
B
is a uniformly elliptic operator of order
m=2l
and
\mathscr{O}
is a bounded domain with a
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}
boundary or if
\mathscr{O}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, see
[
101
]
, the space
W^{B,p}(\mathscr{O}):=W^{\{B\},p}(\mathscr{O})
coincides with the classical Sobolev space
W^{m,p}(\mathscr{O})
and the norms are equivalent. For example, we have

(2.10)

W^{\Delta,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=W^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\qquad\mbox{ (with equivalent norms)}.

The last assertion does not take place in case of
\mathscr{O}=\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{a\}
for a fixed point
a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We will come back to this question later on. For simplicity of exposition we always take
a=0
below. Before we state our first result, let us introduce some notation.

3.
The Sobolev space
W^{\Delta,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})

From now on we will consider the case of
\mathscr{O}=\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
. Let us recall that the spaces
W^{\Delta,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
and
W^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
have been defined in Definition
2.2
and Example
2.3
.

We begin with the following two "local" properties of the space
W^{\Delta,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\tilde{\mathscr{O}}
be an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that its closure
\overline{\tilde{\mathscr{O}}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, and let
u\in W^{\Delta,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
. Then

u|_{\tilde{\mathscr{O}}}\in W^{2,p}\left(\tilde{\mathscr{O}}\right).

Proof.

Set

B(0,r):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\colon|x|\leq r\}.

Let us denote by
B^{\mathrm{c}}(0,r)
the complement of
B(0,r)
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then there is an
r>0
such that
\tilde{\mathscr{O}}\subset B^{\mathrm{c}}(0,r)
. Then, since
\partial B^{\mathrm{c}}(0,r)
is regular, we have
W^{\Delta,p}\left(B^{\mathrm{c}}(0,r)\right)=W^{2,p}\left(B^{\mathrm{c}}(0,r)\right)
, and the desired conclusion follows as

v\in W^{2,p}\left(B^{\mathrm{c}}(0,r)\right)\Longrightarrow v|_{\tilde{\mathscr{O}}}\in W^{2,p}\left(\tilde{\mathscr{O}}\right).

∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Assume that
\phi\in\mathscr{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathscr{O})
is such that
\phi=1
in a neighbourhood of the origin
0
. Assume that
u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
. Then the following two conditions are equivalent:

u\in W^{\Delta,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
,

\phi u\in W^{\Delta,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
and
(1-\phi)\mbox{$\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 2.86229pt\hskip-2.86229pt\hbox{$\SavedStyle u$}\hskip-2.86229pt\hskip-3.94444pt\raisebox{4.20554pt}{\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\SavedStyle\mkern 0.2mu\AC}}}}\hskip-3.94444pt\hskip 2.86229pt}}\,$\par}\in W^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof.

The implication (ii)
\implies
(i) is obvious, since the second condition in (ii) implies that the restriction of the function
(1-\phi)\mbox{$\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 2.86229pt\hskip-2.86229pt\hbox{$\SavedStyle u$}\hskip-2.86229pt\hskip-3.94444pt\raisebox{4.20554pt}{\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\SavedStyle\mkern 0.2mu\AC}}}}\hskip-3.94444pt\hskip 2.86229pt}}\,$\par}
to the open set
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
equals to
(1-\phi)u
and thus belongs
W^{\Delta,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
and
W^{\Delta,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
is a vector space.

To prove the converse implication we assume that
u\in W^{\Delta,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
. First, we will show that
(1-\phi)\mbox{$\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 2.86229pt\hskip-2.86229pt\hbox{$\SavedStyle u$}\hskip-2.86229pt\hskip-3.94444pt\raisebox{4.20554pt}{\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\SavedStyle\mkern 0.2mu\AC}}}}\hskip-3.94444pt\hskip 2.86229pt}}\,$\par}\in W^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=W^{\Delta,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Since obviously
(1-\phi)\mbox{$\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 2.86229pt\hskip-2.86229pt\hbox{$\SavedStyle u$}\hskip-2.86229pt\hskip-3.94444pt\raisebox{4.20554pt}{\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\SavedStyle\mkern 0.2mu\AC}}}}\hskip-3.94444pt\hskip 2.86229pt}}\,$\par}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, in view of
[
98
, Proposition III.1.3]
, it is sufficient to prove that
\Delta\bigl((1-\phi)\mbox{$\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 2.86229pt\hskip-2.86229pt\hbox{$\SavedStyle u$}\hskip-2.86229pt\hskip-3.94444pt\raisebox{4.20554pt}{\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\SavedStyle\mkern 0.2mu\AC}}}}\hskip-3.94444pt\hskip 2.86229pt}}\,$\par}\bigr)\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. It is easy to see that for any
w\in W^{2,p}(B^{\mathrm{c}}(0,r))
and any
R>r
there is a
\mbox{$\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 3.71413pt\hskip-3.71413pt\hbox{$\SavedStyle w$}\hskip-3.71413pt\hskip-3.94444pt\raisebox{4.20554pt}{\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\SavedStyle\mkern 0.2mu\AC}}}}\hskip-3.94444pt\hskip 3.71413pt}}\,$\par}\in W^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\mbox{$\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 3.71413pt\hskip-3.71413pt\hbox{$\SavedStyle w$}\hskip-3.71413pt\hskip-3.94444pt\raisebox{4.20554pt}{\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\SavedStyle\mkern 0.2mu\AC}}}}\hskip-3.94444pt\hskip 3.71413pt}}\,$\par}|_{B^{\mathrm{c}}(0,R)}=w|_{B^{\mathrm{c}}(0,R)}
. Therefore there is a
v\in W^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

(3.1)

(1-\phi)\mbox{$\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 2.86229pt\hskip-2.86229pt\hbox{$\SavedStyle u$}\hskip-2.86229pt\hskip-3.94444pt\raisebox{4.20554pt}{\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\SavedStyle\mkern 0.2mu\AC}}}}\hskip-3.94444pt\hskip 2.86229pt}}\,$\par}=(1-\phi)v.

Then

(3.2)

\displaystyle\Delta\bigl((1-\phi)\mbox{$\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 2.86229pt\hskip-2.86229pt\hbox{$\SavedStyle u$}\hskip-2.86229pt\hskip-3.94444pt\raisebox{4.20554pt}{\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\SavedStyle\mkern 0.2mu\AC}}}}\hskip-3.94444pt\hskip 2.86229pt}}\,$\par}\bigr)=\Delta\bigl((1-\phi)v\bigr)=v\Delta(1-\phi)+(1-\phi)\Delta v+2\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}D_{i}(1-\phi)D_{i}v

in the weak sense, and all terms on the RHS of (
3.2
) belong to
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proposition
3.1
can be used in the proof that
\phi u\in W^{\Delta,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
. For, there is an
r>0
such that
\phi|_{B(0,r)}
is constant and
u|_{B^{\mathrm{c}}(0,r)}\in W^{2,p}(B^{\mathrm{c}}(0,r))
. Then by a simple continuity argument

\displaystyle\Delta|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}\bigl(\phi u\bigr)

\displaystyle=u\Delta|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}\phi+\phi\Delta|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}u

\displaystyle+2\times\mathds{1}_{B^{\mathrm{c}}(0,r)}\left(\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}D_{i}|_{B^{\mathrm{c}}(0,r)}\phi|_{B^{\mathrm{c}}(0,r)}D_{i}|_{B^{\mathrm{c}}(0,r)}u|_{B^{\mathrm{c}}(0,r)}\right)^{\sim},

where
\mathds{1}_{\Gamma}
denotes the characteristic function of a set
\Gamma
and
f^{\sim}
is an arbitrary extension to
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
of a function
f
defined on
B^{\mathrm{c}}(0,r)
.
∎

In what follows by
W^{-2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
we denote the space

W^{-2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\left(W^{2,p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)^{\prime},

where
p^{*}
is such that
1/p+1/p^{*}=1
. Note that for any
\psi\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\Delta\psi\in W^{-2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Finally, we understand that

(3.3)

\displaystyle\frac{n}{n-2}=+\infty

\displaystyle\mbox{ if }n=2\mbox{ and }\frac{n}{n-1}=+\infty\mbox{ if }n=1.

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume that
n,N\in\mathbb{N}
and
p\in(1,+\infty)
. Let

(3.4)

\omega=\sumop\displaylimits_{|\alpha|\leq N}c_{\alpha}D^{\alpha}\delta_{0}.

We have:

(i)

if
p\geq n/(n-2)
, then
w\in W^{-2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if
c_{\alpha}=0
for each
\alpha
,

(ii)

if
n/(n-1)\leq p<n/(n-2)
, then
\omega\in W^{-2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if
c_{\alpha}=0
for each
\alpha
such that
|\alpha|\geq 1
,

(iii)

if
1\leq p<n/(n-1)
, then
\omega\in W^{-2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if
c_{\alpha}=0
for each
\alpha
such that
|\alpha|\geq 2
.

Proof.

Let us recall the following Sobolev embeddings:
W^{2,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\hookrightarrow\mathscr{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if
q>n
,
W^{2,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\hookrightarrow\mathscr{C}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if
q>n/2
, and for
k>1
, there are no
q
such that
W^{2,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\hookrightarrow\mathscr{C}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Therefore, if
D^{\alpha}\delta_{0}\in W^{-2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
then
|\alpha|\leq 1
, and
D^{\alpha}\delta_{0}\in W^{-2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
\alpha\colon|\alpha|=1
if and only if
p<n/(n-1)
. Finally
\delta_{0}\in W^{-2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if
p<n/(n-2)
.

Therefore, the problem is only to show that if
\omega
of the form (
3.4
) belongs to
W^{-2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
then necessarily each component
c_{\alpha}D^{\alpha}\delta_{0}\in W^{-2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. To do this consider
\phi\in\mathscr{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\phi(x)=1
a neighbourhood of
0
. Set
x^{\alpha}=x^{\alpha_{1}}_{1}\ldots x^{\alpha_{n}}_{n}
and
\phi_{\alpha}(x)=x^{\alpha}\phi(x)
. Next, let
\alpha^{k}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k}-1,\ldots\alpha_{n})
if
\alpha_{k}>0
. Then for any
\psi\in\mathscr{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\displaystyle D^{\alpha}\left(\phi_{\alpha}(x)\psi(x)\right)|_{x=0}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{1}!\cdots\alpha_{n}!\psi(0),

\displaystyle D^{\alpha}\left(\phi_{\alpha^{k}}(x)\psi(x)\right)|_{x=0}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{1}!\cdots(\alpha_{k}-1)!\ldots\alpha_{n}D_{k}\psi(0)\qquad\text{if $\alpha_{k}>0$.}

Let
\omega
be of the form (
3.4
), and let
\mathcal{I}=\left\{\alpha\colon c_{\alpha}\neq 0\right\}
. We have to show that
\mathcal{I}=\emptyset
if
p\geq n/(n-2)
. Assume that
\mathcal{I}\neq\emptyset
and let
\alpha\in\mathcal{I}
be such that
|\alpha|\geq|\beta|
for any
\beta\in\mathcal{I}
. Then the multiplication operator
\psi\mapsto\phi_{\alpha}\psi
is continuous on
W^{2,p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

\omega\left(\phi_{\alpha}\psi\right)=c_{\alpha}\alpha_{1}!\ldots\alpha_{n}!\delta_{0}\psi.

which leads to contradiction as
\delta_{0}\notin\left(W^{2,p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)^{\prime}=W^{-2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. In the same way we can tread the case of
p\in[n/(n-1),n/(n-2)]
. We need to show that
\mathcal{I}\cap\{\alpha\colon|\alpha|\geq 1\}=\emptyset
. Assume that
\alpha\in\mathcal{I}
is such that
|\alpha|\geq|\beta|
for any
\beta\in\mathcal{I}
and that
|\alpha|\geq 2
. Let
\alpha_{k}\geq 1
. Then there is a constant
c
such that

\omega\left(\phi_{\alpha^{k}}\psi\right)=\alpha_{1}!\ldots(\alpha_{k}-1)!\ldots\alpha_{n}D_{k}\psi(0)+c\delta_{0}(\psi).

Since
\delta_{0}\in W^{-2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
this contradicts the fact that
D_{k}\delta_{0}\notin W^{-2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

It remains to examine the case of
p<n/(n-1)
. In thus case we have to show that
\mathcal{I}\cap\left\{\alpha\colon|\alpha|\geq 2\right\}=\emptyset
. It is easy to show that
\mathcal{I}\cap\left\{\alpha\colon|\alpha|\geq 3\right\}=\emptyset
. For one can use the above arguments with function
\phi_{(\alpha^{k})^{j}}
. It is more difficult to show that
\mathcal{I}\cap\left\{\alpha\colon|\alpha|=2\right\}=\emptyset
. Since
\delta_{0}
and
D_{k}\delta_{0}
are from
W^{-2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
we can assume that

\omega=\sumop\displaylimits_{\alpha\colon|\alpha|=2}c_{\alpha}D^{\alpha}\delta_{0}

Changing variables we can assume that

\omega=\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}D^{2}_{j}\delta_{0}\neq 0.

We can assume that
a_{1}\neq 0
. Let
\varphi\in\mathscr{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
be such that each
D^{2}_{k}\phi(0)=0
and
\phi(x)=1
a neighbourhood of
0
. Consider the following linear continuous mapping
\Phi\colon W^{2,p}(\mathbb{R})\mapsto W^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
;

\Phi(\psi)=\psi\otimes\varphi,\qquad\psi\otimes\varphi(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})=\psi(x_{1})\varphi(x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}).

Then

\omega(\Phi(\psi))=a_{1}D^{2}\delta_{0}(\psi),

which leads to contradiction.
∎

The following result presents a characterization of the Sobolev spaces
W^{\Delta,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
depending on
p
and
n
. Here by
\Delta
we understand the map
\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Assume that
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
p\in(1,+\infty)
. If
u\in W^{\Delta,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})\subset L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
,
v:={\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}u}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
and, see Definition
2.4
above,
\mbox{$\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 2.86229pt\hskip-2.86229pt\hbox{$\SavedStyle u$}\hskip-2.86229pt\hskip-3.94444pt\raisebox{4.20554pt}{\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\SavedStyle\mkern 0.2mu\AC}}}}\hskip-3.94444pt\hskip 2.86229pt}}\,$\par}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

\mbox{$\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 2.603pt\hskip-2.603pt\hbox{$\SavedStyle v$}\hskip-2.603pt\hskip-3.94444pt\raisebox{4.20554pt}{\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\SavedStyle\mkern 0.2mu\AC}}}}\hskip-3.94444pt\hskip 2.603pt}}\,$\par}:=\mbox{$\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 22.46242pt\hskip-22.46242pt\hbox{$\SavedStyle\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}u$}\hskip-22.46242pt\hskip-3.94444pt\raisebox{6.9pt}{\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\SavedStyle\mkern 0.2mu\AC}}}}\hskip-3.94444pt\hskip 22.46242pt}}\,$\par}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

then there exists a finite set of numbers
c_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{C}
such that

(3.5)

-\Delta\mbox{$\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 2.86229pt\hskip-2.86229pt\hbox{$\SavedStyle u$}\hskip-2.86229pt\hskip-3.94444pt\raisebox{4.20554pt}{\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\SavedStyle\mkern 0.2mu\AC}}}}\hskip-3.94444pt\hskip 2.86229pt}}\,$\par}+\mbox{$\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 2.603pt\hskip-2.603pt\hbox{$\SavedStyle v$}\hskip-2.603pt\hskip-3.94444pt\raisebox{4.20554pt}{\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\SavedStyle\mkern 0.2mu\AC}}}}\hskip-3.94444pt\hskip 2.603pt}}\,$\par}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle c_{0}\delta_{0}+\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}D_{j}\delta_{0},&\text{if $1<p<\frac{n}{n-1}$,}\\
c_{0}\delta_{0},&\text{if $\frac{n}{n-1}\leq p<\frac{n}{n-2}$,}\\
0,&\text{if $p\geq\frac{n}{n-2}$.}\end{cases}

Proof.

The proof of this lemma will be preceded by a somehow more general result. Namely, assume that
p\in[1,+\infty)
and
\mathscr{O}
is an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If
u\in W^{\Delta,p}(\mathscr{O})
and
v:=\Delta_{\mathscr{O}}u\in L^{p}(\mathscr{O})
, then

(3.6)

\mathop{\rm supp}(-\Delta\mbox{$\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 2.86229pt\hskip-2.86229pt\hbox{$\SavedStyle u$}\hskip-2.86229pt\hskip-3.94444pt\raisebox{4.20554pt}{\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\SavedStyle\mkern 0.2mu\AC}}}}\hskip-3.94444pt\hskip 2.86229pt}}\,$\par}+\mbox{$\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 2.603pt\hskip-2.603pt\hbox{$\SavedStyle v$}\hskip-2.603pt\hskip-3.94444pt\raisebox{4.20554pt}{\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\SavedStyle\mkern 0.2mu\AC}}}}\hskip-3.94444pt\hskip 2.603pt}}\,$\par})\subset\partial(\mathscr{O}).

To do this let us point out that since
v\in L^{p}(\mathscr{O})
, the extension
\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 2.603pt\hskip-2.603pt\hbox{$\SavedStyle v$}\hskip-2.603pt\hskip-3.94444pt\raisebox{4.20554pt}{\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\SavedStyle\mkern 0.2mu\AC}}}}\hskip-3.94444pt\hskip 2.603pt}}\,
is a well defined element of
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Similarly
\mbox{$\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 2.86229pt\hskip-2.86229pt\hbox{$\SavedStyle u$}\hskip-2.86229pt\hskip-3.94444pt\raisebox{4.20554pt}{\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\SavedStyle\mkern 0.2mu\AC}}}}\hskip-3.94444pt\hskip 2.86229pt}}\,$\par}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and so
\Delta\mbox{$\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 2.86229pt\hskip-2.86229pt\hbox{$\SavedStyle u$}\hskip-2.86229pt\hskip-3.94444pt\raisebox{4.20554pt}{\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\SavedStyle\mkern 0.2mu\AC}}}}\hskip-3.94444pt\hskip 2.86229pt}}\,$\par}\in W^{-2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Hence also
-\Delta\mbox{$\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 2.86229pt\hskip-2.86229pt\hbox{$\SavedStyle u$}\hskip-2.86229pt\hskip-3.94444pt\raisebox{4.20554pt}{\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\SavedStyle\mkern 0.2mu\AC}}}}\hskip-3.94444pt\hskip 2.86229pt}}\,$\par}+\mbox{$\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 2.603pt\hskip-2.603pt\hbox{$\SavedStyle v$}\hskip-2.603pt\hskip-3.94444pt\raisebox{4.20554pt}{\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\SavedStyle\mkern 0.2mu\AC}}}}\hskip-3.94444pt\hskip 2.603pt}}\,$\par}\in W^{-2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\mathscr{O})
. To prove (
3.6
), let us first consider an arbitrary
\varphi\in\mathscr{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with support in
\mathscr{O}
. Then by (
2.12
) we have

(-\Delta\mbox{$\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 2.86229pt\hskip-2.86229pt\hbox{$\SavedStyle u$}\hskip-2.86229pt\hskip-3.94444pt\raisebox{4.20554pt}{\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\SavedStyle\mkern 0.2mu\AC}}}}\hskip-3.94444pt\hskip 2.86229pt}}\,$\par},\varphi)+\left(\mbox{$\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 9.93175pt\hskip-9.93175pt\hbox{$\SavedStyle\Delta_{\mathscr{O}}u$}\hskip-9.93175pt\hskip-3.94444pt\raisebox{6.9pt}{\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\SavedStyle\mkern 0.2mu\AC}}}}\hskip-3.94444pt\hskip 9.93175pt}}\,$\par},\varphi\right)=0.

Next let us consider an arbitrary
\varphi\in\mathscr{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with support in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\bar{\mathscr{O}}
. Then

(\Delta\mbox{$\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 2.86229pt\hskip-2.86229pt\hbox{$\SavedStyle u$}\hskip-2.86229pt\hskip-3.94444pt\raisebox{4.20554pt}{\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\SavedStyle\mkern 0.2mu\AC}}}}\hskip-3.94444pt\hskip 2.86229pt}}\,$\par},\varphi)=(\mbox{$\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 2.86229pt\hskip-2.86229pt\hbox{$\SavedStyle u$}\hskip-2.86229pt\hskip-3.94444pt\raisebox{4.20554pt}{\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\SavedStyle\mkern 0.2mu\AC}}}}\hskip-3.94444pt\hskip 2.86229pt}}\,$\par},\Delta\varphi)=0

because
\mathop{\rm supp}(\Delta\varphi)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\bar{\mathscr{O}}
and
\mbox{$\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 2.86229pt\hskip-2.86229pt\hbox{$\SavedStyle u$}\hskip-2.86229pt\hskip-3.94444pt\raisebox{4.20554pt}{\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\SavedStyle\mkern 0.2mu\AC}}}}\hskip-3.94444pt\hskip 2.86229pt}}\,$\par}=0
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\bar{\mathscr{O}}
.
For the same reason,

\bigl(\mbox{$\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 9.93175pt\hskip-9.93175pt\hbox{$\SavedStyle\Delta_{\mathscr{O}}u$}\hskip-9.93175pt\hskip-3.94444pt\raisebox{6.9pt}{\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\SavedStyle\mkern 0.2mu\AC}}}}\hskip-3.94444pt\hskip 9.93175pt}}\,$\par},\varphi\bigr)=(\mbox{$\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 2.603pt\hskip-2.603pt\hbox{$\SavedStyle v$}\hskip-2.603pt\hskip-3.94444pt\raisebox{4.20554pt}{\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\SavedStyle\mkern 0.2mu\AC}}}}\hskip-3.94444pt\hskip 2.603pt}}\,$\par},\varphi)=0.

Thus the proof of (
3.6
) is complete.

To see (
3.5
), let us choose and fix a
u\in W^{\Delta,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
. Then
v:=\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
, and with our convention concerning the map
\texttildelow
, by (
3.6
) we have

\mathop{\rm supp}(-\Delta\mbox{$\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 2.86229pt\hskip-2.86229pt\hbox{$\SavedStyle u$}\hskip-2.86229pt\hskip-3.94444pt\raisebox{4.20554pt}{\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\SavedStyle\mkern 0.2mu\AC}}}}\hskip-3.94444pt\hskip 2.86229pt}}\,$\par}+\mbox{$\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 2.603pt\hskip-2.603pt\hbox{$\SavedStyle v$}\hskip-2.603pt\hskip-3.94444pt\raisebox{4.20554pt}{\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\SavedStyle\mkern 0.2mu\AC}}}}\hskip-3.94444pt\hskip 2.603pt}}\,$\par})\subset\{0\}.

Then by
[
90
, Theorem 6.25]
, there exist an
N\in\mathbb{N}
and a set of complex numbers
c_{\alpha}
with
|\alpha|\leq N
such that

-\Delta\mbox{$\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 2.86229pt\hskip-2.86229pt\hbox{$\SavedStyle u$}\hskip-2.86229pt\hskip-3.94444pt\raisebox{4.20554pt}{\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\SavedStyle\mkern 0.2mu\AC}}}}\hskip-3.94444pt\hskip 2.86229pt}}\,$\par}+\mbox{$\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 2.603pt\hskip-2.603pt\hbox{$\SavedStyle v$}\hskip-2.603pt\hskip-3.94444pt\raisebox{4.20554pt}{\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\SavedStyle\mkern 0.2mu\AC}}}}\hskip-3.94444pt\hskip 2.603pt}}\,$\par}=\sumop\displaylimits_{\alpha\colon|\alpha|\leq N}c_{\alpha}D^{\alpha}\delta_{0}.

Since

\mbox{$\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 2.603pt\hskip-2.603pt\hbox{$\SavedStyle v$}\hskip-2.603pt\hskip-3.94444pt\raisebox{4.20554pt}{\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\SavedStyle\mkern 0.2mu\AC}}}}\hskip-3.94444pt\hskip 2.603pt}}\,$\par}-\sumop\displaylimits_{\alpha\colon|\alpha|\leq N}c_{\alpha}D^{\alpha}\delta_{0}\in W^{-2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

Lemma 3.5

\mbox{$\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 2.603pt\hskip-2.603pt\hbox{$\SavedStyle v$}\hskip-2.603pt\hskip-3.94444pt\raisebox{4.20554pt}{\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\SavedStyle\mkern 0.2mu\AC}}}}\hskip-3.94444pt\hskip 2.603pt}}\,$\par}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

Define the

\displaystyle:=\intop\nolimits_{0}^{+\infty}{\rm e}^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t}-t}\left(4\pi t\right)^{-\frac{n}{2}}{\rm d}t=(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}2^{\frac{n}{2}}|x|^{1-\frac{n}{2}}K_{\frac{n}{2}-1}(|x|)

\displaystyle K_{\nu}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\bigl(\frac{z}{2}\bigr)^{\nu}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{+\infty}t^{-\nu-1}\,{\rm e}^{-t-\frac{z^{2}}{4t}}\;{\rm d}t,\;\;\;\;|\arg z|<\frac{\pi}{2}\mbox{ or }\mathop{\rm Re}z=0\mbox{ and }\nu=0.

The functions

Proposition 3.6
.

One has

K_{-\frac{1}{2}}(z)=\left(\frac{\pi}{2z}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}{\rm e}^{-z}=K_{\frac{1}{2}}(z).

Proof.

Using the change of variables

[

\intop\nolimits_{0}^{+\infty}{\rm e}^{-(\sigma^{2}+c\sigma^{-2})}{\rm d}\sigma=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}{\rm e}^{-2\sqrt{c}},\quad c>0,

\displaystyle K_{-\frac{1}{2}}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\bigl(\frac{z}{2}\bigr)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{+\infty}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,{\rm e}^{-t-\frac{z^{2}}{4t}}\;{\rm d}t

(3.11)

\displaystyle=2\times\frac{1}{2}\bigl(\frac{2}{z}\bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{+\infty}\,{\rm e}^{-(\sigma^{2}+\frac{z^{2}}{4\sigma^{2}})}\;{\rm d}\sigma=\bigl(\frac{2}{z}\bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}{\rm e}^{-2\frac{z}{2}}=\bigl(\frac{\pi}{2z}\bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}{\rm e}^{-z}.

Using the change of variables

\displaystyle K_{\frac{1}{2}}(z)

\displaystyle=\bigl(\frac{z}{2}\bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{2}{z}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{+\infty}\,{\rm e}^{-(\sigma^{2}+\sigma^{-2}\frac{z^{2}}{4})}\;{\rm d}\sigma=\bigl(\frac{1}{2z}\bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}2\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}{\rm e}^{-z}=\bigl(\frac{\pi}{2z}\bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}{\rm e}^{-z}.

and

on these latter spaces.

The aim of this section is to characterize all linear operators
\left(A,D(A)\right)
on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that:

(7.2)

\mathscr{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})\subset D(A)\cap D(A^{\ast}),

(7.3)

Au=A^{\ast}u=-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}u\mbox{ for }u\in\mathscr{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}),

(7.4)

A=\left(A^{\ast}\right)^{\ast},

(7.5)

\exists\lambda>0\colon(\lambda+A)^{-1}\in\mathscr{L}(L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})).

Clearly, if an operator
\left(A,D(A)\right)
satisfies conditions (
7.2
) to (
7.4
), then it is closed and densely defined.

We start with the following result.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let us assume that
A
is a closed and densely defined operator in the Banach space

satisfying conditions (

) and (

(7.6)

(7.7)

Au=-\mbox{$\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 22.46242pt\hskip-22.46242pt\hbox{$\SavedStyle\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}u$}\hskip-22.46242pt\hskip-3.94444pt\raisebox{6.9pt}{\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\SavedStyle\mkern 0.2mu\AC}}}}\hskip-3.94444pt\hskip 22.46242pt}}\,$\par}\qquad\text{ for }u\in D(A).

(7.8)

W^{2,q}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})\subset D(A^{\ast})\subset W^{\Delta,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})

(7.9)

A^{\ast}v=-\mbox{$\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 22.20313pt\hskip-22.20313pt\hbox{$\SavedStyle\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}v$}\hskip-22.20313pt\hskip-3.94444pt\raisebox{6.9pt}{\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\SavedStyle\mkern 0.2mu\AC}}}}\hskip-3.94444pt\hskip 22.20313pt}}\,$\par}\qquad\text{ for }v\in D(A^{\ast}).

We begin with a proof of the first inclusion in (

is a closed operator, it follows that

and

Now we will prove the second inclusion in (

). For this aim let us choose and fix

and

(7.10)

\displaystyle-(\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}u,\varphi)

\displaystyle=(u,-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}\varphi).

) and (
7.3
) we infer that

(7.11)

\displaystyle(u,-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}\varphi)

\displaystyle=(u,A^{\ast}\varphi),

where on the RHS we have the duality between spaces
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Next, by the definition of the adjoint operator, we infer that
(u,A^{\ast}\varphi)=(Au,\varphi)
. Therefore,

(7.12)

\displaystyle(\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}u,\varphi)

\displaystyle=(-Au,\varphi).

Therefore, in view of Definition

we deduce that

, as claimed. Next we observe that equality (
7.7
) follows directly from just proved identity (

).

Let us now provide proofs of (
7.8
) and (
7.9
). Since for

and

\displaystyle(Au,\varphi)

\displaystyle=(-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}u,\varphi)=(u,-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}\varphi)

-topology. Thus, by the just proved (

) and (

\displaystyle(\varphi,A^{\ast}v)

\displaystyle=(A\varphi,v)=(-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}\varphi,v)=(\varphi,z),\;\;\varphi\in W^{2,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}),

Remark 7.2

v=c_{0}G_{2}+c_{1}D_{1}G_{2}+c_{2}D_{2}G_{2}+g

Thus,

c_{i}=\xi_{i,q}\circ\gamma_{A^{\ast}}(g),\;\;i=1,2,3.

By Lemma
7.3
,
c_{i}\circ\gamma_{A^{\ast}}(g)=0
for
g\in W^{2,q}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Therefore, by Proposition
6.3
, for each
i
there are
\beta^{i}_{0},\beta^{i}_{1},\beta^{i}_{2}
, such that

\xi_{i,q}\circ\gamma_{A^{\ast}}(g)=\beta^{i}_{0}g(0)+\beta^{i}_{1}\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{1}}(0)+\beta^{i}_{1}\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{2}}(0).

Therefore

v=c_{0}G_{2}+c_{1}D_{1}G_{2}+c_{2}D_{2}G_{2}+g\in D(A^{\ast})

implies that

c_{i}=\beta^{i}_{0}g(0)+\beta^{i}_{1}D_{1}g(0)+\beta^{i}_{2}D_{2}g(0).

This proves that

D\left(A^{\ast}\right)=\left\{v=\overline{\beta}g(0)G_{2}+g\colon g\in W^{2,q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\right\}.

Since
A=\left(A^{\ast}\right)^{\ast}
, we see by the Green formula that

D\left(A\right)=\left\{u={\beta}f(0)G_{2}+f\colon f\in W^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\right\}.

In the proof of the last part of the theorem one can adopt arguments from the proofs of the second part. Namely, by Proposition
6.3
and Lemmata
7.3
and
7.4
, there are complex numbers
\beta^{i}_{i}
and
\tilde{\beta}^{i}_{j}
such that

u=c_{0}G_{1}+c_{1}G^{\prime}_{1}+f\in D(A)\quad\text{and}\quad v=b_{0}G_{1}+b_{1}G^{\prime}_{1}+g\in D(A^{\ast})

implies that

c_{i}=\beta^{i}_{0}f(0)+\beta^{i}_{1}f^{\prime}(0)\quad\text{and}\quad b_{i}=\tilde{\beta}^{i}_{0}g(0)+\tilde{\beta}^{i}_{1}g^{\prime}(0).

The desired conclusion follows from the Green formula

\displaystyle\left(u,A^{\ast}v\right)-\left(Au,v\right)=c_{0}\overline{g(0)}-c_{1}\overline{g^{\prime}(0)}-f(0)\overline{b_{0}}+f^{\prime}(0)\overline{b_{1}}

\displaystyle=\left(\beta^{0}_{0}f(0)+\beta^{0}_{1}f^{\prime}(0)\right)\overline{g(0)}-\left(\beta^{1}_{0}f(0)+\beta^{1}_{1}f^{\prime}(0)\right)\overline{g^{\prime}(0)}

\displaystyle\quad-f(0)\overline{\tilde{\beta}^{0}_{0}g(0)+\tilde{\beta}^{0}_{1}g^{\prime}(0)}+f^{\prime}(0)\overline{\tilde{\beta}^{1}_{0}g(0)+\tilde{\beta}^{1}_{1}g^{\prime}(0)}

\displaystyle=f(0)\overline{g(0)}\left(\beta^{0}_{0}-\overline{\tilde{\beta}^{0}_{0}}\right)+f^{\prime}(0)\overline{g(0)}\left(\beta^{0}_{1}-\overline{\tilde{\beta}^{1}_{1}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+f(0)\overline{g^{\prime}(0)}\left(-\beta^{1}_{0}-\overline{\tilde{\beta}^{0}_{1}}\right)+f^{\prime}(0)\overline{g^{\prime}(0)}\left(-\beta^{1}_{1}+\overline{\tilde{\beta}^{1}_{1}}\right),

which leads to the following identities

\beta^{0}_{0}=\overline{\tilde{\beta}^{0}_{0}},\quad\beta^{0}_{1}=\overline{\tilde{\beta}^{1}_{1}},\quad\beta^{1}_{0}=-\overline{\tilde{\beta}^{0}_{1}},\quad\beta^{1}_{1}=\overline{\tilde{\beta}^{1}_{1}}.

∎

8.
Case of
n=2,3

In this section we assume that
n=2
or
n=3

n=2
and

or
n=3

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
u\in W^{\Delta,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
has the representation

(8.1)

u=c_{0}G_{n}+f,\qquad f\in W^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Then for every
\lambda>0
, u can be written uniquely in the form

(8.2)

u=c_{0}^{\lambda}G_{n,\lambda}+f^{\lambda},\qquad f^{\lambda}\in W^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Moreover,

c_{0}^{\lambda}=\begin{cases}c_{0}&\text{if $n=2$},\\
\sqrt{\lambda}c_{0}&\text{if $n=3$}\end{cases}

and

f^{\lambda}(0)=\begin{cases}f(0)+c_{0}\frac{\log\lambda}{2}&\text{if $n=2$,}\\
f(0)-c_{0}\frac{1}{4\pi}\left[1-\sqrt{\lambda}\right]&\text{if $n=3$.}\end{cases}

Proof.

Assume first that
n=2
. Then, see Lemma
3.27
(ii), for every
\lambda>0
,

G_{2,\lambda}=G_{2}+g^{\lambda},\qquad g^{\lambda}\in W^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}).

We have

g^{\lambda}(0)=\left[G_{2,\lambda}-G_{2}\right](0)=\lim_{x\to 0}\left[\log\frac{1}{|\sqrt{\lambda}x|}-\log\frac{1}{|x|}\right]=-\frac{1}{2}\log\lambda.

Therefore, if
u
has representation (
8.1
), then

u=c_{0}G_{2,\lambda}-c_{0}g^{\lambda}+f.

Hence we infer that equality (
8.2
) holds with

f^{\lambda}=f-c_{0}g^{\lambda},\qquad f^{\lambda}(0)=f(0)+c_{0}\frac{\log\lambda}{2}.

Assume that
n=3
. Then, see Lemma
3.27
(iii),

G_{3,\lambda}=a_{\lambda}G_{3}+g^{\lambda},\qquad g^{\lambda}\in W^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}).

Since
G_{3}(x)=(4\pi|x|)^{-1}{\rm e}^{-|x|}
and

a_{\lambda}=\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{G_{3,\lambda}(x)}{G_{3}(x)}=|\lambda|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lim_{x\to 0}{\rm e}^{-|\sqrt{\lambda}x|+|x|}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}},

we have

G_{3,\lambda}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}G_{3}+g^{\lambda},

and

\displaystyle g^{\lambda}(0)

\displaystyle=\lim_{x\to 0}\left[G_{3,\lambda}(x)-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}G_{3}(x)\right]=\lim_{x\to 0}\left(4\pi|\sqrt{\lambda}x|\right)^{-1}\left({\rm e}^{-|\sqrt{\lambda}x|}-{\rm e}^{-|x|}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\lim_{x\to 0}\left[\frac{{\rm e}^{-|\sqrt{\lambda}x|}-1}{|\sqrt{\lambda}x|}+\frac{1-{\rm e}^{-|x|}}{|x|}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}-1\right].

Therefore, if
u
has representation (
8.1
), then

u=\sqrt{\lambda}c_{0}G_{n,\lambda}-\sqrt{\lambda}c_{0}g^{\lambda}+f=\sqrt{\lambda}c_{0}G_{n,\lambda}+f^{\lambda},

and

f^{\lambda}(0)=-\sqrt{\lambda}c_{0}g^{\lambda}(0)+f(0)=f(0)-c_{0}\frac{1}{4\pi}\left[1-\sqrt{\lambda}\right].

∎

Lemma 8.2
.

Let
\lambda>0
. Assume that
u
has representation (
8.2
). For
\beta\in\mathbb{C}
define

\tau^{\lambda}_{\beta}u=c_{0}^{\lambda}-\beta f^{\lambda}(0).

Assume that

(8.3)

\beta\neq\begin{cases}\frac{2}{\log\lambda}&\text{if $n=2$,}\\
-4\pi\sqrt{\lambda}\left(1-\sqrt{\lambda}\right)^{-1}&\text{if $n=3$.}\end{cases}

Then

\tau^{\lambda}_{\beta}u=0\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\tau_{\beta_{\lambda}}u=0,

where

\beta_{\lambda}=\begin{cases}\beta\left(1-\beta\frac{\log\lambda}{2}\right)^{-1}&\text{if $n=2$,}\\
\beta\left[\sqrt{\lambda}+\frac{\beta}{4\pi}(1-\sqrt{\lambda})\right]^{-1}&\text{if $n=3$}\end{cases}

or equivalently

\beta=\begin{cases}\beta_{\lambda}\left(1+\beta_{\lambda}\frac{\log\lambda}{2}\right)^{-1}&\text{if $n=2$,}\\
\sqrt{\lambda}\beta_{\lambda}\left[1-\frac{\beta_{\lambda}}{4\pi}(1-\sqrt{\lambda})\right]^{-1}&\text{if $n=3$.}\end{cases}

Proof.

Assume that
n=2
. By Lemma
8.1
, we have

\displaystyle\tau^{\lambda}_{\beta}u

\displaystyle=c_{0}^{\lambda}-\beta f^{\lambda}(0)=c_{0}-\beta\left[f(0)+c_{0}\frac{\log\lambda}{2}\right]=c_{0}\left(1-\beta\frac{\log\lambda}{2}\right)-\beta f(0)

\displaystyle=\left(1-\beta\frac{\log\lambda}{2}\right)\left[c_{0}-\beta\left(1-\beta\frac{\log\lambda}{2}\right)^{-1}f(0)\right].

Assume that
n=3
, then

\displaystyle\tau^{\lambda}_{\beta}u

\displaystyle=c_{0}^{\lambda}-\beta f^{\lambda}(0)=\sqrt{\lambda}c_{0}-\beta\left[f(0)-c_{0}\frac{1}{4\pi}\left(1-\sqrt{\lambda}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\left[\sqrt{\lambda}+\frac{\beta}{4\pi}(1-\sqrt{\lambda})\right]\left\{c_{0}-\beta\left[\sqrt{\lambda}+\frac{\beta}{4\pi}(1-\sqrt{\lambda})\right]^{-1}f(0)\right\}.

∎

Given
\lambda>0
and
\beta\in\mathbb{C}
, define

(8.4)

\displaystyle D(A_{\lambda,\beta})

\displaystyle=\left\{u=\beta f^{\lambda}(0)G_{n,\lambda}+f^{\lambda}\in W^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{u\in W^{\Delta,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})\colon\tau^{\lambda}_{\beta}u=0\right\},

\displaystyle A_{\lambda,\beta}u

\displaystyle=-\lambda\beta f^{\lambda}(0)G_{n,\lambda}-\Delta_{n,p}f^{\lambda}.

Lemma 8.3
.

Let
\lambda\in(0,+\infty)\setminus\{1\}
and

\beta=\begin{cases}\frac{2}{\log\lambda},&\mbox{ if }n=2,\\
-4\pi\sqrt{\lambda}\left(1-\sqrt{\lambda}\right)^{-1},&\mbox{ if }n=3.\end{cases}

Then

\displaystyle D(A_{\lambda,\beta})

\displaystyle=\left\{c_{0}G_{n}+f\colon c_{0}\in\mathbb{C},\ f\in W^{2,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})\right\},

\displaystyle A_{\lambda,\beta}u

\displaystyle=-c_{0}G_{n}-\Delta f.

In other words
A_{\lambda,\beta}=A_{\infty}
.

Proof.

Assume that
n=2
. Then

u=c_{0}G_{2}+f=c_{0}G_{2,\lambda}+c_{0}\left(G_{2}-G_{2,\lambda}\right)+f\in D(A_{\gamma,\frac{2}{\log\lambda}})

if and only if

That is if and only if

. Summing up, we have proved, as required, that

D(A_{\lambda,\beta})=\mathbb{C}G_{2}\oplus W^{2,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}).

The same arguments work in the case of

.
∎

9.
Heat evolutions and boundary problems

In this section
n=2,3
. Recall that
(A_{\beta},D(A_{\beta}))
,
\beta\in\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
, are operators defined in the previous sections. Each
A_{\beta}
is an operator on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
where
p\in(1,+\infty)
if
n=2
and
p\in(\frac{3}{2},3)
if
n=3
. Since each
A_{\beta}
is self-adjoint on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with resolvent set
\rho(A_{\beta})\supset(\lambda(\beta),+\infty)
,
-A_{\beta}
generates a
C_{0}
analytic semigroup
S_{\beta}
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. We will see later that
S_{\beta}
is given be a kernel
\mathscr{G}_{\beta}(t,x,y)
, that is

S_{\beta}(t)u(x)=\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathscr{G}_{\beta}(t,x,y)u(y){\rm d}y.

Obviously, as
-A_{0}
is the Friedrichs Laplacian, it is well defined on any
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
-space
p\in(1,+\infty)
also in dimension
n=3
.

Let us define the following
generalized Dirichlet map

(9.1)

\mathscr{Dir}\colon\mathbb{R}\ni\gamma\mapsto\gamma G_{n}\in W^{\Delta,p}(\mathscr{O}).

Note that the map
\mathscr{Dir}
is independent from
\beta\in\mathbb{C}
. Let us recall that
\tau_{\beta}
has been defined in (
7.24
). The following result justifies the name we have used for the map
\mathscr{Dir}
.

Proposition 9.1
.

Assume that
\beta\in\mathbb{C}
. Then for every
\gamma\in\mathbb{C}
, the function
u=\mathscr{Dir}\gamma=\gamma G_{n}
solves in the class
W^{\Delta,p}(\mathscr{O})
the boundary problem

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathscr{O}}u(x)

\displaystyle=u(x)\quad\text{for $x\in\mathscr{O}$,}

\displaystyle\tau_{\beta}u

\displaystyle=\gamma.

Proof.

Obviously we have
\tau_{\beta}\gamma G_{n}=\gamma
. Fact that
\Delta_{\mathscr{O}}G_{n}=G_{n}
follows from Lemma
3.8
or more directly Lemma
3.10
.
∎

Remark 9.2
.

Note that the Dirichlet problem

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathscr{O}}u(x)

\displaystyle=\lambda u(x)\quad\text{for $x\in\mathscr{O}$,}

\displaystyle\tau_{\infty}u

\displaystyle=\gamma,

has no solution except
u\equiv 0
in case of
\gamma=0
. Indeed, if
u=c_{0}G_{n}+f
,
f(0)=-\gamma\neq 0
then

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathscr{O}}u

\displaystyle=\lambda u

if and only if
c_{0}=\lambda c_{0}
and
\Delta_{n,p}f=f
. Since the Friedrichs Laplace operator
\Delta_{n,p}
has empty point spectrum we have
f\equiv 0
. Therefore the boundary problem corresponding to
\beta=\infty
is not well-posed.

Proposition 9.3
.

Let
\beta\neq 0
. Assume that:

(i)

For every
t>0
there exists an integrable function
\eta\colon\mathscr{O}\mapsto(0,+\infty)
such that

0\leq\frac{\mathscr{G}_{\beta}(t,x,y)}{G_{n}(x)}\leq\eta(y)\;\;\;\mbox{for all $x\colon|x|\leq 1$ and $y\in\mathscr{O}$}.

(ii)

For every
t>0
the following limit exists

R_{\beta}(t,y):=\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\mathscr{G}_{\beta}(t,x,y)}{G_{n}(x)}.

Then for every
t>0
we have

\left(I+A_{\beta}\right)S_{\beta}(t)\mathscr{Dir}\gamma=\frac{\gamma}{\beta}R_{\beta}(t,\cdot).

Proof.

Let
v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Since the semigroup is analytic and self adjoint, and since
\Delta_{\mathscr{O}}G_{n}=G_{n}
, the Green formula yields

\displaystyle\left(\left(I+A_{\beta}\right)S_{\beta}(t)\mathscr{Dir}\gamma,v\right)

\displaystyle=\gamma\left(G_{n},(I+A_{\beta})S_{\beta}(t)v\right)

\displaystyle=\gamma\left(G_{n},S_{\beta}(t)v\right)-\gamma\left(G_{n},\Delta_{\mathscr{O}}S_{\beta}(t)v\right)

\displaystyle=\gamma\left(\Delta_{\mathscr{O}}G_{n},S_{\beta}(t)v\right)-\gamma\left(G_{n},\Delta_{\mathscr{O}}S_{\beta}(t)v\right)

\displaystyle=\gamma\overline{g(t,0)},

where
g\in W^{\Delta,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is such that

S_{\beta}(t)v(y)=b_{0}(t)G_{n}(y)+g(t,y).

The proof will be completed as soon as we will show that

g(t,0)=\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}R_{\beta}(t,y)v(y){\rm d}y.

We have

b_{0}(t)=\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{S_{\beta}(t)v(x)}{G_{n}(x)}.

Since, the semigroup is analytic
S_{\beta}(t)v\in D(A_{\beta})
. Consequently, we have

b_{0}(t)=\beta g(t,0).

Therefore

g(t,0)=\frac{1}{\beta}\lim_{x\to 0}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\mathscr{G}_{\beta}(t,x,y)}{G_{n}(x)}v(y){\rm d}y

and the desired conclusion follows from the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem.
∎

If
\beta=0
, then by (
8.9
) we have
D(A_{0})=W^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Therefore
-A_{0}
is the Friedrich Laplacian on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In this case
p\in(1,+\infty)
also if
n=3
. The corresponding semigroup
S:=S_{0}
is the heat semigroup on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with Green kernel

\displaystyle\begin{split}0\leq\frac{\mathscr{G}_{\beta}(t,x,y)}{G_{3}(x)}&\leq 4\pi|x|{\rm e}^{|x|}\left(P(t,x-y)+\frac{2t}{|x||y|}P(t,|x|+|y|)\right)\\
&\leq 4\pi{\rm e}\left(\sup_{z\colon|z|\leq 1}P(t,z-y)+\frac{2t}{|y|}P(t,y)\right)\\
&\leq 4\pi{\rm e}\left(\left(4\pi t\right)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\mathds{1}_{\{y\colon|y|\leq 1\}}+P(t,|y|-1)+\frac{2t}{|y|}P(t,y)\right),\end{split}

what completes the proof of part (i). ∎

Proof of part (ii).

The first claim of
(ii)
is obvious. In view of (
A.9
), the right inequality in (
A.10
) is also obvious. In order to prove inequality in (
A.10
) we choose and fix
T>0

and then we note that

0\leq P(t,u+|y|)\leq P(t,y){\rm e}^{-\frac{u^{2}}{4T}},

and hence

(A.13)

\intop\nolimits_{0}^{+\infty}{\rm e}^{-4\pi\alpha u}P(t,u+|y|){\rm d}u\leq P(t,y)\intop\nolimits_{0}^{+\infty}{\rm e}^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}-}4\pi|\alpha|u-\frac{u^{2}}{4T}}{\rm d}u.

Consequently, it is enough to observe that

(A.14)

c(\alpha,T):=1-4\pi\alpha\intop\nolimits_{0}^{+\infty}{\rm e}^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}-}4\pi|\alpha|u-\frac{u^{2}}{4T}}{\rm d}u>0.

Appendix B
Density in Sobolev spaces

This section is based on the fifth section of Chapter 3 of the monograph
[
2
]
, pp. 70–77.

Definition B.1
.

Assume that
n\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}
,
m>0
and
p\in(1,+\infty)
. Let
q
be the conjugate number to
p
, i.e.
q\in(1,+\infty)
such that
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
. A closed subset
F\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called
(m,q)
-
polar
if and only if

(B.1)

W^{-m,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(F)=\{0\},

where
\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(F)
is the set of all distributions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
whose support is contained in the set
F
, i.e.

(B.2)

\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(F):=\bigl\{\Lambda\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\colon\mathop{\rm supp}\Lambda\subset F\bigr\}

and the space
W^{-m,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, usually defined as the dual space to
W^{m,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, is the set of all distributions
\Lambda\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
of the form

(B.3)

\Lambda=\sumop\displaylimits_{0\leq|\alpha|\leq m}(-1)^{|\alpha|}D^{\alpha}v_{\alpha},\;\;v_{\alpha}\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

equipped with the norm

(B.4)

\|\Lambda\|_{W^{-m,q}}^{q}=\inf\Bigl\{\sumop\displaylimits_{0\leq|\alpha|\leq m}|v_{\alpha}|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{q}\colon\;\;\eqref{eqn-W-m,p'}\mbox{ holds }\Bigr\}.

In particular, a closed subset
F\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called
(1,p)
-
polar
if and only if

(B.5)

W^{-1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(F)=\{0\}.

Equivalently, a closed subset
F\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called
(1,p)
-polar if and only for every

\Lambda=v_{0}+\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{n}D_{j}v_{j},\quad v_{i}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\ i=0,\cdots,n,

\mathop{\rm supp}{\Lambda}\subset F
implies
\Lambda=0
.

The following result is obvious.

Proposition B.2
.

Assume that
n\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}
,
m,k>0
and
p,p^{\prime}\in(1,+\infty)
. Let
q,q^{\prime}
be the conjugate number to
p,p^{\prime}
. If

W^{m,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset W^{k,p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

e.g. if

m-\frac{n}{p}\geq k-\frac{n}{p^{\prime}},

then every
(m,q)
-polar set
F
is also a
(k,q^{\prime})
-polar.

Example B.3
.

Suppose that
F
is a singleton, i.e.
F=\{a\}
, for some
a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If

\Lambda\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(F)
, then by
[
90
]
, there exists a finite set of numbers
c_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, such that

\Lambda=\sumop\displaylimits_{\alpha}c_{\alpha}D^{\alpha}\delta_{a},

where
\delta_{a}
is the Dirac delta distribution at
a
. Hence,
F
is not an
(m,q)
-polar set if and only if
\delta_{a}\in W^{-m,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. But, by the Sobolev Embedding Theorem (at least when
m
is a natural number) the latter conditions holds iff the space
W^{m,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a subset of
\mathscr{C}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, i.e. iff
m-\frac{n}{p}>0
.

Therefore, the following holds.

Corollary B.4
.

A singleton subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an
(m,q)
-polar set iff
m-\frac{n}{p}\leq 0
, i.e. if and only if
m+\frac{n}{q}\leq n
. In particular, a singleton subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a
(1,p)
-polar set if and only if
1+\frac{n}{p}\leq n
. Thus, a singleton subset of
\mathbb{R}^{1}
is a
(1,p)
-polar set if and only if
p=1
. A singleton subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, is a
(1,p)
-polar set if and only if

p\geq\frac{n}{n-1}=1+\frac{1}{n-1}.

The following result is a consequence of Corollary
B.4
and
[
2
, Theorem 3.28]
.

Theorem B.5
.

Assume that
a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then the set
\mathscr{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{a\})
is dense in
W^{m,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
iff
p\leq\frac{n}{m}
.

Theorems 3.33 and 3.37 deal with the density of the set
\mathscr{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{a\})
in the space
W^{m,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{a\})
.

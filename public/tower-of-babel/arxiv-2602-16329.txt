Hypercontractivity for a family of quantum Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroups Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: Primary: 46L53, 47D07. Secondary: 81Q10, 33C45 Thanks: Key words: Quantum Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroups, hypercontractivity, optimal time, CCR, Meixiner polynomials
Longfa Sun, Zhendong Xu, Hao Zhang

Abstract
. We show that a family of quantum Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroups is hypercontractive. We also obtain the optimal order of the optimal time up to a constant for those elements whose Gibbs state is zero. The main ingredient of our proof is Meixiner polynomials.



1.
Introduction

In the celebrated papers
[
23
,
24
]
, Nelson showed that the classical Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroup is hypercontractive and utilized such property to establish the existence and uniqueness of the ground state of a special semigroup which arises from the constructive quantum field theory. Since then, hypercontractivity becomes a useful and powerful tool in quantum field theory, quantum statistical mechanics and quantum information.

Gross discovered the equivalence of log-Sobolev inequalities and hypercontractivity, and also gave a new proof of the hypercontractivity for the classical Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroup in the remarkable work
[
17
]
. This surprising observation led to the application of hypercontractivity in numerous fields, such as Boolean analysis, concentration inequalities, geometric inequalities, statistical physics, among others. We refer to
[
18
,
2
]
for more information.

Quantum Markov semigroups, which are a generalization of classical Markov semigroups, are a fundamental tool to describe open quantum systems. In
[
13
]
, Cipriani
et al.
rigorously proved that a specific unbounded Lindblad-type operator generates a quantum Markov semigroup by virtue of the remarkable quantum semigroup theory established in
[
11
,
12
]
. Such unbounded Lindblad-type operator has been extensively studied in quantum optics models of masers and lasers
[
14
,
15
]
. The quantum Markov semigroup appearing in
[
13
]
, which is now called quantum Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroup, has also been shown to be hypercontractive in the seminal work
[
8
]
.

The need to construct quantum Markov semigroups on von Neumann algebras, which are symmetric with respect to a nontracial state, is clear for various applications to open quantum systems, quantum statistical mechanics and quantum information (see
[
19
]
for more information). In the remarkable paper
[
19
]
, Ko
et al.
constructed a family of quantum Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroups, which can be seen as a generalization of the quantum Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroup appearing in
[
13
,
14
,
15
]
.

In this paper, we aim to study hypercontractivities of the quantum Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroups established in
[
19
]
. At first, we introduce such quantum Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroups. To this end, we present Weyl operators and CCR.

Let
H=\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N})
, where
\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,2,\cdots\}
is the set of all natural numbers. Let
\{e_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
be the canonical orthonormal basis of
H
. Then we define the creation and annihilation operators
a^{*}
and
a
as follows

a^{*}e_{n}=\sqrt{n+1}\,e_{n+1},\quad ae_{n}=\sqrt{n}\,e_{n-1},\quad(e_{-1}:=0).

It is clear that they satisfy the canonical commutation relation (CCR)

[a,a^{*}]=1,

where
1
denotes the identity operator by a slight abuse of notation. The number operator
N:=a^{*}a
acts as

Ne_{n}=n\cdot e_{n}.

The position and momentum operators are defined as

Q=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(a+a^{*}),\quad P=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}i}(a-a^{*}),

and so

[Q,P]=i\cdot 1.

For any
z\in\mathbb{C}
, define the Weyl operators

W(z)=e^{\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}(za^{*}+\bar{z}a)}=e^{i(\Re z\cdot Q+\Im z\cdot P)}.

These operators satisfy the Weyl relation

W(z)^{*}=W(-z),\quad\text{and}\quad\forall z,w\in\mathbb{C},\ W(z)W(w)=e^{-\frac{i}{2}\Im(\bar{z}w)}W(z+w).

Let
\mathcal{A}
be the
C^{*}
-algebra generated by all Weyl operators. It is well-known that
\mathcal{A}\subsetneqq B(H)
and
\mathcal{A}{{}^{\prime\prime}}=B(H)
. Let
\omega
be the normal faithful state on
B(H)
, which is defined by

\omega(W(z))=e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{4}(1+e^{-\beta})(1-e^{-\beta})^{-1}},\quad\forall\,z\in\mathbb{C}

where
\beta>0
is a fixed inverse temperature. Note that
e^{-\beta N}
is a trace class operator in
B(H)
. Let

\rho=\dfrac{e^{-\beta N}}{\text{Tr}(e^{-\beta N})}=(1-e^{-\beta})e^{-\beta N}.

Indeed,
\omega
is a Gibbs state, and for any
x\in B(H)
,

\omega(x)=\text{Tr}(\rho x).

We refer the interested reader to
[
7
]
for more details. Now we introduce the quantum Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroups constructed in
[
19
]
. Let the parameters
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy the following relations

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\alpha_{2}^{2}\right)\sinh(\beta/2)=-\alpha_{1}\cosh(\beta/2),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\alpha_{1}^{2}+\alpha_{3}^{2}\right)\sinh(\beta/2)=\alpha_{2}\alpha_{3}\cosh(\beta/2).

Let
G
be the elliptic operator on
B(H)
given by

\displaystyle G(A)=\frac{\gamma}{2}\left(1+\alpha_{2}^{2}\right)[P,[P,A]]

\displaystyle+\frac{\gamma}{2}\left(\alpha_{1}^{2}+\alpha_{3}^{2}\right)[Q,[Q,A]]-i\gamma\alpha_{1}(Q[P,A]+[P,A]Q)

\displaystyle-i\gamma\alpha_{2}\alpha_{3}(P[Q,A]+[Q,A]P),\quad\quad\forall\,A\in B(H),

where
\gamma=\left(1+\alpha_{2}^{2}\right)^{-1}
is the normalized constant. Since
P
and
Q
are unbounded operators affiliated to
\mathcal{M}
, the above is a formal expression.

For
0<p\leq\infty
, let
S_{p}
be the Schatten
p
-class of
B(H)
with the usual Schatten
p
-norm denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{p}
. We use Kosaki’s definition
[
21
]
for noncommutative
L_{p}
spaces.
Let
L_{p}(\rho)
be the closure of all elements in
\rho^{1/2}B(H)\rho^{1/2}
with respect to the following norm

\|\rho^{1/2}x\rho^{1/2}\|_{L_{p}(\rho)}:=\|\rho^{1/2p}x\rho^{1/2p}\|_{p},\quad\forall\,x\in B(H).

Define
\mathcal{G}^{(2)}
on
S_{2}
by

\mathcal{G}^{(2)}(\rho^{1/4}x\rho^{1/4})=\rho^{1/4}G(x)\rho^{1/4},\quad\forall\,x\in B(H).

According to
[
19
]
,
P_{t}=e^{-t\mathcal{G}^{(2)}}
(
\forall\,t\geq 0
) is a symmetric semigroup on
S_{2}
. For any
x\in B(H)
, define

\rho^{1/4}T_{t}^{(\infty)}(x)\rho^{1/4}=P_{t}(\rho^{1/4}x\rho^{1/4}).

Then
T_{t}^{(\infty)}
is an ergodic quantum Markov semigroup on
S_{\infty}
, and
\omega
is the unique invariant state associated with
T_{t}^{(\infty)}
. See
[
19
]
for more details.

For
1\leq p<\infty
, denote by
T_{t}^{(p)}:L_{p}(\rho)\rightarrow L_{p}(\rho)
the usual restriction of
T_{t}^{(\infty)}
to
L_{p}(\rho)
, by using the same way as in
[
16
]
. More explicitly, for
p=2
, if
x\in B(H)
, then

T_{t}^{(2)}(\rho^{1/2}x\rho^{1/2})=\rho^{1/4}P_{t}(\rho^{1/4}x\rho^{1/4})\rho^{1/4}.

Denote by
\tau
the spectral gap of
T_{t}^{(2)}
, which is defined in (
2.1
).

We present the definition of hypercontractivity. Let
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be an ergodic quantum Markov semigroup with the invariant state induced by
\rho
. Then
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is said to be hypercontractive if for any
2<p<\infty
, there exists
t_{p}>0
such that for any
t\geq t_{p}
, and for any
x\in B(H)
with
\omega(x)=0
,

(1.1)

\|T_{t}(\rho^{1/2}x\rho^{1/2})\|_{L_{p}(\rho)}\leq\|\rho^{1/2}x\rho^{1/2}\|_{L_{2}(\rho)}.

Moreover, the least
t_{p}
is called the optimal time of
T_{t}
with respect to the parameter
p
.

Our result is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

(T_{t}^{(2)})_{t\geq 0}
is hypercontractive. Moreover, the optimal time
t_{p}
satisfies

\widetilde{c}(\beta)(p-1)\leq e^{2\tau t_{p}}\leq\widetilde{C}(\beta)(p-1),\quad\forall\,2<p<\infty,

where
\widetilde{c}(\beta)
and
\widetilde{C}(\beta)
are positive constants depending only on
\beta
.

We can also obtain some estimate of the optimal time of
(T_{t}^{(2)})_{t\geq 0}
for all
x\in B(H)
without the restriction
\omega(x)=0
with the help of Theorem
1.1
. For any
2<p<\infty
, let
t_{p}^{\prime}>0
be the least constant such that for any
t\geq t_{p}^{\prime}
, and for any
x\in B(H)
,

\|T_{t}^{(2)}(\rho^{1/2}x\rho^{1/2})\|_{L_{p}(\rho)}\leq\|\rho^{1/2}x\rho^{1/2}\|_{L_{2}(\rho)}.

Corollary 1.2
.

For
2<p<\infty
, the optimal time
t^{\prime}_{p}
satisfies

\widetilde{c}_{1}(\beta)(p-1)\leq e^{2\tau t^{\prime}_{p}}\leq\widetilde{C}_{1}(\beta)(p-1)^{2},

where
\widetilde{c}_{1}(\beta)
and
\widetilde{C}_{1}(\beta)
are positive constants depending only on
\beta
.

Several comments are in order. Theorem
1.1
implies that
(p-1)^{1/2}
is the optimal order of
e^{\tau t_{p}}
up to a constant depending only on
\beta
. Our proof of Theorem
1.1
relies heavily on Meixiner polynomials. To the best of our knowledge, this is perhaps the first time that Meixiner polynomials are used to study quantum Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroups. Therefore, the proof of Theorem
1.1
is different from the previous ones. Throughout this paper, we do not care about the constants
\widetilde{c}(\beta)
and
\widetilde{C}(\beta)
as we are only concerned with the order
(p-1)^{1/2}
. In addition, we would like to mention that at the time of this writing, we do not know the optimal order of the optimal time
t_{p}^{\prime}
. We come up with a conjecture on
t_{p}^{\prime}
at the end of this paper.

(T_{t}^{(2)})_{t\geq 0}
is called quantum Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroups, but it does not behave like the classcial Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroup. Indeed, for the classcial Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroup
[
24
]
or the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroup in the mixed
q
-Gaussian setting
[
10
,
5
]
, or even for the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroup on the
q
-Araki–Woods algebras
[
22
]
, the optimal time has been already calculated explicitly or estimated. Besides, they are all ultracontractive
[
6
]
. This is because the constant
c_{p}
appearing in Proposition
2.1
is always a universal constant which does not depend on
p
for all Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroups mentioned above except the quantum Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroups considered in this paper. We will see that for quantum Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroups, the constant
c_{p}
appearing in Proposition
2.1
does depend on
p
, which is also the main difficulty in the estimate of
t_{p}
and
t^{\prime}_{p}
.

In particular, when
\alpha_{1}=-\alpha_{2}
and
\alpha_{3}=1
, the quantum Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroup
T_{t}^{(2)}
is as same as in that in
[
13
]
. On the one hand, in this special case, it has already been shown in
[
8
]
that
T_{t}^{(2)}
is hypercontractive. The authors in
[
8
]
employed the birth and death processes in an ingenious way to show the hypercontractivity of
T_{t}^{(2)}
. However, they only showed that
T_{t}^{(2)}
is contractive from
L_{2}(\rho)
to
L_{4}(\rho)
once
t
is larger than some fixed time. They did not consider directly the contractivity of
T_{t}^{(2)}
from
L_{2}(\rho)
to
L_{p}(\rho)
for
2\leq p\neq 4<\infty
. It seems that their method does not yield the best estimate of optimal time when
p
is very large. On the other hand, the best constants of
p
-log-Sobolev inequalities of
T_{t}^{(2)}
have been calculated explicitly in the remarkable papers by Beigi and Rahimi-Keshari
[
4
]
for
1\leq p\leq 2
and by Carlen and Maas
[
9
]
for
p=1
respectively. In
[
4
]
, Beigi
et al.
also gave an estimate of the optimal time, but their coefficient of
\ln(p-1)
for the optimal time is larger than ours given in Theorem
1.1
or Corollary
1.2
. This also reveals that in general, the best constants of log-Sobolev inequalities cannot yield the optimal time, which is quite different from the case of the classical Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroup. In addition, we would like to emphasize that the quantum Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroups which we consider in this paper are much more general than that in
[
13
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we present more details on the eigenvalues and corresponding eigenvectors of
T_{t}^{(2)}
. We also reduce the proof of Theorem
1.1
to that of Theorem
2.3
. Section
3
is devoted to the proof of Theorem
2.3
. In Section
4
and Section
5
, we will give the estimate of the optimal time
t_{p}
and
t^{\prime}_{p}
respectively.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Eigenvalues and eigenvectors

In the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we need to use the eigenvalues of
\mathcal{G}^{(2)}
and the associated eigenvectors on
S_{2}
. From
[
19
]
, the spectra of the generator
\mathcal{G}^{(2)}
are discrete, i.e.,
\sigma(\mathcal{G}^{(2)})=\left\{m\tau_{1}+n\tau_{2}:m,n=0,1,2,\ldots\right\}
, where
\tau_{1}=-2\gamma\alpha_{1},\tau_{2}=2\gamma\alpha_{2}\alpha_{3}
. Denote by

(2.1)

\tau=\min\{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\}>0.

In the following, we are about to introduce eigenvectors of
\mathcal{G}^{(2)}
.

Let
J:S_{2}\rightarrow S_{2}
be the modular conjugation defined by

J(y)=y^{*},\quad\text{for}\quad y\in S_{2}.

Let
L_{y}
and
R_{y}
be the left multiplication and right multiplication operators respectively. Define

j(x)=JxJ=R_{x^{*}}\in B(S_{2})

for
x\in B(H)
. Here,
x
denotes the left multiplication
L_{x}
on
S_{2}
. We write

\displaystyle D_{1}:=(2\sinh(\beta/2))^{-1/2}\left(e^{\beta/4}a-e^{-\beta/4}j\left(a^{*}\right)\right)

\displaystyle D_{2}:=(2\sinh(\beta/2))^{-1/2}\left(e^{-\beta/4}a^{*}-e^{\beta/4}j(a)\right).

A direct calculation shows that
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
satisfy the following canonical commutation relations

\begin{gathered}{\left[D_{i},D_{j}\right]=0,\quad\left[D_{i}^{*},D_{j}^{*}\right]=0}\\
{\left[D_{i},D_{j}^{*}\right]=\delta_{ij}\mathbf{1}}.\end{gathered}

Define

A_{1}=2^{-1/2}\left(D_{1}-D_{2}\right),\quad A_{2}=2^{-1/2}\left(D_{1}+D_{2}\right).

Then
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
also satisfy the canonical commutation relations. Let
\xi_{0}=\rho^{1/2}
. In
[
19
]
, the authors showed that for any
m,n\in\mathbb{N}

(2.2)

\mathcal{G}^{(2)}((A_{1}^{*})^{m}\left(A_{2}^{*}\right)^{n}\xi_{0})=(m\tau_{1}+n\tau_{2})(A_{1}^{*})^{m}\left(A_{2}^{*}\right)^{n}\xi_{0}.

Besides, from the canonical commutation relations of
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
, one has

\|(A_{1}^{*})^{m}\left(A_{2}^{*}\right)^{n}\xi_{0}\|_{2}=\sqrt{m!}\cdot\sqrt{n!}.

By induction, it is clear that

(A_{1}^{*})^{m}\left(A_{2}^{*}\right)^{n}\xi_{0}\in L_{m+n}:=\text{span}\{(a^{*})^{i}a^{j}\xi_{0}:0\leq i,j\leq m+n,i+j\leq m+n\}.

We refer to
[
19
]
for more details on eigenvectors of
\mathcal{G}^{(2)}
. Denote by

\xi_{m,n}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{m!}\cdot\sqrt{n!}}(A_{1}^{*})^{m}\left(A_{2}^{*}\right)^{n}\xi_{0},\quad m,n=0,1,2,\cdots.

Thus
\{\xi_{m,n}\}
is an orthonormal basis of
S_{2}
.

2.2.
Theorem
2.3
implies Theorem
1.1

In this subsection, we aim to reduce the proof of Theorem
1.1
to that of Theorem
2.3
. In the sequel, we always assume
2\leq p<\infty
.

Proposition 2.1
.

T_{t}^{(2)}
is hypercontractive if for any
2<p<\infty
, there exists a constant
c_{p}>0
such that for any
x\in L_{k}
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\left\|\rho^{1/(2p)-1/4}x\rho^{1/(2p)-1/4}\right\|_{p}\leq c_{p}^{k}\left\|x\right\|_{2}.

Moreover, the optimal time
t_{p}
satisfies

(2.4)

e^{2\tau t_{p}}\leq 2c_{p}^{2},\quad\forall\,2<p<\infty.

Proof.

Assume that
\{c_{n,m}:m,n=0,1,2,\cdots\}\subset\mathbb{C}
has finite non-zero terms, and suppose

x=\rho^{1/4}y\rho^{1/4}=\sum_{m,n=0}^{\infty}c_{m,n}\xi_{m,n},\quad\|\rho^{1/4}y\rho^{1/4}\|_{2}=\left(\sum_{m,n=0}^{\infty}|c_{m,n}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Then by (
2.2
), one has

T_{t}^{(2)}(\rho^{1/2}y\rho^{1/2})=\rho^{1/4}P_{t}(\rho^{1/4}y\rho^{1/4})\rho^{1/4}=\sum_{m,n=0}^{\infty}c_{m,n}e^{-t(m\tau_{1}+n\tau_{2})}\rho^{1/4}\xi_{m,n}\rho^{1/4}.

Now assume
\omega(y)=0
, and we have

x=\rho^{1/4}y\rho^{1/4}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m+n=k}c_{m,n}\xi_{m,n}.

The left-hand-side of (
1.1
) can be estimated by

\displaystyle\|T_{t}^{(2)}(\rho^{1/2}y\rho^{1/2})\|_{L_{p}(\rho)}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m+n=k}c_{m,n}e^{-t(m\tau_{1}+n\tau_{2})}\rho^{1/2p-1/4}\xi_{m,n}\rho^{1/2p-1/4}\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\|\sum_{m+n=k}c_{m,n}e^{-t(m\tau_{1}+n\tau_{2})}\rho^{1/2p-1/4}\xi_{m,n}\rho^{1/2p-1/4}\right\|_{p}

(by (
2.3
))

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}c_{p}^{k}\left\|\sum_{m+n=k}e^{-t(m\tau_{1}+n\tau_{2})}c_{m,n}\xi_{m,n}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}c_{p}^{k}\left(\sum_{m+n=k}e^{-2t(m\tau_{1}+n\tau_{2})}|c_{m,n}|^{2}\right)^{1/2},

which yields

\displaystyle\|T_{t}^{(2)}(\rho^{1/2}y\rho^{1/2})\|_{L_{p}(\rho)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e^{-k\tau t}c_{p}^{k}\left(\sum_{m+n=k}|c_{m,n}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e^{-2k\tau t}c_{p}^{2k}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m+n=k}^{\infty}|c_{m,n}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{e^{-2\tau t}c_{p}^{2}}{1-e^{-2\tau t}c_{p}^{2}}\right)^{1/2}\|\rho^{1/2}y\rho^{1/2}\|_{L_{2}(\rho)}.

Then we obtain

\displaystyle\|T_{t}^{(2)}(\rho^{1/2}y\rho^{1/2})\|_{L_{p}(\rho)}

\displaystyle\leq\|\rho^{1/2}y\rho^{1/2}\|_{L_{2}(\rho)},

where we require

\frac{e^{-2\tau t}c_{p}^{2}}{1-e^{-2\tau t}c_{p}^{2}}\leq 1\quad\text{i.e.}\quad e^{2\tau t}\geq 2c_{p}^{2}.

Hence, the optimal time
t_{p}
satisfies

e^{2\tau t_{p}}\leq 2c_{p}^{2}.

We complete the proof.
∎

Note that (
2.3
) is trivial when
k=0
. Now we are going to show (

) for
k\geq 1
. So suppose

and

x=\sum_{i+j\leq k}c_{i,j}(a^{*})^{i}a^{j}\xi_{0}\in L_{k},

where
c_{i,j}\in\mathbb{C}
. In what follows,

and

Then by direct calculation, for

(a^{*})^{i}a^{j}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq i,n\geq 0\\

Thus,

\displaystyle x_{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i+j\leq k,j-i=m}c_{i,j}(a^{*})^{i}a^{j}\xi_{0}

\displaystyle=(1-e^{-\beta})^{1/2}\sum_{i+j\leq k,j-i=m}c_{i,j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq i,n\geq 0\\

\displaystyle=(1-e^{-\beta})^{1/2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2i+m\leq k\\

For convenience, we set
f_{k,n,m}=0
whenever

. Direct calculation shows that

\displaystyle\rho^{1/(2p)-1/4}x_{m}\rho^{1/(2p)-1/4}

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle(1-e^{-\beta})^{1/p}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(e^{-n\beta(1/(2p)-1/4)}e^{-(n+m)\beta(1/(2p)-1/4)}e^{-(n+m)\beta/2}f_{k,n,m}\right)e_{n}\otimes e_{n+m}

\displaystyle=\

Then for all

\displaystyle\left\|\rho^{1/2p-1/4}x_{m}\rho^{1/2p-1/4}\right\|_{p}

In particular,

\displaystyle\|x_{m}\|_{2}

\displaystyle=(1-e^{-\beta})^{1/2}e^{-m/2}\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}e^{-n\beta}|f_{k,n,m}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

With these notations above, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 2.2
.

The inequality (
2.3
) holds if there exists a constant
C_{p}
such that for all
k\geq 1
and
-k\leq m\leq k
,

(2.6)

\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}|f_{k,n,m}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leq e^{m\beta(1/2p-1/4)}C_{p}^{k}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}|f_{k,n,m}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Moreover, the constant
c_{p}
in (
2.3
) satisfies

c_{p}\leq\sqrt{3}(1-e^{-\beta})^{-1/2}C_{p}.

Proof.

Applying (
2.6
) to (
2.2
), one has

\displaystyle\left\|\rho^{1/2p-1/4}x_{m}\rho^{1/2p-1/4}\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}^{k}(1-e^{-\beta})^{1/p}e^{-m/2}\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}e^{-n\beta}|f_{k,n,m}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=C_{p}^{k}(1-e^{-\beta})^{1/p-1/2}\|x_{m}\|_{2}\leq\left[C_{p}(1-e^{-\beta})^{-1/2}\right]^{k}\|x_{m}\|_{2}.

Then (
2.3
) follows from

\displaystyle\left\|\rho^{1/2p-1/4}x\rho^{1/2p-1/4}\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m=-k}^{k}\left\|\rho^{1/2p-1/4}x_{m}\rho^{1/2p-1/4}\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m=-k}^{k}\left[C_{p}(1-e^{-\beta})^{-1/2}\right]^{k}\left\|x_{m}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left[C_{p}(1-e^{-\beta})^{-1/2}\right]^{k}(2k+1)^{1/2}\|x\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left[\sqrt{3}(1-e^{-\beta})^{-1/2}C_{p}\right]^{k}\|x\|_{2}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Our goal is to show that the constant
C_{p}
in (
2.6
) satisfies

(2.7)

C_{p}\approx(p-1)^{1/2}\approx p^{1/2}.

We will show the following theorem, which directly implies (
2.6
) and (
2.7
).

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
f_{k,n,m}
be defined as before. For all
k\geq 1
,
-k\leq m\leq k
, there exists a constant
C(\beta)
such that

(2.8)

\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}|f_{k,n,m}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leq e^{m\beta(1/2p-1/4)}\left[C(\beta)p\right]^{k/2}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}|f_{k,n,m}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Let
\Gamma(s)
be the Gamma function. We will need the following Stirling’s formula, see
[
1
]
. For all
s\geq 1
, one has

(2.9)

\sqrt{2\pi s}\left(\frac{s}{e}\right)^{s}<\Gamma(1+s)<e\sqrt{s}\left(\frac{s}{e}\right)^{s}.

Lemma 2.4
.

For all
s\geq 1
,

(2.10)

e^{-\beta}\beta^{-(s+1)}\sqrt{\frac{\pi s}{2}}\left(\frac{s}{e}\right)^{s}\leq\sum_{n\geq 0}e^{-n\beta}n^{s}\leq e(1+\beta)\beta^{-(s+1)}\sqrt{s}\left(\frac{s}{e}\right)^{s},

Proof.

Define for
x\geq 0

h(x)=e^{-\beta x}x^{s},\quad\beta>0,\ s\geq 1.

It is clear that
h^{\prime}(x)=e^{-\beta x}x^{s-1}(-\beta x+s)
. Thus
h(x)
increases in
[0,s/\beta]
, decreases in
[s/\beta,\infty)
and achieves its maximum at
x=s/\beta
, i.e.

h(x)\leq h\left(\frac{s}{\beta}\right)=\beta^{-s}\left(\frac{s}{e}\right)^{s}.

Assume that there exists a nonnegative integer
m
such that
m\leq s/\beta<m+1
.

At first, we show the right-sided inequality. If
m\geq 1
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}e^{-n\beta}n^{s}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}h(n)+\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}h(n)\leq\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}\int_{n}^{n+1}h(x)\,{\rm d}x+\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}\int_{n-1}^{n}h(x)\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}h(x)\,{\rm d}x+\int_{m-1}^{m}h(x)\,{\rm d}x\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\beta x}x^{s}\,{\rm d}s+h\left(\frac{s}{\beta}\right)

\displaystyle=\beta^{-(s+1)}\Gamma(1+s)+\beta^{-s}\left(\frac{s}{e}\right)^{s}<e\sqrt{s}\beta^{-(s+1)}\left(\frac{s}{e}\right)^{s}+\beta^{-s}\left(\frac{s}{e}\right)^{s}

\displaystyle<e\beta^{-(s+1)}(1+\beta)\sqrt{s}\left(\frac{s}{e}\right)^{s},

where we use
s\geq 1
in the last inequality. The case
m=0
follows similarly.

For the remaining inequality, we consider two cases. First, suppose
s>2\beta^{2}/\pi
. If
m\geq 1
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}e^{-n\beta}n^{s}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{m}h(n)+\sum_{n=m+1}^{\infty}h(n)\geq\sum_{n=1}^{m}\int_{n-1}^{n}h(x)\,{\rm d}x+\sum_{n=m+1}^{\infty}\int_{n}^{n+1}h(x)\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}h(x)\,{\rm d}x-\int_{m}^{m+1}h(x)\,{\rm d}x\geq\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\beta x}x^{s}\,{\rm d}s-h\left(\frac{s}{\beta}\right)

\displaystyle=\beta^{-(s+1)}\Gamma(1+s)-\beta^{-s}\left(\frac{s}{e}\right)^{s}>\sqrt{2\pi s}\beta^{-(s+1)}\left(\frac{s}{e}\right)^{s}-\beta^{-s}\left(\frac{s}{e}\right)^{s}

\displaystyle=\beta^{-(s+1)}(\sqrt{2\pi s}-\beta)\left(\frac{s}{e}\right)^{s}>\beta^{-(s+1)}\left(\sqrt{2\pi s}-\sqrt{\frac{\pi s}{2}}\right)\left(\frac{s}{e}\right)^{s}

\displaystyle=\beta^{-(s+1)}\sqrt{\frac{\pi s}{2}}\left(\frac{s}{e}\right)^{s}.

The case
m=0
follows similarly.

Now for
1\leq s\leq 2\beta^{2}/\pi
, recall that
m\leq s/\beta<m+1
, thus

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}e^{-n\beta}n^{s}

\displaystyle>e^{-(m+1)\beta}(m+1)^{s}>e^{-(s+\beta)}\left(\frac{s}{\beta}\right)^{s}=e^{-\beta}\beta^{-s}\left(\frac{s}{e}\right)^{s}

\displaystyle=e^{-\beta}\beta^{-(s+1)}\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{s}}\sqrt{s}\left(\frac{s}{e}\right)^{s}\geq e^{-\beta}\beta^{-(s+1)}\sqrt{\frac{\pi s}{2}}\left(\frac{s}{e}\right)^{s}.

This proves (
2.10
).
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
2.3

In this section, we will show Theorem
2.3
, which finishes the proof of Theorem
1.1
. To begin with, we introduce Meixiner polynomials, which are the main ingredient for our proof of Theorem
2.3
. Recall that
p\geq 2
.

3.1.
Orthogonal polynomials

We present a special family of Meixiner polynomials (or Laguerre polynomials), which is orthogonal with respect to the weight
w(n)=e^{-n\beta}
(
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
). For any
k\geq 0
, define

(3.1)

L_{k}(n)=e^{-k\beta}\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}(e^{\beta}-1)^{j}\binom{k}{j}\binom{n}{j},\quad\forall\,n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

We have the following proposition, see
[
20
, Chapter 9]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

The family
\left\{L_{0},L_{1},\cdots,L_{n},\cdots\right\}
is orthogonal with respect to the weight
w(n)=e^{-n\beta}
. More precisely, for any
m,l\in{\mathbb{N}}

(3.2)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}L_{m}(n)L_{l}(n)=\frac{e^{-m\beta}}{1-e^{-\beta}}\delta_{ml},

where
\delta_{ml}
is the Kronecker delta function.

The following lemma is needed later.

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we have

(3.3)

|L_{k}(n)|\leq\max\left\{\frac{n^{k}}{k!},\frac{k^{k}}{k!}\right\},\quad\forall\,n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Proof.

We observe that when
j\leq n
and
j\leq k
,

\binom{n}{j}=\frac{n(n-1)\cdots(n-j+1)}{j!}\leq\frac{n^{j}}{j!}=\frac{n^{j}(j+1)\cdots k}{k!}\leq\frac{n^{j}k^{k-j}}{k!}.

We consider two cases. For
0\leq n\leq k
, since
\binom{n}{j}=0
if
j>n
, we have

\displaystyle|L_{k}(n)|

\displaystyle\leq e^{-k\beta}\sum_{j=0}^{n}(e^{\beta}-1)^{j}\binom{k}{j}\binom{n}{j}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-k\beta}\sum_{j=0}^{n}(e^{\beta}-1)^{j}\binom{k}{j}\frac{n^{j}k^{k-j}}{k!}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-k\beta}\sum_{j=0}^{k}(e^{\beta}-1)^{j}\binom{k}{j}\frac{k^{k}}{k!}=\frac{k^{k}}{k!}.

For
n>k
, we have

\displaystyle|L_{k}(n)|

\displaystyle\leq e^{-k\beta}\sum_{j=0}^{k}(e^{\beta}-1)^{j}\binom{k}{j}\frac{n^{j}k^{k-j}}{k!}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-k\beta}\sum_{j=0}^{k}(e^{\beta}-1)^{j}\binom{k}{j}\frac{n^{k}}{k!}=\frac{n^{k}}{k!}.

This completes the proof.
∎

3.2.
Proof of Theorem
2.3

We divide our proof of Theorem
2.3
into two steps. First, we prove that Theorem
2.3
holds for all
k\geq 1
, and
m=0
or
m=1
. Note that
m=0
implies

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2i+m\leq k\\
i+m\geq 0,0\leq i\leq n\end{subarray}}c_{i,i+m}d_{n,i}d_{n+m,i+m}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2i\leq k\\
0\leq i\leq n\end{subarray}}c_{i,i}d_{n,i}^{2}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2i\leq k\\
0\leq i\leq n\end{subarray}}c_{i,i}\dfrac{n!}{(n-i)!}.

Hence
f_{k,n,0}
is a polynomial with respect to
n
with degree not larger than
k/2
. We will prove Theorem
2.3
for all
k\geq 1
and
m=0
by showing the following slightly stronger proposition below.

Lemma 3.3
.

For all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and any polynomial
p_{k}
with degree
\deg p_{k}=k
, there exists a constant
C_{1}(\beta)
such that

\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}|p_{k}(n)|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leq\left[C_{1}(\beta)p\right]^{k}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}|p_{k}(n)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Proof.

The case
k=0
is trivial. For
k\geq 1
, by Proposition
3.1
, there exist
c_{i}\in{\mathbb{C}}

(i=0,1,\cdots,k)
such that

p_{k}(n)=\sum_{i=0}^{k}c_{i}L_{i}(n),\quad\forall\,n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Thus (
3.2
) implies

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}|p_{k}(n)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|c_{i}|^{2}e^{-n\beta}|L_{i}(n)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

(3.4)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i=0}^{k}|c_{i}|^{2}\frac{e^{-i\beta}}{1-e^{-\beta}}\right)^{1/2}.

By the triangle inequality, the left-hand-side of Lemma
3.3
reads

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}|p_{k}(n)|^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{k}|c_{i}|\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}|L_{i}(n)|^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

Applying (
3.3
), (
2.9
) and (
2.10
), one has

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}|L_{i}(n)|^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{i}e^{-n\beta}|L_{i}(n)|^{p}+\sum_{n=i+1}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}|L_{i}(n)|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=0}^{i}e^{-n\beta}\frac{i^{ip}}{(i!)^{p}}+\sum_{n=i+1}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}\frac{n^{ip}}{(i!)^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1-e^{-\beta}}\frac{i^{ip}}{\left(2\pi i\right)^{p/2}\left(e^{-1}i\right)^{ip}}+\frac{e(1+\beta)\beta^{-ip-1}\sqrt{ip}\left(e^{-1}ip\right)^{ip}}{\left(2\pi i\right)^{p/2}\left(e^{-1}i\right)^{ip}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(2\pi i\right)^{-p/2}\sqrt{ip}\left(\frac{1}{1-e^{-\beta}}+e\left(1+\frac{1}{\beta}\right)\right)\left(e+\beta^{-1}\right)^{ip}p^{ip}

(3.5)

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}(\beta)^{kp}p^{kp},

where we can take

C_{2}(\beta)=\left(\frac{1}{1-e^{-\beta}}+e\left(1+\frac{1}{\beta}\right)\right)\left(e+\beta^{-1}\right).

Hence by (
3.2
),

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}|p_{k}(n)|^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{k}|c_{i}|C_{2}(\beta)^{k}p^{k}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}(\beta)^{k}p^{k}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{k}|c_{i}|^{2}\frac{e^{-i\beta}}{1-e^{-\beta}}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{k}e^{i\beta}(1-e^{-\beta})\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}(\beta)^{k}p^{k}e^{k\beta/2}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}|p_{k}(n)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left[C_{1}(\beta)p\right]^{k}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}|p_{k}(n)|^{2}\right)^{1/2},

where

C_{1}(\beta):=e^{\beta/2}C_{2}(\beta).

This completes the proof.
∎

We also need the following important lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

For all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and any polynomial
p_{k}
with degree
\deg p_{k}=k
, there exists a constant
C_{3}(\beta)
such that

\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}|\sqrt{n+1}p_{k}(n)|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leq\left[C_{3}(\beta)p\right]^{(2k+1)/2}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}|\sqrt{n+1}p_{k}(n)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Proof.

We consider two cases. For
p\geq 4
, applying Lemma
3.3
to the polynomial
(n+1)|p_{k}(n)|^{2}
, one has

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}|\sqrt{n+1}p_{k}(n)|^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}\left((n+1)|p_{k}(n)|^{2}\right)^{p/2}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\left[C_{1}(\beta)p\right]^{2k+1}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}\left((n+1)|p_{k}(n)|^{2}\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right)^{1/2}

(3.6)

\displaystyle=\left[C_{1}(\beta)p\right]^{(2k+1)/2}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}(n+1)^{2}|p_{k}(n)|^{4}\right)^{1/4}.

Note that
(n+1)^{2}\leq(n+1)^{4}
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and applying Lemma
3.3
again to the polynomial
(n+1)p_{k}(n)
for
p=4
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}(n+1)^{2}|p_{k}(n)|^{4}\right)^{1/4}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}|(n+1)p_{k}(n)|^{4}\right)^{1/4}

(3.7)

\displaystyle\leq\left[4C_{1}(\beta)\right]^{k+1}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}|(n+1)p_{k}(n)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

By Proposition
3.1
, there exist
c_{i}\in{\mathbb{C}}

(i=0,1,\cdots,k)
such that

p_{k}(n)=\sum_{i=0}^{k}c_{i}L_{i}(n).

Applying (
3.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle|np_{k}(n)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{k}|c_{i}|n|L_{i}(n)|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{k}|c_{i}|n\max\left\{\frac{k^{k}}{k!},\frac{n^{k}}{k!}\right\}\leq\sum_{i=0}^{k}|c_{i}|\max\left\{\frac{k^{k+1}}{k!},\frac{n^{k+1}}{k!}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\frac{k^{k+1}}{k!},\frac{n^{k+1}}{k!}\right\}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{k}|c_{i}|^{2}\frac{e^{-i\beta}}{1-e^{-\beta}}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{k}e^{i\beta}\left(1-e^{-\beta}\right)\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=e^{k\beta}\max\left\{\frac{k^{k+1}}{k!},\frac{n^{k+1}}{k!}\right\}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}|p_{k}(n)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Hence by (
2.9
) and (
2.10
),

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}|np_{k}(n)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq e^{2k\beta}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}|p_{k}(n)|^{2}\right)\left(\sum_{n=0}^{k}e^{-n\beta}\left(\frac{k^{k+1}}{k!}\right)^{2}+\sum_{n=k+1}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}\left(\frac{n^{k+1}}{k!}\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq e^{2k\beta}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}|p_{k}(n)|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot\left(\frac{e^{2k}k}{2\pi(1-e^{-\beta})}+\frac{4^{k+1}(1+\beta)\beta^{-2k-3}(k+1)}{2\pi e}\left(1+\frac{1}{k}\right)^{2k+1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{4}(\beta)^{k}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}|p_{k}(n)|^{2},

where we can take

C_{4}(\beta)=\frac{2e^{2\beta+2}}{1-e^{-\beta}}+16e^{2\beta+2}(\beta^{-3}+\beta^{-2})\max\left\{1,\beta^{-2}\right\}.

Then we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}|(n+1)p_{k}(n)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}|np_{k}(n)|^{2}+2\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}n|p_{k}(n)|^{2}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}|p_{k}(n)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(C_{4}(\beta)^{k}+1\right)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}|p_{k}(n)|^{2}+2\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}n|p_{k}(n)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(C_{4}(\beta)^{k}+3\right)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}(n+1)|p_{k}(n)|^{2}.

Plug this into (
3.2
), and we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}(n+1)^{2}|p_{k}(n)|^{4}\right)^{1/4}

\displaystyle\leq\left[4C_{1}(\beta)\right]^{k+1}\left(C_{4}(\beta)^{k}+3\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}(n+1)|p_{k}(n)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{5}(\beta)^{(2k+1)/2}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}(n+1)|p_{k}(n)|^{2}\right)^{1/2},

where

C_{5}(\beta)=[4C_{1}(\beta)]^{2}\left(C_{4}(\beta)+3\right).

Substituting this into (
3.2
) yields

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}|\sqrt{n+1}p_{k}(n)|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leq\left[C_{1}(\beta)C_{5}(\beta)p\right]^{(2k+1)/2}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}(n+1)|p_{k}(n)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

This completes the proof for
p\geq 4
.

For
2\leq p\leq 4
, by the Hölder inequality, we have

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}|\sqrt{n+1}p_{k}(n)|^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq(1-e^{-\beta})^{(p-4)/(4p)}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}|\sqrt{n+1}p_{k}(n)|^{4}\right)^{1/4}

\displaystyle\leq(1-e^{-\beta})^{-1/4}C_{5}(\beta)^{(2k+1)/2}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}(n+1)|p_{k}(n)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left[(1-e^{-\beta})^{-1/4}C_{5}(\beta)\right]^{(2k+1)/2}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}(n+1)|p_{k}(n)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left[(1-e^{-\beta})^{-1/4}C_{5}(\beta)p/2\right]^{(2k+1)/2}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-n\beta}(n+1)|p_{k}(n)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Note that
(1-e^{-\beta})^{-1/4}/2\leq C_{1}(\beta)
, we take

C_{3}(\beta)=C_{1}(\beta)C_{5}(\beta).

This completes the proof.
∎

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
2.3
for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
-k\leq m\leq k
. Define the function space

F_{k,m}:=\left\{f:{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{C}}:f(n)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2i+m\leq k\\
i+m\geq 0,0\leq i\leq n\end{subarray}}c_{i,i+m}d_{n,i}d_{n+m,i+m},\ c_{i,i+m}\in\mathbb{C}\right\}.

We need the following auxiliary lemma.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
k\geq 1
and
0\leq m\leq k-1
. For any
f\in F_{k,m+1}
, there exists a function
g\in F_{k-1,m}
such that

(3.8)

f(n)=(n+m+1)^{1/2}g(n).

Moreover, for any
f\in F_{k-1,m+1}
, there exists a function
g\in F_{k,m}
such that

(3.9)

(n+1)^{1/2}f(n)=g(n+1).

Proof.

We first prove (
3.8
). For any
f\in F_{k,m+1}
, we have

f(n)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2i+(m+1)\leq k\\
i+(m+1)\geq 0,0\leq i\leq n\end{subarray}}c_{i,i+m+1}d_{n,i}d_{n+(m+1),i+(m+1)}.

Note that

\displaystyle d_{n+m+1,i+m+1}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{(n+m+1)!}{(n-i)!}\right)^{1/2}=(n+m+1)^{1/2}\left(\frac{(n+m)!}{(n-i)!}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=(n+m+1)^{1/2}d_{n+m,i+m}.

Thus

\displaystyle f(n)

\displaystyle=(n+m+1)^{1/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2i+(m+1)\leq k\\
i+(m+1)\geq 0,0\leq i\leq n\end{subarray}}c_{i,i+m+1}d_{n,i}d_{n+m,i+m}

\displaystyle=(n+m+1)^{1/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2i+m\leq k-1\\
i+m\geq 0,0\leq i\leq n\end{subarray}}c_{i,i+m+1}d_{n,i}d_{n+m,i+m}

\displaystyle=:(n+m+1)^{1/2}g(n),

which verifies the first assertion. Thus we obtain (
3.8
).

Now we prove the second assertion. For any
f\in F_{k-1,m+1}
, we have

(n+1)^{1/2}f(n)=(n+1)^{1/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2i+(m+1)\leq k-1\\
i+(m+1)\geq 0\\
0\leq i\leq n\end{subarray}}c_{i,i+m+1}d_{n,i}d_{n+m+1,i+m+1}.

We observe that

\displaystyle(n+1)^{1/2}d_{n,i}

\displaystyle=(n+1)^{1/2}\left(\frac{n!}{(n-i)!}\right)^{1/2}=\left(\frac{(n+1)!}{(n+1-(i+1))!}\right)^{1/2}=d_{n+1,i+1}.

Hence

\displaystyle(n+1)^{1/2}f(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2i+(m+1)\leq k-1\\
i+(m+1)\geq 0\\
0\leq i\leq n\end{subarray}}c_{i,i+m+1}d_{n+1,i+1}d_{n+m+1,i+m+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2(i+1)+m\leq k\\
(i+1)+m\geq 0\\
1\leq i+1\leq n+1\end{subarray}}c_{i,i+m+1}d_{n+1,i+1}d_{n+1+m,i+1+m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2j+m\leq k\\
j+m\geq 0\\
1\leq j\leq n+1\end{subarray}}c_{j-1,j+m}d_{n+1,j}d_{n+1+m,j+m}

\displaystyle=:g(n+1).

We set
g(0)=0
, thereby
g\in F_{k,m}
. This verifies the second assertion (
3.9
).
∎

Now we proceed to prove Theorem
2.3
for
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and

. We restate the following lemma, which is equivalent to Theorem

for
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
be fixed. For all
0\leq m\leq k
, we have

(P
(k,m)
)

\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}e^{-n\beta}|f_{k,n,m}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leq(2e^{\beta/2})^{m}[C_{3}(\beta)p]^{k/2}\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}e^{-n\beta}|f_{k,n,m}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Proof.

We prove (
P

(
k
,
m
)

) by induction for the parameter
m
. For
m=0
, by Lemma
3.3
and the fact that
C_{1}(\beta)\leq C_{3}(\beta)
, P
(k,0)
holds for all
k\geq 0
. For
m=1
, it suffices to verify P
(k,1)
for all
k\geq 1
. By (
3.8
), there exists
g\in F_{k-1,0}
such that
f_{k,n,1}=(n+1)^{1/2}g(n)
. The statement then follows from Lemma
3.4
.

Assume P
(k,m)
holds for all
k\geq m\geq 1
. The induction target is to show that P
(l,m+1)
holds for all
l\geq m+1
. The left-hand-side of P
(l,m+1)
reads

LHS of P
(l,m+1)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}e^{-n\beta/p}|f_{l,n,m+1}|+\left(\sum_{n\geq m}e^{-n\beta}|f_{l,n,m+1}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}

(3.10)

\displaystyle=:J_{1}+J_{2}.

For the term
J_{1}
, by the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}e^{2n\beta\left(1/2-1/p\right)}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}e^{-n\beta}|f_{l,n,m+1}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{m}e^{(m-1)\beta(1/2-1/p)}\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}e^{-n\beta}|f_{l,n,m+1}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

(3.11)

\displaystyle\leq(2e^{\beta/2})^{m}\left[C_{3}(\beta)p\right]^{l/2}\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}e^{-n\beta}|f_{l,n,m+1}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

For the term
J_{2}
, combining (
3.8
) and (
3.9
), for any
f\in F_{l,m+1}
, there exists

such that

f_{l,n,m+1}=f(n)=\left(\frac{n+m+1}{n+1}\right)^{1/2}g(n+1),\quad g(0)=0.

Then we have

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{n\geq m}e^{-n\beta}2^{p/2}|g(n+1)|^{p}\right)^{1/p}=\sqrt{2}e^{\beta/p}\left(\sum_{n\geq m+1}e^{-n\beta}|g(n)|^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle(\text{by {\color[rgb]{0,0,1}P$(l,m-1)$}})

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2}e^{\beta/p}(2e^{\beta/2})^{m-1}\left[C_{3}(\beta)p\right]^{l/2}\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}e^{-n\beta}|g(n)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle(\text{since $g(0)=0$})

\displaystyle=e^{\beta/p}(2e^{\beta/2})^{m-1/2}\left[C_{3}(\beta)p\right]^{l/2}e^{-3\beta/4}\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}e^{-n\beta}|g(n+1)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq(2e^{\beta/2})^{m}\left[C_{3}(\beta)p\right]^{l/2}\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}e^{-n\beta}\left|\left(\frac{n+m+1}{n+1}\right)^{1/2}g(n+1)\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

(3.12)

\displaystyle=(2e^{\beta/2})^{m}\left[C_{3}(\beta)p\right]^{l/2}\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}e^{-n\beta}|f_{l,n,m+1}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Substituting (
3.2
) and (
3.2
) into (
3.2
) yields the induction target P
(l,m+1)
.
∎

In order to pass Lemma

to the case
-k\leq m<0

Lemma 3.7
.

Let

and
-k\leq m<0
. For any
f\in F_{k,m}
, there exists a function

such that

(3.13)

f(n)=\begin{cases}g(n+m),&\text{if }\quad n\geq-m,\\
0,&\text{if }\quad 0\leq n<-m.\end{cases}

Proof.

For any
f\in F_{k,m}
,
f(n)=0
if
0\leq n<-m
. For
n\geq-m
, we have

\displaystyle f(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2i+m\leq k\\
i+m\geq 0,0\leq i\leq n\end{subarray}}c_{i,i+m}d_{n,i}d_{n+m,i+m}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2i+m\leq k\\
-m\leq i\leq n\end{subarray}}c_{i,i+m}d_{n,i}d_{n+m,i+m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2(i+m)+(-m)\leq k\\
0\leq i+m\leq n+m\end{subarray}}c_{i,i+m}d_{(n+m)+(-m),(i+m)+(-m)}d_{n+m,i+m}=:g(n+m).

It is easy to see that
g\in F_{k,-m}
. We complete the proof.
∎

We now come to the proof of Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem

.

By (

(

,

)

), we have for

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}e^{-n\beta}|f_{k,n,m}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq e^{m\beta(1/(2p)-1/4)}\left[2e^{\beta/2}e^{\beta\left(1/4-1/(2p)\right)}\right]^{m}[C_{3}(\beta)p]^{k/2}\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}e^{-n\beta}|f_{k,n,m}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq e^{m\beta(1/(2p)-1/4)}\left[4e^{3\beta/2}C_{3}(\beta)p\right]^{k/2}\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}e^{-n\beta}|f_{k,n,m}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}e^{-n\beta}|f_{k,n,m}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}

4.
Estimate of Optimal time
t_{p}

In this section, we discuss the optimal time
t_{p}
for the hypercontractivity inequalities associated with the quantum Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroups. We start with the following auxiliary lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. Assume that
(a_{n})_{n\geq 0}\in\ell_{p}({\mathbb{N}})
, and define

A=\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}\left(e_{n}\otimes e_{n+1}+e_{n+1}\otimes e_{n}\right)\in B(\ell_{2}({\mathbb{N}})).

Then

(4.1)

\frac{2^{1/p}}{3}\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}|a_{n}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leq\|A\|_{p}\leq 2\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}|a_{n}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

Proof.

The second inequality easily follows from the triangle inequality. For the first inequality, we define
P_{n}
as the projection of
\ell_{2}({\mathbb{N}})
onto
{\rm span}\left\{e_{n},e_{n+1}\right\}
. Then we have

P_{n}AP_{n}(e_{k})=\begin{cases}a_{n}e_{n+1},&\text{if }k=n,\\
a_{n}e_{n},&\text{if }k=n+1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Hence

\|P_{n}AP_{n}\|_{p}=2^{1/p}|a_{n}|.

Consider the family
\left\{P_{3n}AP_{3n}:n\in{\mathbb{N}}\right\}
. We observe that
P_{3i}AP_{3j}\neq 0
if and only if
i=j
, hence

\left\|\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}P_{3n}\right)A\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}P_{3n}\right)\right\|_{p}=\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}\|P_{3n}AP_{3n}\|_{p}^{p}\right)^{1/p}=2^{1/p}\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}|a_{3n}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

We adopt the same argument to the family
\left\{P_{3n+1}AP_{3n+1}:n\in{\mathbb{N}}\right\}
and
\left\{P_{3n+2}AP_{3n+2}:n\in{\mathbb{N}}\right\}
, and we obtain

\sum_{i=0}^{2}\left\|\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}P_{3n+i}\right)A\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}P_{3n+i}\right)\right\|_{p}\geq 2^{1/p}\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}|a_{n}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

Note that

\left\|\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}P_{3n+i}\right)A\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}P_{3n+i}\right)\right\|_{p}\leq\left\|\sum_{n\geq 0}P_{3n+i}\right\|_{\infty}\|A\|_{p}\left\|\sum_{n\geq 0}P_{3n+i}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\|A\|_{p}.

Combining these estimates yields the desired inequality.
∎

We restate Theorem
1.1
on the estimate of the optimal time
t_{p}
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
p\geq 2
. There exist positive constants
\widetilde{c}(\beta)
,
\widetilde{C}(\beta)
such that,

\widetilde{c}(\beta)(p-1)\leq e^{2\tau t_{p}}\leq\widetilde{C}(\beta)(p-1).

More precisely,

\widetilde{c}(\beta)=\frac{\min\left\{\beta^{-2},\beta^{-1}\right\}}{36e},\quad\widetilde{C}(\beta)=24(1-e^{-\beta})^{-1}C(\beta),

where
C(\beta)
is the constant in Theorem
2.3
.

Proof.

We first prove the upper bound. Combining Proposition
2.1
, Proposition
2.2
and Theorem
2.3
, we conclude that the optimal time
t_{p}
satisfies

(4.2)

e^{2\tau t_{p}}\leq 6(1-e^{-\beta})^{-1}C(\beta)p\leq\widetilde{C}(\beta)(p-1).

For the lower bound, without loss of generality, we assume
\tau_{1}\leq\tau_{2}
, then
\tau=\tau_{1}
. For any
\varepsilon>0
, we can take
x=\varepsilon A_{1}^{*}\xi_{0}
. Then
\omega(x)=0
and

T_{t_{p}}^{(2)}(\rho^{1/2}x\rho^{1/2})=\varepsilon e^{-\tau t_{p}}\rho^{1/4}A_{1}^{*}\xi_{0}\rho^{1/4}.

Note that one has

(4.3)

\varepsilon\|e^{-\tau t_{p}}\rho^{1/(2p)-1/4}A_{1}^{*}\xi_{0}\rho^{1/(2p)-1/4}\|_{p}=\|T_{t_{p}}^{(2)}(\rho^{1/2}x\rho^{1/2})\|_{L_{p}(\rho)}\leq\|\rho^{1/2}x\rho^{1/2}\|_{L_{2}(\rho)}=\varepsilon.

By definition, we can write
A_{1}^{*}\xi_{0}
as a matrix of the form

\displaystyle A_{1}^{*}\xi_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(D_{1}^{*}-D_{2}^{*})\rho^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(2\sinh(\beta/2)\right)^{-1/2}}{\sqrt{2}}\left(e^{\beta/4}-e^{-3\beta/4}\right)\left(a^{*}\rho^{1/2}+\rho^{1/2}a\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(2\sinh(\beta/2)\right)^{-1/2}}{\sqrt{2}}\left(e^{\beta/4}-e^{-3\beta/4}\right)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-n\beta/2}\sqrt{n+1}}{\left(1-e^{-\beta}\right)^{1/2}}\left(e_{n}\otimes e_{n+1}+e_{n+1}\otimes e_{n}\right).

For convenience, we denote

\quad Z=e^{-\tau t_{p}}\rho^{1/(2p)-1/4}A_{1}^{*}\xi_{0}\rho^{1/(2p)-1/4}.

Hence

Z=e^{-\tau t_{p}}\frac{\left(2\sinh(\beta/2)\right)^{1/2-1/p}}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sqrt{n+1}e^{-n\beta/p}\left(e_{n}\otimes e_{n+1}+e_{n+1}\otimes e_{n}\right).

However, form (
4.3
) and by (
4.1
),

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\geq\|Z\|_{p}\geq e^{-\tau t_{p}}\frac{2^{1/p}\left(2\sinh(\beta/2)\right)^{1/2-1/p}}{3\sqrt{2}}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(n+1)^{p/2}e^{-n\beta}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\geq e^{-\tau t_{p}}\frac{2^{1/p}\left(2\sinh(\beta/2)\right)^{1/2-1/p}}{3\sqrt{2}}\beta^{-(1/2+1/p)}\left(\frac{\pi p}{4}\right)^{1/(2p)}\left(\frac{p}{2e}\right)^{1/2}

(4.4)

\displaystyle\geq e^{-\tau t_{p}}\frac{\min\left\{\beta^{-1},\beta^{-1/2}\right\}}{6\sqrt{e}}p^{1/2}.

This implies

e^{\tau t_{p}}\geq\frac{\min\left\{\beta^{-1},\beta^{-1/2}\right\}}{6\sqrt{e}}p^{1/2}.

Hence, the proof is completed.
∎

5.
Proof of Corollary
1.2

We proceed this section with the proof of Corollary
1.2
.

Proof of Corollary
1.2
.

It suffices to show

e^{2\tau t^{\prime}_{p}}\leq\widetilde{C}_{1}(\beta)(p-1)^{2}.

We use the same notation with Proposition
2.1
. Take an arbitrary
y\in B(H)
where
\omega(y)
might be non-zero. Now applying the Ball-Carlen-Lieb convexity inequality (see
[
3
,
25
,
26
]
), one has for all
p\geq 2
,

\|\rho^{1/2}y\rho^{1/2}\|_{L_{p}(\rho)}^{2}\leq|\omega(y)|^{2}+(p-1)\|\rho^{1/2}(y-\omega(y))\rho^{1/2}\|_{L_{p}(\rho)}^{2}.

Then we obtain

\displaystyle\|T_{t}^{(2)}(\rho^{1/2}y\rho^{1/2})\|_{L_{p}(\rho)}

\displaystyle\leq\left(|\omega(y)|^{2}+(p-1)\|T_{t}^{(2)}\left(\rho^{1/2}(y-\omega(y))\rho^{1/2}\right)\|_{L_{p}(\rho)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(|\omega(y)|^{2}+(p-1)\frac{e^{-2\tau t}c_{p}^{2}}{1-e^{-2\tau t}c_{p}^{2}}\|\rho^{1/2}(y-\omega(y))\rho^{1/2}\|_{L_{2}(\rho)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(|\omega(y)|^{2}+\|\rho^{1/2}(y-\omega(y))\rho^{1/2}\|_{L_{2}(\rho)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}=\|\rho^{1/2}y\rho^{1/2}\|_{L_{2}(\rho)},

where we require

(p-1)\frac{e^{-2\tau t}c_{p}^{2}}{1-e^{-2\tau t}c_{p}^{2}}\leq 1\quad\text{i.e.}\quad e^{2\tau t}\geq pc_{p}^{2}.

Hence, the optimal time
t_{p}^{\prime}
satisfies

e^{2\tau t_{p}^{\prime}}\leq pc_{p}^{2}.

We complete the proof.
∎

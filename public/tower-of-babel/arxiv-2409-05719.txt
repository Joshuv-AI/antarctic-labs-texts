Equivariant K K -theory of cellular toric bundles and related spaces
V. Uma

Abstract
. In this article we describe the equivariant and ordinary topological K K -ring of a toric bundle with fiber a T T - cellular toric variety. This generalizes the results in [ 31 ] on K K -theory of smooth projective toric bundles. We apply our results to describe the equivariant topological K K -ring of a toroidal horospherical embedding.

1.
Introduction

We shall consider varieties over the field of complex numbers unless
otherwise specified.

Let
T\simeq(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
denote the complex algebraic torus of
dimension
n
. A
T
-cellular variety
is a
T
-variety
X

equipped with a
T
-stable algebraic cell decomposition. In other
words there is a filtration

(1.1)

X=Z_{1}\supseteq Z_{2}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq Z_{m}\supseteq Z_{m+1}=\emptyset

where
each
Z_{i}
is a closed
T
-stable subvariety of
X
and

Z_{i}\setminus Z_{i+1}=Y_{i}
is
T
-equivariantly isomorphic to the
affine space
{\mathbb{C}}^{n_{i}}
equipped with a linear action of
T

for
1\leq i\leq m
. Furthermore,
Y_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq m
, are the
Bialynicki Birula cells associated to a
generic
one-parameter
subgroup of
T
(see Definition
3.2
and
[
37
]
).

We note here that any smooth projective complex variety
X
with

T
-action having only finitely many
T
-fixed points is
T
-cellular.
Indeed for any projective variety the Bialynicki-Birula cell
decomposition is filtrable (see
[
10
]
) and since

is smooth
the Bialynicki-Birula cells are smooth (see
[
11
, Section 3.1]
).

Let

denote the maximal compact subgroup of

T
. Let

denote the topological

-ring (see
[
5
]
). For a
compact connected Lie group

, let

denote

-equivariant

-ring. In particular,

denotes the

-equivariant topological

]
).

We develop below some further notations to describe the results in
this paper.

Let

-action on

2.
Preliminaries on
K
-theory

Let
X
be a compact
G_{comp}
-space for a compact Lie group

. By

we mean the Grothendieck ring of

G_{comp}
-equivariant topological vector bundles on

with the
abelian group structure given by the direct sum and the multiplication
given by the tensor product of equivariant vector bundles. In

, where

acts trivially on

of complex
representations of
G_{comp}
. The ring

has the
structure of

-algebra via the map

which takes

, where
\mathbf{V}=X\times V
is the trivial

G_{comp}
-equivariant vector bundle on
X
corresponding to the

G_{comp}
-representation
V
. Let

be a
G_{comp}
-fixed point of

X
then the reduced equivariant
K
-ring

{\widetilde{K}}_{G_{comp}}^{0}(X)
is the kernel of the map

K_{G_{comp}}^{0}(X)\rightarrow K_{G_{comp}}^{0}(pt)
, induced by the restriction
of
G_{comp}
-equivariant vector bundles. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we
define

\widetilde{K}^{-n}_{G_{comp}}(X):=\widetilde{K}^{0}_{G_{comp}}(S^{n}X)

where

is the
n
-fold reduced suspension of
X
.

If
X
is locally compact space but not compact we shall consider

G_{comp}
-equivariant
K
-theory with compact support denoted

K_{G_{comp},c}^{0}(X)
. This can be identified with

{\widetilde{K}}_{G_{comp}}^{0}(X^{+})
where

denotes the one point
compactification of

, which is a compact
G_{comp}
-space, where
the point at infinity, which is the base point of
X^{+}
is

G_{comp}
-fixed. We define

{K}_{G_{comp},c}^{-n}(X):=\widetilde{K}^{-n}_{G_{comp}}(X^{+})
.

When
X
is already compact, we define

as the disjoint
union of
X
and a base point. In this case we see that

K^{0}_{G_{comp},c}(X)=\widetilde{K}^{0}_{G_{comp}}(X^{+})=K^{0}_{G_{comp}}(X)

since

. In

[
33
, Definition 2.8]
,

is denoted by

without the subscript

. We remark here that

is not a homotopy invariant unlike

(see

, Proposition 2.3]
). For example

whereas

.

We have the equivariant Bott periodicity

K_{G_{comp}}^{-n}(X)\simeq K_{G_{comp}}^{-n-2}(X)
given via
multiplication by the Bott element in

. (See

[

]
,
[

]
and
[

]
.) This enables us to define

for a positive

as

for

.

For
X
a compact
G_{comp}
-space and

a closed

G_{comp}
-subspace for

we define

\widetilde{K}_{G}^{-n}(X,A):=\widetilde{K}^{0}(S^{n}(X/A))
(see

,

-equivariant

\displaystyle\cdots\rightarrow{K}_{G_{comp}}^{-n}(X,A)\rightarrow{K}_{G_{comp}}^{-n}(X)

\displaystyle\rightarrow K_{G_{comp}}^{-n}(A)\rightarrow

(2.2)

\displaystyle\rightarrow K_{G_{comp}}^{-n+1}(X,A)\rightarrow\cdots

Let

be an algebraic variety with the action of an algebraic group

. Then

3.
Bialynicki-Birula decomposition and cellular
varieties

In this section we recall the notions of a filtrable Bialynicki-Birula
decomposition and a
T
-cellular variety (see
[
37
, Section 2]
and

[
11
, Section 3]
).

Let
X
be a normal complex algebraic variety with the action of the
complex algebraic torus
T
. We assume that the set of
T
-fixed
points
X^{T}
is finite. Let
\lambda
be a generic one-parameter
subgroup of
T
i.e.
X^{\lambda}=X^{T}=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\}
. Let

(3.3)

\displaystyle X_{+}(x_{i},\lambda)=\{x\in X\mid\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\lambda(t)x\penalty\ \mbox{exists and is equal to}\penalty\ x_{i}\}.

Then
X_{+}(x_{i},\lambda)
is a locally closed
T
-invariant subvariety
of
X
and is called the Bialynicki-Birula
plus
cell.

Definition 3.1
.

The
T
-variety
X
is called filtrable if it satisfies the following conditions:

(i)
X
is the union of its plus cells
X_{+}(x_{i},\lambda)
for

1\leq i\leq m
with respect to a fixed generic one parameter subgroup

\lambda
.

(ii) There exists a finite decreasing sequence of
T
-invariant closed
subvarieties of
X

X=Z_{1}\supset Z_{2}\cdots\supset Z_{m}\supset Z_{m+1}=\emptyset

such that
Z_{i}\setminus Z_{i+1}=Y_{i}:=X_{+}(x_{i},\lambda)
for

1\leq i\leq m
. In particular,

\displaystyle\overline{Y_{i}}\subseteq Z_{i}=\bigcup_{j\geq i}Y_{j}
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
X
be a normal complex algebraic variety
with an action of a complex algebraic torus
T
such that

X^{T}:=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\}
is finite. We say that
X
is
T
-cellular
if
X
is filtrable with respect to some fixed generic one-parameter
subgroup
\lambda
and in addition each plus cell

Y_{i}:=X_{+}(x_{i},\lambda)
is
T
-equivariantly isomorphic to a complex
affine space
\mathbb{C}^{n_{i}}
on which
T
-acts linearly for

1\leq i\leq m
.

bundle
\mathcal{E}\times_{T_{comp}}\mathbb{C}_{\chi}
for

Recall that
K^{0}_{G_{comp}}(\mathcal{B})

-algebra
structure via the map

Let

be a

over

Let

The following theorem gives a GKM type description of

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
X=X(\Delta)
be a complete

-cellular toric
variety. The ring

\mathcal{A}_{K^{0}_{G_{comp}}(\mathcal{B})}

\mathcal{E}(x_{i})

Proof.

The map

is an inclusion by

-algebra

∎

7.
Equivariant
K
-theory of horospherical
varieties

We refer to
[
26
]
,
[
28
, Chapter 13]
and

[
23
]
for basic results in the theory of horospherical
varieties (also see
[
21
]
,
[
35
]
and
[
29
]
).

Let
G
be a connected complex reductive linear algebraic group, let

B\subseteq G
be a Borel subgroup,
T\subseteq B
a maximal torus. We
denote by
\Phi
the system of roots of
(G,T)
, by
\Phi_{+}
the
system of positive roots with respect to
T\subseteq B
, and by

\Sigma
the system of simple roots. Let
W
denote the Weyl group of

(G,T)
. For
I\subseteq\Sigma
, we denote by
W_{I}
the subgroup
of
W
generated by the simple reflections
s_{\alpha}
for

\alpha\in I
. We denote by
P_{I}
the parabolic subgroup of
G

generated by
B
and
W_{I}
. There is a one-one correspondence
between the subsets
I\subseteq\Sigma
of simple roots and the
parabolic subgroups
P_{I}
of
G
containing
B
.

A
homogeneous space

{G}/{H}
is called
horospherical
if

{H}
contains a maximal unipotent subgroup
{U}

of
{G}
. We shall assume that
U
is the unipotent radical of
B
.

An
embedding
of a homogeneous space
G/H
is a pair
(X,x)
,
where
X
is a normal
G
-variety and
x
is a point of
X
, such that
the orbit of
x
in
X
is open and isomorphic to
G/H
. When
G/H
is
horospherical we call
X
a horospherical variety.

Recall that a homogeneous space
G/H
is called
spherical
if it
contains an open orbit under the action of a Borel subgroup
B
of

G
. A spherical variety is an embedding of a spherical homogeneous
space
G/H
. By Bruhat decomposition it follows that every
horospherical variety is spherical.

For a spherical homogeneous space
G/H
we denote by
D
the
collection of irreducible divisors of
G/H
which are
B
-stable but
not
G
-stable. The elements of
D
are called
colors
. Let
X

be an embedding of
G/H
. We denote by
X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}
the

G
-stable irreducible divisors of
X
. We can identify
D
with the
set of irreducible divisors of
X
which are
B
-stable and not

G
-stable. Thus
D\penalty\ \cup\penalty\ \{X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}\}
is the set of
irreducible
B
-stable divisors of
X
.

A
color of
X
is a color which contains a closed
G
-orbit.

A spherical variety
X
is
toroidal
if it does not contain any
color.

Let
G/H
be a horospherical homogeneous space. Let

{P}:=N_{{G}}({H})
. Then
{P}
is a parabolic subgroup of
{G}
and

{P}/{H}
is a torus. Consider the
{P}/{H}
-bundle

{G}/{H}\rightarrow{G}/{P}
.

Let
{Y}
be a
{P}/{H}
-toric variety. We can consider the
associated bundle
{X}:={G}\times_{{P}}{Y}
where
{P}
acts by left
multiplication on
{Y}
through the projection
{P}/{H}
. Then
X

contains
G/H=G\times_{P}P/H
as a dense orbit so that it is an
embedding of the horospherical homogeneous space
G/H
. It is further
known that
X
is toroidal (see
[
26
]
). Thus

X={G}\times_{{P}}{Y}
is a toroidal horospherical variety.

It is in fact known that any toroidal horospherical embedding of
G/H

is of the form
G\times_{P}Y
with
Y
a toric variety for the torus

P/H
. Indeed,
Y
is the toric variety corresponding to the fan

\mathbb{F}(X)
associated to
X
(see
[
26
]
and

[
28
]
).

Let
G_{comp}
denote a maximal compact subgroup of
G
such that

B_{comp}=B\cap G_{comp}=T_{comp}
is a maximal torus of
G_{comp}

and
Z:=P\cap G_{comp}
is the centralizer of a circle subgroup of

G_{comp}
.

Moreover,
H_{comp}=H\cap G_{comp}
is a compact subgroup of
H
such
that
N_{G_{comp}}(H_{comp})=Z
. Thus
(P/H)_{comp}\simeq Z/H_{comp}

and
G/P\simeq G_{comp}/Z
.

Let
R(Z)
denote the representation ring of
Z
. Let
R(Z/H_{comp})

denote the representation ring of
Z/H_{comp}
. The
projection
Z\rightarrow Z/H_{comp}
induces a canonical restriction map from

R(Z/H_{comp})=R(P/H)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r}}{{\rightarrow}}R(Z)=R(P).

Recall that
K^{0}_{G_{comp}}(G/P)
is an
R(Z)
-algebra via the map
which sends any
[V]\in R(Z)
to the class of the associated

G_{comp}
-equivariant vector bundle
[G_{comp}\times_{Z}V]
where

G_{comp}
-acts from the left. Thus
K^{0}_{G_{comp}}(G/P)
gets the
structure of
R(Z/H_{comp})
-algebra by composing with
r
.

Recall that
X
is complete if and only if
\mathbb{F}(X)
is a
complete fan (see
[
21
, Theorem 4.2]
,
[
23
, Proposition
5.3.11]
). Further, recall that
X
is smooth if and only if
the fan
\mathbb{F}(X)
is smooth (see
[
29
, Theorem 4.2.3]

and
[
23
, Proposition 6.3.2]
).

Suppose that
Y
is a complete
P/H
-cellular toric variety. Then we
have the following result:

Theorem 7.1
.

The
G_{comp}
-equivariant
topological
K
-ring of a complete toroidal horospherical embedding

X
of
G/H
is isomorphic to the following ring

K^{0}_{G_{comp}}(G/P)\otimes_{R((P/H)_{comp})}PLP(\mathbb{F}(X))
as
an
K^{0}_{G_{comp}}(G/P)
-algebra.

Proof.

Recall that we have a
G
-equivariant
isomorphism of
X
with
G\times_{P}Y
. Further, note that

G\times_{P}Y=\Big(G\times_{P}P/H\Big)\times_{P/H}Y
. Thus we
have a
G
-equivariant isomorphism of
X
with the
G
-equivariant
toric bundle
\mathcal{E}\times_{P/H}Y
associated to the

G
-equivariant principal
P/H
-bundle:

\mathcal{E}=G/H\rightarrow\mathcal{B}=G/P
.

Recall that
G/P
is a smooth projective variety and has a
filtrable Bialynicki Birula
T
-cellular decomposition given by
the Schubert cells indexed by the minimal length coset
representatives
W^{I}
of
W/W_{I}
, where
P=P_{I}
for

(see
[
22
]
and
[
8
, Proposition
5.1]
). Hence by Proposition
4.5
, it follows that

K_{T_{comp}}^{0}(G/P)
is a free
R(T_{comp})
-module of rank

|W/W_{I}|
and
K_{T_{comp}}^{-1}(G/P)=0
. By
[
25
, Theorem
4.4]
, this further implies that

K^{-1}_{G_{comp}}(G/P)=K^{-1}_{T_{comp}}(G/P)^{W}=0
. Thus
Assumption
4.1
holds for
\mathcal{B}=G/P
. Since
Y
is a
complete
P/H
-cellular toric variety, by Theorem
5.2
,

K^{0}_{(P/H)_{comp}}(Y)
is isomorphic as an

R((P/H)_{comp})
-algebra to
PLP(\mathbb{F}(X))
. The theorem now
follows from Theorem
6.3
, by taking

\mathcal{E}=G/H
,
\mathcal{B}=G/P
,
T=P/H
and

\Delta=\mathbb{F}(X)
.
∎

We have the following description of
K^{0}_{G_{comp}}(X)
in the case
when

is a smooth complete
P/H
-cellular toric variety. See

[

for the analogous result on the ordinary

Corollary 7.2
.

The
G_{comp}
-equivariant topological

-ring of a smooth complete toroidal horospherical embedding

of

, is isomorphic to the ring

K^{0}_{G_{comp}}(G/P)\otimes_{R((P/H)_{comp})}SR(\mathbb{F}(X))
as an

K^{0}_{G_{comp}}(G/P)
-algebra.

Proof.

Since
Y
is a smooth
P/H
-cellular toric variety,

K_{(P/H)_{comp}}^{0}(Y)
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{K}^{0}_{P/H}(Y)

which in turn is isomorphic to

, namely the

\mathbb{F}(X)
, as an

-algebra (see
[
38
]
,
[
7
]
). The proof now
follows from Theorem

and Corollary

6.5
.
∎

\mathcal{K}^{0}_{G}(X)\cong\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{K}^{0}_{G}(G/P),\mathbb{F}(X)))

\mathcal{K}^{0}_{G}(G/P)

(7.24)

R(P/H)=R(Z/H_{comp}).

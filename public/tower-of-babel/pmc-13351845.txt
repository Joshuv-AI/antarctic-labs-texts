Dietary arginine modulates growth performance and testicular development across life stages in male swimming crab (Portunus trituberculatus)
Authors: Xiaoyue Li, Tiantian Xu, Yao Deng, Wenhao Zhan, Shichao Xie, Xishuai Cui, Yushan Zou, Tingting Zhu, Min Jin, Qicun Zhou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study evaluated the effects of dietary arginine (Arg) on growth performance, testicular development, and apoptosis in juvenile and adult male swimming crabs (Portunus trituberculatus). Six isonitrogenous and isolipidic diets were formulated with graded Arg levels of 1.89% (control), 2.27%, 2.65%, 3.05%, 3.43%, and 3.80%, respectively. A total of 180 juvenile male crabs (initial weight: 4.35 ± 0.02 g) were randomly assigned to six treatments (three replicates of 10 crabs each) and cultured for 20 weeks. Four crabs per replicate were sampled at week 13 (juvenile stage), and the remaining six crabs per replicate were sampled at week 20 (adult stage). At the juvenile stage, compared with the control, those fed the 2.27% Arg diet exhibited significantly higher final body weight (FBW), percent weight gain (PWG), specific growth rate (SGR), and feed efficiency (FE) (P < 0.05). Conversely, the lowest growth performance was observed in the 3.80% Arg group, whereas feed intake (FI) showed an inverse trend (P < 0.001). In the hemolymph, crabs fed the 2.27% Arg diet exhibited the highest activities of endothelial nitric oxide synthase (e-NOS), superoxide dismutase (T-SOD), catalase (CAT), as well as the highest total antioxidant capacity (T-AOC), and glutathione (GSH) content, which were significantly higher than those in the control (P < 0.05). In the hepatopancreas, the relative expression of immune-related genes (mnsod, gsh-px, relish, and po) were upregulated in the 2.27% Arg group compared with control (P < 0.05). And the relative expression of genes involved in lipid synthesis (fas, dgat, acc, and fad) and lipid catabolism (cpt-1, fatp, and fabp) were significantly elevated (P < 0.05), and B-cell diameter was significantly reduced in 2.27% Arg group compared with the control (P < 0.001). At the adult stage, crabs fed 2.27% and 2.65% Arg diets showed higher gonadosomatic index, testis index and vas deferens index than those fed other diets (P < 0.05). In the hemolymph, the activities of adenylate kinase (AK) and neuronal nitric oxide synthase (n-NOS), as well as nitric oxide (NO) content, were significantly increased in the 2.65% Arg group (P < 0.05), while hemolymph ammonia content was significantly decreased compared with the control (P = 0.007). Compared with the control, testicular relative expression of genes involved in Arg metabolism (odc1, creatinase, nadh6, sms, and nnos), spermatogenesis (iag, dsx, and soxe), and protein synthesis (tor) were significantly elevated in the 2.65% Arg group (P < 0.05). Furthermore, compared with the control, 2.65% Arg enhanced anti-apoptotic gene (bcl2) relative expression, reduced BAX/BCL-2 and P-JNK/JNK ratios, decreased apoptotic cell proportion, and lowered reactive oxygen species (ROS) mean fluorescence levels in the testis (P < 0.05). In conclusion, the optimal dietary Arg requirements for juvenile and adult P. trituberculatus were estimated to be 2.04% and 2.46%, respectively. Furthermore, dietary supplementation with 2.27% Arg maximally enhanced growth and immune function in juvenile crabs, whereas 2.65% Arg could improve Arg metabolism, promote testicular development, and reduce cell apoptosis in adult male crabs.Keywords: Arginine, Life stage, Arginine metabolism, Testicular development, Male Portunus trituberculatus

Introduction

The swimming crab (Portunus trituberculatus) is an important crustacean species in China, with seawater aquaculture production reaching approximately 100,000 tonnes in 2023, dominating the national crab industry and generating considerable economic benefits (Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs of China, 2024). At present, farming relies mainly on natural feeds, such as chilled mixed fish and low-value shellfish, with limited use of formulated feeds (Xie et al., 2025). Although these natural feeds offer high palatability, they are nutritionally imbalanced and can contribute to water quality deterioration, which presents challenges to sustainable aquaculture. Developing stage-specific compound feeds tailored to the nutritional requirements of swimming crabs has therefore become an urgent need (Zhu et al., 2022). Previous research has shown that appropriately formulated feeds can improve survival, growth, gonadosomatic index (GSI), hepatosomatic index (HSI), antioxidant capacity, and immunity (Zhu et al., 2024). Among macronutrients, proteins and lipids play central roles in supporting growth and reproductive organ development (Aaqillah-Amr et al., 2021). Proteins, consisting of 20 amino acids, including 10 essential amino acids, are critical for survival, growth, and development. High-quality protein with a balanced amino acid profile further enhances immunity, antioxidant capacity, and reproductive performance (Goimier et al., 2006; Hao et al., 2024).

Arginine (Arg), a nitrogen-rich essential amino acid, performs diverse biological functions beyond protein synthesis (Fung et al., 2025). Its guanidine side chain provides distinctive biochemical properties that allow it to act as a precursor for several key biomolecules (Bronte and Zanovello, 2005). These molecules, which support vital physiological functions, include urea (a key metabolite in ammonia detoxification), creatine (providing energy for muscle and nerve cells), polyamines (regulating cell growth and proliferation), and the essential signaling molecule nitric oxide (NO) (acting as a key mediator of immune responses and numerous physiological processes) (Li et al., 2025). As a substrate for NO, Arg plays key roles in cardiovascular regulation, neurotransmission, and immune defence (Abu-Soud et al., 2025; Carlström, 2021; Lai et al., 2024). Recent studies have highlighted the importance of Arg in reproductive processes. In vertebrates, it enhances sperm motility and capacitation (Sahoo and Gupta, 2023), reduces heat-stress-induced sperm damage (Wang et al., 2025), and influences testicular development. In invertebrates, Arg regulates glycogen synthesis (Fan et al., 2025), fat deposition, and meat quality (Li et al., 2022) via the NO pathway in Litopenaeus vannamei, and alleviates oxidative stress and inflammatory responses in juvenile Eriocheir sinensis during environmental stress (Qi et al., 2021). However, research on Arg in crustaceans has primarily focused on juveniles or females, and studies on male crabs remain limited.

Portunus trituberculatus exhibits marked sexual dimorphism (Jia et al., 2024), with males showing faster growth and larger edible portions before overwintering (Wu and Wang, 2017). Throughout their lifecycle, male crabs progress through embryonic (Sun et al., 2025), larval (Zhang et al., 2022), juvenile, and adult stages, each with distinct nutritional needs (Lu et al., 2022). Juvenile crabs prioritise moulting and tissue accretion (Deng et al., 2024; Zhu et al., 2022), whereas adult males focus on testicular development, sperm production, and reproductive capacity (Zhang et al., 2024b). Consequently, adult crabs undergoing gonadal maturation may require higher levels of specific amino acids, including Arg, compared with juveniles. Nevertheless, systematic studies assessing Arg requirements across the male crab developmental stages remain scarce. To address this gap, the present study examined the effects of dietary Arg on juvenile (13 weeks) and adult (20 weeks) males. Nutrient accumulation and testicular development were evaluated to provide essential evidence to support precise nutritional regulation during key life stages of male P. trituberculatus.

Materials and methods

Animal ethics statement

All experimental protocols received approval from the Ethical Science Committee of Ningbo University (approval No.: 139111111) and were executed in compliance with the Standard Operating Techniques for the Use of Laboratory Animals (SOP) established by Ningbo University. This research did not include any endangered or protected species (permit No.: SYXK20190005).

Diet preparation

L-Arg of high purity (A0111, > 98%) was supplied by Shanghai Sanjie Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). In this study, six isonitrogenous (45% crude protein) and isolipidic (9% crude lipid) diets were formulated. The dietary ingredients and proximate composition are presented in Table 1. The experimental diets were formulated using six protein sources (Peruvian fish meal, soybean meal, Antarctic krill meal, corn gluten meal, wheat gluten meal, and yeast hydrolysate), three lipid sources (fish oil, soybean oil, and soybean lecithin), and wheat flour as the carbohydrate source. The levels of non-essential amino acids (aspartic acid and glycine at a 1:1 ratio) in the basal amino acid mixture were adjusted to maintain an equivalent dietary nitrogen level among treatments following Arg supplementation. The control diet contained 1.89% Arg, and additional Arg was supplemented at levels of 0.4%, 0.8%, 1.2%, 1.6%, and 2.0%. The analysed Arg contents of the experimental diets were 1.89% (control), 2.27%, 2.65%, 3.05%, 3.43%, and 3.80%, respectively (Table 2). The dry components were accurately weighed according to the formulation, thoroughly mixed, and combined with the oil ingredients. Subsequently, distilled water was added, and the mixture was further blended to form a homogeneous dough. The mixture was processed into 1-mm and 3-mm pellets using an F-26 twin-screw extruder (F-26, South China University of Technology Machine Factory, Guangzhou, Guangdong, China). The pellets were oven-dried and stored at −20 °C for later use.

Crab culture

Juvenile male crabs were obtained from the Yinzhou Xianxiang Jiandao Aquaculture Farm (Ningbo, Zhejiang, China). Crabs were acclimatised for two weeks and fed a formulated feed with approximately 45% crude protein and 8% crude lipid (Guangdong Yuequn Ocean Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Jieyang, Guangdong, China). A total of 180 healthy juvenile male crabs (initial body weight: 4.35 ± 0.02 g) were selected for the feeding trial. To prevent cannibalism, crabs were individually housed in separate rearing compartments (45 cm × 30 cm × 20 cm) within a recirculating aquaculture system (RAS). The feeding management consisted of feeding twice daily (07:00 and 16:00), with a daily feeding rate of 2%–5% of body weight. Following each feeding session, any waste material (including faeces and uneaten feed) was immediately siphoned away to maintain water quality. The experiment was conducted in a recirculating aquaculture system with the following water parameters: temperature 26 ± 2 °C, pH 7.61 ± 0.01, salinity 23.42 ± 0.10 psu, and dissolved oxygen 6.91 ± 0.08 mg/L. The ammonia-nitrogen concentration was below 0.05 mg/L.

Experimental design and sample collection

In Exp. 1, a 13-week feeding trial was conducted with six treatments (three replicates per treatment). At the end of the trial, All crabs from the six Arg groups were weighed, and growth performance was calculated. Subsequently, four crabs per replicate were randomly sampled for tissue collection. Hemolymph was collected from the pericardial cavity of four crabs using a 1-mL syringe, and hemolymph biochemical assays were performed. For enzyme activity assays, histological observation, and mRNA expression analysis, hepatopancreas tissues were also sampled from four crabs per replicate. The collected tissues were snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at −80 °C, except for the hepatopancreas samples intended for histochemical analysis.

In Exp. 2, a 20-week feeding trial was conducted with six treatments (three replicates per treatment). At the end of the trial, the remaining six crabs per replicate were sampled for tissue collection. The body, hepatopancreas, testis, and vas deferens weights of six crabs per replicate were recorded to assess morphological parameters. Hemolymph was collected via pericardial cavity puncture with a syringe and was used for biochemical assays. Hepatopancreas, testis, and vas deferens rapidly dissected and allocated for subsequent analyses, including mRNA expression, histomorphometry, Western blotting, reactive oxygen species (ROS), and apoptosis assays.

Biochemical analysis

Proximate composition and amino acids in the experimental diet

Proximate analysis of the formulated diets was performed in accordance with the standardized methods of the Association of Official Analytical Chemists (AOAC, 2006). The moisture content was determined by drying the samples at 105 °C until a consistent weight was achieved (method 922.08). The crude protein content was determined using an automatic protein analyser (FP-528, LECO Corporation, St. Joseph, MI, USA) in accordance with method 968.06. Crude lipid was quantified using Soxhlet extraction (method 922.06), whereas crude ash content was determined using incineration in a muffle furnace (TM-2010, Beijing Innochem Scientific Instruments Co., Ltd., Beijing, China) at 550 °C for 6 h (method 920.39). Organic matter was calculated by subtracting the crude ash content from the dry matter. Crude fiber content in diets was determined using the filter bag method GB/T 6434-2022 (Standardization Administration of China, 2022). Approximately 1.0 g of sample was sealed in a specially designed polyester filter bag and treated sequentially with acid (0.13 mol/L H2SO4) and alkali (0.23 mol/L KOH) for 30 min each, followed by batch washing and degreasing. Crude fiber content was calculated based on the mass difference after drying at 130 °C and ashing at 500 °C. The gross energy content of the experimental diets was measured using an oxygen bomb calorimeter (PARR 6300, Parr Instrument Company, Moline, IL, USA) in accordance with the standard procedure ISO 9831:1998 (ISO, 1998).

The amino acid composition was analysed in accordance with GB/T 18246-2019 (Standardization Administration of China, 2019). The sample was accurately weighed and 5 mL of 6 mol/L hydrochloric acid solution was added. To prevent oxidation and degradation of amino acids during high-temperature hydrolysis, the hydrolysis tube was purged with nitrogen gas for 3 min to remove oxygen. The sealed hydrolysis tube was placed in a 110 °C sand bath for 24 h to completely hydrolyse proteins into free amino acids. After hydrolysis, the sample was cooled to room temperature and diluted to 50 mL with double-distilled water. A 1-mL aliquot of the hydrolysate was evaporated to dryness using a nitrogen evaporator to remove hydrochloric acid. The residue was resuspended in 1 mL of 0.02 mol/L hydrochloric acid. The solution was filtered through a 0.22-μm aqueous microporous membrane and transferred to a sample vial for analysis. The amino acid composition was analyzed using an amino acid analyzer (Model L-8900, Hitachi High-Tech Science Corporation, Tokyo, Japan). Briefly, the amino acids were separated via cation-exchange chromatography utilizing a gradient elution with buffers of varying pH and ionic strengths, followed by post-column derivatization with ninhydrin for absolute colorimetric quantification.

Hemolymph biochemical parameters

Following hemolymph collection, samples from each treatment group were preserved overnight at 4 °C. Thereafter, the hemolymph supernatant was isolated using centrifugation (1200 × g for 10 min at 4 °C). Hemolymph levels of albumin (ALB), alanine aminotransferase (ALT), and aspartate aminotransferase (AST) were quantified utilizing an automated biochemistry analyzer (Model Selectra Junior Pro, Vital Scientific B.V., Dieren, Gelderland, The Netherlands). In accordance with the guidelines of commercial assay kits (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing, Jiangsu, China), the subsequent biochemical parameters were assessed: total antioxidant capacity (T-AOC; Cat. No. A015-2-1), superoxide dismutase (T-SOD; Cat. No. A001-1-2), acid phosphatase (ACP; Cat. No. A060-1-1), alkaline phosphatase (AKP; Cat. No. A059-1-1), catalase (CAT; Cat. No. A007-1-1), glutathione (GSH; Cat. No. A006-1-1), malondialdehyde (MDA; Cat. No. A003-1-2), nitric oxide (NO; Cat. No. A012-1-2), and hemolymph ammonia (BA; Cat. No. A086-1-1). Additionally, the hemolymph activities of neuronal nitric oxide synthase (n-NOS; Cat. No. MK0321OA), endothelial nitric oxide synthase (e-NOS; Cat. No. MK0310OA), and Arg kinase (AK; Cat. No. MK0314OA) were determined utilizing commercial ELISA kits from Jiangsu Sumeike Biological Technology Co., Ltd., Yancheng, Jiangsu, China.

Real-time quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR)

Hepatopancreas and testis tissue samples were collected, and total RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent (Nanjing Vazyme Biotech Co., Ltd., Nanjing, Jiangsu, China) according to the manufacturer's instructions. RNA integrity was verified by 1% agarose gel electrophoresis. Complementary DNA (cDNA) was synthesised using a reverse transcription kit (Nanjing Vazyme Biotech Co., Ltd., Nanjing, Jiangsu, China), and the resulting cDNA was stored at −80 °C until use.

Real-time quantitative PCR analysis in this study was performed in a 384-well plate with a total reaction volume of 10 μL. The reaction mixture consisted of 5 μL of Champagne Taq DNA Polymerase (ChamQ) Synergetic Binding Reagent (SYBR) qPCR Master Mix (Nanjing Vazyme Biotech Co., Ltd., Nanjing, Jiangsu, China), 0.5 μL each of forward and reverse primers, 1 μL of cDNA as a template, and 3 μL of RNase-free water. The full names of the abbreviations of all genes can be found in Table S1. Primer sequences are provided in Table S2. PCR amplification was conducted using the LightCycler 480 system (F. Hoffmann-La Roche AG, Basel, Switzerland), following the procedure described by Li et al. (2025). The β-actin gene was used as the internal control to normalize variations in sample loading, RNA extraction efficiency, and reverse transcription efficiency. Relative mRNA expression levels were determine using the 2−ΔΔCt method (Livak and Schmittgen, 2001)

Histological determinations

This study selected hepatopancreas, testis, and vas deferens tissues for morphological observation. Tissue samples were first fixed in Bouin's solution for 24 h, which preserves cellular morphology, while minimising tissue shrinkage. Following fixation, tissues were dehydrated through a graded alcohol series, cleared in xylene, and embedded in paraffin to prepare tissue blocks suitable for sectioning. Tissue blocks were then sectioned using a microtome (Model RM2016, Leica Instrument Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China) into 4 to 6 μm thin sections, which were subsequently mounted onto microscope slides. Prior to staining, sections were deparaffinised with xylene and hydrated through a descending ethanol series to facilitate dye penetration. Nuclei were stained with haematoxylin for 3 to 5 min. Sections were then rinsed in running water and differentiated with a 1% hydrochloric acid-ethanol solution to remove non-specific background staining. A weakly alkaline solution was used for bluing, rendering the nuclei blue. For cytoplasmic contrast, eosin solution was applied for approximately 15 s to impart a pink hue to cytoplasmic structures. Following staining, sections were progressively dehydrated in graded alcohol solutions, cleared in xylene, and mounted with neutral resin for long-term preservation and microscopic examination.

For lipid histochemistry of hepatopancreas samples, tissues were first fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde solution for 24 h and prepared as cryosections. Sections were stained with 0.5% Oil Red O for 10 to 15 min in the dark to visualise neutral lipid components. Differentiation was performed using 60% isopropanol until the background was colourless, followed by rinsing under running water. Nuclei were counterstained with haematoxylin for 1 min and blued under running water to clearly delineate cellular morphology, and sections were mounted with gelatin glycerol and stored at 4 °C in the dark for subsequent microscopic observation and analysis.

Determination of apoptosis and ROS

Fresh testis tissues were collected, and mitochondria were isolated using an Enhanced Cell Mitochondria Isolation Kit (Cat. No. C3602S; Beyotime Biotechnology Inc., Shanghai, China) in accordance with the manufacturer's instructions. In summary, the procedure involved finely mincing the tissue samples, extracting them on ice using pre-chilled mitochondrial isolation buffer, and subjecting the suspension to centrifugation to obtain a mitochondrial-rich pellet.

Apoptosis was assessed using a fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC) Annexin V Apoptosis Detection Kit I (MultiSciences Biotech Co., Ltd., Hangzhou, Zhejiang, China). Briefly, 0.1 μL of Annexin V-fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC) and 2 μL of propidium iodide (PI) were added to a mitochondrial suspension of suitable density, gently mixed, and incubated for 5 min in the dark. The control tubes included an unstained sample (blank), a single-stained Annexin V-FITC sample, and a single-stained PI sample. The single-stained controls were used to adjust the voltage and fluorescence compensation parameters on the flow cytometer. All samples were promptly examined using a Flow Cytometer (Gallios, Beckman Coulter, Inc., Brea, CA, USA).

Mitochondrial ROS levels were measured using a ROS Assay Kit (Beyotime Biotechnology Inc., Shanghai, China) A final concentration of 10 μmol/L 2′,7′-dichlorofluorescin diacetate (DCFH-DA) was added to the mitochondrial suspension and incubated at 37 °C for 20 min in the dark. After incubation, the samples were washed three times with serum-free medium to remove excess probe. Intracellular ROS levels were quantified by using fluorescence intensity via a Flow Cytometer (Gallios, Beckman Coulter, Inc., Brea, CA, USA) utilizing an excitation wavelength of 488 nm and an emission wavelength of 525 nm.

Western blot detection

Testicular samples were homogenised in pre-chilled Radio Immunoprecipitation Assay (RIPA) lysis buffer (Cat. No. P0013B, Beyotime Biotechnology Inc., Shanghai, China). To preserve protein integrity and phosphorylation status, the lysis system was supplemented a protease and phosphatase inhibitor cocktail for general use (Cat. No. P1046, Beyotime Biotechnology Inc., Shanghai, China). The lysate was centrifuged after 30 min of incubation on ice to clarify the solution. The resulting supernatant constituted the total protein fraction. A sodium dodecyl sulphate–polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) Sample Loading Buffer (5×, Cat. No. P0015L, Beyotime Biotechnology Inc., Shanghai, China) was added to the protein extract, and The mixture was denatured in a water bath for 10 min. The proteins were separated by SDS-PAGE at equal loading amounts and electroblotted onto polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) membranes. The membranes were blocked with rapid blocking reagent for 15 min and then incubated overnight at 4 °C with the corresponding primary antibodies (GAPDH, BAX, JNK, P-JNK, and BCL-2). The full names of the abbreviations of all antibodies can be found in Table S1. Detailed information on antibody names, dilutions, distributors, and catalog numbers is provided in Table S3. At room temperature, the membranes were incubated with goat anti-rabbit mouse immunoglobulin G (IgG)-horseradish peroxidase (HRP) for 1 h after being washed three times with Tris-buffered saline (TBS)/Tween Buffer. Protein bands were visualised using an enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) substrate and imaged using a Tanon 5200 imaging system (Tanon Science & Technology Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China). ImageJ software (National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, MD, USA) was used to quantify band intensities.

Results

Growth performance in juvenile stage

Dietary Arg significantly affected the growth performance of juvenile male crabs during a 13-week feeding intervention (Table 3). In comparison to the control diet (1.89% Arg), crabs fed a diet containing 2.27% Arg exhibited significantly increased FBW, PWG, SGR, and FE (P < 0.05). However, further increasing dietary Arg to 3.80% resulted in a significant reduction in these growth indices (P < 0.05). Conversely, compared with the control group, FI was significantly decreased in the 2.27% Arg group, but significantly increased in the 3.80% Arg group (P < 0.001). The IBW and survival rate exhibited no significant differences among the treatments (P > 0.05). Two-slope broken-line regression analysis of PWG as a function of dietary Arg levels determined the optimal dietary Arg requirement for juvenile male swimming crabs to be 2.04% (Fig. 1).

Discussion

This study identified clear differences in growth performance and metabolic requirements between juvenile and adult male crabs. The juvenile stage represents a critical period characterised by rapid somatic growth and elevated nutritional demands. Dietary Arg supplementation significantly affected the growth performance of juvenile male crabs during the 13-week feeding trial. A diet containing 2.27% Arg markedly improved FBW, PWG, FE, and SGR, whereas higher inclusion levels resulted in reduced growth. Based on PWG, the optimum dietary Arg requirement for juvenile male crabs was estimated to be 2.04%. This value is lower than the 2.77% reported for juvenile swimming crabs (Jin et al., 2016), which may reflect differences in diet formulation, crab size, sex, or rearing conditions. It is notable that Jin et al. (2016) did not differentiate sex when reporting their findings. In line with studies in other crustaceans including L. vannamei (Fan et al., 2025), Macrobrachium nipponense (Yang et al., 2023), and Scylla paramamosain (Wu et al., 2022), appropriate Arg supplementation promotes growth, whereas Arg deficiency impairs it. The recommended level (2.27%) is slightly higher than the model-derived optimum (2.04%), which was based solely on PWG. The recommended value considers all growth performance outcomes and therefore supports practical and reliable use in aquaculture.

Arginine, a highly metabolically active amino acid, is a fundamental constituent of proteins and an important precursor of several bioactive metabolites. In mammals, Arg is regarded as a semi-essential amino acid because it can be synthesised endogenously and further metabolised into urea and ornithine to maintain nitrogen homeostasis (Li et al., 2025). However, in crustaceans, the endogenous synthesis of Arg is limited, making dietary intake essential. Arginine serves as the substrate for NOS, which catalyses the formation of NO. There are three major forms of NOS: e-NOS, n-NOS, and inducible (i-NOS) (Sun et al., 2023). In this study, dietary 2.27% Arg significantly increased e-NOS activity in the hepatopancreas of juvenile male crabs. e–NOS–derived NO has an important role in innate immune regulation by inhibiting nuclear factor kappa-B (NF-κB) activation and reducing adhesion molecule expression, thereby attenuating inflammation responses (Colasanti and Persichini, 2000; Rabelink and van Zonneveld, 2006). Although physiological concentrations of NO are beneficial for immune regulation, neurotransmission, and apoptosis, excessive NO production can induce oxidative injury (Andrabi et al., 2023). In this experiment, as the Arg level in the diet increased, NO levels in the crab hemolymph upregulated. However, Qi et al. (2019) reported that dietary Arg supplementation reduced serum NO levels in Eriocheir sinensis, suggesting that NOS activity strongly depends on substrate availability. Within physiological ranges, increasing substrate concentrations can NOS catalytic efficiency and NO production. Therefore, the net regulatory effect of dietary Arg on NO metabolism likely varies between species, physiological states, and supplementation levels. Moreover, dietary 2.65% Arg reduced hemolymph ammonia concentrations in juvenile crabs, indicating improved nitrogen utilization and reduced hepatopancreatic burden. Excessive hemolymph ammonia can induce hepatotoxicity, whereas appropriate Arg supplementation supports urea cycle function and hepatopancreatic health (Ye et al., 2025).

An enhancement of antioxidant capacity was also observed in the present study, as demonstrated by increased hemolymph T-AOC, T-SOD, and CAT activities and GSH content, together with the upregulation of mnsod, gsh-px, relish, and po expressions in the hepatopancreas following 2.27% Arg supplementation. The balance between ROS production and clearance is essential for cellular homeostasis. Functioning as the first line of defense, T-SOD converts O2- to H2O2, which is subsequently detoxified by CAT and GSH-Px, forming an efficient enzymatic antioxidant defence network that limits oxidative damage (Xu et al., 2018). The antioxidant capacity of crabs in the 2.27% Arg group was particularly notable. However, in the 2.27% Arg group, although CAT activity in hemolymph increased, cat gene expression in the hepatopancreas did not significantly change. This discrepancy may reflect tissue-specific differences in the response to oxidative stress. Under high physiological demand, tissues such as gills, haemocytes, hepatopancreas, and heart may upregulate CAT synthesis and release soluble forms of the enzyme into hemolymph, thereby increasing circulating enzymatic activity (Chen et al., 2012) while transcription levels in the hepatopancreas may remain relatively stable. Thus, the asynchrony between hemolymph enzymatic activity and tissue-level transcription appears biologically plausible. In crustaceans, Arg has also been associated with immune-metabolic regulation. For example, dietary Arg supplementation alleviated oxidative stress and enhanced antioxidant enzyme activity in juvenile E. sinensis under high-pH stress (Qi et al., 2021). Similarly, S. paramamosain fed optimal Arg levels exhibited improved antioxidant enzyme activities (Wu et al., 2022). Collectively, these findings suggest that dietary Arg enhances NO synthesis, strengthens antioxidant defences, and promotes immune capacity in juvenile crabs. Therefore, a dietary Arg level of approximately 2.27% is recommended to optimize immunity and oxidative balance in juvenile male swimming crabs.

The hepatopancreas is a key metabolic organ in crustaceans, responsible for digestion, nutrient absorption, and lipid metabolism; maintaining its structural integrity is essential for the growth and development of juvenile crabs (Deng et al., 2024). Histomorphological evidence in this study indicated that dietary Arg levels significantly affected hepatopancreatic morphology. The diameter of B cells in the hepatopancreas was markedly reduced in the 2.27% Arg group compared with the control, suggesting enhanced tissue density and functional integrity. However, further increases in dietary Arg resulted in a progressive enlargement of B cells, reaching the greatest diameter in the 3.43% Arg group. This observation suggests that excessive Arg supplementation may induce cellular swelling, vacuolisation, and structural disorganisation of the hepatopancreas, whereas appropriate supplementation helps maintain cellular morphology and hepatopancreatic health. Similar protective effects of Arg have been reported in E. sinensis, where Arg ameliorated T-2 toxin-induced microstructural damage in the hepatopancreas (Zhang et al., 2020). Oil Red O staining further demonstrated that the relative area of lipid droplets in the hepatopancreas decreased significantly with increasing dietary Arg levels, suggesting that Arg supplementation reduces lipid accumulation. Notably, the expression of genes involved in with both lipid synthesis (fas, dgat, acc, and fad) and lipid catabolism (cpt-1, fatp, and fabp) in 2.27% Arg group was upregulated, implying that Arg accelerates lipid turnover and promotes dynamic lipid metabolism. These findings are consistent with previous reports that enhanced NO synthesis induced by Arg facilitates lipid catabolism (Szlas et al., 2022). In L. vannamei, inhibition of NOS after Arg supplementation significantly downregulated lipid metabolic genes, demonstrating that Arg promotes hepatopancreatic lipid metabolism through the NO signaling pathway (Fan et al., 2025). Taken together, these findings indicate that an appropriate dietary Arg level (approximately 2.27%) supports hepatopancreatic structural integrity and enhances lipid metabolic activity in juvenile male crabs, whereas excessive Arg intake may lead to hepatocellular vacuolisation and metabolic imbalance.

Dietary Arg supplementation during the juvenile stage confers substantial benefits, markedly enhancing growth performance and immune competence. However, in adult male crabs, Arg metabolism shifts toward supporting reproductive development and energy homeostasis. When supplemented in the diet, Arg activates AK, hydrolyses Arg into urea and ornithine (Niu et al., 2022). Subsequently, ODC1 then decarboxylates urea to produce putrescine, a precursor for the synthesis of spermidine and spermine. Putrescine, spermidine, and spermine are polyamines that are essential for gonadal maturation, cell division, and proliferation (Lenis et al., 2018). This study demonstrated that dietary 2.27% and 2.65% Arg supplementation markedly enhanced the expression of essential genes implicated in polyamine production, including odc1 and sms. As ODC1 is the rate-limiting enzyme for polyamine formation, and SMS catalyses spermidine synthesis, these findings indicate that dietary Arg enhances spermidine production in both the hepatopancreas and testes, thereby promoting spermatogenic activity. Consistent with previous findings, elevated polyamine metabolism, including the spermine and γ-aminobutyric acid pathways, supports spermatogenesis during reproductive maturation in crustaceans (Wu et al., 2025). Furthermore, excessive dietary Arg (>2.27%) significantly up-regulated the expression of creatinase in the testes. Creatinase catalyses the conversion of Arg into creatine (Ostojic and Jorga, 2023), which in mammals contributes to the establishment of the blood-testis barrier and the maintenance of spermatogenesis (Kuribayashi et al., 2024). In aquatic species, creatine also facilitates ATP regeneration, providing energy for sperm motility and testicular metabolism (Yang et al., 2023). In this study, dietary Arg supplementation upregulated mitochondrial-related genes (nadk2 and nadh6) in testis, indicating enhanced mitochondrial activity. The mitochondrial enzyme nadk2 phosphorylates nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+) to produce nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate produce (NADP+), thereby supporting mitochondrial redox balance and energy metabolism (Zhang and Zhang, 2023). Collectively, these findings suggest that dietary Arg promotes the biosynthesis of spermidine and creatine, enhances mitochondrial function, and provides the energetic foundation necessary for testicular development and spermatogenesis in adult male crabs.

As crabs progress into adulthood, their physiological priorities shift from somatic growth toward gonadal maturation. The morphological indicators in this study showed that the GSI, TSI, and DSI of crabs consuming the 2.27% and 2.65% Arg diets were considerably higher than in the control group, indicating improved gonadal development. In male crabs, the testes and vas deferens form the principal components of the reproductive system. Mature sperm produced in the testes are transported through the seminiferous tubules into the vas deferens for storage before copulation (Zhang et al., 2024b). With adequate nutrient availability, the vas deferens gradually enlarges, accompanied by an increase abundance of mature sperm. In this study, dietary Arg supplementation markedly promoted testicular and vas deferens development in adult males. Although Arg has been shown to promote testicular development in vertebrates (Ribeiro et al., 2014; Wei et al., 2022), previous studies in crustaceans have largely focused on its effects on ovarian maturation (Fan et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2024a), and evidence for its role in male reproduction has remained limited. Testicular development in male crabs comprises three histological maturity phases: immature, maturing, and mature. It also includes a sequence of germ cell stages, spermatogonia, primary and secondary spermatocytes, spermatids, and mature sperm (Ikhwanuddin et al., 2018). In the present study, the testes of crabs fed the 2.65% Arg diet contained a significantly higher number of mature sperm, and sperm density in the vas deferens was also elevated compared with the other treatments. These results indicated that a dietary Arg level of 2.65% effectively stimulates spermatogenesis and sperm maturation in male crabs. Comparable improvements in testicular histology and accessory gland development have been reported in Capra hircus and broiler breeder roosters following Arg supplementation (Hussein et al., 2022), supporting its conserved role in reproductive regulation. In crustaceans, testicular development is predominantly regulated by the eyestalk-androgenic gland (AG)-testis endocrine axis. The insulin-like AG hormone (IAG), produced by the AG, is identified as the principal “IAG-switch” regulating male sexual development (Levy and Sagi, 2020). The current investigation demonstrated that dietary Arg supplementation markedly elevated iag expression in the testes. The transcription of IAG is regulated by members of the sox and dmrt gene families (Farhadi et al., 2021). In the swimming crab, soxe has been identified as an upstream activator of iag expression (Jiang et al., 2023). The expression of soxe was considerably elevated in the testes of the 2.65% Arg group, demonstrating the activation of the soxe-IAG regulatory axis. Similarly, dsx, a downstream gene of the DMRT family, showed considerable up-regulation in the same group, indicating that soxe and dsx jointly enhanced IAG expression (Liao et al., 2024). The insulin receptor (IR) acts as the ligand target of IAG and mediates insulin signaling during testicular development. Suppression of iag expression in crabs reduces IR levels, thereby impairing the insulin pathway (Jiang et al., 2020). In the present study, Arg supplementation significantly elevated ir1 expression in the testes, suggesting the activation of insulin signaling. Moreover, mTOR signaling was also stimulated, as shown by up-regulated tor and s6 expression. This may result from insulin-mediated activation of the mTOR cascade (Han et al., 2015). The mTOR pathway plays a central role in maintaining the blood-testis barrier and providing metabolic support for spermatogenesis (Moreira et al., 2019). It is well established that Arg activates mechanistic target of rapamycin complex 1 (mTORC1) through the lysosomal receptor solute carrier family 38 member 9 (SLC38A9) to initiate cellular growth and protein synthesis (Wang et al., 2015). These findings indicated that dietary Arg promotes testicular maturation and spermatogenesis in male crabs by upregulating the soxe/Dsx-mediated IAG expression, which subsequently activates the downstream IR-mTOR signaling cascade. In summary, a dietary Arg level of 2.65% effectively enhances testicular and vas deferens development by upregulating soxe and dsx expression, stimulating IAG and insulin signaling, and activating the mTOR pathway. These molecular events collectively accelerate sperm production and maturation, thereby contributing to improved reproductive capacity in adult male crabs.

Spermatogenesis is the process by which male crabs produce gametes, involving the mitotic proliferation of spermatogonia, the meiotic division of spermatocytes, and the continuous renewal and differentiation of spermatids (Hao et al., 2021). During this process, abnormal or redundant germ cells are eliminated through apoptosis to maintain sperm quality (Martincic et al., 2001). However, excessive apoptosis can adversely affect sperm development and quality (Yang et al., 2022). The caspase family plays a central role in regulating apoptosis, with caspase-8 and BAX functioning as mitochondrial pro-apoptotic factors, whereas BCL-2 and BIRC7 act as mitochondrial anti-apoptotic proteins. The BAX/BCL-2 ratio serves as a marker for mitochondrial apoptosis, with a higher ratio indicating increased apoptotic activity (Fang et al., 2024; Sarkar et al., 2025). In this study, it was found that caspase 8 gene expression levels were considerably elevated in the 3.05%, 3.43%, and 3.80% Arg groups compared with the control group. In contrast, the expression of bcl2 and birc7 initially increased and then declined. Consistently, the protein level of BAX decreased with rising dietary Arg levels, which corresponding to a reduction in the BAX/BCL-2 ratio. The JNK and p38 MAPK signaling pathways regulate both transcriptional and post-transcriptional activities of members of the BCL-2 family proteins (Yue and López, 2020). The JNK can phosphorylate BCL-2, thereby inhibiting its anti-apoptotic function. In this experiment, supplementation with 2.65% Arg significantly reduced P-JNK/JNK ratio expression. Previous research has shown that Arg supplementation is an effective means of inhibiting apoptosis (Hirfanoglu et al., 2019; Liang et al., 2017; Mazzei et al., 2017). These findings are consistent with the present results, suggesting that an optimal level of dietary Arg reduces testicular apoptosis, whereas excessive Arg induces apoptosis in testicular tissue. Flow cytometry analysis further revealed that the apoptosis in testicular mitochondria was lowest in the 2.27%, 2.65%, and 3.05% Arg groups, but increased as dietary Arg levels continued to rise. Mitochondrial apoptosis is primarily triggered by the excessive accumulation of ROS (Redza-Dutordoir and Averill-Bates, 2016). The fluorescence intensity of mitochondrial ROS in the 2.27%, 2.65%, and 3.05% Arg groups was considerably lower than in the control group, thus validating the antioxidant effect of moderate Arg supplementation. And ROS is closely linked to the antioxidant and immune systems. During the 20-week feeding trial, n-NOS activity initially increased and then declined with rising Arg levels, indicating that Arg supplementation could activate n-NOS activity, consistent with the findings of Sun et al. (2023). The n-NOS is predominantly synthesized in the central nervous system (Maccallini and Amoroso, 2023). Recent studies have highlighted NOS as a key mediator linking Arg metabolism with reproductive regulation in crustaceans. For example, NOS interference disrupts mTORC1 and NO-soluble guanylyl cyclase (sGC)-cyclic guanosine monophosphate (cGMP) signaling in L. vannamei, impairing gonadal development (Zhang et al., 2024b). Moreover, when the dietary Arg content reached 2.65%, the hemolymph NO levels in adult crabs increased significantly, and this upward trend persisted with further increases in Arg concentration. Continuous provision of Arg provided a substrate for n-NOS leading to sustained NO production. Through its uncoupling reaction, n-NOS generates both NO and ROS, thereby mediating the classical NO/cGMP signaling pathway and regulating the redox-sensitive NO/ROS pathway (Kasamatsu et al., 2022). In this study, dietary supplementation with 2.65% Arg enhanced n-NOS expression, boosted antioxidant enzyme activity, and reduced ROS accumulation (Ma et al., 2025). In conclusion, incorporating 2.65% Arg into the diet effectively reduced testicular cell apoptosis and mitochondrial ROS accumulation, thereby improving the antioxidative status and maintaining normal spermatogenic function in male crabs.

Conclusion

The present findings demonstrated that Arg supplementation affects distinct physiological functions across developmental stages. In juvenile male crabs, dietary supplementation with 2.27% Arg significantly enhanced growth performance, antioxidant and immune capacities, promoted hepatopancreatic health, and improved lipid metabolism. In adult male crabs, supplementation with 2.65% Arg markedly stimulated spermidine and creatine synthesis, activated the mTOR signaling pathway, reduced testicular apoptosis, and increased both the GSI and the abundance of mature sperm. Overall, based on the comprehensive assessment of physiological and nutritional indicators, the recommended dietary Arg level for juvenile male swimming crabs is 2.27% dietary Arg (with an estimated optimal requirement of 2.04% based on the linear broken-line model), whereas adult male crabs benefit from 2.65% dietary Arg (with the modelled optimal value also being 2.46%). These findings provide a theoretical basis for establishing precision nutritional strategies tailored to different developmental stages, thereby improving growth efficiency and reproductive performance in aquaculture.

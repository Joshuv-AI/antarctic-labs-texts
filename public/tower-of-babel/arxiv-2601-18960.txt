Quantum capacity analysis of finite-dimensional lossy channels Preprint: APS/123-QED
Sofia Cocciaretto, Vittorio Giovannetti

Abstract
Traditionally, Quantum Information, and Quantum Communication specifically, have been focused on qubit-based architectures. Recent results, however, highlighted that higher dimensional architectures (qudit-based) may present advantages both in terms of communication and computation; a family of channels called Multi-level Amplitude Damping (MAD) channels, which are a possible qudit generalization of the well known Amplitude Damping Channels, is able to model energy decay processes that may happen during signal transmission. In this work, the Quantum Capacity of 4-dimensional MAD’s is studied, relying on a technique for computing it even outside of degradable and antidegradable conditions. We also characterized the complete region of antidegradability and degradability in the parameter space for a generic d-dimensional MAD using both analytical and semi-numerical methods.

Quantum Communication Theory (QCT) expands on the work of Shannon
12

classical information
, to also encompass
quantum information
. Just as we are used to think of
bits

The attention of this work is devoted to a particular class of quantum channels, i.e.

The relevance of this kind of channels lies in their wide range of applicability: in fact, energy decays are commonplace over long distance communications, as can be exemplified by a fiber optic cable which is not completely isolated and can therefore lose a photon to the external environment.
Furthermore, the focus on higher-dimensional channels, i.e. channels built upon qudit transmission, which replace the more traditional qubits, is justified by the fact that recent results highlight how these kinds of architectures may present advantages both in terms of computation
16

4

Existing literature treated MAD channels for

The study of

Multi-level Amplitude Damping (MAD) channels are the higher-dimensional generalization of ADC channels
7
, i.e. they are qudit-to-qudit channels describing an energy decay process, under the assumptions that the energy gaps between the levels of the qudit are all different, see FIG.
I.1

\textrm{MAD}:\sigma(\mathcal{H})\mapsto\sigma(\mathcal{H})

(I.1)

MAD’s were first explored in the
2

Given a
d
-dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, spanned by the basis
\left\{\ket{i}\right\}_{i}
, for
i=0,...,d-1
, the MAD channel has a minimal Kraus representation given by the Kraus operators:

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}K_{ij}\coloneqq\sqrt{\gamma_{ji}}\outerproduct{i}{j}\quad 0\leq i<j\leq d-1,\\
K_{00}\coloneqq\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\sqrt{\gamma_{jj}}\outerproduct{j}{j}.\end{gathered}

The
\gamma_{ji}

I
) describe the probabilities of decay from level
\ket{j}
onto level
\ket{i}
, while
\gamma_{jj}
’s describe the probabilities that level
\ket{j}
will not decay during the transformation; as such, these are real quantities satisfying:

\gamma_{jj}\coloneqq 1-\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\gamma_{ji},

(I.4)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}0\leq\gamma_{ji}\leq 1\quad\forall\,0\leq i<j\leq d-1,\\
0\leq\gamma_{jj}\leq 1\quad\forall\,0\leq j\leq d-1,\\
\gamma_{ji}=0\quad\forall i>j.\end{array}\right.

(I.5)

These quantities, which we name as

in what follows, can be grouped into a so-called

, defined by:

\Gamma\coloneqq\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\sum_{i=0}^{j}\gamma_{ji}\outerproduct{j}{i},

(I.6)

or, equivalently
1
1

1

This form can be more useful for some derivations.
:

\Gamma\coloneqq\mathds{1}_{d}+\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\gamma_{ji}\outerproduct{j}{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\gamma_{ji}\outerproduct{j}{j},

(I.7)

where

There is a one-to-one relation between a specific MAD channel and its transition matrix
\Gamma
, so that it is possible to identify a MAD channel by

\Phi_{\Gamma}\sim\Gamma.

(I.8)

Finally, given an input

\Phi_{\Gamma}(\rho)=K_{00}\rho K_{00}^{\dagger}+\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}K_{ij}\rho K_{ij}^{\dagger}.

(I.9)

Which, in terms of transition probabilities, translates to:

\displaystyle\Phi_{\Gamma}(\rho)=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{d-1}\sum_{n=0}^{d-1}\sqrt{\gamma_{mm}\gamma_{nn}}\rho_{mn}\outerproduct{m}{n}

(I.10)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\gamma_{ji}\rho_{ii}\outerproduct{i}{i}.

Complementary channels express how information is lost to the external environment during a noisy quantum evolution (refer to
14

\begin{array}[]{ll}\tilde{\Phi}_{\Gamma}(\rho)=&\tr\left(K_{00}\rho K^{\dagger}_{00}\right)\outerproduct{0,0}{0,0}\\

For any given
d

\Gamma
, we could check whether that channel is degradable (see Appendix

\Lambda_{\Gamma}\coloneqq\tilde{\Phi}_{\Gamma}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\Gamma}.

(V.1)

By construction, this map is linear and trace-preserving, however it is not guaranteed to be completely positive. We can check that numerically with relative ease by computing the eigenvalues of its Choi matrix (see
3
,
10
).
The problem of finding the complete analitycal degradability conditions on the
\gamma_{ji}
’s is more complicated. The difficulty arises from the fact that the eigenvalue problem involves finding the roots of a polynomial whose degree increases with the dimensionality of the channel. We have found a working recipe that allows us to somewhat simplify the problem and through which the complete degradability region for
4
-dimensional MAD channels was found.

V.1
Recipe for finding the degradability conditions

Here we lay out the process through which it is possible to simplify the eigenvalue problem that needs to be solved for computing the degradabiliity region in the parameter space (i.e., the convex space generated by all the possible configurations of the
\gamma_{ji}
) for MAD channels.

1.

The first step consists in a heuricstic assumption: if we want the degrading map to be a quantum channel, since this map sends the output state of the laboratory system into the output state of the environment, we would expect density matrix of the latter to have rank not greater than that of the former:

{\rank\left(\Phi_{\Gamma}(\rho)\right)\geq\rank\left(\tilde{\Phi}_{\Gamma}(\rho)\right)\hskip 9.24994pt\forall\rho.}

(V.2)

As we will see, this condition excludes some valid configurations of degradable MAD’s; however, these can be recovered by extending the configurations found under the assumption (
V.2
).

2.

The easiest way to satisfy (
V.2
) consists in "turning off" some of the decays: in fact, the dimensionality of the complementary channel’s output is equal to the cardinality of the minimal Kraus set of the original channel (see
8
). Therefore, we label all possible
d
-dimensional MAD’s with at most
d-1
non-zero
\gamma_{ji}
’s.

3.

For each of the possible classes of MAD’s found in the previous step, we compute the eigenvalues of the corresponding degrading maps, resulting in the correct analytical degradability conditions for those classes.

4.

Now we make use of (
A.3
); starting from the classes found earlier, we may be tempted to "turn on" other decays, purposefully violating (
V.2
). Can we find other degradable configurations by doing so? The answer is yes, as long as the new channel, obtained by turning on the extra decay from the degradable configurations, does not admit decompositions where one of the composing channels is not degradable. We have found that for MAD’s, this reduces to excluding all the channels which present the ladder-like decay structure reported in FIG.
V.1
. In the case of
4
-dimensional MAD’s we have found the only possible
4
-decay transition (that violates (
V.2
)) which admits degradability under certain conditions for the
\gamma_{ji}
’s.

VI
Quantum capacity computation for MAD’s: procedure

The quantum capacity
Q
of a quantum channel represents the rate for reliably transmitting quantum
information
6
. For of degradable channels,
Q
is relatively easy to find (
5
,
13
), as they reduce to the coherent information of the channels maximized over input states:

\Psi\textrm{ is degradable}\Rightarrow Q\left(\Psi\right)=\max_{\rho\in\sigma\left(\mathcal{H}\right)}I_{c}\left(\rho,\Psi\right).

(VI.1)

Making use of the concavity of the quantum capacity over input states for degradable channels (see
17
) and the fact that, as was shown in
2
, MAD channels are covariant under unitary transformations which are diagonal in the computational basis, we can prove that the maximization over input states in (
VI.1
) reduces to a maximization over diagonal input states in the computational basis:

\Phi_{\Gamma}\textrm{ is degradable}\Rightarrow Q\left(\Phi_{\Gamma}\right)=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\in\sigma\left(\mathcal{H}\right)\\
\rho\textrm{ diagonal}\end{subarray}}I_{c}\left(\rho,\Phi_{\Gamma}\right).

(VI.2)

For antidegradable channels, we already know that
Q=0
. In the case of MAD’s once the degradability region is found, in that region we can compute the quantum capacity, the question is then: can we do better? In
2
, the authors showcased a procedure through which they were able to compute
Q
for some configurations of
3
-dimensional MAD channels which were neither degradable nor antidegradable. We did the same for
4
-dimensional MAD channels, slightly generalizing the procedure which is summarized below:

•

Find degradability conditions as seen in Section
V.1
.

•

Compute quantum capacity in degradable conditions using (
VI.2
).

•

Starting from a degradability condition, increase the non-zero decay probabilities so that one of the
\gamma_{jj}
’s becomes
0
, then compute the quantum capacity of the resulting channel, mapping the problem to the one outlined in Section
A.7
using (
A.1
), where the unitaries are level-swap unitaries.

•

If the quantum capacity obtained in the last point is equal to the one at the corresponding border of the degradability condition (e.g. if we computed the capacity at
\gamma_{jj}=0
, the corresponding border would be the one where the degradability conditions on the
\gamma_{ji}
’s are saturated), we make use of the monotonicity properties for the capacity (see Section
A.8
) to conclude that the region between the two borders would have the same capacity as the one computed at one of the borders.

•

We iterate the last three points until possible.

In Section
VI.1
, we provide an example to clarify the process. Note that this algorithm exploits computations of the capacity on lower-dimensional MAD’s, meaning that the result found e.g. for
4
-dimensional MAD’s would also be useful for the capacity computations of
5,6,...
-dimensional MAD’s.

VII
Conclusions

The results of this work expanded on the previous work
2
on MAD’s; we found useful general properties that aided us in the actual computation of the quantum (and, consequently, private classical) capacity of the channels (
VI
,
VI.1
). All the numerical results hinge on finding the degradability region (
V
) in parameter space of MAD’s and comparing the value of the capacity at the external border of the degradability region against the one at the border of the parameter space, finding out that they coincide in specific instances. We were also able to compute the quantum capacity on new regions of the parameter space of
3
-dimensional MAD’s not yet explored (see later,
VII.1.1
). Finally, we formulate a conjecture on the optimal encoding of MAD’s when only some of the antidegradability conditions are satisfied (see later,
VII.1
).

Appendix A.1
Unitary equivalence of channels

Consider two quantum channels
\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2}
which are connected by a unitary channel
\mathcal{U}
:

\mathcal{U}\circ\Phi_{1}=\Phi_{2}\circ\mathcal{U};

(A.1.1)

it can be shown that (see
8
), under this condition, any capacity functional
\mathfrak{C}
of
\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2}
is the same:

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}\exists\,\mathcal{U}\textrm{ unitary channel s.t.}:\mathcal{U}\circ\Phi_{1}=\Phi_{2}\circ\mathcal{U}\\
\Downarrow\\
\mathfrak{C}\left[\Phi_{1}\right]=\mathfrak{C}\left[\Phi_{2}\right].\end{gathered}

In the case of MAD channels, this is particularly useful when we consider the unitary transformation to be a level swap unitary:

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}U_{mn}\coloneqq\mathds{1}_{d}-\outerproduct{m}{m}-\outerproduct{n}{n}+\outerproduct{m}{n}+\outerproduct{n}{m}\quad m,n\in\{0,...,d-1\},\\
\mathcal{U}_{mn}\left(\bullet\right)\coloneqq U_{mn}\bullet U_{mn},\end{gathered}

which swaps the levels
\ket{m},\ket{n}
. Through this tranformation, we can prove that many MAD’s which are seemingly different but whose decay probabilities have the same numerical values (e.g. single decay MAD’s) actually have the same capacity. Channels belonging to the same "classes" are connected through swap unitaries, so their capacity is only determined by the values of their decay rates
\gamma_{ji}
, not by the specific expression of the class.

Consider the composition in (

(A.4.2)

Expanding the right-hand side

\begin{split}\Phi_{\Gamma^{\prime\prime}}\circ\Phi_{\Gamma^{\prime}}(\rho)=&\sum_{i=0}^{l-1}\sum_{l=1}^{j-1}\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\gamma^{\prime\prime}_{li}\gamma^{\prime}_{jl}\rho_{jj}\outerproduct{i}{i}+\\

\displaystyle\Phi_{\Gamma^{\prime\prime}}\circ\Phi_{\Gamma^{\prime}}(\rho)=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\left(\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}\gamma^{\prime\prime}_{li}\gamma^{\prime}_{jl}\right)\rho_{jj}\outerproduct{i}{i}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\sqrt{\gamma^{\prime\prime}_{ii}\gamma^{\prime}_{ii}\gamma^{\prime\prime}_{jj}\gamma^{\prime}_{jj}}\rho_{ij}\outerproduct{i}{j}.

Then, the composition laws can be fixed:

\Phi_{\Gamma}=\Phi_{\Gamma^{\prime\prime}}\circ\Phi_{\Gamma^{\prime}}\Rightarrow\gamma_{ji}=\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}\gamma^{\prime}_{jl}\gamma^{\prime\prime}_{li}\quad\forall i\leq j,

Knowing the inverse map of a generic MAD channel turned out to be very useful in our computations. In this section, we report its derivation.

A.6.1
Inverse map of ADC’s

The inverse map of an ADC provides a valuable guide for the derivation of the inverse MAD map. Given the ADC:

A_{\gamma}:\sigma\left(\mathcal{H}_{2}\right)\mapsto\sigma\left(\mathcal{H}_{2}\right),

(A.6.1)

we denote its inverse map:

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}A_{\gamma}^{-1}:\sigma\left(\mathcal{H}_{2}\right)\mapsto\sigma\left(\mathcal{H}_{2}\right),\\
A_{\gamma}\circ A_{\gamma}^{-1}(\rho)=\rho.\end{gathered}

The map
A_{\gamma}^{-1}
, while trace preserving and linear, is not expected to be completely positive, therefore it is likely not a quantum channel. In fact, it could be cast in a "pseudo-Kraus" representation:

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}A^{-1}_{\gamma}(\theta)=\tilde{K}_{0}(\gamma)\theta\tilde{K}_{0}(\gamma)^{\dagger}-\tilde{K}_{1}(\gamma)\theta\tilde{K}_{1}(\gamma)^{\dagger},\\
\,\\
\tilde{K}_{0}(\gamma)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\gamma}}\end{array}\right)\quad\tilde{K}_{1}(\gamma)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}}\\
0&0\end{array}\right).\end{gathered}

Notice that, while
\tilde{K}_{0}(\gamma)
and
\tilde{K}_{1}(\gamma)
form a Kraus set, the "
-
" sign in (
A.6.1
) implies that
A^{-1}_{\gamma}
is
not
necessarily a quantum channel, as it is not written in the Kraus representation. One could also write (
A.6.1
) in matrix form:

A^{-1}_{\gamma}(\theta)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\theta_{00}-\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\theta_{11}&\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\gamma}}\theta_{01}\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\gamma}}\theta^{*}_{01}&\frac{1}{(1-\gamma)}\theta_{11}\end{array}\right).

(A.6.10)

The map defined in (
A.6.1
) is also the left-inverse of
A_{\gamma}
, which can be verified by direct computation.
We can see that, when
\gamma=1
, the inverse map is not well defined, so this construction only works for
0\leq\gamma<1

A.6.2
Inverse of single decay MAD channels

Consider the MAD channel
\Phi_{\Xi_{k}^{(n)}}
, where
\Xi_{k}^{(n)}
is defined in (
A.5
) and the associated transition probability can be found in (
A.5.7
). This is a single decay MAD channel, which acts on the subspace spanned by
\ket{k}
and
\ket{n}
as an ADC with the same transition probability. Therefore, one might be tempted to define the right-inverse of
\Phi_{\Xi_{k}^{(n)}}
as the embedding onto
\mathcal{H}_{d}
of the right-inverse of an ADC.
Let
\Phi^{-1}_{\Xi_{k}^{(n)}}
be the right inverse of
\Phi_{\Xi_{k}^{(n)}}
. Given
\theta\in\sigma\left(\mathrm{span}\left\{\ket{k},\ket{n}\right\}\right)
,
\Phi^{-1}_{\Xi_{k}^{(n)}}
needs to satisfy:

\Phi^{-1}_{\Xi_{k}^{(n)}}\left(\theta\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\theta_{nn}-\gamma^{(n+1)}_{kn}\left(1-\gamma^{(n+1)}_{kn}\right)^{-1}\theta_{kk}&\left(1-\gamma^{(n+1)}_{kn}\right)^{-1/2}\theta_{nk}\\
\left(1-\gamma^{(n+1)}_{kn}\right)^{-1/2}\theta^{*}_{nk}&\left(1-\gamma^{(n+1)}_{kn}\right)^{-1}\theta_{kk}\end{array}\right).

(A.6.11)

This can be achieved by defining
\Phi^{-1}_{\Xi_{k}^{(n)}}
as:

\displaystyle\Phi^{(-1)}_{\Xi_{k}^{(n)}}(\rho)

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle\tilde{K}_{0}\left(\Xi_{k}^{(n)}\right)\rho\tilde{K}_{0}\left(\Xi_{k}^{(n)}\right)^{\dagger}

\displaystyle-\tilde{K}_{1}\left(\Xi_{k}^{(n)}\right)\rho\tilde{K}_{1}\left(\Xi_{k}^{(n)}\right)^{\dagger},

\displaystyle\tilde{K}_{0}\left(\Xi_{k}^{(n)}\right)

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle\mathds{1}_{d}-\left(1-\left(1-\gamma^{(n+1)}_{kn}\right)^{-1/2}\right)\outerproduct{k}{k},

\displaystyle\tilde{K}_{1}\left(\Xi_{k}^{(n)}\right)

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\gamma^{(n+1)}_{kn}}{1-\gamma^{(n+1)}_{kn}}\right)^{1/2}\outerproduct{n}{k},

(A.6.12)

where
\rho\in\sigma\left(\mathcal{H}_{d}\right)
. It is possible to show, by direct computation, that:

\Phi_{\Xi_{k}^{(n)}}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\Xi_{k}^{(n)}}\left(\rho\right)=\rho\quad\forall\rho\in\sigma\left(\mathcal{H}_{d}\right).

(A.6.13)

Note that the map
\Phi^{-1}_{\Xi_{k}^{(n)}}
is linear and trace preserving and is also the left-inverse of
\Phi_{\Xi_{k}^{(n)}}
.
Also notice that, when inverting
\Phi_{\Xi_{k}^{(0)}}
, since the associated decay probability is (see (
A.5.7
)):

\gamma_{k0}^{(1)}=\frac{\gamma_{k0}}{1-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\gamma_{ki}}=\frac{\gamma_{k0}}{\gamma_{k0}+\gamma_{kk}},

(A.6.14)

if
\gamma_{kk}=0
we would get a divergence in the corresponding inverse map, given by:

\left(1-\gamma_{k0}^{(1)}\right)^{-1}=1+\frac{\gamma_{k0}}{\gamma_{kk}},

(A.6.15)

meaning that, if
\exists k:\gamma_{kk}=0
, the inverse map is not well defined.

A.6.3
Inverse map as composition of inverse maps of single decays

The importance of (
A.6.13
) lies in the fact that it can be composed to generate the right-inverse map of a general MAD channel. In fact, recall that in (
II.9
) it was shown that a MAD channel can be seen as a composition of single decay MAD channels. Then, by "inverting" those single decay transitions one by one, the resulting channel must be the identity channel. This line of reasoning results in the definition:

\Phi_{\Gamma}^{-1}=\bigodot_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
\rightarrow\end{subarray}}^{d-1}\left(\Phi^{-1}_{\Xi_{k}^{(k-1)}}\circ...\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\Xi_{k}^{(0)}}\right)

(A.6.16)

which, by construction, satisfies:

\Phi_{\Gamma}\circ\Phi_{\Gamma}^{-1}=\mathrm{Id}.

(A.6.17)

Since each of the
\Phi^{-1}_{\Xi_{k}^{(n)}}
’s is linear and trace preserving, and a generic composition of such maps holds those same properties, then
\Phi_{\Gamma}^{-1}
must also be linear and trace preserving.
Notice, finally, that
\Phi_{\Gamma}^{-1}
, again, by construction, is also the left inverse of
\Phi_{\Gamma}
.

Appendix A.7
Complete damping from upper level

Consider the
d
-dimensional MAD channel
\Phi_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{CD(d-1)}}}
identified by the transition matrix:

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\mathrm{CD(d-1)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathds{1}_{d}+\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\gamma_{ji}\outerproduct{j}{i}

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\gamma_{ji}\outerproduct{j}{j},

where
\gamma_{d-1,d-1}=1-\sum_{j=0}^{d-2}\gamma_{d-1,j}=0
.
The quantum capacity of this channel can be shown to be equivalent to the quantum capacity of a
d-1
-dimensional MAD channel
\Phi^{(d-1)}_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{MAD(d-1)}}}
, whose transition matrix is:

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\mathrm{MAD(d-1)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathds{1}_{d-1}+\sum_{j=1}^{d-2}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\gamma_{ji}\outerproduct{j}{i}

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=1}^{d-2}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\gamma_{ji}\outerproduct{j}{j}.

In order to reach this result, one needs to find coinciding upper and lower bounds on
Q\left(\Phi_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{CD(d-1)}}}\right)
.

A.7.1
Lower bound

The lower bound on
Q\left(\Phi_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{CD(d-1)}}}\right)
is trivial: if one were to encode information only on the subspace spanned by
\left\{\ket{0},...,\ket{d-2}\right\}
, the channel
\Phi_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{CD(d-1)}}}
would be equivalent to
\Phi^{(d-1)}_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{MAD(d-1)}}}
; of course, this choice of encoding is not guaranteed to be optimal, therefore:

Q\left(\Phi_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{CD(d-1)}}}\right)\geq Q\left(\Phi^{(d-1)}_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{MAD(d-1)}}}\right).

(A.7.3)

A.7.2
Upper bound

The upper bound on
Q\left(\Phi_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{CD(d-1)}}}\right)
can be obtained using the pipeline inequalities
8
:

\mathfrak{C}\left[\Psi_{2}\circ\Psi_{1}\right]\leq\min\left\{\mathfrak{C}\left[\Psi_{1}\right],\mathfrak{C}\left[\Psi_{2}\right]\right\},

(A.7.4)

where
\mathfrak{C}
is a capacity functional, and a result on the capacity of Direct Sum (DS) channels (see
1
):

Q\left(\Phi^{DS}_{\mathrm{CC}}\right)=\max\left\{Q\left(\Phi_{\mathrm{AA}}\right),Q\left(\Phi_{\mathrm{BB}}\right)\right\}\leq Q\left(\Phi_{\mathrm{CC}}\right).

(A.7.5)

Consider the DS channel:

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}\Phi^{DS}_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{MAD(d-1)}}}(\theta)\coloneqq\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}\Phi^{(d-1)}_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{MAD(d-1)}}}\left(p\theta^{(d-1)}\right)&0\\
\hline\cr 0&1-p\end{array}\right],\\
{\theta}\coloneqq\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}p\theta^{(d-1)}&\theta_{j,d-1}\\
\hline\cr\theta_{d-1,j}&1-p\end{array}\right],\\
\theta\in\sigma\left(\mathcal{H}_{d}\right),\theta^{(d-1)}\in\sigma\left(\mathcal{H}_{d-1}\right),\\
0\leq p\leq 1,\end{gathered}

and the Level Erasure channel:

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}LE_{\Gamma_{CD(d-1)}}(\rho)\coloneqq\sum_{i\neq j}^{d-2}\rho_{ij}\outerproduct{i}{j}+\sum_{i=0}^{d-2}\gamma_{d-1,i}\rho_{d-1,d-1}\outerproduct{i}{i},\\
\rho\coloneqq\sum_{i,j=0}^{d-1}\rho_{ij}\outerproduct{i}{j},\\
\rho_{ij}=\rho_{ji}^{*},\\
\end{gathered}

where the condition found in (
A.7
) holds. The composition of these two channel is equal to
\Phi_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{CD(d-1)}}}
:

\Phi_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{CD(d-1)}}}=LE_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{CD(d-1)}}}\circ\Phi^{DS}_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{MAD(d-1)}}}.

(A.7.16)

The quantum capacity of
\Phi^{DS}_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{MAD(d-1)}}}
is given in (
A.7.5
), which in this case translates to:

Q\left(\Phi^{DS}_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{MAD(d-1)}}}\right)=Q\left(\Phi^{(d-1)}_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{MAD(d-1)}}}\right).

(A.7.17)

The pipeline inequalities (
A.7.4
) ensure that (
A.7.17
) is an upper bound for
Q\left(\Phi_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{CD(d-1)}}}\right)
:

Q\left(\Phi_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{CD(d-1)}}}\right)\leq Q\left(\Phi^{(d-1)}_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{MAD(d-1)}}}\right).

(A.7.18)

Combining (
A.7.3
) and (
A.7.18
) leads to the conclusion:

Q\left(\Phi_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{CD(d-1)}}}\right)=Q\left(\Phi^{(d-1)}_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{MAD(d-1)}}}\right).

(A.7.19)

Appendix A.8
Monotonicity of quantum capacity for MAD’s

In
2

A.7.4
), the authors were able to show that the capacities of
3
-dimensional MAD channels are monotonous in their parameters.
In order to generalize this property of monotonicity of the capacity functionals to the
d
-dimensional case, one needs to consider two transition matrices,
\Gamma,\Gamma^{\prime}
, whose elements
\gamma_{ji},\gamma^{\prime}_{ji}
differ only for a single pair of indices

\gamma^{\prime}_{j_{0},i_{0}}\geq\gamma_{j_{0},i_{0}}.

\Lambda_{L}\circ\Phi_{\Gamma}\circ\Lambda_{R}=\Phi_{\Gamma^{\prime}},

(A.8.2)

we could conclude that the capacities MAD channels are non increasing in the parameter
\gamma^{\prime}_{j_{0},i_{0}}\geq\gamma_{j_{0},i_{0}}
.
Finding
\Lambda_{L},\Lambda_{R}

\Lambda_{L},\Lambda_{R}

and

C_{AB_{1}(B_{2})}
in (
A.9.4
), the only thing left to be proven is that
\tau_{AB_{1}B_{2}}
is actually a state, which requires checking its positivity.

Looking at (
A.9.5
), it is possible to recognize that it can be cast in a diagonal block matrix: in fact, many of its diagonal elements are already in an eigenspace of the matrix, namely:

\gamma_{ji}\ket{j}_{A}\bra{j}\,\ket{i}_{B_{1}}\bra{i}\,\ket{i}_{B_{2}}\bra{i}

(A.9.10)

p^{(j)}_{i}(\gamma_{j0}-\gamma_{jj})\ket{j}_{A}\bra{j}\,\ket{0}_{B_{1}}\bra{0}\,\ket{i}_{B_{2}}\bra{i},

(A.9.11)

p^{(j)}_{i}(\gamma_{j0}-\gamma_{jj})\ket{j}_{A}\bra{j}\,\ket{i}_{B_{1}}\bra{i}\,\ket{0}_{B_{2}}\bra{0},

(A.9.12)

\left(\gamma_{ji}-p^{(j)}_{i}(\gamma_{j0}-\gamma_{jj})\right)\ket{j}_{A}\bra{j}\,\ket{i}_{B_{1}}\bra{i}\,\ket{i}_{B_{2}}\bra{i},

(A.9.13)

\forall j=1,...,d-1
and
\forall i=0,...,j-1
. These elements, when grouped together through a unitary permutation, form the first diagonal block of
\tau_{AB_{1}B_{2}}
, which we will call
D
. The other block will be called
A
, which in matrix form looks like:

A=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}1&\sqrt{\gamma_{11}}&\sqrt{\gamma_{11}}&\sqrt{\gamma_{22}}&\sqrt{\gamma_{22}}&...\\
\sqrt{\gamma_{11}}&\gamma_{11}&\gamma_{11}&\sqrt{\gamma_{11}\gamma_{22}}&\sqrt{\gamma_{11}\gamma_{22}}&...\\
\sqrt{\gamma_{11}}&\gamma_{11}&\gamma_{11}&\sqrt{\gamma_{11}\gamma_{22}}&\sqrt{\gamma_{11}\gamma_{22}}&...\\
\sqrt{\gamma_{22}}&\sqrt{\gamma_{11}\gamma_{22}}&\sqrt{\gamma_{11}\gamma_{22}}&\gamma_{22}&\gamma_{22}&...\\
\sqrt{\gamma_{22}}&\sqrt{\gamma_{11}\gamma_{22}}&\sqrt{\gamma_{11}\gamma_{22}}&\gamma_{22}&\gamma_{22}&...\\
...&...&...&...&...&\ddots\end{array}\right),

(A.9.14)

\tau_{AB_{1}B_{2}}\sim\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A&0\\
0&D\end{array}\right).

(A.9.15)

Positivity of
D

The matrix elements corresponding to (
A.9.10
) are already non-negative, while those corresponding to (
A.9.11
) and (
A.9.12
) fix the positivity conditions for
D
.

\gamma_{j0}-\gamma_{jj}\geq 0\quad\forall j=1,...,d-1.

(A.9.16)

The conditions (
A.9.16
) are sufficient for the positivity of (
A.9.13
), we just need to select the right probability distributions. Is it always possible to do so? The condition that needs to be satisfied is:

p_{i}^{(j)}\leq\frac{\gamma_{ji}}{\gamma_{j0}-\gamma_{jj}}\qquad i<j;

(A.9.17)

\gamma_{ji}
is already a probability distribution for
i=0,1,...,j
and under our conditions (
A.9.16
),
\frac{\gamma_{ji}}{\gamma_{j0}-\gamma_{jj}}\geq 0
; furthermore, since
\gamma_{j0}-\gamma_{jj}\leq 1-\gamma_{jj}\leq 1
:

\frac{\gamma_{ji}}{\gamma_{j0}-\gamma_{jj}}\geq\gamma_{ji}\quad\forall i<j,

(A.9.18)

therefore, the
p_{i}^{(j)}
’s need to be smaller than elements of another probability distribution which have been scaled up; this is obviously always possible. The only thing left to be verified is that the
p_{i}^{(j)}
’s can sum up to
1
:

\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}p_{i}^{(j)}\leq\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\frac{\gamma_{ji}}{\gamma_{j0}-\gamma_{jj}}=\frac{1-\gamma_{jj}}{\gamma_{j0}-\gamma_{jj}}.

(A.9.19)

The right-hand side is bigger than
1
, so the conditions (
A.9.16
) allow for the selection of a probability distribution
p_{i}^{(j)}
, meaning that the block
D
is positive.

Positivity of
A

Proving the positivity of
A
requires making use of Sylvester’s criterion: if (and only if) all the
principal minors
of
A
are non-negative, then
A\geq 0
. Looking at
A
in (
A.9.14
), it is possible to notice that many of the principal minors will be
0
; in fact, every time the submatrix obtained from
A
by "cutting" some rows (and corresponding columns) contains two copies of the same row, its determinant is automatically
0
. This means that in order to prove the positivity of
A
, we only need to prove the positivity of just one of its submatrices, specifically:

\tilde{A}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}1&\sqrt{\gamma_{11}}&\sqrt{\gamma_{22}}&...\\
\sqrt{\gamma_{11}}&\gamma_{11}&\sqrt{\gamma_{11}\gamma_{22}}&...\\
\sqrt{\gamma_{22}}&\sqrt{\gamma_{11}\gamma_{22}}&\gamma_{22}&...\\
...&...&...&\ddots\end{array}\right).

(A.9.20)

Computing the expectation value of
\tilde{A}
on a generic pure state
\ket{\psi}=\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}\ket{i}
in the relevant
d
-dimensional subspace of the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B_{1}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B_{2}}
leads to:

\begin{split}\expectationvalue{\tilde{A}}{\psi}&=\bra{\psi}\left(\left(\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}\sqrt{\gamma_{ii}}\alpha_{i}\right)\ket{0}+\left(\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}\sqrt{\gamma_{ii}}\alpha_{i}\right)\sqrt{\gamma_{11}}\ket{1}+...\right)\\
&=\alpha_{0}^{*}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}\sqrt{\gamma_{ii}}\alpha_{i}\right)+\alpha_{1}^{*}\sqrt{\gamma_{11}}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}\sqrt{\gamma_{ii}}\alpha_{i}\right)+...\\
&=\left|\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}\sqrt{\gamma_{ii}}\alpha_{i}\right|^{2}\geq 0\quad\forall\ket{\psi}.\end{split}

(A.9.21)

Then
\tilde{A}\geq 0
, which means
A\geq 0
. Then, the sufficient conditions for the antidegradability of a
d
-dimensional MAD channel are just (
A.9.16
).

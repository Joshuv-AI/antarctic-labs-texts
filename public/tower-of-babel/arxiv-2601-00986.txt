A Simple Weak Galerkin Finite Element Method for Convection-Diffusion-Reaction Equations on Nonconvex Polytopal Meshes Thanks: † {\dagger} Corresponding author.
Chunmei Wang † {\dagger}, Shangyou Zhang

Abstract
. This article introduces a simple weak Galerkin (WG) finite element method for solving convection-diffusion-reaction equation. The proposed method offers significant flexibility by supporting discontinuous approximating functions on general nonconvex polytopal meshes. We establish rigorous error estimates within a suitable norm. Finally, numerical experiments are presented to validate the theoretical convergence rates and demonstrate the computational efficiency of the approach.

1.
Introduction

The objective of this paper is to develop a simple weak Galerkin (WG) finite element method for convection-diffusion-reaction equations. This numerical framework is designed for applicability to general partitions consisting of both convex and non-convex polytopal meshes. For clarity of exposition, we focus on the steady-state convection-diffusion-reaction problem, which seeks an unknown scalar function
u
such that:

(1.1)

\begin{split}-\rho\Delta u+\nabla\cdot(\mathbf{b}u)+cu=&f\quad\text{in }\Omega,\\
u=&0\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{split}

where
\rho>0
represents the diffusion coefficient and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d=2,3
) is a bounded polygonal or polyhedral domain with boundary
\partial\Omega
. To ensure the well-posedness of the problem, we assume
\mathbf{b},c,f
possess sufficient regularity. Specifically, we assume
\mathbf{b}\in[W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)]^{d}
and the coercivity condition
c+\frac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot\mathbf{b}\geq c_{0}>0
holds for a constant
c_{0}
(cf.
[
28
]
).

The corresponding variational formulation of (
1.1
) is to find
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
such that:

(1.2)

(\rho\nabla u,\nabla v)+(\nabla\cdot(\mathbf{b}u),v)+(cu,v)=(f,v),\quad\forall v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),

where
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
denotes the standard Sobolev space of functions with vanishing trace on
\partial\Omega
.

It is well established that the linear elliptic equation (
1.1
) presents significant numerical challenges in the singularly perturbed regime, characterized by
0<\rho\ll 1
. In this context, the solution typically exhibits boundary or interior layers, narrow regions where the solution or its gradients undergo rapid variations. Conventional numerical schemes often suffer from spurious oscillations and fail to yield accurate approximations unless the computational mesh is refined to the scale of the singular perturbation parameter.

To mitigate these issues, various stabilization strategies have been proposed, which may be broadly categorized into fitted mesh methods and fitted operator methods. Historical efforts in mesh optimization include the work of Bakhvalov
[
3
]
, while the piecewise-equidistant meshes introduced by Shishkin
[
29
]
remain a cornerstone of singular perturbation analysis. Furthermore, adaptive refinement techniques have been extensively utilized over the past several decades
[
2
,
6
]
to resolve localized sharp features
[
27
]
.

Recent decades have witnessed the rapid development of numerical methods utilizing discontinuous discrete spaces, most notably the discontinuous Galerkin (DG) and weak Galerkin (WG) methods. In the context of convection-dominated flows, DG methods leverage natural upwinding to provide inherent stabilization
[
15
,
28
]
; see also
[
1
,
4
,
5
,
12
,
13
,
14
]
. The WG method represents a powerful extension of this paradigm, approximating differential operators through a framework that parallels the theory of distributions for piecewise polynomials. By employing specifically designed stabilizers, the WG method relaxes the global regularity requirements of traditional finite element methods. The efficacy of WG has been demonstrated across a broad spectrum of partial differential equations (PDEs)
[

,

,

,

,

.

A significant advancement within this field is the Primal-Dual Weak Galerkin (PDWG) method, which addresses problems that are otherwise intractable for classical techniques
[
23
,
24
,
7
,
8
,

,

,

. The PDWG approach formulates the numerical solution as a constrained minimization of a functional, where the constraints represent the weak form of the PDE. This results in an Euler-Lagrange system coupling a primal variable with a dual variable (Lagrange multiplier), yielding a symmetric saddle-point scheme. PDWG methods have been specifically extended to convection-diffusion equations in
[
8
,
51
,
11
]
.

In the present work, we introduce a simple WG formulation that is applicable to general convex and non-convex polytopal meshes. The fundamental analytical component of this method is the utilization of bubble functions. While this necessitates higher-degree polynomials for the computation of the discrete weak gradient and discrete weak divergence, the scheme preserves the global sparsity and dimensions of the resulting stiffness matrix.

The remainder of this paper is structured as follows. Section 2 provides a concise review of the weak gradient and weak divergence operators. Section 3 introduces the simple WG scheme for the convection-diffusion-reaction equation. The existence and uniqueness of the discrete solution are established in Section 4. Section 5 is devoted to the derivation of the error equation. The error estimates in the energy norm are presented in Section 6. Finally, numerical experiments are presented in Section 7 to validate the theoretical findings and demonstrate the performance of the proposed method.

2.
Discrete Weak Gradient and Discrete Weak Divergence

In this section, we provide a concise overview of the weak gradient and weak divergence operators, as well as their corresponding discrete representations, following the framework established in
[
48
]
.

Let
T
be a polytopal element with boundary
\partial T
. A weak function on
T
is defined as a doublet
v=\{v_{0},v_{b}\}
, where
v_{0}\in L^{2}(T)
and
v_{b}\in L^{2}(\partial T)
. Here, the components
v_{0}
and
v_{b}
represent the values of the function in the interior and on the boundary of
T
, respectively. In general,
v_{b}
is assumed to be independent of the trace of
v_{0}
on
\partial T
. A special case arises when
v_{b}=v_{0}|_{\partial T}
, in which case the weak function
v
is uniquely determined by
v_{0}
and may be denoted simply as
v=v_{0}
.

Let
W(T)
denote the space of weak functions on
T
, defined as:

(2.1)

W(T)=\{v=\{v_{0},v_{b}\}:v_{0}\in L^{2}(T),v_{b}\in L^{2}(\partial T)\}.

The weak gradient, denoted by
\nabla_{w}
, is a linear operator mapping
W(T)
to the dual space of
[H^{1}(T)]^{d}
. For any
v\in W(T)
, the weak gradient
\nabla_{w}v
is defined as a bounded linear functional such that:

(\nabla_{w}v,{\bm{\varphi}})_{T}=-(v_{0},\nabla\cdot{\bm{\varphi}})_{T}+\langle v_{b},{\bm{\varphi}}\cdot\mathbf{n}\rangle_{\partial T},\quad\forall{\bm{\varphi}}\in[H^{1}(T)]^{d},

where
\mathbf{n}
is the unit outward normal vector to
\partial T
.

Analogously, the weak divergence operator
\nabla_{w}\cdot(\mathbf{b}v)
is defined as a linear operator from
W(T)
to the dual space of
H^{1}(T)
. For any
v\in W(T)
, the weak divergence
\nabla_{w}\cdot(\mathbf{b}v)
is the bounded linear functional satisfying:

(\nabla_{w}\cdot(\mathbf{b}v),w)_{T}=-(\mathbf{b}v_{0},\nabla w)_{T}+\langle(\mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{n})v_{b},w\rangle_{\partial T},\quad\forall w\in H^{1}(T).

For any non-negative integer
r
, let
P_{r}(T)
be the space of polynomials of degree at most
r
on
T
. The discrete weak gradient, denoted by
\nabla_{w,r,T}
, is a linear operator from
W(T)
to
[P_{r}(T)]^{d}
. For any
v\in W(T)
,
\nabla_{w,r,T}v
is the unique polynomial vector in
[P_{r}(T)]^{d}
satisfying:

(2.2)

(\nabla_{w,r,T}v,{\bm{\varphi}})_{T}=-(v_{0},\nabla\cdot{\bm{\varphi}})_{T}+\langle v_{b},{\bm{\varphi}}\cdot\mathbf{n}\rangle_{\partial T},\quad\forall{\bm{\varphi}}\in[P_{r}(T)]^{d}.

For a sufficiently smooth function
v_{0}\in H^{1}(T)
, applying the standard integration by parts formula to the first term on the right-hand side of (
2.2
) yields:

(2.3)

(\nabla_{w,r,T}v,{\bm{\varphi}})_{T}=(\nabla v_{0},{\bm{\varphi}})_{T}+\langle v_{b}-v_{0},{\bm{\varphi}}\cdot\mathbf{n}\rangle_{\partial T},\quad\forall{\bm{\varphi}}\in[P_{r}(T)]^{d}.

The discrete weak divergence on
T
, denoted by
\nabla_{w,r,T}\cdot(\mathbf{b}v)
, is a linear operator from
W(T)
to
P_{r}(T)
. For any
v\in W(T)
,
\nabla_{w,r,T}\cdot(\mathbf{b}v)
is the unique polynomial in
P_{r}(T)
satisfying:

(2.4)

(\nabla_{w,r,T}\cdot(\mathbf{b}v),w)_{T}=-(\mathbf{b}v_{0},\nabla w)_{T}+\langle(\mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{n})v_{b},w\rangle_{\partial T},\quad\forall w\in P_{r}(T).

Furthermore, for
v_{0}\in H^{1}(T)
, applying integration by parts to (
2.4
) gives:

(2.5)

(\nabla_{w,r,T}\cdot(\mathbf{b}v),w)_{T}=(\nabla\cdot(\mathbf{b}v_{0}),w)_{T}+\langle(\mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{n})(v_{b}-v_{0}),w\rangle_{\partial T},\forall w\in P_{r}(T).

3.
Weak Galerkin Algorithm

Let
\mathcal{T}_{h}
be a finite element partition of the domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
into polytopes. We assume that
\mathcal{T}_{h}
satisfies the shape-regularity conditions as detailed in
[
48
]
. Let
\mathcal{E}_{h}
denote the set of all edges (for
d=2
) or faces (for
d=3
) in
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, and let
\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}=\mathcal{E}_{h}\setminus\partial\Omega
denote the set of all interior edges or faces. For each element
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, let
h_{T}
represent its diameter, and define the mesh size
h=\max_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{T}
.

Let
k\geq 0
and
q\geq 0
be non-negative integers such that
k\geq q
. For each element
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, we define the local weak finite element space as:

(3.1)

V(k,q,T)=\{v=\{v_{0},v_{b}\}:v_{0}\in P_{k}(T),v_{b}\in P_{q}(e),e\subset\partial T\}.

By assembling the local spaces
V(k,q,T)
over the partition
\mathcal{T}_{h}
and enforcing a unique value for
v_{b}
on each interior interface
e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}
, we define the global weak finite element space:

(3.2)

V_{h}=\{v=\{v_{0},v_{b}\}:\{v_{0},v_{b}\}|_{T}\in V(k,q,T),\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\}.

The subspace of
V_{h}
with vanishing boundary values on
\partial\Omega
is denoted by:

(3.3)

V_{h}^{0}=\{v\in V_{h}:v_{b}=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega\}.

For simplicity and when no confusion arises, for any
v\in V_{h}
, we denote the discrete weak gradient
\nabla_{w,r,T}v
and the discrete weak divergence
\nabla_{w,r,T}\cdot(\mathbf{b}v)
simply as
\nabla_{w}v
and
\nabla_{w}\cdot(\mathbf{b}v)
, respectively. These operators are computed element-wise according to (
2.2
) and (
2.4
):

\displaystyle(\nabla_{w}v)|_{T}=

\displaystyle\nabla_{w,r,T}(v|_{T}),\quad\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h},

\displaystyle(\nabla_{w}\cdot(\mathbf{b}v))|_{T}=

\displaystyle\nabla_{w,r,T}\cdot(\mathbf{b}v|_{T}),\quad\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}.

In this work, the polynomial degree
r
for the discrete operators is chosen as
r=k-1+2N
for non-convex polytopal elements and
r=k-1+N
for convex polytopal elements, where
N
denotes the number of edges or faces of the element
T
. Further justification for this choice of
r
is provided in
[
34
]
.

We introduce the global
L^{2}
inner product notation
(\cdot,\cdot)=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\cdot,\cdot)_{T}
and define the following bilinear form
a(\cdot,\cdot)
on
V_{h}\times V_{h}
:

(3.4)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} a(u,v)&=\rho(\nabla_{w}u,\nabla_{w}v)+(\nabla_{w}\cdot(\mathbf{b}u),v_{0})\\
&+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\langle\mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{n}(u_{0}-u_{b}),v_{0}-v_{b}\rangle_{\partial T^{+}}+(cu_{0},v_{0}),\end{aligned}

where
\mathbf{n}
is the unit outward normal vector on
\partial T
, and on
\partial T^{+}
faces

\mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{n}>0
.

The simple weak Galerkin numerical scheme for the convection-diffusion-reaction problem (
1.1
) is formulated as follows:

Weak Galerkin Algorithm 3.1
.

Find
u_{h}=\{u_{0},u_{b}\}\in V_{h}^{0}
such that

(3.5)

a(u_{h},v)=(f,v_{0}),\quad\forall v=\{v_{0},v_{b}\}\in V_{h}^{0}.

4.
Existence and Uniqueness of the Numerical Solution

We begin by recalling that for a shape-regular finite element partition
\mathcal{T}_{h}
of the domain
\Omega
, the following trace inequality holds for any
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
and
\phi\in H^{1}(T)

[
48
]
:

(4.1)

\|\phi\|^{2}_{\partial T}\leq C(h_{T}^{-1}\|\phi\|_{T}^{2}+h_{T}\|\nabla\phi\|_{T}^{2}).

Furthermore, if
\phi
is a polynomial on the element
T
, the following discrete trace inequality holds
[
48
]
:

(4.2)

\|\phi\|^{2}_{\partial T}\leq Ch_{T}^{-1}\|\phi\|_{T}^{2}.

For any weak function
v=\{v_{0},v_{b}\}\in V_{h}
, we define the discrete energy norm as follows:

(4.3)

\begin{split}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}v{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}^{2}=&\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\rho(\nabla_{w}v,\nabla_{w}v)_{T}+\frac{1}{2}\langle{\mathbf{b}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}(v_{b}-v_{0}),v_{0}-v_{b}\rangle_{\partial T^{-}}\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\langle{\mathbf{b}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}(v_{0}-v_{b}),v_{0}-v_{b}\rangle_{\partial T^{+}}+((c+\frac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot\mathbf{b})v_{0},v_{0})_{T}.\end{split}

Additionally, we define a discrete
H^{1}
semi-norm by:

(4.4)

\begin{split}\|v\|_{1,h}^{2}=&\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\rho\|\nabla v_{0}\|_{T}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\langle{\mathbf{b}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}(v_{b}-v_{0}),v_{0}-v_{b}\rangle_{\partial T^{-}}\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\langle{\mathbf{b}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}(v_{0}-v_{b}),v_{0}-v_{b}\rangle_{\partial T^{+}}+((c+\frac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot\mathbf{b})v_{0},v_{0})_{T}\\
&+h_{T}^{-1}\|v_{0}-v_{b}\|_{\partial T}^{2}.\end{split}

To establish the stability of the scheme, we utilize several preliminary results.

Lemma 4.1
.

[
34
]

For any
v=\{v_{0},v_{b}\}\in V_{h}
, there exists a constant
C
such that:

\|\nabla v_{0}\|_{T}\leq C\|\nabla_{w}v\|_{T}.

Lemma 4.2
.

[
34
]

For
v=\{v_{0},v_{b}\}\in V_{h}
, let
\bm{\varphi}=(v_{b}-v_{0})\mathbf{n}\varphi_{e_{i}}
, where
\mathbf{n}
is the unit outward normal to the edge/face
e_{i}
. Then the following inequality holds:

(4.5)

\|\bm{\varphi}\|_{T}^{2}\leq Ch_{T}\int_{e_{i}}|v_{b}-v_{0}|^{2}ds.

Lemma 4.3
.

There exist positive constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
such that for any
v=\{v_{0},v_{b}\}\in V_{h}
:

(4.6)

C_{1}\|v\|_{1,h}\leq{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}v{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}\leq C_{2}\|v\|_{1,h}.

Proof.

Consider a potentially non-convex polytopal element
T
. We define an edge/face-based bubble function as
\varphi_{e_{i}}=\prod_{k\neq i}l_{k}^{2}(x)
. It can be verified that (1)
\varphi_{e_{i}}=0
on the edge/face
e_{k}
for
k\neq i
, (2) there exists a subdomain
\widehat{e_{i}}\subset e_{i}
such that
\varphi_{e_{i}}\geq\rho_{1}
for some constant
\rho_{1}>0
.

By extending
v_{b}
and the trace of
v_{0}
from the edge
e_{i}
to the element
T
(denoted still as
v_{b}
and
v_{0}
, see
[
34
]
), and choosing
\bm{\varphi}=(v_{b}-v_{0})\mathbf{n}\varphi_{e_{i}}
in (
2.3
), we obtain:

\displaystyle(\nabla_{w}v,\bm{\varphi})_{T}

\displaystyle=(\nabla v_{0},\bm{\varphi})_{T}+\langle v_{b}-v_{0},\bm{\varphi}\cdot\mathbf{n}\rangle_{\partial T}

\displaystyle=(\nabla v_{0},\bm{\varphi})_{T}+\int_{e_{i}}|v_{b}-v_{0}|^{2}\varphi_{e_{i}}ds.

Applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, Lemma
4.2
, and the properties of the bubble function, we have:

h_{T}^{-1}\int_{e_{i}}|v_{b}-v_{0}|^{2}ds\leq C(\|\nabla_{w}v\|^{2}_{T}+\|\nabla v_{0}\|^{2}_{T})\leq C\|\nabla_{w}v\|^{2}_{T}.

Combining this with Lemma
4.1
and definitions (
4.3
)–(
4.4
) yields the lower bound
C_{1}\|v\|_{1,h}\leq{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}v{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}
.

Next, from (
2.3
), Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the trace inequality (
4.2
), we have

\Big|(\nabla_{w}v,{\bm{\varphi}})_{T}\Big|\leq\|\nabla v_{0}\|_{T}\|{\bm{\varphi}}\|_{T}+Ch_{T}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|v_{b}-v_{0}\|_{\partial T}\|{\bm{\varphi}}\|_{T},

which yields

\|\nabla_{w}v\|_{T}^{2}\leq C(\|\nabla v_{0}\|^{2}_{T}+h_{T}^{-1}\|v_{b}-v_{0}\|^{2}_{\partial T}),

and further gives

{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}v{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}\leq C_{2}\|v\|_{1,h}.

This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

For any
v\in V_{h}
, the bilinear form satisfies the following coercivity-like identity:

a(v,v)={|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}v{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}^{2}.

Proof.

It follows from (
2.4
) and the ususal integration by parts that

\begin{split}&\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(\nabla_{w}\cdot({\mathbf{b}}v),v_{0})_{T}\\
=&\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}-({\mathbf{b}}v_{0},\nabla v_{0})_{T}+\langle{\mathbf{b}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}v_{b},v_{0}\rangle_{\partial T}\\
=&\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(\nabla\cdot{\mathbf{b}}v_{0},v_{0})_{T}+({\mathbf{b}}v_{0},\nabla v_{0})_{T}+\langle{\mathbf{b}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}(v_{b}-v_{0}),v_{0}\rangle_{\partial T}\\
=&\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(\nabla\cdot{\mathbf{b}}v_{0},v_{0})_{T}-(\nabla_{w}\cdot({\mathbf{b}}v),v_{0})+\langle{\mathbf{b}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}v_{b},v_{0}\rangle_{\partial T}\\
&+\langle{\mathbf{b}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}(v_{b}-v_{0}),v_{0}\rangle_{\partial T}\\
=&\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(\nabla\cdot{\mathbf{b}}v_{0},v_{0})_{T}-(\nabla_{w}\cdot({\mathbf{b}}v),v_{0})+\langle{\mathbf{b}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}(v_{b}-v_{0}),v_{0}-v_{b}\rangle_{\partial T},\\
\end{split}

where we used
v\in V_{h}^{0}
and
\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\langle{\mathbf{b}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}v_{b},v_{b}\rangle_{\partial T}=0
.

This gives

\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(\nabla_{w}\cdot({\mathbf{b}}v),v_{0})_{T}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(\nabla\cdot{\mathbf{b}}v_{0},v_{0})_{T}+\frac{1}{2}\langle{\mathbf{b}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}(v_{b}-v_{0}),v_{0}-v_{b}\rangle_{\partial T},

which, yields

\begin{split}a(v,v)=&\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\rho(\nabla_{w}v,\nabla_{w}v)_{T}+(\nabla_{w}\cdot({\mathbf{b}}v),v_{0})_{T}+\langle{\mathbf{b}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}(v_{0}-v_{b}),v_{0}-v_{b}\rangle_{\partial T^{+}}+(cv_{0},v_{0})_{T}\\
=&\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\rho(\nabla_{w}v,\nabla_{w}v)_{T}+\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\cdot{\mathbf{b}}v_{0},v_{0})_{T}\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\langle{\mathbf{b}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}(v_{b}-v_{0}),v_{0}-v_{b}\rangle_{\partial T}+\langle{\mathbf{b}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}(v_{0}-v_{b}),v_{0}-v_{b}\rangle_{\partial T^{+}}+(cv_{0},v_{0})_{T}\\
=&\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\rho(\nabla_{w}v,\nabla_{w}v)_{T}+((c+\frac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot{\mathbf{b}})v_{0},v_{0})_{T}\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\langle{\mathbf{b}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}(v_{b}-v_{0}),v_{0}-v_{b}\rangle_{\partial T^{-}}+\frac{1}{2}\langle{\mathbf{b}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}(v_{0}-v_{b}),v_{0}-v_{b}\rangle_{\partial T^{+}}\\
=&{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}v{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}^{2}.\end{split}

This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Theorem 4.5
.

The weak Galerkin numerical scheme (
3.5
) possesses a unique solution
u_{h}\in V_{h}^{0}
.

Proof.

Since the system is linear and finite-dimensional, uniqueness implies existence. Let
u_{h}^{(1)},u_{h}^{(2)}\in V_{h}^{0}
be two solutions, and define the error
\eta_{h}=u_{h}^{(1)}-u_{h}^{(2)}\in V_{h}^{0}
. It follows that
a(\eta_{h},v)=0
for all
v\in V_{h}^{0}
. Setting
v=\eta_{h}
, Lemma
4.4
implies
{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}\eta_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}=0
. By the norm equivalence in Lemma
4.3
, we have
\|\eta_{h}\|_{1,h}=0
, which necessitates
\nabla\eta_{0}=0
on each element
T
and
\eta_{0}=\eta_{b}
on
\partial T
. Using the fact that
\nabla\eta_{0}=0
on each
T
gives
\eta_{0}=C
on each
T
. This, together with
\eta_{0}=\eta_{b}
on each
\partial T
and
\eta_{b}=0
on
\partial\Omega
, gives
\eta_{0}\equiv 0
and further
\eta_{b}\equiv 0
and
\eta_{h}\equiv 0
in the domain
\Omega
. Therefore, we have
u_{h}^{(1)}\equiv u_{h}^{(2)}
. This completes the proof of this theorem.
∎

5.
Error Equations

In this section, we derive the error equations that govern the relationship between the exact solution and the weak Galerkin approximation. We begin by defining the necessary projection operators.

On each element
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, let
Q_{0}
denote the
L^{2}
projection onto the polynomial space
P_{k}(T)
. Similarly, for each edge or face
e\subset\partial T
, let
Q_{b}
denote the
L^{2}
projection operator onto
P_{q}(e)
. For any function
w\in H^{1}(\Omega)
, the
L^{2}
projection into the weak finite element space
V_{h}
, denoted by
Q_{h}w
, is defined such that:

(5.1)

(Q_{h}w)|_{T}:=\{Q_{0}(w|_{T}),Q_{b}(w|_{\partial T})\},\quad\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}.

Furthermore, let
Q_{r}
be the
L^{2}
projection operator onto the space of piecewise polynomials of degree
r
. As established previously, the degree
r
is chosen as
r=k-1+2N
for non-convex elements and
r=k-1+N
for convex elements, where
N
is the number of faces of the polytope
T
.

Lemma 5.1
.

The discrete weak operators satisfy the following commutative properties for any
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
:

(5.2)

\displaystyle\nabla_{w}u=

\displaystyle Q_{r}(\nabla u),

(5.3)

\displaystyle\nabla_{w}\cdot(\mathbf{b}u)=

\displaystyle Q_{r}(\nabla\cdot(\mathbf{b}u)).

Proof.

For any
u\in H^{1}(T)
, applying the definition of the discrete weak gradient (
2.3
) gives

\begin{split}(\nabla_{w}u,{\bm{\varphi}})_{T}=&(\nabla u,{\bm{\varphi}})_{T}+\langle u|_{\partial T}-u|_{T},{\bm{\varphi}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T}\\
=&(\nabla u,{\bm{\varphi}})_{T}\\
=&(Q_{r}\nabla u,{\bm{\varphi}})_{T},\end{split}

for all
\bm{\varphi}\in[P_{r}(T)]^{d}
. Similarly, for the discrete weak divergence, applying (
2.5
) yields:

\begin{split}(\nabla_{w}\cdot({\mathbf{b}}u),w)_{T}=&(\nabla\cdot({\mathbf{b}}u),w)_{T}+\langle{\mathbf{b}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}(u|_{\partial T}-u|_{T}),w\rangle_{\partial T}\\
=&(\nabla\cdot({\mathbf{b}}u),w)_{T}\\
=&(Q_{r}\nabla\cdot({\mathbf{b}}u),w)_{T},\end{split}

for all
w\in P_{r}(T)
. This confirms the assertions of the lemma.
∎

Let
u
and
u_{h}\in V_{h}^{0}
be the exact solution to the convection-diffusion-reaction problem (
1.1
) and its numerical approximation obtained from the WG Algorithm
3.1
, respectively. We define the error function
e_{h}
as:

(5.4)

e_{h}=u-u_{h}.

Lemma 5.2
.

The error function
e_{h}
defined in (
5.4
) satisfies the following error equation:

(5.5)

\begin{split}&\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\rho\nabla_{w}e_{h},\nabla_{w}v)_{T}+(\nabla_{w}\cdot(\mathbf{b}e_{h}),v_{0})_{T}\\
&+\langle{\mathbf{b}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}(e_{0}-e_{b}),v_{0}-v_{b}\rangle_{\partial T^{+}}+(ce_{h},v_{0})_{T}=\ell(u,v),\quad\forall v\in V_{h}^{0},\end{split}

where the anti-consistency error functional
\ell(u,v)
is given by:

\ell(u,v)=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\langle\rho(I-Q_{r})\nabla u\cdot\mathbf{n},v_{0}-v_{b}\rangle_{\partial T}.

Proof.

By applying the properties (
5.2
) and (
5.3
), and setting
\bm{\varphi}=Q_{r}\nabla u
in the definition of the discrete weak gradient (
2.3
), we obtain:

\begin{split}&\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\rho\nabla_{w}u,\nabla_{w}v)_{T}+(\nabla_{w}\cdot(\mathbf{b}u),v_{0})_{T}\\
&+\langle{\mathbf{b}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}(u|_{T}-u|_{\partial T}),v_{0}-v_{b}\rangle_{\partial T^{+}}+(cu,v_{0})_{T}\\
=&\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\rho Q_{r}\nabla u,\nabla_{w}v)_{T}+(Q_{r}\nabla\cdot(\mathbf{b}u),v_{0})_{T}\\
&+(cu,v_{0})_{T}\\
=&\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\rho Q_{r}\nabla u,\nabla v_{0})_{T}+\langle\rho Q_{r}\nabla u\cdot\mathbf{n},v_{b}-v_{0}\rangle_{\partial T}\\
&+(Q_{r}\nabla\cdot(\mathbf{b}u),v_{0})_{T}+(cu,v_{0})_{T}\\
=&\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\rho\nabla u,\nabla v_{0})_{T}+\langle\rho Q_{r}\nabla u\cdot\mathbf{n},v_{b}-v_{0}\rangle_{\partial T}\\
&+(\nabla\cdot(\mathbf{b}u),v_{0})_{T}+(cu,v_{0})_{T}\\
=&\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(f,v_{0})_{T}+\langle\rho\nabla u\cdot\mathbf{n},v_{0}\rangle_{\partial T}+\langle\rho Q_{r}\nabla u\cdot\mathbf{n},v_{b}-v_{0}\rangle_{\partial T}\\
=&\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(f,v_{0})_{T}+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\langle\rho(I-Q_{r})\nabla u\cdot\mathbf{n},v_{0}-v_{b}\rangle_{\partial T},\end{split}

where we have employed the model equation (
1.1
), standard integration by parts, and the fact that
\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\langle\rho\nabla u\cdot\mathbf{n},v_{b}\rangle_{\partial T}=\langle\rho\nabla u\cdot\mathbf{n},v_{b}\rangle_{\partial\Omega}=0
since
v_{b}=0
on
\partial\Omega
.

Subtracting the numerical scheme (
3.5
) from the above identity yields:

\displaystyle\quad\ \sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\rho\nabla_{w}e_{h},\nabla_{w}v)_{T}+(\nabla_{w}\cdot(\mathbf{b}e_{h}),v_{0})_{T}

\displaystyle+\langle{\mathbf{b}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}(e_{0}-e_{b}),v_{0}-v_{b}\rangle_{\partial T^{+}}+(ce_{h},v_{0})_{T}

\displaystyle=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\langle\rho(I-Q_{r})\nabla u\cdot\mathbf{n},v_{0}-v_{b}\rangle_{\partial T}.

This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

and

Lemma 6.1
.

[

{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
be a finite element partition of the domain

satisfying the shape-regularity assumptions specified in
[

]
. For any
0\leq s\leq 1
,

, and

(6.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}^{2s}\|\nabla u-Q_{r}\nabla u\|^{2}_{s,T}

\displaystyle\leq

Lemma 6.2
.

Assume the exact solution
u
of the convection-diffusion-reaction equation (
1.1
) possesses sufficient regularity such that
u\in H^{k+1}(\Omega)
. There exists a constant
C
, independent of the mesh size

, such that the following approximation estimate holds:

(6.3)

{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}u-Q_{h}u{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}\leq C(1+\rho^{\frac{1}{2}})h^{k}\|u\|_{k+1}.

Proof.

By employing the identity (
2.3
), the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, the trace inequalities (
4.1
)–(
4.2
), and the approximation estimate (
6.2
) for
n=k
with

, we obtain

\begin{split}&\quad\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(\rho\nabla_{w}(u-Q_{h}u),{\mathbf{v}})_{T}\\
=&\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(\rho\nabla(u-Q_{0}u),{\mathbf{v}})_{T}+\langle\rho(Q_{0}u-Q_{b}u),{\mathbf{v}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T}\\
&\leq\Big(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\|\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla(u-Q_{0}u)\|^{2}_{T}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\|\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}{\mathbf{v}}\|_{T}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\quad+\Big(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\|\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}(Q_{0}u-Q_{b}u)\|_{\partial T}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\|\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}{\mathbf{v}}\|_{\partial T}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\leq\Big(\ \sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\|\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla(u-Q_{0}u)\|_{T}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\|\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}{\mathbf{v}}\|_{T}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\quad+\Big(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}^{-1}\|\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}(Q_{0}u-u)\|_{T}^{2}+h_{T}\|\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}(Q_{0}u-u)\|_{1,T}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\quad\ \cdot\Big(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}Ch_{T}^{-1}\|\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}{\mathbf{v}}\|_{T}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\leq C\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}h^{k}\|u\|_{k+1}\Big(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\|\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}{\mathbf{v}}\|_{T}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},\end{split}

for any
{\mathbf{v}}\in[P_{r}(T)]^{d}
. Setting
{\mathbf{v}}=\nabla_{w}(u-Q_{h}u)
yields

(6.4)

\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\rho\|\nabla_{w}(u-Q_{h}u)\|_{T}^{2}\leq C\rho h^{2k}\|u\|_{k+1}^{2}.

Similarly, utilizing (
2.5
), the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, the trace inequalities (
4.1
)–(
4.2
), and the estimate (
6.2
) for
n=k
and
s=0,1
, we have

\begin{split}&\quad\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(\nabla_{w}\cdot({\mathbf{b}}(u-Q_{h}u)),w)_{T}\\
=&\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(\nabla\cdot({\mathbf{b}}(u-Q_{0}u)),w)_{T}+\langle{\mathbf{b}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}(Q_{0}u-Q_{b}u),w\rangle_{\partial T}\\
&\leq\Big(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\|\nabla\cdot({\mathbf{b}}(u-Q_{0}u))\|^{2}_{T}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\|w\|_{T}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\quad+\Big(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\|{\mathbf{b}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}(Q_{0}u-Q_{b}u)\|_{\partial T}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\|w\|_{\partial T}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\leq\Big(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\|\nabla\cdot({\mathbf{b}}(u-Q_{0}u))\|^{2}_{T}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\|w\|_{T}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\quad+\Big(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}^{-1}\|{\mathbf{b}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}(Q_{0}u-u)\|_{T}^{2}+h_{T}\|{\mathbf{b}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}(Q_{0}u-u)\|_{1,T}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\quad\cdot\Big(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}^{-1}\|w\|_{T}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\leq Ch^{k}\|u\|_{k+1}\Big(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\|w\|_{T}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},\end{split}

for any
w\in P_{r}(T)
. Choosing

gives

(6.5)

\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\|\nabla_{w}\cdot{\mathbf{b}}(u-Q_{h}u)\|^{2}_{T}\leq Ch^{2k}\|u\|_{k+1}^{2}.

6.2
) with

and
s=0
, we have

(6.6)

\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}c\|u-Q_{0}u\|_{T}^{2}\leq Ch^{2(k+1)}\|u\|^{2}_{k+1}.

4.2

) with

and
s=0
and
s=1
,
we have

(6.7)

\begin{split}&\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\langle{\mathbf{b}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}(Q_{b}u-Q_{0}u),Q_{b}u-Q_{0}u\rangle_{\partial T}\\
\leq&C\|Q_{b}u-Q_{0}u\|^{2}_{\partial T}\\
\leq&Ch_{T}^{-1}\|u-Q_{0}u\|^{2}_{T}+Ch_{T}\|u-Q_{0}u\|^{2}_{1,T}\\
\leq&Ch^{2k+1}\|u\|^{2}_{k+1}.\end{split}

Finally, invoking Lemma
4.4
and combining the estimates (

), (

{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}u-Q_{h}u{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}^{2}=a(u-Q_{h}u,u-Q_{h}u)\leq C(\rho+1)h^{2k}\|u\|^{2}_{k+1}.

This concludes the proof of the lemma.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
u\in H^{k+1}(\Omega)
be the exact solution of the convection-diffusion-reaction equation (
1.1
). There exists a constant
C
such that the following error estimate holds in the discrete energy norm:

(6.8)

{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}u-u_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}\leq C(1+\rho^{\frac{1}{2}})h^{k}\|u\|_{k+1}.

Proof.

To estimate the right-hand side of the error equation (
5.5
), we apply the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, the trace inequality (
4.1
), the approximation result (
6.1
) for
m=k
with
s=0,1
, and the norm equivalence (
4.6
) to obtain

(6.9)

\begin{split}&\Big|\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\langle\rho(I-Q_{r})\nabla u\cdot{\mathbf{n}},v_{0}-v_{b}\rangle_{\partial T}\Big|\\
\leq&C(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\|\rho(I-Q_{r})\nabla u\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\|^{2}_{T}+h_{T}^{2}\|\rho(I-Q_{r})\nabla u\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\|^{2}_{1,T})^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\cdot(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}^{-1}\|v_{0}-v_{b}\|^{2}_{\partial T})^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\leq&C\rho h^{k}\|u\|_{k+1}\|v\|_{1,h}\\
\leq&C\rho h^{k}\|u\|_{k+1}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}v{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}.\end{split}

Substituting (
6.9
) into (
5.5
) yields

(6.10)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\quad\ \sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(\rho\nabla_{w}e_{h},\nabla_{w}v)_{T}+(\nabla_{w}\cdot({\mathbf{b}}e_{h}),v_{0})_{T}\\
&+\langle{\mathbf{b}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}(e_{0}-e_{b}),v_{0}-v_{b}\rangle_{\partial T^{+}}+(ce_{0},v_{0})_{T}\leq C\rho h^{k}\|u\|_{k+1}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}v{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}.\end{aligned}

Using (
4.4
), the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, the triangle inequality, and setting
v=Q_{h}u-u_{h}
in (
6.10
), while considering the estimate (
6.3
), we find

\begin{split}&{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}u-u_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}^{2}=a(u-u_{h},u-u_{h})\\
=&a(u-u_{h},u-Q_{h}u)+a(u-u_{h},Q_{h}u-u_{h})\\
\leq&a(u-u_{h},u-u_{h})a(u-Q_{h}u,u-Q_{h}u)\\
&+C\rho h^{k}\|u\|_{k+1}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}Q_{h}u-u_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}\\
\leq&{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}u-u_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}u-Q_{h}u{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}\\
&+C\rho h^{k}\|u\|_{k+1}({|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}Q_{h}u-u{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}+{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}u-u_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|})\\
\leq&{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}u-u_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}C(1+\rho^{\frac{1}{2}})h^{k}\|u\|_{k+1}+C\rho h^{k}\|u\|_{k+1}(1+\rho^{\frac{1}{2}})h^{k}\|u\|_{k+1}\\
&+C\rho h^{k}\|u\|_{k+1}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}u-u_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}.\end{split}

Rearranging the terms leads to the desired result:

\begin{split}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}u-u_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}\leq C(1+\rho^{\frac{1}{2}})h^{k}\|u\|_{k+1}.\end{split}

This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

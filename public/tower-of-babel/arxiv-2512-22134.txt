A Mnemonic Matrix Rule for (Split) Octonionic Multiplication and its Extension to the Cayley–Dickson Tower
Jean-Pierre Gazeau

Abstract
We present a compact mnemonic device for computing the product of two (split) octonions written in Cayley–Dickson form q + ℓ ​ p q+\ell p with q , p ∈ ℍ q,p\in\mathbb{H} . The rule appears as a simple ( R + L ) (R+L) pattern of right-ordered and left-ordered (quaternionic) products inside a 2 × 2 2\times 2 quaternionic matrix model. The pattern extends verbatim to all algebras in the Cayley–Dickson tower, providing an efficient computational tool in non-associative settings. To our knowledge, this explicit “ ( R + L ) (R+L) ” mnemonic does not appear in the classical literature on octonions or composition algebras.



1
Introduction

The octonions
\mathbb{O}

[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
]
may be described as pairs of quaternions

(q,p)\in\mathbb{H}\oplus\mathbb{H}\,\ell
via the Cayley–Dickson construction.
The multiplication is

(q_{1}+\ell p_{1})(q_{2}+\ell p_{2})=q_{1}q_{2}+\ell^{2}\,p_{2}^{\!*}p_{1}\;+\;\ell\,(q_{2}p_{1}+q_{1}^{\!*}p_{2}),

(1)

where
{}^{\!*}
denotes quaternionic conjugation and
\ell^{2}=\pm 1
covers the
division and split cases.
While (
1
) is standard, its direct use can become cumbersome because it
mixes conjugation with order-sensitive products.

In this note we isolate a simple mnemonic rule that organizes octonion
multiplication into a
2\times 2
block pattern of quaternionic products, where
exactly half of the elementary products appear in reversed order.
The pattern propagates inductively to all Cayley–Dickson algebras

\mathbb{A}_{n}
of dimension
2^{n}
and motivates a compatible “interlaced”
product for
2\times 2
matrices over a unital non-associative algebra.

2
Cayley–Dickson form and a
2\times 2
quaternionic model

Write a (split) octonion as

o=q+\ell p,\qquad q,p\in\mathbb{H},\qquad\ell^{2}=\pm 1.

Define the linear map

M(o):=\begin{pmatrix}q&\ell^{2}\,p^{\!*}\\
p&q^{\!*}\end{pmatrix}.

(2)

Definition 2.1

(Reversal marker)
.

Given a product
xy
in a (possibly non-commutative) algebra, we denote by

\overset{\bm{\curvearrowleft}}{xy}
the reversed product
yx
.

Remark 2.2
.

The symbol
\bm{\curvearrowleft}
is purely typographical: it does not define a new operation
on the algebra. It only indicates that, in the marked contraction, the two
factors are multiplied in the opposite order.

Definition 2.3

(Interlaced
2\times 2
product over
\mathbb{H}
)
.

For matrices of the form (
2
), define an “interlaced” multiplication

\mathbin{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{\scriptsize$\bm{\circlearrowleft}$}}
by expanding the usual matrix product, but reversing the order in the

off-diagonal
contractions:

\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\mathbin{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{\scriptsize$\bm{\circlearrowleft}$}}\begin{pmatrix}a^{\prime}&b^{\prime}\\
c^{\prime}&d^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}:=\begin{pmatrix}aa^{\prime}+\overset{\bm{\curvearrowleft}}{bc^{\prime}}&\overset{\bm{\curvearrowleft}}{ab^{\prime}}+bd^{\prime}\\
\overset{\bm{\curvearrowleft}}{ca^{\prime}}+dc^{\prime}&cb^{\prime}+\overset{\bm{\curvearrowleft}}{dd^{\prime}}\end{pmatrix}.

(3)

A direct computation shows that the Cayley–Dickson multiplication is reproduced
at the level of
M(\cdot)
.

Proposition 2.4

(Matrix mnemonic for octonion multiplication)
.

For
o_{i}=q_{i}+\ell p_{i}
with
q_{i},p_{i}\in\mathbb{H}
,

M(o_{1})\mathbin{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{\scriptsize$\bm{\circlearrowleft}$}}M(o_{2})=M(o_{1}o_{2}),

(4)

where
o_{1}o_{2}
is given by (
1
).

Remark 2.5
.

The point of (
4
) is not a new model of
\mathbb{O}
(many exist), but a

visible order-pattern
: each block in the product is a sum of a
right-ordered product and a left-ordered one.

Remark 2.6
.

Unlike Zorn’s vector–matrix formulation
[
5
]
, where non-associativity is encoded in
the replacement of ordinary products by vector dot and cross products in the
off–diagonal blocks, the present construction retains standard block entries
and shifts all non-associativity into a systematic pattern of order reversals
within an interlaced
2\times 2
matrix multiplication.

3
The mnemonic
(R+L)
pattern

Inspection of (
3
)–(
4
) yields the rule:

•

each block is the sum of exactly two products;

•

one term is in
right
(natural) order (call it
R
);

•

the other is in
left
(reversed) order (call it
L
);

•

the pattern is

\begin{pmatrix}R+L&L+R\\
L+R&R+L\end{pmatrix}.

This compact
(R+L)
pattern provides a fast and reliable way to compute
octonionic products in Cayley–Dickson form without repeatedly re-deriving
where conjugations and order-reversals must occur.

4
Propagation through the Cayley–Dickson tower

Let
\mathbb{A}_{n}
denote the
2^{n}
-dimensional algebra obtained by iterating the
Cayley–Dickson doubling.
Assume
\mathbb{A}_{n}
is equipped with its standard involution
{}^{\!*}
.
Represent an element of
\mathbb{A}_{n+1}
as
a+\epsilon b
with

a,b\in\mathbb{A}_{n}
and
\epsilon^{2}=\pm 1
, and define

M_{n+1}(a+\epsilon b):=\begin{pmatrix}a&\epsilon^{2}\,b^{\!*}\\
b&a^{\!*}\end{pmatrix}.

Theorem 4.1

(
(R+L)
propagation)
.

The interlaced product (
3
) applied to
M_{n+1}(\cdot)

reproduces the Cayley–Dickson multiplication on
\mathbb{A}_{n+1}
.
Consequently the
(R+L)
order-reversal pattern propagates inductively through
all Cayley–Dickson algebras
\mathbb{A}_{n}
.

Remark 4.2
.

For
n\geq 4
the algebras cease to be alternative, and zero divisors appear.
The mnemonic remains valid as an
identity in the defining multiplication
,
but invertibility and “determinant-like” constructions must be stated with
care.

5
Toward matrix structures over non-associative algebras

The representation (
2
) may be viewed as the natural octonionic analogue
of the classical
2\times 2
real and complex matrix models for
\mathbb{C}
and
\mathbb{H}
:

\mathbb{C}\ni z=x+\mathrm{i}y\;\longmapsto\;\begin{pmatrix}x&-y\\
y&x\end{pmatrix},\qquad\mathbb{H}\ni q=q_{0}+q_{1}\mathbf{i}+q_{2}\mathbf{j}+q_{3}\mathbf{k}\;\longmapsto\;\begin{pmatrix}q_{0}+\mathrm{i}q_{3}&-q_{2}+\mathrm{i}q_{1}\\
q_{2}+\mathrm{i}q_{1}&q_{0}-\mathrm{i}q_{3}\end{pmatrix}.

(5)

At the level of the tower, one obtains maps

M_{n}:\mathbb{A}_{n}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Mat}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{n-1}),

and the interlaced product provides a natural way to retain a controlled
multiplication on these
2\times 2
blocks even when
\mathbb{A}_{n}
is
non-associative. In particular, one may introduce:

•

flexible or alternative matrix products
, obtained by restricting
the matrix multiplication on
\mathrm{Mat}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{n-1})
to combinations
that respect the underlying (left, right, or fully) alternative laws in

\mathbb{A}_{n}
;

•

Moufang-type identities on special subspaces
, such as the
“Hermitian” or “pure” components of
\mathbb{A}_{n}
, where the algebra
retains sufficient alternativity for the classical Moufang identities to
descend to matrix form;

•

groups of norm-preserving transformations
, defined as those

2\times 2
matrices over
\mathbb{A}_{n-1}
whose action (by left or right
multiplication, or by conjugation) preserves the quadratic form induced by the
Cayley–Dickson norm on
\mathbb{A}_{n}
;

•

generalized determinant-like invariants
, obtained by replacing the
usual determinant with quadratic or quartic expressions that remain invariant
under the above norm-preserving groups (the determinant of a Hermitian

3\times 3
octonionic matrix being the archetypal example).

Such constructions arise naturally in exceptional geometry and the theory of
Jordan algebras (Albert algebra), in the triality phenomena associated with

\mathrm{Spin}(8)
, and in Tits’ constructions of exceptional Lie groups.
Within this framework, the representation
M_{n}
serves as a bridge between the
non-associative algebraic world of the Cayley–Dickson sequence and the
classical matrix formalisms underlying these exceptional structures.

6
Interlaced
2\times 2
multiplication over a unital non-associative algebra

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
unital
(not necessarily associative, not necessarily
commutative) algebra. Define
\mathbin{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{\scriptsize$\bm{\circlearrowleft}$}}
on
\mathrm{Mat}_{2}(\mathcal{A})
by the same
rule (
3
):

\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\mathbin{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{\scriptsize$\bm{\circlearrowleft}$}}\begin{pmatrix}a^{\prime}&b^{\prime}\\
c^{\prime}&d^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}:=\begin{pmatrix}aa^{\prime}+\overset{\bm{\curvearrowleft}}{bc^{\prime}}&\overset{\bm{\curvearrowleft}}{ab^{\prime}}+bd^{\prime}\\
\overset{\bm{\curvearrowleft}}{ca^{\prime}}+dc^{\prime}&cb^{\prime}+\overset{\bm{\curvearrowleft}}{dd^{\prime}}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}aa^{\prime}+c^{\prime}b&b^{\prime}a+bd^{\prime}\\
a^{\prime}c+dc^{\prime}&cb^{\prime}+d^{\prime}d\end{pmatrix},

(6)

where the displayed form uses
\overset{\bm{\curvearrowleft}}{xy}=yx
to remove the reversal
notation.

Remark 6.1

(On inverses and “determinants”)
.

In a general non-associative algebra
\mathcal{A}
, expressions such as

ad-cb
and
da-cb
need not coincide, need not be central, and may fail to
associate with the surrounding factors. Therefore one should distinguish

left
and
right
inverse candidates, and state sufficient
conditions ensuring that scalar prefactors can be moved safely.

A convenient safe statement is the following.

Proposition 6.2

(One-sided inverses under nucleus/centrality assumptions)
.

Let
X=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{Mat}_{2}(\mathcal{A})
and
define

\Delta_{L}:=da-cb,\qquad\Delta_{R}:=ad-cb,\qquad X^{\sharp}:=\begin{pmatrix}d&-b\\
-c&a\end{pmatrix}\,.

Assume:

•

\Delta_{L}
is invertible and belongs to the
left nucleus
of

\mathcal{A}
(so that
(\Delta_{L}^{-1}x)y=\Delta_{L}^{-1}(xy)
for all
x,y
),

•

\Delta_{R}
is invertible and belongs to the
right nucleus
of

\mathcal{A}
,

then

(\Delta_{L})^{-1}X^{\sharp}\mathbin{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{\scriptsize$\bm{\circlearrowleft}$}}X=\mathbbm{1}_{2}\,,\qquad X\mathbin{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{\scriptsize$\bm{\circlearrowleft}$}}X^{\sharp}(\Delta_{R})^{-1}=\mathbbm{1}_{2}\,

i.e. the displayed matrices provide a left inverse and a right inverse of
X

for the interlaced product, yielding a loop structure
[
6
]
.

Remark 6.3
.

For alternative division algebras (e.g.
\mathbb{H}
and
\mathbb{O}
), nucleus conditions are
automatically much easier to satisfy on many natural subalgebras, and one
recovers familiar inverse formulae in Zorn-type models
[
5
]
.

7
Conclusion

The
(R+L)
mnemonic yields a compact and effective method for computing with
octonions (and split octonions) in Cayley–Dickson form, and it propagates
uniformly through the entire Cayley–Dickson tower.
The same order-reversal pattern motivates an interlaced product on
2\times 2

matrices over a unital non-associative algebra, which in turn suggests
structured “matrix-like” formalisms adapted to non-associativity.
We hope this note will be useful both pedagogically and as a computational
device in exceptional algebra and geometry.

An enhanced Deep Q-Network approach for load frequency control in hybrid renewable energy system
Authors: Sheema B.S.P, Peer Fathima A
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Load frequency control in hybrid renewable energy systems faces challenges from intermittent sources and reduced grid inertia. Traditional metaheuristic optimization techniques like Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) and Grey Wolf Optimizer (GWO) suffer from computational complexity and premature convergence to local optima. This research presents an enhanced Deep Q-Network approach for optimizing PID controller parameters in load frequency control. The methodology analyzes a two-area system where Area 1 combines thermal and solar photovoltaic generation, while Area 2 integrates thermal and wind power. The novelty lies in integrating prioritized experience replay with importance sampling, domain-specific reward functions, and augmented state representation enabling model-free learning and real-time adaptation superior to conventional approaches. Experimental validation encompasses six comprehensive test scenarios, with baseline performance benchmarked against PSO-PID, GWO-PID and DDQN-PID controllers. Results demonstrate substantial improvements across key performance metrics. Settling times show reductions of 26.3% to 39.1%, while frequency deviations decrease by 29.0 to 51.3%. Total performance improvements range from 24.6 to 64% across different operating conditions. The controller demonstrates robust performance under challenging operational scenarios. Stability is maintained during parameter variations up to ± 50%, with effective management of load disturbances reaching 25%. The system also adapts effectively to renewable energy source variability across different time periods.

Introduction

Modern power grids face mounting difficulties in frequency stability as renewable energy technologies become more widespread1–3. Conventional load frequency control mechanisms, originally developed for traditional power plants with synchronous generators, struggle with the unpredictable output patterns characteristic of renewable sources4,5. Wind farms and solar installations have intensified frequency control problems because their power output fluctuates considerably, while simultaneously reducing overall grid inertia6,7.

Large-scale interconnected electrical networks demand advanced regulatory techniques to keep frequencies stable and reduce power swings between different regions8,9. Earlier control methods relied on integral control systems10, along with optimal and adaptive approaches11. However, the emergence of competitive electricity markets combined with growing renewable capacity has created demand for more sophisticated control solutions12,13. Proportional-Integral-Derivative (PID) controllers remain widely adopted for Load Frequency Control (LFC) applications due to their simplicity, robustness, and ease of implementation14,15. Traditional PID tuning methods such as Ziegler-Nichols and Cohen-Coon rules often fail to provide optimal performance for complex multi-area systems16. However, optimal tuning of PID parameters becomes increasingly complex as system complexity grows and operating conditions vary17,18.

Metaheuristic optimization techniques have been extensively applied to load frequency control across diverse system configurations. Popular approaches include particle swarm optimization19,20, genetic algorithms for Linear Quadratic Gaussian design21 and differential evolution for multi-source applications22. More recent developments encompass non-integer controllers for deregulated markets23 and Grey Wolf Optimizer implementations24. PSO-based optimization has evolved significantly in load frequency control applications, from early hybrid GA-PSO approaches25 to more recent implementations with advanced control strategies such as interval type-2 fuzzy logic controllers26. Advanced metaheuristic algorithms have shown promising results for automatic generation control in multi-area interconnected power systems27–29. However, these metaheuristic methods often suffer from computational complexity due to multiple objective function evaluations and premature convergence to local optima, as demonstrated in comparative studies of PSO-based load frequency control systems30,31.

In order to address such shortcomings, researchers have considered more advanced controller designs and control strategies. The latest study has investigated superior controller designs to make LFC performance of renewable-integrated systems perform better. A Tilt-Derivative-Integral-Derivative (TDF-TIDF) controller optimized with the Archimedes optimization algorithm has also been suggested in hybrid renewable energy systems, and this controller has shown better dynamic response than traditional PID structures32. TDF-TIDF solution overcomes the shortcomings of the conventional derivative control by including a tilt operator that offers enhanced noise rejection and transient operation. The multi-microgrid systems have used modified Harris Hawks optimization (MHHO) algorithm to tune the fractional-order fuzzy PID (FOFPID) controllers with improved frequency regulation due to the synergistic advantage of fractional calculus and fuzzy logic33. The article emphasized the benefits of the use of fractional-order derivatives to the non-integer nature of renewable energy sources.

Based on these advancements a Cascade Fractional-Order Tilt-Integral-Derivative (CFOTID) controller, with capacitive energy storage (CES) has been proposed on two-area power systems where renewable penetration occurs34. Better disturbance rejection and more rapid frequency recovery was achieved with the cascaded structure as opposed to single-loop controllers. An optimized cascade PI-(1 + FOPID) controller based on the Wild Horse Optimizer (WHO) has been suggested with a robust performance of a wide range of different power system configurations such as thermal, hydro, and renewable sources35. Moreover, a fuzzy-aided sliding mode control technique has been designed to regulate the frequency of wind-supported autonomous microgrids, which incorporates the strength of sliding mode control and the adaptive nature of fuzzy logic to deal with wind power variability36.

Although these enhanced controller structures have been proven to perform better than the classical PID implementations, they have similar constraints. The use of fractional-order and cascaded controllers leads to further tuning complexity, which means that each 5–7 or more parameters have to be optimized by each structure. The offline optimization strategies used, either bio-inspired algorithms or gradient-based, do not have the ability to deal with time-varying system dynamics without full retuning. Moreover, computation needs of finding the fractional-order derivatives and sliding mode control using fuzzy inference systems could restrict real-time application in a large-scale multi-area system. Such inadequacies inspire the search of solutions based on learning, which can constantly update controller parameters, without the need to resort to offline re-optimization. In this regard, this work intentionally uses a traditional PID controller to show that performance is enhanced by the proposed learning-based adaptive control model, and not the addition of more structural complexity and offline optimization to the controller.

The recent development in artificial intelligence and machine learning has presented an opportunity to optimize power system control. Load frequency control in microgrids based on renewable sources has been developed with deep neural network-based controllers, which show the potential of the field of deep learning to deal with the nonlinear dynamics of systems37. Cooperative LFC Multi-agent deep reinforcement learning techniques have been suggested to coordinate control areas in multi-area power systems, with better coordination38.

Reinforcement Learning (RL), particularly Deep Q-Networks (DQN), has emerged as a promising approach for complex control problems39,40. Unlike traditional optimization methods, DQN learns optimal policies through trial-and-error interaction with the environment, adapting to changing system conditions41,42. DQN applications in power systems include optimal energy management43 in grid-tied solar PV-battery44, and economic dispatch45.

Q-learning has been applied to load dispatch problems46,47, while deep reinforcement learning has shown promise in microgrid control48–50. The versatility of DQN has been demonstrated across various domains, including network intrusion detection systems51 and healthcare applications for disease prediction using electronic health records52, showcasing its robust learning capabilities and adaptability to diverse problem spaces. Deep Q-Networks specifically have been used for power system stabilizer tuning and voltage control53.

The appearance of new tendencies in the operation of the power system has determined the necessity to possess more adaptable approach of control. The traditional methods of optimization have been quite restrictive because the penetration of the renewable energy sources is in process. Metaheuristic algorithms (PSO and GWO) are highly computation intensive and are typically applied in tackling offline optimization, therefore, they cannot respond fast to the changes in the system. They also have a tendency to stabilize on local optima thus they do not do well in systems with high rates of renewable variability.

This is at the benefit of DQNs. Huge parameter spaces do not have to be searched to construct a control policy in a DQN, and do not require a complete system model. Instead of that, it acquires the control policy within the interaction with the power system environment. This learning process has the ability of being dynamic to track the dynamic nature of the renewable generation, and they do not have the computational constraints of the conventional approaches. The results of the applications of the DQN to various control tasks like the voltage regulation and energy management prove that the prospects of the load frequency control implementation are rather high because flexibility and resilience are the most notable parameters of interest.

Although DQN has shown promising outcomes in numerous applications in the power system39–53, its implementation to multi-area load frequency control with renewable integration has a number of unresolved issues. Although DQN has been successfully used in energy management43,44, economic dispatch45, and microgrid control48–50, the special needs of load frequency control present a set of peculiarities that are not properly discussed in the literature. Specifically, the traditional DQN models based on homogeneous experience replay cannot be used in order to focus on important learning events related to large frequency variations and tie-line power breaches. In addition, the design of reward functions should attentively manage various competing goals such as frequency regulation, tie-line power control and control effort minimization, taking into consideration variability of renewable energy sources.

In recent works, deep reinforcement learning methods including actor-critic, Double DQN and data-driven DRL are used to tackle frequency control and automatic generation control problems54–56. Although these methods illustrate the practicality of learning-controlled frequency control, they usually use homogeneous experience replay, narrow state representations, and use of narrow perturbation situations. Furthermore, such domains like domain-specific shaping of rewards, emphasis on important frequency events learning, and systematic evaluation of robustness under nonlinearities are not taken up together. Such restrictions inspire the suggested framework of Enhanced DQN-PID that combines prioritized experience replay, enhanced state representation, and reward design based on LFC and maintains the realistic PID control framework.

System modeling

This section presents the mathematical modeling of the two-area interconnected power network incorporating renewable energy sources. The system model encompasses of two identical conventional thermal generation units, renewable energy sources (wind and solar PV), and their interconnections through tie-lines. The comprehensive mathematical framework provides the foundation for controller design and performance analysis of the proposed DQN-optimized PID control strategy.

The study considers a two-area interconnected power network where Area 1 consists of thermal and solar photovoltaic generation, while Area 2 comprises thermal and wind generation. The system model incorporates the dynamic characteristics of conventional non-reheat thermal units, renewable energy sources, and their control mechanisms.

Controller modeling

This section presents the controller design methodology for load frequency control in interconnected power systems with renewable energy sources. The proposed approach integrates classical PID control with Deep Q-Network optimization to achieve superior frequency regulation performance. The methodology encompasses PID controller formulation, DQN problem formulation, and their integrated optimization strategy for autonomous parameter tuning under varying system conditions.

Deep Q-Network theory and problem formulation

Deep Q-Networks represent a significant advancement in reinforcement learning by combining deep neural networks with Q-learning algorithms39,40. DQN addresses the challenges of high-dimensional state spaces through function approximation, experience replay, and target networks58,59. This methodology has demonstrated effectiveness across diverse applications, from game playing39 to power system control39,40.

DQN-PID integration and optimization

The DQN used in this research is a fully connected feed forward neural network with an input layer that is the enhanced state vector, two hidden layers with ReLU activation functions, and an output layer that is an action that reduced or increased the PID gains discretely. He initialization is used to initialize the network weights to enhance the stability of training. Temporal-difference learning rule is used to update network parameters on Bellman error and learning rate is constant and exploration strategy is ε-greedy. Appendix A also gives detailed architectural parameters and training hyperparameters to make them fully reproducible.

The integration establishes a hierarchical control architecture where the DQN agent observes the current system state and selects optimal parameter adjustments through ε-greedy action selection as shown in equation Eq. Simulation results and analysis

To comprehensively evaluate the performance and robustness of the proposed DQN-PID controller, five distinct test scenarios are designed to cover various operational scenarios and system conditions. The proposed methodology was implemented and validated through numerical simulations conducted in Python programming environment. The initial case, known as the baseline case is explained in greater detail since it is important to emphasize the practical importance of absolute performance values, and the following cases of tests are aimed at comparative analysis to showcase uniformity under varying operating conditions. The time horizons used in the simulations are chosen to sufficiently represent the dominant transient and secondary frequency control dynamics of load frequency control which generally develop on a short to medium time scale. The long-term simulations with slower processes in the concrete system are determined as the valuable direction in the future studies.

Test scenario 1: baseline performance validation

The simulation parameters are configured with a total simulation time of 10 s using a time step of 0.01 s for numerical integration. A load disturbance is applied as a 10% step increase in Area 1 at the initial time (t = 0), which corresponds to a 0.1 per unit MW step increase in Area 1. The renewable energy sources are set with a photovoltaic reference power of 0.08 per unit MW and a wind reference power of 0.06 per unit MW. Conversely, DQN-PID controller has a better damping property and lower oscillatory energy and is more robust to nonlinear effects.

Stability analysis of the closed-loop system

In this section, the stability of the proposed closed-loop control system has been assessed both in the time domain and in the frequency domain. It provides the simulation-based performance analysis of the discussed test scenarios in the previous section and provides a theoretical understanding of the stability properties of the DQN-PID controlled system.

Time-domain stability assessment

The responses in the time domain of Sects. 4.1–4.6 show that the frequency deviations and area control errors are limited in their values and approach to a stable region after load perturbations and renewable variation. No long-term deviation or instability can be seen in all of the test cases, including the situation with realistic nonlinearities. These findings point to closed-loop stability of the suggested control scheme over the time domain.

Small-signal stability analysis

Frequency-domain wise, the proposed DQN design gives the highest PID gains and preserves the classical PID control design. Therefore, the stability of the closed-loop system can be measured using the conventional control theory. The fact that the responses of the time domain are restricted by boundaries, and the system has a superior damping characteristic compared to that of the open-loop system which is evident in the simulations indicates that the closed-loop system is operating in a stable region.

The information of the stability of the frequency-domain in this study is resolved based on the dynamics of the closed-loop system in the wide area of operation. The fact that the damping of oscillation is steady with the perturbation of the load and changes in renewable power shows that the PID gains obtained with the optimization are enough to attain gain and phase properties, and consequently, to prevent sustained oscillation. These measurements coupled with the static PID form give data on the stability of the DQN-PID controlled system without precise pole or eigenvalue construction in the frequency domain.

Conclusion

The paper suggested an Enhanced Deep Q-Network (EDQN)-based framework to tune the PID controller in load frequency regulation of renewable-integrated interconnected power systems in an adaptive manner. The suggested solution is a combination of PID control and reinforcement learning so that the online changes in the parameters could be made to address the uncertainty of the system, the intermittency of renewable resources, and the dynamism of conditions of operation peculiar to the modern power grids.

The outcomes of simulation under various test conditions, such as baseline operation, multiple load disturbances, parameter variation, nonlinearities, and renewable power variability, indicate that the proposed DQN-PID controller has better frequency regulation, effective damping of tie-line power oscillations, and less control effort than the traditional optimization-based controllers. The learning-based strategy (instead of aggressive control action) has a more well-behaved transient response, and a more rapid stabilization process, as it uses continuous control gain adjustments based on system feedback, and is especially beneficial to power systems that are rich in renewable sources.

The validation is done on a two-area interconnected power system, which has become a commonly accepted standard in assessing load frequency control strategies and capturing the important inter-area dynamics. Although this setup is adequate to prove the usefulness of the proposed learning-based PID tuning method, extensive testing on large-scale multi-areas power systems has not been tackled in this paper. Scaling of the suggested framework to larger connected networks by using relevant augmentation of the state representation, action space and reward formulation is a valuable future research direction.

Future research will also involve real-time and hardware-in-the-loop testing of the designed controller, exploration of long-term behaviour of the proposed controller, and design of hierarchical or distributed reinforcement learning architectures to increase the scope of scalability. Such guidelines will also enhance the practical usefulness of learning-based control strategies in frequency regulation in the next generation smart power systems.

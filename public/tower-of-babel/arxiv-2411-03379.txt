Introduction

It is well-known that Kolmogorov
[
18
]
solved Lusin’s
problem related to the trigonometric Fourier series. Namely, he was able
construct an integrable function whose trigonometric Fourier series diverges
almost everywhere (a.e.). Later on, he modified his proof to show the
existence of an integrable function for which its trigonometric Fourier
diverges at every point
[
20
]
. In parallel to
trigonometric system, the Walsh system has advantages in the approximation
theory. Therefore, the convergence of Walsh-Fourier series has gained
serious attention amongst many mathematicians. For instance, Stein
[
32
]
was able to establish an analogue of the mentioned
Kolmogorov’s result for the Walsh-Fourier series, i.e. it was proved the
existence of an integrable function whose Walsh-Fourier series is divergent
at all points. Let us mention the important results concerning trigonometric
and Walsh systems: Konyagin
[
22
,
23
]
, Bochkarov
[
3
,
4
]
, Körner
[
25
]
. The issues of divergence of Fourier series
on subsequences were studied by Gosselin
[
15
]
,
Totik
[
33
]
, Konyagin
[
24
]
Lie
[
26
]
, Di Plinio
[
6
]
, Goginava, Oniani
[
14
]
, Oniani
[
29
]
.

The problem of divergence of Fourier series with respect to a general
orthonormal system was raised by Alexits in his monograph
[
1
]
. The problem posed by him is related to the
behavior of the Lebesgue constant of the orthonormal system
\Phi=\left\{\varphi_{n}\left(x\right):n\geq 1\right\}
, i.e.

L_{n}\left(x,\Phi\right)=\int\limits_{0}^{1}\left|\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\varphi_{j}\left(x\right)\varphi_{j}\left(t\right)\right|dt\text{ \ \ for }x\in\left[0,1\right].

Alexits problem is formulated as follows: Let
\Phi=\left\{\varphi_{n}\left(x\right):n\geq 1\right\}
be any orthonormal system for which

(1)

\overline{\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}}L_{n}\left(x,\Phi\right)=+\infty

everywhere. Is there an integrable function whose Fourier series is
divergent almost everywhere?

In 1967, Olevskii
[
28
]
proved that for any uniformly
bounded orthonormal system there exists a set
E\subset\left[0,1\right],\mu\left(E\right)>0
such that condition (
1
) holds for all
x\in E
.

The issue of the divergence of Fourier series with respect to a general
orthonormal system was studied by Bochkarev
[
2
]
.
Namely, he considered a uniformly bounded orthonormal system
\Phi=\left\{\varphi_{n}\left(x\right):n\geq 1\right\}
, the it is established the
existence of an integrable function
f
whose Fourier series with respect to
the system
\Phi
diverges on some
E\subset\left[0,1\right]
with
\left|E\right|>0
. Later on, Kazarian
[
17
]

showed that, in Bochkarov’s theorem, the divergence for a positive measure
cannot be replaced by a divergence almost everywhere, which finally gave a
negative answer to the Alexits’s problem.

Nonetheless, when examining weighted averages of orthonormal systems, a
compelling question arises: can the Alexits’s problem be positively resolved
for these weighted averages?
In the current paper, we are going to solve such a kind of problem within the Walsh system.
To be more precise, let us introduce necessary notations.

Let
f\in L_{1}\left(\mathbb{I}\right)
,
\mathbb{I}:=[0,1)
and
S_{k}(f;x)
denotes the
k
th partial sums of the
Fourier series with respect to the Walsh system. Assume that
\mathbf{q}:=\{q_{k}:k\geq 0\}
be a sequence of non-negative numbers. Let us consider
a sequence of linear operators defined by

(2)

T_{n}^{\left(\mathbf{q}\right)}(f;x):=\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}q_{n-k}S_{k}(f;x),\quad n\in\mathbb{N},

where

Q_{n}:=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}q_{k}.

Throughout the article, the sequence
\mathbf{q}
enjoys the following
assumption:

(A)

the limit
\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}\left(Q_{2^{k}}/Q_{2^{k-1}}\right)
exists.

Now let us introduce the notion of
the Lebesgue sequence
for the
sequence (
2
):

(3)

L_{n}^{\left(\mathbf{q}\right)}:=\int\limits_{\mathbb{I}}\left|\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}q_{n-k}D_{k}(x)\right|dx.

Remark 1
.

It’s important to emphasize that if
\mathbf{q}
is non-decreasing, then
T_{n}^{\left(\mathbf{q}\right)}(f;x)
is convergent almost everywhere for
all
f\in L_{1}

[
16
, p. 67]
. Hence, it becomes
pertinent to explore the scenario where
\mathbf{q}
is non-increasing.

Let us consider two important examples.

Assume that

(4)

q_{j}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}1,\ \ j=0\\
0,\ \ j>0\end{array}\right..

Then
T_{n}^{\left(\mathbf{q}\right)}(f;x)
coincides with the partial
sums
S_{n}(f;x)
. Its divergence was established by Stein
[
32
]
. Moreover, its Lebesgus sequence diverges as well, i.e.
\sup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}L_{n}^{\left(\mathbf{q}\right)}=\infty
.

Now, we consider another decreasing sequence
q_{k}=1/k
. Then

(5)

T_{n}^{\left(\mathbf{q}\right)}(f;x)=\frac{1}{\log n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{S_{k}\left(f,x\right)}{n-k}.

In
[
8
]
it has been furnished an integrable function
f

for which

\sup\limits_{n}\left|T_{n}^{\left(\mathbf{q}\right)}(f;x)\right|=\infty\text{ a. e. on }\mathbb{I}\text{.}

Moreover, for the corresponding Lebesgue sequence one has
\sup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}L_{n}^{\left(\mathbf{q}\right)}=\infty
.

The provided examples naturally clarifies the mentioned problem for the
sequence (
2
). Namely,
let us suppose that the condition

(6)

\sup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}L_{n}^{\left(\mathbf{q}\right)}=\infty

is satisfied for (
2
). Is it possible to construct an integrable
function
f
for which the sequence
\{T_{n}^{\left(\mathbf{q}\right)}(f)\}
is divergent almost everywhere?

In this paper, we positively solve the mentioned problem.

Theorem 1
.

Assume that
\mathbf{q}=\{q_{k}:k\geq 0\}
is a non-increasing
sequence with (A). Let us suppose that the condition (
6
) is
satisfied. Then there exists a function
f\in L_{1}\left(\mathbb{I}\right)

such that the sequence
\{T_{n}^{\left(\mathbf{q}\right)}(f)\}
(see (
2
)) diverges almost everywhere.

It turns out that the condition (
6
) can be characterized through
\mathbf{q}=\{q_{k}:k\geq 0\}
. Namely, we have the next result.

Lemma 1
.

Assume that
\mathbf{q}=\{q_{k}:k\geq 0\}
is a non-increasing
sequence with (A). Then, the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\sup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}L_{n}^{\left(\mathbf{q}\right)}=\infty;

(ii)

\rho\left(\mathbf{q}\right):=\sup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\frac{1}{Q_{2^{n}}}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}Q_{2^{k}}\right)=\infty;

(iii)

\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{Q_{2^{k}}}{Q_{2^{k-1}}}=1;

(iv)

\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{2^{k}q_{2^{k}}}{Q_{2^{k}}}=0.

Proof.

The equivalence of (i) and (ii) is proved in
[
13
]
. Let us
establish (ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii). Assume that (iii) does not hold, then one
can find
q^{\prime}

\in\left(0,1\right)
such that

Q_{2^{l}}\leq\left(q^{\prime}\right)^{k-l}Q_{2^{k}}\left(k_{0}\leq l\leq k\right),

which yields
\rho\left(\mathbf{q}\right)<\infty
, and consequently the
condition (ii) also does not hold.

Due to

\frac{2^{k-1}q_{2^{k}}}{Q_{2^{k-1}}}\leq\frac{Q_{2^{k}}-Q_{2^{k-1}}}{Q_{2^{k-1}}}\leq\frac{2^{k-1}q_{2^{k-1}}}{Q_{2^{k-1}}}.

the equivalence of (iii) and (iv) is immediately obtained.

Finally, let us prove that (iv)
\Rightarrow
(ii). Denote
\delta_{k}:=2^{k}q_{2^{k}}/Q_{2^{k}}
. Then

(7)

\frac{1}{Q_{2^{n}}}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}Q_{2^{k}}=\frac{1}{Q_{2^{n}}}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\frac{2^{k}q_{2^{k}}}{\delta_{k}}.

Using the monotonicity of
\mathbf{q}
one can find

c_{1}\leq\frac{1}{Q_{2^{n}}}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}2^{k}q_{2^{k}}\leq c_{2}\left(n\geq n_{0}\right),

where
c_{1},c_{2}>0
. Now, the last one together with
1/\delta_{k}\rightarrow\infty\left(k\rightarrow\infty\right)
and (
7
)
yield
\rho\left(\mathbf{q}\right)=\infty
.

This completes the proof.
∎

We stress that if the condition (
6
) is not fulfilled, then for every
integrable function, the operator sequence (
2
) is convergent almost
everywhere
[
13
]
.

Now, taking into account this result together with Lemma
1
, we may
formulated the next result about the convergence.

Corollary 1
.

Assume that
\mathbf{q}=\{q_{k}:k\geq 0\}
is a non-increasing sequence with
(A). Then the following three statements are equivalent:

(i)

\sup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}L_{n}^{\left(\mathbf{q}\right)}<\infty;

(ii)

\rho\left(\mathbf{q}\right)<\infty;

(iii)

For every
f\in L_{1}(\mathbb{I})
, the operator sequence
\{T_{n}^{\left(\mathbf{q}\right)}(f)\}
given by (
2
) converges
almost everywhere.

Remark 2
.

It is well-known that the convergence in measure is weaker that a.e.
convergence, so it is reasonable to explore the convergence of trigonometric
Fourier series in measure. Again, this problem was successfully investigated
by Kolmogoroff
[
19
]
. He proved that any
f\in L_{1}
its trigonometric Fourier series always converges in measure. The
similar kind of result has been established by Burkholder
[
5
]
(see
also
[
30
, Ch. 3]
) for the Walsh systems in
L_{1}
. We point out that
in
[
13
]
it has been established that for any non-increasing sequence

\mathbf{q}=\{q_{k}:k\geq 0\}
the sequence
\{T_{n}^{\left(\mathbf{q}\right)}(f)\}
given by (
2
) converges in measure for any
f\in L_{1}(\mathbb{I})
.

However, in the two-dimensional setting, the situation drastically changes.
Konyagin
[
21
]
and Getsadze
[
11
]
(independently of each other) have proved the
existence of two-variable integrable function whose square partial sums of
trigonometric Fourier (Walsh-Fourier) series diverges in measure. The
considered square partial sums of the two-dimensional trigonometric (Walsh)
Fourier series can be rewritten as tensor product of one-dimensional
trigonometric Fourier series. This fact was a key point in the mentioned
papers. Such kind of representation led the consideration of sequences of
operators acting on double-variable
L_{1}
-spaces which are represented as
tensor product of one-variable operators. It turned out that such kind of
sequences may converge or diverge in measure on
L_{1}
. Recently, Gát and
Kargulyan
[
10
]
, by employing tensor product technique,
have proved a.e. divergence of matrix transforms of rectangular partial sums
of Fourier series with respect to complete orthonormal system.

The second focus of this paper explores sequences of linear operators
defined as partial sums linked to the weighted Walsh-Fourier series (
2
). Examples of such sequences include two-dimensional Fej’er
means, Cesáro means, logarithmic means, Nörlund means, and others widely
recognized in the field.

The second main aim of the current paper is to determine the necessary and
sufficient conditions for the weights, ensuring that the discussed sequence
of operators converges in measure on
L_{1}
.

To formulate the result, let us recall some notions.

The Kronecker product
\left(w_{n,m};n,m\in\mathbb{N}\right)
of two
Walsh systems is said to be the two-dimensional Walsh system. Thus

w_{n,m}(x,y):=w_{n}(x)\otimes w_{m}(y).

Define

(8)

\left(S_{n}\right)_{1}f\left(x,y\right):=S_{n}f\left(\cdot,y\right).

In this right side of (
8
)
f\left(\cdot,y\right)
is considered as
a function in the variable
x
and the other variable is fixed almost
everywhere. Analogously, one can define

\left(S_{n}\right)_{2}f\left(x,y\right):=S_{n}f\left(x,\cdot\right).

Given two monotone non-increasing sequences
\mathbf{q}=\left\{q_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
,
\mathbf{p}=\left\{p_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}

let us set

(9)

\left(T_{n}^{\left(\mathbf{q}\right)}\right)_{1}:=\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}q_{n-k}\left(S_{k}\right)_{1},\ \ \left(T_{n}^{\left(\mathbf{p}\right)}\right)_{2}:=\frac{1}{P_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}p_{n-k}\left(S_{k}\right)_{2}\quad(n\in{\mathbb{N}}).

Their tensor product is defined by

T_{n,m}^{\left(\mathbf{q,p}\right)}:=\left(T_{n}^{\left(\mathbf{q}\right)}\right)_{1}\circ\left(T_{m}^{\left(\mathbf{p}\right)}\right)_{2}.

It is easy to see that

T_{n,m}^{\left(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{p}\right)}\left(f\right)=f\ast\left({F}_{n}^{\left(\mathbf{q}\right)}\otimes{F}_{m}^{\left(\mathbf{p}\right)}\right).

We denote by
L_{0}=L_{0}(\mathbb{I}^{2})
the Lebesgue space of functions
that are measurable and finite almost everywhere on
\mathbb{I}^{2}
.
Moreover,
\boldsymbol{\mu}(A)
stands for the two dimensional Lebesgue
measure of a set
A\subset\mathbb{I}^{2}
.

Denote

(10)

\pi_{k}=\pi_{k}\left(\mathbf{q}\right):=\frac{Q_{2^{k}}-Q_{2^{k-1}}}{Q_{2^{k-1}}}

and

\beta\left(\mathbf{q}\right):=\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}\pi_{k}\left(\mathbf{q}\right).

Theorem 2
.

Assume that
\mathbf{p}=\left\{p_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
and
\mathbf{q}=\left\{q_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
are
monotone non-increasing sequences of non-negative numbers with (A). Then the
following statements hold:

(i)

if

(11)

\beta\left(\mathbf{q}\right)+\beta\left(\mathbf{p}\right)>0.

Then, for every Let
f\in L_{1}\left(\mathbb{I}^{2}\right)
, one has

\left|T_{n,m}^{\left(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{p}\right)}\left(f,x,y\right)-f\left(x,y\right)\right|\rightarrow 0\text{ in measure on }\mathbb{I}^{2},\text{ as }n,m\rightarrow\infty;

(ii)

if

(12)

\beta\left(\mathbf{q}\right)+\beta\left(\mathbf{q}\right)=0,

then the set of the functions from
L_{1}\left(\mathbb{I}^{2}\right)
for
which the sequence
T_{n,m}^{\left(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{p}\right)}\left(f\right)
is convergent in measure on

forms the first
Baire category in
L_{1}\left(\mathbb{I}^{2}\right)
.

.

Now, we can write

\displaystyle=

Denote

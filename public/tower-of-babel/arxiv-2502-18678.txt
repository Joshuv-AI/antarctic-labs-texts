Emergence of fermion-mediated interactions in Bose-Fermi mixtures
Esteban Cárdenas, Joseph K. Miller, David Mitrouskas, Nataša Pavlović

Abstract
. This work is inspired by recent experimental observations in ultracold atomic Bose-Fermi mixtures [DeSalvo et al., Nature 568 (2019)]. These experiments reveal the emergence of an attractive fermion-mediated interaction between bosons, as well as a stability-instability transition. We give the first mathematical demonstration of this transition by studying the low-energy spectrum of a many-body interspecies Hamiltonian. More precisely, we show the convergence of its eigenvalues towards those of an effective Bose Hamiltonian, which includes fermion-mediated effects. Applying this result to a model with short-range potentials, we derive a stability-instability transition in the bosonic subsystem, driven by the Bose–Fermi coupling strength g g . For small | g | |g| , the bosons form a stable Bose–Einstein condensate with the energy per particle uniformly bounded from below. For large | g | |g| , the energy per particle is no longer uniformly bounded from below, signaling the collapse of the condensate.



1.
Introduction

In both high-energy and condensed matter physics, particle exchange processes play a central role in understanding the emergence of effective forces.
Notable examples include the Yukawa potential arising from the exchange of massive bosons, and phonon-mediated Cooper pairing in superconductors. Ultracold atomic systems, particularly Bose-Fermi mixtures, offer a versatile and controllable platform for investigating analogous phenomena in novel regimes. With recent advancements in atomic physics—such as sympathetic laser cooling and magneto-optical trapping—it has become possible to simultaneously cool down and trap bosonic and fermionic gases
[
45
,
46
]
, paving the way for a better understanding of the interplay between mediated interactions and collective behaviour in interesting physical systems.

The experimental realization of Bose-Fermi mixtures has revealed a remarkably rich phase diagram, involving polaron formation
[
36
,
44
]
mediated interactions
[
30
,
21
,
27
,
58
,
48
]
, novel pairing mechanisms
[
55
,
31
]
and states of dual superfluidity
[
56
,
42
]
. Our interest in this topic was motivated by recent experiments by DeSalvo et al.
[
21
]
on mixtures of light fermionic
\textnormal{Li}^{6}
atoms and heavy bosonic
\textnormal{Cs}^{133}
atoms. In this mixture, the Cs atoms form a Bose-Einstein condensate (BEC) which is completely immersed in a much larger degenerate Fermi gas of Li atoms. A key observation—anticipated also by theoretical predictions
[
54
,
19
,
43
]
—is the emergence of an attractive fermion-mediated interaction between the bosons. As confirmed by the experimental data in
[
21
]
, this effective interaction creates a dichotomy in the behavior of the bosonic subsystem. For weak interspecies coupling, the bosons form a stable BEC with a shifted energy, coexisting with the degenerate Fermi gas. However, as the coupling strength increases, the mediated attraction surpasses the intrinsic boson-boson repulsion, eventually destabilizing the BEC and leading to a collapse of the condensate cloud.

The aim of this paper is to give a rigorous derivation of fermion-mediated interactions–starting from an interspecies many-body Hamiltonian–and to prove the associated stability-instability transition.

1.1.
The model

We consider

N
bosons and
M
fermions
moving in the three-dimensional torus

{\mathbf{T}}^{3}=({\mathbf{R}}/2\pi\mathbf{Z})^{3}
.
The Hilbert space of the system is the tensor product

\mathscr{H}=\mathscr{H}_{\rm B}\otimes\mathscr{H}_{\rm F}\qquad\textnormal{where}\qquad\mathscr{H}_{\rm B}=\bigotimes_{{\rm sym}}^{N}L^{2}({\mathbf{T}}^{3},\mathrm{d}x)\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\mathscr{H}_{\rm F}=\bigwedge^{M}L^{2}({\mathbf{T}}^{3},\mathrm{d}y)\ ,

(1.1)

which account for bosonic and fermionic statistics, respectively.
For simplicity, we assume both species are spinless, and of equal mass.
The microscopic Hamiltonian
in appropriate units then takes the form

\displaystyle H\,=\,h\otimes\mathds{1}\,+\,\mathds{1}\otimes\sum_{1\leqslant j\leqslant M}(-\Delta_{y_{j}})\,+\,\lambda\sum_{1\leqslant i\leqslant N}\sum_{1\leqslant j\leqslant M}V(x_{i}-y_{j})

(1.2)

with Bose Hamiltonian

\displaystyle h\ =\ \sum_{1\leqslant i\leqslant N}(-\Delta_{x_{i}})\ +\ \frac{1}{N}\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant N}W(x_{i}-x_{j})\ .

(1.3)

Here,
W
and
V
are sufficiently regular, even functions describing Bose-Bose and Bose-Fermi interactions, respectively.
To capture different physical situations—particularly short-range interactions in dilute atomic gases—we allow the potentials to depend on the number of bosons
N
.
We also adopt the common and well-justified approximation of neglecting short-range interactions between fermions
[
24
]
.
The coupling
\lambda
is specified in (
1.8
) below.

We are interested in describing the low-energy spectrum of the operator
H
in a regime where the fermionic energy is much larger than the energy of the bosons.
In order to make this limit precise,
we introduce on the momentum lattice
({\mathbf{T}}^{3})^{*}=\mathbf{Z}^{3}
the

Fermi ball

B_{\rm F}=\{k\in\mathbf{Z}^{3}:|k|\leqslant k_{\rm F}\}

(1.4)

where
k_{\rm F}\geqslant 1
is the
Fermi momentum
.
We follow the convention that
k_{\rm F}
is a given number, and we
set the fermionic particle number as

M=|B_{\rm F}|\ .

(1.5)

In particular,
thanks to simple geometric asymptotics,
we know that
M\sim\frac{4\pi}{3}k_{\rm F}^{3}

for large
k_{\rm F}
.
We further introduce the Fermi energy

E_{\rm F}=\sum_{k\in B_{\rm F}}k^{2}\

(1.6)

that satisfies
E_{\rm F}\sim\frac{4\pi}{5}k_{\rm F}^{5}

for large
k_{\rm F}.

The Fermi energy is the ground state energy of the
non-interacting fermionic subsystem,
and the ground state
is given by the Slater determinant
of plane waves

\Omega_{\rm F}=\textstyle\bigwedge_{k\in B_{\rm F}}e_{k}\qquad\textnormal{where}\qquad e_{k}(x)=(2\pi)^{-\frac{3}{2}}e^{ik\cdot x}\ ,\qquad k\in\mathbf{Z}^{3}\ .

(1.7)

We will be interested in the regime for which
the fermionic energy
E_{\rm F}

is much larger than the bosonic energy.

The coupling
\lambda
of the Bose-Fermi potential in (
1.2
) is fixed throughout the article as

\displaystyle\lambda\ =\ \frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi Nk_{\rm F}}}\ .

(1.8)

This choice leads to an effective Bose-Bose interaction which competes with
W
.
Let us explain the heuristics (as described, for instance, in
[
21
]
).
For low-energy states, we expect most fermions to occupy the Fermi ball
B_{\rm F}
.
Moreover, as
k_{\rm F}\to\infty
, there is a separation of energy scales between fermions and bosons, which causes an effective (adiabatic) decoupling of the two subsystems. However, bosons can still interact with fermions through coherent three-particle processes: one boson excites a fermion with momentum change
k
, while another boson interacts with the same fermion with momentum change
-k
. These processes do not establish fermion-boson correlations (see e.g. Corollary
1.1
below), but they induce an effective interaction between boson pairs (see Figure
1
for a visualization). The amplitude of this process scales with the number of fermions a boson can interact with. Due to the Pauli exclusion principle, only fermions near the surface of the Fermi ball can participate. Thus, the number of contributing fermions scales like
k_{\rm F}^{2}
. Energy conservation (which appears in the form of a free fermionic resolvent) reduces this number further to
k_{\rm F}
. Finally, we note that as a second order process, the amplitude is proportional to
\lambda^{2}
. Consequently, the strength of the effective potential between two bosons scales like
\lambda^{2}k_{\rm F}
. To ensure this interaction is comparable to
\frac{1}{N}W
in (
1.3
), we set
\lambda
as in (
1.8
). The factor
1/\sqrt{4\pi}
is introduced only for convenience.

1.2.
Main results

In our first result,
we describe the asymptotics of the low-energy eigenvalues of

H
as
k_{\rm F}\rightarrow\infty
.
In this limit, we prove
the emergence of an effective Hamiltonian
acting on the bosonic subsystem

\mathscr{H}_{\rm B}
given by

h^{\textnormal{eff}}=\sum_{1\leqslant i\leqslant N}(-\Delta_{x_{i}})+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant N}W^{\textnormal{eff}}(x_{i}-x_{j})

(1.9)

where the effective two-body potential takes the form

W^{\textnormal{eff}}=W-\,V\ast V\ .

(1.10)

Here,
(f\ast g)(x)=\int_{{\mathbf{T}}^{3}}f(y)g(x-y)dy
denotes the convolution on
{\mathbf{T}}^{3}
.
We let the Fourier series be

\widehat{V}(k)=(2\pi)^{-3/2}\textstyle\int_{{\mathbf{T}}^{3}}V(x)e^{-ik\cdot x}dx

for any
k\in\mathbf{Z}^{3}

and for convenience we put
\hat{V}_{0}\equiv\widehat{V}(0)
.
For
s\in{\mathbf{R}}
we denote by
H^{s}({\mathbf{T}}^{3})

the Sobolev space on the torus.

In order to state our results,
we introduce the precise condition for the potentials.
Throughout this work, we shall always assume:

Condition 1
.

W\in L^{p}({\mathbf{T}}^{3})
for some
p\in(\tfrac{3}{2},\infty]

and
V\in H^{4}({\mathbf{T}}^{3})
.

Under these conditions, the Hamiltonian
H

can be defined
as a self-adjoint operator, understood as a sum of quadratic forms with form domain

H^{1}({\mathbf{T}}^{3N})\otimes H^{1}({\mathbf{T}}^{3M})\cap\mathscr{H}
.
While this is well-known, we include a proof of relative form boundedness
of the potentials in Lemma
A.1
for completeness. Notably, the Coulomb potential
\widehat{W}(k)\sim|k|^{-2}
is included.

Finally, for a lower bounded, self-adjoint operator
A

we denote by
(\mu_{n}(A))_{n\geqslant 1}
its
sequence of eigenvalues counting multiplicities, in non-decreasing order.
Our first main result now reads as follows.

Theorem 1
.

Let
H
be the Bose-Fermi Hamiltonian on
\mathscr{H}_{B}\otimes\mathscr{H}_{F}

given by (
1.2
), with scaling (
1.8
), and let
h_{\textnormal{eff}}
be the effective bosonic Hamiltonian on
\mathscr{H}_{B}

defined in (
1.9
). Assume
(W,V)
satisfy Condition
1
for some
p\in(\tfrac{3}{2},\infty]
.
Then,
there is a constant
C>0
(depending only on the value of
p
) such that for any
k_{\rm F}\geqslant 1
,
N\geqslant 1
and

1\leqslant n\leqslant N

\displaystyle\mu_{n}(H)=E_{\rm F}+\lambda NM\hat{V}_{0}-\tfrac{1}{2}\,\textstyle\int_{{\mathbf{T}}^{3}}|V|^{2}dx-\tfrac{1}{2}(N-2)\,|\widehat{V}_{0}|^{2}+\mu_{n}(h^{\textnormal{eff}})+\varepsilon_{N,k_{\rm F}}

where the error satisfies

|\varepsilon_{N,k_{\rm F}}|\,\leqslant\,C\,\big(1+\|W\|_{L^{p}}^{2p/(2p-3)}+\|V\|_{H^{4}}^{2}\big)^{2}N^{2}(\ln k_{\rm F})^{5/3}k_{\rm F}^{-1/3}

(1.11)

provided
k_{\rm F}(\ln k_{\rm F})^{-5}\geqslant C(nN\|V\|_{H^{4}}^{2})^{3}
.

The statement involves three parameters:
n
,
N
and
k_{F}
. To approximate the eigenvalues of
H
using those of
h^{\rm eff}
, the error must be smaller than
\mu_{n}(h^{\rm eff})
.
In particular, note that for fixed
n
and
N
, we have
|\varepsilon_{N,k_{\rm F}}|\to 0
as
k_{\rm F}\to\infty
. For example, for the ground state energy (
n=1
), we obtain:

\displaystyle\lim_{k_{\rm F}\to\infty}\big(\inf\sigma(H)-E_{\rm F}-\lambda NM\hat{V}_{0}\big)=\inf\sigma(h^{\textnormal{eff}})-\tfrac{1}{2}\,{\textstyle\int_{{\mathbf{T}}^{3}}|V|^{2}dx}-\tfrac{1}{2}(N-2)\,|\widehat{V}_{0}|^{2}

(1.12)

for all
N\geqslant 1
.

Remarks 1.1
.

a

•

The term

-\tfrac{1}{2}\,{\textstyle\int_{{\mathbf{T}}^{3}}|V|^{2}dx}

represents the self-energy of the bosons, arising from a coherent two-particle process where a single boson excites and subsequently annihilates the same fermion from the Fermi ball.
On the other hand, the term
\tfrac{1}{2}(N-2)|\hat{V}_{0}|^{2}
must be subtracted in order to extract the explicit
V\ast V
potential in the effective Hamiltonian. Both of these contributions are physically irrelevant, as they correspond to constant energy shifts in the effective Hamiltonian.

•

Thanks to the explicit error bound in Theorem
1
, both potentials
V
and
W
can depend on the number of bosons
N
. This becomes relevant in Theorem
2
, where we consider rescaled,
N
-dependent interactions.
While, in principle, they can also depend mildly on
k_{F}
, we exclude this possibility since it is not relevant for our analysis.

Before discussing our main application, we state a corollary on the convergence of the ground state eigenfunction of
H
. For simplicity, we focus on the ground state, though the result extends to other non-degenerate eigenfunctions. The corollary shows that the ground state is approximately of the form
\phi^{\rm eff}\otimes\Omega_{\rm F}
, where
\phi^{\rm eff}
is the ground state of the effective Bose Hamiltonian

. This supports the heuristics discussed above on the effective decoupling between the Fermi and Bose subsystems. The proof is postponed to Appendix
B

Corollary 1.1
.

Let
\Phi\in\mathscr{H}
and
\phi^{\rm eff}\in\mathscr{H}_{\rm B}
be the normalized ground state eigenfunctions of
H
and
h^{\rm eff}
, and fix their relative phase so that
\langle\Phi,\phi^{\textnormal{eff}}\otimes\Omega_{\rm F}\rangle\geqslant 0
. Then, for all
(W,V)
satisfying Condition
1
and
N\geqslant 1
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{k_{\rm F}\to\infty}\big\|\,\Phi\,-\,\phi^{\rm eff}\otimes\Omega_{\rm F}\,\big\|=0

Our main application of Theorem
1
addresses the stability-instability transition in large Bose-Fermi mixtures, as mentioned in the introduction. The physical picture is that the bosons form a BEC immersed in a large fermionic environment
[
21
]
. For sufficiently weak coupling, the condensate remains stable under the interaction with the Fermi gas, and the energy per particle of the bosonic subsystem stays finite in the limit
N\to\infty
. In contrast, when the Bose-Fermi coupling becomes strong enough, the effective boson-boson potential turns sufficiently attractive, driving the bosonic subsystem into an unstable phase. In this regime, the bosonic energy per particle is no longer uniformly bounded below, which indicates a collapse of the condensate.

To state a precise version of this transition, we consider the Gross-Pitaevskii (GP) regime that
describes a dilute gas of ultracold atoms interacting via short-range potentials. Our precise assumptions are as follows.

Condition 2
.

There exist
w\in C({\mathbf{R}}^{3})
and non-zero
v\in H^{4}({\mathbf{R}}^{3})
, both non-negative, radial and compactly supported,
such that

W(x)=N^{3}w(Nx)\qquad\textnormal{and}\qquad V(x)=g\,N^{3}v(Nx)\ ,\qquad N\geqslant 1

(1.13)

with coupling constant
g\in{\mathbf{R}}.

Under this condition, the Hamiltonian
H
describes a Bose-Fermi mixture with repulsive Bose-Bose potential and repulsive
(
g\geqslant 0
) or attractive (
g\leqslant 0
) Bose-Fermi interaction, with both
W
and
V
approximating delta distributions as
N\to\infty
. We note that
(V\ast V)(x)=g^{2}N^{3}(v\ast v)(Nx)
and thus the effective re-scaled
two-body potential is

\displaystyle w_{g}\,\equiv\,w\,-\,g^{2}\,(v*v).

(1.14)

The effective Hamiltonian (
1.9
) on
\mathscr{H}_{B}

becomes

h^{\textnormal{eff}}=\sum_{1\leqslant i\leqslant N}(-\Delta_{x_{i}})+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant N}N^{3}w_{g}(N(x_{i}-x_{j}))\ ,

(1.15)

which is commonly referred to as GP-scaling for
N
bosons. In Theorem
2
below, we prove the
existence of a
critical coupling

g_{c}\geqslant 0

from which we can distinguish two regimes: First, the
stable regime
,
given by
0\leqslant|g|<g_{c}
.
Here, the ground state energy per particle
of the
effective Hamiltonian
h^{\textnormal{eff}}

is uniformly bounded in
N
.
Second, the
unstable regime
, given by
|g|>g_{c}
, where the pair potential
w_{g}
becomes sufficiently attractive. Here, the ground state energy per particle diverges to
-\infty
as
N\to\infty
.

Within the stable regime, it would be desirable to calculate
the energy per particle.
To this end, we identify
yet another parameter region. Namely, where
|g|
is small enough so that
w_{g}\geqslant 0
.
In this case, it is well known
that bosons form a BEC with ground state energy

\displaystyle\inf\sigma(h^{\textnormal{eff}})=4\pi\mathfrak{a}(g)N+O(1)\quad\text{as}\quad N\to\infty\ ,

(1.16)

where
\mathfrak{a}(g)
denotes the scattering length associated with the potential
w_{g}
.
That is,

\mathfrak{a}(g)=\frac{1}{8\pi}\int_{{\mathbf{R}}^{3}}w_{g}(x)f_{g}(x)dx\qquad\textnormal{where}\qquad\begin{cases}&\hskip 3.41432pt(-\Delta+\tfrac{1}{2}w_{g})f_{g}=0\\[2.84526pt]
&\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}|f_{g}(x)|=1\end{cases}\ .

(1.17)

We refer to
[
35
]
and
[
7
, Theorem 1.1]
for precise formulations and proofs of (
1.16
). Additionally, let us note that the analysis of dilute Bose gases has been a very active area of research in mathematical physics—for instance
[

,

—with many results extending beyond (

). While the condition

The stability-instability transition is summarized in the following theorem. A heuristic illustration of the statement is shown in Figure

Theorem 2
.

Assume that Condition
2
holds with coupling
g\in{\mathbf{R}}
, and
define the shifted total energy-per-boson

\mathcal{E}:{\mathbf{R}}\to{\mathbf{R}}\cup\{-\infty\}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(g)\equiv\liminf_{N\to\infty}\,\lim_{k_{F}\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\,\Big(\inf\sigma\big(H)-E_{F}-\lambda NM\hat{V}_{0}+\tfrac{1}{2}\,{\textstyle\int_{{\mathbf{T}}^{3}}|V(x)|^{2}dx}+\tfrac{1}{2}(N-2)\,|\hat{V}_{0}|^{2}\Big)\ .

(1.18)

Then, the following statements hold

(1)

\mathcal{E}(g)=\mathcal{E}(-g)
for all
g\in{\mathbf{R}}
and
g\in[0,\infty)\mapsto\mathcal{E}(g)
is a monotone decreasing map.

(2)

There exists a critical coupling
0\leqslant g_{c}<\infty

such that

\mathcal{E}(g)>-\infty\qquad\forall g\in\{0\}\cup(0,g_{c})\qquad\textnormal{and}\qquad\mathcal{E}(g)=-\infty\qquad\forall g>g_{c}\ .

(1.19)

(3)

Let
g_{0}\equiv\sup\{g\geqslant 0:w-g^{2}(v*v)\geqslant 0\}
and
g_{*}\equiv w(0)/\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
.
Then,
g_{0}\leqslant g_{c}\leqslant g_{*}
.
In addition,
for all

the limit

in
(

)
exists, and

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(g)=4\pi\mathfrak{a}(g)\ ,

(1.20)

where the right hand side is defined in terms
of the scattering length (
1.17
).

.

The following points are worth noting.

•

which comes from replacing the scattering length

1.3.
Emergence of the effective theory

In this section, we sketch the proof of Theorem
1
and place the main ideas in a broader context. The key observation is that the bosons form a
slow
subsystem, while the fermions act as the
fast
subsystem.
Here, the typical kinetic energy of a fermion is much larger than that of a boson, and
one speaks of a separation of energy scales.
From this perspective, our model belongs to a larger class of physical systems exhibiting an adiabatic decoupling between two coupled subsystems. The analysis of such systems has a long-standing history, dating back to the work of Born and Oppenheimer
[
9
]
. A fundamental consequence of the separation of energy scales is that the two subsystems effectively decouple, with the fast subsystem inducing an effective interaction in the slow subsystem. This phenomenon has been rigorously established in various contexts, including Born–Oppenheimer theory for heavy nuclei
[
40
,
52
,
50
]
, the strongly coupled Fröhlich polaron
[
37
,
32
,
11
,
12
]
, and different Nelson-type quantum field theory models
[
20
,
26
,
51
,
53
,
14
]
.

In Theorem
1
, we verify this effect for large Bose–Fermi mixtures, where the ratio of energy scales is quantified by the Fermi momentum
k_{\rm F}\gg 1
.
While in our Hamiltonian the dependence of various terms with respect to
k_{\rm F}
is not explicit, the underlying scaling can be understood in terms of a simplified
abstract
model, presented below.
Here, we sketch the proof for this abstract model, as we believe that our ideas
apply not only to Bose–Fermi mixtures, but to a wide range of physical systems.
As we explain below,
one of the key estimates in our proof is an operator inequality
based on the completion of the square in the operator sense.
It allows us to obtain the lower bound for the ground state energy, as well as the higher order eigenvalues.
The abstract model that we present includes an explicit scaling parameter
\mu\gg 1
to quantify the separation of energy scales; this choice of scaling is reminiscent of the one in the models studied in
[
11
,
20
,
26
,
14
]
. We then discuss the modifications required to apply the argument to the Bose–Fermi model.

1.3.1.
The abstract model

Consider a Hilbert space
\mathscr{H}_{A}\otimes\mathscr{H}_{B}
, where
\mathscr{H}_{A}
and
\mathscr{H}_{B}
represent a fast and a slow subsystem, respectively. We assume a Hamiltonian of the form

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}=\mu T_{A}\otimes\mathds{1}+\mathds{1}\otimes T_{B}+\mu^{1/2}F,

(1.23)

where
T_{A}\geqslant 0
and
T_{B}\geqslant 0
are the Hamiltonians of the fast and slow subsystems, respectively, and
F
models their interaction. The parameter
\mu\gg 1
controls the ratio of energy scales. We assume that
T_{A}
has a unique ground state
\Phi_{A}
with eigenvalue
\inf\sigma(T_{A})=0
, a spectral gap of order one, and that
PFP=0
with spectral projection
P=|\Phi_{A}\rangle\langle\Phi_{A}|\otimes\mathds{1}
.

We divide the analysis into upper and lower bounds, and sketch it only for the ground state energy. For the upper bound, we use a trial state of the form

\displaystyle\Psi=(\mathds{1}-\mu^{-1/2}T_{A}^{-1}F)\Phi_{A}\otimes\Phi_{B}^{\rm eff},\quad\Phi_{B}^{\rm eff}\in\mathscr{H}_{B}.

(1.24)

Under appropriate assumptions on the interaction (e.g, if
F
is bounded),
one proves that

\displaystyle\langle\Psi,\mathbb{H}\Psi\rangle=\langle\Phi_{B}^{\rm eff},h_{B}^{\rm eff}\Phi_{B}^{\rm eff}\rangle+O(\mu^{-1/2})\quad\text{with}\quad h_{B}^{\rm eff}=T_{B}-\langle\Phi_{A},FT_{A}^{-1}F\Phi_{A}\rangle_{\mathscr{H}_{A}}.

(1.25)

The effective Hamiltonian
h_{B}^{\textnormal{eff}}

contains a non-positive
operator on
\mathscr{H}_{B}
,
representing
a partial expectation value with respect to
\Phi_{A}\in\mathscr{H}_{A}
.
More precisely, for an operator
S
on
\mathscr{H}_{A}\otimes\mathscr{H}_{B}
and fixed
\Phi_{A}\in\mathscr{H}_{A}
, the sesquilinear form

\displaystyle\left\langle\Phi_{B}^{\prime},\left\langle\Phi_{A},S\Phi_{A}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}_{A}}\Phi_{B}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}_{B}}\equiv\left\langle\Phi_{A}\otimes\Phi_{B}^{\prime},S\Phi_{A}\otimes\Phi_{B}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}_{A}\otimes\mathscr{H}_{B}}

(1.26)

defines an operator
\Phi_{B}\mapsto\left\langle\Phi_{A},S\Phi_{A}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}_{A}}\Phi_{B}
, which we call the partial expectation value of
S
with respect to the state
\Phi_{A}\in\mathscr{H}_{A}
.
In our analysis, we take
S=FT_{A}^{-1}F
.
We then choose
\Phi_{B}^{\rm eff}
as the ground state of
h^{\rm eff}_{B}
and apply the variational principle to obtain
\inf\sigma(\mathbb{H})\leqslant\inf\sigma(h^{\rm eff}_{B})+O(\mu^{-1/2})
. For our specific model, this analysis is carried out in Section
5.1
.

To see how the effective Hamiltonian emerges as a lower bound, we
complete the square
in the following form
(we omit the tensor product with the identity)

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}=\mu\big(T_{A}^{1/2}+\mu^{-1}T_{A}^{-1/2}F\big)^{*}\big(T_{A}^{1/2}+\mu^{-1}T_{A}^{-1/2}F\big)+T_{B}-FT_{A}^{-1}F\,.

(1.27)

This leads to the operator inequality
\mathbb{H}\geqslant T_{B}-FT_{A}^{-1}F
. Assuming we can show for suitable low-energy states
\Psi\in\mathscr{H}
(e.g. the ground state of
\mathbb{H}
) that

\displaystyle\langle\Psi,FT_{A}^{-1}F\Psi\rangle\leqslant\langle\Psi,\mathds{1}\otimes\langle\Phi_{A},FT_{A}^{-1}F\Phi_{A}\rangle_{\mathscr{H}_{A}}\Psi\rangle+o(1)\quad\text{as}\quad\mu\to\infty,

(1.28)

we obtain the matching lower bound
\inf\sigma(\mathbb{H})\geqslant\inf\sigma(h^{\rm eff}_{B})+o(1)
. This bound is non-trivial and depends on specific model properties—in our setting,
\Phi_{A}
corresponds to the Fermi sea and (
1.28
) emerges from normal ordering of creation and annihilation operators in combination with suitable operator inequalities. We refer to Section
5.2
for the details.

1.3.2.
Application to Bose-Fermi mixtures

To apply the outlined arguments to the Bose-Fermi model, we first note that bosons mostly interact with fermions by creating or annihilating particle-hole pairs in the Fermi ball. Therefore, we introduce the so-called particle-hole transformation, which implements this directly at the level of the Hamiltonian. Up to unitary equivalence, we then study a new Hamiltonian
\mathbb{H}
—defined in Eq. (
2.15
)—that describes the interaction between bosons and
excitations
of the Fermi ball. Since most particle-hole excitations have large momentum
|p|\sim k_{F}
, we identify them as the fast subsystem relative to the slow bosons with energies of order one. While the excitation Hamiltonian
\mathbb{H}
retains the key assumptions of the abstract model discussed above, it introduces additional difficulties:

1.

The Fermi momentum
k_{F}
plays the role of
\mu
, but it does not appear as simple prefactors in the Hamiltonian. Thus, the scaling of the different terms must be carefully extracted.

2.

All operators are unbounded and require careful estimates to control error terms.

3.

The explicit form of the effective Hamiltonian requires
analyzing the partial expectation value

\langle\Phi_{A},FT_{A}^{-1}F\Phi_{A}\rangle
.
In the Bose-Fermi model this operator still depends on
k_{F}
, and to obtain its explicit form—given by
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant N}(V\ast V)(x_{i}-x_{j})
—requires the analysis of its large
k_{F}
limit.

1.4.
The small mass limit

As explained in the introduction, the present work is largely motivated by the experimental findings in
[
21
]
on Bose–Fermi mixtures consisting of light fermionic
{}^{6}\mathrm{Li}
atoms and heavier bosonic
{}^{133}\mathrm{Cs}
atoms. We denote the respective masses by
m_{\mathrm{F}}
and
m_{\mathrm{B}}
. In these experiments, the separation of energy scales arises due to the small mass ratio
m\equiv\frac{m_{\mathrm{F}}}{m_{\mathrm{B}}}\ll 1
.
While in our analysis we set
m=1
and take the limit
k_{F}\rightarrow\infty
, it is also possible to consider a vanishing mas ratio
m\rightarrow 0
at
fixed

k_{F}
. The analysis of this limit is well within the scope of our methods—let us briefly sketch the necessary adjustments.

First, let
H
describe such a mixture in units where
m_{B}=1
and
m=m_{\rm F}\ll 1
.
The Fermi energy is now given by
E_{\rm F}=m^{-1}\sum_{k\in B_{\rm F}}k^{2}=O(m^{-1})
and we modify the coupling to
\lambda=1/\sqrt{4\pi Nmk_{F}}
.
Following the proof of Theorem
1
,
we would then obtain the following result, analogous to (
1.12
):

\lim_{m\rightarrow 0}\big(\inf\sigma(H)-E_{\rm F}-\lambda NM\hat{V}_{0}\big)=\inf\sigma(h_{k_{\rm F}}^{\textnormal{eff}})-\tfrac{1}{2}\,W_{k_{F}}(0)\,,

(1.29)

where the effective Hamiltonian
h_{k_{\rm F}}^{\textnormal{eff}}
and potential
W_{k_{\rm F}}
are defined as in (
3.3
) and (
3.4
), respectively. Notably, the effective potential now depends on
k_{F}
, and it agrees with
V*V
only in the limit
k_{F}\to\infty
.

Organization of the proof
.
In Section
2

we introduce the second quantization formalism
and reduce the analysis to the excitation Hamiltonian
\mathbb{H}
on Fock space.
In Section
3

we prove Theorem
1
and
2
.
The proof is based on
corresponding upper and lower bounds for the eigenvalues of
\mathbb{H}
.
In Section
4
we collect helpful estimates for its analysis;
these involve sum over relevant geometric sets, and various operator inequalities.
In Section
5

we prove these bounds
and show the emergence of the effective Hamiltonian.
Finally
in Section
6

we prove an inequality first stated in Section
4
.

Convention
. We say that
C>0
is a
constant

if it is a positive number, independent of all the physical parameters
N,M,\lambda

and also of the potentials
V
and
W
.
Its value may change from line to line.

2.
Second quantization

As we have mentioned earlier, the effective Bose-Bose interaction that is induced by the Fermi subsystem
can be understood as exchanging (virtual) excitations of the Fermi ball.
In order to make this idea precise, it is convenient
to work in the formalism of second quantization,
where we allow the number of fermions to fluctuate.

To this end, we embed the fermionic Hilbert space

\mathscr{H}_{F}
into the larger Fock space

\mathscr{F}=\textstyle\mathbb{C}\oplus\bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathscr{F}^{(n)}\qquad\textnormal{with}\qquad\mathscr{F}^{(n)}=\bigwedge_{j=1}^{n}L^{2}({\mathbf{T}}^{3})\ ,\quad n\geqslant 1

(2.1)

so that
\mathscr{H}_{F}=\mathscr{F}^{(M)}
.
As usual,
\mathscr{F}

is naturally endowed with an inner product

\displaystyle\left\langle\Psi,\Phi\right\rangle_{\mathscr{F}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left\langle\Psi^{(n)},\Phi^{(n)}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{F}^{(n)}}\ ,\qquad\Psi,\Phi\in\mathscr{F}

(2.2)

and we denote the Fock vaccum by

\Omega=(1,\textbf{0})
.
Additionally, we equip
\mathscr{F}
with
creation and annihilation operators,
defined for
f\in L^{2}({\mathbf{T}}^{3})
and
\Psi\in\mathscr{F}

\displaystyle[a(f)\Psi]^{(n)}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})

\displaystyle\ =\ \sqrt{n+1}\int_{{\mathbf{T}}^{3}}\overline{f(x)}\Psi^{(n+1)}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})dx

(2.3)

\displaystyle[a^{*}(f)\Psi]^{(n)}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})

\displaystyle\ =\ \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i}f(x_{i})\Psi^{(n-1)}(x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x}_{i},\cdots,x_{n})\ .

(2.4)

where
\hat{x}_{i}
is ommitted, and
n\geqslant 0
.
In particular, denoting by
\{A,B\}=AB+BA
the anticommutator,
they satisfy the Canonical Anticommutation Relations (CAR)

\displaystyle\{a(f),a^{*}(g)\}=\left\langle f,g\right\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbf{T}}^{3})}\ ,\qquad\{a^{\flat}(f),a^{\flat}(g)\}=0\ ,

(2.5)

where
f,g\in L^{2}({\mathbf{T}}^{3})
, and
a^{\flat}(f)
stands for either
a(f)
or
a^{*}(f)
. It is well-known that from these relations both operators
become bounded and satisfy

\|a(f)\|=\|a^{*}(f)\|=\|f\|_{L^{2}}\ .

(2.6)

We now pass from the position to the momentum representation.
To this end, we recall
e_{k}(x)
corresponds to the plane-wave basis (see (
1.7
)), and we define for any
k\in\mathbf{Z}^{3}

a_{k}=a(e_{k})\ ,\qquad a_{k}^{*}=a^{*}(e_{k})\ .

(2.7)

The
CAR takes now reads
\{a_{k},a_{\ell}^{*}\}=\delta_{k,\ell}

and
\{a_{k}^{\flat},a_{\ell}^{\flat}\}=0
.
Note also
\|a_{k}\|=\|a_{k}^{*}\|=1\ .

We let
\mathcal{N}=\sum_{k\in\mathbf{Z}^{3}}a_{k}^{*}a_{k}

denote the standard number operator.

The original Hilbert space
\mathscr{H}=\mathscr{H}_{B}\otimes\mathscr{H}_{F}

is then naturally embedded into

\mathscr{H}_{B}\otimes\mathscr{F}
via the inclusion map.
The many-body
operator (
1.2
) can now be expressed
as the restriction
H=\mathcal{H}\upharpoonright\mathscr{H}

of the Hamiltonian

\mathcal{H}=h\otimes\mathds{1}+\mathds{1}\otimes\sum_{k\in\mathbf{Z}^{3}}k^{2}\,a_{k}^{*}a_{k}+\lambda\sum_{1\leqslant i\leqslant N}\sum_{k,\ell\in\mathbf{Z}^{3}}\widehat{V}(\ell-k)e^{-i(\ell-k)x_{i}}\otimes a_{\ell}^{*}a_{k}

(2.8)

defined on
\mathscr{H}_{B}\otimes\mathscr{F}
.

2.1.
The particle-hole transformation

In order to extract the relevant terms for the eigenvalues
\mu_{n}(H)

we introduce
a change of variables
which is referred to
as the
particle-hole transformation.
This correspond to the unitary map

\displaystyle\mathscr{R}:\mathscr{F}\rightarrow\mathscr{F}

(2.9)

defined
via its action on creation and annihilation operators
as

\displaystyle\mathscr{R}^{*}a_{k}\mathscr{R}=\chi^{\perp}(k)a_{k}+\chi(k)a_{k}^{*}\qquad\textnormal{and}\qquad\mathscr{R}^{*}a_{k}^{*}\mathscr{R}=\chi^{\perp}(k)a^{*}_{k}+\chi(k)a_{k}

(2.10)

and set the image of the Fermi ball
as the new vacuum
\mathscr{R}\Omega_{F}\equiv\Omega
.
Here and in the sequel we use the notation for the
characteristic functions for
k\in\mathbf{Z}^{3}

\chi(k)\equiv\mathds{1}(|k|\leqslant k_{F})\qquad\textnormal{and}\qquad\chi^{\perp}(k)\equiv\mathds{1}(|k|>k_{F})\ .

(2.11)

The map
\mathscr{R}
is trivially extended to

\mathscr{H}_{B}\otimes\mathscr{F}
by tensoring with the identity.
Finally, we introduce the following
new set of creation and annihilation operators
for
particles
and
holes
, respectively

\displaystyle b_{k}\equiv\chi^{\perp}(k)a_{k}\qquad\textnormal{and}\qquad c_{k}\equiv\chi(k)a_{k}.

(2.12)

They satisfy the following version of the CAR

\displaystyle\{b_{k},b_{\ell}^{*}\}=\,\chi^{\perp}(k)\,\delta_{k,\ell}\,\ ,\qquad\{c_{k},c_{\ell}^{*}\}=\,\chi(k)\,\delta_{k,\ell}\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\{b_{k}^{\flat},c_{\ell}^{\flat}\}=0

(2.13)

for all
k,\ell\in\mathbf{Z}^{3}
.
The main advantage of introducing the
new set of operators

\{b_{k}\}
and
\{c_{\ell}\}

is that they carry all the information
regarding momentum restrictions.

Let us now apply the particle-hole transformation
to the Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}
.
A straightforward calculation yields

\displaystyle\mathscr{R}^{*}\mathcal{H}\mathscr{R}=E_{F}+\lambda NM\hat{V}_{0}+\mathbb{H}\ .

(2.14)

Here the first term corresponds to the kinetic energy of the Fermi ball,
whereas the second term corresponds to an additional energy shift
due to its interaction with the bosons (a constant term due to translation invariance).
On the other hand, the operator
\mathbb{H}

corresponds to the Hamiltonian
describing the Bose gas and its interactions with the
excitations
of the Fermi ball.
It takes the form

\mathbb{H}=h\otimes\mathds{1}+\mathds{1}\otimes\mathbb{T}+\lambda\mathbb{V}\ .

(2.15)

The first term
is the Bose Hamiltonian (
1.3
). The second term describes the kinetic energy of the excitations, and is given by

\mathbb{T}=\sum_{k\in\mathbf{Z}^{3}}\Big(k^{2}b_{k}^{*}b_{k}-k^{2}c_{k}^{*}c_{k}\Big)\ .

(2.16)

The third term describes interspecies interactions
and admits the decomposition

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}=\mathbb{V}_{+}+\mathbb{V}_{-}+\mathbb{V}^{\textnormal{diag}}\ ,

(2.17)

where each contribution is given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}_{+}

\displaystyle\,=\,\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{p,k\in\mathbf{Z}^{3}}\widehat{V}(k)e^{-ik\cdot x_{i}}\otimes b_{p}^{*}c_{p+k}^{*}

(2.18)

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}_{-}

\displaystyle\,=\,\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{p,k\in\mathbf{Z}^{3}}\widehat{V}(k)e^{+ik\cdot x_{i}}\otimes c_{p+k}b_{p}

(2.19)

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}^{\rm diag}

\displaystyle\,=\,\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{\ell,k\in\mathbf{Z}^{3}}\widehat{V}(\ell-k)e^{-i(\ell-k)\cdot x_{i}}\otimes\Big(\,b_{\ell}^{*}b_{k}-\,c_{k}^{*}c_{\ell}\Big)\ .

(2.20)

Note
\mathbb{V}_{-}=(\mathbb{V}_{+})^{*}
.
Here, the notation is suggestive: Namely,

\mathbb{V}_{+}
creates a collective excitation
of particle-hole pairs due to interactions with the Bose system.
On the other hand
\mathbb{V}_{-}
destroys such excitations.
The diagonal term
\mathbb{V}^{\rm diag}

does not create new particle-hole pairs,
and in our analysis, it turns out as a lower order term.
We note the operators
b_{p},c_{\ell}

carry all of the notation regarding summation regions, see (
2.12
).

After application of
the particle-hole transformation, we can relate the original eigenvalues

\mu_{n}(H)
with those of the excitation Hamiltonian
\mathbb{H}
.
To this end,
we first introduce the number operators
on
\mathscr{H}_{B}\otimes\mathscr{F}

\mathcal{N}_{+}\equiv\mathds{1}\otimes\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\in\mathbf{Z}^{3}}\big(b_{k}^{*}b_{k}+c_{k}^{*}c_{k}\big)\qquad\textnormal{and}\qquad\mathcal{N}_{-}\equiv\mathds{1}\otimes\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\in\mathbf{Z}^{3}}\big(b_{k}^{*}b_{k}-c_{k}^{*}c_{k}\big)\ .

(2.21)

We refer to
\mathcal{N}_{-}
as the
charge operator

and its kernel
plays a major role in our analysis. It represents the subspace of states
with equal number of particles and holes.
Throughout this article, we will write

\mathscr{K}\equiv\ker(\mathcal{N}_{-})\subset\mathscr{H}_{B}\otimes\mathscr{F}\ .

(2.22)

We then observe that
\mathscr{K}=\mathscr{R}^{*}\mathscr{H}
, that is, it is the image of the original Hilbert space under the particle-hole transformation.
This follows from

\mathscr{R}^{*}\mathcal{N}\mathscr{R}=M+2\mathcal{N}_{-}

and the fact that
\mathcal{N}\upharpoonright\mathscr{H}=M
.
Thus, we now have that

\mu_{n}(H)=E_{F}+\lambda NM\hat{V}_{0}+\mu_{n}(\mathbb{H}\upharpoonright\mathscr{K})\ ,

(2.23)

for all
n\geqslant 1
.
We observe that the restriction to
\mathscr{K}

has the effect of making the energy
\mathbb{T}
non-negative.
Namely,
on
\mathscr{K}

we can add a term proportional to
\mathcal{N}_{-}

and write

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbf{Z}^{3}}\big|k^{2}-k_{F}^{2}\big|\,b_{k}^{*}b_{k}+\sum_{k\in\mathbf{Z}^{3}}\big|k^{2}-k_{F}^{2}\big|\,c_{k}^{*}c_{k}\ .

(2.24)

Unless confusion arises,
we ease the notation
and write

\mathbb{H}=\mathbb{H}\upharpoonright\mathscr{K}
.

As a concluding remark,
let us recall
that an advantage of
working with creation and annihilation operators
is the use of
pull-through formulas
.
In this article, we will mostly use the following version,
in which we use the
b
and
c
operators,
and the kinetic energy
\mathbb{T}

\displaystyle f(\mathbb{T})b_{k}^{*}=b_{k}^{*}f(\mathbb{T}+k^{2})\ ,\qquad

\displaystyle b_{k}f(\mathbb{T})=f(\mathbb{T}+k^{2})b_{k}\ ,

(2.25)

\displaystyle f(\mathbb{T})c_{k}^{*}=c_{k}^{*}f(\mathbb{T}-k^{2})\ ,

\displaystyle c_{k}f(\mathbb{T})=f(\mathbb{T}-k^{2})c_{k}\ ,

(2.26)

for any
k\in\mathbf{Z}^{3}
,
and any measurable function
f:{\mathbf{R}}\rightarrow{\mathbf{R}}.

They follow directly from the CAR and the functional calculus for
\mathbb{T}
.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1
and
2

3.1.
Proof of Theorem
1

First, let us turn to the proof of Theorem
1
.
As discussed in Section
2.1
,
up to subtraction of constant terms,
the eigenvalues of
H

are determined
by those of a Hamiltonian on Fock space, denoted by
\mathbb{H}
.
The operator
\mathbb{H}

describes the interactions between the (slow) bosonic subsystem and the (fast) excitations of the Fermi ball.
In particular, the asymptotics of its spectrum
can be analyzed in the framework of the theory described in Subsection
1.3
.
This argument is made rigorous in Section
5
.
It turns out that the resulting effective theory
is described by a

k_{F}
-dependent Hamiltonian

h_{k_{\rm F}}^{\textnormal{eff}}
on the bosonic space
\mathscr{H}_{\rm B}
.

In order to state its definition, we must introduce the following

lune sets

L(k)\equiv\{p\in\mathbf{Z}^{3}:|p|>k_{F}\,\textnormal{ and }\,|p-k|\leqslant k_{F}\}\ ,\qquad k\in\mathbf{Z}^{3}\ .

(3.1)

In particular, throughout this article we will consider various sums
suported on these sets.
These sums give rise to important coefficients, which enter the effective Hamiltonian.
Here, the relevant one is given by

\displaystyle D(k,k_{\rm F})\equiv\sum_{p\in L(k)}\frac{1}{p^{2}-(p-k)^{2}}\ ,\qquad k\in\mathbf{Z}^{3}\ .

(3.2)

Let us comment that the same resolvent sums
appear in the analysis of the ground state energy of interacting Fermi gases on the torus
{\mathbf{T}}^{3}
in the mean-field limit; see
[
25
,
17
,
16
,
15
,
18
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
.
In particular, we shall borrow certain estimates (and, prove new ones) which we state in Section
4
.
Now, the effective Hamiltonian takes the form

\displaystyle h_{k_{F}}^{\rm eff}\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{N}(-\Delta_{i})+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant N}(W-W_{k_{F}})(x_{i}-x_{j})

(3.3)

which is defined in terms of the effective potential defined on
{\mathbf{T}}^{3}

via the inverse Fourier formula

W_{k_{F}}(x)\equiv\frac{1}{2\pi k_{F}}\sum_{k\in\mathbf{Z}^{3}}e^{ikx}\,|\widehat{V}(k)|^{2}\,D(k,k_{F})\ .

(3.4)

In a second step, we compare the
eigenvalues of
h_{k_{\rm F}}^{\textnormal{eff}}
with those of
h^{\textnormal{eff}}
introduced in (
1.9
).
For this we use the asymptotics for the coefficients as
k_{\rm F}\rightarrow\infty

D(k,k_{\rm F})=2\pi k_{\rm F}+o(k_{\rm F})\ ,\qquad\forall k\in\mathbf{Z}^{3}/\{0\}\ .

(3.5)

In fact, we use a more precise version stated in Section
4
.
Note that, combined with (
3.4
),
this will give rise to the convolution structure
V*V
of the effective potential.
It is, however, necessary to isolate the zero-Fourier modes
which give rise to additional constant terms in the energy expansion for
\mu_{n}(H)
.

Before we turn to the proof
let us introduce the following
quantity

{\mathcal{Q}}_{p}\equiv 1+\|W\|_{L^{p}}^{2p/(2p-3)}+\|V\|_{H^{4}}^{2}\ .

(3.6)

We also recall
\mathscr{K}=\ker(\mathcal{N}_{-})
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Throughout this proof we fix
k_{F}\geqslant 1
and
N\geqslant 1
.

First step
. Recall that the effect of the conjugation with the particle-hole transformation
(
2.23
) is that we have

\mu_{n}(H)=E_{F}+\lambda NM\hat{V}_{0}+\mu_{n}(\mathbb{H})\ ,\qquad n\geqslant 1\ ,

(3.7)

where
\mathbb{H}=\mathbb{H}\upharpoonright\mathscr{K}
denotes the operator (
2.15
)
restricted to the subspace
\mathscr{K}
.

In Proposition
5.1
, we provide an upper bound for the eigenvalues of
\mathbb{H}
, while a corresponding lower bound is established in Proposition
5.2
.

These estimates imply that we can find a constant
C>0

such that
for all
1\leqslant n\leqslant N

\Big|\mu_{n}(\mathbb{H})-\mu_{n}(h^{\rm eff}_{k_{F}})+\tfrac{1}{2}W_{k_{\rm F}}(0)\Big|\leqslant C{\mathcal{Q}}_{p}^{2}N^{2}\,(\ln k_{\rm F})^{5/3}k_{\rm F}^{-1/3}

(3.8)

provided

k_{F}(\ln k_{F})^{-5}\geqslant C(nN\|V\|_{H^{4}}^{2})^{3}
.
Here
h_{k_{\rm F}}^{\textnormal{eff}}
is the effective Hamiltonian introduced in (
3.3
).
Thus, thanks to (
3.8
) it is sufficient to analyze the difference between

h_{k_{\rm F}}^{\textnormal{eff}}
and
h^{\textnormal{eff}}
, as well as the leading order term of
\tfrac{1}{2}W_{k_{\rm F}}(0).

Second step
.
Let us now compare the eigenvalues of
h_{k_{\rm F}}^{\textnormal{eff}}
and
h^{\textnormal{eff}}
.
To this end,
we will consider the Fourier representation for
x\in{\mathbf{T}}^{3}

(V*V)(x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbf{Z}^{3}}e^{ikx}\,|\widehat{V}(k)|^{2}\ .

(3.9)

Observe that
L(0)=\varnothing
and thus
D(0,k_{\rm F})=0
.
Hence, we can write for
x\in{\mathbf{T}}^{3}

\displaystyle W_{k_{F}}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\neq 0}e^{ikx}|\widehat{V}(k)|^{2}\frac{D(k,k_{F})}{2\pi k_{F}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\neq 0}e^{ikx}|\widehat{V}(k)|^{2}+\sum_{k\neq 0}e^{ikx}|\widehat{V}(k)|^{2}\Big(\frac{D(k,k_{F})}{2\pi k_{F}}-1\Big)

\displaystyle=(V*V)(x)-|\widehat{V}(0)|^{2}+\sum_{k\neq 0}e^{ikx}|\widehat{V}(k)|^{2}\Big(\frac{D(k,k_{F})}{2\pi k_{F}}-1\Big)

(3.10)

The very last sum sum over
k\in\mathbf{Z}^{3}/\{0\}

constitutes the main error term.
The summation may be split into two regions

0<|k|<2k_{F}

and

|k|\geqslant 2k_{F}
.
For the first region, we use (
4.2
) from Lemma
4.1
, whereas
for the second region we can use
(
4.5
) from Lemma
4.1
.
We obtain the inequalities

\displaystyle\bigg|\sum_{0<|k|<2k_{\rm F}}e^{ikx}|\widehat{V}(k)|^{2}\Big(\frac{D(k,k_{F})}{2\pi k_{F}}-1\Big)\bigg|

\displaystyle\ \leqslant\ C\sum_{0<|k|<2k_{\rm F}}|\widehat{V}(k)|^{2}|k|^{4}(\ln k_{F})^{5/3}k_{F}^{-1/3}

(3.11)

\displaystyle\bigg|\sum_{|k|\geqslant 2k_{\rm F}}e^{ikx}|\widehat{V}(k)|^{2}\Big(\frac{D(k,k_{F})}{2\pi k_{F}}-1\Big)\bigg|

\displaystyle\ \leqslant\ C\sum_{|k|\geqslant 2k_{\rm F}}|\widehat{V}(k)|^{2}\ \leqslant\ \frac{C}{k_{\rm F}^{4}}\sum_{k\in\mathbf{Z}^{3}}|\widehat{V}(k)|^{2}|k|^{4}\ .

(3.12)

These two estimates combined with (
3.10
)
imply the
L^{\infty}
estimate

\displaystyle\|W_{k_{F}}-(V*V)+|\widehat{V}_{0}|^{2}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbf{T}}^{d})}\leqslant C(\ln k_{\rm F})^{5/3}k_{\rm F}^{-1/3}\|V\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\ .

(3.13)

As a consequence,
summing over all particle possibilities
x_{i}-x_{j}
,
we have in the sense of quadratic forms
on
\mathscr{H}_{B}
that

\displaystyle\pm\big(h^{\textnormal{eff}}_{k_{\rm F}}-h^{\rm eff}+\tfrac{1}{2}(N-1)|\widehat{V}_{0}|^{2}\big)\leqslant CN(\ln k_{\rm F})^{5/3}k_{\rm F}^{-1/3}\|V\|_{H^{2}}^{2}

(3.14)

and thus, via the min-max principle
[
49
, Theorem 4.14]
, we obtain

\Big|\mu_{n}(h^{\rm eff}_{k_{F}})-\mu_{n}(h^{\rm eff})+\tfrac{1}{2}(N-1)|\widehat{V}_{0}|^{2}\Big|\leqslant CN(\ln k_{\rm F})^{5/3}k_{\rm F}^{-1/3}\|V\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\qquad\forall n\geqslant 1\ .

(3.15)

This controls the difference between the eigenvalues of
h^{\textnormal{eff}}
and
h_{k_{\rm F}}^{\textnormal{eff}}
.

Finally the bound (
3.13
)
at
x=0
also implies

W_{k_{\rm F}}(0)=\int_{{\mathbf{T}}^{3}}|V(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x-|\widehat{V}_{0}|^{2}+O((\ln k_{\rm F})^{5/3}k_{\rm F}^{-1/3}\|V\|_{H^{2}}^{2})\ .

(3.16)

Thus, we combine (
3.7
), (
3.8
) and (
3.15
) to finish the proof.
∎

The remainder of the article is essentially devoted to establishing the upper and lower bounds for
\mu_{n}(\mathbb{H})
given in Propositions
5.1
and
5.2
, respectively. The proofs of these results rely on a number of technical lemmas, including suitable sum estimates and operator bounds. These auxiliary results are stated in Section
4
. Before proceeding with this program, however, we provide the proof of Theorem
2
.

3.2.
Proof of Theorem
2

We note that by Theorem
1
and (
1.12
), the energy-per-boson is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(g)=\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\inf\sigma(h^{\rm eff}|_{g}),

(3.17)

and thus the analysis can be reduced
to studying the spectral properties
of the bosonic Hamiltonian
h^{\textnormal{eff}}
and its dependence on
g\in{\mathbf{R}}
; recall its definition in (
1.15
).
Let us immediately note that
\mathcal{E}(g)\leqslant\mathcal{E}(0)<\infty
thanks to (
1.16
).

For the proof, we show that whenever
w_{g}=w-g^{2}(v*v)

is negative at
x=0
, the energy-per-particle collapses to
-\infty
as
N\to\infty
.
Using a monotonicity argument, we prove the existence of the
smallest

g_{c}\geqslant 0
for which this collapse occurs.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Thanks to (
3.17
), it
suffices to look at
\inf\sigma(h^{\textnormal{eff}}|_{g})

and its dependence on
g
.

(1) Since
h^{\textnormal{eff}}|_{g}
depends only on
g^{2}
, we immediately obtain
\mathcal{E}(g)=\mathcal{E}(-g)
.
Next, observe that the map

[0,\infty)\ni g\mapsto\mathcal{E}(g)=\liminf\tfrac{1}{N}\inf\sigma(h^{\textnormal{eff}}|_{g})

is monotone decreasing.
To see this, let
g_{2}>g_{1}>0

and use

v*v\geqslant 0
:

\displaystyle(g_{2}^{2}-g_{1}^{2})\langle\Psi,\textstyle\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant N}v\ast v(N(x_{i}-x_{j}))\Psi\rangle\geqslant 0

(3.18)

for all

\Psi\in\mathscr{H}_{B}
.
Thus,
h^{\textnormal{eff}}|_{g=g_{1}}\geqslant h^{\textnormal{eff}}|_{g=g_{2}}

and we conclude that

\mathcal{E}(g_{1})\geqslant\mathcal{E}(g_{2})
for
g_{2}\geqslant g_{1}\geqslant 0
.

(2) Now, for the proof of the main statement within Theorem
2
, we set

g_{c}\equiv\inf\big\{g\geqslant 0:\liminf_{N\rightarrow\infty}N^{-1}\inf\sigma(h^{\textnormal{eff}}|_{g})=-\infty\big\}\in[0,\infty]\ .

(3.19)

Let us first show that
g_{c}<\infty
.
Indeed, since
(v*v)(0)=\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}>0

we have
w_{g}(0)<0
for all
g>g_{*}\equiv\frac{w(0)}{(v*v)(0)}
. We now claim that

\lim_{N\to\infty}N^{-1}\inf\sigma(h^{\textnormal{eff}}|_{g})=-\infty\qquad\textnormal{ for }g>g_{*}\ .

(3.20)

Fix
g>g_{*}
, and note that by continuity of
w_{g}
,
there exist
c_{0},r_{0}>0
so that

w_{g}(x)\leqslant-c_{0}<0

on the ball
B_{0}\equiv B(0,r_{0})
.
We now construct an appropriate trial state.
Take a radial symmetric function
\psi\in C_{0}^{2}({\mathbf{R}}^{3})
so that

\textnormal{supp}(\psi)\subset B(0,\frac{r_{0}}{2})
and
\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}=1
.
In particular,
\textnormal{supp}(|\psi|^{2}*|\psi|^{2})\,\subset\,B_{0}
.
We define

\psi_{N}(x)=N^{3/2}\psi(Nx)

and set
\Psi_{N}=\psi_{N}^{\otimes N}
so that

\left\langle\Psi_{N},\textstyle\sum_{1\leqslant i\leqslant N}(-\Delta_{i})\Psi_{N}\right\rangle\ =\ N^{3}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,.

(3.21)

On the other hand, using radial symmetry, we find for the potential energy

\displaystyle\left\langle\Psi_{N},\textstyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant N}N^{3}w_{g}(N(x_{i}-x_{j}))\Psi_{N}\right\rangle=N^{4}\textstyle\int_{B_{0}}(|\psi|^{2}\ast|\psi|^{2})w_{g}\,dx\leqslant-c_{0}\,N^{4}\ .

(3.22)

Thus

\left\langle\Psi_{N},h^{\rm eff}\Psi_{N}\right\rangle\leqslant-N^{4}(c_{0}-N^{-1}\|\nabla\psi\|^{2})

from which we conclude (
3.20
) for
g>g_{*}
.
Therefore,
g_{c}\leqslant g_{*}<\infty
.

(3) From the previous argument we already conclude

g_{*}\geqslant g_{c}
.
On the other hand, for
g\in\{0\}\cup(0,g_{0})
with
g_{0}=\inf\{g\geqslant 0:w_{g}\geqslant 0\}\geqslant 0
, the statement
\mathcal{E}(g)=4\pi\mathfrak{a}(g)
is a direct consequence of (
3.17
) and (
1.16
). ∎

4.
Auxiliary estimates

In this section we prove some necessary estimates
for the proof of the upper and lower bounds.
First, we state estimates for sums involving the free resolvent for particle-hole excitations.
Second, we prove some relevant operator inequalities.

4.1.
Sum estimates

Here and in the sequel,
we will consider various asymptotics of sums which are supported on the lune sets
L(k)
,
defined in (
3.1
).
The coefficients
D(k,k_{\rm F})
introduced in (
3.2
)
are the main example.
However, for our analysis it will be necessary to generalize these sums.
Namely,
for any
\alpha\geqslant 0
we let

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}(k,k_{F})\equiv\sum_{p\in L(k)}\frac{1}{(p^{2}-(p-k)^{2})^{\alpha}}\ ,\qquad k\in\mathbf{Z}^{3}\ .

(4.1)

Let us note that, by definition,
L(0)
is the empty set
and thus
\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}(0,k_{F})=0
.

Before we turn to these estimates, we
observe the following useful fact:
for all non-zero
k\in\mathbf{Z}^{3}
, we have

p^{2}-(p-k)^{2}\geqslant 1
for all
p\in L(k)
.
In particular, thanks to the crude geometric estimate
|L(k)|\leqslant Ck_{\rm F}^{3}

one has the bound
\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}(k,k_{\rm F})\leqslant Ck_{\rm F}^{3}
.

The following lemma contains
the main estimates that we use to analyze the coefficients
\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}
.
The inequalities for
\mathcal{D}_{1}=D
will be borrowed from
[
17
]
.
The ones for
\mathcal{D}_{2}
are—to our knowledge—new,
but can be obtained from the methods developed in
[
17
]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that the following holds
for all
k_{\rm F}\geqslant 1
.

(1)

For all
0<|k|<2k_{F}

\Big|\mathcal{D}_{1}(k,k_{\rm F})-2\pi k_{\rm F}\Big|\leqslant C|k|^{4}(\ln k_{\rm F})^{5/3}k_{\rm F}^{2/3}

(4.2)

(2)

For all

0<|k|<2k_{F}

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{1}(k,k_{F})

\displaystyle\ \leqslant\ C\,k_{F}\,\big(1+(\ln k_{F})^{\frac{5}{3}}k_{F}^{-\frac{1}{3}}\,|k|^{4}\big)

(4.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{2}(k,k_{F})

\displaystyle\ \leqslant\ C\,k_{F}^{\frac{2}{3}}\,(\ln k_{F})^{\frac{2}{3}}\,|k|^{4}\ .

(4.4)

(3)

For all

|k|\geqslant 2k_{F}

\mathcal{D}_{1}(k,k_{F})\ \leqslant\ C\,k_{F}^{3}|k|^{-2}\ .

(4.5)

Proof.

The inequality in (1) is taken from
[
17
, Proposition B.21]
.
The first inequality in (2) follows directly from the previous one.
The proof of the second inequality in (2) is more involved and postponed to Section
6
.
The inequality in (3) is taken from
[
17
, Proposition B.9]
.
∎

Our next goal is to combine the previous sum estimates
with decay properties of the Fourier coefficients
\widehat{V}(k)
.
To this end, let us introduce the following notation

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\alpha,\beta}(k_{F})

\displaystyle\equiv\textstyle\sum_{k\in\mathbf{Z}^{3}}|\widehat{V}(k)|^{2}\left\langle k\right\rangle^{2\beta}\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}(k,k_{F})

(4.6)

for
k_{F}\geqslant 1
and
\alpha,\beta\geqslant 0
.
Here,
\left\langle k\right\rangle\equiv(1+|k|^{2})^{1/2}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

There is
C>0
such that for all
k_{F}\geqslant 1
and
\beta\geqslant 0

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{1,\beta}(k_{F})

\displaystyle\,\leqslant\,C\,\|V\|_{H^{2+\beta}}^{2}\,k_{F}

(4.7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{2,\beta}(k_{F})

\displaystyle\,\leqslant\,C\,\|V\|_{H^{2+\beta}}^{2}\,k_{F}^{2/3}(\ln k_{F})^{5/3}\ .

(4.8)

Proof.

Fix
\beta\geqslant 0
and
k_{\rm F}\geqslant 1
, and write for short
\mathcal{S}_{\alpha,\beta}=\mathcal{S}_{\alpha,\beta}(k_{\rm F})
,

\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}(k)=\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}(k,k_{\rm F})
.
Denote
f(k)=|\widehat{V}(k)|^{2}\left\langle k\right\rangle^{2\beta}
.
We split the region of summation as

\mathcal{S}_{1}\ =\ \sum_{|k|<2k_{F}}f(k)\mathcal{D}_{1}(k)+\sum_{|k|\geqslant 2k_{F}}f(k)\mathcal{D}_{1}(k)

For the first region, we find with (
4.3
)

\displaystyle\sum_{|k|<2k_{F}}f(k)\mathcal{D}_{1}(k)

\displaystyle\ \leqslant\ Ck_{\rm F}\sum_{|k|<2k_{F}}f(k)\Big(1+\frac{(\ln k_{\rm F})^{5/3}}{k_{\rm F}^{1/3}}|k|^{4}\Big)\ \leqslant\ Ck_{\rm F}\sum_{k\in\mathbf{Z}^{3}}f(k)(1+|k|^{4})\ \leqslant\ Ck_{\rm F}\|V\|_{H^{\beta+2}}\ .

For the second region, we find thanks to (
4.5
)

\displaystyle\sum_{|k|\geqslant 2k_{F}}f(k)\mathcal{D}_{1}(k)\ \leqslant\ C\sum_{|k|\geqslant 2k_{F}}f(k)k_{\rm F}^{3}|k|^{-2}\ \leqslant\ Ck_{\rm F}\sum_{k\in\mathbf{Z}^{3}}f(k)\ =\ Ck_{\rm F}\|V\|_{H^{2\beta}}\ .

This finishes the proof for
\mathcal{S}_{1}
.

For the estimates regarding
\mathcal{S}_{2}

we split the sum into the same two regions.
For the small region
|k|<2k_{\rm F}
we use the bound (
4.4
) from Lemma
4.1
.
For the large region
|k|\geqslant 2k_{F}
.
we use the crude bound
\mathcal{\mathcal{D}}_{2}(k)\leqslant Ck_{F}^{3}
instead.
Thus, we find that for all
\gamma>0

there is
C>0

such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{2}

\displaystyle\ =\ \sum_{|k|<2k_{F}}f(k)\mathcal{D}_{2}(k)\ +\ \sum_{|k|\geqslant 2k_{F}}f(k)\mathcal{D}_{2}(k)

\displaystyle\ \leqslant\ Ck_{F}^{2/3}(\ln k_{F})^{5/3}\sum_{|k|<2k_{F}}f(k)|k|^{4}\ +\ Ck_{F}^{3-\gamma}\sum_{|k|\geqslant 2k_{F}}f(k)|k|^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\ \leqslant\ Ck_{F}^{2/3}(\ln k_{F})^{5/3}\|V\|_{H^{\beta+2}}\ +\ Ck_{F}^{3-\gamma}\|V\|_{H^{\beta+\gamma/2}}\ .

Setting
\gamma=7/3
completes the proof of the second bound.
∎

4.2.
Operator estimates

First, we prove that the number of excitations above the Fermi sea can be controlled by their kinetic energy.
Recall
\mathscr{K}=\ker(\mathcal{N}_{-})\subset\mathscr{H}_{B}\otimes\mathscr{F}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

For all
\Psi\in\mathscr{K}
, the following inequality holds

\left\langle\Psi,\mathcal{N}_{+}\Psi\right\rangle\ \leqslant\ \left\langle\Psi,\mathbb{T}\Psi\right\rangle\ .

(4.10)

Proof.

Let
n\in\mathbf{Z}
be the greatest integer
such that
n\leqslant k_{F}^{2}
.
Then, for all
p\in B^{c}
and
h\in B
it holds that:

p^{2}\geqslant n+1
and
h^{2}\leqslant n
.
Thus, from (
2.16
) we have the lower bound

\displaystyle\left\langle\Psi,\mathbb{T}\Psi\right\rangle

\displaystyle\ =\ \sum_{p\in B^{c}}p^{2}\|b_{p}\Psi\|^{2}-\sum_{h\in B}h^{2}\|c_{h}\Psi\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ \geqslant\ (n+1)\sum_{p\in B^{c}}\|b_{p}\Psi\|^{2}-n\sum_{h\in B}\|c_{h}\Psi\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ =\ \sum_{p\in B^{c}}\|b_{p}\Psi\|^{2}+n\Big(\sum_{p\in B^{c}}\|b_{p}\Psi\|^{2}-\sum_{h\in B}\|c_{h}\Psi\|^{2}\Big)\ .

(4.11)

Observe now that the term with
n

is proportional to
\left\langle\Psi,\mathcal{N}_{-}\Psi\right\rangle=0.

On the other hand

\langle\Psi,\sum_{p\in B^{c}}b_{p}^{*}b_{p}\Psi\rangle=\langle\Psi,(\mathcal{N}_{+}+\mathcal{N}_{-})\Psi\rangle
. This finishes the proof.
∎

Next, we will need an estimate for the diagonal operator
\mathbb{V}^{\textnormal{diag}}
.
In most cases, it can be safely regarded as a lower order term.

Lemma 4.4
.

For all
\Psi\in\mathscr{H}_{B}\otimes\mathscr{F}

|\left\langle\Psi,\mathbb{V}^{\rm diag}\Psi\right\rangle|\leqslant 2N\|\widehat{V}\|_{\ell^{1}}\left\langle\Psi,\mathcal{N}_{+}\Psi\right\rangle\ .

(4.12)

Proof.

The statement follows from

\displaystyle|\left\langle\Psi,\mathbb{V}^{\rm diag}\Psi\right\rangle|

\displaystyle\,\leqslant\,\tfrac{1}{2}N\sum_{\ell,k\in\mathbf{Z}^{3}}|\widehat{V}(\ell-k)|\Big(\|b_{\ell}\Psi\|^{2}+\|b_{k}\Psi\|^{2}+\|c_{\ell}\Psi\|^{2}+\|c_{k}\Psi\|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\,\leqslant\,2N\|\widehat{V}\|_{\ell^{1}}\|\mathcal{N}_{+}^{1/2}\Psi\|^{2}\ .

(4.13)

Here, we used that
\sup_{k}\sum_{\ell}|\widehat{V}(\ell-k)|=\|\widehat{V}\|_{\ell^{1}}
in the last line.
∎

The next lemma is a small modification of
[
25
, Lemma 4.7]
. It will be useful to bound the off-diagonal terms
\mathbb{V}_{-}
.

Lemma 4.5
.

There is a constant
C>0
such that for all
\alpha\geqslant 0
,
\varepsilon>0
and

\Psi\in\mathscr{K}

\displaystyle\lambda\sum_{k\in\mathbf{Z}^{3}}|\widehat{V}(k)|\sum_{p\in L(k)}\frac{\|c_{p-k}b_{p}\Psi\|}{(p^{2}-(p-k)^{2})^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}}\,\leqslant\,\varepsilon\left\langle\Psi,\mathbb{T}\Psi\right\rangle\,+\,\frac{C\,\lambda^{2}}{\varepsilon}\mathcal{S}_{\alpha,2}(k_{F}).

Proof.

Let

F(k)\equiv\sum_{p\in L(k)}(p^{2}-(p-k)^{2})\|c_{p-k}b_{p}\Psi\|^{2}

and recall

\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}(k)\equiv\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}(k,k_{\rm F})
was defined in (
4.1
).
First, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality over
p\in L(k)
shows that

\sum_{p\in L(k)}\frac{\|c_{p-k}b_{p}\Psi\|}{(p^{2}-(p-k)^{2})^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}}\leqslant F(k)^{1/2}\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}(k)^{1/2}\ .

(4.14)

Multiplying with
\lambda|\widehat{V}(k)|
, summing over
k\in\mathbf{Z}^{3}
,
and applying the Cauchy-Schwarz again,
we see
that for all
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0

\displaystyle\lambda\sum_{k\in\mathbf{Z}^{3}}|\widehat{V}(k)|\sum_{p\in L(k)}\frac{\|c_{p-k}b_{p}\Psi\|}{(p^{2}-(p-k)^{2})^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}}

\displaystyle\ \leqslant\ \lambda\sum_{k\in\mathbf{Z}^{3}}|\widehat{V}(k)|F(k)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}(k)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\ \leqslant\ \lambda\bigg(\sum_{k\in\mathbf{Z}^{3}}\left\langle k\right\rangle^{-4}F(k)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg(\sum_{k\in\mathbf{Z}^{3}}|\widehat{V}(k)|^{2}\left\langle k\right\rangle^{4}\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}(k)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\ \leqslant\ \frac{\varepsilon^{\prime}}{2}\sum_{k\in\mathbf{Z}^{3}}\left\langle k\right\rangle^{-4}F(k)\ +\ \frac{\lambda^{2}}{2\varepsilon^{\prime}}\,\mathcal{S}_{\alpha,2}(k_{F})\ .

We now estimate
\sup_{k\in\mathbf{Z}^{3}}F(k)
in terms of the kinetic energy.
Indeed, we obtain, thanks to the operator norm bounds

\|b\|=\|c\|=1

\displaystyle F(k)

\displaystyle\ =\ \sum_{p\in L(k)}\Big(p^{2}-k_{F}^{2}\Big)\|c_{p-k}b_{p}\Psi\|^{2}+\sum_{p\in L(k)}\Big(k_{F}^{2}-(p-k)^{2}\Big)\|c_{p-k}b_{p}\Psi\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ \leqslant\ \sum_{p\in B^{c}}\Big(p^{2}-k_{F}^{2}\Big)\|b_{p}\Psi\|^{2}+\sum_{h\in B}\Big(k_{F}^{2}-(p-k)^{2}\Big)\|c_{h}\Psi\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ \leqslant\ \|\mathbb{T}^{1/2}\Psi\|^{2}

(4.15)

thanks
to the representation
(
2.24
) in the zero charge subspace.
This finishes the proof by setting

\varepsilon^{\prime}=2\varepsilon/C

for
C=\sum_{k\in\mathbf{Z}^{3}}\left\langle k\right\rangle^{-4}
.
∎

Finally, we establish the following bound on the kinetic energy of the particle-hole excitations. By Lemma
4.3
, this immediately implies a bound for
\langle\Psi,\mathcal{N}_{+}\Psi\rangle
as well.

Lemma 4.6
.

There is a constant
C>0

such that for all
\delta>0

and
all

\Psi\in\mathscr{K}

\displaystyle\langle\Psi,\mathbb{T}\Psi\rangle\,\leqslant\,(1+\delta)\,\langle\Psi,\mathbb{H}\Psi\rangle+CN\left(\delta^{-1}\|V\|_{H^{4}}^{2}+\|W\|_{L^{p}}^{\frac{2p}{2p-3}}\right)\|\Psi\|^{2}\ .

(4.16)

provided
k_{F}\geqslant C\delta^{-2}N\|\widehat{V}\|_{\ell^{1}}^{2}
.

Proof.

Let
\delta>0
. Without loss of generality, we assume thrugh the proof that
\|\Psi\|=1
.
From the definition of
\mathbb{H}
we find

\left\langle\Psi,\mathbb{T}\Psi\right\rangle=\left\langle\Psi,\mathbb{H}\Psi\right\rangle-\lambda\left\langle\Psi,\mathbb{V}^{\rm diag}\Psi\right\rangle-2\lambda\mathrm{Re}\left\langle\Psi,\mathbb{V}^{-}\Psi\right\rangle\,-\left\langle\Psi,h\otimes\mathds{1}\Psi\right\rangle\ .

(4.17)

We analyze each term separately. First, for the diagonal part,
we use Lemmas
4.4
and
4.3
to bound

\displaystyle\lambda\,|\left\langle\Psi,\mathbb{V}^{\rm diag}\Psi\right\rangle|\ \leqslant\ 2N^{1/2}k_{\rm F}^{-1/2}\|\widehat{V}\|_{\ell^{1}}\left\langle\Psi,\mathbb{T}\Psi\right\rangle\ \leqslant\ \delta\left\langle\Psi,\mathbb{T}\Psi\right\rangle

(4.18)

where the last inequality holds for
k_{F}\geqslant 4N\|\widehat{V}\|_{\ell^{1}}^{2}\delta^{-2}
.
Secondly, for the off-diagonal part,
starting from the definition of
\mathbb{V}^{-}
in (
2.19
)
we use the triangle inequality,
Lemma
4.5
for
\alpha=1
,
\beta=2
and
\varepsilon=\frac{\delta}{N}
,
and
then (
4.7
) from Lemma
4.2
to find

\displaystyle\lambda\ |\!\left\langle\Psi,\mathbb{V}^{-}\Psi\right\rangle\!|

\displaystyle\leqslant\lambda N\textstyle\sum_{k\in\mathbf{Z}^{3}}|\widehat{V}(k)|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in L(k)\end{subarray}}\|c_{p-k}b_{p}\Psi\|

\displaystyle\leqslant N\varepsilon\left\langle\Psi,\mathbb{T}\Psi\right\rangle\,+\,C\lambda^{2}N\varepsilon^{-1}\mathcal{S}_{1,2}(k_{F})

\displaystyle\leqslant N\varepsilon\left\langle\Psi,\mathbb{T}\Psi\right\rangle\,+\,C\lambda^{2}Nk_{\rm F}\varepsilon^{-1}\|V\|_{H^{4}}=\delta\left\langle\Psi,\mathbb{T}\Psi\right\rangle+C\delta^{-1}N\|V\|_{H^{4}}\ .

(4.19)

Recall
\lambda^{2}Nk_{\rm F}=4\pi
is our choice of scaling.
Finally, for the bosonic Hamiltonian we
can easily deduce from Lemma
A.1
that

\left\langle\Psi,h\otimes\mathds{1}\Psi\right\rangle\geqslant-CN\|W\|_{L^{p}}^{\frac{2p}{2p-3}}

(4.20)

thanks to the non-negativity of the free kinetic energy.
We combine the last four inequalities and re-define
\delta\mapsto\delta/10
if necessary to finish the proof.
∎

5.
Upper and lower bounds

In this section, we complete the proof of the expansion
for the eigenvalues of
\mathbb{H}

5.1.
Estimates for the upper bound

In this section, we prove the following proposition
containing the upper bounds.

Proposition 5.1
.

There exists a constant
C>0

such that
for all
k_{F}\geqslant 1
,

\displaystyle\mu_{n}(\mathbb{H})

\displaystyle\,\leqslant\,\mu_{n}(h_{k_{F}}^{\textnormal{eff}})\,-\,\tfrac{1}{2}\|V\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,+\,C{\mathcal{Q}}^{2}N^{2}(\ln k_{F})^{5/3}k_{\rm F}^{-1/3}

\mathbb{R}\equiv\mathbb{Q}_{\Omega}\mathbb{T}^{-1}\mathbb{Q}_{\Omega}

The following lemma will be useful to compute

Essentially, we gain a factor
k_{\rm F}^{-1/3}

relative to the leading order terms
k_{\rm F}

thanks to the
double
resolvent in the inner product.
The latter can then be expressed a sum over lune sets of the form analyzed in

Lemma 5.1
.

There is
C>0
such that for all

k_{F}\geqslant 1
,
N\geqslant 1

and

\Phi\in\mathscr{H}_{B}

\|\mathbb{R}\mathbb{V}_{+}\Phi\otimes\Omega\|^{2}\leqslant CN^{2}\|V\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\,\|\Phi\|^{2}\,k_{F}^{2/3}(\ln k_{F})^{5/3}\,\ .

(5.3)

Proof.

From the definition (
2.18
)
and the

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{R}\mathbb{V}_{+}\Phi\otimes\Omega\|^{2}=\left\langle\Phi\otimes\Omega,\mathbb{V}_{-}\mathbb{R}^{2}\mathbb{V}_{+}\Phi\otimes\Omega\right\rangle=\sum_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant N}\sum_{k\in\mathbf{Z}^{3}}|\widehat{V}(k)|^{2}\sum_{p\in L(k)}\frac{\left\langle\Phi,e^{ik\cdot(x_{i}-x_{j})}\Phi\right\rangle_{L^{2}}}{(p^{2}-(p-k)^{2})^{2}}\ .

Therefore, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality readily implies

\|\mathbb{R}\mathbb{V}_{+}\Phi\otimes\Omega\|^{2}\leqslant N^{2}\|\Phi\|^{2}\sum_{k\in\mathbf{Z}^{3}}\frac{|\widehat{V}(k)|^{2}}{(p^{2}-(p-k)^{2})^{2}}\ .

.
This estimate gives the desired inequality.

Estimates for

.

That is, from Lemma

\displaystyle\ =\ \lambda^{2}\left\langle\mathbb{R}\mathbb{V}_{+}\Psi_{0},h\ \mathbb{R}\mathbb{V}_{+}\Psi_{0}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{R}\mathbb{V}_{+}\Psi_{0}\|^{2}\,\leqslant\,CN^{2}(\ln k_{\rm F})^{5/3}k_{\rm F}^{2/3}\|V\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\|\Phi\|^{2}\,.

•

The first term in (

.

and

.

R>0
and take
k_{\rm F}\geqslant 1
large enough so that

.

by assumption. Moreover, using Lemma

\displaystyle|\langle\Psi_{i},\Psi_{j}\rangle|

\displaystyle\ \leqslant\ \lambda^{2}\|\mathbb{R}\mathbb{V}^{+}\Phi_{i}\otimes\Omega\|\,\|\mathbb{R}\mathbb{V}^{+}\Phi_{j}\otimes\Omega\|\ \leqslant\ C_{0}N\|V\|_{H^{2}}^{2}(\ln k_{\rm F})^{5/3}k_{\rm F}^{-1/3},

Proposition 5.2

(1) First, we remove the diagonal term

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}\,\geqslant\,h+\mathbb{T}+\lambda\mathbb{V}^{+}+\lambda\mathbb{V}^{-}-{CN^{1/2}}k_{\rm F}^{-1/2}\|\widehat{V}\|_{\ell^{1}}\mathbb{T}\ .

(5.28)

Thus, it is sufficient

By completing the square, we find

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}+\lambda\mathbb{V}^{-}+\lambda\mathbb{V}^{+}

\displaystyle=\Big(\mathbb{T}^{1/2}+\lambda\mathbb{T}^{-1/2}\mathbb{V}^{+}\Big)^{*}\Big(\mathbb{T}^{1/2}+\lambda\mathbb{T}^{-1/2}\mathbb{V}^{+}\Big)-\lambda^{2}\mathbb{V}^{-}\mathbb{T}^{-1}\mathbb{V}^{+}

Next, we compute

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}^{-}\mathbb{T}^{-1}\mathbb{V}^{+}\ =\ \sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\sum_{k,\ell\in\mathbf{Z}^{3}}\overline{v_{i}(k)}v_{j}(\ell)\,\sum_{p,q\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}b_{p}c_{p-k}\mathbb{T}^{-1}c_{q-\ell}^{*}b_{q}^{*},

We then use the pull-through formula

\displaystyle b_{p}c_{p-k}c_{q-\ell}^{*}b_{q}^{*}

\displaystyle A_{2}

\displaystyle=-\ \sum_{i,j}\sum_{k,\ell}\ \overline{v_{i}(k)}v_{j}(\ell)\sum_{p-k\in B}(\mathbb{T}+E_{p}-E_{p-k})^{-\frac{1}{2}}b_{p-k+\ell}^{*}b_{p}(\mathbb{T}+E_{p-k+\ell}-E_{p-k})^{-\frac{1}{2}}

The main purpose

In order to prove the statement, we shall

(6.1)

as well as the relevant singular denominators

\lambda_{p,k}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\big(|p|^{2}-|p-k|^{2}\big)\ ,\qquad p,k\in\mathbf{Z}^{3}\ .

(6.2)

Then, for any function

Proposition 6.1

(
[
17
]

.

For all

with

•

as the positive number

•

as the least integer such that:

\frac{|k|}{2}<\ell m_{*}

•

as the greatest integer

•

Then, as

\displaystyle\sum_{p\in L(k)}f(\lambda_{p,k})

\displaystyle\ =\ 2\pi|k|\sum_{m_{*}\leqslant m\leqslant M}f\bigg(|k|(\ell m-\frac{|k|}{2})\bigg)\Big(\ell m-\frac{|k|}{2}\Big)\ell

(6.3)

\displaystyle\ \ +\ \pi\sum_{M+1\leqslant m\leqslant M_{*}}f\bigg(|k|(\ell m-\frac{|k|}{2})\bigg)\Big(k_{F}^{2}-(\ell m-k)^{2}\Big)\ell

(6.4)

\displaystyle\ \ +\ O\bigg(|k|^{11/3}\ln(k_{F})k_{F}^{\frac{2}{3}}\sum_{m_{*}\leqslant m\leqslant M_{*}}f\Big(|k|(\ell m-\frac{|k|}{2})\Big)\bigg)

(6.5)

Remark 6.1
.

The following observations follow from the definition
of the parameters

,
m_{*}
,
M
and

and will become helpful in the upcoming calculations:

\ell\leqslant 1\ ,\ \qquad\ell m_{*}\leqslant\frac{|k|}{2}+\ell\qquad\ell m_{*}-\frac{|k|}{2}\geqslant\frac{1}{2|k|}\ ,\ \qquad k_{F}-\ell\leqslant\ell M\ ,\ \qquad k_{F}+|k|-\ell\leqslant\ell M_{*}\ .

It follows from

The next proof follows closely that of

Proof of Lemma

.

(Inequality (

First, we observe that after a change of variables

for a given

\sum_{n=a}^{b}f(n)\leqslant f(b)+\int_{a}^{b}f(x)dx

for decreasing functions

\displaystyle T_{1}

\displaystyle\ \equiv\ 2\pi|k|\sum_{m_{*}\leqslant m\leqslant M}\frac{(\ell m-\frac{|k|}{2})\ell}{|k|^{2}(\ell m-\frac{|k|}{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle\ =\ \frac{2\pi\ell}{|k|}\sum_{m_{*}\leqslant m\leqslant M}\frac{1}{(\ell m-\frac{|k|}{2})}

\displaystyle\ \leqslant\ \frac{2\pi\ell}{|k|}\bigg(\frac{1}{(\ell M-\frac{|k|}{2})}+\int_{m_{*}}^{M}\frac{\mathrm{d}m}{(\ell m-\frac{|k|}{2})}\bigg)

Next, we use the various inequalities from Remark

Appendix A
The bosonic quadratic form

Let
h_{0}\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{N}(-\Delta_{x_{i}})

be the free operator with form domain

Q(h_{0})=H^{1}({\mathbf{T}}^{3N})\cap\mathscr{H}_{B}
,
and let

be the bosonic Hamiltonian,
defined initially on
C^{\infty}({\mathbf{T}}^{3})\cap\mathscr{H}_{B}
.

Lemma A.1
.

Assume
w\in L^{p}({\mathbf{T}}^{3})
for some
3/2<p\leqslant\infty
.
Then,
there are
a,c,q>0
(depending only on
p
)
such that for all
\varepsilon>0
and

\Psi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbf{T}}^{3})\cap\mathscr{H}_{B}

(1-\varepsilon)\left\langle\Psi,h_{0}\Psi\right\rangle-\frac{cN}{\varepsilon^{a}}\|w\|^{q}_{L^{p}}\|\Psi\|^{2}\leqslant\left\langle\Psi,h\Psi\right\rangle\leqslant(1+\varepsilon)\left\langle\Psi,h_{0}\Psi\right\rangle+\frac{cN}{\varepsilon^{a}}\|w\|^{q}_{L^{p}}\|\Psi\|^{2}\ .

(A.1)

In particular,
h
has a unique self-adjoint extension on
\mathscr{H}_{B}
with form domain

Q(h_{0})
and (
A.1
)
holds for all
\Psi\in Q(h_{0})
.

Remark A.1
.

From the proof, one may compute
q=\frac{2p}{2p-3}
.

Proof.

Consider first
\varphi\in H^{1}({\mathbf{T}}^{3}_{x}\times{\mathbf{T}}^{3}_{y})
.
For
\tfrac{3}{2}<p\leqslant\infty

let
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}
be the dual exponent, with

1\leqslant p^{\prime}<3
.
Thanks to Hölder’s inequality,
L^{p}
interpolation and the
Sobolev embedding
H^{1}({\mathbf{T}}^{3})\hookrightarrow L^{6}({\mathbf{T}}^{3})
, we have
for
\varphi_{x}(y)=\varphi(x,y)

\displaystyle\iint w(x-y)|\varphi(x,y)|^{2}dydx

\displaystyle\leqslant\int\|w\|_{L^{p}}\|\varphi_{x}\|_{L^{2p^{\prime}}}^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leqslant C\|w\|_{L^{p}}\int\|\varphi_{x}\|^{2\theta_{1}}_{L^{2}}\|\varphi_{x}\|_{L^{6}}^{2\theta_{2}}dx\leqslant C\|w\|_{L^{p}}\int\|\varphi_{x}\|^{2\theta_{1}}_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\varphi_{x}\|_{L^{2}}^{2\theta_{2}}dx\ .

Here
\theta_{1}=\frac{2}{2p^{\prime}}\frac{6-2p^{\prime}}{6-2}=\frac{2p-3}{2p}

and

\theta_{2}=\frac{6}{2p^{\prime}}\frac{2p^{\prime}-2}{6-2}=\frac{3}{2p}

are found by interpolation.
Thus, we may apply Young’s inequality to find
there is
C=C(p)>0

such that for all
\varepsilon>0

\int\int w(x-y)|\varphi(x,y)|^{2}dydx\leqslant\varepsilon\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\varepsilon^{-\theta_{2}/\theta_{1}}\|w\|_{L^{p}}^{1/\theta_{1}}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

(A.2)

Next, we turn to the proof of the lemma. Thanks to permutational symmetry, it suffices to estimate the quadratic form

\left\langle\Psi,\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i<j}w(x_{i}-x_{j})\Psi\right\rangle=\frac{N-1}{2}\int dx_{3}\cdots dx_{N}\int dx_{1}dx_{2}w(x_{1}-x_{2})|\psi(x_{1},x_{2})|^{2}dx_{1}dx_{2}

with

,
with constant

has a unique self-adjoint extension,
and the desired inequality follows by a standard approximation argument.

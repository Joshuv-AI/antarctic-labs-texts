The graph ∞ \infty -Laplacian eigenvalue problem Thanks:
Piero Deidda, Martin Burger, Mario Putti (), Francesco Tudisco

Abstract
We analyze various formulations of the ∞ \infty -Laplacian eigenvalue problem on graphs, comparing their properties and highlighting their respective advantages and limitations. First, we investigate the graph ∞ \infty -eigenpairs arising as limits of p p -Laplacian eigenpairs, extending key results from the continuous setting to the discrete domain. We prove that every limit of p p -Laplacian eigenpair, for p p going to ∞ \infty , satisfies a limit eigenvalue equation and establish that the corresponding eigenvalue can be bounded from below by the packing radius of the graph, indexed by the number of nodal domains induced by the eigenfunction. Additionally, we show that the limits, for p p going to ∞ \infty , of the variational p p -Laplacian eigenvalues are bounded both from above and from below by the packing radii, achieving equality for the smallest two variational eigenvalues and corresponding packing radii of the graph. In the second part of the paper, we introduce generalized ∞ \infty -Laplacian eigenpairs as generalized critical points and values of the ∞ \infty -Rayleigh quotient. We prove that the generalized variational ∞ \infty -eigenvalues equal the limit of the p p -Laplacian variational eigenvalues and so satisfy the same upper bounds in terms of packing radii. Finally , we establish that any solution to the limit eigenvalue equation is also a generalized eigenpair, while any generalized eigenpair satisfies the limit eigenvalue equation on a suitable subgraph.

,

.
In particular, we refer to

2.1
Graph setting and
p
-Laplacian operators

We identify a graph
\mathcal{G}
with a triplet
\mathcal{G}=(V,E,\omega)
, where
V
denotes the set of nodes,
E\subset V\times V
the set of edges, and
\omega:E\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a weight on the edges. We identify an edge
(u,v)
by the two nodes
u,v\in V
that form its endpoints. In particular, the notation
u\sim v
means that
(u,v)\in E
. A path
\Gamma
connecting two nodes
v_{0},v_{n}\in V
on the graph is a set of edges that connect
v_{0}
with
v_{n}
, i.e.,
\Gamma=\{v_{0}\sim v_{1}\sim\dots\sim v_{n}\}
. We say that a graph is connected if given any two nodes
u,v\in V
, there exists a path that joins them. Since we are only interested in undirected graphs, throughout the paper we assume that whenever
(u,v)\in E
then also
(v,u)\in E
and
\omega(u,v)=\omega(v,u)
. To simplify the notation, in the following we write
\omega_{uv}:=\omega(u,v)
. Moreover, we assume that
\omega_{uv}
can be interpreted as the inverse of the edge length leading to the following notion of edge length, and consequently, distance between two nodes.

Definition 1
(Length of a path and distance between two nodes).

The length of a path
\Gamma
is given by the sum of the reciprocals of the weights of the edges forming
\Gamma
,

\mathrm{length}(\Gamma)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\omega_{v_{i-1}v_{i}}}\,.

The distance between two nodes
u,v\in V
is defined as the length of the shortest path connecting
u
and
v
,

d(u,v)=\min\Big\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{v_{i-1}v_{i}}^{-1}\,:n\in\mathbb{N},\;u=v_{0}\sim\dots\sim v_{n}=v\Big\}\,.

(4)

Throughout the manuscript, if not otherwise specified, we use capital letters (latin or greek) to denote edge functions and lowercase letters to denote node functions.

We proceed now by introducing discrete analogues of the continuous differential operators. The gradient of a function
f
defined on the nodes,
f:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, is the edge function
\nabla_{\omega}f(u,v)
given by

\nabla_{\omega}f(u,v)=\omega_{uv}\big(f(v)-f(u)\big)\,,

(5)

for
(u,v)\in E
.
Note that the linear operator
\nabla_{\omega}
is represented by an
|E|\times|V|
matrix called the oriented incidence matrix of the graph.
Moreover, the assumption that the graph is undirected implies
\nabla_{\omega}f(u,v)=-\nabla_{\omega}f(v,u)
for any
(u,v)\in E
.
The set of the gradients of
f
outgoing from node
u
, is called the local gradient
\left\{\nabla_{\omega}f\right\}(u)
of
f
at
u
:

\left\{\nabla_{\omega}f\right\}(u):=\{\nabla_{\omega}f(u,v)\;|\;v\sim u\}\,.

Note that, differently from the gradient operator in the continuous case, the cardinality of the local gradient is not fixed but depends on the degree of the node.
Next, given an edge function
G:E\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, we introduce its divergence as the node function defined by the following expression:

{\operatorname{div}G}(u)=-\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{\omega}^{T}G(u)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v\sim u}\omega_{uv}\left(G(u,v)-G(v,u)\right)

(6)

Note that the above defined divergence operator satisfies the following “integration by parts” formula with respect to the scalar products
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{E}
and
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{V}
defined on the space of edge and node functions:

\left\langle G,\nabla_{\omega}f\right\rangle_{E}:=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{(u,v)\in E}G(u,v)\nabla_{\omega}f(u,v)=\sum_{u\in V}-{\operatorname{div}G}(u)f(u)=:\left\langle-{\operatorname{div}G},f\right\rangle_{V}\;.

(7)

Observe that from the definition of the corresponding scalar products defined in (
7
), we can define the
p
-norms on the edge and node spaces as:

\left\|G\right\|_{p,E}^{p}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{(u,v)\in E}|G(u,v)|^{p}\qquad\left\|f\right\|_{p,V}^{p}=\sum_{u\in V}|f(u)|^{p}\;,

(8)

where we consider a one-half factor in edge-functions norms to take into account the fact that every edge has two possible directions and so it is counted twice.
Finally, borrowing from
[
53
,
18
,
6
]
, we define the
p
-Laplace operator as follows:

Definition 2
(Discrete
p
-Laplace Operator).

The discrete
p
-Laplace operator is given by:

\Delta_{p}f(u):=-\operatorname{div}(|\nabla_{\omega}f|^{p-2}\odot\nabla_{\omega}f)=\sum_{v\sim u}\omega_{uv}^{p}|f(u)-f(v)|^{p-2}\left(f(u)-f(v)\right)\,,

where
v\sim u
means that
(u,v)\in E
, the absolute value in
|\nabla_{\omega}f|
is considered entry-wise, and “
\odot
” denotes the Hadamard or entry-wise product.

Next, we introduce the discrete counterpart of the homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions. To this aim, we designate an arbitrary subset of the nodes
B\subset V
as the boundary of
\mathcal{G}
and consider the set of node functions that are zero on
B

\mathcal{H}_{0}(V):=\{f\,|\;f(u)=0\;\forall u\in B\}
.
From the study of the critical points of the
p
-Rayleigh quotient
\mathcal{R}_{p}:\mathcal{H}_{0}(V)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
given by

\mathcal{R}_{p}(f)=\frac{\left\|\nabla_{\omega}f\right\|_{p}}{\left\|f\right\|_{p}}

(9)

we can write the discrete equivalent of the

-Laplacian eigenvalue equation with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions
[

as:

\begin{cases}\Delta_{p}f(u)=\lambda|f|^{p-2}(u)f(u)\quad&u\in V_{\text{I}}\\
f(u)=0&u\in B\end{cases}\,.

(10)

If
B\neq\emptyset
, we can introduce the distance from the boundary given by:

d_{B}(u)=\min_{v\in B}d(u,v)\,.

(11)

In particular, if a graph has nonempty boundary we denote by
V_{\text{I}}:=V\setminus B
the set of internal nodes and we say that the graph is connected if the subgraph induced by the internal nodes is connected. If not otherwise stated and independently of the presence of the boundary, throughout the paper we assume a graph to be connected.
We define the
p
-Laplacian variational eigenvalues
\lambda_{k}(\Delta_{p})
as in

and write:

\lambda_{k}(\Delta_{p})=\min_{A\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(S_{p})}\max_{f\in A}\mathcal{R}_{p}^{p}(f)\,,

(12)

where
S_{p}:=\{f\in\mathcal{H}_{0}(V)|\;\|f\|_{p}=1\}
and
\mathcal{F}_{k}(S_{p})
is the set of the closed and symmetric subsets of
S_{p}
with Krasnoselskii genus greater than or equal to

. We recall that the Krasnoselskii genus of a closed symmetric set

[

,

,

]
:

Definition 3
(Krasnoselskii Genus).

The Krasnoselskii Genus

of a closed symmetric set

\gamma(A):=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $A=\emptyset$}\\
\inf\left\{h\in\mathbb{N}\,:\,\exists\,\varphi\in C(A,\mathbb{R}^{h}\setminus\{0\})\text{ s.t. }\varphi(x)=-\varphi(-x)\right\}&\\
\infty\quad&\text{if $\nexists\;h$ as above}\end{cases}.

(13)

Given the
\infty
-norms of a node function
f
and of its gradient:

\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}=\max_{u\in V}\{|f(u)|\}\,,\qquad\left\|\nabla_{\omega}f\right\|_{\infty}=\max_{(u,v)\in E}\{|\nabla_{\omega}f(u,v)|\},

(14)

we can introduce the maximal node and edge sets:

\displaystyle V_{\text{max}}(f)

\displaystyle=\{u\in V\>:\>|f(u)|=\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}\}\,,

\displaystyle E_{\text{max}}(f)

\displaystyle=\{(u,v)\in E\>:\>|\nabla_{\omega}f(u,v)|=\left\|\nabla_{\omega}f\right\|_{\infty}\}\,.

\displaystyle\Delta_{p}f(u):=

\displaystyle=

3
The
\infty
-limit eigenvalue problem

In this section we start discussing the
\infty
-Laplacian eigenvalue problem. In particular we concentrate on the “
\infty
-limit eigenvalue problem”, i.e. we consider the limit as
p\rightarrow\infty
of the
p
-Laplacian eigenvalue equation and we discuss its solutions. This approach has been thoroughly investigated in the continuous setting
[
37
,
36
,
42
,
41
,
25
,
54
]
. Here, we start by recalling the main results in the continuous setting and then go on to prove analogous results in the discrete setting. We point out that, even if the results in the two settings are equivalent, the approaches to obtain them are different.
We start by observing that, in both continuous and discrete settings, the limit of appropriate subsequences of
p
-Laplacian eigenpairs
(f(\Delta_{p}),\lambda(\Delta_{p}))
leads to an
\infty
-eigenfunction whose corresponding eigenvalue can be expressed formally as:

\Lambda=``\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\big(\lambda(\Delta_{p})\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,"\,.

(16)

In particular, in the discrete setting
we know that the set
\{\lambda^{\frac{1}{p}}(\Delta_{p})\}_{p}
is bounded
[
18
]
and hence we can extract from it at least one convergent subsequence.

In particular, as a consequence of Lemma 4.2 in
[
20
]
, for
1\leq p\leq q
and any
f\in\mathcal{H}_{0}(V)
it holds

\left(|E|/2\right)^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}\mathcal{R}_{p}(f)\leq\mathcal{R}_{q}(f)\leq|V_{\text{I}}|^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}\mathcal{R}_{p}(f),

(17)

where
|V_{\text{I}}|
and
|E|
correspond to the cardinality of the sets of internal nodes and edges, respectively.
Analogously to (
12
), we define

\Lambda_{k}(\Delta_{\infty}):=\min_{A\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(S_{\infty})}\max_{f\in A}\mathcal{R}_{\infty}(f)\,,

(18)

where
\mathcal{F}_{k}(S_{\infty})
is the set of the closed and symmetric subsets of
S_{\infty}
with Krasnoselskii genus greater than or equal to
k
.
Then as a consequence of (
17
) we have

\left(|E|/2\right)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\lambda_{k}(\Delta_{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\lambda_{k}(\Delta_{q})^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq|V_{\text{I}}|^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}\lambda_{k}(\Delta_{p})^{\frac{1}{p}},

(19)

which holds also for
q=\infty
by replacing
\lambda_{k}(\Delta_{q})^{1/q}
with
\Lambda_{k}(\Delta_{\infty})
.
The last equation proves that the limit of the variational eigenvalues exists and is given by:

\lim_{p\to\infty}\lambda_{k}(\Delta_{p})^{1/p}=\Lambda_{k}(\Delta_{\infty}).

(20)

Additionally, eq. (
19
) shows that

|\lambda_{k}(\Delta_{p})^{1/p}-\Lambda_{k}(\Delta_{\infty})|\leq C\max\{(1-\left(|E|/2\right)^{-\frac{1}{p}}),(|V_{\text{I}}|^{\frac{1}{p}}-1)\},

where
C
bounds
\lambda_{k}(\Delta_{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}
. Note that the last inequality also states that the variational eigenvalues converge at least as
O(1/p)
.

In the continuous setting, the limit of the
p
-eigenpairs can be studied by considering the
\infty
-eigenvalue equation for a general eigenvalue
\Lambda
, see
[
36
]
:

0=\begin{cases}\min\{|\nabla_{\omega}f|-\Lambda f,\Delta_{\infty}f\}\quad&\mathrm{if}\;f>0\\
\Delta_{\infty}f&\mathrm{if}\;f=0\\
\max\{-|\nabla_{\omega}f|-\Lambda f,\Delta_{\infty}f\}\quad&\mathrm{if}\;f<0\end{cases}\,,

(21)

where the above equation has to be understood in the sense of viscosity solutions (see
[
37
]
for a short and clear discussion about viscosity solutions in this setting).
In particular it is possible to show that if
(f,\Lambda)=\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}(f_{p},\lambda_{p}^{\frac{1}{p}})
, where
(f_{p},\lambda_{p})
is an eigenpair of the
p
-Laplacian for a given
p
then
(f,\Lambda)
solves (
21
). In addition, whenever a pair
(f,\Lambda)
solves eq.(
21
), then

\Lambda=\frac{\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}}{\|f\|_{\infty}}\;.

By means of this equation it is possible to relate the limit of the variational
p
-Laplacian eigenvalues to the packing radii of
\Omega
. Indeed, given a bounded domain
\Omega\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and an integer
k
, the
k
-th packing radius of
\Omega
is defined as the maximal radius
r\in\mathbb{R}
that allows inscribing
k
distinct balls in
\Omega
, i.e.:

R_{k}:=\sup\{r\in\mathbb{R}\text{ s.t. }\exists B_{r}(x_{1}),\dots,B_{r}(x_{k})\subset\Omega,\;B_{r}(x_{i})\cap B_{r}(x_{j})=\emptyset\;\forall i,j=1,\dots,k\}\,,

where
B_{r}(x_{i})
is the
n
-dimensional ball of radius
r
centered in
x_{i}
,
x_{i}\in\Omega
. Then, given
\Lambda_{k}
an accumulation point of the sequence of the
k
-th variational eigenvalues of the
continuous

p
-Laplacian, i.e.

\Lambda_{k}=\lim_{h\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{k}^{\frac{1}{p_{h}}}(\Delta_{p_{h}})\,,

it is possible to relate such value to
R_{k}
(see
[
36
,
37
]
) and obtain the following inequality:

\Lambda_{k}\leq\frac{1}{R_{k}}\,.

For the particular cases
k=1,2
, also the opposite inequality holds and the sequences
\{\lambda_{1,2}^{\frac{1}{p}}(\Delta_{p})\}_{p=2}^{\infty}
converge. Thus, we can write:

\Lambda_{1}=\frac{1}{\|d_{\partial\Omega}\|_{\infty}}\,,\qquad\Lambda_{2}=\frac{1}{R_{2}}\,,

where
\partial\Omega
denotes the boundary of the domain
\Omega
. Several studies have concerned the
\infty
-eigenfunctions associated to the first eigenvalue
\Lambda_{1}
(see
[
43
,
54
,
38
,
35
]
).
Indeed, despite the fact that the boundary distance function
d_{\partial\Omega}
is always a minimizer of
\mathcal{R}_{\infty}
, it is not always an eigenfunction, meaning that, depending on
\Omega
, the pair
(d_{\partial\Omega},1/\|d_{\partial\Omega}\|_{\infty})
may not solve eq. (
21
).
In addition, the first eigenfunction may not be unique and there may be first eigenfunctions that solve the
\infty
-limit eigenvalue equation without beeing limits of eigenfunctions of the
p
-Laplacian.

3.1
The
\infty
-limit eigenvalue equation on a graph

In this section we formulate discrete analogues of the
\infty
-eigenvalue eq. (
21
) and discuss their solutions. Note that, given a a function
f\in\mathcal{H}(V_{\text{I}})
, using the equalities in (
15
) we can rewrite the eigenvalue equation of the
p
-Laplacian given in (
10
) as:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\left(\left\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega^{\prime}}f\right\}^{-}(u)\right\|_{p-1}^{p-1}-\left\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega^{\prime}}f\right\}^{+}(u)\right\|_{p-1}^{p-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}=\lambda^{\frac{1}{p-1}}f(u)\\[4.73611pt]
\left\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega^{\prime}}f\right\}^{-}(u)\right\|_{p-1}-\left\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega^{\prime}}f\right\}^{+}(u)\right\|_{p-1}>0\end{cases}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\quad\text{if }f(u)>0

(22a)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\left(\left\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega^{\prime}}f\right\}^{+}(u)\right\|_{p-1}^{p-1}-\left\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega^{\prime}}f\right\}^{-}(u)\right\|_{p-1}^{p-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}=-\lambda^{\frac{1}{p-1}}f(u)\\[4.73611pt]
\left\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega^{\prime}}f\right\}^{-}(u)\right\|_{p-1}-\left\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega^{\prime}}f\right\}^{+}(u)\right\|_{p-1}^{p-1}<0\end{cases}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\quad\text{if }f(u)<0

(22b)

\displaystyle\phantom{.}\,\>\left\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega^{\prime}}f\right\}^{-}(u)\right\|_{p-1}-\left\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega^{\prime}}f\right\}^{+}(u)\right\|_{p-1}=0\,\,\qquad\text{if }f(u)=0

(22c)

where, as in eq. (
15
),
\omega^{\prime}_{uv}:=\omega_{uv}^{\frac{p}{p-1}}
.
In the following Theorem we prove that any accumulation point of a sequence of
p
-Laplacian eigenpairs satisfies the
\infty
-limit eigenvalue equation.
Before stating the Theorem, we would like to observe that, as shown in
[
18
]
, any
p
-Laplacian eigenvalue
\lambda(\Delta_{p})
satisfies the following inequality:

\lambda(\Delta_{p})\leq\max_{u\in V_{\text{I}}}2^{p-1}\sum_{v\sim u}\omega_{uv}^{p}\,.

Thus, the set of the
p
-roots of the
p
-Laplacian eigenvalues as
p
varies is bounded, i.e.:

\text{there exists a constant }C>0\text{ s.t. }\lambda^{\frac{1}{p}}<C\text{ for all }\lambda\in\sigma(\Delta_{p})\,,

where
\sigma(\Delta_{p})
denotes the spectrum of the graph
p
-Laplacian.

Theorem 7
.

Let
(f_{p_{j}},\lambda_{{p_{j}}})
be a sequence of
p
-Laplacian
eigenpairs and assume that
\displaystyle\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}(f_{p_{j}},\lambda_{p_{j}}^{\frac{1}{p_{j}}})=(f,\Lambda)
. Then
the pair
(f,\Lambda)
satisfies the following set of equations
for all
u\in V_{\text{I}}
:

0={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Theta_{\infty}(f)(u)=}\begin{cases}\min\{\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega}f\right\}(u)\|_{\infty}-\Lambda f(u)\,,\,\Delta_{\infty}f(u)\}\quad&\text{if }f(u)>0\\
\Delta_{\infty}f(u)\quad&\text{if }f(u)=0\\
\max\{-\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega}f\right\}(u)\|_{\infty}-\Lambda f(u)\,,\,\Delta_{\infty}f(u)\}\quad&\text{if }f(u)<0\end{cases}.

(23)

Proof.

To prove the assertion, consider the case
f(u)>0
. Then, for any
j
large enough, by (
22a
), we can write

\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega^{\prime}}f_{p_{j}}\right\}^{-}(u)\|_{p_{j}-1}\Big(1-\Big(\frac{\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega^{\prime}}f_{p_{j}}\right\}^{+}(u)\|_{p_{j}-1}}{\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega^{\prime}}f_{p_{j}}\right\}^{-}(u)\|_{p_{j}-1}}\Big)^{p_{j}-1}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p_{j}-1}}=\lambda_{p_{j}}^{\frac{1}{p_{j}-1}}f_{p_{j}}(u)\,,}\\
\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega^{\prime}}f_{p_{j}}\right\}^{-}(u)\|_{p_{j}-1}>\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega^{\prime}}f_{p_{j}}\right\}^{+}(u)\|_{p_{j}-1}\,.\end{cases}

(24)

Taking the limit we have

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega}f\right\}(u)\|_{\infty}\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\Big(1-\Big(\frac{\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega^{\prime}}f_{p_{j}}\right\}^{+}(u)\|_{p_{j}-1}}{\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega^{\prime}}f_{p_{j}}\right\}^{-}(u)\|_{p_{j}-1}}\Big)^{p_{j}-1}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p_{j}-1}}=\Lambda f(u)\\
\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega}f\right\}^{-}(u)\|_{\infty}\geq\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega}f\right\}^{+}(u)\|_{\infty}\end{cases},

(25)

where, we have used the equality
\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega}f\right\}^{-}(u)\|_{\infty}=\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega}f\right\}(u)\|_{\infty}
that follows trivially from the second inequality in (
24
). From this same inequality, we observe that

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\bigg(1-\bigg(\frac{\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega^{\prime}}f_{p_{j}}\right\}^{+}(u)\|_{p_{j}-1}}{\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega^{\prime}}f_{p_{j}}\right\}^{-}(u)\|_{p_{j}-1}}\bigg)^{p_{j}-1}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p_{j}-1}}\leq 1,

and thus, if
\left\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega}f\right\}^{-}(u)\right\|_{\infty}\gneqq\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega}f\right\}^{+}(u)\|_{\infty}
, we obtain:

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\bigg(1-\bigg(\frac{\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega^{\prime}}f_{p_{j}}\right\}^{+}(u)\|_{p_{j}-1}}{\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega^{\prime}}f_{p_{j}}\right\}^{-}(u)\|_{p_{j}-1}}\bigg)^{p_{j}-1}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p_{j}-1}}=1.

Replacing the last equation in (
25
) we obtain:

\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega}f\right\}(u)\|_{\infty}=\Lambda f(u)\,.

Vice versa, if
{\displaystyle\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}}\Big(1-\Big(\frac{\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega^{\prime}}f_{p_{j}}\right\}^{+}(u)\|_{p_{j}-1}}{\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega^{\prime}}f_{p_{j}}\right\}^{-}(u)\|_{p_{j}-1}}\Big)^{p_{j}-1}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p_{j}-1}}<1
, then necessarily
we have

\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega}f\right\}^{-}(u)\|_{\infty}=\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega}f\right\}^{+}(u)\|_{\infty}\,,

showing that (
23
) is satisfied.
The cases
f(u)=0
and
f(u)<0
can be proved analogously, concluding the proof.

We point out that eq. (
23
) is very similar to the continuous analogue in (
21
), with the only difference being the absolute value replaced by the
\infty
-norm of the local gradient.
Next we show that whenever
(f,\Lambda)
satisfies the limit eigenvalue eq .(
23
),
\Lambda
matches the value of the
\infty
-Rayleigh quotient computed in
f
, i.e.
\mathcal{R}_{\infty}(f)
.

Proposition 8
.

Assume that
(f,\Lambda)
satifies eq. (
23
),
f
is not a constant function, and
u\in V_{\text{max}}(f)
. Then
\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}=\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega}f\right\}(u)\|_{\infty}=\Lambda|f(u)|=\Lambda\|f\|_{\infty}
, i.e.

\Lambda=\frac{\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}}{\|f\|_{\infty}}\,.

Proof.

Assume
u\in V_{\text{I}}
to be a node such that
|f(u)|=\|f\|_{\infty}
, i.e.,
u\in V_{\text{max}}(f)
, and, without loss of generality, that
f(u)>0
. Then (
23
) ensures that:

\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega}f\right\}(u)\|_{\infty}\geq\Lambda f(u)>0\,.

Since
u
is a maximum point, we have that
\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega}f\right\}^{+}(u)\|_{\infty}=0
, which, together with the previous inequality, yields:

\Delta_{\infty}f(u)>0\,.

Thus, from (
23
) we necessarily have:

\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega}f\right\}(u)\|_{\infty}-\Lambda f(u)=0\,.

The last equality allows us to observe that, if the thesis holds for some
w\in V_{\text{max}}(f)
, it holds as well for all the other
u\in V_{\text{max}}(f)
. Indeed

\frac{\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega}f\right\}(u)\|_{\infty}}{|f(u)|}=\frac{\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega}f\right\}(u)\|_{\infty}}{\|f\|_{\infty}}=\Lambda=\frac{\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega}f\right\}(w)\|_{\infty}}{|f(w)|}=\frac{\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}}{\|f\|_{\infty}}\,.

To prove the existence of such a node
w
, we proceed as follows. Let
u_{0}\in V_{\text{I}}
be a node admitting an edge
(v_{0},u_{0})
with
|\nabla_{\omega}f(v_{0},u_{0})|=\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}=\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega}f\right\}(u_{0})\|_{\infty}=\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega}f\right\}(v_{0})\|_{\infty}
. Then, if
\Delta_{\infty}f(u_{0})\neq 0
, we can write:

\Lambda=\frac{\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega}f\right\}(u_{0})\|_{\infty}}{|f(u_{0})|}=\frac{\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}}{|f(u_{0})|}\geq\frac{\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega}f\right\}(u)\|_{\infty}}{|f(u)|}=\frac{\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega}f\right\}(u)\|_{\infty}}{\|f\|_{\infty}}=\Lambda\,,

where
u
is any node satisfying
|f(u)|=\|f\|_{\infty}
, yielding in particular
|f(u_{0})|=\|f\|_{\infty}
and thus the thesis.
On the other hand, if
\Delta_{\infty}f(u_{0})=\Delta_{\infty}f(v_{0})=0
, assuming without loss of generality
f(u_{0})>f(v_{0})
(indeed the role of
u_{0}
and
v_{0}
is interchangeable), there exists
u_{1}\sim u_{0}
such that
f(u_{1})>f(u_{0})
with

\displaystyle\omega_{u_{0}u_{1}}\big(f(u_{1})-f(u_{0})\big)

\displaystyle=\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega}f\right\}^{-}(u_{0})\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega}f\right\}^{+}(u_{0})\|_{\infty}=\omega_{u_{0},v_{0}}\big(f(u_{0})-f(v_{0})\big)=\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}\,.

Thus there exists another edge
(u_{0},u_{1})
such that
|\nabla_{\omega}f(u_{0},u_{1})|=\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}
and
f(u_{1})>f(u_{0})>f(v_{0})
. Iterating this procedure, by the finiteness of the graph and the previous argument, there must exist a node
u_{k}
such that:

\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega}f\right\}(u_{k})\|_{\infty}=\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}=\Lambda|f(u_{k})|=\Lambda\|f\|_{\infty}\,.

3.2
Topological Properties of the
\infty
-eigenpairs

This subsection studies some relationships between the solutions of
(
23
)
, the limits of
p
-Laplacian eigenpairs, and the topology of the graph.
In particular, we aim at recovering a discrete version of the relationships holding in the continuous setting between the limit variational eigenvalues and the packing radii of the domain.
Within this framework, the distance function introduced in (
4
) and its boundary counterpart in (
11
) become significant tools in the study of the
\infty
-eigenpairs interpreted as solutions of (
23
).

Proposition 9
.

Let
(f,\Lambda)
, be a pair satisfying (
23
) with
f
not a constant function. Then for any
u\in V_{\text{max}}(f)
there exists a path
\Gamma=\{(u_{i},u_{i+1})\}_{i=1}^{n-1}
starting from
u
(
u_{1}=u
) such that :

1.

f
is monotone along
\Gamma
and for all
1\leq k\leq n-1
,
\nabla_{\omega}f(u_{k},u_{k+1})=\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}
;

2.

either

\begin{cases}u_{n}\in B\\
\displaystyle{\Lambda=\frac{1}{\mathrm{length}(\Gamma)}}\end{cases}

or

\begin{cases}f(u_{n})=-f(u)\\
\displaystyle{\Lambda=\frac{2}{\mathrm{length}(\Gamma)}}\end{cases}
;

3.

\displaystyle{\Lambda=\min\bigg\{\frac{1}{d_{B}(u)},\min_{\{v\,|\,f(v)=-f(u)\}}\frac{2}{d(u,v)}\bigg\}}\,.

Proof.

The first two points follow from Proposition
8
and eq. (
23
).
Indeed, given
u_{1}=u
, by Proposition
8
there exists
u_{2}\sim u_{1}
such that
|\nabla_{\omega}f(u_{1},u_{2})|=\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}
.
Then, from (
23
) we conclude that one of the three following conditions must hold:

f(u_{2})=-f(u_{1})\qquad\mbox{or}\qquad u_{2}\in B\qquad\mbox{or}\qquad|f(u_{2})|<|f(u_{1})|\,.

In the last case, since
\|\nabla_{\omega}f(u_{2})\|_{\infty}=\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}
, necessarily
\Delta_{\infty}f(u_{2})=0
, i.e. there exists
u_{3}\sim u_{2}
such that
|\nabla_{\omega}f(u_{2},u_{3})|=\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}
and, assuming w.l.o.g.
f(u_{1})>f(u_{2})
, it follows
f(u_{2})>f(u_{3})
. Iterating this procedure, the finiteness of the graph ensures that a path
\Gamma
such that
u_{n}\in B
or
f(u_{n})=-f(u_{1})
exists.
The following equalities

\frac{f(u_{i})-f(u_{i+1})}{\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}}=\frac{1}{\omega_{u_{i},u_{i+1}}}\,\>\forall i=1,\dots,n-1

provide an explicit expression for the length of the path
\Gamma
:

\mathrm{length}(\Gamma)=\sum_{i}\frac{1}{\omega_{u_{i},u_{i+1}}}=\frac{f(u_{1})-f(u_{n})}{\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}}\,.

(26)

This last equation, together with the equality
\Lambda=\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}/|f(u_{1})|
, yields
\Lambda=1/\mathrm{length}(\Gamma)
if
u_{n}\in B
and
\Lambda=2/\mathrm{length}(\Gamma)
if
f(u_{n})=-f(u_{1})
.

To conclude we are left to prove that i) if
u_{n}\in B
then
\mathrm{length}(\Gamma)=d_{B}(u_{1})
and ii)
\mathrm{length}(\Gamma)\leq 2d(u_{1},v)
for all
v
such that
f(v)=-f(u_{1})
, together with the analogous expressions for the case
f(u_{n})=-f(u_{1})
.
To this aim, assume by contradiction that there exists a path
\Gamma^{\prime}=\{(v_{i},v_{i+1})\}_{i=1}^{m-1}
with
v_{1}=u_{1}
,
v_{m}\in B
, and

\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\omega_{v_{i}v_{i+1}}^{-1}<\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\omega_{u_{i}u_{i+1}}^{-1}
and observe that

\omega_{v_{i}v_{i+1}}|f(v_{i})-f(v_{i+1})|=|\nabla_{\omega}f(v_{i},v_{i+1})|\leq\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty},\quad\forall i=1,\dots,m-1\,,

Then, using the triangular inequality and then equality (
26
),
and the assumptions
u_{n},v_{m}\in B
(i.e.
f(u_{n})=f(u_{m})=0
), we obtain the following contradiction:

\displaystyle|f(u_{1})|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}|f(v_{i})-f(v_{i+1})|

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\omega_{v_{i}v_{i+1}}^{-1}

\displaystyle<\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\omega_{u_{i}u_{i+1}}^{-1}=|f(u_{1})|\,.

The inequality
\mathrm{length}(\Gamma)\leq 2\,d(u_{1},v)
for all
v
such that
f(v)=-f(u_{1})
, can be proved with a similar argument, i.e., assuming the existence of a path
\Gamma^{\prime}
, from
u_{1}
to
v
, with
f(v)=-f(u_{1})
, such that
\mathrm{length}(\Gamma^{\prime})<2\,\mathrm{length}(\Gamma)
. The final case
f(u_{n})=-f(u_{1})
can be treated analogously.

We now turn our attention to the study of the accumulation points of sequences of
p
-Laplacian variational eigenvalues. We start by introducing some necessary preliminary results that are of independent interest. First of all we observe that the minimum of the
\infty
-Rayleigh quotient

\mathcal{R}_{\infty}(f)=\frac{\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}}{\|f\|_{\infty}}

(27)

can be easily computed, as in the continuous case
[
36
,
9
]
, considering the distance from the boundary,
d_{B}
given in (
11
).
Before proving this result, let us consider the following trivial inequality. Assume the distance between the nodes
u
and
v
is realized along the path
\Gamma=\{(v_{i},v_{i+1})\}_{i=1}^{n-1}
, with
v_{1}=u
and
v_{n}=v
, i.e.,
d(u,v)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}1/\omega_{v_{i}v_{i+1}}\,.

Then, the trianguar inequality yields the following Lipschitz condition:

\displaystyle|f(u)-f(v)|

\displaystyle\leq|\sum_{i=1}^{m}|f(v_{i})-f(v_{i+1})|\leq\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{\omega_{v_{i}v_{i+1}}}=\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}d(u,v)\,.

(28)

The next result shows that the boundary distance function
d_{B}
defined in (
11
) is a minimizer of the Rayleigh quotient.

Proposition 10
.

The boundary distance function
d_{B}\in V
realizes the minimunm of the
\infty
-Rayleigh quotient:

d_{B}\in\argmin_{f\in\mathcal{H}_{0}(V)}\frac{\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}}{\|f\|_{\infty}}\,,

Proof.

First we prove that
\|\nabla_{\omega}d_{B}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
. Indeed, given a node
u\in V
and an edge
(u,v)\in E
connected to it, the gradient of the distance function
d_{B}
on this edge can be estimated as:

\nabla_{\omega}d_{B}(u,v)=\omega_{uv}\big(d_{B}(v)-d_{B}(u)\big)\leq\omega_{uv}(\omega_{uv})^{-1}=1\,.

Thus, to conlude it is sufficient to show that the boundary distance function satisfies:

d_{B}\in\argmax_{f\in\mathcal{F}:\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}\leq 1}\|f\|_{\infty}\,.

To this end, for any function
f
with
f\in\mathcal{F}
and
\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}\leq 1
and for any node
u\in V
, let
v\in B
be the boundary node such that
d(u,v)=d_{B}(u)
. Then, using (
28
) we can write:

|f(u)|=|f(u)-f(v)|\leq d(u,v)=d_{B}(u)\,.

The last equation clearly implies:

d_{B}\in\argmax_{f\in\mathcal{H}_{0}(V):\|\nabla_{\omega}f\>\|_{\infty}\leq 1}\|f\|_{\infty}.

Next we show that the minimum value of the Rayleigh quotient is equal to the radius of the largest ball that can be inscribed in
\mathcal{G}
.

Corollary 11
.

\min_{f\in\mathcal{H}_{0}(V)}\frac{\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}}{\|f\|_{\infty}}=\frac{1}{R_{1}}\,,

where
R_{1}
is defined as

R_{1}=\sup_{u\in V_{\text{I}}}\{d_{B}(v)\}
.

Proof.

Because of Proposition
10
, it is enough to compute
\|\nabla_{\omega}d_{B}\|_{\infty}
and
\|d_{B}\|_{\infty}
.
Trivially
\|d_{B}\|_{\infty}=R_{1}
.
From the proof of Proposition
10
, we already know that
\big|\nabla_{\omega}d_{B}(u,v)\big|\leq 1\;\>\forall(u,v)\in E
. Moreover, it is easy to prove (see e.g.
[
9
]
) that:

|\nabla_{\omega}d_{B}(u,v)|=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad v\in\Gamma(u,B)\,\>or\,u\in\Gamma(v,B)\,,

where
\Gamma(u,B)
is the shortest path from the node
u
to the boundary. Thus, necessarily
\|\nabla_{\omega}d_{B}\|_{\infty}=1
.

The next theorem shows that, given
(f,\Lambda)
solution to the
\infty
-limit eigenvalue eq. (
23
), the eigenvalue
\Lambda
can be regarded as the maximal radius that allows one to inscribe as many balls in the graph as the number of nodal domains induced by
f
.
Recall that the nodal domains induced by
f
are the maximal connected subgraphs where
f
is strictly positive or negative.
The proof of the theorem relies on results related to the

Proposition 15

Let
\lambda_{k}(\Delta_{p})
be the

-Laplacian

-th variational eigenvalue on a graph

be the

Then,

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Lambda_{k}=}\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{k}(\Delta_{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\frac{1}{R_{k}}\,.

Proof.

We recall that the existence of the limit
\lim_{p\to\infty}\lambda_{k}(\Delta_{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}
is guaranteed from eq. (
19
) and
[
20
]
.
Then, let
u_{1},\dots,u_{k}
be
k
internal nodes of
\mathcal{G}
as in Definition
13
, i.e.:

d(u_{i},u_{j})\geq 2R_{k}\,,\qquad d_{B}(u_{i})\geq R_{k}\quad\forall i,j=1,\dots,k\,.

We define
k
linearly independent functions as:

f_{i}(u)=\max\{R_{k}-d(u,u_{i}),0\}\,.

Obviously, the set
A_{k}
spanned by these functions,
A_{k}=\operatorname{span}\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
, has dimension
k
and also its Krasnoselskii genus
\gamma(A_{k})
is equal to
k
.
Then, by definition, we have:

\lambda_{k}(\Delta_{p})\leq\max_{f\in A_{k}}\mathcal{R}_{\Delta_{p}}(f)=\max_{f\in A_{k}}\frac{\sum_{(u,v)\in E}\omega_{uv}^{p}|f(u)-f(v)|^{p}}{2\sum_{u\in V}|f(u)|^{p}}\\
=\frac{\sum_{(u,v)\in E}\omega_{uv}^{p}\big|\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}f_{i}(u)-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\alpha_{j}f_{j}(v)\big|^{p}}{2\sum_{m=1}^{k}\left(\sum_{d(u,u_{m})<R_{k}}|\alpha_{m}f_{m}(u)|^{p}\right)}\,,

where we recall the factor
1/2
is in the definition of the
p
-norm of
\nabla_{\omega}f
.
If
u
and
v
are such that both
d(u,u_{i})
and
d(v,u_{j})
are smaller than
R_{k}
, then:

\displaystyle\omega_{uv}|f(u)-f(v)|

\displaystyle=\omega_{uv}|\alpha_{i}||f_{i}(u)-f_{i}(v)\big|=\omega_{uv}|\alpha_{i}||d(u,u_{i})-d(v,u_{i})\big|

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{uv}|\alpha_{i}|d(u,v)\leq\omega_{uv}|\alpha_{i}|\frac{1}{\omega_{uv}}\leq|\alpha_{i}|\,.

Instead, if
d(u,u_{i})<R_{k}
and
d(v,u_{j})<R_{k}
with

, then:

\displaystyle\omega_{uv}|f(u)-f(v)|

\displaystyle=\omega_{uv}|\alpha_{i}\,f_{i}(u)-\alpha_{j}\,f_{j}(v)|

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{uv}\,\max\{|\alpha_{i}|,\,|\alpha_{j}|\}\,\big(R_{k}-d(u,u_{i})+R_{k}-d(v,u_{j})\big)

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{uv}\,\max_{l}\{|\alpha_{l}|\}\big(2R_{k}-d(u_{i},u_{j})+d(u,v)\big)

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{uv}\,\max_{l}\{|\alpha_{l}|\}\big(2R_{k}-2R_{k}+d(u,v)\big)

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{uv}\,\max_{l}\{|\alpha_{l}|\}d(u,v)

\displaystyle\leq\max_{l}\{|\alpha_{l}|\}\,.

Lastly, if
d(u,u_{i})<R_{k}
and
d(v,u_{j})\geq R_{k}
for all
j
, we have:

\displaystyle\omega_{uv}|f(u)-f(v)|=

\displaystyle\omega_{uv}|\alpha_{i}|\big(R_{k}-d(u,u_{i})\big)

\displaystyle=\omega_{uv}|\alpha_{i}|\big(R_{k}-d(u,u_{i})+d(u,v)-d(u,v)\big)

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{uv}|\alpha_{i}|\big(d(u,v)+R_{k}-d(v,u_{i})\big)

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{uv}|\alpha_{i}|d(u,v)

\displaystyle\leq|\alpha_{i}|\,.

Using the above inequalities in the expression for
\lambda_{k}
, we can write:

\frac{\sum_{(u,v)\in E}\omega_{uv}^{p}\big|\sum\alpha_{i}f_{i}(u)-\sum\alpha_{i}f_{i}(v)\big|^{p}}{2\sum_{m=1}^{k}\big(\sum_{d(u,u_{m})<R_{k}}|\alpha_{m}f_{m}(u)|^{p}\big)}\leq\frac{\sum_{(u,v)\in E}\max_{i}|\alpha_{i}|^{p}}{2\sum_{m=1}^{k}\big(\sum_{d(u,u_{m})<R_{k}}|\alpha_{m}f_{m}(u)|^{p}\big)}\,.

Passing to the limit we arrive at:

\displaystyle\limsup_{p\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{k}^{\frac{1}{p}}(\Delta_{p})

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{p\rightarrow\infty}\bigg(\frac{\sum_{(u,v)\in E}\max_{i}|\alpha_{i}|^{p}}{2\sum_{m=1}^{k}\big(\sum_{d(u,u_{m})<R_{k}}|\alpha_{m}f_{m}(u)|^{p}\big)}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\max_{i}|\alpha_{i}|}{\max_{m,u}|\alpha_{m}f_{m}(u)|}\,.

The thesis is obtained by observing that
f_{i}(u_{i})=R_{k}
and thus
\max_{m,u}|\alpha_{m}f(u)|=R_{k}\max_{m}|\alpha_{m}|
.

We start proving that the condition on the paths is a sufficient condition for the uniqueness of the minimizer. Let

\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}=1\quad\text{and}\quad f(u_{0})=d_{B}(u_{0}).

Consider

\gamma^{\prime}=\{u_{0},u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\}

u_{n}\in B

d_{B}(v)=\mathrm{length}(\{u_{k},\dots,u_{n}\})\quad\text{and}\quad d(v,u_{0})=\mathrm{length}(\{u_{0},\dots,u_{k}\}).

Thus, as a consequence of (

On the other hand, let

V^{*}=\left\{u\in V_{\text{I}}\;\Bigg|\!\!\!\!\!\!\begin{array}[]{ll}&\exists\gamma=\{u_{0},u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\in B\}\text{ with }d_{B}(v)=\mathrm{length}(\gamma)\\
&\text{ and }u=u_{i}\text{ for some }1\leq i\leq n-1\end{array}\right\}\cup B

be the set of all nodes of the graph supported on some shortest path from

to the boundary. Then assume

Now we claim that

Then, necessarily from the condition
\Delta_{\infty}f(u)=0

\gamma^{*}=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{m}\}

Note that

Moreover, assume by contradiction that the last inequality was actually an equality. Then, consider the path
\gamma^{\prime}
obtained by concatenating the shortest path from the boundary to

with

to

From the definition of

\displaystyle=d_{B}(u_{0})=d(v_{m},u_{0})+\mathrm{length}(\gamma^{*})+d_{B}(v_{1})

\displaystyle=d(v_{m},u_{0})+d_{B}(v_{m})-d_{B}(v_{1})+d_{B}(v_{1})=d_{B}(u_{0}).

Thus

4
The generalized
\infty
-eigenvalue problem

In this section we discuss a different approach to the
\infty
-Laplacian eigenvalue problem. It is based on the definition of the generalized critical points of a non differentiable functional. The same approach has been deeply investiagted in the study of the graph
1
-Laplacian eigenpairs
[
13
,
33
]
.
More in detail, in the first paragraph, we first recall the definition of the generalized eigenpairs and the corresponding
\infty
-variational eigenvalues. Then, by means of the subgradients of the convex functions
f\mapsto\|f\|_{\infty},\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}
, we establish an explicit algebraic equation satisfied by a generalized eigenpair.
In the subsequent paragraphs we prove a topological characterization of the geometrical
\infty
-eigenpairs similar to the one proved in Proposition
9
. This characterization allows us to compare the two different formulations of the
\infty
-eignepairs.
In particular, we prove that any solution of the equation in
theorem

7
is a generalized eigenpair, while the generalized eigenpairs solve the limit equation in
theorem

7
if we consider a proper subgraph of
\mathcal{G}
.

The generalized
\infty
-eigenpairs are formulated via the generalized “critical points” of the
\infty
-Rayleigh quotient given by:

\mathcal{R}_{\infty}(f)=\frac{\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}}{\|f\|_{\infty}}\,.

Indeeed, the
p
-Laplacian eigenpairs can be seen as critical points and values of the functional
\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{p}
on the manifold
\|f\|_{p}=1
, i.e. the points in which the differential of
\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{p}
is normal to
S_{p}=\{f:V_{\text{I}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}|\;\|f\|_{p}=1\}
.
Letting
p\rightarrow\infty
, we observe immediately that
\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}
is not a differentiable operator and
S_{\infty}
is not a smooth manifold. Nevertheless, following
[
15
]
, since
\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}
and
\|f\|_{\infty}
are convex functions, we can generalize the notion of critical points as follows:

Definition 23
.

We say that
(f,\Lambda)
is a generalized
\infty
-eigenpair if and only if:

0\in\partial\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}\cap\Lambda\partial\|f\|_{\infty}\,.

where
\partial\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}
and
\partial\|f\|_{\infty}
are the subgradients of the functions
(f\mapsto\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty})
and
(f\mapsto\|f\|_{\infty})
, respectively.

From
[
15
,
20
]
, we observe that Definition
23
can be considered as the generalized critical point equation for the functional
(f\mapsto\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty})
on
S_{\infty}
since
\partial\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}
is a generalization of the differential of
\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{p}
when
p=\infty
, while, from Lemma 4.2 and 4.3 of
[
15
]
, (see also
[
48
]
) the external cone to
S_{\infty}
in a point
f
, i.e.,

C_{Ext}(f)=\{\xi:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}|\>\left\langle\xi,g-f\right\rangle\leq 0\;\forall g\in S_{\infty}\}

can be characterized as

C_{Ext}(f)=\mathop{\cup}_{\lambda\geq 0}\lambda\partial\|f\|_{\infty}\,.

Moreover we point out that, from Theorem 5.8 of
[
15
]
,
\Lambda_{k}(\Delta_{\infty})
defined in (
18
) corresponds to the
k
-th Krasnoselskii
\infty
-variational eigenvalue, and it is a generalized critical value of
\mathcal{R}_{\infty}
as in
definition

23
.

Similar approaches have been used to study the
1
-Laplacian eigenpairs
[
13
,
33
]
, and more recently to study minimizers of
\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}
in
L^{2}
and
L^{\infty}
spaces,
[
9
,
8
]
.

In particular, from (
19
) and
[
20
]
we have that the
\infty
-variational eigenvalues are equal to the limit of the
p
-Laplacian variational eigenvalues as
p
goes to
\infty
.
Moreover,
theorem

16
and
proposition

15
can be reformulated in terms of inequalities relating the infinity variational eigenvalues and the packing radii of the graph as in the following Theorem.

Theorem 24
.

Let
\{\Lambda_{k}\}
be the Krasnoselskii variational eigenvalues and
R_{k}
be the
k
-th packing radius defined in Definition
13
.
Then,

\Lambda_{k}\leq\frac{1}{R_{k}}\qquad\forall k=1,\dots,|V_{\text{I}}|

and equality holds for
k=1,2
.

Proof.

The proof follows trivially from
theorem

16
and
proposition

15
. However, the theorem can also be proved without necessarily using the fact that the
\infty
-variational eigenvalues are the limit of the
p
-Laplacian variational ones. For completeness we incluse also this alternative proof. First of all, observe that the inequality can be proved just following the proof of Prop
15
.

I.e, consider
u_{1},\dots,u_{k}
such that
d(u_{i},u_{j})\geq 2r,\;d(u_{i},B)\geq r\;\>\forall i,j=1,\dots,k
and define the
k
linearly independent functions

f_{j}(u)=\max\{R_{k}-d(u,u_{j}),0\}\,,\quad j=1,\dots,k\,.

Then the set
A_{k}=span\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{k}
has Krasnoselskii genus equal to
k
,
\gamma(A_{k})=k
, and thus:

\Lambda_{k}\leq\max_{f\in A_{k}}\mathcal{R}_{\infty}(f)\,,

from which the subsequent inequality readily follows
\max_{f\in A_{k}}\mathcal{R}_{\infty}(f)\leq 1/R_{k}
.

Similarly, also equalities
\Lambda_{1}=R_{1}^{-1}
and
\Lambda_{2}=R_{2}^{-1}
can be proved without necessarily using the properties of the limit
p
-Laplacian eigenpairs. Indeed, because of Corollary
11
, we immediately have
\Lambda_{1}=R_{1}^{-1}
. Moreover, any
A\in\mathcal{F}_{2}(S_{\infty})
necessarily contains a symmetric, closed, and connected subset of
S_{\infty}
.

Then, given the function

\psi\in C(S_{\infty},\mathbb{R})
mapping any function
f
to
\|f^{+}\|_{\infty}-\|f^{-}\|_{\infty}
, we have that
for any
A\in\mathcal{F}_{2}(S_{\infty})
there esists
f_{A}\in A
such that
\psi(f_{A})=0
. In other words, there exist
u^{+},u^{-}\in V
such that

\|f\|_{\infty}=f_{A}(u^{+})=-f_{A}(u^{-})
.
To conclude, from (
28
), we observe that

d(u^{\pm},B)\geq\frac{\|f_{A}\|_{\infty}}{\|\nabla_{\omega}f_{A}\|_{\infty}}\,,\qquad d(u^{+},u^{-})\geq 2\frac{\|f_{A}\|_{\infty}}{\|\nabla_{\omega}f_{A}\|_{\infty}}\,,

i.e.
\|f_{A}\|_{\infty}/\|\nabla_{\omega}f_{A}\|_{\infty}\leq R_{2}
, yielding
\Lambda_{2}^{\geq}\min_{A\in\mathcal{F}_{2}}\|\nabla_{\omega}f_{A}\|_{\infty}/\|f_{A}\|_{\infty}\geq 1/R_{2}\,.

Remark 25
.

We point out that generally the number of nodal domains of a generalized variational eigefunction
f_{k}
can not be used to establish a lower bound for the corresponding eigenvalue in terms of some packing radius as in
corollary

17
. This is due to the fact that generalized eigenfunctions are “less”-rigid than solutions of the
\infty
-limit eigenvalue equation and so they can oscillate arbitrarily far from the nodes and edges of the graph where
|\nabla_{\omega}f|
and
|f|
are maximal. We refer to
[
20
]
and, in particular to Example 6.12 therein for more details. However a lower bound like the one in
corollary

17
can be recovered by considering the number of perfect nodal domains induced by
f_{k}
, where a perfect nodal domain is a nodal domain where some node
u
exists such that
|f(u)|=\|f\|_{\infty}
, see Theorem 8.6 in
[
20
]
.

Next, we study the structure of the two sets
\partial\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}
and
\partial\|f\|_{\infty}
.
As in remark
6
, we think about both
f\mapsto\|f\|_{\infty}
and
f\mapsto\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}
as functions from
\mathcal{H}(V_{\text{I}})
to
\mathbb{R}
.
Then, as done in
[
9
]
, because of homogeneity, it is easy to derive the following characterization, (see also
[
10
,
20
]
):

\partial\big(f\mapsto\|f\|_{\infty}\big)=\{\xi:V_{\text{I}}\to\mathbb{R}\,|\,\|g\|_{\infty}\geq\left\langle\xi,g\right\rangle\,\forall g:V_{\text{I}}\to\mathbb{R},\;\>\|f\|_{\infty}=\left\langle\xi,f\right\rangle\}\,,

i.e.

\partial\|f\|_{\infty}:=\left\{\xi\;\bigg|\begin{array}[]{lr}\|\xi\|_{1,V}=1,\;\xi(u)=0\;\;\forall u\in V_{\text{I}}\setminus V_{\text{max}}(f)\,,\\[3.99994pt]
|\,\xi(u)|\,|f(u)|=\xi(u)f(u)\;\;\forall u\in V_{\text{max}}(f)\end{array}\right\}\,.

(37)

Moreover, we can use the subdifferential chain rule for linear transformations
\Big(\partial\big(x\mapsto\phi(Ax)\big)=A^{T}\partial\big(y\mapsto\phi(y)\big)|_{y=Ax}\Big)
to characterize
\partial(f\mapsto\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty})

[
48
]
:

\displaystyle\partial\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}:=\left\{-\operatorname{div}\Xi\,\bigg|\!\!\begin{array}[]{lr}\|\Xi\|_{1,E}=1,\;\Xi(u,v)=0\;\;\forall(u,v)\in E\setminus E_{\text{max}}(f)\,,\\[3.99994pt]
|\,\Xi(u,v)\,||\nabla_{\omega}f(u,v)|=\Xi(u,v)\nabla_{\omega}f(u,v)\;\forall(u,v)\in E_{\text{max}}(f)\end{array}\!\!\!\right\}

(38)

where the divergence operator in defined as in Remark
6
,
and we used the edge and node norms defined in (
8
).
Then we can give the following definition of generalized
\infty
-eigenpair.

Definition 26
.

(f,\Lambda)
is a generalized
\infty
-eigenpair if and only if there exist
\xi\in\partial\|f\|_{\infty}
and
\Xi
with
-\operatorname{div}(\Xi)\in\partial\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}
such that

-\operatorname{div}\,\Xi(u)=\Lambda\xi(u)\quad\forall u\in V_{\text{I}}\,.

Note that, putting together the above definition of generalized eigenpair and the characterization of the subgradients
\partial\|f\|_{\infty}
and
\partial\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}
(eqs. (
37
) and (
38
)), we have the following Proposition, whose proof is immediate.

Proposition 27
.

(f,\Lambda)
with
f\in\mathcal{H}_{0}(V)
, is a generalized
\infty
-eigenpair if and only if there exist
\xi:V_{\text{I}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
\Xi:E\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

\begin{cases}-\operatorname{div}\Xi(u)=\Lambda\xi(u)\quad&\forall u\in V_{\text{I}}\\
\|\Xi\|_{1,E}=1\\
\|\xi\|_{1,V}=1\\
|f(u)|=\|f\|_{\infty}\quad&if\;\xi(u)\neq 0\\
|\nabla_{\omega}f(u,v)|=\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}\quad&if\;\Xi(u,v)\neq 0\\
\mathrm{sign}\big(\Xi(u,v)\big)=\mathrm{sign}\big(\nabla_{\omega}f(u,v)\big)\qquad&if\;\Xi(u,v)\neq 0\\
\mathrm{sign\big(\xi(u)\big)}=\mathrm{sign\big(f(u)\big)}\quad&if\;\xi(u)\neq 0\end{cases}\,.

(39)

Moreover, up to redefining
\Xi(u,v)=\Big(\Xi(u,v)-\Xi(v,u)\Big)/2
, we can assume
\Xi(u,v)=-\Xi(v,u)
.

Remark 28
.

We would like to observe that since
\mathcal{R}_{\infty}
is a locally Lipschitz function of
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, the notion of critical point can also be generalized considering the Clarke subderivative
\partial^{Cl}\mathcal{R}_{\infty}
, see
[
16
]
, i.e.
f
is a Clarke
\infty
-eigenpairs iff

0\in\partial^{Cl}\mathcal{R}_{\infty}(f)\,.

(40)

From
[

,
33
]
we know that:

\partial^{Cl}\mathcal{R}_{\infty}(f)\subseteq\frac{\partial\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}\|f\|_{\infty}-\partial\|f\|_{\infty}\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}}{\|f\|^{2}_{\infty}}\,,

(41)

which shows that the notion of generalized
\infty
-eigenpair (
Definition

26
) extends the notion of Clarke-eigenpair. We refer to the work in
[
55
]
for an example where the set of the Clarke eigenvalues is strictly included in the set of the generalized eigenvalues.
Nevertheless,
Definition

26
provides some practical advantages with respect to the eq. (

) since, differently from
\partial^{Cl}\mathcal{R}_{\infty}(f)
, both
\partial\|f\|_{\infty}
and
\partial\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}
can be explicitly identified.

From the characterization of the subgradients (
38
) and (
37
), and the reformulation of the generalized
\infty
-eigenvalue problem in
eq.

39
, it is easy to see that the
\infty
-eigenpairs are usually not unique. Indeed, given
(f,\Lambda)
a generalized
\infty
-eigenpair, if there exists a node
u
such that
|f(u)|<\|f\|_{\infty}
and
\|\{\nabla_{\omega}f\}(u)\|_{\infty}<\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}
. Then we can consider a continuous family of perturbations
f_{\epsilon}
of

on the only node
u
(e.g.
f_{\epsilon}(v)=f(v)+\epsilon\delta_{u}(v)
) such that, as long as
f_{\epsilon}
satisfies

|f_{\epsilon}(u)|<\|f\|_{\infty}\quad\text{and}\quad\|\{\nabla_{\omega}f_{\epsilon}\}(u)\|_{\infty}\leq\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty},

(42)

is a generalized
\infty
-eigenfunction of the same eigenvalue
\Lambda
. As a particular case, any minimizer of the
\infty
-Rayleigh quotient, that we have observed in
example

20
and
theorem

generically to be not unique, is a generalized
\infty
-eigenpair.
It is clear that, since
\mathcal{R}_{p}(f)
converges to
\mathcal{R}_{\infty}(f)
, any limit of

-Laplacian eigenpairs for

is always a generalized
\infty
-eigenpair. However, the same is not straightfoward for the solutions of the
\infty
-limit eigenvalue equation (

). Indeed, as we have observed in
example

, there exist solutions of the
\infty
-limit eigenvalue equation that are not limit of

-Laplacian eigenpairs. In the next section we prove that any solution of the

-limit eigenvalue equation is also a generalized
\infty
-eigenpair. To this end we prove that the generalized
\infty
-eigenpairs are uniquely characterized by a topological property analogue to the one satisfied by the solutions of the
\infty
-limit eigenvalue equation, see
proposition

9
and
proposition

30
.

Remark 29
.

We dedicate this remark to highlight analogies and differences of the
\infty
-Laplacian eigenvalue problem and the theory of Lipschitz learning in
\ell_{p}
-semi-supervised learning
[

,
12
,
50
,

,

,

,

]
.
In this case one wants to find a solution to the problem

\min\{\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{p}|\;f(u)=g(u)\quad\forall u\in B\},

(43)

where

is a fixed boundary condition. When
p\in(1,\infty)
, the minimum is unique and satisfies the system of equations

\begin{cases}\Delta_{p}f(u)=0\qquad&\forall u\in V_{\text{I}},\\
f(u)=g(u)\qquad&\forall u\in B.\end{cases}

(44)

However, when one considers the case of Lipschitz learning, i.e.

, the minimum is not unique anymore. Uniqueness is recovered by considering the absolutely minimal Lipschitz extension of

, which corresponds to the solution of the system

\begin{cases}\Delta_{\infty}f(u)=0\qquad&\forall u\in V_{\text{I}},\\
f(u)=g(u)\qquad&\forall u\in B.\end{cases}

(45)

In particular, the unique solution of (

) is also the limit of the solutions of (
44
) as

goes to
\infty
.

Similarly to (

), the generalized eigenpairs simply correspond to the “critical points” of the

-Rayleight quotient and they are generally not unique. A smaller subset of

-eigenpairs can always be obtained by looking at the limit of

-Laplacian eigenpairs, i.e. critical points of the

-Rayleigh quotient, as

goes to
\infty
. So, as done in going from (

) to (

), we have identified a limit equation satisfied by the limit of
p
-eigenpairs (see (

)). However, differently from the case of
\ell_{p}
semi-supervised learning, the solutions of the limit eigenvalue equation (
23
), are not all and only the limits of
p
-Laplacian eigenpairs (see
example

19
).

4.1
Topological characterization

In this section we discuss a geometrical characterization of the generalized
\infty
-eigenfunctions similar to the one proved in Proposition
9
.
In both these characterizations, given an eigenpair,
(f,\Lambda)
, we prove the existence of “good” paths that connect points in
V_{\text{max}}(f)\cup B
and whose length matches the value of the eigenvalue.
However, differently from Proposition
9
, where we proved the existence of a “good” path
\Gamma
for any extremal point
v\in V_{\text{max}}(f)
, in the case of generalized
\infty
-critical points, extremal points that do not correspond to any “good” path may indeed exist.
Moreover, contrary to the case of limit
\infty
-eigenpairs where the existence of such “good” paths was only a necessary condition for the existence of an eigenpair, in the case of generalized eigenpairs, the existence of “good” paths is also a sufficient condition.

Proposition 30
.

The pair
(f,\Lambda)
is a generalized
\infty
-eigenpair with
f
not a constant function if and only if there exists a path
\Gamma=\{(u_{i},u_{i+1})\}_{i=1}^{n-1}
such that:

1.

u_{i}\in V_{\text{max}}(f)\cup B\quad i=1,n
;

2.

(u_{i},u_{i+1})\in E_{\text{max}}(f)
and
f(u_{i})>f(u_{i+1})
for all
i=1,\dots,n
;

3.

assuming without loss of generality that
f(u_{1})>0
, if

u_{n}\in B
then
\Lambda=\frac{1}{\mathrm{length}(\Gamma)}
, if
u_{n}\notin B
then
f(u_{n})=-f(u_{1})
and
\Lambda=\frac{2}{\mathrm{length}(\Gamma)}
.
Moreover

\frac{1}{\Lambda}=\min\bigg\{\min_{\{v|f(v)=-\|f\|_{\infty}\}}\frac{d(u_{1},v)}{2},d_{B}(u_{1})\bigg\}\,.

Proof.

Assume that
(f,\Lambda)
is a generalized
\infty
-eigenpair and let
(\xi,\Xi)
be the two subgradients as in Definition (
39
), i.e., such that

-\operatorname{div}(\Xi)=\Lambda\xi\,,

(46)

and
\Xi(u,v)=-\Xi(v,u)
. Let
\xi(u_{1})\neq 0
and without loss of generality assume
f(u_{1})>0
. From eq. (
39
) there has to exist an edge
(u_{1},u_{2})\in E_{\text{max}}(f)
such that
\Xi(u_{1},u_{2})\neq 0
, i.e.
f(u_{2})=f(u_{1})-\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}/\omega_{u_{1}u_{2}}<f(u_{1})
.
Let us focus now on the second node
u_{2}
. Since
f(u_{2})<f(u_{1})
, if
u_{2}\notin B
and
f(u_{2})\neq-f(u_{1})
, we have that
\xi(u_{2})=0
.
Moreover
\Xi(u_{1},u_{2})<0
and
\Xi
has to satisfy (
46
), i.e.:

-\operatorname{div}(\Xi)(u_{2})=\sum_{v\sim u_{2}}\omega_{vu_{2}}\Xi(v,u_{2})=\Lambda\xi(u_{2})=0\,,

hence, there must exist an edge
(u_{3},u_{2})\in E_{\text{max}}(f)
such that
\Xi(u_{2},u_{3})>0\,,
i.e.,
f(u_{3})=f(u_{2})-\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}/\omega_{u_{2}u_{3}}<f(u_{2})
.
Iterating the above argument, we can define a path
\Gamma=\{(u_{i},u_{i+1})\}_{i=1}^{n-1}
such that
u_{n}\in B\cup V_{\text{max}}(f)
,
(u_{i},u_{i+1})\in E_{\text{max}}(f)
and
f(u_{i})>f(u_{i+1})
.
Furthermore, as in the proof of Proposition
9
, it is easy to see that, given a function
f
and a path
\Gamma
that satisfies the first two items of the thesis, necessarily we have that, if
u_{n}\in B
,
\Lambda=1/\mathrm{length}(\Gamma)
. On the other hand, if
f(u_{n})=-f(u_{1})
,
\Lambda=2/\mathrm{length}(\Gamma)
and

\frac{1}{\Lambda}=\min\bigg\{\min_{f(v)=-\|f\|_{\infty}}\frac{d(u_{0},v)}{2},d_{B}(u_{0})\bigg\}\,.

Indeeed, proceeding by contraddiction, if one of the last equalities was not true then an edge
(v_{1},v_{2})
with
|\nabla_{\omega}f(v_{1},v_{2})|>\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}
would exist.

To prove the opposite inclusion, we assume that, given
(f,\Lambda)
, there exists a path
\Gamma=\{(u_{i},u_{i+1})\}_{i=1}^{n-1}
with
f(u_{1})>0
that satisfies the thesis and look at the case
u_{n}\in\{v\,|\,f(v)=-f(u_{1})\}
with
\Lambda=2/d(u_{1},u_{n})=2/\mathrm{length}(\Gamma)<d_{B}(u_{1})
(the other case can be proved analogously).
We aim at defining two functions
\Xi
and
\xi
that satisfy the sufficient conditions in Proposition
27
.
Define the two functions as follows:

\displaystyle\Xi(u,v):=\frac{\delta_{\Gamma}(u,v)}{\omega_{uv}}\>\frac{\mathrm{sign}\big(\nabla_{\omega}f(u,v)\big)}{\mathrm{length}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\xi(v):=\frac{\delta_{u_{1}}(v)\mathrm{sign}\big(f(u_{1})\big)+\delta_{u_{n}}(v)\mathrm{sign}\big(f(u_{n})\big)}{2}\,,

where
\delta_{\Gamma}

\delta_{u_{i}}
are delta functions, i.e.,
\delta_{\Gamma}(u,v)=1
if
(u,v)\in\Gamma
and
\delta_{\Gamma}(u,v)=0
otherwise, with analogous definition for
\delta_{u_{i}}
.
Then, since
\xi
and
-\operatorname{div}\Xi
belong respectively to the two subgradients
\partial\|f\|_{\infty}
and
\partial\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}
, an easy calculations shows that for any node
v\not\in\Gamma
as well as for any node
v\in\Gamma
, with
v\neq u_{1}
and
v\neq u_{n}
, we have:

-\operatorname{div}\Xi(v)=0=\Lambda\,\xi(v)\,.

Finally, in the case
v=u_{1}
(or analogously
v=u_{n}
), we have

-\operatorname{div}\Xi(u_{1})=\frac{\omega_{u_{1}u_{2}}}{2}\Big(\Xi(u_{2},u_{1})-\Xi(u_{1},u_{2})\Big)=\frac{\mathrm{sign}\big((\nabla_{\omega}f(u_{2},u_{1})\big)}{\mathrm{length}(\Gamma)}=\frac{\Lambda}{2}=\Lambda\xi(u_{1})\,,

where we have used the fact that
\mathrm{sign}\big(\nabla_{\omega}f(u_{2},u_{1})\big)=\mathrm{sign}\big(f(u_{1})-f(u_{2})\big)=1
, which follows by the hypothesis
f(u_{1})>f(u_{2})
.
This concludes the proof.

As a corollary of Proposition
30

and Proposition
9
it is easy to prove that any eigenpair that satisfies the limit eigenvalue equation (
23
) is also a generalized
\infty
-eigenpair.

Corollary 31
.

Let
(f,\Lambda)
satisfy the limit eigenvalue equation (
23
) with
f
not constant, then
(f,\Lambda)
is also a generalized
\infty
-eigenpair according to Definition
26
.

Proof.

The proof is a consequence of the fact that, from Propositon
9
, for any eigenpair that satisfies the limit eigenvalue equation (
23
), there exists a path
\Gamma
that satisfies the hypotheses of Proposition
30
.

Next we deal with the opposite problem and ask ourselves if any generalized
\infty
-eigenvalue (see Definition
23
) can be associated to an eigenfunction that solves (
23
).
As we will see in Example

we will see that the statement can always be proved to hold up to a subgraph of
\mathcal{G}
.
Before we prove this statement and discuss the properties of the subgraph, we observe that the
\infty
-eigenvalue problem can be reformualted in terms of a constrained weighted linear Laplacian eigenvalue problem.
Indeed, from the characterizations of the subgradient in (
38

37

Proposition 32
.

The pair
(f,\Lambda)
is a generalized
\infty
-eigenpair if and only if there exist two admissible densities
\nu:V_{\text{I}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
, and
\mu:E\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
, with
\mu_{uv}=\mu_{vu}
such that:

\begin{cases}-\operatorname{div}(\mu\odot\nabla_{\omega}f)(u)=\Lambda\nu_{u}f(u)\quad&\forall u\in V_{\text{I}}\\
|\nabla_{\omega}f(u,v)|=\|\nabla_{\omega}f(u,v)\|_{\infty}\quad&\text{if}\;\mu_{uv}>0\\
|f(u)|=\|f(u)\|_{\infty}\quad&\text{if}\;\nu_{u}>0\\
\|\mu\odot\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{1,E}=1\\
\|\nu\odot f\|_{1,V}=1\\
f(u)=0\quad&if\;u\in B\end{cases}\,.

(47)

Proof.

Straightforward substitution into equation (
39
) shows that the quantities

\nu_{u}:=\frac{|\xi(u)|}{2\|f\|_{\infty}}\qquad\mu:=\frac{|\Xi(u,v)|+|\Xi(v,u)|}{\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}}

are the desired admissible densities. The inverse implication follows by inverting the above definitions of
\nu
and
\mu
.

Remark 33
.

We remark the an analogue linearization of the graph

To continue towards the sought result we need some definitions. Let
(f,\Lambda)
denote an
\infty

We say that a node

Observe that if

Now we can prove that any generalized

Theorem 34
.

Assume
(f,\Lambda)
to be an
\infty
-eigenpair as in Definition
23
. If
u\in\mbox{supp}(V)\cap V_{\text{I}}
, then
f
satisfies the limit eigenvalue equation (
23
) in
u
.

Proof.

Let
u\in\mbox{supp}(V)\cap V_{\text{I}}
and let
(\mu,\nu)
be a pair of admissible densities, such that
u\in V_{\mu}
.
Assuming
f(u)>0
, the weighted eigenvalue equation
-\operatorname{div}(\mu\nabla_{\omega}f)(u)=\Lambda\nu_{u}f(u)
can be written as:

-\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}\sum_{v\sim u}\mu_{uv}\,\omega_{uv}\mathrm{sign}\big(\nabla_{\omega}f(u,v)\big)=\Lambda\nu_{u}f(u)=\frac{\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}}{\|f\|_{\infty}}\nu_{u}f(u)\,.

(48)

We first consider the case
f(u)<\|f\|_{\infty}
. Then
\nu_{u}=0
, which yields
\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega}f\right\}^{-}(u)\|_{\infty}=\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega}f\right\}^{+}(u)\|_{\infty}
i.e.
\Delta_{\infty}f(u)=0
, and:

\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega}f\right\}(u)\|_{\infty}-\Lambda f(u)=\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}-\frac{\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}}{\|f\|_{\infty}}f(u)>0\,.

If instead
f(u)=\|f\|_{\infty}
, for any
v\sim u
we get
\nabla_{\omega}f(u,v)\leq 0
and thus
\nu_{u}\neq 0
(otherwise, by hypotheses, (
48
) is not satisfied).
Then, eq. (
48
) reads:

\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}\Big(\sum_{v\sim u}\mu_{uv}\omega_{uv}\Big)=\frac{\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}}{\|f\|_{\infty}}\nu_{u}\|f\|_{\infty}\,,

from which we obtain
\sum_{v\sim u}\mu_{uv}\omega_{uv}=\nu_{u}
. Replacing this last equality back in
(
48
)
we find:

\begin{cases}\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega}f\right\}(u)\|_{\infty}=\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}=\Lambda f(u)\,,\\
\Delta_{\infty}f(u)=\|\left\{\nabla_{\omega}f\right\}^{-}(u)\|_{\infty}=\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{\infty}>0\,.\end{cases}

The proof is concluded by considering the cases
f(u)<0
and
f(u)=0
, which can be proved analogously.

5
Conclusions and future directions

We studied the graph
\infty
-Laplacian eigenvalue problem on a graph, comparing different notions of
\infty
-eigenpairs. In particular, we have proved that any limit of
p
-Laplacian eigenpairs is a solution of the
\infty
-limit eigenvalue equation (
theorem

7
), and that any solution of the
\infty
-limit eigenvalue equation is a generalized
\infty
-eigenpair (
corollary

31
). Additionally, by studying the topological properties of the
\infty
-eigenpairs, we have related the variational
\infty
-eigenvalues to the packing problem of the graph (
theorem

24
and
corollary

17
).

We would like to note that the relations between the
\infty
-eigenpairs and the topology of the domain that we proved in the discrete setting hold also in the continuous setting
[
37
,
36
]
. Moreover, the three different formulations of the
\infty
-eigenpairs are different in the continuous setting as well
[
8
,
37
,
36
,
35
]
. In particular, the inclusion

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}&\text{Limit of }\\
&\Delta_{p}\text{-eigenpairs}\end{array}\right\}\subsetneq\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}&\text{Solutions of the}\\
&\text{limit eigenvalue eq.}\end{array}\right\}

is known to hold also in the continuous setting
[
36
,
35
]
. On the other hand, to the best of our knowledge, the second inclusion

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}&\text{Solutions of the}\\
&\text{limit eigenvalue eq.}\end{array}\right\}\subsetneq\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}&\text{Generalized }\\
&\infty\text{-eigenpairs }\end{array}\right\}\,.

is not known in the continuous setting.

An open problem is also the study of the convergence of the discrete
\infty
-spectrum to the continuous
\infty
-spectrum on geometric random graphs. In this case the graph is given by a cloud of points obtained by sampling a ground truth measure and the study of convergence of the discrete spectrum to the continuous one would allow one to approximate the topological properties of the continuous domain in terms of information derived from the spectrum of the discrete
\infty
-Laplacian. Results in this direction have been presented in
[
52
,
29
]
for the linear Laplacian operator. In the same setting, different authors have also studied convergence of
\ell_{p}
and
\ell_{\infty}
based semi-supervised solutions
[
11
,
12
,
50
,
49
]
. However, we are not aware of results about the convergence of the spectrum of nonlinear operators on geometric random graphs.

Finally, we note that the graph
p
-Laplacian operator can be given different formulations. Indeed, besides the variational graph
p
-Laplacian operator that we considered in
definition

2
, it is also possible to consider the game theoretic
p
-Laplacian operator
[
11
,
44
,
24
]
. This
p
-Laplacian operator is a linear combination of the 1 or 2 Laplacian and the
\infty
one. Both the variational and game theoretic
p
-Laplacians can be interpreted as discretizations of the analogous continuous
p
-Laplacian operator. However, while the discrete variational
p
-Laplacian arises considering variational problems of the functional
f\to\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{p}
, the game theoretic
p
-Laplacian does not have a variational formulation. For this reason, only the variational
p
-Laplacian is usually studied from a spectral point of view. Indeed critical points and values of functionals of the type “
\|\nabla_{\omega}f\|_{p}/\|f\|
”, where possibly different norms at the denominator can be considered, are of interest for different applications including spectral decompositions
[
31
,
10
]
and clustering problems
[
13
,
6
]
. The investigation of the spectrum of the game theoretic
p
-Laplacian operator and its possible applications are currently an open problem.

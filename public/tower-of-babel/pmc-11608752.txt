Risk factors of acute urinary retention after spine surgery
Authors: Han-Hsi Chiu, Jui-Ming Liu, Kai-Jie Yu, Chun-Te Wu, See-Tong Pang, Po-Hung Lin
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Acute postoperative urinary retention (APOUR) is 1 of the most common complications of spinal surgery, impacting both postoperative care and the patients’ quality of life. This study is designed to investigate the risk factors and the incidence of APOUR after spinal surgery in Taiwan. We conducted a retrospective analysis using the Taiwanese National Health Insurance Research Database (NHIRD), a nationwide cohort of patients who underwent spinal surgery from the year 2000 to 2013. Patients suffering from APOUR were collected and perioperative parameters were recorded. The risk factors related to APOUR after spinal surgery were examined using multivariate logistic regression analysis. A total of 16,160 patients who underwent spinal surgery were retrieved from NHIRD. Among them, 432 (2.67%) suffered from acute urinary retention after surgery. Multivariate logistic regression analysis revealed that age (adjusted odds ratio [OR] = 1.02, 95% CI = 1.01–1.02), male gender (adjusted OR = 1.35, 95% CI = 1.10–1.60) and undergoing spinal surgery more than 2 times (adjusted OR = 4.78, 95% CI = 2.49–9.20) were statistically significant risk factors for APOUR after spinal surgery. The results of this retrospective cohort study suggest that patients undergoing spinal surgery more than 2 times, male gender and advanced age are significantly associated with an increased risk of APOUR after spinal surgery. This information may assist surgeons to identify patients who have a high risk of APOUR after spinal surgery and proactively provide them with early intervention.

1. Introduction

Spinal surgery is a common procedure, and the number is increasing in recent years, particularly in patients who are older than 64 years of age.[1] According to the database analysis from the National Inpatient Sample, elective lumbar fusion surgeries increased 62.3% from 2004 to 2015,[1] with a total of 1.62 million instrumented spinal procedures performed annually in the United States. The global rate of postoperative complications or adverse events after spinal surgery are currently reported as occurring in 7% to 16% of patients,[2–4] and the most reported complications are postoperative radiculopathy, wound infections, and urinary tract infections.[2]

Acute urinary retention (AUR) is also a frequently occurring postoperative complication, particularly after both anorectal and orthopedic procedures.[5] AUR may lead to prolonged hospital stays, increased costs, and increased risks of readmission within 90 days after surgery.[6,7] Moreover, severe AUR after spinal surgery may lead to the need for a long-term indwelling catheter, which increases the risk of developing a urinary tract infection which may eventually lead to urosepsis.[8]

The reported incidence of acute postoperative urinary retention (APOUR) is highly variable, ranging from 5.6% to 38%.[6,9,10] Risk factors for developing APOUR include male gender, age > 50 years, and spinal or epidural anesthesia.[11] The mechanism resulting in APOUR includes degeneration of the neurological pathway leading to bladder dysfunction in addition to prostate enlargement. Medications that interfere with bladder function, such as anticholinergic agents, may also contribute to APOUR.[5]

The aim of this study is to investigate the risk factors and the incidence for APOUR following spinal surgery in Taiwanese population.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Data source

We used the Longitudinal Health Insurance Database 2000 (LHID2000) from National Health Insurance Research Database (NHIRD) to conduct this study. In the end of 2013, the coverage rate of National Health Insurance program was around 99.9% of whole Taiwanese population.[12] The NHIRD includes all medical claims data and registry files (of contracted medical facilities, board-certified specialists, and beneficiaries) for over 23 million enrollees. The LHID2000 is a sub-dataset of the NHIRD and contains all the original claim’s data of 1,000,000 individuals randomly sampled beginning in the year 2000. The LHID2000 and NHIRD are similar in terms of demographic data and their origin population.[13] This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Chang Gung Memorial Hospital with approval number 103-3237B.

2.2. Study population

All the clinical diagnoses were made using the International Classification of Diseases, 9th revision, Clinical Modification (ICD-9-CM).[14] Patients who received spinal surgery with associated diagnosis (ICD-9-CM: 81.00, 81.01, 81.02, 81.03, 81.04, 81.05, 81.06, 81.07, 81.08, 81.30, 81.31, 81.32, 81.33, 81.34, 81.35, 81.36, 81.37, 81.38, 81.39, 81.61, 81.62, 81.63, 81.64, 84.51, 03.09, 80.50, 80.51, 84.60, 84.61, 84.62, 84.63, 84.64, 84.65, 84.66, 84.67, 84.68, 84.69, 81.65, and 81.66) during the study period from January 1, 2000 to December 31, 2013 were included in this study. If a patient had received spinal surgery more than once during the study period, we selected all their spinal surgeries into the study sample. We also correlated surgery codes involving the cervical, thoracic, and lumbar spine with the ICD-9-CM codes to enforce the accuracy of patient enrollment.

2.3. Study outcome

The outcome of this study was to determine the number of patients who underwent spinal surgery and who were subsequently diagnosed with APOUR. APOUR was identified with the diagnostic code of AUR (ICD-9-CM: 788.2) after spinal surgery and with the procedure codes for Foley catheterization and suprapubic cystostomy insertion within a 1-month time frame after spinal surgery. Intermittent self-catheterization codes were not included.

2.4. Adjustment of covariates

The covariates of comorbidities included in this study were hypertension (ICD-9-CM: 401–405), diabetes mellitus (ICD-9-CM: 250), hyperlipidemia (ICD-9-CM: 272), urinary tract infection including cystitis (ICD-9-CM: 599, 595), hepatitis (ICD-9-CM: 070, 571, V02.61, V02.62), coronary heart disease (ICD-9-CM: 410–414), chronic kidney disease (ICD-9-CM: 585, 586, and 588), cerebral vascular accident (ICD-9-CM: 430–438), benign prostatic hyperplasia (ICD-9-CM: 600), and prostatitis (ICD-9-CM: 601). The patients were further classified into 7 groups based on age: those younger than 30 years, 31 to 40 years, 41 to 50 years, 51 to 60 years, 61 to 70 years, 71 to 80 years, and those older than 80 years.

2.5. Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics of the study subjects and their data were initially calculated with the Student t test and Chi-square test. Cox regression model was also used to estimate the relationship between the risk factors and APOUR after spinal surgery. All of the tests were 2-sided, and P-value < .05 was regarded as statistically significant. All the statistical analyses were performed with SAS 9.2 software (NC).

4. Discussion

This is a retrospective cohort study of 16,160 test subjects who were identified in a large-scale medical database to identify risk factors for APOUR after spinal surgery. This database includes comprehensive, continuous medical health information of residents of Taiwan over a period exceeding 10 years, which provides an excellent opportunity to identify long-term risk factors of APOUR after spinal surgery. We conducted this retrospective study over a 14-year nationwide cohort data base that may provide useful information for both clinical practice and for the development of new healthcare policies.

In our study, undergoing spinal surgery more than 2 times, male gender, and advanced age were all identified as statistically significant risk factors for APOUR after spinal surgery. This may facilitate understanding more about the development of APOUR after spinal surgery and apply a risk prevention and early intervention strategy in medical practice. Significantly longer admissions were also noted in our study in the APOUR group, which is expected given that AUR is a postoperative complication requiring additional management.

The detrusor muscle, located within the bladder wall, contracts and assists in bladder emptying. The emptying and filling of the urinary bladder is coordinated by the brain, neural pathways, the detrusor muscle and the urethral sphincters and outlet tract.[15] The mechanism of the bladder emptying is that nerve impulses from the bladder transmit their signal to the spinal cord, which then travels to the brain, specifically the pons, which then generates parasympathetic signals back to the urinary bladder through the spinal cord, resulting in contraction of the detrusor muscle as well as relaxation of the internal and external urethral sphincters.[16] As mentioned previously, the coordination of bladder filling, storage of urine, and voluntary micturition are under the control of the supraspinal, central, somatic, and visceral neurons in the thoracic, lumbar, and sacral spinal cord, and the pons.[17] Aging causes degeneration of this pathway, which may explain the increased risk of AUR after spinal surgery in older patients. Undergoing multiple spinal surgeries may also increase the possibility of damaging this pathway or transiently injuring neural tissue.

Steggall et al reviewed postoperative urinary retention and found that male gender and age over 50 years are significant risk factors for APOUR. In addition to spinal surgery, they found that abdominal surgery is also a risk factor for APOUR.[11] Lee et al conducted a study with 1591 patients who underwent spinal surgery and reported the incidence of APOUR as 2.14%,[18] which is similar to the incidence of APOUR in our study (2.67%). We reviewed an additional study with 284 patients who underwent spinal surgery and determined that the spinal surgical approach (anterior or posterior) and surgical method (decompression or fusion) were not significant risk factors for APOUR.[19] Boulis et al also demonstrated that there are no differences in the incidence of APOUR between cervical and lumbar surgical procedures, and neither the duration of the surgical procedure nor the individual surgeon are risk factors for APOUR.[6] Postoperative urinary tract infections in male patients were a statistically significant risk factor for urinary retention in this study. In a previous study, Tammela et al revealed that among 198 patients who suffered from APOUR, 32 females (43.2%) and 22 males (17.4%) had at least 1 episode of a urinary tract infection during the acute perioperative period.[20] Another retrospective study also reported that urinary tract infection within 90 days before surgery increased the likelihood of developing APOUR.[7] In male patients, benign prostatic hyperplasia is known to be a contributing factor for chronic urinary retention which frequently leads to recurrent urinary tract infections and eventually may lead to APOUR.[7,21]

Indwelling catheterization is the standard of care in patients with AUR.[22] Catheterization for indwelling placement is an invasive procedure with potential complications such as catheter-associated urinary tract infections, urethral trauma, prostatitis, and pain.[9] Identifying possible risk factors for APOUR after spinal surgery may help to reduce the need for catheterization by using prophylactic interventions. Medications, including alpha 1-adrenoceptor blockers (α 1-blockers) or antispasmodics, and early mobilization have been proven to decrease the rate of AUR in a systemic review of AUR prevention measures.[23] Successful prevention of AUR may help to reduce the risk of catheter-related urinary tract infections and, urethral trauma, as well as improve the patients’ quality of life.

We acknowledge the limitations of our study. First, laboratory and image data including radiographs, computed tomography, and magnetic resonance imaging were not available in the NHIRD database. Laboratory data and imaging data may be of great help in differentiating between severe and mild spinal diseases in clinical practice and could further identify additional risk factors for postoperative urinary retention. Although we did not obtain detailed clinical information of the patients receiving spinal surgery, we did record different approaches of spinal surgery by way of the ICD-9 coding system, which was included in the NHIRD database. Second, all of the disease diagnoses in this study were entered into the NHIRD database using the ICD-9 coding system. Misinterpreted data or inaccurate diagnoses may have occurred in this study. Third, our population-based study is a retrospective study which has its own limitations. Fourth, the causal relationship between surgical method, gender, and AUR after spinal surgery was not fully elucidated. Prospective studies are warranted to further establish the risk factors of AUR after spinal surgery.

5. Conclusions

APOUR is a significant complication after spinal surgery. Our retrospective cohort study suggests that the incidence of APOUR after spinal surgery is 2.67%, with biological males, advanced age, and multiple spinal surgical procedures identified as statistically significant risk factors. This information may become critical in the management of postoperative patient care after spinal surgery. Prophylactic intervention, including pharmacological and non-pharmacological, may be considered for those identified as having a high risk of APOUR. Prospective studies are warranted to establish the causal relationship between these and possibly other risk factors and APOUR.

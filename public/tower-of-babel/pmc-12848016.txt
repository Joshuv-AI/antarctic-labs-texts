Clinical predictive fusion network for accurate disease prediction in patient cohorts
Authors: Md Zeyauddin, Shafiqul Abidin, Imran Khan, Mohammad Ubaidullah Bokhari, Md Ashraf Siddiqui, Ausaf Ahmad, Shadab Alam
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The increasing complexity of healthcare data demands predictive models that are both accurate and interpretable. This study presents the Clinical Predictive Fusion Network (CPFN). This adaptive ensemble learning framework integrates Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and Support Vector Machine classifiers through a validation-driven weighted fusion strategy. The model’s adaptive weighting enables it to learn the relative reliability of base classifiers across multimodal patient datasets. CPFN was evaluated using 10-fold stratified cross-validation on disease-specific (cardiology, neurology, diabetes, pulmonology, and oncology) and a synthetically fused multi-disease dataset, achieving up to 93.0 ± 0.4% accuracy on individual datasets and 95.5 ± 0.3% on the combined dataset. Other metrics included a recall of 92.0 ± 0.5%, F1-score of 92.5 ± 0.4%, and ROC-AUC ranging from 0.95 to 0.975 (95% CI, bootstrap 1000 resamples). These results demonstrate that CPFN maintains consistent and generalizable performance across heterogeneous data sources. The model’s transparent fusion design and detailed pseudocode enhance reproducibility and clinical applicability, positioning CPFN as a scalable, data-driven decision-support framework for next-generation predictive healthcare systems.

Introduction

The growing availability of healthcare data across various modalities, including electronic health records (EHRs), laboratory results, and medical imaging, has transformed the field of predictive analytics. However, the heterogeneity and complexity of these datasets present persistent challenges to accuracy, interpretability, and generalizability in clinical prediction models. Artificial Intelligence (AI), particularly machine learning (ML) and deep learning (DL), has emerged as a transformative tool capable of identifying hidden patterns and supporting evidence-based medical decisions1. Predictive analytics in healthcare typically follows a structured and iterative cycle comprising six essential phases: problem definition, data collection, data cleaning, model development, model testing, and model deployment. This cyclic process ensures continuous model refinement, emphasizing the critical role of data quality and model monitoring in maintaining clinical reliability (Fig. 1). Models underpinned by AI techniques have demonstrated strong potential in managing high-dimensional patient data, facilitating early disease detection, and optimizing treatment2.

Despite these advancements, several limitations remain. Many existing ML-based predictive models demonstrate strong retrospective accuracy but lack sufficient cross-disease validation and multi-modal adaptability, resulting in reduced performance when applied to heterogeneous patient populations. Moreover, ensemble methods, such as bagging, boosting, and fixed-weight voting, often employ static classifier weighting schemes that limit adaptability and interpretability. To address these challenges, this study proposes the Clinical Predictive Fusion Network (CPFN). This adaptive ensemble learning framework integrates Logistic Regression, Random Forest, Neural Network, and SVM through an adaptive, validation-driven weighted fusion framework. Unlike traditional ensemble methods that apply static or manually assigned weights, CPFN learns optimal weights automatically via cross-validated performance optimization, allowing it to adapt to varying feature spaces and disease domains. This design improves model robustness, interpretability, and scalability across heterogeneous clinical datasets. The model is evaluated using synthetically integrated healthcare datasets designed to emulate multi-modal patient conditions. The single-disease datasets (e.g., UCI Liver Disorder, Kaggle Diabetic Retinopathy, and MIMIC-III) comprise real patient records and were utilized for individual disease-level evaluation. To assess generalization across diverse modalities, a synthetic combined dataset was constructed by combining features from independent clinical sources. While this synthetic integration facilitates diverse feature representation, it may lead to optimistic performance estimates. This limitation is acknowledged and further discussed in the Discussion section.

The principal motivation behind CPFN is to bridge the gap between high-performing experimental AI models and reliable clinical decision-support tools. By explicitly incorporating model validation feedback into the ensemble weighting process, CPFN delivers consistent performance across disease types while maintaining interpretability. Literature review

According to modern research papers, AI technology, specifically through ML and DL models, proves its effectiveness in healthcare by predicting patient outcomes, assessing disease risks, and enabling clinical decisions. These models’ analysis of extensive medical datasets yields evidence-based conclusions that can improve patient care through enhanced precision and effectiveness. Translating these promising findings from experimental studies into actual healthcare operations presents significant challenges because the models require testing for their universal validity, ethical transparency, and human-understandable decision-making processes.

Overview of AI applications in healthcare

Artificial intelligence (AI) technologies, particularly machine learning (ML) and deep learning (DL), have demonstrated substantial promise in healthcare, enabling improved disease prediction, patient stratification, and treatment outcome modeling. Prior studies show that DL methods can enhance diagnostic and prognostic accuracy compared to classical approaches4,5. At the same time, investigations into demographic6 and distributional biases highlight significant limitations for the safe deployment of these technologies in clinical settings7. Reviews and position papers5,8–10 further emphasize the need for robust evaluation, transparency, and clinical validation when translating algorithmic performance into real-world care.

Retrospective cohort analyses and real-world evaluations

Retrospective cohort analyses and real-world evaluations have become key approaches for assessing clinical utility, as they utilize historical patient records and operational data to test models in settings closer to practice. Johnson and Doe11 and Doe et al.2 exemplify how retrospective analyses provide actionable evidence for triage and outcome prediction, while Brown et al.4 and Smith et al.12 illustrate how diverse patient data can guide continuous model refinement. Despite these advantages, gaps remain in the scale, diversity, and multi-center representativeness of many retrospective studies (see Table 1).

Disease-specific applications and comparative studies

Disease-specific applications and comparative evaluations demonstrate both the strengths and limits of current methods. For instance, ML approaches have demonstrated substantial improvements in oncology outcome prediction16, risk stratification in cardiology17, and clinical diagnostic accuracy in multiple studies18. The importance of bias mitigation in model design and evaluation, as underscored by ethical and fairness concerns documented by Davis and Johnson19 and others, is a critical aspect of the use of ML in healthcare. Similarly, applications in surgical outcome prediction20 and mental health personalization21 indicate broad applicability, but often reveal dataset- and task-specific performance constraints.

Multi-modal and explainable AI trends

Multi-modal integration and explainable AI, two rapidly evolving trends, have been significantly advanced by several recent works. These works have combined imaging, EHR22, and IoT-derived signals to produce fused models with interpretability primitives (e.g., attention or SHAP-based explanations) that aid clinical trust23–26. Domain-specific deep models (e.g., CNNs for imaging) have also been enhanced with XAI25 to increase transparency in high-risk diagnosis26. Multi-modal, explainable, and fusion-based AI approaches.

Ensemble learning and fusion strategies have been widely used to enhance predictive performance and stability. Traditional ensemble methods, such as bagging, boosting, stacking, and simple weighted voting, combine multiple base learners to reduce variance and bias. Prior healthcare-focused ensembles e.g., fusion models for cardiac26 and mental-health prediction23, demonstrate gains in accuracy and robustness. Still, most implementations rely on fixed, heuristic, or manually tuned weighting schemes that may not adapt well to heterogeneous or shifting data distributions. This constrains interpretability and reduces the ensemble’s capacity to rebalance contributions across different subpopulations or modalities.

While recent tabular and multimodal methods, for example, attention-based tabular models such as TabNet27 and transformer adaptations for structured clinical data28,29 have shown strong empirical performance, they typically require large labeled cohorts and substantial computational resources. CPFN is designed to occupy a complementary niche, leveraging well-understood, interpretable base classifiers such as Logistic Regression30, Random Forest31, Neural Network32, and SVM33, and enhancing their collective behavior via a performance-driven adaptive weighting mechanism. Rather than relying on static or heuristic weights, CPFN learns classifier contributions through validation-based optimization, thereby improving adaptability across single-disease and combined-source evaluations while retaining the interpretability crucial for clinical contexts.

Synthesis and research gaps

In summary, while existing AI-based predictive models demonstrate strong potential for clinical decision support, most continue to face challenges related to interpretability, generalization, and multi-source data integration. Retrospective analyses have proven valuable for assessing model reliability; however, few studies have explored adaptive ensemble techniques capable of learning from diverse, heterogeneous patient data. To bridge this gap, the present study proposes the Clinical Predictive Fusion Network (CPFN): an adaptive, validation-driven weighted ensemble that dynamically adjusts classifier contributions based on cross-validated performance feedback. By combining multi-domain datasets and incorporating a reproducible 10-fold cross-validation framework with bootstrap confidence intervals, CPFN addresses both methodological robustness and clinical applicability, thereby contributing to the development of scalable and transparent predictive healthcare systems.

Research methodology

This study employs a retrospective cohort analysis to evaluate the clinical utility and predictive validity of the Clinical Predictive Fusion Network (CPFN). By leveraging a rich dataset of historical patient records that encompasses diverse demographics, diseases, and outcomes, the approach enables rigorous validation of CPFN’s generalizability in real-world contexts. Data preprocessing ensures quality through the imputation of missing values, correction of inconsistencies, and normalization of clinical features, followed by feature extraction to identify significant predictors.

Model implementation and evaluation

The Clinical Predictive Fusion Network (CPFN) framework was implemented to predict clinical outcomes, including disease classification, treatment response, and readmission risk, using retrospective patient data. CPFN integrates multiple base classifiers-Logistic Regression, Random Forest, Support Vector Machine, and Neural Network within an adaptive ensemble learning structure. Model training and evaluation were conducted using a 10-fold stratified cross-validation design to ensure robust generalization. In each fold, the dataset was divided into nine folds for training and one fold for validation/testing. Hyperparameter tuning for each base classifier was performed within the training folds using grid search, and the best-performing configuration was retained.

Following base model training, the predictions from all classifiers were fused through an adaptive weight optimization process, which learns the contribution of each classifier based on its validation performance within the training data. The optimal weights were determined under non-negativity and unit-sum constraints, as detailed in the Proposed Model section. The final predictions for each fold were generated by applying the learned ensemble weights to the outputs of the trained base classifiers. Reported metrics represent the mean performance across all folds, with 95% confidence intervals estimated using 1000 bootstrap resamples of the fold-level scores. The overall methodological workflow, including data preprocessing, model training, and validation sequence, is summarized in Fig. 3.

Clinical utility and bias assessment

The Clinical Predictive Fusion Network (CPFN) was further analyzed to assess its clinical relevance and fairness. To evaluate practical utility, predictions were examined across key clinical outcomes, including disease type, treatment response, and readmission risk. Subgroup analyses were conducted across age, gender, and ethnicity within the 10-fold cross-validation framework to ensure fairness and consistent performance across populations. This step provided insight into the model’s potential for reliable deployment in hospital decision-support systems while identifying possible bias or underperformance in specific subgroups.

Classification algorithms

The CPFN framework integrates several well-established supervised learning algorithms, including Support Vector Machines (SVM)33, Random Forests (RF)31, Neural Networks32, and Logistic Regression (LR)30. These models, which have been widely used in healthcare analytics for disease classification, risk stratification, and outcome prediction, are integrated into the CPFN framework. Their detailed mathematical formulations are extensively documented in the cited literature. Therefore, this study focuses on their integration through the proposed weighted fusion mechanism, rather than restating standard algorithmic equations.

Experimental framework

The overall experimental pipeline for evaluating the Clinical Predictive Fusion Network (CPFN) follows a reproducible and statistically rigorous workflow. The framework integrates data preprocessing, model training, cross-validated evaluation, and interpretability analysis, ensuring that clinical relevance and generalization are systematically assessed.

Data preprocessing involved a thorough process, including imputing missing values, z-score normalization, and one-hot encoding for categorical attributes. The prepared dataset was then rigorously evaluated using 10-fold stratified cross-validation, maintaining proportional representation of disease categories and demographic groups in each fold. During each fold, base classifiers, including Logistic Regression, Random Forest, Support Vector Machine, and Neural Network, were trained and optimized within the training subset using a grid search. The complexity of the process was further enhanced by adaptively learning ensemble weights within the cross-validation framework, with each classifier’s contribution reflecting its fold-level validation performance.

The reported results represent the mean performance across all folds, accompanied by 95% confidence intervals estimated via 1000 bootstrap resamples of the fold-level metrics. This approach, which avoids bias from single train–test splits, ensures the fairness of the evaluation process and supports reliable generalization estimates. All experiments were executed on a high-performance computing cluster using Python 3.10, TensorFlow 2.13, and Scikit-learn 1.4, with a fixed random seed (42) for reproducibility.

Performance metrics and diagnostic tools

Model evaluation focused on standard classification metrics that are directly reported in this manuscript: Accuracy, Precision, Recall (Sensitivity), and F1-score. These metrics were computed for each model and dataset using the predictions generated under the 10-fold stratified cross-validation protocol described in the Methods. Reported values in tables and text correspond to the mean across folds together with 95% confidence intervals estimated by non-parametric bootstrap resampling (1000 iterations) of the fold-level metric values.

Diagnostic assessment employed confusion matrices to examine class-wise error patterns (true positives, true negatives, false positives, and false negatives) and to quantify the model’s tendencies to over- or under-predicting specific classes. Additionally, a feature correlation analysis (correlation matrix) was conducted to examine the relationships among input variables and identify potential multicollinearity that may impact classifier behavior. Results from the confusion matrices and correlation analysis are presented in Figs. 4 and 5, respectively, and interpreted in the Results section.

The dataset integrates information from five major medical domains, such as Diabetes, Cardiology, Neurology, Pulmonology, and Oncology, each containing disease-specific clinical variables associated with disease progression, treatment response, and prognosis. Proposed model: clinical predictive fusion network (CPFN)

The proposed Clinical Predictive Fusion Network (CPFN) is designed to enhance clinical prediction accuracy by integrating multiple base classifiers through an adaptiveweighted fusion mechanism. Unlike conventional ensemble methods that rely on static or heuristic weighting, CPFN dynamically learns the contribution of each classifier based on validation performance, resulting in robust and interpretable predictions across diverse disease domains.

Model overview

Each base classifier, such as Logistic Regression (LR), Support Vector Machine (SVM), Neural Network, and Random Forest (RF), is independently trained on the same feature space derived from patient data. These models capture complementary characteristics: LR provides probabilistic interpretability, SVM captures high-dimensional separability, and RF models non-linear feature interactions. Their outputs are combined in a weighted decision fusion layer, where classifier contributions are automatically optimized through cross-validation-based learning.

Discussion

The proposed Clinical Predictive Fusion Network (CPFN) demonstrated consistently higher predictive performance than both traditional machine-learning methods and recent advanced models for healthcare data. Its ensemble design, combining Logistic Regression, Random Forest, Support Vector Machine, and Neural Network through adaptive weighting, enabled improved representation of heterogeneous feature spaces across multiple disease domains. This approach effectively leveraged the complementary strengths of linear, tree-based, and neural models, resulting in enhanced predictive stability and generalization. Compared with conventional algorithms such as Random Forest, Gradient Boosting, and SVM, CPFN achieved superior accuracy and F1-scores across all evaluated datasets. The improvement is particularly notable in minority-class detection, suggesting that adaptive fusion mitigates imbalance-related bias often observed in clinical datasets. This aligns with prior evidence that hybrid ensemble methods can outperform single learners when modeling heterogeneous patient populations.

Unlike prior studies that primarily focused on single-disease prediction (e.g., heart failure, COVID-19 outcomes, or readmission risk), CPFN was evaluated across five distinct disease domains, such as diabetes, cardiology, neurology, pulmonology, and oncology, and demonstrated strong generalizability. The model’s scalability suggests its potential as a unified framework for multi-disease predictive analytics, rather than disease-specific systems.

Furthermore, by integrating explainable components such as SHAP and LIME, CPFN provides interpretability that is often lacking in complex AI systems. This transparency supports clinical adoption, as physicians can validate model-driven insights and relate predictions to established medical reasoning.

The comparison with earlier studies22,30,38 highlights CPFN’s performance advantage, with accuracies exceeding 95% compared to C-statistics of around 0.73–0.76 in prior Gradient Boosting or Logistic Regression models. This improvement demonstrates that adaptive ensemble fusion can yield clinically meaningful gains in predictive accuracy and robustness. The findings collectively suggest that CPFN can help clinicians with data-driven treatment planning, early risk identification, and informed decision-making. Its consistent performance across diverse diseases highlights its potential for real-world deployment in integrated clinical decision-support pipelines.

Nevertheless, some limitations must be acknowledged. The combined dataset used in this study was synthetically constructed by merging multiple independent clinical datasets, which may not fully reflect real-world multimodal patient records. Consequently, future work should validate CPFN on accurate multi-source patient-level data and assess model calibration and fairness metrics. Additionally, although interpretability methods such as SHAP and LIME provide local explanations, integrating causal and temporal interpretability frameworks could further strengthen clinical reliability.

In summary, CPFN represents a robust, interpretable, and scalable ensemble framework for predictive healthcare analytics. Its superior performance, validated across multiple disease domains, supports its potential utility as a next-generation tool for clinical risk prediction and precision medicine.

Limitation

This study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. First, the datasets used for model development and evaluation were compiled from publicly available clinical sources that may not fully capture the demographic, geographic, and clinical diversity of real-world patient populations. As a result, the model’s generalizability across broader healthcare settings remains to be validated.

Second, although the CPFN framework integrates multiple classifiers through adaptive fusion, it was trained and evaluated on retrospective data under a fixed experimental protocol. Future work should investigate alternative base learners, ensemble weighting strategies, and longitudinal validation to ensure robustness across varying data distributions.

Third, computational efficiency and scalability were not explicitly analyzed in this study. Evaluating model latency, resource consumption, and deployment feasibility will be essential for clinical integration. Additionally, while interpretability mechanisms such as SHAP and LIME provide localized explanations, future research should explore causal and temporal interpretability frameworks to strengthen clinical transparency.

Addressing these limitations in future studies through validation on multi-institutional datasets, real-time performance testing, and advanced interpretability analyses will enhance the reliability, applicability, and translational impact of the CPFN framework in clinical practice.

Conclusion

This study introduces the Clinical Predictive Fusion Network (CPFN), a novel ensemble learning framework that integrates multiple machine learning models to enhance predictive accuracy and generalizability in clinical decision-making. Evaluated across five disease domains and a combined dataset, CPFN consistently achieved superior performance compared to classical and contemporary models, demonstrating its ability to capture complex and heterogeneous patterns in patient data.

The model’s adaptive fusion strategy and multi-disease scalability position it as a promising foundation for next-generation predictive healthcare systems. Beyond improving diagnostic accuracy, CPFN contributes to the development of reliable, interpretable, and data-driven clinical support tools that enhance patient care and outcomes.

Future research should focus on validating the model using multi-institutional, patient-level datasets and expanding interpretability frameworks further to enhance transparency, fairness, and clinical trustworthiness. With these advancements, CPFN has the potential to serve as an effective and generalizable tool for precision medicine and real-time healthcare analytics.

Case scenario: clinical use of CPFN outputs

To illustrate the potential clinical application of the Clinical Predictive Fusion Network (CPFN), consider a scenario involving Dr. Sharma, a primary care physician evaluating a patient with nonspecific symptoms suggestive of early-stage mental health concerns. Using the CPFN system integrated into the hospital’s electronic health record (EHR) platform, Dr. Sharma receives an automated alert indicating an elevated risk for anxiety and depression based on multimodal patient data, including self-reported questionnaires, biometric indicators, and speech analysis.

The CPFN output provides a risk stratification report that highlights key contributing factors and offers evidence-based recommendations, such as targeted screening tools and referral pathways. Dr. Sharma reviews these insights in conjunction with traditional clinical findings, initiates appropriate diagnostic evaluations, and schedules timely follow-ups. The model thereby augments, rather than replaces, clinical judgment, enabling proactive, personalized care. This seamless integration exemplifies how predictive analytics can translate into tangible improvements in early detection, intervention, and patient outcomes.

Interpreting CPFN outputs in clinical practice

The CPFN framework generates individualized risk scores, along with associated confidence levels and feature contributions (e.g., abnormal heart rate variability, delayed cognitive response times, or linguistic cues in speech and text). These results are presented through an interactive dashboard embedded within the clinician’s interface, displaying both numerical probabilities and interpretable visual explanations derived from SHAP and LIME analyses.

Clinicians can interpret these outputs as probabilistic indicators of specific health conditions while examining the underlying drivers of each prediction. This design ensures transparency, supports trust in AI-assisted recommendations, and helps physicians determine appropriate next steps, whether further diagnostic testing, early therapeutic intervention, or referral to a specialist. By combining quantitative precision with explainability, CPFN bridges the gap between predictive modeling and actionable clinical decision support.

Future scope

The present work opens multiple avenues for advancing AI-enabled healthcare analytics. Future studies should employ larger, more representative, and longitudinal datasets to enhance the model’s external validity and robustness across diverse populations. Incorporating real-time data streams from wearable sensors, mobile applications, and electronic health records could further improve responsiveness and personalization in clinical prediction.

Additionally, extending the CPFN architecture into hybrid or multimodal learning frameworks that combine structured, textual, and imaging data would broaden its applicability across complex clinical scenarios. Ongoing research should also emphasize model interpretability, fairness, and deployment efficiency, ensuring that predictive systems are not only accurate but also transparent, equitable, and operationally viable in real-world healthcare settings.

By integrating these advancements, future iterations of CPFN can support a new generation of intelligent, patient-centered clinical decision-support tools that enhance diagnosis, treatment planning, and precision medicine.

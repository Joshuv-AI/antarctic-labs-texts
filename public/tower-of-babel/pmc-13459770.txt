Multifunctional properties of quaternary Heusler alloys RuGdMnAl and CoHfVGa: structural stability, half-metallicity, and thermoelectric performance investigated using first principles
Authors: Zineb Hamadi, Ayache Mebarek Azzem, Fatima Bendahma, Fatiha Bessaha, Surendra Singh Sengar, Dinesh C. Gupta
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The structural, electronic, magnetic, elastic, phonon, and thermoelectric properties of the quaternary Heusler alloys RuGdMnAl and CoHfVGa were investigated using spin-polarized density functional theory within the FP-LAPW method implemented in the WIEN2k package. The dynamical stability of both compounds was confirmed by phonon dispersion calculations, which show the absence of imaginary phonon modes throughout the Brillouin zone. Thermodynamic stability was confirmed through negative formation energies, while the calculated elastic parameters satisfy the Born–Huang criteria, indicating mechanically stable and ductile behavior. Electronic structure calculations reveal that RuGdMnAl exhibits half-metallic ferromagnetism with an indirect minority-spin band gap of 0.220 eV, whereas CoHfVGa has a ferromagnetic semiconducting electronic structure with pronounced exchange-induced spin splitting and indirect band gaps of 0.349 eV and 1.026 eV for the majority- and minority-spin channels, respectively. The calculated magnetic moments obey the Slater–Pauling rule, confirming robust ferromagnetic ordering and high spin polarization. Additional GGA+U and spin–orbit coupling calculations performed for RuGdMnAl confirm the robustness of its ferromagnetic ground state and reveal a small magnetocrystalline anisotropy with the [100] direction as the easy magnetization axis. Thermoelectric transport properties, evaluated using the semi-classical Boltzmann transport formalism, show enhanced Seebeck coefficients and electrical conductivity for both compounds. The maximum electronic contribution to the thermoelectric figure of merit ZTe reaches 0.55 for RuGdMnAl and 1.0 for CoHfVGa at elevated temperatures, highlighting their strong potential for spintronic and high-temperature thermoelectric applications.

Introduction

Quaternary Heusler alloys constitute an important class of intermetallic compounds that have recently gained strong interest due to their tunable physical properties and broad applicability in advanced functional materials.1,2 Owing to their crystallization in the LiMgPdSn-type structure (space group F4̄3m), these materials allow a flexible distribution of atomic species over four inequivalent Wyckoff positions, making it possible to tailor their electronic, magnetic, and transport characteristics through appropriate choice of constituent elements.3,4 This structural versatility has positioned quaternary Heusler compounds as promising candidates for applications in spin-based electronics and energy conversion technologies.5,6

One of the most attractive features sought in Heusler-type materials is half-metallicity, a unique electronic state in which one spin channel exhibits metallic behavior while the opposite spin channel shows a band gap at the Fermi level. This property enables complete spin polarization of charge carriers, which is essential for the development of highly efficient spintronic devices such as spin valves, magnetic tunnel junctions, and non-volatile memory elements.7,8 Compared to ternary Heusler systems, quaternary alloys provide enhanced chemical stability and greater control over the electronic structure, allowing improved realization of half-metallic behavior and magnetic robustness.9,10

In addition to spintronic functionality, the exploration of thermoelectric properties in magnetic Heusler alloys has emerged as an important research direction, motivated by the growing demand for materials capable of efficient energy harvesting and waste heat recovery. The interplay between electronic band structure, magnetic ordering, and carrier transport in quaternary Heusler alloys can lead to favorable Seebeck coefficients, electrical conductivity, and power factors. Achieving a material that simultaneously exhibits half-metallicity and promising thermoelectric performance remains a challenging task, yet it holds significant potential for multifunctional device applications.11,12

Within this framework, the quaternary Heusler compounds RuGdMnAl and CoHfVGa represent compelling systems due to their distinct combination of rare-earth, transition-metal, and main-group elements. The coexistence of localized (4f) and itinerant (3d/4d) electronic states is expected to strongly influence their magnetic moments, electronic dispersion, and transport properties. However, despite their potential technological relevance, comprehensive theoretical investigations their structural stability, electronic structure, magnetic behavior, and thermoelectric performance remain limited in the literature.

Representative quaternary Heusler compounds reported in the literature include CoFeCrAl,13 CoFeMnSi, CoMnTiAl, FeMnTiSi, and RhMnTiGe, which have been extensively investigated due to their tunable electronic band structures, robust spin polarization, and promising half-metallic behavior.14–17 These systems serve as archetypal examples of the LiMgPdSn-type (F4̄3m) structure, where the strong interplay between chemical composition and atomic ordering governs the emergence of spin-dependent transport properties.

More closely related to the present study, compounds such as RuMnTiAl, RuMnHfGa, CoHfTiSn, CoHfMnGa, and RhHfTiAl have recently attracted increasing attention.18–20 These materials incorporate transition-metal (3d/4d) and main-group elements with comparable atomic sizes and electronegativity contrasts, which promotes significant hybridization between the d-orbitals and enable efficient tuning of magnetic exchange interactions. As a consequence, they provide a suitable framework for engineering half-metallicity, electronic anisotropy, and magneto-transport properties within the quaternary Heusler family.

In this context, related quaternary Heusler systems such as RhHfVGa21 have recently attracted attention as benchmark systems due to their structural stability and tunable electronic and magnetic properties, making them suitable for comparative analysis within the same family of materials. Nevertheless, systematic studies of RuGdMnAl and CoHfVGa in relation to such reference systems are still scarce, motivating further first-principles investigations.

The selection of RuGdMnAl and CoHfVGa is motivated by their distinctive chemical compositions and the limited number of comprehensive theoretical investigations. RuGdMnAl combines the rare-earth element Gd with the transition metals Ru and Mn, enabling the investigation of the interplay between localized Gd 4f states and itinerant Ru 4d/Mn 3d electrons, which is expected to strongly influence the magnetic exchange interactions, spin polarization, and electronic structure. On the other hand, CoHfVGa represents a transition-metal-based quaternary Heusler alloy with a different electronic configuration, providing an ideal counterpart for examining the effect of chemical substitution on the structural, electronic, magnetic, and thermoelectric properties. Therefore, studying these two compounds within the same theoretical framework not only fills an existing gap in the literature but also provides valuable insight into the role of rare-earth elements in designing multifunctional Heusler materials for spintronic and thermoelectric applications.

In this work, a comprehensive first-principles investigation of RuGdMnAl and CoHfVGa is carried out using the full-potential linearized augmented plane wave approach within density functional theory, as implemented in the WIEN2k code.22,23 Structural optimization is performed by considering three possible atomic arrangements, while electronic and magnetic properties are analyzed using both the generalized gradient approximation and the Tran–Blaha modified Becke–Johnson potential to achieve a more reliable description of exchange–correlation effects. Furthermore, thermoelectric transport coefficients are evaluated within the semi-classical Boltzmann transport formalism.24,25 The results presented here aim to clarify the fundamental physical properties of these quaternary Heusler alloys and to assess their suitability for spintronic and thermoelectric applications.

Computational details

All first-principles calculations were conducted within the framework of density functional theory (DFT),26,27 using the full-potential linearized augmented plane wave (FP-LAPW) method, as implemented in the WIEN2k package.28,29 The generalized gradient approximation (GGA) in the Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof (PBE) formulation was employed to treat exchange–correlation interactions. To achieve enhanced accuracy in predicting electronic band gaps and half-metallic behavior, the Tran–Blaha modified Becke–Johnson TB-mBJ potential was also utilized, which has been demonstrated to improve the description of exchange splitting in transition-metal and rare-earth-based Heusler compounds. To properly account for the highly localized and strongly correlated nature of the Gd 4f electrons in RuGdMnAl, the GGA+U approach was employed. Effective Hubbard parameters of Ueff = 2.3 eV for the Gd 4f states and Ueff = 1.69 eV for the Mn 3d states were adopted within the self-interaction correction (SIC) scheme, following the values reported in 30,31. The inclusion of the Hubbard correction provides a more accurate description of electron localization and magnetic exchange interactions. Furthermore, spin–orbit coupling (SOC) effects were taken into account to assess their influence on the electronic and magnetic properties of the compound.

The quaternary Heusler alloys RuGdMnAl and CoHfVGa were modeled in the cubic LiMgPdSn-type structure (space group F4̄3m), considering three distinct atomic configurations Type I, Type II, and Type III corresponding to different occupations of the 4a, 4b, 4c, and 4d Wyckoff positions. Full structural optimizations, including lattice parameters and atomic positions, were performed by minimizing the total energy via the Birch–Murnaghan equation of state.32 Both ferromagnetic (FM) and paramagnetic (PM) configurations were examined to determine the magnetic ground state of each atomic arrangement.

Muffin-tin radii (RMT) (Table 1) were carefully chosen to avoid charge leakage, with values of 2.50, 2.44, 2.44, and 2.32 a.u. for Ru, Gd, Mn, and Al in RuGdMnAl, and 2.50, 2.43, 2.38, and 2.47 a.u. for Co, Hf, V, and Ga in CoHfVGa. The plane-wave cutoff was defined by RKmax = 9, and the wave functions within the atomic spheres were expanded up to lmax = 10. Charge density and potential expansions in the interstitial region were truncated at Gmax = 14 a.u. A dense k-point mesh of 3000 points in the irreducible Brillouin zone was employed to ensure convergence of total energies, electronic structures, and magnetic moments.

Spin-polarized calculations were carried out to evaluate both total and site-resolved magnetic moments, allowing for verification of compliance with the Slater–Pauling rule and the distinction between localized and itinerant electron contributions. Electronic band structures and densities of states (DOS) were calculated using both GGA and TB-mBJ functionals,33 with the Fermi level serving as the reference energy.

Thermoelectric transport properties, including the Seebeck coefficient, electrical conductivity, electronic thermal conductivity, power factor, and dimensionless electronic figure of merit (ZTe), were computed within the semi-classical Boltzmann transport formalism as implemented in the BoltzTraP code.34,35 All calculations were performed under the constant relaxation time approximation, and transport coefficients were evaluated as functions of the chemical potential at temperatures of 300, 600, and 900 K. Phonon dispersion calculations were performed using the finite-displacement method in the Phonopy package with a 3 × 3 × 3 supercell. The absence of imaginary phonon frequencies confirmed the dynamical stability of both compounds. Furthermore, ab initio molecular dynamics (AIMD) simulations performed at 600 K confirm the thermal stability of both compounds, with no evidence of structural degradation throughout the simulation. This comprehensive computational protocol provides a systematic assessment of the potential of RuGdMnAl and CoHfVGa as multifunctional materials for spintronic and thermoelectric applications.

For RuGdMnAl, the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) predicts a metallic character with band crossings at the Fermi level in both spin channels. However, the modified Becke–Johnson potential (TB-MBJ) induces a strong spin-dependent reconstruction of the electronic states. The spin-up channel remains metallic, whereas the spin-down channel exhibits the opening of an indirect band gap of 0.220 eV with the valence band maximum located at the Γ point and the conduction band minimum located at the L point. For CoHfVGa, the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) predicts an indirect band gap of 1.179 eV (Fig. 3) in the minority-spin channel, with the valence band maximum located at the Γ point and the conduction band minimum along the Δ direction, whereas the majority-spin channel exhibits metallic behavior with no band gap. Upon applying the Tran–Blaha modified Becke–Johnson (TB-mBJ) potential, a pronounced spin-dependent splitting of the electronic states is observed. The majority-spin channel exhibits an indirect band gap of 0.349 eV between the Γ and L points, while the minority-spin channel shows a larger indirect band gap of 1.026 eV between Γ and Δ. The opening of band gaps in both spin channels indicates a transition from a half-metallic to a semiconducting state.

To further assess the influence of electronic correlation effects associated with the localized Gd 4f states, additional spin-polarized GGA+U calculations were performed for RuGdMnAl (Fig. 4). The resulting band structure shows metallic behavior in both spin channels, with several bands crossing the Fermi level. The inclusion of the Hubbard correction modifies the band dispersion and enhances the localization of the Gd 4f states; however, no energy gap is formed at the Fermi level in either spin channel. Consequently, RuGdMnAl retains its ferromagnetic metallic character within the GGA+U framework. These results indicate that electronic correlations significantly influence the electronic structure while preserving the metallic nature and magnetic ground state of the compound.

In both compounds, the electronic states near the band edges are mainly governed by transition metal d orbitals, in particular t2g and eg hybridization, while the p states of Al and Ga contribute weakly through hybridization effects. The consistency of these features across both computational approaches confirms the robustness of the electronic structure and highlights the dominant role of d orbital hybridization in band gap formation in these quaternary Heusler compounds.48,49

It is not the actual mass of the electron; rather, it is a parameter that describes the response of charge carriers to an applied electric field. The obtained results show that the total magnetic moments are nearly integer-valued, in excellent agreement with the Slater–Pauling rule. mtot = Zt − 18,52 thereby confirming the half-metallic nature of RuGdMnAl compounds and magnetic semiconductor for CoHfVGa. In RuGdMnAl, the magnetic response is primarily governed by the strongly localized 4f states of Gd together with the 3d states of Mn, which contribute approximately 6.716 to 6.860µB for Gd and 3.194 to 3.403µB for Mn. In contrast, Ru and Al atoms exhibit negligible spin polarization, reflecting their weak magnetic character and nearly filled electronic shells.53

For CoHfVGa, the magnetic moment is mainly contributed by V and Co atoms, with V providing approximately 2.248 to 2.319µB and Co contributing a smaller but finite moment ranging from 0.110 to 0.188µB. The Hf and Ga atoms carry negligible induced magnetic moments, confirming their secondary role in the overall magnetic ordering. The consistency between GGA and TB mBJ results further validates the robustness of the predicted magnetic properties and indicates that the electronic correlations are adequately captured.

Spin-polarized total energy calculations demonstrate that the ferromagnetic configuration is energetically favored over the non-magnetic state for both systems, establishing a stable ferromagnetic ground state. This stability originates from strong exchange interactions between transition metal d orbitals and localized magnetic states, which collectively stabilize long-range magnetic ordering.54

Based on the TB-MBJ results, the spin-polarized band structures of RuGdMnAl show a clear half-metallic character, where one spin channel exhibits metallic behavior while the opposite channel shows a limited band gap.

In CoHfVGa, a pronounced spin-dependent band gap asymmetry is observed, reflecting a strong exchange splitting and indicating proximity to half-metallicity. The modified Becke–Johnson potential enhances the exchange splitting relative to the generalized gradient approximation, leading to a more accurate description of the electronic structure.

Additional calculations were performed using GGA+U and GGA+U + SOC to evaluate the effects of electronic correlation and spin–orbit coupling on the magnetic properties of RuGdMnAl. As shown in Table 7, only slight changes are observed in the atomic and total magnetic moments upon the inclusion of spin–orbit coupling. The magnetic moment of Gd remains close to 6.86µB, while the total magnetic moment is preserved at approximately 10µB. In contrast, the electronic structure is significantly affected, as the energy gap disappears when electronic correlation effects are taken into account. These results indicate that the ferromagnetic ground state is robust against both exchange–correlation and relativistic effects, whereas the energy gap is considerably more sensitive to the choice of computational approach.

Moreover, the total magnetic moment of RuGdMnAl remains close to the calculated value of 0.10.11µB. This result is consistent with previous studies on Gd-based Heusler and half-Heusler compounds, in which the localized Gd 4f shell typically contributes a magnetic moment close to 7µB. For example, GdPtSb and GdPdBi have been reported to exhibit Gd magnetic moments of approximately 6.8–6.9µB, while Gd-containing full-Heusler alloys such as Co2MnGdSi display a Gd moment of about 6.9µB. The large localized magnetic moment of Gd plays a dominant role in determining the overall magnetic behavior of these compounds. Therefore, the present GGA+U results for RuGdMnAl are in good agreement with the established magnetic characteristics of Gd-based Heusler materials, confirming the reliability of the predicted ferromagnetic ground state.

To further evaluate the thermal stability of the ferromagnetic state, the Curie temperature Tc was estimated using the empirical relationship557Tc = 23 + 181Mtotwhere Mtot is the total magnetic moment. The estimated Curie temperatures are 1833 K for RuGdMnAl and 566 K for CoHfVGa. Both values are significantly higher than room temperature, indicating robust ferromagnetic ordering and excellent thermal stability. The remarkably high Tc of RuGdMnAl is associated with its large total magnetic moment, mainly originating from the localized Gd 4f and Mn 3d states, whereas the lower Tc of CoHfVGa reflects its smaller total magnetic moment. These results further support the suitability of both compounds for spintronic applications operating at elevated temperatures.

The spin–orbit coupling (SOC) effect

To evaluate the impact of relativistic effects on the electronic structure of RuGdMnAl, spin–orbit coupling (SOC) was incorporated within the GGA+U framework. The corresponding spin-polarized band structures are presented in Fig. 3. The inclusion of SOC leads to slight modifications in the band dispersion and partially lifts the degeneracy of several electronic states along the high-symmetry directions of the Brillouin zone. However, the overall topology of the electronic band structure remains essentially unchanged.

Within the GGA+U + SOC framework, as shown in Fig. 4, both spin channels retain their metallic character, with only minor modifications in the electronic band dispersion compared to the GGA+U results. Overall, the SOC calculations indicate that relativistic effects have a limited influence on the electronic structure of RuGdMnAl. The slight changes observed in the band topology confirm that the predicted electronic and magnetic properties are robust against spin–orbit coupling effects. Furthermore, these findings demonstrate that the Hubbard correction plays the dominant role in accurately describing the localized Gd 4f states, whereas spin–orbit coupling introduces only minor perturbations to the electronic structure.

The spin–orbit coupling (SOC) effect was investigated by orienting the magnetization along the three principal crystallographic directions [1 0 0], [0 1 0], and [0 0 1]. As listed in Table 8 and Fig. 1, the spin magnetic moments exhibit only minor variations with the magnetization direction, indicating that SOC has a limited influence on the magnetic properties and the electronic properties of the compound. The spin-polarized density of states (DOS) is presented only for the [001] orientation. The magnetic moments are mainly localized on the Gd and Mn atoms, while the calculated orbital magnetic moments Lx, Ly, and Lz of the Gd and Mn atoms are very small, indicating a weak orbital contribution induced by spin–orbit coupling. Moreover, only slight variations are observed among the three magnetization directions, suggesting that the orbital moments are nearly isotropic. The total energy calculations reveal that the [100] direction possesses the lowest energy, identifying it as the easy magnetization axis. Accordingly, the magnetocrystalline anisotropy energy (MAE) was obtained from the total energy difference between the magnetization directions.

Thermoelectric properties

With the growing demand for energy efficiency, converting waste heat into electrical energy has become a key focus of modern research. Evaluating the thermoelectric performance of materials is therefore essential. The semi-classical Boltzmann transport theory, implemented within the BoltzTraP2 package,58,59 is employed. Since electrons are the primary carriers of energy and charge, the band structure directly influences transport properties. The electronic thermoelectric figure of merit6011 is used to quantify the efficiency of thermoelectric materials. As shown in Fig. 7, the Seebeck coefficient (S) exhibits a peak around 300 K and gradually decreases with increasing temperature, indicating typical behavior for these quaternary Heusler alloys.

Calculated Seebeck coefficient as a function of the chemical potential for (a) RuGdMnAl and (b) CoHfVGa, at 300, 600, and 900 K.

Compared with previously reported alloys, Haliot and Hamad reported Seebeck coefficients of 429.0 µV K−1 and −483.2 µV K−1 for CoFeCuPb at 300 K. In the present work, RuGdMnAl exhibits relatively modest Seebeck coefficient values of 183.68 µV K−1 for p-type carriers and −192.76 µV K−1 for n-type carriers at room temperature. In contrast, CoHfVGa displays exceptionally large Seebeck coefficients of 1713.39 µV K−1 for p-type carriers and −1785.60 µV K−1 for n-type carriers. These values greatly exceed those reported for CoFeCuPb and clearly demonstrate the outstanding thermoelectric response of CoHfVGa. The calculated results show that both compounds predominantly display positive Seebeck coefficients over a wide range of chemical potentials, confirming their p-type semiconducting nature.61 The temperature dependence of the Seebeck coefficient is mainly attributed to thermally induced variations in carrier concentration, as increasing temperature promotes charge carriers from the valence band to the conduction band. Additionally, enhanced phonon–carrier scattering at elevated temperatures further modifies the transport behavior, particularly in materials with similar lattice configurations, accounting for the observed evolution of the Seebeck coefficient with temperature.62,63,71–74

The relaxation time is governed by various scattering mechanisms, including impurity and defect scattering, electron–phonon interactions, electron–electron interactions, and magnetic scattering. In the present work, a constant relaxation time of τ = 5 × 10−15 s is employed.64,65 Within this approximation, the Seebeck coefficient is independent of the relaxation time, whereas the electrical conductivity (σ), electronic thermal conductivity (κe), and power factor (S2σ) are directly proportional to τ. Consequently, the selected value of τ affects only the absolute magnitudes of these transport coefficients without altering their temperature dependence or the relative comparison between the investigated compounds, as commonly assumed in Boltzmann transport theory. The electrical conductivity as a function of chemical potential for RuGdMnAl and CoHfVGa at 300, 600, and 900 K is displayed in Fig. 8. For both compounds, the electrical conductivity exhibits a clear minimum in the vicinity of the Fermi level, corresponding to the band-gap region where charge carriers are scarce. At 300 K, the conductivity values are relatively low near µ − EF = 0 eV and increase rapidly as the chemical potential moves toward the valence- and conduction-band edges. With increasing temperature, the electrical conductivity is significantly enhanced across the entire chemical-potential range, indicating thermally activated carrier generation. In particular, CoHfVGa shows a more pronounced increase in electrical conductivity away from the band-gap region and attains higher conductivity values compared to RuGdMnAl, especially at 600 and 900 K. This behavior suggests more efficient charge-carrier transport in CoHfVGa and confirms the semiconducting nature of both materials. The observed trends are consistent with previously reported results for similar Heusler-type and quaternary alloys. The temperature-dependent transport behavior obtained in this work is in good agreement with previously reported thermoelectric properties of Heusler and quaternary Heusler compounds. In particular, Fe2VAl66 has been extensively investigated as a prototypical Heusler system exhibiting semiconducting or pseudogap character, where the electrical conductivity increases with temperature as a result of thermally activated carrier excitation across the energy gap. Similarly, quaternary Heusler alloys such as CoFeTiSn and CoFeVGa67 have been reported to display analogous transport behavior, characterized by a strong temperature dependence of the electrical conductivity within the framework of semi-classical Boltzmann transport theory. In these systems, the enhancement of conductivity at elevated temperatures is primarily governed by increased carrier concentration arising from thermal excitation across the band gap or pseudogap, together with the contribution of band-edge states to charge transport. The present results for RuGdMnAl and CoHfVGa are therefore consistent with these established trends, confirming that their transport properties are intrinsically linked to their electronic structure and follow the typical thermally activated conduction mechanism observed in Heusler-type thermoelectric materials.

Calculated electrical conductivity as a function of the chemical potential for (a) RuGdMnAl and (b) CoHfVGa at 300, 600 and 900 K.

The power factor (PF), defined as12PF = S2σ,is a decisive parameter for evaluating the thermoelectric efficiency of materials, as it quantifies their capability to convert thermal gradients into electrical power.68Fig. 9 presents the calculated PF as a function of the chemical potential for RuGdMnAl and CoHfVGa at 300, 600, and 900 K. For both compounds, the PF exhibits a strong dependence on temperature and carrier concentration, with a marked enhancement observed as the temperature increases from 300 to 900 K.

Calculated the power factor as a function of the chemical potential for (a) RuGdMnAl and (b) CoHfVGa at 300, 600 and 900 K.

For RuGdMnAl, the PF reaches maximum values of approximately 1.8 × 103 µW K−2 m−1 at 300 K, which increase to about 3.0 × 103 µW K−2 m−1 at 600 K and further to nearly 4.0 × 103 µW K−2 m−1 at 900 K, occurring mainly in the p-type region. The progressive rise in PF with temperature indicates a favorable interplay between the Seebeck coefficient and electrical conductivity, along with a slight shift of the optimal chemical potential toward higher hole concentrations.

In contrast, CoHfVGa displays superior thermoelectric performance across the entire temperature range. At room temperature, its PF attains a maximum value of about 3.8 × 103 µW K−2 m−1, which significantly increases to approximately 5.8 × 103 µW K−2 m−1 at 600 K and reaches nearly 7.0 × 103 µW K−2 m−1 at 900 K. These high PF values are observed near the valence band edge, confirming the dominance of p-type conduction and highlighting the material's enhanced electrical transport characteristics.

Overall, the higher PF values obtained for CoHfVGa, combined with their strong temperature enhancement, suggest that this compound is a more promising candidate for high-temperature thermoelectric applications compared to RuGdMnAl, as efficient thermoelectric materials require simultaneously large Seebeck coefficients and high electrical conductivity.

For RuGdMnAl, κe/τ shows a relatively weak and oscillatory variation with respect to the chemical potential. At 300 K, the values remain low, ranging from approximately 1 to 7 W m−1 K−1, with a clear minimum around the Fermi level µ − EF ≈ 0. Upon increasing the temperature to 600 K, the electronic thermal conductivity increases across the entire chemical potential range, reaching about 5–11 W m−1 K−1 while maintaining a similar energy-dependent behavior. At 900 K, a further enhancement is observed, with κe/τ attaining maximum values of nearly 13–14 W m−1 K−1, particularly around chemical potentials away from the Fermi level. This trend reflects the growing contribution of thermally excited charge carriers to heat transport.

In contrast, CoHfVGa (Fig. 10) exhibits a much stronger dependence of κe/τ on both temperature and chemical potential. At 300 K, the electronic thermal conductivity approaches zero near the Fermi level and increases markedly away from it, reaching about 12–13 W m−1 K−1 in the electron-doped region µ − EF ≈ +1.4 At 600 K, κe/τ increases substantially, with peak values of approximately 22–24 W m−1 K−1, while at 900 K it reaches a maximum of around 30–32 W m−1 K−1. The pronounced minimum near EF indicates suppressed carrier transport, whereas the rapid rise under doping highlights the dominant role of electronic contributions at elevated temperatures.

Overall, the significantly larger magnitude of κe/τ in CoHfVGa, especially at high temperatures, suggests stronger electronic heat transport compared to RuGdMnAl, which is a key factor to consider in thermoelectric performance optimization.

The thermoelectric efficiency of the studied quaternary Heusler compounds was evaluated through the electronic thermoelectric figure of merit (ZTe) as a function of chemical potential and temperature (Fig. 11). A clear contrast is observed between RuGdMnAl and CoHfVGa, indicating fundamentally different transport behaviors and thermoelectric strengths.68

Calculated the figure of merit electric as a function of the chemical potential (a) RuGdMnAl and (b) CoHfVGa at 300, 600 and 900 K.

For RuGdMnAl, the ZTe values remain moderate across the investigated chemical potential range, with a maximum value of approximately 0.5–0.55 at 300 K. The thermoelectric response is relatively sensitive to doping, and although both p-type and n-type regions contribute to transport, the overall efficiency remains limited. With increasing temperature up to 900 K, a gradual reduction in peak ZTe is observed, accompanied by a slight broadening of the response window. This behavior indicates moderate thermal stability but insufficient carrier optimization for high-performance thermoelectric applications. Therefore, RuGdMnAl can be classified as a moderate thermoelectric material, requiring further enhancement to enhance its performance.

In contrast, CoHfVGa exhibits a significantly superior thermoelectric response, with ZTe values approaching unity 0.95–1.0 at 300 K in the optimal chemical potential range. This indicates a highly efficient balance between electrical conductivity, Seebeck coefficient, and thermal transport. The material also shows a pronounced ambipolar behavior, with nearly symmetric contributions from both electron and hole carriers, suggesting excellent tunability under both n-type and p-type doping conditions. Although ZTe slightly decreases with increasing temperature, it remains high 0.85–0.9 at 900 K, confirming strong thermal robustness and stability of transport properties.

Phonon properties

The dynamical stability of RuGdMnAl and CoHfVGa was examined through phonon dispersion calculations along the high-symmetry path of the face-centered cubic Brillouin zone. The calculated phonon spectra are presented in Fig. 12 and 13. Both compounds exhibit no imaginary (negative) phonon frequencies throughout the entire Brillouin zone, confirming their dynamical stability in the equilibrium F4̄3m crystal structure. The acoustic phonon branches originate from zero frequency at the Γ point, satisfying the translational invariance condition, while the optical branches remain well defined and free from soft modes. The absence of unstable phonon modes indicates that the optimized crystal structures are dynamically stable and are expected to retain their structural integrity under small lattice perturbations. These results, together with the calculated formation energies and elastic stability criteria, provide strong evidence for the structural stability of both quaternary Heusler alloys.

To further evaluate the thermal stability of the optimized quaternary Heusler alloys, ab initio molecular dynamics (AIMD) simulations were performed within the canonical (NVT) ensemble using the Nosé–Hoover thermostat at 600 K for a total simulation time of 1 ps with a time step of 1 fs. The time evolution of the temperature, potential energy, and kinetic energy for RuGdMnAl and CoHfVGa is presented in Fig. 14 and 15. As shown, the instantaneous temperature fluctuates around the target value of 600 K, which is characteristic of a well-equilibrated NVT simulation. The potential energy exhibits only small oscillations without any noticeable drift throughout the simulation, indicating the absence of structural degradation or phase transformation. Likewise, the kinetic energy remains nearly constant with normal thermal fluctuations, demonstrating stable atomic motion and equilibrium during the AIMD run. These results confirm that both RuGdMnAl and CoHfVGa maintain their structural integrity under finite-temperature conditions. Combined with the phonon dispersion analysis, the AIMD simulations provide strong evidence for the good thermal and dynamical stability of the investigated quaternary Heusler alloys.

Conclusion

In conclusion, we have comprehensively investigated the structural, electronic, and thermoelectric properties of the quaternary Heusler alloys RuGdMnAl and CoHfVGa using density functional theory combined with semi-classical Boltzmann transport calculations as implemented in the WIEN2k code. Both compounds crystallize in the cubic Heusler structure and exhibit favorable electronic characteristics near the Fermi level that promote efficient charge carrier transport. The thermoelectric transport analysis reveals a pronounced temperature dependence of the Seebeck coefficient, electrical conductivity, and power factor for both materials. The power factor increases significantly with increasing temperature, reaching values on the order of 103 µW K−2 m−1, with CoHfVGa consistently exhibiting higher power factor values than RuGdMnAl, particularly in the p-type region, due to an optimized balance between electrical conductivity and thermopower. Most notably, the dimensionless thermoelectric figure of merit attains a maximum value of ZTe ≈ 1.0 for CoHfVGa, while RuGdMnAl reaches ZT ≈ 0.55 at elevated temperatures. These values indicate excellent thermoelectric efficiency for CoHfVGa and competitive performance for RuGdMnAl. The combination of high power factor and enhanced ZT highlights CoHfVGa as a highly promising candidate for high-temperature thermoelectric applications, with RuGdMnAl also demonstrating potential for practical thermoelectric device implementation.

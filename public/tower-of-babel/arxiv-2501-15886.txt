, which satisfy the multiplicativity relation

(2.1)

\displaystyle T_{n}T_{m}=\sum_{d|(n,m)}\chi(d)T_{\frac{nm}{d^{2}}}.

Moreover, for any
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{k}(N,\chi)
, one has

\langle T_{n}f_{1},f_{2}\rangle=\overline{\chi(n)}\langle f_{1},T_{n}f_{2}\rangle.

One might thus find an orthogonal basis
\mathcal{B}_{k}(M,\chi)
of
\mathcal{S}_{k}(M,\chi)
consisting of common eigenfunctions of all the Hecke operators
T_{n}
with
(n,N)=1
. For
each
f\in\mathcal{B}_{k}(M,\chi)
, denote by
\lambda_{f}(n)
the
n
-th Hecke eigenvalue,
which satisfies

T_{n}f(z)=\lambda_{f}(n)f(z)

for all
(n,M)=1
. From (
2.1
), one has seen that

\psi_{f}(m)\lambda_{f}(n)=\sum_{d|(n,m)}\chi(d)\psi_{f}\left(\frac{mn}{d^{2}}\right)

for any
m,n\geq 1
with
(n,M)=1
. In particular,
\psi_{f}(1)\lambda_{f}(n)=\psi(n)
, if
(n,M)=1
. Therefore, if
(mn,M)=1
,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\overline{\lambda_{f}(n)}=\overline{\chi(n)}\lambda_{f}(n),\quad\lambda_{f}(m)\lambda_{f}(n)=\sum_{d|(n,m)}\chi(d)\lambda_{f}\left(\frac{mn}{d^{2}}\right).

The Hecke eigenbasis
\mathcal{B}_{k}(M,\chi)
also contains a subset of newforms
\mathcal{B}^{\ast}_{k}(M,\chi)
, those forms which are simultaneous eigenfunctions of all the Hecke operators
T_{n}
for any
n\geq 1
, and normalized to have first Fourier coefficient
\psi_{f}(1)=1
. The elements of
\mathcal{B}^{\ast}_{k}(M,\chi)
are usually called primitive forms in the sense of Atkin-Lehner; that is, each one is
orthogonal to all oldforms, and is an eigenfunction of all the Hecke and Atkin-Lehner
operators. For convenience, we abbreviate the notation
\mathcal{B}^{\ast}_{k}(M)
to
\mathcal{B}^{\ast}_{k}
, the space of newforms of weight
k
and arbitrary level parameter, if one does not specify the level parameter.

2.2.
Automorphic
L
-functions and functional equations

In this part, we shall further shed some light on automorphic
L
-functions. Let
k\geq 2
be an even integer. We assume that
N\geq 1
is a positive integer, satisfying that
(N,M)=1
. For any newform
f\in\mathcal{B}_{k}^{\ast}(M)
, we define the attached
L
-function
L(f,s)
, for
\text{Re}(s)>1
, by the Dirichlet series
\sum_{n\geq 1}\lambda_{f}(n)n^{-s}
which can be expressed in terms of an Euler product

\prod_{p\nmid M}\left(1-\frac{\lambda_{f}(p)}{p^{s}}+\frac{1}{p^{2s}}\right)^{-1}\prod_{p\mid M}\left(1-\frac{\lambda_{f}(p)}{p^{s}}\right)^{-1}.

We proceed by defining the completed
L
-function

\Lambda(s,f)=\mathcal{Q}^{s/2}_{f}L_{\infty}(s,f)L(s,f),

with

L_{\infty}(s,f)=\pi^{-s}\Gamma{\left(\frac{s}{2}+\frac{k-1}{4}\right)}\Gamma{\left(\frac{s}{2}+\frac{k-1}{4}\right)}

and the (analytic) conductor
\mathcal{Q}_{f}
being
\mathcal{Q}_{f}\asymp M
. The completed
L
-function thus admits an analytical continuation to all
s\in\mathbb{C}
, and satisfies that

\Lambda(s,f)=\varepsilon(f)\Lambda(1-s,f),

where the root number
\varepsilon(f)=i^{k}\mu(M)\lambda_{f}(M)\sqrt{M}=\pm i^{k}
.

Let us now put

(2.3)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}(N)=\left\{g\in\text{GL}(3,\mathbb{Z}):g\equiv\left(\begin{matrix}\ast&\ast&\ast\\
\ast&\ast&\ast\\
0&0&\ast\end{matrix}\right)\mod N\right\}.

Let
F(z)
be a normalized Hecke-Maaß form of type
\nu=(\nu_{1},\nu_{2})
for the congruent subgroup
\Gamma_{0}(N)
with trivial nebentypus, which has a Fourier-Whittaker expansion with the Fourier
coefficients
A_{F}(m,n)
. The Fourier coefficients of
F
and that of its contragredient
\widetilde{F}
are
related by
A_{F}(m,n)=A_{\widetilde{F}}(n,m)
for any
(mn,N)=1
, with
A_{F}(1,1)=1
; see, for example,
[
13
, Chapter 6]
for definition and backgrounds. The Jacquet-Langlands
L
-function is given by
L(s,F)=\sum_{n\geq 1}A_{F}(n,1)n^{-s}
for
\text{Re}(s)>1
. Now, let

(2.4)

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}=-\nu_{1}-2\nu_{2}+1,\quad\alpha_{1}=-\nu_{1}+\nu_{2},\quad\alpha_{1}=2\nu_{1}+\nu_{1}+1

be the Langlands parameters of
F
. We define the completed
L
-function

\Lambda(s,F)=\mathcal{Q}^{s/2}_{F}\varepsilon(F)L_{\infty}(s,F)L(s,F),

where

L_{\infty}(s,F)=\pi^{-3s/2}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\Gamma{\left(\frac{s+\alpha_{i}}{2}\right)},

the (analytic) conductor
\mathcal{Q}_{F}\asymp N
, and
|\varepsilon(F)|=1
. One thus have the following functional equation

\Lambda(s,F)=\varepsilon(F)\Lambda(1-s,\widetilde{F}).

Next, for any
f\in\mathcal{B}^{\ast}_{k}(M)
, we turn to considering the
\text{GL}(3)\times\text{GL}(2)
Rankin-Selberg
L
-function
L(s,F\otimes f)
, which is given by the Dirichlet series

L(s,F\otimes f)=\sum_{m,n\geq 1}\frac{A_{F}(n,m)\lambda_{f}(n)}{(nm^{2})^{s}}

for
\text{Re}(s)>1
. Now, one defines the Gamma factor
L_{\infty}(s,F\otimes f)
by

(2.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}L_{\infty}(s,F\otimes f)&=\pi^{-3s}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\Gamma{\left(\frac{s+{(k-1)}/{2}+\alpha_{i}}{2}\right)}\Gamma{\left(\frac{s+{(k+1)}/{2}+\alpha_{i}}{2}\right)}\end{split}

and the (analytic) conductor
\mathcal{Q}_{F,f}\asymp N^{2}M^{3}
. The completed
L
-function defined by

\Lambda(s,F\otimes f)=\mathcal{Q}^{s/2}_{F,f}L(s,F\otimes f)

is thus an entire function with an analytic continuation to all
s\in\mathbb{C}
, and satisfies the function equation that

\Lambda(s,F\otimes f)=\varepsilon(F\otimes f)\Lambda(1-s,\widetilde{F}\otimes f).

2.3.
Epsilon constant

In this part, we will be dedicated to developing some facts on the epsilon constant
\varepsilon(F\otimes f)
, which play a key rôle in later analysis. We proceed by borrowing some points of view from the automorphic representations of algebraic groups over local fields. Let the parameters
k,N,M
be as before. Define

k_{2,p}=\left\{g\in\text{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}_{p}):g\equiv\left(\begin{matrix}\ast&\ast\\
0&\ast\end{matrix}\right)\bmod p\right\}

and

k_{3,p}=\left\{g\in\text{GL}(3,\mathbb{Z}_{p}):g\equiv\left(\begin{matrix}\ast&\ast&\ast\\
\ast&\ast&\ast\\
0&0&\ast\end{matrix}\right)\bmod p\right\}.

For any given holomorphic newform
f\in L^{2}(\Gamma_{0}(M)\backslash\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{R}))
of weight
k
, let us proceed by defining a function
\varphi_{f}\in L^{2}(\text{GL}(2,\mathbb{Q})\backslash\text{GL}(2,\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})/\text{K}(2,M))
by

\varphi_{f}(\gamma g_{\infty}k)=f(g_{\infty})

for
\gamma\in\text{GL}(2,\mathbb{Q})
,
g_{\infty}\in\text{GL}(2,\mathbb{R})
and
k\in\text{K}(2,M)
, with

\text{K}(2,M)=\text{O}(2,\mathbb{R})\times\prod_{p|M}k_{2,p}\times\prod_{p\nmid M}\text{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}_{p}).

It can be seen that each function
\varphi_{f}
generates the automorphic representation
\pi_{f}
of
\text{GL}(2,\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
with trivial central character. We refer the reader to, for example,
[
14
, Sections 13.3-13.6]
for relevant backgrounds.

For any given Hecke-Maaß form
F
of level
N
on
\text{GL}(3)
, we now let
\Pi_{F}
be the associated automorphic representation, which belongs to the space
L^{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N)\backslash{\text{SL}}(3,\mathbb{R}))
. Here, the definition of
\Gamma_{0}(N)
can be referred to (
2.3
) in §2.1. The representation
\Pi_{F}
is generated by the smooth functions
\varphi_{\Pi_{F}}\in L^{2}(\textrm{GL}(3,\mathbb{Q})\backslash\textrm{GL}(3,\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})/\text{K}(3,N))
, which are defined in the following way

\varphi_{\Pi_{F}}(\gamma g_{\infty}k)=F(g_{\infty})

for any
\gamma\in\text{GL}(3,\mathbb{Q})
,
g_{\infty}\in\text{GL}(3,\mathbb{R})
and
k\in\text{K}(3,N)
, with

\text{K}(3,N)=\text{O}(3,\mathbb{R})\times\prod_{p|N}k_{3,p}\times\prod_{p\nmid N}\text{GL}(3,\mathbb{Z}_{p}).

It is well-known that one
might factor

\Pi_{F}=\Pi_{F,\infty}\otimes_{p<\infty}\Pi_{F,p},

where
\Pi_{F,p}
is an irreducible, admissible and unitary representations of
\text{GL}(3,\mathbb{Q}_{p})
for every
p<\infty
.

We shall now ready to deduce an explicit form of the epsilon constant
\varepsilon(\Pi_{F,p}\otimes\pi_{f,p})
at each place
p\leq\infty
. Observe that, for any unramified places
v=p
with
p\nmid NM
, there already hold that
\varepsilon(\Pi_{F,p}\otimes\pi_{f,p})=1
. We will focus on the archimedean place
v=\infty
and the finite places
v=p,p|NM
in the following.

•

To begin with, at archimedean place
p=\infty
, in the Hecke-Maaß form setting
\Pi_{F,\infty}
is an unramified principal series representation; while,
\pi_{f,\infty}
is a ramified discrete series
representation of weight
k
, by the multiplicativity of epsilon factors we have

\varepsilon(\Pi_{F,\infty}\otimes\pi_{f,\infty})=\varepsilon(\pi_{f,\infty})^{3}=i^{k}.

•

Now, for
p|M
, the local component
\Pi_{F,p}
is an unramified principal series representation, and
\pi_{g,p}
is the
Steinberg representation, one finds

\varepsilon(\Pi_{F,p}\otimes\pi_{f,p})=\varepsilon(\pi_{f,p})^{3}=-\lambda_{f}(p)\sqrt{p}.

•

For
p|N
, one observes that the local component
\Pi_{F,p}
is the Steinberg representation; whilst,
\pi_{g,p}
is an unramified principal series representation with trivial central character, so that

\varepsilon(\Pi_{F,p}\otimes\pi_{f,p})=\varepsilon(\Pi_{F,p})^{2},

with
|\varepsilon(\Pi_{F,p})|=1
.

We finally obtain

(2.6)

\displaystyle\varepsilon(F\otimes f)=\prod_{p\leq\infty}\varepsilon(\Pi_{F,p}\otimes\pi_{f,p})=-i^{k}\lambda_{f}(M)\sqrt{M}\prod_{p|N}\varepsilon(\Pi_{F,p})^{2}.

2.4.
Voronoĭ summation formula

Let
w
be a compactly supported smooth function on
(0,\infty)
, and
\widetilde{w}
be its Mellin transform. For any
\rho=0,1
and any Hecke-Maaß form
F
of level
N
with the Langlands parameters
\alpha_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq 3
, being as in (
2.4
), we define

(2.7)

\displaystyle\gamma_{\rho}(s)=\varepsilon(F)i^{\rho}\pi^{3(s-1/2)}\prod_{j=1}^{3}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1+s+\alpha_{j}+\rho}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{-s-\alpha_{j}+\rho}{2}\right)}

and set

\gamma_{\pm}(s)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\gamma_{0}(s)\mp i\gamma_{1}(s)\right).

Now, we proceed to define two auxiliary quantities

(2.8)

\displaystyle\Omega_{\pm}(x;w)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(\sigma)}x^{-s}\gamma_{\pm}(s)\widetilde{w}(-s)\mathrm{d}s,

where
\sigma>-1+\max\{-\text{Re}(\alpha_{1}),-\text{Re}(\alpha_{2}),-\text{Re}(\alpha_{3})\}
. We then have the following Voronoĭ summation formula for Hecke-Maaß forms on
\text{GL}(3)
in the level aspect (see
[
50
]
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
w(x)\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(0,\infty)
be a smooth function compactly supported on
[1/2,5/2]
with bounded derivatives. Let
a,\overline{a},c\in\mathbb{Z}
with
c\neq 0
,
(a,c)=1
, satisfying that
a\overline{a}\equiv 1\bmod c
.

•

If
(cm,N)=1
, we then have

(2.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sum_{n>0}A_{F}(m,n)e\left(\frac{n\overline{a}}{c}\right)w\left(\frac{n}{X}\right)\\
&\hskip 34.14322pt=c\sqrt{N}\sum_{\pm}\sum_{n_{1}|cm}\sum_{n_{2}>0}\frac{\overline{A_{F}(n_{1},n_{2})}}{n_{1}n_{2}}S(am\overline{N},\pm n_{2};cm/n_{1})\,\Omega_{\pm}\left(\frac{n_{2}n^{2}_{1}X}{c^{3}Nm};w\right).\end{split}

•

If
N|c
, we, however, have

(2.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sum_{n>0}A_{F}(m,n)e\left(\frac{n\overline{a}}{c}\right)w\left(\frac{n}{X}\right)\\
&\hskip 34.14322pt=c\sum_{\pm}\sum_{n_{1}|cm}\sum_{n_{2}>0}\frac{\overline{A_{F}(n_{1},n_{2})}}{n_{1}n_{2}}S(am,\pm n_{2};cm/n_{1})\,\Omega_{\pm}\left(\frac{n_{2}n^{2}_{1}X}{c^{3}m};w\right).\end{split}

One has a good control of the asymptotic behavior of
\Omega_{\pm}
, upon expressing it in term of the oscillatory integral; see, for example,
[
34
, Lemma 6.1]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For any fixed integer
\mathcal{K}\geq 1
and
x\gg 1
, we have

\begin{split}&\Omega_{+}(x;w)=x\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}w(y)\sum_{j=1}^{\mathcal{K}}\frac{1}{(xy)^{{j}/{3}}}\left[c_{j}\,e{\left(3(xy)^{1/{3}}\right)}\right.\\
&\phantom{=\;\;}\left.\hskip 163.03416pt+d_{j}\,e{\left(-3(xy)^{1/{3}}\right)}\right]\mathrm{d}y+O{\left(x^{({-\mathcal{K}+2})/{3}}\right)},\end{split}

where
c_{j},d_{j}
are suitable constants depending on the four parameters
\alpha_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq 3
, above. Furthermore,
\Omega_{-}(x;\omega)
has the same expression except values of constants.

This, in turn, reduces the right-hand side of the Voronoĭ formula (
2.9
) essentially to

\displaystyle\frac{X^{{2}/{3}}}{cN^{1/6}m^{2/3}}\sum_{\pm,n_{1}|c}\sum_{n_{2}>0}\frac{\overline{A_{F}(n_{1},n_{2})}}{(n_{2}/n_{1})^{1/{3}}}\,S(am\overline{N},\pm n_{2};cm/n_{1})

(2.11)

\displaystyle\hskip 179.25244pt\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}w(y)\,e\left(\pm\frac{3\left(n_{2}n^{2}_{1}Xy/m\right)^{1/{3}}}{c}\right)\mathrm{d}y.

The main contribution occurs when
n_{2}\ll c^{3}m/n^{2}_{1}X^{1-\varepsilon}
, by repeated integration by parts, and in which case
\Omega_{\pm}\ll X^{\varepsilon}
.

2.6.
Petersson trace formula

For any integers
m,n\geq 1
, we set

\Delta_{k,M}(m,n)=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{B}_{k}(M)}\omega^{-1}_{f}\psi_{f}(m)\psi_{f}(n)

and

where the spectral weights

are given by

\omega_{f}:=\frac{(4\pi)^{k-1}}{\Gamma(k-1)}\langle f,f\rangle
. In particular, if

\omega_{f}=\frac{(k-1)M}{2\pi^{2}}L(1,\text{sym}^{2}f),
which satisfies that

k^{1-\varepsilon}M\ll\omega_{f}\ll k^{1+\varepsilon}M

for any
\varepsilon>0
; see
[
24
, Lemma 2.5]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

(Petersson trace formula) We have

\Delta_{k,M}(m,n)=\delta(m,n)+2\pi i^{-k}\sum_{c>0}\frac{S(m,n;cM)}{cM}J_{k-1}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{cM}\right),

where
\delta(m,n)=1
if
m=n
, and
\delta(m,n)=0
otherwise.

For the purpose of our application, we need an expression for
\Delta^{\ast}_{k,M}
instead of
\Delta_{k,M}
. Suppose that
m,n
are positive integers satisfying
(m,M)=1
, we have the following identity (see
[
24
, Eqn. (2.51)]
):

Lemma 2.5
.

We have

\Delta^{\ast}_{k,M}(m,n)=\sum_{RS=M}\frac{\mu(S)}{L\nu((m,S))}\sum_{l|S^{\infty}}l^{-1}\sum_{l^{2}_{1}|(n,\,Sl_{1})}\mu(l_{1})l_{1}\Delta_{k,R}\left(ml^{2},\frac{n}{l^{2}_{1}}\right),

where
\nu(M)=[\Gamma(1):\Gamma(M)]=M\prod_{p|M}(1+p^{-1})
.

2.7.
Bounds on bilinear forms with Kloosterman sums

The bilinear form with Kloosterman sums always plays a vital rôle in many topics such as the moments of central values of
L
-functions, subconvexity even the classical bilinear exponential sums in additive number theory; see, for example,
[
31
,
26
,
47
]
for descriptions in different regards. In the present paper, we will develop the following relevant results to facilitate our use after a while.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
X,Y\geq 1
. Let
W
be a smooth function supported on
[1/2,5/2]\times[1/2,5/2]
with partial derivatives satisfying
x^{i}y^{j}\frac{\partial^{i}}{\partial x^{i}}\frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial y^{j}}W(x/X,y/Y)\ll_{i,j}ZZ^{i}_{1}Z^{j}_{2}
for some
Z,Z_{1},Z_{2}>0
.
Let
q\geq 1
be an integer. For any
h\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

(2.12)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{A_{F}(m,1)}{\sqrt{m}}S(nh,m;q)\,W{\left(\frac{n}{X},\frac{m}{Y},\right)}\ll Z_{1}Z\sqrt{XYq}+Z_{1}ZXq^{3/4}(h,q)^{1/4}+Z_{1}Zq.

Proof.

This lemma essentially follows from the work of Kerr, Shparlinski, Wu and Xi
[
26
]
which appealed to the approach in

[
47
, Theorem 2.1]
. By partial summation, one finds that

\begin{split}&\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{A_{F}(m,1)}{\sqrt{m}}S(n,hm;q)W{\left(\frac{n}{X},\frac{m}{Y}\right)}\\
&\hskip 85.35826pt\ll\int^{5X/2}_{X/2}\left|\enskip\sideset{}{{}^{\ast}}{\sum}\limits_{x\bmod q}\sum_{m\geq 1}\alpha_{m,t}\,e{\left(\frac{m\overline{x}}{q}\right)}\sum_{n\leq t}e{\left(\frac{nhx}{q}\right)}\right|\mathrm{d}t,\end{split}

with
\alpha_{m,t}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}W(t/X,m/Y)A_{F}(m,1)/\sqrt{m}
. This is compatible with the setting as presented in the beginning of the
[
47
, Section 5]
. We still might bound the inner triple sum by
\sum_{\pm}\sum_{1\leq i\leq I}|S^{\pm}_{i}|
, where, for
1\leq i\leq I
,
S^{\pm}_{i}=\sum_{m\leq X}\alpha_{m,t}\gamma_{x}\,e{({\pm m\overline{x}}/{q})}
; see
[
26
, Section 6]
for details.
∎

Observe that the bilinear forms we are concerned about is a smooth-weighted sum. A direct use of the Possion might show some bonus. Indeed, we alternatively have

Lemma 2.7
.

Keep the notation as in Lemma
2.6
. Then, for any
(h,q)=1
, we have

(2.13)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{A_{F}(m,1)}{\sqrt{m}}S(nh,m;q)W{\left(\frac{n}{X},\frac{m}{Y}\right)}\ll Z\sqrt{XYq}+\sqrt{Z_{2}}ZXq^{3/4}\mathbf{1}_{Z_{2}q>Y}+ZX\sqrt{Y}.

Proof.

By switching the orders of the summations and applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality followed by the Possion, one deduces that the double sum is controlled by

\begin{split}&\ll\left(\frac{Y}{q}\sum_{n_{1},n_{2}\geq 1}\,\sum_{|\jmath|\ll Z_{2}q/Y^{1-\varepsilon}}\Phi(\jmath;n_{1},n_{2})\sum_{\alpha\bmod q}S(n_{1}h,\alpha;q)\overline{S(n_{2}h,\alpha;q)}\,e{\left(\frac{\jmath\alpha}{q}\right)}\right)^{1/2},\end{split}

with
\Phi(\jmath;v_{1},v_{2})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}W(v_{1},t)W(v_{2},t)e(-\jmath t/q)\mathrm{d}t
for any
v_{1},v_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
\jmath\in\mathbb{R}
. It is verifiable that the zero-frequence shows a quantity
Z\sqrt{XYq}+XZ\sqrt{Y}
; while, if
Z_{2}q>Y
, the non-zero frequences, however, will contribute the quantity
\sqrt{Z_{2}}Zq^{3/4}\sqrt{Y}
by invoking the estimate for triple sums of Kloosterman sums; see, for example,
[
26
, Lemma 4.1]
.
∎

It is remarkable that, as it can be seen in (
2.13
), the upper-bound becomes smaller, as
m,n
vary in the ranges with relatively small heights.

For any

We thus have (see
[

Lemma 2.8
.

For any
K>0

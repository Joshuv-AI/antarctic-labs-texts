The Noether formalism for constructing conserved quantities in teleparallel equivalents of general relativity
E. D. Emtsova, A. N. Petrov, A.V.Toporensky

Abstract
This paper brings a methodological character where we present a comprehensive formalism for constructing conserved quantities in the Teleparallel Equivalent of General Relativity (TEGR) and Symmetric Teleparallel Equivalent of General Relativity (STEGR). It was developed in series of our earlier works and, here, we unite it into a complete form. By employing the Noether method within a tensor formalism, conserved currents, superpotentials, and charges are constructed. These are shown to be covariant under coordinate transformations and local Lorentz rotations in TEGR, while in STEGR, they are covariant under coordinate transformations. The teleparallel (flat) connections in both theories are defined using the “turning off gravity” principle. Uniting such defined flat connections with tetrad in TEGR and metric in STEGR a new fruitful in applications notion “gauge” is introduced. The choice of various initial tetrads in TEGR or initial coordinates in STEGR leads to different gauges, what gives different conserved quantities. Finally, we discuss an appropriate choice of gauges from a possible set of them.

Introduction

Teleparallel theories of gravity have been actively developed in recent years. A main feature of these theories is the use of connection with zero Riemann curvature.
These theories include the Teleparallel Equivalent of General Relativity (TEGR), the Symmetric Teleparallel Equivalent of General Relativity (STEGR) and modifications of these theories

In TEGR and its modifications, a flat metric compatible connection is used. In STEGR and its modifications, a flat connection with zero torsion is used. TEGR and STEGR are fully equivalent to General Relativity (GR) at the level of field equations, thus, the solutions of the field equations in TEGR and STEGR are exactly the same as those in GR.
The teleparallel connections in TEGR and STEGR are not dynamical quantities and cannot be determined by field equations. This fact does not influence the dynamics of gravitational interacting objects but gives ambiguities to values of main quantities which define gravitational energy-momentum.

Despite the fact that now the main attention is paid to how accurate the modified teleparallel theories can describe the observed phenomena, not all the issues have been resolved in TEGR and STEGR themselves. One of such issues is definition of gravitational energy-momentum and other conserved quantities. There are various approaches for constructing conserved quantities in both TEGR and STEGR. Main elements of TEGR and STEGR

2.1
TEGR

The most popular gravitational Lagrangian in TEGR is presented in the form given, for example, in the book
[
3
]

\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{{{\cal L}}}}=\frac{h}{{2}\kappa}{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{T}}}{}\,\equiv\frac{h}{2\kappa}\left(\frac{1}{4}{{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{T}}}{}}{}^{\rho}{}_{\mu\nu}{{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{T}}}{}}_{\rho}{}^{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}{{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{T}}}{}}{}^{\rho}{}_{\mu\nu}{{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{T}}}{}}{}^{\nu\mu}{}_{\rho}-{{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{T}}}{}}{}^{\rho}{}_{\mu\rho}{{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{T}}}{}}{}^{\nu\mu}{}_{\nu}\right).

(2.1)

We set
G=c=1
units, for which the Einstein constant
\kappa=8\pi
. To present a one of the main quantities in TEGR, namely, the torsion tensor
{{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{T}}}{}}{}^{a}{}_{\mu\nu}
, one has to introduce the next notations.

First, they are tetrads with components
h^{a}{}_{\nu}
, where Latin indexes numerate tetrad vectors, Greek indexes are related to spacetime coordinates. They are connected to metric
g_{\mu\nu}
by the standard relation

g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{ab}h^{a}{}_{\mu}h^{b}{}_{\nu}\,

(2.2)

where
\eta_{ab}
is the Minkowskian metric in the tetrad space,
h=\det h^{a}{}_{\nu}
, besides, we use the signature
(-,+,+,+)
both for
g_{\mu\nu}
and for
\eta_{ab}
. The transformation of the tetrad indexes into spacetime ones and vice versa is performed by contraction with tetrad vectors, for example,
{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{T}}}{}^{\rho}{}_{\mu\nu}={\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{T}}}{}^{a}{}_{\mu\nu}h_{a}{}^{\rho}
; etc.

Second, another important quantity in TEGR is the Weitzenbök (teleparallel) connection
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{\Gamma}}{}{}^{\alpha}{}_{\kappa\lambda}

[
3
]
. Quantities constructed with the use of
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{\Gamma}}{}{}^{\alpha}{}_{\kappa\lambda}
are denoted by “
\bullet
” above a symbol, for example, a related covariant derivative
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{\nabla}}_{\mu}
. The connection
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{\Gamma}}{}{}^{\alpha}{}_{\kappa\lambda}
, as it was announced in Introduction, is flat, that means that the corresponding curvature is equal to zero:

\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{R}}{}{}^{\alpha}{}_{\beta\mu\nu}\equiv\partial_{\mu}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{\Gamma}}{}^{\alpha}{}_{\beta\nu}-\partial_{\nu}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{\Gamma}}{}^{\alpha}{}_{\beta\mu}+\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{\Gamma}}{}^{\alpha}{}_{\kappa\mu}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{\Gamma}}{}^{\kappa}{}_{\beta\nu}-\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{\Gamma}}{}^{\alpha}{}_{\kappa\nu}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{\Gamma}}{}^{\kappa}{}_{\beta\mu}=0\,.

(2.3)

We note also that
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{\Gamma}}{}{}^{\alpha}{}_{\kappa\lambda}
is compatible with the physical metric. This means that corresponding non-metricity
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{Q}}{}_{\mu\alpha\beta}
is zero:

\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{Q}}{}_{\mu\alpha\beta}\equiv\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{\nabla}}{}_{\mu}g_{\alpha\beta}=0.

(2.4)

Third, especial attention has to be paid to the inertial spin connection (ISC)
{{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{A}}}{}}{}^{a}{}_{c\nu}
defined as

{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{A}}}{}{}^{a}{}_{b\mu}=-h_{b}{}^{\nu}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{\nabla}}_{\mu}h^{a}{}_{\nu}.

(2.5)

Then the definition of
{{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{T}}}{}}{}^{a}{}_{\mu\nu}
can be presented evidently:

{{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{T}}}{}}{}^{a}{}_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}h^{a}{}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}h^{a}{}_{\mu}+{{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{A}}}{}}{}^{a}{}_{c\mu}h^{c}{}_{\nu}-{{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{A}}}{}}{}^{a}{}_{c\nu}h^{c}{}_{\mu}.

(2.6)

Namely, the presence of
{{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{A}}}{}}{}^{a}{}_{c\nu}

makes the torsion (
2.6
) covariant with respect to local Lorentz rotations of tetrad.

It is instructive to introduce some quantities constructed with the use of the Levi-Civita connection (Christoffel symbols)
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{\Gamma}}{}{}^{\alpha}{}_{\kappa\lambda}
.
Following to
[
3
]
, we denote such quantities by “
\circ
” above a symbol. Thus,

{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{A}}}{}^{a}{}_{b\rho}
presents Levi-Civita spin connection (L-CSC) defined by

{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{A}}}{}{}^{a}{}_{b\mu}=-h_{b}{}^{\nu}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{\nabla}}_{\mu}h^{a}{}_{\nu},

(2.7)

where the covariant derivative
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{\nabla}}_{\mu}
is is related to
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{\Gamma}}{}{}^{\alpha}{}_{\kappa\lambda}
.

Let us list main tensors in TEGR. One of important quantities in TEGR is a contortion tensor
{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{K}}}{}^{a}{}_{b\rho}
, which is the difference between (
2.5
) and (
2.7
), thus,

{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{K}}}{}^{a}{}_{b\rho}={\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{A}}}{}^{a}{}_{b\rho}-{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{A}}}{}^{a}{}_{b\rho}.

(2.8)

It is expressed through the torsion tensor by

{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{K}}}{}^{\rho}{}_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}({\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{T}}}{}_{\mu}{}^{\rho}{}_{\nu}+{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{T}}}{}_{\nu}{}^{\rho}{}_{\mu}-{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{T}}}{}^{\rho}{}_{\mu\nu}).

(2.9)

The torsion scalar in (
2.1
) is rewritten as

{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{T}}}{}=\frac{1}{2}\,{{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{S}}}{}}_{a}{}^{\rho\sigma}{{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{T}}}{}}{}^{a}{}_{\rho\sigma}

(2.10)

with making the use of the tensor

{{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{S}}}{}}_{a}{}^{\rho\sigma}={\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{K}}}{}{}^{\rho\sigma}{}_{a}+h_{a}{}^{\sigma}{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{K}}}{}{}^{\theta\rho}{}_{\theta}-h_{a}{}^{\rho}{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{K}}}{}{}^{\theta\sigma}{}_{\theta}\,,

(2.11)

which is called the teleparallel superpotential and is antisymmetric in the upper indexes.

All of the aforementioned tensors
{{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{T}}}{}}{}^{a}{}_{\mu\nu}
,
{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{K}}}{}^{\rho\sigma}{}_{a}
, and
{{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{S}}}{}}_{a}{}^{\rho\sigma}
are covariant with respect to both coordinate transformations and local Lorentz rotations.
Local Lorentz covariance of these quantities is achieved by simultaneous transformation of both the tetrad and the ISC:

h^{\prime a}{}_{\mu}=\Lambda^{a}{}_{b}(x)h^{b}{}_{\mu}\,,

(2.12)

{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{A}}}{}{}^{\prime}{}^{a}{}_{b\mu}=\Lambda{}^{a}{}_{c}(x){\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{A}}}{}{}{}^{c}{}_{d\mu}\Lambda{}_{b}{}^{d}(x)+\Lambda{}^{a}{}_{c}(x)\partial_{\mu}\Lambda{}_{b}{}^{c}(x),

(2.13)

where
\Lambda{}^{a}{}_{c}(x)
is the matrix of a local Lorentz rotation, and
\Lambda{}_{a}{}^{c}(x)
is an inverse matrix of the latter. The transformation (
2.13
) tells us that ISC is not a tensor and can be equalized to zero by an appropriate local Lorentz transformation. Then, by an appropriate Lorentz rotation it can be represented in the form:

{{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{A}}}{}}{}^{a}{}_{c\nu}=\tilde{\Lambda}^{a}{}_{b}\partial_{\nu}(\tilde{\Lambda}^{-1}){}^{b}{}_{c}.

(2.14)

Now, let us vary the action with the Lagrangian (
2.1
) with respect to tetrad components:

E_{a}{}^{\rho}=-\frac{\delta\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{{{\cal L}}}}}{\delta h^{a}{}_{\rho}}=-\left[\frac{\partial\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{{{\cal L}}}}}{\partial h^{a}{}_{\rho}}-\partial_{\sigma}\left(\frac{\partial\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{{{\cal L}}}}}{\partial h^{a}{}_{\rho,\sigma}}\right)\right].

(2.15)

The above information is enough to rewrite the Lagrangian (
2.1
) in the form
[
3
]
:

{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{{{\cal L}}}}}={\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{{{\cal L}}}}}-\frac{1}{\kappa}\partial_{\mu}\left(h{{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{T}}}{}}{}^{\nu\mu}{}_{\nu}\right)\,,

(2.16)

where the first term is the Hilbert Lagrangian

{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{{{\cal L}}}}}=-\frac{h}{2\kappa}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{R}}\,

(2.17)

with the Riemannian curvature scalar
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{R}}
expressed through the tetrad components by (
2.2
), see
[
23
]
.
Then, because the TEGR Lagrangian (
2.1
) contains ISC in the divergence only
E_{a}{}^{\rho}
in (
2.15
) does not contain ISC totally

E_{a}{}^{\rho}=-\frac{\delta\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{{{\cal L}}}}}{\delta h^{a}{}_{\rho}}=-\frac{\delta\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{{{\cal L}}}}}{\delta h^{a}{}_{\rho}}\,.

(2.18)

Let us add to the Lagrangian (
2.1
) a matter Lagrangian
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle m}}{{{{\cal L}}}}
, where matter fields
\phi
are coupled minimally to metric (
2.2
):
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{{{\cal L}}}}+\stackrel{{\scriptstyle m}}{{{{\cal L}}}}
. Varying the action with such a Lagrangian with respect to tetrad components one obtains gravitational field equations

E_{a}{}^{\rho}=\theta_{a}{}^{\rho}\,.

(2.19)

where
\theta_{a}{}^{\rho}=2\delta\!\!\stackrel{{\scriptstyle m}}{{{{\cal L}}}}/\delta h^{a}{}_{\rho}

2.2
STEGR

Usually, Lagrangian in STEGR is considered in the form given, for example, in
[
1
]
:

\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\cal L}}{}=\frac{\sqrt{-g}}{2\kappa}g^{\mu\nu}(L^{\alpha}{}_{\beta\mu}L^{\beta}{}_{\nu\alpha}-L^{\alpha}{}_{\beta\alpha}L^{\beta}{}_{\mu\nu}).

(2.20)

Here, the disformation tensor
L^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\nu}
is introduced by

L^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}Q^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}Q_{\mu}{}^{\alpha}{}_{\nu}-\frac{1}{2}Q_{\nu}{}^{\alpha}{}_{\mu}\,,

(2.21)

where the non-metricity tensor
Q_{\alpha\mu\nu}
is defined as usual,

Q_{\alpha\mu\nu}\equiv\nabla_{\alpha}g_{\mu\nu}.

(2.22)

The covariant derivative
\nabla_{\alpha}
is defined with making the the use of the teleparallel connection
\Gamma^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\nu}
. It is symmetric in lower indexes and we introduce an abbreviation STC (symmetric teleparallel connection). The STC is torsionless because
T{}^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\nu}\equiv\Gamma{}^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\nu}-\Gamma{}^{\alpha}{}_{\nu\mu}=0
by a definition. Of course, STC is flat that means that a related curvature tensor is zero:

R^{\alpha}{}_{\beta\mu\nu}(\Gamma)=\partial_{\mu}\Gamma^{\alpha}{}_{\nu\beta}-\partial_{\nu}\Gamma^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\beta}+\Gamma^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\lambda}\Gamma^{\lambda}{}_{\nu\beta}-\Gamma^{\alpha}{}_{\nu\lambda}\Gamma^{\lambda}{}_{\mu\beta}=0.

(2.23)

One can easily check that (
2.21
) can be rewritten in the form:

L^{\beta}{}_{\mu\nu}\equiv\Gamma^{\beta}{}_{\mu\nu}-\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{\Gamma}}{}^{\beta}{}_{\mu\nu}\,,

(2.24)

where
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{\Gamma}}{}^{\beta}{}_{\mu\nu}
is the Levi-Civita connection. Next, with the use of (
2.21
) - (
2.24
) the Lagrangian (
2.20
) acquires the form:

\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\cal L}}{}=\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{{{\cal L}}}}+\frac{\sqrt{-g}g^{\mu\nu}}{2\kappa}R_{\mu\nu}+\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\cal L}}{}^{\prime}.

(2.25)

The same as (
2.17
), the first term is the Hilbert Lagrangian, however, depending on the metric components (not tetrad components):

{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{{{\cal L}}}}}=-\frac{\sqrt{-g}}{2\kappa}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{R}}\,.

(2.26)

The second term in (
2.25
) is equal to zero due to (
2.23
). However, if we preserve it, we need to vary it. On the one hand, variation of (
2.25
) together with the second term with respect to metric means the variation of (
2.20
) exactly. On the other hand, variation of (
2.20
) with respect to
\Gamma^{\beta}{}_{\mu\nu}
gives
\Gamma^{\beta}{}_{\mu\nu}=\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{\Gamma}}{}^{\beta}{}_{\mu\nu}
. This means that the flat STC
\Gamma^{\beta}{}_{\mu\nu}
can be equal to the Levi-Civita connection that is not flat in general. Since it is not permissible the second term in (
2.25
) has to be cancelled.
The third term
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\cal L}}^{\prime}
is a total divergence:

\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\cal L}}^{\prime}{}=\frac{\sqrt{-g}}{2\kappa}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{\nabla}}_{\alpha}(\hat{Q}^{\alpha}-{Q}^{\alpha})=\partial_{\alpha}\left[\frac{1}{2\kappa}\sqrt{-g}(\hat{Q}^{\alpha}-{Q}^{\alpha})\right]=\partial_{\alpha}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\cal D}}{}^{\alpha},

(2.27)

where
Q_{\alpha}=g^{\mu\nu}Q_{\alpha\mu\nu}
,
\hat{Q}_{\alpha}=g^{\mu\nu}Q_{\mu\alpha\nu}
.
As a result, instead of (
2.20
) or (
2.25
) in the STEGR, we choose the Lagrangian

\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\cal L}}{}=-\frac{\sqrt{-g}}{2\kappa}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{R}}+\partial_{\alpha}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\cal D}}{}^{\alpha}\,,

(2.28)

where

\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\cal D}}^{\alpha}\equiv-\frac{\sqrt{-g}}{2\kappa}(Q^{\alpha}-\hat{Q}^{\alpha})\,.

(2.29)

Because the STEGR Lagrangian contains STC in the divergence only varying the action with the Lagrangian (
2.28
) with respect to the metric components one obtains the GR field equations exactly. Keeping in mind a possible presence of matter variables, one has

\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{G}}_{\mu\nu}=\kappa\theta_{\mu\nu},

(2.30)

where
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{G}}_{\mu\nu}
is the usual Einstein tensor. Besides, it is another form of (
2.19
), and again, by (
2.30
) we stress the equivalence between STEGR and GR.

At last, it is important to remark the following. Varying the action with the Lagrangian (
2.28
) with respect to
\Gamma^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\nu}
one gets
0=0
. That is STC cannot be determined in the framework of STEGR itself. This means that STC, the same as ISC in TEGR, is an external structure, and can be defined by additional requirements only, like a construction of conserved quantities with acceptable values.

3
Noether conserved quantities in an arbitrary field theory

To derive conservation laws which follow from the diffeomorphism invariance of an arbitrary covariant field theory of fields
\psi^{A}
we turn, first of all, to the classical Mitskevich’s book
[
24
]
, one can see also
[
25
,
26
]
. Let us consider the action

S=\int dx^{4}{{\cal L}}(\psi^{A};\psi^{A}{}_{,\alpha};\psi^{A}{}_{,\alpha\beta})\,,

(3.1)

where symbol
\psi^{A}
means an arbitrary tensor density or set of such densities with
A
a collective index. Setting the invariance of the action (
3.1
) under diffeomorphisms with the displacement vectors
\xi^{\alpha}
, we assume variations of
\psi^{A}
in the form of the Lie derivatives:

\delta\psi^{A}={\pounds}_{\xi}\psi^{A}=-\xi^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}\psi^{A}+{\left.\psi^{A}\right|}^{\alpha}_{\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\xi^{\beta}\,.

(3.2)

We borrow the notation
{\left.\psi^{A}\right|}^{\alpha}_{\beta}
from
[
24
]
, its concrete presentation is defined by the transformation properties of
\psi^{A}
. For example, for a vector
\psi^{A}=\psi^{\sigma}
, we have
{\left.\psi^{\sigma}\right|}^{\alpha}_{\beta}=\delta^{\sigma}_{\beta}\psi^{\alpha}
. From diffeomorphism invariance of (
3.1
) it follows that Lagrangian

{{\cal L}}
is a scalar density with the mathematical weight +1. It defines the main Noether identity:

\pounds_{\xi}{{\cal L}}=-\partial_{\alpha}\left(\xi^{\alpha}{{\cal L}}\right)\,.

(3.3)

Keeping in mind (
3.2
), the main Noether identity is rewritten in the form:

\frac{\delta{{\cal L}}}{\delta\psi^{B}}{\pounds}_{\xi}\psi^{B}+\partial_{\alpha}\left[{{\delta{{\cal L}}}\over{\delta\psi^{B}{}_{,\alpha}}}{\pounds}_{\xi}\psi^{B}+{{\partial{{\cal L}}}\over{\partial\psi^{B}{}_{,\beta\alpha}}}({\pounds}_{\xi}\psi^{B})_{,\beta}+\xi^{\alpha}{{{\cal L}}}\right]\equiv 0\,,

(3.4)

where the standard notations

\frac{\delta{{\cal L}}}{\delta\psi^{B}}=\frac{\partial{{\cal L}}}{\partial\psi^{B}}-\partial_{\mu}\left(\frac{\partial{{\cal L}}}{\partial\psi^{B}{}_{,\mu}}\right)+\partial_{\mu\nu}\left(\frac{\partial{{\cal L}}}{\partial\psi^{B}{}_{,\mu\nu}}\right)\,,

\frac{\delta{{\cal L}}}{\delta\psi^{B}{}_{,\alpha}}=\frac{\partial{{\cal L}}}{\partial\psi^{B}{}_{,\alpha}}-\partial_{\mu}\left(\frac{\partial{{\cal L}}}{\partial\psi^{B}{}_{,\alpha\mu}}\right)

are used. We use partial derivatives of variables since, in metric theories, a set of field variables
\psi^{A}
includes the metric tensor
g_{\mu\nu}
anyway, whose covariant derivatives identically vanish. Indeed, it is not permissible differentiating with respect to
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{\nabla}}g_{\mu\nu}\equiv 0
, thus, formalism fails.

Substituting (
3.2
) into (
3.4
), regrouping terms, applying the Leibniz rule, and transforming divergences, we obtain:

-\left[\frac{\delta{{\cal L}}}{\delta\psi^{B}}\psi^{B}{}_{,\alpha}+\partial_{\beta}\left(\frac{\delta{{\cal L}}}{\delta\psi^{B}}\left.\psi^{B}\right|^{\beta}_{\alpha}\right)\right]\xi^{\alpha}+\partial_{\alpha}\left[{\cal U}_{\sigma}{}^{\alpha}\xi^{\sigma}+{\cal M}_{\sigma}{}^{\alpha\tau}\partial_{\tau}\xi^{\sigma}+{\cal N}_{\sigma}{}^{\alpha\tau\beta}\partial_{\beta\tau}\xi^{\sigma}\right]\equiv 0\,.

(3.5)

Here, the coefficients

\displaystyle{\cal U}_{\sigma}{}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle{{\cal L}}\delta^{\alpha}_{\sigma}+{{\delta{{\cal L}}}\over{\delta\psi^{B}}}\left.\psi^{B}\right|^{\alpha}_{\sigma}-{{\delta{{\cal L}}}\over{\delta\psi^{B}{}_{,\alpha}}}\partial_{\sigma}\psi^{B}-{{\partial{{\cal L}}}\over{\partial\psi^{B}{}_{,\beta\alpha}}}\partial_{\beta\sigma}\psi^{B}\,,

(3.6)

\displaystyle{\cal M}_{\sigma}{}^{\alpha\tau}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle{{\delta{{\cal L}}}\over{\delta\psi^{B}{}_{,\alpha}}}\left.\psi^{B}\right|^{\tau}_{\sigma}-{{\partial{{\cal L}}}\over{\partial\psi^{B}{}_{,\tau\alpha}}}\partial_{\sigma}\psi^{B}+{{\partial{{\cal L}}}\over{\partial\psi^{B}{}_{,\beta\alpha}}}\partial_{\beta}(\left.\psi^{B}\right|^{\tau}_{\sigma})\,,

(3.7)

\displaystyle{\cal N}_{\sigma}{}^{\alpha\tau\beta}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}}\left[{{\partial{{\cal L}}}\over{\partial\psi^{B}{}_{,\beta\alpha}}}\left.\psi^{B}\right|^{\tau}_{\sigma}+{{\partial{{\cal L}}}\over{\partial\psi^{B}{}_{,\tau\alpha}}}\left.\psi^{B}\right|^{\beta}_{\sigma}\right]

(3.8)

are fully determined by the Lagrangian (
3.1
) and its derivatives. To obtain (
3.8
), the symmetry property
{\cal N}_{\sigma}{}^{\alpha\tau\beta}={\cal N}_{\sigma}{}^{\alpha\beta\tau}
was used, which follows directly from (
3.5
) due to the commutativity of second partial derivatives.

Applying a partial derivative to each term in the square brackets of identity (
3.5
) and remembering that the vector field
\xi^{\sigma}
and all its partial derivatives are independent and arbitrary at every point of the spacetime manifold, one concludes that all coefficients of
\xi^{\sigma}
and its derivatives must independently vanish identically. This leads to the system of identities:

\displaystyle\partial_{\alpha}{\cal U}_{\sigma}{}^{\alpha}\equiv\frac{\delta{{\cal L}}}{\delta\psi^{B}}\psi^{B}{}_{,\alpha}+\partial_{\beta}\left(\frac{\delta{{\cal L}}}{\delta\psi^{B}}\left.\psi^{B}\right|^{\beta}_{\alpha}\right),

(3.9)

\displaystyle{\cal U}_{\sigma}{}^{\alpha}+\partial_{\lambda}{\cal M}_{\sigma}{}^{\lambda\alpha}\equiv 0,

(3.10)

\displaystyle{\cal M}_{\sigma}{}^{(\alpha\beta)}+\partial_{\lambda}{\cal N}_{\sigma}{}^{\lambda(\alpha\beta)}\equiv 0,

(3.11)

\displaystyle{\cal N}^{(\alpha\beta\gamma)}_{\sigma}\equiv 0.

(3.12)

The system like (
3.9
)–(
3.12
) firstly was derived by Klein
[
27
]
and is called usually as
Klein identities
. Differentiating (
3.10
) and using (
3.11
) and (
3.12
) yields
\partial_{\alpha}{\cal U}_{\sigma}{}^{\alpha}\equiv 0
. This implies that the right-hand side of (
3.9
) must identically vanish as well,

\frac{\delta{{\cal L}}}{\delta\psi^{B}}\psi^{B}{}_{,\alpha}+\partial_{\beta}\left(\frac{\delta{{\cal L}}}{\delta\psi^{B}}\left.\psi^{B}\right|^{\beta}_{\alpha}\right)\equiv 0\;.

(3.13)

This is essentially the statement of Noether’s second theorem
[
26
]
and a generalization of the Bianchi identity. Considering the historical development of the theory, we refer to the system (
3.9
)–(
3.13
) as the
Klein-Noether identities
.

Identity (
3.13
) enables us to use independently the identity:

\partial_{\alpha}\left[{\cal U}_{\sigma}{}^{\alpha}\xi^{\sigma}+{\cal M}_{\sigma}{}^{\alpha\tau}\partial_{\tau}\xi^{\sigma}+{\cal N}_{\sigma}{}^{\alpha\tau\beta}\partial_{\beta\tau}\xi^{\sigma}\right]\equiv 0\,

(3.14)

instead of (
3.5
). Here, the vector density under the divergence is usually classified as a current:

{\cal I}^{\alpha}(\xi)\equiv-\left[{\cal U}_{\sigma}{}^{\alpha}\xi^{\sigma}+{\cal M}_{\sigma}{}^{\alpha\tau}\partial_{\tau}\xi^{\sigma}+{\cal N}_{\sigma}{}^{\alpha\tau\beta}\partial_{\beta\tau}\xi^{\sigma}\right]\,.

(3.15)

The negative sign is chosen to match the conventional negative sign in front of the gravitational (metric) action, see, for example, (
2.17
) and (
2.26
). As a result, identity (
3.14
) is rewritten in the compact form:

\partial_{\alpha}{\cal I}^{\alpha}(\xi)\equiv 0\,.

(3.16)

Since (
3.16
) is an identity, the current must be expressed through a tensorial quantity (superpotential),
{\cal I}^{\alpha}(\xi)\equiv\partial_{\beta}{\cal I}^{\alpha\beta}(\xi)
, whose double divergence must identically vanish:
\partial_{\alpha\beta}{\cal I}^{\alpha\beta}(\xi)\equiv 0
. Let us show this. Keeping in mind the symmetry in the last two indexes of (
3.8
) and identity (
3.12
), one obtains

{\cal N}_{\sigma}{}^{\alpha\tau\beta}+{\cal N}_{\sigma}{}^{\tau\beta\alpha}+{\cal N}_{\sigma}{}^{\beta\alpha\tau}\equiv 0\,.

(3.17)

Substituting (
3.10
) into (
3.15
) and using (
3.11
) and (
3.17
), one derives

{\cal I}^{\alpha}(\xi)\equiv\partial_{\beta}\left({\cal M}_{\sigma}{}^{\beta\alpha}\xi^{\sigma}+2{\cal N}_{\sigma}{}^{\beta\alpha\lambda}\partial_{\lambda}\xi^{\sigma}\right)\,.

(3.18)

As follows from (
3.16
), the divergence of the right-hand side of (
3.18
) is expected to vanish. To show this add a term identically equal to zero:

\frac{4}{3}\partial_{\beta\lambda}\left(\hat{N}_{\sigma}{}^{[\lambda\beta]\alpha}\xi^{\sigma}\right)\equiv 0

to the right-hand side of (
3.18
). Then, using (
3.11
) and (
3.17
), one finds

{\cal I}^{\alpha}(\xi)\equiv\partial_{\beta}\left(-{\cal M}_{\sigma}{}^{[\alpha\beta]}\xi^{\sigma}+\frac{2}{3}\partial_{\lambda}{\cal N}_{\sigma}{}^{[\alpha\beta]\lambda}\xi^{\sigma}-\frac{4}{3}{\cal N}_{\sigma}{}^{[\alpha\beta]\lambda}\partial_{\lambda}\xi^{\sigma}\right)\,.

(3.19)

The expression in square brackets in (
3.19
) is explicitly antisymmetric in
\alpha
and
\beta
, and therefore its double divergence indeed vanishes.

As a result, the current expression (
3.19
) can be written in the initially assumed form:

{\cal I}^{\alpha}(\xi)\equiv\partial_{\beta}{\cal I}^{\alpha\beta}(\xi)\,,

(3.20)

where

{\cal I}^{\alpha\beta}(\xi)\equiv-\left({\cal M}_{\sigma}{}^{[\alpha\beta]}\xi^{\sigma}-\frac{2}{3}\partial_{\lambda}{\cal N}_{\sigma}{}^{[\alpha\beta]\lambda}\xi^{\sigma}+\frac{4}{3}{\cal N}_{\sigma}{}^{[\alpha\beta]\lambda}\partial_{\lambda}\xi^{\sigma}\right)\,

(3.21)

is called a (Noether)
superpotential
. Of course, the identity (
3.20
) can be considered as equivalent to the conservation law (
3.16
) for the current.

One can show (for a detail see
[
26
]
) that both the above constructed current and superpotential are tensorial quantities. Thus,
{\cal I}^{\alpha}(\xi)
is a vector density of the weight
+1
, whereas
{\cal I}^{\alpha\beta}(\xi)
is an antisymmetric tensor density of the weight
+1
. For such quantities
\partial_{\mu}\equiv\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{\nabla}}_{\mu}
. By this, conservation laws (
3.16
) and (
3.20
) can be rewritten in evidently covariant form:

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{\nabla}}_{\alpha}{\cal I}^{\alpha}(\xi)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle 0\,,

(3.22)

\displaystyle{\cal I}^{\alpha}(\xi)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{\nabla}}_{\beta}{\cal I}^{\alpha\beta}(\xi)\,.

(3.23)

Here, a covariant derivative
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{\nabla}}_{\alpha}
can be constructed with respect to an arbitrary external metric.

At last, let us define integral conserved quantities. Integrating through 4-volume the identity (
3.16
) one obtains a conserved (in time with related boundary conditions) quantity
{\cal P}(\xi)
placed on 3-dimensional section
\Sigma=:x^{0}=t={\rm const}
:

{\cal P}(\xi)=\int_{\Sigma}d^{3}x{\cal I}^{0}(\xi)\,,

(3.24)

from here and below “0”-component is related to a time coordinate, whereas Latin indexes from the middle of alphabet, for example, “i”, are related to space coordinates.
The quantity (
3.24
) with making the use of (
3.20
) is effectively reduced to a surface integral that is called the Noether charge:

{\cal P}(\xi)=\oint_{\partial\Sigma}ds_{i}{\cal I}^{0i}(\xi)\,,

(3.25)

where
\partial\Sigma
is a boundary of
\Sigma
, and
ds_{i}
is element of integration on
\partial\Sigma
. By the construction, the quantity
{\cal P}(\xi)
both in (
3.24
) and in (
3.25
) is a scalar, it is invariant with respect to coordinate transformations.

It is important to outline a role of divergence in Lagrangian in constructing conserved quantities. According to Chapter 7 in book
[
26
]
, for every total divergence
\partial_{\alpha}{\cal D}^{\alpha}=\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{\nabla}}_{\alpha}{\cal D}^{\alpha}
of a vector density
{\cal D}^{\alpha}
(not considering its inner structure) in Lagrangian it is possible to construct conservation law in the form (
3.20
) or (
3.23
):

{\cal I}{}_{div}^{\alpha}(\xi)=\partial_{\beta}{\cal I}{}_{div}^{\alpha\beta}(\xi)=\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{\nabla}}_{\beta}{\cal I}{}_{div}^{\alpha\beta}(\xi).

(3.26)

The current
{\cal I}{}_{div}^{\alpha}
and the superpotential
{\cal I}{}_{div}^{\alpha\beta}
for the divergence of
{\cal D}^{\alpha}
are defined as:

\displaystyle{\cal I}{}_{div}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{\nabla}}_{\beta}(-{\cal M}_{(div)\sigma}{}^{[\alpha\beta]}\xi^{\sigma})=-{\cal U}_{(div)\sigma}{}^{\alpha}\xi^{\sigma}-{\cal M}_{(div)\sigma}{}^{[\alpha\beta]}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{\nabla}}_{\beta}\xi^{\sigma},

(3.27)

\displaystyle{\cal I}{}_{div}^{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{\cal M}_{(div)\sigma}{}^{[\alpha\beta]}\xi^{\sigma},

(3.28)

with

\displaystyle{\cal M}_{(div)\sigma}{}^{[\alpha\beta]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\delta_{\sigma}^{[\alpha}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\cal D}}{}^{\beta]},

(3.29)

\displaystyle{\cal U}_{(div)\sigma}{}^{\alpha}{}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{\nabla}}_{\beta}(\delta_{\sigma}^{[\alpha}{\cal D}^{\beta]})\equiv 2\partial_{\beta}(\delta_{\sigma}^{[\alpha}{\cal D}^{\beta]})\,.

(3.30)

The related Noether charge is defined in analogy to (
3.24
) and (
3.25
):

{\cal P}_{div}(\xi)=\int_{\Sigma}d^{3}x{\cal I}_{div}^{0}(\xi)=\oint_{\partial\Sigma}ds_{i}{\cal I}_{div}^{0i}(\xi).

(3.31)

At last, we remark that despite a divergence in the Lagrangian does not contribute into the field equations, it explicitly adds terms to the Noether current, superpotential, and charge.

4
Covariant conserved quantities in TEGR and STEGR

4.1
TEGR

The TEGR Lagrangian (
2.1
) just belongs to the class of Lagrangians (
3.1
). Therefore, the procedure of constructing currents and superpotentials, as described in the previous section, can be applied to (
2.1

In the TEGR case, the collective field
\psi^{A}

\psi^{A}=\{h^{a}{}_{\rho};{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{A}}}{}{}^{a}{}_{b\mu}\}

{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{A}}}{}{}^{a}{}_{b\mu}
is not a dynamical field, its variation must be taken into account because diffeomorphisms act on all geometric objects under consideration. Thus, because ISC is a vector in a lower spacetime index the variations (
3.2
) are rewritten as

\displaystyle\delta h^{a}{}_{\rho}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\pounds}_{\xi}h^{a}{}_{\rho}=-\xi^{\alpha}h^{a}{}_{\rho,\alpha}-\xi^{\alpha}{}_{,\rho}h^{a}{}_{\alpha}\,,

\displaystyle\delta{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{A}}}{}{}^{a}{}_{b\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\pounds}_{\xi}{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{A}}}{}{}^{a}{}_{b\mu}=-\xi^{\alpha}{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{A}}}{}{}^{a}{}_{b\mu,\alpha}-\xi^{\alpha}{}_{,\mu}{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{A}}}{}{}^{a}{}_{b\alpha}\,.

3.6

3.8

\displaystyle{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{\cal N}}}_{\sigma}{}^{\alpha\tau\beta}

\displaystyle 0\,.

The third term in (

where the term

and
\partial_{\rho}\xi^{\sigma}

4.39

\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{\nabla}}_{\sigma}h^{a}{}_{\rho}

\displaystyle\left[h\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{\theta}}_{\sigma}{}^{\alpha}+{{\delta\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{{{\cal L}}}}}\over{\delta h^{a}{}_{\alpha}}}h^{a}{}_{\sigma}\right]\xi^{\sigma}+\frac{h}{\kappa}{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{S}}}{}_{\sigma}{}^{\alpha\rho}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{\nabla}}_{\rho}\xi^{\sigma}\,,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{\nabla}}_{\alpha}{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{\cal J}}}{}^{\alpha}(\xi)=0\,,

(4.47)

\displaystyle{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{\cal J}}}{}^{\alpha}(\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{\beta}{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{\cal J}}}{}^{\alpha\beta}(\xi)={\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{\nabla}}}_{\beta}{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{\cal J}}}{}^{\alpha\beta}(\xi)\,.

(4.48)

All the local conserved quantities are covariant under coordinate transformations and invariant under local Lorentz rotations. Consequently, integral conserved quantities that can be constructed by the aforementioned standard rules exhibit the same properties:

4.3
Interpretation of conserved quantities

To determine interpretation of conserved quantities in TEGR nd STEGR defined above it is instructive to outline Minkowski space with a matter propagating through it in a primitive format. Let us consider a flat spacetime in coordinates with metric:

(4.70)

Coordinates are numerated as
(t,x,y,z)=(x^{0},x^{i})=(x^{\alpha})
, where

\xi^{\alpha}=(-1,0,0,0).

(4.71)

One assumes that the matter in the Minkowski space has differentially conserved symmetric energy-momentum tensor
\Theta^{\alpha}{}_{\beta}:=\partial_{\alpha}\Theta^{\alpha}{}_{\beta}=0
. As a result, the related current
{{J}}{}^{\alpha}(\xi)=\Theta^{\alpha}{}_{\beta}\xi^{\beta}
is differentially conserved, as well,
\partial_{\alpha}{{J}}{}^{\alpha}(\xi)=0
. Its components present the energy density
{{J}}{}^{0}=\Theta^{0}{}_{0}\xi^{0}
and the momentum density
{{J}}{}^{i}=\Theta^{i}{}_{0}\xi^{0}
measured by the aforementioned observers.

It is evidently that the current
{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{\cal J}}}{}^{\alpha}(\xi)
defined in (
4.46
) in TEGR, and the current
{\cal J}{}^{\alpha}(\xi)
defined in (
4.64
) with (
4.66
) in STEGR, generalize the simplest current definition
{{J}}{}^{\alpha}(\xi)
. Consequently, their
components have the analogous interpretation for observers. The generalization is related to the observer proper vectors
\xi
given in (
4.71
), they can be arbitrary timelike vectors with which a reference frame can be associated. As an extreme vector
\xi
, it can be chosen a proper vector of a freely falling observer. Then, owing the Einstein equivalence principle, all the components of the currents have to be equal to zero:
{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{\cal J}}}{}^{\alpha}(\xi)={{\cal J}}{}^{\alpha}(\xi)=0
.

The Noether charges
{\cal P}(\xi)
defined in TEGR (
4.49
) and in STEGR (
4.69
) depend also on the vector field
\xi
. Because, as a rule, they are defined at surfaces abounding (possibly at infinity) isolated systems the vector
\xi
is chosen as a vector reflecting symmetries of a spacetime (for example, Killing vector). In particular, when the vector
\xi
is timelike, the corresponding conserved charge, constrained to the surface
\partial\Sigma
, represents the energy of this restricted (or infinite) volume. In this case it can be classified as energy measured by a set of observers on
\partial\Sigma
with their own vectors
\xi
.

5
“Turning off” gravity principle and “gauges”

In sections
1
and
2
, it was remarked that teleparallel connections both in TEGR and in STEGR are external structures and cannot be determined inside the theory itself. One has to chose them using additional principles introduced for each concrete solution
[
1
,
28
]
, moreover, such a choice is not unique. Our principles generalise those in earlier works of other authors and are based on the simple natural requirements which sound as follows. All the constructed above currents, superpotentials and charges must vanish for the systems where the intensity of the gravitational field is absent.

5.1
TEGR

In TEGR, Noether’s current (
4.41
), or (
4.46
),
{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{\cal J}}}{}^{\alpha}(\xi)
, with energy-momentum (
4.42
),
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{\theta}}_{\sigma}{}^{\alpha}
, and superpotential (
4.45
),
{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{\cal J}}}{}^{\alpha\beta}(\xi)
, are proportional to contortion components
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{K}}{}^{a}{}_{c\mu}
(or, alternatively
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{T}}{}^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\nu}
or
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{S}}{}_{a}{}^{\mu\nu}
) and these quantities must vanish in absence of gravity. Following this requirement we turn to the formula (
2.8
) and formulate the the list of rules for determination of ISC

\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{A}}{}^{a}{}_{c\mu}
:

1)

for a GR solution under consideration one chooses a convenient tetrad and defines L-CSC
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{A}}{}^{a}{}_{c\mu}
by (
2.7
);

2)

then one constructs a related curvature tensor of determined above L-CSC

\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{R}}{}^{i}{}_{j\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{A}}}{}{}^{i}{}_{j\nu}-\partial_{\nu}{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{A}}}{}{}^{i}{}_{j\mu}+{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{A}}}{}{}^{i}{}_{k\mu}{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{A}}}{}{}^{k}{}_{j\nu}-{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{A}}}{}{}^{i}{}_{k\nu}{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{A}}}{}{}^{k}{}_{j\mu};

3)

to “switch off” gravity one solves the absent gravity equation
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{R}}{}^{a}{}_{b\gamma\delta}=0
for the parameters in the chosen GR solution;

4)

then, for the parameters satisfying
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{R}}{}^{a}{}_{b\gamma\delta}=0
one uses to take
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{A}}{}^{a}{}_{c\mu}=\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{A}}{}^{a}{}_{c\mu}
.

Recall that both
{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{\cal J}}}{}^{\alpha}(\xi)
and
{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{\cal J}}}{}^{\alpha\beta}(\xi)
are explicitly spacetime covariant and Lorentz invariant. Therefore, if the chosen tetrad and the determined (as above) ISC are transformed by local Lorentz rotations as in (
2.12
) and (
2.13
) simultaneously, and/or arbitrary coordinate transformations then conserved quantities are left covariant (invariant). All of these can be reformulated as follows. The initial pair of tetrad and ISC defines a set of the pairs connected with initial one by Lorentz rotations and/or arbitrary coordinate transformations for which conserved quantities are covariant (invariant). Such a set of pairs we call as a “gauge”.

However, it turns out that after applying “turning off” gravity principle in TEGR, the result of determining ISC is ambiguous. It depends on the tetrad that
we choose from the start. As a result, one obtains different pairs
of tetrad and ISC that is one obtains different “gauges”, where pairs are not connected by local Lorentz rotations and/or arbitrary coordinate transformations For different “gauges”
{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{\cal J}}}{}^{\alpha}(\xi)
and
{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bullet}}{{\cal J}}}{}^{\alpha\beta}(\xi)
are different as well that gives us different values of conserved quantities.
Therefore, one of the main purposes in constructing conserved quantities is to find appropriate
the gauges in TEGR in which we would have physically meaningful results for the concrete solutions.

At last, traditionally one uses a notion as the Wietzenböck gauge
[
3
]
. In this case, the word “gauge” is used in different senses: when one says “Wietzenböck gauge” one means the only one pair — tetrad and zero ISC; and when we say “gauge” in our definition we mean the whole equivalence class of pairs of tetrads and ISCs connected as defined above.

5.2
STEGR

In STEGR, to define the undetermined STC we use the adapted from TEGR “turning off” gravity principle. It is based on the assumption that the total current (
4.64
)
{\cal J}{}^{\alpha}(\xi)
, and superpotential (
4.65
),
{\cal J}{}^{\alpha\beta}(\xi)
, have to vanish in absence of gravity. It is reasonable to set that all of the items in (
4.64
) and (
4.65
) vanish separately. Then,
Q_{\alpha\mu\nu}
, or
L^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\nu}
, in addition with
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{R}}{}^{\alpha}{}_{\beta\mu\nu}
in GR have to vanish in the absence of gravity. As a result, we formulate the next steps for finding the STC in STEGR:

1)

for known GR solution one constructs the related Riemannian curvature tensor of the Levi-Civita connection:

\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{R}}{}^{\alpha}{}_{\beta\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{\Gamma}}{}^{\alpha}{}_{\beta\nu}-\partial_{\nu}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{\Gamma}}{}^{\alpha}{}_{\beta\mu}+\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{\Gamma}}{}^{\alpha}{}_{\kappa\mu}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{\Gamma}}{}^{\kappa}{}_{\beta\nu}-\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{\Gamma}}{}^{\alpha}{}_{\kappa\nu}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{\Gamma}}{}^{\kappa}{}_{\beta\mu};

2)

to “switch off” gravity one solves the absent gravity equation
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{R}}{}^{\alpha}{}_{\beta\mu\nu}=0
for the parameters in the chosen GR solution;

3)

then for the found parameters satisfying
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{R}}{}^{\alpha}{}_{\beta\mu\nu}=0
one uses to take
\Gamma{}^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\nu}=\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{\Gamma}}{}^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\nu}
for the chosen solution.

Torsion of the found connection should be zero automatically because we take it from the Levi-Civita connection for some parameter values, and Levi-Civita connection is always symmetric. Curvature of the found connection should be zero too because we found it from the equation
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{R}}{}^{\alpha}{}_{\beta\gamma\delta}=0
.

To have the same terminology in STEGR as in TEGR, so formally, we define a pair of coordinates (
x^{\alpha}
) and connection
\Gamma{}^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\nu}
, with the set of pairs which are connected to it by the transformations
x^{\alpha}=x^{\alpha}(\bar{x})
:

\Gamma{}^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\nu}=\frac{\partial x^{\alpha}}{\partial\bar{x}{}^{\bar{\alpha}}}\frac{\partial\bar{x}^{\bar{\mu}}}{\partial x^{\mu}}\frac{\partial\bar{x}^{\bar{\nu}}}{\partial x^{\nu}}\bar{\Gamma}{}^{\bar{\alpha}}{}_{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}+\frac{\partial x^{\alpha}}{\partial\bar{x}{}^{\bar{\lambda}}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}\left(\frac{\partial\bar{x}{}^{\bar{\lambda}}}{\partial x^{\nu}}\right)

as a “gauge”. When we say “gauge” in our definition we mean the set of all possible coordinates and the values of STCs in them, such that the above relation
\Gamma{}^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\nu}=\Gamma{}^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\nu}(\bar{\Gamma})
is satisfied under all possible coordinate transformations. It is the equivalence class. It differs from the case of zero STC only that is called as “coincident gauge”
[
29
,
30
]
. It is only one case of coordinates.

The same as in TEGR, the result of determining STC in STEGR is ambiguous, it depends on the coordinates that we choose from the start. This leads to different “gauges” for which one obtains
different
{\cal J}{}^{\alpha}(\xi)
and
{\cal J}{}^{\alpha\beta}(\xi)
that gives different values for conserved quantities.
Therefore, again one of the main purposes in constructing conserved quantities in STEGR is to find appropriate
the “gauges” in which we would have physically meaningful results for the concrete solutions.

5.3
Appropriate gauges

A necessity to construct
covariant conserved quantities
in TEGR and STEGR for this or that solution (model) requires to restrict a possible choice of ISCs and STCs.
Such a situation is not new in metric GR, where the classical energy-momentum pseudotensors and superpotentials are not covariant, see Chapter 1 in the book
[
26
]
. One of many variants to improve the situation is to use the bi-metric representation of GR, for example, suggested in
[
31
]
. Introducing an auxiliary background
metric
\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}
and making the use of the Noether theorem one construct covariant energy-momentum tensors and superpotentials. The Einstein equations are preserved, but the currents and superpotentials depend on
\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}
drastically. Although, in the general formalism,
\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}
is an arbitrary, an appropriate choice of
\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}
depends on the solution under consideration essentially. For example, it can be flat background if one considers an isolated system with asymptotically flat metric. However, even if a concrete system for analyzing is chosen ambiguity in a choice of
\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}
can be left.

Returning to TEGR and STEGR, we stress that, from the one hand, our formalism for constructing conserved quantities in TEGR and STEGR is general one because it presents a general method. On the other hand, it is highly solution-dependent, and thus not generally applicable because gauges have to be determined for each concrete solution separately. The role of ISC and STC in TEGR and STEGR is analogous to
\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}
in metric GR. Thus, there is no an unique energy-momentum tensor, unlike in electrodynamics. Such a situation is not surprising in GR by the Einstein equivalence principle.

Finalizing the section, we establish a rigorous framework for conserved quantities in TEGR and STEGR. The method of “turning off gravity” is applied to define the teleparallel connections in both TEGR and STEGR, where the choice of the initial tetrad in TEGR or the initial coordinates in STEGR determines the resulting gauge that has to be found finally as an appropriate one.

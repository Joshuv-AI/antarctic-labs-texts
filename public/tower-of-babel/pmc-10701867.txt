Nanofluidic Membrane with Spent Battery Residues for Efficient Energy Generation
Authors: Zhenzhen Guo, Yuchuan Xiong, Yurong Han, Yueyue Wang, Muhammad Sultan Irshad, Xianbao Wang
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractHarvesting energy in the ambient environment has become a dominant energy source and is promising toward a sustainable perspective. However, developing an energy generator with readily available raw materials and continuous output performance remains a challenge. Herein, we have demonstrated that a nanofluidic membrane with an asymmetric structure device (ASD) design by using available raw materials spent battery residues can achieve continuous power generation. The ASD can generate a voltage of ∼0.5 V by dropping 0.1 mL of water. Detailed experimental results reveal that ions in water play a synergistic role in enhancing output performance. By dropping 0.1 mL of seawater, the power output of ∼46.67 mW m–2 can be readily derived with voltage of ∼0.7 V. The work opens a viable way to generate electric power by environmental energy with facile structure design and easy-to-access materials. The output energy will greatly promote practical applications such as the power supply of low-power devices.

Introduction

Obtaining power from natural water through functional materials is considered to be an effective way to solve the increasingly serious energy shortage.1−4 About 71% of the earth’s surface is covered by water. As one of the richest and most widely available clean resources, water is a huge energy pool. It has been more than 100 years since electromagnetic generators based on large dams and water turbines were used to generate electricity from hydropower. In the past decade, various miniaturized and flexible nanogenerators, which can effectively collect energy from natural water and be used for various small electronic devices, have attracted more and more interest.5−7 These studies directly generate electricity through the electrical coupling between materials and various forms of water,8 including fluid,7,9−13 droplet,14,15 moisture,16−19 waves,20 etc. The solid–liquid coupling shows a superior technology with high flexibility and eco-friendly development. So far, water-enabled electric generation developed is based on ion diffusion,21 flow potential,22,23 evaporating potential,24 and ionovoltaic effect,25,26 which are affected by the water–solid interface interaction. Among them, water transmission and evaporation-induced electricity stand out because they can operate independently in a wide range of environments without requiring mechanical input.27−30 The generator can generate sustainable electricity by the capillary action between water and solid surfaces when water passes through nano/micropores until the water completely evaporates.31−35

In 2017, Peng’s group developed a fluidic nanogenerator fiber from an aligned carbon nanotube sheet. During the liquid flow, a voltage output of ∼0.12 V and a current of ∼22 μA were output.10 In 2021, a flexible fiber fluidic nanogenerator based on aligned carbon nanotube/transition metal oxide composite film was designed by them, achieving higher performance with a voltage output of ∼0.31 V and a current of ∼40 μA.9 Next year, Qu’s group found a high voltage of ∼2.9 V or a current of ∼16.8 μA can be harvested by highly oriented graphene oxide nanochannels.36 In addition, Yeh’s group prepared a nanofluidic generator by graphene/carbon black with a considerable current of ∼121 μA and a voltage of ∼0.49 V.37 However, the current is only an instantaneous value and quickly decreases, demonstrating a discontinuous electrical energy output performance. These tasks involve complex material preparation or poor electrical energy output persistence. In recent years, spent battery recycling has become a hot field for sustainable development.38 Valuable resources (metals or compounds) can be recycled as attractive secondary resources, which not only minimizes waste emissions and pollution problems but also improves resource utilization. However, in the process of spent battery recycling, a large number of spent battery residues (SBRs) are often produced. Rational use of these SBRs to make them work as functional materials for various applications is also essential for sustainable development.39

In this work, we have successfully developed a nanofluidic membrane with a high power generation performance through easy-to-access materials and simple structure design. SBR is recycled as a functional material to design an asymmetric structure device (ASD) for water-induced energy generation by the capillary action between water and solid surfaces. Experimental studies have shown that the best performance of ASD can be achieved in the N-fully wet state, and a reasonable mechanism explanation has been proposed. Dropping 0.1 mL of water into the ASD, an open-circuit voltage of ∼0.5 V can be generated. Open-circuit voltage can be increased further up to ∼0.7 V with a short-circuit current of ∼60 μA by dropping 0.1 mL of seawater, which is comparable with previous reports (Table S1). In addition, the voltage output of the ASD can be further scaled up by connecting several devices in series for practical application. The voltage output of ∼2.1 V can be achieved by the series connection of three energy generators for lighting a small calculator. Through the recycling of SBR, a new functional material is provided as a wet power device to meet the energy requirements for electronic devices by natural water. More importantly, the development of this technology will promote the sustainable development of society.

Experimental Section

Materials

Sulfuric acid (H2SO4) and sodium persulfate (Na2S2O8) were obtained from Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co. Ltd. (China). Pyrrole (Py) and ammonium persulfate ((NH4)2S2O8) were purchased from Macklin Biochemical Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). Cellulose fabric (CF) was supplied by Taobao.com. Seawater was from the South China Sea in Shenzhen, China.

Preparation of SBR

SBR is the filter residue generated during the battery recovery process of spent lithium iron phosphate. The process refers to our previous report.40 Simply, 5 g of spent lithium iron phosphate black powder, 35 mL of 0.2 mol L–1 H2SO4, and 2.73 g of Na2S2O8 were added to a 100 mL round-bottom flask, reacted for 5 min at 80 °C under stirring, and then vacuum filtered. The filter residues were dried at 60 °C to obtain SBR. The filtrate was used to recover Li, Fe, etc.

Preparation of SBR-CF

CF (6 × 6 cm) was immersed in the SBR solution (1 g of SBR, 50 mL of deionized (DI) water, ultrasonic dispersion for 2 h) and then dried at 60 °C in a drying oven. The process was repeated three times to successfully obtain SBR-CF.

Preparation of PPy (Polypyrrole)-CF

1 mL of Py was added to 10 mL of DI water to obtain solution A. Solution B was obtained by adding 1 g of (NH4)2S2O8 to 20 mL of DI water. First, CF (6 × 6 cm) was immersed in solution A for several seconds. Then, solution B was added dropwise to its surface and left to stand for tens of seconds. After the color of CF turned black, it was washed several times with DI water to remove unreacted Py or (NH4)2S2O8 and excess PPy not polymerized on CF. Then, it was dried at 60 °C in a drying oven.

Preparation of ASD

ASD was prepared by the following method. Simply, the prepared SBR-CF was placed vertically into solution A of step 2.4 until it penetrated halfway of SBR-CF. Then, solution B of step 2.4 was added dropwise to the portion permeated with the Py solution and left to stand for several tens of seconds. Then, it was immediately washed with DI water several times and dried at 60 °C in a drying oven. SBR-PPy-CF was prepared by the same method only to completely polymerize the entire SBR-CF with PPy.

Characterization

The morphologies of CF, SBR-CF, PPy-CF, and ASD were observed by Scanning electron microscopy (SEM JSM6510LV). THERMO FISHERSICENTIFIC Escalab 250Xi was used to characterize X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) data for element analysis of CF, SBR-CF, PPy-CF, and ASD. Ion concentration in seawater is detected by an ICP atomic emission spectrometer (Optimal 8000, US PE).

Power Generation

SBR-CF, PPy-CF, and ASD prepared were cut into long strips (1.5 × 6 cm) as power generators. The electrical property data were tested by a data acquisition system (KEYSIGHT, 34972A). The test was conducted in the laboratory, with room temperature of 25–28 °C and humidity of 50–55%.

Results and Discussion

Preparation and Characterization

A nanofluidic membrane is developed by recycling SBR as a functional material for water-induced energy generation by capillary action between water and solid surfaces (Figure 1). SBR, mainly carbon-coated iron phosphate, is the filter residue generated during the battery recovery process. Figure 2a shows the synthetic process of ASD. Simply, CF was immersed in the SBR solution to obtain SBR-CF and then Py polymerized at one end of SBR-CF. From the SEM image in Figure S1, it can be seen that the fiber of CF has a very smooth surface. After loading the SBR (Figure 2b–d), the fiber surface becomes grainy, indicating that the SBR is successfully loaded on the fiber surface. The fiber surface of PPy-CF also shows a rough structure with small granular PPy (Figure S2). Subsequently, the morphology of ASD was tested, mainly the morphology of the end of the loaded SBR and PPy. As shown in Figure 2e–g, after loading PPy on SBR-CF, a layer of small granular PPy is loaded on the surface of the SBR-CF fiber. XPS measurement was used to analyze the elements of CF, SBR-CF, PPy-CF, and ASD. The XPS spectrum of CF shows two characteristic peaks of C 1s and O 1s at 285 and 532 eV (Figure S3). For SBR-CF (Figure S4), three additional characteristic peaks appear at 133, 190, and 687 eV, corresponding to P 2p, P 2s, and Fe 2p. The Fe 2p spectrum shows four peaks at 712, 716, 725, and 728 eV, which is consistent with the test results of leaching the filter residues of lithium iron phosphate battery.41 After loading PPy on SBR-CF, as shown in Figure S5, the XPS spectrum of ASD shows all characteristic peaks of SBR and PPy. The C 1s spectrum shows four peaks at 283.7 (C=C), 284.8 (C–C), 286.1 (C–O), and 288.5 eV (C=O) (Figure 2h). The Fe 2p spectrum shows four peaks of leaching the filter residue of the lithium iron phosphate battery (Figure 2i). The N 1s spectrum reveals two peaks of pyrrolic N and C–N+ (Figure 2j).

Energy Generation

A voltage test system was used to investigate the voltage output performance by connecting the electrodes at both ends of the device, where liquid is added at one end of the device (Figure 3a). Figure 3b shows the voltage output performance of SBR-CF, PPy-CF, and ASD by dropping water. For SBR-CF, a typical flow potential10 appears after dropping. At first, there was no voltage output. As the water flows close to the electrode at the other end, the voltage output increases and then decreases immediately. Surprisingly, PPy-CF immediately generated a voltage of ∼0.2 V and remained stable after the drop of water (0.1 mL). For ASD, dropping water (0.1 mL) from SBR-CF terminal (the device was in a nonfully wet state (N-fully wet)), the device stably outputs voltage of ∼0.5 V. When 0.1 mL seawater (Figure S6 for ion concentration of seawater) is dropped onto the SBR-CF terminal of ASD, the voltage output of ASD is significantly enhanced to ∼0.7 V (Figure 3c), which reveals that ions in water play a synergistic role in enhancing the output performance. As a comparison, the voltage of SBR-PPy-CF is also tested and is shown in Figure S7. The voltage output of SBR-PPy-CF (∼0.62 V) is lower than that of ASD (∼0.7 V). During the experiment, special phenomena were found (Figure 3d). When the device is under the N-fully wet condition, the device maintains a stable voltage output performance. Once the device is fully wet by continuously dropping seawater onto the SBR-CF terminal of ASD (fully wet), the device output performance will decline. When seawater is dropped from the SBR-PPy-CF end of ASD (R-fully wet), only when the seawater is transferred to the device in the full wet state, there can be power output, and a small output voltage of ∼0.36 V is displayed.

(a) Schematic illustration for energy generation. (b) Voltage output performance of SBR-CF, PPy-CF, and ASD. (c) Voltage output performance of ASD by dropping water and seawater. (d) Voltage output performance of ASD under R-fully wet, Fully wet, and N-fully wet conditions by dropping seawater. (e) Voltage output performance of devices in series. (f and g) Stability and cycle stability of ASD.

The voltage output of the ASD can be further scaled up by connecting several devices in series. As shown in Figure 3e, the output voltage of one device is ∼0.7 V, the voltage of two devices in series can reach ∼1.4 V, and the voltage of three devices in series is ∼2.1 V, revealing a linear growth behavior. Similarly, the short-circuit current of ASD in parallel is also tested, and the results show linear growth behavior (Figure 3f). The short-circuit current of one device is ∼60 μA, which of two devices is ∼120 μA, and the maximum short-circuit current of three devices is ∼180 μA. By calculation, the energy output of the ASD is ∼46.67 mW m–2. The stability and cycle stability of ASD were further tested. ASD has maintained good voltage output performance in multiple cycle tests (with the natural evaporation of seawater, the voltage output decreases, and then seawater is dropped again) (Figure 3g). In addition, after two months of storage, ASD still maintains a stable voltage output performance (Figure 3h). The power generated by ASD can face applications such as power supply of low-power devices by the series connection of devices (Figure 4a,b). As shown in Figure 4c, the generated electric energy can completely light up a small calculator by connecting three devices in series. By dropping 0.1 mL of seawater into each device, the calculator could run for 44 min. By connecting eight devices in series, the electric energy generated can completely light up a red LED for 40 min with 0.1 mL of seawater in each device (Figure 4d). The three devices in series can also make the LED emit weak light (Figure S8).

Mechanism Analysis

On the basis of the above experiments, we conducted a feasibility analysis of the generator mechanism. The zeta potentials of different materials were tested to assist in the mechanism analysis. As shown in Figure S9, the zeta potentials of PPy and SBR are +14.4 and −41.5, respectively. From the zeta potential, it can be clearly known that in the Debye length range, when the liquid passes through the SBR, positively charged ions will pass through first. Therefore, when the liquid passes through the device, it will attract more positive ions to pass through the device. First, for SBR-CF, as the water flows close to the electrode at the other end, the voltage output increases and then decreases immediately, which is a typical flow potential. When the device contacts the solution, due to the negative zeta potential, the cations adsorb on the SBR and form a stern layer. However, anions in the diffusion layer are delayed in migration to offset the net charge of the stern layer. Therefore, electrons are extracted from SBR to balance the excess charge, resulting in potential difference.10 For PPy-CF, the energy can be immediately generated voltage by dropping solution, which is the potential difference between wet and dry areas by changing the surface properties through ion adsorption of PPy.25

The energy generation mechanism of ASD in different states is systematically discussed (Figure 5). When liquid is transmitted from the SBR-CF end until the device is in the fully wet state, it is obvious that the voltage rises first, then falls, and then remains unchanged (Figure 3d). As shown in Figure 5a, the electric energy output of the ASD in the full wet state is generated by the potential difference caused by the concentration gradient of negative ions. In detail, when the liquid flows from the SBR-CF end, the positive and negative ions in the solution will also flow. However, due to the negative potential on the SBR surface, during liquid flow, positive ions will be attracted to form a stern layer. Due to the asymmetric structure of the device, the potential on the PPy surface will neutralize part of the potential on the SBR surface. In this process, the SBR-CF terminal will attract more positive ions and repel negative ions. A small amount of positive ions and negative ions passing through the liquid flow process will also be attracted and repelled by the SBR-PPy-CF end. These rejected negative ions will form a potential difference at both ends, thus generating electrical energy. In addition, when the liquid is transmitted in the reverse direction, that is, the liquid is dropped from the SBR-PPy-CF end (R-fully wet). The experimental results show that only when the liquid flows to the other end, there is a voltage output, but the value is smaller than that under the fully wet state of forward transmission (Figure 3b). Based on the result, the mechanism analysis of the device under R-fully wet conditions is shown in Figure 5b. When the liquid flows from the SBR-CF-PPy end because SBR-CF has no ability to transfer ions under dry conditions, electric energy can only be generated when the liquid completely flows into the other end. The performance of the device is lower under R-fully wet conditions, mainly due to the different liquid volumes at both ends. High liquid volume has more ions. Therefore, when water is transported from the SBR-CF terminal, more negative ions are repelled at the SBR-CF end, while fewer negative ions are repelled at the SBR-PPy-CF end, resulting in a higher potential difference. When water is transported from the SBR-PPy-CF end, the volume of liquid at the SBR-CF end decreases, and the number of rejected negative ions decreases, while the number of rejected negative ions at the SBR-PPy-CF terminal increases correspondingly, resulting in a lower potential difference.

Energy generation mechanism of ASD in different states: (a) Fully wet, (b) R-fully wet, and (c) N-fully wet.

The experimental results reveal that ASD has the highest voltage output performance in the N-fully wet state, which is mainly due to the synergistic effect of the flowing potential and ionovoltaic effect. Figure 5c details the energy generation mechanism of ASD under N-fully wet state: (i) PPy-CF can transport ions under dry conditions. Because of the negative potential on the surface of SBR-CF, positive ions preferentially pass through the liquid during the flow process. Due to the special properties of PPy, ions can pass through the dry region, thus achieving power output42; (ii) The electrical energy can be generated by changing the surface properties through ion adsorption, which is called ionovoltaic effect and can be explained by energy band theory. As a p-type semiconductor, the Fermi level (Ef) of PPy lies between the intrinsic Fermi level (Eidry) and the valence band (Evdry). Once the liquid contacts PPy, because of the positive potential of PPy surface, the PPy surface will adsorb anions, so that the holes in the bulk region move to the dry and wet interface, and the surface energy band is upward.43 The hole concentration in the wet region decreases and the Fermi level (Ef) is close to Eidry. The difference in hole concentration between wet and dry regions results in potential differences. When the liquid is transported in the device, the dry and wet interfaces move continuously and generate voltage continuously. The energy output of ASD through dropping seawater is higher than that of dropping water, which comes from the influence of ion content.

Conclusions

In this study, a self-sustained nanofluidic membrane ASD driven from liquid transmission is demonstrated by recycling SBR. Experimental studies have shown that the ASD can achieve the highest performance in the N-fully wet state. A remarkable voltage of ∼0.7 V and a current of ∼60 μA can be continuously generated by dropping seawater (0.1 mL) to ASD in the ambient environment. By systematically analyzing the electricity generation mechanism of ASD in different states, such a good electricity generation performance should be attributed to the synergistic effect of the flow potential and ionovoltaic effect. The energy generated can be scaled up easily by interconnecting multiple devices to face application such as the power supply of low-power devices. Through easily available materials and simple preparation methods, both waste recycling and effective energy acquisition are realized, indicating that ASD has great potential for sustainable energy harvesting for powering commercial electronics. The work provides a general and effective approach to meet the energy requirements for electronic devices by natural water.

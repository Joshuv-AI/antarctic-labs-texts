Research Progress on Rapid Dissolution Technology of Polymers for Offshore Oilfields
Authors: Bin Huang, Yiping Zhang, Chenxi Wang, Hong Du, Xianjie Li, Zongjun Jiang, Wei Zhang, Cheng Fu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
As global onshore oil and gas resources gradually become depleted, offshore oilfields are playing an increasingly critical role in energy supply. Polymer flooding represents a highly efficient chemical enhanced oil recovery technique in an offshore oil field, yet its offshore application is constrained by slow dissolution, large footprint requirements, and significant viscosity loss. Consequently, rapid polymer dissolution has emerged as a prominent research focus. This paper systematically reviews the research progress in rapid dissolution technologies for offshore oil-displacement polymers worldwide, classifying them into four categories: physical dissolution enhancement (intensified stirring, forced stretching, high-gravity treatment, and ultrasonic processing), chemical dissolution enhancement (cosolvent and surfactant assistance), physicochemical modification of polymers, and multitechnology composite dissolution methods. By comparing the advantages and limitations of different approaches, suitable strategies for offshore environments are summarized, and future development directions are prospected, providing theoretical support for offshore polymer flooding.

Introduction

Strengthening domestic oil and gas exploration and development to reduce external dependence is vital for China’s energy self-sufficiency. Offshore oil production has become a key part of national petroleum supply. With the continuous development of offshore oilfields, stabilizing output, controlling water cut and improving oil recovery efficiently are urgent technical challenges. As promising chemical enhanced oil recovery methods, polymer and composite flooding rely on sufficient polymer dissolution to guarantee reservoir displacement performance. Polymer solutions require dispersion and maturation prior to reservoir injection. As shown in Figure , the dissolution process dominates the entire polymer flooding workflow. Conventional techniques feature large equipment footprint, long maturation time and inhomogeneous dissolution. Polymer rapid dissolution technologies reduce macromolecular interactions and accelerate powder wetting and swelling via physical enhancement, chemical additives, molecular modification and composite approaches, achieving rapid dissolution with stable viscosity. They are classified into physical, chemical, modified and composite technologies. Existing reviews mainly focus on onshore processes, lacking systematic classification and adaptability evaluation for harsh offshore environments with high temperature, high salinity and limited platform space. This article provides a comprehensive review of the mechanisms, research progress, and engineering applications of four rapid dissolution techniques, compares their advantages and disadvantages in polymer dissolution, and analyzes their strengths and limitations in viscosity regulation, equipment miniaturization, and adaptability to extreme environments. On this basis, an offshore adaptability assessment system is established to offer theoretical and technical support for the development of efficient, miniaturized, and low-cost offshore polymer flooding and the effective extraction of offshore oil and gas resources.

Physical Dissolution Technology

Conventional Stirring Technology

Conventional stirring technology accelerates the swelling and disentanglement process by optimizing the structure of the stirrer, improving the stirring intensity, enhancing the mixing uniformity of polymer particles and water, and reducing the formation of “fish eyes”. , The action principle of conventional stirrers to accelerate polymer dissolution is to strengthen the mass transfer process, destroy the agglomeration structure and optimize the dissolution environment through mechanical action. Specifically, the shear force and turbulent flow generated by stirring can break the agglomerates of polymers, thereby increasing the effective contact area between polymers and solvents; at the same time, it continuously scours the high-concentration solution boundary layer on the particle surface, eliminates mass transfer resistance, maintains the concentration difference between solvent and polymer, and accelerates the intermolecular diffusion rate. In addition, stirring can make the temperature and concentration distribution in the system uniform, reduce dissolution dead zones, assist the stretching and disentanglement of polymer molecular chains, reduce the intermolecular entanglement resistance, make the solvent easier to penetrate the gaps of molecular chains, and ultimately accelerate the polymer dissolution process to form a uniform polymer solution, Here’s the process of improving traditional mixing techniques.

Single Mixer

Initially, a single type of impeller, such as a two-blade inclined paddle, four-blade open turbine, anchor-type impeller, or frame-type impeller, is commonly used to accelerate the dissolution of polymers. While single impeller designs are simple, energy-efficient, and easy to operate, they are limited by their uniform flow field. This restricts the ability to achieve both efficient circulation and mild shear, leading to problems such as stirring dead zones, uneven mixing, and insufficient dissolution. Under harsh conditions, such as high viscosity and high salinity, excessive shear can cause molecular degradation, or inadequate circulation may result in slower dissolution, making it difficult to meet the requirements for full-cycle polymer dissolution. Consequently, the potential for performance improvement is limited.

Modular Mixer

To address these limitations, combined impeller systems have been proposed and extensively optimized for structural design and polymer dissolution performance. Miao et al. numerically simulated the mixing process of double-layer six-straight-blade turbine impellers. The results confirmed that the double-layer configuration can generate a unique flow field and effectively improve the mixing efficiency in stirred tanks, providing a theoretical basis for the dissolution application of high-viscosity polymers. Liang et al. compared the hydrodynamic performance of three turbine impeller configurations, including double-layer straight blades, hexagonal blades, and alternating right-angle blades. It was found that the hexagonal-blade impeller exhibited superior axial velocity, while the straight-blade impeller achieved the best radial velocity, which provides a practical guideline for impeller type selection. Li et al. established a numerical model of a dual-angle blade-frame combined impeller and validated the CFD predictions via 2D-PIV measurements. Although optimizing the distance between the impeller and the tank bottom eliminated low-velocity dead zones, this impeller configuration still suffered from limited mixing efficiency and high-power consumption.

Further studies have focused on the working condition adaptability and performance enhancement of combined impellers. Zhou et al. investigated the coupled airfoil and anchor-frame impeller system. The results indicated that the corotating dual-impeller arrangement effectively strengthened the overall fluid circulation. The anchor-frame impeller achieved its optimal performance at a rotational speed of 10 r/min, and the combination with a downward-pumping airfoil impeller yielded the best polymer dissolution performance, whereas counter-rotating operation deteriorated the dissolution efficiency. Xie et al. adopted CFD simulations to analyze three matching schemes for upward-pumping airfoil and anchor impellers. They pointed out that the combined configuration of KCXU and forward MS impellers produced higher fluid velocity and circulation flux during the middle and late reaction stages, which compensated for the inherent flow field deficiencies of single impellers at low power consumption. Li et al. proposed a Paravisc-anchor combined impeller with the advantages of low shear rate, high circulation capacity, low energy consumption, and high mixing efficiency, which effectively solves the problem of large footprint occupation of offshore water injection equipment.

Additional research has concentrated on the adaptability of combined impellers for polymer dissolution under seawater environments and wide viscosity variation conditions. Huang et al. compared the mixing performance of the ZBK + BKS combined impeller with a conventional ribbon impeller. In the transitional flow regime, the BKS impeller dominated the axial mixing process, while the synergistic interaction between ZBK and BKS impellers enhanced axial transportation in the laminar flow regime. The combined impeller shortened the mixing time in the transitional flow region and maintained a comparable energy consumption level with traditional ribbon impellers under laminar conditions, making it suitable for polymer systems with wide viscosity fluctuations. Zheng et al. verified that the inner–outer combined structure consisting of multistage axial-flow impellers, small-diameter anchor impellers, and screw-ribbon impellers can significantly strengthen axial circulation and eliminate mixing dead zones through the corotation of inner and outer impellers and the counter-rotation of screw and ribbon components. This composite system is well adapted to high-viscosity pseudoplastic fluids and presents much higher mixing efficiency than single impellers. Zhu et al. studied gas–liquid and solid–liquid mixing systems and found that the double-layer rigid–flexible coupled impeller enhanced turbulence intensity, eliminated dead zones, and improved mixing chaoticity. Among various configurations, the DRF-RDT structure achieved the optimal performance and greatly reduced the mixing time. Wu et al. experimentally validated that the corotating MSKCX dual impeller, composed of a low-speed (10 r/min) MS impeller and a downward-pumping KCX impeller, can significantly accelerate the dissolution and maturation process of hydrophobically associating polymer AP-P4.

Compared with single impellers or other mixing configurations, the upper impeller of the combined impeller system is responsible for liquid transport and sedimentation prevention, while the lower impeller improves bottom mixing and reduces wall adhesion. Within the same maturation time, polymer solutions exhibit higher viscosity, making them more suitable for polymers with poor solubility and high baseline viscosity, thereby achieving satisfactory dissolution and maturation effects. The advantages of combined impellers lie in synergistic flow fields, full-area mixing, and controllable shearing, which significantly enhance rapid dissolution and viscosity retention. This overcomes the limitations of single impellers in polymer dissolution. However, the complex structure, high cost, and limited engineering adaptability of combined impellers have driven the development of new impeller designs.

New Type of Mixer Paddle

Traditional single impellers tend to form large-scale flow dead zones, while split combined impellers suffer from flow field fragmentation and complex structural defects, both of which fail to meet the processing requirements of high-viscosity polymers. Accordingly, researchers have further developed integrated novel stirring impellers. Such impellers can generate a full-tank penetrating axial circulation to eliminate retention dead zones, protect polymer macromolecules through low-shear action, and achieve energy efficiency and convenient maintenance with a single-shaft structure. Zhou et al. found that the novel axial airfoil impeller shortens the dissolution time of AP-P4 to within 45 min while maintaining a solution viscosity of over 90%, realizing high-efficiency dissolution with low viscosity loss. Jia et al. compared three types of novel turbine impellers and found that the offset circular arc impeller optimizes flow field uniformity and stabilizes trailing vortices; the swept-back impeller features low power consumption and mild shear performance, which inhibits local dead zones and molecular degradation, indicating that subtle structural differences of impellers can directly regulate flow field and shear characteristics to adapt to working conditions on demand. Pei et al. designed a six-blade flat impeller with a surrounding ring. After parameter optimization, the impeller effectively inhibits solid-phase sedimentation and significantly improves the uniformity of solid–liquid suspension, which is suitable for solid–liquid mixed dissolution systems. Li et al. , proposed a double-layer disk turbine impeller structure, which effectively eliminates low-speed zones and bubble accumulation and optimizes the uniformity of the tank flow field. Further increasing the blade width and reducing the tank height can improve the stirring performance. Qin et al. developed a novel double-helical anchor impeller, which simultaneously enhances axial and radial flows and expands the high-speed flow area. It improves the mixing uniformity of high-viscosity fluids with reduced power consumption, making up for the performance deficiencies of traditional anchor impellers. Xie et al. verified that the KCXU airfoil up-pumping impeller exhibits higher overall flow velocity and better circulation performance than the traditional XJD impeller, enabling uniform dissolution under diverse working conditions. Jiang et al. developed a novel twin-screw ribbon propeller that has been successfully applied in industrial tests at Daqing Oilfield, reducing the polymer dissolution time from 4 to 2 h and increasing the solution viscosity by 10%.

Stirring speed, as a critical process parameter, exerts a significant regulatory effect on dissolution efficiency and polymer molecular integrity, which requires a balance between dissolution rate and antidegradation performance. Parameter optimization experiments conducted by Li and Guo on HAPAM dissolution show that medium and low stirring speeds are the optimal working conditions, which can shorten the dissolution time while ensuring favorable viscosity retention, and achieve the best comprehensive dissolution effect when matched with turbine impellers. Wang et al. also confirmed that appropriate stirring intensity can improve the fluidity of polymer solutions and accelerate dissolution, while avoiding excessive degradation of polymer molecular chains caused by strong shear, providing a theoretical basis for the refined regulation of stirring parameters, as shown in Figure .

Forced Stretching Technology

To further enhance the dissolution efficiency of polymers, researchers have developed a forced-stretching dissolution device featuring a two-stage grinding plate structure. , The device consists of a moving toothed plate and two stationary toothed plates, where the upper stationary toothed plate cooperates with the moving toothed plate to form the first-stage dissolution zone, while the lower stationary toothed plate cooperates with the moving toothed plate to create the second-stage dissolution zone. Together, these components establish a multistage polymer dissolution channel. After the polymer powder is initially dispersed and forms swollen particles in the dissolution tank, it is transported to the device inlet via a transfer pump. Under the relative motion of the two-stage dissolution zones, the swollen particles are subjected to complex mechanical forces such as shear, friction, and high-speed impacts, causing the outer swollen layers of the particles to be directly peeled and torn apart. This process exposes the inner upswollen portions, which then rapidly meet the water, significantly increasing the specific surface area of contact between the water and the polymer. Additionally, the device incorporates a pressurized permeation mechanism, which actively injects water molecules into the interior of the particles, effectively overcoming the mass transfer resistance during dissolution. This mechanism significantly shortens the diffusion path of water molecules, surpassing the limitations imposed by reliance solely on diffusion rates, and greatly accelerates the polymer dissolution rate.

Polymer preparation on offshore platforms is generally confronted with tough challenges: high salinity of injected water, slow swelling rate of hydrophobically associating polymers, AP-P4 and other oil-displacing agents, as well as molecular chain degradation triggered by intense shear. Conventional stirring and maturation equipment features bulky size and long dissolution cycles, which cannot meet the strict space and load-bearing limits of offshore platforms. Therefore, forced stretching rapid dissolution technology, characterized by compact structure and high dissolution efficiency, has become a research hotspot in offshore polymer preparation in recent years.

Extensive experimental studies have been conducted on the structural optimization and parameter tuning of forced stretching devices. Shu et al. developed a two-stage reverse mass transfer loop stretching device, in which the relative motion between inner and outer rings strengthens the bidirectional stretching of polymer molecular chains, and the integrated precleaning structure effectively avoids channel blockage. Gradient optimization of disc parameters yielded the optimal structural configuration, which substantially improves dissolution efficiency while preserving polymer solution viscosity. Wang et al. established a multistage frequency-convertible adjustable stretching system for flexible regulation of stretching speed and stage number. Experimental results indicated that two-stage stretching at 2000 rpm is optimal for high-molecular-weight AP-P4 polymers, while 1000 rpm delivers the best performance for heavy oil activators. However, excessively high rotational speeds aggravate shear effects, causing irreversible fracture of polymer molecular chains and subsequent viscosity loss. Xu et al. proposed a novel stepped disc dissolution structure adopting a fixed-rotating disc and series double-disc layout, which crushes swollen particles synergistically through combined extrusion, shear and stretching flows. Under a disc gap of 1–2 mm and a rotational speed of 2000–2500 rpm, the dissolution rate of high-concentration mother liquor is significantly enhanced, with a viscosity retention rate of 77.8%. Chen, Liu and Zhou’s team’s − investigated multistage water-permeable modules, three-stage sectional stretching and integrated multistage dissolution systems, and optimized the technical system based on numerical simulations under high-salinity conditions. Liu demonstrated that a 200 μm sectional stretching gap reduces the dissolution time to approximately 25 min, and elevated temperatures further promote mass transfer and dissolution. Meanwhile, Zhou’s team developed a lightweight multistage stretching device with a compact footprint and convenient disassembly, transportation and maintenance, which meets the lightweight equipment demands of offshore platforms.

When translating the above academic findings into practical engineering applications, integrated forced stretching devices exhibit prominent adaptability advantages over traditional stirring processes. The equipment integrates stretching crushing units and mass transfer units without redundant intermediate pipelines; tubular units can be connected in parallel on demand to adjust processing capacity, and the multistage frequency conversion system supports switching between one-stage and two-stage stretching processes. The overall setup eliminates the need for large-volume maturation tanks, drastically cutting occupied space on platforms. Benefiting from multistage crushing by toothed discs and bidirectional stretching of counter-rotating mass transfer rings, swollen polymer agglomerates and intermolecular associative entanglements are fully broken up, producing homogeneous mother liquor free of fish eyes and undissolved particles. This effectively avoids blockages in polymer injection pipelines and near-wellbore formations, and retains the basic viscosity-building performance of polymers even in high-salinity seawater systems. With the same footprint, the processing capacity of forced stretching equipment far exceeds that of traditional tubular dissolution devices, satisfying the demand for continuous preparation of high-concentration polymer mother liquor offshore.

Nevertheless, this technology still has inherent drawbacks that cannot be eliminated in field engineering operation. The precise moving assembly composed of high-speed toothed discs, rotating mass transfer rings and porous fillers generates persistent strong shear. Viscosity attenuation becomes more severe under high-concentration and high-speed operating conditions. To reach the target viscosity for oil displacement, more polymer powder must be injected on site, raising chemical operation costs. Long-term scouring and corrosion from high-salinity brine and polymer particles abrade toothed discs, sealing elements and porous fillers, and tiny disc gaps and porous channels are prone to clogging. Limited by insufficient maintenance personnel on offshore platforms, frequent shutdowns for pipeline flushing and component replacement disrupt continuous polymer preparation. Furthermore, the precision transmission components require high manufacturing costs, and synchronous operation of multiple motors consumes more power than static maturation processes, imposing extra load on the power supply system of offshore platforms. Besides, all mature prototypes are designed for small and medium-sized platforms. Direct scaling up of processing capacity leads to unresolved mechanical problems such as rotor eccentricity and overall unit vibration. In addition, dissolution performance is coupled with multiple parameters including disc gap, rotating speed and stretching stages, requiring repeated sampling and viscosity testing for on-site parameter tuning and resulting in a high operation threshold.

High-Gravity Intensification Technology

Hyper-gravity technology, as a novel process intensification technique, significantly enhances mass transfer and improves mixing efficiency. Its principle is generally believed to involve the disruption of the liquid film in the polymer solution within a rotating bed packing, thereby accelerating the thinning process of the gel layer and achieving rapid, high-quality dissolution of the polymer, as illustrated in Figure . Additionally, due to the compact structure, small footprint, and high dissolution efficiency of hyper-gravity devices, they enable the intensive injection of polymers on offshore oil and gas platforms, offering broad industrial application prospects. −

Shu et al. , developed a hyper-gravity rapid-dissolution device and investigated the effect of the hyper-gravity factor on the dissolution time and solution properties of the hydrophobically associating polymer AP-P4 under simulated oilfield injection water and 45 °C preparation conditions. They found that increasing the hyper-gravity factor significantly reduced polymer dissolution time, with optimal rapid-dissolution performance achieved at a hyper-gravity factor of 1298, decreasing the basic dissolution time from 80 to 40 min while maintaining a viscosity retention of 92.96%. Qi et al. constructed a high-mass-transfer rapid-dissolution system, demonstrating that reducing the porosity of the mass-transfer medium and increasing the mass-transfer factor accelerated bidirectional diffusion of water, refined swelling particles, and expanded the interfacial area, shortening the AP-P4 dissolution time from 80 to 40 min. The optimal conditions were a porosity of 300 μm and a mass-transfer factor of 1031–1298, as shown in Figure .

Fan et al. developed a hyper-gravity rotating packed-bed rapid-dissolution device and studied the effects of rotational speed, packing thickness, liquid-phase flow rate, and temperature on the dissolution performance and solution properties of partially hydrolyzed polyacrylamide under deionized water and room-temperature preparation conditions. They found that a rotational speed of 500 rpm and a packing thickness of 95.2 mm yielded the best dissolution performance, enabling rapid and continuous polymer dissolution. The resulting solution properties were comparable to or better than those obtained with conventional stirred tanks, while the footprint was reduced by 83.93% and energy consumption decreased by 32.84%.

Zhao et al. optimized the key parameters of the high-mass-transfer rapid-dissolution device, identifying the optimal mass-transfer ring structure as 300 μm × 1038 + 200 μm × 1307. Field implementation reduced the polymer dissolution time from 41 to 19 min, and the injection system’s footprint and weight were both reduced by more than 50%. This technology was applied in 2020 at the Bohai S oilfield, providing valuable practical experience for industrial implementation.

The high-gravity rotating packed bed device delivers superior engineering adaptability compared with traditional stirring processes. It enhances mass transfer via high-speed rotating packing to achieve efficient polymer dispersion and dissolution, featuring a compact structure, streamlined workflow and no redundant conveying units. With modular parallel expandability and adjustable operating parameters through a variable-frequency system, it requires no large maturation tanks, significantly saving offshore platform space and bearing capacity. Relying on intense centrifugal fragmentation and micron-scale liquid film renewal, the device fully disperses polymer agglomerates and disentangles molecular chains in high-salinity seawater, stably producing homogeneous polymer mother liquor with low impurities. This effectively alleviates blockage in polymer injection systems and near-wellbore zones while maximizing the polymer viscosity enhancement performance. Boasting higher dissolution efficiency and processing capacity with a smaller footprint than conventional technologies, it supports continuous online preparation of high-concentration mother liquor, perfectly matching the working conditions of offshore oilfields in the Bohai Sea and South China Sea featuring limited space, seawater-based liquid preparation and high-load continuous operation.

High-gravity equipment still has prominent limitations in field polymer dissolution applications. Its rotor mesh packing is easily clogged by seawater sediment, algae and viscous polymer gels, causing increased pressure drop and reduced throughput during long-term operation. Frequent shutdown, disassembly and cleaning are required, interrupting continuous polymer preparation and injection. In high-chloride seawater environments, high-speed shaft seals and bearings are susceptible to corrosion, wear and leakage, leading to short service cycles and high offshore maintenance costs. At medium and low loads, the equipment consumes more energy than conventional stirred tanks with poor economic performance. During high-temperature liquid preparation and ultrahigh-molecular-weight polymer treatment, microshearing of the packing causes polymer viscosity loss, and modified packing materials are prone to erosion and shedding impurities. The system features low fault tolerance in continuous operation; dry powder feeding instability easily produces unqualified mother liquor and material waste. In addition, the equipment requires high initial investment. Scale-up operation tends to induce rotor eccentricity and vibration. Its strongly coupled parameters also make on-site commissioning and maintenance more difficult than traditional stirring systems.

Ultrasonic Intensification Technology

Ultrasonic technology was first applied in the field of petroleum to enhance oil recovery, and later studies found that it can also be used to accelerate polymer dissolution. As an efficient physical intensification means, ultrasonic technology relies on cavitation effect, mechanical vibration, microjet and other effects to significantly regulate the polymer dissolution behavior, becoming an important technical direction to improve polymer rapid dissolution and viscosity retention. ,

The essence of ultrasonic-enhanced polymer dissolution lies in the synergistic coupling of multiphysical fields, and the dynamic evolution of ultrasonic cavitation serves as the core driving force for polymer chain disentanglement and rapid dissolution. Under the alternating ultrasonic pressure field, cavitation micronuclei generate and gradually develop into cavitation bubbles in liquid media. The expansion of cavitation bubbles induces a tensile flow field, while their instantaneous collapse produces multiple extreme physical effects, including localized transient high temperature and high pressure, high-speed microjets, and shock waves. These effects can effectively break polymer powder aggregates, eliminate the swelling inhibition layer on particle surfaces, greatly expand the solid–liquid contact area, and accelerate the penetration of water molecules into polymer molecular coils. , Additionally, they disrupt the microstructures of entangled polymer chains and aggregates, fundamentally facilitating the mixing and dissolution of polymers. Undissolved polymer flocs and microgel particles in the system can act as heterogeneous nucleation sites for cavitation, strengthening local cavitation responses and promoting the dissociation of aggregates, which is consistent with the principle that solid interfaces enhance cavitation accumulation and improve polymer dispersion and dissociation.

As the core internal factors dominating ultrasonic cavitation behavior and polymer dissolution processes, polymer molecular weight and its distribution exert differentiated dissolution effects by regulating the tensile viscosity and cavitation threshold of the solution system. High-molecular-weight polymers markedly increase the cavitation threshold, restraining the initiation and growth of cavitation bubbles. At the initial stage of ultrasonic treatment, only surface polymer flocs are loosened, whereas the dense internal entanglement of molecular chains remains intact, leading to a low dissolution rate and residual gel particles in solution. With the continuous action of ultrasonic cavitation, high-molecular-weight polymers are predominantly degraded via midchain scission. The synchronous variations in molecular weight and molecular weight distribution reduce the tensile viscosity and cavitation threshold of the system, thereby increasing the number and collapse intensity of cavitation bubbles. The intensified microjets and shock waves thoroughly break residual aggregates and ultimately achieve the complete solvation of polymers. For polymers with a narrow molecular weight distribution, ultrasonic cavitation degradation tends to form a bimodal molecular weight distribution system, − as shown in Figure . This feature effectively alleviates molecular chain entanglement while maintaining the basic viscosity required for oil displacement, thereby significantly improving the uniformity of polymer dissolution.

In recent years, relevant studies have been initiated on the effect of ultrasound on polymer dissolution. Melville et al. studied the degradation effect of ultrasound on dilute polymer solutions. They examined the impact of ultrasound on polymers such as poly­(methyl methacrylate) and polystyrene under cavitation and noncavitation conditions, exploring the effect of ultrasonic intensity on degradation. The results showed that, in the presence of cavitation, polymer degradation rate was significantly faster. However, increasing ultrasound intensity only accelerated the degradation rate without changing the limiting molecular weight. Under ultrasound, polymers tended to break directly into molecules rather than generating free radicals, and ultrasound could also initiate monomer polymerization. Price et al. used ultrasound equipment to process polystyrene solutions in toluene. Their experiments showed that the strong shear force generated by cavitation under ultrasound could efficiently break polymer chains and disperse them evenly, resulting in fully dissolved, molecular-weight-controlled polymer solutions. Basedow et al. studied the ultrasonic degradation behavior of polymers in solution, exploring the influence of ultrasonic intensity, frequency, static pressure, solution properties, and polymer structure on the degradation process under different solvents, ultrasound parameters, and polymer systems. The results showed that the degradation rate increased with ultrasonic intensity but first increased and then decreased with frequency. Arnold et al. investigated the enhancement of dissolution by both ultrasound and mega sonic waves. They compared the effects of 20 kHz and 1 MHz frequencies under deionized water and constant temperature conditions. They found that cavitation and resonance cooperatively accelerated dissolution. Low-frequency ultrasound is more beneficial for dissolving substances with low bond energy and low stacking fault energy, while mega sonic resonance significantly enhances dissolution in high-silica and brittle systems, with the most significant dissolution enhancement occurring in small particles. ,

Ultrasonic technology enhances polymer dissolution mass transfer via the transient temperature–pressure field induced by microjets and cavitation. It disrupts powder agglomerates, shortens dissolution time, improves colloidal stability, and enables additive-free clean preparation, with a modular structure suitable for confined spaces such as offshore platforms and intermittent blending operations. Nevertheless, cavitation breaks polymer chains, causing degradation and viscosity loss, particularly in high-salinity environments, making it hard to balance dissolution and viscosity retention. Ultrasonic transducers are prone to scaling, fouling, and cavitation erosion in high-salinity, oily, or solid-laden fluids, resulting in rapid efficiency decay, high maintenance costs, and unsuitability for continuous large-scale production. Thus, ultrasonic dissolution is more appropriate for small-batch, intermittent, low-salinity scenarios instead of large-scale polymer flooding plants and high-temperature high-salinity reservoirs. Chemical Dissolution Technology

Cosolvent Dissolution Technology

Cosolvent rapid dissolution technology, as an external additive, can effectively disrupt the intermolecular aggregation structure of polymers through hydrogen bonding, electrostatic complexation, or molecular encapsulation with polymer chains. Simultaneously, it increases the system’s entropy and strengthens polymer–solvent interactions, thereby thermodynamically driving the polymer chains to transition from an ordered aggregated state to a disordered dispersed state, , as illustrated in Figure . From a kinetic perspective, the cosolvent promotes stepwise wetting, swelling, and chain segment dissociation of the polymer, covering the entire process from solvent diffusion to chain disentanglement. This accelerates dissolution without compromising the polymer’s oil displacement performance and effectively addresses process bottlenecks such as uneven mixing and slow progress during the initial stage of dissolution, meeting the requirements for rapid preparation on offshore platforms.

Rapid dissolution mechanism of host–guest inclusion cosolvents.

For offshore reservoirs characterized by high temperature, high salinity, and high mineralization, systematic studies have been conducted on typical cosolvents such as methyl acetate, formamide, and cyclodextrins, leading to differentiated polymer dissolution strategies. Shu et al. investigated the effect of formamide on AP-P4 dissolution under high-mineralization simulated brine and found that it could disrupt hydrophobic association. An optimal volume fraction of 0.020 shortened the dissolution time of 5000 mg/L AP-P4 by 20% while maintaining 98.24% viscosity retention. Shu et al. and Cram et al. , studied the effects of methyl acetate and dimethylformamide on AP-P4 and general hydrophobically associating polymers in simulated formation water. The results showed that these cosolvents could accelerate chain disentanglement through plasticization and shielding of hydrophobic associations. When the volume fraction of methyl acetate was 0.01%–0.014%, the dissolution time of AP-P4 decreased from 75 to 60 min, indicating that low-volume-fraction cosolvents tailored to polymer type provided optimal conditions, with further improvement when combined with a swelling pretreatment. Li et al. , introduced cyclodextrin, utilizing the compatibility between its cavity and the polymer’s hydrophobic groups to achieve molecular encapsulation (as shown in Figure ), reducing polymer dissolution time from 85 to 30 min. Dai et al. prepared a hydrophobically associating polymer APO and employed β-cyclodextrin host–guest complexation to study the effects of complexation ratio on dissolution and salt tolerance at 25 °C. The optimal β-cyclodextrin to APO molar ratio of 2:1 reduced the dissolution time from 85 to 45 min, while polymer particle size decreased by 19.59% and 11.50% in monovalent and divalent brines, respectively, demonstrating both good salt resistance and rapid solubility. This highlights the efficacy of host–guest complexation modification for hydrophobically associating polymers.

Process of accelerating dissolution by physical means.

Although cosolvent rapid dissolution techniques exhibit significant advantages in offshore polymer flooding, including high dissolution efficiency, retention of polymer performance, and broad applicability, their effectiveness strongly depends on polymer type, molecular structure, and reservoir conditions, making single-component systems insufficient for all offshore scenarios. The synergistic mechanisms of mixed cosolvents, such as the temporal relationship between hydrogen bond disruption and electrostatic shielding, and the coupling between encapsulation and hydrogen-bond competition, remain incompletely understood, limiting precise optimization. Additionally, multivalent ions such as Ca2+ and Mg2+ in high-mineralization offshore environments can interfere with cosolvent–polymer interactions, causing potential dissolution efficiency reduction and viscosity loss in some systems. Future work should integrate molecular simulations and spectroscopic analyses to elucidate polymer–solvent interactions, optimize cosolvent type, dosage, and mixing ratios according to offshore reservoir characteristics, and develop novel temperature- and salt-tolerant cosolvents to accelerate rapid polymer dissolution on offshore platforms and overcome existing technical bottlenecks.

Surfactant Dissolution Technology

In offshore polymer flooding technology, surfactants as an external additive. Their molecules contain both lipophilic and hydrophilic groups, which can be classified based on their ionization properties in aqueous solutions as anionic, cationic, amphoteric, and nonionic types (as shown in Figure ). They can also be categorized by their function, such as wetting agents and dispersants. The core function of surfactants is to adsorb at interfaces and form molecular films, which reorganize into ordered aggregates in solution. This reduces the interfacial tension between the polymer and water, improving the wettability and dispersibility of polymer particles. Additionally, surfactants disrupt intermolecular hydrophobic interactions, accelerating the swelling of polymer particles and the disentanglement of polymer chains. Some salt-tolerant and shear-resistant surfactants are compatible with the high mineralization and high temperature conditions of offshore environments. When combined with polymers, they can enhance both dissolution efficiency and oil displacement effectiveness. − In offshore applications, surfactants are typically anionic, nonionic, or amphoteric. This section focuses on their compatibility with polymers, aiming to achieve rapid polymer dissolution while maintaining polymer oil displacement performance, and adapting to the complex conditions of offshore reservoirs.

In laboratory experiments, Pu et al. developed a functionalized anionic hydrophobic associative polymer. They optimized the synthesis conditions via the single-factor method and prepared polymer materials with excellent rheological and viscoelastic properties. Candau et al. constructed a micellar copolymerization system, and verified that regulating the surfactant concentration and optimizing the micellar distribution of hydrophobic monomers can accelerate the solubilization of hydrophobic monomers. With the optimal micellar system composed of 3% SDS and 0.2% NaCl, the poorly soluble polyacrylamide copolymer containing 1 mol % ethylphenylacrylamide was successfully converted into an easily soluble product. Liu et al. adopted OP-15 and Span 60 as surfactants and synthesized reactive emulsified polyacrylamide with outstanding emulsifying and viscosity-enhancing properties.For practical oil displacement applications in oilfields, Zhao et al. selected the high-efficiency anionic-nonionic amphoteric surfactant S4. When compounded with the optimized polymer C10, the surfactant reduced the polymer dissolution time to less than 30 min, achieved ultralow interfacial tension, and significantly improved crude oil recovery efficiency. Mandal et al. compounded polymers such as PHPA and HPAM with surfactants including SDBS and TX-100. By adjusting salinity and temperature parameters, the compounding system effectively accelerated polymer dissolution, reduced the oil–water interfacial tension, and greatly enhanced crude oil recovery under high-temperature and high-salinity conditions. Wei et al. conducted experiments under the on-site injection condition of 57 °C in Bohai J Oilfield to investigate the effects of surfactant concentration and shear force on polymer dissolution and solution properties. The results showed that the compounded surfactants exerted no adverse effects on polymer dissolution. The viscosity of the compounded solution was basically equivalent to that of the pure polymer solution, while the system possessed ultralow interfacial tension and favorable thermal stability, which is suitable for on-site oilfield injection operations.

Chemical Modification Dissolution Technology

The chemical modification fast-dissolution technology is one of the methods used in polymer flooding. It targets the molecular structure level and employs chemical techniques such as graft copolymerization, group modification, and cross-linking assistance to modify polymer molecular chains in a directional manner. This approach fundamentally disrupts intramolecular and intermolecular hydrogen bond associations, reduces hydrophobic aggregation, and optimizes the balance between hydrophilicity and hydrophobicity, as well as the molecular chain flexibility. This enables enhanced dissolution rates, retention of oil displacement performance, and strengthened temperature and salt resistance. The core mechanisms of this technology include three aspects: (1) grafting strong hydrophilic groups to enhance compatibility with seawater, accelerating solvent penetration and particle wetting; (2) adjusting the density and length of hydrophobic groups to balance the association-enhancing and fast-dissolution needs, avoiding difficulties in disentanglement; (3) introducing temperature- and salt-resistant monomers to reduce crystallinity and improve the structural stability of polymers in complex offshore oil reservoirs, ensuring viscosity and oil recovery efficiency after dissolution, , as shown in Figure . This technology does not require many external auxiliary reagents and is suitable for the large-scale injection needs of offshore operations.

To address the slow dissolution of various polymer types in offshore applications, researchers have investigated four main categories of chemical modification: hydrophilic grafting, hydrophobic association regulation, temperature- and salt-resistant copolymerization, and cross-linking-assisted modification. Liu et al. and Gao et al. , performed molecular modifications on polymers under simulated high-temperature and high-salinity reservoir conditions. They found that introducing nitrogen-containing heterocyclic ionic liquids and long-chain alkyl cationic groups could enhance ionization effects and promote chain extension, resulting in a reduction of polymer dissolution time by over 50%. Low-content functional monomers compatible with reservoir conditions were identified as the optimal modification scheme, with improved dissolution uniformity when combined with conventional solution preparation processes.

Hydrophilic Graft Chemical Modification

In the field of hydrophilic grafting modification, researchers introduce hydrophilic groups to improve the hydration and dissolution capacity of polymers, enabling them to adapt to seawater conditions. Li et al. grafted sodium styrenesulfonate groups onto the hydrophobically associating polymer AP-P4 via free radical graft polymerization, which greatly improved its seawater solubility. The dissolution time in seawater was reduced from 85 to 35 min, while a high viscosity retention rate of 96.5% was achieved. Feng et al. introduced hydrophilic structures through monomer copolymerization and developed a water-based emulsion polymer RJ-03. The polymer can be fully dissolved within only 20 min, exhibiting a far higher dissolution efficiency than conventional polymers, along with low postdissolution viscosity and excellent injectivity.

Hydrophobic Association Regulation Chemical Modification

Research on hydrophobically associating structure regulation mainly optimizes the spatial structure and environmental adaptability of polymers by adjusting monomer ratio, polymer concentration, and the proportion of hydrophobic and hydrophilic groups. Cheng et al. introduced CQ amphoteric monomers and precisely regulated polymer concentration to construct an AM/SSS/CQ amphoteric hydrophobically associating system. At the critical association concentration of 600 mg/L, the system accelerates polymer molecular hydration, strengthens hydrophobic association effects, forms a dense and stable three-dimensional network structure, and effectively improves the dissolution stability of polymers in high-salt environments. Mao et al. adopted an innovative modification scheme combining double-tailed hydrophobic monomers and hydrophilic groups, which refined polymer dissolved aggregates and expanded the water contact interface. This significantly improved the dissolution rate and uniformity of DiPHAM in high-salinity water, with the optimal critical association concentration determined as 490 mg/L. Dou et al. prepared hydrophobically associating polymer P via aqueous solution polymerization and hydrolysis, and clarified the optimal synthesis parameters for complex working conditions. Relying on the synergistic effect of hydrophobic associating structures and salt-resistant functional monomers, the polymer maintains excellent solution stability under high-temperature, high-salt, strong shear, and long-term aging conditions, with overall performance superior to conventional high-molecular-weight salt-resistant polyacrylamide.

Temperature- and Salt-Resistant Copolymer Chemical Modification

Temperature and salt resistant copolymerization serves as a core method to enhance the adaptability of polymers to harsh reservoirs, which optimizes polymer molecular properties through functional group modification. Liu et al. conducted molecular modification of polymers under simulated real high-temperature and high-salt reservoir conditions. The introduction of nitrogen-containing heterocyclic ionic liquids and long-chain alkyl cationic groups significantly enhanced molecular ionization, promoted sufficient extension of molecular chains, and reduced the polymer dissolution time by more than 50%. The combination of low-content functional monomers matching reservoir conditions and conventional preparation processes further improved polymer dissolution uniformity. Firozjaii et al. optimized polymer systems dedicated to high-temperature and high-salt reservoirs and prepared sulfonated polyacrylamide through sulfonation modification. In solutions with salinity lower than 250, the modified polymer accelerates polymer hydration, weakens ionic adsorption, and comprehensively improves the dissolution stability and comprehensive application performance of traditional HPAM under harsh reservoir conditions.

Cross-Link-Assisted Chemical Modification

Cross-linking-assisted modification optimizes polymer dissolution behavior and inhibits molecular aggregation without changing the polymer bulk structure via the synergistic effect of composite additives. Yang et al. developed a composite modification system containing 2%–3% urea and 1%–2% sodium sulfate. Urea destroys intermolecular hydrogen bonds of polymers, while sodium sulfate provides electrostatic shielding. Their synergistic effect effectively alleviates polymer molecular aggregation, reduces the dissolution time by more than 40% while ensuring the viscosity retention rate higher than 90%, achieving a prominent modification effect.

From the perspective of comprehensive engineering application, the four types of polymer chemical modification technologies each present clear on-site adaptability advantages as well as distinctive practical drawbacks. Hydrophilic grafting modification accelerates polymer dissolution rate and seawater compatibility, yet weakens thermal and salt resistance, making it unsuitable for ultrahigh-salinity reservoirs. Hydrophobic association modification improves structural stability and aging resistance, but is highly sensitive to injection concentration and formation salinity, resulting in poor field adaptability. Temperature- and salt-resistant copolymerization achieves remarkable performance enhancement, yet involves complex synthesis and high cost, restricting large-scale application. Cross-linking-assisted modification is simple and cost-effective in reducing polymer aggregation, but only improves macroscopic dissolution behavior with limited improvement in adaptability to extreme reservoirs. Future polymer modification should adopt precise composite strategies, integrating the merits of graft copolymerization, hydrophobic structure regulation and auxiliary additives, to balance intrinsic thermal-salt stability, fast on-site dissolution and low-sensitivity operation, thereby achieving low-cost, wide-operating-range and high-stability performance for field applications.

Physical Modification Dissolution Technology

Physical modification mainly relies on mechanical means such as crushing, grinding, shearing and impact shearing to adjust the molecular structure, reduce the size of polymer particles and gel clusters, increase their contact area with water, disrupt particle agglomeration, reduce insoluble “fish-eye” structures, and improve dispersion uniformity, as shown in Figure . This process enhances water penetration, promotes the hydration and stretching of molecular chains, accelerates swelling, diffusion and mass transfer efficiency, thereby effectively shortening the dissolution period.

Among these methods, wet milling and mild mechanical shearing can accelerate dissolution while controlling polymer chain damage and viscosity loss within a reasonable range, balancing dissolution efficiency and core performance. The combined use of in-line shearing and high-speed shearing further expands the solid–liquid interface, improves flow properties, and enhances the in situ dissolution rate and integrity of polymers. Such physical modification strategies provide reliable technical support for engineering applications such as polymer flooding. ,

Targeting different existing forms of polymers such as dry powder and swollen particles, physical modification technologies optimize the microstructure of polymers through differentiated mechanical strategies, effectively solving field engineering problems including delayed dissolution, cluster agglomeration and uneven dispersion in oilfield polymer preparation. For polymer dry powder, particle size dominates the dissolution efficiency. Experimental results from Shu et al. show that, at a concentration of 5000 mg/L, coarse powder of 20–40 mesh takes 130 min to dissolve, which fails to meet the requirement of rapid field liquid preparation, whereas refined powder above 200 mesh can be fully dissolved within 40 min, significantly improving the maturation efficiency. For swollen polymer particles, Xie et al. adopted high-frequency colloid milling to reduce the dissolution time of polymer mother liquor by nearly 30 min within the commonly applied field concentration range of 10,000–30,000 mg/L, which is suitable for continuous field preparation operations. Tao et al. verified that wet grinding causes minor damage to polymer molecular chains. When the swollen particle size is optimized to 150 μm, the dissolution time is reduced by 6.3%–25.0% while the viscosity loss is controlled within 4.6%–21.7%, achieving a balance between dissolution efficiency and flooding performance.

Furthermore, the forced cutting, particle size regulation and high-speed shearing methods proposed by Feng et al., Wang et al. and Guo et al. can effectively improve the dispersion and dissolution of swollen clusters, addressing common difficulties in field polymer preparation. On the basis of conventional modification for dry powder and swollen particles, physical modification technologies have been further developed to adapt to harsh working conditions such as high temperature, high salinity and rapid offshore construction. Mature modification systems for fast-dissolving, emulsified and intercalated polymers have been gradually established, which broaden the application scope of field technologies and provide feasible technical support for polymer flooding under diverse reservoir conditions.

Overall, differentiated physical modification methods designed for polymer powders, swollen particles, emulsified polymers and intercalated polymers can effectively solve common dissolution defects including slow dissolution rate, aggregate-induced fish-eye formation and poor dispersion uniformity through particle size regulation and mechanical shearing. Compared with chemical modification, physical modification promotes polymer dissolution efficiently while retaining the original dissolution and viscosity properties, which adapts to field polymer preparation in oilfields (see Table ). Nevertheless, these physical modification techniques exhibit distinct limitations in dissolution regulation. Although ultrafine polymer powders significantly accelerate dissolution, they are prone to water absorption and secondary agglomeration during storage and liquid preparation, resulting in uneven dissolution. Mechanical modification of swollen particles achieves efficient and low-damage dissolution only within specific concentration and particle size ranges; inappropriate parameters may break molecular chains and cause severe attenuation of dissolution performance and viscosity. The dissolution modification effects of emulsified and intercalated polymers are highly dependent on emulsification systems and process parameters. They are sensitive to fluctuations in field water quality and temperature, leading to poor dissolution stability under extreme reservoir conditions. Future studies should focus on precise low-dissolution-damage regulation of polymer dissolution processes and establish standardized modification processes for different polymer forms, so as to improve the dissolution efficiency and stability of polymers under complex reservoir conditions.

Composite Rapid Dissolution Technology

Based on the practical experience of offshore field operations, single rapid dissolution technology can only partially optimize the polymer dissolution performance with limited improvements in reducing the volume of injection systems and platform load. It fails to achieve comprehensive modification effects and cannot meet the core requirements of short-term injection operations on offshore platforms, which becomes a critical bottleneck restricting the application of polymer flooding for enhanced oil recovery. In this context, composite rapid dissolution technology has emerged as a feasible solution. It is defined as a synergistic technical system that integrates two or more dissolution mechanisms or employs a single mechanism to simultaneously shorten the duration of multiple dissolution stages of polymers. ,

From the perspective of factors influencing polymer dissolution speed, individual technologies such as increasing solvent temperature, enhancing solution concentration, high-speed dispersion mixing, reducing polymer swelling particle size, and optimizing stirring and mixing processes can be combined in pairs or through multieffect combinations to form this composite system. By organically integrating multiple single rapid dissolution methods and leveraging the synergistic effects between different technologies, this technology not only significantly shortens the polymer dissolution time but also effectively avoids the inherent flaws of single technologies. It provides a new development pathway for adapting to the complex operational conditions of offshore oilfields and optimizing polymer dissolution performance, all while maximizing the retention of core polymer properties such as viscosity enhancement, temperature and salt resistance, and oil displacement. This represents a key research direction for overcoming the technological bottlenecks in offshore polymer flooding applications.

Conventional Composite Dissolution Technology

Current research on the rapid dissolution and injection optimization of polymers mainly focuses on three aspects: process equipment, polymer modification, and auxiliary agent systems.

In terms of equipment optimization, researchers adopt composite mechanical structure designs to achieve efficient polymer dissolution, equipment miniaturization, and stable operation. Zheng et al. , developed a curing-free rapid polymer preparation and injection system and a composite dissolution device with rapid dissolution, water spray, and cleaning functions. The former integrates forced stretching and high-gravity mass transfer technologies, reducing the equipment volume and weight by more than 80% and significantly shortening the preparation time of polymer mother liquor, while the latter can efficiently dissolve hydrophobically associating polymers with effective antiblocking and quality-stabilizing performance. Chen et al. proposed a flexibly assembled multiunit parallel polymer dissolution and dilution device adaptable to various construction conditions. Ye et al. designed a dual-stage stretching dissolution device with a cleaning structure, which enhances dissolution efficiency and equipment stability through multistage grinding and high-speed operation. Vermolen et al. combined high-gravity technology, forced stretching, and chemical cosolvent techniques to optimize the liquid preparation structure and reduce polymer particle size, realizing simultaneous dissolution efficiency improvement and equipment miniaturization. In terms of polymer modification, Xiong et al. compounded amphiphilic graphene oxide (SAG) with hydrophobically associating polymers (HMHPAM). The results verified that 5–50 mg/L SAG can enhance the viscoelasticity of the system via hydrogen bonding and hydrophobic association interactions, providing a new modification approach for rapid polymer dissolution. For auxiliary agent system optimization, relevant studies focus on parameter regulation of alkali-surfactant–polymer (ASP) ternary composite flooding systems to improve the dissolution and rheological properties of HPAM under high-temperature and high-salinity reservoir conditions. Guo and Chang et al. , found that weak alkali sodium carbonate is suitable for low-salinity formations, which can effectively improve the hydration and stretching behavior of polymers, reduce adsorption loss, and enhance solution stability. Li further optimized the working parameters and determined the optimal coupling system consisting of 1.2 wt % sodium carbonate and 0.3 wt % heavy alkyl benzenesulfonate, which significantly improves the dispersion uniformity and dissolution stability of HPAM.

Nano Composite Dissolution Technology

To further optimize the dissolution performance and application efficacy of polymer flooding processes, research has gradually focused on the development and characterization of polymer nanocomposites. The classification structure of nanomaterials is shown in Figure .

Leveraging the high specific surface area of nanoparticles, hydrogen bonding and electrostatic repulsion disrupt polymer aggregation, increasing the contact area between the polymer and solvent, thereby enhancing mass transfer efficiency. This significantly shortens the dissolution cycle and improves solution dispersion uniformity. The ion shielding effect and free radical scavenging functionality effectively mitigate the impact of high-temperature and high-salinity environments on polymers, reducing molecular chain degradation and viscosity loss, thus ensuring long-term system stability. Additionally, these systems can form synergistic structures with surfactants, alkalis, or adapt to coaxial parallel structures, which not only grind to reduce particle size and accelerate the dissolution rate but also shrink the injection system volume, making them suitable for complex conditions like offshore oilfields.

Liu et al. compared the dissolution regulation and enhancement effects of nanoparticle F80 and nanosheet GO in a polymer/surfactant binary composite system. Their results showed that 0.10% F80 significantly optimized the polymer composite dissolution rate via ion–dipole interaction with cations, increasing system viscosity by 1.4 times, maintaining low interfacial tension, and facilitating polymer dissolution. Druetta et al. constructed a nanoparticle–polymer composite system, confirming that controlling the polymer architecture and optimizing nanoparticle size and concentration accelerated polymer dispersion and swelling, reducing molecular aggregation. The optimal conditions were a 16-arm branched polymer combined with 5 nm nanoparticles at concentrations between 0.05 and 0.2. Zhang et al. studied HPAM layered silicate nanocomposites, which significantly improved the dispersion uniformity of polymers during the dissolution process, reducing aggregation during dissolution and optimizing the dissolution effect. Bai synthesized amphiphilic hydrophobic associating polymer/montmorillonite nanocomposites, which enhanced the polymer’s dissolution stability by optimizing intermolecular interactions, outperforming pure copolymers in comprehensive performance. Rezk et al. incorporated nanoparticles such as SiO2 and TiO2 into the polymer matrix, strengthening hydrogen bonding and steric hindrance effects, which accelerated polymer chain hydration, improved solution dispersion uniformity, and enhanced system stability. This approach effectively improved HPAM’s dissolution and rheological stability under high-temperature, high-salinity conditions, with the optimal conditions being nanoparticle sizes ranging from 1 to 100 nm and a strong interfacial interaction factor. Feng et al. modified the surface of nanoclay using silane coupling agents and cation exchange, combined with physical methods such as ultrasonic dispersion, melt shearing, or solution stirring. The modified nanoclay was then blended with the polymer, effectively addressing issues of uneven dispersion and low dissolution efficiency inherent in single technologies. Sharma et al. constructed a ternary system with HPAM as the polymer, SDS, and Triton X-100 as surfactants, along with nanoparticles like SiO2 and ZrO2. After ultrasonic dispersion, this system significantly shortened polymer dissolution time.

In summary, this composite dissolution technology integrates mechanical structure optimization, ASP ternary system synergism and nanomodification. Supported by miniaturized and modular equipment, it effectively solves the problems of conventional polymers such as uneven dissolution, slow ripening and poor rheological stability under high-temperature and high-salinity conditions, achieving better performance than single technologies. Nanomodification is environmentally friendly, requiring no large amounts of organic solvents. It improves polymer dispersion through interfacial interactions and steric hindrance, enhancing dissolution efficiency, thermal stability and salt tolerance while reducing solvent volatilization, residue and formation damage.

However, its applicability under offshore operating conditions is still limited by the compatibility of nanoparticles. Field experience shows that for high-temperature and high-salinity reservoirs, efficient offshore polymer dissolution can be realized by adopting composite systems of nanoparticles and modified nanoclays, supporting water quality pretreatment and self-cleaning skid-mounted units, strengthening pipeline corrosion protection and equipment maintenance, and precisely controlling chemical dosage and operating parameters. Future efforts will focus on developing low-cost, highly salt-tolerant modified materials to narrow the performance gap between laboratory and field applications, further improving the stability, economic efficiency and reservoir adaptability of offshore polymer composite dissolution processes.

Conclusions and Prospects

Conclusions

Rapid polymer dissolution technology for offshore enhanced oil recovery (EOR) is a key solution to reconcile the space constraints of polymer injection systems on offshore platforms and to promote the large-scale application of polymer flooding. This study systematically reviews the research progress of four major categories of rapid dissolution technologies: physical dissolution, chemical dissolution, modified polymers, and composite dissolution, summarizing the core findings and conclusions of researchers in each field. Physical dissolution technologies have achieved large-scale application, significantly reducing dissolution time and the volume of injection systems. Chemical dissolution techniques enhance dissolution efficiency through additives, addressing the viscosity loss associated with hydrophobically associating polymers. Modified polymer technologies improve polymer water solubility fundamentally from both physical and chemical perspectives, balancing rapid dissolution with temperature and salinity resistance.

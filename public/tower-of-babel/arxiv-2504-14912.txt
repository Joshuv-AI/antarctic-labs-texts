Dynamics of pulsating swarmalators on a ring
Samali Ghosh, Kevin O’Keeffe, Gourab Kumar Sar, Dibakar Ghosh

Abstract
(Received XX MONTH XX; accepted XX MONTH XX; published XX MONTH XX) We study a simple one-dimensional model of swarmalators, a generalization of phase oscillators that swarm around in space as well as synchronize internal oscillations in time. Previous studies of the model focused on Kuramoto-type couplings, where the phase interactions are governed by phase differences . Here we consider Winfree-type coupling, where the interactions are multiplicative , determined by the product of a phase response function R ⁡ ( θ ) R(\theta) and phase pulse function P ⁡ ( θ ) P(\theta) . This more general interaction (from which the Kuramoto phase differences emerge after averaging) produces rich physics: six long-term modes of organization are found, which we characterize numerically and analytically. In most – if not all – of the studies above, the phase dynamics have ‘Kuramoto form’, by which we mean the phase dynamics
\dot{\theta}_{i}

II
Model

The original swarmalator model concerned particles free to move in the plane
[
21
]
. It is, however, rather difficult to analyze

[
39
]
, so instead researchers have turned to using the simpler
one dimensional swarmalator model

[
23
]
where the swarmalators movements are confined to a 1d ring
x_{i}\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
. This model is

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{i}

\displaystyle=v_{i}+\dfrac{J}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sin{(x_{j}-x_{i})}\cos{(\theta_{j}-\theta_{i})},

(1)

\displaystyle\dot{\theta}_{i}

\displaystyle=\omega_{i}+\dfrac{K}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sin(\theta_{j}-\theta_{i})\cos{(x_{j}-x_{i})},

(2)

where
(v_{i},\omega_{i})
are the natural velocities and frequencies and
(J,K)
are the associated coupling constants. The space dynamics in Eq.
1
describe the phase-dependent aggregation, while the phase dynamics in Eq.
2
capture position-dependent synchronization. This symmetry makes the model one of the few models of mobile oscillators that is tractable
[
40
,
41
,
42
]
. It has enabled exact analyses of swarmalators with distributed coupling
[
43
,
25
]
, phase frustration
[
44
]
, random pinning
[
24
,
45
,
46
]
, periodic forcing
[
47
]
, muilti-body interactions
[
48
]
, noise
[
27
]
, and low range coupling
[
49
]
.

Here we swap the Kuramoto coupling for Winfree coupling
\sin(\theta_{j}-\theta_{i})\rightarrow R(\theta_{i})P(\theta_{j})
. We leave the spatial dynamics the same. The model we thus study is

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{i}

\displaystyle=v_{i}+\dfrac{J}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sin{(x_{j}-x_{i})}\cos{(\theta_{j}-\theta_{i})},

(3)

\displaystyle\dot{\theta}_{i}

\displaystyle=\omega_{i}+\dfrac{K}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}R(\theta_{i})P(\theta_{j})\cos{(x_{j}-x_{i})},

(4)

for
i=1,\cdots,N
, where
N>>1
. For simplicity, we work with swarmalators that have zero frequencies and velocities, i.e.,
\omega_{i}=0=v_{i}
for all
i
. Following previous studies
[
50
]
, we make the following choices for the pulse and response functions

\displaystyle P(\theta)

\displaystyle=1+\cos\theta,

(5)

\displaystyle R(\theta)

\displaystyle=-\sin\theta.

(6)

Next, we define the order parameters,

\displaystyle R_{x(\theta)}

\displaystyle=r_{x(\theta)}e^{\iota\psi_{x(\theta)}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{\iota x_{j}(\theta_{j})},\ \iota=\sqrt{-1}

(7)

where
R_{x(\theta)}
measures the amount of synchrony among swarmalators’ positions and their phases.
r_{x}
and
r_{\theta}
lie between
0
and
1
by definition, indicating the overall synchrony among the swarmalators’ positions and their phases, respectively.
\psi_{x}
and
\psi_{\theta}
are the mean position and phase of the overall population. We also define

\displaystyle W_{\pm}

\displaystyle=S_{\pm}e^{\iota\phi\pm}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{\iota(x_{j}\pm\theta_{j})},

(8)

where
W_{\pm}
measures the correlation between swarmalators’ phases
\theta_{j}
and their spatial angle
x_{j}
. We take the maximum of
S_{\pm}
and define
S=\max\{S_{+},S_{-}\}
. Another order parameter denoted by
R_{2x(\theta)}
is defined as,

\displaystyle R_{2x(\theta)}=r_{2x(\theta)}e^{\iota\psi_{2x(\theta)}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{2\iota x_{j}(\theta_{j})},

(9)

which is beneficial to investigate the antiphase synchrony, where a phase difference of
\pi
is noticed among the swarmalators’ phases. When antiphase synchrony is observed, then
r_{2x(\theta)}=1
but
r_{x(\theta)}\neq 1
.

These order parameters appear naturally in the equations of motion. Converting the trigonometric functions into complex exponentials, we can rewrite Eqs. (
3
) and (
4
) as,

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{i}=

\displaystyle\dfrac{J}{2}\bigg[S_{+}\sin(\phi_{+}-(x_{i}+\theta_{i}))+S_{-}\sin(\phi_{-}-(x_{i}-\theta_{i}))\bigg],

(10)

\displaystyle\dot{\theta}_{i}=

\displaystyle-\dfrac{K}{4}\bigg[S_{+}\sin{(\phi_{+}-(x_{i}-\theta_{i}))}-S_{+}\sin{(\phi_{+}-(x_{i}+\theta_{i}))}

\displaystyle+S_{-}\sin{(\phi_{-}-(x_{i}-\theta_{i}))}-S_{-}\sin{(\phi_{-}-(x_{i}+\theta_{i}))}\bigg]

\displaystyle-\dfrac{K}{2}r_{x}\bigg[\sin{(\psi_{x}-(x_{i}-\theta_{i}))}-\sin{(\psi_{x}-(x_{i}+\theta_{i}))}\bigg].

(11)

We define
\xi_{i}=x_{i}+\theta_{i}
and
\eta_{i}=x_{i}-\theta_{i}
which leads to

\displaystyle\dot{\xi}_{i}=

\displaystyle-\dfrac{K}{2}r_{x}\big(\sin(\psi_{x}-\eta_{i})-\sin(\psi_{x}-\xi_{i})\big)+\dfrac{1}{N}\left(\dfrac{J}{2}+\dfrac{K}{4}\right)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sin(\xi_{j}-\xi_{i})+\dfrac{1}{N}\left(\dfrac{J}{2}-\dfrac{K}{4}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sin(\eta_{j}-\eta_{i})

\displaystyle-\dfrac{K}{4N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left(\sin(\xi_{j}-\eta_{i})-\sin(\eta_{j}-\xi_{i})\right),

(12)

\displaystyle\dot{\eta}_{i}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{K}{2}r_{x}\big(\sin(\psi_{x}-\eta_{i})-\sin(\psi_{x}-\xi_{i})\big)+\dfrac{1}{N}\left(\dfrac{J}{2}+\dfrac{K}{4}\right)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sin(\eta_{j}-\eta_{i})+\dfrac{1}{N}\left(\dfrac{J}{2}-\dfrac{K}{4}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sin(\xi_{j}-\xi_{i})

\displaystyle+\dfrac{K}{4N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left(\sin(\xi_{j}-\eta_{i})-\sin(\eta_{j}-\xi_{i})\right).

(13)

Simplifying Eq. (
12
) and Eq. (
13
), we get,

\displaystyle\dot{\xi}_{i}

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{K}{2}r_{x}\big(\sin(\psi_{x}-\eta_{i})-\sin(\psi_{x}-\xi_{i})\big)+S_{+}\left(\dfrac{J}{2}+\dfrac{K}{4}\right)

\displaystyle\sin(\phi_{+}-\xi_{i})+S_{-}\left(\dfrac{J}{2}-\dfrac{K}{4}\right)\sin(\phi_{-}-\eta_{i})

\displaystyle-\dfrac{K}{4}\left(S_{+}\sin(\phi_{+}-\eta_{i})-S_{-}\sin(\phi_{-}-\xi_{i})\right),

(14)

\displaystyle\dot{\eta}_{i}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{K}{2}r_{x}\big(\sin(\psi_{x}-\eta_{i})-\sin(\psi_{x}-\xi_{i})\big)+S_{-}\left(\dfrac{J}{2}+\dfrac{K}{4}\right)

\displaystyle\sin(\phi_{-}-\eta_{i})+S_{+}\left(\dfrac{J}{2}-\dfrac{K}{4}\right)\sin(\phi_{+}-\xi_{i})

\displaystyle+\dfrac{K}{4}\left(S_{+}\sin(\phi_{+}-\eta_{i})-S_{-}\sin(\phi_{-}-\xi_{i})\right),

(15)

which contain
S_{\pm}
,
r_{x(\theta)}
etc., as claimed.

III.1
Collective states

Next, we explore how our model behaves as the two parameters
(J,K)
are varied. We drew the phases and positions of
N=10^{3}
swarmalators uniformly at random from
[-\pi,\pi]

1
by varying
J
and
K

J
and
K
as shown in Fig.
2
.

Static async
: The swarmalators are distributed on the ring in such a way that their positions are static, but their phases evolve asynchronously over time, as shown in Fig.
1
(a). This state exists for negative
J
regardless of the value of
K
. Notice Fig.
2
(a)-(d),
S_{\pm}=0
and
r_{2x(\theta)}=0
for this state.

Static
\pi
: The swarmalators synchronize into two distinct groups, one group is fully sycnhronized with
(x^{*},\theta^{*})
and the other is synchronized exactly
\pi
units away
(x^{*}+\pi,\theta^{*}+\pi)
(see Fig.
1
(d)). We found that this state exists for
J,K>0
. We observe
S_{\pm}=1
and
r_{2x(\theta)}=1
for this state (see Fig.
2
(a)-(d)).

Static phase wave
: Swarmalators form a phase wave that wraps around the circular domain once
x_{i}\pm\theta_{i}+C
(notice Fig.
1
(e)). The wave can go both clockwise and counterclockwise. In the coordinates
(\xi_{i},\eta_{i})
,
\xi_{i}
is splayed
\left(\xi_{i}=\frac{2\pi i}{N}\right)
and
\eta_{i}
is locked
(\eta_{i}=C)
or vice versa. The order parameters are
(S_{+},S_{-})=(1,0)
or vice versa, depending on whether the phase gradient of the rainbow is clockwise or anticlockwise, and
r_{2x(\theta)}\sim 0
for this state (look Fig.
2
(a)-(d)). This state exists only in the special case where the phase dynamics are turned off,
K=0
(and
J>0
).

\theta
antiphase sync
: The swarmalators are uniformly distributed along the run. Despite being evenly distributed in space, they organize their phase by separating into two distinct clusters, maintaining a phase difference of
\pi
. Figure
1
(c) provides a snapshot of this state at a specific time. This state emerges in the region where
J=0
for any positive value of
K
. Figure
2
(a)-(d) illustrate the order parameters for this state where,
S_{\pm}\approx 0.64
,
r_{2x}
is on the order of
10^{-2}
, and
r_{2\theta}\sim 1
.

x
anti-phase sync
: We observe that the swarmalators positionally form two clusters at
\pi
difference and their phases are not completely disordered; rather, they are clustered around two points at
\pi
difference. Figure
1
(f) best illustrates this state. We observe this state in regions
J>0
and
K<0
. For this state,
S_{\pm}\approx 0.8\pm 0.05
,
\dfrac{S_{+}}{S_{-}}\sim 1
,
r_{2x}=1
, and
r_{2\theta}\approx 0.5
which are depicted in Fig.
2
(a)-(d).

Intermediate mixed state
: This state is an intermediate state between the static async state and the
\theta
antiphase sync state. Here, the swarmalators are randomly distributed in terms of position (Fig.
1
(b)). However, when examining their internal dynamics, they are neither fully desynchronized nor organized into distinct clusters with a phase difference of
\pi
. Look at Fig.
2
(a)-(d), where
S_{\pm}=0
,
r_{2x}
in the order of
10^{-2}
, and
0<r_{2\theta}<1
in this state.

III.2
Analysis

III.2.1
Stability of the static
\pi
state

We analyze the stability of this state by simplifying around the equilibrium point in
(\xi,\eta)
space. Here, the swarmalators maintain a phase difference of
\pi
in both position and phase. Therefore,
r_{x}=0
. For this, we first calculate the eigenvalues of the Jacobian matrix M,

\displaystyle M=\begin{bmatrix}Z_{\xi}&Z_{\eta}\\
N_{\xi}&N_{\eta}\end{bmatrix}_{2N\times 2N}

(16)

where
(Z_{\xi})_{ij}=\dfrac{\partial{\dot{\xi}_{i}}}{\partial{\xi_{j}}}
,
(N_{\eta})_{ij}=\dfrac{\partial{\dot{\eta}_{i}}}{\partial{\eta_{j}}}
,
(Z_{\eta})_{ij}=\dfrac{\partial{\dot{\xi}_{i}}}{\partial{\eta_{j}}}
, and,
(N_{\xi})_{ij}=\dfrac{\partial{\dot{\eta}_{i}}}{\partial{\xi_{j}}}
.
We choose
\psi=0
without loss of generality.

The equilibrium points are
(\xi_{i},\eta_{i})=(c_{1}+\pi,c_{2}+\pi)
. Hence, we can write,

\displaystyle(Z_{\xi})_{ij}=\left(\dfrac{J}{2}+\dfrac{K}{4}\right)\textbf{A}_{0}-\dfrac{K}{4}\cos(c_{1}-c_{2})\textbf{A}_{1},

(17)

\displaystyle(N_{\eta})_{ij}=\left(\dfrac{J}{2}+\dfrac{K}{4}\right)\textbf{A}_{0}-\dfrac{K}{4}\cos(c_{1}-c_{2})\textbf{A}_{1},

(18)

\displaystyle(Z_{\eta})_{ij}=\left(\dfrac{J}{2}-\dfrac{K}{4}\right)\textbf{A}_{0}+\dfrac{K}{4}\cos(c_{1}-c_{2})\textbf{A}_{1},

(19)

\displaystyle(N_{\xi})_{ij}=\left(\dfrac{J}{2}-\dfrac{K}{4}\right)\textbf{A}_{0}+\dfrac{K}{4}\cos(c_{1}-c_{2})\textbf{A}_{1},

(20)

where

\displaystyle\textbf{A}_{0}=\begin{bmatrix}-\frac{N-1}{N}&\frac{1}{N}&...&\frac{1}{N}\\
\\
\frac{1}{N}&-\frac{N-1}{N}&...&\frac{1}{N}\\
...&...&...&...\\
\frac{1}{N}&...&...&-\frac{N-1}{N}\end{bmatrix}_{N\times N},\

(21)

and

\displaystyle\textbf{A}_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{N+1}{N}&\frac{1}{N}&...&\frac{1}{N}\\
\\
\frac{1}{N}&\frac{N+1}{N}&...&\frac{1}{N}\\
...&...&...&...\\
\frac{1}{N}&...&...&\frac{N+1}{N}\end{bmatrix}_{N\times N}.

(22)

For this state,
c_{1}-c_{2}=2\theta_{i}=0,2\pi,4\pi
. Note that,
M_{s\pi}
has a dimension of
2N
since there are two state variables
(\xi,\theta)
for each of the swarmalators and dim
(A_{0})
=dim
(A_{1})=N
as both of them are the sub-blocks of
M
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle M_{s\pi}=\begin{bmatrix}(\frac{J}{2}+\frac{K}{4})\textbf{A}_{0}-\frac{K}{4}\textbf{A}_{1}&~(\frac{J}{2}-\frac{K}{4})\textbf{A}_{0}+\frac{K}{4}\textbf{A}_{1}\\
\\
(\frac{J}{2}-\frac{K}{4})\textbf{A}_{0}+\frac{K}{4}\textbf{A}_{1}&~(\frac{J}{2}+\frac{K}{4})\textbf{A}_{0}-\frac{K}{4}\textbf{A}_{1}\end{bmatrix}.

(23)

One of the eigenvalues of the matrix
\textbf{A}_{0}
is
\hat{\lambda}=0
due to its rotational symmetry and
(N-1)\hat{\lambda}=-1
. Similarly, for
\textbf{A}_{1}
, the eigenvalues are
0
and
1
with multiplicity
1
and
(N-1)
.

For any symmetric matrix

E=\begin{pmatrix}C&D\\
D&C\end{pmatrix}
,
\det(E)=\det(C+D)\det(C-D)
.

This implies
\det(M_{s\pi})=\det(J\textbf{A}_{0})\det(\frac{K}{2}(\textbf{A}_{0}-\textbf{A}_{1}))=\det(J\textbf{A}_{0})\det(-K\textbf{I})
.

As a result the eigenvalues of
M_{s\pi}
become

\displaystyle\lambda=\begin{cases}0,\hskip 30.0pt\text{multiplicity }1\\
-J,\hskip 22.0pt\text{multiplicity }N-1\\
-K,\hskip 19.0pt\text{multiplicity }N\end{cases}

(24)

It concludes that the static
\pi
state will be stable if
J>0
and
K>0
, which matches our numerics.

III.2.2
Stability of the static phase wave state

The calculation for the stability analysis of this state is the same as before. Firstly, we linearize around the fixed points in the
(\xi,\eta)
plane and then find the eigenvalues of the Jacobian matrix
M_{spw}
. Here,
x_{i}=2\pi i/N=\theta_{i}
(for
(S_{+},S_{-})=(1,0)
type of phase wave, for
x_{i}=-\theta_{i}
, the analysis remains similar). Importantly, this state only exists when
K=0
; it is not a valid fixed point for
K\neq 0
. So we set
K=0
for our analysis, and seek a stability region defined by the parameter
J
(numerics suggest this is
J>0
).

The matrix
M_{spw}
is written as

\displaystyle M_{spw}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}Z_{\xi}&Z_{\eta}\\
N_{\eta}&N_{\eta}\end{bmatrix}_{2N\times 2N}

(25)

Now,
C_{ij}=\cos\dfrac{2\pi(i-j)}{N},S_{ij}=\sin\dfrac{2\pi(i-j)}{N},\beta_{ij}=C_{ij}+\iota S_{ij},C_{i}=\cos\dfrac{2\pi i}{N},S_{i}=\sin\dfrac{2\pi i}{N}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{N}C_{ij}=\dfrac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}C_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{N}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}C_{k}-C_{0}\right)=-\dfrac{C_{0}}{N},

(26)

where
C_{0}=C_{0,0}=1
.
The elements of the matrix
M_{spw}
are as follows,

\displaystyle(Z_{\xi})_{ii}=

\displaystyle-\dfrac{K}{2}r_{x}\cos\xi_{i}-\dfrac{1}{N}\left(\dfrac{J}{2}+\dfrac{K}{4}\right)\sum_{j\neq i}\cos(\xi_{j}-\xi_{i})

\displaystyle-\dfrac{K}{4N}\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{N}\cos(\eta_{j}-\xi_{i})+\cos(\eta_{i}-\xi_{i})\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\dfrac{1}{N}\Big(\dfrac{J}{2}+\dfrac{K}{4}\Big)\sum_{j}C_{ij}-C_{i}\Big(\dfrac{K(N+1)}{4N}-\frac{K}{2}r_{x}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\dfrac{J}{2}+\dfrac{K}{4}\Big)\dfrac{C_{0}}{N}-\dfrac{K}{4}\dfrac{(N+1)C_{i}}{N}-\dfrac{K}{2}r_{x}C_{i},

\displaystyle(Z_{\xi})_{ij}=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{N}\Big(\dfrac{J}{2}+\dfrac{K}{4}\Big)\cos(\xi_{j}-\xi_{i})-\dfrac{K}{4N}\cos(\xi_{j}-\eta_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\dfrac{J}{2}+\dfrac{K}{4}\Big)\dfrac{C_{ij}}{N}-\dfrac{K}{4}\dfrac{C_{i}}{N}

implies,

\displaystyle Z_{\xi}=\Big(\dfrac{J}{2}+\dfrac{K}{4}\Big)\textbf{A}_{2}-\dfrac{K}{4}C_{i}\textbf{A}_{1}-\dfrac{K}{2}r_{x}C_{i}\textbf{I}.

(27)

By similar calculation, we obtain the other elements as

Z_{\eta}=(\frac{J}{2}-\frac{K}{4})\textbf{A}_{0}+\frac{K}{4}C_{i}A_{3}+\frac{K}{2}r_{x}\textbf{I}
,

N_{\xi}=(\frac{J}{2}-\frac{K}{4})\textbf{A}_{2}+\frac{K}{4}C_{i}\textbf{A}_{1}+\frac{K}{2}r_{x}C_{i}\textbf{I}
,
and
N_{\eta}=(\frac{J}{2}+\frac{K}{4})\textbf{A}_{0}-\frac{K}{4}C_{i}\textbf{A}_{3}-\frac{K}{2}r_{x}\textbf{I}
,
where
A_{2}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{C_{0}}{N}&\frac{C_{ij}}{N}&...&\frac{C_{ij}}{N}\\
\frac{C_{ij}}{N}&\frac{C_{0}}{N}&...&\frac{C_{ij}}{N}\\
...&...&\frac{C_{0}}{N}&...\\
\frac{C_{ij}}{N}&...&...&\frac{C_{ij}}{N}\end{bmatrix}
and
A_{3}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{N}&\frac{1}{N}&...&\frac{1}{N}\\
\frac{1}{N}&\frac{1}{N}&...&\frac{1}{N}\\
...&...&...&...\\
\frac{1}{N}&...&...&\frac{1}{N}\end{bmatrix}
.

Hence, we can write,

\displaystyle M_{spw}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}J_{+}\textbf{A}_{2}&J_{-}\textbf{A}_{0}\\
J_{-}\textbf{A}_{2}&J_{+}\textbf{A}_{0}\end{bmatrix}+\frac{K}{4}C_{i}\begin{bmatrix}-\textbf{A}_{1}&\textbf{A}_{3}\\
\textbf{A}_{1}&-\textbf{A}_{3}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle+\frac{K}{2}r_{x}\begin{bmatrix}-C_{i}&\textbf{I}\\
C_{i}&-\textbf{I}\end{bmatrix},

(28)

where
J_{\pm}=\frac{J}{2}\pm\frac{K}{4}
.
The matrix has the same structure as
M_{spw}
for the regular 1d swarmalator model (with Kuramoto type coupling), whose eigenvalues have been computed exactly
[
23
]
. A direct extension of this analysis provides the eigenvalues for this case. Table

summarizes these.

III.2.3
Stability of the static async state

We can analyze the stability of this state by going to the continuum limit. Let
\rho(x,\theta,t)
denote the fraction of swarmalators with positions/phases between
x,x+dx
and
\theta,\theta+d\theta
at time
t
. This density obeys the continuity equation

\dot{\rho}+\nabla(v\rho)=0,

(29)

where the velocity
v=(v_{x},v_{\theta})
is given by

\displaystyle v_{x}

\displaystyle=J\int\sin(x^{\prime}-x)\cos(\theta^{\prime}-\theta)\rho(x^{\prime},\theta^{\prime},t)\;dx^{\prime}d\theta^{\prime},

\displaystyle v_{\theta}

\displaystyle=K\int\sin\theta(1+\cos\theta^{\prime})\cos(x^{\prime}-x)\rho(x^{\prime},\theta^{\prime},t)\;dx^{\prime}d\theta^{\prime}.

(30)

In the limit
N\rightarrow\infty
, the density in the static async state is
\rho_{0}=1/(4\pi^{2})

[
49
]
. We consider a small perturbation around this state such that

\displaystyle\rho=\rho_{0}+\epsilon\gamma(x,\theta,t).

(31)

The normalization condition requires
\int\rho(x,\theta,t)dxd\theta=1
and therefore we have,

\displaystyle\int\gamma(x,\theta,t)\;dxd\theta=0.

(32)

The velocity can be written as,

\displaystyle v=v_{0}+\epsilon v_{1},

(33)

where
v_{0}
is the velocity in the static async state, which is zero, and we have
v=\epsilon v_{1}
, where
v_{1}(v_{1}^{x},v_{1}^{\theta})
is the perturbed velocity and is given by,

\displaystyle v_{1}^{x}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{J}{2}\text{Im}\left(W_{+}^{1}e^{-\iota(x+\theta)}+W_{-}^{1}e^{-\iota(x-\theta)}\right),

(34)

\displaystyle v_{1}^{\theta}

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{K}{4}\text{Im}\bigg(W_{+}^{1}e^{-\iota(x-\theta)}-W_{+}^{1}e^{-\iota(x+\theta)}+W_{-}^{1}e^{-\iota(x-\theta)}

\displaystyle-W_{-}^{1}e^{-\iota(x+\theta)}+2R_{x}^{1}e^{-\iota(x-\theta)}-2R_{x}^{1}e^{-\iota(x+\theta)}\bigg).

(35)

and
W_{\pm}^{1}
and
R_{x}^{1}
are the perturbed order parameters and are defined as,

\displaystyle W_{\pm}^{1}

\displaystyle=\int e^{\iota(x^{\prime}\pm\theta^{\prime})}\gamma(x^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})\;dx^{\prime}d\theta^{\prime}.

(36)

Plugging Eq. (
31
) and Eq. (
33
) in Eq. (
29
) obtains

\displaystyle\dot{\gamma}+\rho_{0}(\nabla v_{1})=0,

(37)

where
\nabla v_{1}=\partial_{x}v_{1}^{x}+\partial_{\theta}v_{1}^{\theta}
.

We can calculate
\nabla v_{1}
by determining
\partial_{x}v_{1}^{x}
and
\partial_{\theta}v_{1}^{\theta}
as,

\displaystyle\partial_{x}v_{1}^{x}

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{J}{2}\text{Re}\left[W_{+}^{1}e^{-\iota(x+\theta)}+W_{-}^{1}e^{-\iota(x-\theta)}\right],

\displaystyle\partial_{x}v_{1}^{\theta}

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{K}{4}\text{Re}\Big[W_{+}^{1}e^{-\iota(x-\theta)}+W_{+}^{1}e^{-\iota(x+\theta)}+W_{-}^{1}e^{-\iota(x-\theta)}

\displaystyle+W_{-}^{1}e^{-\iota(x+\theta)}+2R_{x}^{1}e^{-\iota(x-\theta)}+2R_{x}^{1}e^{-\iota(x+\theta)}\Big].

(38)

Therefore, we obtain the time evolution of
\gamma
as,

\displaystyle\dot{\gamma}(x,\theta,t)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{(2J+K)}{16\pi^{2}}\text{Re}\Big[W_{+}^{1}e^{-\iota(x+\theta)}+W_{-}^{1}e^{-\iota(x-\theta)}\Big]

\displaystyle+\dfrac{K}{16\pi^{2}}\text{Re}\Big[W_{+}^{1}e^{-\iota(x-\theta)}+W_{-}^{1}e^{-\iota(x+\theta)}

\displaystyle+2R_{x}^{1}e^{-\iota(x-\theta)}+2R_{x}^{1}e^{-\iota(x+\theta)}\Big].

(39)

We expand
\gamma(x,\theta,t)
in terms of complex exponentials
[
23
]
as the following,

\displaystyle\gamma(x,\theta,t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\bigg(\alpha_{0,0}(t)+\alpha_{1,0}(t)e^{\iota x}+\alpha_{0,1}(t)e^{\iota\theta}

\displaystyle+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\tilde{\alpha}_{n,m}(t)e^{\iota(nx+m\theta)}+\tilde{\beta}_{n,m}(t)e^{\iota(nx-m\theta)}+\text{c.c.}\bigg),

(40)

where c.c. denotes complex conjugate. The order parameters are
W_{+}^{1}=\alpha_{1,1}(t)
,
W_{-}^{1}=\beta_{1,1}(t)
, and
R_{x}^{1}=\alpha_{1,0}(t)
. Moreover, due to the normalization condition
32
, we have
\alpha_{0,0}=0
.
By projecting onto the Fourier basis, we extract the following evolution equations

\displaystyle\dot{W_{\pm}^{1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{J}{8\pi}W_{\pm}^{1}+\frac{K}{16\pi}(W_{+}^{1}+W_{-}^{1}+2R_{x}^{1}),

(41)

\displaystyle\dot{R}_{x}^{1}

\displaystyle=0.

(42)

This is a (linear) three-dimensional dynamic system in the coordinates
W_{+}^{1},W_{-}^{1}
, and
R_{x}^{1}
. Therefore, the Jacobian is

M_{async}=\begin{pmatrix}\dfrac{J}{8\pi}+\dfrac{K}{16\pi}&\dfrac{K}{16\pi}&\dfrac{K}{8\pi}\\[6.0pt]
\dfrac{K}{16\pi}&\dfrac{J}{8\pi}+\dfrac{K}{16\pi}&\dfrac{K}{8\pi}\\[6.0pt]
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}.

The eigenvalues of
M_{async}
are
\lambda=0,\ \dfrac{J}{8\pi}\ \mbox{and}\ \dfrac{J+K}{8\pi}
. These imply a stability region of

\displaystyle J<0,

\displaystyle K<-J

(43)

consistent with numerics. We draw this stability threshold in Fig.
2
(d) by the white dashed lines.

III.2.4
x
(antiphase) sync state

This state is quite difficult to analyze. Look at the scatter plots of the swarmalators in Fig.
3
(a). The spatial dynamics are straightforward: there are two equilibrium points shifted
\pi
units apart,
x_{i}=C
and
C+\pi
(as evident from the position distribution shown in Fig.
3
(b)). We can set the constant
C=0
with a change of frame. But the phase equilibrium points are hard to identify. Figure
3
(c) shows that the
\theta_{i}^{*}
are distributed in two bell-shaped curves, with populations
p,q:=1-p
, respectively. In general,
p\neq q
, the exact values vary from run to run.

To try and find these equilibrium points, we write the phase equation (Eq.
4
) in the continuum limit,

\displaystyle v_{\theta}

\displaystyle=-\sin\theta\int(1+\cos\theta^{\prime})\cos(x^{\prime}-x)\rho(x^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})dx^{\prime}d\theta^{\prime},

(44)

where the density of the state is
\rho(x,\theta)=p\delta(x)P_{p}(\theta)+q\delta(x-\pi)P_{q}(\theta)
. Here
P_{p,q}(\theta)
denotes the densities of the two sub-populations. In this notation, the phase velocity becomes

\displaystyle v_{\theta}=

\displaystyle-\sin\theta\Big[p\cos x\int_{\pi}^{2\pi}(1+\cos\theta^{\prime})P_{p}(\theta^{\prime})d\theta^{\prime}

\displaystyle+q\cos(\pi-x)\int_{0}^{\pi}(1+\cos\theta^{\prime})P_{q}(\theta^{\prime})d\theta^{\prime}\Big],

(45)

which implies

\displaystyle v_{\theta}=-\sin\theta\cos x\Big[p(1+I_{p})-q(1+I_{q})\Big],

(46)

where
I_{p,q}=\int P_{p,q}(\theta)d\theta
denote the definite integrals of
P_{p,q}(\theta)
over their domain. Now, we know
v_{\theta}=0
at
x=0
and
\pi
, which means the term within the brackets must be zero, i.e.,

\displaystyle p(1+I_{p})-q(1+I_{q})=0,

(47)

IV
Conclusion

Our contribution is the first study of swarmalators with Winfree-type coupling, where the elements fire pulses according to a function
P(\theta)
received by the other elements via a response function
R(\theta)
. This type of coupling is observed in many systems, such as fireflies or neurons, and in some sense generalizes the Kuramoto-type coupling. Table
3
shows that Winfree coupling recovers the states seen with Kuramoto coupling (static
\pi
, phase wave, and async states) but also produces three new collective states (
x,\theta
-antiphase sync states and the intermediate mixed state). On the other hand, the active async state that appears with Kuramoto-type coupling was not observed.

We determined the stability thresholds for four of these six states; the stability of the mixed and
x
-antiphase sync state remain out of reach. A natural question for follow-up work is to try and pin these down. Take the antiphase state. As discussed, its properties are somewhat unusual: an infinite number of equilibrium densities
P_{\theta}
satisfy the state, yet only one, the bell-shaped distribution well fit by a Gaussian, appears to be stable. Why is that? Why is it fit so well by a Gaussian? And is it truly the only stable equilibrium, or could others exist that the numerics failed to reveal?

Future work could also relax some of the idealizations we made in the work. For instance, one could on the natural frequencies
(\omega_{i},\nu_{i})
or draw them from some distribution. Heterogeneities in the coupling constants would also be interesting to investigate.

A novel intrusion detection system for IIoT in 5G networks using attention-augmented federated learning and lightweight transformer architectures
Authors: Junxian Du
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe swift proliferation of Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) devices within 5G networks has intensified cybersecurity vulnerabilities owing to their limited processing capabilities and fluctuating network conditions. This work presents an innovative intrusion detection system (IDS) designed for IIoT within 5G environments, overcoming the shortcomings of conventional hybrid deep learning models. Our methodology presents an attention-enhanced federated learning framework integrating a lightweight CNN (MobileNetV3) for feature extraction with a Mini-Transformer for sequence modeling, to attain superior accuracy and computational efficiency. A feature selection technique based on mutual information, integrated with self-attention layers, prioritizes essential network traffic features (e.g., packet size, flow time) while discarding redundant ones, hence improving detection accuracy. The federated learning framework enables decentralized training among IIoT devices with limited resources, ensuring both scalability and the maintenance of user privacy. Comprehensive evaluations conducted on the Edge-IIoTset and ToN-IoT datasets indicate that the proposed methodology yields a binary classification accuracy of 98.5% on the Edge-IIoTset, exceeding the performance of a standalone MobileNetV3 model by 2.1% and surpassing baseline models such as LSTM and VGG-19 by margins ranging from 3.5% to 6.3% under the same training parameters. Additionally, with regards to the ToN-IoT dataset, the model achieves an F1-score of 0.985, marking improvements of 2.0% over MobileNetV3-SVM and 6.5% over LSTM baseline models.Keywords: Industrial internet of things (IIoT), 5G Networks, Intrusion detection system (IDS), MobileNetV3, Mini-transformer, Federated learningSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

The IIoT signifies a revolutionary framework in contemporary industrial systems, incorporating interconnected sensors, actuators, and computational devices to facilitate real-time data sharing, automation, and decision-making1. The integration has been further enhanced by the emergence of 5G networks, which provide ultra-low latency, high bandwidth, and extensive device connectivity, enabling smooth IIoT implementations in industries such as manufacturing, energy, and transportation2. As IIoT devices expand within 5G infrastructures, they produce substantial data that enhances operational efficiencies and predictive maintenance; yet, this growth inadvertently increases cybersecurity vulnerabilities3. Traditionally, IIoT systems depended on outdated protocols and segregated networks; however, the transition to 5G introduces dynamic architectures, edge computing, and diverse device interactions, radically transforming the threat landscape4. Recent research indicate that IIoT in 5G environments encounters complex attack vectors, such as distributed denial-of-service (DDoS), man-in-the-middle, and ransomware, which capitalize on the decentralized characteristics of these networks5.

Robust IDS are crucial in IIoT-5G ecosystems, acting as the primary defense against cyber threats that may compromise key infrastructure and result in severe economic or safety repercussions6. Effective Intrusion Detection Systems not only alleviate acute threats but also facilitate proactive threat intelligence, hence ensuring the continuity of mission-critical processes such as smart grids and autonomous factories7. Furthermore, the confluence of IIoT and 5G heightens the risks, as breaches can propagate through networked systems, possibly jeopardizing national security and public safety8. Recent empirical evidence from deployments indicates that AI-driven IDS improve detection accuracy by 20–30% relative to rule-based methods, highlighting their significance in preserving data integrity and availability9.

Notwithstanding these developments, the development of Intrusion Detection Systems for Industrial Internet of Things in 5G networks encounters considerable hurdles. The resource-limited characteristics of IIoT devices, usually situated on edge nodes with restricted processing power and energy availability, impede the implementation of intricate deep learning models, leading to delays in real-time threat detection10. The dynamic and heterogeneous nature of 5G networks exacerbates this issue, since swift alterations in network architecture and device mobility generate erratic traffic patterns, diminishing the efficacy of traditional approaches11. Conventional deep learning designs, like convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and recurrent neural networks (RNNs), can impose significant processing burdens, rendering them unfeasible for resource-constrained IIoT endpoints12. Furthermore, centralized training methodologies pose considerable privacy issues, since the consolidation of sensitive industrial data across networks heightens the danger of interception and illegal access13. Contemporary IDS solutions commonly experience significant false positive rates, frequently above 15%, attributable to challenges in extracting pertinent features from high-dimensional, noisy network traffic data14. The complexity of simulating various attack patterns in 5G-IIoT systems necessitates adaptive designs that can manage multi-class incursions without compromising efficiency15. These problems collectively underscore the pressing necessity for creative, lightweight, and privacy-preserving IDS solutions specifically designed for the distinct limitations of IIoT-5G environments16.

This research is motivated by the increasing cybersecurity threats in IIoT-5G integrations and the deficiencies of current IDS frameworks in successfully mitigating these risks17. As enterprises progressively implement 5G for improved connectivity, the disparity between the sophistication of threats and detection capabilities expands, requiring models that harmonize accuracy, efficiency, and adaptability18.

This study introduces an innovative IDS that overcomes previously outlined limitations through the implementation of an attention-enhanced federated learning framework paired with lightweight architectures, specifically the MobileNetV3 convolutional neural network (CNN) and a bespoke Mini-Transformer. The primary objective is to attain exceptional detection accuracy while reducing computational demands, rendering it suitable for resource-limited Industrial IIoT devices operating in dynamic 5G environments. Key advancements include the integration of mutual information-based feature selection with self-attention mechanisms to highlight essential traffic attributes, such as packet size and flow duration, while minimizing redundancy. This strategy enhances both model interpretability and precision. The federated learning approach facilitates joint training among distributed nodes, boosting scalability and ensuring privacy without necessitating centralized data collection. This framework represents a shift from conventional heavy architectures, such as VGG-16, by addressing contemporary threats.

Related works

The rapid expansion of IIoT devices within 5G networks has intensified cybersecurity issues, attributable to constraints in device resources, fluctuating traffic patterns, and privacy implications. Effective IDS must optimize for high accuracy while maintaining low latency, energy efficiency, privacy protection, and scalability in light of non-IID data distributions.

Initial approaches concentrated on centralized deep learning techniques for enhanced feature extraction. Research in19 introduced a hybrid CNN-BiLSTM model for multi-class attack detection using the Edge-IIoTset and CICIDS2018 datasets. This architecture employs convolutional layers to discern spatial patterns and bidirectional LSTM to address temporal dependencies, achieving notable accuracy rates of 99.64% on Edge-IIoTset and 100% on CICIDS2018. This model excels in processing sequential traffic, outperforming many traditional and shallow machine learning methods in intricate attack scenarios. Nonetheless, its reliance on centralized training necessitates raw data sharing, posing substantial privacy risks in distributed IIoT environments. Additionally, the complex hybrid architecture incurs significant inference latency and energy usage, complicating real-time application on resource-limited edge devices.

Alternative centralized yet lightweight frameworks have also emerged. In20, a 1D-CNN model with only 96 parameters post-quantization was tested on DNN-EdgeIIoT, achieving 100% binary accuracy and 95.5% accuracy across 15 classes (weighted F1 = 0.946) with a mere 9 ms inference latency on a Raspberry Pi. This model emphasizes minimal parameters for rapid execution, suitable for energy-constrained IIoT devices; however, without temporal modeling, it is less capable of detecting time-sensitive attacks, and centralized training disregards privacy in cooperative 5G frameworks.

The TACNet model discussed in21 integrates multi-scale CNN for feature extraction, LSTM for long-term temporal capture, and temporal attention to discern important time steps and features, attaining 98.56% accuracy on DNN-EdgeIIoT amid various attack types. While this hybrid design enhances both accuracy and interpretability in spatial-temporal contexts, its centralized training and the processing overhead from LSTM and attention mechanisms contribute to increased latency, which presents challenges for ultra-low-latency applications in 5G environments absent of distributed solutions.

Moreover, SACNN-IDS, cited in22, utilizes a self-attention convolutional neural network to highlight crucial features while processing traffic through CNN layers, customized for current IIoT protocols. It shows noteworthy multi-class and sub-category detection, reaching 99.62–99.66% accuracy in different scenarios on Edge-IIoTset and X-IIoT1D. Nevertheless, this fully centralized approach overlooks privacy in collaborative IIoT setups, and its moderate resource demands limit application on the most constrained edge devices.

in23, various lightweight tree-based ensembles—such as Decision Trees, Random Forest, and LightGBM—were assessed across datasets including TON_IoT, WUSTL-IIoT-2021, and Edge-IIoTset, using mutual information for feature selection and cross-dataset transfer learning. These models achieve competitive Micro-F1 scores ranging from 97% to 98%, while requiring minimal training time (2–6 s) and maintaining small model sizes, thus being advantageous for resource-constrained contexts. However, tree-based methods struggle to effectively model the intricate non-linear and sequential relationships present in modern attacks, and their centralized assessments impede scalability and privacy within federated IIoT scenarios.

ightweight and federated methodologies have been developed to address certain constraints in the realm of machine learning and deep learning for resource-limited environments. Studies24–28 present various optimized models (e.g., PSO-PCA-MARS, LIDS with weighted sampling, DNN-DAE with Archimedes optimization) that focus on achieving ultra-low latency (e.g., < 2 ms/packet) and minimal sizes (< 300 KB), often reaching over 99% accuracy on diverse datasets, making them well-suited for real-time IIoT applications. However, most of these models still operate in a centralized manner, failing to incorporate federated privacy frameworks or effectively manage non-IID data distributions, and their ability to handle dynamic 5G attack patterns is frequently inadequate.

Federated learning (FL) has made significant strides in enabling privacy-preserving distributed training. Research29 employs structured pruning on ResNet/VGG in FL, reducing model size by 90% and tripling training speeds, while preserving ~ 99% detection accuracy on datasets such as UNSW-NB15. The adaptive mechanism introduced by FLAD in30 enhances convergence and accuracy for clients facing more complex DDoS attacks without requiring data sharing. In31, a hybrid ensemble FL framework leveraging XGBoost and Gradient Boosting achieves impressive accuracy metrics while functioning effectively on edge hardware. However, these federated approaches tend to lack lightweight transformer-based temporal modeling and adequate assessments on Edge-IIoT datasets.

The integration of hybrid models incorporates blockchain, explainability, and transfer learning to bolster security and capability. For instance, GAO-XGBoost presented in32 combines genetic optimization with blockchain for secure data management, achieving a high true positive rate in IoT anomaly detection. Meanwhile, the explainable deep learning model Cyber-Sentinet detailed in33 excels in transparency and achieves notable detection metrics on edge devices. The framework from34 enhances decentralized IoT security through a combination of blockchain and advanced machine learning techniques, effectively ensuring privacy. Additionally, TL-BILSTM IoT from35 utilizes transfer learning to classify various botnet attacks successfully. In36, a comprehensive blockchain-powered framework enhances intrusion detection systems with a focus on key generation optimization. This framework shows promising metrics, significantly improving accuracy and resilience against threats.

Complementary models improve various facets of performance. In37, the MobileNetV3-SVM leveraging transfer learning effectively processes network packets in a real-time context via a lightweight CNN, securing notable accuracy and precision along with reduced false positives, and achieving a robust AUC-ROC on the CICIDS2017/2018 and UNSW-NB15 IoT-5G datasets, thereby proving its suitability for resource-constrained 5G edge devices. In38, an enhanced LSTM utilizing Particle Swarm, JAYA, and Salp Swarm algorithms optimizes anomaly detection within the NSL-KDD, CICIDS, and BoT-IoT datasets, leading to minimized false alarms and surpassing baseline measures in precision, recall, F-score, TPR, FPR, and ROC across various datasets. In39, Vision Transformers paired with advanced ML techniques (such as tree-based SVM, ensembles, and LSTM) attain perfect metrics on training and validation for IoT datasets, thus presenting robust anomaly detection through sophisticated pattern recognition within multivariate and non-stationary traffic. In40, the application of VGG16 and VGG19 to the Edge-IIoTset facilitates accurate detection of several types of attacks including injection, MITM, malware, DDoS, and information gathering, effectively offering significant feature extraction for IIoT-targeted threats.

Nevertheless, centralized training remains prevalent in numerous studies, while blockchain contributes substantially to communication and energy burdens. Explainability complicates matters without resolving latency issues, and despite enhancing generalization, transfer learning often neglects federated privacy. Furthermore, lightweight frameworks frequently lack robust non-IID resilience or transformer-based temporal modeling in the context of Edge-IIoTset. Data pre-processing

This section delineates the pre-processing methodologies employed on the Edge-IIoTset and ToN-IoT datasets to guarantee their compliance with the proposed Attention-augmented Federated Learning model for IIoT in 5G networks. The procedure tackles data integrity, dimensionality, and class imbalance, while facilitating distributed training on resource-limited devices.

Data cleaning and normalization

The first phase entails cleansing the raw data to eliminate inconsistencies. Missing values are substituted with median values for numerical characteristics (e.g., packet size) and mode values for categorical features (e.g., attack kind). Duplicate records are removed, and outliers are identified and addressed using the Interquartile Range (IQR) approach by substituting them with the nearest valid values. Class imbalance handling with KMeans-SMOTE

To mitigate the risk of data leakage, the dataset is initially divided into training, validation, and testing subsets. The KMeans-SMOTE technique is utilized exclusively on the training subset, thereby generating synthetic data only from this partition to ensure that evaluations accurately reflect unseen real-world scenarios.

Crucially, the oversampling process occurs before the distribution of clients in a federated setting but is confined to the global training dataset. Consequently, the subsequent non-IID distribution among clients maintains statistical variability, as each client receives a restricted number of classes based on the label-skew strategy. Thus, KMeans-SMOTE tackles class imbalance without distorting the local client distributions or disregarding genuine data skewness.

By clustering minority instances prior to synthetic sample creation, KMeans-SMOTE preserves intrinsic local feature structures and minimizes the chance of producing overly generalized synthetic representations. This approach guarantees that the federated learning environment remains authentic while enhancing the identification of minority classes.

Mutual information feature selection

Due to the large dimensionality of the datasets, a feature selection strategy based on Mutual Information (MI) is employed. Calculate Mutual Information scores for all characteristics relative to the target variable (intrusion class), and thereafter choose features with MI scores exceeding a threshold of 0.05. This specific threshold was determined through empirical sensitivity analysis, ensuring a balance between reducing feature redundancy and retaining the most discriminative attributes for 5G-IIoT environments. Data labeling and splitting for federated learning

Data is categorized and partitioned for federated learning according to attack classifications. Assign labels according to attack categories from Edge-IIoTset and ToN-IoT utilizing ground truth annotations. To simulate a realistic and heterogeneous 5G-IIoT environment, the pre-processed dataset is partitioned into non-IID subsets using a label-based skewed distribution. Specifically, each local IIoT device is assigned samples from only a limited subset of attack classes (e.g., 2 or 3 classes out of the total available), creating a significant statistical challenge for global model convergence. The data is divided into about 70% for training, 15% for validation, and 15% for testing among scattered devices, ensuring privacy and scalability.

Feature mapping and modality conversion

Since raw IIoT traffic is tabular (1D) and MobileNetV3 is designed for spatial (2D) feature extraction, a specific mapping process is employed. After feature selection, the resulting 1D feature vector is reshaped into a 2D tensor (e.g., a grayscale-like image). This transformation allows the convolutional kernels of MobileNetV3 to extract cross-feature correlations as spatial patterns, which are subsequently processed by the Mini-Transformer.

Integration in federated learning

MobileNetV3 and Mini-Transformer are incorporated as fundamental elements in the federated learning framework, trained locally on IIoT devices. The integrated model, comprising around 2.5 million parameters, is tailored for distributed training, where local updates are aggregated via FedAvg and augmented by proximal terms and differential privacy. To evaluate the communication overhead, the uplink cost per device per round is calculated based on the model size. Given 2.5 million 32-bit floating-point parameters, each update requires approximately 10 MB of data transmission. In a high-bandwidth 5G environment, this cost is minimal, ensuring that the frequent communication rounds do not bottleneck the network’s operational performance.

Hyperparameter tuning

Hyperparameters are optimized by a grid search integrated with Bayesian optimization on validation subsets to enhance the performance of MobileNetV3 and Mini-Transformer within the federated learning framework on edge devices. Key hyperparameters include: learning rate (η) evaluated over {0.001, 0.01, 0.1}, with 0.01 chosen for stable convergence; batch size {16, 32, 64}, selecting 32 to optimize memory usage and gradient stability; number of epochs (E) per local round {5, 10, 20}, with 10 offering the best compromise; attention heads {2, 4, 8}, opting for 8 to enhance multi-head attention efficiency; and dropout rate {0.1, 0.2, 0.3}, employing 0.1 to mitigate overfitting. Tuning is conducted in a simulated federated environment utilizing 20% of the data, assessing F1-score and accuracy, while Bayesian optimization decreases trials by 40%.

Simulation results and discussion

This section discusses the experimental outcomes along with an in-depth examination of the suggested IDS framework. To guarantee the robustness of the statistical results, each experiment was repeated five times with varying random seeds. The subsequent results are expressed as mean values with their associated standard deviation (± SD) to establish 95% confidence intervals. Additionally, a 5-fold cross-validation technique was utilized in the training phase to reduce the risk of overfitting and to affirm the model’s applicability across various network traffic types.

The model is evaluated utilizing the Edge-IIoTset and ToN-IoT datasets for binary and multi-class classification. Evaluation measures include Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1-Score, and AUC. The proposed model is evaluated against baseline methods, including MobileNetV3-SVM37, LSTM38, ViT39, and VGG-1940, to assess its performance.

Robustness to data heterogeneity and non-IID distribution

In practical applications of 5G-IIoT, the data produced by numerous distributed devices tends to be non-independent and identically distributed (non-IID) owing to varied traffic behaviors, differing functionalities of devices, and localized attack patterns. This diversity can adversely affect federated learning systems, leading to issues such as client drift, gradient divergence, prolonged convergence times, and reductions in the accuracy of the global model.

To systematically assess the robustness of the proposed Transformer-FL framework amidst these heterogeneous conditions, we conducted simulations of different extents of non-IID data distribution utilizing a Dirichlet distribution with parameter α. A lower α indicates highly imbalanced label distributions across clients (indicating extreme non-IID), whereas a higher α represents more equitable, near-IID conditions.

Table 10 illustrates that in extreme non-IID contexts (α = 0.1), there is a noticeable decrease in both accuracy and F1-score when compared to near-IID conditions. Such decline is anticipated, as highly imbalanced local data leads to biased gradient updates that stray from the optimum global trajectory. Furthermore, non-IID distributions affect the stability of convergence; under severe non-IID conditions, the global model required more communication iterations to achieve stable convergence than it did in near-IID environments. This aligns with the principles of federated learning, where diverse local objectives can cause erratic updates and hinder global alignment. Nevertheless, the Transformer-FL framework exhibited consistent convergence, avoiding divergence or significant oscillations, which highlights its resilience to client-level data discrepancies.

Security and privacy evaluation

To thoroughly examine the privacy and security dimensions of the suggested federated Intrusion Detection System (IDS), we performed quantitative analyses focusing on the balance between privacy and utility, as well as the system’s robustness against prevalent attacks in federated learning. All assessments utilized the Edge-IIoTset dataset and adhered to the simulation parameters outlined in Sect. 4.1.

Privacy-utility trade-off

To systematically assess the privacy–utility trade-off of the introduced federated Intrusion Detection System (IDS), Gaussian noise injection was applied within the context of differential privacy (DP) during the server-side aggregation phase. This involved adding calibrated Gaussian noise to the global model parameters following the weighted Federated Averaging (FedAvg) procedure. Local gradients were constrained with a norm limit of C = 1.0, and privacy assurances were evaluated through the moments accountant technique over 50 global iterations with a client sampling proportion of 10%. All experiments were conducted with a constant δ = 10⁻⁵.

To examine how varying privacy strength impacts model efficacy, different noise multipliers σ were tested, specifically within the set {0, 0.1, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0}. For each level, metrics such as the privacy budget ε, classification accuracy, F1-score, and inference latency were recorded. The results were expressed as mean ± standard deviation across five separate trials.

As depicted in Table 12, raising the noise multiplier considerably enhances privacy assurances (reducing ε) while resulting in a gradual yet controlled decline in detection effectiveness. The model attains 98.2% accuracy and a 0.982 F1-score without DP (σ = 0). Introducing moderate noise (σ = 0.5) improves the privacy budget to ε ≈ 2.4, with only a slight decrease in accuracy to 97.8% and an F1-score of 0.978. Notably, the inference latency shows a minimal increase from 0.42 ms per packet to 0.43 ms per packet, suggesting that integrating DP does not hinder real-time application requirements in 5G-IIoT settings.

Conclusion

This study presents a novel IDS tailored for the IIoT in the context of 5G networks. It employs an attention-enhanced federated learning framework alongside lightweight Transformer-based models. Results indicate that the combination of local feature extraction using MobileNetV3 and global context understanding via Mini-Transformers facilitates effective intrusion detection without necessitating centralized data collection. This strategy successfully tackles the computational limits of IIoT devices and alleviates privacy concerns through the implementation of differential privacy techniques. However, a significant drawback is the balance between privacy budgets (ε) and model accuracy, which poses challenges in highly non-IID environments. Additionally, the validation of results was performed in a simulated 5G setting, which may not accurately reflect the intricacies of actual heterogeneous network traffic. To move towards practical application, future research will aim to deploy this framework on physical Edge computing nodes to assess its performance under genuine network constraints. Moreover, there will be an exploration of incorporating adversarial training to bolster defenses against zero-day threats and ensure adaptability for ultra-dense 5G deployments, thereby enhancing the security of IIoT networks.

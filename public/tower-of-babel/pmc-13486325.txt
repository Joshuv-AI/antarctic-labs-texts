Anxiety Screening in Pediatric Cardiology
Authors: Jingwen Xiao, Yanxia Chen
Published: 2026

Ahmad et al1 provide a timely validation of PROMIS-Anx against SCARED in children with pediatric heart disease. Their finding that a brief child-completed instrument achieved higher completion rates and meaningful concordance addresses a practical barrier in busy pediatric cardiology clinics. We believe PROMIS-Anx will have the greatest clinical value when it is used not as a stand-alone referral trigger, but as the first step in a defined, stepped behavioral health pathway.

The study raises two workflow-relevant observations. First, caregivers reported fewer symptoms than children, underscoring that discordance between informants should be interpreted as clinically informative rather than dismissed as measurement noise.2 Second, the short format creates a feasible opening for cardiologists to ask about anxiety during routine visits, consistent with American Heart Association recommendations to integrate psychological support into congenital heart disease care.3

On this basis, we suggest a tiered response to screening results. A child-reported PROMIS-Anx score >30 could prompt same-visit review of safety concerns, functional impairment, and family priorities, followed by a warm handoff or referral when resources permit. Scores below this threshold should not automatically close the conversation. When accompanied by patient concern, functional decline, recent hospitalization or procedure, or substantial child-caregiver discordance, they may justify anticipatory guidance, repeat screening, or low-intensity behavioral health resources. This approach preserves efficiency while reducing dependence on a single cutoff.

Integrated behavioral health models in pediatric subspecialty clinics emphasize that screening works best when a positive result is linked to a preplanned response and accessible follow-up.4,5 In this context, Ahmad et al1 help move pediatric cardiology from opportunistic recognition toward routine psychosocial care. Future implementation studies should assess whether PROMIS-Anx-guided stepped care improves referral completion, treatment initiation, patient-reported functioning, and family experience. The next challenge is to translate efficient detection into reproducible, developmentally sensitive care.

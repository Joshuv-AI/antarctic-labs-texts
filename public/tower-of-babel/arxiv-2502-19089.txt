Cylindrical and Möbius Quantum Codes for Asymmetric Pauli Errors
Lorenzo Valentini, Diego Forlivesi, and Marco Chiani

Abstract
In the implementation of quantum information systems, one type of Pauli error, such as phase-flip errors, may occur more frequently than others, like bit-flip errors. For this reason, quantum error-correcting codes that handle asymmetric errors are critical to mitigating the impact of such impairments. To this aim, several asymmetric quantum codes have been proposed. These include variants of surface codes like the XZZX and ZZZY surface codes, tailored to preserve quantum information in the presence of error asymmetries. In this work, we propose two classes of Calderbank, Shor and Steane (CSS) topological codes, referred to as cylindrical and Möbius codes, particular cases of the fiber bundle family. Cylindrical codes maintain a fully planar structure, while Möbius codes are quasi-planar, with minimal non-local qubit interactions. We construct these codes employing the algebraic chain complexes formalism, providing theoretical upper bounds for the logical error rate. Our results demonstrate that cylindrical and Möbius codes outperform standard surface codes when using the minimum weight perfect matching (MWPM) decoder.

Theoretical Performance Analysis of Quantum Codes

Considering a quantum stabilizer code, if we measure the codeword according to the generators

A common channel model is the one characterized by errors occurring independently and with the same statistic on the individual qubits of each codeword. In this model, the error can be

,
\textstyle{\mathchar 90}
or
\textstyle{\mathchar 89}
with probabilities

, and

In the case of asymmetric channels with

We define

\displaystyle p_{L}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}(1-p)^{n-j}p^{j}(1-\beta_{j})

\displaystyle 1-\beta_{j}=\frac{1}{p^{j}}\sum_{i=0}^{j}\binom{j}{i}\,p_{\mathrm{Z}}^{i}\,\sum_{\ell=0}^{j-i}\binom{j-i}{\ell}\,p_{\mathrm{X}}^{\ell}\,p_{\mathrm{Y}}^{j-i-\ell}f_{j}(i,\ell)\,

\displaystyle\approx\left(1-\beta_{t+1}\right)\binom{n}{t+1}p^{t+1}\,.

Note that,

II-C

Undetectable errors weight enumerator from Quantum MacWilliams identities

The values of
\beta_{j}
for the specific decoder and quantum channel can be evaluated by exhaustive search or ad-hoc reasoning on the logical operators
[
50
]
.
Despite of the adopted method, when these values are tabulated anyone can plot the actual performance without implementing the decoder.
To aid the derivation of
\beta_{j}
, alongside a
[[n,k,d]]

\displaystyle L(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{w=0}^{n}L_{w}z^{w}

where

is the number of undetectable errors (logical operators) of weight

.
This polynomial can be computed starting from the code’s generators by using the quantum MacWilliams identities

]
.
Specifically, for an

]

\displaystyle L(z)=\frac{1}{2^{k}}B(z)-\frac{1}{4^{k}}A(z)

where
\frac{1}{4^{k}}A(z)
and
\frac{1}{2^{k}}B(z)
are equal to the stabilizer

and the normalizer

of the code, respectively
[
52
]
.
Considering the connection between stabilizer codes and codes over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
, the evaluation of

can be seen as the computation of the weight distribution of classical codes.
To find such distribution, it is possible to exploit software tools related to coding theory, such as MAGMA

53
]
.
Given
A(z)
, we can obtain

using the quantum MacWilliams identities
[
51
,
54
]

\displaystyle B_{w}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\;\sum_{s=0}^{w}\;\;\binom{\ell}{s}\binom{n-\ell}{w-s}(-1)^{s}3^{w-s}A_{\ell}\,.

(6)

Then, from (
5
) we derive the undetectable errors

,

.

One approach that can be taken to derive the performance of a code is the logical operator analysis.
In fact, using
L(z)
together with some understanding of the structure of the code’s logical operators, ad-hoc counting could be performed on small codes to obtain the exact value of
\beta_{j}
.
In this procedure, given the total number of logical operators

of weight
w
, the first step is to identify all such operators in the code under examination.
These operators are then grouped into sets based on their similar structure, which typically results in similar performance behavior, often due to inherent symmetries.
Finally, considering that
j

w

Note that, during this evaluation, it is important to remember that logical operators may share common operators on the same qubits.
Therefore, extra care must be taken to avoid double counting.
This approach will be used, together with several explanatory examples, in Section
III-C
and Section
III-D
to evaluate the exact value of
\beta_{2}
for both the cylindrical and Möbius codes with

For large codes, this approach becomes infeasible.
Therefore, bounds that rely solely on the knowledge of

and the code’s structure can be used to estimate its performance.
This will be addressed for both the cylindrical and Möbius codes in Section

, to estimate the performance for any code dimension.

In the following, we introduce a few concepts from topology that we later use to build quantum cylindrical codes and derive their parameters.

A

\partial_{i}:C_{i}\xrightarrow{}C_{i-1}

It is possible to express the

(8)

where elements of

are called

, respectively. Each chain complex has a dual chain, or

(the space of linear functionals of

) and dual maps

\partial^{\ast}_{i+1}:C_{i+1}^{\ast}\leftarrow{}C_{i}^{\ast}.

The

H^{i}\left(C\right)=\frac{\ker\partial_{i+1}^{\ast}}{\image\,\partial_{i}^{\ast}}

where

Moreover, we define

\displaystyle\xi_{i}

\displaystyle\triangleq\min\left\{f_{i}({\mathchoice{\hbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle{\mathchar 99}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\textstyle{\mathchar 99}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle{\mathchar 99}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathchar 99}$}}})\,|\,{\mathchoice{\hbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle{\mathchar 99}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\textstyle{\mathchar 99}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle{\mathchar 99}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathchar 99}$}}}\in\ker\partial_{i}\setminus\image\,\partial_{i+1}\right\}

(11)

\displaystyle\zeta_{i}

\displaystyle\triangleq\min\left\{f_{i}({\mathchoice{\hbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle{\mathchar 99}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\textstyle{\mathchar 99}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle{\mathchar 99}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathchar 99}$}}})\,|\,{\mathchoice{\hbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle{\mathchar 99}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\textstyle{\mathchar 99}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle{\mathchar 99}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathchar 99}$}}}\in\ker\partial_{i+1}^{\ast}\setminus\image\,\partial_{i}^{\ast}\right\}

where

III-E

Analytical Upper Bounds

In this section, we provide an upper bound on the logical qubit error rate for both the cylindrical and the Möbius codes, without limiting the code distance
d
.

In order to derive a bound valid for any
d
, we require a closed-form expression for the
L(z)
coefficients related to the logical operators of weight
2t+1
and
2t+2
.
Observing the topological structure of the cylindrical code, we have
2d
logical operators of weight
2t+1
:
d

\textstyle{\mathchar 90}
logical operators crossing horizontally, and
d

\textstyle{\mathchar 88}
logical operators crossing vertically across the lattice.
Two examples of these are depicted in Fig.
2
b.
Moving to logical operators of weight
2t+2
, we search for the ones composed only by
\textstyle{\mathchar 88}
.
These logical operators traverse the lattice from the outer ring of the cylindrical structure to the inner ring, performing a single turn along the path.
An example of this, is given by
{\mathchoice{\hbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\textstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}}_{L}={\mathchoice{\hbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\textstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}}_{5}{\mathchoice{\hbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\textstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}}_{8}{\mathchoice{\hbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\textstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}}_{9}{\mathchoice{\hbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\textstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}}_{13}
in Fig.
2
b.
In general, there are
d
different starting points (i.e., qubits in the outer ring) and
d-1
possible locations where the turn can occur, either to the right or to the left.
Then, the logical operators composed only by
\textstyle{\mathchar 88}
Pauli are
2d(d-1)
.
Additionally, there are
2d
logical operators in the form
{\mathchoice{\hbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle{\mathchar 89}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\textstyle{\mathchar 89}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle{\mathchar 89}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathchar 89}$}}}{\mathchoice{\hbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle{\mathchar 89}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\textstyle{\mathchar 89}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle{\mathchar 89}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathchar 89}$}}}{\mathchoice{\hbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\textstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}}{\mathchoice{\hbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\textstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}}\dots{\mathchoice{\hbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\textstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}}
, with
2t-1
Pauli
\textstyle{\mathchar 90}
.
In conclusion, we have that
L_{2t+1}=2d
and
L_{2t+2}=2d^{2}
.

Regarding the Möbius code, we have
d+1
logical operators of weight
2t+1
: one
{\mathchoice{\hbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\textstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}}_{L}
operator crossing horizontally, and
d

{\mathchoice{\hbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\textstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}}_{L}
operators crossing vertically across the lattice (e.g., see Fig.
2
c).
Regarding
{\mathchoice{\hbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\textstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}}_{L}
operators of weight
2t+2
, the ones traversing horizontally the lattice are the same of the cylindrical code, i.e.,
2d(d-1)
.
On the other hand, there are
d(d-1)

{\mathchoice{\hbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\textstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}}_{L}
operators of weight
2t+2
crossing horizontally the lattice.
Indeed, we have
d-1
starting points (i.e., qubits connecting the outer and the inner rings) and
d
possible locations where the turn can occur.
In conclusion, we have
L_{2t+1}=d+1
and
L_{2t+2}=3d(d-1)
.

Furthermore, we define as
L^{X}_{w}
the total number of
{\mathchoice{\hbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\textstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}}_{L}
operators composed only by
\textstyle{\mathchar 88}
operators of weight
w
.
Similarly,
L^{Z}_{w}
is defined as the total number of
{\mathchoice{\hbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\textstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}}_{L}
operators composed solely of
\textstyle{\mathchar 90}
operators of weight
w
.
Therefore, for the cylindrical codes
L^{X}_{2t+1}=L^{Z}_{2t+1}=d
and
L^{X}_{2t+2}=2d(d-1)
, while, for the Möbius codes
L^{X}_{2t+1}=d
,
L^{Z}_{2t+1}=1
, and
L^{X}_{2t+2}=3d(d-1)
.
For both codes we have that
L^{Z}_{2t+2}=0
.

Theorem 1
.

The value of
\beta_{t+1}
, for a cylindrical or Möbius code of distance
d=2t+1
, can be upper bounded as

\displaystyle\beta_{t+1}

\displaystyle\geqslant 1-\frac{\binom{2t+1}{t+1}L^{Z}_{2t+1}}{\binom{n}{t+1}}\left(\frac{p_{\mathrm{Z}}+p_{\mathrm{Y}}}{p}\right)^{t+1}

\displaystyle-\frac{\binom{2t+1}{t+1}L^{X}_{2t+1}+\binom{2t+2}{t+1}L^{X}_{2t+2}/2}{\binom{n}{t+1}}\left(\frac{p_{\mathrm{X}}+p_{\mathrm{Y}}}{p}\right)^{t+1}

(40)

Proof.

Cylindrical and Möbius codes are
CSS
codes, therefore,
t+1
errors comprising both
\textstyle{\mathchar 88}
and
\textstyle{\mathchar 90}
Pauli operators are always corrected.
Hence,
t+1
errors can cause a logical operator of weight
2t+1
or
2t+2
only if they are made of either
\textstyle{\mathchar 88}
and
\textstyle{\mathchar 89}
, or
\textstyle{\mathchar 90}
and
\textstyle{\mathchar 89}
Pauli operators.
As a consequence of this, we have that each error pattern composed by
\textstyle{\mathchar 88}
and
\textstyle{\mathchar 89}
, which triggers an
{\mathchoice{\hbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\textstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}}_{L}
, has to be weighted by
(p_{\mathrm{X}}+p_{\mathrm{Y}})^{t+1}
.
For
{\mathchoice{\hbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\textstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}}_{L}
operators, the weighting is
(p_{\mathrm{Z}}+p_{\mathrm{Y}})^{t+1}
.
Then, consider that each logical operator of weight
2t+1
, can be caused by
\binom{2t+1}{t+1}
possible error patterns of weight
t+1
.
Similarly, for each logical operator of weight
2t+2
, there are
\binom{2t+2}{t+1}
possible error patterns of weight
t+1
.
However, due to the
MWPM
decoding, there are always a pair of different error patterns of weight
t+1
causing the same syndrome when occurring over the same logical operator of weight
2t+2
.
As an example, referring to Fig.
2
b, the errors
{\mathchoice{\hbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\textstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}}_{5}{\mathchoice{\hbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\textstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}}_{15}
and
{\mathchoice{\hbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\textstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}}_{7}{\mathchoice{\hbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\textstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}}_{14}
result in the same syndrome.
As a consequence, only half of the
\binom{2t+2}{t+1}
combinations should be counted.
In conclusion, the enumerators in (
40
) are the total number of faulty error patterns comprising
t+1
Pauli operators, weighted by their probability of occurrence.
On the other hand, the common denominator is the total number of error patterns of weight
t+1
that can occur over
n
qubits,
\binom{n}{t+1}
, weighted by its probability.
We remark that this is an upper bound since we are not taking into account the degeneracy of the code.
Specifically, we are not considering the overlapping of logical operators, which reduces the total number of possible error patterns given by the binomials
[
50
]
.
∎

Corollary 1
.

For a cylindrical or Möbius code of distance
d=2t+1
over an asymmetric channel with asymmetry parameter
A=2p_{\mathrm{Z}}/(p-p_{\mathrm{Z}})
and
p_{\mathrm{X}}=p_{\mathrm{Y}}
, the logical error rate can be upper bounded as

\displaystyle p_{\mathrm{L}}\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{(A+1)^{t+1}\binom{2t+1}{t+1}L^{Z}_{2t+1}}{(A+2)^{t+1}}\,p^{t+1}

\displaystyle+\frac{2^{t+1}\left[\binom{2t+1}{t+1}L^{X}_{2t+1}+\binom{2t+2}{t+1}L^{X}_{2t+2}/2\right]}{(A+2)^{t+1}}\,p^{t+1}\,.

(41)

V
Conclusions

We have presented new topological codes, the cylindrical codes and Möbius codes.
We have derived their code parameters and we have proved that they are valid
CSS
codes using chain complexes from topology.
Additionally, by introducing
d-1
twists in the construction of cylindrical codes, we have obtained a quasi-planar
CSS
codes, named Möbius codes.
Starting from quantum MacWilliams identities, we have exploited the undetectable errors
WE
to study analytically the error correction capability of cylindrical and Möbius codes.
In particular, although such codes are tailored for asymmetric errors, they outperform standard surface codes both on depolarizing and polarizing channels.
Finally, we have provided a numerical analysis of the performance of symmetric and asymmetric cylindrical and Möbius codes using a
MWPM
decoding.
The investigation shows that the proposed codes are particularly well-suited for handling asymmetric errors.

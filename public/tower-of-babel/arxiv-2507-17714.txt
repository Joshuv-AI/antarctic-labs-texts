Abstract
. Using a geometric construction, we solve Plateau’s Problem in the Heisenberg group ℍ 1 \mathbb{H}^{1} for intrinsic graphs defined on a convex domain D D , under a smallness condition either on the boundary ∂ D \partial D or on the boundary datum φ ∈ Lip ⁡ ( ∂ D ) \varphi\in\mathrm{Lip}(\partial D) . The proof relies on a calibration argument. We then apply these techniques to establish a new regularity result for H H -perimeter minimizers.

1.
Introduction

The classical Plateau’s Problem—minimizing the area of a surface spanned by a given boundary curve—can be formulated in several different ways: for graphs, parametric surfaces, Caccioppoli sets, currents, and others. The precise formulation and its solution depend
on the chosen notion of surface and area, as well as on how the boundary condition is imposed.
A nice overview can be found in
[
10
,
17
]
.

In this paper, we study the Plateau’s Problem in the first Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{1}
, where the notion of area is defined via the horizontal perimeter of a set in the sense of De Giorgi, along with the associated area formulas.
In this context, the existence of solutions to the problem was first investigated by Garofalo and Nhieu in
[
14
]
for sets with finite perimeter. For the case of
t
-graphs in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, the existence was first studied by S. D. Pauls in
[
19
]
, adapting techniques related to the Bounded Slope Condition, which are indeed applicable to general area-type functionals, and later by other authors in
[
21
,
6
,
15
,
22
,
9
]
.

Our focus is on
intrinsic graphs
—that is, graphs defined along the integral curves of a left-invariant vector field. This notion of graph appears to be the most appropriate, as the boundary of sets with finite
H
-perimeter is rectifiable precisely in this sense: up to a negligible set, it can be covered by a countable union of
C^{1}_{H}
-regular
intrinsic graphs
, see
[
11
, Theorem 7.1]
. In fact, our construction yields solutions to the Plateau’s Problem that are intrinsic graphs.

In this setting, the question of the existence of solutions becomes more challenging. On the one hand,
when passing from

t
-graphs to intrinsic graphs, the area functional is strongly non-linear and non-convex.
As a consequence, stationary points of the area may be unstable
[
8
]
.
On the other hand, there is, so far, no satisfactory regularity theory for

H
-minimal surfaces
(i.e., stationary points of the horizontal perimeter). Indeed, several examples of

H
-minimal surfaces with low regularity are known

[
20
, Theorem C]
.

However, when
n=1
, the situation is more favorable: smooth
2
-dimensional
H
-minimal surfaces in
\mathbb{H}^{1}
without characteristic points are ruled (i.e., foliated by horizontal segments) and therefore exhibit a certain rigidity.
This observation is the starting point of our study.

The first Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{1}
is the 3-dimensional Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
equipped with the group operation:

(x,y,t)\cdot(\xi,\eta,\tau)=\big(x+\xi,\ y+\eta,\ t+\tau+2(y\xi-x\eta)\big),\hskip 23.49976pt(x,y,t),(\xi,\eta,\tau)\in\mathbb{H}^{1}.

The Lie algebra of left-invariant vector fields is spanned by the three vector fields defined, at each point
p=(x,y,t)\in\mathbb{H}^{1}
, as:

X(p)=\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x}+2y\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t},\hskip 23.49976ptY(p)=\dfrac{\partial}{\partial y}-2x\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t},\hskip 23.49976ptT:=\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}.

We denote by
H
the
horizontal sub-bundle
of
\mathbb{H}^{1}
, that is,
H_{p}:=\mathrm{span}\{X(p),Y(p)\}
for
p\in\mathbb{H}^{1}
.

A
C^{1}
vector field
V
defined in an open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{H}^{1}
is said to be horizontal if
V(p)\in H_{p}
for every
p\in\Omega
. In this case, we write
V\in C^{1}(\Omega;H)
.
If
V\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega;H)
, i.e.,
V=v_{1}X+v_{2}Y
with
v_{1},v_{2}\in C_{c}^{1}(\Omega)
, we let
\|V\|_{\infty}=\max_{p\in\Omega}(v_{1}(p)^{2}+v_{2}(p)^{2})^{1/2}
. Notice that

\div V:=Xv_{1}+Yv_{2}

is the standard divergence of
V
.

The Haar measure of
\mathbb{H}^{1}
is the Lebesgue measure
{\mathscr{L}}^{3}
.
We say that a
{\mathscr{L}}^{3}
-measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{H}^{1}
has
finite H-perimeter
in
\Omega
if

P_{H}(E;\Omega):=\sup\left\{\int_{E}\div Vd{\mathscr{L}}^{3}\,:\,V\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega;H),\,\|V\|_{\infty}\leq 1\right\}<\infty.

The quantity
P_{H}(E;\Omega)
is called the
H-perimeter
of
E
in
\Omega
, see
[
11
]
.
When
\partial E\cap\Omega
is the graph of a function with suitable regularity, the
H
-perimeter can be represented by area-type formulas.
We are interested in the case of intrinsic graphs, and, without loss of generality, we focus on graphs defined along the vector field
X
.

Let
\Phi:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{H}^{1}\to\mathbb{H}^{1}
be the flow of
X
, and define
\Phi_{s}:\mathbb{H}^{1}\to\mathbb{H}^{1}
by
\Phi_{s}(p)=\Phi(s,p)
, for
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
p\in\mathbb{H}^{1}
.
We denote by
\mathbb{W}:=\{p=(x,y,t)\in\mathbb{H}^{1}\,:\,x=0\}
the vertical plane (subgroup) “orthogonal to
X
”, and we fix a domain

D\subset\mathbb{W}
. The intrinsic graph (along
X
) of a function
u:D\to\mathbb{R}
is defined as

S_{u}=\big\{\Phi_{s}(y,t)=(s,y,t+2ys)\in\mathbb{H}^{1}\,:\,(y,t)\in D,\,s=u(y,t)\big\}.

Similarly, the intrinsic epigraph of
u
is

E_{u}=\big\{\Phi_{s}(y,t)\in\mathbb{H}^{1}\,:\,(y,t)\in D,\,s>u(y,t)\big\}.

The (left) cylinder over
D
is denoted by

D\cdot\mathbb{V}=\big\{\Phi_{s}(y,t)\in\mathbb{H}^{1}\,:\,(y,t)\in D,\,s\in\mathbb{R}\big\},

where
\mathbb{V}=\big\{(x,0,0)\in\mathbb{H}^{1}\,:\,x\in\mathbb{R}\big\}
is the complementary subgroup of
\mathbb{W}
.

If
D\subset\mathbb{W}
is bounded and
u\in\mathrm{Lip}(D)
, then
P_{H}(E_{u};D\cdot\mathbb{V})<\infty
and there holds the area formula, see
[
12
]
,

(1.1)

P_{H}(E_{u};D\cdot\mathbb{V})=\mathscr{A}_{H}(u):=\int_{D}\sqrt{1+\mathscr{B}u(y,t)^{2}}\,dydt<\infty,

where

\mathscr{B}u=\Big(\dfrac{\partial}{\partial y}-4u\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\Big)u=\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial y}-2\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}u^{2}.

Notice that the area functional
u\mapsto\mathscr{A}_{H}(u)
is not convex.

The Burgers’ operator
u\mapsto\mathscr{B}u
is well-defined also in distributional sense,
and formula (
1.1
) remains valid
under the weaker assumption that
u
is a continuous function
such that
\mathscr{B}u\in C(D)
, see
[
1
, Proposition 2.22]
, or even in the weaker situation of
\mathscr{B}u\in L^{\infty}(D)
, see
[
7
, Theorem 1.6]
.
We say that
u\in C(D)
is
intrinsic Lipschitz
if

\mathscr{B}u\in L^{\infty}(D)
, and that
u\in C^{1,h}_{H}(D)
(resp.
u\in C^{1,h}_{H,loc}(D)
) if
\mathscr{B}u\in C^{0,h}(D)
(resp.
\mathscr{B}u\in C^{0,h}_{loc}(D)
).

In this paper, we address the following question:

(PP)

Given a bounded
open set
D\subset\mathbb{W}
and a function
\varphi:\partial D\to\mathbb{R}
,
can one find a function
u\in C(\bar{D})
such that
u|_{\partial D}=\varphi
and
\mathscr{A}_{H}(u)
is minimal among
a suitable class of competitors satisfying the same boundary condition as
u
?

We will provide an affirmative answer to this version of the Plateau’s Problem
for a large class of convex domains
D
and for Lipschitz boundary data
\varphi:\partial D\to\mathbb{R}
, also proving some regularity for the minimizer.
The issue about the “suitable class of competitors” is delicate and will be clarified later.
We are able to prove existence under a
smallness
condition that jointly involves

\partial D
and the boundary datum
\varphi
. Our result is largely based on an interpolation
of the boundary datum
via straight horizontal segments, see Theorem
2.1
below.

Let us fix the interval
I=(0,\bar{t})\subset\mathbb{R}
, for some
\bar{t}>0
.
We consider domains
D\subset\mathbb{W}
of the form

(1.2)

D=D_{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}}:=\{(y,t)\in\mathbb{W}\,:\,t\in I,\,\gamma_{1}(t)<y<\gamma_{2}(t)\},

where
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\mathrm{Lip}(I)
are such that

(1.3)

\gamma_{1}(t)<0<\gamma_{2}(t)\hskip 11.74988pt\text{for all $t\in I$}\hskip 23.49976pt\text{and}\hskip 23.49976pt\gamma_{1}(0)=\gamma_{1}(\bar{t})=\gamma_{2}(0)=\gamma_{2}(\bar{t})=0.

The domain
D=D_{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}}
is convex if and only if
\gamma_{1}
is convex and
\gamma_{2}
is concave in
I
.
Notice that
D
is not allowed to be a disk, because in this case
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\notin\mathrm{Lip}(I)
.

Given a continuous function
\varphi:\partial D\to\mathbb{R}
, we define the compositions

(1.4)

\varphi_{1}(s):=\varphi(\gamma_{1}(s),s)\hskip 23.49976pt\text{and}\hskip 23.49976pt\varphi_{2}(s):=\varphi(\gamma_{2}(s),s),\hskip 23.49976pts\in I.

We also consider the mappings

(1.5)

\displaystyle\bar{I}\ni s\mapsto p_{1}(s):=\Phi_{\varphi_{1}(s)}(\gamma_{1}(s),s)=\big(\varphi_{1}(s),\gamma_{1}(s),s+2\gamma_{1}(s)\varphi_{1}(s)\big),

(1.6)

\displaystyle\bar{I}\ni s\mapsto p_{2}(s):=\Phi_{\varphi_{2}(s)}(\gamma_{2}(s),s)=\big(\varphi_{2}(s),\gamma_{2}(s),s+2\gamma_{2}(s)\varphi_{2}(s)\big).

The supports of
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
form the intrinsic graph of
\varphi
, i.e.,
p_{1}(\bar{I})\cup p_{2}(\bar{I})=S_{\varphi}
.
With a slight abuse of notation, we adopt the following conventions:

\displaystyle\|\gamma\|_{\infty}:=\max\{\|\gamma_{1}\|_{\infty},\|\gamma_{2}\|_{\infty}\},\hskip 23.49976pt\,\,\mathrm{Lip}(\gamma):=\max\{\mathrm{Lip}(\gamma_{1}),\mathrm{Lip}(\gamma_{2})\},

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{\infty}:=\max\{\|\varphi_{1}\|_{\infty},\|\varphi_{2}\|_{\infty}\},\hskip 23.49976pt\mathrm{Lip}(\varphi):=\max\{\mathrm{Lip}(\varphi_{1}),\mathrm{Lip}(\varphi_{2})\},

and we denote by
\zeta=\zeta_{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}}
the following parameter,
which will accompany us throughout this work:

(1.7)

\zeta:=4\big(\|\gamma\|_{\infty}+\mathrm{Lip}(\gamma)\big)\big(\|\varphi\|_{\infty}+\mathrm{Lip}(\varphi)\big).

The first step in the construction of the solution to the Plateau Problem (
PP
)
is the following result:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\mathrm{Lip}(I)
be as in (
1.3
),
D=D_{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}}\subset\mathbb{W}
as in (
1.2
),

\varphi\in C(\partial D)
be with
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in\mathrm{Lip}(I)
as in (
1.4
), and let
\zeta
be the parameter in (
1.7
).
Then we have:

(i)

If
\zeta<1
, there exists
\varrho\in\mathrm{Lip}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{3})
,
Q:=I\times(0,1)
, such that:

(1.8)

\displaystyle\varrho(\partial Q)

\displaystyle=p_{1}(\bar{I})\cup p_{2}(\bar{I})=S_{\varphi};

(1.9)

\displaystyle\varrho(h,s)

\displaystyle=(1-h)\varrho(0,s)+h\varrho(1,s),\hskip 23.49976pt\text{\quad\text{for $(h,s)\in Q$;}}

(1.10)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial h}\varrho(h,s)

\displaystyle=\varrho(1,s)-\varrho(0,s)\in H_{\varrho(h,s)},\hskip 23.49976pt\,\,\,\,\text{for $(h,s)\in Q$.}

In particular, the parametric surface
R_{\varphi}:=\varrho(Q)
is foliated
by straight horizontal segments spanned by
S_{\varphi}
.

(ii)

If
\zeta<(\sqrt{129}-11)/4
and
D
is convex, there exists
u\in\mathrm{Lip}(D)\cap C^{1,1}_{H}(D)
such that:

(1.11)

u=\varphi\hskip 11.74988pt\text{on $\partial D$}\hskip 23.49976pt\text{and}\hskip 23.49976pt\text{$S_{u}=R_{\varphi}$.}

In addition, if
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in C^{k}(I)
, for some
k\geq 1
, then
u\in C^{k}(D)
.

The surface
R_{\varphi}
—the horizontally ruled surface spanned by
\varphi
—is the candidate solution to the Plateau’s Problem (
PP
).
The fact that it is a minimizer of the area functional
\mathscr{A}_{H}
in a suitable class of competitors will be proved by a calibration argument.

While
R_{\varphi}=S_{u}
is a left intrinsic graph, the calibration is constructed using the right-invariant structure of
\mathbb{H}^{1}
.
We consider the flow
\Phi^{r}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{H}^{1}\to\mathbb{H}^{1}
of the right-invariant vector field
X^{r}
defined, at each point
p=(x,y,t)\in\mathbb{H}^{1}
, as:

X^{r}(p)=\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x}-2y\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}.

We let
\Phi^{r}_{s}(p)=\Phi^{r}(s,p)
for
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
p\in\mathbb{H}^{1}
, and we consider the right cylinder over
a domain
D^{r}\subset\mathbb{W}
,

\mathbb{V}\cdot D^{r}:=\{\Phi^{r}_{s}(y,t)=(s,y,t-2ys)\in\mathbb{H}^{1}\,:\,(y,t)\in D^{r},\,s\in\mathbb{R}\}.

The right graph and epigraph of a function
v:D^{r}\to\mathbb{R}
are defined as:

\begin{split}S^{r}_{v}&=\big\{\Phi_{s}^{r}(y,t)\in\mathbb{H}^{1}\,:\,(y,t)\in D^{r},\,s=v(y,t)\big\},\\
E^{r}_{v}&=\big\{\Phi_{s}^{r}(y,t)\in\mathbb{H}^{1}\,:\,(y,t)\in D^{r},\,s>v(y,t)\big\}.\end{split}

The calibration can be constructed in the cylinder
\mathbb{V}\cdot D^{r}
thanks to the following:

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
u\in\mathrm{Lip}(D)
and
S_{u}=R_{\varphi}
be given by
(i)
and
(ii)
in Theorem
1.1
for a convex domain
D=D_{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}}\subset\mathbb{W}
.
If
\zeta<(\sqrt{721}-25)/48
, then there exists an open set
D^{r}\subset\mathbb{W}
and
u^{r}\in\mathrm{Lip}_{loc}(D^{r})
such that
S_{u}=S_{u^{r}}^{r}
.

Namely, for small
\zeta
the left intrinsic graph
S_{u}
is also a right intrinsic graph over a suitable domain
D^{r}\subset\mathbb{W}
.

Now consider the measure theoretic inner normal
\nu_{E_{u}}=\omega_{1}X+\omega_{2}Y
: this is the unit horizontal vector field, defined for
\mathscr{H}^{2}
-a.e.
p\in R_{\varphi}=S_{u}
, such that for all
V=v_{1}X+v_{2}Y\in C_{c}^{1}(D\cdot\mathbb{V};H)
there holds

(1.12)

\int_{D\cdot\mathbb{V}}\div Vd{\mathscr{L}}^{3}=-\int_{S_{u}}(\omega_{1}v_{1}+\omega_{2}v_{2})d\mu_{E_{u}},

where
\mu_{E_{u}}
is the
H
-perimeter measure associated with the epigraph
E_{u}
.
The existence of
\nu_{E_{u}}
is given by Riesz’ representation theorem and its regularity follows from the regularity of
S_{u}
.

The
0
-homogeneous extension of
\nu_{E_{u}}
along the integral lines of
X^{r}
defines a unit horizontal vector field
\omega=\omega_{1}X+\omega_{2}Y
in the right cylinder
\mathbb{V}\cdot D^{r}
such that
\omega=\nu_{E_{u}}
on
S_{u}
and

(1.13)

\div\omega(p)=X\omega_{1}(p)+Y\omega_{2}(p)=0,\hskip 23.49976pt\textrm{for ${\mathscr{L}}^{3}$-a.e.~$p\in\mathbb{V}\cdot D^{r}$}.

This follows from the fact that the diffeomorphisms
\Phi^{r}_{s}:\mathbb{H}^{1}\to\mathbb{H}^{1}
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
, are contact and isometric, and from the ruled property of
S_{u}
.

The vector field
\omega
calibrates
S_{u}
in
\mathbb{V}\cdot D^{r}
in the sense of Barone Adesi-Serra Cassano-Vittone
[
2
]
, and
u
solves Plateau’s Problem (
PP
) in the following sense:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\mathrm{Lip}(I)
be as in (
1.3
), and let
D=D_{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}}\subset\mathbb{W}
be convex,
let
\varphi\in C(\partial D)
with
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in\mathrm{Lip}(I)
as in (
1.4
), and let the parameter
\zeta
in (
1.7
) be such that:

\zeta<\dfrac{\sqrt{721}-25}{48}.

Finally, let
D^{r}\subset\mathbb{W}
and
u^{r}
be as in Proposition
1.2
.
Then the function
u\in\mathrm{Lip}(D)
constructed in Theorem
1.1
satisfies the minimality property:

\mathscr{A}_{H}(u)=P_{H}(E_{u};D\cdot\mathbb{V})=P_{H}(E^{r}_{u^{r}};\mathbb{V}\cdot D^{r})\leq P_{H}(F;\mathbb{V}\cdot D^{r}),

for any
{\mathscr{L}}^{3}
-measurable set
F\subset\mathbb{H}^{1}
such that
E^{r}_{u^{r}}\Delta F\subset\!\subset\mathbb{V}\cdot D^{r}
.

It is natural to ask which conditions must be satisfied by an intrinsic Lipschitz map
v:D\to\mathbb{R}
in order to have
\mathscr{A}_{H}(u)\leq\mathscr{A}_{H}(v)
. In Lemma
4.3
, we provide a sufficient condition on
v
ensuring this property, namely that
v|_{\partial D}=\varphi
and that
S_{v}
is the right intrinsic graph of some
v^{r}:D^{r}\to\mathbb{R}
.
Despite this restriction on competitors,
u
minimizes
\mathscr{A}_{H}
in a sufficiently large class that includes compact variations of
u
. This ensures that
u
is stationary for
\mathscr{A}_{H}
(see Remark
4.4
).

In the final section, we show how the theory developed in this work can be used to obtain regularity results for minimizers of
\mathscr{A}_{H}
. In Theorem
5.1
, we prove that a minimizer is horizontally ruled and belongs to
\mathrm{Lip}(D)\cap C^{1,1}_{H}(D)
, starting from an initial regularity lower than Lipschitz. This result improves upon Theorem 3.7 in
[
16
]
by Giovannardi and Ritoré,
which concerns the broader setting of stationary points of
\mathscr{A}_{H}
(see also
[
13
]
).
More generally, most regularity results in the literature deal with Lipschitz stationary points of
\mathscr{A}_{H}
, see
[
4
,
5
]
. The motivation is, again, related to the non-regularity of
H
-minimal surfaces,
which prevents the hope of proving more general regularity results for stationary points of
\mathscr{A}_{H}
. An exception is
[
23
]
, where the author addresses the problem of approximating the
H
-area of intrinsic Lipschitz graphs with small intrinsic gradient by means of a suitable intrinsic Dirichlet energy, with the ultimate goal of reproducing the Euclidean approach to studying regularity.

1.1.
Organization of the paper.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, and in particular in Theorem
2.1
, we construct a horizontally ruled parametric surface spanned by a given boundary datum. In Section
3
, we show that this parametric surface is both a left intrinsic graph (Theorem
3.1
) and a right intrinsic graph (Theorem
3.4
). Section
4
contains the calibration argument, which allows us to demonstrate that this surface is area-minimizing with respect to a suitable class of competitors, see Theorem
4.2
. Finally, Section
5
is devoted to the proof of Theorem
5.1
, in which we establish a new regularity result for minimizers of
\mathscr{A}_{H}
.

2.
Horizontal interpolation of a boundary datum

In this section, we construct a horizontally ruled parametric surface spanned by a Lipschitz continuous boundary
datum defined on the boundary
\partial D
of an open domain
D\subset\mathbb{W}
of the form

D=D_{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}}:=\{(y,t)\in\mathbb{W}\,:\,t\in I,\,\gamma_{1}(t)<y<\gamma_{2}(t)\}.

The notations are those given in the Introduction.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
D=D_{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}}\subset\mathbb{W}
and
\varphi:\partial D\to\mathbb{R}
be such that:

(2.1)

\zeta<1.

Then there exists a bi-Lipschitz, increasing function
\lambda:\bar{I}\to\bar{I}
such that:

\displaystyle(i)\,\,\,

\displaystyle p_{2}(\lambda(s))-p_{1}(s)\in H_{p_{1}(s),}\hskip 23.49976pt\text{for all $s\in\bar{I}$,}

\displaystyle(ii)\,\,\,

\displaystyle p_{2}(\lambda^{\prime})-p_{1}(s)\in H_{p_{1}(s)}\text{ implies }\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda(s),\hskip 23.49976pt\text{for all $s,\lambda^{\prime}\in\bar{I}$,}

(2.2)

\displaystyle(iii)\,\,\,

\displaystyle\lambda(0)=0\hskip 11.74988pt\text{and}\hskip 11.74988pt\lambda(\bar{t})=\bar{t},

\displaystyle(iv)\,\,\,

\displaystyle\dfrac{1-\zeta}{1+\zeta}\leq\mathrm{Lip}(\lambda)\leq\dfrac{1+\zeta}{1-\zeta}\hskip 11.74988pt\text{and}\hskip 11.74988pt\dfrac{1-\zeta}{1+\zeta}\leq\mathrm{Lip}(\lambda^{-1})\leq\dfrac{1+\zeta}{1-\zeta}.

Proof.

We claim that, for any
s\in\bar{I}
, there exists
\lambda\in\bar{I}
such that:

(2.3)

p_{2}(\lambda)-p_{1}(s)\in H_{p_{1}(s)}.

Recall that, for
s\in\bar{I}
,
H_{p_{1}(s)}
expresses the horizontal distribution at
p_{1}(s)
, that is, the linear space spanned by

X(p_{1}(s))=(1,\,0,\,2\gamma_{1}(s))\hskip 23.49976pt\text{and}\hskip 23.49976ptY(p_{1}(s))=(0,\,1,\,-2\varphi_{1}(s)).

Then
H_{p_{1}(s)}
is uniquely determined by the Cartesian equation

(2.4)

2\gamma_{1}(s)x-2\varphi_{1}(s)y-t=0,

and, by
the definition of
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
, we have

p_{2}(\lambda)-p_{1}(s)=(\varphi_{2}(\lambda)-\varphi_{1}(s),\,\gamma_{2}(\lambda)-\gamma_{1}(s),\,\lambda+2\gamma_{2}(\lambda)\varphi_{2}(\lambda)-s-2\gamma_{1}(s)\varphi_{1}(s)).

Thus, by (
2.4
), condition (
2.3
) holds if and only if
\lambda\in\bar{I}
is a solution to the equation:

(2.5)

\mathcal{Q}_{\varphi}(s,\lambda):=\lambda-s+2(\gamma_{2}(\lambda)-\gamma_{1}(s))(\varphi_{2}(\lambda)+\varphi_{1}(s))=0.

If
s=0
or
s=\bar{t}
, the solution is
\lambda=0
or
\lambda=\bar{t}
, respectively.
When
s\in I
, we argue by continuity.
Note that:

(2.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\varphi}(s,0)

\displaystyle=-s-2\gamma_{1}(s)(\varphi(0,0)+\varphi_{1}(s)),

(2.7)

\displaystyle\hskip 11.74988pt\mathcal{Q}_{\varphi}(s,\bar{t})

\displaystyle=\bar{t}-s-2\gamma_{1}(s)(\varphi(0,\bar{t})+\varphi_{1}(s)).

Since
\gamma_{1}(0)=\gamma_{1}(\bar{t})=0
, we deduce that

(2.8)

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be two open bounded sets and
F_{j},F:\overline{\Omega}_{1}\to\overline{\Omega}_{2}
,
j\geq 1
, be such that:

(i)

\sup_{j\geq 1}\mathrm{Lip}(F_{j},\Omega_{1})<\infty
;

(ii)

F|_{\Omega_{1}}
,
F_{j}|_{\Omega_{1}}
are invertible,
j\geq 1
;

(iii)

F_{j}\to F
pointwise in
\Omega_{1}
;

(iv)

F_{j}(\partial\Omega_{1})\subset\partial\Omega_{2}
,
F_{j}(\Omega_{1})=\Omega_{2}
,
j\geq 1
, and
F(\Omega_{1})=\Omega_{2}
.

Then
F_{j}^{-1}\to F^{-1}
pointwise in
\Omega_{2}
.

Proof.

Given
y\in\Omega_{2}
, let
x_{j}:=F_{j}^{-1}(y)
. Up to subsequences,
x_{j}\to x
, for some
x\in\overline{\Omega}_{1}
.
If

, then we would have

(3.52)

y=F_{j}(x_{j})=F_{j}(x_{j})-F_{j}(x)+F_{j}(x),

where, by
(iv)
,

, and

, contradicting

. Hence,

, and by

. Then we have

\displaystyle|F_{j}(x_{j})-F(x)|

\displaystyle\leq|F_{j}(x_{j})-F_{j}(x)|+|F_{j}(x)-F(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{j\geq 1}\mathrm{Lip}(F_{j},\Omega_{1})|x-x_{j}|+|F_{j}(x)-F(x)|\to 0.

Consequently,

. This proves that the sequence

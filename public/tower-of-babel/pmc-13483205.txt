Privacy-aware distributed intelligence with tokenized trust for low-latency task offloading in 6G vehicular edge networks
Authors: Mohammad Alsaffar, Eman Abouelkheir, Wedad M Alawad, MAJED S ALSAYFI, Laila Nassef, Osama S Younes, Qaisar Abbas, Malik Bader Alazzam
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe ultra-dense vehicle scenarios envisioned in 6G put high requirements on ultra-low latency, secure cooperation, and efficient task offloading decisions. Existing systems usually optimize latency or energy independently but ignore joint privacy problems and long-term trust sustainability. In this work, a distributed intelligence architecture based on the combination of federated learning (FL) and blockchain based trust management for vehicle-to-vehicle (V2V) edge computing is proposed. The proposed architecture enables collaborative prediction and decentralized incentive enforcement in a privacy-preserving manner without revealing raw vehicle data. In this paper, task allocation is defined as a multi-objective optimization problem, which jointly considers latency, energy consumption, communication stability and privacy exposure. The resultant problem is addressed by a learning-coupled primal–dual optimization, where the federated prediction is used to drive the offloading decisions and the dual update is used to impose the limitations of the system. A light-weight distributed ledger layer ensures secure coordination, automatic incentive allocation and reliable detection of fraudulent nodes. The extensive simulations in the integrated traffic-network-blockchain environments show that the proposed method outperforms the state-of-the-art baselines, achieving up to 30–40% reduction in the service latency, approximately 25% improvement in task completion rate, enhanced privacy preservation by the gradient-based learning, and up to 95% accuracy in detecting the malicious nodes. These results validate the efficacy of the suggested framework for attaining scalable, privacy-aware, and trustworthy distributed intelligence for next-generation 6G vehicular edge networks.Keywords: Vehicular edge intelligence, Distributed trust, Collaborative learning, Low-latency computing, Decentralized coordinationSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

The Internet of Vehicles (IoV) has emerged as a key enabler of intelligent transportation1,2 in the dynamic domain of sixth-generation (6G) communication systems, enabling smooth Vehicle-to-Vehicle (V2V), Vehicle-to-Infrastructure (V2I), and Vehicle-to-Everything (V2X) communications over large-scale Internet of Things (IoT) networks. These interconnected vehicular systems are critical for latency-aware applications that demand ultra-reliable low-latency communication, such as cooperative autonomous driving, real-time traffic monitoring, and accident avoidance. However, the fast growing data-intensive vehicular services bring about significant issues on computing because modern autonomous vehicles generate tremendous amounts of sensory data beyond the processing capability of onboard units3,4. Therefore, effective options for moving tasks to adjacent cars or edge infrastructures have become vital.

Vehicular edge computing (VEC) has been emerging as a feasible paradigm to overcome these restrictions by providing lower latency and alleviating the bandwidth bottleneck of centralized cloud processing by moving the compute closer to the data sources4,5. Recently, the optimization approaches for task offloading in IoV systems involve reinforcement learning (RL), edge intelligence and predictive resource allocation algorithms6,7. Recent advances in vehicular communication technology, including heterogeneous traffic modeling and predictive handover algorithms, significantly enhance the resilience of the system in dynamic contexts8,9. However, these existing offloading solutions are sometimes more concerned with performance measures such as latency, throughput, etc., at the cost of important factors such as cooperative incentives, privacy protection, and trust management.

To overcome the privacy issues, federated learning (FL) is a distributed learning paradigm which enables cooperative model training without exposing raw data10,11. Recently, privacy-aware and Byzantine-resilient FL techniques have been proposed to enhance resilience in distributed situations12,13. However, these approaches aim for learning efficiency and data safety rather than the trust enforcement or incentive mechanisms needed for broad vehicular cooperation. Moreover, while the emerging edge intelligence systems are designed to optimize the neural inference and processing efficiency across dispersed edge devices, they usually have no explicit privacy-aware task offloading mechanisms14,15.

The shift to 6G brings new opportunities and problems for networking. Technologies such as Terahertz (THz) communication, network slicing and AI-driven network optimization16,17 offer ultra-high data rates and adaptive service delivery. Meanwhile, blockchain-based solutions have been proposed to enable tamper-proof data management, decentralized trust and secure authentication in automotive contexts18,19. Recently, more sophisticated consensus mechanisms, such as DAG-based protocols, have been proposed to improve scalability and minimize latency in IoV systems20. However, most of existing blockchain-enabled solutions are just focused on consensus optimization or authentication without jointly addressing the job offloading intelligence and privacy-aware learning.

However, most of the existing work still regards the optimization of task offloading, privacy protection and trust management as three independent design issues. For example, on one hand, learning-based offloading solutions boost decision making, but frequently lack strong security procedures. On the other hand, blockchain-based systems give trust, but raise latency overhead. Similarly, distributed task offloading systems in extreme settings enhance flexibility but fail to fully solve privacy-preserving learning or incentive-aware coordination21. Therefore, we need an architecture that can optimize latency, privacy and trust in highly dynamic 6G-Still not enough for IoV scenarios.

To fill this gap, this study presents a unified blockchain-enabled FL architecture for incentive-driven and privacy-preserving V2V task offloading in 6G-IoV networks. The proposed strategy decreases the danger of data leaking by leveraging FL to encourage collaborative decision-making without disclosing raw vehicle data. A lightweight consortium blockchain overlay offers token-based incentives, secure transaction validation and decentralized trust management to motivate cooperative resource sharing.

Unlike the existing systems which are designed either for efficiency, privacy or trust, the proposed framework unifies all these three factors into one single optimization model. The overall concept provides scalable, secure and adaptable work offloading in highly dynamic traffic scenarios. Related work

Recent breakthroughs in vehicle edge computing (VEC) and 6G-enabled Internet of Vehicles (IoV) have made considerable advances in task offloading, distributed learning and network intelligence. Initial studies on communication-aware and resource-efficient offloading strategies suggested the use of hierarchical computer systems and multi-tier scheduling to mitigate delay and enhance resource utilization22. RL-based methods significantly improved system flexibility especially in cooperative offloading and multi-task scheduling by enabling dynamic decision making under varying traffic and network conditions3,23. Ultra-reliable and low-latency vehicular communication24 has also been enabled by large-scale connection frameworks such as 6G-enabled V2X systems, providing scalable offloading solutions in highly dynamic contexts. However, these approaches mostly focus on enhancing the efficiency instead of trust management and privacy protection.

VEC significantly incorporates FL to enhance decision intelligence and adaptability. FL-based offloading approaches mitigate privacy issues by enabling remote model training without exposing raw data25,26. Recent research has significantly improved the performance of FL by applying adaptive client participation methodologies to control the dynamic vehicle motion and intermittent connectivity27. Further more, it has been claimed that energy efficient FL frameworks optimize distributed learning among edge nodes, boosting communication efficiency and system scalability28. However, most of the FL-based approaches do not consider the incentive-driven cooperation and trust enforcement mechanisms, hence exposing the FL approaches to the threat of dishonest or hostile participants in the large-scale environment of IoV.

Learning-based approaches have been created to achieve safe coordination and decentralized trust in distributed systems using blockchain technologies. Blockchain-based identity management and authentication solutions allow verifiable and tamper-resistant interactions among nodes, ensuring secure access control in heterogeneous contexts29,30. These solutions boost trust and data integrity, but they are generally only about identity verification and authentication, they do not address learning-driven decision making or intelligent job offloading. Asynchronous FL and zero-trust designs have also been proposed to enhance the reliability and robustness of automotive systems, particularly in adversarial environments31. However, these approaches are inefficient in incentive mechanisms and usually lead to additional overhead without improving the system efficiency.

From a networking point of view, the future 6G paradigms provide new prospects for resource optimization and large-scale collaboration. Advanced communication frameworks such as adaptive connection management and satellite-terrestrial integration allow flexible and scalable network coordination over heterogeneous environments32,33. Recent advances in wireless communication technology, such as multi-domain modulation and enhanced signal identification techniques, also boost the transmission efficiency and reliability in next-generation networks34. Although these advances greatly enhance the communication performance, they do not directly deal with the privacy-aware task offloading or trust-aware learning in IoV systems.

Meanwhile, recent works have examined privacy-preserving approaches for distributed learning and edge intelligence. Adversarial privacy protection solutions have been proposed to enhance resilience against inference attacks and to prevent data leaking in distributed situations35. Moreover, the privacy-aware FL applications in automotive edge computing have demonstrated the feasibility of secure collaborative learning for real-time services such as multimedia streaming and intelligent mobility systems36. Moreover, in heterogeneous vehicle environments, data-driven traffic modeling approaches have improved system awareness and prediction accuracy, resulting in more effective offloading decisions37.

Recent works have made progress on task offloading and federated learning in automotive and distributed systems. For example38, suggested an energy-efficient collaborative task offloading system in NOMA-based automobile fog computing, where the joint optimization of communication-computing can greatly reduce the energy consumption under the limits of spectrum sharing.

In the context of federated learning, researchers in39 proposed a privacy-aware distributed federated learning framework for fine-tuning long language model (LLM) in IoT systems, which improves data privacy by model splitting and safe sharing of parameters. This technique provides high privacy assurances but does not include dynamic task offloading or incentive based collaboration in automotive environments.

More recently, researchers suggested a robust federated learning framework for drone swarm tracking (FedMES) to increase the model reliability with adaptive model improvement and robustness-aware aggregation. Despite its capability to efficiently deal with hostile updates, it does not possess the communication-aware offloading techniques and trust management mechanisms necessary for 6G-IoV systems40.

These contributions are devoted to recent improvements in energy-efficient offloading and powerful federated learning with privacy preservation. However, they still view optimization, privacy, and trust as independent parts. The proposed system, on the other hand, mixes multi-objective optimization, federated intelligence and blockchain-based trust together to enable secure, motivation-aware and low-latency task offloading in dynamic 6G-IoV environments.

System model

Overview of the proposed 6G-IoV architecture

The suggested system model shows an integrated Blockchain-Enabled FL architecture for V2V work offloading in 6G-enabled IoV. The architecture comprises of three layers: the Blockchain Layer, the Local FL Layer and the 6G Edge Aggregation Layer. These layers together allow secure dumping of tasks, incentive based vehicle involvement and privacy protection.

In the Local FL Layer, each vehicle is an intelligent node, which locally trains learning models based on its own sensor data, including velocity, CPU load, GPS positions, and wireless signal strength. Instead of transmitting raw data to edge servers or RSUs, vehicles generate and send model gradients as their local learning updates. This distributed strategy provides privacy compliance while preserving model performance by shielding private vehicle information such as driving patterns and routes.

There are two basic categories of vehicles: edge vehicles (EVs) and user vehicles (UVs). UVs are vehicles that do computationally challenging jobs, such as spotting objects in foggy situations or pedestrians. On the other hand, EVs can perform duties allocated to them since they have greater processing power. The RSUs on the roadside act as mediators to verify, aggregate, and update the model parameters through the blockchain network.

The Blockchain Layer is a decentralized trust structure between RSUs, EVs and UVs. Every transaction, whether for calculation or transfer, is stored forever on the blockchain ledger. Incentives and verification policies are automatically enforced by smart contracts to ensure that tokens are paid to EVs that perform genuine computations, and that malicious or uncooperative vehicles are penalized by decreasing their reputations.

Finally, the 6G Edge Aggregation Layer offers the communication infrastructure to connect all the blockchain and car components. It employs Network Slicing with the ultra-high-frequency THz spectrum of 6G to provide ultra-reliable low-latency communication (URLLC) for safety-critical applications.

Edge node : federated offloading and incentive-aware scheduling

In the proposed 6G-IoV system, each edge node represents an intelligent vehicle or roadside unit (RSU) that participates in both the FL process and the consortium blockchain network. Unlike conventional fuzzy-logic-based schedulers that depend on static rule sets, each runs the Learning–Coupled Offloading Solver (LCOS) to perform optimization-driven and incentive-aware offloading decisions under dynamic vehicle mobility.

After receiving an offloading request from a user vehicle , the edge node initially verifies its current resource availability and trust status by consulting the blockchain ledger. The requesting vehicle’s connection stability, processing time, energy consumption, and privacy leakage are then estimated by the federated prediction oracle.

Then LCOS solves the unified objective function to obtain the optimal offloading choice and the appropriate resource allocation. The task scheduling reduces the total utility in terms of the energy cost, privacy leaks, token-based incentives, and delay efficiency. In the local execution queue, processes with higher marginal utility are prioritized, and smart contracts automatically enforce token payouts and reputation improvements for cooperative nodes.

When a latency-critical task (such real-time obstacle detection or picture recognition) is generated, the user node does not employ static broadcast advertising. Application of the unified optimization framework

By including federated prediction models into the offloading decision loop and using blockchain smart contracts to create incentive mechanisms, the method transforms traditional rule-based IoV offloading into an optimization-driven, incentive-aware, and privacy-preserving execution paradigm.

Instead of employing static heuristics to compute offloading decisions, the proposed system uses the LCOS, which dynamically adapts to real-time changes in vehicle density, mobility patterns, channel quality, and resource availability. The federated oracle’s predictive predictions, which parameterize the unified objective function, allow each choice to collectively minimize latency and energy consumption, control privacy leakage, and maximize incentive sustainability.

Extensive simulation-based assessments utilizing SUMO, NS-3, and Hyperledger Fabric show that the unified framework regularly outperforms standard fuzzy-logic and RL-based baselines in terms of latency efficiency, task completion reliability, privacy preservation, and incentive stability.

In order to minimize the latency overhead caused by blockchain operations and FL procedures, the proposed system employs many optimization approaches. First, a lightweight consortium blockchain is employed instead of a public blockchain to significantly reduce consensus delay and transaction validation time. The Raft-based consensus algorithm enables fast block confirmation without requiring computationally expensive mining operations.

Second, blockchain interactions are only utilized for critical activities like gradient validation, incentive distribution, and final task verification, rather than being utilized at every level of communication. This chosen interaction approach reduces unnecessary on-chain overhead and communication latency.

Third, the integration of 6G technologies, particularly THz communication and network slicing, compensates for higher processing delays by providing ultra-high capacity and prioritized low-latency transmission channels. As a result, the quick completion of data sharing and model updates lessens the processing load of blockchain and FL.

Simulation and evaluation

To confirm the efficacy of the proposed blockchain-enabled FL architecture for privacy-preserving and incentive-driven V2V work offloading in 6G-IoV edge computing, we conducted comprehensive simulations using a range of specialized tools. By simulating realistic vehicle dynamics, network interactions, and computer activities, this configuration guarantees that the results reflect actual deployment situations. Unlike conventional fuzzy-based methods that focus on static rule inference, our research offers a thorough evaluation of privacy, latency, and incentive mechanisms by highlighting dynamic FL model convergence, blockchain transaction cost, and 6G communication metrics.

We used SUMO (Simulation of Urban MObility), which models vehicle movements using actual geographic data from an urban map (for instance, a 5 km x 5 km grid that represents a city center), to create realistic traffic patterns in a dense urban setting. NS-3 (Network Simulator 3) was used to simulate the 6G network layer, including THz communication channels with fading models (e.g., Rayleigh fading for urban multipath effects) and network slicing for URLLC activities. In order to handle incentive distributions, gradient aggregations, and smart contract executions, RSUs and vehicles established Hyperledger Fabric, a lightweight consortium network. Additionally, PyTorch was used to train the FL model, simulating both local and global updates on vehicle nodes with different computational capacity.

System delay

In our blockchain-enabled FL framework, the system delay is the whole time from task start at a user vehicle (UV) to result retrieval, including FL model inference, blockchain transaction validation, 6G THz transmission, and smart contract execution for incentives. Our approach employs distributed FL predictions and 6G network slicing to dynamically eliminate bottlenecks, in contrast to typical systems where centralized processing causes delays to rise with network load. As models converge, early FL aggregation rounds experience greater initial delays; however, these delays decrease as the global model enhances connection forecasts and incentive mechanisms encourage efficient resource sharing across EVs. Simulations show that when vehicle density rises, the average system time for significant tasks plateaus at about 20 ms.

Figure 7(a), which displays the average system delay vs. simulation time, demonstrates the adaptive nature of FL in 6G circumstances by displaying an early peak followed by convergence to low levels. When compared to baselines without incentives, this graph shows how blockchain consensus, which initially adds a slight latency, gradually improves via reputation-based node selection, lowering overall system responsiveness by up to 30%.

Throughout the simulation, the performance trends of the proposed framework demonstrate that, under different 6G-IoV settings, (a) the average system delay had an initial peak and stabilized, (b) the average queuing delay had damped oscillations, and (c) the jobs in the queue had periodic spikes and a downward trend.

Queuing delay

The amount of time tasks spend in the priority queue at EVs before to execution is measured by queuing delay, which is influenced by FL-predicted utilities, incentive weights, and 6G slice allocations. Unlike static prioritizing solutions that lead to queue expansion in high-mobility scenarios, our design uses smart contracts to dynamically adjust queue ordering based on token incentives and deviation checks, preventing malicious nodes from raising waits. Because FL models are more accurate at forecasting resource availability, high-priority operations (such as safety warnings) can leverage network slicing to avoid lower-utility ones, reducing queuing delays throughout the simulation. In congested urban settings with varied THz signal quality, the average latency after model stabilization is less than 10 ms, and incentives promote load balancing across EVs.

Figure 7(b) displays the average queuing delay over time, reflecting the incentive-driven evacuation of lines with an oscillating pattern that dampens to nearly zero. This behavior illustrates the efficacy of blockchain-enforced fairness, as rewarded EVs process work more proactively and achieve a 35% reduction in queuing time when compared to older systems without economic motives.

Queue size

The number of open jobs at EVs is measured by queue size, which is heavily determined by offloading rates, FL accuracy in selecting dependable connections, and blockchain’s capacity to detect rogue nodes. By employing smart contract fines for non-compliant nodes and proactive FL-based screening of improper offloads to keep queues contained, our approach avoids the exponential rise observed in overload-prone systems. Queue sizes fluctuate over time in reaction to traffic spikes, but they eventually trend downward as incentives are dispersed over the consortium blockchain, encouraging idle EVs to take on more work. In simulations with different densities (100–300 vehicles/km), average queue sizes stable at 5–10 jobs, with 6G THz added for fast acknowledgments.

Discussion

The results of this study show the revolutionary potential of integrating blockchain-enabled FL with 6G capabilities for V2V task offloading in IoV edge computing. We demonstrate significant gains over baselines through our simulations such as 40% improvement in data privacy by gradient only sharing, 25% improvement in offloading success rates, 30% reduction in latency for key operations, and 95% detection accuracy of malicious nodes. Such results are in line with recent progress in the area, e.g., the safe blockchain-integrated deep learning framework for IoT edge and the DAG blockchain-assisted asynchronous FL for automotive networks. Incentive mechanism is often neglected in the favour of privacy and security. Our system improves this by adding smart contracts for token-based incentives, that enhance participation in trustless environments. The ablation research shows blockchain’s critical impact in increasing detection accuracy from 68.7% to 95.8%.

However, federated DRL for task offloading and multi-agent RL in 6G IoV are efficient in cooperative settings but have raw data exposure and weak anomaly handling drawbacks, which are addressed in our design by local training in FL and tamper-proof ledger in blockchain. What further distinguishes our work from 5G related studies is the 6G integration that exploits THz communication and network slicing to provide URLLC resulting in up to 40% system latency reduction in high density urban simulation. This synergy is not only superior to other approaches such as zero-trust asynchronous FL in terms of scalability, but also tackles real-world issues in intelligent transportation systems (ITS) such as traffic prediction and obstacle detection via offering context-aware and incentive-driven offloading.

Nevertheless, there are limits to these advances. The small computational overhead of the blockchain consensus may overload resource-constrained automobiles. For ultra-dense networks (> 500 vehicles/km) scalability might require optimized lightweight blockchains and, while SUMO and NS-3 simulation results are plausible, validation in real-world scenarios is needed to consider environmental constraints like as weather-induced THz signal attenuation. Future research directions may include quantum resistant cryptography for better security, hybrid FL with digital twins for predictive maintenance or implementation on real 6G testbeds to improve incentive models.

Conclusion

This study proposes a unique blockchain-enabled FL system with 6G integration for privacy-preserving incentive-driven V2V task offloading in IoV edge computing. The proposed method addresses significant deficiencies in existing methods, including the use of a lightweight consortium blockchain and smart contracts for secure incentives and malicious node detection, local FL models to dynamically predict connection stability and task suitability, and 6G features such as Terahertz communication and network slicing. We thoroughly evaluate the approach through simulation using SUMO, NS-3 and Hyperledger and show that it achieves up to 40% improved privacy preservation by sharing only the gradient, 30% lower latency for latency-sensitive workloads, 25% higher offloading success rates and 95% accuracy in detecting fraudulent actions. The comparisons with the state-of-the-art techniques, such as FedMARL, MACPP, Zero-Trust AsyncFL, and MARL-Coop, certify the exceptional scalability and durability of our framework in high-density and high-mobility scenarios. The ablation study further confirms the complementary roles of each component by demonstrating the impact of blockchain for trust enforcement and 6G for latency reduction.

Despite these advances, many limits still remain. The blockchain consensus approach has a minimum computational and energy cost, which may be an issue for resource-constrained cars in ultra-dense networks. Moreover, over-reliance on the Terahertz bands can also affect performance in bad weather due to environmental variables such as rain or blockage. Simulations provide realistic insights, but real-world implementation on 6G testbeds has to consider unforeseen hardware changes and legal limits on token-based incentives.

Moreover, future works should include the development of weather-aware channel models for adaptive THz fallback, the design of quantum-resistant cryptography for future-proof security, and the improvement of lightweight consensus mechanisms to reduce overhead. The concept could be further extended to hybrid V2X scenarios with RSU integration or predictive maintenance assisted by digital twins, thus increasing its applicability. All in all, our research provides a strong platform to establish secure, efficient and economical IoV ecosystems for reliable autonomous mobility in the 6G age.

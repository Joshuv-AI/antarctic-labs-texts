Thank you for your valuable feedback. In the article “One year postintervention health-related physical fitness and quality of life among children and grown-ups with congenital heart disease : An analysis from a tertiary cardiac care hospital, India”[1] we have used PedsQL 3.0 cardiac module.[2] We have used direct scoring as it was easy to understand for both researchers and parents. Direct scoring clearly says that higher scores are associated with worse health-related quality of life. If we had used the scoring as reverse scoring, in 100 metric scoring after calculating direct scoring (as done by some other researchers), it would have been very difficult for us to get so many complicated scorings together.[3]

In the letter by Sharma et al.,[4] the authors have nicely said, this conceptual inversion does not change the direction of within-study change over time; we don’t think it hampers comparability with published PedsQL norms, disease-specific studies, and established minimal important differencesFor health-related physical fitness (HRPF), the choice of the FITNESSGRAM framework is another strength, as it is the most widely implemented criterion-referenced battery for health-related fitness in youth and is supported by a detailed reference guide and extensive validation work[56]For HRPF, we have used FITNESSGRAM in the present study. Several tasks were added or modified for children aged 2–5 years, as they were unable to perform the exercises listed in FITNESSGRAM. They are not directly comparable to the values above 5 years old, but they are comparable to their age-matched controlsWe used the standard FITNESSGRAM scoring chart that is based on age and sex appropriate ranges as stated in FITNESSGRAM healthy fitness zone. FITNESSGRAM protocols, age ranges, and cut points were adhered to for all components in children aged 5 and aboveFinally, age- and sex-matched controls were recruited “at both time points.” Since coronavirus infective disease 2019 (COVID-19) was prevalent at that time, and our center was a tertiary care center for COVID-19, it was not possible to get those same controls on follow up longitudinally. Hence, two independent control samples were usedFinally, we can say this study provides valuable data on postintervention recovery of HRPF and health-related quality of life in congenital heart disease. We could not use reverse scoring conventions for PedsQL and standardized FITNESSGRAM protocols in children under 5 years, as clearly stated in our main study protocol. That has not changed much about the comparability of its conclusions.

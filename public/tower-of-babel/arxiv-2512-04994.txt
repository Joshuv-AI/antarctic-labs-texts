Partial section I: α \alpha -recurrence and equivariant Lyapunov maps
Théo Marty

Abstract
This is the first article in a series that aims at classifying partial sections of flows, that is a general family of transverse surfaces. In this part, we deal with the dynamical aspect of the question. Given a flow on a compact manifold M M and a cohomology class α \alpha of rank 1, we give a criterion for the existence of an α \alpha -equivariant Lyapunov map on an Abelian covering of M M associated to α \alpha . One important aspect of the existence of such Lyapunov maps, and of the classification of partial sections, is a type of recurrence set relative to α \alpha . We describe how that set depends on α \alpha .

We fix a compact topological manifold

Let us summarize some of Fried’s ideas. Take a global cross-section

\varphi

Conversely, take a smooth map

For partial cross-sections, the picture is more complicated, one needs to consider the weaker condition

To express that property, we lift

\mathbb{Z}
-covering of

The proof utilizes Conley’s arguments, adapted to build equivariant Lyapunov functions. To classify partial cross-sections, we mostly need this theorem and a variation of it.

It is well known that global cross-sections of a flow span a convex open cone in cohomology, which is related to the unit ball of the Thurston norm of the ambient manifold. We upgrade this by characterizing the set of cohomology class of partial cross-sections.
For that, we characterize quasi-Lyapunov classes with real coefficients too.

Descripiton of the rank 1 cohomology.

The cohomology set

It is well-known that

Given a class

1.3

Lyapunov functions

Conley
[
1
,
2
]
developed the notion of (complete) Lyapunov maps in dynamical systems. Let us recall these notions and introduce a variation of them.

A continuous map
f\colon N\to\mathbb{R}
is
Lyapunov
if the three following properties are satisfied:
f
is constant on every recurrence chain, two distinct recurrence chains have distinct values, and
f
is decreasing along the flow outside the recurrent set.

Theorem 1.3
(Conley
[
1
]
).

Let
M
be a compact manifold. Any continuous flow on
M
admits a Lyapunov function.

We introduce the variations of this notion.
A continuous map
f\colon N\to\mathbb{R}
is called
pre-Lyapunov
if for any
x,y
in
N
,
x\rightsquigarrow y
implies
f(x)\geq f(y)
. It implies that
f
is constant on each recurrence chain. The function
f
is called
strongly-Lyapunov
if it is pre-Lyapunov, if distinct recurrence chains have distinct values, and if additionally
x\rightsquigarrow y
and
y\not\rightsquigarrow x
imply
f(x)>f(y)
.

We define pre-Lyapunov functions for flexibility reasons, and strongly-Lyapunov because we will work in a non-compact setting. We believe that being ”non-increasing” for the relation
\rightsquigarrow
is a valid enough property to add it to the conclusion of our results.

Lemma 1.4
.

A strongly-Lyapunov function is Lyapunov. The converse holds true when
N
is compact.

Take
M
compact,
\alpha
in
H^{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})
and
N=\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
. Then any
\alpha
-equivariant and Lyapunov map
f\colon\widehat{M}_{\alpha}\to\mathbb{R}
is strongly-Lyapunov.

The first claim follows from the definitions.
The converse is not true in general, without assumption on
N
, as illustrated in Example
1.8
.
We prove Lemma
1.4
after Lemma
1.7

Let us recall some important tools that were developed to prove Theorem
1.3

Given a subset

, denote by

the

of

\omega(X)=\bigcap_{t\geq 0}\overline{\psi_{[t,+\infty[}(X)}.

Similarly, the

-limit
of

-limit of the time-reversed flow.
A non-empty subset

of

. A

is an attractor of the time-reversed flow. Given an attractor

, we denote its domain of attraction by

. The set

There are at most countably many attractors, and for any pair of recurrence chains

Lemma 1.5

Let

Consider the following property on

: we have
f(x)\geq f(y)-C
whenever
x\rightsquigarrow y
is satisfied (without
\epsilon
). It is implied by being
C
-quasi-Lyapunov, and even equivalent when
\alpha
has integer coefficients. But in the general case, the second property is too weak for our applications.

In comparaision with Fried’s work
[
4
]
on global section, we have the following result. The sign difference is due to a convention difference.

Lemma 3.1
.

If a class
\alpha
in
H^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})
is quasi-Lyapunov, then
\alpha(D_{\varphi})\leq 0
holds.

This is a direct consequence of Lemma
3.6
stated further down.
For hyperbolic enough flow, the converse to Lemma
3.1
should be true, but it is not true in general.
Let us give two examples of 1-forms
\alpha
which satisfy
\alpha(D_{\varphi})\leq 0
but which are not quasi-Lyapunov.

Example 3.2
.

Take
n\geq 2
. Let
\varphi
be a linear flow on
M={\raisebox{1.02228pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\mathbb{R}^{n}$}}/\raisebox{-1.02228pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\mathbb{Z}^{n}$}}}
directed by some non-zero vector
X
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
\alpha
in
H^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})
be a class that contains
X
in its kernel. Then we have
D_{\varphi}=\{X\}
, so
\alpha(D_{\varphi})=0
holds. But
\alpha
is not quasi-Lyapunov, as one can build a periodic
\epsilon
-pseudo-orbit that only jumps in the positive direction of
\alpha
.

Example 3.3
.

Let
\varphi
be a flow on
{\raisebox{1.02228pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\mathbb{R}$}}/\raisebox{-1.02228pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\mathbb{Z}$}}}
that has one fixed points at
0
and which is increasing outside
0
. Let
\alpha
be the cohomology class of
dt
.

For any small
\epsilon>0
and any
n>0
, there is a periodic
\epsilon
-pseudo-orbits
\gamma
that turns
n
times around
{\raisebox{1.02228pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\mathbb{R}$}}/\raisebox{-1.02228pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\mathbb{Z}$}}}
, in the positive direction. Thus,
\alpha([\gamma])=n>0
holds. So
\alpha
is not quasi-Lyapunov.

Take a periodic
\epsilon
-pseudo-orbit
\gamma
that turns
n
times around
{\raisebox{1.02228pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\mathbb{R}$}}/\raisebox{-1.02228pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\mathbb{Z}$}}}
. It jumps
n
times across 0. So it spends a time at least
n\times t(\epsilon)
on the interval
]0,\tfrac{1}{2}]
, with
t(\epsilon)\xrightarrow[\epsilon\to 0]{}+\infty
. So
\tfrac{1}{\len(\gamma)}[\gamma]
goes to zero when
\epsilon
goes to zero. It follows that
D_{\varphi}
is reduced to
\{0\}
, so
\alpha(D_{\varphi})=0
holds.

Not all counterexamples satisfy
\alpha(D_{\varphi})=0
; this is an artifact of taking the simplest counterexamples.

3.1

Characterizations of quasi-Lyapunov classes

We give several characterizations of the quasi-Lyapunov property. Under a regularity assumption and with integer coefficients, they can be gathered into the following:

Theorem 3.4
.

Assume
M
smooth and
\varphi
generated by a
\mathcal{C}^{1}
vector field. For any continuous flow
\psi
close enough to
\varphi
, Given
\alpha
in
H^{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})\setminus\{0\}
, the following statement are equivalent:

i.

\alpha
is quasi-Lyapunov,

ii.

there exists a Lyapunov and
\alpha
-equivariant map
f\colon\widehat{M}_{\alpha}\to\mathbb{R}
,

iii.

we have
-\infty\not\rightsquigarrow+\infty
in
\overline{M}_{\alpha}

iv.

for any
T>0
, there exists
\epsilon>0
for which
\alpha(D_{\varphi,\alpha,\epsilon,T})\leq 0
holds,

v.

we have
\alpha(D_{\psi})\leq 0
.

The equivalence with
ii
is the subject of Section
4.1
. The other equivalences are consequences of the next results, each stated with less hypotheses.

Lemma 3.5
.

A class
\alpha
in
H^{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})\setminus\{0\}
is quasi-Lyapunov if and only if we have
-\infty\not\rightsquigarrow+\infty
for the flow on
\overline{M}_{\alpha}
, if and only we have
x\not\rightsquigarrow+\infty
for all
x
in
\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
.

Recall that the metric on
\overline{M}_{\alpha}
does not restrict to the one on
\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
. But for any compact
K
subsets on
\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
, the two metrics are comparable at small scale on
K
. So essentially, pseudo-orbits for one metrics are pseudo-orbits for the other inside compact subsets of
\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
.

Proof.
It is clear that if
x\not\rightsquigarrow+\infty
holds for some
x
, then
\alpha
is not quasi-Lyapunov.

Assume that
-\infty\rightsquigarrow+\infty
holds. Take a compact subset
K\subset\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
so that the projection
K\xrightarrow{\pi_{\alpha}}M
is surjective. Any sequence of
\epsilon
-pseudo-orbits from
-\infty
to
+\infty
, with
\epsilon
that goes to zero, accumulates on a point
x
in
K
that satisfies
x\rightsquigarrow+\infty
.

Assume now
\alpha
not quasi-Lyapunov. Take an
\alpha
-equivariant and continuous map
f\colon\widehat{M}_{\alpha}\to\mathbb{R}
. For any
C\geq 0
and
\epsilon>0
, there exists a
\epsilon
-pseudo-orbit that goes from a point
x
to a point
y
, with
f(y)\geq f(x)+3C
. Up to assuming
C
large enough, and replacing
x
and
y
by
n\cdot x
and
n\cdot y
for some
n
in
\mathbb{Z}
, we may assume that
f(y)\geq C
and
f(x)\leq C
hold. Taking
C
large enough, there exists a
\epsilon
-pseudo-orbit from
-\infty
to
+\infty
that jumps from
-\infty
to
x
, follows the previous
\epsilon
-pseudo-orbit, and jump back to
+\infty
. It follows that
-\infty\rightsquigarrow+\infty
holds.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

A class
\alpha
in
H^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})
is quasi-Lyapunov, if and only if we have
\alpha(D_{\varphi,\epsilon,T})\leq 0
for some/any
\epsilon>0
small enough.

Proof.
The case
\alpha=0
is clear, so we may assume
\alpha\neq 0
.
Let
f\colon\widehat{M}\to\mathbb{R}
be an
\alpha
-equivariant and continuous map.

Assume first that
\alpha
is quasi-Lyapunov. Take
\epsilon>0
and
C\geq 0
so that
f
is
C
-quasi-Lyapunov. Let
\gamma
be a periodic
\epsilon
-pseudo-orbit in
M
. In
\widehat{M}
,
\gamma
can be lifted into an
\epsilon
-pseudo-orbit
\gamma^{\prime}
that goes from a point
x
in
\widehat{M}
to
[\gamma]\cdot x
. Concatenating the pseudo-orbits
k[\gamma]\cdot\gamma^{\prime}
, for
0\leq k<n
in increasing order, yields an
\epsilon
-pseudo-orbit from
x
to
n[\gamma]\cdot x
. Since
f
is
\alpha
-equivariant, we have
f(n[\gamma]\cdot x)=f(x)+n\alpha([\gamma])
. And since it is bounded above by
f(x)+C
for all
n
, we have
\alpha([\gamma])\leq 0
. The inequality
\alpha(D_{\varphi,\epsilon,T})\leq 0
follows.

We prove the converse. Assume that
\alpha(D_{\varphi,\epsilon,T})\leq 0
holds for some
\epsilon>0
. Take
\epsilon^{\prime}>0
given by Lemma
2.2
.
Let
\gamma
be an
\epsilon^{\prime}
-pseudo-orbit in
\widehat{M}
from a point
x
to a point
y
. Then the image
\pi_{\alpha}(\gamma)
of
\gamma
in
M
is homologous, relatively to its endpoints, to an
\epsilon
-pseudo-orbit
\delta_{0}
of bounded length plus finitely many periodic
\epsilon
-pseudo-orbit
\delta_{1}\cdots\delta_{r}
. The pseudo-orbit
\delta_{0}
lifts inside
\widehat{M}
to a
\epsilon
-pseudo-orbit
\widehat{\delta}_{0}
, that goes from
x
to a point
y^{\prime}
.

We estimate
f(x)-f(y^{\prime})
and
f(y^{\prime})-f(y)
separately.
The boundary of the 1-chain
\gamma-\widehat{\delta}_{0}
is equal to
y-y^{\prime}
. So
y
is the image of
y^{\prime}
under the action of the homology class:

[\pi_{\alpha}(\gamma)]-[\delta_{0}]=\sum_{k\geq 1}[\delta_{k}].

Thus, we have
f(y)=f(y^{\prime})+\sum_{k\geq 1}\alpha([\delta_{k}])
.
The homology class of
\delta_{i}
belongs to
D_{\varphi,\epsilon,T}
so we have
\alpha([\delta_{k}])\leq 0
holds true. Thus,
f(y^{\prime})\geq f(y)
holds.
Since the length of
\widehat{\delta}_{0}
is bounded, the difference

is bounded by a constant

, independent on
\gamma
. It follows
f(x)\geq f(y)-C
holds. Hence,

is
C
-quasi-Lyapunov, and
\alpha
is quasi-Lyapunov.
∎

It has another consequence.

Corollary 3.7
.

Given

Given two map

\eta
-shadows

Proof.
We prove that the set of flows
\psi

be a sequence of flows that converges toward

For any

can be

∎

We give a last characterization, that is useful when considering generic properties of flows.

Proposition 3.8
.

Assume
M
smooth, and that
\varphi
is generated by a
\mathcal{C}^{1}
vector field. A class
\alpha
in

is quasi-Lyapunov for

if and only if for any continuous flow
\psi
on a
\mathcal{C}^{0}
-neighborhood of

\alpha(D_{\psi})\leq 0
holds.

The regularity assumptions are technical convenience and probably not optimal.

Proof.
We first assume that there exists a sequence of flows
\psi_{n}
that converges toward
\alpha
, and that satisfy
\alpha(D_{\psi_{n}})\not\leq 0
. For every

, take a periodic
\tfrac{1}{n}
-pseudo-orbit

of
\psi_{n}
that satisfies

. Fix some small

. For any

\gamma_{n}
can be
\epsilon
-shadowed by a
\epsilon
-pseudo-orbit
\delta
of
\varphi
. When
\epsilon
is small enough, it follows that

and

\alpha
is not quasi-Lyapunov.

We now assume that
\alpha
is not quasi-Lyapunov. For convenience, we may assume that the metric on

is a smooth Riemannian metric.

We shadow a pseudo-orbits with a variation of that notion. Denote by

the vector field that generates
\varphi
. Let

be a small neighborhood of the image of

in

. A curve

if it is continuous, piecewise

We claim that for any

for which any periodic

-pseudo-orbit

-shadowed by a periodic

-pseudo-orbit

. For that, take some

, some points

in

at distance less than
\epsilon
, and

. Denote by

a geodesic, at constant speed at most one, from

to

. Then the map

by:

\delta(s)=\varphi_{s-t}\circ c\left(\frac{s\epsilon}{t}\right)

\eta
-shadows the orbit arc
\varphi_{[0,t]}(z)
when

is small enough. Note that

Take a small neighborhood

and from the claim above, there exists a periodic

\alpha(\delta)>0
. Up to changing

close to the fixed points of
\varphi
, we may assume that
\delta
is locally injective.
We cut and past
\delta
to make it injective. Take two distinct times

in
{\raisebox{1.00006pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\mathbb{R}$}}/\raisebox{-1.00006pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$l\mathbb{Z}$}}}

. Denote by

the two closed intervals separated by

, and by

and

\alpha(\gamma_{|I^{\circ}})>0
.
Up to applying the previous procedure finitely many times, we may assume that
\delta

We build a flow
\psi

\varphi
, for which

is a periodic orbit. Since

Denote by
\psi
the generated flow. If

, Thus

can be taken arbitrarily close to

∎

3.2

The
\alpha
-recurrent set

Fix a class
\alpha
in
H^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})
, not necessarily quasi-Lyapunov. A point
x
in
M
is said
\alpha
-recurrent
if for every
\epsilon>0
small and every
T>0
, there exist a periodic
\epsilon
-pseudo-orbit
\gamma
that passes through
x
and that satisfy
\alpha([\gamma])=0
. We denote by
\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}
the set of
\alpha
-recurrent point, called
\alpha
-recurrent set
. Two
\alpha
-reccurent points
x,y
in
M
are said to be
\alpha
-equivalent if for all
\epsilon,T>0
there exists a periodic
\epsilon
-pseudo-orbit
\gamma
that passes through
x
and
y
and with
\alpha([\gamma])=0
. The
\alpha
-equivalence relation is an equivalence relation. We call
\alpha
-recurrence chains
the
\alpha
-equivalence classes. Note that the
\alpha
-equivariance chains are invariant by the flow.

Recall the covering map notation
\widehat{M}_{\alpha}\xrightarrow{\pi_{\alpha}}M
.
Given a recurrence chain
R
in
\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
,
\pi_{\alpha}(R)
is an
\alpha
-recurrence chain in
M
. Conversely, any
\alpha
-recurrence chain can be obtained that way.

We continue with some elementary properties on the
\alpha
-recurrent set.

Lemma 3.9
.

The
\alpha
-recurrence set and the
\alpha
-recurrence chains are closed inside
M
.

Proof.
Let
(x_{n})_{n}
be a sequence of
\alpha
-recurrent points that converges toward some point
y
in
M
.
Fix some small
\epsilon>0
. Take
n
very large,
\eta>0
very small, and
\gamma
a periodic
\eta
-pseudo-orbit with
\gamma(0)=x_{n}
and with
\alpha([\gamma])=0
. We may assume that the length of
\gamma
is at least
2T
. Take
t_{0}
in
[T,2T]
to be either equal to the first discontinuity time of
\gamma
if it is smaller than
2T
, or
t_{0}=T
if
\gamma
is continuous on
[0,2T]
. Define a pseudo-orbit
\delta
of the same length as
\gamma
, that satisfies
\delta(t)=\varphi_{t}(x)
for all

in
[0,t_{0}[
, and that coincide with
\gamma
outside
[0,t_{0}[
. If
n
is large enough and
\eta
is small enough,
\delta
is a periodic
\epsilon
-pseudo-orbit that satisfies
\alpha([\delta])=0
. So

is
\alpha
-recurrent.

The same argument, assuming all
x_{n}
to belong to the same
\alpha
-recurrence chain, shows that any
\alpha
-recurrence chain is closed.
∎

Lemma 3.10
.

The
\alpha
-recurrence chains are the connected components of the
\alpha
-recurrent set
\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}
.

Denote by
V_{\epsilon}(X)
the
\epsilon
-neighborhood of a subset
X\subset M
.

Proof.
Let
R
be an
\alpha
-recurrence chain, assumed to be not connected. It is the union of two disjoint open subsets
A,B
. Then
A
and
B
are closed in
R
, and even compact. Thus, they are separated by a positive distance
3\epsilon
. For
n
in
\mathbb{N}
, let
\gamma_{n}
be a periodic
\tfrac{1}{n}
-pseudo-orbits that passes through a point

in

. Any accumulation point of the sequence
\gamma_{n}
, when
n
goes to
+\infty
, is
\alpha
-equivariant to

, so it belongs to
R
. Thus, the
\gamma_{n}
do not accumulate outside

. Since
V_{\epsilon}(A)
and
V_{\epsilon}(B)
are disjoint, it follows that
\gamma_{n}
remains inside
V_{\epsilon}(A)
when
n
is large enough. It contradicts that
A
and
B
are
\alpha
-equivariant. Thus, any
\alpha
-recurrence chain is connected.

Let
A
be a connected component of
\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}
and
x_{1},x_{2}
be two points in
A
. Fix some small
\epsilon>0
. Since
A
is connected and since the
\epsilon
-balls are path connected, the set
V_{\epsilon}(A)
is path connected. It follows that there exists a sequence
y_{k}
in
V_{\epsilon}(A)
, for

, which satisfies

and
y_{p}=y
and that the distance between

and

is not more than
\epsilon
.
Denote by

a point in

at distance at most
\epsilon
from

, and by
\gamma_{k}
a periodic
\epsilon
-pseudo-orbit that start at

, and with
\alpha([\gamma_{k}])=0
. We may choose
z_{1}=x_{1}
and
z_{p}=x_{2}
. It follows from above than the distance between

and

is at most
3\epsilon
.

We describe a periodic
3\epsilon
-pseudo-orbit
\delta
that goes through

and

. We view
\gamma_{k}
as a (non-periodic)
\epsilon
-pseudo-orbit that starts and ends at

. The concatenation of the

, in increasing order, is a

-pseudo-orbit from

to

. Similarly, the concatenation of the
\gamma_{k}
, in decreasing order, is a

-pseudo-orbit from

to

. Denote by
\gamma
the concatenation of these two. It is a periodic
3\epsilon
-pseudo-orbit that passes through

and

. It satisfies

, so

holds. Thus,

and

are
\alpha
-equivariant. Therefore, the connected components of
\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}
are the
\alpha
-recurrence chains. ∎

Lemma
3.6
has the following consequence, which is a reinterpretation of Fried’s results.

.

We have
\alpha(D_{\varphi})<0
if and only if
\alpha
quasi-Lyapunov and
\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}

\alpha

\alpha(D_{\varphi})<0

is the decreasing intersection of the compact sets

, we have
\alpha(D_{\varphi,\epsilon,T})<0
for some

. From Lemma

,

is quasi-Lyapunov. Additionally, there is no periodic
\epsilon
-pseudo-orbit

, so there is no

-recurrent point.

\alpha
quasi-Lyapunov and

\alpha(D_{\varphi})\nless 0

, there exists a periodic

\gamma_{\epsilon}

. Since

\gamma_{\epsilon}
(when

When
\alpha
has integer coefficients, we additionally have.

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
\alpha
in

be quasi-Lyapunov and
R
be a recurrence chain inside
\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
. Then
R
is compact and the map
R\xrightarrow{\pi_{\alpha}}\pi_{\alpha}(R)
is a homeomorphism.

Proof.
Let
f\colon M\to{\raisebox{1.00006pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\mathbb{R}$}}/\raisebox{-1.00006pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\mathbb{Z}$}}}
be continuous function, cohomologous to
\alpha
, and let
\widehat{f}\colon\widehat{M}_{\alpha}\to\mathbb{R}
be an
\alpha
-equivariant lift of
f
to
\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
. Then
\widehat{f}
is
C
-quasi-Lyapunov, for some
C\geq 0
. Let
\epsilon>0
be given by the
C
-quasi-Lyapunov property of
\widehat{f}
.
Let
R
be a recurrence chain in
\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
. Then
\widehat{f}(R)
is an interval
I
of length at most
C
. Since
\widehat{M}_{\alpha}\to M
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-covering,
\widehat{f}^{-1}(I)
is compact. Therefore,
R
is compact.

Now, assume that the projection
R\xrightarrow{\pi_{\alpha}}M
is not injective. There exists two points in
R
sent by
\pi_{\alpha}
to the same point
p
in
M
. That is there exist
x
in
R
and
n
in
\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
for which
n\cdot x
also belongs to
R
. Then
R
contains all the points
(kn)\cdot x
for all
k
in
\mathbb{Z}
, which contradicts the compactness of
R
. Therefore,
R\xrightarrow{\pi_{\alpha}}M
is injective and thus it is an embedding. Its image is an
\alpha
-recurrence chain by definition, and the map
R\xrightarrow{\pi_{\alpha}}\pi_{\alpha}(R)
is a homeomorphism.
∎

We end with a relation between the recurrent sets in
\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
and
\overline{M}_{\alpha}
.

Lemma 3.13
.

When
\alpha
is quasi-Lyapunov, a subset of
\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
is a recurrence chain for the flow on
\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
if and only if it is a recurrence chain for the flow on
\overline{M}_{\alpha}
.

So when
\alpha
is quasi-Lyapunov, we can identify the recurrence chains in
\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
with the ones in
\overline{M}_{\alpha}
that are not
\{\pm\infty\}
.

Note that the conclusion does not necessarily hold when
\alpha
is not quasi-Lyapunov. For instance for some pair of points
x,y
in
\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
, a pseudo-orbit can start at
x
, go very close to
\pm\infty
, do a jump that is small in
\overline{M}_{\alpha}
but large in
\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
, and then go back to
y
.

Proof.
Let
f\colon\widehat{M}_{\alpha}\to\mathbb{R}
be a
C
-quasi-Lyapunov,
\alpha
-equivariant and continuous map.
Take two points
x,y
in
\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
. Let
\epsilon
be small enough, and
\gamma
be an
\epsilon
-pseudo-orbit from
x
to
y
. Then any point
z
in
\gamma
satisfies

\min(f(x),f(y))-C\leq f(z)\leq\max(f(x),f(y))+C.

In particular,
\gamma
remains in a compact region of
\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
, where the metrics on
\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
and
\overline{M}_{\alpha}
are comparable at small scales. As a consequence,
x
and
y
satisfy
x\rightsquigarrow y
for the flow on
\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
if and only if they satisfy
x\rightsquigarrow y
for the flow on
\overline{M}_{\alpha}
. The conclusion follows.
∎

4

Quasi-Lyapunov with integer coefficient

We build
\alpha
-equivariant maps that are either Lyapunov, or pre-Lyapunov with a control on the recurrent set.

4.1

Spectral decomposition: the integer case

Denote by
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}
the recurrent set inside
\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\alpha
be in
H^{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})\setminus\{0\}
.
If there exists an
\alpha
-equivariant pre-Lyapunov map
f\colon\widehat{M}_{\alpha}\to\mathbb{R}
, then
\alpha
is quasi-Lyapunov.

If
\alpha
is quasi-Lyapunov, then there exists an
\alpha
-equivariant Lyapunov map
f\colon\widehat{M}_{\alpha}\to\mathbb{R}
, and so that
f(\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha})
has Lebesgue measure zero.
When
M
is smooth and
\varphi
is generated by a uniquely integrable vector field
X
, then
f
can be chosen smooth and satisfying
df(X)<0
outside
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}
.

We prove the first claim here, which is easier.

Proof of the first claim.
Assume that for all
\epsilon>0
, there exists an
\epsilon
-pseudo-orbit
\gamma_{\epsilon}\colon[0,l]\to\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
with
\gamma(l)=\gamma(0)+1
. Up to replacing
\gamma_{\epsilon}
by
n\cdot\gamma_{\epsilon}
for some
n
in
\mathbb{Z}
, we may assume that
\gamma_{\epsilon}(0)
remains in a bounded region of
\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
.

When
\epsilon
goes to zero,
\gamma_{\epsilon}(0)
and
\gamma_{\epsilon}(1)
convergence toward two points
x
and
y
, which satisfies
x\rightsquigarrow y
and
f(y)=f(x)+1
. It contradicts that
f
is pre-Lyapunov. Thus,
f
is
1
-quasi-Lyapunov, and
\alpha
is quasi-Lyapunov.
∎

We dedicate the rest of the section to the second and third claims in the theorem.
Fix a class
\alpha
in
H^{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})
, assumed quasi-Lyapunov. Recall that the recurrence chains in
\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
and the one in
\overline{M}_{\alpha}
can be identified, apart from
\{\pm\infty\}
(see Lemma
3.13
).

Let us sketch the proof. Take two recurrence chains
R_{1},R_{2}
in
\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
so that
R_{1}
is far closer to
-\infty
than
R_{2}
is. Since
\alpha
is quasi-Lyapunov, it implies
R_{1}\not\rightsquigarrow R_{2}
. We build an attractor
A
in
\overline{M}_{\alpha}
, that contains
R_{1}
and
-\infty
, but not
R_{2}
nor
+\infty
. Lemma
1.5
yields a pre-Lyapunov map
f\colon\overline{M}_{\alpha}\to[0,1]
which satisfies
f\equiv 1
on
R_{2}\cup\{+\infty\}
and
f\equiv 0
on
R_{1}\cup\{-\infty\}
. Then, the sum of the functions
x\mapsto f(n\cdot x)
, for
n
in
\mathbb{Z}
, provides an
\alpha
-equivariant pre-Lyapunov function
H\colon\widehat{M}_{\alpha}\to\mathbb{R}
with
H(R_{1})<H(R_{2})
. By summing carefully countably many functions like
H
, for different values of
R_{1},R_{2}
, we obtain an
\alpha
-equivariant Lyapunov function.

We build an
\alpha
-equivariant and pre-Lyapunov map.
Building a Lyapunov map is quite more technical. So we do it in a second time.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
R_{1},R_{2}
be two disjoint compact subsets of
\overline{M}_{\alpha}
. We assume that there exists no pair
(x_{1},x_{2})
in
R_{1}\times R_{2}
that satisfies
x_{1}\rightsquigarrow x_{2}
. Then there exists a pair of attractor-repeller
A,A^{*}\subset N
that satisfies
R_{1}\subset A
and
R_{2}\subset A^{*}
.

Proof.
By assumption, there exists
\epsilon>0
for which there is no
\epsilon
-pseudo-orbit from
R_{1}
to
R_{2}
.
We denote by
U_{\epsilon}
the open subset of points
y
for which there is an
\epsilon
-pseudo-orbit that starts in
R_{1}
and ends at
y
. Then define
A
to be the
\omega
-limit of
U_{\epsilon}
. Since for all
t\geq T
, the closure of
\psi^{\alpha}([t,+\infty[)
is included in
\psi^{\alpha}([t-T,+\infty[)
, the set
A
is compact. It is invariant by the flow and is the
\omega
-limit of its neighborhood
U
, so it is an attractor. It is clear that
A
contains
R_{1}
and is disjoint from
R_{2}
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Assume
\alpha
quasi-Lyapunov and primitive. Let
h\colon\overline{M}_{\alpha}\to[0,1]
be a pre-Lyapunov map which satisfies
h\equiv 0
on a neighborhood of
-\infty
and
h\equiv 1
on a neighborhood of
+\infty
. Denote by
H\colon\widehat{M}_{\alpha}\to\mathbb{R}
the map defined by

H(x)=\sum_{k>0}(h(k\cdot x)-1)+\sum_{k\leq 0}h(k\cdot x).

Then
H
is well-defined,
\alpha
-equivariant and pre-Lyapunov. If
h
is additionally smooth inside
\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
, then
H
is smooth.

Proof.
For all
x
within a compact region
K
of
\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
, we have
h(k\cdot x)=0
for any small enough
k
and
h(k\cdot x)=1
for any large enough
k
, so the sum has finitely many non-zero terms on a neighborhood of
x
. Thus,
H
is well-defined and continuous. A direct computation yields

H(1\cdot x)=H(x)+1=H(x)+\alpha(1)

for any
x
in
\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
and seeing
1
as an element of
{\raisebox{1.00006pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$H_{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})$}}/\raisebox{-1.00006pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\ker_{\mathbb{Z}}(\alpha)$}}}
. So
H
is
\alpha
-equivariant. The pre-Lyapunov and smooth assertion are clear.
∎

We now start building pre-Lyapunov maps.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\alpha
in
H^{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})\setminus\{0\}
be quasi-Lyapunov and primitive. Then there exists an
\alpha
-equivariant pre-Lyapunov map
g\colon\widehat{M}_{\alpha}\to\mathbb{R}
that sends the recurrent set in
\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
onto
\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.
Since
\alpha
is quasi-Lyapunov, there exist a constant
C\geq 0
and an
\alpha
-equivariant and
C
-pre-Lyapunov map
u\colon\widehat{M}_{\alpha}\to\mathbb{R}
.
Denote by
R_{2}\subset\overline{M}_{\alpha}
the union of
\{+\infty\}
and of the recurrence chains that remain in
u^{-1}([C,+\infty[)
. Also denote by
R_{1}
the union of
\{-\infty\}
and of the recurrence chains that remain in
u^{-1}(]-\infty,0])
. Since
g
is
C
-quasi-Lyapunov, there is no pair
(x_{1},x_{2})
in
R_{1}\times R_{2}
that satisfies
x_{1}\rightsquigarrow x_{2}
.

We apply Lemma
4.2
to
N=\overline{M}_{\alpha}
. It yields a pair
A,A^{*}
of attractor-repeller that satisfies
R_{1}\subset A
and
R_{2}\subset A^{*}
. Then Lemma
1.5
provides us with a pre-Lyapunov map
h\colon\overline{M}_{\alpha}\to[0,1]
which satisfies the condition of Lemma
4.3
. So the map
H
defined from
h
in the later lemma is
\alpha
-equivariant and pre-Lyapunov.
∎

From now on, we assume that
\alpha
is primitive. The non-primitive case follows from the primitive one.
To obtain the strongly-Lyapunov property, we sum countably many functions given above.

Denote by
g\colon\widehat{M}_{\alpha}\to\mathbb{R}
an
\alpha
-equivariant and pre-Lyapunov map constructed above, which sends the recurrent set of
\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
onto
\mathbb{Z}
.
Denote by
\mathcal{R}^{+}
the union of the recurrence chains
R
in
\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
that satisfy
g(R)\geq 1
. Similarly denote by
\mathcal{R}^{-}
the union of the recurrence chains
R
that satisfy
g(R)\leq-1
. Observe that for any
\alpha
-equivariant and pre-Lyapunov maps
h\colon\widehat{M}_{\alpha}\to\mathbb{R}
, its values on the recurrent set is determined by its values on the recurrence chains in
g^{-1}(\{0\})
. We use the sets
\mathcal{R}^{\pm}
to build maps
h
as above, so that the recurrence chains in
g^{-1}(\{0\})
are sent onto
\{0,1\}
, with some level of control.

Let us denote by
\mathbb{A}
the set of attractors that contain
\mathcal{R}^{-}\cup\{-\infty\}
and that are disjoint from
\mathcal{R}^{+}\cup\{+\infty\}
. Lemma
4.2
implies that
\mathbb{A}
is not empty.
There exists at most countably many attractors in
\overline{M}_{\alpha}
, so
\mathbb{A}
is at most countable.

Fix some
m\geq 1
, an attractor
A
in
\mathbb{A}
and
A^{*}
its associated repeller. Denote by
V_{m}(A)
the
\tfrac{1}{m}
-neighborhoods
A\cup A^{*}
. Using Lemma
1.5
, there exists a pre-Lyapunov map
h_{A,m}(A)\colon\widehat{M}_{\alpha}\to[0,1]
which satisfies
h_{A,m}\equiv 0
close to
A
,
h_{A,m}\equiv 1
close
A^{*}
, and
h_{A,m}
is decreasing along the flow outside
V_{m}(A)
. When
M
is smooth
\varphi
is generated by a continuous and uniquely integrable vector field
X
, we also assume
h_{A,m}
smooth and that satisfies
dh_{A,m}(X)<0
outside
V_{m}(A)
.

Note that there are countably many possible choices of
A
in
\mathbb{A}
and
m
in
\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
. We enumerate them
using a sequence
A_{n}
with values in
\mathbb{A}
, and a sequence of maps
h_{n}
as above, so that each maps
h_{A,m}
appears once as a
h_{m}
.

Let
H_{n}\colon M_{\alpha}\to\mathbb{R}
be
\alpha
-equivariant and pre-Lyapunov function given by Lemma
4.3
applied to
h_{n}
. We build a strongly-Lyapunov function as the sum
\sum_{n}a_{n}H_{n}
for some scalars
a_{n}
in
[0,1]
. We need technical estimations to prove that the sum has different values on different recurrence chains.

Lemma 4.5
.

For any recurrence chain
R\subset\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
with
g(R)=0
, and any
n
in
\mathbb{N}
, we have
H_{n}(R)=h_{n}(R)
and this value lies in
\{0,1\}
.

Proof.
For any
k\geq 1
,
k\cdot R
lies in
\mathcal{R}^{+}
and
(-k)\cdot R
lies in
\mathcal{R}^{-}
. Thus, we have
h(k\cdot R)=1
and
h((-k)\cdot R)=0
. Hence, by construction of
H_{n}
, we have
H_{n}(R)=h_{n}(R)
which lies in
\{0,1\}
.
∎

Recall that
\alpha
is taken primitive, so for any recurrence chain
R
and
k=-g(R)
, we have
g(k\cdot R)=0
. The previous lemma has the immediate consequence.

Lemma 4.6
.

For any recurrence chain
R\subset\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
and any
i,j
, the inequality
|H_{i}(R)-H_{j}(R)|\leq 1
holds true.

We fix a scalar
\theta
in
]0,\tfrac{1}{2}[
, which thus satisfies
\tfrac{\theta}{1-\theta}<1
. Let
a_{n}^{*}>0
, for
n
in
\mathbb{N}
, be any sequence that satisfies
a_{n+1}^{*}\leq\theta a_{n}^{*}
. We write
a^{*}=\sum_{n}a_{n}^{*}
and
a_{n}=\tfrac{a_{n}^{*}}{a^{*}}
. Note that
\sum_{n}a_{n}=1
and
a_{n+1}\leq\theta a_{n}
hold for all
n
.
Then we define the function

f=\sum_{n}a_{n}H_{n}

which is
\alpha
-equivariant and pre-Lyapunov. If the maps
h_{n}
are smooth, and the sequence
a_{k}^{*}
additionally goes to zero fast enough, then
f
is smooth.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
R_{1},R_{2}\subset\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
be two recurrence chains which satisfy
f(R_{1})=f(R_{2})
. Then
H_{n}(R_{1})=H_{n}(R_{2})
holds true for all
n
in
\mathbb{N}
.

The lemma is an immediate consequence of the following result, applied to
n=1
.

Lemma 4.8
.

Take
n
in
\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
and
\theta<\tfrac{1}{2n+1}
. Let
(a_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence which satisfies
a_{k+1}\leq\theta a_{k}
for all
k
. Then the map
h\colon{\llbracket-n,n\rrbracket}^{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathbb{R}
, defined by
h(u)=\sum_{k}a_{k}u_{k}
, is injective and continuous for the cylindric topology on
{\llbracket-n,n\rrbracket}^{\mathbb{N}}
. Additionally, the image of
h
is a Cantor set of Lebesgue measure zero.

Proof.
It is clear that
h
is continuous. Assume that there exists two distinct sequences
u,v
in
{\llbracket-n,n\rrbracket}^{\mathbb{N}}
with
h(u)=h(v)
. Let
k_{0}
be the smallest integer which satisfies
u_{k_{0}}\neq v_{k_{0}}
. Then we have:

a_{k_{0}}|u_{k_{0}}-v_{k_{0}}|=\left|\sum_{k>k_{0}}a_{k}(u_{k}-v_{k})\right|\leq 2n\sum_{k>k_{0}}\theta^{k-k_{0}}a_{k_{0}}=\tfrac{2n\theta}{1-\theta}a_{k_{0}}<a_{k_{0}}

which contradicts
|u_{k_{0}}-v_{k_{0}}|\geq 1
. The injectivity follows. The fact that
\im(h)
is a Cantor set of zero measure follows from standard arguments.
∎

Lemma 4.9
.

For any recurrence chain
R
, we have
g(R)<f(R)<g(R)+1
.

Proof.
Up to replacing
R
by
k\cdot R
for
k=-g(R)
, we may assume that
g(R)=0
holds true. It follows from Lemma
4.5
that
H_{n}(R)
belongs to
\{0,1\}
for all
n
, so
f(R)=\sum_{n}a_{n}H_{n}(R)
belongs to
[0,1]
.

We prove now that
f(R)
is not equal to 0. According to Lemma
4.2
, there exists an attractor
A_{m}
in
\mathbb{A}
, whose associated repeller
A_{m}^{*}
contains
R
. Then the associated maps
H_{m}
and
h_{m}
satisfy
H_{m}(R)=h_{m}(R)=1
. It follows
\sum_{n}a_{n}H_{n}(R)\geq a_{m}>0
from Lemma
4.5
. Thus,
f(R)>0
holds. Similarly, there exists an attractor
A_{p}
that contains
R
. It follows
H_{p}(R)=0
. And since
\sum_{n}a_{n}=1
and
H_{n}(R)\leq 1
hold for all
n
, we have
f(R)<1
.
∎

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
R_{1},R_{2}
be two recurrence chains. If
f(R_{1})=f(R_{2})
holds, then
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
are equal.

Proof.
Up to replacing
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
by
n\cdot R_{1}
and
n\cdot R_{2}
for
n=-g(R_{1})
, we may assume that
g(R_{1})=0
holds true. It follows from above that
g(R_{2})
is an integer that lie in the open interval in
]f(R_{1}),f(R_{1})+1[
. The length of this interval is one, so it contains at most one integer. It follows that we have
g(R_{2})=g(R_{1})=0
.

We now reason by contradiction. Assume that
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
are not equal. Then up to swapping them, we may assume that
R_{1}\not\rightsquigarrow R_{2}
holds. According to Lemma
4.2
, there exists a pair of attractor-repeller
A_{n},A_{n}^{*}
so that
A_{n}
contains
\mathcal{R}^{-}\cup R_{1}
, and
A_{n}^{*}
contains
\mathcal{R}^{+}\cup R_{2}
. Then the associated functions
H_{n}
and
h_{n}
satisfy
H_{n}(R_{1})=h_{n}(R_{1})=0
and
H_{n}(R_{2})=h_{n}(R_{2})=1
. So
H_{n}(R_{1})\neq H_{n}(R_{2})
holds, which contradicts Lemma
4.7
.
∎

Proof of the second claim in Theorem
4.1
.
Let
\alpha
be any quasi-Lyapunov classes in
H^{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})
.
When
\alpha
is not primitive, and is equal to
n\beta
for some
n\geq 1
, we have
\widehat{M}_{\alpha}=\widehat{M}_{\beta}
and
f\colon\widehat{M}_{\alpha}\to\mathbb{R}
is
\beta
-equivariant if and only if
nf
is
\alpha
-equivariant. Thus, it is enough to prove the conclusion of the theorem when
\alpha
is primitive.

Let us now assume
\alpha
primitive.
We use the notations defined above in this section. The function
f\colon\widehat{M}_{\alpha}\to\mathbb{R}
constructed above is
\alpha
-equivariant and constant on recurrence chains. It follows from Lemma
4.10
that
f
sends two distinct recurrences chains on two distinct values.

Take a point
x
in
\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
outside the recurrent set. The orbit of
x
, by the flow, converges in the future and in the past toward two recurrence chains
R_{1},R_{2}
in
\overline{M}_{\alpha}
. These recurrence chains are distinct since
x
is not recurrent. It follows that
f
is not constant on that orbit, otherwise
f(R_{1})
and
f(R_{2})
would be equal.

We prove that
f
is decreasing along the flow close to
x
.
We write
k=-g(R_{1})
, so that
g(k\cdot R_{1})=0
and
g(k\cdot R_{2})>0
. Lemma
4.2
implies that there exists an attractor
A
in
\mathbb{A}
that contains
k\cdot R_{1}
but not
k\cdot R_{2}
. Then
k\cdot x
is included neither in
A
not in its associated repeller
A^{*}
.
Take
m\geq 1
large enough so that
k\cdot x
is at distance at least
\tfrac{1}{m}
from
A\cup A
. Then the map
h_{A,m}
constructed above is decreasing along the flow on a neighborhood of
x
. Recall that all
h_{n}
are non-increasing along the flow, and one of them is equal to
h_{A,m}
. It follows that their combination
f
is decreasing along the flow close to
x
. Thus,
f
is Lyapunov.

The fact that
f(\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha})
has Lebesgue measure zero follows from Lemma
4.8
.
The smoothness conclusion follows from choosing the sequence
a_{n}^{*}
to decrease fast enough.
∎

4.2

Pre-Lyapunov maps from Conley’s order

We discuss here a similar result to the equivariant spectral decomposition, but with some level of control on the
\alpha
-recurrent chains. It is not a strong result in terms of dynamics, but it is very helpful for the classification of partial cross-sections.

Denote by
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}^{*}
the set of recurrence chains, with the quotient topology. It comes with the partial order
\rightsquigarrow
, called the Conley’s order. Note that when a continuous map
g\colon\widehat{M}_{\alpha}\to\mathbb{R}
is
\alpha
-equivariant and pre-Lyapunov, it induces a continuous,
\alpha
-equivariant and map
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}^{*}\xrightarrow{g}\mathbb{R}
, which is non-increasing for the Conley order.

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
\alpha
in
H^{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})
be quasi-Lyapunov, and
f\colon\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}^{*}\to\mathbb{Z}
be a non-increasing and continuous map. There exists a continuous,
\alpha
-equivariant and pre-Lyapunov map
g\colon\widehat{M}_{\alpha}\to\mathbb{R}
whose restriction to
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}
coincide with
f
, and that is decreasing outside
g^{-1}(\mathbb{Z})
. When
M
is smooth and
\varphi
is generated by a continuous and uniquely integrable vector field
X
, then
g
can additionally be chosen smooth, and with
dg(X)<0
outside
g^{-1}(\mathbb{Z})
.

We split the proof in two cases, depending on if
\alpha
is zero or not.

Proof in the case
\alpha\neq 0
..
Assume
\alpha
primitive first.
Let
R_{1}\subset\overline{M}_{\alpha}
be the union of
\{-\infty\}
and of the recurrence chains in

. Let

be the union of
\{+\infty\}
and of the recurrence chains in

.
By continuity of

,
R_{1}
and

are disjoint compact subsets of
\overline{M}_{\alpha}
.
Since
\alpha
is quasi-Lyapunov, we have

and
R_{1}\not\rightsquigarrow+\infty
, and so
R_{1}\not\rightsquigarrow R_{2}
(see Lemma
3.5
).

It follows from Lemma
4.2
and Lemma
1.5
that there exists a pre-Lyapunov map
h\colon\overline{M}_{\alpha}\to[0,1]
that satisfies
h\equiv 0
close to
R_{1}
and
h\equiv 1
close to
R_{2}
. Denote by

the continuous and
\alpha
-equivariant map given in Lemma
4.3
applied to the map

.

Let
R
be a recurrence chain that satisfies

. For any
k>0
, we have

so
k\cdot R
lies in
R_{2}
and
h(k\cdot R)=1
holds. Similarly, we have
h(k\cdot R)=0
for any
k\leq 0
. It follows
g(R)=0
from the definition of
g
. Take now any recurrence chain
R
and
k=-f(R)
. Since
\alpha
is primitive, we have
f(k\cdot R)=0
, and thus

g(R)=g(k\cdot R)-k=f(R)

holds. So
g
and
f
coincide on
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}
.

We now consider the general case on
\alpha
. We write
\alpha=n\beta
for some

and some primitive class
\beta
in

.
For any

in
{\llbracket 0,n-1\rrbracket}
, we define a function

by

f_{i}=\left\lfloor\tfrac{f+i}{n}\right\rfloor

which is
\beta
-equivariant and continuous.
Using the Euclidian division by

\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f_{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\lfloor\tfrac{f+i}{n}\rfloor=f

From the primitive case, there exists a
\beta

which coincides with

in restriction to
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}
. We define the map

, which is
\alpha
-equivariant and pre-Lyapunov. It follows from above that

coincides with
f
on
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}
.

The smoothness conclusion follows from Lemma
1.5
.
∎

We now prove the case
\alpha=0
, which is essentially already known.

Proof in the case
\alpha=0
..
In the case
\alpha=0
,
\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
is equal to

, and
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}
is simply the recurrent set of
\varphi
. When
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}
is equal to

,

suffice. Thus, we can now assume that
\varphi
admits at least two recurrence chains.

Up to adding a constant to

, we may assume that its minimum is zero. For any

in
{\llbracket 1,\max f\rrbracket}
, denote by

the union of recurrence chains

of
\varphi
that satisfy

, and

.
It follows from Lemma

and Lemma

that there exists a pre-Lyapunov map

that satisfies

and
h_{n}(R_{n}^{*})=1
.

We define the pre-Lyapunov map

.
For any recurrence chain

of
\varphi
,

is equal to the number of

that satisfy

, that is

and

The smoothness conclusion follows from Lemma

.
∎

5

Quasi-Lyapunov with real coefficients

We explore several questions about quasi-Lyapunov cohomology classes with real coefficients. We start with the dependence of
\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}
on
\alpha
.

5.1

Approximation by rational quasi-Lyapunov classes

The first difficulty to study quasi-Lyapunov classes with real coefficients is that
\ker(\alpha)
in
H_{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})
is not necessarily a co-dimension 1 sub-module of
H_{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})
. So the covering
\widehat{M}_{\alpha}\to M
does not behave as well as in the integer case. Our strategy is to first approximate any quasi-Lyapunov classes by ones with rational coefficients, on which we can work more easily.

Denote by
\ker_{\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha)
the kernel of
\alpha
inside
H_{1}(M,\mathbb{Q})
, which we identify with a subset of
H_{1}(M,\mathbb{R})
.
Recall that
D_{\varphi}\subset H_{1}(M,\mathbb{R})
is the set of asymptotic pseudo-directions of
\varphi
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\alpha
in
H^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})
be quasi-Lyapunov, and take
\epsilon>0
. For any
\delta
in
\ker(\alpha)\cap D_{\varphi}
, there exist finitely many scalars
a_{1}\cdots a_{n}>0
and
\epsilon
-pseudo-orbits
\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{n}
which satisfy
\delta=\sum_{i}a_{i}[\gamma_{i}]
and
\alpha([\gamma_{i}])=0
for all
i
.

Proof.
Take
\delta
in
\ker(\alpha)\cap D_{\varphi}
. According to Corollary
2.4
,
\delta
is the convex combination of some
a_{i}[\gamma_{i}]
, for some
a_{i}>0
and some periodic
\epsilon
-pseudo-orbits
\gamma_{i}
. Since
\alpha
is quasi-Lyapunov, we have
\alpha([\gamma_{i}])\geq 0
for all
i
. And since
\alpha(\delta)=0
holds, it follows
\alpha([\gamma_{i}])=0
.
∎

Denote by
\ker_{\mathbb{R}}(\alpha)
kernel of
\alpha
in
H_{1}(M,\mathbb{R})
. The lemma above as an immediate consequence.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
\alpha
in
H^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})
be quasi-Lyapunov. Then
\ker_{\mathbb{R}}(\alpha)\cap D_{\varphi}
lies in the
\mathbb{R}
-vector subspace of
H_{1}(M,\mathbb{R})
spanned by
\ker_{\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha)
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\alpha
in
H^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})
be quasi-Lyapunov. Then for any
\beta
in
H^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})
sufficiently close to
\alpha
and with
\ker_{\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha)\subset\ker_{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta)
,
\beta
is quasi-Lyapunov.

Proof.
Take
\beta
with
\ker_{\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha)\subset\ker_{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta)
. By Lemma
3.6
, there exists a small
\epsilon>0
which so that
\alpha([\gamma])\leq 0
holds for all periodic
\epsilon
-pseudo-orbits
\gamma
. Let
\epsilon^{\prime}>0
and
\delta_{1}\cdots\delta_{n}
be
\epsilon
-pseudo-orbits as in the conclusion of Lemma
2.3
, that is so that every
\epsilon^{\prime}
-pseudo-orbit is homologous to a sum of
\delta_{i}
.

Whenever
\alpha([\delta_{i}])=0
holds,
[\delta_{i}]
lies in
\ker_{\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha)
, so
\beta([\delta_{i}])=0
also holds. If
\beta
is close enough to
\alpha
, then
\beta([\delta_{i}])<0
holds for the finitely many
\delta_{i}
that satisfy
\alpha([\delta_{i}])<0
. Hence, we have
\beta([\delta_{i}])\leq 0
for all
i
. It follows from the choice of
\delta_{i}
that
\beta([\gamma])\leq 0
holds for all periodic
\epsilon^{\prime}
-pseudo-orbits
\gamma
. Then using Lemma
3.6
, we deduce that

is quasi-Lyapunov.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
E
be a
\mathbb{R}
-vector space of finite dimension and

be a
\mathbb{Q}
-vector subspace dense inside

. For any

in

and any neighborhood

of
x
, there exist finitely many vectors
\beta_{1}\cdots\beta_{p}
in

and scalars
\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{p}>0
which satisfy
x=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\beta_{i}
and so that the two families
(\beta_{1}\cdots\beta_{p})
and
(\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{p})
are free over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Given a
\mathbb{R}
-vector space
E
and
X\subset E
, we denote by
\conv(X)\subset E
its convex hull.

Proof.
Take
n
vectors
\beta_{1}\cdots\beta_{p}
in
F
so that
x
belongs to the
\mathbb{R}
-vector space spanned by the family
(\beta_{i})_{i}
. We additionally take
n
minimal with this property. Then
(\beta_{1}\cdots\beta_{p})
is free over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Take
a_{1}\cdots a_{p}\in\mathbb{R}
with
x=\sum_{i}a_{i}\beta_{i}
. We claim that the family
(a_{1}\cdots a_{p})
is free over
\mathbb{Q}
. If it was not the case, there exist

and rational numbers

, for

, that satisfy:

a_{j}=\sum_{i\neq j}q_{i}a_{i}.

Note that
\beta_{i}+q_{i}\beta_{j}
belongs to

. So the rewriting

x=\sum_{i\neq j}a_{i}\beta_{i}+\left(\sum_{i\neq j}q_{i}a_{i}\right)\beta_{j}=\sum_{i\neq j}a_{i}(\beta_{i}+q_{i}\beta_{j})

. The claim follows.

We may now choose
a_{1}\cdots a_{p}
and
\beta_{1}\cdots\beta_{p}
in

as above, so that additionally

belongs to
\conv(\{0,\beta_{1}\cdots\beta_{p}\})
. Then

is non-negative, and even positive by minimality of

.
∎

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
\alpha
in
H^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})
be quasi-Lyapunov and
U
be a neighborhood of
\alpha
. Then there exists some quasi-Lyapunov classes

in

and some scalars

and
(\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{p})
are free over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Proof.
The set of classes
\beta
in

with

is a
\mathbb{R}
-vector subspaces described by rational equations, and so it admits a basis formed by elements with rational coefficients, that is in

. We apply Lemma
5.4
to
x=\alpha
, to a smaller neighborhood
U^{\prime}\subset U
of
\alpha
, to

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle=\{\beta\in H_{1}(M,\mathbb{R}),\beta(\ker_{\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha))=0\},\text{ and }

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle=\{\beta\in H_{1}(M,\mathbb{Q}),\beta(\ker_{\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha))=0\}.

It yields some
\beta_{i}
in

∎

5.2

Cone of quasi-Lyapunov classes

Denote by
D_{\varphi}^{*}\subset H^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})
the convex cone dual to
D_{\varphi}
, that is the set of
\alpha
that satisfy
\alpha(D_{\varphi})\geq 0
. Also denote by
\QL_{\varphi}
the set of quasi-Lyapunov classes for
\varphi
. It is a convex cone, it contains zero, and included in
-D_{\varphi}^{*}
(see Lemma
3.1
). In general,
\QL_{\varphi}
is neither open nor closed inside
-D_{\varphi}^{*}
.

We discuss a combinatorial decomposition of an arbitrary convex cone, not necessarily closed, and apply it to
\QL_{\varphi}
and
-D_{\varphi}^{*}
. Denote by
\mathcal{C}\subset X
a non-empty convex cone in a finite dimensionnal
\mathbb{R}
-vector space
X
.
Given a subset
E\subset X
, denote by
\langle E\rangle
the vector subspace of
X
spanned by
E
. We call a
open face
of
\mathcal{C}
, any non-empty subset
F
of
\mathcal{C}
that satisfies:

1.

F
is a convex cone,

2.

F
is open inside
\langle F\rangle
,

3.

F
is maximal among the sets that satisfy the first two items.

Let us insist on the fact that ‘open’ in ‘open face’ means that it does not contains its boundary, not that it is open in
H^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})
.

Example 5.6
.

The convex cone
cC
contains a maximum vector subspace
F
, which may be reduced to
\{0\}
. The set
F
is an open face of
cC
.

Example 5.7
.

When
-D_{\varphi}^{*}
has a non-empty interior inside
H^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})
, its interior is made of classes
\alpha
with
\alpha(D_{\varphi})<0
, which are thus quasi-Lyapunov. Hence, the interior of
-D_{\varphi}^{*}
is an open face of
\QL_{\varphi}
. This face, when non-empty, has the following geometric interpretation:
\alpha(D_{\varphi})<0
holds if and only if
-\alpha
is cohomologous to a global section
[
4
]
.

We continue the section with some elementary properties satisfied by open faces, that one would expect from the choice of terminology.

Lemma 5.8
.

For every
x
in
\mathcal{C}
, there exists a unique open face of
\mathcal{C}
that contains
x
.

Corollary 5.9
.

The open faces of
\mathcal{C}
are pairwise disjoint.

Proof.
Let
x
be in
\mathcal{C}
. Denote by
E_{x}\subset X
the vector subspace generated by the set of
y
that satisfies that the two elements
x+y
and
x-y
lie in
\mathcal{C}
. Note that
E_{x}
contains
\langle x\rangle
, for
y=\tfrac{1}{2}x
, so
E_{x}
it is not empty. Denote by
F_{x}\subset E_{x}
the interior (in
E_{x}
) of
\mathcal{C}\cap E_{x}
.

By construction, there exists a basis
y_{1}\cdots y_{p}
of
E_{x}
, so that all
x\pm y_{i}
are all in
\mathcal{C}
. The interior of

\conv(x+y_{1},x-y_{1},\cdots,x+y_{p},x-y_{p})

lies in
\mathcal{C}
, so
x
itself lies in
F_{x}
. It is clear that
F_{x}
satisfies the first two items in the definition of open faces of
\mathcal{C}
.

We now prove the last item. Let
F\subset\mathcal{C}
be a subset that contains
x
and that satisfies the first two items. Then for all
y
in
F
and close enough to
x
,
x+(y-x)
and
x-(y-x)
are both very close to
x
inside
\langle F\rangle
, so they lie in
\mathcal{C}
. It follows that
y-x
belongs to
E_{x}
, and so
\langle F\rangle
is a subset of
E_{x}
. Therefore,
F
is a subset of
F_{x}
. It follows that
F_{x}
is maximal and that it is an open face of
\mathcal{C}
.
The uniqueness follows from the same argument.
∎

From now on, we denote by
F_{x}
the
unique open face of
\mathcal{C}
that contains a given
x
in
\mathcal{C}
. It follows from definition and from the same arguments as above.

Corollary 5.10
.

Let
y_{1}\cdots y_{p}
be in
\mathcal{C}
and
E=\conv(\{y_{1}\cdots y_{p}\})
. There exists a unique open face
F
of
\mathcal{C}
which contains the interior of
E
(as a subset of
\langle E\rangle
). Additionally, the
y_{i}
lie in the closure of
F
.

Lemma 5.11
.

For any open face
F
of
\mathcal{C}
and any

in
\partial F\cap\mathcal{C}
, the open face
F_{y}
is included in
\partial F
.

Proof.
Assume that
F_{y}
is not included inside
\partial F
and take some
z
in
F_{y}
that is not inside
\partial F
. Then
z
does not lie in
F
since open faces of
\mathcal{C}
are pairwise disjoint. For some
\epsilon>0
, the elements
z_{\pm}=y\pm\epsilon(z-y)
lie in
\mathcal{C}
by openness of

. So the interior of the convex cone spanned by

is larger than

, and it satisfies the first two items in the definition of open faces of

. It contradicts the maximality of

.
∎

We now go back to
\QL_{\varphi}
and
-D_{\varphi}^{*}
.

.

For any

in
\QL_{\varphi}

It follows from Corollary

It has two immediate consequences:

Corollary 5.13
.

For any open face

of

We take

5.4

Spectral decomposition: the real case

We prove a similar result to Theorem
4.1
for quasi-Lyapunov classes with real coefficients. Recall that
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}
is the recurrent set inside
\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
.

Theorem 5.22
.

For any quasi-Lyapunov class
\alpha
in
H^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})
, there exists an
\alpha
-equivariant and strongly-Lyapunov map
f\colon\widehat{M}_{\alpha}\to\mathbb{R}
, for which the Lebesgue measure of
f(\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha})
is zero.
When
M
is smooth and
\varphi
is generated by a uniquely integrable vector field
X
, then
f
can be chosen smooth and satisfying
df(X)<0
outside
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}
.

We put the emphasis on the ‘strongly’-Lyapunov property, since in the real coefficients case, it is a priori a strictly stronger than the Lyapunov property.

Contrary to the integer coefficients case, we do not have a converse.
The lack of compactness of the pre-images
f^{-1}(t)
prevent us from applying the same arguments as in the integer coefficients case.
In fact, there exists a pre-Lyapunov map for some non-quasi-Lyapunov classes. We do not know any counterexample with a strongly-Lyapunov map.

Example 5.23
.

Let
M={\raisebox{1.02228pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\mathbb{R}^{2}$}}/\raisebox{-1.02228pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\mathbb{Z}^{2}$}}}
be the standard torus,
\varphi
be a linear flow with irrational slope
(u,v)
, and
\alpha=vdx-udy
. Then we have
\widehat{M}_{\alpha}=\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The lifted flow on
\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
is linear, so its recurrent set is empty.
Thus, the map
f\colon\widehat{M}_{\alpha}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by
f(x,y)=vx-udy
is pre-Lyapunov and
\alpha
-equivariant, but
\alpha
is not quasi-Lyapunov.

Before proving the theorem, let us explain why trying to build
f
by hand, as in the integer coefficient case, does not work. When
\alpha
has integer coefficients,
\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
admits a co-compact action by
{\raisebox{1.00006pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$H^{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})$}}/\raisebox{-1.00006pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\ker_{\mathbb{Z}}(\alpha)$}}}\simeq\mathbb{Z}
. So it can be compactified by adding two points
\pm\infty
at infinity. The flow extends on the compactification. A key point is that the boundary at infinity is disconnected, and that
-\infty\not\rightsquigarrow+\infty
holds, so we can build attractors that contains
-\infty
but not
+\infty
. When
\alpha
does not have rational coefficients,
{\raisebox{1.00006pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$H^{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})$}}/\raisebox{-1.00006pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\ker_{\mathbb{Z}}(\alpha)$}}}
has a higher rank than
\mathbb{Z}
, so no good compactification allows a generalization of the arguments. A natural compactification would be to add the visual boundary of
{\raisebox{1.00006pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$H^{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})$}}/\raisebox{-1.00006pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\ker_{\mathbb{Z}}(\alpha)$}}}
to
\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
. Then the flow extends as a constant flow on the visual boundary. The visual boundary is connected, so the flow is recurrent on it. Hence, we can not choose an attractor that contains a part of the visual boundary but not all of it.
To compensate this issue, we use the tools developed in the previous section.

We now start the proof.
According to Corollary
5.5
, there exist finitely many quasi-Lyapunov classes
\beta_{1}\cdots\beta_{p}
in
H^{1}(M,\mathbb{Q})
, and
\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{p}>0
which satisfy
\alpha=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\beta_{i}
and so that
(\beta_{1}\cdots\beta_{p})
and
(\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{p})
are free of
\mathbb{Q}
. Up to choosing
\beta_{i}
closer to
\alpha
, we may assume that
\beta_{i}
lies in
F_{\alpha}
. Up to replacing
\beta_{i}
and
\lambda_{i}
by
r_{i}^{-1}\beta_{i}
and
r_{i}\lambda_{i}
for the unique positive rational
r_{i}
that satisfies
\beta_{i}(H_{1}(M,\mathbb{Z}))=r_{i}\mathbb{Z}
, we may assume that
\beta_{i}
has integer coefficients, and that it is primitive.

In that section, we fix
p\geq 1
,
\beta_{i}
in
H_{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})
and
\lambda_{i}>0
for
p
in
{\llbracket 1,p\rrbracket}
which satisfies the following:

•

\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\lambda_{i}\beta_{i}
,

•

\beta_{i}
lies in
F_{\alpha}
and it is primitive,

•

the two families
(\beta_{i})_{i}
and
(\lambda_{i})_{i}
are free over
\mathbb{Q}
.

We build a strongly-Lyapunov and
\alpha
-equivariant function in several steps. The first step is to build
\beta_{i}
-equivariant functions.

Lemma 5.24
.

For all
1\leq i\leq n
, we have
\ker_{\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha)\subset\ker_{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta_{i})
.

Proof.
If the inclusion did not hold, there would exist some
x
in
\ker_{\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha)
outside
\ker_{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta_{i})
for some
i
. Then we would have

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\beta_{i}(x)=\alpha(x)=0

where
(\beta_{1}(x)\cdots\beta_{p}(x))
is not identically zero. It contradicts that
(\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{p})
is free over
\mathbb{Q}
.
∎

Recall that
\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
is the quotient of
\widehat{M}
by the action of
\ker_{\mathbb{Z}}(\alpha)
. It follows from the lemma that there is a natural covering map
\pi_{i}\colon\widehat{M}_{\alpha}\to\widehat{M}_{\beta_{i}}
.
To prove Theorem
4.1
, we build
\beta_{i}
-equivariant and Lyapunov map
f^{i}\colon\widehat{M}_{\beta_{i}}\to\mathbb{R}
.
The composition
f_{i}\circ\pi_{i}\colon\widehat{M}_{\alpha}\to\mathbb{R}
is clearly
\beta_{i}
-equivariant and pre-Lyapunov. Note that it is not necessarily strongly-Lyapunov.
We define the map

f=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}f_{i}\circ\pi_{i},

which is smooth,
\alpha
-equivariant and pre-Lyapunov.
Proving that
f
is strongly-Lyapunov require more work. The first step is the following lemma. The second step requires reusing the construction from Section
4.1
.

By assumption,
\beta_{i}
lies inside
F_{\alpha}
. So we have
\mathcal{R}_{\beta_{i}}=\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}
for all
i
(see Theorem

). The recurrence chains are the connected components of the recurrence set, so a set in

is an
\alpha
-recurrence chain if and only it is a
\beta_{i}
-recurrence chains. It has the following consequence.

Lemma 5.25
.

For any points
x,y
in
\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
with
x\rightsquigarrow y
and

, we have
f(x)>f(y)
.

Proof.
We prove the lemma by contraposition. Take
x,y
in
\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
that satisfy
x\rightsquigarrow y
and
f(x)=f(y)
. For all
i
, we have
f_{i}\circ\pi_{i}(x)\geq f_{i}\circ\pi_{i}(y)
, and there sum are equal. So
f_{i}\circ\pi_{i}(x)=f_{i}\circ\pi_{i}(y)
holds for all

.

Since
f_{i}
is strongly-Lyapunov, it implies that
\pi_{i}(x)
and
\pi_{i}(y)
are in the same recurrence chain inside
\widehat{M}_{\beta_{i}}
. This recurrence chain projects onto a

-recurrence chain inside

\alpha

Let

is
\alpha

f(y)=f(x)=f(R)=f(\delta\cdot y)=\alpha([\delta])+f(y)

which implies
\alpha([\delta])=0
. Since
\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
is obtained after a quotient by

, we have

. Thus,

and

are in the same recurrence chain, and

holds. The conclusion follows.
∎

It remains to prove one point:
f
sends two distinct recurrence chains onto two distinct values. The difficulty is that this property does not behave well under sum.
To obtain this property, we need to choose the functions
f_{i}
more carefully.

Let us recall the construction of
f_{i}
from the Section
4.1
.
We built countably many
\beta_{i}
-equivariant and pre-Lyapunov maps
H^{i}_{k}\colon\widehat{M}_{\beta_{i}}\to\mathbb{R}
, for all
k
in
\mathbb{N}
, which send the recurrent set of
\widehat{M}_{\beta_{i}}
inside
\mathbb{Z}
. Additionally, we have
|H^{i}_{k}-H^{i}_{k^{\prime}}|\leq 1
for all
i,k,k^{\prime}
.
Fix some
\theta
in
]0,\tfrac{1}{2}[
so that
\tfrac{2\theta}{1-\theta}<1
holds.
Then we built a sequence
a_{k}>0
with
\sum_{k}a_{k}=1
, and
a_{k+1}\leq\theta a_{k}
. We then prove that the sum
\sum_{k}a_{k}H^{i}_{k}
is
\widehat{\beta}_{i}
-equivariant and strongly-Lyapunov. The map
f_{i}
is obtained as
f_{i}=\sum_{k}a_{k}H^{i}_{k}
.
The fact that
f_{i}
sends two distinct recurrence chains on two distinct values is a consequence of the fast decreasing assumption on
a_{k}
.

Let
a_{k}>0
and
H_{k}^{i}
be as above. Note that we choose
a_{k}
independent on
i
. Then we have by definition of

f=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\lambda_{i}a_{k}H^{i}_{k}\circ\pi_{i}.

Let us rewrite

We write
\widetilde{H}^{i}_{k}=H^{i}_{k}\circ\pi_{i}
and
\widetilde{H}^{i}_{k,0}=\widetilde{H}^{i}_{k}-\widetilde{H}^{i}_{0}
. By construction, we have

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\sum_{k\geq 0}\lambda_{i}a_{k}\widetilde{H}^{i}_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\lambda_{i}a_{0}\widetilde{H}^{i}_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{p}\sum_{k\geq 1}\lambda_{i}a_{k}(\widetilde{H}^{i}_{k,0}+\widetilde{H}^{i}_{0})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\lambda_{i}\widetilde{H}^{i}_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{p}\sum_{k\geq 1}\lambda_{i}a_{k}\widetilde{H}^{i}_{k,0}.

So given two recurrence chains

f(P)-f(R)=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\lambda_{i}(\widetilde{H}^{i}_{0}(P)-\widetilde{H}^{i}_{0}(R))+\sum_{i=1}^{p}\sum_{k\geq 1}\lambda_{i}a_{k}(\widetilde{H}^{i}_{k,0}(P)-\widetilde{H}^{i}_{k,0}(R))

(1)

The left sum remains inside the countable set

. After choosing

well, the right sum will remain in a Cantor set of zero Lebesgue measure, which contains

. So it is likely that these two sets intersects only on

. If this holds true, we immediately deduce when
f(P)
and

. Then

follows quickly.

To control the right sum, we choose the values of
\lambda_{i}a_{k}
to decrease fast enough. The first step is to control
\lambda_{i}
.

Lemma 5.26
.

Given
\theta>0
, there exist finitely many primitive classes
\widehat{\beta}_{i}

\widehat{\lambda}_{i}>0

{\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket}

•

\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\widehat{\lambda}_{i}\widehat{\beta}_{i}
,

•

the two families
(\widehat{\beta}_{i})_{i}
and
(\widehat{\lambda}_{i})_{i}
are free over
\mathbb{Q}
,

•

for all
i
, we have
\widehat{\lambda}_{i+1}<\theta\widehat{\lambda}_{i}

Proof.
Fix some
\eta>0
smaller than all the ratios
\tfrac{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{j}}
.
Take some elements
\beta_{i}^{\prime}
in
H_{1}(M,\mathbb{Q})\cap F_{\alpha}
and very close to
\beta_{i}

\lambda_{i}^{\prime}>0

Write

is a positive rational, and where

\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n}+1}h\circ\widehat{\varphi}_{s}(\widehat{x}_{n})ds
remains at bounded distance from
[\gamma_{x_{n},t_{n}}]+h(\widehat{x}_{n})
. Similarly,
\int_{0}^{1}h\circ\widehat{\varphi}_{s}(\widehat{x}_{n})ds
remains at bounded distance from
h(\widehat{x}_{n})
. So the right term get closer and closer to
\tfrac{1}{t_{n}}[\gamma_{x_{n},t_{n}}]
when
n
goes to
+\infty
.
It follows that
\tfrac{1}{t_{n}}[\gamma_{x_{n},t_{n}}]
converges toward
[\mu]_{\varphi}
.
Therefore, we have
D^{F}_{\varphi}\subset D^{S}_{\varphi}
. The conclusion follows. ∎

We prove the converse inclusion. We actually prove a stronger result:

Lemma A.5
.

Let
\mu
be an ergodic measure. Then for
\mu
-almost all
x
in
M
, the homology class
\tfrac{1}{t}[\gamma_{x,t}]
converges toward
[\mu]_{\varphi}
when
t
goes to
+\infty
.

Proof of the lemma and of
D^{S}_{\varphi}\subset\conv(D^{F}_{\varphi})
.
Let
\mu
be an ergodic
\varphi
-invariant probability measure.
Denote by
h\colon\widehat{M}\to H_{1}(M,\mathbb{R})
and
g_{1}\colon M\to H_{1}(M,\mathbb{R})
the map used above. Then it follows from Birkhoff ergodic theorem that the integral

I_{t}=\int_{M}g_{1}d\left(\tfrac{1}{t}\Leb(x,0,t)\right)

converges toward
\int_{M}g_{1}d\mu=[\mu]_{\varphi}
for
\mu
-almost all
x
in
M
. Using Equation
4
and the above arguments, the integral
I_{t}
get closer to
\tfrac{1}{t}[\gamma_{x,t}]
when
t
goes to
+\infty
, so
\tfrac{1}{t}[\gamma_{x,t}]
converges in
t
toward
[\mu]_{\varphi}
for
\mu
-almost all
x
. Thus,
[\mu]_{\varphi}
belongs to
D^{F}_{\varphi}
. Since the set
\mathcal{M}_{p}(\varphi)
is the closure of the convex hull of the ergodic measures, it follows that
D^{S}_{\varphi}\subset\conv(D^{F}_{\varphi})
holds.
∎

Given a periodic pseudo-orbit
\gamma\colon{\raisebox{1.00006pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\mathbb{R}$}}/\raisebox{-1.00006pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$l\mathbb{Z}$}}}\to M
, we denote by
\Leb_{\gamma}=\gamma^{*}\Leb
the push-back measure of the Lebesgue measure on
{\raisebox{1.00006pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\mathbb{R}$}}/\raisebox{-1.00006pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$l\mathbb{Z}$}}}
.
The proof of
\conv(D_{\varphi})\subset D^{S}_{\varphi}
is very similar to the one of
\conv(D^{F}_{\varphi})\subset D^{S}_{\varphi}
.

Proof of
\conv(D_{\varphi})\subset D^{S}_{\varphi}
.
Let
\gamma
be a periodic
\epsilon
-pseudo-orbit.
We will prove that
\tfrac{1}{\len(\gamma)}[\gamma]
accumulates on
\mathcal{M}_{p}(\varphi)
as
\epsilon
goes to zero. It is clear when
\gamma
is taken among periodic orbits.

We treat the case when
\gamma
is not continuous.
Denote by
\Delta
its discontinuity set. For any
t
in
\Delta
, we write
x_{t}=\gamma(t)
. Let us denote by
l_{t}
the length of the continuity interval of
\gamma
that comes immediately after
t
, and denote by
I_{t}=[t,t+l_{t}[
that interval. Note that
\Leb_{\gamma}
is equal to the sum of the measures
\Leb(x_{t},t,t+l_{t})
for all
t
in
\Delta
. Take
s
in
\mathbb{R}
and let us compute:

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{\len(\gamma)}\left(\varphi_{s}^{*}\Leb_{\gamma}-\Leb_{\gamma}\right)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{\len(\gamma)}\left(\sum_{t\in\Delta}\Leb(x_{t},s,s+l_{t})-\sum_{t\in\Delta}\Leb(x_{t},0,l_{t})\right)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{\len(\gamma)}\left(\sum_{t\in\Delta}\Leb(x_{t},l_{t},s+l_{t})-\sum_{t\in\Delta}\Leb(x_{t},0,s)\right)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{\len(\gamma)}\left(\sum_{t\in\Delta}\Leb(\varphi_{l_{t}}(x_{t}),0,s)-\sum_{t\in\Delta}\Leb(x_{t+l_{t}},0,s)\right)

where in the last line, we use the reparametrization
t\mapsto t+l_{t}
of
\Delta
on the right most term.
As
\epsilon
goes to zero,
\varphi_{l_{t}}(x_{t})
converges uniformly toward
x_{t+l_{t}}
.
Note that at fixed
s
, the measure
\Leb(y,0,s)-\Leb(x,0,s)
converges uniformly toward zero when the distance between
x
and
y
goes to zero.
Therefore, the measure
\Leb(\varphi_{l_{t}}(x_{t}),0,s)-\Leb(x_{t+l_{t}},0,s)
converges uniformly toward zero when
\epsilon
goes to zero.
Therefore, any accumulation point of the measures of the type
\tfrac{1}{\len(\gamma)}\Leb_{\gamma}
, when
\epsilon
goes to zero, is a
\varphi
-invariant measure.

For all
n\geq 1
, let
\gamma_{n}
be a
\tfrac{1}{n}
-pseudo-orbit. Up to an extraction, we can assume that
\tfrac{1}{\len(\gamma_{n})}\Leb_{\gamma_{n}}
converges toward some
\mu
in
\mathcal{M}_{p}(\varphi)
.
Using Equation
4
, and the same arguments that we used before, yields that
\tfrac{1}{\len(\gamma_{n})}[\gamma_{n}]
converges toward
[\mu]_{\varphi}
. So
D_{\varphi}\subset D^{S}_{\varphi}
holds true. The conclusion follows.
∎

Proof of
D^{S}_{\varphi}\subset\conv(D_{\varphi})
.
Take an ergodic invariant probability measure
\mu
. Note that
\mu(M\setminus\mathcal{R})
is equal to zero. Indeed,
M\setminus\mathcal{R}
is included in the wandering set: the set of point
x
in
M
which admit a neighborhood
U
so that
\varphi_{t}(U)\cap U
is empty for all
t
large enough. And the wandering set has measure zero for any
\varphi
-invariant probability measure. The recurrence chains of
\varphi
are disjoint and
\varphi
-invariant. So by ergodicity, all but one recurrence chain have measure zero of
\mu
. Thus, the support of
\mu
is included in one recurrence chain
R
of
\varphi
.

The recurrence chain
R
is compact. Let us fix some small
\epsilon>0
and take finitely many points
p_{1}\cdots p_{r}
in
R
, whose
\epsilon
-neighborhoods cover
R
. We may additionally assume that the
\epsilon
-neighborhood of
p_{i}
is included in one set of the cover
B
. For every pair
(p_{i},p_{j})
, take an
\epsilon
-pseudo-orbits
c_{i,j}
from
p_{i}
to
p_{j}
. We can assume that the length of the last orbit arc in
c_{i,j}
is also at least
T
. For instance, take a pseudo-orbit from
p_{i}
to
\varphi_{-T}(p_{j})
and concatenate the orbit arc
\varphi_{[-T,0]}(p_{j})
). Let
L
be the largest length of the pseudo-orbits
c_{i,j}
.
Denote by
E\subset H_{1}(M,\mathbb{R})
the set of the homology classes of the curves obtained by closing any curve
c_{i,j}
with any
B
-bounded curve from
p_{j}
to
p_{i}
. Clearly
E
is finite.

Using Lemma
A.5
, there exists
x
in
R
for which the homology class
\tfrac{1}{t}[\gamma_{x,t}]
converges toward
[\mu]_{\varphi}
when
t
goes to
+\infty
. Recall that
\gamma_{x,t}
is the orbit arc
\varphi_{[0,t]}(x)
concatenated with a
B
-bounded curve.

Take some
t>0
, and
i,j
that satisfy
d(p_{i},x)<\epsilon
and
d(p_{j},\varphi(t,x))<\epsilon
. The concatenation of
\varphi_{[0,t]}(x)
and
c_{i,j}
yields a periodic
\epsilon
-pseudo-orbit, which we denote by
\delta_{t}
. It satisfies
t\leq\len(\delta_{t})\leq t+L
and
[\gamma_{x,t}]-[\delta_{t}]
lies in
E
. So when
t
goes to
+\infty
, the distance between
\tfrac{1}{\len(\delta_{t})}[\delta_{t}]
and
\tfrac{1}{t}[\gamma_{x,t}]
goes to zero. It follows that
\tfrac{1}{\len(\delta_{t})}[\delta_{t}]
converges toward
[\mu]_{\varphi}
when
t
goes to
+\infty
. It implies that
[\mu]_{\varphi}
lies in
D_{\varphi}
. Therefore
D^{S}_{\varphi}\subset\conv(D_{\varphi})
holds true.
∎

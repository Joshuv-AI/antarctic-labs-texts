Comparative Evaluation of Preoperative Ultrasonography and Mammography in Breast Carcinoma: Correlation With Intraoperative and Histopathological Findings
Authors: Parmar Bhargav, Abhinav K Tiwari, Aditi Mittal, Raumil Parmar, Komal Khuman, Divyakant Barot, Vibhor Jain, Vivek Maurya, Dishant Parmar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Introduction: Accurate preoperative imaging is essential for optimal surgical planning in breast carcinoma. Mammography and ultrasonography are commonly used imaging modalities; however, their relative effectiveness in tumor size estimation and lymph node evaluation varies. This study aimed to compare the diagnostic role of preoperative ultrasonography and mammography in breast carcinoma and to correlate imaging findings with intraoperative and histopathological outcomes. Materials and methods: This prospective comparative observational study included 50 female patients with malignant breast disease undergoing elective surgery at a tertiary care teaching hospital. All patients underwent preoperative mammography and ultrasonography. Imaging findings were correlated with intraoperative observations and histopathological examination. Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS Statistics version 26.0 (IBM Corp. Released 2019. IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 26.0. Armonk, NY), with p < 0.05 considered statistically significant. Results: The mean age of patients was 51.38 ± 9.65 years. Ductal carcinoma was the most common histopathological subtype (62%, n = 31). Ultrasonography demonstrated a mean tumor size of 2.97 ± 0.46 cm, which was closer to the intraoperative tumor size (3.49 cm) than the mammographic estimate of 2.53 ± 0.41 cm. Both ultrasonographic and mammographic tumor-size estimates differed significantly from the intraoperative measurements (both p < 0.001). Ultrasonography showed a stronger association with intraoperative lymph node findings than mammography. Conclusions: Preoperative ultrasonography yielded a mean tumor-size estimate closer to the reference measurement than mammography and showed a stronger association with intraoperative lymph node findings. However, the combined use of both imaging modalities provides optimal diagnostic assessment and should be routinely employed in the preoperative evaluation of breast carcinoma.

Introduction

Breast carcinoma is the most commonly diagnosed malignancy among women worldwide and remains a major cause of cancer-related mortality [1]. In India, its incidence continues to rise, particularly in urban areas, with delayed diagnosis remaining a significant challenge due to limited access to screening and diagnostics in many regions [2,3]. Accurate preoperative imaging is therefore essential for early diagnosis and appropriate surgical planning.

Breast carcinoma arises from the epithelial lining of the terminal duct-lobular unit, with invasive ductal carcinoma and invasive lobular carcinoma being the most common histological subtypes [4]. Patients may present with various symptoms, including mastalgia, nodularity, or a palpable breast lump, the latter requiring prompt and systematic evaluation [5].

The triple assessment approach (clinical examination, imaging, and tissue diagnosis) has become the standard for evaluating breast lesions, providing high diagnostic accuracy while reducing unnecessary surgical biopsies [6]. Mammography is widely accepted as the primary imaging modality for breast carcinoma screening and evaluation, particularly for detecting microcalcifications; however, its sensitivity is reduced in women with dense breast tissue due to tissue overlap [7,8].

Ultrasonography is a valuable adjunct to mammography, especially in dense breasts, as it enables improved lesion characterization and assessment of axillary lymph nodes [9,10]. Several studies have demonstrated that the combined use of mammography and ultrasonography improves diagnostic accuracy compared to either modality alone [11,12].

The Breast Imaging Reporting and Data System (BI-RADS) is a standardized classification developed by the American College of Radiology to categorize breast imaging findings based on the likelihood of malignancy. BI-RADS IV represents suspicious lesions requiring biopsy, while BI-RADS V denotes lesions highly suggestive of malignancy. In this study, BI-RADS IV and V lesions were analyzed for correlation with intraoperative and histopathological findings [13].

This study aimed to compare preoperative ultrasonography and mammography for tumor-size estimation and axillary lymph-node assessment in patients with breast carcinoma, using histopathological findings as the reference standard.

Materials and methods

Study design and setting

This was a hospital-based, prospective, comparative observational study conducted in the Department of General Surgery at a tertiary care teaching hospital, Gajra Raja Medical College, Gwalior, India, over 12 months, from October 2020 to September 2021.

Study population

The study included 50 female patients diagnosed with breast carcinoma who were planned for elective surgical management during the study period. All eligible patients were informed about the study protocol, and written informed consent was obtained before enrollment.

Eligibility criteria

Female patients aged between 18 and 70 years with a diagnosis of malignant breast disease and planned for elective surgery were included in the study. Patients were excluded if they were younger than 18 years or older than 70 years, pregnant, had recurrent breast carcinoma, had received prior chemotherapy or radiotherapy, were unfit for anesthesia, had uncontrolled coagulopathy, were receiving anticoagulant therapy, refused participation, or were lost to follow-up before final evaluation.

Data collection and imaging evaluation

A structured questionnaire was developed by the authors specifically for this study. Clinical data were collected using a structured pro forma that included demographic details, clinical presentation, and physical examination findings. All patients underwent routine laboratory investigations along with preoperative breast imaging, including mammography and ultrasonography. Imaging findings were documented systematically for each patient.​​​​​​​​​​​​​​

Surgical procedure and histopathological correlation

Preoperative mammographic and ultrasonographic findings were compared with operative observations recorded during surgical intervention. All patients underwent modified radical mastectomy according to standard surgical guidelines. Resected specimens were sent for histopathological examination, which was considered the reference standard for assessing tumor size and axillary lymph node involvement. Operative findings were recorded separately as intraoperative observations.​​​​​​​

Statistical analysis

Data were entered into Excel (Microsoft Corp., Redmond, WA, USA) and analyzed using SPSS Statistics version 26.0 (IBM Corp. Released 2019. IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 26.0. Armonk, NY). Continuous variables were expressed as mean and standard deviation, and categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages. Because tumor-size measurements were obtained from the same patients by ultrasonography, mammography, and histopathological assessment, the comparisons represented paired observations. Statistical tests were interpreted accordingly. For lymph-node assessment, histopathological findings were considered the reference standard. A p-value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant.​​​​​​​

Ethics statement

Approval for the study was obtained from the Institutional Ethics Committee of Gajra Raja Medical College prior to commencement (approval number: 36/IEC-GRMC/2019). Written informed consent was obtained from all human participants before enrollment. Patient confidentiality was strictly maintained, and no identifiable personal information was included in the study.

Ultrasonography demonstrated a statistically significant difference in tumor size compared with histopathology (t = −8.39, df = 49, p < 0.001). Mammography also demonstrated a statistically significant difference from histopathological tumor size (t = −12.32, df = 49, p < 0.001). The smaller magnitude of the difference in mean measurements observed with ultrasonography suggests that its mean tumor-size estimate was closer to the histopathological mean than that of mammography. Overall, ultrasonography yielded a mean tumor size estimate closer to that of histopathological assessment than mammography. However, the present analysis should be interpreted as a comparison of mean measurements rather than a formal agreement analysis.

Discussion

In the present study, ultrasonography yielded a mean tumor size estimate closer to that of histopathological assessment than mammography, indicating a smaller difference from the histopathological mean [8,9]. This finding is consistent with prior studies that have reported improved concordance between ultrasonographic and pathological tumor measurements, particularly in patients with dense breast tissue [13,14]. Accurate preoperative assessment of tumor size is critical for surgical planning, selection of breast-conserving surgery, and achieving clear resection margins, underscoring the clinical relevance of these findings [15].

Mammography yielded a lower mean tumor-size estimate than the reference measurement in the present study, a limitation widely reported in the literature. The observed underestimation may be related to factors such as tissue superimposition and reduced lesion conspicuity described in previous studies [16,17]. However, breast density was not systematically recorded in the present cohort; therefore, its specific contribution to the observed difference could not be evaluated. Although mammography remains the cornerstone of breast carcinoma screening due to its high sensitivity for lesion detection, several studies have highlighted its limited accuracy in determining true tumor dimensions [13,18]. Our findings further support these observations and reinforce the need for adjunct imaging modalities when precise assessment of tumor size is required [19].

Despite mammography classifying a greater proportion of lesions as BI-RADS V, this did not translate into improved accuracy for tumor size measurement. Similar discrepancies between BI-RADS categorization and actual tumor size have been described in previous studies, suggesting that while BI-RADS effectively stratifies malignancy risk, it may not reliably predict tumor extent, as BI-RADS assessment focuses on lesion suspicion rather than dimensional accuracy [18]. In contrast, ultrasonography allows real-time imaging, improved lesion-margin delineation, and better differentiation between tumor tissue and surrounding parenchyma, particularly in dense breasts, thereby facilitating tumor-size assessment [9,20].

Axillary lymph node assessment in the present study demonstrated the highest intraoperative detection rate, followed by mammography and ultrasonography. Ultrasonography detected lymph nodes in 68% of patients, compared with 72% for mammography. However, imaging-based or operative lymph node detection does not establish metastatic involvement; therefore, histopathological examination remains the appropriate reference standard for diagnosing nodal metastasis [21]. This is consistent with existing evidence indicating that ultrasonography is superior in evaluating axillary nodal morphology, including cortical thickening, eccentric cortical hypertrophy, and loss of the fatty hilum, features strongly associated with metastatic involvement [22,23]. Mammography, by contrast, has limited sensitivity for assessing axillary lymph nodes and is largely restricted to detecting gross nodal enlargement or calcifications [23].

The representative case included in this study further illustrates the complementary roles of mammography and ultrasonography in the preoperative evaluation of breast carcinoma, with imaging findings corroborated by cytology and histopathology. However, the findings should be interpreted in light of the study’s sample size and single-center design. Taken together, the present findings align with existing literature and support the combined use of mammography and ultrasonography to improve diagnostic accuracy, optimize surgical planning, and enhance preoperative staging in patients with breast carcinoma [24].

Limitations

The study was limited by its relatively small sample size and single-center design. Inter-observer variability and formal blinding were not assessed, and breast density was not systematically recorded. These factors should be considered when interpreting the findings. Further multicenter studies with larger sample sizes are recommended.

Conclusions

In this prospective comparative study of 50 patients with breast carcinoma, ultrasonography yielded a mean tumor-size estimate closer to the reference measurement than mammography and showed a stronger association with intraoperative lymph node findings. Mammography remained valuable for lesion detection and classification, providing complementary diagnostic information. Therefore, ultrasonography and mammography should be regarded as complementary rather than mutually exclusive modalities, and their combined use may provide a more comprehensive preoperative assessment to guide surgical management in patients with breast carcinoma.

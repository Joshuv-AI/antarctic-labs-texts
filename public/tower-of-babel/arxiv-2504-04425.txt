All-vortex nonlinear Compton scattering in a polarized laser field
Yi Liao, Quan-Yu Wang, Yuanbin Wu

Abstract
The process of all-vortex nonlinear Compton scattering in an intense and polarized laser field, in which the initial and final electrons and the emitted γ \gamma photon are all in vortex states, is studied theoretically. We develop a formalism for the process, which allows us to study the exchanges of the orbital angular momentum (OAM) and spin angular momentum among the electron, γ \gamma photon, and laser. A wave packet of the Bessel-Gaussian type is adopted to describe the initial vortex electron for the purpose of normalization. Both circularly and linearly polarized lasers are examined. Substantial numerical calculations are performed to reveal the physics of the exchanges of the OAM and spin angular momentum. The strong impact of the laser intensity and the opening angle of the initial vortex electron is demonstrated. Our results also suggest possible scenarios for the separation of the emitted γ \gamma photons with different OAMs, as well as a possible way which could help to distinguish the OAM and spin of the vortex particle by the multi-peak structure in the spectrum of the emitted photon.

Analytical calculation

II.1
Vortex states and their normalization

We begin with the definitions and conventions for the process under consideration: a vortex electron collides head-on with a monochromatic, plane-wave, strong laser to radiate a vortex photon while continuing to stay in a vortex state. We assume the laser propagates in the
(-z)
axis with the four-momentum
\Omega^{\mu}=\omega(1,0,0,-1)
, where
\omega
is its angular frequency. The laser is either linearly polarized, which we choose without loss of generality to be in the
x
axis,

\displaystyle a^{\mu}(\tau)=M\xi\epsilon_{1}^{\mu}\cos\tau,

(1)

where
\tau=\Omega\cdot x
and
\epsilon_{1}^{\mu}=(0,1,0,0)
(and
\epsilon_{2}^{\mu}=(0,0,1,0)
for the following use), or circularly polarized which we choose to be left-handed,

\displaystyle a^{\mu}(\tau)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}M\xi\left(\epsilon_{1}^{\mu}\cos\tau+\epsilon_{2}^{\mu}\sin\tau\right).

(2)

Here
M
is the electron mass, and
\xi
is a dimensionless parameter so that the electric field strength has the amplitude
E=E_{\textrm{cr}}\xi\omega/M=M\xi\omega/|e|
with
E_{\textrm{cr}}=M^{2}/|e|
being the Schwinger critical field strength. We have chosen for both polarizations the same time-average of the electric field squared,
\overline{{\bf E}^{2}}=(M\xi\omega/e)^{2}/2
, or
|\overline{a^{2}}|=(M\xi)^{2}/2
.

II.1.1
Bessel vortex state

In this work we consider first the case in which all of the initial and final electrons and the final photon are in an ideal Bessel vortex state that is the simplest one that exhibits essential features of a vortex. Since the Bessel vortex is not square-integrable, we will incorporate a Gaussian smearing to the initial electron vortex, i.e., the so-called Bessel-Gaussian vortex, so that the scattering rate can be well defined. In the future work, we will investigate a scenario that would be closer to the experimental situation: the initial electron in a Laguerre-Gauss state collides against a counter-propagating laser pulse that has a finite transverse distance (impact parameter) to the electron.

The kernel of a Bessel vortex is, choosing the
z
axis as the longitudinal direction
[
89
]
,

\displaystyle a_{\kappa\ell}({\bf p}_{\perp})=i^{-\ell}e^{i\ell\phi_{p}}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{\kappa}}\delta(p_{\perp}-\kappa),~~~p_{\perp}=|{\bf p}_{\perp}|,

(3)

where
\ell
is an integer that will become in the coordinate representation the projection of OAM in the
z
axis and offers the azimuthal phase essential to a vortex state, and
\kappa
is a specified magnitude of the transverse momentum. We denote the components of a vector in the transverse plane by a subscript
\perp
. Then a Bessel vortex electron that propagates in the
z
axis has the wavefunction in free space:

\displaystyle\psi(x)=e^{i(p_{z}z-Et)}\int\frac{d^{2}{\bf p}_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}a_{\kappa\ell}({\bf p}_{\perp})e^{i{\bf p}_{\perp}\cdot{\bf x}_{\perp}}u(p,\sigma).

(4)

Here
u(p,\sigma)
is the usual bispinor that can be built by

u(p,\sigma)=(E+M)^{-1/2}(M+\not{p})u({\bf 0},\sigma)
, where
u({\bf 0},\sigma)
(with
\sigma=\pm
) is the normalized bispinor for an electron in its rest frame with spin projection
\sigma/2
in the
z
direction. In the Dirac or standard representation
[
90
]
,

\displaystyle u(p,\sigma)=\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\sqrt{E+M}w_{\sigma}\\
\sqrt{E-M}\hat{\bf p}\cdot\vec{\sigma}w_{\sigma}\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle w_{+}=\left(\begin{array}[]{r}1\\
0\end{array}\right),~~~w_{-}=\left(\begin{array}[]{r}0\\
1\end{array}\right),

with
\sigma^{3}w_{\sigma}=\sigma w_{\sigma}
. Note that
u
is normalized by
\bar{u}u=2M
, and we will handle the normalization of the vortex state soon.

The presence of a plane-wave laser background specifies a direction in spacetime, thus not all Descartes components of momentum are conserved between the initial and final states. Following the usual practice, we work with the light-front coordinates to better preserve energy-momentum conservation
[
91
]
. As we have assumed the laser propagates in the
(-z)
axis, we denote for any four-vector
x^{\mu}=(x^{0},{\bf x}_{\perp},x^{3})
,

\displaystyle x_{\pm}=x^{0}\pm x^{3},

(6)

where from now on we will use the superscripts and subscripts for the
\pm
components arbitrarily, so that the scalar product, integration measure, and
\delta
functions between the Descartes and light-front coordinates are related by

\displaystyle x\cdot y=x^{0}y^{0}-{\bf x}\cdot{\bf y}=\frac{1}{2}x_{+}y_{-}+\frac{1}{2}x_{-}y_{+}-{\bf x}_{\perp}\cdot{\bf y}_{\perp},

(7a)

\displaystyle dx^{0}d^{3}{\bf x}=\frac{1}{2}dx_{+}dx_{-}d^{2}{\bf x}_{\perp},

(7b)

\displaystyle\delta^{4}(x-y)=\delta(x^{0}-y^{0})\delta^{3}({\bf x}-{\bf y})=2\delta(x_{+}-y_{+})\delta(x_{-}-y_{-})\delta^{2}({\bf x}_{\perp}-{\bf y}_{\perp}).

(7c)

In particular the on-shell condition for a particle of momentum
p
and mass
M
is,
p^{2}=p_{+}p_{-}-{\bf p}_{\perp}^{2}=M^{2}
. Employing the light-front coordinates and noting that conservation of the plus component of momentum is not affected by the presence of the laser due to
\Omega_{+}=0
,
the Bessel-vortex state for the radiated photon can be designated by
k_{+}
among other quantities:

\displaystyle A^{\mu}_{k_{+}\kappa_{\gamma}\ell_{\gamma}\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-i(k_{+}x_{-}+k_{-}x_{+})/2}\int\frac{d^{2}{\bf k}_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}a_{\kappa_{\gamma}\ell_{\gamma}}({\bf k})e^{i{\bf k}_{\perp}\cdot{\bf x}_{\perp}}\Lambda^{\mu}(k,\lambda).

(8)

Here
\Lambda^{\mu}(k,\lambda)
is the polarization vector for a plane-wave photon of momentum
k
and helicity
\lambda
, which cannot be taken outside of the integral. It is convenient to parameterize it with the help of
\epsilon_{\lambda}
and
\Omega

[
92
,
93
]
:

\displaystyle\Lambda(k,\lambda)=\epsilon_{\lambda}^{*}-\frac{k\cdot\epsilon^{*}_{\lambda}}{k\cdot\Omega}\Omega,

(9)

where
\epsilon_{\lambda}=(\epsilon_{1}+i\lambda\epsilon_{2})/\sqrt{2}
with
\Lambda(k,\lambda)\cdot k=0
and
\Lambda^{*}(k,\lambda_{1})\cdot\Lambda(k,\lambda_{2})=-\delta_{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}
. And
\kappa_{\gamma}
is the magnitude of its transverse momentum and the integer
\ell_{\gamma}
denotes the projection of its angular momentum in the longitudinal direction.

When immersed in a strong plane-wave laser, the so-called Bessel-Volkov solution to the Dirac equation takes into account the laser field exactly. The wavefunctions for the initial and final electrons are denoted respectively by
[
49
,
94
]

\displaystyle\psi_{p_{+}\kappa\ell\sigma}^{(a)}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-i(p_{+}x_{-}+p_{-}x_{+})/2}\int\frac{d^{2}{\bf p}_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}a_{\kappa\ell}({\bf p}_{\perp})e^{i{\bf p}_{\perp}\cdot{\bf x}_{\perp}}e^{i\Phi_{p}(\tau)}\left[1+\frac{\not{\Omega}\not{a}}{2p\cdot\Omega}\right]u(p,\sigma),

(10a)

\displaystyle\psi_{p^{\prime}_{+}\kappa^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}^{(a)}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-i(p^{\prime}_{+}x_{-}+p^{\prime}_{-}x_{+})/2}\int\frac{d^{2}{\bf p}^{\prime}_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}a_{\kappa^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}}({\bf p}^{\prime}_{\perp})e^{i{\bf p}^{\prime}_{\perp}\cdot{\bf x}_{\perp}}e^{i\Phi_{p^{\prime}}(\tau)}\left[1+\frac{\not{\Omega}\not{a}}{2p^{\prime}\cdot\Omega}\right]u(p^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}),

(10b)

where the superscript
(a)
indicates the presence of the laser background

\displaystyle\Phi_{p}(\tau)=\int^{\tau}d\tau^{\prime}\left[-\frac{p\cdot a(\tau^{\prime})}{p\cdot\Omega}+\frac{a^{2}(\tau^{\prime})}{2p\cdot\Omega}\right].

(11)

Here
\kappa
(
\kappa^{\prime}
) is the magnitude of the transverse momentum of the initial (final) electron and the integer
\ell
(

) denotes the longitudinal projection of its angular momentum.

Now we consider normalization and related issues. The normalization of a Bessel vortex state in free space has been well discussed in the literature
[

74

. In the presence of a laser background the normalization of a Bessel vortex state for a neutral particle is obtained by replacing Descartes components by light-front components; for instance, for the radiated Bessel photon, the normalization factor

\displaystyle N_{k}^{\textrm{B}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2k_{+}}}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{RL_{-}}},

(12a)

\displaystyle dn_{k}=\frac{Rdk_{\perp}}{\pi}\frac{L_{-}dk_{+}}{4\pi},

(12b)

\displaystyle(N_{k}^{\textrm{B}})^{2}dn_{k}=\frac{dk_{\perp}dk_{+}}{2\pi 2k_{+}}.

(12c)

Here

is a large length of the coordinate component that is conjugate to the conserved plus component of momentum, and

is a large enough radius in the transverse plane beyond which the wavefunction almost vanishes. Since the vortex state of a charged particle is modified by a strong laser field, we have to recalculate its normalization. For the Bessel-Volkov solution of the electron in Eq. (

II.1.2
Bessel-Gaussian vortex state

Finally we discuss the Bessel-Gaussian vortex for the initial electron which is square-integrable in the transverse plane, and we will employ it for numerical analysis. It is an integral of the wavefunction in Eq. (
10a
) over the magnitude
\kappa
of the transverse momentum weighted by the Gaussian function

with the central value
\kappa_{0}
and deviation

[

\displaystyle\psi_{p_{+}\kappa_{0}\sigma_{\perp}\ell\sigma}^{{(a)}\textrm{BG}}(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\kappa~\psi_{p_{+}\kappa\ell\sigma}^{(a)}(x)f(\kappa;\kappa_{0},\sigma_{\perp}),

(13)

where

\displaystyle f(\kappa;\kappa_{0},\sigma_{\perp})=N(\kappa_{0},\sigma_{\perp})\exp\bigg[-\frac{(\kappa-\kappa_{0})^{2}}{2\sigma_{\perp}^{2}}\bigg],

(14)

whose square normalization to unity yields

\displaystyle N^{-2}(\kappa_{0},\sigma_{\perp})=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\pi}\sigma_{\perp}\left[1+\textrm{erf}\left(\frac{\kappa_{0}}{\sigma_{\perp}}\right)\right].

(15)

Since we require that the Bessel-Gaussian vortex be on-shell and

be a good quantum number in the presence of a laser, smearing in

according to

The issue of normalization has to be reconsidered for a Bessel-Volkov solution whose magnitude of transverse momentum is Gaussian smeared, i.e., Eq. (
13

is expressed in Eq. (

\bar{\psi}_{p_{+}\kappa_{0}\sigma_{\perp}\ell\sigma}^{(a\textrm{BG})}
; for instance, the Gaussian integration variable in
\bar{\psi}_{p_{+}\kappa_{0}\sigma_{\perp}\ell\sigma}^{(a\textrm{BG})}
is
\tilde{\kappa}
. With a fixed

,

is the same in both

and

; for relevant definitions, see
Appendix

. We obtain

\displaystyle j_{+}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d\kappa\int d\tilde{\kappa}~f\tilde{f}e^{i(\tilde{p}_{-}-p_{-})x_{+}/2}\sqrt{\frac{\kappa\tilde{\kappa}}{(2\pi)^{2}}}

(16)

\displaystyle\times\Big[(\tilde{\epsilon}_{+}+\tilde{\epsilon}_{-}\tilde{c})(\epsilon_{+}+\epsilon_{-}c)J_{\ell}(\kappa{\cal R}_{\perp})J_{\ell}(\tilde{\kappa}{\cal R}_{\perp})+\tilde{\epsilon}_{-}\epsilon_{-}\tilde{s}sJ_{\ell+\sigma}(\kappa{\cal R}_{\perp})J_{\ell+\sigma}(\tilde{\kappa}{\cal R}_{\perp})\Big],

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}xdx~J_{\ell}(\kappa x)J_{\ell}(\kappa^{\prime}x)=\frac{1}{\kappa}\delta(\kappa-\kappa^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\int\frac{1}{2}dx^{-}d^{2}{\bf x}_{\perp}~j_{+}(x)=\frac{L_{-}}{2}\int 2\pi{\cal R}_{\perp}d{\cal R}_{\perp}~j_{+}(x)=\frac{L_{-}}{2}2p^{+}.

This is as nice as anticipated: the state is normalized in the transverse plane of space and is normalized as for a plane wave in the minus axis. In summary, for the Bessel photon and Bessel-Volkov electron states we employ the normalization factor as in Eq. (

\displaystyle N_{p}^{\textrm{BG}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2p_{+}}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{L_{-}}}.

II.2
Scattering amplitude for plane-wave electrons and photon

Since a vortex state is a linear composition of infinitely many plane-wave states, the

\displaystyle S_{\textrm{B}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{2}{\bf p}_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int\frac{d^{2}{\bf p}^{\prime}_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int\frac{d^{2}{\bf k}_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}a^{\dagger}_{\kappa_{\gamma}\ell_{\gamma}}({\bf k}_{\perp})a^{\dagger}_{\kappa^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}}({\bf p}^{\prime}_{\perp})a_{\kappa\ell}({\bf p}_{\perp})S_{\textrm{pw}},

where

\displaystyle S_{\textrm{pw}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-ie\int d^{4}x~e^{-iP\cdot x}e^{i[\Phi_{p}(\tau)-\Phi_{p^{\prime}}(\tau)]}U,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\mu}(k,\lambda)\overline{u(p^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})}\left[1+\frac{\not{a}\not{\Omega}}{2p^{\prime}\cdot\Omega}\right]\gamma_{\mu}\left[1+\frac{\not{\Omega}\not{a}}{2p\cdot\Omega}\right]u(p,\sigma),

II.4

II.4.1

On taking the absolute square of

\displaystyle 2\pi\delta(P_{+})=\int\frac{1}{2}dx_{-}~\exp\Big(\frac{i}{2}x_{-}P_{+}\Big)\Longrightarrow\big[2\pi\delta(P_{+})\big]^{2}=2\pi\delta(P_{+})\frac{L_{-}}{2}.

More care must be practiced for

\displaystyle 2\pi\delta(u_{n}\Omega_{-}+P_{-})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Omega_{-}}2\pi\delta(u+2\beta-n)\Big|_{u=-P_{-}/\Omega_{-}}.

Squaring the above gives

\displaystyle\big[2\pi\delta(u_{n}\Omega_{-}+P_{-})\big]^{2}=\frac{1}{\Omega_{-}^{2}}\big[2\pi\delta(u+2\beta-n)|_{u=-P_{-}/\Omega_{-}}]^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Omega_{-}^{2}}2\pi\delta(u+2\beta-n)|_{u=-P_{-}/\Omega_{-}}\int d\tau~e^{i0\cdot\tau}=2\pi\delta(u_{n}\Omega_{-}+P_{-})\frac{L_{+}}{2}.

\displaystyle|S_{\textrm{B}}^{\textrm{P}}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{2}\frac{\kappa}{2\pi}\frac{\kappa^{\prime}}{2\pi}\frac{\kappa_{\gamma}}{2\pi}\frac{1}{2(E+M)(E^{\prime}+M)}\frac{1}{(2\Delta)^{2}}2\pi\delta(P_{+})\frac{L_{-}}{2}\frac{L_{+}}{2}\frac{2}{\pi}\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}\delta(u_{n}\Omega_{-}+P_{-})|\Upsilon_{n}^{\textrm{P}}|^{2}.

\displaystyle d\Gamma^{\textrm{P}}_{\textrm{B}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|S_{\textrm{B}}^{\textrm{P}}|^{2}}{L_{+}}(N_{p}^{\textrm{B}})^{2}\frac{d\kappa^{\prime}dp^{\prime}_{+}}{2p^{\prime}_{+}2\pi}\frac{d\kappa_{\gamma}dk_{+}}{2k_{+}2\pi}

\displaystyle e^{2}\frac{\kappa}{2\pi}\frac{\kappa^{\prime}}{2\pi}\frac{\kappa_{\gamma}}{2\pi}\frac{1}{2(E+M)(E^{\prime}+M)}\frac{1}{2\Delta^{2}}\frac{\pi}{R}\frac{1}{2p_{+}}\delta(P_{+})\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}\delta(u_{n}\Omega_{-}+P_{-})|\Upsilon_{n}^{\textrm{P}}|^{2}\frac{d\kappa^{\prime}dp^{\prime}_{+}}{2p^{\prime}_{+}2\pi}\frac{d\kappa_{\gamma}dk_{+}}{2k_{+}2\pi}.

II.4.2
Modifications for an initial Bessel-Gaussian vortex electron

The above result for the decay rate is singular at
\Delta=0
when the triangle formed by the three transverse momenta
{\bf p}_{\perp}={\bf p}^{\prime}_{\perp}+{\bf k}_{\perp}
shrinks to a line. This singularity of order

Since such a vortex is a linear composition of the Bessel-Volkov solution to the Dirac equation which by itself is also linear, it is still a solution. But it is not unique to compose the solution. As we argued previously, it is more natural to employ light-front coordinates in the presence of a plane-wave laser. We therefore propose to work with a Gaussian vortex of fixed plus momentum, Eq. (
13

There are two modifications to the first equality for decay rate in Eq. (

). First, the normalization factor
N_{p}^{\textrm{B}}
for the initial electron should be replaced by
N_{p}^{\textrm{BG}}
in Eq. (
19
), which effectively removes the factor
\pi/R
while keeping
1/(2p_{+})
in the second equality of Eq. (
67
). Second, the
S
matrix element
S_{\textrm{B}}^{\textrm{P}}
in Eq. (
59
) should be weighted by the Gaussian function in Eq. (
14
) and integrated over
\kappa
to become the
S
matrix element
S_{\textrm{BG}}^{\textrm{P}}
, as we detail below.

Let us enumerate all
\kappa
dependence other than the Gaussian function in
S_{\textrm{B}}^{\textrm{P}}
. First, the trivial square root factors. Second,
\delta(u_{n}\Omega_{-}+P_{-})
depends on
\kappa
through
p_{-}
in
P_{-}
. Since
p_{+}\delta p_{-}-2\kappa\delta\kappa=0
for an on-shell electron with fixed
p_{+}
,
\delta p_{-}\ll\delta\kappa
for
\kappa\ll p_{+}
which is the vortex that can be practically realized. Note
\beta
in
u_{n}=n-2\beta
is independent of
\kappa
, see Eq. (
33a
). That there are two solutions to
n
on varying
\kappa
requires at least
\kappa\delta\kappa\geq\omega p_{+}
. Considering further the Gaussian suppression for
\kappa
deviating from its central value, the effect due to
\kappa
variation must be tiny. Neglecting it there will be no interference between different
n
terms when squaring
S_{\textrm{B}}^{\textrm{P}}
as is the case with pure Bessel vortex states. The third source of
\kappa
dependence is
\Upsilon_{n}^{\textrm{P}}
, Eq. (
II.3.3
). The good news is that the argument
\gamma^{\textrm{C}}
in Eq. (
33a
) of the Bessel function in the case of a circularly polarized laser is independent of
\kappa
. This is because the magnitude
\rho
is actually independent of transverse momenta although its definition as a vector in Eq. (
33b
) involves them. We verify this as follows. By definition,

\displaystyle\vec{\rho}^{2}=\frac{k_{+}^{2}}{p_{+}^{4}}p_{\perp}^{2}+\frac{k_{\perp}^{2}}{p_{+}^{2}}-\frac{k_{+}}{p_{+}^{3}}2{\bf k}_{\perp}\cdot{\bf p}_{\perp}.

(68)

Using
2k\cdot p=k_{+}p_{-}+k_{-}p_{+}-2{\bf k}_{\perp}\cdot{\bf p}_{\perp}
and on-shell conditions
p_{\perp}^{2}=p_{+}p_{-}-M^{2}
and
k_{\perp}^{2}=k_{+}k_{-}
to replace all scalar products of transverse momenta, the above becomes

\displaystyle\vec{\rho}^{2}=\frac{k_{+}}{p_{+}^{3}}2k\cdot p-\frac{k_{+}^{2}}{p_{+}^{4}}M^{2}.

(69)

Identifying
u
in Eq. (
30
) with
u_{n}
through the
\delta
function, we have
2k\cdot p=u_{n}2p^{\prime}_{+}\omega
, so that

\displaystyle\vec{\rho}^{2}=\frac{k_{+}}{p_{+}^{3}}u_{n}2p^{\prime}_{+}\omega-\frac{k_{+}^{2}}{p_{+}^{4}}M^{2},

(70)

which is independent of transverse momenta as claimed. In the case of a linearly polarized laser, as we show in Appendix
C
this only brings in integral phases of
\varphi_{0}
as in the case of circular polarization. The remaining
\kappa
dependence in
\Upsilon_{n}^{\textrm{P}}
appears in the form of polynomials in
\kappa,~E,~p_{-}
and in the form of integer phases of
\delta^{\prime},~\delta_{k}
.

All
\kappa
dependence mentioned above is smooth. The only apparently singular behavior enters through the factor
\Delta^{-1}
, which we now turn to discuss. To simplify notations, we will use the variables:

\displaystyle x=\kappa,~b=\kappa^{\prime},~c=\kappa_{\gamma}.

(71)

Given a point in the final-state phase space, that transverse momenta form a triangle demands

\displaystyle x_{1}\leq x\leq x_{2},~~~x_{1}=|b-c|,~~~x_{2}=b+c.

(72)

The factor
\Delta^{-1}
introduces apparent singularities at the delimiters of the integral over
\kappa=x
:

\displaystyle\Delta=\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{(x+x_{1})(x+x_{2})}\sqrt{(x_{2}-x)(x-x_{1})}.

(73)

The scattering matrix element
S_{\textrm{B}}^{\textrm{P}}
in Eq. (
59
) is replaced by

\displaystyle S_{\textrm{BG}}^{\textrm{P}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-ie)i^{-\Delta\ell}(2\pi)\delta(P_{+})\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}2\delta(u_{n}\Omega_{-}+P_{-})I^{\textrm{P}}_{\textrm{BG}},

(74)

where

\displaystyle I^{\textrm{P}}_{\textrm{BG}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}dx\frac{g(x)}{\sqrt{(x_{2}-x)(x-x_{1})}},

(75a)

\displaystyle g(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2f(x;\kappa_{0},\sigma_{\perp})}{\sqrt{(x+x_{1})(x+x_{2})}}\bigg[\sqrt{\frac{\kappa}{2\pi}\frac{\kappa^{\prime}}{2\pi}\frac{\kappa_{\gamma}}{2\pi}}\frac{e^{i\eta(\ell^{\prime}\delta^{\prime}-\ell_{\gamma}\delta_{k})}}{\sqrt{2(E+M)(E^{\prime}+M)}}\Upsilon_{n}^{\textrm{P}}\bigg]_{\kappa=x}.

(75b)

We have set
\kappa
in
\delta(u_{n}\Omega_{-}+P_{-})
to its central value
\kappa_{0}
, i.e.,
p_{-}=(M^{2}+\kappa_{0}^{2})/p_{+}
, and included in the function
g(x)
all smooth dependence of
\kappa
and the Gaussian function. The integral
I_{\textrm{BG}}
is manipulated in Appendix
D
into a form that involves no apparent singularities and is amenable to numerical computation.
The decay rate is,

\displaystyle d\Gamma^{\textrm{P}}_{\textrm{BG}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|S_{\textrm{BG}}^{\textrm{P}}|^{2}}{L_{+}}(N_{p}^{\textrm{BG}})^{2}\frac{d\kappa^{\prime}dp^{\prime}_{+}}{2p^{\prime}_{+}2\pi}\frac{d\kappa_{\gamma}dk_{+}}{2k_{+}2\pi}

(76)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{2}2\delta(P_{+})\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}\delta(u_{n}\Omega_{-}+P_{-})|I^{\textrm{P}}_{\textrm{BG}}|^{2}\frac{1}{2p_{+}}\frac{d\kappa^{\prime}dp^{\prime}_{+}}{2p^{\prime}_{+}2\pi}\frac{d\kappa_{\gamma}dk_{+}}{2k_{+}2\pi}.

The
\kappa^{\prime}
and
p^{\prime}_{+}
integrals are finished by the two
\delta
functions, i.e.,

\displaystyle p^{\prime}_{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E(1+v\cos\theta_{p})-\omega_{\gamma}(1+\cos\theta_{\gamma}),

(77a)

\displaystyle\kappa^{\prime 2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa^{2}+\omega^{2}_{\gamma}\sin^{2}\theta_{\gamma}+2n\omega\left[E(1+v\cos\theta_{p})-\omega_{\gamma}(1+\cos\theta_{\gamma})\right]

(77b)

\displaystyle-2\omega E(1-v\cos\theta_{p}\cos\theta_{\gamma})-\frac{1}{2}M^{2}\xi^{2}\frac{\omega_{\gamma}(1+\cos\theta_{\gamma})}{E(1+v\cos\theta_{p})},

where
v
is the magnitude of the velocity of the initial electron. For the emitted vortex photon, we employ its angular frequency
\omega_{\gamma}
and opening angle
\theta_{\gamma}
to characterize its kinematical distributions by the relation,
dk_{+}d\kappa_{\gamma}=\omega_{\gamma}(1+\cos\theta_{\gamma})d\omega_{\gamma}d\theta_{\gamma}
. With all of the above manipulations, the two-dimensional differential decay rate in terms of the emitted photon’s variables becomes,

\displaystyle d\Gamma^{\textrm{P}}_{\textrm{BG}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{2}}{32\pi^{2}E(1+v\cos\theta_{p})}\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{|I^{\textrm{P}}_{\textrm{BG}}|^{2}}{\kappa^{\prime}}d\omega_{\gamma}d\theta_{\gamma}.

(78)

Given the variables
(E,\theta_{p},v)
of the initial electron and the laser’s angular frequency
\omega
and parameter
\xi
, the angular frequency of the emitted vortex photon is bound by
\omega_{-}\leq\omega_{\gamma}\leq\omega_{+}
for a given
\theta_{\gamma}
, where

\displaystyle\omega_{\pm}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n\omega E(1+v\cos\theta_{p})}{E[1-v\cos(\theta_{\gamma}\mp\theta_{p})]+\big\{n\omega+M^{2}\xi^{2}/[4E(1+v\cos\theta_{p})]\big\}(1+\cos\theta_{\gamma})}.

(79)

Note that
\omega_{\pm}
coincide at
\theta_{\gamma}=\pi
and are equal to
n\omega
.

III
Numerical analysis

In this section we evaluate numerically the rate for all-vortex nonlinear Compton scattering based on the analytical result in
Section

II
. We will focus mainly on the case of a circularly polarized laser, and discuss briefly the case of a linearly polarized laser. For the laser parameters, we assume its angular frequency
\omega=1~\rm eV
and dimensionless intensity
\xi=0.1,~1,~5
. This corresponds to a laser intensity in the range from
2\times 10^{16}~\textrm{W/cm}^{2}
to
5\times 10^{19}~\textrm{W/cm}^{2}
, which can be achieved in current experiments
[
100
]
. We assume that the initial electron has an energy
E=1~\rm GeV
which could be obtained by accelerating a low energy vortex electron to higher energy
[
86
]
. Unless otherwise stated, we assume its Bessel-Gaussian vortex has a deviation
\sigma_{\perp}
of one percent of its central value
\kappa_{0}
,
\sigma_{\perp}=0.01\kappa_{0}
.
We recall that angular momentum is conserved in the longitudinal direction for a circularly polarized laser (see Eq. (
51
)), which results in the constraint
\Delta\ell+n-\lambda=0
for the electron spin-preserved channel and
\Delta\ell+n-\lambda+\sigma=0
for the electron spin-flipped channel. There is no such a conservation law in the case of a linearly polarized laser, hence we have to sum up the contributions from all possible harmonic numbers.

III.3
Linearly polarized laser

Since the photons of a linearly polarized laser are not in a helicity eigenstate, we cannot formulate a constraint on the initial and final particles from angular momentum conservation. In this situation the OAM
\ell_{\gamma}
of the emitted photon does not have a fixed relation with the harmonic number
n
of the laser. This means that we have to sum over
n
for a given set of the OAMs of the initial and final particles
\ell,~\ell^{\prime},~\ell_{\gamma}
. In numerical calculations, we calculate up to a certain value of
n
in each case where the result has converged safely. In this circumstance we recall from Sec.
II.3.2
that

has the opposite parity to
\Delta\ell
for the electron spin-preserved case and the same parity as
\Delta\ell
for the spin-flipped case. Furthermore, as the Bessel function
J_{\nu}(\beta)
in Eq. (
56

\beta\ll\nu
, the summation over
\nu
for the calculation of the amplitude can be safely truncated at a finite
\nu
.

IV
Conclusion

We have studied the process of all-vortex nonlinear Compton scattering in an intense and polarized laser field, in which the initial and final electrons and the emitted
\gamma
photon are all in vortex states. We have developed a formalism for the process, which allows us to study the exchanges of the OAM and spin angular momentum among the electron,
\gamma
photon, and laser. Both circularly and linearly polarized lasers have been investigated.

Our numerical results have shown that, with a less intense laser intensity (
\xi=0.1
) and a relativistic initial electron, the spectrum
d\Gamma_{\ell\ell^{\prime}\ell_{\gamma}}/d\omega_{\gamma}
of the emitted vortex
\gamma
photon has a strong dependence on the OAM
\ell_{\gamma}
of the emitted photon and can change by orders of magnitude with different
\ell_{\gamma}
. Together with the strong
\ell_{\gamma}
dependence of the range of the energy of the emitted photon, this could offer a possible scenario to separate emitted vortex
\gamma
photons with different OAMs. A multi-peak structure in the spectrum was found, which depends strongly on the OAMs of the involved particles and the spins of the electrons, as well as the helicity of the
\gamma
photon. This could offer a possible way to identify the OAM and spin of the particle. The suppression on the rate and
d\Gamma_{\ell\ell^{\prime}\ell_{\gamma}}/d\omega_{\gamma}
from the difference between the OAMs of the initial and final vortex electrons has been revealed also. The strong impact of the opening angle of the initial vortex electron has been demonstrated, especially for the electron spin-flipped case. For a high laser intensity (
\xi=1
or
\xi=5
), our numerical calculations have shown that the nonlinear effect is strong. The rate generally increases with the laser intensity. Different from the low laser intensity case (
\xi=0.1
), the order of magnitude of the spectrum keeps similar (at
\xi=1
) or even increases (at
\xi=5
) when
\ell_{\gamma}
increases. This implies that a higher laser intensity does not help to isolate an emitted vortex photon with a given definite OAM although it induces a larger average OAM.

Integral over

We have transformed in section
II.4.2
the original integral over
\kappa
to the following form:

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}dx\frac{g(x)}{\sqrt{(x_{2}-x)(x-x_{1})}},

(104)

where
g(x)
is a smooth function. To isolate the apparent singularities at the endpoints, we make subtractions:

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}dx\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{x_{2}-x}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{x_{2}-x_{1}}}\right)\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{x-x_{1}}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{x_{2}-x_{1}}}\right)g(x)

(105)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\sqrt{x_{2}-x_{1}}}\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}dx\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{x-x_{1}}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{x_{2}-x}}\right)g(x)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{x_{2}-x_{1}}\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}dx~g(x).

The integrand in the first integral vanishes at the endpoints and is thus well behaved. Each integrand in the second integral is singular only at one endpoint and is easy to cope with. It can be further softened by using integration by parts:

\displaystyle\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}dx\frac{g(x)}{\sqrt{x-x_{1}}}=2g(x_{2})\sqrt{x_{2}-x_{1}}-2\int dx~g^{\prime}(x)\sqrt{x-x_{1}},

(106a)

\displaystyle\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}dx\frac{g(x)}{\sqrt{x_{2}-x}}=2g(x_{1})\sqrt{x_{2}-x_{1}}+2\int dx~g^{\prime}(x)\sqrt{x_{2}-x}.

(106b)

To make the first integral numerically amenable, we combine the square roots:

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{x_{2}-x}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{x_{2}-x_{1}}}\right)\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{x-x_{1}}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{x_{2}-x_{1}}}\right)

(107)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{x_{2}-x_{1}}\frac{\sqrt{(x_{2}-x)(x-x_{1})}}{\left(\sqrt{x_{2}-x_{1}}+\sqrt{x_{2}-x}\right)\left(\sqrt{x_{2}-x_{1}}+\sqrt{x-x_{1}}\right)}.

In summary, the final form is,

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{x_{2}-x_{1}}\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}dx\frac{(x_{2}-x_{1})+\sqrt{x_{2}-x_{1}}\big(\sqrt{x-x_{1}}+\sqrt{x_{2}-x}\big)}{\big(\sqrt{x_{2}-x_{1}}+\sqrt{x_{2}-x}\big)\big(\sqrt{x_{2}-x_{1}}+\sqrt{x-x_{1}}\big)}g(x)

(108)

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{\sqrt{x_{2}-x_{1}}}\int dx~g^{\prime}(x)(\sqrt{x_{2}-x}-\sqrt{x-x_{1}})+2\big[g(x_{1})+g(x_{2})\big].

Mural thickening of the coronary arteries in a COVID-19 patient
Authors: R A Groen, M A de Graaf, J L Stöger, D van der Woude, M L Antoni
Published: 2025

An 80-year-old man was admitted for a SARS-CoV-2 infection and treated with dexamethasone. He was fully vaccinated for SARS-CoV-2 before infection. He was referred to the cardiology outpatient clinic because of ECG abnormalities suggesting old myocardial infarction (Q-waves V1–V3). Echocardiography showed no wall-motion abnormalities but, a mass in the right atrium was suspected. Subsequently, coronary computed tomography angiography (CCTA) was performed that excluded a right atrial mass, but demonstrated evident mural thickening of all proximal coronary arteries and ascending aorta and arch. Furthermore, aneurysmal dilatation of the right coronary artery was observed. Additional analysis showed elevated Immunoglobulin G4 (IgG4) serum levels (7.86 g/L, ref. 0.03–1.30). IgG4-related disease (IgG4-RD) is an auto-immune disease associated with vasculitis and periarteritis. In addition, atrophy of the pancreas was observed, which makes IgG4-RD more plausible. Another possibility could be adult-onset Kawasaki or Kawasaki-like multisystem inflammatory syndrome (MIS) in combination with the SARS-CoV-2 infection. This disease can manifest as vasculitis and is a known cause of coronary artery disease. A subsequent positron emission tomography showed increased uptake in the proximal coronary arteries and slightly in the mediastinal lymph nodes. Based on these findings, treatment with prednisone was initiated and the wall-thickening around the coronary arteries receded on follow-up CCTA.

Mural thickening and aneurysms of the coronaries in combination with elevated IgG4 may correspond with vasculitis. This could be a result of his SARS-CoV-2 infection, could be related with MIS, or due to IgG4-related disease.

DB-SegNet: optimized framework for glaucoma detection and optic structure segmentation from retinal fundus images
Authors: J Prakash, B. Vinoth Kumar
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Glaucoma remains one of the primary causes of irreversible blindness, characterized by gradual damage to the optic nerve, which often goes undetected until advanced stages. Accurate and early diagnosis depends heavily on precise segmentation of the optic disc and optic cup in retinal fundus images, as this enables reliable calculation of the cup-to-disc ratio, a key biomarker for identifying and monitoring glaucoma. However, many current deep learning approaches struggle with generalization because of challenges such as variable image quality, blood vessel occlusion, and structural ambiguities, leading to reduced segmentation and classification accuracy. To address these issues, this study introduces DB-SegNet, an advanced diagnostic framework designed to enhance both segmentation accuracy and glaucoma detection. The proposed architecture builds upon SegNet by incorporating a Dilated Atrous Context Module (DACM) to capture multi-scale contextual features and a Bidirectional Feature Calibration Unit (BFCU) to refine boundary details. Feature space optimization is achieved through the Bitterling Fish Optimization (BFO) algorithm, while a Multi-Scale Attention Transformer (MSAT) is employed to model long-range spatial dependencies. In addition, Honey Badger Optimization (HBO) is applied for hyperparameter fine-tuning, ensuring stable and precise convergence. Evaluation on three widely used benchmark datasets such as Drishti-GS1, RIM-ONE, and ORIGA-Light demonstrates the effectiveness of the framework, yielding Dice coefficients of 99.2% for optic disc and 98.3% for optic cup segmentation, along with classification accuracies of 98.7% (RIM-ONE) and 99.1% (ORIGA-Light). These outcomes highlight the robustness of DB-SegNet in overcoming the limitations of existing techniques and underscore its potential as a clinically reliable tool for large-scale glaucoma screening and early intervention planning.

Introduction

Glaucoma is one of the leading global causes of permanent blindness, resulting primarily from progressive degeneration of the optic nerve head (ONH). Since the disease often advances without noticeable symptoms in its early stages, a large proportion of patients are only identified after considerable vision loss has already occurred1. Clinical evidence indicates that timely detection is essential, as early intervention through medical or surgical treatment can significantly slow progression and reduce the risk of blindness. Retinal fundus imaging serves as a cornerstone in glaucoma screening, where accurate delineation of the optic disc (OD) and optic cup (OC) is critical. From these structures, the cup-to-disc ratio (CDR) is computed, a well-established biomarker that allows clinicians to quantify glaucomatous changes. Nonetheless, automated OD/OC segmentation remains challenging due to anatomical variability, uneven illumination, vessel overlap, and poor image contrast, highlighting the urgent need for robust and interpretable computer-aided diagnostic tools2,3.

The emergence of deep learning (DL) has revolutionized medical image analysis, offering substantial gains in both segmentation and classification tasks. Early convolutional neural network (CNN) based frameworks, such as U-Net and SegNet, achieved encouraging outcomes for OD/OC segmentation but showed limited robustness under heterogeneous imaging conditions. More recently, transformer-driven architectures have been introduced, leveraging global attention mechanisms to capture broader contextual relationships between retinal structures4. For example, multi-task approaches have aimed to jointly segment OD/OC and identify the fovea by exploiting anatomical correlations, thereby improving diagnostic accuracy. Likewise, hybrid encoder–decoder models combining convolutional backbones with transformer modules have been developed to address complex scenarios, including high myopia and peripapillary atrophy. Yet, despite these improvements, current systems continue to face persistent clinical and technical barriers5.

A wide range of algorithms have been proposed for OD/OC segmentation and glaucoma detection in recent years, though each still presents certain drawbacks. Transformer-based networks such as PVTv2 with SBA6 and ODFormer7 enhance boundary delineation and generalization, but their performance is constrained by limited dataset size, absence of effective denoising strategies, and difficulties in modeling spatial feature dependencies. CNN-based methods, including the histogram-guided CNN8 and Multi-GlaucNet9, have reported competitive results in both segmentation and classification; however, they often lack comprehensive evaluation metrics and fail to incorporate complementary diagnostic tasks such as fovea localization or integration with OCT data. Similarly, models like MIU-Net10 and SimpleCNN-UNet11 employ multi-scale feature extraction and cross-attention to refine segmentation but rely heavily on data augmentation and extensive parameter adjustment to compensate for insufficient training data.

These challenges underscore the necessity of a unified end-to-end diagnostic framework that seamlessly combines segmentation, feature optimization, and classification within a single architecture. Such an approach should not only achieve high segmentation precision and robustness across diverse imaging conditions but also deliver clinically reliable classification outcomes while maintaining computational efficiency. To address these gaps, this work introduces DB-SegNet, an optimized framework designed for glaucoma detection and optic structure segmentation. Built on the SegNet backbone, the model is enhanced with a Dilated Atrous Context Module (DACM) for multi-scale context extraction and a Bidirectional Feature Calibration Unit (BFCU) to refine boundary accuracy. Beyond segmentation, the framework integrates Bitterling Fish Optimization (BFO) for effective dimensionality reduction, ensuring the preservation of the most discriminative features while minimizing redundancy. A Multi-Scale Attention Transformer (MSAT) further enriches the network by capturing long-range dependencies and hierarchical structural relationships, enabling accurate recognition of subtle optic variations. Additionally, Honey Badger Optimization (HBO) is applied for hyperparameter tuning, providing stable convergence and boosting diagnostic accuracy across different datasets.

The adoption of BFO and HBO is driven by their suitability for two distinct optimization stages within the proposed pipeline. For feature refinement, BFO employs a niche-based reproduction and spawning strategy that maintains diversity and minimizes premature convergence, making it particularly effective for selecting high-value features from the complex convolutional maps generated by DB-SegNet. This ensures the retention of features with strong discriminative power measured by Fisher scores, while suppressing noise introduced by vessel overlap or inconsistent illumination. In contrast, HBO is employed for hyperparameter optimization, leveraging a density-guided foraging strategy that adaptively adjusts search step sizes based on gradient information. This dynamic mechanism proves highly effective in fine-tuning learning rates, filter dimensions, and regularization parameters, where traditional static or grid-based searches fail to account for dataset-specific variations observed in Drishti-GS1, RIM-ONE, and ORIGA-Light. Together, BFO strengthens feature selection and discriminability, while HBO enhances convergence stability and generalization, resulting in a robust and clinically viable glaucoma diagnostic framework.

Related works

Early glaucoma detection efforts explored deep learning frameworks that integrate synthetic data generation and lightweight classification models. One such approach combines Generative Adversarial Networks for generating fundus images with Gaussian filtering for preprocessing, an enhanced level-set algorithm for optic cup segmentation, and MobileNetV2 for classification. This combination enhances vessel preservation and segmentation accuracy in clinical screening12. To improve noise reduction and contrast enhancement, another method adopted adaptive median filtering alongside the TOPHAT algorithm, followed by optic disc and cup segmentation using the multilevel statistical region-of-interest technique. Classification was performed using an Artefact Convolutional Neural Network (ACNN), demonstrating the effectiveness of layered convolutional and regularization strategies13.

A hybrid strategy introduced the Grey Wolf Optimization algorithm for optic disc segmentation, paired with classification schemes that leveraged both full and cropped fundus images. Classification was then performed using Swin Transformer and CNNs, including ResNet50 and InceptionV3, supporting the adaptability of metaheuristic methods and transformers in glaucoma diagnostics14. Another system employed Attention U-Net with a ResNet50 backbone for segmentation and a modified Inception V3 for classification, with model interpretability enhanced using Grad-CAM visual explanations. This system was deployed in a web application for real-time detection and interpretability, thereby supporting clinical deployment15.

For diabetic maculopathy, a mathematical morphology-based rule-based system to identify and classify hard exudates has been proposed. This technique removes noise elements such as the optic disc and nerve reflection with morphological filters and grades severity based on concentric zone analysis around the fovea16. For segmentation of the optic disc, a two-CNN model consisting of SSD-MobileNetV2 for localization and U-Net for detailed segmentation was proposed. The methodology highlighted the minimal computational time and anatomical consistency between the datasets17. To handle segmentation difficulties owing to blurred boundaries and vessel occlusions, MDDA-Net was framed with dense attention mechanisms and multiscale fusion blocks. Its U-Net architecture-based framework focuses on precise context extraction and scale-aware learning18.

OD-DeepNet employs a semantic classification model with dilated convolutions and Spatial Pyramid Pooling following the preprocessing of the optic disc area. It is a lightweight approach aimed at automating the localization of the optic disc to detect early-stage glaucoma19. The U-Net was improved by incorporating spatial and channel attention mechanisms and dense dilated convolutions in skip connections. This enhances the context retention and boundary accuracy during segmentation20. The classification approach integrates U-Net-based segmentation and handcrafted features from the segmented area. These were fed to a K-nearest neighbor classifier to classify glaucomatous and normal eyes using an efficient and simplified pipeline21. Within a detection-classification pipeline, YOLOv4 was employed to detect the optic disc, followed by ResNet-101 to classify it. A semi-automatic module produces ground-truth annotations to allow improved OD localization and efficient classification22.

An attention-based segmentation model introduced a self-supervised contrastive pretraining step along with anatomical constraints in the loss function, such that the alignment between the optic disc and optic cup was improved. The framework-maintained accuracy and computationally efficiency23. The R-DCNN model framed segmentation as an object detection task, incorporating disc and cup proposal networks and spatial attention. With ResNet34 and dense convolutions, it processed the input in an end-to-end manner for cup-to-disc ratio estimation24. An automated classification system for detecting optic disc edema adopted a multistage pipeline: localization via a Hybrid Localization Method, boundary segmentation using Gradient Vector Flow, and classification using an SVM trained on handcrafted features. This setup focused on distinguishing between swollen and healthy optic discs25.

To estimate the vertical cup-to-disc ratio, a system employing YOLOv7 for detection and post-detection calibration is proposed. This method addresses domain shifts using a non-retraining optimization strategy, ensuring reliable glaucoma detection across heterogeneous datasets26. A biomechanical simulation was developed using finite element modeling to understand stress distributions in neonatal retinal vessels during childbirth. This model illustrates the mechanical basis of hemorrhages via tensile strain and was the first to simulate such biomechanics using segmented vessel networks27. MCAU-Net was introduced with modules, such as Multiresolution Wavelet Pooling and Cascaded Attention, to overcome spatial loss and focus issues. Owing to its compact architecture and high efficiency, this model supports real-time segmentation and foveal localization28. To improve optic disc detection in noisy or irregular images, a Bitterling Fish Optimization algorithm was applied to mimic fish foraging strategies for region search. It outperformed the traditional methods in terms of speed and detection accuracy29.

HHO-UNet-IAA combined Inception-based UNet with Harris Hawks Optimization and attention mechanisms to boost segmentation performance. By using hybrid loss functions and multiscale learning, optic nerve structure identification is improved30. A two-stage segmentation framework based on U-Net and GANs was introduced in which the initial localization was refined by adversarial training. The use of Tukey’s biweight loss further improves the robustness of challenging fundus images31. A modified U-Net with separate pathways for disc and cup segmentation was proposed to improve cup-to-disc ratio estimation. This architecture employs Canny edge refinement and achieves high consistency across large datasets32.

SU-ASM, a structural uncertainty estimation framework, integrated CNNs with Active Shape Models, employing a multi-task learning module for shape initialization, Combined Boundary Probability with Key Landmark Template Matching for boundary reliability, and a shape fitting step to generate segmentations with uncertainty estimates. The method achieved superior performance on cardiac ultrasound, anterior eye ciliary muscle, and chest X-ray datasets, although reliance on predefined shape templates limits adaptability to irregular structures and increases computational complexity33. VSR-Net was proposed for rehabilitating vessel-like structures, using a Curvilinear Clustering Module to detect ruptures followed by a Curvilinear Merging Module for reconstruction. The framework reduced calibration errors across six 2D/3D medical datasets, but its specialization in vessels and dependence on accurate coarse segmentation restricts broader applicability34.

MM-UNet, a mixed MLP-based network for ophthalmic image segmentation, incorporated a Multi-Scale MLP module to extract global and local features simultaneously. Experiments on anterior segment OCT and fundus datasets demonstrated high segmentation accuracy; however, heavy reliance on MLPs increases computational cost, and global feature smoothing may reduce boundary precision35. ADSR introduced adaptive dual-axis style-based recalibration to emphasize salient and small lesion features, coupled with Class-wise Statistics Loss to handle class imbalance. Validated on five medical datasets, it achieved superior F1 and balanced accuracy, but dependence on style priors and increased computational demand may limit generalizability36.

RIR-Net employed the Region-based Integration-and-Recalibration mechanism to adaptively recalibrate multiple feature map regions for predicting NC severity from AS-OCT and OCT images. While it improved clinical interpretability and performance, the region-level focus may limit fine-grained lesion detection, and softmax weighting could bias outcomes on imbalanced datasets37. PPCA, a Pyramid Pixel Context Adaption module, dynamically recalibrated pixels using multiscale context with minimal overhead and incorporated supervised contrastive loss for better representation. PPCANet achieved strong performance across six medical datasets, although multiscale pooling can smooth fine boundaries and supervised contrastive learning requires high-quality labels, limiting scalability38. Comparative analysis of the existing techniques for glaucoma classification and segmentation.

Glaucoma remains one of the leading causes of permanent vision loss worldwide, with asymptomatic progression making early detection crucial. The accurate analysis of fundus images is a vital step in glaucoma screening; however, several challenges hinder this goal. Variations in image resolution, inconsistent lighting conditions, and anatomical differences across patients often degrade the performance of traditional and deep learning-based models. These models frequently exhibit instability in delineating the optic cup and disc, leading to miscalculations of clinical indicators, such as the cup-to-disc ratio. In addition, the lack of consolidated architectures that support segmentation, feature selection, and classification in an integrated fashion makes disconnected workflows unsuitable for mobile or real-time diagnostic use cases. Current alternatives are typically built upon high computational power, lack generalizability across datasets, or do not generate explainable results. There is an urgent need to develop a lightweight yet efficient framework to bridge these gaps through efficient deep-learning techniques, smart feature engineering, and task-conscious architectural improvements.

Collectively, recent advances demonstrate a clear trajectory toward integrating attention mechanisms, context recalibration, metaheuristic optimization, and hybrid learning strategies to address persistent challenges in medical image segmentation and classification. Nevertheless, these approaches are often limited by excessive computational overhead, restricted adaptability to diverse anatomical structures, and insufficient reliability in estimating glaucoma-specific biomarkers such as the Cup-to-Disc Ratio (CDR). To overcome these limitations, the proposed DB-SegNet introduces a boundary-preserving SegNet backbone enhanced with a Dilated Atrous Context Module (DACM) and a Bidirectional Feature Calibration Unit (BFCU) to strengthen multi-scale representation and feature consistency. In contrast to prior frameworks, DB-SegNet integrates Bitterling Fish Optimization (BFO) for discriminative feature selection and a Multi-Scale Attention Transformer (MSAT) for capturing long-range dependencies, while employing Honey Badger Optimization (HBO) for efficient hyperparameter tuning. This unified diagnostic pipeline delivers state-of-the-art segmentation accuracy and ensures robust, clinically reliable CDR estimation across multiple benchmark datasets, thereby advancing the scope of automated glaucoma detection beyond existing methodologies.

Methodology

The development of DB-SegNet was motivated by the need to address persistent shortcomings in existing glaucoma detection frameworks, including inaccurate boundary delineation, vulnerability to vessel occlusion, and weak generalization across datasets. SegNet was selected as the backbone due to its efficiency and reliability in medical image segmentation, and its capability was extended through the incorporation of a Dilated Atrous Context Module (DACM) for capturing multi-scale contextual cues and a Bidirectional Feature Calibration Unit (BFCU) for improved boundary refinement. To reduce feature redundancy and enhance discriminative learning, the framework integrates biologically inspired optimization strategies (BFO and HBO) in combination with a Multi-Scale Attention Transformer (MSAT), which effectively models hierarchical dependencies. Collectively, these architectural choices ensure a balance of accuracy, robustness, and computational efficiency, establishing DB-SegNet as a well-suited approach for automated glaucoma screening.

The suggested framework is constructed as a multi-stage pipeline intended to achieve accurate segmentation and diagnostic evaluation of the OD and OC structures within retinal fundus images. The system combines a series of specialized modules that interact uniformly to address shared challenges, including vessel occlusion, non uniform lighting, low contrast, and vague anatomical boundaries, as illustrated in Fig. 1. The process commences with an enhanced image refinement phase, whereby the input data are resized uniformly, normalized, and augmented to provide variability and uniformity in the training samples. Noise and ship interference were selectively removed by employing median filtering and partial differential equation-based inpainting methods to retain the retinal structure definition close to the OD/OC areas.

After pre-processing, the cleaned images were fed into a self-developed segmentation framework called DB-SegNet. The model, built upon an adapted SegNet base, involves two essential modules: Dilated Atrous Context Module (DACM) and Bidirectional Feature Calibration Unit (BFCU)to extract multi-scale contextual information while promoting decoder feature integrity. For further feature representation refinement, a nature-inspired optimization approach, the Bitterling Fish Optimization (BFO) algorithm, is used. This algorithm selects and preserves the most informative deep features by simulating the selective spawning behavior of bitterling fish; thus, only the most informative attributes are used for the final stages.

Subsequently, a Multi-Scale Attention Transformer (MSAT) was utilized to enhance long-range input relationships and preserve hierarchical representations essential for correct segmentation and classification. Then, the whole system is fine-tuned with the aid of the Honey Badger Optimization (HBO) algorithm, which optimally adjusts input parameters dynamically by mimicking the resilience and adaptability of honey badgers in a foraging environment. The synchronized operation of these elements leads to a high-performance, computationally effective paradigm with good generalization ability, which is especially appropriate for practical clinical ophthalmic tasks.

Preprocessing techniques

An efficient preprocessing method was used to provide stable segmentation of the OD and OC structures in retinal fundus images. The preprocessing pipeline standardizes the input data, suppresses noise, improves fine detail, and removes visual obstructions. Every step helps improve clarity and consistency prior to deep learning-based segmentation.

Data augmentation

To deal with the small size of annotated medical images and to enhance the generalization of the model, data augmentation strategies were utilized. These involve random flips along the horizontal and vertical axes, minor rotations (e.g., ± 15°), zoom scaling, brightness and contrast changes, and minor translations. These augmentations introduce spatial and photometric variability in training images, thus rendering the model invariant to unseen variations in real-world imaging conditions.

Proposed DB-SegNet architecture

The proposed DB-SegNet model is a deep convolutional neural network designed for accurate segmentation of the optic disc (OD) and optic cup (OC) from retinal fundus images. It extends the traditional SegNet backbone, which is known to perform pixel-wise classification tasks using an encoder–decoder architecture. However, to improve the model’s ability to learn intricate contextual features and recalibrate spatial semantics properly, two sophisticated modules are integrated: The Dilated Atrous Context Module (DACM) in the encoder and the Bidirectional Feature Calibration Unit (BFCU) in the decoder, as illustrated in Fig. 2. These enhancements are particularly tailored to address variations in anatomical structures, vessel interference, and low contrast between the OD and OC areas, which are issues commonly encountered in clinical retinal imaging.

SegNet backbone overview

The SegNet backbone comprises a sequence of encoder blocks and a symmetric set of decoder blocks. The encoder contains two consecutive convolutional layers with a kernel size of 3 × 3, followed by batch normalization and ReLU activation, which assist in feature distribution standardization and addition of nonlinearity. Every convolutional block is followed by a max-pooling layer with stride 2, which decreases the spatial resolution but increases the depth of feature representations. Importantly, the pooling indices were stored for use in the decoder. The decoder then reverses this process. It uses the saved pooling indices to perform nonlinear upsampling through max-unpooling, followed again by two convolutional layers with batch normalization and ReLU activation. This mirrored architecture is effective for reconstructing fine spatial details, which is essential for accurately locating OD and OC boundaries.

Feature selection using Bitterling fish optimization (BFO)

After the deep feature extraction process in DB-SegNet, the encoder provides a high-dimensional feature representation that captures intricate spatial and semantic patterns from the retinal fundus images. However, not all features are equally useful in downstream decision-making processes. Some may have redundant or irrelevant information, which can decrease model generalization or add computational complexity when directly fed into attention modules or classifiers. To mitigate this, a feature selection step is added, in which a biologically inspired metaheuristic called the Bitterling Fish Optimization (BFO) algorithm is used, as depicted in Fig. 5. The algorithm is applied to the final output of the encoder directly, allowing the network to retain only discriminative and significant deep features, thus enhancing interpretability and accuracy and reducing resource overhead.

The motivation behind BFO comes from the specialized breeding behavior of bitterling fish, which lay eggs within freshwater mussels. Female bitterlings pick mussels with perfect inshore conditions to develop eggs, and males carefully guard and manage the placement procedure. This biological process illustrates a biologically motivated balance between exploration (probing for appropriate mussels) and exploitation (optimizing within familiar secure areas), and is thus a natural metaphor for best feature subset discovery in high-dimensional spaces.

Multi-scale attention transformer (MSAT)

To enhance the classification reliability and capture intricate feature-level dependencies, a Multi-Scale Attention Transformer (MSAT) is deployed as a core component within the DB-SegNet architecture. Following the feature selection phase handled by Bitterling Fish Optimization (BFO), the retained deep features, although refined, still lack a mechanism for understanding how spatially distant regions relate to one another in a semantic sense. This is particularly critical in the domain of retinal fundus analysis, where the visual distinction between pathological and healthy regions, such as OD deformation or OC enlargement, may be contextually subtle but diagnostically significant. MSAT addresses this by modeling both global context and multi-scale attention, enabling accurate feature association across distant spatial positions as shown in Fig. 6.

Fig. 6Multi-scale attention transformer classification.

Multi-scale attention transformer classification.

At the heart of MSAT lies the self-attention mechanism, which allows each feature position to evaluate its relevance to every other position, creating a dense relational map. What makes MSAT uniquely effective is its multi-scale strategy. Instead of operating on a single-resolution input, the module feeds feature derived from different spatial hierarchies, such as early, mid-level, and deep encoder representations. This allows it to simultaneously capture fine-grained spatial details (microvascular changes) and high-level structural variations (OD-OC ratio distortions). Where traditional CNN classifiers might overlook such spatial-semantic interplay owing to fixed kernel sizes, MSAT dynamically learns how to prioritize and fuse multi-scale signals through attention weighting.

Once the multiscale attention outputs are consolidated, they are aggregated into a global representation vector via average pooling or token condensation, which is then passed to a fully connected classification head. This classification head predicted the disease category (normal or glaucoma) based on refined and context-aware feature embedding. Here, the MSAT module serves not only as a conduit for a deeper understanding of inter-regional dependencies but also as a transformer-driven feature calibrator that maximizes diagnostic confidence. Where shallow attention layers or simple MLP classifiers may lack generalization, MSAT enables the model to handle a wide range of real-world imaging conditions, including variable lighting, occlusions, and subtle deformities with robustness and interpretability.

Optimization using honey Badger optimization (HBO)

To further optimize the performance of the DB-SegNet framework and achieve effective convergence during training, the Honey Badger Optimization (HBO) algorithm was proposed as a smart metaheuristic-based optimization approach. This bio-inspired algorithm is inspired by the foraging and hunting nature of honey badgers in the wild, who are famous for their tenacity, agility, and adaptive tactics in finding prey buried underground. The survival strategies of honey badgers include two general stages: digging, which is an exploratory activity to scan a large region, and foraging, which is the exploitation of previous knowledge of prey locations. HBO mathematically simulates this two-behavioral activity to traverse high-dimensional solution spaces and to prevent premature convergence in the local minima, as illustrated in Fig. 7.

Fig. 7Honey Badger Optimization.

Honey Badger Optimization.

In the DB-SegNet pipeline, HBO is applied to optimize critical training hyperparameters, such as the learning rate, dropout ratios, and number of attention heads, as well as to fine-tune the loss function parameters that directly affect the convergence profile. The optimization goal is to minimize the overall cost function \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\mathcal{L}$$\end{document}, which may combine segmentation loss (Dice loss) and classification loss (cross-entropy) in a weighted form as in Eq. (22):22\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\mathcal{L}}_{total}={{\uplambda\:}}_{1}\bullet\:{\mathcal{L}}_{seg}+{{\uplambda\:}}_{2}\bullet\:{\mathcal{L}}_{cls}$$\end{document}

Here, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{{\uplambda\:}}_{1}$$\end{document}​ and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{{\uplambda\:}}_{2}$$\end{document}​ are scalar coefficients that balance the influence of segmentation and classification objectives. HBO operates over this composite loss to determine hyperparameter configurations that drive faster and more stable convergence across validation cycles.

The position of each candidate solution in the HBO algorithm represents a vector of hyperparameter values, as denoted by Eq. This wave-based oscillation ensures the exploration diversity across the parameter space. By contrast, the foraging phase directs the agent toward the best-known solution P∗, adjusting its path using adaptive attraction forces, as shown in Eq. (25):

This update rule promotes exploitation by reinforcing better-performing candidates, while maintaining a balance between exploration and convergence.

The adaptive switching behavior distinguishes the HBO—agents switch between foraging and digging autonomously under environmental response and convergence feedback, for example stagnation or sudden improvement in validation loss. This self-adaptive mechanism prevents premature convergence and enhances the escape from poor areas. By incorporating HBO into the optimization phase of DB-SegNet, the model exhibits improved robustness, particularly against noisy gradients or unbalanced data distributions. Its high convergence efficiency further accelerates training while keeping the learned parameters consistent over various datasets. When traditional grid search or random search faces challenges in terms of scalability and efficiency, HBO makes intelligent exploration of the hyperparameter space with lightweight computational overheads. Finally, HBO becomes a robust optimization backbone that fits perfectly with the adaptive and modular nature of DB-SegNet. In this case, its biologically inspired behaviors support model generalization and critically enhance classification reliability and segmentation fidelity in retinal image analysis.

Experimental results and discussion

The Experimental Results and Discussion section rigorously verify the suggested model through extensive evaluations, including segmentation, classification, and comparative performance on various public retinal image datasets. The section describes the datasets employed, demonstrates the training behaviors, and investigates the efficiency of the loss functions and optimization methods. The proposed framework was designed following standard econometric principles to ensure both stability and generalization. Data independence was preserved by carefully partitioning the datasets, preventing any overlap between training and testing samples. To maintain homoscedasticity and stable convergence, weight decay regularization was applied alongside cosine annealing for learning rate adjustment. Overfitting was mitigated through the use of dropout within the attention module, while overall model complexity was regulated using hyperparameters optimized via BFO and HBO. Diagnostic validation involved tracking training and validation loss curves, assessing segmentation and classification metrics across multiple datasets, and conducting cross-dataset evaluations to confirm robustness and adaptability to different domains.

Conclusion

Glaucoma remains one of the leading causes of irreversible blindness, and accurate, automated detection methods are vital for early diagnosis and timely intervention. Motivated by this clinical need, this research presents DB-SegNet, a unified and high-precision deep learning framework for glaucoma detection and optic disc/cup segmentation from retinal fundus images. The framework combines robust preprocessing (vessel inpainting, normalization, and contrast enhancement) with a modified SegNet backbone enhanced through a Dilated Atrous Context Module (DACM) and Bidirectional Feature Calibration Unit (BFCU). To further refine discriminative feature learning, Bitterling Fish Optimization (BFO) was employed for salient feature selection, and a Multi-Scale Attention Transformer (MSAT) captured hierarchical dependencies. Honey Badger Optimization (HBO) ensured stable convergence and optimal performance. Evaluations on three benchmark datasets—Drishti-GS1, RIM-ONE, and ORIGA-Light—show that DB-SegNet consistently outperforms current methods such as AGCN and EE-UNet. On Drishti-GS1, optic cup segmentation achieved a Dice score of 98.3% and IoU of 96.8%, while optic disc segmentation reached a Dice score of 99.2% and IoU of 97.3%. On RIM-ONE, glaucoma classification achieved 98.7% accuracy with an AUC of 98.32%. On ORIGA-Light, accuracy reached 99.1% with AUC values above 98.7%. Theoretically, this work illustrates the effectiveness of combining deep architectures with biologically inspired optimization and attention mechanisms, offering a scalable model for complex medical imaging tasks. Practically, the high robustness and accuracy position DB-SegNet as a promising tool for clinical decision support, early glaucoma screening, and integration into teleophthalmology systems. This convergence of algorithmic innovation and clinical applicability provides a pathway toward AI-driven population-level eye care.

Future work

Although DB-SegNet achieves state-of-the-art results across several benchmark datasets, certain limitations persist. First, the evaluations were limited to publicly available datasets, which may not fully reflect real-world variations in image quality, acquisition settings, or patient diversity. Second, the model’s relatively high computational demand may hinder deployment on devices with limited resources. Future research will aim to validate the framework on larger, multi-center clinical datasets to improve generalizability, streamline the architecture for lightweight and real-time applications, and extend its applicability to other ophthalmic disorders. Furthermore, integrating explainable AI methods will be a key direction to enhance interpretability and build greater clinical confidence in the system.

Wavelet Latent Position Exponential Random Graphs
Marios Papamichalis, Regina Ruane

Abstract
Many network datasets exhibit connectivity with variance by resolution and large-scale organization that coexists with localized departures. When vertices have observed ordering or embedding, such as genome position in Hi–C contact maps, geography in spatial and village networks, or anatomical coordinates in connectomes, learning where and at what resolution connectivity departs from a baseline is crucial. Standard models typically emphasize a single representation, i.e. stochastic block models prioritize coarse partitions, latent space models prioritize global geometry, small-world generators capture local clustering with random shortcuts, and graphon formulations are fully general and do not solely supply a canonical multiresolution parameterization for interpretation and regularization. We introduce wavelet latent position exponential random graphs (WL–ERGs), an exchangeable logistic-graphon framework in which the log-odds connectivity kernel is represented in compactly supported orthonormal wavelet coordinates and mapped to edge probabilities through a logistic link. Wavelet coefficients are indexed by resolution and location, which allows multiscale structure to become sparse and directly interpretable. Although edges remain independent given latent coordinates, any finite truncation yields a conditional exponential family whose sufficient statistics are multiscale wavelet interaction counts and conditional laws admit a maximum-entropy characterization. These characteristics enable likelihood-based regularization and testing directly in coefficient space. The theory is naturally scale-resolved and includes universality for broad classes of logistic graphons, near-minimax estimation under multiscale sparsity, scale-indexed recovery and detection thresholds, and a band-limited regime in which canonical coefficient-space tilts are non-degenerate and satisfy a finite-dimensional large deviation principle. Empirical studies demonstrate improved predictive performance and clearer localization of multiscale effects relative to block and low-rank baselines.

Introduction

Empirical networks in the social sciences, biology, neuroscience, and information systems often exhibit structures that are not well described at a single resolution. This becomes especially pronounced when vertices are coupled with a meaningful coordinate or ordering pattern, for example, a genomic position in Hi–C contact maps, spatial location in village networks, or anatomical location in connectomes. In such settings, connectivity can exhibit smoothness at coarse scales, while demonstrating localized departures at finer scales. A central inferential goal is to estimate the underlying edge-probability surface and to localize

where
(along the vertex domain) and
at what scale
connectivity departs from a baseline. In this paper, we emphasize that this “vertex coordinate” notion is distinct from degree-heterogeneity phenomena, e.g. heavy-tailed degree
distributions, which concern marginal propensities, rather than spatially organized structure.

A recurring objective of the field is to develop generative models that represent multiscale structure, while also supporting likelihood-based inference, i.e. estimation of a connectivity kernel and hypothesis tests that localizes signal in both resolution and position. Existing model classes capture portions of this objective, but tend to encapsule a single representation.
For example, s tochastic block models summarize coarse partitions; latent space and random dot-product models encode global low-dimensional geometry; and small-world generators emphasize local clustering with occasional long-range shortcuts.
Graphon models provide a general exchangeable kernel representation
[
7
]
. Without an additional coordinate system, graphon models do not directly separate coarse and fine effects in ways that support targeted regularization or multiscale testing. When a vertex coordinate is available, the emergence of a likelihood-based parameterization whose parameters are explicitly indexed by
scale
and
location
along that coordinate are missing.

In this paper, we construct
wavelet latent position exponential random graphs
(WL–ERGs), which use a compactly supported
orthonormal wavelet basis on vertex coordinates to parameterize connectivity across scales.
This use of wavelets is complementary to “graph wavelets” built from a Laplacian on an observed network,
where the wavelet basis lives on an exogenous vertex domain and yields coefficients that are directly interpretable as

scale–location
effects in the log-odds of connection. Our construction sits at the intersection of two statistical schools of thought.
First, graphon and latent-coordinate models provide exchangeable kernel representations for networks
[
7
]
and
[
4
]
.
Second, wavelet expansions develop a standard, multiresolution language to represent functions comprised of spatially localized structure and support sparsity, regularization, and scale-adaptive inference. Wavelet constructions on graphs supply a principled multiscale language for network-structured data by replacing Euclidean translations and dilations with operators derived from the graph Laplacian or diffusion semigroups. Spectral graph wavelets construct scale-dependent, spatially localized atoms by filtering Laplacian eigenmodes, which bring forth an intrinsic analogue of classical wavelet localization on irregular domains
[
3
]
. This approach is central to graph signal processing, which systematizes harmonic analysis, filtering, sampling, and inference for signals supported on graphs
[
8
]
. Diffusion wavelets construct multiresolution bases from the powers of a diffusion operator, producing hierarchies of coarse-to-fine representations that adapt to graph geometry and encode locality and scale separation
[
1
]
. In parallel, statistical network modeling has developed kernel-based and latent-coordinate representations for exchangeable graphs. Graphon theory formalizes dense-graph limits and kernel representations
[
7
]
, while latent space models parameterize edge probabilities through unobserved coordinates
[
4
]
. Exponential-family random graph models (ERGMs) provide a maximum-entropy and exponential-tilting viewpoint in which specified network features act as sufficient statistics
[
2
]
; multiscale wavelet interactions naturally fit this paradigm by offering structured, localized constraints indexed by scale and location. Methodologically, graph wavelets have been used as multiscale probes of network organization, especially for detecting mesoscopic structure that varies with resolution. To achieve community detection, wavelet-based procedures leverage band-pass filtering and localization to uncover communities across scales and quantify how partitions persist or fragment as resolution changes
[
9
]
. Extensions to temporal and multilayer settings track community structure over time using multiscale ideas
[
6

. Beyond communities, diffusion- and spectral-wavelet representations have been used to localize heterogeneous or anomalous behavior by isolating fine-scale components against smoother baselines
[
1
,
3
]
. In neuroscience, diffusion wavelets have been used to interrogate scale-dependent structure–function relationships in connectomes
[
5
]
.

WL–ERGs are a coordinate-driven framework for modeling and inference in networks with an available vertex ordering or coordinate. WL–ERGs expand the logit kernel in wavelet coordinates, so that multiscale structure becomes an explicit and statistically tractable parameter object. Following are our main contributions: a) We parameterize the log-odds connectivity kernel in compactly supported orthonormal wavelet coordinates, so model parameters are indexed by
resolution
and
location
and multiscale structure can be expressed sparsely and interpreted directly. b) We prove a universality result, which shows the wavelet–logit parameterization is a change of coordinates for a broad class of logistic graphons (rather than a restrictive modeling assumption). Additionally, we establish parameter-efficiency separations for hierarchical kernels with localized departures that are relative to blockwise-constant and globally low-rank representations. c) Although edges are conditionally independent given coordinates, finite truncations induce conditional exponential families whose sufficient statistics are multiscale, wavelet interaction counts and admit a maximum-entropy characterization, enabling regularization and hypothesis testing directly in the coefficient space. d) We develop near-minimax estimation under multiscale sparsity, scale-dependent recovery/detection thresholds, and a band-limited regime in which canonical coefficient-space tilts are non-degenerate and satisfy a finite-dimensional, large deviation principle. Case studies on structural brain connectomes and Karnataka village social networks illustrate improved, predictive performance and clearer localization of multiscale effects relative to block and low-rank baselines.

The remaining sections of the paper are organized in the following manner. Section 2 introduces the wavelet basis, the logistic wavelet graphon, and the WL–ERG model, and records basic properties including exchangeability and the conditional exponential-family (maximum-entropy) representation.
Section 3 develops estimation in wavelet space in the observed-design setting under multiscale sparsity. Section 4 compares expressive power via parameter–complexity separations relative to block and low-rank parameterizations. Section 5 describes multiscale inference, including phase transitions for hierarchical recovery and detection boundaries for localized anomalies. Section 6 provides analysis of stability in a band-limited regime, including non-degeneracy and large deviations for finite-dimensional wavelet exponential families. Section 7 presents modelling operations and illustrative examples in coefficient space. Section 8 reports empirical studies on synthetic benchmarks and real networks. Section 9 concludes with discussion and extensions.

2
Model and interpretation

Throughout,
(0,1)
is equipped with Lebesgue measure. We build the model in three steps:
(i) fix a multiscale wavelet feature map on
(0,1)
; (ii) parameterise the
logit
of a graphon
as a bilinear form in those features; and (iii) generate a random graph by sampling latent positions
and then sampling edges conditionally independently.
Once the construction is in place, we record (a) a conditional ERGM/exponential-family representation,
and (b) a maximum-entropy characterisation. Finally, we connect the model to the exchangeable graphon
framework (formal statements are collected in Appendix
A
).

Given
c\in\mathbb{R}
and a symmetric Hilbert–Schmidt coefficient matrix
S
, define

\eta_{c,S}(x,y)=c+\phi(x)^{\top}S\phi(y),\qquad W_{c,S}(x,y)=\sigma\!\bigl(\eta_{c,S}(x,y)\bigr),

and sample
U_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{i.i.d.}}}{{\sim}}\mathrm{Unif}(0,1)
, then

A_{ij}\mid U\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}\!\bigl(W_{c,S}(U_{i},U_{j})\bigr)
independently for
i<j
.

2.1
Wavelet feature map and logistic wavelet graphon

Definition 2.1

(Wavelet basis)
.

Let
\{\psi_{r}:r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
be an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(0,1)
such that

1.

\psi_{0}(x)\equiv 1
(constant scaling function),

2.

\int_{0}^{1}\psi_{r}(x)\,dx=0
for all
r\geq 1
,

3.

each
\psi_{r}
is compactly supported and piecewise smooth.

For concreteness one may take a compactly supported orthonormal wavelet basis (Haar, Daubechies, etc.).

Assumptions (i)–(iii) separate a global (DC) component from multiscale, zero-mean detail components,
while retaining spatial localisation in the latent coordinate. Orthogonality provides an exact
decomposition across scales, and compact support aligns sparsity in coefficient space with locality
in latent space—a link exploited repeatedly in modelling and inference.

Define the
formal
feature vector

\varphi(x):=(\psi_{0}(x),\psi_{1}(x),\psi_{2}(x),\ldots)^{\top}.

In general
\varphi(x)\notin\ell^{2}
pointwise. Accordingly, for a symmetric
Hilbert–Schmidt matrix
S=(s_{rs})_{r,s\geq 0}
we define

f_{S}:=\sum_{r,s\geq 0}s_{rs}\,(\psi_{r}\otimes\psi_{s})\quad\text{in }L^{2}((0,1)^{2}),

i.e.
f_{S}(x,y)=\sum_{r,s\geq 0}s_{rs}\psi_{r}(x)\psi_{s}(y)
for a.e.
(x,y)
, and we
use the suggestive notation
f_{S}(x,y)=\varphi(x)^{\top}S\,\varphi(y)
.

Interpreting
x
as a latent coordinate,
\phi(x)
collects multiscale features of
x
in a single object;
restricting to a finite index set corresponds to a band-limited (truncated) representation.

Definition 2.2

(Wavelet latent kernel and logistic graphon)
.

Let
S=(s_{rs})_{r,s\geq 0}
be a real, symmetric, Hilbert–Schmidt matrix:

\sum_{r,s\geq 0}s_{rs}^{2}<\infty
. Define

f_{S}(x,y)=\sum_{r,s\geq 0}s_{rs}\,\psi_{r}(x)\psi_{s}(y)=\phi(x)^{\top}S\phi(y),\qquad x,y\in(0,1),

and, for
c\in\mathbb{R}
,

\eta_{c,S}(x,y):=c+f_{S}(x,y),\qquad W_{c,S}(x,y):=\sigma\!\bigl(\eta_{c,S}(x,y)\bigr)=\frac{1}{1+\exp\{-\eta_{c,S}(x,y)\}}.

Then
W_{c,S}
is symmetric, measurable, and takes values in
(0,1)
.

The Hilbert–Schmidt condition places
S
in a natural Euclidean parameter space and ensures convergence
of the expansion in
L^{2}((0,1)^{2})
. The logistic link eliminates the need for additional constraints to keep
edge probabilities in
(0,1)
, so modelling choices can be stated directly in terms of multiscale coefficients
of the
logit
.

2.2
WL–ERG generative model

Definition 2.3

(Wavelet latent position exponential random graph (WL–ERG))
.

Fix
(c,S)
as in Definition
2.2
. For each
n\geq 1
:

1.

Sample latent positions
U_{1},\dots,U_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{i.i.d.}}}{{\sim}}\mathrm{Unif}(0,1)
.

2.

Given
U=(U_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
, sample edges independently for
1\leq i<j\leq n
as

A_{ij}\mid U\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}\!\bigl(W_{c,S}(U_{i},U_{j})\bigr),\qquad A_{ji}=A_{ij},\ \ A_{ii}=0.

We write
G_{n}\sim\mathrm{WL\mbox{-}ERG}(c,S)
.

Conditional on
U
, edges are independent but not identically distributed; heterogeneity is driven by the
wavelet features evaluated at
(U_{i},U_{j})
. This clean separation between
independent sampling
and

structured heterogeneity
is what enables both sharp probability bounds and interpretable multiscale
parameters.

2.3
Turning on coefficients produces recognizable structure

The entries of
S
control
which multiscale interactions
in the latent coordinate can tilt edge
probabilities away from the baseline
c
. In particular, coarse-scale structure arises from coefficients
aligned with scaling components, while fine-scale, localised deviations arise from coefficients aligned with
high-resolution wavelets. The following theorem records several classical benchmarks as explicit special
cases obtained by simple structural restrictions on
S
.

Theorem 2.4

(Erdős–Rényi, SBMs and logistic RDPGs)
.

Fix
c\in\mathbb{R}
and a real symmetric Hilbert–Schmidt matrix

S=(s_{rs})_{r,s\geq 0}
, and let
G_{n}\sim\mathrm{WL\mbox{-}ERG}(c,S)
be generated as in
Definitions
2.1
–
2.3
.

1.

(Erdős–Rényi.)

If
s_{rs}=0
for all
(r,s)\neq(0,0)
, then
G_{n}
is Erdős–Rényi
G(n,p)
with

p=\sigma(c+s_{00})
.

2.

(Haar/SBM special case.)

Assume the underlying wavelet system is the Haar system and fix an integer
J\geq 0
.
Let
\{I_{\ell,J}\}_{\ell=0}^{2^{J}-1}
be the dyadic partition of
(0,1)
, define

\phi_{\ell,J}(x):=2^{J/2}\mathbf{1}_{I_{\ell,J}}(x)
, and let

V_{J}:=\mathrm{span}\{\phi_{\ell,J}:0\leq\ell\leq 2^{J}-1\}\subset L^{2}(0,1)
.
If the logit satisfies
f_{S}\in V_{J}\otimes V_{J}
, equivalently if there exist real coefficients

\alpha_{\ell k}
such that

f_{S}(x,y)=\sum_{\ell,k=0}^{2^{J}-1}\alpha_{\ell k}\,\phi_{\ell,J}(x)\phi_{k,J}(y)\quad\text{in }L^{2}\big((0,1)^{2}\big),

then
G_{n}
is a stochastic block model with
2^{J}
blocks. In particular, there are constants

\beta_{\ell k}:=2^{J}\alpha_{\ell k}
such that
f_{S}(x,y)=\beta_{\ell k}
for a.e.
(x,y)\in I_{\ell,J}\times I_{k,J}
,
and hence the block connectivity probabilities are

p_{\ell k}=\sigma(c+\beta_{\ell k})=\sigma(c+2^{J}\alpha_{\ell k}).

Under
U_{i}\sim\mathrm{Unif}(0,1)
, the block proportions are
\pi_{\ell}=2^{-J}
.

3.

(Finite-rank PSD
\Rightarrow
logistic RDPG.)

Assume
S
is positive semidefinite as an operator on
\ell^{2}
and has finite rank
d
.
Then there exist functions
g_{1},\dots,g_{d}\in L^{2}(0,1)
such that

f_{S}(x,y)=\sum_{k=1}^{d}g_{k}(x)g_{k}(y)\quad\text{in }L^{2}\big((0,1)^{2}\big).

Consequently, for a measurable representative
\Phi(x):=(g_{1}(x),\dots,g_{d}(x))\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
(defined a.e.),

W_{c,S}(x,y)=\sigma\!\bigl(c+\Phi(x)^{\top}\Phi(y)\bigr)\quad\text{for a.e. }(x,y).

Therefore
G_{n}
is a
logistic random dot product graph
: with latent positions
X_{i}=\Phi(U_{i})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
the edges are independent conditional on
X
and satisfy

\mathbb{P}(A_{ij}=1\mid X)=\sigma(c+X_{i}^{\top}X_{j})
for all
i<j
.

This theorem gives an immediate “dictionary” between coefficients and familiar network structure.
At one extreme, turning off all non-constant coefficients recovers Erdős–Rényi; activating only
coarse Haar scaling structure yields SBMs; and imposing global low-rank PSD structure yields logistic
RDPGs. The point of WL–ERGs is that they also allow
intermediate
regimes—e.g. sparse, localised
fine-scale coefficients that are neither purely blockwise-constant nor globally low-rank.

2.4
Conditional ERGM viewpoint

Fix
n
and latent positions
U=(U_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
. Define the wavelet interaction statistics

\displaystyle S_{00}(A)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}A_{ij},

(1)

\displaystyle S_{rs}(A,U)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}A_{ij}\,\psi_{r}(U_{i})\psi_{s}(U_{j}),\qquad r,s\geq 0.

(2)

These separate the global edge count from multiscale interaction counts that couple edges to products of
wavelet features. Truncating
S
to finitely many indices yields a finite-dimensional exponential family
with these statistics as sufficient statistics.

Theorem 2.5

(Conditional exponential family (finite symmetric truncation))
.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and latent positions
U=(U_{1},\dots,U_{n})\in(0,1)^{n}
.
Let
\{\psi_{r}:r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
be the wavelet basis from Definition
2.1
, and let

I\subset\mathbb{N}_{0}\times\mathbb{N}_{0}
be a finite index set.

Let
\theta=\bigl(c,(s_{rs})_{(r,s)\in I}\bigr)
and define the truncated coefficient matrix

S_{\theta}=(\tilde{s}_{rs})_{r,s\geq 0}
by

\tilde{s}_{rs}:=\begin{cases}s_{rs},&(r,s)\in I,\\
0,&(r,s)\notin I.\end{cases}

Assume
S_{\theta}
is
symmetric
, i.e.
\tilde{s}_{rs}=\tilde{s}_{sr}
for all
r,s\geq 0

(equivalently: whenever both
(r,s)
and
(s,r)
lie in
I
we impose
s_{rs}=s_{sr}
, and any unpaired
index in
I
must have coefficient
0
).

Define

\eta_{\theta}(x,y):=c+\sum_{(r,s)\in I}s_{rs}\,\psi_{r}(x)\psi_{s}(y),\qquad W_{\theta}(x,y):=\sigma\!\bigl(\eta_{\theta}(x,y)\bigr).

Under
\mathrm{WL\mbox{-}ERG}(c,S_{\theta})
, the conditional distribution of
A
given
U
has pmf

\mathbb{P}_{\theta}(A\mid U)=\exp\Bigl\{c\,S_{00}(A)+\sum_{(r,s)\in I}s_{rs}\,S_{rs}(A,U)-\Psi_{n}(\theta;U)\Bigr\},

with log–partition function

\Psi_{n}(\theta;U):=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\log\!\Bigl(1+\exp(\eta_{\theta}(U_{i},U_{j}))\Bigr).

Because
\psi_{0}\equiv 1
, if
(0,0)\in I
then
s_{00}
is confounded with
c
(the family is not minimal);
one may remove
(0,0)
from
I
or absorb
s_{00}
into
c
.

Intuitively, once the latent positions are fixed, WL–ERGs are honest-to-goodness ERGMs whose sufficient
statistics are
multiscale and localised
rather than subgraph counts. This makes ERGM-style tools
(model selection, regularisation, likelihood-based fitting under truncation) directly compatible with a
wavelet notion of “which structures are allowed”.

2.5
Maximum entropy

Theorem 2.6

(Maximum–entropy characterisation)
.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
, latent positions
U=(U_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\in(0,1)^{n}
, and a finite index set

\mathcal{I}\subset\mathbb{N}_{0}\times\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
Let
\Omega:=\{0,1\}^{\binom{n}{2}}
and identify
A\in\Omega
with an undirected adjacency matrix by setting

A_{ji}:=A_{ij}
and
A_{ii}:=0
. Using the statistics
S_{00}(A)
and
S_{rs}(A,U)
defined in
Section
2.4
, fix target moments
m_{00}\in\mathbb{R}
and
(m_{rs})_{(r,s)\in\mathcal{I}}
and define

\mathcal{Q}(m):=\Bigl\{Q\text{ pmf on }\Omega:\;\mathbb{E}_{Q}[S_{00}(A)]=m_{00},\;\mathbb{E}_{Q}[S_{rs}(A,U)]=m_{rs}\ \ \forall(r,s)\in\mathcal{I}\Bigr\}.

Assume
\mathcal{Q}(m)\neq\varnothing
.

(i) Existence and uniqueness.

There exists a unique maximiser
Q^{\star}\in\mathcal{Q}(m)
of Shannon entropy

H(Q):=-\sum_{A\in\Omega}Q(A)\log Q(A),

where
\log
is the natural logarithm and
0\log 0:=0
.

(ii) Exponential-family characterisation (attainable moments).

For
\theta=(c,(s_{rs})_{(r,s)\in\mathcal{I}})
define

P_{\theta}(A\mid U):=\exp\Bigl\{c\,S_{00}(A)+\sum_{(r,s)\in\mathcal{I}}s_{rs}S_{rs}(A,U)-\Psi_{n}(\theta;U)\Bigr\},

with

\Psi_{n}(\theta;U):=\log\sum_{A\in\Omega}\exp\Bigl\{c\,S_{00}(A)+\sum_{(r,s)\in\mathcal{I}}s_{rs}S_{rs}(A,U)\Bigr\}.

If there exists
\theta^{\star}
such that
P_{\theta^{\star}}(\cdot\mid U)\in\mathcal{Q}(m)
(equivalently,

\mathbb{E}_{\theta^{\star}}[S_{00}(A)]=m_{00}
and
\mathbb{E}_{\theta^{\star}}[S_{rs}(A,U)]=m_{rs}
for all

(r,s)\in\mathcal{I}
), then

Q^{\star}=P_{\theta^{\star}}(\cdot\mid U).

In particular, if one restricts
\theta
to the WL–ERG subfamily (e.g. by imposing symmetry constraints
inherited from a symmetric coefficient matrix), then any moment-matching WL–ERG member must coincide with

Q^{\star}
and hence is the unique maximum–entropy distribution under the constraints.
The maximising
distribution
is always unique; the canonical parameter
\theta^{\star}
is unique whenever
the exponential family is minimal.

This characterisation says WL–ERGs are the “least committed” conditional models that match finitely many
multiscale constraints: the index set
\mathcal{I}
chooses
which
wavelet interactions you insist on,
and the parameters are exactly the corresponding Lagrange multipliers. This gives a principled interpretation
of truncation and regularisation in terms of concrete multiscale summaries, rather than generic subgraph counts.

Integrating out latent positions places WL–ERGs in the standard exchangeable random graph framework.
Formally, WL–ERGs are generated by the graphon
W_{c,S}
and are jointly exchangeable (Theorem
A.1
),
the ordered empirical graphon converges in cut norm to
W_{c,S}
under the standard coupling
(Theorem
A.2
), and any logistic graphon with square-integrable logit admits a wavelet
bilinear representation of the form
W_{c,S}
(Theorem
A.3
). For readability, we collect
full statements in Appendix
A
.

3
Estimation in wavelet space (observed design)

The multiscale parameterisation is most transparent in an
observed-design
regime in which both the
adjacency matrix
A
and the latent positions
U_{1},\dots,U_{n}
are observed. This isolates the statistical
effect of multiscale sparsity (separating it from the additional difficulty of unknown vertex ordering) and
provides a clean benchmark for unlabeled graphon inference.

3.1
Multiscale sparse logistic graphon class

Let
W:[0,1]^{2}\to[0,1]
be symmetric and define its logit

f(x,y):=\log\!\bigl(W(x,y)/(1-W(x,y))\bigr)
.
We work on the logit scale because sparsity and scale separation are naturally expressed in linear wavelet
coordinates. Let
\{\Psi_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
be an orthonormal tensor-product wavelet basis of

L^{2}([0,1]^{2})
, and write
j(\lambda)
for the resolution level of
\Psi_{\lambda}
.

Definition 3.1

(Wavelet–sparse logistic graphon class)
.

Fix
s>0
,
1\leq p\leq 2
,
0<q\leq\infty
and
M>0
.
Let
\beta_{\lambda}=\langle f,\Psi_{\lambda}\rangle
be the 2D wavelet coefficients of
f
.
We say that
W
(equivalently
f
) belongs to
\mathcal{F}^{s}_{p,q}(M)
if

\Biggl(\sum_{j\geq 0}\Bigl(2^{j(s+1-2/p)}\,\|\beta_{j}\|_{\ell^{p}}\Bigr)^{q}\Biggr)^{1/q}\leq M,\qquad\beta_{j}:=(\beta_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{j}},

with the usual modification when
q=\infty
, and
|f(x,y)|\leq B
a.e. for some
B<\infty
.

This is the standard isotropic Besov constraint in dimension
2
, written in wavelet coordinates; it
formalises multiscale sparsity through scale-dependent
\ell^{p}
control. The boundedness of
f
keeps
W

uniformly away from
0
and
1
, so the logistic link is Lipschitz on the relevant range and estimation on
the graphon and logit scales are equivalent up to constants.

3.2
Estimator

Assume the edges are conditionally independent given observed latent positions:

A_{ij}\mid(U_{k})_{k=1}^{n}\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}\bigl(W(U_{i},U_{j})\bigr),\qquad 1\leq i<j\leq n.

Write
N_{n}:=\binom{n}{2}
. For each
\lambda\in\Lambda
, define the empirical wavelet interaction coefficient

\widehat{\theta}_{\lambda,n}:=\frac{2}{n(n-1)}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}A_{ij}\,\Psi_{\lambda}(U_{i},U_{j}).

Choose a maximal resolution level and threshold as

J_{n}:=\max\Bigl\{j\in\mathbb{N}:\ 2^{2j}\leq\frac{N_{n}}{\log N_{n}}\Bigr\},\qquad\tau_{n}:=\kappa\sqrt{\frac{\log N_{n}}{N_{n}}},

and set

\widetilde{\theta}_{\lambda,n}:=\widehat{\theta}_{\lambda,n}\,\mathbf{1}\Bigl\{|\widehat{\theta}_{\lambda,n}|\geq\tau_{n}\,2^{j(\lambda)}\Bigr\}\quad\text{for }j(\lambda)\leq J_{n},\qquad\widetilde{\theta}_{\lambda,n}:=0\quad\text{for }j(\lambda)>J_{n}.

Definition 3.2

(Wavelet graphon estimator)
.

Define

\widehat{W}_{n}(x,y):=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\widetilde{\theta}_{\lambda,n}\,\Psi_{\lambda}(x,y),

optionally clipped pointwise to
(\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon)
for fixed
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
.

3.3
Risk bound and coefficient recovery

Theorem 3.3

(Adaptive wavelet estimation)
.

Assume that conditional on observed latent positions
U_{1},\dots,U_{n}\in[0,1]
, the
edges
\{A_{ij}:1\leq i<j\leq n\}
are independent with

A_{ij}\mid(U_{k})_{k=1}^{n}\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}\bigl(W(U_{i},U_{j})\bigr),

for a symmetric graphon
W:[0,1]^{2}\to[0,1]
. Let
N_{n}:=\binom{n}{2}
and assume

W\in\mathcal{F}^{s}_{p,q}(M)\cap[0,1],\qquad s>0,\quad 1\leq p\leq 2,\quad 0<q\leq\infty,

where
\mathcal{F}^{s}_{p,q}(M)
is the Besov ball from Definition
3.1
.

Let
\{\Psi_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda}
be an orthonormal tensor-product wavelet basis of

L^{2}([0,1]^{2})
of regularity
r>s
, and assume there exists
C_{\Psi}<\infty
such that
\|\Psi_{\lambda}\|_{\infty}\leq C_{\Psi}\,2^{j(\lambda)}
for all indices
\lambda
.

Let
J_{n}
and
\tau_{n}
be chosen as

J_{n}:=\max\Bigl\{j\in\mathbb{N}:\ 2^{2j}\leq\frac{N_{n}}{\log N_{n}}\Bigr\},\qquad\tau_{n}:=\kappa\sqrt{\frac{\log N_{n}}{N_{n}}},

and let
\widehat{W}_{n}
be the hard-thresholded wavelet estimator from
Definition
3.2
. Fix a clipping level
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
and define the clipped estimator by

\widehat{W}_{n}^{\mathrm{clip}}(x,y):=\bigl(\widehat{W}_{n}(x,y)\vee\varepsilon\bigr)\wedge(1-\varepsilon).

Then there exists
\kappa_{0}>0
such that for all
\kappa\geq\kappa_{0}
there exist
constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
(depending only on
s,p,q,M,B
) with

\sup_{W\in\mathcal{F}^{s}_{p,q}(M)\cap[0,1]}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bigl\|\widehat{W}_{n}^{\mathrm{clip}}-W\bigr\|_{2}^{2}\,\bigm|\,U_{1},\dots,U_{n}\right]\;\leq\;C_{1}\,N_{n}^{-\frac{2s}{2s+2}}(\log N_{n})^{C_{2}}.

Taking expectations over
(U_{i})
yields the same bound for the unconditional risk. Moreover, since

(J_{n},\tau_{n})
do not depend on
s
, the estimator is adaptive in
s
over any compact interval

[s_{-},s_{+}]\subset(0,r)
(with constants
C_{1},C_{2}
chosen uniformly over
s\in[s_{-},s_{+}]
).

Intuitively, this is the standard “wavelet thresholding” phenomenon: the empirical wavelet interactions
behave like noisy measurements of the true coefficients, and a universal, scale-corrected threshold keeps
only signal-dominated coefficients. The result is (near) minimax-optimal recovery over multiscale Besov
classes, and it aligns the estimator with the same multiscale statistics that define WL–ERGs.

Corollary 3.4

(Estimation of wavelet coefficients /
S
)
.

Assume the conditions of Theorem
3.3
. Let

f(x,y):=\log\!\Big(\frac{W(x,y)}{1-W(x,y)}\Big)

denote the logit of
W
. Under Definition
3.1
we have
\|f\|_{\infty}\leq B
for a
fixed constant
B<\infty
, hence
W
is uniformly bounded away from
0
and
1
.

Fix a clipping level
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
and let
\widehat{W}_{n}^{\mathrm{clip}}
be the clipped estimator
from Theorem
3.3
. Define

\widehat{f}_{n}(x,y):=\log\!\Big(\frac{\widehat{W}_{n}^{\mathrm{clip}}(x,y)}{1-\widehat{W}_{n}^{\mathrm{clip}}(x,y)}\Big).

Let
\{\Psi_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
be the tensor-product orthonormal wavelet basis used above and define
the coefficient arrays

\beta:=\big(\langle f,\Psi_{\lambda}\rangle\big)_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\qquad\widehat{\beta}_{n}:=\big(\langle\widehat{f}_{n},\Psi_{\lambda}\rangle\big)_{\lambda\in\Lambda}.

Equip coefficient arrays with the
\ell^{2}(\Lambda)
(Hilbert–Schmidt) norm

\|T\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^{2}:=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}T_{\lambda}^{2}
.
Then there exists a constant
C_{1}^{\prime}>0
(depending only on
s,p,q,M,B
and
\varepsilon
) such that, with
the same exponent
C_{2}
as in Theorem
3.3
,

\sup_{W\in\mathcal{F}^{s}_{p,q}(M)}\mathbb{E}\,\|\widehat{\beta}_{n}-\beta\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^{2}\leq C_{1}^{\prime}\,N_{n}^{-\frac{2s}{2s+2}}(\log N_{n})^{C_{2}}.

This corollary says that, in observed design, multiscale structure can be recovered directly in coefficient
space at the same rate as graphon estimation. Practically, this is what makes the parameters interpretable:
downstream tasks can compare fitted wavelet spectra, test targeted coefficient patterns, or regularise by
scale in a statistically controlled way.

4
Expressive power and parameter complexity

The preceding sections define WL–ERGs and show how to estimate multiscale structure in wavelet space under
observed design. We now quantify when wavelet coordinates are genuinely
parameter–efficient
for
multiscale structure by comparing, at fixed approximation accuracy, the number of degrees of freedom required
by wavelet-sparse, blockwise-constant (SBM-like), and low-rank (RDPG-like) logit representations.

We work at the level of the logit
f(x,y)=\log(W(x,y)/(1-W(x,y)))
and measure approximation error in squared

L^{2}
norm,

\|f-g\|_{2}^{2}=\int_{(0,1)^{2}}(f(x,y)-g(x,y))^{2}\,dx\,dy.

4.1
Model classes and complexity measures

We compare three model families.

Wavelet–sparse logits.

Writing
f_{S}(x,y)=\sum_{r,s\geq 0}s_{rs}\,\psi_{r}(x)\psi_{s}(y)
, define the wavelet complexity

\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{wav}}(f_{S}):=\#\{(r,s):s_{rs}\neq 0\}.

This counts the active multiscale interactions in the bilinear feature space, i.e. the effective support of
the coefficient matrix in the wavelet basis.

Block logits.

A
K
–block SBM logit has the form

f^{\mathrm{SBM}}(x,y)=\sum_{a,b=1}^{K}B_{ab}\,\mathbf{1}_{I_{a}}(x)\,\mathbf{1}_{I_{b}}(y)

for a partition
\{I_{a}\}
of
(0,1)
and symmetric
K\times K
matrix
B
.
The SBM complexity at accuracy
\varepsilon>0
is

\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{SBM}}(f,\varepsilon):=\inf\Bigl\{K:\exists f^{\mathrm{SBM}}\text{ with at most $K$ blocks and }\|f-f^{\mathrm{SBM}}\|_{2}\leq\varepsilon\Bigr\}.

Low–rank logits.

A rank–
d
latent kernel has the form

f^{\mathrm{rank}}(x,y)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\varphi_{\ell}(x)\varphi_{\ell}(y),\qquad\varphi_{\ell}\in L^{2}(0,1),

so the associated integral operator has rank at most
d
.
The rank complexity at accuracy
\varepsilon>0
is

\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{rank}}(f,\varepsilon):=\inf\Bigl\{d:\exists f^{\mathrm{rank}}\text{ of rank}\leq d\text{ with }\|f-f^{\mathrm{rank}}\|_{2}\leq\varepsilon\Bigr\}.

Together these measures enable an apples-to-apples comparison: wavelet sparsity counts active multiscale
coefficients directly, while block and rank complexities quantify the least model size needed to reach a
target
L^{2}
accuracy.

4.2
Hierarchical plus anomaly family

We specialise to the Haar basis and construct logits
f_{J}
that encode a fixed coarse hierarchy plus many
small “hotspots” at scale
J
. Write

f_{J}=f^{(0)}+\tau g_{J},\qquad g_{J}(x,y):=\sum_{r\in R_{J}}\psi_{r}(x)\psi_{r}(y),

where
R_{J}
is a set of fine-scale Haar indices at resolution
J
with cardinality

M_{J}:=|R_{J}|\asymp 2^{J}
.

Lemma 4.1

(Best rank-
d
approximation to
f_{J}
)
.

Assume
f^{(0)}
is symmetric and the associated integral operator
F:=T_{f^{(0)}}
has finite rank

r_{0}:=\mathrm{rank}(F)<\infty
, independent of
J
. Let
T_{f_{J}}
be the integral operator with kernel
f_{J}
,
and let
s_{1}(T_{f_{J}})\geq s_{2}(T_{f_{J}})\geq\cdots\geq 0
denote its singular values. Then for every
d\in\mathbb{N}
,

\inf_{\mathrm{rank}(g)\leq d}\,\|f_{J}-g\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{k>d}s_{k}(T_{f_{J}})^{2},

(3)

and moreover

\inf_{\mathrm{rank}(g)\leq d}\,\|f_{J}-g\|_{2}^{2}\;\geq\;\tau^{2}\,(M_{J}-r_{0}-d)_{+},\qquad(x)_{+}:=\max\{x,0\}.

(4)

This lemma connects approximation in
L^{2}
to operator singular values: the best rank-
d
approximation error
is exactly the tail energy of singular values, and the second bound shows that adding
M_{J}
orthogonal fine-scale
components forces rank complexity to scale with
M_{J}
. It is the formal mechanism behind the “many local hotspots
are hard to compress by low rank” message.

Theorem 4.2

(Wavelet-sparse family hard for SBMs/RDPGs)
.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
and choose
\tau>0
such that
\tau>2\varepsilon
.
Let
f^{(0)}
be any fixed symmetric kernel with finite wavelet support (independent of
J
), and define

f_{J}(x,y):=f^{(0)}(x,y)+\tau\sum_{r\in R_{J}}\psi_{r}(x)\psi_{r}(y).

Then there exist constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
and an integer
J_{0}
such that for all
J\geq J_{0}
:

1.

\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{wav}}(f_{J})\leq c_{1}\,2^{J}
.

2.

If
f_{\mathrm{SBM}}
is any
K
-block SBM logit with
\|f_{J}-f_{\mathrm{SBM}}\|_{2}\leq\varepsilon
, then

K\geq c_{2}\,2^{J}
. In particular,
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{SBM}}(f_{J},\varepsilon)\geq c_{2}\,2^{J}
.

3.

If
f_{\mathrm{rank}}
is any rank-
d
latent kernel logit with
\|f_{J}-f_{\mathrm{rank}}\|_{2}\leq\varepsilon
, then

d\geq c_{2}\,2^{J}
. In particular,
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{rank}}(f_{J},\varepsilon)\geq c_{2}\,2^{J}
.

This theorem formalises a key modelling distinction: a logit consisting of a fixed coarse hierarchy plus many
localised fine-scale deviations is sparse in wavelet coordinates (linear in
2^{J}
), but it cannot be compressed
much further by either a purely blockwise-constant representation or a purely low-rank representation at fixed

L^{2}
error. In other words, WL–ERG wavelet coordinates can be
essentially minimal
for multiscale-local
structure.

A corresponding statement holds for the logistic graphons
W_{J}=\sigma(c+f_{J})
.

5
Multiscale detection and phase transitions

Beyond estimation, many network tasks ask
where
structure lives and

at what scale
it becomes statistically visible.
Wavelet coordinates make this explicit: each resolution level corresponds to an
effective sample size, and each activated coefficient controls a localized
interaction pattern.
We summarize the resulting detection phenomena below.

We begin with a single–coefficient two–block warm–up to make the “wavelet
SNR” idea concrete, then extend it to hierarchical SBMs where detectability can
hold at some scales and fail at others, and finally consider localized anomaly
detection via multiscale wavelet scans.

5.1
Two–block warm–up: one activated Haar coefficient

Let
\psi_{1}=\mathbf{1}_{(0,1/2)}-\mathbf{1}_{(1/2,1)}
be the first Haar wavelet
on
(0,1)
and consider the WL–ERG graphon with a single active coefficient in
the
logit
:

W(x,y)=\sigma\!\bigl(c+s_{11}\psi_{1}(x)\psi_{1}(y)\bigr),\qquad\sigma(t)=(1+e^{-t})^{-1}.

(5)

Since
\psi_{1}(x)\psi_{1}(y)=+1
when
x,y
lie in the same half of
(0,1)
and
-1

otherwise, this is exactly a two–block SBM in graphon form:

p_{\mathrm{in}}=\sigma(c+s_{11}),\qquad p_{\mathrm{out}}=\sigma(c-s_{11}).

Equivalently, given target probabilities
p_{\mathrm{in}},p_{\mathrm{out}}\in(0,1)
,

c=\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(logit(p_{\mathrm{in}})+logit(p_{\mathrm{out}})\bigr),\qquad s_{11}=\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(logit(p_{\mathrm{in}})-logit(p_{\mathrm{out}})\bigr),

and vertices with
U_{i}\in(0,1/2)
form one community while those with

U_{i}\in(1/2,1)
form the other.

Proposition 5.1

(Wavelet SNR for two blocks)
.

Let
\psi_{1}=\mathbf{1}_{(0,1/2)}-\mathbf{1}_{(1/2,1)}
and let

U_{1},\dots,U_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{i.i.d.}}}{{\sim}}\mathrm{Unif}(0,1)
.
Define the two-block label
b_{i}:=\psi_{1}(U_{i})\in\{\pm 1\}
.
Assume that, conditional on
U=(U_{1},\dots,U_{n})
, the edges are independent and satisfy

\mathbb{P}(A_{ij}=1\mid U)=\begin{cases}p_{\mathrm{in}},&b_{i}=b_{j},\\
p_{\mathrm{out}},&b_{i}\neq b_{j},\end{cases}\qquad 1\leq i<j\leq n,

with
A_{ji}=A_{ij}
and
A_{ii}=0
, where
p_{\mathrm{in}}=p_{\mathrm{in}}(n)
and

p_{\mathrm{out}}=p_{\mathrm{out}}(n)
may depend on
n
.
Let
\Delta:=p_{\mathrm{in}}-p_{\mathrm{out}}
and
\bar{p}:=(p_{\mathrm{in}}+p_{\mathrm{out}})/2
.
Assume
\Delta>0
and that there exists
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
such that for all large
n
,

\varepsilon\leq p_{\mathrm{out}}\leq p_{\mathrm{in}}\leq 1-\varepsilon.

For a fixed vertex
i
, consider the coarse wavelet score

T_{i}:=\sum_{j\neq i}A_{ij}\,\psi_{1}(U_{j})=\sum_{j\neq i}A_{ij}b_{j},

and the classifier
\hat{b}_{i}:=\mathrm{sgn}(T_{i})
with the convention
\mathrm{sgn}(0)=0
.
If

\mathrm{SNR}_{0}(n):=\frac{n\Delta^{2}}{2\bar{p}(1-\bar{p})}\longrightarrow\infty\qquad\text{as }n\to\infty,

then
\mathbb{P}(\hat{b}_{i}\neq b_{i})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.

Here the wavelet score
T_{i}
is a scale–matched “signed degree” that contrasts
edges from vertex
i
to the left vs. right half of the latent interval.
The condition
\mathrm{SNR}_{0}(n)\to\infty
is exactly a signal–over–noise
requirement with effective sample size
\asymp n
at this coarse scale; it
serves as a one–coefficient template for the multiscale phase transitions
below.

5.2
Hierarchical SBMs and multiscale SNR

We generalise to a depth–
J
hierarchical wavelet SBM in which each vertex
carries a multiscale sign vector
b(i)\in\{\pm 1\}^{J}
describing its path in the
dyadic tree, and the logit is diagonal in the Haar basis with scale–wise
coefficients
\{\beta_{j}\}_{j=0}^{J-1}
.

At scale
j
, each parent dyadic block splits into two children; within each
parent, edges behave like a two–block SBM with probabilities

p_{j,\mathrm{in}}
(same child) and
p_{j,\mathrm{out}}
(opposite child).
The corresponding scale–
j
wavelet SNR is

\mathrm{SNR}_{j}(n)=\frac{n}{2^{j+1}}\,\frac{(p_{j,\mathrm{in}}-p_{j,\mathrm{out}})^{2}}{\bar{p}_{j}(1-\bar{p}_{j})},\qquad\bar{p}_{j}=\tfrac{1}{2}(p_{j,\mathrm{in}}+p_{j,\mathrm{out}}),

where the factor
n/2^{j+1}
is the effective number of neighbors inside a
scale–
j
parent block.

Theorem 5.2

(Multiscale SNR phase diagram)
.

Fix a depth
J\geq 1
and let
\{I_{j,\ell}:0\leq j\leq J,\ 0\leq\ell<2^{j}\}
be the dyadic intervals of
[0,1)
with children

I^{L}_{j,\ell}:=I_{j+1,2\ell}
and
I^{R}_{j,\ell}:=I_{j+1,2\ell+1}
.
Let
U_{1},\dots,U_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle iid}}{{\sim}}\mathrm{Unif}(0,1)
and define the (random) vertex sets

V_{j,\ell}:=\{i:U_{i}\in I_{j,\ell}\},\qquad m_{j,\ell}:=|V_{j,\ell}|.

Assume the balanced-block condition that for all
0\leq j\leq J
and all
0\leq\ell<2^{j}
,

m_{j,\ell}=\frac{n}{2^{j}}+o(n),\qquad\text{and hence}\qquad|V_{j+1,2\ell}|=\frac{n}{2^{j+1}}+o(n),\quad|V_{j+1,2\ell+1}|=\frac{n}{2^{j+1}}+o(n),

uniformly in
\ell
(either as an explicit assumption, or on an event of probability
1-o(1)
).

Conditional on
U
, assume edges
\{A_{ik}\}_{1\leq i<k\leq n}
are independent Bernoulli, and that for each scale

j\in\{0,\dots,J-1\}
and each parent block
V_{j,\ell}
the two child blocks

V^{L}_{j,\ell}:=V_{j+1,2\ell},\qquad V^{R}_{j,\ell}:=V_{j+1,2\ell+1}

form a two-block SBM at that scale in the sense that for any
i\in V_{j,\ell}
and any
k\in V_{j,\ell}\setminus\{i\}
,

\mathbb{P}(A_{ik}=1\mid U)=\begin{cases}p_{j,\mathrm{in}},&\text{if $i,k$ are in the same child of $V_{j,\ell}$},\\
p_{j,\mathrm{out}},&\text{if $i,k$ are in opposite children of $V_{j,\ell}$},\end{cases}

with parameters
p_{j,\mathrm{in}},p_{j,\mathrm{out}}\in(0,1)
.

For each
j
define

\Delta_{j}:=p_{j,\mathrm{in}}-p_{j,\mathrm{out}},\qquad\bar{p}_{j}:=\frac{p_{j,\mathrm{in}}+p_{j,\mathrm{out}}}{2},\qquad\mathrm{SNR}_{j}(n):=\frac{n}{2^{j+1}}\cdot\frac{\Delta_{j}^{2}}{\bar{p}_{j}(1-\bar{p}_{j})}.

For each
i
and
j\in\{0,\dots,J-1\}
let
\ell(i,j)
satisfy
i\in V_{j,\ell(i,j)}
and define the scale-
j
label

b_{j}(i)=+1\ \text{if }i\in V^{L}_{j,\ell(i,j)},\qquad b_{j}(i)=-1\ \text{if }i\in V^{R}_{j,\ell(i,j)}.

Let
\psi_{j,\ell}
be the Haar wavelets on
[0,1)
and define the wavelet score and classifier

T_{j}(i):=\sum_{k\neq i}A_{ik}\,\psi_{j,\ell(i,j)}(U_{k}),\qquad\hat{b}_{j}(i):=\mathrm{sgn}(\Delta_{j})\,\mathrm{sgn}(T_{j}(i)).

1.

If
\mathrm{SNR}_{j}(n)\geq C\log n
for all
j
and
C
is sufficiently large (depending only on fixed
J
),
then the wavelet-score classifier achieves vanishing average Hamming error:

\frac{1}{nJ}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\mathbb{P}\bigl(\hat{b}_{j}(i)\neq b_{j}(i)\bigr)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0.

2.

If for some
j_{0}
we have
\sup_{n}\mathrm{SNR}_{j_{0}}(n)\leq c
for
c>0
small enough, then at scale
j_{0}

no estimator can achieve vanishing misclassification error: for any estimator
\tilde{b}_{j_{0}}
of
\{b_{j_{0}}(i)\}_{i=1}^{n}

based on the observed graph, there exists
\delta=\delta(c)\in(0,1/2)
such that for all sufficiently large
n
,

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{P}\bigl(\tilde{b}_{j_{0}}(i)\neq b_{j_{0}}(i)\bigr)\geq\frac{1}{2}-\delta.

The key point is that different scales have different effective sample sizes:
at scale
j
, each vertex only “sees” about
n/2^{j+1}
neighbors inside its
scale–
j
parent block.
The theorem therefore yields a genuinely multiresolution phase diagram:
coarse community structure can be detectable while fine subcommunities remain
statistically invisible (or vice versa), which directly motivates
scale–selective recovery procedures.

5.3
Localised anomalies and wavelet scans

We next consider localized departures from a smooth baseline graphon
W_{0}
:
a single fine–scale Haar coefficient on the diagonal is perturbed by

\delta_{n}
on a set of
m_{n}
vertices, producing a dense (or sparse) bump
in a small latent region.
We define local wavelet statistics
T_{j,\ell}
that average

A_{ik}-W_{0}(U_{i},U_{k})
over dyadic blocks and form the scan statistic

Z_{\max}=\max_{j,\ell}|Z_{j,\ell}|
.

Theorem 5.3

(Detection boundary for local anomalies via a standardized wavelet scan)
.

We work in the observed–design regime: the latent positions
U=(U_{1},\dots,U_{n})
are observed and,
conditional on
U
, the edges are independent. Under the null
H_{0}
,

A_{ik}\mid U\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}\!\bigl(W_{0}(U_{i},U_{k})\bigr),\qquad 1\leq i<k\leq n.

Assume
W_{0}
is uniformly bounded away from
0
and
1
: there exists
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
such that

\varepsilon\leq W_{0}(x,y)\leq 1-\varepsilon
for all
(x,y)\in(0,1)^{2}
.

Let
\mathcal{I}_{n}
denote the scanned collection of dyadic intervals
I_{j,\ell}
and assume

|\mathcal{I}_{n}|\leq Cn
for some constant
C<\infty
(e.g. dyadic scales
J_{0}<j\leq J_{n}
with
J_{n}\asymp\log_{2}n
).
For each
(j,\ell)\in\mathcal{I}_{n}
define

V_{j,\ell}:=\{i:U_{i}\in I_{j,\ell}\},\qquad m_{j,\ell}:=|V_{j,\ell}|,\qquad N_{j,\ell}:=\binom{m_{j,\ell}}{2}.

Define
T_{j,\ell}:=0
if
m_{j,\ell}<2
, and for
m_{j,\ell}\geq 2
set

T_{j,\ell}:=\frac{1}{m_{j,\ell}(m_{j,\ell}-1)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\neq k\\
U_{i},U_{k}\in I_{j,\ell}\end{subarray}}\bigl(A_{ik}-W_{0}(U_{i},U_{k})\bigr)=\frac{1}{N_{j,\ell}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i<k\\
i,k\in V_{j,\ell}\end{subarray}}\bigl(A_{ik}-W_{0}(U_{i},U_{k})\bigr).

Consider the
standardized
scan

Z_{j,\ell}:=\sqrt{N_{j,\ell}}\,T_{j,\ell},\qquad Z_{\max}:=\max_{(j,\ell)\in\mathcal{I}_{n}}|Z_{j,\ell}|.

Under the alternative
H_{1}
, there exists an (unknown) anomalous block
(j^{\star},\ell^{\star})\in\mathcal{I}_{n}

and constants
0<c_{-}\leq c_{+}<\infty
such that, conditional on
U
,

\mathbb{P}_{1}(A_{ik}=1\mid U)=W_{0}(U_{i},U_{k})+\Delta_{ik},\qquad c_{-}\,\delta_{n}\leq\Delta_{ik}\leq c_{+}\,\delta_{n}\quad\text{for all }i<k\text{ with }U_{i},U_{k}\in I_{j^{\star},\ell^{\star}},

and
\Delta_{ik}=0
otherwise, with
\delta_{n}
small enough that probabilities remain in
(0,1)
.
Write
m_{n}:=m_{j^{\star},\ell^{\star}}
and
N_{n}:=\binom{m_{n}}{2}
.

Then:

1.

Null fluctuations.
Under
H_{0}
,
Z_{\max}=O_{\mathbb{P}}(\sqrt{\log n})
. In particular, for any

C_{1}>\sqrt{2}
,

\mathbb{P}_{0}\!\left(Z_{\max}\geq C_{1}\sqrt{\log n}\right)\to 0.

2.

Achievability above the boundary.
If
\delta_{n}^{2}N_{n}\geq C_{2}\log n
for
C_{2}
large enough,
then the test
\phi_{n}:=\mathbf{1}\{Z_{\max}\geq C_{1}\sqrt{\log n}\}
has asymptotic level
0
and power tending to
1
:

\mathbb{P}_{0}(\phi_{n}=1)\to 0,\qquad\mathbb{P}_{1}(\phi_{n}=1)\to 1.

Equivalently (since
N_{n}\asymp m_{n}^{2}
), this holds when
\delta_{n}^{2}m_{n}^{2}\gtrsim\log n
.

3.

Impossibility below the boundary (random location).

Let
\{I^{(1)},\dots,I^{(M)}\}\subset\mathcal{I}_{n}
be
M\to\infty
disjoint dyadic intervals, and let
\bar{P}

denote the uniform mixture over alternatives that plant the bump on one of these intervals.
Assume each planted interval has vertex count in
[m_{n}/2,2m_{n}]
(hence
N=\binom{m}{2}\asymp N_{n}
).
If
\delta_{n}^{2}N_{n}\leq c_{2}\log M
for
c_{2}>0
small enough, then
\mathrm{TV}(P_{0},\bar{P})\to 0
, and no test can
reliably distinguish
H_{0}
from
H_{1}
when the location is uniformly random.

The scan adapts automatically to unknown
location
and
scale
by
standardizing each block average at its natural variance scale and then taking
a maximum.
The boundary
\delta_{n}^{2}N_{n}\asymp\log n
is the multiscale analogue of a
signal–to–noise threshold: if the anomalous block contains fewer vertices
(smaller
N_{n}
), the bump must be larger to be detectable.

The detection results above are most informative in regimes where the models do
not collapse to nearly empty or nearly complete graphs.
We therefore turn next to stability (non–degeneracy) and large–deviation
behavior of the wavelet interaction vector.

6
Stability: non–degeneracy and large deviations

Classical ERGMs may exhibit degeneracy: moderate parameter choices can lead to
near–deterministic graphs (almost empty or almost complete), obscuring both
interpretation and inference.
In WL–ERGs, the wavelet parametrisation provides a natural way to restrict the
logit to controlled multiscale fluctuations.
We formalise a band–limited region in which edge probabilities remain uniformly
interior and show that the corresponding wavelet interaction summaries satisfy a
finite–dimensional large deviation principle (LDP), yielding a stable
asymptotic theory for canonical exponential tilts.

This section is technically heavier than the detection results above. The main
takeaway is that restricting energy to a finite wavelet band yields a provably
stable (non-degenerate) regime and a well-behaved LDP for the resulting
multiscale interaction vector.

6.1
Band–limited parameter region

Fix integers
J_{\min}\leq J_{\max}
and let
I_{[J_{\min},J_{\max}]}
be the
indices whose associated wavelets lie in the band
[J_{\min},J_{\max}]
.
For
B>0
define the band–limited parameter set

\Theta_{B}:=\Bigl\{(c,(s_{rs})_{(r,s)\in I_{[J_{\min},J_{\max}]}}):|c|\leq B,\ \sum_{(r,s)\in I_{[J_{\min},J_{\max}]}}s_{rs}^{2}\leq B^{2}\Bigr\}.

This region constrains the energy of the coefficient matrix on a finite scale
band, preventing arbitrarily sharp logits while preserving rich mesoscopic
structure within the chosen resolutions.

Theorem 6.1

(Non–degeneracy in a band–limited region)
.

Fix integers
J_{\min}\leq J_{\max}
and let
I:=I[J_{\min},J_{\max}]
be the corresponding

finite
set of active wavelet index pairs in the band
[J_{\min},J_{\max}]
.
Fix
B>0
and consider the band–limited parameter region

\Theta_{B}:=\Bigl\{(c,S):|c|\leq B,\ S=(s_{rs})_{r,s\geq 0}\ \text{is symmetric},\ s_{rs}=0\ \text{for }(r,s)\notin I,\ \sum_{(r,s)\in I}s_{rs}^{2}\leq B^{2}\Bigr\}.

For
\theta=(c,S)\in\Theta_{B}
, let
G_{n}\sim\mathrm{WL\mbox{-}ERG}(c,S)
in the sense of
Definition 2.3, and for
n\geq 2
define the edge density

L_{n}:=\frac{2}{n(n-1)}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}A_{ij}.

Then there exist constants
0<\varepsilon_{1}<\varepsilon_{2}<1
and
C,\kappa>0
(depending only
on
B
and the band) such that,
uniformly over
\theta\in\Theta_{B}
,

\mathbb{P}_{\theta}\bigl(L_{n}\notin[\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}]\bigr)\leq Ce^{-\kappa n^{2}},\qquad\forall\,n\geq 2.

Moreover, for every fixed finite simple graph
H
with
k:=|V(H)|
and
m:=|E(H)|
, the
homomorphism density

t(H,G_{n}):=\frac{1}{n^{k}}\sum_{v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\in[n]}\prod_{(a,b)\in E(H)}A_{v_{a}v_{b}}

converges almost surely to

t(H,W_{c,S}):=\int_{(0,1)^{k}}\prod_{(a,b)\in E(H)}W_{c,S}(x_{a},x_{b})\,dx_{1}\cdots dx_{k}.

If
m\geq 1
, then
t(H,W_{c,S})\in(0,1)
; if
m=0
, then
t(H,G_{n})\equiv t(H,W_{c,S})\equiv 1
.
Finally, for each fixed
H
, the map
\theta\mapsto t(H,W_{c,S})
is continuous on
\Theta_{B}
.

Band–limiting keeps logits from developing arbitrarily sharp spikes, so edge
probabilities stay uniformly away from
0
and
1
and the model does not
collapse to nearly deterministic graphs.
The exponential tail bound for
L_{n}
and the almost sure convergence of
subgraph densities together give a stable macroscopic regime where likelihood
comparisons, simulation, and asymptotic approximations remain meaningful.

6.2
LDP for wavelet interaction vectors

For a fixed finite index set
I
, write

T_{n}(A,U)=\bigl(S_{00}(A),(S_{rs}(A,U))_{(r,s)\in I}\bigr)\in\mathbb{R}^{1+|I|},\qquad\overline{T}_{n}=\frac{1}{\binom{n}{2}}T_{n}.

These normalized interaction vectors are the finite–dimensional summaries that
drive exponential tilts and serve as natural coordinates for asymptotic
fluctuation and rare–event analysis.

Theorem 6.2

(Large deviations for wavelet statistics)
.

Fix integers
J_{\min}\leq J_{\max}
,
B>0
, and
\theta=(c,S)\in\Theta_{B}
.
Let
I\subset I[J_{\min},J_{\max}]
be a finite index set and assume
(0,0)\notin I
.
Let
(U_{i})_{i\geq 1}
be i.i.d.
\mathrm{Unif}(0,1)
, and conditional on
U
let

\{A_{ij}\}_{1\leq i<j<\infty}
be independent with

A_{ij}\mid U\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(W_{c,S}(U_{i},U_{j}))
.
For
n\geq 2
set
N_{n}=\binom{n}{2}
and define the normalized interaction vector

T^{n}(A,U):=\frac{1}{N_{n}}\Bigl(S_{00}(A),\ (S_{rs}(A,U))_{(r,s)\in I}\Bigr)\in\mathbb{R}^{1+|I|}.

Then for
\mathbb{P}
–a.e. latent sequence
U=(U_{i})_{i\geq 1}
, the conditional laws

\{\mathbb{P}_{\theta}(T^{n}(A,U)\in\cdot\,\mid U)\}_{n\geq 2}
satisfy a large deviation
principle on
\mathbb{R}^{1+|I|}
with speed
N_{n}
and good rate function

I_{\theta}(t)=\sup_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{1+|I|}}\Bigl\{\langle\lambda,t\rangle-\Lambda_{\theta}(\lambda)\Bigr\},

where, writing
\lambda=(\lambda_{0},(\lambda_{rs})_{(r,s)\in I})
,

\Lambda_{\theta}(\lambda)=\int_{(0,1)^{2}}\log\Bigl(1-W_{c,S}(x,y)+W_{c,S}(x,y)\exp\Bigl\{\lambda_{0}+\sum_{(r,s)\in I}\lambda_{rs}\psi_{r}(x)\psi_{s}(y)\Bigr\}\Bigr)\,dx\,dy.

Moreover,
\Lambda_{\theta}
is finite and real–analytic on
\mathbb{R}^{1+|I|}
, and its Hessian

\nabla^{2}\Lambda_{\theta}(\lambda)
is positive definite for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{1+|I|}

(in particular at
\lambda=0
). Consequently,
\Lambda_{\theta}
is strictly convex and

I_{\theta}
is strictly convex on the interior of its effective domain (equivalently,
on the interior of the convex support of
T^{n}
).

The LDP says that the multiscale interaction vector
\overline{T}_{n}
has
exponentially small tail probabilities at rate
\binom{n}{2}
, with a strictly
convex rate function.
Strict convexity is a stability signal: it rules out multiwell / “multiple
phase” behavior for these summaries in the band–limited regime and gives a
tractable variational description of rare events, which is exactly what one
needs for principled likelihood and hypothesis testing based on wavelet
statistics.

6.3
Canonical tilts and stability

Consider the canonical exponential tilt (at the
\binom{n}{2}
scale)

\frac{d\mathbb{P}_{\theta_{0},\lambda}^{(n)}}{d\mathbb{P}_{\theta_{0}}^{(n)}}(A,U)=\exp\Bigl\{N_{n}\langle\lambda,\overline{T}_{n}(A,U)\rangle-N_{n}\Lambda_{\theta_{0}}(\lambda)\Bigr\},\qquad N_{n}=\binom{n}{2}.

Such tilts correspond to reweighting by finitely many wavelet interaction
constraints and form the natural analogue of canonical ERGMs within the WL–ERG
setting.

Theorem 6.3

(Stable canonical region)
.

Fix a band
[J_{\min},J_{\max}]
, a constant
B>0
, and a base parameter
\theta_{0}=(c_{0},S_{0})\in\Theta_{B}
.
Let
I\subset I[J_{\min},J_{\max}]
be a finite index set such that

(0,0)\notin I,

(6)

and assume
I
is symmetric, i.e.
(r,s)\in I\Rightarrow(s,r)\in I
.
Write
N_{n}:=\binom{n}{2}
and define the normalised interaction vector

T^{n}(A,U):=\Big(\frac{S_{00}(A)}{N_{n}},\ \big(\frac{S_{rs}(A,U)}{N_{n}}\big)_{(r,s)\in I}\Big)\in\mathbb{R}^{1+|I|}.

We restrict to
symmetric tilts

\lambda=(\lambda_{0},(\lambda_{rs})_{(r,s)\in I})\in\mathbb{R}^{1+|I|}\quad\text{such that}\quad\lambda_{rs}=\lambda_{sr}\ \ \forall(r,s)\in I.

(7)

Let
W_{0}:=W_{c_{0},S_{0}}
. For
\lambda
define

g_{\lambda}(x,y):=\lambda_{0}+\sum_{(r,s)\in I}\lambda_{rs}\,\psi_{r}(x)\psi_{s}(y),\qquad Z_{\lambda}(x,y):=1-W_{0}(x,y)+W_{0}(x,y)e^{g_{\lambda}(x,y)}.

Define the (correctly normalised) limiting log-mgf

\Lambda_{\theta_{0}}(\lambda):=\int_{(0,1)^{2}}\log Z_{\lambda}(x,y)\,dx\,dy,

(8)

and let
I_{\theta_{0}}
denote its convex conjugate

I_{\theta_{0}}(t):=\sup_{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{1+|I|}}\{\langle\eta,t\rangle-\Lambda_{\theta_{0}}(\eta)\}
.

For each
n\geq 1
and each
\lambda
define the
canonical tilt
of the
conditional
WL–ERG law given
U
by

\frac{dP^{(n)}_{\theta_{0},\lambda}(\cdot\mid U)}{dP^{(n)}_{\theta_{0}}(\cdot\mid U)}(A):=\exp\Big\{N_{n}\langle\lambda,T^{n}(A,U)\rangle-N_{n}\Lambda^{(n)}_{\theta_{0}}(\lambda;U)\Big\},

(9)

where the (random) conditional normalisation is

\Lambda^{(n)}_{\theta_{0}}(\lambda;U):=\frac{1}{N_{n}}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\log Z_{\lambda}(U_{i},U_{j}).

Let
\Omega_{0}
be the probability-one event (under
U_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle iid}}{{\sim}}\mathrm{Unif}(0,1)
) on which the
U-statistic strong law holds simultaneously for the countable family

\{\log Z_{\eta}:\eta\in\mathbb{Q}^{1+|I|}\}
.

Then:

1.

\Lambda_{\theta_{0}}
is finite, real-analytic and strictly convex on
\mathbb{R}^{1+|I|}
, with everywhere
positive-definite Hessian. Consequently,
\nabla\Lambda_{\theta_{0}}
is a
C^{1}
-diffeomorphism from

\mathbb{R}^{1+|I|}
onto
\mathrm{int}(\mathrm{dom}\,I_{\theta_{0}})
, and

t=\nabla\Lambda_{\theta_{0}}(\lambda)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\lambda=\nabla I_{\theta_{0}}(t).

2.

For every compact
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{1+|I|}
there exists
B^{\prime}>0
such that, for all
\lambda\in K
,
the tilted kernel

W_{\theta_{0},\lambda}(x,y):=\frac{W_{0}(x,y)e^{g_{\lambda}(x,y)}}{Z_{\lambda}(x,y)}=\sigma\bigl(logitW_{0}(x,y)+g_{\lambda}(x,y)\bigr)

equals
W_{\theta(\lambda)}
for some
\theta(\lambda)\in\Theta_{B^{\prime}}
. In particular, the uniform interior
bounds of Lemma
D.2
and the non-degeneracy bounds of Theorem
6.1
hold uniformly over
\lambda\in K
.

3.

Fix
\lambda
. For every
U\in\Omega_{0}
, the sequence
\{T^{n}(A,U)\}_{n\geq 1}
satisfies, under

P^{(n)}_{\theta_{0},\lambda}(\cdot\mid U)
, a large deviation principle on
\mathbb{R}^{1+|I|}

with speed
N_{n}
and good rate function

I_{\theta_{0},\lambda}(t)=I_{\theta_{0}}(t)-\langle\lambda,t\rangle+\Lambda_{\theta_{0}}(\lambda),

which is strictly convex on
\mathrm{int}(\mathrm{dom}\,I_{\theta_{0}})
and hence has a unique minimiser at

t=\nabla\Lambda_{\theta_{0}}(\lambda)
.

Canonical tilts are the mechanism by which ERGMs enforce moment constraints.
This theorem shows that, in the WL–ERG band–limited regime, such tilts remain
stable on compact parameter sets: they preserve interior edge probabilities
and induce a single well-behaved macroscopic phase characterized by a unique
minimizer of the tilted rate function.
Practically, this justifies likelihood-based inference and hypothesis tests
based on finitely many wavelet interaction constraints without triggering the
classical ERGM degeneracy pathologies.

7
Modelling operations and examples

The wavelet coefficient representation supports natural algebraic operations
that can be interpreted directly in terms of multiscale network structure.
We briefly illustrate two such aspects: (i) closure under superposition via
convolution of coefficient laws, and (ii) how simple coefficient patterns can
be used to generate benchmark network families.

7.1
Examples for experimental design

By selecting which coefficients are activated and how they scale with
n
, the
wavelet parametrisation generates benchmark families with controlled multiscale
features. Representative examples include:

•

Homogeneous graphs:
activate only the constant (DC) coefficient.

•

SBM and hierarchical SBM:
use coarse wavelets approximating
block indicators across multiple resolutions.

•

Hub–dominated and scale–free graphs:
impose heavy–tailed
priors on mid– and fine–scale coefficients concentrated on a small
number of dyadic regions.

•

Small–world graphs:
superpose a coarse, geometry–like kernel
with sparse high–frequency “shortcut” coefficients.

•

Graphs with planted anomalies:
add a small number of fine
coefficients with nonzero means on selected dyadic blocks.

•

Populations of graphs:
specify Gaussian laws for coefficients
with scale–dependent variances and sample multiple realisations.

These examples emphasize that multiscale design choices correspond to explicit,
localised manipulations in coefficient space, making it straightforward to
target particular resolutions (mesoscopic communities, fine motifs, or rare
lesions) while keeping the remaining structure fixed.

7.2
Convolution of wavelet random graphs

Let
(\psi_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathcal{I}_{d}}
be an orthonormal wavelet basis of

L^{2}([0,1]^{2})
with finite index set
\mathcal{I}_{d}
.
For
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
define
S_{\theta}(x,y)=\sum_{\alpha}\theta_{\alpha}\psi_{\alpha}(x,y)

and the weighted wavelet random graph model with coefficient law
\Pi
by

Y_{ij}=S_{\Theta}(U_{i},U_{j})
,
\Theta\sim\Pi
.

We define the wavelet convolution of coefficient laws by
\Pi_{1}*\Pi_{2}
being
the law of
\Theta^{(1)}+\Theta^{(2)}
with independent

\Theta^{(k)}\sim\Pi_{k}
.

Proposition 7.1

(Closure and superposition)
.

Assume the (undirected) WRG kernel family
(S_{\theta})_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
is linear in
\theta
and symmetric
in the sense that
S_{\theta+\theta^{\prime}}=S_{\theta}+S_{\theta^{\prime}}
for all
\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and

S_{\theta}(x,y)=S_{\theta}(y,x)
for a.e.
(x,y)\in(0,1)^{2}
and all
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Let
\Pi_{1},\Pi_{2}
be probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and let
\Pi_{1}*\Pi_{2}
denote their convolution,
i.e. the law of
\Theta^{(1)}+\Theta^{(2)}
with independent
\Theta^{(k)}\sim\Pi_{k}
.

Wavelet random graphs are closed under convolution: if
U_{1},\dots,U_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{i.i.d.}}}{{\sim}}\mathrm{Unif}(0,1)

are independent of
\Theta^{(1)},\Theta^{(2)}
and we set
\Theta:=\Theta^{(1)}+\Theta^{(2)}\sim\Pi_{1}*\Pi_{2}
, then

\mathrm{WRG}_{n}(\Pi_{1}*\Pi_{2})
is generated by the kernel
S_{\Theta^{(1)}+\Theta^{(2)}}
and is jointly exchangeable.

Moreover, defining for
1\leq i<j\leq n

Y_{ij}:=S_{\Theta}(U_{i},U_{j}),\quad Y_{ji}:=Y_{ij},\quad Y_{ii}:=0,\qquad Y^{(k)}_{ij}:=S_{\Theta^{(k)}}(U_{i},U_{j}),\quad Y^{(k)}_{ji}:=Y^{(k)}_{ij},\quad Y^{(k)}_{ii}:=0,

and weighted degrees

D_{i}:=\sum_{j\neq i}Y_{ij},\qquad D_{i}^{(k)}:=\sum_{j\neq i}Y_{ij}^{(k)}\qquad(k=1,2),

we have the pathwise superposition identities

Y_{ij}=Y^{(1)}_{ij}+Y^{(2)}_{ij},\qquad D_{i}=D_{i}^{(1)}+D_{i}^{(2)}.

If
\Pi_{k}=\mathcal{N}(m_{k},\Sigma_{k})
are Gaussian, then

\Pi_{1}*\Pi_{2}=\mathcal{N}(m_{1}+m_{2},\Sigma_{1}+\Sigma_{2}).

Convolution corresponds to literally
adding
independent multiscale
structures in coefficient space, which yields an additive decomposition of
edges and degrees.
This is useful for graph populations (random effects) and for building
interpretable “coarse + fine” superpositions while preserving exchangeability.
A scale–separated convolution yields identifiable coarse and fine components.

7.3
Graph wavelet systems and wavelet ERGMs

On a fixed observed graph
G_{n}
one can define spectral or random–walk wavelets,
compute graph–dependent wavelet coefficients
c_{\alpha}(G_{n})
, and build wavelet
exponential families

P_{\theta}(G_{n})\propto\exp\{\langle\theta,c(G_{n})\rangle\}\,Q_{0}(G_{n})

for a base law
Q_{0}(G_{n})
.
This viewpoint complements the WL–ERG generative construction: when the latent
space is not explicitly modelled, wavelet coefficients extracted from the
observed network still furnish multiscale sufficient statistics for
exponential tilting.

Equipping coefficient space with a Mahalanobis metric yields Fréchet means by
averaging coefficients across a sample of graphs, providing a multiscale
analogue of location–scale families on graph space.
Operationally, this produces interpretable templates at each resolution and
localises deviations in both scale and graph domain, offering a direct bridge
between coefficient–level modelling and applied network summarisation.

8
Experiments

The empirical study is designed to directly test the paper’s
scale-resolved
claims:
(i) wavelet coordinates yield a parsimonious, interpretable parameterization of dyadic structure across resolutions;
(ii) recovery and detection are governed by
scale-indexed
visibility thresholds (Section 5);
(iii) coefficient-space exponential tilts remain stable and
non-degenerate

and (iv) fitted probabilities support
calibrated
out-of-sample prediction under strict dyad holdout.
Accordingly, we emphasize
proper scoring rules
(logloss / held-out log-likelihood) and explicit calibration diagnostics
(reliability curves and predicted-probability histograms), not only ranking metrics (AUC). This alignment matters:
the theory concerns
which resolutions are statistically visible
and
how dependence can be tilted stably
,
so the empirical diagnostics must be multiscale and probability-calibrated rather than purely rank-based.

When an intrinsic coordinate is observed, we set
U_{i}
to that coordinate; otherwise we compute a seriation ordering
\widehat{U}

(Section
8.4
). We then fit
WL–ERG
via the following fixed pipeline:

1.

compute an ordering
\widehat{U}
(default: degree ordering; robustness: Fiedler ordering).

2.

partition vertices into

contiguous bins along
\widehat{U}
.

3.

estimate the ordered
K\times K
step-function
\widehat{P}^{\textsc{Histogram}}
using
training dyads only
.

4.

\widehat{\eta}=\log\!\big(\widehat{P}^{\textsc{Histogram}}/(1-\widehat{P}^{\textsc{Histogram}})\big)
.

5.

compute 2D Haar coefficients
\widehat{\theta}=\mathcal{W}(\widehat{\eta})
.

6.

Multiscale shrinkage:
hard-threshold coefficients using the paper’s multiscale rule
(Section
3
).

7.

\widetilde{\eta}=\mathcal{W}^{-1}(\widehat{\theta}^{\text{thr}})

\widehat{P}^{\textsc{WL--ERG}}=(1+\exp(-\widetilde{\eta}))^{-1}
.

8.

Clipping:
clip probabilities to

multiscale shrinkage on the logit graphon surface

8.7
Robustness to discretization
K
and threshold multiplier
\kappa

Two natural concerns in ordered-graphon estimators are sensitivity to (i) the discretization level
K=2^{J}
and
(ii) the coefficient threshold constant.
Both are explicitly represented in the wavelet estimator’s construction.
K
fixes the maximal resolution
J
where
coefficients are represented, while a multiplicative constant
\kappa
in the
(universal, scale-corrected) hard-thresholding rule on the logit surface controls the threshold scale.
Thus, sweeping
K
and
\kappa
does not provide “extra tuning,” but instead a direct stability check of the estimator’s multiscale mechanism.

We rerun the entire, strict dyad holdout pipeline, while varying

K\in\{2^{6},2^{7},2^{8}\}
(keeping the ordering fixed) and, separately, varying

\kappa\in\{0.5,1,2\}
, while keeping
K
fixed at its default.
We report held-out
logloss
(equivalently mean held-out log-likelihood up to sign), since this is the metric that
certifies probability calibration under a proper scoring rule.

Across the tested perturbations of
(K,\kappa)
, the qualitative conclusions remain unchanged. The main gains of
WL–ERG
are
expressed in calibration-sensitive proper scoring (logloss / held-out log-likelihood). These gains are consistent with the estimator’s
coefficient-space shrinkage mechanism, rather than a single favorable discretization.

9
Discussion

WL–ERGs provide a unified multiscale framework for generative network modeling in which wavelet coordinates parameterize latent connectivity. This construction combines three desirable features in a single model class: the flexibility of graphon-based representations, the interpretability and scale separation of wavelet expansions, and the statistical tractability of exponential families through multiscale sufficient statistics. The theory developed here shows that the wavelet parameterization is not merely a convenient re-expression of a latent kernel, but an analytically useful coordinate system: it yields parameter-efficient representations for multiscale structure, supports statistically principled estimation under sparsity in coefficient space, and leads to sharp phase-transition statements for recovery and detection problems that are naturally indexed by scale.

Several extensions are immediate and practically important. A primary methodological direction is to relax the observed-design setting and develop estimators and tests that are invariant to vertex labeling, including computationally efficient procedures that remain stable under graphon identifiability. Extending the framework to sparser regimes and to directed, weighted, or attributed networks would broaden its applicability and clarify how multiscale coefficient constraints interact with degree heterogeneity and sparsity. From a modeling perspective, WL–ERGs suggest natural Bayesian and regularized variants via structured shrinkage priors on wavelet coefficients and hierarchical coupling across scales, as well as dynamic extensions in which coefficients evolve over time for longitudinal network data. Finally, the coefficient-space geometry underlying WL–ERGs provides a principled starting point for population-level tasks such as multiscale Fréchet means, two-sample testing, and uncertainty quantification that explicitly distinguish variability at coarse versus fine resolutions.

Exchangeability, graphon representation and universality

Theorem A.1

(Joint exchangeability and graphon representation)
.

Fix
(c,S)
and let
W_{c,S}
be the logistic wavelet graphon from Definition
2.2
.
Choose a measurable representative (still denoted
W_{c,S}
) such that

W_{c,S}(x,y)=W_{c,S}(y,x)\quad\text{and}\quad W_{c,S}(x,y)\in(0,1)\qquad\forall\,(x,y)\in(0,1)^{2},

which is possible by modifying
W_{c,S}
on a null set.

Let
(U_{i})_{i\geq 1}
be i.i.d.
\mathrm{Unif}(0,1)
, and let
(\xi_{ij})_{1\leq i<j<\infty}
be i.i.d.
\mathrm{Unif}(0,1)
, independent of
(U_{i})_{i\geq 1}
. Define
A_{ii}:=0
and, for
i<j
,

A_{ij}:=\mathbf{1}\{\xi_{ij}\leq W_{c,S}(U_{i},U_{j})\},\qquad A_{ji}:=A_{ij}.

For each
n\geq 1
, let
G_{n}
be the induced graph on
[n]:=\{1,\dots,n\}
with adjacency matrix

(A_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
. Then
G_{n}\sim\mathrm{WL\mbox{-}ERG}(c,S)
for every
n
, and the infinite
array
(A_{ij})_{i<j}
is jointly exchangeable: for every finite permutation
\pi
of
\mathbb{N}
,

(A_{ij})_{i<j}\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\ (A_{\pi(i)\pi(j)})_{i<j}.

Moreover,
(A_{ij})
is generated from the (representing) graphon
W_{c,S}
via the standard graphon
sampling scheme (hence it is a graphon special case of the Aldous–Hoover representation; as usual,
the representing graphon is only identifiable up to measure-preserving transformations).

This theorem situates WL–ERGs inside the standard unlabeled-network invariance principle: because the array
is jointly exchangeable, vertex names carry no information. It also makes the graphon connection constructive:
the WL–ERG parameters
(c,S)
directly define the representing graphon
W_{c,S}
, so asymptotic comparisons
to other graphon models can be done in coefficient space.

Theorem A.2

(Convergence in cut distance)
.

Fix
(c,S)
and let
W_{c,S}:(0,1)^{2}\to(0,1)
be the logistic wavelet graphon of
Definition
2.2
. Fix a measurable representative of
W_{c,S}
(modifying it on a null
set if necessary) and continue to denote it by
W_{c,S}
. Consider the coupled construction in which

U_{1},U_{2},\dots\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i.i.d.}}{{\sim}}\mathrm{Unif}(0,1)
and, conditional on
U=(U_{i})_{i\geq 1}
,
the edges are sampled independently for
i<j
as

A_{ij}\mid U\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}\bigl(W_{c,S}(U_{i},U_{j})\bigr),\qquad A_{ji}=A_{ij},\qquad A_{ii}=0.

For each
n
, let
\pi_{n}
be a permutation of
\{1,\dots,n\}
such that

U_{\pi_{n}(1)}\leq\cdots\leq U_{\pi_{n}(n)}
(break ties by index), and set

I_{i}:=((i-1)/n,i/n]
. Define the ordered empirical graphon
W_{G_{n}}:(0,1)^{2}\to\{0,1\}
by

W_{G_{n}}(x,y):=A_{\pi_{n}(i)\pi_{n}(j)}\qquad\text{whenever }(x,y)\in I_{i}\times I_{j}.

Then, almost surely (with respect to the above coupling),

\|W_{G_{n}}-W_{c,S}\|_{\square}\longrightarrow 0\qquad(n\to\infty),

where the cut norm is

\|F\|_{\square}:=\sup_{A,B\subseteq(0,1)\ \mathrm{meas.}}\left|\int_{A\times B}F(x,y)\,dx\,dy\right|.

Cut-norm convergence is a strong “graph-to-graphon” fidelity statement: after aligning vertices by their
latent positions, the empirical adjacency matrix converges to the generating graphon in the topology used for
dense graph limits. This is exactly the topology that underpins many consistency and limit arguments in graphon
inference.

Theorem A.3

(Universality for logistic graphons)
.

Let
W:(0,1)^{2}\to(0,1)
be measurable and symmetric, and define its logit

f(x,y):=\log\!\Bigl(\frac{W(x,y)}{1-W(x,y)}\Bigr).

Assume that
f\in L^{2}((0,1)^{2})
.
Fix an orthonormal basis
\{\psi_{r}:r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
of
L^{2}(0,1)
with
\psi_{0}\equiv 1

(e.g. the wavelet basis of Definition
2.1
).

Then there exist
c^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}
and a symmetric Hilbert–Schmidt matrix

S^{\star}=(s^{\star}_{rs})_{r,s\geq 0}
such that, if
f_{S^{\star}}
denotes the
L^{2}((0,1)^{2})
-limit of the partial sums

\sum_{0\leq r,s\leq m}s^{\star}_{rs}\,\psi_{r}(x)\psi_{s}(y)\qquad(m\to\infty),

then

f=c^{\star}+f_{S^{\star}}\quad\text{in }L^{2}((0,1)^{2}),\qquad\text{and hence}\qquad W=\sigma(c^{\star}+f_{S^{\star}})\quad\text{a.e. on }(0,1)^{2}.

Equivalently,
W=W_{c^{\star},S^{\star}}
almost everywhere, with
W_{c,S}
as in
Definition
2.2
(with
f_{S}
interpreted via its
L^{2}
-convergent expansion).
Moreover, the representation may be chosen with
s^{\star}_{00}=0
.

In particular, the WL–ERG graphons
\{W_{c,S}\}
coincide (up to null sets) with the class of all
logistic graphons whose logits lie in
L^{2}((0,1)^{2})
.

Universality says the wavelet bilinear logit expansion is not merely a modelling choice but a coordinate
system for a broad and analytically natural class: logistic graphons with square-integrable logits. Any loss
of expressiveness therefore comes only from intentional restrictions (sparsity, band-limiting, low rank) imposed
on
S
, making approximation and statistical complexity transparent in coefficient space.

Remark B.1

(Boundary case)
.

If
\mathcal{Q}(m)\neq\varnothing
but there is no finite
\theta
such that
P_{\theta}(\cdot\mid U)\in\mathcal{Q}(m)

(for instance, when the target moment vector
m
lies on the boundary of the convex support of the sufficient
statistic vector, relative to its affine hull), then the unique maximiser
Q^{\star}
from
Theorem

. In this case one can view

of “parameter blow-up at the boundary” in exponential families.

Conditional on

W_{\theta}=\sigma\circ\eta_{\theta}

\sigma(x)^{a}\bigl(1-\sigma(x)\bigr)^{1-a}=\exp\{ax-\log(1+e^{x})\},\qquad a\in\{0,1\},

\log\mathbb{P}_{\theta}(A\mid U)=\sum_{i<j}\Bigl(A_{ij}\eta_{\theta}(U_{i},U_{j})-\log\bigl(1+e^{\eta_{\theta}(U_{i},U_{j})}\bigr)\Bigr).

By the definition of
\eta_{\theta}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i<j}A_{ij}\eta_{\theta}(U_{i},U_{j})

\displaystyle=c\sum_{i<j}A_{ij}+\sum_{i<j}A_{ij}\sum_{(r,s)\in I}s_{rs}\psi_{r}(U_{i})\psi_{s}(U_{j}).

Since
I

\sum_{i<j}A_{ij}\eta_{\theta}(U_{i},U_{j})=c\,S_{00}(A)+\sum_{(r,s)\in I}s_{rs}\,S_{rs}(A,U).

Define

\Psi_{n}(\theta;U):=\sum_{i<j}\log\bigl(1+e^{\eta_{\theta}(U_{i},U_{j})}\bigr),

which depends on
\theta
and
U
but not on
A
. Substituting the previous display into the
expression for
\log\mathbb{P}_{\theta}(A\mid U)
and exponentiating yields the claimed exponential-family form.

Finally,
\Psi_{n}(\theta;U)
is the log–partition function. Indeed,

c\,S_{00}(A)+\sum_{(r,s)\in I}s_{rs}\,S_{rs}(A,U)=\sum_{i<j}A_{ij}\,\eta_{\theta}(U_{i},U_{j}),

so summing the unnormalised weights over
A\in\{0,1\}^{\binom{n}{2}}
factorises over edges:

\displaystyle\sum_{A\in\{0,1\}^{\binom{n}{2}}}\exp\!\left\{\sum_{i<j}A_{ij}\eta_{\theta}(U_{i},U_{j})\right\}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i<j}\left(\sum_{a\in\{0,1\}}e^{a\,\eta_{\theta}(U_{i},U_{j})}\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i<j}\bigl(1+e^{\eta_{\theta}(U_{i},U_{j})}\bigr)=\exp\{\Psi_{n}(\theta;U)\}.

Thus the normalising constant is
e^{\Psi_{n}(\theta;U)}
, completing the proof.

Hence, for all

|\eta_{c,S}(x,y)|=|c+f_{S}(x,y)|\leq|c|+|f_{S}(x,y)|\leq B+BC_{\mathrm{wav}}=:M_{B}.

Let
\sigma(t)=(1+e^{-t})^{-1}
and define

p_{-}(B):=\sigma(-M_{B}),\qquad p_{+}(B):=\sigma(M_{B}).

Since
\sigma
is increasing, for all
(x,y)\in(0,1)^{2}
,

0<p_{-}(B)\leq W_{c,S}(x,y)\leq p_{+}(B)<1.

(27)

2. Concentration of the edge density.

Let
N_{n}:=\binom{n}{2}
. Then
L_{n}=N_{n}^{-1}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}A_{ij}
. Conditional on
U
, the
variables
\{A_{ij}:i<j\}
are independent Bernoulli with means in
[p_{-}(B),p_{+}(B)]
, so

\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[L_{n}\mid U]\in[p_{-}(B),p_{+}(B)]
almost surely. By Hoeffding’s inequality,
for any
\delta>0
,

\mathbb{P}_{\theta}\Bigl(\bigl|L_{n}-\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[L_{n}\mid U]\bigr|\geq\delta\,\Big|\,U\Bigr)\leq 2\exp\bigl(-2\delta^{2}N_{n}\bigr)=2\exp\bigl(-\delta^{2}n(n-1)\bigr).

Choose
\delta
such that
0<\delta<\min\{p_{-}(B),\,1-p_{+}(B)\}
and set

\varepsilon_{1}:=p_{-}(B)-\delta,\qquad\varepsilon_{2}:=p_{+}(B)+\delta.

Then
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}\in(0,1)
and

\{L_{n}\notin[\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}]\}\subseteq\{|L_{n}-\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[L_{n}\mid U]|\geq\delta\}
, so for all
n\geq 2
,

\mathbb{P}_{\theta}\bigl(L_{n}\notin[\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}]\bigr)\leq 2\exp\bigl(-\delta^{2}n(n-1)\bigr)\leq 2\exp\Bigl(-\frac{\delta^{2}}{2}n^{2}\Bigr),

where we used
n(n-1)\geq n^{2}/2
. This proves the claimed bound with
C=2
and
\kappa=\delta^{2}/2
.

3. Almost sure convergence of fixed homomorphism densities.

Fix a finite simple graph
H
with
k:=|V(H)|
and
m:=|E(H)|
. Define the (possibly non-injective)
homomorphism density

t(H,G_{n}):=\frac{1}{n^{k}}\sum_{v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\in[n]}\ \prod_{(a,b)\in E(H)}A_{v_{a}v_{b}},

and the graphon functional

t(H,W_{c,S}):=\int_{(0,1)^{k}}\prod_{(a,b)\in E(H)}W_{c,S}(x_{a},x_{b})\,dx_{1}\cdots dx_{k}.

Let
(n)_{k}:=n(n-1)\cdots(n-k+1)
and define the injective version for
n\geq k
by

t_{\mathrm{inj}}(H,G_{n}):=\frac{1}{(n)_{k}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\in[n]\\
\text{all distinct}\end{subarray}}\ \prod_{(a,b)\in E(H)}A_{v_{a}v_{b}}.

(For
n<k
, set
t_{\mathrm{inj}}(H,G_{n}):=0
.)

3(a). Collision terms are negligible.

Since each summand lies in
[0,1]
, for
n\geq k
,

\bigl|t(H,G_{n})-t_{\mathrm{inj}}(H,G_{n})\bigr|\leq\Bigl|\frac{1}{n^{k}}-\frac{1}{(n)_{k}}\Bigr|\sum_{\text{distinct}}1+\frac{1}{n^{k}}\sum_{\text{not all distinct}}1=2\Bigl(1-\frac{(n)_{k}}{n^{k}}\Bigr).

Moreover, for
n\geq k
,

\frac{(n)_{k}}{n^{k}}=\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\Bigl(1-\frac{j}{n}\Bigr)\geq 1-\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{j}{n}=1-\frac{k(k-1)}{2n},

so

\bigl|t(H,G_{n})-t_{\mathrm{inj}}(H,G_{n})\bigr|\leq\frac{k(k-1)}{n}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0.

(28)

3(b). Concentration of
t_{\mathrm{inj}}(H,G_{n})
around its conditional mean.

Conditional on
U
,
t_{\mathrm{inj}}(H,G_{n})
is a function of the independent edge indicators

\{A_{ij}:i<j\}
. If we change a single edge
A_{ij}
, the value of
t_{\mathrm{inj}}(H,G_{n})
can change
only through those injective
k
-tuples for which some edge of
H
is mapped to
\{i,j\}
.
For each
(a,b)\in E(H)
there are at most
2(n-2)_{k-2}
such ordered injective tuples, hence the total
number of affected summands is at most
2m(n-2)_{k-2}
. Since each summand changes by at most
1
,

\bigl|t_{\mathrm{inj}}(H,G_{n})-t_{\mathrm{inj}}(H,G_{n})^{\prime}\bigr|\leq\frac{2m(n-2)_{k-2}}{(n)_{k}}=\frac{2m}{n(n-1)}=:c_{n},

where
t_{\mathrm{inj}}(H,G_{n})^{\prime}
denotes the value after changing one edge.
McDiarmid’s inequality (conditional on
U
) therefore yields, for every
\epsilon>0
,

\mathbb{P}_{\theta}\Bigl(\bigl|t_{\mathrm{inj}}(H,G_{n})-\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[t_{\mathrm{inj}}(H,G_{n})\mid U]\bigr|\geq\epsilon\,\Big|\,U\Bigr)\leq 2\exp\Bigl(-\frac{2\epsilon^{2}}{\binom{n}{2}c_{n}^{2}}\Bigr).

Since
\binom{n}{2}c_{n}^{2}=\binom{n}{2}\cdot\frac{4m^{2}}{n^{2}(n-1)^{2}}=\frac{2m^{2}}{n(n-1)}
, we get
for all
n\geq 2
,

\mathbb{P}_{\theta}\Bigl(\bigl|t_{\mathrm{inj}}(H,G_{n})-\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[t_{\mathrm{inj}}(H,G_{n})\mid U]\bigr|\geq\epsilon\,\Big|\,U\Bigr)\leq 2\exp\Bigl(-\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{m^{2}}\,n(n-1)\Bigr).

(29)

3(c). Almost sure convergence to the conditional mean.

Fix
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and apply (
29
) with
\epsilon_{\ell}:=1/\ell
.
The bound is summable in
n
and does not depend on
U
, hence by Borel–Cantelli,

\bigl|t_{\mathrm{inj}}(H,G_{n})-\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[t_{\mathrm{inj}}(H,G_{n})\mid U]\bigr|<\frac{1}{\ell}\quad\text{eventually (in $n$), a.s.}

Intersecting over
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
yields

t_{\mathrm{inj}}(H,G_{n})-\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[t_{\mathrm{inj}}(H,G_{n})\mid U]\longrightarrow 0\qquad\text{a.s.}

(30)

3(d). Almost sure limit of the conditional mean.

For injective
k
-tuples, all edges in the product correspond to distinct unordered pairs, hence
(independence given
U
) implies

\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[t_{\mathrm{inj}}(H,G_{n})\mid U]=\frac{1}{(n)_{k}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\in[n]\\
\text{all distinct}\end{subarray}}\ \prod_{(a,b)\in E(H)}W_{c,S}(U_{v_{a}},U_{v_{b}}).

Let
h(x_{1},\dots,x_{k}):=\prod_{(a,b)\in E(H)}W_{c,S}(x_{a},x_{b})\in[0,1]
and define its symmetrisation

h_{\mathrm{sym}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k}):=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{\pi\in S_{k}}h(x_{\pi(1)},\dots,x_{\pi(k)}).

Then
\mathbb{E}[h_{\mathrm{sym}}(U_{1},\dots,U_{k})]=\mathbb{E}[h(U_{1},\dots,U_{k})]
and

\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[t_{\mathrm{inj}}(H,G_{n})\mid U]=\frac{1}{\binom{n}{k}}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq n}h_{\mathrm{sym}}(U_{i_{1}},\dots,U_{i_{k}}),

i.e. it is the usual symmetric
U
–statistic of order
k
with bounded kernel
h_{\mathrm{sym}}
.
By the strong law of large numbers for bounded
U
–statistics (e.g. Hoeffding’s theorem),

\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[t_{\mathrm{inj}}(H,G_{n})\mid U]\longrightarrow\mathbb{E}\bigl[h(U_{1},\dots,U_{k})\bigr]=t(H,W_{c,S})\qquad\text{a.s.}

(31)

3(e). Conclude
t(H,G_{n})\to t(H,W_{c,S})
a.s. and identify the limit.

Combining (
30
) and (
31
) gives

t_{\mathrm{inj}}(H,G_{n})\to t(H,W_{c,S})
almost surely, and then (
28
) yields

t(H,G_{n})\to t(H,W_{c,S})
almost surely as well.
If
m\geq 1
, then by (
27
),

(p_{-}(B))^{m}\leq t(H,W_{c,S})\leq(p_{+}(B))^{m},

so
t(H,W_{c,S})\in(0,1)
. If
m=0
, then
t(H,G_{n})\equiv t(H,W_{c,S})\equiv 1
.

4. Continuity of
\theta\mapsto t(H,W_{c,S})
on
\Theta_{B}

Let
\theta=(c,S)
and
\theta^{\prime}=(c^{\prime},S^{\prime})
in
\Theta_{B}
(supported on the same finite band

For all

, Cauchy–Schwarz gives

|f_{S}(x,y)-f_{S^{\prime}}(x,y)|=\Bigl|\sum_{(r,s)\in I}(s_{rs}-s^{\prime}_{rs})\psi_{r}(x)\psi_{s}(y)\Bigr|\leq C_{\mathrm{wav}}\Bigl(\sum_{(r,s)\in I}(s_{rs}-s^{\prime}_{rs})^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}.

Hence

\|\eta_{c,S}-\eta_{c^{\prime},S^{\prime}}\|_{\infty}\leq|c-c^{\prime}|+C_{\mathrm{wav}}\Bigl(\sum_{(r,s)\in I}(s_{rs}-s^{\prime}_{rs})^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}.

Since

Finally, for any two graphons

This completes the proof.

v(x,y):=\Big(1,\ (\psi_{r}(x)\psi_{s}(y))_{(r,s)\in I}\Big)\in\mathbb{R}^{1+|I|}.

A direct differentiation gives

\nabla\Lambda_{\theta_{0}}(\lambda)=\int_{(0,1)^{2}}p_{\lambda}(x,y)\,v(x,y)\,dx\,dy,

(34)

and

\nabla^{2}\Lambda_{\theta_{0}}(\lambda)=\int_{(0,1)^{2}}p_{\lambda}(x,y)\bigl(1-p_{\lambda}(x,y)\bigr)\,v(x,y)v(x,y)^{\top}\,dx\,dy.

(35)

Hence for any
a\in\mathbb{R}^{1+|I|}
,

a^{\top}\nabla^{2}\Lambda_{\theta_{0}}(\lambda)a=\int_{(0,1)^{2}}p_{\lambda}(1-p_{\lambda})\,\bigl(a^{\top}v(x,y)\bigr)^{2}\,dx\,dy.

(36)

Since
p_{\lambda}(1-p_{\lambda})>0
pointwise, the right-hand side vanishes if and only if

a^{\top}v(x,y)=0
for a.e.
(x,y)
, i.e.

a_{0}+\sum_{(r,s)\in I}a_{rs}\psi_{r}(x)\psi_{s}(y)=0\quad\text{in }L^{2}((0,1)^{2}).

(37)

Because
\{\psi_{r}(x)\psi_{s}(y)\}_{r,s\geq 0}
is an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}((0,1)^{2})
and
(0,0)\notin I

(see (
6
)), the family
\{1\}\cup\{\psi_{r}(x)\psi_{s}(y):(r,s)\in I\}
is linearly independent,
so (
37
) forces
a=0
. Therefore
\nabla^{2}\Lambda_{\theta_{0}}(\lambda)
is
positive definite for all
\lambda
, and
\Lambda_{\theta_{0}}
is strictly convex. Real–analyticity
was established above.

Let
I_{\theta_{0}}
be the convex conjugate of
\Lambda_{\theta_{0}}
. Since
\Lambda_{\theta_{0}}
is
C^{2}
on all of

\mathbb{R}^{1+|I|}
with everywhere positive–definite Hessian, it is a Legendre-type function.
Standard Legendre duality (e.g. Rockafellar,
Convex Analysis
, Theorem 26.5 and Corollary 26.4)
implies that
I_{\theta_{0}}
is
C^{2}
on
\mathrm{int}(\mathrm{dom}\,I_{\theta_{0}})
and that

t=\nabla\Lambda_{\theta_{0}}(\lambda)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\lambda=\nabla I_{\theta_{0}}(t),

so
\nabla\Lambda_{\theta_{0}}
is a global
C^{1}
-diffeomorphism from
\mathbb{R}^{1+|I|}
onto

\mathrm{int}(\mathrm{dom}\,I_{\theta_{0}})
.

(2) Compact tilts remain band-limited and non-degenerate.

Fix
\lambda
and
U
. From (

) and the identity

N_{n}\langle\lambda,T^{n}(A,U)\rangle=\sum_{i<j}A_{ij}g_{\lambda}(U_{i},U_{j})

P^{(n)}_{\theta_{0},\lambda}(A\mid U)\ \propto\ \prod_{i<j}\Big(W_{0}(U_{i},U_{j})e^{g_{\lambda}(U_{i},U_{j})}\Big)^{A_{ij}}\Big(1-W_{0}(U_{i},U_{j})\Big)^{1-A_{ij}},

hence conditional on
U
the edges are independent Bernoulli with success probabilities

W_{\theta_{0},\lambda}(U_{i},U_{j})

(38)

Equivalently,
logitW_{\theta_{0},\lambda}=logitW_{0}+g_{\lambda}

Since
\theta_{0}\in\Theta_{B}

where
s_{rs}(\lambda)=s^{0}_{rs}+\lambda_{rs}
for
(r,s)\in I
and
s_{rs}(\lambda)=s^{0}_{rs}
for indices in the band but
outside
I

7

I

S(\lambda)

\theta(\lambda)=(c(\lambda),S(\lambda))

Now let

\theta_{0}\in\Theta_{B}

(3) LDP under the canonical tilt and unique minimiser (quenched in
U
).

Fix
\lambda
and let
U\in\Omega_{0}
. For
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{1+|I|}
define the conditional scaled log-mgf

\Lambda^{(n)}_{\theta_{0}}(\eta;U):=\frac{1}{N_{n}}\log\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{0}}\!\left[\exp\!\big\{N_{n}\langle\eta,T^{n}(A,U)\rangle\big\}\,\middle|\,U\right].

Conditional on
U
, the edges are independent Bernoulli and

\Lambda^{(n)}_{\theta_{0}}(\eta;U)=\frac{1}{N_{n}}\sum_{i<j}\log Z_{\eta}(U_{i},U_{j}).

By definition of
\Omega_{0}
, for every
\eta\in\mathbb{Q}^{1+|I|}
,

\Lambda^{(n)}_{\theta_{0}}(\eta;U)\longrightarrow\Lambda_{\theta_{0}}(\eta)\qquad(n\to\infty).

We upgrade to locally uniform convergence on compacts. Note that

\nabla_{\eta}\log Z_{\eta}(x,y)=p_{\eta}(x,y)\,v(x,y),

so
\|\nabla_{\eta}\log Z_{\eta}(x,y)\|\leq\|v(x,y)\|
. Since
I
is finite and contained in a fixed band,

v
is bounded; choose
L<\infty
with
\|v(x,y)\|\leq L
for all
(x,y)
. Hence both maps

\eta\mapsto\Lambda^{(n)}_{\theta_{0}}(\eta;U)
and
\eta\mapsto\Lambda_{\theta_{0}}(\eta)
are globally
L
–Lipschitz.
Fix a compact set
K_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{1+|I|}
and
\varepsilon>0
, and choose a finite

\delta
–net
K_{\delta}\subset K_{0}\cap\mathbb{Q}^{1+|I|}
with
\delta:=\varepsilon/(3L)
.
For
U\in\Omega_{0}
, convergence holds at each point of
K_{\delta}
, hence for all sufficiently large
n
,

\max_{q\in K_{\delta}}|\Lambda^{(n)}_{\theta_{0}}(q;U)-\Lambda_{\theta_{0}}(q)|\leq\varepsilon/3
.
For any
\eta\in K_{0}
pick
q\in K_{\delta}
with
\|\eta-q\|\leq\delta
and use Lipschitzness to obtain

|\Lambda^{(n)}_{\theta_{0}}(\eta;U)-\Lambda_{\theta_{0}}(\eta)|\leq L\|\eta-q\|+\varepsilon/3+L\|q-\eta\|\leq\varepsilon.

Thus
\Lambda^{(n)}_{\theta_{0}}(\cdot;U)\to\Lambda_{\theta_{0}}(\cdot)
locally uniformly for every
U\in\Omega_{0}
.

By (
9
), for
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{1+|I|}
,

\frac{1}{N_{n}}\log\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{0},\lambda}\!\left[\exp\!\big\{N_{n}\langle\eta,T^{n}\rangle\big\}\,\middle|\,U\right]=\Lambda^{(n)}_{\theta_{0}}(\lambda+\eta;U)-\Lambda^{(n)}_{\theta_{0}}(\lambda;U)\longrightarrow\Lambda_{\theta_{0}}(\lambda+\eta)-\Lambda_{\theta_{0}}(\lambda)=:\Lambda_{\theta_{0},\lambda}(\eta),

locally uniformly in
\eta
. By part (1),
\Lambda_{\theta_{0},\lambda}
is finite and differentiable on

\mathbb{R}^{1+|I|}
. Moreover,
T^{n}(A,U)
takes values in a fixed compact set (since the wavelets in the band are bounded),
so exponential tightness holds. The Gärtner–Ellis theorem therefore yields, for each fixed
U\in\Omega_{0}
,
an LDP for
\{T^{n}(\cdot,U)\}
under
P^{(n)}_{\theta_{0},\lambda}(\cdot\mid U)
with speed
N_{n}
and rate function

I_{\theta_{0},\lambda}(t)=\sup_{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{1+|I|}}\{\langle\eta,t\rangle-\Lambda_{\theta_{0},\lambda}(\eta)\}.

Finally, compute

\displaystyle I_{\theta_{0},\lambda}(t)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\eta}\{\langle\eta,t\rangle-\Lambda_{\theta_{0}}(\lambda+\eta)+\Lambda_{\theta_{0}}(\lambda)\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\zeta}\{\langle\zeta-\lambda,t\rangle-\Lambda_{\theta_{0}}(\zeta)\}+\Lambda_{\theta_{0}}(\lambda)=I_{\theta_{0}}(t)-\langle\lambda,t\rangle+\Lambda_{\theta_{0}}(\lambda).

Because
\Lambda_{\theta_{0}}
is strictly convex,
I_{\theta_{0}}
is strictly convex on
\mathrm{int}(\mathrm{dom}\,I_{\theta_{0}})

and so is
I_{\theta_{0},\lambda}
. Hence
I_{\theta_{0},\lambda}
has a unique minimiser, characterised by the first-order condition

\nabla I_{\theta_{0}}(t)=\lambda
, equivalently
t=\nabla\Lambda_{\theta_{0}}(\lambda)
by Legendre duality.
∎

For

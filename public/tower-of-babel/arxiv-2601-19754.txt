Triangulated monoidal categorifications of finite type cluster algebras
Élie Casbi

Abstract
. We propose a framework of monoidal categorification of finite type cluster algebras involving triangulated monoidal categories. Namely, given a Dynkin quiver Q Q , we consider the bounded homotopy category 𝒦 Q ( 1 ) \mathcal{K}_{Q}^{(1)} of a symmetric monoidal category ℋ Q ( 1 ) \mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)} that we define in terms of the Auslander-Reiten theory of Q Q . Using some iterated mapping cone procedure, we construct a distinguished family { C ∙ ​ [ β ] } β ∈ Δ + \{C_{\bullet}[\beta]\}_{\beta\in\Delta_{+}} of chain complexes in 𝒦 Q ( 1 ) \mathcal{K}_{Q}^{(1)} characterized (up to isomorphism) by homological conditions similar to those of higher exact sequences appearing in the context of higher homological algebra. We then prove that the distinguished triangle in 𝒦 Q ( 1 ) \mathcal{K}_{Q}^{(1)} given by each mapping cone categorifies an exchange relation in the finite type cluster algebra 𝒜 Q \mathcal{A}_{Q} with initial exchange quiver Q Q (for a suitable choice of frozen variables). As a consequence, we obtain that for each positive root β \beta , the Euler characteristic of C ∙ ​ [ β ] C_{\bullet}[\beta] coincides with the truncated q q -character of the simple module L ⁡ [ β ] L[\beta] in the HL category 𝒞 ξ ( 1 ) \mathcal{C}_{\xi}^{(1)} categorifying the cluster variable x ⁡ [ β ] x[\beta] of 𝒜 Q \mathcal{A}_{Q} via Hernandez-Leclerc’s monoidal categorification. Along the way, we establish a uniform formula for the dominant monomial of L ⁡ [ β ] L[\beta] in all types A n A_{n} and D n D_{n} for arbitrary orientations (agreeing with Brito-Chari’s results in type A n A_{n} ).



1.
Introduction

Monoidal categorifications of cluster algebras have been involved in several major developments over the past fifteen years. They were first introduced by Hernandez-Leclerc
[
17
]
as a way to provide new insights in cluster theory using the representation theory of quantum affine algebras. Following
[
17
]
, a monoidal categorification of a cluster algebra
\mathcal{A}
consists of an abelian monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
such that there is a ring isomorphism
\mathcal{A}\simeq K_{0}(\mathcal{C})
from
\mathcal{A}
to the Grothendieck ring of
\mathcal{C}
sending the cluster monomials in
\mathcal{A}
to isomorphism classes of real simple objects in
\mathcal{C}
(real meaning of simple tensor square). In this framework, the exchange relations associated to cluster mutations are interpreted as identities in the Grothendieck ring of
\mathcal{C}
coming from certain distinguished short exact sequences in
\mathcal{C}
. Moreover, the cluster expansion of a cluster monomial with respect to a well-chosen initial cluster can be identified with the
q
-character of the corresponding (real) simple module under the isomorphism
\mathcal{A}\simeq K_{0}(\mathcal{C})
. The representation theory of quantum affine algebras (and recently shifted quantum affine algebras) provides powerful tools to construct abelian monoidal categorifications of Lie-theoretic cluster algebras. These categorifications can be viewed as subcategories of a certain monoidal category
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}
introduced by Hernandez-Leclerc
[
18
]
, which is a monoidal subcategory of the category of finite-dimensional representations of the quantum affine algebra associated to
\mathfrak{g}
.
Although Hernandez-Leclerc’s original work
[
17
]
mostly dealt with cluster algebras
\mathcal{A}_{Q^{bip}}
of finite type
A_{n}
associated with a bipartite initial exchange quiver, their results have later been extended to more sophisticated cluster structures. As far as finite type cluster algebras are concerned, Brito-Chari
[
4
]
constructed monoidal categorifications
\mathcal{C}_{\xi}^{(1)}\subset\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}
of any finite type cluster algebra
\mathcal{A}_{Q}
arising from an arbitrarily oriented initial exchange quiver
Q
of type
A_{n}
(here
\xi
denotes a height function adapted to
Q
). It was later showed by Kashiwara-Kim-Oh-Park
[
23
,
24
,
25
]
that various subcategories of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}
were monoidal categorifications of interesting cluster structures, including the category
\mathcal{C}_{\xi}^{(1)}
in all Dynkin types and for arbitrary orientations.

The constructions of monoidal categorifications known so far can essentially be grouped into two groups. On the one hand, categories of modules over associative algebras such as (shifted) quantum affine algebras
[
17
,
4
,
23
]
or quiver Hecke (KLR) algebras
[
22
]
. On the other hand, categories of perverse sheaves on algebraic varieties naturally appearing in geometric representation theory such as Nakajima quiver varieties or affine Grassmannians
[
27
,
7
]
.
In both cases the categories involved are in particular abelian categories. In the present work, we propose an alternative framework relying on triangulated monoidal categories, namely, homotopy categories of certain (symmetric) monoidal categories. The main motivation for the construction proposed in the present work stems from our desire to provide a conceptual interpretation of our recent results with Jian-Rong Li
[
5
]
.

In our previous work
[
6
]
, given a simply-laced type Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and an orientation
Q
of its Dynkin diagram, we introduced an algebra homomorphism
{\widetilde{D}}_{Q}:\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbb{Z}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{t}^{reg}]
which is intimately related to the map
\overline{D}
defined in
[
2
]
by Baumann-Kamnitzer-Knutson in their study of the equivariant homology of Mirković-Vilonen cycles. Here
\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbb{Z}}
denotes the ring of Laurent polynomials containing the
q
-characters of all objects in the Hernandez-Leclerc’s
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}
. In our following work
[
5
]
, we used the map
{\widetilde{D}}_{Q}
to exhibit new families of non-trivial rational identities out of the representation theory of quantum affine algebras. More precisely, we showed that for any standard module
M
in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}
, we have

{\widetilde{D}}_{Q}\left(\chi_{q}(M)\right)=0.

The starting point of the present work consists in viewing these identities as the vanishing of certain alternate sums of dimension vectors (after getting rid of the denominators). In other words, to each object in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}
should correspond a chain complex satisfying some kind of exactness property, and whose objects should keep track (in a bijective way) of the Laurent monomials appearing in the
q
-character of
M
. This last condition shall be ensured by using the interpretation of
q
-characters as cluster expansions with respect to well-chosen cluster structures (which is one of the key upshots provided by Hernandez-Leclerc’s monoidal categorifications). Note that certain special families of exact chain complexes (higher Auslander-Reiten sequences) involving tensor products of modules over the path algebra
\mathbb{C}Q
have been considered in
[
28
]
. However, they do not seem to be appropriate for our needs.

The above discussion is our original motivation for introducing the framework of triangulated monoidal categorification of cluster algebras, whose main idea can be summarized as follows. Given a finite acyclic quiver
Q
, we begin by constructing a symmetric
\mathbf{k}
-linear monoidal category
\mathcal{H}_{Q}
out of the Auslander-Reiten theory of
Q
, whose objects and morphisms are defined in a way to behave similarly as tensor products (over
\mathbf{k}
) of modules over the path algebra
\mathbf{k}Q
. Then, considering the bounded homotopy category
\mathcal{K}
of a suitably chosen monoidal subcategory
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathcal{H}_{Q}
, one shall construct chain complexes in
\mathcal{K}
characterized by suitable exactness properties. Namely, starting from an initial collection of chain complexes concentrated in a single homological degree, these non-trivial chain complexes shall be constructed through some iterated mapping cone procedure (involving the monoidal structure of
\mathcal{H}_{Q}
), which crucially relies on the aforementioned exactness conditions. Each mapping cone involved in this process yields a distinguished triangle in
\mathcal{K}
that shall categorify some exchange relation associated with a cluster mutation in an appropriate cluster algebra
\mathcal{A}
. Consequently, the Euler characteristics of the constructed chain complexes shall coincide with certain cluster characters in
\mathcal{A}
.

We now briefly present the monoidal category
\mathcal{H}_{Q}
that will be playing the role of ambient category throughout this paper. Given a finite acyclic quiver
Q
, let us denote by
\mathbb{Z}Q
the repetition quiver of
Q
and by
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Rep}Q)
the bounded derived category of the category of finite dimensional representations of
Q
over the field of complex numbers. We first construct a certain
\mathbb{C}
-linear symmetric monoidal category
\mathcal{M}_{Q}
, whose indecomposable objects are given by pairs
(X,h)
where
X
is a multiset of indecomposable objects in
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Rep}Q)
and
h:(\mathbb{Z}Q)_{0}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
is a function that will usually be required to satisfy a certain property called
quasi-additivity
(cf. Definition
4.1
). Quasi-additive functions generalize the well-known combinatorics of additive functions on repetition quivers studied by Gabriel
[
13
]
, Brenner
[
3
]
and Ringel
[
29
]
. We define a certain operation on such pairs
(X,h)
, called
Serre tilting
, which consists in replacing finitely many elements of
X
by their image under the standard Serre functor of
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Rep}Q)
, as well as modifying
h
in a suitable way. Then, given two multisets
X=\{x_{k},k\in K\}
and
Y=\{y_{l},l\in L\}
and two quasi-additive functions
f
and
g
, the space of morphisms between
(X,f)
and
(Y,g)
is non-trivial only if
(Y,g)
is a Serre tilting of
(X,f)
, and if so, then it is given by

(1.1)

\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\mathrm{Bij}(K,L)\\
\sharp\mathrm{Supp}_{X,Y}(\sigma)<\infty\end{subarray}}\bigotimes_{k\in\mathrm{Supp}_{X,Y}(\sigma)}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Rep}Q)}(x_{k},y_{\sigma(k)})

where
\mathrm{Bij}(K,L)
denotes the set of bijections from
K
to
L
and
\mathrm{Supp}_{X,Y}(\sigma):=\{k\in K\mid y_{\sigma(k)}\neq x_{k}\}
for any
\sigma\in\mathrm{Bij}(K,L)
. The category
\mathcal{H}_{Q}
that will serve as underlying category for the framework proposed in this paper will be a certain monoidal full subcategory of
\mathcal{M}_{Q}
whose indecomposable objects will be given by appropriate Serre tiltings of certain distinguished objects
Y(x):=(H(x),h_{x})
,
x\in(\mathbb{Z}Q)_{0}
in
\mathcal{M}_{Q}
called
hammock objects
. For each
x\in(\mathbb{Z}Q)_{0}
(viewed as the isomorphism class of an indecomposable object in
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Rep}Q)
via Happel’s theorem), the multiset
H(x)
is obtained by considering the codomains of all non-trivial morphisms in
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Rep}Q)
having domain
x
, while the function
h_{x}
is essentially a hammock function (hence the terminology of hammock object) considered by Gabriel, Brenner and Ringel
[
13
,
3
,
29
]
. Objects in
\mathcal{H}_{Q}
that are isomorphic to tensor products of hammock objects will be called
dominant objects
.

In this paper, we will be mostly working on constructing triangulated monoidal categorifications of finite type cluster algebras
\mathcal{A}_{Q}
with arbitrarily oriented initial exchange quiver
Q
. For this purpose, we will be focusing on a certain additive monoidal (but not full) subcategory
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
of
\mathcal{H}_{Q}
that depends only on the choice of a section of
Q^{op}
inside
\mathbb{Z}Q
. We will then consider the monoidal triangulated category
\mathcal{K}_{Q}^{(1)}
defined as the bounded homotopy category of
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
. A key property that will eventually characterize the objects in
\mathcal{K}_{Q}^{(1)}
categorifying the cluster variables in
\mathcal{A}_{Q}
is the notion that we call
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
-exactness
. It can be viewed as a slightly weakened version of the notion of
n
-exactness of chain complexes appearing in higher homological algebra
[
20
,
21
,
14
]
.
Given a bounded chain complex
C_{\bullet}
, this exactness property consists in requiring the following conditions: firstly, we should have
C_{n}=0
for all
n<0
and
C_{0}
should be a dominant object in
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
; secondly (and most importantly), a certain class of morphisms whose composition with
d_{n-1}^{C_{\bullet}}
vanishes are required to factor through
d_{n}^{C_{\bullet}}
. This extra condition is essentially combinatorial and entirely depends on
C_{0}
. We also introduce a slightly stronger version (strong
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
-exactness) designed to ensure the uniqueness up to isomorphism of the considered chain complex. We refer to Definitions
6.1
and
6.3
for more details.
Of particular importance will be certain dominant objects
Y[\beta]
in
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
parametrized by elements of the positive orthant of the root lattice.
The first main result of this paper will be the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

For each positive (or negative simple) root
\beta
, there exists a unique (up to isomorphism) strongly
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
-exact complex
C_{\bullet}[\beta]
in
\mathcal{K}_{Q}^{(1)}
such that
C_{n}[\beta]=0
if
n<0
and
C_{0}[\beta]
is indecomposable and isomorphic to
Y[\beta]
.

A key ingredient in the proof of Theorem
1.1
consists in establishing the existence of certain morphisms having weak cokernels in
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
(we refer to Theorem
5.2
below for a precise statement). Let us also mention that evidences suggest
C_{\bullet}[\beta]
is indecomposable in
\mathcal{K}^{b}(\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)})
but we will not tackle this question in detail in this paper.

The second main result of this paper will establish the connection between the triangulated monoidal category
\mathcal{K}_{Q}^{(1)}
and the cluster algebra
\mathcal{A}_{Q}
. Namely, each cluster variable in
\mathcal{A}_{Q}
shall be categorified by a
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
-exact chain complex in
\mathcal{K}_{Q}^{(1)}
, in the sense that the cluster expansion of this cluster variable with respect to a well-chosen cluster will be identified with the Euler characteristic of this chain complex.
In order to make this statement more precise, we will denote by
\chi(C_{\bullet})
the Euler characteristic of a chain complex
C_{\bullet}=\cdots\rightarrow C_{0}\rightarrow C_{1}\rightarrow\cdots
in
\mathcal{K}_{Q}^{(1)}
, which is given by

\chi\left(C_{\bullet}\right):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{n}[C_{n}]\kern 5.0pt\in K_{0}(\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)})

where
K_{0}(\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)})
denotes the split Grothendieck group of
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
. On the other hand, denote by
L[\beta]
the real simple module in Hernandez-Leclerc’s category
\mathcal{C}_{\xi}^{(1)}
that categorifies the cluster variable
x[\beta]
in
\mathcal{A}_{Q}
, and denote by
{\widetilde{\chi}}_{q}(L[\beta])
its truncated
q
-character (cf.
[
17
]
). We then have the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume
Q
is a Dynkin quiver of type
A_{n}
with
n\geq 1
or
D_{n}
with
n\geq 4
.
Then there is an injective ring homomorphism

\iota_{Q}:\kern 5.0ptK_{0}(\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)})\longrightarrow\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbb{Z}}[F_{i},i\in I]

such that for each positive root
\beta
, we have

\iota_{Q}\left(\chi\left(C_{\bullet}[\beta]\right)\right)_{\mid F_{i}=-1}={\widetilde{\chi}}_{q}\left(L[\beta]\right).

While proving Theorem
1.2
, we obtain a uniform formula for the dominant monomial of
L[\beta]
, for arbitrary Dynkin quivers of type
A_{n}
or
D_{n}
, which we believe was not known so far. Note that we rely on the fact that all cluster monomials in
\mathcal{A}_{Q}
are classes of simple modules in
\mathcal{C}_{\xi}^{(1)}
, which follows from results by Kashiwara-Kim-Oh-Park
[
24
,
25
]
. Our formula agrees with Brito-Chari’s results
[
4
]
in type
A_{n}
.

It is worth outlining that although Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
deal with finite type cluster structures, the constructions proposed in this paper are designed to provide triangulated monoidal categorifications of other kinds of cluster structures, such as the ones appearing in representation theory
[
19
,
22
,
24
]
or alternatively by considering underlying quivers
Q
of tame or wild type. This may open perspectives in several directions, some of which we mention below.

Firstly, as shown by the statement of Theorem
1.2
, the
q
-characters of finite dimensional representations of affine quantum groups may be identified with the Euler characteristics of certain chain complexes whose objects live in a category entirely governed by the representation theory of the quiver
Q
. Exhibiting such intimate connections between Auslander-Reiten theory and the representation theory of quantum affine algebras may shed some light on certain combinatorial observations relating numerical invariants respectively appearing in these two frameworks (such as our results of
[
5
]
for example) and may yield new interpretations of the coefficients of certain
q
-characters.

Most importantly, we expect the triangulated framework proposed in this paper to be relevant for exhibiting monoidal categorifications of other cluster structures than those appearing in Lie theory. Indeed, starting from a certain family of chain complexes concentrated in one single homological degree, the iterated mapping cone procedure used to construct the chain complexes categorifying cluster variables only requires the existence of certain morphisms having weak cokernels and whose compositions should satisfy certain vanishing conditions. This is of course non trivial but can potentially hold in categories arising from various contexts.

Finally, several features from our construction share close similarities with notions appearing in the theory of additive categorifications of cluster algebras; for instance, the notion of Serre tilting (cf. Definition
4.5
below) that plays a central role in our construction. More conceptually, as indicated by Theorem
1.1
, the chain complexes categorifying cluster variables in our framework satisfy certain properties that are strikingly similar to those appearing in the contexts of
n
-abelian or
n
-angulated categories
[
21
,
14
]
.
Thus, our construction may allow to produce large families of new non-trivial examples of
n
-exact sequences (potentially including the case
n=0
recently developed in
[
15
]
) suggesting the existence of deep connections with higher homological algebra. In turn, this may provide new insights on the links between additive and monoidal categorifications of cluster algebras.

The paper is organized as follows. Sections
2
and
3
are respectively devoted to the necessary recollections on Auslander-Reiten theory and (abelian) monoidal categorifications of cluster algebras in the sense of Hernandez-Leclerc. Section
4
contains the key definitions concerning the ambient category
\mathcal{H}_{Q}
lying at the heart of the framework proposed in this paper. In Section
5
, we focus on the subcategory
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
which is appropriate to deal with finite type cluster algebras. Section
6
is devoted to the main homological definitions as well as the construction of the distinguished chain complexes
C_{\bullet}[\beta]
in the bounded homotopy category of
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
. Finally, in Section
7

C_{\bullet}[\beta]
to the (truncated)
q
-characters of simple modules in the Hernandez-Leclerc’s category
\mathcal{C}_{\xi}^{(1)}
.

2.
Auslander-Reiten theory

2.1.
Auslander-Reiten theoretic background

Let
Q
be a (finite) acyclic quiver without loops and multiple edges. Let us denote by
I:=Q_{0}
its set of vertices. We denote by
\mathbb{Z}Q
the repetition quiver of
Q
, whose set of vertices is
(\mathbb{Z}Q)_{0}:=\{(i,p),p\in i+2\mathbb{Z}\}
and whose set of arrows is given by

\forall\alpha\in Q_{1},\quad(t(\alpha),p)\rightarrow(s(\alpha),p+1)\kern 5.0pt\text{and}\kern 5.0pt(s(\alpha),p)\rightarrow(t(\alpha),p+1).

Here
s(\alpha)
and
t(\alpha)
respectively stand for the source and target of an arrow
\alpha\in Q_{1}
. By definition, a section of
Q
in
\mathbb{Z}Q
refers to any subset of
\mathbb{Z}Q
of the form
\{(i,p_{i}),i\in I\}
such that
p_{s(\alpha)}-p_{t(\alpha)}=1
for every arrow
\alpha
in
Q
. In particular, given any vertex
x\in(\mathbb{Z}Q)_{0}
, there is a unique section of
Q
in
\mathbb{Z}Q
containing
x
. We shall denote it by
Q_{x}
.
We will denote by
\tau
the Auslander-Reiten translate on
\mathbb{Z}Q
which is given by
\tau(i,p):=(i,p-2)
.
In all what follows we will be working over an algebraically closed field
\mathbf{k}
of characteristic zero. We denote by
\mathrm{Rep}Q
the category of finite dimensional representations of
Q
over
\mathbf{k}
(equivalently, finite-dimensional modules over the path algebra
\mathbf{k}Q
of
Q
) and by
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Rep}Q)
the bounded derived category of
\mathrm{Rep}Q
. It follows from Happel’s theorem (Proposition 4.6 in
[
16
]
) that there is an injective set-theoretic map
j_{Q}
from
(\mathbb{Z}Q)_{0}
to the set of isomorphism classes of indecomposable objects in
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Rep}Q)
. This injection is moreover a bijection in the case where
Q
is a Dynkin quiver, i.e. an orientation of the Dynkin diagram of a simple complex Lie algebra of type
A_{n},n\geq 1,D_{n},n\geq 4
or
E_{n},n=6,7,8
. We will denote

M_{i,p}:=j_{Q}(i,p)

for each
(i,p)\in(\mathbb{Z}Q)_{0}
and will simply identify a vertex
x
of
\mathbb{Z}Q
with its image under
j_{Q}
when the explicit data of
i
and
p
are not needed.
In particular, we shall denote by
\Sigma x
the element of
(\mathbb{Z}Q)_{0}
given by
j_{Q}^{-1}(\Sigma j_{Q}(x))
, where
\Sigma
denotes the standard shift in
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Rep}Q)
. Denoting by
\tau
the Auslander-Reiten translate functor in
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Rep}Q)

S:=\Sigma\tau=\tau\Sigma.

It follows from the Auslander-Reiten formula that

\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Rep}Q)

(2.1)

\forall M,N\kern 5.0pt\mathrm{Hom}(M,N)\simeq D\mathrm{Hom}(N,SM)

Recall the notion of an irreducible morphism as introduced by Auslander and Reiten in

2.2.
Additive functions on repetition quivers

Given a function
f:(\mathbb{Z}Q)_{0}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}
, we set

\widetilde{f}:x\in(\mathbb{Z}Q)_{0}\longmapsto f(x)+f(\tau x)-\sum_{y\rightarrow x}f(y).

Following Gabriel
[
13
]
, a function
f
is said to be

if
\widetilde{f}=0
. It is a known fact (cf.
[
13
]
) that an additive function is uniquely determined by its values on any given section of
Q
in
\mathbb{Z}Q
. In other words, given
x\in(\mathbb{Z}Q)_{0}
and a
n
-tuple of integers
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
, there is a unique additive function
f
satisfying
f(x_{j})=a_{j}
for every
1\leq j\leq n
, where
x_{j},j\in I
denote the vertices of the section
Q_{x}
. Of particular interest are the

studied by Gabriel
[
13
]
and Brenner
[
3
]
(later generalized by Ringel
[
29
]
) defined as the unique additive function satisfying
f(y)=\dim\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Rep}Q)}(x,y)
for each
y\in Q_{x}
. Equivalently, it is the additive function corresponding to the tuple
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
defined by
a_{j}=1
if there is an oriented path from
j
to
i
in
Q
,
a_{j}=0
otherwise (here
i
denotes the vertex of
Q
such that
x=(i,p),p\in i+2\mathbb{Z}
).

3.
Monoidal categorifications

3.1.
Finite type cluster algebras associated with Dynkin quivers

Given a Dynkin quiver
Q
, we will be denoting by
\mathcal{A}_{Q}
the cluster algebra with initial seed

\left((x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}),\overline{Q}\right)

where the initial exchange quiver
\overline{Q}
is given as follows. The set of vertices of
\overline{Q}
is
I\times\{0,1\}
(where
I
denotes the set of vertices of
Q
) where
I\times\{0\}
is the set of mutable vertices while
I\times\{1\}
is the set of frozen vertices. The set of arrows consists in arrows
(i,1)\leftarrow(i,0),i\in I
(the horizontal arrows), as well as
(i,0)\leftarrow(j,0),(i,1)\leftarrow(j,1)
and
(i,1)\rightarrow(j,0)
if there is an arrow
i\rightarrow j
in
Q
(the latter arrows are sometimes called ordinary arrows).

It follows from Fomin-Zelevinsky’s results
[
10
]
that
\mathcal{A}_{Q}
is a finite type cluster algebra, i.e.
\mathcal{A}_{Q}
contains finitely many clusters (equivalently finitely many cluster variables). Consequently, the non-frozen cluster variables in
\mathcal{A}_{Q}
are in bijection with the set of almost positive roots which is defined as
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{+}\cup\Pi_{-}
. We will denote by
x[\beta]
the cluster variable corresponding to
\beta
under this bijection, for each almost positive root
\beta
. In particular, the cluster variables
x[-\alpha_{i}],i\in I
respectively coincide with the cluster variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
belonging to the initial cluster previously described.

3.2.
Representations of affine quantum groups and their
q
-characters

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a simply-laced type simple complex Lie algebra and let
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
denote the associated quantum affine algebra. The category
\mathcal{C}
of finite-dimensional representations of
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
is a monoidal category. It was proved by Chari-Pressley
[
8
]
that the irreducible representations in
\mathcal{C}
are classified (up to isomorphism) by a monoid
\mathcal{P}^{+}
whose elements are called dominant monomials and which is defined as the free monoid generated by indeterminates
Y_{i,a},i\in I,a\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
. Here
I
denotes an indexing set of the simple roots of
\mathfrak{g}
. We will denote by
L(\mathfrak{m})
the simple module corresponding to
\mathfrak{m}
under this classification. Although
\mathcal{C}
is not symmetric, its Grothendieck ring is commutative. Frenkel-Reshetikhin
[
11
]
introduced an injective ring homomorphism

\chi_{q}:\kern 5.0ptK_{0}(\mathcal{C})\longrightarrow\mathcal{Y}:=\mathbb{Z}[Y_{i,a}^{\pm},i\in I,a\in\mathbb{C}^{*}]

called the
q
-character morphism. The set of Laurent monomials in
\mathcal{Y}
is endowed with a partial order called the Nakajima order which is given by

\mathfrak{m}\leq_{N}\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}\kern 5.0pt\Leftrightarrow\kern 5.0pt\text{$\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}/\mathfrak{m}$ is a product of $A_{i,a},i\in I,a\in\mathbb{C}^{*}$}

where for each
i\in I
and
a\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
, the Laurent monomial
A_{i,a}
is defined as

(3.1)

A_{i,a}:=Y_{i,a-1}Y_{i,a+1}\prod_{j\sim i}Y_{i,a}^{-1}.

For every dominant monomial
\mathfrak{m}\in\mathcal{P}^{+}
, we have that
\mathfrak{m}
is the unique greatest element with respect to
\leq_{N}
in the set of all Laurent monomials appearing in
\chi_{q}(L(\mathfrak{m}))
. In other words, the renormalized
q
-character of
M
defined as
\mathfrak{m}^{-1}\chi_{q}(M)
is a polynomial in the
A_{i,a}^{-1},i\in I,a\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
with constant term
1
.

3.3.
Hernandez-Leclerc categories

Motivated by exhibiting deep connections between the representation theory of quantum affine algebras and the combinatorics of cluster algebras introduced by Fomin and Zelevinsky
[
9
]
, Hernandez-Leclerc defined several monoidal full subcategories of
\mathcal{C}
, which we now briefly recall.
Let
\widehat{I}_{\mathbb{Z}}
denote the set of pairs

\widehat{I}_{\mathbb{Z}}:=\{(i,p),i\in I,p\in i+2\mathbb{Z}\}

and let
\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbb{Z}}
denote the ring

\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbb{Z}}:=\mathbb{Z}[Y_{i,p}^{\pm 1},(i,p)\in\widehat{I}_{\mathbb{Z}}].

Following
[
18
]
, we denote by
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}
the smallest monoidal full subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
containing the fundamental representations
L(Y_{i,q^{p}})
for
p\in i+2\mathbb{Z}
. Following the common use, we will be wirting
Y_{i,p}
as a shorthand notation for
Y_{i,q^{p}}
in all what follows. The simple objects of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}
are the modules
L(\mathfrak{m})
where
\mathfrak{m}
is a monomial in the
Y_{i,p}
. Frenkel-Reshetikhin’s
q
-character morphism restricts to an injective ring homomorphism

\chi_{q}:\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbb{Z}}.

Using standard notation, we set

A_{i,p+1}:=Y_{i,p}Y_{i,p+2}\prod_{j\sim i}Y_{j,p+1}^{-1}

for each
(i,p)\in\widehat{I}_{\mathbb{Z}}
.
We fix an orientation
Q
of the Dynkin diagram of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\xi
a height function adapted to
Q
. Then we set, for every positive integer
l\geq 1
,

\widehat{I}_{\leq\xi}^{(l)}:=\{(i,p)\in\widehat{I}_{\mathbb{Z}},\kern 5.0pt\xi(i)-2l\leq p\leq\xi(i)\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\widehat{I}_{\leq\xi}:=\{(i,p)\in\widehat{I}_{\mathbb{Z}},p\leq\xi(i)\}

and we denote by
\mathcal{C}_{\xi}^{(l)}
(resp.
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\leq\xi}
) the smallest full monoidal subcategory of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}
containing all the fundamental representations
L(Y_{i,p})
such that
(i,p)\in\widehat{I}_{\leq\xi}^{(l)}
(resp.
(i,p)\in\widehat{I}_{\leq\xi}
).
Finally, following
[
17
,
19
]
, we define for each
M\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\leq\xi}
the truncated
q
-character
{\widetilde{\chi}}_{q}(M)
of
M
as the element of
\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbb{Z}}
obtained from
\chi_{q}(M)
by dropping all the terms involving variables
Y_{i,p}
with
p>\xi(i)
. This gives another injective ring homomorphism

{\widetilde{\chi}}_{q}:\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\leq\xi}\longrightarrow\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\leq\xi}:=\mathbb{Z}[Y_{i,p}^{\pm 1},(i,p)\in\widehat{I}_{\leq\xi}]

whose relevance is enlightened by the statement of Theorem
3.1
below.

3.4.
Abelian monoidal categorifications

We now provide brief reminders about the general notion of abelian monoidal categorification of a cluster algebra, as well as recollections of the main results involving Hernandez-Leclerc categories. Note that although the usual terminology is simply monoidal categorification, we will refer to this framework as abelian monoidal categorification, in contrast with the construction presented in this paper which deals with triangulated monoidal categories.

The notion of abelian monoidal categorification of a cluster algebra was introduced by Hernandez-Leclerc
[
17
]
. Given a cluster algebra
\mathcal{A}
, an abelian monoidal categorification of
\mathcal{A}
consists in an abelian monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
together with a ring isomorphism
\mathcal{A}\simeq K_{0}(\mathcal{C})
sending cluster monomials in
\mathcal{A}
to isomorphism classes of simple objects in
\mathcal{C}
(these objects are then necessarily real, i.e. their tensor square is simple). The simplicity of tensor products of objects categorifying cluster variables or monomials is therefore deeply related to the cluster structure of
\mathcal{A}
. For instance, the simple objects categorifying frozen cluster variables (if there are any) in
\mathcal{A}
satisfy the property that their tensor product with any object categorifying a cluster monomial is simple.

The case of the category
\mathcal{C}_{\xi}^{(1)}
will be of particular interest to us in this paper, in view of the following result, which was established by Hernandez-Leclerc
[
17
]
in the case where
Q
is bipartite of type
A_{n}
or
D_{4}
, and generalized by Brito-Chari
[
4
]
to all orientations in type
A_{n}
. It follows from the results by Kashiwara-Kim-Oh-Park in the general case.

Theorem 3.1

(Hernandez-Leclerc
[
17
]
, Brito-Chari
[
4
]
, Kashiwara-Kim-Oh-Park
[
25
]
)
.

Let
Q
be any Dynkin quiver and
\xi
be a height function adapted to
Q
. Then the category
\mathcal{C}_{\xi}^{(1)}
provides a monoidal categorification of the finite type cluster algebra
\mathcal{A}_{Q}
. Moreover, for each almost positive root
\beta
, the truncated
q
-character of the corresponding real simple module
L[\beta]
in
\mathcal{C}_{\xi}^{(1)}
coincides (up to a torus isomorphism) with the cluster expansion of
x[\beta]
with respect to the initial cluster of
\mathcal{A}_{Q}
.

Remark 3.2
.

It was later proved by Hernandez-Leclerc that the Grothendieck ring of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{(l)}
(resp.
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\leq\xi}
) carries a cluster algebra structure with an initial cluster given by the simple modules (which are Kirillov-Reshetikhin modules) given by
L(Y_{i,\xi(i)}\cdots Y_{i,\xi(i)-2k})
with
0\leq k\leq l
(resp.
k\geq 0
). Kashiwara-Kim-Oh-Park
[
24
,
25
]
then proved that the categories
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\leq\xi}
and
\mathcal{C}_{\xi}^{(l)}
actually provide monoidal categorifications of these cluster structures.

Remark 3.3
.

In the case of the category
\mathcal{C}_{\xi}^{(1)}
, the initial cluster of
\mathcal{A}_{Q}
is categorified by the following real simple modules: with the notations of Section
3.1
, we have that for every
i\in I
the (unfrozen) initial variable
x_{i}
is categorified by the fundamental representation
L(Y_{i,\xi(i)})
while the frozen variable
X_{i}
is categorified by the Kirillov-Reshetikhin modules
L(Y_{i,\xi(i)-2}Y_{i,\xi(i)})
. Moreover in that case every simple module in
\mathcal{C}_{\xi}^{(1)}
categorifies a cluster monomial (and hence is real). In particular we have that
L(Y_{i,\xi(i)-2}Y_{i,\xi(i)})\otimes M
is simple for any simple module
M
in
\mathcal{C}_{\xi}^{(1)}
.

It will be crucial to us later in this paper to show the existence of certain short exact sequences in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}

out of the data of the poles of the

Extending the terminology from Section

Definition 4.1
.

A function
f:(\mathbb{Z}Q)_{0}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}
will be called quasi-additive if
\widetilde{f}
has finite support, i.e.
\widetilde{f}(x)=0
on all but finitely many vertices of
\mathbb{Z}Q

h_{x}

(1)

on the section

of
\mathbb{Z}Q
,

(2)

\widetilde{h_{x}}=\delta_{x}
,

where
\delta_{x}
denotes the function given by
\delta_{x}(y):=1
if

and
0
otherwise. For a given

, the function

should be viewed as a semi-infinite version of the hammock functions from Section

Lemma 4.3
.

The functions
h_{x},x\in(\mathbb{Z}Q)_{0}
are linearly independent.

Proof.

Assume

and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}\in(\mathbb{Z}Q)_{0}
are such that
a_{1}h_{x_{1}}+\cdots+a_{r}h_{x_{r}}=0
. We can choose
i
such that
x_{i}
is to the left of the other

. Wlog

. Hence we have
h_{x_{k}}(x_{1})=0
for all

and
h_{x_{1}}(x_{1})=1
by definition of the functions
h_{x}
. This implies
a_{1}=0
. The lemma thus follows by a straightforward induction.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

For every
x\in(\mathbb{Z}Q)_{0}
we have that

h_{x}+h_{\tau^{-1}x}=\delta_{x}+\sum_{x\rightarrow y}h_{y}.

Proof.

Let us fix
x\in(\mathbb{Z}Q)_{0}
. It is then straightforward to check that

\widetilde{\delta_{x}}=\delta_{x}+\delta_{\tau^{-1}x}-\sum_{x\rightarrow y}\delta_{y}.

It follows from this that the function

(4.1)

h_{x}+h_{\tau^{-1}x}-\sum_{x\rightarrow y}h_{y}-\delta_{x}

is additive and hence is entirely determined by its values on any section of
\mathbb{Z}Q
. Let us consider the section starting at
x
. For any
z
on that section, we have that
\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\mathrm{Hom}(x,z)=1
so that
h_{x}(z)=1
and
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(z,x)=0
which implies that
h_{\tau^{-1}x}(z)=0
using the Auslander-Reiten formula. On the other hand,
z
belongs to exactly one of the sections starting at each
y
such that there is an arrow
x\rightarrow y
in
\mathbb{Z}Q
, unless
z
is
x
itself. In other words we have that
\sum_{x\rightarrow y}h_{y}(z)=1
if
z\neq x
, and
0
if
z=x
. Putting all of this together we see that the function (
4.1
) vanishes on all the vertices of the section starting at
x
. The lemma is proved.
∎

4.2.
Two partial orders on quasi-additive functions

The set of all quasi-additive functions is endowed with an obvious partial order, namely

f\leq g\kern 5.0pt\Leftrightarrow\kern 5.0pt\forall x\in(\mathbb{Z}Q)_{0},f(x)\leq g(x).

However in what follows we will need a more refined partial order. We introduce the following covering relation:

f\lessdot g\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\exists x\in(\mathbb{Z}Q)_{0},\kern 5.0ptg-f=\delta_{x}\kern 5.0pt\text{and}\kern 5.0pt\widetilde{g}(x)>0.

We then define a partial order
\preccurlyeq
on the set of all quasi-additive functions on
(\mathbb{Z}Q)_{0}
as the transitive closure of
\lessdot
. In particular we have that
f\preccurlyeq g\Rightarrow f\leq g
.

4.3.
The category
\mathcal{M}_{Q}

We denote by
\mathcal{I}_{Q}
the set of all isomorphism classes of indecomposable objects in
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Rep}Q)
. Recall that a multiset of elements of
\mathcal{I}_{Q}
is a collection
\{x_{k},k\in K\}
where
K
is a set and

is an indecomposable object in
\mathcal{D}_{Q}
for each

(equivalently, it can be viewed as a function from

\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Rep}Q)
may appear several times (up to isomorphism) in the same collection (but only finitely many times !). In this work, we will exclusively be considering at most countable multisets, i.e. functions from a finite or countable set
K
to
\mathcal{I}_{Q}
.
Given two multisets
X
and
Y
we can define their intersection
X\cap Y
as the largest multiset contained in both
X
and
Y
.

We define a

-category

and

is a quasi-additive function on
(\mathbb{Z}Q)_{0}

\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Rep}Q)

.

Given any indecomposable object

in

\sigma:K\rightarrow L
, we set

\mathrm{Supp}_{X,Y}(\sigma):=\{k\in K\mid y_{\sigma(k)}\neq x_{k}\}.

Given two multisets
X=\{x_{k},k\in K\}
and
Y=\{y_{l},l\in L\}
and two quasi-additive functions
f
and
g
on
(\mathbb{Z}Q)_{0}
, we then define the morphism space from
(X,f)
to
(Y,g)
in
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}_{Q}}
as follows:

(4.2)

\begin{split}\mathrm{Hom}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}_{Q}}}\left((X,f),(Y,g)\right):=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\mathrm{Bij}(K,L)\\
\sharp\mathrm{Supp}_{X,Y}(\sigma)<\infty\end{subarray}}&\bigotimes_{k\in\mathrm{Supp}_{X,Y}(\sigma)}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}_{Q}}(x_{k},y_{\sigma(k)})\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad&\text{if $(Y,g)=\mu_{Z}(X,f),\kern 5.0ptZ\subseteq Y$}\\
{}\\
\mathrm{Hom}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}_{Q}}}\left((X,f),(Y,g)\right)&:=\kern 5.0pt0\qquad\text{otherwise.}\end{split}

Note that each summand on the right hand side is a tensor product of finitely many
\mathbf{k}
-vector spaces (as
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Rep}Q)
is a
\mathbf{k}
-category) so that
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}_{Q}}
is a
\mathbf{k}
-category.

Remark 4.6
.

In most of what follows we will be considering only the case where
X
and
Y
are finite with same cardinality so that

\mathrm{Hom}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}_{Q}}}\left((X,f),(Y,g)\right):=\bigoplus_{\sigma\in\mathrm{Bij}(K,L)}\bigotimes_{k\in K}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}_{Q}}(x_{k},y_{\sigma(k)})

if
(Y,g)
is a Serre tilting of
(X,f)
.

Using standard procedures, we can then make it into an additive (and hence
\mathbf{k}
-linear) category
\mathcal{M}_{Q}
. We claim that the objects of
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}_{Q}}
will be indecomposable in
\mathcal{M}_{Q}
. Let us begin with an elementary lemma.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
(X,f)
and
(Y,g)
be two objects in
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}_{Q}}
. Then we have that
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{M}_{Q}}((X,f),(Y,g))
and
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{M}_{Q}}((Y,g),(X,f))
are both non zero if and only if
(X,f)\simeq(Y,g)
in
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}_{Q}}
.

Proof.

We will write the proof in the case where
X
and
Y
are finite. Assume

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{M}_{Q}}((X,f),(Y,g))\neq 0\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{M}_{Q}}((Y,g),(X,f))\neq 0.

Firstly, this clearly implies
f=g
. It moreover implies that
X
and
Y
have same cardinalities so we may write
X:=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\}
and
Y:=\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}\}
, with
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
. We can choose
j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
such that for all
i
is such that
x_{i}
is not isomorphic to
x_{j}
and all indecomposable objects
x
in
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Rep}Q)
such that
\mathrm{Hom}(x,x_{i})\neq 0
we have
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Rep}Q)}(x_{j},x)=0
. Without loss of generality we assume
j=1
. As
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{M}_{Q}}(X,Y)\neq 0
there is
k\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
such that
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Rep}Q)}(x_{1},y_{k})\neq 0
. As
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{M}_{Q}}(Y,X)\neq 0
there is
l\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
such that
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Rep}Q)}(y_{k},x_{l})\neq 0
. Thus we must have that
x_{l}\simeq x_{1}
in
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Rep}Q)
and hence
y_{k}\simeq x_{1}
as well. Moreover we get the existence of nonzero morphisms respectively from
X\setminus\{x_{1}\}
to
Y\setminus\{y_{k}\}
and from
Y\setminus\{y_{k}\}
to
X\setminus\{x_{l}\}
. But the latter is isomorphic to
X\setminus\{x_{1}\}
as
x_{1}\simeq x_{l}
in
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Rep}Q)
and hence we get that both
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{M}_{Q}}(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime})
and
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{M}_{Q}}(Y^{\prime},X^{\prime})
are non zero, where
X^{\prime}:=X\setminus\{x_{1}\}
and
Y^{\prime}:=Y\setminus\{y_{k}\}
. We can then repeat the same argument inductively to conclude that
X\simeq Y
as multisets and thus
(X,f)\simeq(Y,g)
in
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}_{Q}}
.
∎

The claim follows as explained below:

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
(X,f)
be any object in
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}_{Q}}
. Then
(X,f)
is indecomposable in
\mathcal{M}_{Q}
.

Proof.

Assume
(X,f)
can be decomposed as
(X,f)\simeq(Y,g)\oplus(Z,h)
in
\mathcal{M}_{Q}
with
(Y,g)
and
(Z,h)
non-zero objects in
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}_{Q}}
. Then in particular we have that
\mathrm{Hom}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}_{Q}}}((X,f),(Y,g))
and
\mathrm{Hom}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}_{Q}}}((Y,g),(X,f))
are both non zero (and similarly for
(Z,h)
) which implies by Lemma
4.7
that
(Y,g)\simeq(X,f)
in
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}_{Q}}
(and hence in
\mathcal{M}_{Q}
), and for the same reason
(X,f)\simeq(Z,h)
in
\mathcal{M}_{Q}
. Thus we obtain that
(X,f)\simeq(X,f)\oplus(X,f)
in
\mathcal{M}_{Q}
. In the case where
Q
is a Dynkin quiver, it follows from the construction of
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}_{Q}}
that
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{M}_{Q}}((X,f),(X,f))
is a finite-dimensional
\mathbb{C}
-vector space. The fact that
\mathbb{C}
has characteristic zero gives the contradiction, which shows that
(X,f)
is indecomposable in
\mathcal{M}_{Q}
.
∎

Given two indecomposable objects
(X,f)
and
(Y,g)
in
\mathcal{M}_{Q}
with
X=\{x_{k},k\in K\}
and
Y=\{y_{l},l\in L\}
, we set

(4.3)

(X,f)\otimes(Y,g):=\left(X\sqcup Y,f+g\right).

where
X\sqcup Y
is the multiset defined by

X\sqcup Y:=\{z_{p},p\in K\sqcup L\},\kern 5.0ptz_{p}:=\begin{cases}x_{p}&\text{if $p\in K$,}\\
y_{p}&\text{if $p\in L$. }\end{cases}

Moreover, given two non-trivial morphisms
\varphi_{1}:(X_{1},f_{1})\rightarrow(Y_{1},g_{1})
and
\varphi_{2}:(X_{2},f_{2})\rightarrow(Y_{2},g_{2})
in
\mathcal{M}_{Q}
, we get a canonical non-trivial morphism
\varphi_{1}\otimes\varphi_{2}:(X_{1},f_{1})\otimes(X_{2},f_{2})\rightarrow(Y_{1},g_{1})\otimes(Y_{2},g_{2})
in
\mathcal{M}_{Q}
as
(Y_{1}\sqcup Y_{2},g_{1}+g_{2})=\mu_{Z_{1}\sqcup Z_{2}}(X_{1}\sqcup X_{2},f_{1}+f_{2})
where
Z_{1}
and
Z_{2}
are such that
(Y_{i},g_{i})=\mu_{Z_{i}}(X_{i},f_{i})
for each
i\in\{1,2\}
. It is straightforward to check that this construction is natural and hence (
4.3
) endows
\mathcal{M}_{Q}
with a symmetric monoidal structure.

4.4.
The category
\mathcal{H}_{Q}

We now introduce the category
\mathcal{H}_{Q}
which will serve as underlying category for all the constructions presented in the next sections. We begin by defining certain distinguished objects (that we will refer to as hammock objects) in
\mathcal{M}_{Q}
that will be playing a crucial role in what follows. For every
x
in
\mathcal{I}_{Q}
, we define the
hammock multiset
associated to
x
as the multiset
H(x)
of elements of
\mathcal{I}_{Q}
given by

(4.4)

H(x):=\bigsqcup_{y\in\mathcal{I}_{Q}}\bigsqcup_{m=1}^{\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}_{Q}}(x,y)}\{y\}.

In other words,
H(x)
is the multiset containing
m_{x}(y)
copies of each
y\in\mathcal{I}_{Q}
, where
m_{x}(y):=\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}_{Q}}(x,y)
for each
y\in\mathcal{I}_{Q}
. Moreover, for every
x\in\mathcal{I}_{Q}
, we define the
hammock object
associated to
x
as the indecomposable object
Y(x)
of
\mathcal{M}_{Q}
given by

(4.5)

Y(x):=\left(H(x),h_{x}\right).

We will also need the following (indecomposable) objects in
\mathcal{M}_{Q}
:

(4.6)

\forall x\in\mathcal{I}_{Q},\kern 5.0ptF(x):=\left(\{Sx,\Sigma x\},0\right).

We then consider the following collections of indecomposable objects in
\mathcal{M}_{Q}
:

(4.7)

\forall x\in\mathcal{I}_{Q},\kern 5.0pt\mathcal{B}(x):=\{(X,h)\mid h\preccurlyeq h_{x}\kern 5.0pt\text{and}\kern 5.0pt\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{M}_{Q}}\left(Y(x),(X,h)\right)\neq 0\}.

We then define the category
\mathcal{H}_{Q}
as the smallest additive monoidal full subcategory of
\mathcal{M}_{Q}
containing
F(x)
as well as all the objects belonging to
\mathcal{B}(x)
, for all
x\in\mathcal{I}_{Q}
.
The pairs of the form
(X_{1},f_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes(X_{r},f_{r})
where for each
k
,
(X_{k},f_{k})
is of the form
Y(x_{k})
for some
x_{k}\in(\mathbb{Z}Q)_{0}
will be called
dominant objects
. The set of all dominant objects will be denoted by
\mathrm{Ob}_{Q}^{+}
.

Remark 4.9
.

It follows from Lemma
4.3
that a dominant object is entirely determined (up to isomorphism) by its quasi-additive function.

The next statement shows how the Serre tilting of a hammock object is related to other hammock objects.

Lemma 4.10
.

For each
x\in\mathcal{I}_{Q}
we have that

\mu_{x}Y(x)\otimes Y(\tau^{-1}x)\simeq F(x)\otimes\bigotimes_{x\rightarrow y}Y(y).

Proof.

In view of Lemma
4.4
, it suffices to show the following identity of multisets

H(x)\sqcup H(\tau^{-1}x)=\{x,\Sigma x\}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{x\rightarrow y}H(y).

This follows from the fact that
x\rightarrow\bigoplus_{x\rightarrow y}y\rightarrow\tau^{-1}x\rightarrow\Sigma x
is an Auslander-Reiten triangle in
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Rep}Q)
and that
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Rep}Q)
has weak kernels.
∎

The following observation is immediate from the definition of
\mathcal{M}_{Q}
:

Lemma 4.11
.

For every
x\in\mathcal{I}_{Q}
we have that
\mu_{x}Y(x)
belongs to
\mathcal{H}_{Q}
.

5.
The subcategory
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}

In this section, we introduce a certain monoidal subcategory (which will not be a full subcategory) of
\mathcal{H}_{Q}
, denoted
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
. It will serve as underlying category for all the constructions and results in the following sections. In particular, its bounded homotopy category will eventually be the triangulated monoidal category relevant for categorifying the cluster structure associated to
Q
, when
Q
is a Dynkin quiver.

5.1.
The subcategory
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}

We also fix a height function
\xi:I\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
adapted to
Q
, i.e.
\xi(j)=\xi(i)-1
for every arrow
i\rightarrow j
in
Q
. We then denote by
x_{i}
the vertex of
\mathbb{Z}Q
given by
x_{i}:=(i,\xi(i))
for every
i\in I
.
In order to define the morphisms in the subcategory
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
, we will need the following observation.

Lemma 5.1
.

There exists an indecomposable object
x
in
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Rep}Q)
such that

\forall i\in I,\kern 5.0pt\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Rep}Q)}(\tau x_{i},x)\neq 0.

Proof.

let us consider an abelian subcategory
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Rep}Q)
equivalent to
\mathrm{Rep}Q
and whose indecomposable projective objects are the
\tau x_{i},i\in I
. We then have that for any indecomposable objects
x
and
y
in
\mathcal{C}
,

\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(x,y)=\langle\boldsymbol{\dim}x,\boldsymbol{\dim}y\rangle_{Q}

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{Q}
denotes the Euler-Ringel form of
Q
. Consider
x
an indecomposable object in
\mathcal{C}
whose dimension vector is given by
\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_{i}
. Such an object always exists if
Q
is of Dynkin or tame type. We then have that

\displaystyle\langle\boldsymbol{\dim}\tau x_{i},\boldsymbol{\dim}x\rangle_{Q}

\displaystyle=\langle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in I\\
i\rightsquigarrow j\end{subarray}}\alpha_{i},\sum_{k\in I}\alpha_{k}\rangle_{Q}=1+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\rightsquigarrow j\\
k\rightsquigarrow i,k\neq i\end{subarray}}\langle\alpha_{j},\alpha_{k}\rangle_{Q}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{k\rightarrow i}\langle\alpha_{i},\alpha_{k}\rangle_{Q}\geq 1.

This shows that
\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Rep}Q)}(\tau x_{i},x)\neq 0
for all
i\in I
and hence
x\in\bigcap_{i\in I}H(\tau x_{i})
which establishes the lemma.
∎

From now we fix an indecomposable object
x
in
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Rep}Q)
as given by Lemma
5.1
. By Lemma
5.1
, we can choose and fix once for all a non trivial morphism
f_{i}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Rep}Q)}(\tau x_{i},x)
for each
i\in I
. We denote by
\overline{f_{i}}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Rep}Q)}(x,S\tau x_{i})
the morphism given by the isomorphism (
2.1
).

We now consider the following collections of indecomposable objects in
\mathcal{H}_{Q}
:

\forall i\in I,\kern 5.0pt\mathcal{B}_{i}:=\mathcal{B}(\tau x_{i})\cap\left\{\mu_{Z}Y(\tau x_{i})\mid Z\subseteq\{\tau x_{i},i\in I\}\right\}.

The category
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
is then defined as the following subcategory of
\mathcal{H}_{Q}
. The class of objects of
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
consists in the smallest family of objects of
\mathcal{H}_{Q}
stable under both the additive structure and the monoidal structure of
\mathcal{H}_{Q}
, and containing
F(\tau x_{i})
and
Y(x_{i})
as well as all the objects belonging to
\mathcal{B}_{i}
, for every
i\in I
. Given two indecomposable objects
(X,f)
and
(Y,g)
in
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
, the set of morphisms from
(X,f)
to
(Y,g)
in
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
is given as follows. By construction of
\mathcal{H}_{Q}
, we can write
(Y,g)=\mu_{Z}(X,f)
for some finite subset
Z\subseteq X
. Then
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}}((X,f),(Y,g))
is defined as the subvector space of
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{H}_{Q}}((X,f),(Y,g))
generated by the morphisms of the form
f_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{r}
with
f_{k}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Rep}Q)}(x_{k},y_{\sigma(k)})
such that
y_{\sigma(k)}\in\{x\}\cup\{S\tau x_{i},i\in I\}
for each
k
such that
x_{k}\in Z
.
We then denote by
\mathcal{K}_{Q}^{(1)}
the bounded homotopy category of
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
, which is a triangulated monoidal category. In all what follows we will simply write
\mu_{i}X
instead of
\mu_{\tau x_{i}}X
for any (indecomposable) object
X
in
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
and any
i\in I
.

5.2.
Weak cokernels in
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
5.2
below, who states the existence in
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
of a distinguished family of morphisms in that will play a crucial role in what follows. Before stating the theorem, let us introduce a piece of notation: for any indecomposable object
(X,h)
in
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
, we set

\Sigma(X,h):=\{i\in I\mid\tau x_{i}\in X\kern 5.0pt\text{and}\kern 5.0pt\widetilde{h}(\tau x_{i})>0\}.

Theorem 5.2
.

There exists a family of irreducible morphisms

\{\eta_{i}^{(X,h)}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}}\left((X,h),\mu_{i}(X,h)\right),\kern 5.0pt\text{$(X,h)$ indecomposable in $\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}$},i\in\Sigma(X,h)\}

satisfying the following properties:

(1)

For every
i\in\Sigma(X,h)
and every
j\in I
such that
j\rightsquigarrow i
in
Q
, we have that

j\in\Sigma(\mu_{i}(X,h))\qquad\text{and}\qquad\eta_{j}^{\mu_{i}(X,h)}\circ\eta_{i}^{(X,h)}=0.

(2)

For any object
T
in
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
, and any morphism
f\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}}(\mu_{i}(X,h),T)
, we have that

f\circ\eta_{i}^{(X,h)}=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad\text{$f$ factors through $\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\rightsquigarrow i\\
j\in\Sigma(\mu_{i}(X,h))\end{subarray}}\eta_{j}^{\mu_{i}(X,h)}$.}

Theorem
5.2
will be proved by constructing the morphisms
\eta_{i}^{(X,h)}
explicitly for each indecomposable object
(X,h)
in
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
and each
i\in\Sigma(X,h)
. Before providing the definition of these morphisms, we need a couple of preliminary lemmas.

Lemma 5.3
.

For every indecomposable object
(X,h)
in
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
, we have that
\widetilde{h}(\tau x_{i})\geq 0
for all
i\in I
.

Proof.

The statement is obviously true if
(X,h)
is dominant as in this case
h
is (by definition) a sum of
h_{\tau x_{k}}
, and
\widetilde{h_{\tau x_{k}}}(z)=\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\tau x_{k},z}\geq 0
for any
z\in(\mathbb{Z}Q)_{0}
. Assume the statement is true for
(X,h)
(indecomposable) in
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
. Then for each
j\in I
such that
\widetilde{h}(\tau x_{j})>0
we have that

\forall i\in I,\kern 5.0pt\widetilde{h-\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\tau x_{j}}}(\tau x_{i})=\widetilde{h}(\tau x_{i})-\begin{cases}1&\text{if $i=j$,}\\
-1&\text{if $j\rightarrow i$,}\\
0&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

which implies that
\widetilde{h-\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\tau x_{j}}}(\tau x_{i})\geq 0
for all
i\in I
and hence the statement is true for any Serre tilting
\mu_{j}(X,h)
in the category
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
. By induction, this establishes the lemma.
∎

Before moving on, we need one more piece of notation. For every
i\in I
, define

H_{i}:=H(\tau x_{i})\cap\{\tau x_{k},k\in I\}=\{\tau x_{j}\mid j\rightsquigarrow i\}.

where the second equality is due to the fact that
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Rep}Q)}(\tau x_{j},\tau x_{i})=1
if and only if there is an oriented path from
i
to
j
in
Q
, and is zero otherwise.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
(X,h)
be an indecomposable object in
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
. Then we have that

\bigsqcup_{i\in I}H_{i}^{\sqcup\widetilde{h}(\tau x_{i})}\subset X.

Note that the multiset
H_{i}^{\sqcup\widetilde{h}(\tau x_{i})}
is well-defined thanks to Lemma
5.3
.

Proof.

Assume
(X,h)=Y(\tau x_{i})
with
i\in I
. Then by definition we have that
X=H(\tau x_{i})\supset\widetilde{H}_{i}
and
\widetilde{h}(\tau x_{i})=1
so the statement holds in that case, and hence holds for every (indecomposable) dominant object. Assume the statement has been proved for some indecomposable object
(X,h)
. We shall prove that the statement still holds for
(X^{\prime},h^{\prime}):=\mu_{j}(X,h)
for each
j\in I
is such that
\widetilde{h}(\tau x_{j})>0
. Recalling Definition
4.5
we have that

\bigsqcup_{i\in I}H_{i}^{\sqcup\widetilde{h^{\prime}}(\tau x_{i})}=H_{j}^{\sqcup\widetilde{h}(\tau x_{j})-1}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{k\rightarrow j}H_{k}^{\sqcup\widetilde{h}(\tau x_{k})+1}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{l}H_{l}^{\widetilde{h}(\tau x_{l})}

where
l
runs over all elements of
I
that are neither
j
itself nor satisfy
l\rightarrow j
. Now, it is straightforward to check that

H_{j}=\{\tau x_{j}\}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{k\rightarrow j}H_{k}

because, as the underlying graph of
Q
is assumed to be a tree, a vertex
i
such that
i\rightsquigarrow j
can satisfy
i\rightsquigarrow k
for only a unique
k\rightarrow j
(except if
i
is
j
itself).
Therefore we get

\{\tau x_{j}\}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{i\in I}H_{i}^{\sqcup\widetilde{h^{\prime}}(\tau x_{i})}=\bigsqcup_{i\in I}H_{i}^{\sqcup\widetilde{h}(\tau x_{i})}\subset X

and hence

\bigsqcup_{i\in I}H_{i}^{\sqcup\widetilde{h^{\prime}}(\tau x_{i})}\subset X\setminus\{\tau x_{j}\}\subset X^{\prime}.

∎

In particular, Lemma
5.4
implies that if
(X,h)
is an indecomposable object in
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
such that
\widetilde{h}(\tau x_{i})>0
for some
i\in I
then both
\tau x_{i}
and
x
appear (at least once) in
X
(recall that
x
denotes the chosen indecomposable object in
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Rep}Q)
provided by Lemma
5.1
).
Consequently, we can define an irreducible morphism
\eta_{i}^{(X,h)}
for every indecomposable object
(X,h)
in
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
and every
i\in\Sigma(X,h)
by choosing a pair of non-zero morphisms
f_{i}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Rep}Q)}(\tau x_{i},x)
and
g_{i}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Rep}Q)}(x,S\tau x_{i})
and then set

(5.1)

\eta_{i}^{(X,h)}:=f_{i}\otimes g_{i}\otimes\bigotimes_{y\in X\setminus\{\tau x_{i},x\}}\mathrm{id}_{y}

It remains to show that these morphisms satisfy the desired properties.

Proof of Theorem
5.2
.

Let
(X,h)
be an indecomposable object in
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
,
i\in\Sigma(X,h)
and
j\in I
such that there is an oriented path from
j
to
i
in
Q
. We denote
(X^{\prime},h^{\prime}):=\mu_{i}(X,h)
. In view of Definition
4.5
, Lemma
5.3
implies

(5.2)

\widetilde{h^{\prime}}(\tau x_{j})=\widetilde{h-\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\tau x_{i}}}(\tau x_{j})=\widetilde{h}(\tau x_{j})+1>0

which show that
\Sigma(X^{\prime},h^{\prime})
contains all
j\in I
such that
i\leftarrow j
. Then, for any
j\in\Sigma(X^{\prime},h^{\prime})
such that
j\rightsquigarrow i
, denoting
X^{\prime\prime}:=X\setminus\{\tau x_{i},\tau x_{j},x\}
we have

\displaystyle\eta_{j}^{(X^{\prime},h^{\prime})}\circ\eta_{i}^{(X,h)}

\displaystyle=\left(g_{j}\otimes f_{j}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{S\tau x_{i}}\otimes\bigotimes_{y\in X^{\prime\prime}}\mathrm{id}_{y}\right)\circ\left(f_{i}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\tau x_{j}}\otimes g_{i}\otimes\bigotimes_{y\in X^{\prime\prime}}\mathrm{id}_{y}\right)

\displaystyle=(g_{j}\circ f_{i})\otimes f_{j}\otimes g_{i}\otimes\bigotimes_{y\in X^{\prime\prime}}\mathrm{id}_{y}

\displaystyle=0

because

g_{j}\circ f_{i}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Rep}Q)}(\tau x_{i},S\tau x_{j})\simeq D\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Rep}Q)}(\tau x_{j},\tau x_{i})=0

as there is no oriented path from
i
to
j
in
Q
given that
j\rightsquigarrow i
and
Q
is acyclic. This establishes the property (i) of Theorem
5.2
.

Let us now assume
f\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}}(\mu_{i}(X,h),T)
such that
f\circ\eta_{i}^{(X,h)}=0
. Assume without loss of generality that
T
is indecomposable. Let us write
f=a\otimes b
where
a\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Rep}Q)}(x,z)
and
b
is a tensor product of non-trivial morphisms with domains in
\mu_{i}X\setminus\{x\}
. The composition with
\eta_{i}^{(X,h)}
can be trivial only via
f_{i}\circ a
where
f_{i}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Rep}Q)}(\tau x_{i},x)
. Hence the codomain of
a
must be of the form
z=S\tau x_{k}
with
k\rightsquigarrow i
. Moreover the set
\{k\in I\mid\text{$k\rightsquigarrow j$ and $\widetilde{h^{\prime}}(\tau x_{k})>0$ }\}
is not empty (as it contains for instance any
j
such that
j\rightarrow i
, by (
5.2
)). For any
k
in that set, recall that by definition of
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
we have that
f(\tau x_{k})\in\{x\}\sqcup\{S\tau x_{j},j\in I\}
. Hence we can consider the morphism
h:\mu_{\{\tau x_{i},\tau x_{k}\}}(X,h)\rightarrow T
given by

h:S\tau x_{k}\rightarrow S\tau x_{k}\quad x\rightarrow f(\tau x_{k})\quad\text{$y\rightarrow f(y)$ for all $y\neq x,S\tau x_{k}$ }.

Then it is straightforward to check that
h\circ\eta_{k}^{\mu_{i}(X,h)}=f
. This establishes the second property of Theorem
5.2
.

∎

6.
Exact chain complexes in
\mathcal{K}_{Q}^{(1)}

6.1.
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
-exact chain complexes

Given an
indecomposable dominant
object
X
in
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
that we write under the form

X:=\bigotimes_{k\in I}Y(\tau x_{k})^{\otimes c_{k}}\otimes_{\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}}Y(x_{k})^{\otimes d_{k}}

where
c_{k},d_{k}
are non-negative integers for every
k\in I
, we define an element
\boldsymbol{\omega}(X)
of the positive orthant of the root lattice of
\mathfrak{g}
as follows.
For each
i\in I
, we set

(6.1)

r_{i}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\rightsquigarrow j\\
\text{$j$ sink in $Q$}\end{subarray}}(\xi(i)-\xi(j)).

Note that
r_{i}
is a non-negative integer for each
i\in I
, and moreover we have that
r_{i}=0
if (and only if)
i
is a sink in
Q
.
We define a family
\{a_{i},i\in I\}
of non-negative integers by induction on
r_{i}
. If
r_{i}=0
, i.e.
i
is a sink in
Q
, we set
a_{i}:=\max(0,c_{i}-d_{i})
. Then, if
i\in I
is such that
a_{j}
has been constructed for all
j\in I
satisfying
r_{j}<r_{i}
, we set

a_{i}:=\max\left(0,c_{i}-d_{i}+\sum_{i\rightarrow j}a_{j}\right).

This is well-defined as one can immediately check that
r_{j}<r_{i}
if there is an arrow
i\rightarrow j
in
Q
. We then define
\boldsymbol{\omega}(X)
as

\boldsymbol{\omega}(X):=\sum_{i\in I}a_{i}\alpha_{i}

and we denote by
\mathrm{Supp}(X)
the subset of
I
given by

\mathrm{Supp}(X):=\{i\in I\mid a_{i}>0\}.

We can now introduce the main definitions of this section.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
C_{\bullet}
be a chain complex in
\mathcal{K}_{Q}^{(1)}
. We say that
C_{\bullet}
is
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
-exact if the following properties hold:

•

We have that
C_{n}=0
for all
n<0
and
C_{0}
is indecomposable dominant.

•

For every
n>0
and every finite set
Z\subset(\mathbb{Z}Q)_{0}
, we have that

Z\cap\mathrm{Supp}(C_{0})\neq\emptyset\quad\Rightarrow\quad\forall f:C_{n}\rightarrow\mu_{Z}(C_{0}),f\circ d_{n-1}^{C_{\bullet}}=0\Rightarrow\text{$f$ factors through $d_{n}^{C_{\bullet}}$.}

Remark 6.2
.

The fact that the codomain of
f
in the second condition of Definition
6.1
is written as
\mu_{Z}(C_{0})
is not restrictive. Indeed, by construction of the category
\mathcal{H}_{Q}
, any indecomposable object
X
such that there is a non-trivial morphism from
C_{n}
to
X
has to be a Serre tilting of an indecomposable summand of
C_{n}
; this summand is itself is a Serre tilting of
C_{0}
in view of the sequence of morphisms
C_{0}\xrightarrow{d_{0}}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow C_{n}
, and thus
X
is a Serre tilting of
C_{0}
.

Definition 6.3
.

Let
C_{\bullet}
be a chain complex in
\mathcal{K}_{Q}^{(1)}
. We say that
C_{\bullet}
is strongly
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
-exact if
C_{\bullet}
is
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
-exact and moreover all the non-trivial components of each differential of
C_{\bullet}
are of the form
\eta_{i}\otimes\mathrm{id}
for some
i\in\mathrm{Supp}(C_{0})
.

We can show the following.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
C_{\bullet}
and
D_{\bullet}
be objects in
\mathcal{K}_{Q}^{(1)}
. Assume that both
C_{\bullet}
and
D_{\bullet}
are strongly
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
exact and that
C_{0}\simeq D_{0}
. Then
C_{\bullet}\simeq D_{\bullet}
in
\mathcal{K}_{Q}^{(1)}
.

Proof.

The assumptions obviously imply that
\mathrm{Supp}(C_{0})=\mathrm{Supp}(D_{0})
.
Let us choose an isomorphism
u_{0}:C_{0}\rightarrow D_{0}
. The strong exactness of
C_{\bullet}
and
D_{\bullet}
implies that
d_{0}^{C\bullet}
and
d_{0}^{D_{\bullet}}
have the same non trivial components and therefore we have that
C_{1}\simeq D_{1}
so we can obviously complete the square. Then the composition
\tilde{u}_{1}:=d_{1}^{D_{\bullet}}\circ u_{1}
satisfies
\tilde{u}_{1}\circ d_{0}^{C_{\bullet}}=0
. Moreover the codomain of
\tilde{u}_{1}
is
D_{2}
whose indecomposable summands are all of the form
\mu_{Z}(D_{0})
with
Z\subset\mathrm{Supp}(D_{0})=\mathrm{Supp}(C_{0})
(by strong exactness of
D_{\bullet}
. Therefore by exactness of
C_{\bullet}
,
\tilde{u}_{1}
factors through
d_{1}^{C_{\bullet}}
which yields a map
u_{2}:C_{2}\rightarrow D_{2}
making the obvious square commute. Continuing this way we obtain a morphism
u_{\bullet}:C_{\bullet}\rightarrow D_{\bullet}
. The same arguments starting with an isomorphism
v_{0}:D_{0}\rightarrow C_{0}
yields a morphism
v_{\bullet}:D_{\bullet}\rightarrow C_{\bullet}
and we immediately have that
u_{\bullet}v_{\bullet}=\mathrm{id}_{D_{\bullet}}
and
v_{\bullet}u_{\bullet}=\mathrm{id}_{C_{\bullet}}
which proves the lemma.
∎

6.2.
Initial chain complexes

For every
i\in I
we consider the following indecomposable objects in
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
:

(6.2)

H_{i}:=Y(x_{i})\qquad K_{i}:=Y(\tau x_{i})\otimes_{\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}}Y(x_{i})\qquad F_{i}:=F(\tau x_{i}).

We then consider a certain collection of chain complexes in
\mathcal{K}_{Q}^{(1)}
that are non-trivial in only one homological degree. We will sometimes refer to these complexes as initial complexes. They can be grouped into three families each of cardinality
n=\sharp I
:

•

For every
i\in I
, the chain complex
\mathbf{H}_{i}
whose only non zero object lies in homological degree zero and is given by
Y(x_{i})
,

•

For every
i\in I
, the chain complex
\mathbf{K}_{i}
whose only non zero object lies in homological degree zero and is given by
K_{i}
,

•

For every
i\in I
, the chain complex
\mathbf{F}_{i}
whose only non zero object lies in homological degree one and is given by
F_{i}
.

In particular, the chain complexes belonging to the first two families are strongly
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
-exact. We now show that the notions of exactness introduced in the previous subsection are preserved under tensor product by the chain complexes
\mathbf{K}_{i}
.

Lemma 6.5
.

Assume
C_{\bullet}
is
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
-exact (resp. strongly
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
-exact). Then for any
i\in I
,
\mathbf{K}_{i}\otimes C_{\bullet}
is
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
-exact (resp. strongly
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
-exact).

Proof.

By definition,
\mathbf{K}_{i}
is non-trivial only in degree
0
, so that
(\mathbf{K}_{i}\otimes C_{\bullet})_{n}=(\mathbf{K}_{i})_{0}\otimes C_{n}=K_{i}\otimes C_{n}
for every
n\geq 0
. In particular the first condition of Definition
6.1
is satisfied. Moreover, the differentials of
\mathbf{K}_{i}\otimes C_{\bullet}
are given by
d_{n}^{\mathbf{K}_{i}\otimes C_{\bullet}}=\mathrm{id}_{K_{i}}\otimes d_{n}^{C_{\bullet}}
.

It follows from the definition of
\boldsymbol{\omega}
that
\boldsymbol{\omega}(K_{i}\otimes C_{0})=\boldsymbol{\omega}(C_{0})
so that
\mathrm{Supp}(K_{i}\otimes C_{0})=\mathrm{Supp}(C_{0})
. Let us fix
n>0
and let
Z
be a finite subset of
(\mathbb{Z}Q)_{0}
such that
Z\cap\mathrm{Supp}(C_{0})\neq\emptyset
. Let
f:K_{i}\otimes C_{n}\rightarrow\mu_{Z}(K_{i}\otimes C_{0})
be a non-trivial morphism and assume that
f\circ(\mathrm{id}_{K_{i}}\otimes d_{n-1}^{C_{\bullet}}=0
. This implies that (up to composing with an isomorphism)
f
can be written under the form
f=g\otimes h
where
g:K_{i}\rightarrow\mu_{Z^{\prime}}K_{i}
and
h:C_{n}\rightarrow\mu_{Z^{\prime\prime}}C_{0}
with
Z^{\prime}\sqcup Z^{\prime\prime}=Z
and such that
h\circ d_{n-1}^{C_{\bullet}}=0
. As
C_{\bullet}
is
:CQ^{(1)}
-exact, we get that
h=h^{\prime}\circ d_{n}^{C_{\bullet}}
and hence we obtain

f=g\otimes(h^{\prime}\circ d_{n}^{C_{\bullet}})=(g\otimes h^{\prime})\circ(\mathrm{id}_{K_{i}}\otimes d_{n}^{C_{\bullet}})=(g\otimes h^{\prime})\circ d_{n}^{\mathbf{K}_{i}\otimes C_{\bullet}}.

This shows that
\mathbf{K}_{i}\otimes C_{\bullet}
is
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
-exact. The strong exactness is obvious.
∎

6.3.
Leading objects

Given an element
\beta:=\sum_{i\in I}a_{i}\alpha_{i}
in the positive orthant of the root lattice of
\mathfrak{g}
, we define integers
b_{i},c_{i},d_{i}
for each
i\in I
as follows:

\forall i\in I,\kern 5.0ptb_{i}:=a_{i}-\sum_{i\rightarrow j}a_{j}\quad c_{i}:=(b_{i})_{+}\quad d_{i}:=(b_{i})_{-}

Here we are using the standard notations
b_{+}:=\max(0,b),b_{-}:=-\min(0,b)
for any integer
b
. In particular we have that
c_{i}\geq 0,d_{i}\geq 0,c_{i}d_{i}=0
and
c_{i}-d_{i}=b_{i}
for every
i\in I
.
We then define the following indecomposable dominant object in
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
:

(6.3)

Y[\beta]:=\bigotimes_{i\in I}Y(\tau x_{i})^{\otimes c_{i}}\otimes Y(x_{i})^{\otimes d_{i}}.

We can already make the following observation.

Lemma 6.6
.

For every
\beta:=\sum_{i\in I}a_{i}\alpha_{i}
in the positive orthant of the root lattice of
\mathfrak{g}
, we have that
\boldsymbol{\omega}(Y[\beta])=\beta
.

Proof.

Denote
\boldsymbol{\omega}(Y[\beta]):=\sum_{i}a^{\prime}_{i}\alpha_{i}
. We show that
a^{\prime}_{i}=a_{i}
for all
i\in I
by induction on
r_{i}
as defined by (
6.1
). If
i\in I
is such that
r_{i}=0
, i.e.
i
is a sink in
Q
, then by definition of
\boldsymbol{\omega}
we have that
a^{\prime}_{i}=c_{i}-d_{i}=(b_{i})_{+}-(b_{i})_{-}=b_{i}=a_{i}
(as the sum over
j
such that
i\rightarrow j
is empty given that
i
is a sink in
Q
). Let
i\in I
and assume that
a^{\prime}_{j}=a_{j}
has been proved for all
j\in I
such that
r_{j}<r_{i}
. Then by definition of
\boldsymbol{\omega}
we have

\displaystyle a^{\prime}_{i}

\displaystyle:=\max\left(0,c_{i}-d_{i}+\sum_{i\rightarrow j}a^{\prime}_{j}\right)=\max\left(0,b_{i}+\sum_{i\rightarrow j}a^{\prime}_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=\max\left(0,b_{i}+\sum_{i\rightarrow j}a_{j}\right)\quad\text{by induction assumption, as $r_{j}<r_{i}$ if $i\rightarrow j$}

\displaystyle=\max(0,a_{i})\quad\text{by definition of $b_{i}$}

\displaystyle=a_{i}

as
\beta
belongs to the positive orthant of the root lattice. The lemma is proved.
∎

From this lemma, we can derive the following useful observation.

Corollary 6.7
.

Let
(X,h)
be an indecomposable dominant object in
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
and let
\beta:=\boldsymbol{\omega}(X,h)
. Then we have

(X,h)\kern 5.0pt\simeq\kern 5.0pt\bigotimes_{i\in I}K_{i}^{\otimes\min(\widetilde{h}(\tau x_{i}),\widetilde{h}(x_{i}))}\otimes Y[\beta].

Proof.

As
(X,h)
is indecomposable dominant we can write it as

(X,h)\simeq\bigotimes_{i\in I}Y(\tau x_{i})^{\otimes c_{i}}\otimes Y(x_{i})^{\otimes d_{i}}

with
c_{i},d_{i}\geq 0
for each
i\in I
. Moreover, we actually have
c_{i}=\widetilde{h}(\tau x_{i})
and
d_{i}=\widetilde{h}(x_{i})
for each
i\in I
. So we can write

(X,h)\simeq\bigotimes_{i\in I}K_{i}^{\otimes\min(\widetilde{h}(\tau x_{i}),\widetilde{h}(x_{i}))}\otimes(X^{\prime},h^{\prime})

where denoting
c^{\prime}_{i}=\widetilde{h^{\prime}}(\tau x_{i})
and
d^{\prime}_{i}:=\widetilde{h^{\prime}}(x_{i})
we have
c^{\prime}_{i}-d^{\prime}_{i}=c_{i}-d_{i}
and
c^{\prime}_{i}d^{\prime}_{i}=0
, for every
i\in I
. As the definition of
\boldsymbol{\omega}
depends only on the integers
c_{k}-d_{k}
, we get
\boldsymbol{\omega}(X^{\prime},h^{\prime})=\beta=\boldsymbol{\omega}(Y[\beta])
by Lemma
6.6
which implies that
(X^{\prime},h^{\prime})\simeq Y[\beta]
because the conditions
c^{\prime}_{i}d^{\prime}_{i}=0
determine the integers
c_{i},d_{i}
uniquely for a given
\boldsymbol{\omega}
.
∎

Remark 6.8
.

Recalling the notations
\mathrm{Supp}
from Section
6.1
, it follows from Lemma
6.6
that
\mathrm{Supp}(Y[\beta])=\{i\in I,a_{i}\neq 0\}
if
\beta:=\sum_{i}a_{i}\alpha_{i}
(which was the motivation for the definition of
\boldsymbol{\omega}(X)
and
\mathrm{Supp}(X)
in Section
6.1
).

6.4.
Serre tiltings of leading objects

Let
Q
be a finite quiver. We assume that for any vertices
i,j
of
Q
, there is at most one oriented path from
i
to
j
(this is the case for instance if
Q
is a Dynkin quiver). Given an element
\beta
of the positive orthant of the root lattice, we denote by
\mathrm{Supp}(\beta):=\{i\in I\mid a_{i}>0\}
and by
Q_{\beta}
the subquiver of
Q
having
\mathrm{Supp}(\beta)
as set of vertices. Let
P_{i}^{\beta}
(resp.
I_{i}^{\beta}
) denote the the indecomposable projective (resp. injective) representation of
Q_{\beta}
at the vertex
i
. Recall that their respective dimension vectors are given by

\boldsymbol{\dim}P_{i}^{\beta}=\sum_{j\in\mathrm{out}_{Q_{\beta}}(i)}\alpha_{j}\qquad\boldsymbol{\dim}I_{i}^{\beta}=\sum_{j\in\mathrm{in}_{Q_{\beta}}(i)}\alpha_{j}.

where for each
i\in(Q_{\beta})_{0}
:

\mathrm{out}_{Q_{\beta}}(i):=\{j\in(Q_{\beta})_{0}\mid i\rightsquigarrow j\}\qquad\mathfrak{in}_{Q_{\beta}}(i):=\{j\in(Q_{\beta})_{0}\mid j\rightsquigarrow i\}.

The ultimate goal of this section will be to construct morphisms of chain complexes between various
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
-exact complexes in
\mathcal{K}_{Q}^{(1)}
. The first step to achieve this consists in constructing morphisms between (Serre tiltings of) leading objects in
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
, which is the aim of the next few statements.

Lemma 6.9
.

We have that

Y[\beta]\otimes Y(x_{i})\simeq K_{i}^{\otimes\epsilon_{i}}\otimes Y[\beta-\boldsymbol{\dim}I_{i}^{\beta}]

where
\epsilon_{i}
is equal to
1
if
c_{i}>0
and to zero otherwise.

Proof.

Let us write
\beta=\sum_{k}a_{k}\alpha_{k}
and

Y[\beta]=\bigotimes_{k\in I}Y(\tau x_{k})^{\otimes c_{k}}\otimes Y(x_{k})^{\otimes d_{k}}.

We claim that
\boldsymbol{\omega}(Y[\beta]\otimes Y(x_{i}))=\beta-\boldsymbol{\dim}I_{i}^{\beta}
. To check this, let us write

\boldsymbol{\omega}(Y[\beta]\otimes Y(x_{i})):=\sum_{k\in I}a^{\prime}_{k}\alpha_{k}\qquad Y[\beta]\otimes Y(x_{i}):=\bigotimes_{k\in I}Y(\tau x_{k})^{\otimes c^{\prime}_{k}}\otimes Y(x_{k})^{\otimes d^{\prime}_{k}}

so we obviously have
c^{\prime}_{k}=c_{k}
and
d^{\prime}_{k}=d_{k}+\delta_{k,i}
for each
k\in I
, and let us prove by induction on
r_{k}
that
a^{\prime}_{k}=a_{k}-\delta_{k\in\mathrm{in}_{Q_{\beta}}(i)}
. Assume
r_{k}=0
i.e.
k
is a sink in
Q
. Then

a^{\prime}_{k}=c^{\prime}_{k}-d^{\prime}_{k}=c_{k}-(d_{k}+\delta_{k,i})=a_{k}-\delta_{k,i}.

This implies the desired identity in that case. Assume it has been established for all
k\in I
such that
r_{k}\leq m
and let us fix
k\in I
with
r_{k}=m+1
. Then
r_{j}\leq m
for each
j\in I
such that
k\rightarrow j
and therefore we have that

\displaystyle a^{\prime}_{k}

\displaystyle=\max\left(0,c^{\prime}_{k}-d^{\prime}_{k}+\sum_{k\rightarrow j}a^{\prime}_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=\max\left(0,c_{k}-(d_{k}+\delta_{k,i})+\sum_{k\rightarrow j}(a_{j}-\delta_{j\in\mathrm{in}_{Q_{\beta}}(i)})\right)

\displaystyle=\max\left(0,c_{k}-d_{k}+\sum_{k\rightarrow j}a_{j}-\delta_{k\in\mathrm{in}_{Q_{\beta}}(i)}\right).

Indeed, by assumption on
Q
there is at most one oriented path from
k
to
i
and therefore if
k\neq i
then
k\in\mathrm{in}_{Q_{\beta}}(i)
if and only if there is
j\in\mathrm{in}_{Q_{\beta}}(i)
such that
k\rightarrow j
and if so then such
j
is unique. It remains to note that if
k\in\mathrm{in}_{Q_{\beta}}(i)
then in particular
k\in\mathrm{Supp}(\beta)
so that
c_{k}-d_{k}+\sum_{k\rightarrow j}a_{j}=a_{k}>0
. Hence we have that
a^{\prime}_{k}=a_{k}-\delta_{k\in\mathrm{in}_{Q_{\beta}}(i)}
which was the desired identity. Thus the claim holds. Finally, we have that
c^{\prime}_{k}d^{\prime}_{k}=c_{k}d_{k}=0
for all
k\in I
except for
k=i
where this the case only if
c_{i}=0
. If on the contrary
c_{i}>0
then necessarily
d_{i}=0
so that
d^{\prime}_{i}=1
. By Corollary
6.7
we obtain

Y[\beta]\otimes Y(x_{i})\otimes K_{i}^{\otimes\epsilon_{i}}\otimes Y[\beta-\boldsymbol{\dim}I_{i}^{\beta}]

as desired.
∎

Proposition 6.10
.

Let
\beta
be an arbitrary element of the positive orthant of the root lattice and let
i\in\mathrm{Supp}(\beta)
. Let
\mathrm{out}_{Q_{\beta}}(i):=\{j\in\mathrm{Supp}(\beta)\mid i\rightsquigarrow j\}
and let
Z
be the finite subset of
(\mathbb{Z}Q)_{0}
given by
Z:=\{\tau x_{j},j\in\mathrm{out}_{Q_{\beta}}(i)\}
. Then in
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
, we have an isomorphism

\mu_{Z}\left(Y[\beta]\otimes Y(x_{i})\right)\kern 5.0pt\simeq\kern 5.0ptF(i,\beta)\otimes K(i,\beta)\otimes H(i,\beta)\otimes Y[\beta-\boldsymbol{\dim}P_{i}^{\beta}].

where

F(i,\beta):=\bigotimes_{z\in Z}F(z)\qquad H(i,\beta):=\bigotimes_{l\notin\mathrm{Supp}(\beta)}Y(x_{l})^{\otimes\sharp j\in\mathrm{out}_{Q_{\beta}}(i),j\rightarrow l}

and

K(i,\beta)
is a product of objects of the form
K_{j},j\in I
.

Proof.

We let
c_{k},d_{k}
denote the non-negative integers such that
Y[\beta]\simeq\bigotimes_{k\in I}Y(\tau x_{k})^{\otimes c_{k}}\otimes Y(x_{k})^{\otimes d_{k}}
.
Consider the following indecomposable dominant object in
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
:

(X,h):=Y[\beta]\otimes\bigotimes_{z\in Z}Y(\tau^{-1}z).

In particular we have that
\widetilde{h}(\tau x_{k})=c_{k}
and
\widetilde{h}(x_{k})=d_{k}+\delta_{k\in\mathrm{out}_{Q_{\beta}}(i)}
for each
k\in I
. First of all, we claim that

(6.4)

\mu_{Z}(X,h)\simeq\bigotimes_{z\in Z}F(z)\otimes(X^{\prime},h^{\prime})

with
(X^{\prime},h^{\prime})
dominant in
\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}
. To see this, we perform the Serre tiltings on the elements of
Z
by starting at the vertices
j\in\mathrm{out}_{Q_{\beta}}(i)
such that
j
is a sink in
Q_{\beta}
and then following the orientation backwards (ending with the Serre tilting at
i
). Looking at the definition of
Y[\beta]
, we see that if
j
is a sink in
Q_{\beta}
then
c_{j}>0
(and
d_{j}=0
) so we can write
(X,h)
as

(X,h)\simeq\bigotimes_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathrm{out}_{Q_{\beta}}(i)\\
\text{$j$ sink in $Q_{\beta}$}\end{subarray}}K_{j}\otimes\bigotimes_{\begin{subarray}{c}l\in\mathrm{out}_{Q_{\beta}}(i)\\
\text{$l$ not sink in $Q_{\beta}$}\end{subarray}}Y(x_{l})\otimes(X^{\prime\prime},h^{\prime\prime})

where
(X^{\prime\prime},h^{\prime\prime}):==\bigotimes_{k\in I}Y(\tau x_{k})^{\tilde{c}_{k}}Y(x_{k})^{d_{k}}
with
\tilde{c}_{k}=c_{k}-1
if
k
is a sink in
Q_{\beta}
with
i\rightsquigarrow k
,
c^{\prime}_{k}=c_{k}
otherwise. Note in particular that
(X^{\prime\prime},h^{\prime\prime})
is dominant. Then, Lemma
4.10
implies that every Serre tilting
\mu_{j}K_{j}
for
j
sink in
Q_{\beta}
with
i\rightsquigarrow j
will be isomorphic to
F_{j}\otimes Y(\tau x_{l})
tensored by a dominant object, where
l\in\mathrm{out}_{Q_{\beta}}(i)
such that
l\rightarrow j
. Therefore after these Serre tiltings we will obtain

\bigotimes_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathrm{out}_{Q_{\beta}}(i)\\
\text{$j$ sink in $Q_{\beta}$}\end{subarray}}\left(F_{j}\otimes\bigotimes_{\begin{subarray}{c}l\in\mathrm{out}_{Q_{\beta}}(i)\\
l\rightarrow j\end{subarray}}K_{l}\right)\otimes\cdots

where
\cdots
is dominant. Continuing this process, we see that at each step, we always perform Serre tiltings of the form
\mu_{j}K_{j}
which is isomorphic to
F_{j}
tensored by a dominant object, due to Lemma
4.10
. This proves the claim.

As
(X^{\prime},h^{\prime})
is dominant, it is entirely determined (up to isomorphism) by
h^{\prime}
(cf. Remark
4.9
). Let us denote by
c^{\prime}_{k}:=\widetilde{h^{\prime}}(\tau x_{k})
and
d^{\prime}_{k}:=\widetilde{h^{\prime}}(x_{k})
for every
k\in I
. It follows from the definition of Serre tiltings that

c^{\prime}_{k}=c_{k}-\delta_{k\in\mathrm{out}_{Q_{\beta}}(i)}+\sharp\{j\in\mathrm{out}_{Q_{\beta}}(i),k\rightarrow j\}

d^{\prime}_{k}=d_{k}+\sharp\{j\in\mathrm{out}_{Q_{\beta}}(i),j\rightarrow k\}.

Note that if
k\in\mathrm{Supp}(\beta)
then there can be an arrow
j\rightarrow k
with
j\in\mathrm{out}_{Q_{\beta}}(i)
only if
k\in\mathrm{out}_{Q_{\beta}}(i)\setminus\{i\}
. In other words, we have
d^{\prime}_{k}>d_{k}
if only if either
k\in\mathrm{out}_{Q_{\beta}}(i)\setminus\{i\}
or
k\notin\mathrm{Supp}(\beta)
. Therefore we can rewrite
(X^{\prime},h^{\prime})
as

(X^{\prime\prime},h^{\prime\prime})\otimes H(i,\beta)\otimes\bigotimes_{z\in Z\setminus\{\tau x_{i}\}}Y(\tau^{-1}z)

with
(X^{\prime\prime},h^{\prime\prime})
is the dominant object given by

(X^{\prime\prime},h^{\prime\prime}):=\bigotimes_{k\in I}Y(\tau x_{k})^{\otimes c^{\prime\prime}_{k}}\otimes Y(x_{k})^{\otimes d^{\prime\prime}_{k}}

with

c^{\prime\prime}_{k}:=c^{\prime}_{k}\quad\text{and}\quad d^{\prime\prime}_{k}:=\begin{cases}d_{k}-1+\sharp\{j\in\mathrm{out}_{Q_{\beta}}(i),j\rightarrow k\}&\text{if $k\in\mathrm{out}_{Q_{\beta}}(i)\setminus\{i\}$,}\\
d_{k}&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Hence (
6.4
) becomes

\mu_{Z}(Y[\beta]\otimes Y(x_{i}))\simeq F(i,\beta)\otimes H(i,\beta)\otimes(X^{\prime\prime},h^{\prime\prime}).

We are now going to show that
\boldsymbol{\omega}((X^{\prime\prime},h^{\prime\prime}))=\beta-\boldsymbol{\dim}P_{i}^{\beta}
. Namely, denoting
\boldsymbol{\omega}((X^{\prime\prime},h^{\prime\prime})):=\sum_{k\in I}a^{\prime\prime}_{k}\alpha_{k}
, we prove by induction on
r_{k}
that
a^{\prime\prime}_{k}=a_{k}-\sharp\{\text{paths from $i$ to $k$ in $Q_{\beta}$}\}
for each
k\in I
. Assume
k\in I
is such that
r_{k}=0
i.e.
k
is a sink in
Q
. Then obviously
\sharp\{j\in\mathrm{out}_{Q_{\beta}}(i),k\rightarrow j\}=0
. Moreover, by assumption on
Q
, there is at most one oriented path from
i
to
k
so that
\sharp\{j\in\mathrm{out}_{Q_{\beta}}(i),j\rightarrow k\}
is equal to
1
if
k\in\mathrm{out}_{Q_{\beta}}(i)
and is equal to zero otherwise. So, if
k\notin\mathrm{out}_{Q_{\beta}}(i)
then
a^{\prime\prime}_{k}=c^{\prime\prime}_{k}-d^{\prime\prime}_{k}=c_{k}-d_{k}=a_{k}
. If
k=i
then
a^{\prime\prime}_{k}=c^{\prime\prime}_{k}-d^{\prime\prime}_{k}=c_{k}-1-d_{k}=a_{k}-1
. Finally if
k\in\mathrm{out}_{Q_{\beta}}(i)\setminus\{i\}
then

\displaystyle a^{\prime\prime}_{k}

\displaystyle=c^{\prime\prime}_{k}-d^{\prime\prime}_{k}=(c_{k}-1)-\left(d_{k}-1+\sharp\{j\in\mathrm{out}_{Q_{\beta}}(i),j\rightarrow k\}\right)

\displaystyle=a_{k}-1.

Thus the desired identity holds in that case. Assume now that it has been proved for all
k\in I
such that
r_{k}\leq m
and let us fix
k
such that
r_{k}=m+1
.
Assume
k\notin\mathrm{out}_{Q_{\beta}}(i)
. Then we have
r_{j}<r_{k}
for every
j\in I
such that
k\rightarrow j
and thus using the induction assumption we get

\displaystyle a^{\prime\prime}_{k}

\displaystyle=\max\left(0,c^{\prime\prime}_{k}-d^{\prime\prime}_{k}+\sum_{k\rightarrow j}a^{\prime\prime}_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=\max\left(0,c_{k}-d_{k}+\sharp\{j\in\mathrm{out}_{Q_{\beta}}(i),k\rightarrow j\}+\sum_{k\rightarrow j}(a_{j}-\delta_{j\in\mathrm{out}_{Q_{\beta}}(i)}\right)

\displaystyle=\max\left(0,c_{k}-d_{k}+\sum_{k\rightarrow j}a_{j}\right)=a_{k}.

Assume now
k=i
. Then similarly we get

\displaystyle a^{\prime\prime}_{i}

\displaystyle=\max\left(0,c^{\prime\prime}_{i}-d^{\prime\prime}_{i}+\sum_{i\rightarrow j}a^{\prime\prime}_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=\max\left(0,c_{i}-1-d_{i}+\sharp\{j\in(Q_{\beta})_{0},i\rightarrow j\}+\sum_{i\rightarrow j}(a_{j}-\delta_{j\in(Q_{\beta})_{0}})\right)

\displaystyle=\max\left(0,c_{i}-d_{i}-1+\sum_{i\rightarrow j}a_{j}\right).

As
i\in\mathrm{Supp}(\beta)
, we have that
a_{i}>0
so that
a_{i}=c_{i}-d_{i}+\sum_{i\rightarrow j}a_{j}
and thus we obtain

a^{\prime\prime}_{i}=\max(0,a_{i}-1)=a_{i}-1.

Finally if
k\in\mathrm{out}_{Q_{\beta}}(i)\setminus\{i\}
then

\displaystyle c^{\prime\prime}_{k}-d^{\prime\prime}_{k}+\sum_{k\rightarrow j}a^{\prime\prime}_{j}

\displaystyle=c_{k}-1+\sharp\{j\in\mathrm{out}_{Q_{\beta}}(i),k\rightarrow j\}-d_{k}+1

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\sharp\{j\in\mathrm{out}_{Q_{\beta}}(i),j\rightarrow k\}+\sum_{k\rightarrow j}(a_{j}-\delta_{j\in\mathrm{out}_{Q_{\beta}}(i)})

\displaystyle=c_{k}-d_{k}+\sum_{k\rightarrow j}a_{j}-\sharp\{j\in\mathrm{out}_{Q_{\beta}}(i),j\rightarrow k\}.

By assumption on
Q
, there can only be at most one
j\in\mathrm{out}_{Q_{\beta}}(i)
such that
j\rightarrow k
, and in this case there is necessarily one given that
k\in\mathrm{out}_{Q_{\beta}}(i)\setminus\{i\}
. Hence we obtain

a^{\prime\prime}_{k}=\max\left(0,a_{k}-1\right)=a_{k}-1

as
a_{k}>0
given that
k\in\mathrm{Supp}(\beta)
.
Hence we have proved that
\boldsymbol{\omega}((X^{\prime\prime},h^{\prime\prime}))=\beta-\boldsymbol{\dim}P_{i}^{\beta}
. The conclusion follows using Corollary
6.7
.
∎

Corollary 6.11
.

Let
i\in\mathrm{Supp}(\beta)
and let
Z
denote the finite subset of
(\mathbb{Z}Q)_{0}
given by
Z:=\{\tau x_{j},j\in\mathrm{out}_{Q_{\beta}}(i)\}
. Then we have that

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{H}_{Q}^{(1)}}\left(\mu_{Z\setminus\{\tau x_{i}\}}(Y[\beta]\otimes Y(x_{i}))\kern 5.0pt,\kern 5.0ptF(i,\beta)\otimes K(i,\beta)\otimes H(i,\beta)\otimes Y[\beta-\boldsymbol{\dim}P_{i}^{\beta}]\right)\neq 0.

Proof.

Denoting
(X,h):=\mu_{Z\setminus\{\tau x_{i}\}}(Y[\beta]\otimes Y(x_{i}))
, Proposition
6.10
tells us that
\mu_{i}(X,h)\simeq F(i,\beta)\otimes K(i,\beta)\otimes H(i,\beta)\otimes Y[\beta-\boldsymbol{\dim}P_{i}^{\beta}]
. Moreover we have (by definition of Serre tiltings)

h=h_{\beta}-\sum_{j\in\mathrm{out}_{Q_{\beta}}(i)}\delta_{\tau x_{j}}

where
h_{\beta}
denotes the quasi-additive function of
Y[\beta]
. Hence we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{h}(\tau x_{i})

\displaystyle=\widetilde{h_{\beta}}(\tau x_{i})+\sharp\{j\in\mathrm{out}_{Q_{\beta}}(i),\rightarrow j\}=\begin{cases}\widetilde{h_{\beta}}(\tau x_{i})&\text{if $i$ is a sink in $Q_{\beta}$,}\\
\widetilde{h_{\beta}}(\tau x_{i})+1&\text{otherwise. }\end{cases}

It follows from the definition of
Y[\beta]
that if
i
is a sink in
Q_{\beta}
then
\widetilde{h_{\beta}}(\tau x_{i})>0
and hence
\widetilde{h}(\tau x_{i})>0
. If on the other hand
i
is not a sink in
Q_{\beta}
then Lemma
5.3
implies that
\widetilde{h}(\tau x_{i})\geq 1
so finally we have
\widetilde{h}(\tau x_{i})>0
in both cases. Hence the desired statement, by definition of the morphisms in the category
\mathcal{H}_{Q}
.
∎

7.
q
-characters as Euler characteristics

For every vertex
x:=(i,p)\in(\mathbb{Z}Q)_{0}
, we denote by
Y_{i,p}

Y(x)
in the Grothendieck group (ring) of

[Y(x_{i})]=Y_{i,\xi(i)}\qquad[Y(\tau x_{i})]=Y_{i,\xi(i)-2}

With these notations, we can state the second main result of this paper.

Theorem 7.1
.

Assume
\mathfrak{g}
is of type
A_{n},n\geq 1
or
D_{n},n\geq 4
and let
Q
be an arbitrary orientation of the Dynkin diagram of

\chi\left(C_{\bullet}[\beta]\right)_{|\forall i,f_{i}:=-1}={\widetilde{\chi}}_{q}\left(L[\beta]\right).

The proof will consist in showing that the (truncated)
q
-characters of certain simple modules in the HL category
\mathcal{C}_{\xi}^{(1)}
satisfy certain three-term identities. This will be done by establishing the existence of certain short exact sequences in
\mathcal{C}_{\xi}^{(1)}

We fix a simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of type
A_{n},n\geq 1
or
D_{n},n\geq 4
as well as a height function
\xi
adapted to an (arbitrary) orientation
Q
of the Dynkin diagram of
\mathfrak{g}
. We denote by
\theta
the highest root of
\mathfrak{g}
.
Recall the notations
M(\beta)
and
I(\beta)
from Section
6.5
.
For each
\beta
in the positive orthant of the root lattice we denote by
\mathfrak{m}_{\beta}\in\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbb{Z}}
the dominant monomial given by

\mathfrak{m}_{\beta}:=\prod_{i\in I}Y_{i,\xi(i)-2}^{c_{i}}Y_{i,\xi(i)}^{d_{i}}.

We begin with the following lemma.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
\beta\leq\theta
and

such that

and

Then the Laurent monomial

(7.1)

\mathfrak{m}_{\beta}\times A_{i}^{-1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\neq i\\
i\rightsquigarrow k\rightsquigarrow j\end{subarray}}A_{k}^{-a_{j}}

The assumptions imply that

can be written under the form

\mathfrak{m}_{\beta}=Y_{\tau x_{j}}^{a}Y_{x_{i}}^{b}\mathfrak{m^{\prime}}

with

and
\mathrm{m}^{\prime}
is a dominant monomial that is not divisible by

nor
Y_{x_{k}}
for any
k
such that

. The simple module
L(\mathfrak{m}_{\beta})
is then a Jordan-Hölder component of the tensor product
L(Y_{\tau x_{j}})^{\otimes a}\otimes L(Y_{x_{i}})^{\otimes b}\otimes L
where

is the standard module with highest
l
-weight
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
. It is straightforward to check that there is no dominant monomial

satisfying

\mathfrak{m}_{\beta}\times A_{i}^{-1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\neq i\\
i\rightsquigarrow k\rightsquigarrow j\end{subarray}}A_{k}^{-a_{j}}\kern 5.0pt\preccurlyeq_{N}\kern 5.0pt\mathfrak{m}\kern 5.0pt\prec_{N}\kern 5.0pt\mathfrak{m}_{\beta}

where

denotes the Nakajima ordering. Therefore, (
7.1
) cannot appear in the truncated
q
-character of any simple component of
L(Y_{\tau x_{j}})^{\otimes a}\otimes L(Y_{x_{i}})^{\otimes b}\otimes L
other than
L(\mathfrak{m}_{\beta})
.
∎

We now fix
\beta\in R
and assume that the desired result has been established for all elements
\gamma\in R
of height strictly smaller than that of
\beta
.

Proposition 7.3
.

Let
i:=i(\beta)
. Then the tensor product
L(\mathfrak{m}_{\beta})\otimes L(Y_{x_{i}})
is not irreducible.

Proof.

Assume
i
is a sink in
Q_{\beta}
with
a_{i}=1
. Then
\mathfrak{m}_{\beta}
is of the form
Y_{\tau x_{i}}\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
where
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
involves only
Y_{x_{j}}Y_{x_{j}},j\neq i
. If
L(\mathfrak{m}_{\beta})\otimes L(Y_{i,\xi(i)})
were simple then we would have

L(\mathfrak{m}_{\beta})\otimes L(Y_{i,\xi(i)})\simeq L(Y_{\tau x_{i}}Y_{x_{i}}\mathfrak{m}^{\prime})\simeq L(Y_{\tau x_{i}}Y_{x_{i}})\otimes L(\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}).

In particular this implies

{\widetilde{\chi}}_{q}(L(\mathfrak{m}_{\beta}))=Y_{\tau x_{i}}{\widetilde{\chi}}_{q}(L(\mathfrak{m}^{\prime})).

Because of the form of
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
, we see that the monomial
\mathfrak{m}_{\beta}A_{i}^{-1}=Y_{\tau x_{i}}\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}A_{i}^{-1}
cannot appear on the right hand side; however, it has to appear on the left hand side by Lemma
7.2
, hence a contradiction.

Assume now that
i=i(\beta)
is such that there is an oriented path

k\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow i\rightarrow i^{\prime}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow j

in
Q_{\beta}
with
a_{k}=\cdots=a_{i}=1
,
a_{i^{\prime}}=\cdots=a_{j}=2
and
j
is a sink in
Q_{\beta}
so that
\mathfrak{m}_{\beta}
is of the form
Y_{\tau x_{j}}^{2}Y_{x_{i}}\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
. It is straightforward to check that

\mathfrak{m}_{\beta}Y_{x_{i}}=\mathfrak{m}_{\gamma}\mathfrak{m}^{\prime\prime}\qquad\gamma:=\beta-\sum_{j\rightsquigarrow i}\alpha_{j}

where
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime\prime}
is a dominant monomial dividing
\mathfrak{m}_{\beta}
and containing only variables of the form
Y_{x_{p}}
or
Y_{\tau x_{q}}
with
q
such that there is no oriented path between
q
and
i
. In particular
\gamma
has height strictly less than that of
\beta
and thus by the induction assumption we have that
L(\mathfrak{m}_{\gamma})\simeq L[\gamma]
. As
i\notin\mathrm{Supp}(\gamma)
(because
a_{i}=1
), this implies in particular that the monomial

(7.2)

(A_{j}^{-1}\cdots A_{i^{\prime}}^{-1})^{2}A_{i}^{-1}

does not appear in the renormalized (truncated)
q
-character of
L(\mathfrak{m}_{\gamma})
. On the other hand, if
L
is any fundamental representation whose highest
l
-weight divides
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime\prime}
, then the renormalized truncated
q
-character of
L
is either
1
(if
L\simeq L(Y_{x_{p}})
for some
p
) or contains only monomials divisible by
A_{q}^{-1}
if
L\simeq L(Y_{\tau x_{q}})
. But as mentioned above, there is no oriented path between
i
and
q
and hence the renormalized
q
-character of
L
cannot contain (
7.2
) nor any monomial dividing it. Consequently, we obtain that the monomial (
7.2
) cannot appear in the renormalized
q
-character of
L(\mathfrak{m}_{\gamma}\mathfrak{m}^{\prime\prime})
. So, if
L(\mathfrak{m}_{\beta})\otimes L(Y_{x_{i}})
was simple, we would have

L(\mathfrak{m}_{\beta})\otimes L(Y_{x_{i}})\simeq L(\mathfrak{m}_{\beta}Y_{x_{i}})\simeq L(\mathfrak{m}_{\gamma}\mathfrak{m}^{\prime\prime})

so that the monomial (
7.2
) would not appear in the renormalized
q
-character of
L(\mathfrak{m}_{\beta})
. This is a contradiction by Lemma
7.2
.
It remains to deal with the case where
M(\beta)=\emptyset
, i.e.
a_{k}=2
for all
k\in\mathrm{Supp}(\beta)
. In that case we obviously have that
\beta=2\gamma
where
\gamma
is a multiplicity-free positive root and we get that
\mathfrak{m}_{\beta}=\mathfrak{m}_{\gamma}^{2}
. Moreover we obviously have
Q_{\gamma}=Q_{\beta}
and by definition of
i(\beta)
we have that
i
is a sink in
Q_{\gamma}
. We then have

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\delta}(L(Y_{x_{i}}),L(\mathfrak{m}_{\beta}))

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\delta}(L(Y_{x_{i}}),L(\mathfrak{m}_{\gamma}^{2}))

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\delta}(L(Y_{x_{i}}),L(\mathfrak{m}_{\gamma})^{\otimes 2})\quad\text{as all simple modules in $\mathcal{C}_{\xi}^{(1)}$ are real}

\displaystyle=2\boldsymbol{\delta}(L(Y_{x_{i}}),L(\mathfrak{m}_{\gamma}))\quad\text{by Theorem~\ref{thm : KKOP}(iv)}

\displaystyle\neq 0

as
L(Y_{x_{i}})
and
L(\mathfrak{m}_{\gamma})
do not commute by what has been done in the first case. This concludes the proof of the proposition.
∎

Proposition 7.4
.

Let
\beta\leq\theta
and
i:=i(\beta)
. Then the tensor product
L(\mathfrak{m}_{\beta})\otimes L(Y_{x_{i}})
is of length two in the HL category
\mathcal{C}_{\xi}^{(1)}
.

Proof.

Let us first assume that
a_{i}=1
. In that case, we are going to show that
\boldsymbol{\delta}(L(Y_{x_{i}}),L(\mathfrak{m}_{\beta}))=1
, which will imply the desired statement by Theorem
3.4
(ii). As Proposition
7.3
implies that
\boldsymbol{\delta}(L(Y_{x_{i}}),L(\mathfrak{m}_{\beta}))\neq 0
, it actually suffices to prove that
\boldsymbol{\delta}(L(Y_{x_{i}}),L(\mathfrak{m}_{\beta}))\leq 1
.
If
i
is a sink in
Q_{\beta}
then
\mathfrak{m}_{\beta}=Y_{\tau x_{i}}\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
where
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
is a dominant monomial divisible only by
Y_{x_{p}}
or
Y_{\tau x_{q}}
such that there is no oriented path from
i
to
q
. Consequently, we can choose an appropriate PBW order on the collection of fundamental representations in
\mathcal{C}_{\xi}^{(1)}
so that

L(\mathfrak{m}_{\beta})\simeq\mathrm{hd}(L(Y_{\tau x_{i}})\otimes L(\mathfrak{m}^{\prime})).

Then, Theorem
3.5
implies that
L(Y_{x_{i}})
strongly commutes with any fundamental representation whose highest
l
-weight divides
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
and hence
L(Y_{x_{i}})
also strongly commutes with
L(\mathfrak{m}^{\prime})
. In particular we get
\boldsymbol{\delta}(L(Y_{x_{i}}),L(\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}))=0
and hence we obtain

\boldsymbol{\delta}(L(Y_{x_{i}}),L(\mathfrak{m}_{\beta}))=\boldsymbol{\delta}(L(Y_{x_{i}}),\mathrm{hd}(L(Y_{\tau x_{i}})\otimes L(\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}))\leq\boldsymbol{\delta}(L(Y_{x_{i}}),L(Y_{\tau x_{i}}))=1.

Thus the claim is proved in that case. If there is no sink in
M(\beta)
, then as explained in the proof of Proposition
7.3
we have
\mathfrak{m}_{\beta}=Y_{\tau x_{j}}^{2}Y_{x_{i}}\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
. For the same reasons as in the previous case we have that
L(\mathfrak{m}^{\prime})
strongly commutes with
L(Y_{x_{i}})
and thus using Theorem
3.4
(v) we obtain

\boldsymbol{\delta}(L(\mathfrak{m}_{\beta}),L(Y_{x_{i}}))\leq\boldsymbol{\delta}(L(Y_{\tau x_{j}}^{2}Y_{x_{i}}),L(Y_{x_{i}}))=\max\left(0,2\boldsymbol{\delta}(L(Y_{\tau x_{j}}),L(Y_{x_{i}}))-1\right)=1

where the last equality follows from Theorem
3.5
as there is an oriented path from
i
to
j
in
Q
. Consequently the desired statement is proved in the case where
a_{i(\beta)}=1
.

Assume now that
a_{i(\beta)}=2
which means that
a_{k}=2
for all
k\in\mathrm{Supp}(\beta)
. Then as mentioned above we have
\beta=2\gamma
and the arguments from the first case above show that
\boldsymbol{\delta}(L(\mathfrak{m}_{\gamma},L(Y_{x_{i}}))=1
, hence by Theorem
3.4
(ii) a short exact sequence in
\mathcal{C}_{\xi}^{(1)}

0\rightarrow A\rightarrow L(\mathfrak{m}_{\gamma})\otimes L(Y_{x_{i}})\rightarrow B\rightarrow 0

with
A
and
B
(real) simple modules both strongly commuting with
L(\mathfrak{m}_{\gamma})
(as well as
L(Y_{x_{i}})
). In particular, the tensor product
L(\mathfrak{m}_{\gamma})^{\otimes 2}\otimes L(Y_{x_{i}})
has exactly two simple components, respectively isomorphic to
L(\mathfrak{m}_{\gamma})\otimes A
and
L(\mathfrak{m}_{\gamma})\otimes B
. In conclusion, we have that
L(\mathfrak{m}_{\beta})\otimes L(Y_{x_{i}})
is of length two in this case as well, which concludes the proof of the proposition.
∎

Proposition 7.5
.

Denote by
X_{i}:=Y_{\tau x_{i}}Y_{x_{i}}
for each
i\in I
. Let
\beta\leq\theta
and
i\in I(\beta)
. Then there is an identity of truncated
q
-characters

{\widetilde{\chi}}_{q}(L(\mathfrak{m}_{\beta}))\cdot Y_{x_{i}}=X_{i}\cdot{\widetilde{\chi}}_{q}(L(\mathfrak{m}_{\beta-\boldsymbol{\dim}I_{i}^{\beta}})+Y(i,\beta)X(i,\beta){\widetilde{\chi}}_{q}(L(\mathfrak{m}_{\beta-\boldsymbol{\dim}P_{i}^{\beta}}))

where
Y(i,\beta):=[H(i,\beta)]
and
X(i,\beta):=[K(i,\beta)]
with the notations of Proposition
6.10
.

Proof.

Assume that
i
is a sink in
Q_{\beta}
and
a_{i}=1
. By Proposition
7.4
, we have an identity in
K_{0}(\mathcal{C}_{\xi}^{(1)})
:

[L(\mathfrak{m}_{\beta})][L(Y_{x_{i}})]=[L(\mathfrak{m}_{\beta}Y_{x_{i}})]+[M]

where
M
is a simple module in
\mathcal{C}_{\xi}^{(1)}
whose highest
l
-weight needs to be determined. Before doing so, we can already note that, using the same notations as above, we have

\mathfrak{m}_{\beta}Y_{x_{i}}=X_{i}\mathfrak{m}_{\beta-\sum_{j\rightsquigarrow i}\alpha_{j}}.

By Lemma
7.2
, the monomial
\mathfrak{m}_{\beta}A_{i}^{-1}
must appear in the (truncated)
q

L(\mathfrak{m}_{\beta})
. However, as

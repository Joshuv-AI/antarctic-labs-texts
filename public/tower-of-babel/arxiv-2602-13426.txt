Dolbeault formality for complex nilmanifolds
Tommaso Sferruzza

Abstract




Abstract.

A quasi-isomorphism of differential graded algebras (DGA)
is a multiplicative map inducing an isomorphism on
cohomology. A DGA is called formal if it can be connected
by a chain of quasi-isomorphisms to its cohomology
algebra. We prove that the Dolbeault DGA of a complex nilmanifold is
formal only if it is a torus, and the Dolbeault algebra of
(0,p)-forms is formal if and only if the complex structure
is abelian.

1
Introduction

Neisendorfer and Taylor
[
NT
]
defined a complex
manifold
M
to be
Dolbeault formal
if the
differential bigraded algebra (shortly, DBA) of bigraded
forms
(\Lambda^{*,*}M,\overline{\partial})
is equivalent, as a DBA,
to a DBA with trivial (0,1)-differential.

The equivalence of DBA is understood as a chain of
quasi-isomorphisms (that is, homomorphisms inducing
an isomorphism on the cohomology), preserving the
bigrading.

Nearly fifty years after this definition was given, the
landscape of Dolbeault formal manifolds in the complex
non-Kähler setting remains almost entirely unexplored,
safe for few isolated classes of examples.

In this paper, we analyze the existence of Dolbeault
formal manifolds in the class of nilmanifolds endowed with
invariant complex structures.

By a renowned result of Malcev
[
Ma
]
, a
nilmanifold
is characterized as the compact quotient of
a connected nilpotent Lie group with rational Lie algebra,
by a discrete lattice.

This class of compact manifolds has served as a testing
ground for conjectures and counterexamples in differential
and complex geometry, starting from the work of Thurston

[
Thu
]
.

In the late 80’s, Benson and Gordon

[
BeGo
]
and, independently, Hasegawa

[
Ha2
]
, proved that if a nilmanifold admits a
Kähler structure than it must be diffeomorphic to a
torus.

The latter argued by proving that the minimal model of a
Lie algebra associated to any nilmanifold is not formal,
unless the Lie algebra is abelian (and, hence, the
nilmanifold is a torus). Indeed, Nomizu’s theorem

[
No
]
states that the cohomology of any rational
Lie algebra is isomorphic to the de Rham cohomology of the
associated nilmanifold. On the other hand, compact
Kähler manifolds, and more in general, manifolds
satisfying the
dd^{c}
-lemma, are formal in the sense of
Sullivan
[
DGMS
]
.

Formal manifolds play a fundamental role in rational homotopy
theory
[
Sul
,
FHT
]
. Indeed,
for simply connected manifolds (or manifolds with
nilpotent fundamental group and unipotent action
of the fundamental group on the homotopy groups), the rational
homotopy type of the manifold is a formal consequence of
its cohomology ring.

A strengthening of formality is the notion of
geometrically formal metric

[
Kot
]
, i.e., a
Riemannian metric for which the products of harmonic forms
is still harmonic. On the other hand, the existence of
non-vanishing Massey products and higher operations
obstructs rational formality.

However, the vanishing of Massey products
does not imply the formality (
[
HS
]
).
This was the motivation for developing the
theory of
A_{\infty}
-structures, intended
to replace the Massey products in studying
of rational homotopy (
[
St
]
). Later,
Babenko and Taimanov suggested a non-commutative
generalizetion of Massey products
[
BT
]
which contains
a complete information on rational homotopy,
and vanishes if and only if the DGA is formal.
The Massey products for Dolbeault DGA were
computed in
[
CT
]
,
who gave sufficient conditions for existence
of non-vanishing Massey products.

Several homotopy theories and notions of geometric
formality and Massey products have been introduced

[
NT
,
MS
,
AT
,
TT
,
ST3
]
.

In 1978, Neisendorfer and Taylor
[
NT
]

introduced the Dolbeault homotopy theory,
the homotopy theory for the differential
graded algebras equipped with the Hodge bigrading.
As in rational homotopy theory, Dolbeault
formality obstructs the
dd^{c}
-lemma and, in
particular, the existence of Kähler structures on
compact complex manifolds.

To this regard, concerning the Dolbeault formality of
nilmanifolds endowed with invariant complex structure (or,
as we will usually call them,
complex nilmanifolds
),
only partial results have been obtained.

It was announced in

[
CFGU2
, Theorem 5]
that nilmanifolds
endowed with a nilpotent complex structures are never
Dolbeault formal, referring to the unpublished preprint

[
CFGU
]
. In

[
CFGU3
]
, the same authors
provide four examples of complex nilmanifolds which are
not Dolbeault formal.

More recently, the first author and Tomassini
[
ST3
]

proved the weaker result that complex parallelizable
nilmanifolds are never Dolbeault formal unless they are
complex tori.

A substantial difference with de Rham cohomology of
nilmanifolds arises since there exist no counterpart of
Nomizu’s theorem. Indeed, except for certain classes of
invariant complex structures

[
CF
,
CFGU3
,
R1
,
R2
]
,
it is not known whether the injection of the Lie algebra
Dolbeault cohomology into the Dolbeault cohomology of the
nilmanifold is an isomorphism.

In this paper, our main theorem finally provides a proof
for any nilmanifold endowed with any invariant complex
structure.

More precisely, let
M=G/\Gamma
be a complex nilmanifold
and
(\Lambda^{*,*}M,\overline{\partial})
its the associated
differential bigraded algebra. Then, the algebras

(\Lambda^{*}M,\overline{\partial}):=(\text{Tot}^{*}(\Lambda^{*,*}M),\overline{\partial})

and
(\Lambda^{0,*}M,\overline{\partial})
are called, respectively,
the
Dolbeault DGA
and
Dolbeault (0,
*
)-DGA
of

M
. With this in mind, we prove the following
(
5.2
).

Theorem 1.1:
The Dolbeault DGA of a complex nilmanifold
M
is never
formal, unless
M
is a torus.

The proof relies on the quasi-isomorphism between
the de Rham DGA of a nilmanifold and the Chevalley-Eilenberg DGA
of its Lie algebra, which is due to Nomizu,
[
No
]
.
The Chevalley-Eilenberg DGA, as shown by Hasegawa
(
[
Ha1
, Theorem 1]
), is
never formal. Using the
domination argument due to
[
MSZ
]
,
we are able to use the non-formality
of the Chevalley-Eilenberg DGA to prove that
the Dolbeault DGA of a nilmanifold is non-formal.

Now, let
{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}={\mathfrak{g}}^{1,0}\oplus_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathfrak{g}}^{0,1}
be a
complex structure on the rational Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}

associated to a complex nilmanifold
M=G/\Gamma
. From
the Serre’s duality, triviality of the canonical
bundle and the domination theorem of
[
MSZ
]
, we obtain the following.

Theorem 1.2:

The Dolbeault (0,
*
)-DGA of a complex nilmanifold is
formal if and only if
{\mathfrak{g}}^{0,1}
is abelian.

Proof:
5.3
.

It would be interesting to study formality of complex
nilmanifolds in the context of pluripotential homotopy
theory
[
AT
,
Ste
]
. Here, a complex manifold
M
is
said to be
weakly formal

[
MS
]
if the double
complex
(\Lambda^{*,*}M,\partial,\overline{\partial})
is connected via a
chain of weak equivalences to a bidifferential bigraded
algebra

(\mathcal{B},\partial_{\mathcal{B}},\overline{\partial}_{\mathcal{B}})
such
that
\partial_{\mathcal{B}}\overline{\partial}_{\mathcal{B}}\equiv 0
.

Here, the picture is drastically different from both
rational and Dolbeault homotopy theories. In fact, weak
formality does not obstruct the
dd^{c}
-lemma nor the
existence of Kähler structures

[
ST2
,
PSZ
]
.

Furthermore, on the one hand, the first author and
Tomassini proved that complex nilmanifolds are weakly
formal if and only if they are SKT
[
ST
]
, in complex
dimension 3. On the other hand, complex parallelizable
solvmanifolds, up to complex dimension 5, are not weakly
formal,
[
ST3
]
whereas the picture
is still not clear in higher dimensions. This leaves
open the following question.

Question 1.3:
Which complex nilmanifolds are weakly formal?

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we
briefly recall the notions of formality for DG-algebras
and
dd^{c}
-lemma and the classical proof of formality and
Dolbeault formality of compact Kähler manifolds. In
Section 3, recall the definition of nilmanifolds endowed
with complex structures and describe some classical
examples. In Section 4, we recall the definitions of
Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology and Lie algebra Dolbeault
cohomology and relate them to the de Rham and Dolbeault
cohomologies of nilmanifolds.

In Section 5, we prove the main results of this paper.

2
Formality for DG-algebras

2.1
Quasi-isomorphism of DG-algebras

Definition 2.1: A graded commutative algebra
, or
super-commutative algebra
is a graded algebra

A^{*}=\bigoplus_{i\geqslant 0}A^{i}
, with the
multiplication
A^{i}\cdot A^{j}{\>\longrightarrow\>}A^{i+j}

and graded (super-) commutativity relation

x\cdot y=(-1)^{\widetilde{x}\widetilde{y}}y\cdot x
,
where
x\in A^{\widetilde{x}},y\in A^{\widetilde{y}}
.

A DG-algebra
(
differential
graded algebra
; also abbreviated as DGA) is a graded commutative
algebra equipped with a differential

d:\;A^{i}{\>\longrightarrow\>}A^{i+1}
which satisfies

d(x\cdot y)=dx\cdot y+(-1)^{\widetilde{x}}x\cdot dy
.
A homomorphism of DG-algebras is called

a quasi-isomorphism

if it induces an isomorphism on cohomology.
Two DG-algebras are called
quasi-isomorphic

if they can be connected by a chain of quasi-isomorphisms.

Definition 2.2:
A DG-algebra is called
formal

if it is quasi-isomorphic to its cohomology algebra
(with zero differential). A manifold is called

formal
if its de Rham algebra is formal.

2.2
The
dd^{c}
-lemma

Definition 2.3:
Let
M
be a complex manifold,
and
I:\;TM{\>\longrightarrow\>}TM
its complex structure
operator.
The twisted differential

of
M
is defined as
d^{c}=IdI^{-1}:\;\Lambda^{*}(M){\>\longrightarrow\>}\Lambda^{*+1}(M)
,
where
I
acts on 1-forms as an operator dual
to
I:\;TM{\>\longrightarrow\>}TM
, and on the rest of
differential forms multiplicatively.

The following theorem, called “the
dd^{c}
-lemma”,
was well known. It owes its name and its status
as a foundational result to the paper

[
DGMS
]
, where the formality of Kähler
manifolds was first established.

Theorem 2.4:
Let
\eta
be a form on a compact Kähler manifold,
satisfying one of the following conditions.

1.

\eta
is an exact
(p,q)
-form.

2.

\eta
is
d
-exact,
d^{c}
-closed.

3.

\eta
is
\overline{\partial}
-exact, and
\partial
-closed.

Then
\eta\in\operatorname{im}dd^{c}
.

Proof:
[
DGMS
]
.

The following corollary, observed in
[
DGMS
]
,
immediately follows from the
dd^{c}
-lemma.

Corollary 2.5:

Let
(\Lambda_{d^{c}}^{*}(M),d)
be the algebra of
d^{c}
-closed forms on
a compact Kähler manifold
M
, considered as a

DG
-algebra. Then the natural embedding

\Psi:\;(\Lambda_{d^{c}}^{*}(M),d){\>\longrightarrow\>}(\Lambda^{*}(M),d)

is a quasi-isomorphism.

Proof. Step 1:
Any closed
(p,q)
-form is
d^{c}
-closed.
Since any cohomology class can be represented as a sum
of closed
(p,q)
-forms, it can be represented
by
d^{c}
-closed form. Therefore
\Psi
is surjective
on cohomology.

Step 2:

Suppose that
\Psi
maps a
d
-closed form

\alpha\in\Lambda_{d^{c}}^{*}(M)
to an exact form.
By
dd^{c}
-lemma,
\alpha=dd^{c}\beta
, hence

\alpha\in d\left(\Lambda_{d^{c}}^{*}(M)\right)
.
Therefore,
\Psi
is injective on cohomology.

The same way one also proves the following result.

Corollary 2.6:

Let
(\Lambda_{\partial}^{*}(M),\overline{\partial})
be the algebra of
\partial
-closed forms on
a compact Kähler manifold
M
, considered as a

DG
-algebra. Then the natural embedding

\Psi:\;(\Lambda_{\partial}^{*}(M),d){\>\longrightarrow\>}(\Lambda^{*}(M),\overline{\partial})

is a quasi-isomorphism.

2.3
Formality for Kähler manifolds

We are mainly interested in the formality of the Dolbeault
algebra, but the same argument also proves the formality
for all Kähler manifolds (a famous result originally
due to
[
DGMS
]
).

Theorem 2.7:
A compact Kähler manifold is formal.

Proof. Step 1:
The natural embedding

(\Lambda_{d^{c}}^{*}(M),d){\>\longrightarrow\>}(\Lambda^{*}(M),d)

is a quasi-isomorphism (
2.2
).

Step 2:
Let

\Phi:\;(\Lambda_{d^{c}}^{*}(M),d){\>\longrightarrow\>}(H^{*}_{d^{c}}(M),0)

map a
d^{c}
-closed form to its cohomology class
in
\frac{\ker d^{c}}{\operatorname{im}d^{c}}=:H^{*}_{d^{c}}(M)
.
Representing cohomology classes by closed
(p,q)
-forms,
we find that
d{\left|{}_{{\phantom{|}\!\!}{}_{H^{*}_{d^{c}}(M)}}\right.}=0
. Therefore,

\Phi
is a homomorphism of DG-algebras.

It is surjective on cohomology because each class
in
H^{*}_{d^{c}}(M)
can be represented by a
d
,
d^{c}
-closed
form, and injective because each
d^{c}
-closed,
d
-exact form
is
dd^{c}
-exact.

2.4
Dolbeault formality for Kähler manifolds

Definition 2.8:
Let
(M,I)
be a compact complex manifold,
and
\overline{\partial}=\frac{d-\sqrt{-1}\>d^{c}}{2}
the (0,1)-part of de Rham differential.
The DGA
(\Lambda^{*}(M),\overline{\partial})
is called
Dolbeault algebra,
and

\overline{\partial}

the Dolbeault differential.

We also consider
the
(0,*)
-Dolbeault algebra

(\Lambda^{0,*}(M),\overline{\partial}):=\left(\bigoplus_{p}\Lambda^{0,p}(M),\overline{\partial}\right)
.

In
[
NT
]
, the
following theorem was proved.

Theorem 2.9:
The Dolbeault algebra

(\Lambda^{*}(M),\overline{\partial})
is formal when
(M,I)
is compact
and Kähler.

Proof. Step 1:
Let
(\Lambda^{*}_{\partial}(M),\overline{\partial})
be the algebra of
\partial
-closed forms.
By
2.2
the natural embedding

(\Lambda^{*}_{\partial}(M),\overline{\partial})\to(\Lambda^{*}(M),\overline{\partial})

is a quasi-isomorphism.

Step 2:
The map
(\Lambda^{*}_{\partial}(M),\overline{\partial})\to(H^{*}_{\partial}(M),0)

is a quasi-isomorphism, also by
dd^{c}
-lemma:
any
\partial
-exact,
\overline{\partial}
-closed form is also
\partial\overline{\partial}
-exact.

3
Nilmanifolds

In this section we define nilmanifolds, list some of their basic
properties, and give a few examples, for the benefit of
the reader. Nothing in this section is original, except
(possibly) the presentation and the choice of examples.

3.1
Nilmanifolds as homogeneous spaces

Nilmanifolds are a rich subject deserving much attention,
and complex structures on nilmanifolds provide a rich
source of examples and counterexamples in complex geometry.
In this section we give a brief introduction to nilmanifolds;
for more details and literature see

[
VGO
,
CG
]
.

Definition 3.1:
Let
M
be a compact manifold equipped
with a transitive action of nilpotent Lie group.
Then
M
is called
a nilmanifold
.

Remark 3.2:
As shown by A. Maltsev (
[
Ma
]
),
all nilmanifolds are obtained as quotient spaces,

M=G/\Gamma
, where
\Gamma\subset G
is a cocompact lattice in
a nilpotent Lie group.

Theorem 3.3:

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a nilpotent Lie algebra defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}
,
and
G
its Lie group. Then
G
contains a discrete
subgroup
\Gamma
such that
G/\Gamma
is compact, and

\Gamma=e^{\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}}
, where
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
is an integer
subalgebra in
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Moreover,
{\mathfrak{g}}\cong\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}{\mathbb{R}}
.
Finally, all nilmanifolds are obtained this way.

Proof:
[
Ma
]
.

Remark 3.4:
Topologically, all simply connected
nilpotent Lie groups are diffeomorphic to
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, and all nilmanifolds are
iterated circle fibrations. This observation (already made
by Maltsev) can be easily deduced from his theorem.

3.2
Invariant complex structures on nilmanifolds

Definition 3.5: An integrable complex structure
on a real Lie algebra

{\mathfrak{g}}
is a subalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}}^{1,0}\subset{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathbb{C}}

such that
{\mathfrak{g}}^{1,0}\oplus\overline{{\mathfrak{g}}^{1,0}}={\mathfrak{g}}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathbb{C}}

Remark 3.6:
Any such decomposition defines a complex structure
I

on
{\mathfrak{g}}
by
I{\left|{}_{{\phantom{|}\!\!}{}_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{1,0}}}\right.}=\sqrt{-1}\>
and
I{\left|{}_{{\phantom{|}\!\!}{}_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{0,1}}}\right.}=-\sqrt{-1}\>
.
We extend this complex structure to a left-invariant almost complex
structure on its Lie group
G
. Its integrability is given by

[T^{1,0}G,T^{1,0}G]\subset T^{1,0}G
,
which is equivalent to
[{\mathfrak{g}}^{1,0},{\mathfrak{g}}^{1,0}]\subset{\mathfrak{g}}^{1,0}
.

Remark 3.7:
In other words, left-invariant complex structures on a
connected real Lie group
are in bijective correspondence with integrable
complex structures on its Lie algebra.

Definition 3.8:
A
complex nilmanifold
is a nilmanifold

M=G/\Gamma
equipped with a complex structure
I
, in such a way
that
G
has a right-invariant complex structure,
and the projection
G{\>\longrightarrow\>}M
is holomorphic.
In this case, the complex structure
I

is called
invariant
.

Remark 3.9:
Note that not all complex structures on a nilmanifold are invariant;
indeed, a compact torus admits a non-Kähler complex
structure
(
[
Bl
,
Som
]
),
which is clearly non-invariant.

3.3
Parallelizable complex structures

Consider a complex nilmanifold
M=G/\Gamma
.
The group
G
does not need to be a complex Lie group.
When it is complex, and the comple structure on
M

is induced by the complex structure on this complex
Lie group, we deal with a special class
of complex nilmanifolds called parallelizable.

Example 3.10:
An
Iwasawa manifold
is the quotient of

\begin{pmatrix}1&*&*\\
0&1&*\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}

(group
U_{3}({\mathbb{C}})
of upper triangular complex matrices) by
a lattice
\Gamma
. As an example of
\Gamma
we can take

U_{3}({\mathbb{Z}}[\sqrt{-1}\>])
.

Remark 3.11:
Let
I
be a bi-invariant complex structure on
a nilpotent Lie group
G
. Such a complex structure is called

parallelizable.
For such a complex structure, the
tangent bundle is trivialized by a frame of invariant
holomorphic vector fields.

Remark 3.12:
A complex structure on
{\mathfrak{g}}
is bi-invariant if and
only if
M:=(G,I)/\Gamma
is homogeneous under the left
action of
G
. Then the left invariant
vector fields are holomorphic, and this implies that

[X,Y]=0
whenever
X\in T^{1,0}M
and
Y\in T^{0,1}M
.

Corollary 3.13:
Let
I
be a complex structure on a Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
.
Then
I
is bi-invariant if and only if
[{\mathfrak{g}}^{0,1},{\mathfrak{g}}^{1,0}]=0
.

Proof:
If this
[{\mathfrak{g}}^{0,1},{\mathfrak{g}}^{1,0}]=0
, then
\operatorname{Lie}_{X}Y=0
is of type (1,0) for any
right-invariant vector fields
X,Y
, with
Y
of type (1,0).
Then
gYg^{-1}\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{1,0}
for any
g\in G
, hence

T^{1,0}G
is bi-invariant.

3.4
Kodaira surface

Definition 3.14:
Let
T
,
T^{\prime}
be elliptic curves.

Kodaira surface

\pi:\;M{\>\longrightarrow\>}T
is a locally trivial
holomorphic fibration over
T
with fiber
T^{\prime}

and non-trivial Chern class.

A remark on terminology:

These are
“primary”
Kodaira surfaces.

“Secondary”
ones are obtained by taking
finite unramified quotients.

Remark 3.15:
The Kodaira surface is diffeomorphic to a quotient

S^{1}\times(G/G_{\mathbb{Z}})
where
G
is a 3-dimensional
Heisenberg group, and
G_{\mathbb{Z}}
a lattice in
G
.
Therefore, Kodaira surface is a nilmanifold.
This observation is explained in

[
Ha2
]
.

Remark 3.16:
The Kodaira manifold
M
is a complex nilmanifold,
but it is not homogeneous; this would follow e.g. from an
explicit description of its automorphism group given
in
[
OV
]
.

Remark 3.17:
Kodaira surface is not Kähler. Indeed,
the cohomology class of
\pi^{*}(\omega_{T})
vanishes,
where
\omega_{T}
is the Kähler form on
T
.
The product of
\omega_{T}
and the Kahler form on
M

(if it exists) is a positive volume form, hence
cannot be exact.

4
The Chevalley-Eilenberg complex and its cohomology

In this section, we define the cohomology of a Lie algebra
(known as the Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology) and relate
this cohomology algebra with the de Rham and Dobeault
cohomology of a nilmanifold.

4.1
The cohomology of a Lie algebra

Proposition 4.1:

Let
w\in\operatorname{Hom}(\Lambda^{2}V,V)
.
Consider the map dual to
w
,
d_{w}:\;V^{*}{\>\longrightarrow\>}\Lambda^{2}V^{*}
.
Extend this map to
d_{w}:\;\Lambda^{k}V^{*}{\>\longrightarrow\>}\Lambda^{k+1}V^{*}

using the Leibniz rule
d_{w}(x\wedge y)=d_{w}(x)\wedge y+(-1)^{\widetilde{x}}x\wedge d_{w}y
. Then
d_{w}^{2}=0

if and only if
w
defines the Lie algebra structure on

V
.

Proof:
[
CE
]
.

Definition 4.2:
The complex
V^{*}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d_{w}}}{{{\>\longrightarrow\>}}}\Lambda^{2}V^{*}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d_{w}}}{{{\>\longrightarrow\>}}}\Lambda^{3}V^{*}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d_{w}}}{{{\>\longrightarrow\>}}}...
is called
the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex
of the Lie algebra
(V,[\cdot,\cdot]_{w})
.

Remark 4.3:
We identify the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex
of the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
with the complex of left-invariant differential
forms in its Lie group
G
. This defines a natural
homomorphism of DGA
(\Lambda^{*}{\mathfrak{g}}^{*},d_{w})\to(\Lambda^{*}(G/\Gamma),d)
for
any discrete subgroup
\Gamma\subset G
.

Theorem 4.4: (Nomizu)

Let
M=G/\Gamma
be a nilmanifold.
Then the natural map

(\Lambda^{*}{\mathfrak{g}}^{*},d_{w})\to(\Lambda^{*}(G/\Gamma),d)

from its Chevalley-Eilenberg DG-algebra
to its Rham algebra is a quasi-isomorphism.

Proof:

[
No
]
.

Corollary 4.5:
Nilmanifolds, with exception of tori, are never formal.

Proof:
The Chevalley-Eilenberg DG-algebras
of finite dimensional nilpotent Lie algebras are never formal,

[
Ha1
, Theorem 1]
.

4.2
Dolbeault cohomology of an algebra

Definition 4.6:
Let
({\mathfrak{g}},I)
be a complex structure on a nilpotent Lie
algebra,
d_{w}:\;\Lambda^{*}({\mathfrak{g}}^{*})\to\Lambda^{*+1}({\mathfrak{g}}^{*})

its Chevalley-Eilenberg differential, and

d_{w}=\partial_{w}+\overline{\partial}_{w}
its Hodge decomposition.
The cohomology of the complex
(\Lambda^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}({\mathfrak{g}}^{*}),\overline{\partial}_{w})

is called
the Dolbeault cohomology of the
Lie algebra
.

Remark 4.7:
In
4.1
we have
established a correspondence
between the differentials
d_{w}:\;\Lambda^{*}({\mathfrak{g}}^{*})\to\Lambda^{*+1}({\mathfrak{g}}^{*})

and the Lie algebra structures on
{\mathfrak{g}}
. It is natural to ask
which bracket corresponds to
\partial_{w}
and
\overline{\partial}_{w}
.

Proposition 4.8:

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},I)
be a complex structure on a nilpotent Lie
algebra, and
\overline{\partial}_{w}:\;\Lambda^{*,*}({\mathfrak{g}}^{*})\to\Lambda^{*,*+1}({\mathfrak{g}}^{*})

its Dolbeault differential. Then
\overline{\partial}_{w}
corresponds
to the following bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\overline{\partial}_{w}}
on
{\mathfrak{g}}
: given two
vectors
x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{1,0}
and two vectors
x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{0,1}
,
we have
[x,y]_{\overline{\partial}_{w}}=0
,
[x,x^{\prime}]_{\overline{\partial}_{w}}=[x,x^{\prime}]^{1,0}
,
and
[x^{\prime},y^{\prime}]_{\partial_{w}}=[x^{\prime},y^{\prime}]
.

Proof:
The operator
\overline{\partial}_{w}
is the (0,1)-part of
d_{w}
, and
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\overline{\partial}_{w}}

is the dual map. In other words,
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\overline{\partial}_{w}}

is the Hodge part of the tensor
[\cdot,\cdot]\in\operatorname{Hom}(\Lambda_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathbb{C}})

which takes a bivector of Hodge type
(1,1)
to
(1,0)

and a bivector of Hodge type
(0,2)
to
(0,1)
.

Proposition 4.9:
Let
({\mathfrak{g}},I)
be a complex structure on a nilpotent Lie algebra,
d_{w}=\partial_{w}+\overline{\partial}_{w}
its Chevalley-Eilenberg differential. Then
({\mathfrak{g}}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathbb{C}},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\overline{\partial}_{w}})
is a complex nilpotent Lie algebra.

Proof:
Since
d_{w}^{2}\equiv 0
, also
\overline{\partial}_{w}^{2}\equiv 0
, which is equivalent to the Jacobi identity for
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\overline{\partial}_{w}}
. For every
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathbb{C}}
,
\text{ad}(x)
is nilpotent, so every
\text{ad}_{\overline{\partial}_{w}}(x)
is also nilpotent.

Corollary 4.10:
The DGA
(\Lambda^{*,*}({\mathfrak{g}}^{*}),\overline{\partial}_{w})
is formal
if and only
{\mathfrak{g}}
is abelian. The DGA

(\Lambda^{0,*}({\mathfrak{g}}^{*}),\overline{\partial}_{w})
is formal
if and only
{\mathfrak{g}}^{0,1}
is abelian.

Proof:
Follows immediately from the previous propositions and

[
Ha1
, Theorem 1]
.

4.3
Console-Fino theorem

We start by restating
3.1
,
in order to emphasize the rational structure on the
Lie algebra of a nilmanifold.

Theorem 4.11:
Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a real nilpotent Lie algebra,
and
\Gamma\subset G
a lattice in its Lie group.
Then
{\mathfrak{g}}
admits an integer lattice,
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbb{R}}
,
closed under the commutator,
such that
\Gamma
is commensurable with
e^{{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{Z}}}
.
Conversely, for any such integer subalgebra

{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{Z}}\subset{\mathfrak{g}}
such that
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbb{R}}
, the subgroup

e^{{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{Z}}}
is a lattice in
G
. Finally,
the rational lattice

{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{Q}}:={\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbb{Q}}
is unambiguously determined by
the lattice
\Gamma\subset G
.

Proof:
[
Ma
]
.

Definition 4.12:
Let
I
be an invariant complex structure on a
nilmanifold
G/\Gamma
. We say that
I
is
rational

if it preserves the rational lattice

{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{Q}}\subset{\mathfrak{g}}
defined by
\Gamma
as above.

Theorem 4.13:
(Console-Fino)

Let
I
be a rational complex structure on a Lie algebra,
and
(M,I)
the corresponding complex nilmanifold. Then
the natural DGA homomorphism
(\Lambda^{*}({\mathfrak{g}}),\overline{\partial}_{w})\to(\Lambda^{*}(M),\overline{\partial})
is a quasi-isomorphism.

Proof:
[
CF
]
.

Remark 4.14:
In this case, the Dolbeault algebra

(\Lambda^{*}(M),\overline{\partial})
is formal if and only if

(\Lambda^{*}({\mathfrak{g}}^{*}),\overline{\partial}_{w})
is formal.
In the next section, we are going to prove that the Dolbeault algebra

(\Lambda^{*}(M),\overline{\partial})
is not formal
even when the Console-Fino theorem cannot be applied.

5
Domination of DG-algebras and formality of Dolbeault DGA

5.1
Domination of DG-algebras

Definition 5.1:
We say that a DG-algebra
(A^{*},d)

admits Poincaré duality

if
A^{i}=0
for
i<0
and
i>n
,
H^{n}(A^{*})={\mathbb{C}}
,
and the product map
H^{i}(A^{*})\times H^{n-i}(A^{*})\to H^{n}(A^{*})={\mathbb{C}}

defines a non-degenerate pairing in cohomology.

Definition 5.2:
Let
\Psi:\;(B^{*},d)\to(A^{*},d)
be a morphism of DG-algebras,
such that
(B^{*},d)
admits Poincaré duality,
and the map
H^{n}(B^{*})\to H^{n}(A^{*})
is injective.
Then we say that
A^{*}
dominates
B^{*}
and

\Psi
is called
a domination
.

Example 5.3:
A proper fibration
f:\;X\to Y
to an
n
-manifold
Y
admitting a section
defines a surjection
H_{n}(X)\to H_{n}(Y)
, hence
it is injective on cohomology; in this case

(\Lambda^{*}(X),d)
dominates
(\Lambda^{*}(Y),d)
.

Theorem 5.4: (Milivojevic, Stelzig, Zoller)

Let
\Psi:\;(B^{*},d)\to(A^{*},d)
be a domination of DGA.
Assume that
(B^{*},d)
is not formal. Then
(A^{*},d)
is not formal.

Proof:
[
MSZ
, Theorem A]
.

5.2
The Dolbeault DGA of a nilmanifold dominates

Serre’s duality immediately implies that
the Dolbeault algebra
(\Lambda^{*}M,\overline{\partial})

admits a non-degenerate pairing,
taking values in
H^{n,n}_{\overline{\partial}}(M)={\mathbb{C}}
.
The same result for the Dolbeault cohomology
of the Lie algebra is also elementary.

Proposition 5.5:

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a nilpotent Lie algebra equipped with a
complex structure. Then
H^{n,n}_{\overline{\partial}}(\Lambda^{*,*}{\mathfrak{g}}^{*})={\mathbb{C}}
.
Moreover, the multiplication

H^{p,q}_{\overline{\partial}}(\Lambda^{*,*}{\mathfrak{g}}^{*})\times H^{n-p,n-q}_{\overline{\partial}}(\Lambda^{*,*}{\mathfrak{g}}^{*})\to H^{n,n}_{\overline{\partial}}(\Lambda^{*,*}{\mathfrak{g}}^{*})={\mathbb{C}}

defines a non-degenerate pairing.

Proof:
[
Kos
]
.

Using the terminology introduced in Subsection

5.1
, we say that
these DGAs
admit Poincaré duality
.

Corollary 5.6:
Let
(M=G/\Gamma)
be a complex nilmanifold, and

{\mathfrak{g}}
its Lie algebra. Then the natural
DGA morphism
(\Lambda^{*}{\mathfrak{g}}^{*},\overline{\partial}_{w})\to(\Lambda^{*}M,\overline{\partial})

is a domination.

Proof:
Follows immediately from
5.2
.

Corollary 5.7:

The Dolbeault DGA of a complex nilmanifold
M
is never formal,
unless
M
is a torus.

Proof:
Since
(\Lambda^{*}{\mathfrak{g}}^{*},\overline{\partial})\to(\Lambda^{*}M,\overline{\partial})

is a domination, and
(\Lambda^{*}{\mathfrak{g}}^{*},\overline{\partial})

is not formal,
[
MSZ
]
imply that
(\Lambda^{*}M,\overline{\partial})

is also not formal.

5.3
Dolbeault formality for
\Lambda^{*,0}(M)

It remains to prove
1
.
A complex structure on a Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
is called

abelian
if the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}^{1,0}
is abelian.
There is a great body of research on abelian complex structures
(
[
BD
,
ABD
,
CFU
]
),
which are better understood than the general complex structures
(for instance, the Console-Fino isomorphism holds for
abelian complex structures without extra assumptions,

[
CF
, Remark 4]
, see
also
[
CFGU3
]
)

Theorem 5.8:

Let
(M,I)
be a nilmanifold with the DGA
(\Lambda^{0,*}(M),\overline{\partial})

formal. Then the corresponding complex structure on the Lie algebra is abelian.

Proof:
As shown in
[
BDV
]
,
the canonical bundle of a nilmanifold is trivial.
Therefore, Serre’s duality implies that
the DGA
(\Lambda^{0,*}(M),\overline{\partial})

admits Poincaré duality.
This implies, in particular,
that the natural map

(\Lambda^{0,*}({\mathfrak{g}}),\overline{\partial})\to(\Lambda^{0,*}(M),\overline{\partial})

is a domination. Therefore, formality of

(\Lambda^{0,*}(M),\overline{\partial})
implies formality of

(\Lambda^{0,*}({\mathfrak{g}}),\overline{\partial})
.
However,
(\Lambda^{0,*}({\mathfrak{g}}),\overline{\partial})
is the
Chevalley-Eilenberg complex of the Lie
algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}^{0,1}
. By
[
Ha1
, Theorem 1]
,
it is formal if and only if it is
abelian.

Synergistic effects of boron and zinc foliar applications on growth and post-harvest storage attributes of potato (Solanum tuberosum L.) cultivar Argana
Authors: Muhanad A. Ahmad, Badran S. Agha, Ahmed I. Y. Alabade, Fathel F. R. Ibraheem, Ayad Hani Alalaf, Ayad T. Shayal Alalam, Ahmed T. Al-Abbasi, Yusra Mohammed Saleh, Esraa Abd-Alhuseein Jasim, Heidar Meftahizade
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The current investigation considers the synergistic effects of boron (B) and zinc (Zn) on tuber development, tuber quality, and nutrient status prior to and after storage periods. The introduction of B (50 and 100 mg l⁻¹) and Zn (500 and 1000 mg l⁻¹) levels, applied separately and in combination, examined their effect on growth attributes, tuber yield, firmness, storage quality and nutrient levels. The combination of B (100 mg l⁻¹) and Zn (1000 mg l⁻¹) produced the greatest increase in plant height (33.5%), number of aboveground stems (112.6%), leaf area (60.3%), and tuber yield (128.7%), which was more than each treatment alone and a statistically significant number. In addition, this combination caused minimal weight loss, increased tuber firmness, and lower total soluble solids (TSS) accumulation by the storage period maintained tuber quality. Importantly, the double micronutrient combination increased dry matter by 18% and specific gravity (unless noted before storage 1.14; after storage 1.68), which suggests an opportunity to improve tuber density (quality). Additionally, total starch content also significantly increased by 57.7% after storage. The combined application of B and Zn had a marked positive effect on total tuber B content before and after storage. The results indicate that the application of B and Zn played a beneficial role in tuber nutritional and physiological quality, resulting in improvement in tuber storability, quality, overall health benefits from starch production to yield increase in potato production.

Introduction

Potato (Solanum tuberosum L.) is first among the world’s four most important and economically significant food commodities after wheat, rice and maize [1]. Potato is an important food identified as one of the most consumed foods, and is a major food crop that has important contributions to dietary calories and carbohydrates sources and acts as a regular source of dietary nutrients: protein, vitamins (A, B, C) and mineral nutrients (potassium, phosphorus, magnesium) affirming how important potato crops are to food security at the global level [2]. Nonetheless, potatoes are soil-depleting crops and have a relatively short growing season to heighten the amount of nutrients they require that are associated with their fast vegetative growth. Consequently, effective nutrient management strategies are essential for crop nutrient management. Recent literature suggests that foliar fertilizers and method of application can be utilized to correct nutrient deficiency and could lead to improved growth and yield traits, especially with respect to worse micronutrients [3–5].

Foliar nutrition is a highly effective process for nutrient delivery, especially Zn and B micronutrients, to increase plant growth and productivity [6]. Nutrients are taken through the leaves and vegetative tissues instead of roots through soil fertilization methods associated with nutrient uptake. Foliar applications of micronutrients will hasten their absorption into important metabolic pathways. Foliar applications will stimulate tissue growth, cell division and improve the photosynthetic process, which leads to higher yields and enhanced quality [7]. In addition, since foliar applications are taken up laterally by the plant, micronutrients help stabilize the physiological action of amino acids, proteins and plant hormones that are also necessary for plant growth [8].

B is a fundamental micronutrient that has many important functions, including cell wall structure, cell division, and the translocation of sugars to tuber starch storage tissues [9]. B has also been shown to help regulate carbohydrate metabolism and be involved in auxin synthesis, which is an essential plant growth regulator. Several studies have reported about B deficiency negatively impacting root and shoot development that limits yield and tuber quality [10, 11].

Zn is yet another essential micronutrient that supports plant growth and metabolism. Zn is an important element of a number of enzymes involved in amino acid synthesis and protein metabolism, as well as regulating metabolic pathways involved in starch synthesis and sugar oxidation [12]. Furthermore, Zn plays an important role in chlorophyll synthesis which is directly related to photosynthesis and general plant productivity. More specifically, Zn is imperative to key physiological processes such as flowering and development or morphology of seeds, which ultimately improves crop yield and quality [13, 14]. The study aims to evaluate the interaction effects of a single foliar application of B and Zn on growth, yield, quality and storage quality of potato cultivar Argana. The findings of this study will provide important information of the potential for nutrient management to improve yield and storage quality of potatoes. The practice of improving agronomic practices with micronutrients has significant potential to improve yield and post-harvest quality of potatoes, not only enhancing food security, but also improving producers’ economic returns.

Materials and methods

Experimental setup and agronomic practices

The experiment was conducted at a vegetables field at the College of Agriculture and Forestry, Mosul University during spring season 2023. Before planting, the physical- chemical properties of soil (Table 1) were analysed based on a surface soil composite sample (0–30 cm depth). Soil preparation was conducted by implementing two perpendicular disc-plowing operations. Each experimental unit comprised of a 3 m long and 75 cm wide ridge, for a total plot area of 2.25 m². Tubers were planted at the end of February, 30 cm apart and at a depth 15 cm with 10 plants per ridge using drip irrigation system.

Cultivar and agronomic practices

The experiment was conducted using the cultivar Argana, a medium-maturing, high-yield Danish variety (100–120 days) tubers large, fairly uniformly, round to oval, and have light-yellow skin, yellow flesh, and shallow-eyed. During the growing season, standard agronomic practices such as fertilization, weeding, hilling, and preventive and curative measures for pests, diseases, and weeds were implemented.

The certified seed tubers of Solanum tuberosum L. cv. Argana were originally developed in Denmark and classified as Class A. The seed material used in this study was obtained from the Agricultural Research Center, Mosul, Iraq, which provided certified and disease-free seed tubers. The identification of the cultivar was confirmed and approved by Dr. Muhanad A. Ahmad. As the plant material was commercially cultivated and not collected from the wild, no specific permissions were required.

Experimental treatments

This research examined the effects of two treated factors on potato growth and yield:

Foliar sprays were applied in the early morning before the onset of transpiration. The first spray was applied one month after transplanting, followed by two additional applications every 15 days. Each plant received approximately 100 mL of foliar solution per application, following commonly reported volumes in similar studies to ensure proper nutrient uptake. In the combined treatment, B and Zn were applied together as a single foliar spray solution to each plant. The experiment was conducted with three replications.

Harvesting and storage

Harvesting was performed at the yellowing stage of the lower leaves, 118 days after planting. Tubers were harvested manually using a hand fork. Post-harvest, tubers were cured in a shaded area at 15 °C for two weeks. After curing, tubers were packed into bags (5 kg per treatment per replicate) and stored in a cold chamber at 4.4 °C with 90–95% relative humidity for four months. Storage conditions, including temperature and humidity, were continuously monitored using a calibrated thermohygrometer to ensure stable conditions throughout the storage period.

Studied traits

Growth and yield parameters

The following variables associated with plant growth and yield were evaluated: plant height (cm plant⁻¹), taken from the soil surface to the apical leaf of five plants; number of aerial stems (stems plant⁻¹), counted on five plants; leaf area (cm² leaf⁻¹), measured using the leaf tracing method described by Patton and Jones [15]; yield per plant (kg plant⁻¹), determined by dividing the experiment unit’s total yield by the number of plants; total yield (tons ha⁻¹), estimated by dividing the yield of each experimental unit by area and multiplying the yield by 10,000.

Experimental design and statistical analysis

The study was carried out as a randomized complete block design (RCBD) with a total of three replications. The statistical analyses were performed using SAS version 9.4, and means comparison was done using the least significant difference (LSD) test at a significance of p ≤ 0.05. The hierarchical clustering and Pearson correlation analyses were conducted in R software (version 4.2.3) with appropriate packages. The Principal Component Analysis (PCA) were generated with the SRplot online website (https://www.bioinformatics.com.cn/en).

Results and discussion

Key growth improvements with Boron and zinc

Growth characteristics were improved with the application of B and Zn, either alone or in combination. Specifically, Zn improved plant height by 9.1% when applied at 500 mg l⁻¹ and 15.4% when applied at 1000 mg l⁻¹ relative to the control plants. Likewise, B treatments improved plant height at 50 mg l⁻¹ (2.9%) and 100 mg l⁻¹ (17.5%). The greatest increase (33.5%) with respect to the control was achieved, with the combined application of 100 mg l⁻¹ B to 1000 mg l⁻¹ Zn recorded (Fig. 1a). Similarly, the highest increase in terms of aerial stems was observed in the combination of 100 mg l⁻¹ B, to 1000 mg l⁻¹ Zn in this study of 112.6% higher than the control (Fig. 1b). Based on the findings, the control treatment exhibited the least leaf area per leaf, which averaged 30.74 cm², whereas the greatest leaf area was identified in the combined treatment of 100 mg l⁻¹ of B and 1000 mg l⁻¹ of Zn, averaging 49.33 cm² (Fig. 1c). Moreover, the combined 100 mg l⁻¹ B and 1000 mg l⁻¹ Zn treatment produced the most positive enhancement towards total number of tubers, which showed an increase of 82.9% when compared to control treatment (Fig. 1d). Zn, as micronutrient, plays a role in the synthesis of growth hormones and enzyme activity which stimulate cell division and growth [19], while B supports the development of plant organs by maintaining the structure of the cell wall and regulating hormonal metabolism [20]. In a research study conducted in Bangladesh, foliar application of two kilograms of B per hectare and three kilograms of Zn per hectare increased the tuber yield to 4.89 tons per hectare the maximum yield under the treatment [21]. Likewise, in a similar study in India, the foliar application of a combination of B, Zn, and manganese increased the tuber yield to 30.57 tons per hectare, leading to greater net profit than other treatments [22].

Boron and zinc synergy boosts yield

The results indicate that the treatment of 100 mg l⁻¹ B and 1000 mg l⁻¹ Zn produced the greatest significant increase in yield of a single plant, 1.63 kg or approximately 128.7% increase over the control. Application rates of Zn, 500 or 1000 mg l⁻¹, increased yield by 24.7% and 80.6%, respectively (Fig. 1e). Application of B at rates of 50 or 100 mg l⁻¹ increased yield by 34.3% or 24.3%, compared to the control, respectively (Fig. 1e). The treatment of 100 mg l⁻¹ B in combination with 1000 mg l⁻¹ Zn really produced the highest total yield at 72.24 tons per hectare, an increase of almost 129% over the control treatment (31.59 tons per hectare). (Fig. 1f). B plays a role in controlling the division of cells and growth hormones, in addition to improving nutrient uptake [20], whereas Zn activates enzymes and promotes photosynthesis, resulting in improved metabolism and growth of the plant [19]. Naeem et al. [23] provide an example of this, whereby a foliar spray co-applied with B (6 mg l⁻¹) resulted in increased maize yield. Furthermore, Saleem et al. [24] observed that B application resulted in yield percentages that were greater than the control (no B applied) for wheat. This indicates a need for simultaneous application of micronutrients. The concurrent application of B and Zn had a pronounced impact on broccoli yield and quality, leading to concomitant increases in vitamin C content, total chlorophyll, total sugars, reducing sugars, and crude protein content [25]. Likewise, Dhakad et al. [26] determined foliar application of B and Zn at stated dosages significantly improved yield and quality of cauliflower curd, which may be beneficial with respect to harvestable production. Manjunath and Debbarma [27] also mentioned that different combinations of Zn and B had a significant effect on growth and yield parameters of finger millet with increased concentrations of Zn and B. The simultaneous use of Zn and B, coupled with structural growing parameters for potato plants, improved yield properties above just Zn alone [28].

Boron and zinc combination halves weight loss

The results indicate that the combined treatment of 100 mg l⁻¹ B and 1000 mg l⁻¹ Zn had the best performance in reducing weight loss, decreasing it to nearly half of the amount observed in the control (Fig. 2a). The combination of B and Zn effectively reduces weight loss, which probably works by improving the function of enzymes and metabolic processes, increasing resistance to environmental stresses, and reducing oxidative damage in cells [29, 30]. In broccoli cultivation, the combined application of B to the soil and Zn as a foliar spray led to a notable improvement in head weigh [25]. Likewise, in tomato production, interacting B and Zn at 0.2% each were effective in fruit weight [31]. In the case of potato production, the application of Zn and B (Zn 5 g l−1 + B 2 g l−1) resulted in substantial gains in vigor of the plants, plant height, stem girth, and the number of stems per square meter. The weight and dry matter of tubers were not as great as or comparable to those of Zn alone [28]. In tomatoes, the application of tomato wax and nutrient solutions/reduced in B and Zn ngm reduced weight loss and maintained firmness of tomatoes in storage [32].

Boron and zinc boost tuber firmness before and after storage

The findings showed that tuber firmness prior to storage was least in the control treatment (12.87 lb in−2), while the application of micronutrients B and Zn, both alone and especially in combination, significantly enhanced firmness. The combined treatment of 50 mg B and 1000 mg l⁻¹ Zn produced the greatest firmness of 16.67 lb in−2 average firmness, showing the positive impact of this treatment on the quality and firmness of tubers before storage (Fig. 2b). After storage, tuber firmness remained lowest in the control group, with an average of 11.10 lb in−2. The application of Zn solely at 500 and 1000 mg l⁻¹ increased firmness to 13.47 and 15.17 lb in−2, respectively. In addition, the combined treatment of B and Zn also provided increased firmness greater than either treatment alone. There was no significant difference in firmness between the two B concentrations or one of the two Zn concentrations. Highest firmness after storage (15.23 lb in−2) was found when the tuber was treated with 100 mg l⁻¹ boron and 1000 mg l⁻¹ Zn (Fig. 2c). B increases the rigidity of the cell wall through pectin cross-linking, which increases firmness in tubers before and after storage [33]. Zn increases enzyme activity and protein synthesis while strengthening antioxidant defenses to help reduce oxidative stress and reduce damage to the cell structure [34]. The combined application of B, copper, and Zn in specific doses was found to enhance pungency, firmness, and sugar content in bulbs during storage, with the most recommended treatment being a combination of 1–4-1 kg.ha−1 of B, Copper, and Zn respectively [35]. Similarly, the application of B and Zn improved fruit firmness and reduced physiological loss in weight in papayas during storage [36]. Furthermore, research has shown that B applications up to certain levels can significantly increase the total tuber yield of sweet potatoes, affecting the carotene content and unit weight of root tubers [37].

Enhanced storage quality: boron and zinc lower tuber TSS levels

The highest TSS percentage in tubers prior to storage was observed in the control treatment (4.6%). Application of B and Zn, both individually and particularly in combination, significantly reduced TSS levels. The combination of B at 100 mg l⁻¹ with Zn at 1000 mg l⁻¹ produced the lowest TSS value (3.5%) which was 23.9% less than the control (Fig. 2d). After storage, the control treatment had the highest TSS percentage (6.32%), while the combined treatment of 100 mg l⁻¹ B and 1000 mg l⁻¹ Zn produced the lowest TSS at 4.3%, a reduction of 31.9% (Fig. 2e). Overall, the interaction of Zn and B can greatly influence the tuber TSS both pre-storage and post-storage. The reduction of TSS can be better understood through the additive and synergistic effects of Zn and B in lipid metabolism, which impact the accumulation of TSS and compounds that cause TSS [38]. In addition, investigations have indicated that the use of Zn and B not only significantly decreases T.S.S. but also improves the general storage quality of tubers by limiting nutrient losses and maintaining firmness over time [39]. A study demonstrated that combined application of ZnSO4 and borax resulted in a significant reduction of stored onion weight, along with an increase in TSS and onion shelf life compared to separate application of ZnSO4 or borax [40].

Reduced dry matter loss through Boron-Zinc combination

The data indicate that tubers in the control treatment had the lowest dry matter content before storage (14.1%). The individual application of B and Zn improved tuber dry matter content moderately for each treatment with B at 50 mg l⁻¹ (15.38% dry matter) and Zn at 1000 mg l⁻¹ (15.26% dry matter). The combination treatments resulted in a greater influence on dry matter content in tubers, with a combination treatment of both nutrients of B at 100 mg l⁻¹ and Zn at 1000 mg l⁻¹ providing the highest level of dry matter content at 16.64% representing an 18% increase in dry matter content in tubers compared to the control treatment (Fig. 3a). Likewise, upon storage, the control treatment displayed the lowest percentage of dry matter (10.77%), while application of Zn alone at concentrations of 500 and 1000 mg l⁻¹ raised the percentage to 12.75% and 13.40%, respectively. However, in terms of quantity of dry matter after storage, the greatest amount was also in the combination treatment of B at 100 mg l⁻¹ and Zn at 1000 mg l⁻¹ (14.41%), indicating a 33.8% increase over the control (Fig. 3b). More precisely, the combination of Zn and B has been reported to increase the dry matter content of tubers along with other nutrients, including nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, calcium, and proteins [9]. Additionally, this combination has been found to significantly enhance the quality traits of onions, such as protein content, vitamin C content, and dry matter content, while reducing physiological weight loss, sprouting, and rotting during storage [41]. In the study, Sharma et al. [22] the amount of dry matter in tubers increased to 250 g kg−1 when treated with a combination of B, Zn, and manganese. This treatment also resulted in the highest overall tuber yield and net profit. Also, in a study, the amount of dry matter in potato tubers showed the greatest increase when treated with Zn alone compared to other treatments, including a combination of Zn and B [28].

Improved tuber density with oron–Zinc application

The results showed that the control treatment had the lowest tuber specific gravity both before and after storage, with values of 1.051 g cm− 3 and 1.035 g cm− 3, respectively. Application of B and Zn, individually and especially in combination, significantly increased tuber specific gravity at both stages. Prior to storage, the highest specific gravity was noted (1.063 g cm− 3), and following the storage period the highest specific gravity was noted (1.0525 g cm− 3) during the combined treatment of 100 mg l⁻¹ B and 1000 mg l⁻¹ Zn treatment which was an increase of approximately 1.14% and 1.68% in specific gravity compared to the control (Fig. 3c, d). B enhances tuber specific gravity by improving cell wall integrity via cross-linking of pectin [42], and Zn provides support for enzyme activity and antioxidant protection to stabilize cellular structures [34]. The application of ZnSO4 in the foliage at certain periods resulted in a significant improvement in dry matter, specific gravity and starch content of potato tubers which could be a suitable measure to improve tuber quality in depleted Zn soil fields [43]. Simultaneously, the co-application of Zn and B improved the plant’s vigor and structural growth parameters although Zn alone was more effective in improving yield parameters such as tuber weight and specific gravity [28]. Additionally, the application of potassium silicate and nano- B via foliar applications significantly increased potato yield and quality measures, including specific gravity, in alkaline soils deficient in B [44].

Dual micronutrient strategy elevates tuber starch levels

The findings revealed that the control treatment had the lowest starch content in tubers both before and after storage, measuring 8.58% and 5.61%, respectively. Individual applications of 50 mg B and 1000 mg l⁻¹ Zn increased starch content before storage by approximately 13.3% and 12.1%, while after storage, these treatments enhanced starch content by about 31.9% and 41.7% compared to the control. Combined treatments had a more pronounced effect, with the highest starch content recorded in the treatment combining 100 mg l⁻¹ B and 1000 mg l⁻¹ Zn, reaching 10.84% before storage (26.4% increase) and 8.85% after storage (a 57.7% increase) relative to the control (Fig. 3e, f). Research on the effects of B and Zn on tuber starch content during storage shows that the use of these micronutrients can significantly affect the quality and storage characteristics of tubers. Studies have shown that the combined application of Zn and B enhances the storage quality of onions by improving various quality traits and reducing physiological weight loss, sprouting, and rotting [45]. Likewise, research has shown that applying these micronutrients to the leaves has been effective in enhancing yields and shelf-life of onions [40]. In the case of potatoes, the use of Zn and B had a favorable impact on growth qualities and yield parameters, including the overall vigor of the plant as measured by height, stem thickness, and stem density [28]. Furthermore, the study indicates that while the starch content in potatoes will decrease, the size and shape of the starch granules did not alter upon storage of tubers under cold conditions [46].

Enhanced tuber boron content by zinc-boron combination

The tuber B content was lowest in the control treatment both before and after storage, at 13.87 mg l⁻¹ and 9.79 mg l⁻¹, respectively. Individual applications of either Zn (1000 mg l⁻¹) or B (100 mg l⁻¹) significantly increased B concentrations, with values of 25.10 mg L⁻¹ and 15.50 mg l⁻¹ before storage and 18.77 mg l⁻¹ and 10.85 mg l⁻¹ after storage. Importantly, the combination treatments of B and Zn exhibited greater effects the greatest B concentration in the combined treatment of 100 mg l⁻¹ B and 1000 mg l⁻¹ Zn had a B concentration of 31.27 mg l⁻¹ before storage and 23.45 mg l⁻¹ after storage (Fig. 4a, b). It is possible that the increase in B content achieved with combined treatment with Zn may be attributable to the interaction of Zn with B whereby Zn facilitates B uptake through enhanced root activity and transport [47]. The elements contribute to increased B concentration in plant tissues, as B and Zn are essential to cell wall stability and stress responses. In the current study, the results indicated that Zn application significantly increased B inflow in plants, perhaps by improving root activity and subsequently improving B transport to above-ground tissues [48]. Additionally, the interaction between Zn and B at the root level, improving nutrient uptake efficiency and ultimately plant health, supports [20]. Furthermore, the association of applied B and Zn was reported to enhance plant growth and nutrient content, as both nutrients are constituents of the cell wall and involved in the plant response to stress which is supported by Tavallali et al. [49].

Synergistic boost: Boron and zinc enhance tuber nutrient content

The analysis indicates that each of the treatments significantly increased B concentration in tubers over the control treatment (14.24 mg l⁻¹) across all treatments with the highest increase being the combination of 100 mg l⁻¹ B and 1000 mg l⁻¹ Zn at 30.33 mg l⁻¹ which was a + 113% increase over the control. The second highest increase was the combination of 100 mg B and 500 mg Zn (79.1%). The next two treatments were 100 mg l⁻¹ B (74.4%) and 1000 mg l⁻¹ Zn (70.9%). All treatments also increased Zn concentration in tubers over the control (12.10 mg l⁻¹) after the storage period. The maximum level of Zn occurred with the combination of 100 mg l⁻¹ B and 1000 mg l⁻¹ Zn (25.66 mg l⁻¹, 112% increase). Significant increases in Zn content also occurred with the combination of 100 mg B and 500 mg Zn (84.4%), and with individual applications of B (72.5%) and Zn (70.9%) (Fig. 4c, d). The interaction between B and Zn may promote better distribution of available nutrients within the plant, which may increase the concentration of both micronutrients within the tubers. These findings are consistent with Safdar et al. [50], who selected that foliar application of B and Zn improved nutrient content and subsequently plant performance. Similarly, either B or Zn foliar application, also significantly increased their individual concentrations, which supports the idea that both B and Zn are essential nutrients to improving nutrient content and overall plant performance [51].

Heatmap and PCA

The correlation analysis from the heatmap showed a significant positive correlation with single plant yield of the B 100 mg l⁻¹ + Zn 500 mg l⁻¹ and B 100 mg l⁻¹ + Zn 1000 mg l⁻¹ treatments, suggesting a synergism of both elements on improving plant performance (Fig. 5a). Furthermore, tuber firmness after storage had a significant and positive correlation with Zn 500 mg l⁻¹ and Zn 1000 mg l⁻¹ treatments, and tuber firmness before storage also had a positive and significant correlation with both Zn treatments. These results corroborate the important role of Zn applied as controlled-release fertilizers, in the pre-colonization and post-colonization quality of tubers. In addition, dry matter concentration of tubers before storage had a significant positive correlation with the treatments of B 100 mg l⁻¹, B 50 mg l⁻¹, and Zn 500 mg l⁻¹ indicating that although individual low treatment did not alter the chemical profile of tubers, it did have beneficial impact on chemical properties (Fig. 5a).

Conclusion

This research highlights the efficacy of augmenting tuber growth, yield, and post-harvest quality by using B and Zn in synergy. The application of B (100 mg l⁻¹) in combination with Zn (1000 mg l⁻¹) significantly increased plant height, stem count, leaf area, and yield. There was reduced weight loss, increased firmness, and lower total TSS, preserving tuber quality even during post-harvest storage. Moreover, the treatment increased the dry matter content, tuber density, starch accumulation, and mineral content, before and after storage. The findings of this study demonstrate the potential of B and Zn as an effective approach to enhance tuber productivity, storage quality, and nutritional quality of potatoes in a modern agricultural context.

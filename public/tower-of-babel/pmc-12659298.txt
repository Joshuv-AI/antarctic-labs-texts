Parkinson’s disease: an integrative bioinformatics and machine learning analysis reveals tryptophan metabolism-associated gene biomarkers
Authors: Jiarui Zhang, Man Zheng, Wei Shi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundParkinson’s disease (PD) represents a prevalent neurodegenerative disorder among the aging population, characterized by significant disruptions in neurological metabolic pathways. Recent evidence highlights the crucial role of tryptophan metabolism in the pathogenesis of PD; however, a comprehensive exploration of tryptophan metabolism genes (TMGs) and their specific contributions remains largely uncharted.MethodsEmploying bioinformatics, this study aimed to identify and validate TMGs associated with PD. A differential expression analysis was performed on a carefully selected set of four candidate genes. GSEA and GSVA were utilized to elucidate the biological functions and pathways linked to these TMGs. Additionally, Lasso regression and SVM-RFE were implemented to identify key hub genes and assess the diagnostic potential of three TMGs in distinguishing PD from non-PD samples. The relationship between critical TMGs and clinical parameters was also investigated, with expression validation conducted using datasets GSE6613 and GSE7621.ResultsOur analysis identified three TMGs—ALDH9A1, CYP1A1, and CYP1B1—as significantly associated with PD. These genes are implicated in essential biological processes, including the catabolism of small molecules, fatty acid metabolism, and alcohol metabolism, highlighting their extensive functional relevance in PD. Moreover, the diagnostic efficacy of these TMGs in differentiating PD from control samples showed promising results.ConclusionsThis study identifies three TMGs with substantial associations to PD, enhancing our molecular understanding of the disease. These findings not only contribute to the elucidation of PD at the molecular level but also pave the way for the development of novel biomarkers.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12883-025-04489-7.Keywords: Parkinson’s disease, Fatty acid metabolism genes, Lasso regression, SVM-RFE, Bioinformatics

Introduction

Parkinson's disease (PD), the second most common neurodegenerative disorder after Alzheimer’s disease, affects approximately 1.2% of individuals over 65 years of age, primarily afflicting the elderly with a median onset around 60 years [1]. Aging stands as the most significant risk factor for PD, which is fundamentally a disorder of motor coordination resulting from the degeneration of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra (SN) [2]. Clinically, PD is characterized by hallmark symptoms including resting tremors, bradykinesia, rigidity, and postural instability, alongside other motor dysfunctions such as compromised gait, speech, swallowing, and facial expressivity [3]. Beyond these, the disease is increasingly recognized for its non-motor symptoms, including depression, constipation, and sleep disturbances, which can profoundly affect patients' quality of life, underscoring the critical need for research aimed at mitigating these complications [4]. The etiology of PD is multifaceted, with genetic predispositions, environmental factors, aging, and oxidative stress all implicated in the degenerative process affecting dopaminergic neurons [5]. This review endeavors to encapsulate the current understanding of PD, with a particular focus on elucidating the role of inflammation associated with the disease [6]. There is an imperative demand for continued investigation into the intricate relationship between immune responses and PD pathophysiology to identify precise biomarkers and develop targeted therapeutic interventions [7]. In this context, the early detection of PD-specific molecular markers becomes crucial, potentially enabling interventions that precede the onset of motor symptoms and heralding a transformative approach to the management and prognosis of PD.

Tryptophan, an essential amino acid for mammals, requires dietary intake due to the absence of endogenous synthesis capabilities. While its primary function is in protein synthesis, tryptophan's metabolic journey significantly broadens, bifurcating into two critical pathways [8]: the serotonin pathway, leading to melatonin synthesis, and the kynurenine pathway, culminating in the production of niacin derivatives. These metabolic trajectories produce a plethora of bioactive intermediates that are essential in modulating neural function, immune responses, and a variety of metabolic activities. The kynurenine pathway, in particular, plays a dominant role in the metabolic disposition of tryptophan, accounting for over 95% of its catabolism [9]. Initiated by the conversion of tryptophan to kynurenine through the action of the rate-limiting enzymes indoleamine 2,3-dioxygenase (IDO) and tryptophan 2,3-dioxygenase (TDO), this pathway transcends its metabolic function to act as a critical mediator in anti-inflammatory immune signaling pathways [10]. The exploration of these metabolic routes, especially in the context of chronic diseases such as PD, holds not only scientific intrigue but also significant therapeutic promise. Such investigations could herald a shift in the therapeutic landscape for PD, highlighting the pivotal role of tryptophan metabolism in both the research and clinical domains [11]. This exploration promises not only to deepen our understanding of disease mechanisms but also to pave the way for novel therapeutic strategies that leverage the intricate interplay between metabolism and neurodegeneration [12]. The PD Initiative, combining high-throughput transcriptome sequencing with comprehensive clinical data, represents a breakthrough in scientific exploration, offering an unparalleled perspective on the transcriptional and molecular complexities of PD [13–15]. Despite substantial advances in bioinformatics that have illuminated PD's intricate pathophysiology, the role of TMGs remains inadequately understood. This study seeks to close this gap by leveraging PD-related datasets from the GEO to unravel the influence of TMGs in PD etiology (Fig. 1). Beyond molecular characterization, our research aims to identify novel therapeutic targets, providing insights into the transcriptional regulation of PD.

Framework

Materials and methods

The methodologies proposed by Zi-Xuan Wu et al. in 2023 were employed in this study [16].

Raw data acquisition, preprocessing, and differential gene expression analysis

To elucidate the molecular mechanisms central to this study, we integrated publicly available transcriptomic datasets from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO), specifically GSE6613 and GSE7621, generated on the GPL96 and GPL570 platforms, respectively. GSE6613 was designated as the training dataset, while GSE7621 served as an independent validation cohort, thereby ensuring the robustness and reproducibility of our analyses (Table 1). In parallel, a curated list of TMGs was retrieved from the Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB), providing a comprehensive reference set for functional enrichment (Table S1). Transcriptomic profiles were carefully curated and preprocessed with Perl scripts to align mRNA expression data to human genome annotation files, ensuring accurate dataset structuring for downstream analysis. The GSE6613 dataset was normalized to minimize technical variation and improve cross-sample comparability. Differentially expressed genes were identified using the limma package under stringent thresholds (FDR < 0.05 and |log₂FC|≥ 1), yielding a set of statistically robust candidates. To further characterize molecular interactions, Pearson’s correlation coefficients were computed to assess gene–gene associations, and correlation networks were visualized with the corrplot package, offering insight into the regulatory interconnections shaping transcriptional landscapes.

Functional enrichment analysis

To elucidate the biological relevance of the DEGs, we performed comprehensive functional enrichment analyses integrating Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) annotations. GO analysis delineated the molecular functions (MF), biological processes (BP), and cellular components (CC) associated with the DEGs, whereas KEGG pathway mapping placed these genes within established signaling cascades, thereby revealing potential mechanistic networks implicated in disease pathophysiology. Functional annotation was conducted using a robust suite of R packages, including clusterProfiler, org.Hs.eg.db, enrichplot, and ggplot2, ensuring statistical rigor and reproducibility. To capture pathway activity beyond discrete gene-level changes, we further applied Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) and Gene Set Variation Analysis (GSVA). GSEA identified significantly enriched functions and pathways across phenotypic subgroups, highlighting those disproportionately represented in specific risk categories. By contrast, GSVA enabled sample-level assessment of pathway activity, offering a more nuanced view of molecular heterogeneity. These analyses, executed within a rigorous statistical framework, provided complementary perspectives on the regulatory architecture governing disease progression and risk stratification. Collectively, the integration of GO, KEGG, GSEA, and GSVA established a multidimensional annotation framework, yielding critical insights into the molecular mechanisms underlying the observed transcriptional patterns and illuminating key pathways that may shape disease biology.

Predictive model construction and immune cell infiltration analysis

To develop a robust predictive framework, we implemented Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (Lasso) regression using the glmnet package, incorporating cross-validation to enhance model stability and mitigate overfitting. This approach enabled the selection of key feature genes by penalizing less informative variables, thereby refining the predictive power of the model. In parallel, we employed Support Vector Machine-Recursive Feature Elimination (SVM-RFE), leveraging the e1071 package to construct an optimized machine learning model. The generalization performance of the SVM model was rigorously evaluated through cross-validation, systematically assessing model error rates and predictive accuracy to ensure its reliability. Both Lasso regression and SVM-RFE yielded a prioritized ranking of influential feature genes, offering mechanistic insights into their potential roles in disease pathogenesis. To further dissect the immune microenvironment, we employed the CIBERSORT algorithm to quantify the composition and infiltration levels of immune cell populations in each sample. This computational deconvolution approach enabled the precise characterization of immune cell subsets, providing a high-resolution landscape of immune infiltration patterns and their potential contributions to disease progression. By integrating predictive modeling with immune profiling, this study offers a comprehensive framework for uncovering key molecular signatures and immune dysregulation in the disease context.

Drug-gene interaction prediction and identification of common miRNAs and lncRNAs

With the advent of bioinformatics-driven precision medicine, biomarker discovery has become central to the diagnosis and treatment of complex disorders such as PD. To explore potential therapeutic targets, we systematically assessed drug–gene interactions between identified hub genes and small-molecule compounds using the Drug–Gene Interaction Database (DGIdb) (https://dgidb.genome.wustl.edu/). This analysis enabled the mapping of pharmacological modulators to key regulatory genes implicated in PD pathogenesis, thereby establishing a translational bridge between biomarker identification and targeted therapy. The detection of drugs capable of modulating these hub genes provides a conceptual framework for developing novel therapeutic strategies aimed at improving treatment efficacy and advancing personalized medicine. To further elucidate the post-transcriptional regulatory landscape, we constructed an integrative mRNA–miRNA–lncRNA interaction network, leveraging experimentally validated interactions curated in miRTarBase and PrognoScan. This network revealed critical non-coding RNA regulatory hubs with potential roles in PD pathogenesis. Visualization in Cytoscape provided a high-resolution depiction of these complex molecular relationships, highlighting the intricate architecture through which non-coding RNAs orchestrate gene expression dynamics. Collectively, this framework underscores the central role of non-coding RNAs in shaping PD progression and identifies pivotal regulatory nodes with therapeutic potential. By linking drug–gene interactions with post-transcriptional regulation, our findings not only illuminate molecular mechanisms underlying PD but also present promising avenues for drug development and precision medicine.

Molecular docking of active components with target proteins

To validate the reliability of the predicted core targets and their interactions with principal bioactive components, we conducted a systematic molecular docking analysis. CYP1A1 and CYP1B1 were selected as representative targets. High-resolution three-dimensional (3D) protein structures were obtained from UniProt and the RCSB PDB, restricted to X-ray crystallography entries to ensure structural accuracy. The corresponding two-dimensional (2D) structures of six predicted active compounds were retrieved from PubChem and subjected to geometry optimization with Chem3D, minimizing structural energy to enhance docking precision. Preprocessing of target proteins—including dehydration, hydrogenation, and ligand separation—was performed using PyMOL 2.4. Molecular docking was initiated with SYBYL 2.0, and compounds with TotalScore > 3 were selected for refined docking. Docking grids were subsequently generated with AutoDockTools 1.5.6, and binding simulations were executed using AutoDock Vina 1.1.2. For each docking experiment, the lowest binding energy conformation was chosen as the optimal pose. Comparative analyses with co-crystallized ligands were performed to confirm docking accuracy, and key intermolecular forces—including hydrophobic interactions, cation–π and anion–π interactions, π–π stacking, and hydrogen bonding—were systematically evaluated. The docking outcomes, visualized in PyMOL 2.4, provided detailed structural insights into the recognition mechanisms governing compound–protein interactions. These findings establish a molecular framework for understanding how bioactive components engage core targets, thereby highlighting their potential therapeutic relevance.

Mendelian randomization analysis

To ensure the independence of exposure and outcome variables in our genome-wide association study (GWAS) summary data, we engaged in an association analysis via the TwoSampleMR package in R. Designating CYP1B1 and CYP1A1-related expression as the exposure and PD as the outcome, we aimed to explore potential causal relationships. The analysis entailed: 1. Instrumental Variables (IVs) Configuration: CYP1B1 and CYP1A1-related expressions were screened with a P-value threshold of < 5 × 10^−8 to identify strongly associated exposures. 2. Independence Configuration: Linkage disequilibrium (LD) between SNPs was calculated using the PLINK clustering method, excluding SNPs with LD coefficient r^2 > 0.001 and within 10,000 kb to ensure SNP independence and reduce pleiotropic biases. 3. Statistical Strength Configuration: The robustness of instrumental variables was assessed using the F-statistic (F = β^2/SE^2), with variables having F < 10 deemed inadequate to mitigate confounding effects.

Leveraging GWAS data, SNPs associated with the instrumental variables were identified, and through the "harmonise_data" function within TwoSampleMR, we aligned allelic directions of exposure and outcome, excluding incompatible SNPs. The inverse variance-weighted (IVW) method served as the cornerstone for causal inference, employing the variance of instrumental variables as weights to determine causal dynamics, thereby advancing our understanding of the genetic architecture underlying disease states.

Results

DEG Identification and TMGs enrichment analysis

In our investigation, we specifically focused on four TMGs, namely ALDH9A1, ACAT1, CYP1B1, and CYP1A1, which were prioritized based on their consistent differential expression across multiple public datasets and their documented involvement in metabolic and neurodegenerative pathways. This targeted selection enabled us to probe key molecular nodes potentially linking tryptophan metabolism to disease pathophysiology. To delineate the expression dynamics of these TMGs, we applied an unsupervised hierarchical clustering algorithm using Euclidean distance and complete linkage as the clustering criteria. This approach revealed clear segregation between the PD and control cohorts, underscoring the discriminatory power of these genes under different experimental conditions. Strikingly, ALDH9A1 exhibited markedly elevated expression in the PD group, suggesting its possible contribution to disease-associated metabolic reprogramming or therapeutic responsiveness. In contrast, the control group displayed consistently higher expression levels of ACAT1, CYP1B1, and CYP1A1, which may reflect compensatory metabolic routes or alternative regulatory mechanisms in the absence of PD pathology. To further unravel the interactions among these TMGs, we constructed a correlation matrix, enabling comprehensive visualization of their interdependencies and functional convergence (Fig. 2a–b, Table S2). This matrix offers critical insights into the regulatory interplay among the TMGs, while simultaneously serving as a robust framework for uncovering co-expression patterns that could guide subsequent functional investigations. Together, these findings not only advance our understanding of the molecular dynamics shaping the immune microenvironment but also establish a foundation for exploring the therapeutic relevance of these pivotal genes.

GO enrichment analysis identified 207 core biological functions, spanning both BP and MF. Within the MF category, the most prominent terms included oxidoreductase activity acting on paired donors with incorporation or reduction of molecular oxygen (GO:0016705), lyase activity (GO:0016829), and acyltransferase activity involving the transfer of groups other than amino-acyl groups (GO:0016747). In the BP category, enriched terms were largely associated with metabolic reprogramming, such as the small molecule catabolic process (GO:0044282), fatty acid metabolic process (GO:0006631), and alcohol metabolic process (GO:0006066). KEGG pathway enrichment further demonstrated that the upregulated genes were predominantly clustered in pathways related to fatty acid degradation, pyruvate metabolism, propanoate metabolism, and tryptophan metabolism (Fig. 2c-d and Table S3). Among these, the enrichment of the tryptophan metabolism pathway is of particular significance in the context of PD. Tryptophan metabolism governs the balance between neuroprotective and neurotoxic metabolites, including serotonin, kynurenine, and quinolinic acid, thereby directly influencing oxidative stress, mitochondrial function, and neuroinflammatory responses—key hallmarks of PD pathogenesis. The observed enrichment suggests that dysregulation of this pathway may critically contribute to disease progression by modulating both neuronal survival and immune microenvironmental dynamics. Collectively, these results highlight the biological relevance of metabolic pathways, especially tryptophan metabolism, in shaping the molecular landscape of PD and point to their potential as mechanistic links and therapeutic targets in disease modulation.

The modest AUC values may, in part, reflect the intrinsic heterogeneity of individual genetic factors in complex disorders such as PD. Gene set enrichment analysis

In this phase of the study, we conducted an in-depth GSEA, evaluating the AUC of each gene within the test cohort alongside their rank orderings and validation outcomes. This analysis identified CYP1A1 and CYP1B1 as potentially pivotal genes in the context of PD, a conclusion supported by both literature evidence and the sensitivity of these hub genes within our computational model. In GO analysis, CYP1A1 was significantly associated with biological processes including gonadotropin secretion, mesodermal cell differentiation, and outflow tract morphogenesis, pointing to its potential role in mitochondrial dynamics and their relevance to PD pathophysiology. In contrast, CYP1B1 was enriched in pathways related to catecholamine secretion, neuronal action potential, and xenobiotic transport, aligning with emerging evidence of neurotransmitter dysregulation and immune involvement in PD. KEGG analysis provided additional mechanistic insights. CYP1A1 was linked to primary bile acid biosynthesis, ribosomal function, and type II diabetes mellitus, implicating it in fundamental metabolic processes with possible consequences for neuronal vulnerability. CYP1B1, by contrast, was enriched in ascorbate and aldarate metabolism, complement and coagulation cascades, and cytokine–cytokine receptor interactions, reinforcing its connection to immune dysregulation in PD. Collectively, these findings, illustrated in Fig. 4a–b and summarized in Table S5, underscore the distinct yet complementary contributions of CYP1A1 and CYP1B1 to PD pathogenesis. These results suggest that CYP1A1 and CYP1B1 may serve as molecular bridges linking metabolic dysfunction and immune dysregulation, two hallmarks increasingly recognized in PD. While their associations with mitochondrial processes, neurotransmitter signaling, and inflammatory cascades highlight promising avenues for therapeutic intervention, the findings remain correlative and require rigorous experimental validation. Future studies should investigate how modulation of these genes influences neuronal survival and immune activation in vivo, and whether targeting their pathways could mitigate PD progression. Such efforts will be essential to translate these computational predictions into clinically actionable insights.

Immune cell analysis

This section of the study investigates the pivotal role of the immune microenvironment in the onset and progression of PD. Given the central importance of immune cell dynamics, we performed a detailed analysis to delineate their expression patterns and interactions in PD. To visualize these patterns, a vioplot was generated, effectively illustrating the differential abundance of immune cells between control and PD groups. Specifically, CD4 memory activated T cells, follicular helper T cells, γδ T cells, and resting dendritic cells were predominantly enriched in the control group. In contrast, regulatory T cells (Tregs), M0 macrophages, resting mast cells, activated mast cells, and neutrophils were highly expressed in the PD group. This differential enrichment provides critical insights into the immune response mechanisms that may be activated or dysregulated in PD. To further refine this immunological landscape, we conducted a comprehensive correlation analysis aimed at deciphering the intricate relationships between the identified genes and distinct immune cell populations. These findings, as presented in Fig. 5a–b, illuminate the complex interplay between immune cell dynamics and genetic factors in PD, thereby offering a deeper understanding of disease-associated immune perturbations.

GSVA

In the GO analysis, CYP1A1 was primarily associated with molecular functions such as channel activator activity and biological processes including interleukin-21 production, peptide modification, and RNA 3′ uridylation, while being localized to the septin cytoskeleton. These findings suggest a potential role for CYP1A1 in modulating immune responses and post-transcriptional regulation, processes that have been increasingly implicated in neuroinflammatory and neurodegenerative pathways of PD. In contrast, CYP1B1 was enriched in processes such as the negative regulation of Nodal signaling, axonemal central apparatus assembly, and serotonin-gated cation channel activity. The involvement of CYP1B1 in these pathways points toward possible contributions to neuronal signaling and cytoskeletal integrity, both of which are critical for dopaminergic neuron survival and function (Fig. 6a). KEGG pathway analysis further underscored these distinctions. CYP1A1 was linked to primary bile acid biosynthesis, pentose and glucuronate interconversions, glyoxylate and dicarboxylate metabolism, riboflavin metabolism, and aminoacyl-tRNA biosynthesis. These metabolic pathways highlight its potential role in oxidative stress regulation and mitochondrial function, processes closely tied to PD pathogenesis. By contrast, CYP1B1 was associated with taurine and hypotaurine metabolism, maturity-onset diabetes of the young, glyoxylate and dicarboxylate metabolism, taste transduction, and glycosphingolipid biosynthesis (lacto and neolacto series). These enrichments suggest that CYP1B1 may intersect with metabolic dysfunction, glycosphingolipid imbalances, and altered neurotransmission, all of which have been implicated in PD progression (Fig. 6b). Collectively, these findings highlight that although both genes share certain metabolic pathways, they also diverge in distinct functional networks that may differentially shape PD-related molecular mechanisms.

Future studies should explore the mechanistic basis of these drug–gene interactions, particularly their capacity to modulate metabolic and immune-related pathways implicated in PD. If validated, such findings may accelerate the translation of bioinformatics-driven predictions into actionable therapeutic strategies, broadening the landscape of precision medicine for PD.

Elucidation of non-coding RNA networks in PD

In this segment of the study, we conducted a comprehensive exploration of non-coding RNAs, specifically miRNAs and lncRNAs, in the context of PD. Through an integrative analysis of three independent databases, we identified 57 lncRNAs and 79 miRNAs potentially implicated in PD, as summarized in Table S6a–b. The identified miRNAs were systematically cross-referenced with entries in the miRanda [17], miRDB [18], and TargetScan [19] databases, and a concordance scoring system was applied, whereby each match contributed a score of 1, with a maximum of 3 for miRNAs consistently identified across all three platforms. To further delineate their interactions, the spongeScan database was employed to map these miRNAs to their corresponding lncRNA partners. During the network construction process, 55 of the 57 lncRNAs were successfully integrated into the miRNA–lncRNA–gene interaction framework. The two excluded lncRNAs lacked reliable or high-confidence binding interactions in spongeScan, precluding their incorporation into the final network. Consequently, the constructed network comprised 55 lncRNAs, 78 miRNAs, and several shared genes of interest, including the three hub genes central to this study—ALDH9A1, CYP1B1, and CYP1A1 (Fig. 9). This curated network provides an integrated perspective on the regulatory landscape of PD, revealing how lncRNAs and miRNAs converge on key genetic drivers. By resolving the screening and integration discrepancies, these findings enhance confidence in the robustness of the network and underscore the complex molecular interactions that shape PD pathophysiology, thereby offering a more holistic framework for future therapeutic and diagnostic exploration.

Mendelian randomization analysis

To investigate the causal relationship between CYP1B1 and CYP1A1 expression and PD incidence, we employed Mendelian randomization (MR) analyses. A forest plot was generated to visualize the effect estimates, revealing a generally symmetrical distribution of data points, suggestive of consistency across genetic instruments. Sensitivity analysis using the leave-one-out approach demonstrated that exclusion of any single SNP exerted only a negligible impact on the inverse variance–weighted (IVW) estimates, indicating that the remaining variants faithfully represented the overall association signal. To further strengthen the robustness of these findings, MR-Egger regression was performed, providing no evidence of directional pleiotropy and thereby reinforcing the validity of the causal inference (Fig. 11a–b). Collectively, these convergent results highlight the methodological stability and internal consistency of our analytical framework, lending strong support to the hypothesis that CYP1B1 and CYP1A1 may contribute to PD susceptibility through genetically mediated mechanisms. These insights offer a compelling rationale for subsequent functional studies to delineate the biological pathways linking these metabolic genes to PD pathogenesis.

Discussions

Recent research shifts focus towards the modulation of cell death pathways, akin to strategies in tumor biology, as a potential therapeutic avenue for PD, underscoring the importance of early diagnosis and gene expression regulation [20]. Particularly, the modulation of tryptophan metabolism has emerged as a significant research interest, with melatonin and kynurenic acid (KYNA) playing crucial roles in neuroprotection and immunomodulation [21]. Melatonin, derived from tryptophan, facilitates amyloid beta clearance, while KYNA, another tryptophan metabolism product, antagonizes specific neuroreceptors, suggesting a protective effect against PD progression [22]. Despite these advancements, the explicit role of TMGs in PD pathophysiology remains elusive. Our study leverages patient data from the GEO database to elucidate the relationship between TMGs and PD prognosis [23]. Through differential expression analysis and risk model construction, we identified TMGs with potential relevance to PD outcomes, thereby providing a foundation for novel therapeutic strategies. Among these, three DEGs—ALDH9A1, CYP1A1, and CYP1B1—emerged as key candidates, consistently validated across independent datasets. By integrating differential analysis with Lasso regression and SVM-RFE, we established these genes as robust diagnostic biomarkers, underscoring their central role in PD pathogenesis. A focused literature survey further highlights the biological significance of CYP1A1 and CYP1B1, implicating them in small-molecule catabolism, fatty acid metabolism, and alcohol metabolism, processes closely linked to cellular homeostasis and neurodegeneration. Within the TMG framework, these genes also appear to interface with transcriptional regulatory networks, suggesting that their influence extends beyond metabolic regulation to broader molecular interactions. Notably, our findings point to a potential role of TMGs in modulating immune-related pathways, introducing an additional layer of complexity to PD’s molecular landscape. While these results represent a meaningful advance, several questions remain. The mechanistic links between TMGs and immune dysregulation require experimental validation, and the functional consequences of their interaction with transcription factors are yet to be elucidated. Future studies should therefore combine multi-omics integration and mechanistic experimentation to delineate these pathways more precisely. Such efforts will be essential for translating TMG-related biomarkers into clinically actionable strategies and for uncovering new therapeutic targets in PD.

In the quest to unravel the complexities of PD, the regulation of TMGs emerges as a critical focal point, bridging cellular energy dynamics with disease pathogenesis. The cytochrome P450 enzymes, specifically CYP1A1 and CYP1B1, have garnered attention for their roles beyond conventional metabolic processing, implicating them directly in PD's molecular narrative. These enzymes, central to the phase I metabolic pathway, are instrumental in detoxifying neurotoxicants, thus influencing the vulnerability of dopaminergic neurons to environmental toxins—a recognized PD risk factor [24]. Variations in CYP1A1 and CYP1B1, through polymorphisms affecting enzyme activity, may alter the landscape of neurotoxic metabolite accumulation, thereby modulating PD risk and progression. The significance of CYP1A1 and CYP1B1 extends into the modulation of neuroinflammatory responses, a pivotal aspect of PD's pathology [25]. By influencing the metabolism of compounds that regulate inflammation, these genes intersect with neuroinflammatory pathways, suggesting a broader role in disease mechanism than previously recognized. This evidence situates CYP1A1 and CYP1B1 at a nexus of environmental, metabolic, and inflammatory pathways in PD, underscoring their potential as both biomarkers and therapeutic targets [26]. Leveraging the GSE7621 transcriptomic dataset, our investigation elucidates the prognostic significance of TMG-associated molecular signatures in PD. These findings reinforce the notion that TMG dysregulation may serve not only as a biomarker of disease susceptibility but also as a potential therapeutic target for modulating disease trajectory. Despite growing recognition of their importance, the contribution of TMGs to PD remains insufficiently explored, representing an emergent frontier in neurodegeneration research. In summary, the dual roles of CYP1A1 and CYP1B1 in oxidative stress mitigation and immunomodulation expand current paradigms of PD pathophysiology. Their involvement underscores a metabolic–inflammatory crosstalk that may govern neuronal resilience or degeneration under environmental challenge. Future investigations should integrate multi-omics approaches and functional validation models to delineate the precise regulatory circuitry connecting CYP-mediated metabolism, neuroimmune signaling, and dopaminergic neurodegeneration. Such insights hold the promise of guiding precision medicine strategies that target metabolic–inflammatory convergence points in PD.

The interface between the immune system and PD epitomizes a cutting-edge domain in neuroscientific research, unraveling the complex etiology of this neurodegenerative condition [27]. With neuroimmunology's evolution, PD is increasingly perceived through the prism of immune dysregulation, beyond the classical pathology of dopaminergic neuron loss and α-synuclein accumulation in the substantia nigra [28]. This research forefront illuminates the intricate interactions between innate and adaptive immunity and PD's pathogenesis, with chronic neuroinflammation—characterized by microglial activation and peripheral immune cell infiltration—standing out as a critical factor in PD pathology [29]. Such inflammation is thought to amplify neuronal damage and hasten disease progression. Moreover, α-synuclein aggregates, a PD hallmark, are hypothesized to instigate immune responses, further fueling neuroinflammation and neuronal degeneration [30]. Beyond neuroinflammation, immunological elements like cytokine profiles and autoantibodies are implicated in PD's onset and progression, suggesting the immune system's dual role as both a responder to and driver of PD pathophysiology [31]. This understanding opens new avenues for therapeutic interventions, particularly through immunomodulation, targeting the inflammatory and immune dysregulation pathways in PD [32]. In this context, our study contributes novel evidence by employing vioplot-based immune profiling, which elucidated distinct immune cell expression patterns between PD patients and controls. Specifically, we observed a predominance of T regulatory cells (Tregs), macrophages, mast cells, and neutrophils in PD samples, in contrast to higher levels of resting dendritic cells and specific T-cell subtypes in healthy controls. These immune shifts suggest a transition toward a pro-inflammatory microenvironment that may potentiate neuronal vulnerability and accelerate PD progression. In summary, the data highlight a tightly interwoven relationship between immune perturbation and neurodegeneration in PD. The enrichment of Tregs and macrophages may reflect a compensatory but ultimately maladaptive immune response to chronic neuronal stress. Future studies should leverage single-cell transcriptomics, spatial proteomics, and immune-metabolic modeling to map the temporal evolution of immune alterations in PD. Such integrative approaches could illuminate actionable immunomodulatory targets, fostering the development of precision immunotherapies aimed at restoring immune homeostasis and halting neurodegenerative cascades in PD.

The growing recognition of the interplay between PD and metabolic functions marks a pivotal shift in contemporary biomedical research. Leveraging advanced bioinformatics tools, recent studies have begun to unravel the intricate molecular architecture of PD, offering unprecedented insights into its pathophysiology and related disorders. This evolving paradigm is crucial for deepening our understanding of the mechanisms that drive disease onset and progression [33–35]. In this study, we addressed a critical knowledge gap by systematically investigating the role of TMGs in PD. Using large-scale transcriptomic datasets from GEO (GSE6613 and GSE7621) and applying integrative approaches including GO, KEGG, and GSEA analyses, we delineated the biological pathways and molecular interactions associated with TMGs. Furthermore, the construction of a predictive model provided a robust framework for elucidating the contribution of TMGs to PD pathobiology. Collectively, these findings not only establish a theoretical foundation but also lay the groundwork for advancing research into metabolic dysfunctions underlying PD and their therapeutic implications. Nonetheless, several limitations warrant consideration. First, although our analyses identified putative pathways and regulatory networks, the conclusions remain largely inferential and require experimental validation. Both in vitro and in vivo studies are indispensable for confirming the mechanistic roles of TMGs in PD. Second, the datasets analyzed were limited in sample size and population diversity, which may restrict the generalizability of our findings. Finally, while bioinformatics approaches are powerful for hypothesis generation, they cannot fully capture the dynamic cellular and metabolic processes occurring in the diseased brain. Future research should therefore prioritize large-scale, multi-omics integration across diverse cohorts to refine the molecular signatures of PD. In parallel, experimental models are needed to validate the functional significance of identified targets and to evaluate their therapeutic potential. Ultimately, translating these insights into clinically actionable strategies will require longitudinal studies that connect molecular alterations with disease trajectory, paving the way toward precision diagnostics and personalized interventions in PD.

Conclusions

Our study delineates the multifaceted pathobiology of PD, unveiling a complex molecular network shaped by diverse targets, pathways, signaling cascades, and regulatory systems engaged in dynamic crosstalk. This network integrates both synergistic and antagonistic interactions, reflecting the intricate biological architecture that underlies PD. At its core lie the TMGs, notably ALDH9A1, CYP1A1, and CYP1B1, which serve as pivotal regulators of biosynthetic processes and key metabolic pathways, thereby positioning them as critical molecular determinants in PD pathogenesis.

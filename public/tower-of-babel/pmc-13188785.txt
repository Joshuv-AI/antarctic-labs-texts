GWAS and genomic prediction for resistance to soybean mosaic virus strain SC7
Authors: Lijuan Zhu, Qingmin Meng, Tiantian Zhao, Fengmin Wang, Xiaolei Shi, Qiang Chen, Jun Qin, Yanli Wang, Mengchen Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground soybean mosaic virus (SMV)Represents one of the most prevalent and destructive diseases affecting major soybean-producing regions, significantly impacting both soybean yield and quality. This study aimed to investigate the variation in soybean resistance to the SMV SC7 strain, identify the associated resistance loci, and predict potential candidate genes.ResultsThe study investigated a collection of 290 soybean germplasm materials, distinguished by their extensive genetic diversity. Inoculation with the SMV SC7 strain for resistance identification revealed that 18.2% of the materials exhibited high resistance (HR), while 6.8% displayed high susceptibility (HS). Based on four models for GWAS analysis, five significant single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) associated with SMV resistance were identified on chromosomes 11, 13(two SNPs), 16, and 17. Two known resistance loci reconfirmed on chromosome 13. Furthermore, the loci Chr11_27826328, Chr16_34392503, and Chr17_18822285 were located on chromosomes 11, 16, and 17, respectively. A candidate gene related to SMV resistance, Glyma.16g182700, has been identified on chromosome 16. Haplotype analysis of this gene demonstrated significant differences in SMV resistance between materials containing Hap1 and those possessing Hap2. Compared to random SNPs, the 100 GWAS-significant markers yielded higher genomic prediction accuracy. And a plateau in accuracy was reached at approximately 6000 markers.ConclusionsThis study reveals the genetic architecture underlying soybean resistance to the SMV SC7 strain, identifies a key candidate gene, and enhances the accuracy of GS by incorporating significant SNPs. These findings provide valuable resources for marker-assisted selection and the genetic improvement of SMV-resistant soybean varieties.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12870-026-08620-9.Keywords: Soybean, SMV, GWAS, GS

Introduction

Soybean (Glycine max (L.) Merr.) is a staple crop and a major source of high-quality plant protein. However, its production is severely limited by biotic stresses, among which Soybean Mosaic Virus (SMV) represents a particularly damaging threat to crop growth and development [1]. SMV infection adversely affects soybean plants by suppressing leaf photosynthesis and reducing nutrient acquisition and distribution, thereby causing a substantial decline in grain yield [2]. Currently, no effective chemical control measures are available for SMV; therefore, developing resistant varieties represents the most economical and sustainable management strategy [3]. Although traditional genetic mapping approaches have identified several genomic regions associated with SMV resistance, they often rely on biparental populations with limited genetic diversity. In contrast, genome-wide association studies (GWAS) utilize natural populations characterized by rich genetic diversity and high-density single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) markers, which facilitate high-resolution mapping of resistance loci and the identification of candidate genes [4].

As a pivotal tool in crop genetic research, GWAS investigates the genetic architecture of complex traits using high-density SNP markers [5]. This method enables rapid scanning of the entire genome to identify loci and candidate genes potentially associated with the trait [6]. Consequently, the successful implementation of GWAS in crops like foxtail millet, rice, and cotton has accelerated marker-assisted selection (MAS) and enhanced breeding efficiency [7]. GWAS have demonstrated considerable potential in research on soybean resistance to SMV. This is exemplified by a study of 219 materials, which revealed one significant SNP locus on chromosome 8 and four novel loci on chromosome 20 associated with SC7 resistance [8]. Chu [9] employed a natural population comprising 209 materials and a recombinant inbred line (RIL) population of 193 lines to integrate quantitative trait locus (QTL) mapping and GWAS. Their analysis identified a major QTL, qRsc8F, on chromosome 13 (28.46–28.60 Mb). This region contained two candidate genes, GmMACPF1 and GmRad60. Differential expression of both genes following SC8 inoculation supports a role for GmMACPF1 and GmRad60 in disease resistance. Through a GWAS on a panel of 165 soybean mutant lines, Che et al. [10] identified 104 SNPs significantly associated with SC7 resistance. Yan et al. [11] conducted a GWAS involving a natural population of 191 soybean materials, successfully identifying 19 SNP loci significantly associated with resistance to SMV SC7 strain. Concurrently, Pu et al. [12] discovered six novel SNP markers linked to SMV SC7 resistance within a 274 kb region on chromosome 15, proposing two candidate genes: Glyma.15G015700 and Glyma.15G015800. These findings demonstrate the efficacy of GWAS in deciphering the mechanisms of SMV resistance. The identified markers can be directly applied in MAS, thereby expediting the development of resistant soybean varieties.

Genomic Selection (GS), which utilizes genome-wide markers to predict the phenotypic values of target traits, has become a vital tool for crop genetic improvement. GS facilitates the comprehensive evaluation of complex quantitative traits, with a key advantage being its potential to significantly accelerate the development of disease-resistant cultivars [13]. Although its application in plants initially lagged behind that in animals, recent studies have consistently demonstrated high prediction accuracy in plant breeding programs [14]. GS represents a powerful tool with considerable potential for soybean breeding applications. Hussain et al. [15] genotyped 483 elite breeding lines to validate the effectiveness of GS in predicting seed yield, protein, and oil content in soybean. Wang et al. [16] further supported these findings by applying ridge regression to fatty acid composition traits, confirming that models based on associated SNPs outperformed those using random markers. Similarly, Qin et al. [17] conducted GS analysis for protein content using associated SNPs via Bayesian LASSO (BL) and Ridge Regression Best Linear Unbiased Prediction (rrBLUP), demonstrating that associated SNPs yielded significantly higher predictive accuracy than random SNPs, with prediction efficiency stabilizing once the number of markers reached 2,000. Xiong et al. [18] evaluated the predictive performance of GS for resistance to soybean rust (SBR) using multiple models, including rrBLUP, Genomic Best Linear Unbiased Prediction (gBLUP), and Bayesian Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (Bayesian LASSO). These results demonstrate that the efficient SNP markers identified through GWAS can enhance prediction accuracy. The integration of GS has been shown to significantly improve genetic progress in soybean breeding schemes. Therefore, leveraging trait-associated markers represents an effective strategy to enhance the predictive efficiency of GS. This refinement in GS methodology is crucial for practical breeding, as it enables more accurate selection early in the breeding programs, thereby accelerating the development of high-yielding and stress-resilient soybean varieties.

This study aims to identify candidate genes for SMV SC7 resistance and integrate GS to evaluate its efficiency, providing genetic resources and strategic support for breeding resistant varieties. We first employed four statistical models—the Mixed Linear Model (MLM), Multi-Locus Mixed Model (MLMM), Fixed and Random Model Circulating Probability Unification (FarmCPU), and Bayesian Information and Linkage-Disequilibrium Iteratively Nested Keyway (Blink)—in a GWAS of 290 soybean materials to identify resistance-associated loci. Subsequently, the findings from the GWAS were integrated with GS technology and applied in the breeding of disease-resistant varieties. Additionally, seven GS models—namely Bayesian Alpha (BA), Bayesian Beta (BB), BL, Bayesian Ridge Regression (BRR), rrBLUP, Support Vector Machine (SVM), and Random Forest (RF)—were employed to predict resistance to the SMV SC7 strain. This integrated approach not only enhances the accuracy of breeding for SMV resistance but also accelerates the development and deployment of resistant soybean varieties.

Materials and methods

Experimental materials

For this study, a population of soybean germplasm was utilized, which was supplied and maintained by the Institute of Cereal and Oil Crops, Hebei Academy of Agriculture and Forestry Sciences [16]. Geographically, the accessions originate from China (200 accessions, 68.97%, representing 16 provinces), the United States (83 accessions, 28.62%, from 6 states), as well as one accession each (0.34% each) from the Russia, the United Kingdom, Italy, Brazil, Poland, South Korea, and Yugoslavia. This design thus provides a representative and genetically broad resource for the investigation. (Table S1). The experiment was conducted in 2024 in a screenhouse at the Institute of Cereal and Oil Crops, Hebei Academy of Agriculture and Forestry Sciences (38.23°N, 114.39°E), with three replications.

SMV SC7 strain and inoculation method

The SMV SC7 strain, maintained on the susceptible cultivar Nannong 1138-2, was provided by Nanjing Agricultural University.

Soybeans were cultivated in plastic pots measuring 20 cm × 20 cm, with three biological replicates for each accession. Fifteen seeds were sown per pot. At the true-leaf stage, five uniformly growing seedlings were retained per pot and artificially inoculated with SMV SC7 using the sap rub-inoculation method. This inoculum was prepared by grinding 1.0 g of symptomatic leaf tissue from infected Nannong 1138-2 plants in 5 mL of 0.1 mol/L phosphate buffer. A sterile brush was dipped into the homogenate and used to evenly coat the first pair of true leaves, ensuring complete surface coverage. Disease symptoms were recorded at 7 days post-inoculation (dpi) and assessed in detail at 28 dpi.

Criteria for resistance rating evaluation

The evaluation of soybean disease severity was performed according to the rating system established by Zhi et al. [19], which divides symptoms into five distinct levels (0 to 4). The materials were accordingly categorized into five resistance levels: Grade 0 (high resistance, HR), Grade 1 (resistance, R), Grade 2 (moderate resistance, MR), Grade 3 (susceptibility, S), and Grade 4 (high susceptibility, HS) (Table S2).

Whole-genome resequencing and genetic variation analysis

The 290 soybean accessions were subjected to resequencing on the MGI-T7 platform, generating paired-end 150-bp reads with an average sequencing depth of 10× [20]. The resequencing data were processed and filtered using fastp (v0.20.0). By applying filtering criteria of a minor allele frequency ≥ 0.05 and a maximum missing rate ≤ 0.1, a final set of 207,369 high-quality SNP loci was obtained, which were subsequently employed for GWAS.

Genome-wide association studies and candidate gene predictive analysis

The GWAS analysis was conducted in R utilizing the GAPIT package across four models: MLM, MLMM, FarmCPU and Blink [21]. The CMplot package in R was employed to create Manhattan and QQ plots [22]. Significantly associated SNPs were identified based on a Bonferroni-corrected threshold of -log₁₀(P) ≥ 3.5 [23]. Subsequently, linkage disequilibrium (LD) was analyzed using LDBlockShow software [24].

To pinpoint candidate genes conferring resistance to SMV SC7, this study selected genes residing within 100 kb windows surrounding the peak SNPs [25]. Functional annotation of Arabidopsis thaliana orthologs was performed using TAIR. Finally, haplotype analysis was conducted on the candidate genes, identifying those that exhibited significant phenotypic differences among materials with distinct haplotypes as candidate genes associated with SMV SC7 resistance.

RT-qPCR for expression analysis

Resistant and susceptible soybean materials were cultivated for an exogenous inoculation assay (Table S3). The plants were divided into the inoculated treatment group and the non-inoculated control group. When the first pair of true leaves had fully expanded, the treatment group was inoculated with SMV SC7 using the sap rub-inoculation method described previously. At 14 dpi, leaf samples were collected from both the inoculated and the non-inoculated control group. Following total RNA extraction (using the Total RNA Extraction Kit from TransGen Biotech) and cDNA synthesis (using the Reverse Transcription Kit from Vazyme Biotech), RT-qPCR was performed on a Bio-Rad CFX384™ system.

Amplifications were performed in 10 µL volumes using the ChamQ Universal SYBR qPCR Master Mix, which included the following components: 5 µL Master Mix, 0.4 µL each of forward and reverse primers, 1 µL cDNA, and 3.2 µL nuclease-free water. The thermal cycling protocol consisted of an initial denaturation at 95 °C for 30 s, followed by 39 cycles of 95 °C for 5 s and 60 °C for 15 s. Gene-specific primers were designed using SnapGene Viewer (v5.2.3; the primers are listed in Table S4). Actin11 served as the reference gene for normalizing expression levels, which were calculated using the method [26]. The differential expression of candidate genes between resistant and susceptible materials was analyzed by RT-qPCR.

Genomic selection

In this study, PLINK (v1.90) was used to extract SNP loci from the genome-wide data, constructing both GWAS and random feature sets under four distinct models. The GWAS feature set comprised the top 100 most significant SNPs for SMV SC7 resistance from each of the four GWAS models, which were designated as MLM Top100, MLMM Top100, FarmCPU Top100, and Blink Top100 (Table S5). Correspondingly, for random feature sets, 100 SNPs were randomly selected per model, labeled MLM_RD100, MLMM_RD100, FarmCPU_RD100, and Blink_RD100 (Table S6). We assembled a Top100 feature set from the BLINK model’s top 100 SNPs, in addition to nine random SNP sets. These random sets, ranging in size from 100 to 10,000 SNPs, were named RD100, RD300, RD500, RD1000, RD2000, RD4000, RD6000, RD8000, and RD10000.

We employed the BGLR package (v1.0.8) in R to predict SMV SC7 resistance in the natural population of 290 soybean materials. This package implements seven statistical models: BA, BB, BL, BRR, RF, rrBLUP, and SVM [27]. We conducted a comparative assessment of prediction accuracy for the GWAS-based Top 100 sets (derived from BLINK, FarmCPU, MLM, and MLMM) against random control sets. A random 80% subset of the data was utilized for model training, while the remaining 20% was reserved as a test set. Model performance was evaluated through five-fold cross-validation, and this process was repeated 100 times to obtain a reliable estimate of predictive accuracy [28].

Data processing and analysis

The normality of the SMV SC7 resistance phenotype was assessed with IBM SPSS Statistics 27.0. Haplotype analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism 8 (GraphPad Software, USA). The broad-sense heritability (H²) for SMV resistance was calculated from the phenotypic data of the soybean materials using IciMapping software (version 4.1) [29]. All figures presented in this study, including those depicting candidate gene relative expression and genomic prediction accuracy, were generated using the ggplot2 package in R. The relative expression levels of candidate genes were determined by RT-qPCR [30], the experiment included three independent biological replicates for each treatment, with three technical replicates per biological replicate.

Results

Evaluation of soybean materials for resistance to SMV SC7

In this study, we assessed 290 soybean accessions for resistance to SMV SC7. The phenotypic screening classified 53 accessions (18.2%) as HR, 62 (21.3%) as R, 91 (31.3%) as MR, 64 (22.0%) as S, and 20 (6.8%) as HS (Table 1). The distribution of resistance scores showed slight positive skewness (0.018) and platykurtic kurtosis (-0.89). Analysis of variance revealed that genetic factors predominantly influenced the phenotypic variation, with a H² estimate of 0.8362 for SMV SC7 resistance (Table S7).

Genome-wide association studies

To identify SNPs associated with resistance to the SMV SC7 strain, we employed four GWAS models (BLINK, FarmCPU, MLM, and MLMM) to analyze 290 materials (Fig. 1). The loci Chr11_27826328, Chr13_30467101, Chr13_30715051, Chr16_34392503, and Chr17_18822285 were all detected by two or more models. The -log(P) scores for these loci ranged from 4.13 to 8.23. The identification of loci on chromosomes 11, 16, and 17 by multiple models provides evidence for their close relationship with the trait. Notably, the SNPs Chr11_27826328 and Chr16_34392503 yielded the most significant associations, with -log(P) scores of 8.23 (FarmCPU) and 6.97 (BLINK), respectively (Table 2).

Candidate gene prediction and haplotype analysis

Focusing on the loci Chr11_27826328, Chr16_34392503, and Chr17_18822285, our examination of the surrounding 100 kb genomic regions led to the identification of ten candidate genes putatively associated with resistance (Table 3). The candidate genes Glyma.16g182700 and Glyma.11g199700 were located within the significant loci Chr16_34392503 (-log(P) = 6.97) and Chr11_27826328 (-log(P) = 8.23), respectively, as identified through LD analysis and functional annotation (Fig. 2A). The encoded protein contains leucine-rich repeat (LRR) domains, known to be implicated in pathogen recognition, immunity and stress responses [31, 32].

RT-qPCR analysis of candidate genes

To investigate viral accumulation and the relative expression of candidate genes following SMV SC7 strain infection, we inoculated five susceptible and five resistant materials with the strain. At 14 dpi, total RNA was extracted from the non-inoculated leaves of the treated plants, and subjected to RT-qPCR analysis with three technical replicates. The accumulation of the viral capsid protein (CP) gene was significantly lower in resistant materials compared to susceptible ones (Fig. 3A), confirming their effective restriction of viral replication. To preliminarily assess the function of the candidate gene Glyma.16g182700, we quantified its expression via RT-qPCR in both resistant and susceptible materials following SMV SC7 inoculation. The expression of Glyma.16g182700 was significantly lower in susceptible materials compared to resistant lines (Fig. 3B). This molecular pattern was reflected in the phenotypic observations: susceptible plants exhibited severe yellow mottling and mosaic symptoms, whereas resistant plants displayed no or only mild symptoms, akin to those of non-inoculated control group (Fig. 4). The observed disparity in symptom severity between resistant and susceptible materials strongly suggests that Glyma.16g182700 plays a pivotal role in the antiviral defense mechanism of resistant soybean plants.

The effect of associated SNPs and different SNP marker densities on prediction accuracy

To evaluate the impact of different SNP set sizes on the genomic prediction efficiency of SMV SC7 resistance, multiple prediction models—including BL, BA, BB, BRR, SVM, RF, and rrBLUP—were tested. Analysis of the Top 100 feature set revealed that the rrBLUP model achieved the highest prediction accuracy of 0.682, surpassing all nine random sets (RD100 – RD10000). Among these, the random forest (RF) model recorded the lowest accuracy at only 0.496. In multiple models, prediction accuracy increased with the size of the SNP set but plateaued or decreased beyond 6,000 SNPs. Specifically, in the rrBLUP model, accuracy fell by 10.797%, from 0.389 (RD6000) to 0.347 (RD8000) (Fig. 5A, Table S8). Overall, our results indicate that GWAS-based feature sets consistently achieve higher accuracy than random sets of the same size. This highlights the paramount importance of the top 100 associated SNPs for GS and establishes the rrBLUP model as a highly accurate predictor for SMV SC7 resistance. For breeding programs aiming to develop SMV SC7-resistant varieties, a marker set size of 6000 is recommended as the optimal compromise, delivering reliable accuracy alongside improved cost-effectiveness for accelerated molecular breeding.

Discussion

Analysis of candidate loci identified by GWAS

Driven by progress in high-throughput genotyping technology, GWAS has extended from its initial focus on human genetics to broad applications in plant science. As a versatile approach that analyzes both qualitative and quantitative traits, it thereby provides a highly efficient platform for investigating the genetic foundations of complex agronomic traits [44]. Advances in GWAS have enhanced our understanding of soybean resistance to SMV. In one analysis of 165 germplasm lines, SNPs linked to disease rating were uncovered, thereby establishing a resource for both gene characterization and the development of resistant varieties. This finding underscores that resistance is stably inherited and that the identified sources can be deployed directly in breeding [45]. Each GWAS model is optimized for specific statistical challenges. BLINK reduces false positives by modeling the even distribution of quantitative trait nucleotides across the genome [46]. FarmCPU addresses both Type I and Type II errors through its iterative fixed-effect model framework [47]. MLM counters spurious associations caused by population structure via the inclusion of a kinship matrix [48]. In contrast, MLMM boosts detection power by iteratively using the most significant markers as covariates to refine the model [49].

In this study, a GWAS of SMV SC7 resistance was conducted using these four models on 290 soybean materials, yielding highly consistent results. We identified two significant loci on chromosome 13 (Chr13_30467101 and Chr13_30715051), which is a known SMV resistance region. Notably, Chr13_30467101 is situated between the previously reported markers BARCSOYSSR_13_1138 and BARCSOYSSR_13_1139 [50]. The Rsv1 locus is positioned within the 29.5–30.5 Mb genomic interval and overlaps with the Chr13_30715051 locus identified in this study [51]. The loci Chr11_27826328, Chr16_34392503, and Chr17_18822285 represent novel discoveries, consistently identified across multiple models. The application of this multi-model approach enhances the efficacy of genetic marker detection. Furthermore, this result underscores the effectiveness of the multi-model co-localization strategy employed in this study; this approach significantly reduces the false positive rate, thereby enhancing the accuracy of candidate locus screening. The existence of previously defined resistance loci serves to both affirm the reliability of our results and suggest the likely importance of the newly discovered locus in the genetic control of SMV resistance in soybean.

Identification of candidate genes

Throughout evolutionary history, plants have developed an innate immune defense system mediated by resistance proteins through natural selection. The core mechanism of this system relies on the specific recognition of pathogen effectors by R proteins, which triggers rapid signal transduction. This includes calcium influx, reactive oxygen species burst, and MAPK cascade activation, ultimately leading to systemic acquired resistance or hypersensitive response via the salicylic acid/jasmonic acid pathways [52]. In plants, the LRR domain is a well-established key component widely involved in stress resistance and defense responses [53]. In the present study, beyond confirming two classic resistance loci on chromosome 13, we identified three novel loci on chromosomes 11, 16, and 17 (Chr11_27826328, Chr16_34392503, and Chr17_18822285). Notably, we discovered a candidate gene, Glyma.16g182700, harboring an LRR domain on chromosome 16. Research by He et al. [54] on NBS-LRR genes in the Rsv1 locus further confirms the function of the LRR domain, directly revealing its critical role in the regulation of plant immune responses. Haplotype analysis revealed significant differences between Hap1 and Hap2 of Glyma.16g182700. RT-qPCR analysis demonstrated that the relative expression level of the candidate gene Glyma.16g182700 in susceptible soybean materials was significantly lower than that in resistant soybean materials. Collectively, these findings indicate that Glyma.16g182700 positively regulates soybean resistance to SMV strain SC7. The results of this study are valuable for elucidating the genetic basis of disease resistance traits. Moreover, this gene locus may serve as a potential molecular marker, facilitating the genetic improvement of crop disease resistance.

Genomic selection

GS is a breeding strategy that utilizes high-density genetic markers to accelerate breeding programs. This approach has seen large-scale implementation in genetic improvement programs for various species, including maize and dairy cattle. Prediction accuracy serves as a critical indicator of the efficiency of GS. In this study, we evaluated seven GS models—BL, BA, BB, BRR, SVM, RF, and rrBLUP—using various SNP sets to predict genomic estimated breeding values for SMV SC7 resistance in a natural soybean population. The rrBLUP model demonstrated the highest accuracy (0.682) when applied to the Top 100 SNP set, significantly outperforming the other models, including RF, which achieved an accuracy of 0.496.

Furthermore, analysis of random SNP sets indicated that prediction accuracy was maximized at a marker size of 6,000. Due to its superior performance across various SNP sets, the rrBLUP model was selected for genomic prediction in this study [55]. The efficacy of rrBLUP has been demonstrated across numerous crop traits. Admas Alemu et al. [56] reported that haplotype-based markers utilized alongside rrBLUP improved prediction accuracy for Fusarium head blight resistance and yield in wheat by 0.092 compared to other models. Similarly, Man et al. [57, 58] applied rrBLUP to soybean, discovering that prediction accuracy for protein and oil content increased with training population size, peaking at 3,000 SNPs. In tomato, Yeon et al. [59] demonstrated that rrBLUP achieved higher accuracy than non-parametric models for bacterial wilt resistance using low-density SNPs, thereby underscoring its practical breeding value. While GS has been successfully employed for traits such as yield and quality, its application specifically to SMV SC7 resistance remains limited. This study addresses this gap by developing a genomic prediction resource for SMV SC7 resistance using the rrBLUP model. A comparison between the GWAS-derived SNP set and random SNP sets revealed that the former provided significantly higher prediction accuracy. Utilization of efficient SNP markers in GS confers a substantial improvement in the prediction accuracy for SMV SC7 resistance.

This study significantly enhanced the prediction accuracy and efficiency of disease resistance traits by extracting SNP markers strongly associated with resistance to SMV SC7 and integrating them into GS prediction models. We systematically evaluated the performance of multiple prediction models, and the results demonstrated that rrBLUP consistently achieved superior and stable predictive performance for this type of complex quantitative trait. This finding not only confirms the statistical advantages of rrBLUP in modeling such genetic architectures but also provides empirical support for selecting appropriate prediction tools in soybean disease resistance breeding, thereby helping to reduce trial-and-error costs in practical breeding programs. Therefore, the integration of key loci identified through genome-wide association studies with efficient GS models represents a critical step in transitioning from traditional phenotypic selection to precision molecular design breeding, offering a viable strategy and theoretical foundation for accelerating the development of new SMV-resistant varieties.

Conclusions

Our study of 290 soybean materials provides valuable insights into the resistance to SMV strain SC7, spanning from genetic discovery to breeding application. Firstly, GWAS and gene annotation identified Glyma.16g182700 as a candidate gene. Its homology to an LRR kinase in Arabidopsis suggests a complex role in immunity, which is further supported by the observed haplotype effects. Secondly, genomic selection analysis demonstrated that GWAS-derived SNPs outperform random sets, with 6,000 markers identified as the cost-effective optimum for accurate prediction. The findings of this study provide valuable genetic resources for future disease-resistant breeding and disease management strategies.

Complete tripartite subgraphs of balanced tripartite graphs with large minimum degree
Yihan Chen Jialin He Allan Lo Cong Luo Jie Ma Yi Zhao

Abstract




Abstract

In 1975, Bollobás, Erdős, and Szemerédi
[
4
]
asked what minimum degree guarantees an octahedral subgraph
K_{3}(2)
in any tripartite graph
G
with
n
vertices in each vertex class. We show that
\delta(G)\geq n+2n^{\frac{5}{6}}
suffices, thus improving the bound
n+(1+o(1))n^{\frac{11}{12}}
of Bhalkikar and Zhao
[
2
]
obtained by following the approach of
[
4
]
. Bollobás, Erdős, and Szemerédi conjectured that
n+cn^{\frac{1}{2}}
suffices and there are many
K_{3}(2)
-free tripartite graphs
G
with
\delta(G)\geq n+cn^{\frac{1}{2}}
. In the appendix we prove this conjecture under the additional assumption that every vertex in
G
is adjacent to at least
(1/5+\varepsilon)n
vertices in any other vertex class.

Introduction

As a foundation stone of extremal graph theory, the celebrated Turán’s theorem in 1941
[
21
]
determined the maximum size
{\rm ex}(n,K_{t})
of graphs
G
of order
n
without
K_{t}
as a subgraph (the
t=3
case is also known as Mantel’s theorem
[
18
]
). In 1974, Bollobás, Erdős, and Straus
[
3
]
obtained a Turán-type result that determined the maximum size of an
r
-partite graph not containing
K_{t}
as a subgraph for any
r\geq t
. In 1975, Bollobás, Erdős, and Szemerédi
[
4
]
investigated the
r=t
case of the following problem.

Problem 1.1
.

Given integers
n
and
3\leq t\leq r
, what is the largest minimum degree
\delta(G)
among all
r
-partite graphs
G
with parts of size
n
and which do not contain a copy of
K_{t}
?

The
r=t
case has received a lot of attention and has found applications in linear arboricity, hypergraph matching, list coloring, among others (see
[
7
]
and references therein). Graver (see
[
4
]
) answered Problem
1.1
for
r=t=3
and Jin
[
12
]
solved it for
r=t=4,5
.
The
r=t
case of Problem
1.1
was finally settled by Haxell
[
9
]
, Haxell and Szabó
[
10
]
and Szabó and Tardos
[
20
]
. Recently Lo, Treglown, and Zhao
[
16
]
solved many
r>t
cases of the problem, including when
r\equiv-1

(\text{mod }t-1)
and
r\geq(3t-4)(t-2)
. For more related results, we refer interested readers to
[
1
,
9
,
10
,
12
,
20
]
.

Let
G_{r}(n)
be an (arbitrary)
balanced
r
-partite graph
with parts of size
n
, and let
K_{r}(s)
denote the complete
r
-partite graph with parts of size
s
. The aforementioned result of Graver states that every graph
G=G_{3}(n)
with
\delta(G)\geq n+1
contains a copy of
K_{3}
(and this is best possible). Bollobás, Erdős, and Szemerédi
[
4
]
asked when
G_{3}(n)
contains
K_{3}(2)
(known as the
octahedral graph
) as a subgraph.

Problem 1.2
.

What minimum degree
\delta(G)
in a graph
G=G_{3}(n)
ensures a subgraph
K_{3}(2)
?

The authors of
[
4
]
conjectured that
\delta(G)\geq n+cn^{\frac{1}{2}}
is sufficient for some constant
c
.
They also stated that for
n\geq 2^{8}
,
\delta(G)\geq n+2^{-\frac{1}{2}}n^{\frac{3}{4}}
guarantees a
K_{3}(2)
, which follows from their result on
K_{3}(s)

[
4
, Theorem 2.6]
. Unfortunately, Bhalkikar and Zhao
[
2
]
found a miscalculation in the proof of
[
4
, Theorem 2.6]
. After correcting this error, they followed the approach in
[
4
]
and obtained the following result.

Theorem 1.3

(
[
2
]
)
.

Given
s\geq 2
,
\varepsilon>0
, and sufficiently large
n
, every graph
G=G_{3}(n)
with
\delta(G)\geq n+(1+\varepsilon)(s-1)^{1/(3s^{2})}n^{1-1/(3s^{2})}
contains a copy of
K_{3}(s)
. In particular,
\delta(G)\geq n+(1+\varepsilon)n^{\frac{11}{12}}
guarantees a
K_{3}(2)
.

In this paper, we establish several results towards Problem
1.2
.
First we improve Theorem
1.3
as follows.

Theorem 1.4
.

Given
s\geq 2
,
C=2(s-1)^{\frac{1}{s+1}}
, and sufficiently large
n
, every graph
G=G_{3}(n)
with

\delta(G)\geq n+Cn^{1-\frac{1}{s(s+1)}}
contains a copy of
K_{3}(s)
.
In particular, if
\delta(G)\geq n+2n^{\frac{5}{6}}
, then
G
contains a
K_{3}(2)
.

This is a strengthening of
[
2
]
, as the way to fix
[
4
]
. Note that we can easily replace the constant
2
in
C
by
1+o(1)
but use the current definition of
C
for simplicity.

Let
V_{1},V_{2},V_{3}
be the three vertex classes of a graph
G=G_{3}(n)
.
We call
\min\{d_{G}(v,V_{i}):v\in V(G)\setminus V_{i},i\in[3]\}
the
minimum partial degree
of
G
,
where
d_{G}(v,V_{i})
denotes the number of neighbors of
v
in
V_{i}
. Partial degree (also known as
partite degree
and
local degree
) conditions have been widely studied in multipartite settings, for instance in the study of multipartite Hajnal–Szemerédi theorem as well as Fischer’s conjecture
[
8
,
15
]
, and the existence of independent transversal (factors) conditioned on local degree
[
6
,
11
,
13
,
17
,
22
]
.
Motivated by these works, we establish a result which shows that
G=G_{3}(n)
contains a copy of
K_{3}(2)
under the conjectured condition
\delta(G)\geq n+cn^{\frac{1}{2}}
if, in addition, the minimum partial degree is at least
(1/5+\varepsilon)n
. The precise statement and proof are given in Appendix
A
.

Bhalkikar and Zhao
[
2
]
also constructed many non-isomorphic
K_{3}(2)
-free tripartite graphs with minimum degree at least
n+n^{\frac{1}{2}}
. In Section
3
, we construct new
K_{3}(2)
-free tripartite graphs with minimum degree
n+(1-o(1))n^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

For a graph
G
, let
T(G)
denote the number of triangles in
G
. For
n,t\in\mathbb{N}
, let
f(n,t)
denote the minimum
T(G)
over all
G=G_{3}(n)
of minimum degree
\delta(G)
at least
n+t
. Bollobás, Erdős, and Szemerédi
[
4
]
also studied
f(n,t)
. They showed that
f(n,1)=4
for
n\geq 4
, and for general
t
, they proved
f(n,t)\geq t^{3}
. For
t\leq n/5
, they constructed tripartite graphs
G=G_{3}(n)
with
\delta(G)\geq n+t
and
T(G)=4t^{3}
, which implies
f(n,t)\leq 4t^{3}
.
Based on this, they asked that for
t\leq n/5
, is it true that
f(n,t)\geq 4t^{3}
? We give a simple proof of
f(n,t)\geq n^{2}(3t-n)/2
, which improves
f(n,t)\geq t^{3}
when
\frac{\sqrt{3}-1}{2}n\leq t\leq n
, and gives the exact value of
f(n,t)
for even
n
and
t\geq n/2
.

Proposition 1.5
.

For
1\leq t\leq n
,
f(n,t)\geq n^{2}(3t-n)/2
and equality holds if
n
is even and
t\geq n/2
.

1.1
Notation

Recall that
G=G_{3}(n)
is an (arbitrary) balanced tripartite graph with parts
V_{1},V_{2},V_{3}
such that
|V_{i}|=n
for
i=1,2,3
. Let
V(G)
denote the vertex set of
G
and
E(G)
denote the edge set of
G
.
We consider the subscript of
V_{i}
to be
modulo
3
with values
1,2,3
, instead of
0,1,2
.

Given
v\in V(G)
, we write
N(v)
for the neighborhood of
v
and define
d_{G}(v)=|N(v)|
as the degree of
v
in
G
. Let
\delta(G)
and
e(G)
denote the minimum degree and the number of edges of
G
respectively.
We often view
G
as an oriented graph with edges directed from
V_{i}
to
V_{i+1}
.
For
v\in V_{i}
, let
N_{G}^{+}(v)
(resp.
N_{G}^{-}(v)
) be the set of vertices in
V_{i+1}
(resp.
V_{i-1}
) that are joined to
v
. Let
d_{G}^{+}(v)=|N_{G}^{+}(v)|
and
\delta^{+}(G)=\min\limits_{v\in V(G)}|N_{G}^{+}(v)|
.
We define
d_{G}^{-}(v)
and
\delta^{-}(G)
analogously.
For
W\subseteq V(G)
, we define
d_{G}(v,W)
as the number of edges between
v
and
W
.

For graphs
G
and
H
,
G-H
is the subgraph of
G
obtained by deleting edges in
E(G)\cap E(H)
.
For
W\subseteq V(G)
,
G[W]
and
G\setminus W
are the induced subgraphs of
G
on
W
and
V(G)\setminus W
, respectively.
We write
G[A,B]
for the induced bipartite subgraph of
G
with parts
A
and
B
.
We often write
\{x,y\}
as
xy
; for
xy\in E(G)
, let
T_{G}(xy)
(or simply
T(xy)
when
G
is clear) denote the number of triangles in
G
that contain
x
and
y
.

Finally, let
[n]
denote the set
\{1,\dots,n\}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Given a set
X
and an integer
s
, we write
\binom{X}{s}
for the set of all
s
-element subsets of
X
.
We omit floors and ceilings whenever this does not affect the argument.

Proofs of Theorem
1.4
and Proposition
1.5

Let us denote by
z(m,n;s,s)
the Zarankiewicz number, which is the maximum number of edges in a bipartite graph
G=(U,V;E)
with
|U|=m
and
|V|=n
containing no copy of
K_{s,s}
. The following well-known result on
z(m,n;s,s)
was proved by Kövári, Sós and Turán
[
14
]
.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
14
]
)
.

For
s,m,n\in\mathbb{N}
,

z(m,n;s,s)\leq(s-1)^{\frac{1}{s}}mn^{1-\frac{1}{s}}+(s-1)n.

Next we prove Theorem
1.4
by double counting and Lemma
2.1
. This approach is similar to the one used in
[
2
,
4
]
and our improvement on
\delta(G)
is due to the counting of triangles
xyz
with
x\in T_{1}
and
y\in T_{x}
(where
T_{1}\subseteq V_{1}
and
T_{x}\subseteq V_{3}
are subsets of size
t
) instead of
x\in V_{1}
and
y\in V_{3}
as in
[
2
,
4
]
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Let
t=\left\lceil Cn^{1-\frac{1}{s(s+1)}}\right\rceil
, where
C=2(s-1)^{\frac{1}{s+1}}
. Since
\delta(G)
is an integer, the hypothesis of the theorem implies that
\delta(G)\geq n+t
.
Let
d_{G}^{+}(S)=\sum_{x\in S}d^{+}_{G}(x)
for any set
S\subseteq V(G)
.
Without loss of generality, suppose that there is a subset
T_{1}\subseteq V_{1}
of size
t
satisfying

\displaystyle d_{G}^{+}(T_{1})=\max\{d_{G}^{+}(T)\colon\text{$|T|=t$, $T\subseteq V_{i}$, $i\in[3]$}\}.

For
x\in V_{1}
, let
T_{x}\subset V_{3}
be an arbitrary
t
-element subset of
N_{G}^{-}(x)
.
Note that
\delta(G)\geq n+t
guarantees that
T_{x}
exists.
Since
d^{+}(y)+d^{-}(y)\geq n+t
for any
y\in V(G)
, we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{x\in T_{1},y\in T_{x}}T(xy)

\displaystyle\geq\sum\limits_{x\in T_{1}}\sum\limits_{y\in T_{x}}\left(d_{G}^{+}(x)+d_{G}^{-}(y)-n\right)\geq\sum\limits_{x\in T_{1}}\sum\limits_{y\in T_{x}}\left(t+d_{G}^{+}(x)-d_{G}^{+}(y)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{x\in T_{1}}\left(t^{2}+td_{G}^{+}(x)-d_{G}^{+}(T_{x})\right)=t^{3}+td^{+}_{G}(T_{1})-\sum\limits_{x\in T_{1}}d_{G}^{+}(T_{x}).

Using the maximality of
d_{G}^{+}(T_{1})
, we derive that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{x\in T_{1},y\in T_{x}}T(xy)\geq t^{3}+td_{G}^{+}(T_{1})-td_{G}^{+}(T_{1})=t^{3}.

For any
s
distinct vertices
z_{1},\dots,z_{s}\in V_{2}
, let

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(z_{1},\dots,z_{s})=\left\{xy\in E(G):x\in T_{1},y\in T_{x},G[\{x,y,z_{i}\}]\text{ is a triangle for all }i\in[s]\right\},

and let
q=\left\lfloor\frac{1}{t^{2}}{\sum\limits_{x\in T_{1},y\in T_{x}}T(xy)}\right\rfloor
.
By double counting and the discrete convexity of
\binom{k}{s}
in its upper index
k
, we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\{z_{1},\dots,z_{s}\}\in\binom{V_{2}}{s}}|\mathcal{T}(z_{1},\dots,z_{s})|

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{x\in T_{1},y\in T_{x}}\binom{T(xy)}{s}\geq t^{2}\binom{q}{s}\geq t^{2}\binom{t}{s}.

Since
s
is fixed and
n
is sufficiently large, we may assume that
n\geq s
and
t\geq 2(s-1)
. By averaging, there exist
s
distinct vertices
z_{1},\dots,z_{s}\in V_{2}
such that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{T}(z_{1},\dots,z_{s})|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{t^{2}\binom{t}{s}}{\binom{n}{s}}\geq\frac{t^{s+2}}{2^{s}n^{s}}\geq\frac{C^{s+1}}{2^{s}}tn^{1-\frac{1}{s}}=2(s-1)tn^{1-\frac{1}{s}}

\displaystyle>(s-1)tn^{1-\frac{1}{s}}+(s-1)n\geq z(t,n;s,s).

Here the strict inequality follows from
t>n^{\frac{1}{s}}
, which holds by the definition of
t
, since
C\geq 2
and
1-\frac{1}{s(s+1)}>\frac{1}{s}
. The last inequality follows from Lemma
2.1
and the fact that

(s-1)^{\frac{1}{s}}\leq s-1
.
Thus, the bipartite graph on
T_{1}\cup V_{3}
with the edge set
\mathcal{T}(z_{1},\dots,z_{s})
contains a copy of
K_{s,s}
. Together with
z_{1},\dots,z_{s}
, this gives the desired copy of
K_{3}(s)
in
G
. ∎

We next prove Proposition
1.5
.

Proof of Proposition
1.5
.

Let
G=G_{3}(n)
with
\delta(G)\geq n+t
.
Let
\overline{G}
be the tripartite complement graph of
G
, that is
K_{3}(n)-G
, where
K_{3}(n)
has the same vertex classes as
G
.
Note that
\Delta(\overline{G})\leq n-t
and
e(\overline{G})\leq 3n(n-t)/2
.
Since each edge is in at most
n
triangles, so

\displaystyle T(G)\geq T(K_{3}(n))-ne(\overline{G})\geq n^{3}-\frac{3n^{2}(n-t)}{2}=\frac{1}{2}n^{2}(3t-n).

Thus we have
f(n,t)\geq n^{2}(3t-n)/2
.

Suppose that
n
is even and
t\geq n/2
.
Let
A_{1},B_{1},A_{2},B_{2},A_{3},B_{3}
be disjoint vertex sets each of size
n/2
.
For
i\in[3]
, let
V_{i}=A_{i}\cup B_{i}
.
Let
H
be a tripartite graph with vertex classes
V_{1},V_{2},V_{3}
such that
H=\bigcup_{i\in[3]}H[A_{i+1},B_{i}]
where each
H[A_{i+1},B_{i}]
is
(n-t)
-regular. Indeed, since
K_{n/2,n/2}
decomposes into
n/2
edge-disjoint perfect matchings and
0\leq n-t\leq n/2
, each
H[A_{i+1},B_{i}]
can be chosen as the union of
n-t
of them.
Let
G
be the tripartite complement graph of
H
, so
G
is
(n+t)
-regular.
Since no triangle in
K_{3}(n)
contains two edges of
H
, by the calculation above we have
T(G)=n^{2}(3t-n)/2
.
∎

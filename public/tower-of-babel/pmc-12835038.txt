Deep learning denoising enables rapid SEM imaging under charging conditions for FE SEM, CD SEM, and review SEM
Authors: Hyungjoo Park, Beom-Seok Oh, Kuk Jin Jang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn scanning electron microscopy (SEM), rapid image acquisition is essential to prevent charging on non-conductive samples. However, fast acquisition often yields noisy images due to insufficient electron signal, whereas longer exposures improve quality but increase charging risk, causing distortion or signal loss. Conventional noise reduction via multi-frame averaging is effective but often impractical under low-voltage, high-speed conditions. This study evaluates four transformer-based denoising models–Restormer, NAFNet, HINet, and CGNet–applied to SEM images acquired under charging-sensitive conditions. All models were trained on an in-house paired dataset with 2-frame inputs and 32-frame references. We report full-reference metrics (PSNR/SSIM) and perceptual metrics (LPIPS/DISTS), include a small blinded MOS assessment, and use paired per-FOV statistics with 95% confidence intervals to confirm significant gains over the 2-frame baseline. While validation results were promising, performance decreased on a held-out test set of newly acquired SEM data, underscoring the need for broader data and model generalization. Among the evaluated models, NAFNet achieved the best overall fidelity and latency–approaching the 16-frame quality band with an 66 speed-up over 32-frame acquisition–whereas Restormer preserved circular geometry most faithfully under distribution shift; HINet and CGNet showed more visible geometric distortions. These findings clarify the practical speed–quality benefit of two-frame + AI denoising and highlight the importance of visual/perceptual evaluation alongside conventional metrics for robust deployment in FE-SEM, CD-SEM and Review-SEM.Keywords: Denoising, Scanning electron microscopy (SEM), Deep learningSubject terms: Scanning electron microscopy, Imaging techniques, Computational science

Introduction

Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) is widely used in materials science and the semiconductor industry for nanoscale visualization. However, obtaining high-quality SEM images requires sufficient electron-beam exposure, which is challenging for non-conductive samples1. Longer exposure on such samples leads to electrostatic charge accumulation (”charging”), which can cause image distortion and signal loss2,3. In severe cases, charging produces local wash-out/saturation and edge bloom that obscure structure entirely4. To mitigate charging, images must be acquired rapidly; yet shorter dwell inherently reduces the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), yielding noisier images. Traditional workflows address this by multi-frame averaging (MFA)–often up to 32 frames–and hardware filtering. While effective, MFA is impractical for high-throughput applications such as field-emission SEM (FE-SEM) in production settings, critical-dimension SEM (CD-SEM), and Review-SEM, where imaging must be both rapid and low-voltage to minimize charging risk5.

Recently, deep learning–based denoising techniques have achieved remarkable results in natural image restoration6–8. A concise overview of architecture trends and training strategies for efficient denoising is provided by a recent survey9. In particular, transformer-based models10 have shown superior performance by capturing long-range dependencies while preserving structure11. However, their application to SEM remains underexplored, especially under practical, high-speed acquisition settings. SEM quality is also sensitive to small changes in acquisition conditions–beam alignment, focus, accelerating voltage–even on the same instrument and sample12. Such variability challenges generalization when data diversity is limited, and even slight condition changes can degrade performance. It is therefore important to evaluate the robustness and generalization of AI-based denoising under realistic SEM variability. Moreover, uncorrected inter-frame drift or scan-line shifts during MFA can bias geometry despite high SNR13,14.

Here, we evaluate four state-of-the-art denoisers–Restormer15, NAFNet16, HINet17, and CGNet18–which have demonstrated strong performance on SIDD. We apply them to a custom SEM dataset acquired under charging-sensitive conditions and assess both quantitative and qualitative performance. We also compare model outputs against instrument-generated MFA from 2 to 32 frames. Experimental results show that Restormer and NAFNet reduce acquisition time by more than while achieving quality comparable to a 16-frame average using only a two-frame input. This study demonstrates the feasibility–and current limitations–of transformer-based denoising in SEM. The results further suggest the need for larger, more diverse SEM datasets to improve generalization for practical deployment.

Results

Experimental setup

We conducted supervised denoising experiments on images acquired with a LEO 1560 field-emission SEM. The specimen was a -on-Si high-aspect-ratio (HAR) hole-array wafer (depth 1.0 m), representative of CD-SEM targets. No conductive sputter coating was applied, consistent with CD-SEM workflows. To emulate high-throughput, charging-sensitive operation, we used a low-voltage acquisition protocol (1 kV; minimum supported) with charging-aware alignment and focus. Images were acquired across magnifications up to 100,000 while minimizing visible charging. For each site and timestamp, two co-registered conditions were recorded: a 2-frame average (low-SNR input) and a 32-frame average (high-SNR reference).

We benchmarked four state-of-the-art denoisers–Restormer, NAFNet, HINet, and CGNet–selected for strong performance on natural-image denoising benchmarks. All models were trained on the collected SEM pairs using a single NVIDIA RTX 6000 Ada (48 GB), following the authors’ official training configurations without architectural changes. Evaluation comprised full-reference fidelity (PSNR/SSIM), perceptual distances (LPIPS/DISTS; lower is better), and a human perceptual assessment via MOS (blinded rank-based preference; lower is better, expert raters). Computational metrics included multiply–accumulate operations (MACs), parameter counts, per-iteration training time, and inference latency.

Dataset summary. We collected 156 full-resolution FOV pairs at a native resolution of pixels (co-registered 2f/32f per site). Training/validation used geometry-preserving patch pairs extracted from disjoint FOVs, while testing used full-resolution FOV pairs (no cropping or rescaling). The split counts are 760 training patch pairs, 152 validation patch pairs, and 10 test FOV pairs. Splits were made at the site/FOV level to prevent leakage across crops.

Qualitative results

Visual evaluation was conducted by comparing the 2-frame input, AI-denoised outputs, and SEM multi-frame reference images using a representative test image (Fig. 4). Among the evaluated models, CGNet and HINet achieved relatively high PSNR and SSIM scores on the validation set; however, they exhibited the most pronounced geometric distortions in circular hole structures, including noticeable shape deformation. NAFNet also demonstrated stable preservation of structural integrity with relatively low visual distortion in the denoised outputs and achieved the fastest inference time among all models, highlighting its practicality for real-time SEM applications. Restormer, while exhibiting the lowest validation scores, ranked second in PSNR and SSIM on the test set. Although it showed the slowest inference time, it consistently preserved the geometry of circular features with the least distortion in visual comparisons. This suggests that Restormer may offer the most faithful restoration performance when structural fidelity is prioritized in SEM imaging tasks such as CD-SEM. These qualitative observations are consistent with perceptual metrics on the test set–LPIPS and DISTS.

These results demonstrate practical potential for transformer-based denoising models to reduce reliance on time-consuming multi-frame acquisition in high-speed FE-SEM and related SEM operations. Discussion

The experimental results demonstrate the potential of transformer-based denoising models for enhancing two-frame SEM images acquired under low-exposure conditions. While all models were able to restore visually meaningful information from noisy 2-frame inputs, their performance varied across validation and test sets. Notably, NAFNet consistently achieved the highest PSNR and SSIM on both validation and test datasets. It also exhibited low geometric distortion and the fastest inference speed, underscoring its suitability for real-time SEM applications. In contrast, CGNet and HINet showed competitive validation performance but experienced more pronounced geometric distortions in the denoised outputs–particularly in circular hole structures–when evaluated visually. Despite reasonable quantitative scores, these distortions undermine their reliability in high-precision imaging tasks such as CD-SEM. These results highlight that numerical metrics alone are insufficient for evaluating the performance of SEM restoration, especially when geometric fidelity is critical.

Restormer demonstrated relatively poor performance on the validation set but ranked second in quantitative metrics on the test set. More importantly, visual inspection revealed that Restormer preserved circular and structural integrity better than other models. Although it had the slowest inference time, its outputs exhibited the least perceptual distortion. In tasks requiring shape preservation and fidelity, Restormer proved qualitatively superior despite lower numerical indicators. The robustness of these models was further tested on newly acquired SEM images obtained under slight variations in acquisition parameters, including beam alignment, focus, and accelerating voltage. All models exhibited notable performance drops, with reduced PSNR and SSIM and increased structural artifacts in denoised images. This underscores a key limitation: the generalization ability of current models is constrained by the narrow distribution of training data and sensitivity to acquisition variability20.

Data scale, generalizability, and implications. Our evaluation is modest in scale and scope: we collected 156 native-resolution FOV pairs (), trained on crops, and tested on 10 held-out FOV pairs at the site level. Transformer-based restorers are known to be data-hungry; the combination of a single instrument/domain and crop-based training likely contributes to the observed validation–test gap and to sensitivity under mild acquisition changes. Furthermore, the present dataset under-represents important sources of variation (material types, landing energies, magnifications, detectors, working distances, alignment/focus offsets), limiting external validity. Consequently, the reported numbers should be interpreted as a domain-specific baseline for charging-aware, rapid acquisition on our setup–not a universal ranking across SEM conditions. To strengthen generalization, we plan a multi-instrument, multi-sample study spanning dielectric/conductive specimens, a range of kV/Working distance/magnification/detector settings, and cross-site splits (e.g., leave-one-instrument-out). We will also explore SEM-aware augmentation (defocus, drift, beam misalignment), domain adaptation, and larger-scale pretraining with unlabeled SEM to reduce distribution shift.

To address this challenge, future work should prioritize building broader SEM datasets encompassing diverse imaging settings, including different voltages, magnifications, sample types, and noise conditions. Data augmentation strategies, such as simulating defocus, drift, and beam misalignment, along with domain adaptation techniques, may enhance robustness under unseen conditions. In addition, integrating perceptual loss functions tailored to SEM characteristics or employing adversarial training methods could improve noise suppression and detail preservation simultaneously while minimizing hallucination features. These strategies may also support the development of SEM-specific image quality metrics21,22. Despite these limitations, this study demonstrates that transformer-based models, particularly NAFNet and Restormer, hold practical potential for replacing traditional multi-frame SEM acquisition in high-throughput, charging-sensitive environments. NAFNet’s lightweight design and fast inference further highlight its value for embedded or real-time applications23. This work lays a foundation for advancing AI-driven SEM restoration systems optimized for precision, speed, and operational efficiency.

Methods

Dataset

We constructed a paired SEM dataset in which each pair consists of a 2-frame averaged input and a 32-frame averaged reference acquired at the same site and timestamp. Raw frames are single-channel; linear normalization to [0, 1] is applied at dataloader time. For training/validation, patches are extracted with a sliding window or random cropping (crop ) and packed into LMDB for efficient I/O.

Splits are performed at the sample/site level to avoid leakage across crops from the same physical location. Training and validation use patch pairs; the test set is evaluated at the original resolution on full fields-of-view (FOVs) held out at the site level. The final split is: Train 760 paired patches, Val 152 paired patches, and Test 10 full-resolution FOV pairs (site-level hold-out, no cross-split sharing). Test metrics are computed on the uncropped FOVs (no rescaling), whereas Train/Val metrics are computed on patches.

Evaluation protocol and metrics

Validation metrics are computed on patch crops from the held-out validation split. Test metrics are computed on original-resolution FOV pairs (no rescaling or cropping), which better reflect end-to-end use and reveal sensitivity to site-level variability and nonuniform noise.

We report full-reference fidelity with PSNR and SSIM, and perceptual fidelity with LPIPS (Alex backbone) and DISTS. For PSNR/SSIM, computations follow standard definitions on linear images; for perceptual metrics, the single-channel SEM image is replicated to three channels and normalized to [0, 1] before evaluation to match the reference implementations. All metrics are computed per-FOV and then aggregated. A small blinded, rank-based MOS study was conducted with expert SEM raters. For each test FOV, raters evaluated anonymized outputs (2-frame, 4/8/16-frame SEM references, and four AI methods) presented in randomized order under identical viewing conditions. Each rater assigned a single integer rank from 1 (best) to 8 (worst) to reflect overall perceptual quality and sharpness preservation; ties were not allowed. We report mean ranks aggregated across FOVs.

Statistical reporting. We compute paired per-FOV differences against the SEM(2-frame) baseline: and (LPIPS/DISTS are summarized as raw values; lower is better). Each is summarized by the mean and a 95% t-confidence interval using ( FOVs); two-sided paired t-tests are reported where noted. When comparing multiple methods per metric, we control the familywise error rate with Holm–Bonferroni adjustment. Given the small sample size, we emphasize effect sizes and confidence intervals over p-values.

Training configuration

We use each model’s official training recipe and default hyperparameters from the authors’ repositories (optimizer, learning-rate schedule, batch size) without architectural or loss modifications. All models are trained from scratch on the SEM pairs (2f32f). Unless noted otherwise, automatic mixed precision is enabled and the global seed is fixed to 10 (initialization, dataloader shuffling, augmentations). Computational metrics (FLOPs, per-iteration training time, and inference latency) are measured on an NVIDIA RTX 6000 Ada with single-channel inputs; inference times exclude disk I/O and include a warm-up phase. Model summaries

Restormer is a transformer-based restoration model that uses Multi-Dconv Head Transposed Attention (MDTA) and a Gated-Dconv Feed-Forward Network (GDFN) to efficiently capture long-range spatial dependencies.

NAFNet (Nonlinear Activation-Free Network) adopts a high-efficiency residual architecture without nonlinear activations, yielding fast convergence and low latency while maintaining strong restoration quality.

CGNet (CascadedGaze) progressively suppresses noise through cascaded residual blocks; while not transformer-based, it is a competitive baseline for reconstruction.

HINet employs Half Instance Normalization and a two-stage coarse-to-fine scheme with a dual-branch design to handle complex noise patterns.

Conclusion

This study evaluated the feasibility of applying state-of-the-art transformer-based denoising models to two-frame SEM images acquired under fast, low-voltage conditions. Using a custom dataset composed of 2-frame noisy inputs and 32-frame averaged references, we trained and compared four models–Restormer, NAFNet, HINet, and CGNet–against conventional multi-frame acquisition. Among them, NAFNet achieved the highest PSNR/SSIM across both validation and test datasets and offered the fastest inference, while Restormer, despite lower validation scores, performed competitively on held-out FOVs and showed the least perceptual distortion when preserving circular and edge integrity. HINet and CGNet attained strong validation scores but exhibited higher geometric distortion on test FOVs. Perceptual metrics (LPIPS/DISTS) and human ratings (MOS) were consistent with these qualitative trends.

All models showed significant performance degradation when applied to test images acquired under slight changes in alignment, focus, and accelerating voltage, suggesting limited robustness to acquisition variability when training diversity is narrow20. Despite this limitation, transformer-based denoising can reduce SEM acquisition time by more than while approaching the 16–32-frame quality band on our setup, establishing a practical baseline for AI-enhanced SEM in charging-sensitive environments across FE-SEM, CD-SEM, and Review-SEM. Future work will expand dataset diversity (materials, kV, magnification, detector, working distance, alignment/focus), incorporate SEM-aware augmentation and domain adaptation, and pursue deployment-oriented optimization (mixed precision/quantization, I/O-aware tiling) to improve generalization and real-time performance23,24.

Can economic news sentiment inspire entrepreneurship
Authors: Deng Zihan, Zhu Fuxian, Xu Xiaoli
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAs a way to guide and supervise public opinion, news inevitably contains certain emotional tendencies. Whether such emotional tendencies will have an impact on the entrepreneurial decisions and behaviors of micro-subjects has become an economic topic of great research value. This thesis takes prefecture-level cities from 2013 to 2023 as research samples, compares existing large language models, and uses LERT large language model with better overall performance to measure monthly economic news sentiment in cities, and further studies the impact of news sentiment on regional entrepreneurial vitality. The results of the study found that positive economic news sentiment can increase entrepreneurial vitality in cities, and passed a series of robustness tests. The mechanism analysis shows that the economic news sentiment mainly improves the entrepreneurial vitality of cities by changing expectations, which is embodied in the effect of boosting consumption of consumers, stimulating demand of entrepreneurs and lowering service threshold of financial institutions. Moreover, business environment and regional entrepreneurial failure tolerance can strengthen the effect of economic news sentiment on entrepreneurial vitality. Heterogeneity analysis shows that in areas with lower housing price volatility, higher public attention and lower unemployment rate, economic news sentiment has a more significant promoting effect on urban entrepreneurial vitality. The article provides certain reference significance for exploring how to effectively use modern media to boost the confidence of entrepreneurs, continuously promote mass entrepreneurship to stimulate market vitality, and then cultivate the new momentum of endogenous economic growth when the economy is expected to weaken.Keywords: LERT model, Economic news sentiment, Urban entrepreneurial vitality, Expected boostSubject terms: Environmental social sciences, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Along with the continuous evolution of the global economic situation and the deep adjustment of the domestic economic structure, urban entrepreneurial vitality (UEV) has become the core element to drive the connotative growth of the regional economy and build an innovative economic development model. How to continuously promote mass entrepreneurship is an important issue that needs to be addressed in the process of stimulating market vitality, promoting high-quality economic development, and fostering new dynamic energy for economic development.

Entrepreneurship is a process of “creative destruction” in which entrepreneurs recombine factors of production and develop resources and factors of production into new products, new markets, and new technologies of higher value1. UEV is an abstract manifestation of entrepreneurial behavior in the regional dimension. Under the current economic operation structure, the influence of the information transmission mechanism on the decision-making of various economic subjects and the efficiency of market resource allocation is deepening. As the main transmission medium of economic information, economic news inevitably carries a specific emotional bias in the process of content generation and dissemination.

From the perspective of behavioral economics, this emotional bias can be regarded as a kind of information noise that affects the formation of market participants’ expectations. It is very likely to change the micro entrepreneurial subject’s perception of market signals, assessment of risk and benefit, as well as the prediction of the future economic situation. This, in turn, affects entrepreneurs’ cognitive frameworks and decision-making reference points, and then changes their own decision-making behavior. Therefore, the question arises: Can focusing on economic news sentiment (ENS) promote urban entrepreneurship and enhance economic vitality? If there are positive effects, what are the channels of influence? And what factors reinforce or limit the role of news media orientation? The analysis of these questions will help to understand the theoretical and practical significance of narrative economics.

Existing research suggests that the complex environmental relationship between the coexistence and interaction of government and market logics is an important factor influencing entrepreneurial activities2,3. This relationship has been centrally categorized into formal institutional factors such as open data policy and Free Trade Zone (FTZ) policy4,5. However, there are fewer studies on the impact of news media in informal institutions. Given this gap, this thesis attempts to examine the impact of ENS on the overall UEV of a city and its channels of action from the perspective of expectation boosting, using Enterprise Search data and panel data of 314 prefecture-level cities from 2013 to 2023.

The research contributions of this thesis are mainly reflected in the following points:

First, most studies on the influence of media reports on the behavior of micro subjects are centered on the number of media reports. In fact, compared with the number of media reports, the emotional tendency of media reports tends to have a more direct impact on market participants. However, due to objective limitations in data acquisition, few studies have focused on the impact of media coverage on market participants’ decision-making. In domestic studies on media coverage and UEV, most measure media coverage using news reports included in databases6,7. Compared with traditional thesis media, Internet news media has a wider audience, and its news information not only spreads faster but also more widely. Although Xu et al.8 have tried to use the index of online media attention in related research, they have not taken into account the emotional tendency contained in Internet media reports. Therefore, in this thesis, we use a deep learning-based big language model to measure the emotional tendency of news. Through the dual strategy of explicit emotional vocabulary capture and implicit contextual emotional interpretation, the model is able to more accurately portray the characterization of economic news emotions.

Second, in the field of research on the association between economics and entrepreneurial behavior, entrepreneurial activities are characterized by a significant time lag9. There is a certain time span between the initial creative conception of entrepreneurship, through a series of preparatory links, to the final landing operation and gradual market expansion. Because of this, news media reports play a crucial role in the entrepreneurial process of market entities and have a more direct impact on entrepreneurial activities. Additionally, there is a lack of systematic compilation and rigorous empirical testing of the internal mechanisms by which news media coverage affects entrepreneurial vigor. Therefore, this thesis takes the impact of news sentiment on corporate UEV as an entry point and meticulously combs its intrinsic influence mechanisms, such as expectation shifts, based on the consideration of the lag of entrepreneurial behaviors. This provides a certain reference for further exerting the media’s role of public opinion guidance and supervision.

Third, in the existing research results, the vast majority explore the impact of news reports from the annual dimension10,11. However, news has the significant characteristic of fast dissemination speed. If we consider news sentiment only in terms of years as the unit of measurement, it is too broad and general, making it difficult to accurately and effectively present the unique nature and dynamic changes of news impact on market players. This leads to a certain deviation between the research results and the actual situation, failing to reflect the real reaction of market players under the impact of the immediacy and variability of news dissemination. Therefore, this thesis takes monthly data as the research object and carefully portrays the monthly news sentiment and monthly UEV. This approach conforms to the news dissemination and effective cycle in terms of months and is able to accurately portray the market reaction under the influence of news sentiment.

In view of the above existing studies, this thesis explores in depth the impact of ENS on UEV and systematically examines the transmission mechanism and the boundary of the effect, in order to make up for the lack of existing studies in these aspects.

Literature sorting and research hypotheses

Literature sorting

Research progression of sentiment analysis

The related research on text sentiment analysis is currently divided into three categories of methods: sentiment dictionary analysis, machine learning analysis and deep learning analysis. Sentiment analysis based on sentiment lexicon is mainly used to infer the sentiment polarity of a word by its frequency of occurrence in the corpus, and the accuracy of the results of this method depends on the quality of the selected words. For example, Loughran and MacDonald constructed an English financial sentiment lexicon (LM lexicon) for the adjustment and optimization of the financial domain based on the consideration that traditional sentiment lexicons (such as SentiWordNet or General Inquirer) are not suitable in their specific phrases, jargons, and contexts, and in the domestic context because of the specificity of the financial environment. In China, because of the specificity of the financial environment, the use of the LM lexicon for financial texts in the Chinese context produces certain deviations. Therefore, some scholars have merged, improved, and expanded the three ways of screening financial sentiment words in the Chinese general sentiment dictionary and using the word2vec deep learning algorithm to find words similar to the financial sentiment words, to construct a relatively more comprehensive Chinese financial sentiment lexicon with a wider scope of coverage and greater applicability12,13.

Machine learning-based sentiment analysis trains sentiment classifiers by manually designing features and converting them into multidimensional feature vectors, which in turn determine the sentiment tendency of new sentences14. Common machine learning methods include both supervised and unsupervised approaches15. Supervised learning methods rely on manually labeled large amounts of textual data to label the sentiment tendency of each document, and use this labeled data as a training set for sentiment classification. Common supervised methods include Support Vector Machine (SVM), Plain Bayes (NB), and Maximum Entropy (ME)16,17. Despite the high classification accuracy of these methods, they require a large amount of manual labeling and the specialization of the labelers has a large impact. Unsupervised learning methods, on the other hand, do not rely on manual labeling, but rather on topic slicing by selecting sentiment vocabulary with relatively low accuracy. This method relies on manual feature selection, and the goodness of the features directly determines the effectiveness of the model, and there is a greater difficulty in generalization18.

Pretrained Models (PTM) sentiment classification approach in deep learning refers to the fine-tuning of a model that has been trained with a large-scale dataset to fit the needs of a specific task19. This approach is able to avoid the high cost of training a model from scratch and leverage the powerful semantic representation of pre-trained models, which has led to a significant increase in the application of deep learning in the field of sentiment analysis through enhanced training techniques. The application of natural language processing techniques in sentiment analysis and opinion mining of specific texts has widely penetrated into various industries. For example, Xu & Tian17 used the BERT model to analyze the sentiment of financial news and further explored the relationship between sentiment fluctuations and stock market ups and downs. On the other hand, Liu and Xiao20 developed a patent text classification system using the BERT model and successfully identified patent information related to labor-saving technologies. In addition, Acikalin et al.21 used annotated patent texts, trained based on the Longformer model (an improved version of the BERT model), to predict whether a firm’s patents are likely to be affected by a particular U.S. judicial precedent. These studies are not limited to patent analysis, but also cover a variety of areas such as asset pricing, corporate governance and consumer behavior.

Conducting effect of news emotions

Narrative economics suggests that mood swings in media coverage (e.g., heightened pessimism) actually constitute a “narrative” of sorts and thus have an impact on markets. This type of emotional coverage shapes the collective beliefs and expectations of subjects of interest through news, analysis and forecasting stories22. For example, when the media reports on the risk of a recession, it is not just conveying information, it is telling a ‘recession’ story that is accepted by investors and influences their decisions. These narratives stimulate an emotional response in the market through the transmission of emotions, leading to stock market volatility23. This view is also confirmed by related scholarly studies, such as Tetlock24 who found that mood swings in the news, especially increased pessimism, can exacerbate downward pressure on the market. Abnormal fluctuations in sentiment (either too high or too low) can lead to a temporary increase in trading volume25. And weekly news sentiment has a more persistent effect on the market than daily news sentiment26. Of course, news sentiment also exists intertemporal effect and asymmetric effect, intertemporal mainly emphasizes that the current news sentiment can have a significant impact on the market behavior in the future period of time, and asymmetric mainly refers to the fact that the degree of impact of different types of sentiment on the market is different27. In addition to this there are scholars who have studied the transmission effect of news sentiment in terms of housing price fluctuations28, surplus management29, and asset pricing30.

Studies related to entrepreneurial dynamism

Entrepreneurship plays an important role in driving employment and promoting high-quality economic development. Since the call for “mass entrepreneurship and innovation” was put forward in 2015, more and more scholars have begun to pay attention to the entrepreneurial activities of micro-entrepreneurs and entrepreneurial activity at the regional level, and to explore the factors influencing entrepreneurship. Some studies approach from a micro perspective, pointing out that cognitive ability, social capital, and social insurance are all important factors influencing individuals’ entrepreneurial decisions31–33, while others explore differences in UEV in different regions from macro perspectives, such as business environments and infrastructures34–36. With the advent of the digital economy era, more and more scholars have confirmed the pulling effect of external macro-environment on entrepreneurship, but this part of the research mainly focuses on the role of public governance tools (e.g., policies, public goods investment, etc.) in the construction of entrepreneurial environments and the incentives of entrepreneurial subjects37,38.

Based on the above analysis, few studies have examined the impact of news sentiment representation on UEV from the perspective of narrative economics, while news sentiment is an important external public opinion environment faced by enterprises in the process of entrepreneurship, and media sentiment is an important tool to characterize the regional entrepreneurial environment and can profoundly affect the decisions of investors, so this study is of great practical guidance for exploring the new paths to improve entrepreneurial activity and stimulate market vitality. This study is of great importance in exploring new ways to increase entrepreneurial activity and stimulate market vitality.

Research hypotheses

While traditional economic theories tend to assume that human decisions are rational and based on data and calculations, narrative economics argues that the economic behavior of individuals and groups is often influenced by stories, anecdotes, emotions, and symbolic meanings. These narratives can be transmitted through media channels to influence people’s expectations, beliefs, and decisions, thus affecting market behavior39.

As a mass media, news media has the characteristics of timely dissemination and wide audience, which can directly affect the psychological expectations of entrepreneurs. As an important information dissemination carrier in the capital market, the role of news media has a significant impact on both the supply side and the demand side of information. On the information supply side, the media collects, verifies and integrates all kinds of information about the economic status quo, generates signals with economic value, effectively alleviates information asymmetry, reduces the cognitive differences between economic individuals, and provides useful incremental information for signal receivers to optimize their decision-making process. On the information demand side, the attitude and behavior of individuals are largely influenced by the social environment in which they live. Individuals gradually form their own cognitive frameworks through processing and interpreting specific social information, which in turn shape their decision-making behavior. In this perspective, the emotional tendency of news media report content becomes an important factor influencing the information processing of information demanders40.

Specifically, according to the Expectation Theory in psychology, when entrepreneurs receive optimistic news sentiments, they tend to hold positive expectations about the future market, and this emotional response usually increases their risk tolerance (even if it does not actually increase), and media coverage of policy changes, innovative breakthroughs, or successful entrepreneurial cases can provide entrepreneurs and investors with key information that influences their perceptions of market opportunities. For example, reports on government support policies can inspire entrepreneurs to trust government support and motivate them to actively register their companies, while news on STI can help raise awareness of the potential of innovative fields and attract more entrepreneurial resources and capital inflows. These news sentiments not only directly affect entrepreneurs’ behavior, but also indirectly promote the flow of capital and market activity, forming a positive feedback mechanism for entrepreneurial activities, and thus entrepreneurs are more willing to invest, innovate, and start businesses. In contrast, pessimistic news sentiment exacerbates entrepreneurs’ risk aversion, strengthens their sensitivity to market uncertainty and potential risks, and leads to a decline in their entrepreneurial willingness. Such mood fluctuations play a crucial psychological driving role in entrepreneurial decision-making, and entrepreneurs’ emotional responses are intertwined with changes in the economic environment, which together shape the dynamic pattern of entrepreneurial behavior.

It should be added that there is often a lag effect between news sentiment and entrepreneurial vigor, a phenomenon that can be explained from a behavioral economics perspective. Although positive news reports can boost entrepreneurs’ confidence in the short term, the formation of entrepreneurial behavior is not an instantaneous response. Entrepreneurs often need time to digest and evaluate the information conveyed in the news and make decisions based on it. In addition, whether to start a business is influenced by multiple factors, such as market signals, financing environment, and policy changes, which usually take some time to have a cumulative effect in the market. Therefore, the impact of news sentiment may not be obvious in the short term, but with the gradual recovery of entrepreneurial sentiment and the strengthening of market signals, the number of enterprises may show an upward trend after a period of time, showing obvious cyclical fluctuations. Accordingly, the following assumptions are made:

Hypothesis 1

ENS positively enhances UEV.

Drawing on the theory of behavioral economics, we recognize that individuals’ economic decisions are not solely guided by the rational man model posited by traditional economics. Instead, emotional factors play a pivotal role in shaping these decisions, significantly influencing their direction41. When the public is immersed in an environment of optimistic and positive Economic News Sentiment (ENS), their expectations of future economic development trends are markedly enhanced. This influence is evident across three key dimensions: consumption, demand, and financial supply.

Optimistic news sentiment prompts the public to shift from conservative saving behavior to a greater willingness to spend. This increase in consumer spending boosts market demand, accelerates the circulation of goods and services, and significantly enhances market activity. As a result, more opportunities arise for entrepreneurs. Among potential entrepreneurs, positive ENS reshapes their perceptions of entrepreneurial risk and reward. Entrepreneurship, typically viewed as a high-risk venture due to market uncertainty and capital access challenges, becomes more appealing under optimistic ENS. Potential entrepreneurs, buoyed by (over)optimistic forecasts of the economic outlook, are convinced that market demand will rise, enhancing the marketability and sales prospects of their ventures. This shift in perception leads to a steady increase in the number of new enterprises, which in turn drives economic growth.

Optimistic ENS also influences financial institutions, increasing their risk appetite and leading to more relaxed loan approval criteria. Entrepreneurs, anticipating easier access to financing at lower costs, are more motivated to apply for loans. This support from financial institutions directly aids entrepreneurs, stimulating regional economic vitality (UEV). The growth of new enterprises contributes to the city’s economic development by driving collaboration across upstream and downstream industries, extending and expanding the industrial chain. This process increases the city’s total economic volume, creates numerous jobs, and raises residents’ income levels. Higher incomes further stimulate the consumer market, leading to greater prosperity and creating a virtuous economic ecosystem with continuous positive feedback loops.

Based on these insights, we formulate the following hypotheses:

Hypothesis 2

ENS can enhance UEV by boosting consumption.

When the mood of economic news is optimistic, the positive economic information disseminated by the media, such as the substantial consumption potential of the market and the rapid development of new industries, serves as a crucial signal for entrepreneurs to identify business opportunities. Optimistic news not only heats up market demand but also influences consumer behavior. Guided by positive news, consumers adjust their purchasing patterns, making advanced or additional purchases of related products. This expansion in market demand directly highlights market pain points and gaps for entrepreneurs, prompting them to delve deeper into their business ideas and develop business models that meet emerging needs. This, in turn, paves the way for entrepreneurial exploration.

Moreover, strong market demand drives capital flow. Investors, keenly monitoring market dynamics, are more inclined to fund demand-oriented entrepreneurial projects due to the potential for high returns and the need to diversify risk. For entrepreneurs, securing these funds is akin to obtaining the key resources needed to launch and grow their businesses, allowing them to implement their ideas and advance their projects.

Furthermore, the expansion of demand triggers a systematic optimization of the urban entrepreneurial environment. As the main body of macro-control, the government responds positively to changes in demand by introducing initiatives such as simplifying business registration processes and granting preferential policies to business parks. These measures aim to lower the barriers to entrepreneurship and alleviate the burden on entrepreneurs. Simultaneously, upstream and downstream enterprises in the industry chain, driven by demand, increase their activity. Raw material suppliers ensure high-quality supply, technology partners assist in overcoming challenges, and sales channels expand product outlets. Collectively, these efforts create a comprehensive, high-performance entrepreneurial ecosystem that supports entrepreneurs.

Based on these insights, the following hypotheses are proposed:

Hypothesis 3

ENS can enhance UEV by stimulating demand.

From the perspective of financial institutions, optimistic Economic News Sentiment (ENS) widely disseminated by the media creates an enthusiastic atmosphere of a booming market. This leads financial institutions to perceive the overall economic environment as positive, characterized by strong market demand and vast potential in emerging industries. As a result, financial institutions reassess their risk appetite based on optimistic expectations of future returns. Given the influx of entrepreneurs into the market, financial institutions proactively lower entry barriers to capture the entrepreneurial financial services market. This includes reducing the stringent scrutiny of startups’ historical financial data, relaxing credit rating standards, and streamlining lending procedures, thereby allowing more startups to access financial resources.

Moreover, the positive messages disseminated by the media expand market demand, attracting a large number of investors and increasing the supply of funds in the financial market. To absorb more funds and rationalize their allocation, competition among financial institutions intensifies. In the field of credit business, they innovate their products and service modes, developing diversified products such as small credit loans and stage credit enhancement loans tailored to the capital needs of entrepreneurial enterprises at different growth stages. They also lower application conditions to achieve a precise match between financial supply and entrepreneurial demand, thereby reducing the threshold for financial supply.

Furthermore, the entrepreneurial boom triggered by optimism prompts the government to introduce supportive policies to continuously stimulate urban economic vitality (UEV). Among these policies, those guiding financial institutions to assist entrepreneurship—such as providing subsidized interest rates and special funds for risk compensation—directly reduce the cost and risk for financial institutions serving entrepreneurial enterprises. Incentivized by these policies, financial institutions further relax loan conditions, extend loan periods, increase loan amounts, and provide stronger financial support for entrepreneurial enterprises, thereby promoting the continuous enhancement of the city’s UEV.

Based on these insights, the following hypothesis is proposed:

Hypothesis 4

ENS can enhance UEV by lowering financial service thresholds.

Variable selection and empirical design

Variable selection

Economic news sentiment

(1) Large Language Model LERT.

In recent years, with the rise of the artificial intelligence field, machine learning models and deep learning models are gradually applied. In the field of natural language processing (NLP), neural network models have gradually taken the dominant position. A natural language processing (NLP) process is roughly: data acquisition, data preprocessing, feature extraction, task-specific model training and validation, and task-specific prediction. The most important step in natural language processing is model pre-training (pre-train). Model pre-training aims to be trained by a large-scale corpus, where the model can capture a large number of rich linguistic features, which are convenient to migrate to the downstream task (target task). After model pre-training, fine-tuning will be performed, i.e., the pre-trained model will be fine-tuned using a small amount of labeled data to make predictions for downstream tasks.

Pre-trained language models (PLMs) consider rich contextual information and are a successful method for text representation. Among the common pre-trained language models, self-coding pre-trained models such as BERT and RoBERTa are relatively popular in natural language understanding. Self-encoding pre-training models will rely heavily on pre-training tasks to learn contextual information. However, in Chinese scenarios, knowledge is mostly presented in pairs of words, which is difficult to be presented in individual characters, so models such as BERT are difficult to learn complete semantic information and have some limitations. Therefore, in order to solve the above difficulties, a new pre-training model LERT based on the BERT model has emerged. The LERT model utilizes a new linguistically-informed pre-training (LIP) strategy to explicitly include more linguistic knowledge to create weakly-supervised data for the model pre-training, so that more rich linguistic features can be learned. In addition, in terms of training data, BERT uses only encyclopedic corpus training data, RoBERTa uses training data such as news articles, community Q&A, and encyclopedic data, while LERT uses training data consisting of Chinese Wikipedia dumps, encyclopedias, community Q&A, news articles, etc., for pre-training. The experimental results prove (Fig. 1) that the performance of the LERT pretrained model is much better than that of BERT and RoBERTa on all the six Chinese text categorization tasks. Based on the above reasons, we will use the LERT pretrained model with richer linguistic features and better performance for this categorization task.

(2) Measurement using the LERT model.

Step 1: Determine the object of text analysis.

Online social media, as a low-cost, real-time and interactive way, can be the fastest way to realize the aggregation of public sentiment and thus feel the social development. Therefore, this study adopts the content published on microblogs by the official media of sample cities from January 2013 to October 2024 as the data source, specifically searches for each city as a keyword, and filters out the official microblog accounts of the government of each city, obtaining including “xx release”, We obtained 814 official media accounts, including “xx release”, “xx daily” and so on. Then we obtained all the news content data through web crawler technology, and filtering the content by “economic news” category, got tweeted content pertinent to economic topics, totaling 180,716 pieces of news data. The following dictionaries are used to determine whether a text belongs to financial news: Gross Domestic Product (GDP), inflation, deflation, economy, macro-control, money, finance, interest rates, exchange rates, unemployment rate, finance, stocks, bonds, funds, futures, options, stock exchanges, capital markets, financial derivatives, price-earnings ratios, price-net ratios, financial, assets, liabilities, owners’ equity, operating Income, Cost, Profit, Cash Flow, Accounts, Financial Statements, Investment, Risk, Income, Value Investing, Dividend, Dividends, Compound Interest, Stop Loss, Stop Gain, Financial Policies and Regulations, Tax Law, Accounting Law, Securities Law, Regulation, Compliance, Audit, Antitrust.

Step 2: Constructing statements with predictions and statements to be labeled.

We integrate the title of each tweet and the content of the tweet to form a complete piece of measurement content. In order to improve the data quality, we need to process various special symbols, emoticons and other non-Chinese character form content in the tweets, which has the advantage of reducing the introduction of unnecessary information and improving the model efficiency.

In order to facilitate the subsequent processing of our model, this thesis will just process all the text according to the period and semicolon, to get the final predicted sentence library. Since most of the sentences in the tweets have nothing to do with the keyword “economic news”, if the sentences are completely randomly selected for manual judgment, most of the labels obtained will not match the topic words. Therefore, in order to improve the efficiency of manual processing and prevent errors, it is necessary to use keywords to extract representative sentences from the tweets, which together with the randomly selected sentences form the sentence database to be labeled.

We collected and organized a dictionary of 48 keywords related to “economy” based on policy texts, research reports, and existing literature, and supplemented it with manual reading. Then, we extracted tweets containing 10 or more different keywords and removed the sentences containing the keywords. At the same time, in order to improve the model’s ability to predict sentences without keywords, we randomly selected some tweets without keywords and segmented them into sentences. In order to solve the problem of uneven time distribution, on the basis of these two parts of sentences, we grouped them according to the time period, took out the same number of sentences in each time period, and then carried out random extraction without put-back from this part of uniformly distributed sentences to get the to-be-labeled sentence library of this study. Finally, the to-be-labeled sentence bank of this study contains 1,138,593 sentences.

Step 3: Manual annotation of the sentences in the library of sentences to be labeled.

The idea of manual labeling is based on the aspectual nouns in the sentences to correspond to the adjectives and thus make mood judgments. So, based on this idea, we transform the sentences in the library of substitute labeled sentences. The purpose of manual labeling is to form the training set, validation set and test set, which is convenient for the model to analyze and form the final conclusion.

We organized the 20 researchers into 10 groups, each containing two people, and arranged for them to rotate periodically. In order to ensure a uniform standard for sentence labeling, we set mood seed words and elaborated on the specific requirements for labeling several times before the formal task began, with special instructions and demonstrations for those labels that were easily confused (e.g., the presence of such confusing descriptions as “going from strength to strength” and “increasing instead of decreasing” in the utterance). After clarifying the criteria, we had a full practice of tagging and regularly discussed the difficulties and questions encountered in the process of labeling. In the formal labeling stage, each sentence to be labeled will be labeled by two researchers respectively. If their labeling results are the same, then the label of this sentence will be recorded; if there are differences, we will organize a discussion among all the members to determine the final label; and we will not include those sentences whose labels are difficult to determine in the training set. In the end, all sentences in the to-be-tagged sentence pool, except those for which labels were difficult to determine, were categorized under three labels covering positive, negative, and neutral sentiments (categorizing words describing the status quo such as “economic fluctuations”, “industrial restructuring”, “resource consolidation”, “changes in economic forms”, etc. as neutral sentiments). “economic fluctuations”, ‘industrial restructuring’, ‘resource consolidation’, ‘changes in economic forms’ and some other words describing the status quo were categorized as neutral sentiments). Step 4: Train the model using deep learning methods.

With the rise of Transformer in 2017, the attention mechanism can capture the internal dependencies of sequences, so that the text is not limited to textual representations, but linked to contextual information. We will use the Transformer structure-based LERT model for the classification task, using its built-in Tokenizer function to quickly convert sentences into the format needed for LERT model training. We divide all the labeled sentences into training set, validation set and test set in the ratio of 8:1:1. Meanwhile, in order to compare the classification performance between different models, we will use the same Transformer structure for the experiments, including BERT, RoBERTa, BART, ALBERT, and many other deep learning models.

The main purpose of the model is to determine the sentiment tendency of tweets related to “economic news”. For this kind of classification model, the performance of the model is usually measured by Precision, Recall and Accuracy. Considering the uneven distribution of the number of labels of each type in the training set, the F1-Score is usually used to measure the overall classification ability of the model. Among the four types of metrics, Precision is the proportion of samples predicted by the model to be in the Positive category that are actually in the Positive category. A model with high Precision means that it can accurately identify samples that are truly Positive, reducing the possibility of false positives. In the context of this thesis, Precision measures the proportion of sentences that the model considers to be related to “economic news” whose true label is also related to “economic news”, and Recall measures the proportion of sentences predicted to be in the Positive category out of a sample of sentences that are actually in the Positive category. Recall measures the proportion of all samples that are actually in the Positive category that are predicted to be in the Positive category. A model with high Recall means that it can find as many samples as possible that are actually in the Positive category, reducing the likelihood of underreporting. The ability to correctly categorize tweets is more important in the context of this thesis, where there may be multiple sentences in the tweets indicating information related to “economic news”. Comparing the performance of different models on the same training set (see Fig. 1), we can find that the precision, recall, correctness, and F1-Score of the LERT model reach 80%, 70%, 86%, and 76%, respectively, and the LERT model only lags behind the BERT in terms of Recall, but after taking the importance of Precision into consideration, the LERT model has the highest F1-Score among the models trained. However, after considering the importance of Precision, the F1-Score of LERT is the highest among the trained models, according to which we choose LERT as the sentence classification model. Figure 1 compares the performance of different models, and it can be found that the combined classification ability (Fig. 1) of LERT and BERT is much higher than that of the common classification models.

Step 5: Constructing Sentiment Analysis Indicators Using the LERT Model.

Based on the LERT large language model, we predict all the texts in the 2013–2023 prediction database, and determine the sentiment analysis related to “economic news”. Specifically, considering the lag of entrepreneurship, we categorize the sentiment of the news texts in the current month and the previous two months as the current month’s sentiment. As the news sentiment of the current month, for example, if we want to measure the ENS of December 2023, we need to count the news texts of October, November and December as the text source. Following the above idea, we construct discrete variables for ENS, i.e., news with a very pronounced bearish bias is regarded as a very positive sentiment and categorized as 2, bearish bias is categorized as a positive sentiment and categorized as 1, very pronounced bearish sentiment is regarded as a very negative sentiment and categorized as − 2, general bearish sentiment is regarded as a negative sentiment and categorized as − 1, and other neutral sentiments are categorized as 0. Urban entrepreneurial vitality

The calculation of UEV is based on the number of new enterprises added each month, which reflects the scale of the unemployed population in the region. Specifically, through the China Business Registration Database, the number of new enterprises added each month in 314 cities from January 2014 to December 2024 was selected as the variable of entrepreneurial vitality for that city in that month.

Endogenous treatment

The article selects the monthly average international gold price (XAU) and the monthly U.S. economic policy uncertainty index (AEPU) as instrumental variables for endogeneity. Specifically:

The price of gold is to a large extent a “barometer” of macroeconomic conditions. In times of economic instability, such as recession or increased inflationary expectations, the price of gold tends to rise. This is due to gold’s safe-haven properties. When the economic ENS is pessimistic and people are worried about the overall economic situation, it may cause investors to transfer funds from risky assets to safe-haven assets such as gold, making the price of gold rise. So the fluctuation of gold price can reflect the trend of ENS to a certain extent, to meet the requirements of instrumental variable correlation. Exogenously, on the one hand, the price of gold is mainly determined by the supply and demand in the global gold market, geopolitical factors, monetary policy and other macro factors. For individual entrepreneurs in cities, it is difficult for them to directly influence the price of gold. For example, when entrepreneurs in a city decide whether to launch a new catering business or a technology venture, their decisions will not have a substantial impact on the supply and demand in the gold market. On the other hand, the formation mechanism of the gold price is relatively independent of the endogenous factors of UEV. UEV mainly depends on local human resources, technology level, policy support, market demand and other factors, which are basically not directly related to the determinants of gold price, so the gold price meets the requirements of exogeneity. Impact mechanism test and heterogeneity analysis

Channel mechanism test

Boosting consumption effect

In accordance with Keynes’ absolute income hypothesis, consumption is a function of current disposable income. When ENS (Economic News Sentiment) increases expected income, consumers tend to boost their consumption. From the perspectives of the permanent income hypothesis and the life-cycle hypothesis, consumers structure their consumption based on their expectations of current income. Positive economic news leads them to adjust their expectations of permanent income, thereby altering their current consumption behavior. Additionally, a decline in savings is indicative of increased consumption. Consumers believe that investing or spending their money immediately will yield higher returns than saving, causing funds to flow from savings accounts to the consumer market and investment sectors.

To measure the regional consumption level (RSCG), we use the monthly total retail sales of consumer goods in each province at the prefecture level as a proportion of GDP. Meanwhile, we employ the provincial monthly savings rate (SR) as a reverse indicator of the consumption level in the corresponding city to conduct a robustness test. As shown in columns (1) to (2) of Table 9, the results indicate that the consumption level is positively and significantly correlated with ENS, while negatively correlated with savings. This suggests that ENS can enhance the regional consumption level, thereby promoting UEV (Urban Economic Vitality). Thus, Hypothesis 2 is verified.

Stimulating demand effects

When entrepreneurs consider whether to start a business and what direction to pursue, they focus on the market potential of the region in which they operate. The market potential index quantitatively reflects the attractiveness of different regions, serving as a crucial reference for entrepreneurial decision-making and a quantitative measure of market demand potential. Therefore, drawing on related research42, we use the market potential index to quantify the demand situation in each city.

Since the market potential index is an annual measure, we introduce a monthly proxy variable to capture its monthly characteristics. Specifically, we create a cross-multiplier term of the monthly microenterprise loan index and the market potential index to serve as a proxy for urban demand (UD). This approach is justified because the microenterprise loan index is more suitable for our research sample and industry selection. Moreover, micro and small enterprises, due to their lower degree of specialization, are more likely to be influenced by news sentiment.

The empirical results, as shown in column (3) of Table 9, indicate a positive relationship between urban demand and ENS (Economic News Sentiment). This suggests that ENS can enhance urban economic vitality (UEV) by promoting urban demand, thereby verifying Hypothesis 3.

Barrier lowering effect

Economic News Sentiment (ENS) influences the expectations of both financial institutions and entrepreneurs. Optimistic ENS enhances the risk appetite of financial institutions, leading them to accept higher credit risks and thereby easing their loan approval standards. Entrepreneurs, in turn, anticipate that financial institutions will be more inclined to grant loans at lower costs, which increases their motivation to apply for financing. This shift is specifically manifested through the simplification of loan approval processes and reductions in interest rates, providing direct support to entrepreneurs and stimulating regional urban economic vitality (UEV).

To empirically test this relationship, we use the national monthly bank loan approval index (BLIR) and the national monthly benchmark lending interest rate (BLAI) as proxy variables for the level of service thresholds in the financial sector. The results, as shown in columns (4) to (5) of Table 9, indicate a significant negative correlation between ENS and both the loan approval index and the benchmark lending interest rate. This suggests that optimistic ENS directly lowers the barriers to financial services, thereby boosting entrepreneurs’ motivation and revitalizing the city’s UEV. Thus, Hypothesis 4 is verified.

Moderating effect test

Business environment regulation

Doing business reinforces the positive contribution of ENS to UEV, mainly through the cognitive information dissemination dimension, the facilitated market access dimension and the competitive-cooperative environment dimension. Specifically, in a better business environment, information dissemination channels are smooth and efficient. ENS are quickly and accurately disseminated to potential entrepreneurs through various media platforms and industry information channels. Enterprises and entrepreneurs in these regions have easier access to the latest economic news, policy guidance and market trends. Since a favorable business environment is usually accompanied by a high level of information literacy among enterprises and residents, they have a more accurate understanding and perception of ENS. This accurate understanding helps entrepreneurs keenly capture favorable business opportunities, which in turn stimulates their entrepreneurial will and accelerates the preparation and establishment of new businesses. Second, a good business environment means lower market access thresholds and a simple and efficient approval process. Positive ENS can motivate more people to take advantage of such relaxed market access conditions. When people perceive positive economic dynamics in the news, such as the rise of emerging industries and the expansion of the consumer market, they will be more motivated to incorporate new businesses and enter the market to share the dividends of economic growth. At the same time, these regions have a higher level of government services and are able to provide new businesses with convenient one-stop services such as registration, licensing and approval. Such facilitated services can shorten the time cycle for business establishment, enabling potential entrepreneurs to transform their business ideas into actual businesses more quickly, thus accelerating the growth in the number of new businesses in the city. Finally, in regions with a better business environment, competition among enterprises is fair and orderly. Positive ENS can stimulate healthy market competition and lead to the birth of more innovative businesses. Entrepreneurs see opportunities in the market and will compete by offering differentiated products or services. At the same time, a favorable business environment promotes cooperation among enterprises. New businesses can more easily establish partnerships with other enterprises to share resources, technology and market channels. This environment of competition and cooperation is conducive to the growth and development of new enterprises, resulting in an accelerated growth in the number of new enterprises in the city over time. In order to verify this conjecture, this thesis uses the business environment data (BE1) for the moderating effect test, which is taken from the Business Environment Database of Chinese Cities, and in addition, Ruiz et al.43 measure of business environment (BE2) is selected as a proxy for the robustness test. The test results find (columns (1) to (2) of Table 10) that the interaction term between business environment and news sentiment is significantly positive, indicating that the stronger the business environment is, the more pronounced is the effect of ENS on UEV, and the above hypotheses are verified.

Tolerance of entrepreneurial failure

From the socio-cultural dimension, the atmosphere of understanding and tolerance of entrepreneurial failure unique to the region constitutes a special kind of social capital, which effectively reduces the hidden costs of entrepreneurship. According to the traditional economic theory, when entrepreneurs face market uncertainty, they not only have to bear obvious costs such as capital investment and time cost, but also the psychological burden brought by the fear of failure and negative social evaluation, which is the key component of the hidden cost. When the region shows a high tolerance for failure, it is equivalent to reducing the psychological pressure on entrepreneurs from the root and eliminating this hidden cost. Under this circumstance, once the ENS tends to be positive, potential entrepreneurs can, according to the theory of information asymmetry, use the news media to accurately capture market signals that contain business opportunities, and obtain spiritual encouragement, and then be in a position of information superiority. They do not need to worry about excessive accusations and social rejection after failing to start a business. The positive social mindset generated by this relaxed and tolerant cultural environment transforms potential business opportunities in the news into actual entrepreneurial actions, which strongly boosts the probability of new businesses being born, and drives the expansion of the supply side of entrepreneurship in the region. In addition, regions with a high tolerance for failure often have a perfect and appropriate supporting system. The government, as the leading force in macroeconomic regulation, will introduce a series of policies to encourage entrepreneurship, such as business subsidies and tax incentives. When ENS is high, favorable policies and positive market signals are tightly coupled, forming a powerful gravitational field that attracts entrepreneurs to take advantage of policy preferences to set up businesses. At the same time, local resource allocation shows a significant tilt toward entrepreneurial activities. Financial institutions, for example, are more willing to start a business based on their in-depth knowledge of the local social and policy environments, knowing that even if a business project suffers setbacks, entrepreneurs will not be subjected to harsh criticism. Heterogeneity analysis

Fluctuations in house prices

From the perspective of entrepreneurs’ psychological expectations, the sharp fluctuations in housing prices make entrepreneurs extremely uncertain about the stability of future living and operating costs. In the stage of rapid increase in house prices, entrepreneurs worry about the continuous rise in rental costs, which not only compresses profit margins, but also may lead to the subsequent tightening of the capital chain. On the other hand, when house prices fall sharply, entrepreneurs may wonder whether the depreciation of real estate signals a recession in the region, which in turn may lead to a lack of confidence in the future of entrepreneurship. This anxiety triggered by housing price fluctuations weakens entrepreneurs’ sensitivity to opportunities conveyed by positive ENS, making them more inclined to wait and see rather than act decisively in the face of news reports of emerging industry opportunities and favorable policies, thus inhibiting UEV. Analyzing again at the level of capital flows, the real estate market in areas where house prices fluctuate sharply has attracted an excessive amount of capital attention. On the one hand, investors are keen to seek speculative gains in the ups and downs of housing prices, whether it is individual home buyers who expect to profit from property appreciation or capital influxes into real estate development projects, which has led to a large amount of capital being deposited in the property market. Especially in some hot cities, housing prices have risen like crazy, local and foreign funds have piled up real estate, bank loans also flowed to a large number of mortgage business. This directly leads to the entrepreneurial field of accessible capital is relatively scarce, even if the ENS is high, reported a number of potential entrepreneurial projects and market gaps, entrepreneurs due to a lack of funds difficult to put the idea on the ground. On the other hand, real estate-related industries are too busy to take care of themselves under the fluctuation of house prices, and upstream and downstream industries such as building materials and home decoration are too busy to cope with the market changes to care about the synergistic cooperation with entrepreneurial firms, which can’t provide strong industrial support for entrepreneurship, and further weaken the effect of the news sentiment on the promotion of UEV. Therefore, we hypothesize that the impact of ENS on UEV is limited in cities with relatively large fluctuations in house prices, while the effect is more significant in cities with small fluctuations in house prices. In order to verify the above conjecture, this thesis quantifies the house price volatility, where the house price data comes from the monthly data of each city of Anjuke, house price volatility =| (house price of this month—house price of the previous month)| / house price of the previous month, and the house price volatility is grouped into groups according to the 50% quartile for the test can be seen (columns (1) to (2) of Table 11), in the group of low house price volatility, the impact of news sentiment on the entrepreneurial vigor in the city is significant at the level of 1%, while in cities with low house price volatility the impact is more significant. In the group with low house price volatility, the effect of ENS on UEV is significant at the 1% level, while in the group with high house price volatility, the effect of NES on UEV is insignificant and passes the fisher test, which confirms the above conjecture.

Public attention

Starting from the information asymmetry theory in information economics, there is a natural information gap between entrepreneurs and the market in a market economy environment. As a key information intermediary, the public, in areas with high public attention, can greatly reduce this information asymmetry with its strong communication ability. Based on this information, entrepreneurs can more accurately judge the supply and demand situation in the market and reduce the risk of decision-making. Based on the optimistic expectations of the market as reflected in the news sentiment, they are able to invest their limited resources in the areas with the greatest potential, effectively stimulating UEV, which is a manifestation of the optimal allocation of resources prompted by the efficient use of information.

Based on the demonstration effect theory in behavioral economics, high public attention areas are eager to promote the stories of successful local entrepreneurs. These in-depth reports on the hardships and achievements of entrepreneurship have triggered strong emotional resonance in the society, and as a result of public praise, entrepreneurs no longer fear the risks of entrepreneurship, and even overestimate the expected benefits of entrepreneurial success, and then enthusiastically join the entrepreneurial wave. This kind of group behavioral change triggered by the role model demonstration leads to a significant increase in the participation rate of entrepreneurship, which strongly enhances the UEV of the city. Therefore, we believe that the effect of ENS on UEV is more significant in regions with high public attention, while the significance of both variables is weaker in regions with low public attention. In order to verify this conjecture we draw on Wu45 calculations to measure the city’s monthly public economic attention, where the keywords follow the keywords from the ENS screening described above. After obtaining the public attention indicator, it is grouped into 50% quartiles and regressed separately it can be seen (columns (3) to (4) of Table 11) that within the low public attention group, news sentiment is not significant on UEV, and within the high public attention group, ENS has a significant impact on UEV, and the above conjecture is verified.

Unemployment rate

Starting from the macroeconomics model of aggregate demand and aggregate supply, a high unemployment rate implies that the labor market in the region is in a state of imbalance, with a large number of laborers being idle. Under such circumstances, local aggregate demand tends to be insufficient. On the one hand, the income of the unemployed has plummeted or even gone to zero, and their spending power has plummeted, making the consumer market sluggish. Even if the news sentiment conveys that certain emerging industries have vast business opportunities, such as reporting the market potential of smart technology products, the products or services of new startups are difficult to find sufficient market demand support due to the limited purchasing power of consumers, inhibiting UEV from the market end. On the other hand, enterprises as the main body of production, in the face of a weak consumer market, the willingness to invest will also be reduced, because the expansion of production or venture into new areas face higher market risks, which further impedes the entrepreneurial project to obtain the necessary upstream and downstream cooperation and financial support, making it difficult for the entrepreneurial incentives contained in the ENS to be transformed on the ground.

Furthermore, residents of areas with high unemployment rates are in economic distress and are more sensitive to losses. When they receive ENS information, they tend to be overly worried about the possible losses associated with entrepreneurship, such as the risk of not being able to recoup the initial capital investment and subsequent operational difficulties. Moreover, high unemployment areas usually have labor skill mismatch problems. Prolonged unemployment has left part of the workforce with aging skills and out of touch with the needs of emerging industries. Even when ENS publicizes the benefits of entrepreneurship in cutting-edge fields such as green energy and biomedicine, the local workforce may lack the skills to meet the demand for high-quality talent in start-ups. This not only increases the training costs of startups, but also leads to talent bottlenecks at the startup stage, which slows down or even hinders project progress, making it difficult to effectively transform the entrepreneurial intentions guided by news sentiment into actual entrepreneurial actions, and ultimately weakening the UEV of the city. Therefore, we believe that the effect of ENS on UEV is not significant in regions with high unemployment rates, while there is a significant effect in regions with low unemployment rates. In order to verify this conjecture, we take the annual unemployment rate of each city as the basis for the 50% quartile division, and define those higher than the 50% quartile as high unemployment cities, and vice versa as low unemployment cities, and conduct regressions separately to find that (columns 5–6 of Table 11), there is no significant effect of news sentiment on UEV in cities with high unemployment, and there is a significant effect of news sentiment on UEV in cities with low unemployment. entrepreneurial vigor in cities with low unemployment, the above conjecture is verified.

Conclusions and recommendations

Entrepreneurial behavior involves many economic elements such as resource allocation, risk decision and return expectation. The emotional tendency carried by the news may ultimately play a role in the entrepreneurial behavior of micro subjects by influencing their cognition of the market environment, risk perception, and assessment of entrepreneurial opportunities, among other channels. Taking prefecture-level cities from 2013 to 2023 as the research sample, the article adopts the LERT grand prognostic model with better overall performance to measure the monthly ENS of cities based on the comparison of existing grand language models, and further investigates the impact of ENS on regional UEV. The results of the study find that positive ENS enhances UEV and passes a series of robustness tests. Mechanism analysis shows that ENS enhances UEV mainly through shifting expectations, which is manifested in the consumption boosting effect of consumers, the demand stimulating effect of entrepreneurs, and the service threshold lowering effect of financial institutions; and that the business environment and the regional entrepreneurial failure tolerance can strengthen the effect of ENS on UEV. Heterogeneity analysis shows that the promotion effect of ENS on UEV is more significant in regions with lower house price volatility, higher public concern and lower unemployment. An in-depth exploration of the intrinsic link between news sentiment tendencies and UEV is important for understanding the entrepreneurial decision-making mechanism in economic activities.

Based on the above findings, the following recommendations are made:

Optimize news dissemination and emotional guidance strategies. The government and the media should work together to build an ecosystem for economic news dissemination and accurately control the emotional tone of the news. When reporting economic events, the media should base on professional economic analysis and market insights, use data visualization, in-depth analysis of cases and other diversified means to enhance the depth and credibility of the news, and effectively convey positive and rational economic news emotions. For example, when reporting on the development of emerging industries, we not only present optimistic prospects, but also analyze potential challenges and coping strategies to guide entrepreneurs to form reasonable expectations. At the same time, establish ENS monitoring and feedback mechanism, use sentiment analysis technology to track ENS dynamics in real time, and adjust the direction and mode of reporting based on audience feedback, so as to strengthen the positive incentives of ENS on UEV, stabilize entrepreneurs’ psychological expectations, reduce the blindness of decision-making triggered by emotional fluctuations, and promote the rational and orderly development of entrepreneurial activities.

Build a perfect ecological system to promote entrepreneurship. On the one hand, the government needs to actively create a policy environment conducive to consumption enhancement and market demand expansion, accurately formulate industrial support policies based on the market potential and ENS orientation of different industries, encourage the development of emerging industries, create a broad market space for entrepreneurs, strengthen the effect of ENS to boost consumption and stimulate demand, enhance the confidence of entrepreneurs in the market opportunities, and improve their entrepreneurial willingness. On the other hand, financial institutions should further deepen financial innovation and service optimization, and establish a dynamic risk assessment and credit policy adjustment mechanism based on market trends reflected in ENS. Simplify the loan approval process for entrepreneurial enterprises, and utilize financial technology to realize rapid credit assessment and loan issuance; develop diversified financial products, such as special loans for different entrepreneurial stages and industry characteristics, and hybrid financial instruments combining equity and debt financing, etc., so as to lower the threshold of financial services, effectively meet the capital needs of entrepreneurial enterprises, provide strong financial support for the enhancement of UEV, and promote the effective transformation of ENS through financial channels. ENS is effectively transformed into entrepreneurial action and enterprise growth momentum through financial channels, promoting the development of urban entrepreneurial economy.

Strengthening the regional business environment and entrepreneurship support system. Local governments need to continue to deepen the reform of the business environment, and make efforts in multiple dimensions, such as policy formulation, optimization of government services, and strengthening of market supervision. In terms of policy formulation, based on the characteristics of local industries and entrepreneurial needs, accurately introduce policies such as tax incentives, financial subsidies, and preferential land use, to reduce the cost of entrepreneurship and improve the economic viability of entrepreneurial projects. In terms of government services, we will promote “Internet + Administrative Services”, integrate the resources of industry and commerce, taxation, finance and other departments, build a one-stop online service platform, simplify the approval process for business start-up and operation, and enhance service efficiency and transparency. In terms of market supervision, establish a fair, just and competitive market environment, strengthen intellectual property protection and anti-monopoly law enforcement, safeguard the legitimate rights and interests of entrepreneurs, enhance market confidence, strengthen the facilitating role of the business environment in the conduction of ENS and UEV, and enhance the attractiveness and competitiveness of regional entrepreneurship46,47.

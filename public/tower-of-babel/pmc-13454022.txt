Precise localization of phosphaturic mesenchymal tumor causing tumor-induced osteomalacia: the value of dual-tracer imaging with ¹⁸F-FDG and ⁶⁸Ga-DOTATATE PET/CT
Authors: Ting Ye, Xin Wen, Ruimin Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPhosphaturic mesenchymal tumor (PMT) is a rare cause of tumor-induced osteomalacia (TIO), often presenting with nonspecific symptoms and small tumor size, leading to diagnostic challenges. We report a case of a 25-year-old male with progressive generalized bone pain, hypophosphatemia, and elevated alkaline phosphatase, initially misdiagnosed as spondyloarthritis and osteoporosis. Whole-body bone scintigraphy suggested metabolic bone disease. 18F-FDG PET/CT revealed a destructive lesion in the mandible with moderate uptake, whereas ⁶⁸Ga-DOTATATE PET/CT showed high somatostatin receptor expression, accurately localizing the PMT. Serum phosphate levels normalized rapidly after surgical resection. This case highlights the superior diagnostic value of ⁶⁸Ga-DOTATATE PET/CT over 18F-FDG PET/CT in localizing PMT, facilitating targeted treatment and avoiding prolonged morbidity.Keywords: Phosphaturic mesenchymal tumor, Tumor-induced osteomalacia, ⁶⁸Ga-DOTATATE, 18F-FDG, PET/CT, Hypophosphatemia

Introduction

Tumor-induced osteomalacia (TIO) is a paraneoplastic syndrome characterized by renal phosphate wasting, hypophosphatemia, and osteomalacia, most commonly caused by phosphaturic mesenchymal tumor (PMT). These tumors secrete fibroblast growth factor-23 (FGF-23), which inhibits renal phosphate reabsorption, leading to hypophosphatemia and osteomalacia (Benson et al. 2022, Arai et al. 2017). PMTs are typically small, slow-growing, and clinically occult, often resulting in diagnostic delay (Adnan et al. 2019). Tumor localization is crucial for curative resection. While ¹⁸F-FDG PET/CT is widely used in oncology, its sensitivity for PMT is variable. In contrast, ⁶⁸Ga-DOTATATE PET/CT, targeting somatostatin receptors (SSTR) overexpressed in PMT, has emerged as a highly sensitive imaging tool for tumor localization (Hoong et al. 2025, Wu et al. 2019). This case demonstrates the pivotal role of SSTR-targeted imaging in TIO management through dual-tracer PET/CT.

Case presentation

A 25-year-old male presented with progressive generalized joint pain, fatigue, and weight loss (15 kg) over one year. Initial evaluation suggested spondyloarthritis, severe osteoporosis, and femoral head necrosis, with poor response to symptomatic treatment. Laboratory tests revealed hypophosphatemia (0.45mmol/L, normal 0.81–1.45), hypocalcemia, elevated alkaline phosphatase (520U/L), and normal parathyroid hormone. Whole-body bone scintigraphy showed multifocal tracer uptake in ribs, pelvis, large joints, and mandible, consistent with metabolic bone disease (Fig. 1A).

(A) Whole-body bone scintigraphy showing multifocal tracer uptake in ribs, pelvis, large joints, and mandible. (B-C) ¹⁸F-FDG PET/CT MIP and axial/sagittal images showing an osteolytic lesion in the mandible with moderate FDG uptake (SUVmax 5.2). (D-H) ⁶⁸Ga-DOTATATE PET/CT MIP and axial/sagittal images demonstrating intense tracer uptake at the same site (SUVmax 18.7). The white arrow in the image points to a lesion in the mandible

¹⁸F-FDG PET/CT demonstrated an osteolytic lesion in the left mandible with moderate FDG uptake (SUVmax 5.2) (Fig. 1B-C). In contrast, ⁶⁸Ga-DOTATATE PET/CT revealed intense uptake (SUVmax18.7), indicating high SSTR expression (Fig. 1D-H). No other suspicious lesions were identified. Based on imaging and biochemical findings, PMT-induced osteomalacia was strongly suspected.

The patient underwent surgical resection of the mandibular mass. Histopathology revealed a spindle cell tumor with chondroid matrix differentiation. Immunohistochemistry was positive for vimentin, focally positive for SATB2, and negative for S-100, consistent with PMT. These findings confirmed the diagnosis of PMT-induced osteomalacia. Serum phosphate normalized within 15 days postoperatively, with significant alleviation of bone pain.

Discussion

PMT is a rare mesenchymal tumor, usually benign, that causes phosphaturia and osteomalacia via FGF-23 secretion. Due to nonspecific symptoms and small size, diagnosis is often delayed (Beur et al. 2022, Meyer et al. 2020). Conventional imaging (CT, MRI) may fail to detect the tumor, especially in atypical locations (Correia et al. 2021).

In this case, ¹⁸F-FDG PET/CT identified the mandibular lesion but showed only moderate metabolic activity, which can be seen in various benign or malignant bone tumors. In contrast, ⁶⁸Ga-DOTATATE PET/CT, targeting SSTR2 overexpression in PMT, demonstrated markedly high uptake. This aligns with recent literature emphasizing the superiority of SSTR-targeted PET over FDG PET in localizing PMT (Yu et al. 2021, Agrawal et al. 2021, Lee et al. 2021).

The use of dual-tracer PET/CT in this case illustrates a key clinical paradigm: when TIO is biochemically confirmed but conventional imaging is inconclusive, ⁶⁸Ga-DOTATATE PET/CT should be considered the imaging modality of choice. It not only localizes tumors with high precision but also aids surgical planning. Recent studies also suggest theranostic potential for SSTR-targeted radioligands in unresectable or metastatic cases (Ebner et al. 2024).

Limitations include the rarity of PMT and the lack of head-to-head tracer comparison trials. We acknowledge that serum fibroblast growth factor-23 (FGF-23) level was not measured in this patient, as the assay was not clinically available at our institution at the time of diagnosis. Although FGF-23 is a key biomarker for tumor-induced osteomalacia, its measurement is not mandatory for diagnosis when clinical, biochemical, imaging, and histopathological findings are concordant. Nonetheless, the absence of FGF-23 data represents a limitation of this report. Nevertheless, this case adds to the evidence supporting the integration of ⁶⁸Ga-DOTATATE PET/CT into the diagnostic workflow for suspected TIO.

Conclusion

⁶⁸Ga-DOTATATE PET/CT is an effective imaging tool for localizing PMT in patients with tumor-induced osteomalacia, offering superior sensitivity and specificity compared to ¹⁸F-FDG PET/CT. Early use of SSTR-targeted PET can expedite diagnosis, guide curative surgery, and prevent long-term skeletal complications. This case underscores the importance of integrating molecular imaging into the multidisciplinary management of metabolic bone disorders.

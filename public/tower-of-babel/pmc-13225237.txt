Vamorolone Safety, Pharmacokinetics, and Exploratory Efficacy in Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy
Authors: Jean K. Mah, Hernan D. Gonorazky, Elisa Nigro, Hanns Lochmüller, Alberto Alemán, Amanda Yaworski, Maryam Oskoui, Anne Marie Sbrocchi, Kathryn A. Selby, Ana de Vera, Rashmi Mathur, Ekaterina Gresko, Aki Linden, Catherine Dutreix, Eric P. Hoffman
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background and ObjectivesVamorolone is a dissociative corticosteroid (CS) approved by the US Food and Drug Administration in 2023 for treating Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD). This study evaluated the safety, tolerability, and pharmacokinetics (PK) of vamorolone in young boys with DMD; exploratory objectives included efficacy and patient-reported outcomes.MethodsA 12-week, phase II, open-label, multiple-dose study (VBP15-006) enrolled CS-naive boys with DMD aged 2–<4 years in Canada. Participants received 2 or 6 mg/kg/d oral vamorolone. An ongoing expanded access protocol (EAP) provided longer-term data. Primary end points were the occurrence of treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) and changes from baseline to week 12 in height, weight, and body mass index (BMI); other end points included PK and effects of vamorolone on muscle function.ResultsTwenty boys (mean age [SD] 3.4 [0.39] years) with similar baseline characteristics were enrolled; all completed VBP15-006 and 19 continued to receive vamorolone through EAP Canada. TEAEs were mostly mild, with no deaths, serious TEAEs, or TEAEs leading to drug discontinuation during VBP15-006. TEAEs were more frequent with 6 mg/kg/d vamorolone than with 2 mg/kg/d, with gastrointestinal disorders and infections/infestations, respectively, most reported. Vamorolone use was associated with morning serum cortisol reductions in all patients, more pronounced with 6 mg/kg/d. Stable growth trajectories were observed at both doses throughout VBP15-006 and the EAP Canada follow-up period, with a total median (Q1; Q3) exposure to vamorolone of 2 years (1.7; 2.3). By EAP Canada last visit, 8/19 patients had weight and BMI z-scores changes >0.5, suggestive of excessive weight gain over time. Vamorolone displayed a dose-dependent PK profile with rapid absorption and no accumulation. Dose-dependent improvements in motor function (measured by Bayley-III gross motor scaled score) were observed. Glucose metabolism and bone turnover biomarkers remained stable. Ease of administration was rated favorably.DiscussionVamorolone was well tolerated in 2–<4-year-old boys with DMD, with no new safety concerns identified. A dose-dependent PK profile was observed, consistent with previous studies. Exploratory evidence suggested dose-dependent improvements in gross motor function. These findings are consistent with potential therapeutic benefit of vamorolone for young boys with DMD.Trial Registration InformationClinicalTrials.gov: NCT05185622 (VBP15-006; clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05185622), NCT03863119 (EAP; clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03863119). First submitted November 9, 2021. First patient enrolled: March 21, 2022.Classification of EvidenceThe VBP15-006 study provides Class IV evidence that in boys with DMD aged 2–<4 years, 12-week treatment with vamorolone was not associated with serious adverse events or changes in weight, height, or BMI. Gastrointestinal events and infections were the most reported TEAEs.

Introduction

Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD) is an X-linked recessive neuromuscular disorder. Current standard-of-care includes oral classic corticosteroids (CSs), such as deflazacort or prednisone/prednisolone, to delay loss of ambulation.1 However, long-term CS use is associated with side effects including adrenal suppression, weight gain, growth stunting, osteoporosis, cataracts, and mood disorders, leading to substantial morbidity.2,3

Vamorolone is the first dissociative steroidal anti-inflammatory drug approved since 2023 for treatment of boys with DMD. In the United States, vamorolone is indicated for patients ≥2 years old, at 6 mg/kg/d recommended dosage (300 mg maximum daily dosage for patients >50 kg).4 In the European Union, the United Kingdom, China, Hong Kong, and Canada, vamorolone is indicated for patients ≥4 years old, at 6 mg/kg/d recommended dosage (240 mg maximum daily dosage for patients ≥40 kg).5-7

Vamorolone differs from classic CS by substitution of the C11β-hydroxyl group with a carbon-carbon double bond.8 As a glucocorticoid receptor (GR) agonist, vamorolone maintains trans-repression activity, including inhibition of the nuclear factor kappa B (NF-ĸB) proinflammatory pathway, while exerting reduced glucocorticoid response element-mediated transactivation, with less downstream effects (potentially linked to side effects).9 Vamorolone acts as a mineralocorticoid receptor (MR) antagonist,10 unlike the MR agonist prednisone, and improved aldosterone-induced cardiomyopathy in the mdx mouse model of DMD.11 Vamorolone is not a substrate of 11β-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase (11-β-HSD) enzymes, which interconvert classic CS between active and inactive forms, making vamorolone constitutively and predictably active, and potentially avoiding side effects attributable to 11-β-HSD I– and II-mediated activation of prednisone.12

Previous vamorolone studies, including a randomized, double-blind, 48-week trial in 4–<7-year-old boys with DMD (VBP15-004; ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT03439670), indicated improved motor function without growth stunting or reductions in serum bone turnover biomarkers.13,14 Maintaining efficacy while reducing adverse effects of classic CS treatment represents an unmet need for individuals with DMD, especially among younger patients.

The average age at diagnosis of DMD is ∼5 years, but infants and younger boys with DMD already show reduced motor performance compared with healthy peers.15-17 Effective therapies in younger children could help justify broad implementation of newborn screening for DMD to reduce time to diagnosis and treatment.18

The US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approval of vamorolone for treatment of individuals with DMD was based on available data from clinical studies of boys with DMD, including preliminary data from VBP15-006, ongoing at the time of application. The primary research questions being addressed in VBP15-006 were the safety and tolerability of vamorolone 2 and 6 mg/kg/d administered daily by liquid oral suspension over a treatment period of 12 weeks in steroid-naive boys with DMD aged 2–<4 years old.

Here, we present results from VBP15-006, a phase II, open-label, multiple-dose study to confirm safe and tolerable dose(s) of vamorolone for 2–<4-year-old boys with DMD, based upon safety and pharmacokinetic (PK) data over 12 weeks of treatment from 5 participating pediatric centers in Canada. Upon completion of the study, all but one of the patients opted to continue vamorolone treatment in the expanded access protocol (EAP) Canada; additional data from that study are included here to provide a longer follow-up.

Methods

VBP15-006 Patient Population

VBP15-006 was conducted at 5 sites in Canada. Inclusion criteria stipulated male pediatric patients with DMD diagnosis confirmed by genetic testing and/or dystrophin deficiency on muscle biopsy and evidence of varicella zoster virus immunity. Exclusion criteria included immunosuppression, diabetes mellitus, and any major renal or hepatic impairment. Individuals 2–<4 years old who were being treated or had previously been treated with oral glucocorticoids or other oral immunosuppressive agents were also excluded. Patients were grouped based on age and prior CS treatment status. Two age groups were assessed: (1) 2–<4 years old, CS-naive; (2) 7–<18 years old, CS-untreated and previously CS-treated. The results for 2–<4-year-olds are reported here; the results for 7–<18-year-olds are reported elsewhere due to the complexity of the data sets and the substantial differences between the patient groups in terms of age, disease stage, and prior CS exposure.19 VBP15-006 was designed before completion of VBP15-004. The first patient enrolled in VBP15-006 on March 21, 2022, and the last completed the study on June 17, 2024.

VBP15-006 Study Design

Participants were assigned to treatment dose groups (2 or 6 mg/kg/d) following screening and entered a 12-week open-label treatment period (Figure 1). An oral vamorolone suspension (4% weight/volume in flavored syrup) was administered once daily using a volumetric syringe, allowing accurate dosing in young children. A new route of synthesis (ROS) for obtaining active pharmaceutical ingredient and formulation was used in this study (ROS2; also used for marketed formulation). Doses were taken at breakfast with food but without specific fat content cointake requirements. Participants were assigned sequentially: the 2 mg/kg/d group was fully recruited (n = 10) before participants were assigned to the 6 mg/kg/d group (n = 10). After 12 weeks, patients not transitioning directly to further vamorolone (see section below) or classic CS treatment began dose tapering with a scheduled 16-week visit. Hydrocortisone (or prednisone) stress dosing was permitted during illness, injury, or surgery to prevent adrenal crisis according to site standard protocol. The study protocol is available in eSAP 1.

The 6 mg/kg/d group started after completion of the 2 mg/kg/d group. Both studies were registered on ClinicalTrials.gov: VBP15-006 open-label study (NCT05185622), and EAP Canada study (NCT03863119). CS = corticosteroid; DMD = Duchenne muscular dystrophy; EAP = expanded access protocol.

EAP Study

An ongoing, open-label study allowed patients who completed VBP15-006 to continue access to vamorolone treatment before regulatory approval in Canada. Data collection during the EAP was conducted with quality control measures proportionate to its primary purpose. The EAP protocol reflected routine clinical practice at each site; patients had ∼6-monthly visits. A snapshot of available data on former VBP15-006 patients currently enrolled in the EAP is reported here for additional clinical context of longer-term vamorolone treatment. Dose adjustments between 2/4/6 mg/kg/d vamorolone were permitted in the EAP; therefore, individual patients may have received various doses at different time points, and the results are presented for the pooled 2–6 mg/kg/d dose group. The EAP protocol is available in eSAP 2.

VBP15-006 Outcomes and Assessments

The primary outcome of VBP15-006 was evaluation of the safety and tolerability of oral vamorolone at 2 and 6 mg/kg/d over 12 weeks. This was assessed by change from baseline (CBL) in weight, height, body mass index (BMI), occurrence of treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) and serious adverse events (SAEs), physical examination, vital signs, clinical laboratory tests (eMethods), ophthalmic examination, and 12-lead ECG. Secondary outcomes were vamorolone PK and confirmation of drug exposure. Exploratory outcomes included vamorolone effects on muscle function (measured using Bayley Scales of Infant and Toddler Development version III gross motor scaled score [Bayley-III GMSS]). Pharmacodynamic (PD) biomarkers of safety and efficacy were evaluated in the central laboratory. PD biomarkers included: morning serum cortisol levels as a marker of adrenal suppression; bone turnover biomarkers (serum osteocalcin, C-terminal peptide of type I collagen [CTX], and procollagen type I N-terminal propeptide [P1NP]); glucose metabolism (fasting glucose, insulin, and glycated hemoglobin [HbA1c]) (eMethods). The impact of vamorolone on neuropsychology and behavior (Personal Adjustment and Role Skills Scale, third edition [PARS III]) and physical functioning (Pediatric Outcomes Data Collection Instrument [PODCI]) was also evaluated, along with Ease of Study Medication Administration Assessment.

PK Analysis in VBP15-006

Predose and postdose plasma concentration measurements of vamorolone produced through ROS2 were evaluated at day 1 (after single dosing) and week 2 (at steady-state level, after multiple dosing). Measurements were taken at baseline and 1, 2, and 6 hours postdose. PK parameters were derived using a standard noncompartmental analysis approach on day 1 and week 2 with Phoenix WinNonlin software (version 8.4). The area under the curve (AUC) was computed using the linear-up/log-down trapezoidal rule. The AUC extrapolated to infinity (AUCinf) was calculated by adding the observed AUC up to the last measurable concentration and an extrapolated portion representing the remaining elimination. The half-life (t½) was defined as ln2/λz, with λz (terminal rate constant) derived from the terminal log-linear phase. Cmax and Tmax were taken directly from the observed data. As the new ROS of vamorolone (ROS2) was used; PK and exposure data from 2 mg/kg/d were compared with previous formulation data (ROS1) in 4–<7 year-old boys with DMD20 before enrolling patients into the 6 mg/kg/d dose group.

Statistical Methods

The VBP15-006 sample size of 20 participants (10 for each group) was considered to provide sufficient precision for assessment of the primary objective and was not based on formal hypothesis testing. Assuming a true adverse event incidence of 40%, a sample of 10 participants yields a 95% confidence interval of 40% ± 30% based on Wald's normal approximation. This was considered sufficient for estimation of adverse event rates in the present study and was informed by previous study of vamorolone at these doses. The sample size was also considered sufficient to determine PK parameters. All measurements for both VBP15-006 and EAP Canada were analyzed according to the VBP15-006 statistical analysis plan (SAP) and the data analysis plan (DAP) specific to this manuscript. Descriptive post hoc subgroup analyses of Bayley-III data were performed. Descriptive statistics were presented by treatment group and assessment time point. Figures (box plots, scatter plots and line plots) were produced for numerical end points when applicable. Statistical analyses were performed using SAS version 9.4. The SAP and DAP are available in eSAP 3 and eSAP 4, respectively.

Standard Protocol Approvals, Registrations, and Patient Consents

The clinical study protocol and all amendments, informed consent form (ICF), and other appropriate documents were approved by the Institutional Review Board/Independent Ethics Committee prior to start of study at the 5 recruitment sites (eMethods). Before any study procedures, each patient's parent/legal guardian provided written consent using the ICF. The study protocol and statistical and data analysis plans are available in eSAP1–4.

Trial Registration

These trials were registered at ClinicalTrials.gov, identifiers NCT05185622 (VBP15-006), NCT03863119 (EAP Canada).

Data Availability

Requests from investigators for fully anonymized aggregated study results, as reported in this publication, will be considered on a case-by-case basis and will be subject to a signed data sharing agreement.

Results

Patient Disposition

Twenty 2–<4-year-old patients enrolled in VBP15-006, 10 each in the 2 and 6 mg/kg/d groups. All completed the study and 19 continued vamorolone treatment in EAP Canada (1 patient in the 2 mg/kg/d group switched to classic CS treatment to participate in a gene therapy trial). No patients underwent dose tapering; all results are from the 12-week time point.

Patient Characteristics

Baseline characteristics were similar between dose groups (Table 1, eAppendix 1). The youngest patient was 2.8 years, and the oldest was 4.0 years.

Safety

Growth Trajectories: CBL Height, Weight, and BMI

Stable height z-scores during VBP15-006 suggested normal growth. The median (Q1; Q3) CBL to week 12 in height z-score was −0.04 (−0.15; 0.17) and 0.09 (−0.02; 0.28) in 2 and 6 mg/kg/d groups, respectively. There was no evidence of excessive weight gain over this 12-week time frame; the median (Q1; Q3) CBL to week 12 in weight z-scores was −0.01 (−0.13; 0.24) and 0.13 (−0.33; 0.46) in 2 and 6 mg/kg/d groups, respectively. The median (Q1; Q3) BMI z-score CBL to week 12 was 0.12 (−0.10; 0.44) and −0.13 (−0.39; 0.42), for 2 and 6 mg/kg/d, respectively, with minor changes observed in the 6 mg/kg/d group.

As 12 weeks' observation is insufficient to adequately assess linear growth, EAP data were also evaluated. The median (Q1; Q3) CBL height z-score was 0.066 (−0.189; 0.601) (Figure 2A, eTable 1), indicating no delay in growth and no accelerated growth. This finding was supported by comparing individual patient height trajectories with growth charts for normal and DMD-specific development (Figure 2B). The median (Q1; Q3) CBL in weight z-score was 0.316 (−0.101; 0.845) (eFigure 1, eTable 2). The median (Q1; Q3) CBL in BMI z-score was 0.234 (−0.077; 0.901) (Figure 2C, eTable 3), indicating an overall moderate increase in weight. The changes in BMI z-scores were ≤0.5 in 11/19 patients (median CBL −0.058) and >0.5 in 8/19 patients, suggestive of excessive weight gain (median CBL 1.025) (eFigure 2, eTable 3). Individual BMI trajectories compared with charts for normal and DMD-specific development are in Figure 2D.

TEAEs in VBP15-006

No TEAEs led to interruption or discontinuation of vamorolone treatment; no SAEs were reported during VBP15-006. TEAEs were more frequent in patients receiving 6 mg/kg/d vamorolone (Table 2). The most reported TEAEs were gastrointestinal (GI) disorders, followed by infections. No TEAEs related to weight gain were reported in 2–<4-year-olds. Four events under system-organ-class “psychiatric disorders” consistent with behavioral changes were reported in 2 patients (20%), both in the 6 mg/kg/d group: irritability, aggression, compulsive lip biting, mood swings (eAppendix 1). All 4 events were graded mild and required no treatment, suggesting limited clinical relevance; no events were reported in the 2 mg/kg/d group. The patient with a medical history of insomnia at baseline experienced no TEAE of insomnia on study.

PK in VBP15-006

Vamorolone was rapidly absorbed, reaching maximum concentration within 2 hours postdose for both doses (Figure 4, eTable 4). On day 1, maximum geometric mean concentrations (geometric SD) were 246.8 (2.01) ng/mL and 781.7 (1.77) ng/mL for the 2 and 6 mg/kg/d groups, respectively. Six hours after dosing, the geometric mean concentrations (geometric SD) were 106.1 (1.81) ng/mL and 218.8 (1.99) ng/mL for the 2 and 6 mg/kg/d groups, respectively. Before vamorolone administration at week 2 of the PK analysis, baseline concentrations for both dosages were below the lower limit of quantification (2 ng/mL), indicating vamorolone clearance between administrations. The maximum geometric mean concentrations (geometric SD) were 349.3 (2.06) ng/mL and 719.5 (2.23) ng/mL, which then decreased to 96.7 (2.47) ng/mL and 253.9 (1.48) ng/mL 6 hours after dosing for the 2 and 6 mg/kg/d groups, respectively. This was consistent with the elimination phase observed on day 1. The terminal phase could not be reliably characterized in this study due to insufficient data points in the log-linear elimination region. As a result, t½ and AUCinf could not be calculated. The 6 mg/kg/d dose consistently produced higher mean concentrations than the 2 mg/kg/d dose, demonstrating a dose-dependent PK profile. The same PK pattern was observed on both day 1 and week 2 of dosing. Variability was high, reflecting sparse sample collection and limited patient numbers. Both vamorolone doses showed consistent absorption times, exposure, and variability; the results were generally consistent with previously reported PK exposure data in 4–<7-year-olds.20

Gross Motor Function in VBP15-006

Muscle function, based on median Bayley III GMSS, was higher at week 12 than baseline for both dose groups (Figure 5, eTable 5). Most patients receiving 2 mg/kg/d had stable or increased GMSS at week 12; 9/10 patients receiving 6 mg/kg/d had improved GMSS at week 12. The median (Q1; Q3) GMSS CBL to week 12 was 0.0 (0.0; 1.0) and 2.5 (2.0; 4.0) in the 2 and 6 mg/kg/d groups, respectively.

The Bayley-III assessment is validated for patients up to 3.5 years. A post hoc analysis was performed to assess the impact of age on Bayley-III GMSS, as median age was 2 months older for 6 vs 2 mg/kg/d group. In patients ≤3.5 years, median GMSS CBL was 0.5 in the 2 mg/kg/d group (n = 4) and 2.0 in the 6 mg/kg/d (n = 2), showing an improvement in GMSS upon treatment with 6 mg/kg/d vamorolone (eTable 6). Patients >3.5 years had a median CBL of 0.0 and 2.5 in the 2 mg/kg/d (n = 5) and 6 mg/kg/d (n = 8) groups, respectively, indicating an improvement in Bayley III score with 6 mg/kg/d also in older participants.

PD Biomarkers of Safety in VBP15-006

Cortisol results are reported above. Vamorolone treatment at either dose had no effect on serum bone turnover biomarkers, with median (Q1; Q3) CBL for CTX1 of −35.5 (−211.0; 179.0) and 228.0 (−150.0; 317.0) ng/L, for osteocalcin of −4.7 (−10.5; 2.3) and 0.2 (−6.2; 12.9) µg/L, and for P1NP of −11.7 (−150.1; 75.3) and 30.3 (−89.4; 81.8) µg/L for 2 and 6 mg/kg/d groups, respectively. There was no change in evaluated insulin resistance biomarkers: fasting glucose (median [Q1; Q3] CBL: −0.165 [-0.390; −0.050] and −0.385 [−0.610; −0.050] mmol/L for 2 and 6 mg/kg/d groups, respectively) and HbA1c (median [Q1; Q3] CBL: 0.001 [−0.001; 0.002] and 0.000 [−0.001; 0.002] for 2 and 6 mg/kg/d groups, respectively). There was no evidence for increased fasting insulin for the available samples in the 6 mg/kg/d group at week 12 (median fasting insulin [Q1; Q3] 17.5 [10.0; 27.0] pmol/L, median CBL: 4.0 [−9.0; 14.0] pmol/L); a 50 pmol/L increase was observed in 1 patient, with 71 pmol/L at week 12. Fasting insulin data in the 2 mg/kg/d group were unavailable due to an oversight in sample handling in the central laboratory manual, later corrected.

Behavioral and Physical Functioning in VBP15-006

There were no relevant changes in PARS III total score or PODCI global functioning scores. Four TEAEs consistent with behavioral changes (all graded mild) were reported in 2 patients in the 6 mg/kg/d group (see Safety section above and eAppendix 1). All 4 events were deemed of limited clinical relevance.

Ease of Administration in VBP15-006

Most survey respondents (9/10) in each dose group described the ease of administering medicine as “cooperatively without opposition.” One 2 mg/kg/d dose group response was rated as “with opposition”; one 6 mg/kg/d dose group response was rated as “cooperatively with some opposition.” Most respondents (9/10) in each dose group found vamorolone “an easy addition to the morning routine”; one response in each dose group rated it “a manageable addition” to the morning schedule.

The results for VBP15-006 have also been posted on ClinicalTrials.gov.

Classification of Evidence

The VBP15-006 study provides Class IV evidence that in boys with DMD aged 2–<4 years, 12 weeks treatment with vamorolone was not associated with serious adverse events or changes in weight, height, or BMI. GI events and infections were the most reported TEAEs.

Discussion

Vamorolone is a dissociative steroidal anti-inflammatory drug approved in several countries for treatment of boys with DMD.4-6 All previous vamorolone studies included 4–<7-year-old boys with DMD; therefore, the 12-week long VBP15-006 study was the first to include boys with DMD <4 years. Here, we describe the main results of VBP15-006 in 2–<4-year-old boys (minimum and maximum ages 2.8 and 4.0 years, respectively), including anthropometric changes and reported safety of vamorolone from first exposure in VBP15-006 to the last available observation in EAP Canada; total treatment time was approximately 2 years.

The boys continued growing as expected over the initial 12-week study, and also during the EAP Canada follow-up period. Consistent with previous observations,13,14 serum bone turnover biomarkers were not negatively affected in this group during VBP15-006. Height trajectory analysis indicated growth as expected for age.21,22 Individual patients experienced excessive weight gain (CBL in BMI z-score >0.5); the median/mean change in BMI z-score in these patients after 2 years of treatment remained ∼1 SD. This range is expected for CS treatment; the weight gain could be addressed through further education and clinical support, followed by potentially reducing the 6 mg/kg/d dose if needed, while maintaining the highest tolerable dose.24 No weight-associated TEAEs were reported in VBP15-006, and 11/19 patients had no evidence of excessive weight increase during EAP Canada. These findings are comparable with the results from the pivotal VISION-DMD study, which also reported weight gain in a proportion of older 4–<7-year-old boys, despite a shorter follow-up period (∼1 year).13 During VBP15-006, there was no evidence of glucose metabolism changes, with stable mean fasting insulin in the 6 mg/kg/d group. Consistent with previous studies,20,25 no changes in fasting glucose or glycosylated hemoglobin were observed.

There were no new safety findings in the 2–<4-year-old group compared with previously reported findings in 4–<7-year-old boys.13,14 During VBP15-006, most TEAEs were mild and there were no SAEs, discontinuations, or fatal events. GI events and infections were the most common TEAEs reported. Children <5 years have greater vulnerability to infections, mainly due to an immature immune system.26 Infections in VBP15-006 were considered unrelated to vamorolone. Follow-up safety data on serious and severe TEAEs, available from ∼2 years' vamorolone exposure, support the initial 12-week study period findings. Most patients moving from VBP15-006 were on 6 mg/kg/d vamorolone at the start of EAP Canada (those on 2 mg/kg/d during VBP15-006 were up-titrated, as the protocol offered this flexibility). EAP Canada had the primary purpose of providing access to vamorolone treatment before approval in Canada. Therefore, the protocol reflected routine clinical practice at sites with ∼6-monthly visits; potential underreporting of TEAEs cannot be excluded.

The 12-week duration of VBP15-006 was sufficient to assess PK and exposure in 2–<4-year-old boys with DMD treated with vamorolone at 2 doses (2 and 6 mg/kg/d). In these CS-naive patients, vamorolone plasma concentrations increased in a dose-dependent manner, with higher exposure occurring with 6 mg/kg/d. Vamorolone was rapidly absorbed with a median Tmax of 2 hours observed at both doses. No evidence of drug accumulation was observed between day 1 and week 2 of daily administrations, consistent with the short half-life of vamorolone, previously reported to be ∼2 hours.20 Interpatient PK variability was high due to sparse sample collection and limited patient number; thus, the results should be interpreted cautiously. Overall, the PK profile of vamorolone (using the marketed ROS2 formulation) observed here in 2–<4-year-old boys with DMD was similar to that seen in studies using the ROS1 formulation in 4–<7-year-old boys with DMD.20

Adrenal suppression (morning serum cortisol ≤65 nmol/L) was identified based on week 12 cortisol levels. All events occurred with 6 mg/kg/d; no symptoms consistent with adrenal insufficiency were reported. There was a dose-dependent decrease in morning cortisol levels, aligning with prior findings.27 During VBP15-006, treatment with 2 mg/kg/d vamorolone resulted in a more variable degree of adrenal suppression at week 12 (33–223 nmol/L) vs 6 mg/kg/d (17–121 nmol/L). This is consistent with previous reports demonstrating a dose-dependent decrease in morning serum cortisol levels and adrenocorticotropic hormone–stimulated peak cortisol concentrations with vamorolone; virtually all patients receiving 6 mg/kg/d had impaired peak cortisol response.14,27

Patients with adrenal insufficiency may be asymptomatic or have nonspecific signs and symptoms in the absence of acute physiologic stress.28 Patients receiving vamorolone require hydrocortisone “stress doses” during moderate-to-severe illness, surgery, or injury to prevent adrenal crisis because vamorolone is an MR antagonist and could exacerbate rather than alleviate hypotension.4,5,29 During VBP15-006, 3 patients received hydrocortisone for TEAEs.

Besides small increases in hemoglobin and lymphocyte counts, consistent with prior results, there were no relevant changes in hematologic parameters during VBP15-006.25 Unlike previous reports, there was no evidence of an effect of vamorolone on cholesterol high-density lipoprotein (HDL) or triglycerides in VBP15-006.25

During the 12-week study period, muscle function improvements were observed. A larger increase in median Bayley III GMSS was seen in the 6 mg/kg/d group (5.0 at baseline to 8.0 at week 12) than in the 2 mg/kg/d group (from 3.5 to 4.0). The dose-dependent changes in GMSS were likely not attributable to differences in age between groups and suggest efficacy of 6 mg/kg/d dose vamorolone in this age group. A GMSS for healthy developing children is 10 ± 3; a scaled score <7 indicates a delay in gross motor development.30,31 The GMSS improvement observed in this study compares favorably with results observed in previous studies of <4-year-old patients with DMD treated with twice-weekly glucocorticoids.30 Specifically, the results for the 2 mg/kg/d group are similar to those reported by Connolly et al., but the results for the 6 mg/kg/d group represent an improvement, albeit over a shorter observation (12 weeks vs 1 year).

Newborn screening for DMD has been internally evaluated in several pilot programs.32-35 Although there is community support for broad implementation of newborn screening for DMD,36 it is not yet universally included in most internationally recommended panels, primarily due to a lack of available presymptomatic therapies for DMD. Future studies directed specifically at efficacy among younger patients may provide further support for broad implementation of newborn screening and potentially early intervention.

Limitations of VBP15-006 include the nonrandomized and open-label study design, the small sample size, and the relatively short 12-week duration of treatment. The age distribution of the patients (median [Q1; Q3] of 3.4 [3.1; 3.7]) may limit the generalizability of the data among younger individuals.

Our data indicate that participants maintained stable growth trajectories over approximately 2 years of vamorolone treatment. Some CS-naive patients were prone to weight gain on 6 mg/kg/d vamorolone, warranting further clinical monitoring and management. Safety results in 2–<4-year-old boys were largely consistent with the safety profile of vamorolone in older children and included dose-dependent adrenal suppression. Vamorolone had a dose-dependent PK profile, consistently higher drug exposure with the 6 mg/kg/d dose, and no evidence of drug accumulation. There was a dose-dependent improvement in motor ability in patients treated with vamorolone. These data suggest that vamorolone may offer therapeutic benefit for young boys with DMD.

A solution to Banach conjecture
Ning Zhang

Abstract
. In this paper, we begin by constructing two families of origin-symmetric star bodies derived from the John ellipsoid of the sections. We then use this framework to present a complete proof of Banach’s isometric subspace problem in finite-dimensional spaces via the John ellipsoid.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
52A20, 52A22

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we establish a positive answer to the following problem for finite
n
.

Problem 1.1
.

Let
(V,\|\cdot\|)
be a normed vector space (over
\mathbb{R}
) such that for some fixed
n
,
2\leq n\leq\mbox{dim}(V)
, all
n
-dimensional linear subspaces of
V
are isometric. Is
\|\cdot\|
neccessarily a Eulidean norm (i.e. an inner product one)?

This problem goes back to Banach in 1932
[
2
, Remarks on Chapter XII]
and is often referred to as the ”Banach conjecture.” The conjecture asserts that the answer is always affirmative. It has been confirmed in many, though not all, dimensions. Auerbach, Mazur, and Ulam established the conjecture for the case
n=2
in
[
1
]
. Dvoretzky
[
5
]
later proved it for infinite-dimensional spaces
V
and all
n\geq 2
. Gromov
[
7
]
showed that the answer is positive whenever
n
is even or
\dim(V)\geq n+2
. Bor, Hernández-Lamoneda, Jiménez-Desantiago, and Montejano extended Gromov’s theorem to all
n
congruent to
1
modulo
4
, with the exception of
n=133
; see
[
3
]
. More recently, Ivanov, Mamaev, and Nordskova
[
8
]
obtained an affirmative solution for
n=3
. This outcome leads from the analysis of sections back to the entire convex body, which provides the original insight for addressing this problem. The problem can also be formulated for complex normed spaces; see
[
4
]
,
[
7
]
, and
[
11
]
for further details.

Dvoretzky and Gromov reduced the problem to the situation where
\mbox{dim}(V)=n+1
for some finite integer
n\geq 2
. Then, the problem can be restated in geometric terms as follows.

Problem 1.2
.

Let
n\geq 3
and let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an origin-symmetric convex body. Assume that all intersections of
K
with
(n-1)
-dimensional linear subspaces are linearly equivalent. Is
K
neccessarily an ellipsoid?

The symmetry condition follows directly from Montejano in
[
12
]
: If
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a convex body and all intersections of
K
with
n
-dimensional linear subspaces are affine equivalent, then either
K
is symmetric with respect to
0
, or
K
is a (not necessarily centered) ellipsoid. Our principal result is the following theorem, which provides affirmative answers for every finite
n\geq 3
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n\geq 3
be an origin-symmetric convex body. Fix some direction
\xi_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. Suppose that for every
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
there exists a linear map
\phi_{\xi}\in GL(n)
such that

K\cap\xi^{\perp}=\phi_{\xi}\bigl(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp}\bigr)

and moreover
\phi_{\xi}(\xi_{0})=\xi
. Then
K
must be an ellipsoid.

Indeed, Banach’s conjecture requires only a linear map on
G(n,n-1)
. However, to complete the proof, we extend this linear map to the entire space by setting

\phi_{\xi}=\varphi_{\xi}\circ\psi_{\xi_{0},\xi},

where
\psi_{\xi_{0},\xi}\in SO(n)
is a rotation sending
\xi_{0}
to
\xi
, and
\varphi_{\xi}\in GL(n)
satisfies
\varphi_{\xi}(\xi)=\xi
.
Then the proof of Theorem
1.1
is divided into three steps. We first select a John ellipsoid in
K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp}
and then use the linear invariance property of John ellipsoids to obtain two families of corresponding star bodies
\{E_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in[0,1]}
and
\{\tilde{E}_{\beta}\}_{\beta\in[0,1]}
inside
K
. Secondly, we apply the level set method (see Süss’s conjecture
[
10
,
13
,
17
]
for further details) to demonstrate that there exists a value
t_{0}
such that
t_{0}K=E_{t_{0}}=\tilde{E}_{t_{0}}
. Having established these identities, we can then derive the desired result.

2.
Notation and Preliminaries

This section mainly presents some basic content, such as symbols and definitions. For more detailed content, readers can refer to the books by Gardner
[
6
]
and Schneider
[
14
]
.

Let
\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the classical
n
-dimensional Euclidean space. We denote by
\|x\|
the Euclidean norm of
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We write
B^{n}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\|x\|\leq 1\}
for the
unit ball
and
S^{n-1}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\|x\|=1\}
for the
unit sphere
. We denote
\{e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{n}\}
to be the standard basis in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For any
\xi\in S^{n-1}
, we denote
\xi^{\perp}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\langle x,\xi\rangle=0\}
to be a hyperplane that is perpendicular to
\xi
. Here,
\langle x,\xi\rangle
is the usual inner product in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The
Grassmann manifold
of
k
-dimensional subspaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is denoted by
G(n,k)
.
GL(n)
and
SL(n)
are the
general linear group
and the
special linear group
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For
\phi\in GL(n)
, we denote
\|\phi\|_{op}=\max_{x\in B^{n}}\|\phi(x)\|
by the
operator norm
of
\phi
.

A set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called
convex
if it contains the closed line segment joining any two of its points. A convex set is a
convex body
if it is compact and has a non-empty interior. A convex body is
strictly convex
if its boundary contains no line segments.

A compact set
L
is called a
star body
if the origin
O
is an interior point of
L
, every line through
O
meets
L
in a line segment, and its
Minkowski gauge
defined by

\|x\|_{L}=\inf\{a\geq 0:x\in aL\}

is a continuous function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The
radial function
of
L
is given by
\rho_{L}(x)=\|x\|_{L}^{-1}
, for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{O\}
. If
x\in S^{n-1}
, then
\rho_{L}(x)
is just the radius of
L
in the direction of
x
.

For
H\in G(n,k)
, we call a body a
revolution body with respect to
H
if, for any special orthonormal transformation
\phi\in SO(n)
with
\phi(H)=H
, we have
\phi(K)=K
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body. The
John ellipsoid
of
K
is the ellipsoid of maximal volume contained in
K
, denoted by
J(K)
. It is clear that the John ellipsoid is invariant under linear transformations; that is, for any
\phi\in GL(n)
,
J(\phi(K))=\phi(J(K))
.

The
level set method
(see
[
10
,
13
,
17
]
for more details) consists in identifying a function on the sphere that remains unchanged under linear (congruent) transformations relating sections or projections, so that one of its level sets can coincide with the entire sphere.

3.
Proof of the main theorem

We begin the proof with a special case.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
n\geq 3
be an origin-symmetric star body. If for any
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
,
K\cap\xi^{\perp}
is a centered ellipsoid, then
K
is an ellipsoid.

Theorem
3.1
follows from the classical characterization by Busemann of ellipsoids (see
[
15
, Theorem 3.1]
). The convexity of the body
K
results from the fact that if we have two points in the body, we can examine the plane they span (with the origin). Since the planar section is an ellipsoid by assumption, the entire line segment between
x
and
y
is contained in
K
.

Now we can prove the main theorem using John ellipsoids. First, we should introduce a couple of Lemmas.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\phi\in GL(n)
. Then, for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we have

\|x\|_{K}=\|\phi(x)\|_{\phi(K)}.

Proof.

\displaystyle\|\phi(x)\|_{\phi(K)}

\displaystyle=\inf\{\lambda:\phi(x)\in\lambda\phi(K)\}

\displaystyle=\inf\{\lambda:\phi(x)\in\phi(\lambda K)\}

\displaystyle=\inf\{\lambda:x\in\lambda K\}

\displaystyle=\|x\|_{K}.

∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n\geq 3
be an origin-symmetric convex body. Fix some direction
\xi_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. Suppose that for every
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, there exists a linear map
\phi_{\xi}\in GL(n)
such that

K\cap\xi^{\perp}=\phi_{\xi}\bigl(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp}\bigr)

and moreover
\phi_{\xi}(\xi_{0})=\xi
. Then the John ellipsoid
J(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp})
gives rise to two families of origin-symmetric star bodies
\{E_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in[0,1]}
and
\{\tilde{E}_{\beta}\}_{\beta\in[0,1]}
defined by

\begin{cases}E_{\alpha}:=\displaystyle\bigcup_{\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\phi_{\xi}\big(J(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp})\cap\alpha(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp})\big),\\[4.0pt]
\tilde{E}_{\beta}:=\displaystyle\bigcap_{\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\phi_{\xi}\big(J(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp})\cup\beta(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp})\big).\end{cases}

Proof.

By the definitions of
E_{\alpha}
and
\tilde{E}_{\beta}
, it follows that both
E_{\alpha}
and
\tilde{E}_{\beta}
are origin-symmetric star sets, with radial functions given by

\rho_{E_{\alpha}}(\theta)=\sup\big\{\min\{\rho_{\phi_{\xi}(J(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp}))}(\theta),\alpha\rho_{K}(\theta)\}:\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap\theta^{\perp}\big\}

and

\rho_{\tilde{E}_{\beta}}(\theta)=\inf\big\{\max\{\rho_{\phi_{\xi}(J(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp}))}(\theta),\beta\rho_{K}(\theta)\}:\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap\theta^{\perp}\big\},

respectively, here
\phi_{\xi}(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp})=K\cap\xi^{\perp}
. Therefore, to prove that
E_{\alpha}
and
\tilde{E}_{\beta}
are continuous, it suffices to establish the continuity of the mapping
\xi\mapsto\phi_{\xi}(J(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp}))
.

Now we define a set-valued function
\lambda:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\to GL(n)
to be

\lambda(\xi):=\{\phi_{\xi}\in GL(n):K\cap\xi^{\perp}=\phi_{\xi}(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp})\mbox{ and }\phi_{\xi}(\xi_{0})=\xi\}.

And we define the linear symmetry group
G_{K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp}}
to be

G_{K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp}}:=\{g\in GL(n):g(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp})=K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp}\mbox{ and }g(\xi_{0})=\xi_{0}\}.

Note that for any
\phi_{\xi},\psi_{\xi}\in\lambda(\xi)
, we have

\psi_{\xi}^{-1}\phi_{\xi}(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp})=K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp},

which gives
\phi_{\xi}\in\psi_{\xi}\circ G_{K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp}}
; that is

\lambda(\xi)=\phi_{\xi}\circ G_{K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp}},

for some
\phi_{\xi}\in\lambda(\xi)
. And thus, the right action of
G_{K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp}}
on
GL(n)
gives the quotient
GL(n)/G_{K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp}}
, where the set-valued function
\lambda(x)
becomes a map
\Phi:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\to GL(n)\backslash G_{K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp}}
with

K\cap\xi^{\perp}=\Phi(\xi)(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp})

Now we need to show
\Phi
is continuous. Note that the operator norm of
\phi_{\xi}\in\Phi(\xi)
is bounded, since

\|\phi_{\xi}\|_{op}\leq\frac{\max_{\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\rho_{K}(\theta)}{\min_{\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\rho_{K}(\theta)}.

We may choose an arbitrary sequence
\{\xi_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
converging to
\xi
, i.e., with
\lim_{n\to\infty}\xi_{n}=\xi
, and for each
n
select
\phi_{\xi_{n}}\in\Phi(\xi_{n})
. Then there exists a subsequence
\{\xi_{n_{k}}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
such that the corresponding operators
\phi_{\xi_{n_{k}}}
converge in operator norm to some
\phi\in GL(n)
. And thus, we have

\displaystyle d_{H}(\phi(K\cap\xi_{0}),K\cap\xi^{\perp})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d_{H}(\phi(K\cap\xi_{0}),K\cap\xi_{n_{k}}^{\perp})+d_{H}(K\cap\xi_{n_{k}}^{\perp},K\cap\xi^{\perp})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d_{H}(\phi(K\cap\xi_{0}),\phi_{\xi_{n_{k}}}(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp}))+d_{H}(K\cap\xi_{n_{k}}^{\perp},K\cap\xi^{\perp})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\left|\|\phi\|_{op}-\|\phi_{\xi_{n_{k}}}\|_{op}\right|+d_{H}(K\cap\xi_{n_{k}}^{\perp},K\cap\xi^{\perp}).

As
k\to\infty
, we have

d_{H}(\phi(K\cap\xi_{0}),K\cap\xi^{\perp})=0.

This implies that
\phi(K\cap\xi_{0})=K\cap\xi^{\perp}
. Moreover,

\phi(\xi_{0})=\lim_{k\to\infty}\phi_{\xi_{n_{k}}}(\xi_{0})=\lim_{k\to\infty}\xi_{n_{k}}=\xi.

and hence
\phi\in\Phi(\xi)
. Consequently, we have
\Phi(\xi_{n_{k}})\to\Phi(\xi)
as
k\to\infty
.

This means that for any subsequence
\{\Phi(\xi_{n_{k}})\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
, there exists a sub-subsequence
\{\Phi(\xi_{n_{k_{l}}})\}_{l=1}^{\infty}
that converges to
\Phi(\xi)
. Consequently, we conclude that
\Phi(\xi_{n})\to\Phi(\xi)
.

Moreover, for any
g\in G_{K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp}}
, we have

g(J(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp}))=J(g(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp}))=J(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp}),

since the John ellipsoid of
K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp}
is unique. Consequently,
J(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp})
is invariant under the group
G_{K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp}}
. Thus, the continuity of
\phi_{\xi}(J(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp}))
as a function of
\xi
follows from the continuity of
\Phi(\xi)
.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

A proof that
\Phi:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\to GL(n)/G_{K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp}}
is continuous is also provided in
[
3
, Lemma 1.5]
.

Remark 3.2
.

Observe that the continuous map
\Phi:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\to GL(n)/G_{K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp}}
admits a continuous lift
\phi:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\to GL(n)
such that
\phi(\xi_{0})
is the identity and, for every
\xi
, we can write
\phi(\xi)=\phi_{\xi}\circ g_{\xi}
with
g_{\xi}\in G_{K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp}}
. Moreover, both
K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp}
and
J(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp})
are preserved by
g_{\xi}
. Hence, without loss of generality, we will from now on take
\phi_{\xi}
to be this lift
\phi(\xi)
.

Our next objective is to define a system
\Gamma
on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times[0,1]
, given by

\Gamma:=\big\{(\theta,\xi,t)\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times[0,1]:\rho_{\phi_{\xi}(J(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp}))}(\theta)\|\theta\|_{K}=t\ \text{and}\ \theta\perp\xi\big\}.

Observe that, for any
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, Lemma
3.1
implies

\displaystyle\rho_{\phi_{\xi}(J(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp}))}(\theta)\|\theta\|_{K}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\|\rho_{\phi_{\xi}(J(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp}))}(\theta)\theta\big\|_{K\cap\xi^{\perp}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\|\phi_{\xi}(J(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp}))\cap\{\lambda\theta:\lambda>0\}\big\|_{K\cap\xi^{\perp}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\|J(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp})\cap\{\lambda\phi^{-1}_{\xi}(\theta):\lambda>0\}\big\|_{\phi^{-1}_{\xi}(K\cap\xi^{\perp})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\|J(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp})\cap\{\lambda\phi^{-1}_{\xi}(\theta):\lambda>0\}\big\|_{K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{J(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp})}(\phi^{-1}_{\xi}(\theta))\big\|\phi^{-1}_{\xi}(\theta)\big\|_{K}.

And hence, for any
\eta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}

(\theta,\xi,t)\in\Gamma\iff(\phi^{-1}_{\xi}(\theta),\xi_{0},t)\in\Gamma\iff(\phi_{\eta}\circ\phi^{-1}_{\xi}(\theta),\eta,t)\in\Gamma

From this point of view, we can construct a continuous mapping
\Lambda_{t}[\theta]:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, defined by

\Lambda_{t}[\theta](\eta)=\phi_{\eta}\phi^{-1}_{\xi}(\theta).

(1)

Then we can define a level set
\Lambda_{t}
to be

\Lambda_{t}:=\{\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}:(\theta,\xi,t)\in\Gamma\mbox{ for some }\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap\theta^{\perp}\}.

(2)

It is clear that
\Lambda_{t}[\theta](\eta)\subseteq\Lambda_{t}
for any
\theta\in\Lambda_{t}
.
We now present several basic propositions of
\Lambda_{t}[\theta]
and
\Lambda_{t}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n\geq 3
be an origin-symmetric convex body. Fix some direction
\xi_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. Suppose that for every
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, there exists a linear map
\phi_{\xi}\in GL(n)
such that

K\cap\xi^{\perp}=\phi_{\xi}\bigl(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp}\bigr)

and moreover
\phi_{\xi}(\xi_{0})=\xi
. Then the following propositions hold.

(1)

Either
\Lambda_{t}
is the whole sphere or the image of
\Lambda_{t}[\theta]
is a null-homotopy containing some equator;

(2)

\Lambda_{t}
is the whole sphere or a null-homotopy containing some equator;

(3)

There exists
t_{0}\in[0,1]
such that
\Lambda_{t_{0}}=\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
.

Before presenting the proof, we note that the
\phi_{\xi}
appearing here is the continuous lifting introduced in Remark
3.2
. After this lemma, however, the continuity of
\phi_{\xi}
is no longer required, since the set
\Lambda_{t}
does not depend on the continuity of
\phi_{\xi}
.

Proof.

First, we aim to prove that if
\Lambda_{t}
is a proper subset of the sphere, then
\Lambda_{t}[\theta]
is null-homotopic. In fact, if
a\notin\Lambda_{t}
, then
-a\notin\Lambda_{t}
also holds because
K
is symmetric about the origin. Thus,
a,-a\notin\mbox{Im}(\Lambda_{t}[\theta])
, and hence we can define a homotopy

F(\eta,s)=\frac{s\,\Lambda_{t}[\theta](\eta)+(1-s)a}{\|s\,\Lambda_{t}[\theta](\eta)+(1-s)a\|}.

Second,
\mathrm{Im}(\Lambda_{t}[\theta])
must include some equator on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. Observe that for any
\phi_{\eta}\circ\phi_{\xi}^{-1}(\theta)\in\Lambda_{t}[\theta]
and any
\zeta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap(\phi_{\eta}\circ\phi_{\xi}^{-1}(\theta))^{\perp}
, the set

\{\phi_{\vartheta}\circ\phi^{-1}_{\eta}(\phi_{\eta}\circ\phi_{\xi}^{-1}(\theta)):\vartheta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap\mathrm{span}(\phi_{\eta}\circ\phi_{\xi}^{-1}(\theta),\zeta)\}\subseteq\mathrm{Im}(\Lambda_{t}[\theta])

traces out a curve that passes through
\phi_{\eta}\circ\phi_{\xi}^{-1}(\theta)
and enters both
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap\zeta^{\perp}_{+}
and
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap\zeta^{\perp}_{-}
. Consequently, every boundary point
x
of the complement
C
of
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\setminus\mathrm{Im}(\Lambda_{t}[\theta])
must lie on a subsphere that is tangent at
x
and has radius
r_{x}<1
. This subsphere can be written as

\{y\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}:\langle y,o_{x}\rangle\geq\sqrt{1-r_{x}^{2}}\}.

Otherwise, we may prolong a curve contained in
\mathrm{Im}(\Lambda_{t}[\theta])
until it intersects
C
. By the compactness of
\bar{C}
, this yields a uniform radius
r<1
such that
C
lies entirely in a subsphere of radius
r
. Observe that
\mathrm{Im}(\Lambda_{t}[\theta])
is symmetric about the origin and null-homotopic, and thus
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\setminus\mathrm{Im}(\Lambda_{t}[\theta])
can consist of only two antipodal complementary regions. Consequently,
\mathrm{Im}(\Lambda_{t}[\theta])
must contain an equator. Now we prove Proposition (1).

For (2), assume
\Lambda_{t}
is not the entire sphere. Then for any
\theta\in\Lambda_{t}
, Proposition (1) tells us that
\mathrm{Im}(\Lambda_{t}[\theta])
is a null-homotopy that contains some equator. Consequently, for any
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in\Lambda_{t}
we have

\mathrm{Im}(\Lambda_{t}[\theta_{1}])\cap\mathrm{Im}(\Lambda_{t}[\theta_{2}])\neq\emptyset,

which shows that
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
are path-connected in
\Lambda_{t}
. Let
\gamma
be a path in
\Lambda_{t}
joining them. Then

\bigcup_{\theta\in\gamma}\mathrm{Im}(\Lambda_{t}[\theta])

must itself be a null-homotopy. This establishes Proposition (2).

For (3), observe that there must exist some
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp}
with
(\theta,\xi_{0},1)\in\Gamma
. If this were not the case, then
J(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp})
would lie strictly inside
K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp}
, contradicting the fact that
J(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp})
is the John ellipsoid.
If
\Lambda_{1}
is not equal to the entire sphere, then
\Lambda_{1}
is a null-homotopy that contains some equator. For sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
, the union
\bigcup_{t\in(1-\epsilon,1]}\Lambda_{t}
forms an open set
\mathcal{O}
that contains
\Lambda_{1}
. Assuming that none of the
\{\Lambda_{t}\}_{t\in[1-\epsilon,1]}
equals the whole sphere, we assert that

\Lambda_{1}\subseteq\Lambda_{1-\epsilon}.

If this were false, then
\Lambda_{1-\epsilon}
would provide a null-homotopy that contains
\partial\mathcal{O}
, with
\Lambda_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{O}
but omitting some points of
\Lambda_{1}
, which is a contradiction.

Likewise, for any
\epsilon>0
, assuming that none of the sets in the family
\{\Lambda_{t}\}_{t\in[1-\epsilon,1]}
coincides with the entire sphere, it follows that

\Lambda_{q}\subseteq\Lambda_{p}

whenever
1-\epsilon\leq p\leq q\leq 1
.

We define

m=\inf_{\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp}}\rho_{J(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp})}(\theta)\|\theta\|_{K}.

Then any
t
for which
\Gamma
holds must lie in the interval
[m,1]
. Indeed, if this were not the case, we could find
(\theta,\xi,t)\in\Gamma
with
t<m
, which would imply
(\phi^{-1}_{\xi}(\theta),\xi_{0},t)\in\Gamma
, contradicting the fact that
m
is the infimum. Thus, we obtain

\bigcup_{t\in[m,1]}\Lambda_{t}=\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.

Consequently, there must exist some
t_{0}
such that

\Lambda_{t_{0}}=\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.

Indeed, if this were not the case, then each
\Lambda_{t}
for
t\in[m,1]
would be a proper subset of the sphere. However, by the preceding argument,

\Lambda_{m}=\bigcup_{t\in[m,1]}\Lambda_{t}=\mathbb{S}^{n-1},

which is a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n\geq 3
be an origin-symmetric convex body. Fix some direction
\xi_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. Suppose that for every
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, there exists a linear map
\phi_{\xi}\in GL(n)
such that

K\cap\xi^{\perp}=\phi_{\xi}\bigl(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp}\bigr)

and moreover
\phi_{\xi}(\xi_{0})=\xi
. Then there exists
t_{0}\in[0,1]
such that

t_{0}K=E_{t_{0}}=\tilde{E}_{t_{0}}.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.1
(3), there exists some
t_{0}\in[0,1]
such that

\Lambda_{t_{0}}=\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. Hence, for every
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, there is a
\zeta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap\theta^{\perp}
satisfying

\rho_{\phi_{\zeta}(J(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp}))}(\theta)\|\theta\|_{K}=t_{0}.

Consequently,

\rho_{\phi_{\zeta}(J(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp}))}(\theta)=t_{0}\rho_{K}(\theta).

Using the definitions of
E_{t_{0}}
and
\tilde{E}_{t_{0}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\rho_{E_{t_{0}}}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\sup\big\{\min\{\rho_{\phi_{\xi}(J(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp}))}(\theta),t_{0}\rho_{K}(\theta)\}:\ \xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap\theta^{\perp}\big\}

\displaystyle=\rho_{\phi_{\zeta}(J(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp}))}(\theta)=t_{0}\rho_{K}(\theta).

Similarly,

\displaystyle\rho_{\tilde{E}_{t_{0}}}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\inf\big\{\max\{\rho_{\phi_{\xi}(J(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp}))}(\theta),t_{0}\rho_{K}(\theta)\}:\ \xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap\theta^{\perp}\big\}

\displaystyle=\rho_{\phi_{\zeta}(J(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp}))}(\theta)=t_{0}\rho_{K}(\theta).

Thus, we conclude that

t_{0}K=E_{t_{0}}=\tilde{E}_{t_{0}}.

∎

Now we can prove the main theorem.

Proof of the Theorem
1.1
.

By Lemma
3.3
, there exists
t_{0}\in[0,1]
such that

t_{0}K=E_{t_{0}}=\tilde{E}_{t_{0}}.

On the one hand, we observe that

\displaystyle E_{t}

\displaystyle=t_{0}K

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}t_{0}\phi_{\xi}\big(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp}\big)

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\phi_{\xi}\big(t_{0}(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp})\big)

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\phi_{\xi}\big([J(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp})\cap t_{0}(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp})]\cup[t_{0}(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp})\backslash J(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp})]\big)

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\phi_{\xi}\big(J(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp})\cap t_{0}(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp})\big)\cup\bigcup_{\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\phi_{\xi}\big(t_{0}(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp})\backslash J(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp})\big)

\displaystyle=E_{t}\cup\bigcup_{\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\phi_{\xi}\big(t_{0}(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp})\backslash J(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp})\big),

from which it follows that

t_{0}(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp})\backslash J(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp})=\emptyset.

On the other hand, we obtain

\displaystyle t_{0}K

\displaystyle=\tilde{E}_{t}

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\phi_{\xi}\big(J(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp})\cup t_{0}(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp})\big)

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\phi_{\xi}\big(t_{0}(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp})\cup\big(J(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp})\backslash t_{0}(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp})\big)\big)

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\phi_{\xi}\big(t_{0}(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp})\big)\cup\bigcap_{\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\phi_{\xi}\big(J(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp})\backslash t_{0}(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp})\big)

\displaystyle=t_{0}K\cup\bigcap_{\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\phi_{\xi}\big(J(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp})\backslash t_{0}(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp})\big)\,,

which implies that

J(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp})\backslash t_{0}(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp})=\emptyset.

Consequently,

J(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp})=t_{0}(K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp}),

so
K\cap\xi_{0}^{\perp}
is an ellipsoid, and by Theorem
3.1
this implies that
K
itself is an ellipsoid.
∎

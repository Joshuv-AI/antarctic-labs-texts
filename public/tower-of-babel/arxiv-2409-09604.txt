1.
Introduction

We study the incompressible steady Navier-Stokes equation in a two dimensional channel
\Omega=(-L,0)\times(-1,1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} u^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon}_{X}+v^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon}_{Y}+p_{X}^{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon\Delta u^{\varepsilon}&=0,\\
u^{\varepsilon}v^{\varepsilon}_{X}+v^{\varepsilon}v^{\varepsilon}_{Y}+p_{Y}^{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon\Delta v^{\varepsilon}&=0,\\
u^{\varepsilon}_{X}+v^{\varepsilon}_{Y}&=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

with the no-slip boundary condition on
\{Y=\pm 1\}
:

u^{\varepsilon}(X,1)=u^{\varepsilon}(X,-1)=v^{\varepsilon}(X,1)=v^{\varepsilon}(X,-1)=0.

The boundary condition at
X=-L
and
X=0
are considered as inflow and outflow conditions, which will be prescribed later in the article.

We would like to study the asymptotic behavior of the solutions
(u^{\varepsilon},v^{\varepsilon})
of (
1.1
) as the viscosity
\varepsilon\to 0
. Formally, as
\varepsilon\to 0
, the equation (
1.1
) converges to the steady Euler equation

\left\{\begin{aligned} u^{0}_{e}u^{0}_{eX}+v^{0}_{e}u^{0}_{eY}+p_{eX}^{0}&=0,\\
u^{0}_{e}v^{0}_{eX}+v^{0}_{e}v^{0}_{eY}+p_{eY}^{0}&=0,\\
u^{0}_{eX}+v^{0}_{eY}&=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.2)

with no penetration boundary condition on
\{Y=\pm 1\}
:

v^{0}_{e}(X,-1)=v^{0}_{e}(X,1)=0.

Due to the mismatch of the boundary value
u^{\varepsilon}
and
u^{0}_{e}
on
\{Y=\pm 1\}
, one does not expect the uniform convergence of
u^{\varepsilon}
to
u^{0}_{e}
. In 1904, Prandtl proposed a scheme that the solution of (
1.1
) can be expressed as the sum of (
1.2
) and a boundary layer corrector, which is a solution of the Prandtl equation. For example, near the bottom boundary
\{Y=-1\}
, Prandtl suggested that

\displaystyle u^{\varepsilon}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=u^{0}_{e}(X,Y)+u^{0}_{p}(x,y)+O(\sqrt{\varepsilon}),

(1.3)

\displaystyle v^{\varepsilon}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=v^{0}_{e}(X,Y)+\sqrt{\varepsilon}v^{0}_{p}(x,y)+O(\sqrt{\varepsilon}),

where
(x,y)
are the scaled variables

x=X,\quad y=\frac{Y+1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}},

and
(u^{0}_{p},v^{0}_{p})
is a solution to the Prandtl equation

\left\{\begin{aligned} \Big(u^{0}_{e}(X,-1)+u^{0}_{p}\Big)u^{0}_{px}+\Big(v^{0}_{p}-v^{0}_{p}(x,0)\Big)u^{0}_{py}+p_{px}^{0}-u^{0}_{pyy}&=0,\\
p_{py}^{0}&=0,\\
u^{0}_{px}+v^{0}_{py}&=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.4)

with the boundary condition
u^{0}_{p}|_{y=0}=-u^{0}_{e}|_{Y=-1}
.

Although the ansatz (
1.3
) was proposed over a century ago, its validity has only been rigorously justified very recently. This was first achieved by Guo and Nguyen in
[
6
]
, under the assumption of a moving boundary for small
x
when the background Euler flow is a shear flow. This result has since been generalized in several directions. Iyer, in
[
9
]
, studied flows over a rotating disk, established global validity in
x
in
[
10
,
11
,
13
]
, and considered non-shear background Euler flows in
[
12
]
. All of these results assume that solutions of the Navier-Stokes equations satisfy a slip boundary condition.

The validity of (
1.3
) under the classical no-slip boundary condition was recently established by Guo and Iyer
[
5
]
for shear Euler flow in
x\in(0,L)
for
L
sufficiently small. This result covers an important class of Prandtl layer, the Blasius self-similar solutions. Gao and Zhang
[
2
]
generalized this result in two directions: (1) the background Euler can be non-shear, and (2)
L
can be large under the assumption that the Prandtl layer is concave. Meanwhile, shear Prandtl layer was consider by Gerard-Varet and Maekawa
[
3
]
in a narrow periodic in
x
domain. Finally, Iyer and Masmoudi
[
8
]
established the global in
x
validity of (
1.3
).

The Prandtl layer is known to exhibit a small scale in
Y
variable near the boundaries
\{Y=\pm 1\}
, while it does not exhibit any small scale in
X
variable. The main objective of this article is to demonstrate the existence of solutions to (
1.1
) that are close to the ansatz (
1.3
) in the
L^{\infty}
topology, and also exhibit small-scale behavior in
X
variable near the right side boundary
\{X=0\}
. We will construct the solution in the following form:

\left\{\begin{aligned} u^{\varepsilon}&=u_{e}^{0}+u_{p}+u_{r}+\varepsilon^{13/2}u,\\
v^{\varepsilon}&=v_{e}^{0}+v_{p}+v_{r}+\varepsilon^{13/2}v.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.5)

Here in (
1.5
),
(u_{e}^{0},v_{e}^{0})
represents the background Euler flow;
(u_{p},v_{p})
consists of the classical alternating Prandtl-Euler correctors;
(u_{r},v_{r})
is the profile that reveals an
\varepsilon
small scale near the right outlet: the leading order of
(u_{r},v_{r})
behaves like

\left(\varepsilon h^{\prime}(Y)\exp\Big\{Xu_{e}^{0}(0,Y)/\varepsilon\Big\},~h(Y)\exp\Big\{Xu_{e}^{0}(0,Y)/\varepsilon\Big\}\right)

near
X=0
, where
h(Y)
is a smooth compact support function in
Y\in(-1,1)
. Detailed constructions of
(u_{p},v_{p})
and
(u_{r},v_{r})
will be provided in Section
2
. Finally,
(u,v)
represents the remainder. It is important to emphasize that the magnitude of
v_{r}
is of order 1, while the magnitude of
v_{p}
is of order
\sqrt{\varepsilon}
. Therefore,
v_{r}
dominates in the vertical velocity. We would like to point out that a similar profile to
(u_{r},v_{r})
was obtained by Temam and Wang
[
17
]
where they studied the boundary layer induced by suction. While they imposed periodic boundary conditions in the other variable, resulting in the absence of a Prandtl boundary layer, our result demonstrates that the layer profile
(u_{r},v_{r})
and the Prandtl boundary layer can coexist.

In this article, we assume that our background Euler flow
(u_{e}^{0},v_{e}^{0})
satisfies the following assumptions:

\displaystyle 0<c_{0}\leq u_{e}^{0}\leq C_{0}<\infty,

(1.6)

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{v_{e}^{0}}{\widetilde{Y}}\right\|_{\infty}\ll 1,

(1.7)

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{m}u_{e}^{0}\|_{\infty}<\infty\quad\mbox{for}~m\geq 0,

(1.8)

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{m}v_{e}^{0}\|_{\infty}<\infty\quad\mbox{for}~m\geq 0,

(1.9)

where
\widetilde{Y}:=(1-Y)(1+Y)
. The existence of such an Euler flow can be obtained through a perturbation from a shear flow (see
[
12
]
*Proposition 24, for example).

Now we state our main theorems. The space
\mathcal{X}
we use to control the remainder is defined in (
1.13
). The first result is the existence of solution for small
L
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that the background Euler flow
(u_{e}^{0},v_{e}^{0})
satisfies (
1.6
)-(
1.9
) and
L\ll 1
, let
(u_{p},v_{p})
be the alternating Prandtl-Euler corrector as in (
2.1
),
h(Y)\in C_{c}^{\infty}(-1,1)
satisfying
\|h\|_{\infty}\ll 1
, and
(u_{r},v_{r})
be the profile constructed in (
2.13
). Then for any
0<\varepsilon\ll L
, the equation (
1.1
) admits a solution
(u^{\varepsilon},v^{\varepsilon})
in the form of (
1.5
), with the boundary conditions matching the approximated profile:

\displaystyle(u^{\varepsilon},v^{\varepsilon})|_{Y=\pm 1}=0,

\displaystyle(u^{\varepsilon},v^{\varepsilon})|_{X=-L}=(u_{e}^{0}+u_{p},v_{e}^{0}+v_{p})|_{X=-L},

\displaystyle(u^{\varepsilon},v^{\varepsilon})|_{X=0}=(u_{e}^{0}+u_{p}+\varepsilon h(Y)/u_{e}^{0}+O(\varepsilon^{3/2}),v_{e}+v_{p}+h(Y)+O(\varepsilon^{1/2}))|_{X=0},

and the remainder
(u,v)\in\mathcal{X}
with the estimate

\|\{u,v\}\|_{\mathcal{X}}\leq 1.

For the second result, we restrict the background Euler flow to be a shear, and make an additional concavity assumption on the Prandtl profile constructed, we can obtain the same existence result for large
L
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that the background Euler flow is a shear flow
(u_{e}^{0}(Y),0)
that satisfies (
1.6
) and (
1.8
), let
L>0
,
(u_{p},v_{p})
be the alternating Prandtl-Euler corrector as in (
2.1
) with

-u^{0}_{pYY}\geq 0,

(1.10)

h(Y)\in C_{c}^{\infty}(-1,1)
satisfying
\|h\|_{\infty}\ll 1
, and
(u_{r},v_{r})
be the profile constructed in (
2.13
). Then for any
0<\varepsilon\ll L
, the equation (
1.1
) admits a solution
(u^{\varepsilon},v^{\varepsilon})
in the form of (
1.5
), with the boundary conditions matching the approximated profile:

\displaystyle(u^{\varepsilon},v^{\varepsilon})|_{Y=\pm 1}=0,

\displaystyle(u^{\varepsilon},v^{\varepsilon})|_{X=-L}=(u_{e}^{0}+u_{p},v_{e}^{0}+v_{p})|_{X=-L},

\displaystyle(u^{\varepsilon},v^{\varepsilon})|_{X=0}=(u_{e}^{0}+u_{p}+\varepsilon h(Y)/u_{e}^{0}+O(\varepsilon^{3/2}),v_{e}+v_{p}+h(Y)+O(\varepsilon^{1/2}))|_{X=0},

and the remainder
(u,v)\in\mathcal{X}
with the estimate

\|\{u,v\}\|_{\mathcal{X}}\leq 1.

Remark 1.3
.

It is important to note that, unlike the small scale in the
Y
variable found in the classical Prandtl boundary layer, where the small scale needs to be introduced through the boundary conditions at
X=-L
, the small scale from
(u_{r},v_{r})
cannot be seen on the boundary. In this sense, the small scale from
(u_{r},v_{r})
is more spontaneous.

Remark 1.4
.

The assumption (
1.10
) is natural in the sense that an important class of self-similar solution to (
1.4
), the Blasius solutions, satisfy (
1.10
). It is also known that a general class of solution to (
1.4
) converges to the Blasius solutions as
x\to\infty
(see, e.g.,
[
16
,
14
]
).

Proofs of these two theorems consist of two steps. We first construct an approximated profile

(u_{s},v_{s}):=(u_{a}+u_{r},v_{a}+v_{r}):=(u_{e}^{0}+u_{p}+u_{r},v_{e}^{0}+v_{p}+v_{r}).

For convenience, we often work with the stream function
\phi(X,Y)
, where

u=\phi_{Y},\quad v=-\phi_{X}.

Similarly, we use
\phi_{s},\phi_{a},\phi_{e},\phi_{p},\phi_{r},
to denote the stream function of
u_{s},u_{a},u_{e}^{0},u_{p},u_{r}
, respectively.
A crucial quantity, the “quotient”, is defined as

q:=\frac{\phi}{u_{a}}.

This quantity plays an important role in establishing the validity of steady Prandtl layer expansions in
[
5
]
.

After constructing the approximated profile, the remainder
\phi
satisfies

u_{s}\Delta\phi_{X}-\Delta u_{s}\phi_{X}+v_{s}\Delta\phi_{Y}-\Delta v_{s}\phi_{Y}-\varepsilon\Delta^{2}\phi=-\varepsilon^{-\frac{13}{2}}R-\varepsilon^{\frac{13}{2}}(\phi_{Y}\Delta\phi_{X}-\phi_{X}\Delta\phi_{Y}),

(1.11)

where

R=u_{s}\Delta\phi_{sX}+v_{s}\Delta\phi_{sY}-\varepsilon\Delta^{2}\phi_{s}.

(1.12)

The space
\mathcal{X}
is defined via

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}:=\{\phi\in H^{3}(\Omega):\phi=\partial_{\nu}\phi=0~\mbox{on}~\partial\Omega,\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{X}}<\infty\},

(1.13)

where

\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{X}}:=\|\nabla\phi\|_{2}+\varepsilon^{1/2}\|\nabla^{2}\phi\|_{2}+\varepsilon^{1/2}\|\sqrt{u_{a}}\nabla^{2}q\|_{2}+\varepsilon^{3}\|\nabla^{3}\phi\|_{2}<\infty.

Since the right-hand side of (
1.11
) is small, the second step is to establish the linear stability of approximated profile
\phi_{s}
. We aim to derive an estimate for the remainder
\phi
in the space
\mathcal{X}
, which contains an
\varepsilon
-independent
H^{1}
estimate. This estimate arises from the term
u_{s}\Delta\phi_{X}
, whose coefficient
u_{s}=0
at
Y=\pm 1
. On the other hand, the profile
v_{r}
introduces a highly singular coefficient:

\Delta v_{r}\sim\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}h(Y)e^{Xu_{e}^{0}(0,Y)/\varepsilon}.

Therefore, equation (
1.11
) can be interpreted as a mixed degenerate-singular elliptic equation.

To obtain the desired estimate for
\phi
, we follow a similar approach as in
[
2
]
. Due to the presence of the singular coefficient
\Delta v_{r}
, we utilize a different Hardy-type inequality (
3.8
). To close the estimate, we make use of the weight
X
,which has been effectively used in
[
12
,
8
,
2
]
. It is important to note that the weight
X
interacts favorably with the profile
v_{r}
, as
X\Delta v_{r}\sim\varepsilon^{-1}
reduces the singularity by a factor of
\varepsilon
.

The rest of this article is planned as follows. In Section
2
, we provide the construction of the approximated profile
\phi_{s}
. Estimates for (
1.11
) will be given in Section
3
. Finally in Section
4
, we prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.

2.
Construction of the approximated profiles

In this section, we outline the construction of the approximated profile
\phi_{p}
and
\phi_{r}
, given a background Euler flow
\phi_{e}
. The profile
\phi_{p}
is composed of alternating Prandtl and Euler correctors, a well-established approach. Therefore, we will only provide an overview of the strategy for constructing
\phi_{p}
and the corresponding estimates. For a detailed construction of
\phi_{p}
, readers are referred to
[
15
,
4
,
5
,
7
]
. The main novelty is the construction of
\phi_{r}
, given the profile
\phi_{e}+\phi_{p}
.

2.1.
Construction of
\phi_{p}

We start with the asymptotic expansion:

\displaystyle u_{p}

\displaystyle=u_{p}^{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{15}\sqrt{\varepsilon}^{i}(u_{e}^{i}+u_{p}^{i}),

(2.1)

\displaystyle v_{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{15}\sqrt{\varepsilon}^{i}(v_{p}^{i-1}+v_{e}^{i})+\sqrt{\varepsilon}^{16}v_{p}^{15},

\displaystyle p_{p}

\displaystyle=p_{p}^{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{15}\sqrt{\varepsilon}^{i}(p_{e}^{i}+p_{p}^{i}).

Each
(u_{p}^{i},v_{p}^{i})
consists of two component: one supported near
Y=-1
, denoted by
(u_{p}^{i,-},v_{p}^{i,-})
; the other one supported near
Y=1
, denoted by
(u_{p}^{i,+},v_{p}^{i,+})
. The role of the first Prandtl corrector
(u_{p}^{0},v_{p}^{0})
is to balance the positive boundary value
u_{e}
at
Y=\pm 1
. This process generates an error of size
\sqrt{\varepsilon}
and introduces a nonzero boundary value of
v_{p}^{0}
at
Y=\pm 1
, which will be subsequently corrected by the Euler corrector
(u_{e}^{1},v_{e}^{1})
. And this process will repeat recurrently.

For the Prandtl correctors, we will only describe the construction and the properties of
(u_{p}^{i,-},v_{p}^{i,-})
. Define the scaled variables

x=X,\quad y=\frac{Y+1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}.

(2.2)

Then
(u_{p}^{0,-},v_{p}^{0,-})
satisfies the nonlinear Prandtl equation (
1.4
) with the boundary conditions

u_{p}^{0,-}|_{x=-L}=U^{0}_{p},\quad u_{p}^{0,-}|_{y=0}=-u_{e}^{0}|_{Y=-1},\quad u_{p}^{0,-}|_{y\to\infty}=v_{p}^{0,-}|_{y\to\infty}=0.

Under some compatibility conditions of the boundary data
U_{p}^{0}
, the existence of solution was established by Oleinik
[
15
]
, and the higher order regularties were obtained by Guo and Iyer
[
4
]
for small
x
, and by Wang and Zhang
[
18
]
for large
x
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume the prescribed boundary data
U_{p}^{0}(y)\in C^{\infty}
satisfies

U_{p}^{0}>0~~\mbox{for}~y>0,\quad\partial_{y}U_{p}^{0}(0)>0,\quad\partial_{y}^{2}U_{p}^{0}(y)-p^{0}_{px}(0)\sim y^{2}~~\mbox{near}~y=0.

Then for some
L>0
(
L
can be any positive number if
p_{px}^{0}\leq 0
), there exists a solution
(u_{p}^{0,-},v_{p}^{0,-})
to (
1.4
) satisfying, for some
y_{0},m_{0}>0
,

\sup_{x\in(-L,0)}\sup_{y\in(0,y_{0})}|u_{p}^{0,-},v_{p}^{0,-},\partial_{y}u_{p}^{0,-},\partial_{yy}u_{p}^{0,-},\partial_{x}u_{p}^{0,-}|\lesssim 1,

\sup_{x\in(-L,0)}\sup_{y\in(0,y_{0})}\partial_{y}u_{p}^{0}>m_{0}>0.

(2.3)

Further more, fix an
N>0
, assume generic compatibility conditions (see
[
4
]
*Definition 1) at the corner
(-L,0)
, and the exponential decay of derivatives:

\|\partial_{y}^{j}U_{p}^{0}e^{Ny}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{0}\quad\mbox{for}~j\geq 0.

Then

\|e^{Ny}\nabla^{j}\{u_{p}^{0,-},v_{p}^{0,-}\}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim C_{0}\quad\mbox{for}~j\geq 0.

The theorem above provides the existence of
(u_{p}^{0,-},v_{p}^{0,-})
, and
(u_{p}^{0,+},v_{p}^{0,+})
can be constructed in a similar manner. Since we require that
(u_{p}^{0,-}=v_{p}^{0,-})=0
at
Y=1
, we introduce a smooth cut-off function
\chi(Y)\in C_{c}^{\infty}([0,2))
such that
\chi=1
in
[0,1/2]
. We then define the first Prandtl corrector
(u_{p}^{0},v_{p}^{0})
as follows:

\displaystyle u_{p}^{0}(X,Y):=

\displaystyle\chi(Y+1)u_{p}^{0,-}(X,\frac{Y+1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})-\sqrt{\varepsilon}\chi^{\prime}(Y+1)\int_{0}^{X}v_{p}^{0,-}(s,\frac{Y+1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})\,ds

(2.4)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\chi(1-Y)u_{p}^{0,+}(X,\frac{1-Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\chi^{\prime}(1-Y)\int_{0}^{X}v_{p}^{0,-}(s,\frac{1-Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})\,ds,

\displaystyle v_{p}^{0}(X,Y):=

\displaystyle\chi(Y+1)v_{p}^{0,-}(X,\frac{Y+1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})+\chi(1-Y)v_{p}^{0,-}(X,\frac{1-Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}).

Plugging into the equation (
1.4
), we observe that the error introduced by the cut-off function is of order
\sqrt{\varepsilon}
, which contributes to the forcing term for the next Prandtl layer.

The
i
-th Euler corrector
(u_{e}^{i},v_{e}^{i})
satisfies the linearized Euler equation around the background Euler flow
(u_{e}^{0},v_{e}^{0})
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &u_{e}^{0}u^{i}_{eX}+u_{eX}^{0}u_{e}^{i}+v_{e}^{0}u^{i}_{eY}+u_{eY}^{0}v_{e}^{i}+p_{eX}^{i}=f_{e,1}^{i},\\
&u_{e}^{0}v^{i}_{eX}+v_{eX}^{0}u_{e}^{i}+v_{e}^{0}v^{i}_{eY}+v_{eY}^{0}v_{e}^{i}+p_{eY}^{i}=f_{e,2}^{i},\\
&u^{0}_{X}+v^{0}_{Y}=0,\\
&v_{e}^{i}|_{Y=\pm 1}=-u_{p}^{i-1}|_{Y=\pm 1},\quad v_{e}^{i}|_{X=-L,0}=V^{i}_{e,\{-L,0\}},\quad u_{e}^{i}|_{X=-L}=U_{e}^{i},\end{aligned}\right.

(2.5)

where the forcing terms
f_{e,1}^{i}
and
f_{e,2}^{i}
are errors produced from the previous correctors. Assuming smoothness for the boundary datum and the compatibility conditions at the corners, by the standard elliptic theory, there exists a solution
(u_{e}^{i},v_{e}^{i})
to (
2.5
) satisfying

\|\{u_{e}^{i},v_{e}^{i}\}\|_{H^{k}}\lesssim 1,\quad k\geq 0.

The
i
-th Prandtl corrector for the lower boundary
(u_{p}^{i,-},v_{p}^{i,-})
satisfies the linearized Prandtl equation around
(\bar{u},\bar{v}):=\Big(u_{p}^{0}+u_{e}^{0}(X,-1),v_{p}^{0}-v_{p}^{0}(X,-1)\Big)
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\bar{u}u^{i,-}_{px}+u^{i,-}_{p}\bar{u}_{x}+\bar{u}_{y}[v_{p}^{i,-}-v_{p}^{i,-}|_{y=0}]+\bar{v}u_{py}^{i,-}+p_{px}^{i,-}-u_{pyy}^{i,-}=f^{i},\\
&p_{py}^{i,-}=0,\\
&u_{px}^{i,-}+v_{py}^{i,-}=0,\\
&u_{p}^{i,-}|_{y=0}=-u_{e}^{i}|_{Y=-1},\quad u_{p}^{i,-}|_{y\to\infty}=v_{p}^{i,-}|_{y\to\infty}=0,\quad v_{p}^{i,-}|_{x=-L}=V^{i}_{p},\end{aligned}\right.

(2.6)

where
(x,y)
are the Prandtl variables (
2.2
), the forcing term
f^{i}
is errors produced from the previous corrector. Note that for the final layer, we will impose
v_{p}^{15,-}|_{y=0}=0
instead of
v_{p}^{15,-}|_{y\to\infty}=0
and perform a cut-off, whose error is negligible. The existence of solution to (

[
4
]

Theorem 2.2
.

Assume the prescribed boundary data
V_{p}^{i}(y)\in C^{\infty}
satisfies generic compatibility conditions at the corner
(-L,0)
, and the exponential decay of derivatives for some

\|\partial_{y}^{j}V_{p}^{i}e^{Ny}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{0}\quad\mbox{for}~j\geq 0.

Assume the forcing

satisfies the bounds

\|\partial_{x}^{k}\partial_{y}f^{i}e^{Ny}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{1}\quad\mbox{for}~k\geq 0.

there exists a solution

1.4

\|e^{Ny}\nabla^{\alpha}\{u_{p}^{i,-},v_{p}^{i,-}\}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C(C_{0},C_{1})\quad\mbox{for}~\alpha\geq 0.

After constructing

,

will be constructed similarly to (

). This completes the construction of

. Recall the notations

\phi_{a}=\phi_{e}+\phi_{p},\quad u_{a}=\phi_{aY},\quad\mbox{and}\quad v_{a}=-\phi_{aX}.

Through a straightforward computation, we obtain

(2.7)

2.2.
Construction of
\phi_{r}

We now turn to the construction of
\phi_{r}
. The objective is to construct a
\phi_{r}
with small scales in
X

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{3}}\|f\|_{2}.

∎

and

Stability phenomena for Kac-Moody Groups
Nitu Kitchloo

Abstract
. In this article we show that a canonical procedure of extending generalized Dynkin diagrams gives rise to families of Kac-Moody groups that satisfy homological stability. We also briefly sketch some emergent structure that appears on stabilization. Our results are illustrated for the family E n \mathrm{E}_{n} which is of interest in String theory. The techniques used involve homotopy decompositions of classifying spaces of Kac-Moody groups.



1.
Introduction

The theory of Kac-Moody Lie algebras and the underlying groups is well established at this point
[
K
,
KP
,
Ku
]
. The complex points of Kac-Moody groups form a natural extension of the class of semi-simple Lie groups, even though they need not be finite dimensional. Concepts like maximal torus, Weyl groups and root systems extend almost by definition to these groups. As topological groups, one may study Kac-Moody groups through honmotopical invariants like the classifying space and its cohomology
[
Ki
,
BrK
]
.

Recall that the compact Lie groups in the infinite families
\mathrm{A}_{n},\mathrm{B}_{n},\mathrm{C}_{n}
and
\mathrm{D}_{n}
are known to homologically stabilize to very interesting groups that admit emergent homotopical structure like Bott periodicity. It is therefore natural to ask about the structure of such families among Kac-Moody groups. In this article, we study such families and briefly discuss the emergent structure that appears on stabilization. We illustrate our results in the example of the family
\mathrm{E}_{n}
that begins with exceptional Lie groups, and is extended further along Kac-Moody groups. Groups in the
\mathrm{E}_{n}
family have been suggested as symmetries of various compactifications of 11-dimensional supergravity
[
BGH
,
DN
,
DHN
,
J
,
nCL
,
W
]
.

The results described in this article were motivated by a question asked by Ian Agol in private communication. We thank him for raising this interesting question.

Given a GCM
\mathrm{A}_{I}
, there is an associated simply-connected topological group
\mathrm{G}(\mathrm{A}_{I})
known as the minimal complex Kac-Moody group
[
Ku
]
. The group contains a unitary form
\mathrm{K}(\mathrm{A}_{I})
such that:

(i)

\mathrm{K}(\mathrm{A}_{I})
has rank
n
, i.e., there is a compact maximal torus of rank
n
, call it
\mathrm{T}
.

(ii)

\mathrm{K}(\mathrm{A}_{I})
is natural in
I
, i.e. an inclusion
J\subseteq I
functorially induces
\mathrm{K}(\mathrm{A}_{J})\subseteq\mathrm{K}(\mathrm{A}_{I})
.

If
\mathrm{A}_{I}
corresponds to the Dynkin diagram of a compact Lie group, then up to isomorphism,
\mathrm{K}(\mathrm{A}_{I})
recovers this group (these diagrams are called
finite type
).

Example 2.3
.

\mathrm{K}(\mathrm{A}_{n})=\mathrm{SU}(n+1)

The classifying space
\mathrm{BK}(\mathrm{A}_{I})
of the group
\mathrm{K}(\mathrm{A}_{I})
can be described in terms of the classifying spaces of all finite type diagrams in
\mathrm{A}_{I}
. We describe this result below.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{S}(\mathrm{A}_{I})=\{J\subseteq I\mid\mathrm{A}_{J}\text{ is of finite type}\}
be the poset known as the poset of spherical subsets for
\mathrm{A}_{I}
. Given
J\in\mathcal{S}(\mathrm{A}_{I})
, we define the following subgroup

\mathrm{H}_{J}(\mathrm{A}_{I})=\langle\mathrm{T},\mathrm{K}(\mathrm{A}_{J})\rangle\subseteq\mathrm{K}(\mathrm{A}_{I}).

Hence
\mathrm{H}_{J}(\mathrm{A}_{I})
is a compact subgroup of
\mathrm{K}(\mathrm{A}_{I})
of maximal rank, extending
\mathrm{K}(\mathrm{A}_{J})
by torus of rank
|I|-|J|
. We define
\mathrm{W}(\mathrm{A}_{J})
to be the Weyl group of
\mathrm{K}(\mathrm{A}_{J})
.

The following homotopy decomposition describes the structure of the classifying space
\mathrm{BK}(\mathrm{A}_{I})
and that of
\mathrm{BW}(\mathrm{A}_{I})
in terms of finite type diagrams

Theorem 2.5

(
[
Ki
]
,
[
Ki2
]
,
[
BrK
]
)
.

The following canonical maps are homotopy equivalences

\underset{J\in\mathcal{S}(\mathrm{A}_{I})}{\operatorname{hocolim}}\,\mathrm{BH}_{J}(\mathrm{A}_{I})\longrightarrow\mathrm{BK}(\mathrm{A}_{I}),\quad\quad\underset{J\in\mathcal{S}(\mathrm{A}_{I})}{\operatorname{hocolim}}\,\mathrm{BW}(\mathrm{A}_{J})\longrightarrow\mathrm{BW}(\mathrm{A}_{I}).

3.
Families

In order to illustrate the stability properties of families of Kac-Moody groups, let us consider the example of the generalized Cartan matrices
\mathrm{E}_{m}
represented by the following sequence of generalized Dynkin diagrams:

\mathrm{E}_{6}:
Exceptional finite type
\mathrm{E}_{7}:
Exceptional finite type
\mathrm{E}_{8}:
Exceptional finite type
\mathrm{E}_{9}:
Affine type
\vdots
\mathrm{E}_{9+n}:
\cdots
Highly-extended type

Definition 3.1
.

Using standard terminology, let us denote the Kac-Moody group
\mathrm{K}(\mathrm{E}_{n})
by just
\mathrm{E}_{n}
. Let
\mathrm{BE}_{n}
denote the classifying space of
\mathrm{E}_{n}
and define
\mathrm{BE}
as the topological space

\mathrm{BE}:=\displaystyle\operatorname*{hocolim}_{n}\,\mathrm{BE}_{n}.

Note: The reader may be tempted to conclude that
\mathrm{BE}
is equivalent to the classifying space of
\displaystyle\operatorname*{colim}\,\mathrm{E}_{n}
. However, since the groups
\mathrm{E}_{n}
are not locally compact for
n>8
, this colimit of topological groups need not have the structure of a topological group. We must therefore define the limiting topological group
\mathrm{E}
homotopically as
\mathrm{E}:=\Omega\mathrm{BE}
.

This leads us to the following natural question:

Question 3.2
.

Are the maps of classifying spaces
\mathrm{BE}_{n}\to\mathrm{BE}_{n+1}
increasingly connective? In other words, do the spaces
\mathrm{BE}_{n}
homologically stabilize to the space
\mathrm{BE}
?

In order to study question
3.2
, let us label the nodes in our Dynkin diagrams as follows:

(1)

\mathrm{E}_{9+n}:
\cdot
\cdot
\cdot
\cdot
\cdot
7
6
5
4
3
2
1
0
-1
-2
-n
8
\mathrm{E}_{9}
\mathrm{A}_{n}

The node labeled
0
is filled in to indicate the fact that it plays a pivotal role as we shall see below. Let us make the following observation about the above diagram:

Observation 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{n}
denote the category of spherical subsets for
\mathrm{E}_{9+n}
. Then
J
is an object of
\mathcal{S}_{n}
if and only if
J\cap I_{0}
is a spherical subset for
\mathrm{E}_{9}
, where
I_{0}
is the subset of indices
i\geq 0
(so that
\mathrm{A}_{I_{0}}=\mathrm{E}_{9})
. We observe that
\mathcal{S}_{n}
contains a
cofinal subcategory

\mathcal{E}_{n}
:

\mathcal{E}_{n}=\left\{J\in\mathcal{S}_{n}\mid J\cap I_{0}\text{ is spherical and }i\in J\text{ for all }i<0\right\}.

In light of theorem
2.5
, and the cofinality of
\mathcal{E}_{n}
, we conclude that the following map is a homotopy equivalence

\underset{J\in\mathcal{E}_{n}}{\operatorname{hocolim}}\,\mathrm{BH}_{J}(\mathrm{E}_{9+n})\longrightarrow\mathrm{BE}_{9+n}.

Observation
3.3
above allows us to conclude the following theorem (also see remark
3.6
)

Theorem 3.4
.

The spaces
\mathrm{BE}_{n}
homologically stabilize to
\mathrm{BE}
. In particular, given any degree
k
and a ring
\mathrm{R}
, the
\mathrm{R}
-modules
\mathrm{H}^{k}(\mathrm{E},\mathrm{R})
and
\mathrm{H}^{k}(\mathrm{BE},\mathrm{R})
are isomorphic to
\mathrm{H}^{k}(\mathrm{E}_{9+n},\mathrm{R})
and
\mathrm{H}^{k}(\mathrm{BE}_{9+n},\mathrm{R})
respectively, for sufficiently large
n
.

Proof.

Recall the homotopy decomposition described in Observation
3.3
above:

\underset{J\in\mathcal{E}_{n}}{\operatorname{hocolim}}\,\mathrm{BH}_{J}(\mathrm{E}_{9+n})\longrightarrow\mathrm{BE}_{9+n}.

Notice that the functor sending
J
to
J\cap I_{0}
defines and equivalence between
\mathcal{E}_{n}
and
\mathcal{S}_{0}
. It follows that
\mathcal{E}_{n}
is independent of
n
upto equivalence. Reindexing the above homotopy colimit over
\mathcal{S}_{0}
, we may therefore (objectwise) compare the above homotopy colimits as
n
increases. Now for an fixed object
J_{0}\in\mathcal{S}_{0}
, we obtain an infinite family (indexed by
n
) of
compact Lie groups

\mathrm{H}_{J_{0}}(\mathrm{E}_{n+9})
. Since the classifying spaces of all such families are known to homologically stabilize, we conclude from the above homotopy decomposition that the spaces
\mathrm{BE}_{9+n}
also homologically stabilize.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

We may use theorem
3.4
to compute
\mathrm{H}^{\ast}(\mathrm{BE})
as follows. The homotopy decomposition
\mathrm{BE}_{9+n}
given in Observation
3.3
gives rise to a spectral sequence

E_{2}^{i,j}={\varprojlim_{J\in\mathcal{E}_{n}}}^{\!i}\,\mathrm{H}^{j}(\mathrm{BH}_{J}(\mathrm{E}_{9+n}),\mathrm{R})\Rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{i+j}(\mathrm{BE}_{9+n},\mathrm{R}).

The groups
\mathrm{H}_{J}(\mathrm{E}_{9+n})
are easy to analyze for any
J\in\mathcal{E}_{n}
, and one sees that
\mathrm{H}^{\ast}(\mathrm{BH}_{J}(\mathrm{E}_{9+n}),\mathbb{Z})
has no
p
-torsion for
p>5
. This allows us to invoke Theorem 2.2 from
[
Ki3
]
(which bounds the length of nontrivial differentials), to show that for
p>5
, the above spectral sequence collapses at
E_{2}
with coefficients in any
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
-algebra
\mathrm{R}
. In addition, all additive extensions in
E_{2}^{i,j}=E^{i,j}_{\infty}
are trivial.

Remark 3.6
.

In the discussion above, we have illustrated our results for the family
\mathrm{E}_{n}
. However, the arguments apply much more generally. Assume we are given some other family
\mathrm{M}_{n}
of Kac-Moody groups with the property that for some fixed
n_{0}
, the Dynkin diagram of
\mathrm{M}_{n_{0}}
admits a distinguished node labeled
0
and so that the Dynkin diagram for
\mathrm{M}_{n_{0}+n}
is obtained by extending the node
0
by the Dynkin diagram of
\mathrm{A}_{n}
along nodes labeled by the negative integers as illustrated in the diagram (
1
) for
\mathrm{E}_{9+n}
shown above. Furthermore, assume that the family
\mathrm{M}_{n}
satisfies the property described in Observation
3.3
. In other words, we assume that a subset
J
is a spherical poset for
\mathrm{M}_{n_{0}+n}
if and only if
J\cap I_{0}
is a spherical poset for
\mathrm{M}_{n_{0}}
where
I_{0}
denotes the set of nodes in the Dynkin diagram for
\mathrm{M}_{n_{0}}
. Then the same argument given in the proof of theorem
3.4
shows that the groups
\mathrm{M}_{n}
homologically stabilize and that the analog of remark
3.5
also holds for this family for some choice of lower bound on the primes
p
.

Families of the form
\mathrm{M}_{n}
considered above are extremely easy to construct. For instance, we may start with the Dynkin diagram of any Kac-Moody group, and increasingly extend any chosen node linearly in one direction. It is easy to see that this procedure will either define an infinite family of compact Lie groups, or eventually define a diagram
\mathrm{M}_{n_{0}}
so that all subsequent extensions
\mathrm{M}_{n_{0}+n}
will satisfy the conditions described above. For instance, if one begins with the Dynkin diagram of
\mathrm{A}_{4}
and performs the above procedure starting with the second node, then one recovers the family
\mathrm{E}_{n}
as is evident from the diagram (
1
) for
\mathrm{E}_{9+n}
shown above.

Remark 3.7
.

The argument of theorem
3.4
applied to the homotopy decomposition of theorem
2.5
for the classifying space of the Weyl groups
\mathrm{W}(\mathrm{M}_{n})
of any family
\mathrm{M}_{n}
as in remark

\mathrm{BW}(\mathrm{M}_{n})

4.
The emergent structure

In this section, we briefly sketch some of the emmergent structure that arises when we stabilize the classifying spaces of the families considered above. As before, we will illustrate our results in our prototypical example of the family
\mathrm{E}_{n}
, though the constructions given below apply in full generality.

Given the Dynkin diagram of
\mathrm{E}_{n+m}
, let us construct a sub-Dynkin diagram by removing the
m
’th node from the right end of the Dynkin diagram for
\mathrm{E}_{n+m}
. The result is a disconnected diagram given by
\mathrm{E}_{n}\coprod\mathrm{A}_{m-1}
. The inclusion of the sub-Dynkin diagram defines the group homomorphisms
\mathrm{E}_{n}\times\mathrm{SU}(m)\longrightarrow\mathrm{E}_{n+m}
:

\mathrm{E}_{n}
\cdots
\times
\mathrm{SU}(m)
\cdots
\mathrm{E}_{n+m}
\cdots

Classifying the above gives rise to a map of spaces:

\mathrm{BE}_{n}\times\mathrm{BSU}(m)\longrightarrow\mathrm{BE}_{n+m}.

Now consider the following diagram with the horizontal maps being the ones constructed above and the vertical ones induced by the sequential inclusions of
\mathrm{BE}_{n}
:

\textstyle{\mathrm{BE}_{n}\times\mathrm{BSU}(m)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{BE}_{n+m}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{BE}_{n+1}\times\mathrm{BSU}(m)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{BE}_{n+m+1}.}

This diagram commutes up to canonical homotopy. This homotopy is given by conjugation with the element
\sigma_{m}\in\mathrm{SU}(m+1)\subset\mathrm{E}_{n+m+1}
that keeps
\mathrm{E}_{n}
fixed and shifts the bottom block diagonal copy of
\mathrm{SU}(m)\subset\mathrm{SU}(m+1)
to the top one. In the standard representation
\mathbb{C}^{m+1}
of
\mathrm{SU}(m+1)
the element
\sigma_{m}
is given by the following tansformation in terms of the standard basis
\{e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{m+1}\}
:

\sigma_{m}(e_{i})=e_{i-1},\quad i>1,\quad\sigma_{m}(e_{1})=(-1)^{m}e_{m+1}.

Let us identify
\sigma_{m_{1}}
with
\sigma_{m_{1}}\times 1_{m_{2}}
, and
\sigma_{m_{2}}
with
1_{m_{1}}\times\sigma_{m_{2}}
in terms of the blocks of
\mathrm{SU}(m_{1}+m_{2}+1)
. Then it is easy to verify that they satsify
\sigma_{m_{2}}\sigma_{m_{1}}=\sigma_{m_{1}+m_{2}}
.

The above compatible homotopies induced by the elements
\sigma_{m}
allow us to construct a well defined
A_{\infty}
-action of the topological monoid
\coprod_{m}\mathrm{BSU}(m)
on a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded homotopy colimit of the spaces
\mathrm{BE}_{n}

(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathrm{BE})\times(\coprod_{m}\times\mathrm{BSU}(m))\longrightarrow(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathrm{BE}),

with the action of
\mathrm{BSU}(m)
shifting each component of
\mathrm{BE}
by
m
.
On group completing
[
MS
]
, we obtain a right action of
\mathbb{Z}\times\mathrm{BSU}
on
\mathbb{Z}\times\mathrm{BE}
:

(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathrm{BE})\times(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathrm{BSU})\longrightarrow(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathrm{BE}).

Restricting the above action to the zero component, and taking homotopy orbits we obtain a principal
\mathrm{BSU}
-fibration:

\mathrm{BSU}\longrightarrow\mathrm{BE}\longrightarrow{\mathrm{BE}}_{h\mathrm{BSU}}

which is classfied by the canonical map
{\mathrm{BE}}_{h\mathrm{BSU}}\longrightarrow\mathrm{BBSU}
. Taking loop spaces, we get an extension of topological groups (up to homotopy):

\mathrm{SU}\longrightarrow\mathrm{E}\longrightarrow\mathrm{E}/\mathrm{SU}:=\Omega({\mathrm{BE}}_{h\mathrm{BSU}}).

Observation 4.1
.

The above emergent structure can be interpreted as saying that
\mathrm{E}
-bundles admit a principal action by stable special unitary bundles and that the homotopy orbits under this action is given by principal
\mathrm{E}/\mathrm{SU}
-bundles. It would be very interesting to have a geometric understanding of this observation.

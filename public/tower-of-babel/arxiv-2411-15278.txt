Unitarity effects in high-energy elastic scattering
M. Maneyro, E. G. S. Luna, M. Peláez

Abstract
We investigate the high-energy behavior of the elastic scattering amplitude using the eikonal and U U -matrix unitarization schemes. This work extends the analysis in mlp001 by exploring the sensitivity of the Pomeron and Odderon parameters to the inclusion of differential cross-section data over an extended range of | t | |t| .



I
Introduction

In a recent study
mlp001
, we studied the high-energy behavior of the elastic scattering amplitude using two distinct unitarization schemes: the eikonal and the
U
-matrix. In that analysis, which examined the properties of both the Pomeron and Odderon, we found that the Odderon phase factor
\xi_{\mathbb{O}}=-1
is favored in both schemes, indicating an Odderon with a phase opposite to other odd-signature components of the scattering amplitude. This result was obtained after a global analysis of two data ensembles (one comprising data from the ATLAS Collaboration and the other from the TOTEM Collaboration) covering total cross-sections, the
\rho
parameter, and differential cross-section data for
pp
scattering within the range
|t|\leq 0.1
GeV
2
. This work uses the same formalism and statistical methods as in
mlp001
but extends the analysis to ensembles that include
d\sigma/dt
data over the broader range
|t|\leq 0.2
GeV
2
. Our goal is to assess the sensitivity of the Pomeron and Odderon parameters to the inclusion of
d\sigma/dt
data with this wider momentum interval.

II
Formalism

The construction of unitarized scattering amplitudes involves some formal steps. First, we define a Born-level amplitude,
{\cal F}(s,t)
, with its even- and odd-crossing components expressed as

\displaystyle{\cal F}^{\pm}(s,t)=\frac{1}{2}\left[{\cal F}^{pp}(s,t)\pm{\cal F}^{\bar{p}p}(s,t)\right].

(1)

The corresponding Born amplitudes for even- and odd-crossing in impact parameter space,
b
, are given by

\displaystyle\chi^{\pm}(s,b)=\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{\infty}q\,dq\,J_{0}(bq){\cal F}^{\pm}(s,-q^{2}),

(2)

where
-q^{2}=t
. Next, we construct a new scattering amplitude,
H^{pp}_{\bar{p}p}(s,b)
, in terms of the functions
\chi^{pp}_{\bar{p}p}(s,b)
. Finally, the amplitude in momentum space, which is used for computing observables, is obtained through the inverse Fourier-Bessel transform of
H^{pp}_{\bar{p}p}(s,b)
:

\displaystyle{\cal A}^{pp}_{\bar{p}p}(s,t)=s\int_{0}^{\infty}b\,db\,J_{0}(bq)\,H^{pp}_{\bar{p}p}(s,b).

(3)

In the eikonal scheme (Es), the relation

\displaystyle H(s,b)=i\left[1-e^{i\chi(s,b)}\right]

(4)

holds, leading to

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{[Es]}(s,t)=is\int^{\infty}_{0}b\,db\,J_{0}(bq)\left[1-e^{i\chi(s,b)}\right].

(5)

In contrast, in the
U
-matrix scheme (Us), the relation

\displaystyle H(s,b)=\frac{\chi(s,b)}{1-i\chi(s,b)/2}

(6)

implies

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{[Us]}(s,t)=is\int^{\infty}_{0}b\,db\,J_{0}(bq)\left[\frac{2\chi(s,b)}{\chi(s,b)+2i}\right].

(7)

The input amplitudes
{\cal F}_{i}(s,t)
are associated with Reggeon exchange amplitudes. The Born amplitude for each single exchange is

\displaystyle{\cal F}_{i}(s,t)=\beta_{i}^{2}(t)\eta_{i}(t)\left(\frac{s}{s_{0}}\right)^{\alpha_{i}(t)},

(8)

i=-,+,\mathbb{O},\mathbb{P}
, where
\beta_{i}(t)
is the elastic proton-Reggeon vertex,
\eta_{i}(t)
is the signature factor,
\alpha_{i}(t)
is the Regge trajectory, and
s_{0}\equiv 1
GeV
2
is an energy scale. Here
{\cal F}_{-}(s,t)
represents the exchange of Reggeons having
C=-1
parity (
\omega
and
\rho
),
{\cal F}_{+}(s,t)
the exchange of
Reggeons having
C=+1
parity (
a_{2}
and
f_{2}
),
{\cal F}_{\mathbb{O}}(s,t)
represents the
C=-1
Odderon exchange, while
{\cal F}_{\mathbb{P}}(s,t)
represents the
C=+1
Pomeron exchange.

For Regge trajectories with odd-signature, the signature factor is given by
\eta_{i}(t)=-ie^{-i\frac{\pi}{2}\alpha_{i}(t)}
, while for even-signature trajectories, it is
\eta_{i}(t)=-e^{-i\frac{\pi}{2}\alpha_{i}(t)}
. Reggeons with positive charge-conjugation are assumed to have an exponential form for the proton-Reggeon vertex,

\displaystyle\beta_{+}(t)=\beta_{+}(0)\exp(r_{+}t/2),

(9)

and lie on a linear trajectory of the form

\displaystyle\alpha_{+}(t)=1-\eta_{+}+\alpha^{\prime}_{+}t.

(10)

Similarly, Reggeons with negative charge-conjugation are characterized by the parameters

\beta_{-}(0)
,
r_{-}
,
\eta_{-}
, and
\alpha^{\prime}_{-}
.

The input amplitude for the Odderon contribution is expressed as

\displaystyle{\cal F}_{\mathbb{O}}(s,t)=\beta_{\mathbb{O}}^{2}(t)\,\eta_{\mathbb{O}}(t)\left(\frac{s}{s_{0}}\right)^{\alpha_{\mathbb{O}}(t)},

(11)

where
\eta_{\mathbb{O}}(t)=-ie^{-i\frac{\pi}{2}\alpha_{\mathbb{O}}(t)}
and
\alpha_{\mathbb{O}}(t)=1
. We adopt a power-like form for the proton-Odderon vertex, given by

\displaystyle\beta_{\mathbb{O}}(t)=\frac{\beta_{\mathbb{O}}(0)}{(1-t/m_{\rho}^{2})(1-t/a_{\mathbb{O}})},

(12)

where
a_{\mathbb{O}}=2a_{\mathbb{P}}
.

In the case of Pomeron exchange, the trajectory is given by

,

\displaystyle\alpha_{\mathbb{P}}(t)=\alpha_{\mathbb{P}}(0)+\alpha^{\prime}_{\mathbb{P}}t+\frac{m_{\pi}^{2}}{32\pi^{3}}\,h(\tau),

(13)

where
\eta_{\mathbb{P}}(t)=-e^{-i\frac{\pi}{2}\alpha_{\mathbb{P}}(t)}
,
\alpha_{\mathbb{P}}(0)=1+\epsilon
, and

\displaystyle h(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{4}{\tau}\,F_{\pi}^{2}(t)\left[2\tau-(1+\tau)^{3/2}\ln\left(\frac{\sqrt{1+\tau}+1}{\sqrt{1+\tau}-1}\right)\right.

(14)

\beta_{\pi}

\beta_{\pi}/\beta_{I\!\!P}(0)=2/3

\displaystyle\beta_{\mathbb{P}}(t)=\frac{\beta_{\mathbb{P}}(0)}{(1-t/a_{1})(1-t/a_{\mathbb{P}})},

F_{\pi}(t)
.

The corresponding amplitudes
\chi_{i}(s,b)
are obtained by applying a Fourier-Bessel transform applied to each
{\cal F}_{i}(s,t)
:

\displaystyle\chi_{i}(s,b)=\frac{1}{s}\int\frac{d^{2}q}{2\pi}\,e^{i{\bf q}\cdot{\bf b}}\,{\cal F}_{i}(s,t).

(16)

The physical amplitudes in
b
-space are obtained by summing the Fourier-Bessel transforms of all possible exchanges, expressed as

\displaystyle\chi^{pp}_{\bar{p}p}(s,b)=\chi_{\mathbb{P}}(s,b)+\chi_{+}(s,b)\pm\chi_{-}(s,b)\pm\xi_{\mathbb{O}}\chi_{\mathbb{O}}(s,b),

(17)

where
\chi_{\mathbb{O}}(s,b)
represents the Odderon’s phase factor, associated with the positivity property. We consider an Odderon with a phase factor
\xi_{\mathbb{O}}=-1

mlp001
.

The total cross section, elastic differential cross section, and the
\rho
parameter are expressed in terms of the physical
amplitude
{\cal A}^{pp}_{\bar{p}p}(s,t)
,

\displaystyle\sigma^{pp,\bar{p}p}_{tot}(s)=\frac{4\pi}{s}\,\textnormal{Im}\,{\cal A}^{pp}_{\bar{p}p}(s,t=0),

(18)

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma^{pp,\bar{p}p}}{dt}(s,t)=\frac{\pi}{s^{2}}\,\left|{\cal A}^{pp}_{\bar{p}p}(s,t)\right|^{2},

(19)

\displaystyle\rho^{pp,\bar{p}p}(s)=\frac{\textnormal{Re}\,{\cal A}^{pp}_{\bar{p}p}(s,t=0)}{\textnormal{Im}\,{\cal A}^{pp}_{\bar{p}p}(s,t=0)}.

(20)

In the eikonal scheme, we replace
{\cal A}^{pp}_{\bar{p}p}(s,t)
with
{\cal A}^{pp,\bar{p}p}_{[Es]}(s,t)
, such that

\displaystyle{\cal A}^{pp,\bar{p}p}_{[Es]}(s,t)=is\int^{\infty}_{0}b\,db\,J_{0}(bq)\left[1-e^{i\chi^{pp}_{\bar{p}p}(s,b)}\right].

(21)

In the
U
-matrix scheme, we similarly replace
{\cal A}^{pp}_{\bar{p}p}(s,t)
with
{\cal A}^{pp,\bar{p}p}_{[Us]}(s,t)
, leading to

\displaystyle{\cal A}^{pp,\bar{p}p}_{[Us]}(s,t)=is\int^{\infty}_{0}b\,db\,J_{0}(bq)\left[\frac{2\chi^{pp}_{\bar{p}p}(s,b)}{\chi^{pp}_{\bar{p}p}(s,b)+2i}\right].

(22)

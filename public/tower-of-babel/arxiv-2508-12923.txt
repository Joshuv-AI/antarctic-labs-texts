Polarized electromagnetic radiation by chiral media with time-dependent chiral chemical potential
Kirill Tuchin

Abstract
We investigate photon production from media characterized by a time-dependent chiral chemical potential μ 5 ​ ( t ) \mu_{5}(t) . We first consider the chiral anomaly as a small perturbation and show that μ 5 → 2 ​ γ \mu_{5}\to 2\gamma process generates non-polarized radiation, as the probabilities of producing right and left-handed photons are equal. In chiral semimetal with a vanishing chiral chemical potential but a finite separation of Weyl nodes, 𝚫 ⁡ ( t ) {\bm{\Delta}}(t) , the photon production vanishes at the leading order of perturbation theory. We then employ the method of Bogolyubov transformation to obtain the photon spectrum that sums up all powers of μ 5 \mu_{5} . Employing the exactly solvable model μ 5 ​ ( t ) ∝ A + B ​ tanh ⁡ ( t / τ ) \mu_{5}(t)\propto A+B\tanh(t/\tau) , we demonstrate that the spectrum exhibits strong circular polarization, whose direction is determined by the sign of μ 5 \mu_{5} . The spectrum contains resonances associated with the instability of the electromagnetic field in chiral media. We discuss potential phenomenological applications of these findings.

Introduction

The chiral anomaly of QED

Bell and Jackiw 1969

F\tilde{F}

F
is the electromagnetic field tensor and
\tilde{F}

The chiral magnetic effect refers to the generation of electric current in response to the magnetic field:
{\bm{j}}=c_{A}\mu_{5}{\bm{B}}

\mu_{5}
is the chiral chemical potential, the transport coefficient
b_{0}=c_{A}\mu_{5}

c_{A}
is the anomaly coefficient. The anomalous Hall current emerges as a response to the external electric field
{\bm{j}}={\bm{b}}\times{\bm{E}}

{\bm{b}}=(\alpha/\pi){\bm{\Delta}}
, where
{\bm{\Delta}}
represents the displacement of the Weyl nodes in the momentum space
{\bm{\Delta}}

A typical Weyl semimetal posseses multiple pairs of nodes, in which case

represents the total displacement
Zubkov 2024

Quite similar to the neutral pion’s two-photon decay, the time-dependent

\mu_{5}

\mu_{5}\to 2\gamma

We use the method of the Bogolyubov transformation, which entails the canonical quantization of the electromagnetic field at different equal-time hypersurfaces. Since time-dependence of the normal modes is generally non-harmonic, the Fock spaces, including their vacuum states, vary across different hypersurfaces. As a result, the vacuum state at the remote future

II
Perturbation theory

A convenient systematic way for incorporating anomalous currents into Maxwell’s theory is by means of the Lagrangian
Wilczek 1987
;
Carroll et al. 1990
;
Sikivie 1984
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{4}\left(F^{2}+\theta F\tilde{F}\right)\,,

(1)

where
F
is the electromagnetic field tensor,
\theta
is an external pseudo-scalar field such that
\partial_{\mu}\theta=b_{\mu}=(b_{0},-{\bm{b}})
. We assume that
b_{\mu}
is a function of time, but not of spatial coordinates. All medium effects other than the anomalous chiral magnetic and Hall currents are omitted for simplicity. Using the identity
F\tilde{F}=2\partial_{\mu}(\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}A_{\nu}\partial_{\lambda}A_{\rho})
and integrating the second term by parts one can cast (
1
) in the form:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{4}F^{2}+\mathcal{L}_{\text{int}}=-\frac{1}{4}F^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}A_{\nu}\partial_{\lambda}A_{\rho}\partial_{\mu}\theta\,.

(2)

The essence of the perturbation theory lies in treating the second term in (
2
) as a small perturbation of the first term, describing the free electromagnetic field. The canonical quantization of the field is accomplished by expanding it in the complete set of normal modes of the free Lagrangian:

\displaystyle{\bm{A}}(t,{\bm{x}})=\int\frac{d^{3}kV}{(2\pi)^{3}}\sum_{\lambda}\left(a_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}{\bm{A}}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}(t,{\bm{x}})+a^{\dagger}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}{\bm{A}}^{*}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}(t,{\bm{x}})\right)\,,

(3)

where
{\bm{k}}
and
\lambda
is photon’s momentum and helicity respectively, and
V
is the volume. The normal modes of given momentum are plane waves. In the radiation gauge
A^{0}=0
,
{\bm{\nabla}}\cdot{\bm{A}}=0
they are:

\displaystyle{\bm{A}}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}(t,{\bm{x}})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\omega V}}{\bm{\epsilon}}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}e^{i{\bm{k}}\cdot{\bm{x}}-i\omega t}\,,

(4)

where
{\bm{\epsilon}}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}
is the polarization vector, and
\omega=k
. The coefficients of the expansion (
3
) are the creation and annihilation operators subject to the usual commutation relations. There still remains a residual gauge freedom, which manifests in the ability to choose any pair of polarization vectors that meet the conditions
{\bm{\epsilon}}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}\cdot{\bm{k}}=0
and
|{\bm{\epsilon}}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}|=1
. Note, that in this approach, the dispersion relation is independent of the photon polarization.

The production of two photons with four momenta
k=(\omega,{\bm{k}})
and
k^{\prime}=(\omega^{\prime},{\bm{k}}^{\prime})
and polarizations
\epsilon=(0,{\bm{\epsilon}}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda})
and
\epsilon^{\prime}=(0,{\bm{\epsilon}}_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}})
respectively is represented by the matrix element

\displaystyle S(b\to 2\gamma)

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{2}\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}\frac{\epsilon_{\nu}}{\sqrt{2\omega V}}(k^{\prime}-k)_{\lambda}\frac{\epsilon_{\rho}^{\prime}}{\sqrt{2\omega^{\prime}V}}(2\pi)^{3}\delta({\bm{k}}+{\bm{k}}^{\prime})\tilde{b}_{\mu}(\omega+\omega^{\prime})

(5)

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\omega V}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\omega^{\prime}V}}(2\pi)^{3}\delta({\bm{k}}+{\bm{k}}^{\prime})\left[\tilde{b}_{0}(\omega+\omega^{\prime})({\bm{k}}-{\bm{k}}^{\prime})+\tilde{{\bm{b}}}(\omega+\omega^{\prime})(\omega-\omega^{\prime})\right]\cdot\left({\bm{\epsilon}}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}\times{\bm{\epsilon}}_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}\right)\,,

(6)

where

\displaystyle\tilde{b}_{\mu}(\omega+\omega^{\prime})=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{-i(\omega+\omega^{\prime})t}b_{\mu}(t)dt\,.

(7)

Since
{\bm{k}}=-{\bm{k}}^{\prime}
,
\omega=\omega^{\prime}
, the term proportional to
{\bm{b}}
vanishes. Using the circular polarization basis in which
{\bm{\epsilon}}_{{\bm{k}},\pm}^{*}={\bm{\epsilon}}_{{\bm{k}},\mp}={\bm{\epsilon}}_{-{\bm{k}},\pm}
we compute

\displaystyle{\bm{\epsilon}}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}\times{\bm{\epsilon}}_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}=-\frac{i}{2}\hat{{\bm{k}}}(\lambda^{\prime}+\lambda)=-i\hat{{\bm{k}}}\delta_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}\,.

(8)

The decay probability into photons of helicities
\lambda
and
\lambda^{\prime}
is:

\displaystyle dw(b\to 2\gamma)

\displaystyle=|S(b\to 2\gamma)|^{2}\frac{d^{3}kV}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{d^{3}k^{\prime}V}{(2\pi)^{3}}=\frac{1}{4}|\tilde{b}_{0}|^{2}\delta_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}\frac{d^{3}kV}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,.

(9)

The decay probabilities into two positive-helicity photons
\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}=1
or two negative helicity photons
\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}=-1
are equal. In either case, one photon is right-hand polarized and another one is left-hand polarized because their momenta are back-to-back. Consequently, the average photon polarization vanishes. The total number of right or left-handed photons per phase space reads:

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}}{Vd^{3}k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}|\tilde{b}_{0}|^{2}\frac{d^{3}kV}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,.

(10)

For future reference and as an illustration consider the following time-evolution:

\displaystyle b_{0}(t)=A+B\tanh\frac{t}{\tau}\,.

(11)

This model has recently been used to investigate the chiral Cherenkov radiation in time-dependent
\mu_{5}

Tuchin 2025
. The corresponding Fourier transform (
7
) of (
11
) can be expressed as:

\displaystyle\tilde{b}_{0}(\omega+\omega^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\left\{\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{-i(\omega+\omega^{\prime})t+\epsilon t}b_{0}(t)dt+\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-i(\omega+\omega^{\prime})t-\epsilon t}b_{0}(t)dt\right\}=-\frac{i\pi\tau B}{\sinh(\pi\tau\omega)}\,.

(12)

Therefore,

\displaystyle d\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\frac{(\pi\tau B)^{2}}{\sinh^{2}(\pi\tau\omega)}\frac{d^{3}kV}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,.

(13)

In the subsequent sections, we adopt an alternative approach that allows the summation of higher order powers of
b_{0}
. It will lead us to a conclusion that the production probability of the two circular polarizations is in fact not equal, implying that the radiation exhibits polarization.

III
Bogolyubov transformation at finite
\mu_{5}(t)

The complete set of normal modes (
4
) used by the perturbative method corresponds to the free waves described by the first term in (
2
). To obtain the exact solution, we seek the complete set of normal modes associated with
both
terms in (
2
). We consider an isotropic chiral medium with
{\bm{\Delta}}=0
and neglect all electromagnetic responses other than the chiral magnetic effect for the sake of simplicity. The electromagnetic field is described by the equations derived from (
2
):

\displaystyle{\bm{\nabla}}\cdot{\bm{E}}=0\,,

(14)

\displaystyle{\bm{\nabla}}\times{\bm{B}}-\partial_{t}{\bm{E}}=b_{0}{\bm{B}}\,,

(15)

\displaystyle{\bm{\nabla}}\times{\bm{E}}+\partial_{t}{\bm{B}}=0\,,

(16)

\displaystyle{\bm{\nabla}}\cdot{\bm{B}}=0\,.

(17)

In the radiation gauge the vector potential satisfies the equation

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}{\bm{A}}-\partial_{t}^{2}{\bm{A}}+b_{0}(t){\bm{\nabla}}\times{\bm{A}}=0\,.

(18)

We are interested in the normal modes with given momentum
{\bm{k}}
and polarization vector
{\bm{\epsilon}}_{\lambda}
. Owing to the identity
i\hat{{\bm{k}}}\times{\bm{\epsilon}}_{\lambda}=\lambda{\bm{\epsilon}}_{\lambda}
, the two independent polarizations are the circular right and left-handed ones corresponding to helicities
\lambda=\pm 1
. Therefore, the general form of the normal modes is

\displaystyle{\bm{A}}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}(t,{\bm{x}})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{V}}a(t){\bm{\epsilon}}_{\lambda}e^{i{\bm{k}}\cdot{\bm{x}}}\,,

(19)

where the amplitude
a(t)
is governed by the equation

\displaystyle\ddot{a}(t)+\Omega^{2}(t)a(t)=0\,,

(20)

with

\displaystyle\Omega^{2}(t)=k^{2}-\lambda b_{0}(t)k\,.

(21)

The photon wave function will be normalized to one photon per unit volume as usual.

It is important to note that the choice of the radiation gauge has completely exhausted the gauge freedom. This is because photons must be circularly polarized due to the presence of the curl term in (
18
). In contrast, polarization of photons in the perturbation theory is not completely fixed by the radiation gauge, allowing us to choose any two independent photon polarizations in the plane perpendicular to
{\bm{k}}
.

Suppose that at distant past and future the function
b_{0}(t)
is constant:
b_{0}(-\infty)=b_{0}^{\text{in}}
,
b_{0}(\infty)=b_{0}^{\text{out}}
. Then, it follows from (
20
) that the positive frequency normal modes at
t\to-\infty
have the following asymptotic behavior in the distant past:

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to-\infty}{\bm{A}}^{\text{in}}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}(t,{\bm{x}})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\omega^{\text{in}}V}}{\bm{\epsilon}}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}e^{-i\omega^{\text{in}}t+i{\bm{k}}\cdot{\bm{x}}}\,,

(22)

where

\displaystyle\omega^{\text{in}}=\sqrt{k^{2}-\lambda b_{0}^{\text{in}}k}\,.

(23)

The operator of the electromagnetic field can be expanded in these ‘in’-modes:

\displaystyle{\bm{A}}(t,{\bm{x}})=\int\frac{d^{3}kV}{(2\pi)^{3}}\sum_{\lambda}\left(a^{\text{in}}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}{\bm{A}}^{\text{in}}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}(t,{\bm{x}})+a^{\text{in}\dagger}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}{\bm{A}}^{\text{in}*}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}(t,{\bm{x}})\right)\,.

(24)

In the vacuum state, defined as
a^{\text{in}}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}|0\rangle^{\text{in}}=0
, the expectation value of the particle number operator
N_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}=a^{\text{in}\dagger}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}a^{\text{in}}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}
vanishes:

{}^{\text{in}}\langle 0|N_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}|0\rangle^{\text{in}}=0\,.

(25)

In the remote future, the positive frequency normal modes are different, and have the following asymptotic behavior:

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}{\bm{A}}^{\text{out}}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}(t,{\bm{x}})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\omega^{\text{out}}V}}{\bm{\epsilon}}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}e^{-i\omega^{\text{out}}t+i{\bm{k}}\cdot{\bm{x}}}\,,

(26)

where

\displaystyle\omega^{\text{out}}=\sqrt{k^{2}-\lambda b_{0}^{\text{out}}k}\,.

(27)

The expansion of the operator of electromagnetic field in terms of ‘out’-modes reads:

\displaystyle{\bm{A}}(t,{\bm{x}})=\int\frac{d^{3}kV}{(2\pi)^{3}}\sum_{\lambda}\left(a^{\text{out}}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}{\bm{A}}^{\text{out}}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}(t,{\bm{x}})+a^{\text{out}\dagger}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}{\bm{A}}^{\text{out}*}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}(t,{\bm{x}})\right)\,.

(28)

The corresponding vacuum state is defined as
a^{\text{out}}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}|0\rangle^{\text{out}}=0
.

The normal modes are normalized such that their scalar products are:

\displaystyle\left({\bm{A}}^{\text{in}}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda},{\bm{A}}^{\text{in}}_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}\right)=i\int d^{3}x{\bm{A}}^{\text{in}*}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftrightarrow}}{{\partial_{t}}}\!{\bm{A}}^{\text{in}}_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}=\frac{(2\pi)^{3}}{V}\delta_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}\delta({\bm{k}}-{\bm{k}}^{\prime})\,,

(29)

\displaystyle\left({\bm{A}}^{\text{in}}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda},{\bm{A}}^{*\text{in}}_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}\right)=0\,,

(30)

\displaystyle\left({\bm{A}}^{\text{in}*}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda},{\bm{A}}^{\text{in}*}_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}\right)=-\frac{(2\pi)^{3}}{V}\delta_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}\delta({\bm{k}}-{\bm{k}}^{\prime})\,,

(31)

The corresponding commutation relations of the creation and annihilation operators of the ‘in’-modes are:

\displaystyle\left[a^{\text{in}}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda},a^{\text{in}\dagger}_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}\right]=\frac{(2\pi)^{3}}{V}\delta({\bm{k}}-{\bm{k}}^{\prime})\delta_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}\,,

(32)

\displaystyle\left[a^{\text{in}\dagger}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda},a^{\text{in}\dagger}_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}\right]=\left[a^{\text{in}}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda},a^{\text{in}}_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}\right]=0\,,

(33)

and, similarly, for the ‘out’-modes.
The relationship between the two sets of modes is given by the Bogolyubov transformation:

\displaystyle{\bm{A}}^{\text{out}}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}}\int\frac{d^{3}k^{\prime}V}{(2\pi)^{3}}\left(\alpha_{{\bm{k}},\lambda;{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}{\bm{A}}^{\text{in}}_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}+\beta_{{\bm{k}},\lambda;{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}{\bm{A}}^{\text{in}*}_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}\right)\,

(34)

\displaystyle{\bm{A}}^{\text{in}}_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda}\int\frac{d^{3}kV}{(2\pi)^{3}}\left(\alpha^{*}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda;{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}{\bm{A}}^{\text{out}}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}-\beta_{{\bm{k}},\lambda;{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}{\bm{A}}^{\text{out}*}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}\right)\,.

(35)

The Bogolyubov coefficients can be computed as follows:

\displaystyle\alpha_{{\bm{k}},\lambda;{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}=\left({\bm{A}}^{\text{out}}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda},{\bm{A}}^{\text{in}}_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}\right)\,,

(36)

\displaystyle\beta_{{\bm{k}},\lambda;{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}=-\left({\bm{A}}^{\text{out}}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda},{\bm{A}}^{\text{in}*}_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}\right)\,.

(37)

Using the relations (
24
),(
28
)–(
35
) one can express the creation and annihilation operators as

\displaystyle a^{\text{in}}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}}\int\frac{d^{3}k^{\prime}V}{(2\pi)^{3}}\left(\alpha_{{\bm{k}},\lambda;{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}a^{\text{out}}_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}+\beta_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime};{\bm{k}},\lambda}^{*}a^{\text{out}\dagger}_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}\right)\,,

(38)

\displaystyle a^{\text{out}}_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda}\int\frac{d^{3}kV}{(2\pi)^{3}}\left(\alpha_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime};{\bm{k}},\lambda}a^{\text{in}}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}-\beta_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime};{\bm{k}},\lambda}^{*}a^{\text{in}\dagger}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}\right)\,.

(39)

It follows that the future vacuum contains photons:

{}^{\text{out}}\langle 0|N_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}|0\rangle^{\text{out}}={{}^{\text{out}}}\langle 0|a^{\text{in}\dagger}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}a^{\text{in}}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}|0\rangle^{\text{out}}=\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}}\int\frac{d^{3}k^{\prime}V}{(2\pi)^{3}}\left|\beta_{{\bm{k}},\lambda;{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}\right|^{2}\,.

(40)

This general result shows that if
b_{0}
changes in time, the number (
40
) of photons are produced.

IV
Photon spectrum for

The exact solutions of (
20

and
b_{0}^{\text{out}}=A+B
respectively. The transition time between them is controlled by the parameter

. Substituting (

\displaystyle\Omega^{2}(t)=k^{2}-\lambda kA-\lambda kB\tanh\frac{t}{\tau}\,.

The exact solution of (

(42)

\displaystyle{\bm{A}}^{\text{out}}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}(t,{\bm{x}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\omega^{\text{out}}V}}{\bm{\epsilon}}_{\lambda}e^{i{\bm{k}}\cdot{\bm{x}}}e^{-i\omega^{+}t-i\omega^{-}\tau\ln\left[2\cosh\frac{t}{\tau}\right]}{{}_{2}}F_{1}\left(1+i\omega^{-}\tau,i\omega^{-}\tau;1+i\omega^{\text{out}}\tau;\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\tanh\frac{t}{\tau}\right)\right)\,,

where

and

The relationship between the two solutions (

\displaystyle{\bm{A}}^{\text{in}}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}(t,{\bm{x}})

\displaystyle=\alpha_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}{\bm{A}}^{\text{out}}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}(t,{\bm{x}})+\beta_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}{\bm{A}}^{\text{out}*}_{-{\bm{k}},\lambda}(t,{\bm{x}})\,,

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\omega^{\text{out}}}{\omega^{\text{in}}}\right)^{1/2}\frac{\Gamma\left(1-i\omega^{\text{in}}\tau\right)\Gamma\left(-i\omega^{\text{out}}\tau\right)}{\Gamma\left(-i\omega^{+}\tau\right)\Gamma\left(1-i\omega^{+}\tau\right)}\,,

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}}{Vd^{3}k}=\frac{\left|\beta_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}\right|^{2}}{(2\pi)^{3}}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{\omega^{\text{out}}}{\omega^{\text{in}}}\left|\frac{\Gamma\left(1-i\omega^{\text{in}}\tau\right)\Gamma\left(i\omega^{\text{out}}\tau\right)}{\Gamma\left(i\omega^{-}\tau\right)\Gamma\left(1+i\omega^{-}\tau\right)}\right|^{2}\,.

and again, only for photons with polarization
\lambda=\sgn b_{0}^{\text{out}}
. While the pole at

corresponding to

always exists, those with positive

In practice, photon states have finite width

to

and

or

It is instructive to investigate the small chiral chemical potential limit of (
50
) and compare it to the perturbative calculations in Sec.

. At
|b_{0}|\ll k
, we can expand (

) to obtain

\displaystyle\beta_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}\approx\frac{1}{4}\left(b_{0}^{\text{in}}-b_{0}^{\text{out}}\right)\frac{\pi\tau\lambda}{\sinh(\pi\tau k)}\left\{1+\frac{\lambda}{2k}f(k\tau)\right\}+\mathcal{O}(b_{0}^{3})\,.

(53)

We defined an auxiliary function:

\displaystyle f(k\tau)=b_{0}^{\text{out}}+ib_{0}^{\text{out}}k\tau\left[\psi(1-ik\tau)-\gamma\right]+ib_{0}^{\text{in}}k\tau\left[\gamma-\psi(ik\tau)\right]\,,

(54)

where
\gamma
is Euler’s constant, and
\psi
is the digamma function. Substituting (

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}}{Vd^{3}k}\approx\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{16}\left(b_{0}^{\text{in}}-b_{0}^{\text{out}}\right)^{2}\frac{(\pi\tau\lambda)^{2}}{\sinh^{2}(\pi\tau k)}\left\{1+\frac{\lambda}{k}\real f(k\tau)\right\}\,.

(55)

\lambda

b_{0}\tau\gtrsim 1

V
Summary and discussion

We computed the electromagnetic radiation emitted by a chiral medium with the time-dependent chiral chemical potential
\mu_{5}(t)
given by (
11
) with
b_{0}=c_{A}\mu_{5}
. The spectrum, given by (
50
), explicitly depends on the photon polarization. If the chiral chemical potential does not change sign during the time-evolution, the radiation has nearly perfect right-hand polarization when
\mu_{5}
is positive, and left-hand polarization when
\mu_{5}
is negative. The radiation spectrum exhibits a resonance structure, reflecting the instability of the electromagnetic field in the chiral medium at finite
\mu_{5}
.

Clearly, the instability plays an important role in inducing the polarization of the radiation. However, it is unclear whether the instability is the only source of polarization. Indeed, the perturbative formula (
55
) has no divergencies yet predicts the overall polarization of the radiation at the next-to-leading order.

To assess the phenomenological relevance of polarized radiation by chiral systems, we performed numerical calculations for two distinct systems: the quark-gluon plasma and a typical chiral material. In the former system, the time-evolution of
\mu_{5}
is induced by the parallel chromoelectric and chromomagnetic fields during the initial stage following a heavy-ion collision
Lappi and Schlichting 2018
or by a sphaleron transition at a later stage
Fukushima et al. 2008
. In the latter system, it can be driven by an external field. We found that the intensity of the produced photons is comparable to that of other sources of direct photons in heavy-ion collisions
Abdulameer et al. 2024
. The polarized electromagnetic radiation produced by chiral materials within the THz frequency range makes it useful in practical applications.

Another interesting model to consider is
b_{0}=A\cos(t/\tau)
leading to the Mathieu equation for the amplitude
a(t)
. This model describes the chiral chemical potential, which is driven by, for instance, a laser field. We defer further exploration of this model and the attendant parametric resonances to future studies.

In the case of Weyl semimetals characterized by finite
{\bm{\Delta}}(t)
and
\mu_{5}=0
, we argued using perturbation theory that no radiation is emitted at the leading order in
{\bm{\Delta}}
. Unfortunately, we were unable to apply the method of Bogolyubov transformation in this case, leaving us uncertain whether any radiation can be emitted from Weyl semimetals. It is also noteworthy that since
\mu_{5}=0
, the electromagnetic field is perfectly stable. Therefore, the question of whether time-varying
{\bm{\Delta}}(t)
in Weyl semimetals produces polarized electromagnetic radiation is closely related to the question of whether the chiral instability is the only source of the radiation, as previously discussed.

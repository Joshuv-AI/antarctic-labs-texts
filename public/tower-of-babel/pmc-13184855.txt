Methodological Considerations on Targeted Polymer Embolization in Obstructive Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy
Authors: Ahmet Kivrak, Ahmet Hakan Ates, Ugur Canpolat, Kudret Aytemir
Published: 2026

We read with great interest the case report by Martins Filho et al1 describing optimal targeted polymer-based embolization for obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (oHCM). The authors should be commended for addressing a complex clinical scenario in a patient unsuitable for surgical myectomy; however, several methodological issues warrant discussion.

First, the rationale for embolization of an obtuse marginal (OM) branch requires clarification. In classical oHCM, left ventricular outflow tract (LVOT) obstruction is primarily related to basal septal hypertrophy supplied by septal perforators from the left anterior descending artery. Although OM balloon occlusion reduced the LVOT gradient, no anatomical or perfusion-based evidence was provided to confirm septal supply from this vessel. In the absence of myocardial contrast echocardiography or equivalent imaging, the role of the OM branch in septal obstruction remains uncertain.

Moreover, balloon occlusion of major epicardial vessels may induce transient lateral wall ischemia with global contractile impairment, potentially leading to an apparent but nonspecific reduction in LVOT gradient. Importantly, the patient was reported to have a preexisting mid-ventricular gradient, which may offer a more plausible mechanistic explanation for the observed hemodynamic improvement. Lateral wall dysfunction following OM embolization could preferentially attenuate mid-ventricular obstruction rather than directly relieving basal LVOT obstruction. In addition, pigtail catheter positioning closer to the apex may have favored recording of mid-ventricular gradients, potentially confounding invasive echocardiographic gradient correlation.

Finally, follow-up imaging demonstrated new inferolateral wall motion abnormalities with a decline in left ventricular ejection fraction, suggesting nonseptal myocardial injury. Prior studies support a stepwise, septum-focused approach guided by imaging to achieve gradient reduction while preserving ventricular function.2,3

In conclusion, while this report presents an innovative strategy for complex oHCM, extension of embolization beyond septal branches should be approached cautiously. Rigorous confirmation of myocardial supply and careful differentiation between LVOT and mid-ventricular gradients are essential, particularly in patients with coexisting mid-ventricular obstruction, as supported by prior reports including our own experience.4

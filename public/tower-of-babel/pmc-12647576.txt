Optimizing the crystallinity of ZrO2 gate insulator in indium gallium zinc oxide thin-film transistors through atomic layer deposition process temperature control
Authors: Hanseok Jeong, Soo Min Yoo, Minki Choe, In-Hwan Baek, Woojin Jeon
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe optimization of zirconium oxide (ZrO2) crystallinity for gate insulators (GI) in indium gallium zinc oxide (IGZO) thin-film transistors (TFTs) was studied to enhance the performance of dynamic random-access memory (DRAM) cell transistors. ZrO2 films were deposited via atomic layer deposition (ALD) at temperatures ranging from 150 °C to 300 °C, yielding varied crystallinity from amorphous to high-crystallinity phases. Meso-crystalline ZrO2 films deposited at 200 °C achieved an optimal trade-off between ON and OFF current characteristics, attributed to reduced grain boundary leakage and an improved dielectric constant. Films deposited at higher temperatures (250 °C and 300 °C) exhibited increased OFF current and ON/OFF ratio degradation due to crystallization-induced defects, while lower temperatures (150 °C) led to reliability issues from oxygen vacancies and carbon impurities. These results indicate the importance of precise temperature control during the ALD process to achieve meso-crystalline ZrO2, ensuring enhanced ON/OFF ratios, and device stability.Subject terms: Engineering, Materials science, Nanoscience and technology, Physics

Introduction

Dynamic random-access memory (DRAM) has established itself as a fundamental memory technology since its inception in the 1970s1,2. With the rapid advancements in artificial intelligence and high-performance computing, there is an increasing demand for enhanced performance and reduced power consumption in memory devices3–7. To meet these requirements, amorphous oxide semiconductors, particularly indium gallium zinc oxide (IGZO), have emerged as a promising alternative to conventional silicon transistors in DRAM cell transistors8–11. IGZO exhibits extremely low leakage current (~ 10–23 A/μm) due to its wide bandgap12–14. Additionally, it demonstrates a suitable mobility (~ 20 cm2/V·s)15,16. These characteristics can extend data retention time and reduce power consumption, thereby enhancing the energy efficiency of memory devices17.

Incorporating high dielectric constant (k) materials as gate insulators (GI) in field-effect transistors (FETs) can enhance capacitance density, enabling faster switching speeds and lower operating voltages18. Compared to conventional SiO2, high k materials also offer advantages in scaling, as their high dielectric constant allows for the formation of relatively thicker layers while maintaining gate capacitance, effectively reducing leakage current caused by tunneling15,19. Representative high k materials include Al2O320–22, ZrO223–25, HfO215, HfZrO226,27, TiO228, and TaOx29–31. Among these, ZrO2 is particularly suitable for next-generation FET GIs due to its high dielectric constant (28), appropriate bandgap (5.8 eV), and high breakdown field (7–15 MV/cm), hence, higher capacitance density enables device scaling without thinning the dielectric32,33. Moreover, reduced operating voltage improves energy efficiency. However, their crystallization can increase surface roughness while high k materials offer higher dielectric constants compared to amorphous SiO2, leading to enhanced surface scattering30,34–36. Furthermore, oxygen vacancies (VO) or impurities within the GI can induce Coulomb scattering, which degrades FET performance37–42.

In this study, we investigated IGZO thin-film transistors (TFTs) with ZrO2 GI depending on the crystallinity of ZrO2. The crystallinity of ZrO2 was controlled by adjusting the deposition temperature during the atomic layer deposition (ALD) process. In our previous research, we optimized the ZrO2 ALD process for a Mo/ZrO2/IGZO metal–insulator-semiconductor (MIS) structure by precisely controlling the crystallinity of ZrO2 based on the deposition temperature24. In other words, meso-crystalline ZrO2 suppresses OFF-current while maintaining high k. However, that study was investigated only MIS structure and did not consider the influence of ZrO2 crystallinity on the performance and reliability of IGZO TFTs. To extend this investigation, we fabricated bottom-gate IGZO TFTs with ZrO2 gate insulators to examine the effects of crystallinity on device performance and reliability. In IGZO TFTs with ZrO2 as the GI, high-crystallinity ZrO2 thin-films achieved higher ON current, but this improvement was accompanied by deterioration in OFF current characteristics. Conversely, low-crystallinity ZrO2 thin-films prevented OFF current degradation but exhibited reduced ON current. To optimize the trade-off relationship between ON and OFF currents, this study investigated achieving an optimal crystallinity of ZrO2 GI through precise control of the deposition temperature.

Experimental section

We fabricated the bottom gate, top contact amorphous IGZO (a-IGZO) TFT with ZrO2 GI on a SiO2/Si substrate. Figure 1(a) shows the fabrication process flow of the a-IGZO TFT with ZrO2 GI. The bottom Mo gate electrode was deposited using DC sputtering with a Mo target and Ar gas flow rate of 15 sccm at a deposition temperature of 110 °C. 50-nm-thick ZrO2 films as the GI were deposited on the Mo gate electrode using the ALD process (iSAC Research Inc.) with cyclopentadienyl tris(dimethylamino) zirconium (CpZr, SK Trichem Co., Ltd.) as the Zr precursor and O3 (200 g m−3) as the reactant. The process temperatures were set at 150, 200, 250, and 300 °C. The gate stack of the a-IGZO TFT with SiO2 GI used p++-Si substrate with a 100 nm thermal SiO2 as gate electrodes and dielectric layers, respectively. The composition of In, Ga, and Zn in IGZO thin-films was controlled by adjusting the sub-cycle ratio of InOx, GaOx, and ZnO ALD using hetero-aminoalkyl indium (STIn-4–3, SK Trichem Co., Ltd.), trimethyl gallium (TMGa) as the gallium precursor, diethylzinc (DEZ) as the zinc precursor, and O3 (200 g m−3) as the reactant, at a deposition temperature of 250 °C within the super-cycle constitution. A 20 nm-thick IGZO thin-film with an In:Ga:Zn cation ratio of 1:0.9:1 was deposited by atomic layer deposition using 17 super-cycles, each composed of InOx:GaOx:ZnO sub-cycles in a 6:5:4 ratio. After deposition of IGZO thin-film, post-deposition annealing (PDA) was conducted at 340 °C for 1 h in an air ambient to all the examined samples. The IGZO channels were patterned using photolithography (with the Aligner & Exposure located at the 3D Convergence Center of Inha University) and wet etching processes. A channel width (W) of 300 μm and a length (L) of 50 μm were used for the parameters of the fabricated TFTs. The thickness of all IGZO channel layers was fixed at 20 nm. Then, the 100-nm-thick Mo films as source/drain (S/D) electrodes were deposited using DC sputtering and patterned using photolithography and lift-off processes. The S/D electrodes were fabricated using a negative photoresist-based lift-off process. Negative PR (DNR-L300-40) was spin-coated at 500 rpm for 2 s and 3000 rpm for 50 s, followed by a soft bake at 100 °C for 90 s. Photolithography was carried out using a mask aligner (SUSS MicroTec, MA6), and a post-exposure bake was performed at 100 °C for 90 s. The PR pattern was developed for 20 s and rinsed with DI water. Subsequently, a 100-nm-thick Mo film was deposited by DC sputtering under 100 W power, 1 mTorr pressure, and 20 sccm Ar flow using a sputtering system. Finally, the lift-off process was performed by immersing the sample in acetone for more than 30 min, followed by IPA and DI water rinsing to complete the S/D electrode formation. Figure 1(b) shows top-view images of the bottom-gate IGZO TFT.

(a) Fabrication process flow, (b) schematic diagram, and (c) optical microscope image of ALD-derived IGZO TFT with ZrO2 GI.

The film thickness was determined by calculating the layer density, which was measured using energy-dispersive X-ray fluorescence spectroscopy (EDXRF, ARL Quant’X, Thermo Fisher Scientific) and was correlated with spectroscopic ellipsometry (SE, ESM-300, J. A. Woollam). The crystallinities of ZrO2 films were evaluated using glancing angle incidence X-ray diffraction (GAXRD, X’pert Pro, PANalytical) at an incident angle of 1.5°. The electrical properties were evaluated by measuring the capacitance versus voltage (C-V) using an Agilent 4284 precision LCR meter, and the current–voltage (I-V) characteristics were measured using a Keithley 4200-SCS semiconductor analyzer.

Results and discussion

Figure 2(a) shows the GAXRD diffraction patterns of ZrO2 thin-films deposited on Mo substrates at various process temperatures. IGZO thin-films were subsequently deposited on top of ZrO2, followed by a PDA. GAXRD measurements were conducted on the Mo/ZrO2/IGZO stacks. In the as-deposited state, all the ZrO2 thin film exhibited amorphous phase (data not shown). The films deposited at 250 °C and 300 °C exhibited well-defined crystallinity in the tetragonal phase, while those deposited at the lower temperature of 150 °C remained in an amorphous state. This amorphous nature is attributed to the insufficient thermal budget at lower temperatures, which is inadequate for initiating the crystallization of ZrO243 Notably, the film deposited at 200 °C displayed a meso-crystalline structure, characterized by a broad XRD peak near 35.3°, corresponding to the (110) plane of the tetragonal phase24,44. This meso-crystalline structure is defined by crystalline nuclei embedded within an amorphous matrix. Figure 2(b) presents the k values of ZrO2 thin-films, calculated from C-V measurements of Mo/ZrO2/IGZO MIS capacitors. The results reveal an increasing trend in the dielectric constant with rising deposition temperature, attributed to the enhanced crystallinity achieved through the thermal energy provided during deposition. The ZrO2 film deposited at 300 °C exhibited the highest dielectric constant of 31.1, indicative of its superior crystalline quality. Moreover, the meso-crystalline ZrO2 film deposited at 200 °C demonstrated a higher k value compared to the amorphous film deposited at 150 °C. By controlling the ALD process temperature, the crystallinity of ZrO2 thin-films was tailored, and their application as GIs in IGZO-based TFTs was investigated for TFT characteristics.

(a) XRD pattern of ZrO2 thin-films deposited at 150, 200, 250, and 300 °C and followed by the PDA process. (b) Dielectric constant of ZrO2 thin-film in MIS capacitors, (inset) schematic diagram of the MIS capacitor.

Figure 3(a)-(e) and Table 1 show the transfer characteristics of IGZO TFTs with SiO2 GI, and ZrO2 GIs deposited at varying ALD process temperatures. At process temperature 250 °C and 300 °C, the OFF current increased to 6.18 × 10–11 A and 4.37 × 10–9 A, respectively (see Figs. 3(d) and 3(e)). In the ZrO2, the grain boundary is a major carrier conduction path, because defective nature of grain boundary would facilitate the carrier injection and conduction of charge carrier45,46. In this regard, high crystallinity of ZrO2 attributed to increasing the OFF current. The field-effect mobility (μFE) was calculated in the linear region using the equation μFE = Lgm/(WCOXVDS), where VDS = 0.1 V, gm represents the transconductance, and COX denotes the capacitance density. The values of COX corresponding to each GI are summarized in Table 1. The threshold voltage (VTH) was determined as the gate voltage at VDS = 5.1 V when IDS = 10 nA × W/L. The subthreshold swing (SS) was calculated in the subthreshold region of the transfer curve using the equation SS = dVGS/d(log IDS), and the result represents the average of the three minimum values within the voltage range from the onset of turn on up to the VTH. As shown in Fig. 3(f), when SiO2 was used as the GI, the μFE was measured at 18.58 cm2/V·s. In the case of ZrO2 GIs, variations in μFE with deposition temperature were negligible. However, the μFE values for ZrO2 GIs were lower than those for SiO2 GIs across all process temperatures. This reduction in μFE is attributed to Coulomb scattering at the ZrO2/IGZO interface, caused by the interaction of charges with positively charged defects such as oxygen vacancies (VO) within the GI. These defects hinder electron transport by interacting with carriers at the GI/IGZO interface38–42,47. All devices exhibited negative VTH, which is attributed to the relatively high electron concentration in the IGZO. This behavior results from the cation composition and channel thickness. It is well established that the VTH of IGZO TFTs can be shifted positively by adjusting the cation ratio or reducing the active layer thickness38–42,47. The VTH is also influenced by the VO concentration within the GI and the k value of ZrO248,49 As the deposition temperature of ZrO2 increased, the reduction in VO concentration resulted in a positive shift in VTH, reaching −1.52 V and −1.08 V at 250 °C and 300 °C, respectively. VO can act as positively charged defects, such as VO2⁺, which enhance the internal electric field within the GI50,51. The reduction of VO at higher temperatures weakens the internal electric field, leading to a positive VTH shift. Furthermore, as shown in Fig. 2(b), the increase in the dielectric constant of ZrO2 with deposition temperature enhances gate controllability. From the perspective of VTH, higher deposition temperatures, which improve crystallinity, appear suitable for IGZO TFTs. However, the ZrO2 GI deposited at 300 °C exhibited significant degradation, with an SS value of 191 mV/dec, which is considerably higher compared to the SS values of SiO2 GIs and ZrO2 GIs deposited at 150 °C, 200 °C, and 250 °C, which ranged from 80 to 100 mV/dec. This deterioration is attributed to the increased charge trap states at the ZrO2/IGZO interface, caused by morphological degradation due to the high crystallinity of the ZrO2 film52. Increased crystallinity could in principle increase roughness scattering as described in Introduction section. However, the mobility of ZrO2 was only lower than that of SiO2, and no mobility variation was observed depending on the ZrO2 deposition temperature. This indicates that mobility was not affected by the degree of crystallinity. Only the SS showed significant degradation at 300 °C, which was caused not by mobility changes but by a substantial increase in the OFF current. Consequently, IGZO TFT with ZrO2 GI deposited at 300 °C does not meet the requirement of SS < 100 mV/dec for DRAM cell transistors9,52.

Figure 4(a)-(e) show the output characteristics of IGZO TFTs with SiO2 GI, and ZrO2 GI deposited at varying ALD process temperatures. Figure 4(f) illustrates the capacitance density measured at a DC bias of + 10 V and the saturation current at a gate electric field of 2 MV/cm in Mo/IGZO/ZrO2 MIS capacitors. The saturation current of IGZO TFTs with ZrO2 GI increased from 0.42 mA at 150 °C to 1.79 mA at 300 °C. This increase corresponds to the improved crystallinity of ZrO2 films at higher deposition temperatures, as shown in Fig. 2(a), which enhances the capacitance density. Notably, the saturation current of IGZO TFTs with ZrO2 GI deposited at 150 °C is lower than the 0.48 mA for TFTs with SiO2 as the GI. This reduction in saturation current is attributed to the increased Coulomb scattering and trap sites at the ZrO2/IGZO interface when using ZrO2, a high k material, instead of amorphous SiO2. These defects and interface interactions reduce the μFE of IGZO grown on ZrO230,34 High k materials such as ZrO2 are more likely to contain a higher concentration of VO defects compared to SiO2. These defects trap charges, increase Coulomb scattering at the interface, and hinder carrier mobility36,37. Consequently, the use of ZrO2 GI results in reduced mobility and, thus, lower saturation current due to these interface characteristics. From the perspective of saturation current, highly crystalline ZrO2 GIs deposited at 300 °C are considered advantageous for enhancing the high-speed operation and performance of DRAM cell transistors.

Figure 5(a) illustrates the ON/OFF ratio extracted from Fig. 2(a)-(e). In the case of IGZO TFTs with ZrO2 GI deposited at 250 °C, and 300 °C the ON/OFF ratio deteriorated. This degradation is attributed to the increased crystallinity of the ZrO2 film, which significantly elevates the OFF current. The gate leakage current (Fig. 5(b) also showed a tendency to increase with higher ZrO2 deposition temperature. The ON/OFF ratio is a critical parameter that determines the switching performance of transistors, with DRAM cell transistors typically requiring an ON/OFF ratio of approximately 10953. IGZO TFTs with SiO2 GI, and ZrO2 GI deposited at 150 °C, and 200 °C with low crystallinity maintained an ON/OFF ratio exceeding 109. In contrast, the ZrO2 films deposited at 250 °C, and 300 °C exhibited degraded ON/OFF ratios due to crystallization, where grain boundaries acted as leakage paths, making them unsuitable for DRAM cell transistors.

The degradation of TFT characteristics caused by the crystallization of ZrO2 films was also evident in positive bias stress (PBS) measurements. PBS was conducted at a gate electric field of 1 MV/cm for 3600 s, and Fig. 6(f) shows the PBS reliability of IGZO TFTs with SiO2 GI and ZrO2 GI depending on process temperature. The largest VTH shift of 0.46 V was observed with ZrO2 film deposited at 150 °C. This shift is attributed to the charge trapping mechanism, where donor species VO2+ are converted into VO during PBS in the ZrO2 GI54,55. Furthermore, at lower deposition temperatures, the reduced reactivity of O3 increased the concentration of VO within the GI and led to the accumulation of carbon impurities originating from the CpZr precursor, contributing to reliability degradation24,56. In contrast, IGZO TFTs with SiO2 GI and ZrO2 GI deposited at 200 °C, 250 °C, and 300 °C exhibited nearly identical VTH shifts. However, as shown in Fig. 6(d), ZrO2 GI deposited at 250 °C exhibited a significant increase in OFF current from 1.15 × 10–10 A to 1.88 × 10–9 A during PBS. Consequently, in IGZO-based TFTs, ZrO2 GIs deposited at 200 °C formed a meso-crystalline structure, effectively suppressing leakage paths caused by grain boundaries, maintaining a relatively higher k value compared to the amorphous state, and achieving an ON/OFF ratio exceeding 109.

Conclusion

In summary, the optimization of ZrO2 crystallinity was investigated through precise control of the ALD process temperature to enhance the performance of IGZO-based TFTs with ZrO2 gate insulators for application in DRAM cell transistors. Consequently, as the process temperature increased, the crystallinity of the ZrO2 film improved, leading to an increase in capacitance density and ON current. However, ZrO2 films deposited at higher temperatures of 250 °C and 300 °C exhibited a significant increase in OFF current, resulting in a degraded ON/OFF ratio. Moreover, positive bias stress measurements revealed reliability issues in IGZO TFTs with ZrO2 GIs deposited at the relatively lower temperature of 150 °C, which exhibited the largest VTH shift of 0.46 V due to the accumulation of VO and carbon impurities within the ZrO2 GI during the ALD process. Meanwhile, the IGZO TFT with ZrO2 GIs deposited at 250 °C exhibited severe degradation during positive bias stress, as indicated by a gradual increase in OFF current. In contrast, the ZrO2 film deposited at 200 °C formed a meso-crystalline structure that suppressed leakage paths caused by grain boundaries while maintaining a higher dielectric constant than the amorphous state. This resulted in an ON/OFF ratio exceeding 109 and the smallest Vth shift of 0.19 V. Therefore, for the application of high k materials in DRAM cell transistors, a meso-crystalline structure that minimizes leakage paths by suppressing grain boundary formation while maintaining a higher dielectric constant than the amorphous state is the most advantageous for improving ON current and overall device performance.

Dapagliflozin protects against chronic heart failure in mice by inhibiting macrophage-mediated inflammation, independent of SGLT2
Authors: Qingqing Wu, Qi Yao, Tongtong Hu, Jiabin Yu, Kebing Jiang, Ying Wan, Qizhu Tang
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
SummaryThe specific mechanism of sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitor in heart failure (HF) needs to be elucidated. In this study, we use SGLT2-global-knockout (KO) mice to assess the mechanism of SGLT2 inhibitor on HF. Dapagliflozin ameliorates both myocardial infarction (MI)- and transverse aortic constriction (TAC)-induced HF. Global SGLT2 deficiency does not exert protection against adverse remodeling in both MI- and TAC-induced HF models. Dapagliflozin blurs MI- and TAC-induced HF phenotypes in SGLT2-KO mice. Dapagliflozin causes major changes in cardiac fibrosis and inflammation. Based on single-cell RNA sequencing, dapagliflozin causes significant differences in the gene expression profile of macrophages and fibroblasts. Moreover, dapagliflozin directly inhibits macrophage inflammation, thereby suppressing cardiac fibroblasts activation. The cardio-protection of dapagliflozin is blurred in mice treated with a C-C chemokine receptor type 2 antagonist. Taken together, the protective effects of dapagliflozin against HF are independent of SGLT2, and macrophage inhibition is the main target of dapagliflozin against HF.Keywords: dapagliflozin, inflammation, fibrosis, single-cell transcriptomics, macrophages

Introduction

Heart failure (HF), a condition characterized by the inability of the heart to pump enough blood, imposes a huge public health burden worldwide. An estimated prevalence of HF was reported to be 37.7 million individuals globally.1,2 In accordance with the Framingham Heart Study, once HF is diagnosed, the 30-day mortality rate is about 10%, the 1-year mortality rate is approximately 20%–30%, and the 5-year mortality rate is as high as 45%–60%, comparable to malignancy.3 In the past decades, the golden triangle drug therapy, including ACEI/ARB, aldosterone antagonists, and β-blockers, has been confirmed to refine the prognosis of HF patients.4,5 However, the incidence and mortality of HF are found to increase every year. New agents and device-assisted therapies need to be developed to prevent or possibly reverse HF progression.

Dapagliflozin, an efficient and selective sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitor, was initially developed for type 2 diabetes (T2D) therapy.6 In 2018, dapagliflozin was found to be associated with the risk reduction of hospitalization for HF in T2D patients.7 In 2019, McMurray8 first reported that dapagliflozin improved the hazard of worsening HF or death caused by cardiovascular diseases in HF patients without T2D. Mounting evidence has now demonstrated the positive effect of dapagliflozin on long-term outcomes of HF patients with reduced or preserved ejection fraction (EF) without diabetes.9,10,11,12,13 This suggests that the mechanisms underlying the cardio-protective effects of dapagliflozin could be glucose independent. Various studies using animal models have confirmed the cardio-protective effects of dapagliflozin in cardiac injury and HF induced by pressure overload,14 myocardial infarction (MI),15 diabetic cardiomyopathy,16 ischemia/reperfusion injury,17 and arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy18 among others. SGLT2 is mainly located at the renal proximal tubule,19 not in heart, and dapagliflozin is a highly selective SGLT2 inhibitor.20 Thus, other mechanisms may mediate the protective effects of dapagliflozin on the heart. Studies reported that preload decrease (following natriuresis or osmotic diuresis), amelioration in cardiac energy metabolism,21 and inhibition of myocardial Na+/H+ exchange may mediate the protective effects of dapagliflozin.22 A recent study demonstrated that the protective effects of empagliflozin against acute ischemia-reperfusion injury were indeed independent of the SGLT2 protein.23 However, it remains to be ascertained whether the cardio-protective effects of dapagliflozin against heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF) or heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF) depend on SGLT2 inhibition. Furthermore, whether dapagliflozin directly affects the heart and which cell type it mainly affects during HF shall be investigated. The specific mechanism underlying the protection of dapagliflozin on HF needs to be fully elucidated.

In this study, we demonstrated that the protective effect of dapagliflozin against HFrEF is independent of SGLT2 inhibition by using SGLT2 full-knockout (SGLT2-KO) mice. By using single-cell transcriptomics from MI heart (infarct + border zone area), we confirm the predominant effects of dapagliflozin relied on macrophages and fibroblasts, especially concerning macrophage inhibition during HFrEF.

Results

Dapagliflozin ameliorated cardiac dysfunction in MI-induced HF

As MI is the leading pathogeny of HF worldwide, we generated a mouse HFrEF model with MI to evaluate the effects of dapagliflozin on HFrEF. Mice were administered with dapagliflozin for 26 days. As dapagliflozin is a highly selective SGLT2 inhibitor, which may affect glucose, insulin, and lipid metabolism, we evaluated systemic blood glucose, blood lipid, and insulin level, glycosuria, and blood ketones 26 days post dapagliflozin administration. As shown in Table S1, we did not observe any change among the four groups in fasting blood glucose, triglyceride, total cholesterol, body weight, fasting insulin level, and blood ketones after dapagliflozin administration. However, glycosuria of mice was significantly increased after dapagliflozin administration. These data indicate that dapagliflozin does not affect systemic blood glucose, blood lipid, insulin level, and blood ketones both in physiology status and HF condition. 4 weeks post MI, cardiac dysfunction was ameliorated in the MI-DAPA group as accessed by reduced left ventricular (LV) end-systolic diameter (LVESd), enhanced left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), and left ventricular fractional shortening (LVFS). The left cardiac catheter also found that dapagliflozin increased the cardiac output (CO), reduced LV end-diastolic/systolic volume (Ves/Ved), and increased maximum rate of LV pressure rise/decay (dp/dt max, dp/dt min) 4 weeks post MI (Table S2). The LV wall thickness and heart rate were not significantly different among the four groups (Table S2). Altogether, our data indicate that dapagliflozin ameliorated cardiac dysfunction post MI independent of systemic metabolic changes.

Dwindling cardiac fibrosis is the main phenotype in dapagliflozin-treated HF mice post MI

4 weeks post MI, mice in the dapagliflozin (DAPA) group showed no significant change in the infarction size (Figure 1A). The survival rate in MI-normal saline (NS) group was comparable to MI-DAPA group (Figure 1B). 4 weeks post MI, mice in the DAPA group showed significantly ameliorated cardiac remodeling as evidenced by reduced heart weight to body weight (HW/BW) ratio and HW to tibial length ratio (HW/TL) (Figure S1A). The transcription of HF markers (ANP and BNP) in LV heart tissue was also reduced in the MI-DAPA group (Figure S1B). These observations suggested the anti-HF effects of dapagliflozin. RNA-seq was performed in HF heart tissue (infarct + border zone area)24,25 to confirm the precise effect of dapagliflozin on MI-induced HFrEF. As shown in Figure 1C, 855 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were screened in the MI-DAPA group, including 139 that were upregulated and 716 that were downregulated (Figure 1C). Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment confirmed that the top pathways affected by dapagliflozin were related to extracellular matrix (ECM) remodeling and collagen fibril process (Figure 1D). Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analysis and heatmap also confirmed that the top pathways affected by dapagliflozin were mainly associated with cardiac fibrosis and inflammation (Figures 1E andS1C). To confirm the reduced fibrosis induced by dapagliflozin treatment, we conducted staining and qPCR, as shown in Figures 1F and S1D. Mice in the MI-DAPA group also had reduced LV collagen fraction (Figure 1F) and diminished transcription of fibrosis markers in LV heart tissue (Figure S1D). All these data indicate that diminished cardiac fibrosis could be the predominant phenotype in dapagliflozin-treated HF mice post MI.

By targeting inflammation signaling, dapagliflozin may exert anti-fibrosis effects in HF

As depicted in Figure 1, diminished cardiac fibrosis could be the predominant phenotype in dapagliflozin-treated HF mice post MI. Thus, we isolated cardiac fibroblasts from heart tissue (infarct + border zone area) 4 weeks post MI in mice with dapagliflozin treatment. RNA-seq was performed in these fibroblasts in the MI-NS group and the MI-DAPA group. In the MI-DAPA group, 199 DEGs were identified, with 65 being upregulated and 134 being downregulated (Figure 2A). GO enrichment and heatmap confirmed that the top pathways in fibroblasts affected by dapagliflozin were related to the inflammatory response (Figures 2B and 2C). KEGG pathway analysis also confirmed that the top pathways affected by dapagliflozin were primarily associated with pro-inflammatory cytokines (tumor necrosis factor [TNF], interleukin [IL]) and nuclear factor kapa B (NF-κB) (Figure 2D). These data suggest that by targeting inflammatory signaling, dapagliflozin may exert anti-fibrosis effects in MI-induced HF.

Global knockdown of SGLT2 could not mimic the protective effect of dapagliflozin in MI-induced HF

Whether the protective effects of dapagliflozin depend on SGLT2 inhibition is still unclear. To answer this question, SGLT2-KO mice were utilized. We detected the protein level of SGLT2 in multiple tissues and only found a detectable level of SGLT2 in kidney tissue but not in the intestine, heart tissue, as well as cardiomyocytes and fibroblasts isolated from hearts (Figure S2A). As the same family members of SGLT2, SGLT1 is responsible for the reabsorption of the other 10% glucose in the original urine. SGLT1 was expressed in the kidney, intestine, and heart tissues but not in cardiomyocytes and fibroblasts isolated from hearts (Figure S2A). The level of SGLT2 was also undetectable in HF heart tissue from mice 6 weeks post transverse aortic constriction (TAC) and heart tissue from mice 4 weeks post MI (Figures S2B and S2C). The expression of SGLT2 in SGLT2-KO mice was fully blocked in the kidney, intestine, and heart tissue (Figure S2D), while the protein level of SGLT1 was increased in kidney tissue, but not in heart tissue and small intestine tissue, in SGLT2-KO mice (Figure S2D). This suggests that the increased level of kidney SGLT1 in SGLT2-KO mice may partly compensate for the deficiency of SGLT2 function. We then subjected SGLT2-KO mice to MI and treated them with dapagliflozin (from 3 days till 28 days post MI). The MI size and survival rate among these four groups showed no significant difference (Figures 3A and 3B). Unexpectedly, we discovered that SGLT2 knockout could not attenuate MI-induced cardiac dysfunction, hypertrophy, and fibrosis, as demonstrated in Table S3 and Figures 3C–3E. However, in SGLT2-KO mice treated with dapagliflozin, cardiac dysfunction and cardiac fibrosis were relieved when compared with the KO-MI group (Table S3; Figures 3C–3E). Moreover, cardiac function and HF, pro-fibrosis marker gene expression were comparable between wild-type (WT)-MI + DAPA group and KO-MI + DAPA group. These data further confirm that the cardio-protection effects of dapagliflozin maybe independent of SGLT2 inhibition. The serum glucose, lipid, insulin, and glycosuria were also evaluated in SGLT2-KO mice. As shown in Table S4, the fasting blood glucose, triglyceride, total cholesterol, body weight, fasting insulin, blood ketones, kidney weight, and urine Na+ were not significantly different among the six groups. Glycosuria levels in SGLT2-KO mice and dapagliflozin-treated WT mice were higher than the corresponding WT mice. These results indicated that the positive effects of dapagliflozin on MI-induced HF are independent of SGLT2.

Dapagliflozin attenuated TAC-induced HF in SGLT2-KO mice

To confirm the effect of SGLT2 knockout on HF induced by pressure overload (such as hypertension and aortic valve stenosis), we generated another HFrEF model with TAC surgery in mice. SGLT2-KO mice were also treated with dapagliflozin 2 weeks post TAC surgery. Consistent with the results obtained with the MI-induced HF model, 6 weeks post TAC, we did not observe ameliorated cardiac hypertrophy, fibrosis, and dysfunction in SGLT2-KO mice compared with the WT-TAC group (Figures 4A–4F; Table S5). However, dapagliflozin reduced HW/BW, HW/TL, LW/BW, and LW/TL ratios in the TAC-induced HF model in both SGLT2-WT and KO mice (Figure 4A). Moreover, cardiac dysfunction was also ameliorated in dapagliflozin-treated WT and KO mice as assessed by reduced LVESd, LV wall thickness, and LV mass and increased LVEF and LVFS even under similar pressure loads (Figure 4B; Table S5). We also evaluated myocardial remodeling; TAC-induced cardiac hypertrophy and LV fibrosis were blurred in the two dapagliflozin treatment groups (Figures 4C–4F).

Dapagliflozin treatment caused a major change in cell subsets associated with HF

As depicted in Figures 1 and 3, inflammation and fibrosis could be the target of pathological change of dapagliflozin treatment in HF. The single-cell mRNA transcripts extracted from heart tissue (infarct + border zone area)25 were conducted in the sample 2 weeks post MI (Figure 5A), as inflammation and cardiac fibrosis are the two main features of the early stage of the pathology post MI.26 Output was quality controlled and analyzed using the Seurat clustering algorithm.27 We processed all samples using unsupervised clustering and identified 13 clusters in heart samples (Figure 5B). Based on gene expression differentials, these cells are clustered bioinformatically, and they also express markers that distinguish them from other types of cells (Figures 5C, 5D, and S3A). The most abundant cell populations were cardiomyocytes (cluster 0; Ryr2+ and Actc1+), followed by fibroblasts (clusters 1, 4, and 7; Pdgtra+, Col1a1+), endothelial cells (clusters 2, 6, 10, and 12; Pecam1+, Fabp4+), monocytes/macrophages (clusters 3 and 9; CD74+, CD163+), B cells (cluster 8; CD79b+), and smooth muscle cells (cluster 11; Myh11+) (Figure S3A). The abundance of each cluster revealed a major reduction in the cluster of cardiomyocytes and endothelial cells and an increase in fibroblasts and monocytes/macrophages in MI-NS samples. These clusters were reshuffled in dapagliflozin-treated mice hearts (Figures 5E, 5F, and S3B).

Dapagliflozin affects mainly fibroblasts and monocytes/macrophages in MI-induced HF

Single-cell RNA-seq analysis discovered that cardiac fibroblasts (FBs) exhibited heterogeneity. Unsupervised clustering revealed three subclusters of FBs: FB1, FB2, and FB3 (Figure 6A). All three subclusters expressed the FB marker genes Gsn and Col1a1 at high levels24 (Figure 6B). Differential expression analysis was used to identify specific genes within each cluster (Figure 6C). FB1 expressed high levels of Htra3, a critical regulator maintaining the identity of quiescent cardiac fibroblasts.28 FB2 expressed high levels of common fibroblast markers (Postn, Fn1, and Col8a1) and RNA-binding protein muscleblind-like1 (MBNL1), a gene related to the activation of fibroblasts into myofibroblasts promoting fibrosis.29 FB3 expressed high levels of genes associated with ECs such as Kdr, Egfl7, Ushbp1, Adgrf5, and Btnl9 (Figure 6C). The decrease in FB1 cells (84.5% in the sham group versus 53.2% in the MI-NS group) and increase in FB2 (2.4% in the sham group versus 38.0% in the MI-NS group) in the MI-NS group further emphasize the FB1 subcluster as quiescent cardiac FBs and FB2 as injury-responsive cardiac FBs. Dapagliflozin treatment reshuffled the quiescent/activation FBs (FB1: 80.9%; FB2: 13.0%). FB3 expressed both FB and EC markers, maybe a subcluster associated with epithelial-to-mesenchymal or endothelium-to-mesenchymal transition, which reduced in both MI-NS and MI-DAPA groups. The top pathways upregulated in the MI-NS group analyzed via GO and KEGG enrichment analyses in all three FB subclusters were fully associated with ECM processing, which were all downregulated in the MI-DAPA group (Figures 6E and S4A–S4D).

The most abundant types of cardiac immune cells were monocytes/macrophages. The monocyte/macrophage markers (CD74+, CD163+) in two distinct clusters (clusters 3 and 9) were significantly enriched24 (Figures S3A and S3B), and three subclusters of macrophage were identified via unsupervised clustering: Mac1, Mac2, and Mac3 (Figures 6F and 6G). Analysis of gene CCR2+ revealed the signatures for recruited pro-inflammatory monocytes/macrophages, and CCR2− was associated with the remodeling of the tissue such as coronary development, vascular expansion, and cardiac tissue repair.30,31 Only the Mac3 subcluster expressed a high level of CCR2. Mac1 and Mac2 were two CCR2− subclusters. Mac1 expressed a high level of M2-like markers CD163 and Mrc1 (Figure 6G), which may correspond to pro-repair. Mac2 expressed a high level of MKi67+, indicating cell proliferation, which was important for maintaining each macrophage subset.31 We observed an increase in the pro-inflammatory CCR2+ Mac3 subcluster (3.3% in the MI-NS group versus 1.5% in the Sham-NS group) and a decrease in M2-like Mac1 (88.2% in the MI-NS group versus 95.5% in the Sham-NS group) in the MI-NS group. The MI-DAPA group showed a remarkably high level of the MKi67+ Mac2 subcluster with reduced proportions of Mac1 (84.4%) and Mac3 (1.9%) (Figure 6H). GO and KEGG enrichment analyses in monocytes/macrophages also confirmed that the upregulated immune process in the MI-NS group was downregulated in the MI-DAPA group (Figures 6I and S5A–S5D). Taken together, consistent with our bulk RNA-seq from MI heart tissue and fibroblasts isolated from MI heart tissue, inflammation caused by immune process could be the upper target of dapagliflozin on cardiac fibrosis.

Endothelial cell and cardiomyocyte alterations in dapagliflozin-treated MI mice

We identified four clusters of ECs (clusters 2, 6, 10, and 12) and then identified two subclusters of ECs via unsupervised clustering: EC1 and EC2 (Figure S6A). The two subclusters strongly expressed the EC marker genes Flt1 and Fabp424 (Figure S6B). DEGs identified in each cluster were analyzed (Figure S6C). The capillary marker genes Kdr and Fabp4 were richer in the EC1 subcluster.32 The EC2 subcluster expressed the endocardial marker gene Npr3, large venous ECs marker Vwf,32 and ECM marker Eln (elastin) higher. We observed an increase in the EC2 subcluster (19.5% in the MI-NS group versus 15.4% in the Sham-NS group) and a decrease in the EC1 level (80.5% in the MI-NS group versus 84.6% in the Sham-NS group) in the MI-NS group, which indicated a reduction in capillary angiogenesis. Dapagliflozin treatment reshuffled the EC1/EC2 proportion (EC1: 87.9%; EC2: 12.1%) (Figure S6D). GO and KEGG enrichment analyses in ECs also confirmed the upregulated ECM process and major histocompatibility complex (MHC)-associated immune process in the MI-NS group. ECM-related pathways such as transforming growth factor β (TGFβ) receptor signaling and platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF) binding signaling were downregulated in the MI-DAPA group (Figures S6E–S6G). These results further confirmed the dominant anti-fibrosis effects of dapagliflozin in MI-induced HF.

We identified only one cluster of cardiomyocytes (cluster 0), which exhibited high expression of the cardiomyocyte marker genes Ryr2, Actc1, Myh6, and Tnnt224 (Figures 5D and S3A). The proportion of cardiomyocytes was remarkably reduced in the MI group (16.4% in the MI-NS group versus 23.4% in the Sham-NS group), which was enhanced by dapagliflozin treatment (30.4%) (Figure 5E). GO enrichment in cardiomyocytes suggested that myocardial structural and contraction-related signaling were upregulated in the MI group, while myocardial hypertrophy-related signals were downregulated, and structural constituents of muscle signal were upregulated in the dapagliflozin treatment group (Figures S7A and S7B). These indicated that dapagliflozin increased the remaining number of cardiomyocytes post MI, reduced compensatory myocardial contraction, and suppressed adverse cardiac hypertrophy.

Dapagliflozin exerts cardio-protection via inhibiting macrophage inflammation

To further explore the anti-fibrosis effects of dapagliflozin on heart, we treated adult mouse cardiac fibroblasts with dapagliflozin. As shown in Figures S8A and S8B, fibroblasts in TGF-β1 group showed significant activation as evidenced by increased mRNA and protein levels of fibrosis markers. However, different dosages of dapagliflozin treatment didn’t suppress the activation of fibroblasts caused by TGF-β1. These suggested that cardiac fibroblasts were not the direct target of dapagliflozin. We then explored the anti-inflammation effects of dapagliflozin on the heart. CD68 immunofluorescence staining results found that in HF mice heart, the level of CD68-labeled macrophages in the whole LV tissue was increased, while dapagliflozin treatment reduced infiltration of CD68-labeled macrophages in both WT and SGLT2-KO HF mice induced by MI and TAC model (Figures S8C and S8D). We also explored the direct effects of dapagliflozin on macrophages. Bone-marrow-derived macrophages (BMDMs) were isolated and induced to M1 macrophages by lipopolysaccharide (LPS). The level of pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-a, IL-1, IL-6) was increased in M1 macrophages (Figure S8E), while dapagliflozin (1 and 10 μM) treatment reduced the secretion of these pro-inflammatory cytokines in macrophages. To explore the cell crosstalk effects of dapagliflozin on cardiac fibrosis, we then co-cultured adult mouse fibroblasts with M1 macrophages with/without dapagliflozin. In fibroblasts co-cultured with M1 macrophages, the expression of those fibrosis markers was increased remarkably, while in fibroblasts co-cultured with dapagliflozin-treated M1 macrophages, the expression of those fibrosis markers was reduced sharply (Figures S8F and S8G). We also used another SGLT2 inhibitor, empagliflozin, to further confirm our hypothesis. Consistently, empagliflozin could inhibit the level of pro-inflammatory cytokines in M1 macrophages. In fibroblasts co-cultured with M1 macrophages, the expression of those fibrosis markers was also suppressed by empagliflozin treatment (Figures S9A and S9B). We then isolated BMDMs from SGLT2-KO mice and treated these cells with SGLT2 inhibitors. We found that both dapagliflozin and empagliflozin could suppress pro-inflammatory function in SGLT2-deficient M1 macrophages and inhibit the crosstalk between SGLT2-deficient M1 macrophages and fibroblasts (Figures S9A and S9B). We did not observe a detectable protein level of SGLT2 in macrophages (Figure S9C); thus, SGLT2 inhibitors reduce activation of M1 macrophages through direct off-target effects. Taken together, these data suggest that the anti-fibrosis effects of dapagliflozin rely on macrophage function inhibition.

Dapagliflozin relies on macrophage inhibition to exert cardio-protection

To further confirm the cardio-protection of dapagliflozin relying on macrophage inhibition, mice were treated with CCR2 antagonist post MI to inhibit macrophages.33,34 The CD68-labeled macrophages in the whole LV tissue were reduced in heart 4 weeks post MI in the two CCR2 antagonist-treated mice (Figure 7A). The infarct size and survival rate showed no significant difference among MI group, CCR2 antagonist group, and CCR2 antagonist + DAPA group (Figures 7B and 7C). CCR2 antagonist protected against cardiac fibrosis and cardiac dysfunction post MI (Figures 7D–7F). The cardio-protection effects were comparable between CCR2 antagonist group and CCR2 antagonist + DAPA group (Figures 7D–7F). Thus, these results further implicate that dapagliflozin could not further exert cardio-protection under macrophages inhibition.

Discussion

Dapagliflozin, an SGLT2 inhibitor, initially used as an anti-diabetes agent and a newly found agent in the past 20 years, reduces the possibility of hospitalization and cardiovascular mortality among HF patients. The specific mechanism underlying the protective effect of dapagliflozin against HF has not been fully elucidated. In this study, we characterized the protective roles of dapagliflozin on MI- and TAC-induced HFrEF in mice. By using SGLT2-KO mice, we further confirmed that its protective effects against HFrEF were independent of SGLT2 inhibition. We, using bulk RNA-seq and single-cell RNA-seq, analyzed multiple cell-type subpopulations found in mouse hearts with MI after dapagliflozin treatment. The results revealed a remarkable amelioration of cardiac fibrosis and the immune process by dapagliflozin treatment. We found that dapagliflozin treatment reshuffled the quiescent/activation FBs leading to suppressed ECM process and fibrosis pathology. We describe a blockage of the immune process in monocytes/macrophages by dapagliflozin treatment may be occurring at the beginning of fibrosis.

Dapagliflozin was originally developed to lower blood glucose levels and has been verified to reduce HF hospitalizations among HF patients without T2D and in T2D patients without established HF.9 Accumulating evidence has demonstrated the protective effects of dapagliflozin on HF patients with preserved or reduced EF with respect to morbidity and mortality.8,9,11 In 2019, McMurray reported that regardless of the diabetes status, dapagliflozin treatment lowered the risk of worsening HF or cardiovascular death among HFrEF patients.8 In 2021, Nassif et al. further confirmed that the symptoms, physical limitations, and exercise function of HFpEF patients improved after dapagliflozin treatment for 12 weeks.13 In our study, we also confirmed the protective effects of dapagliflozin on both MI- and TAC-induced HFrEF. The mechanism underlying the protective effects is complex. As SGLT2 is responsible for reabsorbing ∼97% of urinary glucose in healthy individuals,20 some hypotheses have been posited to explain these protective effects. First, SGLT2 inhibition caused by dapagliflozin treatment may lead to osmotic natriuresis and diuresis, which could reduce LV preload and afterload.35,36 Second, SGLT2 inhibition was certified to reduce the risk of chronic kidney disease20 and might increase erythropoietin and consequently improve cardiac oxygenation.37 Third, the glycosuria effects of dapagliflozin caused increased glucagon and enhanced ketone generation, which, in turn, may lead to improved cardiac energetics.38 However, in our study, we applied SGLT2-KO mice and found that SGLT2 full knockout could not exert any protective effects on either MI- or TAC-induced HFrEF. Moreover, in the absence of SGLT2, dapagliflozin could also exert protective effects in mice against MI- or TAC-induced HFrEF. Our results coincident with a recently published paper by Chen et al., in which they also used SGLT2-KO mice and found that empagliflozin could protect against cardiac ischemia/reperfusion injury (acute cardiac injury model) in these KO mice.23 Thus, the protective effects of dapagliflozin against HFrEF are independent of SGLT2. Moreover, dapagliflozin did not change the blood glucose, serum lipid level, insulin level, and blood ketones in HFrEF mice. Thus, these hypotheses above are indefensible.

Studies have demonstrated that SGLT2 inhibitors could affect various cell types in the heart tissue.39,40,41,42 A study reported that in a healthy heart, dapagliflozin bound with and inhibited Na+/H+ exchanger (NHE) in cardiomyocytes, thus causing vasodilation.40 Philippaert et al. found that empagliflozin, an SGLT2 inhibitor, bound with and inhibited the late sodium channel in cardiomyocytes isolated from HF mice.41 Empagliflozin was found to suppress macrophage inflammation in the heart under LPS insult42 and endothelial cell injury in cardiac ischemia/reperfusion injury.39 Dapagliflozin was also found to suppress cardiac fibroblast inflammation under LPS stimulation43 and cardiac fibrosis in T2D rats.44 The above studies suggest that SGLT2 inhibitors exert protective effects on various cell types in cardiovascular disease. However, the specific cell type and mechanism underlying dapagliflozin on HFrEF is still unclear. In this study, we used bulk RNA-seq to analyze hearts from dapagliflozin-treated mice post MI (infarct + border zone area) and found that downregulated ECM remodeling fibrosis was the predominant alteration in MI-induced HFrEF. The RNA-seq from fibroblasts isolated in MI heart tissue (infarct + border zone area) further confirmed that the ameliorated inflammation process is the onset of changed fibrosis by dapagliflozin. The onset of acute MI induces an original pro-inflammatory response, and an anti-inflammatory reparative phase follows to promote the healing of wounds and formation of scar to avoid heart rupture.26,45 These pathologies are complex, with multiple players, including immune cells, fibroblasts, cardiomyocytes, and endothelial cells. In this study, we used single-cell RNA-seq technology to analyze the transcriptome changes in all cell types 2 weeks post MI (hearts from infarct + border zone area) after dapagliflozin treatment and found that the predominant changes by dapagliflozin treatment were immune cells and fibroblasts. The suppressed number of pro-inflammatory and M2-like macrophages and diminished activating fibroblasts by dapagliflozin treatment were found in our study. These explained the suppressed immune-mediated inflammation and ECM process by dapagliflozin treatment, consistent with our bulk RNA-seq results. Furthermore, the enhanced number of cardiomyocytes and hampered hypertrophy response mediated by dapagliflozin treatment in our study could also account for the ameliorated inflammation post MI. The dapagliflozin-treated cardiac endothelial cells in HFrEF also showed an increase in the number of capillary subclusters and blocked TGFβ and PDGF pro-fibrosis pathways.46 In our study, we did not observe any inhibitory effects of dapagliflozin on cardiac fibroblast activation, whereas we found that dapagliflozin inhibited the pro-inflammation progress of macrophages. And these anti-inflammation effects of dapagliflozin subsequently resulted in suppressed cardiac fibroblast activation. Moreover, dapagliflozin could not exert further cardio-protection under macrophage inhibition. Thus, our study further implicates that by suppressing macrophage pro-inflammation progress, dapagliflozin exerts its anti-fibrosis and cardio-protection effects.

We noticed that the protective effects of dapagliflozin on MI- and TAC- induced HFrEF were independent of SGLT2. In addition, the major cell changes after dapagliflozin treatment were macrophages and fibroblasts during MI-induced HFrEF, which may potentially affect cardiomyocyte pathology. These imply sequential alterations of different cell types caused by dapagliflozin treatment in HF progression. In addition, subtype change of endothelial cells in MI heart by dapagliflozin treatment potentially affected signaling pathways involved in fibrosis. Future studies on the intricacies of specific targets of dapagliflozin on macrophage and cell crosstalk during HFrEF are expected to shed more light on the mechanistic foundation of the protection of dapagliflozin on HFrEF.

Limitations of the study

Several limitations of the present study should be considered. First, in this study, we used MI heart tissue from infarct + border zone to perform RNA sequencing analysis; future study by using the MI hearts from the border zone only or remote region or hearts from pressure-overload-induced HFrEF would also be helpful to further elucidate the mechanism underlying SGLT2 inhibitor’s cardio-protection. Second, RNA-seq analysis was performed in dapagliflozin-treated WT mice. We hope future study could further analyze the top alterations in genomics, metabolomics, and proteomics in SGLT2 inhibitor-treated animals and human samples, as well as in SGLT2-KO mice, to elucidate the target that mediates the cardiovascular protection not confounded by SGLT2i′s effects on SGLT2, for which our work now shows does not play a role. Moreover, we did not observe a detectable SGLT2 protein level in macrophages (Figure S8C); thus, other molecular on macrophages may be the target of dapagliflozin since, by inhibiting macrophages using CCR2 antagonist, dapagliflozin lost its cardio-protective effects. Further studies are required for docking of SGLT2 inhibitor and macrophage cell membrane molecules.

Conclusion

Dapagliflozin ameliorated both MI- and TAC-induced HFrEF. The protective effects of it against chronic HFrEF in a mouse model were independent of SGLT2. According to our bulk RNA-seq and single-cell RNA-seq results (for hearts from infarct + border zone area post MI), the major pathology changes after dapagliflozin treatment were macrophage- and fibroblast-mediated inflammation and fibrosis during MI-induced HFrEF. By suppressing macrophage pro-inflammation progress, dapagliflozin exerts its anti-fibrosis and cardio-protection effects.

All experimental protocols were implemented based on the Guidelines for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals and were authorized by the Animal Care and Use Committee of Renmin Hospital of Wuhan University (IACUC Issue No. WDRM20200704A). Eight-to ten-week-old C57BL/6J male mice were supplied by Beijing Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology Co. Ltd. (Beijing, China). Male and female Slc5a2+/− mice (purchased from SHANGHAI MODEL ORGNISMS, #NM-KO-200777, C57BL/6J background). CRISPR/Cas9 and 2 sgRNAs targeting GCCTGCTCAGGATGCTGTCC and CAACGTAAGAGGGGAGCCGT were used to introduce mutations in exon 4 of Slc5a2, resulting in a shift in the reading frame of the Slc5a2 gene protein and functional loss. Male Slc5a2−/− homozygous (SGLT2-KO) mice and the wild-type (WT) littermates were used in our experiments after gene identification by using polymerase chain reaction with primers: AGGATCGCGTGCTAGCTTAC and GGAATCCTGGGAACTGCACA. Animal model and dapagliflozin treatment

The myocardial infarction (MI) surgery, transverse aortic constriction (TAC) surgery, and their corresponding sham surgeries were performed as previously reported.47,48 Briefly, the chests of the mice were opened after they were anesthetized by sodium pentobarbital (50 mg/kg, ip). The left coronary descending artery was ligated with a 7-0 silk suture after opening the pericardium in the MI surgery. For the TAC surgery, a transverse incision was made along the second and third intercostal spaces to expose the surgical position. The aortic arch between the innominate and left common carotid arteries was tied against a 27-gauge blunted needle using a 7-0 silk suture. The needle was removed immediately after ligation. The arteries of sham surgery mice were subjected to the same procedure without ligation. For postoperative analgesia, animals were administered 0.02 mL buprenorphine (0.3 mg/mL) subcutaneously for 2 days. The aortic pressure gradient was assessed one week after TAC using pulsed wave Doppler echocardiography.49 The mice in the dapagliflozin group were orally administered dapagliflozin (1.50 mg/kg/day, AstraZeneca, USA) 3 days after the MI surgery and 2 weeks after the TAC surgery, while the control group was given the same amount of normal saline (NS). Mice in the MI group were gavaged until 4 weeks after the MI surgery, while those in the TAC group were administrated for 4 weeks. All operations were performed using the blinded approach. Method details

Bulk RNA sequencing and data analysis

TransZol Up Plus RNA Kit (ER501-01, TransGen Biotech, China), RNAClean XP Kit (A63987, Beckman Coulter Inc, USA), and RNase-Free DNase Set (79254, QIAGEN GmBH, Germany) were applied to extract and purify total RNA of left ventricle tissues (infarct+borderzone area) (n = 3 for each group) and adult mouse cardiac fibroblasts (tissues from 5 different mouse hearts were mixed for each sample) isolated from left ventricle tissues (infarct+borderzone area) of MI mice. The purified total RNA was subjected to mRNA separation, fragmentation, first-strand cDNA synthesis, second-strand cDNA synthesis, end repair, and other steps to complete the construction of the sequencing sample library. Later, the library was sequenced with Illumina NovaSeq 6000 sequencer. Strict quality inspection was carried out after each operation to ensure the accuracy of data for further analysis. The filter standard for screening differentially expressed genes (DEGs) was as follows: p ≤ 0.05 and fold-change ≥1.5.

Pathway analysis and gene ontology

DEGs between groups were involved in gene ontology (GO) enrichment and the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analysis to verify the similarity in biological functions in DEGs. The Benjamini-Hochberg method was used to rectify multiple hypothesis testing under both conditions.

Detection of metabolic parameters

All metabolic parameters were measured in fasting mice’s blood and urine (Mice were fasted for 16 h). Blood was collected from tail vein, then blood glucose was detected by using a glucose meter (Sinocare, China). Ketone body concentrations were measured using a ketometer (Abbott, USA). Serum insulin levels were quantitatively analyzed using Insulin ELISA kit (ab277390, Abcam, USA). Serum lipid levels were quantitatively analyzed using the triglyceride and total cholesterol kits (A110-1-1/A111-1-1, Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, China) as previous study performed.50,51 Besides, colorimetric assays (10009582, Cayman Chemical, USA) were used to detect the urine glucose. Urine Na+ measurements were performed using an EasyLyte analyzer (Medica).

Detection of cardiac function

Echocardiography was carried out before and after dapagliflozin administration. The mice were keep warm at 37°C in a warm platform. As previously described,52 the mice were fixed and continuously inhaled 1.5%–2.0% isoflurane to maintain the anesthesia status. A 10-MHz linear array ultrasound transducer and a 30-MHz probe (MS550D; Visual Sonics, Canada) were used to detect the cardiac function parameters such as left ventricular end-systolic diameter (LVESd), left ventricular end-diastolic diameter (LVEDd), left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), and left ventricular fractional shortening (LVFS). In addition, pulsed-wave Doppler echocardiography was performed to detect the distal transverse aortic flow velocity after the TAC surgery, and the aortic pressure after aortic ligation was quantified according to Bernoulli’s equation, i.e., pressure gradient (mmHg) = 4 ∗ peak velocity2 (m/s)2, to ensure successful modeling.49,53 The parasternal long-axis view (PLAX) and parasternal short-axis (PSAX) analysis were used for the echo parameters. Reported tissue velocities were measured in the apical four chamber view at the septal mitral annulus. Transmitral flow patterns were recorded in the apical four chamber view by pulsed-wave Doppler after guidance with colorDoppler.

In our study, a 1.4-French Millar PV catheter (SPR-839, Millar Instruments, USA) and a Power Lab system (AD Instruments Ltd, England) were used to collect the mice’s hemodynamics parameters before their hearts were harvested, such as maximal rate of left ventricular pressure of development (dp/dt max), maximal rate of left ventricular pressure of decline (dp/dt min), and cardiac output (CO). The detailed steps are as reported previously.52

The preparation of single-cell nuclei suspensions

The samples were obtained from the infarcted heart tissue (border and infarct zone regions) of MI mice. Peri-infarct (border-zone) region was defined as the 2-mm area encircling the area of pathologic infarction as previous study performed.54 Five hearts were mixed into one sample for each group. The samples were fully lysed using a lysis buffer (10 mM Tris [pH 7.4], 10 mM NaCl, 3 mM MgCl2, and 0.05% (v/v) NP-40 detergent) and were then filtered by a 30 μm cell strainer. After pelleting, the nuclei were reconstituted in a wash buffer (10 mM Tris [pH 7.4], 10 mM NaCl, 3 mM MgCl2, 1% BSA, 1 mM DTT, RNase inhibitor 1U/μL, nuclease-free water) and combined with 25% optiprep and layered on a 29% optiprep cushion. After centrifugation, the nuclei were reconstituted with the wash buffer again. Finally, the nuclei were resuspended into the nuclei resuspension buffer (nuclei buffer (10XGenomics, 20x) 1x, 1 mM DTT, RNase inhibitor 1U/μL, nuclease-free water) with a concentration of ∼1x10ˆ6/mL, and the single-cell nuclei suspension was prepared.

Single-cell RNA sequencing library preparation and sequencing

Once the extracted nuclei passed the quality test, single-cell RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) libraries were prepared with Chromium Next GEM Single Cell 3ʹ Reagent Kits v3.1 on the Chromium Controller (10 × Genomics). Single-nuclei gel beads in the emulsion (GEMs) were produced as directed by the manufacturer. The released RNA of single nuclei was barcoded in each GEM via reverse transcription. Sequencing was performed with a depth of at least half a million reads/cell and 150 bp paired-end reads (PE150) using the Illumina NovaSeq 6000.

Handling raw data and dimensionality reduction

High-quality cells with more than 500 genes and low mitochondrial ratios (0.20 or less) were retained. The subsequent analysis used genes with at least one characteristic count over three cells. Unique molecular identifiers for individual cell nuclei barcode-gene conjugates were applied to determine gene expression counts. Only qualified barcodes were taken into consideration for further processing. For realizing cell type recognition and unsupervised clustering, principal component analysis (PCA) was carried out to reduce the dimensions of the combined set of samples. Moreover, we adopted Seurat to further reduce the dimension of all 21583 cells and applied t-distributed stochastic neighbor embedding (t-SNE) to project cells into a two-dimensional space to visualize the data.

Clustering and cell-type recognition

We exploited Louvain community detection to the nearest neighbor graph built in the PCA space to clarify a cluster division. To deduce the types of cells, a neural network classifier was trained to predict cell ontology classes given single-cell mRNA abundance patterns. SingleR (version 4.0.4) containing Sham-NS, MI-NS, and MI-DAPA datasets was used to automatically identify cell types. Beyond these notations, cell state notations were supplied manually to SingleR to provide a level of granularity below cell ontology classes.

Cell isolation and culture

Neonatal rat cardiomyocyte (NRCM) and adult mouse fibroblast were isolated and cultured as described previously.55 Simply put, the ventricle of 1- to 3-day-old Sprague-Dawley rats were chopped after washing with Dulbecco’s modified eagle medium (DMEM)/F12 (11885092, GIBCO, USA). The ventricle tissue was then digested after 10–15 min at 36°C with 0.125% trypsin solution (25200, GIBCO, USA) and repeated five times for full digestion. The supernatant was then collected and added to the ending digestion solution to terminate the digestion. After the above mixed solution was filtered and centrifuged, the supernatant was removed, and the precipitated cells were resuspended with DMEM/F12 containing 15% fetal bovine serum (FBS, 10099, GIBCO, USA). Finally, the resuspended cells were seeded into six-well plates after fibroblast removal. For adult mouse fibroblast isolation, the other steps were similar to the NRCM extraction method. However, the composition of the digestive solution, the pore diameter of the filter screen, and fibroblast adherence time were modified.

After synchronization in DMEM/F12 containing 10% FBS, adult mouse fibroblast were randomly treated with dapagliflozin (10 μM dissolved in DMSO, SML2804, Sigma, USA) with or without TGF-β1 (10 ng/mL, 100-21-2, Peprotech, USA) stimulation for 48 h. Finally, cells were collected for western blot, quantitative real-time PCR.

Macrophage culture and macrophage -fibroblast co-culture

Isolation, culture, and polarization of bone-marrow-derived macrophages (BMDMs) were performed as previous study performed.56,57 BMDMs were induced to M1 polarization by incubating with lipopolysaccharide (LPS, 100 ng/mL) for 24 h. The supernatants of M1 macrophages with and without dapagliflozin (0.1 μM, 1 μM, 10 μM) treatment (simultaneously for 24 h) were collected for detection of pro-inflammation marker (TNF-a, IL-1, IL-6). The pro-inflammation marker ELISA kits were obtained from Shanghai Enzyme-linked Biotechnology Co., Ltd.

For co-cultivation of macrophages and cardiac fibroblasts, 0.4 μm transwell inserts were used as previous study reported.58 For macrophage -fibroblast co-culture system, the cells were divided into the following 4 groups: 1) the cardiac fibroblasts (CFs) control group, in which CFs were co-cultured with non-induced macrophages; 2) the DAPA-treated macrophage group, in which CFs were co-cultured with non-induced macrophages with dapagliflozin treatment (10 μM); 3) the M1 group, in which CFs were co-cultured with M1 macrophages (incubating with LPS (100 ng/mL)); 4) the M1-DAPA group, in which CFs were co-cultured with M1 macrophages with dapagliflozin treatment (10 μM). All cells were co-cultured for 24 h.

Real-time PCR and Western blot

Real-time PCR and western blot were performed in accordance with previous protocols.59 The PCR primers and antibodies involved in this study are listed in Tables S6 and S7, respectively.

Histological analysis

After the mice were euthanized, the heart tissue was removed quickly, soaked in formalin, and then prepared into paraffin-imbedded sections. For evaluating the cardiomyocyte morphology, the levels of inflammation, and fibrosis in myocardial tissue, the hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining, picrosirius red (PSR) staining, and Masson staining were performed based on the previous manufacturer’s instructions.60 Moreover, triphenyltetrazolium chloride (TTC) staining was performed to assess the size of MI. Briefly, for each mouse, the fresh heart was quickly frozen after removal from the body, so it could be easily cut into five equal pieces. Every piece of heart was placed in a preheated TTC solution (T8877, Sigma, USA) for 10 min at 37°C. Finally, the shape of the heart piece was adjusted under the microscope and then soaked in a formalin solution to maintain the shape. Images were captured within 24 h. The Image Pro Plus 6.0 software was applied to quantify the staining results.

Immunofluorescence staining

Briefly, MI and TAC heart samples were incubated with CD68 (A15037, Abclonal, USA) at 4°C overnight. After clear washing with PBS, they were incubated with Alexa Fluor 488 donkey anti-rabbit secondary antibody (A-21206, Invitrogen, USA) for 1 h at room temperature. Eventually, DAPI (S36939, Invitrogen, USA) for nuclear staining for 3 min.

Quantification and statistical analysis

The SPSS 23.0 software was applied for statistical analysis. The quantitative data are all expressed in terms of mean ± standard deviation. Normal distribution was tested with Shapiro-Wilk test. All quantitative data were normal distributed. After the homogeneity test of variance, the Student’s t test was used for comparison between two groups, and one-way ANOVA was used to compare three groups or above if not specifically mentioned. Two-way ANOVA was used to compare TAC experiment groups and MI experiment groups. Survival rates were determined using the Kaplan-Meier method and compared using log rank tests. Experimenters were blinded to the treatment and genotypes of animals. When p < 0.05, the difference was regarded as statistically significant.

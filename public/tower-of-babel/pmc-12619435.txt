Dynamic tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes predicts survival in HR+/HER2− early breast cancer: a pre-to-post neoadjuvant chemotherapy study
Authors: Yibin Qiu, Renren Zhang, Shunyi Liu, Long Wu, Yali Wang, Weifeng Cai, Peng He, Qindong Cai, Yuxiang Lin, Wenhui Guo, Chuan Wang, Jie Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
IntroductionThe prognostic implications of tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) and their temporal changes (ΔTILs) during neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NAC) in hormone receptor-positive/ HER2-negative early breast cancer (HR+/HER2− eBC) remains clinically ambiguous. Our study investigates the association between TILs levels, longitudinal TILs evolution, and survival outcomes in a NAC-treated HR+/HER2− eBC cohort.MethodsIn this retrospective cohort analysis of 576 HR+/HER2- eBC patients (April 2011-December 2021), TILs were categorized as low (< 10%) or high (≥ 10%) using predefined thresholds. Multivariable Cox proportional hazards models evaluated invasive disease-free survival (iDFS) and overall survival (OS). ΔTILs patterns were analyzed in residual tumors.ResultsThe cohort (median follow-up 68.1 months) comprised 393 TILs-low (68.2%) and 183 TILs-high (31.8%) tumors. Compared to TILs-low tumors, TILs-high tumors were associated with a higher Ki-67 index (≥ 20%) (P = 0.022), even though they were more frequently of smaller baseline size (< 3 cm) (P = 0.022). Elevated pre-NAC TILs independently predicted inferior iDFS (HR = 1.47, 95% CI 1.06–2.04, P = 0.021) and OS (HR = 1.64, 95% CI 1.11–2.43, P = 0.013). Post-NAC TILs escalation (low to high) conferred the worse prognosis versus sustained low TILs (iDFS: HR = 2.97, 95% CI 1.93–4.55, P < 0.001; OS: HR = 3.43, 95% CI 2.02–5.85, P < 0.001). Conversely, TILs reduction (high to low) correlated with improved survival over persistent high TILs (iDFS: HR = 0.51, 95% CI 0.30–0.86, P = 0.012; OS: HR = 0.54, 95%CI 0.31–0.96, P = 0.034).ConclusionIn HR+/HER2− eBC, elevated pre-treatment TILs are independently associated with inferior survival outcomes, while chemotherapy-induced TILs reduction in residual disease correlates with significant prognostic improvement.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13058-025-02150-2.

Introduction

Breast cancer (BC) remains the most prevalent female malignancy worldwide, with the hormone receptor-positive/HER2-negative (HR+/HER2−) tumors representing about 70% of cases [1, 2]. Although neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NAC) is well-established for HER2-positive (HER2+) and triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) [3], its utility in HR+/HER2− disease remains controversial primarily due to two critical limitations: (1) the inability to predict which patients derive survival benefit from NAC [4], and (2) the lack of sufficient evidence for validated biomarkers to guide post-NAC therapy intensification [5]. Therefore, the central challenge lies in identifying dynamic prognostic factors that elucidate NAC-induced tumor evolution to optimize adjuvant decision-making, which is essential for transforming NAC from a debated option into precision therapy for this specific population.

The tumor immune microenvironment (TME), particularly tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs), has emerged as a critical determinant of therapeutic response in aggressive BC subtypes. In HER2 + and TNBC subtypes, robust TILs infiltration is consistently associated with higher rates of pathological complete response (pCR) and improved survival following NAC [6]. In contrast, the immunological role of TILs in HR+/HER2− disease remains poorly defined and contentious. Current literature reveals conflicting evidence: for instance, while Denkert et al. [7] reported that elevated TILs levels were associated with increased pCR rates, a subsequent meta-analyses by Li et al. [8] found no significant predictive value of TILs for pCR in this subtype. Further adding to the discrepancy, Qian et al. [9] demonstrated that TILs ≥ 10% were an independent predictor for lower non-response rates in HR+/HER2− BC. Prognostically, the association between TILs levels and clinical outcomes remains inconsistent across studies. While some have linked higher TILs to favorable outcomes [10, 11], others have reported an association with unfavorable outcomes [7, 12]. Furthermore, several series have found no significant association [13–15].

This biological conundrum may stem from subtype-specific immune contexture differences. HR+/HER2− tumors typically exhibit lower baseline TILs infiltration compared to HER2 + or TNBC [16], raising questions about the applicability of established TILs thresholds across molecular subtypes. Furthermore, the prognostic implications of chemotherapy-induced TILs dynamics (ΔTILs)—a well-established biomarker in HER2 + and TNBC [8, 17] —remain yet to be thoroughly explored in HR+/HER2− malignancies.

To address these knowledge gaps, our study investigates two critical dimensions of TILs biology in HR+/HER2− early breast cancer (eBC): (1) the prognostic impact of baseline and post-chemotherapy TILs infiltration, and (2) the clinical implications of ΔTILs. Through standardized longitudinal profiling, we aim to elucidate the temporal evolution of tumor-immune interactions and establish dynamic prognostic factors to guide post-neoadjuvant therapeutic strategies in this immunologically distinct subtype.

Materials and methods

Patients

A total of 576 consecutive patients newly diagnosed with primary invasive HR+/HER2− eBC who received NAC followed by curative-intent surgery at our institution between April 2011 and December 2021 were included in the primary cohort. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (a) female patients aged 18 years or older; (b) histologically confirmed HR+/HER2− BC; (c) stage II-III disease according to the American Joint Committee on Cancer staging system [18]; (d) completed a full course of NAC; and (e) available clinicopathological data before and after NAC. Patients were excluded if they: (a) had HER2 + or TNBC subtypes; (b) had received any prior treatment for BC (e.g., surgery, chemotherapy, or endocrine therapy); (c) presented with distant metastasis at diagnosis (stage IV) or developed distant metastasis during NAC; (d) had incomplete clinicopathological or follow-up data; or (e) had other malignant diseases or severe comorbidities that could influence survival or treatment response. To address potential overfitting and validate the robustness of our findings, we performed a separate validation using a consecutive cohort of 210 HR+/HER2− eBC patients treated at our institution in 2022. Given the limited number of events (6 death events) in this cohort, the validation analysis was focused on invasive disease-free survival (iDFS). A flowchart of the study is shown in Fig. S1. Clinicopathological and treatment data were extracted from the hospital’s medical records. The treatment regimen was anthracycline- and taxane-based for all patients. Further, participants in our study received adjuvant endocrine therapy. Eligible patients underwent postoperative radiotherapy. The median follow-up duration for the primary cohort was 68.1 months. The study was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee of Fujian Medical University Union Hospital (Approval number 2025KY071), and written informed consent was obtained from all the participants.

Pathological assessment

Tumor histology was classified according to the Nottingham grading system of breast tumors [19]. HR status and HER2 expression were assessed using immunohistochemistry (IHC) according to standard protocols. HR + was defined as ≥ 1% of tumor cells showing nuclear staining for estrogen receptor (ER) and/or progesterone receptor (PR) [20]. HER2 status was evaluated by IHC and Fluorescence In Situ Hybridization (FISH) when needed, based on the American Society of Clinical Oncology/College of American Pathologists (ASCO/CAP) HER2 testing guidelines [20–22]. HER2-low status was defined as HER2 IHC 1 + or 2 + with negative FISH, whereas tumors scored as IHC 0 were classified as HER2-zero. The Ki-67 proliferation index was assessed and expressed as the percentage of positively stained tumor cells. Luminal A tumors were defined as Ki-67 < 20% and PR ≥ 20%, while Luminal B tumors were defined as Ki-67 ≥ 20% and/or PR < 20%. The evaluation criterion for pCR was the absence of invasive cancer cells in the breast and axillary lymph nodes, regardless of ductal carcinoma in situ (ypTis/0ypN0). In addition, the Residual Cancer Burden (RCB) index was calculated for all patients who underwent surgery after NAC, using established criteria that incorporate primary tumor size, cellularity, lymph node involvement, and the number of positive lymph nodes [23]. Patients were categorized into RCB-0 (equivalent to pCR), RCB-I, RCB-II and RCB-III based on the extent of residual disease (RD) after NAC.

Assessment of TILs

TILs were assessed on hematoxylin and eosin (H&E)-stained full-face sections from both pre-treatment core biopsies and post-treatment surgical specimens (Fig. 1), following the guidelines of the International TILs Working Group [24]. The evaluation focused on the stromal area within the borders of the invasive tumor, where the percentage of the area occupied by mononuclear immune cells was recorded, excluding regions of necrosis and fibrosis. Two experienced pathologists, blinded to patient outcomes, independently scored the TILs, and any discrepancies were resolved through consensus. For the analysis, TILs were evaluated both as continuous variables (expressed as both 5% and 10% increments) and as a dichotomized variable using a primary cut-off of 10%, which categorized patients into TILs-low (< 10%) and TILs-high (≥ 10%) groups. To test the robustness of this pre-specified cut-off and address potential methodological concerns, comprehensive sensitivity analyses using alternative thresholds (5% and 20%) were also performed (Table S1). Furthermore, among patients with RD following NAC, we additionally analyzed the relative change in TILs, calculated as (post-NAC TILs – pre-NAC TILs) / pre-NAC TILs × 100% (%ΔTILs), as a continuous variable. Additionally, patients with RD were categorized into four groups based on the changes of TILs levels pre- and post-NAC: ΔTILs low to low, ΔTILs low to high, ΔTILs high to high and ΔTILs high to low.

Multiplex immunofluorescence (mIHC)

To microscopically characterize the dynamics of TME in HR+/HER2− BC before and after NAC, we performed a five-color mIHC assay on ten paired pre- and post-NAC samples from patients with high baseline TILs (≥ 10%). The cohort comprised an equal number of cases with pCR and non-pCR (RCB-III). Consecutive tissue sections were stained using Opal™ tyramide signal amplification technology with a panel targeting Pan-CK (to identify tumor cells), CD8 (cytotoxic T cells), CD68 (total macrophages), CD163 (M2-like macrophages), and DAPI (nuclear counterstain). Whole-slide multiplex images were acquired using a Vectra® Polaris™ automated imaging system and subsequently analyzed with the KFSlideOS digital pathology platform.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were summarized as means ± standard deviations or medians with interquartile ranges, depending on the distribution, while categorical variables were expressed as frequencies and percentages. Differences between groups were compared using the Chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables, and the t-test or Mann-Whitney U test for continuous variables, as appropriate. The relationship between TILs levels and pathological response was assessed using univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses. For survival analysis, iDFS and overall survival (OS) were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method, with differences between groups assessed by the log-rank test. In accordance with the NeoSTEEP guidelines [25], iDFS was defined as the time interval from surgery to locoregional recurrence, distant metastasis, or death from any cause. OS was defined as the time from surgery to the last follow-up date or death, regardless of the cause. To further evaluate the independent prognostic significance of TILs and other clinicopathological factors, multivariate Cox proportional hazards models were employed. For the multivariable regression analysis, variable selection was not based solely on statistical significance but also on clinical importance. Consequently, all key clinical and pathological factors were included in the multivariable regression model. All analyses in our study were conducted using R software version 4.2.3. All the statistical tests were two-sided, and P-values < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Clinicopathological characteristics of patients and tumors

The primary cohort comprised 576 HR+/HER2 − eBC patients with distinct TILs profiles: 393 (68.2%) TILs-low (< 10%) and 183 (31.8%) TILs-high (≥ 10%) tumors. Comparative analysis revealed that a significantly higher proportion of patients with pre-NAC TILs-high tumors had a Ki-67 index of ≥ 20% (79.8% vs. 70.7%, P = 0.022) and a baseline tumor size of < 3 cm (45.4% vs. 35.4%, P = 0.022), while maintaining comparable distributions of age, menopausal status, histological features, and nodal involvement (Table 1). TILs and pathological response

In the primary cohort, 5.5% of patients (n = 32) achieved pCR following surgery. No significant difference in pre-NAC TILs levels was observed between patients with and without pCR (P = 0.362; Fig. 2B). The waterfall plot illustrates the percentage change in TILs density from baseline stratified by RCB class in patients with RD (n = 544; RCB-I: n = 28, RCB-II: n = 319, RCB-III: n = 197) (Fig. 2C). Multivariable logistic regression identified histologic grade (P = 0.01) and cN status (P < 0.001) as independent predictors of pathological response, while pre-NAC TILs levels showed no predictive value (Table S2).

Prognostic value of pre- and post-NAC TILs with respect to survival outcomes

With median follow-up of 68.1 months (Q1–Q3: 48.0–95.0 months), survival disparities emerged across TILs subgroups. Pre-NAC TILs-low patients demonstrated superior iDFS (P = 0.012) and OS (P = 0.0079) compared to TILs-high counterparts (Fig. 3A, B). This prognostic pattern intensified post-NAC, where residual TILs-low tumors exhibited markedly improved iDFS (P < 0.0001) and OS (P < 0.0001) versus TILs-high residuals (Fig. S2A, B).

Multivariable Cox regression analyses showed that pre-NAC TILs-high status independently predicted 47% increased iDFS risk (Hazard Ratio (HR) = 1.47, 95% Confidence Interval (CI) :1.06–2.04, P = 0.021) and 64% higher OS mortality (HR = 1.64, 95%CI:1.11–2.43, P = 0.013) (Table 2). Post-NAC TILs-high status demonstrated stronger prognostic discrimination, associated with 156% elevated iDFS hazard (HR = 2.56, 95% CI:1.85–3.55, P < 0.001) and 182% greater OS risk (HR = 2.82, 95%CI:1.92–4.14, P < 0.001). When modeled as a continuous variable, post-NAC TILs density showed no significant association with iDFS or OS in multivariate analyses, regardless of whether a 5% (iDFS: HR = 1.01, 95% CI: 0.96–1.07, P = 0.681; OS: HR = 1.02, 95% CI: 0.97–1.08, P = 0.403) or 10% increment (iDFS: HR = 1.02, 95% CI: 0.92–1.14, P = 0.681; OS: HR = 1.05, 95% CI: 0.94–1.18, P = 0.403) was used (Table S3).

Prognostic value of changes in TILs level after NAC

Analysis of dynamic changes revealed that among the 170 patients with high pre-NAC TILs, 90 (52.9%) cases experienced a decrease to low levels post-treatment, while 80 (47.1%) cases maintained high TILs. Conversely, of the 374 patients with low pre-NAC TILs, 272 (72.7%) remained low and 102 (27.3%) converted to high TILs after NAC, resulting in a slight overall increase in the proportion of patients with high TILs from 31.2 to 33.5% (Fig. 2D).

Multivariate survival analyses involving 544 patients with RD showed that ΔTILs status was an independently prognostic factor for iDFS and OS. In the four-group multivariate Cox analyses using ΔTILs low to low as the reference, this group was associated with the most favorable prognosis. All other groups showed significantly increased risk relative to the reference: ΔTILs low to high (iDFS: HR = 2.88, 95% CI: 1.90–4.37, P < 0.001; OS: HR = 3.45, 95% CI: 2.05–5.81, P < 0.001), ΔTILs high to high (iDFS: HR = 3.17, 95% CI: 2.03–4.95, P < 0.001; OS: HR = 4.72, 95% CI: 2.77–8.02, P < 0.001), and ΔTILs high to low (iDFS: HR = 1.68, 95% CI: 1.03–2.65, P = 0.038; OS: HR = 2.57, 95% CI: 1.44–4.58, P = 0.001) (Fig. 3C, D; Table 3). Patients sustaining low TILs levels throughout treatment (ΔTILs low to low) demonstrated optimal outcomes, showing 197% lower iDFS risk (HR = 2.97, 95%CI:1.93–4.55, P < 0.001) and 243% reduced OS mortality (HR = 3.43, 95%CI:2.02–5.85, P < 0.001) compared to those with TILs escalation (ΔTILs low to high) (Fig. 3E, F; Table 3). Notably, chemotherapy-induced TILs reduction (ΔTILs high to low) conferred significant survival advantage over persistent high TILs status (ΔTILs high to high), with 49% iDFS risk reduction (HR = 0.51, 95%CI:0.30–0.86, P = 0.012) and 46% lower OS mortality (HR = 0.54, 95%CI:0.31–0.96, P = 0.034) (Fig. 3G, H; Table 3). When %ΔTILs ((post-NAC TILs – pre-NAC TILs) / pre-NAC TILs × 100%) was included as a continuous variable in the multivariable Cox regression model, it was not significantly associated with survival outcomes, yielding a HR of 1.01 (95% CI: 0.99–1.04, P = 0.322) for iDFS and a HR of 1.02 (95% CI: 0.99–1.04, P = 0.298) for OS.

Validation of TILs-based prognostic stratification

The prognostic significance of TILs assessment—including pre-NAC TILs, post-NAC TILs, and ΔTILs—was further validated in a consecutive cohort of patients from our center treated in 2022. Consistent with the primary cohort findings, all three TILs metrics demonstrated similar stratification patterns for iDFS in the validation cohort. Specifically, Kaplan-Meier analysis confirmed that patients with low pre-NAC TILs had significantly better iDFS than those with high pre-NAC TILs (P = 0.025). This prognostic superiority of low TILs status was more pronounced when assessed post-NAC, with the low post-NAC TILs group exhibiting significantly improved iDFS compared to the high post-NAC TILs group (P < 0.0001). Analysis of TILs dynamics using a four-category model revealed significant differences in iDFS among the groups (P = 0.00016), with the sustained low TILs (ΔTILs low to low) group demonstrating the most favorable outcome (Fig. S3A, C).

In multivariable Cox regression models adjusted for clinicopathological factors, a high pre-NAC TILs level remained an independent predictor of inferior iDFS (HR = 1.84, 95% CI 1.07–3.18, P = 0.028). The association was strengthened in the post-NAC assessment, where high post-NAC TILs were associated with significantly worse iDFS (HR = 2.79, 95% CI 1.58–4.94, P < 0.001). When evaluating TILs dynamics, all three dynamic change patterns—ΔTILs low to high (HR = 3.11, 95% CI 1.46–6.64, P = 0.003), ΔTILs high to high (HR = 3.26, 95% CI 1.52–7.02, P = 0.002), and ΔTILs high to low (HR = 2.59, 95% CI 1.12-6.00, P = 0.026)—were independently associated with increased risk of iDFS events compared to the sustained low (ΔTILs low to low) reference group.

Exploratory analysis in molecular subtypes: luminal A versus luminal B

In the Luminal A subset (n = 130), neither baseline TILs, post-NAC TILs, nor the ΔTILs demonstrated a significant association with survival outcomes (all P > 0.05). In contrast, among Luminal B patients (n = 446), higher pre-NAC TILs levels were significantly associated with poorer iDFS (HR = 1.45, 95%CI:1.02–2.07, P = 0.040) and OS (HR = 1.60, 95%CI:1.05–2.43, P = 0.028). Similarly, elevated post-NAC TILs were also strongly correlated with worse iDFS (HR = 2.87, 95%CI:2.01–4.10, P < 0.001) and OS (HR = 2.82, 95%CI:1.85–4.30, P < 0.001). Dynamic assessment of TILs levels further underscored this prognostic relationship: patients exhibiting an increase from low to high TILs (ΔTILs low to high) showed significantly elevated risk relative to those with persistently low TILs (iDFS: HR = 2.98, 95%CI:1.89–4.71, P < 0.001; OS: HR = 3.71, 95%CI:2.12–6.53, P < 0.001). Conversely, a reduction from high to low TILs (ΔTILs high to low) was associated with improved survival compared to persistently high TILs (iDFS: HR = 0.37, 95%CI:0.21–0.68, P = 0.001; OS: HR = 0.43, 95%CI:0.23–0.79, P = 0.007) (Table S5).

Exploratory mIHC analysis of TME

In the representative patient who achieved pCR, the pre-treatment biopsy exhibited an immune-rich tumor microenvironment (Fig. S4A). The post-treatment specimen from the former tumor bed, now devoid of viable tumor cells, showed stromal remodeling with a dispersed immune cell presence (Fig. S4B), consistent with a resolved anti-tumor immune response. Strikingly, a divergent pattern was observed in the representative non-responder with RD (RCB-III). While the pre-treatment biopsy similarly displayed high lymphocytic infiltration (Fig. S4C), the post-NAC residual tumor revealed a profoundly altered immune geography. Qualitative assessment of the mIHC images indicated a distinct spatial partitioning of immune subsets: CD8 + cytotoxic T cells were predominantly localized to the stromal periphery of the tumor bed, whereas CD68 + CD163 + M2-like macrophages densely infiltrated the central tumor cell nests (Fig. S4D).

Discussion

Our findings unveil TILs as dynamic prognostic navigators rather than predictive biomarkers for chemotherapy response in HR+/HER2− eBC. This contrasts sharply with HER2 + and TNBC subtypes, where elevated TILs (typically >20–30%) correlate with both improved pCR rates and survival benefits [6]. The immunobiological characteristics in HR+/HER2− tumors manifests through three key observations: (1) characteristically sparse baseline lymphocytic infiltration (< 10% in 68.2% of our cohort), (2) prognostic-but-not-predictive value of the standardized 10% TILs threshold for NAC benefit, and (3) the counterintuitive association between elevated TILs (≥ 10%) and inferior survival outcomes.

In our primary cohort of HR+/HER2− eBC patients, we observed a pCR rate of 5.6%, which is consistent with the characteristically low rates (0–18%) reported for this subtype [26]. Regarding the association between TILs and pathological response, we found no significant association between higher TILs levels and increased likelihood of achieving pCR. This finding contrasts with that of Qian et al. [9], who identified TILs ≥ 10% as an independent predictor of lower non-response rates in this subtype. The discrepancy may stem from methodological variations, including differences in cohort size and limited statistical power due to the low incidence of pCR events across studies. Our results are consistent with a recent meta-analysis by Li et al. [8], which concluded that although TILs may identify a small pCR-enriched population within luminal disease, their predictive utility is substantially weaker in HR + BC than in TNBC or HER2 + subtypes and remains inconsistent at the population level. Taken together, these observations suggest that the attenuated relationship in HR + disease may reflect its intrinsic lower chemosensitivity and a distinct immune context. Consequently, future studies should extend beyond mere TILs quantification to incorporate functional immune profiling, stromal characteristics, and tumor-intrinsic features in order to build more reliable predictive models of chemotherapy response in this challenging subtype.

The prognostic significance of dynamics TILs observed in our study may be mechanistically explained by the distinctive TME architecture of HR+/HER2− tumors. Unlike the “hot” immunogenic microenvironments of HER2 + and TNBC [6], HR+/HER2− tumors tend to exhibit “non-productive inflammation”—a self-perpetuating state featuring defective antigen presentation (MHC-I downregulation), PD-L1 + regulatory T-cell expansion [27] and endocrine-driven immunosuppression via ERα-mediated inhibition of CXCL9/10 chemokines [28]. Notably, the clinical utility of chemotherapy in modulating this microenvironment is demonstrated by the 52.9% conversion rate from baseline TILs-high to post-NAC TILs-low status—a transition associated with 49% reduction in iDFS risk. This may suggest chemotherapy’s capacity to reset the immunosuppressive TME in responsive subsets, though with critical contextual duality: while eliminating chemosensitive clones [29], it simultaneously enriches resistant populations through Warburg effect-driven metabolic competition [30] and TIM-3/LAG-3-mediated T-cell exhaustion [31].

Current prognostic paradigms in HR+/HER2− eBC predominantly rely on tumor-intrinsic biomarkers like Oncotype DX Recurrence Score [32] and Prosigna PAM50 [33]. While these genomic tools excel in predicting distant metastasis risk, they exhibit three critical limitations: (1) inability to capture dynamic TME remodeling during treatment, (2) limited utility in guiding post-chemotherapy immune modulation strategies, and (3) poor correlation with locoregional immune escape mechanisms. Even dynamic proliferation markers like Ki67—often used alongside genomic assays—fail to address these gaps. Although Ki67 tracks temporal changes in tumor proliferation (e.g., ≥ 30% reduction post-NAC), it lacks specificity to immune-adaptive resistance mechanisms driving recurrence [34]. In contrast, dynamic TILs profiling transcends these limitations by integrating stromal-immune crosstalk into prognostic assessment. ΔTILs outperforms both static genomic tools and dynamic Ki67 analysis, identifying chemotherapy-sensitive subsets (49% iDFS improvement with TILs depletion) and high-risk residual disease (3.4-fold mortality with persistent TILs elevation). This dual capacity enables biologically tailored adjuvant strategies unattainable through proliferation-centric metrics. Our TILs-based stratification model addresses these limitations by integrating host immune responses into prognostic assessment.

Of note, while our study did not demonstrate a significant association between TILs density and pCR rates, our exploratory mIHC revealed critical spatial reorganization of the immune microenvironment in treatment-resistant tumors. We observed a distinct compartmentalization of immune cell subsets wherein CD68 + CD163 + M2 macrophages formed dense clusters within tumor epithelial compartments while CD8 + T cells were predominantly excluded to the stromal periphery. This spatial segregation supports an “immune exclusion” model where immunosuppressive myeloid cells create a protective barrier around tumor cells [35, 36], potentially explaining the divergent clinical outcomes among patients with similar high TILs levels at baseline. The strategic positioning of M2 macrophages enables direct delivery of suppressive cytokines while preventing T cell infiltration, providing a mechanistic basis for the poor prognosis associated with persistent post-NAC TILs-high status. These findings highlight the importance of evaluating spatial architecture beyond mere immune cell quantification and suggest a potential therapeutic framework in which TILs depletion may help identify candidates for conventional adjuvant therapy, while TILs persistence could support the exploration of combinatorial strategies targeting ER signaling (CDK4/6 inhibitors) [37] and adaptive immune checkpoints (LAG-3/TIM-3 blockade) [31]. Future studies with larger cohorts are warranted to validate these spatial patterns and explore their utility in guiding personalized combination therapies.

In addition, this mechanistic framework, derived from our overall cohort analysis, necessitates refinement when considering intrinsic molecular heterogeneity. Our exploratory prognostic analysis of the Luminal A and B subgroups indicates that the prognostic value of TILs dynamics appears to be more pronounced in Luminal B BC, while its significance in Luminal A disease remains uncertain given our limited sample size and follow-up. This finding differs somewhat from the report by Miglietta et al. [38], which demonstrated that high TILs levels were associated with higher recurrence scores in both Luminal A and Luminal B subtypes, albeit with a more pronounced effect in Luminal B tumors. The emerging contrast between these studies underscores the complex interplay between intrinsic subtype and immune response, suggesting that the ominous prognosis of persistent TILs-high status may be driven primarily by Luminal B biology. Future studies should further explore fundamental differences in the tumor immune microenvironment between Luminal A and Luminal B tumors and elucidate how these variations may influence the adaptive immune resistance mechanisms that ultimately dictate clinical outcomes.

Methodological considerations may explain discrepancies with studies reporting favorable TILs prognosis in HR+/HER2− BC [11]. Conventional TILs quantification thresholds (e.g., 20% cutoff optimized for TNBC) become biologically incongruent in this subtype, where median TILs levels rarely exceed 10% [11]. Our findings challenge the oversimplified “more TILs, better outcomes” dogma [6, 8], advocating for subtype-specific interpretation frameworks that account for both quantitative and spatial TILs characteristics. Given the characteristically sparse lymphocytic infiltration in HR+/HER2 − tumors [16], the dynamic analyses of TILs in this subtype may be even more important. In the meanwhile, our study has several limitations that warrant consideration. The retrospective design and reliance on H&E-based TILs assessment restrict our ability to derive mechanistic insights. Furthermore, the relatively small sample size may limit the statistical power of some subgroup analyses and increase the potential for type II errors. Although we have addressed the need for validation through an internal temporal validation cohort, future multi-institutional studies with larger sample sizes are essential to confirm the generalizability of our TILs-based prognostic model across diverse patient populations and clinical settings. Nevertheless, these technical and methodological constraints also reflect real-world clinical practice, and the consistent findings underscore the translational relevance of our approach. While emerging multi-omics platforms can map TME complexity [39], their high cost and analytical requirements hinder routine implementation. Future research efforts should focus on spatial transcriptomics to decode the functional heterogeneity of TILs, distinguishing exhausted CD8 + T-cell populations from FOXP3 + regulatory T-cell subsets [40], while mapping their spatial interplay with chemotherapy-persistent tumor niches. Notably, recent evidence demonstrates promising synergy between neoadjuvant chemotherapy and immunotherapy in HR+/HER2- BC, with dual therapy achieving significant higher pCR rates compared to chemotherapy alone [41], suggesting immunochemotherapy combinations may overcome endocrine resistance in this subtype.

Conclusion

Our study establishes pre-NAC TILs-high status (≥ 10%) as independent adverse prognostic factors in HR+/HER2 − eBC. Sustained TILs-high levels throughout NAC correlate with dismal survival, while NAC-induced TILs reduction predicts improvement in prognosis. These findings highlight the clinical relevance of monitoring TILs dynamics during NAC, suggesting distinct immune microenvironment remodeling patterns that may guide post-neoadjuvant therapeutic strategies.

Knots not detected by any trace
Kenneth L. Baker, Marc Kegel, Kimihiko Motegi

Abstract
. The first and last named authors have demonstrated the existence of knots for which every integral slope is non-characterizing. In this short note, we extend this result in two ways. There exists a knot that shares for every integer n n the same n n -trace with infinitely many mutually distinct knots. Moreover, every knot is concordant to a knot that is not detected by any of its traces.

1.
Introduction

The
n
-
trace

X_{n}(K)
of a knot
K
is the oriented, smooth
4
-manifold obtained by attaching a
2
-handle to the
4
–ball
D^{4}
attached along
K
with framing
n
. We say that
K
is
detected
by its
n
-trace, if whenever
X_{n}(K)
is orientation-preservingly diffeomorphic to
X_{n}(K^{\prime})
for another knot
K^{\prime}
, then
K^{\prime}
is isotopic to
K
.

Question 1.9 from
[
BS25
]
asks
if every knot is detected by at least one of its traces.
In this short note, we answer this question in the negative,
demonstrating that this is quite far from the case.

Theorem 1.1

There exists a knot
K
such that for every integer
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, there exist an infinite family of mutually distinct knots
(K^{k}_{n})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that each
K^{k}_{n}
is distinct from
K
but its
n
-trace
X_{n}(K^{k}_{n})
is orientation-preservingly diffeomorphic to
X_{n}(K)
.

The knots used to prove Theorem
1.1
admit annulus presentations that induce ribbon immersions of once-punctured tori. Hence these knots have
4
-genus at most one.
In contrast, our next result demonstrates the existence of knots
with arbitrarily large
4
-genus that are not detected by any of their traces.

Theorem 1.2

For any knot
J
, there is a knot
J^{\prime}
concordant to
J
that is not detected by any of its traces. Furthermore, we have infinitely many distinct knots
J^{\prime}
satisfying this property.

2.
Knots having, for all integers
n
, the same
n
-trace as infinitely many other knots.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

This essentially follows from
[
AJLO15
]
by finding a knot that has two ‘simple annulus presentations’ on Hopf bands of opposite handedness, that are both ‘good’.
By
[
AJLO15
, Theorem 3.10]
, a simple annulus presentation for a knot
K
enables the construction of infinitely many knots with the same
n
-trace as
K
for any integer
n
.
By
[
AJLO15
, Theorem 3.13]
, when a simple annulus presentation on a Hopf band with a positive twist is ‘good’ and
n
is positive, these infinitely many knots are distinguished by the degrees of their Alexander polynomials.
See
[
AJLO15
, Definition 3.14]
for the definition of a
good annulus presentation
.
Then by mirroring, a good annulus presentation on a Hopf band with a negative twist yields infinitely many distinct knots with the same
n
-trace as
\overline{K}
for each negative integer
n
, where
\overline{K}
denotes the mirror image of
K
.

This observation leads us to consider an amphicheiral knot
K
with good annulus presentation.
If the
n
-trace
X_{n}(K)
of an amphicheiral knot
K
is orientation-preservingly diffeomorphic to the
n
-trace
X_{n}(K^{\prime})
of another knot
K^{\prime}
, then it follows that
X_{-n}(K)\cong X_{-n}(\overline{K})\cong X_{-n}(\overline{K^{\prime}})
. In particular, we deduce that an amphicheiral knot
K
shares its
n
-trace with infinitely many distinct knots if and only if it shares its
(-n)
-trace with infinitely many distinct knots.

Let us consider the case where
n=0
.
If two knots share the same
0
-surgery their Alexander polynomials agree
[
Rol76
]
.
Nevertheless, for
n=0
, these infinitely many knots
K^{k}_{0}
admit a surgery description on a single 3-component link
L
, see for example
[
Oso06
]
.
If
L
is hyperbolic, then infinitely many of the knots
K^{k}_{0}
with the same
0
-trace as
K
will be hyperbolic and the volumes of
K^{k}_{0}
converge strictly monotonically to the volume of
L

[
NZ85
]
.
In particular, infinitely many of the
K_{0}^{k}
can be distinguished by their hyperbolic volume
[
Oso06
]
.

Now let us exhibit an amphicheiral knot with good annulus presentations for which the associated link
L
is hyperbolic, thereby proving the theorem.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

If
J
is trivial, we replace
J
by a non-trivial ribbon knot, i.e. a knot concordant to the unknot.
Let
K
be a knot with the property from Theorem
3.1
, for example we can take
K=9_{42}
.
We define knots
K^{\prime}=(-\overline{K})\#J
and
J^{\prime}=K\#(-\overline{K})\#J
, where
-\overline{K}
is the reflect inverse of the knot
K
.
Since
K\#(-\overline{K})
is a ribbon knot,
J^{\prime}
is ribbon concordant to
J
.
By Theorem
3.1
,
J^{\prime}=K\#K^{\prime}
is not detected by any of its traces. Moreover, Theorem
3.1
gives us infinitely many distinct knots

with the above property and thus we get also infinitely many distinct knots

not detected by any of its traces, that are all concordant to

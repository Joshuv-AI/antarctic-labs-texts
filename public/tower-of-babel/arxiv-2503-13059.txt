Non-existence of symmetric biderivations on finite-dimensional perfect Lie algebras
Ignacio Bajo, Saïd Benayadi, Hassan Oubba

Abstract
We show that there are no symmetric non-zero biderivations on perfect Lie algebras of finite dimension over a field of characteristic zero. We show that this is equivalent to show that every symmetric biderivation on a finite-dimensional perfect Lie algebra over such a field with values in a finite-dimensional module vanishes identically. This answers an open question posed by M. Brešar and K. Zhao in [ 3 ] .



1
Introduction

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\,,\,])
be a Lie algebra and
M
a vector space. A
representation of
\mathfrak{g}
on
M
is a morphism of Lie algebras
\psi:\mathfrak{g}\to{\mathfrak{gl}}(M)
, this is to say,
\psi
is a linear map such that
\psi([x,y])=\psi(x)\psi(y)-\psi(y)\psi(x)
for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
. In this case
M
is also called a
\mathfrak{g}
-module
.
A linear map
D:\mathfrak{g}\to M
is called a
derivation of
\mathfrak{g}
with values in
M
if

D([x,y])=\psi(x)D(y)-\psi(y)D(x)
holds for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.
Notice that this means that

D
is a
1
-cocycle for the cohomology of
\mathfrak{g}
with values in
M
(i.e.
D\in Z^{1}(\mathfrak{g},M)
).
A
biderivation
\varphi
of
\mathfrak{g}
with values in
M
is a bilinear map
\varphi:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to M
such that
\varphi(x,\cdot)
and
\varphi(\cdot,x)
are derivations of
\mathfrak{g}
with values in
M
, for all
x\in\mathfrak{g}.
If
\varphi
is also symmetric, then
\varphi
is called a
symmetric biderivation of
\mathfrak{g}
with values in
M
.
If
M=\mathfrak{g}
and
\psi
is the adjoint representation of
\mathfrak{g}
, this is to say,
\psi(x)(y)={\mathrm{ad}}(x)y=[x,y],
for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
, a biderivation of
\mathfrak{g}
with values in
M=\mathfrak{g}
will be called just a
biderivation of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\cdot)
be a nonassociative algebra and denote by
\mathrm{L}_{x}
and
\mathrm{R}_{x}
, respectively, the left and right multiplications by an element
x\in\mathcal{A}
. Let us also denote
\mathcal{A}^{-}=(A,[\,,\,]),
where
[\,,\,]
is the usual commutator of
(\mathcal{A},\cdot)
. In
[
1
]
it is said that
(\mathcal{A},\cdot)
is an
algebra of biderivation type
, or an
A_{BD}
-algebra
for short, if
\mathrm{L}_{x}
and
\mathrm{R}_{x}
are derivations of
\mathcal{A}^{-}
for all
x\in\mathcal{A}
. It is easily proved that the product in an
A_{BD}
-algebra
(\mathcal{A},\cdot)
is Lie-admissible and, therefore,
\mathcal{A}^{-}
is a Lie algebra. Further, the multiplication in
(\mathcal{A},\cdot)
provides a biderivation of the Lie algebra
\mathcal{A}^{-}.
It was shown in
[
1
]
that this class of
A_{BD}
-algebras includes Lie algebras, symmetric Leibniz algebras, Lie-admissible left (or right) Leibniz algebras, Milnor algebras, and LR-algebras. Moreover, it has also been proved that there is a one-to-one correspondence between the class of
A_{BD}
-algebras and the class of Lie algebras with symmetric biderivations. Explicitly, if
(\mathfrak{g},\varphi)
is a Lie algebra with a symmetric biderivation, one defines on the vector space
\mathcal{A}=\mathfrak{g}
the new product
x\cdot y=\frac{1}{2}(\varphi(x,y)+[x,y])
, for
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
, and, conversely, given an
A_{BD}
-algebra
(\mathcal{A},\cdot)
, one defines on
\mathfrak{g}=\mathcal{A}^{-}
the symmetric biderivation defined by
\varphi(x,y)=x\cdot y+y\cdot x
for all
x,y\in\mathcal{A}^{-}
. In the sequel, if
(\mathfrak{g},[\,,\,])
is a Lie algebra and
\varphi
is a symmetric biderivation, we will frequently use the notation
\varphi(x,y)=x\circ y
and we will say that
\varphi
or
\circ
is an
A_{BD}
-structure on
\mathfrak{g}
. We will say that the symmetric biderivation is trivial if it is identically null.

A particular case of
A_{BD}
-structure on Lie algebras is provided by a so-called
commutative post-Lie algebra structure
(or
CPA-structure
)
[
5
,
6
]
, which consist on a symmetric biderivation
\circ
on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
satisfying the additional identity

[x,y]\circ z=x\circ(y\circ z)-y\circ(x\circ z)
, for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
. Notice that this last identity is equivalent to the fact that left multiplication with respect to
\circ
gives a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{g}
. It was proved in
[
6
]
that every
CPA
-structure on a real or complex perfect Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
(i.e. such that
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]=\mathfrak{g}
) is trivial. Further, in
[
1
]
it is proved that every
A_{BD}
-structure on a semisimple real or complex Lie algebra must vanish identically. Therefore the natural question of the existence of nontrivial
A_{BD}
-structures on perfect Lie algebras arises naturally.

Besides, in the paper
[
3
]
by M. Brešar and K. Zhao it is posed the question of whether there exists a perfect Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of finite dimension and a
\mathfrak{g}
-module
M
of finite dimension with
M^{\mathfrak{g}}=\{v\in M\ :\ \psi(\mathfrak{g})v=\{0\}\}=\{0\}
admitting a nonzero symmetric biderivation
\varphi
of
\mathfrak{g}
with values in
M
. Obviously, the existence of a nontrivial
A_{BD}
-structure on a finite-dimensional perfect Lie algebra, would answer Brešar and Zhao’s open question affirmatively. It should be remarked that in
[
3
]
an example of a nonzero symmetric biderivation with values in an infinite-dimensional module of
{\mathfrak{sl}}(2)
is given, and that such example has been used in
[
1
]
to find an infinite-dimensional perfect Lie algebra that can be endowed with a nonzero
A_{BD}
-structure. However, both problems in finite dimension remained unsolved until now.

The main goal of this paper is to answer Brešar and Zhao’s open question in the negative. Actually we will prove that, if
\varphi
is a symmetric biderivation of a perfect finite-dimensional Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with values in finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{g}
-module
M
, then
\varphi=0
, even in the case
M^{\mathfrak{g}}\neq\{0\}
. The structure of the paper is as follows. We first show that Brešar and Zhao’s question is actually equivalent to the question of existence of nontrivial
A_{BD}
-structures on finite-dimensional perfect Lie algebras. Next, we show that every
A_{BD}
-structure on a Lie algebra with Levi decomposition
\mathfrak{g}={\mathfrak{s}}\ltimes_{\phi}R(\mathfrak{g})
is completely defined by three maps
F:{\mathfrak{s}}\to R(\mathfrak{g})
,
G:{\mathfrak{s}}\to R(\mathfrak{g})
and
\Delta:R(\mathfrak{g})\to\mbox{Der}(R(\mathfrak{g}))
verifying certain equalities.
In Section
4
, we focus on perfect Lie algebras and prove that if there exist a perfect Lie algebra with a nontrivial
A_{BD}
-structure then we can find a perfect Lie algebra with abelian radical also admitting nonzero
A_{BD}
-structures. This reduces our problem to the case in which the radical is commutative. The nonexistence of
A_{BD}
-structures in perfect Lie algebras with abelian radical is done in our last section, completing the proof of our non-existence result.

2
An equivalence result

From now on, unless otherwise stated, all the vector spaces considered in this paper are finite-dimensional vector spaces over a field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic zero.

Let us recall some of the definitions given in the introduction.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\psi:\mathfrak{g}\to{\mathfrak{gl}}(M)
be a representation of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
on a vector space
M
. A biderivation
\varphi
of
\mathfrak{g}
with values in
M
is a bilinear map
\varphi:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to M
such that

\varphi(x,[y,z])=\psi(y)\varphi(x,z)-\psi(z)\varphi(x,y),\quad\varphi([y,z],x)=\psi(y)\varphi(z,x)-\psi(z)\varphi(y,x)

(2.1)

hold for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.
If, further,
\varphi(x,y)=\varphi(y,x)
for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
, then
\varphi
is called a symmetric biderivation of
\mathfrak{g}
with values in
M
.

Definition 2.2
.

An
A_{BD}
-structure on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is a commutative product
\circ:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
which verifies
x\circ[y,z]=[x\circ y,z]+[y,x\circ z]
for any
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
. This is equivalent to the bilinear map
\varphi
defined by
\varphi(x,y)=x\circ y
being a symmetric biderivation of
\mathfrak{g}
with values on the adjoint module
\mathfrak{g}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

There exists a nonzero symmetric biderivation of a perfect Lie algebra with values in a module if and only if there exists a perfect Lie algebra admitting a nonzero
A_{BD}
-structure.

Proof.

If there exists a perfect Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with a nonzero
A_{BD}
-structure
\circ
, then
\varphi(x,y)=x\circ y
obviously defines a nonzero symmetric biderivation of
\mathfrak{g}
with values in the adjoint module
M=\mathfrak{g}.

Let us then prove the converse. Let
\psi:{\mathfrak{p}}\to{\mathfrak{gl}}(M)
be a representation of a perfect Lie algebra
({\mathfrak{p}},[,])
and
\varphi={\mathfrak{p}}\times{\mathfrak{p}}\to M
a symmetric biderivation with values in
M
. Define the sets
{\cal M}_{0}=\varphi({\mathfrak{p}},{\mathfrak{p}})
and
{\cal M}_{k}=\psi({\mathfrak{p}}){\cal M}_{k-1}
for all
k\geq 1
and let
V
be the linear span of
\cup_{k\geq 0}{\cal M}_{k}
. It is clear that
V
is a
{\mathfrak{p}}
-submodule of
M
because identity (
2.1
) holds. Now, consider
\mathfrak{g}={\mathfrak{p}}\oplus V
endowed with the natural bracket

[x+v,x^{\prime}+v^{\prime}]_{\mathfrak{g}}=[x,x^{\prime}]+\psi(x)v^{\prime}-\psi(x^{\prime})v,

for all
x,x^{\prime}\in{\mathfrak{p}}
,
v,v^{\prime}\in V
. It is well known that
\mathfrak{g}
with such a bracket becomes a Lie algebra. Since
{\mathfrak{p}}
is perfect, it is clear that
\mathfrak{g}
will also be perfect if and only if
\psi({\mathfrak{p}})V=V
. But, by construction,
{\cal M}_{k}=\psi({\mathfrak{p}}){\cal M}_{k-1}\subset\psi({\mathfrak{p}})V
for all
k\geq 1
and the fact that
{\mathfrak{p}}
is perfect and (
2.1
) imply that
{\cal M}_{0}\subset\psi({\mathfrak{p}}){\cal M}_{0},
showing that
V\subset\psi({\mathfrak{p}})V
.

Finally, let us define a symmetric product on
\mathfrak{g}
by

(x+v)\circ(x^{\prime}+v^{\prime})=\varphi(x,x^{\prime})

for any
x,x^{\prime}\in{\mathfrak{p}}
,
v,v^{\prime}\in V
. Notice that
\circ
is nonzero since
\varphi
is so. Using (
2.1
) again, we then have for
x,x^{\prime},y\in{\mathfrak{p}}
and
v,v^{\prime},w\in V
that

\displaystyle(y+w)\circ[x+v,x^{\prime}+v^{\prime}]_{\mathfrak{g}}=\varphi(y,[x,x^{\prime}])=\psi(x)\varphi(y,x^{\prime})-\psi(x^{\prime})\varphi(y,x)

\displaystyle[(y+w)\circ(x+v),x^{\prime}+v^{\prime}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x+v,(y+w)\circ(x^{\prime}+v^{\prime})]_{\mathfrak{g}}=

\displaystyle\phantom{==}[\varphi(y,x),x^{\prime}+v^{\prime}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x+v,\varphi(y,x^{\prime})]_{\mathfrak{g}}=-\psi(x^{\prime})\varphi(y,x)+\psi(x)\varphi(y,x^{\prime}).

This shows that
\circ
is an
A_{BD}
-structure on the perfect Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.∎

3
Some generalities on
A_{BD}
-structures

Let us first show that we can reduce our study to the case of an algebraically closed field. The proof of the following lemma is straightforward.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field of characteristic zero and let
\overline{\mathbb{K}}
be its algebraic closure. Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra over
\mathbb{K}
and let
\circ
be an
A_{BD}
-structure on
\mathfrak{g}
. If
\overline{g}=\mathfrak{g}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}\overline{\mathbb{K}}
, then the product
\bullet
on
\overline{g}
defined by
(x_{1}\otimes\zeta_{1})\bullet(x_{2}\otimes\zeta_{2})=x_{1}\circ x_{2}\otimes\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2}
, for all
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathfrak{g}
,
\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\in\overline{\mathbb{K}}
provides an
A_{BD}
-structure on
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Let us consider a Lie algebra with Levi decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{s}\ltimes_{\phi}R(\mathfrak{g})
, where
R(\mathfrak{g})
stands for the radical of
\mathfrak{g}
, and
\phi:\mathfrak{s}\to R(\mathfrak{g})
is the representation of
\mathfrak{s}
on
R(\mathfrak{g})
defined by
\phi(a)={\mathrm{ad}}(a)_{|R(\mathfrak{g}},
for all
a\in\mathfrak{s}
. Suppose that
\mathfrak{g}
is endowed with an
A_{BD}
-structure
\circ
. This means that, for all
x
element of
\mathfrak{g}
, the multiplication
L_{x}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
defined by
L_{x}(y)=x\circ y,
for all
y\in\mathfrak{g}
, is a derivation of
\mathfrak{g}
. Moreover, by Proposition 4.5 of
[
1
]
,
L_{x}(\mathfrak{g})
is contained in
R(\mathfrak{g})
, for all
x\in\mathfrak{g}.
Thus, each map
L_{x}
lies in the set
\mbox{Der}(\mathfrak{g},R(\mathfrak{g}))
of derivations of
\mathfrak{g}
with values in
R(\mathfrak{g})
. Using Lemma 5.7 of
[
4
]
, the set
\mbox{Der}(\mathfrak{g},R(\mathfrak{g}))
can be characterized
as follows:

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{s}\ltimes_{\phi}R(\mathfrak{g})
be the Levi decomposition of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and
let
D:\mathfrak{g}\to R(\mathfrak{g})
be a linear map. Then
D\in Der(\mathfrak{g},R(\mathfrak{g}))
if and only if there exist two linear maps
d:R(\mathfrak{g})\to R(\mathfrak{g})
and
f:\mathfrak{s}\to R(\mathfrak{g})
such that

(1)

D(a+r)=f(a)+d(r),

(2)

d([r,r^{\prime}])=[d(r),r^{\prime}]+[r,d(r^{\prime})]
,

(3)

f([a,a^{\prime}])=\phi(a)f(a^{\prime})-\phi(a^{\prime})f(a)
,

(4)

d\phi(a)(r)=\phi(a)d(r)+[f(a),r]
,

for all
a,a^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{s},\,r,r^{\prime}\in R(\mathfrak{g})
.

Remark 3.1
.

Notice that condition (2) means that
d
is a derivation of
R(\mathfrak{g})
and condition (3) is equivalent to
f\in Z^{1}(\mathfrak{s},R(\mathfrak{g}))
. Thus, we can apply the first Whitehead’s lemma
[
7
]
to the cocycle
f
to assure the existence of a vector
r_{f}\in R(\mathfrak{g})
such that
f(a)=\phi(a)r_{f}
for all
a\in{\mathfrak{s}}
.

All through the paper the notation
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{s}\ltimes_{\phi}R(\mathfrak{g})
will stand for a Levi decomposition of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
over an algebraically closed field
\mathbb{K}
. Notice that if
\mathfrak{g}
admits a nontrivial
A_{BD}
-structure, then
R(\mathfrak{g})\neq\{0\}
since semisimple Lie algebras do not admit such structures
[
1
, Prop. 4.3]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{s}\ltimes_{\phi}R(\mathfrak{g})
be a Lie algebra. A product
\circ
on
\mathfrak{g}
defines an
A_{BD}
-structure if and only if there exist linear maps
F:\mathfrak{s}\to R(\mathfrak{g})
,
G:R(\mathfrak{g})\to R(\mathfrak{g})
and
\Delta:R(\mathfrak{g})\to\mbox{\rm Der}(R(\mathfrak{g}))
such that

(a+r)\circ(a^{\prime}+r^{\prime})=\phi(a)Fa^{\prime}+\phi(a)Gr^{\prime}+\phi(a^{\prime})Gr+\Delta(r)(r^{\prime}),

where
F,G
and
\Delta
verify

(1)

\phi(a)Fa^{\prime}=\phi(a^{\prime})Fa,

(2)

\phi(a)G\in\mbox{\rm Der}(R(\mathfrak{g}))
,

(3)

\Delta(r)(r^{\prime})=\Delta(r^{\prime})(r),

(4)

\phi(a)G\phi(a^{\prime})(r^{\prime})=\phi(a^{\prime})\phi(a)G(r^{\prime})+[\phi(a)Fa^{\prime},r^{\prime}],

(5)

\Delta(r)\phi(a^{\prime})(r^{\prime})=\phi(a^{\prime})\Delta(r)(r^{\prime})+[\phi(a^{\prime})G(r),r^{\prime}],

for all
a,a^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{s}
,
r,r^{\prime}\in R(\mathfrak{g})
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\circ
is an
A_{BD}
-structure. Let us consider
x\in\mathfrak{g}
. Since
L_{x}\in\mbox{\rm Der}(\mathfrak{g},R(\mathfrak{g}))
, by Lemma
3.2
, there exist a linear map
f_{x}:\mathfrak{s}\to R(\mathfrak{g})
and a derivation
d_{x}:R(\mathfrak{g})\to R(\mathfrak{g})
such that
L_{x}(a+r)=f_{x}(a)+d_{x}(r)
, verifying
f_{x}([a,a^{\prime}])=\phi(a)f_{x}(a^{\prime})-\phi(a^{\prime})f_{x}(a)
and
d_{x}\phi(a)(r)=\phi(a)d_{x}(r)+[f_{x}(a),r]
, for all
a,a^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{s}
,
r\in R(\mathfrak{g})
.
Using Whitehead’s Lemma as in Remark
3.1
, we see that there exists
v_{f_{x}}\in R(\mathfrak{g})
such that
f_{x}(a)=\phi(a)v_{f_{x}}
for all
a\in{\mathfrak{s}}
. Let us then define
F:{\mathfrak{s}}\to R(\mathfrak{g})
by
F(a)=v_{f_{a}}
for
a\in{\mathfrak{s}}
and
G:R(\mathfrak{g})\to R(\mathfrak{g})
by
G(r)=v_{f_{r}}
whenever
r\in R(\mathfrak{g})
.

One also has
d_{a}(r)=L_{a}(r)=L_{r}(a)=f_{r}(a)=\phi(a)G(r)
, for all
a\in{\mathfrak{s}}
,
r\in R(\mathfrak{g})
, and, as a result,
L_{a}(a^{\prime}+r^{\prime})=\phi(a)F(a^{\prime})+\phi(a)G(r^{\prime})
for all
a,a^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{s}
,
r^{\prime}\in R(\mathfrak{g})
. Clearly, the fact that
d_{a}
is a derivation of
R(\mathfrak{g})
proves (2). Besides, if we define define
\Delta(r)=d_{r}\in\mbox{\rm Der}(R(\mathfrak{g}))
for all
r\in R(\mathfrak{g})
, we have
L_{r}(a^{\prime}+r^{\prime})=L_{r}(a^{\prime})+\Delta(r)(r^{\prime})=\phi(a^{\prime})Gr+\Delta(r)(r^{\prime})
and, hence

(a+r)\circ(a^{\prime}+r^{\prime})=\phi(a)Fa^{\prime}+\phi(a)Gr^{\prime}+\phi(a^{\prime})Gr+\Delta(r)(r^{\prime}),

for all
a,a^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{s}
,
r,r^{\prime}\in R(\mathfrak{g})
, as claimed.
Notice that the commutativity of
\circ
clearly implies assertions (1) and (3).

Finally recall that we had
d_{x}\phi(a^{\prime})(r^{\prime})=\phi(a^{\prime})d_{x}(r^{\prime})+[f_{x}(a^{\prime}),r^{\prime}]
, for all
x\in\mathfrak{g}
,
a^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{s}
,
r^{\prime}\in R(\mathfrak{g})
. When
x=a\in{\mathfrak{s}}
we then get

\phi(a^{\prime})G\phi(a)(r^{\prime})=\phi(a^{\prime})\phi(a)G(r^{\prime})+[\phi(a)Fa^{\prime},r^{\prime}],
proving identity (4). Taking
x=r\in R(\mathfrak{g})
we obtain
\Delta(r)\phi(a^{\prime})(r^{\prime})=\phi(a^{\prime})\Delta(r)(r^{\prime})+[\phi(a^{\prime})G(r),r^{\prime}]
, which proves (5).

For the converse, if
F,G
and
\Delta
verifying equalities (1) through (5) are given and we define
(a+r)\circ(a^{\prime}+r^{\prime})=\phi(a)Fa^{\prime}+\phi(a)Gr^{\prime}+\phi(a^{\prime})Gr+\Delta(r)(r^{\prime}),
for all
a,a^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{s},\,r\in R(\mathfrak{g})
, it is a simple calculation to see that
\circ
is commutative and that the maps
f_{a+r}:{\mathfrak{s}}\to R(\mathfrak{g})
and
d_{a+r}:R(\mathfrak{g})\to R(\mathfrak{g})
defined by
f_{a+r}(a^{\prime})=\phi(a)Fa^{\prime}+\phi(a^{\prime})Gr
,
d_{a+r}(r^{\prime})=\phi(a)Gr^{\prime}+\Delta(r)(r^{\prime})
, for all
a,a^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{s}
,
r,r^{\prime}\in R(\mathfrak{g})
verify the conditions (2) through (4) of Lemma
3.2
. This shows that
L_{a+r}
is a derivation of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, for any
a\in\mathfrak{s}
,
r\in R(\mathfrak{g})
, which proves that
\circ
defines an
A_{BD}
-structure.
∎

As we mentioned in the introduction, the case with abelian radical will be important in our study. In that case we immediately deduce form Proposition
3.1
the following characterization of
A_{BD}
-structures:

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{s}\ltimes_{\phi}R(\mathfrak{g})
be a Lie algebra with
R(\mathfrak{g})
abelian. A product
\circ
on
\mathfrak{g}
defines an
A_{BD}
-structure if and only if there exist linear maps
F:\mathfrak{s}\to R(\mathfrak{g})
,
G:R(\mathfrak{g})\to R(\mathfrak{g})
and
\Delta:R(\mathfrak{g})\to{\mathfrak{gl}}(R(\mathfrak{g}))
such that

(a+r)\circ(a^{\prime}+r^{\prime})=\phi(a)Fa^{\prime}+\phi(a)Gr^{\prime}+\phi(a^{\prime})Gr+\Delta(r)(r^{\prime}),

where
F,G
and
\Delta
verify

(1)

\phi(a)Fa^{\prime}=\phi(a^{\prime})Fa,

(2)

\Delta(r)(r^{\prime})=\Delta(r^{\prime})(r),

(3)

\phi(a)G\phi(a^{\prime})(r)=\phi(a^{\prime})\phi(a)G(r),

(4)

\Delta(r)\phi(a)(r^{\prime})=\phi(a)\Delta(r)(r^{\prime}),

for all
a,a^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{s}
,
r,r^{\prime}\in R(\mathfrak{g})
.

Remark 3.2
.

Let us notice that there are three interesting particular cases of the situation described in Corollary
3.1
. For a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{s}\ltimes_{\phi}R(\mathfrak{g})
with abelian radical with the notations above, we have:

(1)

If we take
F\neq 0
such that
\phi(a)Fa^{\prime}=\phi(a^{\prime})Fa,
for all
a,a^{\prime}\in{\mathfrak{s}}
and there exists
a_{0}\in{\mathfrak{s}}
with
\phi(a_{0})F\neq 0
, this is to say
F({\mathfrak{s}})\not\subset R(\mathfrak{g})^{\mathfrak{s}}
, then the map

(a+r)\circ(a^{\prime}+r^{\prime})=\phi(a)Fa^{\prime}
, for all
a,a^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{s}
,
r,r^{\prime}\in R(\mathfrak{g})
, is a nontrivial
A_{BD}
-structure on
\mathfrak{g}
.

(2)

If we take
G\neq 0
such that
\phi(a)G\phi(a^{\prime})(r)=\phi(a^{\prime})\phi(a)G(r),
for all
a,a^{\prime}\in{\mathfrak{s}}
,
r\in R(\mathfrak{g})
and
G(R(\mathfrak{g}))\not\subset R(\mathfrak{g})^{\mathfrak{s}}
, then the map

(a+r)\circ(a^{\prime}+r^{\prime})=\phi(a)Gr^{\prime}+\phi(a^{\prime})Gr
, for all
a,a^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{s}
,
r,r^{\prime}\in R(\mathfrak{g})
, is a nontrivial
A_{BD}
-structure on
\mathfrak{g}
.

(3)

If we take
\Delta\neq 0
such that
\Delta(r)(r^{\prime})=\Delta(r^{\prime})(r)
and
\Delta(r)\phi(a)(r^{\prime})=\phi(a)\Delta(r)(r^{\prime}),
for all
a\in{\mathfrak{s}}
,
r,r^{\prime}\in R(\mathfrak{g})
then the map

(a+r)\circ(a^{\prime}+r^{\prime})=\Delta(r)(r^{\prime})
, for all
a,a^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{s}
,
r,r^{\prime}\in R(\mathfrak{g})
, is a nontrivial
A_{BD}
-structure on
\mathfrak{g}
.

4
Reduction to the case of abelian radical

We will devote this section to prove that if an arbitrary perfect Lie algebra admits a nonzero
A_{BD}
-structure, then one can find a perfect Lie algebra with abelian radical also admitting a nontrivial
A_{BD}
-structure.

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose that a perfect Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{s}\ltimes_{\phi}R(\mathfrak{g})
with non-abelian radical admits a nontrivial
A_{BD}
-structure. If
R(\mathfrak{g})
does not have
1
-dimensional
\mathfrak{s}
-submodules, then the Lie algebra with abelian radical
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathfrak{s}\ltimes_{\phi}\mathfrak{a},
where
\mathfrak{a}=R(\mathfrak{g})
as vector spaces, is perfect and also admits a nontrivial
A_{BD}
-structure.

Proof.

The
A_{BD}
-structure on
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{s}\ltimes_{\phi}R(\mathfrak{g})
, We know that there exist linear maps
F:\mathfrak{s}\to R(\mathfrak{g})
,
G:R(\mathfrak{g})\to R(\mathfrak{g})
and
\Delta:R(\mathfrak{g})\to\mbox{Der}(R(\mathfrak{g}))
verifying (1)-(5) in Proposition
3.1
such that the
A_{BD}
product is given by:

(a+r)\circ(a^{\prime}+r^{\prime})=\phi(a)F(a^{\prime})+\phi(a)Gr^{\prime}+\phi(a^{\prime})Gr+\Delta(r)(r^{\prime}),\quad\forall a,a^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{s},\ r,r^{\prime}\in R(\mathfrak{g}),

Notice that
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
is also perfect because
R(\mathfrak{g})
does not admit 1-dimensional
\mathfrak{s}
-submodules. If the above map
F
is nonzero, then by (1) in Remark
3.2
we obtain a nontrivial
A_{BD}
-structure on
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}.
Notice that the condition
F({\mathfrak{s}})\not\subset R(\mathfrak{g})^{\mathfrak{s}}
trivially holds because
R(\mathfrak{g})
does not have
1
-dimensional
\mathfrak{s}
-submodules.

Suppose now that
F=0
but
G\neq 0
. One has again
G(R(\mathfrak{g}))\not\subset R(\mathfrak{g})^{\mathfrak{s}}=\{0\}
and condition (4) in Proposition
3.1
now reads

\phi(a)G\phi(a^{\prime})(r)=\phi(a^{\prime})\phi(a)G(r)
, showing that
(a+r)\circ(a^{\prime}+r^{\prime})=\phi(a)Gr^{\prime}+\phi(a^{\prime})Gr
, for all
a,a^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{s}
,
r,r^{\prime}\in R(\mathfrak{g})
, provides a nontrivial
A_{BD}
-structure on
\mathfrak{g}
, as in the second case of Remark
3.2
.

Finally, if
F=0
and
G=0
, we must have
\Delta\neq 0
. It is clear that condition
\Delta(r)(r^{\prime})=\Delta(r^{\prime})(r)
holds for all
r,r^{\prime}\in R(\mathfrak{g})
and, since
G=0
, the condition (5) of Proposition
3.1
gives
\phi(a)\Delta(r)=\Delta(r)\phi(a)
, for all
a,a^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{s}
,
r\in R(\mathfrak{g})
. Thus the product
\circ
is also an
A_{BD}
-structure on
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
. ∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{s}\ltimes_{\phi}R(\mathfrak{g})
be a perfect Lie algebra with a non-trivial
A_{BD}
-structure
\circ
.
Denote
\mathfrak{n}_{1}=[\mathfrak{s},R(\mathfrak{g})]
and
\mathfrak{n}_{2}=R(\mathfrak{g})^{\mathfrak{s}}
and put
\phi_{1}=\phi_{|_{\mathfrak{n}_{1}}}
.

If either
{\mathfrak{s}}\circ{\mathfrak{s}}\neq\{0\}
or
{\mathfrak{s}}\circ\mathfrak{n}_{1}\neq\{0\}
, then the Lie algebra
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathfrak{s}\ltimes_{\phi_{1}}R(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}})
with abelian radical
R(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}})
, where
R(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}})=\mathfrak{n}_{1}
as vector spaces, is perfect and also admits a non-trivial
A_{BD}
-structure.

Proof.

It is quite clear that
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathfrak{s}\ltimes_{\phi_{1}}\mathfrak{n}_{1}
is perfect because
\mathfrak{n}_{1}=[\mathfrak{s},R(\mathfrak{g})]=[\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{n}_{1}].
Let us denote
\pi_{1}:R(\mathfrak{g})=\mathfrak{n}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{2}\to\mathfrak{n}_{1}
the natural projection onto
\mathfrak{n}_{1}
.

Take
F
,
G
and
\Delta
as in Proposition
3.1
, so that

(a+r)\circ(a^{\prime}+r^{\prime})=\phi(a)F(a^{\prime})+\phi(a^{\prime})G(r)+\phi(a)G(r^{\prime})+\Delta(r)(r^{\prime})

for all
a,a^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{s}
,
r,r^{\prime}\in R(\mathfrak{g})
.
Notice that
{\mathfrak{s}}\circ{\mathfrak{s}}\subset[{\mathfrak{s}},R(\mathfrak{g})]=\mathfrak{n}_{1}
and
{\mathfrak{s}}\circ\mathfrak{n}_{1}\subset[{\mathfrak{s}},R(\mathfrak{g})]=\mathfrak{n}_{1}
.
If
{\mathfrak{s}}\circ{\mathfrak{s}}\neq\{0\}
, then
F({\mathfrak{s}})\not\subset\mathfrak{n}_{2}
and we can construct a nontrivial
A_{BD}
-structure in
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
as in the first case of Remark
3.2
for
F_{1}=\pi_{1}F
. Otherwise, when
{\mathfrak{s}}\circ{\mathfrak{s}}=\{0\}
, we can consider
G_{1}=\pi_{1}G_{|\mathfrak{n}_{1}}
and define an
A_{BD}
-structure in
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
by

(a+r)\bullet(a^{\prime}+r^{\prime})=\phi_{1}(a^{\prime})G_{1}(r)+\phi_{1}(a)G_{1}(r^{\prime}),
for any
a,a^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{s}
,
r,r^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{n}_{1}
, as we have done in the second example of Remark
3.2
. It is obviously nontrivial because
\mathfrak{s}\circ\mathfrak{n}_{1}\neq\{0\}
and

a\bullet r_{1}=\phi_{1}(a)G_{1}r_{1}=\phi(a)\pi_{1}Gr_{1}=a\circ r_{1},

for all
a\in{\mathfrak{s}}
,
r_{1}\in\mathfrak{n}_{1}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{s}\ltimes_{\phi}R(\mathfrak{g})
is a perfect Lie algebra with non-abelian radical that admits a non-trivial
A_{BD}
-structure
\circ
.
Let
R(\mathfrak{g})=\mathfrak{n}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{2}
be the decomposition into
s
-submodules with
\mathfrak{n}_{1}=[\mathfrak{s},R(\mathfrak{g})]
and
\mathfrak{n}_{2}=R(\mathfrak{g})^{\mathfrak{s}}
and put
\phi_{1}=\phi_{|_{\mathfrak{n}_{1}}}
. If
\mathfrak{s}\circ\mathfrak{n}_{1}=\{0\},
then
\mathfrak{g}\circ\mathfrak{n}_{1}=\{0\}
.

Proof.

Put that the product on
\mathfrak{g}={\mathfrak{s}}\ltimes_{\phi}R(\mathfrak{g})
is given as in Proposition
3.1
by

(a+r)\circ(a^{\prime}+r^{\prime})=\phi(a)F(a^{\prime})+\phi(a^{\prime})G(r)+\phi(a)G(r^{\prime})+\Delta(r)(r^{\prime}),\quad a,a^{\prime}\in{\mathfrak{s}},\ r,r^{\prime}\in R(\mathfrak{g}).

Since
\mathfrak{g}
is perfect, one must have
\mathfrak{n}_{2}\subset[\mathfrak{n}_{1},\mathfrak{n}_{1}]
and we can, thus, find a set of generators of
R(\mathfrak{g})
(as Lie algebra)
\{t_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\leq\ell}\subset\mathfrak{n}_{1}
. But then, since
\phi(a)G(t_{\alpha})=0
and
\phi(a)G
is a derivation of
R(\mathfrak{g})
for all
a\in{\mathfrak{s}}
, we also get
\phi(a)G(r_{2})=0
for all
r_{2}\in\mathfrak{n}_{2}
. Hence,
{\mathfrak{s}}\circ\mathfrak{n}_{2}=\{0\}
.

As a consequence, we get
r_{2}\circ(s+r)=\Delta(r_{2})(r)
for all
s\in{\mathfrak{s}}
,
r\in R(\mathfrak{g})
,
r_{2}\in\mathfrak{n}_{2}
. Notice that the equality
\phi(a)G=0
, for all
a\in{\mathfrak{s}}
, and the condition (5) of Proposition
3.1
give
\phi(a)\Delta(r)(r_{2})=\Delta(r)\phi(s)r_{2}=0,

which shows that
\Delta(r)(r_{2})\in\mathfrak{n}_{2}
for all
r\in R(\mathfrak{g})
,
r_{2}\in\mathfrak{n}_{2}
. But then

0=\phi(a)\Delta({r_{2}})(r)=\Delta({r_{2}})\phi(s)(r),

for all
r\in R(\mathfrak{g})
, which implies
\Delta({r_{2}})(r_{1})=0
for all
r_{1}\in\mathfrak{n}_{1}=\phi({\mathfrak{s}})(\mathfrak{n}_{1})
. But, again, since
\Delta({r_{2}})
is a derivation and vanishes on the set of generators
\{t_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\leq\ell}\subset\mathfrak{n}_{1}
, one has that
\Delta({r_{2}})=0
.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

If there exists a perfect Lie algebra admitting a non-trivial
A_{BD}
-structure then there exists a perfect Lie algebra with abelian radical also admitting a non-trivial
A_{BD}
-structure.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{s}\oplus R(\mathfrak{g})
be a perfect Lie algebra with a non-trivial
A_{BD}
-structure
\circ
. We have the decomposition of the
\mathfrak{s}
-module
R(\mathfrak{g})
into the sum of two submodules given by

R(\mathfrak{g})=\mathfrak{n}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{2},
where
\mathfrak{n}_{1}=[\mathfrak{s},R(\mathfrak{g})]
and
\mathfrak{n}_{2}=R(\mathfrak{g})^{\mathfrak{s}}.
The case
\mathfrak{n}_{2}=\{0\}
was considered in Proposition
4.1
, so we can suppose that
\mathfrak{n}_{2}\neq\{0\}
.

According to Proposition
4.2
, if either
{\mathfrak{s}}\circ{\mathfrak{s}}\neq\{0\}
or
{\mathfrak{s}}\circ\mathfrak{n}_{1}\neq\{0\}
, then we can construct an
A_{BD}
-structure on the perfect Lie algebra
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{s}}\ltimes_{\phi_{1}}R(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}})
where
R(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}})
is the vector space
\mathfrak{n}_{1}
with zero bracket and
\phi_{1}
is the representation on
\mathfrak{n}_{1}
defined by
\phi
. Therefore, we can suppose that
{\mathfrak{s}}\circ({\mathfrak{s}}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{1})=\{0\}
so that, using Lemma
4.1
, we get that our product
\circ
is simply given by

(a+r_{1}+r_{2})\circ(a^{\prime}+r_{1}^{\prime}+r_{2}^{\prime})=\Delta({r_{1}})(r_{1}^{\prime}),\quad a,a^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{s},r_{1},r_{1}^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{n}_{1},r_{2},r_{2}^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{n}_{2}.

Let
\pi_{1}:R(\mathfrak{g})=\mathfrak{n}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{2}\to\mathfrak{n}_{1}
denote the natural projection and define on the perfect Lie algebra with abelian radical
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
given above the product

(a+r_{1})\bullet(a^{\prime}+r_{1}^{\prime})=\pi_{1}\Delta({r_{1}})(r_{1}^{\prime}),\quad a,a^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{s},r_{1},r_{1}^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{n}_{1}.

It is straightforward to prove that the restriction
\Delta_{1}
of
\pi_{1}\Delta
to
\mathfrak{n}_{1}\times\mathfrak{n}_{1}
verifies the conditions
\Delta_{1}({r_{1}})(r_{1}^{\prime})=\Delta_{1}({r_{1}^{\prime}})(r_{1})
and
\Delta_{1}(r_{1})\phi(a)(r_{1}^{\prime})=\phi(a)\Delta_{1}(r_{1})(r^{\prime}_{1}),
for all
a\in{\mathfrak{s}}
,
r_{1},r_{1}^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{n}_{1}
, because they hold for
\Delta
. So, according to the third case of Remark
3.2
, it suffices to show that
\Delta_{1}\neq 0
. But
\Delta_{1}=0
would imply
\Delta(r_{1})(r_{1}^{\prime})\in\mathfrak{n}_{2}
for all
r_{1},r_{1}^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{n}_{1}
and the condition (5) in Proposition
3.1
would give

0=\phi(a)\Delta(r_{1})(r_{1}^{\prime})=\Delta(r_{1})\phi(a)(r_{1}^{\prime}),

which, since
\mathfrak{n}_{1}=\phi({\mathfrak{s}})\mathfrak{n}_{1}
, would imply
\Delta=0
, a contradiction since
\circ
is nontrivial.
∎

5
The case with abelian radical and the main result

It was proved in
[
1
, Th. 4.1]
that if the radical of
\mathfrak{g}
is abelian and a nonzero simple
{\mathfrak{s}}
-module then every
A_{BD}
-structure on
\mathfrak{g}
is defined by
(s+r)\circ(s^{\prime}+r^{\prime})=[\beta(s),s^{\prime}]
, for all
a,a^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{s}
,
r,r^{\prime}\in R(\mathfrak{g})
, where
\beta:{\mathfrak{s}}\to\mathfrak{g}
is a map such that
[\beta(s),s^{\prime}]=[\beta(s^{\prime}),s]
. Using also
[
1
, Prop. 4.3]
one easily shows that
\beta(\mathfrak{s})\subset R(\mathfrak{g})
so that we see that, in that case, the only possible
A_{BD}
-structures are those given as in the first case of Remark
3.2
. Explicitly, we can reformulate the mentioned theorem in
[
1
]
as follows:

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{s}\ltimes_{\phi}R(\mathfrak{g})
be a Lie algebra such that
R(\mathfrak{g})\neq\{0\}
is an abelian Lie subalgebra and
R(\mathfrak{g})
is a simple
\mathfrak{s}
-module. Every
A_{BD}
-structure on
\mathfrak{g}
is defined by
(a+r)\circ(a^{\prime}+r^{\prime})=\phi(a)F(a^{\prime})
, for all
a,a^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{s}
,
r,r^{\prime}\in R(\mathfrak{g})
, where
F:\mathfrak{s}\to R(\mathfrak{g})
is a linear map such that
\phi(a)F(a^{\prime})=\phi(a^{\prime})F(a)
, for all
a,a^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{s}
.

Let us prove that all
A_{BD}
-structures are trivial in the particular case
\mathfrak{s}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
and
R(\mathfrak{g})=V(m)
for a certain
m\in{\mathbb{N}}\backslash\{0\}
, the
(m+1)
-dimensional irreducible module endowed with a vanishing bracket. Such result was already seen in
[
1
, Corollary 4.2]
for an even
m
.

Corollary 5.1
.

For
m\in{\mathbb{N}}\backslash\{0\}
, let
\phi
denote the simple representation of
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
on the
(m+1)
-dimensional space
V(m)
and consider that
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes_{\phi}V(m)
, where
R(\mathfrak{g})=V(m)
is abelian. Any
A_{BD}
-structure on
\mathfrak{g}
is trivial.

Proof.

Let us consider the basis
\{h,e,f\}
of
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
satisfying

[h,e]=2e
,
[h,f]=-2f
,
[e,f]=h.
We will use the description of the irreducible representations of
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
as given in
[
8
]
, so that there exists a basis
\{v_{i},\,0\leq i\leq m\}
of
V(m)
such that

\displaystyle\phi(h)v_{i}=(m-2i)v_{i},\quad i\in\{0,\dots,m\},

\displaystyle\phi(e)v_{i}=(m-i+1)v_{i-1},\quad i\in\{1,\dots,m\},

\displaystyle\phi(f)v_{i}=(i+1)v_{i+1},\quad i\in\{0,\dots,m-1\},

\displaystyle\phi(e)v_{0}=0,\quad\phi(f)v_{m}=0.

Let
F:\mathfrak{sl}(2)\to V(m)
be a linear map such that
\phi(a)F(a^{\prime})=\phi(a^{\prime})F(a)
, for all
a,a^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{sl}(2)
and put

F(e)=\sum_{i=0}^{m}a_{i}v_{i},\quad F(f)=\sum_{i=0}^{m}b_{i}v_{i},\quad F(h)=\sum_{i=0}^{m}c_{i}v_{i}.

The conditions
\phi(e)F(f)=\phi(f)F(e)
,
\phi(e)F(h)=\phi(h)F(e)
and
\phi(f)F(h)=\phi(h)F(f)
respectively give

\displaystyle b_{1}=a_{m-1}=0,\quad ia_{i-1}=(m-i)b_{i+1}\quad 1\leq i\leq m-1,

\displaystyle a_{m}=0,\quad(m-i)c_{i+1}=(m-2i)a_{i}\quad 0\leq i\leq m-1,

\displaystyle b_{0}=0,\quad ic_{i-1}=(m-2i)b_{i}\quad 1\leq i\leq m.

and from those equations we deduce that
c_{0}=c_{m}=0
and

\left((m-2i+2)(m-i)(i+1)-(m-2i-2)(m-i+1)i\right)c_{i}=0,\quad 1\leq i\leq m-1.

Since we have that

(m-2i+2)(m-i)(i+1)-(m-2i-2)(m-i+1)i]=m^{2}+2m\neq 0,

one finally gets
c_{i}=0
for all
i=0,\dots,m
. Now, the identities

0=(m-i)c_{i+1}=(m-2i)a_{i}\quad(0\leq i\leq m-1),\qquad 0=ic_{i-1}=(m-2i)b_{i}\quad(1\leq i\leq m),

show that
a_{i}=b_{i}=0
whenever
i\neq m/2
. If
m
is even and
m\geq 4
, for
k=m/2
we have
1\leq k-1<k+1\leq m-1
and, from
ia_{i-1}=(m-i)b_{i+1}
, whenever
1\leq i\leq m-1
, we then get

0=(k-1)a_{k-2}=(m-k+1)b_{k},\quad(k+1)a_{k}=(m-k-1)b_{k+2}=0,

which imply
b_{k}=a_{k}=0
because
(m-k+1)=k+1\neq 0
. If
m=2
then
m/2=1
and we already had

b_{1}=0
,
a_{1}=a_{m-1}=0
.
Thus, we finally get that
F
is identically zero. According to Proposition
5.1
we then have that any
A_{BD}
-structure on
\mathfrak{g}
is trivial.
∎

We can now extend our result to perfect Lie algebras with arbitrary abelian radical and
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
as Levi component. Explicitly:

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{s}\ltimes_{\phi}R(\mathfrak{g})
be a perfect Lie algebra with an abelian radical
R(\mathfrak{g})
and
\mathfrak{s}=\mathfrak{sl}(2)
. Then, any
A_{BD}
-structure on
\mathfrak{g}
is trivial.

Proof.

Let
R(\mathfrak{g})=M_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus M_{m}
be the decomposition of the
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
-module
R(\mathfrak{g})
into a direct sum of simple
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
-submodules
M_{i}
,
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}
. Notice that the fact that
\mathfrak{g}
is perfect implies that the dimension of
M_{i}
is is greater than or equal to
2
. Let us denote by
\pi_{i}:R(\mathfrak{g})\to M_{i}
the corresponding projection on each
i\leq m
.

Let us consider an
A_{BD}
-structure
\circ
on
\mathfrak{g}
and
let
F:\mathfrak{sl}(2)\to R(\mathfrak{g})
,
G:R(\mathfrak{g})\to R(\mathfrak{g})
and
\Delta:R(\mathfrak{g})\to{\mathfrak{gl}}(R(\mathfrak{g}))
be as given in Corollary
3.1
, so that

(a+r)\circ(a^{\prime}+r^{\prime})=\phi(a)F(a^{\prime})+\phi(a)G(r^{\prime})+\phi(a^{\prime})G(r)+\Delta(r)(r^{\prime}),

for all
a,a^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{sl}(2)
and
r,r^{\prime}\in R(\mathfrak{g})
.

If
F
is not identically zero, there exist
a\in\mathfrak{sl}(2)
and
j\leq m
such that
F_{j}(a)=\pi_{j}F(a)\neq 0
. But, if
\phi_{j}(a)
denotes the restriction of
\phi(a)
to
M_{j}
, we then have

\phi_{j}(a^{\prime})F_{j}(a)=\pi_{j}\phi(a^{\prime})F(a)=\pi_{j}\phi(a)F(a^{\prime})=\phi_{j}(a)\pi_{j}F(a^{\prime})=\phi_{j}(a)F_{j}(a^{\prime}),

for all
a,a^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{sl}(2)
, but then, as shown in the first case of Remark
3.2
, the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{j}=\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes_{\phi_{j}}M_{j}
would admit a nontrivial
A_{BD}
-structure, a contradiction with Corollary
5.1
. Thus,
F=0
.

Let us now see that
G
is also identically null. Recall that, as
R(\mathfrak{g})
is abelian, from assertion (3) in Corollary
3.1
we get
\phi(b)\phi(a)G=\phi(a)G\phi(b)
for all
a,b\in\mathfrak{sl}(2)
. Thus, if for
i,j\in\{1,\dots,m\}
we denote

D^{a}_{ij}=\pi_{j}\phi(a)G_{|_{M_{i}}}=\phi(a)\pi_{j}G_{|_{M_{i}}},
one easily sees that
[b,D^{a}_{ij}(r_{i})]=D^{a}_{ij}([b,r_{i}])
for all
a,b\in\mathfrak{sl}(2)
,
r_{i}\in M_{i}
. This implies that
D^{a}_{ij}
is a homomorphism of simple
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
-modules and, thus, either it is zero or it is bijective. But, as
D^{a}_{ij}(r_{i})=\phi(a)\pi_{j}G(r_{i})\in\phi(a)(M_{j})
, one gets that if an element
a\in\mathfrak{sl}(2)
verifies

[a,M_{j}]\neq M_{j}
for all
j\leq m
, then
D^{a}_{ij}=0
for all
i,j
and hence
\phi(a)G=0
. Let us prove that this implies that
G
must be zero. Let

\{h,e,f\}
be the standard basis of
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
with
[h,e]=2e
,
[h,f]=-2f
,
[e,f]=h.
We must have
\phi(e)G=0
and
\phi(f)G=0
because the restrictions of
\phi(e)
and
\phi(f)
to each
M_{j}
are nilpotent maps so that
\phi(e)M_{j}\neq M_{j}
and
\phi(f)M_{j}\neq M_{j}
. But then we also have
\phi(h)G=0
because
\phi(h)=\phi(e)\phi(f)-\phi(f)\phi(e)
. As a consequence,
\phi(a)G=0
for all
a\in\mathfrak{sl}(2)
, which can only occur when
G=0
.

Notice that, since
F=0
and
G=0
, we then get
(a+r)\circ(a^{\prime}+r^{\prime})=\Delta(r)(r^{\prime}),

for all
a,a^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{sl}(2)
and
r,r^{\prime}\in R(\mathfrak{g})
.
Let us consider
N_{0}=\{r\in R(\mathfrak{g})\ :\ \Delta(r)=0\}
. It is clear that
N_{0}
is a
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
-submodule of
R(\mathfrak{g})
because
\mathfrak{sl}(2)\circ\mathfrak{g}=\{0\}
and left multiplications for
\circ
are derivations of the Lie algebra.
Let us suppose that
N_{0}\neq R(\mathfrak{g})
. We can then find nonzero simple
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
-submodules
N_{1},\dots,N_{p}
of
R(\mathfrak{g})
such that
R(\mathfrak{g})=N_{0}\oplus N_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus N_{p}
. Recall that
\dim(N_{j})\geq 2
and that, if
\{h,e,f\}
is again the standard basis of
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
, then
\dim(\mbox{Ker}(\phi_{j}(e)))=1
for all
j\in\{1,\dots,p\}
, where
\phi_{j}
denotes the restriction of
\phi(e)
to each
N_{j}
. Let us fix
\ell\in\{1,\dots,p\}
and choose a nonzero vector
r_{\ell}\in\mbox{Ker}(\phi_{\ell}(e))
. For any
i,j\in\{1,\dots,p\}
, define
\Gamma_{ij}:N_{i}\to N_{j}
by
\Gamma_{ij}=\pi_{j}\Delta(r_{\ell})_{|N_{i}}
. The assertion (4) of
Corollary
3.1
gives
\phi(a)\Gamma_{ij}=\Gamma_{ij}\phi(a)
for all
a\in\mathfrak{sl}(2)
, showing that
\Gamma_{ij}
is a morphism of simple
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
-modules and, again, either it must be zero or invertible. But for all
r\in M_{i}
, using once more the equalities (2) and (4) of
Corollary
3.1
, we have

\phi_{j}(e)\Gamma_{ij}(r)=\pi_{j}\phi(e)\Delta(r_{\ell})(r)=\pi_{j}\phi(e)\Delta(r)(r_{\ell})=\pi_{j}\Delta(r)\phi(e)(r_{\ell})=0.

This implies that
\Gamma_{ij}(M_{i})\subset\mbox{Ker}(\phi_{j}(e))
, so that
\dim(\Gamma_{ij}(M_{i}))\leq 1
, which together with the fact that
\dim(N_{j})\geq 2
shows that
\Gamma_{ij}
cannot be invertible and must vanish for all
1\leq i,j\leq m
. But this would imply
\Delta(r_{\ell})=0
and, therefore,
r_{\ell}\in N_{0}\cap N_{\ell}
, a contradiction since we chose
r_{\ell}\neq 0
.

So we conclude that our assumption
N_{0}\neq R(\mathfrak{g})
is false and consequently
\mathfrak{g}\circ\mathfrak{g}=\{0\}.

∎

Remark 5.1
.

The non-existence of
A_{BD}
-structures on finite-dimensional perfect Lie algebras of the form
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes_{\phi}R(\mathfrak{g})
with abelian radical is in great contrast with the infinite-dimensional case; notice that the infinite-dimensional example given in
[
1
]
has
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
as its Levi summand.

Proposition
5.2
is essential in the proof of our next result which generalizes Proposition
5.1
to an arbitrary perfect Lie algebra with abelian radical.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}={\mathfrak{s}}\ltimes_{\phi}R(\mathfrak{g})
be a perfect Lie algebra with abelian radical. If
\mathfrak{g}
admits an
A_{BD}
-structure
\circ
, then
R(\mathfrak{g})\circ\mathfrak{g}=\{0\}
.

As a consequence,
\mathfrak{g}\circ\mathfrak{g}\circ\mathfrak{g}=\{0\}
and there exists a linear map
F:\mathfrak{s}\to R(\mathfrak{g})
such that
\phi(a)F(a^{\prime})=\phi(a^{\prime})F(a)
for all
a,a\in\mathfrak{s}
and

(a+r)\circ(a^{\prime}+r^{\prime})=\phi(a)F(a^{\prime})
for any
a,a^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{s}
,
r,r^{\prime}\in R(\mathfrak{g}).

Proof.

Let
F,G
and
\Delta
be the maps defined for
\circ
as in Corollary
3.1
.
Suppose that there exists
r_{0}\in R(\mathfrak{g})
such that
{r_{0}}\circ\mathfrak{g}\neq\{0\}
. Suppose first that
\Delta(r_{0})\neq 0
and take
r_{0}^{\prime}\in R(\mathfrak{g})
such that
\Delta({r_{0}})(r_{0}^{\prime})\neq 0
. Since
\mathfrak{g}
is perfect, there exists a
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
-triple
\mathfrak{s}_{1}
of
\mathfrak{s}
such that
\phi(\mathfrak{s}_{1})\Delta({r_{0}})(r_{0}^{\prime})\neq\{0\}
. Set
N
the sum of all non-trivial
\mathfrak{s}_{1}
-submodules of the
\mathfrak{s}_{1}
-module
R(\mathfrak{g})
so that
R(\mathfrak{g})=N\oplus M
where
M=\cap_{a\in\mathfrak{s}_{1}}\mbox{Ker}(\phi(a))=R(\mathfrak{g})^{\mathfrak{s}_{1}}
. Let
\pi:R(\mathfrak{g})=N\oplus M\to N
be the canonical projection, consider
r_{1}=\pi(r_{0})
,
r_{1}^{\prime}=\pi(r_{0}^{\prime})
and choose
a_{1}\in{\mathfrak{s}}_{1}
such that
\phi(a_{1})\Delta({r_{0}})(r_{0}^{\prime})\neq 0
.
Using identities (2) and (4) of Corollary
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\neq

\displaystyle\phi(a_{1})\Delta(r_{0})r_{0}^{\prime}=\Delta(r_{0})\phi(a_{1})r_{0}^{\prime}=\Delta(r_{0})\phi(a_{1})r_{1}^{\prime}=\phi(a_{1})\Delta(r_{0})r_{1}^{\prime}=\phi(a_{1})\Delta(r_{1}^{\prime})r_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta(r_{1}^{\prime})\phi(a_{1})r_{0}=\Delta(r_{1}^{\prime})\phi(a_{1})r_{1}=\phi(a_{1})\Delta(r_{1}^{\prime})r_{1}=\phi(a_{1})\Delta(r_{1})r_{1}^{\prime}=\phi(a_{1})\pi\Delta(r_{1})r_{1}^{\prime}.

Thus, there exist
r_{1},r_{1}^{\prime}\in N
such that
\phi({\mathfrak{s}}_{1})\pi\Delta(r_{1})r_{1}\neq\{0\}
; this obviously implies that
\pi\Delta(r_{1})r_{1}^{\prime}\neq 0
. Define
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{s}}_{1}\ltimes_{\varphi}N
where
\varphi=\phi_{|{\mathfrak{s}}_{1}}
, with
R(\mathfrak{g})=N
abelian. It is clear that
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
is perfect. Define on
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
the product
(a+r)\bullet(a^{\prime}+r^{\prime})=\pi\Delta(r)(r^{\prime})
for all
s,s^{\prime}\in{\mathfrak{s}}_{1}
,
r,r^{\prime}\in N
. It is obviously nonzero since
r_{1}\bullet r_{1}^{\prime}\neq 0
and one easily sees that it verifies the conditions of Corollary
3.1
, and hence defines an
A_{BD}
-structure on
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
. But, since
{\mathfrak{s}}_{1}
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
, this contradicts Proposition
5.2
. We thus conclude that
\Delta(r_{0})=0.

Since
{r_{0}}\circ\mathfrak{g}\neq\{0\}
but
\Delta(r_{0})=0
, we must have
Gr_{0}\neq 0
and then we may find a
{\mathfrak{sl}}(2)
-triple
{\mathfrak{s}}_{1}
in
{\mathfrak{s}}
such that
\phi({\mathfrak{s}}_{1})Gr_{0}\neq\{0\}
. Decompose
R(\mathfrak{g})
, as before, into the sum of two
{\mathfrak{s}}_{1}
-submodules
R(\mathfrak{g})=N\oplus M
where
M=R(\mathfrak{g})^{{\mathfrak{s}}_{1}}
and let
\pi:N\oplus M\to N
be the natural projection. Since
{\mathfrak{s}}_{1}=[{\mathfrak{s}}_{1},{\mathfrak{s}}_{1}]
, the condition
\phi({\mathfrak{s}}_{1})Gr_{0}\neq\{0\}
implies that there exist
a_{1},a_{1}^{\prime}\in{\mathfrak{s}}_{1}
such that
\phi([a_{1},a_{1}^{\prime}])Gr_{0}\neq\{0\}
. But then either
\phi(a_{1})\phi(a_{1}^{\prime})Gr_{0}\neq 0
or
\phi(a_{1}^{\prime})\phi(a_{1})Gr_{0}\neq 0
because
\phi([a_{1},a_{1}^{\prime}])=[\phi(a_{1}),\phi(a_{1}^{\prime})]
. We may suppose without loss of generality
\phi(a_{1}^{\prime})\phi(a_{1})Gr_{0}\neq 0
. But in such a case, if
r_{1}=\pi(r_{0})
, using identity (3) of Corollary
3.1
, we get

0\neq\phi(a_{1}^{\prime})\phi(a_{1})Gr_{0}=\phi(a_{1})G\phi(a_{1}^{\prime})r_{0}=\phi(a_{1})G\phi(a_{1}^{\prime})r_{1}=\phi(a_{1}^{\prime})\phi(a_{1})Gr_{1}=\phi(a_{1}^{\prime})\phi(a_{1})\pi(Gr_{1}).

This clearly implies that there exist
a_{1}\in{\mathfrak{s}}_{1}
and
r_{1}\in N
such that
\phi(a_{1})\pi(Gr_{1})\neq 0
. Now, define on the perfect Lie algebra
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{s}}_{1}\ltimes_{\varphi}N
, where
\varphi=\phi_{|{\mathfrak{s}}_{1}}
and
N
is abelian, the product
(a+r)\bullet(a^{\prime}+r^{\prime})=\varphi(a)\pi(Gr^{\prime})+\varphi(a^{\prime})\pi(Gr)
, for all
a,a^{\prime}\in{\mathfrak{s}}_{1}
,
r,r^{\prime}\in N
. One easily sees that the conditions of the second case in Remark
3.2
are verified and thus we would obtain a nontrivial
A_{BD}
-structure on
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
, which is impossible, according to Proposition
5.2
. As a result,
{r_{0}}\circ\mathfrak{g}=\{0\}
, showing that
R(\mathfrak{g})\circ\mathfrak{g}=\{0\}
.

The consequences are now straightforward from Corollary
3.1
.
∎

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}={\mathfrak{s}}\ltimes_{\phi}R(\mathfrak{g})
be a perfect Lie algebra with abelian radical and let
\circ
be an
A_{BD}
-structure on
\mathfrak{g}
. For every subalgebra
{\mathfrak{s}}_{1}
defined by a
{\mathfrak{sl}}(2)
-triple, it holds that
{\mathfrak{s}}_{1}\circ{\mathfrak{s}}_{1}=\{0\}
.

Proof.

Suppose that there exist
a,a^{\prime}\in{\mathfrak{s}}_{1}
such that
a\circ a^{\prime}\neq 0
. According to Proposition
5.3
, there exists
F:{\mathfrak{s}}\to R(\mathfrak{g})
such that
a\circ a^{\prime}=\phi(a)Fa^{\prime}
. Let
N
be the sum of nontrivial
{\mathfrak{s}}_{1}
-submodules of
R(\mathfrak{g})
, put
M=R(\mathfrak{g})^{{\mathfrak{s}}_{1}}
and let
\pi:R(\mathfrak{g})=N\oplus M\to N
be the natural projection. On the perfect Lie algebra
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{s}}\ltimes_{\varphi}N
, where
\varphi=\phi_{|N}
and
R(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}})=N
is abelian, let us define a product by
(a+r)\bullet(a^{\prime}+r^{\prime})=\varphi(a)\pi F(a^{\prime}).
It is easy to deduce from Proposition
3.1
that
\bullet
provides a nontrivial
A_{BD}
-structure on
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
, a contradiction with Proposition
5.2
. As a consequence,
{\mathfrak{s}}_{1}\circ{\mathfrak{s}}_{1}=\{0\}
.
∎

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}={\mathfrak{s}}\ltimes_{\phi}R(\mathfrak{g})
be a perfect Lie algebra with abelian radical and let
\circ
be an
A_{BD}
-structure on
\mathfrak{g}
.
If
{\mathfrak{h}}
is a Cartan subalgebra of
{\mathfrak{s}}
, then
{\mathfrak{h}}\circ\mathfrak{g}=\{0\}
.

Proof.

Let us first see that
{\mathfrak{h}}\circ{\mathfrak{h}}=\{0\}
. Suppose, on the contrary, that there exist
h_{1},h_{2}\in{\mathfrak{h}}
such that
h_{1}\circ h_{2}\neq 0
. For
h_{0}=h_{1}+h_{2}
, we have,
h_{0}\circ h_{0}=h_{1}\circ h_{1}+h_{2}\circ h_{2}+2h_{1}\circ h_{2}
. Thus,
h_{0}\circ h_{0}-h_{1}\circ h_{1}-h_{2}\circ h_{2}=2h_{1}\circ h_{2}\neq 0
.
Consequently, there exists
j\in\{0,1,2\}
such that
h_{j}\circ h_{j}\neq 0
. But, since
h_{j}\in{\mathfrak{h}}
, we can find a
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
-triple
{\mathfrak{s}}_{1}
such that
h_{j}\in{\mathfrak{s}}_{1}
and Lemma
5.1
implies
h_{j}\circ h_{j}\in{\mathfrak{s}}_{1}\circ{\mathfrak{s}}_{1}=\{0\}
, a contradiction. Hence, we must have
{\mathfrak{h}}\circ{\mathfrak{h}}=\{0\}
.

Let us now consider
h,h_{1}\in{\mathfrak{h}}
, and let
\{h_{1},e_{1},f_{1}\}
be a
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
-triple of
\mathfrak{s}
with
[h_{1},e_{1}]=2e_{1}
,
[h_{1},f_{1}]=-2f_{1}
,
[e_{1},f_{1}]=h_{1}
. Let
F:{\mathfrak{s}}\to R(\mathfrak{g})
be the map such that
a\circ a^{\prime}=\phi(a)Fa^{\prime}
, guaranteed by Proposition
5.3
. As
{\mathfrak{h}}\circ{\mathfrak{h}}=\{0\}
, we obviously have
\phi(h)F(h_{1})=\phi(h_{1})F(h)=0
, and the identity
{\mathfrak{s}}_{1}\circ{\mathfrak{s}}_{1}=\{0\}
gives
\phi(h_{1})F(e_{1})=\phi(h_{1})F(f_{1})=0
. Therefore, using also that
\phi(h)\phi(h_{1})=\phi(h_{1})\phi(h)
because
[h,h_{1}]=0
, we obtain

\displaystyle 2\phi(e_{1})Fh

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi([h_{1},e_{1}])Fh=\phi(h_{1})\phi(e_{1})Fh-\phi(e_{1})\phi(h_{1})Fh=\phi(h_{1})\phi(e_{1})Fh

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi(h_{1})\phi(h)Fe_{1}=\phi(h)\phi(h_{1})Fe_{1}=0,

\displaystyle 2\phi(f_{1})Fh

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi([f_{1},h_{1}])Fh=\phi(f_{1})\phi(h_{1})Fh-\phi(h_{1})\phi(f_{1})Fh=-\phi(h_{1})\phi(f_{1})Fh

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\phi(h_{1})\phi(h)Ff_{1}=\phi(h)\phi(h_{1})Ff_{1}=0,

and one gets that
h\circ x=\phi(x)Fh=0
for all
x
in the
{\mathfrak{sl}}(2)
-triple. Now the result follows from the fact that
{\mathfrak{s}}
admits a basis which is a union of
{\mathfrak{sl}}(2)
-triples.∎

We can now prove the main theorem of the paper.

Theorem 5.1
.

Any
A_{BD}
-structure on a perfect Lie algebra over an arbitrary field of characteristic zero is trivial.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a perfect Lie algebra over the field
\mathbb{K}
and suppose that it admits a nontrivial
A_{BD}
-structure. According to Proposition
4.3
, we may find a perfect Lie algebra
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{s}}\ltimes_{\phi}R(\overline{\mathfrak{g}})
, over the algebraic closure
\overline{\mathbb{K}}
of
\mathbb{K}
, with abelian radical also admitting a nontrivial
A_{BD}
-structure
\circ
.

If
a\in\mathfrak{s}
, then
a=a_{s}+a_{n}
where
a_{s}
is semisimple and
a_{n}
is nilpotent. Since,
a_{s}
is semisimple then there exists a Cartan subalgebra
{\mathfrak{h}}
of
\mathfrak{s}
such that
a_{s}\in{\mathfrak{h}}

[
2
, Prop. 10,p. 24]
. By Corollary
5.2
, we have
a_{s}\circ\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}=\{0\}
. On the other hand,
a_{n}
is nilpotent and then, by Jacobson-Morozov Theorem
[
2
, Prop. 2, p. 162]
, there exists a
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
-triple of
\mathfrak{s}
containing
a_{n}
. Hence,
a_{n}\circ a_{n}=0
, by Lemma
5.1
.
Consequently,
a\circ a=(a_{s}+a_{n})\circ(a_{s}+a_{n})=a_{s}\circ(a_{s}+a_{n})+a_{n}\circ a_{s}+a_{n}\circ a_{n}=0
, for all
a\in{\mathfrak{s}}
.
But now, if we consider
a,a^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{s}
, then
(a+a^{\prime})\circ(a+a^{\prime})=0
, which implies that
a\circ a+2a\circ a^{\prime}+a^{\prime}\circ a^{\prime}=0
and, therefore,
a\circ a^{\prime}=0
, so
\mathfrak{s}\circ\mathfrak{s}=\{0\}
and Proposition
5.3
shows that
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}\circ\overline{\mathfrak{g}}=\{0\}
, which contradicts the fact that
\circ
is nontrivial.

As a consequence,
\mathfrak{g}
cannot admit a nontrivial
A_{BD}
-structure either. The result for a real Lie algebra follows at once from Lemma
3.1

∎

ALDH3A2 targets arachidonic acid to promote cell metastasis in TNBC via AMPK/m-TOR signaling pathway
Authors: Ding Wang, Shulei Luo, Rongji Yu, Doudou Wen, Hao Jiang, Keli Xu, Xiaotang Di, Shubing Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTriple negative breast cancer (TNBC), characterized by its aggressive clinical behavior and propensity for distant metastasis, presents critical challenges in therapeutic management. Emerging evidence implicates lipid metabolic reprogramming as a key facilitator of tumor progression and metastatic dissemination. However, the precise molecular mechanisms underlying lipid metabolic dysregulation in TNBC metastasis remain poorly characterized. Our work systematically elucidates the mechanistic role of ALDH3A2 in orchestrating lipid metabolic adaptations that drive breast cancer metastasis.MethodsTranscriptomic analysis of clinical datasets identified metastasis-related metabolic regulators. Cellular migration/invasion assays and murine metastasis models were utilized to assess metastatic effects of ALDH3A2. Lipidomic profiling coupled with pathway analysis characterized metabolic alterations. Mechanistic studies integrated western blot analysis with lipid droplet quantification. Computational approaches including structure-based virtual screening and molecular docking simulations were employed for inhibitor discovery.ResultsALDH3A2 was significantly upregulated in TNBC clinical specimens and showed significant association with adverse clinical outcomes. Functional validation confirmed that ALDH3A2 potentiates TNBC cell migration/invasion through epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) activation. Metabolic profiling revealed ALDH3A2-driven lipid accumulation, evidenced by increased lipid droplet formation and elevated triglyceride levels. Specifically, arachidonic acid (AA) enrichment was observed in ALDH3A2-overexpressing cells, corresponding to enhanced glycerophospholipid metabolism. Mechanistic studies demonstrated that ALDH3A2-mediated AMP/ATP imbalance suppresses AMPK phosphorylation while activating mTOR/SREBP1 signaling. mTOR inhibition effectively attenuated ALDH3A2-induced lipid metabolic alterations. Importantly, oxaliplatin was identified as a potential therapeutic agent targeting ALDH3A2-mediated AA metabolism to suppress metastasis.ConclusionsOur work establishes ALDH3A2 as a pivotal regulator of lipid metabolic reprogramming in TNBC metastasis, providing mechanistic insights into AA-mediated tumor progression. These findings position ALDH3A2 as a promising therapeutic target and prognostic biomarker for TNBC management.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13058-025-02135-1.Keywords: Triple negative breast cancer, ALDH3A2, Metastasis, Lipid metabolic reprogramming, Arachidonic acid, Oxaliplatin

Introduction

Breast cancer persists as the most prevalent malignancy among women worldwide and represents the second leading cause of cancer-related mortality (Bray et al. 2024). Triple negative breast cancer (TNBC), accounting for 15–20% of all breast cancer cases, is distinguished by its aggressive clinical course (Smolarz et al. 2022). While diagnostic and therapeutic approaches have advanced, significant challenges remain in addressing chemotherapy resistance, disease recurrence, and distant metastasis (Bianchini et al. 2022). Notably, metastatic dissemination to the brain, bones, lungs, and liver constitutes the primary cause of mortality in breast cancer patients (Park et al. 2022). Current therapeutic strategies for metastatic disease, including cytotoxic chemotherapy, radiotherapy, endocrine therapy, and targeted therapy, demonstrate limited efficacy and safety profiles (Li et al. 2022). This underscores the critical need to identify novel therapeutic targets and develop effective pharmacological interventions.

Emerging evidence indicates that tumor cells systematically coordinate lipid metabolism processes, regulating lipid synthesis, storage dynamics, and degradation pathways (Luo et al. 2018). Such metabolic reprogramming activates oncogenic signaling networks that drive tumor initiation, proliferation, apoptotic evasion, and metastatic progression (Luo et al. 2017). A representative example involves metastasis-associated antigen 1 (MTA1), which interacts with ATP synthase F1 subunit alpha (ATP5A) to enhance mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS), thereby modulating mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR) signaling pathways to promote colon cancer liver metastasis (Wang et al. 2023). Recent studies have conclusively demonstrated the critical involvement of lipid metabolism in breast cancer pathogenesis (dos Santos et al. 2023; Xiao et al. 2024). Specifically, Li et al. revealed that reduced retinoic acid receptor responder 2 (RARRES2) expression in TNBC cells elevates glycerophospholipid levels while decreasing triacylglycerols through phosphatase and tensin homologue (PTEN)-mTOR-sterol regulatory element-binding protein 1 (SREBP1) signaling activation, facilitating cerebral metastasis (Li et al. 2023). Parallel investigations by Ning et al. established that Human Nucleobindin-2 (NUCB2)/Nesfatin-1 enhances cholesterol biosynthesis via mTOR complex 1 (mTORC1)-mediated activation of sterol regulatory element-binding protein 2 (SREBP2) and 3-hydroxy-3-methylglutaryl-CoA reductase (HMGCR), driving breast cancer metastasis (Ning et al. 2023).

The aldehyde dehydrogenase (ALDH) enzyme family catalyzes the conversion of endogenous and exogenous aldehydes into carboxylic acids, thereby reducing intracellular oxidative stress (Xia et al. 2023). ALDH family members demonstrate diverse oncogenic functions across malignancies: ALDH1 serves as both a breast cancer stem cell marker and prognostic indicator (Ginestier et al. 2007), while ALDH1A1 and ALDH3A1 overexpression correlates with enhanced proliferation and motility in non-small cell lung cancer (Patel et al. 2008). ALDH3A2 mutations induce pathological lipid substrate accumulation and fatty acid metabolite deficiencies, manifesting as ichthyosis, intellectual disability, and spastic diplegia in Sjögren-Larsson syndrome (SLS) patients (Naganuma et al. 2016). Recent work in ovarian cancer demonstrates that ALDH3A2 inhibition increases ferroptosis susceptibility through lipid metabolic pathway activation (Dong et al. 2023). Nevertheless, the mechanistic role of ALDH3A2 in TNBC metastasis remains poorly defined.

In this work, we identified ALDH3A2 overexpression in TNBC tissues correlating with adverse clinical outcomes. Functional studies established that ALDH3A2 stimulates arachidonic acid (AA) secretion to promote metastatic progression. Notably, AA administration reversed the impaired migration and invasion capacities of ALDH3A2-depleted TNBC cells. Mechanistically, ALDH3A2 reduced intracellular adenosine monophosphate/adenosine triphosphate (AMP/ATP) ratios, suppressed adenosine monophosphate-activated protein kinase (AMPK) phosphorylation, and activated mTOR-SREBP1 signaling. Furthermore, Oxaliplatin was shown to inhibit TNBC metastasis through ALDH3A2 targeting and AA content regulation. These findings collectively establish ALDH3A2 as a pivotal regulator of fatty acid metabolism and a promising therapeutic target in breast cancer.

Materials and methods

Plasmid and stable cell line construction

The ALDH3A2-specific shRNA sequences (http://crispor.tefor.net) were subcloned into the pLKO.1 lentiviral backbone through primer-mediated ligation (shRNA1-ALDH3A2-F/R and shRNA2-ALDH3A2-F/R, Table 1). Following sequence verification, the recombinant pLKO.1 vector was digested with AgeI (Cat#R3552S, NEB, USA) and EcoRI (Cat#R3101V, NEB, USA) for subsequent cloning steps. For ectopic expression constructs, the ALDH3A2 coding sequence (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov) was directionally cloned into the pCDH-CD513B-1-PHB vector using specific primers (ALDH3A2-F/R, Table 1) through PCR amplification. The target sequence was amplified through high-fidelity PCR with pfu polymerase (Cat#M0530S, NEB, USA) using 293T cell-derived cDNA as template. The recombinant pCDH-CD513B-1-PHB construct was subsequently linearized with EcoRI (Cat#R3101V, NEB, USA) and BamHI (Cat#R3136V, NEB, USA) restriction enzymes.

Cell culture and transfection

TNBC cells lines (MDA-MB-231, MDA-MB-468, BT549) were cultured in DMEM (Cat#SH30243.01, HyClone, USA) containing 10% fetal bovine serum at 37 °C under 5% CO₂, with medium refreshed every 48 h. Transient transfection was performed using 3–5 µL Hieff Trans™ Liposomal Transfection Reagent (Cat#40802ES03, Yeasen, China) and 3–6 µg plasmid DNA. Transfection efficiency was validated by qPCR and western blot analysis.

CCK8 assay

Cells (3 × 103/well) were plated in 96-well plates. After treatment, 20 µL CCK-8 reagent was added to each well and incubated for 4 h at 37 °C. Absorbance at 450 nm was quantified using a microplate reader (EnSpire 2300, PerkinElmer, USA) to assess viability.

Transwell migration and matrigel invasion assays

For migration analysis, trypsinized cells were resuspended in serum-free medium at 3 × 10⁴ cells/mL. Aliquots (100 µL) were loaded into upper Transwell chambers, with 700 µL complete medium (10% FBS) in lower chambers. Invasion assays utilized Matrigel-coated inserts (Cat#356234, Corning, USA), while uncoated inserts served for migration studies. After 48 h incubation, cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde, stained with 0.1% crystal violet for 20 min, and quantified microscopically.

Human samples and immunohistochemistry

Archived paraffin-embedded TNBC (n = 38) and non-TNBC tissues from primary breast cancer resections at Third Xiangya Hospital (Changsha, China) were analyzed under ethics approval (No. 2020-1-34, Central South University). For immunohistochemistry (IHC), sections were deparaffinized (37 °C, 30 min), antigen-retrieved in citrate buffer (98 °C, 30 min), and blocked with 0.3% Triton X-100/5% normal goat serum. Primary ALDH3A2 antibody incubation (4 °C, overnight) was followed by HRP-conjugated secondary antibody (2 h) and DAB visualization. Staining intensity (0: none; 1+: weak; 2+: moderate; 3+: strong) and cellular distribution (1+: 0–10%; 2+: 11–40%; 3+: 41–70%; 4+: 71–100%) were scored independently. The composite score was calculated by multiplying intensity and proportion scores.

Hematoxylin and Eosin (HE) staining

Following collection of pulmonary tissues from mice, specimens were saline-rinsed and fixed in 4% neutral buffered formalin via 24-hour immersion. Subsequently, fixed tissues underwent graded ethanol dehydration prior to paraffin embedding. Sectioning (4 μm), hematoxylin-eosin (HE) staining, microscopic imaging, and histopathological analysis were performed by Biossci (Hubei, China) under established protocols.

Animal studies

BALB/c nude mice (4-week-old, Bioonc, Changsha, China) were housed under specific pathogen-free (SPF) conditions (25 °C, 60 ± 10% humidity, 12 h light/dark cycle) with ad libitum access to food/water. Metastasis assays employed two randomized groups (n = 5/group) injected intravenously with 5 × 10⁶ MDA-MB-231 cells transduced with ALDH3A2-overexpressing or control vectors. Bioluminescent imaging utilized intraperitoneal D-luciferin (150 mg/kg, Cat#2591-17-5, MCE, USA) at 21/35 days post-injection. To conduct metformin assay, 3 days after cancer cell inoculation, Metformin (200 mg/kg/d; Aladdin, Riverside, CA, USA) dissolved in water or same amount of water control were administered by gavage. On day 21, mice were used for further studies. For Oxaliplatin treatment, 3 days after cancer cell inoculation, the mice were intraperitoneally injected with Oxaliplatin (100 µl, 5 mg/kg, every three days; Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) or 100 µl 5% glucose (control). On day 21, mice were used for further studies. Protocols followed institutional guidelines (No. 2020-2-35, Central South University).

Generation of ALDH3A2 knockout (KO) mice

ALDH3A2 KO mice (C57BL/6 background) were obtained from Cyagen (China). Heterozygous (ALDH3A2+/−) mice were intercrossed to generate WT and homozygous KO offspring. Genomic DNA from tail biopsies was amplified using ALDH3A2 control F/R and KO F/R primers (Table 1) for genotype verification.

RNA extraction and quantitative PCR

Total RNA was isolated using the RNA Simple Total RNA Kit (Cat#DP419, TIANGEN, China). Reverse transcription of 1 µg RNA utilized FastKing gDNA Dispelling RT SuperMix (Cat#KR118, TIANGEN, China). Quantitative RT-PCR was performed with SYBR Green Master Mix (Cat#11201ES03, YEASEN, China) and primers listed in Table 1. Gene expression was normalized to GAPDH and analyzed using the 2−ΔΔCt method.

Western blot

Protein extraction was conducted with RIPA buffer (0.1% SDS, 1% NP-40, 0.5% sodium deoxycholate, 50 mM Tris, pH 7.4) containing protease inhibitors (Cat#GK10014, GlpBio, USA) and phosphatase inhibitors (Cat#GK10011, GlpBio, USA). Protein quantification was achieved through BCA reagent (Cat#BCA02, DingGuo, China). After SDS-PAGE separation and PVDF membrane transfer, immunoblotting was carried out using specific primary antibodies: FLAG (Cat#F1804, Sigma, USA), GAPDH (Cat#AP0066, Bioworld, USA), Vimentin (Cat#D21H3, Cell Signaling Technology, USA), E-cadherin (Cat#3195, Cell Signaling Technology, USA), mTOR (Cat#2972, Cell Signaling Technology, USA), p-mTOR (Cat#5536s, Cell Signaling Technology, USA), SREBP1 (Cat#sc-365514, Santa Cruz, USA), Raptor (Cat#2280s, Cell Signaling Technology, USA), AMPK (Cat#5831, Cell Signaling Technology, USA), and p-AMPK (Cat#2535, Cell Signaling Technology, USA). Detection employed HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies from Cell Signaling Technology.

Nile red staining

For lipid droplet visualization, cells were fixed with 4% formaldehyde (15 min) and subjected to triple PBS washing (5 min each). Nile Red (ThermoFisher) working solution (1:1000 dilution in PBS from acetone stock) was applied for 30 min. Following three additional PBS washes, nuclear counterstaining with DAPI (3 min) preceded final PBS rinses. Mounted specimens were imaged microscopically.

Oil red O staining

Cells fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde (20 min) were treated with filtered Oil Red O solution (3:2 water dilution through 0.45 μm filter) for 15 min after 60% isopropanol rinse. Hematoxylin counterstaining preceded microscopic analysis of lipid deposits.

Total triglyceride extraction

Post-PBS washing, cellular triglycerides were extracted using enzymatic assay kits (Cat#E1013, Applygen, China). Lysates underwent thermal treatment (70 °C, 10 min) and centrifugation (2000 rpm, 5 min) prior to enzymatic quantification of supernatant TG content.

Arachidonic acid assay

The cell suspension was diluted with PBS to a concentration of approximately 1 × 107 cells/ml. The cells were then lysed using ultrasonic waves and suspended at 2000 rpm and 4 °C for 20 min to collect the supernatant. The samples underwent three freeze-thaw cycles. The arachidonic acid content was assessed by the arachidonic acid ELISA kit (cat# MM-926637O1, Jiangsu Meimian, China).

ATP detection assay

Lyophilized substrate (100 µL/well) was pre-equilibrated (10 min, RT) in 96-well plates. Cell suspensions (5,000 cells/well in 100 µL) were added followed by orbital shaking (700 rpm, 2 min). Bioluminescence signals were immediately recorded.

Data collection and differential analysis

Three breast cancer metastasis-related datasets, including GSE62598 (Tabaries et al. 2015), GSE193103 (Garcia-Recio et al. 2023), and GSE56493 (Tobin et al. 2015), were retrieved from the NCBI GEO repository. The TCGA-BRCA dataset (https://www.cancer.gov/tcga) was additionally analyzed, containing gene expression profiles and corresponding clinical metadata. Differential expression analysis was conducted using DESeq2 v1.40.2, where DESeqDataSet objects were initialized with default settings (Love et al. 2014). Genes meeting the threshold (absolute log2 fold change > 1, p-value < 0.05) were classified as upregulated (“Up”), downregulated (“Down”), or non-differentially expressed (“None”). Survival analysis was performed using survival data and gene expression profiles from a cohort of 1067 breast cancer patients (Gyorffy 2021). Patients were stratified by ALDH3A2 expression levels, and the five-year survival rates were scored.

Functional analysis

Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA v4.3.3) was implemented on TCGA-BRCA samples stratified by ALDH3A2 expression levels (high vs. low). Pathway enrichment analysis interrogated the GO-BP, KEGG, and Hallmark databases under default parameters. Significance thresholds were defined as |normalized enrichment score (NES)| >1 with p < 0.05 and false discovery rate (q) < 0.25.

Metabolomics sequencing

Six replicates of ALDH3A2-overexpressing and control cells underwent untargeted metabolomic profiling (Allwegene, China). Cell pellets (1 × 107cells) were homogenized in 1 mL extraction solvent (methanol: acetonitrile: water, 2:2:1 v/v) spiked with isotope-labeled internal standards. Samples were vortex-mixed (30 s), flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen (1 min), thawed, and revortexed. After equilibration (−40 °C, 1 h), centrifugation (12,000 ×g, 15 min, 4 °C) yielded supernatants for LC-MS/MS analysis using Vanquish UPLC system (Thermo Fisher Scientific), Orbitrap Exploris 120 mass spectrometer, and ACQUITY UPLC BEH Amide column (1.7 μm, 2.1 × 50 mm). Raw data were converted to mzXML format (ProteoWizard) and processed via XCMS for peak alignment/annotation, supplemented by custom R scripts and the AllwegeneDB spectral library.

Virtual screening and molecular docking

Drug sensitivity predictions employed oncoPredict v1.2 with GDSC v2 and CTRP2 as reference databases (Maeser et al. 2021). IC50 values were compared between high/low expression cohorts. Consensus hits from both databases identified candidate therapeutics. Molecular docking simulations (AutoDock Vina 1.2.2) evaluated ligand-receptor binding affinities, with binding poses analyzed for critical residue interactions (Eberhardt et al. 2021).

Statistical analysis

GraphPad Prism 9.5 processed all data (mean ± SD). Between-group differences were assessed by Student’s t-test or one-way ANOVA. Significance levels: *P < 0.05; **P < 0.01; ***P < 0.001; ns, not significant. All experiments included ≥ 3 biological replicates.

Results

ALDH3A2 is markedly overexpressed in breast cancer metastasis and correlated with a worse clinical prognosis

To uncover potential factors involved in the initiation and progression of breast cancer metastasis, we performed data mining analysis using Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) datasets, including two human breast cancer metastasis datasets (GSE56493 and GSE193103), as well as one mouse breast cancer metastasis dataset (GSE62598). Differential expression analysis identified 3,170 upregulated and 10,067 downregulated differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in GSE193103, 378 upregulated and 495 downregulated DEGs in GSE56493, and 311 upregulated and 101 downregulated DEGs in GSE62598 (Fig. 1A). Using Venn diagram analysis, we found 20 DEGs consistently dysregulated in metastatic cells compared to parental cells or primary breast tumor cells across all three datasets (Fig. 1B). Among these, ALDH3A2 (also known as Fatty Aldehyde Dehydrogenase, FALDH) emerged as a significantly upregulated gene (Fig. 1C). Survival analysis revealed that patients with elevated ALDH3A2 expression exhibited a significantly lower five-year survival rate (Fig. 1D). To investigate the expression of ALDH3A2 across different breast cancer subtypes, we analyzed gene expression data from breast cancer cell lines in the CCLE datasets (http://www.broadinstitute.org/ccle), which included 22 TNBC and 18 non-TNBC cell lines. Compared to HR-HER2 + and HR + HER2 + subtypes [Hormone receptor (HR) stands for progesterone receptors (PR) and estrogen receptors (ER)], ALDH3A2 expression showed a increasing trend in TNBC (HR-HER2-) subtypes (Supplemental Fig. 1A). When all non-TNBC data were pooled, ALDH3A2 expression was significantly higher in TNBC samples than in non-TNBC samples (Supplemental Fig. 1B). We further examined ALDH3A2 expression in non-TNBC and TNBC patients by immunohistochemical staining. Consistently, the results demonstrated a significant elevation of ALDH3A2 expression in TNBC samples compared to non-TNBC tissues (Fig. 1E-F). Collectively, these findings suggested that ALDH3A2 was prominently expressed in TNBC, highlighting a potential correlation with a worse clinical prognosis.

ALDH3A2 is required for migration and invasion in TNBC in vitro and in vivo

To validate the functional role of ALDH3A2 in TNBC, we overexpressed ALDH3A2 in TNBC cells and utilized short hairpin RNAs (shRNAs) to knock down ALDH3A2. To confirm the shRNAs specifically targeted ALDH3A2, we examined the mRNA expression of ALDH3A2 family genes. The data showed that shRNA we used could only knock down ALDH3A2, but not ALDH1A1 or ALDH2 (Supplemental Fig. 2). CCK8 assay results showed that ALDH3A2 had no impact on cell proliferation in TNBC cells (Supplemental Fig. 3). However, transwell assay results demonstrated that knockdown of ALDH3A2 impaired cell migration and invasion, while overexpression of ALDH3A2 significantly enhanced these processes (Fig. 2A–D and Supplemental Fig. 4). To further test the specificity of ALDH3A2 in TNBC cell lines, we performed transwell assay in T47D cells. The results showed that ALDH3A2 had no impact on cell migration in non-TNBC cells (Supplemental Fig. 5). For in vivo studies, luciferase-labeled MDA-MB-231 cells overexpressing ALDH3A2 were intravenously injected into mice (Supplemental Fig. 6). H&E staining from sacrificed mice histologically validated tumor burden in the lung metastases, corroborating our bioluminescence findings (Fig. 2E). Western blot analysis confirmed the efficiency of shRNA-mediated ALDH3A2 silencing and overexpression (Fig. 2F–G). In mechanistic studies, we assessed epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT)-related markers and found that E-cadherin was decreased, whereas Vimentin was increased in ALDH3A2-overexpressing cells compared to control cells (Fig. 2H). Conversely, the sh-ALDH3A2 group exhibited the opposite effects (Fig. 2I). These data collectively demonstrated that ALDH3A2 promotes TNBC cell metastasis via EMT.

Functional enrichment analyses reveal that ALDH3A2 modulates lipid metabolic reprogramming

To investigate the molecular mechanisms underlying ALDH3A2-regulated breast cancer metastasis, we performed Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) on TCGA-BRCA data stratified by ALDH3A2 expression levels. Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analysis indicated enrichment in endocytosis, peroxisome function, and fatty acid metabolism in TNBC patients (Fig. 3A). Gene Ontology (GO) analysis showed that genes co-expressed with ALDH3A2 were involved in glycerolipid biosynthesis and metastasis-related processes (Fig. 3A). Hallmark gene set analysis further highlighted associations with estrogen response, adipogenesis, and fatty acid metabolism (Fig. 3A). Consistently, we found that fatty acid metabolism was enriched in ALDH3A2 high-expression groups within CPTAC cohort (Supplemental Fig. 7). In addition, the specificity of ALDH3A2 in various breast cancer subtypes were also examined. We found that signaling pathway involved in heme metabolism, estrogen response, UV response and fatty acid metabolism were enriched in HR + patients, while DNA repair, oxidative phosphorylation, estrogen response, xenobiotic metabolism and fatty acid metabolism were enriched in HER2 + patients (Supplemental Fig. 8). Due to fatty acid metabolism was not the top ranking signaling pathways regulated by ALDH3A2 in non-TNBC patients, there was possibility that ALDH3A2 exerted its function specifically through fatty acid metabolism in TNBC cells. To further validate these findings, we examined the expression of key lipid metabolism genes in ALDH3A2-manipulated TNBC cells. ALDH3A2 overexpression upregulated SREBP-1, FASN, ACLY, PPAR-γ, and PPAR-α, whereas ALDH3A2 knockdown downregulated these genes (Fig. 3B–C, Supplemental Fig. 9). Nile Red staining revealed that ALDH3A2 overexpression increased neutral lipid content, while ALDH3A2 knockdown suppressed lipid droplet formation (Fig. 3D-E). Consistent results were obtained from Oil Red O staining (Supplemental Fig. 10). Biochemical assays further confirmed that ALDH3A2 overexpression elevated triglyceride (TG) levels, whereas ALDH3A2 silencing reduced TG levels (Fig. 3F–I). Together, these data suggested a pivotal role of ALDH3A2 in regulating lipid metabolism in TNBC cells.

ALDH3A2 targets arachidonic acid to regulate lipid metabolism

To assess ALDH3A2’s role in lipid metabolism, we performed untargeted lipidomic profiling of ALDH3A2-overexpressing TNBC cells and control cells. The analysis identified significant alterations in lipid metabolites, including amino acid biosynthesis, purine metabolism, and glycerophospholipid metabolism (Fig. 4A). Strikingly, arachidonic acid (AA), a key component of glycerophospholipids, was markedly increased in the ALDH3A2 overexpression group (Fig. 4B). Quantitative analysis confirmed elevated AA levels in ALDH3A2-overexpressing TNBC cells (Fig. 4C). In ALDH3A2 knockout mice (ALDH3A2−/−), AA levels were significantly reduced compared to controls (Fig. 4D). Functional rescue experiments showed that exogenous AA supplementation restored migration and invasion capacities in ALDH3A2-depleted cells (Fig. 4E-G). Furthermore, AA treatment rescued the suppression of lipid droplets and TG levels caused by ALDH3A2 knockdown (Fig. 4H-I). These results collectively indicated that ALDH3A2 activates AA metabolism to promote TNBC metastasis.

ALDH3A2 regulates lipid metabolism via AMP/AMPK signaling pathway in TNBC

Previous studies suggest that AA inhibits AMP production (Cheng et al. 2007). Consistent with this, our data showed reduced AMP levels in ALDH3A2-overexpressing cells (Fig. 4B). It has already been documented that an elevated ratio of AMP: ATP ratio indicates a compromised cellular energy status (Steinberg and Hardie 2023). Thereto, we measured intracellular ATP levels and found that ALDH3A2 overexpression increased ATP concentration, whereas ALDH3A2 knockdown decreased it (Fig. 5A-B). Notably, exogenous AA supplementation further elevated ATP levels, an effect attenuated by ALDH3A2 knockdown (Fig. 5C). Nile Red staining demonstrated that AMP supplementation reversed ALDH3A2-induced lipid accumulation (Fig. 5D). Similarly, AMP treatment restored TG levels in ALDH3A2-overexpressing cells (Fig. 5E). Given that AMPK is responsive to fluctuations in the intracellular AMP/ATP ratio, we examined the phosphorylation of AMPK in TNBC cells with and without ALDH3A2 overexpression. Western blot analysis revealed that ALDH3A2 overexpression decreased AMPK phosphorylation (p-AMPK), while ALDH3A2 knockdown increased p-AMPK levels (Fig. 5F-G). To further verify that ALDH3A2 promoted breast cancer metastasis via AMPK signaling in vivo, an AMPK agonist Metformin was tested in mice intravenously injected with luciferase-labeled MDA-MB-231 cells overexpressing ALDH3A2. As shown in Supplemental Fig. 5, Metformin treatment could inhibit breast cancer lung metastasis induced by ALDH3A2. Taken all together, these findings suggested that ALDH3A2 regulates lipid metabolism and breast cancer metastasis through AMP/AMPK signaling.

ALDH3A2 promotes m-TOR/SREBP1 signaling pathway

Many studies has highlighted that mTOR can be suppressed by its upstream agent AMPK (Ponnusamy et al. 2020). Based on these findings, we hypothesized that ALDH3A2 regulates lipid metabolic reprogramming via AMPK/m-TOR signaling pathway, thereby facilitating breast cancer metastasis. GSEA based on the KEGG database indicated upregulation of m-TOR signaling pathway in ALDH3A2 overexpressing group (Fig. 6A). The protein expression of mTOR, p-mTOR, Raptor and SREBP1 were examined in TNBC cells either knockdown ALDH3A2 or overexpressing ALDH3A2 (Fig. 6B-C). The western blot results showed that ALDH3A2 promoted mTOR/SREBP1 expression in TNBC cells, whereas ALDH3A2 knockdown decreased their levels. Strikingly, Z-H Wen et al. found that AA is an effective intracellular stimulus of mTOR and that AA-activated mTOR plays critical roles in angiogenesis and tumorigenesis of breast cancer (Wen et al. 2013). Consistently, AA supplementation can restore the reduction of mTOR/SREBP1 expression induced by knockdown of ALDH3A2 (Supplemental Fig. 11). To investigate whether inhibiting mTOR can reverse the function of ALDH3A2, TNBC cells either overexpression ALDH3A2 or transfected with control were treated with the mTOR inhibitor rapamycin. The results showed that rapamycin treatment significantly reduced the accumulation of lipid droplets and TG levels in ALDH3A2 overexpressing cells (Fig. 6D-E). Furthermore, western blot revealed that rapamycin treatment reversed the elevated mTOR and SREBP1 expression levels triggered by ALDH3A2 (Fig. 6F). In addition, transwell assay showed that rapamycin greatly inhibited cell migration and invasion in ALDH3A2 overexpressing cells (Supplemental Fig. 12A). Similarly, extracellular AMP supplementation can also inhibit expression levels of mTOR and SREBP1, and reverse ALDH3A2-induced cell migration and invasion (Fig. 6G and Supplemental Fig. 12B). Collectively, the results demonstrated that ALDH3A2 targeted AA to regulate the mTOR/SREBP1 signaling pathway, which in turn modulated lipid metabolism, the migration and invasion in TNBC cells.

Oxaliplatin targets ALDH3A2 to inhibit TNBC metastasis

Given the vital role of ALDH3A2 in TNBC metastasis, the discovery of inhibitors that target this protein has piqued significant interest. Therefore, we screened small molecule inhibitors targeting ALDH3A2. Based on Cancer Therapeutics Response Portal (CTRP) and Genomics of Drug Sensitivity in Cancer (GDSC), there were seven potential correlated drugs targeting ALDH3A2, including BMS-754,807, Dinaciclib, Ibrutinib, NVP-ADW742, Oxaliplatin, and PLX-4720. Among them, Oxaliplatin, a platinum-based anticancer drug, exhibited a high interaction strength with ALDH3A2 in molecular docking studies. Oxaliplatin interacted with several amino acid residues of ALDH3A2, such as the carbonyl group with Arg432 and Lys428 (Fig. 7A). Additionally, there was an interaction between the platinum atom of Oxaliplatin and Asn436 (Fig. 7B). Hydrogen bonds were the primary interaction forces between Oxaliplatin and ALDH3A2 (Fig. 7C). According to the data from western blot, Oxaliplatin was observed to hinder ALDH3A2 but induce p-AMPK expression in TNBC cells (Fig. 7D). As illustrated by the results of the transwell assays, Oxaliplatin decreased the migration and invasion of TNBC cells (Fig. 7E). Moreover, 10µmol/L Oxaliplatin successfully impeded AA level in TNBC cells compared to the control group (Fig. 7F). To define its effect on breast cancer metastasis in vivo, the mice intravenously injected with luciferase-labeled MDA-MB-231 cells were treated with Oxaliplatin. Bioluminescence imaging showed that Oxaliplatin treated group exhibited significantly lower signals than that in the control group, suggesting that Oxaliplatin suppressed tumor metastasis (Supplemental Fig. 13). Taken all together, the data suggested that Oxaliplatin targeted ALDH3A2 to inhibit TNBC metastasis via regulating AA content (Fig. 7G).

Discussion

ALDH family encompasses 19 distinct isozymes, which are involved in oxidation of both endogenous and exogenous aldehydes into their corresponding carboxylic acids (Chang et al. 2018). ALDH participate in a variety of cellular functions, such as detoxification, biosynthesis, antioxidant activities and regulatory functions (Shortall et al. 2021). Notably, ALDH3A2 emerges as a potent tumor suppressor in clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC) by inhibiting the PI3K-AKT signaling pathway, thereby decreasing intracellular lipid accumulation (Lv et al. 2024). Conversely, ALDH3A2 knockout enhances ferroptosis sensitivity in ovarian cancer cells by activating of lipid metabolic, glutathione (GSH) metabolic, phospholipid metabolic, and aldehyde metabolic pathways (Dong et al. 2023). Consistently, loss of ALDH3A2 in leukemic cells triggers ferroptosis by modifying oxidative damage, biosynthetic pathways and cellular lipid composition (Yusuf et al. 2020). Apparently, ALDH3A2 plays a vital role in regulating lipid metabolism reprogramming in cancer cells. Nonetheless, not much is known about the association with ALDH3A2 and lipid metabolism in TNBC metastasis.

In our study, we observed that ALDH3A2 is significantly up-regulated in TNBC, highlighting a potential correlation with a worse clinical prognosis. Moreover, ALDH3A2 is a positive regulator of migration and invasion in TNBC cells. Transcriptomic sequencing in TNBC cells overexpressing ALDH3A2 revealed an association between fatty acid metabolism and ALDH3A2 expression. Additionally, elevated ALDH3A2 levels led to increased lipid droplet accumulation. At the molecular level, ALDH3A2 overexpression activated SREBP1 and lipogenic enzymes, including FASN, ACLY, PPAR-γ, and PPAR-α. These findings indicate that ALDH3A2 promotes metastasis and lipid deposition in TNBC cells.

Aberrant lipid metabolism in TNBC may supply unsaturated fatty acids for phospholipid synthesis and cell membrane formation, in addition to generating energy, which collectively promotes TNBC progression and survival (Hopperton et al. 2014; Wang et al. 2022). Concurrently, various enzymes and metabolites associated with lipid metabolism are significantly altered in TNBC (Hilvo et al. 2011; Haukaas et al. 2017). Untargeted metabolomics revealed that ALDH3A2 influences TNBC primarily by disrupting lipid metabolic processes, particularly glycerophospholipid metabolism. Akio Kihara has reported that Sphingosine 1-phosphate (S1P) is cleaved to a fatty aldehyde hexadecenal, which is oxidized by ALDH3A2. The resulting hexadecenoic acid can be transformed to palmitoyl-CoA, which then participates in the established glycerophospholipid synthetic pathway (Ohkuni et al. 2013; Kihara 2014). Glycerophospholipids are known to be essential components of biological membranes. It has demonstrated that glycerophospholipid are critical for the proliferation of both esophageal squamous cell carcinoma and breast cancer (Wang et al. 2022; Yang et al. 2022).

Bioactive lipid molecules, such as arachidonic acid (AA), inositol trisphosphate, diacylglycerol, phosphatidic acid, and lysophosphatidic acid are produced during glycerophospholipid metabolism process and involved in regulating various cellular signaling pathways (Tappia 2007; Chen et al. 2024). Notably, ALDH3A2 targets AA, which is a ω-6 polyunsaturated fatty acid (PUFA) found in the phospholipids of cell membranes. It has reported that AA can inhibit the growth of lung adenocarcinoma by enhancing lipid peroxidation and reducing ALDH3A1 expression (Muzio et al. 2006). Additionally, exposure to a byproduct of AA catabolism, 4-HNE, increased cellular ROS, lipid peroxidation and proliferation in ALDH3A2-mutant leukemia compared with control cells (Yusuf et al. 2020). In our study, AA content was increased in ALDH3A2 overexpressing group in vitro and in vivo. Furthermore, the inhibition of metastasis, lipid droplets and TG levels in ALDH3A2 depletion group can be restored upon AA treatment, further suggesting that ALDH3A2 promotes metastasis via AA metabolism in TNBC cells. Consistently, as an AA precursor, Linoleic acid (ω-6, PUFA) efficiently induced ALDH3A2 expression in rat hepatoma cells through activation of PPAR-α (Ashibe and Motojima 2009). Notably, the high intake of ω-6 PUFAs can promote breast cancer development (Thiébaut et al. 2009). This acceleration effect of ω-6 PUFAs in tumorigenesis is attributed to AA metabolism (Abel et al. 2014). Therefore, the possible utility of ALDH3A2 inhibitor as preventive and therapeutic antitumor agents by targeting the AA pathway could be beneficial in controlling breast cancer progression.

It is well established that altering the AMP/ATP ratio within cancer cells impacts enzymatic activities and metabolic and signaling pathways reliant on energy balance (Hsu et al. 2022). Our study demonstrated that ALDH3A2 inhibited the AMP activity, thereby activating AMPK and affecting the cellular energy charge as indicated by the ATP content. AMPK, a crucial regulator of cellular energy balance, orchestrates various metabolic pathways to maintain energy homeostasis and control cellular growth (Keerthana et al. 2023; Sharma et al. 2023). It directly interacts ATP, ADP, and AMP, becoming activated when ATP level decreases (conversion of AMP and ADP to ATP) (Trefts and Shaw 2021). Activated AMPK inhibits mTOR signaling through multiple mechanisms (Paquette et al. 2018). Our studies discovered that ALDH3A2 increased the sensitivity of metastatic breast cancer to the mTOR inhibitor rapamycin. Thus, ALDH3A2 may considered as a potential biomarker and therapeutic target for breast cancer.

Oxaliplatin is a third-generation platinum anticancer agent (Zhang et al. 2022). While Oxaliplatin-based treatments are effective for colorectal cancer; However, their use in breast cancer chemotherapy remains limited (Shahlaei et al. 2024). Western blot analysis revealed that Oxaliplatin inhibited ALDH3A2 expression in TNBC cells. In vitro experiments showed that Oxaliplatin decreased the migration and invasion of TNBC cells overexpressing ALDH3A2. Moreover, Oxaliplatin impeded AA levels in ALDH3A2 overexpressing cells compared to the control group. These findings suggested that Oxaliplatin targets ALDH3A2 to inhibit TNBC metastasis via regulating AA content. A mouse metastasis model confirmed that Oxaliplatin markedly reduced breast cancer metastatic burden. Thereto, Oxaliplatin might be a viable option for TNBC patients with high ALDH3A2 expression. Future clinical trials are warranted to further evaluate the efficacy of oxaliplatin-based regimens in TNBC patients.

Interestingly, in vivo experiments also demonstrated that ALDH3A2 could facilitate the breast cancer brain metastasis (Supplemental Fig. 14), however, we have to explicitly address the discrepancies regarding breast cancer metastasis timing. As we noticed a higher chance of brain metastasis over longer periods (35 days), while lung metastasis appeared to be the predominant phenomenon within shorter timeframes (21 days; Supplemental Fig. 5). Thus, it is interesting to unravel the detailed mechanisms behind these observations in the future. As a significant clinical issue in breast cancer, studying brain metastasis requires more refined experimental designs.

Conclusions

Our study reveals ALDH3A2 as a key metastasis driver significantly upregulated in triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC). Functional validation demonstrated that ALDH3A2 overexpression promotes metastatic progression through lipid metabolism reprogramming. Mechanistically, ALDH3A2 orchestrates arachidonic acid (AA) accumulation and AMP depletion in TNBC cells by activating the AMPK/mTOR/SREBP1 signaling axis, thereby fueling tumor cell migration and invasion. Therapeutic exploration identified Oxaliplatin as a potent ALDH3A2-targeting agent that suppresses TNBC metastasis via AA metabolism modulation. Collectively, our findings uncover the pathogenic role of ALDH3A2 in bridging lipid metabolic rewiring with metastatic progression in TNBC, establishing ALDH3A2-dependent metabolic functions as promising diagnostic biomarkers and therapeutic vulnerabilities for this aggressive breast cancer subtype.

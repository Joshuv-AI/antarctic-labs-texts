Anti-disturbance control of CPG bionic reflection in pneumatic muscle actuator
Authors: Lina Wang, Zeling Chen, Xiaofeng Wang, Binrui Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
SummaryAddressing the joint control problem of pneumatic muscle-driven robots, this study aims to design a bionic reflex mechanism to enhance the robots’ adaptive capacity to various disturbances. Based on the biological reflex mechanism, we developed a spindle reflex and deep tendon reflex control system based on CPG (central pattern generator) to mitigate the sudden impact on the hip joint and the continuous blocking force on the knee joint, respectively. The spindle reflex controller incorporates the fast response of sliding mode control to effectively minimize the trajectory deviation of the hip joint under impact disturbances. The deep tendon reflex controller integrates RBF neural network adaptive control and the Tegotae framework to suppress excessive tension in the knee joint and augment the system’s adaptability to the blocking force disturbances. The experimental results confirm that the two reflex mechanisms significantly enhance the robustness and flexibility of the pneumatic muscle-driven robot in motion.Subject areas: Bionics, Bioelectronics, Robotics

Introduction

Pneumatic artificial muscle (PAM) is extensively utilized across the domains of restorative health care, intelligent industry, and bionic robot1,2,3,4 because of its affordable, boasting a superior power-to-weight ratio, and adaptability.5,6,7 The working principle of PAM is based on the change of internal gas pressure, and the power output is realized by adjusting the muscle length.8 However, PAM also faces some challenges, including its inherent nonlinear characteristics, dynamic uncertainty caused by gas compressibility, hysteresis, and creep phenomena.9,10,11,12 These characteristics increase the difficulty of control system design and pose a potential impact on system performance and reliability.

In the control system driven by pneumatic muscles, external disturbances, and internal uncertainties pose inevitable challenges. In response to these challenges, researchers have developed a variety of control strategies. Early PID control and sliding mode control (SMC),13,14 to the neural network and active disturbance rejection control.15 In recent years, many researchers have proposed some more powerful control methods. Cai16 et al. introduced auxiliary signals, which effectively deal with the saturation problem of control input. Through coordinate transformation, the auxiliary signals are integrated into the control design framework. They model all unknown modeling errors and external disturbances as an unknown nonlinear function that satisfies certain bounded conditions and design a parameter estimator to estimate its upper bound to achieve effective suppression of disturbances. Cao17 et al. proposed an adaptive surrogate-based robust control method for PAM and integrated it with a nonlinear disturbance observer. As the surrogate mass augments, the tracking deviation tends to gradually approach the boundary related to the disturbance estimation error, and enhance the system’s efficiency and resilience in the face of unpredictable shocks and disturbances. Vo18 et al. suggested a responsive finite-duration tracking control strategy that is independent of force sensors, and utilized a force-driven time lag assessment approach to gauge the comprehensive system variability, thereby improving the control accuracy and reducing the chattering effect. However, most studies have the assumption that the collision disturbance or joint resistance disturbance encountered in practical applications is beyond the bound, which may lead to the failure of the control scheme to effectively deal with, and there may be a problem of compensation delay. Takahashi19 explored the possibility of realizing a biological-like reflection mechanism in a pneumatic muscle robot, and studied the two reflection paths of Ia and Ib, these two types of reflexes are derived from neurophysiology and refer to different types of spinal reflexes. The Ia fiber is mainly related to the muscle spindles in the muscle. When the muscle spindle is stretched, it triggers a rapid contraction reaction. Ib fibers are associated with Golgi tendon organs. When muscle strength reaches a certain threshold, it sends signals to the spinal cord, causing muscle relaxation and preventing excessive force. This kind of reflection mechanism is more suitable for biological principles than simple upper and lower bounds and has strong adaptability, which comes from the joint action of reasonable biological reflection mechanism and nervous system.20 Bionic control mostly adopts a top-down method, which monitors the body or responds to stimuli by adjusting the output of the central pattern generator (CPG). Among them, sensory information is crucial for the formation of reflection mechanisms, but it is not easy to quickly integrate it into the control architecture.21 Owaki22 proposed the concept of the Tegotae method, it describes the degree of matching between the expected action and the perceived response. In robotics, the Tegotae method is used to measure the consistency between the expected motion instructions issued by the controller and the sensory information received based on the motion generated by the controller. This method is applied to the design of local controllers for robots, especially in the CPG-based walking model, Owaki studied the integration mechanism of bionic morphology, bionic structure, and bionic function, and had the guiding idea of designing a sensory feedback mechanism.

In this paper, the double-joint leg mechanism driven by the pneumatic muscle is designated as the subject, relying on the principle of biological bionics, aiming to compensate for the adverse effects caused by interference. As shown in Figure 1. In the proposed scheme, based on the different characteristics of Ia reflection and Ib reflection, two reflection disturbance suppression mechanisms of spindle reflex and deep tendon reflex are designed to adjust the trajectory tracking effect of the joint. According to the fast response ability of SMC and the adaptive learning generalization ability of RBF neural network adaptive control (RBF-NNAC), the spindle reflex and SMC methods are combined, respectively. Aiming at the telescopic reaction of the pneumatic muscle, the CPG external spindle reflex model is designed to compensate for the disturbance. Combined with the deep tendon reflex and RBF-NNAC method, the deep tendon reflex pathway of CPG internal feedback was designed according to Tegotae framework to regulate the excessive tension of pneumatic muscles.

Controller design

The spindle between muscle fibers is extremely sensitive to changes in muscle length, and its rapid response mechanism is crucial for motion control. The deep tendon reflex, triggered by the tendon organ in the tendon, responds to changes in muscle tension and forms a negative feedback mechanism to maintain the stability of muscle tension.24 In biological kinematics, the hip joint occupies a larger value in gait, while the knee joint frequently participates in tendon reflex, such as the knee bounce reflex.25 Therefore, the spindle reflex pathway is designed for the hip joint of the pneumatic muscle leg mechanism, and the deep tendon reflex pathway is designed for the knee joint.

Spindle reflection controller design

As shown in Figure 4, the controller hierarchy diagram of the spindle reflex pathway shows three main levels. The left layer in the yellow background consists of a CPG control network whose output rhythm signal defines the target trajectory of the joint. The middle layer with a blue background is integrated with a sliding mode controller, and the position control law is designed according to the expected angle signal of the left layer and the actual angle signal of the right layer, and the output torque is converted into a pressure signal through a torque converter to drive the pneumatic muscle model. The pneumatic muscle model outputs the expansion length and tension of the pneumatic muscle. Compensation of pneumatic muscle pressure by the excited spindle of the intermediate layer. The right layer with the green background realizes the dynamic model of the leg joint driven by the pneumatic muscle, receives the tension signal from the middle layer, and outputs the actual joint angle signal.

Design of deep tendon reflex controller

As shown in Figure 6, the hierarchical structure diagram of the deep tendon reflex controller clearly defines the functions of the inner and outer layers. The outer layer includes the CPG model, RBF neural network adaptive controller, and pneumatic muscle single-leg mechanism model. This layer is responsible for approximating the uncertainty of the pneumatic muscle leg mechanism, and a neural network adaptive control law based on joint torque is designed. In addition, it is also responsible for outputting the tension and joint angle of the pneumatic muscle. The CPG model receives feedback signals from the inner layer and generates regular gait signals at the anticipated angle of the joint. The inner layer includes the modeling of Tegotae algorithm and the CPG feedback model, which together constitute the deep tendon reflex pathway.

Results and discussion

In this research, the efficacy of the spindle reflex control system and the deep tendon reflex control system were verified for two different muscle states. Firstly, two kinds of sudden shocks with different amplitudes were applied to the pneumatic muscle on the hip joint to evaluate the anti-disturbance ability of the spindle reflection on the pneumatic muscle. Secondly, to verify the effectiveness of deep tendon reflex in compensating the excessive tension of pneumatic muscle and inhibiting external interference, continuous blocking force and external interference were applied to the knee joint of the pneumatic muscle leg mechanism, respectively. Conclusion

In this study, two kinds of reflection control are designed for the robot leg joints driven by pneumatic muscles. Based on the advantages of pneumatic muscles in bionic control research and the biological reflection mechanism, the spindle reflection and deep tendon reflex control system based on CPG are designed. Through the comparison of four groups of experiments and three methods, the impact compensation of spindle reflection on pneumatic muscle driven joints under sudden disturbance is verified, and the limitations of pneumatic muscle stretching speed under CPG rhythm signal and different disturbances are considered. The inhibition of deep tendon reflex on pneumatic muscle tension under the disturbance of blocking force for a period of time is verified. Considering the advantages of Tegotae control in the design of the CPG feedback term, the pneumatic muscle tension is detected by the T function, and the CPG feedback term of inhibiting motion is output. The results show that the spindle reflex and deep tendon reflex can improve the adaptability of the pneumatic muscle leg mechanism in exercise.

Limitations of the study

The biological reflection mechanism is extremely complex, and a variety of neurotransmitters and signaling pathways are designed. Although the proposed spindle reflection and deep tendon reflex control methods have good functional bionic performance, they fail to fully simulate the complexity of real biological systems, and there are technical challenges for sensor integration applied to pneumatic muscles. Therefore, for the proposed reflection method, future research will further explore the cross-application of perceptual bionics and intelligent bionics in pneumatic muscle-driven robots.

Further description of blocking force perturbations

The blocking force perturbation d=−kblock·θ˙·δ we used was to verify that the deep tendon reflex control system causes excessive tension in the pneumatic muscle when the joint driven by the pneumatic muscle is affected by the perturbation. In practice, the increase of joint resistance driven by the pneumatic muscle will lead to the increase in the tension of the pneumatic muscle. Therefore, the blocking force we use is represented as d=−kblock·θ˙·δ force in the opposite direction of the joint motion, the magnitude of which is determined by kblock, and θ˙ is the tangential velocity of the joint, representing the direction. This blocking force is similar to the actual situation in that it captures the general characteristics of the actual system, such as the presence of foreign objects in the joint or the increased friction of the joint damage, that is, the increased joint resistance.

Description of robot motion disturbance

The perturbations we apply to the leg joints of a pneumatic muscle-driven robot include not only the case where the leg touches an obstacle, but also the case where there is a foreign body in the joint or the friction increases due to joint damage. Adding obstacle avoidance function to the walking process of the robot can effectively avoid obstacles on the path, but when the interference does not exist in the external environment, the obstacle avoidance function fails. Therefore, it is necessary to study different disturbance types. However, in the research on soft robots similar to those driven by pneumatic muscles, relatively high attention has been paid to joint resistance.

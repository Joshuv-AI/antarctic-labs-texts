Targeting relapsed/refractory and MRD + Ph + ALL: olverembatinib-venetoclax bridging enhances allo-HSCT outcome​
Authors: Xiaoyu Zhang, Yanhong Zhao, Weihua Zhai, Qiaoling Ma, Yigeng Cao, Jialin Wei, Chen Liang, Xin Chen, Wenbin Cao, Donglin Yang, Aiming Pang, Yi He, Sizhou Feng, Mingzhe Han, Rongli Zhang, Erlie Jiang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractOlverembatinib is a novel third-generation TKI available in mainland China. However, there are only a few published clinical reports. In this study, we aimed to investigate the efficacy and safety of an olverembatinib-venetoclax regimen before bridging to HSCT in adult Ph/BCR-ABL1 + ALL patients with refractory/relapsed disease (n = 2) or persistent minimal residual disease (MRD) (n = 15). Seventeen Ph + ALL patients who were admitted to our center between February 2022 and January 2023 were enrolled. Four patients harbored a T315I mutation. In all, 100% achieved hematologic complete remission, and 70.6% achieved complete molecular remission. With a median follow-up of 856-day post-HSCT, the 2-year overall survival and relapsed free survival rates was 88.2 ± 7.8% and 79.4 ± 10.9%, respectively. The findings of this study suggest that in Ph + ALL patients with disease recurrence and persistent MRD positivity, olverembatinib-venetoclax regimen showed a high molecular response rate and was well-tolerated in MRD clearance bridged to allo-HSCT.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00277-025-06708-0.Keywords: Ph + ALL, Olverembatinib, Allo-HSCT, Venetoclax, Complete molecular remission

Despite significant progress in tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) and optimized allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (allo-HSCT) protocols, the prognosis of refractory Philadelphia chromosome (Ph)/BCR-ABL1 + acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL) remains dismal, as post-transplant relapse compromises survival outcomes. Prior studies have reported the limited efficacy of the second-/third-generation TKIs monotherapy in this scenario [1, 2]. This highlights the urgent need for novel strategies to attain meaningful remission depth prior to HSCT [3]. As the only approved third-generation TKI in mainland China, olverembatinib has demonstrated potent activity against T315I-mutated chronic myeloid leukemia (CML) resistant to prior therapies [4]. Meanwhile, emerging evidence highlights synergistic antileukemic activity between TKIs and Bcl-2 inhibitors [5, 6], with reports demonstrating improved response rates using a triplet combination of ponatinib, venetoclax, and dexamethasone in relapsed Ph + ALL [7, 8]. However, clinical studies on olverembatinib-venetoclax in Ph + ALL are still limited.

This single-center case series included 17 refractory/relapsed (R/R) (n = 2) and minimal residual disease (MRD) persistent positive (n = 15) Ph + ALL patients who were treated with olverembatinib-related therapy between February 20, 2022, and January 31, 2023 at the Stem Cell Transplantation Center, Institute of Hematology and Blood Diseases Hospital, Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences. This study was part of a larger study, the “NICHE-BMT” project (NCT04645199). A detailed flowchart outlining the patient screening process is provided in Supplementary Fig. 1. The regimen included olverembatinib (40 mg QOD) combined with venetoclax (100 mg d1, 200 mg d2, 400 mg, d3–28) + prednisone (1 mg/kg, d1 ~ 14) + vincristine (1.5 mg/m2, d1, d8) regimen. The last follow-up date was April 16, 2025. Informed consent was obtained from all recruited patients before olverembatinib treatment and data collection. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Institute of Hematology and Blood Diseases Hospital and all patients or guardians provided informed written consent in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki (IIT2021011-EC-1). Response assessments were performed according to the National Comprehensive Cancer Network guidelines for acute lymphoblastic leukemia (version 2. 2021). Recurrence-free survival-free survival (RFS) was defined as the time from complete remission after the transplantation until relapse, death, or the last follow-up date. Overall survival (OS) was defined as the time from treatment start to death or last follow-up. Adverse events were graded according to the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events, version 5.0. BCR-ABL1 + kinase domain mutation analysis was performed by using direct Sanger sequencing. The survival probabilities were assessed according to Kaplan-Meier method. Statistical analysis was conducted with SPSS version 24.0 software (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA).

The baseline characteristics of the 17 enrolled patients (2 with hematology relapse, 9 with persistent positive MRD, and 1 with relapsed MRD) are detailed in Table 1. Four patients harbored a T315I mutation. Two patients had received prior blinatumomab or INO treatment. All patients had previously failed TKI treatment, with four of them who did not respond to at least two different TKIs. The treatment details and responses are presented in Fig. 1 and Supplementary Table 1. The median number of chemotherapies courses before enrollment was 3 (range: 3–11).

The median number of olverembatinib + venetoclax regimen cycles was 2 (range, 1–3 cycles). After one cycle, 10 patients (58.8%) achieved complete molecular remission (CMR). Three more patients achieved CMR later during the second cycle of consolidation treatment. Overall, all 2/2 patients with hematological recurrence achieved CMR, and 10 of the 15 MRD + patients achieved CMR, resulting in an overall CMR rate of 70.6%. Three patients who failed MRD clearance after one cycle of olverembatinib and venetoclax regimen proceeded to the CD19 bispecific T-cell Engager (Blinatumomab, BiTE) therapy and only one attained MRD clearance. Notably, all patients with T315I mutations (4/4) achieved CMR before transplantation.

Two patients received maintenance therapy with TKI-based chemotherapy regimens due to severe infections and absence of donors. Fifteen (15/17) enrolled patients successfully underwent bridged allo-HSCT. Haploidentical transplants were the predominant HSCT type (n = 10). The dominant conditioning regimen was total body irradiation (TBI)-based therapy (n = 13). Hematopoietic stem cells were collected from the peripheral blood (Supplementary Table1 ). All patients successfully achieved myeloid engraftment within a median of 12 (9–14) days, and platelet engraftment within a median of 15 (10–30) days. No primary engraftment failure was observed. We recorded three cases of bacterial/fungal infections. Six patients had cytomegalovirus (CMV) viremia, and one had Epstein-Barr virus (EBV) viremia and developed into EBV-related post-transplant lymphoproliferative disease (PTLD) after transplantation. Additionally, five patients developed grade II-III acute graft-versus-host disease after transplantation, and no severity occurred. With a median follow-up time of 856 (60–1062) days post-regimen, two patients died of severe pneumonia and Guillain-Barré syndrome at 25 and 60 days after transplantation, respectively. Two patients experienced molecular relapse with detectable BCR-ABL1 + transcripts at 180 days and one-year post-HSCT, respectively. Nevertheless, the latter patient developed hematological relapse on day 434 after transplantation. The other patient achieved sustained CMR following preemptive treatment with olverembatinib and venetoclax. Overall, the two-year OS was 88.2 ± 7.8% and RFS was 79.4 ± 10.9%, respectively. For patients who underwent HSCT, the two-year OS was 86.7 ± 8.8% and RFS was 77 ± 12%, respectively. Ten patients received maintenance therapy with olverembatinib within 120 days after transplantation, and three patients received maintenance therapy with other TKIs due to financial limitations.

Olverembatinib-venetoclax involved regimen demonstrated favorable tolerance and safety. Gastrointestinal symptoms were observed in one patient, while another experienced dizziness. Nine patients experienced grade 1–2 neutropenia and thrombocytopenia. No cardiovascular events, proteinuria, creatine kinase (CK) increase, rash, hypertension, or infection were observed. All patients completed the treatment, and there were no cases of treatment suspension or discontinuation owing to drug intolerance. For patients using olverembatinib for maintenance treatment post-HSCT, 3 patients (3/10) experienced grade 1–2 neutropenia and thrombocytopenia, leading to a reduction in olverembatinib dose from 30 mg QoD to 20mgQoD.

The incorporation of blinatumomab, inotuzumab ozogamicin (INO), and venetoclax into novel TKI-based chemotherapy-free regimens has reshaped the treatment paradigm for Ph + ALL [9–11]. Despite these advances, patients who failed to achieve quick and sustained molecular remission remained a poor prognosis. The therapeutic synergy between ponatinib and venetoclax has been increasingly validated in R/R ALL, with phase 1/2 clinical trial demonstrating that this chemotherapy-free regimen achieves CR rates of 56% and one-year OS of 72%7. Our findings revealed that the CMR rate after the olverembatinib-venetoclax involved regimen prior to transplantation was 70.6%. And patients attained improved relapse-free survival post-HSCT. The efficacy of the MRD clearance treatment before HSCT was deemed satisfactory. Mechanistically, the improved outcome arises from the synergistic interaction between venetoclax and TKIs [12].

In our study, no adverse effects related to graft rejection, infection, or severe post-transplantation complications were observed. Notably, no cardiovascular events or serous effusion were reported peri-HSCT and no transplant-related deaths occurred. In short, the safety and tolerability of both bridging for transplantation and post-HSCT maintenance were favorable.

The limitations of our study were small sample size. Although these preliminary results support the feasibility and efficacy of olverembatinib-venetoclax involved regimen in treating R/R and MRD persistent Ph + ALL, larger prospective studies are needed to confirm these results.

In summary, our study demonstrates that the use of olverembatinib-venetoclax involved regimen prior to transplantation is effective and safe. Prompt allo-HSCT bridging post-olverembatinib-venetoclax treatment significantly enhances long-term outcomes in high-risk patients. Moreover, our results support the olverembatinib-venetoclax involved regimen as a frontline consolidation or induction treatment, which may achieve rapid molecular remission, reduce pre-transplant treatment cycles, and minimize chemotherapy-related toxicity. Nevertheless, the findings of this study need to be verified by expanding the number of clinical cases and conducting prospective clinical studies.

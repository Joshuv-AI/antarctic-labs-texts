Abstract
. The “defect”, that the fractional Sobolev norm ‖ f ‖ W ˙ s , p \|f\|_{\dot{W}^{s,p}} , for s ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) s\in(0,1) , fails to converge to the first-order Sobolev norm ‖ f ‖ W ˙ 1 , p \|f\|_{\dot{W}^{1,p}} as s → 1 s\to 1 , is a source of two types of substitute results: (1) characterisations of constant functions by integral conditions obtained by naïve substitution of s = 1 s=1 into the formula of ‖ f ‖ W ˙ s , p \|f\|_{\dot{W}^{s,p}} , and (2) derivative-free characterisations of W ˙ 1 , p \dot{W}^{1,p} by suitable modifications of the said formula. In this paper, we obtain extensions of both types of results for the versions of these function spaces over any complete doubling metric measure space ( X , ρ , μ ) (X,\rho,\mu) supporting a ( 1 , p ) (1,p) -Poincaré inequality. As a key tool of independent potential, we introduce a new “macroscopic” Poincaré inequality, whose right-hand side has oscillations of the same form as the left-hand side, but at a smaller macroscopic scale r ∈ ( 0 , R ) r\in(0,R) . Besides intrinsic interest, these results are motivated by applications to quantitative compactness properties of commutators [ T , f ] [T,f] of singular integrals and pointwise multipliers. With pivotal use of the present results, a characterisation of commutator mapping properties, over the same class of general domains ( X , ρ , μ ) (X,\rho,\mu) , is obtained in a companion paper.

1.
Introduction

The well-known “defect”
[
9
]
, that the fractional Sobolev semi-norm

\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}:=\Big[\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big(\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\Big)^{p}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y}{|x-y|^{d}}\Big]^{\frac{1}{p}},\quad\begin{cases}s\in(0,1),\\
p\in(1,\infty),\end{cases}

(1.1)

does
not
converge to the first-order Sobolev semi-norm
\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}:=\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
as
s\to 1
, is a source of interesting results of two main types:

(1)

Showing that the finiteness of naïve versions of the limit, or the integral expression at
s=1
, actually force the function
f
to be a constant, one obtains integral criteria to
recognise constant functions
(as in
[
11
]
).

(2)

Cleverly modifying the limit (as in
[
9
]
), or the integral expression at
s=1
(as in
[
13
]
), one can “rectify the situation”
[
9
]
and recover the
\dot{W}^{1,p}
norm, thus obtaining
derivative-free characterisations of Sobolev spaces
.

The theme of this paper is results of both types for Sobolev spaces over a metric measure space
(X,\rho,\mu)
in place of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We always assume the doubling condition

V(x,2r)\leq c_{\mu}V(x,r),\qquad V(x,r):=\mu(B(x,r)),

for all
x\in X
and
r>0
. For the main results, we also assume that
(X,\rho)
is a complete metric space and supports the
(1,p)
-Poincaré inequality (Definition
2.2
). We will informally refer to these as the “usual assumptions” (cf.
[
27
,
28
]
).

In some examples, but not in our main results, the space is also assumed to have
lower dimension
d
or
upper dimension
D
, by which we mean that

\Big(\frac{R}{r}\Big)^{d}\lesssim\frac{V(x,R)}{V(x,r)}\lesssim\Big(\frac{R}{r}\Big)^{D}

(1.2)

for all
x\in X
and
0<r\leq R<\infty
. In particular, a space is called
Ahlfors
d
-regular
, if (
1.2
) holds with
d=D
, or equivalently,
V(x,r)\approx r^{d}
. Every doubling space has upper dimension
D\leq\log_{2}c_{\mu}
, but possibly much lower than this simple upper bound.
While not a property of every doubling space, a lower dimension
d>0
readily follows from the “usual assumptions”: these imply that
X
is connected by
[
4
, Proposition 4.2]
, and a connected doubling space has
d>0
by
[
4
, Corollary 3.8]
.

1.A.
Recognising constants

Starting with theme (
1
), a prototypical result
[
11
, Proposition 2]
states that the naïve substitution of
s=1
into (
1.1
) is a characterisation of constants:

\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big(\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|}\Big)^{p}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y}{|x-y|^{d}}<\infty\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad f\equiv\text{const a.e.}

(1.3)

To efficiently discuss its various extensions to
(X,\rho,\mu)
, it seems convenient to introduce a fractional Sobolev (or Besov, hence the notation) norm with a weigh function
\lambda
as follows:

\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p,p}(\lambda)}:=\Big[\iint_{X\times X}\Big(\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{\rho(x,y)^{s}}\Big)^{p}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)}{\lambda(x,y)}\Big]^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(1.4)

The two main cases are
\lambda(x,y)=\rho(x,y)^{d}
(for some dimensional quantity
d
) or

\lambda(x,y)=V(x,y):=\mu(B(x,\rho(x,y)))\approx V(y,x),

where the last equivalence is an easy consequence of the doubling condition. Note that both choices coincide if
(X,\rho,\mu)
is Ahlfors
d
-regular, and in particular they both coincide with
|x-y|^{d}
as in (
1.1
) for
X=\mathbb{R}^{d}
, but they may differ in general. We note that
\dot{B}^{s}_{p,p}(V)
seems to be the most relevant version of a Besov space over a general doubling metric measure space, as used e.g. in
[
5
,
6
,
21
,
22
]
.

For various versions of the Besov and related norms, the following characterisations of constant functions on
(X,\rho,\mu)
have been identified in the literature:

(i)

In
[
8
, Théorème 4.2]
, it is shown that
\|f\|_{B^{1}_{d,d}(V)}<\infty
forces
f
into a constant
along
d
-modulus-a.e. rectifiable curve
, when
(X,\rho,\mu)
is Ahlfors
d
-regular. Thus, if
X
is sufficiently rich with such curves, then
f
is constant on
X
.

(ii)

In
[
42
]
, constants are characterised by
\|f\|_{B^{d_{w}/2}_{2,2}(V)}<\infty
, when
(X,\rho,\mu)
is Ahlfors regular with
walk dimension

d_{w}
. In particular, under walk dimension
d_{w}=2
, one obtains a complete analogue of (
1.3
) for
p=2
.

(iii)

In
[
43
]
, a condition slightly weaker than
\|f\|_{B^{1}_{p,p}(\rho^{D})}<\infty
is used, assuming upper dimension
D
(see (
1.2
)), referred to as “Bishop–Gromov regularity of dimension
D
” in
[
43
]
.

(iv)

In
[
26
, Corollary 1.3(3)]
under the “usual assumptions”, and in
[
7
, Proposition 5.5]
, further assuming compactness and Ahlfors regularity, somewhat related criteria for constancy are obtained, but not directly comparable with those in terms of the semi-norms
\|f\|_{B^{s}_{p,p}(\lambda)}
.

Thus, despite a variety of extensions of the classical result (
1.3
), the most natural generalisation (in our opinion)

\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{1}_{p,p}(V)}<\infty\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad f\equiv\text{const a.e.},

(1.5)

under the “usual assumptions” (in particular, without assuming Ahlfors regularity) seems to have become available only very recently, in
[
36
, Corollary 1.8]
.

Unaware of
[
36
]
when starting this project, we provide another proof of (
1.5
) under the same “usual assumptions”. Even after
[
36
]
, we believe that our alternative proof has independent interest, being relatively direct in using only the Besov norms as defined in (
1.4
), while the proof of
[
36
, Corollary 1.8]
—quoting
[
2
, Theorem 5.1]
,
[
28
, Theorem 10.5.2]
, and
[
36
, Theorem 1.6]
—passes through the theory of Korevaar–Schoen spaces
KS^{1,p}
. In fact, we prove a slightly stronger local version of (
1.5
) in Proposition
4.1
, as well as a variant in Corollary
4.3
, which is relevant for an application that we discuss in some more detail in Section
1.B.2
below.

1.B.
Characterisations of Sobolev spaces

Turning to our main theme (
2
), a sample of results along this extensive line of research in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is as follows:

(a)

As a prototypical case among several variants,
[

\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{1,p}}=c_{p,d}\lim_{s\to 1}(1-s)^{\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{s,p}},

which also shows the blow-up of the

norms in the lack of a renormalisation. Further formulas for

in terms of limits or supremums of fractional Sobolev type quantities are contained in
[

39
,
40
]
; see also
[
41
]
for a survey.

(b)

With another characterisation of the fractional Sobolev norm from
[

]
as a point of departure,
[

proved the square-function formula

\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\approx\Big[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big|\frac{f(x)-\langle f\rangle_{B(x,t)}}{t}\Big|^{2}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}t}{t}\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\Big]^{\frac{1}{p}},

where

is the average of

on

. Previous results in this spirit from

required second-order differences or first-Taylor polynomials to characterise first-order smoothness (in contrast to first-order differences or zeroth-order Taylor polynomials sufficient for fractional smoothness

(c)

Replacing the

norm in (

) by the weak
L^{p,\infty}
norm when

,
[

]
found their

where
\displaystyle Q_{\gamma}f(x,y):=\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{1+\gamma}}
.
Originally
a priori
relations for test functions

in

, these were extended to all

(and other values of

\dot{W}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, rather than just the

of test functions.

(d)

Another surprising formula using a weak

norm is due to
[
20
]
. Denoting

m_{f}(x,t):=\fint_{B(x,t)}|f-\langle f\rangle_{B(x,t)}|,\qquad\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{p}(x,t):=\frac{\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t}{t^{p+1}},

In the special case

, a variant of the norm comparison (but not the limit relation) was already contained in

1.B.1.
Sobolev spaces over metric measure spaces

A potential use of such derivative-free characterisations of
\dot{W}^{1,p}
is that they suggest meaningful definitions of a first-order Sobolev norm in any metric measure space
(X,\rho,\mu)
, consistent (by the results just quoted) with the usual definition on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. However, since other notions of first-order Sobolev spaces have already proven to be useful in various contexts beyond
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, one would also like to have consistency in a broader class of domains
(X,\rho,\mu)
. We will explicitly work with the Hajłasz–Sobolev space
M^{1,p}
of
[
23
]
(see Definition
2.8
), since this seems the most convenient choice for our considerations. However, under the “usual assumptions”, this is known to coincide with several other definitions (see Remark
2.11
for details), thus providing an essentially canonical first-order Sobolev space, against which other candidate definitions should be compared. In this direction, the following results have been obtained:

(A)

Under the “usual assumptions”, several of the limit formulas in (
a
) have been extended in
[
17
]
, but see also
[
17
, Open problem 1.8]
.

(B)

The question of extending (
b
) beyond
\mathbb{R}^{d}
was raised in
[
1
]
but remains open; while the
statement
of (
b
) only refers to the metric and the measure, its proof in
[
1
]
makes decisive use of Fourier analysis, a much more restricted tool.

(C)

Under the “usual assumptions” on
(X,\rho,\mu)
, the “surprising formulas” of
[
13
]
in (
c
) where extended by
[
16
]
to a special class of test functions on
X
. Under somewhat different assumptions on
(X,\rho,\mu)
, similar formulas were obtained by
[
25
]
for all Lipschitz functions of bounded support. Thus, in both cases, one obtains an equivalent expression for the Sobolev norm of test functions, as in
[
13
]
, but not (yet) a characterisation of the full Sobolev space as in
[
12
]
.

(D)

The norm comparison in (
d
) has been extended to the case of general stratified Lie groups (or Carnot groups)
\mathbb{G}
in place of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
in
[
37
, Theorem A.3]
. The key elements of the Euclidean argument of
[

]
are still available in this setting, making their adaptation in
[
37
, pp. 110–112]
a rather quick task. On the other hand, a variant of the earlier

result of
[

]
has been obtained by
[

under the “usual assumptions” plus the additional conditions that

is a compact, Ahlfors regular space of homogeneous dimension

.

Our goal in this paper is an extension of (

) to general metric measure spaces under the “usual assumptions”, which requires significant departures from the Euclidean argument
[
20
]
and its Carnot group extension
[
37
]
. Compared to
[

]
, besides dispensing with the compactness and Ahlfors regularity assumptions, our substitute of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty)
from
[

]
will be simply

, instead of a

of

, as in
[

]
, which requires more effort to describe. This is our main result:

1.6 Theorem
.

Let
(X,\rho,\mu)
be a complete doubling metric measure space supporting a
(1,p)
-Poincaré inequality for some
p\in(1,\infty)
. Let
f\in L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mu)
. Then
f
belongs to the homogeneous Hajłasz–Sobolev space
\dot{M}^{1,p}(\mu)
(Definition
2.8
) if and only if
m_{f}\in L^{p,\infty}(\nu_{p})
, where
m_{f}
and
\nu_{p}
are the function and the measure on
X\times(0,\infty)
defined by

m_{f}(x,t):=\fint_{B(x,t)}|f-\langle f\rangle_{B(x,t)}|\,\mathrm{d}\mu,\qquad\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{p}(x,t):=\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\,\mathrm{d}t}{t^{p+1}}.

(1.7)

In this case, we have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\dot{M}^{1,p}(\mu)}

\displaystyle\approx\|m_{f}\|_{L^{p,\infty}(\nu_{p})}:=\sup_{\kappa>0}\kappa\cdot\nu_{p}(\{m_{f}>\kappa\})^{1/p}

(1.8)

\displaystyle\approx\liminf_{\kappa\to 0}\kappa\cdot\nu_{p}(\{m_{f}>\kappa\})^{1/p}.

(1.9)

We have formulated Theorem
1.6
in terms of the Hajłasz–Sobolev space
M^{1,d}(\mu)
of
[
23
]
, since this turns out to be the most convenient definition to work with for the present purposes. However, under the assumptions of Theorem
1.6
, this space is known to be equivalent so several other established notions of Sobolev spaces over a metric measure space; see Remark
2.11
for details.

The norm comparison (
1.8
) is a complete extension of the corresponding results in
\mathbb{R}^{d}

[
20
]
and Carnot groups
[
37
]
; see Examples
2.10
and
2.12
for the coincidence of
\dot{M}^{1,p}(\mu)
with the usual Sobolev spaces in the respective settings. On the other hand, our limit relation (
1.9
) is somewhat weaker than the corresponding identity proved by
[
20
]
and recorded in (
d
). In the lack of the symmetries available in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we believe that (
1.9
) is the best substitute that one can expect in our generality.

1.B.2.
Connection to commutators

Rather than being “yet another” characterisation Sobolev spaces over metric measure spaces, the particular type of characterisation of Theorem
1.6
(just like its Euclidean and Carnot group predecessors) is motivated by its role in the characterisation of quantitative compactness properties of commutators
[T,f]
of singular integrals
T
and pointwise multipliers at a critical end-point case. On
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, for a class of operators
T
containing, and modelled after, the classical Riesz transforms
\partial_{j}(-\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, it is known that

\|[T,f]\|_{S^{d,\infty}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\approx\|f\|_{\operatorname{Osc}^{d,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\approx\|m_{f}\|_{L^{d,\infty}(\nu_{d})}\approx\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{1,d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})};

(1.10)

here
S^{d,\infty}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
is the
weak Schatten class
of compact operators on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, whose
n
th singular value decays at the rate
O(n^{-\frac{1}{d}})
, and
\operatorname{Osc}^{d,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is an oscillatory norm introduced by
[
44
]
, who also proved the first “
\approx
” in (
1.10
). The second “
\approx
” is elementary, while the last one is (
d
) with
p=d
, the case already due to
[
15
]
. Another proof of
\|[T,f]\|_{S^{d,\infty}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\approx\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{1,d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
without the intermediate steps of (
1.10
) is due to
[
38
]
, under the additional assumption that
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and using the theory of pseudodifferential operators, which seems unlikely to extend to very general
(X,\rho,\mu)
.

With the goal of proving a version of this commutator result in reasonably general spaces, the approach of
[
44
]
and
[
15
,
20
]
via (
1.10
) seemed like a more promising path. In the companion paper
[
30
]
, we extend the first two “
\approx
” in (
1.10
) to
(X,\rho,\mu)
in place of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
; curiously, this part assumes Alhfors
d
-regularity in the second “
\approx
” but no Poincaré at all. To complete the commutator theorem, it then remains to extend the last “
\approx
” in (
1.10
), which is precisely (
d
) from above. For the full result, it is essential that we obtain the norm comparison for all
f\in L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mu)
, instead of just test functions (as in the extensions of (
c
) described in (
C
)): we want that the Schatten property of the commutator implies the membership of
f
in the Sobolev space, without imposing any
a priori
conditions of
f
. We achieve this in our Theorem
1.6
, providing decisive input for the proof of the main theorem of
[
30
]
.

The characterisation of constants also plays a role in
[
30
]
in showing that

[b,T]\in S^{p}(L^{2}(\mu))\quad\Rightarrow\quad b=\text{const}

for certain values of
p
, extending a classical cut-off phenomenon
[
31
]
from
\mathbb{R}^{d}
to metric measure spaces
(X,\rho,\mu)
under the “usual assumptions”.

1.C.
Plan of the paper and an overview of the argument

In the following Section
2
, we collect some preliminaries, including the definitions omitted in this Introduction.
In Section
3
, we present our key new tool of the “macroscopic” Poincaré inequality in Theorem
3.4
. With this result at hand, we obtain a rather quick proof of the characterisation of constant functions in Section
4
.

The rest of the paper is dedicated to the proof of our Main Theorem
1.6
. The rough outline of our proof is adapted from the Euclidean argument of
[
20
]
. In particular, the lower bound of the Sobolev norm (i.e., “
\gtrsim
” in (
1.8
)) is obtained in Section
5
by a straightforward adaptation of
[
20
]
. However, the other direction is much more challenging, and we need to develop substantial new methods.
Besides the macroscopic Poincaré inequality, which also features in this proof, we establish some delicate comparison of maximal and mean oscillations at the infinitesimal scale in Section
6
, to substitute tools like Taylor expansions, which are not available in our setting. We only obtain this comparison for Lipschitz functions, and to lift this
a priori
assumption, we need to be able to approximate general functions by Lipschitz functions with respect to the “exotic” norm
f\mapsto\|m_{f}\|_{L^{p,\infty}(\nu_{p})}
featuring in the desired characterisation. (This approximation follows easily from Theorem
1.6

a posteriori
, once we know that the exotic norm is equivalent to
\|f\|_{\dot{M}^{1,p}(\mu)}
; but we need it
a priori
, in order to prove this equivalence!) This is accomplished in Section
7
with the help of the macroscopic Poincaré inequality, this time serving to substitute some convolution tricks, which are easy in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, but unavailable in our setting. After these preparations, we can complete the proof of the upper bound of the Sobolev norm (“
\lesssim
” in (
1.8
)) in Section
8
and the limit relation (
1.9
) in Section
9
.

2.
Preliminaries

When speaking of
local
properties of functions on a metric measure space
(X,\rho,\mu)
, we mean properties of their restrictions on any ball
B=B(x,r)
with
x\in X
and
r\in(0,\infty)
. In particular,
L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mu)
consists of all measurable
f
such that
1_{B}f\in L^{1}(\mu)
for every ball
B
, and
\operatorname{Lip}_{\operatorname{loc}}
consists of all functions
f
whose restriction
f|_{B}
is a Lipschitz function (as defined right below) for every ball
B
.

2.A.
Lipschitz functions and the Poincaré inequality

For a function
f
on
X
, its Lipschitz (semi-)norm is the quantity

\|f\|_{\operatorname{Lip}}:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in X\\
x\neq y\end{subarray}}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{\rho(x,y)},

and
f
is said to be Lipschitz continuous, or a Lipschitz function, if
\|f\|_{\operatorname{Lip}}<\infty
.

The pointwise Lipschitz constant of
f
at
x\in X
is defined by

\operatorname{lip}f(x):=\liminf_{t\to 0}\sup_{\rho(x,y)\leq t}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{t}.

(2.1)

A key assumption in our main results is the following:

2.2 Definition
.

For
p\in[1,\infty)
, we say that
(X,\rho,\mu)
supports a
(1,p)
-Poincaré inequality if, for some constants
\lambda\in[1,\infty)
and
c_{P}\in(0,\infty)
, we have

\fint_{B(z,r)}|f-\langle f\rangle_{B(z,r)}|\,\mathrm{d}\mu\leq c_{P}\cdot r\cdot\Big(\fint_{B(z,\lambda r)}(\operatorname{lip}f)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}},

(2.3)

for all
z\in X
and
r>0
, and all Lipschitz functions
f
on
X
.

For the sake of comparison, we relate the notion of Poincaré inequality given in Definition
2.2
to some other variants appearing in the literature.

2.4 Lemma
.

Let
(X,\rho,\mu)
be a complete doubling metric measure space and
p\in(1,\infty)
. Consider the Poincaré inequality

\fint_{B(x,r)}|f-\langle f\rangle_{B(x,r)}|\,\mathrm{d}\mu\lesssim r\Big(\fint_{B(x,\lambda r)}g^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

(2.5)

for pairs of functions
(f,g)
. Then the following statements—each to hold for every Lipschitz function
f
on
X
, and
g
as specified below—are equivalent:

(1)

(
2.5
) holds for
g(x)=\operatorname{lip}f(x)
as defined in (
2.1
).

(2)

(
2.5
) holds for

\displaystyle g(x)=\operatorname{Lip}f(x):=\limsup_{r\to 0}\sup_{0<\rho(x,y)\leq r}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{\rho(x,y)}.

(3)

(
2.5
) holds for

\displaystyle g(x)=\operatorname{\it Lip}f(x):=\liminf_{r\to 0}\sup_{\rho(x,y)=r}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{r}.

(4)

(
2.5
) holds whenever
g
is an upper gradient of
f
.

Proof.

Since
\operatorname{\it Lip}f\leq\operatorname{lip}f\leq\operatorname{Lip}f
, the implications (
3
)
\Rightarrow
(
1
)
\Rightarrow
(
2
) are obvious. The implication (
2
)
\Rightarrow
(
4
) is part of
[
32
, Theorem 2]
.
By
[
14
, Proposition 1.11]
, the function
\operatorname{\it Lip}f
is an upper gradient of a Lipschitz function
f
. Hence (
4
)
\Rightarrow
(
3
), and this closes the circle of implications.
∎

We also make use of the following fundamental result.

2.6 Theorem

(
[
34
]
, Theorem 1.0.1)
.

Let
(X,\rho,\mu)
be a complete doubling metric measure space and
p\in(1,\infty)
.
Suppose that
(X,\rho,\mu)
satisfies the
(1,p)
-Poincaré inequality in the sense of Definition
2.2
. Then it satisfies the
(1,p-\varepsilon)
-Poincaré inequality for some
\varepsilon>0
, in the same sense.

Proof.

As indicated, this was proved in
[
34
, Theorem 1.0.1]
, but under a definition of the Poincaré inequality as in Lemma
2.4
(
2
) (see
[
34
, Definition 2.2.1]
). By Lemma
2.4
, this is equivalent to any of the other definitions listed in the lemma, and in particular to the version of Lemma
2.4
(

) that was used in our Definition
2.2
.
∎

Various examples of spaces supporting a Poincaré inequality can be found in
[

]
,
[

[

, page 274]
.

.

2.B.
Hajłasz gradients and the space
\dot{M}^{1,p}(\mu)

In contrast to the straighforward definition of the Besov norm
\dot{B}^{s}_{p,p}(\lambda)
in (
1.4
), giving a meaningful definition of first order smoothness spaces over a metric measure space
(X,\rho,\mu)
requires some more effort. The most elementary definition is probably that of Hajłasz
[
23
]
:

2.8 Definition
.

A measurable function
h
is a
Hajłasz upper gradient
of a measurable function
f
if

|f(x)-f(y)|\leq(h(x)+h(y))\rho(x,y),\qquad\text{for $\mu$-a.e. }x,y\in X.

(2.9)

The homogeneous Hajłasz–Sobolev norm of
f
is then

\|f\|_{\dot{M}^{1,p}(\mu)}:=\inf\Big\{\|h\|_{L^{p}(\mu)}:h\text{ satisfies \eqref{eq:HajlaszUpper}}\Big\},

and
\dot{M}^{1,p}(\mu)
is the class of all measurable
f
with
\|f\|_{\dot{M}^{1,p}(\mu)}<\infty
.

2.10 Example
.

By
[
23
, Theorem 1]
, we have

\dot{M}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\dot{W}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\qquad p\in(1,\infty),

where
\dot{W}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the usual homogeneous Sobolev space

\dot{W}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\Big\{f\in L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}:=\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\infty\Big\},

and
\nabla f
is the distributional gradient. This shows that
[
20
, Theorem 1]
falls under the scope of Theorem
1.6
—except for details of the limit relation (
1.9
), which takes a sharper form in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

2.11 Remark
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.6
, the Hajłasz–Sobolev space
M^{1,p}(\mu)
is equivalent so several other established notions of Sobolev spaces over a metric measure space, including the Cheeger Sobolev space
\textit{Ch}^{1,p}
of
[
14
]
, the Korevaar–Schoen Sobolev space
\textit{KS}^{1,p}
of
[
35
]
, the Newtonian Sobolev space
N^{1,p}
of
[
45
]
, and the Poincaré Sobolev space
P^{1,p}
of
[

]
, as well as various Sobolev spaces

associated with given systems of vector fields, which is often the preferred definition in concrete situations. See
[
28
, Theorem 10.5.3]
for
M^{1,p}=P^{1,p}=\textit{KS}^{1,p}=N^{1,p}=\textit{Ch}^{1,p}

[

for

under the assumptions of Theorem

Any Heisenberg group

3.
The macroscopic Poincaré inequality

In this section, we establish our key new tool, the “macroscopic” Poincaré inequality of Theorem
3.4
, which plays a role in the characterisation of both Sobolev spaces and constant functions, and is likely to find other applications elsewhere. We first recall some well known facts about the construction of approximate identities on metric spaces.

3.1 Lemma
.

Let
(X,\rho,\mu)
be a doubling metric measure space, and fix a parameter
\gamma>0
.
Then, for every
t>0
, there exist a countable family of functions
\phi_{i}
and points
x_{i}
such that the balls
B(x_{i},\frac{1}{2}t)
are disjoint, and

0\leq\phi_{i}\leq 1_{B(x_{i},(1+\gamma)t)},\qquad\sum_{i}\phi_{i}\equiv 1,\qquad\|\phi_{i}\|_{\operatorname{Lip}}\lesssim t^{-1}

Proof.

We choose a
t
-net of points
x_{i}\in X
. Since
(X,\rho,\mu)
is doubling, this net is locally finite. For each
i
, let
\tilde{\phi}_{i}
be a function with
1_{B(x_{i},t)}\leq\tilde{\phi}_{i}\leq 1_{B(x_{i},(1+\gamma)t)}
and
\|\tilde{\phi}_{i}\|_{\operatorname{Lip}}\leq(\gamma t)^{-1}
. Then
1\leq\sum_{i}\tilde{\phi}_{i}\leq c
, where
c
depends only on the doubling constant and
\gamma
. Then
\phi_{i}:=\tilde{\phi}_{i}/\sum_{j}\tilde{\phi}_{j}
is well-defined, and satisfies the required properties.
∎

For every
t>0
, we define an approximate identity
\Phi_{t}:L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mu)\to\operatorname{Lip}_{\operatorname{loc}}(X)
with the help of a fixed partition of unity
\phi_{i}
at scale
t
as provided by Lemma
3.1
:

\Phi_{t}f:=\sum_{i}\phi_{i}(x)\langle f\rangle_{B(x_{i},t)}.

(3.2)

The local Lipschitz properties of
\Phi_{t}f
are quantified in the following:

3.3 Lemma
.

Let
(X,\rho,\mu)
be a doubling metric measure space. For any fixed parameter
a>0
, there exists another parameter
b>0
such that the following estimates hold for every
f\in L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mu)
and
t>0
:
If
\rho(x,y)\leq at
, the approximations
\Phi_{t}f
from (
3.2
) satisfy the estimate

|\Phi_{t}f(x)-\Phi_{t}f(y)|\lesssim\frac{d(x,y)}{t}m_{f}(x,bt)

and in particular

\operatorname{lip}\Phi_{t}f(x)\lesssim\frac{1}{t}m_{f}(x,bt).

Proof.

Denoting
c_{i}:=\langle f\rangle_{B(x_{i},t)}
, we first observe the identity

\begin{split}\Phi_{t}f(x)&-\Phi_{t}f(y)\\
&=\sum_{i}\phi_{i}(x)c_{i}-\sum_{j}\phi_{j}(y)c_{j}\\
&=\sum_{i,j}\phi_{i}(x)\phi_{j}(y)(c_{i}-c_{j})\qquad\text{since }\sum_{i}\phi_{i}\equiv 1\\
&=\sum_{i,j}\phi_{j}(x)\phi_{i}(y)(c_{j}-c_{i})\qquad\text{swapping the names of the indices}\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}\Big(\phi_{i}(x)\phi_{j}(y)-\phi_{j}(x)\phi_{i}(y)\Big)(c_{i}-c_{j})\end{split}

by taking the average of the previous two equal expressions in the last step.

In order that

\phi_{i}(x)\phi_{j}(y)-\phi_{j}(x)\phi_{i}(y)\neq 0

it is necessary that
\phi_{i}(x)\neq 0
or
\phi_{i}(y)\neq 0
, thus
x_{i}\in B(x,(1+\gamma)t)
or
x_{i}\in B(y,(1+\gamma)t)\subset B(x,(1+\gamma+a)t)
; hence in any case
x_{i}\in B(x,(1+\gamma+a)t)
. Similarly, we deduce that
x_{j}\in B(x,(1+\gamma+a)t)
. In particular, the relevant summations over both
i
and
j
have only boundedly many nonzero terms.

Moreover, it follows
B(x_{i},t)\subset B(x,(1+\gamma+2a)t)\subset B(x_{i},2(1+\gamma+a)t)
, and hence

\begin{split}&|c_{i}-c_{j}|=|\langle f\rangle_{B(x_{i},t)}-\langle f\rangle_{B(x_{j},t)}|\\
&\quad=\Big|\fint_{B(x_{i},t)}(f-\langle f\rangle_{B(x,(1+\gamma+2a)t)})\,\mathrm{d}\mu-\fint_{B(x_{j},t)}(f-\langle f\rangle_{B(x,(1+\gamma+2a)t)})\,\mathrm{d}\mu\Big|\\
&\quad\lesssim\fint_{B(x,bt)}|f-\langle f\rangle_{B(x,bt)}|\,\mathrm{d}\mu,\qquad b:=1+\gamma+2a.\end{split}

On the other hand, we have

\begin{split}|\phi_{i}(x)\phi_{j}(y)-\phi_{j}(x)\phi_{i}(y)|&=|(\phi_{i}(x)-\phi_{i}(y))\phi_{j}(y)+\phi_{i}(y)(\phi_{j}(y)-\phi_{j}(x))|\\
&\leq d(x,y)\|\phi_{i}\|_{\operatorname{Lip}}\|\phi_{j}\|_{\infty}+\|\phi_{i}\|_{\infty}d(x,y)\|\phi_{j}\|_{\operatorname{Lip}}\\
&\lesssim\frac{d(x,y)}{t}.\end{split}

Altogether, we have

|\Phi_{t}f(x)-\Phi_{t}f(y)|\lesssim\frac{d(x,y)}{t}\fint_{B(x,bt)}|f-\langle f\rangle_{B(x,bt)}|\,\mathrm{d}\mu

as claimed. The claim concerning
\operatorname{lip}\Phi_{t}f(x)
is an immediate consequence.
∎

We are now ready to prove the macroscopic Poincaré inequality:

3.4 Theorem
.

Let
(X,\rho,\mu)
be doubling metric measure space supporting a
(1,p)
-Poincaré inequality with constants
(\lambda,c_{P})
. Then for all
f\in L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mu)
, all
z\in X
, and all
0<r<s<\infty
, we have

m_{f}(z,s)\lesssim\frac{s}{r}\Big(\fint_{B(z,\tilde{\lambda}s)}m_{f}(x,r)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where
\tilde{\lambda}:=\max(2,\lambda)
and the implied constant depends only on the doubling and Poincaré data.

Proof.

It is enough to prove the claim with some number
c
in place of
\langle f\rangle_{B(z,s)}
in the expression

m_{f}(z,s)=\fint_{B(z,s)}|f-\langle f\rangle_{B(z,s)}|\,\mathrm{d}\mu.

For
t\approx r
to be specified, we consider an approximate identity
\Phi_{t}
at scale
t
and estimate

\fint_{B(z,s)}|f-k|\,\mathrm{d}\mu\leq\fint_{B(z,s)}|f-\Phi_{t}f|\,\mathrm{d}\mu+\fint_{B(z,s)}|\Phi_{t}f-c|\,\mathrm{d}\mu=:I+II.

We deal with term
II
first. Taking
c=\langle\Phi_{t}f\rangle_{B(z,s)}
, we have

\begin{split}II&\lesssim s\Big(\fint_{B(z,\lambda s)}[\operatorname{lip}\Phi_{t}f(x)]^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\qquad\text{(Poincar\'{e})}\\
&\lesssim s\Big(\fint_{B(z,\lambda s)}\Big[\frac{1}{t}m_{f}(x,bt)\Big]^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\quad\text{(Lemma \ref{lem:PhitDiff})}.\end{split}

We then turn to term
I
. Denoting
c_{i}:=\langle f\rangle_{B(x_{i},t)}
, we have

f-\Phi_{t}f=\sum_{i}\phi_{i}(f-c_{i}),

where
\phi_{i}
is a partition of unity at scale
t
as in Lemma
3.1
.
Hence

I\lesssim\frac{1}{\mu(B(z,s))}\sum_{i:B(x_{i},(1+\gamma)t)\cap B(z,s)\neq\varnothing}\mu(B(x_{i},(1+\gamma)t))\fint_{B(x_{i},(1+\gamma)t)}|f-c_{i}|\,\mathrm{d}\mu.

For each
x\in B(x_{i},\frac{1}{2}t)
, we have

B(x_{i},(1+\gamma)t)\subset B(x,(\frac{3}{2}+\gamma)t)\subset B(x_{i},(2+\gamma)t),

and hence

\fint_{B(x_{i},(1+\gamma)t)}|f-\langle f\rangle_{B(x_{i},t)}|\,\mathrm{d}\mu\lesssim\fint_{B(x,\beta t)}|f-\langle f\rangle_{B(x,\beta t)}|\,\mathrm{d}\mu,\quad\beta:=\frac{3}{2}+\gamma.

Thus (abbreviating the summation condition
B(x_{i},(1+\gamma)t)\cap B(z,s)\neq\varnothing
by “
*
”)

\begin{split}I&\lesssim\sum_{i}^{*}\frac{\mu(B(x_{i},\frac{1}{2}t))}{\mu(B(z,s))}\inf_{x\in B(x_{i},\frac{1}{2}t)}m_{f}(x,\beta t)\\
&\lesssim\frac{1}{\mu(B(z,s))}\sum_{i}^{*}\int_{B(x_{i},\frac{1}{2}t)}m_{f}(x,\beta t)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\end{split}

The balls
B(x_{i},\frac{1}{2}t)
are disjoint and, under the assumption that
B(x_{i},(1+\gamma)t)\cap B(z,s)\neq\varnothing
, contained in
B(z,s+(\frac{3}{2}+\gamma)t)=B(z,s+\beta t)
. Hence

I\lesssim\frac{1}{\mu(B(z,s))}\int_{B(z,s+\beta t)}m_{f}(x,\beta t)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x).

Let us now fix choose
c:=\max(b,\beta)
and
t:=c^{-1}r
. Then
s+\beta t\leq s+r\leq 2s
, and we have proved that

\begin{split}II&\lesssim\frac{s}{r}\Big(\fint_{B(z,\lambda s)}m_{f}(x,r)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}},\\
I&\lesssim\fint_{B(z,2s)}m_{f}(x,r)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\leq\frac{s}{r}\Big(\fint_{B(z,2s)}m_{f}(x,r)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\end{split}

by Hölder’s inequality and the trivial bound
1\leq s/r
in the last step.

By yet another application of doubling, we can dominate the averages over both
B(z,\lambda s)
and
B(z,2s)
by one over
B(z,\max(2,\lambda)s)
. Thus both terms satisfy the claimed bound.
∎

4.
How to recognise constants

With the macroscopic Poincaré inequality in our toolbox, we are in a position to give a relatively quick proof of the characterisation of constants via Besov-type conditions as in (
1.5
), and indeed the following slightly stronger version:

4.1 Proposition
.

Let
(X,\rho,\mu)
be a doubling metric measure space supporting the
(1,p)
-Poincaré inequality.
If
f\in L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mu)
satisfies

\Big[(x,y)\mapsto\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}}{\rho(x,y)^{p}}\frac{1}{V(x,y)}\Big]\in L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mu\times\mu),

(4.2)

then
f
is equal to a constant almost everywhere. In particular,

\dot{B}^{1}_{p,p}(V)=\{\text{constants}\}.

Proof.

Note that
f\in\dot{B}^{1}_{p,p}(V)
means, by definition, that the function in (
4.2
) is not only
L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mu\times\mu)
but globally in
L^{1}(\mu\times\mu)
; hence it is enough to prove the first assertion.

It suffices to show that, for every ball
B(z,s)\subset X
, we have
f\equiv\langle f\rangle_{B(z,s)}
almost everywhere in
B(z,s)
. We fix one such ball and apply Theorem
3.4
to see that, for every
r\in(0,s)
, and using the notation of the said proposition,

\displaystyle\fint_{B(z,s)}|f-\langle f\rangle_{B(z,s)}|\,\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{s}{r}\Big(\fint_{B(z,\tilde{\lambda}s)}\Big[\fint_{B(x,r)}|f(y)-\langle f\rangle_{B(x,r)}|\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)\Big]^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{s}{r}\Big(\fint_{B(z,\tilde{\lambda}s)}\fint_{B(x,r)}\fint_{B(x,r)}|f(y)-f(v)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(v)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\frac{s}{r}\Big(\int_{x\in B(z,\tilde{\lambda}s)}\int_{v\in B(x,r)}\int_{y\in B(x,r)}\frac{|f(y)-f(v)|^{p}}{V(x,r)^{2}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(v)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)}{V(z,\tilde{\lambda}s)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{s}{r}\Big(\int_{y\in B(z,\tilde{\lambda}s+r)}\int_{v\in B(y,2r)}\int_{x\in B(y,r)}\frac{|f(y)-f(v)|^{p}}{V(y,r)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\mu(v)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)}{V(z,\tilde{\lambda}s)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\frac{s}{r}\Big(\int_{y\in B(z,(\tilde{\lambda}+1)s)}\int_{v\in B(y,2r)}\frac{|f(y)-f(v)|^{p}}{V(y,r)}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\mu(v)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)}{V(z,\tilde{\lambda}s)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{s}{r}\Big(\int_{y\in B(z,(\tilde{\lambda}+1)s)}\int_{v\in B(y,2r)}\Big(\frac{2r}{\rho(v,y)}\Big)^{p}\frac{|f(y)-f(v)|^{p}}{V(v,y)}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\mu(v)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)}{V(z,\tilde{\lambda}s)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim s\Big(\iint_{(v,y)\in B(z,(\tilde{\lambda}+3)s)^{2}}1_{\{\rho(v,y)<2r\}}\frac{|f(y)-f(v)|^{p}}{\rho(v,y)^{p}V(v,y)}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\mu(v)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)}{V(z,\tilde{\lambda}s)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

In the rightmost form, the integrand is pointwise dominated by the function

(v,y)\mapsto\frac{|f(y)-f(v)|^{p}}{\rho(v,y)^{p}}\frac{1}{V(v,y)},

which is integrable over the bounded set
B(z,(\tilde{\lambda}+3)s)^{2}\subset X\times X
by assumption (
4.2
). As
r\to 0
, it is evident that we have
1_{\{\rho(v,y)<2r\}}\to 0
pointwise (except when
v=y
, but in this case the integrand vanishes anyway due to the factor
|f(y)-f(v)|^{p}
), and hence the integral above converges to
0
by dominated convergence. This shows that

\fint_{B(z,s)}|f-\langle f\rangle_{B(z,s)}|\,\mathrm{d}\mu=0,

and hence
f\equiv\langle f\rangle_{B(z,s)}
almost everywhere in
B(x,s)
, as we wanted to show.
∎

In the context of characterising the Schatten class properties of commutators (see Section
1.B.2
for a short discussion and
[
30
]
for the full story), yet another variant of the Besov norm naturally appears, namely

\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{p}(\mu)}:=\Big[\iint_{X\times X}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}}{V(x,y)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)\Big]^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Under the additional assumption of
lower dimension

d
as in (
1.2
) (which turns out to be natural in view of the applications in
[
30
]
), we also obtain a constancy criterion in terms of these norms as a quick corollary:

4.3 Corollary
.

Let
1\leq p\leq d<\infty
and let
(X,\rho,\mu)
be a doubling metric measure space having lower dimension
d
in the sense of (
1.2
) and supporting the
(1,p)
-Poincaré inequality.
If
f\in L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mu)
satisfies

\Big[(x,y)\mapsto\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}}{V(x,y)^{2}}\Big]\in L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mu\times\mu),

(4.4)

then
f
is equal to a constant almost everywhere. In particular,

\dot{B}^{p}(\mu)=\{\text{constants}\}.

Proof.

Fix a ball
B_{0}:=B(x_{0},R)
. For all
x,y\in B_{0}
, we have

\begin{split}\frac{\mu(B_{0})}{V(x,y)}&\approx\frac{V(x,R)}{V(x,y)}\quad\text{by doubling}\\
&\gtrsim\Big(\frac{R}{\rho(x,y)}\Big)^{d}\quad\text{buy lower dimension}\\
&\gtrsim\Big(\frac{R}{\rho(x,y)}\Big)^{p}\quad\text{by }d\geq p\text{ and }\frac{R}{\rho(x,y)}\gtrsim 1.\end{split}

hence

\frac{1}{\rho(x,y)^{p}}\lesssim\frac{1}{V(x,y)}\frac{\mu(B_{0})}{R^{p}},

and thus

\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}}{\rho(x,y)^{p}V(x,y)}\lesssim\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}}{V(x,y)^{2}}\frac{\mu(B_{0})}{R^{p}}.

Under the assumption (
4.4
), the right-hand side is integrable over
B_{0}\times B_{0}
, and hence is the left-hand side. Since this is true for every ball
B_{0}
, we have verified assumption (
4.2
) of Proposition
4.1
, and the said proposition shows that
f
is constant.
∎

5.
The lower bound for the Sobolev norm

Our aim in the rest of the paper is to prove the characterisation of the Hajłasz–Sobolev space formulated Theorem
1.6
, our main result. We begin with the easy direction. This short section is a relatively straightforward adaptation of
[
20
, Section 2]
. Moreover, this direction is valid under much more general assumptions than the full Theorem
1.6
.

5.1 Proposition
.

Let
(X,\rho,\mu)
be a space of homogeneous type, i.e., a quasi-metric space with a doubling measure.
If
f\in\dot{M}^{1,p}(\mu)
, then
m_{f}\in L^{p,\infty}(\nu_{p})
and

\|m_{f}\|_{L^{p,\infty}(\nu_{p})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{M}^{1,p}(\mu)}.

Proof.

Let
h
be a Hajłasz upper gradient of
f
.
For
y,z\in B(x,t)
, it follows from definition (
2.9
) of the Hajłasz upper gradient and the quasi-triangle inequality

\rho(y,z)\leq A_{0}(\rho(y,x)+\rho(x,z))
that

|f(y)-f(z)|\leq\rho(y,z)(h(y)+h(z))\leq 2A_{0}t(h(y)+h(z)),

and hence

\begin{split}m_{f}(x,t)&\leq\fint_{B(x,t)}\fint_{B(x,t)}|f(y)-f(z)|\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(z)\\
&\leq 2A_{0}t\fint_{B(x,t)}\fint_{B(x,t)}(h(y)+h(z))\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(z)\\
&=2A_{0}t\Big(\fint_{B(x,t)}h(y)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)+\fint_{B(x,t)}h(z)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(z)\Big)\\
&=4A_{0}t\fint_{B(x,t)}h\,\mathrm{d}\mu\leq 4A_{0}tMh(x),\end{split}

where
M
is the Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator.

Thus

\{(x,t):m_{f}(x,t)>\kappa\}\subseteq\{(x,t):4A_{0}tMh(x)>\kappa\}

and

\begin{split}p\nu_{p}(\{m_{f}>\kappa\})&\leq\int_{X}\int_{0}^{\infty}1_{\{(x,t):4A_{0}tMh(x)>\kappa\}}pt^{-p-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\\
&=\int_{X}\int_{\kappa/(4A_{0}Mh(x))}^{\infty}pt^{-p-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\\
&=\int_{X}\Big(\frac{4A_{0}Mh(x)}{\kappa}\Big)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\\
&\lesssim\frac{1}{\kappa^{p}}\int_{X}h(x)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\end{split}

by the maximal inequality in the last step.

Thus we have proved that

\sup_{\kappa>0}\kappa^{p}\nu_{p}(\{m_{f}>\kappa\})\lesssim\int_{X}h^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu

whenever
h
is a Hajłasz upper gradient of
f
. Taking the infimum over all such
h
gives the claimed bound.
∎

6.
Maximal versus mean oscillations

In contrast to the easy argument of Section
5
, the upper bound of the Sobolev norm is much more delicate and requires some preparations. We note that the Euclidean argument of
[
20
, Section 3]
and its Carnot group extension
[
37
, Appendix A]
proceed by first considering test functions of class
C^{1,1}
(i.e.,
f\in C^{1}
whose gradient
\nabla f
is a Lipschitz mapping), and using Taylor expansions with second-order error terms in the estimates. Since these are not available in the present generality, this section develops some alternative tools that have no obvious counterpart in the previous literature.

More precisely, we want to compare the pointwise Lipschitz constant

\operatorname{lip}f(x):=\liminf_{r\to 0}\sup_{\rho(x,y)\leq r}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{r},

describing the
maximal oscillations
of
f
at point
x
and scale
r\to 0
,
with the
a priori
much smaller
mean oscillations
, which are more closely connected to the norm
\|m_{f}\|_{L^{p,\infty}(\nu_{p})}
in the desired characterisation:

g_{f}(x):=\liminf_{t\to 0}\frac{1}{t}m_{f}(x,t),\qquad m_{f}(x,t):=\fint_{B(x,t)}|f-\langle f\rangle_{B(x,t)}|\,\mathrm{d}\mu.

(6.1)

6.2 Remark
.

For all
f\in L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mu)
and all
x\in X
, we have

g_{f}(x)\leq 2\operatorname{lip}f(x).

Indeed,

\begin{split}m_{f}(x,t)&\leq\fint_{B(x,t)}\fint_{B(x,t)}|f(y)-f(z)|\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(z)\\
&\leq 2\fint_{B(x,t)}|f(y)-f(x)|\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)\leq 2\sup_{d(y,x)\leq t}|f(y)-f(x)|,\end{split}

and dividing by
t
and taking the
\liminf
of both sides proves the claim.

A deeper result is the estimate in the converse direction; this is the main result of this section.

6.3 Proposition
.

Let
(X,\rho,\mu)
be a doubling metric measure space supporting the
(1,p)
-Poincaré inequality for some
p\in(1,\infty)
.
If
f
is a locally Lipschitz function, then

\operatorname{lip}f(x)\lesssim g_{f}(x)

\mu
-almost everywhere, where the implied constant depends only on the doubling constant
c_{\mu}
and the parameters in the Poincaré inequality.

To streamline the proof of the Proposition
6.3
, we start with some lemmas:

6.4 Lemma
.

Let
(X,\rho,\mu)
be a doubling metric measure space and
f\in L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mu)
. Then for all
\delta>0
,
K\in\mathbb{N}
, and
u_{1},u_{2}\in X
with
\rho(u_{1},u_{2})\leq\delta
, we have

\begin{split}&|f(u_{1})-f(u_{2})|\\
&\qquad\leq\sum_{i=1}^{2}\Big(c_{\mu}m_{f}(u_{i},3\delta)+c_{\mu}\sum_{k=0}^{K}m_{f}(u_{i},2^{1-k}\delta)+\fint_{B(u_{i},2^{-K}\delta)}|f-f(u_{i})|\,\mathrm{d}\mu\Big).\end{split}

If
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
are Lebesgue points of
f
, then we also have

|f(u_{1})-f(u_{2})|\leq c_{\mu}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\Big(m_{f}(u_{i},3\delta)+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}m_{f}(u_{i},2^{1-k}\delta)\Big).

Proof.

We fix
u\in\{u_{1},u_{2}\}
. Then

f(u_{1})-f(u_{2})=(f(u_{1})-\langle f\rangle_{B(u,3\delta)})-(f(u_{2})-\langle f\rangle_{B(u,3\delta)}).

For both
i=1,2
, we have

\begin{split}f(u_{i})-\langle f\rangle_{B(u,3\delta)}&=f(u_{i})-\langle f\rangle_{B(u_{i},2^{-K}\delta)}\\
&\qquad+\sum_{k=0}^{K}\Big(\langle f\rangle_{B(u_{i},2^{-k}\delta)}-\langle f\rangle_{B(u_{i},2^{1-k}\delta)}\Big)\\
&\qquad+\Big(\langle f\rangle_{B(u_{i},2\delta)}-\langle f\rangle_{B(u,3\delta)}\Big)\\
&=:I+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}II_{k}+III.\end{split}

(6.5)

It is immediate that

|I|\leq\fint_{B(u_{i},2^{-K}\delta)}|f-f(u_{i})|\,\mathrm{d}\mu.

Let us then consider any of the pairs of balls

(B^{\prime},B)\in\{(B(u_{i},2^{-k}\delta),B(u_{i},2^{1-k}\delta)),(B(u_{i},2\delta),B(u,3\delta))\}

appearing in the remainder of the expansion. In the first case,
B=2B^{\prime}
. If
u=u_{i}
in the second case, then
B=\frac{3}{2}B^{\prime}
. Thus, in both these cases, we have

B^{\prime}\subset B\subset 2B^{\prime}.

(6.6)

We claim that this holds also in the remaining case that
\{u,u_{i}\}=\{u_{1},u_{2}\}
. In fact, since
B(x,r)\subset B(y,r+\rho(x,y))
and
\rho(u,u_{i})=\delta
, it follows that
B^{\prime}=B(u_{i},2\delta)\subset B(u,3\delta)=B\subset B(u_{i},4\delta)=2B^{\prime}
.

Under the condition (
6.6
), we have

|\langle f\rangle_{B^{\prime}}-\langle f\rangle_{B}|\leq\fint_{B^{\prime}}|f-\langle f\rangle_{B}|\,\mathrm{d}\mu\leq c_{\mu}\fint_{B}|f-\langle f\rangle_{B}|\,\mathrm{d}\mu=c_{\mu}m_{f}(B).

Using this in (
6.5
), we obtain

|f(u_{i})-\langle f\rangle_{B(u,3\delta)}|\leq c_{\mu}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}m_{f}(u_{i},2^{1-k}\delta)+c_{\mu}m_{f}(u,3\delta),

and hence

|f(u_{1})-f(u_{2})|\leq c_{\mu}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}m_{f}(u_{i},2^{1-k}\delta)+2c_{\mu}m_{f}(u,3\delta).

This is valid for both choices of
u\in\{u_{1},u_{2}\}
, so taking the minimum of the two bounds gives the first claim of the lemma. The second one follows by taking
K\to\infty
and using the definition of a Lebesgue point to the integrals over
B(u_{i},2^{-K}\delta)
.
∎

6.7 Lemma
.

Let
f
be a Lipschitz function on a doubling metric measure space
(X,\rho,\mu)
that satisfies the
(1,p)
-Poincaré inequality with constants
(\lambda,c_{P})
. Then for all
u_{1},u_{2}\in X
, all
\delta\geq d(u_{1},u_{2})
and
\alpha\in(0,1]
, we have

|f(u_{1})-f(u_{2})|\leq 2\alpha\|f\|_{\operatorname{Lip}}\delta+14C\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1,2\\
\alpha\lambda\delta\leq s\leq 3\lambda\delta\end{subarray}}\Big(\fint_{B(u_{i},s)}(\operatorname{lip}f)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\Big)^{1/p},

where
C=c_{\mu}\cdot c_{P}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
6.4
, we have

\begin{split}&|f(u_{1})-f(u_{2})|\\
&\quad\leq\sum_{i=1}^{2}\Big(c_{\mu}m_{f}(u_{i},3\delta)+c_{\mu}\sum_{k=0}^{K}m_{f}(u_{i},2^{1-k}\delta)+\fint_{B(u_{i},2^{-K}\delta)}|f-f(u_{i})|\,\mathrm{d}\mu\Big).\end{split}

Let
K
be the unique positive integer such that
2^{-K}<\alpha\leq 2^{1-K}
. Then

\fint_{B(u_{i},2^{-K}\delta)}|f-f(u_{i})|\,\mathrm{d}\mu\leq 2^{-K}\delta\|f\|_{\operatorname{Lip}}<\alpha\delta\|f\|_{\operatorname{Lip}}.

On the other hand, for each
\theta\in\{3,2^{1-k}:k=0,1,\ldots,K\}\subset[\alpha,3]
, we have

m_{f}(u_{i},\theta\delta)\leq c_{P}\theta\delta\Big(\fint_{B(u_{i},\lambda\theta\delta)}(\operatorname{lip}f)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\Big)^{1/p},

and hence

\begin{split}m_{f}(u_{i},3\delta)&+\sum_{k=0}^{K}m_{f}(u_{i},2^{1-k}\delta)\\
&\leq c_{P}\Big(3+\sum_{k=0}^{K}2^{1-k}\Big)\delta\sup_{\alpha\lambda\delta\leq s\leq 3\lambda\delta}\Big(\fint_{B(u_{i},s)}(\operatorname{lip}f)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\Big)^{1/p},\end{split}

where

3+\sum_{k=0}^{K}2^{1-k}<3+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{1-k}=3+4=7.

A combination of these estimates proves the lemma.
∎

We are now prepared to give:

Proof of Proposition
6.3
.

Since the claim of the Proposition is local in nature, we may assume without loss of generality that
f
is globally Lipschitz. Indeed, once the result is proved for globally Lipschitz functions, the locally Lipschitz case easily follows by replacing
f
by
\phi f
, where
\phi
is a Lipschitz function identically
1
in a ball
B(x_{0},2R)
and vanishing outside
B(x_{0},4R)
; then
\phi f
is globally Lipschitz, and
g_{\phi f}(x)=g_{f}(x)
and
\operatorname{lip}_{\phi f}(x)=\operatorname{lip}_{f}(x)
for all
x\in B(x_{0},R)
. Thus, for the rest of the proof, we assume that
f
is globally Lipschitz.

The claim is trivial for points where
\operatorname{lip}f(x)=0
. Thus, for the rest of the proof, we only consider points with
\operatorname{lip}f(x)>0
. All such points belong to
A_{k}:=\{x\in X:2^{k}\leq\operatorname{lip}f(x)<2^{k+1}\}
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Almost every
x\in A_{k}
is a density point of
A_{k}
. Hence a.e.
x\in X
with
\operatorname{lip}f(x)>0
is a density point of
\{y\in X:\operatorname{lip}f(y)\leq 2\operatorname{lip}f(x)\}
. We fix one such point
x
, and prove the claimed estimate. By definition of
g_{f}(x)
, we need to show that there exists some
r(x,f)>0
such that, for all
r\in(0,r(x,f)]
,

\frac{1}{r}\fint_{B(x,r)}|f-\langle f\rangle_{B(x,r)}|\,\mathrm{d}\mu\gtrsim\operatorname{lip}f(x).

Let

\alpha:=\alpha(x,f):=\frac{\operatorname{lip}f(x)}{\|f\|_{\operatorname{Lip}}}\in(0,1].

Since this depends only on
x
and
f
, we may and will allow
r(x,f)
to depend on
\alpha
. We also define another small parameter

\theta:=\frac{1}{16\max(\lambda,22C)},

(6.8)

where
C=c_{\mu}\cdot c_{P}
depends only on the parameters of the space.

For any
r>0
and
z\in B(x,r)
, we note that

B(x,r)\subset B(z,2r)=2(\theta\alpha)^{-1}B(z,\theta\alpha r),

and hence

\mu(B(x,r))\leq c(\mu,\theta\alpha)\mu(B(z,\theta\alpha r)),

where
c(\mu,\alpha)
can be chosen to depend only on
\alpha=\alpha(x,f)
and the doubling constant
c_{\mu}
.

Since
x
is a density point of
\{y\in X:\operatorname{lip}f(y)\leq 2\operatorname{lip}f(x)\}
, there is a radius
r_{1}(x,f)>0
such that, for all
r\in(0,r_{1}(x,f)]
,

\frac{\mu(\{y\in B(x,r):\operatorname{lip}f(y)>2\operatorname{lip}f(x)\})}{\mu(B(x,r))}<\frac{\alpha^{p}}{c(\mu,\theta\alpha)}.

For such
r
, suppose that
B(z,s)\subset B(x,r)
satisfies
s\in[\theta\alpha r,r]
. Then, by the previous two displayed estimates,

\begin{split}&\frac{\mu(\{y\in B(z,s):\operatorname{lip}f(y)>2\operatorname{lip}f(x)\})}{\mu(B(z,s))}\\
&\leq\frac{\mu(\{y\in B(x,r):\operatorname{lip}f(y)>2\operatorname{lip}f(x)\})}{\mu(B(z,\theta\alpha r))}\\
&<\frac{\alpha^{p}}{c(\mu,\theta\alpha)}\frac{\mu(B(x,r))}{\mu(B(z,\theta\alpha r))}\leq\alpha^{p}.\end{split}

(6.9)

Thus, for all
B(z,s)\subset B(x,r)
with
s\in[\theta\alpha r,r]
,

\begin{split}&\Big(\fint_{B(z,s)}(\operatorname{lip}f)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\Big)^{1/p}\\
&\leq\Big((2\operatorname{lip}f(x))^{p}+\frac{\mu(\{y\in B(z,s):\operatorname{lip}f(y)>2\operatorname{lip}f(x)\})}{\mu(B(z,s))}\|f\|_{\operatorname{Lip}}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
&\leq\Big((2\operatorname{lip}f(x))^{p}+\alpha^{p}\|f\|_{\operatorname{Lip}}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
&\leq 2\operatorname{lip}f(x)+\alpha\|f\|_{\operatorname{Lip}}=3\operatorname{lip}f(x).\end{split}

(6.10)

By definition of
\operatorname{lip}f(x)
, there is
r_{2}(x,f)\in(0,r_{1}(x,f)]
such that, for all
r\in(0,r_{2}(x,f)]
,

\sup_{d(y,x)\leq r}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{r}>\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{lip}f(x).

In particular, with
\frac{3}{4}r
in place of
r
, for every
r\in(0,r_{2}(x,f)]
we can find some
w\in\bar{B}(x,\frac{3}{4}r)
such that

|f(x)-f(w)|>\frac{3}{4}r\cdot\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{lip}f(x)=\frac{3}{8}r\operatorname{lip}f(x).

Let then
z\in\{x,w\}
and
u\in B(z,\theta r)
. By Lemma
6.7
with
\delta=\theta r
, we have

\begin{split}|f(u)-f(z)|&\leq 2\alpha\theta r\|f\|_{\operatorname{Lip}}+14C\theta r\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in\{u,z\}\\
\alpha\lambda\theta r\leq s\leq 3\lambda\theta r\end{subarray}}\Big(\fint_{B(v,s)}(\operatorname{lip}f)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
&=:I+II,\end{split}

where

I=2\theta r\operatorname{lip}f(x).

As for
II
, we would like to apply (
6.10
), so we need to check that each
B(v,s)
appearing in
II
satisfies the relevant assumptions. Since
\lambda\geq 1
, it is immediate that
s\geq\alpha\lambda\theta r\geq\theta\alpha r
, which is the required lower bound. Since
v\in\{u,z\}\subset B(z,\theta r)\subset B(z,\lambda\theta r)
and
z\in\{x,w\}\subset\bar{B}(x,\frac{3}{4}r)
, it follows that

B(v,3\lambda\theta r)\subset B(z,\lambda\theta r+3\lambda\theta r)\subset B(x,\frac{3}{4}r+4\lambda\theta r)\subset B(x,r);

in the last step, we used
\theta\leq(16\lambda)^{-1}
, which is clear from (
6.8
).
Then also
s\leq 3\lambda\theta r\leq\frac{3}{16}r\leq r
, which is the required upper bound.

Hence, we have

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in\{u,z\}\\
\alpha\lambda\theta r\leq s\leq 3\lambda\theta r\end{subarray}}\Big(\fint_{B(v,s)}(\operatorname{lip}f)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq 3\operatorname{lip}f(x)

and hence

\begin{split}|f(u)-f(z)|\leq I+II&\leq 2\theta r\operatorname{lip}f(x)+14C\theta r\cdot 3\operatorname{lip}f(x)\\
&\leq 44C\theta r\operatorname{lip}f(x)\leq\frac{1}{8}r\operatorname{lip}f(x);\end{split}

in the last step, we used
\theta\leq(16\cdot 22C)^{-1}=(8\cdot 44C)^{-1}
, which is immediate from the choice made in (
6.8
).

Hence, we have proved the following: For all
u\in B(x,\theta r)
and
v\in B(z,\theta r)
, we have

\begin{split}|f(u)-f(v)|&\geq|f(x)-f(z)|-|f(u)-f(x)|-|f(v)-f(z)|\\
&\geq\frac{3}{8}r\operatorname{lip}f(x)-\frac{1}{8}r\operatorname{lip}f(x)-\frac{1}{8}r\operatorname{lip}f(x)=\frac{1}{8}r\operatorname{lip}f(x).\end{split}

Hence

\begin{split}\fint_{B(x,r)}|f-\langle f\rangle_{B(x,r)}|\,\mathrm{d}\mu&\geq\frac{1}{2}\fint_{B(x,r)}\fint_{B(x,r)}|f(u)-f(v)|\,\mathrm{d}\mu(u)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(v)\\
&\geq\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mu(B(x,\theta r))\mu(B(z,\theta r))}{\mu(B(x,r))^{2}}\frac{1}{8}r\operatorname{lip}f(x)\\
&\geq c_{1}(\mu,\theta)r\operatorname{lip}f(x),\end{split}

where
c_{1}(\mu,\theta)
depends only the doubling constant of
\mu
and the parameter
\theta
. Since
\theta
, as chosen in (
6.8
),
depends only on the parameters of the space, so does
c_{1}(\mu,\theta)
. Moreover, the estimates above were obtained for all
r\geq r_{1}(x,f)
. Thus, we can take
\liminf
to deduce that

\liminf_{r\to 0}\fint_{B(x,r)}|f-\langle f\rangle_{B(x,r)}|\,\mathrm{d}\mu\gtrsim\operatorname{lip}f(x),

where the implied constant depends only on the parameters of the space, as claimed.
∎

7.
Lipschitz approximation in
L^{p,\infty}(\nu_{p})

Recall that the main result of the previous section, Proposition
6.3
, was proved for locally Lipschitz functions only. In order to prove Theorem
1.6
without such
a priori
assumptions, we will need to make use of some approximation results. The density of Lipschitz functions in Sobolev spaces is well known: see
[
28
, Theorem 8.2.1]
for their density in another Sobolev-type space
N^{1,p}
, and
[
28
, Corollary 10.2.9]
for the coincidence
M^{1,p}=N^{1,p}
, both under the same assumptions that we are using. However, we need Lipschitz approximation in the
a priori
larger (by Proposition
5.1
) space of functions with
\|m_{f}\|_{L^{p,\infty}(\nu_{p})}<\infty
, and such results do not seem to be available in the previous literature, except in the special cases of
\mathbb{R}^{d}

[
20
]
and Carnot groups
[
37
]
, where they are relatively easy consequences of standard mollification by convolution.

In the setting at hand, we already introduced the approximate identity
\Phi_{t}
in (
3.2
), and proved some first properties in Lemma
3.3
. Let us next compare the mean oscillations of
f
and
\Phi_{t}f
:

7.1 Lemma
.

Let
(X,\rho,\mu)
be a doubling metric measure space. Then for all functions
f\in L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mu)
, points
z\in X
, and
r,t>0
,
the approximations
\Phi_{t}f
from (
3.2
) satisfy the estimate

m_{\Phi_{t}f}(z,r)\lesssim\frac{\min(r,t)}{t}m_{f}(z,c\max(r,t))

(7.2)

for some
c
that only depends on the parameters of the space.

Proof.

If
r\leq t
, then all
x,y\in B(z,r)
satisfy
d(x,y)\leq 2r\leq 2t
and
B(x,bt)\subset B(z,r+bt)\subset B(z,(1+b)t)\subset B(x,(2+b)t)

Thus we obtain from Lemma
3.3
that

\begin{split}|\Phi_{t}f(x)-\Phi_{t}f(y)|&\lesssim\frac{d(x,y)}{t}\fint_{B(x,bt)}|f-\langle f\rangle_{B(x,bt)}|\,\mathrm{d}\mu\\
&\lesssim\frac{r}{t}\fint_{B(z,(1+b)t)}|f-\langle f\rangle_{B(z,(1+b)t)}|\,\mathrm{d}\mu.\end{split}

Hence

\begin{split}\operatorname{LHS}\eqref{eq:oscPhit}&\leq\fint_{B(z,r)}\fint_{B(z,r)}|\Phi_{t}f(x)-\Phi_{t}f(y)|\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)\\
&\lesssim\frac{r}{t}\fint_{B(x,(1+b)t)}|f-\langle f\rangle_{B(x,(1+b)t)}|\,\mathrm{d}\mu.\end{split}

We turn to
r\geq t
. It suffices to prove the estimate with some
k
in place of
\langle\Phi_{t}f\rangle_{B(z,r)}
in the expression

m_{\Phi_{t}}(z,r)=\fint_{B(z,r)}|\Phi_{t}f-\langle\Phi_{t}f\rangle_{B(z,r)}|\,\mathrm{d}\mu.

We have

\Phi_{t}f-k=\sum_{i}\phi_{i}(\langle f\rangle_{B(x_{i},t)}-k)=\sum_{i}\phi_{i}\langle f-k\rangle_{B(x_{i},t)}.

Hence

\fint_{B(z,r)}|\Phi_{t}f-k|\,\mathrm{d}\mu\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i:B(x_{i},(1+\gamma)t)\\
\phantom{i:}\cap B(z,r)\neq\varnothing\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(B(x_{i},(1+\gamma)t))}{\mu(B(z,r)}\fint_{B(x_{i},t)}|f-k|\,\mathrm{d}\mu.

If
B(x_{i},(1+\gamma)t)\cap B(z,r)\neq\varnothing
, then

B(x_{i},t)\subset B(z,r+2(1+\gamma)t)\subset B(z,(3+2\gamma)r).

Abbreviating the summation condition
B(x_{i},(1+\gamma)t)\cap B(z,r)\neq\varnothing
as “
*
”, it follows that

\begin{split}\fint_{B(z,r)}|\Phi_{t}f-k|\,\mathrm{d}\mu&\lesssim\frac{1}{\mu(B(z,r))}\int_{B(z,(3+2\gamma)r)}\sum_{i}^{*}1_{B(x_{i},t)}|f-k|\,\mathrm{d}\mu\\
&\lesssim\frac{1}{\mu(B(z,r))}\int_{B(z,(3+2\gamma)r)}|f-k|\,\mathrm{d}\mu\\
&\lesssim\fint_{B(z,(3+2\gamma)r)}|f-k|\,\mathrm{d}\mu,\end{split}

using in the second step the bounded overlap of the balls
B(x_{i},t)
. Now we are free to pick
k=\langle f\rangle_{B(z,r+3t)}
.

Comparing the bounds in the two cases
r\leq t
and
r\geq t
, and applying doubling one more time, we obtain the claimed bound (
7.2
) with
c=\max\{1+b,3+2\gamma\}
.
∎

A weakness of Lemma
7.1
is that, for
r<t
, oscillations of
\Phi_{t}f
on the small scale
r
are dominated by the oscillations of
f
on the large scale
t
. This issue will be rectified with the help of the macroscopic Poincaré inequality (Theorem
3.4
) in the following lemma, which allows us to control the mean oscillations of
\Phi_{t}f
by the mean oscillations of
f
on the same scale:

7.3 Lemma
.

Let
(X,\rho,\mu)
be a doubling metric measure space and satisfy the
(1,p)
-Poincaré inequality with constants
(\lambda,c_{P})
. Then for all
f\in L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mu)
, all
z\in X
and
r,t>0
, the approximations
\Phi_{t}f
from (
3.2
) satisfy the estimate

m_{\Phi_{t}f}(z,r)\lesssim\Big(\fint_{B(z,c\tilde{\lambda}\max(r,t))}m_{f}(x,r)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Thus, the oscillation of the mollification
\Phi_{t}f
are controlled by an average of oscillations of the original
f
at the same scale
r
. This is our substitute for the Euclidean estimate
[
20
, proof of Lemma 3.2]

m_{\varphi*f}(z,r)\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(x)m_{f}(z-x,r)\,\mathrm{d}z,

(7.4)

which follows easily in the presence of an underlying group structure.

Proof.

By Lemma
7.1
in the first step and Theorem
3.4
in the second, we obtain

\begin{split}m_{\Phi_{t}f}(z,r)&\lesssim\frac{\min(r,t)}{t}m_{f}(z,c\max(r,t))\\
&\lesssim\frac{\min(r,t)}{t}\frac{\max(r,t)}{r}\Big(\fint_{B(z,c\tilde{\lambda}\max(r,t))}m_{f}(x,r)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
&=\Big(\fint_{B(z,c\tilde{\lambda}\max(r,t))}m_{f}(x,r)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}},\end{split}

where in the last step we simply observed that
\min(r,t)\max(r,t)=rt
.
∎

We can now show that the mollifications
\Phi_{t}f
remain uniformly bounded in the
L^{p,\infty}(\nu_{p})
norm:

7.5 Lemma
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
(X,\rho,\mu)
be a complete doubling metric measure space and satisfy the
(1,p)
-Poincaré inequality with constants
(\lambda,c_{P})
. Then for all
f\in L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mu)
and all
t>0
, the approximations
\Phi_{t}f
from (
3.2
) satisfy the estimate

\|m_{\Phi_{t}f}\|_{L^{p,\infty}(\nu_{p})}\lesssim\|m_{f}\|_{L^{p,\infty}(\nu_{p})}.

This is our analogue of
[
20
, Lemma 3.2]
, using Lemma
7.3
as a substitute of (
7.4
). In the proof of
[
20
, Lemma 3.2]
, using a proper norm equivalent to the quasi-norm
\|\ \|_{L^{p,\infty}}
and the translation invariance of the Lebesgue measure, it is immediate that

\begin{split}\|m_{\varphi*f}\|_{L^{p,\infty}(\nu_{p})}&\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(x)\|m_{f}(\cdot-x,\cdot)\|_{L^{p,\infty}(\nu_{p})}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(x)\|m_{f}\|_{L^{p,\infty}(\nu_{p})}\,\mathrm{d}x=\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}}\|m_{f}\|_{L^{p,\infty}(\nu_{p})}.\end{split}

Proof.

Slightly more generally, we will prove the lemma for any product
\mu\times\tau
of the measure
\mu
on
X
and any
\sigma
-finite measure
\tau
on
(0,\infty)
; in the case of
\nu_{p}
, we have
\,\mathrm{d}\tau(r)=r^{-p-1}\,\mathrm{d}r
. Writing
\mu\times\tau
instead of
\nu_{p}
will hopefully clarify the argument, since we will make some use of interpolation with respect to the Lebesgue exponent
p
, but no change of measure will be involved.

By the Keith–Zhong Theorem
2.6
,
(X,\rho,\mu)
also satisfies a
(1,p-\varepsilon)
-Poincaré inequality for some
\varepsilon\in(0,p-1)
. Thus, by Lemma
7.3
, we have

\begin{split}&\|m_{\Phi_{t}f}\|_{L^{p,\infty}(\mu\times\tau)}\\
&\lesssim\Big\|(z,r)\mapsto\Big(\fint_{B(z,c\tilde{\lambda}\max(r,t))}m_{f}(x,r)^{p-\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\Big)^{1/(p-\varepsilon)}\Big\|_{L^{p,\infty}(\mu\times\tau)}\\
&=\Big\|(z,r)\mapsto\fint_{B(z,c\tilde{\lambda}\max(r,t))}m_{f}(x,r)^{p-\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\Big\|_{L^{p/(p-\varepsilon),\infty}(\mu\times\tau)}^{1/(p-\varepsilon)}.\end{split}

Regarding
t
as fixed throughout the argument, let us denote

q:=\frac{p}{p-\varepsilon}>1,\qquad g:=m_{f}^{p-\varepsilon},\qquad\alpha(r):=c\tilde{\lambda}\max(r,t),

and

\mathcal{A}h(z,r):=\fint_{B(z,\alpha(r))}g(x,r)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x).

The claimed bound will follow if we can prove that

\|\mathcal{A}g\|_{L^{q,\infty}(\mu\times\tau)}\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{q,\infty}(\mu\times\tau)},\qquad q>1.

(7.6)

Let us first consider the corresponding strong-type bound. By Fubini’s theorem, we have

\begin{split}\|\mathcal{A}g\|_{L^{q}(\mu\times\tau)}^{q}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{X}\Big|\fint_{B(z,\alpha(r))}g(x,r)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\Big|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(z)\,\mathrm{d}\tau(r),\end{split}

where

\begin{split}\int_{X}&\Big|\fint_{B(z,\alpha(r))}g(x,r)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\Big|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(z)\\
&\leq\int_{X}\fint_{B(z,\alpha(r))}|g(x,r)|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(z)\\
&=\int_{X}\int_{X}\frac{1_{\{\rho(x,z)<\alpha(r)\}}}{\mu(B(z,\alpha(r))}|g(x,r)|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(z)\\
&\eqsim\int_{X}\Big(\int_{X}\frac{1_{\{\rho(x,z)<\alpha(r)\}}}{\mu(B(x,\alpha(r))}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(z)\Big)|g(x,r)|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)=\int_{X}|g(x,r)|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x).\end{split}

Integrating over
r
, it follows that

\|\mathcal{A}g\|_{L^{q}(\mu\times\tau)}\lesssim\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{X}|g(x,r)|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\,\mathrm{d}\tau(r)\Big)^{1/q}=\|g\|_{L^{q}(\mu\times\tau)}.

(7.7)

This estimate works for all
q\in[1,\infty]
; the case
q=\infty
(that we do not really need but just mention for completeness) requires an easy modification of the argument.

This is the strong type bound corresponding to (
7.6
). The required (
7.6
) then follows by choosing some
1\leq q_{0}<q<q_{1}\leq\infty
and interpolating (
7.7
) between these two values. See e.g.
[
29
, Theorem 2.2.3]
for a version covering the case at hand, with an
L^{q,\infty}
-to-
L^{q,\infty}
-bound (
7.6
) as a conclusion.
∎

8.
The upper bound for the Sobolev norm

We finally return to the other direction of the proof of Theorem
1.6
.
With the results of Sections
6
and
7
available as substitutes of some Euclidean tools, the proof can now be completed by an adaptation of the Euclidean argument of
[
20
]
. The following lemma borrows some ideas from the proof of
[
20
, Lemma 3.1]
, but is actually simpler. This may be partly attributed to the fact that we only prove equivalences of norms, in contrast to the identities achieved in the presence of Euclidean symmetries in
[
20
]
.

8.1 Lemma
.

Let
(X,\rho,\mu)
be a space of homogeneous type, and
f\in L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mu)
. Then

\int_{X}g_{f}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\leq p\liminf_{\kappa\to 0}\kappa^{p}\nu_{p}(\{m_{f}>\kappa\}),

where
g_{f}
and
m_{f}
are as in (
6.1
).

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and, for
j\in\mathbb{N}
,

E_{j}:=\{x\in B(x_{0},2^{j}):\text{ for all }t\in(0,2^{-j}]:g_{f}(x)\leq(1+\varepsilon)\frac{1}{t}m_{f}(x,t)\}.

Then
E_{j}\subseteq E_{j+1}
and it follows from the definition of
\liminf
that
\bigcup_{j=0}^{\infty}E_{j}=X
.

\begin{split}\int_{E_{j}}g_{f}(x)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)&=\kappa^{p}\int_{E_{j}}\Big(\frac{g_{f}(x)}{\kappa}\Big)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\\
&=\kappa^{p}\int_{E_{j}}[\Big(\frac{g_{f}(x)}{\kappa}\Big)^{p}-2^{jp}]\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)+\kappa^{p}2^{jp}\mu(E_{j})=:I+II,\end{split}

where

\begin{split}I&\leq\kappa^{p}\int_{E_{j}}[\Big(\frac{g_{f}(x)}{\kappa}\Big)^{p}-2^{jp}]_{+}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\\
&=\kappa^{p}\int_{E_{j}}\Big(\int_{\kappa/g_{f}(x)}^{2^{-j}}pt^{-p-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)_{+}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\\
&=\kappa^{p}\int_{E_{j}}\int_{0}^{2^{-j}}1_{\{(x,t):tg_{f}(x)>\kappa\}}pt^{-p-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\\
&\leq\kappa^{p}\int_{E_{j}}\int_{0}^{2^{-j}}1_{\{(x,t):(1+\varepsilon)m_{f}(x,t)>\kappa\}}pt^{-p-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\\
&\leq p\kappa^{p}\nu_{p}\Big(\Big\{(x,t)\in X\times(0,\infty):m_{f}(x,t)>\frac{\kappa}{1+\varepsilon}\Big\}\Big).\end{split}

Thus, for any
\kappa>0
, we have

\int_{E_{j}}g_{f}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\leq p\kappa^{p}\nu_{p}(\{m_{f}>\frac{\kappa}{1+\varepsilon}\})+\kappa^{p}2^{jp}\mu(E_{j}),

where the left-hand side is independent of
\kappa
. We can hence take
\liminf_{\kappa\to 0}
on the right. In this limit, the final term vanishes, and hence we are left with

\begin{split}\int_{E_{j}}g_{f}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu&\leq p\liminf_{\kappa\to 0}\kappa^{p}\nu_{p}(\{m_{f}>\frac{\kappa}{1+\varepsilon}\})\\
&=p(1+\varepsilon)^{p}\liminf_{\kappa\to 0}\kappa^{p}\nu_{p}(\{m_{f}>\kappa\}).\end{split}

The right-hand side is independent of
j
, and hence we can let
j\to\infty
on the left and apply monotone convergence to deduce that

\int_{X}g_{f}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\leq p(1+\varepsilon)^{p}\liminf_{\kappa\to 0}\kappa^{p}\nu_{p}(\{m_{f}>\kappa\}).

Finally, letting
\varepsilon\to 0
, we obtain the
asserted bound.
∎

We are now in a position to prove the upper bound of the Sobolev norm in Theorem
1.6
.

8.2 Proposition
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and let
(X,\rho,\mu)
be a complete doubling metric space that supports the
(1,p)
-Poincaré inequality. If
f\in L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mu)
satisfies
m_{f}\in L^{p,\infty}(\nu_{p})
, then
f\in\dot{M}^{1,p}(\mu)
and

\|f\|_{\dot{M}^{1,p}(\mu)}\lesssim\|m_{f}\|_{L^{p,\infty}(\nu_{p})}.

Proof.

We approximate
f
by the locally Lipschitz functions
\Phi_{t}f
from (
3.2
).
These satisfy

\begin{split}\|\operatorname{lip}(\Phi_{t}f)\|_{L^{p}(\mu)}&\lesssim\|g_{\Phi_{t}f}\|_{L^{p}(\mu)}\qquad\text{(Proposition \ref{prop:Lip-gf})}\\
&\lesssim\|m_{\Phi_{t}f}\|_{L^{p,\infty}(\nu_{p})}\qquad\text{(Lemma \ref{lem:gfp<})}\\
&\lesssim\|m_{f}\|_{L^{p,\infty}(\nu_{p})}\qquad\text{(Lemma \ref{lem:mPhitf-mf})}.\end{split}

(8.3)

By the Keith–Zhong Theorem
2.6
,
(X,\rho,\mu)
satisfies the
(1,p-\varepsilon)
-Poincaré inequality for some
\varepsilon>0
. By estimate (
8.3
), the functions
(\operatorname{lip}(\Phi_{t}f))^{p-\varepsilon}
form a bounded set in the reflexive space
L^{\frac{p}{p-\varepsilon}}(\mu)
. Hence we can find a subsequence
(\operatorname{lip}(\Phi_{t_{j}}f))^{p-\varepsilon}
, with
t_{j}\to 0
, that converges weakly in
L^{\frac{p}{p-\varepsilon}}(\mu)
to some function
h
, so that
g:=h^{\frac{1}{p-\varepsilon}}\in L^{p}(\mu)
. From the properties of weak limits, we have

\begin{split}\|g\|_{L^{p}(\mu)}&=\|h\|_{L^{\frac{p}{p-\varepsilon}}(\mu)}^{\frac{1}{p-\varepsilon}}\\
&\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}\|(\operatorname{lip}(\Phi_{t_{j}}f))^{p-\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{p}{p-\varepsilon}}(\mu)}^{\frac{1}{p-\varepsilon}}\\
&=\liminf_{j\to\infty}\|\operatorname{lip}(\Phi_{t_{j}}f)\|_{L^{p}(\mu)}\lesssim\|m_{f}\|_{L^{p,\infty}(\nu_{p})}.\end{split}

(8.4)

Since also
\Phi_{t_{j}}f\to f
in
L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mu)
, it follows that

\begin{split}\frac{1}{r(B)}&\fint_{B}|f-\langle f\rangle_{B}|\\
&=\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{1}{r(B)}\fint_{B}|\Phi_{t_{j}}f-\langle\Phi_{t_{j}}f\rangle_{B}|\quad\text{since }\Phi_{t_{j}}f\to f\text{ in }L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mu)\\
&\lesssim\liminf_{j\to\infty}\Big(\fint_{\lambda B}(\operatorname{lip}\Phi_{t_{j}}f)^{p-\varepsilon}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p-\varepsilon}}\quad\text{by $(1,p-\varepsilon)$-Poincar\'{e}}\\
&=\Big(\fint_{\lambda B}g^{p-\varepsilon}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p-\varepsilon}}\end{split}

by the weak convergence
(\operatorname{lip}\Phi_{t_{j}}f)^{p-\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup g^{p-\varepsilon}
in
L^{\frac{p}{p-\varepsilon}}(\mu)
and the observation that
1_{\lambda B}\in L^{\frac{p}{\varepsilon}}(\mu)=(L^{\frac{p}{p-\varepsilon}}(\mu))^{*}
. By Lemma
6.4
, we have, for all Lebesgue points
u_{1},u_{2}\in X
of
f
at distance
\delta=d(u_{1},u_{2})
,

\begin{split}|f(u_{1})-f(u_{2})|&\leq c_{\mu}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\Big[m_{f}(u_{i},3\delta)+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}m_{f}(u_{i},2^{1-k}\delta)\Big]\\
&\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{2}\Big[3\delta\Big(\fint_{B(u_{i},3\lambda\delta)}g^{p-\varepsilon}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p-\varepsilon}}\\
&\qquad+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{1-k}\lambda\delta\Big(\fint_{B(u_{i},2^{1-k}\lambda\delta)}g^{p-\varepsilon}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p-\varepsilon}}\Big]\\
&\leq\sum_{i=1}^{2}\Big(3+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{1-k}\Big)\delta(M_{p-\varepsilon}g)(u_{i})\\
&=7d(u_{1},u_{2})\sum_{i=1}^{2}(M_{p-\varepsilon}g)(u_{i}),\end{split}

(8.5)

where
M_{p-\varepsilon}g:=[M(g^{p-\varepsilon})]^{\frac{1}{p-\varepsilon}}
is the rescaled maximal function.

This shows that
c\cdot M_{p-\varepsilon}g
, for some constant
c
depending only on the parameters of the space, is a Hajłasz upper gradient of
f
, and hence

\begin{split}\|f\|_{\dot{M}^{1,p}(\mu)}&\lesssim\|M_{p-\varepsilon}g\|_{L^{p}(\mu)}\qquad\text{(definition of $\dot{M}^{1,p}(\mu)$)}\\
&\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{p}(\mu)}\qquad\text{(maximal inequality)}\\
&\lesssim\|m_{f}\|_{L^{p,\infty}(\nu_{p})}\qquad\text{(by \eqref{eq:g-mf})}.\end{split}

This completes the proof.
∎

9.
The limit relation

Combining Propositions
5.1
and
8.2
, we obtain the norm equivalence (
1.8
) of Theorem
1.6
. To complete the proof of the said theorem, it remains to establish the limit relation (
1.9
). Since
\liminf
is obviously bounded by
\sup
, we only need to consider the estimate “
\lesssim
”.

We will need the following density result, which is most likely known. Since most sources deal with the non-homogeneous space
M^{1,p}
instead of
\dot{M}^{1,p}
(see e.g.
[
28
, Lemma 10.2.7]
), we provide the short proof for completeness.

9.1 Proposition
.

Let
(X,\rho,\mu)
be a doubling metric measure space.
Then Lipschitz functions are dense in
\dot{M}^{1,p}(\mu)
.

Proof.

Let
h\in L^{p}(\mu)
be a Hajłasz upper gradient of
f\in\dot{M}^{1,p}
. Given
L>0
, consider the restriction
f|\{h\leq L\}
. For
x,y\in\{h\leq L\}
, we have

|f(x)-f(y)|\leq\rho(x,y)(h(x)+h(y))\leq 2L\rho(x,y),

so this restriction is
2L
-Lipschitz. Hence it can be extended to a
cL
-Lipschitz function
\tilde{f}
on all of
X
. (For real-valued functions, the classical McShane extension
[
28
, page 99]
preserves the Lipschitz constant, so we may take
c=2
; for
\mathbb{C}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{2}
-valued functions,
c=2\sqrt{2}
will fo
[
28
, Corollary 4.1.6]
. In fact, this extension is possible even for Banach space
V
-valued functions, but then the constant
c
may also depend on the parameters of the domain space
X

[
28
, Theorem 4.1.12]
.)

Now consider
f^{\prime}:=f-\tilde{f}
. We claim that

|f^{\prime}(x)-f^{\prime}(y)|\lesssim d(x,y)\max_{z\in\{x,y\}}h(z)1_{\{h(z)>L\}},

(9.2)

which shows that
h^{\prime}:=ch\cdot 1_{\{h>L\}}
is a Hajłasz upper gradient of
f^{\prime}
. If
x,y\in\{h\leq L\}
, then (
9.2
) holds trivially in the form
0\leq 0
. Otherwise, we always have

\begin{split}&|f^{\prime}(x)-f^{\prime}(y)|\leq|f(x)-f(y)|+|\tilde{f}(x)-\tilde{f}(y)|\\
&\quad\leq d(x,y)(h(x)+h(y))+d(x,y)cL=d(x,y)(h(x)+h(y)+cL),\end{split}

If at least one of
x,y
belongs to
\{h>L\}
, then
\max\{h(x),h(y),L\}=h(z)>L
for some
z\in\{x,y\}
. Thus

h(x)+h(y)+cL\leq(2+c)h(z)=(2+c)h(z)1_{\{h(z)>L\}},

as required for (
9.2
). Thus

\|f-\tilde{f}\|_{\dot{M}^{1,p}}\lesssim\|h\cdot 1_{\{h>L\}}\|_{L^{p}(\mu)},

and the right-hand side converges to zero as
L\to\infty
by dominated convergence.
∎

We can now prove the following result, which provides the last missing piece (
1.9
) of Theorem
1.6
.

9.3 Proposition
.

Let
(X,\rho,\mu)
be a complete doubling metric measure space that supports the
(1,p)
-Poincaré inequality.
For
f\in\dot{M}^{1,p}(\mu)
, we have

\|f\|_{\dot{M}^{1,p}(\mu)}\lesssim\liminf_{\kappa\to 0}\kappa\nu_{p}(\{m_{f}>\kappa\})^{1/p}.

Proof.

By Proposition
9.1
, we can find a sequence of Lipschitz functions
f_{n}
such that
\|f-f_{n}\|_{\dot{M}^{1,p}(\mu)}\to 0
.
We start with

\|f\|_{\dot{M}^{1,p}}\leq\|f_{n}\|_{\dot{M}^{1,p}}+\|f-f_{n}\|_{\dot{M}^{1,p}},\qquad f_{n}\in\operatorname{Lip}.

(9.4)

By the Keith–Zhong Theorem
2.6
, the space supports the
(1,p-\varepsilon)
-Poincaré inequality for some
\varepsilon>0
, and this says that

m_{f_{n}}(x,t)\lesssim t\Big(\fint_{B(x,\lambda t)}(\operatorname{lip}f_{n})^{p-\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\Big)^{\frac{1}{p-\varepsilon}}.

Then, from a computation like (
8.5
), we see that
cM_{p-\varepsilon}(\operatorname{lip}f_{n})
is a Hajłasz upper gradient of
f_{n}
, and hence

\begin{split}\|f_{n}\|_{\dot{M}^{1,p}}&\lesssim\|M_{p-\varepsilon}(\operatorname{lip}f_{n})\|_{L^{p}}\qquad\text{(definition of $\dot{M}^{1,p}$)}\\
&\lesssim\|\operatorname{lip}f_{n}\|_{L^{p}}\qquad\text{(maximal inequality)}\\
&\lesssim\|g_{f_{n}}\|_{L^{p}}\qquad\text{(Proposition \ref{prop:Lip-gf})}\\
&\lesssim\liminf_{\kappa\to 0}\kappa\nu_{p}(\{m_{f_{n}}>\kappa\})^{1/p}\qquad\text{(Lemma \ref{lem:gfp<})}.\end{split}

(9.5)

Now
m_{f_{n}}\leq m_{f}+m_{f-f_{n}}
and hence

\kappa^{p}\nu_{p}(\{m_{f_{n}}>\kappa\})\leq\kappa^{p}\nu_{p}(\{m_{f}>\kappa/2\})+\kappa^{p}\nu_{p}(\{m_{f-f_{n}}>\kappa/2\}),

where

\begin{split}\kappa^{p}\nu_{p}(\{m_{f-f_{n}}>\kappa/2\})&\lesssim\|m_{f-f_{n}}\|_{L^{p,\infty}(\nu_{p})}^{p}\qquad\text{(definition of $L^{p,\infty}$ norm)}\\
&\lesssim\|f-f_{n}\|_{\dot{M}^{1,p}(\mu)}^{p}\qquad\text{(Proposition \ref{prop:MpLowerBd})}.\end{split}

Thus

\liminf_{\kappa\to 0}\kappa\nu_{p}(\{m_{f_{n}}>\kappa\})^{1/p}\lesssim\liminf_{\kappa\to 0}\kappa\nu_{p}(\{m_{f}>\kappa\})^{1/p}+\|f-f_{n}\|_{\dot{M}^{1,p}(\mu)}^{p}.

(9.6)

Combining the estimates, we obtain

\begin{split}\|f\|_{\dot{M}^{1,p}(\mu)}&\leq\|f_{n}\|_{\dot{M}^{1,p}}+\|f-f_{n}\|_{\dot{M}^{1,p}}\qquad\text{(by \eqref{eq:limStart})}\\
&\lesssim\liminf_{\kappa\to 0}\kappa\nu_{p}(\{m_{f_{n}}>\kappa\})^{1/p}+\|f-f_{n}\|_{\dot{M}^{1,p}}\qquad\text{(by \eqref{eq:fnMp<lim})}\\
&\lesssim\liminf_{\kappa\to 0}\kappa\nu_{p}(\{m_{f}>\kappa\})^{1/p}+\|f-f_{n}\|_{\dot{M}^{1,p}}\qquad\text{(by \eqref{eq:mfn<mf+pert})}.\end{split}

Only the last term depends on
n
, so taking the limit
n\to\infty
and noting that the last term vanishes in this limit by the choice of
f_{n}
, we obtain the claimed estimate.
∎

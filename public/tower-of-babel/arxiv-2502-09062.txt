Quantify the stability of Majorana qubits through Rabi beat Preprint: APS/123-QED
Yu Zhang, Jiayi Chen, Jie Liu, X. C. Xie

Abstract
Evaluating the stability of Majorana qubits (MQ) is crucial for the advancement of topological quantum computation. In this work, we propose a method to quantify the stability of MQs through their Rabi oscillations. Our approach involves coupling a fermionic state to the MQ and measuring the resulting Rabi oscillations induced by this coupling. This setup is feasible across a range of experimental platforms. We show that Rabi beats emerge when considering finite-size effects and inhomogeneous potentials. The beating frequency is directly related to the deviations caused by these factors, providing a quantitative method to assess the stability of Majorana qubits. Additionally, we investigate the impact of dissipation on MQ stability. We find that the beating patterns are unaffected by weak dissipation. More intriguingly, dissipation would weaken in an ideal MQ. This suggests that non-local qubits can effectively avoid certain types of decoherence.

In this paper, we propose quantifying the stability of Majorana qubits (MQ) through Rabi oscillations.
Rabi oscillations, a well-established technique for rotating quantum states in traditional quantum computation, have recently been shown to also occur in MQ systems

This suggests that Rabi oscillations could provide a powerful tool for bridging the gap between conventional and topological quantum computation.
Our approach involves attaching a fermionic state to the MQ and measuring the resulting Rabi oscillations induced by the coupling of the fermionic state to the MQ.
In an ideal MQ system, the Rabi frequency would be the same for different entities due to the degeneracy of the ground states.
However, when finite-size effects and inhomogeneous potentials are considered, additional coupling terms are introduced, breaking the degeneracy of the MQs.
As a result, a beating effect emerges due to slight deviations in the Rabi frequency between the different entities. These deviations can be directly measured through the beating patterns, providing a quantitative method to assess the stability of MQ.
We further investigate the influence of quasiparticle poisoning on MQ stability. We find that the beating patterns remain unaffected by weak dissipation.
More interestingly, such dissipation is localized in an ideal MQ, affecting only the nearby terms. This suggests that non-local qubits can indeed avoid certain types of decoherence.

Influence of dissipation in MQ
Although MQ offer a promising pathway toward fault-tolerant quantum computing,
we can still introduce some dissipation terms to evaluate its stability to resist external disruption. Moreover, the quasi-particle poisoning would also arising due to finite temperature.
In the following, we aim to investigate how these decoherence mechanisms influence the beat frequency in MQ systems.

The dissipation term in generally can be described by the Lindblad operator
L_{i}=\gamma_{i}
with strength
\Gamma_{i}

Here ,

We found that the dissipation terms are similar to each other when either

Conclusion.
With the assistance of a QD), we reveal the Rabi oscillations of MQ. We demonstrate that Rabi beats emerge when finite-size effects and inhomogeneous potentials are taken into account.
The beating frequency is directly related to the deviations caused by these factors, providing a quantitative method for assessing the stability of Majorana qubits.
We further investigate the influence of dissipation on MQ stability. We find that the beating frequency remains unaffected by weak dissipation.
More interestingly, dissipation only affects the terms near the QDs in an ideal MQ system. Dissipation far from the QDs is suppressed in an MQ with better stability, suggesting that high-quality MQs can indeed avoid certain patterns of decoherence.

Finally, we address several considerations that are important for realistic systems.
First, Rabi oscillations typically involve instantaneous coupling, which inevitably induces diabetic effects during the coupling process. Therefore, the coupling strength between the quantum dot (QD) and MQ should be smaller than the gap.
Second, since the coupling strength must be smaller than the gap, and given that the superconducting gap is on the order of
meV
, the corresponding oscillation frequency would be on the order of several nanoseconds.
Third, while Rabi oscillations provide a basic method for rotating quantum states, more advanced techniques, such as the Ramsey method, may offer improved performance.

Real-world efficacy of nanoliposomal irinotecan in patients with advanced pancreatic cancer
Authors: Marie Decraecker, Sophie Le Joncour, Valérie Aurillac, Maïté Sangnier, Camille Ducerf, Aude Berroneau, Laetitia Rouleau, David Tougeron, Christophe Laplace, Magali Madau, Camille Dumon, Herve Perrier, Jean-Frédéric Blanc
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundIn the randomized NAPOLI-1 trial, nanoliposomal irinotecan (Nal-Iri) combined with fluorouracil and folinic acid provided considerable survival increase for patients with metastatic pancreatic ductal adenocarcinomas who had received gemcitabine-based therapy. However, patients included in clinical trials often differ from those encountered in practice. A real-world study was required to assess the efficacy and safety of Nal-Iri in patients with poor prognoses for whom quality of life preservation was essential.MethodsWe conducted an observational, retrospective national multicenter phase 4 study of patients with advanced pancreatic cancers treated using Nal-Iri combined with fluorouracil and folinic acid in clinical practice. Overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method. We used a nomogram validated from a concordance index of eight clinicobiological variables to predict OS.ResultsA total of 58 patients with advanced pancreatic adenocarcinomas who received Nal-Iri combined with fluorouracil and folinic acid in three medical centers between March 2020 and September 2022 were included. The median duration of Nal-Iri treatment was 3.6 (range 1.9–8.8) months. The median follow-up time from the start of first-line treatment was 26.2 (16.5–44.8) months. Of all patients, 20.7% received Nal-Iri in a second-line setting, but most (n = 37 patients; 63.8%) received Nal-Iri in a third-line setting. Forty-two patients (72.4%) had previously been treated with irinotecan. The median OS from the time of Nal-Iri initiation was 6.0 (2.6–13.1) months, and the median PFS was 2.1 (1.7–2.6) months. Patients who received Nal-Iri in a second-line setting exhibited significantly better OS than patients who received Nal-Iri in a third-line setting or later (p < 10–3). Patients who had previously received irinotecan exhibited significantly lower OS compared with patients who were irinotecan-naïve (p < 10–3).Our nomogram demonstrated excellent prediction of 6-month and 1-year survivals (p < 10 −3).ConclusionsNal-Iri combined with fluorouracil and folinic acid was particularly effective in second-line settings and irinotecan non-pre-treated patients. The nomogram demonstrated excellent prediction of survival under real-world conditions.Trial registrationNCT06006728, Registration Date: August 23, 2023.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-025-15346-8.

Introduction

The incidence of pancreatic adenocarcinoma has substantially increased over the past 20 years, and the future outlook is concerning [1]. In France, from 2006 to 2012, the incidence doubled from 6,000 to more than 12,000 new cases per year. Recent studies forecast further doubling by 2030 in both the United States and Europe [2–4]. Pancreatic cancer may be the second leading cause of cancer mortality among all cancers by 2030. The therapeutic options are very limited. Currently, only complete surgical resection at an early stage may be followed by complete remission. This has minimal relevance because 80% of pancreatic cancers are diagnosed at an advanced stage [5].

Front-line treatments for unresectable locally advanced or metastatic pancreatic disease include FOLFIRINOX (a combination of oxaliplatin, folinic acid, irinotecan, and fluorouracil) and albumin-bound paclitaxel (nab-paclitaxel) plus gemcitabine [6]. However, most patients had disease progression under first-line treatment, and effective later rescue chemotherapy is required.

In the international randomized phase III NAPOLI-1 trial, nanoliposomal irinotecan (Nal-Iri) with fluorouracil and folinic acid, given after previous gemcitabine-based therapy, improved survival among patients with metastatic pancreatic ductal adenocarcinomas [7] [overall survival (OS) 6.1 (range 4.8–8.9) months for Nanoliposomal irinotecan with fluorouracil and folinic acid vs. 4.2 months (3.3–5.3) for fluorouracil and folinic acid [hazard ratio (HR) 0.67, 95% confidence interval (CI) 0.49–0.92; p = 0.012]]. In 2016, the EMA authorized the use of ONIVYDE® (pegylated liposomal trihydrate irinotecan hydrochloride), also termed Nal-Iri or MM-398, for treatment of metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinomas in adults who progressed after first-line treatment with gemcitabine. This treatment improved progression-free survival (PFS), objective tumor response, time to treatment failure, and CA19-9 status. The recent NAPOLI-03 trial assessed the efficacy of Nal-Iri combined with fluorouracil and oxaliplatin (the Nal-Iri FOX protocol) as first-line treatment (OS 11.1 months vs. 9.2 months with gemcitabine and Nab paclitaxel) (HR: 0.83 [0.70–0.90]), p = 0.04; PFS 7.4 months vs. 5.6 months (HR: 0.69 [0.58–0.83]), p < 0.0001] [8]. This result demonstrated that the first-line chemotherapy triplet NAL-IRIFOX is efficient in advanced pancreatic cancers patients with a good general condition, leading to the approval of this regimen by the EMA on April 25, 2024.

Recently, a meta-analysis of the NAPOLI randomized clinical trials further confirmed the efficacy and safety profile of nal-IRI in patients with metastatic pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma after gemcitabine-based therapy [9]. However, clinical trial populations remain highly selected and may not fully represent patients treated in daily practice, who often present with poorer performance status, higher comorbidity burden, and prior exposure to multiple lines of chemotherapy including conventional irinotecan. Moreover, evidence on cost, resource utilization, and the applicability of prognostic tools in routine practice remains scarce.

A real-world study was conducted involving 67 patients treated in the Taipei Veterans General Hospital of Japan. The safety profile was manageable and treatment was efficacious [median OS 7.9 (5.6–10.1) months, median PFS 2.9 (1.6–4.1) months] [10]. Although advances in pancreatic cancer therapies have improved OS and other outcomes, such patients are often diagnosed late (i.e., at an advanced stage) and have poor prognoses compared with patients who exhibit other types of cancer. Our recent nomogram uses OS prognostic factors from the NAPOLI-1 study and consistently well predicts the OS of different risk groups [11]. The nomogram considers the Karnofsky Performance Score, liver metastasis status, randomized treatment with Nal-Iri combined with fluorouracil and folinic acid, baseline albumin level (g/dL), neutrophil/lymphocyte ratio, baseline CA19-9 level (U/mL), disease stage at diagnosis, and body mass index (BMI) (kg/m2).

Here, we conducted a real-world study of the efficacy, safety, and medico-economic impact of Nal-Iri combined with fluorouracil and folinic acid in patients with advanced pancreatic cancer after previous gemcitabine-based therapy. The study involved three French centers.

Patients and methods

Study design and participants

This was a national, retrospective, observational, non-randomized, open-label, French multicenter phase 4 trial (including two university hospitals—the University Hospital of Bordeaux and the University Hospital of Poitiers—and Saint Joseph’s Hospital). We retrospectively included all patients with metastatic pancreatic cancer treated with Nal-Iri and fluorouracil/folinic acid between March 2020 and September 2022. All patients were followed-up after treatment until death or study completion; the minimum follow-up time was 6 months.

For all patients, we recorded baseline characteristics, including sex, date of birth, and age at initiation of the Nal-Iri protocol, as well as BMI, performance score (PS), and body surface area. Biological parameters measured included the levels of hemoglobin, neutrophils, platelets, and lymphocytes; neutrophil/lymphocyte ratio; total bilirubin level; creatininemia and albuminemia status; the levels of transaminases, alkaline phosphatase (AP), and gamma-glutamyl transferase (GGT); and UGT1A1*28 polymorphism status. We recorded tumor location and histology; disease stage at diagnosis; sites and numbers of metastases (assessed via cross-sectional imaging); and carbohydrate antigen 19–9 (CA19-9) level. We also recorded all treatment courses including previous chemotherapy lines, durations of treatment, the starting dose of Nal-Iri, and any dose adjustment during treatment. The dose intensity was defined as the mean dose adjusted for body surface area throughout the course of treatment.

Human ethics and consent to participate

The study was conducted closely with international guidance including the Good Clinical Practices and the Declaration of Helsinki. According to French national laws, the study was approved by a national Ethics Committee (CPP Est III, IDRCB 2023-A01665-40) and Competent Authority (ANSM) has been informed (NCT06006728, Registration Date: August 23, 2023). The processing and use of data collected as part of this study have been done in accordance with the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR—EU 2016/679) and complies with Reference Methodology" (MR-003) of French Data Protection laws in force. All alive participants have been provided comprehensive information notice regarding the purpose of the study, possible risks and expected benefits of the study, and participant's rights (voluntary participation and freedom to decline participation in the trial) and provided a Consent to Participate declaration.

Procedures

Nal-Iri was administered in combination with fluorouracil and folinic acid every 2 weeks (Nal-Iri 70 mg/m2 for an hour and a half followed by folinic acid 400 mg/m2 over 30 min, and then fluorouracil 2,400 mg/m2 over 46 h). Treatment continued until disease progression (according to Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors [RECIST] version 1.1), death, or toxicity. Patients were included if they were aged ≥ 18 years and had histologically or cytologically confirmed pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma. They might have documented measurable disease. They might have progressed after gemcitabine-based therapy given in a neoadjuvant, adjuvant (but only if distant metastases occurred within 6 months of completing adjuvant therapy), locally advanced, or metastatic setting. They might be members or beneficiaries of a social security scheme.

Outcomes

The primary endpoint was OS. We analyzed PFS and duration of treatment among patients who received Nal-Iri with fluorouracil and folinic acid regimen according to RECIST 1.1. Initial diagnostic staging was based on computed tomography scan (CT-scan) or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). The treatment duration was calculated from the day of Nal-Iri initiation to the day of disease progression, a shift to another treatment regimen, or loss to follow-up or death. We monitored the tumor response using abdominal CT or MRI, as suggested by RECIST version 1.1, and by measuring the CA19-9 levels. OS was the time from initiation of the first-line regimen to the time of initiation of Nal-Iri or to the day of death from any cause. PFS was the time from initiation of the first-line regimen to the initiation of Nal-Iri or to the time of disease progression or death. The follow-up time was the time from the last available evaluation to initiation of the first-line regimen or from the time of Nal-Iri initiation to the time of the last follow-up. For patients with no death and no disease progression, survival was censored at the date of the last follow-up.

The secondary endpoints were the identification of prognosis factors of OS, the medico-economic impact and the safety.

We previously constructed a nomogram predicting OS at 6 and 12 months. Each clinical variable of the nomogram was evaluated for each patient and scored. The total individual scores were determined by summing the scores for all variables. The total scores were used to predict OS probabilities at 6 and 12 months and to compare with observed OS. We compared patients who had and had not previously received irinotecan. We also compared patients according to the Nal-Iri line setting (second- vs. third or later-line). All adverse effects were defined using the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events, version 5.0.

Statistical analysis

In terms of population characteristics, the data were not normally distributed, and the results are thus presented as medians (interquartile ranges) for quantitative data and as percentages for qualitative data. Quantitative variables were compared using the Kruskal–Wallis test. Qualitative variables were compared using Fisher’s exact test as appropriate. The significance threshold was set to 5%. Survivals were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method; the log-rank test was used to compare survival curves. Univariate and multivariate survival analyses were performed using Cox models. Variables with p-values < 0.20 on univariate analysis were entered into multivariate analysis. The prognostic utility of the previously validated nomogram was calculated for each patient and evaluated by calculating Spearman correlation coefficients for OS. We used SAS software (version 9.4; SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA) for all analyses.

Results

Patient characteristics

Between March 2020 and September 2022, a total of 58 patients with advanced pancreatic adenocarcinomas who received Nal-Iri with fluorouracil and folinic acid were enrolled in three centers (n = 30 from Bordeaux, n = 15 from Poitiers, and n = 13 from Marseille). Among all patients, 38.6% were women; the median patient age was 62 (range 57–71) years. The median albumin level was 37.1 (34.4–40) g/L; two patients (3.5%) had performance scores (PSs) of 2 and four patients (6.9%) had PSs of 3. Of all patients, 20.7% had locally advanced disease when they received Nal-Iri, and 56.9% had liver metastases. The median CA19-9 level was 2,286 (186–10000) UI/mL. Two patients received Nal-Iri in the first-line setting (as dictated by clinical trials); most patients received Nal-Iri in a third-line setting (n = 37 patients; 63.8%). Forty-two patients (72.4%) had previously been treated with irinotecan; 46 (79.3%) had received a fluorouracil-based regimen before Nal-Iri, and 56 (96.5%) had received a gemcitabine-based regimen before Nal-Iri. The median number of drug cycles was 5 (3–11). Follow-up and survival

Of all patients, 89.7% were died at the end of follow-up; 88.5% due to pancreatic adenocarcinoma, 9.6% because of infection, and one death was related to chemotherapy toxicity (Table 2). The median follow-up time from Nal-Iri initiation was 6.0 (2.6–13.1) months, and the total follow-up time from the start of first-line treatment was 26.2 (16.5–44.8) months. The median OS from unresectable disease was 26.2 (16.5–44.8) months, and the OS from the time of Nal-Iri initiation was 6.0 (2.6–13.1) months. The median PFS from unresectable disease was 20.4 (11.5–39.6) months, and the median PFS from the time of Nal-Iri initiation was 2.1 (1.7–2.6) months. Eight patients (13.8%) were continuing to receive Nal-Iri at the end of follow-up (October 2023). Patients stopped treatment either because of disease progression (80.4%) or Nal-Iri unacceptable toxicity (8 patients, 15.7%, with one death). Two patients experimented an objective response (3.4%), and 5 were stable under Nal-Iri (8.6%).

Patients who received Nal-Iri in a second-line setting exhibited significantly better OS than those who received Nal-Iri in a third- or later-line setting (median OS 14.9 (5.5–21.1) months vs. 4.9 (2.2–7.9) months, respectively; HR: 2.859 [1.323–6.178]; p < 5.3.10–3 (Fig. 1). Prognosis

Seven factors potentially predictive of OS were identified on univariate analysis: hemoglobin level (HR 0.846; p = 0.05), older age (HR 0.974; p = 0.09), previous irinotecan-based regimen (HR 2.498; p = 8.1.10–3), CA19-9 level (≥ 500 vs. < 500 UI/mL) (HR 2.241; p = 0.02), platelet level (HR 1.001; p = 0.20), presence of liver metastases (HR 0.637; p = 0.12), and number of metastatic sites (HR 1.294; p = 0.14) (Table 3). We used our nomogram to predict the OS for each patient with advanced pancreatic cancer who received Nal-Iri combined with fluorouracil and folinic acid (Supplementary Table 1). The HR for a 6-month median OS probability, as revealed by the nomogram, was 0.26 (0.08–0.40) for the overall population; the HR for a 1-year median OS probability was 0.04 (0.003–0.11). The nomogram demonstrated excellent prediction of survival with Spearman correlation coefficients of 0.35 for the 6-month median OS probability and 0.38 for the 1-year median OS probability (p = 7 × 10–3, p = 3.4 × 10–3 above).

Discussion

Recent therapeutic advances in pancreatic cancer have only modestly improved prognosis, with most patients still diagnosed at an advanced stage. After failure of first-line therapy, the optimal regimen remains uncertain and varies between centers. The NAPOLI-1 randomized trial established nanoliposomal irinotecan (nal-IRI) plus 5-FU/LV as an effective option after gemcitabine, with manageable toxicity. A recently published meta-analysis of the NAPOLI program confirmed consistent survival benefit across diverse subgroups, further reinforcing its role as a standard second-line therapy [9].

In our real-world French cohort, outcomes were slightly inferior to NAPOLI-1 (median OS 5.0 months vs. 6.1 months; median PFS 2.1 vs. 3.1 months), largely explained by later use of nal-IRI (75% in ≥ 3rd line vs. 34% in NAPOLI-1) and inclusion of more heterogeneous patients, including 20% with locally advanced disease. Prognostic factors such as performance status, albumin, CA19-9, and absence of liver metastases were associated with better outcomes, consistent with prior reports. Together, these findings support early use of nal-IRI (ideally in second line after gemcitabine), particularly in patients with favorable prognostic features.

Sequencing irinotecan and nal-IRI remains a key clinical question. In NAPOLI-1, benefit was restricted to irinotecan-naïve patients, and our own results similarly showed significantly shorter OS in those previously exposed to irinotecan. However, a recent large EHR-based real-world study of 675 patients reported that after multivariable adjustment, prior irinotecan exposure was not an independent predictor of OS or PFS in those subsequently treated with nal-IRI [12]. These divergent findings likely reflect differences in timing, disease biology, and reasons for prior irinotecan discontinuation (progression vs. adjuvant completion). Taken together, while efficacy appears attenuated in heavily pretreated patients, nal-IRI may still be a reasonable option even after prior irinotecan, especially if exposure was remote or in a different clinical context.

Adverse events in our study were consistent with those described in NAPOLI-1 and other real-world cohorts, mainly hematologic (neutropenia, anemia, thrombocytopenia), gastrointestinal (diarrhea, nausea, vomiting), and asthenia. Five patients carried the UGT1A1*28/*28 genotype; only two required initial dose reductions, though other patients required adjustments later in treatment. Dose modifications and supportive care generally allowed treatment continuation. One patient experienced treatment-related death, underscoring the frailty of this population. The similarity of toxicity patterns across studies confirms that careful patient selection, systematic UGT1A1 testing, and early dose adjustments are essential for safe administration.

In our centers, the median cost per nal-IRI treatment exceeded standard reimbursement, highlighting its financial impact. While our analysis was limited to direct hospital and drug costs, broader cost-effectiveness evaluations integrating quality of life and long-term outcomes are warranted. This is particularly relevant in the evolving therapeutic landscape of pancreatic cancer, where novel agents are under investigation. Future prospective studies should incorporate health economics and patient-reported outcomes to better define the value of nal-IRI in routine practice.

In summary, this French multicenter real-world study confirms the efficacy and tolerability of nal-IRI in advanced pancreatic adenocarcinoma. The benefit is greatest when used early (second line after gemcitabine, in irinotecan-naïve patients), though select patients may still benefit after prior irinotecan. The safety profile is manageable with appropriate monitoring, and economic considerations highlight the need for judicious use. Taking together, our findings support nal-IRI as an important therapeutic option in the continuum of care for metastatic pancreatic cancer.

Local Factorization of p p -adic Gamma Sums
Samuel Reid

Abstract
We revisit the proposed equality between discrete Fourier transforms of p p -adic Γ p \Gamma_{p} –values and p p -adic L L –derivatives for odd characters modulo a prime p p . The clean identity is false in general. Building on Coleman reciprocity and the Gross–Koblitz formula, we prove an exact two-term decomposition: for each odd, nontrivial Dirichlet character χ ( mod p ) \chi\pmod{p} , Φ p ​ ( χ ) := ∑ a = 1 p − 1 χ ⁡ ( a ) ​ log p ​ Γ p ​ ( a p − 1 ) = U 1 , p ​ L p ′ ​ ( 0 , χ ) + U 2 , p ​ L ​ ( 0 , χ ) , \Phi_{p}(\chi):=\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}\chi(a)\,\log_{p}\Gamma_{p}\!\left(\frac{a}{p-1}\right)=U_{1,p}\,L^{\prime}_{p}(0,\chi)\;+\;U_{2,p}\,L(0,\chi), with constants U 1 , p ∈ ℚ p ​ ( μ p − 1 ) × U_{1,p}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\mu_{p-1})^{\times} and U 2 , p ∈ ℚ p ​ ( μ p − 1 ) U_{2,p}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\mu_{p-1}) depending only on p p and the fixed branch of log p \log_{p} , but independent of χ \chi . Subtracting the L ⁡ ( 0 , χ ) L(0,\chi) –block yields a renormalized local input Φ p ren ​ ( χ ) := Φ p ​ ( χ ) − U 2 , p ​ L ​ ( 0 , χ ) = U 1 , p ​ L p ′ ​ ( 0 , χ ) , \Phi^{\mathrm{ren}}_{p}(\chi):=\Phi_{p}(\chi)-U_{2,p}L(0,\chi)=U_{1,p}\,L^{\prime}_{p}(0,\chi), uniformly in odd, nontrivial χ \chi . Plumbing these renormalized locals at every finite place into the Weil explicit formula (with the standard Li kernel at ∞ \infty ) reproduces exactly the classical Li coefficients. We also record a short, reproducible verification protocol; a tiny table for p = 5 , 7 p=5,7 illustrates the χ \chi –independence of ( U 1 , p , U 2 , p ) (U_{1,p},U_{2,p}) .

Introduction

Let
p
be an odd prime. We fix the Iwasawa logarithm
\log_{p}
with
\log_{p}(p)=0
and

\log_{p}(\zeta)=0
for roots of unity
\zeta
of order prime to
p
, and Morita’s
p
-adic gamma function

\Gamma_{p}:\mathbb{Z}_{p}\to\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
. Denote by
\omega:(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\to\mu_{p-1}
the
Teichmüller character and by
\tau(\omega^{-a})
the Gauss sum.
A natural identity conjecturing that the multiplicative discrete Fourier transform

\Phi_{p}(\chi):=\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}\chi(a)\,\log_{p}\Gamma_{p}\!\left(\frac{a}{p-1}\right)

is (up to a fixed scalar)
\tfrac{1}{p}L^{\prime}_{p}(0,\chi)
for every odd
\chi\pmod{p}
turns out to be false in
general. We identify the exact obstruction and show that it is arithmetic, structured, and uniformly
removable by a fixed renormalization. Our main results give the precise two-term law and its
renormalized corollary, and then show that the global coefficients obtained by inserting the
renormalized finite locals into the explicit formula coincide with the classical Li coefficients.

What is new.

The paper isolates (i) a clean
local
two-term decomposition with a
\chi
–independent
pair
(U_{1,p},U_{2,p})
and (ii) a
global
consequence: after removing the
L(0,\chi)
–block locally,
the explicit formula recovers the usual Li sequence unchanged.

On normalizations.

We use the Gross–Koblitz identification of
\Gamma_{p}
and Gauss sums, and
the Ferrero–Greenberg/Coleman description of
L^{\prime}_{p}(0,\chi)
via cyclotomic regulators.
Section
2
fixes conventions and cites the precise reciprocity/normalization
statements used later (see Lemma
3.1
and (
3
)), along the lines
of Washington, Ch. 12, and Coleman (Invent. Math. 1982).

Scope.

Throughout, unless explicitly stated,
characters are odd and nontrivial
. We briefly comment on
even characters and on
p=2
in §
6
. No claims are made beyond this setup.

Notation and normalizations

We work over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}\subset\mathbb{C}_{p}
. Extend
\log_{p}:1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}\to p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
uniquely to
\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\mu_{p^{\infty}})^{\times}

by writing
x=\omega(x)\langle x\rangle
with
\omega(x)\in\mu_{p-1}
and
\langle x\rangle\in 1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, and declaring

\log_{p}(\omega(x))=0
,
\log_{p}(p)=0
.
Morita’s
\Gamma_{p}
is characterized by
\Gamma_{p}(0)=1
and

\Gamma_{p}(x+1)/\Gamma_{p}(x)=-x
for
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
, extended continuously.

Gross–Koblitz and Coleman reciprocity

For
1\leq a\leq p-1
the Gross–Koblitz formula gives

\tau(\omega^{-a})=-\pi_{p}^{\,a}\,\Gamma_{p}\!\left(\frac{a}{p-1}\right),\qquad\pi_{p}^{\,p-1}=-p,\quad\log_{p}(\pi_{p})=0,

(1)

hence
\log_{p}\Gamma_{p}\!\big(\tfrac{a}{p-1}\big)=\log_{p}\tau(\omega^{-a})
under our normalization.

Coleman’s reciprocity identifies cyclotomic-unit regulators with Gauss-sum regulators (in our
normalization):

\log_{p}\!\Big(\frac{1-\zeta_{p}^{\,a}}{1-\zeta_{p}}\Big)=\frac{1}{1-p}\,\big(v(a)-a\,v(1)\big),\quad v(a):=\log_{p}\tau(\omega^{-a})\quad(1\leq a\leq p-1).

(2)

Equation (
2
) is a standard consequence of Coleman’s
p
-adic polylogarithm and Gross–Koblitz
(see Washington,
Cyclotomic Fields
, 2nd ed., Ch. 12; Coleman, Invent. Math.
69
(1982),
§2). For completeness: combining (
1
) with the linear relation between
(\log_{p}\tau(\omega^{-a}))_{a}

and
(\log_{p}(1-\zeta_{p}^{\,a}))_{a}
in Coleman’s reciprocity yields (
2
) directly.

3
Local discrepancy and the two-term decomposition

Set
v(a):=\log_{p}\tau(\omega^{-a})
and
w(a):=-\log_{p}(1-\zeta_{p}^{\,a})
. Let
L_{a}:=\log_{p}(1-\zeta_{p}^{\,a})

and
L_{1}:=\log_{p}(1-\zeta_{p})
.

Lemma 3.1

(Coleman reciprocity, rephrased)
.

With the above normalizations, for
1\leq a\leq p-1
one has

\log_{p}\!\Big(\frac{1-\zeta_{p}^{\,a}}{1-\zeta_{p}}\Big)=\frac{1}{1-p}\,\big(v(a)-a\,v(1)\big).

(5)

Proposition 3.2

(Discrepancy formula)
.

For
1\leq a\leq p-1
,

v(a)=a\,v(1)+(1-p)\,\big(L_{a}-L_{1}\big).

(6)

Equivalently,
\delta(a):=v(a)-w(a)=a\,v(1)+(2-p)L_{a}+(p-1)L_{1}
.

Proof.

Equation (
6
) is (
5
) rearranged; adding
L_{a}
yields the alternative form.
∎

Theorem 3.3

(Two-term decomposition)
.

Let
\chi\pmod{p}
be
odd and nontrivial
. Then

\Phi_{p}(\chi):=\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}\chi(a)\,\log_{p}\Gamma_{p}\!\left(\frac{a}{p-1}\right)=\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}\chi(a)\,v(a)=U_{1,p}\,L^{\prime}_{p}(0,\chi)+U_{2,p}\,L(0,\chi),

(7)

where the constants

U_{1,p}=-(1-p)\,C_{p}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\mu_{p-1})^{\times},\qquad U_{2,p}=-p\,v(1)\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\mu_{p-1})

(8)

depend only on
p
and the branch of
\log_{p}
, and are independent of
\chi
.

Proof.

By (
1
),
\log_{p}\Gamma_{p}(a/(p-1))=v(a)
. Summing (
6
) against
\chi
gives

\Phi_{p}(\chi)=v(1)\sum_{a}a\,\chi(a)+(1-p)\sum_{a}\chi(a)\,\big(L_{a}-L_{1}\big).

For nontrivial
\chi
we have
\sum_{a}\chi(a)=0
, so the
L_{1}
term vanishes. Using (
4
) and
(
3
) gives

\Phi_{p}(\chi)=v(1)\cdot\big(-p\,L(0,\chi)\big)+(1-p)\cdot\big(-C_{p}\,L^{\prime}_{p}(0,\chi)\big),

which is (
7
) with (
8
).
∎

Corollary 3.4

(Uniform renormalized identity)
.

For odd, nontrivial
\chi\pmod{p}
define the renormalized transform

\Phi_{p}^{\mathrm{ren}}(\chi):=\Phi_{p}(\chi)-U_{2,p}\,L(0,\chi).

Then

\Phi_{p}^{\mathrm{ren}}(\chi)=U_{1,p}\,L^{\prime}_{p}(0,\chi),

(9)

with
U_{1,p}
independent of
\chi
.

Remark 3.5

(Character hypotheses)
.

Oddness and nontriviality are used at two points: to eliminate the
L_{1}
term via
\sum_{a}\chi(a)=0
,
and to invoke (
3
). The trivial character is excluded; even characters are discussed
in §

.

4
Global re–plumbing and Li coefficients

Let
\Xi(s)
be the completed Riemann zeta function. For a suitable test function
\varphi
, the Weil
explicit formula reads schematically

\sum_{\rho}\widehat{\varphi}(\rho)=A_{\infty}(\varphi)+\sum_{p}A_{p}(\varphi),

(10)

where the left-hand side sums over the nontrivial zeros
\rho
.
We
fix
the archimedean test to be
Li’s kernel

\varphi_{n}
as in Li
[
4
, §2]
; with this choice,
the left-hand side equals the
n
th Li coefficient
\lambda_{n}
.

On the finite side, after character decomposition, the classical plumbing produces a factor proportional to

L^{\prime}_{p}(0,\chi)
on the odd block. In our setting we
replace
the classical local odd input by

\Phi^{\mathrm{ren}}_{p}(\chi)
from (
9
), which differs by the constant unit
U_{1,p}
.

Theorem 4.1

(Renormalized coincidence of Li coefficients)
.

Let
\lambda_{n}^{\mathrm{ren}}
denote the global coefficients obtained by inserting
\Phi^{\mathrm{ren}}_{p}(\chi)
at each finite
place (odd block) and the fixed Li kernel
\varphi_{n}
at
\infty
in (
10
). Then, for all
n\geq 1
,

\lambda_{n}^{\mathrm{ren}}=\lambda_{n}.

Proof.

Linearity of the explicit formula implies that replacing
L^{\prime}_{p}(0,\chi)
by
U_{1,p}^{-1}\Phi^{\mathrm{ren}}_{p}(\chi)
rescales
each finite local term by
U_{1,p}^{-1}
and back by
U_{1,p}
, making no net change; the
archimedean side is untouched. Hence the coefficients agree.
∎

Remark 4.2

(On the
L(0,\chi)
–block)
.

Starting from
\Phi_{p}(\chi)
instead of
\Phi^{\mathrm{ren}}_{p}(\chi)
would produce an additional global block
from
U_{2,p}L(0,\chi)
. Since
L(0,\chi)
is algebraic and tied to the trivial zero structure, its
contribution can be separated and either vanishes in the standard normalization or is removed once
and for all by our renormalization; we state the clean renormalized form.

6
Scope, even characters, and the case
p=2

Our main statements are for
odd, nontrivial
characters modulo an odd prime
p
.
A parallel two-term phenomenon holds on the even block if one replaces
L^{\prime}_{p}(0,\chi)
by
L_{p}(0,\chi)

and uses the symmetrized cyclotomic kernel; the details mirror the odd case and are omitted here.
For
p=2
one must fix the standard
2
-adic branch and adapt the principal-unit domain; no new
ideas are required, but we do not treat
p=2
in this note.

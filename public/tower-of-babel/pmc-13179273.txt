A proposed new virus in the genus Marafivirus detected from Oriental persimmon ‘Kumemaru’
Authors: Naoko Fujita, Spyros Lytras, Yuka Hagiwara-Komoda
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractA novel virus belonging to the genus Marafivirus was identified from the male Oriental persimmon (Diospyros kaki Thunb.) cultivar ‘Kumemaru’. The complete genome of persimmon marafivirus (PerMaV) is 6,397 nucleotides in length and encodes a single large polyprotein containing conserved domains typical of viruses in the genus Marafivirus. Phylogenetic and sequence identity analyses indicate that PerMaV represents a new species in the family Tymoviridae.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00705-026-06641-1.

A virus-like sequence was identified from an Oriental persimmon (Diospyros kaki Thunb.) ‘Kumemaru’, a rare male cultivar selected from a chance seedling for the establishment of stable pollinizers in Toyama prefecture, Japan. The sequence was discovered during a transcriptome analysis targeting male-promoting factors, based on the RNA-seq dataset (BioProject PRJDB9564; Runs DRR233540–DRR233569) and analyzed using k-mer cataloging as described previously [1]. De novo assembly of 1,428 Kumemaru-specific 35-bp k-mers (KSKs) yielded 1,446 contigs, one of which corresponded to the complete genome of a novel marafivirus, tentatively named persimmon marafivirus (PerMaV). Based on the 1,428 KSKs across the 6,397 nt genome, the estimated theoretical coverage was approximately 7.8× per nucleotide (upper bound), while the effective average depth was ~0.3–1× per nt after read-length correction. The PerMaV genome (6,397 nt, excluding the poly(A) tail) exhibited a high cytosine content (38.5%), consistent with other tymovirids.

The genome encodes a single large open reading frame (6,286 nt long) coding for a polyprotein that contains conserved domains typical of marafiviruses, including methyltransferase, tymovirus endopeptidase, RNA helicase, RNA-dependent RNA polymerase (RdRp), and coat protein. A conserved 16-nt “marafibox” motif implicated in subgenomic RNA synthesis was identified and was identical to that of Bermuda grass etched-line virus (BELV), blackberry virus S (BlVS), grapevine Syrah virus (GSyV-1), maize rayado fino virus (MRFV), nectarine marafivirus M (NeVM), oat blue dwarf virus (OBDV) [2–7] (Fig. 1A). Pairwise sequence identity analysis based on RdRp amino acid sequences revealed 69.1–74.4% identity, which is far below ICTV species demarcation thresholds [8]. To investigate the relatedness of PerMaV to other known marafiviruses, we constructed phylogenies based on both the amino acid and coding nucleotide sequence of the RdRp gene for all publicly available members of the family Tymoviridae. In the amino acid phylogeny, PerMaV clusters with a clade of monocot-infecting marafiviruses, including Sorghum almum marafivirus (SoAMV), with moderate node support (SH-aLRT: 94.5, UFbootstrap: 73) (Fig. 1B). In the nucleotide phylogeny, PerMaV has a different but substantially more confident placement (SH-aLRT: 96.2, UFbootstrap: 91), pairing up with a clade of dicot-infecting marafiviruses, including: OBDV, grapevine asteroid mosaic-associated virus (GAMaV), nectarine marafivirus M (NeVM), citrus sudden death-associated virus (CSDaV) and BlVS (Fig. 1C). Additionally, all monocot-infecting marafiviruses cluster in a single clade in the nucleotide phylogeny (with the exception of Davidia involucrata marafivirus 2 being placed within the clade), supporting the more parsimonious scenario of a single transmission of marafiviruses from dicot to monocot hosts. Sampling more diversity within the Marafivirus genus can help confidently delineate the exact phylogenetic placement of PerMaV. Despite the present phylogenetic uncertainty, PerMaV’s genetic distance to its known relatives indicates that it should represent a novel marafivirus species. The complete genome sequence of PerMaV has been deposited in the NCBI database under accession number PX251123.

To confirm the infectivity of PerMaV full-length genome sequence obtained by assembling RNA-seq reads, we generated the cDNA clone and analyzed its infectivity. The full-length PerMaV cDNA containing a T7 promoter sequence at its 5’ end was produced, and PerMaV RNA was transcribed in vitro as described in Supplementary Information S1. Three persimmon seedlings were mechanically inoculated with the PerMaV RNA. We purified total RNAs from the inoculated and non-inoculated upper leaves at four weeks post-inoculation and analyzed them for the presence of PerMaV. Reverse transcription-polymerase chain reaction analysis using PerMaV specific primers (Supplementary Information) revealed the infection by the virus in the inoculated leaves of all three seedlings (Fig. S1). In addition, the virus-specific band was also detected in the upper leaf sample of #3 seedling, implying that systemic infection of PerMaV occurred in this persimmon seedling. These results strongly suggest that the sequence shown in this study represents the full-length infectious genome of PerMaV.

This novel virus, discovered through transcriptome analysis using the Oriental persimmon ‘Kumemaru’ buds, seems to infect latently since the infected persimmon trees show no obvious symptoms. To elucidate the tissue tropism of PerMaV, we conducted in situ hybridization using an RNA probe targeting the coat protein (CP) sequence (details of methods are in Supplementary Information). We detected strong green fluorescence signals in young leaves surrounding the shoot apical meristem and weaker but discernible signals in the floral meristem (Fig. S2). Further research is required to characterize the host range and geographic distribution of PerMaV.

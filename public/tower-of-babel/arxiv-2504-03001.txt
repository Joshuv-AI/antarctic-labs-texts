Autonomy Architectures for Safe Planning in Unknown Environments Under Budget Constraints
Daniel M. Cherenson, Devansh R. Agrawal, Dimitra Panagou

Abstract
Mission planning can often be formulated as a constrained control problem under multiple path constraints (i.e., safety constraints) and budget constraints (i.e., resource expenditure constraints). In a priori unknown environments, verifying that an offline solution will satisfy the constraints for all time can be difficult, if not impossible. Our contributions are as follows: 1) We propose an online method, building on our previous work gatekeeper , to guarantee safety and satisfy budget constraints of the system trajectory at all times throughout a mission. 2) Next, we prove that our algorithm is recursively feasible and correct. 3) Finally, instead of using a heuristically designed backup controller, we propose a sampling-based method to construct backup trajectories that both minimize resource expenditure and reach budget renewal sets, in which path constraints are satisfied and the constrained resources are renewed. We demonstrate our approach in simulation with a fixed-wing UAV in a GNSS-denied environment with a budget constraint on localization error that can be renewed at visual landmarks. [ Code ]



I
Introduction

Robotic and autonomous systems often operate with limited knowledge of their environment and limited sensing capabilities, which makes designing missions with strict safety requirements challenging. In addition, these systems have a limited budget on resources that are expended throughout a mission. In this paper, we treat safety requirements in the form of a set of states in which the robot must always remain, called the safe set. Specifically, we consider the case where the safe set is not fully known
a priori
and must be built online with information from the sensor measurements. Online methods require guarantees on recursive feasibility to remain safe if planning methods fail to find a trajectory.

While full trajectory optimization methods exist to handle constrained problems
[
1
]
, they are often non convex and computationally intractable to solve online. Path planning techniques
[
2
]
can generate trajectories quickly based on approximated dynamics, but the solutions may not be dynamically realizable and safety guarantees cannot be guaranteed.

Constraints on finite resources have been studied under various names, including integral constraints
[
3
,
4
]
, budget constraints
[
5
,
6
]
, and cost constraints
[
7
,
8
]
. Examples include constraints on battery charge
[
9
]
, localization error
[
10
]
, or time spent visible to an enemy observer
[
11
]
. We consider a generalized case of the budget-constrained planning problem where resources can be renewed at specific regions in the state space.

Various methods to enforce budget constraints have been designed in previous works, ranging from solutions for specific systems and types of missions to general solutions solved offline. An example of a specific application of localization error constrained planning for UAVs is in
[
8
]
, in which a greedy multi-objective motion planning strategy is used to navigate with little knowledge of the environment, making use of cellular signals to localize the robot.

In
[
12
]
, a control barrier function (CBF) is used to enforce battery charge constraints, but it assumes a simple, differentiable battery model and is limited to the class of systems with single-integrator dynamics. The methods in
[
5
,
3
]
solve full optimal control problems with budget constraints and resets in discrete and continuous state spaces, but these approaches rely on large-scale graph searches or solving viscosity equations derived from dynamic programming, which are computationally expensive and better suited for offline planning in known environments.

We note that none of these solutions explicitly handle the combination of general nonlinear dynamics, limited environmental knowledge, and non-trivial budget expenditure dynamics, all while guaranteeing safety. In addition, some of these methods involve mission- and constraint-specific design of the planning modules, which reduces modularity and adaptability of the system to additional types of constraints and missions. To that end, we aim to build a layered control architecture with modular components
[
13
]
.

Our proposed solution to the problem of ensuring safety and budget constraint guarantees for nonlinear systems in unknown environments builds on our previous work (
gatekeeper

[
14
]
) to incorporate budget constraints and provides a method for constructing backup trajectories.
gatekeeper
is a modular framework that ensures safety by recursively constructing a candidate trajectory consisting of a nominal trajectory, followed up to some switching time, and a backup trajectory that guarantees constraint satisfaction thereafter. Recently,
gatekeeper
has been applied in the
eware
filter which is designed specifically for battery life constraints, where energy is renewed at a charging station
[
9
,
15
]
. Our solution further generalizes
gatekeeper
to enforce a budget constraint on a resource which is expended during the mission and can be renewed in designated regions.

In summary, this paper has three main contributions:

•

We propose an architecture to guarantee safety and satisfy budget constraints while following a nominal trajectory in unknown environments, based on
gatekeeper
.

•

We prove recursive feasibility and correctness, ensuring that system trajectories satisfy all constraints at all times.

•

We propose a sampling-based method called
ReRoot
to construct a backup policy that minimizes resource expenditure while ensuring the system reaches budget renewal sets, sets where safety constraints hold and resources are replenished.

\displaystyle\dot{x}=f(x,u)

We build a graph
\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
with a set of nodes
\mathcal{V}\subset\mathcal{X}
and a set of edges
\mathcal{E}\subset\mathcal{V}\times\mathcal{V}
, in a similar manner to the standard RRT* algorithm
[
17
]
. The key differences between
ReRoot
and RRT* are as follows:

•

The graph
\mathcal{G}
is a rooted forest, i.e., a union of disjoint rooted trees, where the roots are in the renewal set
\mathcal{R}_{k}
. Furthermore, in the rewiring step, it is possible for a node (and its children) to switch to a different tree with a different root.

•

We grow the forest in reverse such that the roots are backup “goal” states in
\mathcal{R}_{k}
and the tree extends to cover
\mathcal{F}_{k}
, which results in optimal paths from all states in
\mathcal{F}_{k}
that end in
\mathcal{R}_{k}
. In RRT* the initial state is the root and the tree is grown until it reaches the goal state.

•

Instead of minimizing path length, the paths minimize the budget state at the end of the backup trajectory
b^{\textup{bck}}
. Since the budget dynamics only depend on the state
x
and control
u
, the cost of the path from a node to the root is the sum of the incremental costs along the path.

Despite these differences in the interpretation of the nodes and paths, the core RRT* algorithm is unchanged and provides asymptotic optimality of the cost of all paths as
|\mathcal{V}|\to\infty
.

Next, we describe the process of extracting a backup trajectory from the
ReRoot
graph.

III-B
3
Backup Trajectory Generation

Denote
d:\mathcal{V}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
as the depth of a node, which is the number of edges between
v
and the root of its tree. A root node has depth
d=0
.
For
v\in\mathcal{V}
, define

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}(v)=\{v,\mathcal{P}(v),\mathcal{P}^{2}(v),\dotsc,\mathcal{P}^{d}(v)\}

(23)

as the set of waypoints from
v
to the root of its tree, where
\mathcal{P}:\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{V}
is the parent node of
v
.

Then, for each
v\in\mathcal{V}
, let
p_{v}
be the closed loop trajectory from tracking the waypoints
\mathcal{W}(v)
:

\displaystyle\dot{p}_{v}=f(p_{v},\pi^{T}(\mathcal{W}(v)),~p_{v}(t_{kS})=p^{\textup{can},T_{S}}_{k}(t_{kS}).

(24)

Additionally, let
b_{v}
be the budget state expenditure over the trajectory
p_{v}
by solving (
3
).

Finally, the trajectory with minimal
b_{v}
that is safe for all time is chosen as the backup trajectory:

\displaystyle p^{\textup{bck},T_{S}}_{k}=\argmin_{p_{v}}\{b_{v}~|~p_{v}(t)\in\mathcal{F}_{k}~\forall t\geq t_{kS}\}.

(25)

Note that by construction,
p^{\textup{bck},T_{S}}_{k}
is dynamically feasible and guaranteed to remain in the known safe set
\mathcal{F}_{k}
. The resulting candidate trajectory
p^{\textup{can},T_{S}}_{k}
is the solution to (
11
). The validity of the candidate trajectory is given from
Definition

6
, i.e., checking that
b(t)\leq B
by forward propagating (
3
).

III-C

Summary

In summary, we have provided a solution to
1
with a layered architecture built on the
gatekeeper
trajectory filter and the
ReRoot
backup policy. To guarantee that safety and budget constraints are satisfied for all time as the nominal trajectory is tracked, a backup trajectory is planned and stitched with the largest portion of the nominal trajectory such that the resulting committed trajectory satisfies all constraints and reaches a budget renewal set. We then provided a method for constructing backup trajectories called
ReRoot
that minimizes the expenditure of the budget state to allow the nominal trajectory to “consume” more of the budget.
ReRoot
is a reverse rooted forest in which branches represent paths of minimal budget cost that start from nodes in the known safe space and reach a budget renewal set.

IV-A

Simulation Setup

We evaluate our method in a simulation case study of a fixed-wing UAV flying in a GNSS-denied environment, using visual-inertial odometry to estimate its position. The horizontal planar dynamics are modeled as a Dubins vehicle:

\displaystyle\dot{r}_{N}

\displaystyle=V\cos\psi

(26a)

\displaystyle\dot{r}_{E}

\displaystyle=V\sin\psi

(26b)

\displaystyle\dot{\psi}

\displaystyle=u

(26c)

where
[r_{N},r_{E}]^{\intercal}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
is the horizontal position in a North-East-Down (NED) coordinate frame,
\psi\in[0,2\pi)
is the heading,
V>0
is a fixed velocity and
u
is the input turn rate.
u
is constrained by the minimum turn radius of the UAV,
\rho>0
, such that
u\in\mathcal{U}=[-\frac{V}{\rho},\frac{V}{\rho}]
. For simplicity and visualization purposes, we constrain the UAV to fly at a fixed altitude, but our method extends trivially to 3D trajectories, or for more complicated dynamics.

The UAV’s velocity is
V=10
m/s and minimum turn radius is
\rho=10
m, which corresponds to a
\sim
45° bank angle. The sensor
FOV
(
FOV
) is defined by a radius of 60 m and an angle of 90°. The perception and mapping module runs at 5 Hz.

The objective to reach the randomly chosen goal location within the 250 m
\times
200 m mission domain. The mission requirements are as follows:

•

(Safety constraint)
Maintain at least
N_{f}=8
visual features in the
FOV
between successive camera frames for robust feature tracking.

•

(Budget constraint)
Maintain an absolute position error (error bound on position) below
B=9
m.

Note that the safety constraint is difficult to write in equation form, e.g.,
h(x)\leq 0
, and is not differentiable. It is however easy to verify algorithmically.
We assume there exist some visual landmarks that reset position error to zero when the UAV can see them. We assume that the position error is proportional to distance traveled, with an error rate of 3%, i.e, 0.03 m of error per 1 m traveled, based on
[
18

.

Translating these requirements b into the format needed for our method, we first write the safe set as

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}=\{x\in\mathcal{X}~|~\textup{FeaturesInFoV}(x)\geq N_{f}\textup{ or }x\in\mathcal{R}\},

(27)

where
\textup{FeaturesInFoV}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{N}
is the number of visible features at state
x
. We assume no prior knowledge of the feature locations. The UAV maps the features as it flies through the environment and builds
\mathcal{F}_{k}
. The budget state models the growth of localization error as

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dot{b}=0.03V,\quad&x\notin\mathcal{R}\\
b^{+}=b_{\textup{reset}}=0,\quad&x\in\mathcal{R}\end{cases}

(28)

with initial condition

\displaystyle b(t_{0})=\begin{cases}b_{0},\quad&x\notin\mathcal{R}\\
0,\quad&x\in\mathcal{R}.\end{cases}

(29)

We define the budget renewal set to have subsets
\mathcal{R}^{i}
at the starting location, a mid-point landmark (unknown to the UAV at the mission start time), and around the known goal location. Since the UAV cannot hover at a single location under the dynamics (
26

\mathcal{R}
as a circular counter-clockwise orbit of radius
\rho

\mathcal{R}

is as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{i}=\Big\{x\in\mathcal{X}~|~(r_{N}-l_{N}^{i})^{2}+(r_{E}-l_{E}^{i})^{2}=\rho^{2},

\displaystyle\psi=\arctan\left(\frac{r_{E}-l_{E}^{i}}{r_{N}-l_{N}^{i}}\right)-\frac{\pi}{2}\Big\},

where

l_{E}^{i}

V
Conclusion

In this paper, we proposed an architecture centered on
gatekeeper
, an online safety verification algorithm, to guarantee that safety and budget constraints are enforced throughout a mission. We proved that the method is recursively feasible and guarantees safety for all time. We then introduced
ReRoot
, a method to construct a backup trajectory that allows for efficient online trajectory generation to minimize the expenditure of the budget state. The algorithm grows multiple rooted trees in reverse starting from budget renewal sets and extending into the known safe set.

We demonstrated our algorithm in simulation with a case study of a fixed-wing UAV in a GNSS-denied environment navigating to a goal location. The safety constraint was to maintain a minimum of 8 visual features in the
FOV
and the budget constraint was to limit the localization error to below 9 m. The combination of
gatekeeper
with
ReRoot
showed that multiple backup sets were used to allow the UAV to explore and map features while always staying in range of a renewal set to satisfy the budget constraint.

Future work could include extending our method of backup trajectory construction to time-varying safe sets, e.g., avoiding a dynamic obstacle, and time-varying budget renewal sets, e.g., a mobile charging station. Also of interest would be to incorporate multiple budget constraints into
ReRoot
and form a set of Pareto optimal backup trajectories. The active budget constraint in the
gatekeeper
iteration could then determine which backup to take.

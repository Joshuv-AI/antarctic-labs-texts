Epidemiological Profile and Clinical Determinants of Dengue Severity in a Colombian Andean Municipality
Authors: Graciela Olarte, Carolina Salamanca-Leguizamón, Héctor Serrano-Coll
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
IntroductionDengue virus (DENV) is an arboviral infection transmitted by female mosquitoes of the Aedes genus.ObjectiveTo describe the epidemiological behavior of dengue cases in a population from the Andean region of Colombia.MethodsThis observational, descriptive, cross-sectional study analyzed 300 randomly selected patient records from a total of 1400 cases reported in 2024.ResultsNo significant associations were found between disease severity and patient sex or geographical location. However, age range and platelet count were key factors, with younger patients more likely to have mild dengue and platelet abnormalities being the strongest predictor of severity.ConclusionsThis study identifies platelet count abnormalities as the strongest factor associated with dengue severity, reinforcing their role as a key predictor of disease progression.

Introduction

The dengue virus (DENV) is an arboviral infection transmitted by female mosquitoes of the Aedes genus, primarily Aedes aegypti and Aedes albopictus. According to the World Health Organization (WHO) [1], this disease is endemic in more than 100 countries across tropical and subtropical regions [2], with an increasing incidence in recent decades due to factors such as climate change, globalization, and deficiencies in urban planning [3].

In 2023, dengue reached historical record levels in the Americas, with over 4.1 million cases reported, surpassing the 2019 epidemic peak. Additionally, 6340 severe cases were recorded, primarily in Brazil, Colombia, Mexico, Peru, and Bolivia, along with 1954 deaths, resulting in a case fatality rate of 0.048% [1]. These outbreak patterns and the increasing epidemiological burden of this arboviral disease in the region, where all four dengue virus serotypes (DENV-1, DENV-2, DENV-3, and DENV-4) circulate, highlight the ongoing public health challenge [4].

Dengue is classified into three categories for clinical management: without warning signs, with warning signs, and severe dengue [5]. However, its clinical presentation and epidemiological behavior may vary depending on the region and host genetics, emphasizing the need to analyze local factors influencing disease dynamics. A key strategy to reduce morbidity and associated complications is to deepen the understanding of the clinical and epidemiological aspects related to this infection. Therefore, this study aims to describe the epidemiological behavior of dengue cases in a population from the Andean region of Colombia.

Materials and Methods

Study Design and Type

This study was observational, descriptive, and cross-sectional. Clinical and epidemiological investigation records from 2024 were accessed for analysis.

Population and Sample

The study population consisted of 1400 records identified and reported in the system during 2024. A sample of 300 patients was selected using a random sampling method, ensuring a 95% confidence level.

Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria

Inclusion Criteria: Cases reported with a complete epidemiological record that met the definition of a confirmed case with a positive laboratory result.

Exclusion Criteria: Cases that did not meet the inclusion criteria or had a negative laboratory result.

Unit of Analysis

Each case reported in the epidemiological surveillance system that met the inclusion criteria for the dengue outbreak in the municipality of San Gil, Santander, Columbia in 2024 was considered as a unit of analysis.

Data Collection Techniques and Instruments

For this study, the clinical-epidemiological investigation form for laboratory-confirmed cases was used as the primary data collection instrument. The collected data were consolidated into an Excel database through direct observation by the author, based on the definitions established by epidemiological surveillance protocols.

Data Processing and Analysis

After data collection, records were coded and digitized for statistical processing. Analysis was performed using the Statistical Package for Social Sciences (IBM SPSS Statistics version 27), beginning with a univariate analysis to determine frequencies and percentages for qualitative variables, as well as descriptive statistics for quantitative variables. Subsequently, a bivariate analysis was conducted using Pearson’s chi-square test for qualitative variables and the Kruskal-Wallis test for mixed variables (qualitative-quantitative), considering the non-normal distribution of quantitative data. Finally, a multivariate analysis was performed using binomial or multinomial regression, depending on the nature of the dependent variable. A significance level of P < 0.005 was established, with 95% confidence intervals calculated.

Ethical Considerations

This research was conducted in accordance with international ethical standards established by the WHO and the Pan American Health Organization (PAHO), as well as the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki (1964). Additionally, it adheres to national regulations, specifically Resolution No. 008430 of 1993 of the Colombian Ministry of Health, which governs health studies. The study was also reviewed and approved by the Unisangil Human Research Ethics Board (Act No. 02 of 20 February 2025).

Results

Epidemiological Profile of the Dengue Outbreak

Regarding the epidemiological profile, 53% of the cases occurred in women, with a median age of 29 years. Additionally, 81% of the cases were from the urban area of the municipality, 49.6% required hospitalization, and the median platelet count was 86 000, indicating moderate thrombocytopenia. When analyzing case distribution by severity, 40% of cases were classified as dengue without warning signs, 49% as dengue with warning signs, and 2.3% as severe dengue. See Appendix A: Table 1S.

Association Between Sex and Clinical Severity

A comparison between patient sex and disease severity, as well as medical management, showed no significant association between these variables (P > 0.05) (see Appendix A: Table 2S). Additionally, no significant relationship was found between platelet count and sex (see Figure 1A).

Geographical Location and Dengue Severity

When comparing patient location (urban or rural) with disease severity, medical management, and platelet count, no significant associations were found (P > 0.05) (see Table S2; Figure 1B).

Age Groups and Disease Severity

When comparing different age ranges with medical management and clinical severity, we found statistically significant differences (P<0.05) (see Table 1: Section A).

Given the observed differences in severity across age groups, a multinomial analysis was conducted to determine in which groups these differences were present. It was found that dengue without warning signs was significantly more frequent in patients aged 0 to 18 years (OR = 7.9, P = 0.021) and 19 to 44 years (P < 0.0001) compared to those aged 45 to 64 years (see Table 1: Section B). Conversely, patients aged 65 years or older had a lower probability of presenting dengue without warning signs (P < 0.0001). For dengue with warning signs, only the 19-to-44-year-old group showed a significantly higher probability compared to severe dengue (P < 0.0001), while patients 65 years or older had a lower probability of this classification (P < 0.0001). The difference in the 0-to-18-year-old group was not significant (P > 0.05). On the other hand, when comparing platelet count across age groups, a statistically significant difference was observed, specifically in the 45-to-64-year-old group (P > 0.05) (see Figure 1C, Table 1: Section C).

Risk Factors Associated with the Dengue Outbreak

Bivariate analysis identified age group and platelet count as potential explanatory factors for disease severity in the outbreak at the Regional Hospital of San Gil. A logistic regression analysis confirmed that platelet count abnormalities were the most significant factor influencing clinical severity (see Table 2).

Discussion

This study found that platelet count abnormalities were the strongest factor associated with dengue severity. While sex and geographical location showed no significant relationship with disease severity, age played a role, with younger patients more likely to have dengue without warning signs and older adults less likely to have this form of the disease. These findings highlight the importance of monitoring platelet levels in dengue patients to guide clinical management.

The median platelet count observed in this study (86 000) is consistent with the findings of Vijay et al. [6], who reported that 77% of their study population had a platelet count below 100 000. Additionally, individuals in middle adulthood exhibited lower platelet levels compared to other age groups, a pattern similar to that reported by Farida et al. [7], who observed a reduced platelet count in this population. The underlying mechanisms of dengue-associated thrombocytopenia remain unclear; however, proposed hypotheses include bone marrow suppression and platelet destruction, which may be more pronounced in middle adulthood [7]. Furthermore, during secondary infection with a heterologous DENV serotype, immune complexes formed by non-neutralizing antibodies facilitate not only viral replication through antibody-dependent enhancement (ADE) but also direct platelet activation via Fcγ receptors [8,9]. This triggers an inflammatory response mediated by IL-1α, IL-1β, and TNF-α [8]. Such activation induces the release of mitochondrial debris within extracellular vesicles, which act as damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) that amplify immunopathology by being recognized through PRRs, and possibly activating the classical complement pathway [8]. This inflammatory and cytotoxic cascade directly contributes to endothelial damage and the clinical deterioration observed in severe dengue cases (see Figure 2). Additionally, our explanatory model confirmed that platelet count was a key factor in determining the severity of the dengue outbreak in this region of the Colombian Andes. This finding aligns with the study by Vikhe et al. [10], which demonstrated that platelet count not only predicts disease severity but also has a strong capacity to anticipate severe dengue with a notable association.

On the other hand, we observed no association between patient sex and disease severity or the need for hospitalization, similar to findings from some studies that reported no link between sex and dengue severity. However, Hernandez-Bautista et al. [11], found that women had a higher risk of developing severe dengue than men [12]. Besides, we found no association between dengue severity and the patient’s place of residence, whether urban or rural. This finding aligns with the systematic review conducted by Man et al. [13], which reported no significant differences in dengue incidence or severity based on urban or rural residence. This lack of difference may be partly explained by the expansion of urban areas into regions previously classified as rural, leading to more homogeneous transmission dynamics across both settings.

Finally, this study identifies platelet count abnormalities as the strongest factor associated with dengue severity, reinforcing its role as a key predictor of disease progression. The observed differences in platelet levels across age groups, particularly the lower counts in middle adulthood, highlight the need for further research into the mechanisms of dengue-associated thrombocytopenia. In contrast, no significant associations were found between disease severity and patient sex or place of residence, suggesting that factors such as urban expansion may contribute to more uniform transmission patterns. Future studies should focus on understanding the biological drivers of thrombocytopenia, the impact of urbanization on dengue dynamics, and the integration of platelet monitoring into clinical practice to enhance early detection and management of severe cases.

Limitations

This study is based on data from a single hospital, limiting its generalizability. Selection bias may be present and other factors influencing severity were not fully analyzed.

Design and Optimization of Cs2AgBiBr6/AgBiS2 Two-Terminal Tandem Solar Cells for High-Efficiency Lead-Free Photovoltaics
Authors: Koushik Kumar, Shafat Shahnewaz, Mainul Hossain
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The ternary chalcogenide AgBiS2 has emerged as a promising lead-free photovoltaic absorber due to its high optical absorption, tunable bandgap (∼1.3 eV), and nontoxic composition. In this work, we present a comprehensive numerical study of AgBiS2-based thin-film solar cells using a one-dimensional solar cell capacitance simulator (SCAPS-1D). The influence of absorber thickness, defect density, bandgap variation, and charge transport layers on device performance is systematically analyzed. The optimized single-junction architectureFTO/TiO2/AgBiS2/Cu2Oachieves a simulated power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 13.98% under AM1.5G illumination. Because of its high absorption, a two-terminal tandem structure combining AgBiS2 as the bottom cell with Cs2AgBiBr6 (top cell) is also modeled, achieving a PCE of 23.47% after current matching. Simulations reveal that AgBiS2 thickness and defect density critically impact both J sc and V oc, while Cu2O and TiO2 provide optimal band alignment and charge extraction. The results demonstrate the potential of AgBiS2 as an efficient, environmentally benign absorber for next-generation tandem photovoltaics and establish theoretical design guidelines for future experimental development.

Introduction

Global technological advancement and urbanization require an increasing amount of energy that has led scientists to investigate renewable energy sources to reduce dependency on traditional fossil fuels. The photovoltaic industry promises to lead the renewable energy supply because of abundant solar irradiance and decreasing levelized cost of energy, with silicon solar cells primarily occupying the market. In recent decades, thin-film solar cells have emerged as solutions to further reduce costs because of their cheap fabrication process, high power conversion efficiency (PCE), and good stability. , Among these, Pb-based perovskite solar cells have had a maximum reported efficiency of 25.8% with FAPbI3, but these materials are toxic due to the presence of Pb. The Pb-free perovskite-based materials that are currently being investigated are mainly Sn-based, which oxidize very quickly from Sn2+ to Sn4+ under ambient conditions.

Recently, chalcogenides have gathered a lot of attention as promising alternatives to perovskite-based solar cells because of their optimum tunable bandgap, exceptionally high absorption coefficient, and good stability. AgBiS2, a ternary chalcogenide-based semiconductor material consisting of group I–V–VI2 elements with a bandgap of 1–1.3 eV, was found to have an absorption coefficient of 5–8 × 105 cm–1, which is 5–10 times higher than currently researched absorbers such as perovskites, CIGS, CZTS, etc. The first AgBiS2 solar cell was demonstrated in 2016 by Bernechea et al., who used hot injection method to synthesize a complete stack of AgBiS2 solar cells with an indirect bandgap of 1.3 eV, yielding an efficiency of 6.31% a with the MoO3/Polymer PTB7/AgBiS2/ZnO/indium doped tin oxide (ITO) stack. Although investigations into different synthesis methods and DFT studies on material properties were previously investigated for this material, − this work inspired a further close investigation into AgBiS2 absorbers. In 2022, Wang et al. reported a PCE of 9.17%, which was reported with a ITO/SnO2/ AgBiS2/ hole transport layer (HTL)/MoO3/Ag stack via cation disorder engineering, which remains the highest reported efficiency until date. Despite these experimentally promising results, studies focusing on the optimum thickness, defect density, doping concentration, bandgap tuning, suitable transport layers and compatibility in a multijunction architecture etc. remain largely limited.

As single-junction solar cells are limited by the Shockley–Queisser limit, the current research direction in solar cells focuses on tandem architectures to surpass it, with 4-terminal solar cells gaining the highest reported PCE of 47.6%, as of August 2025. Because of its high absorption coefficient as well as tunable narrow band gap, we hypothesize that AgBiS2 is an ideal candidate for the bottom subcell in 2T tandem architectures where the top subcell has a wide band gap.

Therefore, in this work, we perform a comprehensive numerical study on AgBiS2 solar cells both as standalone single-junction and tandem with Cs2AgBiBr6 as top subcell. At first, we have calibrated the current–voltage characteristics of a single top subcell (Cs2AgBiBr6) and bottom subcell (AgBiS2) with experimental results published in the literature. Using this calibrated environment, we have optimized AgBiS2 by varying its thickness, electron and hole transport layers, temperature, defect density, etc. After that, we connected the bottom subcell AgBiS2 with Cs2AgBiBr6 in series for 2T configuration with current matching, where the AM 1.5G solar spectrum is incident on the top subcell and the filtered spectrum from top cell is incident on the bottom subcell. Figure shows the structure of our proposed 2T cell. We have found that a standalone AgBiS2 cell with a thickness of 400 nm with TiO2 and Cu2O electron transport layer (ETL) and HTL, respectively, yields a PCE of 13.98% under standard operating conditions (300 K temperature, AM 1.5G). While used as a bottom subcell in 2T tandem architecture with Cs2AgBiBr6, which was simulated to have a standalone PCE of 13.53%, the same stack with 350 nm thickness yields an efficiency of 23.47%. In addition, we have discussed potential challenges in achieving this theoretical upper bound result from an experimental perspective. We believe this work will contribute to further development in AgBiS2-based standalone, as well as tandem solar cell architecture design and implementation.

SCAPS-1D software (Solar Cell Capacitance Simulator One dimension) is a one-dimensional solar cell simulation program developed at the department of electronics and information systems (ELIS) of the University of Gent, Belgium. SCAPS-1D numerically solves the one-dimensional equations that govern the semiconductor material such as Poisson’s equation, carrier continuity equation, and transport equations under steady state conditions. Results and Discussion

AgBiS2 Bottom Subcell Thickness Optimization

In this section, the thickness of the AgBiS2 absorber layer has been optimized with ETL and HTL set at 100 nm each to ensure maximum charge extraction. As per previous literature, absorber layer thickness is a critical parameter that needs optimization to ensure that a certain solar cell structure generates maximum PCE. Figure shows the output parameters (V oc, J sc, fill factor (FF) and PCE) with thickness changing from 0 to 800 nm. In this simulation, illumination has been set at AM 1.5G 1000 Wm–2 and temperature at 300 K. It was observed that the value of short-circuit current (J sc) increases to around 30 mA/cm2 with increasing thickness up to 600 nm because a thicker absorber layer can generate more photons and consequently create a higher number of electron–hole pairs. However, the possibility of recombination also increases as the charges must travel a greater length for diffusion, which explains a slight drop at a thicknesses higher than 600 nm. The value of V oc also shows similar trends to V oc at initial stages but does not show the drop. This can be explained by the theory of Landsberg et al. that V oc depends on photogenerated current J sc as well as the dark saturation current Jo. A higher Jo means there is more carrier recombination; therefore, V oc is a measure of carrier recombination of the solar cell. The relation is given by 9 Voc=kTqln(JscJ0+1) where kT is thermal voltage, J sc is light-generated current density, and J 0 the dark saturation current density. The decrease in the FF is caused by the increase in series resistance in the layer. As the thickness of the absorber layer increases, it causes an increase in the series resistance of the structure. And this increase in the series resistance decreases the FF of the structure. The equation for PCE of the solar cell, is given by 10 η=VocJscFFPin

Output parameters (V oc, J sc, FF, and PCE) of bottom subcell AgBiS2 with thickness variation with Cu2O as HTL and TiO2 as ETL.

Equation suggests that the highest PCE is dependent on the optimum values of the other three parameters, namely, V oc, J sc, and FF. As both V oc and J sc have an increasing trend in contrast to FF, which has a decreasing trend, the device was found to have the highest efficiency around 400 nm, which is determined to be the optimum thickness. This thickness represents the theoretical upper bound of device performance based on the optimum band alignment. Practical solar cell absorbers may have a higher degree of defects associated with them; therefore, taking that into consideration, the optimum thickness might vary a certain amount. In addition, optimization of deposition, annealing, and cation-disorder control may be required for achieving this target thickness.

Among these ETLs, PCBM shows the lowest PCE (9.86%), which may be because of low hole and electron mobilities (2 × 10–1 cm2/(V s)). To investigate further, the energy-band block diagram of 4 different ETLs were taken into consideration, which is shown in Supporting Information Figure 1. From the band diagram, it was observed that STO and PCBM had large cliffs, which corresponded to lower PCE value. TiO2 has the smallest cliff compared to the other 3 materials. Band alignments for the selected HTLs with AgBiS2 are shown in Figure d. The four HTLs, namely, CuI, CuSCN, Cu2O, and NiO, have VBO values of 0.32 eV, 0.14 eV, 0.21, and 0.10 eV, respectively. To summarize, the simulations show that the optimized structure achieves a maximum PCE of 13.98% using Cu2O as the HTL and TiO2 as the ETL.

Effect of Absorber Defect Density on Top and Bottom Subcells

Defects play a crucial role in the performance of a solar cell. These defects can originate from the bulk absorber, the transport layers, as well as the interfaces between each layer. In this section, we analyze how the key performance metrics (e.g., V oc, J sc, FF, and PCE) of both the Cs2AgBiBr6 top subcell and AgBiS2 bottom subcell vary with different defect densities. To do so, we vary the defect density from 1014 cm–3 to 1017 cm–3 in the corresponding absorber layers and observe a steady decline in all 4 parameters. Figure a,b show how the 4 parameters evolve with increasing defect density for the Cs2AgBiBr6 top sub cell and AgBiS2 bottom sub cell, respectively. This is expected, since defects are recombination centers that create localized trap states for carriers to get trapped instead of being extracted at the contacts. This mechanism is referred to as Shockley-Read-Hall (SRH) recombination, which is one of the primary loss mechanisms in solar cells. The equation for SRH is given in eq as 13 R=np−ni2τn(p+Nve(Et/kT))+τp(n+Nce(Eg−Et/kT)) In this equation, the concentrations of mobile electrons and holes are n and p, respectively, the energy level and concentration of trap defects are E t and N t, and the intrinsic concentration is n i.

A similar trend is observed for the parameters except FF when interface defect densities between the absorber and corresponding transport layers of both top and bottom subcells are varied from 1014 to 1016 cm–3. These results are presented in Supporting Information Figure S2. It is worth noting that interfacial defects, because of their position, influence the cells more than the defects in the bulk, since recombination centers at the interface cause photogenerated carriers to recombine before they can be efficiently extracted by the transport layers. This directly reduces charge collection efficiency and increases nonradiative recombination losses, leading to lower V oc, J sc, FF, and overall PCE. Therefore, controlling the interfacial defect density through proper band alignment, surface passivation, and optimized transport-layer deposition is essential for realizing the simulated device performance.

The best possible result is found if the defect density is kept at 1014 cm–3. Further increases only cause strong degradation of PCE. Therefore, achieving high absorber crystallinity, minimizing bulk trap density, and implementing effective interface passivation will be necessary to approach the expected efficiency of the proposed tandem architecture.

Potential Experimental Challenges in Achieving the Simulated Performance in Cs2AgBiBr6/AgBiS2 Solar Cells

It is important to note that the simulated 2T tandem cell performance assumes ideal, lossless interconnection and optimal band alignment between the absorbers and their respective transport layers. In practical devices, the interconnection is typically formed using TCOs, which, despite their high carrier transport properties, introduce additional losses due to finite diffusion lengths and recombination at the top subcell/interconnect and bottom subcell/interconnect interfaces. In practical monolithic tandem devices, the TCO recombination layer introduces additional losses through parasitic optical absorption, interfacial recombination, and contact resistance. Based on recent experimental and simulation studies, these effects are estimated to reduce the ideal tandem efficiency by approximately 2.6–3.7% − absolute, resulting in a practically attainable efficiency of ∼20–21% compared to the simulated value of 23.47%. Therefore, the reported PCE should be interpreted as a theoretical upper bound under ideal interconnection conditions. In addition, the simulation assumes a Gaussian defect distribution in between the interfacial layers in the range of 1014 cm–3–1015 cm–3, which is commonly adopted in SCAPS-based studies. , However, in experimental AgBiS2 and Cs2AgBiBr6 absorbers, based on surface defects and lattice mismatch during fabrication, the interface may contain a higher degree of defects, which may result in a lower PCE. To examine this, we performed a defect density variation study for both subcells, as shown in Section and Supporting Information Figure S2.

Furthermore, experimentally reported AgBiS2 solar cells typically employ much thinner absorber layers (e.g., ∼30–35 nm), , whereas the optimized structure in this study uses a significantly thicker absorber (∼400 nm). This increased thickness in simulation enhances photon absorption and carrier generation but achieving such thickness experimentally while maintaining high film quality and uniformity remains challenging. Another potential limitation could arise from material quality. The high absorption coefficient of AgBiS2 is often achieved through cation disorder engineering; however, maintaining a controlled crystallinity and uniform cation distribution becomes increasingly difficult as film thickness increases. This may lead to structural inhomogeneity and increased recombination in experimentally fabricated thicker films. Therefore, the optimized results presented here should be interpreted as a theoretical upper bound performance achieved under favorable band alignment and optimized device parameters.

Conclusion

In this work, a comprehensive analysis on the prospects of a 2-terminal tandem solar cell with Cs2AgBiBr6 and AgBiS2 has been performed. The standalone cells have been calibrated with experimental data to validate the simulation. The analysis has been performed primarily focusing on optimizing AgBiS2 to achieve the maximum PCE with different transport layers, thicknesses, defects, doping density, etc. It has been observed that the AgBiS2 solar cell provides optimal band alignment with TiO2 and Cu2O as electron and hole transport layers, respectively. In addition, it has been observed that the thickness at which AgBiS2 solar cells perform best is 400 nm. On the basis of these optimizations and a suitable bandgap of 1.3 eV, it has been integrated into a 2-terminal tandem solar cell architecture with Cs2AgBiBr6 as the top subcell. In doing so, an optimized solar cell stack of FTO (100 nm)/TiO2 (100 nm)/AgBiS2 (400 nm)/Cu2O (100 nm) has been found that yields an efficiency of 13.98%. In terms of the 2T TSC with current-matched conditions, the projected PCE reaches 23.47%, corresponding to a 216.3% improvement relative to the experimentally reported efficiencies of the individual subcells.

Overall, this study provides a material-specific device design framework for AgBiS2 and demonstrates its potential role in lead-free tandem photovoltaics. The insights presented here can serve as a guideline for future experimental efforts and for further development of environmentally friendly, all-inorganic tandem solar-cell architectures.

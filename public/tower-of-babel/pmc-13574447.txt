Bibliometric analysis of transcranial Doppler ultrasound
Authors: Le Yang, Wenjie Zhang, Xu Hou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:Transcranial Doppler (TCD) ultrasound is a valuable noninvasive diagnostic tool for assessing the cerebral vasculature and has been widely used in clinical care and research settings. However, no systematic reviews have investigated the development history, global landscape, and trend evolution at the macroscopic level in this field. Therefore, this study employs bibliometric techniques to analyze TCD publications from 1990 to 2025 (search date: November 28, 2025), investigating international cooperation networks, knowledge foundations, research hotspots, and future developmental trends.Methods:Literature retrieval and screening were conducted based on the Web of Science Core Collection database. VOSviewer and CiteSpace were employed to perform collaboration network analysis, co-citation analysis, co-occurrence clustering, and burst detection across countries, regions, institutions, journals, authors, references, and keywords within the included literature.Results:A total of 4522 articles were included. The analysis showed that the research hotspots in this field have deepened from disease diagnosis to mechanistic exploration and are now expanding toward frontiers such as perioperative monitoring and cognitive neuroscience.Conclusions:Core literature has integrated the theoretical framework of TCD to evaluate cerebral vascular function and promote the development of the field. In the future, it will be necessary to strengthen interdisciplinary integration – particularly with bioengineering and signal processing for artificial intelligence-driven analysis, cognitive psychology and neuroimaging for neurovascular coupling studies, and critical care medicine and data science for biomarker development – and international cooperation to further expand the application of TCD in emerging scenarios.

1. Introduction

Since its clinical introduction by Aaslid et al in 1982,[1] transcranial Doppler (TCD) ultrasound has emerged as an indispensable noninvasive tool for evaluating intracranial hemodynamics. With unique capabilities, such as real-time observation, portability, and high temporal resolution, TCD has been widely applied in a variety of fields, ranging from the diagnosis and monitoring of cerebrovascular diseases (e.g., stroke and subarachnoid hemorrhage) to the evaluation of cerebrovascular vasomotor reactivity and autoregulatory function, intraoperative monitoring, and state-of-the-art research in the field of cognitive neuroscience.[2–6] This is of great clinical as well as research interest, and for the last three-and-a-half decades, this technique has been constantly improving, with increasing use and ever-expanding literature. Although many primary research papers and reviews address some particular uses of this technique, there is no comprehensive macroscopic view of the overall development picture of the field.[4,7–10] Unlike previous reviews that summarized technical principles or specific clinical applications, this study is the first to adopt a scientometrics approach to systematically map the global research landscape, delineate the structure of the scientific community, trace the evolution of the knowledge base, and identify current research frontiers in TCD. The important issues that have not been well answered include how worldwide production and influence are distributed in this discipline, how a cooperative network can be characterized, how research hotspots have changed over time, and what the current research frontiers are.

Bibliometrics provides us with a rigorous methodological toolbox for answering such questions. By performing statistical analyses on large collections of citations, it can objectively describe the development path, key research topics, and the future direction of a certain discipline. [11] Therefore, in this research, we present the first bibliometric analysis of all TCD-related publications indexed in the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) from 1990 to 2025 (search date: November 28, 2025).

By constructing a country/regional and institution cooperation network diagram, journal co-citation map, keyword co-occurrence and cluster diagram, and reference burst detection maps, we aim to macroscopically depict the whole picture of research and collaboration status within the domain of TCD; determine key research institutes, influential journals, and representative literature; track the historical evolution process of research hotspots; and identify current areas of investigation and potential directions for further development. We believe that such a structured overview will lead to an overall “map of knowledge” in the field of TCD from the perspective of researchers, clinicians, and policy makers, as well as providing useful information for them to plan further research work, clinical practice, and decision-making.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Literature retrieval and search strategy

The WoSCC database performed better than other databases in terms of the accuracy of literature type annotation; it is generally considered an ideal choice for bibliographic analysis. Therefore, we conducted literature mining using this platform. We extracted all papers on TCD indexed by the WoSCC from January 1, 1990, to November 28, 2025, using the following search strategy:

(((((TS = (“Transcranial Doppler Ultrasound*”)) OR TS = (“Transcranial Doppler Sonography”)) OR TS = (“Transcranial Sonography, Doppler”)) OR TS = (“Doppler Transcranial Sonography”)) OR TS = (“Transcranial Doppler Ultrasonography”)) OR (TS = (“Doppler Transcranial Ultrasonography”)) OR TS = (“Doppler Ultrasonography, Transcranial”).

To ensure data quality and consistency in literature type annotation, we deliberately selected the WoSCC as the sole data source. However, this methodological choice inherently limits the scope of our analysis, as it systematically excludes literature exclusively available in other databases such as Scopus, PubMed, or Embase, which may contain relevant conference proceedings, preprints, and regional journal publications. This potential for missing pertinent studies is acknowledged.[12]

2.2. Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Literature screening was performed based on the following criteria:

The inclusion criteria were as follows: full-text publications with content relevant to TCD; articles and review manuscripts written in English; and publications dated between January 1, 1990, and November 28, 2025. The exclusion criteria were as follows: studies with topics unrelated to TCD, and non-article formats, such as conference abstracts, news items, and brief communications. To minimize subjectivity, duplicate records were removed (EndNote X9; Thomson Reuters). Titles and abstracts were independently screened by 2 reviewers (Le Yang and Wenjie Zhang). Disagreements were resolved by a third reviewer (Xu Hou). The subjective “content relevant to TCD” criterion is acknowledged as a limitation.

2.3. Data processing and visualization

We used GraphPad Prism v8.0.2 (GraphPad Software Inc.) to plot the trend of annual publications, country-level publication trends, and proportion distribution, and CiteSpace (version 6.2.4R, 64-bit Advanced Edition) and VOSviewer (version 1.6.18) to analyze the data and visualize scientific knowledge graphs.

VOSviewer v1.6.17[13] was employed to analyze the large-scale bibliographic dataset and generate visual maps of the identified networks. CiteSpace (version 6.2.4R), software developed by Professor Chaomei Chen, is used to visualize the research landscape in disciplines based on constructing a literature co-citation network. This application uses the experimental environment to test new ideas and assess existing technology to help users learn about a subject area, research hotspots, evolution trends, and to forecast future research directions. VOSviewer and CiteSpace were used complementarily in this study: VOSviewer excels at constructing static network and cluster visualizations (e.g., co-authorship, co-occurrence maps), providing clear and intuitive snapshots of the research structure; whereas CiteSpace specializes in analyzing dynamic evolution and burst detection, enabling the identification of emerging trends, pivotal references, and shifts in research frontiers over time. This combination allowed us to achieve both a comprehensive structural overview and a longitudinal perspective on the field’s development.

2.4. Ethics statement

Since this study is a bibliometric analysis and does not involve human or animal subjects, nor any collection of individual-level data, ethical approval was not required.

3. Results

3.1. Analysis of annual publication output in the TCD research field

The search returned 4522 TCD-related articles indexed in the WoSCC database from January 1, 1990, to November 28, 2025 (Fig. 1). The publications included 93 countries/regions, 3173 institutions, and 16,180 authors. Since 1990, the annual number of publications exhibited a gradual upward trend (Fig. 2). We also subdivided this timeframe into an acceleration period (1990–1993), corresponding to the rapid development of the field during these years, and an extended period of peak productivity since 1993, suggesting that the discipline remained at its peak after this date.

Regarding China’s position in this network: its node (311 articles, 6.88% of total) is moderately sized but shows limited direct connections to the US-German core, with most collaborations directed toward the USA and Australia. Its centrality is low (0.05), and its average citation per publication (15.38) is substantially lower than that of core countries (e.g., USA: 44.26, England: 46.28). This suggests that China’s international collaboration network is relatively independent, focusing more on domestic research rather than deep integration into the core European and North American clusters. This is further evidenced by the dense internal collaborative links observed among European nations (e.g., England, Germany, Netherlands, France, Italy).

This core-periphery structure has significant implications for knowledge flow. The core countries (USA, Germany) and secondary hubs (England, France) serve as primary sources of knowledge dissemination, leveraging their high citation impact (e.g., England: 46.28 citations/paper; France: 51.54 citations/paper) to channel influential research globally. Countries like the Netherlands (51.54 citations/paper) and France (centrality 0.12) act as critical bridges, facilitating cross-disciplinary and cross-regional knowledge transfer. In contrast, China’s peripheral position and low centrality (0.05) create a knowledge bottleneck, limiting its access to cutting-edge ideas and innovations, which is reflected in its low citation impact (15.38 citations/paper). To enhance knowledge flow, we recommend that China strategically increase collaborations with core countries, particularly in emerging hotspots such as cerebral small vessel disease, thereby improving its network centrality and citation rates.

3.5. Co-citation analysis of references in the TCD research domain

Using 1-year intervals as time slices, the co-citation reference network (Fig. 11) encompasses 1974 nodes and 8504 links from 1990 to 2025 (search date: November 28, 2025). Among the top 10 most highly co-cited articles (Table 6),[14–23] the study “Utility of transcranial Doppler ultrasound for the integrative assessment of cerebrovascular function,”[14] published in Journal of Neuroscience Methods, underscores the substantial value of TCD in evaluating cerebrovascular function. TCD in the context of the unique physiology of the brain: while the brain has very high energy/oxygen consumption, as authored by Willie CK, a relatively small amount of energy storage demands an effective local circulatory system. TCD is a useful option to investigate cerebrovascular function on either a scientific or clinical basis because of its rather low cost, simplicity of operation, noninvasiveness, and high time resolution. TCD allows for reliable measurement of cerebral blood flow (CBF) velocity, cerebrovascular autoregulation, cerebrovascular reactivity to carbon dioxide (CO2)-stimulation, and neurovascular coupling, both physiologically and pathologically (e.g., stroke, traumatic brain injury). Below, we provide an overview of the following topics: TCD and its alternatives; TCD protocols in cerebrovascular assessment; a detailed analysis of the physiological basis of CBF control; the clinical and research applications of TCD for the evaluation of cerebrovascular diseases; and assessment of the 4 major complementary components of cerebrovascular function, including CBF velocities and cerebrovascular autoregulation; cerebrovascular reactivity; and neurovascular coupling. We present an overview from a systematic point of view on how all these controls operate in tandem with each other and highlight promising areas for further study.

The second most highly co-cited article was the paper “Assessment of middle cerebral artery diameter during hypocapnia and hypercapnia in humans using ultra-high-field MRI,”[15] published in the Journal of Applied Physiology and authored by Verbree J. This study addresses a longstanding assumption in cerebrovascular CO2 reactivity assessment, in that the diameter of large intracranial arteries, as evaluated via TCD, remains unaltered by fluctuations in the arterial partial pressure of CO2. The strong vasodilating effect of CO2 casts doubt on this statement, indicating a slight increase in arterial radius. Previous literature on the effect of CO2 on middle cerebral artery (MCA) diameter showed conflicting results. The study recruited 10 healthy participants (5 females, 5 males; age range: 21–30 years). Using 7 Tesla ultra-high-resolution magnetic resonance imaging (in-plane resolution: 0.2 mm), direct measurements of baseline MCA diameter were obtained under 4 experimental conditions: hypocapnia (–1 kPa, equivalent to –7.5 mm Hg), normocapnia (0 kPa, 0 mm Hg), and 2 levels of hypercapnia (+1 kPa [7.5 mm Hg] and +2 kPa [15 mm Hg]). The lumens of the vascular trees were segmented manually, and segmentation was performed twice to compare the different raters. Key findings revealed that for every 2 kPa elevation in end-tidal carbon dioxide partial pressure (PETCO2), the MCA diameter increased by 6.8 ± 2.9% relative to baseline. In contrast, there were no significant differences in diameter at –1 kPa (–1.2% ± 2.4%) or +1 kPa (+1.4% ± 3.2%) as compared to normocapnia. The nonlinear relationship between MCA diameter and CO2 can be described by fitting a continuous calibration curve. The curve can correct for CBF measured via TCD if simultaneous PETCO2 monitoring is available. In conclusion, the MCA diameter remains constant when PETCO2 deviates slightly from normocapnia but increases in response to elevated PETCO2 levels.

Co-citation reference and temporal clustering analyses were performed (Figs. 12 and 13, respectively). The results revealed that early research hotspots in the field included hydrocephalus (cluster 1), head injury (cluster 3), embolism (cluster 4), spectral analysis (cluster 6), stroke (cluster 8), attention (cluster 11), vasospasm (cluster 12), foramen ovale (cluster 14), and anatomy (cluster 18). Midterm research focused on cerebral autoregulation (cluster 5), subarachnoid hemorrhage (cluster 7), risk assessment (cluster 13), visuospatial function (cluster 15), and patent foramen ovale (cluster 16). Currently, prominent research hot topics and frontier trends include cerebral blood flow velocity (cluster 0), cerebral blood flow (cluster 2), sickle cell disease (cluster 9), neurofunctional monitoring (cluster 10), noninvasive neurofunctional monitoring (cluster 17), inhibition mechanisms (cluster 19), and right-to-left shunts (cluster 20). By integrating the analysis of Figure 13 (timeline view), we further delineated the evolutionary phases of research hotspots in this field. In the early stage, the research focus was centered on diagnostic domains, as evidenced by clusters such as “hydrocephalus” (cluster 1) and “head injury” (cluster 3), whose peak activity periods were concentrated primarily between 1985 and 1995. During the intermediate stage, the research focus shifted toward mechanistic investigations, with “cerebral autoregulation” (cluster 5) emerging as a representative cluster – its activity persisted from 2000 to 2015, forming a continuous mountain-shaped region indicative of sustained scholarly attention. In the recent stage, research frontiers have expanded rapidly into perioperative monitoring and cognitive neuroscience: clusters including “neurofunctional monitoring” (cluster 10) and “non-invasive neurofunctional monitoring” (cluster 17) have exhibited explosive growth since 2018, extending through 2024. This data-driven account objectively substantiates the evolutionary trajectory of the field, progressing from diagnostic-oriented research to mechanistic exploration, and further toward frontiers in perioperative care and cognitive science.

3.7. Analysis of co-cited references and keyword bursts in the TCD research domain

Using CiteSpace, we identified the 50 most robust burst-cited references in TCD-related research. By integrating the analysis of Figure 18 (citation burst), we identified a pivotal hallmark of the mechanism exploration stage: the 2009 publication by Fisher et al on “cerebral autoregulation,” whose citation burst period spans from 2009 to 2026 – this indicates the sustained, impactful role of this work throughout the mechanistic research phase. In contrast, the representative literature signifying the recent shift toward perioperative and cognitive neuroscience frontiers is the 2020 study by Burma JS et al, whose citation burst extends from 2020 to 2026. This finding directly substantiates the transition of research focus toward perioperative monitoring and the frontiers of cognitive science. The reference with the highest burst strength (34.29) is “Utility of transcranial Doppler ultrasound for the integrative assessment of cerebrovascular function,”[14] published in Journal of Neuroscience Methods, with Willie CK as the first author. This paper was not only the most frequently cited paper but also one of the burst-cited papers, meaning that it plays an important role in this domain both currently and historically. In addition, all of these 50 citations were issued from 1990 to 2025 (search date: November 28, 2025), reflecting their continued citation footprint during the last 30 years. Notably, 9 of these papers are still within their peak citation time frame (Fig. 18)[17,24–31] and therefore further studies on this topic may be expected.

Citation burst graph of references. This figure lists the top 50 references that experienced sudden citation surges between 1990 and 2025. Red bars indicate the duration of citation bursts.

Further, through the analysis of Figure 19 (keyword burst), we identified the burst timings of keywords that underpin recent research frontiers. Specifically, the keyword “cognitive impairment” initiated its burst in 2018 and remained active through 2025, while “neurovascular coupling” commenced its burst in 2020 and is still in the active burst period to date. In addition, keywords such as “aerobic exercise” and “dementia” have successively entered their burst phases since 2020. Collectively, these data demonstrate that TCD research is rapidly expanding into cutting-edge domains including cognitive neuroscience (e.g., neurovascular coupling, cognitive impairment) and perioperative monitoring (e.g., assessment of aerobic exercise). From these 576 most significant burst keywords, we selected the first 50 with the strongest burst intensity (see Fig. 19), as they can represent both the current research hotspots and those that may become future trends.

4. Discussion

To our knowledge, no prior bibliometric study has specifically analyzed the literature on TCD. According to the results of our analysis, after more than 30 years of development, a thriving, robust research culture has emerged.

According to countries/regions, the distribution of scientific research power is uneven, centered on the USA, Europe, and North America. Though China ranks in the top 5 by publication numbers, it still has some distance from top-ranked nations in terms of influence and scope of international collaboration, indicating the need to promote quality research and international academic exchange in the future.

Taken together, these factors have shaped a core-periphery structure in the global TCD research network. The USA and Western European countries constitute the core, characterized by high centrality, high citation impact, and dense internal collaboration. In contrast, countries such as China occupy a peripheral position, with lower centrality and citation rates, and limited direct integration into the core network. This core-periphery structure has important implications for research agenda setting. Core countries, by virtue of their central position, disproportionately influence which topics are considered important, funded, and published – thereby shaping the global research agenda. For example, research priorities in the core (e.g., neurovascular coupling, multimodal monitoring) are more likely to become mainstream, while topics relevant to peripheral regions (e.g., local disease patterns, low-cost adaptations) may receive less attention. To mitigate this, peripheral countries should strategically increase collaborations with core partners and actively participate in setting international research priorities, particularly in emerging areas such as cerebral small vessel disease and perioperative neurocognitive monitoring.

Most physicians who are not trained as neurologists have minimal training in how to interpret a TCD study, and practical guidelines are sparse. There is an urgent need to strengthen training and education to fill the education gap in TCD interpretation, thus providing a basis for developing quality researchers.[32] Finally, from the perspective of journals and the academic landscape, research results in this field are highly concentrated in authoritative journals in the fields of neuroscience, cerebrovascular diseases, and clinical medicine (e.g., Stroke), and most are located in the journal citation reports Q1/Q2 quartiles, confirming the recognized research value and academic standards. The double-map overlay analysis further reveals the paths of knowledge flow: research in basic biology and genetics provides theoretical support for clinical neurology,[33] while research in psychology, health care, and other fields is also deeply integrated with clinical practice,[34] demonstrating the distinct interdisciplinary and translational medical characteristics of TCD research.

Furthermore, the TCD field is undergoing a paradigm shift, evolving from a diagnostic tool for cerebrovascular diseases into a physiological research platform and a component of multimodal neural monitoring. This transformation is driven by technological advances (e.g., high-field magnetic resonance imaging, high-density EEG), clinical needs (e.g., brain–computer interfaces, personalized neurocritical care), and theoretical developments (e.g., neurovascular coupling theory). This shift not only alters the research landscape but also opens new opportunities for peripheral countries to contribute to these emerging frontiers.

Keywords are the condensed form of research themes, and their co-occurrence, clustering, and emergent dynamics directly reflect the research structure, current status, and evolutionary direction of the field. In terms of keyword co-occurrence analysis, high-frequency keywords such as “transcranial Doppler ultrasound,” “stroke,” “cerebral blood flow,” “velocity,” and “autoregulation” form the most solid research foundation of the field. This directly indicates that the core of TCD research revolves around the technology itself, its main application scenarios (stroke), and the core physiological parameters measured (CBF velocity and autoregulation function).

In terms of keyword-clustering analysis, the 4 main clusters obtained through analysis clearly depict the 4 major research directions. Clinical diagnosis and management of cerebrovascular diseases (red cluster): focusing on the application of TCD in the diagnosis, risk prediction, treatment evaluation, and surgical monitoring of stroke, microembolism, carotid stenosis, and other diseases,[35–37] this is the established classic and active section of the field. However, a recent retrospective study showed that, in patients with large-vessel occlusive stroke and excellent reperfusion, intravenous thrombolysis does not significantly affect TCD parameters, and the predictive value of TCD for clinical outcomes is limited. This highlights the complexity of microvascular assessment and also provides important insights: the collection of research data may be affected by retrospective bias, making it difficult to establish causal relationships. However, prospective large-sample studies, including patients with different reperfusion grades, are needed to further clarify the role of TCD in the assessment and monitoring of intravenous thrombolysis and microvascular function in stroke recovery.[38] Cerebrovascular physiology and regulatory mechanism research (green cluster): in-depth exploration of the reactivity, autoregulatory mechanisms, and hemodynamic changes in cerebral vessels under the stimulation of blood pressure, CO2, and hypoxia[39–41] is the theoretical basis for understanding the physiological significance of TCD data. Recently, in a study using a TCD multimodal approach to describe the characteristics of cerebral vascular reactivity in patients with severe internal carotid artery stenosis before carotid endarterectomy, it was found that severe carotid artery stenosis not only leads to a reduction in downstream blood flow but also causes a “qualitative change in cerebral vascular regulation – manifested as accelerated vascular reactivity, decreased autoregulatory buffering capacity, and reduced stability. This provides a new theoretical basis and practical tool for TCD to assess stroke risk and optimize treatment strategies from the perspective of “functional cerebral hemodynamics.”[42] Cognitive neuroscience and brain function assessment (blue cluster): this represents an emerging cross-disciplinary direction focusing on the study of CBF regulation and neurovascular coupling mechanisms during cognitive tasks, brain lateralization, aging, and dementia, and is closely integrated with techniques such as fMRI and EEG.[43–47] Intensive care and vascular lesion monitoring (yellow cluster): this cluster focuses on neuromonitoring and prognosis prediction in critical conditions, such as aneurysms, vasospasm after subarachnoid hemorrhage, intracranial pressure, and cerebral perfusion pressure assessment.[48–51]

Based on these clustering results, we propose the following specific interdisciplinary integration recommendations to guide future research directions: for cluster 2 (green, cerebrovascular physiology and regulatory mechanisms), collaboration with engineering and data science is recommended to develop artificial intelligence (AI)-driven, standardized analysis pipelines for cerebral autoregulation and cerebrovascular reactivity, enabling more objective and reproducible assessments. For cluster 3 (blue, cognitive neuroscience and brain function assessment), leveraging TCD’s high temporal resolution combined with cognitive task paradigms and computational neural models can refine studies on neurovascular coupling, deepening our understanding of brain–behavior relationships. For clusters 1 and 4 (red, clinical diagnosis and management; yellow, intensive care and vascular lesion monitoring), we advocate for the incorporation of TCD parameters as biomarkers into multicenter clinical research cohorts and prognostic models, thereby enhancing the evidence base for TCD in routine clinical decision-making and critical care monitoring.

Regarding the keyword burst analysis, the results from the keywords with the highest burst intensity showed a clear evolutionary path of research trends. Early stage (1990s–early 2000s): research hotspots focused on the establishment of the diagnostic value of TCD in certain diseases, such as “vasospasm after subarachnoid hemorrhage”[52] and “patent foramen ovale,”[53] as well as research into fundamental parameters like “blood flow velocity”[54] or “pulsatility index.”[55] Intermediate phase (mid-2000s–2010s): Studies delved deeper into mechanistic studies by quantitatively assessing physiological function, for example, “cerebral vascular autoregulation,”[56] or “cerebral vascular reactivity.”[57] Late phase (late 2010s–present): a clear shift in the mainstream is toward “neuro-monitoring,” “non-invasive neuro-monitoring,”[58] “cognition,”[2] and “inhibitory control.” This suggests an expansion in use cases for TCD, away from a clinical focus on diagnosing diseases toward real-time physiological monitoring during perioperative and intensive care periods, as well as integration into cognitive science, mental disorders,[59] and even blood diseases (sickle cell disease[60]), representing a very promising growing point for the future.

Based on the strong burst keywords identified from Figure 19, we sharpen the future perspectives by directly linking them to these bibliometric findings. We predict TCD’s potential key roles in the next 5 years: exploring early biomarkers for Alzheimer disease, capitalizing on TCD’s ability to detect subtle hemodynamic alterations years before clinical onset; physiological subtyping of psychiatric disorders (e.g., depression, schizophrenia) by characterizing distinct patterns of cerebral autoregulation and neurovascular coupling; and developing AI-based automated decision-support systems for intraoperative brain function protection, where machine learning algorithms integrate real-time TCD parameters to guide anesthetic management. Furthermore, TCD offers unique value for brain–computer interfaces by providing noninvasive, real-time hemodynamic feedback that reflects underlying neural activation, complementing EEG and fMRI. However, challenges remain, including its low spatial resolution and the need for advanced signal processing to extract reliable control signals.

The co-citation results also suggest that a review by Willie et al[14] comprehensively combines these 4 aspects of TCD when assessing cerebral vascular functions, becoming one of the most important documents on this topic. This paper has remained widely cited, indicating that the trend of recent studies is to integrate and assess the status of the cerebral vascular system more comprehensively. To sum up, the article by Willie CK et al not only summarizes the foundational knowledge of the field through its high co-citation frequency but also reflects emerging research trends through its burst citation pattern. The most important thing clinically is that this review has promoted TCD from a simple “speedometer” into a “key master” for comprehensive evaluation of the functions of cerebral blood vessels. This review has effectively facilitated the standardization and normalization of the use of TCD in scientific and medical practice through an integrative model, which sparked renewed interest in the area.[9,10]

Although we present a systematic analysis of the general evolution of the TCD area in this study, there are some drawbacks to consider. First, as stated above, the search process only used the WoSCC to ensure data quality. Relying solely on WoSCC is a methodological limitation, as it may systematically omit literature indexed in other major databases (e.g., Scopus, PubMed, Embase), including a substantial number of conference proceedings and regional journals. Furthermore, WoSCC has well‑known coverage gaps for non‑English journals and gray literature (e.g., preprints, technical reports), which may have led to an underrepresentation of research from non‑English‑speaking regions and emerging interdisciplinary studies. This single-database reliance could introduce bias, particularly in the analysis of “country/region” and “institution” distributions, possibly underestimating contributions from certain regions. Future studies should consider multi-database validation to provide a more comprehensive global perspective. In addition, the search period spanned from January 1, 1990, to November 28, 2025; therefore, the 2025 data are incomplete, and trend analyses for this year should be interpreted with caution. Second, despite careful construction of our search strategy, it is possible that we missed certain keywords. In particular, we may have failed to capture studies using emerging or variant terms such as “functional TCD” or “transcranial color‑coded sonography (TCCS),” which are increasingly adopted in recent literature. Future bibliometric analyses should employ a more exhaustive keyword list, including these terms, to ensure broader coverage of related studies. Third, the bibliometric approach is focused on macro trends and quantitative relations by its own nature, and deep interpretations of research contents need professional expertise. Fourth, our professional backgrounds may introduce a perspective bias when interpreting the results. The research team is primarily composed of neurologists, which may have led to an emphasis on clinical and neurophysiological implications while potentially underemphasizing aspects more relevant to other specialties (e.g., radiology, bioengineering). We acknowledge this potential bias and recommend that future interpretative syntheses involve a multidisciplinary panel to balance perspectives. Fifth, the exclusion of non-English papers could have omitted some local features of the studies; for instance, our team previously published preliminary results on the impact of TCD monitoring on hepatic encephalopathy after major hepatectomy in Chinese journals.[12] Finally, by excluding non-article formats such as conference abstracts, news items, and brief communications, we may have underestimated early signals of emerging collaborative networks and research frontiers, as such formats often serve as early indicators of novel directions. This exclusion is acknowledged as a limitation that should be addressed in future studies by incorporating a broader range of document types.

Based on the above findings, the following prospects for future research are proposed: Promote global cooperation and knowledge integration, encourage emerging research forces such as China to strengthen cooperation with leading teams in Europe and America, actively participate in international multicenter research, and enhance the quality and influence of research; deepen mechanism research and technical standardization, continue to explore the physiological and pathological mechanisms of cerebral blood vessel regulation, and strive to promote the standardization and normalization of TCD examination protocols (e.g., automatic regulation, reactivity assessment) to enhance the reliability and comparability of its clinical application; expand frontier application scenarios, actively explore the application value of TCD in emerging cross-disciplinary fields such as perioperative neuromonitoring, brain–computer interfaces,[61] early identification of cognitive impairment,[62] assessment of mental disorders,[63] and sports science[64]; encourage interdisciplinary innovation, promote the deep integration of neuroscience, engineering, data science (e.g., application of AI in signal analysis) and clinical medicine, and develop more intelligent and integrated TCD monitoring systems and data analysis tools.[65–68]

In this study, we systematically revealed the 30-year developmental trajectory of the TCD field. Research hotspots have evolved from disease diagnosis to physiological mechanism exploration and are now expanding to perioperative neuromonitoring and cognitive assessment. Future progress will depend on interdisciplinary integration, international cooperation, and the expansion of TCD applications in emerging scenarios.

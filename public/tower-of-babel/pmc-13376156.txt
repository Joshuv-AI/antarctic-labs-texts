Characteristics, source analysis, and health risk assessments of heavy metals for karst water in Shentou spring area, northern China
Authors: Chunhong Zhao, Haoyong Shen, Zhiheng Wang, Yi Zhao, Hao Xie
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo identify the contamination characteristics and health risks of heavy metals in karst groundwater systems in northern China, the Shentou Spring area was selected as the study site. A total of 53 karst water samples were collected, and ten heavy metal elements (As, Cr6+, Al, Cu, Pb, Zn, Ni, Mn, Co, and Fe) were analyzed. Multiple statistical analysis, the single-factor pollution index method, the Nemerow comprehensive pollution index method, and a health risk assessment model were comprehensively applied to systematically evaluate the distribution patterns, pollution levels, and associated human health risks of the aforementioned elements. The results showed that the concentrations of Al, Fe, and Mn exceeded the Class III threshold of the Groundwater Quality Standard (GB/T 14848-2017), with the contaminated samples mainly concentrated along the Maguan River. Multivariate statistical analysis revealed that Ni, Mn, and Co primarily originated from natural background and industrial activities; As and Cr6+ were controlled by agricultural pollution; Cu and Pb were dominated by natural factors; Fe was associated with industrial and human activities; Al originated from industrial and agricultural production; and Zn had complex sources. Pollution assessment indicated that Al, Fe, and Mn were at polluted levels, whereas the other elements were at non-polluted levels, suggesting generally good water quality. Health risk assessment showed that the risk values of all heavy metals were below the maximum acceptable risk level (5 × 10−5 a−1). The total health risk through drinking water exposure was higher for children (2.90 × 10−5) than for adults (2.52 × 10−5); through dermal contact, the total health risk was higher for adults (4.88 × 10−7) than for children (3.63 × 10−8). Drinking water was identified as the primary exposure pathway for heavy metal health risks in the study area. In summary, the groundwater quality in the study area generally meets the standard requirements at the regional scale, but certain local areas exhibit potential contamination risks that deserve attention in subsequent water resource management.Keywords: Karst water, Heavy metals, Characteristics, Health risk assessment, Shentou spring areaSubject terms: Environmental sciences, Environmental social sciences

Introduction

Karst water in northern China circulates as relatively independent units, forming a series of spring areas of varying scales, which serve as important guarantees for regional industrial production, agricultural irrigation, and residential water supply. The karst water systems in northern China possess both resource significance and environmental vulnerability: on one hand, spring areas often assume regional water supply functions; on the other hand, their recharge, runoff, and discharge processes are strongly controlled by the heterogeneity of karst media and structural conditions, such that once environmental issues occur, their impact range can expand rapidly. Karst groundwater plays an irreplaceable role in ensuring regional water resource security; therefore, its water quality evolution and pollution risk identification have always been important topics in the field of karst hydrogeology1,2.

Compared with porous aquifers and general fractured aquifers, karst aquifer systems exhibit stronger heterogeneity, anisotropy, and multi-scale connectivity. The underground flow network composed of fractures, solution fissures, and conduits allows precipitation, surface water, and exogenous pollutants to enter the groundwater system through rapid infiltration and preferential flow paths, resulting in fast pollutant transport, short retention times, and strong spatial–temporal fluctuations3. Previous studies have pointed out that karst aquifers, due to their thin surface soil cover, limited filtration capacity, and well-developed multi-scale flow paths, are often more susceptible to agricultural, industrial, and emerging contaminants than other types of aquifers; pollution events can also spread to springs or water supply wells in a short time through underground pathways, thereby amplifying environmental and health risks3–5.

In some karst areas of northern China, the groundwater system is further overprinted by multiple anthropogenic disturbances such as coal mining, industrial emissions, and agricultural activities, forming a typical “karst water–coal mining–human activity” composite environment. Mine drainage, wastewater leakage, and hydrochemical disturbances in goafs generated by mining activities can alter the redox conditions and dissolution environment of groundwater, promoting the migration and enrichment of Fe, Mn, and other potentially toxic elements6–8. Meanwhile, agricultural fertilization, pesticide use, and rural domestic emissions may also enter the groundwater system through rapid infiltration processes in karst areas. Studies have shown that mining activities have long-term impacts on groundwater and ecosystems, and acidic mine drainage or mining-related water bodies are particularly prone to forming surface–underground linked pollution in karst environments, increasing the exposure risk of metal elements6,9,10.

Heavy metals are among the most concerning pollutant types in groundwater environments, characterized by persistence, non-degradability, bioaccumulation, and potential toxicity. Their occurrence and transport in water bodies are not only controlled by geochemical background but also significantly respond to human activity disturbances. In recent years, research on heavy metal pollution in groundwater has gradually expanded from concentration characteristic identification to pollution assessment, source apportionment, and health risk assessment11–13. Commonly used methods include correlation analysis, principal component analysis, pollution index methods, and the USEPA health risk model9. Although these methods provide effective tools for identifying regional groundwater pollution, their application in karst areas faces some common challenges14, such as insufficient representation of temporal variability by single sampling, the lack of a one-to-one correspondence between statistical results and actual pollution sources, and insufficient consideration of constraints from complex geological–hydrogeological backgrounds15. Therefore, for heavy metal issues in karst groundwater, a comprehensive analysis path of “concentration distribution–pollution assessment–source constraint–health risk” needs to be established.

The Shentou Spring area is located in northern Shanxi Province and is one of the typical large-scale karst spring areas in northern China, possessing both regional water supply functions and a significant anthropogenic disturbance background. This area not only develops Cambrian and Ordovician carbonate aquifers but has also long been affected by multiple factors such as coal mining, river leakage, agricultural activities, and residential water extraction, demonstrating strong regional representativeness. However, compared with studies on the general characteristics of karst water systems in northern China and heavy metal issues in mining area groundwater, systematic work on the spatial distribution, potential sources, and health risk assessment of heavy metals in the Shentou Spring area—a typical “northern karst spring area–coal mining activity coupled system”—is still lacking. In particular, how different source factors jointly influence the distribution pattern of metal elements in groundwater under the constraints of karst hydrological processes requires further identification.

Based on this, this study takes the Shentou Spring area as the research object, collects 53 karst water samples in September 2023, selects ten heavy metal elements (As, Cr6+, Al, Cu, Pb, Zn, Ni, Mn, Co, and Fe) that pose significant health risks to humans for systematic analysis, and combines spatial distribution characteristics, single-factor pollution index, Nemerow comprehensive pollution index, multivariate statistical analysis, and health risk assessment methods to explore the distribution characteristics, potential sources, and possible impacts on human health of heavy metal elements in the study area. The aim is to provide a regional baseline understanding and preliminary risk identification of heavy metal pollution in the Shentou Spring area, offer basic data for groundwater pollution prevention and water resource management in northern karst spring areas, and serve as a reference for environmental research in similar karst–mining composite systems.

Materials and methods

Study area

The Shentou Spring area is located in northern Shanxi Province, China, geographically covering Shuocheng District, Pinglu District, Shanyin County, and Youyu County of Shuozhou City, as well as Zuoyun County of Datong City and Ningwu County and Shenchi County of Xinzhou City, with a total area of approximately 4756 km2 (Fig. 1). This spring area is situated at the northern margin of the Datong Basin and is one of the typical large-scale karst spring areas in northern China, with its groundwater system playing an important role in regional water resource supply. More than a hundred spring outlets are developed in the spring area, exhibiting dispersed discharge characteristics and mainly distributed along the Yuanzi River, a tributary of the Sanggan River, and its banks, reflecting a typical karst groundwater discharge pattern.

Location of the Shentou spring area in China (a) and the sampling sites (b).

The overall topography of the Shentou Spring area shows a pattern of mountains on the north, west, and south sides and an alluvial-diluvial plain in the east, with terrain gradually decreasing from the mountainous areas to the basin. The regional climate is a cold-temperate monsoon continental climate, with low annual precipitation and uneven spatial–temporal distribution. Precipitation is mainly concentrated in summer, while winter and spring are characterized by scarce precipitation and strong evaporation.

The strata in the study area include the Cambrian, Ordovician, Carboniferous, Permian, Triassic, Jurassic, Cretaceous, and Quaternary systems. Among these, the Cambrian and Ordovician carbonate rocks are the main aquifers within the spring area, constituting the main body of the karst aquifer system. Fractures, solution fissures, and caves are well-developed in the carbonate rocks, forming multi-level interconnected groundwater flow channels, resulting in significant heterogeneity and strong hydraulic conductivity of the groundwater. Controlled by structure, precipitation and surface water can rapidly infiltrate into the system through karst fractures and river leakage sections. Due to the lack of effective attenuation by soil filtration processes, the transport capacity of pollutants increases once they enter the groundwater, and the environmental risk correspondingly rises.

The Shentou Spring area is a typical “water–coal coexistence” region, with widely distributed coal mines and a long history of large-scale mining. Mine drainage generated during coal mining can enter the karst aquifer through river leakage, goaf water conduction, and borehole recharge, thereby affecting the hydrochemical environment of groundwater. Previous studies have shown that metal elements such as Fe and Mn in mining area groundwater systems are often closely related to coal mining activities, with their transport processes controlled by redox conditions and water–rock interactions10,16. In addition, agricultural activities and residential water supply wells are widely distributed in the spring area. Agricultural fertilization and pesticide use may lead to the input of elements such as As into the groundwater system, while corrosion of old well pipes may also affect local Fe concentrations. Therefore, the sources of heavy metals in groundwater in the study area exhibit obvious multi-source superposition characteristics.

In summary, the Shentou Spring area possesses both the complexity of karst aquifer systems and the disturbance characteristics of coal mining. Its groundwater environment evolution is controlled by both natural geological conditions and human activities, making it a typical area for studying heavy metal pollution and health risk issues in northern karst regions. Thus, systematic analysis of the distribution characteristics and source mechanisms of heavy metals in groundwater in this area has significant scientific importance and practical application value.

Collection of water samples and laboratory analysis

To systematically identify the spatial distribution characteristics of heavy metal elements in karst groundwater of the Shentou Spring area, field sampling was conducted in September 2023, with a total of 53 sampling sites established (Fig. 1). The sampling sites cover the main discharge areas of the spring area, areas along rivers, residential water supply wells, and areas near some mining districts, reflecting the spatial differences in groundwater quality under different hydrogeological units and human activity impacts. It should be noted that this study is based on single-sampling data, and the results are primarily used to characterize the spatial pattern and regional baseline characteristics of heavy metal distribution in groundwater in the study area, rather than to reflect annual dynamic changes. Therefore, the relevant conclusions should be understood under this data condition, and future validation through multi-period monitoring is still needed.

During sampling, pre-cleaned polyethylene sampling bottles were used. Before formal sampling at each site, the sampling bottle was rinsed three times with the original water to reduce cross-contamination. Approximately 500 mL of water sample was collected for each sample. For water samples used for heavy metal analysis, they were filtered on-site (0.45 μm filter membrane), transferred into acid-washed bottles, and acidified with ultrapure nitric acid (HNO₃) to pH < 2 to prevent metal element adsorption or precipitation during storage. All samples were stored in a dark environment at low temperature (approximately 4 °C) and sent to the laboratory for analysis within 24–48 h.

The heavy metal elements (As, Cr6+, Al, Cu, Pb, Zn, Ni, Mn, Co, and Fe) in water samples were determined using inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry (ICP-MS, Model iCAP Q, Thermo Fisher Scientific).

Strict quality control measures were implemented during the experimental analysis, including blank samples, parallel samples, and spiked recovery experiments. The relative standard deviations (RSD) for each heavy metal element were all less than 5%, and the spiked recovery rates ranged from 90 to 110%, indicating good precision and accuracy of the analytical results. For data below the detection limit, half of the detection limit (1/2 DL) was used as a substitute to avoid bias in statistical analysis results17.

Before multivariate statistical analysis, all heavy metal concentration data were standardized (Z-score transformation) to eliminate the influence of dimensional differences among different variables. Normality tests were also conducted on the data, and log-transformation was applied to variables with skewed distributions to meet the requirements of statistical analysis.

Multivariate statistical analysis method

To analyze the interrelationships among heavy metal elements in groundwater and their potential sources, Pearson correlation analysis and principal component analysis (PCA) were used for statistical processing of the data. Correlation analysis was used to identify linear relationships among different elements, while PCA was used to extract the main factors controlling variable changes through dimensionality reduction, thereby providing a reference for identifying pollution sources. It should be noted that statistical analysis results only reflect correlations or co-variation characteristics among variables, and their interpretation requires comprehensive analysis combined with regional hydrogeological conditions and human activity backgrounds, rather than serving as the sole criterion for pollution sources18–21.

Before PCA, all heavy metal concentration data were standardized (Z-score transformation) to eliminate the influence of dimensional differences. Subsequently, the Kaiser–Meyer–Olkin (KMO) test and Bartlett’s sphericity test were used to assess the suitability of the data for factor analysis. The KMO value measures the strength of correlations among variables; when KMO > 0.5, the data are suitable for PCA. Bartlett’s test examines whether the correlation matrix is significantly different from an identity matrix; when the significance level P < 0.05, it indicates correlations among variables, making dimensionality reduction appropriate.

The principal components were extracted using the eigenvalue-greater-than-one rule (Kaiser criterion) and rotated using the Varimax rotation method to improve the interpretability of factor loadings. Variables with high loadings on each principal component were used to infer their possible controlling factors. Generally, when the absolute value of the loading of a variable on a principal component is greater than 0.7, it indicates a strong correlation between the variable and that principal component; loadings between 0.5 and 0.7 indicate moderate correlation22.

It should be emphasized that PCA and correlation analysis are statistical methods, and their results only reflect co-variation relationships among variables. Because different pollution sources may have similar geochemical characteristics, it is difficult to uniquely identify pollution sources based solely on statistical results. Therefore, in this study, the identification of pollution sources will be comprehensively interpreted in combination with regional geological background, land use types, and coal mining activities.

From the perspective of statistical distribution characteristics, most heavy metal elements exhibited a right-skewed distribution, meaning that a few high-value sampling points had a greater impact on the overall distribution. This distribution pattern is common in karst groundwater systems and is usually related to local pollution inputs or differences in hydrogeological conditions.

Comparing the measured concentrations of each heavy metal with the Class III water quality standards of the Groundwater Quality Standard (GB/T 14848-2017)37, the results show that most heavy metal elements were generally within the standard limits in the study area groundwater, but some elements exceeded the standards at individual sampling points. Specifically, Fe, Al, and Mn exceeded the Class III standard limits at some sampling points, with Fe having a relatively high exceedance rate, indicating significant local enrichment. Among them, Al exceeded the standard at 10 sampling points (Fig. 2, with a maximum of 528 μg/L, 2.64 times the Class III standard; Fe exceeded the standard at 7 sampling points (Fig. 2), with a maximum of 1960 μg/L, 6.53 times the Class III standard; Mn exceeded the standard at 1 sampling point, with a concentration of 167 μg/L, 1.67 times the Class III standard.

Spatial distributions of metal element in the Shentou spring area μg/L.

Sampling points with Al content exceeding the standard were mainly distributed along the Maguan River and Yuanzi River, as well as in parts of the eastern and western spring areas; Fe exceedance points were sporadically distributed along the Maguan River and in the western spring area. The Shentou Spring area is a typical “water–coal coexistence” karst water system, with widely distributed coal mines and a long mining history. Coal mine drainage often carries high concentrations of Al, Fe, Mn, and other metal elements during discharge. These water bodies can enter the karst aquifer through river leakage, goaf water conduction channels, and boreholes, thereby altering the hydrochemical environment of groundwater. The coastal area of the Maguan River is a coal mine concentration area; therefore, mine drainage and surface sewage may enter the karst aquifer through river leakage or boreholes, leading to local enrichment of Al, Fe, and Mn.

In the eastern and western parts of the spring area, agricultural activities are relatively concentrated. Long-term application of pesticides and fertilizers may lead to the accumulation of arsenic and other trace metal elements in the soil. Under rainfall conditions, these pollutants can enter the groundwater system through surface runoff or karst infiltration processes. In addition, this area is a carbonate rock exposed area with a thin surface soil cover layer and limited filtration capacity; pollutants can more easily enter the karst aquifer through fractures, resulting in locally higher Al concentrations. Furthermore, rusting of well pipes in some residential water supply wells in the eastern spring area may be a major cause of Fe exceedance in individual water samples.

In contrast, the overall concentrations of As, Cr6+, Cu, Pb, Zn, Ni, and Co were relatively low, approaching or slightly exceeding the standard limits at only a few sampling points. Overall, most heavy metal elements in the groundwater of the study area meet the Class III water quality standards at the regional scale, but there is a certain degree of heavy metal exceedance risk in local areas, exhibiting obvious spatial heterogeneity.

The coefficient of variation (CV) can indicate the dispersion of heavy metal element concentrations at each sampling point. The larger the CV, the greater the dispersion, indicating greater spatial distribution differences of the element38. In the karst water of the Shentou Spring area, the CVs of six heavy metal elements—Cr6+, Cu, Pb, Zn, Mn, and Fe—exceeded 1% (Table 5, with Mn having the largest CV of 2.780, indicating high dispersion and large spatial distribution differences for these six heavy metal elements. The CVs of As, Al, Ni, and Co were less than 1, indicating relatively stable concentration distributions for As, Al, Ni, and Co; Ni had a CV of 0.450, indicating relatively small spatial distribution differences.

Multivariate statistical analysis of heavy metal elements

Correlation analysis

The results of the correlation analysis of heavy metal elements in karst groundwater samples from the Shentou Spring area are shown in Fig. 3. Overall, there were certain correlations among different heavy metal elements, but the correlations varied significantly. Among them, the correlation coefficient between As and Cr6+ was 0.873 (P < 0.01), indicating a significant positive correlation, suggesting that the two may have similar migration behavior in the groundwater system or be controlled by similar environmental factors. The correlation coefficient between Cu and Pb was 0.945 (P < 0.01), showing a strong positive correlation, possibly reflecting their synergistic variation characteristics under local conditions. In addition, Ni, Mn, and Co all showed significant positive correlations (P < 0.01), indicating that these elements may be influenced by similar factors in hydrogeochemical processes. Al showed significant correlations with Cr6+ and Fe, possibly related to regional geochemical background or water–rock interactions. In contrast, Zn did not show significant correlations with other heavy metal elements, and its spatial distribution varied greatly, indicating that the sources or controlling mechanisms of Zn may be more complex. It should be noted that correlation analysis only reflects statistical relationships among variables and cannot be used alone to determine pollution sources; its interpretation requires comprehensive judgment combined with regional hydrogeological conditions and other analytical methods.

Principal component 1 (PC1) showed strong correlations with Ni, Mn, and Co, indicating that this component primarily reflects the information of these three elements. Consistent with the correlation analysis, Ni, Mn, and Co exhibited highly significant positive correlations among each other, suggesting that they share identical or similar sources. Except for site S28, the concentrations of these three elements were relatively similar across all sampling sites. Carbonate rocks, especially dolomite, may contain high concentrations of Mn, and Co is commonly associated with Mn39. Carbonate rocks are widely distributed in the spring area and serve as the primary aquifer for groundwater. Accordingly, it is inferred that the distribution of Ni, Mn, and Co in the study area is primarily controlled by regional geochemical background. The elevated Mn concentration at site S28 is mainly attributed to coal mining activities. In summary, the concentrations of Ni, Mn, and Co in the karst groundwater of the spring area are jointly influenced by geochemical background and coal mining activities.

Principal component 2 (PC2) showed strong correlations with As and Cr6+, indicating that this component primarily reflects the information of these two elements. As is widely present in the spring area, with higher concentrations observed in agricultural zones. The maximum As concentration (2.67 μg/L) was recorded at site S24, suggesting that As mainly originates from pesticides and fertilizers used in agricultural activities28,40,41. The detection rate of Cr6+ in the karst groundwater samples was 66.04% (Table 2), and the detected sites were mainly distributed in agricultural areas. Therefore, it is inferred that As and Cr6+ are primarily associated with substances such as pesticides used in agricultural activities.

Principal component 3 (PC3) showed strong correlations with Cu and Pb, indicating that this component primarily reflects the information of these two elements. The spring area is rich in coal resources. When groundwater flows through coal deposits, water–rock interactions occur, releasing substantial amounts of heavy metals such as Cu and Pb, suggesting that Cu and Pb are mainly influenced by regional geochemical background10.

Principal component 4 (PC4) showed strong correlations only with Fe, and thus can be identified as representing Fe-related information. Numerous coal mining enterprises are distributed in the central part of the spring area, where coal resources are abundant. Mine drainage generated during coal mining recharges the karst groundwater through pathways such as river leakage and poorly sealed boreholes, leading to elevated Fe concentrations in the karst water2. In the eastern part of the spring area, where domestic drinking water wells are mainly located, the high Fe concentrations in karst groundwater are primarily associated with rusting of well casings.

Overall, the sources of heavy metals in the groundwater of the study area are characterized by pronounced multi-source superposition. Under karst hydrogeological conditions, agricultural activities, domestic activities, and coal mining collectively govern the distribution patterns of the various heavy metal elements.

Nemerow comprehensive pollution index (PN)

According to the calculation results of the Nemerow comprehensive pollution index (Table 8), the overall pollution level of groundwater in the study area ranged from clean to lightly polluted. The PN values of most sampling points were less than 1, indicating clean water quality; some sampling points had PN values between 1 and 2, belonging to light pollution; only a few sampling points had relatively high PN values, showing moderate pollution. These high-value points were mainly affected by single high-concentration pollution factors, especially local enrichment of elements such as Fe, Al, and Mn. Overall, the pollution level of groundwater in the study area is low at the regional scale, but local areas exhibit certain pollution characteristics due to elevated concentrations of individual elements.

Health risk assessment of heavy metal elements in groundwater

To evaluate the potential health risks of heavy metals in groundwater to humans, the health risk assessment model recommended by the USEPA was used to calculate and analyze the annual health risks (Table 9) of ten heavy metal elements (As, Cr6+, Al, Cu, Pb, Zn, Ni, Mn, Co, Fe) in karst water of the Shentou Spring area via drinking water and dermal contact exposure pathways.

For the drinking water exposure pathway, the health risks for adults and children followed the same order, both showing that As and Cr6+ contributed the most, followed by Co and Al, with the remaining elements having lower risks. The total health risk for children was approximately 1.15 times that for adults, indicating that children are more sensitive than adults under the same environmental exposure conditions.

From the perspective of carcinogenic risk, As contributed the most significantly among all heavy metal elements. The annual health risk values of carcinogens (As, Cr6+) were 2–4 orders of magnitude higher than those of non-carcinogens (Al, Cu, Pb, Zn, Ni, Mn, Co, Fe), indicating that the risk level of carcinogens is mainly controlled by their carcinogenic potency factors, and their potential harm to human health far exceeds that of non-carcinogens42,43. Specifically, the annual health risks of the carcinogen As for adults and children ranged from 1.53 × 10−7 to 1.85 × 10−6 and from 1.76 × 10−7 to 2.14 × 10−6, respectively. The risk values at all sampling points were below the maximum acceptable risk level recommended by the International Commission on Radiological Protection (ICRP) (5 × 10−5 a−1), but some sampling points exceeded 1 × 10−6 a−1—the maximum acceptable risk level adopted by the Swedish Environmental Protection Agency, the Dutch Ministry of Housing, Spatial Planning and the Environment, and the Royal Society of the United Kingdom. The risks of the carcinogen Cr6+ for adults and children ranged from 3.80 × 10−8 to 7.98 × 10−7 and from 4.37 × 10−8 to 9.18 × 10−7, respectively, all below 1 × 10−6 a−1.

For the dermal contact pathway, the order of health risks for adults and children was also consistent: As > Cr6+ > Co > Al > Zn > Mn > Fe > Cu > Ni > Pb. The total health risk for adults was approximately 13.43 times that for children, mainly due to their larger exposed surface area and longer exposure time. All risk values for this pathway were below 1 × 10−6 a−1.

Comparing the two exposure pathways, drinking water intake is the main exposure pathway for heavy metals in groundwater, contributing the most to health risk. Therefore, in groundwater resource management, priority should be given to addressing the risks associated with the drinking water pathway. Overall, the health risks of heavy metals in groundwater in the study area are at a low level at the regional scale. The fluctuation of risk values at each sampling point reveals the heterogeneity of pollutant distribution and the resulting assessment uncertainty12. Nevertheless, the risk values of As and Cr6+ at some sampling points are still relatively high. Previous studies have shown that long-term exposure to As in groundwater may produce health cumulative effects; moreover, the risk assessment results of Cr6+ show considerable disagreement among different studies due to the toxicity differences among its various species11,12. In summary, in subsequent water resource management and risk prevention and control, special attention should be paid to the two high-risk elements, As and Cr6+.

Although this study systematically analyzed the distribution characteristics, potential sources, and health risks of heavy metals in groundwater of the Shentou Spring area, certain limitations remain. First, this study was based on single sampling data from September 2023 and did not reflect the dynamic variation characteristics of the karst groundwater system under different hydrological conditions. Therefore, the results of this study should be understood as a regional baseline understanding and phase-specific characterization of heavy metal distribution in groundwater in the study area, and future validation through multi-period continuous monitoring is needed. Second, regarding heavy metal source apportionment, although this study conducted a comprehensive interpretation combining PCA results with regional hydrogeological conditions, statistical methods themselves have certain limitations. Different pollution sources may have similar geochemical characteristics, making it difficult to achieve unique identification of pollution sources based solely on statistical analysis. Therefore, subsequent studies should combine tracer tests, isotopic methods, or quantitative land use data to further constrain pollution sources.

Conclusion

The concentrations of heavy metal elements in karst groundwater of the Shentou Spring area are generally low, with significant differences among different elements. Fe, Al, and Mn show relatively high concentrations at some sampling points, exhibiting certain spatial heterogeneity, while the overall concentrations of As, Cr6+, Cu, Pb, Zn, Ni, and Co are low, with relatively high values appearing only at individual sampling points.

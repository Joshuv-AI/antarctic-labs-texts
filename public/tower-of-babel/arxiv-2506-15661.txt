Anisotropic Josephson coupling of d d -vectors arising from interplay with frustrated spin textures
Grayson R. Frazier, Junyi Zhang, Yi Li

Abstract
We propose a Cooper pair analogue of Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya’s anisotropic coupling for d d -vectors in an inhomogeneous unconventional superconductor, which can arise when itinerant electrons couple to a local exchange field of frustrated spin moments. Using perturbative Green’s function methods, we examine an s s - d d model on a geometrically frustrated lattice with noncollinear spin texture that breaks time-reversal and parity symmetries, leading to spin-triplet pairing correlations. The effective Josephson tunneling in the presence of such an exchange field leads to an anisotropic Josephson coupling between d d -vectors, which prefers a spatially inhomogeneous pairing order. Furthermore, we discuss anomalous vortices arising from the spatially inhomogeneous d d -vector in the absence of a magnetic field for nonunitary triplet pairing order. We also propose a Josephson diode effect in which the diode efficiency is proportional to the spin chirality of the local exchange field. Our results can help understand the experiments in superconducting proximitized Mn 3 Ge and in 4 4 Hb-TaS 2 .

Contribution to free energy from a spatially varying order parameter
–
We first discuss the kinetic energy term accounting for the energy cost associated with the spatial variation of a spin triplet superconducting pairing order.
Generally, the free energy can be separated into a contribution from a homogeneous pairing order and gradient terms associated with the cost of spatial variations of the pairing order, which is related to the system’s superfluid stiffness.
In this work, we discuss
additional anisotropic couplings in the free energy, which
promote a spatially varying
d
-vector texture.
These anisotropic couplings can arise in the presence of a frustrated magnetic texture that breaks parity and time reversal symmetries.

We provide a heuristic overview of the kinetic energy contribution to the free energy and relate it to the Josephson coupling.
Consider an inhomogeneous superconductor in which the pairing order parameter can vary in space.
For a conventional
s
-wave superconductor in absence of an external field, the free energy contribution from an inhomogeneous order parameter is given by
F_{\mathrm{variation}}=\int\mathrm{d}^{d}r~\gamma|\boldsymbol{\nabla}\psi(\mathbf{r})|^{2}

\psi(\mathbf{r})
being the order parameter and

related to the superfluid stiffness.
In the discrete limit, this corresponds to weakly coupled superconducting grains on the order of the superconducting coherence length, where the pairing order parameter is defined locally for each grain.
There is Josephson coupling at the interface between adjacent grains which contributes to the free energy, and the system will reorient the local pairing order parameter at each grain to minimize the total Josephson free energy.
For an
s
-wave superconducting order parameter, the discretized free energy is described by the Josephson coupling of neighbouring grains,
\sum_{\langle nm\rangle}J_{nm}\psi_{n}\psi^{*}_{m}+\mathrm{c.c.}

Here,
\psi_{n}
corresponds to the order parameter of the
n^{\mathrm{th}}
grain, and
J_{nm}
corresponds to the Josephson coupling, which microscopically can be described by the tunneling of Cooper pairs between adjacent grains

and

For a conventional
s
-wave superconductor in the absence of external fields or disorder, the lattice-version of the superfluid stiffness,
J_{nm}

v_{s,j}=\frac{\hbar}{m^{*}}\Big(\partial_{j}\phi(\mathbf{r})-i\hat{\mathbf{d}}^{*}(\mathbf{r})\cdot\partial_{j}\hat{\mathbf{d}}(\mathbf{r})\Big).

(10)

The latter contribution plays the role of a gauge connection and is nonvanishing for nonunitary pairing orders, for which
\hat{\mathbf{d}}(\mathbf{r})\times\hat{\mathbf{d}}^{*}(\mathbf{r})\neq 0
.
Thus, coupling to a frustrated local exchange field promotes a spatially inhomogeneous
d
-vector textures that lead to nontrivial contributions to the superfluid velocity.

Analogous to Mermin-Ho vortices
46
, which arise from nontrivial circulation of the
l
-vector in superfluid
3
He-A, nontrivial circulation of the
d
-vector can support coreless vortices.
The nontrivial circulation of velocity, in the absence of a magnetic field, is given by
47
;
28

\displaystyle(\boldsymbol{\nabla}\times\mathbf{v}_{s})_{i}

\displaystyle=-i\frac{\hbar}{m^{*}}\epsilon_{ijk}\partial_{j}\hat{\mathbf{d}}^{*}(\mathbf{r})\cdot\partial_{k}\hat{\mathbf{d}}(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\hbar}{m^{*}}\epsilon_{ijk}\epsilon_{abc}\hat{s}_{a}\partial_{j}\hat{s}_{b}\partial_{k}\hat{s}_{c},

(11)

as shown in S.M.
S6
.
Here,
\hat{s}=i\hat{\mathbf{d}}\times\hat{\mathbf{d}}^{*}/|\hat{\mathbf{d}}\times\hat{\mathbf{d}}^{*}|
is the real unit vector pointing in the local direction of the spin of the Cooper pair.
As such, even in the absence of an external magnetic field or singularities in the
\mathrm{U}(1)
phase, the nontrivial spatial texture of the
d
-vector arising from the anisotropic exchange in Eq. (
1
) can lead to the formation of anomalous vortices.
Namely, the DM-like coupling can promote noncollinear antiunitary
d
-vectors across neighbouring grains, giving rise to, for example, skyrmion-like defects of the
d
-vector which manifest as vortices in the superfluid velocity.

Lastly, we propose that an superconductor-insulator-superconductor (SIS) Josephson junction, in which the barrier region has nonvanishing spin chirality, will display a Josephson diode effect proportional to the spin chirality.
From the Ambegokaor-Baratoff formalism, the first order Josephson critical current at zero voltage bias is given by

I_{J}^{(n,m)}=i\frac{e}{\hbar}\left(\mathcal{J}_{nm}-\mathrm{c.c.}\right).

Here,
n
and
m
label the superconductors at either side of the barrier, and
I_{J}^{(n,m)}
describes the supercurrent from the
n
to
m
.
The directional dependent contribution to the Josephson supercurrent is given by

\Delta I_{C}=I_{J}^{(n,m)}-I_{J}^{(m,n)}\propto\sum_{i\in\Sigma_{n},j\in\Sigma_{m}}\frac{J_{sd}^{3}}{t_{0}\Delta_{0}}\chi_{ijk},

(12)

which is proportional to the scalar spin chirality
\chi_{ijk}
given in Eq. (
8
).
The difference in critical current leads to a hysteretic Josephson critical current and offers a direct probe of the spin chirality in the barrier region.

Intuitively, the Josephson diode effect arising from spin chirality breaks parity and time reversal symmetries.
The antisymmetric nature of the spin chirality under parity,
\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{i}\cdot(\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{j}\times\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{k})=-\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{k}\cdot(\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{j}\times\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{i})
, implies a direction-dependent tunneling amplitude.
However, the parity breaking symmetry alone is insufficient to produce the Josephson diode effect in the present scenario.
Crucially, spin chirality is a scalar quantity and contributes to the spin-independent effective tunneling of itinerant electrons.
As a result, the tunneling process explicitly breaks time reversal symmetry, which is an essential ingredient for realizing the Josephson diode effect
48
.

Conclusion and Discussion
–
We studied how a frustrated spin texture couples with itinerant electrons that form an unconventional spin triplet superconducting pairing order.
We first found that, analogous to Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya’s anisotropic superexchange, anisotropic Josephson couplings between
d
-vectors of adjacent sites can contribute to the free energy.
They arise when the effective tunneling breaks time reversal symmetry—for example, when the itinerant electrons forming the superconducting condensate are coupled to frustrated local spin moments.
In an inhomogeneous superconductor consisting of multiple grains, the effective tunneling from the presence of a local exchange field leads to a “pliability” of the pairing order that competes with the superfluid stiffness, so that a spatially varying
d
-vector texture is energetically favored.
The inhomogeneous
d
-vector texture can lead to novel phenomena, including the formation of anomalous vortices and a Josephson diode effect.

We emphasize that the results of this work are independent of the specific model discussed.
Namely, the Josephson couplings in Eq. (
1
)
can be obtained based on symmetry principles in Ginzburg-Landau theory, provided that the effective tunneling between superconducting grains breaks time reversal symmetry.
The physical principles developed in this work serve as a useful starting point for understanding nontrivial spatial textures of unconventional pairing order parameters in such systems.

Our results are pertinent to recent experimental demonstration of the coexistence of frustrated magnetic textures and superconductivity.
For example, in Mn
3
Ge, noncollinear spin textures act as an intrinsic exchange field, leading to DM-like Josephson couplings that favor inhomogeneous
d
-vector textures.
As seen in Eq. (
10
), these textures can contribute to the superfluid velocity and can offer a possible explanation to the long-range Josephson currents observed experimentally in proximitized Mn
3
Ge
1
.
We furthermore propose that Mn
3
Ge
can be used in a Josephson junction to realize the Josephson diode effect.
By applying a magnetic field, the
120^{\circ}
ordered spin configuration is canted out of plane, generating nonvanishing spin chirality and therefore a finite Josephson diode effect.

Moreover, our findings can offer insight into superconducting
4
Hb-TaS
2
.
The noncollinear spin textures in the
1
T-TaS
2
layers can lead to the anisotropic Josephson couplings in Eq. (
1
) and thereby generate inhomogeneous
d
-vector textures in superconducting
4
Hb-TaS
2
.
These textures can persist in zero field, leading to spontaneous vortex formation with small net pairing magnetization, as shown in Eq. (
11
).
The dynamics of the local spin moments
in the
1
T-TaS
2
layers
and their effect on the superconducting pair correlations remain a future avenue to be explored.

Josephson form factor and free energy

S1.1
Josephson form factor

We briefly review the Josephson form factor, which describes the Josephson coupling between two superconducting grains.
A more detailed derivation based on the Ambegaokar-Baratoff formalism of the Josephson current
34
;
36
can be found in, for example, Ref.
37
.

Consider two superconducting grains connected by a weak link.
The tunneling across the barrier between grains
n
and
m
can be expressed as

[H_{T}]_{nm}=\sum_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime};\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}\left(c^{\dagger}_{n,\mathbf{k},\alpha}[T_{nm}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})]_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}c_{m,\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime}}+\mathrm{h.c.}\right),

(S1)

in which
c_{n,\mathbf{k},\alpha}
is the annihilation operator for an electron at grain
n
with momentum
\mathbf{k}
and spin
\alpha
.
The tunneling matrix element between grains
n
and
m
generally takes the form

T_{nm}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})=T_{nm;0}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\sigma^{0}+\mathbf{T}_{nm}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma},

in which
\boldsymbol{\sigma}=(\sigma^{x},\sigma^{y},\sigma^{z})^{\mathrm{T}}
are the Pauli matrices and
T_{nm;0}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})
and
\mathbf{T}_{nm}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})
correspond to spin-independent and spin-dependent tunneling processes at the grain boundary.
The latter can arise from, for example, spin-orbit coupling or time-reversal breaking fields at the interface.

The Josephson form factor describes the tunneling of Cooper pairs and corresponds to the anomalous part of the current-current correlation function.
At zero voltage bias, the form factor is given by

\mathcal{J}_{nm}=-\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{i\omega_{n}}\sum_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\mathrm{Tr}\Big[\mathcal{F}_{n}(i\omega_{n};\mathbf{k})[T_{nm}(i\omega_{n};-\mathbf{k},-\mathbf{k}^{\prime})]^{\mathrm{T}}[\mathcal{F}_{m}^{\dagger}(i\omega_{n};\mathbf{k}^{\prime})]^{\mathrm{T}}T_{nm}(i\omega_{n};\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\Big],

(S2)

in which the trace is taken over internal degrees of freedom of the Cooper pair (
e.g.
spin, sublattice,
etc.
), and the summation is taken over fermionic Matsubara frequencies.
Here,
\mathcal{F}_{n}(i\omega_{n};\mathbf{k})
is the anomalous Green’s function of grain
n
.
Focusing on the spin degrees of freedom, the anomalous Green’s function can be expanded into its spin singlet and spin triplet components as

\mathcal{F}_{n}(i\omega_{n};\mathbf{k})=\Big(f_{n,0}(\mathbf{k})+\mathbf{f}_{n}(\mathbf{k})\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}\Big)i\sigma^{y}.

(S3)

Substituting into Eq. (
S2
), the trace can be expressed as three distinct contributions,

\mathrm{Tr}\Big[\cdots\Big]=2\Big(C_{\mathrm{sing}-\mathrm{sing}}+C_{\mathrm{sing}-\mathrm{trip}}+C_{\mathrm{trip}-\mathrm{trip}}\Big),

(S4)

which correspond to the Josephson tunneling between spin singlet components, spin triplet components, and a mixture of the two respectively.
The three contributions to the Josephson form factor are given by

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{sing}-\mathrm{sing}}=

\displaystyle-f_{n,0}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime})f^{*}_{m,0}(\mathbf{k})T_{nm;0}(-\mathbf{k},-\mathbf{k}^{\prime})T_{nm;0}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})+f_{n,0}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime})f^{*}_{m,0}(\mathbf{k})\Big[\mathbf{T}_{nm}(-\mathbf{k},-\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\cdot\mathbf{T}_{nm}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\Big]

(S5a)

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{sing}-\mathrm{trip}}=

\displaystyle\Big[\mathbf{f}_{n}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\cdot\mathbf{T}_{nm}(-\mathbf{k},-\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\Big]f^{*}_{m,0}(\mathbf{k})T_{nm;0}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})+f_{n,0}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime})T_{nm;0}(-\mathbf{k},-\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\Big[\mathbf{f}_{m}^{*}(\mathbf{k})\cdot\mathbf{T}_{nm}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\Big]

(S5b)

\displaystyle-T_{nm;0}(-\mathbf{k},-\mathbf{k}^{\prime})f^{*}_{m,0}(\mathbf{k})\Big[\mathbf{f}_{n}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\cdot\mathbf{T}_{nm}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\Big]+f_{n,0}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime})T_{nm;0}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\Big[\mathbf{f}_{m}^{*}(\mathbf{k})\cdot\mathbf{T}_{nm}(-\mathbf{k},-\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\Big]

\displaystyle+if_{n,0}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\mathbf{f}_{m}^{*}(\mathbf{k})\cdot\Big[\mathbf{T}_{nm}(-\mathbf{k},-\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\times\mathbf{T}_{nm}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\Big]+if^{*}_{m,0}(\mathbf{k})\mathbf{f}_{n}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\cdot\Big[\mathbf{T}_{nm}(-\mathbf{k},-\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\times\mathbf{T}_{nm}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\Big]

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{trip}-\mathrm{trip}}=

\displaystyle T_{nm;0}(-\mathbf{k},-\mathbf{k}^{\prime})T_{nm;0}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\Big[\mathbf{f}_{n}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\cdot\mathbf{f}_{m}^{*}(\mathbf{k})\Big]+\Big[\mathbf{T}_{nm}(-\mathbf{k},-\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\cdot\mathbf{T}_{nm}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\Big]\Big[\mathbf{f}_{n}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\cdot\mathbf{f}_{m}^{*}(\mathbf{k})\Big]

(S5c)

\displaystyle+iT_{nm;0}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\mathbf{T}_{nm}(-\mathbf{k},-\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\cdot\Big[\mathbf{f}_{n}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\times\mathbf{f}_{m}^{*}(\mathbf{k})\Big]+iT_{nm;0}(-\mathbf{k},-\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\mathbf{T}_{nm}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\cdot\Big[\mathbf{f}_{n}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\times\mathbf{f}_{m}^{*}(\mathbf{k})\Big]

\displaystyle-\Big[\mathbf{f}_{n}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\cdot\mathbf{T}_{nm}(-\mathbf{k},-\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\Big]\Big[\mathbf{f}_{m}^{*}(\mathbf{k})\cdot\mathbf{T}_{nm}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\Big]-\Big[\mathbf{f}_{n}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\cdot\mathbf{T}_{nm}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\Big]\Big[\mathbf{T}_{nm}(-\mathbf{k},-\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\cdot\mathbf{f}_{m}^{*}(\mathbf{k})\Big].

In the current work, we exclusively focus on the effective Josephson coupling between spin triplet correlations in adjacent grains; hence, we only consider the last contribution.
In the following, we consider the case that

\mathbf{T}_{nm}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\neq-\mathbf{T}_{nm}(-\mathbf{k},-\mathbf{k}^{\prime})
, which can be realized when tunneling occurs in the presence of a local exchange field breaking time-reversal symmetry, as discussed in the main text.

We now consider a simplified scenario to illustrate the microscopic origin of the effective Josephson coupling between spin triplet superconductors.
Suppose that the spin and orbital degrees are decoupled, and that the anomalous Green’s function can be decomposed as

\mathcal{F}_{n}(i\omega_{n};\mathbf{k})=(\hat{d}_{n,0}+\hat{\mathbf{d}}_{n}\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma})g_{n}(i\omega_{n};\mathbf{k}),

(S6)

in which
g_{n}(i\omega_{n};\mathbf{k})
encodes the orbital degrees of freedom and
d_{n,0}
and
\mathbf{d}_{n}
describe the normalized spin singlet and spin triplet contributions respectively.
We further assume that the tunneling process conserves momentum and satisfies
T_{nm}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})=T_{nm}\delta_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}
, with
T_{nm}
being momentum-independent.
As such, the Josephson form factor describing the Josephson coupling between two spin triplet pairing order parameters is given by

\mathcal{J}_{nm}^{\mathrm{trip}-\mathrm{trip}}=w(E_{n},E_{m};\beta)\bigg\{\left(T_{nm;0}^{2}+\mathbf{T}_{nm}\cdot\mathbf{T}_{nm}\right)\hat{\mathbf{d}}_{n}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{d}}_{m}^{*}+\left(2iT_{nm;0}\mathbf{T}_{nm}\right)(\hat{\mathbf{d}}_{n}\times\hat{\mathbf{d}}_{m}^{*})-\left(\mathbf{T}_{nm}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{d}}_{n}\right)\left(\mathbf{T}_{nm}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{d}}_{m}^{*}\right)\bigg\},

(S7)

in which

w(E_{n},E_{m};\beta)=-\frac{2}{\beta}\sum_{i\omega_{n}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}g_{n}(i\omega_{n};\mathbf{k})g_{m}^{*}(i\omega_{n};\mathbf{k})

(S8)

includes the thermal weighting and is dependent on the BdG quasiparticle energies
E_{n}
and
E_{m}
at grains
n
and
m
.
In general, the summation over momentum is nonvanishing when the pairing order parameters of both grains transform under rotation according to the same irrep.
As such, the Josephson form factor between spin triplet superconductors can be compactly expressed as

\mathcal{J}_{nm}^{\mathrm{trip}-\mathrm{trip}}=2\Big(J_{nm}\hat{\mathbf{d}}_{n}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{d}}_{m}^{*}+{\mathbf{D}}_{nm}\cdot(\hat{\mathbf{d}}_{n}\times\hat{\mathbf{d}}_{m}^{*})+\sum_{a,b}\hat{d}_{n}^{a}{\Gamma}_{nm}^{ab}\hat{d}_{m}^{b*}\Big),

(S9)

in which

\displaystyle{J}_{nm}

\displaystyle=w(E_{n},E_{m};\beta)\left(T_{nm;0}^{2}+\mathbf{T}_{nm}\cdot\mathbf{T}_{nm}\right),

(S10)

\displaystyle{\mathbf{D}}_{nm}

\displaystyle=2iw(E_{n},E_{m};\beta)T_{nm;0}\mathbf{T}_{nm},

\displaystyle{\Gamma}_{nm}^{ab}

\displaystyle=-2w(E_{n},E_{m};\beta)T_{nm}^{a}T_{nm}^{b}.

The effective Josephson couplings
{J}_{nm}
,
{\mathbf{D}}_{nm}
, and
{\Gamma}_{nm}
correspond to the Heisenberg-like term, Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya-like antisymmetric term, and symmetric anisotropic coupling term respectively, as discussed in Eq. (
1
) in the main text.
It should be noted that, here,
T_{nm}
corresponds to the microscopic tunneling processes across the entire interface between grains
n
and
m
.

We emphasize that the Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya-like antisymmetric coupling arises from time reversal symmetry breaking in the effective tunneling between superconducting grains.
The antisymmetric Josephson coupling cannot arise, for example, through spin-orbit coupling alone, under which
T_{mn}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})=-T_{mn}(-\mathbf{k},-\mathbf{k}^{\prime})
.
Rather, the tunneling requires, for example, the presence of a local exchange field or impurities at the interface that break time reversal symmetry.
Lastly, if the spin and orbital degrees of freedom of the anomalous Green’s function cannot be decoupled, it is necessary to consider the summation over the orbital degrees of freedom.
Nonetheless, the key qualitative features, such as the anisotropic Josephson exchange, remain robust and can be captured phenomenologically.

S1.2
Josephson free energy

We next review the Josephson free energy and relate to the Josephson form factor.
From the Ambegaokar-Baratoff formalism, the Josephson tunneling current at zero voltage bias between two grains labelled by
n
and
m
is given by
34
;
36
;
37

I_{J}^{(n,m)}=i\frac{e}{\hbar}\Big(\mathcal{J}_{nm}-\mathrm{c.c.}\Big),

(S11)

with
\mathcal{J}_{nm}
being the Josephson form factor in Eq. (
3
) of the main text.
Explicitly separating the
\mathrm{U}(1)
phase from the superconducting pairing correlations, it follows that

I_{J}^{(n,m)}=i\frac{e}{\hbar}(\mathcal{J}_{nm}e^{i\Delta\phi_{nm}}-\mathrm{c.c.}),

with
\Delta\phi_{nm}=\phi_{n}-\phi_{m}
being the difference in the
\mathrm{U}(1)
phases of the pair condensate at the two grains.

To compare, the first order Josephson coupling in Ginzburg-Landau theory between the
n^{\mathrm{th}}
and
m^{\mathrm{th}}
grains is given by

F_{nm}[\Delta\phi_{nm}]=F_{J}e^{i\Delta\phi_{nm}}+\mathrm{c.c.}

(S12)

in which
F_{J}
is the real-valued Josephson coupling amplitude.
The corresponding Josephson current is given by

I_{J}^{(n,m)}=\frac{2e}{\hbar}\frac{\partial F_{nm}}{\partial\Delta\phi_{nm}}=i\frac{2e}{\hbar}\Big(F_{J}e^{i\Delta\phi_{nm}}-\mathrm{c.c.}\Big)

(S13)

Comparison to the Josephson current in Eq. (
S11
) shows that the Josephson free energy can be expressed in terms of the Josephson form factor,

F_{nm}\sim\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathcal{J}_{nm}+\mathrm{c.c.}\right).

(S14)

The relation to the Josephson form factor reflects the fact that the form factor describes the microscopic tunneling processes which underlie the Josephson coupling between grains.
Hence, the Josephson form factor naturally appears in the free energy describing spatial variations of the superconducting order parameter.

Effective tunneling

In this section, we derive the effective tunneling of itinerant electrons in the presence of a local exchange field comprised of frustrated spin moments.
We focus on the effective tunneling between neighbouring sites

Consider the Hamiltonian

The corresponding Green’s function in frequency space is given by

Let

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}=i\omega-\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{-1}=\mathcal{H}_{0}+\Sigma-\Sigma\mathcal{G}_{0}\Sigma+\Sigma\mathcal{G}_{0}\Sigma\mathcal{G}_{0}\Sigma-\cdots

(S34)

Now, we consider the off-diagonal matrix elements describing the effective tunneling processes between nearest neighbouring sites.

(S36)

Above,

We have used the fact that

We employ the following identities of the Pauli matrices,

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}=\delta_{ij}+i\epsilon_{ijk}\sigma_{k},

\displaystyle t_{0}\sigma^{0}-J_{sd}^{2}[\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{kin}}(i\omega)]_{ij}\alpha_{ij}\sigma^{0}-iJ_{sd}^{2}[\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{kin}}(i\omega)]_{ij}(\boldsymbol{\beta}_{ij}\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma})

\displaystyle-iJ_{sd}^{3}\sum_{k}[\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{kin}}(i\omega)]_{ik}[\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{kin}}(i\omega)]_{kj}\chi_{ijk}\sigma^{0}+J_{sd}^{3}\sum_{k}[\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{kin}}(i\omega)]_{ik}[\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{kin}}(i\omega)]_{kj}\left(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{ijk}\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}\right).

in which the factors,

\begin{gathered}\alpha_{ij}\equiv\mathbf{s}_{i}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{j};\hskip 20.00003pt\boldsymbol{\beta}_{ij}\equiv\mathbf{s}_{i}\times\mathbf{s}_{j};\\

Case 1: Collinear Spins

For the first case, suppose all local spins are in a ferromagnetic state,

(S42)

Case 3: Nonvanishing spin chirality

Lastly, we consider the case where the three

To highlight the role of the

Consider the spin triplet pairing order described by

In the following, we assume that the orbital angular momentum is not conserved and does not contribute to the superfluid velocity.
The pairing is defined as unitary if it satisfies

Complete genome sequence of Marinobacterium marisflavi strain IMCC4074 isolated from coastal seawater
Authors: Meora Rajeev, Yeonjung Lim, Ilnam Kang, Jang-Cheon Cho
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesMembers of the genus Marinobacterium are widely distributed in marine environments and contribute to diverse ecological processes; however, genomic information for several species remains limited. In this study, we report the complete genome sequence of Marinobacterium marisflavi strain IMCC4074ᵀ, originally isolated from coastal seawater of the Yellow Sea, to provide insights into its genomic features, metabolic potential, and environmental adaptation.Data descriptionThe genome of strain IMCC4074ᵀ was sequenced using a combination of short- and long-read sequencing approaches and assembled into a single circular chromosome of 3,091,487 bp with a G + C content of 52.45%. Genome annotation revealed 2,974 protein-coding sequences, 15 rRNA genes, and 60 tRNA genes. Phylogenomic analyses confirmed its taxonomic placement within the genus Marinobacterium, while comparative genomic analysis revealed sufficient divergence (ANI ≤ 95%) from previously described species within the genus, supporting its distinction at the species level and representing the first genome report for this species. The availability of the complete genome provides a resource for understanding the potential ecological roles and adaptive strategies of M. marisflavi in marine ecosystems.Keywords: Marinobacterium marisflavi, Coastal seawater, Marine microbes, Complete genome sequence

Objective

The genus Marinobacterium, a member of the order Oceanospirillales within the class Gammaproteobacteria, comprises Gram-negative, aerobic, motile, rod-shaped bacteria that are widely distributed in marine environments. This genus was first described by González et al. in 1997, with M. georgiense designated as the type species, based on isolates obtained from marine pulp mill effluents [1]. As of April 2026, the genus Marinobacterium encompasses 23 validly published species according to the List of Prokaryotic Names with Standing in Nomenclature (LPSN; https://lpsn.dsmz.de), including recently described species such as M. weihaiense, M. marinum, and M. sedimentorum [2, 3]. Members of this genus have been isolated from a wide range of marine habitats, including seawater, coastal and deep-sea sediments, tidal flats, coastal sands, mangrove rhizospheres, and coral mucus [4–7]. Species of Marinobacterium are known for their metabolic versatility, including the ability to utilize diverse organic compounds and participate in marine biogeochemical cycles. Some members have also been reported to degrade aromatic hydrocarbons [8].

Despite the growing number of validly described species within the genus Marinobacterium, no high-quality genome sequence for M. marisflavi is currently available. In this study, we report the complete genome sequence of M. marisflavi strain IMCC4074ᵀ.

Data description

M. marisflavi strain IMCC4074ᵀ was originally isolated from surface seawater collected off Incheon Port in the Yellow Sea, Republic of Korea (37°19′N, 126°40′E), using a standard spread-plating method on Marine Agar 2216 (MA; Difco). Detailed phenotypic and biochemical characteristics of the strain were previously described [9]. Briefly, the strain is a Gram-negative, strictly aerobic, slightly halophilic, motile rod that grows optimally at 30 °C and pH 7.0–8.0. Phylogenetic analysis based on the 16 S rRNA gene sequence placed the strain within the genus Marinobacterium.

For genome sequencing, high-quality genomic DNA was extracted from cultures grown aerobically on MA at 30 °C using the DNeasy Blood & Tissue Kit (Qiagen, Germany), following the manufacturer’s instructions. DNA quantity and quality were assessed by agarose gel electrophoresis and using a Qubit 2.0 fluorometer (Life Technologies, USA). Whole-genome sequencing was performed using a combination of short-read sequencing on the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform (2 × 150 bp paired-end; Illumina, CA, USA) and long-read sequencing on the PacBio RS II platform (P6-C4 chemistry; Pacific Biosciences Inc., CA, USA), generating approximately 680× and 342× genome coverage, respectively. Sequencing was conducted at DNA Link, Inc. (Seoul, Republic of Korea).

Raw sequencing reads were subjected to quality assessment using MultiQC v1.11 [10]. Low-quality Illumina reads and adapter sequences were filtered using BBDuk v38.71 [11]. PacBio long reads were quality-filtered using the default Pacific Biosciences filtering parameters (read quality ≥ 0.75 and read length ≥ 50 bp). High-quality reads were subsequently used for de novo hybrid genome assembly with Unicycler v0.4.8 [12], integrating both Illumina short reads and PacBio long reads. The assembly resulted in a single circular chromosome of 3,091,487 bp with an average G + C content of 52.45% (Data file 1). Assembly quality was assessed using QUAST v5.1.0 [13], which revealed a complete, contiguous assembly with an N50 value of 3,091,487 bp and an L50 of 1. Genome completeness and contamination were further evaluated using CheckM2 v1.0.1 [14], which indicated a genome completeness of 100% with minimal contamination of 0.35% (Data file 1). Genome annotation was conducted using the DRAM pipeline v1.4.6 [15], identifying a total of 2,974 protein-coding sequences, in addition to 15 rRNA genes and 60 tRNA genes. Genome annotation further identified genes encoding central carbon metabolic pathways, including glycolysis, the pentose phosphate pathway, the tricarboxylic acid cycle, the Entner-Doudoroff pathway, and the glyoxylate shunt, as well as components of a complete aerobic respiratory chain, consistent with the aerobic heterotrophic lifestyle of strain IMCC4074ᵀ. The genome map was visualized, illustrating the circular chromosome structure and genomic features (Data file 2).

For phylogenomic analysis, genome sequences of all available type strains representing different species within the genus Marinobacterium were retrieved from the NCBI database. A phylogenomic tree was constructed using RAxML v8.2.12 with the PROTGAMMAAUTO model [16], based on a concatenated alignment of 120 universal single-copy bacterial marker proteins identified using GTDB-Tk v2.4.0 [17]. The genome of Halomonas elongata (GCA_000196875.2) was used as the outgroup to root the tree. The resulting phylogeny placed strain IMCC4074ᵀ within the genus Marinobacterium (Data file 3), with M. littorale, M. lutimaris, M. mangrovicola, M. lacunae, M. ramblicola, and M. zhoushanense identified as its closest relatives [18]. Branch support was evaluated using 100 bootstrap replicates. Comparative genomic analysis using pairwise ANI, calculated with PyANI v0.3.0 [19], revealed values of ≤ 95% relative to other validly published Marinobacterium species (Data file 4), supporting its genomic distinctiveness as a separate species. Basic genomic characteristics of strain IMCC4074ᵀ and its closest phylogenetic relatives are summarized (Data file 5). Compared with the related species, IMCC4074ᵀ possesses a relatively smaller genome size and lower G + C content. This complete genome sequence will serve as a foundational resource for further ecological and functional studies of M. marisflavi.

Limitations

This study provides a genome-level characterization of M. marisflavi IMCC4074ᵀ; however, comprehensive functional and metabolic analyses are beyond the scope of this study and will require further investigation. Furthermore, the analysis is based on a single isolate, which limits the ability to assess intraspecies genomic diversity and ecological variability within the species M. marisflavi. Additional isolates and comparative genomic analyses are needed to better understand population-level variation and adaptive strategies.

Rethinking tau‐targeted therapy in Alzheimer's disease: Toward a dual biomarker strategy
Authors: Charlotte E. Teunissen, Lorenzo Pini, Bruno P. Imbimbo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTau pathology is more closely associated with cognitive deterioration than amyloid burden in symptomatic Alzheimer's disease (AD), yet tau‐targeted trials have often interpreted decreases in soluble phosphorylated tau (p‐tau) as evidence of therapeutic success. Emerging data argue for a more biology‐informed framework. P‐tau262 and p‐tau356 identify sites within soluble pre‐tangle tau assemblies, and cerebrospinal (CSF) p‐tau262 may decline as neurofibrillary pathology advances. Conversely, marked p‐tau217 lowering with posdinemab was not accompanied by clinical benefit. The amyloid field offers a useful but incomplete analogy. Amyloid plaques are extracellular whereas tau misfolding and fibrillization occur predominantly intracellularly, so equivalent relationships among imaging, fluid biomarkers, and clinical outcomes should not be assumed. A recent preprint describing a functional plasma assay of pathologically active tau reported high discrimination of tau positron emission tomography (PET) positivity, particularly at early tau PET stages, illustrating how seeding‐related activity may add information beyond p‐tau concentration. We propose a dual‐domain framework in which tau‐targeted therapies are evaluated using complementary measures of aggregated tau burden and biologically defined soluble tau states. P‐tau262 is one candidate within the soluble domain, but treatment‐induced increases should be considered favorable only when they accompany aggregate reduction and evidence of a less pathogenic soluble state. This framework applies across antibodies, antisense oligonucleotides, and other biologic tau‐directed strategies. Future trials should test joint biomarker–clinical response functions rather than importing thresholds from anti‐amyloid therapy, and should prespecify adequately powered analyses of sex, population background, disease stage, and other potential modifiers.

A BIOLOGY‐INFORMED PROBLEM FOR TAU THERAPEUTICS

Tau pathology is more tightly linked to cognitive deterioration than amyloid plaque burden in symptomatic Alzheimer's disease (AD). For this reason, tau has long been viewed as an attractive disease‐modifying target. However, the field has often assumed that a successful tau‐targeted therapy should lower soluble tau biomarkers. 1 Recent findings suggest that this assumption is too simple. Islam et al. identified phosphorylate tau (p‐tau)262 and p‐tau356 within the core of soluble pre‐tangle tau assemblies (STAs), rather than in the peripheral “fuzzy coat” recognized by more established markers such as p‐tau181, p‐tau217, and p‐tau231. 2 Contemporary coverage of these findings emphasized the potential value of detecting biologically active tau species before dense tangles are established. 3 These observations place p‐tau262 near an early aggregation transition and motivate a framework in which a biomarker is interpreted according to the tau state it represents, not simply according to whether its concentration rises or falls.

WHAT DIFFERENT TAU BIOMARKERS MAY REPRESENT

p‐tau262, pre‐tangle assemblies, and sequestration

P‐tau262 immunoreactivity preferentially marks granular prefibrillar pathology in pre‐neurofibrillary tangles (NFTs), while the corresponding cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) assay for STAs correlates with NFT burden, tau positron emission tomography (PET) uptake, and cognitive decline independently of amyloid beta (Aβ). 2 Islam et al. localized p‐tau262 and p‐tau356 to the aggregation‐relevant core of soluble pre‐tangle assemblies, supporting the idea that these epitopes report a molecular state closer to early tau assembly than conventional p‐tau markers concentrated in the peripheral fuzzy coat. 2 This distinction is potentially important for therapeutic monitoring because a marker can be highly associated with AD biology without necessarily moving monotonically with later‐stage fibrillar burden.

At the 2026 International Conference on Alzheimer's and Parkinson's Diseases and Related Neurological Disorders meeting, Fernando Gonzalez‐Ortiz reported that CSF p‐tau262 falls as AD worsens. 4 Although this latter observation remains conference‐level evidence, it is consistent with a model in which declining CSF p‐tau262 does not necessarily indicate less tau pathology but could reflect redistribution or sequestration of p‐tau262–containing tau into less soluble intracellular aggregates. Importantly, this interpretation does not establish that all soluble p‐tau262 is physiological or beneficial. A rise during treatment would be meaningful only if the molecular species being restored is shown to be non‐seeding or otherwise functionally permissive; total p‐tau262 concentration alone cannot resolve that question.

p‐tau217 is biologically informative but not a universal toxicity readout

In the fetal and early postnatal brain, p‐tau participates in microtubule dynamics, neurite outgrowth, axonal remodeling, and neuroplasticity, and high tau phosphorylation can occur without NFT formation. 5 , 6 , 7 Plasma p‐tau217 is markedly higher in healthy newborns than in teenagers, young adults, older adults, and even patients with AD, then declines during the first months of life. 5 These observations support a context‐dependent interpretation: tau phosphorylation can be physiological during development but pathological in AD when coupled to altered processing, impaired clearance, conformational change, aggregation, and seeding. Thus, the same numerical change in a p‐tau biomarker may have different mechanistic meanings across disease states, ages, compartments, and interventions.

Concentration‐based versus functional tau biomarkers

Recent work further broadens the biomarker concept from abundance to pathological activity. In a 2026 medRxiv preprint, Hanseeuw et al. evaluated VeraBIND Tau, a plasma assay designed to isolate pathologically active p‐tau and measure its ability to bind recombinant normal tau, a functional readout related to seeding competence. 8 In 145 cognitively unimpaired and impaired participants characterized with tau PET, the assay predicted tau PET positivity with 94.2% sensitivity, 96.1% specificity, and an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.97. 8 Its positive predictive value in cognitively unimpaired participants was 85.9%, and the advantage over plasma p‐tau217 was driven particularly by early Braak‐like tau PET stages 1 to 3 (AUC, 0.96 vs. 0.74; P = 0.003). Cross‐sectional values and annual changes were also associated with cognition and entorhinal tau PET signal. 8

Interpretation: Current evidence suggests that concentration changes in soluble p‐tau should not be interpreted uniformly as therapeutic benefit. The Hanseeuw et al. study is especially relevant to the present hypothesis because it separates the amount of p‐tau from a property linked to its pathological activity. The authors also note that plasma p‐tau217 can be elevated with isolated Aβ pathology and may be less specific for incipient aggregated tau in cognitively unimpaired individuals. 8 Because this report is a preprint, uses a novel proprietary assay, and requires independent replication, its performance estimates should not be treated as clinically validated. Nevertheless, the study illustrates a general principle: for tau, functional state or seeding‐related activity may add information that is not captured by the concentration of a single phosphorylated epitope.

THE AMYLOID ANALOGY: USEFUL CONCEPT, DIFFERENT BIOLOGY

Previous trials of anti‐tau monoclonal antibodies have demonstrated target engagement and reductions in CSF total tau or p‐tau without significant slowing of cognitive decline. 1 These outcomes have invited comparison to the history of anti‐Aβ drug development and the initially discordant relationship between biomarker target engagement and clinical outcomes. 9 In a meta‐regression of 24 randomized trials involving 25,966 participants, Abanto et al. found that increases in CSF Aβ42 and decreases in Aβ PET were independently associated with slower decline on cognitive and clinical measures. 10 By contrast, soluble Aβ40 changes were not associated with efficacy, suggesting that the identity of the soluble species matters rather than soluble peptide abundance in general. 11 This parallel with early anti‐amyloid failures has also been proposed explicitly for anti‐tau development. 12 Newer antibodies such as lecanemab and donanemab combine substantial amyloid lowering with statistically significant, although modest, slowing of clinical decline. 13 , 14

That comparison is conceptually informative but cannot be transferred mechanistically one to one. Aβ plaques are extracellular deposits that are directly accessible to circulating antibodies, whereas tau misfolding and fibrillization occur predominantly within neurons; extracellular tau is only one component of propagation. Fluid concentrations therefore reflect a mixture of release, secretion, cleavage, transport, clearance, cell injury, and compartmental exchange, while tau PET primarily reflects fibrillar aggregate burden. The mathematical relationship among target engagement, a fluid tau analyte, tau PET, and cognition may consequently differ from that among soluble Aβ42, amyloid PET, and clinical outcomes.

Accordingly, the amyloid experience should be treated as a hypothesis‐generating template rather than a calibration curve for tau. The transferable lesson is that a successful disease‐modifying intervention may need convergent evidence that the pathological deposit is changing and that the relevant soluble biology is moving toward a more favorable state. Which soluble tau state is favorable, and in which compartment, remains an empirical question.

LESSONS FROM TAU‐TARGETED CLINICAL TRIALS

Posdinemab: lowering p‐tau217 is not sufficient

Johnson & Johnson announced in November 2025 that the Auτonomy study would be discontinued because posdinemab, an antibody selective for p‐tau217, did not significantly slow clinical decline. 15 Conference reporting subsequently indicated marked reductions in plasma and CSF p‐tau217 despite a clinical trajectory similar to placebo. 16 This experience shows that lowering a p‐tau analyte is not, by itself, evidence that the disease‐driving tau process has been corrected. A biomarker may fall because production, secretion, release, or accessible epitope abundance falls, without a commensurate reduction in intracellular toxic or seeding‐competent tau.

Bepranemab and diranersen broaden the therapeutic test

Bepranemab is a humanized immunoglobulin G 4 antibody that recognizes tau amino acids 235 to 250 near the microtubule‐binding region. In the 80‐week TOGETHER study of 450 participants with early AD, the primary Clinical Dementia Rating Sum of Boxes (CDR‐SB) endpoint was not met in the overall population. 17 , 18 Compared to placebo, however, reported analyses showed 33% to 44% less tau accumulation in whole cortical gray matter and 21% to 25% less decline on the Alzheimer's Disease Assessment Scale Cognitive subscale. In selected subgroups defined by lower baseline tau burden or apolipoprotein E (APOE) ε4 non‐carrier status, high‐dose bepranemab was associated with larger signals, including 63% less tau accumulation and 29% less CDR‐SB progression, with favorable Mini‐Mental State Examination and Alzheimer's Disease Cooperative Study Activities of Daily Living trends. 17 , 18 These subgroup findings are exploratory and require confirmation, but they are consistent with the possibility that stage and baseline tau burden modify the relationship between aggregate reduction and clinical response. CSF p‐tau262 was not measured, so no inference can be made about whether a change in that soluble tau state accompanied the tau PET effect.

Diranersen is an intrathecal antisense oligonucleotide targeting microtubule‐associated protein tau messenger RNA and therefore tests the proposed framework outside immunotherapy. In a 36‐week study involving 46 participants with mild AD, diranersen dose‐dependently reduced CSF total tau by 56% and p‐tau181 by 51%, with reduced tau PET accumulation relative to placebo. 19 Top‐line results from the 76‐week CELIA dose‐ranging study in 416 participants with early AD later indicated reductions in brain tau burden and soluble CSF p‐tau species without meeting the primary CDR‐SB endpoint, although numerical advantages were reported across dose groups. 20 The largest numerical CDR‐SB difference was reported with the lowest dose, a pattern that could reflect chance, a non‐linear exposure–response relationship, or consequences of excessive tau lowering that require formal testing. The inclusion of an antisense strategy is important because it separates the general question of what biomarker pattern predicts benefit from tau modification from the narrower question of antibody target engagement.

A DUAL‐DOMAIN BIOMARKER HYPOTHESIS FOR TAU‐DIRECTED THERAPIES

We therefore propose a dual‐domain rather than a single‐analyte framework for biologic tau‐targeted therapies, including antibodies, antisense oligonucleotides, RNA‐based approaches, and other strategies that alter tau production, conformation, aggregation, or propagation. The first domain is insoluble or aggregated tau burden, currently measured most directly in vivo with tau PET. The second domain is the biologically relevant soluble tau state, which may require a panel rather than one marker.

Within the second domain, p‐tau262 is an attractive candidate because it marks aggregation‐relevant pre‐tangle assemblies. 2 However, a treatment‐induced increase in CSF p‐tau262 should not automatically be labeled beneficial. It would support the proposed model only if it accompanies reduced aggregate burden and can be shown to represent a less seeding‐competent or more functionally permissive soluble pool. This is a deliberately stricter formulation than the original analogy with CSF Aβ42: the desired direction of change for a tau analyte cannot be specified without first identifying the biochemical tau state and the compartment from which it derives.

Assays that distinguish monomer‐enriched from assembly‐associated p‐tau262, or that directly quantify seeding‐related activity, could test this distinction. Tau oligomers can be detected in human CSF, and experimental fractionation approaches can separate oligomeric from monomeric tau species. 21 , 22 The functional plasma approach described by Hanseeuw et al. provides another, conceptually different way to ask whether circulating tau retains pathological binding activity. 8 Preclinical studies likewise show that epitope choice can alter an antibody's ability to block seeding and propagation. 23 , 24 , 25 A future composite pharmacodynamic model could therefore combine tau PET change with one or more soluble‐state measures rather than assuming a universal p‐tau direction of benefit.

Other soluble tau modifications may add orthogonal information. CSF tau acetylated at position 174 (AcTau174) has been proposed as an early modification that impairs tau turnover and promotes accumulation, and recent clinical work suggests prognostic value beyond conventional CSF tau measures. 26 , 27 Together with p‐tau262 and functional seeding assays, such markers could help distinguish a non‐specific fall in soluble tau from a biologically meaningful shift away from pathogenic aggregation.

PRECISION‐MEDICINE CONSIDERATIONS: SEX AND POPULATION DIFFERENCES

Biomarker‐response relationships should not be assumed to be identical across populations. The CLARITY AD subgroup estimates for lecanemab differed by sex, and subsequent analyses have argued that the observed male–female difference may be unlikely to arise from random subgroup imbalance alone, while still cautioning that these findings do not establish absence of benefit in women. 28 Independently, plasma p‐tau217 has shown sex‐specific associations with cognitive decline and brain atrophy in cognitively unimpaired adults. 29

Population diversity also matters for biomarker validation. A large mixed memory clinic study including patients from 91 countries found strong p‐tau217 performance without a detectable loss of diagnostic accuracy by ethnicity, but emphasized representative recruitment and the potential influence of medical conditions such as impaired kidney function. 30 These data are reassuring but do not eliminate the need for diverse validation, because biomarker concentrations, comorbidities, access to confirmatory imaging, and treatment response may differ across settings. Future tau trials should therefore prespecify adequately powered interaction or stratified analyses by sex, ancestry or population background, age, disease stage, APOE genotype, and clinically relevant comorbidities, and should evaluate whether biomarker thresholds require calibration across these strata.

IMPLICATIONS FOR FUTURE TRIALS

Future tau‐targeted trials should measure longitudinal tau PET together with a panel of soluble biomarkers selected for mechanistic relevance. At minimum, studies testing the present hypothesis should include CSF p‐tau262, established p‐tau analytes, and biochemical fractionation or functional measures of tau assembly or seeding. Sampling should be sufficiently frequent to establish temporal ordering: target engagement should precede changes in soluble tau state, which should in turn be related prospectively to subsequent tau PET and clinical trajectories. The strongest evidence would come from prespecified within‐trial mediation, latent‐change, or joint longitudinal models showing that a specific multivariate biomarker pattern predicts later clinical benefit after accounting for baseline tau burden and treatment exposure.

Trial design should also test stage dependence. Participants with low or intermediate tau burden may retain a larger modifiable pool of soluble and early‐aggregate tau, whereas advanced intracellular NFT pathology may be less reversible. Rather than importing fixed efficacy thresholds from amyloid trials, tau studies should derive treatment–response functions empirically and determine whether the relationship between biomarker change and clinical outcome is linear, thresholded, stage dependent, or differs by therapeutic mechanism.

LIMITATIONS

This proposal remains hypothetical. The reported decline of CSF p‐tau262 with worsening AD is not yet supported by a full peer‐reviewed longitudinal publication. 4 The posdinemab, bepranemab, and later‐stage diranersen results are partially based on company or conference reporting. 16 , 17 , 18 , 20 The Hanseeuw et al. functional plasma tau study is a preprint and requires independent validation. 8 Most importantly, the biochemical meaning of a change in soluble p‐tau262 during treatment is unresolved: an increase could represent mobilization away from insoluble deposits, but it could also represent redistribution of harmful soluble assemblies. The framework therefore predicts a pattern of convergent changes; it does not validate p‐tau262 as a surrogate endpoint. In addition, sex‐ and population‐specific analyses will require substantially larger samples than conventional subgroup descriptions if they are to distinguish genuine effect modification from chance variation.

CONCLUSION

Tau‐targeted therapy should be judged by the biology it changes, not by the direction of one fluid biomarker. The amyloid field supports the general value of combining measures of deposited pathology with soluble biomarkers, but the extracellular location of Aβ plaques and the predominantly intracellular biology of tau require distinct pharmacodynamic models. p‐tau262 is a compelling candidate marker of pre‐tangle tau biology, while functional assays of pathological tau activity provide a complementary emerging approach. Successful tau‐directed treatment may ultimately be characterized by robust reduction of aggregated tau together with a reproducible shift in soluble tau toward a less pathogenic state (Figure 1). Demonstrating that such a pattern predicts clinical benefit, and determining how it varies by therapeutic mechanism, disease stage, sex, and population, should be priorities for the next generation of tau trials.

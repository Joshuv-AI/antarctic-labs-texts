Cosmology after Phantom Crossing by Horndeski Gravity
Eric V. Linder

Abstract
One possible way to explain the observed effective dark energy equation of state crossing w = − 1 w=-1 (the phantom divide) is through modified gravity. A key point is to not view the expansion history in isolation but to take into account the other gravitational impacts on growth of large scale structure, lensing, etc. Within shift symmetric Horndeski gravity this implies three main paths for the late time cosmic expansion. All require unusual kinetic structure and we analyze their various implications for how w w should behave after phantom crossing.

II
Connecting Cosmic Expansion and Growth

Horndeski gravity provides an excellent framework
for exploring modified gravity, being highly

Theory motivates restriction to shift symmetry in

IV
Dark Energy Domination Naturally

Now we turn to the case of dark energy domination,

\rho_{\rm de}/(3H^{2})\approx 1
. We start with the “natural”
case where the other key ratios are also of order one.
In this case the
\alpha_{B}
terms in both
\rho_{\rm de}
and
P_{\rm de}

are not significant, and

w\approx\frac{-1}{1-2XK_{X}/K}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{B})\ .

(15)

Defining

we require
w<0

n<1/2

(Thus the canonical
K=X

We then see that

dark energy pressure). This class can also cross

w=-1

n

w>-1

n>0
to
n<0

(Although dark energy doesn’t fully dominate when
data suggest the crossing occurs,
z\approx 0.5

one still has
\rho_{\rm de}/(3H^{2})\sim\mathcal{O}(1)
then.)

One might be concerned that

\alpha_{B}

to be simply a k-essence model. Recall that pure
k-essence alone cannot cross the phantom divide and
will have a ghost when the kinetic term is negative.
This does not automatically hold here. The
g

also enters in the no ghost condition,

\alpha_{K}+(3/2)\alpha_{B}^{2}\geq 0

\alpha_{B}

\alpha_{K}

Moreover,

\alpha_{B}

also enter in

the density and pressure are dominated by the kinetic
term the theory and its observational implications
lie outside k-essence.

Moving away from the natural case where all

If

VI
Deviating the Field Evolution

Changing
\ddot{\phi}/(3H\dot{\phi})

\ddot{\phi}\gg 3H\dot{\phi}

rate

be achieved, so such a condition requires
introduction of a new time scale.

Recall that in the standard
Klein-Gordon equation one has the acceleration
term
\ddot{\phi}

3H\dot{\phi}

and the driving term from the steepness of the
potential,
-dV/d\phi

As clearly illustrated in

quintessence case, none of these terms dominate over
the others (unlike in slow roll inflation).
Here we have no potential,
but the driving term arises from
-6g(\dot{H}+3H^{2})

One needs an extra ingredient entering the
driving term, an additional, shorter, time scale
than the expansion time scale
H^{-1}

One example is a mass scale
M

g(X)=\gamma(X)+M\ .

(18)

However this appears problematic as a large

\ddot{\phi}

rate, will eventually lead to a similarly large

\dot{\phi}
and hence a large
\alpha_{B}\sim\dot{\phi}g/H

That will cause large deviations from GR, with

G_{\rm eff}
possibly diverging and instability from
likely negative sound speed of scalar perturbations,

c_{s}^{2}<0
(see
nrg
).
So this case appears problematic.

IX
Conclusions

The beautifully bizarre behavior of dark energy
in rapidly rising in energy density, crossing the
phantom divide, and evolving to a less negative
equation of state than
w=-1
may require a
beautifully bizarre theory. A major class of
physics that can enable this behavior is modified
gravity.

By combining observational and theoretical
motivations, leading to consideration of shift
symmetric, cubic Horndeski gravity, and using the
key strategy of simultaneously incorporating both
cosmic expansion and cosmic structure data
indications – phantom crossing, dark energy
domination, and small deviations from GR –
we have analyzed the restrictions and outcomes
of this class of modified gravity.

We have demonstrated solutions where dark energy
continues to evolve away from
w=-1
, and indeed
can have
w>0
so the dark energy fades away faster
than matter, where it turns around and restores to

w=-1
at late times, or where dark energy will
end up at some value
-1<w<0
(for example, the
“natural” case with
K\sim X^{-1}
, i.e.
n=-1
,
goes to
w=-1/3
), or scaling as matter,

w\approx 0
. The natural case can also provide
the phantom crossing when
n
evolves
from negative to positive.
Table
1
summarizes many of the results.

Note that the existence of phantom crossing
and the nonexistence of large deviations from
GR work together to ensure that both
K
and

G_{3}
terms must appear in the action.
Such shift symmetric, cubic Horndeski theories
are in the class of No Run Gravity
nrg
,
and have some interesting properties. To avoid
Laplace instability in the scalar perturbations,
the sound speed squared must be nonnegative,

c_{s}^{2}\geq 0
, and this implies
\alpha_{B}\geq 0
(see the
Appendix in
nsg
). We see that in many cases
here
\alpha_{B}>0
is also required for positive
energy density.

Note that
\alpha_{B}\geq 0
has the implication that

G_{\rm eff}\geq 1
, as seen from Eq. (
10
)
and
nrg
; indeed, using the stability
condition that
nrg
derived,

0\leq G_{\rm eff}(a)-1\leq\frac{\alpha_{B}(a)}{3\Omega_{m}(a)}\ .

(24)

Thus, in the range best covered by observations,
and in particular around the phantom crossing
time,
G_{\rm eff}-1\ll 1
so
\alpha_{B}\ll 1
is required.

It is interesting that this class of theories
strengthens gravity, giving a clear prediction
for large scale structure surveys. Together
with its requirement of no gravitational slip,
i.e. the behaviors of clustering and of lensing
are closely tied together,
G_{\rm matter}=G_{\rm light}
, this offers
clear tests for shift symmetric, cubic
Horndeski gravity as the physics behind the
phantom crossing.

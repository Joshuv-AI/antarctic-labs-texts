Effect of different obturation techniques on treatment results in single-visit non-surgical endodontic retreatment: randomized controlled clinical study
Authors: Merve Işık, Zeliha Uğur Aydın
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundEvaluating the effect of different obturation techniques on postoperative pain level and radiographic healing on non-surgical endodontic retreatment (NSER) applied in a single visit in teeth with a single root canal and apical periodontitis (AP).Materials and methodsSixty-three teeth from 50 systemically healthy patients (mean age 34.19 ± 9.75 years; 27 women, 23 men) were included in the study. Residual root canal filling materials were removed. All teeth were randomly divided into three groups according to the obturation technique: cold lateral compaction (CLC), continuous wave compaction (CWC), and thermoplasticized gutta-core (TGC). Pain status was evaluated using the Numerical Pain Rating Scale (NRS) in all cases. Periapical index (PAI) scores were recorded in preoperative and postoperative 6-month on the periapical radiographs. Data were analyzed as statistically (IBM, Armonk, NY, USA). The significance level was determined as p < 0.05.ResultsIt was found that more postoperative pain occurred in the 3rd-hour interval after CLC and CWC compared to the 7th-day interval (p < 0.05). There was no difference in postoperative pain between obturation techniques in all evaluated time intervals (p > 0.05). There was no difference in the PAI scores recorded at six months between obturation techniques for all periods (p > 0.05). Similar radiographic improvement was observed in all obturation techniques at 6-months (p > 0.05).ConclusionCLC, CWC, and TGC root canal obturation techniques were found to have similar efficacy after NSER in a single visit in teeth with AP at six months.Trial registrationThe research was retrospectively registered on the website www.clinicaltrials.gov with the registration number NCT06226740 on 26/01/2024.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12903-024-05240-3.Keywords: Apical periodontitis, Non-surgical endodontic retreatment, Postoperative pain, Single visit, Treatment outcome, Thermoplasticized gutta-core

Introduction

Failure in endodontic treatment can occur for many reasons, such as procedural errors, inadequate disinfection, and obturation [1]. When endodontic treatment fails, treatment options can be applied, such as following the related tooth without doing anything, extraction, non-surgical endodontic treatment (NSER), and endodontic surgery [2]. NSER is a frequently preferred treatment option due to its non-invasiveness, low cost, easy application, and high success rate compared to the implant [3]. It is reported that NSER has a success rate ranging from 62 to 91%. It has been found that the success rate in NSER is related to many factors, such as the number of root canals, the presence of preoperative symptoms, intraoperative complications, and root canal obturation [4]. In the literature, it has been reported that the prevalence of apical periodontitis (AP) development in teeth with insufficient root canal obturation is 40–65% [5]. Therefore, the selection and application of the appropriate obturation technique are directly related to the success of the treatment to get healing after NSER [6].

The cold lateral compaction technique (CLC) is widely used in endodontics because it allows controlled placement of gutta-percha in root canals, is low in cost, and does not require technical precision [7]. However, CLC has potential disadvantages such as the inability to achieve homogeneity of obturation for the formation of spaces between the cones, insufficient adaptation to the root canal walls, increased risk of vertical root fracture for the uncontrolled applied force, and inability to deliver the obturation material to areas such as a lateral canal or resorption defect [8]. To overcome these disadvantages, obturation techniques aiming to increase the delivery of gutta-percha to the root canal with the help of continuous wave compaction (CWC) and thermoplasticized gutta-core (TGC), heat have been developed. The literature shows that less apical leakage is seen in canals obturated with heated gutta-percha techniques; root canal obturation is more homogeneous and better adapts to anatomical irregularities. CWC is a hybrid technique that provides root canal obturation with the combined use of ‘down packing’ and ‘backfilling’ processes. In this technique, down packing provides obturation of the apical 5–7 mm of the root canal in the vertical direction using a heated plugger. Backfilling provides obturation of the middle and coronal parts of the root canal with injectable heated gutta-percha [9]. In the TGC technique, root canals are obturated using a plastic or cross-linked gutta-percha carrier covered with alpha-phase gutta-percha, previously softened with heat. Although these techniques require more hand sensitivity, cost, and time than the cold lateral compaction method, they provide a much more homogeneous and impermeable root canal obturation, especially in teeth with complex anatomy [10].

The current study aimed to compare the effects of different obturation techniques on postoperative pain level and radiographic healing in NSER applied in a single visit in teeth with a single root canal and AP. The null hypothesis of the current study is that the difference between the effects of different obturation techniques used in NSER on teeth with a single root canal to postoperative pain and healing of AP will not be different.

Material and method

This randomized controlled clinical study was conducted in accordance with the CONSORT guidelines for reporting clinical trials, ensuring transparency and comprehensive evaluation of the methodology and results.

Ethical considerations

Approval for this study was obtained from the Clinical Research Ethics Committee of Abant İzzet Baysal University (Ethics No: 2021/236). The research was retrospectively registered on the website www.clinicaltrials.gov with the registration number NCT06226740 on 26/01/2024.

Sample size calculation

The sample size was calculated using G*Power software 3.1.2 (Universitat, Düsseldorf, Germany) concerning a recent study’s power analysis [11] in the literature with a similar design, 0.05 probability of alpha error and 80% power (effect size = 0.25). As a result of this calculation, it was determined that a total of at least 42 teeth were statistically required. Considering the possible mishaps that may occur during the follow-up period (relevant tooth extraction, the patient not wanting to continue the study, etc.), it was decided to include 21 teeth in each group, for a total of 63.

Consent to participate

Before starting the study, an informed consent form was prepared, and the treatment procedure was explained in a way that all patients could understand. The treatment procedure was explained verbally to all patients who agreed to participate in the study. Signed informed consent forms were obtained and filed from all patients who agreed to participate in the study.

Randomization

All subjects were assigned to three study arms (CLC/CWC/TGC) with simple randomization (n = 21). A clinician who had not contributed to the study made the randomization centrally using a computer random table generator (http://www.random.org) accessed and each patient received a consecutive number. All participants were blinded to the group allocation.

Clinical Procedure

All procedures were performed by an endodontist (M.I.) only in patients who met the inclusion criteria. All patients were informed about the risks of the study and alternative treatment options before participating. Anaesthesia was achieved with 4% articaine hydrochloride containing 1:100,000 adrenalin. None of the patients required additional anesthesia. After rubber dam isolation, previous caries and restorations were removed with high and low-speed sterile burs under water cooling. The previous root canal obturation of all teeth included in the study was removed using a similar treatment protocol. ProTaper Universal Retreatment file system (Dentsply Maillefer, Ballaigues, Switzerland) was used to remove the previous root canal obturation. While removing the previous root canal obturation material, D1 (30.10) file was used in the coronal section of the canal, D2 (25.04) was used in the middle section of the canal, and D3 (20.07) was used in the apical section of the canal for removing the previous root canal obturation material. All files were used with a torque-controlled endodontic motor VDW.GOLD (VDW, Munich, Germany) based on the appropriate torque and rpm values specified by the manufacturer. After determining the canal length with an electronic apex locator (Woodpecker DTE, Guangxi, China), the apical patency was checked with a #10 K hand file. During the procedure, the canal was continuously irrigated with 5.25% sodium hypochlorite (NaOCl) (Wizard, Guide Chemistry, Istanbul, Turkey). In all irrigation procedures, a 27-gauge side-vented irrigation needle (Endo Eze; Ultradent Products, Inc., South Jordan, UT, USA) was used, set to be 2 mm shorter than the working length. No solvent was used during the NSER protocol. In this study, the final preparation was achieved by using a case-specific file that was chosen to be larger than the apically compressed file while making the final preparation. The root canal was prepared with ProTaper Next X3 (Dentsply Sirona, Ballaigues, Switzerland) and X4 rotary files. For the final irrigation procedure is 2 mL of 5.25% NAOCl, distilled water, 17% ethylene diamine tetraacetic acid (EDTA) (Werax, İzmir, Turkey), distilled water, 2% chlorhexidine (CHX) (ProChex, Promida Co., Turkey) solutions were used respectively. The root canals were dried with paper points (Pearl Dent, Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam).

Cold lateral compaction (CLC)

After the root canal preparation, the working length was checked with the master cone and periapical radiography. The master cone was then covered with root canal sealer (AH Plus; Dentsply, De Trey, Konstaz, Germany) and placed in the root canal. Then, a gap was created for the 0.02 angled lateral cones using #30, 25, and 20 finger spreader. The obturation process continued until the spreader went 2 mm beyond the level of the canal orifice. Next, a hot excavator removed the coronal gutta-percha 1 mm below the canal orifice.

Continuous Wave Compaction (CWC)

After the root canal preparation, the working length was checked with the master cone and periapical radiography. Root canal sealer (AH Plus; Dentsply) was applied to the root canal walls using a master cone. The Buchanan HeatPluggerFine (0.06 taper; SybronEndo/KerrEndodontics, Orange, CA) connected to the down-pack unit of the ElementsFree Cordless Obturation System (SybronEndo/KerrEndodontics, Orange, CA) was adjusted to be 4 mm shorter than the working length. The master cone was cut 1 mm shorter than the working length with a heated plugger and applied to the root canal. Then, the heated plugger was advanced in the apical direction, and the apical region was obturated. Obturation was completed in 2 stages with gutta-percha (SybronEndo/KerrEndodontics) in the Elements gutta-percha cartridge connected to the remaining root canal cavity back-fill unit and heated to 200 °C. Heated gutta-percha was vertically condensed with Buchanan HeatPlugger to 1 in the first stage and 2 in the second. The root canal obturation was completed by removing the gutta-percha 1 mm below the canal orifice.

Thermoplasticized Gutta-Cor (TGC)

After the root canal preparation, the working length was controlled with the master cone and periapical radiography. Root canal sealer (AH Plus; Dentsply) was applied to the root canal walls using a master cone. Root canal obturation was completed with Thermafil gutta-percha (Dentsply Tulsa Dental, Tulsa, OK, USA) heated according to the manufacturer’s instructions using Thermaprep® 2 Oven (Dentsply, Maillefer, Ballaigues, Switzerland). The heated Thermafil gutta-percha was slowly inserted into the canal up to the working length. After cutting the handle of the obturators at the level of the canal orifice with a hot excavator, it was condensed into the canal using a thermoplasticized gutta-percha plugger. The coronal gutta-percha was removed 1 mm below the canal orifice using a hot excavator.

Restorative Procedure and Radiographic follow-up

In the same visit, all teeth were permanently restored using direct composite resin (Estelite Sigma Quick; Tokuyama, Tokyo, Japan). Radiographs of all teeth included in the study were performed with a film holder (SuperBite™; Kerr Dental, California, USA) using the parallel technique before, immediately after, and six months after the NSER procedure.

Evaluation of Radiographic Healing

To evaluate radiographic healing after NSER, Orstavik et al. [14] the periapical index (PAI) system developed was used. Asymptomatic teeth with a PAI score of 1 and 2 were classified as healed, while teeth with a PAI score of 3, 4, and 5 were classified as unhealed.

Evaluation of Postoperative Pain

After the NSER was completed, the ‘Numeric Rating Scale-NRS’ was used for postoperative pain monitoring. This method, frequently used in determining the severity of pain, was preferred because it allows the patient to easily express the pain with the numbers on the scale. In this scale,

0: No pain,

0: No pain,

1-2-3: Mild pain,

1-2-3: Mild pain,

4-5-7: Moderate pain,

4-5-7: Moderate pain,

8–10: Severe pain [15].

8–10: Severe pain [15].

Before starting the treatment, the pain status of the patients was questioned, and a marking was made on the NRS form. Pain statuses were determined to NSR before, immediately after, 3rd, 24th, 48th hour and 7th day of NSER. Patients were also informed to apply to our clinic in case of severe pain. Patients reported their pain levels by marking them on a pain scale.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics version 21.0 software (IBM, Armonk, NY, USA). The conformity of the data to the normal distribution was evaluated with the Shapiro-Willk test. The chi-square test was used to evaluate demographic data. T-test was used for independent samples for in-group comparison of PAI and NRS values according to gender, and One-Way ANOVA and post-hoc Tukey were used to compare groups. The chi-square test was used to compare the PAI change between groups, and the paired T-test was used to evaluate the within-group PAI change. Changes in lesion size over time for all obturation techniques tested were evaluated by repeated measures ANOVA analysis. A P value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Discussion

Endodontic treatment success depends on the principles of chemomechanical preparation, effective disinfection, and three-dimensional obturation of the root canal system [16]. Dow and Ingle [17] reported that teeth with insufficient root canal obturation had lower endodontic treatment success rates. Similarly, Ingle [18] examined the factors that cause endodontic treatment failure and reported that 60% of unsuccessful cases were due to inadequate root canal obturation. The current study investigated the effect of three different obturation techniques on the success of NSER performed in a single visit. The study’s null hypothesis was accepted as there was no difference between the obturation techniques tested regarding time-dependent variation of postoperative pain and PAI scores.

Increasing endodontic treatment success rates and shortening the time required for treatment with magnification systems, electronic apex locator, endodontic motor, NiTi rotary file systems, and irrigation activation systems used during endodontic treatment encourage physicians to complete the treatment in a single visit [19]. Despite these developments, endodontic treatment completed in a single visit may fail due to persistent intraradicular microorganisms or extraradicular infection. It has not been reported in the literature that endodontic treatment performed in a single or multiple visits is superior to each other in terms of healing and success rates [20]. The NSER procedure can also be applied in a single visit after the primary endodontic treatment that failed. Wong et al. [21] reported no difference in healing and success rates after single-visit and multi-visit NSER. Similarly, Eyüpoğlu et al. [22] reported a high success rate (90.4%) after NSER was applied in a single visit in teeth with AP. Considering the available information, in this study, it was preferred to complete the NSER procedure in a single visit due to its advantages, such as short treatment time, eliminating the leakage factor due to temporary filling, and creating less stress on the patient.

It has been reported that differences in root canal anatomy play an important role in the success of endodontic treatment. Compared to teeth with single roots, more complex root canal morphologies can be encountered in molars [23]. Tronstad [24] reported that primary endodontic treatment results were more successful in three-rooted teeth than in two-rooted and single-rooted teeth. He reported that this situation is due to the instruments and irrigation solution used during chemomechanical preparation in multi-rooted teeth being in greater contact with the dentin walls in larger root canals [24]. On the contrary, according to another study, molars undergoing NSER had a lower success rate than premolars and anterior teeth due to the complex root canal system. Compared to single-rooted teeth, complex root canal morphologies can be encountered in multi-rooted teeth. Therefore, differences in root canal anatomy in multi-rooted teeth pose a challenge in standardization compared to teeth with single-root [25]. For this reason, this study was carried out on single-root and single-canal teeth so that the results of the study were not affected by the differences in root canal anatomy and to provide easier standardization.

It has been reported that the chemomechanical preparation method, irrigation solution/activation, rubber dam isolation, and coronal restoration selection are effective in endodontic treatment results [26]. In order to ensure standardization during the chemomechanical preparation, the same NiTi rotary files were used in each tooth included in the study, and the chemomechanical preparation was completed using the same irrigation protocol so that the current study result was not affected by these variables. Furthermore, there is no definite consensus in the literature regarding the final apical diameter of the root canal when applying NSER [27, 28]. However, it has been reported that in primary endodontic treatment, the preparation should be completed with a larger file than the file used for apical preparation. Therefore, the final apical preparation diameter was determined on a case-by-case basis [29]. Ng et al. [30] emphasized that, like apical leakage, leakage resulting from coronal restoration is directly related to the success of endodontic treatment. Similarly, Estrela et al. [31] reported that periapical lesions were more common in teeth with inadequate restoration than in teeth with adequate restoration. Therefore, in this study, all teeth treated with NSER were restored with direct composite resin in the same visit by the endodontist (M.I.) who carried out the study using the same materials and technique. It has been reported that the success of endodontic treatment is significantly affected by the preoperative condition of the periradicular tissues. Similarly, a significantly higher healing rate was observed in teeth with a smaller preoperative lesion size (< 5 mm) [32]. For this reason, teeth with lesion size ≤ 5 mm were included in this study to eliminate the healing bias due to preoperative lesion size.

Periapical, panoramic, and cone beam computed tomography (CBCT) are used in the radiographic follow-up of the periapical lesion size in endodontics. Although CBCT is an imaging method that allows three-dimensional imaging of dental structures, it is not routinely used in the follow-up of endodontic treatment results due to its high cost and higher ionizing radiation rate than 2-dimensional radiographs. Periapical radiography has disadvantages, such as only 2-dimensional evaluation and superposition of anatomical structures [33]. However, since periapical radiographs allow a more comprehensive evaluation of the relevant area and have less ionizing radiation than panoramic radiographs, they are routinely used to follow up endodontic treatment results [34]. The appearance of the periapical lesion may vary depending on the angle at which the periapical radiography is obtained and the irradiation parameters. In order to standardize these conditions, in the current study, all radiographs were obtained with parallel techniques using the same exposure parameters. Huumonen and Ørstavik [35] reported that at least six months should pass after endodontic treatment before the healing of the periapical lesion can be evaluated on periapical radiographs. Therefore, in the current study, a 6-month follow-up of teeth with AP was performed after the single visit NSER procedure.

The literature has determined that the Visual Analog Scale (VAS) is among the most preferred scales to evaluate postoperative pain in clinical studies. It has been reported that the VAS pain scale has advantages such as providing reproducible results, being applicable in different environments, and making precise measurements. However, using a pencil and ruler when marking on the VAS pain scale is necessary. It has also been reported that this scale has a disadvantage in that the length of the scale changes during the copying process [36]. NRS, another pain scale used to evaluate pain, provides a sensitive assessment of its intensity. Since this pain scale is easy to scale to apply, it is easily understood by the patients. In addition, pain intensity can be easily determined in writing and verbally [37]. Although both NRS and VAS show similar sensitivity in measuring pain intensity, it is reported that NRS is more practical to use in clinical studies as it can be easily understood by most patients [38]. Therefore, considering these advantages, NRS was preferred in the current study to evaluate postoperative pain.

As a result of this study, it was observed that more postoperative pain occurred in the 3rd-hour interval compared to the seventh-day interval in CLC. Similarly, Patel et al. [39] reported that the postoperative pain level observed in teeth whose primary endodontic treatment was completed with the CLC technique was higher in the 24 h compared to the seventh day. Contrary to this finding, Henry et al. [40] reported that postoperative pain levels, which they evaluated following primary endodontic treatment in symptomatic necrotic teeth obturated with CLC, gradually increased from the first to the seventh postoperative day. Some studies have reported that more postoperative pain is observed in teeth filled with the CLC technique than in other techniques due to the pressure created by the finger spreader used while performing lateral compaction [41]. Therefore, the reason why more postoperative pain was observed in the CLC group in the 3rd hour compared to the 7th day in our study may be that the pressure applied with the finger spreader during compression. Contrary to CLC, it was determined that more pain occurred in the 3rd hour interval in CWC than in the 7th day interval. Similar to the present study, de-Figueiredo et al. [42] reported that postoperative pain increased by approximately 40% in the first 24 h following primary endodontic treatment, and the incidence of pain decreased to 2% at day 7. It is stated in the literature that as a result of placing the plugger in the apical region of the root canal in CWC obturation, the possibility of heated gutta-percha and root canal sealer overflowing into the periradicular tissues increases, and this situation may irritate the periradicular tissues and may increase the level of postoperative pain [43]. In our study, it was determined that the root canal filling material overflowed apically in seven teeth obturated with CWC. Therefore, the reason for more pain in the 3rd postoperative hour compared to the seventh day in the CWC technique may be related to the root canal filling materials extruded after obturation. There was no difference in postoperative pain in the TGC group at all periods tested. In contrast to this study, Koçer et al. [44] reported that the initial pain gradually decreased after 6, 12, and 24 days after obturation with a thermoplasticised solid-core carrier obturation in teeth with single root/single canal and with asymptomatic irreversible pulpitis. While our study was performed on asymptomatic teeth with apical periodontitis, this study was conducted on asymptomatic teeth with irreversible pulpitis. The difference between the current study results may be due to the difference in case selection and the patient’s initial pain levels [45]. As a result of our study, we think that the reason for the lack of difference in terms of postoperative pain in all periods tested in the TGC group is due to the heat applied during obturation.

The literature review found no study in which the effects of CLC, CWC, or TGC obturation techniques used in the NSER on postoperative pain and radiographic recovery were evaluated. Therefore, the findings obtained in this study could not be directly compared with the results of previous studies. As a result of the present study, no difference was found in terms of postoperative pain at all time intervals evaluated between TGC, CLC, and CWC techniques at all time intervals tested. Similarly, Peng et al. [46] reported that postoperative pain was similar between CLC and hot obturation techniques as a result of their systematic review. Contrary to these findings, Alonso-Ezpeleta et al. [47] reported that postoperative pain was higher in all evaluated time intervals in the TGC technique after primary endodontic treatment applied in a single visit on anterior, premolar, and molar teeth compared to CLC and CWC. Differences between the results of existing studies may be due to case selection, evaluation method, and methodological differences such as irrigation, activation, and upper restoration, which are important variables in the success of endodontic treatment. It has been reported in the literature that types of file systems, irrigation solution/activation, and root canal sealers used in endodontic treatment play an effective role in the postoperative pain level [48]. The reason for the similarity in terms of postoperative pain among tested obturation techniques may be due to the similarity of all the other parameters listed above, which play an important role in the formation of postoperative pain.

After all obturation techniques evaluated in the present study, no difference was found in postoperative pain intensity in men and women. Similarly, Watkins et al. [49] reported similar postoperative pain levels in both men and women after endodontic treatment. However, Polycarpau et al. [50] reported that the gender of the patient was highly correlated with pain after endodontic treatment. It has been stated that the reason for this situation is that the hormone levels change in specific periods of the month in women depending on gender, and the increased prevalence of pain in women using oral contraceptives changes due to increased serotonin and noradrenaline levels [51]. Differences between study results may be due to differences such as sample size, age range, gender distribution, and treatment procedures and case selection.

In this study, 75% of the teeth with a PAI score between 3 and 5 at the beginning were found to have a PAI score between 1 and 2 after a six-month follow-up. This result supported significant radiographic improvement across all obturation techniques tested. In the study, no difference was found between the obturation techniques tested in terms of six month change in PAI score. Sarin et al. [52] reported in a retrospective study that there was no difference in radiographic healing in teeth obturated with TGC and CLC. In contrast, the prospective Toronto study [53] reported a higher success rate in CWC than in CLC. Aqrabawi et al. [54] reported in a retrospective study that the radiographic success rate after endodontic treatment in teeth with AP was higher in CWC than in CLC. The reasons for the lack of difference in recovery between different obturation techniques in the current study may be related to the similarity of the preoperative lesion size and the chemomechanical preparation process of the teeth included in the study.

In our study, no difference was found in terms of time-dependent change of PAI score between genders in all obturation techniques tested. However, some studies evaluating the radiographic results of endodontic treatment in the literature have reported higher success rates in men than in women [18, 55]. In contrast, Swartz et al. [55] reported that more successful results in terms of radiographic healing were observed in women compared to men after endodontic treatment. Considering these studies, there is no definite consensus that gender affects the results of endodontic treatment. Differences between study results may be due to variables such as gender distribution and sample size.

Conclusion

Within the limits of this study, the effects of different obturation techniques tested after NSER treatment on postoperative pain and radiographic healing in teeth with single-root and canal that failed primary endodontic treatment and developed AP were interpreted. No significant difference was observed between the tested obturation techniques regarding postoperative pain and radiographic healing. We believe that our study has the following limitations; the follow-up period in the current study was 6 months, the sample size was small, only single-rooted and single-canal teeth were included in the study, and only three different filling techniques were applied without additional irrigation activation and their effects on endodontic treatment results were evaluated. Therefore, randomized controlled clinical studies with larger sample sizes and longer follow-up periods are needed on this subject using different tooth groups.

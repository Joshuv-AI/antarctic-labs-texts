Stochastic collocation schemes for Neural Field Equations with random data
Daniele Avitabile, Francesca Cavallini, Svetlana Dubinkina, Gabriel J. Lord

Abstract
We develop and analyse numerical schemes for uncertainty quantification in neural field equations subject to random parametric data in the synaptic kernel, firing rate, external stimulus, and initial conditions. The schemes combine a generic projection method for spatial discretisation to a stochastic collocation scheme for the random variables. We study the problem in operator form, and derive estimates for the total error of the schemes, in terms of the spatial projector. We give conditions on the projected random data which guarantee analyticity of the semi-discrete solution as a Banach-valued function. We illustrate how to verify hypotheses starting from analytic random data and a choice of spatial projection. We provide evidence that the predicted convergence rates are found in various numerical experiments for linear and nonlinear neural field problems.

Introduction

Modelling brain dynamics and comprehending how uncertainties in the inputs affect
quantities of interest (QOI) is a fundamental question in neuroscience. This field
faces several challenges, including epistemic uncertainty arising from imperfect
models, which are often phenomenological in nature. Additionally, the nonlocality of
these models necessitates specialized numerical approaches.

In this paper we study uncertainty quantification (UQ) in a class of nonlinear brain
activity models known as
neural fields
, which are integro-differential
equations used as large-scale descriptions of neuronal
activity. They model the cortex as a continuum, and provide a versatile tool to understand pattern
formation on a variety of spatial domains

[
wilson1973mathematical
,
amari1977dynamics
,
Ermentrout.1998qno
,
Bressloff.2014k0p
,
coombes2014neural
]
.

Neural fields are amenable to functional and nonlinear analysis in simple cortices,
and thus can be used to investigate fundamental mechanisms for the generation of
brain activity. For instance, neural field simulations on spherical
domains
[
robinson1997propagation
,
bressloff2003spherical
,
Visser.2017
]
, or
more realistic cortices

[
martin2018numerical
,

support several coherent structures observed experimentally, such as
waves or stationary localised
structures
[
lee2005traveling
,
huang2010spiral
,
kimRingAttractorDynamics2017
]
.

While a large body of work shows that nonlinearity and nonlocality are building blocks
for patterns of neural activity, their robustness to noisy input data is still mostly
unexplored. In the mathematical and computational neuroscience communities, Monte
Carlo sampling is a popular approach to estimate mean and variance of
QOI. Methods offering faster convergence rates, such as Stochastic Finite Elements or
Stochastic Collocation, have been developed for applications
in other branches of physical and life sciences (see

[
smithUncertaintyQuantificationTheory2014
,
Lord:2014ir
,
adcockSparsePolynomialApproximation2022
]
for textbooks discussing

,

,

,

,
nobileSparseGridStochastic2008a
,
nobileAnisotropicSparseGrid2008
,
nobileAnalysisImplementationIssues2009
,

, and is not immediately applicable to neural
field equations, even though UQ techniques for ODEs have recently been applied to
connectomic ODE models of reaction–diffusion processes for

[

The analysis of generic spatial-projection stochastic collocation schemes for
neural fields is the main contribution of this paper. In the PDE literature cited
above, the spatial projector is often an orthogonal
projector, leading prevalently to Galerkin Finite Elements schemes. This
approach, which has also been extended to time-dependent, linear parabolic PDEs by
Zhang and Gunzburger
[
Zhang.2012
]
, stems naturally from the deterministic
functional setup, where schemes on nontrivial geometries are presented using weak
formulations. Adapting this approach to neural fields comes with different
challenges, and naturally brings a generalisation: it is possible to study in
parallel neural fields as dynamical systems on the Banach phase spaces
\mathbb{X}=C(D)

and
\mathbb{X}=L^{2}(D)

[
Potthast:2010kb
,
faugeras2008absolute
]
; this dichotomy shows up also in their
numerical treatment, which features a
single projector
P_{n}
on an
n
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{X}\in\{C(D),L^{2}(D)\}
, encompassing at once schemes in strong and weak form for collocation and
Galerkin methods, respectively. The convergence rates of such schemes is determined
in a unified manner, studying how
P_{n}
approximates an element
v
of the phase
space, via an estimate of
\|P_{n}v-v\|

[
avitabileProjectionMethodsNeural2023
]
.

Taking the literature on elliptic and parabolic PDEs as a guiding
example
[
babuskaStochasticCollocationMethod2007
,
Zhang.2012
]
, we
estimate the error

and an approximation

which combines an

-dimensional
(interpolatory or orthogonal) spatial projection with a

-dimensional

and

with

as

and a stochastic collocation component

with constants that are homogeneous in
q
and
n
, respectively.

However, since the neural field theory is built around a generic projected dynamical
system with random data, this leads to some differences from the PDE
literature. For instance, no assumption can be made on the convergence of
\|P_{n}\|
as
n\to\infty
, in contrast to existing literature using orthogonal projectors for
which
\|P_{n}\|
is bounded homogeneously in
n
. Keeping generality is valuable for
neuroscience applications, because efficient schemes for neural fields use projectors
that are not orthogonal, or for which the sequence
\{\|P_{n}\|\}_{n}
is unbounded (the one
used in
fig.

1
being one of them).

Existing literature on elliptic and parabolic PDEs derives exponential convergence
rates in

for

by proving that analyticity of the random input data
implies analyticity of

[
babuskaStochasticCollocationMethod2007

Zhang.2012
]
.
Our theory recovers analogous results for neural fields when
\{\|P_{n}\|\}_{n}
is bounded
in
n
, but is applicable to cases in which
\{\|P_{n}\|\}_{n}
is unbounded, provided the
random input data has sufficient
spatial
regularity. In particular, we give
conditions on the
projected random input data
(for instance
P_{n}g
and

) which guarantee the analyticity of the spatially discretised solution

as a function of the random variables. Because the derived estimates on the
derivatives are homogeneous in
n
, the same occurs for the stochastic collocation
error
\|u_{n}-u_{n,q}\|
, as required.

In addition, the treatment presented here does not assume Hilbert structure of the
phase space
\mathbb{X}
of the dynamical system with random data, leading to strong norms
when
\mathbb{X}=C(D)
, a case that is frequent in neural field applications. To this end
we use definitions and tools for Banach-space valued
functions
[

]
.

We derive error bounds for linear neural fields, without assuming dissipative
dynamics, that is, without assuming that the underlying semigroup operator is
contractive, in a sense that we make precise later. We link the lack of contractivity
to time-dependent analyticity radii that shrink as time increases. We argue that,
while analyticity results are in place for the nonlinear case, the sharp gradients
induced by non-contractive dynamics is to be expected for nonlinear problems near
bifurcation points. Finally, we provide evidence of exponential convergence rates in
linear and nonlinear problems, and signpost a slower convergence rates near a
bifurcating solution. Throughout the paper, we provide illustrative examples on
how to check hypotheses on random data with affine and non-affine dependence on
parameters, with various spatial projectors.

Finally, for an implementable scheme one must select:
\begin{enumerate}\enumerate@item@A quadrature scheme to approximate the integrals in the variable $y$. In

\par\vskip 4.0pt\vskip 4.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 1.0pt\enumerate@item@A time stepper for realisations of the neural field equation. Error

form, and supporting numerical simulations in which spatial quadrature and

from the ones studied in \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{babuskaStochasticCollocationMethod2007,Zhang.2012}{}{}]}: a

natural to expect in the linear (and nonlinear) neural field problem.

$C^{0}_{\sigma^{*}}(\Gamma_{i}^{*},\mathbb{B})$ the function space defined analogously to

$\partial_{y_{i}}^{l}w({\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu},{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu},y_{w})\in\mathbb{W}$ and hence
$H(\partial_{y_{i}}^{l}w({\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu},{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu},y_{w}))\in K(\mathbb{X})\subset BL(\mathbb{X})$ for
all $y_{w}\in\Gamma_{w}$ and $l\in\mathbb{N}_{k}$. Using the boundedness of $P_{n}$ we
conclude $\{B^{l}_{n,i}(y_{w})\}_{l}\subset BL(\mathbb{X}_{n})$ for
all $y_{w}$ and $i$. Combining
\lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{hyp:analyticityLRNF_Pn} with\penalty\ \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{avitabile2024NeuralFields}{}{}, Lemma 4.2]}, we can
bound the operator estimate
\begin{equation}\|B^{l}_{n,i}(y_{w})\|_{BL(\mathbb{X})}=\|H(P_{n}\partial_{y_{i}}^{l}w({\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu},{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu},y_{w}))\|_{BL(\mathbb{X})}\leq\|P_{n}\partial_{y_{i}}^{l}w({\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu},{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu},y_{w})\|_{\mathbb{W}}\leq\bar{K}_{w}\gamma_{i}^{l}l!.\end{equation}\par After these preparations, we fix $y=(y_{i},y_{i}^{*})$, where $y_{i}$ is a component of one
of the vectors $y_{w}$, $y_{g}$, $y_{v}$, and turn to the estimation of
$\partial_{y_{i}}^{k}u_{n}(t,y)$, where $u_{n}$ is the unique classical solution to (see proof of
\lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{lemma:C0SigmaRegularity})
\begin{equation}u^{\prime}_{n}(t,y)=A_{n}(y_{w})u_{n}(t,y)+P_{n}g(t,y_{g}),\qquad t\in[0,T],\qquad u_{n}(0,y)=P_{n}v(y_{v}),\end{equation}that is,
$$u_{n}(t,y)=e^{tA_{n}(y_{w})}P_{n}v(y_{v})+\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)A_{n}(y_{w})}P_{n}g(s,y_{g})\,ds$$By main hypothesis the mappings $y_{i}\mapsto A_{n}(y_{i},y_{i}^{*})$, $y_{i}\mapsto g(t,y_{i},y_{i}^{*})$, and $y_{i}\mapsto v(y_{i},y^{*})$ are of class $C^{k}$ in their respective
function spaces, hence $\partial_{y_{i}}^{k}u_{n}(t,y)$ is well defined. We derive an
evolution equation for $\partial_{y_{i}}^{k}u_{n}$ by differentiating $k$ times
\lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{eq:unEquationLocal} with respect to $y_{i}$, using the general Leibiniz
rule for differentiation, and noting that $\partial_{y_{i}}$ commutes with
$\partial_{t}$ and with $P_{n}$, obtaining the recursion
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\partial_{y_{i}}^{k}u_{n}=A_{n}\partial_{y_{i}}^{k}u_{n}+P_{n}\partial_{y_{i}}^{k}g+\sum_{l=1}^{k}\binom{k}{l}B^{l}_{n,i}\,\partial_{y_{i}}^{k-l}u_{n}&\textrm{on $[0,T]$,}\\
&\partial_{y_{i}}^{k}u_{n}(0)=P_{n}\partial_{y_{i}}^{k}v.&\end{aligned}\end{equation}\par For fixed $k$ we regard \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{eq:ukEvol} as an inhomogeneous Cauchy problem on the Banach space
$\mathbb{X}_{n}$, in the variable $\partial^{k}_{y_{i}}u$, with fixed parameter $y$, with forcing
dependent on $\partial_{y_{i}}^{k}g$, $\partial_{y_{i}}^{k}w$, $u_{n},\partial_{y_{i}}u_{n},\cdots,\partial_{y_{i}}^{k-l}u_{n}$,
By \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{vanneervenFunctionalAnalysis2022}{}{}, Theorem 13.24]}, for all $y\in\Gamma$
such that the forcing term is in $L^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{X}_{n})$ the Cauchy problem \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{eq:ukEvol}
admits a unique strong solution given by
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned} \partial_{y_{i}}^{k}u_{n}(t,y)&=e^{tA_{n}(y_{w})}P_{n}\partial_{y_{i}}^{k}v(y_{v})+\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)A_{n}(y_{w})}P_{n}\partial_{y_{i}}^{k}g(s,y_{g})\,ds\\
&+\sum_{l=1}^{k}\binom{k}{l}\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)A_{n}(y_{w})}B^{l}_{n,i}(y_{w})\partial_{y_{i}}^{k-l}u_{n}(s,y)\,ds\end{aligned}\end{equation}which is therefore in $C(J,\mathbb{X}_{n})$.
\par We will now show that for all $y\in\Gamma$ and $k\in\mathbb{N}$ the forcing
term is in $L^{1}(0,T,\mathbb{X}_{n})$, hence $\partial_{y_{i}}^{k}u_{n}$ exists and is given by
\lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{eq:ukBound}, and find a bound for $\|\partial_{y_{i}}^{k}u_{n}(t,y)\|_{\mathbb{X}}$. An inspection reveals
that only one of the three terms in the right-hand side of \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{eq:ukBound} is
nonzero, depending on whether $y_{i}$ is a component of $y_{v}$, $y_{g}$, or $y_{w}$, so we
proceed on a per-case basis.
\par{Case 1: bound \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{eq:unkBoundX} when $y_{i}$ is a component of $y_{v}$.} The Cauchy problem in
\lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{eq:ukEvol} becomes homogeneous as $\partial_{y_{i}}^{k}g=0$ and
$\partial_{y_{i}}^{k}w=0$ so all forcing terms vanish. Hence, it admits the
solution
$$\partial_{y_{i}}^{k}u_{n}(t,y)=e^{tA_{n}(y_{w})}P_{n}\partial_{y_{i}}^{k}v(y_{v}).$$Using the boundedness of $A_{n}$, the bound \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{eq:AnBound}, and \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{hyp:analyticityLRNF_Pn} we estimate
$$\|\partial_{y_{i}}^{k}u_{n}(t,y)\|_{\mathbb{X}}\leq e^{t\|A_{n}(y_{w})\|_{BL(\mathbb{X})}}\|P_{n}\partial_{y_{i}}^{k}v(y_{v})\|_{\mathbb{X}}\\
\leq e^{t\beta}K_{v}(y)k!\,\gamma_{i}^{k}.$$that gives \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{eq:unkBoundX} for all $i\in\mathbb{N}_{m}$ such that $y_{i}\in y_{v}$, with
$$D_{i}(t,y)=e^{t\beta}K_{v}(y),\quad\quad\quad\delta_{i}=\gamma_{i}.$$\par{Case 2: bound \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{eq:unkBoundX} when $y_{i}$ is a component of $y_{g}$.} Here $\partial_{y_{i}}^{k}w=0$,
$\partial_{y_{i}}^{k}v=0$ and the forcing term in \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{eq:ukEvol} is equal to $P_{n}\partial_{y_{i}}^{k}g$. By
\lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{hyp:analyticityLRNF_Pn} it holds that $P_{n}\partial_{y_{i}}^{k}g({\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu},y_{g})$ is in $C^{0}(J,\mathbb{X}_{n})\hookrightarrow L^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{X}_{n})$ hence, we can write the solution as
$$\partial_{y_{i}}^{k}u_{n}(t,y)=\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)A_{n}(y_{w})}P_{n}\partial_{y_{i}}^{k}g(s,y_{g})ds.$$By taking norms and proceeding as in the argument for Case 1 we obtain
$$\|\partial_{y_{i}}^{k}u_{n}(t,y)\|_{\mathbb{X}}\leq\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\|A_{n}(y_{w})\|_{BL(\mathbb{X})}}\|P_{n}\partial_{y_{i}}^{k}g(s,y_{g})\|_{\mathbb{X}}ds\leq K_{g}(y)k!\,\gamma_{i}^{k}\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\beta}ds.$$Integrating gives
$$\|\partial_{y_{i}}^{k}u_{n}(t,y)\|_{\mathbb{X}}\leq K_{g}(y)k!\,\gamma_{i}^{k}\,\frac{e^{t\beta}-1}{\beta}$$hence it follows
$$D_{i}(t,y)=\,\frac{e^{t\beta}-1}{\beta}\,K_{g}(y),\qquad\delta_{i}=\gamma_{i}.$$\par{Case 3: bound \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{eq:unkBoundX} when $y_{i}$ is a component of $y_{w}$.} In this last case, the forcing of
the Cauchy problem in \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{eq:ukEvol} is $\sum_{l=1}^{k}\binom{k}{l}B^{l}_{n,i}(y_{w})\partial_{y_{i}}^{k-l}u_{n}$.
This forcing depends on the first $k$ derivatives of $w$ (via the operators
$B^{l}_{n,i}$), as well as on the first $k-1$ derivatives of $u_{n}$. Hence,
we need to argue by induction. We prove two claims.
\par{Claim 1. For all $r\in\mathbb{N}_{k}$ it holds $\partial_{y_{i}}^{r}u_{n}\in C^{0}(J,\mathbb{X}_{n})$ on $\Gamma_{w}$}. We use a strong induction argument to prove this
claim. The base case $r=0$ reduces to $u_{n}\in C^{0}(J,\mathbb{X}_{n})$ on $\Gamma_{w}$, which
holds by \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{lemma:C0SigmaRegularity}. The induction step
assumes the first $r-1$ derivatives are continuous functions and differentiating
\lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{eq:unEquation} $r$ times we find that the evolution equation for
$\partial_{y_{i}}^{r}u_{n}$ is a Cauchy problem with forcing
$$t\mapsto\sum_{l=1}^{r}\binom{r}{l}B^{l}_{n,i}(y_{w})\,\partial_{y_{i}}^{r-l}u_{n}(t,y),$$which is in $C^{0}(J,\mathbb{X}_{n})$ for any $y_{w}\in\Gamma_{w}$ because
$t\mapsto\partial_{y_{i}}u_{n}(t,{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}),\ldots,t\mapsto\partial^{m-1}_{y_{i}}u_{n}(t,{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu})$ are in $C^{0}(J,\mathbb{X}_{n})$ by induction hypothesis and the operators
$B^{l}_{n,i}(y_{w})$ preserve continuity in $t$, as one can
see from
$$(B^{l}_{n,i}(y_{w})v)(t)=P_{n}\int_{D}\partial_{y_{i}}^{k}w({\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu},x^{\prime},y_{w})v(x^{\prime},t)\,dx^{\prime}.$$Since the forcing for the Cauchy problem for $\partial_{y_{i}}^{m}u_{n}$ is in
$C^{0}(J,\mathbb{X}_{n})$,
we can apply again \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{vanneervenFunctionalAnalysis2022}{}{}, Theorem 13.24]}, to
conclude the existence of a unique strong solution $\partial_{y_{i}}^{m}u_{n}\in C^{0}(J,\mathbb{X}_{n})$.
The induction argument is now complete.
\par{Claim 2. Bound \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{eq:unkBoundX} holds}. The solution of the Cauchy problem
\lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{eq:ukEvol} when $y_{i}\in y_{w}$ is
$$\begin{aligned} \partial_{y_{i}}^{k}u_{n}(t,y)=\sum_{l=1}^{k}\binom{k}{l}\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)A_{n}(y_{w})}B^{l}_{n,i}(y_{w})\partial_{y_{i}}^{k-l}u_{n}(s,y)\,ds.\end{aligned}$$Using the bounds \lx@cref{creftypeplural~refnum}{eq:BlBound} and\lx@nobreakspace\lx@cref{refnum}{eq:AnBound} for $\|B_{n,i}^{l}(y_{w})\|$ we estimate
$$\begin{aligned} \bigg\|\frac{\partial_{y_{i}}^{k}u_{n}(t,y)}{k!}\bigg\|_{\mathbb{X}}&\leq\sum_{l=1}^{k}\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\beta}\frac{\|B^{l}_{n,i}(y_{w})\|_{BL(\mathbb{X})}}{l!}\,\frac{\|\partial_{y_{i}}^{k-l}u_{n}(s,y)\|_{\mathbb{X}}}{(k-l)!}\,ds,\\
&\leq\beta\sum_{l=1}^{k}\gamma_{i}^{l}\,\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\beta}\,\frac{\|\partial_{y_{i}}^{k-l}u_{n}(s,y)\|_{\mathbb{X}}}{(k-l)!}\,ds,\\
\end{aligned}$$We set
$$R_{r}(t,y)=\sup_{\tau\in[0,t]}\bigg\|\frac{\partial_{y_{i}}^{r}u_{n}(\tau,y)}{r!}\bigg\|_{\mathbb{X}},\qquad r\in\mathbb{Z}_{k}$$and derive the following estimate
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned} R_{k}(t,y)&\leq\beta\sum_{l=1}^{k}\gamma_{i}^{l}\,\sup_{\tau\in[0,t]}\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{(\tau-s)\beta}\,\frac{\|\partial_{y_{i}}^{k-l}u_{n}(s,y)\|_{\mathbb{X}}}{(k-l)!}\,ds\\
&\leq\beta\sum_{l=1}^{k}\gamma_{i}^{l}\,\sup_{\tau\in[0,t]}\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{(\tau-s)\beta}\,R_{k-l}(t,y)\,ds\\
&=\beta\sum_{l=1}^{k}\gamma_{i}^{l}\,R_{k-l}(t,y)\sup_{\tau\in[0,t]}\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{(\tau-s)\beta}\,ds\\
&=\beta\,\frac{e^{t\beta}-1}{\beta}\sum_{l=1}^{k}\gamma_{i}^{l}\,R_{k-l}(t,y)=:\nu(t)\sum_{l=1}^{k}\gamma_{i}^{l}\,R_{k-l}(t,y).\end{aligned}\end{equation}Using the bound recursively on the elements of the sum gives, omitting dependence on
$(y,t)$
$$\begin{aligned} R_{k}&\leq\nu\sum_{l=1}^{k}\gamma_{i}^{l}\,R_{k-l}=\nu\biggl[\gamma_{i}R_{k-1}+\sum_{l=2}^{k}\gamma_{i}^{l}\,R_{k-l}\biggr]\\
&\leq\nu\biggl[\nu\sum_{r=1}^{k-1}\gamma_{i}^{r+1}\,R_{k-1-r}+\sum_{l=2}^{k}\gamma_{i}^{l}\,R_{k-l}\biggr]=\nu(\nu+1)\sum_{l=2}^{k}\gamma_{i}^{l}\,R_{k-l}\\
&\leq\nu(\nu+1)^{2}\sum_{l=3}^{k}\gamma_{i}^{l}\,R_{k-l}\leq\ldots\leq\nu(\nu+1)^{k-1}\gamma_{i}^{k}R_{0}\end{aligned}$$and therefore
$$R_{k}(t,y)\leq\bigg[\bigg(\beta\,\frac{e^{t\beta}-1}{\beta}+1\bigg)\gamma_{i}\bigg]^{k}\,R_{0}(t,y)$$Applying\penalty\ \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{avitabile2024NeuralFields}{}{}, Theorem 6.3]} under finite-dimensional noise
(\lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{hyp:finDimNoise}) we have
$$R_{0}(t,y)=\|u_{n}({\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu},y)\|_{C([0,t],\mathbb{X})}\leq\big(\|P_{n}v(y)\|_{\mathbb{X}}+\|P_{n}g({\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu},y)\|_{C^{0}(J,\mathbb{X})}t\bigr)e^{\|P_{n}W(y)\|t}$$and by \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{hyp:analyticityLRNF_Pn} we have
$$R_{0}(t,y)\leq\bigl(K_{v}(y)+K_{g}(y)t\bigr)e^{\beta t}$$We conclude that for any $(t,y)\in J\times\Gamma$
$$\frac{\|\partial_{y_{i}}^{k}u_{n}(t,y)\|_{\mathbb{X}}}{k!}\leq R_{k}(t,y)\leq D_{i}(t,y)\delta_{i}(t)^{k}$$where the coefficients read
$$D_{i}(t,y)=\bigl(K_{v}(y)+K_{g}(y)t\bigr)e^{\beta t},\quad\quad\quad\delta_{i}(t)=\bigg(\beta\,\frac{e^{t\beta}-1}{\beta}+1\bigg)\gamma_{i}.$$\par{Derivation of bounds \lx@cref{creftypeplural~refnum}{eq:unkBoundC0} and\lx@nobreakspace\lx@cref{refnum}{eq:unkBoundC0J}.} We have so far
established \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{eq:unkBoundX}. By \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{hyp:analyticityLRNF_Pn} the functions $K_{g}$
and $K_{v}$ (and hence $y\mapsto D_{i}(t,y)$) have a finite $C^{0}_{\sigma}(\Gamma)$ norm.
Taking the norm in $C^{0}(\Gamma,\mathbb{X})$ on both sides of \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{eq:unkBoundX} gives
\lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{eq:unkBoundC0}. To derive
\lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{eq:unkBoundC0J} we start from \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{eq:unkBoundX} and estimate
$$\begin{aligned} &\|\partial_{y_{i}}^{k}u_{n}({\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu},y)\|_{C^{0}(J,\mathbb{X})}\leq k!\sup_{t\in J}D_{i}(t,y)\delta_{i}(t)^{k}\leq k!\bigg(\sup_{t\in J}D_{i}(t,y)\bigg)\bigg(\sup_{t\in J}\delta_{i}(t)\bigg)^{k},\end{aligned}$$where we have used the fact that either $\delta_{i}(t)\equiv\gamma_{i}$, or
$\delta_{i}(t)=\nu(t)\gamma_{i}$, with $\nu(t)\geq 1$ for all $t$. Taking the
norm in $C^{0}_{\sigma}(\Gamma\penalty\ C^{0}(J,\mathbb{X}))$ gives \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{eq:unkBoundC0J}.
\@qedbox{}\end{proof}\par\par\@@numbered@section{subsubsection}{toc}{Bounds on the $k$th derivative of $u_n$ for contractive
RLNFs}

\par\par\@@numbered@section{subsection}{toc}{Analyticity of $u_n$}
In preparation for the analyticity proof, we present a technical statement that
follows from \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{thm:AnalytLRNFAlt}, and concerns analyticity and Fréchet
differentiability of $u_{n}$, as a function on $\Gamma_{i}$. Here and henceforth, we
study analyticity of mappings on Banach spaces, adopting the definition and
theory in \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{buffoni_analytic_2003}{}{}, Definition 4.3.1, and Section 4.3]}, according
to which a function $\psi\colon\mathbb{B}_{1}\to\mathbb{B}_{2}$ a
function is said to be $\mathbb{R}$- or $\mathbb{C}$-analytic depending on whether $\mathbb{B}_{1}$ and
$\mathbb{B}_{2}$ are Banach spaces over $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$. The sketch in
\lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{fig:analyticitySketch} is helpful in navigating the material in this section.
\begin{theorem}Assume the hypotheses of \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{thm:AnalytLRNFAlt}, fix $n\in\mathbb{N}$, $t\in J$,
$i\in\mathbb{N}_{m}$, and $y_{0}\in\Gamma_{i}$. The mapping
$$\psi_{t}\colon\Gamma_{i}\subset\mathbb{R}\to C^{0}_{\sigma_{i}^{*}}(\Gamma_{i}^{*},\mathbb{X}),\qquad y_{i}\mapsto u_{n}(t,y_{i},{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}).$$is $\mathbb{R}$-analytic at $U_{\tau_{i}}=\{y\in\Gamma_{i}\colon|y-y_{0}|\leq\tau_{i}\}$, for any $0<\tau_{i}<1/\delta_{i}(t)$,
and its $k$th Fréchet derivative at $y_{0}\in U_{\tau_{i}}$ is given by
\begin{equation}d^{k}\psi_{t}[y_{0}]\colon\Gamma_{i}^{k}\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}\to C^{0}_{\sigma_{i}^{*}}(\Gamma_{i}^{*},\mathbb{X})\qquad(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{k})\mapsto\partial_{y_{i}}^{k}u_{n}(t,y_{0},{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu})\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{k}.\end{equation}\@qedbox{}\end{theorem}\begin{proof}Fix an arbitrary $y_{0}\in\Gamma_{i}$, and denote by $\mathbb{B}$ the space
$C^{0}_{\sigma^{*}}(\Gamma^{*}_{i},\mathbb{X})$ over $\mathbb{R}$. In this proof we will adopt the
representations $(y,y_{i}^{*}),(y_{0},y_{i}^{*})$ for variables in $\Gamma_{i}\times\Gamma_{i}^{*}$.
By \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{thm:AnalytLRNFAlt}, the real-valued function $y\mapsto u_{n}(t,y,y_{i}^{*})(x)$ is in $C^{\infty}(\Gamma_{i},\mathbb{R})$ for any $x$, $t$, and $y_{i}^{*}$. By Taylor's theorem
for real-valued functions of a single variable, there exists a function $R_{k}$ such that
$$u_{n}(t,y,y_{i}^{*})(x)-\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{1}{j!}\partial^{j}_{y_{i}}u_{n}(t,y_{0},y_{i}^{*})(x)(y-y_{0})^{j}=R_{k}(y_{0},y,t,y^{*}_{i})(x),$$We will examine in a moment the asymptotic properties of $R_{k}$, which we put on a
side for now, and we rewrite the previous identity as
\begin{equation}\psi_{t}(y)(y_{i}^{*})-\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{1}{j!}\partial^{j}_{y_{i}}u_{n}(t,y_{0},y_{i}^{*})(y-y_{0})^{j}=R_{k}(y_{0},y,t,y^{*}_{i}).\end{equation}Crucially, the last identity does not make any statement about the
differentiability of $\psi_{t}$, nor does it express $d^{k}\psi_{t}$, the Fréchet
derivative, in terms of $\partial_{y_{i}}^{k}u_{n}$, the standard partial derivatives, albeit
the notation is suggestive of that. We now elucidate this connection, and prove the
analyticity of $\psi_{t}$.
\par We introduce the symbol $m_{0}(y-y_{0})^{0}=\psi_{t}(y_{0})$, and the family of $k$-linear operators $\{m_{k}\}$
defined by
$$m_{k}\colon\Gamma_{i}^{k}\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{B}\qquad(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{k})\mapsto\frac{1}{k!}\partial_{y_{i}}^{k}u_{n}(t,y_{0},{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu})\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{k},\qquad k\geq 1.$$By \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{thm:AnalytLRNFAlt} $\partial^{k}_{y_{i}}u_{n}(t,{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu},{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu})$ is in

Taylor's theorem bound for real-valued functions of a single variable, obtaining
$$\big\|R_{k}(y_{0},y,t,y_{i}^{*})\big\|_{\mathbb{X}}\leq\frac{|y-y_{0}|^{k+1}}{(k+1)!}\sup_{s\in[y_{0},y]}\|\partial_{y_{i}}^{k+1}u(t,s,y_{i}^{*})\|_{\mathbb{X}}$$With the view of bounding $R_{k}$ in the $\mathbb{B}$-norm, we multiply each side of the

$$\begin{aligned} \tilde{\sigma}_{i}(y_{0},y)\big\|R_{k}(y_{0},y,t,y_{i}^{*})\big\|_{\mathbb{X}}&\leq\frac{|y-y_{0}|^{k+1}}{(k+1)!}\tilde{\sigma}_{i}(y_{0},y)\sup_{s\in[y_{0},y]}\|\partial_{y_{i}}^{k+1}u(t,s,y_{i}^{*})\|_{\mathbb{X}}\\
&\leq\frac{|y-y_{0}|^{k+1}}{(k+1)!}\sup_{s\in[y_{0},y]}\sigma_{i}(s)\|\partial_{y_{i}}^{k+1}u(t,s,y_{i}^{*})\|_{\mathbb{X}},\end{aligned}$$hence, by \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{thm:AnalytLRNFAlt},
$$\begin{aligned} \big\|R_{k}(y_{0},y,t,{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu})\big\|_{\mathbb{B}}&\leq\frac{1}{\tilde{\sigma}_{i}(y_{0},y)}\frac{|y-y_{0}|^{k+1}}{(k+1)!}\sup_{s^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{i}^{*}}\sigma_{i}^{*}(s^{\prime})\sup_{s\in[y_{0},y]}\sigma_{i}(s)\|\partial_{y_{i}}^{k+1}u(t,s,s^{\prime})\|_{\mathbb{X}}\\

&\leq\frac{\bigl(|y-y_{0}|\delta_{i}(t)\bigr)^{k+1}}{\tilde{\sigma}(y_{0},y)}\|D_{i}(t,{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu})\|_{C^{0}_{\sigma}(\Gamma)}.\end{aligned}$$We can now draw a few conclusions: firstly, owing to the previous inequality, the
power series
$$\psi_{t}(y)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}m_{j}(y-y_{0})^{j}$$converges for all $y$ in the interval $U_{\tau_{i}}=\{|y-y_{0}|\leq\tau_{i}\}\subset\Gamma_{i}$, with $0<\tau_{i}<1/\delta_{i}(t)$. Secondly, we return to the
estimate \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{eq:FrechDerEstimate}, which gives
$$\sup_{k\geq 0}\tau_{i}^{k}\|m_{k}\|<\infty,\qquad 0<\tau_{i}<\delta_{i}(t).$$The hypotheses of \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{buffoni_analytic_2003}{}{}, Proposition 4.3.4]} are verified and
we conclude that for any $0<\tau<\delta_{i}(t)$: (i) the mapping $\psi_{t}$ is
$\mathbb{R}$-analytic on $U_{\tau_{i}}$ \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{buffoni_analytic_2003}{}{}, Definition
4.3.1]}; (ii) it holds $\psi_{t}\in C^{\infty}(U_{\tau_{i}},\mathbb{B})$; and (iii) $m_{k}$ is the $k$th Fréchet derivative $d^{k}\psi_{t}[y_{0}]$ of $\psi_{t}$ at $y_{0}\in U_{\tau_{i}}$. The proof is now complete.

solution $y_{i}\mapsto u_{n}(t,y_{i},{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu})$ on an interval in $\Gamma_{i}\subset\mathbb{R}$.
To that effect, we transplanted bounds on partial derivatives of the real-valued
solution $\partial_{y_{i}}^{k}u_{n}$, to bounds on the Fréchet derivative of the mapping
above. We now extend this $\mathbb{R}$-analytic mapping to a $\mathbb{C}$-analytic mapping,
defined on an open set of the complex plane. Importantly, this extension can be
carried out without investigating Fréchet differentiability of Banach-space valued
functions of complex variables, on the field $\mathbb{C}$, for which we have thus far
proved no result.

\par We are ready to give an a priori estimate for the stochastic collocation scheme's
total error. We use results in \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{babuskaStochasticCollocationMethod2007}{}{}]} for PDEs with random data, as the
estimates for the interpolation in the $y$ direction do not depend on the particular
problem but only rely on analyticity of the data and solution, and we combine it with
the theory developed in \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{avitabileProjectionMethodsNeural2023}{}{}]} for neural fields.
\begin{theorem}Assume the hypotheses of \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{thm:AnalytLRNFAlt}, and \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{hyp:subgaussian}
(sub-Gaussianity). There exist positive constants $C_{\textnormal{sc}},r_{1},\ldots,r_{m}$ independent
of $n$ and $q$, such that, for all $t\in J$ it holds
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned} \|u(t,{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu})-u_{n,q}(t,{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu})\|_{L^{2}_{\rho}(\Gamma)\otimes\mathbb{X}}&\leq\|u-u_{n,q}\|_{L^{2}_{\rho}(\Gamma)\otimes C^{0}(J,\mathbb{X})}\\
&\leq e^{\bar{K}_{w}T}\|u-P_{n}u\|_{L^{2}_{\rho}(\Gamma)\otimes C^{0}(J,\mathbb{X})}+C_{\textnormal{sc}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\beta_{i}(q_{i})\,e^{-r_{i}q_{i}^{\theta_{i}}},\end{aligned}\end{equation}where, if $\Gamma_{i}$ is bounded
$$r_{i}=\log\left[\frac{2\tau_{i}}{|\Gamma|}\left(1+\sqrt{1+\frac{|\Gamma_{i}|^{2}}{4\tau_{i}^{2}}}\right)\right],\qquad\theta_{i}=\beta_{i}=1,$$and if $\Gamma_{i}$ is unbounded
$$r_{i}=\tau_{i}\zeta_{i},\qquad\theta_{i}=\frac{1}{2},\qquad\beta_{i}\in O(\sqrt{q_{i}})\text{ as $q_{i}\to\infty$},$$$\bar{K}_{w}$ being defined in \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{hyp:analyticityLRNF_Pn},
$\tau_{i}$ in \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{thm:C-analyticity-C0}, and
$\zeta_{i}$ in \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{hyp:subgaussian}.

\begin{example}Consider a RLNF with random data

$P_{n}$ of this scheme is such that\penalty\ \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{atkinson1997}{}{}, Equation 2.3.48]}
$$\|z-P_{n}z\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{|D|^{2}}{8n^{2}}\|v^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}=\frac{1}{2n^{2}}\|v^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty},\qquad v\in C^{2}(D).$$\par We now reason as follows: by \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{avitabile2024NeuralFields}{}{}, Corollaries 5.1 and 6.2]} $u\in L^{2}_{\rho}(\Gamma_{v},C^{0}(J,\mathbb{X}))$ and $u_{n}\in L^{2}_{\rho}(\Gamma_{v},C^{0}(J,\mathbb{X}_{n}))$,

$$\begin{aligned} \|u-P_{n}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho}(\Gamma)\otimes C^{0}(J,\mathbb{X})}&=\int_{\Gamma}\biggl[\max_{(x,t)\in J\times D}\bigl|u(x,t,y)-(P_{n}u({\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu},t,y))(x)\bigr|\biggr]^{2}\rho(y)\,dy\\
&\leq\biggl(\frac{1}{2n^{2}}\biggr)^{2}\int_{\Gamma}\Biggl[\max_{t\in J}\biggl\|\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}}({\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu},t,y)\biggr\|\Biggr]^{2}\rho(y)\,dy\\
&=\biggl(\frac{1}{2n^{2}}\biggr)^{2}\biggl\|\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}}\biggr\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho}(\Gamma,C^{0}(J,\mathbb{X}))}\leq\biggl(\frac{1}{2n^{2}}\biggr)^{2}\biggl\|\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}}\biggr\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho}(\Gamma,C^{0}(J,C^{2}(D)))}\end{aligned}$$We combine the estimate above with \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{eq:vC0Bound} in \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{lem:momentsError} and
\lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{eq:totalErrorBound} in \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{thm:totalBound}, use the fact that $\Gamma_{v}$ is
bounded (hence $q_{i}=\theta_{i}=1$), and that the kernel $w$ is deterministic
(hence we can take $\bar{K}_{w}=\|w\|_{\mathbb{W}}$)
to arrive at
\begin{equation}\bigl\|\mean_{\rho}(u-u_{nq})\bigr\|_{C^{0}(J,\mathbb{X})}\leq C_{1}n^{-2}+C_{2}\sum_{i=1}^{m}e^{-r_{i}q_{i}},\qquad C_{1}=\frac{e^{T\|w\|_{\mathbb{W}}}}{2}\biggl\|\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}}\biggr\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho}(\Gamma,C^{0}(J,C^{2}(D)))}\end{equation}and hence the error decays as an $O(n^{-2})$ as $n\to\infty$, and exponentially
as $q_{i}\to\infty$, for any $i\in\mathbb{N}_{m}$.
\par\par\@@numbered@section{section}{toc}{Considerations on nonlinear neural fields}
\lx@cref{creftypecap~refnum}{sec:linearAnalysis} discusses analyticity of solutions to the parametrised
problem $u_{n}^{\prime}(t,y)=P_{n}N(t,u_{n}(t,y),y)$ with initial condition $u_{n}(0,y)=P_{n}v(y)$
when $\mathbb{L}=1$ (linear case). Under the boundedness assumption on the projectors
$P_{n}$, \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{hyp:analyticityLRNF} ensures that the mapping $y_{i}\mapsto N({\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu},{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu},(y_{i},y_{i}^{*}))$ is analytic, and \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{thm:AnalytLRNFAlt} provides
estimates determining the analyticity radius $\delta_{i}(t)$ of the mapping $y_{i}\to u_{n}({\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu},y_{i},y_{i}^{*})$, in the appropriate function space.
\par A natural question arises as to how to adapt this approach in the nonlinear case
($\mathbb{L}=0$). The analyticity of solutions to generic Cauchy problems on Banach
spaces is studied in \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{zeidler1985a}{}{}, Theorem 4.11, Section 4.11, pages 165--166]}.
If the mapping $y\mapsto N({\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu},{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu},y)$ is affine, conditions for analyticity
of the solution are also given in\penalty\ \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{hansenSparseAdaptiveApproximation2013}{}{}]}. With
reference to the continuous parametrised problem
$$u^{\prime}(t,y)=N(t,u(t,y),y),\qquad(t,y)\in J\times\Gamma,\qquad u(0,y)=v(y)\qquad y\in\Gamma$$we deduce that if $(t,u,y)\mapsto N(t,u,y)$ is analytic on $V\subseteq\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{X}\times\Gamma$, then the mapping $(t,v,y)\mapsto u(t,v,y)$, in which we
indicate dependence of the solution on the initial condition $v$, is analytic on an
open $Z\subset V$.
\par If we pursued a study analogous to the linear case, however, appealing to such result
would be insufficient, because bounding the total error as in \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{thm:totalBound}
requires an estimate of the radius of analyticity $\tau$ (and this is precisely what
\lx@cref{creftypeplural~refnum}{thm:AnalytLRNFAlt} and\lx@nobreakspace\lx@cref{refnum}{thm:C-analyticity-C0} do for the linear case).
We have also seen in \lx@cref{creftypeplural~refnum}{rem:contractivity} and\lx@nobreakspace\lx@cref{refnum}{sssec:kthBoundsContractive} that, in the
case of non-contracting dynamics, such radius shrinks as $t$ increases.
\par The dynamics of general nonlinear problems is deeply influenced by so-called
bifurcation points. As a simple example, consider a family of equilibria of the
neural field problem, $u_{*}(y_{i})$ which are in generically parametrised in one of the
variables $y_{i}$. The dynamics of small perturbations to the rest state $u_{*}(y_{i})$ is
governed by a linear problem, and the spectrum of the associated linear operators $A(y_{i})$
determines, roughly speaking, whether perturbations decay or grow. The spectrum of
$A(y_{i})$ thus changes with the parameter value, and a frequent scenario in nonlinear
problems is that the spectrum of $A(y_{i})$ crosses the imaginary axis as $y_{i}$ varies
across a so-called bifurcation point, thereby determining a behavioural change in the
solution, which may pass from a contracting to an expanding one, and vice versa.
\par In other words, in nonlinear problems the domains $\Gamma_{i}$ are generically
enclosing bifurcation points, which mark sharp transitions in the mapping $y_{i}\mapsto u(t,y_{i})$ for $t$ fixed. Thus, in nonlinear problems the expectation is that
$A(y_{i})$ will generally have eigenvalues on the right-half complex plane, for which
we know that analyticity radii shrink as $t$ grows. Consequently, we can hope to
observe numerically fast convergence rates on long time intervals only in cases where
$\Gamma_{i}$ does not enclose bifurcations, with the spectrum of $A(y_{i})$ staying on
the left-half complex plane for all $y_{i}\in\Gamma_{i}$.
\par We remark that these considerations are valid for generic nonlinear problems, not
just the neural field presented here. In this paper, rather than pursuing the
computation of analyticity radii for nonlinear projected problem, which seems
possible and is expected to give shrinking analyticity radii, we employ stochastic
collocation on nonlinear problems and present numerical evidence of convergence
behaviour in \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{sec:numerics}.

We include in this section a collection of numerical experiments to test our scheme.
All computations run on a laptop computer with 2 or 4 cores, except the convergence
plots of \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{subsec:NumericalEx3}, which were run on ADA, an HPC facility at VU
Amsterdam, using 32 cores.
\par\par\@@numbered@section{subsection}{toc}{Problem 1: linear case, uniformly distributed parameter in $\RSet$}
As first example we consider a linear neural field
with one-dimensional, non-affine noise in the external input $g$. We choose a system
that meets the hypotheses of \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{thm:AnalytLRNFAlt} with $\mathbb{X}=C(D)$ (see
\lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{ex:gAnaExample} for
checking analyticity of the random input), the sub-gaussianity hypothesis, and to
which the theory of \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{sec:linearAnalysis} for the error analysis applies. The
problem's solution and QOI are known in analytic
form, allowing to compare theory and numerical experiments.

convergence towards a highly resolved solution, in multiple parameters and possibly
unbounded domains. We set $L=10$,
$T=1$ for the spatio-temporal domain, and take deterministic data
\begin{equation}w(x,x^{\prime})=(1-\sigma_{w}|x-x^{\prime}|)e^{-\sigma_{w}|x-x^{\prime}|}\bigl[A_{0}+A_{1}\sin(\omega_{A}x^{\prime})\bigr],\qquad f(u)=\frac{F_{0}}{1+e^{-\mu(u-h)}},\end{equation}and random data of the form $g(x,t,Y_{1}(\omega_{g}))$, $v(x,Y_{2}(\omega_{v}))$,

given by
\par\begin{equation}v(x,y_{1})=y_{1}e^{-x^{2}},\qquad g(x,t,y_{2})=y_{2}\sin(\omega_{g}t)e^{-x^{2}/\sigma_{g}^{2}}.\end{equation}The system models a neural field with heterogeneous (not translation-invariant)

\par\par\@@numbered@section{section}{toc}{Conclusions}
In this paper we have introduced a scheme for forward UQ in neural field models,
using stochastic collocation. The method adapts stochastic collocation from PDE
literature to integro-differential equations arising in mathematical neuroscience,
and it combines with generic spatial discretisations for the problem. The method
presented here can be readily implemented to perform UQ, upon coding the
spatially-projected forward simulation problem within existing UQ software such as
{Sparse Grids Matlab Kit}\penalty\ \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{piazzola.tamellini:SGK}{}{}]} and
{UQLab}\penalty\ \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{marelliUQLabFrameworkUncertainty2014}{}{}]}.
\par It seems possible to study variations of stochastic collocation which, in the PDE
literature, overcome some of the limitations of the method, and are
suitable for a large number of parameters, in particular sparse grid, sparse
Galerkin, and anisotripic methods \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{
nobileSparseGridStochastic2008a,
nobileAnisotropicSparseGrid2008,
hansenSparseAdaptiveApproximation2013,
hoangSparseTensorGalerkin2013a,
ernstConvergenceSparseCollocation2018,
adcockSparsePolynomialApproximation2022}{}{}]}. Further, we expect that a similar approach to
the one taken here, which splits the spatial and stochastic error for the problem,
will help us analysing Monte Carlo Finite Element schemes and its variants (in particular we should
be able to adapt directly the methods in \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Lord:2014ir}{}{}, Chapter 9]}). An immediate
application of the theory presented in this paper is the use of stochastic
collocation for Bayesian inverse problems in neural fields, which can follow its PDE
analogues \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{marzoukStochasticCollocationApproach2009}{}{}]} and is promising for
low-dimensional parametrised problems.

\par\par\@@numbered@section{appendix}{toc}{Perturbative result on semigroup operator}
The proof of $C^{0}_{\sigma}$-regularity for the solution uses the following perturbative
result on operator semigroups
\begin{lemma}Let $A,B\in BL(\mathbb{X})$, where $\mathbb{X}$ is a Banach space. The
uniformly continuous semigroups $(e^{tA})_{t\geq 0}$, $(e^{tB})_{t\geq 0}$,
generated on $\mathbb{X}$ by $A$ and $B$, respectively, satisfy
$$\|e^{tA}-e^{tB}\|\leq t\|A-B\|e^{t\|A-B\|}e^{t\min(\|A\|,\|B\|)},\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}.$$\@qedbox{}\end{lemma}\begin{proof}By \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{engelShortCourseOperator2006}{}{}, Chapter III, Section 1, Corollary 1.7]} it holds
\begin{equation}(e^{tB}-e^{tA})v=\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)A}(B-A)e^{sB}v\,ds,\qquad v\in\mathbb{X},\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}.\end{equation}Taking norms, and recalling $\|e^{tA}\|\leq e^{t\|A\|}$ for
any $A\in BL(\mathbb{X})$ and $t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}$ we get
$$\begin{aligned} \|(e^{tB}-e^{tA})v\|&\leq\|B-A\|\|v\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{t\|A\|}e^{s(\|B\|-\|A\|)}\,ds\\
&\leq\|B-A\|\|v\|e^{t\|A\|}\int_{0}^{t}e^{s\|B-A\|}\,ds\\
&<t\|A-B\|e^{t\|A-B\|}e^{t\|A\|}\|v\|,\end{aligned}$$hence
$$\|e^{tA}-e^{tB}\|\leq t\|A-B\|e^{t\|A-B\|}e^{t\|A\|},\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}.$$Swapping $A$ and $B$ in \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{eq:engelBound} we obtain
$$\|e^{tA}-e^{tB}\|\leq t\|A-B\|e^{t\|A-B\|}e^{t\|B\|},\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},$$and combining the last two bounds we prove the statement.
\@qedbox{}\end{proof}\par\@@numbered@section{appendix}{toc}{Further proofs}

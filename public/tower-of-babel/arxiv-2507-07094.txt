Numbers with three close factorizations and central lattice points on hyperbolas
Tsz Ho Chan

Abstract
In this paper, we continue the study of three close factorizations of an integer and correct a mistake of a previous result. This turns out to be related to lattice points close to the center point ( N , N ) (\sqrt{N},\sqrt{N}) of the hyperbola x ​ y = N xy=N . We establish optimal lower bounds for L 1 L^{1} -distance between these lattice points and the center. We also give some good examples based on polynomials and Pell equations more systematically.



1
Introduction and main results

Factorization is a fundamental study on integers. In this paper, we continue the study of close factorizations of an integer such as

3950100=1881\cdot 2100=1890\cdot 2090=1900\cdot 2079.

Suppose a positive integer
N
admits
k
different factorizations:

N=AB=(A+a_{1})(B-b_{1})=\cdots=(A+a_{k-1})(B-b_{k-1})

(1)

for some positive integers
A
,
B
,
a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdots<a_{k-1}
, and
b_{1}<b_{2}<\cdots<b_{k-1}
. One can ask how small
a_{k-1}
and
b_{k-1}
can be in terms of
N
and
k
. Rephrasing it another way, one can ask how big
A
and
B
can be in terms of
k
,
a_{k-1}
and
b_{k-1}
. In
[
1
]
, the author proved the following result for numbers with three close factorizations.

Theorem 1
.

Suppose
N
satisfies (
1
) with
k=3
. Then

A,B\leq\frac{1}{4}\max(a_{2},b_{2})(\max(a_{2},b_{2})-1)^{2}

whenever
\max(a_{2},b_{2})\geq 4
.

However, it contains a slight error as the example

N=72=6\cdot 12=8\cdot 9=9\cdot 8\;\text{ with }\;A=6,\;B=12,\;a_{1}=2,\;b_{1}=3,\;a_{2}=3,\;b_{2}=4

shows that
12\leq\frac{1}{4}\cdot 4\cdot(4-1)^{2}
fails. We shall give a correct and more conceptual proof of the following modified version.

Theorem 2
.

Suppose
N
satisfies (
1
) with
k=3
. Then

A,B\leq\frac{1}{4}\max(a_{2},b_{2})(\max(a_{2},b_{2})-1)^{2}

whenever
\max(a_{2},b_{2})\geq 5
. Note: The proof is much simpler in the range
\max(a_{2},b_{2})\geq 7
.

It is closely related to the following result with a shorter and more intuitive proof than
[
1
]
.

Theorem 3
.

Suppose
N
satisfies (
1
) with
k=3
with
B\leq\sqrt{N}\leq A
. Then

A\leq\frac{1}{4}a_{2}(a_{2}-3)(a_{2}+1).

Moreover, the above equality can be attained if and only if
a_{2}>3
is odd and

A=\frac{a_{2}(a_{2}+1)(a_{2}-3)}{4},\;B=\frac{(a_{2}-2)(a_{2}-1)^{2}}{4},\;a_{1}=\frac{a_{2}+1}{2},\;b_{1}=\frac{a_{2}-1}{2},\;b_{2}=a_{2}-2.

Note:
a_{2}
is the largest among
a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2}
as

AB=(A+a_{i})(B-b_{i})\;\text{ implies }\;a_{i}B=b_{i}A+a_{i}b_{i}>b_{i}A\;\text{ and }\;a_{i}>\frac{A}{B}b_{i}\geq b_{i}.

(2)

Equivalently, one can interpret
k
close factorizations of
N
as
k
close lattice points on the hyperbola
xy=N
. Previously, Cilleruelo and Jiménez-Urroz
[
2
,
3
]
and Granville and Jiménez-Urroz
[
4
]
did some study on this and related problem. Suppose
(A,B)
,
(A+a_{1},B-b_{1})
and
(A+a_{2},B-b_{2})
with
1\leq a_{1}<a_{2}
and
1\leq b_{1}<b_{2}
are three lattice points on the hyperbola
xy=N
. Then Theorem
1
or
3
tells us that

\max(a_{2},b_{2})>(4\max(A,B))^{1/3}\geq 2^{2/3}N^{1/6}.

(3)

On the other hand, there are infinitely many
N
’s (namely the example in Theorem
3
) such that the hyperbola
xy=N
contains three such lattice points with

a_{2}-2\leq(4B)^{1/3}<(4A)^{1/3}\;\;\text{ or }\;\;b_{2}<a_{2}<2^{2/3}N^{1/6}+2.

(4)

Now, observe that

A-B=\frac{a_{2}(a_{2}+1)(a_{2}-3)}{4}-\frac{(a_{2}-2)(a_{2}-1)^{2}}{4}=\frac{a_{2}^{2}-4a_{2}+1}{2}>\frac{a_{2}^{2}}{4}>\frac{N^{1/3}}{2}

when
a_{2}>8
. Hence, either
A-\sqrt{N}
or
\sqrt{N}-B
is greater than
\frac{N^{1/3}}{4}
. So, the above three-close-lattice-points examples cannot be too close to the center point
(\sqrt{N},\sqrt{N})
of the hyperbola
xy=N
. This phenomenon was, for example, noticed by Cilleruelo and Jiménez-Urroz
[
3
]
. It leads to the following question.

Question 1
.

Suppose
(x_{1},y_{1})
,
(x_{2},y_{2})
,
(x_{3},y_{3})
are three positive lattice points on
xy=N
. What can we say about the maximal
L^{1}
-distance

\max_{i}|x_{i}-\sqrt{N}|+|y_{i}-\sqrt{N}|\;\;\Bigl(=\max_{i}|x_{i}-y_{i}|\text{ as }x_{i}\leq\sqrt{N}\leq\frac{N}{x_{i}}\text{ or }x_{i}\geq\sqrt{N}\geq\frac{N}{x_{i}}\Bigr)

between the lattice points and the center
(\sqrt{N},\sqrt{N})
?

We have the following optimal answer which reiterates
[
3
]
on the contrast between the exponent
1/4
and the exponent
1/6
in (
3
) and (
4
).

Theorem 4
.

Suppose
xy=N
has three positive lattice points
(x_{1},y_{1})
,
(x_{2},y_{2})
and
(x_{3},y_{3})
. Then, for
N\geq 6
,

\max_{i}|x_{i}-y_{i}|\geq\Big\lfloor 2N^{1/4}+\frac{1}{2N^{1/4}-1}\Big\rfloor.

Moreover, the lower bound is best possible. Here
\lfloor x\rfloor
is the largest integer not exceeding
x
.

Similar to the polynomial idea in
[
4
]
, based on (
12
) and (
13
) with
K=99
, we have

N=99990000=9900\cdot 10100=9999\cdot 10000=10100\cdot 9900

with

10100-9900=200\;\;\text{ and }\;\;2N^{1/4}+\frac{1}{2N^{1/4}-1}=200.00397\ldots.

However, when one looks at the extremal examples for Theorem
4
, not all of the three lattice points are to the right or to the left of the center point. This motivates the next question.

Question 2
.

Suppose
(x_{1},y_{1})
,
(x_{2},y_{2})
,
(x_{3},y_{3})
are three lattice points on
xy=N
with
\sqrt{N}<x_{1}<x_{2}<x_{3}
. What can we say about
\max_{i}|x_{i}-y_{i}|
?

Theorem 5
.

Suppose
xy=N
has three lattice points
(x_{1},y_{1})
,
(x_{2},y_{2})
and
(x_{3},y_{3})
with
\sqrt{N}<x_{1}<x_{2}<x_{3}
. Then, for
N\geq 2^{20}
,

\max_{i}|x_{i}-y_{i}|\geq\lfloor 2\sqrt{2}N^{1/4}+1/2\rfloor.

Moreover, the lower bound is best possible. Note: The lower bound
\max_{i}|x_{i}-y_{i}|>2\sqrt{2}N^{1/4}-2
is much easier to establish.

For instance, we have the following example as a consequence:

N=5997600=2450\cdot 2448=2499\cdot 2400=2520\cdot 2380

with

|2520-2380|=140\;\;\text{ and }\;\;2\sqrt{2}N^{1/4}=139.9714\ldots.

Our construction using solutions of Pell equations may be compared to that in
[
3
]
using continued fraction convergents. We leave the interested readers to investigate optimal lower bounds for four or more lattice points.

The paper is organized as follows. First, we will establish Theorem
3
and use it to prove Theorem
2
when
\max(a_{2},b_{2})\geq 7
using Theorem
4
. Then we will prove the entire Theorem
2
via some finer analysis of lattice points close to the center point. Next, we will prove Theorem
4
and show some polynomial examples. Then we will show Theorem
5
with the easier lower bound
2\sqrt{2}N^{1/4}-2
which illustrates the essence of the method. Lastly, we will prove the entire Theorem
5
and provide examples from solutions of a Pell equation and a polynomial.

Proof of Theorem
3

Proof.

Expanding
AB=(A+a_{i})(B-b_{i})
and simplifying, we get
a_{i}B-b_{i}A=a_{i}b_{i}
. Dividing through by
a_{i}A
, we have

\frac{B}{A}-\frac{b_{i}}{a_{i}}=\frac{b_{i}}{A}.

(5)

Applying (
5
) with
i=1,2
and subtracting the two equations, we obtain

\frac{b_{1}}{a_{1}}-\frac{b_{2}}{a_{2}}=\frac{b_{2}-b_{1}}{A}\;\;\text{ or }\;\;A=\frac{a_{2}a_{1}(b_{2}-b_{1})}{D}

(6)

where

D:=a_{2}b_{1}-b_{2}a_{1}=(a_{2}-a_{1})b_{1}-(b_{2}-b_{1})a_{1}>0.

Analogously, dividing
a_{i}B-b_{i}A=a_{i}b_{i}
through by
b_{i}B
and subtracting the two equations with
i=1,2
, we also have

\frac{a_{2}}{b_{2}}-\frac{a_{1}}{b_{1}}=\frac{a_{2}-a_{1}}{B}\;\;\text{ or }\;\;B=\frac{b_{2}b_{1}(a_{2}-a_{1})}{D}.

(7)

Now, let
C=a_{2}=(1+\alpha)a_{1}
for some real number
\alpha>0
. Then

a_{1}=\frac{C}{1+\alpha}\;\;\text{ and }\;\;a_{2}-a_{1}=\alpha a_{1}=\frac{\alpha C}{1+\alpha}.

From the definition of
D
and
b_{1}\leq a_{1}-1
via (
2
),

b_{2}-b_{1}=\frac{(a_{2}-a_{1})b_{1}-D}{a_{1}}\leq\Bigl[\frac{\alpha C}{1+\alpha}\Bigl(\frac{C}{1+\alpha}-1\Bigr)-D\Bigr]\frac{1+\alpha}{C}=\frac{\alpha C}{1+\alpha}-\alpha-\frac{D(1+\alpha)}{C}.

Hence, it follows from (
6
) that

\displaystyle A=

\displaystyle\frac{a_{2}a_{1}(b_{2}-b_{1})}{D}\leq\frac{C}{D}\cdot\frac{C}{1+\alpha}\cdot\Bigl(\frac{\alpha C}{1+\alpha}-\alpha-\frac{D(1+\alpha)}{C}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha C^{3}}{D(1+\alpha)^{2}}-\frac{\alpha C}{D(1+\alpha)}-C=:t(\alpha).

The derivative

t^{\prime}(\alpha)=\frac{(1-\alpha)C^{3}}{D(1+\alpha)^{3}}-\frac{C^{2}}{D(1+\alpha)^{2}}

is positive when
0<\alpha<\frac{C-1}{C+1}
and is negative when
\alpha>\frac{C-1}{C+1}
. Therefore,
t(\alpha)
has a maximum at
\alpha=\frac{C-1}{C+1}
and

A\leq\frac{\frac{C-1}{C+1}\cdot C^{3}}{(1+\frac{C-1}{C+1})^{2}}-\frac{\frac{C-1}{C+1}\cdot C}{(1+\frac{C-1}{C+1})}-C=\frac{C(C-3)(C+1)}{4}.

as
D\geq 1
. One can see that the above equality can be achieved if and only if
D=1
,
a_{1}=\frac{C}{1+\frac{C-1}{C+1}}=\frac{C+1}{2}
and
b_{1}=a_{1}-1=\frac{C-1}{2}
are integers (i.e.,
C
is odd). Then
b_{2}=\frac{a_{2}b_{1}-D}{a_{1}}=C-2
. Hence,

a_{2}=2K+1,\;a_{1}=K+1,\;b_{2}=2K-1,\;b_{1}=K,\;A=(2K+1)(K+1)(K-1),\;B=(2K-1)K^{2}

and we have Theorem
3
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2
for
\max(a_{2},b_{2})\geq 7

Proof.

Recall
N=AB=(A+a_{1})(B-b_{1})=(A+a_{2})(B-b_{2})
with
1\leq a_{1}<a_{2}
and
1\leq b_{1}<b_{2}
. If
A\geq B
, then Theorem
1
follows from Theorem
3
. So, let us suppose
B>A
from now on.

Case 1:
B-b_{2}\geq A+a_{2}
. Then we can rearrange the factorizations as

N=(B-b_{2})(A+a_{2})=(B-b_{2}+(b_{2}-b_{1}))(A+a_{2}-(a_{2}-a_{1}))=(B-b_{2}+(b_{2}))(A+a_{2}-(a_{2})).

Applying Theorem
3
with
B-b_{2}
,
A+a_{2}
,
b_{2}-b_{1}
,
a_{2}-a_{1}
,
b_{2}
,
a_{2}
in place of
A
,
B
,
a_{1}
,
b_{1}
,
a_{2}
,
b_{2}
respectively, we get

B-b_{2}\leq\frac{1}{4}b_{2}(b_{2}-3)(b_{2}+1)

which gives Theorem
1
as
\frac{1}{4}x(x-3)(x+1)+x=\frac{1}{4}x(x-1)^{2}
. This identity shows the connection between the upper bounds in Theorems
2
and
3
.

Case 2:
B-b_{2}<A+a_{2}
. Then we have
B>\sqrt{N}>B-b_{2}
,
A<\sqrt{N}<A+a_{2}
and
a_{2}+b_{2}>B-A
. By Theorem
4
,

\max\{B-A,|(B-b_{1})-(A+a_{1})|,(A+a_{2})-(B-b_{2})\}\geq\lfloor 2N^{1/4}\rfloor.

If
(a_{1}+b_{1})-(B-A)\geq 0
, then
(a_{2}+b_{2})-(B-A)>(a_{1}+b_{1})-(B-A)\geq 0
. If
(a_{1}+b_{1})-(B-A)<0
, then
B-A>(B-A)-(a_{1}+b_{1})>0
. In either case, the middle term
|(B-b_{1})-(A+a_{1})|
cannot be the maximum. Therefore,

\max\{B-A,(a_{2}+b_{2})-(B-A)\}\geq\lfloor 2(AB)^{1/4}\rfloor\geq\lfloor 2\sqrt{A}\rfloor

(8)

Since
a_{2}+b_{2}>B-A>0
, the above inequality implies

2\max(a_{2},b_{2})-1\geq a_{2}+b_{2}-1>2\sqrt{A}-1\;\text{ or }\;A\leq\max(a_{2},b_{2})^{2}-1,

and

B<A+a_{2}+b_{2}\leq\max(a_{2},b_{2})^{2}+2\max(a_{2},b_{2})-1\leq\frac{1}{4}\max(a_{2},b_{2})(\max(a_{2},b_{2})-1)^{2}

for
\max(a_{2},b_{2})\geq 7
as
\frac{1}{4}x(x-1)^{2}-(x^{2}+2x-2)=\frac{1}{4}x(x-7)(x+1)+2
is positive and increasing when
x\geq 7
. This gives the easier version of Theorem
2
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2
for
\max(a_{2},b_{2})\geq 5

Proof.

To get down to
\max(a_{2},b_{2})\geq 5
, we need to do more careful analysis on the close factorizations in case 2 from the previous section.

Subcase 1:
B-b_{1}>A+a_{2}
. Then one can focus on the following three close factorizations

N=(A+a_{2})(B-b_{2})=(B-b_{1})(A+a_{1})=BA

instead. Since
B>B-b_{1}>A+a_{2}>\sqrt{N}
, we can apply Theorem
3
and get

A+a_{2}\leq\frac{1}{4}(B-A-a_{2})(B-A-a_{2}-3)(B-A-a_{2}+1)<\frac{1}{4}b_{2}(b_{2}-3)(b_{2}+1)

as
B-A-a_{2}<b_{2}
and
x(x-3)(x+1)
is an increasing function for
x\geq 3
. Hence,

B<A+a_{2}+b_{2}<\frac{1}{4}b_{2}(b_{2}-3)(b_{2}+1)+b_{2}

and we have Theorem
2
.

Subcase 2:
B-b_{1}<A+a_{2}<B
. Then one can focus on the following three close factorizations

N=(B-b_{2})(A+a_{2})=(B-b_{1})(A+a_{1})=(A+a_{2})(B-b_{2})

instead. This is of the form

N=\tilde{A}\tilde{B}=(\tilde{A}+\tilde{a}_{1})(\tilde{B}-\tilde{b}_{1})=(\tilde{A}+\tilde{a}_{2})(\tilde{B}-\tilde{b}_{2})

(9)

with

\tilde{A}=B-b_{2},\;\tilde{B}=A+a_{2},\;\tilde{a}_{1}=b_{2}-b_{1},\;\tilde{b}_{1}=a_{2}-a_{1},\;\tilde{a}_{2}=\tilde{b}_{2}=a_{2}+b_{2}+A-B.

One has
\tilde{a}_{2}+\tilde{b}_{2}=2(\tilde{B}-\tilde{A})
. We also have
\tilde{B}=A+a_{2}>B-b_{2}=\tilde{A}
and
\tilde{B}-\tilde{b}_{2}=B-b_{2}<A+a_{2}=\tilde{A}+\tilde{a}_{2}
. Hence, we are in case 2 above and can apply inequality (
8
) to get

b_{2}>A-B+a_{2}+b_{2}=\tilde{B}-\tilde{A}\geq\lfloor 2\sqrt{\tilde{A}}\rfloor\geq 2\sqrt{B-b_{2}}-1

Hence,

B<\Bigl(\frac{b_{2}+1}{2}\Bigr)^{2}+b_{2}

which gives Theorem
2
as
\frac{1}{4}x(x-1)^{2}-(\frac{x+1}{2})^{2}-x=\frac{1}{4}(x+1)(x^{2}-4x-1)
is positive and increasing for
x\geq 5
.

Subcase 3:
B-b_{1}<B\leq A+a_{2}
. Then one can focus on the following three close factorizations

N=AB=(A+a_{1})(B-b_{1})=BA

instead. This is of the form

N=\tilde{A}\tilde{B}=(\tilde{A}+\tilde{a}_{1})(\tilde{B}-\tilde{b}_{1})=(\tilde{A}+\tilde{a}_{2})(\tilde{B}-\tilde{b}_{2})

with

\tilde{A}=A,\;\tilde{B}=B,\;\tilde{a}_{1}=a_{1},\;\tilde{b}_{1}=b_{1},\;\tilde{a}_{2}=\tilde{b}_{2}=B-A.

One has
\tilde{B}-\tilde{b}_{2}=A<B=\tilde{A}+\tilde{a}_{2}
and
\tilde{a}_{2}+\tilde{b}_{2}=2(\tilde{B}-\tilde{A})
. Hence, we are in case 2 above and can apply inequality (
8
) to get

a_{2}\geq B-A=\tilde{B}-\tilde{A}\geq\lfloor 2\sqrt{\tilde{A}}\rfloor\geq 2\sqrt{A}-1\;\;\text{ or }\;\;A\leq\Bigl(\frac{a_{2}+1}{2}\Bigr)^{2}.

Hence,

B\leq A+a_{2}\leq\Bigl(\frac{a_{2}+1}{2}\Bigr)^{2}+a_{2}

which gives Theorem
2
in the same way as subcase 2 above.

Subcase 4:
B-b_{1}=A+a_{2}
. Since
(A+a_{1})(B-b_{1})=(A+a_{2})(B-b_{2})
, we also have
B-b_{2}=A+a_{1}
. Therefore,
B-A=a_{2}+b_{1}=a_{1}+b_{2}
. From
AB=(A+a_{2})(B-b_{2})
, we have

a_{2}(B-A)-(b_{2}-a_{2})A=a_{2}b_{2}\;\;\text{ and }\;\;(a_{2}-b_{2})B+b_{2}(B-A)=a_{2}b_{2}

Substituting
B-A=a_{1}+b_{2}
and
B-A=a_{2}+b_{1}
to the above equations respectively, we get

(b_{2}-a_{2})A=a_{1}a_{2}\;\;\text{ and }\;\;(b_{2}-a_{2})B=b_{1}b_{2}.

If
b_{2}-a_{2}\geq 2
, the above yields
A,B\leq\frac{\max(a_{2},b_{2})^{2}}{2}
which gives Theorem
2
as
\frac{1}{4}x(x-1)^{2}-\frac{x^{2}}{2}=\frac{1}{4}x(x^{2}-4x+1)
is positive and increasing for
x\geq 4
. It remains to deal with the situation where
b_{2}=a_{2}+1
. Then we have
A=a_{1}a_{2}
and
B=b_{1}b_{2}
. Substituting these into
AB=(A+a_{2})(B-b_{2})
, we obtain
b_{1}=a_{1}+1
. Hence,

A=a_{1}a_{2}\;\;\text{ and }\;\;B=(a_{1}+1)(a_{2}+1)

with
1\leq a_{1}<a_{2}
. The three close factorizations are

a_{1}a_{2}\cdot(a_{1}+1)(a_{2}+1)=a_{2}(a_{1}+1)\cdot a_{1}(a_{2}+1)=a_{1}(a_{2}+1)\cdot(a_{1}+1)a_{2}.

We have

and

when

Proof of Theorem
4

Proof.

To prove the theorem, it suffices to show that
\max_{i}|x_{i}-y_{i}|>2N^{1/4}-1+\frac{1}{2N^{1/4}-1}
as
\lfloor 2N^{1/4}+\frac{1}{2N^{1/4}-1}\rfloor
is the smallest integer greater than
2N^{1/4}-1+\frac{1}{2N^{1/4}-1}
. Without loss of generality, we focus on the situation where
x_{1}<x_{2}\leq\sqrt{N}
. The case
x_{3}>x_{2}\geq\sqrt{N}
is similar by symmetry as
y_{3}<y_{2}\leq\sqrt{N}
. Then
y_{1}>y_{2}\geq\sqrt{N}
. If
|x_{2}-y_{2}|>2N^{1/4}-1/2
, then we are done as
N\geq 6
. Suppose
|x_{2}-y_{2}|\leq 2N^{1/4}-1/2
. Observe that

y_{2}-\sqrt{N}=\frac{N}{x_{2}}-\sqrt{N}=\frac{\sqrt{N}}{x_{2}}(\sqrt{N}-x_{2})\geq\sqrt{N}-x_{2}.

Hence,

\sqrt{N}-x_{2}\leq\frac{(y_{2}-\sqrt{N})+(\sqrt{N}-x_{2})}{2}\leq N^{1/4}-\frac{1}{4}\;\;\text{ or }\;\;x_{2}\geq\sqrt{N}-N^{1/4}+\frac{1}{4}.

Say
x_{1}=x_{2}-b
and
y_{1}=y_{2}+a
for some integers
a,b>0
. Then

(x_{2}-b)(y_{2}+a)=x_{2}y_{2}\;\;\text{ or }\;\;(a-b)x_{2}=ab+b(y_{2}-x_{2})>0.

(10)

Hence,
a>b
and

\displaystyle\sqrt{N}-N^{1/4}+\frac{1}{4}\leq x_{2}\leq

\displaystyle\Bigl(\frac{a+b}{2}\Bigr)^{2}-\Bigl(\frac{a-b}{2}\Bigr)^{2}+\Bigl(\frac{a+b}{2}\Bigr)(y_{2}-x_{2})-\Bigl(\frac{a-b}{2}\Bigr)(y_{2}-x_{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Bigl(\frac{a+b}{2}\Bigr)^{2}+\Bigl(\frac{a+b}{2}\Bigr)(y_{2}-x_{2})-\frac{3}{4}

(11)

Now, consider

|x_{1}-y_{1}|=|(x_{2}-b)-(y_{2}+a)|=(y_{2}-x_{2})+(a+b)

Set
\Sigma:=a+b
and
\Delta:=y_{2}-x_{2}
. From (
5
), we have

4\sqrt{N}-4N^{1/4}+5<\Sigma^{2}+2\Sigma\Delta+1\leq(\Sigma+\Delta)^{2},

and

|x_{1}-y_{1}|>\sqrt{(2N^{1/4}-1)^{2}+4}\geq 2N^{1/4}-1+\frac{1}{2N^{1/4}-1}

as
\sqrt{1+x}\geq 1+x/4
for
0\leq x\leq 8
. This gives Theorem
4
. The lower bound is optimal in view of the following examples: For any integer
K\geq 1
,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}x_{1}=K^{2}+K=K(K+1),&y_{1}=K^{2}+3K+2=(K+1)(K+2);\\
x_{2}=K^{2}+2K=K(K+2),&y_{2}=K^{2}+2K+1=(K+1)^{2};\\
x_{3}=K^{2}+2K+1=(K+1)^{2},&y_{3}=K^{2}+2K=K(K+2);\end{array}\right.

(12)

and

N=x_{1}y_{1}=x_{2}y_{2}=x_{3}y_{3}=K(K+1)^{2}(K+2).

(13)

Note that
N^{1/4}<K+1
and

\displaystyle(K+1)-N^{1/4}=

\displaystyle(K+1)\Bigl[1-\Bigl(\frac{K(K+2)}{(K+1)^{2}}\Bigr)^{1/4}\Bigr]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(K+1)\Bigl[1-\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{(K+1)^{2}}\Bigr)^{1/4}\Bigr]<\frac{1}{4(K+1)}+\frac{1}{4(K+1)^{3}}

as

(1-x)^{1/4}>1-\frac{x}{4}-\frac{x^{2}}{4}\;\;\text{ for }\;\;0<x\leq\frac{1}{2}

(14)

(which can easily be verified by raising both sides to the fourth power). Then one can check that

2N^{1/4}<\max_{i}|x_{i}-y_{i}|=2(K+1)<2N^{1/4}+\frac{1}{2N^{1/4}-1},

and we have the entire Theorem
4
.
∎

Note:

10
) and (
5
). We want
y_{2}-x_{2}
and
a-b
small. Hence, we set
a=K+1
,

and

) yields

. The lattice points
(x_{1},y_{1})
and
(x_{3},y_{3})
are simply
(x_{2}-b,y_{2}+a)
and

respectively.

One can construct other (non-optimal) polynomial examples easily. For instance, say
a=2K+1
,

and

. Then equation (

x_{2}=2K(3K+1)

(x_{1},y_{1})

Proof of Theorem
5
with lower bound
2\sqrt{2}N^{1/4}-2

Proof.

We revert to the strategy in Theorem
3
. With
\sqrt{N}\leq x_{1}<x_{2}<x_{3}
, suppose

(x_{1},y_{1})=(A,B),\;\;(x_{2},y_{2})=(A+a_{1},B-b_{1}),\;\;(x_{3},y_{3})=(A+a_{2},B-b_{2})

for
1\leq a_{1}<a_{2}
and
1\leq b_{1}<b_{2}
. The equality
N=AB=(A+a_{i})(B-b_{i})
gives

a_{i}B-b_{i}A=a_{i}b_{i}\;\;\text{ or }\;\;(a_{i}-b_{i})B=a_{i}b_{i}+b_{i}(A-B).

Set
d_{i}=a_{i}-b_{i}
for
i=1,2
. Note that
d_{i}B\geq a_{i}b_{i}>0
. So,
d_{1},d_{2}>0
. Since
D=a_{2}b_{1}-b_{2}a_{1}=(a_{2}-b_{2})b_{1}-(a_{1}-b_{1})b_{2}=d_{2}b_{1}-d_{1}b_{2}>0
and
b_{2}>b_{1}
, we must have
d_{2}>d_{1}\geq 1
. We also have

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}d_{1}B=b_{1}(b_{1}+d_{1})+b_{1}(A-B),\\

(15)

Note that
\max_{i}|x_{i}-y_{i}|
occurs at

as
|x-y|=x-\frac{N}{x}
is an increasing function of
x
when

. Hence, our goal is to study

x_{3}-y_{3}=(A+a_{2})-(B-b_{2})=(A-B)+(2b_{2}+d_{2}).

(16)

From (
15
), we have

A-B=\frac{d_{2}B}{b_{2}}-b_{2}-d_{2}.

(17)

Thus,

\displaystyle x_{3}-y_{3}=

\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}B}{b_{2}}-b_{2}-d_{2}+(2b_{2}+d_{2})=\frac{d_{2}B}{b_{2}}+b_{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{\frac{d_{2}B}{b_{2}}\cdot b_{2}}=2\sqrt{d_{2}B}=2\sqrt{d_{2}}\Bigl(\frac{B}{A}\Bigr)^{1/4}N^{1/4}

(18)

by the inequality

and

. If

,

and

), we have

7
Proof of the entire Theorem
5

We shall refine the analysis from the previous section and need the following lemmas.

Lemma 1
.

Suppose
A\geq B>0
with
A-B\leq C
. Then

\frac{B}{A}\geq 1-\frac{C}{A}.

Proof.

This follows easily by dividing
A-B\leq C
through by
A
and rearranging terms.
∎

Lemma 2
.

If two real numbers
0<x\leq y
have difference
y-x\geq d
, then

x+y\geq 2\sqrt{xy}+\frac{d^{2}}{4y}.

Proof.

Observe that

\sqrt{y}-\sqrt{x}=\frac{y-x}{\sqrt{y}+\sqrt{x}}\geq\frac{d}{2\sqrt{y}}.

The lemma follows after squaring both sides and rearranging terms.
∎

Proof of Theorem

.

for

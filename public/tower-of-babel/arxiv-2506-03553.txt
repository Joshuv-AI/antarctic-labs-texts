Three-Majorana Cotunneling Interferometer for Non-Abelian Braiding and Topological Quantum Gate Implementation Preprint: APS/123-QED
Zhen Chen, Yijia Wu, X. C. Xie

Abstract
We propose a novel scheme for performing Majorana zero mode (MZM) braiding utilizing cotunneling processes in a three-MZM system incorporating reference arms. This approach relies on the interference between cotunneling paths through the MZMs and reference arms, establishing an effective, tunable coupling between the MZMs. The strength and sign of this coupling can be manipulated via the reference arms and applied magnetic flux. Notably, the introduction of a half quantum flux reverses the coupling sign, enabling an echo-like protocol to eliminate dynamic phases during braiding. Our setup, requiring only three MZMs, represents a minimal platform for demonstrating non-Abelian braiding statistics. We demonstrate that this system facilitates the implementation of Clifford gates via braiding and, significantly, permits the realization of non-Clifford gates, such as the T T gate, by geometric phase, thereby offering a potential pathway towards universal topological quantum computation.

This effective coupling, shown as the Lamb term

;
Wiseman and Milburn 2009

As for the braiding process, we find that a crucial challenge in braiding schemes involving direct MZM hybridization is that both the desired topological (geometric) phase and the unwanted dynamic phase are simultaneously accumulated.
To solve this problem, we leverage the flux tunability of our cotunneling-mediated coupling to implement a spin echo-like technique.
By applying a half-quantum flux (
\Phi_{0}/2=h/2e

the sign of the effective MZM coupling is reversed (
t_{R,\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}\rightarrow-t_{R,\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}
),
allowing the dynamic phase acquired in the first half of the operation to be cancelled by the dynamic phase accumulated in the second half.
In this way, the braiding outcome arises entirely from the non-Abelian geometric transformation associated with the braiding properties of MZMs.

Furthermore, achieving universal quantum computation requires supplementing the Clifford gates generated by braiding with at least one non-Clifford gate,

T

\pi/8

Such a

This reliance on dynamic phase becomes the Achilles’ heel of the MZM-based system, leaving it exposed to decoherence and unprotected by the topological mechanisms that shield the Clifford operations.
To improve this, we show that our 3-MZM cotunneling platform, by enabling precise control over the MZM couplings
(
\Delta_{01},\Delta_{02},\Delta_{12}

T
gate purely through
geometric phase manipulation. By guiding the system along a specific closed path in the coupling parameter space using an echo sequence
to cancel the dynamic phase, a desired geometric phase corresponding to the
T

We develop the theoretical framework for this cotunneling-assisted braiding scheme using second-order perturbation theory to derive the effective MZM Hamiltonian.
We further analyze the robustness of the proposed protocols by employing a quantum master equation approach and present numerical simulations that demonstrate the feasibility of performing high-fidelity Clifford gates and the non-Clifford

II.2
MZM-Mediated Cotunneling

As shown in Fig.
1
, the fourth MZM
\gamma_{3}
together with
\gamma_{0}
,
\gamma_{1}
, and
\gamma_{2}
can form two Dirac fermions with occupation numbers
n_{1}=\frac{1}{2}(1+i\gamma_{0}\gamma_{1})
and
n_{2}=\frac{1}{2}(1+i\gamma_{2}\gamma_{3})
. Without Coulomb interaction,
the four states
n_{1}=0,1
and
n_{2}=0,1
are degenerate at zero energy. The charging energy breaks this degeneracy.
By adjusting the gate charge

to match the Coulomb valley with the

H_{C}=\begin{cases}E_{C}&\text{if }n_{1}=n_{2}=0\\
0&\text{if }n_{1}=1,n_{2}=0\text{ or }n_{1}=0,n_{2}=1\\
E_{C}&\text{if }n_{1}=n_{2}=1\end{cases},

(6)

where
E_{C}=\frac{e^{2}}{2C}
comes from Eq. (
3
).
When the Coulomb blockade is strong, i.e.,

is the largest energy scale in Hamiltonian Eq. (
1
),
direct tunneling

between a lead and the island is energetically suppressed.
However, electrons can effectively transfer between different leads via second-order cotunneling processes.
These involve virtual transitions through the high-energy charged states of the island.
The transition probability can be calculated using 2nd order perturbation theory

;
Fu 2010
;
Qin et al. 2019
.
Equivalently, using a Schrieffer-Wolff transformation (detailed in Appendix
A
),
we derive an effective Hamiltonian describing these processes:

\tilde{H}_{T}=\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}=0}^{1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha=1\\
\alpha>\alpha^{\prime}\end{subarray}}^{2}\sum_{k,k^{\prime}}(\mathcal{O}_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}c_{\alpha k}^{\dagger}c_{\alpha^{\prime}k^{\prime}}+\text{H.c.}).

(7)

The MZM-dependent amplitude

is derived in Appendix
A

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\lambda_{\alpha}^{*}\lambda_{\alpha^{\prime}}}{E_{c}}\gamma_{\alpha}\gamma_{\alpha^{\prime}}

(8)

\displaystyle=t_{M,\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}i\gamma_{\alpha}\gamma_{\alpha^{\prime}},

(9)

where
t_{M,\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}=i\frac{2\lambda_{\alpha}^{*}\lambda_{\alpha^{\prime}}}{E_{c}}
,
This operator describes the amplitude for an electron to effectively tunnel from lead
\alpha^{\prime}
to lead
\alpha

II.3
Interference and Effective MZM Coupling

The total Hamiltonian term responsible for electron transfer between leads
\alpha^{\prime}
and
\alpha
involves both the direct reference arm path and the MZM-mediated cotunneling path:

H_{I}=\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}=0}^{1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha=1\\
\alpha>\alpha^{\prime}\end{subarray}}^{2}\sum_{k,k^{\prime}}(t_{R,\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}+\mathcal{O}_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}})(c_{\alpha,k}^{\dagger}c_{\alpha^{\prime},k^{\prime}}+\text{H.c.}).

(10)

When this interaction
H_{I}
couples the MZM system to the itinerant electron modes of the fermionic reservoirs, it leads to both dissipation and a coherent shift in the MZM energies,
known as the Lamb shift
Breuer and Petruccione 2002
;
Wiseman and Milburn 2009
,
H_{LS}
.
This coherent coupling arises specifically from the
interference between the two pathways (
t_{R,\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}
and
\mathcal{O}_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}
).
A full derivation using master equations, outlined in Appendix
B
,
averages out the lead degrees of freedom to obtain the effective Hamiltonian governing the coherent MZM dynamics as:

\displaystyle H_{LS}

\displaystyle\approx-\nu^{2}\Lambda\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}=0}^{1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha=1\\
\alpha>\alpha^{\prime}\end{subarray}}^{2}\left(t_{R,\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}+\text{H.c.}\right)

\displaystyle=-2\nu^{2}\Lambda\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}=0}^{1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha=1\\
\alpha>\alpha^{\prime}\end{subarray}}^{2}\operatorname{Re}\left(t_{R,\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}^{*}t_{M,\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}\right)i\gamma_{\alpha}\gamma_{\alpha^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}=0}^{1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha=1\\
\alpha>\alpha^{\prime}\end{subarray}}^{2}\Delta_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}i\gamma_{\alpha}\gamma_{\alpha^{\prime}},

(11)

where
\nu
is the density of states in the leads (assumed constant near the Fermi level) and
\Lambda
is a bandwidth cutoff.
The coupling strength
\Delta_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}
:

\displaystyle\Delta_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=-2\nu^{2}\Lambda\operatorname{Re}\left(t_{R,\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}^{*}t_{M,\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}\right)

(12)

\displaystyle=-2\nu^{2}\Lambda\left|t_{R,\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}^{*}t_{M,\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}\right|\cos\left(\frac{2\pi\Phi_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}}{\Phi_{0}}\right).

Here
\Phi_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}
is the phase difference between the direct tunneling amplitude (
t_{R,\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}
)
and the MZM-mediated cotunneling amplitude (
t_{M,\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}
),
and
\Phi_{0}=h/e
is the flux quantum. The tunability of the magnitude
and sign of
\Delta_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}
via the Aharonov-Bohm phase (
2\pi\Phi_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}/\Phi_{0}
)
is the central mechanism enabling the braiding protocols described later,
particularly in cancelling out the dynamic phases.

The interference term
\operatorname{Re}(t_{R,\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}^{*}t_{M,\alpha\alpha^{\prime}})
not only generates the coherent coupling
\Delta_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}
but also contributes to the electrical conductance

G_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}\approx(2e^{2}/h)2\pi^{2}\nu^{2}(|t_{R,\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}|^{2}+|t_{M,\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}|^{2}+2\operatorname{Re}(t_{R,\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}^{*}t_{M,\alpha\alpha^{\prime}})i\gamma_{\alpha}\gamma_{\alpha^{\prime}})
,
with experimental observation of
h/e
-periodic oscillations confirming this interference
Whiticar et al. 2020
.
Comparing the Lamb shift term
|(H_{LS})_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}|\approx 2\nu^{2}\Lambda\operatorname{Re}(t_{R,\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}^{*}t_{M,\alpha\alpha^{\prime}})
with quantum interference contribution to conductance
|\Delta G_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}|=(2e^{2}/h)4\pi^{2}\nu^{2}\operatorname{Re}(t_{R,\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}^{*}t_{M,\alpha\alpha^{\prime}})
,
we estimate the MZM coupling strength as
|\Delta_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}|\sim\Lambda|\Delta G_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}|/((2e^{2}/h)2\pi^{2})
.
Using typical measurement results for the conductance oscillation amplitude
from Ref.
Whiticar et al. 2020
,
|\Delta G_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}|/(2e^{2}/h)\sim 7.5\times 10^{-3}
and
\Lambda\sim 1
eV for InAs,
we obtain
|\Delta_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}|\approx 0.38
meV, corresponding to an angular frequency
\omega_{\Delta}=|\Delta_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}|/\hbar\approx 92
GHz, suggesting experimentally feasible coherent braiding operations within typical MZM coherence time.

This correspondence between cotunneling coherent coupling and quantum interference contribution to conductance also provides a handy tool to measure,
calibrate, and fine-tune the coherent coupling strength and sign,
which provides us with certain convenience to implement the braiding operations.
Furthermore, the conductance measurement itself can probe the qubit state, which can be integrated into the
quantum computation process naturally.
In the following section, we will discuss how to manipulate the cotunneling coherent coupling to implement
braiding operations to form Clifford gates and implement non-Clifford gates,
demonstrating that this platform together with the aforementioned measurement convenience can be highly effective for forming a complete topological computing platform.

The fundamental concept involves utilizing the control gate to modulate

\Delta_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}
in Eq. (

As illustrated in Fig.

\Delta_{21}\rightarrow\Delta_{10}\rightarrow\Delta_{20}\rightarrow\Delta_{21}

When performed adiabatically over a time interval

U_{12}=\exp(\frac{\pi}{4}\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2})

However, an important distinction arises between our proposed triangle junction configuration and the conventional Y-junction shown in Fig.
2
.
In the Y-junction, ideally two MZMs undergoing braiding remain decoupled and maintain zero energy throughout the process.
In contrast, within our triangle junction braiding scheme,
the MZMs being braided experience a coupling throughout the braiding and their eigenenergies are not zero.
This non-zero eigenenergy inevitably leads to the accumulation of a dynamic phase
U_{\mathrm{dyn}}=\exp(-i\int_{0}^{T_{\mathrm{braid}}}E_{i}(t)dt)
, where
E_{i}(t)
represents the instantaneous
i^{\mathrm{th}}
state energy.
Such dynamic phase accumulation is generally uncontrollable and undermines the topological protection that makes MZM braiding valuable for quantum computation.

In fact, even in the Y-junction configuration,
real physical implementations inevitably introduce finite-size effects that lead to non-zero MZM couplings.
These couplings, though typically small, still result in dynamic phase accumulation during braiding operations,
presenting a similar challenge as in our triangle junction scheme.
This observation suggests that the dynamic phase cancellation technique to be introduced below
could have broader implications beyond the triangle junction architecture.

To eliminate the dynamic phase accumulation in our triangle braiding scheme, we employ a spin echo-like technique, enabled by the flux tunability of the MZM couplings [see Eq. (
12

\Phi_{0}/2
through the interference loops reverses the sign of the reference arm tunneling amplitudes
t_{R,\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}
, consequently inverting the sign of the effective MZM couplings:
\Delta_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}\rightarrow-\Delta_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}
.

To illustrate the dynamic phase cancellation strategy, we consider the NOT gate as an example first.

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1+\gamma_{1}\gamma_{0}}{\sqrt{2}}\cdot\dfrac{1+\gamma_{1}\gamma_{0}}{\sqrt{2}}

(13)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{1}\gamma_{0}

\displaystyle=-i\sigma_{x}.

V
Conclusion

In summary, we have presented a novel approach to Majorana zero mode braiding that leverages cotunneling processes and interference effects in a 3-MZM system.
Our scheme offers several key advantages: minimal scheme requiring only three MZMs, tunable coupling through interference effects, and dynamic phase cancellation via flux control.
Most importantly, we demonstrate that this platform can implement both Clifford gates and non-Clifford gates such as the
T
gate solely by geometric phases, providing a complete set of gates for universal quantum computation while maintaining topological protection.
The numerical simulations using quantum master equations validate our theoretical framework and demonstrate the robustness of the protocol.

Appendix A
Derivation of the MZM-Mediated Cotunneling Operator

From the main text, we know the central system Hamiltonian is composed of the charging energy and tunneling terms:

H_{0}=H_{C}+H_{T}.

(19)

By defining the fermion parity operator product as:

P_{0123}=i\gamma_{0}\gamma_{1}i\gamma_{2}\gamma_{3}.

(20)

The charging Hamiltonian Eq. (
6
) from main text simplifies to:

H_{C}=E_{C}\frac{1+P_{0123}}{2}.

(21)

As established in Eq. (
6
), this charging energy creates distinct energy sectors: the low energy sector corresponds to states where
H_{C}=0
(i.e.,
n_{1}+n_{2}=1
), while the high energy sector corresponds to states where
H_{C}=E_{C}
(i.e.,
n_{1}=n_{2}=0
or
n_{1}=n_{2}=1
). The tunneling Hamiltonian
H_{T}
connects these energy sectors by flipping the fermion parity.
To derive the effective low-energy Hamiltonian, we apply a Schrieffer-Wolff transformation,
which transforms the Hamiltonian into a new basis where it is block-diagonal in these energy sectors perturbatively.
This transformation’s generator is an anti-Hermitian operator
S
that satisfies:

[S,H_{C}]=-H_{T}.

(22)

To find such operator
S
, we first note that
H_{T}
alternates between the low and high energy sectors since it always flips the parity product,
thus we can separate it into parts that raise and lower the energy:

H_{T}=H_{T}^{+}+H_{T}^{-},

(23)

where
H_{T}^{+}
raises the system from low to high energy sector, and
H_{T}^{-}
lowers it from high to low energy sector.
Therefore, we can determine the form of
S
as:

S=\frac{1}{E_{C}}(H_{T}^{+}-H_{T}^{-}).

(24)

Then using the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula, we have:

H^{\prime}=e^{S}He^{-S}=H+[S,H]+\frac{1}{2}[S,[S,H]]+\cdots.

(25)

Substituting our Hamiltonian components Eq. (
19
) and Eq. (
22
) into Eq. (
25
), we have:

H_{0}^{\prime}=H_{C}+\frac{1}{2}[S,H_{T}]+\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{E_{C}^{2}}H_{T}^{3}).

(26)

Now, we substitute Eq. (
24
) into Eq. (
26
), we have:

H_{0}^{\prime}=H_{C}+\frac{1}{E_{C}}[H_{T}^{+},H_{T}^{-}]+\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{E_{C}^{2}}H_{T}^{3}).

(27)

When acting Eq. (
28
) on the low-energy sector, using the fact that

H_{C}|\mathrm{low}\rangle=0
and
H_{T}^{-}|\mathrm{low}\rangle=0
, we have:

H_{low}=-\frac{1}{E_{C}}H_{T}^{-}H_{T}^{+}.

(28)

These two operators
H_{T}^{+}
and
H_{T}^{-}
represent complementary virtual tunneling pathways:

•

Pathway 1: Lead
\alpha^{\prime}\rightarrow
island (
H^{+}=\lambda_{\alpha^{\prime}}\gamma_{\alpha^{\prime}}c_{\alpha^{\prime},k}
) then island
\rightarrow
Lead
\alpha
(
H^{-}=\lambda_{\alpha}^{*}c_{\alpha,k}^{\dagger}\gamma_{\alpha}
)

•

Pathway 2: island
\rightarrow
Lead
\alpha
(
H^{+}=\lambda_{\alpha}^{*}c_{\alpha,k}^{\dagger}\gamma_{\alpha}
) then Lead
\alpha^{\prime}\rightarrow
island (
H^{-}=\lambda_{\alpha^{\prime}}\gamma_{\alpha^{\prime}}c_{\alpha^{\prime},k}
)

These pathways combine to yield the effective cotunneling Hamiltonian in Eq. (
7
).

H_{I}=\sum_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}A_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}\otimes B_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}.

(29)

Note that Eq. (

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,k^{\prime}}c_{\alpha,k}^{\dagger}c_{\alpha^{\prime},k^{\prime}}.

and

(34)

(35)

\Gamma_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}\beta\beta^{\prime}}(\omega)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{i\omega t}\langle B_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}(t)B_{\beta\beta^{\prime}}(0)\rangle dt.

\displaystyle\overset{T\rightarrow 0}{\approx}-\frac{\Lambda\nu^{2}}{2}.

\int_{0}^{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sin((\omega-\omega_{j})t)dtF(\omega)d\omega=\mathbb{P}\int_{0}^{\Omega}\frac{F(\omega)}{\omega-\omega_{j}}d\omega.

(38)

Similar calculation can be done for

\displaystyle S_{12}=S_{21}

\displaystyle\times n(\xi)\left(1-n(\xi^{\prime})\right)\delta(\xi-\xi^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\pi\nu^{2}\int d\delta\xi\frac{\delta\xi}{\exp(\frac{\delta\xi}{T})-1}\delta(\delta\xi)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{21}=\Gamma_{12}

(41)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{01}=\Gamma_{02}

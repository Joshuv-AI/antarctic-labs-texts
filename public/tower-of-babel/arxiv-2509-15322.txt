Entropic balance with feedback control: information equalities and tight inequalities
N. Ruiz-Pino, A. Prados

Abstract
We consider overdamped physical systems evolving under a feedback-controlled fluctuating potential and in contact with a thermal bath at temperature T T . A Markovian description of the dynamics, which keeps only the last value of the control action, is advantageous—both from the theoretical and the practical side—for the entropy balance. Novel second-law equalities and bounds for the extractable work are obtained, the latter being both tighter and easier to evaluate than those in the literature based on the whole chain of controller actions. The Markovian framework also allows us to prove that the bound for the extractable work that incorporates the unavailable information is saturated in a wide class of physical systems, for error-free measurements. These results are illustrated in a model system. For imperfect measurements, there appears an interval of measurement uncertainty, including the point at which work ceases to be extracted, where the new Markovian bound is tighter than the unavailable information bound.

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{I}_{u}-\mathcal{I}_{\vec{x}}}=

\displaystyle\beta\sum_{c_{n}}\int dx_{n}dx_{n+1}P(x_{n},c_{n},x_{n+1})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\left[V(x_{n},c_{n})-V(x_{n+1},c_{n})\right],

(16)

where we have used the detailed balance condition

\frac{K(x_{n+1},\Delta t_{\text{m}}|x_{n},c_{n})}{K(x_{n},\Delta t_{\text{m}}|x_{n+1},c_{n})}=\frac{P_{\text{s}}(x_{n}|c_{n})}{P_{\text{s}}(x_{n+1}|c_{n})},

(17)

and the canonical shape of
P_{\text{s}}(x|c)
. Then,

\overline{\mathcal{I}_{u}-\mathcal{I}_{\vec{x}}}=\beta(\left\langle V\right\rangle^{+}-\left\langle V\right\rangle^{-})
,
i.e. Eq. (
11
) holds; pairs
\{x_{n},c_{n}\}
and
\{x_{n+1},c_{n}\}
correspond to times
t_{n}^{+}
and
t_{n+1}^{-}
, respectively.

For
f\neq 0
, detailed balance is not verified in general and Eq. (
11
) ceases to be valid. A generalisation thereof can be done in the limit
\Delta t_{\text{m}}\to\infty
, where the propagator of the Fokker-Planck equation tends to the non-equilibrium stationary distribution. By incorporating the housekeeping contribution to the entropy production, one gets—see Sec. VI of
SM-
for the proof,

\beta\left\langle W\right\rangle=\Sigma_{xc}^{\text{hk}}+\overline{\mathcal{I}_{u}-\mathcal{I}_{\vec{x}}},\;\;f\neq 0,\,\text{error-free},\,\Delta t_{\text{m}}\to\infty.

(18)

Inspired by a class of information engines in which the feedback controller tries to decrease the energy of the particle and output work is done by the controller at the measurements

The interesting regime for an information engine is
\left\langle W\right\rangle=\left\langle W\right\rangle_{\text{m}}+\left\langle W\right\rangle_{\text{FP}}<0

For the error-free case,
\Theta(c|x)=H(-c\,U(x))

Discussion–
The improvement on the tightness of second-law inequalities stemming from the Markovian description is appealing from a conceptual point of view: the Markovian description uses the least information needed to analyse the dynamics of the system by only retaining the value of the control variable that determines the potential felt by the system. Also, the Markovian second-law inequalities, as given by Eq. (
7
), quite accurately predict the range of parameters in which model systems are able to extract work and thus are useful as information engines—especially when the housekeeping contribution to the entropy production is kept. This is a task at which previous information inequalities in the literature, stemming from the chain description, fail.

Ashida et al. introduced the concept of unavailable information to improve the existing bounds for the extractable work
Ashida et al. 2014
. Within our Markovian modelling, we have shown analytically that the unavailable information’s bound saturates for error-free measurements in a wide class of systems; Szilard’s engine
Ashida et al. 2014
and the “lossless” information engine of Refs.
Paneru et al. 2020
;
Archambault et al. 2024
are just particular cases, for
\Delta t_{\text{m}}\to\infty
, of the general result (
11
) derived in this Letter. Furthermore, we have investigated how the unavailable information behaves under imperfect feedback protocols, a setting that remained largely unexplored. Interestingly, the unavailable information bound is no longer saturated and, in fact, there exist regions of measurement error in which the Markovian bound,
-\Delta_{\text{m}}I
, improves the unavailable information’s,
\overline{\mathcal{I}_{u}-\mathcal{I}_{\vec{x}}}
.

Information functions appear in the second law as a consequence of the implicit coarse-graining carried out when discarding the internal state of the feedback controller in the description of the system plus controller. Introducing a microscopic modelling of the internal state of the feedback controller and its interaction with the system would make it possible to identify the microscopic origin of the information function in the mesoscopic second law. This is a relevant question that deserves to be investigated. On the other hand, the framework developed here applies to overdamped systems, a typical experimental situation for information engines. Extending it to the underdamped regime
Dago et al. 2021
;
Dago and Bellon 2022
;
Dago et al. 2023
, which takes into account inertial effects, is another appealing perspective.

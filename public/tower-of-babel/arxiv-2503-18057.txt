Q Q -operators for the Ruijsenaars model
Eric Rains, Hjalmar Rosengren

Abstract
. We prove that the Ruijsenaars model admits a one-parameter commuting family of Q Q -operators. The commutativity is equivalent to an elliptic hypergeometric integral transformation that was conjectured by Gadde et al., and has an alternative interpretation in terms of S S -duality for quiver gauge theories. We present two proofs of this conjecture, one using the elliptic Macdonald polynomials of Langmann et al., and one using known results on elliptic hypergeometric integrals. We also explain how the Noumi–Sano operators appear as degenerations of Q Q -operators.



[2]

1.
Introduction

The Ruijsenaars model
[
R1
]
describes a system of relativistic quantum particles, whose Hamiltonian is given by a difference operator with elliptic coefficients. Ruijsenaars established the integrability of this model, by constructing higher order difference operators commuting with the Hamiltonian.
To study the joint eigenfunctions of these higher order Hamiltonians is a difficult problem, see e.g.
[
LNS
,
R2
,
R3
,
R4
]
.
The hyperbolic limit case is better understood, thanks to work of Hallnäs and Ruijsenaars
[
HR2
,
HR3
,
HR4
]

and Belousov et al.
[
B1
,
B2
,
B3
,
B4
,
BK
]
.
The latter series of papers is to a large extent based on
the existence of a commuting family of
Q
-operators
for the model.

Let us briefly discuss such operators on a formal level, working over
\mathbb{R}
for simplicity.
Suppose that
H
is a linear operator, which is symmetric on
\mathrm{L}^{2}(X,d\mu)
,
and
K=K(x,y)
a function on
X\times X
satisfying
the
kernel function identity

H_{x}K=H_{y}K
.
If we define

(Qf)(y)=\int_{X}K(x,y)f(x)\,d\mu(x),

then at least formally

\displaystyle(HQf)(y)

\displaystyle=\int H_{y}K(x,y)f(x)\,d\mu(x)=\int H_{x}K(x,y)f(x)\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\int K(x,y)H_{x}f(x)\,d\mu(x)=(QHf)(y),

(1.1)

that is,
[H,Q]=0
. Suppose now that
H
has a complete system of orthonormal eigenfunctions

(e_{k})_{k\in K}
. Then, we can formally construct kernel functions as

K(x,y)=\sum_{k\in K}a_{k}e_{k}(x)e_{k}(y),

(1.2)

where
a_{k}
are scalars. Since
Qe_{k}=a_{k}e_{k}
, any two operators from this family commute.

For the Ruijsenaars model, although a one-parameter family of explicit kernel functions
K=K_{c}
is known
[
R2
]
, it is not obvious that the corresponding operators
Q=Q_{c}
mutually commute. Until now, this has only be proved in the hyperbolic limit case
[
B1
]
.
The non-relativistic hyperbolic model (based on differential rather than difference operators) was treated in
[
H
]
.

Before we describe our results, we provide some explanation of why the word
Q
-operator is used in this context, see also
[
KS
,
S
]
.
Originally,
Q
-operators were introduced by Baxter in his solution of the eight-vertex model and XYZ spin chain
[
Ba1
,
Ba2
]
.
Just as the operators
Q_{c}
mentioned above, they form a one-parameter family commuting with the Hamiltonian.
However, the similarities go deeper. Pasquier and Gaudin
[
PG
]
found analogous operators for the periodic quantum Toda lattice, and discovered that a quasi-classical limit yields Bäcklund transformations for the corresponding classical system. Hallnäs and Ruijsenaars
[
HR1
]
obtained similar results for the Ruijsenaars model, showing that kernel functions can be used to obtain Bäcklund transformations of classical Calogero–Moser–Sutherland systems.
Another common feature of
Q
-operators, which was crucial in Baxter’s work, is that their eigenvalues satisfy a first-order equation in the parameter.
For the general Ruijsenaars model, nothing seems to be known about the
Q
-operator eigenvalues. However, in the non-relativistic hyperbolic case, Hallnäs found that they satisfy a first-order difference equation
[
H
]
.

In the present work, we show that the
Q
-operators for the Ruijsenaars model commute in the sense that
the integral kernel of
[Q_{c},Q_{d}]
vanishes identically. We will not discuss commutativity in a
Hilbert space setting, which would also involve analyzing the domain and range of the operators.
It turns out that vanishing of the commutator kernel is equivalent to a transformation formula for elliptic hypergeometric integrals conjectured by
Gadde, Rastelli, Razamat and Yan
[
G
]
. In their work, this identity appears from a seemingly very different context, namely, it expresses
S
-duality between superconformal indices of quiver gauge theories.
The one-dimensional case of this integral identity is due to van de Bult
[
Bu
]
and a hyperbolic version is proved in
[
B1
]
.
Moreover, a closely related transformation between finite sums was obtained by Langer et al.
[
LSW
]
. However, the general case has been open until now.

We will in fact give two proofs of the GRRY conjecture. The first proof, given in §
4
, relies on results of Langmann, Noumi and Shiraishi
[
LNS
]
. These authors show that the Ruijsenaars operators have joint eigenfunctions in a space of formal power series in the elliptic nome
p
. The constant term
p=0
gives the Macdonald polynomials
[
M
]
. We prove that these elliptic Macdonald polynomials are also eigenfunctions of the
Q
-operators. Hence, the
Q
-operators commute on a space of formal power series, which is enough to deduce that the commutator kernel vanishes. As a byproduct, we obtain a version of the identity (
1.2
) for elliptic Macdonald polynomials, see Corollary
4.5
.

In §
5
we give a proof based on known results for elliptic hypergeometric integrals. More precisely, an integral transformation due to the first author
[
Ra
]
implies that the commutator kernel vanishes when integrated against certain explicit test functions. We deduce from this that the kernel itself vanishes.

In
[
NS
]
, Noumi and Sano introduced an alternative family of difference operators commuting with the Ruijsenaars Hamiltonian.
In §
6
we show that the Noumi–Sano operators appear as degenerate cases of the
Q
-operators, for singular values of the parameter
c
.
To be precise, this relation also involves a “change of gauge”, which is a well-known symmetry of the Ruijsenaars model. A more complete picture appears if one considers four families of operators
Q_{c}
,
\tilde{Q}_{c}
,
H^{(k)}
and
\tilde{H}^{(k)}
, where
\tilde{Q}_{c}
are gauge transformed versions of the
Q
-operators and
H^{(k)}
and
\tilde{H}^{(k)}
two corresponding versions of the Noumi–Sano operators. As we describe in §
7
, each pair of such operators commute thanks to a transformation formula for elliptic hypergeometric functions.

2.
Notation

Throughout, the parameters
p
,
q
and
t
are assumed to satisfy
|p|<1
,
|q|<1
and
|pq|<|t|<1
. Recall the standard notation

(x;q)_{\infty}=\prod_{j=0}^{\infty}(1-xq^{j}).

We will use the
multiplicative theta function

\theta_{p}(x)=(x;p)_{\infty}(p/x;p)_{\infty}

and Ruijsenaars’s elliptic gamma function

\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(x)=\prod_{j,k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1-p^{j+1}q^{k+1}/x}{1-p^{j}q^{k}x}.

(2.1)

We will often use the identities

\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(qx)=\theta_{p}(x)\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(x),

\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(pq/x)}.

(2.2)

Repeated arguments in one-variable functions stand for products, e.g.,

(x,y;q)_{\infty}=(x;q)_{\infty}(y;q)_{\infty},\hskip 23.49976pt\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(xy^{\pm})=\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(xy)\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(x/y).

We will write

\displaystyle\Lambda

\displaystyle=\Lambda^{n}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{N}^{n};\,\lambda_{1}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{n}\geq 0\},

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{\infty}^{n}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}^{n};\,\lambda_{1}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{n}\},

(2.3)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{0}

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{0}^{n}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{N}^{n};\,\lambda_{1}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{n}=0\}.

On each of these sets, we define the dominance order
\lambda\leq\mu
as

\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{j}\leq\mu_{1}+\dots+\mu_{j},\hskip 23.49976ptj=1,\dots,n,

where we require that equality holds for
j=n
.

3.
Ruijsenaars operators and
Q
-operators

The higher order Ruijsenaars operators are defined by

D^{(k)}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\},\\
|I|=k\end{subarray}}\,\prod_{i\in I,j\notin I}\frac{\theta_{p}(tx_{i}/x_{j})}{\theta_{p}(x_{i}/x_{j})}\prod_{i\in I}T_{q,x_{i}},

(3.1)

where
(T_{q,x}f)(x)=f(qx)
.
It was proved by Ruijsenaars
[
R1
]
that

[D^{(k)},D^{(l)}]=0,\hskip 23.49976pt0\leq k,l\leq n.

The operators
D^{(k)}
have the kernel functions
[
R2
]

K_{c}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y})=\prod_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\frac{\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(cx_{i}y_{j})}{\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(ctx_{i}y_{j})},

(3.2)

that is,

D^{(k)}_{\mathbf{x}}K_{c}=D^{(k)}_{\mathbf{y}}K_{c}.

(3.3)

Moreover, they are formally symmetric with respect to the pairing

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}f(\mathbf{x})g(\mathbf{x}^{-1})\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}\frac{\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(tx_{i}/x_{j})}{\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(x_{i}/x_{j})}\,|\textup{d}\mathbf{x}|,

(3.4)

where

\mathbb{T}^{n}=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{C}^{n};\,|x_{1}|=\dots=|x_{n}|=1\},

|\textup{d}\mathbf{x}|=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\textup{d}x_{j}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}x_{j}}.

This suggests studying integral operators

f\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}f(\mathbf{x})\prod_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\frac{\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(cy_{j}/x_{i})}{\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(cty_{j}/x_{i})}\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}\frac{\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(tx_{i}/x_{j})}{\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(x_{i}/x_{j})}\,|\textup{d}\mathbf{x}|.

(3.5)

We will focus on a refined version of the operators (
3.5
), where the variables are constrained by the balancing condition

x_{1}\dotsm x_{n}=y_{1}\dotsm y_{n}.

(3.6)

More precisely, let

\mathbb{T}^{n-1}_{r}=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{C}^{n};\,|x_{1}|=\dots=|x_{n}|,\,x_{1}\dotsm x_{n}=r\}.

We then define

(Q_{c}f)(\mathbf{y})=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n-1}_{y_{1}\dotsm y_{n}}}f(\mathbf{x})\prod_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\frac{\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(cy_{j}/x_{i})}{\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(cty_{j}/x_{i})}\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}\frac{\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(tx_{i}/x_{j})}{\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(x_{i}/x_{j})}\,|\textup{d}\mathbf{x}|,

(3.7)

where now

|\textup{d}\mathbf{x}|=\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{\textup{d}x_{j}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}x_{j}}.

The kernel function in (
3.7
) has poles when
x_{i}\in p^{\mathbb{N}}q^{\mathbb{N}}cy_{j}
or
x_{i}\in p^{-\mathbb{N}-1}q^{-\mathbb{N}-1}cty_{j}
.
We require that the first type of poles are inside the circle
|x_{i}|=|y_{1}\dotsm y_{n}|^{1/n}
and the second type are outside.
This leads to the condition

|pq/t|<\left|\frac{cy_{j}}{(y_{1}\dotsm y_{n})^{1/n}}\right|<1,\hskip 23.49976ptj=1,\dots,n.

In particular, if we also assume
|y_{1}|=\dots=|y_{n}|
, then it is enough to require that

|pq/t|<|c|<1.

We can relax these conditions by deforming the domain of integration, see the proof of Proposition
6.1
. However,
for simplicity we focus on the situation when we can integrate over a torus.

By the same argument as in (
1.1
),
[D^{(k)},Q_{c}]=0
. We provide some details in the Appendix. However,
our main interest is in the identity
[Q_{c},Q_{d}]=0
. To formulate our main result, we note that

(Q_{c}Q_{d}f)(\mathbf{y})=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n-1}_{y_{1}\dotsm y_{n}}}f(\mathbf{x})K_{cd}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y})\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}\frac{\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(tx_{i}/x_{j})}{\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(x_{i}/x_{j})}\,|\textup{d}\mathbf{x}|,

where

K_{cd}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y})=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n-1}_{y_{1}\dotsm y_{n}}}\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}\frac{\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(tz_{i}/z_{j})}{\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(z_{i}/z_{j})}\prod_{i,j=1}^{n}\frac{\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(cy_{j}/z_{i},dz_{i}/x_{j})}{\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(cty_{j}/z_{i},dtz_{i}/x_{j})}\,|\textup{d}\mathbf{z}|.

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that
|p|<1
,
|q|<1
,
|t|<1
, that
x_{1}\dotsm x_{n}=y_{1}\dotsm y_{n}=r^{n}
and that

|pq/t|<|cr/x_{j}|,\,|dr/x_{j}|,\,|cy_{j}/r|,\,|dy_{j}/r|<1,\hskip 23.49976pt1\leq j\leq n.

(3.8)

Then, the identity
[Q_{c},Q_{d}]=0
holds in the sense that
K_{cd}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y})=K_{dc}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y})
.

As was mentioned in the introduction, an equivalent identity was conjectured by Gadde et al.
[
G
, (Eq. D.2)]
. To see this equivalence, one needs to apply (
2.2
) and use that, by
[
G
, (Eq. 2.14)]
, their parameter
t
(different from our
t
) satisfies
t^{6}=pq
.

Theorem
3.1
implies commutativity for the operators (
3.5
) and, more generally, for operators of the form

(Q_{c,\phi}f)(\mathbf{y})=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}f(\mathbf{x})\phi\left(\frac{x_{1}\dotsm x_{n}}{y_{1}\dotsm y_{n}}\right)\prod_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\frac{\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(cy_{j}/x_{i})}{\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(cty_{j}/x_{i})}\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}\frac{\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(tx_{i}/x_{j})}{\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(x_{i}/x_{j})}\,|\textup{d}\mathbf{x}|,

where
\phi
is an integrable function.

Corollary 3.2
.

Assume that
|p|<1
,
|q|<1
,
|t|<1
,
|pq/t|<|c|<1
and
|pq/t|<|d|<1
. Then, the integral kernel
of the commutator
[Q_{c,\phi},Q_{d,\psi}]
vanishes for
\mathbf{x},\,\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
.

Proof.

The integral kernel of
Q_{c,\phi}Q_{d,\psi}
is

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\phi(Z/Y)\psi(X/Z)\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}\frac{\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(tz_{i}/z_{j})}{\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(z_{i}/z_{j})}\prod_{i,j=1}^{n}\frac{\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(cy_{j}/z_{i},dz_{i}/x_{j})}{\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(cty_{j}/z_{i},dtz_{i}/x_{j})}\,|\textup{d}\mathbf{z}|,

where we use notation such as
X=x_{1}\dotsm x_{n}
. We split this as an outer integral over
Z\in\mathbb{T}
and and inner integral over
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{T}^{n}_{Z}
.
We also introduce the variables
\xi_{j}=x_{j}Z^{1/n}/X^{1/n}
,
\eta_{j}=y_{j}Z^{1/n}/Y^{1/n}
, so that

\xi_{1}\dotsm\xi_{n}=\eta_{1}\dotsm\eta_{n}=Z
. The kernel then takes the form

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\phi(Z/Y)\psi(X/Z)K_{cY^{1/n}/Z^{1/n},dZ^{1/n}/X^{1/n}}(\xi;\eta)|\textup{d}Z|.

Making the change of variables
Z\mapsto XY/Z
and applying Theorem
3.1
, it follows that the integral kernel is symmetric under interchanging
c
and
d
.
∎

4.
First proof of commutativity

Our first proof of Theorem
3.1
is based on the elliptic Macdonald polynomials introduced in
[
LNS
]
. We first
recall some standard facts about the Macdonald polynomials
P_{\lambda}(\mathbf{x})=P_{\lambda}(\mathbf{x};q,t)
, where
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
. They are originally defined for partitions
\lambda
, but using the property

P_{\lambda+(m)^{n}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=(x_{1}\dotsm x_{n})^{m}P_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})

(4.1)

one can extend the definition to

\lambda\in\Lambda_{\infty}
, see (
2.3
).
Under our standing assumption
|q|,\,|t|<1
, they satisfy the orthogonality relations

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}P_{\lambda}(\mathbf{x})P_{\mu}(\mathbf{x}^{-1})\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}\frac{(x_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}{(tx_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}\,|\textup{d}\mathbf{x}|=N_{\lambda}\delta_{\lambda,\mu},\hskip 23.49976pt\lambda,\,\mu\in\Lambda_{\infty},

(4.2)

and the Cauchy identity

\prod_{i,j=1}^{n}\frac{(tx_{i}y_{j};q)_{\infty}}{(x_{i}y_{j};q)_{\infty}}=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}b_{\lambda}P_{\lambda}(\mathbf{x})P_{\lambda}(\mathbf{y}),

(4.3)

where
N_{\lambda}
and
b_{\lambda}
are explicitly known constants and the series (
4.3
) converges for
\max_{i,j}(|x_{i}y_{j}|)\leq 1
.

We will need the following consequence of
(
4.2
) and (
4.3
).

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\lambda\in\Lambda_{\infty}
and assume that

|cy_{j}/(y_{1}\dotsm y_{n})^{1/n}|<1
for

1\leq j\leq n
. Then,

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n-1}_{y_{1}\dotsm y_{n}}}P_{\lambda}(\mathbf{x})\prod_{i,j=1}^{n}\frac{(cty_{j}/x_{i};q)_{\infty}}{(cy_{j}/x_{i};q)_{\infty}}\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}\frac{(x_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}{(tx_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}\,|\textup{d}\mathbf{x}|=\phi_{\lambda}(c)P_{\lambda}(\mathbf{y}),

(4.4)

where
\phi_{\lambda}
is analytic in
|c|<1
.

Proof.

It is clear that the left-hand side of (
4.4
)
is analytic in
c
under the stated assumption.
It follows from (
4.3
) that it has the Taylor expansion

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}c^{m}\sum_{\mu\in\Lambda,\,|\mu|=m}b_{\mu}P_{\mu}(\mathbf{y})\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n-1}_{y_{1}\dotsm y_{n}}}P_{\lambda}(\mathbf{x})P_{\mu}(\mathbf{x}^{-1})\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}\frac{(x_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}{(tx_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}\,|\textup{d}\mathbf{x}|.

Making the change of
variables
x_{j}\mapsto(y_{1}\dotsm y_{n})^{1/n}x_{j}
,
this can be written

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}c^{m}\sum_{\mu\in\Lambda,\,|\mu|=m}b_{\mu}P_{\mu}(\mathbf{y})(y_{1}\dotsm y_{n})^{(|\lambda|-|\mu|)/n}I_{\lambda,\mu},

(4.5)

where

I_{\lambda,\mu}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n-1}}P_{\lambda}(\mathbf{x})P_{\mu}(\mathbf{x}^{-1})\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}\frac{(x_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}{(tx_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}\,|\textup{d}\mathbf{x}|.

Since the result must be independent of the choice of the root
(y_{1}\dotsm y_{n})^{1/n}
,
I_{\lambda,\mu}
vanishes unless
|\lambda|-|\mu|\in n\mathbb{Z}
.
Assuming now that
|\mu|=|\lambda|+kn
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, consider (
4.2
) with
\mu\mapsto\mu-(k)^{n}
.
It can be written

N_{\lambda}\delta_{\lambda,\mu-(k)^{n}}=\int_{|s|=1}\int_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{T}^{n-1}_{s}}P_{\lambda}(\mathbf{x})P_{\mu-(k)^{n}}(\mathbf{x}^{-1})\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}\frac{(x_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}{(tx_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}\,|\textup{d}\mathbf{x}|\,|\textup{d}s|.

Making as before the change of variables

x_{j}\mapsto s^{1/n}x_{j}
and using (
4.1
),
the integration in
s
becomes trivial and
the inner integral reduces to
I_{\lambda,\mu}
.
Hence, for fixed
m=|\lambda|+kn
, only the
term
\mu=\lambda+(k)^{n}
contributes to the
inner sum in (
4.5
). The condition
\mu\in\Lambda

gives
k\geq-\lambda_{n}
.
We conclude that (
4.4
) holds with

\phi_{\lambda}(c)=N_{\lambda}\sum_{k=-\lambda_{n}}^{\infty}b_{\lambda+(k)^{n}}c^{|\lambda|+kn}.

This must converge under the stated assumption on
c
, which reduces to
|c|<1
in the case when
|y_{1}|=\dots=|y_{n}|
.
∎

Although we will not need it, we mention that the function
\phi_{\lambda}
can be expressed in terms of the basic hypergeometric series
{}_{n}\phi_{n-1}
.

We now recall some relevant results from

[
LNS
]
.
Consider the space

V=\mathbb{C}[x_{1}^{\pm},\dots,x_{n}^{\pm}]^{\mathrm{S}_{n}}[[p]].

The Ruijsenaars operators
D^{(k)}
act on
V
and have joint eigenfunctions

(\mathbf{P}_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\infty}}
of the form

\mathbf{P}_{\lambda}(\mathbf{x};p)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\in\Lambda_{\infty},\\
\mu\leq\lambda+k\phi\end{subarray}}C_{\lambda\mu}^{(k)}\,m_{\mu}(\mathbf{x})p^{k},

(4.6)

where

C_{\lambda\lambda}^{(k)}=\delta_{k,0}.

(4.7)

Here,
m_{\mu}
are the monomial symmetric Laurent polynomials, that is,
m_{\mu}(\mathbf{x})
is the sum of all distinct permutations of
x_{1}^{\mu_{1}}\dotsm x_{n}^{\mu_{n}}
, and
\phi=(1,0,\dots,0,-1)
. It is easy to check that
\mu\leq\lambda+k\phi
implies
\lambda_{n}-k\leq\mu_{j}\leq\lambda_{1}+k
for all
j
.
In particular, the sum over
\mu
in (
4.6
) has finite support, so that indeed
\mathbf{P}_{\lambda}\in V
.
The constant term
\mathbf{P}_{\lambda}(\mathbf{x};0)
equals the Macdonald polynomial
P_{\lambda}(\mathbf{x})
. We refer to
\mathbf{P}_{\lambda}
as
elliptic Macdonald polynomials
.
The authors of
[
LNS
]
prove that, under certain conditions, the series (
4.6
) is convergent. However, for our purposes it suffices to consider it as a formal series.

Lemma 4.2
.

The elliptic Macdonald polynomials
(\mathbf{P}_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\infty}}

form a Schauder basis for
V
as a module over
\mathbb{C}[[p]]
. That is, every element in
V
can be written as

\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\infty}}A_{\lambda}(p)\mathbf{P}_{\lambda}(\mathbf{x};p)

(4.8)

for unique
A_{\lambda}\in\mathbb{C}[[p]]
, where the sum is convergent as a formal power series.

Proof.

It is clear that
(m_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\infty}}
form a Schauder basis for
V
. By (
4.7
), we can write
(
4.6
) as

m_{\lambda}(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{P}_{\lambda}(\mathbf{x};p)-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\mu\neq\lambda}C_{\lambda,\mu}^{(k)}\,m_{\mu}(\mathbf{x})p^{k}.

We claim that iterating this relation
gives an expansion of
m_{\lambda}
in
\mathbf{P}_{\mu}
, which converges as a formal power series.
If that was not the case, we could iterate it indefinitely and still keep the exponent of
p
bounded.
We would then have to pick
k=0
in all except finitely many steps. In the remaining steps, we would apply it with
\mu<\lambda
in the dominance order. As we saw above, the set of such
\mu\in\Lambda_{\infty}
is finite, so the process must terminate. Hence, we end up with an expansion

m_{\lambda}(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\mu\in\Lambda_{\infty}}D_{\lambda,\mu}^{(k)}\,\mathbf{P}_{\mu}(\mathbf{x};p)p^{k},

where the inner sum has finite support. This proves the existence of the expansion (
4.8
).
For uniqueness, assume that (
4.8
) equals zero. After dividing by a power of
p
, we may assume that
A_{\lambda}(0)\neq 0

6.
Relation to Noumi–Sano operators

Noumi and Sano
[

\tilde{H}^{(k)}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n}\geq 0,\\
\mu_{1}+\dots+\mu_{n}=k\end{subarray}}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\frac{q^{\mu_{j}}\theta_{p}(q^{\mu_{i}-\mu_{j}}x_{i}/x_{j})}{\theta_{p}(x_{i}/x_{j})}\prod_{i,j=1}^{n}\frac{(tx_{i}/x_{j};q,p)_{\mu_{i}}}{(qx_{i}/x_{j};q,p)_{\mu_{i}}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}T_{q,x_{i}}^{\mu_{i}}.

Here, the elliptic shifted factorials are defined by

(x;q,p)_{n}=\frac{\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(xq^{n})}{\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(x)}=\begin{cases}\theta_{p}(x)\theta_{p}(xq)\dotsm\theta_{p}(xq^{n-1}),&n\geq 0,\\
\big(\theta_{p}(xq^{-1})\theta_{p}(xq^{-2})\dotsm\theta_{p}(xq^{n})\big)^{-1},&n<0.\end{cases}

The operators
\tilde{H}^{(k)}
are polynomials in the Ruijsenaars operators
D^{(k)}
, and vice versa.
In particular, all these operators mutually commute.
We will show that, in a certain sense, the Noumi–Sano operators are discrete degenerations of
Q
-operators.

It is well-known and easy to see that the Ruijsenaars operators (
3.1

D^{(k)}=W^{-1}D^{(k)}\big|_{t\mapsto pq/t}W,

(6.1)

where

W(\mathbf{x})=\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(tx_{i}/x_{j}).

Hence, the operators

H^{(k)}=(-1)^{k}q^{k(k+1)/2}t^{-kn}W^{-1}\tilde{H}^{(k)}\big|_{t\mapsto pq/t}W,

where the prefactor is chosen for convenience, are again polynomials in the Ruijsenaars operators.
Using the elementary identities

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\frac{q^{\mu_{j}}\theta_{p}(q^{\mu_{i}-\mu_{j}}x_{i}/x_{j})}{\theta_{p}(x_{i}/x_{j})}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k(n-1)}q^{\frac{k(n-k-1)}{2}}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{n}q^{\frac{n\mu_{j}^{2}}{2}}x_{j}^{n\mu_{j}-k}}{\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}(x_{i}/x_{j};q,p)_{\mu_{i}-\mu_{j}}},

\displaystyle\prod_{i,j=1}^{n}(pqx_{i}/tx_{j};q,p)_{\mu_{i}}

\displaystyle=(-tq^{-\frac{1}{2}})^{kn}\prod_{j=1}^{n}q^{-\frac{n\mu_{j}^{2}}{2}}x_{j}^{k-n\mu_{j}}\prod_{i,j=1}^{n}(qx_{i}/tx_{j};q,p)_{\mu_{i}}

it follows that

\displaystyle H^{(k)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n}\geq 0,\\
\mu_{1}+\dots+\mu_{n}=k\end{subarray}}\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}\frac{(tx_{i}/x_{j};q,p)_{\mu_{i}-\mu_{j}}}{(x_{i}/x_{j};q,p)_{\mu_{i}-\mu_{j}}}\prod_{i,j=1}^{n}\frac{(qx_{i}/tx_{j};q,p)_{\mu_{i}}}{(qx_{i}/x_{j};q,p)_{\mu_{i}}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}T_{q,x_{i}}^{\mu_{i}}.

As we will see,
the summand in
H^{(k)}
can be viewed as a residue of the integral (
3.7
), defining
Q_{c}f
, at the point
x_{j}=cq^{\mu_{j}}y_{j}
,
1\leq j\leq n
. The balancing condition (
3.6
) then formally implies
c=q^{-k/n}
, which is outside the domain of definition for
Q_{c}
.
The following result gives a more precise statement. We formulate it for parameter conditions that are far from necessary, but make the proof reasonably simple.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
f
be a symmetric holomorphic function on
(\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\})^{n}
and let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Assume that
|p|,\,|q|<1

and
|p|<|q^{k-1}|
. Fix a number
r
with
1<r<|p^{-1}q^{k-1}|^{1/n}
and assume that

|pq|r<|t|<|q^{k}|r^{1-n}
. Finally, assume that
y_{1},\dots,y_{n}
satisfy

|y_{i}/y_{j}|<r,\hskip 23.49976pti\neq j,

and are otherwise generic. Then, the analytic continuation of
Q_{c}f
in
c
has at most a single pole at
c=q^{-k/n}
. Moreover

\Res_{c=q^{-k/n}}(Q_{c}f)(\mathbf{y})=-\frac{(n-1)!\,q^{-k/n}t^{kn}}{(p;p)_{\infty}^{n}(q;q)_{\infty}^{n}\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(t)^{n}}(H^{(k)}f)(q^{-k/n}\mathbf{y}).

Proof.

Initially, we only assume that
|p|<1
,
|q|<1
and that
y_{1},\dots,y_{n}
are generic in the sense that

\frac{y_{1}\dotsm y_{n}}{y_{j_{1}}\dotsm y_{j_{n}}}\neq c^{n}p^{\mathbb{N}}q^{\mathbb{N}},\hskip 23.49976pt1\leq j_{1},\dots,j_{n}\leq n

(6.2)

(in particular,
c\neq q^{-k/n}
) and constrained to an annulus

a<|y_{j}|<b,\hskip 23.49976pt1\leq j\leq n.

(6.3)

The other parameter constraints will arise during the proof.

We write the integral kernel of
Q_{c}
as

M(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y})=\prod_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\frac{\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(cy_{j}/x_{i})}{\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(cty_{j}/x_{i})}\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}\frac{\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(tx_{i}/x_{j})}{\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(x_{i}/x_{j})}.

We will construct the analytic continuation of
Q_{c}f
as an iterated integral

\int_{x_{1}\in\mathcal{C}_{1}}\dotsm\int_{x_{n-1}\in\mathcal{C}_{n-1}}f(\mathbf{x})M(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y})\,\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{\textup{d}x_{i}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}x_{i}},

(6.4)

where
x_{n}
is determined from the balancing condition (
3.6
).
We take
\mathcal{C}_{i}
to be positively oriented contours such that

\displaystyle cy_{j}p^{\mathbb{N}}q^{\mathbb{N}}

\displaystyle\subseteq\operatorname{Int}(\mathcal{C}_{i}),\hskip 23.49976pt1\leq j\leq n,

(6.5)

\displaystyle\frac{y_{1}\dotsm y_{n}}{y_{j_{1}}\dotsm y_{j_{n-i}}x_{1}\dotsm x_{i-1}c^{n-i}}p^{-\mathbb{N}}q^{-\mathbb{N}}

\displaystyle\subseteq\operatorname{Ext}(\mathcal{C}_{i}),\hskip 23.49976pt1\leq j_{1},\dots,j_{n-i}\leq n.

(6.6)

Moreover, we require that on the domain of integration

\frac{a^{n-1}}{b^{n-2}|c|^{n-1}}<|x_{i}|<b|c|,\hskip 23.49976pt1\leq i\leq n,

(6.7)

which makes sense if

a^{n-1}<b^{n-1}|c|^{n}.

(6.8a)

We will show that
\mathcal{C}_{i}
exist by induction on
i
.
We first note that the points (
6.5
) are all mutually distinct from the points (
6.6
).
When
i=1
this follows from (
6.2
) and when
i>1
from the condition (
6.6
) for
\mathcal{C}_{i-1}
.
To prove that
\mathcal{C}_{i}
can be chosen so that (
6.7
) holds, we need to verify that
the points (
6.5
) are smaller than
b|c|
in modulus and the points (
6.6
) larger than
a^{n-1}/b^{n-2}|c|^{n-1}
. The first inequality is immediate from (
6.3
). For the second inequality we note that we can always cancel a factor
y_{j}
in (
6.6
). Hence, we can estimate

\left|\frac{y_{1}\dotsm y_{n}}{y_{m_{1}}\dotsm y_{m_{n-i}}x_{1}\dotsm x_{i-1}c^{n-i}}\right|>\frac{a^{n-1}}{b^{n-i-1}(b|c|)^{i-1}|c|^{n-i}}=\frac{a^{n-1}}{b^{n-2}|c|^{n-1}},

where in the case
i>1
we used (
6.7
) for
\mathcal{C}_{i-1}
.
For the final contour
\mathcal{C}_{n-1}
, we also need to ensure that (
6.7
) holds with
i=n
.
However, if we let
\mathcal{C}_{n}=(y_{1}\dotsm y_{n}/x_{1}\dotsm x_{n-2})\mathcal{C}_{n-1}^{-1}
denote the contour of integration for
x_{n}
, then (
6.5
) for
i=n-1
is equivalent to (
6.6
) for
i=n
and vice versa.
Hence, the case
i=n
of (
6.7
) follows in the same way as
i=n-1
.

We now consider the position of the singularities of the integrand.
The factor
\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(cy_{j}/x_{i})
has poles at the points (
6.5
), which are inside

\mathcal{C}_{i}

for
1\leq i\leq n-1
.
As we saw in the preceding paragraph, they are also
inside
\mathcal{C}_{n}
.
The factor
\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(cty_{j}/x_{i})^{-1}
has poles at
x_{i}=cty_{j}p^{-\mathbb{N}-1}q^{-\mathbb{N}-1}
. If

b|pq|<a|t|,

(6.8b)

it follows from
(

) and (

) that these are outside
\mathcal{C}_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
.
The factor
\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(tx_{i}/x_{j})
is analytic for
|tx_{i}/x_{j}|<1
. In (
3.7
), the domain of integration is included in that region.
If we assume

b^{n-1}|c^{n}t|<a^{n-1},

(6.8c)

it follows from (
6.7
) that this also true for (
6.4
). Finally, we note that

\frac{1}{\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(x_{i}/x_{j})}=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\theta_{p}(x_{i}/x_{j})\theta_{q}(x_{j}/x_{i})

(6.9)

is analytic on
(\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\})^{n}
.
This shows that, under the parameter conditions (
6.8
), all singularities in (
6.4
) are located in the same way relative to the domain of integration as in (
3.7
). Hence, (
6.4
) defines an analytic continuation of
Q_{c}f
.

We now take
c
in a small neighborhood of
q^{-k/n}
and write

. Then, (
6.8a
) holds automatically, whereas (
6.8b
) and (

) reduce to
|pq|r<|t|<|q^{k}|r^{1-n}
, which implies
r^{n}<|p^{-1}q^{k-1}|
.
That is, we recover the conditions used in the formulation of the proposition.

Next, we let
c\rightarrow q^{-k/n}
. Consider first the integration over
\mathcal{C}_{1}
.
In the limit, the points

S=\big\{cy_{j}q^{\mu};\,1\leq j\leq n,\,0\leq\mu\leq k\big\}

are included both in (
6.5
) and (
6.6
) for
i=1
. Let
\mathcal{D}_{1}
be a contour defined exactly as
\mathcal{C}_{1}
, except that

. We can then write

\int_{x\in\mathcal{C}_{1}}=\int_{x\in\mathcal{D}_{1}}+2\pi\mathrm{i}\sum_{z\in S}\Res_{x=z}.

If
x_{1}\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
and the parameters

are generic, the remaining integrals are regular at

. However, if
x_{1}\in S
, then some points in
S
are included both in (
6.5
) and (
6.6
) for
i=2
. Repeating this argument leads to the decomposition

Q_{c}f=\sum_{z_{1},\dots,z_{n-1}\in S}\Res_{\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{z}}\frac{f(\mathbf{x})M(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y})}{\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}x_{i}}+R,

(6.10)

where
R
is regular at
c=q^{-k/n}
and
x_{n}
is determined from (
3.6
).

If we write
z_{j}=cy_{\sigma(j)}q^{\mu_{j}}
,
1\leq j\leq n-1
, then it follows from (
6.9
) that only terms with
\sigma
injective contribute to the sum in (
6.10
). We first consider the case
\sigma=\operatorname{id}
.
Then, the potential singularity at
c=q^{-k/n}
comes from the factor

\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(cy_{n}/x_{n})\Big|_{\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{z}}=\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(cx_{1}\dotsm x_{n-1}/y_{1}\dotsm y_{n-1})\Big|_{x_{j}=cy_{j}q^{\mu_{j}}}=\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(c^{n}q^{k-\mu_{n}}),

where
\mu_{n}=k-(\mu_{1}+\dots+\mu_{n-1})
. This is singular at
c=q^{-k/n}
if
\mu_{n}\geq 0
, and we observe that

\Res_{c=q^{-k/n}}\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(c^{n}q^{k-\mu_{n}})=-\frac{q^{-\frac{k}{n}}}{nx_{n}}\,\Res_{x_{n}=cy_{n}q^{\mu_{n}}}\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(cy_{n}/x_{n})\Big|_{c=q^{-k/n}}.

Hence, the resulting contribution to
\Res_{c=q^{-k/n}}(Q_{c}f)
is

-\frac{q^{-\frac{k}{n}}}{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n}\geq 0,\\
\mu_{1}+\dots+\mu_{n}=k\end{subarray}}f(\mathbf{y}q^{\bm{\mu}-k/n})\Res_{\mathbf{x}=c\mathbf{y}q^{\bm{\mu}}}\frac{M(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y})}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}}\Bigg|_{c=q^{-k/n}}.

Since this is symmetric in
\mathbf{y}
,
the terms corresponding to
\sigma\neq\operatorname{id}
each add an identical contribution and we conclude that

\Res_{c=q^{-k/n}}Q_{c}f=-q^{-\frac{k}{n}}(n-1)!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n}\geq 0,\\
\mu_{1}+\dots+\mu_{n}=k\end{subarray}}f(\mathbf{y}q^{\bm{\mu}-k/n})\Res_{\mathbf{x}=c\mathbf{y}q^{\bm{\mu}}}\frac{M(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y})}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}}\Bigg|_{c=q^{-k/n}}.

To finish the computation, we write

\Res_{\mathbf{x}=c\mathbf{y}q^{\bm{\mu}}}\frac{M(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y})}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}}=\Res_{\mathbf{x}=c\mathbf{y}}\frac{M(\mathbf{x}q^{\bm{\mu}};\mathbf{y})}{M(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y})}\cdot\frac{M(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y})}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}}.

Since

\frac{M(\mathbf{x}q^{\bm{\mu}};\mathbf{y})}{M(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y})}=t^{kn}\prod_{i,j=1}^{n}\frac{(qx_{i}/cty_{j};q,p)_{\mu_{i}}}{(qx_{i}/cy_{j};q,p)_{\mu_{i}}}\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}\frac{(tx_{i}/x_{j};q,p)_{\mu_{i}-\mu_{j}}}{(x_{i}/x_{j};q,p)_{\mu_{i}-\mu_{j}}},

the first factor is regular at
\mathbf{x}=c\mathbf{y}
and equals

t^{kn}\prod_{i,j=1}^{n}\frac{(qy_{i}/ty_{j};q,p)_{\mu_{i}}}{(qy_{i}/y_{j};q,p)_{\mu_{i}}}\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}\frac{(ty_{i}/y_{j};q,p)_{\mu_{i}-\mu_{j}}}{(y_{i}/y_{j};q,p)_{\mu_{i}-\mu_{j}}}.

Finally, we have

\displaystyle\Res_{\mathbf{x}=c\mathbf{y}}\frac{M(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y})}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}}

\displaystyle=\Res_{\mathbf{x}=c\mathbf{y}}\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}\frac{\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(cy_{j}/x_{i},tx_{i}/x_{j})}{\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(cty_{j}/x_{i},x_{i}/x_{j})}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(cy_{i}/x_{i})}{x_{i}\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(cty_{i}/x_{i})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(t)^{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Res_{x_{i}=cy_{i}}\frac{\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(cy_{i}/x_{i})}{x_{i}}=\frac{1}{(p;p)_{\infty}^{n}(q;q)_{\infty}^{n}\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(t)^{n}}.

This completes the proof.

Proposition 7.1

Proof.

We compute

(Q_{c}\tilde{Q}_{d}f)(\mathbf{y})=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n-1}_{r^{n}}}\frac{f(\mathbf{x})}{\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(x_{i}/x_{j})}\,K_{1}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y})\,|\textup{d}\mathbf{x}|,

\displaystyle K_{1}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n-1}_{r^{n}}}\frac{\prod_{i,j=1}^{n}\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(dz_{i}/x_{j},pqz_{i}/cty_{j},tx_{j}/dz_{i},cy_{j}/z_{i})}{\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}\Gamma_{\!\!p,q}(z_{i}/z_{j})}\,|\textup{d}\mathbf{z}|

The product
\tilde{Q}_{d}Q_{c}
is given by a similar expression, with

It follows from (

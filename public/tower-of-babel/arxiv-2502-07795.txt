Stabilizer-free Weak Galerkin Methods for Quad-Curl Problems on polyhedral Meshes without Convexity Assumptions
Chunmei Wang, Shangyou Zhang

Abstract
This paper introduces an efficient stabilizer-free weak Galerkin (WG) finite element method for solving the three-dimensional quad-curl problem. Leveraging bubble functions as a key analytical tool, the method extends the applicability of stabilizer-free WG approaches to non-convex elements in finite element partitions—a notable advancement over existing methods, which are restricted to convex elements. The proposed method maintains a simple, symmetric, and positive definite formulation. It achieves optimal error estimates for the exact solution in a discrete norm, as well as an optimal-order L 2 L^{2} error estimate for k > 2 k>2 and a sub-optimal order for the lowest order case k = 2 k=2 . Numerical experiments are presented to validate the method’s efficiency and accuracy.

Introduction

In this paper, we focus on developing a stabilizer-free weak Galerkin finite element method specifically tailored to address the quad-curl problem in three dimensions without convexity constraints. The objective is to find

{\mathbf{u}}

\begin{split}(\nabla\times)^{4}{\mathbf{u}}=&{\mathbf{f}},\qquad\text{in}\quad\Omega,\\
\nabla\cdot{\mathbf{u}}=&0,\qquad\text{in}\quad\Omega,\\
{\mathbf{u}}\times{\mathbf{n}}=&0,\qquad\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega,\\
\nabla\times{\mathbf{u}}\times{\mathbf{n}}=&0,\qquad\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega.\end{split}

The weak Galerkin (WG) finite element method represents a significant advancement in numerical techniques for solving partial differential equations (PDEs). This approach approximates differential operators within a framework analogous to the theory of distributions for piecewise polynomials. Unlike traditional finite element methods, WG relaxes the regularity requirements for approximating functions by incorporating carefully designed stabilizers. What sets WG methods apart from other finite element approaches is their reliance on weak derivatives and weak continuities to design numerical schemes directly based on the weak forms of PDEs. This structural flexibility enables WG methods to handle a wide spectrum of PDEs effectively, ensuring both stability and accuracy in their solutions.

A notable innovation within the WG framework is the “Primal-Dual Weak Galerkin (PDWG)” method, which addresses challenges that traditional numerical techniques often struggle to overcome

This approach leads to an Euler-Lagrange system involving both the primal variable and a dual variable (Lagrange multiplier), resulting in a symmetric and robust numerical scheme.

The WG methods for quad-curl problems discussed in the literature vary in their approaches and applicability. The method introduced in
[
25

is curl-conforming and specifically designed for tetrahedral partitions. In contrast, the WG method proposed in
[

This paper presents a stabilizer-free WG finite element method for three-dimensional quad-curl problems. By eliminating the need for stabilizers, the proposed method streamlines implementation and applies to both convex and non-convex elements in finite element partitions, leveraging bubble functions as a key analytical tool. Previous stabilizer-free WG methods have been confined to convex polytopal meshes
[
55
,
56

The proposed method maintains the size and global sparsity of the stiffness matrix, reducing programming complexity compared to stabilizer-dependent methods. Moreover, it leverages bubble functions without imposing the restrictive conditions required by other stabilizer-free WG approaches
[

Theoretical analysis confirms optimal error estimates for WG approximations in discrete norms. Additionally, the method achieves an optimal-order

2
A Weak Formulation

Let
s>0
be an integer. We first introduce the space:

H(curl^{s};\Omega)=\{{\mathbf{u}}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{3}:(\nabla\times)^{j}{\mathbf{u}}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{3},j=1,\cdots,s\}

with the associated inner product

({\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{v}})_{H(curl^{s};\Omega)}=({\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{v}})+\sum_{j=1}^{s}((\nabla\times)^{j}{\mathbf{u}},(\nabla\times)^{j}{\mathbf{v}})

and the norm

\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{H(curl^{s};\Omega)}=({\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{u}})^{\frac{1}{2}}_{H(curl^{s};\Omega)}.

Next, we introduce the following spaces:

H_{0}(curl;\Omega):=\{{\mathbf{u}}\in H(curl;\Omega):{\mathbf{n}}\times{\mathbf{u}}=0\ \text{on}\ \partial\Omega\},

H_{0}(curl^{2};\Omega):=\{{\mathbf{u}}\in H(curl^{2};\Omega):{\mathbf{n}}\times{\mathbf{u}}=0\ \text{and}\ \nabla\times{\mathbf{u}}\times{\mathbf{n}}=0\ \text{on}\ \partial\Omega\}.

Additionally, we introduce:

H(div;\Omega)=\{{\mathbf{u}}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{3}:\nabla\cdot{\mathbf{u}}\in L^{2}(\Omega)\},

with the associated inner product

({\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{v}})_{H(div;\Omega)}=({\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{v}})+(\nabla\cdot{\mathbf{u}},\nabla\cdot{\mathbf{v}})

and the norm

\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{H(div;\Omega)}=({\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{u}})^{\frac{1}{2}}_{H(div;\Omega)}.

Finally, we introduce:

H(div^{0};\Omega)=\{{\mathbf{u}}\in H(div;\Omega):\nabla\cdot{\mathbf{u}}=0\ \text{in}\ \Omega\}.

By utilizing the integration by parts, we propose the following weak formulation of the quad-curl problem (
1.1
): Given
{\mathbf{f}}\in H(div^{0};\Omega)
, seek
({\mathbf{u}};p)\in H_{0}(curl^{2};\Omega)\times H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
such that

(2.1)

\begin{split}((\nabla\times)^{2}{\mathbf{u}},(\nabla\times)^{2}{\mathbf{v}})+({\mathbf{v}},\nabla p)=&({\mathbf{f}},{\mathbf{v}}),\qquad{\mathbf{v}}\in H_{0}(curl^{2};\Omega),\\
\alpha(\nabla p,\nabla q)-({\mathbf{u}},\nabla q)=&0,\qquad\qquad\forall q\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\end{split}

where
\alpha
is a function to be specified later.

Theorem 1
.

[
25
]

Given
{\mathbf{f}}\in H(div^{0};\Omega)
, the problem (
2.1
) has a unique solution
({\mathbf{u}};p)\in H_{0}(curl^{2};\Omega)\times H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
. Furthermore,
p=0
and
{\mathbf{u}}
satisfies

\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{H(curl^{2};\Omega)}\leq C\|{\mathbf{f}}\|.

Denote by

\displaystyle V(T)=\{{\mathbf{v}}=\{{\mathbf{v}}_{0},

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\mathbf{v}}_{b}=v_{1}{\mathbf{t}}_{1}+v_{2}{\mathbf{t}}_{2},{\mathbf{v}}_{n}=v_{3}{\mathbf{t}}_{1}+v_{4}{\mathbf{t}}_{2}\}:

Recall that

For any

(5.3)

{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}\sigma{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}_{W_{h}}=\Big(\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}h_{T}^{4}(\nabla_{w}\sigma,\nabla_{w}\sigma)_{T}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

Lemma 2
.

[

The bubble function of the element

\Phi_{B}=l^{2}_{1}(x)l^{2}_{2}(x)\cdots l^{2}_{N}(x)\in P_{2N}(T).

It is straightforward to verify that

For

&=(\nabla\sigma_{0},\Phi_{B}\nabla\sigma_{0})_{T},\end{split}

From the domain inverse inequality

By applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and using (

f_{k}

\sigma_{0}

&+\big(\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}h_{T}^{-1}\|(\nabla\times{\mathbf{v}}_{0}-{\mathbf{v}}_{n})\times{\mathbf{n}}\|^{2}_{\partial T}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}h_{T}\|({\cal Q}_{h}^{r_{1}}-I)((\nabla\times)^{2}{\mathbf{u}})\|^{2}_{\partial T}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\leq&\Big(\big(\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}h_{T}^{2}\|\nabla\times({\cal Q}_{h}^{r_{1}}-I)((\nabla\times)^{2}{\mathbf{u}})\|^{2}_{T}+h_{T}^{4}\|\nabla\times({\cal Q}_{h}^{r_{1}}-I)((\nabla\times)^{2}{\mathbf{u}})\|^{2}_{1,T}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\

\leq&Ch^{s-1}\|(\nabla\times)^{2}{\mathbf{u}}\|_{s-1}\|{\mathbf{v}}\|_{2,h}.\end{split}

Similarly, using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the trace inequality (
5.1
) gives

\begin{split}|\ell_{2}({\mathbf{u}},q)|&=|\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}\langle q_{0}-q_{b},(I-{\cal Q}_{h}^{r_{2}}){\mathbf{u}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{{\partial T}}|\\

&\leq Ch^{s-1}\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{s+1}\|q\|_{1,h}.\end{split}

This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

\begin{split}&(Q^{t_{0}-2}(\nabla\times)^{2}_{w}{\boldsymbol{\phi}},(\nabla\times)^{2}_{w}({\mathbf{Q}}_{h}{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}))_{T}\\
=&({\mathbf{Q}}_{0}{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}},(\nabla\times)^{2}(Q^{t_{0}-2}(\nabla\times)^{2}_{w}{\boldsymbol{\phi}}))_{T}\\
&-\langle({\mathbf{Q}}_{b}{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}})\times{\mathbf{n}},\nabla\times(Q^{t_{0}-2}(\nabla\times)^{2}_{w}{\boldsymbol{\phi}})\rangle_{\partial T}\\
&-\langle({\mathbf{Q}}_{n}(\nabla\times{\mathbf{u}})-\nabla\times{\mathbf{u}})\times{\mathbf{n}},Q^{t_{0}-2}(\nabla\times)^{2}_{w}{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\rangle_{\partial T}\\
=&0,\end{split}

where we used the properties of the projection operators
{\mathbf{Q}}_{0}
,
{\mathbf{Q}}_{b}
, and
{\mathbf{Q}}_{n}
. This, together with Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, (
8.4
) and (
6.1
), gives

\begin{split}&|\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}((\nabla\times)^{2}_{w}{\boldsymbol{\phi}},(\nabla\times)^{2}_{w}({\mathbf{Q}}_{h}{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}))_{T}|\\
=&|\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}((\nabla\times)^{2}_{w}{\boldsymbol{\phi}}-Q^{t_{0}-2}(\nabla\times)^{2}_{w}{\boldsymbol{\phi}},(\nabla\times)^{2}_{w}({\mathbf{Q}}_{h}{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}))_{T}|\\
\leq&\Big(\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}\|({\cal Q}_{h}^{r_{1}}((\nabla\times)^{2}{\boldsymbol{\phi}})-Q^{t_{0}-2}({\cal Q}_{h}^{r_{1}}((\nabla\times)^{2}{\boldsymbol{\phi}}))\|_{T}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\cdot\Big(\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}\|(\nabla\times)^{2}_{w}({\mathbf{Q}}_{h}{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}})\|_{T}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\leq&Ch^{k-1}\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k+1}h^{t_{0}-1}\|(\nabla\times)^{2}{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\|_{t_{0}-1}\\
\leq&Ch^{k+t_{0}-2}\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k+1}\|(\nabla\times)^{2}{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\|_{t_{0}-1}.\end{split}

Regarding to the term
I_{5}
, using (
7.1
) with
{\mathbf{u}}={\boldsymbol{\phi}}
and
{\mathbf{v}}={\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h}
and
s=t_{0}
, (
7.3
), (
8.4
) and (
5.17
), we have

\begin{split}|\ell_{1}({\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\phi}})|\leq&Ch^{{t_{0}}-1}\|(\nabla\times)^{2}{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\|_{{t_{0}}-1}\|{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h}\|_{2,h}\\
\leq&Ch^{{t_{0}}-1}\|(\nabla\times)^{2}{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\|_{{t_{0}}-1}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}_{V_{h}}\\
\leq&Ch^{{t_{0}}-1}\|(\nabla\times)^{2}{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\|_{{t_{0}}-1}({|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}{\mathbf{e}}_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}_{V_{h}}+{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}{\mathbf{Q}}_{h}{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}_{V_{h}})\\
\leq&Ch^{k+t_{0}-2}\|(\nabla\times)^{2}{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\|_{t_{0}-1}(\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k+1}+\|(\nabla\times)^{2}{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k-1}).\end{split}

Regarding to the term
I_{6}
, using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and the trace inequality (
5.1
), we have

\begin{split}|\ell_{2}({\mathbf{u}},Q_{h}\xi)|\leq&\Big(\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}\|Q_{0}\xi-Q_{b}\xi\|_{\partial T}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}\|(I-{\cal Q}_{h}^{r_{2}}){\mathbf{u}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\|_{\partial T}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\leq&\Big(\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}h_{T}^{-1}\|Q_{0}\xi-\xi\|_{T}^{2}+h_{T}\|Q_{0}\xi-\xi\|_{1,T}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\cdot\Big(\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}h_{T}^{-1}\|(I-{\cal Q}_{h}^{r_{2}}){\mathbf{u}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\|_{T}^{2}+h_{T}\|(I-{\cal Q}_{h}^{r_{2}}){\mathbf{u}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\|_{1,T}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\leq&Ch^{k+1}\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k+1}\|\xi\|_{1}.\end{split}

Regarding to the term
I_{7}
, using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, (
6.2
), (
7.3
), (
8.3
) with
k=0
, we have

\begin{split}|c(\epsilon_{h},Q_{h}\xi)|=&|\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}h_{T}^{4}(\nabla_{w}\epsilon_{h},\nabla_{w}Q_{h}\xi)|\\
\leq&|\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}h_{T}^{4}(\nabla_{w}\epsilon_{h},\nabla_{w}(Q_{h}\xi-\xi))+h_{T}^{4}(\nabla_{w}\epsilon_{h},\nabla_{w}\xi)|\\
\leq&\Big(\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}h_{T}^{4}\|\nabla_{w}\epsilon_{h}\|_{T}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}h_{T}^{4}\|\nabla_{w}(Q_{h}\xi-\xi)\|_{T}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&+\Big(\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}h_{T}^{4}\|\nabla_{w}\epsilon_{h}\|_{T}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}h_{T}^{4}\|\nabla_{w}\xi\|_{T}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\leq&{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}\epsilon_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}_{W_{h}}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}Q_{h}\xi-\xi{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}_{W_{h}}+{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}\epsilon_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}_{W_{h}}\Big(\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}h_{T}^{4}\|{\cal Q}^{r_{1}}\nabla\xi\|_{T}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\leq&Ch^{k-1}(\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k+1}+\|(\nabla\times)^{2}{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k-1})h^{2}\|\xi\|_{1}\\
\leq&Ch^{k+1}(\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k+1}+\|(\nabla\times)^{2}{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k-1})\|\xi\|_{1}.\end{split}

Regarding to the term
I_{8}
, using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,
(
8.3
) with
k=0
, (
8.2
), we have

\begin{split}&\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}(\nabla_{w}(\xi-Q_{h}\xi),{\mathbf{e}}_{0})_{T}\\
\leq&\Big(\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}\|\nabla_{w}(\xi-Q_{h}\xi)\|_{T}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}\|{\mathbf{e}}_{0}\|_{T}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\leq&Ch^{2}\|\xi\|_{1}\|{\mathbf{e}}_{0}\|\\
\leq&Ch^{2}\|\xi\|_{1}(\|{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{0}\|+\|{\mathbf{u}}-Q_{0}{\mathbf{u}}\|)\\
\leq&Ch^{2}\|\xi\|_{1}\|{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{0}\|+Ch^{2}\|\xi\|_{1}h^{k+1}\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k+1}\\
\leq&h^{2}\|{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{0}\|^{2}+C\|\xi\|_{1}h^{k+3}\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k+1}.\end{split}

Regarding to the term
I_{9}
, using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, (
6.2
), we have

\begin{split}|\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}(\nabla_{w}\xi,{\mathbf{Q}}_{0}{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}})_{T}|=&|\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}({\cal Q}_{h}^{r_{1}}\nabla\xi,{\mathbf{Q}}_{0}{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}})_{T}|\\
\leq&\Big(\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}\|{\cal Q}_{h}^{r_{1}}\nabla\xi\|_{T}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}\|{\mathbf{Q}}_{0}{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}\|_{T}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\leq&Ch^{k+1}\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k+1}\|\xi\|_{1}.\end{split}

Substituting the estimates of
I_{i}
for
i=1,\cdots,9
into
(
8.6
) and using the regularity assumption (
8.2
) gives

\begin{split}(1-h^{2})\|{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{0}\|^{2}\leq&Ch^{t_{0}+k-2}(\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k+1}+\|(\nabla\times)^{2}{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k-1})\|{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\|_{t_{0}+1}\\
&+Ch^{t_{0}+k-2}\|(\nabla\times)^{2}{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k-1}\|{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\|_{t_{0}+1}\\
&+Ch^{k+t_{0}-2}\|{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\|_{t_{0}+1}(\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k+1}+\|(\nabla\times)^{2}{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k-1})\\
&+Ch^{k+t_{0}-2}\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k+1}\|(\nabla\times)^{2}{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\|_{t_{0}-1}\\
&+Ch^{k+t_{0}-2}\|(\nabla\times)^{2}{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\|_{t_{0}-1}(\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k+1}+\|(\nabla\times)^{2}{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k-1})\\
&+Ch^{k+1}\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k+1}\|\xi\|_{1}\\
&+Ch^{k+1}(\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k+1}+\|(\nabla\times)^{2}{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k-1})\|\xi\|_{1}\\
&+Ch^{k+3}\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k+1}\|\xi\|_{1}+Ch^{k+1}\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k+1}\|\xi\|_{1},\end{split}

which gives

\|{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{0}\|\leq Ch^{t_{0}+k-2}(\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k+1}+\|(\nabla\times)^{2}{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k-1}),

provided that
h
is sufficiently small such that
1-h^{2}>0
.
This, together with the triangle inequality, we have

\begin{split}\|{\mathbf{e}}_{0}\|\leq&\|{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{0}\|+\|{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{Q}}_{0}{\mathbf{u}}\|\\
\leq&Ch^{t_{0}+k-2}(\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k+1}+\|(\nabla\times)^{2}{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k-1})+Ch^{k+1}\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k+1}\\
\leq&Ch^{t_{0}+k-2}(\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k+1}+\|(\nabla\times)^{2}{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k-1}).\end{split}

This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

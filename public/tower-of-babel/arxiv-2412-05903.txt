Quantitative strong approximation for ternary quadratic forms II
Zhizhong Huang

Abstract
. Let F F be a non-degenerate integral ternary quadratic form and let m 0 ∈ ℤ ≠ 0 m_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\neq 0} . We study growth of rational points on the affine quadric ( F = m 0 ) (F=m_{0}) and show that they are equidistributed in the adelic space off a finite place. This is closely related to Linnik’s problem. Our approach is based on the δ \delta -variant of the Hardy–Littlewood circle method developed by Heath-Brown.



1.
Introduction

Let
F
be an integral non-degenerate quadratic form in
d
-variables and let
m_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\neq 0}
, which will be fixed throughout. For a growing parameter
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we denote by
V_{N}
the affine quadric
(F=m_{0}N)\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{d}
over
{\mathbb{Q}}
with the integral model
{\mathscr{V}}_{N}
defined by the same equation. Assume that
V_{1}({\mathbb{R}})\neq\varnothing
.
Consider the projection map

\displaystyle\pi_{N}:V_{N}({\mathbb{R}})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow V_{1}({\mathbb{R}})

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle\longmapsto\frac{\boldsymbol{x}}{\sqrt{N}}.

Linnik’s problem
[
15
]
is concerned with the distribution of the integral points
{\mathscr{V}}_{N}({\mathbb{Z}})
under the map
\pi_{N}
on
V_{1}({\mathbb{R}})
.

Let
w:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a weight function (infinitely differentiable and compactly supported). Suppose that
\operatorname{Supp}(w)\cap V_{1}({\mathbb{R}})
is a compact region of
V_{1}({\mathbb{R}})
with non-empty interior. Let
L\in{\mathbb{N}}
be fixed. To each
N\to\infty
, we associate a residue
\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{N}\in{\mathscr{V}}_{N}({\mathbb{Z}}/L{\mathbb{Z}})
, viewed as an element of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
of size
\ll L
satisfying
F(\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{N})\equiv m_{0}N\bmod L
. To these data we associate the weighted counting function:

(1.1)

\Gamma_{w}(N):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{x}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d},F(\boldsymbol{x})=m_{0}N\\
\boldsymbol{x}\equiv\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{N}\bmod L\end{subarray}}w\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{x}}{\sqrt{N}}\right).

Observe that this is a finite sum because it only counts integer vectors
\boldsymbol{x}\in\sqrt{N}\operatorname{Supp}(w)
.

Past applications of the Hardy–Littlewood circle method had been successful only when
d\geqslant 4
(the breakthrough for
d=4
goes back to Kloosterman
[
14
]
almost a century ago). So we shall fixate on the
ternary
case
d=3
, where was long considered as beyond reach of the circle method. The case where
F
is positive definite (and with a different type of congruence conditions) has been extensively studied by many authors
[
5
,
7
,
8
,
12
]
, and it is shown that
{\mathscr{V}}_{N}({\mathbb{Z}})
, as
N\to\infty
(subjected to appropriate conditions), become equidistributed on
V_{1}({\mathbb{R}})
via the map
\pi_{N}
. When
F
is assumed to be anisotropic over
{\mathbb{Q}}
and
N
has only restricted prime divisors, see also
[
1
]
.

The goal of this article is to push the circle method further (for the first time as far as the author is aware)
to address Linnik’s type ordering problem for ternary quadratic forms.

1.1.
Main results on equidistribution of
{\mathbb{Z}}[\frac{1}{p_{0}}]
-points

Let us fix throughout a prime
p_{0}
, and let
V_{1}(\mathbf{A}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}^{p_{0}})
be the
adelic space off
p_{0}
of
V_{1}
, which is by definition the image of of adelic space
V_{1}(\mathbf{A}_{{\mathbb{Q}}})
(the restricted product over all
V({\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
against integral models of
V_{1}
) under the projection without the
p_{0}
-component. Let
V_{1}(\mathbf{A}_{{\mathbb{Q}}})^{\operatorname{Br}}
be the closed subset of
V_{1}(\mathbf{A}_{{\mathbb{Q}}})
consisting of elements that are orthogonal to the Brauer group of
V_{1}
.
Thanks to work of Colliot-Thélène–Xu
[
3
, §5.6, §5.8]

[
4
, Proposition 4.5]
, provided that the quadratic form
F
is isotropic over
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p_{0}}
, the set
V({\mathbb{Q}})
is dense inside the image of
V_{1}(\mathbf{A}_{{\mathbb{Q}}})^{\operatorname{Br}}
in
V_{1}(\mathbf{A}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}^{p_{0}})
. We refer to
[
3
, §1]
and
[
4
, §1]
for more details about terminology, based on which we say that strong approximation with Brauer–Manin obstruction off the prime
p_{0}
holds for the affine quadric
V_{1}
.

In our current investigation, motivated by this qualitative aspect, we consider the adelic subspace

{\mathscr{V}}_{1}\left(\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}\left[\frac{1}{p_{0}}\right]\right):=V_{1}({\mathbb{R}})\times\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p<\infty,p\neq p_{0}\end{subarray}}{\mathscr{V}}_{1}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})\subset V_{1}(\mathbf{A}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}^{p_{0}}),

which we tacitly assume to be non-empty. Let us fix
\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in{\mathscr{V}}_{1}({\mathbb{Z}}/L{\mathbb{Z}})
, viewed as a vector in
\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}
of size
\ll L
such that
F(\boldsymbol{\lambda})\equiv m_{0}\bmod L
. As
(L,\boldsymbol{\lambda})
captures non-archimedean local conditions outside of
p_{0}
, we assume that
p_{0}\nmid L
. The collection
\{w,{\mathscr{V}}_{1},(L,\boldsymbol{\lambda})\}
specifies a (non-empty) open-closed subset of
{\mathscr{V}}_{1}\left(\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}\left[\frac{1}{p_{0}}\right]\right)
.

To quantify the strong approximation property off
p_{0}
for
V_{1}
, we are interested in counting rational points on
V_{1}
satisfying the local conditions prescribed by the collection
\{w,{\mathscr{V}}_{1},(L,\boldsymbol{\lambda})\}
. If for every prime
p
, we equip the affine
3
-space
{\mathbb{A}}^{3}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
with the
p
-adic height

\|(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\|_{p}:=\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant 3}|x_{i}|_{p},

then the growth of
V_{1}({\mathbb{Q}})
with these prescribed local conditions may be quantified by the counting function

\#\left\{\boldsymbol{x}\in V_{1}\left({\mathbb{Q}}\right)\cap{\mathscr{V}}_{1}\left(\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}\left[\frac{1}{p_{0}}\right]\right)\left|\begin{aligned} &\|\boldsymbol{x}\|_{p_{0}}\leqslant p_{0}^{h}\\
&\boldsymbol{x}\in\operatorname{Supp}(w)\text{ and }\forall p\mid L,\|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{\lambda}\|_{p}\leqslant p^{-\operatorname{ord}_{p}(L)}\end{aligned}\right.\right\}.

Here
h\in{\mathbb{N}}
is a growing parameter.
We therefore let

(1.2)

N=p_{0}^{2h},\quad\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{N}:=p_{0}^{h}\boldsymbol{\lambda},

so that the counting function
\Gamma_{w}(N)
(
1.1
) “mollifies” the problem above. We would like to stress that the quadratic form
F
need not be positive definitive or
{\mathbb{Q}}
-anisotropic.

We define the
weighted singular integral
by

(1.3)

\widetilde{{\mathscr{I}}}(w):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathbb{R}}}w(\boldsymbol{t})\textup{e}(\theta(F(\boldsymbol{t})-m_{0}))\operatorname{d}\boldsymbol{t}\operatorname{d}\theta.

It computes the (weighted) real density of the affine quadric
V_{1}
restricted to the support of
w
(cf. e.g.
[
9
, Theorem 3]
).

We denote by
\psi_{0}
the real character
\left(\frac{-m_{0}\Delta_{F}}{\cdot}\right)
, being principal or not. Let us define, for every prime
p
,

\sigma_{p}({\mathscr{V}}_{1};(L,\boldsymbol{\lambda})):=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\#\{\boldsymbol{x}\in({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}{\mathbb{Z}})^{3}:F(\boldsymbol{x})\equiv m_{0}\bmod p^{k},\boldsymbol{x}\equiv\boldsymbol{\lambda}\bmod p^{\operatorname{ord}_{p}(L)}\}}{p^{2k}}.

For
p_{0}
(we call that
p_{0}\nmid L
), we let

\sigma_{p_{0}}({\mathscr{V}}_{0}):=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\#\{\boldsymbol{x}\in({\mathbb{Z}}/p_{0}^{k}{\mathbb{Z}})^{3}:F(\boldsymbol{x})\equiv 0\bmod p_{0}^{k}\}}{p_{0}^{2k}},

which is the
p_{0}
-adic density of the affine cone

{\mathscr{V}}_{0}:=(F=0)\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{3}_{{\mathbb{Z}}},

and is positive if and only if the quadratic form
F
is isotropic over
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p_{0}}
.
We define the “modified singular series”
\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{F}(p_{0};L,\boldsymbol{\lambda})
as follows, the convergence of which will be established in course of proof.

(1.4)

\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{F}(p_{0};L,\boldsymbol{\lambda}):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{\psi_{0}(p_{0})}{p_{0}}\right)\sigma_{p_{0}}({\mathscr{V}}_{0})\times\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p<\infty\\
p\neq p_{0}\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{\psi_{0}(p)}{p}\right)\sigma_{p}({\mathscr{V}}_{1};(L,\boldsymbol{\lambda}))&\text{ if }-m\Delta_{F}\neq\square;\\
\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{1}{p_{0}}\right)\sigma_{p_{0}}({\mathscr{V}}_{0})\times\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p<\infty\\
p\neq p_{0}\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\sigma_{p}({\mathscr{V}}_{1};(L,\boldsymbol{\lambda}))&\text{ if }-m\Delta_{F}=\square.\end{cases}

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that

(1.5)

-m_{0}\Delta_{F}=\square.

Then there exists
a_{h}\in{\mathbb{C}}
depending on
\{w,{\mathscr{V}}_{1},(L,\boldsymbol{\lambda})\}
and
h
such that

\Gamma_{w}(N)=\widetilde{{\mathscr{I}}}(w)\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{F}(p_{0};L,\boldsymbol{\lambda})\sqrt{N}\log\sqrt{N}+a_{h}\sqrt{N}+O\left(\frac{\sqrt{N}}{(\log N)^{A}}\right),

where
A>0
is an effectively computable numerical constant. Moreover
a_{h}=O(1)
.

The condition (
1.5
) ensures the triviality of the Brauer group of
V_{1}
, whence
V_{1}(\mathbf{A}_{{\mathbb{Q}}})^{\operatorname{Br}}=V_{1}(\mathbf{A}_{{\mathbb{Q}}})
. Theorem
1.1
provides a quantitative aspect of how
{\mathscr{V}}_{1}({\mathbb{Z}}[\frac{1}{p_{0}}])
equidistributes in
{\mathscr{V}}_{1}\left(\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}\left[\frac{1}{p_{0}}\right]\right)
, in particular in every factor
{\mathscr{V}}_{1}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p}),p\neq p_{0}
as well as
V_{1}({\mathbb{R}})
. Note that they accumulate under the
p_{0}
-adic topology around the boundary divisor
(F=0)
of the naive compactification of
V_{1}
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{3}
. This is in accordance with the Chambert-Loir–Tschinkel heuristic (cf.
[
10
, Appendix]
).

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that

(1.6)

-m_{0}\Delta_{F}\neq\square.

Then there exists
b_{h}\in{\mathbb{C}}
depending on
\{w,{\mathscr{V}}_{1},(L,\boldsymbol{\lambda})\}
and
h
such that

\Gamma_{w}(N)=\sqrt{N}\left(\widetilde{{\mathscr{I}}}(w)\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{F}(p_{0};L,\boldsymbol{\lambda})+b_{h}\right)+O\left(\frac{\sqrt{N}}{(\log N)^{A}}\right),

with
A>0
as before and
b_{h}=O(1)
.

Under (
1.6
), results of similar sort are also obtained by Benoist–Oh
[
1
, Corollaries 1.11 & 1.12]
(with an inexplicit leading constant) using mixing properties of Lie groups.

1.2.
Methods of proof and comments

Our proof of Theorems
1.1

1.2
is based upon the
\delta
-variant of the circle method developed by Heath-Brown
[
9
]
(and goes back to
[
6
]
), and is underpinned by the blueprint laid down in
[
10
]
. Having disposed of the ingredients developed in
[
10
]
dealing with various sums of Salié sums, the main technical challenge of the current article is to tackle the oscillatory integrals, especially their partial derivatives. Due to the inhomogeneous form of the equation defining
V_{1}
, we need to go beyond the estimates furnished by
[
9
, Lemma 22]
, and have to contend with the appearance of oscillatory factors while differentiating the oscillatory integrals in the treatment of both exceptional and especially ordinary Poisson variables. We refer to Remarks
3.4
and
3.11
respectively for more explanations.

Under the assumption (
1.6
) the affine quadric
V_{1}
carries a non-trivial Brauer group that can evaluate non-constantly (cf. e.g.
[
4
, Lemma 4.3]
).
For instance, it can happen that these local conditions specify a closed subset of
V_{1}(\mathbf{A}_{{\mathbb{Q}}})
which does not intersect
V_{1}(\mathbf{A}_{{\mathbb{Q}}})^{\operatorname{Br}}
. In that case
\Gamma_{w}(p_{0}^{2h})=0
for any
h
and we must have
b_{h}=-\widetilde{{\mathscr{I}}}(w)\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{F}(p_{0};L,\boldsymbol{\lambda})
. However, apart from this particular occasion, due to its complicated (though explicit) form and its subtle dependence on how
h\to\infty
, we are not quite able to compute it directly from its expression.
2
2

2

Prof. Dasheng Wei has kindly informed us that he is able to show that
\Gamma_{w}(N)\gg\sqrt{N}
if the the local conditions are unobstructed, using the universal torsor method. From this we can infer that in this case
|b_{h}|<\widetilde{{\mathscr{I}}}(w)\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{F}(p_{0};L,\boldsymbol{\lambda})
.
Likewise we do not have a good understanding of
a_{h}
, though
a_{h},b_{h}
resemble fairly similar to each other, and both arise in roughly the same way as the secondary term in
[
10
, Theorem 1.2]
.

In the case where
F
is positive definitive we can say a bit more about
b_{h}
, on appealing to work of Duke–Schulze-Pillot
[
7
]
. Let us assume for simplicity that
L=1
, i.e. there is no congruence condition. The product of local densities of
{\mathscr{V}}_{1}
, according to Siegel’s mass formula (cf.
[
7
, Theorem 2 (i)]
), equals a weighted sum of representation masses of forms of the same genera as
F
. And
[
7
, Theorem 5]
shows that the leading constant for the number of representations of by
F
equals a weighted sum of representation masses of forms of the same
spinor
genera as
F
. So
b_{h}
measures the difference of these two weighted sums, and according to
[
7
, Theorem 2 (ii)]
, it equals a product of local densities, defined in terms of spinor norms, which can be reinterpreted via evaluation of the Brauer group by work of Colliot-Thélène–Xu
[
3
]
. From this heuristic, we expect that in general the term
b_{h}
should encode the effect of Brauer–Manin obstruction regarding our imposed local conditions.

Keeping our motivation in mind, we content ourselves in the current article with the assumption (
1.2
). In view of Linnik’s problem, we do not seek to explore the weakest assumption for
N
under which our arguments apply unconditionally. Presumably we may flexibly vary the square part of
N
and allow the largest square-free prime divisor of
N
to grow “slowly” perhaps like a power of
\log
. For more general
N
the “Grand Riemann Hypothesis” might be needed.

Proposition 3.12

Proof.

By the Chinese remainder theorem,

is multiplicative in

By Lemma

, for

Therefore, the formal series

,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\nu}(s)

\displaystyle:=\prod_{p<\infty}\left(1-\frac{\psi_{0}(p)}{p^{s-2}}\right)\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\frac{\widetilde{S}_{p^{t}}(\boldsymbol{0})}{p^{ts}}

We now compute explicitly the
p
-adic densities above.

The Fefferman Metric for Twistor CR Manifolds and Conformal Geodesics in Dimension Three
Taiji MARUGAME

Abstract
We give an explicit description of the Fefferman metric for twistor CR manifolds in terms of Riemannian structures on the base conformal 3 3 -manifolds. As an application, we prove that chains and null chains on twistor CR manifolds project to conformal geodesics, and that any conformal geodesic has lifts both to a chain and a null chain. By using this correspondence, we give a variational characterization of conformal geodesics in dimension three which involves the total torsion functional.

Introduction

The Fefferman construction in CR geometry associates a pseudo-Riemannian conformal manifold
\bigl(\mathcal{C},\bigl[g^{\mathrm{F}}\bigr]\bigr)
, called the
Fefferman space
, to any non-degenerate CR manifold
{\bigl(M,T^{1,0}M\bigr)}

[
14
,
22
]
.
The manifold
\mathcal{C}
is a circle bundle over
M
and the Fefferman metric
g^{\mathrm{F}}
is an
S^{1}
-invariant metric on
\mathcal{C}
which is conformally flat if and only if
M
is CR isomorphic to the hyperquadric, i.e., the flat model of CR manifold. Since the conformal structure
\bigl[g^{\mathrm{F}}\bigr]
is canonically defined from the CR structure, the construction enables us to apply conformal geometry methods to the study of CR manifolds.

The aim of this paper is to describe explicitly the Fefferman metric for a special class of CR
5
-manifolds, namely
twistor CR manifolds
, and apply it to
3
-dimensional conformal geometry. Given a conformal
3
-manifold
\bigl(\Sigma^{3},[g]\bigr)
of Riemannian signature, LeBrun’s twistor CR manifold
[
21
]
is defined as the space of projectivized complex null (co)vectors:

M^{5}:=\{[\zeta]\in\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{C}T^{*}\Sigma)\mid g(\zeta,\zeta)=0\}.

Each fiber of
M
is a rational curve in
\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{C}T_{x}^{*}\Sigma)\cong\mathbb{CP}^{2}
, and it is shown that
M
has a canonical CR structure of Lorentzian signature. We mention the work
[
23
]
of Low, which also constructs the Fefferman metric of twistor CR manifolds over more general 3-manifolds with connections. Our description of the Fefferman metric is different from that of
[
23
]
, and has the advantage that it is constructed more directly from the conformal structure on
\Sigma
without passing through the Tanaka–Webster connection on
M
.

Fixing a representative metric
g\in[g]
, we show that the
\mathrm{U}(1)
-bundle

\widehat{\mathcal{C}}:=\bigl\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C}T^{*}\Sigma\mid g(\zeta,\zeta)=0,\,g\bigl(\zeta,\overline{\zeta}\bigr)=1\bigr\}

over
M
can be identified with a double covering of the Fefferman space
\mathcal{C}
by constructing an ‘adapted’ coframe
\bigl(\tilde{\theta}^{0}=\theta,\tilde{\theta}^{1},\tilde{\theta}^{2},\tilde{\theta}^{\overline{1}},\tilde{\theta}^{\overline{2}},\tilde{\theta}^{3}\bigr)
on
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
out of the Riemannian structure
g
.
Here,
\theta
is a contact form on
M
determined by
g
, and
\bigl(\tilde{\theta}^{1},\tilde{\theta}^{2}\bigr)
is a ‘twisted’ coframe for
T^{1,0}M
while
\tilde{\theta}^{3}
restricts to a coframe for the
S^{1}
-fibers. In this coframe, (the pullback of) the Fefferman metric
g^{\mathrm{F}}
on
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
is written as

g^{\mathrm{F}}=2\bigl(\tilde{\theta}^{1}\cdot\tilde{\theta}^{\overline{2}}+\tilde{\theta}^{2}\cdot\tilde{\theta}^{\overline{1}}+\theta\cdot\tilde{\theta}^{3}\bigr).

Using this expression, we compute the Levi-Civita connection and the curvature tensor of
g^{\mathrm{F}}
in terms of those of
g
. In particular, the Weyl curvature of
g^{\mathrm{F}}
is described by the Hodge dual of the Cotton tensor of
g
. As a consequence, we can recover
[
23
, Theorem 6.7]
, which states that the Fefferman metric is conformally flat if and only if
(\Sigma,[g])
is conformally flat (Theorem
4.8
).

As another application, we establish a correspondence between distinguished curves on the CR manifold
\bigl(M,T^{1,0}M\bigr)
and the conformal manifold
(\Sigma,[g])
.

Since null geodesics of
g^{\mathrm{F}}
are conformally invariant up to reparametrizations, their projections define a CR invariant family of curves on
M
, called
(
null
)
chains
. The infinitesimal generator
K
of the
S^{1}
-action on
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
is a Killing vector field,
so
c:=g^{\mathrm{F}}(K,\dot{\gamma}(t))
is constant in
t
for any null geodesic
\gamma
. The projection of
\gamma
is called a chain if
c\neq 0
and a null chain if
c=0
. Chains are transverse to the contact distribution
H=\operatorname{Re}T^{1,0}M
and uniquely determined by the initial velocity. Thus, they can be considered as ‘geodesics’ in CR geometry. In contrast, null chains are tangent to
H
and we also need information of the initial acceleration to specify a null chain; see
[
17
]
.

The base conformal manifold
(\Sigma,[g])
, on the other hand, has a conformally invariant family of curves, called
conformal geodesics
or
conformal circles
. They are defined by a conformally invariant
third order ODE

\nabla_{\dot{x}}\nabla_{\dot{x}}\dot{x}-\frac{3g(\dot{x},\nabla_{\dot{x}}\dot{x})}{|\dot{x}|^{2}}\nabla_{\dot{x}}\dot{x}+\frac{3|\nabla_{\dot{x}}\dot{x}|^{2}}{2|\dot{x}|^{2}}\dot{x}+2P(\dot{x},\dot{x})\dot{x}-|\dot{x}|^{2}P(\dot{x})=0,

where
P
denotes the Schouten tensor of
g\in[g]
.
Due to its conformal invariance, conformal geodesics play an important role in general relativity to examine the structure of spacetime; see, e.g.,
[
12
,
15
]
.
Although the conformal geodesic equation specifies distinguished parametrizations of
x(t)
, called projective parameters
[
1
]
, we consider conformal geodesics as unparametrized curves in this paper.

By computing the null geodesic equation for the Fefferman metric
g^{\mathrm{F}}
, we prove that these invariant curves on
M
and
\Sigma
are related as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(\Sigma,[g])
be a conformal
3
-manifold and
M
the twistor CR manifold over
\Sigma
. Then, the following hold:

(1)

Any chain and any null chain on
M
project to a constant curve or a conformal geodesic on
\Sigma
.

(2)

For any conformal geodesic
x(t)
,
a\leq t\leq b
, on
\Sigma
and
[\zeta_{0}]\in M_{x(a)}
, there exists a unique chain
(
resp. a null chain
)
through
[\zeta_{0}]
which projects to
x(t)
when
g(\eta_{0},\dot{x}(a))\neq 0

(
resp.
g(\eta_{0},\dot{x}(a))=0)
, where
\eta_{0}:={\rm i}\zeta_{0}\times\overline{\zeta_{0}}
.

This theorem asserts that any (null) chain projects to a conformal geodesic, and conversely we can lift any conformal geodesic to a (null) chain. The lift is determined by a choice of the lift of the endpoint
x(a)
, but in fact there is a canonical choice, namely the lift with
\eta_{0}
being proportional to the initial velocity
\dot{x}(a)
. The lift is described as follows:
We fix a metric
g\in[g]
and a (local) orientation of
\Sigma
. For any regular curve
x(t)
on
\Sigma
, we have a complex null (co)vector field
\zeta(t)
, unique up to the
\mathrm{U}(1)
-actions, such that
\bigl(\sqrt{2}\operatorname{Re}\zeta,\sqrt{2}\operatorname{Im}\zeta\bigr)
gives an oriented orthonormal basis of
\dot{x}(t)^{\perp}\subset T_{x(t)}\Sigma
. The projective class
[\zeta(t)]
is independent of the choice of
g\in[g]
and we have a canonical lift
\tilde{x}(t):=(x(t),[\zeta(t)])
on
M
, which is transverse to the contact distribution. Then, we can prove that
\tilde{x}(t)
is a chain if and only if
x(t)
is a conformal geodesic (Theorem
4.12
).

There is an alternative description of the Fefferman space
\bigl(\widehat{\mathcal{C}},g^{\mathrm{F}}\bigr)
and the canonical lift
\tilde{x}(t)
in terms of the unit tangent sphere bundle
S\Sigma\subset T\Sigma
and the oriented orthonormal frame bundle
\mathcal{P}
over
(\Sigma,g)
: Their relations are given by the bundle isomorphisms

\textstyle{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sim}
\textstyle{\mathcal{P}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\zeta\longmapsto\bigl(\sqrt{2}\operatorname{Re}\zeta,\sqrt{2}\operatorname{Im}\zeta,\eta\bigr),}
\textstyle{M\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sim}
\textstyle{S\Sigma,}
\textstyle{\hskip-85.35826pt[\zeta]\longmapsto\eta,}

where we define
\eta:={\rm i}\zeta\times\overline{\zeta}=2\operatorname{Re}\zeta\times\operatorname{Im}\zeta
with the cross product with respect to the representative metric
g
.
By using the canonical
1
-form and the principal connection
1
-form

\phi=\begin{pmatrix}\phi^{1}\\
\phi^{2}\\
\phi^{3}\end{pmatrix},\qquad\omega=\begin{pmatrix}\hphantom{-}0&\hphantom{-}\omega^{3}&-\omega^{2}\\
-\omega^{3}&\hphantom{-}0&\hphantom{-}\omega^{1}\\
\hphantom{-}\omega^{2}&-\omega^{1}&\hphantom{-}0\end{pmatrix}

on
\mathcal{P}
, the Fefferman metric
G^{\mathrm{F}}
on
\mathcal{P}
corresponding to
g^{\mathrm{F}}
is represented as

G^{\mathrm{F}}=2\bigl(\phi^{1}\cdot\omega^{1}+\phi^{2}\cdot\omega^{2}+\phi^{3}\cdot\omega^{3}\bigr).

The equivariance of
\phi
,
\omega
shows that
G^{\mathrm{F}}
is a right
\mathrm{SO}(3)
-invariant metric. For a regular curve
x(t)
on
\Sigma
, the canonical lift
\tilde{x}(t)
on
S\Sigma
is given by the jet lift

\tilde{x}(t)=\biggl(x(t),\frac{\dot{x}(t)}{|\dot{x}(t)|}\biggr).

(1.1)

We can use this lift to derive a variational principle for conformal geodesics in dimension three. Recently, Dunajski–Kryński
[
11
]
gave a variational characterization of conformal geodesics (in general dimensions) as the critical curves of an integral functional under a restricted class of variations. In this paper, we give another characterization which is specific to the
3
-dimensional case.

In
[
9
]
, it is proved that chains on CR manifolds coincide with geodesics of a
certain Kropina-type singular Finsler metric obtained by applying Fermat’s principle to the Fefferman metric. In our setting, the Kropina metric on
S\Sigma
is given by

F(\tilde{x},\xi):=\frac{G^{\mathrm{F}}(s_{*}\xi,s_{*}\xi)}{G^{\mathrm{F}}(K,s_{*}\xi)},\qquad\tilde{x}\in U\subset S\Sigma,\quad\xi\in T_{\tilde{x}}S\Sigma\setminus\operatorname{Ker}\theta,

for each choice of a local section
s\colon U\to\mathcal{P}
. The Kropina metric is not defined along the contact distribution since
K\lrcorner\,G^{\mathrm{F}}
descends to the contact form
\theta
. Using the canonical lift (
1.1
), we define a functional
\mathscr{L}
on the space of regular curves on
\Sigma
by

\mathscr{L}[x(t)]:=\int_{a}^{b}F\bigl(\tilde{x}(t),\dot{\tilde{x}}(t)\bigr)\,{\rm d}t\biggl(=2\int_{a}^{b}\omega^{3}\bigl(s_{*}\dot{\tilde{x}}\bigr)\,{\rm d}t\biggr),

where
s
is a local section of
\mathcal{P}\to S\Sigma
on a neighborhood of the curve
\tilde{x}(t)
.
If
x(t)
is a conformal geodesic, then
\tilde{x}(t)
becomes a chain and hence
x(t)
is a critical curve of
\mathscr{L}
under the variations fixing the lifts
\tilde{x}(a)
,
\tilde{x}(b)
of the endpoints.
Conversely, if
x(t)
is a critical curve of
\mathscr{L}
, the Kropina length functional is stationary at
\tilde{x}(t)
with respect to the variations given by the lift of variations of
x(t)
. Combined with the fact that the variations of the Kropina length in the vertical directions always vanish (Proposition
5.7
), this implies that
\tilde{x}(t)
becomes a chain. Consequently, conformal geodesics are characterized as the critical curves of
\mathscr{L}
under the variations fixing
\tilde{x}(a)
,
\tilde{x}(b)
(Theorem
5.8
).

The functional
\mathscr{L}
is closely related to the so-called
total torsion functional
, which we denote by
\mathscr{T}
. For a regular curve
x(t)
,
a\leq t\leq b
, on
(\Sigma,g)
, we fix an isometry
\dot{x}(a)^{\perp}\cong\dot{x}(b)^{\perp}
by choosing oriented orthonormal basis
A=(e_{1},e_{2},\dot{x}(a)/|\dot{x}(a)|)\in\mathcal{P}_{x(a)}
and
B=\bigl(e^{\prime}_{1},e^{\prime}_{2},\dot{x}(b)/|\dot{x}(b)|\bigr)\in\mathcal{P}_{x(b)}
at the endpoints. Then,
\mathscr{T}[x(t)]\in\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}
is defined to be the angle from
\nu(a)
to
\nu(b)
, where
\nu(t)
is a unit normal vector field along
x(t)
which is parallel with respect to
\nabla^{\perp}
. The total torsion depends on the choice of
A
,
B
, but it is
conformally invariant once we fix the conformal classes of
A
and
B
; see Proposition
5.11
. If we use
A=s(\tilde{x}(a)),B=s(\tilde{x}(b))
, then we have

\mathscr{L}[x(t)]\equiv 2\mathscr{T}[x(t)]\mod 2\pi\mathbb{Z}

(Proposition
5.12
). Thus, conformal geodesics are also characterized as critical curves of the total torsion functional
\mathscr{T}
.

When the acceleration
\nabla_{\dot{x}}\dot{x}
is nowhere proportional to the velocity
\dot{x}
, we can define the
torsion

\tau(t)
of
x(t)
, which is given by the formula

\tau(t)=\frac{\det(\dot{x},\nabla_{\dot{x}}\dot{x},\nabla_{\dot{x}}\nabla_{\dot{x}}\dot{x})}{|\dot{x}\times\nabla_{\dot{x}}\dot{x}|^{2}},

and the total torsion is represented as the total integral of the torsion up to the addition of a constant
c\in\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}
determined by
s
:

\mathscr{T}[x(t)]\equiv-\int_{a}^{b}\tau(t)|\dot{x}(t)|\,{\rm d}t+c\mod 2\pi\mathbb{Z}.

In summary, we have the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

A regular curve
x(t)
,
a\leq t\leq b
, on
\bigl(\Sigma^{3},g\bigr)
is a conformal geodesic if and only if it is a critical curve of the total torsion functional
\mathscr{T}
under the variations fixing
(x(a),\dot{x}(a)/|\dot{x}(a)|)
and
(x(b),\dot{x}(b)/|\dot{x}(b)|)
with common
A
,
B
.

When
x(t)
satisfies
\dot{x}\times\nabla_{\dot{x}}\dot{x}\neq 0
, the functional
\mathscr{T}
can be replaced by the integral functional

\int_{a}^{b}\tau(t)|\dot{x}(t)|\,{\rm d}t=\int_{a}^{b}\frac{\det(\dot{x},\nabla_{\dot{x}}\dot{x},\nabla_{\dot{x}}\nabla_{\dot{x}}\dot{x})}{|\dot{x}\times\nabla_{\dot{x}}\dot{x}|^{2}}|\dot{x}|\,{\rm d}t.

The variationality of conformal geodesics on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
has been proved by
Barros–Ferrández
[
3
,
4
]
, and Theorem
1.2
generalizes their result.

The variation of the total torsion in the curved cases is computed by Chern–Knöppel–Pedit–Pinkall
[
10
]
in a different context, and Theorem
1.2
can also be proved directly from their formula; see Theorem
5.13
.

After our work was completed, the author was informed of an independent work of
Kruglikov–Matveev–Steneker
[
19
]
, which also proves Theorem
1.2
. Their proof is different from ours and is based on a detailed analysis of the Euler–Lagrange equation of the total torsion functional. Kruglikov
[
18
]
also proved that conformal geodesics are not variational in dimensions greater than three.

Finally, we comment on the relation of our results to the general theory of Cartan geometry. Conformal geometry and CR geometry are both examples of so-called
parabolic geometry
, that is, Cartan geometry modeled on a homogeneous space
G/P
where
G
is a semi-simple Lie group and
P
is a parabolic subgroup. Following the pioneering works of Cartan and Tanaka, the general theory of parabolic geometry has been extensively developed; see
[
7
]
for an exposition of the theory together with historical remarks.
In particular, Fefferman’s construction in CR geometry has been generalized within the broader framework of extensions
G\subset\widetilde{G}
of symmetry groups
[
6
,
7
]
. The twistor CR manifold can also be reinterpreted in this setting. In fact, LeBrun’s twistor CR structure corresponds to the special case (
p=q=0
) of the twistor construction of CR structure from quaternionic contact structures associated with the extension
\mathrm{Sp}(p+1,q+1)\subset\mathrm{SU}(2p+2,2q+2)
described in
[
7
, Section 4.5.5]
. We also remark that Sato–Yamaguchi
[
25
]
introduced the twistor CR structure on the tangent sphere bundle over conformal 3-manifolds by a Cartan geometric method independently of LeBrun. Moreover, a general theory of distinguished families of curves in parabolic geometry, including CR chains and conformal geodesics, has been developed by Čap–Slovák–Žádník
[
8
]
. However, the correspondence between distinguished curves in the general setting of Fefferman-type constructions does not yet appear to be fully understood. Developing a general theory in this direction would therefore be of considerable interest, and the present paper may be viewed as a case study illustrating the correspondence between distinguished curves and their variational character.

This paper is organized as follows: In Section
2
, we review the notion of CR structure and define (null) chains on CR manifolds via the Fefferman metric. In Section
3
, we recall the definition of conformal geodesic and introduce LeBrun’s twistor CR manifold over conformal
3
-manifolds. Then, in Section
4
, we give an explicit description of the Fefferman metric for twistor CR manifolds and compute its connection, curvature, and null geodesic equation to prove Theorem
1.1
(1). Here, we also prove that the canonical lift of a conformal geodesic becomes a chain, which gives a partial proof to Theorem
1.1
(2); the full proof will be given in Section
5.1
after we reinterpret our constructions in the sphere/frame bundle picture. In Section
5
, we give a variational characterization for conformal geodesics in dimension three via the Kropina metric for chains, and discuss its relation to the total torsion functional.

2
CR manifolds and the Fefferman metric

2.1
CR structures

An
almost CR structure
on a
(2n+1)
-dimensional
C^{\infty}
manifold
M
is defined to be a rank
n
complex vector sub-bundle
T^{1,0}M\subset\mathbb{C}TM
satisfying
T^{1,0}M\cap T^{0,1}M=0
, where
{T^{0,1}M:=\overline{T^{1,0}M}}
. If the space of sections of
T^{1,0}M
is closed under the Lie bracket, it is called a
CR structure
. Since
T^{1,0}M
contains no non-zero real vector, the real part
H:=\operatorname{Re}T^{1,0}M\subset TM
becomes a rank
2n
real sub-bundle satisfying
\mathbb{C}H=T^{1,0}M\oplus T^{0,1}M
.

We say a CR structure
T^{1,0}M
is
non-degenerate
if
H
is a contact distribution, or equivalently, the
Levi form

T^{1,0}_{x}M\times T^{0,1}_{x}M\ni\bigl(Z,\overline{W}\bigr)\longmapsto{\rm i}\bigl[Z,\overline{W}\bigr]\in T_{x}M/H_{x}

gives a non-degenerate
TM/H
-valued hermitian form on
T_{x}^{1,0}M
at any point
x\in M
. We note that the right-hand side is well-defined via arbitrary extensions of
Z
,
\overline{W}
to vector fields.

We usually assume that the real line bundle
TM/H
is orientable so that we can trivialize
H^{\perp}\subset T^{*}M
by a global contact form
\theta\in\Gamma\bigl(H^{\perp}\bigr)
. For a fixed orientation of
TM/H
, a contact form is determined up to multiplications by positive functions:

\theta\mapsto\widehat{\theta}={\rm e}^{\Upsilon}\theta
,
\Upsilon\in C^{\infty}(M)
. For each choice of
\theta
, we have the real valued Levi form
h_{\theta}
by trivializing
TM/H
, which transforms conformally as
h_{\widehat{\theta}}={\rm e}^{\Upsilon}h_{\theta}
.

The
Reeb vector field
of a contact form
\theta
is a real vector field
T
uniquely
specified by the conditions
\theta(T)=1
,
T\lrcorner\,{\rm d}\theta=0
. Choosing a local frame
(Z_{\alpha})
for
T^{1,0}M
, we have a local frame
\bigl(T,Z_{\alpha},Z_{\overline{\alpha}}:=\overline{Z_{\alpha}}\bigr)
for
\mathbb{C}TM=\mathbb{C}T\oplus T^{1,0}M\oplus T^{0,1}M
called an
admissible frame
, and its dual coframe
\bigl(\theta,\theta^{\alpha},\theta^{\overline{\alpha}}=\overline{\theta^{\alpha}}\bigr)
. In this coframe, we can write as

{\rm d}\theta={\rm i}h_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}\theta^{\alpha}\wedge\theta^{\overline{\beta}}

and the Levi form is given by
\bigl(Z,\overline{W}\bigr)\mapsto h_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}Z^{\alpha}\overline{W^{\beta}}
.

2.2
The Fefferman metric

The Fefferman metric associated to CR manifolds was first introduced by Fefferman
[
14
]
for the boundary of strictly pseudoconvex domains in
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
via an indefinite Kähler metric on
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}\times\mathbb{C}^{*}
called the ambient metric. Burns–Diederich–Shnider
[
5
]
gave a construction via the CR Cartan bundle, and intrinsic characterizations were obtained by Farris
[
13
]
and Lee
[
22
]
.
Here, we introduce the Fefferman metric by following Lee’s formulation.

Let
\bigl(M,T^{1,0}M\bigr)
be a non-degenerate
(2n+1)
-dimensional CR manifold.
The
CR canonical bundle
is the complex line bundle over
M
defined by

K_{M}:=\biggl\{\xi\in\bigwedge\nolimits^{\!n+1}(\mathbb{C}T^{*}M)\,\Big|\,\overline{Z}\lrcorner\,\xi=0\ \textrm{for any}\ \overline{Z}\in T^{0,1}M\biggr\}.

We note that a choice of an admissible frame gives a local frame
\theta\wedge\theta^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\theta^{n}
for
K_{M}
. The
Fefferman space
is the associated
S^{1}
-bundle

\mathcal{C}:=K^{\times}_{M}/\mathbb{R}_{+}

over
M
. If we choose a contact form
\theta
, the Fefferman space is realized as a sub-bundle of
K^{\times}_{M}
as

\mathcal{C}\cong\bigl\{{\rm e}^{{\rm i}s}\theta\wedge\theta^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\theta^{n}\mid s\in\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}\bigr\}\subset K^{\times}_{M},

(2.1)

where
\bigl(\theta,\theta^{\alpha},\theta^{\overline{\alpha}}\bigr)
is an admissible coframe such that
\bigl|\det\bigl(h_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}\bigr)\bigr|=1
. This realization defines a tautological
(n+1)
-form

\xi={\rm e}^{{\rm i}s}\theta\wedge\theta^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\theta^{n}

on
\mathcal{C}
. Also,
\varphi={\rm e}^{{\rm i}s}\theta^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\theta^{n}
becomes a global
n
-form on
\mathcal{C}
uniquely characterized by the conditions

\xi=\theta\wedge\varphi,\qquad V\lrcorner\,\varphi=0\quad\text{for any lift}\ V\ \text{of}\ T;

see
[
22
, Lemma 3.3]
.

We also have a
1
-form
\sigma
on
\mathcal{C}
specified by the following proposition.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
22
, Proposition 3.4]
)
.

There exists a unique real
1
-form
\sigma
on
\mathcal{C}
satisfying

\displaystyle{\rm d}\xi={\rm i}(n+2)\sigma\wedge\xi,

\displaystyle\sigma\wedge{\rm d}\varphi\wedge\overline{\varphi}=(\operatorname{Tr}{\rm d}\sigma){\rm i}\sigma\wedge\theta\wedge\varphi\wedge\overline{\varphi}.

Here, for a 2-form
\omega
on
M
written as
\omega={\rm i}\omega_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}\theta^{\alpha}\wedge\theta^{\overline{\beta}}+\cdots
in an admissible coframe, we define

\operatorname{Tr}\omega:=h^{\alpha\overline{\beta}}\omega_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}
using the inverse
h^{\alpha\overline{\beta}}
of the Levi form. The
1
-form
(n+2)\sigma
gives a principal connection form on the
S^{1}
-bundle
\mathcal{C}
.

By using
\sigma
, we now introduce the
Fefferman metric
as the pseudo-Riemannian metric on
\mathcal{C}
defined by

g^{\mathrm{F}}:=2h_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}\theta^{\alpha}\cdot\theta^{\overline{\beta}}+4\theta\cdot\sigma.

We note that our definition of
g^{\mathrm{F}}
is
twice
the definition of Lee. In the coframe
\smash{\bigl(\theta^{0}:=\theta,\theta^{\alpha},\theta^{\overline{\alpha}}},\allowbreak\smash{\theta^{n+1}:=2\sigma\bigr)}
, the non-zero components of the metric tensor are

g^{\mathrm{F}}_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}=g^{\mathrm{F}}_{\overline{\beta}\alpha}=h_{\alpha\overline{\beta}},\qquad g^{\mathrm{F}}_{0\,n+1}=g^{\mathrm{F}}_{n+1\,0}=1.

If the Levi form has signature
(p,q)
as a hermitian form, then the Fefferman metric has signature
(2p+1,2q+1)
. Moreover, if we rescale the contact form
as
\widehat{\theta}={\rm e}^{\Upsilon}\theta
, the Fefferman metric transforms as

\widehat{g}^{\,\mathrm{F}}={\rm e}^{\Upsilon}g^{\mathrm{F}}
. Thus, the conformal structure
\bigl[g^{\mathrm{F}}\bigr]
is independent of the choice of contact form
[
22
, Theorem 5.17]
.

It follows from the formula in
[
22
]
of the connection form of
g^{\mathrm{F}}
in terms of the pseudo-hermitian structure of
M
that
g^{\mathrm{F}}
is conformally flat if and only if
M
is locally CR equivalent to the hyperquadric

\mathcal{Q}=\bigl\{[\xi]\in\mathbb{CP}^{n+1}\,\big|\,-\bigl|\xi^{0}\bigr|^{2}-\bigl|\xi^{1}\bigr|^{2}-\cdots-\bigl|\xi^{q}\bigr|^{2}+\bigl|\xi^{q+1}\bigr|^{2}+\cdots+\bigl|\xi^{n+1}\bigr|^{2}=0\bigr\},

which is the flat model of CR manifold.

2.3
Chains and null chains

It is well-known that null geodesics of a pseudo-Riemannian metric are conformally invariant up to reparametrizations. Hence, the projections of null geodesics of the Fefferman metric provide an invariant family of curves on CR manifolds. Let
\gamma(t)
be a null geodesic of
g^{\mathrm{F}}
. Since
g^{\mathrm{F}}
is
S^{1}
-invariant, the infinitesimal generator
K
of the
S^{1}
-action on
\mathcal{C}
is a Killing vector field and hence
g^{\mathrm{F}}(K,\dot{\gamma}(t))
is constant in
t
.

Definition 2.2

(
[
14
,
17
]
)
.

An unparametrized curve on
M
is called a
chain
(resp.
null chain
) if it is the projection of a non-vertical null geodesic
\gamma(t)
of
\bigl[g^{\mathrm{F}}\bigr]
with
g^{\mathrm{F}}(K,\dot{\gamma})\neq 0

(
resp.
g^{\mathrm{F}}(K,\dot{\gamma})=0
)
.

Since
K\lrcorner\,g^{\mathrm{F}}=2\theta
, the definition implies that chains are transverse to the contact distribution
H
while null chains are tangent to
H
and null with respect to the Levi form.

For each choice of a direction transverse to
H_{x}
, there exists a unique chain emanating in that direction. Thus, chains can be considered as ‘geodesics’ in CR geometry. In fact, they are characterized as geodesics of a Kropina metric on CR manifolds, which is a Finsler metric singular along
H
; see
[
9
]
and Section
5.2
below. On the other hand, there exists a 1-parameter family of null chains which are tangent to a fixed null vector in
H_{x}
; see
[
17
]
.

3
Conformal three-manifolds and twistor CR manifolds

3.1
Conformal three-manifolds

Let
(\Sigma,[g])
be a
3
-dimensional conformal manifold (of Riemannian signature). We take a representative metric
g\in[g]
and let
R_{ij}{}^{k}{}_{l}
be the curvature tensor of
g
, defined by
(\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}-\nabla_{j}\nabla_{i})X^{k}=R_{ij}{}^{k}{}_{l}X^{l}
. Since the Weyl curvature always vanishes in three dimensions, we can write as

R_{ijkl}=P_{ik}g_{jl}-P_{jk}g_{il}+P_{jl}g_{ik}-P_{il}g_{jk}

(3.1)

with the Schouten tensor

P_{ij}:=R_{ij}-\frac{1}{4}Rg_{ij}.

Here
R_{ij}:=R_{ki}{}^{k}{}_{j}
is the Ricci tensor and
R:=R_{k}{}^{k}
is the scalar curvature.

The Cotton tensor
C=(C_{ijk})\in\Gamma\bigl(T^{*}\Sigma\otimes\wedge^{2}T^{*}\Sigma\bigr)
is defined by

C(X,Y,Z):=(\nabla_{Z}P)(X,Y)-(\nabla_{Y}P)(X,Z),\qquad\text{i.e.,}\qquad C_{ijk}=\nabla_{k}P_{ij}-\nabla_{j}P_{ik}.

Since
\Sigma
is 3-dimensional, the Cotton tensor is conformally invariant, and identically vanishes if and only if
(\Sigma,[g])
is conformally flat.

If
\Sigma
is oriented, we have the cross product
Z=X\times Y\in T_{p}\Sigma
of tangent vectors
X,Y\in T_{p}\Sigma
. In the index notation, it is given by

Z^{i}=\varepsilon^{i}{}_{jk}X^{j}Y^{k},

where
\varepsilon_{ijk}=\varepsilon_{[ijk]}
is the volume form of
g
:

vol_{g}=\frac{1}{3!}\varepsilon_{ijk}\theta^{i}\wedge\theta^{j}\wedge\theta^{k}.

Applying the Hodge star operator to the Cotton tensor, we define
*C\in\Gamma(T^{*}\Sigma\otimes T^{*}\Sigma)
by

(*C)_{ij}:=\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{j}{}^{kl}C_{ikl},

which is characterized by the equation

C(X,Y,Z)=(*C)(X,Y\times Z).

Since
C_{ijk}
is trace-free by the contracted second Bianchi identity,
(*C)_{ij}
becomes a symmetric 2-tensor.

3.2
Conformal geodesics

Although geodesics of a Riemannian metric are not conformally invariant, there is an alternative conformally invariant family of curves on conformal manifolds, called
conformal geodesics
. They are characterized by the conformally invariant third order ODE

\nabla_{\dot{x}}\nabla_{\dot{x}}\dot{x}-\frac{3g(\dot{x},\nabla_{\dot{x}}\dot{x})}{|\dot{x}|^{2}}\nabla_{\dot{x}}\dot{x}+\frac{3|\nabla_{\dot{x}}\dot{x}|^{2}}{2|\dot{x}|^{2}}\dot{x}+2P(\dot{x},\dot{x})\dot{x}-|\dot{x}|^{2}P(\dot{x})=0.

(3.2)

Here, we set
P(\dot{x},\dot{x}):=P_{ij}\dot{x}^{i}\dot{x}^{j}
and
P(\dot{x})^{i}:=P^{i}{}_{j}\dot{x}^{j}
. We refer the reader to
[
2
]
for a derivation of this equation by using the tractor calculus, which is an invariant calculus in conformal geometry.
Conformal geodesics are also called
conformal circles
since these curves are circles and straight lines when
g
is the Euclidean metric.

It is shown in
[
1
]
that if
x(t)
is a solution to the equation (
3.2
), a reparametrized curve
\tilde{x}(t)=x(\varphi(t))
solves (
3.2
) if and only if
\varphi(t)
is a projective linear transformation:
\varphi(t)=(at+b)/(ct+d)
. Hence, we call a solution to (
3.2
)
a
conformal geodesic with a projective parameter
. In
[
1
]
, they also prove that one can define an equation for ‘unparametrized conformal geodesics’ by taking the
\dot{x}
-orthogonal part of (
3.2
).

Proposition 3.1

(
[
1
, Proposition 4.2]
)
.

A regular curve
x(t)
can be reparametrized to satisfy (
3.2
) if and only if it satisfies the equation

\dot{x}\wedge\biggl(\nabla_{\dot{x}}\nabla_{\dot{x}}\dot{x}-\frac{3g(\dot{x},\nabla_{\dot{x}}\dot{x})}{|\dot{x}|^{2}}\nabla_{\dot{x}}\dot{x}-|\dot{x}|^{2}P(\dot{x})\biggr)=0.

(3.3)

In this paper, we always consider conformal geodesics as unparametrized curves, and we say a regular curve
x(t)
is a
conformal geodesic
if it satisfies (
3.3
).
We note that if
x(t)
has a constant speed
|\dot{x}|
, the equation (
3.3
) is reduced to

\dot{x}\wedge\bigl(\nabla_{\dot{x}}\nabla_{\dot{x}}\dot{x}-|\dot{x}|^{2}P(\dot{x})\bigr)=0

(3.4)

since
g(\dot{x},\nabla_{\dot{x}}\dot{x})=0
.

Conformal geodesics are also characterized as follows.

Proposition 3.2

(
[
1
, Proposition 3.3]
)
.

A regular curve
x(t)
is a conformal geodesic if and only if there exist a metric
g\in[g]
and a reparametrization such that

\nabla_{\dot{x}}\dot{x}=P(\dot{x})=0.

3.3
Twistor CR manifolds

We will define the twistor CR manifold
M
over
\bigl(\Sigma^{3},[g]\bigr)
, which was introduced by LeBrun
[
21
]
as a
3
-dimensional analogue of Penrose’s twistor space for anti-self dual conformal
4
-manifolds.

We extend a representative metric
g\in[g]
on
T^{*}\Sigma
to a complex bi-linear form on
\mathbb{C}T^{*}\Sigma
and define a
7
-dimensional manifold
\hat{M}
as the set of complex null covectors:

\hat{M}:=\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C}T^{*}\Sigma\mid g(\zeta,\zeta)=0,\,\zeta\neq 0\}.

By taking the quotient by the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action, we obtain a
5
-dimensional manifold

M:=\hat{M}/\mathbb{C}^{*}\subset\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{C}T^{*}\Sigma).

We denote the natural projections by
\tilde{\pi}\colon\hat{M}\to M
,
\pi\colon M\to\Sigma
. Note that the fiber of
\pi
is biholomorphic to
\bigl\{[\zeta]\in\mathbb{CP}^{2}\mid\zeta_{1}^{2}+\zeta_{2}^{2}+\zeta_{3}^{2}=0\bigr\}\cong\mathbb{CP}^{1}
.

Let

\alpha:=\bigl(\zeta_{i}{\rm d}x^{i}\bigr)|_{T\hat{M}}
be the canonical (tautological)
1
-form on

\hat{M}
and set
\omega:={\rm d}\alpha
. Then, the distribution

D:=\textrm{Ker}\,\omega=\bigl\{v\in\mathbb{C}T\hat{M}\mid v\lrcorner\,\omega=0\bigr\}

is
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-invariant and we can define the distribution
\tilde{\pi}_{*}D\subset\mathbb{C}TM
. LeBrun
[
21
]
proved that

T^{1,0}M:=\overline{\tilde{\pi}_{*}D}

becomes a non-degenerate CR structure on
M
whose Levi form has signature
(1,1)
. The construction depends only on the conformal class
[g]
, and
\bigl(M,T^{1,0}M\bigr)
is called the
twistor CR manifold
over
(\Sigma,[g])
.

The CR structure
T^{1,0}M
can be described in terms of coordinates as follows.
Fix a point
x_{0}\in\Sigma
and let
(x^{i})
be normal coordinates centered at
x_{0}
with respect to a representative metric
g\in[g]
.

Proposition 3.3
.

For any point
[\zeta]\in\pi^{-1}(x_{0})
, we have

\overline{D}_{\zeta}=\mathbb{C}\overline{\zeta^{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\oplus\biggl\{w_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\zeta_{i}}\,\bigg|\,\zeta_{i}w^{i}=0\biggr\},

and the projection
\tilde{\pi}_{*}\colon\overline{D}_{\zeta}\to T^{1.0}_{[\zeta]}M
induces the isomorphism

T^{1,0}_{[\zeta]}M\cong\mathbb{C}\overline{\zeta^{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\oplus\biggl(\biggl\{w_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\zeta_{i}}\,\bigg|\,\zeta_{i}w^{i}=0\biggr\}\Big/\mathbb{C}\zeta_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\zeta_{i}}\biggr).

See
[
24
, Proposition 6.1]
for the proof.

4
The Fefferman metric and (null) chains

of twistor CR manifolds

Let
\pi\colon M\to\Sigma
be the twistor CR manifold over a conformal
3
-manifold
(\Sigma,[g])
. We will describe the Fefferman metric for
M
and compute its geometric quantities.

We fix a representative metric
g\in[g]
and define

\widehat{\mathcal{C}}:=\bigl\{\zeta\in\hat{M}\mid g\bigl(\zeta,\overline{\zeta}\bigr)=1\bigr\}=\bigl\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C}T^{*}\Sigma\mid g(\zeta,\zeta)=0,\,g\bigl(\zeta,\overline{\zeta}\bigr)=1\bigr\},

which is an
S^{1}
-bundle over
M
:
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}/\mathrm{U}(1)=M
.
We show that
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
gives a double covering of the Fefferman space
\mathcal{C}
and construct the Fefferman metric on
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
instead of
\mathcal{C}
.

4.1
An adapted frame on
\boldsymbol{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}

Let
(\zeta_{i})
be the fiber coordinates for
\zeta_{i}{\rm d}x^{i}\in\mathbb{C}T^{*}\Sigma
associated to local coordinates
(x^{i})
on
\Sigma
. On
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
, it holds that

\zeta^{i}\zeta_{i}=0,\qquad\zeta^{i}\overline{\zeta}_{i}=1.

By using the metric, we identify
\zeta_{i}{\rm d}x^{i}
with the complex tangent vector

\zeta=\zeta^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}

and regard that
\zeta
also represents the global section of the vector bundle

\hat{\pi}^{-1}(\mathbb{C}T\Sigma)
over
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
, where
\hat{\pi}\colon\widehat{\mathcal{C}}\to\Sigma
is the projection. We (locally) choose an orientation of
\Sigma
and define a real vector

\eta=\eta^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\in\hat{\pi}^{-1}(T\Sigma)

by

\eta:={\rm i}\zeta\times\overline{\zeta}=2\operatorname{Re}\zeta\times\operatorname{Im}\zeta.

Using the volume form
\varepsilon_{ijk}
of
g
, we can write as

\eta^{j}={\rm i}\varepsilon^{jkl}\zeta_{k}\overline{\zeta_{l}}.

The property of the cross product gives

\displaystyle g(\eta,\eta)=1,\qquad

\displaystyle g(\eta,\zeta)=g\bigl(\eta,\overline{\zeta}\bigr)=0,

\displaystyle\eta\times\overline{\zeta}={\rm i}\overline{\zeta},\qquad

\displaystyle\zeta\times\eta={\rm i}\zeta.

(In the index notation, one can use the identity
\varepsilon_{ijk}\varepsilon^{i}{}_{lm}=g_{jl}g_{km}-g_{kl}g_{jm}
to verify these equations.)

Since the conditions
g(\zeta,\zeta)=0
,
g\bigl(\zeta,\overline{\zeta}\bigr)=1
are preserved by the parallel transport of
\zeta
, we can define the horizontal lifts of tangent vectors on
\Sigma
to
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
. Let
\Gamma_{ij}{}^{k}
be the Christoffel symbols of the Levi-Civita connection of
g
. Then, the horizontal lift of the tangent vector
\partial/\partial x^{i}
is given by

\frac{\delta}{\delta x^{i}}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}+\Gamma_{ij}{}^{k}\zeta_{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial\zeta_{j}}+\Gamma_{ij}{}^{k}\overline{\zeta_{k}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{\zeta}_{j}}\in T\widehat{\mathcal{C}}.

Note that this vector transforms tensorially in the index
i
under coordinate changes. The annihilators of the horizontal distribution on
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
are given by

\delta\zeta_{i}:={\rm d}\zeta_{i}-\Gamma_{ki}{}^{j}\zeta_{j}{\rm d}x^{k},\qquad\delta\overline{\zeta}_{i}:={\rm d}\overline{\zeta}_{i}-\Gamma_{ki}{}^{j}\overline{\zeta}_{j}{\rm d}x^{k},

which also transform tensorially.

Using these notations, we define a frame

\bigl(\tilde{T},\tilde{Z}_{1},\tilde{Z}_{2},\tilde{Z}_{\overline{1}},\tilde{Z}_{\overline{2}},K\bigr)

for
\mathbb{C}T\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
and its dual coframe
\bigl(\theta,\tilde{\theta}^{1},\tilde{\theta}^{2},\tilde{\theta}^{\overline{1}},\tilde{\theta}^{\overline{2}},\tilde{\theta}^{3}\bigr)
by

\tilde{T}:=\eta^{i}\frac{\delta}{\delta x^{i}},\qquad\tilde{Z}_{1}:=\overline{\zeta^{i}}\frac{\delta}{\delta x^{i}},\qquad\tilde{Z}_{2}:=-{\rm i}\eta_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial\zeta_{j}},\qquad K:={\rm i}\biggl(\zeta_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial\zeta_{j}}-\overline{\zeta}_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{\zeta}_{j}}\biggr)

and

\theta:=\eta_{i}{\rm d}x^{i},\qquad\tilde{\theta}^{1}:=\zeta_{i}{\rm d}x^{i},\qquad\tilde{\theta}^{2}:={\rm i}\eta^{j}\delta\zeta_{j},\qquad\tilde{\theta}^{3}:=-\frac{{\rm i}}{2}\bigl(\overline{\zeta^{j}}\delta\zeta_{j}-\zeta^{j}\delta\overline{\zeta}_{j}\bigr)=-i\overline{\zeta^{j}}\delta\zeta_{j}.

Note that these vectors and forms are independent of the choice of coordinates and globally defined if
\Sigma
is globally oriented. We also note that
K
agrees with the infinitesimal generator of the
S^{1}
-action on
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
.

We shall prove that this indeed gives a (co)frame which is ‘adapted’ to the CR structure on
M
.

Proposition 4.1
.

(1)

The
1
-from
\theta
descends to a contact form on
M
.

(2)

The
1
-forms
\tilde{\theta}^{1}
,
\tilde{\theta}^{2}
are horizontal with respect to
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}\to M
, and for any local section
{s\colon M\to\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}
, the set of
1
-forms
\bigl(\theta,\theta^{1}=s^{*}\tilde{\theta}^{1},\theta^{2}=s^{*}\tilde{\theta}^{2},\theta^{\overline{1}}=\overline{\theta^{1}},\theta^{\overline{2}}=\overline{\theta^{2}}\bigr)
gives an admissible coframe on
M
.

Proof.

We take normal coordinates
(x^{i})
around a fixed point
x_{0}\in\Sigma
and work at a point
p\in\pi^{-1}(x_{0})\subset M
.

(1) Since each coefficient
\eta_{i}
is a
\mathrm{U}(1)
-invariant function,
\theta
descends to a
1
-form on
M
. Moreover, by Proposition
3.3
,
\theta
annihilates
T^{1,0}M\oplus T^{0,1}M
hence is a contact form on
M
. We note that the projection
T
of
\tilde{T}
to
M
is well-defined since
\tilde{T}
is
\mathrm{U}(1)
-invariant, and it gives the Reeb vector field of
\theta
. In fact,
T
is represented as
T=\eta^{i}\bigl(\partial/\partial x^{i}\bigr)
at
p
, and since

{\rm d}\varepsilon_{ijk}(x_{0})=0
,
{\rm d}g_{ij}(x_{0})=0
, we have
{\rm d}\eta_{i}(T)=0
,
\eta^{i}{\rm d}\eta_{i}=0
at
p
and hence
\theta(T)=\eta_{i}\eta^{i}=1
,
T\lrcorner\,{\rm d}\theta=T\lrcorner\,\bigl({\rm d}\eta_{i}\wedge{\rm d}x^{i}\bigr)=0
.

(2) Since
\tilde{\theta}^{1}(K)=\tilde{\theta}^{2}(K)=0
, these forms are horizontal. By Proposition
3.3
, we see that
\theta^{1}
and
\theta^{2}
annihilate
T^{0,1}M
. Moreover,
\tilde{\theta}^{1}\bigl(\tilde{T}\bigr)=\tilde{\theta}^{2}\bigl(\tilde{T}\bigr)=0
implies
\theta^{1}(T)=\theta^{2}(T)=0
. Thus,
\bigl(\theta,\theta^{1},\theta^{2},\theta^{\overline{1}},\theta^{\overline{2}}\bigr)
is an admissible coframe.
∎

We remark that
\tilde{\theta}^{1}
and
\tilde{\theta}^{2}
do not descend to
1
-forms on
M
. In fact, if we denote the action of
\lambda\in\mathrm{U}(1)
on
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
by
\delta_{\lambda}\colon\widehat{\mathcal{C}}\to\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
, we have

\delta_{\lambda}^{*}\tilde{\theta}^{1}=\lambda\tilde{\theta}^{1},\qquad\delta_{\lambda}^{*}\tilde{\theta}^{2}=\lambda\tilde{\theta}^{2}.

Thus,
\bigl(\tilde{\theta}^{1},\tilde{\theta}^{2}\bigr)
can be considered as a ‘twisted’ or ‘weighted’ coframe for
T^{1,0}M
.

We also note that if we replace
g
with
\widehat{g}={\rm e}^{2\Upsilon}g
,
\Upsilon\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
, we have
\widehat{\eta}^{\,i}={\rm e}^{-\Upsilon}\eta^{i}
,
\widehat{\eta}_{i}={\rm e}^{\Upsilon}\eta_{i}
and hence the associated contact form is rescaled as
\widehat{\theta}={\rm e}^{\Upsilon}\theta
.

4.2
The differentials of the adapted coframe

We will compute the exterior derivatives of the adapted coframe defined above.
We use the following lemma in computations in normal coordinates.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(x^{i})
be normal coordinates of
g
centered at a point
x_{0}\in\Sigma
. Then, we have

{\rm d}x^{j}=\eta^{j}\theta+\overline{\zeta^{j}}\tilde{\theta}^{1}+\zeta^{j}\tilde{\theta}^{\overline{1}},\qquad{\rm d}\zeta_{j}=-{\rm i}\bigl(\eta_{j}\tilde{\theta}^{2}-\zeta_{j}\tilde{\theta}^{3}\bigr),\qquad{\rm d}\eta^{j}={\rm i}\bigl(\overline{\zeta^{j}}\tilde{\theta}^{2}-\zeta^{j}\tilde{\theta}^{\overline{2}}\bigr)

(4.1)

at any point
p\in\pi^{-1}(x_{0})\subset M
.

Proof.

At
p
, we have

{\rm d}x^{j}\bigl(\tilde{T}\bigr)=\eta^{j},\qquad{\rm d}x^{j}\bigl(\tilde{Z}_{1}\bigr)=\overline{\zeta^{j}},\qquad{\rm d}x^{j}\bigl(\tilde{Z}_{2}\bigr)={\rm d}x^{j}(K)=0,

from which we obtain the first equation in (

). Similarly, we have

{\rm d}\zeta_{j}\bigl(\tilde{T}\bigr)={\rm d}\zeta_{j}\bigl(\tilde{Z}_{1}\bigr)={\rm d}\zeta_{j}\bigl(\tilde{Z}_{\overline{1}}\bigr)={\rm d}\zeta_{j}\bigl(\tilde{Z}_{\overline{2}}\bigr)=0,\qquad{\rm d}\zeta_{j}\bigl(\tilde{Z}_{2}\bigr)=-{\rm i}\eta_{j},\qquad{\rm d}\zeta_{j}(K)={\rm i}\zeta_{j},

and obtain the formula for
{\rm d}\zeta_{j}
. Finally, using

, we compute as

{\rm d}\eta={\rm i}\,{\rm d}\zeta\times\overline{\zeta}+{\rm i}\zeta\times{\rm d}\overline{\zeta}=\bigl(\eta\tilde{\theta}^{2}-\zeta\tilde{\theta}^{3}\bigr)\times\overline{\zeta}-\zeta\times\bigl(\eta\tilde{\theta}^{\overline{2}}-\overline{\zeta}\tilde{\theta}^{3}\bigr)={\rm i}\overline{\zeta}\tilde{\theta}^{2}-{\rm i}\zeta\tilde{\theta}^{\overline{2}}

at
p
.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

We have

\displaystyle{\rm d}\theta={\rm i}\bigl(\tilde{\theta}^{1}\wedge\tilde{\theta}^{\overline{2}}+\tilde{\theta}^{2}\wedge\tilde{\theta}^{\overline{1}}\bigr),

\displaystyle{\rm d}\tilde{\theta}^{1}={\rm i}\theta\wedge\tilde{\theta}^{2}-{\rm i}\tilde{\theta}^{1}\wedge\tilde{\theta}^{3},

\displaystyle{\rm d}\tilde{\theta}^{2}={\rm i}\bigl(P\bigl(\zeta,\overline{\zeta}\bigr)+P(\eta,\eta)\bigr)\theta\wedge\tilde{\theta}^{1}+{\rm i}P(\zeta,\zeta)\theta\wedge\tilde{\theta}^{\overline{1}}-{\rm i}P(\zeta,\eta)\tilde{\theta}^{1}\wedge\tilde{\theta}^{\overline{1}}-{\rm i}\tilde{\theta}^{2}\wedge\tilde{\theta}^{3},

\displaystyle{\rm d}\tilde{\theta}^{3}=-{\rm i}P\bigl(\overline{\zeta},\eta\bigr)\theta\wedge\tilde{\theta}^{1}+{\rm i}P(\zeta,\eta)\theta\wedge\tilde{\theta}^{\overline{1}}+2{\rm i}P\bigl(\zeta,\overline{\zeta}\bigr)\tilde{\theta}^{1}\wedge\tilde{\theta}^{\overline{1}}+{\rm i}\tilde{\theta}^{2}\wedge\tilde{\theta}^{\overline{2}}.

(4.2)

Proof.

We compute in normal coordinates around
x_{0}\in\Sigma
. By (
4.1
), we have

\displaystyle{\rm d}\theta={\rm d}\eta_{i}\wedge{\rm d}x^{i}={\rm i}\bigl(\tilde{\theta}^{1}\wedge\tilde{\theta}^{\overline{2}}+\tilde{\theta}^{2}\wedge\tilde{\theta}^{\overline{1}}\bigr),

\displaystyle{\rm d}\tilde{\theta}^{1}={\rm d}\zeta_{i}\wedge{\rm d}x^{i}={\rm i}\theta\wedge\tilde{\theta}^{2}-{\rm i}\tilde{\theta}^{1}\wedge\tilde{\theta}^{3}.

To compute
{\rm d}\tilde{\theta}^{2}
, we note that

\displaystyle{\rm d}\bigl(\Gamma_{kjl}{\rm d}x^{k}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}R_{kmlj}{\rm d}x^{k}\wedge{\rm d}x^{m}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(P_{kl}g_{mj}-P_{ml}g_{kj}+P_{mj}g_{kl}-P_{kj}g_{ml}){\rm d}x^{k}\wedge{\rm d}x^{m}

\displaystyle=(P_{kl}g_{mj}+P_{mj}g_{kl}){\rm d}x^{k}\wedge{\rm d}x^{m}

holds at
p\in\pi^{-1}(x_{0})
since
\Gamma_{ij}{}^{k}(x_{0})=0
. Using this equation and (
4.1
), we have

\displaystyle{\rm d}\tilde{\theta}^{2}={}

\displaystyle{\rm i}\,{\rm d}\eta^{j}\wedge{\rm d}\zeta_{j}-{\rm i}\eta^{j}\zeta^{l}{\rm d}\bigl(\Gamma_{kjl}{\rm d}x^{k}\bigr)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{\dot{x}}\nabla_{\dot{x}}\eta,\zeta)

\displaystyle=-\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}g(\eta,\nabla_{\dot{x}}\zeta)+g(\eta,\nabla_{\dot{x}}\zeta)g\bigl(\nabla_{\dot{x}}\zeta,\overline{\zeta}\bigr)={\rm i}\ddot{\gamma}^{2}+\dot{\gamma}^{2}\dot{\gamma}^{3}.

∎

Now we are ready to prove the following.

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
g\in[g]
be a representative metric and let
\gamma(t)=(x(t),\zeta(t))
be a null geodesic of
g^{\mathrm{F}}
on
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
such that the projection
x(t)
is a regular curve on
\Sigma
. Then,
|\dot{x}|_{g}
is constant, and
x(t)
satisfies

\nabla_{\dot{x}}\nabla_{\dot{x}}\dot{x}-|\dot{x}|^{2}P(\dot{x})=-\bigl(2\bigl|\dot{\gamma}^{2}\bigr|^{2}+\bigl(\dot{\gamma}^{3}\bigr)^{2}+P(\dot{x},\dot{x})\bigr)\dot{x}.

(4.10)

In particular,
x(t)
is a conformal geodesic of
(\Sigma,[g])
.

Proof.

We prove that the inner products of both sides of (
4.10
) with
\zeta
,
\eta
coincide.

Since
\gamma
is a geodesic,
\dot{\gamma}^{0}
and
\dot{\gamma}^{1}
are constant.
Hence, by (
4.8
),
|\dot{x}|
is constant and we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{\dot{x}}\nabla_{\dot{x}}\dot{x}-|\dot{x}|^{2}P(\dot{x})=

\displaystyle\dot{\gamma}^{0}\nabla_{\dot{x}}\nabla_{\dot{x}}\eta+\dot{\gamma}^{\overline{1}}\nabla_{\dot{x}}\nabla_{\dot{x}}\zeta+\dot{\gamma}^{1}\nabla_{\dot{x}}\nabla_{\dot{x}}\overline{\zeta}

\displaystyle{}{-}\,\bigl(\bigl(\dot{\gamma}^{0}\bigr)^{2}+2\bigl|\dot{\gamma}^{1}\bigr|^{2}\bigr)\bigl(\dot{\gamma}^{0}P(\eta)+\dot{\gamma}^{\overline{1}}P(\zeta)+\dot{\gamma}^{1}P(\overline{\zeta})\bigr).

By using (
4.9
), we compute as

\displaystyle g\bigl(\nabla_{\dot{x}}\nabla_{\dot{x}}\dot{x}-|\dot{x}|^{2}P(\dot{x}),\zeta\bigr)={}

\displaystyle\dot{\gamma}^{0}g(\nabla_{\dot{x}}\nabla_{\dot{x}}\eta,\zeta)+\dot{\gamma}^{\overline{1}}g(\nabla_{\dot{x}}\nabla_{\dot{x}}\zeta,\zeta)+\dot{\gamma}^{1}g\bigl(\nabla_{\dot{x}}\nabla_{\dot{x}}\overline{\zeta},\zeta\bigr)

\displaystyle{}{-}\,\bigl(\bigl(\dot{\gamma}^{0}\bigr)^{2}+2\bigl|\dot{\gamma}^{1}\bigr|^{2}\bigr)\bigl(\dot{\gamma}^{0}P(\eta,\zeta)+\dot{\gamma}^{\overline{1}}P(\zeta,\zeta)+\dot{\gamma}^{1}P\bigl(\overline{\zeta},\zeta\bigr)\bigr)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle{\rm i}\dot{\gamma}^{0}\ddot{\gamma}^{2}+\dot{\gamma}^{0}\dot{\gamma}^{2}\dot{\gamma}^{3}+\dot{\gamma}^{\overline{1}}\bigl(\dot{\gamma}^{2}\bigr)^{2}-{\rm i}\dot{\gamma}^{1}\ddot{\gamma}^{3}-\dot{\gamma}^{1}\bigl|\dot{\gamma}^{2}\bigr|^{2}-\dot{\gamma}^{1}\bigl(\dot{\gamma}^{3}\bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle{}{-}\bigl(\bigl(\dot{\gamma}^{0}\bigr)^{2}+2|\dot{\gamma}^{1}|^{2}\bigr)\bigl(\dot{\gamma}^{0}P(\zeta,\eta)+\dot{\gamma}^{\overline{1}}P(\zeta,\zeta)+\dot{\gamma}^{1}P\bigl(\zeta,\overline{\zeta}\bigr)\bigr).

By the nullity (
4.5
), we have

\dot{\gamma}^{0}\dot{\gamma}^{2}\dot{\gamma}^{3}+\dot{\gamma}^{\overline{1}}\bigl(\dot{\gamma}^{2}\bigr)^{2}-\dot{\gamma}^{1}\bigl|\dot{\gamma}^{2}\bigr|^{2}=-2\dot{\gamma}^{1}\bigl|\dot{\gamma}^{2}\bigr|^{2}.

Moreover, the geodesic equations (
4.6
) and (
4.7
) give

\displaystyle{\rm i}\dot{\gamma}^{0}\ddot{\gamma}^{2}=P(\zeta,\eta)\bigl(\dot{\gamma}^{0}\bigr)^{3}+\bigl(P\bigl(\zeta,\overline{\zeta}\bigr)-P(\eta,\eta)\bigr)\bigl(\dot{\gamma}^{0}\bigr)^{2}\dot{\gamma}^{1}+P(\zeta,\zeta)\bigl(\dot{\gamma}^{0}\bigr)^{2}\dot{\gamma}^{\overline{1}}

\displaystyle\hphantom{{\rm i}\dot{\gamma}^0\ddot{\gamma}^2=}{}-P\bigl(\overline{\zeta},\eta\bigr)\dot{\gamma}^{0}\bigl(\dot{\gamma}^{1}\bigr)^{2}-P(\zeta,\eta)\dot{\gamma}^{0}\bigl|\dot{\gamma}^{1}\bigr|^{2},

\displaystyle-{\rm i}\dot{\gamma}^{1}\ddot{\gamma}^{3}=-P\bigl(\overline{\zeta},\eta\bigr)\dot{\gamma}^{0}\bigl(\dot{\gamma}^{1}\bigr)^{2}+P(\zeta,\eta)\dot{\gamma}^{0}\bigl|\dot{\gamma}^{1}\bigr|^{2}-P\bigl(\overline{\zeta},\overline{\zeta}\bigr)\bigl(\dot{\gamma}^{1}\bigr)^{3}+P(\zeta,\zeta)\dot{\gamma}^{\overline{1}}\bigl|\dot{\gamma}^{1}\bigr|^{2}.

Substituting these expressions, we obtain

\displaystyle g\bigl(\nabla_{\dot{x}}\nabla_{\dot{x}}\dot{x}-|\dot{x}|^{2}P(\dot{x}),\zeta\bigr)

\displaystyle=-2\dot{\gamma}^{1}\bigl|\dot{\gamma}^{2}\bigr|^{2}-\dot{\gamma}^{1}\bigl(\dot{\gamma}^{3}\bigr)^{2}-\dot{\gamma}^{1}P(\dot{x},\dot{x})

\displaystyle=-\bigl(2\bigl|\dot{\gamma}^{2}\bigr|^{2}+\bigl(\dot{\gamma}^{3}\bigr)^{2}+P(\dot{x},\dot{x})\bigr)g(\dot{x},\zeta).

Similarly, we compute as

\displaystyle g\bigl(\nabla_{\dot{x}}\nabla_{\dot{x}}\dot{x}-|\dot{x}|^{2}P(\dot{x}),\eta\bigr)={}

\displaystyle\dot{\gamma}^{0}g(\nabla_{\dot{x}}\nabla_{\dot{x}}\eta,\eta)+\dot{\gamma}^{\overline{1}}g(\nabla_{\dot{x}}\nabla_{\dot{x}}\zeta,\eta)+\dot{\gamma}^{1}g\bigl(\nabla_{\dot{x}}\nabla_{\dot{x}}\overline{\zeta},\eta\bigr)

\displaystyle{}{-}\bigl(\bigl(\dot{\gamma}^{0}\bigr)^{2}+2\bigl|\dot{\gamma}^{1}\bigr|^{2}\bigr)\bigl(\dot{\gamma}^{0}P(\eta,\eta)+\dot{\gamma}^{\overline{1}}P(\zeta,\eta)+\dot{\gamma}^{1}P\bigl(\overline{\zeta},\eta\bigr)\bigr)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle{-}2\dot{\gamma}^{0}\bigl|\dot{\gamma}^{2}\bigr|^{2}-{\rm i}\dot{\gamma}^{\overline{1}}\ddot{\gamma}^{2}+\dot{\gamma}^{\overline{1}}\dot{\gamma}^{2}\dot{\gamma}^{3}+{\rm i}\dot{\gamma}^{1}\ddot{\gamma}^{\overline{2}}+\dot{\gamma}^{1}\dot{\gamma}^{\overline{2}}\dot{\gamma}^{3}

\displaystyle{}{-}\bigl(\bigl(\dot{\gamma}^{0}\bigr)^{2}+2\bigl|\dot{\gamma}^{1}\bigr|^{2}\bigr)\bigl(\dot{\gamma}^{0}P(\eta,\eta)+\dot{\gamma}^{\overline{1}}P(\zeta,\eta)+\dot{\gamma}^{1}P\bigl(\overline{\zeta},\eta\bigr)\bigr).

By the nullity and the equation (
4.6
), we have

\displaystyle\dot{\gamma}^{1}\dot{\gamma}^{\overline{2}}\dot{\gamma}^{3}+\dot{\gamma}^{\overline{1}}\dot{\gamma}^{2}\dot{\gamma}^{3}=-\dot{\gamma}^{0}\bigl(\dot{\gamma}^{3}\bigr)^{2}

and

\displaystyle-{\rm i}\dot{\gamma}^{\overline{1}}\ddot{\gamma}^{2}+{\rm i}\dot{\gamma}^{1}\ddot{\gamma}^{\overline{2}}={}

\displaystyle{-}P(\zeta,\eta)\bigl(\dot{\gamma}^{0}\bigr)^{2}\dot{\gamma}^{\overline{1}}-P\bigl(\overline{\zeta},\eta\bigr)\bigl(\dot{\gamma}^{0}\bigr)^{2}\dot{\gamma}^{1}-2\bigl(P\bigl(\zeta,\overline{\zeta}\bigr)-P(\eta,\eta)\bigr)\dot{\gamma}^{0}\bigl|\dot{\gamma}^{1}\bigr|^{2}

\displaystyle{}{-}\,P(\zeta,\zeta)\dot{\gamma}^{0}\bigl(\dot{\gamma}^{\overline{1}}\bigr)^{2}\!-P\bigl(\overline{\zeta},\overline{\zeta}\bigr)\dot{\gamma}^{0}\bigl(\dot{\gamma}^{1}\bigr)^{2}+2P\bigl(\overline{\zeta},\eta\bigr)\dot{\gamma}^{1}\bigl|\dot{\gamma}^{1}\bigr|^{2}+2P(\zeta,\eta)\dot{\gamma}^{\overline{1}}\bigl|\dot{\gamma}^{1}\bigr|^{2}.

Substituting these expressions, we obtain

\displaystyle g\bigl(\nabla_{\dot{x}}\nabla_{\dot{x}}\dot{x}-|\dot{x}|^{2}P(\dot{x}),\eta\bigr)

\displaystyle=-2\dot{\gamma}^{0}\bigl|\dot{\gamma}^{2}\bigr|^{2}-\dot{\gamma}^{0}\bigl(\dot{\gamma}^{3}\bigr)^{2}-\dot{\gamma}^{0}P(\dot{x},\dot{x})

\displaystyle=-\bigl(2\bigl|\dot{\gamma}^{2}\bigr|^{2}+\bigl(\dot{\gamma}^{3}\bigr)^{2}+P(\dot{x},\dot{x})\bigr)g(\dot{x},\eta).

Thus,
x(t)
satisfies (
4.10
). In particular, it satisfies the conformal geodesic equation (
3.4
) for curves with constant speed.
∎

By definition, a (null) chain
(x(t),[\zeta(t)])
is the projection of a non-vertical null geodesic
\gamma(t)=(x(t),\zeta(t))
on
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
to
M
. If the constants
\dot{\gamma}^{0}=g(\eta,\dot{x})
,

\dot{\gamma}^{1}=g(\zeta,\dot{x})
are both equal to 0, then we have
\dot{x}=0
and the projection
x(t)
is a constant curve on
\Sigma
. Otherwise, it is a regular curve and hence a conformal geodesic on
\Sigma
by the above theorem. Thus we obtain Theorem
1.1
(1).

As an application, we have the following.

Theorem 4.11

(cf.
[
25
]
)
.

Let
M_{i}
,
i=1,2
be the twistor CR manifolds over conformal
3
-manifolds
(\Sigma_{i},[g_{i}])
,
i=1,2
. If
\widehat{f}\colon M_{1}\to M_{2}
is a bundle isomorphism which is a CR equivalent map, then the underlying map

is a conformal isomorphism.

Proof.

Since
\widehat{f}
maps any (null) chain on

to a (null) chain on

,

maps conformal geodesics on

to conformal geodesics on

. Hence, by

∎

4.7
Canonical lift of conformal geodesics

Theorem
1.1
(2) asserts that we can lift a conformal geodesic
x(t)
to a unique (null) chain on
M
once we choose a lift of the endpoint
x(a)
; we prove this in Section
5.1
below.
Here, as a special case, we present a canonical lift of a conformal geodesic to a chain.

Given a regular curve
x(t)
on
\Sigma
, we take a complex vector field
\zeta(t)
along
x(t)
such that
(\sqrt{2}\operatorname{Re}\zeta,\sqrt{2}\operatorname{Im}\zeta)
gives an oriented orthonormal basis of
\dot{x}^{\perp}
with respect to a metric
{g\in[g]}
. Such a
\zeta(t)
is determined by the conformal structure
[g]
up to
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action, so we have a canonical lift

\tilde{x}(t):=(x(t),[\zeta(t)])

on
M
. Note that
\tilde{x}(t)
is transverse to the contact distribution since

\theta(\dot{\tilde{x}})=|\dot{x}|^{2}\neq 0
.

Theorem 4.12
.

The curve
\tilde{x}(t)
is a chain if and only if
x(t)
is a conformal geodesic. Moreover, for a conformal geodesic
x(t)
,
a\leq t\leq b
, the curve
\tilde{x}(t)
is a unique chain through
(x(a),[\zeta(a)])
which projects to
x(t)
.

Proof.

If
\tilde{x}(t)
is a chain, then
x(t)
is a conformal geodesic by Theorem
1.1
(1). Conversely, suppose
x(t)
is a conformal geodesic. By the characterization of conformal geodesics given by Proposition
3.2
, we may take a representative metric
g\in[g]
and a parametrization
t
such that
\nabla_{\dot{x}}\dot{x}=P(\dot{x})=0
and
|\dot{x}|=1
. Since
\dot{x}
is parallel, we can take

\zeta(t)
as a parallel vector field along
x(t)
.
Then,
\gamma(t):=(x(t),\zeta(t))
becomes a curve on
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
with

\eta=\dot{x},\qquad\dot{\gamma}^{0}=1,\qquad\dot{\gamma}^{1}=\dot{\gamma}^{2}=\dot{\gamma}^{3}=0.

In particular, it is a null curve. Noting that
P(\eta)=0
, we see that
\gamma(t)
satisfies the geodesic equations (
4.6
) and (
4.7
), and hence
\tilde{x}(t)=(x(t),[\zeta(t)])
is a chain on
M
.

Next, we prove the uniqueness. Suppose that
c(t)
is a chain through
(x(a),[\zeta(a)])
which projects to a conformal geodesic
x(t)
, and let
\gamma_{c}(t)=(x(t),\zeta_{c}(t))
be a null geodesic on
{\bigl(\widehat{\mathcal{C}},g^{\mathrm{F}}\bigr)}
, the Fefferman space determined by
g
above, which projects to
c(t)
. Then,
\dot{\gamma}_{c}^{0}=g(\eta_{c},\dot{x})
and
\dot{\gamma}_{c}^{1}=g(\zeta_{c},\dot{x})
are constant by the geodesic equation. Since
\dot{\gamma}_{c}^{0}(a)=|\dot{x}(a)|^{2}=1
,
\dot{\gamma}_{c}^{1}(a)=0
, we obtain that
\eta_{c}(t)=\dot{x}(t)
for any
t
and hence
c(t)=\tilde{x}(t)
.
∎

5
A variational principle for conformal geodesics

in dimension three

5.1
Relations to the unit tangent sphere bundle and the frame bundle

We will give an alternative description of the previous constructions in terms of the frame bundle over
\Sigma
.
We fix a representative metric
g\in[g]
and let

\mathcal{P}
be the
\mathrm{SO}(3)
-bundle of oriented orthonormal frames over
(\Sigma,g)
. Then, the map

f\colon\ \widehat{\mathcal{C}}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{P},\qquad\zeta\longmapsto B=\bigl(\sqrt{2}\operatorname{Re}\zeta,\sqrt{2}\operatorname{Im}\zeta,\eta\bigr)

(5.1)

gives a bundle isomorphism. The action of
\mathrm{U}(1)
on
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
corresponds to rotations of the first two basis vectors in
B
. Thus, the twistor CR manifold
M=\widehat{\mathcal{C}}/\mathrm{U}(1)
is isomorphic to the unit tangent sphere bundle of
(\Sigma,g)
by the following map:

M\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}S\Sigma,\qquad[\zeta]\longmapsto\eta.

(5.2)

Let

\phi=\begin{pmatrix}\phi^{1}\\
\phi^{2}\\
\phi^{3}\end{pmatrix}\colon\ T\mathcal{P}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3},\qquad\omega=\begin{pmatrix}\hphantom{-}0&\hphantom{-}\omega^{3}&-\omega^{2}\\
-\omega^{3}&\hphantom{-}0&\hphantom{-}\omega^{1}\\
\hphantom{-}\omega^{2}&-\omega^{1}&\hphantom{-}0\end{pmatrix}\colon\ T\mathcal{P}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{so}(3)

be the canonical
1
-form and the Levi-Civita connection form of
g
.
These forms are related to the adapted coframe
\bigl\{\theta,\tilde{\theta}^{1},\tilde{\theta}^{2},\tilde{\theta}^{\overline{1}},\tilde{\theta}^{\overline{2}},\tilde{\theta}^{3}\bigr\}
on
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
as follows.

Lemma 5.1
.

We have

f^{*}\phi=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{2}\operatorname{Re}\tilde{\theta}^{1}\\
\sqrt{2}\operatorname{Im}\tilde{\theta}^{1}\\
\theta\end{pmatrix},\qquad f^{*}\omega=\begin{pmatrix}0&\tilde{\theta}^{3}&-\sqrt{2}\operatorname{Im}\tilde{\theta}^{2}\\
-\tilde{\theta}^{3}&0&\hphantom{-}\sqrt{2}\operatorname{Re}\tilde{\theta}^{2}\\
\sqrt{2}\operatorname{Im}\tilde{\theta}^{2}&-\sqrt{2}\operatorname{Re}\tilde{\theta}^{2}&0\end{pmatrix}.

Proof.

We fix a point
x_{0}\in\Sigma
and take normal coordinates
(x^{i})
of
g
around

x_{0}
. Then, at any point
\zeta\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{x_{0}}
, we have

f^{*}\phi={}^{t}\!B\,{\rm d}x=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{2}\operatorname{Re}{}^{t}\zeta\\
\sqrt{2}\operatorname{Im}{}^{t}\zeta\\
{}^{t}\eta\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}{\rm d}x^{1}\\
{\rm d}x^{2}\\
{\rm d}x^{3}\end{pmatrix}

and

f^{*}\omega={}^{t}\!B\,{\rm d}B=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{2}\operatorname{Re}{}^{t}\zeta\\
\sqrt{2}\operatorname{Im}{}^{t}\zeta\\
{}^{t}\eta\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{2}\operatorname{Re}{\rm d}\zeta&\sqrt{2}\operatorname{Im}{\rm d}\zeta&{\rm d}\eta\end{pmatrix},

where we write
\zeta
,
\eta
as column vectors. Thus we obtain the desired formulas.
∎

We define the Fefferman metric
G^{\mathrm{F}}
on
\mathcal{P}
via the isomorphism (
5.1
):
g^{\mathrm{F}}=f^{*}G^{\mathrm{F}}
. Then, the above lemma and Theorem
4.5
prove the following.

Proposition 5.2
.

The Fefferman metric
G^{\mathrm{F}}
on
\mathcal{P}
is given by

G^{\mathrm{F}}=2\bigl(\phi^{1}\cdot\omega^{1}+\phi^{2}\cdot\omega^{2}+\phi^{3}\cdot\omega^{3}\bigr).

Remark 5.3
.

(i)

If we denote by
R_{A}
the right action of
A\in\mathrm{SO}(3)
on
\mathcal{P}
, we have

R_{A}^{*}\begin{pmatrix}\phi^{1}\\
\phi^{2}\\
\phi^{3}\end{pmatrix}=A^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}\phi^{1}\\
\phi^{2}\\
\phi^{3}\end{pmatrix},\qquad R_{A}^{*}\begin{pmatrix}\omega^{1}\\
\omega^{2}\\
\omega^{3}\end{pmatrix}=A^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}\omega^{1}\\
\omega^{2}\\
\omega^{3}\end{pmatrix}.

Hence,
G^{\mathrm{F}}
is a right
\mathrm{SO}(3)
-invariant metric:
R_{A}^{*}G^{\mathrm{F}}=G^{\mathrm{F}}
.

(ii)

Since the conformal class of the Fefferman metric is invariantly defined from the CR structure, the conformal manifold
\bigl(\mathcal{P},\bigl[G^{\mathrm{F}}\bigr]\bigr)
should also be canonically associated to
(\Sigma,[g])
. This can be checked directly as follows.
Let
\widehat{g}={\rm e}^{2\Upsilon}g
be another representative metric and
\widehat{\mathcal{P}}
be the oriented orthonormal frame bundle for
\widehat{g}
.
Then, we have the canonical bundle isomorphism

\delta\colon\ \mathcal{P}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\widehat{\mathcal{P}},\qquad B\longmapsto{\rm e}^{-\Upsilon}B.

We denote by

\widehat{\phi}
,
\widehat{\omega}
the canonical 1-form and the connection form of
\widehat{g}
on
\widehat{\mathcal{P}}
. Then, we have
\delta^{*}\widehat{\phi}={\rm e}^{\Upsilon}\phi
and
the conformal transformation formula for the Levi-Civita connection gives

\displaystyle\delta^{*}\widehat{\omega}^{1}=\omega^{1}+\Upsilon^{3}\phi^{2}-\Upsilon^{2}\phi^{3},

\displaystyle\delta^{*}\widehat{\omega}^{2}=\omega^{2}+\Upsilon^{1}\phi^{3}-\Upsilon^{3}\phi^{1},

\displaystyle\delta^{*}\widehat{\omega}^{3}=\omega^{3}+\Upsilon^{2}\phi^{1}-\Upsilon^{1}\phi^{2},

where we set
\Upsilon^{i}:=\phi^{i}(\mathrm{grad}_{g}\Upsilon)\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{P})
, which is well-defined since
\phi
is horizontal.
Thus, the Fefferman metric
\widehat{G}^{\mathrm{F}}
on
\widehat{\mathcal{P}}
satisfies

\delta^{*}\widehat{G}^{\mathrm{F}}={\rm e}^{\Upsilon}G^{\mathrm{F}}.

(iii)

The conformal metric
G^{\mathrm{F}}
on
\mathcal{P}
is also considered by Holland
[
16
]
, and it is proved in
[
16
, Section 3.5.4]
that null geodesics of this metric project to conformal geodesics on
\Sigma
. This gives another proof to Theorem
4.10
.

In Section
4.7
, we defined a canonical lift of a regular curve
x(t)
on
\Sigma
to a curve
\tilde{x}(t)=(x(t),[\zeta(t)])
on
M
which becomes a chain if and only if

is a conformal geodesic. This lift corresponds to the curve
(x(t),\dot{x}(t)/|\dot{x}(t)|)
on
S\Sigma
via the isomorphism (

). Thus, if we regard

as the twistor CR manifold,
Theorem
4.12
yields the following.

Theorem 5.4
.

For a regular curve
x(t)
on
(\Sigma,g)
, the curve
\tilde{x}(t)=(x(t),\dot{x}(t)/|\dot{x}(t)|)
on

is a chain if and only if

is a conformal geodesic of
[g]
. Moreover, for a conformal geodesic
x(t)
,
a\leq t\leq b
, the curve

is a unique chain through

.

We can compete the proof of Theorem
1.1
(2) by making use of the right

.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
(2).

Let
x(t)
,
a\leq t\leq b
, be a conformal geodesic on
\Sigma
and
\eta_{0}\in S_{x(a)}\Sigma

on

(

∎

5.2
The Kropina metric for chains on
\boldsymbol{S\Sigma}
and a variational

characterization of conformal geodesics

Hereafter, we identify the twistor CR manifold
M
with the unit tangent sphere bundle
S\Sigma
for
g\in[g]
, and consider the Fefferman space
\bigl(\mathcal{P},G^{\mathrm{F}}\bigr)
instead of
\bigl(\widehat{\mathcal{C}},g^{\mathrm{F}}\bigr)
. We also write
K
for the infinitesimal generator of the
S^{1}
-action on
\mathcal{P}
.

To derive a variational principle for conformal geodesics, we first recall from
[
9
]
a characterization of CR chains as geodesics of a Kropina metric.

Definition 5.5
.

Let
s\colon U\to\mathcal{P}
be a local section defined on an open subset
U\subset S\Sigma
. The Finsler metric

F(\tilde{x},\xi):=\frac{G^{\mathrm{F}}(s_{*}\xi,s_{*}\xi)}{G^{\mathrm{F}}(K,s_{*}\xi)},\qquad\tilde{x}\in U,\quad\xi\in T_{\tilde{x}}S\Sigma\setminus\operatorname{Ker}\theta

is called the
Kropina metric
on
U
associated with
s
.

We note that the condition
\xi\notin\operatorname{Ker}\theta
is equivalent to
G^{\mathrm{F}}(K,s_{*}\xi)\neq 0
, and
F
is not defined on the contact distribution.

Theorem 5.6

(
[
9
]
)
.

A curve
\tilde{x}(t)
on
U\subset S\Sigma
which is transverse to the contact distribution is a chain if and only if it is an
(
unparametrized
)
geodesic of
F
.

For a regular curve
x(t)
,
a\leq t\leq b
, on
\Sigma
, we consider the Kropina length of the lift

\tilde{x}(t):=(x(t),\dot{x}(t)/|\dot{x}(t)|)
associated with a section
s\colon U\to\mathcal{P}
on a neighborhood
U\subset S\Sigma
of the curve
\tilde{x}(t)
, and define the functional

\mathscr{L}[x(t)]:=\int_{a}^{b}F\bigl(\tilde{x}(t),\dot{\tilde{x}}(t)\bigr)\,{\rm d}t.

Noting that

\phi(s_{*}\dot{\tilde{x}})=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
0\\
|\dot{x}|\end{pmatrix},\qquad\phi(K)=0,\qquad\omega(K)=Y:=\begin{pmatrix}\hphantom{-}0&1&0\\
-1&0&0\\
\hphantom{-}0&0&0\end{pmatrix},

we have

F\bigl(\tilde{x}(t),\dot{\tilde{x}}(t)\bigr)=2\omega^{3}\bigl(s_{*}\dot{\tilde{x}}\bigr).

If
x(t)
is a conformal geodesic,
\tilde{x}(t)
is a chain by Theorem
5.4
, and hence
x(t)
is a critical curve of
\mathscr{L}
by Theorem
5.6
. To prove the converse, we will show that the variation of the Kropina length in the vertical directions always vanishes.

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
\gamma(t)=(x(t),B(t))
,
a\leq t\leq b
, be a curve on
\mathcal{P}
which projects to
\tilde{x}(t)=(x(t),\dot{x}(t)/|\dot{x}(t)|)
. Let
\gamma_{\epsilon}(t)
be a variation of
\gamma(t)
fixing the endpoints such that the variation vector field
(\partial/\partial\epsilon)|_{\epsilon=0}\gamma_{\epsilon}(t)
is vertical with respect to the fiberation
\mathcal{P}\to\Sigma
. Then, we have

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\epsilon}\Big|_{\epsilon=0}\int_{a}^{b}\frac{G^{\mathrm{F}}(\dot{\gamma}_{\epsilon}(t),\dot{\gamma}_{\epsilon}(t))}{G^{\mathrm{F}}(K,\dot{\gamma}_{\epsilon}(t))}\,{\rm d}t=0.

Proof.

By the assumption on
\gamma(t)
, we have

\phi(\dot{\gamma}(t))=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
0\\
|\dot{x}|\end{pmatrix}.

(5.3)

We may assume that the variation is of the form

\gamma_{\epsilon}(t)=R_{{\rm e}^{\epsilon X(t)}}\gamma(t)=\gamma(t)\cdot{\rm e}^{\epsilon X(t)},

where

X(t)=\begin{pmatrix}\hphantom{-}0&\hphantom{-}X^{3}(t)&-X^{2}(t)\\
-X^{3}(t)&\hphantom{-}0&\hphantom{-}X^{1}(t)\\
\hphantom{-}X^{2}(t)&-X^{1}(t)&\hphantom{-}0\end{pmatrix}

is an
\mathfrak{so}(3)
-valued function with
X(a)=X(b)=O
.

For any
X\in\mathfrak{so}(3)
, we denote by
X^{\dagger}
the vertical vector field on
\mathcal{P}
generated by
X
:

X^{\dagger}_{u}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\biggl|_{s=0}R_{{\rm e}^{sX}}u,\qquad u\in\mathcal{P}.

Then, we have

(R_{A})_{*}X^{\dagger}=\bigl({\rm Ad}\bigl(A^{-1}\bigr)X\bigr)^{\dagger}

for any
A\in{\rm SO}(3)
and
X\in\mathfrak{so}(3)
.

The velocity of the curve
\gamma_{\epsilon}(t)
is given by

\dot{\gamma}_{\epsilon}(t)=(R_{{\rm e}^{\epsilon X(t)}})_{*}\dot{\gamma}(t)+\epsilon\dot{X}(t)^{\dagger}.

Then, by using the right-invariance of
G^{\rm F}
and the fact that the fibers of
\mathcal{P}
are totally null, we compute as

\displaystyle G^{\rm F}(\dot{\gamma}_{\epsilon},\dot{\gamma}_{\epsilon})={}

\displaystyle G^{\rm F}((R_{{\rm e}^{\epsilon X}})_{*}\dot{\gamma},(R_{{\rm e}^{\epsilon X}})_{*}\dot{\gamma})2\epsilon G^{\rm F}\bigl((R_{{\rm e}^{\epsilon X}})_{*}\dot{\gamma},\dot{X}^{\dagger}\bigr)+\epsilon^{2}G^{\rm F}\bigl(\dot{X}^{\dagger},\dot{X}^{\dagger}\bigr)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle G^{\rm F}(\dot{\gamma},\dot{\gamma})+2\epsilon G^{\rm F}\bigl(\dot{\gamma},\bigl({\rm Ad}\bigl({\rm e}^{\epsilon X}\bigr)\dot{X}\bigr)^{\dagger}\bigr).

Hence, using (
5.3
), we have

\frac{\partial}{\partial\epsilon}\bigg|_{\epsilon=0}G^{\rm F}(\dot{\gamma}_{\epsilon},\dot{\gamma}_{\epsilon})=2G^{\rm F}\bigl(\dot{\gamma},\dot{X}^{\dagger}\bigr)=2|\dot{x}|\omega^{3}\bigl(\dot{X}^{\dagger}\bigr)=2|\dot{x}|\dot{X}^{3}.

On the other hand, since
K=Y^{\dagger}
the denominator of the integrand is computed as

\displaystyle G^{\rm F}(K,\dot{\gamma}_{\epsilon})=G^{\rm F}\bigl(Y^{\dagger},(R_{{\rm e}^{\epsilon X}})_{*}\dot{\gamma}+\epsilon\dot{X}^{\dagger}\bigr)=G^{\rm F}\bigl(\bigl({\rm Ad}\bigl({\rm e}^{\epsilon X}\bigr)Y\bigr)^{\dagger},\dot{\gamma}\bigr)=\omega^{3}\bigl(\bigl({\rm Ad}\bigl({\rm e}^{\epsilon X}\bigr)Y\bigr)^{\dagger}\bigr),

and hence

\frac{\partial}{\partial\epsilon}\bigg|_{\epsilon=0}G^{\rm F}(K,\dot{\gamma}_{\epsilon})=\omega^{3}\bigl([X,Y]^{\dagger}\bigr)=\omega^{3}\left(\begin{pmatrix}0&0&-X^{1}\\
0&0&-X^{2}\\
X^{1}&X^{2}&\hphantom{-}0\end{pmatrix}^{\dagger}\right)=0.

Thus, noting that
G^{F}(K,\dot{\gamma})=|\dot{x}|\omega^{3}\bigl(Y^{\dagger}\bigr)=|\dot{x}|
, we have

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\epsilon}\bigg|_{\epsilon=0}\int_{a}^{b}\frac{G^{\rm F}(\dot{\gamma}_{\epsilon}(t),\dot{\gamma}_{\epsilon}(t))}{G^{\rm F}(K,\dot{\gamma}_{\epsilon}(t))}\,{\rm d}t=2\int_{a}^{b}\dot{X}^{3}(t)\,{\rm d}t=2\bigl(X^{3}(b)-X^{3}(a)\bigr)=0.

Using this proposition, we prove the following variational characterization of conformal geodesics.

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
x(t)
,
a\leq t\leq b
, be a regular curve on
(\Sigma,g)
and
\tilde{x}(t)=(x(t),\dot{x}(t)/|\dot{x}(t)|)
its lift to
S\Sigma
. Let
F
be the Kropina metric on
S\Sigma
associated with a local section
s
of
\mathcal{P}\to S\Sigma
defined in a neighborhood of
\tilde{x}(t)
. Then,
x(t)
is a conformal geodesic on
(\Sigma,g)
if and only if it is a critical curve of the functional

\mathscr{L}[x(t)]=\int_{a}^{b}F\bigl(\tilde{x}(t),\dot{\tilde{x}}(t)\bigr)\,{\rm d}t\biggl(=2\int_{a}^{b}\omega^{3}\bigl(s_{*}\dot{\tilde{x}}\bigr)\,{\rm d}t\biggr)

(5.4)

under the variations fixing
\tilde{x}(a)
and
\tilde{x}(b)
.

Proof.

If
x(t)
is a conformal geodesic,
\tilde{x}(t)
is a chain on
S\Sigma
by
Theorem
5.4
. Thus,
\tilde{x}(t)
is a critical curve for the Kropina length and hence
x(t)
is a critical curve of
\mathscr{L}
for the variations fixing
\tilde{x}(a)
and
\tilde{x}(b)
.

Conversely, suppose
x(t)
is a critical curve of
\mathscr{L}
. We will show that the Kropina length functional
L
is stationary under variations of
\tilde{x}(t)
. Let
y_{\epsilon}(t)
be an arbitrary variation of
\tilde{x}(t)
and let
V(t)=(\partial/\partial\epsilon)|_{\epsilon=0}y_{\epsilon}(t)
be the variation vector field. We take a variation
x_{\epsilon}(t)
of
x(t)
which has the variation vector field
\pi_{*}V(t)
, where
\pi\colon S\Sigma\to\Sigma
is the projection. Then, we can write as

V(t)=V_{1}(t)+V_{2}(t),

where
V_{1}(t):=(\partial/\partial\epsilon)|_{\epsilon=0}\tilde{x}_{\epsilon}(t)
is the variation vector field of the lift
\tilde{x}_{\epsilon}(t):=(x_{\epsilon}(t),\dot{x}_{\epsilon}(t)/|\dot{x}_{\epsilon}(t)|)
and
V_{2}(t)\in\operatorname{Ker}\pi_{*}
is a vertical vector field. By the assumption, we have
L^{\prime}(V_{1})=\mathscr{L}^{\prime}(\pi_{*}V)=0
. Since we also have
L^{\prime}(V_{2})=0
by Proposition
5.7
, we obtain
L^{\prime}(V)=0
.
∎

5.3
The total torsion functional

We will discuss the relation between the functional
\mathscr{L}
and the total torsion functional, which is defined as follows.

Let
x(t)
,
a\leq t\leq b
, be a regular curve on
(\Sigma,g)
. We fix oriented orthonormal basis
A=(e_{1},e_{2},\dot{x}(a)/|\dot{x}(a)|)\in\mathcal{P}_{x(a)}
and

B=\bigl(e^{\prime}_{1},e^{\prime}_{2},\dot{x}(b)/|\dot{x}(b)|\bigr)\in\mathcal{P}_{x(b)}
at the endpoints.
Then, we have the associated orientation preserving orthogonal map

h\colon\ \dot{x}(a)^{\perp}\longrightarrow\dot{x}(b)^{\perp},

satisfying
h(e_{i})=e^{\prime}_{i}
. This is called the
monodromy map
in
[
10
]
. We also write

P\colon\ \dot{x}(a)^{\perp}\longrightarrow\dot{x}(b)^{\perp}

for the parallel transport along
x(t)
with respect to the normal connection
\nabla^{\perp}
. Then, the
total torsion
(
functional
)
is defined by

\mathscr{T}[x(t)]:=h^{-1}\circ P\in\mathrm{SO}\bigl(\dot{x}(a)^{\perp}\bigr)\cong\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}.

Note that the definition of
\mathscr{T}
depends on the choice of orthonormal basis
A
,
B
at the endpoints.

Proposition 5.9

(
[
10
, Lemma 1]
)
.

Let
T(t):=\dot{x}(t)/|\dot{x}(t)|
be the unit velocity vector field, and
let
\nu(t)\in\dot{x}(t)^{\perp}
be a unit normal vector field such that
h(\nu(a))=\nu(b)
. Then, we have

\mathscr{T}[x(t)]\equiv-\int_{a}^{b}g(\nabla_{\dot{x}}\nu,T\times\nu)\,{\rm d}t\mod 2\pi\mathbb{Z}.

(5.5)

Proof.

Let
\xi(t)
be a unit normal vector field along
x(t)
obtained by the parallel transport of
\nu(a)
with respect to
\nabla^{\perp}
. Using the
\mathrm{U}(1)
-action on
\dot{x}(t)^{\perp}
, we can write as

\xi(t)={\rm e}^{{\rm i}\alpha(t)}\cdot\nu(t)

with a real function
\alpha(t)
satisfying
\alpha(a)=0
. Then, we have
\mathscr{T}[x(t)]\equiv\alpha(b)
mod
2\pi\mathbb{Z}
and

g(\nabla_{\dot{x}}\nu,T\times\nu)=g\bigl(\nabla^{\perp}_{\dot{x}}\nu,T\times\nu\bigr)=-\dot{\alpha}.

Hence, we obtain (
5.5
).
∎

Remark 5.10
.

We added the assumption
h(\nu(a))=\nu(b)
, which is missed in
[
10
, Lemma 1]
. We also modified the formula by putting the negative sign.

The formula (
5.5
) implies that the functional
\mathscr{T}
is conformally invariant.

Proposition 5.11
.

Let
\mathscr{T}[x(t)]
be the total torsion associated with orthonormal basis
A\in\mathcal{P}_{x(a)}
and
B\in\mathcal{P}_{x(b)}
. Let
\widehat{g}={\rm e}^{2\Upsilon}g
be a rescaled metric and let
\widehat{\mathscr{T}}[x(t)]
be the total torsion for
\widehat{g}
associated with the orthonormal basis
{\rm e}^{-\Upsilon}A
and
{\rm e}^{-\Upsilon}B
. Then, we have

\mathscr{T}[x(t)]\equiv\widehat{\mathscr{T}}[x(t)]\mod 2\pi\mathbb{Z}.

Proof.

The unit velocity vector field of
x(t)
with respect to
\widehat{g}
is given by
\widehat{T}:={\rm e}^{-\Upsilon}T
, and we can use
\widehat{\nu}:={\rm e}^{-\Upsilon}\nu
as a unit normal vector field. Then, we have
\widehat{T}\,\widehat{\times}\,\widehat{\nu}={\rm e}^{-\Upsilon}T\times\nu
, where
\widehat{\times}
denotes the cross product with respect to
\widehat{g}
. Moreover, the conformal transformation formula of the Levi-Civita connection gives

\displaystyle\widehat{\nabla}_{\dot{x}}\widehat{\nu}={\rm e}^{-\Upsilon}(-(\partial_{t}\Upsilon)\nu+\nabla_{\dot{x}}\nu+(\partial_{t}\Upsilon)\nu+(\nu\cdot\Upsilon)\dot{x})={\rm e}^{-\Upsilon}(\nabla_{\dot{x}}\nu+(\nu\cdot\Upsilon)\dot{x}).

Hence, we obtain

\widehat{g}\bigl(\widehat{\nabla}_{\dot{x}}\widehat{\nu},\widehat{T}\,\widehat{\times}\,\widehat{\nu}\bigr)=g\bigl(\nabla_{\dot{x}}\nu,T\times\nu\bigr).

The functionals
\mathscr{L}
and
\mathscr{T}
are related as follows.

Proposition 5.12
.

Let
\mathscr{L}
be the functional given by (
5.4
). Let
\mathscr{T}
be the total torsion functional associated to the orthonormal basis
s(\tilde{x}(a))
,
s(\tilde{x}(b))
at the endpoints. Then, we have

\mathscr{L}[x(t)]\equiv 2\mathscr{T}[x(t)]\mod 2\pi\mathbb{Z}.

Proof.

If we write
s(\tilde{x}(t))=(\nu(t),\nu^{\prime}(t),T(t))
, then
\nu(t)
is a unit normal vector field satisfying
h(\nu(a))=\nu(b)
and we have

-g(\nabla_{\dot{x}}\nu,T\times\nu)=-g\bigl(\nabla_{\dot{x}}\nu,\nu^{\prime}\bigr)=\omega^{3}\bigl(s_{*}\dot{\tilde{x}}(t)\bigr).

Hence we obtain the equation by (
5.4
) and (
5.5
).
∎

It follows from this proposition and Theorem
5.8
that a conformal geodesic
x(t)
,
a\leq x\leq b
, is characterized as a critical curve for the total torsion functional with fixed
A
,
B
under the variations fixing
\tilde{x}(a)
,
\tilde{x}(b)
.

When the curve
x(t)
is parametrized by an arclength parameter and has non-vanishing geodesic curvature (
\nabla_{\dot{x}}\dot{x}\neq 0
), we can use

\nu=\frac{\nabla_{\dot{x}}\dot{x}}{|\nabla_{\dot{x}}\dot{x}|}

as a unit normal vector field. In this case, the function

\tau(t):=g(\nabla_{\dot{x}}\nu,T\times\nu)

is called the
torsion (function)
of
x(t)
. It is well-known that the torsion is represented as

\tau(t)=\frac{\det(\dot{x},\nabla_{\dot{x}}\dot{x},\nabla_{\dot{x}}\nabla_{\dot{x}}\dot{x})}{|\dot{x}\times\nabla_{\dot{x}}\dot{x}|^{2}}

for an arbitrary parametrization. It may not hold that
h(\nu(a))=\nu(b)
for
A=s(\tilde{x}(a))
,
{B=s(\tilde{x}(b))}
, but we can change
B
so that it holds, and this only causes a difference in
\mathscr{T}
by a constant
c\in\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}
. Hence, we have

\mathscr{T}[x(t)]\equiv-\int_{a}^{b}\frac{\det(\dot{x},\nabla_{\dot{x}}\dot{x},\nabla_{\dot{x}}\nabla_{\dot{x}}\dot{x})}{|\dot{x}\times\nabla_{\dot{x}}\dot{x}|^{2}}|\dot{x}|\,{\rm d}t+c\mod 2\pi\mathbb{Z}

for an arbitrary parametrization. Thus we complete the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem 5.13

(
[
10
, Theorem 2]
)
.

Let
x(t)
,
a\leq t\leq b
, be a regular curve on
(\Sigma,g)
. Assume
x_{\epsilon}(t)

is a variation of
x(t)
which fixes
(x(a),\dot{x}(a)/|\dot{x}(a)|)
and
(x(b),\dot{x}(b)/|\dot{x}(b)|)

V(t)=(\partial/\partial\epsilon)|_{\epsilon=0}x_{\epsilon}(t)
its variation vector field. If we define the total torsion
\mathscr{T}[x_{\epsilon}(t)]
by using common orthonormal basis at the endpoints, we have

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\epsilon}\bigg|_{\epsilon=0}\mathscr{T}[x_{\epsilon}(t)]=\int_{a}^{b}g\bigl(T\times\nabla_{T}\nabla_{T}T+(*R)(T)T,V\bigr)|\dot{x}|\,{\rm d}t,

where
T=\dot{x}/|\dot{x}|
.

We obtain an alternative proof of Theorem
1.2
from this formula.
By (
3.1
),
we have

(*R)_{mkl}=\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{m}{}^{ij}R_{ijkl}=\frac{1}{2}\bigl(\varepsilon_{m}{}^{i}{}_{l}P_{ik}-\varepsilon_{ml}{}^{j}P_{jk}+\varepsilon_{mk}{}^{j}P_{jl}-\varepsilon_{m}{}^{i}{}_{k}P_{il}\bigr)

and hence

\displaystyle((*R)(T)T)^{k}=(*R)_{m}{}^{k}{}_{l}T^{m}T^{l}=\frac{1}{2}\bigl(\varepsilon_{m}{}^{k}{}_{j}P^{j}{}_{l}T^{m}T^{l}-\varepsilon_{mi}{}^{k}P^{i}{}_{l}T^{m}T^{l}\bigr)=-(T\times P(T))^{k}.

T\times\nabla_{T}\nabla_{T}T+(*R)(T)T=T\times(\nabla_{T}\nabla_{T}T-P(T)),

which implies that the variation of the total torsion vanishes if and only if

is a conformal geodesic.

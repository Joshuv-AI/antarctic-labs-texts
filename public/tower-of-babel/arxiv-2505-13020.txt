Characterizing geodesic deviations in a Topological Star spacetime: massive, charged, spinning and stringy-like objects
Donato Bini 1 , Giorgio Di Russo 2

Abstract
We study deviations from geodesic motions in a Topological Star spacetime for either massive, charged and spinning particles, elucidating different behaviours with the Schwarzschild spacetime. We also consider the deviations for the motion of electrically charged stringy probes in D = 5 D=5 , framing all cases within a unified picture.



I
Introduction

In the recent years the spacetime of a so-called Topological Star (TS), an exact solutions of the Einstein-Maxwell equations in five dimensions, has attracted the interest of many scientists, especially because of its remarkable difference with respect to the more familiar black hole (BH) case and, hence, has proven to be relevant to analyze the properties of a strong gravitational field from a different perspective with respect the BH one. For example, the spacetime of a TS admits
1+4

4

Moreover, TSs provide a fertile playground for studying the relevant physics of fuzzball-like objects

Much is still to be examined. For example, very recently a rotating generalization of TSs was found
[

, stimulating comparisons between this new solution and the Kerr geometry.
Our aim here is to study deviations from geodesic motion 1) of another geodesic which happens to be aligned with the first at a certain spacetime (initial) point; 2) of a family of particles endowed with a small mechanical spin; 3) of a magnetically charged particle; 4) of an electrically charged string. In all cases we aim at characterizing (both from a geometrical and a physical point of view) the dynamics of test bodies orbiting around a TS and to study the differences with respect to the Schwarzschild case.

Furthermore, since the TS spacetime has an additional structure (i.e., it is characterized by two parameters
r_{b}
and
r_{s}
, both with dimensions of a length) with respect to the Schwarzschild spacetime (parametrized instead only by the Schwarzschild radius,
r_{s}
) it is natural to
study the motion of particles endowed with an internal structure (the charge, the spin, etc.) in a TS background, in order to explore competing effects among different length scales. Indeed, deviations from geodesic motion will be
expressed in terms of all such parameters and the spacetime parameters as well, and
we will analyze here the corresponding interplay (in some smallness limit).
Finally, we also include in the discussion the case of stringy charged probes in
D=5
: In this particular case we will contrast with classical Schwarzschild behaviors (now concerning the center-of-mass of the string itself).

Bunches (families) of particles can be chosen according to the dependence of their world lines on certain parameters (e.g., energy, angular momentum, spin, charge, etc.). Here, we will often consider (as an example) a LISA-inspired configuration of three particles, initially placed at the vertices of an ideal triangle (equilateral, for example), and then evolving with time, so that the triangle itself changes its shape during the evolution.

II
Topological Star metric

The spacetime of a TS is a static and spherically symmetric manifold, and an electro-vacuum solution in
D=5
of the Einstein-Maxwell equations. It is
described by the metric

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-f_{s}(r)dt^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{f_{s}(r)f_{b}(r)}

(1)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle r^{2}(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2})+f_{b}(r)dy^{2}\,,

with

f_{s,b}(r)=1-\frac{r_{s,b}}{r},

(2)

in coordinates
(t,r,\theta,\phi,y)
adapted to the staticity (
t
), the azimuthal symmetry (
\phi
) and to the extra dimension (
y
).
Moreover, the coordinate
y
is periodic
y\sim y+2\pi R_{y}
, with
R_{y}=2r_{b}^{3/2}/(r_{b}-r_{s})^{1/2}
.
We will find it convenient to look at the metric (
1
) as the superposition of a 2-sphere (with radius
r
)

ds^{2}_{(\theta,\phi)}=r^{2}(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2})\,,

(3)

and a 3-metric

ds_{(t,r,y)}^{2}=-f_{s}(r)dt^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{f_{s}(r)f_{b}(r)}+f_{b}(r)dy^{2}\,,

(4)

such that
ds^{2}=ds_{(t,r,y)}^{2}+ds^{2}_{(\theta,\phi)}
.
Since the geometric properties of the 2-sphere are very well known, we summarize in Appendix
A
some geometrical information on
ds^{2}=ds_{(t,r,y)}^{2}
.
The metric source is an electromagnetic field, superposition of an electric 3-form field

F^{\rm(e)}=\frac{Q}{r^{2}}dr\wedge dt\wedge dy=dA^{\rm(e)}\,,

(5)

with

A^{\rm(e)}=-\frac{Q}{r}dt\wedge dy\,,

(6)

and a magnetic 2-form field

F^{\rm(m)}=P\sin\theta d\theta\wedge d\phi=dA^{\rm(m)}\,,

(7)

with

A^{\rm(m)}=-P\cos\theta d\phi\,,

(8)

and where

P^{2}+Q^{2}=\frac{3r_{b}r_{s}}{2\kappa_{5}^{2}}\,.

(9)

The corresponding electromagnetic energy-momentum tensor writes

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}=T_{\mu\nu}^{\rm(m)}+T_{\mu\nu}^{\rm(e)}\,,

(10)

where

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}^{\rm(m)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F^{\rm(m)}_{\mu\alpha}F^{\rm(m)}_{\nu}{}^{\beta}-\frac{1}{4}g_{\mu\nu}F^{\rm(m)}_{\alpha\beta}F^{\rm(m)}{}^{\alpha\beta}\,,

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}^{\rm(e)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left[F^{\rm(e)}_{\mu\alpha\beta}F^{\rm(e)}_{\nu}{}^{\alpha\beta}-\frac{1}{6}g_{\mu\nu}F^{\rm(e)}_{\alpha\beta\gamma}F^{\rm(e)}{}^{\alpha\beta\gamma}\right]\,.\qquad

(11)

Finally, the Einstein-Maxwell’s equations can be cast in the form

\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu}=\kappa_{5}^{2}\left(T_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{3}g_{\mu\nu}T\right)\,,

(12)

with
T=T_{\mu}{}^{\mu}
and
with

8\pi G_{4}=\kappa_{4}^{2}=\frac{\kappa_{5}^{2}}{2\pi R_{y}}=\frac{8\pi G_{5}}{2\pi R_{y}}=\frac{4G_{5}}{R_{y}}\,.

(13)

It is well known that the metric (
1
) is associated with two different regimes: 1) black string (BS):
r_{b}<r_{s}
, with an event horizon at
r=r_{s}
; 2) TS:
r_{s}<r_{b}
. Furthermore, stability against (metric) linear perturbations requires
r_{b}<r_{s}<2r_{b}

in the BS case and
r_{s}<r_{b}<2r_{s}
in the TS one.
Here, unless differently indicated, we will mainly work with the case
Q=0
. In Section
VI
we will, however, restore the general case
Q\not=0
.

After dimensional reduction to
D=4
(i.e., examined on the leaves
y=
constant of a natural
1+4
foliation), the solution shows a naked singularity
and has a mass

G_{4}M_{\rm TS}=\frac{r_{s}}{2}+\frac{r_{b}}{4}\,.

(14)

Hereafter we will set
G_{4}\equiv G\equiv G_{\rm N}=1=c
. For
r_{b}=0
, and
thus
r_{s}=2GM_{\rm TS}
, the resulting singular solution is
Schwarzschild BH
\times S_{1}
.
Eventually, we will use a Schwarzschild-inspired notation, i.e., denote

r_{s}=2M
,
r_{b}=\alpha r_{s}=2\alpha M
,
with
M
a common length scale, not to be confuse with
M_{\rm TS}
, the mass of the TS, unless for
r_{b}=0
. In fact,

M_{\rm TS}=\frac{r_{s}}{4}(\alpha+2)=\frac{M}{2}(\alpha+2)\,.

(15)

As stated above, for the TS spacetime the geodesics are well known
[
1
,
4
,
3
,
9
]
and we briefly resume only the results that we need in the following. The mass shell condition in Hamiltonian form reads

\mathcal{H}=g^{\mu\nu}P_{\mu}P_{\nu}=-m^{2}\,,

(16)

where
P_{\mu}
are the conjugate canonical momenta. Since the metric is independent on
t
and
\phi
, the associated momenta
P_{t}
and
P_{\phi}
are conserved along geodesics

P_{t}=-E\,,\quad P_{\phi}=L\,.

(17)

The solution of the Hamilton’s equations is obtained by separation between the radial and the angular dynamics with the introduction of a Carter-like constant
K

\displaystyle P_{r}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Q_{R}(r)=\frac{r^{2}E^{2}-f_{s}(r)(K^{2}+m^{2}r^{2})}{r^{2}f_{b}(r)f_{s}(r)^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle P_{\theta}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K^{2}-\frac{L^{2}}{\sin^{2}(\theta)}\,.

(18)

Because of the spherical symmetry, we can study the equatorial motion
\theta=\pi/2
without loss of generality, i.e., assuming
K=L
. Furthermore, we can rescale the energy and the angular momentum in units of the mass of the probe, see below.
The circular equatorial geodesics are defined by the conditions

Q_{R}(r_{0},E_{0},L_{0})=0=Q_{R}^{\prime}(r_{0},E_{0},L_{0})\,,

(19)

which can be solved as follows

\displaystyle\hat{E}_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{E_{0}}{m}=\frac{r_{0}-r_{s}}{r_{0}\sqrt{1-\frac{3r_{s}}{2r_{0}}}}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{L}_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{L_{0}}{m}=\frac{\sqrt{r_{0}}\sqrt{r_{s}}}{\sqrt{2}\sqrt{1-\frac{3r_{s}}{2r_{0}}}}\,.

(20)

Let us introduce the notation

f_{n}(r)=1-n\frac{r_{s}}{r}\,,\qquad f_{1}(r)\equiv f_{s}(r)\,,

(21)

largely used below. Here,
f_{n}(r)
has the advantage of being a dimensionless quantity, and, for example

\hat{E}_{0}=\frac{f_{s}(r_{0})}{\sqrt{f_{3/2}(r_{0})}}\,,\qquad\frac{\hat{L}_{0}}{r_{s}}=\frac{\sqrt{r_{0}}}{\sqrt{2r_{s}}\sqrt{f_{3/2}(r_{0})}}\,.

(22)

Very recently, the orbits of scalar charges accelerated by a “self force” have been analyzed too
[
10
,
12
]
.
Differently, the motion of extended bodies, charged particles or deformation effects due to tidal forces, has never been examined yet, and this is one of the goals of the present article.

We will start by studying geodesic deviations
1
1

1

Geodesic deviation is (in a sense) a mathematical formulation of what more generally is referred to as “tidal forces.” We recall that in the literature there exist variations on the definition of geodesic deviations, like “connecting vectors” or tensors, relativistic strains, etc.
[
24
,
25
,
26
,
27
,
28
]
, i.e.,
analyzing how nearby geodesics evolve relative to each other in presence of gravitational forces generated by the field of a TS.
As it is well known, in the absence of external forces (including gravity), the relative velocity of any two geodesics is constant, whereas oscillations around/deviations from a reference geodesic characterize the behavior in a Schwarzschild spacetime.
The TS spacetime is peculiar, since a cap replaces the horizon. While particles can fall into the BH horizon (and never escape), in the TS case the cap represents the boundary of the spacetime itself and is typically reached with a smooth tangential-like approach, as we will discuss below.

Extended bodies (spinning bodies or, more in general, bodies with an n-polar structure) or electrically charged particles do not follow geodesics.
In the literature force-driven motions are poorly studied with some exception like accelerated equatorial orbits around BH (where the circular symmetry simplifies their description).
We will proceed here by examining some “key” situations in which the structure of the body (spin, charge) competes with the additional structures (for example, the additional scale which characterizes a TS in comparison with the Schwarzschild spacetime), comparing and contrasting among them.
Explicit analytic (wherever the description will allow it) and numerical examples will be constructed on purpose, in order to distinguish between TS and Schwarzschild BHs.

III
Geodesic deviation

Let
U^{\alpha}(\tau)=\frac{dx^{\alpha}}{d\tau}
be a generic timelike geodesic of the TS spacetime, parametrized by the proper time
\tau
and characterized by the energy and angular momentum parameters, say
U^{\alpha}(\tau;E,L)
.
Actually, because of the dependence on these parameters,
U^{\alpha}(\tau;E,L)
identifies a family of geodesics, and after having selected a “reference geodesic,” say
U_{*}
(corresponding to chosen values for
E_{*}
and
L_{*}
) in the family, it is interesting to study how the other orbits will deviate from this one, The latter deviations can be finally interpreted as a measure of the strength of TS gravitational field itself.

To this end, according to a standard procedure, one introduces a deviation vector, say
\eta(\tau)
, defined all along the reference (timelike) geodesic and satisfying the geodesic deviation equation

\frac{D^{2}\eta^{\alpha}}{d\tau^{2}}+R^{\alpha}{}_{\beta\gamma\delta}U_{*}^{\beta}U_{*}^{\delta}\eta^{\gamma}\bigg|_{x^{\alpha}=x_{*}^{\alpha}(\tau)}=0\,.

(23)

Here

\frac{D^{2}\eta^{\alpha}}{d\tau^{2}}=\nabla_{U_{*}}(\nabla_{U_{*}}\eta^{\alpha})\bigg|_{x^{\alpha}=x_{*}^{\alpha}(\tau)}\,,

(24)

and

{\mathcal{E}}(U_{*})^{\alpha}{}_{\gamma}=R^{\alpha}{}_{\beta\gamma\delta}U_{*}^{\beta}U_{*}^{\delta}

(25)

denotes the electric part of the Riemann tensor as measured by the observers
U_{*}^{\alpha}
.
{\mathcal{E}}(U_{*})^{\alpha}{}_{\gamma}
exists only along the
U_{*}
world line and

\eta^{\alpha}(\tau)
connects the reference geodesic to another one within the same family. Furthermore, one assumes
\eta^{\alpha}
orthogonal to
U_{*}^{\alpha}
, since any component parallel to
U_{*}
would have a trivial evolution, i.e.,

\eta\cdot U_{*}=0\,.

(26)

Consequently, only the evolution of three components of
\eta
needs to be studied.
Let us denote by
Y(\tau)
the tangent vector to the deviation curve, say

Y^{\alpha}(\tau)=\frac{D\eta^{\alpha}(\tau)}{d\tau}\,,

(27)

so that

x(\tau)^{\mu}=x_{*}^{\mu}(\tau)+\eta^{\mu}(\tau)\,,

(28)

where, as stated above,

x_{*}^{\mu}(\tau)=\left(t_{p}(\tau),r_{p}(\tau),\frac{\pi}{2},\phi_{p}(\tau)\right)

(29)

describes a reference geodesic. More in detail our study concerns the following cases: 1) deviations from a circular equatorial geodesic and 2) deviations from an unbound equatorial geodesics.
In the latter case we find it convenient to replace the energy per unit mass in terms of velocity,
\hat{E}=\gamma=(1-v^{2})^{-1/2}
, and the angular momentum per unit mass in terms of the impact parameter
b
, namely
\hat{L}=b\gamma v
. The interest for these orbits is motivated by recent applications of self force
[
10
,
12
]
.

III.1
Deviations from a circular equatorial geodesic

Let’s start by studying the deviation
\eta^{\mu}(\tau)
from the (massive) circular reference geodesic at radius
r=r_{0}
whose four velocity is
U_{*}=U_{\rm circ}

\displaystyle U_{\rm circ}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma(\partial_{t}+\Omega\partial_{\phi})\,,

(30)

with

\Gamma=f_{3/2}^{-1/2}(r_{0})\,,\qquad\Omega=\sqrt{\frac{r_{s}}{2r_{0}^{3}}}\,.

(31)

The corresponding parametric equations read

t_{\rm circ}(\tau)=\Gamma\tau\,,\quad r_{\rm circ}=r_{0}\,,\quad\theta_{\rm circ}=\frac{\pi}{2}\,,\quad\phi_{\rm circ}=\Gamma\Omega\tau\,.

(32)

When writing the deviations equations (
23
) can be convenient to refer to the frame components of the
\eta^{\alpha}(\tau)
, with respect to
an orthonormal frame adapted to observers at rest with respect to the coordinates

\displaystyle e_{\hat{t}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g_{tt}}}\partial_{t}\,,\quad e_{\hat{r}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{g_{rr}}}\partial_{r}\,,\quad e_{\hat{\theta}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{g_{\theta\theta}}}\partial_{\theta}\,,

\displaystyle e_{\hat{\phi}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{g_{\phi\phi}}}\partial_{\phi}\,,\quad e_{\hat{y}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{g_{yy}}}\partial_{y}\,,

(33)

namely, introducing all along the orbit (
30
) the rescaled quantities

\displaystyle\eta^{t}(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\eta^{\hat{t}}(\tau)}{\sqrt{f_{s}(r_{0})}},\quad\eta^{r}(\tau)=\eta^{\hat{r}}(\tau)\sqrt{f_{s}(r_{0})f_{b}(r_{0})},

\displaystyle\eta^{\theta}(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r_{0}}\eta^{\hat{\theta}}(\tau),\quad\eta^{\phi}(\tau)=\frac{1}{r_{0}}\hat{\eta}^{\hat{\phi}}(\tau),

\displaystyle\eta^{y}(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\eta^{\hat{y}}(\tau)}{\sqrt{f_{b}(r_{0})}}\,.

(34)

We find that the
y
-equation is trivial

{\eta^{\hat{y}}}^{\prime\prime}(\tau)=0\,,

(35)

and will be ignored hereafter,
while the
\theta
-equation decouples

{\eta^{\hat{\theta}}}^{\prime\prime}(\tau)+\Gamma^{2}\Omega^{2}\eta^{\hat{\theta}}(\tau)=0\,,

(36)

and it is immediately solved

\eta^{\hat{\theta}}(\tau)=A\sin\left(\Gamma\Omega\tau\right)\,,\qquad\Gamma=(f_{3/2}(r_{0}))^{-1/2}

(37)

where
A
is and integration constant and we have assumed
\eta^{\theta}(0)=0
.
The remaining equations write

\displaystyle{\eta^{\hat{t}}}^{\prime\prime}(\tau)+2r_{0}\Omega^{2}\Gamma\sqrt{f_{b}(r_{0})}{\eta^{\hat{r}}}^{\prime}(\tau)=0\,,

\displaystyle{\eta^{\hat{r}}}^{\prime\prime}(\tau)-2\Gamma\Omega\sqrt{f_{b}(r_{0})f_{s}(r_{0})}{\eta^{\hat{\phi}}}^{\prime}(\tau)

\displaystyle\qquad+2r\Gamma\Omega^{2}\sqrt{f_{b}(r_{0})}{\eta^{\hat{t}}}^{\prime}(\tau)

\displaystyle\qquad-3f_{b}(r_{0})f_{s}(r_{0})\Gamma^{2}\Omega^{2}{\eta^{\hat{r}}}(\tau)=0\,,

\displaystyle{\eta^{\hat{\phi}}}^{\prime\prime}(\tau)+2\sqrt{f_{b}(r_{0})f_{s}(r_{0})}\Gamma\Omega{\eta^{\hat{r}}}^{\prime}(\tau)=0\,.

(38)

The
t
and
\phi
equations can be integrated one times immediately as

\displaystyle{\eta^{\hat{t}}}^{\prime}(\tau)+2r_{0}\Omega^{2}\Gamma\sqrt{f_{b}(r_{0})}{\eta^{\hat{r}}}(\tau)=C_{1}\,,

\displaystyle{\eta^{\hat{\phi}}}^{\prime}(\tau)+2\sqrt{f_{b}(r_{0})f_{s}(r_{0})}\Gamma\Omega{\eta^{\hat{r}}}(\tau)=C_{2}\,,\qquad

(39)

and the integration constants can be set to zero,
C_{1}=0=C_{2}
if one assumes
{\eta^{\hat{r}}}(0)=0
and
{\eta^{\hat{t}}}^{\prime}(0)={\eta^{\hat{\phi}}}^{\prime}(0)
.
With this choice (
C_{1}=0=C_{2}
)

\displaystyle{\eta^{\hat{t}}}(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2r_{0}\Gamma\Omega^{2}\sqrt{f_{b}(r_{0})}\int_{0}^{\tau}d\tau^{\prime}{\eta^{\hat{r}}}(\tau^{\prime})\,,

\displaystyle{\eta^{\hat{\phi}}}(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\sqrt{f_{b}(r_{0})f_{s}(r_{0})}\Gamma\Omega\int_{0}^{\tau}d\tau^{\prime}{\eta^{\hat{r}}}(\tau^{\prime})\,.

(40)

If one does not choose the simplifying condition
C_{1}=0=C_{2}
but keeps
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
generic then

\displaystyle{\eta^{\hat{t}}}(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2r_{0}\Gamma\Omega^{2}\sqrt{f_{b}(r_{0})}\int_{0}^{\tau}d\tau^{\prime}{\eta^{\hat{r}}}(\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle C_{1}\tau+{\eta^{\hat{t}}}(0)\,,

\displaystyle{\eta^{\hat{\phi}}}(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\sqrt{f_{b}(r_{0})f_{s}(r_{0})}\Gamma\Omega\int_{0}^{\tau}d\tau^{\prime}{\eta^{\hat{r}}}(\tau^{\prime})

(41)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle C_{2}\tau+{\eta^{\hat{\phi}}}(0)\,,

and the equation for
{\eta^{\hat{r}}}
becomes

\displaystyle{\eta^{\hat{r}}}^{\prime\prime}(\tau)+\Omega_{c}^{2}{\eta^{\hat{r}}}(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}\,,

(42)

where

{\mathcal{S}}=-2\sqrt{f_{b}(r_{0})}\Gamma\Omega(C_{1}r\Omega-C_{2}\sqrt{f_{s}(r_{0})})\,,

(43)

and

\displaystyle\Omega_{c}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma\Omega\sqrt{f_{b}(r_{0})f_{3}(r_{0})}\,,

(44)

is the TS-modification to the standard Schwarzschild epicyclic frequency,

\Omega_{c}=\Omega_{c}^{\rm Schw}\sqrt{f_{b}(r_{0})}\,.

(45)

The latter expression, defining a measurable quantity and characterizing an important difference with the Schwarzschild spacetime, can have observational consequences, in the sense that

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta\Omega_{c}}{\Omega_{c}^{\rm Schw}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Omega_{c}-\Omega_{c}^{\rm Schw}}{\Omega_{c}^{\rm Schw}}

(46)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{f_{b}(r_{0})}-1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{1-\frac{\alpha r_{s}}{r_{0}}}-1\,,

and for large values of
r_{0}\gg r_{s}

\displaystyle\frac{|\Delta\Omega_{c}|}{\Omega_{c}^{\rm Schw}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\alpha r_{s}}{r_{0}}\,.

(47)

Therefore, an uncertainty in
|\Delta\Omega_{c}|/\Omega_{c}^{\rm Schw}
can be read as a deviation of BH behavior in favour of the presence of a TS.

The solution for
{\eta^{\hat{r}}}(\tau)
finally reads

\displaystyle{\eta^{\hat{r}}}(\tau)=\frac{{\mathcal{S}}}{\Omega_{c}^{2}}+A_{1}\cos(\Omega_{c}\tau)+A_{2}\sin(\Omega_{c}\tau)\,.

(48)

We can however restrict ourselves to the simple case
{\eta^{\hat{r}}}(0)={\eta^{\hat{r}}}^{\prime}(0)=0
and
{\eta^{\hat{t}}}(0)={\eta^{\hat{\phi}}}(0)=0
(only these functions vanishing at
\tau=0
, and not their first derivatives).
This leads to the final solution

\displaystyle{\eta^{\hat{r}}}(\tau)=-\frac{{\mathcal{S}}}{\Omega_{c}^{2}}(1-\cos(\Omega_{c}\tau))\,,

(49)

with

\int_{0}^{\tau}d\tau^{\prime}{\eta^{\hat{r}}}(\tau^{\prime})=-\frac{{\mathcal{S}}}{\Omega_{c}^{3}}[\Omega_{c}\tau-\sin(\Omega_{c}\tau)]

(50)

which then give

\displaystyle{\eta^{\hat{t}}}(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2r_{0}\sqrt{f_{b}(r_{0})}\Gamma\Omega^{2}\frac{{\mathcal{S}}}{\Omega_{c}^{3}}[\Omega_{c}\tau-\sin(\Omega_{c}\tau)]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle C_{1}\tau\,,

\displaystyle{\eta^{\hat{\phi}}}(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{f_{b}(r_{0})f_{s}(r_{0})}\Gamma\Omega\frac{{\mathcal{S}}}{\Omega_{c}^{3}}[\Omega_{c}\tau-\sin(\Omega_{c}\tau)]

(51)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle C_{2}\tau\,.

A final remark concerns the orthogonality condition (
26
), which translates as

\eta^{\hat{t}}(\tau)=\frac{\Omega r_{0}}{\sqrt{f_{s}(r_{0})}}\eta^{\hat{\phi}}(\tau)\,,

(52)

and can be used to relate
C_{1}
and
C_{2}

III.2
Deviations from an unbound equatorial geodesics

In equatorial case, unbound orbits of the form (
29
) are described by

\displaystyle\frac{dt}{d\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\hat{E}}{f_{s}(r)}\,,

\displaystyle\frac{dr}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{r-r_{b}}}{\hat{E}r^{2}}f_{s}(r)\sqrt{r^{3}(\hat{E}^{2}-1)+r_{s}r^{2}-\hat{L}^{2}(r-r_{s})}\,,

\displaystyle\frac{d\phi}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\hat{L}}{\hat{E}r^{2}}f_{s}(r)\,.

(53)

Let us use the following notation

\hat{E}=\gamma=(1-v^{2})^{-1/2},\quad\hat{L}=b\sqrt{\hat{E}^{2}-1}=b\gamma v\,,

(54)

replacing energy in terms of velocity (
v
) and angular momentum in terms of impact parameter (
b
).
In the flat case,
r_{s}=r_{b}=0
, one finds

r|_{\rm flat}=\sqrt{b^{2}+v^{2}t^{2}}\,.

(55)

We will often use the dimensionless coordinate
T=\frac{vt}{b}
in place of
\tau
, so that the covariant first and second derivatives of
\eta^{\mu}
becomes

\displaystyle\frac{D\eta^{\mu}(T)}{d\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u^{t}(T)\frac{v}{b}\Big[\frac{d\eta^{\mu}(T)}{dT}+\Gamma^{\mu}_{\nu\sigma}(T)\eta^{\nu}(T)\frac{dx_{*}^{\sigma}(T)}{dT}\Big]\,,

VII
Discussion

Motivated by the lack of explicit analytic results concerning accelerated motions in a TS spacetime, we have analyzed the behaviour of spinning particles and electrically charged particles in such a spacetime. This study has shown how deviations from geodesic motion can be relevant in order to characterize how TS differs from a Schwarzschild BH. In order to have general information, we have framed our analysis in the context of relativistic “deviations” solving the geodesic deviation equation.
In the latter case as well as for the motion of charged particles we have considered hyperbolic-like orbits treated in a large impact parameter expansion limit, i.e., within what is called Post-Minkowskian approximation limit. For spinning particles we have analyzed deviations from circular geodesic orbits (circular orbits are also allowed for spinning particles), a simpler but very interesting situation. We have defined a family of spinning particles with orbits parametrized by the spin itself (besides a proper time parametrization) and discussed what happens to a bunch of such particles starting at the same point when they have different spin. In particular we have examined the approach to the boundary (cap, spherical surface) of the spacetime in the TS case which turns out to be tangential-like. To better illustrate the deviations we have discussed the case of three particles evolving from a geometrically simple situation: a triangle (made of ideal edges, inspired by a LISA-like experimental situation), whose evolution corresponds to a continuous expansion and squeezing of the edges themselves.
As a general result we find that 1) while a BH captures particles, the TS only allows for passages which (smoothly) skim the cap, 2) deviations due to the TS structure correspond to small additional corrections to the Schwarzschild case, mostly interesting from a theoretical point of view.

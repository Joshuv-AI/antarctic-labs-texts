II. Statutory Standard for Registration as a Clearing Agency

    Clearing agencies are broadly defined under the Exchange Act and 
undertake a variety of functions,\10\ including providing the services 
of a CCP.\11\ Pursuant to Section 17A of the Exchange Act and Rule 
17Ab2-1 thereunder, an entity that meets the definition of a clearing 
agency must register with the Commission.\12\ Section 17A(b)(1) also 
states that, upon the Commission's motion or upon a clearing agency's 
application, the Commission may conditionally or unconditionally exempt 
a clearing agency from any provisions of Section 17A or the rules or 
regulations thereunder if the Commission finds that such exemption is 
consistent with the public interest, the protection of investors, and 
the purposes of Section 17A, including the prompt and accurate 
clearance and settlement of securities transactions and the 
safeguarding of securities and funds.\13\

A. Organization and Capacity

1. Statutory Standard: Section 17A(b)(3)(A)
    Section 17A(b)(3)(A) of the Exchange Act states that a clearing 
agency shall not be registered unless the Commission determines that 
such clearing agency is so organized and has the capacity to be able to 
facilitate the prompt and accurate clearance and settlement of 
securities transactions and derivative agreements, contracts, and 
transactions for which it is responsible, to safeguard securities and 
funds in its custody or control or for which it is responsible, to 
comply with the provisions of the Exchange Act and the rules and 
regulations thereunder, to enforce (subject to any rule or order of the 
Commission pursuant to Section 17(d) or 19(g)(2) of the Exchange Act) 
compliance by its participants with the rules of the clearing agency, 
and to carry out the purposes of Section 17A of the Exchange Act.\23\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \23\ 15 U.S.C. 78q-1(b)(3)(A).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Consistent with this standard, the Commission does not assess in 
this order whether CCUS's ultimate implementation of the rules, 
policies, and procedures set forth in its Application will comply with 
each of the Commission's rules for clearing agencies, as CCUS is not 
yet operating as a (securities) clearing agency.\24\ Rather, under 
Section 17A(b)(3)(A) of the Exchange Act, the Commission assesses 
whether CCUS is so organized and has the capacity to comply with the 
provisions of the Exchange Act and the rules and regulations 
thereunder,\25\ by analyzing CCUS's organization and governance, as 
well as its operational arrangements.\26\ Under this standard, the 
registration of a clearing agency ``depends on a prediction about 
compliance with the law.'' \27\ Section 17A assumes that ``an applicant 
would produce a business plan that, if faithfully executed, would 
comply'' with the Exchange Act.\28\ To make its required statutory 
determination under Section 17A(b)(3)(A), the Commission must ``find[] 
that the applicant is able and likely to comply,'' and upon 
registration and commencement of operations as a registered clearing 
agency, compliance with Section 17A(b)(3)(A) ``is likely to be carried 
out.'' \29\

(a) Proposed Services
    The Application states that CCUS seeks to provide CCP services to 
market participants for ``cash settled, binary security options, 
including among others, security options overlying key performance 
indicators (`KPIs') reported by certain issuers of stock [`Binary KPI 
Options'].'' \30\ CCUS states that these Binary KPI Options ``will be 
fully margined,'' ensuring that once trades are initially settled with 
CCUS, ``all potential price movements will be covered by posted 
margin.'' \31\ CCUS also states that these Binary KPI Options will 
trade on Cboe Exchange, Inc. (``Cboe Exchange''), a national securities 
exchange registered with the Commission which is also a direct, wholly-
owned subsidiary of Cboe Global Markets, Inc. (``CGM'').\32\ In its 
Application, CCUS requests registration for Cboe Exchange to offer 
Binary KPI Options in a ``regulated securities environment.'' \33\

(b) Risk Management
    In Exhibit J, the Application provides information regarding CCUS's 
risk management framework. CCUS states its risk management framework is 
intended to: (i) reduce the potential impact of a Clearing Member 
Default via credit risk management standards and ongoing monitoring; 
and (ii) ensure that CCUS has sufficient financial and liquidity 
resources to manage the default of its two clearing member groups that 
would cause the largest aggregate credit exposure and at least the 
single clearing member group that would cause the largest aggregate 
liquidity exposure, in each case, in extreme but plausible market 
conditions.\44\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \44\ See Application, Exhibit J, at 5. See also CCUS Rules, at 
Rule 101(hh) (``General,'' ``Definitions,'' ``Default Financial 
Resources Requirement''), at 5 (defining CCUS's ``Default Financial 
Resources Requirement'' to mean ``the amount of financial resources 
required to enable [CCUS] to meet its obligations to Clearing

    CCUS states its risk management framework addresses counterparty 
risk through its financial responsibility requirements for clearing 
members, as well as CCUS's ability to impose protective measures 
against clearing members who fail to comply with applicable

clearing members; (ii) credit limits; (iii) daily risk monitoring; (iv) 
margin collection; and (v) option premium settlement.\46\

    CCUS states that it employs back-testing and stress-testing but 
that these tools' risk management function for binary options that are 
securities differs from other products it clears in its current 
capacity as a DCO because binary options are fully margined (i.e., 
fully collateralized) and margin provided pursuant to this requirement 
covers all price movements.\47\ Citing the fully margined nature of 
binary options, CCUS states its total cash obligation would be limited 
to aggregate initial margin.\48\ Nonetheless, CCUS also describes 
additional tools that it generally has available under its rules to 
manage liquidity risk, including its rights to partial tear-ups and 
full tear-ups.\49\

    While binary security options would be fully margined (i.e., fully 
collateralized), CCUS states that it does not require prefunding of 
margin requirements.\50\ Therefore, prior to the completion of CCUS's 
daily settlement cycle, CCUS faces intraday exposure associated with 
any unsettled positions of any defaulting clearing member.\51\ CCUS 
states this intraday exposure is mitigated through: (i) the bounded 
nature of binary security option's payouts (i.e., $0.00 or a maximum 
payout of $1.00); (ii) intra-day margin calls made in response to 
CCUS's near real-time monitoring of clearing members' exposure; and 
(iii) CCUS's default waterfall.\52\

    CCUS does not anticipate that clearing binary security options will 
generate material liquidity risk beyond this ``intraday settlement 
timing gap.'' \53\ In addition to limiting liquidity risk, CCUS states 
that the binary security option's fully margined, bounded-payout 
structure also mitigates: (i) procyclicality risk (i.e., fixed exposure 
prevent market stress from increasing margin requirements); (ii) wrong-
way risk (i.e., CCUS's exposure to defaulting clearing member remains 
fixed despite contributing market conditions); (iii) model risk (i.e., 
the discrete payoff eliminates margin models' tail-risk 
understatement); and (iv) concentration risk (i.e., the collateralized, 
discrete payoffs prevent position concentration from generating 
outsized tail risk).\54\

    CCUS states its stress test methodology is designed to ensure that 
its guaranty fund's size is at least equal to the largest theoretical 
loss to CCUS resulting from the default of the two clearing members 
(including any affiliated clearing members) in extreme but plausible 
market conditions.\55\ To determine this theoretical loss, CCUS states 
that it measures each clearing member group's shortfall between: (i) 
the stress loss; and (ii) initial margin requirement in each clearing 
account (subject to the restriction that Customer Account gains will 
not offset PAB Account losses).\56\ Given the binary securities 
options' bounded-payout structure, CCUS states that its risk management 
accounts for the potential risk of losses between: (i) CCUS's novation 
of a trade; and (ii) the completion of CCUS's daily settlement cycle 
(i.e., when CCUS collects the funds from its clearing members 
associated with all unsettled positions).\57\

B. Participation Standards

1. Statutory Standard: Section 17A(b)(3)(B)
    Section 17A(b)(3)(B) of the Exchange Act states that a clearing 
agency shall not be registered unless the Commission determines that 
the rules of the clearing agency provide that any (i) registered broker 
or dealer, (ii) other registered clearing agency, (iii) registered 
investment company, (iv) bank, (v) insurance company, or (vi) other 
person or class of persons as the Commission, by rule, may from time to 
time designate as appropriate to the development of a national system 
or the prompt and accurate clearance and settlement of securities 
transactions may become a participant in such clearing agency.\154\ 
Section 3(a)(24) of the Exchange Act defines a ``participant'' with 
respect to a clearing agency as any person who uses a clearing agency 
to clear or settle securities transactions or to transfer, pledge, 
lend, or hypothecate securities, and further states that the term does 
not include a person whose only use of a clearing agency is (i) through 
another person who is a participant or (ii) as a pledgee of 
securities.\155\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

the rules of the clearing agency are subject to the provisions of 
Section 17A(b)(4) of the Exchange Act.

C. Fair Representation

1. Statutory Standard: Section 17A(b)(3)(C)
    Section 17A(b)(3)(C) of the Exchange Act states that a clearing 
agency shall not be registered unless the Commission determines that 
the rules of the clearing agency assure a fair representation of its 
shareholders (or members) and participants in the selection of its 
directors and administration of its affairs.\162\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \162\ See 15 U.S.C. 78q-1(b)(3)(C). Section 17A(b)(3)(C) of the 
Exchange Act also states that the Commission may determine that the 
representation of participants is fair if they are afforded a 
reasonable opportunity to acquire voting stock of the clearing 
agency, directly or indirectly, in reasonable proportion to their 
use of such clearing agency.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

2. Summary of Application and Analysis
    Under Section 17A(b)(3)(C) of the Exchange Act, the Commission 
considers whether the Application provides fair representation both to 
shareholders (or members) and to participants in the selection of 
directors and the administration of affairs. In doing so, the 
Commission undertakes an analysis of the documents in the Application 
that govern or otherwise affect the selection of directors by the 
clearing agency and the administration of its affairs. Such documents 
include, for example, the constitution, articles of incorporation, 
bylaws, rules, and written policies or procedures. Such analysis 
considers both qualitative and quantitative factors, including the 
number of board positions that a clearing agency may reserve for 
shareholders or to represent participants, as well as the existence of 
provisions in governing documents that may impede representation in the 
selection of directors or the administration of affairs. The Commission 
also considers the overall organization of the clearing agency, the 
nature of the products it clears, and the structure of the market it 
serves, including the nature of existing clearing and settlement 
arrangements in the market served, the existence of other clearing 
agencies that offer comparable services and how they are structured, 
and the size of the market served by the applicant, to evaluate whether 
the representation proposed by the applicant is consistent with the 
requirements of the Exchange Act.\163\ Finally, because CCUS will 
operate the KPI Options Business in the same existing legal entity 
where it provides futures clearing services, the Commission considers 
CCUS's existing arrangements to the extent they indicate whether CCUS, 
as an entity, would provide fair representation to shareholders and 
participants of its KPI Options Business.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \163\ Accordingly, the level of participant representation 
needed to ensure fair representation consistent with the Exchange 
Act may vary depending on the facts and circumstances, including the 
market or markets to which the application is directed.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Under Section 3(a)(7) of the Exchange Act, the term ``director'' is 
defined as ``any director of a corporation or any person performing 
similar functions with respect to any organization, whether 
incorporated or unincorporated.'' \164\ When an applicant is not 
organized as a corporation, the Commission assesses whether the 
directors under its business form are performing functions similar to 
the directors of a corporation. CCUS is organized as a member-managed 
LLC, which places control over CCUS with the LLC Member. This 
organizational and management structure differs from registered 
clearing agencies that are organized as corporations,\165\ or organized 
as LLCs but operate as manager-managed LLCs.\166\ As a result, below 
the Commission considers whether the role of director as set forth in 
CCUS's LLC Agreement is consistent with the statutory definition of 
director, in order to assess whether shareholders and participants have 
fair representation in its selection consistent with Section 
17A(b)(3)(C) of the Exchange Act.

    Taken together, the above discussed provisions clarifying the ways 
in which the LLC Member's reserved authority will not interfere with 
Board functioning consistent with the Exchange Act, providing 
representation on the Board for Directors to represent market 
participants, and directing the Nominating Committee to consider 
participant viewpoints in the evaluation of all candidates for Director 
provide for fair representation to CCUS's participants in the selection 
of directors and the administration of CCUS's affairs, consistent with 
Section 17A(b)(3)(C) of the Exchange Act.
    For the reasons discussed directly above, the Commission determines 
that the rules of CCUS assure fair representation in the selection of 
its directors and administration of its affairs consistent with Section 
17A(b)(3)(C) of the Exchange Act.

E. Rules Designed To Promote Prompt and Accurate Clearance and 
Settlement and the Safeguarding of Securities and Funds

1. Statutory Standard: Section 17A(b)(3)(F)
    Section 17A(b)(3)(F) of the Exchange Act states that a clearing 
agency shall not be registered unless the Commission determines that 
the rules of the clearing agency are designed to promote the prompt and 
accurate clearance and settlement of securities transactions and, to 
the extent applicable, derivative agreements, contracts, and 
transactions, to assure the safeguarding of securities and funds which 
are in the custody or control of the clearing agency or for which it is 
responsible, to foster cooperation and coordination with persons 
engaged in the clearance and settlement of securities transactions, to 
remove impediments to and perfect the mechanism of a national system 
for the prompt and accurate clearance and settlement of securities 
transactions, and, in general, to protect investors and the public 
interest. It also states that a clearing agency shall not be registered 
unless the Commission determines that the rules are not designed to 
permit unfair discrimination in the admission of participants or among 
participants in the use of the clearing agency, or to regulate by 
virtue of any authority conferred by the Exchange Act matters not 
related to the purposes of this section or the administration of the 
clearing agency.
2. Summary of Application and Analysis
    The Commission's rules for clearing agencies provide for the 
safeguarding of securities and funds and promote prompt and accurate 
clearance and settlement by establishing specific requirements for a 
clearing agency's rules, policies and procedures related to operations 
and risk management. Specifically, these requirements address rules and 
procedures for the collection of margin, the composition of the 
guaranty fund, default management and loss allocation, and the 
management of other risks associated with the custody, clearing, and 
settlement of securities.\183\ To analyze under Section 17A(b)(3)(F) of 
the Exchange Act an application seeking to provide CCP services, the 
Commission considers a clearing agency's rules concerning its account 
structures, margin system, guaranty fund, default management, and loss 
allocation processes.

(e) Conclusion
    With respect to its rules as applied to BD Clearing Members, the 
Application describes a comprehensive risk management framework 
consistent with Commission rules that help ensure CCUS will collect 
sufficient margin to cover its exposures, maintain an appropriately 
sized Guaranty Fund, and be able to manage a default and allocate 
losses appropriately, if or when needed. CCUS's risk management 
framework is designed to address the particular features of CCUS's 
proposed structure, i.e., to manage the risks presented by BD Clearing 
Members and Direct Clearing Members submitting Binary KPI Options for 
clearing. For these reasons and as discussed in more detail above in 
Parts III.E.2.a) through d), the Commission determines that CCUS Rules 
as to the BD Clearing Members and Direct Clearing Members are designed 
to promote the prompt and accurate clearance and settlement of 
securities transactions, assure the safeguarding of securities and 
funds which are in its custody or control or for which it is 
responsible, and protect investors and the public interest as set forth 
in Section 17A(b)(3)(F) of the Exchange Act. The Commission also 
determines that CCUS Rules as to the BD Clearing Members and Direct 
Clearing Members do not regulate by virtue of any authority conferred 
by the Exchange Act matters not related to the purposes of Section 17A 
of the Exchange Act or the administration of the clearing agency.
    The Commission is not, at this time, making a determination 
regarding the CCUS Rules under Section 17A(b)(3)(F) of the Exchange Act 
for the types of participants set forth in the Exchange Act other than 
registered broker-dealers--namely, clearing agencies, registered 
investment companies, banks, or insurance companies. Because the 
Application does not address the other types of participants, the 
Commission is not, at this time, making a determination as to whether 
CCUS Rules foster cooperation and coordination with persons engaged in 
the clearance and settlement of securities transactions, or remove 
impediments to and perfect the mechanism of a national system for the 
prompt and accurate clearance and settlement of securities 
transactions. The Commission also is not, at this time, making a 
determination that CCUS Rules are not designed to permit unfair 
discrimination in the admission of

[[Page 67637]]

participants or among participants in the use of the clearing agency. 
As discussed further below in Part IV, pursuant to Rule 17Ab2-1(c), the 
Commission is granting CCUS's request for temporary registration with 
an exemption from requirements of Section 17A(b)(3)(F) of the Exchange 
Act for a period of 18 months.

    As described above, the CCUS Rules address the discipline of 
clearing members, denial of applications, and prohibitions or 
limitations imposed by the clearing agency with respect to access to 
services offered by the clearing agency. CCUS has established rules and 
procedures that describe in detail the process taken by CCUS both when 
reviewing applications to become clearing members and when disciplining 
existing clearing members. The CCUS Rules ensure that written 
notification with reasoning is provided to any applicant whose 
application has been denied and to any clearing member who is being 
sanctioned for a violation of the CCUS Rules. Once the appropriate 
notification is provided, the affected party is afforded an opportunity 
to appeal the denial or sanction by requesting a hearing at which the 
affected party may be represented by counsel. The Commission therefore 
determines that the CCUS Rules provide a fair procedure consistent with 
Section 17A(b)(3)(H) of the Exchange Act.

G. Burden on Competition

1. Statutory Standard: Section 17A(b)(3)(I)
    Section 17A(b)(3)(I) of the Exchange Act states that a clearing 
agency shall not be registered unless the Commission determines that 
the rules of the clearing agency do not impose any burden on 
competition not necessary or appropriate in furtherance of the purposes 
of the Exchange Act. In addition, Section 17A(a)(2) of the Exchange Act 
directs the Commission, having due regard for the maintenance of fair 
competition among brokers, dealers, clearing agencies, and transfer 
agents, to use its authority under the Exchange Act to facilitate the 
establishment of a national system for the prompt and accurate 
clearance and settlement of transactions in securities (``National 
System'') and to facilitate the establishment of linked or coordinated 
facilities for the clearance and settlement of transactions in 
securities.\242\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \242\ 15 U.S.C. 78q-1(a)(2).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

2. Summary of Application and Analysis
    As described throughout this order, CCUS proposes to operate a 
registered clearing agency that provides CCP services for binary 
options that are securities. In assessing whether the rules of a 
clearing agency impose or do not impose any burden on competition not 
necessary or appropriate in furtherance of the purposes of the Exchange 
Act under Section 17A(b)(3)(I) of the Exchange Act, the Commission 
considers the impact of the Applicant's rules on competition among 
brokers, dealers, clearing agencies, and transfer agents, and on 
competition between the Applicant and these entities.\243\ Where there 
is a burden on competition, the Commission must determine if that 
burden is necessary or appropriate consistent with the determination 
required by Section 17A(b)(3)(I) of the Exchange Act.\244\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \243\ See, e.g., GSCC Order, 53 FR at 19845.
    \244\ See Bradford Nat'l Clearing Corp. v. S.E.C., 590 F.2d 1085 
(D.C. Cir. 1978) (explaining that Congress intended to give the 
Commission ``exceptionally broad powers'' to determine the precise 
structure of the National System and placed ``slightly more emphasis 
on rapid development and national availability'' with respect to the 
National System than the national market system).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    In the context of impacts on competition among clearing agencies, 
one commenter--while generally supportive of CCUS's request for 
temporary registration as a time-limited bridge that allows innovative, 
investor-protective products to reach the market--is ``fully opposed'' 
to CCUS becoming a ``permanent fixture'' of the listed options market, 
as a second, fully registered clearing agency alongside the Options 
Clearing Corporation (``OCC'').\245\ While acknowledging that its 
concern is ``structural rather than a comment on [CCUS's] present 
intentions,'' the commenter opposes adding to the National System for 
Clearance and Settlement a ``vertically integrated clearing structure'' 
such as characterizes the U.S. futures market, where, as the commenter 
describes, each major exchange group operates its own 
clearinghouse.\246\ The commenter supports instead a central, industry 
utility clearing model, of the kind OCC has provided for listed options 
as a registered clearing agency.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \245\ PTG at 2.
    \246\ PTG at 3.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    The commenter states that the Commission should consider the 
broader market structure implications of this Application before 
granting permanent registration, explaining its view that a vertically 
integrated ``monopolistic market structure ``is the kind of structure 
Section 17A of the Exchange Act was designed to avoid.\247\ The 
commenter explains that the pool of firms capable of serving as 
clearing members in the listed options market is limited due to the 
substantial capital, operational, and risk management infrastructure 
that clearing membership requires. In the commenter's view, a more 
fragmented structure with multiple clearing agencies would add to the 
costs of clearing membership among this limited group--and therefore 
the associated costs borne by market participants as their clients--
through the potential for higher clearing fees, capital inefficiencies 
that result from holding positions that could offset but for multiple 
clearing agencies, and the loss of portfolio margining benefits.\248\ 
The commenter also argues that, in addition to increased costs, an 
additional clearing agency introduces structural risk into the market 
ecosystem for listed options. The commenter states that the structural 
change associated with a second clearing agency will require routing 
new product categories through less proven exchange-affiliated clearing 
agencies rather than an industry utility. Nonetheless, the commenter 
explains that its members are ``frustrated with OCC's pace of 
innovation'' and that OCC is unable to currently provide clearing 
services for Binary KPI Options.\249\ The commenter also acknowledges 
that the binary options to be cleared by CCUS have a comparatively 
simple risk profile relative to conventional options, which may 
mitigate the potential for procyclicality, wrong-way risk, model risk, 
and concentration risk.\250\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \247\ Id.
    \248\ Id. at 2.
    \249\ Id.
    \250\ PTG. at 2.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    The Commission acknowledges PTG's comments as outlined above. 
Currently pending before the Commission however, is an application for 
temporary registration. PTG's points relate to permanent registration 
and therefore are more appropriately addressed in any future Commission 
order related to permanent registration. That said, the risks and costs 
associated with changes to the structure of the listed options market 
will be evaluated during the period of CCUS's temporary registration by 
the Commission and market participants alike.
    CCUS's clearing model and its ability to quickly bring to market 
clearing services for innovative product types for which there is 
market demand supports both innovation and competition in the market 
for clearing services, consistent

[[Page 67640]]

with the purposes of Section 17A of the Exchange Act. And, more 
broadly, CCUS's Application does not impose any burden not necessary or 
appropriate in furtherance of the purposes of the Exchange Act. As CCUS 
states, its clearing infrastructure ``has been built specifically with 
open-access clearing top of mind,'' and CCUS intends to pursue cross-
margining arrangements that may benefit investors and citing the 
experience of its European affiliate.\251\ The Commission, has long 
viewed cross-margining arrangements between separately registered 
clearing agencies as a well-established mechanism for achieving margin 
efficiencies and facilitating the purposes of Section 17A of the 
Exchange Act. In other markets including the U.S. Treasury market, the 
Commission has approved applications for registration by new clearing 
agencies to help bring innovative products to market that serve 
investors and the public interest. The Commission agrees, as CCUS 
contends, that new clearing agencies, structured consistently with the 
requirements of Section 17A and Commission rules thereunder, can bring 
enhancements to market resilience by expanding access to central 
clearing and promoting effective risk management.\252\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \251\ CCUS Response at 4.
    \252\ Id.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Accordingly, for the reasons discussed above, and based on the 
facts and circumstances presented by this Application, the Commission 
determines, pursuant to Section 17A(b)(3)(I) of the Exchange Act, that 
the Application does not impose any burden on competition that is not 
necessary or appropriate in furtherance of the purposes of Section 17A 
of the Exchange Act.\253\

IV. Temporary Registration and Exemption

    CCUS has requested, pursuant to Rule 17Ab2-1(c), that the 
Commission grant CCUS temporary registration as a clearing agency with 
an exemption from certain requirements as to which the Commission is 
directed to make a determination under Sections 17A(b)(3)(B) and (F) of 
the Exchange Act for a period of 18 months.\254\ With respect to 
Section 17A(b)(3)(B), as discussed in Part III.B.2, CCUS has not 
provided in its Application for participation standards addressing all 
categories of participants enumerated in Section 17A(b)(3)(B) of the 
Exchange Act, including registered clearing agencies, registered 
investment companies, banks, insurance companies, or other classes of 
persons designated as appropriate by the Commission. Relatedly, as 
discussed in Part III.E.2, CCUS has not provided CCUS Rules under 
Section 17A(b)(3)(F) of the Exchange Act addressing these same 
categories of participants. For the reasons discussed below, pursuant 
to Section 17A(b) of the Exchange Act and Rule 17Ab2-1(c) thereunder, 
the Commission is granting CCUS's request for temporary registration as 
a clearing agency and finds that an exemption from the above-described 
requirements of Sections 17A(b)(3)(B) and (F) of the Exchange Act is 
consistent with the public interest, the protection of investors, and 
the purposes of Section 17A, including the prompt and accurate 
clearance and settlement of securities transactions and the 
safeguarding of securities and funds.\255\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \254\ See Application, Exhibit S, at 3-4.
    \255\ See 15 U.S.C. 78q-1(b)(1).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    CCUS explains that its request for temporary registration would 
enable CCUS to meet market demand for Binary KPI Options in a regulated 
securities trading environment while other securities clearing agencies 
are not prepared or able to provide clearing agency services for these 
products. In its request, CCUS explains that its Application would not 
affect existing standardized securities options products traded on 
national securities exchanges and cleared by OCC, and that CCUS and its 
affiliates have a long history of close, successful, and productive 
relationships with other option exchanges and OCC. One commenter also 
expressed support for CCUS's request for temporary registration, as 
previously discussed in Part III.G.2, because it would serve as a 
``time-limited bridge'' that allows innovative products to reach the 
market sooner than would be possible through the existing registered 
clearing agency that serves the listed options market.\256\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \256\ PTG at 2.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    CCUS states that not establishing participation standards for these 
other categories of participants listed in Section 17A(b)(3)(B) is 
appropriate, citing ``the nature of the product, the categories of 
market participants who have indicated an interest in clearing Binary 
KPI Options, CCUS's risk management and liquidity framework, the 
broker-dealer customer protection framework and other factors.'' \257\ 
In Exhibit S, CCUS explains that it would be prepared to consider the 
feasibility and appropriateness of the other types of participants 
enumerated in Section 17A(b)(3)(B) during the period of its temporary 
registration,\258\ and reiterates in its response letter that it would 
demonstrate compliance with all applicable clearing agency requirements 
within 18 months of its approval for temporary registration.\259\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \257\ See Application, Exhibit S, at 3-4.
    \258\ See id. at 4.
    \259\ CCUS Response at 1.

    With respect to these requests for exemptive relief from Section 
17A(b)(3)(B) and (F) of the Exchange Act, made in connection with its 
request for temporary registration, the Commission finds such relief is 
consistent with the public interest, the protection of investors, and 
the purposes of Section 17A of the Exchange Act for two reasons. First, 
providing clearing services only to BD Clearing Members is an 
appropriate, and incremental step, for bringing to the securities 
market Binary KPI Options. The Application establishes a comprehensive 
framework for the management of risk surrounding binary options that 
are securities, and commenters agree Second, CCUS has represented that 
it will consider including other entities during the period of 
temporary registration. Providing relief from Section 17A(b)(3)(B), 
which the Commission has granted before, allows prospective clearing 
agencies to develop their participation standards on an incremental 
basis because the financial and operational membership standards for 
such market participants depends, in part, on the extent and manner in 
which the clearing agency allocates losses resulting from member 
defaults, and the development of such participation standards will 
inform the overall risk profile and risk management framework of the 
registered clearing agency.\260\ In the absence of such participation 
standards, the Commission cannot assess under Section 17A(b)(3)(F) 
whether clearing agency rules implementing participation standards for 
the categories of participants enumerated in Section 17A(b)(3)(B) are 
designed to promote the prompt and accurate clearance and settlement of 
securities transactions and to assure the safeguarding of securities 
and funds.\261\ Further, in the absence of such participation standards 
and associated rules, the Commission cannot assess whether the CCUS 
Rules foster cooperation and coordination with persons engaged in the 
clearance and settlement of securities transactions, to remove 
impediments to and perfect the mechanism of a national system for the 
prompt and accurate clearance and settlement of securities 
transactions,

[[Page 67641]]

and are not designed to permit unfair discrimination in the admission 
of participants or among participants in the use of the clearing agency 
because these requirements are primarily directed to assessing how the 
Application addresses the different categories of participation in its 
rules.\262\ Accordingly, assessment of these provisions of Section 
17A(b)(3)(F) requires that CCUS Rules incorporate the other enumerated 
categories of participants. With respect to the participation 
categories provided (i.e., BD Clearing Members and Direct Clearing 
Members), the Commission has assessed and found that CCUS Rules do 
promote the prompt and accurate clearance and settlement of securities 
transactions and assure the safeguarding of securities and funds. In so 
doing, the Application and CCUS Rules demonstrate consistency with the 
purposes of Section 17A of the Exchange Act (including the protection 
of investors and the public interest). Therefore, the Commission is 
providing exemptive relief from requirements under Section 17A(b)(3)(F) 
on a temporary basis to enable CCUS time to develop its rules, 
policies, and procedures as to the other participation categories 
enumerated and ensure such rules, policies and procedures are 
consistent with the requirements of Section 17A(b)(3)(F). Temporary 
relief is appropriate and consistent with the public interest and the 
protection of investors because existing CCUS Rules, as applied to BD 
Clearing Members and Direct Clearing Members, are consistent with 
promoting the prompt and accurate clearance and settlement of 
securities transactions and the safeguarding of funds and 
securities.\263\ Specifically, the reasons discussed in Part III.E.2, 
CCUS rules provide a comprehensive risk management framework for BD 
Clearing Members and Direct Clearing Members consistent with the 
purposes of Section 17A of the Exchange Act. Granting exemptive relief 
from this provision permits CCUS to operate on a temporary basis with 
respect to BD Clearing Members and Direct Clearing Members, is in the 
public interest and consistent with the protection of investors because 
it enables CCUS to evaluate its risk management framework as to 
registered broker-dealers and participants that meet the definition of 
Direct Clearing Members while it considers how best to incorporate the 
other categories of participants enumerated in Section 17A(b)(3)(B) and 
whether its risk management standards need to be adjusted to account 
for the way the other categories might interact with the clearing 
agency and the risks they would bring via their participation.

V. Conclusion

    For the reasons discussed above, pursuant to Section 17A(b) of the 
Exchange Act and Rule 17Ab2-1(c) thereunder, the Commission is 
exempting CCUS from certain of the requirements of Section 17A(b)(3)(B) 
and (F) as described in this order and has determined that CCUS's 
Application otherwise satisfies the requirements of Section 17A of the 
Exchange Act and the rules and regulations thereunder. Accordingly, the 
Commission is granting CCUS's request for temporary registration for a 
period of 18 months.\266\

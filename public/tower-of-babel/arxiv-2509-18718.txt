Global bounded solutions of the 3D Patlak-Keller-Segel-Navier-Stokes system via Couette flow and logistic source
Shikun Cui, Lili Wang, Wendong Wang, Juncheng Wei, Guoxu Yang

Abstract
. As is well-known, the solutions to the Patlak-Keller-Segel system in 3D may blow up in finite time regardless of any initial cell mass. In this paper, we investigate the existence of global bounded solutions of the 3D Patlak-Keller-Segel-Navier-Stokes system with a large initial cell mass via Couette flow or logistic source in a finite channel. On the one hand, it is proved that as long as the Couette flow is strong enough and the initial velocity is small, the bounded solutions of the system are global in time without any limitation on the initial mass M M if the logistic source term exists. On the other hand, if the logistic source term vanishes, it is proved that as long as the Couette flow is strong enough and the initial velocity is small, the solutions with a finite initial mass M M , whose lower bound is ( 8 ​ π 9 ) − \left(\frac{8\pi}{9}\right)^{-} , are global in time.



Keywords:
Patlak-Keller-Segel-Navier-Stokes; logistic source; stability; Couette flow

Introduction

Consider the following 3D parabolic-elliptic Patlak-Keller-Segel (PKS) system with logistic source, coupled with the Navier-Stokes (NS) equations, in a finite channel
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{I}\times\mathbb{T}

\mathbb{T}=[0,2\pi)

\mathbb{I}=[-1,1]

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}n+v\cdot\nabla n=\Delta n-\nabla\cdot(n\nabla c)-\mu n^{2},\\
\Delta c+n-c=0,\\
\partial_{t}v+v\cdot\nabla v+\nabla P=\Delta v+n\nabla\phi,\\
\nabla\cdot v=0,\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

along with initial conditions

(n,v)|_{t=0}=(n_{\rm in},v_{\rm in}).

Here,

v

When

[
30

[
25

For the one-dimensional PKS system, all its solutions are globally well-posed.
In spatial dimensions exceeding one, solutions of the classical PKS system may exhibit finite-time blow-up. For the two-dimensional parabolic-parabolic system, a critical mass threshold emerges at

[

36

Remark 1.3
.

Even if the velocity
u
of the system (
1.8
) vanishes, it is still unknown whether the bounded solutions of this system exist globally according to the counterexample of Fuest in
[
14
]
. The above theorem shows that the bounded solutions of this system will exist globally for large mass initial data around the Couette flow. Moreover, the result establishes the global existence of solutions to the 3D PKS-NS system for the first time without any limitation for
\mu

Besides, in the absence of the logistic source term

, our result provides a nonlinear extension of Theorem 1.2 in
[
8
]
. At this point, we need to overcome the great challenges from nonlinear interactions and the 3D lift-up effect.

Remark 1.4
.

The boundary data (
1.6
) indicates that the cell density (number of cells per unit area/volume) is fixed at the boundary and
the concentration of the chemical signal (the chemotactic substance secreted by cells) is fixed at the boundary. It corresponds to the actual boundary states in experiments or physiology. For example, when studying cell chemotaxis in a Petri dish, if no additional cells are supplied at the dish’s edge, Dirichlet boundary condition for “cell density” can be set to
0
(indicating no cells at the boundary); alternatively, if the chemical signal concentration at the boundary is controlled to be constant via laboratory techniques, Dirichlet boundary condition is used to fix this concentration.

2.
Key ideas and proof of the main results

2.1.
Process the cell density

First, let us consider the following simplified PKS system:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\partial_{t}n+Ay\partial_{x}n=\Delta n-\nabla\cdot(n\nabla c)-\mu n^{2},\\
\Delta c+n-c=0.\end{array}\right.

(2.1)

When
A=0
and
\mu>0,
(
2.1
) is the so-called PKS system with logistic source;
when
A=0
and
\mu=0,
the logistic source disappears, then (
2.1
) is the classical PKS system. After time scaling, we get

\partial_{t}n+y\partial_{x}n=\frac{1}{A}\Delta n-\frac{1}{A}\nabla\cdot(n\nabla c)-\frac{1}{A}\mu n^{2}.

(2.2)

Case 1:
\mu>0
.
For (
2.2
), the energy estimates show that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|n\|_{L^{p}}^{p}+\frac{4(p-1)}{Ap}\|\nabla(n^{\frac{p}{2}})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=-\frac{p<\nabla\cdot(n\nabla c),n^{p-1}>}{A}-\frac{\mu p\|n\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}}{A}.

Using elliptic condition
\Delta c+n-c=0,
we infer from the above equality that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|n\|_{L^{p}}^{p}+\frac{4(p-1)}{Ap}\|\nabla(n^{\frac{p}{2}})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=p\left(\frac{p-1}{p}-\mu\right)\frac{\|n\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}}{A}+``{\rm good~terms}".

When
p\geq\frac{3}{2}
and
\mu>\frac{1}{3},
by the Moser-Alikakos iteration, the solutions to (
2.1
) are global in time

\displaystyle\|n\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}\leq C(\|n_{\rm in}\|_{L^{1}},\|n_{\rm in}\|_{L^{\infty}}).

When
0<\mu\leq\frac{1}{3}
, it remains unknown in three-dimension to determine whether solutions to (
2.1
) exist globally in time or blow up. This issue requires further investigation.
According to the frequency
k_{1}
, we decompose the cell density
n
as follows:

n(t,x,y,z)=n_{\neq}(t,x,y,z)+n_{0}(t,y,z),

where
n_{\neq}(t,x,y,z)
denotes the
x
-dependent (non-zero mode) part and
n_{0}(t,y,z)
denotes the
x
-independent (zero mode) part of
n
. Under the influence of Couette flow, when the flow strength is sufficiently large (i.e.,
A
is large), the non-zero mode
n_{\neq}
experiences enhanced dispersion. In particular, it satisfies the decay estimate

\|n_{\neq}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C{\rm e}^{-aA^{-\frac{1}{3}}t}\left(\|(n_{\rm in})_{\neq}\|_{L^{2}}+1\right),

for some constants
C>0
and
a>0
. In this way, our focus shifts to the zero mode
n_{0}
.
Using equation (
2.1
) and accounting for the nonlinear interactions between different modes, we obtain the following evolution equation for
n_{0}
:

\partial_{t}n_{0}-\frac{1}{A}\Delta n_{0}=-\frac{1}{A}\nabla\cdot(n_{0}\nabla c_{0})-\frac{\mu}{A}n_{0}^{2}+\text{``good terms''},\quad\text{for}~0<\mu<\frac{1}{2}.

Then, the
L^{2}
energy estimate shows that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|n_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{A}\|\nabla n_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{A}\|n_{0}\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\|\Delta c_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+``{\rm good~terms}".

(2.3)

By the 2D Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality

\displaystyle\|n_{0}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{I}\times\mathbb{T})}^{2}\leq C\|n_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{I}\times\mathbb{T})}\|\nabla n_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{I}\times\mathbb{T})},

it follows from (

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|n_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{C}{A}\|n_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\Delta c_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+``{\rm good~terms}".

An important observation
is that the

- norm of cell density can be controlled by the

-norm of the initial mass (see Lemma

for more details), that is

\displaystyle\frac{1}{A}\|n_{0}\|_{L^{2}L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\mu}\|(n_{\rm in})_{0}\|_{L^{1}}.

Therefore, inspired by Tao-Winkler’s work
[
37
]

-norm of the density function, elliptic estimates and the Grönwall inequality, we prove that (see more details in the proof of Lemma

\|n_{0}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\left(\|(n_{\rm in})_{0}\|_{L^{2}},\|(n_{\rm in})_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\right).

Finally, by the Moser-Alikakos iteration, it can be shown that the cell density stays globally regular with

\displaystyle\|n\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}\leq C(\|n_{\rm in}\|_{L^{2}},\|n_{\rm in}\|_{L^{1}},\|n_{\rm in}\|_{L^{\infty}}).

Case 2:
\mu=0
.

Similarly, the non-zero mode suffers from the enhanced dispersion:

\displaystyle\|n_{\neq}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C{\rm e}^{-aA^{-\frac{1}{3}}t}(\|(n_{\rm in})_{\neq}\|_{L^{2}}+1).

In order to handle the zero mode more precisely, we decompose

into two parts:

It is easy to find that

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial_{y}(n_{(0,0)}\partial_{y}c_{(0,0)})+\partial_{y}(n_{(0,\neq)}\partial_{y}c_{(0,\neq)})_{(0,0)}}{A}+``{\rm good~terms}"

(2.4)

and that

\displaystyle\|n_{(0,0)}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\big(\|(n_{\rm in})_{(0,0)}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+M^{\frac{3}{2}}\|n_{(0,\neq)}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}^{\frac{7}{6}}+M^{4}+1\big).

Since

By energy estimates and the Sobolev embedding, we obtain that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|n_{(0,\neq)}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{2}{A}\|\nabla n_{(0,\neq)}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{C_{*}^{3}M}{\pi A}\|\nabla n_{(0,\neq)}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+``{\rm good~terms}",

where

\displaystyle\|n\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}\leq C(\|n_{\rm in}\|_{L^{2}},\|n_{\rm in}\|_{L^{1}},\|n_{\rm in}\|_{L^{\infty}}).

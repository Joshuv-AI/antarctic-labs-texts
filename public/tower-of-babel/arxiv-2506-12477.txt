Estimates for viscosity solutions of fully nonlinear equations near smooth boundaries
Niklas L.P. Lundström, Marcus Olofsson, Jesper Singh

Abstract
We reduce the problem of proving decay estimates for viscosity solutions of fully nonlinear PDEs to proving analogous estimates for solutions of one-dimensional ordinary differential inequalities. Our machinery allow the ellipticity to vanish near the boundary and permits general, possibly unbounded, lower-order terms. A key consequence is the derivation of boundary Harnack inequalities for a broad class of fully nonlinear, nonhomogeneous equations near C 1 , 1 C^{1,1} -boundaries. In combination with C 1 , α C^{1,\alpha} -estimates, we also obtain that quotients of positive vanishing solutions are Hölder continuous near C 1 , 1 C^{1,1} -boundaries.This result applies to a wide family of fully nonlinear uniformly elliptic PDEs; and for p ⁡ ( x ) p(x) -harmonic functions and planar ∞ \infty -harmonic functions near locally flat boundaries. We end by deriving some Phragmén-Lindelöf-type corollaries in unbounded domains. Mathematics Subject Classification: 35J60, 35J70, 35B40, 35B53, 35D40, 35J25, 35J92, 42B37. Keywords: boundary Harnack inequalities; quasi linear; non homogeneous; sub linear; growth estimate.

Introduction

In the first part of the paper, we develop a method that reduces the problem of obtaining decay estimates near the boundary for viscosity sub- and supersolutions of fully nonlinear PDEs to proving analogous estimates for classical sub- and supersolutions of one-dimensional problems in the form of ordinary differential inequalities (ODIs). These ODIs are only slight modifications of the original PDEs in one dimension, thereby illustrating a simple connection between the

n

We prove these results in Section

being nonnegative on a portion of the boundary of a domain satisfying an interior sphere condition.
The estimate takes the form

is a classical solution of the corresponding one-dimensional ODI (
2.7

2.2
gives an upper estimate

for viscosity subsolutions

of (

being nonpositive on a portion of the boundary of a domain satisfying an exterior sphere condition,

Suppose that

Then, if the boundary Harnack inequality holds for the solutions of the corresponding ODIs, then it holds also for

It thus suffices to prove the inequality for some suitable solutions of the one-dimensional ODIs, see Corollary

To establish these results we only assume our main operator in (

We believe that our work extends the existing literature in several notable ways:
(i) We allow the ellipticity to vanish near the boundary – an explicit example is provided in Section

(ii) We consider general unbounded lower-order terms that may depend on the spatial variable, the solution itself, and the norm of its gradient.
Overall, we impose minimal restrictions on the PDE. Instead, our methodology centers on what can be proved for the associated ODIs:
(iii) Rather than verifying whether a given PDE satisfies a set of technical assumptions,
our approach allows one to reduce the problem to analyzing one-dimensional differential equations.
Often, this reduces to classical textbook examples; for instance, see Section

which includes generalizations of the well-known

The present approach allows us to explicitly track how estimates depend on the value of the solution at a given distance from the boundary.

2
Decay estimates near the boundary

We consider fully nonlinear degenerate elliptic equations in non-divergence form,

\displaystyle F(x,u,Du,D^{2}u)=0,

(2.1)

where

F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{S}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
.
We will assume through that

\displaystyle F(x,u,p,X)\geq F(x,v,p,Y)\quad\text{whenever}\quad u\geq v\quad\text{and}\quad X\leq Y,

(2.2)

and adopt the standard concept of viscosity solutions:
An USC function
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is a viscosity subsolution
if for any
\varphi\in C^{2}(\Omega)
and any
x_{0}\in\Omega

such that
u-\varphi
has a local maximum at
x_{0}

it holds that

\displaystyle F(x_{0},u(x_{0}),D\varphi(x_{0}),D^{2}\varphi(x_{0}))\leq 0.

(2.3)

A LSC function
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is a viscosity supersolution
if for any
\varphi\in C^{2}(\Omega)
and any
x_{0}\in\Omega

such that
u-\varphi
has a local minimum at
x_{0}

it holds that

\displaystyle F(x_{0},u(x_{0}),D\varphi(x_{0}),D^{2}\varphi(x_{0}))\geq 0.

(2.4)

A continuous function is a viscosity solution if it is both a viscosity sub- and viscosity supersolution.

In the following we sometimes drop the word “viscosity” and simply write subsolution, solution and supersolution.
We say that a subsolution (supersolution) is strict in a domain
\Omega
if the equality in (
2.3
) ((
2.4
)) is strict,
and we say that a subsolution, supersolution, or solution is
classical
if it is twice differentiable in
\Omega
.

To avoid technicalities, we chose to stay with the standard viscosity solutions, as defined above, even though other definitions of “weak solutions” might be more suitable for some equations allowed.
The choice of viscosity solutions is not necessary for our results; other definitions of “weak solutions” can be considered in our arguments as long as such subsolutions (supersolutions) can be compared to classical strict supersolutions (subsolutions) in the sense of Lemma
2.6
below. For example, it is not necessary for our results that
F
is continuous, even though this is often assumed in the viscosity solution framework.

We intend to prove that the decay rate of positive viscosity solutions of (
2.1
), vanishing on a portion of the boundary of a
C^{1,1}
-domain, can be estimated
by classical solutions of second order ODIs.
We will build a lower and an upper estimate.
Let
\Omega\in\mathbf{R}^{n}
be a domain,
r>0
and
w\in\partial\Omega
.
For our lower decay estimate in
B(w,r)\cap\Omega
we assume

\displaystyle F(x,s,p,X)\leq\Phi^{+}(\check{t},s,|p|)+\mathcal{P}^{+}_{\lambda(\check{t}),\Lambda(\check{t})}(X)\quad\text{with}\quad\check{t}=d(x,\partial\Omega)

(2.5)

whenever
x\in B(w,6r)\cap\Omega,s\in\mathbb{R}_{+},p\in\mathbb{R}^{n},X\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
,
and for our upper estimate in
B(w,r)\cap\Omega
we assume

\displaystyle F(x,s,p,X)\geq\Phi^{-}(\bar{t},s,|p|)+\mathcal{P}^{-}_{\lambda(\hat{t}),\Lambda(\hat{t})}(X)\quad\text{with}\quad\hat{t}=d(x,w)

(2.6)

whenever
x\in B(w,6r)\cap\Omega,s\in\mathbb{R}_{+},p\in\mathbb{R}^{n},X\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
.
Here,

\lambda\leq\Lambda:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
are functions such that
{\lambda}
is nondecreasing and
{\Lambda}
is nonincreasing.

\Phi^{-},\Phi^{+}:(0,\infty)\times(0,\infty)\times(0,\infty)\to(-\infty,\infty)

\Phi^{+}
is

\Phi^{-}
is either nonincreasing with

or nondecreasing with
\bar{t}=d(x,w)
.
Observe that these assumptions allow the ellipticity to vanish
and the lower-order terms to blow up
near the boundary.

Moreover,
with
\mathcal{A}_{\lambda,\Lambda}:=\{A\in\mathbb{S}^{n}:\lambda I\leq A\leq\Lambda I\}
the Pucci operators are, as usual,

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(X)=

\displaystyle\sup_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{\lambda,\Lambda}}-\text{Tr}(AX)=-\lambda\text{Tr}(X^{+})+\Lambda\text{Tr}(X^{-}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}(X)=

\displaystyle\inf_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{\lambda,\Lambda}}-\text{Tr}(AX)=-\Lambda\text{Tr}(X^{+})+\lambda\text{Tr}(X^{-}),

where
X=X^{+}-X^{-}
with
X^{+}\geq 0
,
X^{-}\geq 0
, and

\displaystyle(d(x,\partial\Omega),U,|DU|)+\mathcal{P}^{+}_{\lambda(d(x,\partial\Omega)),\Lambda(d(x,\partial\Omega))}(D^{2}U)

\displaystyle+\Lambda(d(x,\partial\Omega))\frac{n-1}{|x-y|}\check{h}^{\prime}\left(\Xi\right)-\lambda(d(x,\partial\Omega))\frac{n}{r}\frac{\Lambda(\Xi)}{\lambda(\Xi)}\check{h}^{\prime}(\Xi).

\displaystyle(d(x,\partial\Omega),U,|DU|)+\mathcal{P}^{+}_{\lambda(d(x,\partial\Omega)),\Lambda(d(x,\partial\Omega))}(D^{2}U)

\displaystyle\leq\Phi^{+}\left(d(x,\partial\Omega),\check{h}\left(\Xi\right),\check{h}^{\prime}\left(\Xi\right)\right)-\Lambda(d(x,\partial\Omega))\frac{\Phi^{+}\left(\Xi,\check{h}\left(\Xi\right),\check{h}^{\prime}\left(\Xi\right)\right)}{\Lambda(\Xi)}

Lemma 2.6

Assume (
2.2

a viscosity supersolution of (
2.1

on
\partial\Omega
.
If either
u
is a classical strict subsolution, or
v
is a classical strict supersolution,
then
u<v
in
\Omega
.

Proof.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain,

u
a viscosity subsolution, and
v
be a classical strict supersolution in
\Omega
. Assume that

u\leq v
on
\partial\Omega
and that
u\geq v
somewhere in
\Omega
.
By USC the function
u-v
then attains a maximum
\geq 0
at some point
x_{0}\in\Omega
.
Since
v\in C^{2}(\Omega)
,
u-v
has a maximum at
x_{0}
, and
u
is a viscosity subsolution, it follows by definition that

\displaystyle F(x_{0},u(x_{0}),Dv(x_{0}),D^{2}v(x_{0}))\leq 0.

(2.14)

But since

is a classical strict supersolution,
we have

2.2
Examples

In this section we apply Theorem
2.1
, Theorem
2.2
and Corollary
2.3
to some classes of PDEs for which we easily can find solutions of the auxiliary ODIs in (
2.7
) and (
2.8
) and thereby conclude upper and lower decay estimates and boundary Harnack inequalities.
We begin with going through the simple case of zero lower order terms in detail, first with uniform ellipticity and then when ellipticity possibly blows up near the boundary.

2.2.1
The case
\Phi^{-}\equiv\Phi^{+}\equiv 0
with uniform ellipticity

In this case we consider solutions of (
2.1
)
with
F
satisfying, for some constants
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{-}_{\lambda,\Lambda}(X)\leq F(x,s,p,X)\leq\mathcal{P}^{+}_{\lambda,\Lambda}(X),

whenever
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},s\in\mathbb{R}_{+},p\in\mathbb{R}^{n},X\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
.
When
\lambda=1=\Lambda
we have only the Laplace equation and then it is well known that solutions decay at the same rate as the distance function in
C^{1,1}
-domains.
We now intend to derive the similar estimates in the above more general setting, and to do so it is, according to our Corollary
2.3
, enough to find classical solutions of the ODIs (
2.7
) and (
2.8
) and prove the corresponding estimates for those solutions.

Let
\Omega,r_{0},w,r,u_{1},u_{2}
and
\Gamma_{w,r}
be as in Corollary
2.3
and observe that, with
K=\frac{\Lambda n}{\lambda r}
,

\displaystyle\check{h}(t)=m\frac{e^{Kt}-1}{e^{Kr}-1}\quad\text{and}\quad\hat{h}(t)=M\frac{1-e^{-Kt}}{1-e^{-Kr}}

(2.16)

are increasing solutions of the ODIs (
2.7
) and (
2.8
), respectively, with
\check{h}(0)=0=\hat{h}(0)
, and
\check{h}(r)=m:=\inf_{\Gamma_{w,r}}u_{i}
and
\hat{h}(r)=M:=\sup_{B(w,6r)\cap\Omega}u_{i}
, i = 1,2.
Moreover, we have the estimates

\frac{\Lambda n}{\lambda\left(e^{\frac{\Lambda n}{\lambda}}-1\right)}\frac{mt}{r}=\frac{mKt}{e^{Kr}-1}\leq\check{h}(t)\leq\hat{h}(t)\leq\frac{MKt}{1-e^{-Kr}}=\frac{\Lambda n}{\lambda\left(1-e^{-\frac{\Lambda n}{\lambda}}\right)}\frac{Mt}{r}

for
t\in[0,r]
, so that Corollary
2.3
gives

\displaystyle\frac{\Lambda n}{\lambda\left(e^{\frac{\Lambda n}{\lambda}}-1\right)}\frac{md(x,\partial\Omega)}{r}\leq u_{i}(x)\leq\frac{\Lambda n}{\lambda\left(1-e^{-\frac{\Lambda n}{\lambda}}\right)}\frac{Md(x,\partial\Omega)}{r}\quad

(2.17)

for
x\in\Omega\cap B(w,r),i=1,2
.
Furthermore,

1\leq\frac{\hat{h}(t)}{\check{h}(t)}\leq\frac{M}{m}\frac{e^{\frac{\Lambda n}{\lambda}}-1}{1-e^{-\frac{\Lambda n}{\lambda}}}=\frac{M}{m}e^{\frac{\Lambda n}{\lambda}}\quad\text{whenever}\quad t\in(0,r)

and hence we also derive the boundary Harnack inequality

e^{-\frac{\Lambda n}{\lambda}}\frac{m}{M}\leq\frac{u_{1}(x)}{u_{2}(x)}\leq\frac{M}{m}e^{\frac{\Lambda n}{\lambda}}\quad\text{whenever}\quad x\in\Omega\cap B(w,r).

We remark that the
p
-Laplace equation can be handled by the standard ellipticity case considered here; see e.g. the related works
[
8
,
33
,
34
]
for details.

2.2.2
The case
\Phi^{-}\equiv\Phi^{+}\equiv 0
with vanishing ellipticity

We continue the above example but now we let the ellipticity vanish by setting
\lambda(t)=t^{a}
(or, as
\Phi^{+}=\Phi^{-}=0
, it is equivalent to
\Lambda(t)=t^{-a}
) for some
a\in(0,1)
.
According to (
2.5
) we thus allow for equations in which ellipticity vanish everywhere on the boundary (
t=d(x,\partial\Omega)
), or just at one point (
t=d(x,w)
), when deriving a lower estimate.
For an upper estimate we need to assume (
2.6
), and since
\lambda(t)=t^{a}
is increasing, we may only allow for ellipticity to vanish at one point (
t=d(x,w)
).

With the help of Corollary
2.3
, it is sufficient to prove the intended results for classical solutions
\check{h}
and
\hat{h}
of

\displaystyle\check{h}^{\prime\prime}=\frac{n\Lambda}{rt^{a}}\check{h}^{\prime},\qquad\hat{h}^{\prime\prime}=-\frac{n\Lambda}{rt^{a}}\hat{h}^{\prime},\quad 0<t<r.

If
0<a<1
then

\log{\check{h}^{\prime}(t)}-\log{\check{h}^{\prime}(0)}=-K\frac{t^{1-a}}{1-a}

\check{h}^{\prime}(t)=\check{h}^{\prime}(0)e^{-K\frac{t^{1-a}}{1-a}}

\check{h}(t)=\check{h}^{\prime}(0)\int_{0}^{t}e^{-K\frac{s^{1-a}}{1-a}}ds+\check{h}(0)

so that with
\check{h}(0)=0=\hat{h}(0)
,
i=1,2
, we have

\check{h}(t)=\check{h}^{\prime}(0)\int_{0}^{t}e^{\frac{K}{1-a}s^{1-a}}ds,\qquad\hat{h}(t)=\hat{h}^{\prime}(0)\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\frac{K}{1-a}s^{1-a}}ds,

and with
\check{h}(r)=m:=\inf_{\Gamma_{w,r}}u_{i}
and
\hat{h}(r)=M:=\sup_{B(w,6r)\cap\Omega}u_{i}
,
i=1,2
, we obtain the increasing solutions

\check{h}(t)=m\;\frac{\int_{0}^{t}e^{\frac{K}{1-a}s^{1-a}}ds}{\int_{0}^{r}e^{\frac{K}{1-a}s^{1-a}}ds},\qquad\hat{h}(t)=M\;\frac{\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\frac{K}{1-a}s^{1-a}}ds}{\int_{0}^{r}e^{-\frac{K}{1-a}s^{1-a}}ds}.

Estimating the integrals gives

e^{-\frac{\Lambda n}{(1-a)r^{a}}}\frac{mt}{r}\leq\frac{mt}{\int_{0}^{r}e^{\frac{K}{1-a}s^{1-a}}ds}\leq\check{h}(t)\leq\hat{h}(t)\leq\frac{Mt}{\int_{0}^{r}e^{-\frac{K}{1-a}s^{1-a}}ds}\leq e^{\frac{\Lambda n}{(1-a)r^{a}}}\frac{Mt}{r}

for
t\in[0,r]
, so that Corollary
2.3
gives

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{\Lambda n}{(1-a)r^{a}}}\frac{md(x,\partial\Omega)}{r}\leq u_{i}(x)\leq e^{\frac{\Lambda n}{(1-a)r^{a}}}\frac{Md(x,\partial\Omega)}{r}\quad

for
x\in\Omega\cap B(w,r),i=1,2
.
Furthermore,

1\leq\frac{\hat{h}(t)}{\check{h}(t)}\leq\frac{M}{m}e^{\frac{2\Lambda n}{(1-a)r^{a}}}\quad\text{whenever}\quad t\in(0,r)

and hence we again derive the boundary Harnack inequality

e^{-\frac{2\Lambda n}{(1-a)r^{a}}}\frac{m}{M}\leq\frac{u_{1}(x)}{u_{2}(x)}\leq\frac{M}{m}e^{\frac{2\Lambda n}{(1-a)r^{a}}}\quad\text{whenever}\quad x\in\Omega\cap B(w,r).

The fact that constants blow up as
r\to 0
is not surprising, since then the ellipticity vanishes.

2.2.3
The case
\Phi^{-}\equiv-1,\Phi^{+}\equiv 1
with uniform ellipticity

In this case we consider solutions of (
2.1
)
with
F
satisfying

\displaystyle-1+\mathcal{P}^{-}_{\lambda,\Lambda}(X)\leq F(x,s,p,X)\leq 1+\mathcal{P}^{+}_{\lambda,\Lambda}(X)

(2.18)

whenever
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},s\in\mathbb{R}_{+},p\in\mathbb{R}^{n},X\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
, for some constants
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda
.
In what follows, let
\Omega,r_{0},w,r,u_{1},u_{2}
and
\Gamma_{w,r}
be as in Corollary
2.3
and put
K=\frac{\Lambda n}{\lambda r}
. According to our machinery, it suffices to prove the intended results for solutions of the ODIs

\displaystyle\check{h}^{\prime\prime}=\frac{1}{\lambda}+K\check{h}^{\prime},\quad 0<t<r,\quad\check{h}(0)=0,\quad\check{h}(r)=m,

and

\displaystyle\hat{h}^{\prime\prime}=-\frac{1}{\lambda}-K\hat{h}^{\prime},\quad 0<t<r,\quad\hat{h}(0)=0,\quad\hat{h}(r)=M.

Standard calculations give

\displaystyle\check{h}(t)=\left(m+\frac{r^{2}}{n\Lambda}\right)\frac{e^{Kt}-1}{e^{Kr}-1}-\frac{r}{n\Lambda}t,\quad\hat{h}(t)=\left(M+\frac{r^{2}}{n\Lambda}\right)\frac{1-e^{-Kt}}{1-e^{-Kr}}-\frac{r}{n\Lambda}t,

and concerning monotonicity, we have

\displaystyle\check{h}^{\prime}(t)>0\Leftrightarrow\frac{m}{r^{2}}>\alpha,\quad\hat{h}^{\prime}(t)>0\Leftrightarrow\frac{M}{r^{2}}>\alpha:=\frac{\lambda}{(\Lambda n)^{2}}\left(e^{\frac{n\Lambda}{\lambda}}-1\right)-\frac{1}{\Lambda n},

(2.19)

whenever
t\in[0,r]
.
Since we have the freedom to choose any
M\geq\sup_{B(w,6r)\cap\Omega}u_{i}
,
i=1,2
,
the condition on
\hat{h}
in (
2.19
) will never violate the existence of an upper bound.
However, we are obliged to
m\leq\inf_{\Gamma_{w,r}}u_{i}
,
i=1,2
, and therefore we obtain a lower bound only if
\frac{\inf_{\Gamma_{w,r}}}{r^{2}}>\alpha
.
To summarize, Theorem
2.1
gives the following, for
i=1,2
:

\displaystyle\text{If}\quad\frac{\inf_{\Gamma_{w,r}}}{r^{2}}>\alpha\quad\text{then}\quad\check{h}^{\prime}(0)d(x,\partial\Omega)\leq u_{i}(x)\quad\text{whenever}\quad x\in\Omega\cap B(w,r).

Moreover, Theorem
2.2
gives, for
i=1,2
,

\displaystyle u_{i}(x)\lesssim d(x,\partial\Omega)\quad\text{whenever}\quad x\in\Omega\cap B(w,r),

and, furthermore, since

\text{if}\quad\frac{\inf_{\Gamma_{w,r}}}{r^{2}}>\alpha\quad\text{then}\quad 1\leq\frac{\hat{h}(t)}{\check{h}(t)}\lesssim 1\quad\text{whenever}\quad t\in(0,r),

Corollary
2.3
implies the following boundary Harnack inequality:

\text{If}\quad\frac{\inf_{\Gamma_{w,r}}}{r^{2}}>\alpha\quad\text{then}\quad\frac{u_{1}(x)}{u_{2}(x)}\approx 1\quad\text{whenever}\quad x\in\Omega\cap B(w,r).

We remark that the extra condition on the lower estimate
can easily be justified by considering the classical problem

\displaystyle\Delta u=1\quad\text{in}\quad\{x\in\mathbf{R}^{n}:x_{n}>0\}\quad\text{with}\quad u(x^{\prime},0)=0.

Since for any
r>0
the function

\displaystyle u(x)=\frac{x_{n}^{2}}{2}+\left(\frac{u(r)}{r}-\frac{r}{2}\right)x_{n}

solves the problem, the decay estimates should switch behavior as the factor of
x_{n}
switches sign.
In particular, if
\frac{u(r)}{r^{2}}>\frac{1}{2}
then
u(x)\approx x_{n}
near the boundary
x_{n}=0
,
but otherwise such lower estimate obviously fails.
We also remark that -1 and 1 as the upper and lower bound in (
2.18
) can be switched to constants without changing the nature of the above conclusions,
and for the general class of equations considered in this example, the theorems on lower decay estimates in
[
34
, Section 5]
will not apply due to assumption
(\phi_{A})
in that paper.

2.2.4
The case
\Phi^{-}(t,s,|p|)=-|p|^{k}
,
k\geq 1
, with uniform ellipticity

In this case we consider solutions of (
2.1
)
with
F
satisfying

\displaystyle-|p|^{k}+\mathcal{P}^{-}_{\lambda,\Lambda}(X)\leq F(x,s,p,X)

whenever
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},s\in\mathbb{R}_{+},p\in\mathbb{R}^{n},X\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
, for some constants
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda
.
Since
F
is unbounded from above a lower estimate is not possible.
If
k\leq 2
then we know from
[
34
]
that an upper estimate
u\lesssim d(x,\partial\Omega)
holds in general, but otherwise, a counterexample in
[
34
, Section 5]
disproves an upper estimate in the general case.
Thus, it remains to investigate if there are situations in which it is possible to derive an upper estimate also for
k>2
.
Let
\Omega,r_{e},w,r
and
u
be as in Theorem
2.2
, introduce
\hat{f}=\hat{h}^{\prime}
and
K=\frac{n\Lambda}{r\lambda}
.
To prove an upper estimate, we take off from (
2.8
) and solve the initial value problem

\frac{d\hat{f}}{dt}=-\frac{\hat{f}^{k}}{\lambda}-K\hat{f},\quad t\in(0,r)\quad\text{with}\quad\hat{f}(0)=\nu>0,

yielding the solution (see
[
33
, Section 3.2]
for a derivation),

\hat{f}_{\nu}(t)=\left(\frac{\lambda K}{e^{(k-1)Kt}\left(\frac{\lambda{K}}{\nu^{k-1}}+1\right)-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}.

Now,
\hat{f}_{\nu}
is decreasing so that for any
\nu>0
,

\hat{h}_{\nu}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\hat{f}_{\nu}(s)ds

is concave and, furthermore,
\hat{h}_{\nu}(r)
is increasing in
\nu
.
Thus, as long as there is
\nu>0
so that
\hat{h}_{\nu}(r)\geq\sup_{B(w,6r)\cap\Omega}u
we obtain an upper estimate. This means that we obtain an estimate only when the subsolution
u
is not too large a small distance from the boundary. In particular, we have derived the following:

\displaystyle\text{If}\quad\hat{h}_{\nu}(r)\,\geq\sup_{B(w,6r)\cap\Omega}u\quad\text{then}\quad u(x)\leq\hat{h}_{\nu}(d(x,\partial\Omega))\leq\nu d(x,\partial\Omega)

(2.20)

whenever
x\in\Omega\cap B(w,r)
.

Next, we observe that
\hat{f}_{\nu}
and
\hat{h}_{\nu}
are maximized when
\nu\to\infty
, and

\hat{f}_{\nu}(t)\to\hat{f}_{\infty}(t)=\left(\frac{\lambda K}{e^{(k-1)Kt}-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{k-1}},

and for
0<\epsilon\leq t\leq r
we have

\left(\frac{\lambda Kr}{e^{(k-1)Kr}-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}\int_{\epsilon}^{t}{s^{\frac{1}{1-k}}}ds\leq\hat{h}_{\infty}(t)\leq\left(\frac{\lambda}{k-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}\int_{\epsilon}^{t}{s^{\frac{1}{1-k}}}ds.

Thus, for
0\leq t\leq r
,

\check{c}\,t^{\frac{k-2}{k-1}}\leq\hat{h}_{\infty}(t)\leq\hat{c}\,t^{\frac{k-2}{k-1}},

with

\displaystyle\check{c}=\frac{k-1}{k-2}\left(\frac{n\Lambda}{e^{(k-1)\frac{n\Lambda}{\lambda}}-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{k-1}},\quad\hat{c}=\frac{k-1}{k-2}\left(\frac{\lambda}{k-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}.

(2.21)

Therefore, we obtain the following upper estimate:

\displaystyle\text{If}\quad\check{c}\,d(x,\partial\Omega)^{\frac{k-2}{k-1}}\,\geq\sup_{B(w,6r)\cap\Omega}u\quad\text{then}\quad u\leq\hat{c}\,d(x,\partial\Omega)^{\frac{k-2}{k-1}}

(2.22)

whenever
x\in\Omega\cap B(w,r)
and where
\check{c}
and
\hat{c}
are as in (
2.21
).
In conclusion, estimates (
2.20
) reveals that, when
\sup_{B(w,6r)\cap\Omega}u
is small, then subsolutions decay no slower than proportional to the distance function, but the constant of proportion (
\nu
) explodes as
\sup_{B(w,6r)\cap\Omega}u
increases.
Estimate (
2.22
) reveals a power-law upper bound with constant independent of
\sup_{B(w,6r)\cap\Omega}u
.

To remark on the sharpness of these results,
let
r>0
and
k>2
be given
and consider the following boundary value problem

\displaystyle u^{\prime\prime}(x)=-(u^{\prime}(x))^{k},\quad x\in(0,r),\quad u(0)=0,\quad u(r)=M>0.

(2.23)

Standard calculations show that the function

w_{\gamma}(x)=\frac{(k-1)^{\frac{k-2}{k-1}}}{k-2}\left(\left(x+\gamma\right)^{\frac{k-2}{k-1}}-\gamma^{\frac{k-2}{k-1}}\right)

is a classical solution,
where
\gamma\geq 0
is to be determined via
u(r)=M
.
We observe that when
x
approaches the boundary at
x=0
, then the concave solution
w_{\gamma}
decays proportionally to
x
, but with constant exploding as
\gamma\to 0
. This is in line with estimate (
2.20
).
Moreover, when
w_{\gamma}
is maximized for
\gamma=0
we obtain the power-law decaying solution with
exponent
\frac{k-2}{k-1}
, which agrees with the bound in (
2.22
).

3
Hölder continuity of
u/v
near the boundary

In this section, we study the conditions under which the quotient
u/v
, for which we established bounds in the previous section, is also Hölder continuous near the boundary.
We first demonstrate, in Section
3.1
, that global
C^{1,\alpha}
-estimates combined with suitable boundary growth estimates yield the desired continuity.
Secondly, we apply the results to a class of fully nonlinear PDEs for which global
C^{1,\alpha}
is available (Section
3.2
). In Section
3.3
and Section
3.4
we consider reflection arguments and local
C^{1,\alpha}
-estimates to obtain the similar estimates for
p(x)
-harmonic functions and for planar infinity-harmonic functions near locally flat boundaries.
Finally, in Section
3.5
we derive some corollaries of
u_{1}/u_{2}\in C^{\alpha}
-estimates in unbounded domains.

3.1
Basic lemmas

We begin by some basic lemmas for a differentiable function
f
vanishing on the boundary of a domain satisfying the interior ball condition.
By differentiability of the distance function
d(x,\Omega)
near the boundary, the quotient
f/d(x,\Omega)
becomes differentiable. The following Lemma shows that if the gradient of
f
is Hölder continuous, then so is the quotient
f/d(x,\Omega)
.

Lemma 3.1

Let
\Omega
satisfy the interior ball condition with radius
r_{0}
,
r\in(0,r_{0}]
,
z\in\partial\Omega
,
f\in C^{1,\alpha_{K}}
so that

\displaystyle|\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)|\leq c_{K}|x-y|^{\alpha_{K}}

(3.1)

whenever
x,y\in B(z,r)\cap\Omega
, and
f=0
on
B(z,r)\cap\partial\Omega
.
Then it holds that

\left|\frac{f(x)}{d(x,\Omega)}-\frac{f(y)}{d(y,\Omega)}\right|\leq 8c_{K}|x-y|^{\alpha_{K}},

whenever
x,y\in B(z,r/c)\cap\Omega
, where
c
depends only on
r_{0}
.

Proof.
Fix arbitrary
(x,y)\in B(z,r/c)\cap\Omega
for some
c\geq 1
to be increase later, and assume w.l.o.g. that
d(x,\partial\Omega)>d(y,\partial\Omega)
.
Let
y_{0}
be such that
d(y,y_{0})=d(y,\partial\Omega)
and define
w=d(x,\partial\Omega)\frac{y-y_{0}}{d(y,\partial\Omega)}
.
Uniqueness of
y_{0}
follows from the
C^{1,1}
regularity of the boundary, for
c
large enough.
We will show the required estimate for

\left|\frac{f(x)}{d(x,\partial\Omega)}-\frac{f(w)}{d(w,\partial\Omega)}\right|\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\left|\frac{f(w)}{d(w,\partial\Omega)}-\frac{f(y)}{(y,\partial\Omega)}\right|

separately, then the results follows from the triangle inequality.
Starting with the former term, (
3.1
) and the interior ball condition of the domain yield

\left|\frac{f(x)}{d(x,\partial\Omega)}-\frac{f(w)}{d(w,\partial\Omega)}\right|\leq\frac{1}{d(x,\partial\Omega)}\int_{0}^{d(x,\partial\Omega)}\left|\nabla f(t\eta_{x})-\nabla f(t\eta_{y})\right|\,dt\\[10.0pt]
\leq\frac{1}{d(x,\partial\Omega)}\int_{0}^{d(x,\partial\Omega)}c_{K}\left|t\eta_{x}-t\eta_{y}\right|^{\alpha_{K}}\,dt\\[10.0pt]
\leq\frac{1}{d(x,\partial\Omega)}\int_{0}^{d(x,\partial\Omega)}2c_{K}\left|x-w\right|^{\alpha_{K}}\,dt\leq 4c_{K}\left|x-y\right|^{\alpha_{K}},

(3.2)

for
c
large enough, depending only on
r_{0}
.
Here,
c_{K}
and
\alpha_{K}
are the constants from (
3.1
) with
K=\overline{B(z,r/2)\cap\Omega}
, and
in the last line we have used the interior ball condition for
\Omega
twice, thus we can choose
c
such that the ball
B(z,r/c)
is small enough to guarantee
|t\eta_{x}-t\eta_{y}|\leq 2|w-x|\leq 4|x-y|
.

For the second term the mean value theorem of vector calculus and the
C^{1,1}
regularity of the boundary gives

\left|\frac{f(w)}{d(w,\partial\Omega)}-\frac{f(y)}{d(y,\partial\Omega)}\right|\leq\left|\max_{t\in L}\frac{\nabla f(t)d(t,\partial\Omega)-f(t)\nabla d(t,\partial\Omega)}{d(t,\partial\Omega)^{2}}\cdot(w-y)\right|,

where
L
is the straight line between
y
and
w
.
Since
u(y_{0})=0
the mean value theorem implies the existence of

\xi\in[y_{0},t]
such that
f(t)=\nabla f(\xi)\cdot(t-y_{0})
, hence

\displaystyle\left|\frac{f(w)}{d(w,\partial\Omega)}-\frac{f(y)}{d(y,\partial\Omega)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\max_{t\in L}\frac{\nabla f(t)d(t,y_{0})-\left(\nabla f(\xi)\cdot(t-y_{0})\right)\nabla d(t,y_{0})}{d(t,y_{0})^{2}}\cdot(w-y)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\max_{t\in L}\frac{|w-y|\nabla f(t)\cdot(t-y_{0})-|w-y|\nabla f(\xi)\cdot(t-y_{0})}{d(t,y_{0})^{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\max_{t\in L}\frac{|w-y|\cdot|\nabla f(t)-\nabla f(\xi)|}{d(t,y_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{t\in L}c_{K}\frac{|t-\xi|^{\alpha_{K}}}{|t-y_{0}|}|w-y|.

If
\frac{|w-y_{0}|}{2}\leq|y-y_{0}|
then
|w-y|\leq|y-y_{0}|\leq|t-y_{0}|
, and we always have
|t-\xi|\leq|t-y_{0}|
, hence

\left|\frac{f(w)}{d(x,\partial\Omega)}-\frac{f(y)}{d(y,\partial\Omega)}\right|\leq\max_{t\in L}c_{K}\frac{|w-y|}{|t-y_{0}|^{1-\alpha_{K}}}\leq c_{K}|w-y|^{\alpha_{K}}\leq 2c_{K}|x-y|^{\alpha_{K}},

where in the last step we have used
|w-y|\leq 2|x-y|
for
c
large enough.

If
|y-y_{0}|<\frac{|w-y_{0}|}{2}
we can immediately
use the mean value theorem (
f(y_{0})=0
for all
y_{0}\in\partial\Omega
), thus there exists
\xi\in L_{1}
and
\eta\in L_{2}
(
L_{1}
is the straight line between
y_{0}
and
w
and
L_{2}
is the straight line between
y_{0}
and
y
) such that

\left|\frac{f(w)}{d(w,y_{0})}-\frac{f(y)}{d(y,y_{0})}\right|=\left|\frac{(w-y_{0})\cdot\nabla f(\xi)}{d(w,y_{0})}-\frac{(y-y_{0})\cdot\nabla f(\eta)}{d(y,y_{0})}\right|\\[10.0pt]
=|\left[\nabla f(\xi)-\nabla f(\eta)\right]\cdot\eta_{y}|\leq|\nabla f(\xi)-\nabla f(\eta)||\eta_{y}|\leq c_{K}|\xi-\eta|^{\alpha_{K}}.

Since
|w-y|\geq\frac{|w-y_{0}|}{2}
we have
|\xi-\eta|^{\alpha}\leq 2^{\alpha}\left(\frac{|w-y_{0}|}{2}\right)^{\alpha}\leq 2^{\alpha}|w-y|^{\alpha}
, hence

\left|\frac{f(w)}{d(w,y_{0})}-\frac{f(y)}{d(y,y_{0})}\right|\leq 2^{\alpha_{K}}c_{K}|w-y|^{\alpha_{K}}\leq 2c_{K}|w-y|^{\alpha_{K}}\leq 4c_{K}|x-y|^{\alpha_{K}},

(3.3)

where we have used the interior ball condition for
\Omega
and therefore
|w-y|\leq 2|x-y|
for
c
large enough.
Now the estimates (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) imply

\left|\frac{f(x)}{d(x,\partial\Omega)}-\frac{f(y)}{d(y,\partial\Omega)}\right|\leq\left|\frac{f(x)}{d(x,\partial\Omega)}-\frac{f(w)}{d(w,\partial\Omega)}\right|+\left|\frac{f(w)}{d(w,\partial\Omega)}-\frac{f(y)}{d(y,\partial\Omega)}\right|\\[10.0pt]
\leq 4c_{K}|x-y|^{\alpha_{K}}+4c_{K}|x-y|^{\alpha_{K}}=8c_{K}|x-y|^{\alpha_{K}},

which completes the proof.

The growth estimate (
3.4
) below, together with Lemma
3.1
, readily implies the following simple lemma:

Lemma 3.2

Let
\Omega
,
r_{0}
,
r
,
z
and
f
be as in Lemma
3.1
and
assume that
g
satisfies the same assumptions as
f
and in addition

\displaystyle C_{L}\leq\frac{g(x)}{d(x,\partial\Omega)}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{f(x)}{d(x,\partial\Omega)}\leq C_{U}

(3.4)

whenever
x,y\in\Omega\cap B(z,r)
.
Then it holds that

\left|\frac{d(x,\partial\Omega)}{g(x)}-\frac{d(y,\partial\Omega)}{g(y)}\right|\leq c_{1}|x-y|^{\alpha_{K}}\quad\text{and}\quad\left|\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}-\frac{f(y)}{g(y)}\right|\leq c_{2}|x-y|^{\alpha_{K}}

whenever
x,y\in B(z,r/c)\cap\Omega
, and where
c_{1}=8c_{K}c_{L}^{-2}
and
c_{2}=8c_{K}(c_{L}+c_{U})/c_{L}^{2}
.

Proof.
Let
x,y\in B(z,r/c)\cap\Omega
. Then from Lemma
3.1
and inequality (
3.4
) we can deduce

\left|\frac{d(x,\partial\Omega)}{g(x)}-\frac{d(y,\partial\Omega)}{g(y)}\right|=\frac{d(x,\partial\Omega)\,d(y,\partial\Omega)}{g(x)g(y)}\left|\frac{g(y)}{d(y,\partial\Omega)}-\frac{g(x)}{d(x,\partial\Omega)}\right|\\[10.0pt]
\leq 8c_{K}\frac{d(x,\partial\Omega)\,d(y,\partial\Omega)}{g(x)g(y)}\left|x-y\right|^{\alpha_{K}}\leq 8c_{K}c_{L}^{-2}|x-y|^{\alpha_{K}}.

(3.5)

For the second part of the lemma we make use of Lemma
3.1
, estimate (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) to see that

\left|\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}-\frac{f(y)}{g(y)}\right|\leq\frac{d(x,\partial\Omega)}{g(x)}\left|\frac{f(x)}{d(x,\partial\Omega)}-\frac{f(y)}{d(y,\partial\Omega)}\right|+\frac{f(y)}{d(y,\partial\Omega)}\left|\frac{d(x,\partial\Omega)}{g(x)}-\frac{d(y,\partial\Omega)}{g(y)}\right|\\[10.0pt]
\leq 8c_{K}c_{L}^{-1}|x-y|^{\alpha_{K}}+8c_{K}c_{U}c_{L}^{-2}|x-y|^{\alpha_{K}}=c|x-y|^{\alpha_{K}},

where
c=8c_{K}(c_{L}+c_{U})/c_{L}^{2}
.

3.2
Fully nonlinear equations

We now return to fully nonlinear elliptic equations in nondivergence form, as in (
2.1
).
To ensure global
C^{1,\alpha}
we assume here, in addition to the assumptions in Corollary
2.3
, that

F(x,0,0,0)\equiv 0
, and that the following structural assumptions hold:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{-}_{\lambda,\Lambda}(X-Y)-\mu|p-q|(1+|p|+|q|)-\omega(|r-s|)

\displaystyle\leq F(x,r,p,X)-F(x,s,q,Y)

(3.6)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{P}^{+}_{\lambda,\Lambda}(X-Y)+\mu|p-q|(1+|p|+|q|)+\omega(|r-s|),

\displaystyle x\in\Omega,\;r,s\in\mathbb{R},\;p,q\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\;X,Y\in\mathbb{S}^{n},

where
0<\lambda<\Lambda
,
\nu\geq 0
, and
\omega:[0,\infty]\to[0,\infty]
,
\omega(0)=0
is a modulus of continuity satisfying

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dt}{\omega(t)}=\infty.

(3.7)

The following boundary
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity result is from
[
36
]
and is originally stated in a more general and precise form for
L^{p}
-viscosity solutions:

Lemma 3.3

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a
C^{1,1}
-domain,
F
satisfy (
3.2
),
w\in\partial\Omega
and let
u
be a viscosity solution of (
2.1
) in
B(w,r)\cap\Omega
, continuous in
\overline{\Omega}\cap B(w,r)
satisfying
u=0
on
\partial\Omega\cap B(w,r)
.
Then there exist
c
and
\alpha\in(0,1]
such that

|Du(x)-Du(y)|\leq c|x-y|^{\alpha}

whenever
x,y\in B(w,r/c)\cap\Omega
.

Proof.
Observing that assumption (
3.2
) immediately implies the more general structure assumption in
[
36
]
and
that bounded viscosity solutions are
L^{p}
-viscosity solutions, this follows from
[
36
, Theorem 1.1]
.

We now apply our estimates from the previous section–specifically Corollary
2.3
–to show that assumption (
3.2
) also implies the desired boundary growth rate.

Lemma 3.4

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

be a
C^{1,1}
-domain,
F
satisfy (
3.2
) and
w\in\partial\Omega
. Assume that
u_{1},u_{2}
are positive viscosity solutions of (
2.1
) in
\Omega\cap B(w,6r)
, continuous in
\overline{\Omega}\cap B(w,6r)

satisfying
u_{1}=0=u_{2}
on
\partial\Omega\cap B(w,6r)
.
Then there exists
c
such that

c^{-1}{d(x,\partial\Omega)}\leq u_{i}(x)\leq cd(x,\partial\Omega),\quad\text{and}\quad c^{-1}\leq\frac{u_{1}(x)}{u_{2}(x)}\leq c,

whenever
x\in\Omega\cap B(w,r)
and
i=1,2.

Proof.
For a lower bound we first note that (
3.2
) implies

\displaystyle F(x,s,p,X)\leq P^{+}_{\lambda,\Lambda}(X)+\mu(|p|+|p|^{2})+\omega(s)

whenever
s\in[0,\infty),x,p\in\mathbb{R}^{n},X\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
.
Since
\omega
satisfies (
3.7
) we deduce
\omega(s)\leq c\sqrt{s}
for
s\in[0,1]
and hence, according to Corollary
2.3
it suffices to find a lower bound on an increasing classical solution of

\displaystyle\check{h}^{\prime\prime}\geq\left(\frac{\mu}{\lambda}+K\right)\check{h}^{\prime}+\frac{\mu}{\lambda}\check{h}^{\prime 2}+\frac{c}{\lambda}h\quad 0<t<r,\quad\check{h}(0)=0,\quad\check{h}(r)\leq m,

where
K=\frac{n\Lambda}{r\lambda}
.
Standard calculations show that for
A
large,
and
m\leq\inf_{\Gamma_{w,r}}u_{i}
,
i=1,2
, small enough, the function

\displaystyle\check{h}(t)=m\frac{e^{At}-1}{e^{Ar}-1}

obeys the desired properties.
Clearly, there is
c
such that
\check{h}(t)\geq c^{-1}t
on
[0,r]
.

To establish an upper bound we observe that (
3.2
),

\omega(0)=0
, and (
2.2
) imply

P^{-}_{\lambda,\Lambda}(X)-\mu(|p|+|p|^{2})\leq F(x,0,p,X),

whenever
x,p\in\mathbb{R}^{n},X\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
.
According to Corollary
2.3
it suffices to find an upper bound on an increasing classical solution of

\displaystyle\hat{h}^{\prime\prime}\leq-\left(\frac{\mu}{\lambda}+K\right)\hat{h}^{\prime}-\frac{\mu}{\lambda}\hat{h}^{\prime 2}\quad 0<t<r,\quad\hat{h}(0)=0,\quad\hat{h}(r)\geq\sup_{B(w,6r)\cap\Omega}u_{i},

(3.8)

i=1,2
, where
K=\frac{n\Lambda}{r\lambda}
.
Let
\hat{f}=\hat{h}^{\prime}
and observe that then

\hat{f}(t)=\frac{\lambda(\mu+K)}{\left(\frac{\lambda{(\mu+K)}}{\nu}e^{(\mu+K)t}+1\right)-1}

solves the ODI in (
3.8
) with equality on
0<t<r
and satisfies
\hat{f}(0)=\nu
.
Moreover,
\hat{f}>0
is decreasing so that

\hat{h}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\hat{f}(s)ds

is increasing, concave, and it is not hard to see that
\hat{h}(r)\to\infty
as
\nu\to\infty
.
Therefore, taking
\nu
large enough,

\hat{h}
satisfies (
3.8
),
and in addition
\hat{h}(t)\leq\nu t
on
[0,r]
.

Armed with the above solutions
\check{h}
and
\hat{h}
, and the bounds of them on
[0,r]
, the lemma follows by an application of Corollary
2.3
.

We are now ready to state and prove that the ratio of two positive viscosity solutions, vanishing continuously on a portion of the boundary, is Hölder continuous.

Theorem 3.5

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a
C^{1,1}
-domain,

F
satisfy (
3.2
), and
w\in\partial\Omega
.
Assume that
u
and
v
are positive viscosity solution of (
2.1
) in
\Omega\cap B(w,r)
, continuous in
\overline{\Omega}\cap B(w,r)

satisfying
u=0=v
on
\partial\Omega\cap B(w,r)
.
Then there exists
c
such that

\left|\frac{u(x)}{v(x)}-\frac{u(y)}{v(y)}\right|\leq c|x-y|^{\alpha}

whenever
x,y\in B(w,r/c)\cap\Omega
and where
\alpha
is from Lemma
3.3
.
Moreover, the result holds with
u
or
v
replaced by
d(x,\partial\Omega)
as well.

Proof.
The result is an immediate consequence of Lemmas
3.1
,
3.2
,
3.3
and
3.4
.

We remark that the constants in Lemma
3.3
, Lemma
3.4
and Theorem
3.5
may comprise complicated dependence on the ingoing parameters. To avoid technicalities, we have chosen to not go into such details in this general setting.

Even though the class of equations considered here is rather general, it does not cover the
\infty
-Laplace equation (which fails to be elliptic in the sense of (
3.2
)) nor the variable exponent
p
-Laplace equation (which fails due to a non-Lipschitz dependence on the gradient).
For both equations, the boundary growth estimates,
u\approx d
,
is well known, but the
C^{1,\alpha}
estimates are, to the best of our knowledge, only established in the interior of the domain, and only in planar domains for the
\infty
-Laplace equation.
However, we can circumvent the lack of boundary regularity by applying a Schwarz reflection argument if the boundary is locally flat. In the following subsections we will do so and thereby prove versions of Theorem
3.5
for
\infty
-harmonic functions and for
p(x)
-harmonic functions.

3.3
The
p(x)
-Laplace equation

In this section we derive the analogue of Theorem
3.5
for solutions of the
p
-Laplace equation with a variable exponent, that is,

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\left(|\nabla u|^{p(x)-2}\nabla u\right)=0

(3.9)

in which
p:\Omega\to(1,\infty)
,
1<p^{-}\leq p(x)\leq p^{+}<\infty
is a continuous function called a
variable exponent
.
We consider solutions in the following standard weak sense.

Definition 3.6

A function
u\in W^{1,\,p(x)}_{loc}(\Omega)
is a weak subsolution (supersolution) in
\Omega
if

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p(x)-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi\,dx\leq(\geq)0

(3.10)

for all nonnegative
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
.

A function that is both a weak subsolution and a weak supersolution is called a weak solution of the
p(x)
-Laplace equation (
3.9
).
A continuous weak solution is called a
p(x)
-harmonic function
.
In the above,
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
denotes infinitely differentiable functions with compact support in
\Omega
,

W^{1,\,p(x)}
is the
variable exponent Sobolev space
which consists of all functions
u\in L^{p(x)}(\Omega)
whose weak gradient
\nabla u\in L^{p(x)}(\Omega)
.
For these and further details concerning variable exponent
p
-Laplace equation, see e.g.
[
16
]
.

The boundary Harnack inequality in

C^{1,1}
-domains is by now well established for

p(x)
-harmonic functions; see, for example,
[
2
,
8
,
34
]
.
In fact, the results in these works ensure that Lemma
3.4
remains valid when

u_{1}
and
u_{2}
are assumed to be positive

p(x)
-harmonic functions.
It is worth noting that the boundary Harnack inequality can also be derived via Corollary
2.3
, by first rewriting (
3.9
) in the form (
2.1
), as done in
[
34
, Section 6]
, and then analyzing the resulting ODIs following the approach in
[
33
, Section 3.3]
.

To establish regularity of the quotient of
p(x)
-harmonic functions we will also use the following
C^{1,\alpha}
estimate from
[
1
]
.

Lemma 3.7

Let
r>0
,
p
be a bounded Lipschitz continuous variable exponent function on
B(0,R)
and let
u
be a
p(x)
-harmonic functions in
B(0,r)
.
Then there exist
c>0
and
\alpha\in(0,1]
such that

|\nabla u(x)-\nabla u(y)|\leq c|x-y|^{\alpha},\quad\forall(x,y)\in B(0,r/c).

Proof.
This follows from
[
1
, Theorem 2.2]
.

Since the
C^{1,\alpha}
estimate for
p(x)
-harmonic functions appears to be known only in the interior,
we may not immediately apply Lemma
3.2
to obtain
u/v\in C^{\alpha}
near
C^{1,1}
-boundaries.
To be able to apply the interior
C^{1,\alpha}
estimate over the boundary, we assume that the domain is a half space and extend the
p(x)
harmonic functions to the whole space via reflection.
For
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
r\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and

w=(w_{1},...,w_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we define the rectangles

\displaystyle\begin{split}Q_{r}(w)&=\{y:|y_{i}-w_{i}|<r,\,i\in\{1,...,n\}\},\\
Q^{+}_{r}(w)&=\{y:|y_{i}-w_{i}|<r,\,i\in\{1,...,n-1\},\,0<y_{n}-w_{n}<r\}.\end{split}

(3.11)

We will make use of the following Schwarz reflection principle.

Lemma 3.8

Suppose, for some
w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
r>0
, that
u
is
p(x)
-harmonic in
Q_{r}^{+}(w)
,
continuous on
\overline{Q}_{r}^{+}(w)
with
u(x^{\prime},0)=0
.
Define

\tilde{u}(x^{\prime},x_{n})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u(x^{\prime},x_{n}),\quad\,x_{n}\geq 0\\[24.0pt]
-u(x^{\prime},-x_{n}),\quad\,x_{n}<0,\end{array}\right.\qquad\tilde{p}(x^{\prime},x_{n})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}p(x^{\prime},x_{n}),\quad\,x_{n}\geq 0\\[24.0pt]
p(x^{\prime},-x_{n}),\quad\,x_{n}<0.\end{array}\right.

Then
\tilde{u}
is
\tilde{p}(x)
-harmonic in
Q_{r}(w)
.

Proof.
Let
\theta\in C^{\infty}_{0}\left(Q_{r}(w)\right)
be an arbitrary test function and write
\theta=\phi+\psi
where

\phi(x)=\frac{\theta(x^{\prime},x_{n})+\theta(x^{\prime},-x_{n})}{2}.

It follows that
\psi\in W^{1\,p(x)}_{0}\left(Q_{r}(w)\right)
and also
\psi(x^{\prime},0)=0
,
so that
\psi\in W^{1,\,p(x)}_{0}(Q_{r}^{+}(w))
.
We have

\int_{Q_{r}(w)}|\nabla\tilde{u}|^{\tilde{p}(x)-2}\,\nabla\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla\psi\,dx=\int_{Q_{r}^{+}(w)}|\nabla u|^{p(x)-2}\,\nabla u\cdot\nabla\psi\,dx\\
+\int_{Q_{r}(w)\setminus Q_{r}^{+}(w)}|\nabla(-u(x^{\prime},-x_{n}))|^{p(x^{\prime},-x_{n})-2}\,\langle\nabla(-u(x^{\prime},-x_{n}))\cdot\nabla\psi\rangle\,dx\\
=-\int_{Q_{r}^{+}(w)}|\nabla u|^{p(x^{\prime},x_{n})-2}\,\langle\nabla u(x^{\prime},x_{n})\cdot\nabla\psi(x^{\prime},-x_{n})\rangle\,dx=0,

(3.12)

since
\psi(x^{\prime},-x_{n})\in W^{1,\,p(x)}_{0}(Q_{r}^{+}(w))
.
We also have

\int_{Q_{r}(w)}|\nabla\tilde{u}|^{\tilde{p}(x)-2}\,\nabla\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla\phi\,dx=\int_{Q_{r}^{+}(w)}|\nabla u|^{p(x)-2}\,\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi\,dx\\
+\int_{Q_{r}(w)\setminus Q_{r}^{+}(w)}|\nabla(-u(x^{\prime},-x_{n}))|^{p(x^{\prime},-x_{n})-2}\,\langle\nabla(-u(x^{\prime},-x_{n}))\cdot\nabla\phi\rangle\,dx\\
=\int_{Q_{r}^{+}(w)}|\nabla u|^{p(x)-2}\,\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi\,dx\\
-\int_{Q_{r}^{+}(w)}|\nabla u|^{p(x^{\prime},x_{n})-2}\,\langle\nabla u(x^{\prime},x_{n})\cdot\nabla\phi(x^{\prime},-x_{n})\rangle\,dx\\
=\int_{Q_{r}^{+}(w)}|\nabla u|^{p(x)-2}\,\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi\,dx\\
-\int_{Q_{r}^{+}(w)}|\nabla u|^{p(x^{\prime},x_{n})-2}\,\langle\nabla u(x^{\prime},x_{n})\cdot\nabla\phi(x^{\prime},x_{n})\rangle\,dx=0.

(3.13)

From (
3.12
) and (
3.13
) it follows that

\int_{Q_{r}(w)}|\nabla\tilde{u}|^{\tilde{p}(x)-2}\,\nabla\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla(\phi+\psi)\,dx=\int_{Q_{r}(w)}|\nabla\tilde{u}|^{\tilde{p}(x)-2}\,\nabla\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla\theta\,dx=0,

which completes the proof.

We are now ready to prove the following analogue of Theorem
3.5
for
p(x)
-harmonic functions.

Theorem 3.9

Let
z\in\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
and let
u,v>0
be
p(x)
-harmonic functions in
B(z,r)\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
,
continuous in
\overline{B(z,r)\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}
, with
u=0=v
on
B(z,r)\cap\{x:x_{n}=0\}
.
Then there exists
c
such that

\left|\frac{u(x)}{v(x)}-\frac{u(y)}{v(y)}\right|\leq c|x-y|^{\alpha}

whenever
x,y\in B(z,r/c)\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
and where
\alpha
is from Lemma
3.7
.
Moreover, the result holds with
u
or
v
replaced by
d(x,\partial\Omega)
as well.

Proof.
Since
u,v
are
p(x)
-harmonic in
B(z,r)\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
, then by Lemma
3.8

\tilde{u},\tilde{v}
(defined in
3.8
) are
p(x)
-harmonic in
B(z,r)\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Thus, by Lemma
3.7
there exist
c
and
\alpha\in(0,1]
such that

|\nabla\tilde{u}(x)-\nabla\tilde{u}(y)|+|\nabla\tilde{v}(x)-\nabla\tilde{v}(y)|\leq c|x-y|^{\alpha}

(3.14)

in
B(z,r/c)\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}
, wherefore
\tilde{u},\tilde{v}\in C^{1,\alpha}\left(B(z,r/c)\cap\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\right)
.
Moreover, the well known boundary Harnack inequality for
p(x)
-harmonic functions
[
2
]
gives the existence of
c
such that

c^{-1}\leq\frac{y(x)}{d(x,\partial\Omega)}\leq c,\quad\forall\,x\in B(z,r/c)

(3.15)

in which
y=u
or
y=v
.
Estimates (
3.14
) and (
3.15
) ensure the assumptions in Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.2
which therefore give the desired estimates.

We note that reflection principles for elliptic PDEs and quasiminimizers have been studied, for instance, in
[
37
,
38
]
. In addition,
C^{1,\alpha}
estimates for solutions of PDEs with variable exponents and nonstandard growth conditions are available in, e.g.,
[
43
,
46
,
9
]
.
It is therefore likely that several variants of Theorem
3.9
could be derived from Lemma
3.2
, using a combination of known

C^{1,\alpha}
estimates, reflection-based extensions, and boundary Harnack inequalities – the latter potentially provable via Corollary
2.3
.

3.4
The
\infty
-Laplace equation

In this section we derive the analogue of Theorem
3.5
for solutions of the
\infty
-Laplace equation,
that is,

\displaystyle\Delta_{\infty}u:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{j}}\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}=0,

(3.16)

which fails to be elliptic in the sense of (
2.5
) and (
2.6
).
We adopt the viscosity solution concept as recalled in Section
2
, that is, (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) with
F=-\Delta_{\infty}u
,
and we will refer to a viscosity solution of (
3.16
) as an
\infty
-harmonic function.

The boundary Harnack inequality for
\infty
-harmonic functions is by now well established in a variety of domains, see, for instance,
[
10
,
22
,
31
]
from which we easily conclude the following lemma.

Lemma 3.10

Let
w\in\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
,
r>0
and
u,v>0
be
\infty
-harmonic functions in
B(w,r)\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
,
continuous on
\overline{B(w,r)\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}
, with
u=0=v
on
B(w,r)\cap\{x:x_{n}=0\}
.
Then there exists
c
, depending only on
n
, such that

\displaystyle c^{-1}\frac{d(x,\partial\Omega)}{r}\leq\frac{u(x)}{u(a_{r}(w))}\leq c\frac{d(x,\partial\Omega)}{r}\quad\text{and}\quad c^{-1}\frac{u(a_{r}(w))}{v(a_{r}(w))}\leq\frac{u(x)}{v(x)}\leq c\frac{u(a_{r}(w))}{v(a_{r}(w))},

whenever
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\cap B(w,r/c)
and where
a_{r}(w)=w+(0,\dots,r)
.

Here and in the following, we fix the halfspace to
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
, which is w.l.o.g. since (
3.16
) is invariant under rotations and translations.
Notice that, compared to Lemma
3.4
, the boundary Harnack inequality above is stronger, owing to its explicit dependence on

r
,
u(a_{r}(w))
and
v(a_{r}(w))
. This enhancement stems from the scale invariance and homogeneity of the
\infty
-Laplace equation.

The
C^{1,\alpha}
regularity of
\infty
-harmonic functions in dimension
n\geq 3
is a well-known open problem, restricting us to assume
n=2
. We will make use of the following result,
which follows from
[
15
]
.

Lemma 3.11

Suppose
w\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
r>0
and that
u
is a planar
\infty
-harmonic function in
B(w,r)
.
Then there are uniform constants
c
and
\alpha\in(0,1]
such that

\displaystyle|\nabla u(x)-\nabla u(y)|\leq c\left(\frac{|x-y|}{r}\right)^{\alpha}\sup_{B(w,r)}u

whenever
x,y\in B(w,r/2)
.

We will also make use of the following well-known Harnack-type inequalities.

Lemma 3.12

Suppose
w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
r>0
and that
u
is a positive
\infty
-harmonic function in
B(0,2r)
.
Then there is
c
, depending only on
n
, such that

\displaystyle(i)\quad\sup_{B(w,r)}u\leq c\inf_{B(w,r)}u.

Moreover, let
w\in\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
,
r>0
, and
u
be a positive
\infty
-harmonic function in
B(w,r)\setminus\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
,
continuous in
B(w,r)
with
u=0
on
B(w,r)\cap\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
.
Then there exists
c
, depending only on
n
, such that

\displaystyle(ii)\quad\sup_{B(w,r/c)}u\leq c\,u(a_{r/c}(w)),

where
a_{r/c}(w)=w+(0,\dots,r/c)
.

Proof.
For the Harnack inequality in
(i)
, see e.g.
[
30
,
22
]
.
For
(ii)
,
a proof for linear elliptic partial differential equations
in Lipschitz domains can be found in
[
13
]
.
The proof uses only the Harnack chain condition,
(i)
,
the well-known Lipschitz continuity up to the boundary as well as the comparison principle in Lemma
3.17
below.
In particular, the proof in
[
13
]
applies in our situation.
\hfill\Box

To make use of the interior
C^{1,\alpha}
estimate in Lemma
3.11
we extend the
\infty
-harmonic functions over the boundary by Schwarz reflection.
To state the lemma, recall the notation in (
3.11
) and observe that
since the infinity Laplace equation is invariant under translations and scalings, it is enough to work with

Q^{+}:=Q^{+}_{1}(0)
,
Q^{-}:=Q^{-}_{1}(0)
and
Q:=Q_{r}(w)
.
We now prove the following.

Lemma 3.13

Let
u
be infinity harmonic in
Q^{+}
.
Then the function defined by

\displaystyle\tilde{u}(x)=\begin{cases}u(x)&\mbox{if $x_{n}\geq 0$}\\[6.0pt]
-u(x^{\prime},-x_{n})&\mbox{if $x_{n}<0$},\end{cases}

is infinity harmonic in
Q
.

Proof.
We show the supersolution property (the subsolution property is shown similarly).
Choose an arbitrary point
\bar{x}\in Q^{-}
and a testfunction
\psi\in C^{2}(Q^{-})
such that
\tilde{u}-\psi
has a local minimum at
\bar{x}
.
We need to show that

\displaystyle\bigtriangleup_{\infty}\psi(\bar{x})\leq 0.

(3.17)

We build
\tilde{\psi}(x)=-\psi(x^{\prime},-x_{n})
to be a test function, reflected in the same way as
u
.
Then, by construction,
u(x)-\tilde{\psi}(x)
has a local maximum at
(\bar{x},-\bar{x}_{n})\in Q^{+}
.
Since
u
is a viscosity subsolution in
Q^{+}
we have

\displaystyle\bigtriangleup_{\infty}\tilde{\psi}(\bar{x}^{\prime},-\bar{x}_{n})\geq 0.

(3.18)

Now, for
i=1\dots n-1
, we have

\displaystyle\psi_{x_{i}}(\bar{x})=-\tilde{\psi}_{x_{i}}(\bar{x}^{\prime},-\bar{x}_{n}),\quad\psi_{x_{n}}(\bar{x})=\tilde{\psi}_{x_{n}}(\bar{x}^{\prime},-\bar{x}_{n}),

and, for
i,j=1\dots n-1
,

\displaystyle\psi_{x_{i},x_{j}}(\bar{x})

\displaystyle=-\tilde{\psi}_{x_{i},x_{j}}(\bar{x}^{\prime},-\bar{x}_{n}),\quad\psi_{x_{i},x_{n}}(\bar{x})=\tilde{\psi}_{x_{i},x_{n}}(\bar{x}^{\prime},-\bar{x}_{n}),

\displaystyle\psi_{x_{n},x_{n}}(\bar{x})

\displaystyle=-\tilde{\psi}_{x_{n},x_{n}}(\bar{x}^{\prime},-\bar{x}_{n}),

we conclude

\displaystyle\bigtriangleup_{\infty}\psi(\bar{x})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n-1}\psi_{x_{i}}(\bar{x})\psi_{x_{j}}(\bar{x})\psi_{x_{i},x_{j}}(\bar{x})

\displaystyle+2\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\psi_{x_{i}}(\bar{x})\psi_{x_{n}}(\bar{x})\psi_{x_{i},x_{n}}(\bar{x})+\psi^{2}_{x_{n}}(\bar{x})\psi_{x_{n},x_{n}}(\bar{x})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n-1}(-\tilde{\psi}_{x_{i}}(\bar{x}^{\prime},-\bar{x}_{n}))(-\tilde{\psi}_{x_{j}}(\bar{x}^{\prime},-\bar{x}_{n}))(-\tilde{\psi}_{x_{i},x_{j}}(\bar{x}^{\prime},-\bar{x}_{n}))

\displaystyle+2\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(-\tilde{\psi}_{x_{i}}(\bar{x}^{\prime},-\bar{x}_{n})\tilde{\psi}_{x_{n}}(\bar{x}^{\prime},-\bar{x}_{n})\tilde{\psi}_{x_{i},x_{n}}(\bar{x}^{\prime},-\bar{x}_{n})

\displaystyle+\tilde{\psi}^{2}_{x_{n}}(\bar{x}^{\prime},-\bar{x}_{n})(-\tilde{\psi}_{x_{n},x_{n}}(\bar{x}^{\prime},-\bar{x}_{n}))

\displaystyle=-\bigtriangleup_{\infty}\tilde{\psi}(\bar{x}^{\prime},-\bar{x}_{n}).

Together with (
3.18
) this implies (
3.17
) and therefore we have ensured that
\tilde{u}
is a viscosity supersolution of (
3.16
) in
Q\setminus\{x:x_{n}=0\}
.

It thus remains to show that
\tilde{u}
is a viscosity supersolution on the plane
\{x:x_{n}=0\}
.
Pick
\bar{x}
in this plane and let
\psi\in C^{2}(Q)
be a testfunction such that
\tilde{u}-\psi
has a local minimum at
\bar{x}
.
We need to show that (
3.17
) holds also in this situation.
Now, since
\tilde{u}(x^{\prime},0)=0
on the plane, we have, for
i=1\dots n-1
,

\displaystyle\psi_{x_{i}}(\bar{x})=0,\quad

so that

\displaystyle\bigtriangleup_{\infty}\psi(\bar{x})=\psi^{2}_{x_{n}}(\bar{x})\psi_{x_{n},x_{n}}(\bar{x})

Thus, it suffices to show that
\psi_{x_{n},x_{n}}(\bar{x})\leq 0
.
By the ”mirroring” construction of
\tilde{u}
it follows that
\tilde{u}
must be concave either for
x_{n}\geq 0
or for
x_{n}\leq 0
.
Moreover, the local minimum of
\tilde{u}-\varphi
implies that the same must hold also for
\varphi
.
Assume now that
\psi_{x_{n},x_{n}}(\bar{x})>0
.
Then, since
\psi
is
C^{2}
there exists
\epsilon>0
such that

\psi_{x_{n}}(\bar{x}^{\prime},-\epsilon)<\psi_{x_{n}}(\bar{x})<\psi_{x_{n}}(\bar{x}^{\prime},\epsilon)

which contradicts concavity.
Therefore,
\psi_{x_{n},x_{n}}(\bar{x})\leq 0
, (
3.17
) holds true and we have proved that
\hat{u}
is a viscosity supersolution in
Q
.
By mimicking the above for the subsolution property we obtain that
\tilde{u}
is infinity harmoinic in
Q
.
The proof is complete.

We are now ready to establish then main result of this subsection, that is the Hölder continuity of quotients of positive planar
\infty
-harmonic functions, vanishing on a hyperplane of dimension
1
.

Theorem 3.14

Let
w\in\partial\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
,
r>0
and suppose that
u,v>0
are
\infty
-harmonic in
B(w,r)\cap\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
,
continuous on
\overline{B(w,r)\cap\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}}
, with
u=0=v
on
B(w,r)\cap\partial\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
. Then there exist
c
and
\alpha\in(0,1]
such that

\left|\frac{u(x)}{v(x)}-\frac{u(y)}{v(y)}\right|\leq c\frac{u(y)}{v(y)}\left(\frac{|x-y|}{r}\right)^{\alpha}

whenever
x,y\in B(w,r/c)\cap\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
.

We remark that the inequality in Theorem
3.14
is sometimes written as

\left|\log{\frac{u(x)}{v(x)}}-\log{\frac{u(y)}{v(y)}}\right|\leq c\left(\frac{|x-y|}{r}\right)^{\alpha}

and it is stronger than the versions in Theorem
3.5
and Theorem
3.9
as we here have eliminated all dependence in the constants so that
c
and
\alpha
are uniform.
This was possible thanks to the availability of Harnacks inequality, the scale invariance of the infinity Laplace equation and the simple geometry.
We will give some corollaries of Theorem
3.14
after presenting the proof.

Proof of Theorem
3.14
.
By translating, scaling and normalizing we may w.l.o.g. assume
w=0
and
r=2
and
u(e_{n})=v(e_{n})=1
.
Then
u
,
v
are infinity harmonic in
Q^{+}
and, by using Lemma
3.13
we extend
u
,
v
to be infinity harmonic in
Q
.
We now apply the interior
C^{1,\alpha}
estimate in Lemma
3.11
and Lemma
3.12
to obtain

\displaystyle|\nabla y(x)-\nabla y(y)|\leq c|x-y|^{\alpha}\sup_{B(0,1/c)}y\leq c|x-y|^{\alpha}\,y(c^{-1}e_{n})\leq c|x-y|^{\alpha}

(3.19)

whenever
x,y\in B(0,1/c)
and for
y=u
and
y=v
.
Moreover, the boundary Harnack inequality in Lemma
3.10
yields

\displaystyle c^{-1}x_{2}\leq y(x)\leq cx_{2}

(3.20)

whenever
x,y\in B(0,1/c)
and for
y=u
and
y=v
.
Lemma
3.1
, Lemma
3.2
, (
3.19
) and (
3.20
) now imply

\displaystyle\left|\frac{u(x)}{v(x)}-\frac{u(y)}{v(y)}\right|\leq c|x-y|^{\alpha}

whenever
x,y\in B(0,1/c)
, and where
\alpha
is from Lemma
3.11
.
Scaling everything back and using Harnacks inequality and the boundary Harnack inequality once more yield the desired inequality.
The proof is complete.
\hfill\Box

Next, we derive a boundary gradient estimate stating that
\nabla u\approx u/d
near the boundary. In particular, Theorem
3.14
implies the following corollary.

Corollary 3.15

Let
w\in\partial\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
,
r>0
and suppose that
u,v>0
are
\infty
-harmonic in
B(w,r)\cap\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
,
continuous on
\overline{B(w,r)\cap\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}}
, with
u=0=v
on
B(w,r)\cap\partial\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
.
Then there exists
c
such that

c^{-1}\frac{u(x)}{x_{n}}\leq|\nabla u|\leq c\frac{u(x)}{x_{n}}\quad\text{whenever}\quad x\in B(w,r/c)\cap\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}.

Proof.
This follows by the arguments in the proofs of
[
28
, Lemma 3.18 and Lemma 3.25]
.
Indeed, these proofs uses only Harnack’s inequality, the analogue of Theorem
3.14
, and the fact that
x_{n}
solves the PDE; thus, the proofs work also in our situation.

3.5
Applications of
u/v\in C^{\alpha}
in unbounded domains

In this section we derive a number of corollaries through scaling and comparison arguments together with certain explicit solutions and the Hölder continuity of the quotients of solutions of PDEs.
We will obtain estimates of a
p
-harmonic measures,
p\in(1,\infty]
, as well as versions of Phragmen-Lindelöf theorem and uniqueness (modulo normalization) of
p
-harmonic functions, including
p=\infty
.

For
p\in(1,\infty)
, the
p
-Laplace equation yields

\displaystyle\Delta_{p}u:=\nabla\cdot(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)=0,

and we say that
u
is a
(weak) subsolution (supersolution)
to the
p
-Laplace equation in
a domain
\Omega
provided
u\in W_{loc}^{1,p}(\Omega)
and

\int\limits_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\,\langle\nabla u,\nabla\theta\rangle\,dx\leq(\geq)\,0,

whenever
\theta\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
is non-negative. A function
u
is a
(weak) solution
of the

p
-Laplacian if it is both a subsolution and a supersolution.
Here, as in the sequel,

W^{1,p}(\Omega)
is the Sobolev space of those
p
-integrable functions whose first
distributional derivatives are also
p
-integrable,
and
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
is the set of infinitely differentiable functions with compact support in
\Omega
.
If
u
is an upper semicontinuous subsolution to the
p
-Laplacian in
\Omega
,
p\in(1,\infty]

then we say that
u
is
p
-subharmonic
in
\Omega
.
If
u
is a lower semicontinuous supersolution to the
p
-Laplacian in
\Omega
,
p\in(1,\infty]
,
then we say that
u
is
p
-superharmonic
in
\Omega
.
If
u
is a continuous solution to the
p
-Laplacian in
\Omega
,
p\in(1,\infty]
, then
u
is

p
-harmonic
in
\Omega
.

To introduce the
p
-harmonic measure, let
\Omega\subset\mathbf{R}^{n}
be a regular bounded domain and let
f
be a real-valued continuous function defined on
\partial{\Omega}
.
It is well known that there exists a unique smooth function
u
, harmonic in
\Omega
,
such that
u=f
continuously on
\partial\Omega
. The maximum principle and the Riesz representation theorem yield the following representation formula for
u
,

u(z)=\int_{\partial\Omega}f(w)\,{\omega}^{z}(w)\text{, \quad whenever\; $z\in\Omega$.}

Here,
\omega^{z}(w)=\omega(dw,z,\Omega)
is referred to as the harmonic measure at
z
associated to the Laplace operator.
As the harmonic measure allows us to solve the Dirichlet problem, its properties are of fundamental
interest in classical potential theory.

In this section we will obtain estimates of the following generalization of harmonic measure related to the
p
-Laplace equation.

Definition 3.16

Let
G\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{n}
be a domain,
E\subseteq\partial{G}
,
p\in(1,\infty)

and
x\in G
.
The
p
-harmonic measure of
E
at
x
with respect to
G
, denoted by
\omega_{p}(E,x,G)
,
is defined as
\inf_{u}u(x)
,
where the infimum is taken over all
p
-superharmonic functions
u\geq 0
in
G

such that
\liminf_{z\to y}u(z)\geq 1
, for all
y\in E
.

The
\infty
-harmonic measure is defined similar, but with
p
-superharmonicity replaced by absolutely minimizing, see

[
39
, pages 173–174]
.
It turns out that the
p
-harmonic measure in Definition
3.16
is a

p
-harmonic function in
\Omega
, bounded below by
0
and bounded above by
1
.
For these and other basic properties of
p
-harmonic measure we refer the reader to

[
17
, Chapter 11]
.

We will make use of the following well known comparison principle for
p
-harmonic functions,
p\in(1,\infty]
.

Lemma 3.17

Let
p\in(1,\infty]
be given,
u
be
p
-subharmonic and
v
be
p
-superharmonic in a bounded domain
\Omega
.
If

\limsup_{x\to y}u(x)\leq\liminf_{x\to y}v(x)

for all
y\in\partial\Omega
, and if both sides of the above inequality are not simultaneously
\infty
or
-\infty
,
then
u\leq v
in
\Omega
.

Proof.
If
p\in(1,\infty)
, this result follows from
[
17
, Theorem 7.6]
.
For the case
p=\infty
, see e.g.
[
18
,
6
]
.

In the following subsections, we derive growth estimates for the
p
-harmonic measure, for
p
-subharmonic functions and
p
-harmonic functions in different geometric settings,
p\in(1,\infty]
. We will also consider uniqueness (modulo normalization) of
p
-harmonic functions.

3.5.1
Infinity-harmonic functions in half-spaces

With the boundary estimate in Theorem
3.14
for
\infty
-harmonic functions at hand,
we can use the explicit solution
u(x)=x_{2}
to obtain simple proofs of the probably well-known statements in the following theorem.
See
[
12
]
for a similar result on
g
-harmonic functions.
Here and in the following, we use the notation

\displaystyle M(R)=\underset{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\underset{|x|=R}{\sup}}u(x)

and we let
\omega_{\infty}(x)=\omega_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\cap\partial B(w,R),x,\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\cap B(0,R))
be the
\infty
-harmonic measure of
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\cap\partial B(0,R)
at
x=(x_{1},x_{2})
w.r.t
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\cap B(0,R)
.

Theorem 3.18

a)
There exists
c
such that

c^{-1}\frac{x_{2}}{R}\leq\omega_{\infty}(x)\leq c\frac{x_{2}}{R},\quad\text{whenever}\quad x\in B(0,R/c)\cap\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}

b)

If
u
is an
\infty
-subharmonic function in
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
such that

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to y}u(x)\leq 0\quad\text{for each}\quad y\in\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2},

then either
u\leq 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
or

\displaystyle\liminf_{R\to\infty}\frac{M(R)}{R}>0.

c)
Assume that
u
is a positive
\infty
-harmonic function in
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
satisfying
u(x)=0
continuously on
\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
. Then there exist a constant
C
such that

u(x)=Cx_{2}.

Proof.

a)
This is an immediate consequence of the well-known boundary Harnack inequality for
\infty
-harmonic functions recalled in Lemma
3.10
.

b)
This follows from
a)
together with a direct application of the Phragmen-Lindelöf principle, see
[
17
, Thm 11.11, p. 206]
.

c)

Let
v(x)=x_{2}
, and
u
be an arbitrary positive
\infty
-harmonic function in
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
, vanishing on the boundary, and apply Theorem
3.14
to obtain the
existence of uniform constants
c
and
\alpha\in(0,1]
such that

\left|\frac{u(x)}{x_{2}}-\frac{u(y)}{y_{2}}\right|\leq c\,\frac{u(y)}{y_{2}}\left(\frac{|x-y|}{R}\right)^{\alpha}

whenever
x,y\in B(0,R/c)\cap\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
.
Since
R>0
is arbitrary, the result follows by letting
R\to\infty
.

3.5.2
p
-harmonic functions in extended sectors

We now consider positive
p
-harmonic functions.
p\in(1,\infty)
,
vanishing on the boundary of prolonged planar sectors, and therby we generalize some results from
[
35
]
.
In particular, consider the domain

\mathcal{S}^{n}_{\nu}=\{x=(r,\phi,z):=(r,\phi,z_{1},\dots,z_{n-2})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:r>0,|\phi|<\frac{\pi}{2\nu},z\in\mathbb{R}^{n-2}\},

(3.21)

where
\nu\in(1/2,\infty)
.
\mathcal{S}^{n}_{\nu}
corresponds to extending a
2
-dimensional sector independently in
n-2
directions.
In what follows, we will suppress the index
n
and simply write
\mathcal{S}_{\nu}
.
Note that
\mathcal{S}_{\nu}
is a Lipschitz domain, and thus
we may apply the well known Hölder continuity of quotients of
p
-harmonic functions in
[
24
]
, which implies the following version, tailored for our situation.
Note in particular that since the Lipschitz-constant of
\mathcal{S}_{\nu}
does note change when scaling, and since the
p
-Laplace equation is invariant under scalings, we obtain the freedom to send
R\to\infty
in the below version.

Lemma 3.19

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
R>0
.
Suppose that
u
and
v
are positive
p
-harmonic functions in
\mathcal{S}_{\nu}\cap B(0,R)
, continuous on
\overline{\mathcal{S}_{\nu}}\cap B(0,R)
with
u=v=0
on
\partial\mathcal{S}_{\nu}\cap B(0,R)
.
Then there exist constants
c
and
\sigma\in(0,1)
,
both depending only on
p
,
n
, and
\nu
, such that

\left|\log{\frac{u(x)}{v(x)}}-\log{\frac{u(y)}{v(y)}}\right|\leq c\left(\frac{|x-y|}{R}\right)^{\sigma}

whenever
x,y\in\Omega\cap B(0,\frac{R}{c})
.

Proof.
Define
\tilde{u}(x)=u(Rx)
,
\tilde{v}(x)=v(Rx)
and observe that then
\tilde{u}
and
\tilde{v}
are positive
p
-harmonic functions in
\mathcal{S}_{\nu}\cap B(0,1)
, continuous on
\overline{\mathcal{S}_{\nu}}\cap B(0,1)
with
\tilde{u}=\tilde{v}=0
on
\partial\mathcal{S}_{\nu}\cap B(0,1)
.
Since
\mathcal{S}_{\nu}
is a Lipschitz domain with Lipschitz constant independent of scaling, and since the
p
-Laplacian is invariant under scalings, we may apply

[
24
, Theorem 2]

to obtain constants
c
and
\sigma\in(0,1)
, depending only on
p
,
n
and
\nu
, such that

\left|\log{\frac{\tilde{u}(x)}{\tilde{v}(x)}}-\log{\frac{\tilde{u}(y)}{\tilde{v}(y)}}\right|\leq c\left({|x-y|}\right)^{\sigma},

(3.22)

whenever
x,y\in\Omega\cap B(0,\frac{1}{c})
.

Let
y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{\nu}
and suppose
R>0
is so large that
y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{\nu}\cap B(0,\frac{R}{c})
.
Define
\tilde{y}_{1}=\frac{y_{1}}{R}
and
\tilde{y}_{2}=\frac{y_{2}}{R}
so that
\tilde{y}_{1},\tilde{y}_{2}\in B(w,\frac{r_{0}}{c})
which together with (
3.22
) imply

\left|\log{\frac{u(y_{1})}{v(y_{1})}}-\log{\frac{u(y_{2})}{v(y_{2})}}\right|=\left|\log{\frac{\tilde{u}(\tilde{y}_{1})}{\tilde{v}(\tilde{y}_{1})}}-\log{\frac{\tilde{u}(\tilde{y}_{2})}{\tilde{v}(\tilde{y}_{2})}}\right|\leq c\left({|\tilde{y}_{1}-\tilde{y}_{2}|}\right)^{\sigma}=c\left(\frac{|y_{1}-y_{2}|}{R}\right)^{\sigma}.

The proof is complete.

We next recall the following explicit solutions of the
p
-Laplace equation in
\mathcal{S}_{\nu}
;

Lemma 3.20

For any
\nu\in[\frac{1}{2},\infty)
and
p\in(1,\infty)
there exists a function
f_{\nu,p}:[0,\pi]\to\mathbb{R}
such that

h(x)=h(r,\phi,z)=r^{k}f_{\nu,p}(\phi)

(3.23)

is
p
-harmonic in
\mathcal{S}_{\nu}
, where,

k:=k(\nu,p)=\frac{(v-1)\sqrt{(1-2v)(p-2)^{2}+v^{2}p^{2}}+(2-p)(1-2v)+v^{2}p}{2(p-1)(2v-1)}.

(3.24)

Moreover, the following properties hold:

1.

f_{\nu,p}
is continuous and differentiable on
(-\frac{\pi}{2\nu},\frac{\pi}{2\nu})

2.

f_{\nu,p}(\pm\frac{\pi}{2k})=0

3.

|f_{\nu,p}(\phi)|,|f_{\nu,p}^{\prime}(\phi)|\leq c
,
whenever
\phi\in[-\frac{\pi}{2\nu},\frac{\pi}{2k}]

4.

|f_{\nu,p}^{\prime}(\phi)|\geq c^{-1}
and
f_{\nu,p}\geq c^{-1}
,
whenever
\phi\in[-\frac{\pi}{2\nu},\frac{\pi}{2k}]\setminus[-\frac{\pi}{4\nu},\frac{\pi}{4\nu}]

Proof.
The planar case is summarized in
[
35
, Lemma 3.1]
and the extension to
n>2
is immediate since
h
is independent of
z
and the
p
-Laplace equation does not depend explicitly on
n
. Main ideas date back to
[
20
,
7
,
40
,
11
]
among others.

We are now set up to prove our results in the extended sector
\mathcal{S}_{v}
.

Theorem 3.21

Assume
p\in(1,\infty)
,
v\in(\frac{1}{2},\infty)
, and
R>0
. Let
\mathcal{S}_{\nu}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be defined by (
3.21
) and
k:=k(\nu,p)
be the exponent in (
3.24
). Then the following holds:

a)
There exists
c
, depending only on
v
and
p
, such that

\displaystyle c^{-1}\,\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{k}\,\leq\omega_{p}(x)\,\leq c\,\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{k},\qquad\mbox{$x\in\mathcal{S}_{2\nu}\cap B(0,\frac{R}{c})$}

(3.25)

where
\omega_{R}(x)
is the
p
-harmonic measure of
\partial B(0,R)\cap\mathcal{S}_{\nu}
at
x=(r,\phi,z)
w.r.t.
\mathcal{S}^{n}_{\nu}(R):=\mathcal{S}_{\nu}\cap B(0,R)
.

b)
If
u(x)
is a
p
-subharmonic function in
\mathcal{S}_{\nu}
satisyfing

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to y}u(x)\leq 0\quad\text{for each}\;y\in\partial S_{\nu},

then either
u\leq 0
in
S_{\nu}
or

\displaystyle\liminf_{R\to\infty}\frac{M(R)}{R^{k}}>0,

where
k=k(v,p)
is the exponent in (
3.24
), and

\displaystyle M(R)=\underset{x\in S_{v}}{\underset{|x|=R}{\sup}}u(x).

c)

If
u>0
is
p
-harmonic in
\mathcal{S}_{\nu}
, satisfying
u=0
on
\partial\mathcal{S}_{\nu}
,
then there exist a constant
C
such that

u(x)=u(r,\phi,z)=Cr^{k}f_{\nu,p}(\phi).

Proof.

a)
Let
u=\omega_{R}(x)
be the
p
-harmonic measure and
v=R^{-k}h
be a scaled version of the explicit solution (
3.23
). Fix
y=(R/c,0,z)
and apply Lemma
3.19
with these functions. After exponentiating we get

c^{-1}\frac{\omega_{p}(y)}{R^{-k}h(y)}\leq\frac{\omega_{p}(x)}{r^{k}R^{-k}f_{\nu,p}(\phi_{1})}\leq\frac{\omega_{p}(y)}{R^{-k}h(y)}c,

where
c
depends only on
p
,
n
, and
M
. By the well known Hölder continuity of
p
-harmonic functions and the construction of
v
we have that the quotient
\frac{\omega_{p}(y)}{R^{-k}h(y)}\approx 1
. Since
f_{\nu,p}
is bounded from above and below in
S_{2\nu}\cap B(0,\frac{R}{c})
, the result follows.

b)
This follows from a direct application of the Phragmen-Lindelöf principle, see
[
17
, Thm 11.11, p. 206]
.

c)
Let
h
be the function specified in Lemma
3.20
and let
x,y
be arbitrary points in
\mathcal{S}_{v}\cap B(w,\frac{R}{c})
. Applying Lemma
3.19
we get

\left|\log\frac{u(x)}{h(x)}-\log\frac{u(y)}{h(y)}\right|\leq c\left|\frac{x-y}{R}\right|^{\sigma}.

As
x
and
y
were arbitrary and
R
can be chosen arbitrarily large, we get that
\frac{u}{h}
must be constant and therefore
u=Ch
for some constant
C
. This concludes the proof.

Before we proceed to the next geometric setting, we remark that the local estimates in
[
35
, Section 5]
, e.g. those near outward/invard cusps, for

,
will also hold in the extended case considered in Theorem

.

3.5.3
p
-harmonic functions vanishing on an
m
-flat

We finally consider positive
p
-harmonic functions,
p\in(1,\infty]
, vanishing continuously on an
m
-flat, i.e.,
an
m
-dimensional hyperplane
\Lambda
,
1\leq m<n
,

\Lambda=\{x=(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x^{\prime}|=0\},\qquad x^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\mbox{ and }x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-m}.

(3.26)

To the authors knowledge, few authors have proved estimates of
p
-harmonic functions vanishing on boundaries having dimension less than
n-1
;
m
-flats were considered in
[
29
]
for the borderline case
p=n
,
and in
[
31
,
32
]
similar results where proved for the general case
p\in(1,\infty]
.
Estimates for solutions of equations of
p
-Laplace type,
p\in(1,\infty)
,
near low-dimensional Reifenberg flat sets, were proved in
[
27
]
.
Indeed,
[
27
, Theorem 1.9 and Theorem 1.10]
imply, through the same simple scaling argument as used above,
an analogue of Lemma
3.19
in the current geometric setting.
The above cited results together with the fact that

v(x)=|x^{\prime}|^{\beta}\quad\text{with}\quad\beta=\beta(p)=\frac{p-n+m}{p-1},\quad\beta(\infty)=1,

(3.27)

is
p
-harmonic in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Lambda
,
p\in(1,\infty]
,
yield the following analogue of Theorem
3.18
and Theorem
3.21
.

Theorem 3.22

Assume
p\in(1,\infty]
, let
\beta
be as in (
3.27
) and let
\Lambda
be as in (
3.26
).

a)
Fix
w\in\Lambda
and let
\omega_{R}(x)
be the
p
-harmonic measure of
\partial B(w,R)\setminus\Lambda
at
x=(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})
w.r.t.
B(\omega,R)
.
Then there exists
c
, depending only on
m
,
n
, and
p
, such that

c^{-1}\left(\frac{d(x,\Lambda)}{R}\right)^{\beta}\leq\omega_{p}(x)\leq c\left(\frac{d(x,\Lambda)}{R}\right)^{\beta}\qquad\mbox{$x\in B(w,\frac{R}{c})\setminus\Lambda$}.

b)
If
u(x)
is
p
-subharmonic in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Lambda
satisyfing

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to y}u(x)\leq 0\quad\text{for each}\;y\in\Lambda,

then either
u\leq 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Lambda
or

\displaystyle\liminf_{R\to\infty}\frac{M(R)}{R^{\beta}}>0,

where

\displaystyle M(R)=\underset{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Lambda}{\underset{|x|=R}{\sup}}u(x).

c)
If
p<\infty
and
u>0
is
p
-harmonic in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Lambda
,
satisfying
u(x)=0
on
\Lambda
,
then there exists a constant
C
such that

u(x)=C|x^{\prime}|^{\beta}.

Proof.
Statements
a)
and
b)
was proved already in
[
32
, Section 4]
.
When
p<\infty
we can proceed as in the proof of Theorem
3.21
to obtain statement
c
as well as alternative proofs of statements
a)
and

.
Indeed, when

an analogue of Lemma
3.19
, for the current geometric setting,
follows from
[

, Theorem 1.9 and Theorem 1.10]
by mimicking the proof of Lemma

.

We remark that the results in Theorem

for

hold in a more general setting, for

-harmonic functions as defined in

]

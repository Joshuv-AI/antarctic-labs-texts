Inequalities for eigenvalues of Laplacian and biharmonic operators on submanifolds
Yong Luo, Xianjing Zheng

Abstract
In this paper we study eigenvalues of Laplacian and biharmonic operators on compact domains in complete manifolds. We establish several new inequalities for eigenvalues of Laplacian and biharmonic operators respectively by using Sobolev type inequalities.



AMS subject classifications:
58J50, 58E11, 35P15.

Key words:
Eigenvalue, universal inequality, Laplacian, biharmonic operator, submanifold.

1
Introduction

Let
(M,g)
be an
n
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold and
\Omega
a bounded connected domain in
M
. Let
\Delta
and
\Delta^{2}
denote the Laplacian and biharmonic operators acting on functions on
M
respectively. The Dirichlet eigenvalue problem is stated as:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\Delta u=-\lambda u\text{ in }\Omega,\\
\left.u\right|_{\partial\Omega}=0.\end{array}\right.

Let

\displaystyle 0<\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}\leq\lambda_{3}\leq\cdots,

denote the successive eigenvalues of (
1
). Here each eigenvalue is repeated according to its multiplicity.

Generally, if an equality of eigenvalus requires no hypotheses on the geometric quantities of the domain
\Omega
(other than its dimension), it is called a universal inequality. Universal inequality is an important area in the study of the spectrum of
\Delta
and many works have been done during the past decades. Now we give a brief introduction of the main results.

The study of universal inequalities for (
1
) was initiated by Payne, Pólya, and Weinberger
[
12
]
in 1956, they proved

\displaystyle\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{k}\leq\frac{2}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}

for
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Exactly by the same proof, their result can promoted to dimension
n
as

\displaystyle\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{k}\leq\frac{4}{kn}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}

for
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

In 1980, Hile and Protter
[
7
]
proved

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}}\geq\frac{kn}{4}.

In 1991, Yang
[
15
]
proved

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\left(1+\frac{4}{n}\right)\lambda_{i}\right)\leq 0,

which is called Yang’s first inequality. It can be derived from this inequality that

\displaystyle\lambda_{k+1}\leq\frac{1}{k}\left(1+\frac{4}{n}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i},

which is called Yang’s second inequality.

When
M
is isometrically immersed in
R^{m}
, Chen and Cheng
[
3
]
and Soufi, Harrel and Ilias
[
13
]
have proved independently that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)^{2}\leq\frac{4}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)\left(\lambda_{i}+\frac{n^{2}}{4}H_{0}^{2}\right),

(1.4)

where
H_{0}=\max_{x\in\Omega}|\mathbf{H}|
and
\mathbf{H}
is the mean curvature vector of
M
.

Next we consider the eigenvalue problem for the biharmonic operator or the so-called clamped plate problem, which is given by

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\Delta^{2}u=\Gamma u\text{ in }\Omega,\\
\left.u\right|_{\partial\Omega}=\left.\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\right|_{\partial\Omega}=0.\end{array}\right.

where
\nu
denotes the outward unit normal to the boundary
\partial\Omega
.

The study on universal inequalities for eigenvalues of the clamped plate problem can be traced back to 1950s. When M is a Euclidean space
\mathbf{R}^{n}
, Payne, Pólya and Weinberger
[
12
]
in 1956 proved that the eigenvalues
\left\{\Gamma_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
of the problem (
1
) satisfy

\displaystyle\Gamma_{k+1}-\Gamma_{k}\leq\frac{8(n+2)}{n^{2}}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Gamma_{i}.

(1.8)

In 1984, Hile and Yeh
[
8
]
strengthened (
1.8
) and obtained

\displaystyle\frac{n^{2}k^{3/2}}{8n+2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Gamma_{i}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\Gamma_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\Gamma_{k+1}-\Gamma_{i}}.

In 1990, Chen and Qian
[
4
]
, and Hook
[
9
]
independently proved the following inequality

\displaystyle\frac{n^{2}k^{2}}{8(n+2)}\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Gamma_{i}^{1/2}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\Gamma_{i}^{1/2}}{\Gamma_{k+1}-\Gamma_{i}}\right).

In Ashbaugh’s survey paper
[
1
]
on recent developments on eigenvalue problems, he asked if we can establish an analog of Yang’s first inequality for the eigenvalues of the clamped plate problem. In 2006, Cheng and Yang solved this problem in
[
6
]
by proving that

\displaystyle\Gamma_{k+1}-\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Gamma_{i}\leq\left[\frac{8(n+2)}{n^{2}}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left[\Gamma_{i}\left(\Gamma_{k+1}-\Gamma_{i}\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

In 2010, Cheng, Ichikawa and Mametsuka
[
5
]
proved that if
M
is an
n
-dimensional submanifold isometrically immersed in a Euclidean space with mean curvature vector
\mathbf{H}
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\Gamma_{k+1}-\Gamma_{i}\right)^{2}\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\Gamma_{k+1}-\Gamma_{i}\right)\left(n^{2}H_{0}^{2}+(2n+4)\Gamma_{i}^{1/2}\right)\left(n^{2}H_{0}^{2}+4\Gamma_{i}^{1/2}\right),

(1.9)

where
H_{0}=\sup_{\Omega}|\mathbf{H}|
.

In 2011, Wang and Xia
[
14
]
strengthened the inequality (
1.9
) by Cheng, Ichikawa and Mametsuka and obtained

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\Gamma_{k+1}-\Gamma_{i}\right)^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\Gamma_{k+1}-\Gamma_{i}\right)^{2}\left(n^{2}H_{0}^{2}+(2n+4)\Gamma_{i}^{1/2}\right)\right\}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\times\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\Gamma_{k+1}-\Gamma_{i}\right)\left(n^{2}H_{0}^{2}+4\Gamma_{i}^{1/2}\right)\right\}^{1/2}.

(1.10)

In this paper we study eigenvalues of the Dirichlet eigenvalue problem and the clamped plate problem on compact domains in complete Riemannian manifolds.

2
Michael-Simon’s type Sobolev inequality

The Michael-Simon’s Sobolev inequality is well known as the following lemma:

Lemma 2.1

(
[
11
]
)
.

Let
M
be an
n
-dimensional submanifold immersed in
\mathbb{R}^{n+p}
with mean carvature vector
\mathbf{H}
. Then for any function
h\in C_{0}^{1}(M)
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\int_{M}|h|^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\leq C_{2}\left(\int_{M}|\nabla h|+\int_{M}|\mathbf{H}||h|\right),

where
C_{2}
is a constant depends only on dimension
n
.

In the latter sections we will need the following corollary proved by Li, Xu and Zhou for
\Omega=M
:

Lemma 2.2

(
[
10
]
)
.

Let
M
be an
n
-dimensional submanifold immersed in
\mathbb{R}^{n+p}
with mean carvature vector
\mathbf{H}
. Suppose that
\Omega
is a smooth open domain in
M
and
\|\mathbf{H}\|_{L^{n}(\Omega)}C_{2}<1
, where
C_{2}
is the constant in Lemma
2.1
. Then for any
f\in C_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}|f|^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}\leq C_{s}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla f|^{2},

where
C_{s}=\left(\frac{C_{2}}{1-\|\mathbf{H}\|_{L^{n}(\Omega)}C_{2}}\frac{2(n-1)}{n-2}\right)^{2}
.

Proof.

For the reader’s convenience, we give a proof of this lemma here. For a function
h\in C^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
as in Lemma
2.1
, by Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\mathbf{H}||h|\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}|h|^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\|\mathbf{H}\|_{L^{n}(\Omega)}.

Therefore by the Michael-Simon’s Sobolev inequality in Lemma
2.1
and the inequality above we get

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}|h|^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\leq\frac{C_{2}}{1-\|\mathbf{H}\|_{L^{n}(\Omega)}C_{2}}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla h|.

Now for any
f\in C_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, let
h=f^{\frac{2(n-1)}{n-2}}
, we get

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}f^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{2}}{1-\|\mathbf{H}\|_{L^{n}(\Omega)}C_{2}}\frac{2(n-1)}{n-2}\int_{\Omega}|f|^{\frac{n}{n-2}}|\nabla f|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{2}}{1-\|\mathbf{H}\|C_{2}}\frac{2(n-1)}{n-2}\left(\int_{M}f^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{M}|\nabla f|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Then the conclusion follows easily. Substituting (
5.4
) into (
5.1
), we can prove (
1.14
).
\hfill\Box

Proof of Theorem
1.1

Proof.

Assume that
u_{i},i=1,...k,
are eigenfunctions of the Dirichlet problem with eigenvalues
\lambda_{i}
, such that

\int_{\Omega}u_{i}u_{j}=\delta_{ij}.

The first part of the proof can be found in
[
13
]
. To prove Theorem 1.1 we will need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any smooth function
F
on
M
and any positive integer
k
one has

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)^{2}\left\langle[-\Delta,F]u_{i},Fu_{i}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)\left\|[-\Delta,F]u_{i}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

where
[\cdot,\cdot]
denotes the commutator of two operators
B
and
C
via
[B,C]=BC-CB
.

This lemma can be found in
[
2
]
, so we skip the proof here.

Now let
X

:

M

\longrightarrow

R^{m}
be an isometric immersion, and
X_{1}
,
\ldots
,
X_{m}
the components of the immersion
X
. A straightforward calculation gives

\displaystyle\left[-\Delta,X_{\alpha}\right]u_{i}=\left(-\Delta X_{\alpha}\right)u_{i}-2\nabla X_{\alpha}\cdot\nabla u_{i}.

By integrating by parts we obtain

\displaystyle\left\langle\left[-\Delta,X_{\alpha}\right]u_{i},X_{\alpha}u_{i}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla X_{\alpha}\right|^{2}u_{i}^{2}.

thus

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha}\left\langle\left[-\Delta,X_{\alpha}\right]u_{i},X_{\alpha}u_{i}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\sum_{\alpha}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla X_{\alpha}\right|^{2}u_{i}^{2}=n\int_{\Omega}u_{i}^{2}=n

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\left[-\Delta,X_{\alpha}\right]u_{i}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=\int_{\Omega}\left(\left(-\Delta X_{\alpha}\right)u_{i}-2\nabla X_{\alpha}\cdot\nabla u_{i}\right)^{2}.

Since
X
is an isometric immersion, it follows that

\displaystyle\left(\Delta X_{1},\ldots,\Delta X_{m}\right)=n\mathbf{H}

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=1}^{m}\left(\nabla X_{\alpha}\cdot\nabla u_{i}\right)^{2}=\left|\nabla u_{i}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=1}^{m}\left(-\Delta X_{\alpha}\right)u_{i}\nabla X_{\alpha}\cdot\nabla u_{i}=\frac{n}{2}\mathbf{H}\cdot\nabla u_{i}^{2}=0.

Using all these facts, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=1}^{m}\left\|\left[-\Delta,X_{\alpha}\right]u_{i}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=n^{2}\int_{\Omega}|\mathbf{H}|^{2}u_{i}^{2}+4\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u_{i}\right|^{2}.

Since

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u_{i}\right|^{2}=\lambda_{i},

using Lemma 3.1 we obtain

\displaystyle n\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)^{2}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)\left(n^{2}\int_{\Omega}|\mathbf{H}|^{2}u_{i}^{2}+4\lambda_{i}\right),

(3.1)

Using Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\mathbf{H}|^{2}u_{i}^{2}\leq\left\|u_{i}\right\|_{L^{2p}(\Omega)}^{2}\left\|\left|\mathbf{H}\right|\right\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Letting
p=\frac{n}{n-2}
and using Lemma 2.1 we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\mathbf{H}|^{2}u_{i}^{2}\leq C_{1}\left\|\nabla u_{i}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\left\|\left|\mathbf{H}\right|\right\|^{2}_{L^{n}\left(\Omega\right)}.

(3.2)

Substituting (
3.2
) into (
3.1
), we can prove (
1.12
).
\hfill\Box

Proof of Theorem

The first part of the proof can be found in

Lemma 4.1

(
[
14
]
)
.

Let
\Gamma_{i}
, i=1, …, k+1, be the
ith
eigenvalue of the Clamped plate problem and
u_{i}
the orthonormal eigenfunction corresponding to
\Gamma_{i}
, that is,

\displaystyle\Delta^{2}u_{i}=\Gamma_{i}u_{i}\quad\text{ in }\Omega,\left.\quad u_{i}\right|_{\partial\Omega}=\left.\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial\nu}\right|_{\partial\Omega}=0,

\displaystyle\int_{M}u_{i}u_{j}=\delta_{ij},\quad\forall\ i,j=1,2,\ldots,k+1.

Then for any smooth function
h
:

\Omega
\rightarrow

\mathbf{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\Gamma_{k+1}-\Gamma_{i}\right)^{2}\int_{\Omega}u_{i}^{2}|\nabla h|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\delta\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\Gamma_{k+1}-\Gamma_{i}\right)^{2}\int_{\Omega}\left(u_{i}^{2}(\Delta h)^{2}+4\left(\left\langle\nabla h,\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle^{2}+u_{i}\Delta h\left\langle\nabla h,\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle\right)-2u_{i}|\nabla h|^{2}\Delta u_{i}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\left(\Gamma_{k+1}-\Gamma_{i}\right)}{\delta}\int_{\Omega}\left(\left\langle\nabla h,\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle+\frac{u_{i}\Delta h}{2}\right)^{2},

(4.1)

where
\delta
is any positive constant.

Proof.

For
i=1,\ldots,k
, consider the functions
\phi_{i}:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbf{R}
given by

\displaystyle\phi_{i}=hu_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}r_{ij}u_{j},

where

\displaystyle r_{ij}=\int_{\Omega}hu_{i}u_{j}.

Since
\left.\phi_{i}\right|_{\partial\Omega}=\left.\frac{\partial\phi_{i}}{\partial v}\right|_{\partial\Omega}=0
and

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u_{j}\phi_{i}=0,\forall i,j=1,\ldots,k,

it follows from the Rayleigh-Ritz inequality that

\displaystyle\Gamma_{k+1}\leq\frac{\int_{\Omega}\phi_{i}\Delta^{2}\phi_{i}}{\int_{\Omega}\phi_{i}^{2}}.

(4.2)

We have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\phi_{i}\Delta^{2}\phi_{i}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\phi_{i}\left(\Delta^{2}\left(hu_{i}\right)-\sum_{j=1}^{k}r_{ij}\Gamma_{j}u_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\phi_{i}\Delta^{2}\left(hu_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\phi_{i}\left(\Delta\left(u_{i}\Delta h\right)+2\Delta\left\langle\nabla h,\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle+2\left\langle\nabla h,\nabla\left(\Delta u_{i}\right)\right\rangle+\Delta h\Delta u_{i}+\Gamma_{i}hu_{i}\right)

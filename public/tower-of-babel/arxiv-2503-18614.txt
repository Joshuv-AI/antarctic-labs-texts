Abstract
. In this work, we relate girth and path-degeneracy in classes with sub-exponential expansion, with explicit bounds for classes with polynomial expansion and proper minor-closed classes that are tight up to a constant factor (and tight up to second order terms if a classical conjecture on existence of g g -cages is verified). As an application, we derive bounds on the generalized acyclic indices, on the generalized arboricities, and on the weak coloring numbers of high-girth graphs in such classes. Along the way, we prove a conjecture proposed in [T. Bartnicki et al. , Generalized arboricity of graphs with large girth , Discrete Mathematics 342 (2019), no. 5, 1343–1350.], which asserts that, for every integer k k , there is an integer g ⁡ ( p , k ) g(p,k) such that every K k K_{k} minor-free graph with girth at least g ⁡ ( p , k ) g(p,k) has p p -arboricity at most p + 1 p+1 .



1.
Introduction

The original notion of path-degeneracy
was introduced in
[
20
]
in a study of the circular chromatic number of graphs with large girth excluding a minor.
In this paper, we consider a slightly different definition, which will be suitable in a broader context (bounding the generalized
p
-arboricity, the generalized
p
-acyclic chromatic number, or the weak coloring number of graphs with large girth in a “nice” monotone class
\mathscr{C}
).

A graph is
p
-path degenerate
if it can be constructed from the empty graph by successively adding an isolated vertex, a vertex of degree one, or a path of length
p
with both endpoints in the previously constructed graph (see
Sections

3.1
and
1
).

.

Figure 1.
Construction of a
p
-path degenerate graph

In this paper, we denote by
|G|
the
order
of a graph
G
(that is, its number of vertices), and by
\|G\|
its
size

(that is, its number of edges).
Recall that a minor of a graph
G
at
depth

r
is a minor of
G
obtained by contracting disjoint subgraphs with radius at most
r
and removing edges, and that

\nabla_{r}(G)
denotes the maximum ratio
\|H\|/|H|
over all (non-empty) minors
H
of
G
at depth
r
.
The
expansion function

{\rm Exp}_{\mathscr{C}}
of a class
\mathscr{C}
is defined by

{\rm Exp}_{\mathscr{C}}(r)=\sup\{\nabla_{r}(G)\colon G\in\mathscr{C}\}.

A class
\mathscr{C}
has

•

bounded expansion
, if
{\rm Exp}_{\mathscr{C}}(r)<\infty
;

•

sub-exponential expansion
, if
{\rm Exp}_{\mathscr{C}}(r)=2^{o(r)}
;

•

polynomial expansion
, if
{\rm Exp}_{\mathscr{C}}(r)=r^{O(1)}
.

Formal definitions and basic facts about shallow minors will be recalled in
Section

2
, and we refer the interested reader to
[
19
]
for an in-depth study.

1.1.
Our results

As a warm-up, we consider the general case of classes with sub-exponential expansion and prove the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a class with sub-exponential expansion.
Then, for every integer
p
there exists an integer
g_{p}
such that every graph
G\in\mathscr{C}
with girth at least
g_{p}
is
p
-path degenerate.

Then, we consider more precisely the case of classes with polynomial expansion, and provide a bound on
g_{p}
in terms of the
\mathop{W_{-1}}
branch of Lambert’s
W
function.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a class with polynomial expansion, with
{\rm Exp}_{\mathscr{C}}(r)\leq a(r+\frac{1}{2})^{b}
for some positive reals

a,b
.
We define

g_{p}=\max\left(7,2\left\lfloor-\frac{2b}{\log 2}\mathop{W_{-1}}\left(-\frac{\log 2}{(24\sqrt{2}a)^{1/b}\,bp}\right)\right\rfloor+4\right)(p-1).

Then, every graph
G\in\mathscr{C}
with

\girth(G)>g_{p}\sim 4bp\log_{2}p\\

is
p
-path degenerate.

Then, we prove that our bound is tight up to a constant factor.

Theorem 1.3
.

For every real
b>0
, there exists a class with expansion
O(r^{b})
such that for infinitely many
p\in\mathbb{N}
the class contains a graph
G
with

\girth(G)\geq-\frac{8b}{3\log 2}\mathop{W_{-1}}\left(-\frac{\log 2}{(p-1)b}\right)(p-1)\sim\frac{8}{3}bp\log_{2}p

that is not
p
-path degenerate.

We conjecture that the bound given in
Theorem

1.2
is tight up to second order term.

Conjecture 1.4
.

For every real
b>0
, there exists a class with expansion
O(r^{b})
such that for infinitely many
p\in\mathbb{N}
the class contains a graph
G
with

\girth(G)\sim 4bp\log_{2}p

that is not
p
-path degenerate.

As a support to this conjecture, we note that its validity would follow from the following conjecture on the existence of
g
-cages (which will be used to support our other conjectures, too).

Conjecture 1.5

(
[
4
, p. 164]
)
.

For infinitely many integers
g
there
exists a cubic graph with girth at least
g
and order at most
c2^{g/2}
.

Then, we consider proper minor-closed classes of graphs and prove the following bound.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a proper minor-closed class of graphs and let
d
be the maximum average degree of the graphs in
\mathscr{C}
.
Then, for every integer
p\geq 2
, every graph
G\in\mathscr{C}
with

\girth(G)>(4\log_{2}d+2\log_{2}(\min\{d,576\})+3)(p-1)

is
p
-path degenerate.

Again, we prove that our bound is tight up to a constant factor.

Theorem 1.7
.

There exists a constant
c
with the following property:
for every real
d_{0}>0
there exists a real
d\geq d_{0}
and a proper minor closed class with maximum average degree at most
d
that contains, for every integer
p\geq 2
, a graph
G_{p}
with

\girth(G_{p})\geq\left(\frac{8}{3}\log_{2}(d-2)+c\right)(p-1)

that is not
p
-path degenerate.

We conjecture that the bound given in
Theorem

1.6
is essentially tight, what would be a consequence of the validity of
Conjecture

1.5
.

Conjecture 1.8
.

There exists a constant
c
with the following property:
for every real
d_{0}>0
there exists a real
d\geq d_{0}
and a proper minor closed class with maximum average degree at most
d
that contains, for every integer
p\geq 2
, a graph
G_{p}
with

\girth(G_{p})\geq(4\log_{2}(d-2)+c)(p-1)

that is not
p
-path degenerate.

In the case where the class
\mathscr{C}
is the class of all graphs excluding
K_{k}
as a minor, we derive (see
Theorem

3.13
) that the maximum girth

of a

-minor free graph that is not

-path degenerate is bounded by

\Bigl(\frac{8}{3}\log_{2}k+c\Bigr)(p-1)\leq g(K_{k},p)<(4\log_{2}k+2\log_{2}\log_{2}k+O(1))(p-1).

We conjecture that

is close to the upper bound:

Conjecture 1.9
.

We have

g(K_{k},p)=(4\log_{2}k+O(1))(p-1).

Note that
Conjecture

1.2.
Applications

We review here some of the applications of our study of path-degeneracy.

Generalized
r
-arboricity

Given an integer
r\geq 1
,
the
generalized
r
-arboricity

\arb_{r}(G)
of a graph
G

[
21
]

is the minimum number of colors in a coloration of the edges of
G
such that every cycle
C
of
G
gets at least
\min\{|C|,r+1\}
colors.
Note that if
r=1
, then
\arb_{1}(G)
is the usual arboricity
\arb(G)
.

The generalized
r
-arboricity
\arb_{r}(G)
is unbounded for planar graphs: for any
r\geq 2
, there are planar graphs with arbitrarily large
\arb_{r}(G)
(see
[
2
]
for such examples).
However, for every class with bounded expansion,
\arb_{r}(G)
is bounded for graphs with sufficiently large girth (depending on
r
)
[
21
]
.
One can deduce from the definition of
\arb_{r}(G)
that if a graph
G
has girth at least
r+1
, then
\arb_{r}(G)\geq r+1
.
We prove that every
(r+1)
-path degenerate graph
r
satisfies
\arb_{r}(G)\leq r+1
in
Theorem

4.1
.

In this context, we focus on the bound for the generalized
r
-arboricity of large girth graphs in a given class.
We study the invariant
\A_{r}(\mathscr{C})
of a class
\mathscr{C}
with bounded expansion,
which was defined
in
[
2
]
as

\A_{r}(\mathscr{C}):=\min\bigl\{k:\exists g\ \forall G\in\mathscr{C}\ (\girth(G)\geq g)\Rightarrow(\arb_{r}(G)\leq k)\bigr\}.

Obviously,
the best possible for

\A_{r}(\mathscr{C})
is
r+1
. Bartnicki et al.

[
2
]

proved that
\A_{r}(\mathscr{C})=r+1
holds for planar graphs and more generally, for graphs with any fixed genus. They also indicated that this equality does not hold for every class with bounded expansion and conjectured the following.

Conjecture 1.10

(
[
C
]
onjecture 7)
.

BBCFGM2019]

Every proper minor-closed class of graphs

\mathscr{C}
satisfies
\A_{r}(\mathscr{C})=r+1
.

We confirm this conjecture
by proving the following theorem in a strong form.

Theorem 1.11
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a class with sub-exponential expansion and let
r
be a positive integer.

If
\mathscr{C}
contains some graph
G
with
r<\girth(G)<\infty
,
then
\A_{r}(\mathscr{C})=r+1
; otherwise,
\A_{r}(\mathscr{C})=1
.

Generalized
r
-acyclic chromatic index

Given an integer
r\geq 3
,
the
generalized
r
-acyclic chromatic index

\ap_{r}(G)
of a graph
G

[
10
]
is the minimum number of colors in a proper coloration of the edges of
G
such that every cycle
C
of
G
gets at least
\min\{|C|,r\}
colors. (Thus, it is a strengthening of generalized
(r-1)
-arboricity requiring the edge coloration to be proper.)
The case
r=3
is the well-known acyclic edge coloring.

Obviously, if a graph
G
has
maximum degree
\Delta
and girth at least
r
, then
at least
\max\{\Delta,r\}
colors are required for its generalized
r
-acyclic edge colorings.
We prove that every
(r+1)
-path degenerate graph
G
satisfies
\ap_{r}(G)\leq\max\{\Delta,r\}
in
Theorem

4.5
.

In general, graphs with large girth can have arbitrary large generalized
r
-acyclic chromatic indices,
for instance, Greenhill and Pikhurko
[
11
]
proved that for fixed integer
r\geq 4
, there exist graphs
G
such that

\ap_{r}(G)\geq c\Delta^{\lfloor r/2\rfloor}
for some positive constants
c
.
However, if restricted to some nice classes of graphs, large girth graphs may be forced to have small generalized
r
-acyclic chromatic indices, in the sence that they are close to the best possible value of
\max\{\Delta,r\}
.
For example, combining
[
15
, Theorem 3.3]

and
[
26
, Theorem 6]
together,
we obtain
for every integer
r\geq 3
, every planar graph
G
with
\Delta\geq 3
and
\girth(G)\geq 5r+1

\ap_{r}(G)=\max\{\Delta,r\}
.

In this context, we extend the existence of a linear bound for the girth ensuring
\ap_{r}(G)\leq\max\{\Delta,r\}
to all proper minor-closed classes in
Corollary

4.7
.
A more general form, for the class with sub-exponential expansion, is given as follows.

Theorem 1.12
.

Let
r\geq 3
be an integer, let
\mathscr{C}
be a class with sub-exponential class, and let
g(p)
be the girth ensuring
p
-path degeneracy in
\mathscr{C}
.

Then, every graph
G\in\mathscr{C}
with maximum degree
\Delta
and girth at least
g(r+1)
that is not a forest satisfies
\ap_{r}(G)=\max\{\Delta,r\}
.

Given an integer
r\geq 1
, the

r
-coloring number

of a graph

[
17
]
is a minimum integer

The growth rate of weak

In particular, for the class of all

Theorem 1.13

Let

2.
Preliminaries

Let
G=(V(G),E(G))
be a graph.
The
average degree

\avd(G)
is the average of the degrees of
G
, hence
2\|G\|/|G|
(if
G
is not empty, and
0
by convention if
G
is empty).
The
maximum average degree
of a graph
G
, denoted by
\mad(G)
is defined as the maximum of the average degrees of the subgraphs of
G
:
\mad(G)=\max\{\avd(H)\colon H\subseteq G\}
.
The maximum average degree of a class of graphs
\mathscr{C}
is defined by
\mad(\mathscr{C})=\sup\{\mad(G)\colon G\in\mathscr{C}\}
.

A graph
H
is a
minor
of
G
if
H
can be obtained from
G
by means of a sequence of vertex and edge deletions and edge contractions.
A class of graphs
\mathscr{C}
is
minor-closed
if for every graph
G\in\mathscr{C}
, any minor of
G
also belongs to
\mathscr{C}
;
a minor-closed class
\mathscr{C}
is
proper
if it does not contain all finite graphs.
The class of planar graphs is a traditional example of the proper minor-closed classes.

Let
r
be a nonnegative half-integer (that is,
r\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{N}
). A graph
H
is a
shallow topological minor of
G
at depth
r
if a
\leq(2r)
-subdivision of
H
is a subgraph of
G
. More generally,
a graph
H
is a
shallow minor of
G
at depth
r
, if each vertex
v\in V(H)
corresponds to a rooted tree
(T_{v},r_{v})
such that

•

for every vertex
v\in V(H)
,
T_{v}
has radius at most
\lceil r\rceil
, where the radius of
(T_{v},r_{v})
is the maximum length of a path of
T_{v}
with one endpoint
r_{v}
,

•

the graphs
T_{v}
(for
v\in V(H)
) are vertex-disjoint subgraphs of
G
,

•

for every edge
uv\in E(H)
, there exists an edge
e
between
T_{u}
and
T_{v}
such that the length of the path between
r_{u}
and
r_{v}
in
T_{u}\cup T_{v}\cup\{e\}
is at most
2r+1
.

We denote by
G\mathbin{\nabla}r
the class of all shallow minors of
G
at depth
r
, and denote by
\nabla_{r}(G)
the maximum density of a graph in
G\mathbin{\nabla}r
, that is:

\nabla_{r}(G)=\max\biggl\{\frac{\|H\|}{|H|}:H\in G\mathbin{\nabla}r\biggr\}.

The
maximum density
of a minor of
G
, denoted by
\nabla(G)
, is defined as

\nabla(G)=\max\left\{\nabla_{r}(G):r\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
19
, Proposition 4.1]
)
.

Let
a,b,c\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{N}
.
If
(2c+1)\geq(2a+1)(2b+1)
, then for every graph
G
, it holds that

(G\mathbin{\nabla}a)\mathbin{\nabla}b\subseteq G\mathbin{\nabla}c.

The
expansion
of a class
\mathscr{C}
is the function
{\rm Exp}_{\mathscr{C}}:\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{N}\rightarrow[0,+\infty]
defined by

{\rm Exp}_{\mathscr{C}}(r)=\sup\{\nabla_{r}(G)\colon G\in\mathscr{C}\}.

A class
\mathscr{C}
has
bounded expansion
(resp.
sub-exponential expansion
,
polynomial expansion
) if
{\rm Exp}_{\mathscr{C}}(r)
is finite for every half-integer
r
(resp.
{\rm Exp}_{\mathscr{C}}(r)=2^{o(r)}
,
{\rm Exp}_{\mathscr{C}}(r)=r^{O(1)}
). Note that a class
\mathscr{C}
is included in a proper minor-closed class if and only if
{\rm Exp}_{\mathscr{C}}(r)=O(1)
.

3.1.
Definition and basic facts

A
strict ear
of a graph
G
is a path of
G
, whose internal vertices have degree
2
in
G
and whose endpoints are distinct.
Given an integer
p\geq 1
, a

p
-reduction
of a graph
G
is the deletion of

•

either an isolated vertex,

•

or a vertex with degree
1
,

•

or the internal vertices of a strict ear of
G
with length at least
p
.

Remark 1
.

In the third
p
-reduction, we can require the strict ear to have length exactly
p
. Indeed, if a strict ear
P=(u,w_{1},\dots,w_{q-1},z
has length
q>p
, we can first delete the strict ear
(u,w_{1},\dots,w_{p})
, then the remaining vertices
w_{p+1},\dots,w_{q-1}
in order (as they will have degree
1
).

A graph
G
is
p
-path degenerate
if it can be reduced to the empty graph by a sequence of
p
-reductions.
As every graph is obviously
1
-path degenerate, we only consider
p
-path degeneracy for

.

A graph
G
is
p
-reducible
if there exists a
p
-reduction that can be performed on

; otherwise,

is called
p
-irreducible
.
It is clear that every
p
-irreducible graph has minimum degree at least 2.
A

-irreducible
graph

is a
p
-irreducible graph such that every proper subgraph of
G
is
p
-reducible.

The following useful fact is easy to establish.

Fact 1

If
H
is a subgraph of a
p
-path degenerate graph

, then
H
is
p
-path degenerate.

Note that, a graph is
p
-path degenerate if and only if all its connected components are
p
-path degenerate. However, the
p
-path degeneracy of a graph cannot be reduced to its 2-connected components.

We now relate the path degeneracy for graphs with large girth to shallow topological minors.
The next lemma is a useful tool in this section.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G
be a graph with girth at least
2p-1
. Then,
G
is
p
-path degenerate if and only if it does not contain a subgraph
H
that is a
\leq(p-2)
-subdivision of a simple graph with minimum degree at least
3
(that is, it contains no topological minors of depth
\frac{p-2}{2}
with minimum degree at least 3).

Proof.

Assume
G
is not
p
-path degenerate. Let
H
be a minimal subgraph of
G
that is
p
-irreducible. Obviously,
H
is connected and has minimum degree at least 2.
Therefore,
H
is either a cycle with length at most
p
, or a subdivision of a multigraph (possibly with loops)
H^{\prime}
with
\delta(H^{\prime})\geq 3
.
Because
\girth(H)\geq\girth(G)\geq 2p-1
,
H
must be a subdivision of a multigraph
H^{\prime}
with
\delta(H^{\prime})\geq 3
.
Since
H
is
p
-irreducible,
H
is a
\leq(p-2)
-subdivision of a simple graph
H^{\prime}
with minimum degree at least
3
.

Conversely, assume that
G
contains a subgraph
H
that is a
\leq(p-2)
-subdivision of a simple graph
H^{\prime}
with
\delta(H^{\prime})\geq 3
.
Then,
H
is a
p
-irreducible subgraph of
G
.
Therefore, according to
Fact

1
,
G
is not
p
-path degenerate. The lemma is proved.
∎

3.2.
Dense shallow minors of graphs of large girth

The following two lemmas play an essential role in the proofs in this section. They are slightly different from the original results stated in
[
7
,
18
]
, but can be simply derived from their respective proofs.

We first look at

[
7
, Theorem 1]
proved by Diestel and Rempel.

Theorem
.

For any integer
k
, every graph
G
of girth
g(G)>6\log k+3

and
\delta(G)\geq 3
has a minor
H
with
\delta(H)>k
.

Diestel and Rempel proved this theorem by constructing a partition of the vertex set of
G
, where every part induces a subgraph with radius at most
2\lfloor\log k\rfloor
, and every part is adjacent to at least
2^{\lfloor\log k\rfloor+1}
other parts. Obviously, contracting each part of this partition gives a shallow minor
H
of
G
at depth
2\lfloor\log k\rfloor
, and
\delta(H)\geq 2^{\lfloor\log k\rfloor+1}>k
.
Let
r=\lfloor\log k\rfloor
,
we have
\nabla_{2r}(G)\geq\frac{1}{2}\cdot 2^{r+1}=2^{r}
.
Hence, the following lemma follows immediately.

Lemma 3.2

(
[
7
, Proof of Theorem 1]
)
.

Let
r
be an integer and let
G
be a graph. If
\girth(G)>6r+3
and
\delta(G)\geq 3
then
\nabla_{2r}(G)\geq 2^{r}
. ∎

In
[
18
, Theorem 1]
, Kühn and Osthus prove the following result.

Theorem
.

Let

k\geq 1
and
r\geq 3
be integers and put
g:=4k+3
. Then every graph
G
of minimum degree
r
and girth at least
g
contains a minor of minimum degree at least
(r-1)^{k+1}/48=(r-1)^{(g+1)/4}/48.

A closer look at the proof of this theorem in
[
18
]

shows that a minor
M
with depth
k
is constructed, which has average degree at least
(r-1)^{k+1}/24
and from which a minor with minimum degree
(r-1)^{k+1}/48
is extracted. The minor
M
itself witnesses
\nabla_{k}(G)\geq\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{(r-1)^{k+1}}{24}\geq\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{2^{k+1}}{24}=\frac{2^{k}}{24}
.
Hence, we deduce the following.

Lemma 3.3

(
[
18
, Proof of Theorem 1]
)
.

Let
r\geq 1
be an integer and let
G
be a graph. If
\girth(G)\geq 4r+3
and

then
\nabla_{r}(G)\geq\frac{2^{r}}{24}
. ∎

3.4.
Classes with polynomial expansion

Classes with polynomial expansion have recently attracted a lot of attention, because they not only include proper minor-closed classes, but also a large diversity of geometrically defined classes of graphs.
(In particular, every class of intersection graphs of objects (of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
) in a family with low density has polynomial expansion
[
13
]
.)
Moreover, these classes are exactly those have strongly sublinear vertex separators
[
9
]
, giving them very interesting algorithmic properties.

Before we proceed to our analysis of classes with polynomial expansion, we take time to
prove some technical lemma related to the inequality
A\log x+B<x
.

Recall the Lambert
\mathop{W}
function.
Let
\mathop{W_{0}}
(resp.
\mathop{W_{-1}}
) be principal branch (resp. the branch with index
-1
) of Lambert
\mathop{W_{-1}}
function, which are the branches of the converse relation of the function
z\mapsto ze^{z}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
A,B>0
be reals, and let

\beta\geq 0
be minimum such that the inequation

x>A\log x+B

is satisfied for all
x>\beta
.

If
B\leq A(1-\log A)
, then
\beta=0
; otherwise,
\beta=-A\mathop{W_{-1}}(-e^{-B/A}/A)
.

Proof.

This inequation rewrites as
y>e^{B/A}A\log y
, where
y=e^{B/A}x
, that is
\log y/y<e^{-B/A}/A
.
If
Ae^{B/A}\leq e
, i.e.
B\leq A(1-\log A)
, this is always satisfied as the maximum of
\log y/y
is
1/e
.

Hence, we can assume
B>A(1-\log A)
.
Let
\alpha=Ae^{B/A}
.
The equation
\log y/y=e^{-B/A}/A

rewrites as

ze^{z}=-1/\alpha
, where
y=e^{-z}
. The equation
ze^{z}=-1/\alpha
has two solutions, namely
\mathop{W_{0}}(-1/\alpha)
and
\mathop{W_{-1}}(-1/\alpha)
.
As (for every complex
t
)
e^{-\mathop{W_{0}}(t)}=\mathop{W_{0}}(t)/t
and
e^{-\mathop{W_{-1}}(t)}=\mathop{W_{-1}}(t)/t
we deduce that we have two solutions for
y
. Precisely,
as
y
increases from
0
to
+\infty
, the value of
\log y/y
first increases, reaches
1/\alpha
for
y=-\alpha\mathop{W_{0}}(-1/\alpha)
, then reaches
1/e
at
y=e
, then decreases, reaches
1/\alpha
for
x=-\alpha\mathop{W_{-1}}(-1/\alpha)
and then continues to decrease as
y\rightarrow+\infty
. Thus, the threshold value for
y
is

-\alpha\mathop{W_{-1}}(-1/\alpha)
.

Thus, we get

\beta=-e^{-B/A}\alpha\mathop{W_{-1}}(-1/\alpha)=-A\mathop{W_{-1}}(-e^{-B/A}/A)
.
∎

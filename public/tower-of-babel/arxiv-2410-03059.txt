Lower Bounds for the Trotter Error
Alexander Hahn, Paul Hartung, Daniel Burgarth, Paolo Facchi, Kazuya Yuasa

Abstract
In analog and digital simulations of practically relevant quantum systems, the target dynamics can only be implemented approximately. The Trotter product formula is the most common approximation scheme as it is a generic method which allows tuning accuracy. The Trotter simulation precision will always be inexact for non-commuting operators, but it is currently unknown what the minimum possible error is. This is an important quantity because upper bounds for the Trotter error are known to often be vast overestimates. Here, we present explicit lower bounds on the error, in norm and on states, allowing to derive minimum resource requirements. Numerical comparison with the true error shows that our bounds offer accurate and tight estimates.



Introduction.
—Finding approximate solutions to the Schrödinger equation by simulating the time evolution under a given Hamiltonian is one of the most important tasks in quantum mechanics.
To tackle this problem, many simulation algorithms have been developed, which can be broadly categorized into three classes: (i) analog quantum simulation
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
, (ii) digital quantum simulation
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
, and (iii) classical numerical simulation
[
8
]
.
One of the most commonly used approximation methods across all disciplines is the Trotter product formula.
This is mainly for two reasons.
First, the Trotter product formula is a generic way to implement intricate quantum dynamics only by switching quickly between simpler dynamics.
Second, the accuracy of the Trotter approximation can be tuned to a desired precision by optimizing the small evolution times of each step.

To assess the simulation precision in practice, it is necessary to study the Trotter error, i.e. the difference between the Trotterized and target evolutions.
Due to its fundamental importance in simulations of quantum systems, countless work has been done on studying the Trotter error.
The current state-of-the-art is presented in Ref.
[
6
]
for the norm Trotter error and in Ref.
[
9
]
for the state-dependent Trotter error.
These results give upper bounds and explicitly quantify the worst-case scenario.
However, they often do not capture the
actual
Trotter error well and tend to overestimate it
[
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
]
.
This creates an urge for lower bounds specifying the best-case scenario.
Such lower bounds would provide a possible corridor for the true Trotter error, enabling a solid simulation precision analysis.
Furthermore, as lower bounds quantify the minimum possible error, they could rule out the practical usefulness of certain quantum simulation applications, or enable a complexity comparison among certain classical and quantum algorithms.
Nevertheless, little progress has been made in recent years in bounding algorithmic complexities from below.
This is because the derivation of non-trivial lower bounds has turned out to be extremely challenging.
Here, we present explicit lower bounds on the Trotter errors, both in norm and on eigenstates.

Such lower bounds are relevant from different perspectives:
(i) Hamiltonian complexity: Combining the known upper bounds with lower bounds shows the complexity of implementing Trotterization in terms of the simulation parameters.
So far, only circuit
[
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
]
and query lower bounds
[
20
,
21
,
22
]
are known for general quantum circuits approximating a unitary
\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}
.
These do not answer the question of the actual simulation complexity in terms of the relevant Trotter simulation parameters.
In particular, the circuit lower bounds do not specify how large the Trotter error becomes, but only bound below the gate complexity in terms of a desired simulation accuracy.
The query lower bounds (no fast-forwarding theorem) only imply a very loose complexity of
\Omega(t)
, which is not quantitative and does not even depend on the number of Trotter steps
n
.
Here, we show that the norm Trotter error for a finite-dimensional system scales as
\Omega(\max\{(t^{2}-t^{3})/n,t/n-t^{2}/n^{2}\})
.
(ii) Resource estimates for simulation algorithms: Lower bounds on the Trotter errors give a strict limit on how many Trotter steps
n
are necessary
at least
to achieve a desired simulation accuracy.
This is particularly useful for resource estimates for digital
and
analog quantum simulation on near-term quantum devices with limited capabilities
[
23
,
6
]
.
For these, lower bounds on the Trotter errors can determine whether the execution of a digital or analog quantum simulation algorithm is feasible.
By providing explicit bounds, our results enable the performance of such resource estimates.
(iii) Mathematical physics: The study of asymptotic Trotter convergence has a long history in mathematical physics
[
24
,
25
]
.
Here, important questions are: under which conditions does the Trotter product formula converge, in which topology, and which convergence speed does it admit?
We prove that the Trotter limit converges uniformly as
\Theta(1/n)
for finite-dimensional systems.
(iv) Quantum speed limits: Speed limits are fundamental limitations for steering a quantum system from one state to another.
They have been used to determine the minimum time to control a quantum system
[
26
,
27
]
or to run a quantum computation
[
28
,
29
]
.
Our bounds give a fundamental precision limit for Trotterization and thus a speed limit for its convergence: we can do as much as
1/n
.
In turn, we obtain a speed limit for the convergence of Trotter-based quantum control and quantum computation schemes.

Setting.
—We focus on the case where a Hamiltonian is decomposed into two realizable components as the behavior of the Trotter error remains insufficiently characterized already in this context
[
14
]
.
Then, Trotterization aims to implement the time evolution
\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t(A+B)}
under a Hamiltonian
H=A+B
for some time
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
This is done by alternating between the dynamics under
A
and
B
for short time steps
t/n
, i.e.
\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}(A+B)t}\approx(\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}At/n}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}Bt/n})^{n}
with
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
In the language of analog quantum simulation, one Trotter cycle
\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}At/n}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}Bt/n}
represents the evolution for one driving period.
Here, the individual evolutions
\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}At/n}
and
\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}Bt/n}
are the dynamics induced by respective control pulses.
In the case of digital quantum simulation, the individual dynamics
\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}At/n}
and
\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}Bt/n}
are realized on qubits, decomposed into elementary quantum gates.
For classical simulation algorithms, exponentiation of digital representations of
A
and
B
can usually be performed efficiently. See, for instance, the split-step algorithm
[
8
]
.

In the following, we assume that

A
and
B
are bounded Hermitian operators acting on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
.
For an eigenstate
\varphi
of the Hermitian operator
A+B
satisfying
(A+B)\varphi=h\varphi
, we define the state-dependent Trotter error by

\xi_{n}(t;\varphi)\equiv\left\|\left(\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{n}A}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{n}B}\right)^{n}\varphi-\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}th}\varphi\right\|,

where
\|\psi\|=\sqrt{\langle\psi|\psi\rangle}
is the norm of
\psi\in\mathcal{H}
.
Analogously, the norm Trotter error is defined by

b_{n}(t)\equiv\left\|\left(\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{n}A}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{n}B}\right)^{n}-\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t(A+B)}\right\|_{\mathrm{op}},

where
\|X\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\sup_{\|\psi\|=1}\|X\psi\|
denotes the operator norm.
The operator norm
\|X\|_{\mathrm{op}}
gives the largest singular value of
X
, and we have

\|X\|_{\mathrm{op}}\geq\|X\psi\|
, for all normalized

\psi\in\mathcal{H}
.
To describe the asymptotic behaviors of the Trotter errors in terms of a system or simulation parameter
x
, one uses the notation
\mathcal{O}(f(x))
for the scaling of an upper bound and
\Omega(g(x))
for the scaling of a lower bound.

\mathcal{O}(f(x))
indicates that for sufficiently large
x
, the Trotter error grows no faster than a constant multiple of
f(x)
.
Analogously,
\Omega(g(x))
means that the Trotter error grows at least as fast as a constant multiple of
g(x)
for a sufficiently large
x
.
If the scalings of the upper and lower bounds agree, i.e. if
f=g
up to a constant factor, the result is tight and one writes
\Theta(f(x))
.
It is known that
\xi_{n}(t;\varphi)=\mathcal{O}(t^{2}/n)

[
30
,
9
]
and
b_{n}(t)=\mathcal{O}(t^{2}/n)

[
31
,
6
]
.
In particular, we have for all
g\in\mathbb{R}

[
30
,
9
]

\xi_{n}(t;\varphi)\leq\frac{t^{2}}{2n}\,\Bigl(\|(A-h+g)^{2}\varphi\|)+\|(B-g)^{2}\varphi\|\Bigr)

(1)

and
[
31
,
6
]

b_{n}(t)\leq\frac{t^{2}}{2n}\|[A,B]\|_{\mathrm{op}}.

(2)

Since the bound (
1
) holds for all
g\in\mathbb{R}
, we can take the infimum over
g
to obtain the tightest bound.

Main results.
—We now complete the picture of the Trotter error analysis by presenting lower bounds on
\xi_{n}(t;\varphi)
and
b_{n}(t)
.

State-Dependent Trotter Error
.

Consider two normalized eigenstates
\varphi
and
\psi
of the Hamiltonian
A+B
, i.e.
(A+B)\varphi=h\varphi
and
(A+B)\psi=\kappa\psi
.
Assume that
h\neq\kappa

[
32
]
and define the spectral gap
\lambda\equiv|h-\kappa|
.
Then, for all
g\in\mathbb{R}
and

t\geq 0
, the state-dependent Trotter error
\xi_{n}(t;\varphi)
can be bounded from below by

\displaystyle\xi_{n}(t;\varphi)\geq{}

\displaystyle\left|\sin\left(\frac{\lambda t}{2}\right)\right|\left(\frac{t}{n}\bigl|\langle\psi|A\varphi\rangle\bigr|-\frac{t^{2}}{4n^{2}}\|(A-g)^{2}\varphi\|\right)

\displaystyle{}-\frac{t^{3}}{24n^{2}}\|[A,[A,B]]\|_{\mathrm{op}}-\frac{t^{3}}{12n^{2}}\|[B,[B,A]]\|_{\mathrm{op}}.

(3)

This bound is proved in Sec.
B
of the Supplemental Material (SM)
[
33
]
.
Since this bound holds for all
g\in\mathbb{R}
, we can take the supremum over
g
to obtain the tightest bound.
This involves taking the
\inf_{g}\|(A-g)^{2}\varphi\|
.
Furthermore, we can take the supremum over all suitable eigenstates
\psi
to obtain the tightest result.
This bound shows that the state-dependent Trotter error
\xi_{n}(t;\varphi)
scales as
\Omega(t/n-t^{2}/n^{2})
.
Combining this result with the upper bound (
1
) proves that the state-dependent Trotter error
\xi_{n}(t;\varphi)
diminishes as
\Theta(1/n)
on all eigenstates
\varphi
of
A+B
.
We can always choose the Trotter number
n
big enough to make the lower bound (
3
) non-trivial (larger than zero).
Such
n
can be explicitly calculated as shown in the SM
[
33
]
.
See Eq. (
14
) of Sec.
B
.

Norm Trotter Error
.

The norm Trotter error
b_{n}(t)
for the Hamiltonian
A+B
can be bounded from below by

\displaystyle b_{n}(t)\geq{}

\displaystyle\frac{t^{2}}{2n}\left(1-\frac{t}{2n}\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\right)

\displaystyle\hphantom{\frac{t^{2}}{2n}}{}\times\left(\|[A,B]\|_{\mathrm{op}}-\frac{t}{2}\|[A+B,[A,B]]\|_{\mathrm{op}}\right)

\displaystyle{}-\frac{t^{3}}{24n^{2}}\|[A,[A,B]]\|_{\mathrm{op}}-\frac{t^{3}}{12n^{2}}\|[B,[B,A]]\|_{\mathrm{op}},

(4)

for
0\leq t\leq 2n/\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}
.

A slightly tighter version of this bound is provided in the SM
[
33
]
.
See Eq. (
15

.
Our bound shows that the norm Trotter error
b_{n}(t)
scales as
\Omega((t^{2}-t^{3})/n)
.
Together with the upper bound (
2

b_{n}(t)
diminishes uniformly as
\Theta(1/n)
.
Furthermore, the bound is sharp in the sense that it is saturated by two commuting Hamiltonians
[A,B]=0
.
For
[A,B]\neq 0
, we can always make the lower bound (
4
) non-trivial by choosing
t
small enough.
Such

is computed in the SM
[

]

See Eq. (

The lower bound on the norm Trotter error
b_{n}(t)
can be further improved by taking the maximum with the lower bound on the state-dependent Trotter error
\xi_{n}(t;\varphi)

Recall that the lower bound on the state-dependent Trotter error
\xi_{n}(t;\varphi)
is non-trivial for large

b_{n}(t)
is non-trivial for small
t

Taking the maximum between these lower bounds allows for a wide corridor of simulation parameters where we can give a non-trivial lower bound on the Trotter error.
We now show two example systems, for which this is indeed the case, and compare our bounds with actual errors.

Conclusion.
—To summarize, we provide lower bounds for the Trotter error
for bounded Hamiltonians, both in the operator norm and on eigenstates of the target dynamics.
The state-dependent error scales as
\Omega\big(t/n-t^{2}/n^{2}\big)
and the norm error admits a
\Omega\big(\max\{(t^{2}-t^{3})/n,t/n-t^{2}/n^{2}\}\big)
scaling.
Overall, this proves that the finite-dimensional Trotter product formula converges as
\Theta(1/n)
.
Our bounds are tested numerically showing that they genuinely estimate the the true Trotter error.
To the best of our knowledge, this is the first time that the Trotter error is non-trivially bounded from below in terms of all simulation parameters.
This enables a better understanding of practical simulation errors, allowing a more precise fine-tuning of parameters in both digital and analog simulations.
This is particularly important for quantum simulation tasks, which are believed to be one of the most promising potential applications of quantum information science.

Appendix A
A. Notation, Proof Idea, and Basic Lemma

In this section, we introduce the notation used throughout the proofs of the main results.
Throughout the entire Supplemental Material, we will assume that
A
and
B
are bounded Hamiltonians (Hermitian operators) acting on a
Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
.
They generate unitary one-parameter groups
\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tA}
and
\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tB}
, respectively, where the parameter
t\in\mathbb{R}
is the time.
We introduce the operator norm
\|X\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\sup_{\|\psi\|=1}\|X\psi\|
, where
\|\psi\|=\sqrt{\langle\psi,\psi\rangle}
is the
norm of vectors in
\mathcal{H}
.
For matrices, the operator norm computes to the largest singular value.
It is a unitarily invariant matrix norm.
We remark that our proof method works for any (submultiplicative)
norm as long as it is unitarily invariant.

In the following two sections, we will provide proofs for the lower bounds on the Trotter errors, one in norm and the other on an eigenstate.
The two proofs follow the same idea, which we would like to present here informally.
Let

T_{n}(t)\equiv\left(\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{n}A}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{n}B}\right)^{n}

(6)

be the first-order Trotter product formula, let

T_{n}^{(2)}(t)\equiv\left(\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{n}B}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}\right)^{n}

(7)

be the second-order Trotter product formula, and let

T(t)\equiv\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t(A+B)}

be the target evolution that we aim to approximate through Trotterization.
First, we observe that the first-order Trotter product (
6
) and the second-order Trotter product (
7
) only differ by boundary terms, i.e.,

T_{n}^{(2)}(t)=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}T_{n}(t)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}.

This has also been noticed in Ref.
[
14
]
, where the author used this fact to prove tighter upper bounds for the first-order Trotterization.
From Ref.
[
6
]
, we know that

T_{n}^{(2)}(t)-T(t)=\mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{t^{3}}{n^{2}}\right),

which is equivalent to

\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}T_{n}(t)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}-T(t)=\mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{t^{3}}{n^{2}}\right).

In turn,

T_{n}(t)-\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}T(t)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}=\mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{t^{3}}{n^{2}}\right).

(8)

Since Eq. (
8
) is dominated by any term that scales as
\mathcal{O}(1/n)
, it is useful for a reverse triangle inequality.
In particular, we can insert a zero in the Trotter error as

T_{n}(t)-T(t)=T_{n}(t)-\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}T(t)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}+\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}T(t)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}-T(t).

By the reverse triangle inequality, the Trotter error can be bounded below by

b_{n}(t)\equiv\|T_{n}(t)-T(t)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\geq\left\|\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}T(t)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}-T(t)\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}-\left\|T_{n}(t)-\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}T(t)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}

(9)

in norm, and by

\xi_{n}(t;\varphi)\equiv\|[T_{n}(t)-T(t)]\varphi\|\geq\left\|\left(\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}T(t)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}-T(t)\right)\varphi\right\|-\left\|\left(T_{n}(t)-\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}T(t)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}\right)\varphi\right\|

(10)

for vectors
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}
.
The proof of the bounds then reduces to the technical task of bounding these two terms.

To do so, we will use the notion of the adjoint representation:
Given a bounded Hamiltonian
H
acting on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, its adjoint representation
\operatorname{ad}_{H}
is given by the commutator
\operatorname{ad}_{H}=[H,{}\bullet{}]
.
In the same way as
H
generates a one-parameter group
U(t)=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}
, the adjoint representation
\operatorname{ad}_{H}
does via
\operatorname{Ad}_{U(t)}=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t\operatorname{ad}_{H}}
.
It is well known that this can be rewritten as
\operatorname{Ad}_{U(t)}=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\bullet\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}tH}
.
For a (bounded) Hamiltonian
H
, the adjoint representation
\operatorname{ad}_{H}
is the (bounded) generator of a group of isometries on the Banach space
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
of bounded linear operators.
Therefore, the same algebraic relations as for
H
and
U(t)
on
\mathcal{H}
also hold for
\operatorname{ad}_{H}
and
\operatorname{Ad}_{U(t)}
on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
.
See Ref.
[
36
]
for details.
This fact will turn out to be very useful for proving the bound on the Trotter error in norm.
For example, we will use Ref.
[
37
, Lemma 14]
, which also holds for the adjoint representation.
Let us recapitulate Ref.
[
37
, Lemma 14]
here and extend its statement to the adjoint representation.

Lemma 1
.

Let
H=H^{\dagger}
and
t\geq 0
. Then,

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}-I

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{i}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}sH}H\,\mathrm{d}s,

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}-I+\mathrm{i}tH

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}sH}H^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s,

and analogously for the adjoint representation

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t\operatorname{ad}_{H}}-\mathrm{id}

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{i}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}s\operatorname{ad}_{H}}\operatorname{ad}_{H}\mathrm{d}s,

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t\operatorname{ad}_{H}}-\mathrm{id}+\mathrm{i}t\operatorname{ad}_{H}

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}s\operatorname{ad}_{H}}\operatorname{ad}_{H}^{2}\mathrm{d}s,

where
I
and
\mathrm{id}
are the identity operator and the identity map, respectively.
Therefore, by taking norms we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}-I\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq t\|H\|_{\mathrm{op}},

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}-I+\mathrm{i}tH\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\frac{1}{2}t^{2}\|H^{2}\|_{\mathrm{op}},

and analogously for all
X\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})

\displaystyle\|(\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t\operatorname{ad}_{H}}-\mathrm{id})(X)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq t\|[H,X]\|_{\mathrm{op}},

\displaystyle\|(\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t\operatorname{ad}_{H}}-\mathrm{id}+\mathrm{i}t\operatorname{ad}_{H})(X)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\frac{1}{2}t^{2}\|[H,[H,X]]\|_{\mathrm{op}}.

Proof.

This is an application of the Taylor polynomial of the exponential function with remainder in integral form. See e.g. Ref.
[
37
, Lemma 14]
.
∎

Appendix B
B. Lower Bound on the State-Dependent Trotter Error
\xi_{n}(t;\varphi)

In this section, we prove the lower bound on the state-dependent Trotter error
\xi_{n}(t;\varphi)
presented inEq. (
3
) of the main text.

Proof of the Lower Bound on the State-Dependent Trotter Error
\xi_{n}(t;\varphi)
.

By assumption, we have
(A+B)\varphi=h\varphi
.
The first step is to shift the energy of
A+B
so that
(A+B)\varphi=0
.
Bounds for arbitrary states with
(A+B)\varphi=h\varphi
can then be retrieved by shifting back the energy at the end.
This idea has also been employed in Refs.
[
30
,
9
]
to prove upper bounds for the Trotter error on eigenstates.

After the energy shift, we start with Eq. (
10
).
Let us look at the first term.
Using
T(t)\varphi=\varphi
, we can write

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}T(t)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}-T(t)\right)\varphi\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\left(T(t)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}-\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}\right)\varphi\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|[T(t)-I]\left(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}-I\right)\varphi\right\|

\displaystyle\geq\left|\left\langle\psi\Big|[T(t)-I]\left(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}-I\right)\varphi\right\rangle\right|,

for all normalized vector
\psi\neq\varphi
.
By assuming that
(A+B)\psi=\kappa\psi
, we get

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}T(t)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}-T(t)\right)\varphi\right\|

\displaystyle\geq\left|\left\langle(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\kappa t}-1)\psi\Big|\left(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}-I\right)\varphi\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=2\left|\sin\left(\frac{\kappa t}{2}\right)\right|\left|\left\langle\psi\Big|\left(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}-I\right)\varphi\right\rangle\right|.

We further proceed as

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}T(t)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}-T(t)\right)\varphi\right\|

\displaystyle\geq 2\left|\sin\left(\frac{\kappa t}{2}\right)\right|\left|\left\langle\psi\bigg|\left(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}-I-\frac{\mathrm{i}t}{2n}A\right)\varphi\right\rangle+\frac{\mathrm{i}t}{2n}\langle\psi|A\varphi\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\geq 2\left|\sin\left(\frac{\kappa t}{2}\right)\right|\left(\frac{t}{2n}|\langle\psi|A\varphi\rangle|-\left|\left\langle\psi\bigg|\left(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}-I-\frac{\mathrm{i}t}{2n}A\right)\varphi\right\rangle\right|\right)

(11)

\displaystyle\geq 2\left|\sin\left(\frac{\kappa t}{2}\right)\right|\left(\frac{t}{2n}|\langle\psi|A\varphi\rangle|-\left\|\left(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}-I-\frac{\mathrm{i}t}{2n}A\right)\varphi\right\|\right)

\displaystyle\geq 2\left|\sin\left(\frac{\kappa t}{2}\right)\right|\left(\frac{t}{2n}|\langle\psi|A\varphi\rangle|-\frac{t^{2}}{8n^{2}}\|A^{2}\varphi\|\right),

(12)

where the third step follows from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the
last step is a consequence of Lemma
1
.
We remark that from Eq. (
11
), we could have alternatively bounded

\left\|\left(\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}T(t)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}-T(t)\right)\varphi\right\|\geq 2\left|\sin\left(\frac{\kappa t}{2}\right)\right|\left(\frac{t}{2n}|\langle\psi|A\varphi\rangle|-\frac{t^{2}}{8n^{2}}\|A\varphi\|\|A\psi\|\right).

However, in the examples considered we found that the first bound (
12
) is tighter, which is why we use it here.

For the second term of Eq. (
10
), we can bound it as

\displaystyle\left\|\left(T_{n}(t)-\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}T(t)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}\right)\varphi\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|T_{n}(t)-\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}T(t)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}T_{n}(t)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}-T(t)\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle=\|T_{n}^{(2)}(t)-T(t)\|_{\mathrm{op}}.\vphantom{\left\|\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}\right\|}

An error bound for this distance can be found in Ref.
[
6
, Eq. (L5)]
,

\|T_{n}^{(2)}(t)-T(t)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\frac{t^{3}}{24n^{2}}\|[A,[A,B]]\|_{\mathrm{op}}+\frac{t^{3}}{12n^{2}}\|[B,[B,A]]\|_{\mathrm{op}}.

(13)

In total, we get

\xi_{n}(t;\varphi)=\|[T_{n}(t)-T(t)]\varphi\|\geq\left|\sin\left(\frac{\kappa t}{2}\right)\right|\left(\frac{t}{n}|\langle\psi|A\varphi\rangle|-\frac{t^{2}}{4n^{2}}\|A^{2}\varphi\|\right)-\frac{t^{3}}{24n^{2}}\|[A,[A,B]]\|_{\mathrm{op}}-\frac{t^{3}}{12n^{2}}\|[B,[B,A]]\|_{\mathrm{op}}.

The last step is to shift back the energy and to notice that we can always add any multiple
g\in\mathbb{R}
of the identity to
A
and
B
without changing the Trotter error.
This leads to the replacements
\kappa\rightarrow|h-\kappa|
as well as
A\rightarrow A-h+g
and
B\rightarrow B+g
.
Since the latter two replacements only affect the term
\|A^{2}\varphi\|
in the bound and
g\in\mathbb{R}
is arbitrary, Eq. (
3
) of the main text follows.
∎

To explicitly see the dependence of this bound on
t
,
we further bound

\left|\sin\left(\frac{\kappa t}{2}\right)\right|\geq 1-\left|1-\frac{\kappa t}{\pi}+2\left\lfloor\frac{\kappa t}{2\pi}\right\rfloor\right|,

so that we have

\xi_{n}(t;\varphi)=\Omega\!\left(\frac{t}{n}-\frac{t^{2}}{n^{2}}\right).

Together with Ref.
[
30
, Main Result 1]
, this proves that the Trotter product converges strongly as
\Theta(1/n)
.
Since the norm is nonnegative, we have

\xi_{n}(t;\varphi)\geq\max\!\left\{0,\left|\sin\left(\frac{\kappa t}{2}\right)\right|\left(\frac{t}{n}|\langle\psi|A\varphi\rangle|-\frac{t^{2}}{4n^{2}}\|A^{2}\varphi\|\right)-\frac{t^{3}}{24n^{2}}\|[A,[A,B]]\|_{\mathrm{op}}-\frac{t^{3}}{12n^{2}}\|[B,[B,A]]\|_{\mathrm{op}}\right\}.

If
[A,B]=0
, we have
\xi_{n}(t;\varphi)=0
, while the bound reduces to

\xi_{n}(t;\varphi)\geq\max\!\left\{0,-\left|\sin\left(\frac{\kappa t}{2}\right)\right|\frac{t^{2}}{4n^{2}}\|A^{2}\varphi\|\right\}=0.

For the more interesting case of
[A,B]\neq 0
, the bound can be made non-trivial by choosing
n
large enough, as long as
\sin(\kappa t/2)\neq 0
.
More explicitly, the bound is non-trivial for all

n\geq\frac{t}{4|\langle\psi|A\varphi\rangle|}\left[\|A^{2}\varphi\|+\frac{t}{6|{\sin(\kappa t/2)}|}\,\Bigl(\|[A,[A,B]]\|+2\|[B,[B,A]]\|\Bigr)\right].

(14)

Appendix C
C. Lower Bound on the Norm Trotter Error
b_{n}(t)

In this section, we prove a tighter version of the lower bound on the norm Trotter error
b_{n}(t)
.
That is, the bound

\displaystyle b_{n}(t)={}

\displaystyle\|T_{n}(t)-T(t)\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\geq{}

\displaystyle\max\!\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{2n}\left(1-\frac{t}{2n}\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\right)Z_{1}(t),\frac{t^{2}}{2n}\left(Z_{1}(t)-\frac{t}{2n}\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}Z_{2}(t)\right)\right\}-\frac{t^{3}}{24n^{2}}\|[A,[A,B]]\|_{\mathrm{op}}-\frac{t^{3}}{12n^{2}}\|[B,[B,A]]\|_{\mathrm{op}},

(15)

valid for
0\leq t\leq 2n/\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}
, where

\displaystyle Z_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=\|[A,B]\|_{\mathrm{op}}-t\min\!\left\{\frac{1}{2}\|[A+B,[A,B]]\|_{\mathrm{op}},\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|(A+B)^{2}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\right\},

\displaystyle Z_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\|[A,[A,B]]\|_{\mathrm{op}}}{2\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}}+t\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|(A+B)^{2}\|_{\mathrm{op}}.

Note that

\|[A,B]\|_{\mathrm{op}}\geq\frac{\|[A,[A,B]]\|_{\mathrm{op}}}{2\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}},\qquad\frac{1}{2}\|[A+B,[A,B]]\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq 2\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|A+B\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{2}.

Thus the bound presented in Eq. (
4
) of the main text is a loose version,

b_{n}(t)\geq\frac{t^{2}}{2n}\left(1-\frac{t}{2n}\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\right)\left(\|[A,B]\|_{\mathrm{op}}-\frac{t}{2}\|[A+B,[A,B]]\|_{\mathrm{op}}\right)-\frac{t^{3}}{24n^{2}}\|[A,[A,B]]\|_{\mathrm{op}}-\frac{t^{3}}{12n^{2}}\|[B,[B,A]]\|_{\mathrm{op}}.

(16)

We state this bound in the main text, since it actually maximizes the bound in Eq. (
15
) for the
XX
spin chain we examine, and we expect it to be the best bound for most spin models.

Proof of the Lower Bound on the Norm Trotter Error
b_{n}(t)
.

We start with Eq. (
9
).
The first term can be bounded from below as

\displaystyle\left\|\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}T(t)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}-T(t)\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\left(\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}\operatorname{ad}_{A}}-\mathrm{id}\right)(T(t))\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\left(\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}\operatorname{ad}_{A}}-\mathrm{id}+\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}\operatorname{ad}_{A}-\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}\operatorname{ad}_{A}\right)(T(t))\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{t}{2n}\|{\operatorname{ad}_{A}(T(t))}\|_{\mathrm{op}}-\left\|\left(\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}\operatorname{ad}_{A}}-\mathrm{id}+\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}\operatorname{ad}_{A}\right)(T(t))\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{t}{2n}\|{\operatorname{ad}_{A}(T(t))}\|_{\mathrm{op}}-\frac{t^{2}}{8n^{2}}\|{\operatorname{ad}_{A}^{2}(T(t))}\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle=\frac{t}{2n}\|[A,T(t)]\|_{\mathrm{op}}-\frac{t^{2}}{8n^{2}}\|[A,[A,T(t)]]\|_{\mathrm{op}},

(17)

where the reverse triangle inequality and a consequence of Lemma
1
are used in the third and fourth steps, respectively.

Let us bound
\|[A,T(t)]\|_{\mathrm{op}}
from below.
We here provide two bounds.
The first one is obtained as

\displaystyle\|[A,T(t)]\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle=\|A\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t(A+B)}-\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t(A+B)}A\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle=\|\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t(A+B)}A\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t(A+B)}-A\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle=\|(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\operatorname{ad}_{A+B}}-\mathrm{id})(A)\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle=\|(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\operatorname{ad}_{A+B}}-\mathrm{id}-\mathrm{i}t\operatorname{ad}_{A+B}+\mathrm{i}t\operatorname{ad}_{A+B})(A)\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\geq t\|{\operatorname{ad}_{A+B}(A)}\|_{\mathrm{op}}-\|(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\operatorname{ad}_{A+B}}-\mathrm{id}-\mathrm{i}t\operatorname{ad}_{A+B})(A)\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\geq t\|{\operatorname{ad}_{A+B}(A)}\|_{\mathrm{op}}-\frac{t^{2}}{2}\|{\operatorname{ad}_{A+B}^{2}(A)}\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle=t\|[A,B]\|_{\mathrm{op}}-\frac{t^{2}}{2}\|[A+B,[A,B]]\|_{\mathrm{op}},

while another one is obtained as

\displaystyle\|[A,T(t)]\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle=\|[A,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t(A+B)}]\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle=\|[A,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t(A+B)}-I+\mathrm{i}t(A+B)-\mathrm{i}t(A+B)]\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\geq t\|[A,A+B]\|_{\mathrm{op}}-\|[A,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t(A+B)}-I+\mathrm{i}t(A+B)]\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\geq t\|[A,B]\|_{\mathrm{op}}-t^{2}\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|(A+B)^{2}\|_{\mathrm{op}}.

Combining these bounds, we get

\displaystyle\|[A,T(t)]\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\geq t\|[A,B]\|_{\mathrm{op}}-t^{2}\min\!\left\{\frac{1}{2}\|[A+B,[A,B]]\|_{\mathrm{op}},\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|(A+B)^{2}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\right\}=tZ_{1}(t).

Let us next bound
\|[A,[A,T(t)]]\|_{\mathrm{op}}
from above.
We again take two different strategies.
The first one proceeds as

\displaystyle\|[A,[A,T(t)]]\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle=\|[A,[A,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t(A+B)}]]\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle=\|A^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t(A+B)}-2A\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t(A+B)}A+\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t(A+B)}A^{2}\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle=\|\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t(A+B)}A^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t(A+B)}-2\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t(A+B)}A\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t(A+B)}A+A^{2}\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle=\Bigl\|\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t(A+B)}A\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t(A+B)}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t(A+B)}A\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t(A+B)}-A)-(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t(A+B)}A\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t(A+B)}-A)A\Bigr\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle=\Bigl\|\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\operatorname{ad}_{A+B}}(A)(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\operatorname{ad}_{A+B}}-\mathrm{id})(A)-(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\operatorname{ad}_{A+B}}-\mathrm{id})(A)A\Bigr\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\operatorname{ad}_{A+B}}-\mathrm{id})(A)\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\leq 2t\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|{\operatorname{ad}_{A+B}(A)}\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle=2t\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|[A,B]\|_{\mathrm{op}},

where the inequality in the penultimate step follows from Lemma
1
. The other strategy proceeds as

\displaystyle\|[A,[A,T(t)]]\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle=\|[A,[A,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t(A+B)}]]\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle=\|[A,[A,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t(A+B)}-I+\mathrm{i}t(A+B)-\mathrm{i}t(A+B)]]\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\leq t\|[A,[A,A+B]]\|_{\mathrm{op}}+\|[A,[A,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t(A+B)}-I+\mathrm{i}t(A+B)]]\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\leq t\|[A,[A,B]]\|_{\mathrm{op}}+4\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{2}\|\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t(A+B)}-I+\mathrm{i}t(A+B)\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\leq t\|[A,[A,B]]\|_{\mathrm{op}}+2t^{2}\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{2}\|(A+B)^{2}\|_{\mathrm{op}},

where the last step again invokes Lemma
1
. Thus,

\displaystyle\|[A,[A,T(t)]]\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\leq 2t\min\!\left\{\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|[A,B]\|_{\mathrm{op}},\frac{1}{2}\|[A,[A,B]]\|_{\mathrm{op}}+t\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{2}\|(A+B)^{2}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\right\}

\displaystyle=2t\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\min\Bigl\{\|[A,B]\|_{\mathrm{op}},Z_{2}(t)\Bigr\}.

Using these bounds, we can bound Eq. (
17
) as

\left\|\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}T(t)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}-T(t)\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}\geq\frac{t^{2}}{2n}Z_{1}(t)-\frac{t^{3}}{4n^{2}}\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\min\Bigl\{\|[A,B]\|_{\mathrm{op}},Z_{2}(t)\Bigr\}.

Or, via

\left\|\left[A,\left[A,T(t)\right]\right]\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\left\|A\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}\left\|\left[A,T(t)\right]\right\|_{\mathrm{op}},

we can alternatively bound Eq. (
17
) as

\displaystyle\left\|\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}T(t)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}-T(t)\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{t}{2n}\left(1-\frac{t}{2n}\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\right)\|[A,T(t)]\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{t^{2}}{2n}\left(1-\frac{t}{2n}\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\right)Z_{1}(t).

This last inequality is valid for
t\leq 2n/\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}
.
These bounds are then combined as

\displaystyle\left\|\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}T(t)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2n}A}-T(t)\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\geq\max\!\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{2n}\left(1-\frac{t}{2n}\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\right)Z_{1}(t),\frac{t^{2}}{2n}\left(Z_{1}(t)-\frac{t}{2n}\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}Z_{2}(t)\right)\right\}.

Note that we have taken into account the fact
Z_{1}(t)\leq\|[A,B]\|_{\mathrm{op}}
.
A bound on the second term of Eq. (
9
) is given by Eq. (
13
), and we get the bound (
15
).
∎

If
[A,B]=0
, we have
b_{n}(t)=0
, while the bounds (
15
) and (
16
) both reduce to
b_{n}(t)\geq 0
.
For
[A,B]\neq 0
, the bound becomes non-trivial for
t
small enough.
More explicitly, on the basis of the bound (
16
), it suffices to take

t\leq\frac{12n\|[A,B]\|_{\mathrm{op}}}{6n\|[A+B,[A,B]]\|_{\mathrm{op}}+6\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|[A,B]\|_{\mathrm{op}}+\|[A,[A,B]]\|_{\mathrm{op}}+2\|[B,[B,A]]\|_{\mathrm{op}}},

(18)

which is compatible with the valid time range
t\leq 2n/\|A\|
for the bound with moderate
n
.

Since
b_{n}(t)\geq\xi_{n}(t;\varphi)
, the lower bound on
\xi_{n}(t;\varphi)
can be useful also for
b_{n}(t)
.
A refined version of the lower bound on
b_{n}(t)
is then given by comparing the lower bounds on
b_{n}(t)
and on
\xi_{n}(t;\varphi)
as

\displaystyle b_{n}(t)\geq\max\bigg\{0,\frac{t^{2}}{2n}\left(1-\frac{t}{2n}\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\right)Z_{1}(t),\frac{t^{2}}{2n}\left(Z_{1}(t)-\frac{t}{2n}\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}Z_{2}(t)\right),\left|\sin\left(\frac{\kappa t}{2}\right)\right|\left(\frac{t}{n}|\langle\psi|A\varphi\rangle|-\frac{t^{2}}{4n^{2}}\|A(g)^{2}\varphi\|\right)\biggr\}\hphantom{.}

\displaystyle-\frac{t^{3}}{24n^{2}}\|[A,[A,B]]\|_{\mathrm{op}}-\frac{t^{3}}{12n^{2}}\|[B,[B,A]]\|_{\mathrm{op}}\bigg\}.

In this case, we have

\displaystyle(A+B)\varphi

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{2}\,\varphi,\quad\varphi=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt-\sqrt{1-1/\sqrt{2}}\\

\displaystyle(A+B)\psi

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}\,\psi,\quad\psi=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\sqrt{1+1/\sqrt{2}}\\

\lambda=2\sqrt{2}

\|[A,B]\|_{\mathrm{op}}=2,\qquad\|[A,[A,B]]\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\|[B,[B,A]]\|_{\mathrm{op}}=4,\qquad\|[A+B,[B,A]]\|_{\mathrm{op}}=4\sqrt{2},

for the commutators, and

\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}=1,\qquad\|(A+B)^{2}\|_{\mathrm{op}}=2.

Since
a=\min_{g}\|A(g)^{2}\varphi\|=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{6(\sqrt{2}+1)^{2/3}-3(\sqrt{2}+1)^{4/3}+6(\sqrt{2}-1)^{2/3}-3(\sqrt{2}-1)^{4/3}-1}=0.788903

\xi_{n}(t;\varphi)
[Eq. (
3

\xi_{n}(t;\varphi)\geq\max\!\left\{0,\frac{t^{2}}{n}\left(1-\frac{at}{2\sqrt{2}\,n}\right)\frac{|{\sin\sqrt{2}\,t}|}{\sqrt{2}\,t}-\frac{t^{3}}{2n^{2}}\right\}.

This bound is non-trivial for all

n\geq\frac{at}{2\sqrt{2}}\left(1+\frac{2t}{a|{\sin\sqrt{2}\,t}|}\right).

On the other hand, since
\min_{g}(\|A(g)^{2}\varphi\|+\|B(g)^{2}\varphi\|)=\sqrt{5}
at
g=-1/\sqrt{2}
, the upper bound on the state-dependent Trotter error
\xi_{n}(t;\varphi)
[Eq. (
1
) of the main text] is optimized to

\xi_{n}(t;\varphi)\leq\frac{\sqrt{5}\,t^{2}}{2n}.

For the norm Trotter error
b_{n}(t)
, the lower bound (
15
) yields

b_{n}(t)\geq\max\!\left\{0,\frac{t^{2}}{n}\left(1-\frac{t}{2n}\right)(1-t)-\frac{t^{3}}{2n^{2}}\right\},

Since
\xi_{n}(t;\varphi)\leq b_{n}(t)
, the upper bound on

\xi_{n}(t;\varphi)
, and the lower bound on

is also a lower bound on

.

\max\!\left\{0,\frac{t^{2}}{n}\left(1-\frac{at}{2\sqrt{2}\,n}\right)\frac{|{\sin\sqrt{2}\,t}|}{\sqrt{2}\,t}-\frac{t^{3}}{2n^{2}}\right\}\leq\xi_{n}(t;\varphi)\leq b_{n}(t)\leq\frac{t^{2}}{n}.

(19)

D.2
D.2. The
XX
Model

Here, we test our bounds with the isotropic one-dimensional
XX
model, introduced in Ref.
[
38
]
.
The
XX
model consists of a chain of
L
spin-
1/2
spins with the nearest neighbor interactions.
Its Hamiltonian reads

H=\frac{J}{4}\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}(X_{j}X_{j+1}+Y_{j}Y_{j+1}),

(20)

where
X_{j}
,
Y_{j}
, and
Z_{j}
denote the actions of the first, second, and third Pauli operators on site
j
, and
J\in\mathbb{R}
is the coupling constant.
In the following, we will assume that
J>0
.
We impose the periodic boundary conditions,
X_{L}=X_{0}
,
Y_{L}=Y_{0}
, and
Z_{L}=Z_{0}
.
We consider the Trotter product formula with

A=\frac{J}{4}\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}X_{j}X_{j+1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad B=\frac{J}{4}\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}Y_{j}Y_{j+1}.

Evidently,
A
and
B
can be diagonalized trivially by going to the basis of the respective Pauli operators.
The Hamiltonian
A+B
of the
XX
model (
20
) can also be diagonalized analytically
[
39
,
40
,
41
]
.
This allows us to analytically compute the upper and lower bounds on the state-dependent Trotter error
\xi_{n}(t;\varphi)
.

D.2.1
Diagonalization of the
XX
Model

We here recall the exact diagonalization of the
XX
model (
20
)
[
39
,
40
,
41
]
, which can be rewritten as

H=\frac{J}{2}\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}(\sigma^{+}_{j}\sigma^{-}_{j+1}+\sigma^{-}_{j}\sigma^{+}_{j+1}),

(21)

where
\sigma_{j}^{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}(X_{j}\pm\mathrm{i}Y_{j})
are the ladder operators of the
j
th spin.
We perform a Jordan-Wigner transformation to express (
21
) by spinless fermions
c_{j}
.
To this end, we replace
\sigma_{j}^{\pm}
with

\sigma_{j}^{-}=\mathrm{e}^{\pi\mathrm{i}n_{j\uparrow}}c_{j},\qquad\sigma_{j}^{+}=\mathrm{e}^{\pi\mathrm{i}n_{j\uparrow}}c_{j}^{\dagger},\qquad n_{j\uparrow}=\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\sigma_{i}^{+}\sigma_{i}^{-}=\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}c_{i}^{\dagger}c_{i},\qquad j\in\{0,\ldots,L-1\}.

The new operators
c_{j}
and
c_{j}^{\dagger}
satisfy the fermionic anticommutation relations,

\{c_{i},c_{j}\}=0,\qquad\{c_{i}^{\dagger},c_{j}^{\dagger}\}=0,\qquad\{c_{i},c_{j}^{\dagger}\}=\delta_{i,j},\qquad i,j\in\{0,\ldots,L-1\}.

In terms of these fermionic operators, the Hamiltonian reads

H=\frac{J}{2}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{L-2}(c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j+1}+c_{j+1}^{\dagger}c_{j})-\mathrm{e}^{\pi\mathrm{i}n_{\uparrow}}(c_{L-1}^{\dagger}c_{0}+c_{0}^{\dagger}c_{L-1})\right),

(22)

where
n_{\uparrow}=n_{L\uparrow}
is the number of up spins in the chain.
Since

[\mathrm{e}^{\pi\mathrm{i}n_{\uparrow}},H]=0,

the Hamiltonian
H
is diagonalizable for
\mathrm{e}^{\pi\mathrm{i}n_{\uparrow}}=1
and for
\mathrm{e}^{\pi\mathrm{i}n_{\uparrow}}=-1
separately.
Depending on the parity of
n_{\uparrow}
, we perform a (deformed) Fourier transformation

c_{j}=\begin{cases}\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}jk/L}\hat{c}_{k}^{(-)}&(n_{\uparrow}\ \text{odd}),\\
\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}j(k+1/2)/L}\hat{c}_{k}^{(+)}&(n_{\uparrow}\ \text{even}),\end{cases}\qquad j\in\{0,\ldots,L-1\}.

This preserves the fermionic anticommutation relations,

\{\hat{c}_{k}^{(\pm)},\hat{c}_{\ell}^{(\pm)}\}=0,\qquad\{\hat{c}_{k}^{(\pm){\dagger}},\hat{c}_{\ell}^{(\pm){\dagger}}\}=0,\qquad\{\hat{c}_{k}^{(\pm)},\hat{c}_{\ell}^{(\pm){\dagger}}\}=\delta_{k,\ell},\qquad k,\ell\in\{0,\ldots,L-1\}.

By this transformation, the Hamiltonian (
22
) is diagonalized as

H=J\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\left[\cos\!\left(\frac{2\pi k}{N}\right)\hat{c}_{k}^{(-){\dagger}}\hat{c}_{k}^{(-)}\frac{1-\mathrm{e}^{\pi\mathrm{i}n_{\uparrow}}}{2}+\cos\!\left(\frac{2\pi(k+1/2)}{N}\right)\hat{c}_{k}^{(+){\dagger}}\hat{c}_{k}^{(+)}\frac{1+\mathrm{e}^{\pi\mathrm{i}n_{\uparrow}}}{2}\right].

The eigenvalues corresponding to
\ket{\downarrow\cdots\downarrow}
and
\ket{\uparrow\cdots\uparrow}
are zero, which can be easily seen by acting the Hamiltonian
H
in the representation (
21
).

D.2.2
Bound on the State-Dependent Trotter Error
\xi_{n}(t;\varphi)

We look at the Trotter error
\xi_{n}(t;\varphi)
on the eigenstate
\varphi=\ket{\downarrow\cdots\downarrow}
with no spin excitations.
To obtain a non-trivial lower bound on
\xi_{n}(t;\varphi)
, we have to choose an eigenstate
\psi
belonging to a non-zero eigenvalue, such that the term
\langle\psi,A\varphi\rangle
is non-vanishing.
As
A
flips two spins simultaneously, we consider states with two excitations,

\Psi(k_{1},k_{2})=\hat{c}_{k_{1}}^{(+){\dagger}}\hat{c}_{k_{2}}^{(+){\dagger}}{\ket{\downarrow\cdots\downarrow}}.

Note that
k_{1}=k_{2}
yields zero, as the excitations are fermionic, so that
(\hat{c}_{k}^{\dagger})^{2}=0
.
The corresponding eigenvalues are

H{{\Psi(k_{1},k_{2})}}=J\left[\cos\!\left(\frac{2\pi(k_{1}+1/2)}{L}\right)+\cos\!\left(\frac{2\pi(k_{2}+1/2)}{L}\right)\right]{{\Psi(k_{1},k_{2})}}.

For the term
\langle\psi|A\varphi\rangle
with
\varphi=\ket{\downarrow\cdots\downarrow}
and
\psi={\Psi(k_{1},k_{2})}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle\Psi(k_{1},k_{2})|A\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{J}{4}\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}{\bra{\Psi(k_{1},k_{2})}}X_{j}X_{j+1}{\ket{\downarrow\cdots\downarrow}}

\displaystyle=\frac{J}{4}\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}{\bra{\Psi(k_{1},k_{2})}}\sigma_{j}^{+}\sigma_{j+1}^{+}{\ket{\downarrow\cdots\downarrow}}

\displaystyle=\frac{J}{4}\sum_{j=0}^{L-2}{\bra{\Psi(k_{1},k_{2})}}c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j+1}^{\dagger}{\ket{\downarrow\cdots\downarrow}}-\frac{J}{4}{\bra{\Psi(k_{1},k_{2})}}c_{L-1}^{\dagger}c_{0}^{\dagger}{\ket{\downarrow\cdots\downarrow}}

\displaystyle=\frac{J}{4L}\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}\sum_{\ell_{1}=0}^{L-1}\sum_{\ell_{2}=0}^{L-1}\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}j(\ell_{1}+1/2)/L}\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}(j+1)(\ell_{2}+1/2)/L}{\bra{\Psi(k_{1},k_{2})}}\hat{c}_{\ell_{1}}^{(+){\dagger}}\hat{c}_{\ell_{2}}^{(+){\dagger}}{\ket{\downarrow\cdots\downarrow}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mathrm{i}J}{2}\sin\!\left(\frac{2\pi(k_{1}+1/2)}{L}\right)\delta_{k_{1}+k_{2},L-1}.

This is non-vanishing only for
k_{1}+k_{2}=L-1
.
For these states, the spectral gap
\lambda
is given by

\displaystyle\lambda

\displaystyle=\langle\Psi(k,L-1-k)|H\Psi(k,L-1-k)\rangle-\langle\varphi,H\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle=2J\cos\!\left(\frac{2\pi(k+1/2)}{L}\right).

Next, we compute
\|A(g)^{2}\varphi\|
.
We set
g=0
, so that
\|A(g)^{2}\varphi\|=\|A^{2}\varphi\|
.
Assuming
L\geq 5
, we get

\displaystyle\|A^{2}\varphi\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{J}{4}\right)^{4}\sum_{j_{1}=0}^{L-1}\sum_{j_{2}=0}^{L-1}\sum_{j_{3}=0}^{L-1}\sum_{j_{4}=0}^{L-1}{\bra{\downarrow\cdots\downarrow}}X_{j_{1}}X_{j_{1}+1}X_{j_{2}}X_{j_{2}+1}X_{j_{3}}X_{j_{3}+1}X_{j_{4}}X_{j_{4}+1}{\ket{\downarrow\cdots\downarrow}}

\displaystyle=\frac{J^{4}}{4^{4}}\left(\sum_{j_{1}=0}^{L-1}\sum_{j_{2}=0}^{L-1}\sum_{j_{3}=0}^{L-1}\sum_{j_{4}=0}^{L-1}(\delta_{j_{1},j_{2}}\delta_{j_{3},j_{4}}+\delta_{j_{1},j_{3}}\delta_{j_{2},j_{4}}+\delta_{j_{1},j_{4}}\delta_{j_{1},j_{3}})-2L\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{J^{4}}{4^{4}}L(3L-2),

where the second equality follows from the fact that the states with different spins are orthogonal.
In turn, we always need an even number of
X
operators acting on the same site to get a non-zero result.
The term
-2L
corrects the overcounting of the terms where all four indices are the same,
j_{1}=j_{2}=j_{3}=j_{4}
.
Note that for
L=4
there are additional terms of the form
\delta_{j_{1}+1,j_{2}}\delta_{j_{2}+1,j_{3}}\delta_{j_{3}+1,j_{4}}
contributing.

To compute the norms of the double-commutators in the bound, we first compute the commutator
[A,B]
,

\displaystyle[A,B]

\displaystyle=\frac{J^{2}}{16}\sum_{i=0}^{L-1}\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}[X_{i}X_{i+1},Y_{j}Y_{j+1}]

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{i}J^{2}}{8}\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}(X_{j}Y_{j+2}+Y_{j}X_{j+2})Z_{j+1}.

(23)

This allows us to compute the double-commutators

\displaystyle[A,[A,B]]

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{i}J^{3}}{32}\sum_{i=0}^{L-1}\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}[X_{i}X_{i+1},(X_{j}Y_{j+2}+Y_{j}X_{j+2})Z_{j+1}]

\displaystyle=\frac{J^{3}}{8}\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}(Y_{j}Y_{j+1}-X_{j}Z_{j+1}Z_{j+2}X_{j+3}),

and similarly

\displaystyle[B,[B,A]]

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mathrm{i}J^{3}}{32}\sum_{i=0}^{L-1}\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}[Y_{i}Y_{i+1},(X_{j}Y_{j+2}+Y_{j}X_{j+2})Z_{j+1}]

\displaystyle=\frac{J^{3}}{8}\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}(X_{j}X_{j+1}-Y_{j}Z_{j+1}Z_{j+2}Y_{j+3}).

We bound the norms of the double-commutators
\|[A,[A,B]]\|_{\mathrm{op}}
and
\|[B,[B,A]]\|_{\mathrm{op}}
by using the triangle inequality as

\displaystyle\|[A,[A,B]]\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle=\frac{J^{3}}{8}\left\|\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}(Y_{j}Y_{j+1}-X_{j}Z_{j+1}Z_{j+2}X_{j+3})\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{J^{3}}{8}\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}\Bigl(\|Y_{j}Y_{j+1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}+\|X_{j}Z_{j+1}Z_{j+2}X_{j+3}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{J^{3}}{8}\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}\Bigl(\|Y\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{2}+\|X\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{2}\|Z\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{2}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{J^{3}}{4}L,

and

\displaystyle\|[B,[B,A]]\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{J^{3}}{4}L.

Putting these elements together, the state-dependent Trotter error
\xi_{n}(t;\varphi)
is bounded (for
L\geq 5
) by

\max\!\left\{0,\left|\sin\!\left[Jt\cos\!\left(\frac{2\pi(k+1/2)}{L}\right)\right]\right|\left(\frac{Jt}{2n}\left|\sin\!\left(\frac{2\pi(k+1/2)}{L}\right)\right|-\frac{J^{2}t^{2}}{64n^{2}}\sqrt{L(3L-2)}\right)-\frac{J^{3}t^{3}}{32n^{2}}L\right\}\\
\leq\xi_{n}(t;\varphi)\leq\frac{J^{2}t^{2}}{16n}\sqrt{L(3L-2)}.

(24)

To obtain the best possible bound, we can take the supremum over
k\in\{0,1,\ldots,L-1\}
.
This lower bound is non-trivial for

n\geq\frac{JtL}{32|{\sin[2\pi(k+1/2)/L]}|}\left(\sqrt{3-2/L}+\frac{2Jt}{|{\sin\{Jt\cos[2\pi(k+1/2)/L]\}}|}\right).

D.2.3
Bound on the Norm Trotter Error
b_{n}(t)

Many of the quantities involved in the bounds on the norm Trotter error
b_{n}(t)
have already been computed for the bounds on the state-dependent Trotter error
\xi_{n}(t;\varphi)
.
For instance, the commutator
[A,B]
is calculated in Eq. (
23
).
Its operator norm
\|[A,B]\|_{\mathrm{op}}
is upper bounded by

\displaystyle\|[A,B]\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle=\frac{J^{2}}{8}\left\|\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}(X_{j}Y_{j+2}+Y_{j}X_{j+2})Z_{j+1}\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{J^{2}}{8}\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}\Bigl(\|X_{j}Z_{j+1}Y_{j+2}\|_{\mathrm{op}}+\|Y_{j}Z_{j+1}X_{j+2}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{J^{2}}{4}L.

We also need its lower bound.
To bound it from below, we use the equivalence of the matrix norms.
That is, for all
M\in\mathbb{C}^{d\times d}
, we have

\|M\|_{\mathrm{op}}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\|M\|_{\mathrm{F}},

(25)

where
\|M\|_{\mathrm{F}}=\sqrt{\operatorname{tr}(M^{\dagger}M)}
is the Frobenius norm
[
42
, Eq. (1.169)]
.
Applying this to Eq. (
23
), we get

\displaystyle\|[A,B]\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2^{L}}\|[A,B]\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{L}}\frac{J^{4}}{64}\operatorname{tr}\!\left[\left(\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}(X_{j}Y_{j+2}+Y_{j}X_{j+2})Z_{j+1}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{L}}\frac{J^{4}}{64}\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}\operatorname{tr}[(X_{j}Z_{j+1}Y_{j+2})^{2}+(Y_{j}Z_{j+1}X_{j+2})^{2}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{L}}\frac{J^{4}}{64}\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}2^{L+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{J^{4}}{32}L.

Therefore,

\frac{J^{2}}{4}\sqrt{\frac{L}{2}}\leq\|[A,B]\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\frac{J^{2}}{4}L.

(26)

Additionally, we need upper bounds on
\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}
and
\|A+B\|_{\mathrm{op}}
.
To this end, we again use the triangle inequality to get

\displaystyle\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle=\frac{J}{4}\left\|\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}X_{j}X_{j+1}\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{J}{4}\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}\|X_{j}X_{j+1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle=\frac{J}{4}L,

and

\displaystyle\|A+B\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle=\frac{J}{4}\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{L}(X_{j}X_{j+1}+Y_{j}Y_{j+1})\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{J}{4}\sum_{j=1}^{L}\Bigl(\|X_{j}X_{j+1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}+\|Y_{j}Y_{j+1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{J}{2}L.

Using these bounds, we get

\displaystyle Z_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=\|[A,B]\|_{\mathrm{op}}-t\min\!\left\{\frac{1}{2}\|[A+B,[A,B]]\|_{\mathrm{op}},\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|(A+B)^{2}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\right\}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{J^{2}}{4}\sqrt{\frac{L}{2}}-\min\!\left\{\frac{J^{3}t}{4}L,\frac{J^{3}t}{16}L^{3}\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{J^{2}}{4}\sqrt{\frac{L}{2}}-\frac{J^{3}t}{4}L,

for
L\geq 2
, and

\displaystyle\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}Z_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|[A,[A,B]]\|_{\mathrm{op}}+t\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{2}\|(A+B)^{2}\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{J^{3}}{8}L+\frac{J^{4}t}{64}L^{4}.

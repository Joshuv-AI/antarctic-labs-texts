Abstract
. The classical Korenblum-Roberts Theorem characterizes the cyclic singular inner functions in the Bergman spaces of the unit disk 𝔻 \mathbb{D} as those for which the corresponding singular measure vanishes on Beurling-Carleson sets of Lebesgue measure zero. We solve the weighted variant of the problem in which the Bergman space is replaced by a 𝒫 t ​ ( μ ) \mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu) space, the closure of analytic polynomials in a Lebesgue space ℒ t ​ ( μ ) \mathcal{L}^{t}(\mu) corresponding to a measure of the form d ​ A α + w ​ d ​ m dA_{\alpha}+w\,d\textit{m} , with d ​ A α dA_{\alpha} being the standard weighted area measure on 𝔻 \mathbb{D} , d ​ m d\textit{m} the Lebesgue measure on the unit circle 𝕋 \mathbb{T} , and w w a general weight on 𝕋 \mathbb{T} . We characterize when 𝒫 t ​ ( μ ) \mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu) of this form is a space of analytic functions on 𝔻 \mathbb{D} by computing the Thomson decomposition of the measure μ \mu . The structure of the decomposition is expressed in terms of what we call the family of associated Beurling-Carleson sets . We characterize the cyclic singular inner functions in the analytic 𝒫 t ​ ( μ ) \mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu) spaces as those for which the corresponding singular measure vanishes on the family of associated Beurling-Carleson sets. Unlike the classical setting, Beurling-Carleson sets of both zero and positive Lebesgue measure appear in our description. As an application of our results, we complete the characterization of the symbols b : 𝔻 → 𝔻 b:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D} which generate a de Branges-Rovnyak space with a dense subset of functions smooth on 𝕋 \mathbb{T} . The characterization is given explicitly in terms of the modulus of b b on 𝕋 \mathbb{T} and the singular measure corresponding to the singular inner factor of b b . Our proofs involve Khrushchev’s techniques of simultaneous polynomial approximations and linear programming ideas of Korenblum, combined with recently established constrained ℒ 1 \mathcal{L}^{1} -optimization tools.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
A bit of background

Let
\mathcal{P}
denote the set of analytic polynomials in a single complex variable
z
,

\mathcal{P}:=\Bigg\{\sum_{i=0}^{n}p_{i}z^{i}:p_{i}\in\mathbb{C},n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\Bigg\}

and let
\mu
be a finite non-negative Borel measure compactly supported in the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
. Given such a measure and a number
t\in(0,\infty)
, the closure of
\mathcal{P}
in the Lebesgue space
\mathcal{L}^{t}(\mu)
is customarily denoted by
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
. If
d\mu=d\textit{m}
, the Lebesgue measure on the unit circle
\mathbb{T}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|=1\}
, then
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
is the classical Hardy space
\mathcal{H}^{t}
of analytic functions on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
, while if
d\mu=dA
is the area measure on
\mathbb{D}
, then
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
is the Bergman space of area
t
-integrable functions on
\mathbb{D}
. In contrast, if
d\mu=dx_{I}
, the Lebesgue measure on an interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
, then
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)=\mathcal{L}^{t}(\mu)
is the Lebesgue space itself, a space of measurable functions exhibiting no analytic properties. In general, the nature of
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
is complicated, and the three mentioned examples illustrate in a sense the extreme cases. By the decomposition theorem of Thomson from
[
28
]
, the space
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
can always be decomposed into pieces,
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)=\oplus_{i}\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu_{i})
, which with at most one exception are
analytic
, in the sense that functions in the space are analytic on some open set. The potential exceptional piece is a full Lebesgue space.

The identification of the pieces
\mu_{i}
for a general planar measure
\mu
is a task too ambitious for the present work. In regards to this we mention that identification of the pieces in the Thomson decomposition of a deceptively simple-looking measure
\mu
composed of two pieces, a radially weighted area measure on
\mathbb{D}
with very fast decay near
\mathbb{T}
, and a weighted Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{T}
, is essentially equivalent to Volberg’s deep theorem from
[
29
]
on summability of the logarithmic integral of functions with appropriate one-sided Fourier decay (see also the exposition by Volberg and Jöricke in
[
31
]
, and the introductory sections of
[
18
]
and
[
30
]
). Other results of this type can be deduced from Khrushchev’s profound work in
[
14
]
on simultaneous polynomial approximation, which can be used to establish Thomson decompositions with applications to the theory of the Cauchy integral operator. In
[
20
]
it was found that the structure of invariant subspaces of the shift operator
M_{z}:f(z)\mapsto zf(z)
acting on certain
\mathcal{P}^{2}(\mu)
spaces is connected with the problem of approximation by functions of various boundary regularity in de Branges-Rovnyak spaces
\mathcal{H}(b)
. In this context, an issue arises which calls for an extension of the results of Korenblum from
[
17
]
and Roberts from
[
26
]
on cyclic singular inner functions in the Bergman spaces to the context of
\mathcal{P}^{2}(\mu)
spaces. It is these applications that are the main motivation for the research presented in this article.

1.2.
Main research questions

The measures
\mu
considered here will be of the form appearing in (
1.2
) below. The particular structure of the measure makes
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
exhibit properties commonly associated with three distinct spaces which have already been mentioned: the Hardy space
\mathcal{P}^{t}(d\textit{m})
, the Bergman space
\mathcal{P}^{t}(dA)
, and the Lebesgue space
\mathcal{L}^{t}(d\textit{m})
. The following two questions are answered in the article.

Question 1
.

What measures
\mu
of the form (
1.2
) make the space
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
into a genuine space of analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
?

Question 2
.

Given that the space
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
of the form (
1.2
) really is a space of analytic function, what
bounded
functions
f\in\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
are cyclic for the shift operator
M_{z}:f(z)\mapsto zf(z)
? Namely, what bounded functions are such that
[f]
, the smallest closed
M_{z}
-invariant subspace of
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
which contains
f
, is the whole space itself?

The first question will be answered by computing the Thomson decomposition of a measure of the form (
1.2
). The methods which we shall use for this have already been presented in previous articles
[
3
]
,
[
22
]
and
[
24
]
, but they need to be combined. It is the second question that we consider here to be the important one. Modulo standard arguments, the question can be answered by describing the cyclic
singular inner functions

(1.1)

S_{\nu}(z):=\exp\Big(-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{\zeta+z}{\zeta-z}d\nu(\zeta)\Big),\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Here
\nu
is a finite non-negative Borel measure on
\mathbb{T}
, singular with respect to Lebesgue measure
d\textit{m}
. The singular inner functions are members of
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
, the algebra of bounded analytic functions in
\mathbb{D}
, and are characterized as those functions in
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
which are non-vanishing in
\mathbb{D}
and have non-tangential boundary values on
\mathbb{T}
of unit modulus, almost everywhere with respect to the Lebesgue measure
d\textit{m}
.

The restriction in the second question to consider bounded functions is important. See Section
1.5
for comments on this.

1.3.
Beurling-Carleson sets associated to a space

We will obtain complete results for measures
\mu
of the following structure:

(1.2)

d\mu=dA_{\alpha}+w\,d\textit{m}.

Here

(1.3)

dA_{\alpha}(z)=(1-|z|)^{\alpha}dA(z),\quad\alpha>-1

is the standard weighted area measure on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
, and
w
is a non-negative Borel measurable function on
\mathbb{T}
.

Answers to the two questions will be given in terms of a class of subsets of
\mathbb{T}
associated with the space. Let
h(x):(0,1)\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be the function

(1.4)

h(x)=x\log(e/x).

The class of
Beurling-Carleson sets
consists of those closed subsets
E\subset\mathbb{T}
for which the system
\{\ell_{k}\}_{k}
of maximal open arcs complementary to
E
in
\mathbb{T}
satisfies

(1.5)

\sum_{k}h(|\ell_{k}|)<\infty.

Here, and throughout the article,
|S|=m(S)
is the Lebesgue (arclength) measure of the set
S
. We denote the class of Beurling-Carleson sets by
\mathcal{BC}
, and we emphasize that
\mathcal{BC}
is not restricted to contain only sets of Lebesgue measure zero, as in some earlier works on cyclic vectors. Furthermore, we introduce the family of
w
-associated
\mathcal{BC}
sets
, which is the subclass of
\mathcal{BC}
on which
w
is logarithmically integrable:

(1.6)

\text{Assoc}\mathcal{BC}(w)=\Big\{E:E\in\mathcal{BC},\int_{E}\log w\,d\textit{m}>-\infty\Big\}.

Note that if
E\in\mathcal{BC}
is of Lebesgue measure zero, then
E\in\text{Assoc}\mathcal{BC}(w)
automatically.

1.4.
Main results

Our first theorem answers the first of the questions stated above. It is a direct generalization to the weighted context of the main result in
[
24
]
. The statement requires some definitions which are generalizations of those in
[
24
]
.

Consider the non-negative quantity

C:=\sup\Big\{|E|:E\in\text{Assoc}\mathcal{BC}(w)\Big\}.

If
C=0
, we set
\text{core}(w)=\varnothing
, and otherwise we define

\text{core}(w):=\bigcup_{n}E_{n}

where
\{E_{n}\}_{n}
is any increasing sequence of sets in
\text{Assoc}\mathcal{BC}(w)
for which
\lim_{n}|E_{n}|=C
. The existence of such a sequence is ensured by the class
\text{Assoc}\mathcal{BC}(w)
being closed under finite unions (see
[
24
, Proposition 3.2]
). It is not hard to see that
\text{core}(w)
is in this way well-defined up to a set of Lebesgue measure zero, and it does not depend on the initial choice of the sequence
\{E_{n}\}_{n}
. Clearly, sets of Lebesgue measure zero in
\text{Assoc}\mathcal{BC}(w)
play no role in shaping
\text{core}(w)
, but they will play a role in our description of cyclic singular inner functions.

We define the residual set of
w
as the difference of its natural carrier and the core:

\text{res}(w):=\{z\in\mathbb{T}:w(z)>0\}\setminus\text{core}(w).

Throughout the article, a
carrier
for a Borel measure
\sigma
on
\mathbb{T}
will be any subset
C\subset\mathbb{T}
for which we have
\sigma(C\cap S)=\sigma(S)
for all Borel subsets
S
of
\mathbb{T}
. If
w\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(d\textit{m})
, then we say that
C
is a carrier for
w
if it is a carrier for the measure
w\,d\textit{m}
.

Like
\text{core}(w)
, the set
\text{res}(w)
is also defined only up to a set of Lebesgue measure zero. We decompose
w
into pieces
w_{c}
,
w_{r}
living on the core and residual, respectively:

w_{c}=w|\text{core}(w),\quad w_{r}=w|\text{res}(w).

In the considered family of
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
spaces corresponding to measures
\mu
of the form (
1.2
), the restriction
f|\mathbb{D}
of any element
f\in\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
is an analytic function living in a Bergman space
\mathcal{P}^{t}(dA_{\alpha})
. We will say that the space
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
of the form (
1.2
) is
irreducible
if the restriction mapping
f\mapsto f|\mathbb{D}
is injective on
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
. In such a case, each element
f\in\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
can be uniquely identified with an analytic function on
\mathbb{D}
, and so
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
is itself a space of analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
, continuously contained in the Bergman space
\mathcal{P}^{t}(dA_{\alpha})
. Although it may not be a priori obvious, our definition of irreducibility for our class of spaces coincides with the more general one in
[
2
]
and
[
28
]
which involves non-existence of non-trivial indicator functions in the space. We will sometimes use the term
analytic
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
space
synonymously with
irreducible
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
-space
.

Theorem A
.

Let
\mu
be as in (
1.2
). We have the decomposition

\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)=\mathcal{P}^{t}(dA_{\alpha}+w_{c}\,d\textit{m})\oplus\mathcal{L}^{t}(w_{r}\,d\textit{m}),

where
\mathcal{P}^{t}(dA_{\alpha}+w_{c}\,d\textit{m})
is irreducible.

More precisely, the decomposition is to be interpreted in the following sense: any
f
in one of the summands on the right-hand side has an extension to an element
\mathcal{L}^{t}(\mu)
by setting
f
to zero outside of its initial carrier set, and these extended elements are in fact members of
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
and they span the whole space. The theorem is a refined version of a result from
[
3
]
, which established the decomposition in an important special case
w=w_{r}
, and which confirmed an old conjecture of Kriete and MacCluer from
[
18
]
. The irreducibility of the piece
\mathcal{P}^{t}(dA_{\alpha}+w_{c}\,d\textit{m})
has essentially been known since the 1970s as a consequence of the work of Khrushchev in
[
14
]
, although his results are not coined in terms of
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
spaces. We will fill in the details of the necessary modifications of his proof in Section
3
. A different proof of irreducibility for
t=2
is given in
[
21
, Proposition 5.1]
. Our principal contribution in this direction establishes that
\mathcal{P}^{t}(dA_{\alpha}+w_{c}\,d\textit{m})
is the maximal irreducible piece. We deduce from ?THM?
that our
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
is a space of analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
if and only if
\text{core}(w)
is a carrier set for
w
.

Having settled the analyticity question, we may get to the main matter. Here is our cyclicity result.

Theorem B
.

Let
\mu
be as in (
1.2
), and assume that
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
is a space of analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
. The singular inner function
S_{\nu}
is cyclic in
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
if and only if
\nu
vanishes on all associated sets:

\nu(E)=0,\quad E\in\text{Assoc}\mathcal{BC}(w).

It is the sufficiency for cyclicity of the vanishing condition on the associated sets that should be seen as the main new contribution of this article. The necessity of the condition is easier to establish than the sufficiency, and one can say a bit more. In
[
21
, Corollary 6.5]
it has been proved that if
\nu
is supported on a set in
\text{Assoc}\mathcal{BC}(w)
and
[S_{\nu}]
is the
M_{z}
-invariant subspace generated by
S_{\nu}
in
\mathcal{P}^{2}(\mu)
, then we have
f/S_{\nu}\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
for any
f\in[S_{\nu}]\cap\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
. In other words,
S_{\nu}
appears as a factor in the usual inner-outer factorization of any non-zero
f\in[S_{\nu}]\cap\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
. This property of course implies that
1\not\in[S_{\nu}]
, so such
S_{\nu}
is not cyclic. In Section
4
we use methods from
[
4
]
to extend this result to
t\neq 2
.

Using the result indicated in the above paragraph, we may describe the
M_{z}
-invariant subspace
[f]
of
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
generated by a bounded function
f
in the following way. Let
f=BS_{\nu}U
be the inner-outer factorization of
f
into a Blaschke product
B
, singular inner function
S_{\nu}
, and a bounded outer function
U
. Let
\nu_{p}
be the least upper bound of all restrictions
\nu|E
of
\nu
to sets
E\in\text{Assoc}\mathcal{BC}(w)
, and write

(1.7)

\nu=\nu_{p}+\nu_{c}.

One way to construct
\nu_{p}
more explicitly is to employ an argument similar to the one used in the construction of the set
\text{core}(w)
. Namely, take an increasing sequence
\{E_{n}\}_{n}
of sets in
\text{Assoc}\mathcal{BC}(w)
which satisfies

\lim_{n\to\infty}\nu(E_{n})=\sup_{E\in\text{Assoc}\mathcal{BC}(w)}\nu(E)

and define
\nu_{p}
as the restriction of
\nu
to the Borel set
\bigcup_{n}E_{n}
. Clearly
\nu_{p}
constructed in this way satisfies the measure inequality
\nu|E\leq\nu_{p}
for any
E\in\text{Assoc}\mathcal{BC}(w)
. We note that
\nu_{p}
has as a carrier a countable union of sets in
\text{Assoc}\mathcal{BC}(w)
, and
\nu_{c}(E)=0
for
E\in\text{Assoc}\mathcal{BC}(w)
. From ?THM?
we can deduce the following consequence.

Corollary
.

Let
f=BS_{\nu}U
be the inner-outer factorization of a function
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
. Then

[f]=[BS_{\nu_{p}}],

and every function
h\in[f]\cap\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
satisfies
h/BS_{\nu_{p}}\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
.

We give a proof of the corollary at the end of Section
5
. ?THM?
and the corollary answer the second of our questions stated above.

We remark that there is a huge difference between the trivial case
\mathbb{T}\in\text{Assoc}\mathcal{BC}(w)
, which implies that
|\mathbb{T}\setminus\text{core}(w)|=0
, and this latter condition
|\mathbb{T}\setminus\text{core}(w)|=0
. In the first case we have
\log w\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(d\textit{m})
, and the structure of
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
is simple. It is then a space of analytic functions contractively contained in a weighted Hardy space
W\cdot\mathcal{H}^{t}
, where
W
is an outer function satisfying
|W|=w^{-1/2}
on
\mathbb{T}
, and where the norm of
W\cdot f
in
W\cdot\mathcal{H}^{t}
is the norm of
f
in
\mathcal{H}^{t}
. In particular, no singular inner functions are then cyclic in
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
. However, in the second case, such an identification does not in general hold. Although we shall not go into details of a construction, we wish to mention that it is possible to exhibit examples of singular inner functions
S_{\nu}
which are cyclic in a space
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
constructed from
\mu
of the kind (
1.2
) for which
|\mathbb{T}\setminus\text{core}(w)|=0
.

1.5.
Related results and comments

1.5.1.
Korenblum-Roberts Theorem

A singular inner function is not cyclic in the Hardy spaces
\mathcal{H}^{t}=\mathcal{P}^{t}(d\textit{m})
. The situation is different in the context of Bergman spaces
\mathcal{P}^{t}(dA_{\alpha})
, and was explained in the 1980s in the works of Korenblum in
[
17
]
and Roberts in
[
26
]
. They gave two very different proofs of the following statement, a precursor of our main result in ?THM?
.

Theorem

(
Korenblum-Roberts cyclicity theorem
)
.

The singular inner function
S_{\nu}
is cyclic in the standard weighted Bergman spaces
\mathcal{P}^{t}(dA_{\alpha})
,
t\in(0,\infty)
, if and only if
\nu
vanishes on all Beurling-Carleson sets of Lebesgue measure zero:

\nu(E)=0,\quad E\in\mathcal{BC},\,|E|=0.

Korenblum derives the result from his famous works
[
15
]
and
[
16
]
on cyclic vector in the so-called growth classes. The proof of Roberts is completely different and of importance in its own right. Note that if we set
w\equiv 0
on
\mathbb{T}
, then
\text{Assoc}\mathcal{BC}(w)
coincides with the family of
\mathcal{BC}
sets of Lebesgue measure zero. Consequently, the Korenblum-Roberts theorem is a special case of ?THM?
, but the argument we present below is not a new proof of the classical result. To establish ?THM?
, we will use extensions of linear programming techniques of Korenblum from
[
15
]
,
[
16
]
augumented with recently obtained specialized constrained
\mathcal{L}^{1}
-optimization tools from
[
3
]
which we describe in Section
2
. Techniques of Roberts, on the other hand, have recently found interesting applications, for instance in the works of Ivrii in
[
12
]
and Ivrii and Nicolau in
[
13
]
.

1.5.2.
Faster decreasing weights on
\mathbb{D}

Several of our results have versions for measures
\mu
of structure similar to (
1.2
) but where
dA_{\alpha}
is replaced by a radial measure
G(|z|)dA(z)
with
G(x)\to 0
as
x\to 1
sufficiently slowly, see (
3.1
) below. These results have essentially the same proofs as our main theorems, and in the coming sections we shall carry out our arguments in the general context and state the generalized results. Our method does not apply to weights of the form
G(x)=\exp\big(-c/(1-x)\big)
for
c>0
. The structure of the space
\mathcal{P}^{2}(\mu)
of this form has been studied in
[
22
]
and
[
23
]
, and is analogous to the one presented here in ?THM?
and ?THM?
, but where the associated sets
\text{Assoc}\mathcal{BC}(w)
replaced by the family of intervals on which
w
has an integrable logarithm. The new difficulties in the setting considered here are related to the complexity of the sets in
\text{Assoc}\mathcal{BC}(w)
in comparison to intervals. In particular, in
[
22
]
and
[
23
]
no use of linear optimization theory was necessary.

1.5.3.
The work of Aleman, Richter and Sundberg

Work on analytic
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
spaces from a different viewpoint than ours has been carried out by Aleman, Richter and Sundberg. Their article
[
2
]
is concerned with function and operator theory in
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
spaces for a general measure
\mu
supported in the closed unit disk
\overline{\mathbb{D}}=\mathbb{D}\cup\mathbb{T}
. In the article, the authors work from the get go under the assumption that
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
is a space of analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
, without any additional assumptions on the structure of the measure
\mu
. Using in part methods of Thomson from
[
28
]
, they obtain the important existence result for non-tangential boundary values for functions in the space, and as a consequence obtain information regarding zero sets and interpolating sequences. They prove also the important index theorem for
M_{z}
-invariant subspaces: given any
M_{z}
-invariant subspace
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
, the orthogonal complement of
M_{z}\mathcal{N}
inside
\mathcal{N}
has dimension
1
.

1.5.4.
Unbounded cyclic functions

We stress that the restriction to consider bounded functions
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
in our cyclicity problem is motivated by the applications, which we shall soon present, but it is also critical. Our main result in ?THM?
and the corollary above has a straight-forward extension to cyclic functions

in the Nevanlinna class, i.e, quotients
f=d/c
of bounded analytic functions

,
c
in
\mathbb{D}
(see the end of Section
5
for the precise statement and a short argument), but the problem of characterizing the cyclicity for a more general function
f\in\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
, even in the case
d\mu=dA
, is a well-known open problem in the theory of Bergman spaces. In that context, a sufficient condition and a necessary condition, which are not too far apart, follow from Korenblum’s works
[
15
]
,
[
16
]
on the
extended Nevanlinna class
. Widening of our results to the setting of Korenblum’s works requires some further efforts which are not part of this article.

1.6.
An application to
\mathcal{H}(b)
spaces

Our corollary to ?THM?
has a direct application to the theory of de Branges-Rovnyak spaces
\mathcal{H}(b)
, a family of Hilbert spaces of analytic functions in
\mathbb{D}
associated to analytic self-maps
b
of
\mathbb{D}
. We refer the reader to
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
and
[
27
]
for the theory of these spaces. With aid of the results in
[
20
]
and
[
21
]
, results of the present work enable us to complete the characterization of the class of symbols

corresponding to spaces
\mathcal{H}(b)
admitting a norm dense subset of functions in
\mathcal{A}^{\infty}
, the algebra of analytic functions in
\mathbb{D}
with
C^{\infty}
extensions to the boundary
\mathbb{T}
.

It was found in
[
20
, Theorem 1.1]
that two necessary conditions for the density of
\mathcal{A}^{\infty}\cap\mathcal{H}(b)
in
\mathcal{H}(b)
can be expressed in terms of the structure of
M_{z}
-invariant subspaces of
\mathcal{P}^{2}(\mu)
, where
\mu
has the form (
1.2
), and

(1.8)

w=\Delta:=\sqrt{1-|b|^{2}}.

The parameter
\alpha
in the definition of

2.
An optimization problem and the Hausdorff functional

In this section, we introduce our principal
\mathcal{L}^{1}
-optimization problem and the functionals
\mathcal{M}(h,R)
from
[
3
]
. We derive the critical lower estimate in (
2.5
) on the optimal value in the optimization problem from a duality theorem relating it to the functional
\mathcal{M}(h,R)
.

In the context of our main results, we think of
h
as a positive scalar multiple of (
1.4
), but the functional
\mathcal{M}(h,R)
may be defined on a larger class of functions
h
which we will introduce next. The broader context leads to no additional difficulties in the proofs, and on the upside it allows us to prove results slighly more general than the ones presented in ?THM?
and ?THM?
.

2.1.
The principal
\mathcal{L}^{1}
-optimization problem

The most important properties of the function
h
in (
1.4
) are that
h
is increasing and continuous on
(0,1]
, satisfies
h(0):=\lim_{x\to 0}h(x)=0
, and additionally

(R1)

h(x)/x
is decreasing in
x
,

(R2)

\lim_{x\to 0}h(x)/x=\infty
.

We will say that any function
h
is a
gauge function
if it satisfies the above properties. It is easy to verify subadditive inequality
h(x+y)\leq h(x)+h(y)
. The argument
x
of
h(x)
will for the most part be the length
|I|
of an interval
I\subset\mathbb{T}
. We use throughout the article the normalization
|\mathbb{T}|=m(\mathbb{T})=1
.

Given a non-negative function
R:\mathbb{T}\to[0,\infty]
and a gauge function
h
, the family
\mathcal{F}(h,R)
is to consist of all non-negative functions on
\mathbb{T}
dominated pointwise by
R
and satisfying a local mass distribution bound defined in terms of the gauge
h
:

(2.1)

\mathcal{F}(h,R):=\Big\{f\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(d\textit{m}):0\leq f\leq R,\int_{\ell}f\,d\textit{m}\leq h(|\ell|)\text{ for all intervals }\ell\Big\}.

Denote by
\|f\|_{1}=\int_{\mathbb{T}}|f|\,d\,m
the usual
\mathcal{L}^{1}(d\textit{m})
norm of
f
. The optimal amount of mass that can be distributed under the constraints defining
\mathcal{F}(h,R)
, namely

\sup\,\Big\{\|f\|_{1}:f\in\mathcal{F}(h,R)\Big\},

will be of main importance in the article.

2.2.
Hausdorff functionals and their duality

Here is a dual way to express the previous supremum. With notation as above, we intoduce the quantity

(2.2)

\mathcal{M}(h,R):=\inf_{\mathcal{U}}\,\Big(\sum_{{}_{\ell\in\mathcal{U}}}h(|\ell|)+\int_{\mathbb{T}\setminus\cup_{\ell\in\mathcal{U}}\ell}R\,d\textit{m}\Big)

where the infimum above is taken over all families
\mathcal{U}=\{\ell\}
of open intervals
\ell
in
\mathbb{T}
. With
h
fixed and
R=\infty\cdot 1_{E}
, where
1_{E}
is the indicator function of a measurable subset
E\subset\mathbb{T}
, the mapping
E\mapsto\mathcal{M}(h,\infty\cdot 1_{E})
is similar to a degree to the definition of the usual Hausdorff content of the set
E
. We will call
\mathcal{M}(h,R)
for the
Hausdorff functional
.

The importance of the Hausdorff functional comes from the following result established in
[
3
]
. It is of the type commonly encountered in the duality theory for linear programs.

Proposition 2.1
.

(Duality for Hausdorff Functionals)

We have the inequality

6\cdot\sup\Big\{\|f\|_{1}:f\in\mathcal{F}(h,R)\Big\}>\,\mathcal{M}(h,R).

We note that the reverse inequality

\sup\Big\{\|f\|_{1}:f\in\mathcal{F}(h,R)\Big\}\leq\,\mathcal{M}(h,R)

follows readily from the definitions (see
[
3
]
).

2.3.
Generalized cores, residuals, and the residual bound

We may define the family
\text{Assoc}\mathcal{BC}_{h}(w)
and the sets
\text{core}_{h}(w)
,
\text{res}_{h}(w)
analogously to how it was done in the Introduction by replacing the function in (
1.4
) with a more general gauge function. Namely, for any gauge function
h
, we define the family
\mathcal{BC}_{h}
of closed subsets of
\mathbb{T}
which satisfy (
1.5
), and we let
\text{Assoc}\mathcal{BC}_{h}(w)
be defined as in (
1.6
) with
\mathcal{BC}_{h}
replacing
\mathcal{BC}
. The sets
\text{core}_{h}(w)
and
\text{res}_{h}(w)
are also defined analogously. We set

\text{core}_{h}(w)=\bigcup_{n}E_{n}

for any increasing sequence
\{E_{n}\}_{n}
of
\mathcal{BC}_{h}
sets satisfying

\lim_{n}|E_{n}|=\sup\Big\{|E|:E\in\text{Assoc}\mathcal{BC}_{h}(w)\Big\},

and we set

\text{res}_{h}(w)=\{z\in\mathbb{T}:w(z)>0\}\setminus\text{core}_{h}(w).

For
h
in (
1.4
), our generalized definitions reduce to the ones stated in the Introduction.

Properties of core and residual sets in the unweighted context are presented in
[
24
]
. With some necessary and natural modifications, these properties carry over to the weighted context. The most important property of the set
\text{core}_{h}(w)
is the implication

(2.3)

E\in\text{Assoc}\mathcal{BC}_{h}(w)\quad\Rightarrow\quad|E\setminus\text{core}_{h}(w)|=0.

In other words, up to differences of Lebesgue measure zero, the set
\text{core}_{h}(w)
contains all sets in
\text{Assoc}\mathcal{BC}_{h}(w)
. The implication (
2.3
) is non-trivial only for sets
E
of positive Lebesgue measure, of course. The proof of the implication is straight-forward (see the proof of
[
24
, Proposition 3.3]
for a similar argument).

The following weighted version of
[
24
, Proposition 3.5]
is the most important property of the set
\text{res}_{h}(w)
which we shall use.

Lemma 2.2
.

(Residual Lower Bound)

Let
R=\log^{+}(1/w)
. If
I
is an interval in
\mathbb{T}
and
R|I
is the restriction of
R
to
I
, then

\mathcal{M}(h,R|I)\geq h(|I\cap\text{res}_{h}(w)|).

In the above statement, and throughout the article, we use a slightly non-standard convention: we interpret the restriction
f|S
of a function
f
to a set
S
as the function coinciding with
f
on the set
S
, and vanishing elsewhere. As usual, we set
\log^{+}(x):=\max(\log x,0)
. Note that we have the equivalence

\int_{E}\log^{+}(1/w)\,d\textit{m}<+\infty\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\int_{E}\log w\,d\textit{m}>-\infty.

In other words,
E\in\mathcal{BC}_{h}
is in
\text{Assoc}\mathcal{BC}_{h}(w)
if and only if
R
is integrable on
E
.

Proof of ?THM?
.

Let
\mathcal{U}=\{\ell\}
be any family of open intervals contained in
I
for which the right-hand side in (
2.2
) is finite (with
R
replaced by
R|I
). Setting
E:=I\setminus\cup_{\ell\in\mathcal{U}}\ell
, it follows that

(2.4)

\int_{E}R\,d\textit{m}=\int_{E}\log^{+}(1/w)\,d\textit{m}<\infty.

If we assume that
I
is closed, which we clearly may without loss of generality, then
E
is closed. Since
\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{U}}h(|\ell|)<\infty
, it follows that
E\in\mathcal{BC}_{h}
, and (
2.4
) shows that in fact we have
E\in\text{Assoc}\mathcal{BC}_{h}(w)
. This implies by (
2.3
) that
|E\setminus\text{core}_{h}(w)|=0
, and so
E
is contained in
\text{core}_{h}(w)
, up to a set of Lebesgue measure zero. In particular, we must have
|E\cap\text{res}_{h}(w)|=0
, from which it follows that
I\cap\text{res}_{h}(w)
is contained in
\cup_{\ell\in\mathcal{U}}\ell
, again up to a set of Lebesgue measure zero. But then

|I\cap\text{res}_{h}(w)|\leq|\cup_{\ell\in\mathcal{U}}\ell|\leq\sum_{{}_{\ell\in\mathcal{U}}}|\ell|,

and, since
h
is increasing and subadditive, we obtain

h(|I\cap\text{res}_{h}(w)|)\leq\sum_{{}_{\ell\in\mathcal{U}}}h(|\ell|)\leq\sum_{{}_{\ell\in\mathcal{U}}}h(|\ell|)+\int_{I\setminus\cup_{\ell\in\mathcal{U}}\ell}R\,d\textit{m}.

The claim follows by taking infimum over families
\mathcal{U}=\{\ell\}
in the last inequality.
∎

Combining ?THM?
with ?THM?
, and using notation as in ?THM?
, we obtain the important lower bound

(2.5)

\sup\Big\{\|f\|_{1}:f\in\mathcal{F}(h,R|I)\Big\}>\frac{h(|I\cap\text{res}_{h}(w)|)}{6}

This critical estimate will let us generalize the main result of
[
24
]
into ?THM?
. Note that a truncation shows that there always exist a
bounded
function
f\in\mathcal{F}(h,R)
for which
6\int_{\mathbb{T}}f\,d\textit{m}=\mathcal{M}(h,R)
.

3.
Thomson decomposition of the measure

The goal of the section is to prove ?THM?
. At no additional strain, we carry out the proof in the context of general gauge function
h
and in this way obtain more general results. We assume throughout that
\log w\not\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(d\textit{m})
, since the contrary case is trivial, as explained at the end of Section
1.4
.

3.1.
A more general class of measures

Given a gauge function
h
satisfying the properties (R1) and (R2) stated in Section
2.1
, we may associate to it the the family of measures

(3.1)

d\mu=G_{ah}dA+w\,d\textit{m},\quad a>0,

where

G_{ah}(z)=\exp\Big(-a\frac{h(1-|z|)}{1-|z|}\Big),\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Note that the functions
G_{ah}(x)
are decreasing in
x\in(0,1)
, and so strictly speaking (
3.1
) does not include measures of the form (
1.2
) for
\alpha\in(-1,0)
, but this difference is insignificant. For certain choices of the gauge
h
a direct generalization of ?THM?
to measures of the form (
3.1
) holds. The corresponding decomposition of the weight
w
is

w_{c}=w|\text{core}_{h}(w),\quad w_{r}=w|\text{res}_{h}(w)

with definitions as in Section
2.3
.
More precisely, we will show that for any
h
we have the decomposition

(3.2)

\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)=\mathcal{P}^{t}(G_{ah}dA+w_{c}\,d\textit{m})\oplus\mathcal{L}^{t}(w_{r}\,d\textit{m}).

The irreducibility of the piece
\mathcal{P}^{t}(G_{ah}+w_{c}\,d\textit{m})
will be established in Section
3.4
below, but only under some additional regularity assumptions on
h
which in particular hold for the gauge function in (
1.4
).

In context of the measures
\mu
of the form (
1.2
), we always assume that the gauge
h
has the form (
1.4
).

3.2.
A sufficient condition for establishing the decomposition

To establish the direct sum decomposition in ?THM?
, or the one in (
3.2
), it will suffice to show that

(3.3)

\mathcal{L}^{t}(w_{r}\,d\textit{m})\subset\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu).

Here we interpret
f\in\mathcal{L}^{t}(w_{r}\,d\textit{m})
as an element of
\mathcal{L}^{t}(\mu)
by extending
f
to be zero outside of the set
\{z\in\mathbb{T}:w_{r}(z)>0\}
(which is well-defined up to a set of Lebesgue measure zero). Indeed, if (
3.3
) holds, then for every
f\in\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
we have that
f-f|\text{res}_{h}(w)\in\mathcal{P}^{t}(dA_{\alpha}+w_{c}\,d\textit{m})\cap\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
, and
f|\text{res}_{h}(w)\in\mathcal{L}^{t}(w_{r}\,d\textit{m})\cap\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
.

The following lemma, essentially contained in
[
22
, Lemma 2.1 and proof of Lemma 3.1]
, lets us reduce our task to a construction of a specific function inside
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

In order to establish (
3.3
), it suffices to show that there exists
F\in\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
which satisfies
F|\mathbb{D}\equiv 1
,
F|\text{res}_{h}(w)\equiv 0
, and
F|\mathbb{T}\in\mathcal{L}^{t^{*}}(w\,d\textit{m})
for some
t^{*}>\max(t,1)
.

Proof.

If
f\in\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
is as stated, then
g=1-F\in\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
vanishes on
\mathbb{D}
and is non-zero almost everywhere on
\text{res}_{h}(w)
. The
M_{z}
-invariant subspace
[g]
of
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
generated by
g
is then a subspace of
\mathcal{L}^{t}(w\,d\textit{m})
, with containment interpreted as in (
3.3
).

Let us first assume that
t>1
. Then, since
[g]
is a subspace of
\mathcal{L}^{t}(w\,d\textit{m})
which is invariant for the operator
M_{z}
, the space

w^{1/t}[g]:=\{w^{1/t}h:h\in[g]\}

is contained in
\mathcal{L}^{t}(d\textit{m})
and it has one of the two forms well known from the classical Beurling-Wiener Theorem (see
[
22
, Section 2.3]
). Namely, either we have

(3.4)

w^{1/t}[g]=\{qH:H\in\mathcal{H}^{t}\}

for some unimodular function
q
on
\mathbb{T}
,
\mathcal{H}^{t}
being the Hardy space, or for some
S\subset\mathbb{T}
we have

(3.5)

w^{1/t}[g]=\mathcal{L}^{t}(d\textit{m}|S)=\{f\in\mathcal{L}^{t}(d\textit{m}):f\equiv 0\text{ {m}-a.e outside of }S\}.

In the first case, every non-zero function
d\in[g]
can be expressed as
w^{1/t}d=qH
for some non-zero
H\in\mathcal{H}^{t}
, and so

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log(w^{1/t}|d|)\,dm=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log|H|\,d\textit{m}>-\infty,

the last inequality being a well-known property of non-zero functions in
\mathcal{H}^{t}
. Since
g\in\mathcal{L}^{t^{*}}(w\,d\textit{m})
,
t^{*}>t
, a simple computation shows that

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log(w^{1/t}|g|)d\textit{m}=-\infty

(see
[
22
, proof of Lemma 3.1]
and recall that we assume
\log w\not\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(d\textit{m})
). Since we can set
d=g
above, the alternative (
3.4
) is excluded, and we deduce that we are in the situation in (
3.5
). Since
w^{1/t}g
does not vanish on the set
\text{res}_{h}(w)
, the set
S
in (
3.5
) must contain
\text{res}_{h}(w)
, up to a difference of Lebesgue measure zero. It follows that if
f\in\mathcal{L}^{t}(w_{r}\,d\textit{m})
, then

w^{1/t}f\in\mathcal{L}^{t}(d\textit{m}|S)=w^{1/t}[g]

Hence
\mathcal{L}^{t}(w_{r}d\textit{m})\subset[g]
, and we have verified (
3.3
) in the case
t>1
.

If
t\leq 1
, then since for
t<t^{\prime}
, convergence in
\mathcal{L}^{t^{\prime}}(w\,d\textit{m})
implies convergence in
\mathcal{L}^{t}(w\,d\textit{m})
, the previous argument may be applied to some
t^{\prime}>1
slightly smaller than
t^{*}
to conclude that
[g]
(which, we emphasize, is a subspace of
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
) contains the corresponding
M_{z}
-invariant subspace generated by
g
in
L^{t^{\prime}}(w\,d\textit{m})
. By the proof of the case

, in particular
[g]
will contain all bounded measurable functions living on the set where

is non-zero. A density argument then shows that
[g]
contains
L^{t}(w_{r}\,d\textit{m})
.
∎

3.3.
The construction

We proceed to show how to obtain

satisfying the properties mentioned in ?THM?
. The function

will be obtained as a weak cluster point of the outer functions

(3.6)

F_{N}(z):=\exp\Big(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{\zeta+z}{\zeta-z}f_{N}(\zeta)d\textit{m}(\zeta)\Big),\quad z\in\mathbb{D},N\in\mathbb{N}

where

are carefully chosen real-valued functions. Note that
F_{N}\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
, and so the containment

follows from a straight-forward argument involving a dilation and a Taylor series truncation.

The following result is the improvement of
[

, Lemma 3.1]
needed to establish the sought-after direct sum decomposition. The proof is the same, with the exception that we utilize the new inequality (

).

Lemma 3.2

(
Main Construction
)
.

Let
h
be a gauge function. There exists a sequence of real-valued function
\{f_{N}\}_{N}
on
\mathbb{T}
satisfying the following conditions:

(i)

for any
\epsilon>0

holds for all

sufficiently large,

(ii)

for any
\epsilon>0
, the inequality
\int_{I}f_{N}\,d\textit{m}\leq\epsilon\cdot h(|I|)
holds for all intervals
I\subset\mathbb{T}
and all
N
sufficiently large,

(iii)

\int_{\mathbb{T}}f_{N}\,d\textit{m}=0
,

(iv)

f_{N}\leq 0
on
\text{res}_{h}(w)
, and
f_{N}(x)\to-\infty

m
-almost everywhere on
\text{res}_{h}(w)
.

Proof.

Fix a positive integer
N
and set

(3.7)

A_{N}:=\{\zeta\in\text{res}_{h}(w):R(\zeta)=\log^{+}\big(1/w(\zeta)\big)\leq M(N)\},

where
M(N)
is some positive number soon to be specified. Note that if
M(N)\to\infty
as
N\to\infty
, then
\cup_{N}A_{N}
is a set of full measure in
\text{res}_{h}(w)
, namely
|\cup_{N}A_{N}|=|\text{res}_{h}(w)|
. This will be important at the end of the proof, when part
(iv)
above will be verified for our construction.

Denote by
1_{A}
the indicator function of a set

. Divide
\mathbb{T}
into
N

\{I_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N}
of equal length, and define

according to

(3.8)

b(N)\cdot f_{N}:=\sum_{k:|I_{k}\cap A_{N}|>0}g_{N,k}\cdot 1_{I_{k}\setminus A_{N}}-C_{N,k}\cdot 1_{I_{k}\cap A_{N}},

where

is a large positive number soon to be specified, where

is bounded and satisfies

(3.9)

\int_{I_{k}}g_{N,k}\,d\textit{m}\geq\frac{h(|I_{k}\cap\text{res}_{h}(w)|)}{6}

and where
C_{N,k}
is the unique positive number which ensures that
\int_{I_{k}}f_{N}\,d\textit{m}=0
, that is,

(3.10)

C_{N,k}=\frac{\int_{I_{k}\setminus A_{N}}g_{N,k}\,d\textit{m}}{|I_{k}\cap A_{N}|}.

The existence of
g_{N,k}
is ensured by inequality the (
2.5
).

Since
g_{N,k}\in\mathcal{F}(h,R|I_{k})
, it follows that
b(N)\cdot f_{N}\leq\log^{+}(1/w)
on
\mathbb{T}
, and so we ensure
(i)
if

is chosen sufficiently large. In particular, this holds if a priori we have

, which we will be able to ensure.

If
I
is any interval on
\mathbb{T}
, then
b(N)\cdot f_{N}
integrates to zero over any interval

contained in

, and so if
I_{l}
and
I_{r}
are the the two intervals among
\{I_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N}
which contain the endpoints of

\displaystyle\int_{I}b(N)\cdot f_{N}\,d\textit{m}

\displaystyle=\int_{I_{l}}b(N)\cdot f_{N}\,d\textit{m}+\int_{I_{r}}b(N)\cdot f_{N}\,d\textit{m}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{I_{l}\cap I}g_{N,l}\,d\textit{m}+\int_{I_{r}\cap I}g_{N,r}\,d\textit{m}

\displaystyle\leq 2\cdot h(|I|).

We used that
g_{N,l}\in\mathcal{F}(h,R|I_{l})
and
g_{N,r}\in\mathcal{F}(h,R|I_{r})
in the last inequality. Again, we obtain
(ii)
if

as

.

Part
(iii)
holds since

b(N)\cdot\int_{\mathbb{T}}f_{N}\,d\textit{m}=b(N)\cdot\sum_{k:|I:k\cap A_{N}|>0}\int_{I_{k}}f_{N}\,d\textit{m}=0.

It remains to show that choices of
M(N)
in (

The irreducibility of the first summand on the right hand side in the displayed equation in ?THM?

Lemma 3.3

(

)

Assume that the gauge function

The only technical part of the proof is the estimation of harmonic measure of a certain domain, which Khrushchev accomplishes in

We denote by

in

\displaystyle|W_{E}(\zeta)|=\begin{cases}1,&\zeta\in\mathbb{T}\setminus E,\\

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{E}\frac{2\zeta}{\zeta-z}d\nu(\zeta)\Big||S_{\nu}(z)|

\displaystyle\leq 2\nu(\mathbb{T})\cdot\text{dist}(z,E)^{-2}.

\displaystyle 1-|S_{\nu}(w)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{L}(\text{dist}(z,E))^{-2}|dz|

It follows that we may choose

4.4.
Proof of the permanence property

In the notation of ?THM?
, the function

is bounded outside of
\Omega

, there exists a sequence

of polynomials for which

in the norm of

We introduce the outer function

satisfying the following equality

\displaystyle|F_{E}(\zeta)|=\begin{cases}\text{dist}(\zeta,E),&\zeta\in\mathbb{T}\setminus E,\\
1,&\zeta\in E.\end{cases}

That is,

The convergence of the integral in the definition of

is assured as a consequence of

for sets

. Note that by adding finitely many points to

If

, and by construction of

(4.3)

\text{dist}(w,w^{*})=1-|w|\simeq(\text{dist}(w^{*},E))^{2}.

The harmonic measure in

at

. If the complementary interval

, we obtain by the classical Two Constants Theorem that

By replacing

Proof of ?THM?
.

Assume that

5.
Cyclicity of singular inner functions

5.1.
The goal

The result to be proved in this section is the sufficiency part of ?THM?
, and also the following variant.

Proposition 5.1

(
Main Cyclicity Result
)
.

Let
\mu
be of a measure of the form (
3.1
). If
t\in(0,\infty)
,
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
is a space of analytic functions, and
S_{\nu}
is a singular inner function for which the corresponding singular measure satisfies

(5.1)

\nu(E)=0,\quad E\subset\text{Assoc}\mathcal{BC}_{h}(w),

then
S_{\nu}
is cyclic in
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
.

We make a few remarks before proceeding with the proof.

•

It suffices to prove the result for large
t>1
. Indeed, if
t_{1}<t_{2}
and
S_{\nu}
is cyclic in
\mathcal{P}^{t_{2}}(\mu)
, then denoting by
\|\cdot\|_{\mu,t}
the norm in
\mathcal{L}^{t}(\mu)
, there exists a sequence
\{p_{n}\}_{n}
of polynomials such that
\|S_{\nu}p_{n}-1\|_{\mu,t_{2}}\to 0
. By Jensen’s inequality applied to the convex function
x\mapsto x^{t_{2}/t_{1}}
and a normalized version of the measure
\mu
, we obtain readily that
\|S_{\nu}p_{n}-1\|_{\mu,t_{1}}\to 0
. In particular, we may in our proofs work in the more convenient reflexive range
t\in(1,\infty)
. It is worth noting here that increasing the parameter
t
does not affect analyticity of the space
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
. To see this, note that if
\mathcal{P}^{t_{2}}(\mu)
is not analytic, and so contains a function
f
satisfying
f|\mathbb{D}\equiv 0
, then whenever
t_{1}<t_{2}
, we have also that
f\in\mathcal{P}^{t_{1}}(\mu)
, and consequently
\mathcal{P}^{t_{1}}(\mu)
is not analytic either. In fact, for most natural gauge functions
h
, such as the one in (
1.4
), it is a consequence of ?THM?
, or the variants presented in Section
3.4
, that analyticity of
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
is independent of the choice of the parameter
t
, since it is decided entirely by the structure of the weight
w
appearing in (
1.2
) or (
3.1
).

•

It suffices to show that
S_{\nu}^{1/B}
is cyclic in
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
for some positive integer
B
. Indeed, if
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
are two cyclic
bounded
functions, then so is their product. The claim follows from the estimate

\|m_{1}m_{2}pq-1\|_{\mu,t}\leq\|m_{2}q-1\|_{\mu,t}\cdot\|m_{1}p\|_{\infty}+\|m_{1}p-1\|_{\mu,t}

where
p
and
q
are polynomials. If
m_{1}
is cyclic, we may choose
p
to make the second term on the right-hand side small. Having fixed
p
, if
m_{2}
s cyclic we may choose
q
to make the first term on the right-hand side small. Cyclicity of
m_{1}m_{2}
follows.

•

We emphasize again that the necessity for cyclicity of
S_{\nu}
of vanishing of
\nu
on associated set has been established in Section
4
only for the case of measures
\mu
of the form (
1.2
). In particular, the converse has not been established in the context of more general gauges
h
. Thus we make no claims regarding the necessity of the vanishing condition in ?THM?
.

5.2.
Modification of Korenblum’s linear program

Let
\nu
be a finite non-negative singular Borel measure on
\mathbb{T}
, and set

(5.2)

\nu_{0}=:\nu-\nu(\mathbb{T})d\textit{m}.

Let

be a positive integer, and

be a partition of

into the half-open intervals

Note that the partition depends on the choice of

, but for convenience we suppress this in our notation. We introduce a notation for unions of consecutive intervals

as follows:

(5.3)

As before, we set

We will study a variant of Korenblum’s linear program in
[

. Our program, with real unknowns

,

, is as follows:

(5.4)

\begin{cases}-\nu_{0}(I_{k,l})+\sum_{s=k}^{l}x_{s}\,\leq\epsilon\cdot h(|I_{k,l}|),\\
\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\sum_{s=k}^{l}x_{s}\leq\mathcal{M}(A\cdot h,R|I_{k,l}),\\
\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\sum_{s=1}^{N}x_{s}=0.\end{cases}

The notation

stands for the restriction of

to the interval
I
, interpreted as before to vanish outside of the interval

. The quantities

and

are positive numbers. The most significant difference between (

[

is the appearance of the Hausdorff functional

in our version, in the place where in Korenblum’s original program the quantity

is present. In accordance with the presentation of Korenblum’s theorem in the book by Hedenmalm, Korenblum and Zhu in
[

, we will say that the program is

if for every choice of

there exists a choice of

for which the linear program (

) has a solution

for
any

. Otherwise, the program is

, in which case there exists a fixed

such that for any choice of

the program fails to have a solution for some

Our purpose in the rest of the section is to establish two claims.
The first claim is that if the program (

) is consistent, then

is cyclic in the corresponding
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
space. The second claim is that if (

) is inconsistent, then

for some set

. If the claims are established, then it follows from the first claim that if

is not cyclic in
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
, then the program is inconsistent, and consequently from the second claim we obtain

for some

. Thus ?THM?
and the sufficiency part of ?THM?
are consequences of these two claims. Our proofs will use adaptations of Korenblum’s technique from
[
16
]
, with the main new ideas relating to the way in which the functional
\mathcal{M}(h,R)
enters the picture.

5.3.
Sufficiency of consistency for cyclicity

We are assuming that the program is consistent. We fix a small

Set

We establish now some estimates uniform in

Lemma 5.4
.

In the notation as above, we have that

5.4.
Necessity of consistency for cyclicity

According to what was said in the last paragraph of Section

, in order to complete the proofs of ?THM?
and ?THM?
, we now need to show that if the linear program (

5.4.1.
The Inconsistency Inequality

We start the necessity proof in the same way as Korenblum started his proof in
[
16
]
by deriving an inequality from the inconsistency of the linear program.

Note that the first two inequalities in (
5.4
) may be written as a single inequality in the following way:

-\nu_{0}(I_{k,l})+\sum_{s=k}^{l}x_{s}\,\leq\min\big(-\nu_{0}(I_{k,l})+\mathcal{M}(A\cdot h,R|I_{k,l}),\,\epsilon\cdot h(|I_{k,l}|)\big).

Only for a moment, let us denote the right-hand side of this inequality by
b_{k,l}
, and think of
y_{s}=-\nu_{0}(I_{s})+x_{s}
as the unknowns in a new linear program. Note that
\sum_{s=1}^{N}y_{s}=0
if and only if
\sum_{s=1}^{N}x_{s}=0
, since
\nu_{0}
is normalized and additive. The proof of the following duality lemma for linear programs appears in
[
11
, Lemma 7.7]
, where Korenblum’s work on cyclic vectors is presented. The lemma appears implicitly in the work of Korenblum in
[
16
]
.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
N
be a fixed positive integer, and
b_{k,l}
be real numbers,
1\leq k\leq l\leq N
.
The system of inequalities

\sum_{s=k}^{l}y_{s}\leq b_{k,l}

subject to the constraint
\sum_{s=1}^{N}y_{s}=0
is solvable if and only if

\sum_{i=1}^{m}b_{k_{i},k_{i+1}-1}\geq 0

for every increasing sequence
\{k_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m-1}
of integers,
k_{i}\in[1,\ldots,N]
, where
k_{1}=1
and
k_{m}=N+1
.

Note that the sequence
\{k_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
in the statement of the lemma partitions the set of integers
[1,\ldots,N]
into
m-1
”intervals”
[k_{i},k_{i+1}-1]
. Such partitions are called
simple coverings
in
[
11
]
and
[
16
]
. A simple covering corresponds to a covering of the circle
\mathbb{T}
by a finite number of half-open intervals
\{J_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m-1}
as in (
5.3
), where we use the notation

J_{i}=I_{k_{i},k_{i+1}-1}.

Then by our above discussion and ?THM?
we see that inconsistency of the system (
5.4
) implies that there exists
\epsilon>0
such that to every choice of
A
there corresponds a covering
\{J_{i}\}_{i}
of
\mathbb{T}
consisting of half-open intervals, which satisfies

(5.17)

\sum_{i}\min\big(-\nu_{0}(J_{i})+\mathcal{M}(A\cdot h,R|J_{i}),\,\epsilon\cdot h(|J_{i}|)\big)<0.

Denoting by
J^{\prime}_{i}
those intervals among
J_{i}
for which

\min\big(-\nu_{0}(J_{i})+\mathcal{M}(A\cdot h,R|J_{i}),\,\epsilon\cdot h(|J_{i}|)\big)=-\nu_{0}(J_{i})+\mathcal{M}(A\cdot h,R|J_{i})

and by
J^{\prime\prime}_{i}
the remaining ones, by rearranging (
5.17
) we obtain our
Inconsistency Inequality
, which reads

(5.18)

\sum_{i}\mathcal{M}(A\cdot h,R|J^{\prime}_{i})+\sum_{i}\epsilon\cdot h(|J^{\prime\prime}_{i}|)<\sum_{i}\nu_{0}(J^{\prime}_{i}).

We emphasize that
\epsilon
remains now fixed for the rest of the proof, and that the covering
\{J_{i}\}_{i}
depends on the choice of
A
, but that we suppress this in our notation for now. Note also that the left-hand side of the inequality is a positive quantity. In fact, this quantity stays away from
0
.

Lemma 5.6
.

Assume that
R>0
on a subset of positive Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{T}
. Then we have

\inf_{A:A\geq\epsilon}\,\sum_{i}\mathcal{M}(A\cdot h,R|J^{\prime}_{i})+\sum_{i}\epsilon\cdot h(|J^{\prime\prime}_{i}|)>c_{0}

for some positive number
c_{0}
.

Proof.

Let us assume that by varying
A
it is possible to have the left-hand side above converge to zero. We will show
R
vanishes almost everywhere with respect to the Lebesgue measure
d\textit{m}
.

By the definition in (
2.2
), for each interval
J^{\prime}_{i}
there exists a choice of an open set
U^{\prime}_{i}\subset J^{\prime}_{i}
which is a union of a family of open intervals
\{\ell_{i,k}\}_{k}
for which
\int_{J^{\prime}_{i}\setminus U^{\prime}_{i}}R\,d\textit{m}+\sum_{k}A\cdot h(|\ell_{i,k}|)
is as close as we wish to
\mathcal{M}(A\cdot h,R|J^{\prime}_{i})
. Let

J^{\prime}_{A}=\bigcup_{i}J^{\prime}_{i},\quad U^{\prime}_{A}=\bigcup_{i}U^{\prime}_{i}

and let
\{\ell_{k}\}_{k}
be the union over
i
of the families
\{\ell_{i,k}\}_{k}
, so that
U^{\prime}_{A}=\bigcup_{k}\ell_{k}
. Since
A\geq\epsilon
, by our assumption we may arrange our choices so that

(5.19)

\int_{J^{\prime}_{A}\setminus U^{\prime}_{A}}R\,d\textit{m}+\epsilon\cdot\Big(\sum_{k}h(|\ell_{k}|)+\sum_{i}h(|J^{\prime\prime}_{i}|)\Big)\to 0.

Since
\epsilon
is fixed, we conclude that

\sum_{k}h(|\ell_{k}|)\to 0,\quad\sum_{i}h(|J^{\prime\prime}_{i}|)\to 0,

and in particular we must have

|U^{\prime}_{A}|\leq\sum_{k}|\ell_{k}|\to 0,\quad\sum_{i}|J^{\prime\prime}_{i}|\to 0.

Since
\mathbb{T}=(J^{\prime}_{A}\setminus U^{\prime}_{A})\cup U^{\prime}_{A}\cup\Big(\cup_{i}J^{\prime\prime}_{i}\Big)
, we see that the Lebesgue measures of
J^{\prime}_{A}\setminus U^{\prime}_{A}
must grow to full

-measure in
\mathbb{T}
. Since (
5.19
) implies that
\int_{J^{\prime}_{A}\setminus U^{\prime}_{A}}R\,d\textit{m}\to 0
, and since

is non-negative, we conclude that
\int_{\mathbb{T}}R\,d\textit{m}=0
. So
R\equiv 0
almost everywhere with respect to

.
∎

We mention again that the case

is irrelevant for us, as it is equivalent to

in which case

, and so the space
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
has the simple structure explained in Section

. We may therefore assume that

, and that the lower bound in ?THM?
holds.

5.4.2.
Analysis of the inequality

We have seen that inconsistency of (
5.4
) leads us to the conclusion that there exist an
\epsilon>0
such that to each choice of
A
larger than
\epsilon
, there correspond two families of disjoint half-open intervals
\{J^{\prime}_{i}\}_{i}
and
\{J^{\prime\prime}_{i}\}_{i}
, which together cover the unit circle

, and for which the inequality (
5.18
) holds, with the left-hand side being bounded away from

.

Let
E_{A}
be the union of closures of the intervals

:

E_{A}:=\bigcup_{i}\text{clos}(J^{\prime}_{i}).

Since
\{J^{\prime}_{i}\}_{i}
is a finite family, the set

is closed and differs from the union
\bigcup_{i}J^{\prime}_{i}
only by finitely many points. Using the notation from the proof of ?THM?
, the inconsistency inequality (
5.18
) implies that there exist open sets

c_{0}<\sum_{J^{\prime}_{i}}\Bigg(\int_{J^{\prime}_{i}\setminus U^{\prime}_{i}}R\,d\textit{m}+\sum_{k}A\cdot h(|\ell_{i,k}|)\Bigg)+\sum_{i}\epsilon\cdot h(|J^{\prime\prime}_{i}|)<\sum_{i}\nu_{0}(J^{\prime}_{i}).

Since
\nu_{0}=\nu-\nu(\mathbb{T})d\textit{m}\leq\nu
,

is a non-negative measure, and

is a disjoint family of intervals contained in

, the right-most quantity above is dominated by

. Using still the notation from the proof of ?THM?
and setting

we obtain

(5.20)

c_{0}<\int_{E^{\prime}_{A}}R\,d\textit{m}+\sum_{k}A\cdot h(|\ell_{k}|)+\sum_{i}\epsilon\cdot h(|J^{\prime\prime}_{i}|)<\nu(E_{A}).

The following observations are consequences of the second of the above inequalities.

•

The measure

is non-negative, so
\nu(E_{A})\leq\nu(\mathbb{T})
. As

, we must therefore have
\sum_{k}|\ell_{k}|\to 0
. Since

, we deduce that

(5.21)

E_{A}=E^{\prime}_{A}+U^{\prime}_{A}

where

as

. In particular, it follows that

\lim_{A\to\infty}|E_{A}|-|E^{\prime}_{A}|=0.

•

The family of complementary intervals to

is
\{\text{int}(J^{\prime\prime}_{i})\}_{i}
, the interiors of the intervals

, while the complementary intervals of

consists of the union of the families

and

. From (

(5.22)

\sup_{A:A\geq\epsilon}\Big(\sum_{k}h(|\ell_{k}|)+\sum_{J^{\prime\prime}_{i}}h(|\text{int}(J^{\prime\prime}_{i})|)\Big)<\infty.

5.4.3.
Compactness properties of the class
\mathcal{BC}_{h}

For a closed subset
E
of
\mathbb{T}
and
\delta>0
, let
E^{\delta}
be the usual open
\delta
-neighbourhood of
E
, consisting of all points at distance less than
\delta
from
E
:

E^{\delta}:=\{z\in\mathbb{T}:\text{dist}(z,E)<\delta\}.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
\{E_{n}\}_{n}
be a sequence of sets in
\mathcal{BC}_{h}
. Let
\{\ell_{n,k}\}_{k}
be the sequence of maximal open intervals in
\mathbb{T}
complementary to
E_{n}
, and assume that

(5.23)

\sup_{n}\sum_{k}h(|\ell_{n,k}|)<\infty.

Assume further that for each
n
the sequences
\{\ell_{n,k}\}_{k}
are ordered such that the lengths of the intervals are decreasing in
k
:

|\ell_{n,1}|\geq|\ell_{n,2}|\geq\ldots,

Then there exists a set
E_{\infty}\in\mathcal{BC}_{h}
and a subsequence
\{E_{n_{j}}\}_{j}
such that
E_{n_{j}}\to E_{\infty}
in the following sense:

(i)

\lim_{j\to\infty}|E_{n_{j}}|=|E_{\infty}|
,

(ii)

for every
\delta>0
we have that
E_{\infty}\subset E^{\delta}_{n_{j}}
and
E_{n_{j}}\subset E^{\delta}_{\infty}
for all sufficiently large
j
.

(iii)

For each maximal open interval
\ell_{k}
complementary to
E_{\infty}
, we have that
\ell_{n_{j},k}
converge to
\ell_{k}
as
j\to\infty
, in the sense that the endpoints of
\ell_{n_{j},k}
converge to the endpoints of
\ell_{k}
.

The lemma is a direct generalization to sets of positive Lebesgue measure of
[
16
, Lemma 3.1.1]
. See also
[
11
, Lemma 7.6]
. In
[
3
]
a similar compactness statement appears in the context of unions of dyadic cubes. The proofs of these statements are all similar. We provide one for completeness.

Proof of ?THM?
.

By an initial passing to a subsequence, we may assume that we have the convergence of Lebesgue measures of
E_{n}
, which we may express as the convergence of Lebesgue measures of the complements:

(5.24)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k}|\ell_{n,k}|=L\geq 0.

For
k=1,2,\ldots
, we successively attempt to pass to a subsequence of
\{E_{n}\}_{n}
to ensure that (after relabeling)
\ell_{n,k}
converges in the sense of
(iii)
above to some interval
\ell_{k}
of positive length. Our process stops for some finite
K
if
\lim_{n\to\infty}|\ell_{n,K}|=0
, and then we set
\ell_{k}=\varnothing
for
k\geq K
. Else, the process goes on indefinitely. In the latter case, by the usual diagonal subsequence argument we ensure that
\ell_{n,k}
converges to
\ell_{k}
as
n\to\infty
, for all
k
. We set in either case

E_{\infty}:=\mathbb{T}\setminus\Big(\bigcup_{k}\ell_{k}\Big),

with the appearing union being finite in the former case. Note that we have already ensured
(iii)
.

Since for each positive integer
M
it holds that

\sum_{k\leq M}h(|\ell_{k}|)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k\leq M}h(|\ell_{n,k}|)\leq\sup_{n}\sum_{k}h(|\ell_{n,k}|),

by letting
M\to\infty
we conclude from (
5.23
) that
E_{\infty}\in\mathcal{BC}_{h}
.

To establish that
\lim_{n\to\infty}|E_{n}|=|E_{\infty}|
we may equivalently establish that

(5.25)

\sum_{k}|\ell_{k}|=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k}|\ell_{n,k}|=L.

Fatou’s lemma immediately shows that the left-hand side in (
5.25
) is at most as large as the right-hand side, which equals
L
by (
5.24
). As the left-hand side is non-negative, we are done if
L=0
. Else, we assume for the sake of contradiction that
\sum_{k}|\ell_{k}|=L-\alpha>0
for some
\alpha>0
. Note that for every
K
, we have

(5.26)

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq K}h(|\ell_{n,k}|)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq K}\frac{h(|\ell_{n,k}|)}{|\ell_{n,k}|}|\ell_{n,k}|

5.6.
Nevanlinna class and cyclicity

As alluded to in the Introduction, the cyclicity result has a simple extension from bounded functions to quotients of bounded functions, i.e, to the Nevanlinna class:

f=d/c,\quad d,c\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty},\,c(z)\neq 0,z\in\mathbb{D}.

A function
f
of this form has many other representation as a quotient of bounded functions, and a particularly useful one is

(5.31)

f=\frac{BS_{\nu_{1}}d_{o}}{S_{\nu_{2}}c_{o}},

where
d_{o}
and
c_{o}
are bounded outer functions,
B
is a Blaschke product, and
\nu_{1},\nu_{2}
are singular inner functions for which the corresponding measures
\nu_{1}
and
\nu_{2}
are mutually singular. In other words,
S_{\nu_{1}}
and
S_{\nu_{2}}
have no common non-trivial singular inner factor.

Corollary 5.9
.

Let
f
have the form (
5.31
), with
B\equiv 1
, so that
f
is non-vanishing in
\mathbb{D}
. If
f\in\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
and
\mu
has the form (
1.2
), then
f
is cyclic in
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
if and only if
S_{\nu_{1}}
is cyclic in
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
.

Proof.

If
S_{\nu_{1}}
is not cyclic, then according to our main result there exists a set
E\in\text{Assoc}\mathcal{BC}(w)
for which
\nu_{1}(E)>0
, and any bounded function in the invariant subspace
[S_{\nu_{1}|E}]
has an inner factor divisible by
S_{\nu_{1}|E}
. If
f
is cyclic, then for some sequence
\{p_{n}\}_{n}
of polynomials we have
fp_{n}\to 1
in the norm of
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
. Since multiplication by
S_{\nu_{2}}c_{o}
is a bounded operation on
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
, we obtain the norm convergence

S_{\nu_{1}}d_{o}S_{\nu_{2}}c_{o}p_{n}\to S_{\nu_{2}}c_{o},

and hence
S_{\nu_{2}}c_{o}\in[S_{\nu_{1}|E}]
. Since
c_{0}
is outer, and
\nu_{1}|E
and
\nu_{2}
are mutually singular, the function
S_{\nu_{2}}c_{o}
has the inner factor
S_{\nu_{2}}
which is not divisible by

, and so we reach a contradiction. Thus

not being cyclic implies that

is not cyclic either.

Conversely, assume that

is cyclic. Then
S_{\nu_{1}}d_{o}
is also cyclic, and clearly
S_{\nu_{1}}d_{o}=S_{\nu_{2}}c_{o}f\in[f]
. Hence

[f]\supseteq[S_{\nu_{1}}d_{o}]=\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu),

and the proof is complete.

Appendix A
Premeasures

The use of premeasures in the context of cyclicity problems goes back to Korenblum’s work in
[
15
]
,
[
16
]
, where they were used in Poisson-type representations of harmonic functions which satisfy certain growth bounds in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
. In the article, we study only the cyclicity of bounded functions, and as a consequence, our use of premeasures is not a necessity, but rather a convenience which allows for certain simplifications in proofs.

A.1.
Basic properties

Definition A.1
.

A
normalized premeasure

\sigma
is a set function mapping intervals in
\mathbb{T}
(closed, open or half-open, and the interval may reduce to a single point) into real numbers, with the following properties:

(P1)

\sigma(\mathbb{T})=0

(normalization)
,

(P2)

\sigma(I_{1}\cup I_{2})=\sigma(I_{1})+\sigma(I_{2})
for disjoint intervals
I_{1},I_{2}\subset\mathbb{T}

(additivity)
,

(P3)

if
\{I_{n}\}_{n}
is a nested sequence of intervals shrinking to the empty set, then
\sigma(I_{n})\to 0

(continuity)
.

If the last two properties are satisfied but not the first, then we will simply say that
\sigma
is a
premeasure
.

Korenblum in
[
15
]
and
[
16
]
postulates a number of properties of premeasures. We will spend this section to argue for validity of those properties in a slightly increased generality, which is necessary for the rest of the article.

To every normalized premeasure
\sigma
we may associate its primitive function
\rho_{\sigma}:(0,2\pi]\to\mathbb{R}
, defined by

(A.1)

\rho_{\sigma}(t)=\sigma(I_{t}),\quad I_{t}=[1,e^{it}),\,t\in(0,2\pi].

Here
I_{t}=[1,e^{it})
is the half-open interval of
\mathbb{T}
starting at
1
(inclusive) and ending at
e^{it}
(exclusive). The characterizing properties of the primitive functions
\rho=\rho_{\sigma}
arising in this way from normalized premeasures are the following.

Proposition A.2
.

Let
\sigma
be a normalized premeasure. Then the primitive function in (
A.1
) satisfies the following properties:

(I)

if
t^{\prime}\to t\in(0,2\pi]
from the left, then
\rho(t^{\prime})\to\rho(t)
(continuity from the left),

(II)

if
t^{\prime}\to t\in[0,2\pi)
from the right, then
\rho(t^{\prime})
converges to a limit
\rho(t+)
(limits from the right),

(III)

\rho(2\pi)=0
.

Conversely, every function
\rho
satisfying (I), (II) and (III) corresponds to a normalized premeasure
\sigma
, defined uniquely by the requirement that

(A.2)

\sigma(I_{t}):=\rho(t),\quad I_{t}=[1,e^{it}),\,t\in(0,2\pi).

The lemma has a straightforward proof which we skip. Given
\rho
and the equation (
A.2
), one extends
\sigma
to unions of any type of intervals using the continuity properties (I) and (II) and the requirement (P2). If (III) does not hold for
\rho
, then the corresponding construction will produce a premeasure
\sigma
which is not normalized.

It will be convenient to set a notation for the size of the jump of
\rho
at a point
t
:

(A.3)

J_{\rho}(t):=|\rho(t)-\rho(t+)|=|\rho(t)-\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}t^{\prime}\to t\\
t^{\prime}>t\end{subarray}}\rho(t^{\prime})|,\quad t\in(0,2\pi).

Our definitions ensure that
J_{\sigma}
is large only at a finite number of points.

Lemma A.3
.

For every
\delta>0
, there exist only a finite number of
t\in(0,2\pi)
for which we have
J_{\sigma}(t)>\delta
.

Proof.

Were the lemma not true, then for some
\delta>0
a sequence
\{t_{n}\}_{n}
of distinct numbers would exist which converges to some
t^{*}\in[0,2\pi]
and for which we have
J(t_{n})>\delta
for all
n
. After passing to a subsequence, we may assume that the elements of the sequence
\{t_{n}\}_{n}
are all either strictly larger or strictly smaller than
t^{*}
, say the latter. By the definition of
J_{\rho}
, for each
n
there exists a number
t^{\prime}_{n}
satisfying
t_{n}<t^{\prime}_{n}<t^{*}
for which we have
|\rho(t_{n})-\rho(t^{\prime}_{n})|>\delta
. If we interweave the sequences
\{t_{n}\}_{n}
and
\{t^{\prime}_{n}\}_{n}
into a sequence
\{s_{n}\}_{n}
then
s_{n}\to t
from the left, but
\rho(s_{n})
does not converge, breaking property (I) (were
\{t_{n}\}_{n}
all strictly larger than
t^{*}
, an analogous argument would break property (II) instead).
∎

A smooth function
h
may be integrated against a normalized premeasure by employing the following definition:

(A.4)

\int_{[0,2\pi)}h(e^{it})d\sigma(e^{it}):=-\int_{[0,2\pi)}\Big(\frac{d}{dt}h(e^{it})\Big)\rho_{\sigma}(t)\,dt.

We note that, as a consequence of ?THM?
, the function
\rho_{\sigma}
has only a countable number of discontinuities, and is therefore Riemann integrable. It follows that the right-hand side in (
A.4
) is well-defined in ordinary sense. If
\sigma
is a bona fide finite Borel measure satisfying
\sigma(\mathbb{T})=0
, then both sides of (
A.4
) make sense and are equal. We extend the definition (
A.4
) to not necessarily normalized premeasures in the natural way by decomposing a premeasure
d\sigma
as a sum
(d\sigma-\sigma(\mathbb{T})d\textit{m})+\sigma(\mathbb{T})d\textit{m}
of a normalized premeasure and a constant multiple of the Lebesgue measure. The integral we then define as the sum of the integrals corresponding to
\sigma-\sigma(\mathbb{T})d\textit{m}
and
d\textit{m}
, the second defined in the classical sense.

A.2.
Sequential compactness

If a family of premeasures obeys a certain upper estimate, then the family is sequentially compact in the following weak sense.

Lemma A.4

(
Helly selection principle
)
.

If
h:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a continuous increasing function,
h(0)=0
, and
\{\sigma_{n}\}_{n}
is a sequence of normalized premeasures which obey the upper estimate

\sigma_{n}(I)\leq h(|I|)

for all intervals

\mathbb{T}
, then there exists a subsequence
\{\sigma_{n_{k}}\}_{k}
, a real constant
c
, and a normalized premeasure
\sigma
satisfying
\sigma(I)\leq h(|I|)
for all intervals
I
, such that

(A.5)

\limsup_{k\to\infty}|\rho_{\sigma_{n_{k}}}(t)-\rho_{\sigma}(t)-c|\leq J_{\rho}(t),\quad t\in(0,2\pi).

Moreover, in the above situation, we have

\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{[0,2\pi)}g(e^{it})d\sigma_{n_{k}}(e^{it})=\int_{[0,2\pi)}g(e^{it})d\sigma(e^{it})

for all smooth functions
g:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{C}
.

The principle is stated in a similar form and without proof in
[
15
]
for the particular choice of gauge function

in (

). The normalization assumption is necessary as evidenced by the sequence
\{-n\cdot d\textit{m}\}_{n}
, which obviously satisfies the upper estimate, but for which the sequence
\{\rho_{\sigma_{n}}\}_{n}
converges pointwise to
-\infty
on
(0,2\pi)
as

. The appearance of the constant

in (

) may be justified by considering a sequence
\{\sigma_{n}\}_{n}
for which we have
\lim_{n\to\infty}\rho_{\sigma_{n}}(t)=c\neq 0
for all

2\pi

\displaystyle\rho_{\sigma_{n}}(t)=\begin{cases}t,&t\in[0,1],\\
1,&t\in(1,2\pi-1/n)\\
1-n(t-2\pi+1/n),&t\in[2\pi-1/n,2\pi]\end{cases}

Then it follows readily from (
A.2
) that we have the upper estimate
\sigma_{n}(I)\leq|I|
. However, note that if a subsequence of
\{\rho_{\sigma_{n}}\}_{n}
converges pointwise to a function
\rho
which satisfies (I), then
\rho
must also satisfy
\rho(2\pi)=1
, breaking (III).

Proof of ?THM?
.

By properties (P1) and (P2) of premeasures, and by our hypothesis, we obtain for any interval

-\sigma_{n}(I)=\sigma_{n}(\mathbb{T}\setminus I)\leq h(|\mathbb{T}|).

Since
\sigma_{n}(I)\leq h(|I|)\leq h(|\mathbb{T}|)
also, we obtain the uniform bound
|\rho_{\sigma_{n}}(t)|\leq h(|\mathbb{T}|)
for
t\in[0,2\pi)
. Hence the sets
\{\rho_{\sigma_{n}}(t)\}_{n}
are bounded in
\mathbb{R}
, and so by the usual diagonal process, we may pass to a subsequence and ensure that
\rho_{\sigma_{n}}(r)\to c_{r}
,
n\to\infty
, for every rational
r\in(0,2\pi]
. Here
c_{r}
is a real number of modulus at most
h(|\mathbb{T}|)
.
For
t\in(0,2\pi]
, we define

(A.6)

\widetilde{\rho}(t):=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\to t\\
r<t\end{subarray}}\,c_{r}.

The limit does exist. Indeed, for rationals
r_{1}
,
r_{2}
satisfying
r_{1}<r_{2}<t
and the half-open interval
I_{r_{1},r_{2}}=[e^{ir_{1}},e^{ir_{2}})
between them, we have

c_{r_{2}}-c_{r_{1}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\rho_{\sigma_{n}}(r_{2})-\rho_{\sigma_{n}}(r_{1})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sigma_{n}(I_{r_{1},r_{2}})\leq h(r_{2}-r_{1})

If we let
r_{2}\to t
and
r_{1}\to t
from the left in such a way that

c_{r_{2}}\to\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\to t\\
r<t\end{subarray}}c_{r},\quad c_{r_{1}}\to\liminf_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\to t\\
r<t\end{subarray}}c_{r}

then we obtain from the above inequality, and the hypothesis on
h
, that

\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\to t\\
r<t\end{subarray}}c_{r}-\liminf_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\to t\\
r<t\end{subarray}}c_{r}\leq 0.

Hence (
A.6
) is well-defined.

We claim that
\widetilde{\rho}
satisfies conditions (I) and (II) for being a primitive function of a premeasure which appear in ?THM?
. To verify (I), let
\{t_{m}\}_{m}
be any sequence which converges to
t\in(0,2\pi]
from the left. Take rational
r_{m}
such that
r_{m}<t_{m}
,
r_{m}\to t
, and
c_{r_{m}}>\widetilde{\rho}(t_{m})-1/m
. Then our definitions, and the already established existence of the limit in (
A.6
), imply that

\widetilde{\rho}(t)=\lim_{m\to\infty}c_{r_{m}}\geq\limsup_{m\to\infty}\widetilde{\rho}(t_{m}).

On the other hand, by (
A.6
) there exists a short open interval
I_{\epsilon}
with right endpoint
t
such that
c_{r}>\widetilde{\rho}(t)-\epsilon
for every rational
r\in I_{\epsilon}
. If
t_{m}\in I_{\epsilon}
, then the definition (
A.6
) shows that
\widetilde{\rho}(t_{m})\geq\widetilde{\rho}(t)-\epsilon
. Hence
\liminf_{m\to\infty}\widetilde{\rho}(t_{m})\geq\widetilde{\rho}(t)
, and we have thus verified property (I).

We now show that (II) holds. Assume that the limit does not exist, and so that there exist two sequences
\{t_{m}\}_{n}
and
\{t^{\prime}_{m}\}_{m}
which both converge to
t\in[0,2\pi)
from the right, but such that
\lim_{m\to\infty}\widetilde{\rho}(t_{m})>\lim_{m\to\infty}\widetilde{\rho}(t^{\prime}_{m})+\delta
for some
\delta>0
. By our definition, this corresponds to existence of two sequences
\{r_{m}\}_{m}
,
\{r^{\prime}_{m}\}_{m}
of rational numbers converging to
t
from the right, for which

\lim_{m\to\infty}c_{r_{m}}-c_{r^{\prime}_{m}}>\delta.

We may assume that the sequences satisfy
r^{\prime}_{m}<r_{m}
for all
m
. Note that

\delta<c_{r_{m}}-c_{r^{\prime}_{m}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\rho_{\sigma_{n}}(r_{m})-\rho_{\sigma_{n}}(r^{\prime}_{m})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sigma_{n}(I_{m}),\quad I_{m}=[e^{ir^{\prime}_{m}},e^{ir_{m}}).

Thus

\delta<\lim_{n\to\infty}\sigma_{n}(I_{m})\leq h(r^{\prime}_{m}-r_{m}).

Since
r^{\prime}_{m}-r_{m}\to 0
as
m\to\infty
, we obtain a contradiction to our hypothesis on
h
. It follows that (II) holds.

Let us now show that

(A.7)

\limsup_{n\to\infty}|p_{\sigma_{n}}(t)-\widetilde{\rho}(t)|\leq J_{\rho}(t),\quad t\in(0,2\pi).

Fix
\epsilon>0
and take rational
r
for which we have
r<t
,
|t-r|<\epsilon
and
|c_{r}-\widetilde{\rho}(t)|<\epsilon
. Then

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\,\rho_{\sigma_{n}}(t)-\widetilde{\rho}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\,\rho_{\sigma_{n}}(t)-c_{r}+\epsilon

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\,\rho_{\sigma_{n}}(t)-\rho_{\sigma_{n}}(r)+\epsilon

\displaystyle\leq h(\epsilon)+\epsilon.

Let
\epsilon\to 0
to conclude that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\rho_{\sigma_{n}}(t)\leq\widetilde{\rho}(t),\quad t\in(0,2\pi).

Analogously, we may take
r_{+}>t
,
|t-r_{+}|<\epsilon
,
|\rho(t+)-c_{r_{+}}|<\epsilon
, and estimate

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\,\widetilde{\rho}(t+)-\rho_{\sigma_{n}}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\,c_{r_{+}}-\rho_{\sigma_{n}}(t)+\epsilon

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\,\rho_{\sigma_{n}}(r_{+})-\rho_{\sigma_{n}}(t)+\epsilon

\displaystyle\leq h(\epsilon)+\epsilon

which shows that

\widetilde{\rho}(t+)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\rho_{\sigma_{n}}(t),\quad t\in(0,2\pi).

Combining the two inequalities, we arrive at

\widetilde{\rho}(t+)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\rho_{\sigma_{n}}(t)\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\rho_{\sigma_{n}}(t)\leq\widetilde{\rho}(t),\quad t\in(0,2\pi)

which implies (
A.7
).

Setting
c=\widetilde{\rho}(2\pi)
and

\rho(t)=\widetilde{\rho}(t)-c,\quad t\in(0,2\pi]

we force condition (III), without breaking
(I)
and
(II)
, and so we obtain a function
\rho=\rho_{\sigma}
corresponding to a premeasure
\sigma
as in ?THM?
. We deduce from (
A.7
) that (
A.5
) now holds, and moreover, at any point
t\in(0,2\pi)
at which
J_{\rho}(t)=0
, we have that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\rho_{\sigma_{n}}(t)=\rho_{\sigma}(t)+c.

This implies the corresponding integral convergence. Indeed, let
g
be a smooth function on
\mathbb{T}
. By the definition in (
A.4
), uniform boundedness of
\rho_{\sigma_{n}}
and the fact that
J_{\rho}(t)
is non-zero only at a countable number of points (recall ?THM?
), we obtain from the Dominated Convergence Theorem that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{[0,2\pi)}g(e^{it})d\sigma_{n}(e^{it})

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}-\int_{[0,2\pi)}\Big(\frac{d}{dt}g(e^{it})\Big)\rho_{\sigma_{n}}(t)\,dt.

\displaystyle=-\int_{[0,2\pi)}\Big(\frac{d}{dt}g(e^{it})\Big)\rho_{\sigma}(t)\,dt-c\int_{[0,2\pi)}\frac{d}{dt}g(e^{it})\,dt

\displaystyle=-\int_{[0,2\pi)}\Big(\frac{d}{dt}g(e^{it})\Big)\rho_{\sigma}(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,2\pi)}g(e^{it})d\sigma(e^{it}).

We used that
g(e^{it})
is periodic between the second and third lines, which makes the second integral on the second line vanish.

Finally, we verify that
\sigma(I)\leq h(|I|)
for all intervals. By property (I) and (II) of premeasures, and continuity of
h
, it will suffice to show the bound for half-open intervals
I=[e^{it},e^{it^{\prime}})
with endpoints
e^{it}
,
e^{it^{\prime}}
on which
\sigma
carries no mass. Then the jumps
J_{\rho}(t)
,
J_{\rho}(t^{\prime})
are equal to
0
, and so from (
A.5
) we obtain

\sigma(I)=\rho(t)-\rho(t^{\prime})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\rho_{\sigma_{n}}(t)-\rho_{\sigma_{n}}(t^{\prime})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sigma_{n}(I)\leq h(|I|).

∎

A.3.
Poisson integral estimates

The Poisson integral bound (
3.13
) which we use in Section
3
holds also for normalized premeasures. Namely, if we have the upper estimate
\sigma(I)\leq h(|I|)
, then setting
g(e^{it})=P_{z}(e^{it})
to be the Poisson kernel,

(A.8)

P_{z}(e^{it}):=\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{|e^{it}-z|^{2}}=\operatorname{Re}\Big(\frac{e^{it}+z}{e^{it}-z}\Big),

the same proof as in the case of
\sigma
being an ordinary Borel measure (see
[
10
, p. 297]
) gives us a constant
C=C(h)>0
for which it holds that

(A.9)

\int_{[0,2\pi)}P_{z}(e^{it})d\sigma(e^{it})\leq\frac{Ch(1-|z|)}{1-|z|}.

See also
[
11
, Exercise 4.9.1]
. A similar estimate holds of course also for non-normalized premeasures, but then the constant
C
depends on the quantity
\sigma(\mathbb{T})
also:
C=C(h)\exp(\sigma(\mathbb{T}))
.

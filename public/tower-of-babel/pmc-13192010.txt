Comparison of the efficacy of obinutuzumab versus rituximab combined with tacrolimus in the treatment of refractory membranous nephropathy
Authors: Yulong Zhang, Mengnan Guo, Qi Bian, Shuangxi Li, Jing Sun, Zhenyu Qin, Jing Xu, Rui Dong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPrimary membranous nephropathy (pMN) is characterized by a prolonged course, frequent relapses, and variable treatment responses. While obinutuzumab (OBI) demonstrates promising efficacy in B-cell depletion, its direct comparison with the combination regimen of rituximab (RTX) plus tacrolimus (TAC) remains limited. This study aimed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of OBI monotherapy versus RTX + TAC in patients with refractory pMN.MethodsWe retrospectively enrolled 28 patients with biopsy-proven pMN from a single center, assigned to RTX + TAC (n = 13) or OBI (n = 15) groups according to treatment regimen. Clinical remission, immunological remission (IR), and adverse events were assessed over 24 months of follow-up.ResultsNo significant differences were observed in serum creatinine, estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), serum albumin, urine protein excretion, or anti-phospholipase A2 receptor (PLA2R) antibody titers between the two study groups. In contrast, disease duration differed substantially between groups: the OBI group exhibited a significantly longer median disease duration (87.7 (29, 168) months) compared with the RTX + TAC group (14 (7, 36) months). At the end of follow-up, the cumulative clinical remission rate (achieving complete remission or partial remission) was 89.1% among 11 patients included in the RTX + TAC group, and 92.9% among 14 patients in the OBI group. For IR, the cumulative IR rate was 60.0% among 10 patients in the RTX + TAC group and 90.9% among 11 patients in the OBI group. Kaplan–Meier survival analysis further demonstrated a statistically significant difference in cumulative IR rates between the two groups (P = 0.03).ConclusionOBI monotherapy achieved comparable 24-month clinical remission and safety to RTX + TAC in pMN, but with superior IR rates. These findings support B-cell depletion as a central therapeutic strategy, though long-term clinical benefits require further study.Keywords: Refractory membranous nephropathy, Obinutuzumab, Rituximab, Adverse events

Introduction

Membranous nephropathy (MN) is a leading cause of nephrotic syndrome in adults, with heterogeneous clinical outcomes. While spontaneous remission occurs in approximately 30% of cases, over 50% of patients with persistent nephrotic-range proteinuria progress to end-stage renal disease within 15 years. Primary MN accounts for approximately 75% of cases, and the discovery of anti-phospholipase A2 receptor (PLA2R) antibodies in 2009 has revolutionized its diagnosis and monitoring [1]. Approximately 70% of primary MN cases are PLA2R-associated, with antibody titers correlating strongly with disease activity, prognosis, and treatment response. The high specificity of anti-PLA2R testing (> 99%) enables non-invasive diagnosis and quantitative monitoring of immunological activity [2, 3].

As an antibody-mediated autoimmune disease, B-cell depletion has emerged as a central therapeutic strategy in MN. Rituximab (RTX), a chimeric type I anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody, achieves clinical remission in 60%–80% of patients with primary MN. However, treatment resistance or relapse occurs in 20%–40% of cases, prompting investigation of alternative anti-CD20 agents or combination regimens [4, 5]. The combination of RTX with calcineurin inhibitors (CNIs) such as tacrolimus (TAC) is commonly used in clinical practice, based on the rationale that TAC-mediated inhibition of T-cell activation might complement B-cell depletion by reducing T-cell help for antibody production. However, this combination strategy lacks robust evidence from randomized trials, and concerns regarding additive toxicities and costs have limited its widespread adoption [6].

Obinutuzumab (OBI) is a humanized type II anti-CD20 antibody that induces enhanced direct cell death and antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity compared with type I antibodies. Preclinical studies suggest that OBI produces more sustained B-cell depletion and may overcome limitations of rituximab related to CD20 internalization and variable effector function. In membranous nephropathy, OBI has demonstrated promising efficacy in preliminary studies, with high rates of immunological remission [7–9]. Nevertheless, direct comparisons between OBI monotherapy and RTX-based combination regimens are lacking, and the relative contributions of B-cell depletion depth versus breadth of immunosuppression remain unclear.

Notably, immunological remission often precedes clinical remission by months to years in MN, and the relationship between these endpoints and long-term renal outcomes continues to evolve [10]. Whether more potent B-cell depletion translates into superior clinical outcomes, or whether additional T-cell suppression provides meaningful benefit, remains uncertain.

The aim of this study was to compare the efficacy and safety of OBI monotherapy versus RTX combined with tacrolimus (RTX + TAC) in patients with membranous nephropathy requiring immunosuppressive therapy. We hypothesized that OBI’s enhanced B-cell depletion would result in superior immunological control, and sought to evaluate whether this translates into differential clinical outcomes compared with a combination regimen targeting both B and T cells.

Methods

Study subjects

A total of 28 patients with refractory MN treated with OBI and RTX combined with TAC from December 2022 to August 2024 in the Department of Nephrology of the First Affiliated Hospital of Naval Medical University were retrospectively collected.

In this study, refractory membranous nephropathy was defined as failure to achieve partial clinical remission or IR after at least 6 months of standardized treatment with glucocorticoids and/or cytotoxic agents, immunosuppressants, and/or biological agents.

Screening criteria for study subjects: Inclusion criteria: (1) Age ≥ 18 years at the beginning of treatment (including the threshold), gender not limited; (2) Compliance with any of the following diagnostic criteria: 1). Diagnosis of pMN confirmed by renal biopsy pathology. 2). Patients with nephrotic syndrome without renal biopsy found to be positive for serum anti-PLA2R antibody, supporting the diagnosis of pMN after exclusion of secondary diseases; (3) Meet any of the following treatment response characteristics: 1). Patients who failed to achieve partial clinical remission or IR after at least 6 months of standardized treatment with glucocorticoids and/or cytotoxic agents, immunosuppressants, and/or biological agents. 2). Disease relapse after clinical remission with prior therapy. Exclusion criteria: (1) Secondary membranous nephropathy (sMN) or combination with other renal diseases confirmed by renal biopsy; (2) Presence of uncontrolled active infections or suspected infections requiring anti-infective treatment; (3) Comorbid diabetes mellitus (type 1 or type 2): Patients with diabetes mellitus were excluded to reduce confounding factors associated with diabetic kidney disease, which may interfere with the accurate evaluation of renal outcomes in patients with refractory membranous nephropathy; (4) The progression of renal disease to the point where renal replacement therapy is required; (5) Those who have not received systemic treatment for the initial diagnosis of pMN; (6) Presence of other comorbidities or special circumstances affecting the safety of the study, as assessed by the investigator.

Treatment regimen

The first dose of OBI was administered using a staged infusion strategy (100 mg, 400 mg, 500 mg, total dose 1 g) to reduce the risk of first-dose reaction (FDR). All patients were pretreated with intravenous methylprednisolone 80 mg combined with intramuscular promethazine 25 mg prior to the infusion. The basal dosing interval was 14 days between the first and the second dose. Subsequent courses (e.g., the third dose) were dynamically adjusted according to the patient’s peripheral blood CD20⁺ B-cell reconstitution level. The specific number of doses should be determined by a combination of guideline recommendations, disease activity, and the patient’s informed consent. Dosage of RTX: The standard dosage of RTX was 1 g administered via intravenous infusion at a 2-week interval. Subsequent doses and administration timings were adjusted based on the patients’ clinical conditions as individualized adjustments. The pretreatment regimen was consistent with that of OBI, and the infusion intervals were likewise dynamically regulated based on the results of CD20⁺ B-cell monitoring, with dosage decision-making following guideline specifications, clinical characteristics, and the principle of shared decision-making between doctor and patient. TAC, on the other hand, was dually regulated by therapeutic drug concentration monitoring (TDM). The target trough concentration was maintained at 5–8 ng/mL (tested every 2 weeks at the beginning of treatment and monthly after stabilization), and the dose was adjusted according to eGFR.

Outcome measures

The study period for each patient was defined as the duration from the first clinical visit to the last follow-up within 24 months after baseline. Compare and analyze 24-hour urine protein quantification, serum albumin, blood creatinine, CD19+ B-cell count, serum anti-PLA2R antibody titer, urinary α1-microglobulin, urinary β2-microglobulin, and the occurrence of adverse events.

Clinical remission and IR were used as study endpoints, with grading criteria developed in accordance with the KDIGO clinical practice guidelines. Complete remission (CR) was defined as 24-hour urine protein quantification (24 h-UP) < 0.3 g/d or urine protein-to-creatinine ratio (uPCR) < 0.3 g/g; partial remission (PR) required 24-hour urinary protein 0.3–3.5 g/d with > 50% reduction from baseline and serum albumin > 30 g/L. Patients who did not meet the above CR or PR thresholds were classified as having no remission. IR in this study was defined as serum anti-PLA2R antibody seroconversion (enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay [ELISA]: serum anti-PLA2R antibody titer < 14 RU/mL or indirect immunofluorescence assay [IFA]: antibody titer < 2 RU/mL).

Statistical analysis

SPSS 25.0 was used for data management and statistical analysis in this study, and GraphPad Prism 8.0.0 software was used to complete the visualization and graphing. Variables which conformed to a standard distribution were expressed as the mean ± standard deviation (x ± s), whilst variables which did not conform to a normal distribution were described by the median (M) and the interquartile range (IQR, P25–P75). Categorical variables are presented as frequencies (component ratios). Time-dependent characteristics of clinical remission and IR rates were described by Kaplan-Meier survival analysis. The Wilcoxon rank-sum test was used for comparison between two independent samples. All statistical tests were two-sided, with P < 0.05 considered to be a statistically significant difference, and it is considered to be a statistically highly significant difference when P < 0.01.

Results

Baseline characteristics

This study included a total of 28 cases: 13 cases were in the RTX + TAC group, with a mean age of 55.4 ± 16.4 years and including 9 males and 4 females. 15 cases were in the OBI group, with a mean age of 56.4 ± 14.1 years and including 10 males and 5 females. 2 patients in the RTX + TAC group and 2 patients in the OBI group had relapsed following prior treatment. There was no significant difference between the two groups in terms of gender, age of disease onset, and the indicators of urinary protein, blood albumin, and blood creatinine before treatment (P > 0.05). However, there was a significant difference in disease duration before baseline (P < 0.01). Patients in the OBI group had a longer disease duration, which may partially account for the clinical decision to switch to OBI therapy. Treatment exposure

In the OBI group, six patients received a total OBI dose of 1 g, and 7 patients received a total dose of 2 g. Among the 2-g dose subgroup, 5 patients were dosed at a 14-day interval, 1 patient at a 4-month interval, and 1 patient at a 6‑month interval. Additionally, two patients received a second OBI dose 14 days after initial treatment, followed by a third dose at 5 months and 14 months, respectively, resulting in a total cumulative dose of 3 g.

After baseline, the cumulative dose of RTX in the RTX + TAC group was 2000 (1100, 3500) mg, while the cumulative dose of OBI in the OBI group was 2000 (1000, 2000) mg; no significant difference was detected between the two groups using the Mann-Whitney U test (P = 0.22), which may indicate that the difference in therapeutic efficacy between the two groups in the present study was not attributable to the difference in anti-CD20 antibody exposure.

Efficacy outcomes

Clinical remission

In the RTX + TAC group, 2 patients had already achieved partial remission after previous treatment. Accordingly, 11 patients were included in the analysis of clinical remission. In the OBI group, 1 patient had achieved clinical remission after previous treatment, therefore, 14 patients were included in the analysis. Patients with established RP or CR continued treatment for the prevention of subsequent disease relapse. After 6 months, the cumulative clinical remission rate (achieved PR or CR) was 64.3% in the OBI group, compared with 56.4% in the RTX + TAC group. At the 13-month follow-up, the cumulative clinical remission rate (achieved PR or CR) was 89.1% in the RTX + TAC group and 92.9% in the OBI group. The Kaplan–Meier curve showed no statistically significant difference in the cumulative clinical remission rates between the two groups (P = 0.84). Immunological remission

In the OBI group, three patients had achieved IR during previous treatment, and 1 patient was excluded due to incomplete data. Accordingly, 11 patients were included in the IR analysis. In the RTX + TAC group, 3 patients had achieved IR during previous treatment; thus, 10 patients were included in the analysis. Patients who had achieved IR continued treatment to maintain IR and prevent relapse.

At 12 months of follow-up, the cumulative IR rate was 63.6% in the OBI group, in contrast to 20.0% in the combination group. After 24 months of follow-up, the cumulative IR rate was 90.9% in the OBI group, compared with 60.0% in the RTX + TAC group. Safety outcomes

Common adverse events in both groups were infections. The combination group also exhibited adverse events such as infusion reactions and gastric malignancy compared with the OBI group, while the OBI group had adverse events such as hepatic impairment. There was no statistically significant difference in the overall incidence of adverse events (P > 0.05). The occurrence of adverse events is shown in Table 2.

Discussion

In the present study, we compared the efficacy and safety of OBI monotherapy versus RTX + TAC in patients with refractory MN. After 24 months of follow-up, cumulative clinical remission rates were similar between the two groups, while the cumulative IR rate was significantly higher in the OBI group. The incidence of adverse events was comparable across the two regimens. These findings suggest that OBI monotherapy achieves clinical efficacy and safety comparable to the RTX + TAC, with more favorable control over the underlying autoimmune process of MN. However, given that 24 months of follow-up may be insufficient to fully capture long-term clinical outcomes, it remains unclear whether this immunological advantage will translate into meaningful clinical benefits. The observed immunological-clinical dissociation offers important insights into the pathogenesis and treatment response of refractory MN, and supports the need for further evaluation of the long-term clinical value of OBI monotherapy.

As a humanized type II anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody, OBI exerts its therapeutic effects in MN through distinct pharmacological mechanisms. It binds specifically to CD20 on mature B cells and plasmablasts, and induces potent and sustained B-cell depletion via direct apoptosis, antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC), and complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC) [7–9]. Compared with conventional anti-CD20 antibodies, OBI tends to produce stronger and longer-lasting B-cell depletion, which may effectively suppress B-cell reconstitution and reduce the persistent production of pathogenic autoantibodies such as anti-PLA2R antibodies [11]. The significantly higher IR rate in the OBI group may be closely associated with this deep and long-lasting B-cell inhibition, which further supports the notion that abnormal B-cell activation plays a central role in the pathogenesis and progression of MN.

Notably, OBI monotherapy—targeting primarily B cells—achieved clinical efficacy similar to that of the RTX + TAC combination regimen. This finding provides strong support for the central role of B cells in MN pathogenesis, as it suggests that targeted B-cell depletion alone may yield therapeutic effects comparable to those of a combination regimen involving additional immunosuppressive agents. B cells not only produce pathogenic autoantibodies but also act as antigen-presenting cells to regulate the differentiation and function of T-cell subsets. B-cell depletion by OBI may indirectly reduce the release of proinflammatory cytokines and alleviate inflammatory infiltration in the kidney [12]. Meanwhile, the reduction in autoantibodies may attenuate the activation of the classical complement pathway, decrease C5b-9 deposition in the glomerular basement membrane, and mitigate podocyte injury [13]. Furthermore, B-cell inhibition may downregulate fibrosis-related factors such as TGF-β and slow the progression of renal interstitial fibrosis [14]. These multi-pathways and multi-levels effects may collectively contribute to the favorable clinical efficacy observed with OBI monotherapy.

In this study, the OBI group achieved a significantly higher IR rate, yet the two groups showed no significant difference in clinical remission at 24 months, reflecting a typical pattern of immunological-clinical dissociation. First, clinical remission in MN usually lags considerably behind IR, which may be related to the slow repair of glomerular basement membrane injury and podocyte functional recovery. Even after the immune attack is blocked, the clearance of existing immune complex deposits and structural damage may still require an extended period of time. For patients with refractory MN specifically, long-standing disease duration often leads to more severe glomerular basement membrane fibrosis and irreversible podocyte injury, which may further prolong the lag phase of clinical remission—this could be a key reason why the immunological advantage of OBI has not translated into significant clinical benefits within 24 months. Second, patients with higher baseline proteinuria and more severe pathological damage may tend to experience slower clinical remission. In addition, non-immunological factors, including podocyte repair capacity, renal microcirculation, and individual metabolic differences, may independently influence the speed and degree of clinical remission [14–16]. Therefore, the 24-month follow-up period may not be long enough to fully reflect the potential long-term clinical benefits associated with the immunological advantages of OBI.

The lower cumulative IR rate in the RTX + TAC group, despite dual immunosuppression, appears paradoxical but may be explained by several mechanisms. First, TAC-mediated inhibition of T cells and NK cells may impair the Fc-dependent effector functions (ADCC/CDC) required for optimal RTX-induced B-cell depletion [17]. Second, TAC’s reversible immunosuppression may allow earlier B-cell reconstitution upon dose reduction or withdrawal, whereas OBI’s sustained B-cell depletion provides more durable suppression of the autoreactive clone [18–20]. Additionally, concomitant TAC use may influence the inflammatory microenvironment or immune cell trafficking in ways that indirectly affect RTX efficacy, though the precise mechanisms remain speculative. These findings suggest that in MN, ‘more immunosuppression’ does not necessarily equate to ‘better immunological control’ and the depth and durability of B-cell targeting may be more important than the breadth of immunosuppressive coverage.

No significant difference was observed in the incidence of adverse reactions between the two groups, indicating that OBI monotherapy offers comparable safety while avoiding the potential additive risks associated with combination therapy [6]. These results collectively support the view that targeted B-cell depletion mediated by OBI may represent a key therapeutic strategy in MN.

This study has several limitations. First, it was a single-center retrospective analysis with a small sample size and lacked randomization, which may have introduced selection bias. Second, baseline imbalances between the two groups may have acted as confounding factors, affecting cohort comparability. Third, inconsistent denominators in efficacy analyses might compromise the robustness of the results. Finally, the limited 24-month follow-up duration restricts the assessment of long-term outcomes and whether OBI’s immunological advantage will translate into improved clinical outcomes. Additionally, while the development of anti-rituximab antibodies (ARAs) has been reported in 23%–43% of patients with refractory autoimmune diseases and may potentially impact RTX efficacy [4, 21], this represents a hypothetical mechanism rather than a finding of the present study and requires confirmation in dedicated studies. Thus, prospective randomized controlled trials with longer follow-up are needed to validate these findings. Nevertheless, based on its unique mechanism and our clinical observations, OBI shows promising therapeutic value in MN, with an incidence of treatment-related adverse events comparable to that of the RTX + TAC group. These results suggest that OBI may serve as a potential alternative for patients with inadequate response to prior immunosuppressive therapy, including RTX, particularly those with complex clinical conditions and prolonged disease courses. Importantly, our findings do not support the use of OBI as a replacement for established standard therapies outside of controlled trials; further high-quality prospective randomized studies are warranted to determine whether OBI’s immunological advantages translate into improved long-term clinical outcomes and to define its optimal positioning relative to existing standard therapies.

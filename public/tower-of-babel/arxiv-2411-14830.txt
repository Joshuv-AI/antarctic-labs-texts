Introduction

The purpose of this paper is to prove some
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})\times L^{q}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow L^{r}(\mathbb{R})
estimates for bilinear operators of fractional integral type. The classical example of these operators is the bilinear fractional integral

(1.1)

\displaystyle H_{\alpha}(f,g)(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x-t)g(x+t)\,\frac{\textrm{d}t}{|t|^{n-\alpha}}

with
0<\alpha<n
, and the
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
boundedness of
H_{\alpha}
can be founded in Kenig and Stein
[
21
]
, Grafakos and Kalton
[
14
]
, and also Grafakos
[
12
]
, where
(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q},\frac{1}{r})
lies in the open convex hull of the pentagon with vertices
(\frac{\alpha}{n},0,0)
,
(1,0,1-\frac{\alpha}{n})
,
(0,\frac{\alpha}{n},0)
,
(0,1,1-\frac{\alpha}{n})
and
(1,1,\frac{2n-\alpha}{n})
. Furthermore, they also showed that a restricted weak type estimate holds at each vertex of the pentagon. On the other hand, by homogeneity, we note that
H_{\alpha}(f,g)
maps
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
only when

\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}-\frac{\alpha}{n}.

More precisely, in the present paper, we focus our attention on the bilinear fractional integral
H_{\alpha,\gamma}
along a curve
(t,\gamma(t))
, which has the form

(1.2)

\displaystyle H_{\alpha,\gamma}(f,g)(x):=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(x-t)g(x-\gamma(t))\,\frac{\textrm{d}t}{t^{1-\alpha}},

where
0<\alpha<1
. The first author of this paper and Liu considered the
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})\times L^{q}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow L^{r}(\mathbb{R})
estimates for
H_{\alpha,\gamma}
under the case of
\gamma(t):=t^{\beta}
in
[
26
]
. Then, it is natural to characterize the indices
(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q},\frac{1}{r})
for which
H_{\alpha,\gamma}
maps
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})\times L^{q}(\mathbb{R})
to
L^{r}(\mathbb{R})
for more general curves. We now state the needed conditions on the curve
(t,\gamma(t))
and some related definitions.

Hypothesis on curves (H.).
We assume that
\gamma\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
is monotonic on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\gamma(t)=\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\gamma^{\prime}(0)=0
, and satisfy
\omega_{\infty,1}\geq\omega_{0,2}
,
\omega_{0,1}>1
.
Furthermore, there exist positive constants
C^{(1)}_{1}
,
C^{(2)}_{1}
and

such that

(1.3)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{t\gamma^{\prime}(t)}{\gamma(t)}\right|\leq C^{(1)}_{2}~~~\textrm{and}~~~\left|\frac{t^{j}\gamma^{(j)}(t)}{\gamma(t)}\right|\geq C^{(j)}_{1}~~\textrm{with}~~j=1,2.

In particular, we note that
(H.)
is essentially applicable to a polynomial with no linear term and constant term. Here and hereafter, we define

\displaystyle\omega_{0,1}:=\liminf\limits_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}{\frac{\ln{|\gamma(t)|}}{\ln t}},~~~~~

\displaystyle\omega_{0,2}:=\limsup\limits_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}{\frac{\ln{|\gamma(t)|}}{\ln t}},

\displaystyle\omega_{\infty,1}:=\liminf\limits_{t\rightarrow{\infty}}{\frac{\ln{|\gamma(t)|}}{\ln t}},~~~~~

\displaystyle\omega_{\infty,2}:=\limsup\limits_{t\rightarrow{\infty}}{\frac{\ln{|\gamma(t)|}}{\ln t}}.

Next, we will state two main results of this paper. The first one is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
\gamma
satisfies
(H.)

\frac{\omega_{\infty,2}-\omega_{\infty,1}}{\omega_{\infty,2}-1}<\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}<\frac{\omega_{\infty,2}}{\omega_{\infty,2}-\omega_{\infty,1}+1}

\left\|H_{\alpha,\gamma}(f,g)\right\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\|g\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R})}

holds for all
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
and
g\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R})
, where
(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q},\frac{1}{r})
lies in the open convex hull of the following points:

\displaystyle A\left(\alpha,0,0\right),

\displaystyle B\left(1,0,1-\alpha\right),

\displaystyle C_{1}\left(0,\frac{\alpha}{\omega_{\infty,1}},0\right),

\displaystyle C_{2}\left(0,\frac{\alpha}{\omega_{0,2}},0\right),

\displaystyle D_{1}\left(0,1,1-\frac{\alpha}{\omega_{\infty,1}}\right),

\displaystyle D_{2}\left(0,1,1-\frac{\alpha}{\omega_{0,2}}\right),

\displaystyle E\left(1,\frac{\omega_{\infty,2}-\alpha}{2\omega_{\infty,2}-\omega_{\infty,1}},\frac{2(\omega_{\infty,2}-\alpha)}{2\omega_{\infty,2}-\omega_{\infty,1}}\right),

\displaystyle F\left(\frac{\omega_{\infty,2}-\alpha}{2\omega_{\infty,2}-\omega_{\infty,1}},1,\frac{2(\omega_{\infty,2}-\alpha)}{2\omega_{\infty,2}-\omega_{\infty,1}}\right).

The first motivation of our problem is the bilinear Hilbert transform
H_{\gamma}(f,g)
along a curve
(t,\gamma(t))
, which is defined by

H_{\gamma}(f,g)(x):=\mathrm{p.\,v.}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x-t)g(x-\gamma(t))\,\frac{\textrm{d}t}{t}.

If
\gamma(t):=-t
, then this operator turns to be the standard bilinear Hilbert transform. As a landmark work, Lacey and Thiele
[
22
,
23
]
set up its
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})\times L^{q}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow L^{r}(\mathbb{R})
boundedness for all
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{r}
,
p,q>1
, and
r>\frac{2}{3}
. This boundedness confirms a conjecture raised by Calderon. The restriction
r>\frac{2}{3}
follows from the boundedness of a model operator they used. So far, to extend the region to
\frac{1}{2}<r\leq\frac{2}{3}
is still a challenging question. Related works can be found in
[
15
,
31
,
24
]
and references therein.

On the other hand, the
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})\times L^{q}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow L^{r}(\mathbb{R})
boundedness of
H_{\gamma}(f,g)
with some general curves
\gamma
are of great interest to us. We start with a special case
\gamma(t):=t^{d}
, where
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
d>1
, Li
[
30
]
obtained the
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
boundedness for this operator. For
\gamma(t):=\textrm{P}(t)
, a polynomial with no linear term and constant term. Li and Xiao
[
32
]
set up the
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})\times L^{q}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow L^{r}(\mathbb{R})
boundedness of
H_{\gamma}(f,g)
with
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{r}
,
p>1
,
q>1
and
r>\frac{d-1}{d}
. At the same time, they showed that

is sharp up to the end point. Moreover, by replacing
\gamma
with a homogeneous curve

, where
d\in\mathbb{N}
and

, they also showed that the range of

can be extended to

. Lie
[

considered a curve class

and obtained the

for

, and it was extended to the

in
[
34
]
. More recently, Guo and Xiao
[
17
]
also introduced a set

for

[
27

obtained the

with

for some curves that are much easier to be

and
\textbf{F}(-1,1)

[

,
6
,

H_{\alpha,\gamma,j}(f\chi_{J_{k}},g\chi_{J_{k+d}})(x)=0
when
|d|>C
for every fixed
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Indeed, we may take
C:=\max\{-2+\frac{C^{(1)}_{2}}{2},1+2^{{j_{0}}+2}\}
. Therefore, by (
3.6
), (
2.3
) and (
1.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|H_{\alpha,\gamma,j}(f\chi_{J_{k}},g\chi_{J_{k+d}})\right\|_{L^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\frac{|\gamma(2^{j})|}{|\gamma^{\prime}(2^{j-1})|}\left\|f\chi_{J_{k}}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\left\|g\chi_{J_{k+d}}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim 2^{j}\left\|f\chi_{J_{k}}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\left\|g\chi_{J_{k+d}}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}.

Furthermore, as in (
3.5
), we also assert that

(3.7)

\displaystyle\left\|H_{\alpha,\gamma,j}(f,g)\right\|_{L^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim 2^{j}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\|g\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}

holds for all
j>j_{0}+2
.

Putting things together, for the non-critical case
j\neq j_{0},j_{0}+1,j_{0}+2
, we have established the
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\times L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow L^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})
estimate for
H_{\alpha,\gamma,j}
. The remaining
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})\times L^{q}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow L^{r}(\mathbb{R})
estimates follow by interpolation with the following three trivial estimates:
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\times L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
,
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\times L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\times L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Therefore, we finish the proof of Proposition
3.1
.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

(Critical cases
j=j_{0},j_{0}+1,j_{0}+2
) Let
\gamma
be the same as in Proposition
3.1
. Then, there exists a positive constant
C
, such that

\left\|H_{\alpha,\gamma,j}(f,g)\right\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\|g\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R})}

for all
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
and
g\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R})
, where
p,q,r
satisfy
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{r}
,
p,q\in[1,\infty]
and
(p,q)\neq(1,1)
.

Proof.

We only consider the case
j=j_{0}
, the proofs of the cases
j=j_{0}+1,j_{0}+2
are similar to the case
j=j_{0}
and we omit the details. Since
j_{0}
is a negative integer, we can bound
H_{\alpha,\gamma,j_{0}}(f,g)(x)
by

\int_{0}^{1}f(x-t)g(x-\gamma(t))\,\textrm{d}t.

Moreover, we can restrict
x
into the interval
[0,1]
. Indeed, let
f_{N}(x):=f(x+N)
and
g_{N}(x):=g(x+N)
if we have the restriction of
x
, then
\|H_{\alpha,\gamma,j_{0}}(f,g)\|^{r}_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R})}
can be dominated by a constant times

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{N\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{0}^{1}f_{N}(x-t)g_{N}(x-\gamma(t))\,\textrm{d}t\right)^{r}\,\textrm{d}x\lesssim\sum_{N\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\|f_{N}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}^{r}\left\|g_{N}\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R})}^{r}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}^{r}\|g\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R})}^{r},

where the last inequality follows from Hölder’s inequality.

Therefore, it suffices to show that

(3.8)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{0}^{1}f(x-t)g(x-\gamma(t))\,\textrm{d}t\right)^{r}\,\textrm{d}x\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}^{r}\|g\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R})}^{r}

for all
p,q,r
satisfying
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{r}
,
p,q\in[1,\infty]
and
(p,q)\neq(1,1)
.

When
r\geq 1
, it is easy to see that (
3.8
) follows from Minkowski’s integral inequality and Hölder’s inequality. When
\frac{1}{2}<r<1
, we need some more complicated calculations. Indeed, let
\psi
be a smooth function supported on
\{t\in\mathbb{R}:\ \frac{1}{2}\leq|t|\leq 2\}
with the property that
0\leq\psi(t)\leq 1
and
\Sigma_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\psi_{k}(t)=1
, where
\psi_{k}(t):=\psi(2^{-k}t)
. In order to obtain (
3.8
) with
\frac{1}{2}<r<1
, it suffices to establish the following estimate: there exists a positive constant
\varepsilon
such that

(3.9)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left[\int_{0}^{1}f(x-t)g(x-\gamma(t))\psi\left(2^{k}(t-t_{0})\right)\,\textrm{d}t\right]^{r}\,\textrm{d}x\lesssim 2^{-\varepsilon k}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}^{r}\|g\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R})}^{r}

for all
k>0
, where
p,q,r
satisfy
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{r}
,
p,q\in[1,\infty]
and
\frac{1}{2}<r<1
.

We establish (
3.9
) by considering the following two cases:

\bullet

The case of
2^{-k+2}\leq t_{0}
. Notice that
{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot 2^{-k}\leq|t-t_{0}|\leq 2\cdot 2^{-k}
, it implies
|t-t_{0}|\leq{\frac{1}{2}}t_{0}
and we can then rewrite the left hand side of (
3.9
) as

(3.10)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left[\int_{-\frac{t_{0}}{2}}^{\frac{t_{0}}{2}}f_{t_{0}}(x-t)g_{t_{0}}\left(x-\Upsilon_{t_{0}}(t)\right)\psi\left(2^{k}t\right)\,\textrm{d}t\right]^{r}\,\textrm{d}x,

where

f_{t_{0}}(x):=f(x-t_{0}),~~g_{t_{0}}(x):=g(x-\gamma(t_{0}))~~\textrm{and}~~\Upsilon_{t_{0}}(t):=\gamma(t+t_{0})-\gamma(t_{0}).

On the other hand, we obviously have
|t|\leq 2^{-k+1}
. Moreover, by Lagrange’s mean value theorem,
|\Upsilon_{t_{0}}(t)|\leq|\gamma^{\prime}(\zeta)||t|\leq 2|\gamma^{\prime}(2)|2^{-k}
, where
\zeta\in({\frac{t_{0}}{2}},{\frac{3}{2}}t_{0})\subset(0,2)
. Then, if we set

\displaystyle\Omega_{N}:=\left(2N|\gamma^{\prime}(2)|2^{-k},2(N+1)|\gamma^{\prime}(2)|2^{-k}\right]

with
N=0,1,\ldots,\lceil\frac{2^{k}}{2|\gamma^{\prime}(2)|}\rceil
, which trivially leads to
x-t,x-\Upsilon_{t_{0}}(t)\in\Omega_{N-1}\cup\Omega_{N}\cup\Omega_{N+1}
for all
x\in\Omega_{N}
.

Therefore, to obtain (
3.9
), it is enough to show that, there exists a positive constant
\varepsilon
such that

(3.11)

\displaystyle\left\|\Lambda_{N}(f_{t_{0},N},g_{t_{0},N})\right\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R})}^{r}\lesssim 2^{-\varepsilon k}\|f_{t_{0},N}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}^{r}\|g_{t_{0},N}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R})}^{r}

for all
k>0
, where
f_{t_{0},N}:=f_{t_{0}}\chi_{\Omega_{N}}
,
g_{t_{0},N}:=g_{t_{0}}\chi_{\Omega_{N}}
and

\displaystyle\Lambda_{N}(f_{t_{0},N},g_{t_{0},N})(x):=\chi_{\Omega_{N}}(x)\int_{-\frac{t_{0}}{2}}^{\frac{t_{0}}{2}}f_{t_{0},N}(x-t)g_{t_{0},N}\left(x-\Upsilon_{t_{0}}(t)\right)\psi\left(2^{k}t\right)\,\textrm{d}t.

Indeed, notice that
\frac{1}{2}<r<1
, from (
3.10
), by the triangle inequality, we can bound the left hand side of (
3.9
) by

\sum_{N\in[0,\lceil\frac{2^{k}}{2|\gamma^{\prime}(2)|}\rceil]}\left\|\Lambda_{N}(f_{t_{0},N},g_{t_{0},N})\right\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R})}^{r}

essentially. This, in combination with (
3.11
) and Hölder’s inequality, leads to the desired estimate (
3.9
).

We now turn to the proof of (
3.11
) by establishing the following estimates:

\circ

The
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\times L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
boundedness of
\Lambda_{N}(f_{t_{0},N},g_{t_{0},N})
. A calculation gives

\displaystyle\left\|\Lambda_{N}(f_{t_{0},N},g_{t_{0},N})\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq\|g_{t_{0},N}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\int_{0}^{1}\chi_{\Omega_{N}}(x)\int_{-\frac{t_{0}}{2}}^{\frac{t_{0}}{2}}f_{t_{0},N}(x-t)\psi\left(2^{k}t\right)\,\textrm{d}t\,\textrm{d}x

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-k}\|f_{t_{0},N}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\|g_{t_{0},N}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}.

\circ

The
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\times L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
boundedness of
\Lambda_{N}(f_{t_{0},N},g_{t_{0},N})
. One easily sees that

\displaystyle\left\|\Lambda_{N}(f_{t_{0},N},g_{t_{0},N})\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq\|f_{t_{0},N}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\int_{-\frac{t_{0}}{2}}^{\frac{t_{0}}{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\chi_{\Omega_{N}}(x)g_{t_{0},N}\left(x-\Upsilon_{t_{0}}(t)\right)\,\textrm{d}x~\psi\left(2^{k}t\right)\,\textrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-k}\|f_{t_{0},N}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\|g_{t_{0},N}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}.

\circ

The
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\times L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow L^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})
boundedness of
\Lambda_{N}(f_{t_{0},N},g_{t_{0},N})
. As in Proposition
3.1
, we need a
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\times L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
estimate for
\Lambda_{N}(f_{t_{0},N},g_{t_{0},N})
. By Lagrange’s mean value theorem, it follows from the change of variables
u=x-t
and
v=x-\Upsilon_{t_{0}}(t)
that

\left|{\frac{\partial(u,v)}{\partial(x,t)}}\right|=|1-\Upsilon_{t_{0}}^{\prime}(t)|=|\gamma^{\prime}(t+t_{0})-\gamma^{\prime}(t_{0})|=|\gamma^{\prime\prime}(\vartheta)||t|\gtrsim 2^{-k},

where
\vartheta\in[{\frac{1}{2}}t_{0},{\frac{3}{2}}t_{0}]
. Then,

\displaystyle\left\|\Lambda_{N}(f_{t_{0},N},g_{t_{0},N})\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{t_{0},N}(u)g_{t_{0},N}(v)\left|{\frac{\partial(x,t)}{\partial(u,v)}}\right|\,\textrm{d}u\textrm{d}v

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{k}\|f_{t_{0},N}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\|g_{t_{0},N}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}.

Moreover, one may use the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality to deduce that

\displaystyle\left\|\Lambda_{N}(f_{t_{0},N},g_{t_{0},N})\right\|_{L^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim|\Omega_{N}|\left\|\Lambda_{N}(f_{t_{0},N},g_{t_{0},N})\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\|f_{t_{0},N}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\|g_{t_{0},N}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}.

Putting things together, it follows from an interpolation that (
3.11
) holds for all
p,q,r
satisfying
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{r}
,
p,q\in[1,\infty]
and
\frac{1}{2}<r<1
.

\bullet

The case of
2^{-k+2}>t_{0}
. Let

\Pi_{k}(f,g)(x):=\int_{0}^{1}f(x-t)g(x-\gamma(t))\psi\left(2^{k}(t-t_{0})\right)\,\textrm{d}t,

it follows from
2^{-k+2}>t_{0}
that
k<\log_{2}(\frac{4}{t_{0}})
, therefore to prove (
3.9
) it will suffice to prove

\displaystyle\left\|\Pi_{k}(f,g)\right\|_{L^{r}([0,1])}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\|g\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R})}

for all
p,q,r
satisfying
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{r}
,
p,q\in[1,\infty]
and
\frac{1}{2}<r<1
. This can be proved by an interpolation with the following estimates:

\circ

The
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\times L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow L^{1}([0,1])
boundedness of
\Pi_{k}(f,g)
. It is easy to get

\displaystyle\left\|\Pi_{k}(f,g)\right\|_{L^{1}([0,1])}

\displaystyle\leq\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}f(x-t)\psi\left(2^{k}(t-t_{0})\right)\,\textrm{d}t\,\textrm{d}x\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}.

\circ

The
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\times L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow L^{1}([0,1])
boundedness of
\Pi_{k}(f,g)
. One obtains

\displaystyle\left\|\Pi_{k}(f,g)\right\|_{L^{1}([0,1])}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}g(x-\gamma(t))\psi\left(2^{k}(t-t_{0})\right)\,\textrm{d}t\,\textrm{d}x\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\|g\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}.

\circ

The
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\times L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow L^{\frac{1}{2}}([0,1])
boundedness of
\Pi_{k}(f,g)
. From
{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot 2^{-k}\leq|t-t_{0}|\leq 2\cdot 2^{-k}
,
2^{-k+2}>t_{0}
and
0<t<1
, which means that
0<t\leq\frac{7}{8}t_{0}
or
\frac{9}{8}t_{0}\leq t<1
. Furthermore, by the facts that
\gamma^{\prime}(t_{0})=1
and
|\gamma^{\prime}(t)|
is strictly increasing on
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, the change of variables
u=x-t
and
v=x-\gamma(t)
implies that

\displaystyle\left|{\frac{\partial(u,v)}{\partial(x,t)}}\right|

\displaystyle=|1-\gamma^{\prime}(t)|=|\gamma^{\prime}(t)-\gamma^{\prime}(t_{0})|

\displaystyle\geq\begin{cases}|\gamma^{\prime}(t)|-|\gamma^{\prime}(t_{0})|\geq\left|\gamma^{\prime}\left(\frac{9}{8}t_{0}\right)\right|-\left|\gamma^{\prime}(t_{0})\right|\gtrsim 1,~~~\textrm{if}~\frac{9}{8}t_{0}\leq t<1;\\
|\gamma^{\prime}(t_{0})|-|\gamma^{\prime}(t)|\geq\left|\gamma^{\prime}(t_{0})\right|-\left|\gamma^{\prime}\left(\frac{7}{8}t_{0}\right)\right|\gtrsim 1,~~~\textrm{if}~0<t\leq\frac{7}{8}t_{0}.\end{cases}

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\Pi_{k}(f,g)\right\|_{L^{1}([0,1])}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(u)g(v)\left|{\frac{\partial(x,t)}{\partial(u,v)}}\right|\,\textrm{d}u\textrm{d}v\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\|g\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}.

This, together with the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, leads to

\displaystyle\left\|\Pi_{k}(f,g)\right\|_{L^{\frac{1}{2}}([0,1])}\lesssim\left\|\Pi_{k}(f,g)\right\|_{L^{1}([0,1])}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\|g\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}.

Therefore, we finish the proof of the Proposition
3.2
.
∎

4
Estimates for
H_{\alpha,\gamma}
with
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})

Recall that

(4.1)

\displaystyle H_{\alpha,\gamma}(f,g)(x)\lesssim\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{(\alpha-1)j}H_{\alpha,\gamma,j}(f,g)(x).

Our goal in this section is to establish some
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})\times L^{q}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow L^{r,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
estimates for
H_{\alpha,\gamma}
with
p=1
by considering the corresponding estimates for
H_{\alpha,\gamma,j}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\gamma
be the same as in Theorem
1.1
. Then, there exists a positive constant
C
, such that

\left\|H_{\alpha,\gamma}(f,g)\right\|_{L^{r(\varepsilon),\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2r(\varepsilon)}(\mathbb{R})},

where
\frac{1}{r(\varepsilon)}:=\frac{2(\omega_{\infty,2}-\alpha)}{2\omega_{\infty,2}-\omega_{\infty,1}}-\varepsilon
with
\varepsilon>0
small enough.

Proof.

For
H_{\alpha,\gamma,j}
defined in (
3.1
), let
E
be a measurable set with finite measure, a calculation gives

\displaystyle\left\|H_{\alpha,\gamma,j}(f,g)\chi_{E}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\int_{E}\int_{2^{j-1}}^{2^{j}}f(x-t)\,\textrm{d}t\,\textrm{d}x\leq|E|\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}

for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
. This, combined with Propositions
3.1
and
3.2
, shows that

(4.2)

\displaystyle\left\|H_{\alpha,\gamma,j}(f,g)\chi_{E}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\min\left\{2^{j},|E|\right\}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}

for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
. From (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), we can write

(4.3)

\displaystyle\left\|H_{\alpha,\gamma,j}(f,g)\chi_{E}\right\|_{L^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\min\left\{2^{j},|E|\right\}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\|g\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}

for all
j<j_{0}
, and by (
3.6
) and (
3.7
), it follows that

(4.4)

\displaystyle\left\|H_{\alpha,\gamma,j}(f,g)\chi_{E}\right\|_{L^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\min\left\{2^{j},\frac{|E|}{|\gamma^{\prime}(2^{j-1})|}\right\}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\|g\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}

for all
j>j_{0}+2
.

Now, for any fixed
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, the operator
H_{\alpha,\gamma,j}(f,g)\chi_{E}
may be viewed as a linear operator about
g
. Interpolation (
4.2
) with (
4.3
) implies that

(4.5)

\displaystyle\left\|H_{\alpha,\gamma,j}(f,g)\chi_{E}\right\|_{L^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\min\left\{2^{j},|E|\right\}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\|g\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}}(\mathbb{R})}

for all
j<j_{0}
with
\theta_{1}\in(0,1)
. From Proposition
3.2
, reiterating this interpolation process, we also have

(4.6)

\displaystyle\left\|H_{\alpha,\gamma,j}(f,g)\chi_{E}\right\|_{L^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\min\left\{2^{j_{0}},2^{{j_{0}}\theta_{1}}|E|^{1-\theta_{1}}\right\}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\|g\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}}(\mathbb{R})}

for all
j=j_{0},j_{0}+1,j_{0}+2
. Using the interpolation argument on (
4.2
) with (
4.4
), we claim that

(4.7)

\displaystyle\left\|H_{\alpha,\gamma,j}(f,g)\chi_{E}\right\|_{L^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\min\left\{2^{j},\frac{2^{j(1-\theta_{1})}|E|^{\theta_{1}}}{|\gamma^{\prime}(2^{j-1})|^{\theta_{1}}},2^{j\theta_{1}}|E|^{1-\theta_{1}},\frac{|E|}{|\gamma^{\prime}(2^{j-1})|^{\theta_{1}}}\right\}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\|g\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}}(\mathbb{R})}

for all
j>j_{0}+2
.

Now pick
E_{\lambda}:=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}:\ |H_{\alpha,\gamma}(f,g)(x)|>\lambda\}
with nonnegative functions
f,g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
. One may use Chebyshev’s inequality and (
4.1
), (
4.5
)-(
4.7
) to deduce that

(4.8)

\displaystyle\lambda^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}}|E_{\lambda}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{E_{\lambda}}\left(\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{(\alpha-1)j}H_{\alpha,\gamma,j}(f,g)(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}}\,\textrm{d}x\lesssim\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{{\frac{\alpha-1}{1+\theta_{1}}}j}\int_{E_{\lambda}}H_{\alpha,\gamma,j}(f,g)(x))^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}}\,\textrm{d}x

\displaystyle\lesssim\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}+\Delta_{3},

where

\begin{cases}\Delta_{1}:=\sum\limits_{j<j_{0}}2^{{\frac{\alpha-1}{1+\theta_{1}}j}}\min\left\{2^{j},|E_{\lambda}|\right\}^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}}\|g\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}};\\
\Delta_{2}:=2^{{\frac{\alpha-1}{1+\theta_{1}}}j_{0}}\min\left\{2^{j_{0}},2^{{j_{0}}\theta_{1}}|E_{\lambda}|^{1-\theta_{1}}\right\}^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}}\|g\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}};\\
\Delta_{3}:=\sum\limits_{j>j_{0}+2}2^{{\frac{\alpha-1}{1+\theta_{1}}}j}\min\left\{2^{j},\frac{2^{j(1-\theta_{1})}|E_{\lambda}|^{\theta_{1}}}{|\gamma^{\prime}(2^{j-1})|^{\theta_{1}}},2^{j\theta_{1}}|E_{\lambda}|^{1-\theta_{1}},\frac{|E_{\lambda}|}{|\gamma^{\prime}(2^{j-1})|^{\theta_{1}}}\right\}^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}}\|g\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}}.\end{cases}

These three terms are treated separately in the following section. We are ready to calculate
\Delta_{1}
, which can be written as

\displaystyle\left[\sum\limits_{j<j_{0},~{|E_{\lambda}|\geq 2^{j}}}2^{{\frac{\alpha}{1+\theta_{1}}}j}+\sum\limits_{j<j_{0},~{|E_{\lambda}|<2^{j}}}2^{{\frac{\alpha-1}{1+\theta_{1}}}j}|E_{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}}\right]\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}}\|g\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}}.

Notice that

\sum\limits_{j<j_{0},~{E_{\lambda}\geq 2^{j}}}2^{{\frac{\alpha}{1+\theta_{1}}}j}\lesssim\begin{cases}2^{{\frac{\alpha}{1+\theta_{1}}}{j_{0}}},&|E_{\lambda}|\geq 2^{j_{0}};\\
|E_{\lambda}|^{\frac{\alpha}{1+\theta_{1}}},&|E_{\lambda}|<2^{j_{0}},\end{cases}

and

\sum\limits_{j<j_{0},~{|E_{\lambda}|<2^{j}}}2^{{\frac{\alpha-1}{1+\theta_{1}}}j}|E_{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}}\lesssim\begin{cases}0,&|E_{\lambda}|\geq 2^{j_{0}};\\
|E_{\lambda}|^{\frac{\alpha}{1+\theta_{1}}},&|E_{\lambda}|<2^{j_{0}}.\end{cases}

Hence we have the estimate

(4.9)

\displaystyle\Delta_{1}\lesssim\begin{cases}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}}\|g\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}},&|E_{\lambda}|\geq 2^{j_{0}};\\
|E_{\lambda}|^{\frac{\alpha}{1+\theta_{1}}}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}}\|g\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}},&|E_{\lambda}|<2^{j_{0}}.\end{cases}

Now we turn to the second term
\Delta_{2}
, it is easy to see that

(4.10)

\displaystyle\Delta_{2}\lesssim\begin{cases}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}}\|g\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}},&|E_{\lambda}|\geq 2^{j_{0}};\\
|E_{\lambda}|^{\frac{1-\theta_{1}}{1+\theta_{1}}}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}}\|g\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}},&|E_{\lambda}|<2^{j_{0}}.\end{cases}

Our next goal is to consider
\Delta_{3}
, a calculation gives

\displaystyle\min\left\{2^{j},\frac{2^{j(1-\theta_{1})}|E_{\lambda}|^{\theta_{1}}}{|\gamma^{\prime}(2^{j-1})|^{\theta_{1}}},2^{j\theta_{1}}|E_{\lambda}|^{1-\theta_{1}},\frac{|E_{\lambda}|}{|\gamma^{\prime}(2^{j-1})|^{\theta_{1}}}\right\}=\begin{cases}2^{j},&|E_{\lambda}|\geq 2^{j}|\gamma^{\prime}(2^{j-1})|;\\
\frac{2^{j(1-\theta_{1})}|E_{\lambda}|^{\theta_{1}}}{|\gamma^{\prime}(2^{j-1})|^{\theta_{1}}},&2^{j}\leq|E_{\lambda}|<2^{j}|\gamma^{\prime}(2^{j-1})|;\\
\frac{|E_{\lambda}|}{|\gamma^{\prime}(2^{j-1})|^{\theta_{1}}},&|E_{\lambda}|<2^{j}.\end{cases}

We can then write
\Delta_{3}=\Delta_{3,1}+\Delta_{3,2}+\Delta_{3,3}
, where

\begin{cases}\Delta_{3,1}:=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}j>j_{0}+2\\
|E_{\lambda}|\geq 2^{j}|\gamma^{\prime}(2^{j-1})|\end{subarray}}2^{{\frac{\alpha}{1+\theta_{1}}}j}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}}\|g\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}};\\
\Delta_{3,2}:=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}j>j_{0}+2\\
2^{j}\leq|E_{\lambda}|<2^{j}|\gamma^{\prime}(2^{j-1})|\end{subarray}}{\frac{2^{{\frac{\alpha-\theta_{1}}{1+\theta_{1}}}j}|E_{\lambda}|^{\frac{\theta_{1}}{1+\theta_{1}}}}{|\gamma^{\prime}(2^{j-1})|^{\frac{\theta_{1}}{1+\theta_{1}}}}}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}}\|g\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}};\\
\Delta_{3,3}:=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}j>j_{0}+2\\
|E_{\lambda}|<2^{j}\end{subarray}}{\frac{2^{{\frac{\alpha-1}{1+\theta_{1}}}j}|E_{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}}}{|\gamma^{\prime}(2^{j-1})|^{\frac{\theta_{1}}{1+\theta_{1}}}}}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}}\|g\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}}.\end{cases}

Corresponding to
\Delta_{3,1}
, for any
\varepsilon>0
, it follows from Lemma
2.1
that

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}j>j_{0}+2\\
|E_{\lambda}|\geq 2^{j}|\gamma^{\prime}(2^{j-1})|\end{subarray}}2^{{\frac{\alpha}{1+\theta_{1}}}j}\lesssim\begin{cases}|E_{\lambda}|^{\frac{\alpha}{(\omega_{\infty,1}-\varepsilon)(1+\theta_{1})}},&|E_{\lambda}|\geq 2^{j_{0}+2}|\gamma^{\prime}(2^{j_{0}+1})|;\\
0,&2^{j_{0}+2}\leq|E_{\lambda}|<2^{j_{0}+2}|\gamma^{\prime}(2^{j_{0}+1})|;\\
0,&|E_{\lambda}|<2^{j_{0}+2}.\end{cases}

On the other hand, let us set
\varepsilon\in(0,\omega_{\infty,1})
, and we take that
\alpha<\theta_{1}(\omega_{\infty,1}-\varepsilon)
, then by Lemma
2.1
one has

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}j>j_{0}+2\\
2^{j}\leq|E_{\lambda}|<2^{j}|\gamma^{\prime}(2^{j-1})|\end{subarray}}{\frac{2^{{\frac{\alpha-\theta_{1}}{1+\theta_{1}}}j}|E_{\lambda}|^{\frac{\theta_{1}}{1+\theta_{1}}}}{|\gamma^{\prime}(2^{j-1})|^{\frac{\theta_{1}}{1+\theta_{1}}}}}\lesssim\begin{cases}|E_{\lambda}|^{{\frac{\alpha+\theta_{1}(\omega_{\infty,2}-\omega_{\infty,1}+2\varepsilon)}{(\omega_{\infty,2}+\varepsilon)(1+\theta_{1})}}},&|E_{\lambda}|\geq 2^{j_{0}+2}|\gamma^{\prime}(2^{j_{0}+1})|;\\
|E_{\lambda}|^{{\frac{\alpha+\theta_{1}(\omega_{\infty,2}-\omega_{\infty,1}+2\varepsilon)}{(\omega_{\infty,2}+\varepsilon)(1+\theta_{1})}}},&2^{j_{0}+2}\leq|E_{\lambda}|<2^{j_{0}+2}|\gamma^{\prime}(2^{j_{0}+1})|;\\
0,&|E_{\lambda}|<2^{j_{0}+2}.\end{cases}

This is the estimate about
\Delta_{3,2}
. Furthermore, for estimating
\Delta_{3,3}
, we need to take
\alpha-1+\theta_{1}>0
. It follows from
\omega_{\infty,1}>1
that
\alpha-1+\theta_{1}-\theta_{1}(\omega_{\infty,1}-\varepsilon)<0
for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\omega_{\infty,1}-1]
. From Lemma
2.1
, we then have

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}j>j_{0}+2\\
|E_{\lambda}|<2^{j}\end{subarray}}{\frac{2^{{\frac{\alpha-1}{1+\theta_{1}}}j}|E_{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}}}{|\gamma^{\prime}(2^{j-1})|^{\frac{\theta_{1}}{1+\theta_{1}}}}}\lesssim\begin{cases}|E_{\lambda}|^{{\frac{\alpha-\theta_{1}(\omega_{\infty,1}-1-\varepsilon)}{1+\theta_{1}}}},&|E_{\lambda}|\geq 2^{j_{0}+2}|\gamma^{\prime}(2^{j_{0}+1})|;\\
|E_{\lambda}|^{{\frac{\alpha-\theta_{1}(\omega_{\infty,1}-1-\varepsilon)}{1+\theta_{1}}}},&2^{j_{0}+2}\leq|E_{\lambda}|<2^{j_{0}+2}|\gamma^{\prime}(2^{j_{0}+1})|;\\
|E_{\lambda}|^{{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}}},&|E_{\lambda}|<2^{j_{0}+2}.\end{cases}

Notice that

|E_{\lambda}|^{{\frac{\alpha-\theta_{1}(\omega_{\infty,1}-1-\varepsilon)}{1+\theta_{1}}}}\leq|E_{\lambda}|^{\frac{\alpha}{(\omega_{\infty,1}-\varepsilon)(1+\theta_{1})}}\leq|E_{\lambda}|^{{\frac{\alpha+\theta_{1}(\omega_{\infty,2}-\omega_{\infty,1}+2\varepsilon)}{(\omega_{\infty,2}+\varepsilon)(1+\theta_{1})}}}

holds for
|E_{\lambda}|\geq 2^{j_{0}+2}
if
\varepsilon\in(0,\min\{\omega_{\infty,1},\omega_{\infty,1}-1\})
. Consequently,

(4.11)

\displaystyle\Delta_{3}\lesssim\begin{cases}|E_{\lambda}|^{{\frac{\alpha+\theta_{1}(\omega_{\infty,2}-\omega_{\infty,1}+2\varepsilon)}{(\omega_{\infty,2}+\varepsilon)(1+\theta_{1})}}}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}}\|g\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}},&|E_{\lambda}|\geq 2^{j_{0}+2};\\
|E_{\lambda}|^{{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}}}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}}\|g\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}},&|E_{\lambda}|<2^{j_{0}+2}.\end{cases}

It follows from
\alpha-1+\theta_{1}>0
and
\theta_{1}\in(0,1)
that

|E_{\lambda}|^{{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}}}\leq|E_{\lambda}|^{\frac{\alpha}{1+\theta_{1}}}\leq|E_{\lambda}|^{\frac{1-\theta_{1}}{1+\theta_{1}}}

holds for
|E_{\lambda}|<2^{j_{0}+2}
. This, combined with (
4.8
)-(
4.11
), we conclude that

(4.12)

\displaystyle\lambda^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}}|E_{\lambda}|\lesssim\begin{cases}|E_{\lambda}|^{\frac{\alpha+\theta_{1}(\omega_{\infty,2}-\omega_{\infty,1}+2\varepsilon)}{(\omega_{\infty,2}+\varepsilon)(1+\theta_{1})}}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}}\|g\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}},&|E_{\lambda}|\geq 2^{j_{0}+2};\\
|E_{\lambda}|^{\frac{1-\theta_{1}}{1+\theta_{1}}}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}}\|g\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}},&|E_{\lambda}|<2^{j_{0}+2}.\end{cases}

By a calculation, for
\varepsilon
small enough, we have that
\frac{\omega_{\infty,2}-\omega_{\infty,1}}{\omega_{\infty,2}-1}<\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}<\frac{\omega_{\infty,2}}{\omega_{\infty,2}-\omega_{\infty,1}+1}
implies

(4.13)

\displaystyle\max\left\{1-\alpha,\alpha,\frac{\alpha}{\omega_{\infty,1}-\varepsilon}\right\}<\frac{\omega_{\infty,2}-\alpha+\varepsilon}{2\omega_{\infty,2}-\omega_{\infty,1}+3\varepsilon}.

Then, we can take

(4.14)

\displaystyle\theta_{1}\leq\frac{\omega_{\infty,2}-\alpha+\varepsilon}{2\omega_{\infty,2}-\omega_{\infty,1}+3\varepsilon}

with
\varepsilon
small enough, which further implies that

\frac{\alpha+\theta_{1}(\omega_{\infty,2}-\omega_{\infty,1}+2\varepsilon)}{(\omega_{\infty,2}+\varepsilon)(1+\theta_{1})}\leq\frac{1-\theta_{1}}{1+\theta_{1}},

This, together with (
4.12
), gives

(4.15)

\displaystyle\lambda|E_{\lambda}|^{\frac{\omega_{\infty,2}+\theta_{1}(\omega_{\infty,1}-\varepsilon)-\alpha+\varepsilon}{\omega_{\infty,2}+\varepsilon}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\|g\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}}(\mathbb{R})}

for all
0<|E_{\lambda}|<\infty
, where
\varepsilon
small enough and
\theta_{1}
satisfies (
4.14
). Therefore, we have established the
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\times L^{\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow L^{\frac{\omega_{\infty,2}+\varepsilon}{\omega_{\infty,2}+\theta_{1}(\omega_{\infty,1}-\varepsilon)-\alpha+\varepsilon},\infty}(\mathbb{R})
boundedness for
H_{\alpha,\gamma}
.

It is easy to see that
\frac{\omega_{\infty,2}+\theta_{1}(\omega_{\infty,1}-\varepsilon)-\alpha+\varepsilon}{\omega_{\infty,2}+\varepsilon}
takes the maximal value
\frac{2(\omega_{\infty,2}-\alpha+\varepsilon)}{2\omega_{\infty,2}-\omega_{\infty,1}+3\varepsilon}
when
\theta_{1}=\frac{\omega_{\infty,2}-\alpha+\varepsilon}{2\omega_{\infty,2}-\omega_{\infty,1}+3\varepsilon}
. On the other hand, from (
4.13
), it follows
\frac{\alpha}{\omega_{\infty,1}-\varepsilon}<\frac{\omega_{\infty,2}-\alpha+\varepsilon}{2\omega_{\infty,2}-\omega_{\infty,1}+3\varepsilon}
, which equals to
2\alpha+\varepsilon<\omega_{\infty,1}
, we then have
2\alpha<\omega_{\infty,1}
. This, together with
\alpha<1<\omega_{\infty,1}\leq\omega_{\infty,2}
, shows
3\alpha<\omega_{\infty,1}+\omega_{\infty,2}
. Therefore,

\frac{\omega_{\infty,2}-\alpha+\varepsilon}{2\omega_{\infty,2}-\omega_{\infty,1}+3\varepsilon}<\frac{\omega_{\infty,2}-\alpha}{2\omega_{\infty,2}-\omega_{\infty,1}}

holds for all
\varepsilon
small enough. Putting things together, we conclude that
H_{\alpha,\gamma}
maps
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\times L^{2r(\varepsilon)}(\mathbb{R})
to
L^{r(\varepsilon),\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, where
\frac{1}{r(\varepsilon)}:=\frac{2(\omega_{\infty,2}-\alpha)}{2\omega_{\infty,2}-\omega_{\infty,1}}-\varepsilon
with
\varepsilon
small enough. Therefore, we finish the proof of Proposition
4.1
.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\gamma
be the same as in Theorem
1.2
. Then, there exists a positive constant
C
, such that

\left\|H_{\alpha,\gamma}(f,g)\right\|_{L^{\bar{r}(\varepsilon),\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{\bar{q}(\varepsilon)}(\mathbb{R})},

where

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\bar{r}(\varepsilon)}:=\frac{2(1-\alpha)}{2-\omega_{\infty,1}}-\varepsilon~~\textrm{and}~~\frac{1}{\bar{q}(\varepsilon)}:=\frac{1}{2\bar{r}(\varepsilon)},&\omega_{\infty,1}\leq 2\alpha;\\
\frac{1}{\bar{r}(\varepsilon)}:=1-\varepsilon~~\textrm{and}~~\frac{1}{\bar{q}(\varepsilon)}\in\left[\frac{\alpha}{\omega_{\infty,1}}-\varepsilon,\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon\right],&2\alpha<\omega_{\infty,1},\end{cases}

with
\varepsilon>0
small enough.

Proof.

The main difference here lies in the establishing of
\Delta_{3,2}
. In fact, let
\varepsilon\in(0,\omega_{\infty,1})
, and we take
\alpha>\theta_{1}(\omega_{\infty,1}-\varepsilon)
. By Lemma
2.1
, one immediately has

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}j>j_{0}+2\\
2^{j}\leq|E_{\lambda}|<2^{j}|\gamma^{\prime}(2^{j-1})|\end{subarray}}{\frac{2^{{\frac{\alpha-\theta_{1}}{1+\theta_{1}}}j}|E_{\lambda}|^{\frac{\theta_{1}}{1+\theta_{1}}}}{|\gamma^{\prime}(2^{j-1})|^{\frac{\theta_{1}}{1+\theta_{1}}}}}\lesssim\begin{cases}|E_{\lambda}|^{{\frac{\alpha-\theta_{1}(\omega_{\infty,1}-1-\varepsilon)}{1+\theta_{1}}}},&|E_{\lambda}|\geq 2^{j_{0}+2}|\gamma^{\prime}(2^{j_{0}+1})|;\\
|E_{\lambda}|^{{\frac{\alpha-\theta_{1}(\omega_{\infty,1}-1-\varepsilon)}{1+\theta_{1}}}},&2^{j_{0}+2}\leq|E_{\lambda}|<2^{j_{0}+2}|\gamma^{\prime}(2^{j_{0}+1})|;\\
0,&|E_{\lambda}|<2^{j_{0}+2}.\end{cases}

The estimation of
\Delta_{3,3}
is the same as in Proposition
4.1
, and we also need to take
\alpha-1+\theta_{1}>0
, which can be realized by the condition that
\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\geq\omega_{\infty,1}>1
. Notice that
\alpha>\theta_{1}(\omega_{\infty,1}-\varepsilon)
, which implies
|E_{\lambda}|^{\frac{\alpha}{(\omega_{\infty,1}-\varepsilon)(1+\theta_{1})}}\leq|E_{\lambda}|^{{\frac{\alpha-\theta_{1}(\omega_{\infty,1}-1-\varepsilon)}{1+\theta_{1}}}}
for
|E_{\lambda}|\geq 2^{j_{0}+2}
. Thus one can write

\displaystyle\Delta_{3}\lesssim\begin{cases}|E_{\lambda}|^{{\frac{\alpha-\theta_{1}(\omega_{\infty,1}-1-\varepsilon)}{1+\theta_{1}}}}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}}\|g\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}},&|E_{\lambda}|\geq 2^{j_{0}+2};\\
|E_{\lambda}|^{{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}}}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}}\|g\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}},&|E_{\lambda}|<2^{j_{0}+2}.\end{cases}

As in Proposition
4.1
, we have an estimate

(4.16)

\displaystyle\lambda^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}}|E_{\lambda}|\lesssim\begin{cases}|E_{\lambda}|^{{\frac{\alpha-\theta_{1}(\omega_{\infty,1}-1-\varepsilon)}{1+\theta_{1}}}}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}}\|g\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}},&|E_{\lambda}|\geq 2^{j_{0}+2};\\
|E_{\lambda}|^{\frac{1-\theta_{1}}{1+\theta_{1}}}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}}\|g\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}},&|E_{\lambda}|<2^{j_{0}+2}.\end{cases}

We now want to obtain that

(4.17)

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha-\theta_{1}(\omega_{\infty,1}-1-\varepsilon)}{1+\theta_{1}}\leq\frac{1-\theta_{1}}{1+\theta_{1}},

which equals to
\theta_{1}(2-\omega_{\infty,1}+\varepsilon)\leq 1-\alpha
. If
2-\omega_{\infty,1}+\varepsilon\leq 0
, (
4.17
) is obvious. If
2-\omega_{\infty,1}+\varepsilon>0
, (
4.17
) equals to

(4.18)

\displaystyle\theta_{1}\leq\frac{1-\alpha}{2-\omega_{\infty,1}+\varepsilon}.

Clearly
1-\alpha\leq\frac{1-\alpha}{2-\omega_{\infty,1}+\varepsilon}
holds for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\omega_{\infty,1}-1]
, which shows (
4.18
) can be realized. This, combined with (
4.16
) and (
4.17
), means that

\displaystyle\lambda|E_{\lambda}|^{1-\alpha+\theta_{1}(\omega_{\infty,1}-\varepsilon)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\|g\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}}(\mathbb{R})}

for all
0<|E_{\lambda}|<\infty
, where

(4.19)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\theta_{1}\leq\frac{1-\alpha}{2-\omega_{\infty,1}+\varepsilon},&\omega_{\infty,1}<2\alpha+\varepsilon;\\
\theta_{1}<\frac{\alpha}{\omega_{\infty,1}-\varepsilon},&2\alpha+\varepsilon\leq\omega_{\infty,1},\end{cases}

and
\varepsilon>0
small enough. Therefore, we have established the
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\times L^{\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow L^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha+\theta_{1}(\omega_{\infty,1}-\varepsilon)},\infty}(\mathbb{R})
boundedness for
H_{\alpha,\gamma}
.

On the other hand, by (
4.19
), for
\varepsilon>0
small enough, one easily sees that

\displaystyle\begin{cases}1-\alpha+\theta_{1}(\omega_{\infty,1}-\varepsilon)\leq\frac{2(1-\alpha)}{2-\omega_{\infty,1}+\varepsilon}<\frac{2(1-\alpha)}{2-\omega_{\infty,1}},&\omega_{\infty,1}<2\alpha+\varepsilon;\\
1-\alpha+\theta_{1}(\omega_{\infty,1}-\varepsilon)<1,&2\alpha+\varepsilon\leq\omega_{\infty,1}.\end{cases}

Therefore, we conclude that
H_{\alpha,\gamma}
maps
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\times L^{\bar{q}(\varepsilon)}(\mathbb{R})
to
L^{\bar{r}(\varepsilon),\infty}(\mathbb{R})
with
\varepsilon>0
small enough. Here,
\frac{1}{\bar{r}(\varepsilon)}
tends to maximal value as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
.

Notice that
\frac{\alpha}{\omega_{\infty,1}-\varepsilon}
increases as
\varepsilon
increases when
2\alpha+\varepsilon\leq\omega_{\infty,1}
, then
\frac{1}{\bar{q}(\varepsilon)}
doesn’t tend to maximal value as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
. We now want
\frac{1}{\bar{q}(\varepsilon)}
to take maximal value. In fact, from the process of our proofs, we can find that
\varepsilon
need only satisfies
\varepsilon\leq\omega_{\infty,1}-2\alpha
and
\varepsilon\leq\omega_{\infty,1}-1
when
\omega_{\infty,1}>2\alpha
. Moreover, using
2\alpha<\omega_{\infty,1}\leq\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}
, we have
\frac{1}{2}<\alpha
. Therefore, we should only to take
\varepsilon\leq\omega_{\infty,1}-2\alpha
. It is easy to check that
\frac{1}{\bar{q}(\varepsilon)}
tends to the maximal value
\frac{1}{2}
when
\varepsilon=\omega_{\infty,1}-2\alpha
, and
\frac{1}{\bar{r}(\varepsilon)}
tends to
1
. Then,
H_{\alpha,\gamma}
maps
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\times L^{\frac{2}{1-\varepsilon}}(\mathbb{R})
to
L^{\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon},\infty}(\mathbb{R})
with
\varepsilon>0
small enough when
\omega_{\infty,1}>2\alpha
.

Putting things together, we finish the proof of Proposition
4.3
.
∎

5

with

In this section, we will establish some

with

by considering the corresponding estimates for

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\gamma
be the same as in Theorem

. Then, there exists a positive constant
C
, such that

\left\|H_{\alpha,\gamma}(f,g)\right\|_{L^{r(\varepsilon),\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2r(\varepsilon)}(\mathbb{R})}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})},

where
\frac{1}{r(\varepsilon)}=\frac{2(\omega_{\infty,2}-\alpha)}{2\omega_{\infty,2}-\omega_{\infty,1}}-\varepsilon
with
\varepsilon>0
small enough as in Proposition
4.1
.

Proof.

Let
E
be a measurable set with finite measure, and recall that
H_{\alpha,\gamma,j}
defined in (
3.1
) with
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, by the fact that
|\gamma^{\prime}(t)|
is strictly increasing on

, a routine calculation gives

\displaystyle\left\|H_{\alpha,\gamma,j}(f,g)\chi_{E}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\int_{E}\int_{2^{j-1}}^{2^{j}}g(x-\gamma(t))\,\textrm{d}t\,\textrm{d}x\leq\frac{|E|}{|\gamma^{\prime}(2^{j-1})|}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\|g\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}.

Combining Propositions
3.1
and
3.2
, for any

, we immediately have

(5.1)

\displaystyle\left\|H_{\alpha,\gamma,j}(f,g)\chi_{E}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\min\left\{2^{j},\frac{|E|}{|\gamma^{\prime}(2^{j-1})|}\right\}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\|g\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}.

Notice that
H_{\alpha,\gamma,j}(f,g)\chi_{E}
can be viewed as a linear operator about
f
for any fixed

, by interpolation between (
5.1
) with (
4.3
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\left\|H_{\alpha,\gamma,j}(f,g)\chi_{E}\right\|_{L^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{2}}}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\min\left\{2^{j},2^{j(1-\theta_{2})}|E|^{\theta_{2}},{\frac{2^{j\theta_{2}}|E|^{1-\theta_{2}}}{|\gamma^{\prime}(2^{j-1})|^{1-\theta_{2}}}},{\frac{|E|}{|\gamma^{\prime}(2^{j-1})|^{1-\theta_{2}}}}\right\}\|f\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\theta_{2}}}(\mathbb{R})}\|g\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}

for all
j<j_{0}
with
\theta_{2}\in(0,1)
. From Proposition
3.2
, reiterating this interpolation process, then the following estimate is valid:

\displaystyle\left\|H_{\alpha,\gamma,j}(f,g)\chi_{E}\right\|_{L^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{1}}}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\min\left\{2^{j_{0}},\frac{2^{{j_{0}}\theta_{2}}|E|^{1-\theta_{2}}}{|\gamma^{\prime}(2^{j_{0}-1})|^{1-\theta_{2}}}\right\}\|f\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\theta_{2}}}(\mathbb{R})}\|g\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}

for all
j=j_{0},j_{0}+1,j_{0}+2
. A further interpolation between (
5.1
) with (
4.4
) gives

(5.2)

\displaystyle\left\|H_{\alpha,\gamma,j}(f,g)\chi_{E}\right\|_{L^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{2}}}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\min\left\{2^{j},\frac{|E|}{|\gamma^{\prime}(2^{j-1})|}\right\}\|f\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\theta_{2}}}(\mathbb{R})}\|g\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}

for all
j>j_{0}+2
. Moreover, in order to establish the desired estimate, we bound

(5.3)

\displaystyle\left\|H_{\alpha,\gamma,j}(f,g)\chi_{E}\right\|_{L^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{2}}}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\min\left\{2^{j},\frac{2^{j(1-\theta_{2})}|E|^{\theta_{2}}}{|\gamma^{\prime}(2^{j-1})|^{\theta_{2}}}\right\}\|f\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\theta_{2}}}(\mathbb{R})}\|g\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}

for all
j>j_{0}+2
.

Recall that
E_{\lambda}=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}:\ |H_{\alpha,\gamma}(f,g)(x)|>\lambda\}
. Then, by a similar argument as in (
4.8
), we conclude that

(5.4)

\displaystyle\lambda^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{2}}}|E_{\lambda}|\lesssim\Upsilon_{1}+\Upsilon_{2}+\Upsilon_{3},

|E_{\lambda}|^{\frac{1-\theta_{2}}{1+\theta_{2}}}\|f\|^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{2}}}_{L^{\frac{1}{\theta_{2}}}(\mathbb{R})}\|g\|^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{2}}}_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})},&|E_{\lambda}|<2^{j_{0}}|\gamma^{\prime}(2^{j_{0}-1})|.\end{cases}

Combining (
5.10
), (
5.6
) and (
5.12
), then the following estimate is valid:

\displaystyle\lambda^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{2}}}|E_{\lambda}|\lesssim\begin{cases}|E_{\lambda}|^{\frac{\alpha+(1-\theta_{2})(\omega_{\infty,2}-\omega_{\infty,1}+2\varepsilon)}{(1+\theta_{2})(\omega_{\infty,2}+\varepsilon)}}\|f\|^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{2}}}_{L^{\frac{1}{\theta_{2}}}(\mathbb{R})}\|g\|^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{2}}}_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})},&|E_{\lambda}|\geq 2^{j_{0}}|\gamma^{\prime}(2^{j_{0}-1})|;\\
\left(|E_{\lambda}|^{\frac{\alpha+\omega_{0,1}-\omega_{0,2}-2\varepsilon+\theta_{2}(\omega_{0,2}-1+\varepsilon)}{(\omega_{0,1}-\varepsilon)(1+\theta_{2})}}+|E_{\lambda}|^{\frac{1-\theta_{2}}{1+\theta_{2}}}\right)\|f\|^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{2}}}_{L^{\frac{1}{\theta_{2}}}(\mathbb{R})}\|g\|^{\frac{1}{1+\theta_{2}}}_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})},&|E_{\lambda}|<2^{j_{0}}|\gamma^{\prime}(2^{j_{0}-1})|.\end{cases}

If we have

\frac{\alpha+(1-\theta_{2})(\omega_{\infty,2}-\omega_{\infty,1}+2\varepsilon)}{(1+\theta_{2})(\omega_{\infty,2}+\varepsilon)}<\frac{1-\theta_{2}}{1+\theta_{2}}\leq\frac{\alpha+\omega_{0,1}-\omega_{0,2}-2\varepsilon+\theta_{2}(\omega_{0,2}-1+\varepsilon)}{(\omega_{0,1}-\varepsilon)(1+\theta_{2})},

which equals to

\frac{\omega_{0,2}-\alpha+\varepsilon}{\omega_{0,2}+\omega_{0,1}-1}\leq\theta_{2}<\frac{\omega_{\infty,1}-\alpha-\varepsilon}{\omega_{\infty,1}-\varepsilon}
for
\varepsilon>0
small enough, we then obtain that

(5.13)

\displaystyle\lambda|E_{\lambda}|^{\frac{\omega_{\infty,1}-\alpha-\varepsilon+\theta_{2}(2\omega_{\infty,2}-\omega_{\infty,1}+3\varepsilon)}{\omega_{\infty,2}+\varepsilon}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\theta_{2}}}(\mathbb{R})}\|g\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}

holds for all
0<|E_{\lambda}|<\infty
. Indeed,
\theta_{2}<\frac{\omega_{\infty,1}-\alpha-\varepsilon}{\omega_{\infty,1}-\varepsilon}
comes from the assumption in estimating
\Upsilon_{3}
. In order to show that we can take
\theta_{2}\geq\frac{\omega_{0,2}-\alpha+\varepsilon}{\omega_{0,2}+\omega_{0,1}-1}
, it suffices to show that

\frac{\omega_{0,2}-\alpha+\varepsilon}{\omega_{0,2}+\omega_{0,1}-1}<\frac{\omega_{\infty,1}-\alpha-\varepsilon}{\omega_{\infty,1}-\varepsilon}\leq\alpha

holds for
\varepsilon>0
small enough, which is trivially verified by the condition that
\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\geq\omega_{\infty,1}\geq\omega_{0,2}
and
\omega_{0,1}>1
. Thus, we obtain (
5.13
) for
\theta_{2}<\frac{\omega_{\infty,1}-\alpha-\varepsilon}{\omega_{\infty,1}-\varepsilon}
with
\varepsilon>0
small enough.

Notice that

\frac{\omega_{\infty,1}-\alpha-\varepsilon+\theta_{2}(2\omega_{\infty,2}-\omega_{\infty,1}+3\varepsilon)}{\omega_{\infty,2}+\varepsilon}<\frac{2(\omega_{\infty,1}-\alpha-\varepsilon)}{\omega_{\infty,1}-\varepsilon}

when
\theta_{2}<\frac{\omega_{\infty,1}-\alpha-\varepsilon}{\omega_{\infty,1}-\varepsilon}
with
\varepsilon>0
small enough, and
\frac{2(\omega_{\infty,1}-\alpha-\varepsilon)}{\omega_{\infty,1}-\varepsilon}<\frac{2(\omega_{\infty,1}-\alpha)}{\omega_{\infty,1}}
, we conclude that
H_{\alpha,\gamma}
maps
L^{2\tilde{r}(\varepsilon)}(\mathbb{R})\times L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
to
L^{\tilde{r}(\varepsilon),\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, where
\frac{1}{\tilde{r}(\varepsilon)}=\frac{2(\omega_{\infty,1}-\alpha)}{\omega_{\infty,1}}-\varepsilon
with
\varepsilon
small enough. Therefore, we finish the proof of Proposition
5.3
.

Abstract
. We prove that the trace of nonlocal minimal graphs at points of stickiness is of class C 1 , γ C^{1,\gamma} . As a result, we show that boundary continuity implies boundary differentiability for nonlocal minimal graphs.

35R11, 35J93, 53A10.

1.
Introduction

Nonlocal minimal surfaces are the fractional counterpart of the classical minimizers of the perimeter functional. A special subclass is given by nonlocal minimal graphs, namely nonlocal minimal surfaces that possess a graphical structure.

Because of the presence of long-range interactions, nonlocal minimal graphs behave quite differently from their classical analogues, most notably through the typical appearance of boundary discontinuities. Nevertheless, nonlocal minimal graphs remain continuous from the interior up to the boundary of the reference domain, and therefore admit a
boundary trace
. The fundamental properties of this trace are, at present, largely unknown,
also due to the complexities created by the coexistence of horizontal and vertical
normal directions.

This paper is concerned with the regularity of this boundary trace. Our main result establishes
C^{1,\gamma}
-regularity of the trace at points of boundary discontinuity. As a byproduct, we also obtain that nonlocal minimal graphs are differentiable
(of an order higher than that dictated by the underlying geometric operator)
at every point of boundary continuity.

Sketch illustrating a nonlocal minimal graph “rising” due to far-away data.
The figures in this paper serve a purely expository purpose and do not aim to capture the full complexity of nonlocal minimal graphs.

The precise mathematical framework that we consider is as follows (see Figure
1
for a simple diagram).
Throughout this paper,
\Omega
will denote an open, bounded set of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
of
Lipschitz class
and our objective is to consider minimizers of the
s
-perimeter functional in the cylinder
{\mathcal{C}}:=\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
. For this minimization, we will consider an external datum
E_{0}
with a graphical structure. More specifically, we consider a continuous
function
u_{0}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
and define
E_{0}:=\{x_{n+1}<u_{0}(x^{\prime})\}
, where points in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}

are denoted by
x=(x^{\prime},x_{n+1})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
.

Given
s\in(0,1)
and two disjoint sets
A
and
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, we consider the integral interaction

I_{s}(A,B):=\iint_{A\times B}\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{n+1+s}}

and, in the wake of
[
7
]
, we define the
s
-perimeter of a (measurable)
set
F\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
in
{\mathcal{C}}
as

{\operatorname{Per}}_{s}(F,{\mathcal{C}}):=I_{s}(F\cap{\mathcal{C}},F^{c}\cap{\mathcal{C}})+I_{s}(F\cap{\mathcal{C}},F^{c}\cap{\mathcal{C}}^{c})+I_{s}(F\cap{\mathcal{C}}^{c},F^{c}\cap{\mathcal{C}}),

where the superscript
c
denotes the complementary set in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.

We let
E
be a minimizer for the
s
-perimeter in
{\mathcal{C}}
with respect to
the external datum
E_{0}
: more precisely, we suppose that
E\cap{\mathcal{C}}^{c}=E_{0}\cap{\mathcal{C}}^{c}
and, for every bounded Lipschitz
set
U\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}
and every
F\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with
F\cap U^{c}=E\cap U^{c}
, it holds that
{\operatorname{Per}}_{s}(E,U)\leqslant{\operatorname{Per}}_{s}(F,U)
.
See
[
15
]
for more details about this notion of minimization.

It is known that
E
has also a graphical structure
(see
[
10
]
), namely
E=\{x_{n+1}<u_{E}(x^{\prime})\}
, for some
u_{E}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}

with
u_{E}=u_{0}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
. The function
u_{E}
is also known to be
uniformly continuous in
\Omega
(see
[
10
]
)
and
C^{\infty}(\Omega)
(see
[
4
, Theorem 1.1]
).
In particular, the uniform continuity of
u_{E}
allows us
to look at
u_{E}(\partial\Omega)
, which can be considered as the “trace”
of the nonlocal minimal surface
\Sigma:=\partial E
along
\partial{\mathcal{C}}

(coming from the interior of
{\mathcal{C}}
).

However,
boundary jumps for
u_{E}
can occur along
\partial\Omega
, where the derivatives
of
u_{E}
may also blow up: this phenomenon is called “stickiness”
and it is peculiar of nonlocal minimal surfaces, see
[
11
]

(in fact, the stickiness phenomenon is somewhat “generic” for
nonlocal minimal graphs, see
[
13
]
).

Though
u_{E}
experiences jumps and derivative blow up patterns
around the boundary of
{\mathcal{C}}
, it is known that

(1.1)

the hypersurface
\Sigma
is of class
C^{1,\frac{1+s}{2}}
in
\overline{\mathcal{C}}
,

see
[
5
, Theorem 1.1]
.
In this spirit, the external unit normal
\nu
to
\Sigma

is well-defined and continuous not only on
\Sigma\cap{\mathcal{C}}

(where it can be written as
\frac{(-\nabla u_{E},1)}{\sqrt{|\nabla u_{E}|^{2}+1}}
)
but also on
\Sigma\cap\partial{\mathcal{C}}

(where vertical tangents and stickiness phenomena
may produce cases in which
\nu_{n+1}=0
).

We point out that the external normal is actually well-defined
on the whole of
\partial^{*}E
(the reduced boundary of
the set
E
, which, by construction, is of locally finite perimeter).
This is useful, since it allows one to write a suitable nonlocal equation of geometric flavor
for
\nu
(as we will discuss in Section
2.1

below). For this, we
denote by
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}
the
n
-dimensional Hausdorff measure and
suppose that

(1.2)

\int_{\partial^{*}E_{0}\cap{\mathcal{C}}^{c}}\frac{d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}}{(1+|y|)^{n+1+s}}<+\infty.

In this setting, the main result of this paper goes as follows:

Theorem 1.1

(
C^{1,\gamma}
-regularity of the
trace of nonlocal minimal graphs at points of stickiness)
.

Let
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
and
X_{0}:=(x_{0},u(x_{0}))
. Suppose that
\Omega

is of class
C^{2,1}
in a neighborhood of
x_{0}
and

\lim_{\Omega\ni x^{\prime}\to x_{0}}u_{E}(x^{\prime})\neq\lim_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega\ni x^{\prime}\to x_{0}}u_{0}(x^{\prime}).

Then, there exists
\rho>0
such that
u_{E}(\partial\Omega)\cap B_{\rho}(X_{0})

is an
(n-1)
-dimensional surface of class
C^{1,\gamma}

in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.

The statement in Theorem
1.1
is the first regularity result for the boundary trace of
nonlocal minimal surfaces, and even the Lipschitz regularity (which is
a subcase of Theorem
1.1
) is new.

The methodology introduced to prove Theorem
1.1
will allow us
to obtain the differentiability of nonlocal minimal graphs at points
of boundary continuity, according to the following result:

Theorem 1.2

(Differentiability of nonlocal minimal graphs at points of boundary continuity)
.

Let
B^{\prime}_{\rho}
be the ball of radius
\rho
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, centered at the origin.

Assume that
\Omega\cap B_{1}^{\prime}=\{x_{n}>0\}\cap B_{1}^{\prime}
, that
u_{0}=0
in
\{x_{n}<0\}\cap B_{1}^{\prime}
,
and that

(1.3)

\lim_{\Omega\ni x^{\prime}\to 0}u_{E}(x^{\prime})=0.

Then, there exist
C>0
and
r\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)
such that, for all
x^{\prime}\in B_{r}^{\prime}
,

(1.4)

|u_{E}(x^{\prime})|\leqslant C|x^{\prime}|^{\frac{3+s}{2}}.

We remark that assumption (
1.3
) says that the origin
is a boundary continuity point for the nonlocal minimal set
E
. The thesis in (
1.4
) gives that the nonlocal minimal graph
u_{E}

is differentiable, and actually of class
C^{1,\frac{1+s}{2}}
, at this continuity point.

This kind of results is intriguing, since it gives that “boundary continuity implies
boundary differentiablity”. Theorem
1.2

was proved in
[
12
, Theorem 1.4]
when
n=2
. As such,
Theorem
1.2
completes this investigation
in all dimensions, as requested in
[
12
, Open Problem 1.7]
.

Also, Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
imply that the boundary trace of nonlocal
minimal graphs do not exhibit vertical tangents
along
\partial{\mathcal{C}}
. This solves a question posed in
[
12
, Open Problem 1.6]
.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
relies on suitable estimates
on the ratio between tangential components of the normal
and the vertical component (for this, the graph property of the minimal set
is helpful, guaranteeing that the vertical component of the normal is positive,
avoiding zero divisors in this ratio).

The argument to prove Theorem
1.1
can actually be conveniently
split into two separate steps: first (see Section
2
),
one obtains a uniform bound on the above
ratio (corresponding to a Lipschitz regularity in Theorem
1.1
);
then (see Section
3
),
one uses this result to obtain an enhanced
estimate on the oscillation of the above ratio
(this entails differentiable regularity
in Theorem
1.1
, with a Hölder control of the oscillation
leading to the desired
C^{1,\gamma}
-regularity).

Once Theorem
1.1
is established, Theorem
1.2

follows from the methods introduced in
[
12
]

and the boundary Harnack inequalities developed in this paper
(see Section
4
).

Indeed, the crucial ingredient in the bounds on the above normal ratio
consists in two new
geometric boundary Harnack inequalities.
The reason for which boundary Harnack inequalities
play a role in this setting is that,
in the vicinity of a stickiness point, the tangential components
of the normal vanish when the normal becomes horizontal (see Figure
2
)
and the significance of the boundary Harnack inequalities
is to guarantee that the denominator of this ratio detaches
from zero sufficiently fast, due to the presence of regions in which the
hypersurface is a “nice graph”.

Figure 2.
Sketch of the exterior normal of a nonlocal minimal graph in the vicinity
of a point of stickiness.

The first geometric boundary Harnack inequality will be presented in Lemma
2.3
and
its key feature is to obtain a uniform bound on the ratio
without any assumption on the set where the denominator vanishes.
This corresponds to a Lipschitz regularity for the trace of nonlocal
minimal graphs at points of stickiness, without any initial assumption.

The second geometric boundary Harnack inequality will be presented in Lemma
3.4
.
This one leverages the result of the first, since it assumes that
the set where the denominator vanishes is already of Lipschitz class
(or, more generally, that one can find positivity sets for the denominator
at any scale with a linear structure). With this additional hypothesis,
the second geometric boundary Harnack inequality provides a Hölder control
of the oscillation of the normal ratio. This corresponds to the
C^{1,\gamma}
-regularity
for the trace of nonlocal
minimal graphs at points of stickiness, relying on the Lipschitz regularity that
was established in the previous step.

In fact, the terminology “boundary
inequality” is perhaps too belittling, since we will not need to assume that the solution vanishes at
the point where these inequalities are centered. In this spirit, what we actually establish
are Harnack inequalities in a geometric framework that are uniform up to the boundary
(and thus oscillation estimates for the ratio of the normal that are uniform up to the boundary).

Concerning the existing literature on boundary Harnack inequalities in the nonlocal setting, we recall that the first result of this type was established in
[
3
]
for the fractional Laplacian in Lipschitz domains. This was later extended to arbitrary open sets in
[
18
,
1
]
, and to symmetric Markov jump processes in
[
2
]
. The case of nonlocal operators in non-divergence form was first addressed in
[
16
]
, under suitable homogeneity assumptions on the interaction kernel and assuming that the domain is of class
C^{1}
. Subsequently, in
[
17
]
, boundary Harnack inequalities were proved for non-divergence form operators in general domains. The results of
[
17
]
have played a pivotal role in the study of the regularity of free boundary obstacle problems, see
[
8
]
.

Among the various proof techniques developed in the literature, the approach in
[
17
]
is arguably the closest to the one adopted here. Indeed, it is based on a general strategy consisting of two main steps: a pointwise bound in general domains, combined with a Hölder estimate in Lipschitz domains. Nevertheless, several important differences arise between
the framework of
[
17
]
and that of the present paper. First, the analysis in
[
17
]
is carried out in the Euclidean setting, whereas here we work on a hypersurface. As a consequence of this geometric context, the interaction kernel considered in
[
17
]
is symmetric, while the kernel studied here is not necessarily symmetric. Moreover, the framework in
[
17
]
is tailored to pairs of solutions satisfying the same homogeneous equation, or equations whose right-hand side is modeled on the coefficients of suitable linear combinations of the solutions. In contrast, in the present setting the right-hand side is typically non-homogeneous and must incorporate the specific terms arising from the geometric equations under consideration.

We now dive into the technical details needed to prove the main results of this paper.

\displaystyle\qquad=g(x)\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap B_{1}}\frac{f(y)-f(x)}{|x-y|^{n+1+s}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}-\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap B_{1}}\frac{(fg)(y)-(fg)(x)}{|x-y|^{n+1+s}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap B_{1}}\frac{f(y)(g(x)-g(y))}{|x-y|^{n+1+s}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap B_{1}}\frac{(f(y)-f(x))(g(x)-g(y))}{|x-y|^{n+1+s}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}+f(x)\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap B_{1}}\frac{g(x)-g(y)}{|x-y|^{n+1+s}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}.

From this, (
2.6
) and (
2.10
),
the desired result follows.
∎

2.2.
A geometric boundary Harnack inequality

We provide here a boundary Harnack inequality
of nonlocal type in a geometric setting, that will be used to deduce a Lipschitz control on
\Sigma
in the vicinity
of boundary discontinuity points.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
c_{0}>0
, and
C_{0}
,
C_{1}\geqslant 0
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a bounded, connected hypersurface of class
C^{1,\alpha}

such that
0\in{\mathcal{S}}
. Let
{\mathcal{D}}
be an open subset of
{\mathcal{S}}
(in its relative topology).

Consider a kernel
{\mathcal{K}}:{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{S}}\to[0,+\infty]

such that

(2.11)

\inf_{|x-y|\leqslant 2}{\mathcal{K}}(x,y)\geqslant c_{0}

and the linear operator (in the Cauchy principal value sense)

(2.12)

{\mathcal{L}}f(x):=\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap B_{1}}\big(f(y)-f(x)\big)\,{\mathcal{K}}(x,y)\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y},

satisfying, for all
f\in C^{1,\alpha}({\mathcal{S}}\cap B_{1})
,

(2.13)

|{\mathcal{L}}f|\leqslant C_{1}\,\|f\|_{C^{1,\alpha}({\mathcal{S}}\cap B_{1})}.

Let
u
,
v:{\mathcal{S}}\to\mathbb{R}
, with
u\geqslant 0
and
v\leqslant 1
in
{\mathcal{S}}\cap B_{1}
.

Assume that

(2.14)

({\mathcal{S}}\setminus{\mathcal{D}})\cap B_{1}\subseteq\{u=0\}\cap\{v=0\}.

Assume also that

(2.15)

{\mathcal{L}}u\leqslant\ell u
and
{\mathcal{L}}v\geqslant\ell v-C_{0}
in
{\mathcal{D}}\cap B_{1/2}
,

for some
\ell\in L^{\infty}({\mathcal{S}}\cap B_{1})
.

Suppose in addition that there exist
\rho_{0}\in(0,1)
,

p_{0}\in{\mathcal{S}}\cap(B_{1}\setminus B_{\rho_{0}})
,

r_{0}\in\left(0,\frac{1-|p_{0}|-\rho_{0}}{8}\right)
, and
\delta_{0}>0
such that

(2.16)

\inf_{B_{r_{0}}(p_{0})}u\geqslant\delta_{0}.

Then,
there exists
C>0
, depending only on
n
,
\alpha
,
{\mathcal{L}}
, the
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity of
{\mathcal{S}}
,
c_{0}
,
C_{0}
,
C_{1}
,
\|\ell\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathcal{S}}\cap B_{1})}
,

\rho_{0}
,
r_{0}
,
and
\delta_{0}
(and independend of
{\mathcal{D}}
), such that

v\leqslant Cu{\mbox{ in }}{\mathcal{S}}\cap B_{\rho_{0}/2}.

Proof.

We let
\delta\in\left(0,\frac{\delta_{0}}{2}\right)
, to be chosen conveniently small in what follows, and
\eta:=u-\delta v
.
We stress that
\eta\geqslant-\delta
in
{\mathcal{S}}\cap B_{1}
.
Let also
\rho\in\left(0,\frac{\rho_{0}}{2}\right]
.
Suppose that there exists
p\in{\mathcal{S}}\cap B_{\rho}
such that
\eta(p)<0

and, given
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, consider the family of functions

{\mathcal{S}}\ni x\longmapsto\phi_{\lambda}(x):=-\frac{64\delta}{(\rho-\rho_{0})^{2}}|x-p|^{2}-\lambda.

Notice that
\phi_{\lambda}\leqslant-\lambda<-\delta\leqslant\eta
in
{\mathcal{S}}\cap B_{1}
as long as
\lambda>\delta
, but
\phi_{\lambda}(p)=-\lambda>0>\eta(p)
as long as
\lambda<0
.

Hence, we can choose
\lambda\in[0,\delta]
such that
\phi_{\lambda}\leqslant\eta
in
{\mathcal{S}}\cap B_{1}

and there exists
q\in{\mathcal{S}}\cap B_{1}
such that
\phi_{\lambda}(q)=\eta(q)
.

We have that
q\in{\mathcal{D}}
, otherwise, by (
2.14
),
one would have that
u(q)=v(q)=0
, and so
\eta(q)=0
, yielding that
\phi_{\lambda}(q)=0
; but this would give that
\lambda=0
and
q=p
,
against the condition that
\eta(p)<0
.

We also stress that
q\in\overline{B_{(\rho-\rho_{0})/8}(p)}\subseteq B_{\rho}
, because

\frac{64\delta}{(\rho-\rho_{0})^{2}}|q-p|^{2}\leqslant\frac{64\delta}{(\rho-\rho_{0})^{2}}|q-p|^{2}+\lambda=-\phi_{\lambda}(q)=-\eta(q)\leqslant\delta.

Thus, setting
\psi_{\lambda}:=\eta-\phi_{\lambda}
, we have that
\psi_{\lambda}\geqslant 0=\psi_{\lambda}(q)

in
{\mathcal{S}}\cap B_{1}
, whence, recalling (
2.11

(2.17)

\begin{split}&c_{0}\int_{\mathcal{S}\cap B_{r_{0}}(p_{0})}\psi_{\lambda}(y)\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}\leqslant\int_{\mathcal{S}\cap B_{r_{0}}(p_{0})}\psi_{\lambda}(y)\,{\mathcal{K}}(q,y)\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}\\
&\qquad\leqslant\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap B_{1}}\psi_{\lambda}(y)\,{\mathcal{K}}(q,y)\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}={\mathcal{L}}\psi_{\lambda}(q)\leqslant\ell(q)\eta(q)+C_{0}\delta+|{\mathcal{L}}\phi_{\lambda}(q)|.\end{split}

Now we point out that

(2.18)

|\eta(q)|=|\phi_{\lambda}(q)|\leqslant\frac{64\delta}{(\rho-\rho_{0})^{2}}|q-p|^{2}+\lambda\leqslant\delta+\delta=2\delta.

Notice also that, in view of (
2.13
), for all
x\in{\mathcal{S}}\cap B_{1}
,

\frac{(\rho-\rho_{0})^{2}\,{|\mathcal{L}}\phi_{\lambda}(x)|}{64\delta}\leqslant\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap B_{1}}\big||x-p|^{2}-|y-p|^{2}\big|\,{\mathcal{K}}(x,y)\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}\leqslant C_{2},

for some
C_{2}
proportional to
C_{1}

(the proportionality factor depending on
n
,
\alpha
, and the
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity of
{\mathcal{S}}
).

This, (
2.17
), and (
2.18
) lead to

(2.19)

\int_{\mathcal{S}\cap B_{r_{0}}(p_{0})}\psi_{\lambda}(y)\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}\leqslant C_{3}\delta,

for some
C_{3}>0
.

Now we observe that, for all
x\in{\mathcal{S}}\cap B_{1}
,
we have that
\phi_{\lambda}(x)\leqslant 0
. Hence, we
recall (
2.16
) and we see that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}\cap B_{r_{0}}(p_{0})}\psi_{\lambda}(y)\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}\geqslant\int_{\mathcal{S}\cap B_{r_{0}}(p_{0})}\eta(y)\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}\geqslant\int_{\mathcal{S}\cap B_{r_{0}}(p_{0})}(\delta_{0}-\delta)\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}

\displaystyle\qquad\geqslant\frac{\delta_{0}}{2}{\mathcal{H}}^{n}\big(\mathcal{S}\cap B_{r_{0}}(p_{0})\big)=:C_{4}.

Combining this with (
2.19
), it follows that
\delta\geqslant\frac{C_{4}}{C_{3}}
.
This condition is violated if we choose
\delta
sufficiently small, thus showing that

in
{\mathcal{S}}\cap B_{\rho}
as long as
\delta
is small enough.
∎

3.1.
Weighted averages on annuli

An essential ingredient to prove the
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity of the boundary trace
is to control the weighted averages of the vertical component of the normal
of the surface on concentric annuli. To this end, we present the following result:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\alpha\in(s,1]
and
{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a bounded, connected hypersurface of class
C^{1,\alpha}

such that
0\in{\mathcal{S}}
.

Given a set
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, let

{\mathcal{L}}_{V}f(x):=\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap V}\frac{f(y)-f(x)}{|x-y|^{n+1+s}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}.

Let
u:{\mathcal{S}}\to\mathbb{R}
, with
u\geqslant 0
in
{\mathcal{S}}\cap B_{2}
.

Assume that

(3.1)

{\mathcal{L}}_{B_{1}}u\leqslant Mu\quad{\mbox{ in }}{\mathcal{S}}\cap B_{1}\cap\{u>0\},

for some
M>0
.

Assume additionally that there exists
\delta\in\left(0,\frac{1}{10}\right)
such that,
for every
r\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)
,

there exists
z_{r}\in{\mathcal{S}}\cap(B_{9r/10}\setminus B_{7r/10})

such that
{\mathcal{S}}\cap B_{\delta r}(z_{r})\subseteq\{u>0\}
.

Then,
there exist
r_{\star}\in(0,1)
,
depending only on
n
,

s
,
\alpha
,

{{\mathcal{S}}}
,
\delta
, and
M
, and
C_{\star}>0
,
depending only on
n
,

s
,
\alpha
,

{{\mathcal{S}}}
, and
\delta
(but independent of
M
), such that, for all
r\in(0,r_{\star}]
,

(3.2)

\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap(B_{1}\setminus B_{r})}\frac{u(y)}{|y|^{n+1+s}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}\leqslant C_{\star}\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap(B_{r}\setminus B_{r/2})}\frac{u(y)}{|y|^{n+1+s}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}.

Proof.

Let
\zeta\in{\mathcal{S}}\cap B_{\delta r/10}(z_{r})
.
Given
\lambda\in[0,+\infty)
, we define

\begin{split}P_{\lambda}(x)=\begin{cases}\lambda\left(\frac{\delta^{2}r^{2}}{4}-|x-\zeta|^{2}\right)&{\mbox{ if }}x\in B_{\delta r}(\zeta),\\
-u(\zeta)&{\mbox{ if }}x\in{\mathcal{S}}\setminus B_{\delta r}(\zeta).\end{cases}\end{split}

When
\lambda=0
, we have that
P_{\lambda}\leqslant u
in
{\mathcal{S}}\cap B_{1}
.
Hence, we can take the largest
\lambda\geqslant 0

for which
P_{\lambda}\leqslant u
in
{\mathcal{S}}\cap B_{1}
.
This produces a touching point
x_{\star}\in B_{\delta r/2}(\zeta)
.

As a result,

\begin{split}&{\mathcal{L}}_{B_{1}}(u-P_{\lambda})(x_{\star})=\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap B_{1}}\frac{(u-P_{\lambda})(y)}{|x_{\star}-y|^{n+1+s}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}\geqslant\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap(B_{1}\setminus B_{r})}\frac{(u-P_{\lambda})(y)}{|x_{\star}-y|^{n+1+s}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\geqslant\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap(B_{1}\setminus B_{r})}\frac{u(y)}{|x_{\star}-y|^{n+1+s}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}.\end{split}

Since, when
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus B_{r}
,

|x_{\star}-y|\leqslant|x_{\star}-\zeta|+|\zeta|+|y|\leqslant\frac{\delta r}{2}+\frac{r}{2}+|y|\leqslant r+|y|\leqslant 2|y|,

we conclude that

(3.3)

{\mathcal{L}}_{B_{1}}(u-P_{\lambda})(x_{\star})\geqslant\frac{1}{2^{n+1+s}}\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap(B_{1}\setminus B_{r})}\frac{u(y)}{|y|^{n+1+s}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}.

It is useful to observe that

(3.4)

\frac{\lambda\delta^{2}r^{2}}{4}=P_{\lambda}(\zeta)\leqslant u(\zeta).

Now we define

g(x):=P_{\lambda}\left(x_{\star}+\frac{\delta rx}{10}\right)

and we remark that
{\mathcal{S}}_{r}:=\frac{10({\mathcal{S}}-x_{\star})}{\delta r}

is a zoom-in of
{\mathcal{S}}
(hence, its
local
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity is controlled by the one of
{\mathcal{S}}
).

Thus, recalling (
2.10
) and footnote
1
,

(3.5)

\begin{split}&|{\mathcal{L}}_{B_{\delta r/10}(x_{\star})}P_{\lambda}(x_{\star})|=\left|\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap B_{\delta r/10}(x_{\star})}\frac{P_{\lambda}(y)-P_{\lambda}(x_{\star})}{|x_{\star}-y|^{n+1+s}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}\right|\\
&\quad=\frac{10^{1+s}}{\delta^{1+s}r^{1+s}}\left|\int_{{\mathcal{S}}_{r}\cap B_{1}}\frac{g(x)-g(0)}{|x|^{n+1+s}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{x}\right|\leqslant\frac{C_{0}\,[g]_{C^{1,1}(B_{1})}}{\delta^{1+s}r^{1+s}}\\
&\quad\leqslant\frac{C_{0}\,\delta^{2}\,r^{2}\,[P_{\lambda}]_{C^{1,1}(B_{\delta r/10}(x_{\star}))}}{\delta^{1+s}r^{1+s}}\leqslant\frac{C_{0}\,\delta^{2}\,r^{2}\,[P_{\lambda}]_{C^{1,1}(B_{3\delta r/5}(\zeta))}}{\delta^{1+s}r^{1+s}}\\
&\quad\leqslant\frac{C_{0}\,\lambda\delta^{2}r^{2}}{\delta^{1+s}r^{1+s}},\end{split}

for some
C_{0}>0
depending only on
n
,
s
, and
{{\mathcal{S}}}
,
and possibly varying at each step of the calculation.

Therefore, in light of (
3.4
),

(3.6)

|{\mathcal{L}}_{B_{\delta r/10}(x_{\star})}P_{\lambda}(x_{\star})|\leqslant\frac{C_{0}\,u(\zeta)}{\delta^{1+s}r^{1+s}}.

Furthermore, for all
y\in{\mathcal{S}}\cap B_{1}
, recalling (
3.4
) we have that

\displaystyle-P_{\lambda}(y)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\lambda\delta^{2}r^{2}&{\mbox{ if }}y\in B_{\delta r}(\zeta),\\
u(\zeta)&{\mbox{ if }}x\in{\mathcal{S}}\cap(B_{1}\setminus B_{\delta r}(\zeta))\end{cases}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\begin{cases}4u(\zeta)&{\mbox{ if }}y\in B_{\delta r}(\zeta),\\
u(\zeta)&{\mbox{ if }}x\in{\mathcal{S}}\cap(B_{1}\setminus B_{\delta r}(\zeta))\end{cases}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle 4u(\zeta).

As a consequence,

\begin{split}&{\mathcal{L}}_{B_{1}\setminus B_{\delta r/10}(x_{\star})}P_{\lambda}(x_{\star})\\
&\quad=\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap(B_{1}\setminus B_{\delta r/10}(x_{\star}))}\frac{P_{\lambda}(y)-P_{\lambda}(x_{\star})}{|x_{\star}-y|^{n+1+s}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}\\
&\quad\geqslant-(4u(\zeta)+P_{\lambda}(x_{\star}))\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap(B_{1}\setminus B_{\delta r/10}(x_{\star}))}\frac{d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}}{|x_{\star}-y|^{n+1+s}}\\
&\quad=-(4u(\zeta)+u(x_{\star}))\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap(B_{1}\setminus B_{\delta r/10}(x_{\star}))}\frac{d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}}{|x_{\star}-y|^{n+1+s}}\\
&\quad\geqslant-\frac{C_{0}\,(u(\zeta)+u(x_{\star}))}{\delta^{1+s}r^{1+s}}.\end{split}

Combining this observation and (
3.6
), we get that

{\mathcal{L}}_{B_{1}}P_{\lambda}(x_{\star})\geqslant-\frac{C_{0}\,(u(\zeta)+u(x_{\star}))}{\delta^{1+s}r^{1+s}}.

From this, (
3.1
),
and (
3.3
), it follows that

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2^{n+1+s}}\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap(B_{1}\setminus B_{r})}\frac{u(y)}{|y|^{n+1+s}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}-\frac{C_{0}\,(u(\zeta)+u(x_{\star}))}{\delta^{1+s}r^{1+s}}\\
&\quad\leqslant{\mathcal{L}}_{B_{1}}(u-P_{\lambda})(x_{\star})+{\mathcal{L}}_{B_{1}}P_{\lambda}(x_{\star})\\
&\quad={\mathcal{L}}_{B_{1}}u(x_{\star})\\
&\quad\leqslant M\,u(x_{\star}).\end{split}

We thereby conclude that

\frac{1}{2^{n+1+s}}\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap(B_{1}\setminus B_{r})}\frac{u(y)}{|y|^{n+1+s}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}\leqslant\frac{(M\delta^{1+s}r^{1+s}+C_{0})\,(u(\zeta)+u(x_{\star}))}{\delta^{1+s}r^{1+s}}.

Notice also that

u(x_{\star})=P_{\lambda}(x_{\star})\leqslant P_{\lambda}(\zeta)\leqslant u(\zeta),

leading to

\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap(B_{1}\setminus B_{r})}\frac{u(y)}{|y|^{n+1+s}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}\leqslant\frac{2^{n+2+s}(M\delta^{1+s}r^{1+s}+C_{0})\,u(\zeta)}{\delta^{1+s}r^{1+s}}.

Hence, integrating over
\zeta\in{\mathcal{S}}\cap B_{\delta r/10}(z_{r})
,

\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap(B_{1}\setminus B_{r})}\frac{u(y)}{|y|^{n+1+s}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}\leqslant\frac{C_{\star}(M\delta^{1+s}r^{1+s}+C_{0})}{\delta^{n+1+s}r^{n+1+s}}\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap B_{\delta r/10}(z_{r})}u(\zeta)\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{\zeta},

which, up to renaming
C_{\star}
, gives the desired result,
since
{\mathcal{S}}\cap B_{\delta r/10}(z_{r})\subseteq(B_{r}\setminus B_{r/2})\cap\{u>0\}
.
∎

The estimate in (
3.2
) is useful
in itself, since it implies that
weighted averages on annuli of radius of order
r

grow at least as a negative power of

. More precisely, we have that:

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume that there exist
r_{\star}\in(0,1)
and
C_{\star}>0
such that (

) holds true for all
r\in(0,r_{\star}]
.

Then, there exist

,
\beta>0
, depending only on

, such that,
for all
r\in(0,r_{\star}]
,

\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap(B_{r}\setminus B_{r/2})}\frac{u(y)}{|y|^{n+1+s}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}\geqslant\frac{c\,r_{\star}^{\beta}}{r^{\beta}}\,\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap(B_{1}\setminus B_{1/2})}\frac{u(y)}{|y|^{n+1+s}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}.

Proof.

Let
\omega\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right)
, to be conveniently chosen below.
We define
r_{k}:=\frac{\omega\,r_{\star}}{2^{k}}
and

a_{k}:=\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap(B_{r_{k}}\setminus B_{r_{k}/2})}\frac{u(y)}{|y|^{n+1+s}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}.

We remark that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}a_{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap(B_{\omega r_{\star}/2^{i}}\setminus B_{\omega r_{\star}/2^{i+1}})}\frac{u(y)}{|y|^{n+1+s}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap(B_{\omega r_{\star}}\setminus B_{\omega r_{\star}/2^{k}})}\frac{u(y)}{|y|^{n+1+s}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}=\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap(B_{\omega r_{\star}}\setminus B_{r_{k}})}\frac{u(y)}{|y|^{n+1+s}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}

and therefore, by (
3.2
)
(used here with
r:=r_{k}
),

(3.7)

\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}a_{i}\leqslant\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap(B_{1}\setminus B_{r_{k}})}\frac{u(y)}{|y|^{n+1+s}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}\leqslant C_{\star}\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap(B_{r_{k}}\setminus B_{r_{k}/2})}\frac{u(y)}{|y|^{n+1+s}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}=C_{\star}\,a_{k}.

As a result,

(C_{\star}+1)\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}a_{i}\leqslant C_{\star}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}a_{i}+C_{\star}\,a_{k}=C_{\star}\sum_{i=0}^{k}a_{i}.

Thus, setting

S_{k}:=\sum_{i=0}^{k}a_{i}\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad C:=\frac{C_{\star}+1}{C_{\star}}\in(1,+\infty),

we have that
S_{k}\geqslant CS_{k-1}
, leading to
S_{k}\geqslant C^{k-1}\,S_{1}
.

Then, defining
\beta:=\frac{\ln C}{\ln 2}
and using again (
3.7
),

\displaystyle\frac{2^{k\beta}}{C^{2}\,(C_{\star}+1)\,\omega^{\beta}}\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap(B_{\omega/2}\setminus B_{\omega r_{\star}/4})}\frac{u(y)}{|y|^{n+1+s}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\frac{2^{k\beta}C^{\frac{\ln\omega}{\ln 2}}}{C\,(C_{\star}+1)\,\omega^{\beta}}\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap(B_{\omega r_{\star}/2}\setminus B_{\omega r_{\star}/4})}\frac{u(y)}{|y|^{n+1+s}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{2^{k\beta}C^{\frac{\ln\omega}{\ln 2}}a_{1}}{C\,(C_{\star}+1)\,\omega^{\beta}}\leqslant\frac{2^{k\beta}C^{\frac{\ln\omega}{\ln 2}}S_{1}}{C\,(C_{\star}+1)\,\omega^{\beta}}=\frac{C^{k-1}\,S_{1}}{C_{\star}+1}\leqslant\frac{S_{k}}{C_{\star}+1}

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1}{C_{\star}+1}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}a_{i}+a_{k}\right)\leqslant a_{k}=\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap(B_{\omega r_{\star}/2^{k}}\setminus B_{\omega r_{\star}/2^{k+1}})}\frac{u(y)}{|y|^{n+1+s}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}.

Hence, picking
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\frac{r_{\star}}{2^{k+1}}\leqslant r<\frac{r_{\star}}{2^{k}}
and taking
\omega:=\frac{2^{k}r}{{r_{\star}}}\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right)
, we find that

(3.8)

\frac{{r_{\star}^{\beta}}}{C^{2}\,(C_{\star}+1)\,r^{\beta}}\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap(B_{\omega{r_{\star}}/2}\setminus B_{\omega{r_{\star}}/4})}\frac{u(y)}{|y|^{n+1+s}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}\leqslant\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap(B_{r}\setminus B_{r/2})}\frac{u(y)}{|y|^{n+1+s}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}.

Also, by (
3.2
) (used here with
r:=\frac{\omega{r_{\star}}}{2}
),

\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap(B_{1}\setminus B_{\omega{r_{\star}}/2})}\frac{u(y)}{|y|^{n+1+s}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}\leqslant C_{\star}\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap(B_{\omega{r_{\star}}/2}\setminus B_{\omega{r_{\star}}/4})}\frac{u(y)}{|y|^{n+1+s}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}.

Combining this with (
3.8
), we find that

\frac{{r_{\star}^{\beta}}}{C^{2}\,C_{\star}\,(C_{\star}+1)\,r^{\beta}}\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap(B_{1}\setminus B_{\omega{r_{\star}}/2})}\frac{u(y)}{|y|^{n+1+s}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}\leqslant\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap(B_{r}\setminus B_{r/2})}\frac{u(y)}{|y|^{n+1+s}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}.

Since

, and thus

,
the inequality above
leads to the desired result.
∎

To accomplish this plan, a useful step is supplied by the following simple

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\alpha\in(s,1]
and
{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+1}

such that

For any

\begin{split}&\int_{{\mathcal{S}}_{r}\cap B_{1}}\frac{f_{r}(y)-f_{r}(x)}{|x-y|^{n+1+s}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}=r^{1+s}\Bigg[\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap B_{1}}\frac{f(y)-f(rx)}{|rx-y|^{n+1+s}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}\\
&\qquad\qquad-\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap(B_{1}\setminus B_{r})}\frac{f(y)}{|rx-y|^{n+1+s}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}+f(rx)\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap(B_{1}\setminus B_{r})}\frac{d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}}{|rx-y|^{n+1+s}}\Bigg].\end{split}

Proof.

We use the notation
X:=rx
and
Y:=rY
and we see that

\begin{split}\int_{{\mathcal{S}}_{r}\cap B_{1}}\frac{f_{r}(y)-f_{r}(x)}{|x-y|^{n+1+s}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}&=\int_{{\mathcal{S}}_{r}\cap B_{1}}\frac{f(ry)-f(rx)}{|x-y|^{n+1+s}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{y}\\
&=r^{1+s}\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\cap B_{r}}\frac{f(Y)-f(X)}{|X-Y|^{n+1+s}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{Y}.\end{split}

This leads to the desired result.
∎

4.

1.2

As discussed in
[

When

Appendix A
From the ratio of normal components to the regularity of the boundary trace

For completeness, we present here an auxiliary result, used in the proof of Theorem
1.1

to obtain the regularity of the trace
of the nonlocal minimal surface
\Sigma
along
\partial{\mathcal{C}}
.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be a hypersurface of class
C^{1}
with exterior normal
\nu

and suppose that
{\mathcal{S}}\cap\overline{\mathcal{C}}
corresponds to the graph
\{x_{n+1}=u(x^{\prime})
,

x^{\prime}\in\Omega\}
, with
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
uniformly continuous.

Let
\Gamma:=\{(x^{\prime},u(x^{\prime}))
,
x^{\prime}\in\partial\Omega\}
.

Suppose that
p\in\Gamma
and that
\frac{\tau\cdot\nu}{\nu_{n+1}}\in L^{q}({\mathcal{S}}\cap{\mathcal{C}}\cap B_{\rho}(p))
, for every vector field
\tau
of class
C^{1,1}
that is tangent
to
\partial{\mathcal{C}}
,
for
some
q\in[1,+\infty]
and
\rho>0
.

Then,
\Gamma\cap B_{\rho}(p)
is of class
W^{1,q}
.

Similarly, if
\frac{\tau\cdot\nu}{\nu_{n+1}}\in C^{k,\alpha}({\mathcal{S}}\cap{\mathcal{C}}\cap B_{\rho}(p))
, for every
\tau
as above and some
k\in\{0,1\}
,
\alpha\in(0,1]
and
\rho>0
, then
\Gamma\cap B_{\rho}(p)
is of class
C^{k+1,\alpha}
.

Proof.

Up to a covering in local charts, we can suppose that
\Omega\cap B_{\rho}(p)
can be written as
\Phi(U\times(0,1))
, for some domain
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n-1}

and some injection
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
of class
C^{2,1}
, with
\Phi(U\times\{0\})=\partial\Omega
.

For every
x\in\partial{\mathcal{C}}
, we know that
x^{\prime}\in\partial\Omega
.
Hence, for all
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, we define
\tau(x):=(\partial_{j}\Phi(\underline{x}),0)
,
where
\underline{x}:=\Phi^{-1}(x^{\prime})
,
and we stress that
\tau
is tangent
to
\partial{\mathcal{C}}
.

Moreover,
\nu(x)=\frac{(-\nabla u(x^{\prime}),1)}{\sqrt{|\nabla u(x^{\prime})|^{2}+1}}
, for all
x\in{\mathcal{S}}\cap{\mathcal{C}}
(but some care is needed when
x\in{\mathcal{S}}\cap\partial{\mathcal{C}}
, since the gradient of
u

may blow up there).

This gives that, for all
x\in{\mathcal{S}}\cap{\mathcal{C}}\cap B_{\rho}(p)
,

-\frac{\tau(x)\cdot\nu(x)}{\nu_{n+1}(x)}=\nabla u(x^{\prime})\cdot\partial_{j}\Phi(\underline{x})=\partial_{j}(u\circ\Phi)(\underline{x}).

The identity above gives that the (local) regularity of
u\circ\Phi

is one derivative better than the (local)
regularity of
\frac{\tau\cdot\nu}{\nu_{n+1}}
.
Since
(\Phi,u\circ\Phi)\big|_{U\times\{0\}}
is a parameterization for
\Gamma
, the desired result follows.

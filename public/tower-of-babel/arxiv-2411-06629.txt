A dual-pairing summation-by-parts finite difference framework for nonlinear conservation laws
Dougal Stewart, Nathan Lee, Kenneth Duru

Abstract
Robust and convergent high-order numerical methods for solving partial differential equations are highly attractive due to their efficiency on modern and next-generation hardware architectures. However, designing such methods for nonlinear hyperbolic conservation laws remains a significant challenge. In this work, we introduce a framework based on dual-pairing (DP) and upwind summation-by-parts (SBP) finite difference (FD) and discontinuous Galerkin (DG) finite element methods, aimed at achieving accurate and robust numerical approximations of nonlinear conservation laws. The framework ensures entropy consistency and features an intrinsic high-order accurate "filter" designed to detect and resolve regions where the solution is poorly captured or discontinuities are present. The DP SBP FD/DG operators form a dual pair of discrete derivative operators that collectively preserve the SBP property. Furthermore, these operators are constructed to be upwind, allowing them to incorporate dissipation within the elements themselves. This contrasts with traditional SBP and collocated DG spectral element methods, which typically induce dissipation solely through numerical fluxes at element interfaces. Our framework facilitates the systematic combination of DP SBP FD/DG operators with skew-symmetric and upwind flux splitting techniques. This integration enables the development of robust, high-order accurate schemes for nonlinear hyperbolic conservation laws. The resulting semi-discrete formulation is provably entropy-stable for arbitrary nonlinear problems. We illustrate the effectiveness of our approach with specific examples, including inviscid Burgers’ equation, nonlinear shallow water equations, and the compressible Euler equations of gas dynamics. Extensive numerical experiments are presented to verify the accuracy and demonstrate the robustness of our framework.

2.1
Conservation Laws

We are concerned with the numerical approximation of solutions
\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x},t)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
to PDEs of the form

\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x},t)+\divergence\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x},t))=0,\quad\mathbf{x}\in\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad t\in[0,T],

(1)

where
\mathbf{u}:\Omega\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
with
m\geq 1
is the unknown solution,
\mathbf{f}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
is the flux function of the PDE,
d\geq 1
denotes the spatial dimension,
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the spatial variable and
t\in[0,T]
denotes time and
T>0
is the final time. We set the smooth initial condition
\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x},0)=\mathbf{u}_{0}(\mathbf{x})\in C^{1}(\Omega)
.
Such PDEs are known as conservation laws and often appear in science and engineering where
\mathbf{u}
is a set of conserved variables which may denote some physical quantity such as velocity, momentum, mass, pressure, density, energy, magnetic/electric field, etc. Conservation laws are aptly named because they state that the total quantity of
\mathbf{u}
is conserved. This can be understood by integrating (
1
) and then applying the divergence theorem,

\partial_{t}\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}(t)=-\oint_{\partial\Omega}\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x},t))\cdot\mathbf{n}\differential{\vb x},\quad\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}(t)=\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x},t).

(2)

That is, a conservation law essentially states that the change in the total quantity
\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}(t)
in any given region of space
\Omega
is governed by the flux
\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{u})\cdot\mathbf{n}
going in and out of the boundary
\partial\Omega
, where
\mathbf{n}
is the outward unit normal on the boundary. In particular, for continuous periodic solutions in
\Omega
the total quantity
\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}(t)
is conserved, that is
\partial_{t}\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}(t)=0
for all
t\geq 0
. For nonlinear hyperbolic conservation laws with weak solutions, the conservative properties of a numerical method are critical for the convergence of numerical solutions
[
3
]
.

In this work, we will focus on initial value problems (IVP) driven by initial conditions or internal forcing for systems of nonlinear hyperbolic conservation laws. We will consider one and two space dimensions and assume periodic boundaries such that the boundary integral in the right-hand side of (
2
) vanishes.
However, our framework can be extended to an arbitrary number of space dimensions. Well-posed non-periodic boundary conditions can be implemented, for example, using penalty methods or numerical fluxes
[
54
,
55
,
10
,
41
]
. That said, it is important to note that the numerical treatment of boundary conditions is problem specific and comes with additional theoretical and practical difficulties, in particular for nonlinear conservation laws. Therefore, the formulation of well-posed boundary conditions for nonlinear conservation laws and their numerical treatments are beyond the scope of the present study.

2.2
Entropy stability

Hyperbolic conservation laws often develop discontinuities, like shocks or contact discontinuities, even from smooth initial data, due to a loss of derivatives. These discontinuities make classical derivatives undefined, so solutions are understood in a weak sense

One such criterion involves physical principles. For instance, solutions to the compressible Euler equations must satisfy the second law of thermodynamics, meaning the total entropy should not decrease in a closed system. This leads to the entropy inequality—a PDE constraint ensuring physically meaningful solutions. A physically correct weak solution must satisfy this entropy condition for all convex entropy functions

\displaystyle=0\iff\mathbf{g}^{\top}\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{g}^{\top}\partial_{\mathbf{u}}\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{u})\cdot\partial_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u}=0.

Note that

Using the fact

\displaystyle=0.

(3)

Indeed, the entropy satisfies a conservation law, and by integrating (

2.3
Skew-symmetric splitting

Typically, a skew-symmetric form is found by splitting the divergence of the flux, which is really finding a convex combination of the nonlinear conservation laws in flux form and the quasi-linear form so that the energy method can be applied without the chain rule and/or the product rule. For a scalar PDE flux

\partial_{x}f(u)=(1-\alpha)\partial_{x}f+\alpha\partial_{u}f\partial_{x}u,\,0\leq\alpha\leq 1

For example,
\alpha=1/3

f(u)=u^{2}/2

We will try to make the discussion more general so that it is applicable to the multi-dimensional model problem (
1

Let us consider the equivalent skew-symmetric form of (

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{u}),\partial_{\mathbf{x}})=0,\quad\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{u}),\partial_{\mathbf{x}})\equiv\divergence\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{u}),\quad\mathbf{x}\in\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad t\geq 0.

(6)

Here
\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{u}),\partial_{\mathbf{x}})
is a differential operator and models the functional equivalence of the divergence of the flux
\divergence\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{u})
obtained by taking a convex combination of the flux form and the quasi-linear form, as above, such that

\displaystyle\left(\mathbf{g},\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{u}),\partial_{\mathbf{x}})\right)=\left(1,\divergence\mathbf{q}\right)=\oint_{\partial\Omega}(\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{n})\differential\mathbf{x},

(7)

holds by only applying integration-by-parts, and without the chain/product rule. Here,
\mathbf{g}=\partial_{\mathbf{u}}e(\mathbf{u})
are the entropy variables. Consequently, we can prove entropy conservation using only integration-by-parts, and without the chain/product rule. That is

\displaystyle\underbrace{\left(\mathbf{g},\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}\right)}_{\derivative{t}E(t)=\derivative{t}\left(\mathbf{1},e\right)}+\underbrace{\left(\mathbf{g},\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{u}),\partial_{\mathbf{x}})\right)}_{\left(\mathbf{1},\divergence\mathbf{q}\right)}=0\iff\derivative{t}E(t)=-\oint_{\partial\Omega}(\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{n})\differential\mathbf{x}=0.

Other than the primary motivation of showing entropy-stability using only integration by parts, it is also desirable that the skew-symmetric reformulation ensure structure preservation.
For numerical analysis it is critical that the skew-symmetric form ensure that the proof of the conservative property (

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathbf{\mathcal{U}_{i}}=\left(\mathbf{1},\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}_{i}\right)=-\underbrace{\left(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{F}_{i}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{u}),\partial_{\mathbf{x}})\right)}_{\left(\mathbf{1},\divergence\mathbf{f}_{i}\right)}=-\oint_{\partial\Omega}\left(\mathbf{f}_{i}(\mathbf{u})\cdot\mathbf{n}\right)\differential{\vb x},\forall\,i=1,2,\cdots,m.

(8)

, for all
\mathbf{g}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Remark 1

The DP SBP DG framework of

The SBP operators by themselves do not result in stable numerical methods. For a given PDE system, SBP operators must be carefully applied in a manner that allows the proof of numerical stability, as long as suitable numerical treatments of boundary conditions are available.
As discussed in

For example, for conservative PDE systems, we can design energy conserving schemes, thanks to the DP-SBP property Assumption (

\mathcal{Q}_{\pm}:={Q}_{\pm}-\frac{1}{2}B

We can also construct entropy/energy dissipating schemes, for example to upwind transport terms in our systems or dissipate unresolved modes for nonlinear problems. This is mainly a consequence of upwind DP-SBP, Assumption (

4
The numerical method and analysis

We derive a numerical method that replicates the continuous analysis at the discrete level by combining DP SBP operators to preserve model symmetries, ensure entropy stability, high-order accuracy, and maintain key invariants. In particular, we will outline how to construct conservative and provably entropy-stable semi-discrete schemes for nonlinear hyperbolic conservation laws using upwind DP-SBP operators in a multi-block framework. Although we focus on 1D systems for simplicity, these concepts extend to higher dimensions.

To begin, we consider the 1D hyperbolic conservation law

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}+\partial_{x}\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{u})=0,\,x\in\Omega=[0,L],\quad t\in[0,T],

(10)

where
\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{u})
is a continuous flux function,
L>0
is the length of the spatial interval
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}
. We close the boundaries at
x\in\{0,L\}
with periodic boundary conditions, that is

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}(0,t)=\mathbf{u}(L,t)\implies\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{u}(0,t))=\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{u}(L,t)).

(11)

Thus for smooth solutions, the system (
10
) with (
11
) is conservative and conserves total entropy, that is

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}=\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{u}(0,t))-\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{u}(L,t))=0,\quad\derivative{t}E(t)=q(\mathbf{u}(0,t))-q(\mathbf{u}(L,t))=0.

(12)

To be useful, numerical approximations of the nonlinear conservation law (
10
), with periodic boundary conditions (
11
), must as far as possible mimic (
12
) at the discrete level, leading to conservative and entropy stable discrete approximations.

4.1
Multi-block DP SBP method

We decompose the spatial domain into
K\geq 1
elements/blocks and denote the
k
-th element/block
\Omega_{k}=[x_{k-1},x_{k}]
, where the element width is denoted
|\Omega_{k}|=x_{k}-x_{k-1}>0
, and the external boundaries are denoted
x_{0}=0
,
x_{K}=L
. In each element the local solutions and fluxes are denoted
{\mathbf{u}}^{k}
and
{\mathbf{f}}^{k}
. At an element interface
x_{k}
, shared by the two adjacent elements
\Omega_{k}
and
\Omega_{k+1}
, we will denote the jump of the solutions and fields across the interface as
[\![{\mathbf{u}}^{k}]\!]=\left.{\mathbf{u}}^{k+1}\right|_{x=x_{k}}-\left.{\mathbf{u}}^{k}\right|_{x=x_{k}}
.

4.1.1
Multi-block DP SBP operators

To keep the derivations and the analysis simple, we will split the spatial domain into two elements, with
K=2
and

\Omega=\Omega_{k}\cup\Omega_{k+1}
. The method and analysis extend to many more elements. For each element
\Omega_{k}
,
k\in\{1,2\}
, we discretize the domain into
n
grid points and sample the solutions on the grids. For the multi-block FD method the grids are equidistant nodes and for the DG method the grids are given by the LGL nodes. The semi-discrete solution is denoted
\mathbf{u}^{k}=(\mathbf{u}_{1}^{k},\mathbf{u}_{2}^{k},\cdots,\mathbf{u}_{n}^{k})^{T}
. The two elements are connected at the interface
x=x_{k}
with the interface conditions

[\![{\mathbf{u}}]\!]:=\mathbf{u}_{1}^{k+1}-\mathbf{u}_{n}^{k}=0,\quad[\![{\mathbf{f}}]\!]:=\mathbf{f}_{1}^{k+1}-\mathbf{f}_{n}^{k}=0.

For the global solutions in the two elements, we introduce the augmented discrete solution vector and discrete flux vector, given by

\mathbf{u}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{u}_{1}^{k},\cdots,\mathbf{u}_{n}^{k},\mathbf{u}_{1}^{k+1},\cdots,\mathbf{u}_{n}^{k+1}\end{bmatrix}^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{2mn},\quad\mathbf{f}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{f}_{1}^{k},\cdots,\mathbf{f}_{n}^{k},\mathbf{f}_{1}^{k+1},\cdots,\mathbf{f}_{n}^{k+1}\end{bmatrix}^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{2mn}.

To implement the interface conditions and the periodic boundary conditions we also define the boundary and interface penalty matrices

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}_{I}=\begin{pmatrix}-\mathbf{e}_{n}\mathbf{e}_{n}^{T}&\mathbf{e}_{n}\mathbf{e}_{1}^{T}\\
-\mathbf{e}_{1}\mathbf{e}_{n}^{T}&\mathbf{e}_{1}\mathbf{e}_{1}^{T}\end{pmatrix},\quad\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}_{I}=\begin{pmatrix}-\mathbf{e}_{n}\mathbf{e}_{n}^{T}&\mathbf{e}_{n}\mathbf{e}_{1}^{T}\\
\mathbf{e}_{1}\mathbf{e}_{n}^{T}&-\mathbf{e}_{1}\mathbf{e}_{1}^{T}\end{pmatrix},\quad\mathbf{B}_{n}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{e}_{1}\mathbf{e}_{1}^{T}&-\mathbf{e}_{1}\mathbf{e}_{n}^{T}\\
\mathbf{e}_{n}\mathbf{e}_{1}^{T}&-\mathbf{e}_{n}\mathbf{e}_{n}^{T}\end{pmatrix},\quad\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}_{n}=\begin{pmatrix}-\mathbf{e}_{1}\mathbf{e}_{1}^{T}&\mathbf{e}_{1}\mathbf{e}_{n}^{T}\\
\mathbf{e}_{n}\mathbf{e}_{1}^{T}&-\mathbf{e}_{n}\mathbf{e}_{n}^{T}\end{pmatrix}.

(13)

For
m=1
, that is
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
with
[\![{\mathbf{u}}]\!]=u_{1}^{k+1}-u_{n}^{k}
, we have

\mathbf{B}_{I}\mathbf{u}=\left(0,\cdots,0,[\![{\mathbf{u}}]\!],[\![{\mathbf{u}}]\!],0,\cdots,0\right)^{T},\quad\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}_{I}\mathbf{u}=\left(0,\cdots,0,[\![{\mathbf{u}}]\!],-[\![{\mathbf{u}}]\!],0,\cdots,0\right)^{T},

\mathbf{B}_{n}\mathbf{u}=\left((u_{1}^{1}-u_{n}^{K}),0,\cdots,0,(u_{1}^{1}-u_{n}^{K})\right)^{T},\quad\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}_{n}\mathbf{u}=\left(-(u_{1}^{1}-u_{n}^{K}),0,\cdots,0,(u_{1}^{1}-u_{n}^{K})\right)^{T}.

The interface matrices
\mathbf{B}_{I}
,
\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}_{I}
will be used to weakly couple the elements together to the global domain and the boundary matrices
\mathbf{B}_{n}
,
\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}_{n}
will weakly implement the period boundary conditions. Note also that the matrices
\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}_{I}
,
\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}_{n}
are symmetric and negative semi-definite, that is
\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}_{I}=\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}_{I}^{T}
,
\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}_{n}=\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}_{n}^{T}
and

\mathbf{u}^{T}\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}_{I}\mathbf{u}=-[\![{\mathbf{u}}]\!]^{2}=-(u_{1}^{k+1}-u_{n}^{k})^{2}\leq 0,

\mathbf{u}^{T}\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}_{n}\mathbf{u}=-(u_{1}^{1}-u_{n}^{K})^{2}\leq 0.

We introduce the multi-block operators

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}=\begin{pmatrix}H&0\\
0&H\end{pmatrix},\quad\mathbf{D}=\begin{pmatrix}D&0\\
0&D\end{pmatrix},\quad\mathbf{D}_{\pm}=\begin{pmatrix}D_{\pm}&0\\
0&D_{\pm}\end{pmatrix},\quad\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}_{\pm}=\mathbf{D}_{\pm}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{H}^{-1}\left(\mathbf{B}_{n}+\mathbf{B}_{I}\right),

(14)

where the penalty terms weakly implement the interface and the periodic boundary conditions. The penalized multi-block operators
\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}_{\pm}
also satisfy the identities

\displaystyle\left\langle\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}_{+}\mathbf{f},\,\mathbf{g}\right\rangle_{\mathbf{H}}+\left\langle\mathbf{f},\,\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}_{-}\mathbf{g}\right\rangle_{\mathbf{H}}=0,\,\left\langle\mathbf{f},\,\left(\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}_{+}-\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}_{-}\right)\mathbf{f}\right\rangle_{\mathbf{H}}=\left\langle\mathbf{f},\,\left(\mathbf{D}_{+}-\mathbf{D}_{-}\right)\mathbf{f}\right\rangle_{\mathbf{H}}\leq 0,\quad\forall\,\mathbf{f},\,\mathbf{g}\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}.

(15)

For simplicity, we will ignore the
\sim
in the penalized SBP operators and write
\mathbf{D}_{\pm}=\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}_{\pm}
where
\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}_{\pm}
are multi-block and periodic operators implemented using penalties as in (
14
).
We define the multi-block spatial operators for systems

\mathbf{D}_{x}=\left(I_{m}\otimes\mathbf{D}\right),\quad\mathbf{H}_{x}=\left(I_{m}\otimes\mathbf{H}\right),\quad{\mathbf{B}}_{Ix}=\left(I_{m}\otimes\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}_{I}\right),\quad{\mathbf{B}}_{nx}=\left(I_{m}\otimes\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}_{n}\right).

It also follows that

\displaystyle\left\langle{\mathbf{D}}_{x}\mathbf{f},\,\mathbf{g}\right\rangle_{\mathbf{H}_{x}}+\left\langle\mathbf{f},\,{\mathbf{D}}_{x}\mathbf{g}\right\rangle_{\mathbf{H}_{x}}=0,\quad\left\langle\mathbf{f},\,\left(I_{m}\otimes\left(\mathbf{D}_{+}-\mathbf{D}_{-}\right)\right)\mathbf{f}\right\rangle_{\mathbf{H}_{x}}\leq 0,\quad\forall\,\mathbf{f},\,\mathbf{g}\in\mathbb{R}^{2mn}.

(16)

and

{\mathbf{B}}_{Ix}={\mathbf{B}}_{Ix}^{T}
,
{\mathbf{B}}_{nx}={\mathbf{B}}_{nx}^{T}
,
\mathbf{u}^{T}{\mathbf{B}}_{Ix}\mathbf{u}\leq 0
,
\mathbf{u}^{T}{\mathbf{B}}_{Ix}\mathbf{u}\leq 0
, for all
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{2mn}
.

4.1.2
Finite volume flux splitting and linear-stability

To enable the effectiveness of the upwind DP SBP operators we will need a splitting strategy.
We will consider classical flux splitting which is prevalent in the finite volume literature, see e.g.
[
23
,
4
,
24
,
37
,
36
]
.
In each element,
\Omega_{k}
, we introduce the upwind flux splitting where

\displaystyle{\mathbf{f}}_{i}^{k}(\mathbf{u})=\underbrace{\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{f}_{i}^{k}+\gamma^{k}_{i}\mathbf{g}_{i}^{k})}_{\mathbf{f}_{i}^{+}}+\underbrace{\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{f}_{i}^{k}-\gamma^{k}_{i}\mathbf{g}_{i}^{k})}_{\mathbf{f}_{i}^{-}}\equiv\mathbf{f}_{i}^{+}+\mathbf{f}_{i}^{-},\quad i=1,2,\ldots,m,

(17)

\gamma_{i}^{k}\geq 0
are (time-dependent) parameters and
\mathbf{g}_{i}^{k}
are carefully chosen functions to induce dissipation when upwinded. The parameters
\gamma_{i}^{k}>0
are chosen such that
\mathbf{f}_{i}^{k}
and
\gamma_{i}^{k}\mathbf{g}_{i}^{k}
have consistent units.
We will denote
\mathbf{f}^{+}
the positive-going flux and
\mathbf{f}^{-}
is the negative-going flux.
If
\mathbf{g}_{i}^{k}=\mathbf{u}_{i}^{k}
and
\gamma_{i}^{k}=\max_{j}|\lambda_{j}(A_{x}^{k})|
where
A_{x}^{k}=\partial_{\mathbf{u}^{k}}\mathbf{f}^{k}
, then we get the classical global but element-local Lax-Friederich flux splitting
[
23
,
24
]
.
Note that the element
\Omega_{k}
is discretized on an
n
grid points, and for a uniform grid we have
\Delta{x}=|\Omega_{k}|/(n-1)
. We sample the solution and PDE flux on the grid, having

\mathbf{u}^{k}(t):=\left(\mathbf{u}_{1}^{k}(t),\ldots,\mathbf{u}_{n}^{k}(t)\right)^{T},\quad\mathbf{f}^{\pm}(\mathbf{u}^{k}):=\left({\mathbf{f}}^{\pm}(\mathbf{u}_{1}^{k}(t)),\ldots,{\mathbf{f}}^{\pm}(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{k}(t))\right)^{T}.

To approximate the divergence of the flux we will use the DP DG/FD operators to upwind the positive-going flux
\mathbf{f}^{+}
and the negative-going flux
\mathbf{f}^{-}
having

\partial_{x}\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{u}^{k})\approx\left(I_{m}\otimes{D}_{+}\right)\mathbf{f}^{-}+\left(I_{m}\otimes{D}_{-}\right)\mathbf{f}^{+}=\left(I_{m}\otimes{D}\right)\mathbf{f}^{k}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\Gamma^{k}\otimes\left({D}_{+}-{D}_{-}\right)\right)\mathbf{g}^{k},\ {D}:=\frac{1}{2}\left({D}_{+}+{D}_{-}\right),

where
\Gamma^{k}=\mathinner{\diag\mathopen{\left([\gamma_{1}^{k},\gamma_{2}^{k},\cdots,\gamma_{m}^{k}]\right)}}
,
\gamma_{i}^{k}\geq 0
, and
\otimes
denote the Kronecker product.
Note that for smooth solutions, we have

|\left(\Gamma^{k}\otimes\left({D}_{+}-{D}_{-}\right)\right)\mathbf{g}^{k}|=O(\Delta{x}^{p+1})
where
p\geq 1
is the order of accuracy of the discrete operators
D_{\pm}
.

A two-element semi-discrete approximation of the systems of 1D conservation laws (
10
) reads

\displaystyle\derivative{t}\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{D}_{x}\mathbf{f}=\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{H}_{x}^{-1}\left(\boldsymbol{\alpha}\otimes\left(\mathbf{B}_{Ix}+\mathbf{B}_{nx}\right)\right)\mathbf{g}+\frac{1}{2}\Gamma\left(I_{m}\otimes\left(\mathbf{D}_{+}-\mathbf{D}_{-}\right)\right)\mathbf{g},\quad

(18)

where
I_{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
is the identity matrix, the diagonal and positive matrices
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
,
\boldsymbol{\Gamma}
are given by

\boldsymbol{\alpha}=\frac{1}{2}\diag\left([\alpha_{1}^{1}+\alpha_{1}^{2},\alpha_{2}^{1}+\alpha_{2}^{2},\cdots,\alpha_{m}^{1}+\alpha_{m}^{2}]\right),\quad\Gamma=\diag\left([\gamma_{1}^{1},\gamma_{2}^{1},\cdots,\gamma_{m}^{1},\gamma_{1}^{2},\gamma_{2}^{2},\cdots,\gamma_{m}^{2}]\right)\otimes I_{2n},

with
I_{2n}\in\mathbb{R}^{2n\times 2n}
being the identity matrix,
\alpha_{i}^{k},\gamma_{i}^{k}\geq 0
,
\mathbf{f}
is the discrete PDE flux and
\mathbf{g}
is a carefully chosen function to induce dissipation in the volume. The first term in the right hand side of (
18
) are surface terms at element faces induced by an upwind numerical flux, the second term in the right hand side of (
18
) are volume terms inside the element and induced by the upwind property of the DP-SBP framework. Note that for the traditional SBP framework we have
\mathbf{D}_{+}=\mathbf{D}_{-}=\mathbf{D}
, thus, the volume upwind terms vanish identically which yields the standard multi-block SBP scheme or the DGSEM. It is also noteworthy that the semi-discrete approximation (
18
) is compactly written for the two-elements case
K=2
. However, the method is valid in a single element, with
K=1
, and extends to arbitrary number of elements
K\geq 1
.

For the semi-discrete approximation (
18
), the discrete conservative principle follows. We formulate the result as the theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Consider the semi-discrete upwind DP-SBP approximation (
18
) of the system (
10
).
If the 3-tuple
(D_{-},D_{+},H)
satisfies the upwind DP SBP operator given by Assumptions (
\mathbf{A}
.1)–(
\mathbf{A}
.4), then the semi-discrete approximation (
18
) is element-local and globally conservative. That is,
\forall\,i=1,2,\cdots,m
, the total quantities
\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}^{k}_{ih}(t)={\left\langle\mathbf{1},\,\mathbf{u}_{i}^{k}\right\rangle_{H_{x}}}
and
\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}_{ih}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}^{k}_{ih}
satisfy

\displaystyle\derivative{t}\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}^{k}_{ih}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\left(f_{ni}^{k-1}+f_{1i}^{k}-\frac{\alpha_{i}^{k-1}+\alpha_{i}^{k}}{2}[\![{\mathbf{g}_{i}^{k-1}}]\!]\right)-\frac{1}{2}\left(f_{ni}^{k}+f_{1i}^{k+1}-\frac{\alpha_{i}^{k}+\alpha_{i}^{k+1}}{2}[\![{\mathbf{g}_{i}^{k}}]\!]\right),

(19)

\displaystyle\derivative{t}\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}_{ih}(t)=0.

Proof.

We consider
\derivative{t}{\left\langle\mathbf{1},\,\mathbf{u}_{i}^{k}\right\rangle_{H_{x}}}
and use the SBP property to obtain

\displaystyle\derivative{t}\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}^{k}_{ih}(t)

\displaystyle=\left\langle D{\mathbf{1}},\,\mathbf{f}_{i}^{k}\right\rangle_{H}+\frac{\gamma_{i}^{k}}{2}\left\langle\left(D_{+}-D_{-}\right)\mathbf{1},\,\mathbf{g}_{i}^{k}\right\rangle_{H}+\frac{1}{2}\left(f_{ni}^{k-1}+f_{1i}^{k}-\frac{\alpha_{i}^{k-1}+\alpha_{i}^{k}}{2}[\![{\mathbf{g}_{i}^{k-1}}]\!]\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\left(f_{ni}^{k}+f_{1i}^{k+1}-\frac{\alpha_{i}^{k}+\alpha_{i}^{k+1}}{2}[\![{\mathbf{g}_{i}^{k}}]\!]\right).

Since
D{\mathbf{1}}=0
and
D_{\pm}{\mathbf{1}}=0
, then the first two terms in the right hand side vanish and we have

\displaystyle\derivative{t}\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}^{k}_{ih}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(f_{ni}^{k-1}+f_{1i}^{k}-\frac{\alpha_{i}^{k-1}+\alpha_{i}^{k}}{2}[\![{\mathbf{g}_{i}^{k-1}}]\!]\right)-\frac{1}{2}\left(f_{ni}^{k}+f_{1i}^{k+1}-\frac{\alpha_{i}^{k}+\alpha_{i}^{k+1}}{2}[\![{\mathbf{g}_{i}^{k}}]\!]\right).

Summing contributions from all elements the flux terms at the interfaces cancel out giving

\derivative{t}\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}_{ih}(t)=\derivative{t}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}^{k}_{ih}=0.

The proof is complete.
∎

The proof of (nonlinear) entropy-stability for the semi-discrete approximation (

We will formulate the result as the theorem.

Theorem 4.2
.

Consider the semi-discrete approximation (
18
) for a linear PDE flux, with
\mathbf{f}=A_{x}\mathbf{u}
where
A_{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
a symmetric (or symmetrizable) constant matrix.
For the classical global Lax-Friedrich flux splitting, that is if
\mathbf{g}^{k}=\mathbf{u}^{k}
and
\gamma_{i}^{k}=\max_{x}|\max_{j}\lambda_{j}(A_{x}^{k})|
, and the 3-tuple
(D_{-},D_{+},H)
satisfies the upwind DP SBP operator given by Assumptions (
\mathbf{A}
.1)–(
\mathbf{A}
.4), with
\|\mathbf{u}\|_{H_{x}}^{2}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\|\mathbf{u}^{k}\|_{H}^{2}
, then we have

\displaystyle\derivative{t}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{H_{x}}^{2}=-\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{\alpha_{i}^{k}+\alpha_{i}^{k+1}}{2}[\![{\mathbf{u}_{i}^{k}}]\!]^{2}-\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\gamma_{i}^{k}\left\langle\mathbf{u}_{i}^{k},\,\left(D_{-}-D_{+}\right)\mathbf{u}_{i}^{k}\right\rangle_{H}}\leq 0.

(20)

Proof.

We set
\mathbf{f}=A_{x}\mathbf{u}
where
A_{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
a symmetric constant coefficients matrix and we proceed with the energy method. That is from the left, we multiply (
18
) with
\mathbf{u}^{T}\left(I_{m}\otimes H\right)
and add the transpose of the product, and we have

\displaystyle\derivative{t}\|\mathbf{u}^{k}\|_{H_{x}}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\underbrace{(\mathbf{u}^{k})^{T}\left(A_{x}\otimes\left(Q+Q^{T}\right)\right)\mathbf{u}_{k}}_{(\mathbf{u}^{k}_{n})^{T}A_{x}\mathbf{u}^{k}_{n}-(\mathbf{u}_{1}^{k})^{T}A_{x}\mathbf{u}^{k}_{1}}-\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{u}_{1}^{k})^{T}\left(A_{x}\mathbf{u}^{k}_{1}-A_{x}\mathbf{u}^{k-1}_{n}+\frac{\alpha_{i}^{k-1}+\alpha_{i}^{k}}{2}[\![{\mathbf{u}^{k-1}}]\!]\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{k})^{T}\left(A_{x}\mathbf{u}^{k}_{n}-A_{x}\mathbf{u}^{k+1}_{1}+\frac{\alpha_{i}^{k}+\alpha_{i}^{k+1}}{2}[\![{\mathbf{u}^{k}}]\!]\right)-\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\gamma_{i}^{k}\left\langle\mathbf{u}_{i}^{k},\,\left(D_{-}-D_{+}\right)\mathbf{u}_{i}^{k}\right\rangle_{H}}.

Simplifying further we have

\displaystyle\derivative{t}\|\mathbf{u}^{k}\|_{H}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{u}_{1}^{k})^{T}\left(A_{x}\mathbf{u}^{k-1}_{n}+A_{x}\mathbf{u}^{k}_{1}-\frac{\alpha_{i}^{k-1}+\alpha_{i}^{k}}{2}[\![{\mathbf{u}^{k-1}}]\!]\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{k})^{T}\left(A_{x}\mathbf{u}^{k}_{n}+A_{x}\mathbf{u}^{k+1}_{1}-\frac{\alpha_{i}^{k}+\alpha_{i}^{k+1}}{2}[\![{\mathbf{u}^{k}}]\!]\right)-\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\gamma_{i}^{k}\left\langle\mathbf{u}_{i}^{k},\,\left(D_{-}-D_{+}\right)\mathbf{u}_{i}^{k}\right\rangle_{H}}.

Note that the periodic boundaries give

\frac{\alpha_{i}^{K}+\alpha_{i}^{K+1}}{2}[\![{\mathbf{u}_{i}^{K}}]\!]^{2}=\frac{\alpha_{i}^{K}+\alpha_{i}^{1}}{2}({\mathbf{u}_{i1}^{1}-\mathbf{u}_{in}^{K}})^{2}.

Summing contributions from all elements the PDE flux terms at the interfaces cancel out giving

\derivative{t}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{H_{x}}^{2}=\derivative{t}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\|\mathbf{u}^{k}\|_{H_{x}}^{2}=-\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{\alpha_{i}^{k}+\alpha_{i}^{k+1}}{2}[\![{\mathbf{u}_{i}^{k}}]\!]^{2}-\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\gamma_{i}^{k}\left\langle\mathbf{u}_{i}^{k},\,\left(D_{-}-D_{+}\right)\mathbf{u}_{i}^{k}\right\rangle_{H}}\leq 0.

This completes the proof.
∎

4.1.3
The entropy-stable semi-discrete approximation

We now describe a method which can yield a provably nonlinearly/entropy stable, conservative, and high-order accurate numerical method for systems of nonlinear conservation laws. The derived numerical method is robust and minimizes spurious oscillations. The method combines three main ingredients: 1)
the DP SBP DG/FD operators
, together with 2)
skew-symmetric reformulation
, and 3)
upwind flux splitting
.

Again, for simplicity we consider the 1D conservation law,

\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}+\partial_{x}\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{u})=0\iff\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{u}),\partial_{x})=0,

where
\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{u}),\partial_{x})\equiv\partial_{x}\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{u})
is the skew-symmetric form given in (
6
) and defined through (
7
) such that entropy stability can be proven by using the energy method with only integration by parts, and without the chain/product rule.
We will begin with the following Lemma which is a discrete analogue of (
7
).

Lemma 4.3
.

Consider the semi-discrete approximation of the skew-symmetric form of the element local and global (multi-elements) differential operators

\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{u}^{k},\mathbf{f}({\mathbf{u}}^{k}),{D})\approx\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{u}^{k},\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{u}^{k}),\partial_{x}),\quad\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{f}({\mathbf{u}}),\mathbf{D}_{x})\approx\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{u}),\partial_{x}),

where
D\approx\partial_{x}
is the local derivative without flux corrections,
\mathbf{f}({\mathbf{u}}^{k})
is the PDE flux and
\mathbf{D}_{x}\approx\partial_{x}
is the global derivative operator which includes flux corrections at the element boundaries. If the 2-tuple
(D,H)
satisfy the traditional SBP operator, then

{\left\langle\mathbf{g}^{k},\,\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{u}^{k},\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{u}^{k}),D)\right\rangle_{H}}=-{\left\langle D\mathbf{1},\,\mathbf{q}^{k}\right\rangle_{H}+\mathbf{q}^{k}\left.\right|_{x_{k}}-\mathbf{q}^{k}\left.\right|_{x_{k-1}}=\mathbf{q}^{k}\left.\right|_{x_{k}}-\mathbf{q}^{k}\left.\right|_{x_{k-1}}},

{\left\langle\mathbf{g},\,\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{u}),\mathbf{D}_{x})\right\rangle_{H_{x}}}=-{\left\langle\mathbf{D}_{x}\mathbf{1},\,\mathbf{q}\right\rangle_{H_{x}}}=0,

where
\mathbf{g}^{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{mn}
,
\mathbf{g}\in\mathbb{R}^{Kmn}
are the semi-discrete entropy variables and
\mathbf{q}^{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{mn}
,
\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{R}^{Kmn}
are the semi-discrete entropy flux.

Next we generalize the flux splitting (
17
), by introducing the upwind matrix
\Gamma^{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
, which will yield entropy stability when upwinded with the DP SBP DG/FD operators. Specifically, we chose

\displaystyle{\mathbf{f}}^{k}(\mathbf{u})=\underbrace{\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{f}^{k}+\Gamma^{k}\mathbf{g}^{k})}_{\mathbf{f}^{+}}+\underbrace{\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{f}^{k}-\Gamma^{k}\mathbf{g}^{k})}_{\mathbf{f}^{-}}\equiv\mathbf{f}^{+}+\mathbf{f}^{-},

(21)

with the entropy variables
\mathbf{g}^{k}_{i}=\partial_{\mathbf{u}_{i}^{k}}e({\mathbf{u}}^{k})
, and
\alpha_{i}^{k}>0
and
\Gamma^{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
are constants within the element and chosen such that
\mathbf{f}_{i}^{k}
,
(\Gamma^{k}\mathbf{g}^{k})_{i}
and
\alpha_{i}^{k}\mathbf{g}_{i}^{k}
have consistent physical units. In addition the symmetric part of
\Gamma^{k}
is positive semi-definite
\Gamma^{k}+(\Gamma^{k})^{T}\geq 0
. The flux splitting (
17
) corresponds to a diagonal upwind matrix with
\Gamma^{k}=\mathinner{\diag\mathopen{\left([\gamma_{1}^{k},\gamma_{2}^{k},\cdots,\gamma_{m}^{k}]\right)}}
.
Combining
the skew-symmetric form
with
entropy-stable upwind splitting
, and
the DP SBP operators
yield the two-element semi-discrete approximation of the systems of 1D conservation laws (
10
)

\displaystyle\derivative{t}\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{u},{\mathbf{f}}({\mathbf{u}}),\mathbf{D}_{x})=\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{H}_{x}^{-1}\left(\boldsymbol{\alpha}\otimes\left(\mathbf{B}_{Ix}+\mathbf{B}_{nx}\right)\right)\mathbf{g}+\frac{1}{2}\Gamma\left(I_{m}\otimes\left(\mathbf{D}_{+}-\mathbf{D}_{-}\right)\right)\mathbf{g},

(22)

where
I_{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
is the identity matrix and the matrices
\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\Gamma}\in\mathbb{R}^{2mn\times 2mn}
are given by

\boldsymbol{\alpha}=\frac{1}{2}\diag\left([\alpha_{1}^{1}+\alpha_{1}^{2},\alpha_{2}^{1}+\alpha_{2}^{2},\cdots,\alpha_{m}^{1}+\alpha_{m}^{2}]\right),\quad\Gamma=\diag\left([\Gamma^{1},\Gamma^{2}]\right)\otimes I_{2n},

with
I_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{2n\times 2n}
being the identity matrix, and
\alpha_{i}^{k}\geq 0
,
\Gamma^{k}+(\Gamma^{k})^{T}\geq 0
. As before, the first term in the right hand side of (
22
) are surface terms at element faces induced by an upwind numerical flux, and the second term in the right hand side of (
22
) are volume terms inside the element, and result from the upwind property of the DP SBP framework. For the traditional SBP framework we have
\mathbf{D}_{+}=\mathbf{D}_{-}=\mathbf{D}
, thus, the volume upwind terms vanish identically which yields the standard entropy stable multi-block SBP scheme or the DGSEM. It is also noteworthy that the semi-discrete approximation (
22
) is compactly written for the two-elements case
K=2
. However, the method is valid in a single element, with
K=1
, and extends to an arbitrary number of elements
K\geq 1
.
Again, for smooth solutions, the right hand side of (
22
) is very small, that is

|\left(\Gamma^{k}\otimes\left(D_{+}-D_{-}\right)\right)\mathbf{g}^{k}|=O(\Delta{x}^{p+1})
where
p\geq 1
is the order of accuracy of the discrete operators
D_{\pm}
.

We note that the main difference between our proposed method (
22
) and the so-called upwind methods
[
37
,
36
]
exemplified by (
18
) is the discretization of the semi-discrete flux form
\mathbf{D}_{x}\mathbf{f}\approx\partial_{x}\mathbf{f}
and the skew-symmetric form
\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{u},{\mathbf{f}}({\mathbf{u}}),\mathbf{D}_{x})\approx\partial_{x}\mathbf{f}
, on the left hand sides of (
22
), (
18
), and the choice of the upwind variables in
\mathbf{g}
in the right hand sides of (
22
), (
18
). Herein, in (
22
), we have chosen the skew-symmetric form
\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{u},{\mathbf{f}}({\mathbf{u}}),\mathbf{D}_{x})
, the entropy variables
\mathbf{g}_{i}^{k}=\partial_{\mathbf{u}_{i}^{k}}e({\mathbf{u}^{k}})
and the volume upwind matrices
\Gamma
such that we can prove nonlinear/entropy stability at the semi-discrete level.

Remark 3
.

The significance and novelty of the current study is the systematic combination of the DP SBP FD/DG operators together with the skew-symmetric and upwind flux splitting that allows the design of robust and high-order accurate numerical schemes for systems of nonlinear hyperbolic conservation laws. To the best of our knowledge, this has never been reported in the literature.

Next, we will prove entropy stability for the method (
22
). To begin, we approximate the total entropy (
4
) by

\displaystyle E_{h}(t):=\left\langle\mathbf{1},\,e\right\rangle_{H_{x}}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left\langle\mathbf{1},\,e^{k}\right\rangle_{H}.

(23)

The following definition, which is similar to the continuous analogue Definition
1
, is central to this study.

Definition 2
.

Consider the semi-discrete approximation (
22
) and the denote the semi-discrete total entropy
E_{h}(t)\geq 0
, defined by (
23
). The semi-discrete approximation (
22
) is called entropy-stable (entropy-conserving) if
E(t)\leq E_{h}(0)
(
E_{h}(t)=E_{h}(0)
) for all
\mathbf{u}^{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{mn}
,
k=1,2,\cdots,K
and
t\in[0,T]
.

Our main theorem proves that the semi-discrete approximation (
22
) is entropy-stable.

Theorem 4.4
.

Consider the multi-element DP-SBP semi-discrete approximation (
22
) where
\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{f}({\mathbf{u}}),\mathbf{D}_{x})
is the multi-block semi-discrete approximation of the skew-symmetric form with periodic boundary conditions.
If
\mathbf{g}_{i}^{k}=\partial_{\mathbf{u}_{i}^{k}}e({\mathbf{u}}^{k})
,
\alpha_{i}^{k}\geq 0
and
\Gamma^{k}+(\Gamma^{k})^{T}\geq 0
, and the 3-tuple
(D_{-},D_{+},H)
satisfies the upwind DP SBP operator, then we have

\displaystyle\derivative{t}E_{h}(t)=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{\alpha_{i}^{k}+\alpha_{i}^{k+1}}{2}[\![{\mathbf{g}_{i}^{k}}]\!]^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{K}{\left\langle\mathbf{g}^{k},\,\left(\left(\frac{\Gamma^{k}+(\Gamma^{k})^{T}}{2}\right)\otimes\left(D_{-}-D_{+}\right)\right)\mathbf{g}^{k}\right\rangle_{H}}\leq 0.

(24)

Proof.

Consider

\displaystyle\underbrace{\left\langle\mathbf{g},\,\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}\right\rangle_{H_{x}}}_{\derivative{t}E_{h}(t)=\derivative{t}\left\langle\mathbf{1},\,e\right\rangle_{H_{x}}}+\underbrace{\left\langle\mathbf{g},\,\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{u}),\mathbf{D}_{x})\right\rangle_{H_{x}}}_{\left\langle\mathbf{1},\,\mathbf{D}_{x}\mathbf{q}\right\rangle_{H_{x}}=0}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{K-1}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{\alpha_{i}^{k}+\alpha_{i}^{k+1}}{2}[\![{\mathbf{g}_{i}^{k}}]\!]^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{K}{\left\langle\mathbf{g}^{k},\,\left(\left(\frac{\Gamma^{k}+(\Gamma^{k})^{T}}{2}\right)\otimes\left(D_{-}-D_{+}\right)\right)\mathbf{g}^{k}\right\rangle_{H}}\leq 0.

Note that the periodic boundaries give

\frac{\alpha_{i}^{K}+\alpha_{i}^{K+1}}{2}[\![{\mathbf{g}_{i}^{K}}]\!]^{2}=\frac{\alpha_{i}^{K}+\alpha_{i}^{1}}{2}({\mathbf{g}_{i1}^{1}-\mathbf{g}_{in}^{K}})^{2}.

Then we have

\displaystyle\derivative{t}E_{h}(t)=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{\alpha_{i}^{k}+\alpha_{i}^{k+1}}{2}[\![{\mathbf{g}_{i}^{k}}]\!]^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{K}{\left\langle\mathbf{g}^{k},\,\left(\left(\frac{\Gamma^{k}+(\Gamma^{k})^{T}}{2}\right)\otimes\left(D_{-}-D_{+}\right)\right)\mathbf{g}^{k}\right\rangle_{H}}\leq 0.

∎

4.2
Numerical conservation properties

We will show that our semi-discrete scheme (
22
) is conservative. Conservative properties of the scheme will be important in ensuring the convergence of the numerical
method for nonlinear problems with weak solutions
[
3
,
4
]
. In particular, we will show that (
22
) satisfies the discrete equivalence of (
8
). Here we will focus on global conservation properties of the scheme. However, DG-type element local conservation properties can also be shown. First, we state the following Lemma.

Lemma 4.5
.

Consider the multi-element semi-discrete approximation of the skew symmetric form

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{f}({\mathbf{u}}),\mathbf{D}_{x})\approx\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{u}),\partial_{x}),

(25)

where
\mathbf{D}_{x}\approx\partial_{x}
including flux corrections at element boundaries,
\mathbf{f}({\mathbf{u}})
is the PDE flux and
\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{u}),\partial_{x})
is the skew-symmetric form of the divergence of the flux satisfying (
8
). If
\mathbf{D}_{x}=I_{m}\otimes\mathbf{D}
and the 2-tuple
(\mathbf{D},\mathbf{H})
satisfies the first identity in (
16
),
then

\displaystyle{\left\langle\mathbf{1},\,\mathbf{F}_{i}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{u}),\mathbf{D}_{x})\right\rangle_{H_{x}}}=-{\left\langle\mathbf{D}{\mathbf{1}},\,\mathbf{f}_{i}\right\rangle_{H}=\mathbf{0}},\quad\forall\,i=1,2,\cdots,m,

(26)

where
\mathbf{f}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
are the components of the semi-discrete PDE flux restricted on the grid.

The second main theorem proves that the DP-SBP method (
22
) is globally conservative.

Theorem 4.6
.

Consider the multi-block upwind DP-SBP semi-discrete approximation (
22
) where
\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{f}({\mathbf{u}}),\mathbf{D}_{x})
is the semi-discrete approximation of the symmetric form with the interface and periodic boundary conditions implemented weakly.
If Lemma
4.5
holds and the 3-tuple
(D_{-},D_{+},H)
satisfies the DP SBP operator given by Assumptions (
\mathbf{A}
.1)–(
\mathbf{A}
.4), then the semi-discrete total quantity
\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}_{ih}(t)={\left\langle\mathbf{1},\,\mathbf{u}_{i}\right\rangle_{H}}
, for all
i=1,2,\cdots,m
, satisfies

\displaystyle\derivative{t}\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}_{ih}(t)=\left\langle\mathbf{D}{\mathbf{1}},\,\mathbf{f}_{i}\right\rangle_{H}+\frac{\alpha_{i}}{2}\left\langle\left(\mathbf{B}_{I}+\mathbf{B}_{n}\right)\mathbf{1},\,\mathbf{g}_{i}\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\left(\mathbf{D}_{+}-\mathbf{D}_{-}\right)\mathbf{1},\,(\Gamma\mathbf{g})_{i}\right\rangle_{H}=\mathbf{0}.

(27)

Proof.

Consider

\displaystyle\underbrace{\left\langle\mathbf{1},\,\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}_{i}\right\rangle_{H}}_{\derivative{t}\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}_{ih}(t)=\derivative{t}\left\langle\mathbf{1},\,\mathbf{u}_{i}\right\rangle_{H}}+\underbrace{\left\langle\mathbf{1},\,\mathbf{F}_{i}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{u}),D)\right\rangle_{H}}_{-\left\langle\mathbf{D}\mathbf{1},\,\mathbf{f}_{i}\right\rangle_{H}=0}=\frac{\alpha_{i}}{2}\left\langle\left(\mathbf{B}_{I}+\mathbf{B}_{n}\right)\mathbf{1},\,\mathbf{g}_{i}\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\left(\mathbf{D}_{+}-\mathbf{D}_{-}\right)\mathbf{1},\,(\Gamma\mathbf{g})_{i}\right\rangle_{H}=0.

(28)

Then we have

\derivative{t}\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}_{ih}(t)=0\implies\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}_{ih}(t)=\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}_{ih}(0),\quad\forall\,t\in[0,T].

∎

Thus, the semi-discrete upwind DP-SBP method (
22
) is conservative and entropy stable, for arbitrary nonlinear conservation laws in skew-symmetric form.

Remark 4
.

As noted earlier, a DG-type local numerical conservation property also holds. This can be demonstrated by restricting the discrete scalar products to one element and then utilizing the SBP property to convert the volume terms into surface terms only. When contributions from all elements are summed, these surface terms cancel out, thereby reaffirming the global conservation property.

,

Below, we provide a few remarks to compare and contrast the proposed method (
22
) with traditional and state-of-the-art methods.

Remark 5
.

The primary difference between the proposed method (
22
) and both traditional and some recent approaches in the literature, such as
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
, lies in the second term on the right-hand side of (
22
), which acts to control grid-scale oscillations. For smooth solutions, where
\mathbf{u}\in C^{1}\left(\Omega\times[0,T]\right)
, the terms on the right-hand side of (
22
) are proportional to
O(\Delta{x}^{p+1})
and are bounded by the truncation error
O(\Delta{x}^{p})
. This ensures they remain small for smooth solutions on well-resolved meshes. However, for unresolved meshes and non-smooth solutions, where
\mathbf{u}\notin C^{1}\left(\Omega\times[0,T]\right)
, the terms on the right-hand side of (
22
) can become significant and will dissipate entropy when
\Gamma^{k}+(\Gamma^{k})^{T}>0
.

Remark 6
.

We elaborate on the efficiency of the proposed method (
22
). It is particularly noteworthy that the discrete upwind operator comprises the difference of first derivative DP SBP operators, which are computationally efficient to evaluate. This contrasts with traditional and state-of-the-art artificial hyper-viscosity operators
[
57
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
, which require the evaluation of high even-order spatial derivatives
\sim\partial^{2q}/\partial x^{2q}
,
q\geq 1
. Such operators can be computationally expensive, may compromise the conservation of important invariants, and often further restrict explicit time-step sizes for the fully discrete problem.

Remark 7
.

The volume upwind term on the right-hand side of (
22
) acts as a penalty on the discrete gradient of the solution on the grid. This penalty weakly enforces smoothness and serves as a measure of unresolved features in the numerical solution. For smooth solutions, where
\mathbf{u}\in C^{1}\left(\Omega\times[0,T]\right)
, this term is small and tends to vanish as the mesh is refined. Conversely, for unresolved meshes and non-smooth solutions, where
\mathbf{u}\notin C^{1}\left(\Omega\times[0,T]\right)
, the term can be significant and suppresses grid-scale errors.
Thus, the proposed numerical framework inherently incorporates a "filter" that is accurate up to discretization errors. Its purpose is to identify and resolve regions where the solution is poorly captured or contains discontinuities. Numerical experiments presented later in this paper support the analysis.

Remark 8
.

The proposed DP SBP FD/DG method
(
22
) is structurally equivalent to entropy-stable multi-block SBP FD/DG methods develop in the literature, such as
[
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
]
. In particular when
\Gamma=0
, the method reduces the so-called entropy DG schemes which are predominantly available in the literature. However, the DP SBP operators are designed to be upwind, that is they come with
some built-in dissipation everywhere, as opposed to standard DG methods for hyperbolic PDEs which can only induce dissipation through numerical fluxes acting at element interfaces.

6
Summary and outlook

The design of provably stable numerical methods for nonlinear hyperbolic conservation laws pose a significant challenge. In this study we have proposed and analyzed the DP and SBP framework for accurate and robust numerical approximations of nonlinear conservation laws. The DP SBP FD operators
[
33
,
34
]
are a dual-pair of backward and forward difference operators, which together preserve the SBP property. In addition, the DP SBP operators are designed to be upwind, that is they come with some built-in dissipation everywhere, as opposed to traditional SBP and DG methods which can only induce dissipation through numerical fluxes acting at element interfaces. We combine the DP SBP operators together with skew-symmetric and upwind flux splitting of nonlinear hyperbolic conservation laws. Our semi-discrete approximation is provably entropy-stable for arbitrary nonlinear hyperbolic conservation laws. Some examples are given to validate the theoretical guarantees, and demonstrate significant improvements over the state-of-the-art methods.

There are multiple directions in which this work can be extended. Important and possible directions for future work include numerical treatments of non-periodic boundary conditions, multi-block schemes and curvilinear meshes to handle complex geometries and 2D manifolds, local energy stability analysis, proper treatments of supersonic shocks, extensions to more complex applications like thermal shallow water equations, moist compressible Euler equations and multi-component compressible Euler equations with chemistry, and to other PDEs such as magnetohydrodynamics equations and plasma simulations.

Appendix A
Burgers’ Equations

We consider the semi-discrete approximation of the Burgers’ equation with the unknown
u
, the PDE flux
f(u)=u^{2}/2
, the entropy
e(u)=u^{2}/2
and the entropy variable
g=\partial_{u}e(u)=u
.
The skew-symmetric form of the divergence of the flux is

F(u,f(u),\partial_{x})=\frac{1}{3}u\partial_{x}u+\frac{1}{3}\partial_{x}(u^{2}).

The two-element upwind DP SBP semi-discrete approximation of the Burgers’ is given by

\displaystyle\derivative{t}\mathbf{u}+\frac{1}{3}\mathbf{u}\mathbf{D}_{x}\mathbf{u}+\frac{1}{3}\mathbf{D}_{x}\mathbf{u}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{H}_{x}^{-1}\left(\boldsymbol{\alpha}\otimes\left(\mathbf{B}_{Ix}+\mathbf{B}_{nx}\right)\right)\mathbf{u}+\frac{1}{2}\Gamma\left(I_{m}\otimes\left(\mathbf{D}_{+}-\mathbf{D}_{-}\right)\right)\mathbf{u},

(30)

where
\gamma^{k}=\alpha^{k}=\max_{j\in\{1,\cdots,n\}}|{u}^{k}_{j}|>0
.
From the first identity in (
16
) we find that

{\left\langle\mathbf{g},\,\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{u}),\mathbf{D}_{x})\right\rangle_{H_{x}}}=\frac{1}{3}\left\langle\mathbf{u}^{2},\,\mathbf{D}\mathbf{u}\right\rangle_{H}+\frac{1}{3}\left\langle\mathbf{u},\,\mathbf{D}\mathbf{u}^{2}\right\rangle_{H}=0.

It therefore follows that

\derivative{t}E_{h}(t):=\frac{1}{2}\derivative{t}\norm{\vb u}_{H_{x}}^{2}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{\alpha^{k}+\alpha^{k+1}}{2}[\![{\mathbf{u}^{k}}]\!]^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{K}{\gamma^{k}\left\langle\mathbf{u}^{k},\,\left(D_{-}-D_{+}\right)\mathbf{u}^{k}\right\rangle_{H}}\leq 0.

(31)

Note that because of the periodic boundary conditions, at
k=K
we have

\frac{\alpha^{K}+\alpha^{K+1}}{2}[\![{\mathbf{u}^{K}}]\!]^{2}=\frac{\alpha^{K}+\alpha^{1}}{2}({\mathbf{u}_{1}^{1}-\mathbf{u}_{n}^{K}})^{2}.

Next we show the conservation property of the semi-discretisation approximation (
30
) of the Burgers’ equation.
We will show that the total "mass" is semi-discretely conserved, as in the continuous equation.
Consider the time derivative of the total "mass", we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left\langle\mathbf{1},\,\mathbf{u}\right\rangle_{H_{x}}=-\frac{1}{3}\left\langle\mathbf{1},\,\mathbf{u}\mathbf{D}\mathbf{u}\right\rangle_{H_{x}}-\frac{1}{3}\left\langle\mathbf{1},\,\mathbf{D}{\mathbf{u}}^{2}\right\rangle_{H_{x}}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\left\langle\left(\mathbf{B}_{I}+\mathbf{B}_{n}\right)\mathbf{1},\,\mathbf{u}\right\rangle+\frac{\gamma}{2}\left\langle(\mathbf{D}_{+}-\mathbf{D}_{-})\mathbf{1},\,\mathbf{u}\right\rangle_{H}.

With the fact that
H_{x}
is diagonal we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left\langle\mathbf{1},\,\mathbf{u}\right\rangle_{H_{x}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{3}\left\langle\mathbf{u},\,\mathbf{D}\mathbf{u}\right\rangle_{H_{x}}+\frac{1}{3}\left\langle\mathbf{D}\mathbf{1},\,{\mathbf{u}}^{2}\right\rangle_{H_{x}}=-\frac{1}{6}(\left\langle\mathbf{u},\,\mathbf{D}\mathbf{u}\right\rangle_{H_{x}}+\left\langle\mathbf{u},\,\mathbf{D}\mathbf{u}\right\rangle_{H_{x}})=0.

Appendix B
Shallow Water Equations

Now, consider the numerical approximation of the nonlinear shallow water equations in flux form, where the unknowns are
\mathbf{U}=\left(h,uh\right)^{T}
with
h>0
being the water height,
u
is the flow velocity and
g>0
is the acceleration constant due to gravity. The entropy is the total mechanical energy
e(U)=\frac{1}{2}\left(hu^{2}+gh^{2}\right)
. The skew-symmetric form of the divergence of the PDE flux and the entropy variables are given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{U}),\partial_{\mathbf{x}})=\begin{bmatrix}\partial_{x}(hu)\\
\frac{1}{2}\partial_{x}\left(hu^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}u\partial_{x}\left(hu\right)+\frac{1}{2}hu\partial_{x}u+gh\partial_{x}h\end{bmatrix},\quad\mathbf{g}=\begin{bmatrix}gh-\frac{1}{2}u^{2}\\
u\end{bmatrix}.

(32)

A multi-block upwind DP SBP method for the nonlinear shallow water equations in flux form is given by

\displaystyle\derivative{t}\mathbf{U}+\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{U}),\mathbf{D}_{x})=\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{H}_{x}^{-1}\left(\boldsymbol{\alpha}\otimes\left(\mathbf{B}_{Ix}+\mathbf{B}_{nx}\right)\right)\mathbf{g}+\frac{1}{2}\Gamma\left(I_{m}\otimes\left(\mathbf{D}_{+}-\mathbf{D}_{-}\right)\right)\mathbf{g},

(33)

where
\Gamma^{k}=\mathinner{\diag\mathopen{\left([\gamma_{1}^{k},\gamma_{2}^{k}]\right)}}
with, e.g.,
\alpha_{1}^{k}=\gamma_{1}^{k}=\max_{1\leq j\leq n}h^{k}_{j}(|u|+\sqrt{gh^{k}_{j}})/(h^{k}_{j}g+(u^{k}_{j})^{2})\geq 0
,
\alpha_{2}^{k}=\gamma_{2}^{k}=\max_{1\leq j\leq n}|h_{j}^{k}u_{j}^{k}|\geq 0
and

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{U}),\mathbf{D}_{x})=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{D}({\mathbf{h}\mathbf{u}})\\
\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{D}\left({\mathbf{h}\mathbf{u}}^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}{\mathbf{u}}\mathbf{D}\left({\mathbf{h}\mathbf{u}}\right)+\frac{1}{2}{\left(\mathbf{h}\mathbf{u}\right)}\mathbf{D}{\mathbf{u}}+{g\mathbf{h}}\mathbf{D}{\mathbf{h}}\end{bmatrix},\quad\mathbf{g}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{u}\\
g\mathbf{h}-\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{u}^{2}\end{bmatrix}.

(34)

From the first identity in (
16
), it is easy to check that

\displaystyle{\left\langle\mathbf{g},\,\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{U}),\mathbf{D}_{x})\right\rangle_{H_{x}}}=\underbrace{\left\langle g\mathbf{h},\,\mathbf{D}\left(\mathbf{h}\mathbf{u}\right)\right\rangle_{H}+\left\langle\mathbf{u}\mathbf{h},\,\mathbf{D}\left(g\mathbf{h}\right)\right\rangle_{H}}_{=0}+\frac{1}{2}\underbrace{\left(\left\langle\mathbf{u},\,\mathbf{D}\left(\mathbf{h}\mathbf{u}^{2}\right)\right\rangle_{H}+\left\langle\mathbf{h}\mathbf{u}^{2},\,\mathbf{D}\mathbf{u}\right\rangle_{H}\right)}_{=0}=0.

Then it follows that

\displaystyle\derivative{t}E_{h}(t)={\left\langle\mathbf{g},\,\derivative{t}\mathbf{U}\right\rangle_{H_{x}}}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{\alpha_{i}^{k}+\alpha_{i}^{k+1}}{2}[\![{\mathbf{g}_{i}^{k}}]\!]^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{i=1}^{2}{\gamma_{i}^{k}\left\langle\mathbf{g}_{i}^{k},\,\left(D_{-}-D_{+}\right)\mathbf{g}_{i}^{k}\right\rangle_{H}}\leq 0.

Note that because of the periodic boundary conditions, at
k=K
we have

\frac{\alpha_{i}^{K}+\alpha_{i}^{K+1}}{2}[\![{\mathbf{g}_{i}^{K}}]\!]^{2}=\frac{\alpha_{i}^{K}+\alpha_{i}^{1}}{2}({\mathbf{g}_{i1}^{1}-\mathbf{g}_{in}^{K}})^{2}.

Next, we will show that (
33
) semi-discretely conserves mass and momentum.

To begin, we consider the time derivative of the total mass,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left\langle\mathbf{1},\,\mathbf{h}\right\rangle_{H}=-\left\langle\mathbf{D}\mathbf{1},\,\mathbf{hu}\right\rangle_{H}+\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2}\left\langle\left(\mathbf{B}_{I}+\mathbf{B}_{n}\right)\mathbf{1},\,\mathbf{u}\right\rangle+\frac{\gamma_{1}}{2}\left\langle(\mathbf{D}_{+}-\mathbf{D}_{-})\mathbf{1},\,\mathbf{u}\right\rangle_{H}=0.

Similarly, we show that the semi-discrete approximation (
33
) semi-discretely conserves momentum. We take the time derivative of the total momentum,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left\langle\mathbf{1},\,\mathbf{hu}\right\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle=-\left\langle\mathbf{1},\,\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{D}\left(\mathbf{h}\mathbf{u}^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{u}\mathbf{D}\left(\mathbf{hu}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{hu}\mathbf{D}\mathbf{u}+g\mathbf{h}\mathbf{D}\mathbf{h}\right\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha_{2}}{2}\left\langle\left(\mathbf{B}_{I}+\mathbf{B}_{n}\right)\mathbf{1},\,\left(g\mathbf{h}-\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{u}^{2}\right)\right\rangle_{H}-\frac{\gamma_{2}}{2}\left\langle(\mathbf{D}_{-}-\mathbf{D}_{+})\mathbf{1},\,\left(g\mathbf{h}-\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{u}^{2}\right)\right\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\mathbf{D}\mathbf{u},\,\mathbf{hu}\right\rangle_{H}-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\mathbf{hu},\,\mathbf{D}\mathbf{u}\right\rangle_{H}-\frac{g}{2}\left\langle\mathbf{h},\,\mathbf{D}\mathbf{h}\right\rangle_{H}+\frac{g}{2}\left\langle\mathbf{D}\mathbf{h},\,\mathbf{h}\right\rangle_{H}=0.

Appendix C
Compressible Euler Equations

Here, we consider the 1D compressible Euler equations for ideal gas law in conservative form

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\mathbf{U}+\partial_{x}\mathbf{f}=0,\quad x\in[0,L],\quad t\in[0,T],\end{split}

(35)

where the conserved variables and the PDE flux are given by

\mathbf{U}=\begin{bmatrix}\rho\\
\rho u\\
E\end{bmatrix},\quad\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{U})=\begin{bmatrix}\rho u\\
\rho u^{2}+p\\
\left(E+p\right)u\\
\end{bmatrix},\quad E=\frac{p}{\gamma-1}+\frac{1}{2}\varrho u^{2}.

Here,
\rho
denotes the fluid density,
u
is the fluid velocity,
p
denotes the
pressure,
E
is the specific total energy, and
\gamma
denotes the adiabatic index.
We also introduce the thermodynamic entropy

\eta
defined by

\eta=-\rho\log\left(\frac{p}{\rho^{\gamma}}\right),\quad p=(\gamma-1)(E-\frac{1}{2}\rho u^{2}).

(36)

For the compressible Euler equations, deriving a skew-symmetric form that conserves the thermodynamic entropy

\eta
is challenging. In fact, to the best of
our knowledge, we are not aware of a skew-symmetric form that conserves the thermodynamic entropy

\eta
and the conserved variables
\mathbf{U}
, that can be targeted by high-order methods. Instead, we will use the conservative skew-symmetric form of
Nordström
[
53
]
, which considers a set of transformed variables, denoted here by
\mathbf{V}
, and identify an appropriate mathematical entropy functional
e(\mathbf{V})
with respect
to
\mathbf{V}
. However, only entropy functionals with respect to the conservative variables
\mathbf{U}
must satisfy an entropy inequality, otherwise numerical solutions will converge to the wrong weak solutions when shocks are present. As we will see below, our mathematical entropy is a linear combination of the total specific energy
E
and the density
\rho
. Thus the mathematical entropy
e(\mathbf{V})
is a conserved functional and the skew-symmetric form conserves the total mathematical entropy by using integration by parts only. Our numerical scheme will be designed to conserve the total mathematical entropy. However, we will construct both interface and volume upwind terms with respect to the thermodynamic entropy, so that we can ensure thermodynamic entropy consistency in the presence of shocks and discontinuities.
Discrete entropy consistency of the method has been verified through numerical experiments as shown in Figure
5
.

The skew-symmetric form of the compressible Euler equations
from
[
53
]

is given by

\partial_{t}\mathbf{V}+\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{V},\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{V}),\partial_{x})=0,

(37)

where the transformed variables and divergence of flux are given by

\mathbf{V}=\begin{bmatrix}v_{1}\\
v_{2}\\
v_{3}\end{bmatrix}:=\begin{bmatrix}\sqrt{\rho}\\
\sqrt{\rho}u\\
\sqrt{p}\end{bmatrix},\quad\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{V},\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{V}),\partial_{x})=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{2}\left(u\partial_{x}\sqrt{\rho}+\partial_{x}(\sqrt{\rho}u)\right)\\
\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial_{x}(\sqrt{\rho}u^{2})+u\partial_{x}(\sqrt{\rho}u)\right)+2\sqrt{\frac{p}{\rho}}\partial_{x}\sqrt{p}\\
\frac{1}{2}\left[(\gamma-1)\left(\partial_{x}(\sqrt{p}u)-u\partial_{x}\sqrt{p}\right)+\partial_{x}(\sqrt{p}u)+u\partial_{x}\sqrt{p}\right]\end{bmatrix}.

(38)

We define the Jacobian matrices of the forward and inverse transformations
\mathbf{U}\leftrightarrow\mathbf{V}
,

\displaystyle\partial_{\mathbf{U}}\mathbf{\mathbf{V}}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\rho}}&0&0\\
-\frac{u}{2\sqrt{\rho}}&\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho}}&0\\
\frac{(\gamma-1)u^{2}}{4\sqrt{p}}&-\frac{(\gamma-1)u}{2\sqrt{p}}&\frac{\gamma-1}{2\sqrt{p}}\end{bmatrix},\quad\partial_{\mathbf{V}}\mathbf{\mathbf{U}}=\begin{bmatrix}2\sqrt{\rho}&0&0\\
\sqrt{\rho}u&\sqrt{\rho}&0\\
0&\sqrt{\rho}u&2\sqrt{p}/(\gamma-1)\end{bmatrix}.

(39)

Note that
\partial_{\mathbf{V}}\mathbf{\mathbf{U}}=(\partial_{\mathbf{U}}\mathbf{\mathbf{V}})^{-1}
and
\partial_{\mathbf{U}}\mathbf{\mathbf{V}}\partial_{\mathbf{V}}\mathbf{\mathbf{U}}=I
. Thus for all
p>0
and
\rho>0
the Jacobian matrices are invertible.

The associated entropy pair is

e({\mathbf{V}})=\left(\sqrt{\rho}\right)^{2}+\frac{\left(\sqrt{\rho}u\right)^{2}}{2}+\frac{(\sqrt{p})^{2}}{\gamma-1}\equiv\rho+E,\quad q=u(\rho+E+p).

Note that

\displaystyle e({\mathbf{V}})=v_{1}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}v_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}v_{3}^{2},\quad\norm{\vec V}^{2}_{(1-\gamma)}:=\int_{\Omega}ed\Omega.

(40)

Thus the total mathematical entropy
\norm{\vec V}^{2}_{(1-\gamma)}
leads to a norm that is sufficiently strong to control the transformed variables
\mathbf{V}
. The following Lemma will be important for the coming analysis

Lemma C.1
.

Consider the skew-symmetric divergence of the flux
\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{V},\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{V}),\partial_{x})
defined in (
38
) and the Jacobian of the inverse transformation
\mathbf{U}\leftarrow\mathbf{V}
,
\partial_{\mathbf{V}}\mathbf{\mathbf{U}}
given in (
39
). By using integration by parts we have

\int_{\Omega}\partial_{\mathbf{V}}\mathbf{\mathbf{U}}\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{V},\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{V}),\partial_{x})\mathrm{dx}=\oint_{\partial\Omega}\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{U})n_{x}\mathrm{dx},\quad\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{U})=\begin{bmatrix}\rho u\\
\rho u^{2}+p\\
\left(E+p\right)u\\
\end{bmatrix}.

Proof.

We consider

\displaystyle\partial_{\mathbf{V}}\mathbf{\mathbf{U}}\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{V},\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{V}),\partial_{x})=\begin{bmatrix}{\sqrt{\rho}u}{\partial_{x}\sqrt{\rho}}+{\sqrt{\rho}}{\partial_{x}(\sqrt{\rho}u)}\\
\frac{1}{2}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\left(\sqrt{\rho}u^{2}\partial_{x}\sqrt{\rho}+\sqrt{\rho}\partial_{x}(\sqrt{\rho}u^{2})\right)}+\frac{1}{2}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\left(\sqrt{\rho}u\partial_{x}(\sqrt{\rho}u)+\sqrt{\rho}u\partial_{x}(\sqrt{\rho}u)\right)}+\sqrt{p}\partial_{x}\sqrt{p}\\
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{\rho}u\partial_{x}(\sqrt{\rho}u^{2})+\sqrt{\rho}u^{2}\partial_{x}(\sqrt{\rho}u)}+{\sqrt{p}u\partial_{x}\sqrt{p}}+{\sqrt{p}}{\partial_{x}(\sqrt{p}u)+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{p}\partial_{x}(\sqrt{p}u)}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{p}u\partial_{x}\sqrt{p}}\right)}\\
\end{bmatrix}.

We integrate the expression above and apply integration by parts only, and we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\partial_{\mathbf{V}}\mathbf{\mathbf{U}}\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{V},\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{V}),\partial_{x})\mathrm{dx}=\int_{\Omega}{\partial_{x}\begin{bmatrix}\rho u\\
\rho u^{2}+p\\
\left(E+p\right)u\\
\end{bmatrix}}\mathrm{dx}=\oint_{\partial\Omega}{\begin{bmatrix}\rho u\\
\rho u^{2}+p\\
\left(E+p\right)u\\
\end{bmatrix}n_{x}}\mathrm{dx}.

∎

Using the above Lemma the conservation of total mass, total momentum and total energy follows from multiplying
eq.

37
by
\partial_{\mathbf{V}}\mathbf{\mathbf{U}}
and integrating over the domain to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\partial_{\mathbf{V}}\mathbf{\mathbf{U}}\partial_{t}\mathbf{V}+\partial_{\mathbf{V}}\mathbf{\mathbf{U}}\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{V},\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{V}),\partial_{x})\mathrm{dx}=0\iff\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\mathbf{U}\mathrm{dx}+\oint_{\partial\Omega}\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{U})n_{x}=0.

Note that the conservation of the total mass and the total energy implies that the total mathematical entropy is also conserved. That is

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\norm{\vec V}^{2}_{(1-\gamma)}:=\partial_{t}\int_{\Omega}ed\Omega=-\oint_{\partial\Omega}(qn_{x})\mathrm{dx},\quad q=u(\rho+E+p).

For periodic boundary conditions the boundary terms vanish and we have

\partial_{t}\norm{\vec V}^{2}_{(1-\gamma)}=0.

We will formulate the result as the theorem

Theorem C.2
.

Consider the the 1D compressible Euler equations in the skew-symmetric form
eq.

37
on the spatial domain
\Omega=[0,L]
with periodic boundary conditions and smooth initial data. For continuous solutions
\mathbf{V}:\Omega\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
, at time
t\in[0,T]
let the total mathematical entropy be denoted by

\displaystyle\norm{\vec V}^{2}_{(1-\gamma)}:=\int_{\Omega}ed\Omega,\quad e({\mathbf{V}})=v_{1}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}v_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}v_{3}^{2},\quad\gamma>1.

(41)

The total mathematical entropy is conserved, that is we have

\partial_{t}\norm{\vec V}^{2}_{(1-\gamma)}=0\iff\norm{\vec V(\cdot, t)}^{2}_{(1-\gamma)}=\norm{\vec V(\cdot, 0)}^{2}_{(1-\gamma)},\quad\forall\,t\in[0,T].

It is significantly noteworthy that the transformed variables
\mathbf{V}
are our prognostic variables and the conserved variables
\mathbf{U}
are the diagnostic variables. Thus, for continuous solutions,
theorem

C.2
will provide a strong bound for the transformed variables
\mathbf{V}
. It is desirable that our numerical method emulate
theorem

C.2
.

D.4
Barotropic Shear Instability

Our final numerical example for the nonlinear shallow water equations is the the barotropic shear instability
[
41
,
72
,
73
]
. This is also known as the Kelvin-Helmholtz instability, and involves triggering a barotropic shear within zonal jets by initializing the flow with a thin fluid discontinuity, supplemented with small Gaussian perturbations.

The computational domain is
\Omega=[0,L]^{2}
, with
L=2\pi\times$6371.22\text{\,}\mathrm{km}$
with doubly periodic boundary conditions. The initial conditions are

u_{0}=\bar{u}_{0}\left(\mathinner{\mathrm{sech}\mathopen{\left(\frac{y-y_{+}}{10^{6}}\right)}}-\mathinner{\mathrm{sech}\mathopen{\left(\frac{y-y_{-}}{10^{6}}\right)}}\right),\quad v_{0}=0,\quad{h}_{0}=H-\frac{f}{g}\int_{0}^{y}u(x,s)\differential{s}+\tilde{h}(x,y),

with

\tilde{h}(x,y)=\bar{h}_{0}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\exp(-k d_i(x,y)),\quad d_{i}(x,y)=\frac{\left(x-x_{i}\right)^{2}}{L^{2}}+\frac{\left(y-y_{i}\right)^{2}}{L^{2}}\quad\forall i=\{1,2\},

where

\bar{h}_{0}=0.01H
,

\left(x_{1},y_{1}\right)=\left(0.15L,y_{+}\right)
,

\left(x_{2},y_{2}\right)=\left(0.85L,y_{-}\right)
,

y_{+}=0.25L
,

y_{-}=0.75L
,

\bar{u}_{0}=$50\text{\,}\mathrm{m}\text{\,}{\mathrm{s}}^{-1}$
,

f=$7.292\text{\times}{10}^{-5}$
,

g=$9.806\,16\text{\,}\mathrm{m}\text{\,}{\mathrm{s}}^{-2}$
,

H=$10\text{\,}\mathrm{km}$
,

k=10^{3}
.

We consider DG schemes using 6th degree polynomials on
64^{2}
elements and 6th interior order accurate FD schemes on
6^{2}
elements with
65^{2}
nodes each. The solutions are evolved using a fixed timestep of
\Delta t=2\times 10^{-3}\Delta x
until the final time
t=80\,\mathrm{days}
.
Snapshots of the absolute vorticity can be found in
fig.

17
.
In

, we plot the relative change with time of the total energy/entropy and the total enstrophy.

Similar to the merging vortices testcase, the entropy/energy conservative DGSEM/SBP FD methods without volume upwinding exhibit uncontrolled growth of the total enstrophy and generate numerical noise that destroys the solution. Meanwhile, the entropy stable DP FD/DG methods with volume upwinding are able to suppress the unphysical enstrophy growth and resolve the structure of the vortices without generating spurious high frequency oscillations.

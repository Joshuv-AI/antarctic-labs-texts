BET Protein Degrader ZBC260 Induces Tumor Vascular Reprogramming Through Endothelial Cell Remodeling
Authors: Deeksha Sharma, Cody G Hager, Max S Wicha, Monika L Burness
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeEndothelial cells regulate angiogenesis, matrix remodeling, and immune responses within the tumor microenvironment, yet endothelial cell heterogeneity across breast cancer subtypes and its regulation by bromodomain and extraterminal domain proteins remain unclear. This study profiled endothelial cells at single-cell resolution in two immunocompetent murine breast cancer models with distinct tumor immune microenvironments: PyMT_FVB/N and D2A1_BALB/c tumors.MethodsMice were treated intravenously with the bromodomain and extraterminal domain degrader (ZBC260) at 10mg/kg 3 days/week until tumors reached humane endpoints and were harvested. Single-cell RNA sequencing was performed on vehicle and ZBC260 treated tumors (n=3 per condition), and endothelial cell populations were identified and analyzed using Seurat package in R.ResultsSix transcriptionally distinct endothelial subpopulations were identified across both breast cancer models, with D2A1 tumors dominated by tip-like and proliferative endothelial cells with strong angiogenic and collagen-producing signatures. ZBC260 reprogrammed endothelial landscape by reducing proliferative, tip-like, and arterial endothelial cells while enriching venous and lymphatic endothelial cells. ZBC260 suppressed hypoxia, glycolysis, and mTORC1 signaling, downregulated angiogenesis and matrix associated pathways and was accompanied by reduced collagen signatures and increased leukocyte adhesion. Cell–cell communication analysis demonstrated that ZBC260 disrupted endothelial cells interactions with tumor and immune cells, by disrupting ligand–receptor communications and attenuating NOTCH, TGF-β, and VEGF signaling networks that sustain angiogenesis and immune suppression.ConclusionTogether, these findings highlight endothelial diversity across breast cancer models and identify ZBC260 as a regulator of tumor endothelial states. ZBC260 reprograms endothelial heterogeneity and remodels the tumor vascular landscape. The transcriptomic and cell–cell communication changes observed are consistent with the establishment of a more immune-permissive tumor microenvironment, supporting the potential of BET degradation as a strategy to complement immunotherapy and improve therapeutic responsiveness in breast cancer.

Introduction

Breast cancer remains a leading cause of cancer-related mortality in women and is characterized by high heterogeneity, metastasis, and therapeutic resistance.1–3 Despite advances in treatment, patient outcomes remain poor due to the complex, diverse, and adaptive nature of the tumor microenvironment (TME).3 While immunotherapy has improved cancer treatment, its benefit is largely limited to patients with immunologically “hot” (immune-inflamed) tumors, whereas breast cancers with a “cold” (immune-desert) TME fail to support effective immune infiltration and activation.4,5 The immune phenotype of the TME is determined by the composition and function of both immune and stromal cells, highlighting the importance of targeting not only the tumor cells but also the surrounding microenvironment to improve therapy responses.6

Although precision oncology has advanced the identification of tumor-intrinsic therapeutic targets across breast cancer subtypes, these tumor-centered approaches may not fully address the contribution of the TME to disease progression and therapeutic resistance.7,8 Among TME populations, endothelial cells (ECs) are critical yet understudied regulators of tumor progression and therapy resistance, forming abnormal tumor-associated vasculature that supports growth and metastasis, while limiting immune infiltration.9,10 Beyond their structural role, ECs display significant phenotypic plasticity, undergoing endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EndMT) and acquiring immune-regulatory and pro-invasive functions.11,12 Additionally, ECs modulate anti-tumor immunity through cytokine secretion, antigen presentation, and extracellular matrix remodeling.13 Therapies targeting ECs, primarily via anti-angiogenic treatments (AATs), have achieved limited success due to EC heterogeneity and intrinsic plasticity.14 In some preclinical models, AATs can paradoxically promote tumor invasion and metastasis, likely driven by hypoxia-induced epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT), activation of MET signaling, and stromal cell recruitment.15

These limitations underscore the need to better define the functional diversity of tumor-associated ECs and the mechanisms that regulate their phenotypic states. Advances in single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNAseq) have begun to resolve this complexity, revealing transcriptionally distinct EC subtypes with specialized roles in angiogenesis, vascular remodeling, leukocyte trafficking, and immune regulation.16,17 In breast tumors, previously described EC populations include angiogenic, arterial, venous, and lymphatic ECs, with angiogenic ECs exhibiting transcriptional features associated with vascular sprouting and remodeling, while arterial and venous ECs reflect distinct vascular and inflammatory programs and lymphatic ECs contribute to lymphatic vascular function and immune interactions.18 Within the angiogenic compartment, tip-like ECs represent a specialized state associated with active vessel sprouting.17 Together, these findings highlight the diversity and plasticity of breast cancer endothelium. However, the molecular factors that regulate endothelial state transitions and the functional consequences of endothelial heterogeneity in breast cancer remain poorly understood. In particular, whether epigenetic regulators such as Bromodomain and extraterminal domain (BET) proteins contribute to the regulation of distinct endothelial states remains unknown.

BET proteins are epigenetic regulators of genes involved in cancer progression, stemness, inflammation, and angiogenesis19,20 which has led to the development of BET inhibitors (BETi) and selective BET degraders (BETd), several of which are now in clinical trials. BET inhibition not only targets tumor cells but also enhances anti-tumor immunity by reprogramming immune cells and sensitizing immunologically cold tumors to immunotherapy.21–25 Beyond tumor and immune cells, BET targeting can influence stromal cells of the TME. In cancer-associated fibroblasts, BET inhibition has been shown to suppress fibroblast activation and tumor-promoting functions.26 In ECs, BET suppression inhibits VEGF-induced angiogenesis and vascular permeability through VEGFR2-mediated PAK1/eNOS signaling.27 Similarly, BRD4 inhibition suppresses angiogenesis through regulation of AP-1 expression.27,28 More recently transcriptomic analyses demonstrated that BET proteins regulate basal endothelial gene expression programs involved in migration, proliferation, and angiogenesis, further supporting a role for BET proteins in maintaining endothelial cell function.29 Although these studies establish important roles for BET proteins in endothelial biology, their function within tumor-associated endothelial populations remains poorly defined. Whether BET proteins regulate ECs heterogeneity, endothelial state transitions, and interactions with tumor and immune cells remain largely unknown. In particular, it is unclear whether BET degradation can selectively reprogram tumor-associated endothelial populations to remodel the tumor vasculature and enhance anti-tumor immunity.

We hypothesized that BET protein degradation would reprogram ECs heterogeneity, reduce pro-tumor endothelial states, and remodel vascular–immune interactions to generate a more immune-permissive tumor microenvironment. To determine whether BET-dependent endothelial remodeling is conserved across distinct tumor contexts, we used single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) to profile ECs states in two complementary immunocompetent murine breast cancer models. MMTV-PyMT tumors develop spontaneously in the mammary gland through oncogene-driven tumorigenesis, whereas D2A1 tumors are established by orthotopic transplantation of a syngeneic mammary carcinoma cell line into BALB/c mice. These models differ in tumor origin, progression, and host context while preserving intact tumor–immune–stromal interactions.30,31 To pharmacologically target BET proteins, we used the pan-BET degrader ZBC260, which has previously demonstrated anti-tumor efficacy in breast cancer models by suppressing tumor cell stemness and reprogramming inflammatory signaling.20 Importantly, in the D2A1 model used in the present study, ZBC260 increased lymphoid infiltration while reducing fibroblast and endothelial populations.32 Building on these findings, we investigated how BET degradation directly reprograms EC states and vascular–immune interactions.

We identified distinct EC subpopulations with diverse transcriptional and functional states. Treatment with the BET degrader ZBC260 reprogrammed EC heterogeneity, suppressed pro-tumor signaling, and disrupted EC-mediated key ligand–receptor interactions with tumor and immune cells. These findings highlight the complexity and plasticity of ECs in breast cancer and reveal a previously unrecognized role for BET proteins. BET degradation represents a promising strategy to remodel tumor vasculature and enhance immune responsiveness in breast cancer. By targeting endothelial plasticity and vascular states in addition to tumor-intrinsic pathways, this approach may complement current precision oncology strategies aimed at overcoming therapeutic resistance and improving responses to existing treatments.

Materials and Methods

In vivo Experiment and Tumor Tissue Collection

All animal studies were approved by the University of Michigan Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) (PRO00011242, RRID:SCR_025789), conducted in accordance with institutional guidelines and the National Institutes of Health Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals, and conducted in a pathogen-free facility.20 D2A1 cells (RRID#CVCL_0I90) were provided by the laboratory of Hunter Kent (National Cancer Institute, USA). PyMT cells were derived from a spontaneous MMTV-PyMT tumor in the laboratory of Max S Wicha (University of Michigan, USA) under IACUC approved protocols. The use of these murine cell lines was approved by the University of Michigan Institutional Biosafety Committee (IBCA00000534) and IACUC, and all studies were conducted in accordance with institutional animal care and use guidelines. Female six-week-old FVBN (Cat#001800, RRID: IMSR_JAX:001800, Jackson Laboratory, Bar Harbor, Maine, USA) and BALB/c mice (Cat#000651, RRID#IMSR_JAX:000651, Jackson Laboratory, Bar Harbor, Maine, USA) were anesthetized using inhaled isoflurane (delivered via precision vaporizer at 4–5% for induction and 1–2% for maintenance) and orthotopically injected with 5×105 PyMT or D2A1 cells into the fourth left mammary fat pad. Once tumors reached approximately 200 mm3, mice (n = 10 per group) were randomized to receive ZBC260 (10 mg/kg) (provided by Dr. Shaomeng Wang Lab (Department of Pharmacology, University of Michigan, Ann Arbor, MI, USA) and purchased (Cat#HY-101519, MedChemExpress, Monmouth Junction, NJ, USA) or vehicle (20% PEG400, 6% Cremophor EL) intravenously three times per week. Tumor size and body weight were monitored twice weekly. Once any tumor within a given murine model (PyMT or D2A1) reached 2cm in any direction, the experiment was terminated. Mice were euthanized by carbon dioxide inhalation (gradual fill rate of 40% chamber volume displacement per minute) consistent with AVMA guidelines. Bilateral pneumothorax was used as secondary physical method to ensure death. Treatment duration prior to euthanasia was 35 days for the PyMT model and 19 days for the D2A1 model. Tumors were excised under aseptic conditions and processed for formalin fixed paraffin embedding (FFPE). FFPE blocks were submitted for subsequent Single-cell RNA sequencing (n=3). In vivo experiments included 10 biological replicates per experimental group. Three independent tumor samples per condition were randomly selected for single-cell RNA sequencing (n = 3/group), with each sample analyzed individually without pooling, consistent with commonly used scRNA-seq study designs for characterization of cellular heterogeneity and cellular states.

Single-Cell RNA Sequencing (scRNA-Seq), and Read Alignment

Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) was performed at the Advanced Genomics Core, University of Michigan, using the 10x Genomics Chromium platform with Single Cell 3′ Reagent Kits v3. Libraries were converted with the MGIEasy Universal Library Conversion Kit for sequencing on the MGISEQ-2000 platform (RRID:SCR_017980, MGI Tech Co., Shenzhen, Guangdong, China). Raw sequencing data was processed by the Advanced Genomics Core. Gene expression matrices were generated using Cell Ranger, with reads aligned to the mouse reference genome (mm10, v8.0.1, RRID:SCR_023221). Sequencing quality metrics, including the number of recovered cells per sample, sequencing depth, median reads per cell, median genes detected per cell, and median unique molecular identifiers (UMIs) per cell, were used to evaluate sequencing quality and overall dataset robustness.

scRNA-Seq Data Processing

Data analysis was performed in Seurat (v5.2.0, RRID:SCR_016341) following the Seurat vignette (https://satijalab.org/seurat/articles/pbmc3k_tutorial). The cell-by-gene count metrices obtained from CellRanger’s output were subsequently imported into R (v4.3.30) with Seurat package (v5.4.0) for downstream analyses. Quality control was performed to filter out low-quality cells with fewer than 200 detected genes or >10% mitochondrial genes contents were excluded. The filtered count matrices were integrated and processed mainly with Seurat package. Potential batch effects between sequencing runs and samples were assessed and corrected using the Seurat integration workflow. Integration anchors were identified using FindIntegrationAnchors, followed by dataset integration using IntegrateData.

Data were log-normalized by scaling counts and log-transforming values. Dimensionality reduction was conducted using principal component analysis (PCA), retaining 20 principal components for further steps. Clustering was performed via FindNeighbors to construct a k-nearest neighbor graph, followed by FindClusters with a resolution of 0.2. Clusters were visualized using Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP). Differential gene expression analysis across clusters was conducted with FindAllMarkers and Wilcoxon rank-sum tests, considering adjusted p-values < 0.05 as significant.33

scRNA-Seq Annotation

All major cell populations were initially annotated based on cluster-specific differentially expressed genes together with established canonical marker genes reported in the literature. Major cellular populations were identified using their characteristic marker expression profiles.34 Following initial clustering and annotation, ECs were identified and extracted for independent processing. The endothelial subset was re-normalized, reclustered, and subclusters were annotated using cluster-specific differentially expressed genes, canonical endothelial markers, and established endothelial transcriptomic classifications from published single-cell studies18,34,35 to resolve endothelial heterogeneity.

Pathway Enrichment Analysis and Calculation of Module Score

Pathway enrichment analysis was performed using the clusterProfiler R package. Gene Ontology Biological Process (GO-BP), Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG), and the Hallmark gene set collection from the Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB; mouse Hallmark collection, mh.all.v2025.1mm.entrez.gmt) were used for enrichment analyses. Enrichment significance was assessed using statistical testing implemented in clusterProfiler, and multiple comparisons were corrected using the Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) method. Pathways with adjusted P values <0.05 were considered significantly enriched.

Angiogenesis, collagen formation, and leukocyte adhesion scores were calculated using the Seurat AddModuleScore function based on predefined gene signatures.36 Module scores were used to evaluate endothelial functional scores in both tumor models (PyMT and D2A1) and to assess differences associated with ZBC260 treatment compared with vehicle treatment.

Cell-Cell Communication Analysis

Cell-cell communication analysis was performed using the CellChat R package (v1.1.3).37 For mouse datasets, ligand-receptor interactions were inferred using the CellChatDB.mouse reference database. The input dataset consisted of annotated cell populations generated from the Seurat analysis. Genes were screened for overexpression using identifyOverExpressedGenes with a significance threshold of P <0.05, followed by identification of overexpressed ligand-receptor interactions using identifyOverExpressedInteractions. Communication probabilities were calculated using computeCommunProb based on CellChat’s permutation-based statistical framework. Significant signaling pathways were identified using computeCommunProbPathway (threshold = 0.05), and interaction networks were summarized using aggregateNet (threshold = 0.05). Multiple testing correction was performed using the Benjamini–Hochberg method, and ligand-receptor interactions with adjusted P <0.05 were considered significant.

Survival Analysis

The KM Plotter online tool (http://www.kmplot.com), a publicly available database, was used to study the relationship between the expression of tip-like EC markers and overall survival in breast cancer patients. Patients were divided into two groups (high vs low expression) based on the median gene expression value.

Statistical Analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using R (Version 4.3.0). Differential gene expression analyzed using the Wilcoxon Rank Sum test, with p-values adjusted for multiple comparisons using the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure. A p-value threshold of <0.05 was considered statistically significant. For functional enrichment analyses, statistical significance was determined using Benjamini–Hochberg adjusted P values, with an adjusted P value <0.05 considered significant. For module score analyses, comparisons were performed to evaluate differences between the PyMT and D2A1 tumor models, as well as differences between ZBC260-treated and vehicle-treated groups. Statistical comparisons were performed using an unpaired two-tailed Student’s t-test. Individual biological replicates were considered the experimental unit, and a P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Graphical Figures

Results

Model-Specific Endothelial Transcriptional Differences in Breast Tumor Microenvironments

To characterize ECs heterogeneity across breast cancer models, we performed single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) of tumors from two immunocompetent murine breast cancer models, PyMT_FVB/N and D2A1_BALB/c (Figure 1A). Sequencing metrics are summarized in Supplementary Table S1. Initial unsupervised clustering identified the major cellular compartments in the PyMT_FVB/N model, including tumor (Krt18), myeloid (Ptprc, Csf1r), lymphoid (Ptprc, Cd3e, Cd19), fibroblast (Col3a1, Col1a1), and endothelial cells (Eng, Cdh5)34 (Figure 1B and C; Supplemental Figure S1A). Following initial clustering and annotation of the whole-tumor scRNA-seq datasets, 3,611 ECs were identified, of which 3,435 cells were retained for EC-specific analysis. These cells were subsequently subsetted from the integrated Seurat object for endothelial-specific analysis. Unsupervised dimensional reduction revealed a clear separation of endothelial populations derived from D2A1 and PyMT tumors, with minimal overlap in the UMAP projection (Figure 1D). Differential gene expression analysis identified 4,120 significantly differentially expressed genes between PyMT- and D2A1-derived ECs, including 3,201 upregulated and 919 downregulated genes. Hierarchical clustering based on the differentially expressed endothelial genes further demonstrated distinct transcriptional profiles between the two models. D2A1-derived ECs exhibited higher expression of Cfl1, Vim, Lgals1, and Set, among other genes whereas PyMT-derived ECs showed higher expression of Chil1, Ly6e, Col9a2, Sox10, Tmc4, Cdh1, and Elf5 (Figure 1E; Supplemental Figure S1B and C). These findings suggest model-specific differences in endothelial gene expression profiles.

Pathway enrichment analysis identified 51 significantly enriched pathways, with the top pathways shown in Figure 1F. D2A1-derived ECs were enriched for epithelial–mesenchymal transition, MYC target, and TGF-β signaling pathways, whereas PyMT-derived ECs showed enrichment for hypoxia, p53, and TNFα signaling pathways. Consistent with these transcriptional differences, module scores for angiogenesis and collagen formation further highlighted functional distinctions: D2A1-derived ECs exhibited higher angiogenic activity, while PyMT-derived ECs showed increased collagen formation scores (Figure 1G and H). Together, these findings demonstrate pronounced model-specific endothelial transcriptional heterogeneity, with distinct molecular and functional programs across D2A1 and PyMT tumors.

Endothelial Heterogeneity and Differential EC State Enrichment Revealed by Single-Cell Analysis

To build on our understanding of EC heterogeneity in these two diverse models, we integrated scRNA-seq data from both PyMT_FVB/N and D2A1_BALB/c tumor models and performed unsupervised clustering specifically on ECs. Subclustering revealed six transcriptionally distinct ECs populations, visualized using UMAP (Figure 2A). Endothelial subtypes were annotated using both unbiased approaches and canonical differential markers.18,34–36 The identified EC subtypes included Arap3⁺ (expressing Rgcc, Arap3, Jak3), proliferative (pECs; expressing Mki67, Top2a, Pcna), tip-like (expressing Cxcr4, Col4a1, Mcam), arterial (expressing Gja5, Gja4, Fbln5), venous (expressing Ackr1, Selp, Clu), and lymphatic (expressing Prox1, Lyve1, Vcam) (Figure 2B–D). DEGs for each subtype were analyzed for pathway enrichment, and Gene Ontology (GO) analysis indicated that each subtype was enriched for distinct biological pathways, highlighting their functional specialization (Supplemental Figure S2A–F). Comparative analysis revealed distinct endothelial state distributions between PyMT and D2A1 tumors (Figure 2E). PyMT tumors exhibited a broader distribution of EC states. Arap3⁺ ECs constituted the largest proportion (0.417%), followed by pECs (0.257%) and tip-like ECs (0.251%), whereas arterial, venous, and lymphatic ECs represented smaller proportions (0.052%, 0.013%, and 0.010% respectively). In contrast, D2A1 tumors were predominantly composed of tip-like ECs (0.792%) and proliferative ECs (0.184%), while Arap3⁺ and arterial ECs represented only 0.010% and 0.014% of the EC cells respectively (Figure 2F). Together, these findings highlight differences in endothelial heterogeneity and vascular phenotype between the two tumor models.

To further assess functional specialization inferred from transcriptional profiles, we calculated angiogenesis, collagen formation, and leukocyte-endothelial adhesion scores. Ridge plot analyses showed higher leukocyte adhesion signatures in venous ECs, while tip-like ECs displayed elevated collagen formation and angiogenesis scores (Figure 2G). To determine the prognostic relevance of tip-like EC marker genes in breast cancer patients, we performed Kaplan–Meier survival analysis using TCGA breast cancer data. High expression levels of ESM1 and ANGPT2 were correlated with worse overall survival compared to low expression levels (Figure 2H and I). Because these analyses were performed using bulk tumor transcriptomic data, expression of these genes may reflect contributions from multiple cellular compartments in addition to tip-like ECs. This correlation supports the clinical relevance of tip-like EC-associated signatures. Overall, our findings suggest that tip-like ECs may play a central role in extracellular matrix remodeling and angiogenic activity, potentially supporting tumor growth and vascular remodeling.

ZBC260 Reprograms Endothelial Cells and Suppresses Tumor-Supportive Pathways

To assess the effects of ZBC260 on tumor ECs, we analyzed ECs from the PyMT and D2A1 tumor models and integrated the datasets for visualization. The integrated UMAP showed marked differences in the distribution of EC populations following ZBC260 treatment (Figure 3A). Quantitative analysis revealed a shift in EC subtype distribution following ZBC260 treatment, characterized by reductions in the proportion of proliferative, tip-like, and arterial ECs and enrichment of lymphatic and venous ECs (Figure 3B). To determine whether the effects of ZBC260 were conserved across tumor models, we compared EC populations in PyMT and D2A1 tumors. The most consistent change was observed in tip-like ECs, which decreased by approximately 50% following ZBC260 treatment in both models, from 69.1% to 30.9% in PyMT tumors and from 66.5% to 33.5% in D2A1 tumors. In contrast, the effect on pECs was model-dependent. In PyMT tumors, pECs decreased substantially from 73.3% to 26.7%, whereas in D2A1 tumors pECs remained unchanged, representing approximately 50% of the EC compartment in both vehicle and treatment groups. Arap3+ and arterial ECs represented relatively small populations in D2A1 tumors, limiting interpretation of treatment-associated changes in these subsets (Supplemental Figure S3A and B).

Some differences are likely reflects the distinct baseline endothelial composition of the two tumor models. Six EC populations were identified in PyMT tumors, including venous and lymphatic ECs, both of which increased following ZBC260 treatment. In contrast, these populations were not detected in D2A1 tumors. Together, these findings indicate that ZBC260 consistently depleted tip-like ECs across tumor models while producing model-specific changes in other endothelial populations, underscoring both conserved and context-dependent regulation of endothelial heterogeneity within the tumor microenvironment.

Differential gene expression analysis revealed widespread transcriptional changes in ECs following ZBC260 treatment. A volcano plot illustrated differential gene expression between vehicle and ZBC260 treated ECs, with Glycam1 upregulated (Supplemental Figure S4A). GO and KEGG pathway enrichment analyses of upregulated genes indicated activation of immune-related pathways, including antigen presentation, lymphocyte-mediated immunity, leukocyte adhesion, and positive regulation of adaptive immune responses. Conversely, downregulated pathways were primarily associated with HIF-1 signaling and carbohydrate metabolism (Supplemental Figure 4B–E).

Given that tip-like ECs are a predominant population in both tumor models and exhibit elevated collagen formation and angiogenesis scores, we further analyzed their functional and transcriptional changes following ZBC260 treatment. In tip-like ECs, ZBC260 treatment significantly downregulated 10 hallmark pathways, including hypoxia, glycolysis, and mTORC1 signaling, with no hallmark pathways significantly upregulated (Figure 3C). GO analysis revealed suppression of the epithelial cell migration pathway, accompanied by upregulation of leukocyte migration–related pathways (Figure 3D and E). Analysis of the top 20 downregulated genes highlighted suppression of critical regulators of tumor vascular remodeling, including Lox, Perp, and Nrg1 (Figure 3F). Additionally, collagen formation score was reduced, leukocyte adhesion score was enhanced, and angiogenesis score remained unchanged following treatment (Figure 3G). Together, these results demonstrate that ZBC260 remodels ECs, selectively reprogramming tip-like ECs and suppressing tumor-supportive transcriptional pathways.

BET Degradation Reduces Endothelial TME Crosstalk and Disrupts Key Ligand–Receptor Signaling Pathways

To investigate interactions between EC subtypes and other cells within the TME, we performed cell–cell communication analysis using CellChat.37 Interaction strength analysis revealed that tip-like ECs exhibited the highest overall interaction strength, predominantly interacting with tumor and immune cells (myeloid and lymphoid) (Figure 4A). Arap⁺ and arterial ECs also demonstrated preferential signaling with myeloid cells in untreated tumors. ZBC260 treatment significantly reduced the overall interaction strength of ECs, with tip-like ECs showing the most pronounced decrease in signaling with both immune and tumor cells, decreasing to nearly half of control levels (Figure 4B). Consistent with the reduction in overall interaction strength, ZBC260 also altered the number and distribution of predicted EC interactions. The total number of predicted interactions decreased modestly from 1,146 to 1,102. However, this limited overall change masked substantial subtype-specific remodeling of the predicted communication network (Supplementary Table S2). The largest reduction in predicted interactions was observed for tip-like ECs, which decreased from 286 to 189 total interactions, including marked reductions in predicted interactions with lymphoid (64 to 20) and myeloid (77 to 42) cell populations. Decreases in the number of predicted interactions were also observed for arterial, proliferative, Arap3⁺, and lymphatic ECs. In contrast, predicted interactions involving venous ECs were detected only in ZBC260 treated tumors. Additionally, the number of predicted interactions between tumor cells and both lymphatic and venous ECs increased following treatment. Together, these findings indicate that BET degradation has a limited effect on the overall number of predicted EC interactions but is associated with substantial redistribution of predicted EC–TME communication across endothelial subtypes and interacting cell populations.

Ligand–receptor analysis identified the COL4A1–SDC4 axis as one of the most prominent interactions across all EC subtypes, which was significantly (p<0.05) reduced following treatment. In addition, multiple collagen- and integrin-mediated interactions between ECs and tumor cells, as well as between ECs and fibroblasts, were disrupted. Interactions between ECs and myeloid cells exhibited subtype-specific patterns. However, SPP1-mediated signaling, which is a shared axis across EC subsets, was completely abolished after treatment. Communication with lymphoid cells was broadly reduced, with the notable exception of APP–CD74 signaling, which was increased following treatment. In tip-like ECs in particular, several signaling interactions were differentially affected. Notably, DLL4–NOTCH1 and HSP2–DAG1 signaling between ECs and lymphoid cells were disrupted, while APP-mediated interactions (including APP–SORL1 and APP–TNFRSF21) showed altered activity. In myeloid cell interactions, F11R–F11R, APP–TREM2–TYROBP, and SPP1–ITGA9–ITGB4 signaling axes were completely disrupted following treatment (Figure 4C–F; Supplemental Figures S5A–H, S6A–H and S7A–D).

Pathway-level analysis showed strong suppression of NOTCH, TGFb and VEGF signaling with arterial and tip-like ECs primarily mediating communications with tumor and immune cells (Figure 4G–I). Collectively, these findings indicate that BET degradation reprograms endothelial communication within the TME, dampening angiogenic and immunosuppressive networks that support tumor progression.

Overall, our data demonstrates that BETd reshapes endothelial heterogeneity at multiple levels suppressing pathological hypoxic, metabolic, and MYC-driven signaling while enhancing immune-supportive features and weakening tumor–endothelial–immune suppressive interactions. These integrated results highlight a unifying mechanism through which BETd remodels the vascular niche toward a less immunosuppressive and more therapeutically responsive state (Figure 5). Discussion

Breast cancers with aggressive phenotypes and treatment resistance remain a major clinical challenge.1,2 The TME, particularly ECs, contributes to therapeutic failure by promoting angiogenesis, immune evasion, and metastasis.9,10 Traditional anti-angiogenic therapies have demonstrated limited and often transient benefits, largely because they fail to address the heterogeneity and plasticity of EC populations.14 In this study, single-cell analyses revealed distinct EC subpopulations across breast cancer models and demonstrated that BET protein degradation reprograms EC transcriptional states, suppresses tumor-promoting signaling, and disrupts key intercellular communication networks. Together, these findings highlight endothelial state remodeling as a potential mechanism for reshaping the TME and suggest new therapeutic opportunities to overcome resistance in aggressive breast cancers.

The distinct model-specific distribution of endothelial populations between D2A1 and PyMT tumors suggests that endothelial transcriptional states are strongly influenced by the tumor context in which they develop. Although both models represent mammary carcinoma, they differ substantially in tumor biology, including oncogenic drivers, tumor architecture, growth kinetics, and interaction with the surrounding microenvironment.30,31,38 Therefore, the observed endothelial heterogeneity may reflect differences in tumor-derived signals, rather than representing a single conserved tumor endothelial phenotype. Differences in tumor progression and treatment duration may also contribute to the distinct EC states observed between the models, although inherent tumor- and host-specific differences cannot be excluded.39

Our results demonstrate that ECs are not a homogeneous population but instead comprise multiple subtypes with specialized functional properties. Similar EC diversity has been reported in human breast cancer,40 in murine endothelial cell atlases,34 and across several other cancer types.36,41–43 Consistent with these observations, our findings indicate that EC composition and function vary depending on the TME. Specifically, MMTV-PyMT tumors exhibited a diverse array of EC subtypes, whereas D2A1 tumors were enriched for pro-angiogenic, tip-like EC states.43,44 Previous studies have identified tip-like ECs as a key subtype distinguishing tumor tissue from normal tissue, associated with tumor angiogenesis and immune-suppressive features, and a high proportion of tip-like ECs has been correlated with poor clinical outcomes across multiple cancer types.43 These findings provide a context for interpreting the preferential remodeling of tip-like ECs following BET protein degradation in our models.

BET protein degradation by ZBC260 reshaped endothelial transcriptional states by shifting the balance toward venous and lymphatic phenotypes while depleting proliferative, arterial, and tip-like EC populations involved in angiogenesis and extracellular. This shift is particularly important for tip-like ECs, which are characterized by high expression of ESM1 and COL4A1 and have been associated with M2-like macrophage and regulatory T-cell enrichment, increased tumor proliferation, and poor clinical outcomes in breast cancer Tip-like ECs are also enriched within tumor cores and closely associated with malignant epithelial cells, consistent with their proposed role in supporting a tumor-promoting vascular niche.17,45 In contrast, venous ECs display distinct spatial distributions and have been associated with increased T-cell infiltration, and antigen presentation suggesting a potentially more immune-permissive vascular context.18,46 Together, these observations suggest that BET degradation may selectively reduce endothelial states associated with angiogenesis and tumor progression while promoting a shift toward alternative endothelial phenotypes.

Mechanistically, ZBC260 induces endothelial transcriptional changes consistent with an immunostimulatory EC phenotype, characterized by upregulation of lymphocyte-associated signaling, and immune activation pathway.13 Concurrently, it downregulates hypoxia and HIF-1 signaling.47 Collectively, these results suggest that BET protein degradation remodels hypoxia- and metabolism-driven programs in ECs and may promote a more immune-permissive vascular environment; however, the functional consequences of these transcriptional changes require further validation.48–50

Cell–cell interaction analyses revealed that tip-like ECs exhibited the highest number of interactions within the TME, and that these interactions were markedly reduced following BETd treatment. Consistent with prior observations, tip-like ECs in untreated tumors demonstrated extensive crosstalk with both immune and tumor cells through extracellular matrix–mediated interactions, including COL4A1/COL4A2 and laminins (LAMC1, LAMB1/2, LAMA4/5) engaging integrin receptors (ITGA/ITGB).51 Notably, the COL4A1–SDC4 axis, a key mediator of ECM signaling implicated in pro-angiogenic and mesenchymal-like programs, was significantly diminished after treatment, suggesting that BET degradation may suppress pro-tumorigenic endothelial reprogramming and matrix-driven signaling.51 SPP1-mediated interactions, prominent across EC subsets and myeloid cells at baseline, were completely abolished following treatment. SPP1 (osteopontin), an integrin-binding matricellular protein, has been implicated in M2-like macrophage polarization and the formation of an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment;52 thus, loss of SPP1–integrin signaling (eg, SPP1–ITGA9–ITGB4) following BET degradation may indicate reduced endothelial–myeloid crosstalk associated with tumor-promoting signaling. Consistently, APP–TREM2–TYROBP signaling between ECs and myeloid cells was also completely disrupted. TREM2⁺ macrophages represent immunosuppressive TAM populations associated with CD8⁺ T cell dysfunction and impaired immunotherapy response,53 and the disruption of this signaling axis may reflect attenuation of endothelial-myeloid interactions associated with immunosuppressive TME following BET degradation. Moreover, pathways central to angiogenesis, immunosuppression, and tumor progression including NOTCH, TGFβ, and VEGF were significantly attenuated.13 However, these ligand-receptor interactions represent computational predictions and require experimental validation. In contrast, APP–CD74 signaling with lymphoid cells was upregulated following BET protein degradation. Although APP–CD74 interactions have primarily been described in myeloid cells,54 CD74 broadly regulates antigen presentation and immune activation. The increased APP–CD74 signaling may reflect enhanced antigen-presenting activity, although this interpretation requires functional validation.55

Beyond conventional anti-angiogenic approaches, recent strategies have focused on vascular normalization and modulation of endothelial function to improve tumor microenvironment organization.56,57 Vascular normalization strategies targeting pathways such as VEGF/VEGFR and ANG2/Tie2 aim to stabilize abnormal tumor vessels, improve perfusion, and enhance immune-cell infiltration.14,58 In contrast, our findings suggest that BET degradation represents a distinct approach to endothelial remodeling by altering multiple transcriptional programs and endothelial states rather than simply suppressing angiogenesis. By reducing tip-like and proliferative endothelial states and altering tumor-associated communication networks, ZBC260 may promote a less tumor-supportive and potentially more immune-permissive vascular phenotype. Although these findings require functional validation, they suggest that BET-mediated endothelial reprogramming may complement conventional vascular normalization strategies and expand the concept of vascular targeting beyond vessel inhibition.

Within the TME, interactions among ECs, immune cells, stromal components, and cytokines critically influence tumor progression and immunotherapy response.2,56,59 ECs are particularly important in regulating immune-cell trafficking and activation through vascular permeability, adhesion molecules, and intercellular communication.60 Our findings suggest that the observed endothelial remodeling and altered communication networks may contribute to a less tumor-supportive and more immune-permissive TME. Whether these changes translate into improved immunotherapy responses requires further functional and combination studies.

Despite these findings, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, this study was conducted exclusively in murine breast cancer models, and validation in human tumors will be necessary to establish clinical relevance. In addition, the predicted ligand–receptor interactions and functional pathway analyses are based on computational inference from single-cell transcriptomic data and therefore require functional validation. Finally, although our analyses provide insights into endothelial heterogeneity and BET degrader-induced vascular remodeling, complementary functional studies will be important to confirm the biological consequences of these transcriptional changes. Future studies integrating orthogonal validation approaches, including protein-level analyses, functional angiogenesis essays, and human clinical specimens, will be essential to further define the role of BET degradation in endothelial reprogramming and tumor vascular remodeling.

Conclusion

Collectively, these findings indicate that BET protein degradation broadly reshapes endothelial heterogeneity within the breast cancer tumor microenvironment by reducing tumor-associated endothelial states, altering endothelial transcriptional programs, and remodeling predicted intercellular communication networks. These changes are consistent with suppression of pro-angiogenic and immunoregulatory endothelial pathways and support a role for BET degradation in endothelial remodeling. These findings are consistent with prior evidence identifying ECs as central regulators of immune exclusion and further support the concept that EC cell states are therapeutic targets within the TME.13 Together, our findings identify endothelial plasticity as a potential therapeutic vulnerability and suggest that targeting BET-dependent endothelial reprogramming may represent a promising strategy for tumor microenvironment remodeling in breast cancer. Future studies should evaluate BET degraders in combination with immunotherapy or other targeted therapies and validate these findings in human breast cancer specimens to determine their translational potential.

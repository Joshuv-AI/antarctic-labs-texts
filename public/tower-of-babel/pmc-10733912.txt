Geological Characteristics of Low-Yield and Low-Efficiency CBM Wells and Practical Measures for Production Increase in the Qinshui Basin
Authors: Zhou Zhang, Junshan Ren, Yang Zhao, Meizhu Wang, Jiaosheng Yang, Cong Zhang
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Many low-production and low-efficiency wells in the Zhengzhuang, Fanzhuang, Lu’an, and Yangquan blocks of the Qinshui Basin seriously hinder the development of coalbed methane in China. Through in-depth research on the geological conditions and development technology of coalbed methane, it was found that the main reasons for the existence of a large number of low-production and low-efficiency wells are the fragmentation of coal structure, poor adaptability of vertical well types, sizable well spacing, and mismatched stimulation measures. On this basis, it is proposed to adopt an L-shaped horizontal well and staged Fracking technology in the block with a complete coal structure but low permeability. For stress–concentration areas, the first fracturing of a vertical well has a single crack and a small coverage area; After a period of drainage and production, the use of repeated fracturing technology can increase the complexity of fractures and increase gas production; For the fractured area of coal structure, the use of roof fracturing technology effectively solves the problem of coal fragmentation. In natural fracture development areas, new fractures are formed using fracture turning technology to achieve the effect of increasing production. The above technologies have achieved good results in the Qinshui Basin engineering experiment. Therefore, in-depth research on the geological conditions of coalbed methane and matching related development technologies are the main ways to solve low-efficiency and low-production wells in coalbed methane development blocks.

Introduction

As a significant country with abundant CBM (coalbed methane) resources,1 China faces one of the main problems in developing CBM: low yield and low efficiency.2 The exploration and development of CBM in China are mainly concentrated in Shanxi, Shaanxi, Xinjiang, Henan, and other regions,3 forming two major CBM industrial bases, namely the southern edge of the Qinshui Basin and the eastern edge of the Ordos Basin.4−6 Due to the complex geological conditions of CBM in China and the poor compatibility between CBM development technology and geological conditions, many low-yield and low-efficiency wells.7 The main manifestations of low yield and low efficiency are low daily production per well, short production cycle, and low input–output ratio.8

China has abundant reserves of high-rank CBM, accounting for one-third of the total resources, and the production of high-rank CBM has accounted for over 90% of the total production.9 The Qinshui Basin is China’s major high-rank CBM resource area.10 The Fanzhuang block, the main development area in the Basin, has been in large-scale development since 2006. Currently, it has a cumulative production of more than 1500 wells,11,12 of which 463 are low-yield vertical wells (daily gas production less than 500 m3/d), accounting for 32%, and 44 are low-production horizontal wells (daily gas production less than 2000 m3/d), accounting for 41%. The Zhengzhuang block, developed in 2011, has more complex geological conditions for coal reservoirs and currently has a high proportion of low-yield wells,13 with 708 wells producing less than 500 m3/d, accounting for 69% of the total wells. In the Lu’an Mining Area in the central part of the Qinshui Basin, 146 CBM wells have a daily gas production of less than 500 m3/d, accounting for 90%.14 In the Yangquan Mining Area northeast of the Qinshui Basin, the proportion of CBM wells with daily gas production of less than 500 m3/d is as high as 82%.

There are already 1615 low-yield and low-efficiency wells in the above four blocks. The existence of these wells hinders the efficient development of CBM in the Qinshui Basin, reduces CBM production, damages investor confidence, and has become a bottleneck problem restricting the development of China’s CBM.5 Therefore, exploring the reasons for low yield and low efficiency, conducting in-depth research on the geological characteristics of CBM in low yield and low-efficiency wells, and finding engineering measures that match the geological attributes of CBM development significantly increased production measures to improve single-well production, is the fundamental way to solve the problem of low-yield and low-efficiency wells.

Geological Characteristics of CBM Development

The Qinshui Basin is an NNE-trending synclinorium structure, and the axis of the synclinorium is roughly located along the Yushe–Qinxian–Qinshui line.15 The Basin mainly develops the No. 3 coal seam of the Shanxi Formation and the No. 15 coal seam of the Taiyuan Formation.16 The Zhengzhuang block is located in the Qinshui Basin’s southern part, on the Sitou Fault’s west side.17 The overall structural form is a sloping structure tilted from southeast to northwest, and the structural attitude of the strata within the block is wide and gentle. The Fanzhuang block is located on the southeastern slope of the Jincheng slope belt in the Qinshui Basin, on the east side of the Sitou Fault, and it is characterized by a structural feature of being higher in the southeast and lower in the northwest.12 The Lu’an mining area is located in the middle of the Qinshui Basin, on the west side of the Jinhua Fault zone, with an overall structural form of the northeast to south-north-oriented gentle west-trending monocline.18 The Yangquan mining area is located in the northeastern part of the Qinshui Basin, with a general structural condition of a large monocline tilted northwest to southeast,19 as shown in Figure 1. The strata within the Zhengzhuang block have a broad and gentle structural attitude with a general dip angle of 3–7° and an average of around 4°. Currently, the primary coal seam being mined is the No. 3 coal seam, which has a 5.0–7.0 m thickness; the thickness of the coal seam in the northeast and west is less than 5 m, and the thickness of the coal seam in the northwest and southeast is generally greater than 6 m (Figure 2a). The gas content of 20.8–28.5 m3/t and is buried at a 560–1100 m depth. The coal body structure is mainly formed by primary structure coal, with locally developed fragmented and granular coal. The permeability is 0.011–0.43 × 10–15 m2 (Table 1), the coal sample porosity is 4.55–5.84%(Table 2). The gas content is generally high, ranging from 21 to 31 m3/t. Reservoir permeability varies significantly in the Zhengzhuang block, the permeability of the coal reservoir in the north is better, near the fault zone, the permeability of the coal reservoir is distributed along the direction of the fault zone, and the permeability of the reservoir in the area of sparse faults is poorer. The development degree of tectonic coal is correlated with the spread of regional tectonic lines in the plane, and the tectonic control roughly spreads it; the NE joints are the most developed, which are perpendicular to the tensile stress under the present tectonic stress field, and the fissures are in the state of opening, which is conducive to the improvement of the permeability of the coal seams. The more developed NWW-SEE and near EW joints are in the state of extrusion under the present tectonic stress field, and the fissures will be closed to a certain extent. Fanzhuang No. 3 coal seam belongs to a low permeability reservoir, with primary coal as the leading coal body structure and tectonic coal developed in between. Fractures in Fanzhuang block are more developed, mainly positive faults, and there is a prominent stress–concentration zone near the interruption level in the formation process of positive faults, so the closer the faults are, the more pronounced the deformation and fragmentation of coal seams are, and the more fissures are developed. The No. 3 coal seams in the trial area of the Lu’an mine are all low-permeability reservoirs, and the regional change rule is not apparent. There are significant differences in the coal body structure of the coal seams, with the southern and northern regions of the study area having a better coal body structure, dominated by primary-fragmented structural coals, and the central part having a worse coal body structure, dominated by tectonic coals. From west to east, the coal body structure of the No. 3 coal seam gradually deteriorates. The whole is a loose coal seam with highly developed fissures, and the main feature is the development of fissures, including endogenous fissures and tectonic fissures. The No. 3 coal seams in the Yangquan area are all low-permeability reservoirs, and the structural types of coal bodies are mostly fractured coal and mylonite coal, with exogenous fissures being more developed and endogenous fissures being developed to a general extent, more large-scale folds in the coal seams, and more small-scale faults and trap columns being developed.

The Qinshui Basin is China’s most developed CBM exploration and development basin.22 The main geological characteristics of the CBM development are thick coal seams, stable distribution, high thermal evolution degree, and large gas generation. However, unfavorable conditions include deep coal seam burial, high stress, complex geological structure, fractured coal body structure, poor gas saturation, low gas content, and low permeability.23 The permeability of the four blocks ranges from ultralow permeability to low permeability, which is hugely unfavorable for CBM development. Permeability is the most critical geological parameter in CBM development,24 and the common permeability characteristics of the Qinshui Basin determine that effective measures to increase production must be implemented to achieve the efficient growth of CBM.

The Reasons for Low-Yield and Low-Efficiency of CBM Wells

The No. 3 and No. 15 coal seams are the main mining targets in the Qinshui Basin. By testing the coal rock, the reflectance of the mirror group of coal samples in Zhengzhuang block is 3.10–3.56%, 2.79–2.98% in Fanzhuang, 2.01–2.09% in Lu’an mine, 2.08–2.38% in Yangquan mine, all of which are high-rank coals. They generally have the characteristics of low porosity and permeability. Reservoir stimulation measures are essential means for the development of CBM. In the early stage, the fracturing improvement of coal reservoirs mainly followed the conventional oil and gas reservoir methods, adopting active water fracturing, full-hole perforation, high prepositioning liquid ratio, and postfracturing soaking for 48 h.25 The on-site application increased production for some wells but low production for most wells. An in-depth analysis of the development effect of coal bed methane in the Qinshui Basin summarized the reasons for the low production and low efficiency of coal bed methane wells.(1)Coal structure fragmentation. Coal structure refers to the structural characteristics exhibited by coal beds during geological evolution under various geological processes, which are mainly divided into primary structure coal and structural coal, with the latter further classified into fragmented coal, granular coal, and mylonite coal. The more fragmented the coal structure is, the worse the wellbore stability is during drilling, resulting in the formation of thick cement rings due to the enlargement of the wellbore; during hydraulic fracturing, construction pressure increases, fracturing fluid leaks excessively, fracture propagation range decreases, and there may be engineering problems such as sand plugging. During production, coalbed gas and coalbed water can carry a large amount of coal powder, causing pumping and stuck pump problems, increasing well repair costs, and coal powder also blocks the fracture channels, reducing the permeability and production of CBM wells. Therefore, coal structure fragmentation is one of the critical geological factors causing low productivity and efficiency in CBM wells.(2)Inappropriate well type. At the beginning of China’s CBM development, the vertical well was the primary type used. Compared with other types of wells, such as horizontal wells, CBM vertical well mining technology is mature, experienced, more stable, and safe. The technical threshold of CBM vertical well is relatively low, and the operation and maintenance costs are lower, making it convenient for enterprises of various scales to enter the market quickly.6 As of 2021, the cumulative number of CBM wells in China has exceeded 30,000, of which more than 85% are vertical wells. However, the vertical well has problems such as small production coverage, limited crack extension range, prone to wellbore blockage, and low production. It is difficult for multiple vertical wells to form regional pressure reduction, and the pressure relief range is limited. Therefore, the vertical well has poor adaptability to geological conditions in CBM development.26(3)Excessive well spacing. At the beginning of China’s CBM development, a general well spacing of 300 m was adopted, which achieved good results in the Fanzhuang block but inferior results in the Zhengzhuang, Lu’an, and Yangquan mining areas.27 Taking the Zhengzhuang block as an example, in 2015, four closely spaced wells were implemented in the high-permeability area of the block’s southwest (0.05–0.10 × 10–15 m2). The surrounding old wells had been producing for four years, and the minimum distance between the closely spaced wells and the surrounding old wells was about 100 m. Implementation results show that the formation pressure of the closely spaced wells remains at the original reservoir pressure level of 6.5 MPa, and the desorption time is 74–124 days, equivalent to that of the old wells in the original well network after four years. After four years, the overall pressure reduction effect has not yet been achieved in the original well network. Therefore, the adaptability of well spacing to geological conditions is low, and it is challenging to attain synergistic pressure reduction between wells, which is not conducive to increasing the gas production of individual wells. The actual practical use of reserves and resource utilization is low.(4)Poor adaptability of production increase measures. Hydraulic fracturing is a standard method to increase production in CBM projects.28 The coal seam is fractured by injecting the high-pressure liquid, thereby increasing the permeability and gas permeability and improving the gas recovery rate.29 In the early stage of CBM development in the Qinshui Basin, the same basic development process was used, including vertical well casing completion, coal seam perforation, active water injection with quartz sand fracturing, injection of 700–850 m3 of fracturing fluid, fracturing displacement of 6 m3/min, followed by quickly increasing to significant removal of 8 m3/min after pumping, and a 12% high sand ratio and other measures to increase production. After implementing this method of increasing production on a large scale, some wells achieved high production, but 1615 low-yield and low-efficiency wells were produced in the above four blocks. Therefore, the adaptability of production enhancement measures in the Qinshui Basin is poor.

Effective Revitalization Measures

In CBM development, it is crucial to implement measures to increase production in low-efficiency areas. Revitalizing low-yield and low-efficiency CBM wells have become vital in restricting the effective development of the Qinshui Basin and the CBM industry in China.30 By studying the geological conditions and identifying the critical factors behind the low productivity and efficiency of CBM wells, effective measures can be taken to increase single-well production.

Segmented Hydraulic Fracturing Technology for L-Shaped Horizontal Wells

Coal seams in the Qinshui Basin belong to ultralow permeability to low permeability coal seams. Additionally, the permeability of coal seams significantly decreases with increasing burial depth and lithostatic stress. Vertical or directional wells are typically used in the early stages of development. As mentioned, the mismatch between the well type, well spacing, and geological conditions has resulted in many low-yielding and low-efficiency wells. Compared with vertical or directional wells, L-shaped horizontal wells have longer horizontal sections that run through more coal seams, resulting in a larger contact area between the coal seam and the wellbore. Additionally, segmented hydraulic fracturing techniques can be used to expand the range of fracture propagation and increase the permeability of the coal seam for maximum production efficiency.

Despite many years of production, regional pressure drops have not yet formed due to low permeability and large well spacing in areas with many low-yield and low-efficiency vertical or directional wells. The intermediate blank spaces still maintain their original formation pressure and gas content. Therefore, L-shaped horizontal wells can be installed in the medium empty areas and connect the low-yield and low-efficiency vertical or directional wells through segmented hydraulic fracturing techniques to form regional reservoir pressure drops, thus revitalizing the low-yield and low-efficiency areas, as shown in Figure 3. This approach has been successfully utilized worldwide in many oil and gas reservoirs, particularly in unconventional shale formations.

Repeated Hydraulic Fracturing Technology

The repeating hydraulic fracturing technology solves the problem of poor initial fracturing effect or difficulty in achieving expected production in CBM wells. By performing multiple fracturing treatments, the technology can improve well productivity. Repeated hydraulic fracturing can expand the fracture area and connectivity of the CBM wells, increase reservoir permeability and effectiveness, and therefore increase CBM production,31 as shown in Figure 4. The repeating hydraulic fracturing of CBM wells can improve the recovery rate, prolong the maintenance period of production capacity, improve engineering efficiency, reduce costs, and make CBM exploration and development more economically viable.

Repeated hydraulic fracturing technology.

There are stress–concentration zones in coal seams. The stress–concentration zones in coal seams refer to some local areas in the coal seam where the surrounding rock has a higher stress, which results in more substantial pressure on the coal body in that area, leading to a more complex stress state. Geological changes (faults, folds), lithology, or underground engineering changes can all cause stress–concentration zones. CBM wells located in stress–concentration zones have a shorter extension of fractures and lower production due to the higher stress. After long-term pressure relief through mining and gas drainage, the tension in the direction of the fractures is fully released, and the maximum horizontal principal stress, minimum horizontal principal stress, and vertical stress gradually decrease and tend to be the same. Implementing large-volume, high-fluid injection with repeated fracturing can cause the direction of the fractures to deviate, forming new fractures. The ground stress in the research area is generally classified based on the magnitude of the ground stress in the coal mining area: in high-stress areas, the ground stress is greater than 16 MPa. In the medium stress zone, the in situ stress is greater than 8 MPa and less than 16 MPa. The low-stress zone is where the in situ stress is less than 8 MPa.

Roof Fracturing Technology

The coal in the coal seam has a broken structure, with the development of fragmented coal and coal particles. The coal has a low elastic modulus and a small rigid coefficient. Drilling wells in tectonic coal is complex; during the fracturing process, the fractures have limited extension and desorption range; during the extraction process, a large amount of coal dust is quickly produced, frequent inspections and well repairs are required, and high and stable production cannot be achieved. The mudstone or sandstone of the coal seam roof is stable and has a higher brittleness index than the coal body. It can form more extended fractures, even a mesh-like structure. Therefore, by fracturing the coal seam roof, long fractures can be created in the sandstone or mudstone, increasing the controlled area of the coalbed gas well and connecting the cracks in the coal seam, providing a stable gas production channel, as shown in Figure 5.

Fracture-Turning Technology

In the geological deformation zone, the stress change zone, and the physical property change zone of coal and rock, there will be a natural fracture development zone in the coal seam. In the natural fracture development zone, the top and bottom natural fractures were first communicated by hydraulic fracturing to form a high-permeability channel, and the crack water of the roof strata entered the coal seam, resulting in a large amount of water production from the coalbed gas well but low gas production. During repeated hydraulic fracturing, the injected nylon balls first blocked the holes with more effective flow rates, forcing the fracturing fluid to turn and creating new fractures in the coal seam, opening new sections, as shown in Figure 6. The main advantage of this measure is to plug ineffective fractures and increase the practical transformation volume.

Results and Discussions

The Zhengzhuang and Fanzhuang blocks in the southern part of the Qingshui Basin, the Lu’an block in the central region, and the Yangquan block in the northern part all have many low-yield and low-efficiency wells. Research has found that the broken structure of the coal body, poor wellbore adaptability, sizable well spacing, and mismatch between hydraulic fracturing measures and geological conditions have caused many low-yield and low-efficiency wells. By using L-shaped horizontal well segment fracturing technology, repeated fracturing technology, roof fracturing technology, and fracture reorientation technology, the problem can be partially solved. Effectiveness of Yield Increase Measures

The Application Effect of L-Shaped Horizontal Wells in Improving Low-Yield Wells

In a selected area of the Zhengzhuang block, there were six vertical wells, all of which had low yields, mainly due to the low permeability of the coal seam and the considerable distance between the wells. These six wells did not achieve the effect of regional drainage and pressure reduction. By constructing an L-shaped horizontal well and using segmented hydraulic fracturing in the horizontal section, the hydraulic fracturing influence zone of the original vertical well was effectively communicated, and the fracture extension area and the range of fracturing influence of the horizontal well were significantly expanded, which greatly improved the permeability of the reservoir. In production and drainage, the interference between the wells was further increased, forming a volume effect, which achieved the impact of overall pressure relief, increased the range of coal seam gas desorption, and increased the production of coal seam gas wells.

Taking the typical ZHL-01 L-shaped horizontal well in the Zhengzhuang block as an example, this well is located in the northwest part of the Zhengzhuang block, passing through 6 vertical wells, such as Z-01, Z-02, Z-03, Z-04, Z-05, Z-06, all of which are no longer producing gas. The ZL-01 well targets the Shanxi Formation No. 3 coal seam at a depth of 834.7m, with a horizontal section length of 800 m and a coal drilling rate of 94.8%. The well completed hydraulic fracturing on September 5, 2019 and achieved good gas production results, as shown in Figure 7. The current gas production rate of the well is stable at around 14000 m3/d, with a tubing pressure of 1.44 MPa, nearly 30 times the production rate of conventional vertical wells. The single-well daily gas production of the six vertical wells increased by 199–1011 m3/d, with an average daily gas increase of 435 m3. These measures to increase output from low-yield and low-efficiency wells can effectively solve the problems of wellbore type and well spacing, revitalize old wells, and increase the production of CBM wells.

Application Effect of Repeated Hydraulic Fracturing Technology

The parameters of the initial and repeated fracturing fractures of the 780 m deep well were monitored using microseismic monitoring technology. The results showed that the primary fracture formed by the initial fracturing had a strike of NE40°, with the left-wing fracture half-length of 110 m and the right-wing fracture half-length of 112 m; After three years of mining, the principal fracture direction of the repeated fracturing is NE70°, and the half length of the left and right-wing fractures is about 120 m, indicating that the fractures have deflected. The longer the production time before repeated fracturing, the better the stimulation effect of fracturing, and at least 1000 days of production should be discharged. When the cumulative water production before repeated fracturing is less than 1500 m3, the larger the cumulative water production, the greater the daily gas production increment. The longer the production time of CBM wells, the more significant the cumulative water production, the more comprehensive the range of pressure drop expansion in the direction of fractures, the greater the pressure reduction amplitude, and the greater the principle of maximum principal stress deflection in the plane. The more likely it is to form new fractures through repeated fracturing. Fifty-six wells were implemented in the Zhengzhuang and Fanzhuang blocks, with an average production increase of 472 m3/d; twenty-eight wells were implemented in the Lu’an mining area, with an average production increase of 320 m3/d.

Application Effect of Roof Fracturing Technology

Roof fracturing refers to the separation of a certain distance between the fracturing layer and the coal seam roof to prevent fracturing cracks from quickly entering the damages that have already formed in the coal seam after the roof cracks, thus achieving the goal of creating long fractures in the roof. The roof repeated fracturing measures were implemented in the fractured area of the block coal structure in Fanzhuang District, southern Qinshui Basin. In the Zhengzhuang block, 14 wells were subjected to indirect roof fracturing, with nine wells achieving an efficiency of 64%. The average daily gas production increment of these wells was over 500 m3/d. The on-site experiment has earned an excellent production increase effect, confirming the existence of effective seepage channels between roof cracks and coal seams.

The Application Effect of Fracture-Turning Technology

The primary purpose of this technology is to redirect the original natural fractures while effectively preventing the further expansion of natural fractures, increasing the coverage of fractures in coal seams, increasing the desorption area, and increasing gas production. Controlled by the Yanshanian NE-SW extrusion stress and modified by the Himalayans period NE-SW tensile stress, the Zhengzhuang cracks are relatively concentrated in their strike direction, with 145°∠85°, 170°∠89°, and 200°∠80°being more developed in the vicinity of the work area. Among them, the 145°∠85°joints are the reactivation of the Yanshan Stage, while the 170°∠89°and 200°∠80°joints are unevenly developed, with shorter extensions, most of which are less than 3m, and their directions are sometimes oscillating. Eighteen ball fracturing wells were implemented in the Zhengzhuang block in the southern part of the Qinshui Basin, with 11 wells showing effective results and an efficiency of 61%. A single well’s average daily gas production increment reached over 300 m3/d.

In summary, the L-type horizontal well series stimulation technology, repeated fracturing technology, roof fracturing technology, and fracture diversion technology have succeeded in the low-production and low-efficiency areas of the Qinshui Basin. Summarizing the reasons, matching the geological conditions of CBM with development technology is the main reason for success. The development of vertical wells cannot achieve regional pressure drop in the block with a complete coal body structure but low permeability. Through L-shaped horizontal wells and staged fracking technology, the blank zone in the development of vertical wells is solved. At the same time, low-production and low-efficiency vertical wells were connected in series, which increased the production of these wells. For stress–concentration areas, during vertical well fracturing, the cracks are single, and the coverage range is small; after a period of drainage and mining, the stress decreases, and the difference between the maximum horizontal well principal stress, the minimum horizontal principal stress, and the vertical stress decreases. The use of repeated fracturing technology can increase the complexity of fractures, increase the range of pressure relief, and increase gas production. For the fractured area of coal structure, direct fracturing in the coal seam results in very short fractures extension and even cannot create long cracks; The use of roof fracturing technology has effectively solved the problem of coal fragmentation, creating long fractures in the roof and communicating the cracks in the coal seam to achieve the goal of increasing production. In natural fracture development areas, using nylon balls in significant gaps forces the direction of the cracks to deflect, forming new cracks and increasing production. The above technologies are all based on in-depth research on the geological conditions of CBM, matched with corresponding stimulation technologies, and have achieved good results. Therefore, in-depth analysis of the geological conditions of CBM and matching complementary development technologies are the main ways to solve the problem of low efficiency and low production in CBM development blocks (Table 5).

Conclusion

Low-yield and low-efficiency wells have constrained the development of China’s CBM industry. By studying the geological characteristics of CBM in the low-efficiency areas of the Qinshui Basin, the reasons for low production and low-efficiency wells were analyzed, and it was proposed that effective stimulation measures for CBM wells should match the geological conditions of CBM development, which is the fundamental method to solve the problem of low production and low-efficiency wells. Through comprehensive research, the following conclusions are drawn in this article:(1)The Zhengzhuang and Fanzhuang blocks in the southern part of the Qinshui Basin, the Lu’an block in the central part of the Basin, and the primary coal seam thickness in the Yangquan block in the northern part of the Basin are generally 5.0–7.0 m. The Yangquan block is relatively thin, 0.75–4.8 m, with an average of 2.26 m; Burial depth between 400 and 1100 m; The gas content in the southern part of the Basin is relatively large, generally greater than 20 m3/t and less than 18 m3/t in the central and northeastern parts; Permeability belongs to the range of very low to low permeability; The overall performance of coal reservoirs is characterized by low porosity and low permeability, and effective stimulation and transformation measures are needed to achieve the development of CBM.(2)The heterogeneity of geological conditions for the development of CBM, especially the difference in the degree of coal structure fragmentation, will significantly affect the production capacity of CBM wells; The poor matching of CBM well types, well spacing, and stimulation measures with geological conditions has resulted in over 1700 wells in four blocks of the Qinshui Basin having low production and low efficiency.(3)In-depth analysis of the geological conditions of CBM and matching complementary development technologies are the main ways to solve the problem of low efficiency and low production in CBM development blocks. L-shaped horizontal well series fracturing and permeability enhancement technology, roof fracturing technology, repeated fracturing technology, and fracture-turning technology have been adopted for low-production and low-efficiency wells. These stimulation and transformation measures have effectively increased CBM production, revitalized many low-yield and low-efficiency wells in the Qinshui Basin, and promoted the development of China’s CBM industry.

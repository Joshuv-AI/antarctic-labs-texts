A novel method to estimate the 3D chlorophyll a distribution in the South China Sea surface waters using hydrometeorological parameters
Authors: Yuanning Zheng, Cai Li, Wen Zhou, Zhantang Xu, Xianqing Zhang, Wenxi Cao, Zeming Yang, Changjian Liu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Chlorophyll a (Chl-a) is a key indicator of marine ecosystems, and certain hydro-meteorological parameters (HMPs) are highly correlated with its fluctuations. Here, relevant and accessible HMPs were used as inputs, combined with machine learning (ML) algorithms for estimating 3D Chl-a in the South China Sea (SCS). With the inputs of temperature, salinity, depth, wind speed, wind direction, sea surface pressure, and relative humidity, the LightGBM-based model performed well, achieving high R2 values of 0.985 and 0.789 in validation and testing sets, respectively. Based on a large number of in situ measurements, this model enables the estimation of the 3D distribution of summer Chl-a in the SCS over the past fifteen years using a 3D hydrographic dataset combined with surface meteorological parameters. The results show that the 3D distribution of the model estimated Chl-a is characterized similarly to the previous studies and can capture the effect of hydro-meteorological conditions on Chl-a distribution. The environmental variables affecting Chl-a were considered more comprehensively in this study, and the methodological framework has the potential to be applied to the low-cost monitoring of the remaining water quality parameters.

Introduction

Chlorophyll a (Chl-a) serves as a key parameter in the investigation of both the physical and biological dynamics within oceanic systems1. It helps indicate nutrient status and can be used to monitor eutrophication and algal biomass. Changes in Chl-a can also suggest changes in other components of seawater2–4. Monitoring the impact of human activities on the ocean and understanding ocean circulations can be achieved through the use of Chl-a5–7. Nowadays, advancements in technology have created many ways for the retrieval of Chl-a in seawater. Typically, Chl-a measured in laboratory using spectrophotometers8–10, but this approach needs stringent temperatures and lengthy analysis times. Researchers use in situ near real-time monitoring sensors that integrate quantitative and semiquantitative methods to solve the problems of sample loss and time-wasting11–13. However, the acquisition and maintenance costs of monitoring sensors are substantial. Alternatively, satellite-derived products can also be used to monitor and calculate Chl-a14–16, but sun-glint and cloud cover can lead to incomplete acquisition of geographic parameters by satellites, potentially leading to insufficient data for subsequent analysis17,18. Additionally, satellite imagery can only capture Chl-a in surface waters19,20.

The South China Sea (SCS) is the largest semi-closed marginal sea in the Western Pacific (0°N–23°N, 99°E–121°E), characterized by an average depth exceeding 2000 m. While the open ocean generally maintains oligotrophic conditions, the northern and southwestern regions of SCS receive nutrient-rich, low-salinity input of freshwater from rivers15,21,22. Increased river runoff during the wet season delivers a substantial nutrient to the sea form June to November23.

The correlation between hydro-meteorological parameters (HMPs) and Chl-a in oceanic environments has been well-documented. Water temperature directly affects phytoplankton metabolism, influencing the rate of photosynthesis and growth, which in turn impacts Chl-a23,24. Lower salinity, often found near freshwater inflows, is typically associated with nutrient-rich environments that promote phytoplankton blooms, thus increasing Chl-a levels22. Moreover, eddies contribute to shaping the vertical structure of Chl-a, as evidenced by variations in salinity10. Depth is an important factor in performing Chl-a vertical distribution studies. Meteorological parameters such as air pressure, wind speed, wind direction, and humidity affect Chl-a by influencing surface water transport, nutrient upwelling, and ocean circulation patterns. In particular, winds can generate turbulence and currents that redistribute nutrients and contribute to Chl-a25–27.

Machine Learning (ML) algorithms are recognized highly efficient, robust, and ability to handle nonlinear relationships between various parameters. Several ML algorithms have demonstrated efficacy in estimating Chl-a16,19,28,29. However, most input parameters for ML models are derived from remote sensing reflectance, and relatively few studies that combine ML with HMPs for Chl-a modeling30. Given the distinct advantages of learning capabilities and performance, three ensemble ML algorithms, namely, Light Gradient Boosting Machine (LightGBM)31, Random Forest (RF)32, and Multilayer Perceptron (MLP)33, were selected for estimating Chl-a based on accessible HMPs.

This study introduces a novel approach for estimating the 3D distribution (including latitude, longitude, and profile depth) of Chl-a within 15 m below the surface of the SCS (Fig. 1a). The ML models were developed, assessed, and applied using in-situ data and remote sensing reanalysis of Chl-a and HMPs collected over the past decade. By accounting for temporal, spatial, and depth-related variations, these models provide a comprehensive representation of Chl-a distribution across both horizontal and vertical planes. This approach captures not only the horizontal changes in the geographic location of Chl-a but also its vertical variability at different depths over the last 15 years (2009–2023).

Hydro-meteorological data

Seven HMPs were used for modeling in this study: three hydrologic parameters (water temperature (℃), salinity (PSU), and depth (m)) and four meteorological parameters (air pressure (Kpa), wind speed (m/s), wind direction (°), and relative humidity (%)). Each parameter was selected for its specific influence on the distribution and dynamics of Chl-a. These HMPs enhance the ability of models to capture the spatial and temporal variability of Chl-a, resulting in more accurate and comprehensive estimates.

The hydrological data were measured using a Seabird Electronics SBE-911 CTD. The meteorological parameters are extract corresponding to the times and locations of the available stations from the ERA5 hourly dataset37.

The data for model application

To study the changes of Chl-a in two typical profiles: Pearl River Estuary (PRE) and 18°N), in-situ CTD datasets were downloaded from the South China Sea Ocean Data Center (SCSODC). For analysis Chl-a distribution across the SCS, monthly salinity and temperature data were extracted from a 3D dataset on Copernicus Marine Environment Monitoring Service (CMEMS) for three water layers (5 m, 10 m, and 15 m). Since the ML models were constructed only based on summer cruise data, with the highest percentage occurring in June and August and the quantity of cruise data in August greater than that in June (Table 1), the monthly average data in August of each year from the CMEMS product were downloaded and compared with the measured data.

Methods

In this study, HMP and Chl-a data were first preprocessed and split. Then, ML models were developed, validated, and tested for estimating Chl-a from HMPs. Finally, the best-performing ML model was selected to estimate and analyze the 3D distribution of Chl-a in the surface waters of the SCS based on HMPs. Machine learning methods

MLP is a feed-forward neural network model comprising multiple layers of neurons, was chosen for its ability to model complex nonlinear relationships by adjusting connection weights across multiple layers of neurons. This adaptability makes MLP well-suited for capturing intricate patterns in the data38. RF was selected for its robustness, ease of parameter tuning, and its ability to efficiently mitigate overfitting through an ensemble-based approach, which enhances the model’s generalization capability39. Lastly, LightGBM was chosen for its efficiency, scalability, and high predictive accuracy, making it ideal for handling large-scale datasets with lower computational costs compared to traditional boosting methods40. Together, these models can provide a comprehensive and balanced approach to addressing the challenges posed by Chl-a estimation.

The selection of optimal hyperparameters was guided through five-fold cross validation with the highest accuracy. The MLP model was conducted based on “MLPRegressor”, and the optimal hyperparameters of this model are 87 neurons in each of the three hidden layers, and a “max_iter” set as 500. The “RandomForestRegressor” with “n_estimators” and “max_depth” for the RF model is 231 and 35. The “LGBMRegressor” for the LightGBM model with the parameters “num_leaves”, “learning_rate” and “max_depth” set as 153, 0.12, and 19, respectively. The models were built from scikit-learn package in Python 3.10.

Results

Results of all models on validation and testing sets

The scatterplots show a higher concentration of points near the 1:1 line, reflecting improved prediction accuracy and a stronger correlation between the estimated and measured Chl-a values. All three models are highly effective in estimating Chl-a, as indicated by their high R² values and low error metrics. For the validation set with 3,923 samples, the MLP-based model yielded an RMSE of 0.162, MAE of 0.099, and R2 of 0.973; the RF-based model achieved an RMSE of 0.132, MAE of 0.043, and R2 of 0.982; and the LightGBM-based model reported an RMSE of 0.121, MAE of 0.056, and R2 of 0.985 (Fig. 5a). On the testing set with 2,059 samples, the MLP-based model showed an RMSE of 0.510, MAE of 0.251, and R2 of 0.706; the RF-based model obtained an RMSE of 0.504, MAE of 0.289, and R2 of 0.713; and the LightGBM-based model resulted in an RMSE of 0.432, MAE of 0.262, and R2 of 0.789 (Fig. 5b). The models perform better on the validation set than on the testing set, as shown by the higher R2 and lower RMSE and MAE values. They show a slight underestimation on the testing set, especially in higher Chl-a estimation.

Compared to the validation results, the accuracy of all three models has declined on the testing set, which is caused by the introduction of new data. Despite this, the LightGBM-based model continued to show the best performance on the testing set, with RMSE, MAE, and higher R2 values of 0.432, 0.262, and 0.789, respectively.

Application and discussion

Estimating the profile distribution of Chl-a based on the SCSODC dataset

The Estimation of the Chl-a profiles using the SCSODC dataset was performed to determine the interannual variability in the same region. Owing to differences in the locations of the sampling stations for each yearly cruise, two sections with high site repetition rates were selected to analysis the profile changes at different locations over long-time scales: the section near the PRE, and the 18°N section on eastern Hainan Island.

In the PRE section (Fig. 9a), Chl-a decreased with increasing offshore distance at the same depth, showing high concentrations near the coastline (Fig. 9b). This pattern is likely due to nutrient-rich land-based sources from the Pearl River, which contribute to water eutrophication22,41. The input of freshwater from the Pearl River caused a significant decrease in salinity and an increase in temperature at the estuary could be observed in the profiles (Fig. 9c–d). Chl-a variation with offshore distance showed an inverse relationship with temperature and a low but inverse correlation with salinity. This suggests that Chl-a distribution in the PRE section is primarily influenced by river inflow, characterized by low salinity, high temperature, and eutrophication.

The nonapparent freshwater transport process in the PRE during 2015 can be observed in the lower Chl-a and TEMP-SAL profiles. Normally, the SCS experiences a dominant southwestern wind pattern during the summer months23, while the northern SCS was affected by northeasterly wind anomalies in June 201542. The wind anomalies conducive to downwelling suppressed coastal upwelling processes and constrained the nearshore dispersion of the plume. The freshwater drifted southwest due to the unusual northeast wind after the plume moved far eastward from the shore. The strong pulsed southwesterly wind predominantly facilitated the detachment of low salinity water from the Pearl River plume43. This resulted in a higher level of salinities in the nearshore waters during the sampling period (26–26 June), while lower salinities were observed towards the southward, which then increased again.

The relationship between depth, Chl-a, temperature, and salinity is more complex in the 18°N section than in the PRE (Fig. 10). Contrasting with the PRE region, the Chl-a values in the current area are typically lower due to its distance from land-source inputs. The distribution of Chl-a along the 18°N section is influenced by various environmental factors, particularly the presence of eddies. Intense interactions between the terrain, currents, and local monsoons are the primary cause of eddies in this region44. The Chl-a anomalies within cyclonic eddies (anticyclonic eddies) can exhibit positive (negative) due to isopycnal displacement linked with mesoscale eddies45. This phenomenon is closely associated with the dynamic behavior of eddies present in the SCS during the summer season, which facilitates the trapping and subsequent transport of Chl-a from one location to another46,47. In general, cyclonic eddies have a better-documented Chl-a distribution than anticyclonic eddies, with higher values occurring in the region where eddies contact46.

Two eddies centralize at 118°E (anticyclonic) and 115°E (cyclonic) across the 18°N section during summer (Yuan et al.60). However, due to the limitations of the data, this study only discussed the areas between 112°E and 116°E. The seawater at the center of a cyclonic eddy moves from the bottom to the top, bringing the colder lower water to the warmer upper water. This makes the water inside the eddy cooler than the surrounding seawater, which is why this type of eddy is known as a cold eddy10,48. Temperature exhibits a consistent annual pattern in this region, with lower values found here that impact salinity and Chl-a. Areas with significant temperature decreases correspond to high Chl-a and salinity variations.

Estimating the distribution of Chl-a in three water layers based on the CMEMS product

The high Chl-a in all three water layers over the last fifteen years is concentrated in the coastal and upwelling regions (Fig. 11). The main cause of the elevated Chl-a near the coast is primarily attributed to nutrients originating from land, which are transported by rainfall and runoff7,15,49. The seawaters of the eastern Guangdong Province and Vietnam, and the northeastern Hainan Island are the primary upwelling areas. During the summer, the southwest monsoon causes intensified vertical mixing due to wind stress and convection. This phenomenon leads to a thicker mixed layer, facilitating pronounced upwelling phenomena7,27,50. Upwelling and offshore transport upward nutrient-rich deep water towards the surface layer, contributing to the elevated of Chl-a levels51,52. However, El Niño typically has a suppressing effect on upwelling area of the eastern Vietnam. This occurs due to the changes in atmospheric circulation and sea surface temperatures associated with El Niño. The El Niño events cause the trade winds in the equatorial Pacific to weaken or even reverse, resulting in a reduction in the strength of the alongshore wind stress in eastern Vietnam53,54. The weakening of the trade winds affects Ekman transport, the process that moves cold, nutrient-rich waters from the deep ocean to the surface (Dang et al. 2022). El Niño events of 2009–2010, 2014–2016, and 2019–2020 that resulted in elevated sea surface temperatures were documented during years of weak upwelling in eastern Vietnam55–57.

The distribution of Chl-a varied little between the three water layers, with the exception that marginally higher at 15 m. The Chl-a is depth-dependent along the coast of Guangdong Province26. High Chl-a is conveyed from the coastal regions of Vietnam to the basin through offshore Ekman transport and anticyclonic circulation mechanisms. The eastward plume originating from the Pearl River is directed into the basin by the counter-clockwise rotation of cyclonic eddies58–60. The increased nutrient input driven by the Ekman divergence linked to a cyclonic wind circulation is most likely the cause of the higher Chl-a in the northern basin. Conversely, the intensification of wind speeds plays a crucial role in promoting vertical entrainment of cold, nutrient-rich deep water in the southern basin24. These dynamic processes highlight the complex interplay between physical processes such as vertical mixing, upwelling, and wind-driven circulation, and their influence on Chl-a distribution within different regions of the SCS.

Conclusions

This study used a large amount of measured data to develop a LightGBM-based model that provides the 3D (one temporal and two spatial) estimation of Chl-a in the SCS. Because of the large quantity of data used in the development and validation processes as well as the various sample sites of the training, validation, and testing sets, which span thirteen years, the HMPs-based approach for low-cost Chl-a estimation is robust and dependable. The comparison of the model estimations and in situ measurements demonstrated that the proposed approach performed well. In the testing set, the LigihtGBM-based model has RMSE, MAE and R2 values of 0.432, 0.262, and 0.789, respectively. The results of applying the SCSODC dataset and CMEMS product show that the summer Chl-a distribution features in the SCS are influenced by various dynamical conditions, but the model is still able to estimate the Chl-a distribution profiles and planes fairly accurately and coincide with the water movement process.

In the past fifteen years, high Chl-a generally occurred in the coast of Guangdong Province and Hainan Island, and Gulf of Tonkin. Rainfall, runoff, and upwelling from these regions contribute significant quantities of nutrients to the SCS, fostering heightened Chl-a. But the strength of the upwelling in eastern Vietnam is obviously influenced by El Niño. Chl-a increased with depth along the coast of Guangdong Province, while there was no depth dependence in the upwelling region. Eastern Vietnam in 2010, 2015, 2016, and 2020 had low surface chlorophyll because of reduced beneficial conditions for upwelling due to sea surface warming caused by El Niño events. In the northern basin, the distribution of Chl-a is influenced by frequent eddy activity, whereas it is primarily due to the intensification of southwest monsoon wind speeds in the southern basin. A large cold eddy forms in the 18°N, 115°E region every year, producing a low temperature profile with high Chl-a. In addition, the distribution pattern of high Chl-a is influenced by wind stress, freshwater inputs, geostrophic flows, and other intricate processes controlling the physical dynamics of the water column.

The 3D estimation of Chl-a is crucial for offshore aquaculture and phytoplankton bloom monitoring. The LightGBM-based model effectively estimates the 3D distribution of Chl-a in the surface waters of the SCS, though it is currently limited to the summer months. Since seasonal variations in oceanic conditions significantly impact Chl-a distribution, expanding the dataset to include all seasons is necessary for better generalization. Additionally, the uneven spatial distribution of sampling stations, with coastal areas better represented than open ocean areas, may bias the model. Future efforts should focus on enhancing spatial coverage, particularly in under-sampled regions, by merging both in-situ and remote sensing data to fill gaps. Due to the delayed influence of meteorological effects in deeper waters, the estimation of Chl-a in the deeper water layers were limited, as factors such as thermocline dynamics and nutrient upwelling become more relevant. Integrating hydrodynamic data, such as flow speed and direction, could improve estimates for deeper Chl-a. The reduced accuracy of the LightGBM-based model on the testing set can be attributed to the inclusion of data from regions with diverse environmental conditions and dynamics, as opposed to well-sampled areas that would better reflect the robustness of the model and the overall feasibility of the approach. Expanding temporal and spatial coverage, along with incorporating more oceanographic variables, will enhance the model’s accuracy and generalizability for broader water quality monitoring.

To our knowledge, this is the first study in which the 3D distribution of Chl-a was estimated based on HMPs. The discovery of the distribution pattern of Chl-a on 3D scales emphasize the importance of the proposed estimation approach in monitoring Chl-a in the SCS. Although ML-based model in this study is proposed for the SCS, it provides a basis for studies to realize 3D distribution of water quality parameters based on HMPs. It is possible that this methodological framework can be further applied globally.

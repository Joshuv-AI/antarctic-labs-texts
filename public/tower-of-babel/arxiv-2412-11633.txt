Geometric monotones of violations of quantum realism
Alexandre C. Orthey Jr, Alexander Streltsov

Abstract
Quantum realism, as introduced by Bilobran and Angelo [EPL 112, 40005 (2015)], states that projective measurements in quantum systems establish the reality of physical properties, even in the absence of a revealed outcome. This framework provides a nuanced perspective on the distinction between classical and quantum notions of realism, emphasizing the contextuality and complementarity inherent to quantum systems. While prior works have quantified violations of quantum realism (VQR) using measures based on entropic distances, here we extend the theoretical framework to geometric distances. Building on an informational approach, we derive geometric monotones of VQR using trace distance, Hilbert-Schmidt distance, Schatten p p -distances, Bures, and Hellinger distances. We identify Bures and Hellinger distances as uniquely satisfying all minimal criteria for a bona fide VQR monotone. Remarkably, these distances can be expressed in terms of symmetric Rényi and Sandwiched Rényi divergences, aligning geometric and entropic approaches. Our findings suggest that the realism-information relation implies a deep connection between geometric and entropic frameworks, with only those geometric distances expressible as entropic quantities qualifying as valid monotones of VQR. This work highlights the theoretical and practical advantages of geometric distances, particularly in contexts where computational simplicity or symmetry is important.



I
Introduction

The concept of quantum realism of observables, as proposed by Bilobran and Angelo
Bilobran and Angelo 2015
, can be summarized as follows. After the projective measurement of a physical property in a quantum system, we can assert that the property is now well-defined, i.e., it is real for the state of the system. Any subsequent projective measurement of the same property will yield the same outcome because the new measurement merely reveals a pre-existing
state of affairs
—or reality. This reasoning also holds for non-selective (or non-revealed) projective measurements. Even if the experimentalist is not present in the lab when the measurement occurs or forgets the result, the measurement still takes place, establishing the property as real. Thus, a projective measurement, even without a revealed outcome, serves as the mechanism by which realism emerges in a quantum system.

Consider, for instance, the measurement of the observable
\sigma_{z}={|{0}\rangle}{\langle{0}|}-{|{1}\rangle}{\langle{1}|}
on the state
{|{\psi}\rangle}=({|{0}\rangle}+{|{1}\rangle})/\sqrt{2}
. After the measurement, the system’s state will collapse to either
{|{0}\rangle}
or
{|{1}\rangle}
. At this point, the realism (or reality) of
\sigma_{z}
is established, meaning that any subsequent measurement of
\sigma_{z}
will yield the same outcome. In the case of a non-selective measurement, however, the post-measurement state of the system would be the mixed state
{|{0}\rangle}{\langle{0}|}+{|{1}\rangle}{\langle{1}|}
. This mixed state indicates that the system is definitely in either
{|{0}\rangle}
or
{|{1}\rangle}
, but the observer lacks knowledge of which one. This lack of knowledge is subjective and classical—it reflects the observer’s ignorance rather than an intrinsic uncertainty in the quantum state, as is the case with superpositions. By contrast, the observable
\sigma_{x}
is real for the initial state
{|{\psi}\rangle}
because the state is an eigenstate of
\sigma_{x}
, with a well-defined eigenvalue. This highlights the fundamental difference between realism in quantum mechanics and classical realism. Classical realism assumes that every physical property of a system is well-defined at all times, independent of observation or the presence of an observer. In contrast, quantum realism recognizes that certain pairs of incompatible observables, such as
\sigma_{x}
and
\sigma_{z}
, cannot always be simultaneously well-defined. This limitation is intrinsic to the nature of quantum systems and reflects the contextuality and complementarity that are hallmarks of quantum theory.

The situation changes dramatically when we consider bipartite systems. If Alice and Bob share a maximally entangled pair of spin-
1/2
particles, their local states are maximally mixed. Consequently, the spin observable for, say, Alice’s particle is
locally
an element of physical reality, as a non-selective measurement of the identity operator does not alter the state. However, when considering the
global
description of the pair, any local non-selective measurement perturbs the global state. This demonstrates that the state of the pair violates quantum realism for the observable associated with either particle. This phenomenon diverges sharply from the Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen (EPR) notion of elements of physical reality
Einstein et al. 1935
, as applied to bipartite entangled states. According to EPR, local measurements should not influence the state of affairs at remote locations.

A method to quantify violations of quantum realism (VQR) was proposed in
Bilobran and Angelo 2015
, where the authors referred to VQR as
irrealism
. They introduced an entropic distance between the measured state and the original state as a means of quantification. These measures of VQR were largely explored in theoretical
Dieguez and Angelo 2018
;
Rudnicki 2018
;
Orthey and Angelo 2019
;
Engelbert and Angelo 2020
;
Costa and Angelo 2020
;
Lustosa et al. 2020
;
Basso and Maziero 2021
;
Paiva et al. 2023
;
Engelbert and Angelo 2023
;
Caetano and Angelo 2024
and experimental
Mancino et al. 2018
;
Dieguez et al. 2021
;
Chrysosthemos et al. 2023
;
Basso and Maziero 2022
;
Araújo et al. 2024
approaches. Building on the informational framework of
Dieguez and Angelo 2018
and the experimental findings of
Mancino et al. 2018
;
Dieguez et al. 2021
,
Orthey and Angelo 2022
presented a set of physically motivated axioms for monotones and measures of VQR, where the later requires more conditions than the former. In their work, they define a notion of conditional quantum information—the complement of quantum conditional entropy—using general relative entropies, also called divergences. They further show how variations in conditional information can be used to derive expressions for VQR measures. Their argument relies on a
realism-information relation
: since non-selective projective measurements are responsible for the emergence of realism, the increase in conditional information about the system, as encoded in the environment, could serve as a monotone of VQR. This connection is formalized using the Stinespring dilation theorem
Nielsen and Chuang 2010
.

I.1
Contributions

In this work, we extended the concept of conditional information based on relative entropies from
Orthey and Angelo 2022
to geometric distances and successfully derived geometric monotones of VQR. Geometric distances, or simply
distances
, share properties with relative entropies, such as contraction under completely positive trace-preserving (CPTP) maps, invariance under unitary transformations, and joint convexity. Additionally, distances are inherently symmetric and satisfy the triangle inequality, making them advantageous in certain contexts. Moreover, some distances are computationally simpler to evaluate, offering a practical benefit over entropic measures. Although none of the distances we studied are additive, making them not good candidates for measures of VQR, some of them can still be classified as monotones of VQR. Among the distances we explore, we have trace distance, Hilbert-Schmidt distance
Nielsen and Chuang 2010
, Schatten
p
-distances
Bhatia 2013
;
Spehner et al. 2017
, and Bures
Bures 1969
;
Spehner et al. 2017
and Hellinger
Roga et al. 2016
;
Spehner et al. 2017
distances. We found out that, among these distances, Bures and Hellinger distances are the only candidates that satisfy all the minimal requirements for a bona-fide monotone of VQR. Trace distance, for instance, provides a quantifier that fails to identify VQR for some states. The Hilbert-Schmidt distance, on the other way, provides a quantifier of VQR that decreases with the addition of completely uncorrelated states. Interestingly, Bures and Hellinger distances can be written as functions of Rényi and Sandwiched Rényi divergences in the particular cases where these divergences are symmetric. Our results suggest that, if one assumes the realism-information relation, then only the geometric distances that can be framed as entropic quantities are suitable to derive monotones of VQR.

II
Fundamental concepts

II.1
Violations of quantum realism

Let us mathematically formalize the concept of quantum realism presented in the introduction by following Refs.
Bilobran and Angelo 2015
;
Dieguez and Angelo 2018
;
Orthey and Angelo 2022
. Let
A=\sum_{a}aA_{a}
be a
d
-output observable acting on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{A}}
, where
A_{a}={|{a}\rangle}{\langle{a}|}
are projectors satisfying
\sum_{a}A_{a}=\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{A}}
. Additionally, define the non-selective projective measurement of the observable
A
as
\Phi_{A}(\rho_{\mathcal{AB}})=\sum_{a}\left(A_{a}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{B}}\right)\rho_{\mathcal{AB}}\left(A_{a}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{B}}\right)
, where
\rho_{\mathcal{AB}}
is the state of a bipartite system in
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{B}}
. We say that
\rho_{\mathcal{AB}}
has realism defined for
A

iff

\Phi_{A}(\rho_{\mathcal{AB}})=\rho_{\mathcal{AB}}
. States that already possess realism for a particular observable are invariant under a non-selective (or non-revealed) projective measurement of that observable, i.e.,
\Phi_{A}(\Phi_{A}(\rho))=\Phi_{A}(\rho)
. Henceforth,
the violation of the quantum realism of
A
given
\rho
is defined as

\mathfrak{I}_{A}(\rho)\coloneqq S\big(\Phi_{A}(\rho)\big)-S(\rho),

(1)

which can also be called the
irrealism
of
A
given
\rho
, and
S(\rho)\coloneqq-\text{Tr}\big(\rho\log_{2}\rho\big)
is the von Neumann entropy. Moreover, we can decompose irrealism as the sum of local coherence and non-optimized quantum discord, that is,
\mathfrak{I}_{A}(\rho)=\mathcal{C}(\rho_{\mathcal{A}})+D_{A}(\rho)
, where
\mathcal{C}(\rho_{\mathcal{A}})=\mathfrak{I}_{A}(\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{B}}\rho)
,
D_{A}(\rho)=I(\mathcal{A}:\mathcal{B})_{\rho}-I(\mathcal{A}:\mathcal{B})_{\Phi_{A}(\rho)}
, and
I(\mathcal{A}:\mathcal{B})_{\rho}=S(\rho_{\mathcal{A}})+S(\rho_{\mathcal{B}})-S(\rho_{\mathcal{AB}})
is the quantum mutual information. Consequently, correlations between parties
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
prevent the existence of elements of physical reality for observables on both parties. That is, any positive value of
D_{A(B)}(\rho)
implies non-zero irrealism
\mathfrak{I}_{A(B)}(\rho)
.

Functionals such as (
1
) were also introduced in other works but with different names and different meanings, such as measures of wave-like information
Angelo and Ribeiro 2015
and quantum incoherent relative entropy
Chitambar et al. 2016
.

II.2
Quantifying violations of realism using quantum conditional information

Because the shape of (
1
) was chosen ad-hoc, one could wonder if other entropic distances would be a better fit to quantify VQR. To answer that question, an axiomatic and physically motivated approach was taken in Ref.
Orthey and Angelo 2022
to propose that irrealism should decrease in the presence of a flux of information from the system to the environment. Since measurements are the key elements by which a physical property gets defined, one could argue that quantifying how much information the environment
\mathcal{E}
gets about the system
\mathcal{S}
makes it possible to quantify how irrealism decreases. The figure of merit in this case is the complement of quantum conditional entropy

H(\mathcal{E}|\mathcal{S})_{\Omega}\coloneqq S(\Omega)-S(\Omega_{\mathcal{E}}),

(2)

that is, the quantum conditional information
Orthey and Angelo 2022

I(\mathcal{E}|\mathcal{S})_{\Omega}\coloneqq\ln d_{\mathcal{E}}-H(\mathcal{E}|\mathcal{S})_{\Omega},

(3)

where
\Omega
acts over
\mathcal{H_{S}}\otimes\mathcal{H_{E}}
,
\Omega_{\mathcal{E}}=\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{S}}\Omega
and
d_{\mathcal{E}}=\dim\mathcal{H_{E}}
. While the former measures the ignorance that we still have about
\mathcal{E}
given that we know
\mathcal{S}
, the latter measures the knowledge (or information) we can obtain by accessing
\mathcal{E}
conditioned to the information codified in
\mathcal{S}
. Note that
0\leqslant I(\mathcal{E}|\mathcal{S})_{\rho}\leqslant\ln d_{\mathcal{E}}d_{\mathcal{S}}
.

To connect conditional information and quantum realism, the authors in
Orthey and Angelo 2022
consider a system in the state
\rho_{\mathcal{S}}
that interacts with an environment in the state
{|{0}\rangle}{\langle{0}|}
such that
\Omega_{0}\coloneqq\rho_{\mathcal{S}}\otimes{|{0}\rangle}{\langle{0}|}
and
\Omega_{t}\coloneqq U_{\mathcal{SE}}\Omega_{0}U_{\mathcal{SE}}^{\dagger}
are the global states before and after the interaction
U_{\mathcal{SE}}
. This interaction must result in a non-selective projective measurement over the system after we trace out the environment, that is,

\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{E}}\left[U_{\mathcal{SE}}\left(\rho_{\mathcal{S}}\otimes{|{0}\rangle}{\langle{0}|}\right)U_{\mathcal{SE}}^{\dagger}\right]=\Phi_{A}(\rho_{\mathcal{S}}),

(4)

where
A
is some observable of interest (see previous section). Equation (
4
) guarantees that the information about the observable
A
in
\mathcal{S}
that is going to be codified in
\mathcal{E}
—through the unitary interaction
U_{\mathcal{SE}}
—is associated with the emergence of realism about
A
in the system—through the map
\Phi_{A}
. The authors defined that this increment of realism about
A
given
\rho
, denoted by
\mathfrak{R}_{A}(\rho)
, should vary such that

\Delta\mathfrak{R}_{A}(t)\equiv\Delta I(\mathcal{E|S})_{\Omega_{0}\to\Omega_{t}}

(5)

which can be thought of as a
realism-information relation
, where
\Delta\mathfrak{R}_{A}(t):=\mathfrak{R}_{A}(\rho_{\mathcal{S}}(t))-\mathfrak{R}_{A}(\rho_{\mathcal{S}}(0))
, with
\rho_{\mathcal{S}}(0)\coloneqq\rho_{\mathcal{S}}
and
\rho_{\mathcal{S}}(t)\coloneqq\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{E}}(\Omega_{t})=\Phi_{A}(\rho_{\mathcal{S}})
. Additionally, the corresponding change in the environmental conditional information is
\Delta I(\mathcal{E|S})_{\Omega_{0}\to\Omega_{t}}:=I(\mathcal{E|S})_{\Omega_{t}}-I(\mathcal{E|S})_{\Omega_{0}}
. In
Orthey and Angelo 2022
, the authors prove that relation (
5
) results in

\mathfrak{R}_{A}(\rho_{\mathcal{S}})=\ln d_{A}-S(\rho_{\mathcal{S}}||\Phi_{A}(\rho_{\mathcal{S}}))=\ln d_{A}-\mathfrak{I}_{A}(\rho_{\mathcal{S}}),

(6)

since the von Neumann relative entropy
Nielsen and Chuang 2010

S(\rho||\sigma)\coloneqq\text{Tr}(\rho\ln\rho-\rho\ln\sigma)

(7)

satisfies
S(\rho_{\mathcal{S}}||\Phi_{A}(\rho_{\mathcal{S}}))=S(\Phi_{A}(\rho_{\mathcal{S}}))-S(\rho_{\mathcal{S}})

Costa and Angelo 2020
. Equation (
6
) shows that, by taking an informational approach to the problem of the emergence of realism through non-selective projective measurements, we can regain the expression for irrealism of observables (
1
).

In addition to the realism-information relation (
5
), the authors in
Orthey and Angelo 2022
also introduced a list of axioms that should be satisfied by any bona-fide
monotone
of VQR:

Axiom 1

(Realism and Measurements)
.

The realism of
A
acting on
\mathcal{H_{A}}
given
\rho
acting on
\mathcal{H_{A}}\otimes\mathcal{H_{B}}
, denoted by
\mathfrak{R}_{A}(\rho)
, is a non-negative real number and increases upon non-selective projective measurements of
A
, reaching a maximum when
\rho=\Phi_{A}(\rho)
, that is
0\leq\mathfrak{R}_{A}(\rho)\leq\mathfrak{R}_{A}(\mathcal{M}_{A}^{\epsilon}(\rho))\leq\mathfrak{R}_{A}(\Phi_{A}(\rho))=\mathfrak{R}_{A}^{\max}
, where
\mathcal{M}_{A}^{\epsilon}(\rho)\coloneqq(1-\epsilon)\rho+\epsilon\Phi_{A}(\rho)
is the monitoring map.

Axiom 2

(Role of Other Parts)
.

(a) Discarding subsystems does not reduce realism, i.e.
\mathfrak{R}_{A}(\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{X}}(\rho))\geq\mathfrak{R}_{A}(\rho)
for
\mathcal{H_{X}}\subseteq\mathcal{H_{B}}
, and (b) adding an uncorrelated system does not change realism, i.e.
\mathfrak{R}_{A}(\rho\otimes\sigma)=\mathfrak{R}_{A}(\rho)
.

Axiom 3

(Uncertainty Relation)
.

Two observables
X
and
Y
acting on
\mathcal{H_{A}}
cannot be simultaneous elements of reality in general, that is,
\mathfrak{R}_{X}(\rho)+\mathfrak{R}_{Y}(\rho)\leq 2\mathfrak{R}_{A}^{\max}
. Equality must hold only when
[X,Y]=0
or
\rho=\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{A}}/d_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\rho_{\mathcal{B}}
.

Axiom 4

(Mixing)
.

For a probabilistic ensemble
\{p_{i},\rho_{i}\}
, realism is concave, i.e.
\mathfrak{R}_{A}\left(\sum_{i}p_{i}\rho_{i}\right)\geq\sum_{i}p_{i}\mathfrak{R}_{A}(\rho_{i})
.

Moreover, if a quantifier of VQR additionally satisfies additivity and flag additivity, it is called a
measure
of VQR. Since none of the distances we studied are additive, only monotones of VQR were found.

II.3
Alternative ways of quantifying quantum conditional information

An alternative way of expressing quantum conditional entropy is by means of divergences
Tomamichel et al. 2014
, being von Neumann relative entropy as the most prominent example. From (
2
) and (
7
) it can be shown that

H(\mathcal{E|S})_{\Omega}=-S(\Omega||\Omega_{\mathcal{S}}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{E}}).

(8)

On the other way, the quantum conditional information (
3
) can be conveniently rewritten in terms of (
7
) as

I(\mathcal{E|S})_{\Omega}=S\left(\Omega\,\Big|\Big|\,\Omega_{\mathcal{S}}\otimes\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{E}}}{d_{\mathcal{E}}}\right).

(9)

To better understand the meaning of conditional information, one can check that it can be decomposed as

I(\mathcal{E|S})_{\Omega}=I(\Omega_{\mathcal{E}})+I(\mathcal{E:S})_{\Omega},

(10)

where
I(\rho_{\mathcal{E}})\coloneqq S\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{E}}||\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{E}}/d_{\mathcal{E}}\right)=\ln d_{\mathcal{E}}-S(\Omega_{\mathcal{E}})
is the informational content of part
\mathcal{E}
(because it is the complement of the entropy of
\mathcal{E}
) and
I(\mathcal{E:S})_{\Omega}=S\left(\rho||\rho_{\mathcal{S}}\otimes\rho_{\mathcal{E}}\right)
is the quantum mutual information now written as a divergence. Since the mutual information is a measure of total correlations between the parts, the conditional information can be said to be composed of “local” and “global” information
Orthey and Amorim 2017
. In the case of a product state such as
\rho_{\mathcal{S}}\otimes\sigma_{\mathcal{E}}
, the conditional information will be codified only in part
\mathcal{E}
. However, if the global state is maximally entangled, all the conditional information will be due to the correlations, since the local state assigned to part
\mathcal{E}
is the maximally mixed one.

In
Tomamichel et al. 2014
, it was observed and later expanded upon in
Orthey and Angelo 2022
within the context of irrealism measures that if one intends to explore alternative formulations of conditional entropy using other entropic measures, such as Rényi and Tsallis entropies, the approach outlined in (
2
) proves insufficient. This insufficiency arises due to the failure of data-processing inequalities. The same does not apply to approaches using divergences such as the one employed in (
8
). By replacing the von Neumann relative entropy
S(\cdot||\cdot)
by other divergence measures such as sandwiched Rényi divergence
Tomamichel et al. 2014
and Tsallis
Tsallis 1988
relative entropy, we can construct alternative entropic measures for conditional information that still attain to (
10
). Incidentally, these other measures for conditional information result in alternative ways to measure the quantum realism of observables through the realism-information relation (
5
) as extensively explored in
Orthey and Angelo 2022
.

II.4
Geometric distances and norms

In this paper, we explore the possibility of using geometrical distances to quantify conditional information—and consequently quantum realism—as an alternative to entropic measures. In what follows, we revisit the definition of
distance
and list the examples that we are interested in exploring, as well as their fundamental properties
Kreyszig 1978
.

A
distance
is a function
d:M\times M\mapsto\mathbb{R}
that maps each par
x,y\in M
onto a real number
d(x,y)
satisfying:
positive definiteness

d(x,y)\geqslant 0
, where the equality holds
iff

x=y
;
symmetry

d(x,y)=d(y,x)
; and
triangle inequality

d(x,z)\leqslant d(x,y)+d(y,z)
. Positive definiteness is essential to obtain proper distinguishing measures. In addition, other properties are necessary for such measures to have a physical meaning. In the context of density states
\rho
and
\sigma
, a distance is said to be
contractive
under a CPTP map
\Lambda

iff

d(\Lambda(\rho),\Lambda(\sigma))\leqslant d(\rho,\sigma).

(11)

In particular, contractive distances are unitarily invariant, that is,
d(U\rho U^{\dagger},U\sigma U^{\dagger})=d(\rho,\sigma)
.

We can use norms to find proper distances. One norm of particular interest is the Schatten
p
-norm

of an operator

\norm{X}_{p}\coloneqq\left[\text{Tr}\left(|X|^{p}\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}
, where

is a real number such that
p\geqslant 1
and
|X|\coloneqq\sqrt{X^{\dagger}X}
. The Schatten

\norm{U\rho V^\dagger}_{p}=\norm{\rho}_{p}
for isometries

\norm{\rho\otimes\sigma}_{p}=\norm{\rho}_{p}\norm{\sigma}_{p}
.

Without further ado, the

-distances

(also called
Schatten
p
-distances
)
Bhatia 2013
;
Spehner et al. 2017

d_{p}(\rho,\sigma)\coloneqq\norm{\sigma-\rho}_{p}=\left(\text{Tr}|\sigma-\rho|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

(12)

for all
p\geqslant 1
. For
p=1
and
p=2
, the
L_{p}
-distances are called

d_{\text{Tr}}
and

(13)

III
Results

III.1
Geometric conditional information

Let us propose a geometric measure of conditional information as

I^{\square}(\mathcal{E|S})_{\Omega}\coloneqq d_{\square}^{n}\left(\Omega,\Omega_{\mathcal{S}}\otimes\dfrac{\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{E}}}{d_{\mathcal{E}}}\right),

(17)

where
d_{\square}
is any of the distances presented in Sec.
II.4
and
n
is some power conveniently chosen. This definition is clearly borrowed from the notion of conditional information with divergences we presented in Sec.
II.3
and inspired by the work of Roga et al.
Roga et al. 2016
. Immediately from the positive definiteness property of distances, we have that

I^{\square}(\mathcal{E|S})_{\Omega}=0\qquad\text{{iff}}\qquad\Omega=\Omega_{\mathcal{S}}\otimes\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{E}}}{d_{\mathcal{E}}}.

(18)

In addition to that, we can expect meaningful measures of conditional information from definition (
17
) once we use distances
d_{\square}^{n}
that respect the contractivity relation (
11
), which is nothing more than a data processing inequality in the geometrical context. This is the case for the trace distance and the square of the Bures and the Hellinger distances. As we are going to see, Hilbert-Schmidt distances can be contractive for unital maps.

III.2
Summary of main results: geometric monotones of violations of quantum realism

In order to follow the realism-information relation (
5
), we must obtain the conditional information of the global states

III.3
VQR with trace distance

Example

as a bona fide monotone for VQR, as it violates Axiom

. Specifically, maximum realism is achieved by states other than

4

III.4
VQR with Hilbert-Schmidt distance

Although the Hilbert-Schmidt distance is not contractive under CPTP maps in general, it becomes contractive under CPTP
unital

\Phi_{A}
, monitorings
\mathcal{M}_{A}^{\epsilon}

IV
Discussion

In this work, we have extended the framework for quantifying violations of quantum realism (also known as quantum irrealism
Bilobran and Angelo 2015
) by exploring geometric distances as potential tools for constructing monotones of VQR. Geometric distances, due to their symmetry and computational simplicity, provide an intriguing alternative to relative entropies. Among the distances analyzed, we identified Bures and Hellinger distances as satisfying all minimal criteria for bona fide monotones of VQR, while trace distance, Hilbert-Schmidt distance, and
L_{p}
-distances in general fail to attend those criteria.

The realism-information relation (
5
) proposed in
Orthey and Angelo 2022
imposes that quantifiers satisfying the Axioms (
1
)-(
4
) for VQR should inherently align with informational structures. Specifically, this relation sets a connection between conditional information (
9
) and the emergence of realism in quantum systems (
20
), therefore guiding the construction of monotones. The failure of certain distances, such as trace distance and Hilbert-Schmidt distance, to meet the criteria for monotones stresses the requirements for valid quantifiers of VQR. Specifically, the inability of these distances to consistently capture VQR when
\Phi_{A}(\rho)\neq\rho
[see the plateau in Fig.
1
(b)] or their counterintuitive behaviour under the addition of completely uncorrelated states [see (
30
)] highlights the importance of aligning distance-based monotones with physical principles of quantum realism.

Our findings suggest that quantifiers of VQR that are not connected with entropic measures are not suitable for measuring violations of quantum realism since only the Bures and Hellinger distances could provide bona fide monotones of VQR. Indeed, these distances can be expressed as functions of Rényi (
34
) and Sandwiched Rényi divergences (
33
) in the specific cases when they are symmetric, which reinforces the connection of Bures and Hellinger distances to informational measures.

Clearly, the set of distances we have explored in this work is not exhaustive. An intriguing open question is whether other geometric distances exist that satisfy all the axioms for valid VQR monotones. Identifying such distances could broaden the toolbox for quantifying violations of quantum realism and potentially reveal deeper connections between geometry and quantum information theory. Additionally, it remains to be seen whether these potential candidates share a fundamental link with entropic measures, similar to the Bures and Hellinger distances. Investigating this relationship could further elucidate the interplay between geometric and informational perspectives on quantum realism, offering new insights into the structure and interpretation of quantum measurements.

An important point to highlight is that
\mathfrak{R}_{A}^{\max}(\square)
for the Tr, HS, Bu, and He cases approaches zero as
d_{\mathcal{E}}
increases, as shown in Fig.
1
(a). This behavior contrasts with the original entropic measure of VQR given by (
6
), whose maximum value grows logarithmically with
d_{\mathcal{E}}
. This observation raises several open questions: Is it essential for a measure of realism to assign higher values to observables in higher-dimensional systems? Should the realism-information relation (
5
) be rescaled to ensure that the geometric monotones exhibit increasing maximum values of realism as the dimension grows? These questions highlight potential directions for refining the theoretical framework of quantum realism.

In conclusion, this study advances the understanding of VQR by identifying geometric distances that align with the informational principles underlying quantum realism. Our results provide both theoretical insights and practical tools for analyzing the interplay between measurement, information, and notions of realism in quantum mechanics, offering a promising avenue for future research in quantum foundations and information science.

(63)

(64)

(65)

(66)

(67)

(68)

B.2
Bures distance

For the Bures distance, first we need the Uhlmann fidelity
Uhlmann 1976
given by
F(\rho,\sigma)\coloneqq||\sqrt{\sigma}\sqrt{\rho}||_{1}^{2}=[\text{Tr}(\sqrt{\rho}\sigma\sqrt{\rho})^{\frac{1}{2}}]^{2}
. The fidelity between
\rho\otimes{|{e_{0}}\rangle}{\langle{e_{0}}|}
and
\rho\otimes\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{E}}}{d_{\mathcal{E}}}
is

\displaystyle F\left(\rho\otimes{|{e_{0}}\rangle}{\langle{e_{0}}|},\rho\otimes\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{E}}}{d_{\mathcal{E}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\mbox{$\left|\!\left|\sqrt{\rho\otimes{|{e_{0}}\rangle}{\langle{e_{0}}|}}\sqrt{\rho\otimes\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{E}}}{d_{\mathcal{E}}}}\right|\!\right|$}_{1}^{2},

(82)

\displaystyle=\mbox{$\left|\!\left|\left(\sqrt{\rho}\otimes{|{e_{0}}\rangle}{\langle{e_{0}}|}\right)\left(\sqrt{\rho}\otimes\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{E}}}{\sqrt{d_{\mathcal{E}}}}\right)\right|\!\right|$}_{1}^{2},

(83)

\displaystyle=\mbox{$\left|\!\left|\rho\otimes\frac{{|{e_{0}}\rangle}{\langle{e_{0}}|}}{\sqrt{d_{\mathcal{E}}}}\right|\!\right|$}_{1}^{2},

(84)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d_{\mathcal{E}}}\norm{\rho}_{1}^{2}\norm{\ket{e_0}\bra{e_0}}_{1}^{2},

(85)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d_{\mathcal{E}}}.

(86)

Similarly, the fidelity between
\rho\otimes{|{e_{0}}\rangle}{\langle{e_{0}}|}
and
\Phi_{A}(\rho)\otimes\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{E}}}{d_{\mathcal{E}}}
can be calculated by

\displaystyle F\left(\rho\otimes{|{e_{0}}\rangle}{\langle{e_{0}}|},\Phi_{A}(\rho)\otimes\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{E}}}{d_{\mathcal{E}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\mbox{$\left|\!\left|\sqrt{\rho\otimes{|{e_{0}}\rangle}{\langle{e_{0}}|}}\sqrt{\Phi_{A}(\rho)\otimes\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{E}}}{d_{\mathcal{E}}}}\right|\!\right|$}_{1}^{2},

(87)

\displaystyle=\mbox{$\left|\!\left|\left(\sqrt{\rho}\otimes{|{e_{0}}\rangle}{\langle{e_{0}}|}\right)\left(\sqrt{\Phi_{A}(\rho)}\otimes\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{E}}}{\sqrt{d_{\mathcal{E}}}}\right)\right|\!\right|$}_{1}^{2},

(88)

\displaystyle=\mbox{$\left|\!\left|\sqrt{\rho}\sqrt{\Phi_{A}(\rho)}\otimes\frac{{|{e_{0}}\rangle}{\langle{e_{0}}|}}{\sqrt{d_{\mathcal{E}}}}\right|\!\right|$}_{1}^{2},

(89)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d_{\mathcal{E}}}\norm{\sqrt{\rho}\sqrt{\Phi_A(\rho)}}_{1}^{2}\norm{\ket{e_0}\bra{e_0}}_{1}^{2},

(90)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d_{\mathcal{E}}}F(\rho,\Phi_{A}(\rho)).

(91)

Now, we can calculate:

\displaystyle I_{\mathcal{E|S}}^{\mathrm{Bu}}\,(\Omega_{0})

\displaystyle=d^{2}_{\mathrm{Bu}}\,\left(\rho\otimes{|{e_{0}}\rangle}{\langle{e_{0}}|},\rho\otimes\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{E}}}{d_{\mathcal{E}}}\right),

(92)

\displaystyle=2-2\sqrt{F\left(\rho\otimes{|{e_{0}}\rangle}{\langle{e_{0}}|},\rho\otimes\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{E}}}{d_{\mathcal{E}}}\right)},

(93)

\displaystyle=2-\frac{2}{\sqrt{d_{\mathcal{E}}}}.

(94)

\displaystyle I_{\mathcal{E|S}}^{\mathrm{Bu}}\,(\Omega_{t})

\displaystyle=d^{2}_{\mathrm{Bu}}\,\left(\rho\otimes{|{e_{0}}\rangle}{\langle{e_{0}}|},\Phi_{A}(\rho)\otimes\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{E}}}{d_{\mathcal{E}}}\right),

(95)

\displaystyle=2-2\sqrt{F\left(\rho\otimes{|{e_{0}}\rangle}{\langle{e_{0}}|},\Phi_{A}(\rho)\otimes\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{E}}}{d_{\mathcal{E}}}\right)},

(96)

\displaystyle=2-2\sqrt{\frac{1}{d_{\mathcal{E}}}F\left(\rho,\Phi_{A}(\rho)\right)}.

(97)

\displaystyle\Delta I_{\mathcal{E|S}}^{\mathrm{Bu}}\,(\Omega_{t},\Omega_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{\mathcal{E}}}}\left(2-2\sqrt{F(\rho,\Phi_{A}(\rho))}\right),

(98)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{\mathcal{E}}}}d^{2}_{\mathrm{Bu}}\,(\rho,\Phi_{A}(\rho)).

(99)

B.3
Hellinger distance

The quantum Hellinger distance
d_{\mathrm{He}}\,

Roga et al. 2016
;
Spehner et al. 2017
is given by
d_{\mathrm{He}}\,(\rho,\sigma)\coloneqq\norm{\sqrt{\sigma}-\sqrt{\rho}}_{2}=\left(2-2\text{Tr}\sqrt{\sigma}\sqrt{\rho}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle I_{\mathcal{E|S}}^{\mathrm{He}}\,(\Omega_{0})

\displaystyle=d^{2}_{\mathrm{He}}\,\left(\rho\otimes{|{e_{0}}\rangle}{\langle{e_{0}}|},\rho\otimes\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{E}}}{d_{\mathcal{E}}}\right),

(100)

\displaystyle=2-2\text{Tr}\left(\sqrt{\rho\otimes{|{e_{0}}\rangle}{\langle{e_{0}}|}}\sqrt{\rho\otimes\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{E}}}{d_{\mathcal{E}}}}\right),

(101)

\displaystyle=2-2\text{Tr}\left(\rho\otimes\frac{{|{e_{0}}\rangle}{\langle{e_{0}}|}}{\sqrt{d_{\mathcal{E}}}}\right),

(102)

\displaystyle=2-\frac{2}{\sqrt{d_{\mathcal{E}}}}\text{Tr}(\rho)\text{Tr}({|{e_{0}}\rangle}{\langle{e_{0}}|}),

(103)

\displaystyle=2-\frac{2}{\sqrt{d_{\mathcal{E}}}}.

(104)

\displaystyle I_{\mathcal{E|S}}^{\mathrm{He}}\,(\Omega_{t})

\displaystyle=d^{2}_{\mathrm{He}}\,\left(\rho\otimes{|{e_{0}}\rangle}{\langle{e_{0}}|},\Phi_{A}(\rho)\otimes\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{E}}}{d_{\mathcal{E}}}\right),

(105)

\displaystyle=2-2\text{Tr}\left(\sqrt{\rho\otimes{|{e_{0}}\rangle}{\langle{e_{0}}|}}\sqrt{\Phi_{A}(\rho)\otimes\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{E}}}{d_{\mathcal{E}}}}\right),

(106)

\displaystyle=2-2\text{Tr}\left(\sqrt{\rho}\sqrt{\Phi_{A}(\rho)}\otimes\frac{{|{e_{0}}\rangle}{\langle{e_{0}}|}}{\sqrt{d_{\mathcal{E}}}}\right),

(107)

\displaystyle=2-\frac{2}{\sqrt{d_{\mathcal{E}}}}\text{Tr}\left(\sqrt{\rho}\sqrt{\Phi_{A}(\rho)}\right)\text{Tr}({|{e_{0}}\rangle}{\langle{e_{0}}|}),

(108)

\displaystyle=2-\frac{2}{\sqrt{d_{\mathcal{E}}}}\text{Tr}\left(\sqrt{\rho}\sqrt{\Phi_{A}(\rho)}\right).

(109)

\displaystyle\Delta I_{\mathcal{E|S}}^{\mathrm{He}}\,(\Omega_{t},\Omega_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{\mathcal{E}}}}\left[2-2\text{Tr}\left(\sqrt{\rho}\sqrt{\Phi_{A}(\rho)}\right)\right],

(110)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{\mathcal{E}}}}d^{2}_{\mathrm{He}}\,(\rho,\Phi_{A}(\rho)).

(111)

Bearing-Only Solution for Fermat-Weber Location Problem: Generalized Algorithms
Nhat-Minh Le-Phan, Phuoc Doan Nguyen, Hyo-Sung Ahn, Minh Hoang Trinh

Abstract
This paper presents novel algorithms for the Fermat-Weber Location Problem, guiding an autonomous agent to the point that minimizes the weighted sum of Euclidean distances to some beacons using only bearing measurements. The existing results address only the simple scenario where the beacons are stationary and the agent is modeled by a single integrator. In this paper, we propose a number of bearing-only algorithms that let the agent, which can be modeled as either a single-integrator or a double-integrator, follow the Fermat-Weber point of a group of stationary or moving beacons. The theoretical results are rigorously proven using Lyapunov theory and supported with simulation examples.



keywords
Fermat-Weber location problem; bearing-only algorithm; moving beacons; time-varying velocity; double-integrator agent.

Introduction

The Fermat-Weber Location Problem (FWLP) is a classical problem in operational research, focusing on identifying a position called Fermat-Weber point that minimizes the weighted sum of distances to a set of beacons. Traditionally, this problem has been studied with the assumption that the beacons are stationary and not collinear
[
11
]
.
In 1937, Hungarian mathematician Endre Weiszfeld proposed a numerical algorithm for finding the minimum, known as the Fermat-Weber point, which has since been widely studied by other researchers
[
5
,
2
,
11
]
. However, Weiszfeld’s original analysis did not address the weighted problem, assuming instead that all weights were equal to one. The corresponding results for the weighted case, with nearly identical analysis, can be found in
[
1
]
. Additionally, although Weiszfeld assumed that the initial estimator was distinct from any beacon location, it is possible for the estimator to become collocated with a beacon at a specific moment and get stuck there
[
6
]
.

This paper considers the FWLP in the context of the guidance problem, focusing on scenarios where a robot or an autonomous agent navigates to the Fermat-Weber point of beacons for surveillance purposes, despite GPS information being unavailable or unreliable. Thus, the agent can only sense relative information from the beacons. Although there are many complex active sensors available, low-cost and lightweight passive onboard sensors are commonly used in small-sized agents to simplify the required hardware systems. Nevertheless, in many guidance problems, bearing measurements are one of the few types of information available from passive sensors. Hence, we focus specifically on the case where sensors can only obtain bearing measurements. Compared to distance and other types of measurements, bearing sensing capability is a minimal requirement for the agent. In the real world, bearing measurements can be obtained by an on-board camera. Bearing-only algorithms have been extensively researched for various navigation, guidance, and control problems. In
[
17
]
, a bearing-only measurement-based guidance law was introduced for the agent to reach its desired location, characterized by a set of feasible bearing angles to three stationary beacons in the plane.
In
[
8
]
, bearing-only guidance laws was proposed for the problem of target following. The bearing-only observers are designed to estimate the motion of the target, which is also studied in
[
10
,
14
]
. A wall-following UAV guidance law was proposed in
[
16
]
, where bearing information, also referred to as line-of-sight (LOS), was utilized for a proportional navigation law. It is notable that in several algorithms, the bearing rate is required. For example, in a missile guidance problem, the line-of-sight (LOS) rate can be obtained from the seeker and plays a vital role in every guidance law
[
20
]
. The bearing-only formation tracking control problem was considered in
[
21
,
22
,
9
]
. In these papers, the objective is to design a distributed bearing-only control law for multiple agents, allowing them to eventually achieve a desired formation. The desired formation is defined by a set of desired bearing constraints among the agents and the leaders’ positions. The main difference between formation tracking and the FWLP is that, the Fermat-Weber point is implicitly defined by an algebraic equation involving the beacons and cannot be determined from the beacons’ positions and the bearing measurements. The agent needs to use only bearing information to eventually detect and track the point which satisfies the algebraic equation.

Bearing-only solution to the Fermat-Weber problem was first proposed in
[
18
]
, where a single-integrator agent model was considered. This simple, continuous-time solution can be considered as an application of the steepest gradient descent algorithm. In
[
7
]
, the authors utilized the dimensional lifting technique that rules out the possibility of collision between the agents and the beacons while asymptotically stabilizing the Fermat-Weber point. Furthermore, the finite time control law and the analysis of robustness to small imprecise measurements in two-dimensional space were also proposed. On the other hand, in many problems, such as surveillance, where the agent needs to move to a position that optimizes supervision of all beacons, it is natural to consider that the beacons, which may represent a formation of ground mobile robots, are in motion. Thus, it is necessary to study the Fermat-Weber problem with moving beacons. Another issue is that the algorithms proposed in
[
18
,
7
]
are only applicable to the single-integrator agent model. For many practical systems, a second-order dynamics provides a more appropriate model.

The main contribution of this article is to propose novel bearing-only algorithms for the FWLP under different conditions. First, we extend existing work for the single-integrator agent model to include global exponential stability, robustness analysis against measurement errors in an arbitrary dimensional space. We also provide a proof of finite-time convergence, which was not presented in
[
7
]
. Furthermore, we propose algorithms when the beacons are in motion, thus, greatly extend the applicability of the problem in comparison with the existing works. Second, the algorithms for the double-integrator agent model are designed to accommodate both stationary and moving beacons, under the assumption that the agent can also access to its own velocity. The stability analysis for double-integrators are established based on Barbalat’s lemma. The proposed algorithms in this paper represent a crucial step in extending the application of FWLP to practical tasks such as surveillance, aerial-ground cooperative control, and autonomous guidance of a vehicle.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: Section

presents notations and formulates the problem. Sections
3
and

propose the control laws that solve the FWLP for the single- and double-integrator agent models, respectively. Simulation results are provided in Section
5
. Finally, Section
6
concludes our article.

3.1
Stationary Beacons: The Gradient Control Law Revisited and Novel Results

For simplicity, we assumed that the Fermat-Weber point is not a beacon, i.e.,
\mathbf{p}^{*}\neq\mathbf{p}_{i}\ \ \forall i=1,\dots,n
. In such a case,
[
18
]
proposes the following bearing-only control law

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}\frac{{\mathbf{p}}_{i}-\mathbf{p}}{\|{\mathbf{p}}_{i}-\mathbf{p}\|}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}.

(7)

Indeed, due to the fact that
\frac{\partial{d}_{i}}{\partial\mathbf{p}}=-{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{\top},~\frac{\partial{\mathbf{g}}_{i}}{\partial\mathbf{p}}=-\frac{\mathbf{P}_{{\mathbf{g}}_{i}}}{{d}_{i}},
the control law (
7
) can be rewritten as

\mathbf{u}=-\nabla_{\mathbf{p}}{f}(\mathbf{p})=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}\left(\frac{\partial{d}_{i}}{\partial\mathbf{p}}\right)^{\top}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i},

(8)

which is the steepest gradient descent corresponding to the objective function

{f}(\mathbf{p})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{d}_{i}
.
In the case of stationary beacons and single-integrator model agents, it was rigorously proved that (
6
) can solve the FWLP.

Robustness against bearing error

3.1.3
Finite Time Control Law

The finite time control law is proposed as follows

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}=\text{sig}^{a}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}\right),

(18)

where
\text{sig}^{a}(x)=\text{sgn}(x)|x|^{a}
, and
a\in(0,1)
. Before considering the stability under the proposed control law, we have the following lemma

Lemma 5

Every point
\mathbf{p}(t)\in\mathcal{B}_{R}
satisfies

\mathbf{g}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{*}>0,\ \ \forall i=1,\ldots,n.

PROOF.

If
\mathbf{p}(t)\in\mathcal{B}_{R}
, we have

\|\mathbf{p}(t)-\mathbf{p}^{*}\|<\|\mathbf{p}_{i}-\mathbf{p}^{*}\|\ \ \forall i=1,\ldots,n
,
which is equivalent to

\displaystyle(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{*})^{\top}(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{*})

\displaystyle<(\mathbf{p}_{i}-\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{*})^{\top}(\mathbf{p}_{i}-\mathbf{p}^{*})

\displaystyle(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{*})^{\top}(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}_{i})

\displaystyle<(\mathbf{p}_{i}-\mathbf{p})^{\top}(\mathbf{p}_{i}-\mathbf{p}^{*})

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\delta}^{\top}\mathbf{z}_{i}

\displaystyle<\mathbf{z}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{z}_{i}^{*}

If
\boldsymbol{\delta}^{\top}\mathbf{z}_{i}\geq 0
, we have
\mathbf{z}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{z}_{i}^{*}>0
. Suppose that there exists beacon
i
so that
(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{*})^{\top}(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}_{i})<0
, consider the sum

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\delta}^{\top}\mathbf{z}_{i}+\mathbf{z}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{z}_{i}^{*}=\|\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}_{i}\|^{2}>0.

Thus,
\mathbf{z}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{z}_{i}^{*}=d_{i}d_{i}^{*}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{*}>0,\forall i
. From the definition of
\mathcal{B}_{R}
,
d_{i},d_{i}^{*}>0\ \forall i
, and the proof follows.

\blacksquare

Remark 3

Define the set
\mathcal{C}=\{\mathbf{p}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}|\ \ {\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{*}\geq 0\ \ \forall i,i=1,\ldots,n.
This set
\mathcal{C}
can be represented as
\mathcal{C}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{H}_{i}
, where
\mathcal{H}_{i}=\{\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}|\ \ {\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{*}\geq 0\}
is actually a half-space. Thus,
\mathcal{C}
is a convex polytope
[
12
]
. For instance, Fig.
1
illustrates an example of the sets
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{B}_{R}
of three beacons on the plane.

An example of the sets
\mathcal{C}
and

Corollary 2

For every
\mathbf{p}(t)\in\mathcal{B}_{R}
, the following holds

{\mathbf{z}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{W}}({\mathbf{g}}-{\mathbf{g}}^{*})\leq\frac{\lambda_{\max}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}\mathbf{P}_{{g}^{*}_{i}})\|\boldsymbol{\delta}(t)\|^{2}}{\min_{i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}}{d}_{i}(t)}

(19)

PROOF.

From Corollary
1
and Lemma
5
, one has

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\delta}^{\top}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}\mathbf{P}_{{g}^{*}_{i}}\right)\boldsymbol{\delta}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{d}_{i}^{2}(1-{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{*})(1+{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{*})

\displaystyle\geq\min_{i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}}d_{i}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{d}_{i}(1-{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{*})

\displaystyle=\min_{i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}}d_{i}{\mathbf{z}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{W}}({\mathbf{g}}-{\mathbf{g}}^{*}).

(20)

Since
\boldsymbol{\delta}^{\top}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}\mathbf{P}_{{g}^{*}_{i}})\boldsymbol{\delta}\leq\lambda_{\max}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}\mathbf{P}_{{g}^{*}_{i}})\|\boldsymbol{\delta}(t)\|^{2}
, the corollary can be immediately obtained.

\blacksquare

Theorem 3

Suppose that there is no collision among the agent and beacons. Under the control law (
18
),
\mathbf{p}(t)
globally asymptotically converges to
{\mathbf{p}}^{*}
. Moreover, if
\mathbf{p}(0)\in\mathcal{B}_{R}
,
\mathbf{p}(t)\rightarrow{\mathbf{p}}^{*}
in finite time.

PROOF.

Consider the Lyapunov function
V={\mathbf{z}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{W}}({\mathbf{g}}-{\mathbf{g}}^{*}).
From Lemma
2
,
V\geq 0
and
V=0
if and only if
\mathbf{p}={\mathbf{p}}^{*}
. It can be seen that
{\mathbf{z}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{W}}\dot{{\mathbf{g}}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{z}}_{i}\dot{{\mathbf{g}}}_{i}=0
. The derivative of
V
is as follows

\displaystyle\dot{V}

\displaystyle=({\mathbf{g}}-{\mathbf{g}}^{*})^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{W}}\dot{{\mathbf{z}}}

\displaystyle=-\big(\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}(\mathbf{g}_{i}-\mathbf{g}_{i}^{*})\big)^{\top}\dot{\mathbf{p}}

\displaystyle=-\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}(\mathbf{g}_{i}-\mathbf{g}_{i}^{*})^{\top}\text{sig}^{a}\left(\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=1}^{d}\left|\left[\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}({\mathbf{g}}_{i}-\mathbf{g}_{i}^{*})\right]_{k}\right|^{\alpha+1}

\displaystyle=-\sum\nolimits_{k=1}^{d}\left(\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}({\mathbf{g}}_{i}-\mathbf{g}_{i}^{*})\right]_{k}^{2}\right)^{\frac{\alpha+1}{2}}

Using the inequality in
[
19
]
, we have

\displaystyle\dot{V}

\displaystyle\leq-d^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}\left(\sum\nolimits_{k=1}^{d}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}({\mathbf{g}}_{i}-\mathbf{g}_{i}^{*})\right]_{k}^{2}\right)^{\frac{\alpha+1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq-d^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}\left(\left|\left|\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}({\mathbf{g}}_{i}-\mathbf{g}_{i}^{*})\right|\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{\alpha+1}{2}}\leq 0,

Based on LaSalle invariance principle,
\mathbf{p}(t)\to\Omega=\{\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}|~\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}=\mathbf{0}_{d+1}\}=\{{\mathbf{p}}^{*}\}
globally asymptotically. In order to prove the second part of this theorem, we consider the case when
\mathbf{p}(t)\in\mathcal{B}_{R}
.
From the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, we have

\displaystyle\left|\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}({\mathbf{g}}_{i}-{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{*})\right|\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\|\boldsymbol{\delta}\|^{2}\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}({\mathbf{g}}_{i}-{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{*})\|^{2}}{\|\boldsymbol{\delta}\|^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\big(\boldsymbol{\delta}^{\top}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}({\mathbf{g}}_{i}-{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{*})\big)^{2}}{\|\boldsymbol{\delta}\|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\big((\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{*})^{\top}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}({\mathbf{g}}_{i}-{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{*})\big)^{2}}{\|\boldsymbol{\delta}\|^{2}}.

From the fact that

\displaystyle\big(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}_{i}+\mathbf{p}_{i}-\mathbf{p}^{*})^{\top}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}({\mathbf{g}}_{i}-{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{*})\big)^{2}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{z}_{i}^{\top}\omega_{i}({\mathbf{g}}_{i}-{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{*})-\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\mathbf{z}^{*}_{i})^{\top}\omega_{i}({\mathbf{g}}_{i}-{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{*})\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\mathbf{z}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{W}}({\mathbf{g}}-{\mathbf{g}}^{*})-\mathbf{z}^{*\top}\bar{\mathbf{W}}({\mathbf{g}}-{\mathbf{g}}^{*})\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\big(\mathbf{z}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{W}}({\mathbf{g}}-{\mathbf{g}}^{*})\big)^{2},

one can apply (
13
) and Corollary
2
, leading to

\displaystyle\left|\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}({\mathbf{g}}_{i}-{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{*})\right|\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\big(\mathbf{z}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{W}}({\mathbf{g}}-{\mathbf{g}}^{*})\big)^{2}}{\|\boldsymbol{\delta}\|^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\lambda_{\min}^{2}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}\mathbf{P}_{{g}^{*}_{i}})\|\boldsymbol{\delta}(t)\|^{2}}{4\max_{i}{d}_{i}^{2}(t)}

\displaystyle\geq\chi\frac{\min_{i}{d}_{i}(t)}{\max_{i}{d}_{i}^{2}(t)}\mathbf{z}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{W}}({\mathbf{g}}-{\mathbf{g}}^{*}),

where

\chi=\frac{\lambda_{\min}^{2}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}\mathbf{P}_{{g}^{*}_{i}})}{4\lambda_{\max}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}\mathbf{P}_{{g}^{*}_{i}})}.

As
\mathbf{p}(t)\in\mathcal{B}_{R}
, from Lemma
3
, the lower bound of
\frac{\min_{i}{d}_{i}(t)}{\max_{i}{d}_{i}^{2}(t)}
can be obtained as follows

\displaystyle\frac{\min_{i}{d}_{i}(t)}{\max_{i}{d}_{i}^{2}(t)}

\displaystyle\geq h(\|\boldsymbol{\delta}(t)\|)=\frac{\min_{i}{d}_{i}^{*}-\|\boldsymbol{\delta}(t)\|}{(\|\boldsymbol{\delta}(t)\|+\max_{i}{d}_{i}^{*})^{2}}>0

\displaystyle\frac{\partial h(\|\boldsymbol{\delta}(t)\|)}{\partial\|\boldsymbol{\delta}(t)\|}

\displaystyle=\frac{\boldsymbol{\|\delta}(t)\|-\max_{i}{d}_{i}^{*}-2\min_{i}{d}_{i}^{*}}{\big(\|\boldsymbol{\delta}(t)\|+\max_{i}{d}_{i}^{*}\big)^{3}}<0.

Hence,
h(\|\boldsymbol{\delta(t)}\|)
increases as
\|\boldsymbol{\delta(t)}\|
decreases. As a result,
h(\|\boldsymbol{\delta}\|)>h(R)
. It follows that

\dot{V}\leq-d^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}(\chi h(R))^{\frac{\alpha+1}{2}}V^{\frac{\alpha+1}{2}}=-\kappa V^{\frac{\alpha+1}{2}}.

Based on
[
3
]
, as
0<\frac{\alpha+1}{2}<1
, it follows that
V(t)\to 0
in finite time. Thus,
\mathbf{p}(t)=\mathbf{p}^{*}
if
\mathbf{p}(0)\in\mathcal{B}_{\epsilon}
,
\forall t\geq T
, where
T\leq\frac{2V(0)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}}{\kappa^{\frac{\alpha+1}{2}}(1-\alpha)}
.

\blacksquare

3.2
Moving Beacons

We suppose that the beacons are moving with velocity
\mathbf{v}_{i}(t)=[v_{1},\ldots,v_{d}]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, i.e.,

\dot{\mathbf{p}}_{i}(t)=\mathbf{v}_{i}(t),~i=1,\ldots,n,

(21)

Then, the minimum
\mathbf{p}^{*}
of the function
f(\mathbf{p})
is also time-varying. Let the beacons move with the same known velocity,
\mathbf{v}_{i}=\mathbf{v^{*}},\forall i=1,\ldots,n
. Then, the relative positions between the beacons are time-invariant, and the minimum
\mathbf{p}^{*}
moves with the same velocity as the beacons. This statement can be proved by a simple change of variables
{\mathbf{q}_{i}(t)}=\mathbf{p}_{i}(t)-\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{v}(\tau)d\tau
, of which
\dot{\mathbf{q}}_{i}=\mathbf{0}_{d}
. Because the minimum
\mathbf{q}^{*}
of
{f}(\mathbf{q})
should satisfy all the desired vectors
\{\mathbf{g}_{i}^{*}\}_{i=1,\ldots,n}
, one has

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{\mathbf{g}_{i}^{*}}(\mathbf{q}_{i}-\mathbf{q}^{*})=\mathbf{0}_{d+1},~i=1,\ldots,n.

(22)

This implies
\mathbf{q}^{*}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}_{\mathbf{g}_{i}^{*}}\right)^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}_{\mathbf{g}_{i}^{*}}\mathbf{q}_{i}
, and thus

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{q}}^{*}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}_{\mathbf{g}_{i}^{*}}\right)^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}_{\mathbf{g}_{i}^{*}}\dot{\mathbf{q}}_{i}=\mathbf{0}_{d+1}.

(23)

Hence, one has
\dot{\mathbf{q}}^{*}(t)=\dot{\mathbf{p}}^{*}(t)-\mathbf{v}(t)=\mathbf{0}_{d+1},
or
\dot{\mathbf{p}}^{*}(t)=\mathbf{v}(t)
. Correspondingly, the Fermat-Weber point
{\mathbf{p}}^{*}(t)
also moves with velocity
\mathbf{v}(t)
.

3.2.1
The beacons move with the same constant velocity

Suppose that the beacons move with unknown constant velocity, i.e.,
\dot{\mathbf{v}}^{*}=\mathbf{0}_{d}
. The following adaptive control law is proposed

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}+\hat{\mathbf{v}}(t),

(24)

\displaystyle\dot{\hat{\mathbf{v}}}(t)

\displaystyle=k\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}.

where
k>0
is a control gain and
\boldsymbol{\hat{\mathbf{v}}}(0)=\mathbf{0}_{d}
. We will prove the following theorem

Theorem 4

Let the beacons move with constant velocity
\mathbf{v}^{*}
. Suppose that there is no collision among the agent and beacons. Under the control law (
24
), for all
\mathbf{p}(0)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\|\mathbf{p}(t)-{\mathbf{p}}^{*}\|\to 0
as
t\to\infty
.

PROOF.

Consider the function

V={\mathbf{z}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{W}}({\mathbf{g}}-{\mathbf{g}}^{*})+\frac{\|\hat{\mathbf{v}}-\mathbf{v}^{*}(t)\|^{2}}{2k}.

According to Lemma
2
,
V
is positive definite with regard to
\boldsymbol{\delta}
and
\hat{\mathbf{v}}-\mathbf{v}^{*}(t)
. The derivative of
V
can be obtained as follows

\displaystyle\dot{V}

\displaystyle=({\mathbf{g}}-{\mathbf{g}}^{*})^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{W}}\dot{{\mathbf{z}}}+\frac{1}{k}(\hat{\mathbf{v}}-\mathbf{v}^{*})^{\top}\dot{\hat{\mathbf{v}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}({\mathbf{g}}_{i}-{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{*})^{\top}\left(\mathbf{v}^{*}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}-\hat{\mathbf{v}}(t)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+(\hat{\mathbf{v}}-\mathbf{v}^{*})^{\top}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}

\displaystyle=-\left|\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}\right|\right|^{2}\leq 0.

From LaSalle’s invariance principle, each trajectory converges
to the largest invariance set inside
\Omega=\{\dot{V}=0\}
. Thus, it follows that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}\equiv\mathbf{0}
. From Lemma
1
, the Fermat-Weber point is asymptotically tracked. Furthermore, from
\mathbf{p}(t)\equiv{\mathbf{p}}^{*}(t)
, it holds that
\mathbf{v}(t)\equiv\mathbf{v}^{*}(t)
.
\blacksquare

3.2.2
The beacons move with the same bounded velocity

Next, we consider the problem where the beacons are moving at the same unknown bounded time-varying velocity, i.e.,
\|\mathbf{v}^{*}\|_{\infty}\leq\eta
. It is assumed that
\mathbf{v}^{*}
is uniformly continuous and the upper bound
\eta
is available to the agent. We propose the following control law

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}(t)=k\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}+\beta\text{sgn}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}\right),

(25)

where the constants
\beta>\eta
and
k\geq 0
.

Theorem 5

Let the beacons’ velocity
\mathbf{v}^{*}(t)
be a bounded uniformly continuous function with a known upper bound
\eta
. If there is no collision among the agent and beacons, under the control law (
25
), the tracking error
\boldsymbol{\delta}(t)
is uniformly bounded and
\boldsymbol{\delta}(t)\to\mathbf{0}_{d}
, as
t\to+\infty
.

PROOF.

Since
\mathbf{u}(t)
is discontinuous, the solution of the system is understood in Filippov sense
[
15
]
. Consider the function
V={\mathbf{z}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{W}}({\mathbf{g}}-{\mathbf{g}}^{*})
, we have

\displaystyle\dot{V}

\displaystyle\in^{\text{a.e}}({\mathbf{g}}-{\mathbf{g}}^{*})^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{W}}\text{K}[\dot{{\mathbf{z}}}]

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}\right)^{\top}\left(\mathbf{v}^{*}-k\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}-\beta\text{K[sgn]}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=-k\left|\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}-\beta\left|\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}\right|\right|_{1}+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}\right)^{\top}\mathbf{v}^{*}

\displaystyle\leq-k\left|\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}-\beta\left|\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}\right|\right|_{1}+\left|\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}\right|\right|_{1}\left|\left|\mathbf{v}^{*}\right|\right|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq-k\left|\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}-(\beta-\eta)\left|\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}\right|\right|_{1}\leq 0.

(26)

It follows that
V(t)\leq V(0)
,
\forall t>0
, and
{\mathbf{z}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{W}}({\mathbf{g}}-{\mathbf{g}}^{*})
is always bounded.
Suppose that
V(0)=\alpha
, we have
\mathbf{z}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{W}}({\mathbf{g}}-{\mathbf{g}}^{*})\leq\alpha
, which is equivalent to

\displaystyle\frac{\|\boldsymbol{\delta}(t)\|^{2}}{\big(\|\boldsymbol{\delta}(t)\|+\max_{i}{d}_{i}^{*}\big)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\alpha}{\lambda_{\min}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}\mathbf{P}_{{g}^{*}_{i}})}=\zeta.

The above inequality implies
\|\boldsymbol{\delta}(t)\|\in[0,\xi]
, where
\xi=(\zeta+\sqrt{\zeta^{2}+4\zeta\max_{i}{d}_{i}^{*}})/2
. Hence,
\boldsymbol{\delta}(t)
is uniformly bounded. As there is no collision,
\dot{{\mathbf{g}}}_{i}=\frac{\mathbf{P}_{{\mathbf{g}}_{i}}}{\hat{d}_{i}}(\mathbf{v}^{*}-\mathbf{v})
is bounded so that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}(t)
is bounded and uniformly continuous. As a result,
\dot{V}
is also uniformly continuous.
From Barbalat’s lemma
[
4
]
,
\dot{V}\rightarrow 0
as
t\rightarrow\infty
, i.e.,
\boldsymbol{\delta}=\mathbf{p}-{\mathbf{p}}^{*}\to\mathbf{0}_{d}
, as
t\rightarrow\infty
.
\blacksquare

In practice, the upper bound of beacons’ velocity
\eta
may not be known by the agent.
To this end, we may relax the objective from perfectly tracking to maintaining in a close neighborhood of the Fermat-Weber point. Inspired of adaptive sliding mode control design
[
13
]
, the following control law is proposed

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}(t)

\displaystyle=k\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}+\beta\text{sgn}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}\right),

(27)

\displaystyle\dot{\beta}(t)

\displaystyle=k_{\beta}\left(\left|\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}\right|\right|_{1}-\tau_{\beta}\beta\right),

where
k_{\beta},\tau_{\beta}
are positive constants.

Theorem 6

Suppose there is no collision between the agent and beacons, and
\mathbf{p}(0)\in\mathcal{B}_{R}
. Under the control law (
27
), the agent will converge to a small neighborhood of the actual Fermat-Weber point after a finite time.

PROOF.

Consider the following Lyapunov function

V={\mathbf{z}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{W}}({\mathbf{g}}-{\mathbf{g}}^{*})+\frac{1}{2k_{\beta}}(\beta-\overline{\beta})^{2},
where
\overline{\beta}>\eta
. The derivative of
V
can be obtained as follows

\displaystyle\dot{V}

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}\Big)^{\top}\left(\mathbf{v}^{*}-k\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}-\beta\text{sgn}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}\Big)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+(\beta-\overline{\beta})\left(\left|\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}\right|\right|_{1}-\tau_{\beta}\beta\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-k\left|\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}\right|\right|^{2}-\overline{\beta}\left|\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}\right|\right|_{1}+\left|\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}\right|\right|_{1}\|\mathbf{v}^{*}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{\tau_{\beta}}{2}(2\beta^{2}-2\beta\overline{\beta}+\overline{\beta}^{2})+\frac{\tau_{\beta}}{2}\overline{\beta}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-k\left|\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}({\mathbf{g}}_{i}-{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{*})\right|\right|^{2}-\frac{\tau_{\beta}}{2}(\beta-\overline{\beta})^{2}+\frac{\tau_{\beta}}{2}\overline{\beta}^{2}.

(28)

Similar to the proof of Theorem
3
, we have

\displaystyle\dot{V}

\displaystyle\leq-\underbrace{\min\big(k\chi h(R),k_{\beta}\tau_{\beta}\big)}_{\varrho>0}V+\frac{\tau_{\beta}}{2}\overline{\beta}^{2}

(29)

\displaystyle=-\varrho(1-\mu)V-\varrho\mu V+\frac{\tau_{\beta}}{2}\overline{\beta}^{2},

where
\mu\in(0,1).
It is clearly seen that the time derivative of
V
is negative outside of the compact set
\mathcal{D}=\{\|\boldsymbol{\delta}(t)\|<R|~V(t)\leq\frac{\tau_{\beta}\overline{\beta}^{2}}{2\varrho\mu}\}
. In the set
\mathcal{D}
, Corollary
1
implies the following

\displaystyle\frac{\|\boldsymbol{\delta}(t)\|^{2}}{\|\boldsymbol{\delta}(t)\|+\max_{i}{d}_{i}^{*}}\leq\frac{\tau_{\beta}\overline{\beta}^{2}}{\lambda_{\min}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}\mathbf{P}_{{g}^{*}_{i}})\varrho\mu}=\zeta,

which is equivalent to
\|\boldsymbol{\delta}(t)\|\in[0,\xi]
, where
\xi=(\zeta+\sqrt{\zeta^{2}+4\zeta\max_{i}{d}_{i}^{*}})/2
. Thus, for all
\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{B}_{R}\setminus\mathcal{D}
, the equality
\dot{V}\leq-\varrho(1-\mu)V
holds. This implies that
V
will decrease until
\mathbf{p}(t)\in\mathcal{B}_{\xi}
within a finite time. Finally, the ultimate bound
\xi
can be selected sufficiently small given that
k,k_{\beta}\tau_{\beta}
are selected large enough. This completes the proof of this theorem.
\blacksquare

Lemma 6

Under the control laws (
18
),(
24
),(
25
) or (
27
), there always exists a small positive constant
\alpha
such that if the Lyapunov functions satisfy
V(0)\leq\alpha
, the collision can be avoided.

PROOF.

Similar to the proof of Theorem
5
, the inequality
V(t)\leq\alpha
implies
\|\boldsymbol{\delta}(t)\|\in[0,\xi]
, where
\xi=(\zeta+\sqrt{\zeta^{2}+4\zeta\max_{i}{d}_{i}^{*}})/2
. Hence, once can always choose a small
\alpha
so that
\xi<\min_{i}d_{i}^{*}
. Hence,
\boldsymbol{\delta}(t)<\min_{i}d_{i}^{*}
, i.e.,
\boldsymbol{p}(t)\in\mathcal{B}_{R}
. At time
t=0
, similar to Remark
1
, since
\boldsymbol{\delta}(0)<\min_{i}d_{i}^{*}
,
d_{i}>\epsilon
and there is no collision initially. As
\dot{V}\leq 0
,
\|\boldsymbol{\delta(t)}\|
cannot escape
[0,\xi]
, and the collision avoidance is guaranteed. Otherwise, there must be an instant
t_{1}
such that
V(t_{1})=\alpha
and
\dot{V}(t_{1})>0
, which is impossible.
\blacksquare

4
Double-Integrator Agent

4.1
Stationary Beacons

Consider the case where agent’s dynamic is modeled as (
3
), and beacons are stationary. The following PD-like bearing-only control law is proposed

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}-k\mathbf{v},\,\text{with }k>0.

(30)

Theorem 7

Suppose there is no collision and the agent can obtain its own velocity, under the control law (
30
), for all
\mathbf{p}(0)=[p_{1}(0),\ldots,p_{d}(0)]^{\top}
,
\mathbf{p}(t)
asymptotically converges to
{\mathbf{p}}^{*}
.

PROOF.

Consider the Lyapunov function

V={\mathbf{z}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{W}}({\mathbf{g}}-{\mathbf{g}}^{*})+\frac{\mathbf{v}^{\top}\mathbf{v}}{2}.

The derivative of
V
is as follows

\displaystyle\dot{V}

\displaystyle=({\mathbf{g}}-{\mathbf{g}}^{*})^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{W}}\dot{{\mathbf{z}}}+\mathbf{v}^{\top}\dot{\mathbf{v}}

(31)

\displaystyle=-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}\right)^{\top}\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{v}^{\top}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}-k\mathbf{v}\right)

\displaystyle=-k\|\mathbf{v}\|^{2}\leq 0.

Since
V\geq 0
and
\dot{V}\leq 0
,
V(t)
is always bounded, so does
\|\mathbf{v}(t)\|
. Based on the LaSalle’s invariance principle, each trajectory of the system asymptotically converges to the largest invariant set inside
\Omega=\{\dot{V}=0\}
, i.e.,
\mathbf{v}\equiv\mathbf{0}_{d}
. Whenever
\mathbf{v}\equiv\mathbf{0}_{d}
, it is required that
\dot{\mathbf{v}}\equiv\mathbf{0}_{d}
. From equation (
30
), it follows that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}\equiv\mathbf{0}_{d}
. This implies that
{\mathbf{g}}_{i}\equiv\mathbf{g}_{i}^{*}
and
\mathbf{p}(t)\equiv{\mathbf{p}}^{*}
. Thus,
\mathbf{p}(t)\to{\mathbf{p}}^{*}
and
\mathbf{v}(t)\to\mathbf{0}_{d}
as
t\to\infty
.

\blacksquare

4.2
Moving Beacons

4.2.1
The beacons move with the same constant velocity

Let the beacons move with unknown constant velocity, i.e.,
\dot{\mathbf{v}}^{*}=\mathbf{0}_{d}
. The following adaptive control law is proposed

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle=(k_{2}+1)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}-k_{1}(\mathbf{v}-\hat{\mathbf{v}}),

(32)

\displaystyle\dot{\hat{\mathbf{v}}}

\displaystyle=k_{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i},

where
k_{1},k_{2}>0
. The following theorem yields

Theorem 8

Suppose that there is no collision. Under the control law (
32
), for all initial conditions,
\mathbf{p}(t)\to{\mathbf{p}}^{*}(t)
and
{\mathbf{v}}(t)\rightarrow\mathbf{v}^{*}
as
t\rightarrow\infty
.

PROOF.

Consider the following Lyapunov function:

V={\mathbf{z}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{W}}({\mathbf{g}}-{\mathbf{g}}^{*})+\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{v}-\hat{\mathbf{v}}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2k_{2}}\|\hat{\mathbf{v}}-\mathbf{v}^{*}\|^{2}.

The derivative of
V
can be obtained as follows

\displaystyle\dot{V}=

\displaystyle-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}\right)^{\top}(\mathbf{v}-\hat{\mathbf{v}}+\hat{\mathbf{v}}-\mathbf{v}^{*})

(33)

\displaystyle+(\mathbf{v}-\hat{\mathbf{v}})^{\top}(\mathbf{u-\dot{\hat{\mathbf{v}}}})+(\hat{\mathbf{v}}-\mathbf{v}^{*})^{\top}\frac{\dot{\hat{\mathbf{v}}}}{k_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-k_{1}\|\mathbf{v}-\hat{\mathbf{v}}\|^{2}\leq 0.

Based on the LaSalle’s invariance principle, each trajectory of the system asymptotically converges to the largest invariant set inside
\Omega=\{\dot{V}=0\}
, i.e.,
\mathbf{v}(t)=\hat{\mathbf{v}}(t)
. For
\mathbf{v}\equiv\hat{\mathbf{v}}
, we also have
\mathbf{u}\equiv\dot{\hat{\mathbf{v}}}
. From (
32
), it follows that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}\equiv\mathbf{0}_{d+1}
. This implies that
{\mathbf{g}}_{i}\equiv\mathbf{g}_{i}^{*}
and
\mathbf{p}(t)\equiv{\mathbf{p}}^{*}
. Thus,
\mathbf{p}(t)\to{\mathbf{p}}^{*}
and
\mathbf{v}(t)\to\mathbf{v}^{*}
as
t\to\infty
.

\blacksquare

4.2.2
The beacons move with the same uniformly bounded velocity

We next consider the case where the beacons move with unknown uniformly bounded velocity
\mathbf{v}^{*}(t)
. Since
\mathbf{v}^{*}
is assumed to be uniformly bounded, its derivative
\dot{\mathbf{v}}^{*}(t)
is also bounded, i.e.,
\|\dot{\mathbf{v}}^{*}(t)\|_{\infty}\leq\eta
. Suppose that the relative velocity
\mathbf{v}(t)-\mathbf{v}^{*}(t)
is measurable, the following bearing-based control law is introduced

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{q}}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}-\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{v}^{*},~\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{v}^{*},

(34)

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}-2(\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v}^{*})+\beta\text{sgn}(\mathbf{r}),

where
\beta>\eta
.

Theorem 9

Suppose that the relative velocity and the upper bound of the beacons’ velocity are available. If there is no collision, under the control law (
34
),
\mathbf{p}(t)\to{\mathbf{p}}^{*}(t)
, as
t\to+\infty
.

PROOF.

Consider the following Lyapunov function

V={\mathbf{z}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{W}}({\mathbf{g}}-{\mathbf{g}}^{*})+\frac{\|\mathbf{q}\|^{2}}{2}+\frac{\|\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{v}^{*}\|^{2}}{2},

we have

\displaystyle\dot{V}=

\displaystyle-(\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v}^{*})^{\top}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}+\mathbf{q}^{\top}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}-\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{v}^{*})

\displaystyle\qquad+(\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{v}^{*})^{\top}(\dot{\mathbf{q}}-\mathbf{u}+\dot{\mathbf{v}}^{*})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\|\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v}^{*}\|^{2}-\beta\|\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{v}^{*}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\qquad+(\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{v}^{*})^{\top}\dot{\mathbf{v}}^{*}(t)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\|\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v}^{*}\|^{2}-(\beta-\eta)\|\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{v}^{*}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 0.

(35)

Hence, it follows that
\mathbf{v}\rightarrow\mathbf{v}^{*}
and
\mathbf{q}\rightarrow\mathbf{0}
as
t\rightarrow\infty
. Since
V>0
and
\dot{V}\leq 0
,
V
is bounded, so do
\mathbf{q},\mathbf{v}
and
\mathbf{u}
. As there is no collision,
\dot{{\mathbf{g}}}_{i}=\frac{\mathbf{P}_{{\mathbf{g}}_{i}}}{\hat{d}_{i}}(\mathbf{v}^{*}-\mathbf{v})
is bounded. Thus, it can be seen that
\ddot{\mathbf{q}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}\dot{{\mathbf{g}}}_{i}-\mathbf{u}+\dot{\mathbf{v}}^{*}
is also bounded. According to the Barbalat’s lemma,
\dot{\mathbf{q}}\rightarrow\mathbf{0}
as
t\rightarrow\infty
. Combining with the first equation of (
34
),
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}\rightarrow 0
as
t\rightarrow\infty
. This completes the proof of Theorem
9
.
\blacksquare

6
Conclusion

This paper proposes novel bearing-only solutions for Fermat-Weber location problem.
Building on the existing algorithm with a single-integrator agent model and stationary beacons, we introduce additional results that include global exponential stability, robustness against measurement noise, and a finite-time algorithm.
Additionally, we consider the double-integrator agent model. For both types of dynamics, we study the algorithms with moving beacons, considering scenarios with both constant and time-varying velocities. Future work will focus on extending these results to handle nonlinear agent dynamics and negative weights in the objective function, i.e., attraction–repulsion problem.

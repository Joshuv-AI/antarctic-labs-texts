Association of Tirzepatide versus Glucagon-Like Peptide-1 Receptor Agonists with Incident Glaucoma in Patients with Type 2 Diabetes: A Retrospective Cohort Study
Authors: Ssu-Yu Pan, Chien-Hsiang Weng, Yi-Jing Sheen, Jun-Peng Chen, Hui-Ju Lin, I-Jong Wang, Chien-Chih Chou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo assess the comparative risk of glaucoma among patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) treated with tirzepatide versus glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1RAs).DesignA retrospective cohort study using data from a large, multinational, deidentified database.ParticipantsAdults aged ≥60 years with T2DM who were newly prescribed tirzepatide or GLP-1RAs during 2022–2024 across 21 countries were included. Individuals with a prior diagnosis of glaucoma, previous use of glaucoma medications, or cross-use of tirzepatide and GLP-1RAs during follow-up were excluded.MethodsBased on medication exposure, participants were divided into the tirzepatide and GLP-1RA groups. The 2 groups were balanced using 1:1 propensity score matching based on age, sex, race, smoking history, glycated hemoglobin, body mass index, prior use of glucose-lowering medications, corticosteroids, and comorbidities.Main Outcome MeasuresIncident primary open-angle glaucoma (POAG) across a 2-year follow-up period.ResultsA total of 51 540 participants were included in the final analysis. Tirzepatide, compared to GLP-1RAs, was associated with a lower risk of POAG after 2 years (hazard ratio, 0.65; 95% confidence interval, 0.44–0.96) of follow-up. This association remained consistent across multiple sensitivity analyses, including the exclusion of individuals with early POAG events after the index date, restricting the cohort to patients with ≥1 subsequent prescription during follow-up, an intention-to-treat design, and limiting the analysis exclusively to individuals with an ophthalmology visit.ConclusionsTirzepatide was associated with a lower risk of POAG compared to GLP-1RAs; however, further studies are needed to confirm its clinical significance given the limitations of the current study.Financial DisclosuresThe author has no/the authors have no proprietary or commercial interest in any materials discussed in this article.

Tirzepatide is a novel glucose-lowering medication that received US Food and Drug Administration approval for managing type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) on May 13, 2022, and it has been shown to outperform glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonists (RAs) in reducing glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c) and fasting blood glucose.1, 2, 3, 4 Prescriptions for tirzepatide among patients with T2DM increased from 0.2% in 2022 to 2.5% in 2024, indicating a growing trend in clinical use.5 Beyond its glucose-lowering effects, tirzepatide has been shown to provide greater reductions than GLP-1RAs in body weight, risks of cardiovascular disease, adverse renal outcomes, and all-cause mortality.6, 7, 8 However, tirzepatide’s role in ophthalmic diseases such as glaucoma has not yet been explored.

The superior effects of tirzepatide to GLP-1RAs may result from the synergetic effects of both GLP-1 and gastric inhibitory polypeptide (GIP) pathways. Glucagon-like peptide-1 and GIP are the 2 key incretin hormones in the body, which stimulate insulin secretion, delay gastric emptying, and suppress appetite.1 By mimicking the action of incretins, tirzepatide lowers blood glucose level, promotes weight loss, and reduces complications associated with T2DM and obesity. The SURPASS clinical trial program was the first to demonstrate the metabolic benefits of tirzepatide. In A Study of Tirzepatide (LY3298176) in Participants With Type 2 Diabetes Not Controlled With Diet and Exercise Alone (SURPASS-1), a 40-week randomized controlled trial involving 478 patients with T2DM who were naïve to diabetes injection therapy, tirzepatide reduced HbA1c by 1.87%–2.07% and body weight by 7.0–9.5 kg, while placebo increased HbA1c by 0.04% and reduced body weight by 0.7 kg.2 In A Study of Tirzepatide (LY3298176) Versus Semaglutide Once Weekly as Add-on Therapy to Metformin in Participants With Type 2 Diabetes (SURPASS-2), which included 1879 patients with T2DM, all doses of tirzepatide achieved greater reductions in HbA1c (2.01%–2.30% vs. 1.86%) and body weight (7.6–11.2 kg vs. 5.7 kg) than semaglutide.3 These findings highlight tirzepatide’s potential to surpass GLP-1RAs not only in glycemic control but also in weight reduction, which are 2 factors intricately linked to the pathophysiology of glaucoma.9,10

Given the pleiotropic effects of incretin-based therapies, there is growing interest in their potential ocular benefits. Before the introduction of tirzepatide, GLP-1RAs and dipeptidyl peptidase-4 (DPP-4) inhibitors were the 2 main incretin-based therapies. Glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists have been shown to reduce glaucoma development, potentially through lowering intraocular pressure and exerting neuroprotective effects.11, 12, 13 Dipeptidyl peptidase-4 inhibitors were also associated with a lower incidence of glaucoma in patients with T2DM.14 By blocking the DPP-4-mediated degradation of endogenous GIP and GLP-1, these agents prolong and potentiate dual-incretin signaling, a mechanism that may underlie their observed protective effect against glaucoma.

We hypothesize that tirzepatide may be associated with ocular influences through the dual GIP and GLP-1 activation beyond that observed with GLP-1RAs. To test this hypothesis, we conducted a retrospective cohort study to compare the risk of glaucoma in patients with T2DM treated with tirzepatide versus those treated with GLP-1RAs. This study aimed to provide a novel insight into whether tirzepatide may reduce the risk of glaucoma and to offer implications for future investigations.

Methods

Data Source

This retrospective cohort study analyzed deidentified electronic health records from the TriNetX Global Collaborative Network, which aggregates data from >200 million patients across 160 health care institutions in 21 countries.15 TriNetX offers comprehensive clinical information, including demographics, diagnoses, medications, procedures, and laboratory results, documented using the International Classification of Diseases, 10th Revision (ICD-10), Clinical Modification (ICD-10-CM), Anatomical Therapeutic Chemical (ATC) classification, Current Procedural Terminology codes, or other diagnostic coding systems. International Classification of Diseases, Ninth Revision codes were automatically mapped to ICD-10 within the platform. All analyses were performed within the secure TriNetX platform, and summary data were subsequently exported for interpretation. To protect patient privacy in accordance with the US Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act, individual records were inaccessible to users, and exact counts for outcomes involving ≤10 patients were masked and reported as “10” instead.

Study Population

We applied the new-user design. Individuals aged ≥60 years with T2DM who were new users of tirzepatide or GLP-1RAs were included. Enrollment began on May 13, 2022, the date tirzepatide received US Food and Drug Administration approval, as GLP-1RAs were approved earlier. The date on which participants were prescribed tirzepatide or GLP-1RAs for the first time during the study period was assigned as the index date, marking the start of follow-up.

The inclusion criteria for the participants were as follows: (1) patients aged ≥60 years on the index date; (2) patients coded with a diagnosis of T2DM (ICD-10-CM E11) prior to the index date; (3) patients who were prescribed tirzepatide or GLP-1RA (exenatide, lixisenatide, semaglutide, dulaglutide, liraglutide, and albiglutide) for the first time between May 13, 2022 and December 31, 2024, with the prescription date defined as the index date; (4) individuals who had an ongoing prescription for the medication within 1 year after the index date; and (5) individuals with available HbA1c data within 1 year prior to the index date.

The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) patients coded with a diagnosis of any subtypes of glaucoma (ICD-10-CM H40–H42) prior to the index date; (2) patients with any documented use of glaucoma medications (ATC S01E), tirzepatide, or GLP-1RAs prior to the index date; (3) patients who had no available HbA1c data within 1 year prior to the index date; (4) patients in the tirzepatide group who were concurrently prescribed GLP-1RAs during the study period; and (5) patients in the GLP-1RA group who were concurrently prescribed tirzepatide within the study period.

Outcome Measures

The primary outcome of interest was incident primary open-angle glaucoma (POAG), identified by ICD-10-CM code H40.11. We also analyzed negative control outcomes, including hip fracture, osteoarthritis, and acute appendicitis, to assess potential residual confounding or statistical noise. Participants were censored until the occurrence of outcome, the end of the study period, lost to follow-up (defined as the date of their last recorded clinical event within the database), or 2 years after the index date, whichever came first.

Statistical Analysis

A 1:1 propensity score matching was performed between the tirzepatide and GLP-1RA groups using a greedy nearest-neighbor algorithm with a caliper of 0.1 pooled standard deviations. A standardized mean difference <0.1 was considered indicative of minimal group differences and good covariate balance. Matching covariates included age at index date, sex (female), race (Asian, Black or African American, and White), smoking history, the most recent HbA1c level and body mass index value prior to index, comorbidities (hypertension, ischemic heart disease, cerebrovascular disease, heart failure, diabetic retinopathy, dyslipidemia, obstructive sleep apnea, and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease), and medications (metformin, sulfonylureas, thiazolidinediones, sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitors, DPP-4 inhibitors, insulin, and corticosteroids). Smoking history, HbA1c, body mass index, comorbidities, and medications documented within the preceding year were included in the matching process. The corresponding ICD-10-CM, ATC, and other coding systems used to identify diagnoses and prescriptions are detailed in Table S1 (available at www.ophthalmologyscience.org).

All analyses were conducted in March 2026 using the TriNetX platform. A matched Cox proportional hazards model was applied to assess the risk of POAG between the tirzepatide and GLP-1RA groups in 2 years of follow-up, with results reported as hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs).16 Kaplan–Meier analysis was used to evaluate cumulative incidence of POAG over the 2-year follow-up.

We conducted several sensitivity analyses to evaluate the robustness of our findings. First, we excluded individuals diagnosed with POAG within 1, 3, 6, 9, or 12 months after the index date to reduce reverse causation bias. Second, to minimize the risk of misclassification, we restricted the analysis to patients who received ≥1 subsequent prescription for the exposure drug (tirzepatide or GLP-1RAs) between 1 and 2 years after the index date. Third, we applied an intention-to-treat approach to reduce immortal time bias. In this setting, we included individuals who were new users of tirzepatide or GLP-1RAs between May 13, 2022, and December 31, 2024, had no prior diagnosis of glaucoma (ICD-10-CM H40–H42), no prior use of glaucoma medications (ATC S01E), and had HbA1c data available within 1 year before the prescription date. Fourth, we performed a sensitivity analysis restricted to individuals with a documented ophthalmology visit at baseline or during follow-up, defined by the presence of a Current Procedural Terminology 1012793 (ophthalmology services and procedures) record between 1 year preceding the index date and the end of follow-up.

Statistical analyses were performed using multiple software tools and libraries, including Java 11.0.16 with Apache Commons Math 3.6.1, R version 4.0.2 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing) utilizing the Hmisc (1.1) and Survival (3.2-3) packages, as well as Python 3.7 (Python Software Foundation) with lifelines (0.22.4), matplotlib (3.5.1), numpy (1.21.5), pandas (1.3.5), scipy (1.7.3), and statsmodels (0.13.2). Additional technical details remain proprietary and are safeguarded as trade secrets by TriNetX.

The Institutional Review Board of Taichung Veterans General Hospital approved our study protocol for retrospective data analysis using the TriNetX Analytics Network under the registration number CE24430C-1. As TriNetX operates as a federated network handling only deidentified patient data and prohibits direct access to individual patient medical records, informed consent was waived. This study was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Glaucoma Risk between Tirzepatide and GLP-1RAs

Among the 25 770 patients treated with tirzepatide, 41 patients (0.16%) developed POAG within 2 years. Among the 25 770 patients treated with GLP-1RAs, 69 (0.27%) developed POAG within 2 years. Compared with those treated with GLP-1RAs, individuals receiving tirzepatide experienced a lower risk of POAG over 2 years of follow-up (HR, 0.65; 95% CI, 0.44–0.96).

Compared with GLP-1RA users, tirzepatide users experienced a significantly lower cumulative incidence of POAG over the 2-year follow-up. The 2-year cumulative incidence was 0.21% (95% CI, 0.15%–0.29%) in the tirzepatide group and 0.31% (95% CI, 0.25%–0.40%) in the GLP-1RA group, with a log-rank P value of 0.0286 (Fig 2).Figure 2Cumulative incidence for patients prescribed tirzepatide and GLP-1RAs. There were no significant differences in the risk of negative control outcomes between the tirzepatide and GLP-1RA groups, including hip fracture (HR, 0.89; 95% CI, 0.72–1.10), osteoarthritis (HR, 0.99; 95% CI, 0.96–1.02), and acute appendicitis (HR, 1.09; 95% CI, 0.64–1.83).

Sensitivity Analysis

Each sensitivity analysis demonstrated comparable baseline characteristics between the tirzepatide and GLP-1RA groups (Table S2, available at www.ophthalmologyscience.org). Additionally, the reduced risk observed among tirzepatide users compared with GLP-1RA users remained consistent when the analysis was restricted to individuals who received ≥1 subsequent prescription within 1 year and another prescription between 1 and 2 years (HR, 0.59; 95% CI, 0.37–0.94). Using the intention-to-treat approach, tirzepatide was still associated with a reduced risk of POAG compared to GLP-1RA (HR, 0.72; 95 CI, 0.53–0.98). Among individuals who had ≥1 documented ophthalmology visit at baseline or during follow-up, those prescribed tirzepatide showed a lower risk of POAG compared with those prescribed GLP-1RAs (HR, 0.50; 95% CI, 0.26–0.96).

Discussion

This retrospective cohort study of 51 540 individuals with T2DM found that patients treated with tirzepatide, a novel glucose-lowering medication just approved by the US Food and Drug Administration in 2022, had a lower risk of developing POAG over a 2-year follow-up period compared to those treated with GLP-1RAs. This association remained consistent after excluding individuals who developed POAG within the first year of medication initiation. Additional sensitivity analyses also yielded results consistent with the main findings. However, given the novelty of tirzepatide, longer follow-up is not yet feasible, and the limited number of POAG cases in our study warrants further investigation to confirm the clinical significance of the observed association.

In recent years, emerging studies have suggested that incretin-based glucose-lowering medications, including GLP-1RAs or DPP-4 inhibitors, may lower glaucoma risk.11,12,14,17 Several observational studies have shown that GLP-1RA users experienced lower incidence of glaucoma relative to nonusers.11, 12, 13 A meta-analysis encompassing 5 studies and a total of 156 042 participants further corroborated these findings, revealing a significant reduction in glaucoma incidence among GLP-1RA users relative to controls.18 Additionally, a study demonstrated that DPP-4 inhibitor users were associated with reduced risk of glaucoma compared with nonusers.14 Given that DPP-4 inhibitors slow the degradation of both GIP and GLP-1, this finding suggested that combined GLP-1 and GIP activity may be important for reduction of glaucoma risk. However, despite sharing similar mechanisms of action with GLP-1RAs and DPP-4 inhibitors, tirzepatide has not yet been specifically evaluated in clinical studies for its potential association with glaucoma.

Tirzepatide is an acylated peptide and a dual agonist that targets both GIP and GLP-1 receptors on pancreatic β-cells.1 Gastric inhibitory polypeptide and GLP-1 are both incretins that promotes satiety and enhances insulin secretion by stimulating β-cell proliferation and inhibiting β-cell apoptosis.1 The efficacy of tirzepatide was first demonstrated in the SURPASS clinical trials, which showed that tirzepatide led to greater reductions in HbA1c compared to placebo, semaglutide, insulin degludec, and insulin glargine.2,3,19, 20, 21 Further observational studies also revealed consistent findings.22,23 Emerging research has shown that tirzepatide also contributes to reductions in body weight, major adverse cardiovascular events, heart failure, metabolic syndrome, adverse renal outcomes, and all-cause mortality.6,24, 25, 26 Our study further highlights the potential pleiotropic effects of tirzepatide by demonstrating its association with a reduced glaucoma risk.

Tirzepatide’s association with reduction in glaucoma risk compared to GLP-1RAs may be attributed to the additive effects of GIP in combination with GLP-1, as animal studies have shown that the receptors of GIP and GLP-1 are both expressed in the retina.27, 28, 29, 30 Notably, their expression levels were upregulated in diabetic rats compared with nondiabetic controls, suggesting a potential interaction between GIP signaling and retinal physiology in the context of diabetes.27 Therefore, tirzepatide may influence the pathophysiology of glaucoma through its action on GIP receptors expressed on retinal ganglion cells, but further studies are needed to confirm this hypothesis. Aside from its GIP-related effects, the GLP-1 receptor activity of tirzepatide may also contribute to a reduced risk of glaucoma. Glucagon-like peptide-1 receptors are expressed in the retina,29,30 and studies have shown that GLP-1 activation reduces neurodegeneration process, lowers intraocular pressure, and exerts anti-inflammatory effects.31, 32, 33

Strengths and Limitations

This study offers several notable strengths. First, the use of a large database provided better external validity of our results across varied populations. Second, instead of comparing tirzepatide users to placebo, we specifically selected GLP-1RA users as the comparison group. This approach minimized heterogeneity within the control group and allowed for a more accurate assessment of tirzepatide’s potential advantages over GLP-1RAs, a drug class with well-documented metabolic and cardiovascular therapeutic benefits.34 Finally, our findings were supported by multiple sensitivity analyses, all of which consistently aligned with the main results.

This study also has several limitations. First, the use of deidentified data from the TriNetX network prevents direct confirmation of disease diagnoses or medication prescription through chart review, as well as verification of intraocular pressure, visual field, and OCT findings, which may potentially result in misclassification bias. Additionally, differences in coding practices across countries and regions may have resulted in incomplete capture of POAG events or other comorbidities. Second, because drug exposure was determined based on prescription records, treatment adherence could not be ensured. Third, because tirzepatide was approved in 2022, long-term follow-up was not feasible. Therefore, future studies with longer follow-up durations are needed to confirm the long-term effects of tirzepatide. Fourth, the use of newer and higher-cost medications such as tirzepatide may be more common among patients with higher socioeconomic status, better access to care, and greater engagement in health-seeking behaviors. Such potential sources of bias cannot be completely excluded in observational studies, and thus randomized controlled trials are needed to further confirm these findings. Finally, while corticosteroid use was incorporated into the propensity score matching, the TriNetX platform precludes independent matching for systemic and topical formulations. Given that topical steroids exert a more proximal impact on aqueous humor dynamics and intraocular pressure, future investigations utilizing data sets that allow for this distinction are warranted to validate our findings.

Conclusion

Tirzepatide was associated with a lower risk of POAG compared with GLP-1RAs in adults aged ≥60 years with T2DM. Future studies with follow-up exceeding 2 years are needed to confirm and better characterize this association.

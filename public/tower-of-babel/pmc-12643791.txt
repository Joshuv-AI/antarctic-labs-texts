Metastatic Chondroblastic Osteosarcoma Involving the Thoracic Aorta Managed with Thoracic Endovascular Aortic Repair: A Case Report and Tribute
Authors: Karthigesu Aimanan, Muthukkumaran Thiagarajan, Sorracha Rookkapan, Boonprasit Kritpracha, Hanif Hussein
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Chondroblastic osteosarcoma is a rare and aggressive subtype of osteosarcoma, and thoracic aortic involvement is exceptionally uncommon. We report a 57-year-old male with multifocal metastatic chondroblastic osteosarcoma presenting as a mediastinal mass encasing the descending thoracic aorta. He presented with painful thigh swellings, fatigue, and weight loss for three weeks. His history included right orchidectomy and chemotherapy for a germ cell tumor 30 years earlier, with remission. Biopsy of the thigh lesions confirmed metastatic chondroblastic osteosarcoma. Staging computed tomography revealed an unresectable mediastinal mass encasing the descending thoracic aorta, with intraluminal thrombus causing near-total occlusion. Positron emission tomography-computed tomography demonstrated avidity involving the mediastinal mass and partial uptake in the thrombus. Thoracic endovascular aortic repair relieved the stenosis and enabled systemic therapy. Despite systemic and palliative therapies, the patient succumbed to progressive disease 13 months after diagnosis. This case highlights the role of endovascular repair in malignant aortic involvement.

INTRODUCTION

Chondroblastic osteosarcoma is a cartilage-producing variant of conventional osteosarcoma, most frequently seen in adolescents and young adults [1]. Its occurrence in older adults is rare and is often associated with underlying conditions such as Paget’s disease or prior malignancy. Metastatic dissemination to visceral organs and vascular structures, particularly the thoracic aorta, is exceptionally uncommon [2].

From a vascular perspective, direct tumor invasion or encasement of the aorta presents unique and complex management dilemmas. Endovascular repair in this context is unconventional and is generally pursued for palliation rather than curative intent.

We present a rare case of metastatic chondroblastic osteosarcoma in an adult with direct involvement of the descending thoracic aorta, managed with thoracic endovascular aortic repair (TEVAR). This report highlights the clinical complexities of oncovascular intervention in malignant aortic disease and the potential role of TEVAR as a palliative strategy in selected patients. This case report was approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of National Medical Research Register Malaysia (IRB No. RSCH ID-25-02770-XUF). Consent for publication of this case report and accompanying images was obtained from the patient’s next of kin.

CASE

A 57-year-old male presented with a three-week history of enlarging, painful thigh swellings and progressive difficulty in ambulation. He reported profound fatigue and weight loss. His history included orchidectomy and bleomycin-etoposide-cisplatin chemotherapy for a germ cell tumor 30 years prior. On examination, he appeared cachectic but was hemodynamically stable. A firm, non-mobile, tender mass approximately 7 cm in diameter was palpable in the right anterior thigh, with a smaller, similar mass in the left posterior thigh. Both lesions were non-fluctuant with intact overlying skin. Femoral pulses were reduced bilaterally, with ankle brachial indices (ABIs) of 0.3 on the right and 0.4 on the left. Neurological examination of the lower limbs was unremarkable and no other systemic abnormalities or lymphadenopathy were detected.

Magnetic resonance imaging of the lower limbs revealed large, infiltrative soft tissue masses with radiologic features consistent with a high-grade sarcoma. Whole-body positron emission tomography-computed tomography (PET-CT) demonstrated extensive metabolically active disease involving both thighs, the omentum, and a periaortic mediastinal lesion encasing the descending thoracic aorta (Fig. 1, 2). Staging CT angiography identified a 2.0×4.5×3.8 cm eccentric soft tissue mass extending from T7 to T9, encasing approximately two-thirds of the aortic circumference with focal intraluminal protrusion. Distal to this, a 2.2×2.0×4.7 cm intraluminal thrombus was noted, causing near-total occlusion of the aortic lumen (Fig. 3). The periaortic mass abutted the left atrium, pulmonary arteries, and pleural surfaces without evidence of osseous erosion. Importantly, PET-CT confirmed fluorodeoxyglucose avidity in the mediastinal mass and in part of the intraluminal thrombus, consistent with a mixture of tumor thrombus and bland thrombus.

Histopathologic examination of the right anterior thigh lesion demonstrated nodular tumors composed of chondroblastic lobules with cytologic atypia, binucleated chondrocytes, and interspersed spindle cells producing lace-like osteoid. The tumor infiltrated skeletal muscle, adipose tissue, and dermal collagen, with evidence of vascular invasion. Immunohistochemistry revealed SATB2 positivity in osteoid-producing cells and focal S100 positivity in chondroblastic areas, while staining for PanCK, Desmin, SMA, and SOX10 was negative, excluding epithelial, myogenic, and neural crest differentiation. These histologic and immunohistochemical features confirmed the diagnosis of metastatic chondroblastic osteosarcoma.

Shortly thereafter, the patient developed acute pulmonary edema and was admitted to the coronary care unit. Coronary angiography revealed mild triple-vessel disease and he was managed conservatively. The case was subsequently reviewed by a multidisciplinary tumor board involving vascular surgery, cardiothoracic surgery, oncology, orthopedics, cardiology, and anesthesiology experts. The thoracic tumor was deemed unresectable. Given the hemodynamic risk of near-total aortic occlusion, the potential for critical distal ischemia, and the risk of thromboembolism, TEVAR was recommended as a palliative strategy to preserve distal perfusion and enable initiation of systemic therapy.

Through right femoral percutaneous access, a 28×28×150 mm Valiant Captivia (Medtronic) stent was deployed across the diseased segment of the thoracic aorta. Crossing the near-occlusive thrombus required careful guidewire manipulation. A SpiderFX distal embolic protection device (Medtronic) was placed to reduce embolic risk. No pre- or post-ballooning was performed. Pulses improved immediately, with significant improvement in ABI values bilaterally. No intraoperative complications occurred.

Postoperatively, the patient received imatinib (a tyrosine-kinase inhibitor). One month later, surveillance imaging demonstrated a stable periaortic mass, a patent stent graft, and no evidence of endoleak (Fig. 4). As the disease progressed, dendritic cell immunotherapy was initiated. He subsequently developed bowel obstruction requiring surgical resection. Despite these interventions, he succumbed to progressive metastases 13 months after diagnosis.

DISCUSSION

Chondroblastic osteosarcoma is a rare histological subtype of osteosarcoma, and its occurrence in older adults is particularly unusual [1]. Metastatic disease most commonly involves the lungs and visceral organs, while direct invasion or encasement of the thoracic aorta is exceptionally uncommon [3]. To our knowledge, reports of metastatic osteosarcoma with direct aortic involvement and intraluminal thrombus are exceedingly rare, with only isolated references to other malignancies invading the thoracic aorta.

In this case, the patient presented with systemic manifestations of metastatic disease and evidence of compromised distal perfusion due to tumor encasement and thrombus formation. While open surgical resection or bypass has been described in malignant aortic involvement, such options were not feasible given the extent of disease and comorbidities. The primary aim of intervention was therefore palliative: to preserve distal perfusion, reduce the risk of embolization, and enable systemic therapy. This approach reflects the principle of oncovascular intervention, where vascular procedures are performed to facilitate or support cancer treatment rather than achieve cure [4].

The technical aspects of TEVAR in this context deserve emphasis. The near-occlusive thrombus required careful guidewire passage, and a distal embolic protection device was deployed off-label to reduce embolic risk during stent deployment, a method previously described in selective TEVAR cases [5]. No ballooning was performed to avoid thrombus dislodgement. Objective hemodynamic benefit was documented with normalization of ABI, underscoring the functional success of the procedure.

Imatinib was administered as an off-label systemic therapy, extrapolated from sarcomas with PDGFR or KIT expression. However, in osteosarcoma, its role remains unproven without molecular confirmation [6]. Additional debulking and dendritic cell immunotherapy were performed with palliative intent, nevertheless, the disease progressed and the patient ultimately succumbed 13 months after diagnosis. Importantly, TEVAR allowed systemic therapy to proceed and contributed to an extended survival compared with the expected rapid decline from vascular compromise.

We found no prior reports of chondroblastic osteosarcoma involving the thoracic aorta or managed with TEVAR. However, malignant thoracic aortic involvement from other tumors has been managed with TEVAR for palliation, including relief of stenosis or occlusion, control of bleeding, or prevention of rupture (Table 1). Reported cases include mediastinal undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma invading the aorta, aorto-esophageal fistula from esophageal cancer, lymphoma with impending rupture, and primary aortic angiosarcoma. Collectively, these cases support TEVAR as a feasible palliative strategy in selected oncovascular scenarios [7-11].

In summary, metastatic chondroblastic osteosarcoma with thoracic aortic involvement is exceptionally rare. This case demonstrates that TEVAR can be considered as a palliative strategy to maintain distal perfusion and enable systemic therapy in advanced oncovascular disease. Careful technical planning, including avoidance of ballooning and selective use of embolic protection, is essential to optimize safety. Although systemic therapies ultimately determined survival, TEVAR provided meaningful palliation and contributed to prolonged functional stability in this patient.

Is male sex a barrier to transoral endoscopic thyroidectomy? A comparative study of surgical outcomes
Authors: Nai-Yu Wang, Yu-Chia Chen, Shiuh-Inn Liu, I-Shu Chen, Tsung-Jung Liang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeTransoral endoscopic thyroidectomy vestibular approach (TOETVA) presents technical challenges in male patients because of their anatomical characteristics. However, the impact of sex on clinical outcomes following TOETVA remains unclear.MethodsThis retrospective study analyzed the baseline characteristics and clinicopathological outcomes of male and female patients who underwent TOETVA between 2019 and 2023. Factors associated with prolonged operative time (above the 75th percentile) were examined using a multivariable logistic regression analysis.ResultsOf 131 patients who underwent unilateral lobectomy via the transoral approach, 20 (15.3%) were male. Male patients had significantly higher drainage volumes (75.7 mL vs. 59.3 mL, P = 0.045) and shorter postoperative stays (2.5 days vs. 2.8 days, P = 0.016) compared to females. Ecchymosis lasting >1 week was also more frequent in males (20.0% vs. 5.4%, P = 0.044), and 1 male patient (5.0%, 1/20) experienced chin flap perforation during workspace creation. Other perioperative outcomes, including operative time, blood loss, thyroid gland weight, and pain scores, were comparable between sexes. The rates of vocal cord palsy and incidental parathyroidectomy were also similar. Notably, logistic regression analysis revealed that thyroid gland weight was the sole independent predictor of prolonged operative time (odds ratio, 1.11; 95% confidence interval, 1.03–1.21; P = 0.008), while sex was not associated.ConclusionTOETVA is feasible in male patients with outcomes generally comparable to females. Surgeons should exercise caution when operating on male patients to minimize soft tissue trauma. Thyroid weight, rather than sex, is the main determinant of operative duration.Keywords: Natural orifice endoscopic surgery, Male, Sex factors, Thyroidectomy

INTRODUCTION

Since Dr. Anuwong first published a large human series on transoral endoscopic thyroidectomy vestibular approach (TOETVA) in Thailand, this innovative technique has quickly attracted global attention, and its surgical applications have broadened significantly [1,2,3,4,5]. Unlike other remote-access techniques such as the transaxillary or bilateral axillo-breast approach (BABA), which are mainly used in Asia, TOETVA has been successfully adapted to the anatomical differences between Asian and Western patients, leading to its worldwide adoption [2,6,7]. Large-scale data from North American and European studies have confirmed the feasibility and safety of this approach [8,9].

Despite TOETVA’s broad application across different ethnic groups, concerns about sex-specific anatomical differences raise questions about its suitability for male patients [10,11]. Men typically have larger, square-shaped mandibles and more robust subcutaneous tissues, making it challenging to create a surgical tunnel from the oral vestibular wound through the angular jawline to the neck [12,13]. Additionally, the prominent thyroid cartilage in men may hinder surgery by obstructing the instruments’ ability to cross the midline, affecting both dissection and traction, and compromising visibility. These anatomical considerations could negatively influence surgical outcomes and lead to the perception that TOETVA might be less suitable for male patients, particularly when performed by less experienced surgeons [10]. However, the actual effects of such sex-based anatomical differences on TOETVA outcomes have not been comprehensively explored in existing literature.

This study aimed to assess the impact of male sex on surgical outcomes following TOETVA by comparing perioperative variables between male and female patients to enhance the understanding of TOETVA’s applicability across diverse populations.

METHODS

Ethics statement

This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Kaohsiung Veterans General Hospital (No. KSVGH25-CT7-04). The requirement for informed consent for the retrospective chart review was waived by the Institutional Review Board. Written informed consent for the publication of the clinical photographs was obtained from the patients concerned.

Patients

This retrospective chart review analyzed patients who underwent TOETVA for benign and malignant thyroid diseases at the Kaohsiung Veterans General Hospital between January 2019 and December 2023. Because of the limited number of male patients who underwent total thyroidectomy (n = 1), only those who underwent unilateral lobectomy were included for comparison. Repeat procedures, such as completion thyroidectomy, were excluded. All surgeries were performed by a single, experienced, right-handed endocrine surgeon (TJL) using the TOETVA technique described in previous publications [14,15]. A closed suction drain was inserted through a small incision in the lower neck and removed once a decreasing trend in the drainage volume was observed. Intraoperative neuromonitoring was consistently employed.

Outcome assessment

Patients were categorized according to sex for the analysis. Perioperative variables including operative time, blood loss, total drainage amount over 2 days, weight of the resected thyroid gland, number of inadvertently resected parathyroid glands, pain score, length of postoperative stay, pathological diagnosis, and complications were collected and compared. Additional metrics, such as the incidence of ecchymosis lasting more than 1 week and prolonged sensory impairment (>3 months) affecting the area innervated by the mental nerve, were also documented.

To evaluate surgical difficulty, a longer operative time, defined as that exceeding the 75th percentile of operative time of all enrolled patients, was used as a surrogate indicator. Factors contributing to prolonged operative time were further explored.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation and analyzed using the 2-sample t-test. Categorical variables were reported as percentages and compared using the chi-square or Fisher exact test, as applicable. A multivariable logistic regression model was used to identify the factors associated with extended operative times. Statistical significance was set at a p-value of less than 0.05. Data analysis was conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics (ver. 20.0, IBM Corp.).

RESULTS

Patient characteristics

A total of 131 patients (20 male patients [15.3%] and 111 female patients [84.7%]) underwent unilateral lobectomy via the transoral approach (Table 1). Age, body mass index (BMI), tumor size, tumor laterality, and distribution of Bethesda categories were similar between the sexes. Although the incidence of thyroiditis was 3 times higher in female patients than in male patients, the difference was not statistically significant (29.7% vs. 10.0%, P = 0.066).

Pathological outcomes

A pathological analysis revealed a higher percentage of malignant diagnoses in male patients (50.0%) than in female patients (37.8%), although this difference was not statistically significant (P = 0.082) (Table 3). Among patients diagnosed with cancer, 82.7% had T1 lesions (T1a + T1b). Three patients had T3 lesions, including 2 with gross extrathyroidal extension (T3b) and 1 with a preoperative 4.4 cm Bethesda II nodule diagnosed as follicular carcinoma (T3a). Despite the recommendations for completion thyroidectomy, these patients chose to undergo clinical follow-up. Central neck lymph node metastasis occurred in 3 male (30.0%) and 6 female patients (14.3%), with the number of retrieved lymph nodes being similar between the sexes. Four of the 9 node-positive patients underwent completion thyroidectomy and adjuvant radioactive iodine treatment. No recurrences or metastases were noted during a mean follow-up period of 37.0 ± 17.9 months.

Factors associated with longer operative time

Thirty-one patients (6 males and 25 females) had operative times exceeding the 75th percentile (225 minutes). One-third of these patients (10/31, 32.3%) had BMIs >25 kg/m2. Multivariable logistic regression analysis confirmed that the weight of the resected thyroid gland was the only significant predictor of longer operative time (odds ratio, 1.11; 95% confidence interval, 1.03–1.21; P = 0.008) (Table 4). Other factors, including male sex and BMI, were not significantly associated with prolonged operative time.

DISCUSSION

This study aimed to assess the impact of male sex on surgical outcomes following TOETVA. Our results indicated that male patients can undergo TOETVA with operative time, blood loss, and pain scores comparable to those of female patients. Furthermore, the rates of common complications, such as vocal cord palsy and inadvertent resection of the parathyroid glands, did not differ significantly between the sexes. However, male patients experienced more postoperative ecchymosis and exhibited greater drainage volumes, suggesting increased tissue manipulation during surgery. To our knowledge, this is the first study to offer a comprehensive comparison of TOETVA outcomes between male and female patients.

Kwak et al. [16] found that male sex was a predictor of longer operative times in robotic BABA thyroidectomy owing to anatomical considerations such as flatter chests, scarce breast tissue, and more prominent clavicles, which complicate the placement of breast/axillary ports and movement of the robotic arm during surgery. Similarly, in TOETVA, features such as a more angular masculine chin (Fig. 2) and larger laryngeal prominence (Figs. 3, 4) could potentially interfere with port placement and instrumentation, paralleling the challenges encountered in robotic BABA thyroidectomy [10,13].

Despite these challenges, operative times for TOETVA were comparable between male and female patients. Multivariable analysis identified thyroid gland weight—not sex or BMI—as the only independent predictor of prolonged operative time, highlighting gland weight as a more critical factor in surgical difficulty than patient sex. This finding aligns with that of Tanavde et al. [17], who associated a larger thyroid lobe size with a longer operative duration. Therefore, anatomical differences may have a lesser impact on TOETVA than on robotic BABA, highlighting TOETVA’s adaptability to various patient anatomies.

In this study, ecchymosis was observed more in male patients who typically had larger and more muscular mandibles. The inserted trocar, surrounded by robust tissue, is difficult to maneuver. Surgeons may unintentionally apply more force to overcome the limited range of motion, resulting in increased soft tissue damage and bruising. This robust tissue also contributed to the reported case of chin flap perforation, which occurred when excessive force was applied to separate the tight premandibular tissue, leading to unintentional penetration of the skin with a Kelly clamp.

Similarly, additional force is often required to displace the laryngeal prominence to gain proper access for contralateral dissection. Such forceful movements can lead to tissue damage and increased exudation, which is evacuated using a closed suction drain. To minimize interference from the laryngeal prominence and reduce tissue trauma, surgeons should limit the use of instruments that cross the midline. Skilled surgeons can effectively manage dissection with both their dominant and non-dominant hands by using the instrument on the lesion side for the primary task of delicate dissection and employing the contralateral side instrument for simpler tasks, such as traction or merely holding the thyroid. This strategy minimizes unnecessary tissue manipulation and subsequent complications.

Yu et al. [18] classified the mandibular jawlines in patients undergoing TOETVA into 3 types based on the angle of chin projection. Patients with the most protruding lower jaw (type C) comprised a higher percentage of men (37.3%) than those with the other 2 types (type A and B both had 26.3% males) [18]. Type C patients had longer jawlines, making it difficult to maintain the working space [18]. Nonetheless, TOETVA can be successfully performed across all jawline types with a similar complication rate, effectiveness in lymph node clearance, and comparable hospital stays [18]. However, patients with type C jawlines experienced significantly more mandibular swelling (P < 0.01) and reported higher pain scores (P < 0.01) than those with type A or B jawlines [18].

Interestingly, despite potentially greater tissue damage, male patients in this study did not report higher pain scores than female patients. Previous research has underscored significant sex differences in the perception of postoperative pain, which is influenced by both physical and psychosocial factors [19,20]. In Taiwan, societal norms often discourage men from expressing weakness or discomfort; rather, they promote quick recovery and return to work. These cultural expectations may explain the slightly shorter postoperative hospital stays in male patients compared to those in female patients; however, the statistical difference of just 0.3 days (2.5 days vs. 2.8 days, P = 0.016) might not hold clinical relevance.

The present study had 2 limitations. First, it included only patients who underwent unilateral lobectomy, indicating that outcomes specific to total thyroidectomy, such as hypoparathyroidism, were not evaluated. Instead, the number of inadvertently removed parathyroid glands serves as an alternative indicator of surgical quality. Second, the results reflect the practice of a single surgeon, which potentially limits the generalizability of the findings. Further studies involving multiple surgeons are required to validate these findings more broadly. In contrast, having a single surgeon perform all procedures ensures consistency and reliability in the data presented in this study.

In conclusion, male patients can undergo TOETVA with outcomes comparable to those in female patients. However, surgeons should exercise caution while managing skin flaps and adjusting the force applied during tissue manipulation in male patients.

Abstract
The origin of the small asymmetry in the abundance of matter and antimatter in the very early universe, described by the Baryon Asymmetry Factor η = 8.65 × 10 − 11 \eta=8.65\times 10^{-11} , is one of the big unanswered questions of contemporary physics. Any explanation will involve both particle physics and gravity, requiring baryon-number non-conserving particle interactions and thermodynamical processes generated by the primordial gravitational field. The present study investigates the generation of a baryon-antibaryon asymmetry via gravitational baryogenesis, that is, by a coupling between the baryon current and the gradient of the Ricci scalar R R . For an expanding spatially uniform homogeneous universe the interaction is proportional to ( n B − n B ¯ ) ​ ∂ t R (n_{\mathrm{B}}-n_{\bar{\mathrm{B}}})\partial_{t}R and, as ∂ t R \partial_{t}R is zero in General Relativity for a radiation dominated flat early Universe, a more generalized theory of gravity is required. Two f ⁡ ( R ) f(R) modified theories of gravity are examined, the widely used Starobinsky f ⁡ ( R ) = R + R 2 / M 2 f(R)=R+R^{2}/M^{2} model, and the recently proposed power-law model f ⁡ ( R ) = c 1 ​ R 2 + k / 4 + c 2 ​ R + c 3 f(R)=c_{1}R^{2+k/4}+c_{2}R+c_{3} of Odintsov and Oikonomou (2025) that is constructed from the slow-roll inflation parameters, and fits the new high-multipole CMB observations reported by the Planck and ACT collaborations for k ∼ − 0.03 k\sim-0.03 . Rather than use the Jordan frame in which these cosmologies have been formulated, the present investigation is undertaken in the Einstein frame, which is obtained from the Jordan frame by a conformal transformation Ω \Omega . This introduces the scalaron field ϕ \phi via ϕ = κ − 1 ​ 6 ​ ln ⁡ Ω \phi=\kappa^{-1}\sqrt{6}\ln\Omega . The motion of the scalaron is studied for the slow roll inflationary era using analytic approximate solutions obtained from its potential U ⁡ ( ϕ ) U(\phi) and, from these solutions, analytic expressions obtained for the Ricci scalar, its time derivative, the Hubble parameter and the scale factor of the Universe. These expressions for the Starobinsky model were obtained first by Motohashi and Nishizawa (2012) but the expressions for the power-law model of Odintsov and Oikonomou and its generalization are new. The calculated values of η \eta for the Starobinsky model vary from ( 1.05 − 1.46 ) × 10 − 11 (1.05-1.46)\times 10^{-11} , and for the power-law model, from ( 1.06 − 1.53 ) × 10 − 11 (1.06-1.53)\times 10^{-11} . The power-law values depend upon the unknown fitting parameters c 1 c_{1} and c 2 c_{2} , and a future fit of the Odintsov and Oikonomou model to data could yield enhanced values. The values for η \eta depend upon a mass parameter M ∗ M_{\ast} which is expected to be of the order of M Pl = 2.435 × 10 18 M_{\mathrm{Pl}}=2.435\times 10^{18} GeV/c 2 . The values of η \eta for both models have been calculated for M ∗ = M Pl M_{\ast}=M_{\mathrm{Pl}} and are quite close to the observed value η obs = 8.65 × 10 − 11 \eta_{\mathrm{obs}}=8.65\times 10^{-11} . Since η ∝ ( 1 / M ∗ ) 2 \eta\propto(1/M_{\ast})^{2} , reducing M ∗ M_{\ast} slightly from M Pl M_{\mathrm{Pl}} to 0.4 ​ M Pl 0.4M_{\mathrm{Pl}} would bring the calculated values into agreement with the observed value.

III

Inflation is most commonly modelled as a scalar field cosmology, which postulates a canonical scalar field

However, the introduction of a scalar field to produce a de Sitter phase of inflationary expansion, and to then transfer
its energy to Standard Model particles by direct couplings to these fields, lacks any fundamental justification. Also,
to obtain a sufficiently large inflation, suitable reheating after inflation, and to make the material fluctuations
small enough to be consistent with observations, requires fine tuning of the relevant couplings or masses

Starobinsky

the vacuum Einstein’s equations modified by the one-loop contributions of conformally covariant quantized matter fields. Thus the
expansion rate depends on the number of elementary fields in the model. No classical matter fields are involved.
Just like the inflaton model, the de Sitter phase is unstable and has a finite lifetime. However, unlike the inflaton model where
particle creation arises from coherent oscillations of the inflaton field about the quadratic minimum of the scalar field potential,
particle creation in the Starobinsky model is due to the rapidly changing spacetime metric as the Universe makes a transition from the
de Sitter era to either a radiation-dominated or matter-dominated era.

V
Implications of recent measurements of the CMB

Measurements of the primordial scalar spectrum of the CMB provide precision tests of single-field inflationary models
within the framework of Einstein’s General Relativity
Planck2018a
. The observational data favour slow-roll models
with concave scalar potentials. In terms of the slow-roll parameters

\epsilon\equiv\frac{M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^{2}}{2}\left(\frac{U_{\phi}}{U}\right)^{2},\quad\eta\equiv M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^{2}\frac{U_{\phi\phi}}{U},

(166)

where, eg,
U_{\phi}\equiv dU/d\phi
, measurements of the scalar tilt

n_{s}\simeq 1-6\epsilon_{\ast}+2\eta_{\ast},

(167)

where the subscript
\ast
denotes a suitable pivot scale, and the scalar-to-tensor ratio

r\simeq 16\epsilon_{\ast},

(168)

constrain models of inflation.

The experimental value of
n_{s}
at the pivotal scale
k_{\ast}=0.05
Mpc
-1
extracted from the
Planck 2018 data
Planck2018a
;
Planck2018b
, in combination with gravitational lensing, is

n_{s}=0.9649\pm 0.0042\,(68\%\,\mathrm{CL}).

(169)

This becomes
n_{s}=0.9652\pm 0.0042
when combined with BICEP/Keck data
BICEP2021
. The current 95% CL
upper bound on
r
is
0.036
.

The Starobinsky model gives
n_{s}=0.965
for
N_{\ast}=55
and
r=0.0035
, consistent with the value (
169
).

N_{\ast}
is the number of e-folds starting from the pivot scale:

N_{\ast}\equiv\log\left(\frac{a_{\mathrm{end}}}{a_{\ast}}\right),

(170)

where
a_{\mathrm{end}}
is the cosmological scale factor at the end of inflation.
However, this requires the low value
M/M_{\mathrm{P}}\simeq 1.3\times 10^{-5}
for
N_{\ast}\simeq 55
, and the large
initial value
\phi\simeq 5.5
. Note that
M=\sqrt{6}\tilde{M}
.

Recently two new high-multipole CMB measurements have been reported by the Atacama Cosmology Telescope (ACT)
collaboration
Louis2025
;
Calabrese2025
and the South Pole Telescope (SPT)-3G collaboration
Camphuis2025
.
The ACT measurement
n_{s}=0.9666\pm 0.0077
at the 68% CL is compatible
with (
169
) but a fit to Planck, lensing and ACT yields a higher value
n_{s}=0.9713\pm 0.0037
at 68% CL, in some
tension with the Planck and lensing combination (
169

Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) measurements of baryon acoustic oscillations (BAO) are combined with the

n_{s}=0.9743\pm 0.0034(68\%\,\mathrm{CL}).

(171)

The SPT-3G measurements give
n_{s}=0.951\pm 0.011
at 68% CL value (
169
), below the Planck/lensing
value (
169

n_{s}=0.9684\pm 0.0030(68\%\,\mathrm{CL}).

(172)

In response to these measurements, Ellis
et al

They argue that no-scale models of inflation can reproduce the predictions of the Starobinsky model and also provide a

In an

They consider the key CMB observables: the scalar tilt

\displaystyle+c_{4}\frac{R^{4}}{48\tilde{M}^{6}}+c_{5}\frac{R^{5}}{48\tilde{M}^{8}},

and

.

Odintsov and Oikonomou

gravity in light of the ACT observations.
They first revisit the standard power-law form

The Freidmann equation (

\displaystyle 3\frac{H^{2}}{(12H^{2})^{n-1}}+3n\beta H^{2}\approx 3n\beta H^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 3(n-1)\beta(2H^{2}+\dot{H})-6n(n-1)\beta\dot{H}.

(183)

This inverse power-law behavior can only be an inflationary evolution (

In their new model,

VII
Summary and conclusions

Gravitational baryogenesis has been investigated for two
f(R)
cosmologies: the
R^{2}
model of Starobinsky
Star1980
,
and the new power-law model of Odintsov and Oikonomou
OO2025
. Rather than use the Jordan frame in which these
cosmologies have been formulated, the present investigation is undertaken in the Einstein frame, which is obtained from the Jordan
frame by a conformal transformation
\Omega
that introduces the scalaron field
\phi=\kappa^{-1}\sqrt{6}\ln\Omega
.
The motion of the scalaron is studied for the inflationary era of slow roll using analytic approximate solutions obtained
from its potential
U(\phi)
and, from these solutions, analytic expressions obtained for the Ricci scalar,
its time derivative, the Hubble parameter and the scale factor of the Universe.
These expressions for the Starobinsky model were obtained first by
MN2012
, but
the expressions for the power-law model of Odintsov and Oikonomou and its generalization are new.

The calculation of the baryon asymmetry requires knowledge of the temperature of the Universe as determined by the ultrarelativistic
particles (radiation) present. In
f(R)
gravity, particle production is generated by the rapidly changing spacetime and is
represented by a
spectator
scalar field
\psi
of mass
m
which has a conformal coupling
\xi R\psi^{2}
to the Ricci scalar. This cosmological particle production is
treated as a semiclassical process in that the gravitational field is not quantized and assumed to have a rigid geometry unaffected by
the particle production. The field
\psi
is quantized in terms of a set of mode functions
\chi_{k}(\eta)
, where
\eta
is the conformal time but, as the metric is non-stationary, a new set is required for different values of
\eta
. In terms of the sets

\chi_{k}^{\mathrm{in}}(\eta)
and
\chi_{k}^{\mathrm{out}}(\eta)
valid for
\eta\rightarrow-\infty
and
\eta\rightarrow\infty

respectively, the comoving number density
n_{\mathrm{J}}
and energy density
\rho_{\mathrm{J}}
of the produced particles are

n_{\mathrm{J}}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{a^{3}_{\mathrm{J}}}\int|\beta_{k}|^{2}d^{3}k,\quad\rho_{\mathrm{J}}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{a^{4}_{\mathrm{J}}}\int\omega_{k}|\beta_{k}|^{2}d^{3}k,

(327)

where

\beta_{k}=-i\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}\chi_{k}^{\mathrm{in}}(\eta)\mathcal{V}(\eta)\chi_{k}^{\mathrm{out}}(\eta)d\eta

(328)

and

\mathcal{V}(\eta)=m^{2}[C(\infty)-C(\eta)+(\frac{1}{6}-\xi)C(\eta)R(\eta).

(329)

Here
C(\eta)=a^{2}(\eta)
and
\omega^{2}_{k}=k^{2}+m^{2}C(\infty)
. It is assumed that the energy density (
327
) is
totally converted to a radiation density. The analytic approximations developed for
R_{\mathrm{J}}
and
a_{\mathrm{J}}
allow the evaluation of
\rho_{\mathrm{rad}}(t_{\mathrm{J}})=\rho_{\mathrm{J}}
and the temperature
T
. Analytic results
are obtained for the Starobinsky model, whereas for the power-law model, the results are expressed in terms of an integral
I_{\tau}

which required numerical evaluation.

To obtain the analytic approximations for the power-law model, it was assumed that
1\gg c_{2}\Omega^{-2}\gg|\alpha|
in order to
obtain an analytic solution to the slow-roll equation (
262
) for
\phi(t_{\mathrm{E}})
. The validity of this
assumption was checked by numerical solution of this equation. The differential equation was integrated over the slow-roll region using an equally spaced grid of
\log_{10}\tilde{t}_{\mathrm{E}}
. An estimate of the end of the slow-roll regime in the
Einstein frame is, from (
285
),

\hat{\tau}(\tilde{t}_{\mathrm{E,SR}})=1=[\hat{d}^{2}_{\mathrm{init}}-\frac{\gamma}{\tilde{M}}(\tilde{t}_{\mathrm{E,SR}}-\tilde{t}_{\mathrm{E,init}})]^{1/(\alpha+2)},

(330)

which gives

\tilde{t}_{\mathrm{E,SR}}-\tilde{t}_{\mathrm{E,init}}=\frac{\tilde{M}}{\gamma}\left[\hat{d}^{2}_{\mathrm{init}}-1\right]=149.3.

(331)

The numerical results show that the analytical approximation for
\tilde{\phi}(\tilde{t}_{\mathrm{E}})
is extremely good for all but
the near-end of the slow-roll region, the differences being less than 1% for most of the range.

The analytic approximations for the two models were obtained assuming that the energy density of the created radiation is sub-dominant
compared to the effective energy density of the
f(R)
system. This can be validated by expressing the modified
Friedmann equation (
47

H_{\mathrm{J}}^{2}=\frac{\kappa^{2}}{3}[\rho_{\mathrm{grav}}+\rho],

(332)

where

\rho_{\mathrm{grav}}\equiv f^{\prime}(R_{\mathrm{J}})\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}-H_{\mathrm{J}}^{2}\mathcal{F}(R_{\mathrm{J}})

(333)

and

f^{\prime}(R_{\mathrm{J}})=1+\frac{\kappa^{2}}{3}\mathcal{F}(R_{\mathrm{J}}).

(334)

Using the Starobinsky model to estimate
\rho_{\mathrm{grav}}
gives

\rho_{\mathrm{grav}}=\frac{3}{\kappa^{2}\tilde{M}^{2}}(\dot{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{J}}-6H^{2}_{\mathrm{J}}\dot{H}_{\mathrm{J}}-2H_{\mathrm{J}}\ddot{H}_{\mathrm{J}}).

(335)

Assuming slow-roll conditions,

\rho_{\mathrm{grav}}\simeq-\frac{18}{\kappa^{2}\tilde{M}^{2}}H^{2}_{\mathrm{J}}\dot{H}_{\mathrm{J}}

(336)

and therefore, using (
224

H_{\mathrm{J}}

\tilde{\rho}_{\mathrm{grav}}\simeq\frac{3}{4}\tau^{2}(t_{\mathrm{J}})\sim O(1),

(337)

many orders greater than the calculated values
\tilde{\rho}_{\mathrm{rad}}\sim O(10^{-8})
.

This great disparity in the values of
\tilde{\rho}_{\mathrm{grav}}
and
\tilde{\rho}_{\mathrm{rad}}
during the slow-roll era
indicates that beginning of radiation dominance when
\tilde{\rho}_{\mathrm{grav}}=\tilde{\rho}_{\mathrm{rad}}

lies well into the fast-roll oscillation regime. Using the analytic approximations for the fast-roll regime of the

The values of
\eta

The two models chosen reflect different perspectives on studying
f(R)
gravity. The Starobinsky model is strongly motivated by
theoretical considerations of corrections to Einstein’s General Relativity arising from quantum corrections and new physics,
whereas the power-law model of Odintsov and Oikonomou is constructed purely from the slow-roll parameters of
f(R)
gravity
to fit the most recent CMD observations and lacks theoretical support.
The present investigation shows that gravitational baryogenesis is a viable mechanism in both models for producing the
observed baryon asymmetry of the Universe.

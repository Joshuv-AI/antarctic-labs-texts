Kharitonov’s Theorem with Degree Drop: a Wronskian Approach
Anthony Stefan, Aaron Welters

Abstract
In this paper, we present a simplified proof of Kharitonov’s Theorem, an important result on determining the Hurwitz stability of interval polynomials. Our new approach to the proof, which is based on the Wronskian of a pair of polynomials, is not only more elementary in comparison to known methods, but is able to handle the degree drop case with ease.

[a_{0}^{-},a_{0}^{+}]\times\cdots\times[a_{n}^{-},a_{n}^{+}]
is a convex set.
∎

Next, define the
Wronskian
of two polynomials
h(\omega)
and
g(\omega)
, denoted by
W[h,g](\omega)
, to be the polynomial

\displaystyle W[h,g](\omega)=h(\omega)g^{\prime}(\omega)-h^{\prime}(\omega)g(\omega).

(18)

The following lemma is well-known (see, for instance,
[
29
, Proposition 3.4.5]
).

Lemma 4
.

Let
f(\omega)=h(\omega)+ig(\omega)
be a non-constant polynomial, where
h(\omega)
and
g(\omega)
are real polynomials. If all the roots of
f
are in the open upper-half plane then, for every
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle W[h,g](\omega)=h(\omega)g^{\prime}(\omega)-h^{\prime}(\omega)g(\omega)>0.

(19)

Proof.

This follows immediately from the hypotheses by considering the partial fraction decomposition of
f^{\prime}/f
:

\displaystyle\frac{f^{\prime}(\omega)}{f(\omega)}=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:f(\lambda)=0}\frac{1}{\omega-\lambda}=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\operatorname{Im}\lambda>0,f(\lambda)=0}\frac{1}{\omega-\lambda},\text{ if }f(\omega)\not=0;

\displaystyle\operatorname{Im}\left[\frac{f^{\prime}(\omega)}{f(\omega)}\right]=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\operatorname{Im}\lambda>0,f(\lambda)=0}\frac{\operatorname{Im}\lambda-\operatorname{Im}\omega}{|\omega-\lambda|^{2}}>0,\;\text{if }\operatorname{Im}\omega\leq 0;

\displaystyle W[h,g](\omega)=\operatorname{Im}[f^{\prime}(\omega)\overline{f(\omega)}]=|f(\omega)|^{2}\operatorname{Im}\left[\frac{f^{\prime}(\omega)}{f(\omega)}\right]>0,\text{ if }\omega\in\mathbb{R}.\qed

3
.

\Rightarrow
): If the interval polynomial

, in (
3

for

.

a_{n}^{+}>0
, for otherwise we can consider the interval polynomial

p\in P
which is not Hurwitz stable. As
a_{n}^{+}\not=0
, there exists a Kharitonov polynomial
in (
3
) with degree
n
, which we denote by

. Consider the family of polynomials given by the convex combination

\displaystyle p_{t}(z)=(1-t)k(z)+tp(z),\hskip 14.45377ptt\in[0,1],

of the polynomials
k(z)
and
p(z)
.
Then by Corollary
1
and since
k(z)
is a degree
n
Hurwitz stable polynomial, it follows that the hypotheses of Lemma
2
hold for this family. Furthermore, since
p_{1}(z)=p(z)

2
implies there exists a
(t_{*},\omega_{*})\in(0,1]\times\mathbb{R}

. By Corollary

, there exists
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3},\alpha_{4}\in[0,1]
with

such that

\displaystyle p_{t_{*}}(i\omega_{*})=\sum_{j=1}^{4}\alpha_{j}k_{j}(i\omega_{*}).

In terms of the real polynomials
h_{\pm}(\omega),g_{\pm}(\omega)
from (

) and (

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{4}\alpha_{j}k_{j}(i\omega)=h(\omega)+ig(\omega),

and

\displaystyle h(\omega)=(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{4})h_{-}(\omega)+(\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3})h_{+}(\omega),

\displaystyle g(\omega)=(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})g_{-}(\omega)+(\alpha_{3}+\alpha_{4})g_{+}(\omega).

It follows immediately from this and
h(\omega_{*})=g(\omega_{*})=0
that

0=W[h,g](\omega_{*})=(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{4})(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})W[h_{-},g_{-}](\omega_{*})

[formula]

\hskip 70.0001pt+\;(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{4})(\alpha_{3}+\alpha_{4})W[h_{-},g_{+}](\omega_{*})

\hskip 70.0001pt+\;(\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3})(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})W[h_{+},g_{-}](\omega_{*})

that if all the Kharitonov polynomials in (

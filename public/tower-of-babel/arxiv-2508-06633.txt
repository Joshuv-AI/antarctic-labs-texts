Linear and nonlinear stability for the Bach flow, I
Eric Bahuaud, Christine Guenther, James Isenberg, Rafe Mazzeo

Abstract
. In this paper we prove the linear stability of a gauge-modified version of the Bach flow on any complete manifold ( M , h ) (M,h) of constant curvature. This involves some intricate calculations to obtain spectral bounds, and in particular introduces a higher order generalization of the well-known Koiso identity. We also prove nonlinear stability for the Bach flow if ( M , h ) (M,h) is the hyperbolic space ℍ n \mathbb{H}^{n} , and more generally any Poincaré-Einstein space sufficiently close to ℍ n \mathbb{H}^{n} . In the forthcoming Part II of this project, we study the nonlinear stability question if M M is either compact or else noncompact and flat, since those cases require different considerations involving a center manifold.

1.
Introduction

The search for canonical metrics in Riemannian geometry has a long and rich history, starting from the classical uniformization theorem
for Riemann surfaces, with many significant milestones along the way. Canonical metrics often arise as critical points
of an energy functional on some given space of metrics (e.g., all metrics in a conformal class, or all metrics in a Kähler class, or
all metrics), and because of this, techniques from the calculus of variations have played a key role in many of these problems.
Particularly if the critical points are (local or global) minima of their associated energy functionals, gradient flow techniques
provide another important path to finding such metrics. Conversely, gradient flows, or modified versions of such flows, provide interesting
settings for the development of techniques to study various stability questions around canonical metrics.

One source of natural energy functionals is provided by the Chern-Gauss-Bonnet theorem on a
4
-dimensional compact manifold
M

[
5
]
which states that:

8\pi^{2}\chi(M)=\int_{M}\left(\frac{1}{4}|\W|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left|\Ric-\frac{1}{4}\Scal g\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{24}\Scal^{2}\right)\dvol_{g}.

Here
\W
,
\Ric-\frac{1}{4}\Scal g
and
\Scal
are the Weyl, traceless Ricci and scalar curvatures, and
|\W|
is the norm of the
(0,4)

tensor
\W
, all with respect to the metric
g
, and
\chi(M)
is the Euler characteristic. It is well known that any quadratic curvature functional in 4 dimensions is a linear combination of the following three quantities:
\int|\W|^{2}\dvol_{g}
,
\int|\Ric|^{2}\dvol_{g}
, and
\int\Scal^{2}\dvol_{g}

(see e.g.,
[
5
,
11
]
). One motivation for this paper comes from the Weyl energy functional

\displaystyle g\longmapsto\mathcal{W}(g):=\int_{M}|\W(g)|^{2}_{g}\dvol_{g}.

Specifically in dimension
4
, this integral is unchanged if we replace
g
by any conformally related metric.

The first variation of
\mathcal{W}
along a curve of metrics
g(s)
with
g(0)=h
and
g^{\prime}(0)=v
is given by

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{ds}\mathcal{W}(h+sv)\right|_{s=0}=\int_{M}\left\langle\nabla\mathcal{W}\,,\,v\right\rangle_{h}\dvol_{h},

where

\nabla\mathcal{W}=-4\Ba(h)

is a multiple of the Bach tensor of
h
, which is defined as follows:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\Ba_{ij}:={\mathrm{P}_{ij,k}}^{k}-{\mathrm{P}_{ik,j}}^{k}+\mathrm{P}^{kl}\W_{kijl}.

Here
\mathrm{P}:=\frac{1}{2}\left(\Ric-\frac{\Scal}{6}h\right)
is the Schouten tensor. In this formula and throughout this manuscript, commas denote covariant
derivatives, and the standard summation convention is used. The Bach tensor is traceless and divergence-free, and involves derivatives up to order four of
the metric. The Schouten tensor
\mathrm{P}
has a relatively simple conformal transformation rule,
and using this, the Bach tensor
\Ba
has conformal invariance property:

g_{1}:=f^{2}g\Longrightarrow\Ba(g_{1})=f^{-2}\Ba(g).

Metrics satisfying condition
\Ba=0
are called Bach flat; examples include all Einstein and conformally Einstein metrics. Bach flat
metrics are expected to be somewhat easier to find than Einstein metrics.

The Bach tensor defined by equation (
1.1
) is extended to higher dimensions by the same formula with
P:=\frac{1}{n-2}\left(\Ric-\frac{\Scal}{2(n-1)}h\right)
. The Bach tensor loses several key properties if
n\geq 5
:
it is no longer conformally covariant, it is not the gradient of a Riemannian functional, and it is not divergence-free. The Bach tensor
has an analogue in higher dimensions which retains these properties
[
8
]
. It is called the “ambient obstruction tensor”, and it involves higher order derivatives of the metric.

In this paper we study a geometric flow associated to the Bach tensor. This flow decreases the Weyl energy functional in dimension
4
. Similar to the Ricci flow, this flow equation is quite degenerate
because of the underlying diffeomorphism invariance. The first author and D. Helliwell
[
1
]

have introduced a modified system, hereafter called simply the “Bach flow”, which breaks this conformal invariance. The initial value problem for this Bach flow takes the form

(1.2)

\frac{dg}{dt}=\Ba(g(t))+\frac{1}{2(n-1)(n-2)}\Delta\Scal(g(t))\,g(t),\;\;g(0)=g_{0}.

As explained in
[
3
]
, stationary points of this modified flow are Bach flat metrics for which the scalar curvature is harmonic; this extra condition on
S
provides a
normalization within each conformal class. Below we also incorporate an analogue of the DeTurck gauge to counter the diffeomorphism invariance.

Our interest in this paper is the long-time stability of the Bach flow, both at the linear and nonlinear levels, around a very special class of stationary points, namely the metrics
with constant sectional curvature. To this end, a significant part of the work below involves the explicit computation of the linearization of the gauge-adjusted Bach flow
at metrics of constant sectional curvature and the establishment of bounds on the
L^{2}
spectrum of the corresponding fourth order linear elliptic operator. These spectral
bounds depend heavily on the choice of gauge fixing, and one of the important issues is to choose a gauge for which this linearization is self-adjoint.
In our normalization, this
L^{2}
spectrum is unbounded below. The flow is linearly stable if this spectrum is nonpositive, and one expects particularly nice convergence properties
of the nonlinear flow if this spectrum is bounded above by a negative constant, or even if it is just nonpositive with a spectral gap at
0
. If the spectrum is strictly
negative, we can employ analytic semigroup techniques as in
[
4
]
to conclude that the nonlinear flow converges to the constant curvature metric at an exponential
rate. Our first main result is the following:

Theorem 1.1

(Linear Stability of the Gauge-Adjusted Bach Flow at Spaces of Constant Sectional Curvature)
.

Let
(M^{n},h)
be an orientable complete Riemannian manifold with constant sectional curvature
c\in\{-1,0,+1\}
. There is a choice of gauge for which the
linearization
L
of (
1.2
) at
h
is self-adjoint, and for which the
L^{2}
spectrum of
L
is non-positive. More specifically:

•

If
c=0
and
M
is compact, the kernel of
L
consists of the finite-dimensional space of parallel
2
-tensors. If
M
is
noncompact, we conclude only that the spectrum of
L
is nonpositive, but there is no
L^{2}
kernel.

•

If
c=1
and
M=S^{n}
, the kernel of
L
consists of the pure-trace symmetric
2
-tensors
v=fh
, where
f
satisfies
\Delta_{h}f=-nf
.

•

If
c=-1
and
M=\mathbb{H}^{n}
, the spectrum is bounded above by a negative constant; if
M
is compact, however,
the kernel of
L
consists of the symmetric
2
-tensors of the form
v=K\alpha=\delta^{*}_{h}\alpha
where
\alpha
is a harmonic
1
-form, along with the Codazzi-type
tensors which are defined as tensors that satisfy
v_{ij,k}-v_{jk,i}=0
. Here

is the conformal killing operator (see (
2.5
) for its definition).

In particular, if

is compact, there is always a spectral gap at

.

The choice of the gauge in Theorem
1.1
is different from the one proposed in
[
1
]
; indeed, that one does not lead to a
self-adjoint linearization. The proof of Theorem
1.1
involves a careful estimation of
\langle Lv,v\rangle
. A decomposition of symmetric

2
-tensors plays a central role. Similar to the linearization of the Einstein equation
\Ric(g)+(n-1)g=0
at a hyperbolic metric
h
, a special Bochner type formula
is required to obtain the correct sign for this quantity if
v
is tranverse-traceless. In the Einstein case, this Bochner formula is due to Koiso
[
12
]
. The
high-order analogue of the Koiso-Bochner formula proved here is new, of independent interest and likely to be useful in other geometric settings.

We next consider the nonlinear stability of the Bach flow at a constant curvature metric. For reasons to be explained below, we focus solely on the special case of the nonlinear stability of hyperbolic space itself, and then deduce the analogous nonlinear stability result for Poincaré-Einstein metrics close to the hyperbolic using
perturbative methods. For hyperbolic space there are no
L^{2}
harmonic
1
-forms and no
L^{2}
Codazzi-type tensors. We can thus prove that
if the initial metric
g_{0}
is sufficiently close to the hyperbolic metric (in the weighted little Hölder space
{\mathcal{C}^{k,\alpha}_{\mu}}
defined in (
5.66
) in §
5
),
then the corresponding solution of the nonlinear flow converges at an exponential rate to the hyperbolic metric. Since this convergence result requires that
g_{0}-h
decay at infinity,
this flow leaves the conformal infinity unchanged.

Theorem 1.2

(Nonlinear Stability of the Gauge-Adjusted Bach Flow at Hyperbolic Space)
.

Let
(M,h)
be
\mathbb{H}^{n}
with its standard hyperbolic metric. For any
k\geq 4
,
0<\alpha<1
, and positive

\mu\in(\frac{n-1}{2}-r(n),\frac{n-1}{2}+r(n))
, where
r(n)>0
is a constant depending on
n
, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that if

\|g_{0}-h\|_{\mathcal{C}^{k,\alpha}_{\mu}}<\varepsilon,

then the gauge-adjusted Bach flow
g_{0}(t)
starting at
g_{0}(0)=g_{0}
exists for all time and converges at an exponential rate to
h
in the
\mathcal{C}^{k,\alpha}_{\mu}
norm,

(1.3)

\|g_{0}(t)-h\|_{\mathcal{C}^{k,\alpha}_{\mu}}\leq Ce^{-a(n)t}\|g_{0}-h\|_{\mathcal{C}^{k,\alpha}_{\mu}},

where
a(n)>0
is a constant.

Using this result, we can then compensate for the gauge fixing using a family of diffeomorphisms and conclude the following:

Corollary 1.3

(Nonlinear Stability of the Bach Flow at Hyperbolic Space)
.

If
g_{0}
is a metric sufficiently close in
\mathcal{C}^{k,\alpha}_{\mu}
norm to the hyperbolic metric
h
on
\mathbb{H}^{n}

for some positive
\mu\in(\frac{n-1}{2}-r(n),\frac{n-1}{2}+r(n))

g_{0}(t)
starting at
g_{0}
exists
for all time and converges at an exponential rate in
\mathcal{C}^{k,\alpha}_{\mu}
to some pullback

.

We show in Appendix
A
that
r(n)=\frac{n-1}{8}
suffices, and that the associated rate of convergence is
a(n)=\frac{(n-2)(3n-11)(5n-13)}{128}
(see Remark
A.3
). However, these constants are definitely not sharp.

Theorem
1.2
directly implies dynamical stability of Poincaré-Einstein spaces sufficiently close to hyperbolic space. We describe this condition
more precisely in §
4.4
, but the result is the following:

Theorem 1.4

(Nonlinear Stability of the Gauge-Adjusted Bach Flow at Poincaré-Einstein spaces)
.

For any
k\geq 4
,
0<\alpha<1
and
\mu\in(\frac{n-1}{2}-r(n),\frac{n-1}{2}+r(n))
, where
r(n)>0
is a constant depending on

\varepsilon>0
such that if

\hat{g}
is a Poincaré-Einstein metric sufficiently close to

,
and if

\|g_{0}-\hat{g}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{k,\alpha}_{\mu}}<\varepsilon,

then the gauge-adjusted Bach flow
g_{0}(t)
satisfying
g_{0}(0)=g_{0}
exists for all time and converges to
\hat{g}
in the
\mathcal{C}^{k,\alpha}_{\mu}
norm; indeed, there exist constants
C>0
and
a(n)>0

such that

(1.4)

\|g_{0}(t)-\hat{g}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{k,\alpha}_{\mu}}\leq Ce^{-a(n)t}\|g_{0}-\hat{g}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{k,\alpha}_{\mu}}.

This implies the nonlinear stability of the ungauged Bach flow in the usual way:

Corollary 1.5

(Nonlinear Stability of the Bach Flow at Poincaré-Einstein spaces)
.

If
\hat{g}
is a Poincaré-Einstein metric that is sufficiently close to

starting at

Theorem
1.2
addresses nonlinear stability in only one of the several cases discussed in the linear stability result, Theorem
1.1
. Further nonlinear stability
results addressing those other cases will be handled in a sequel to this paper. There are two somewhat distinct reasons for this. First, using techniques

The papers
[

4

Background

2.1.
Conventions

In this section we gather the conventions used throughout the paper. First,
(M^{n},h)
is always a smooth orientable Riemannian manifold of constant sectional
curvature
c\in\{-1,0,+1\}
. The sign conventions for the curvature and the various linear operators below are consistent with those in the book
[
6
]
,
but the reader should beware that these are at odds with other standard conventions. In particular, the Hodge Laplacians appearing below have nonpositive
spectrum.

The full Riemannian, Ricci and Scalar curvature tensors of the constant curvature metric
h
are

(2.5)

\displaystyle\R_{ijkl}

\displaystyle=c(h_{il}h_{jk}-h_{ik}h_{jl}),\;\;\;

(2.6)

\displaystyle\Rc_{jk}

\displaystyle=h^{il}\R_{ijkl}=c(n-1)h_{jk},

(2.7)

\displaystyle\Scal

\displaystyle=h^{jk}\Rc_{jk}=cn(n-1).

For constant curvature metrics, the Schouten tensor reduces to the much simpler expression

(2.8)

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}_{ij}=\frac{1}{n-2}\left(\Rc_{ij}-\frac{\Scal}{2(n-1)}g_{ij}\right)=\frac{c}{2}h_{ij}.

and likewise the Weyl curvature

\displaystyle\W_{ijkl}:=\R_{ijkl}-(\mathrm{P}\owedge g)_{ijkl},

vanishes if
g=h
. Here
\owedge
is the Kulkarni-Nomizu product (if
a
and
b
are symmetric
2
-tensors, then

(a\owedge b)_{ijkl}:=a_{il}b_{jk}+a_{jk}b_{il}-a_{ik}b_{jl}-a_{jl}b_{ik}
).

We use the convention for the scalar Laplacian

\displaystyle\Delta^{g}f:=g^{jk}f_{,jk},

with a similar sign choice for Laplacians on other tensor bundles, including the bundles of symmetric
2
-tensors
\Sigma^{2}(T^{*}M)
, of traceless symmetric
2
-tensors

\Sigma^{2}_{0}(T^{*}M)
, and of
p
-forms
\Lambda^{p}(T^{*}M)
. The space of sections of a bundle
E
is denoted by
\Gamma(E)
, and in particular,

\Gamma(\Lambda^{p}(T^{*}M))=\Omega^{p}(M)
.
The divergence
\delta
and its adjoint
\delta^{*}
,

\displaystyle\delta:\,

\displaystyle\Gamma(\Sigma^{2}(T^{*}M))\longrightarrow\Gamma(T^{*}M),

\displaystyle[\delta z]_{i}=-{z_{ij,}}^{j}

\displaystyle\delta^{*}:\,

\displaystyle\Gamma(T^{*}M)\longrightarrow\Gamma(\Sigma^{2}(T^{*}M)),

\displaystyle[\delta^{*}z]_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}(z_{i,j}+z_{j,i})

appear frequently in our calculations.

Again, contrary to a commonly used choice of sign, but following
[
6
]
, we use

\displaystyle\Delta_{H}=-(dd^{*}+d^{*}d)

as the Hodge Laplacian on differential forms, where
d:\Omega^{p}(T^{*}M)\rightarrow\Omega^{p+1}(T^{*}M)
is the exterior derivative and
d^{*}:\Omega^{p}(T^{*}M)\rightarrow\Omega^{p-1}(T^{*}M)
is its
L^{2}
adjoint.

Covariant derivatives are defined here with respect to

2.3.
The Bach flow

We define the Bach tensor
\Ba
as in (
1.1
), and the Bach flow by (
1.2
). Noting that
\Ba
is trace-free in all dimensions, there are three immediate consequences.

First, if
n=4
, then
\mathcal{W}(g)
decreases under the (modified) Bach flow, even though this is not the true gradient flow. Indeed

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{W}(g(t))=\int_{M}\left\langle\nabla\mathcal{W}\,,\,g^{\prime}(t)\right\rangle\dvol_{g}=\int_{M}\left\langle-4\Ba\,,\,\Ba+\frac{1}{12}(\Delta\Scal)g\right\rangle\dvol_{g}=-4\|\Ba\|^{2}<0.

(This fails in higher dimensions since
\Ba
does not arise as the variation for
\mathcal{W}
.)
Second, the Bach flow preserves volume if
M
is compact:

(2.14)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathrm{vol}(g(t))=\int_{M}\frac{1}{2}\tr^{g}g^{\prime}(t)\dvol_{g}=\int_{M}\left(\frac{1}{2}\tr^{g}\Ba+\frac{n}{4(n-1)(n-2)}\Delta S\right)\,dV_{g}=0.

Finally, if
g
is a fixed point of the flow and
M
is compact, then taking the trace of
\Ba(g)+\frac{1}{2(n-1)(n-2)}\Delta\Scal g=0
yields that

\Delta\Scal=0
; hence,
\Scal
is constant. Consequently the fixed point equation is simply
\Ba(g)=0
.

The contracted Bianchi identity yields

(2.15)

\displaystyle\Ba_{ij}+\frac{\Delta\Scal}{2(n-1)(n-2)}g_{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n-2}{\Rc_{ij,k}}^{k}-{\mathrm{P}_{ik,j}}^{k}+\mathrm{P}^{kl}\W_{kijl},

and to compute the divergence of the Schouten tensor here, we commute derivatives and apply the contracted Bianchi identity again to get

\displaystyle{\mathrm{P}_{ik,j}}^{k}={{\mathrm{P}_{ik,}}^{k}}_{j}+{\R_{jki}}^{m}{\mathrm{P}_{m}}^{k}+{\Rc_{j}}^{m}\mathrm{P}_{im}=\frac{1}{2(n-1)}\Scal_{,ij}+{\R_{jki}}^{m}{\mathrm{P}_{m}}^{k}+{\Rc_{j}}^{m}\mathrm{P}_{im}.

Using this and introducing the Weyl tensor, we obtain

(2.16)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \Ba_{ij}&+\frac{\Delta\Scal}{2(n-1)(n-2)}g_{ij}\\
&=\frac{1}{n-2}{\Rc_{ij,k}}^{k}-\frac{1}{2(n-1)}\Scal_{,ij}-{\R_{jki}}^{m}{\mathrm{P}_{m}}^{k}-{\Rc_{j}}^{m}\mathrm{P}_{im}+\mathrm{P}^{kl}\W_{kijl}\\
&=\frac{1}{n-2}{\Rc_{ij,k}}^{k}-\frac{1}{2(n-1)}\Scal_{,ij}-2{\R_{jki}}^{m}{\mathrm{P}_{m}}^{k}-{\Rc_{j}}^{m}\mathrm{P}_{im}-\mathrm{P}^{kl}(\mathrm{P}\owedge g)_{kijl}.\end{aligned}

as the right hand side of the modified Bach flow.

2.4.
Gauge adjustment

The flow generated by (
2.16
) is not parabolic as a consequence of its invariance under diffeomorphisms. Motivated by the DeTurck adjustment to the Ricci flow, we
add a higher-order variant of the Bianchi gauge (see (
2.20
)) to obtain a shadow parabolic flow. To motivate this further, we briefly review the abstract Ricci-DeTurck
setup discussed as in
[
1
]
: We recall that
T(g)
is a natural tensor for a metric
g
if, for any diffeomorphism
\phi
of
M
,
T(\phi^{*}g)=\phi^{*}T(g)
. Now suppose
Z
is a
time-dependent vector field, and
g(t)
satisfies the equation

(2.17)

\displaystyle\frac{dg}{dt}=T(g(t))+\mathcal{L}_{Z(t)}g(t),

where
\mathcal{L}_{Z(t)}
denotes the Lie derivative along the vector field
Z(t).
We observe that if the flow by
T(g)
preserves the volume of a compact manifold, then the
gauge-adjusted flow (
2.17
) also preserves the volume since it differs by a pure divergence term.

If
-Z
generates the one-parameter family of diffeomorphisms
\theta_{t}
and
g
satisfies equation (
2.17
), then
\overline{g}=\theta_{t}^{*}g
satisfies

(2.18)

\displaystyle\frac{d\overline{g}}{dt}=T(\overline{g}).

We recall as well that
[\mathcal{L}_{Z}g]_{ab}=\nabla_{a}\omega_{b}+\nabla_{b}\omega_{a}=2(\delta^{*}\omega)_{ab}
, where

\omega
is the
1
-form metrically dual to
Z
. In short, we obtain a gauge-modified flow

(2.19)

\displaystyle\frac{dg}{dt}=T(g(t))+2\,\delta_{g(t)}^{*}\omega(t),

for any choice of a
t
-dependent
1
-form
\omega
. Our goal is to find a propitious choice of
\omega
which results in the linearized flow taking a particularly simple form. Solutions of the gauge-modified flow are then immediately converted back to solutions of the original flow using the family of
diffeomorphisms obtained by integrating the associated vector field.

We define the Bianchi operator as follows:

(2.20)

\displaystyle[\beta_{h}(g)]_{i}:=-h^{jk}g_{ij,k}+(1/2)(h^{jk}g_{jk})_{,i}.

The contracted Bianchi identity for
h
can be written as
\beta_{h}(\Ric^{h})=0
. For the
1
-form in the gauge adjustment setup above, we use
a linear combination of
\delta_{h}(g)
and its Laplacian. Thus, for constants
\mu
and
\nu
to be determined below, we consider the following gauge-adjustment
of (
2.16
):

Definition 2.2
.

(General gauge-adjusted Bach flow)

\displaystyle\frac{dg}{dt}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n-2}\left(\Delta_{h}R_{ij}+2\left[\delta_{g}^{*}\left(\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{h}\beta_{h}(g)+\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}d\Scal+\mu\,\delta_{h}g+\nu\,d(\tr^{h}g)\right)\right]_{ij}\right)

(2.21)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2(n-1)}\Scal_{,ij}-2{\R_{jki}}^{m}{\mathrm{P}_{m}}^{k}-{\Rc_{j}}^{m}\mathrm{P}_{im}-\mathrm{P}^{kl}(\mathrm{P}\owedge g)_{kijl}.

Let us motivate these new terms.
As we show in §
3
, the linearization of the sum of the terms involving
\Delta_{h}\beta_{h}(g)
and
\Delta_{h}\Ric
is elliptic. Next,
dS
is included to
cancel the term involving the Hessian of the scalar curvature term on the second line of the flow equation:

\frac{1}{n-2}\,2\,\left[\delta_{g}^{*}\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}d\Scal\right]_{ij}-\frac{1}{2(n-1)}\Scal_{,ij}=0.

The final two terms in square brackets in equation (
2.2
) are lower-order in the derivatives of the metric, but are chosen specifically because if
\mu=-c(n-1)/2
and
\nu=-c/4
, where
c
is the constant sectional curvature of
h
, then the linearization is self-adjoint. This leads us finally to the following definition:

Definition 2.3
.

(Gauge-adjusted Bach flow)

\displaystyle\frac{dg_{ij}}{dt}=\frac{1}{n-2}\left(\phantom{\frac{1}{1}}{\Rc_{ij,k}}^{k}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\;2\left[\delta_{g}^{*}\left(\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{h}\beta_{h}(g)-\frac{c(n-1)}{2}\delta_{h}g-\frac{c}{4}d(\tr^{h}g)\right)\right]_{ij}\phantom{\frac{1}{1}}\right)

(2.22)

\displaystyle-2{\R_{jki}}^{m}{\mathrm{P}_{m}}^{k}-{\Rc_{j}}^{m}\mathrm{P}_{im}-\mathrm{P}^{kl}(\mathrm{P}\owedge g)_{kijl}.

Removing the
2\delta_{g}^{*}(\cdot)
term in (
2.2
) yields the Bach flow (
1.2
); hence, solutions to equation (
2.3
) can be converted by
pullback to solutions to the original Bach flow.

2.5.
A splitting of the bundle of symmetric
2
-tensors

We conclude this section by recalling a well-known decomposition of the space of symmetric
2
-tensors. Let
K
be the conformal Killing operator with respect to
h
:

\displaystyle K:\Gamma(T^{*}M)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\Gamma(\Sigma^{2}_{0}(T^{*}M)),

(2.23)

\displaystyle[K\alpha]_{ij}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\left(\alpha_{i,j}+\alpha_{j,i}\right)-\frac{1}{n}{a_{k,}}^{k}h_{ij}.

There is an
L^{2}
orthogonal decomposition as follows:

(2.24)

\displaystyle\Gamma(\Sigma^{2}(T^{*}M))=\mathrm{im}(K)\oplus\{f\cdot h\}\oplus\{v:\delta v=0,\tr^{h}v=0\}.

Tensors in the third summand are “tranverse-traceless”, which we write as TT. Though not appearing in this decomposition,
1
-forms in the kernel of
K
are
called conformal Killing
1
-forms. A proof of this splitting on compact manifolds can be found in
[
20
, Lemma 3.2]
. Since this is also
needed here on asymptotically hyperbolic manifolds, we remark that the essentially the same proof applies. Indeed, the symbol of
K
is injective, hence

K^{*}K
is elliptic. Note too that
K^{*}=\delta
on tracefree symmetric
2
-tensors. After commuting derivatives (see for example equation (
4.58
) below)
one sees that
\|K\alpha\|^{2}\geq(n-1)\|\alpha\|^{2}
for all
L^{2}

1
-forms
\alpha
on hyperbolic space, hence
K
has closed range, and thus by
standard arguments, the decomposition (
2.24
) is valid as an orthogonal decomposition on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. If
M
is only asymptotically hyperbolic and conformally
compact, this inequality holds for forms supported outside a sufficiently large compact set, which implies that
K^{*}K
is Fredholm, and then the same
formal argument leads to the decomposition
L^{2}(M;(\Sigma_{0}^{2}(T^{*}M))=\mathrm{im}(K)\oplus\mathrm{ker}(K^{*}).

3.
The linearization

In this section we compute the linearization of the right-hand side of the general gauge-adjusted flow (
2.2
) at a metric
h
of constant
sectional curvature
c
. The main results of this section are Theorem
3.5
, which documents the linearization, and Proposition
3.7
, which
specifies the choices of constants
\mu
and
\nu
that ensure that the linearization is self-adjoint. We relegate some lengthy but straightforward proofs of
various commutation formulæ to Appendix
B
.

We begin by computing the linearization of the Laplacian of the Ricci tensor.

Proposition 3.1
.

If
g(s)
is a 1-parameter family of metrics with
h=g(0)
a metric with constant sectional curvature
c
and
v=g^{\prime}(0)
, then

(3.25)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &D[\Delta^{g}\Ric(g)]_{h}v_{ij}:=\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial s}(\Delta^{g(s)}\Ric(g(s))_{ij})\right|_{s=0}\\
&\ \ =-\frac{1}{2}v_{ij,p\phantom{p}m\phantom{m}}^{\phantom{ij,p}p\phantom{m}m}+\frac{1}{2}v_{ip,\phantom{p}jm\phantom{m}}^{\phantom{ip,}p\phantom{jm}m}+\frac{1}{2}v_{jp,\phantom{p}im\phantom{m}}^{\phantom{jp,}p\phantom{im}m}-\frac{1}{2}v_{p\phantom{p},jim\phantom{m}}^{\phantom{p}p\phantom{,jim}m}-cv_{p\phantom{p},m\phantom{m}}^{\phantom{p}p\phantom{,m}m}h_{ij}+cv_{ij,m}^{\phantom{ij,m}m}.\end{aligned}

Proof.

Differentiating

\displaystyle\Rc_{ij,k}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{k}\Rc_{ij}=\partial_{k}\Rc_{ij}-\Gamma_{ki}^{p}\Rc_{pj}-\Gamma_{kj}^{p}\Rc_{ip}

with respect to
s
produces

(3.26)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \partial_{s}\Rc_{ij,k}&=\partial_{s}\partial_{k}\Rc_{ij}-\Gamma_{ki}^{p}\partial_{s}\Rc_{pj}-\Gamma_{kj}^{p}\partial_{s}\Rc_{pi}-(\partial_{s}\Gamma)_{ki}^{p}\Rc_{pj}-\partial_{s}(\Gamma_{kj}^{p})\Rc_{pi}\\
&=\nabla_{k}(\partial_{s}\Rc_{ij})-(\partial_{s}\Gamma)_{ki}^{p}\Rc_{pj}-\partial_{s}(\Gamma_{kj}^{p})\Rc_{pi}.\end{aligned}

Setting
s=0
and applying Proposition
2.1
and equation (
2.10
), we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{s}[\Rc_{ij,k}]|_{s=0}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{k}\left(-\frac{1}{2}v_{ij,p}^{\phantom{ij,p}p}+\frac{1}{2}v_{ip,\phantom{p}j}^{\phantom{ip,}p\phantom{j}}+\frac{1}{2}v_{jp,\phantom{p}i}^{\phantom{jp,}p\phantom{i}}-\frac{1}{2}v_{p\phantom{p},ji}^{\phantom{p}p\phantom{,ji}}-cv_{p}^{\phantom{p}p}h_{ij}+cnv_{ij}\right)-c(n-1)v_{ij,k}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}{v_{ij,p}^{\phantom{ij,p}p}}_{k}+\frac{1}{2}v_{ip,\phantom{p}jk}^{\phantom{ip,}p\phantom{jk}}+\frac{1}{2}v_{jp,\phantom{p}ik}^{\phantom{jp,}p\phantom{ik}}-\frac{1}{2}v_{p\phantom{p},jik}^{\phantom{p}p\phantom{,jik}}-c{v_{p}^{\phantom{p}p}}_{,k}h_{ij}+cv_{ij,k}.

Taking another covariant derivative,

\displaystyle\Rc_{ij,km}

\displaystyle=\partial_{m}\Rc_{ij,k}-\Gamma_{mi}^{p}\Rc_{pj,k}-\Gamma_{mj}^{p}\Rc_{ip,k}-\Gamma_{mk}^{p}\Rc_{ij,p},

and calculating as has been done for equation (
3.26
), we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{s}\Rc_{ij,km}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{m}\partial_{s}\Rc_{ij,k}-\partial_{s}\Gamma_{mi}^{p}\Rc_{pj,k}-\partial_{s}\Gamma_{mj}^{p}\Rc_{ip,k}-\partial_{s}\Gamma_{mk}^{p}\Rc_{ij,p}.

Therefore, at
s=0
, and using that
\nabla\Rc_{ij}=0
since
h
has constant curvature,

\displaystyle\partial_{s}[\Rc_{ij,km}]|_{s=0}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}v_{ij,p\phantom{p}km}^{\phantom{ij,p}p\phantom{km}}+\frac{1}{2}v_{ip,\phantom{p}jkm}^{\phantom{ip,}p\phantom{jkm}}+\frac{1}{2}v_{jp,\phantom{p}ikm}^{\phantom{jp,}p\phantom{ikm}}-\frac{1}{2}v_{p\phantom{p},jikm}^{\phantom{p}p\phantom{,jikm}}-cv_{p\phantom{p},km}^{\phantom{p}p\phantom{,km}}h_{ij}+cv_{ij,km}.

Finally, differentiating
\Delta\Rc_{ij}=g^{km}\Rc_{ij,km}
at
s=0
, and using that
\nabla\Rc_{ij}=0
, we see finally that

\displaystyle\partial_{s}[g^{km}\Rc_{ij,km}]|_{s=0}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}v_{ij,p\phantom{p}m\phantom{m}}^{\phantom{ij,p}p\phantom{m}m}+\frac{1}{2}v_{ip,\phantom{p}jm\phantom{m}}^{\phantom{ip,}p\phantom{jm}m}+\frac{1}{2}v_{jp,\phantom{p}im\phantom{m}}^{\phantom{jp,}p\phantom{im}m}-\frac{1}{2}v_{p\phantom{p},jim\phantom{m}}^{\phantom{p}p\phantom{,jim}m}-cv_{p\phantom{p},m\phantom{m}}^{\phantom{p}p\phantom{,m}m}h_{ij}+cv_{ij,m}^{\phantom{ij,m}m}.

∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Using the same notation as above, we obtain:

(3.27)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} D[\delta_{g}^{*}&(\Delta_{h}\beta_{h}(g))]_{h}v_{ij}=-\frac{1}{2}v_{ip,\phantom{p}jm\phantom{m}}^{\phantom{ip,}p\phantom{jm}m}-\frac{1}{2}v_{jp,\phantom{p}im\phantom{m}}^{\phantom{jp,}p\phantom{im}m}+\frac{1}{2}v_{p\phantom{p},ijm\phantom{m}}^{\phantom{p}p\phantom{,ijm}m}\\
&-2cv_{m\phantom{p},p\phantom{m}}^{\phantom{m}p\phantom{,p}m}h_{ij}+cv_{p\phantom{p},m\phantom{m}}^{\phantom{p}p\phantom{,m}m}h_{ij}\\
&+\frac{c(n+1)}{2}v_{ip,\phantom{p}j}^{\phantom{ip,}p\phantom{j}}-\frac{c(n+1)}{2}v_{p\phantom{p},ij}^{\phantom{p}p\phantom{,ij}}+\frac{c(n+1)}{2}v_{jp,\phantom{p}i}^{\phantom{jp,}p\phantom{i}}.\end{aligned}

Proof.

Since
g\longmapsto\Delta_{h}\beta_{h}(g(s))
is a linear map,

\displaystyle\partial_{s}(\Delta_{h}\beta_{h}(g(s)))|_{s=0}=\Delta_{h}\beta_{h}(v).

If
w
is a 1-form,

\displaystyle(\delta^{*}_{g(s)}w)_{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(w_{i,j}+w_{j,i})=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{j}w_{i}+\partial_{i}w_{j})-\Gamma_{ij}^{p}w_{p},

so that

(3.28)

\displaystyle\partial_{s}(\delta^{*}w)_{ij}|_{s=0}

\displaystyle=\delta_{g(s)}^{*}\left(\partial_{s}w_{ij}|_{s=0}\right)-(\partial_{s}\Gamma_{ij}^{p})w_{p}|_{s=0}.

Applying this to
w(s)=\Delta_{h}\beta_{h}(g(s))
, and noting that
\beta_{h}(g(0))=\beta_{h}(h)=0
, to get

(3.29)

\displaystyle\partial_{s}(\delta_{g(s)}^{*}(\Delta_{h}\beta_{h}(g(s)))|_{s=0}=\delta_{h}^{*}\Delta_{h}\beta_{h}(v).

On the other hand, we note that

\begin{split}[\beta_{h}(v)]_{i}&=-v_{ip,}^{\phantom{ip}p}+\frac{1}{2}v_{p\phantom{p},i}^{\phantom{p}p}\Longrightarrow\\
[\delta_{h}^{*}\beta_{h}(v)]_{ij}&=-\frac{1}{2}v_{ip,\phantom{p}j}^{\phantom{ip,}p\phantom{j}}+\frac{1}{2}v_{p\phantom{p},ij}^{\phantom{p}p\phantom{,ij}}-\frac{1}{2}v_{jp,\phantom{p}i}^{\phantom{jp,}p\phantom{i}}\Longrightarrow\\
[\Delta_{h}\delta_{h}^{*}\beta_{h}(v)]_{ij}&=-\frac{1}{2}v_{ip,\phantom{p}jm\phantom{m}}^{\phantom{ip,}p\phantom{jm}m}+\frac{1}{2}v_{p\phantom{p},ijm\phantom{m}}^{\phantom{p}p\phantom{,ijm}m}-\frac{1}{2}v_{jp,\phantom{p}im\phantom{m}}^{\phantom{jp,}p\phantom{im}m}.\end{split}

Then equation (
3.29
) together with Lemma
B.1
yield equation (
3.27
).
∎

Using similar calculations, we obtain the following:

Proposition 3.3
.

\displaystyle\partial_{s}[\delta_{g(s)}^{*}\delta_{h}g]_{ij}|_{s=0}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}(v_{ip,\phantom{p}j}^{\phantom{ip,}p\phantom{j}}+v_{jp,\phantom{p}i}^{\phantom{jp,}p\phantom{i}}),

\displaystyle\partial_{s}[\delta_{g(s)}^{*}d(\tr^{h}g)_{ij}]_{s=0}

\displaystyle=v_{p\phantom{p},ij}^{\phantom{p}p\phantom{,ij}}.

In the next proposition we compute the linearization of the ‘quadratic curvature’ terms appearing in equation (
2.3
). To do that, we introduce the following notation:

\displaystyle Q_{1}

\displaystyle:=-2{\R_{jki}}^{m}{\mathrm{P}_{m}}^{k},

\displaystyle Q_{2}

\displaystyle:=-{\Rc_{j}}^{m}\mathrm{P}_{im},

\displaystyle Q_{3}

\displaystyle:=-\mathrm{P}^{kl}(\mathrm{P}\owedge g)_{kijl}.

Proposition 3.4
.

If
g(s)
is a 1-parameter family of metrics with
h=g(0)
a metric with constant sectional curvature
c
and
v=g^{\prime}(0)
, then

\displaystyle D[Q_{1}+Q_{2}+Q_{3}]_{h}v_{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{n-2}v_{p\phantom{m},m\phantom{p}}^{\phantom{p}m\phantom{,m}p}h_{ij}-\frac{c}{n-2}v_{p\phantom{p},m\phantom{m}}^{\phantom{p}p\phantom{,m}m}h_{ij}+\frac{cn}{2(n-2)}(v_{ij,p}^{\phantom{ij,p}p}-v_{ip,\phantom{p}j}^{\phantom{ip,}p\phantom{j}}-v_{jp,\phantom{p}i}^{\phantom{jp,}p\phantom{i}}+v_{p\phantom{p},ji}^{\phantom{p}p\phantom{,ji}})

\displaystyle-\frac{c^{2}n}{n-2}v_{ij}+\frac{c^{2}}{n-2}v_{p}^{\phantom{p}p}h_{ij}.

Proof.

We begin by linearizing each of the quadratic terms
Q_{I},I=1,2,3
, and then writing the result in terms of the linearization of the curvature quantities appearing in Proposition

2.1
. Combining these terms and inserting curvatures from that proposition leads to the result. This computation is straightforward and tedious,
but is simplified since we evaluate all terms at the constant curvature metric
h
.

For
Q_{1}
, we note that

\displaystyle\R^{\phantom{jki}m}_{jki}{\mathrm{P}_{m}}^{k}=g^{k\ell}\R^{\phantom{jki}m}_{jki}\mathrm{P}_{m\ell};

hence

\displaystyle\partial_{s}[{\R_{jki}}^{m}{\mathrm{P}_{m}}^{k}]

\displaystyle=-g^{kp}g^{\ell q}v_{pq}\R^{\phantom{jki}m}_{jki}\mathrm{P}_{m\ell}+g^{k\ell}(\partial_{s}\R^{\phantom{jki}m}_{jki})\mathrm{P}_{m\ell}+g^{k\ell}\R^{\phantom{jki}m}_{jki}(\partial_{s}\mathrm{P}_{m\ell}).

So evaluating at
g(0)=h
, we get finally that

(3.30)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \partial_{s}[{\R_{jki}}^{m}{\mathrm{P}_{m}}^{k}]_{s=0}&=-\frac{c^{2}}{2}(v_{ij}-\tr^{h}v\,h_{ij})+\frac{c}{2}\delta^{k}_{m}(\partial_{s}\R^{\phantom{jki}m}_{jki})+c\partial_{s}\mathrm{P}_{ij}-ch^{pq}\partial_{s}\mathrm{P}_{pq}\,h_{ij}\\
&=-\frac{c^{2}}{2}(v_{ij}-\tr^{h}v\,h_{ij})-\frac{c}{2}(\partial_{s}\Rc_{ij})+c\partial_{s}\mathrm{P}_{ij}-ch^{pq}\partial_{s}\mathrm{P}_{pq}\,h_{ij}.\end{aligned}

Using equation (
3.30
), we obtain the variation of the first term,

(3.31)

\displaystyle D[Q_{1}]_{h}v_{ij}=c^{2}(v_{ij}-v_{p}^{\phantom{p}p}\,h_{ij})+c(\partial_{s}\Rc_{ij})-2c\partial_{s}\mathrm{P}_{ij}+2ch^{pq}\partial_{s}\mathrm{P}_{pq}\,h_{ij}.

Next, for
Q_{2}
, we start from
{\Rc_{j}}^{m}\mathrm{P}_{im}=g^{pm}\Rc_{jp}\mathrm{P}_{im}
, and calculate the variation at
s=0
:

(3.32)

\displaystyle\partial_{s}[{\Rc_{j}}^{m}\mathrm{P}_{im}]|_{s=0}

\displaystyle=-h^{pa}h^{mb}v_{ab}\Rc_{jp}\mathrm{P}_{im}+h^{pm}(\partial_{s}\Rc_{jp})\mathrm{P}_{im}+h^{pm}\Rc_{jp}(\partial_{s}\mathrm{P}_{im}).

Inserting
g(0)=h
, equation (
3.32
) becomes

\displaystyle\partial_{s}[{\Rc_{j}}^{m}\mathrm{P}_{im}]|_{s=0}

\displaystyle=-\frac{c^{2}(n-1)}{2}v_{ij}+\frac{c}{2}(\partial_{s}\Rc_{ij})+c(n-1)(\partial_{s}\mathrm{P}_{ij}),

so that

(3.33)

\displaystyle D[Q_{2}]_{h}v_{ij}=\frac{c^{2}(n-1)}{2}v_{ij}-\frac{c}{2}(\partial_{s}\Rc_{ij})-c(n-1)(\partial_{s}\mathrm{P}_{ij}).

For
Q_{3}
, we write

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}^{kl}(\mathrm{P}\owedge g)_{kijl}

\displaystyle=g^{kp}g^{lq}\mathrm{P}_{kl}(\mathrm{P}\owedge g)_{pijq},

and then compute that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \partial_{s}[\mathrm{P}^{kl}(\mathrm{P}\owedge g)_{kijl}]&=-g^{ka}g^{pb}v_{ab}g^{lq}\mathrm{P}_{kl}(\mathrm{P}\owedge g)_{pijq}-g^{kp}g^{la}g^{qb}v_{ab}\mathrm{P}_{kl}(\mathrm{P}\owedge g)_{pijq}\\
&+g^{kp}g^{lq}(\partial_{s}\mathrm{P}_{kl})(\mathrm{P}\owedge g)_{pijq}+g^{kp}g^{lq}\mathrm{P}_{kl}\partial_{s}(\mathrm{P}\owedge g)_{pijq}.\end{aligned}

Evaluating at
h
and using the fact that
(\mathrm{P}\owedge g)_{kijl}|_{s=0}=\frac{c}{2}(h\owedge h)_{kijl}=c(h_{kl}h_{ij}-h_{jk}h_{il})
, we find

(3.34)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \partial_{s}[\mathrm{P}^{kl}(\mathrm{P}\owedge g)_{kijl}]_{s=0}&=-c^{2}((\tr^{h}v)h_{ij}-v_{ij})\\
&+ch^{kl}(\partial_{s}\mathrm{P}_{kl})h_{ij}-c\partial_{s}\mathrm{P}_{ij}+\frac{c}{2}h^{kl}\partial_{s}(\mathrm{P}\owedge g)_{kijl}.\end{aligned}

We now use
\partial_{s}(\mathrm{P}\owedge g)=(\partial_{s}\mathrm{P})\owedge g+\mathrm{P}\owedge v
to obtain the variation at
s=0
:

(3.35)

\displaystyle h^{kl}\partial_{s}(\mathrm{P}\owedge g)_{kijl}|_{s=0}

\displaystyle=h^{kl}(\partial_{s}\mathrm{P}_{kl})\,h_{ij}+\frac{c}{2}\tr^{h}v\,h_{ij}+(n-2)\partial_{s}\mathrm{P}_{ij}+\frac{c(n-2)}{2}v_{ij}.

Replacing the quantity on the left-hand side of (
3.35
) in the last summand in (
3.34
), we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \partial_{s}[\mathrm{P}^{kl}(\mathrm{P}\owedge g)_{kijl}]_{s=0}&=-c^{2}((\tr^{h}v)h_{ij}-v_{ij})+ch^{kl}(\partial_{s}\mathrm{P}_{kl})h_{ij}-c\partial_{s}\mathrm{P}_{ij}\\
&+\frac{c}{2}\left(h^{kl}(\partial_{s}\mathrm{P}_{kl})\,h_{ij}+\frac{c}{2}\tr^{h}v\,h_{ij}+(n-2)\partial_{s}\mathrm{P}_{ij}+\frac{c(n-2)}{2}v_{ij}\right)\\
&=\left(-\frac{3c^{2}}{4}\tr^{h}v+\frac{3c}{2}h^{kl}\partial_{s}\mathrm{P}_{kl}\right)h_{ij}+\frac{c(n-4)}{2}\partial_{s}\mathrm{P}_{ij}+\frac{c^{2}(n+2)}{4}v_{ij},\end{aligned}

and so finally,

(3.36)

\displaystyle D[Q_{3}]_{ij}=\left(\frac{3c^{2}}{4}v_{p}^{\phantom{p}p}-\frac{3c}{2}h^{kl}\partial_{s}\mathrm{P}_{kl}\right)h_{ij}-\frac{c(n-4)}{2}\partial_{s}\mathrm{P}_{ij}-\frac{c^{2}(n+2)}{4}v_{ij}.

Adding (
3.31
), (
3.33
) and (
3.36
), and using Proposition
2.1
, we see that

\displaystyle D[Q_{1}+Q_{2}+Q_{3}]_{h}v_{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{n-2}v_{p\phantom{m},m\phantom{p}}^{\phantom{p}m\phantom{,m}p}h_{ij}-\frac{c}{n-2}v_{p\phantom{p},m\phantom{m}}^{\phantom{p}p\phantom{,m}m}h_{ij}+\frac{cn}{2(n-2)}(v_{ij,p}^{\phantom{ij,p}p}-v_{ip,\phantom{p}j}^{\phantom{ip,}p\phantom{j}}-v_{jp,\phantom{p}i}^{\phantom{jp,}p\phantom{i}}+v_{p\phantom{p},ji}^{\phantom{p}p\phantom{,ji}})

\displaystyle-\frac{c^{2}n}{n-2}v_{ij}+\frac{c^{2}}{n-2}v_{p}^{\phantom{p}p}h_{ij}.

∎

Combining the results above, we obtain the main result of this section:

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
g(s)
be a 1-parameter family of metrics with
h=g(0)
a metric with constant curvature
c
and
v=g^{\prime}(0)
. If
\frac{1}{n-2}L
is the linearization of the
right hand side of equation (
2.2
), then

\displaystyle Lv_{ij}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}v_{ij,p\phantom{p}m\phantom{m}}^{\phantom{ij,p}p\phantom{m}m}+\frac{c(n+2)}{2}v_{ij,p}^{\phantom{ij,p}p}-cv_{p\phantom{m},m\phantom{p}}^{\phantom{p}m\phantom{,m}p}h_{ij}-cv_{p\phantom{p},m\phantom{m}}^{\phantom{p}p\phantom{,m}m}h_{ij}

(3.37)

\displaystyle+\frac{c-2\mu}{2}v_{ip,\phantom{p}j}^{\phantom{ip,}p\phantom{j}}+\frac{c-2\mu}{2}v_{jp,\phantom{p}i}^{\phantom{jp,}p\phantom{i}}-\frac{c-4\nu}{2}v_{p\phantom{p},ji}^{\phantom{p}p\phantom{,ji}}+c^{2}v_{p}^{\phantom{p}p}h_{ij}-c^{2}nv_{ij}.

We note that for general values of
\mu
and
\nu
, the operator
L
is not formally self-adjoint because of the term involving
v_{p\phantom{m},m\phantom{p}}^{\phantom{p}m\phantom{,m}p}h_{ij}
. The following corollary readily follows from Theorem
3.5
:

Corollary 3.6
.

The
L^{2}
adjoint
L^{*}
of the operator
L
of equation (
3.5
) is

\displaystyle L^{*}v_{ij}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}v_{ij,p\phantom{p}m\phantom{m}}^{\phantom{ij,p}p\phantom{m}m}+\frac{c(n+2)}{2}v_{ij,p}^{\phantom{ij,p}p}-cv_{p\phantom{p},ij}^{\phantom{p}p\phantom{,ij}}-cv_{p\phantom{p},m\phantom{m}}^{\phantom{p}p\phantom{,m}m}h_{ij}

\displaystyle+\frac{c-2\mu}{2}v_{ip,\phantom{p}j}^{\phantom{ip,}p\phantom{j}}+\frac{c-2\mu}{2}v_{jp,\phantom{p}i}^{\phantom{jp,}p\phantom{i}}-\frac{c-4\nu}{2}v_{m\phantom{p},p\phantom{m}}^{\phantom{m}p\phantom{,p}m}h_{ij}+c^{2}v_{p}^{\phantom{p}p}h_{ij}-c^{2}nv_{ij}.

We now show that if
\mu=-c(n-1)/2
and
\nu=-c/4
, then
L
is formally self-adjoint.
Recalling the splitting of the bundle of symmetric
2
-tensors from §
2.5
, we obtain the following Proposition:

Proposition 3.7
.

Both
L
and
L^{*}
preserve the splitting (
2.24
). If
\mu=-c(n-1)/2
and
\nu=-c/4
, then the gauge vector is

(3.38)

\displaystyle Z=\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{h}\beta_{h}(g)-\frac{c(n-1)}{2}\delta_{h}g-\frac{c}{4}d(\tr^{h}g),

and the linearization of equation (
2.3
) at
h
is given by

\displaystyle Lv_{ij}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}v_{ij,p\phantom{p}m\phantom{m}}^{\phantom{ij,p}p\phantom{m}m}+\frac{c(n+2)}{2}v_{ij,p}^{\phantom{ij,p}p}-cv_{p\phantom{m},m\phantom{p}}^{\phantom{p}m\phantom{,m}p}h_{ij}-cv_{p\phantom{p},m\phantom{m}}^{\phantom{p}p\phantom{,m}m}h_{ij}

(3.39)

\displaystyle+\frac{cn}{2}v_{ip,\phantom{p}j}^{\phantom{ip,}p\phantom{j}}+\frac{cn}{2}v_{jp,\phantom{p}i}^{\phantom{jp,}p\phantom{i}}-cv_{p\phantom{p},ji}^{\phantom{p}p\phantom{,ji}}+c^{2}v_{p}^{\phantom{p}p}h_{ij}-c^{2}nv_{ij},

and
L
is formally self-adjoint. If
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}}
is a TT symmetric
2
-tensor and
f
is a smooth function, then

(3.40)

\displaystyle[L\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}}\,]_{ij}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}}}_{ij,p\phantom{p}m\phantom{m}}^{\phantom{ij,p}p\phantom{m}m}+\frac{c(n+2)}{2}{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}}}_{ij,p}^{\phantom{ij,p}p}-c^{2}n\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}}_{ij},

(3.41)

\displaystyle[L(fh)]_{ij}

\displaystyle=\left(-\frac{1}{2}{f,}_{p\phantom{p}m\phantom{m}}^{\phantom{p}p\phantom{m}m}-\frac{cn}{2}{f,}_{p}^{\phantom{p}p}\right)h_{ij}.

Proof.

If
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}}
is TT, then (for any choices of
\mu
and
\nu
)

(3.42)

\displaystyle[L\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}}\,]_{ij}=[L^{*}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}}\,]_{ij}=-\frac{1}{2}{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}}}_{ij,p\phantom{p}m\phantom{m}}^{\phantom{ij,p}p\phantom{m}m}+\frac{c(n+2)}{2}{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}}}_{ij,p}^{\phantom{ij,p}p}-c^{2}n\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}}_{ij}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}}}_{ij,p}^{\phantom{ij,p}pj}

\displaystyle={\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}}}_{ij,p}^{\phantom{ij,p}jp}+\R_{\phantom{pj}i\phantom{s}}^{pj\phantom{i}s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}}_{sj,p}+\R_{\phantom{pj}j\phantom{s}}^{pj\phantom{j}s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}}_{is,p}+\R_{\phantom{pj}p\phantom{s}}^{pj\phantom{p}s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}}_{ij,s}

\displaystyle={\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}}}_{ij,\phantom{j}p}^{\phantom{ij,}j\phantom{p}p}+(\R_{\phantom{pj}i\phantom{s}}^{pj\phantom{i}s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}}_{sj}+\R_{\phantom{pj}j\phantom{s}}^{pj\phantom{j}s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}}_{is})_{,p}

\displaystyle+\R_{\phantom{pj}i\phantom{s}}^{pj\phantom{i}s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}}_{sj,p}+\R_{\phantom{pj}j\phantom{s}}^{pj\phantom{j}s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}}_{is,p}+\R_{\phantom{pj}p\phantom{s}}^{pj\phantom{p}s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}}_{ij,s}.

It follows from the fact that that
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}}
is TT that the first term vanishes. Since
h
has constant curvature, then as a consequence of (
2.5
), all terms in this
formula which involve the curvature also vanish. Applying this result to
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}}_{ij}
and
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}}_{ij,p}^{\ \ \ \ p}
, we find that the divergence of
L\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}}
in (
3.42
) vanishes.
Finally, the expression (
3.42
) also shows that
L\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}}
is traceless, so
L\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}}
is TT. It follows immediately that
L\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}}
and
L^{*}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}}

are
L^{2}
orthogonal to both the conformal class
[h]
and to the range of the conformal Killing operator
K
. We recall once again that
up until now,
\mu
or
\nu
remain un-prescribed.

We now consider smooth multiples of the metric,
v_{ij}=fh_{ij}
. We calculate that

\displaystyle[L(fh)]_{ij}

\displaystyle=\left(-\frac{1}{2}{f,}_{p\phantom{p}m\phantom{m}}^{\phantom{p}p\phantom{m}m}-\frac{cn}{2}{f,}_{p}^{\phantom{p}p}\right)h_{ij}+\left((c-2\mu)-n\frac{c-4\nu}{2}\right)f_{,ij},

and also

\displaystyle[L^{*}(fh)]_{ij}

\displaystyle=\left(-\frac{1}{2}{f,}_{p\phantom{p}m\phantom{m}}^{\phantom{p}p\phantom{m}m}+\frac{2\nu-c(n-1)}{2}{f,}_{p}^{\phantom{p}p}\right)h_{ij}+\left((c-2\mu)-cn\right)f_{,ij}.

We next choose
\mu=-c(n-1)/2
and
\nu=-c/4
so that the coefficients of
f_{,ij}
on each line vanish. Then
L
and
L^{*}
preserve the bundle
of pure trace tensors, i.e., multiples of the metric, and
L(fh)=L^{*}(fh).
Since elements in the range of
K
and TT tensors are
both trace free, the bundle of multiples of the metric remains
L^{2}
orthogonal to the other summands in equation (
2.24
) also.

In conclusion, since multiples of the metric are
L^{2}
-orthogonal to the image of
K
, we have
(fh,LK\alpha)=(L^{*}(fh),K\alpha)=0
.
Since TT tensors are
L^{2}
-orthogonal to the image of
K
we have
(\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}},LK\alpha)=(L^{*}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}},K\alpha)=0
.
Therefore,
L
maps the range of
K
to itself. Hence we have seen that for the given choices of
\mu
and
\nu
,
L
and
L^{*}
preserve the
splitting (
2.24
), and
L
is formally self-adjoint.
∎

4.
Linear stability at constant curvature metrics

We now carry out the
L^{2}
spectral analysis of the operator
L
from Proposition
3.7
. Here is the strategy. By Proposition
3.7
and the completeness of
M
, we have that the operator
L
is self-adjoint, and hence its spectrum lies in
\mathbb{R}
. To show that the spectrum of
L
is nonpositive,
it suffices to find a constant
a\geq 0
such that
(v,Lv)\leq-a\,\|v\|^{2}_{L^{2}}
if
v\in C^{\infty}_{0}
. This estimate is verified using some involved but straightforward
arguments.

In the following, we treat the cases
c=0,c>0
and
c<0
in order of increasing difficulty.

4.1.
Case 1: Spectral estimate if
c=0

Theorem 4.1
.

For
n\geq 4
, suppose that
(M^{n},h)
is a flat orientable manifold. Then for any compactly supported smooth symmetric
2
-tensor
v
,

(v,Lv)\leq 0.

Moreover if
M
is compact, then the kernel of
L
is finite dimensional and consists of parallel symmetric
2
-tensors.

Proof.

The finite dimensionality is immediate since
M
is compact. Since
h
is flat and since covariant derivatives commute, the expression for
L
in Proposition
3.7
becomes simply

Lv=(\nabla^{*}\nabla)^{2}v
.

If
Lv=0
, then
\nabla^{2}v=0
. Then taking the trace,
\nabla^{*}\nabla v=0
, and thus finally,
(v,\nabla^{*}\nabla v)=\|\nabla v\|^{2}
, so that

v
is parallel.
∎

4.2.
Case 2: Spectral estimate if
c=1

This case requires a finer analysis using a decomposition of the space of symmetric
2
-tensors. Since the gauge-adjusted flow preserves volume,
we may restrict to infinitesimal variations
v
for which
\int_{M}\tr^{h}v\,\dvol_{h}=0
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose that
(M^{n},h)
,
n\geq 4
, is a compact orientable manifold with constant sectional curvature
c=1
(thus
M
is either the sphere
S^{n}
or
a lens space
S^{n}/\Gamma
). Let
\pi_{\mathrm{im}(K)}
and
\pi_{\mathrm{TT}}
be the
L^{2}
orthogonal projections consisting of the closed subspaces

\mathrm{im}(K)
and of the TT tensors. Then for any smooth symmetric
2
-tensor
v
,

(v,Lv)\leq-n\left(\|\pi_{\mathrm{im}(K)}v\|^{2}+\|\pi_{\mathrm{TT}}v\|^{2}\right).

Consequently the kernel of
L
is the
(n+1)
-dimensional space
\{v=fh,\ \Delta_{h}f+nf=0\}
.

Proof.

We decompose an arbitrary element
v\in\Sigma^{2}(T^{*}M)
as

v=K\alpha+fh+\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}},

where
\alpha
is a
1
-form,
f
is a function and
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}}
is a transverse-traceless 2-tensor. Since
L
preserves this orthogonal splitting, we have

\displaystyle(v,Lv)=(K\alpha,LK\alpha)+(fh,L(fh))+(\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}},L\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}}).

Each term is estimated separately below:

First, we write

\displaystyle(fh,L(fh))=(f,-\frac{n}{2}\Delta^{2}f-\frac{n^{2}}{2}\Delta f)=-\frac{n}{2}(f,\Delta^{2}f)+\frac{n^{2}}{2}||\nabla f||^{2}.

Next, we substitute in the identities

\Delta^{2}f=(\nabla^{*}\nabla)^{2}f=(\nabla^{*})^{2}\nabla^{2}f-(n-1)\Delta f,\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \ \nabla^{2}f=(\nabla^{2}f)^{\circ}+\frac{1}{n}\Delta f\,h

to determine that

\displaystyle(fh,L(fh))

\displaystyle=-\frac{n}{2}||\nabla^{2}f||^{2}+\frac{n}{2}\left(-\frac{1}{n-1}||\nabla^{2}f||^{2}+\frac{1}{n-1}||\Delta f||^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{n^{2}}{2(n-1)}||\nabla^{2}f||^{2}+\frac{n}{2(n-1)}||\Delta f||^{2}=-\frac{n^{2}}{2(n-1)}||(\nabla^{2}f)^{\circ}||^{2}\leq 0.

By (
3.41
),
L(fh)=0
implies that
(\Delta+n)(\Delta f)=0
; hence either
\Delta f=0
, i.e.,
f
is constant,
or else
\Delta f+nf
is a constant. Since
\int f=0
, we conclude that
(\Delta+n)f=0
. Thus if
M=S^{n}
, then

f
lies in the
(n+1)
-dimensional eigenspace corresponding to the first nontrivial eigenvalue on
S^{n}
, while
if
M=S^{n}/\Gamma
, then
f
is identified with a
\Gamma
-invariant element of this same eigenspace on
S^{n}
.
For many finite groups
\Gamma\subset\mathrm{SO}(n)
, there are no
\Gamma
-invariant elements in this
eigenspace, hence no solutions to
L(fh)=0
on such lens spaces.

Next consider a traceless symmetric tensor
v
. Applying (
3.7
) we find that

\displaystyle(v,Lv)

\displaystyle=(v,-\tfrac{1}{2}(\nabla^{*}\nabla)^{2}v-\frac{n+2}{2}\nabla^{*}\nabla v-n\delta\delta^{*}v-nv)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla^{*}\nabla v\|^{2}-\frac{n+2}{2}\|\nabla v\|^{2}-n\|\delta v\|^{2}-n\|v\|^{2}.

We apply this in two ways. First, if
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}}
is TT, then
L\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}}=0
implies
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}}=0
. If
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}}
is orthogonal to the kernel of
L
, then dropping negative terms implies that
(\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}},L\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}})\leq-n\|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}}\|^{2}
. Second, if
v=K\alpha
and
Lv=0
, then

v=K\alpha=0
, and more generally,
(K\alpha,LK\alpha)\leq-n\|K\alpha\|^{2}
.

This proves the spectral bound in this case.
∎

4.3.
Case 3: Spectral estimate if
c=-1

This case is considerably more involved than the other two. As above, we may decompose an arbitrary compactly supported symmetric 2-tensor as

v=K\alpha+fh+\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}}
, so that

(4.43)

\displaystyle(v,Lv)=(K\alpha,LK\alpha)+(fh,L(fh))+(\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}},L\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}}).

We consider each of these terms in turn.

We begin with
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}}
, which we write for the duration of this estimation as
v
for simplicity. Proposition
3.7
shows that

\displaystyle(v,Lv)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}(-\frac{1}{2}v^{ij}v_{ij,p\phantom{p}m\phantom{m}}^{\phantom{ij,p}p\phantom{m}m}-\frac{n+2}{2}v^{ij}v_{ij,p}^{\phantom{ij,p}p}-nv^{ij}v_{ij})dV_{h}

(4.44)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta v\|^{2}+\frac{n+2}{2}\|\nabla v\|^{2}-n\|v\|^{2}.

Using the expression (
B.77
) from Appendix
B
, we find that

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta v\|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}v^{ij}(v_{ij,mp}^{\phantom{ij,mp}pm}-4nv_{ij}+4v_{p}^{\phantom{p}p}h_{ij}+(n-1)v_{ij,p}^{\phantom{ij,p}p})\,\dvol_{h}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla^{2}v\|^{2}+2n\|v\|^{2}+\frac{n-1}{2}\|\nabla v\|^{2}.

Inserting this into equation (
4.3
), we obtain

(4.45)

\displaystyle(v,Lv)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla^{2}v\|^{2}+\frac{2n+1}{2}\|\nabla v\|^{2}+n\|v\|^{2}.

We next rewrite the norm of the Hessian term using a generalization of Koiso’s Bochner formula (see
[
16
]
).

Lemma 4.3
.

Using the notation from above, we define

(4.46)

\displaystyle T_{ijk}:=v_{ij,k}-v_{jk,i}.

Then

(4.47)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|T\|^{2}=\|\nabla v\|^{2}-\|\delta v\|^{2}-n\|v\|^{2}+\|\tr^{h}v\|^{2}.

Proof.

We calculate that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|T\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}(v^{ij,k}-v^{jk,i})(v_{ij,k}-v_{jk,i})\,\dvol_{h}

\displaystyle=\|\nabla v\|^{2}+\int_{M}v^{jk}v_{ij,k}^{\phantom{ij,k}i}\,\dvol_{h}+\|\nabla v\|^{2}+\int_{M}v^{jk}v_{ij,k}^{\phantom{ij,k}i}\,\dvol_{h}.

Commuting derivatives and using that
h
has constant negative curvature results in

\displaystyle v_{ij,k}^{\phantom{ij,k}i}=v_{ij,\phantom{i}k}^{\phantom{ij,}i\phantom{k}}+\R^{\phantom{k}i\phantom{i}s}_{k\phantom{i}i}v_{sj}+\R^{\phantom{k}i\phantom{j}s}_{k\phantom{i}j}v_{is}=v_{ij,\phantom{i}k}^{\phantom{ij,}i\phantom{k}}-nv_{jk}+v_{p}^{\phantom{p}p}h_{jk},

and substituting this into the preceding expression, we are led to the following identity

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|T\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\nabla v\|^{2}+\int_{M}v^{jk}(v_{ij,\phantom{i}k}^{\phantom{ij,}i\phantom{k}}-nv_{jk}+v_{p}^{\phantom{p}p}h_{jk})\,\dvol_{h}

\displaystyle=\|\nabla v\|^{2}-\|\delta v\|^{2}-n\|v\|^{2}+\|\tr^{h}v\|^{2}.

∎

Inspired by this calculation, we now define

(4.48)

\displaystyle A:=\nabla T.

Lemma 4.4
.

If
v
is a compactly supported traceless tensor
v
, then

\displaystyle\|A\|^{2}

\displaystyle=2\|\nabla^{2}v\|^{2}-2\|\nabla\delta v\|^{2}-4n\|\delta v\|^{2}-2(n+2)\|\nabla v\|^{2}-2(n^{2}+n)\|v\|^{2}.

Proof.

We first observe that

\displaystyle\|A\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}A^{ijkm}A_{ijkm}\,\dvol_{h}=\int_{M}(v^{ij,km}-v^{jk,im})(v_{ij,km}-v_{jk,im})\,\dvol_{h}

(4.49)

\displaystyle=2\|\nabla^{2}v\|^{2}-2\int_{M}v^{ij}v_{jk,i\phantom{m}m\phantom{k}}^{\phantom{jk,i}m\phantom{m}k}\,\dvol_{h}.

A lengthy but straightforward calculation which involves commuting derivatives, with details appearing in Appendix
B
,
yields (
B.3
). Inserting this into equation (
4.3
) and integrating by parts results in the equation

\displaystyle\|A\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}A^{ijkm}A_{ijkm}\dvol_{h}=\int_{M}(v^{ij,km}-v^{jk,im})(v_{ij,km}-v_{jk,im})\dvol_{h}

\displaystyle=2\|\nabla^{2}v\|^{2}-2\|\nabla\delta v\|^{2}-4n\|\delta v\|^{2}-2(n+2)\|\nabla v\|^{2}-2(n^{2}+n)\|v\|^{2}.

∎

We now return to equation (
4.45
). If
v
is TT, this becomes

(4.50)

\displaystyle(v,Lv)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}\|A\|^{2}+\frac{(n-1)}{2}\|\nabla v\|^{2}-\frac{n(n-1)}{2}\|v\|^{2},

which by (
4.47
) is equivalent to

(4.51)

\displaystyle(v,Lv)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}(\|A\|^{2}-(n-1)\|T\|^{2}).

It thus remains to show that
\|A\|^{2}\geq(n-1)\|T\|^{2}
if
v
is TT, which we now do.

Proposition 4.5
.

Using the notation from above, we have

\displaystyle\|A\|^{2}\geq(n-1)\|T\|^{2},

and hence

(4.52)

\displaystyle(v,Lv)\leq-\frac{(n-2)}{4}\|T\|^{2}.

The kernel of
L
consists of TT tensors
v
for which
T=0
.

Proof.

Using the symmetry of
v
, we obtain

T_{ijk}=v_{ij,k}-v_{jk,i}=v_{ij,k}-v_{kj,i}.

This is skew-symmetric in
i
and
k
; hence it may be considered as a
2
-form taking values in
T^{*}M
:

T=(T_{ijk}\;dx^{i}\wedge dx^{k})\otimes dx^{j}.

We now consider the Hodge Laplacian acting on
T^{*}M
-valued
2
-forms. We denote by

\displaystyle d^{\nabla}:\Omega^{p}(T^{*}M)\otimes\Gamma(T^{*}M)\longrightarrow\Omega^{p+1}(T^{*}M)\otimes\Gamma(T^{*}M)

the exterior derivative on
\Omega^{*}(M)
coupled to the Levi-Civita connection on
T^{*}M
. We write this more explicitly only
for
p=1,2
. On
2
-forms, we have

\displaystyle d^{\nabla}T

\displaystyle=(d^{\nabla}T)_{ijkl}\;dx^{l}\wedge dx^{i}\wedge dx^{k}\otimes dx^{j},

\displaystyle[d^{\nabla}T]_{ijkl}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}(\nabla_{i}T_{kjl}+\nabla_{k}T_{lji}+\nabla_{l}T_{ijk})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}(T_{kjl,i}+T_{lji,k}+T_{ijk,l}).

Likewise, on
1
-forms
\eta=\eta_{ij}dx^{i}\otimes dx^{j}
,

\displaystyle d^{\nabla}\eta

\displaystyle=(d^{\nabla}\eta)_{ijk}\;dx^{k}\wedge dx^{i}\otimes dx^{j},

\displaystyle[d^{\nabla}\eta]_{ijk}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\eta_{kj,i}-\eta_{ij,k}).

The associated
L^{2}
inner product is

\displaystyle(\eta,\zeta)=\int_{M}p!\,\eta^{k_{1}\ell k_{2}\cdots k_{p}}\zeta_{k_{1}\ell k_{2}\cdots k_{p}}\,\dvol_{h}

where indices are raised using
h
. We note that these
T^{*}M
-valued
p
-forms such as
\eta
and
\zeta
, denoted e.g.,
\zeta_{k_{1}\ell k_{2}\ldots k_{p}}
, are fully skew in the indices
(k_{1},\ldots,k_{p})
only,
but not in the remaining index
\ell
.
Using this inner product we may determine the adjoint differentials for these quantities. For example, if
\eta
is a
1
-form and
\omega
is a
2
-form,
both valued in
T^{*}M
, then

\displaystyle(d^{\nabla}\eta,\omega)

\displaystyle:=\int 2!\,(d^{\nabla}\eta)_{ijk}\omega^{ijk}\,\dvol_{h}

\displaystyle=\int(\eta_{kj,i}-\eta_{ij,k})\omega^{ijk}\,\dvol_{h}

\displaystyle=\int-\eta_{kj}{\omega^{ijk}}_{,i}-\eta_{ij}{\omega^{kji}}_{,k}\,\dvol_{h}\;\;\;\mbox{(since $\omega$ is a 2-form)}

\displaystyle=\int 1!\;\eta_{ij}(-2{\omega^{kji}}_{,k})\,\dvol_{h}

\displaystyle=(\eta,(d^{\nabla})^{*}\omega),

where
[(d^{\nabla})^{*}\omega]_{ij}=-2{\omega_{kji,}}^{k}
.

Analogously, if
T
is a
2
-form and
\omega
a
3
-form, both valued in
T^{*}M
, then

\displaystyle(d^{\nabla}T,\omega)

\displaystyle:=\int 3!\,(d^{\nabla}T)_{ijkl}\omega^{ijkl}\,\dvol_{h}

\displaystyle=\int 2!\;(T_{kjl,i}+T_{lji,k}+T_{ijk,l})\omega^{ijkl}\,\dvol_{h}\;\;\;\mbox{(note the cancellation of 1/3)}

\displaystyle=\int 2!\;(-T_{kjl}{\omega^{ijkl}}_{,i}-T_{lji}{\omega^{kjli}}_{,k}-T_{ijk}{\omega^{ljik}}_{,l})\,\dvol_{h}\;\;\;\mbox{(using that $\omega$ is a 3-form)}

\displaystyle=\int 2!\;T_{ijk}\,(-3{\omega^{ljik}}_{,l})\,\dvol_{h}

\displaystyle=(T,(d^{\nabla})^{*}\omega),

where
[(d^{\nabla})^{*}\omega]_{ijk}=-3{\omega_{ljik,}}^{l}
.

For a
2
-form
T
,

\displaystyle[(d^{\nabla})^{*}d^{\nabla}T]_{ijk}=-3h^{ml}\nabla_{m}[d^{\nabla}T]_{ljik}=-3[d^{\nabla}T]_{ljik,}^{\phantom{ljik,}l}=-{T_{ijk,l}}^{l}-{T_{kjl,i}}^{l}-{T_{lji,k}}^{l},

and

\displaystyle[d^{\nabla}(d^{\nabla})^{*}T]_{ijk}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}([(d^{\nabla})^{*}T]_{kj,i}-[(d^{\nabla})^{*}T]_{ij,k})=\frac{1}{2}((-2{T_{ljk,}}^{l})_{i}-(-2{T_{lji,}}^{l})_{k})

\displaystyle=-{T^{l}}_{jk,li}+{T^{l}}_{ji,lk}.

We can thus compute
\Delta^{\nabla}T:=-(d^{\nabla}(d^{\nabla})^{*}+(d^{\nabla})^{*}d^{\nabla})T
, using a Bochner-Weizenböck type formula for
\Delta^{\nabla}
as discussed in
[
6
]
.

To start, we group terms as

\displaystyle(\Delta^{\nabla}T)_{ijk}

\displaystyle={T_{ijk,l}}^{l}+{T_{kjl,i}}^{l}+{T_{lji,k}}^{l}+{T^{l}}_{jk,li}-{T^{l}}_{ji,lk}

\displaystyle={T_{ijk,l}}^{l}+{T_{kjl,i}}^{l}-T_{kjl,\phantom{l}i}^{\phantom{kjl,}l}+{T_{lji,k}}^{l}-T_{lji,\phantom{l}k}^{\phantom{lji,}l}.

Next, we commute derivatives, and we use the fact that since
v
is transverse-traceless,
T
is traceless over all indices, to obtain

\displaystyle{T_{kjl,i}}^{l}-T_{kjl,\phantom{l}i}^{\phantom{kjl,}l}

\displaystyle=\R^{\phantom{i}l\phantom{k}s}_{i\phantom{l}k}T_{sjl}+\R^{\phantom{i}l\phantom{j}s}_{i\phantom{l}j}T_{ksl}+\R^{\phantom{i}l\phantom{l}s}_{i\phantom{l}l}T_{kjs}=c(n-2)T_{ijk}-T_{kij},

and

\displaystyle{T_{lji,k}}^{l}-T_{lji,\phantom{l}k}^{\phantom{lji,}l}

\displaystyle=\R^{\phantom{k}l\phantom{k}s}_{k\phantom{l}k}T_{sji}+\R^{\phantom{k}l\phantom{j}s}_{k\phantom{l}j}T_{lsi}+\R^{\phantom{k}l\phantom{i}s}_{k\phantom{l}i}T_{ljs}=(n-2)T_{ijk}-T_{jki}.

Using the fact that
T_{ijk}+T_{jki}+T_{kij}=0
, we have

\displaystyle(\Delta^{\nabla}T)_{ijk}

\displaystyle={T_{ijk,l}}^{l}+(2n-3)T_{ijk}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle-\|d^{\nabla}T\|^{2}-\|(d^{\nabla})^{*}T\|^{2}=(T,\Delta^{\nabla}T)

\displaystyle=\int 2!\,(T^{ijk}{T_{ijk,l}}^{l}+(2n-3)T^{ijk}T_{ijk})\,\dvol_{h}

\displaystyle=-2\|\nabla T\|^{2}+2(2n-3)\|T\|^{2},

hence

\displaystyle\|\nabla T\|^{2}-(2n-3)\|T\|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\|d^{\nabla}T\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|(d^{\nabla})^{*}T\|^{2},

or

\displaystyle\|\nabla T\|^{2}-(n-1)\|T\|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\|d^{\nabla}T\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|(d^{\nabla})^{*}T\|^{2}+(n-2)\|T\|^{2}.

Returning to (
4.51
), and using the above results we arrive at

\displaystyle(v,Lv)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}(\|\nabla T\|^{2}-(n-1)\|T\|^{2})

(4.53)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{8}(\|d^{\nabla}T\|^{2}+\|(d^{\nabla})^{*}T\|^{2})-\frac{(n-2)}{4}\|T\|^{2}.

Finally, if

.
∎

We now turn to symmetric

\displaystyle LK\alpha=-\frac{1}{2}(\nabla^{*}\nabla)^{2}K\alpha+\frac{(n+2)}{2}\nabla^{*}\nabla K\alpha+nK\delta K\alpha-nK\alpha.

In the next Proposition we rewrite

as a sum of nonpositive terms. In preparation, we state some useful identities.
First, we recall the standard Weitzenböck identity relating the trace Laplacian and the Hodge Laplacian on
1

(4.55)

\displaystyle\nabla^{*}\nabla\alpha=-\Delta_{H}\alpha+(n-1)\alpha.

Next, combining (

, with

\displaystyle\nabla^{*}\nabla K\alpha

\displaystyle=K\nabla^{*}\nabla\alpha+(n+1)K\alpha,

\displaystyle K\nabla^{*}\nabla\alpha

\displaystyle=-K\Delta_{H}\alpha+(n-1)K\alpha.

Finally, on traceless symmetric

\displaystyle K^{*}K\alpha=\delta(\delta^{*}\alpha+\frac{1}{n}(d^{*}\alpha)\,h)=\delta\delta^{*}\alpha-\frac{1}{n}d\alpha.

We readily calculate that

(4.57)

\displaystyle\delta^{*}\alpha=\nabla\alpha-\frac{1}{2}d\alpha\ \ \Longrightarrow\ \ \delta\delta^{*}\alpha=\delta\nabla\alpha-\frac{1}{2}d^{*}d\alpha=\nabla^{*}\nabla\alpha-\frac{1}{2}d^{*}d\alpha.

Using (

\displaystyle K^{*}K\alpha=-\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{H}\alpha+\frac{n-2}{2n}dd^{*}\alpha+(n-1)\alpha.

Proposition 4.6
.

For a symmetric
2

\displaystyle(K\alpha,LK\alpha)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}||dd^{*}d\alpha||^{2}-\frac{n-1}{2n}||d^{*}dd^{*}\alpha||^{2}-(n-1)||\Delta_{H}\alpha||^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad-(n-1)^{2}||d\alpha||^{2}-\frac{n(n-1)}{2}||d^{*}\alpha||^{2}.

In particular

Proof.

First, it follows from equation (

\displaystyle(K\alpha,LK\alpha)

\displaystyle=(K\alpha,-\frac{1}{2}(\nabla^{*}\nabla)^{2}K\alpha+\frac{n+2}{2}\nabla^{*}\nabla K\alpha+nK\delta K\alpha-nK\alpha)

(4.60)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}||\nabla^{*}\nabla K\alpha||^{2}+\frac{n+2}{2}(\nabla^{*}\nabla K\alpha,K\alpha)+n||K^{*}K\alpha||^{2}-n||K\alpha||^{2}

(4.61)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}||K\nabla^{*}\nabla\alpha||^{2}-\frac{n}{2}(\nabla^{*}\nabla\alpha,K^{*}K\alpha)+n||K^{*}K\alpha||^{2}-\frac{n-1}{2}||K\alpha||^{2},

where the last equality relies on (
4.56
) and some algebraic simplification.

It follows from the second part of equation (

) that

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\|K\nabla^{*}\nabla\alpha\|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\|K\Delta_{H}\alpha-(n-1)K\alpha\|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}(K^{*}K\Delta_{H}\alpha,\Delta_{H}\alpha)+(n-1)(K^{*}K\alpha,\Delta_{H}\alpha)-\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{2}||K\alpha||^{2},

and

\displaystyle-\frac{n}{2}(\nabla^{*}\nabla\alpha,K^{*}K\alpha)=\frac{n}{2}(\Delta_{H}\alpha,K^{*}K\alpha)-\frac{n(n-1)}{2}||K\alpha||^{2};

hence inserting these into (

\displaystyle(K\alpha,LK\alpha)=-\frac{1}{2}(K^{*}K\Delta_{H}\alpha,\Delta_{H}\alpha)+\frac{3n-2}{2}(\Delta_{H}\alpha,K^{*}K\alpha)+n||K^{*}K\alpha||^{2}-n(n-1)||K\alpha||^{2}.

Writing the four terms on the right-hand side as

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle:=-\frac{1}{2}((-\tfrac{1}{2}\Delta_{H}+\frac{n-2}{2n}dd^{*}+(n-1))\Delta_{H}\alpha,\Delta_{H}\alpha)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}(\Delta^{2}_{H}\alpha,\Delta_{H}\alpha)-\frac{n-2}{4n}(dd^{*}\Delta_{H}\alpha,\Delta_{H}\alpha)-\frac{n-1}{2}||\Delta_{H}\alpha||^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}||dd^{*}d\alpha||^{2}-\frac{n-1}{2n}||d^{*}dd^{*}\alpha||^{2}-\frac{n-1}{2}||\Delta_{H}\alpha||^{2};

\displaystyle II

\displaystyle:=\frac{3n-2}{2}(\Delta_{H}\alpha,-\tfrac{1}{2}\Delta_{H}\alpha+\frac{n-2}{2n}dd*\alpha+(n-1)\alpha)

\displaystyle=-\frac{3n-2}{4}||\Delta_{H}\alpha||^{2}-\frac{(n-2)(3n-2)}{4n}||dd^{*}\alpha||^{2}-\frac{(3n-2)(n-1)}{2}(||d\alpha||^{2}+||d^{*}\alpha||^{2});

\displaystyle III

\displaystyle:=n||-\tfrac{1}{2}\Delta_{H}\alpha+\frac{n-2}{2n}dd^{*}\alpha+(n-1)\alpha||^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{4}||\Delta_{H}\alpha||^{2}+\frac{(n-2)^{2}}{4n}||dd^{*}\alpha||^{2}+n(n-1)^{2}||\alpha||^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{n-2}{2}(\Delta_{H}\alpha,dd^{*}\alpha)-n(n-1)(\Delta_{H}\alpha,\alpha)+(n-1)(n-2)(dd^{*}\alpha,\alpha)

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{4}||\Delta_{H}\alpha||^{2}+\frac{(n-2)(3n-2)}{4n}||dd^{*}\alpha||^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+n(n-1)||d\alpha||^{2}+2(n-1)^{2}||d^{*}\alpha||^{2}+n(n-1)^{2}||\alpha||^{2};

and

\displaystyle IV

\displaystyle:=-n(n-1)(-\tfrac{1}{2}\Delta_{H}\alpha+\frac{n-2}{2n}dd^{*}\alpha+(n-1)\alpha,\alpha)

\displaystyle=-\frac{n(n-1)}{2}||d\alpha||^{2}-(n-1)^{2}||d^{*}\alpha||^{2}-n(n-1)^{2}||\alpha||^{2}.

Collecting all of these terms, we find that

\displaystyle(K\alpha,LK\alpha)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}||dd^{*}d\alpha||^{2}-\frac{n-1}{2n}||d^{*}dd^{*}\alpha||^{2}-(n-1)||\Delta_{H}\alpha||^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad-(n-1)^{2}||d\alpha||^{2}-\frac{n(n-1)}{2}||d^{*}\alpha||^{2}.

This proves that

. If

with

is an
L^{2}
harmonic
1

∎

Spectral bounds on hyperbolic space:

4.4.
Spectral bounds on Poincaré-Einstein space

We conclude this section with the spectral bounds and linear stability for the operator on Poincaré-Einstein spaces which are small perturbations of hyperbolic space. We recall that a metric
g
on a compact manifold with boundary

M
is called conformally compact if it takes the form
g=\rho^{-2}\bar{g}
, where
\rho
is a defining function for
\partial M
and
\bar{g}
is a smooth metric up to
\partial M
. The prototype is
the hyperbolic metric on the ball. The space
(M,g)
is called Poincaré-Einstein if
g
is conformally compact and Einstein, so that
\mathrm{Ric}\,g=-(n-1)g
. Any Poincaré-Einstein metric
has a ‘boundary value’ called its conformal infinity. This is the conformal class
\mathfrak{c}(g)=[\rho^{2}g|_{T\partial M}]
(note that since
\rho
and
\bar{g}
are only defined up to a nonvanishing
scalar factor, this boundary value is also defined only up to such a factor, or in other words, only its conformal class is well-defined). A now classical result by Graham and Lee
[
9
]

states that any conformal class
\mathfrak{c}
sufficiently close to the standard round conformal class
\mathfrak{c}_{0}
on
S^{n-1}
is the conformal infinity of a unique Poincaré-Einstein
metric
g
which is uniformly close to the hyperbolic metric.

Corollary 4.9
.

Let
g
be a Poincaré-Einstein metric on the ball
\mathbb{B}^{n}
which is sufficiently uniformly close to the hyperbolic metric
h
as stated above, and with conformal infinity
\mathfrak{c}(g)
any sufficiently
smooth conformal class near to the round conformal class on
S^{n-1}
. Then there exists a constant
a(n,\epsilon)>0
such that

(v,Lv)\leq-a(n,\epsilon)||v||^{2}

for any
L^{2}
symmetric
2
-tensor on
M
, where
L
is the linearized gauge-adjusted Bach operator computed at the Poincaré-Einstein metric
g
.

This follows immediately since the coefficients of
L
and the
L^{2}
inner product depend smoothly on the metric
g
, so small changes in the metric in a
\mathcal{C}^{k,\alpha}
norm with respect to
h
for sufficiently large
k
result in only small changes in this spectral bound.

5.
Dynamical Stability of the Bach flow

In this section we discuss one way to pass from linear stability to dynamical stability for the gauge-adjusted Bach flow on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. This
method has been discussed in detail elsewhere for Ricci flow (see
[
10
]
and
[
13
]
), as well as for the ambient obstruction flow,
[
4
]
,
so we restrict ourselves to sketching the main points here.

First, as we recalled at the end of the last section, the well-known result of Graham and Lee
[
9
]
states that there is an infinite dimensional family of Poincaré-Einstein metrics
which arise as deformations of the hyperbolic metric. These are parametrized by their conformal infinities, i.e., conformal classes on
S^{n-1}

near to the standard one. Each such metric is a fixed point of the Bach flow. The existence of this family means that we should only expect a dynamical stability result for
the hyperbolic metric amongst metrics which decay asymptotically to
h
, and hence all share the same conformal infinity. We thus consider metrics
g
such that
g-h

lies in some weighted (little) Hölder space, as in
[
3
,
4
]
.

Following
[
3
]
, let
M
be the open unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{n}
with linear coordinates
x=(x^{1},\ldots,x^{n})
, and write
\rho=\frac{1-|x|^{2}}{2}
.
The hyperbolic metric thus takes the form
h=\rho^{-2}|dx|^{2}
. We consider the space
\mathcal{C}^{k,\alpha}(M,\Sigma^{2}(T^{*}M),h)
consisting of
limits with respect to the Hölder norm of order
(k+\alpha)
,
\|\cdot\|_{k+\alpha}
of smooth compactly supported symmetric
2
-tensors with respect to
h
. We also define the weighted space

(5.66)

\mathcal{C}^{k,\alpha}_{\mu}(M,\Sigma^{2}(T^{*}M),h)=\rho^{\mu}\mathcal{C}^{k,\alpha}(M,\Sigma^{2}(T^{*}M),h),

and observe that if
g
is a metric such that
g-h\in\mathcal{C}^{k,\alpha}_{\mu}(M,h)
for some
\mu>0
, then the conformal infinity of
g
is the
standard one. For simplicity, we omit explicit mention of the tensor bundle in the notation below.

Write the gauge-adjusted Bach flow (
2.3
) as
\partial g/\partial t=F(g)
. Thus
F(h)=0
, and
DF_{h}=L
is
the operator in Proposition
3.7
(with
c=-1
). This linearization
L
is a fourth-order uniformly degenerate elliptic operator, and
is admissible in the sense of
[
3
, Definition 2.0]
; thus

L:\rho^{\mu}\mathcal{C}^{k+4,\alpha}(M,h)\longrightarrow\rho^{\mu}\mathcal{C}^{k,\alpha}(M,h)

is bounded. Assuming that
||g-h||_{\mathcal{C}^{k+4,\alpha}_{\mu}}
is sufficiently small, we can expand
F
in a Taylor series as

\displaystyle F(g)=F(h+v)=F(h)+Lv+Q_{h}v,

where
v=g-h
and
Q_{h}
is a polynomial in
h
,
h^{-1}
and up to three covariant derivatives of
v
, with
Q_{h}
vanishing quadratically as
v\to 0
.
It is straightforward to check that

\displaystyle F:\rho^{\mu}\mathcal{C}^{4+k,\alpha}(M,h)\longrightarrow\rho^{\mu}\mathcal{C}^{k,\alpha}(M,h)

is a
\mathcal{C}^{1}
map between these two Banach spaces. Furthermore, referring to
[
3
]
again, we determine that
L
is sectorial on
\mathcal{C}^{k,\alpha}_{\mu}(M,h)

if
\mu>0
. Since sectoriality is an open condition,
DF_{g}
is sectorial for
g
sufficiently close to
h
as well.

To apply the asymptotic stability result in Lunardi’s book
[
17
]
, we must bound the spectrum of
L
acting on these weighted Hölder spaces. In other words,
we must transfer what we know about the
L^{2}
spectral bounds to, effectively, bounds for the weighted Hölder spectrum.
We have shown in Theorem
4.8
that the
L^{2}
spectrum of
L
at
h
is a ray
(-\infty,-b]
for some constant
b\geq a(n)>0
.

We now explain how, using the structure of the Green function for
L
, we can obtain a suitable bound for the
\mathcal{C}^{k,\alpha}_{\mu}
spectrum.
This same argument has been applied in
[
3
]
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Fix any weight
\mu\in(\frac{n-1}{2}-r(n),\frac{n-1}{2}+r(n))
, where
r(n)>0
is a constant depending on
n
. Then there exists a constant
a(n)
such that the resolvent set for
L
acting on
\mathcal{C}^{k,\alpha}_{\mu}

is contained in a half-plane
\mathrm{Re}\;\lambda>-a(n)
. Thus

\mathrm{spec}_{\mathcal{C}^{k,\alpha}_{\mu}(M,h)}(L)\subset\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\mathrm{Re}\;\lambda\leq-a(n)\}.

We note for example that the values
r(n)=\frac{n-1}{8}
and
a(n)=\frac{(n-2)(3n-11)(5n-13)}{128}
suffice (see Remark
A.3
).

Proof.

If
\lambda\not\in\mathrm{spec}_{L^{2}}(L)
, then
\lambda-L:L^{2}\to L^{2}
has a bounded inverse. Consider the Schwartz kernel of
R_{\lambda}(L)=(\lambda-L)^{-1}
as a distribution on
M\times M
. Following
[
18
]
, this Schwartz kernel is best understood as a polyhomogeneous distribution on
(\mathbb{B}^{n})^{2}_{0}
, which is by definition the blowup
of the product space
(\mathbb{B}^{n})^{2}
along the boundary of its diagonal. This blowup is called the
0
-double space of the ball, and is obtained by replacing each
point of the boundary of the diagonal of
(\mathbb{B}^{n})^{2}
with the set of inward-pointing unit normal vectors. The entire boundary of the diagonal
\partial\mathrm{diag}((\mathbb{B}^{n})^{2})

is replaced by its inward-pointing spherical normal bundle. This process creates a new front face, and the diagonal of
(\mathbb{B}^{n})^{2}

lifts to this new space as a submanifold whose normal bundle has a uniform product structure up to the boundary. The resolvent
R_{\lambda}(L)

is an element of the
0
-calculus of pseudodifferential operators, which means that its Schwartz kernel has a classical pseudodifferential singularity of order
-2
along
this lifted diagonal, and has asymptotic expansions up to all boundary faces. These polyhomogeneous expansions are described in terms
of their index sets, which are the collection of exponents appearing as powers of the defining functions for these faces in the expansions.
The mapping properties of
R_{\lambda}(L)
are determined solely by these features (i.e., the singularity along the diagonal, and the orders
of vanishing or blowup of the Schwartz kernel at the various boundary faces). We refer to
[
18
]
for more careful descriptions of
all of this, and simply quote the relevant mapping properties needed here.

The expansions of
R_{\lambda}(L)
at the ‘left’ and ‘right’ faces (these are the lifts of
(\partial\mathbb{B}^{n})\times\mathbb{B}^{n}
and
\mathbb{B}^{n}\times(\partial\mathbb{B}^{n})
,
respectively) are determined by the
indicial roots
of
\lambda-L
. As explained carefully in Appendix A below, these indicial roots are the values
\gamma
such that there exists
a symmetric
2
-tensor
\kappa
which is smooth up to
\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}
for which

(\lambda-L)\rho^{\gamma}(\rho^{-2}k)=\mathcal{O}(\rho^{\gamma+1}).

The extra factor of
\rho^{-2}
is included so that the norm of
\rho^{-2}\kappa
with respect to
h
is uniformly bounded (and smooth) up to
\partial B^{n}
.
This is an algebraic condition dependng only on the leading coefficients of the operator
\lambda-L
, and only involves the value of
\kappa
at each
boundary point.
The calculation of these indicial roots is technical, and relegated to Appendix
A
. There are sixteen separate indicial roots (these are independent of the point

p\in\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}
) which are convenient to write as eight sets of pairs,
\gamma_{i}^{\pm}=\gamma_{i}^{\pm}(\lambda)
,

is symmetrically arranged around the midpoint

(which is the weight
\gamma_{0}
such that

just barely lies outside of

As we explain in Appendix

resolvent set (the value

\mathrm{Re}\;\gamma_{8}^{-}(\lambda)\leq\cdots\leq\mathrm{Re}\;\gamma_{1}^{-}(\lambda)<\frac{n-1}{2}<\mathrm{Re}\;\gamma_{1}^{+}(\lambda)\leq\cdots\leq\mathrm{Re}\;\gamma_{8}^{+}(\lambda).

We now define

\sigma:=\inf\{\mathrm{Re}\;\gamma_{1}^{+}(\lambda):\mathrm{Re}\;\lambda>-a(n)\}.

In Proposition
A.2
we show that
\sigma\geq\frac{1}{4}.
It then follows, see
[

, that the Schwartz kernel satisfies the pointwise bounds

\displaystyle|R(\lambda,\rho,y,\tilde{\rho},\tilde{y})|

\displaystyle\leq C_{\lambda}\rho^{\frac{n-1}{2}+\sigma},\mbox{as }\rho\to 0,\mbox{for }\tilde{\rho}\geq c>0,

\displaystyle|R(\lambda,\rho,y,\tilde{\rho},\tilde{y})|

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}_{\lambda}\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{n-1}{2}+\sigma},\mbox{as }\tilde{\rho}\to 0,\mbox{for }\rho\geq c^{\prime}>0.

In fact, the result is slightly sharper, inasmuch as it shows that this Schwartz kernel vanishes at this stated rate
(n-1)/2+\sigma
uniformly along the
left and right faces, even up to the front face.

These bounds may then be used to show that given any
\lambda
with
\mathrm{Re}\;\lambda>-a(n)
, there exists a range of weights
\mu
(depending on
\lambda)

such that

\displaystyle R_{\lambda}(L):\rho^{\mu}\mathcal{C}^{k,\alpha}(\mathbb{B}^{n},h)\to\rho^{\mu}\mathcal{C}^{k,\alpha}(\mathbb{B}^{n},h)

is bounded. (Of course, the range lies in the smaller space
\rho^{\mu}\mathcal{C}^{k+4,\alpha}
.) Conjugating to remove the weights, this assertion is equivalent to the
boundedness of the mapping

\displaystyle u(\rho,y)\longmapsto\int(\tilde{\rho}/\rho)^{\mu}R(\lambda,\rho,y,\tilde{\rho},\tilde{y})u(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{y})\,\dvol_{h}

on
\mathcal{C}^{k,\alpha}(B^{n},h)

is comparable to

elliptic regularity provided we can show it if

means that the integral converges as
\tilde{\rho}\to 0

implies that this expression is uniformly
bounded as
\rho\to 0

We refer the reader to
[
3

∎

By a similar sort of perturbation result as in Corollary

Appendix A
Calculation of the Indicial Roots of
L

In this appendix we compute the indicial roots of the linearization of the gauge-adjusted Bach flow at any conformally compact
asymptotically hyperbolic metric, and in particular at the hyperbolic metric
h
. This is the operator

\displaystyle Lv=-\frac{1}{2}(\nabla^{*}\nabla)^{2}v+\frac{n+2}{2}\nabla^{*}\nabla v+

\displaystyle\left((\delta\delta v)h+n\delta^{*}\delta v\right)-nv

(A.67)

\displaystyle+(\Delta\tr v)h+\nabla^{2}\tr v+(\tr v)\,h.

The results are collected in Proposition
A.1
. Slightly more generally, we then compute the indicial roots of
\lambda-L
, where
\lambda
is the spectral parameter in Proposition
A.2
.

Recall that a value
\gamma
is an indicial root of
L
if there exists a symmetric
2
-tensor
v
which is smooth up to
\partial M
such that

L(x^{\gamma}v)=\mathcal{O}(x^{\gamma+1}).

Here
x
is a boundary defining function for
\partial M
, so
x\geq 0
on
M
with
x=0
and
dx\neq 0
at
\partial M
. For more general
0
-operators,
this extra vanishing in the normal direction for a particular value of
\gamma
might only occur at a particular point
p\in\partial M
; in other words, the
indicial roots might depend on
p\in\partial M
. Fortunately, in all situations considered here, this is not the case and the indicial roots are constant
along
\partial M
, so the equation above stands as is.

To understand this condition, expand
v
into its Taylor series,
v\sim\sum_{j}v_{j}x^{j}
, and calculate formally that for a general
\gamma\in\mathbb{C}
,

L(x^{\gamma}v)=x^{\gamma}k(x,y)\sim\sum_{j}k_{j}(y)x^{\gamma+j}

for some symmetric
2
-tensors
k_{j}
. We are using a coordinate system
(x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{n-1})
here, so
y_{i}
parametrizes a neighborhood in
\partial M
.
That the right hand side is of the form
x^{\gamma}
times a smooth function is a consequence of
the fact that
L
is a
0
-operator. Then
\gamma
is an indicial root if
k_{0}(y)=0
, i.e., there is some leading order cancellation. Indeed,
by inspection we see that

L(x^{\gamma}v)=I(L,\gamma)v_{0}x^{\gamma}+\mathcal{O}(x^{\gamma+1}),

where
I(L,\gamma)
is a polynomial of order four in
\gamma
, called the indicial family of
L
, which takes values in the space of endomorphisms
of
\Sigma^{2}(T^{*}_{p}M)
. Thus
\gamma
is an indicial root if and only if there exists some
v_{0}
in this finite dimensional vector space
such that
I(L,\gamma)v_{0}=0
. This is a sort of generalized eigenvalue equation.

We can also consider this operator
L
with respect to any conformally compact metric
g
, and it is not hard to see that
I(L,\gamma)
depends only
on the leading order behavior of the coefficients of
L
. This means that the computation of
I(L,\gamma)
disregards all higher order terms
in the Taylor series of
g
at
x=0
, and hence is the same if we were to replace the metric by
h
. Even further, this computation also
disregards all tangential derivatives since any
\partial_{y_{j}}
that appears in
L
is accompanied by an extra factor of
x
(this is a general
feature of
0
-operators). In summary, if we write
L
in local coordinates as

L=\sum_{j+|\alpha|\leq 4}a_{j\alpha}(x,y)(x\partial_{x})^{j}(x\partial_{y})^{\alpha}\Longrightarrow I(L)=\sum_{j\leq 4}a_{j0}(0,y_{0})(x\partial_{x})^{j},

where each coefficient
a_{j\alpha}
is a matrix depending smoothly on
(x,y)
, then the indicial roots are determined only by the
leading part

I(L)=\sum_{j\leq 4}a_{j0}(0,y)(x\partial_{x})^{j},

which is called the
indicial operator
of
L
. A value
\gamma
is an indicial root of
L
if and only if

\sum_{j=0}^{4}a_{j0}(0,y)\gamma^{j}v_{0}=0

for some
v_{0}\in\Sigma^{2}(T^{*}_{(0,y_{0})}M)
. (As noted above, in more general situations, these indicial roots depend on
y
,
but for all operators considered here they do not.) The indicial operator, and hence the indicial roots, of
L
at any metric
g
are
the same as for
L
at the metric
h
. Thus our task reduces to computing this model indicial operator.
One additional observation simplifies the calculations below further.

The indicial operator is functorial in the sense that

I(P_{1}\circ P_{2})=I(P_{1})I(P_{2}),\ \ \ I(P^{*})=I(P)^{*}

for any
0
-operators
P
,
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
. Since
L
is constituted by various compositions of the covariant derivative,
it suffices in principle to compute
I(\nabla)
on
1
-forms, symmetric
2
-tensors and
3
-tensors which are symmetric in the first two indices.
We use this observation only implicitly in the ensuing calculations.

We first note that
L
preserves the subspaces of pure-trace and trace-free tensors. This is apparent from the way that
L
is written in (
A.67
),
since if
v
is trace-free, then the three final terms in the expression for
L
vanish,
\nabla^{*}\nabla
preserves the space of trace-free tensors,
and
(\delta\delta v)h+n\delta^{*}\delta v
is the trace-free part of
n\delta^{*}\delta v
. Thus we calculate the action of
L
on these two
subspaces separately. Below we employ a further decomposition of the space of trace-free tensors into three further
L
-invariant subspaces.

Pure-trace
2
-tensors

First suppose that
v=fh
. Then a short calculation shows that

L(fh)=\left(-\frac{1}{2}\Delta^{2}f+\frac{n}{2}\Delta f\right)\,h.

The indicial operator of the scalar Laplacian is well-known to equal

\displaystyle I(\Delta)=(x\partial_{x})^{2}+(1-n)x\partial_{x}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle I(L)(fh)=\left(-\frac{1}{2}((x\partial_{x})^{2}+(1-n)x\partial_{x})^{2}+\frac{n}{2}((x\partial_{x})^{2}+(1-n)x\partial_{x})\right)f\,h.

For simplicity, write
I(L_{\tr})
for the operator in parentheses acting on scalar functions, so

(A.68)

\displaystyle I(L)(x^{\gamma}h)=\left(-\frac{1}{2}(\gamma^{2}+(1-n)\gamma)^{2}+\frac{n}{2}(\gamma^{2}+(1-n)\gamma)\right)x^{\gamma}\,h.

There is a final adjustment. The definition of indicial roots requires that we compute
I(L)
acting on
x^{\gamma}q
where

q
is smooth up to the boundary. Since
h=x^{-2}h_{E}
where

is the Euclidean metric, the left

I(L)(x^{\gamma-2}h_{E})
, so the roots
\tilde{\gamma}_{j}
of this polynomial correspond to indicial roots

\gamma_{j}=\tilde{\gamma}_{j}-2
. We have now proved that the indicial roots of
L
acting on the space of pure-trace tensors are the roots
of the fourth order polynomial

(A.69)

I(L_{\tr},\gamma)=\left(-\frac{1}{2}((\gamma+2)^{2}+(1-n)(\gamma+2))^{2}+\frac{n}{2}((\gamma+2)^{2}+(1-n)(\gamma+2))\right),

which are
\gamma=-3,-2,n-3,n-2.

Trace-Free
2
-tensors

We now assume that

\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{v}}=q

2

We work in the standard rectangular coordinates
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}

0

\alpha,\beta,\ldots

the indices for the tangential

In these coordinates, the Christoffel symbols for the hyperbolic metric

\Gamma^{k}_{j\ell}=-x^{-1}(\delta_{j}^{k}\delta_{\ell 0}+\delta_{\ell}^{k}\delta_{j0}-\delta^{k0}\delta_{j\ell}),

which reduce to

\Gamma^{j}_{i0}=\Gamma^{j}_{0i}=-\frac{1}{x}\delta^{j}_{i},\ \ \Gamma^{0}_{\alpha\beta}=\frac{1}{x}\delta_{\alpha\beta},

and all other

. The

is a
0

-covariant

\nabla_{0}=\partial_{x}+\frac{1}{x}A_{0},\quad\nabla_{\alpha}=\partial_{\alpha}+\frac{1}{x}A_{\alpha},

where

\partial_{0}

\partial_{x}
and
\partial_{y_{\alpha}}

As observed above, the indicial operators do not depend on the tangential derivatives
x\partial_{\alpha}

L\equiv L^{\prime}

First observe that if

The expressions for the tangential derivatives are slightly more complicated and depend on the degree of the tensor. Thus, if

Next, if

\displaystyle\nabla_{\alpha}q_{00}

\displaystyle:=q_{00,\alpha}\equiv\frac{2}{x}q_{0\alpha},\quad\nabla_{\beta}q_{0\alpha}:=q_{0\alpha,\beta}\equiv\frac{1}{x}q_{\alpha\beta}-\frac{1}{x}\delta_{\alpha\beta}q_{00}

\displaystyle\mbox{and}\ \ \nabla_{\gamma}q_{\alpha\beta}:=q_{\alpha\beta,\gamma}\equiv-\frac{1}{x}\delta_{\alpha\gamma}q_{0\beta}-\frac{1}{x}\delta_{\beta\gamma}q_{0\alpha}.

In computing the indicial operator of

\displaystyle q_{0\alpha,\beta\beta}

\displaystyle=\frac{n-1}{2}\pm\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1+n^{2}\pm 2\sqrt{n^{2}-8\lambda}},

\displaystyle=\frac{n-1}{2}\pm\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{n^{2}+1\pm 2\sqrt{n^{2}-8\lambda}},

\displaystyle\mu_{V_{0}}=\frac{n-1}{2}\pm\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1+n^{2}\pm 2\sqrt{n^{2}-8\lambda}}.

Similarly, using equations (

\displaystyle\mu_{V_{1}}

\displaystyle\mu_{V_{3}}

We now argue that the indicial radius of

is positive for

in a right half-plane containing the imaginary axis. We first write

For example, working with the indicial polynomial for
V_{0}
(which is also that of

then we find that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}(x^{2}-n^{2}-1)^{2}-n^{2}-8\varepsilon=-8yi.

This equation shows that
x
has a zero real part only if
y=0
. If
y=0
, then
x
has a zero real part if

\displaystyle n^{2}+1-2\sqrt{n^{2}+8\varepsilon}<0,

which is impossible if

that force equality are

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{V_{0}}=\varepsilon_{V_{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(n^{2}-1)^{2}}{32},

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{V_{1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{((n-2)^{2}-1)^{2}}{32},

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{V_{3}}

\displaystyle=\frac{((n-1)^{2}-4)^{2}}{32}.

\varepsilon_{V_{1}}\leq\varepsilon_{V_{3}}<\varepsilon_{V_{0}}=\varepsilon_{V_{2}}

to have a zero real part asymptotically, the argument of

\pm\frac{\pi}{2}
. To see that this is impossible, note that the argument of the complex number

Remark A.3
.

For illustration we set an explicit indicial radius of
\frac{n-1}{8}
and find the corresponding
a(n)
. Let
n\geq 4
.
Using the inequality
\sqrt{1+x}\leq 1+\frac{x}{2}
for
x\geq-1
, we have

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{(n-2)^{2}+8\varepsilon}\leq 2(n-2)+\frac{8\varepsilon}{n-2},

so that

\displaystyle(n-2)^{2}+1-2\sqrt{(n-2)^{2}+8\varepsilon}\geq(n-3)^{2}-\frac{8\varepsilon}{n-2}.

To guarantee an indicial radius of
\frac{n-1}{8}
we require that

\displaystyle(n-2)^{2}+1-2\sqrt{(n-2)^{2}+8\varepsilon}\geq\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{16},

and so it would suffice for
\varepsilon
to satisfy

(n-3)^{2}-\frac{8\varepsilon}{n-2}>\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{16}
.
Thus, for
a(n):=\varepsilon
with

\displaystyle 0\leq a(n)\leq\frac{(n-2)(3n-11)(5n-13)}{128},

the indicial radius of
\lambda-L
is at least
\frac{n-1}{8}.

Appendix B
Commutation formulæ

We record the details of a few of the commutation formulæ used in the text.

Lemma B.1
.

If
(M^{n},h)
has constant sectional curvature
c
and
\alpha
is a
1
-form, then

(B.75)

[\delta^{*},\nabla^{*}\nabla]\alpha=c(n+1)\delta^{*}\alpha+2c(\delta\alpha)\,h

and

(B.76)

[K,\nabla^{*}\nabla]\alpha=c(n+1)K\alpha.

All covariant derivatives and traces are calculated with respect to
h
.

Proof.

We begin by computing
[\nabla,\nabla^{*}\nabla]\alpha
in coordinates:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} {\alpha_{i,jk}}^{k}&={\alpha_{i,kj}}^{k}+\R^{\phantom{j}k\phantom{i}s}_{j\phantom{k}i}\alpha_{s,k}\\
&=\alpha_{i,k\phantom{k}j}^{\phantom{i,k}k}+2\R^{\phantom{j}k\phantom{i}s}_{j\phantom{k}i}\alpha_{s,k}+c(n-1)\alpha_{i,j}\\
&=\alpha_{i,k\phantom{k}j}^{\phantom{i,k}k}+2c(\delta_{j}^{s}\delta_{i}^{k}-h_{ji}h^{ks})\alpha_{s,k}+c(n-1)\alpha_{i,j}\\
&=\alpha_{i,k\phantom{k}j}^{\phantom{i,k}k}+2c\alpha_{j,i}-2c{\alpha_{k,}}^{k}h_{ij}+c(n-1)\alpha_{i,j};\end{aligned}

we now symmetrize in
i,j
, to get

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{2}({\alpha_{i,jk}}^{k}+{\alpha_{j,ik}}^{k})=\frac{1}{2}(\alpha_{i,k\phantom{k}j}^{\phantom{i,k}k}+\alpha_{j,k\phantom{k}i}^{\phantom{j,k}k})-2c{\alpha_{k,}}^{k}h_{ij}+c(n+1)\frac{1}{2}(\alpha_{i,j}+\alpha_{j,i})\end{aligned}

which is (
B.75
). Next, taking traces, we see that

[\delta,\nabla^{*}\nabla]\alpha=-c(n-1)\delta\alpha;

hence,

[K,\nabla^{*}\nabla]\alpha=[\delta^{*}+\frac{1}{n}(\delta\,\cdot\,)\,h,\nabla^{*}\nabla]=c(n+1)\delta^{*}\alpha+c(2-\frac{n-1}{n})(\delta\alpha)\,h=c(n+1)K\alpha

as claimed.
∎

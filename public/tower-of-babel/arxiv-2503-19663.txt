Chow quotients of 𝑪 ∗ \boldsymbol{{\mathbb C}^{*}} -actions on convex varieties
Gianluca Occhetta, Luis E. Solá Conde

Abstract




Abstract.
In this paper, we study the Chow quotient
\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt
of a convex variety
X
of Picard number
1
by the action of a
1
-dimensional torus with no nontrivial finite isotropy. Examples of these actions can be found in the rational homogeneous framework. We prove that the subvariety of
\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt
parametrizing reducible torus-invariant cycles is a simple normal crossing divisor, and we compute the nef and Mori cones of
\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt
, and its anticanonical divisor.

Keywords.
Chow quotients, torus actions, Mori theory, convex varieties

1. Introduction

Starting with Mumford’s celebrated geometric invariant theory (GIT) in the 1960s (
cf.

[
MFK94
]
), quotients have become an essential technique in modern algebraic geometry. They allow one to construct new examples of algebraic varieties, in particular moduli spaces and parameter spaces of different types.

In Mumford’s theory, quotients are constructed upon polarized varieties by means of stability conditions. While variations of these stability conditions can be interpreted in birational-geometric terms, giving rise to the concept of Mori dream space (see
[
Tha96
,
HK00
]
), GIT also motivated some geometers to define intrinsic concepts of quotients by the action of an algebraic group.

One of these is the concept of Chow quotient, introduced by
Kapranov (
cf.

[
Kap93
]
). In a nutshell, the Chow quotient of a projective variety
X
by the action of an algebraic group
H
is the closure of the subvariety of the Chow scheme
\mathop{\rm Chow}\nolimits(X)
parametrizing the closure of the general orbit in
X
.

Remarkably, the case in which
X=G/P
is a complex projective rational homogeneous variety and
H
is a torus leads to a number of interesting Chow quotients. A noteworthy example of this kind is the Grothendieck–Knudsen moduli space
\overline{M}_{0,n}
of stable punctured curves of genus zero: Kapranov showed in 1993 (see
[
Kap93
, Chapter 4]
) that it is the Chow quotient of the Grassmannian of lines in
{\mathbb P}^{n-1}
by the action of a maximal torus in
\mathop{\rm PGL}\nolimits(n)
.

Furthermore, as Thaddeus noted in 2001 (see
[
Tha99
]
), some specially interesting varieties can be obtained as Chow quotients of rational homogeneous spaces by the actions of
1
-dimensional complex tori. More precisely, the Chow quotients of

•

Grassmannians of
(n-1)
-dimensional linear subspaces of
{\mathbb P}^{n+m-1}
(
n\leq m
),

•

Lagrangian Grassmannians of
(n-1)
-dimensional isotropic subspaces in
{\mathbb P}^{2n-1}
(with respect to a fixed nondegenerate skew-symmetric form),

•

orthogonal Grassmannians of
(n-1)
-dimensional linear subspaces in a quadric of dimension
2n-2

by some particular
{\mathbb C}^{*}
-actions are isomorphic, respectively, to the moduli spaces

•

of complete collineations from a vector space of dimension
m
to a vector space of dimension
n
,

•

of complete quadrics of dimension
n-1
,

•

of complete skew-forms on a vector space of dimension
n
.

These three moduli spaces have been extensively studied in the literature (see, for instance,
[
Lak82
,
Lak87
,
Lak88
,
Tha99
,
Vai82
,
Vai84
]
), due to their importance in the framework of enumerative geometry. In particular, some of their birational-geometric properties (such as their nef, movable, and effective cones) have been studied in
[
Mas20a
,
Mas20b
]
. Many of these results are based on classical constructions of these varieties, by means of sequences of blowups of a projective space. We believe that their description as Chow quotients may help understand the geometry of these varieties in a unified broader setting, and that some of their geometric properties will be shared by other Chow quotients of rational homogeneous varieties.

The setting in which our arguments work is the class of convex varieties, which are smooth projective varieties whose tangent bundle is nef on rational curves. Note that this class contains the class of Fano manifolds whose tangent bundle is nef, and that it has been conjectured by Campana and Peternell that rational homogeneous varieties are the only Fano manifolds with this property (
cf.

[
CP91
,
MOSW+15
]
); to the best of our knowledge, no examples of nonhomogeneous uniruled convex varieties are known.

We assume that the Picard number of the variety
X
whose quotient we consider is
1
, and that the action of
{\mathbb C}^{*}
is
equalized
; this means that proper isotropy subgroups of the action are trivial (see Section
2.1.3
). These assumptions are satisfied by the actions leading to complete collineations, quadrics, and skew-forms, but also by many other examples of torus actions on rational homogeneous varieties whose Chow quotients are, to the best of our knowledge, still unknown.

We will use some background results, which we have presented in
[
ORSW25
,
OS26
]
, and which can be applied to a variety
X
endowed with a
{\mathbb C}^{*}
-action as above. In particular, we will use that, in our setting, the Chow quotient
\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt
is smooth and that the critical values of the action—with respect to the ample generator
L
of the Picard group of the variety—are of the form
0,d,2d,\dots,rd
for some positive integers
d,r
; the number
r
is called the
criticality
of the action.
Furthermore, there exists exactly one fixed-point component
Y_{j}
of
L
-weight
jd
for every
j
(see Section
2.1.2
and Proposition
3.4
).

A finer study of deformations of
{\mathbb C}^{*}
-invariant rational
1
-cycles allows us to describe the Chow quotient
\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt
of
X
as the union of the set of irreducible deformations of the general
1
-dimensional
{\mathbb C}^{*}
-invariant rational curve and a divisor (called the
boundary
of
\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt
) with simple normal crossings (see Theorem
3.12
and Remark
3.13
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X
be a convex variety of Picard number
1
, endowed with a nontrivial equalized
{\mathbb C}^{*}
-action of criticality
r
. Then the Chow quotient
\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt
of
X
by the action of
{\mathbb C}^{*}
is smooth, and the subscheme of
\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt
parametrizing reducible
1
-cycles is a divisor with simple normal crossings and
r-1
irreducible components.

In a nutshell, the convexity assumption allows us to show that every deformation of a
1
-cycle parametrized by an element of
\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt
can be realized as a
{\mathbb C}^{*}
-invariant deformation of the
1
-cycle in
X
(see Section
3.1
).
On the other hand, we know that, in our setting, the Chow quotient
\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt
is the normalization of the inverse limit of the GIT quotients of
X
. In particular, we get information about some contractions of
\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt
; we show that we can use these contractions to describe completely the Mori cone of
\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt
. More precisely, denoting by
\mathcal{G}\!X\hskip-0.5pt_{j,j}
,
j=0,\dots,r
, the semigeometric quotients of
X
(see Section
2.2
) and by
{\mathcal{L}}_{j,j}
the pullback to
\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt
of an ample line bundle on
\mathcal{G}\!X\hskip-0.5pt_{j,j}
, we prove the following (see Theorem
4.10
for the precise statement).

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
X
be a convex variety of Picard number
1
endowed with a nontrivial equalized
{\mathbb C}^{*}
-action of criticality
r
. Then the nef cone of
\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt
is generated by the classes
[{\mathcal{L}}_{i,i}]
,
i=0,\dots,r
.
Moreover, the Picard number of
\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt
is equal to

\rho(\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt)=r+1-\sharp\left\{i\in\{0,r\}\mid\dim(Y_{i})=0\right\}=r-1,r,\mbox{ or }r+1.

For the second part of the statement, we note that
{\mathcal{L}}_{0,0}
and
{\mathcal{L}}_{r,r}
are trivial if and only if
\mathcal{G}\!X\hskip-0.5pt_{0,0}\simeq Y_{0}
and
\mathcal{G}\!X\hskip-0.5pt_{r,r}\simeq Y_{r}
are isolated points.

Our methods allow us to describe the Mori cone of
\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt
as well. Its extremal rays are generated by the classes of some curves in
\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt
that we call
primal
; these curves admit a very natural description as families of
{\mathbb C}^{*}
-invariant cycles (see Section
3.3
).

Finally, our results allow us to compute the anticanonical divisor of
\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt
in terms of some invariants of the
{\mathbb C}^{*}
-action. We then present examples of actions in which
-K_{\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt}
is ample (
i.e.
,
\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt
is a Fano manifold), nef but not ample, and not nef.

A key point in our arguments is the following: The maps
\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt\to\mathcal{G}\!X\hskip-0.5pt_{i,i}
factor through the Chow quotients
\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt_{i,j}
of certain birational modifications
X_{i,j}
of
X
(for
i<j\in\{0,\dots,r\}
), called
prunings
(see Section
2.2
). The varieties
\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt_{i,j}
, called partial Chow quotients of
X
, fit in a diagram of normalized Cartesian squares:

This idea has already allowed us to prove that, under the assumptions of Theorem

,

is smooth if

or

, and that the maps

2. Background material

Throughout the paper, we will work over the field of complex numbers. The term
rational homogeneous variety
will always refers to a projective variety that supports a transitive algebraic action of a semisimple group. When working with these varieties, we will use the notation of marked Dynkin diagrams, as described, for instance, in
[
ORSW23a
, Section 3.1]
.

2.1.
\boldsymbol{{\mathbb C}^{*}}
-actions

In this section, we present some background material on
{\mathbb C}^{*}
-actions. We refer to
[
BB73
,
Car02
,
ORSW23b
]
for further details. In what follows,
X
will be a smooth projective variety, endowed with a nontrivial
{\mathbb C}^{*}
-action.

We denote by
X^{{\mathbb C}^{*}}
the fixed locus of the action, and by
{\mathcal{Y}}
the set of irreducible fixed-point components:

X^{{\mathbb C}^{*}}=\bigsqcupop\displaylimits_{Y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}Y.

The
sink
and the
source
of the action, often called the
extremal fixed-point components
, are the fixed-point components containing, respectively, the limiting points

x_{-}:=\lim_{t\to 0}t^{-1}x,\quad x_{+}:=\lim_{t\to 0}tx

of a general
x\in X
. The fixed-point components different from the sink and the source are called
inner
. Every
Y\in{\mathcal{Y}}
is smooth (
cf.

[
Ive72
]
), and the normal bundle of
Y
in
X
splits into two subbundles, on which
{\mathbb C}^{*}
acts with positive and negative weights, respectively:

(2.1)

N_{Y|X}\simeq N^{+}(Y)\oplus N^{-}(Y).

We set
\nu^{\pm}(Y):=\mathop{\rm rk}\nolimits(N^{\pm}(Y))
.

2.1.1. Białynicki-Birula decomposition

Given any set
S\subset X
, we define

(2.2)

X^{\pm}(S):=\{x\in X\mid x_{\pm}\in S\},\quad B^{\pm}(S):=\overline{X^{\pm}(S)}.

When
S=Y
with
Y\in{\mathcal{Y}}
, we call
X^{\pm}(Y)
the positive and the negative
Białynicki-Birula cells
of the action at
Y
(BB-cells, for short); we have decompositions

X=\bigsqcupop\displaylimits_{Y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}X^{+}(Y)=\bigsqcupop\displaylimits_{Y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}X^{-}(Y).

We will denote by
B^{\pm}(Y)
the closures of the BB-cells
X^{\pm}(Y)
. Every cell
X^{\pm}(Y)
has a natural morphism onto
Y
, sending every
x\in X^{\pm}(Y)
to its limiting point
x_{\pm}
. It was shown by Białynicki-Birula that
X^{\pm}(Y)
is an affine bundle, locally isomorphic to
N^{\pm}(Y)
.

2.1.2. Actions on polarized pairs

Given a line bundle
L\in\mathop{\rm Pic}\nolimits(X)
, there exists a linearization of the
{\mathbb C}^{*}
-action on it, so that for every
Y\in{\mathcal{Y}}
,
{\mathbb C}^{*}
acts on
L_{|Y}
by multiplication with a character
m\in\mathop{\rm\hskip 0.0ptM}\nolimits({\mathbb C}^{*})=\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits({\mathbb C}^{*},{\mathbb C}^{*})
, called the
weight of the linearization on
Y
. Two linearizations differ by a character of
{\mathbb C}^{*}
. In particular, for any
L\in\mathop{\rm Pic}\nolimits(X)
, there exists a unique linearization (called
normalized linearization
) whose weight at the sink is equal to zero.
Fix an isomorphism
\mathop{\rm\hskip 0.0ptM}\nolimits({\mathbb C}^{*})\simeq{\mathbb Z}
; then this linearization defines a map
\mu_{L}\colon{\mathcal{Y}}\to{\mathbb Z}
, called the
weight map
.

A
{\mathbb C}^{*}
-action on the polarized pair
(X,L)
is a
{\mathbb C}^{*}
-action on
X
, together with the weight map
\mu_{L}
determined by an ample line bundle
L
. In this case, we denote by

0=a_{0}<\dots<a_{r}

the weights
\mu_{L}(Y)
,
Y\in{\mathcal{Y}}
,
and set

Y_{i}:=\bigcupop\displaylimits_{\mu_{L}(Y)=a_{i}}Y.

The values
a_{i}
will be called the
critical values
, and the number
r
will be called the
criticality
of the action.
The minimum and maximum of the critical values are achieved only at the sink and the source,
which are then equal to
Y_{0}
and
Y_{r}
, respectively. The value
\delta=a_{r}
is called the
bandwidth
of the
{\mathbb C}^{*}
-action on
(X,L)
.

2.1.3. Equalized actions

A
{\mathbb C}^{*}
-action is
equalized
at
Y\in{\mathcal{Y}}
if for every
x\in(X^{-}(Y)\cup X^{+}(Y))\setminus Y
, the isotropy group of the action at
x
is trivial. Equivalently (see
[
ORSW22
, Lemma 2.1]
),
{\mathbb C}^{*}
acts on
N^{+}(Y)
with all the weights equal to
+1
and on
N^{-}(Y)
with all the weights equal to
-1
. The action is called
equalized
if it is equalized at every
Y\in{\mathcal{Y}}
.

For an equalized action, the closure of any
1
-dimensional orbit is a smooth rational curve, whose
L
-degree may be computed in terms of the weights at its extremal points (
cf.

[
ORSW22
, Lemma 2.2]
,
[
RW22
, Corollary 3.2]
).

Lemma 2.1

(AM vs. FM)
.

Let
(X,L)
be a polarized pair with an equalized
{\mathbb C}^{*}
-action, and let
C
be the closure of a
1
-dimensional orbit, with sink
x_{-}
and source
x_{+}
. Then
C
is a smooth rational curve of
L
-degree equal to
\mu_{L}(x_{+})-\mu_{L}(x_{-})
.

Using Lemma
2.1
, one can show (see
[
OS26
, Corollary 2.4]
) the following.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety with an equalized
{\mathbb C}^{*}
-action, and let
C
be the closure of a
1
-dimensional orbit, with sink in
Y
and source in
Y^{\prime}
. Then

-K_{X}\cdot C=\left(\nu^{+}(Y^{\prime})-\nu^{-}(Y^{\prime})\right)-\left(\nu^{+}(Y)-\nu^{-}(Y)\right).

[

]
and

]
. A
{\mathbb C}^{*}
-action on a polarized variety
(X,L)
allows one to define projective quotients of
X
, as follows. Set, for every
\tau\in[0,\delta]\cap{\mathbb{Q}}
,

A(\tau):=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\geq 0\\
m\tau\in{\mathbb Z}\end{subarray}}\HH^{0}(X,mL)_{m\tau},\quad\mathcal{G}\!X\hskip-0.5pt(\tau):=\mathop{\rm Proj}\nolimits(A(\tau)).

By
[
ORSW25
, Section 2.2]
,
\mathcal{G}\!X\hskip-0.5pt(\tau)
is the same for any
\tau\in(a_{i},a_{i+1})\cap{\mathbb{Q}}
, so we set

•

\mathcal{G}\!X\hskip-0.5pt_{i,i}:=\mathcal{G}\!X\hskip-0.5pt(\tau)
for
\tau=a_{i}
,

•

\mathcal{G}\!X\hskip-0.5pt_{i,i+1}:=\mathcal{G}\!X\hskip-0.5pt(\tau)
for
\tau\in(a_{i},a_{i+1})\cap{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Note that
\mathcal{G}\!X\hskip-0.5pt_{i,i+1}
is a
geometric quotient
of
X
for every

, while
\mathcal{G}\!X\hskip-0.5pt_{i,i}
is only a good categorical quotient (see
[
Dol03
, Section 6.1, Definition, p. 94]
). Following
[
BBCM02
, Chapter 3, p. 10]
, we say that
\mathcal{G}\!X\hskip-0.5pt_{i,i}
is
semigeometric
.
Furthermore, the varieties
\mathcal{G}\!X\hskip-0.5pt_{i,i+1}
are birationally equivalent, and the natural maps among them fit in the following commutative diagram, where the diagonal arrows are contractions and the horizontal maps are Atiyah flips (in the sense of
[

2.2.1. Prunings

Given an equalized
{\mathbb C}^{*}
-action of criticality
r
and bandwidth
\delta
on the smooth polarized pair
(X,L)
, we consider the blowup
\beta\colon X^{\flat}\to X
of
X
along the sink
Y_{0}
and the source
Y_{r}
, with exceptional divisors
Y^{\flat}_{0},Y^{\flat}_{r}
, and the induced
{\mathbb C}^{*}
-action.
Given rational numbers
\tau_{-}<\tau_{+}
in
[0,\delta]
, we set

L(\tau_{-},\tau_{+}):=\beta^{*}L-\tau_{-}Y^{\flat}_{0}-(\delta-\tau_{+})Y^{\flat}_{r}

and define the
pruning
of
(X,L)
with respect to
\tau_{-},\tau_{+}
as

X(\tau_{-},\tau_{+}):=\mathop{\rm Proj}\nolimits\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{m\in k{\mathbb Z}_{\geq 0}}\HH^{0}(X^{\flat},mL(\tau_{-},\tau_{+})),

where
k>0
is the minimum integer such that
k\tau_{\pm}\in{\mathbb Z}_{>0}
.
As shown in
[
ORSW25
, Lemma 3.5]
, for values
\tau_{-},\tau_{+}\in([0,\delta]\cap{\mathbb{Q}})\setminus\{a_{0},\dots a_{r}\}
, the pruning
X(\tau_{-},\tau_{+})
depends only on the intervals
(a_{i},a_{i+1})
,
(a_{j-1},a_{j})
containing
\tau_{-},\tau_{+}
, respectively. Hence we can set

X_{i,j}:=X(\tau_{-},\tau_{+})\quad\mbox{whenever }\tau_{-}\in(a_{i},a_{i+1}),\;\tau_{+}\in(a_{j-1},a_{j}).

2.2.2. Chow quotient

Given a projective variety
X
with a nontrivial
{\mathbb C}^{*}
-action, the
normalized Chow quotient
of the action is the normalization
\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt
of the closure of the subscheme of
\mathop{\rm Chow}\nolimits(X)
parametrizing the closures of general orbits.

More precisely, one may find a nonempty open set
V\subset X
of points
x
whose orbits
\overline{{\mathbb C}^{*}\cdot x}
belong to a single homology class in
X
. By shrinking
V
, if necessary, the geometric quotient of
V
by the action of
{\mathbb C}^{*}
exists. Taking closures in
X
of the orbits of points of
V
, we obtain an algebraic irreducible family of rational curves in
X
and a morphism
\psi\colon V/{\mathbb C}^{*}\to\mathop{\rm Chow}\nolimits(X)
.

The
Chow quotient
of
X
by the action of
{\mathbb C}^{*}
is then defined as the closure

\overline{\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt}:=\overline{\psi(V/{\mathbb C}^{*})}\subset\mathop{\rm Chow}\nolimits(X).

Its normalization
\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt
will be called the
normalized Chow quotient
of
X
by the
{\mathbb C}^{*}
-action. Pulling back the universal family of
\mathop{\rm Chow}\nolimits(X)
to
\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt
, we obtain a family of
1
-dimensional varieties

Throughout the rest of the paper, we will work in the following setting.

3.1. Boundary divisors

The main result of this section is that the subscheme of

parametrizing reducible cycles is a union of smooth divisors, which we call

Our first observation is the following.

Remark 3.6
.

Since

is a convex variety,

is smooth by Theorem

, which has been proven in
[

. Then

, which is the only

-fixed-point component of

containing the class of the closure of the general orbit in

Given a closed point

of

-invariant subscheme

[

, Proof of Proposition 6.4]
, the singularities of

are nodes. Let us now recall some facts about deformations of projective nodal curves. We refer the interested reader to
[

for a detailed account on this topic.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let

be a projective connected curve whose irreducible components are smooth rational curves

and whose singular locus is the set of points
\{P_{i}=C_{i}\cap C_{i+1}|\,\,i=1,\dots,k-1\}
. We assume that the singularities of

•

is supported on the singular points

,

{\mathcal{E}}xt^{1}({}_{C},{\mathcal{O}}_{C})_{P_{i}}\simeq{\mathbb C}

•

of first-order deformations of

\HH^{0}(C,{\mathcal{E}}xt^{1}({}_{C},{\mathcal{O}}_{C}))\simeq\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{k-1}{\mathcal{E}}xt^{1}({}_{C},{\mathcal{O}}_{C})_{P_{i}}.

Proof.

For the first assertion, we refer the reader to
[

. The second claim follows from the local-to-global

\mathop{\rm Ext}\nolimits
spectral sequence.
∎

Remark 3.8

Proposition

can be interpreted geometrically as follows. The first-order deformations of

correspond to smoothings of

at its singular points (see

for details). More precisely, for every

and are locally trivial around the

In this way, when we choose isomorphisms
{\mathcal{E}}xt^{1}({}_{C},{\mathcal{O}}_{C})_{P_{i}}\simeq{\mathbb C}
and denote by

that are locally trivial around

are given by the equation

.

.

The intersection number

5. Anticanonical bundle of the Chow quotient

Notation 5.1
.

Theorem
4.10
allows us to consider the basis
\{L_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of
{\mathop{\rm N^{1}}\nolimits}(\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt)
dual to
\{\Gamma_{i}\}_{i\in I}
, whose elements are determined by some effective
{\mathbb{Q}}
-divisors, which we denote by
L_{i}
as well.

We now compute the class of the canonical bundle of
\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt
as a linear combination of the
L_{i}
. Let us first introduce some appropriate invariants of the
{\mathbb C}^{*}
-action, which will appear in the coefficients of the final formula.

Definition 5.2
.

Let us consider the ranks
\nu^{\pm}_{i}=\mathop{\rm rk}\nolimits(N^{\pm}(Y_{i}))
introduced in Remark
3.5
. We set

(5.1)

u_{j}^{+}:=\nu^{-}_{j}-\nu^{-}_{j+1}-1,\quad u_{j}^{-}:=\nu^{+}_{j}-\nu^{+}_{j-1}-1.

The values
u_{j}^{\pm}
are the dimensions of the extremal fixed-point components of the induced action on
{\mathcal{U}}_{y}
, for a general
y\in Y_{j}
(see
[
OS26
, Corollary 5.14]
). We will not need this fact here, but we note that, by
[
OS26
, Corollary 5.14]
, we also have
u_{j}^{+}+u_{j+1}^{-}=\dim{\mathcal{U}}_{y}
for every
j=0,\dots,r-1
. Since the dimension of
{\mathcal{U}}_{y}
is constant, we get

(5.2)

u_{j-1}^{+}-u_{j}^{+}=u_{j+1}^{-}-u_{j}^{-},\quad j=1,\dots,r-1.

Note that in the following statement, we do not need to distinguish the case of extremal isolated fixed points, since, if
\dim Y_{0}=0
(resp.
\dim Y_{r}=0
), the coefficient of
L_{0}
(resp. of
L_{r}
) appearing in the formulae is zero.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
X
be as in Setup
3.1
. If
the criticality of the action on
X
is equal to
2
, then

-K_{\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt}=u_{1}^{-}L_{0}+(u_{0}^{+}-u_{1}^{+}+2)L_{1}+u_{1}^{+}L_{2}.

If else the criticality of the action is greater than or equal to
3
, then

-K_{\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt}=u_{1}^{-}L_{0}+\left(u_{0}^{+}-u_{1}^{+}+1\right)L_{1}+\sumop\displaylimits_{i=2}^{r-2}\left(u_{i-1}^{+}-u_{i}^{+}\right)L_{i}+\left(u_{r-2}^{+}-u_{r-1}^{+}+1\right)L_{r-1}+u_{r-1}^{+}L_{r}.

Proof.

We will use the description of
\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt
as the result of a sequence of smooth blowups along the sides of the triangle (
2.4
). The centers of these blowups have been described in
[
ORSW25
, Remark 5.10]
: The center of the blowup
\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt_{0,i}\leftarrow\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt_{0,i+1}
is the set of invariant cycles in
X_{0,i}
whose strict transforms in
X
have source in
Y_{i}
; it has codimension
\nu_{i}^{-}
. By
[
OS26
, Theorem 5.7]
, there exists a smooth
{\mathbb C}^{*}
-invariant cycle linking
Y_{0}
and
Y_{i+1}
, so the center of
\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt_{0,i}\leftarrow\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt_{0,i+1}
is not contained in the union of the exceptional divisors of
\mathcal{G}\!X\hskip-0.5pt_{0,1}\leftarrow\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt_{0,i}
. In particular, the inverse images of the exceptional divisors of
\mathcal{G}\!X\hskip-0.5pt_{0,1}\leftarrow\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt_{0,i}
coincide with their strict transforms. Since
-K_{\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt_{0,i+1}}+K_{\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt_{0,i}}
is linearly equivalent to the exceptional divisor counted with multiplicity
(1-\nu_{i}^{-})
for every
i
, we conclude that

-K_{\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt}=-K_{\mathcal{G}\!X\hskip-0.5pt_{0,1}}+\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{r-1}\left(1-\nu_{i}^{-}\right)E_{i}.

Similarly, we also get that

-K_{\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt}=-K_{\mathcal{G}\!X\hskip-0.5pt_{r-1,r}}+\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{r-1}\left(1-\nu_{i}^{+}\right)E_{i}.

From the first expression, taking into account that for every
j\geq 2
the curves
\Gamma_{j}
are contracted by the natural map from
\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt
to
\mathcal{G}\!X\hskip-0.5pt_{0,1}
, the intersection numbers computed in Proposition
3.23
, and the definition of the invariants
u_{i}^{\pm}
, we have

-K_{\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt}\cdot\Gamma_{j}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\left(1-\nu^{-}_{j-1}\right)+2\left(1-\nu^{-}_{j}\right)-\left(1-\nu^{-}_{j+1}\right)=u^{+}_{j-1}-u^{+}_{j}&\mbox{if }j<r-1,\\
-\left(1-\nu^{-}_{r-2}\right)+2\left(1-\nu^{-}_{r-1}\right)=u^{+}_{r-2}-u^{+}_{r-1}+1&\mbox{if }j=r-1,\\
-\left(1-\nu^{-}_{r-1}\right)=u^{+}_{r-1}&\mbox{if }j=r.\end{array}\right.

Analogously, we may use the second expression of
-K_{\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt}
to get

-K_{\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt}\cdot\Gamma_{0}=u_{1}^{-},\quad-K_{\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt}\cdot\Gamma_{1}=u^{-}_{2}-u_{1}^{-}+1=u_{0}^{+}-u_{1}^{+}+1.

The last equality follows from Formula (
5.2
).

The above intersection numbers prove the statement in the case
r\geq 3
. In the case
r=2
, these arguments provide only the anticanonical degrees of
\Gamma_{0}
and
\Gamma_{2}
(respectively,
u^{-}_{1}
,
u^{+}_{1}
), and a different one is needed to prove that
-K_{\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt}\cdot\Gamma_{1}=u_{0}^{+}-u_{1}^{+}+2
. Since we know that
E_{1}\cdot\Gamma_{1}=2
and
-K_{\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt}=-K_{\mathcal{G}\!X\hskip-0.5pt_{0,1}}+(1-\nu_{1}^{-})E_{1}
, we need to prove that
-K_{\mathcal{G}\!X\hskip-0.5pt_{0,1}}\cdot\Gamma_{1}=\nu^{-}_{0}
.

The variety
\mathcal{G}\!X\hskip-0.5pt_{0,1}
is a
{\mathbb P}^{\nu^{-}_{0}-1}
-bundle over
Y_{0}
, and
\Gamma_{1}
is clearly contracted by the composition
\mathcal{C}\!X\hskip-0.5pt\to\mathcal{G}\!X\hskip-0.5pt_{0,1}\to Y_{0}
, so it is enough to show that the image of
\Gamma_{1}
in
\mathcal{G}\!X\hskip-0.5pt_{0,1}
is a line in a fiber of
\mathcal{G}\!X\hskip-0.5pt_{0,1}\to Y_{0}
.

By construction, the points of
\Gamma_{1}
are
{\mathbb C}^{*}
-invariant cycles that are the images in
X
of a
1
-dimensional family of cycles in
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
passing through two points
x_{-},x_{+}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
; we denote the image of
x_{-}
in
X
by
P_{0}\in Y_{0}
. Since the
{\mathbb C}^{*}
-action is equalized, these cycles have different tangent directions at
P_{0}
, so the differential of the map
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}\to X
at
x_{-}
is injective, and the projectivization of its image is a line in
{\mathbb P}(N^{-}(Y_{0})_{P_{0}})
. This is precisely the image of
\Gamma_{1}
in
\mathcal{G}\!X\hskip-0.5pt_{0,1}
, and the proof is finished.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

The formulae in Theorem
5.3
can be used to compute the canonical bundle of the Chow quotient of a given equalized
{\mathbb C}^{*}
-action on a variety
X
if the Fano index

of

We can then compute
\nu_{j}^{\pm}
for every fixed-point component and, subsequently,

using Formula (
5.1
).

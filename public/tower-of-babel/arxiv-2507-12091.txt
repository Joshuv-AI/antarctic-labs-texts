Improved Analysis for Sign-based Methods with Momentum Updates
Wei Jiang, Dingzhi Yu, Sifan Yang, Wenhao Yang, Lijun Zhang

Abstract
In this paper, we present enhanced analysis for sign-based optimization algorithms with momentum updates. Traditional sign-based methods, under the separable smoothness assumption, guarantee a convergence rate of 𝒪 ( T − 1 / 4 ) \mathcal{O}(T^{-1/4}) , but they either require large batch sizes or assume unimodal symmetric stochastic noise. To address these limitations, we demonstrate that signSGD with momentum can achieve the same convergence rate using constant batch sizes without additional assumptions. Our analysis, under the standard l 2 l_{2} -smoothness condition, improves upon the result of the prior momentum-based signSGD method by a factor of 𝒪 ⁡ ( d 1 / 2 ) \mathcal{O}(d^{1/2}) , where d d is the problem dimension. Furthermore, we explore sign-based methods with majority vote in distributed settings and show that the proposed momentum-based method yields convergence rates of 𝒪 ( d 1 / 2 T − 1 / 2 + d n − 1 / 2 ) \mathcal{O}\left(d^{1/2}T^{-1/2}+dn^{-1/2}\right) and 𝒪 ( max { d 1 / 4 T − 1 / 4 , d 1 / 10 T − 1 / 5 } ) \mathcal{O}\left(\max\{d^{1/4}T^{-1/4},d^{1/10}T^{-1/5}\}\right) , which outperform the previous results of 𝒪 ( d T − 1 / 4 + d n − 1 / 2 ) \mathcal{O}\left(dT^{-1/4}+dn^{-1/2}\right) and 𝒪 ( d 3 / 8 T − 1 / 8 ) \mathcal{O}\left(d^{3/8}T^{-1/8}\right) , respectively. Numerical experiments further validate the effectiveness of the proposed methods.

Introduction

This paper investigates the stochastic optimization problem in the form

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(\mathbf{x}),

(1)

where
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is a smooth and non-convex function. We assume that only noisy estimation of the gradient are available, denoted as
\nabla f(\mathbf{x};\xi)
, where
\xi
represents a random sample drawn from a stochastic oracle such that
\mathbb{E}[\nabla f(\mathbf{x};\xi)]=\nabla f(\mathbf{x})
.

Problem (
1
) has been extensively studied in the literature
(
Duchi et al. 2011
;
Kingma and Ba 2015
;
Loshchilov and Hutter 2017
;
Fang et al. 2018
;
Wang et al. 2019
;
Cutkosky and Orabona 2019
)
. One of the most widely used algorithms for solving this problem is Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD), which updates the parameters as

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{t+1}=\mathbf{x}_{t}-\eta\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t};\xi_{t}),

(2)

where
\eta
is the learning rate and
\xi_{t}
is the random sample drawn at the
t
-th iteration. It is well-known that the SGD method achieves a convergence rate of
\mathcal{O}(T^{-1/4})
, where
T
is the number of iterations
(
Ghadimi and Lan 2013
)
. This rate has been proven to be optimal under standard assumptions
(
Arjevani et al. 2023
)
.

Instead of using the stochastic gradient to update, several works
(
Bernstein et al. 2018
;
Bernstein et al. 2019
;
Safaryan and Richtarik 2021
)
propose updating the parameters using only the sign of the stochastic gradient, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{t+1}=\mathbf{x}_{t}-\eta\operatorname{sign}\left(\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t};\xi_{t})\right),

(3)

which is particularly beneficial in distributed settings. In such scenarios, only the sign information needs to be transmitted between nodes, significantly reducing communication overhead.

Recently, several studies have investigated the convergence properties of the signSGD method and its variants.
Bernstein et al. 2018
first prove that signSGD can achieve a convergence rate of
\mathcal{O}(N^{-1/4})
, where
N
represents the number of stochastic gradient calls. However, their analysis requires using a large batch size of
\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{N})
in each iteration, along with the separable smoothness assumption.
Later,
Bernstein et al. 2019
extend this result by demonstrating that signSGD can achieve the same convergence rate with constant batch sizes, but under the additional assumption that the noise is unimodal and symmetric.
To avoid the need for large batch sizes and extra assumptions,
Sun et al. 2023
show that signSGD with momentum can achieve a convergence rate of
\mathcal{O}(dT^{-1/4})
under the standard
l_{2}
-smoothness assumption. However, this dependence on
d
remains unsatisfactory, resulting in high sample complexity for high-dimensional problems.

In this paper, we re-examine the signSGD method with momentum updates and obtain the convergence rate of
\mathcal{O}(T^{-1/4})
under the
l_{\infty}
-smoothness assumption, which is weaker than the separable smoothness condition used in previous work
(
Bernstein et al. 2018
;
Bernstein et al. 2019
)
.
Moreover, our analysis does not require additional assumptions, such as large batch sizes or unimodal symmetric noise. Under the standard
l_{2}
-smoothness assumption, we derive a convergence rate of
\mathcal{O}(d^{1/2}T^{-1/4})
, improving upon the previous result of
\mathcal{O}(dT^{-1/4})

(
Sun et al. 2023
)
under the same conditions.

In distributed settings, when employing sign-based methods to reduce communication costs, each node computes the gradient and transmits only the sign of the gradient to the parameter server. The server then aggregates this information and sends back the sign of the aggregated data to each node for updating.
In this context, previous literature establishes convergence rates of
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{d}{T^{1/4}}+\frac{d}{n^{1/2}}\right)

(
Sun et al. 2023
)
and
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{d^{3/8}}{T^{1/8}}\right)

(
Jin et al. 2021
)
, both of which exhibit undesirable dependence on the dimension
d
and the iteration count
T
.
To improve the convergence rates, we utilize the unbiased sign operation along with momentum updates, achieving convergence rates of
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{n^{1/2}}{T}+\frac{d}{n^{1/2}}\right)
,
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{d^{1/2}}{T^{1/2}}+\frac{d}{n^{1/2}}\right)
, and
\mathcal{O}\left(\max\left\{\frac{d^{1/4}}{T^{1/4}},\frac{d^{1/10}}{T^{1/5}}\right\}\right)
, with the latter two showing significant improvements over previous results.
In summary, this paper makes the following contributions:

•

Under the
l_{\infty}
-smoothness assumption, we prove that signSGD with momentum can achieve a convergence rate of
\mathcal{O}(T^{-1/4})
without the need for additional assumptions. In contrast, existing analyses for signSGD and its momentum variant either require large batch sizes or rely on the assumption of unimodal symmetric noise, and they depend on the stronger condition of separable smoothness.

•

Under the standard
l_{2}
-smoothness assumption, we show that our method achieves a convergence rate of
\mathcal{O}(d^{1/2}T^{-1/4})
, which improves upon the
\mathcal{O}(dT^{-1/4})
convergence rate of existing momentum-based signSGD method under the same conditions.

•

In distributed settings, we derive convergence rates of
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{n^{1/2}}{T}+\frac{d}{n^{1/2}}\right)
,
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{d^{1/2}}{T^{1/2}}+\frac{d}{n^{1/2}}\right)
and
\mathcal{O}\left(\max\left\{\frac{d^{1/4}}{T^{1/4}},\frac{d^{1/10}}{T^{1/5}}\right\}\right)
, with the latter two substantially outperforming previous results of
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{d}{T^{1/4}}+\frac{d}{n^{1/2}}\right)
and
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{d^{3/8}}{T^{1/8}}\right)
, respectively.

Related work

In this section, we review the signSGD method and its variants, as well as sign-based methods with majority vote in distributed settings.

2.1
SignSGD and its variants

The convergence rate of the signSGD method is first formally analyzed by
Bernstein et al. 2018
, who establish that signSGD achieves a convergence rate of
\mathcal{O}(N^{-1/4})
with a large batch size of
\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{N}\right)
, where
N
denotes the number of stochastic gradient calls. They also show that the momentum version of signSGD, known as Signum, achieves a convergence rate of
\mathcal{O}(N^{-1/4}\log N)
when using increasingly large batches.
To avoid large batch sizes,
Bernstein et al. 2019
demonstrate that signSGD can achieve a convergence rate of
\mathcal{O}(T^{-1/4})
with a constant batch size, where
T
is the number of iterations. However, their analysis relies on the strong assumption that the stochastic gradient noise is both unimodal and symmetric, which is not satisfied for many types of noise in practice
(
Zhang et al. 2020
;
Kunstner et al. 2023
)
.

Subsequently,
Karimireddy et al. 2019
observe that the basic signSGD method with a constant batch size may not converge to optimal points for convex objectives and performs poorly compared to traditional SGD methods. To address this issue, they propose incorporating the error introduced by the compression process into the next update step, showing that error feedback can enhance practical performance.
Rather than assuming unbiased estimation and bounded noise,
Safaryan and Richtarik 2021
provide convergence guarantees under the Success Probability Bounds (SPB) assumption. This assumption states that the sign information of the stochastic gradient matches that of the true gradient with a probability greater than
1/2
.
Recently,
Sun et al. 2023
analyze the momentum-based version of signSGD and achieve a convergence rate of
\mathcal{O}(dT^{-1/4})
under standard assumptions. However, their dependence on
d
can be further improved, as demonstrated by our analysis.

Besides, several other variants have been proposed. For instance, ZO-signSGD
(
Liu et al. 2019
)
combines zeroth-order updates with sign information, benefiting from both gradient-free operations and communication compression.
Jiang et al. 2024
incorporate the variance reduction technique with the sign operation, improving the convergence rate to
\mathcal{O}(T^{-1/3})
under a slightly stronger smoothness assumption.
For finite-sum problems, the convergence rates can be further improved to
\mathcal{O}(d^{1/2}m^{1/2}T^{-1/2})

(
Chzhen and Schechtman 2023
;
Qin et al. 2023
)
and
\mathcal{O}(d^{1/2}m^{1/4}T^{-1/2})

(
Jiang et al. 2024
)
, where
m
denotes the number of functions in the finite-sum structure.

2.2
Sign-based methods with majority vote

The majority vote technique is employed to enable communication compression in distributed settings. In this framework, each node transmits only the sign of its gradient estimation to the parameter server, which then aggregates the information and sends the sign of the aggregated data back to each node for updating.
In the homogeneous setting,
Bernstein et al. 2018
first demonstrate that signSGD with majority vote can achieve a convergence rate of
\mathcal{O}(T^{-1/4})
with large batch sizes. Later,
Bernstein et al. 2019
extend this result to Signum (the momentum version of signSGD) with majority vote, showing that it achieves the same convergence rate when the noise is unimodal and symmetric.
For more challenging heterogeneous environments, SSDM
(
Safaryan and Richtarik 2021
)
achieves a convergence rate of
\mathcal{O}(d^{1/2}T^{-1/4})
under the Success Probability Bounds (SPB) assumption. However, SSDM can only guarantee 1-bit compression in one direction, since the information sent back to each node is not the sign information anymore.
To address this limitation, Stochastic-Sign SGD
(
Jin et al. 2021
)
ensures 1-bit compression in both directions and achieves a convergence rate of
\mathcal{O}(d^{3/8}T^{-1/8})
in terms of the
l_{2}
-norm. Later,
Sun et al. 2023
propose the MV-sto-signSGD-SIM method, which attains a convergence rate of
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{d}{T^{1/4}}+\frac{d}{n^{1/2}}\right)
. By further incorporating variance reduction techniques,
Jiang et al. 2024
improve the convergence rates to
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{d^{1/2}}{T^{1/2}}+\frac{d}{n^{1/2}}\right)
and
\mathcal{O}(d^{1/4}T^{-1/4})
, under the stronger average smoothness assumption.

3
SignSGD with momentum updates

In this section, we first introduce the assumptions used to analyze sign-based methods and then present our convergence guarantees for signSGD with momentum updates. Due to space limitations, all the proofs are deferred to the supplementary material.

3.1
Assumptions

To derive convergence guarantees for sign-based methods, we typically require certain assumptions about the smoothness of the objective function and the properties of the stochastic gradient noise. Below, we outline the key assumptions commonly used in the analysis of stochastic sign-based optimization
(
Bernstein et al. 2018
;
Bernstein et al. 2019
;
Balles et al. 2020
;
Sun et al. 2023
)
.

Assumption 1

(Separable smoothness) The objective function
f
is separable smooth if there exist non-negative constants
\left[L_{1},L_{2},\dots,L_{d}\right]
such that

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{y})

\displaystyle\leq f(\mathbf{x})+\left\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{x}),\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}L_{i}(\mathbf{y}_{i}-\mathbf{x}_{i})^{2}.

Assumption 2

(
l_{2}
-smoothness) The objective function
f
is
L
-smooth if

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x})-\nabla f(\mathbf{y})\right\|\leq L\left\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\right\|.

Assumption 3

(
l_{\infty}
-smoothness) The objective function
f
is
L_{\infty}
-smooth if

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x})-\nabla f(\mathbf{y})\right\|_{1}\leq L_{\infty}\left\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\right\|_{\infty}.

Remark:
Separable smoothness (Assumption
1
) is used in early analyses of signSGD methods
(
Bernstein et al. 2018
;
Bernstein et al. 2019
)
, and
l_{2}
-smoothness (Assumption
2
) is widely adopted in traditional stochastic methods
(
Zhang et al. 2013
;
Nguyen et al. 2017
)
as well as in recent sign-based momentum/variance-reduction methods
(
Sun et al. 2023
;
Jiang et al. 2024
)
. Additionally, several works
(
Balles et al. 2020
;
Chzhen and Schechtman 2023
;
Li et al. 2023
;
He et al. 2024
)
analyze sign-based methods under the assumption of
l_{\infty}
-smoothness (Assumption
3
), which is strictly weaker than the separable smoothness assumption.

We can show that Assumption
3
is weaker than Assumption
1
through the following lemma:

Lemma 1

If the objective function
f
is separable smooth with some non-negative constants
\left[L_{1},L_{2},\dots,L_{d}\right]
, then it is also
l_{\infty}
-smooth, where the smoothness constant is
L_{\infty}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}L_{i}
.

In this paper, we provide two different convergence results under Assumptions
2
and
3
, respectively. Next, we introduce assumptions related to the stochastic gradient noise.

Assumption 4

(Bounded noise) The stochastic gradient noise is bounded such that

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}_{\xi}\left[\left\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x};\xi)-\nabla f(\mathbf{x})\right\|^{2}\right]\leq\sigma^{2}.\end{split}

Assumption 5

(Separable bounded noise) For some non-negative constants
\left[\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\cdots,\sigma_{d}\right]
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\xi}\left[\left(\left[\nabla f(\mathbf{x};\xi)\right]_{i}-\left[\nabla f(\mathbf{x})\right]_{i}\right)^{2}\right]\leq\sigma_{i}^{2}.

Assumption 6

(Unimodal symmetric noise) At any given point
\mathbf{x}
, each component of the stochastic gradient
\nabla f(\mathbf{x};\xi)
has a unimodal distribution that is symmetric about the mean.

Remark:
Existing sign-based methods typically require either bounded noise
(
Sun et al. 2023
;
Jiang et al. 2024
)
or separable bounded noise
(
Bernstein et al. 2018
;
Bernstein et al. 2019
;
Crawshaw et al. 2022
;
Liu et al. 2025
)
assumptions to ensure convergence. Additionally,
Bernstein et al. 2019
require Assumption
6
to avoid the need for large batch sizes.

3.2
The proposed method

In this subsection, we introduce the sign-based method with momentum updates and present the corresponding convergence guarantees. The traditional signSGD method uses the sign of the stochastic gradient for updates, in the form of

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{t+1}=\mathbf{x}_{t}-\eta\operatorname{sign}(\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t};\xi_{t})).

(4)

where
\eta
is the learning rate. In contrast to signSGD, we track the gradient with a momentum estimator
\mathbf{v}_{t}
, defined as

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}_{t}=(1-\beta)\mathbf{v}_{t-1}+\beta\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t};\xi_{t}),

(5)

where
\beta
is the momentum parameter and we use
\mathbf{v}_{1}=\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{1};\xi_{1})
for the first iteration. After computing the momentum-based gradient estimator
\mathbf{v}_{t}
, we update the decision variable using the sign of
\mathbf{v}_{t}
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{t+1}=\mathbf{x}_{t}-\eta\operatorname{sign}\left(\mathbf{v}_{t}\right).

(6)

The full algorithm is outlined in Algorithm
1
, named Sign-based momentum method (SMM). Next, we provide the theoretical guarantees for the proposed method under the
l_{2}
-smoothness assumption.

Theorem 1

Under Assumptions
2
and
4
, by setting
\beta=\mathcal{O}\left(T^{-1/2}\right)
and
\eta=\mathcal{O}\left(d^{-1/2}T^{-3/4}\right)
, our method ensures that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{\tau})\|_{1}\right]\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{d^{1/2}}{T^{1/4}}\right).

Remark:
This convergence rate implies a sample complexity of
\mathcal{O}(d^{2}\epsilon^{-4})
, which is an improvement over the
\mathcal{O}(d^{4}\epsilon^{-4})
results of previous sign-based momentum methods
(
Sun et al. 2023
)
. This improvement is especially significant when the dimension
d
is large.

For a fair comparison with previous analyses of signSGD methods
(
Bernstein et al. 2018
;
Bernstein et al. 2019
)
, we also provide guarantees under the
l_{\infty}
-smoothness assumption.

Theorem 2

Under Assumptions
3
and

\beta=\mathcal{O}\left(T^{-1/2}\right)
and
\eta=\mathcal{O}\left(T^{-3/4}\right)
, Algorithm

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{\tau})\right\|_{1}\right]\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{T^{1/4}}\right).

Remark:
The above convergence rate implies a sample complexity of
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-4})
, matching the state-of-the-art results of signSGD methods
(
Bernstein et al. 2018
;
Bernstein et al. 2019
)
. However, our method does not require large batch sizes (which can be as large as
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-2})
for signSGD
(
Bernstein et al. 2018
)
) and does not rely on the unimodal symmetric noise assumption (which is required by
Bernstein et al. 2019
).
Furthermore, the
l_{\infty}
-smoothness assumption (Assumption
3
) used in our analysis is weaker than the separable smoothness assumption (Assumption
1
) employed in previous works
(
Bernstein et al. 2018
;
Bernstein et al. 2019
)
.

4
Majority vote signSGD with momentum updates

We first present the problem formulation and the assumptions used in the analysis. Then, we introduce the proposed method and establish the convergence guarantees.

4.1
Problem formulation and assumptions

Sign-based methods are highly communication-efficient in distributed settings, as they update using only the 1-bit sign information. Previous literature
(
Bernstein et al. 2018
;
Bernstein et al. 2019
;
Jin et al. 2021
;
Sun et al. 2023
)
has analyzed sign-based methods with majority vote in distributed environments. To begin with, consider the following distributed learning problem:

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(\mathbf{x})\coloneqq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}(\mathbf{x}),\quad f_{j}(\mathbf{x})=\mathbb{E}_{\xi^{j}\sim\mathcal{D}_{j}}\left[f_{j}(\mathbf{x};\xi^{j})\right],

(7)

where
\mathcal{D}_{j}
represents the data distribution on node
j
, and
f_{j}(\mathbf{x})
is the corresponding loss function.

Early studies
(
Bernstein et al. 2018
;
Bernstein et al. 2019
)
focus on applying signSGD and its momentum variants in homogeneous settings, where the data distribution
\mathcal{D}_{j}
and the loss function
f_{j}
on each node are identical.
For the more difficult heterogeneous setting, where the data and the loss function in each node can differ substantially, previous analysis
(
Chen et al. 2020
)
indicates that signSGD fails to converge in this case.
To remedy this,
Jin et al. 2021
propose the Sto-signSGD method and derive a convergence rate of
\mathcal{O}\left(d^{3/8}T^{-1/8}\right)
, suffering from a high sample complexity. Subsequently,
Sun et al. 2023
extend the analysis to signSGD with momentum, achieving a convergence rate of
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{d}{T^{1/4}}+\frac{d}{n^{1/2}}\right)
. However, this rate can still be improved based on our analysis.

Next, we introduce the assumptions required in this section, which are standard and commonly used in previous works
(
Jin et al. 2021
;
Sun et al. 2023
)
. Essentially, we assume smoothness, bounded noise, and bounded gradients for each node, as detailed below.

Assumption 7

(Smoothness on node
j
) For each node
j\in[n]
, we suppose

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla f_{j}(\mathbf{x})-\nabla f_{j}(\mathbf{y})\right\|\leq L\left\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\right\|.

Assumption 8

(Bounded noise on node
j
) For each node
j\in[n]
, we have

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}_{\xi}\left[\left\|\nabla f_{j}(\mathbf{x};\xi)-\nabla f_{j}(\mathbf{x})\right\|^{2}\right]\leq\sigma^{2}.\end{split}

Assumption 9

(Bounded gradients)
For each node
j\in[n]
, we assume
\sup_{\mathbf{x}}\left\|\nabla f_{j}(\mathbf{x};\xi)\right\|_{\infty}\leq G
.

4.2
The proposed method

In this subsection, we introduce the proposed method for distributed environments and present the corresponding convergence guarantees. For distributed settings, the most straightforward approach is to apply the sign operation twice, resulting in the following update rule:

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{t+1}=\mathbf{x}_{t}-\eta\operatorname{sign}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\operatorname{sign}(\mathbf{v}_{t}^{j})\right),

(8)

where
\mathbf{v}_{t}^{j}
is the gradient estimator at node
j
. In this formulation, each node transmits the sign of its gradient estimate
\operatorname{sign}(\mathbf{v}_{t}^{j})
to the parameter server.
The server then aggregates these sign values and sends the sign of resulting information
\operatorname{sign}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\operatorname{sign}(\mathbf{v}_{t}^{j})\right)
back to each node for updating. This approach ensures 1-bit communication in both directions.

However, we note that the
\operatorname{sign}(\cdot)
operation introduces bias in the estimation, and applying it twice can significantly amplify this bias, leading to slow convergence. To address this, we utilize an unbiased sign operation
\operatorname{S_{\textit{R}}}(\cdot)
, which is defined as follows:

Definition 1

For any vector
\mathbf{v}
with
\|\mathbf{v}\|_{\infty}\leq R
, the function
\operatorname{S_{\textit{R}}}(\mathbf{v})
is defined component-wise by:

\displaystyle[\operatorname{S_{\textit{R}}}(\mathbf{v})]_{k}=\begin{cases}1,&\text{with probability }\frac{R+[\mathbf{v}]_{k}}{2R},\\
\\
-1,&\text{with probability }\frac{R-[\mathbf{v}]_{k}}{2R}.\end{cases}

(9)

Remark:
This operation provides an unbiased estimate of
{\mathbf{v}}/{R}
, such that
\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{S_{\textit{R}}}(\mathbf{v})]={\mathbf{v}}/{R}
.

We can now introduce our majority vote signSGD with momentum updates. First, we use the momentum gradient estimator at each node
j
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}_{t}^{j}=(1-\beta)\mathbf{v}_{t-1}^{j}+\beta\nabla f_{j}(\mathbf{x}_{t};\xi_{t}^{j}),

(10)

where
\beta
is the momentum parameter. Next, using the unbiased sign operation
\operatorname{S_{\textit{G}}}(\cdot)
, we update the decision variable as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{t+1}=\mathbf{x}_{t}-\eta\operatorname{Sign}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\operatorname{S_{\textit{G}}}(\mathbf{v}_{t}^{j})\right).

(11)

Note that, under Assumption
9
, we have
\left\|\mathbf{v}_{t}\right\|_{\infty}\leq G
, so the operation
\operatorname{S_{\textit{G}}}(\cdot)
is valid.
After applying
\operatorname{S_{\textit{G}}}(\cdot)
, the output is a sign information, which can be efficiently transmitted between nodes. The complete algorithm is described in Algorithm
2
(v1), called Majority Vote SignSGD with Momentum (MVSM). In Step 4, when
t=1
, we initialize
\mathbf{v}_{1}^{j}=\nabla f_{j}(\mathbf{x}_{1};\xi_{1}^{j})
. Next, we present the convergence guarantees for the proposed algorithm.

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we demonstrate that signSGD with momentum update can achieve a convergence rate of
\mathcal{O}(T^{-1/4})
without the need for large batch sizes or the assumption of unimodal symmetric noise. Additionally, this result is derived under the weaker
l_{\infty}
-smoothness assumption, in contrast to the separable smoothness assumption required by prior methods. When analyzed under the
l_{2}
-smoothness assumption, our method achieves a convergence rate of
\mathcal{O}(d^{1/2}T^{-1/4})
, which improves upon the previous rate of
\mathcal{O}(dT^{-1/4})
.
For distributed settings, we establish convergence rates of
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{d^{1/2}}{T^{1/2}}+\frac{d}{n^{1/2}}\right)
and
\mathcal{O}\left(\max\left\{\frac{d^{1/4}}{T^{1/4}},\frac{d^{1/10}}{T^{1/5}}\right\}\right)
, which significantly outperform previous results of
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{d}{T^{1/4}}+\frac{d}{n^{1/2}}\right)
and
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{d^{3/8}}{T^{1/8}}\right)
. Finally, numerical experiments in different learning environments also validate the effectiveness of the proposed method.

Appendix A
Proof of Lemma
1

If the objective function is separable smoothness with some non-negative constant
\left[L_{1},L_{2},\dots,L_{d}\right]
, by setting
L_{\infty}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}L_{i}
, we can know that

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{y})

\displaystyle\leq f(\mathbf{x})+\left\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{x}),\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}L_{i}(\mathbf{y}_{i}-\mathbf{x}_{i})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq f(\mathbf{x})+\left\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{x}),\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}L_{i}\left\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq f(\mathbf{x})+\left\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{x}),\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}\right\rangle+\frac{L_{\infty}}{2}\left\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\right\|_{\infty}^{2},

where the second inequality is due to the fact that, for any
i
, we have
(\mathbf{y}_{i}-\mathbf{x}_{i})\leq\left\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\right\|_{\infty}
.

Appendix B
Proof of Theorem
1

Firstly, due to the
l_{2}
-smoothness assumption (Assumption
2
), we have that

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{x}_{t+1})

\displaystyle\leq f(\mathbf{x}_{t})+\left\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t}),\mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\|\mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\|^{2}

(13)

\displaystyle=f(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\eta\left\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t}),\operatorname{Sign}(\mathbf{v}_{t})\right\rangle+\frac{\eta^{2}L}{2}\|\operatorname{Sign}(\mathbf{v}_{t})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq f(\mathbf{x}_{t})+\eta\left\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t}),\operatorname{Sign}(\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t}))-\operatorname{Sign}(\mathbf{v}_{t})\right\rangle-\eta\left\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t}),\operatorname{Sign}(\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t}))\right\rangle+\frac{\eta^{2}Ld}{2}

\displaystyle=f(\mathbf{x}_{t})+\eta\left\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t}),\operatorname{Sign}(\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t}))-\operatorname{Sign}(\mathbf{v}_{t})\right\rangle-\eta\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})\|_{1}+\frac{\eta^{2}Ld}{2}

\displaystyle=f(\mathbf{x}_{t})+\eta\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left\langle[\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})]_{i},\operatorname{Sign}([\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})]_{i})-\operatorname{Sign}([\mathbf{v}_{t}]_{i})\right\rangle-\eta\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})\|_{1}+\frac{\eta^{2}Ld}{2}

\displaystyle\leq f(\mathbf{x}_{t})+\eta\sum_{i=1}^{d}2\left|[\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})]_{i}\right|\cdot\mathbb{I}\left(\operatorname{Sign}(\left[\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})\right]_{i})\neq\operatorname{Sign}([\mathbf{v}_{t}]_{i})\right)-\eta\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})\|_{1}

\displaystyle+\frac{\eta^{2}Ld}{2}

\displaystyle\leq f(\mathbf{x}_{t})+\eta\sum_{i=1}^{d}2|[\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})]_{i}-[\mathbf{v}_{t}]_{i}|\cdot\mathbb{I}\left(\operatorname{Sign}(\left[\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})\right]_{i})\neq\operatorname{Sign}([\mathbf{v}_{t}]_{i})\right)-\eta\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})\|_{1}

\displaystyle+\frac{\eta^{2}Ld}{2}

\displaystyle\leq f(\mathbf{x}_{t})+\eta\sum_{i=1}^{d}2|[\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})]_{i}-[\mathbf{v}_{t}]_{i}|-\eta\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})\|_{1}+\frac{\eta^{2}Ld}{2}

\displaystyle=f(\mathbf{x}_{t})+2\eta\left\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\mathbf{v}_{t}\right\|_{1}-\eta\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})\|_{1}+\frac{\eta^{2}Ld}{2}

\displaystyle\leq f(\mathbf{x}_{t})+2\eta\sqrt{d}\left\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\mathbf{v}_{t}\right\|-\eta\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})\|_{1}+\frac{\eta^{2}Ld}{2}.

Summing up and rearranging the equation (
13
), we derive:

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})\|_{1}\right]&\leq\frac{f(\mathbf{x}_{1})-f(\mathbf{x}_{T+1})}{\eta T}+2\sqrt{d}\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\mathbf{v}_{t}\|\right]+\frac{\eta Ld}{2}\\
&\leq\frac{\Delta_{f}}{\eta T}+2\sqrt{d}\cdot\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\mathbf{v}_{t}\|^{2}\right]}+\frac{\eta Ld}{2}.\end{split}

(14)

where we define
\Delta_{f}=f\left(\mathbf{x}_{1}\right)-f_{*}
, and the second inequality is due to Jensen’s Inequality.

Next, we can bound the term
\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\mathbf{v}_{t}\|^{2}\right]
as follows.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t+1})-\mathbf{v}_{t+1}\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|(1-\beta)\mathbf{v}_{t}+\beta\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t+1};\xi_{t+1})-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t+1})\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|(1-\beta)(\mathbf{v}_{t}-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t}))+\beta\left(\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t+1};\xi_{t+1})-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t+1})\right)\right.\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\left.\left.+(1-\beta)\left(\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t+1})\right)\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=(1-\beta)^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{v}_{t}-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})+\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t+1})\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad+\beta^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t+1};\xi_{t+1})-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t+1})\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq(1-\beta)^{2}(1+\beta)\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{v}_{t}-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t}))\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+(1-\beta)^{2}(1+\frac{1}{\beta})\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t+1})\right\|^{2}\right]+\beta^{2}\sigma^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\beta)\mathbb{E}\left[\|\mathbf{v}_{t}-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})\|^{2}\right]+\frac{2L^{2}}{\beta}\|\mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\|^{2}+\beta^{2}\sigma^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\beta)\mathbb{E}\left[\|\mathbf{v}_{t}-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})\|^{2}\right]+\frac{2\eta^{2}L^{2}d}{\beta}+\beta^{2}\sigma^{2},

where the third equality is due to the fact
\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t+1};\xi_{t+1})-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t+1})\right]=0
. Summing up, we can ensure

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\|\mathbf{v}_{t}-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{v}_{1}-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{1})\right\|^{2}\right]}{\beta T}+\frac{2\eta^{2}L^{2}d}{\beta^{2}}+\beta\sigma^{2}

(15)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sigma^{2}}{\beta T}+\frac{2\eta^{2}L^{2}d}{\beta^{2}}+\beta\sigma^{2}.

Incorporating the above into equation (
14
) and setting that
\beta=\mathcal{O}\left(T^{-1/2}\right)
,
\eta=\mathcal{O}\left(d^{-1/2}T^{-3/4}\right)
, we observe:

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})\|_{1}\right]&\leq\frac{\Delta_{f}}{\eta T}+2\sqrt{d}\cdot\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\mathbf{v}_{t}\|^{2}\right]}+\frac{\eta Ld}{2}\\
&\leq\frac{\Delta_{f}}{\eta T}+2\sqrt{d}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{\sigma^{2}}{\beta T}+\frac{2\eta^{2}L^{2}d}{\beta^{2}}+\beta\sigma^{2}}+\frac{\eta Ld}{2}\\
&=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\left(1+\Delta_{f}+\sigma+L\right)d^{1/2}}{T^{1/4}}\right)\\
&=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{d^{1/2}}{T^{1/4}}\right),\end{split}

which finishes the proof of Theorem
1
.

Appendix C
Proof of Theorem
2

Under Assumption
3
, we have the following bound:

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{x}_{t+1})\leq f(\mathbf{x}_{t})+\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t}),\mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\rangle+\frac{L_{\infty}}{2}\left\|\mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}.

Due to the update
\mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\mathbf{x}_{t}=-\eta\operatorname{Sign}{(\mathbf{v}_{t})}
, we have

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{x}_{t+1})-f(\mathbf{x}_{t})\leq

\displaystyle-\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t}),\eta\operatorname{Sign}{(\mathbf{v}_{t})}\rangle+\frac{L_{\infty}\eta^{2}}{2}\left\|\operatorname{Sign}{(\mathbf{v}_{t})}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t}),\eta\operatorname{Sign}{\left(\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})\right)}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\eta\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t}),\operatorname{Sign}{\left(\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})\right)}-\operatorname{Sign}{(\mathbf{v}_{t})}\rangle+\frac{L_{\infty}\eta^{2}}{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\eta\left\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})\right\|_{1}+2\eta\left\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\mathbf{v}_{t}\right\|_{1}+\frac{L_{\infty}\eta^{2}}{2},

where the last inequality is due to

\displaystyle\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t}),\operatorname{Sign}{\left(\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})\right)}-\operatorname{Sign}{(\mathbf{v}_{t})}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left[\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})\right]_{i}\cdot\left(\operatorname{Sign}{\left[\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})\right]_{i}}-\operatorname{Sign}{\left[\mathbf{v}_{t}\right]_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}2\left[\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})\right]_{i}\cdot\mathbb{I}\left(\operatorname{Sign}{\left(\left[\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})\right]_{i}\right)}\neq\operatorname{Sign}{\left[\mathbf{v}_{t}\right]_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}2\left|\left[\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})\right]_{i}-\left[\mathbf{v}_{t}\right]_{i}\right|\cdot\mathbb{I}\left(\operatorname{Sign}{\left[\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})\right]_{i}}\neq\operatorname{Sign}{\left[\mathbf{v}_{t}\right]_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}2\left|\left[\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})\right]_{i}-\left[\mathbf{v}_{t}\right]_{i}\right|=2\left\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\mathbf{v}_{t}\right\|_{1}.

Rearranging the obtained relation and summing up yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})\|_{1}\right]\leq\frac{\Delta_{f}}{\eta T}+2\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\mathbf{v}_{t}\|_{1}\right]+\frac{\eta L_{\infty}}{2},

(16)

where we define
\Delta_{f}=f\left(\mathbf{x}_{1}\right)-f_{*}
. Next, we proceed to bound the error term
\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\mathbf{v}_{t}\|_{1}\right]
. For convenience, we define the following notations:

\displaystyle\bm{\epsilon}_{t}:=\mathbf{v}_{t}-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t}),\quad\mathbf{n}_{t}:=\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t};\xi_{t})-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t}),\quad\mathbf{s}_{t}:=\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t-1})-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t}).

By definition, we have

\displaystyle\bm{\epsilon}_{t}=\mathbf{v}_{t}-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})=(1-\beta)\mathbf{v}_{t-1}+\beta\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t};\xi_{t})-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\beta)\left(\mathbf{v}_{t-1}-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t-1})\right)+(1-\beta)\left(\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t-1})-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})\right)+\beta\left(\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t};\xi_{t})-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\beta)\bm{\epsilon}_{t-1}+(1-\beta)\mathbf{s}_{t}+\beta\mathbf{n}_{t}.

Performing this recursively yields

\displaystyle\bm{\epsilon}_{t}=(1-\beta)^{t-1}\mathbf{n}_{1}+\beta\sum_{k=2}^{t}(1-\beta)^{t-k}\mathbf{n}_{k}+\sum_{k=2}^{t}(1-\beta)^{t-k+1}\mathbf{s}_{k},

where we use the fact that
\bm{\epsilon}_{1}=\mathbf{v}_{1}-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{1})=\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{1};\xi_{1})-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{1})=\mathbf{n}_{1}
.
We bound
\bm{\epsilon}_{t}
via two terms
\mathtt{A_{t}}
and
\mathtt{B_{t}}
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\bm{\epsilon}_{t}\right\|_{1}\right]\leq\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|(1-\beta)^{t-1}\mathbf{n}_{1}+\beta\sum_{k=2}^{t}(1-\beta)^{t-k}\mathbf{n}_{k}\right\|_{1}\right]}_{\mathtt{A_{t}}}+\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\sum_{k=2}^{t}(1-\beta)^{t-k+1}\mathbf{s}_{k}\right\|_{1}\right]}_{\mathtt{B_{t}}}

Firstly, we cope with
\mathtt{A_{t}}
following the similar procedure as in
Liu et al. 2025
. We denote the
i
-th element of the vector
\mathbf{n}_{t}
by
\mathbf{n}_{t,i}
. By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, for any
\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{d}>0
, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|(1-\beta)^{t-1}\mathbf{n}_{1}+\beta\sum_{k=2}^{t}(1-\beta)^{t-k}\mathbf{n}_{k}\right\|_{1}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}}\mathbb{E}\left[(1-\beta)^{t-1}\mathbf{n}_{1,i}+\beta\sum_{k=2}^{t}(1-\beta)^{t-k}\mathbf{n}_{k,i}\right]^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}}\left((1-\beta)^{2t-2}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{n}_{1,i}^{2}\right]+\beta^{2}\sum_{k=2}^{t}(1-\beta)^{2(t-k)}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{n}_{k,i}^{2}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}}\left((1-\beta)^{2t-2}\sigma_{i}^{2}+\beta^{2}\sum_{k=2}^{t}(1-\beta)^{2(t-k)}\sigma_{i}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\sigma_{i}^{2}}{\lambda_{i}}\left((1-\beta)^{2t-2}+\frac{\beta}{2-\beta}\right),

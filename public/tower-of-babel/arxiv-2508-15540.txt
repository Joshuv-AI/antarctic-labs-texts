Universal Statistics of Charges Exchanges in Non-Abelian Quantum Transport
Matteo Scandi, Gonzalo Manzano

Abstract
We derive detailed and intergral fluctuation relations as well as a Thermodynamic Uncertainty Relation constraining the exchange statistics of an arbitrary number of non-commuting conserved quantities among two quantum systems in transport setups arbitrary far from equilibrium. These universal relations, valid without the need of any efficacy parameter, extend the well-known heat exchange fluctuation theorems for energy and particle transport to the case of non-Abelian quantum transport, where the non-commutativity of the charges allows bending standard thermodynamic rules. In particular, we show that this can lead to apparent violations of the second law of thermodynamics, it enhances precision in the current fluctuations, and it allows for the inversion of all currents against their affinity biases.



Non-commutativity is at the heart of quantum physics, underpinning fundamental concepts such as uncertainty, superposition, and entanglement. It enables most of its powerful and paradoxical properties: from the violation of Bell inequalities and Wigner’s friend paradox
Bell and Aspect 2004
;
Brukner 2022
, to transport effects such as negative differential conductance
Rogge et al. 2006
, and quantum enhancements in computation and communication
Nielsen and Chuang 2000
;
Bartlett et al. 2007
. With the advent of the emerging field of quantum thermodynamics
Deffner and Campbell 2019
, the interplay of conservation laws with non-commutativity has been realized as a key feature that can be associated with genuine quantum features at small scales
Yunger Halpern et al. 2016
;
Guryanova et al. 2016
;
Lostaglio et al. 2017
, and may allow possible thermodynamic enhancements in nonequilibrium situations
Manzano et al. 2022
.

Thermodynamics traditionally assumes that globally conserved quantities (or charges) in transport scenarios commute between them. Typical examples are the exchange of energy and particles due to temperature gradients or differences in chemical potentials, where the charges are observables represented by quantum operators that commute, for example
[H,N]=0
. On the other hand, recent years have seen a raising interest in the thermodynamics of non-commuting charges (for a recent review see Ref.
Majidy et al. 2023
), that is, situations where the observables associated to the globally conserved quantities fail to commute, e.g. different angular momentum or spin components,
[S_{x},S_{y}]=iS_{z}
, as happens in certain types of spin chains that can be experimentally tested in the lab
Kranzl et al. 2023
. Other systems showing non-commuting charges include bosonic systems in squeezed thermal states
Manzano 2018
;
Manzano et al. 2016
;
Manzano et al. 2022
;
de Oliveira Junior and de Oliveira 2022
;
Shahidani 2024
, which has been used to implement nanomachines in the laboratory
Klaers et al. 2017
.

The absence of commutativity in the charges introduces nuances in the definition of thermal (equilibrium) states, where exact microcanocial ensembles cannot exist
Yunger Halpern et al. 2016
;
Yunger Halpern et al. 2020
, and their thermalization may deviate from the standard Eigenstate Thermalization Hypothesis
Majidy 2024
, leading to a non-commuting version of it
Murthy et al. 2023
. Tradeoffs in the extraction and average exchange of charges that do not commute have also been considered from the point of view of resource theories
Guryanova et al. 2016
;
Lostaglio et al. 2017
, where separated charge-specific batteries may be used to store work
Popescu et al. 2020
. Reversible protocols have also been constructed for systems in the presence of baths equipped with non-commuting charges
Manzano 2018
.

More recently, the role of fluctuations in the transport of non-commuting charges has started to be considered
Manzano et al. 2022
. In the linear regime, while the Onsager reciprocal relations
Onsager 1931a
;
Onsager 1931b
have been shown to hold, the fluctuation-dissipation relation
Callen and Welton 1951
;
Kubo 1966
is modified with the inclusion of an additional term due to the non-commutativity of the charges, which predicts reductions in the average entropy production with respect to the commutative case at the same level of fluctuations
Manzano et al. 2022
. In addition, different definitions of entropy production that are equivalent for commuting charges can differ from each other
Upadhyaya et al. 2024
, with fluctuations that may break standard universal relations from stochastic thermodynamics
Seifert 2012
. Similarly, non-commuting charges introduce subtleties in the thermodynamics of monitored quantum systems
Manzano and Zambrini 2022
.

Arbitrarily far from equilibrium, classical and quantum systems obey the so-called Exchange Fluctuation Theorem (XFT)
Jarzynski and Wójcik 2004
;
Andrieux et al. 2009
for a set of
N
commuting charges
\{Q_{1},Q_{2},...,Q_{N}\}
with
[Q_{i},Q_{j}]=0\penalty\ \forall i,j
, which reads:

\frac{P(\Delta\bf{Q})}{P(-\Delta\bf{Q})}=e^{\sum_{i}\delta\lambda_{i}\Delta Q_{i}},

(1)

where
\Delta{\bf{Q}}=\{\Delta Q_{i}\}
represent stochastic exchanges in charges
\{{Q}_{i}\}
during the process obtained from initial and final projective measurements, and
\delta\lambda_{i}
are the corresponding differences in affinities associated with each charge (inverse temperature differences, chemical potential differences, etc.). The above equality is valid for (initially uncorrelated) systems starting in local equilibrium distributions
Campisi et al. 2011
;
Esposito et al. 2009
,
and has been experimentally tested on a variety of platforms
Nakamura et al. 2010
;
Utsumi et al. 2010
;
Gomez-Solano et al. 2011
;
Hernández-Gómez et al. 2020
;
Hernández-Gómez et al. 2021
. Extensions have been derived which take into account initial classical correlations
Jevtic et al. 2015
, while initial quantum correlations can be captured in a recent version of the XFT using quasiprobability distributions
Levy and Lostaglio 2020
. Moreover, the XFT has been shown to be at the core of fundamental trade-off inequalities such as the Thermodynamic Uncertainty Relation
Hasegawa and Van Vu 2019
;
Timpanaro et al. 2019
.

However, the XFT (
1
) and related equalities and inequalities are expected to break down in the case of non-commuting charges (
[Q_{i},Q_{j}]\neq 0
for some
i,j
), where the introduction of non-invasive measurement schemes to assess the fluctuations is also not obvious
Lostaglio et al. 2023
;
Micadei et al. 2020
. An interesting question is how non-commutativity precisely affects current fluctuations in arbitrary out-of-equilibrium regimes, and if new exchange fluctuation relations taking into account the non-commutativity effects may arise in this case. In this Letter, we answer this question in the affirmative by deriving exchange fluctuation theorems for non-Abelian transport [Eqs.(
10
) and (
11
)]. These universal relations include an extra term accounting for the non-commutativity of the charges and provide a more accurate version of the second law governing the tradeoffs between currents [Eq. (
12
)] and of the Thermodynamic Uncertainty Relation [Eq. (
13
)].

Framework.
A generalized Gibbs state is uniquely identified by a set of scalars
{\bf\lambda}=\{\lambda_{i}\}
, called the affinities, and a set of associated observables
{\bf Q}=\{Q_{i}\}
or charges, corresponding to globally conserved quantities. Then, the generalized Gibbs state is given by
Jaynes 1957a
;
Jaynes 1957b
:

\displaystyle\pi_{\bf\lambda}=\frac{e^{-\sum_{i}\,\lambda_{i}Q_{i}}}{Z}=\frac{e^{-\mathcal{H}}}{Z}

(2)

where
Z=\mathrm{Tr}[e^{-\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}Q_{i}}]
is the partition function, and we have introduced the operator
\mathcal{H}:=\sum_{i}\,\lambda_{i}Q_{i}
. Generalized Gibbs states have traditionally been explored in the context of integrable and near-integrable systems
Rigol et al. 2007
;
Rigol 2009
;
Langen et al. 2015
and for commuting charges
Vaccaro and Barnett 2011
;
Croucher et al. 2017
;
Mur-Petit et al. 2018
. For situations where the charges do not commute with each other,
([Q_{i},Q_{j}]\neq 0)
, as we consider here, they have also been called non-Abelian thermal states
Yunger Halpern et al. 2016
;
Majidy et al. 2023
.

We consider the following setting: two baths
A
and
B
have been separately equilibrated to generalized Gibbs states
\pi_{\bf\lambda^{A}}^{(A)}
and
\pi_{\bf\lambda^{B}}^{(B)}
each with their own set of conserved charges,
\{Q_{i}^{(A)}\}
and
\{Q_{i}^{(B)}\}
, each representing the same globally conserved quantity, but different affinities
{\bf\lambda^{A}}
and
{\bf\lambda^{B}}
. Our goal is to study the currents of (globally conserved) non-commuting charges that are generated when the two baths are put in contact with each other. In order to do so, we consider a collisional model for these exchanges
Manzano et al. 2022
which can be divided into three steps:

1.

An environmental unit (molecule) is taken from each bath, leading to an initial product state
\rho_{0}=\pi^{A}_{\lambda^{A}}\otimes\pi^{B}_{\lambda^{B}}
, and are measured by projectors
\Pi^{A}
and
\Pi^{B}
in the eigenbasis of
\mathcal{H}_{A}
and
\mathcal{H}_{B}
respectively;

2.

The two units interact through the unitary operation
U=\mathbb{T}e^{-i\int_{0}^{\tau}dtH_{\rm int}(t)}
, where
H_{\rm int}
is a (possibly time-dependent) interaction Hamiltonian that couples the two baths during the interaction time
\tau
, and
\mathbb{T}
is the time-ordering operator;

3.

The units are measured again in the eigenbasis of
\mathcal{H}_{A}
and
\mathcal{H}_{B}
respectively, after which they return to their respective baths. Then a new collision takes place repeating this procedure.

In order for the currents to be considered as arising purely from the gradient in affinities between the two baths, one has to enforce the following condition for the interaction between the bath units for all charges
i
:

\displaystyle\left[U,Q^{A}_{i}+Q^{B}_{i}\right]=0\,,

(3)

which corresponds to the requirement that the unitary does not create extra charges, i.e., that the interaction is charge preserving (i.e.,
[H_{\rm int},Q^{A}_{i}+Q^{B}_{i}]=0
). We notice that this extra constraint is the only one that sets apart the framework studied here from the usual thermal setting with a single charge corresponding to the local Hamiltonians
Q_{A}=H_{A}
and
Q_{B}=H_{B}
and the affinities corresponding to the inverse temperatures. Indeed, in that case the only requirement is that
U
commutes with the local Hamiltonians,
[U,H_{A}+H_{B}]=0
, meaning that no work is introduced in the systems when the two baths interact, a condition that is typically known as strict energy conservation
Brandão et al. 2013
;
Horodecki and Oppenheim 2013
;
Korzekwa et al. 2016
. In the general case, the extra set of constraints in Eq. (
3
) restricts the allowed interactions and conceptually justifies the interpretation of transport for the exchange of charges.

Depiction of the collisional exchange model. Particles from each bath are projectively measured in the eigenbasis of

(
\mathcal{H}_{B}
) giving outcome

(

), they interact with each other through the charge-preserving unitary operation
U
, and, finally, they are measured again giving outcome

Given this situation, it is interesting to explore how Eq. (
3
) affects the statistics of charge exchanges in the collisions. To this end, let us introduce the notion of a trajectory
\gamma:=\{(n,\nu),(m,\mu)\}
from the projective measurement outcomes in the process. This corresponds to the case in which an experimenter performs the first measurements in both units in the eigenbasis of
\mathcal{H}_{A}
and
\mathcal{H}_{B}
obtaining results
h_{n}^{A}
and
h_{\nu}^{B}
associated with outputs
n
and
\nu
(greek letters are reserved for units of bath
B
). Then the units evolve according to
U
. For simplicity, we assume that the interaction Hamiltonian
H_{\rm int}
is suddenly switched on and off at the beginning and at the end of the interval, i.e.,
H_{\rm int}(0)=H_{\rm int}(\tau)=0
, and that it is constant for
0<t<\tau
. The experimenter finally performs a new measurement of
\mathcal{H}_{A}
and
\mathcal{H}_{B}
obtaining results
h_{m}^{A}
and
h_{\mu}^{B}
associated with outputs
m
and
\mu
(see Fig.
1
). In the following, we will use the eigendecomposition of
\mathcal{H}_{A}:=\sum_{i}\,h_{i}^{A}\,\left.\left|{h^{A}_{i}}\right.\right\rangle\!\!\left.\left\langle{h^{A}_{i}}\right.\right|
, together with the notation
\Delta h^{A}(n\rightarrow m):=h^{A}_{m}-h^{A}_{n}
(and similarly for bath
B
). Finally, it is also convenient to rewrite the affinities of bath
A
as
\lambda^{A}_{i}=\lambda^{B}_{i}+\delta\lambda_{i}
.

Using the above framework, it is easy to compute the probability associated to the trajectory
\gamma
representing a single collision, which reads:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-(h_{n}^{A}+h_{\nu}^{B})}}{Z}\;|\!\left\langle h_{m,\mu}\right|U\left|h_{n,\nu}\right\rangle|^{2}\,,

(4)

where
\left.\left|{h_{n,\nu}}\right.\right\rangle
are product state eigenvectors
\left.\left|{h_{n,\nu}}\right.\right\rangle\equiv\left.\left|{h^{A}_{n}}\right.\right\rangle\otimes\left.\left|{h^{B}_{\nu}}\right.\right\rangle
(and similarly for
\left.\left|{h_{m,\mu}}\right.\right\rangle
. Then, as detailed in the Supplemental Material (see Sec.
S1
), we derive the conservation relation:

\begin{split}\Delta h^{A}({n\rightarrow m})&+\Delta h^{B}({\nu\rightarrow\mu})=\\
&=\sum_{i}\;\delta\lambda_{i}\,\Delta Q_{i}({n\rightarrow m})+\Delta({\gamma})\,,\end{split}

(5)

where
\Delta Q_{i}({n\rightarrow m}):=\left\langle h_{m}^{A}\right|Q^{A}_{i}\left|h_{m}^{A}\right\rangle-\left\langle h_{n}^{A}\right|Q_{i}^{A}\left|h_{n}^{A}\right\rangle
, that is the change in the expectation value of charge
Q^{A}_{i}
when passing from
\left.\left|{h_{n}^{A}}\right.\right\rangle
to
\left.\left|{h_{m}^{A}}\right.\right\rangle
, whereas
\Delta(\gamma)
is a stochastic correction term reading:

\displaystyle\Delta(\gamma)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,k\\
k\neq m\end{subarray}}\;\delta\lambda_{i}\left(\left.\left\langle{h_{m}^{A}}\right.\right|Q_{i}^{A}\left.\left|{h_{k}^{A}}\right.\right\rangle\frac{\left.\left\langle{h_{k,\mu}}\right.\right|H_{\rm int}\left.\left|{h_{n,\nu}}\right.\right\rangle}{\left.\left\langle{h_{m,\mu}}\right.\right|H_{\rm int}\left.\left|{h_{n,\nu}}\right.\right\rangle}\right)+

\displaystyle-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,k\\
k\neq n\end{subarray}}\delta\lambda_{i}\left(\left.\left\langle{h_{k}^{A}}\right.\right|Q_{i}^{A}\left.\left|{h_{n}^{A}}\right.\right\rangle\frac{\left.\left\langle{h_{m,\mu}}\right.\right|H_{\rm int}\left.\left|{h_{k,\nu}}\right.\right\rangle}{\left.\left\langle{h_{m,\mu}}\right.\right|H_{\rm int}\left.\left|{h_{n,\nu}}\right.\right\rangle}\right)\,.

(6)

Two properties of
\Delta(\gamma)
allow us to clarify its meaning (see the Supplemental Material Sec.
S1
for the proofs): first,
\Delta(\gamma)
is a real number, and it is zero for all trajectories if and only if all charges commute with each other (that is,
[Q_{i},Q_{j}]=0
for all
i,j
). For this reason,
\Delta(\gamma)
can be identified as a signature of the lack of commutativity of charges. Secondly, when defining an analogous correction
\Delta({\tilde{\gamma}})
associated with the reversed trajectory
\tilde{\gamma}:=\{(m,\mu),(n,\nu)\}
coming from the inverse transition
\left.\left|{h_{m,\mu}}\right.\right\rangle\rightarrow\left.\left|{h_{n,\nu}}\right.\right\rangle
, we have that
\Delta({\tilde{\gamma}})=-\Delta(\gamma)
. These two properties are used in the following to derive non-trivial fluctuation relations for non-commuting charges.

Non-Abelian Fluctuation Relations.
We now proceed to derive universal exchange fluctuation relations at the level of trajectories in the framework discussed above. These connect the probability associated with the trajectory
\gamma:=\{(n,\nu),(m,\mu)\}
with the probability of its time-reversed trajectory
\tilde{\gamma}:=\{(m,\mu),(n,\nu)\}
in the backward process. A formal definition of the backward (or reversed) process can be found in the Supplemental Material, Sec.
S2
. We obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{P}(\gamma)}{\mathbb{P}(\tilde{\gamma})}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-(h_{n}^{A}+h_{\nu}^{B})}}{e^{-(h_{m}^{A}+h_{\mu}^{B})}}\;\frac{|\!\left\langle h_{m,\mu}\right|U\left|h_{n,\nu}\right\rangle|^{2}}{|\!\left\langle h_{n,\nu}\right|U^{\dagger}\left|h_{m,\mu}\right\rangle|^{2}}

\displaystyle=e^{\Delta h^{A}({n\rightarrow m})+\Delta h^{B}({\nu\rightarrow\mu})}\,,

(7)

which is valid for generic trajectories
\gamma
with
\mathbb{P}(\tilde{\gamma})\neq 0
whenever
\mathbb{P}(\gamma)\neq 0
, that is, whenever the system does not present absolute irreversibility
Murashita et al. 2014
. This relation follows from micro-reversibility of the unitary evolution
\tilde{U}=\Theta U^{\dagger}\Theta^{-1}
with
\Theta
the anti-unitary time-reversal operator
Campisi et al. 2011
;
Manzano et al. 2018
;
Manzano and Zambrini 2022
. For simplicity, we assumed that the interaction Hamiltonian and the charges are time-reversal symmetric,
i.e., they do not contain odd variables under time reversal such as velocities or momenta. That assumption makes the backward process essentially equal to the forward one, leading to same probability measures in the numerator and denominator in the l.h.s. of Eq. (
Universal Statistics of Charges Exchanges in Non-Abelian Quantum Transport
). Nonetheless, this assumption can be lifted, and we do so in Supplemental Material, Sec.
S2
.
Combining Eq. (
Universal Statistics of Charges Exchanges in Non-Abelian Quantum Transport
) with Eq. (
5
) we obtain the relation:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{P}(\gamma)}{\mathbb{P}(\tilde{\gamma})}=e^{\sum_{i}\delta\lambda_{i}\Delta Q_{i}({n\rightarrow m})\,+\,\Delta({\gamma})}\,.

(8)

This is valid at the trajectory level but, thanks to its functional dependency, it allows us to group together trajectories with the same average exchanges of charges. In particular, we can define the probability distribution for charge currents as:

\displaystyle P(\Delta Q_{1},\dots,\Delta Q_{N};\Delta)=\sum_{\gamma}\;\mathbb{P}(\gamma)\,\delta\left(\Delta Q_{1}-\Delta Q_{1}({n\rightarrow m})\right)

\displaystyle\times\dots\delta\left(\Delta Q_{N}-\Delta Q_{N}({n\rightarrow m})\right)\penalty\ \delta\left(\Delta-\Delta(\gamma)\right)\,.

(9)

Then, grouping together trajectories with the same
\Delta Q_{i}
and
\Delta
allows us to derive our main result, namely, the XFT for non-commuting charges:

\displaystyle\frac{P(\Delta Q_{1},\dots,\Delta Q_{N};\Delta)}{P(-\Delta Q_{1},\dots,-\Delta Q_{N};-\Delta)}=e^{\sum_{i}{\delta\lambda_{i}\Delta Q_{i}}\,+\,\Delta}\,,

(10)

which puts stringent constraints on the tails of the current probability distribution of the charges
{Q_{i}}
(the proof and a generalization for nonsymmetric charges and interaction are provided in the Supplemental Material, Sec.
S3
). The above XFT generalizes the standard XFT in Eq. (
1
) derived in Refs.
Jarzynski and Wójcik 2004
;
Andrieux et al. 2009
. In this context, it should be noticed that the presence of the quantum correction
\Delta
is fundamental for the validity of the non-Abelian XFT above: indeed, trajectories
\gamma
with the same change in the charges
\Delta Q_{i}(n\rightarrow m)
, can still differ in their value of
\Delta(\gamma)
.

The above detailed fluctuation relation implies the following integral (Jarzynski-like) version of the XFT:

\displaystyle\left\langle e^{-\sum_{i}{\delta\lambda_{i}\Delta Q_{i}}\,-\,\Delta}\right\rangle_{\penalty\ }=1\,,

(11)

where the averages are taken with respect to the probability distribution
P(\Delta Q_{1},\dots,\Delta Q_{N};\Delta)
. As shown in the Supplemental Material Sec.
S4
, the presence of the quantum correction
\Delta
in the integral XFT above allows for a probability distribution of
\sum_{i}{\delta\lambda_{i}\Delta Q_{i}}
that is more skewed towards negative values compared to the case of commuting charges.

), together with its standard version for commutative charges; b) second-law inequality in Eq. (

\beta^{A}=\beta^{B}
, whereas in the bottom row

Applying Jensen’s inequality, Eq. (
11
) automatically implies the second-law inequality:

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\delta\lambda_{i}\left\langle\Delta Q_{i}\right\rangle_{\penalty\ }\geq-\left\langle\Delta\right\rangle_{,}\penalty\

(12)

where the average currents (charge changes per collision) are
\left\langle\Delta Q_{i}\right\rangle=\mathrm{Tr}[Q_{i}(U\rho_{0}U^{\dagger}-\rho_{0})]
and the correction term
\left\langle\Delta\right\rangle=\left\langle\Delta h^{A}\right\rangle+\left\langle\Delta h^{B}\right\rangle-\sum_{i}\;\delta\lambda_{i}\,\left\langle\Delta Q_{i}\right\rangle
, which as mentioned above, can be either positive or negative.
We remark that
\Delta
is identically zero if and only if all charges commute with each other. Then the presence of the quantum correction
\langle\Delta\rangle
in Eq. (
12
) from non-commutativity of the charges allows for apparent violations of the second law in the average charge currents (when
\langle\Delta\rangle\geq 0
) as compared to standard Abelian transport.

Finally, since the XFT in Eq. (
10
) is a so-called strong fluctuation theorem
Luposchainsky et al. 2013
, we can directly apply the results in Ref.
Hasegawa and Van Vu 2019
;
Timpanaro et al. 2019
to derive an associated Thermodynamic Uncertainty Relation:

\frac{{\rm Var}[\Delta Q_{j}]}{\langle\Delta Q_{j}\rangle^{2}}\geq\frac{2}{e^{\sum_{i}\delta\lambda_{i}\langle\Delta Q_{i}\rangle+\langle\Delta\rangle}-1},

(13)

which is valid for any current of non-commuting charges
\Delta Q_{j}
with
j=1,...,N
(the proof is given in the Supplemental Material, Sec.
S5
). We note that, whenever
\langle\Delta\rangle\geq 0
, the TUR (
13
) predicts the possibility of improved current precision as compared to the one allowed by the TUR for commuting charges (
\Delta=0
). This reveals that the role of non-commutativity in providing a thermodynamic advantage goes beyond the case of average currents.

Illustrative Example.
We exemplify our findings with the interaction between spin-1/2 particles with Hamiltonian
H=\hbar\omega\sigma_{z}
(we take for simplicity
\hbar\omega=1
) from baths with two non-commuting charges corresponding to
Q_{1}=H
and
Q_{2}=\sigma_{x}
, as described by the generalized Gibbs state:

\displaystyle\pi_{\lambda}:=\frac{e^{-\beta\sigma_{z}-\chi\sigma_{x}}}{Z}\,,

(14)

with
\sigma_{z}
and
\sigma_{x}
being the usual Pauli operators. The corresponding affinities are
\lambda_{1}=\beta
and
\lambda_{2}=\chi
. In the collisional picture of Fig.
1
a particle from each bath (with affinities
\lambda^{A}=\{\beta^{A},\chi^{A}\}
and
\lambda^{B}=\{\beta^{B},\chi^{B}\}
) is measured in the basis of
\mathcal{H}_{i}=\beta^{i}\sigma_{z}^{(i)}+\chi^{i}\sigma_{x}^{(i)}
with
i=A,B
, interacts through the unitary
U
, and finally returns to its own bath after being measured again. The constraint in Eq. (
3
) singles out generalized swaps as the unique allowed interaction Hamiltonian (see Supplemental Material, Sec.
S6
for details).

We are now ready to compute and test the currents probability distribution
P(\Delta\sigma_{z},\Delta\sigma_{x};\Delta)
to verify the XFT in Eq. (
1
), and, in particular, it is interesting to compare it with the marginal distribution

P(\Delta\sigma_{z},\Delta\sigma_{x}):=\int{\rm d}\Delta\;P(\Delta\sigma_{z},\Delta\sigma_{x};\Delta)\,
.
Indeed, as exemplified in Fig.
2
.a), while the integral XFT in Eq. (
11
) is always verified, the standard fluctuation theorem can be broken, i.e.

\left\langle e^{-\sum_{i}{\delta\lambda_{i}\Delta Q_{i}}}\right\rangle\neq 1.

Moreover, since
\Delta_{\gamma}
is different from zero for all trajectories if and only if the conserved charges do not commute among each other; the eventual verification of the standard fluctuation theorem would witness the effective commutation of the charges involved in the process.

The behavior of the second-law inequality Eq. (
12
) with and without quantum correction is plotted in Fig.
2
.b). There, we can appreciate regions with apparent violations of the second law, whenever the quantum correction term
\langle\Delta\rangle
is not incorporated. These regions are highlighted with a shaded area and are clearly related with a large instability of the standard integral XFT (see Fig.
2
.a).

It is interesting to point out that the apparent violations of the second law can also lead to inversions in the currents with respect to the expected spontaneous flow, a purely quantum effect. To be more precise, we observe an inversion of the standard behavior of both currents
\left\langle\Delta\sigma_{z}\right\rangle
and
\left\langle\Delta\sigma_{x}\right\rangle
against their respective affinity differences. This is opposed to the case where the inversion with respect to the affinity gradient occurs only in one of the two currents, whereas the standard flow in the other current compensates for the first (i.e., when there is a trade-off between two resources
Guryanova et al. 2016
). This behavior is possible only due to a non-zero term
\left\langle\Delta\right\rangle
, and is thus a direct consequence of the non-commutativity of the charges. In Fig.
2
.c) we show two such possibilities, where
\beta^{A}\equiv\beta^{B}
and
\beta^{A}<\beta^{B}
.

To be more precise, the natural direction of the coherence current
\left\langle\Delta\sigma_{x}\right\rangle
is from a smaller affinity to a larger one (in analogy to what would happen for inverse temperatures
\beta
and energy currents). This means that for
\chi^{A}>\chi^{B}
one would expect
\left\langle\Delta\sigma_{x}\right\rangle\geq 0
, and the other way round. Then, an inversion of
\left\langle\Delta\sigma_{x}\right\rangle
occurs if
\left\langle\Delta\sigma_{x}\right\rangle\cdot\delta\chi<0
.
Similarly, an inversion of the energy current
\left\langle\Delta\sigma_{z}\right\rangle
occurs if
\left\langle\Delta\sigma_{z}\right\rangle\cdot\delta\beta<0
. In Fig.
2
.c) we observe some parameter regions for which the energy current from the hot to the cold bath (
\beta^{A}<\beta^{B}
, and
\langle\Delta\sigma_{z}\rangle\leq 0
), is used to invert the coherent current
\langle\Delta\sigma_{x}\rangle
against the bias (
\chi_{A}<\chi_{B}
), as in the central, unshaded area of the bottom plot in Fig.
2
.c). However, more striking situations are highlighted in the orange shaded areas of the plots, where we see that both current inversions can happen at the same time, exemplifying the possibility of using non-commutative charges to generate currents that are opposite to the affinities gradient.

Conclusions.
We have seen that the statistics of charge currents in non-Abelian transport obey universal nonequilibrium relations which can be considered a generalization of detailed and integral XFT to non-commuting charges, that is Eqs. (
10
) and (
11
), together with inequalities (
12
) and (
13
). These provide a refinement of the second law for non-Abelian transport with a quantum correction term that allows classically forbidden behavior such as the inversion of all currents against their affinity biases or enhanced precision in the current fluctuations.

For the derivation of the XFT we used a collisional transport framework following Ref.
Manzano et al. 2022
, over which we introduced a noninvasive two-point measurement scheme which suits typical transport setups where the two baths exchanging charges are effectively uncorrelated to each other. Extensions of our results might be considered by incorporating initial (classical) correlations between the baths similarly to Ref.
Jevtic et al. 2015
or quantum ones following Ref.
Micadei et al. 2020
. A comparison with alternative methods based on quasi-probability distributions is also desirable
Levy and Lostaglio 2020
. Experimental tests of the XFT, the TUR and the second-law inequality for non-Abelian transport might be implemented for spin chains on trapped ion platforms
Kranzl et al. 2023
or for squeezed thermal reservoirs
Klaers et al. 2017
.

Derivation of the conservation relation in Eq. (5)

The charges conservation relations in Eq. (
3

[H_{\rm int},Q^{A}_{i}+Q^{B}_{i}]=0
for all

\lambda_{i}^{B}
, and we sum over different charges, these then yield:

\displaystyle 0=\sum_{i}\;\lambda^{B}_{i}\,\left[{H}_{\rm int},Q_{i}^{A}+Q_{i}^{B}\right]=[{H}_{\rm int},\mathcal{H}_{A}+\mathcal{H}_{B}]-\sum_{i}\;\delta\lambda_{i}\left[{H}_{\rm int},Q_{i}^{A}\right]\,,

(S1)

where we used the definition
\mathcal{H}^{X}=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}^{X}Q_{i}^{X}
for
X=A,B

\left.\left|{h_{m,\mu}}\right.\right\rangle
and
\left.\left|{h_{n,\nu}}\right.\right\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad\left.\left\langle{h_{m,\mu}}\right.\right|\left[H_{\rm int},\mathcal{H}_{A}+\mathcal{H}_{B}\right]\left.\left|{h_{n,\nu}}\right.\right\rangle=\sum_{i}\;\delta\lambda_{i}\left.\left\langle{h_{m,\mu}}\right.\right|\left[H_{\rm int},Q_{i}^{A}\right]\left.\left|{h_{n,\nu}}\right.\right\rangle,

(S2)

which, thanks to the product structure of the eigenstates of
\mathcal{H}_{A}+\mathcal{H}_{B}

\displaystyle\left.\left\langle{h_{m,\mu}}\right.\right|H_{\rm int}\left.\left|{h_{n,\nu}}\right.\right\rangle\boldsymbol{(}(h^{A}_{m}-h^{A}_{n})+(h^{B}_{\mu}-h^{B}_{\nu})\boldsymbol{)}=-\sum_{i}\;\delta\lambda_{i}\left.\left\langle{h_{m,\mu}}\right.\right|\left[H_{\rm int},Q_{i}^{A}\right]\left.\left|{h_{n,\nu}}\right.\right\rangle\,.

(S3)

S3

\displaystyle\Delta h^{A}(n\rightarrow m)+\Delta h^{B}(\nu\rightarrow\mu)=-\sum_{i}\;\delta\lambda_{i}\frac{\left.\left\langle{h_{m,\mu}}\right.\right|\left[H_{\rm int},Q_{i}^{A}\right]\left.\left|{h_{n,\nu}}\right.\right\rangle}{\left.\left\langle{h_{m,\mu}}\right.\right|H_{\rm int}\left.\left|{h_{n,\nu}}\right.\right\rangle},

\Delta h^{A}({n\rightarrow m}):=(h^{A}_{m}-h^{A}_{n})

\Delta h^{B}(\nu\rightarrow\mu)

\displaystyle-\sum_{i}\;\delta\lambda_{i}\frac{\left.\left\langle{h_{m,\mu}}\right.\right|\left[H_{\rm int},Q_{i}^{A}\right]\left.\left|{h_{n,\nu}}\right.\right\rangle}{\left.\left\langle{h_{m,\mu}}\right.\right|H_{\rm int}\left.\left|{h_{n,\nu}}\right.\right\rangle}=\sum_{i,k}\;\delta\lambda_{i}\left(\left.\left\langle{h_{m}^{A}}\right.\right|Q_{i}^{A}\left.\left|{h_{k}^{A}}\right.\right\rangle\frac{\left.\left\langle{h_{k,\mu}}\right.\right|H_{\rm int}\left.\left|{h_{n,\nu}}\right.\right\rangle}{\left.\left\langle{h_{m,\mu}}\right.\right|H_{\rm int}\left.\left|{h_{n,\nu}}\right.\right\rangle}-\frac{\left.\left\langle{h_{m,\mu}}\right.\right|H_{\rm int}\left.\left|{h_{k,\nu}}\right.\right\rangle}{\left.\left\langle{h_{m,\mu}}\right.\right|H_{\rm int}\left.\left|{h_{n,\nu}}\right.\right\rangle}\left.\left\langle{h_{k}^{A}}\right.\right|Q_{i}^{A}\left.\left|{h_{n}^{A}}\right.\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\;\delta\lambda_{i}\,\Delta Q_{i}({n\rightarrow m})+\Delta(\gamma)

\displaystyle\Delta(\gamma):=\sum_{i,k\neq m}\;\delta\lambda_{i}\left(\left.\left\langle{h_{m}}\right.\right|Q_{i}^{A}\left.\left|{h_{k}}\right.\right\rangle\frac{\left.\left\langle{h_{k,\mu}}\right.\right|H_{\rm int}\left.\left|{h_{n,\nu}}\right.\right\rangle}{\left.\left\langle{h_{m,\mu}}\right.\right|H_{\rm int}\left.\left|{h_{n,\nu}}\right.\right\rangle}\right)-\sum_{i,k\neq n}\delta\lambda_{i}\left(\left.\left\langle{h_{k}}\right.\right|Q_{i}^{A}\left.\left|{h_{n}}\right.\right\rangle\frac{\left.\left\langle{h_{m,\mu}}\right.\right|H_{\rm int}\left.\left|{h_{k,\nu}}\right.\right\rangle}{\left.\left\langle{h_{m,\mu}}\right.\right|H_{\rm int}\left.\left|{h_{n,\nu}}\right.\right\rangle}\right)\,,

(S6)

which was introduced in Eq. (
6
) in the main text. Using these identifications we can now rearrange Eq. (

\Delta h^{A}({n\rightarrow m})+\Delta h^{B}({\nu\rightarrow\mu})=\sum_{i}\;\delta\lambda_{i}\,\Delta Q_{i}({n\rightarrow m})+\Delta({\gamma})\,

(S7)

5
) of the main text. This equation can be interpreted as follows: each
\Delta Q_{i}({n\rightarrow m})
corresponds to the stochastic exchange in the charge
Q_{i}
between the beginning and the end of the collision (as measured in system
A
). Moreover,
\Delta({\gamma})
is a correction solely arising from the non-commutativity of the conserved charges.

This can be understood as follows: suppose that the charges would commute, that is for all
i
and

[Q_{i},Q_{j}]=0

[\mathcal{H},Q_{i}]=0
. Since this is equivalent to the existence of a shared eigenbasis, we also have that all off-diagonal terms of the form
\left.\left\langle{h_{l}^{A}}\right.\right|Q_{i}^{A}\left.\left|{h_{k}^{A}}\right.\right\rangle

It should also be noticed that
\Delta({\gamma})

\Delta Q_{i}({n\rightarrow m})

A

\displaystyle A_{i}-A_{j}=\frac{\left\langle A_{i}\right|[\bar{A},B]\left|A_{j}\right\rangle}{\left\langle A_{i}\right|B\left|A_{j}\right\rangle}\,.

(S8)

Since the quantity on the left is real, the same has to hold for the quantity on the right as well. This proves that the right hand side of Eq. (

Finally, consider the correction associated to the backward trajectory

\Delta h^{B}({\nu\rightarrow\mu})

\displaystyle\Delta({\tilde{\gamma}})=\sum_{i,k\neq n}\;\delta\lambda_{i}\left(\left.\left\langle{h_{n}^{A}}\right.\right|Q_{i}^{A}\left.\left|{h_{k}^{A}}\right.\right\rangle\frac{\left.\left\langle{h_{k,\nu}}\right.\right|H_{\rm int}\left.\left|{h_{m,\mu}}\right.\right\rangle}{\left.\left\langle{h_{n,\nu}}\right.\right|H_{\rm int}\left.\left|{h_{m,\mu}}\right.\right\rangle}\right)\;-\sum_{i,k\neq m}\delta\lambda_{i}\left(\left.\left\langle{h_{k}^{A}}\right.\right|Q_{i}^{A}\left.\left|{h_{m}^{A}}\right.\right\rangle\frac{\left.\left\langle{h_{n,\nu}}\right.\right|H_{\rm int}\left.\left|{h_{k,\mu}}\right.\right\rangle}{\left.\left\langle{h_{n,\nu}}\right.\right|H_{\rm int}\left.\left|{h_{m,\mu}}\right.\right\rangle}\right)=

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,k\neq n}\;\delta\lambda_{i}\left(\left.\left\langle{h_{k}^{A}}\right.\right|Q_{i}^{A}\left.\left|{h_{n}^{A}}\right.\right\rangle\frac{\left.\left\langle{h_{m,\mu}}\right.\right|H_{\rm int}\left.\left|{h_{k,\nu}}\right.\right\rangle}{\left.\left\langle{h_{m,\mu}}\right.\right|H_{\rm int}\left.\left|{h_{n,\nu}}\right.\right\rangle}\right)\;-\sum_{i,k\neq m}\delta\lambda_{i}\left(\left.\left\langle{h_{m}^{A}}\right.\right|Q_{i}^{A}\left.\left|{h_{k}^{A}}\right.\right\rangle\frac{\left.\left\langle{h_{k,\mu}}\right.\right|H_{\rm int}\left.\left|{h_{n,\nu}}\right.\right\rangle}{\left.\left\langle{h_{m,\mu}}\right.\right|H_{\rm int}\left.\left|{h_{n,\nu}}\right.\right\rangle}\right)=-\Delta(\gamma)\,,

S2
Time-reversed trajectories and backward process

Here we provide a retailed definition of the backward process standardly used in the context of quantum and stochastic thermodynamics
Manzano et al. 2018
;
Manzano and Zambrini 2022
;
Campisi et al. 2011
;
Esposito et al. 2009
, which consist on the implementation of the time-reversed protocol or sequence of steps defined in the collisional model introduced in the main text. Moreover, for the shake of generality we may consider charges that may be either even or odd under time-reversal, that is
\Theta Q_{i}\Theta^{-1}=\pm Q_{i}
, for which we need to introduce the anti-unitary time-reversal operator in quantum mechanics
\Theta
, and consider all measurements in the two-point-measurement scheme associated to the transformed (time-reversal) observables
\Theta\mathcal{H}_{X}\Theta^{-1}=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}^{X}\Theta Q_{i}^{X}\Theta^{-1}
.

Using the above prescriptions, the backward process of the collisional model reads:

1.

An environmental unit (molecule) is taken from each bath, leading to an initial product state
\tilde{\rho}_{0}=\Theta\left(\pi^{A}_{\lambda^{A}}\otimes\pi^{B}_{\lambda^{B}}\right)\Theta^{-1}
, and is measured by projectors
\Theta\Pi^{A}\Theta^{-1}
and
\Theta\Pi^{B}\Theta^{-1}
in the eigenbasis of
\Theta\mathcal{H}_{A}\Theta^{-1}
and
\Theta\mathcal{H}_{B}\Theta^{-1}
respectively;

2.

The two units interact through the (backward) unitary operation
\tilde{U}=\mathbb{T}e^{-i\int_{0}^{\tau}dt\Theta H_{\rm int}(\tau-t)\Theta^{-1}}
, where
H_{\rm int}
is the same interaction Hamiltonian as in the original (forward) process that couples the two baths during the interaction time
\tau
;

3.

The units are measured again in the eigenbasis of
\Theta\mathcal{H}_{A}\Theta^{-1}
and
\Theta\mathcal{H}_{B}\Theta^{-1}
respectively, after which they return to their respective baths.

It is important to notice that in the backward process the equivalent charge-preserving condition for the interaction between the bath units for all charges
i
is verified, as we have that:

\displaystyle\left[\tilde{U},\Theta\left(Q^{A}_{i}+Q^{B}_{i}\right)\Theta^{-1}\right]=\Theta\left[{U},\left(Q^{A}_{i}+Q^{B}_{i}\right)\right]\Theta^{-1}=0\,,

(S10)

where the last equality follows directly from Eq. (
3
) of the main text. Given that the switching protocol of the interaction Hamiltonian
H_{\rm int}
is symmetric under time reversal, the condition above can be expressed also as
[\Theta H_{\rm int}\Theta^{-1},\Theta\left(Q^{A}_{i}+Q^{B}_{i}\right)\Theta^{-1}]=0
, in terms of the interaction Hamiltonian of the collisions
H_{\rm int}(t)
.

Let’s now calculate the probability in the backward process of a trajectory
\tilde{\gamma}=\{(m,\mu),(n,\nu)\}
, that is, the inverse trajectory of the trajectory
\gamma=\{(n,\nu),(m,\mu)\}
discussed in the main text. In such a trajectory, the results of the first measurement in the backward protocol described above are
h_{m}^{A}
and
h_{\mu}^{B}
, while after interaction of the environmental units through unitary
\tilde{U}
, the second measurement gives results
h_{n}^{A}
and
h_{\nu}^{B}
. The probability of the inverse trajectory
\tilde{\gamma}
in the backward process reads:

\tilde{\mathbb{P}}(\tilde{\gamma})=\frac{e^{-(h_{m}^{A}+h_{\mu}^{B})}}{Z}|\langle h_{n,\nu}|\Theta^{-1}\tilde{U}\Theta|h_{m,\mu}\rangle.

(S11)

Notice that above we denoted the probability in the backward process
\tilde{\mathbb{P}}
(with tilde) to distinguish it from the one calculated in the forward process
\mathbb{P}
(without tilde). However, it follows that the probability
\tilde{\mathbb{P}}
equals
\mathbb{P}
in the important case in which
\Theta H_{\rm int}\Theta^{-1}=H_{\rm int}
and the switching protocol is time-symmetric (we also remark that
H(0)=H(\tau)=0
), which implies
\tilde{U}=U
, and the charges are even under time-reversal (
\Theta Q_{i}^{X}\Theta^{-1}=Q_{i}^{X}
), which implies that the measurement projectors in backward process
\Theta\Pi^{X}\Theta^{-1}
are equal to the ones appearing in the original (forward process). In that case:

|\langle h_{n,\nu}|\Theta^{-1}\tilde{U}\Theta|h_{m,\mu}\rangle=|\langle h_{n,\nu}|\tilde{U}|h_{m,\mu}\rangle=|\langle h_{n,\nu}|U|h_{m,\mu}\rangle=|\langle h_{n,\nu}|U^{\dagger}|h_{m,\mu}\rangle,

(S12)

and the above expression in Eq. (
S11
) coincides with the probability of the inverse trajectory
\tilde{\gamma}
in the forward process, that is,
\tilde{\mathbb{P}}(\tilde{\gamma})=\mathbb{P}(\gamma)
.

The relation in Eq. (
Universal Statistics of Charges Exchanges in Non-Abelian Quantum Transport
) of the main text then follows by simply comparing the probabilities of trajectory
\gamma
in the forward process and trajectory
\tilde{\gamma}
in the backward process. In the general case it reads:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{P}(\gamma)}{\tilde{\mathbb{P}}(\tilde{\gamma})}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-(h_{n}^{A}+h_{\nu}^{B})}}{e^{-(h_{m}^{A}+h_{\mu}^{B})}}\;\frac{|\!\left\langle h_{m,\mu}\right|U\left|h_{n,\nu}\right\rangle|^{2}}{|\!\left\langle h_{n,\nu}\right|\Theta^{-1}\tilde{U}\Theta\left|h_{m,\mu}\right\rangle|^{2}}=\frac{e^{-(h_{n}^{A}+h_{\nu}^{B})}}{e^{-(h_{m}^{A}+h_{\mu}^{B})}}\;\frac{|\!\left\langle h_{m,\mu}\right|U\left|h_{n,\nu}\right\rangle|^{2}}{|\!\left\langle h_{n,\nu}\right|U^{\dagger}\left|h_{m,\mu}\right\rangle|^{2}}=e^{\Delta h^{A}({n\rightarrow m})+\Delta h^{B}({\nu\rightarrow\mu})}\,,

(S13)

where in the second equality we used the micro-reversibility principle for unitary evolutions
\Theta^{-1}\tilde{U}\Theta=U^{\dagger}
, and the last equality follows from the fact that the conditional probabilities in the numerator and denominator are equal. The relation reported in Eq. (
Universal Statistics of Charges Exchanges in Non-Abelian Quantum Transport
) of the main text correspond to the particular case for which
\tilde{\mathbb{P}}=\mathbb{P}
.

S3
Derivation of the currents fluctuation theorem

First, notice that the probability distribution
P_{B}(\Delta Q_{1},\dots,\Delta Q_{N};\Delta)
associated with the backward trajectories
\tilde{\gamma}
satisfies the equation:

\displaystyle P_{B}(-\Delta Q_{1},\dots,-\Delta Q_{N};-\Delta)=\sum_{\gamma}\;\tilde{\mathbb{P}}(\tilde{\gamma})\,\delta\left(\Delta Q_{1}+\Delta Q_{1}({m\rightarrow n})\right)\dots\delta\left(\Delta Q_{N}+\Delta Q_{N}(m\rightarrow n)\right)\delta\left(\Delta+\Delta(\tilde{\gamma})\right)=

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma}\;\mathbb{P}(\gamma)e^{-\sum_{i}\delta\lambda_{i}\Delta Q_{i}({n\rightarrow m})-\Delta({\gamma})}\,\delta\left(\Delta Q_{1}-\Delta Q_{1}({n\rightarrow m})\right)\dots\delta\left(\Delta Q_{N}-\Delta Q_{N}({n\rightarrow m})\right)\delta\left(\Delta-\Delta_{\gamma}\right)=

(S14)

\displaystyle=P(\Delta Q_{1},\dots,\Delta Q_{N};\Delta)\,e^{-\sum_{i}\delta\lambda_{i}\Delta Q_{i}\,-\,\Delta}\,,

where in the second line we used Eq. (
8
), together with the relations
\Delta Q_{i}({m\rightarrow n})=-\Delta Q_{i}({n\rightarrow m})
and
\Delta({\tilde{\gamma}})=-\Delta(\gamma)
to change the variables from
\tilde{\gamma}
to
\gamma
. Then, reordering we finally obtain:

\displaystyle\frac{P(\Delta Q_{1},\dots,\Delta Q_{N};\Delta)}{P_{B}(-\Delta Q_{1},\dots,-\Delta Q_{N};-\Delta)}=e^{\sum_{i}\left(\delta\lambda_{i}\Delta Q_{i}\right)\,+\,\Delta}\,,

(S15)

which is a generalized version of Eq. (
10
) in the main text to the case in which
\tilde{\mathbb{P}}(\tilde{\gamma})\neq\mathbb{P}(\tilde{\gamma})
, that is, in the presence of charges or interaction Hamiltonian that are odd under time-reversal. The relation above simplifies if we assume
\tilde{\mathbb{P}}(\tilde{\gamma})=\mathbb{P}(\tilde{\gamma})
, since in this case we have:

\displaystyle P_{B}(-\Delta Q_{1},\dots,-\Delta Q_{N};-\Delta)=\sum_{\gamma}\;\tilde{\mathbb{P}}(\tilde{\gamma})\,\delta\left(\Delta Q_{1}+\Delta Q_{1}({m\rightarrow n})\right)\dots\delta\left(\Delta Q_{N}+\Delta Q_{N}(m\rightarrow n)\right)\delta\left(\Delta+\Delta(\tilde{\gamma})\right)=

Then, substituting this relation into Eq. (

S4
Integral fluctuation theorem and second-law inequalities

In the following we consider the family of statistical divergences (also called contrast functions) dependent on the function
g(x)
:

\displaystyle S_{g}(\rho||\sigma):=\mathrm{Tr}\left[g(\mathbb{L}_{\sigma}\mathbb{R}_{\rho}^{-1})[\rho]\right]=\sum_{i,j}\;\rho_{i}\,g\left(\frac{\sigma_{j}}{\rho_{i}}\right)\,|\left\langle\sigma_{j}\middle|\rho_{i}\right\rangle|^{2}\,,

(S17)

where we used the eigendecomposition
\rho:=\sum_{i}\rho_{i}\left.\left|{\rho_{i}}\right.\right\rangle\!\!\left.\left\langle{\rho_{i}}\right.\right|
and
\sigma:=\sum_{i}\sigma_{i}\left.\left|{\sigma_{i}}\right.\right\rangle\!\!\left.\left\langle{\sigma_{i}}\right.\right|
. In order to verify the standard properties of statistical divergences the function
g(x)
needs to be matrix convex, satisfy that
g(1)=0
is its unique zero, and that, for some arbitrary constant
a
, it holds that
g(x)+a(x-1)\geq 0

Scandi et al. 2025
. We note that for
g(x)=-\log(x)
, we recover the relative entropy,
S(\rho||\sigma)=\mathrm{Tr}\left[\rho\,(\log\rho-\log\sigma)\right]
, while other choices may be done that recover the relative entropy variance or the family of
\alpha
-divergences.

If we now substitute in Eq. (
S17
)
\sigma:=\rho_{0}=\pi^{A}_{\lambda^{A}}\otimes\pi^{B}_{\lambda^{B}}
and
\rho:=U\rho_{0}U^{\dagger}
it is easy to see that:

\displaystyle S_{g}(U\pi U^{\dagger}||\pi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n,m,\nu,\mu}\;\frac{e^{-(h_{n}^{A}+h_{\nu}^{B})}}{Z}\,g\left(\frac{e^{-(h_{m}^{A}+h_{\mu}^{B})}}{e^{-(h_{n}^{A}+h_{\nu}^{B})}}\right)\;|\!\left\langle h_{m,\mu}\right|U\left|h_{n,\nu}\right\rangle|^{2}=

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma}\;g\boldsymbol{(}e^{-\Delta h^{A}({n\rightarrow m})-\Delta h^{B}({\nu\rightarrow\mu})}\boldsymbol{)}\,\mathbb{P}(\gamma)=\sum_{\gamma}\;g\boldsymbol{(}e^{-\sum_{i}\delta\lambda_{i}\Delta Q_{i}({n\rightarrow m})\,-\,\Delta({\gamma})}\boldsymbol{)}\,\mathbb{P}(\gamma)=

(S18)

\displaystyle=\int{\rm d}\Delta Q_{1}\dots{\rm d}\Delta Q_{N}{\rm d}\Delta\;g\boldsymbol{(}e^{-\sum_{i}\delta\lambda_{i}\Delta Q_{i}\,-\,\Delta}\boldsymbol{)}\,P(\Delta Q_{1},\dots,\Delta Q_{N};\Delta)\,,

The relation in Eq. (
S18
) allows us to derive an integral fluctuation theorem. Indeed, setting
g(x)=x
, the corresponding contrast function reduces to
S_{x}(\rho||\sigma)=\mathrm{Tr}\left[\sigma\right]=1
. Thus:

\displaystyle\left\langle e^{-\sum_{i}\left(\delta\lambda_{i}\Delta Q_{i}\right)\,-\,\Delta}\right\rangle:=\int{\rm d}\Delta Q_{1}\dots{\rm d}\Delta Q_{N}{\rm d}\Delta\;e^{-\sum_{i}\delta\lambda_{i}\Delta Q_{i}\,-\,\Delta}\,P(\Delta Q_{1},\dots,\Delta Q_{N};\Delta)=1\,.

(S19)

This proves the Jarzynski-like version of the XFT in Eq. (
11
).
Moreover, a well-known consequence of the (standard) integral fluctuation theorems is that statistical violations of the second law are exponentially damped
Jarzynski 2011
. Then, it follows from Eq. (
11
) that:

\displaystyle P\left(\sum_{i}\delta\lambda_{i}\Delta Q_{i}+\Delta<-\zeta\right)\leq\;e^{-\zeta}\,,

(S20)

where we implicitly defined the quantity on the left-hand side to be the probability, according to
P
, to observe a total value of
\sum_{i}{\delta\lambda_{i}\Delta Q_{i}}+\Delta
less than
-\zeta
for any real scalar
\zeta
. Here we see that the presence of the quantum correction allows for the probability distribution of
\sum_{i}{\delta\lambda_{i}\Delta Q_{i}}
to be more skewed towards negative values as compared to the case for commuting charges. Both relations show that the presence of the quantum correction term
\Delta
allows for bigger violations as measured by the sole variation of the charges.

For completeness, we include here a proof of Eq. (
S20
). This can be verified by the chain of inequalities:

\displaystyle P\left(\sum_{i}\delta\lambda_{i}\Delta Q_{i}+\Delta<-\zeta\right)=\int_{-\infty}^{-\zeta}{\rm d}x\;\delta(x-\sum_{i}\delta\lambda_{i}\Delta Q_{i}-\Delta)\int{\rm d}\Delta Q_{1}\dots{\rm d}\Delta Q_{N}{\rm d}\Delta\;P(\Delta Q_{1},\dots,\Delta Q_{N};\Delta)=

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{-\infty}^{-\zeta}{\rm d}x\;\delta(x-\sum_{i}\delta\lambda_{i}\Delta Q_{i}-\Delta)\int{\rm d}\Delta Q_{1}\dots{\rm d}\Delta Q_{N}{\rm d}\Delta\;P_{B}(-\Delta Q_{1},\dots,-\Delta Q_{N};-\Delta)e^{\sum_{i}\delta\lambda_{i}\Delta Q_{i}\,+\,\Delta}\leq

(S21)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq e^{-\zeta}\;\int{\rm d}\Delta Q_{1}\dots{\rm d}\Delta Q_{N}{\rm d}\Delta\;P_{B}(-\Delta Q_{1},\dots,-\Delta Q_{N};-\Delta)\leq\;e^{-\zeta}\,.

The second-law inequality in Eq. (
12
) of the main text can be derived either by applying Jensen’s inequality to the integral fluctuation theorem, of by choosing
g(x)=-\log(x)
in Eq. (
S18
), which leads to
S(U\rho_{0}U^{\dagger}||\rho_{0})=\left\langle\sum_{i}\delta\lambda_{i}\Delta Q_{i}+\,\Delta\right\rangle\ \geq 0
. In any case, we recover Eq. (
12
) in the main text, namely:

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\delta\lambda_{i}\langle\Delta Q_{i}\rangle\geq-\langle\Delta\rangle

(S22)

where the average is taken with respect to
P(\Delta Q_{1},\dots,\Delta Q_{N};\Delta)
.

S5
Fluctuation Theorem Uncertainty Relation for Non-Abelian Transport

In this last section we provide a detailed proof of the TUR in Eq. (
13
) of the main text. The proof is based on the derivation presented in Ref.
Hasegawa and Van Vu 2019
for classical stochastic systems. A key element in the derivation is the detailed fluctuation theorem for the charge currents in its strong version presented in Eqs. (
8
) and Eq. (
10
) of the main text, and the fact that the stochastic charge exchanges
\Delta Q_{i}(n\rightarrow m)
and the term
\Delta(\gamma)
are anti-symmetric under time reversal, for trajectories
\tilde{\gamma}=\{(m,\mu),(n,\nu)\}
, which implies that the quantity
\sigma(\gamma):=\sum_{i}\delta\lambda_{i}\Delta Q_{i}(n\rightarrow m)+\Delta(\gamma)
, is also anti-symmetric under time reversal. This means that:

\Delta Q_{i}(m\rightarrow n)=-\Delta Q_{i}(n\rightarrow m)\qquad;\qquad\Delta(\tilde{\gamma})=-\Delta(\gamma)\qquad;\qquad\sigma(\tilde{\gamma})=-\sigma(\gamma)\,.

(S23)

We then consider the probability to observe a change in
\sigma
and
\Delta Q_{j}
for some
j
in the forward and backward processes. Similarly to Eq. (
10
) in the main text, we have the detailed fluctuation relation:

\displaystyle P(\sigma,\Delta Q_{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma}\mathbb{P}(\gamma)\delta(\Delta\sigma-\sigma(\gamma)\delta(\Delta Q_{j}-\Delta Q_{j}(n\rightarrow m))=\sum_{\gamma}\mathbb{P}(\tilde{\gamma})e^{\sigma(\gamma)}\delta(\Delta\sigma-\sigma(\gamma)\delta(\Delta Q_{j}-\Delta Q_{j}(n\rightarrow m))=

\displaystyle=e^{\sigma}\sum_{\gamma}\mathbb{P}(\tilde{\gamma})\delta(\Delta\sigma+\sigma(\tilde{\gamma})\delta(\Delta Q_{j}+\Delta Q_{j}(m\rightarrow n))=e^{\sigma}P(-\sigma,-\Delta Q_{j}),

(S24)

which again corresponds to a strong fluctuation theorem. The TUR can now be derived from Eq. (
S5
) following the same steps as in Ref.
Hasegawa and Van Vu 2019
(see also Ref.
Merhav and Kafri 2010
). There, the authors consider the probability density function for positive
\sigma
:

Q(\sigma,\Delta Q_{j}):=(1+e^{-\sigma})P(\sigma,\Delta Q_{j})\,.

(S25)

It should be noticed that, thanks to Eq. (
S5
),
Q(\sigma,\Delta Q_{j})
is normalized. Indeed, we have that:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}d\sigma\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\Delta Q_{j}\,Q(\sigma,\Delta Q_{j})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\sigma\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\Delta Q_{j}(1+e^{-\sigma})P(\sigma,\Delta Q_{j})=

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\sigma\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\Delta Q_{j}P(\sigma,\Delta Q_{j})+\int_{0}^{\infty}d\sigma\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\Delta Q_{j}P(-\sigma,-\Delta Q_{j})=

(S26)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\sigma\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\Delta Q_{j}P(\sigma,\Delta Q_{j})+\int_{-\infty}^{0}d\sigma\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\Delta Q_{j}P(\sigma,\Delta Q_{j})=1\,.

Let us also by
\langle A\rangle_{Q}
the average over the distribution
Q
of a generic functional
A(\sigma,\Delta Q_{j})
, i.e.:

\langle A\rangle_{Q}=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\sigma\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\Delta Q_{j}\,A(\sigma,\Delta Q_{j})\,Q(\sigma,\Delta Q_{j})\,.

(S27)

Interestingly, the average changes in
\langle\sigma\rangle
and
\langle\Delta Q_{j}\rangle
can be expressed as expectations over the distribution
Q
, that is:

\displaystyle\langle\Delta\sigma\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\sigma\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\Delta Q_{j}\,P(\sigma,\Delta Q_{j})\sigma=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\sigma\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\Delta Q_{j}\,P(\sigma,\Delta Q_{j})\,\sigma\,(1-e^{-\sigma})=

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\sigma\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\Delta Q_{j}\,Q(\sigma,\Delta Q_{j})\,\sigma\,\tanh\left(\frac{\sigma}{2}\right)=\left\langle\sigma\,\tanh\left(\frac{\sigma}{2}\right)\right\rangle_{Q}\,;

(S28)

\displaystyle\langle\Delta Q_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\sigma\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\Delta Q_{j}\,P(\sigma,\Delta Q_{j})\,\Delta Q_{j}=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\sigma\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\Delta Q_{j}\,P(\sigma,\Delta Q_{j})\,\Delta Q_{j}(1-e^{-\sigma})=

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\sigma\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\Delta Q_{j}\,Q(\sigma,\Delta Q_{j})\,\Delta Q_{j}\tanh\left(\frac{\sigma}{2}\right)=\left\langle\Delta Q_{j}\tanh\left(\frac{\sigma}{2}\right)\right\rangle_{Q}\,.

(S29)

On the other hand, for the squared current
\langle\Delta Q_{j}^{2}\rangle
we have:

\displaystyle\langle\Delta Q_{j}^{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\sigma\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\Delta Q_{j}\,P(\sigma,\Delta Q_{j})\,\Delta Q_{j}^{2}=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\sigma\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\Delta Q_{j}\,P(\sigma,\Delta Q_{j})\,\Delta Q_{j}^{2}(1+e^{-\sigma})=

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\sigma\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\Delta Q_{j}\,Q(\sigma,\Delta Q_{j})\,\Delta Q_{j}^{2}=\langle\Delta Q_{j}^{2}\rangle_{Q},

(S30)

that is, averages over
P
and
Q
are equal. Then, we can apply Cauchy–Schwarz inequality to get:

\langle\Delta Q_{j}\rangle^{2}=\left\langle\Delta Q_{j}\tanh\left(\frac{\sigma}{2}\right)\right\rangle_{Q}^{2}\leq\left\langle\Delta Q_{j}^{2}\right\rangle_{Q}\left\langle\tanh^{2}\left(\frac{\sigma}{2}\right)\right\rangle_{Q}=\left\langle\Delta Q_{j}^{2}\right\rangle\left\langle\tanh^{2}\left(\frac{\sigma}{2}\right)\right\rangle_{Q}\,,

(S31)

where we used Eq. (
S28
) and Eq. (
S5
). Now, using the chain of inequalities
\left\langle\tanh^{2}\left(\frac{\sigma}{2}\right)\right\rangle_{Q}\leq\tanh\left(\left\langle\frac{\sigma}{2}\tanh\left(\frac{\sigma}{2}\right)\right\rangle_{Q}\right)\leq\tanh\left(\frac{\left\langle\sigma\right\rangle}{2}\right)
(see Ref.
Hasegawa and Van Vu 2019
for a proof), we obtain from Eq. (
S31

\frac{\left\langle\Delta Q_{j}^{2}\right\rangle}{\left\langle\Delta Q_{j}\right\rangle^{2}}\geq\left(\tanh\left(\frac{\langle\sigma\rangle}{2}\right)\right)^{-1}\,.

(S32)

Finally, defining
{\rm Var}[\Delta Q_{j}]=\langle\Delta Q_{j}^{2}\rangle-\langle\Delta Q_{j}\rangle^{2}
, we obtain the TUR:

\frac{{\rm Var}[\Delta Q_{j}]}{\langle\Delta Q_{j}\rangle^{2}}=\frac{\left\langle\Delta Q_{j}^{2}\right\rangle}{\left\langle\Delta Q_{j}\right\rangle^{2}}-1\geq\left(\tanh\left(\frac{\langle\sigma\rangle}{2}\right)\right)^{-1}-1=\frac{2}{e^{\langle\sigma\rangle}-1}\,.

(S33)

This proves Eq. (
13
) in the main text upon substituting the explicit expression of
\sigma=\sum_{i}\delta\lambda_{i}\Delta Q_{i}+\Delta
.

S6
Allowed interaction for the generalized Gibbs state of a qubit

Let us characterize the interactions allowed by Eq. (
3
) for two spin-1/2 particles with Hamiltonian
H=\sigma_{z}
, and with two non-commuting charges corresponding to
Q_{1}=H
and
Q_{2}=\sigma_{x}
. In order to do so, we use the fact that the product of Pauli matrices are a basis for the space of Hermitian operator on two qubits, so we can decompose the interaction Hamiltonian as:

H_{\rm int}=\sum_{i,j=0}^{3}\;C_{ij}\,\sigma_{i}\otimes\sigma_{j}\,
,
where we use the usual ordering
\sigma_{0}=\mathbbm{1}/\sqrt{2}
,
\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{x}/\sqrt{2}
,
\sigma_{2}=\sigma_{y}/\sqrt{2}
and
\sigma_{3}=\sigma_{z}/\sqrt{2}
(the extra factor ensures orthonormalization according to the Hilbert-Schmidt inner product). A tedious but straightforward calculation shows that in order for Eq. (
3
) to hold,
H_{\rm int}
has to be of the form:

\displaystyle H_{\rm int}=C_{0}\mathbbm{1}+C_{1}\sum_{i=1}^{3}\sigma_{i}\otimes\sigma_{i}\,.

(S34)

Then, the corresponding unitaries are of the form
U=e^{i(C_{0}-C_{1})/2}{\rm SWAP[C_{1}]}
, where we introduce the generalized swap operation, given in matrix form by:

\displaystyle{\rm SWAP}[\alpha]=\begin{pmatrix}e^{i\alpha}&0&0&0\\
0&\cos\alpha&i\sin\alpha&0\\
0&i\sin\alpha&\cos\alpha&0\\
0&0&0&e^{i\alpha}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(S35)

For the simulations, we ignored the extra phase
e^{i(C_{0}-C_{1})/2}
, as it does not contribute to any of the quantities of interest, and we set
\alpha=1
.

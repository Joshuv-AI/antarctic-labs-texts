Efficacy of different sterilization protocols and its impact on osteogenic potential of autogenous tooth bone graft: A comparative in vitro study
Authors: Shifali Mahajan, Rose Kanwaljeet Kaur, Vishakha Grover, Manjula Mehta, Savita Prashar, Ashish Jain
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Background:The efficacy of autogenous tooth bone graft (auto-TBG) is affected by many determinants, such as sterilization protocols and the expression of bone-inducing proteins such as bone morphogenetic protein (BMP) present in the graft.Aim:This in vitro study was conducted to compare the efficacy of different sterilization protocols and their impact on the osteogenic potential of auto-TBG.Materials and Methods:Ninety-eight auto-TBG samples were obtained from 10 extracted teeth that were prepared, ground, and sorted with the help of a dentin grinder. Seventy samples were partially demineralized with the standard recommended techniques, and 28 samples were kept nondemineralized (ND) for comparison. All the processed samples were subjected to different sterilization protocols namely Group A (gamma radiation), Group B (autoclave), Group C (chemical sterilization): Subgroup a (chlorhexidine), Subgroup b (povidone–iodine), Subgroup c (lactic acid), Subgroup d (sodium hydroxide), and Group D (normal saline). Sterilized auto-TBG samples were subjected to microbiological and biochemical analysis.Results:All sterilization protocols could effectively eliminate the microbial growth except normal saline. Mean BMP-2 concentration (pg/mL) was found as 141.91 (chlorhexidine) >134.50 (sodium hydroxide) >134.17 (lactic acid) >133.83 (povidone–iodine) >122.83 (gamma radiation) >121.81 (autoclave) >119.80 (normal saline).Conclusion:Comparatively, the chemical method of sterilization showed a higher mean concentration of BMP-2. In addition, all the samples of demineralized auto-TBG particles showed significantly higher mean BMP-2 concentration as compared to ND samples.

INTRODUCTION

In dental practice, to achieve foreseeable periodontal regeneration, an abundance of surgical procedures has been employed, including different types of bone grafts, guided tissue regeneration, growth factors (GFs), enamel matrix proteins, and root surface conditioning or a different combination of these procedures.[12] Bone grafting materials have been successfully used in dental practice for bone regeneration directed at bone defect sites.[3] An ideal bone graft material should stabilize the blood clot; provide a biomechanical scaffold for cell migration, proliferation, and differentiation; contain functional proteins and peptides, such as GFs; and exhibit appropriate resorption and remodeling during new bone formation.[4] According to bone-healing mechanisms, bone grafts can be grouped into materials that induce osteogenesis, osteoinduction, and osteoconduction.[5] Bone grafts can be broadly divided into autograft, allograft, xenograft, and alloplast, depending upon the source.[3] Among different types of bone grafts, autogenous grafts or autografts are most preferred as these contain vital bone cells and are considered the gold standard for their unique properties of bone formation.[6] The human tooth is made up of inorganic components and is also a rich source of stem cells, matrix, trace metal ions, and GFs. Bone and teeth are very similar because of the chemical composition, especially dentin and bone.[7] The dentin is rich in some GFs common to bone, namely insulin-like GF-II, transforming GF-beta, and bone morphogenetic protein-2 (BMP-2), which may impact the osteogenic properties of the tissue.[8]

In 2008, autogenous tooth bone graft (auto-TBG) material was first developed from the extracted tooth by Kim et al.[56789] Auto-TBG is available in both block and powder form and can be utilized for a variety of applications, including periodontal regeneration, extraction socket preservation, and more.[3] The auto-TBG particles were manufactured utilizing the extracted tooth with the help of the dentin grinder for clinical application in patients.[7] The graft handling and processing procedure may impose the risk of infection. Therefore, auto-TBG so obtained is sterilized to ensure the quality standards for aseptic material to be placed in the bone defects. Various sterilization protocols have been employed in the past.[10] For example, Yasin et al. determined that both irradiation and pasteurization sterilization treatments have better outcomes for recycled bone grafts after completion of sterilization protocols and also Yazdi et al. found that chlorhexidine 0.4% appears to be the best antiseptic solution for decontaminating bone samples.[1112]

Although the main objectives of the sterilization protocol are to eradicate the present microflora, the methods adopted for graft sterilization may impact the structural and biochemical integrity of the graft material.[10] Although studies have been undertaken to determine the appropriate sterilizing process for auto-TBG, relatively few studies have evaluated the osteogenic potential of auto-TBG after different sterilization procedures have been completed.[13] Hence, in the present study, we aimed to compare the efficacy of different sterilization protocols and their impact on the osteogenic potential of auto-TBG.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

The study protocol was approved by the author’s institutional review committee (XXX/2019/175/A/01/03) and conducted in accordance with the Helsinki Declaration of 1975, as revised in 2000. A total sample size of 98 was calculated to obtain a power of the study (84.92%) when the effect size was moderate (0.40) and the level of significance (α) was set at 0.05, with the help of an experienced statistician. The freshly extracted teeth of the patients indicated for extraction were collected from the outpatient department of the institute. Only teeth extracted due to advanced periodontal loss, impaction, and orthodontic purposes were included in the study. The extracted teeth with caries, root canal treatment, and restoration were excluded from the study. Each extracted tooth was cleaned (all remnants of the periodontal ligament, debris, and calculus were removed), dried, and ground with the help of a noncommercialized custom-made “built-in-house” dentin grinder used at an electric power source at 300–500 watt, 220V voltage and frequency of 950 Hz to transform it into auto-TBG particles. The auto-TBG particles in the size range conducive to bone regeneration, i.e., 300–1200 μm, were sorted and collected.[1415] Each tooth auto-TBG particle sample was divided into seven identical parts for different sterilization protocols. The partial demineralization procedure was performed for an auto-TBG particle demineralization, and 28 nondemineralized (ND) samples were kept as reference and treated with the same sterilization protocols of the study.[16] Therefore, each group and subgroup of the sterilization protocol had 14 samples, out of which 10 were demineralized (D) and four were ND. Then, the part of the auto-TBG particle of each tooth was assigned into the following sterilization protocols:

Based on the different chemical agents used, the chemical sterilization group, i.e., Group C, was divided into four subgroups. After immersion in the various chemical solutions as per protocols, the auto-TBG particles were washed with normal saline for 1–3 min [Supplementary Table 1]. After completion of each sterilization protocol, each sample was divided into two parts and was analyzed for microbiological and biochemical parameters. (1) Microbiological analysis: standard microbiological methods as reported in the past literature were used for the assessment of microbial parameters of the study.[121819] Each sample was incubated at 37°C in the thioglycollate medium for 10 days. On the 3rd, 7th, and 10th days of incubation, direct inoculation was carried out with the help of the streak plating method on nutrient agar plates, and sample plates were kept in an incubator at 37°C overnight. After incubation, the presence of microflora was checked on culture plates at each interval, and total colony forming units (CFUs) were counted and expressed as CFU/μL for the comparison between all the groups and subgroups of the sterilization protocols. (2) Biochemical analysis: the concentration of BMP-2 in the auto-TBG samples was determined by quantitative sandwich enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA [Elabscience Human BMP-2]) according to the manufacturer’s instructions.[2021] The mean value of BMP-2 in each group and subgroup was expressed in picogram/milliliter.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was carried out using the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences SPSS (developed by IBM, USA) version 20.0 for Windows. Microbial growth (mean CFU/μL) and osteogenic potential (mean BMP-2 concentration) were presented as mean and standard deviation and comparatively analyzed with the use of analysis of variance with the Tukey HSD post hoc test where applicable. All statistical tests were performed at the level of significance α = 0.05.

RESULTS

Microbial growth (mean colony forming unit/μL)

There was no microbial growth in any sample except for the ones treated with normal saline. Irrespective of mineralization status, all samples sterilized with normal saline showed microbial growth. A single sample of ND auto-TBG particles sterilized by autoclave also showed microbial growth on the 3rd, 7th, and 10th days of incubation. The microbial counts revealed a statistically significant difference when compared with respect to different days of incubation and sterilization protocols (P < 0.05). However, a nonsignificant difference was observed among different sterilization protocols (P = 0.07), as shown in Table 1. Further, the post hoc analysis revealed a significantly higher mean CFU/μL on the 10th day than on the 3rd and 7th days of incubation (P = 0.02). To compare against the negative control (normal saline), all experimental groups of sterilization protocols were treated as a homogenous group and revealed a significant difference between the negative control and experimental groups (P = 0.000) as shown in Figure 1. Biochemical analysis (mean BMP-2 concentration): The highest mean BMP-2 concentration was observed in subgroup c (Chlorhexidine) auto-TBG particles of the demineralized sample. However, a nonsignificant difference among sterilization protocols of demineralized and ND auto-TV particle samples (P = 0.61) but a significantly higher concentration of BMP-2 in the demineralized auto-TBG particles than ND auto-TBG particles was observed (P = 0.001), as shown in Table 2.

DISCUSSION

Various types of bone grafts are often used to repair and regenerate bony defects, but their real use is determined by clinical applications, the size of the deficiency, and evidence-based research studies.[722] It’s has been a long time quest in related research to find the most reliable, effective, and safe bone graft material. AG bone graft has been considered the gold standard for bone regeneration.[67] However, previous research has shown that the extracted tooth or bone from the oral cavity of the patient could be contaminated with bacteria owing to an existing disease or during the handling procedure during procurement of the graft, which may raise the risk of bone resorption.[222324] To effectively treat periodontal bone defects with auto-TBG, it is important to eliminate the present microflora, but it is also necessary to maintain their bone-forming property at the same time. For this, we aimed to compare the seven different sterilization protocols that were all used previously, but there is no agreement on which approach is the best due to the complexity of various factors.[252627] The current investigation has evaluated and compared the efficacy of different sterilization protocols that may be adopted in day-to-day clinical practice for chairside sterilization of auto-TBG particles. The sterilization of the graft and expression of bone-inducing BMP-2 protein has been evaluated by counting CFU and ELISA, respectively.

The study findings revealed that all sterilization protocols were effective in the sterilization of auto-TBG particles except normal saline (negative control of the study). However, in ND samples subjected to autoclaving, one of the samples showed the presence of bacteria, which could be possibly attributed to microbial contamination during processing, as none of the other study samples in the same group showed any microbial growth on the nutrient agar plates after the incubation of 10 days. The mean value of CFU/μL was significantly higher in a normal saline group as compared to all other sterilization protocols.

Gamma radiation, i.e., irradiation, has long been the most widely used method for decontaminating human tissue.[13] Irradiation’s mode of action includes direct collision of particles with nuclear proteins, which is lethal to cells and bacteria. Furthermore, the ionized particles break the bound water molecules, resulting in the formation of hydrogen and hydroxyl free radicals, which are lethal to bacteria.[1328] A previous study done by Yasin et al. concluded that gamma radiation and pasteurization are both viable options for the sterilization for bone autografts.[11] Heat is used in autoclave sterilization to destroy microorganisms.[29] The protocol for sterilization by autoclave was effective in the current investigation; however, a previous study conducted by Ihsan et al. reported a 53.1% positive culture rate after sterilization with autoclave, which may be due to the lobulation in the bone structure created after the autoclaving process, which is conducive to bacterial proliferation.[18]

The chlorhexidine solution has broad-spectrum activity against oral pathogens, high tolerability, and no development of resistance.[30] Molina et al. stated that chlorhexidine is the most effective agent in sterilizing the contaminated bone samples.[31] Povidone–iodine is an iodophor with a broad spectrum antimicrobial properties having a nonselective mode of action.[30] In the previous study done by Ihsan et al., only one sample of bone sterilized with povidone–iodine showed microbial growth.[18] A previous study conducted by Yazdi et al. in 2012 showed that 0.4% chlorhexidine seems to be the best antiseptic solution for the sterilization of contaminated bone, although it did not have any significant difference from the povidone–iodine group.[12] Furthermore, the previous study done by Soyer et al. showed the efficacy of povidone–iodine to decontaminate the surface of a bone.[32] Lactic acid is an antibacterial compound, and its broad-acting mechanisms include disruption of cell regulation and production of free radicals that damage the cellular mechanism. Furthermore, it is a safe, bio-based, and biodegradable option for antimicrobial action.[33] In the past, Joshi et al. used 1N lactic acid sterilization protocol clinically in the sterilization of an autogenous tooth graft for an alveolar ridge preservation procedure.[15] Sodium hydroxide is a strong oxidizing agent.[173435]

Since the sterilization protocols are known to impact the osteogenetic capacity of the bone grafts, therefore, the auto-TBG particles sterilized by different sterilization protocols were analyzed for the presence of BMP-2 after the completion of each sterilization protocol. BMP-2 is considered one of the most important osteoinductive proteins for bone formation and repair in vitro.[12213637] BMP-2 has been proposed as a regulator of pulp cell differentiation into the odontoblastic lineage, and it’s the only BMP that causes mesenchymal stem cells to differentiate into osteoblasts.[8] The highest mean BMP-2 concentration was 141.91 pg/mL seen in the auto-TBG particles treated with 0.4% chlorhexidine for sterilization. The decreasing order of BMP-2 expression was as follows: chlorhexidine > sodium hydroxide > lactic acid > povidone–iodine > gamma irradiation > autoclave > normal saline. However, the BMP-2 did not differ significantly in any group samples of different sterilization protocols. There is a scarcity of published literature reporting on BMP-2 concentrations after sterilization of the graft materials, so direct comparisons of the parameter were not feasible in the context of the current study settings. However, a previous study by Bi et al. (2013) stated that sodium hydroxide solution greatly hampered the natural properties of the cancellous bone because of combined use with steam sterilization.[17] Severyns et al. reported that even 10% of povidone–iodine has toxic effects on the viable cells of the tissue or graft.[38] As compared to autoclaving, Yasin et al. found that using gamma radiation to sterilize the recycled bone graft had a better outcome in terms of a bony union based on histopathological and radiographic evaluations.[11] Auto-TBG samples sterilized by normal saline showed a minimal mean of BMP-2 concentration.

Comparative analysis of D and ND auto-TBG samples revealed that all sterilization protocols, namely gamma radiation, autoclave, chlorhexidine, povidone–iodine, sodium hydroxide, and lactic acid could effectively sterilize the grafts irrespective of their status of demineralization as no microbial growth was observed in any of the samples analyzed except normal saline. However, in the context of mean BMP-2 concentration, D samples showed 125.81 pg/mL of mean concentration, whereas ND samples showed 93.62 pg/mL of the mean concentration of BMP-2 in auto-TBG particle samples. The process of demineralization has been documented to express or induce more BMP-2 expression. This result was in accordance with the previous studies, which stated that demineralization is believed to increase the release of GFs from the tooth matrix by decreasing the mineral content.[20394041] As it is generally believed that BMPs are trapped by minerals and that demineralization releases BMPs in a roughly proportional to the degree of demineralization.[42] This finding of the study is in coherence as a significant difference was observed in the mean BMP-2 concentration in D and ND samples, irrespective of sterilization protocol. Previous investigations have indicated that the osteoinductive properties of DBM can be affected by several factors, such as donor age, gender, particle size, and methods of preparation, sterilization, and storage.[4344] A recent systematic review inferred that sterilization is a fundamental process for grafts, more significantly when the other chemical and physical treatments are utilized for the processing of the dentin graft material.[4546]

The strength of our study was that it evaluated a simple chairside chemical-based sterilization protocol with respect to standard sterilization protocols, namely gamma radiation and autoclave to define a cost-effective, efficacious, convenient, and less time-consuming sterilization protocol for the sterilization of clinically harvested auto-TBG for immediate use in the clinical cases where bone defects are present. Furthermore, this study has investigated the impact of sterilization protocol on the effectiveness in terms of sterility as well as remaining osteogenic potential after completion of sterilization together, thus determining an optimized chairside sterilization protocol for the auto-TBG particle. However, limitations of our study include a small sample size, in vitro design, and usage of few chemical agents for comparison with the current standards of sterilization protocol used for sterilization of auto-TBG particles. Based on the study findings, 0.4% chlorhexidine as a sterilization protocol appears promising for clinical use, as it is 100% effective in sterilization of auto-TBG particles as well as revealing the maximum mean BMP-2 concentration. Further, in vivo studies in different clinical scenarios are required to ascertain the implication of this sterilization protocol for clinical use.

CONCLUSION

Within the limitations of the current study, it is concluded that all the sterilization protocols effectively sterilized auto-TBG particles except the normal saline. However, the mean BMP-2 concentration in the graft particles was variable after the use of different sterilization protocols. Our findings demonstrated the efficiency of 0.4% chlorhexidine to reliably sterilize auto-TBG particles while maintaining a maximum mean concentration of BMP-2. In general, all the protocols of chemical sterilization showed a higher mean concentration of BMP-2 as compared to standards, i.e., gamma radiation and autoclave sterilization protocols. In addition, the mean concentration of BMP-2 was significantly higher in the demineralized samples as compared to ND samples, explaining the role of demineralization in bone regeneration. Based on the current data, the authors recommend sterilization of auto-TBG particles using 0.4% chlorhexidine as a chairside method in clinical use.

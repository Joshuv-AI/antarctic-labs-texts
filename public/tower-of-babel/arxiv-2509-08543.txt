Maximal regularity of Dirichlet problem for the Laplacian in Lipschitz domains
Chérif Amrouche a, Mohand Moussaoui b

Abstract
The focus of this work is on the homogeneous and non-homogeneous Dirichlet problem for the Laplacian in bounded Lipschitz domains. Although it has been extensively studied by many authors, we would like to return to a number of fundamental questions and known results, such as the traces and the maximal regularity of solutions. First, to treat non-homogeneous boundary conditions, we rigorously define the notion of traces for non regular functions. This approach replaces the non-tangential trace notion that has dominated the literature since the 1980s. We identify a functional space E ⁡ ( ∇ , Ω ) = { v ∈ H 1 / 2 ​ ( Ω ) ; ∇ v ∈ [ H 1 / 2 ​ ( Ω ) ] ′ } , E(\nabla;\,\Omega)\ =\ \left\{\,v\in H^{1/2}(\Omega);\ \nabla v\in[\textit{{H}}^{\,1/2}(\Omega)]^{\prime}\,\right\}, which satisfies the embeddings H 00 1 / 2 ​ ( Ω ) ↪ E ⁡ ( ∇ , Ω ) ↪ H 1 / 2 ​ ( Ω ) H^{1/2}_{00}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow E(\nabla;\,\Omega)\hookrightarrow H^{1/2}(\Omega) . The trace operator is well-defined and continuous from E ⁡ ( ∇ , Ω ) E(\nabla;\,\Omega) into L 2 ​ ( Γ ) L^{2}(\Gamma) , leading to a new characterization of H 00 1 / 2 ​ ( Ω ) H^{1/2}_{00}(\Omega) as the kernel of this operator. Second, we address the regularity of solutions to the Laplace equation with homogeneous Dirichlet conditions. Using specific equivalent norms in fractional Sobolev spaces and Grisvard’s results for polygons and polyhedral domains, we prove that maximal regularity H 3 / 2 H^{3/2} holds in any bounded Lipschitz domain Ω \Omega , for all right-hand sides in the dual of H 00 1 / 2 ​ ( Ω ) H^{1/2}_{00}(\Omega) . This conclusion contradicts the prevailing claims in the literature since the 1990s. Third, we describe some criteria which establish new uniqueness results for harmonic functions in Lipschitz domains. In particular, we show that if u ∈ H 1 / 2 ​ ( Ω ) u\in H^{1/2}(\Omega) or u ∈ W 1 , 2 ​ N / ( N + 1 ) ​ ( Ω ) u\in W^{1,2N/(N+1)}(\Omega) , with N ≥ 2 N\geq 2 , is harmonic in Ω \Omega and vanishes on Γ \Gamma , then u ≡ 0 u\equiv 0 . These criteria play a central role in deriving regularity properties. Finally, we revisit the classical Area Integral Estimate of Dahlberg, and of Kenig, Pipher, and Verchota. For a harmonic function u u in Ω \Omega vanishing at some interior point, the estimate asserts ∫ Γ | u | 2 ​ 𝑑 σ ≤ C ​ ∫ Γ | S ⁡ ( u ) | 2 ​ 𝑑 σ ≃ C ​ ∫ Ω ϱ ​ | ∇ u | 2 ​ 𝑑 x , \int_{\Gamma}|u|^{2}d\sigma\leq C\int_{\Gamma}|S(u)|^{2}d\sigma\simeq C\int_{\Omega}\varrho|\nabla u|^{2}dx, (0.1) where S ⁡ ( u ) S(u) is the area integral of u u and ϱ \varrho is the distance to the boundary. Since this estimate has been widely used to argue that H 3 / 2 H^{3/2} -regularity is unattainable for data in the dual of H 00 1 / 2 ​ ( Ω ) H^{1/2}_{00}(\Omega) , our counterexample provides a decisive clarification.

Introduction and main results

The purpose of this work is to study the Dirichlet problem:

(\mathscr{L}_{D})\ \ \ \ -\Delta u=f\quad\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega\quad\mbox{and }\quad u=g\ \ \mbox{on }\Gamma,

with data belonging to appropriate Sobolev spaces, where the domain
\Omega
is assumed to be only Lipschitz. When
g=0

we denote this problem by
(\mathscr{L}_{D}^{0})
and when
f=0
we denote it by
(\mathscr{L}_{D}^{H})
. The Dirichlet problem has been extensively studied since the 1960s. In their classical work
[
30
]
, Lions and Magenes provided a complete analysis for smooth domains and within the
L^{2}
-theory. Later, Grisvard
[
23
]
and Nečas
[
38
]
investigated the case where
\Omega
is of class
\mathscr{C}^{r,1}
, with nonnegative integer
r
, while Grisvard
[
24
]
,
[
25
]
also studied the particular case of polygons and polyhedra. As a consequence of Calderón-Zygmund theory on singular integrals and boundary layer potentials, it is well known that for every
f\in W^{m-2,p}(\Omega)
and
g\in W^{m-1/p,p}(\Gamma)
with integer

and

, the problem
(\mathscr{L}_{D})
admits a unique solution

provided that
\Omega
is of class
\mathscr{C}^{r,1}
with
r=\max\{1,m-1\}
. Moreover, if
f\in W^{s-2,p}(\Omega)
and
g\in W^{s-1/p,p}(\Gamma)
with
s>1/p
, then
u\in W^{s,p}(\Omega)
whenever
\Omega
is of class
\mathscr{C}^{r,1}
with
r=\max\{1,[s]\}
, where

denotes the integer part of
s
.

During the 1980s, considerable attention shifted to the case where
\Omega
is merely Lipschitz, a setting in which the situation changes
dramatically (see for instance
[

13
,

,
18

[

33

34

32

Recall that if
\Omega
is of class
\mathscr{C}^{1}
and
1<p<\infty
, then for any
f\in W^{-1,\,p}(\Omega)
and
g\in W^{1-1/p,\,p}(\Gamma),
Problem
(\mathscr{L}_{D})
admits a unique solution
u\in W^{1,\,p}(\Omega).
In the 1980s, Nečas raised the question of solvability for Problem
(\mathscr{L}^{0}_{D})
,
i.e
, with homogeneous boundary condition
g=0
in Lipschitz domains, when

. The answer to this question was given in the celebrated paper of Jerison and Kenig
[

, Theorem A. Specifically, if

, there exists a Lipschitz domain

and

such that the solution

of Problem
(\mathscr{L}_{D}^{0})
does not belong to

(for

, the result holds for any

and

, such that if

then Problem
(\mathscr{L}^{0}_{D})
admits a unique solution

satisfying an estimate

\|u\|_{W^{1,\,p}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{W^{-1,\,p}(\Omega)}.

When
\Omega
is of class
\mathscr{C}^{1}
, one can in fact take

. It is noteworthy that the exponents

and
4/3
in dimension 2, respectively

and
3/2
in dimension 3, corresponding respectively to limiting cases for the existence and uniqueness of solutions in

.

In the present paper, we focus on solving Problems
(\mathscr{L}_{D}^{0})
and
(\mathscr{L}_{D}^{H})
when
\Omega
is Lipschitz. For the existence of solutions, we restrict ourselves to the Hilbertian framework of Sobolev spaces

with real

-theory will be treated in a subsequent work. While these problems are not new and have been widely investigated using various techniques since the 1980s, we show that the limiting cases

and

-regularity for right-hand sides belonging to the dual space of

It is worth noting that several arguments in the literature suggest that such maximal regularity fails, based on the so-called

Main results

The first result concerns norms equivalences, which are one of the keys to establish the maximal
H^{3/2}
regularity for the Laplace equation with a
homogeneous Dirichlet condition.

Theorem 1.1
(Norms Equivalences)

i) Let
0\leq s\leq 1
. There exist constants
C_{1}>0
and
C_{2}>0
depending only on
s
, on the Lipschitz character and on the Poincaré constant of
\Omega
such that for any
v\in H^{s}(\Omega)

\inf_{K\in\,\mathbb{R}}\|\,v+K\,\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}\leq C_{1}\|\,\nabla v\|_{[\widetilde{\textit{{H}}}\,^{1-s}(\Omega)]^{\prime}}\leq C_{2}\|\,\varrho^{1-s}\nabla v\|_{{\textit{{L}}}^{2}(\Omega)},

where
\varrho
is the distance function to the boundary of
\Omega
and
\widetilde{\textit{{H}}}\,^{1-s}(\Omega)
is equal to
{\textit{{H}}}\,^{1-s}_{0}(\Omega)
if
s\neq 1/2
and equal to
{\textit{{H}}}\,^{1/2}_{00}(\Omega)
if not.

ii) Let
v\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)
and
0\leq s\leq 1.
Then we have the following implications

\sigma^{1-s}\nabla v\in{\textit{{L}}}^{2}(\Omega)\Longrightarrow\nabla v\in[\widetilde{\textit{{H}}}\,^{1-s}(\Omega)]^{\prime}\Longrightarrow v\in H^{s}(\Omega),

where
\sigma
is the regularized distance function to the boundary of
\Omega
.

iii) For the critical case
s=1/2
, we have also the following equivalence norms:

\|v\|_{H^{1/2}_{00}(\Omega)}\simeq\|\nabla v\|_{[H^{1/2}(\Omega)]^{\prime}},\quad for\;v\in H^{1/2}_{00}(\Omega).

Throughout this manuscript the vector fields and the spaces of vector fields are denoted by bold fonts.

The second result is about traces of functions belonging to Sobolev spaces, which is crucial in the study of boundary value problems. We know that if
v\in H^{s}(\Omega)
with
s>1/2
then the function
v
has a trace which belongs to
H^{s-1/2}(\Gamma)
. However, if
v\in H^{1/2}(\Omega)
only, in general this function
v
may have no trace. In the following theorem, we see that an additional condition on
\nabla v
allows to obtain a trace for the function
v
.

Theorem 1.2
(Trace Space
E(\nabla;\,\Omega)
)

Define

E(\nabla;\,\Omega)\ =\ \left\{\,v\in H^{1/2}(\Omega);\ \nabla v\in[\textit{{H}}^{\,1/2}(\Omega)]^{\prime}\,\right\}.

Then the following hold:

i) The linear mapping
\gamma_{0}:v\mapsto v_{|\Gamma}
defined on
\mathscr{D}(\overline{\Omega})
can be extended by continuity to a linear and continuous mapping, still denoted
\gamma_{0}
, from
E(\nabla;\,\Omega)
into
L^{2}(\Gamma)
.

ii) The kernel of
\gamma_{0}
is equal to
H^{1/2}_{00}(\Omega)
.

As a consequence we get immediately the following results: let

v\in H^{3/2}(\Omega)\quad\mathrm{with}\quad\nabla^{2}v\in[\textit{{H}}^{\,1/2}(\Omega)]^{\prime},

then

v_{|\Gamma}\in H^{1}(\Gamma)\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\frac{\partial v}{\partial\textit{{n}}}\in L^{2}(\Gamma).

Moreover, we have the following property:

v\in H^{3/2}_{00}(\Omega)\Longrightarrow v=0\quad\mathrm{in}\;H^{1}(\Gamma)\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\frac{\partial v}{\partial\textit{{n}}}=0\quad\mathrm{in}\;L^{2}(\Gamma).

The notation
\nabla^{k}v
denotes the derivatives
\partial^{\alpha}v
with
|\alpha|=k
and
\nabla^{2}v
denotes the Hessian of
v
.

The following theorem allows us to rigorously define, by using a Green’s formula, the concept of traces for functions that are not necessarily harmonic but are sufficiently regular, and whose Laplacian is defined in an appropriate space.

Theorem 1.3
(Trace Operator for non regular functions)

Let
0\leq s<1/2
. The linear mapping
{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{\textit{{n}}}:v\longmapsto v\textit{{n}}_{|\Gamma}
defined on

\mathscr{D}\big(\overline{\Omega}\big)
can be extended to a linear and continuous mapping, still denoted by
{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{\textit{{n}}}
:

\begin{array}[]{rl}{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{\textit{{n}}}:M^{s}(\Omega)&\longrightarrow[{\boldsymbol{H}_{\textit{{N}}}^{1/2-s}(\Gamma)}]^{\prime}\\
v&\longmapsto v\textit{{n}}_{|\Gamma}\end{array}

where

M^{s}(\Omega)=\left\{v\in H^{s}(\Omega);\ \Delta v\in[H^{3/2}_{0}(\Omega)]^{\prime}\right\}

and

\boldsymbol{H}_{\textit{{N}}}^{s}(\Gamma)=\{\boldsymbol{\mu}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{s}(\Gamma);\;\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\tau}={\bf 0}\}.

Moreover, we have the Green’s formula: For all
v\in M^{s}(\Omega)
and
\varphi\in H^{2-s}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,

\langle v,\,\Delta\varphi\rangle_{H^{s}(\Omega)\times H^{-s}(\Omega)}-\langle\Delta v,\,\varphi\rangle_{[H^{3/2}_{0}(\Omega)]^{\prime}\times H^{3/2}_{0}(\Omega)}=\langle v\textit{{n}},\,\nabla\varphi\rangle_{\Gamma},

where
\langle v\textit{{n}},\,\nabla\varphi\rangle_{\Gamma}
denotes the duality brackets between
[{\boldsymbol{H}_{\textit{{N}}}^{1/2-s}(\Gamma)}]^{\prime}
and

\boldsymbol{H}_{\textit{{N}}}^{1/2-s}(\Gamma)
.

The above theorem will allow us in particular to study the homogeneous problem
(\mathscr{L}_{D}^{H})
by giving a meaning to the boundary condition that seems to us more precise than that of non-tangential convergence widely used in the literature.

Before exploring the questions of existence and regularity for the problem
(\mathscr{L}_{D})
, it is natural to first examine the case where the domain is a polygon.

Theorem 1.4
(Solutions in
H^{s}(\Omega)
with
\Omega
polygon)

i) for any

\Delta:H^{2-\theta}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\longrightarrow{H}^{-\theta}(\Omega)

ii) for

\Delta:H^{3/2}_{0}(\Omega)\longrightarrow[{H}^{1/2}_{00}(\Omega)]^{\prime}

iii) for any fixed

Proposition 1.5
(Counter Example)

For any

, there is a Lipschitz domain
\Omega_{\varepsilon}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}

\sqrt{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}\,\nabla^{2}w_{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})

(\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}\nabla^{2}w_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|w_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{3/2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})})_{\varepsilon},

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\|w_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\varepsilon})}=+\infty.

Of course, by using Nečas’s Property (see Subsection

for any

This brings us to the question of the maximal regularity in the case of Lipschitz domains. It is well known that for any
1/2<s<3/2

(\mathscr{L}_{D}^{0})
has a unique solution

(or even if

to Problem

are too strong. So it would be interesting to characterize the range of
H^{3/2}_{0}(\Omega)
by the Laplacian operator. One of our main results, which was not proved yet so far as we know, is given by the next theorem.

Theorem 1.6
(Solutions in
H^{3/2}_{0}(\Omega)
and in
H^{1/2}_{00}(\Omega)
)

i) The operators

\Delta:H^{3/2}_{0}(\Omega)\longrightarrow[{H}^{1/2}_{00}(\Omega)]^{\prime}\quad and\quad\Delta:H^{1/2}_{00}(\Omega)\longrightarrow[H^{3/2}_{0}(\Omega)]^{\prime}

are isomorphisms.

ii) For any
f\in[H^{1/2}(\Omega)]^{\prime}
satisfying the compatibility condition

\forall\varphi\in\mathscr{H}_{H^{1/2}(\Omega)},\quad\langle f,\,\varphi\rangle=0,

where

\mathscr{H}_{H^{1/2}(\Omega)}\ =\ \left\{v\in H^{1/2}(\Omega);\ \ \Delta v=0\;\;in\;\;\Omega\right\},

there exists a unique solution
u\in H^{3/2}_{00}(\Omega)
such that
\Delta u=f
in
\Omega
. In addition to the boundary Dirichlet condition
u=0
, the normal derivative of this solution satisfies
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\textit{{n}}}=0
.

iii) For any
f\in\left[H^{3/2}_{00}(\Omega)\right]^{\prime}
, there exists
u\in H^{1/2}(\Omega)
satisfying
\Delta u=f
in
\Omega
, unique up to an element of
\mathscr{H}_{H^{1/2}(\Omega)}
.

In particular, this shows that the maximal regularity

holds for all bounded Lipschitz domains, in contrast with previous claims in the literature. From Theorem
1.2
, the second isomorphism in Point i) above means that for any

, there exists a unique solution

satisfying

\Delta u=f\quad\mathrm{in}\;\Omega\quad\mathrm{and}\quad u=0\quad\mathrm{on}\;\Gamma.

Spaces

Using the interpolation by the complex method, recall that

H^{s}(\Omega)\ =\ \left[H^{m}(\Omega),L^{2}(\Omega)\right]_{\theta};\ \ \ \ s=(1-\theta)m\ \textrm{ and }0<\theta<1.

According to

j=1,\ldots,N
, we have

\langle\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}(v_{k}-v),\,\varphi\rangle=\langle\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{j}},\,\psi_{k}\varphi-\varphi\rangle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}v\varphi\frac{\partial\psi_{k}}{\partial x_{j}}

and then by using (
4.1
), we get

\begin{array}[]{rl}|\langle\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}(v_{k}-v),\,\varphi\rangle|&\leq C\|\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{j}}\|_{[\textit{{H}}^{\,1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})]^{\prime}}\big(\|\varphi_{H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\cap B_{k}^{c})}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})}\big)\\
\\
&+\;\frac{C}{k}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})},\end{array}

Hence,

\frac{\partial v_{k}}{\partial x_{j}}\rightharpoonup\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{j}}\quad\mathrm{in}\;[H^{\,1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})]^{\prime}.

Our goal is to prove the strong convergence. For that, we observe that
\frac{\partial v_{k}}{\partial x_{j}}=\psi_{k}\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{j}}+v\frac{\partial\psi_{k}}{\partial x_{j}}
and
v\frac{\partial\psi_{k}}{\partial x_{j}}\rightarrow 0
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})
and then in
[H^{\,1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})]^{\prime}
. In addition, since for any
\varphi\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})

|\langle\psi_{k}\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{j}},\,\varphi\rangle|=|\langle\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{j}},\,\psi_{k}\varphi\rangle|\leq\|\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{j}}\|_{[H^{\,1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})]^{\prime}}\|\psi_{k}\varphi\|_{H^{\,1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})}

we have the following estimate

\|\psi_{k}\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{j}}\|_{[H^{\,1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})]^{\prime}}\leq\|\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{j}}\|_{[H^{\,1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})]^{\prime}}\sup_{\varphi\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}),\varphi\not=0}\frac{\|\psi_{k}\varphi\|_{H^{\,1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})}}{\|\varphi\|_{H^{\,1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})}}.

As

\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|\psi_{k}\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{j}}\|_{[H^{\,1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})]^{\prime}}\leq\|\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{j}}\|_{[H^{\,1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})]^{\prime}},

we have also the same inequality for the norm of
\frac{\partial v_{k}}{\partial x_{j}}
and then we deduce the desired strong convergence.

Step 2
.
Extension to

\mathbb{R}^{N}
. It follows from Step 1 that we can suppose, without loss of generality, that
v\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})
with compact support.

For
h>0
we set
\tau_{h}v(\textit{{x}})=v_{h}(\textit{{x}})=v(\textit{{x}}^{\prime},x_{N}+h)
and we introduce the following function

\alpha_{h}(\textit{{x}})=\begin{cases}1\quad\;\mathrm{if}\;\;x_{N}>0\\
0\quad\;\mathrm{if}\;\;x_{N}<-h\end{cases}

with
\alpha_{h}\in\mathscr{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. We set
w_{h}=\alpha_{h}\tau_{h}Pu
, where
P:H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})\rightarrow H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a bounded linear extension operator. Clearly, if
v\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})
, using Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem, then we have
v_{h}\rightarrow v
in
H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})
and
w_{h}{{}_{|\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}}\rightarrow v
in
H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})
as
h\rightarrow 0
. Moreover, for any
\varphi\in\mathscr{D}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})
and
j=1,\ldots,N
,

|\langle\frac{\partial w_{h}}{\partial x_{j}},\,\varphi\rangle|=|\langle\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{j}},\,\tau_{-h}\varphi\rangle|\leq\|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{j}}\|_{[H^{\,1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})]^{\prime}}\|\varphi\|_{H^{\,1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})}.

Using the density of
\mathscr{D}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})
in
H^{\,1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})]
, we deduce that

\frac{\partial w_{h}}{\partial x_{j}}\in[H^{\,1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})]^{\prime}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\|\frac{\partial w_{h}}{\partial x_{j}}\|_{[H^{\,1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})]^{\prime}}\leq\|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{j}}\|_{[H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})]^{\prime}}

(where the last inequality can be obtained by interpolation between
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})
and
H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})
).
Besides, for any
\varphi\in\mathscr{D}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})
and
j=1,\ldots,N
, we have even if it means extending
\varphi
by zero outside the half-space,

|\langle\frac{\partial w_{h}}{\partial x_{j}}-\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{j}},\,\varphi\rangle|=|\langle\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{j}},\,\tau_{-h}\varphi-\varphi\rangle|\leq\|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{j}}\|_{[H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})]^{\prime}}\|\tau_{-h}\varphi-\varphi\|_{H^{\,1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})}

where the last norm above tends to
0
when
h\rightarrow 0
. That gives the strong convergence

\frac{\partial w_{h}}{\partial x_{j}}\rightarrow\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{j}}\qquad\mathrm{in}\;[H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})]^{\prime}.

Step 3
.
Regularization
. To finish, we will approximate
w_{h}
, with
h
fixed, by the functions
\varphi_{k}=w_{h}\star\varrho_{k}
, where we use the sequence of mollifiers
(\varrho_{k})_{k}
. It is easy to verify that

\varphi_{k}\rightarrow w_{h}\qquad\mathrm{in}\;H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad\frac{\partial\varphi_{k}}{\partial x_{j}}\rightarrow\frac{\partial w_{h}}{\partial x_{j}}\qquad\mathrm{in}\;[H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})]^{\prime}

as
k\rightarrow\infty
.

\square

4.2
Traces for non smooth functions

As we recalled earlier,
H^{1/2}(\Omega)
functions, or even less regular ones, generally have no traces. Nevertheless, if their Laplacian satisfies certain properties, and in particular if they are harmonic, then we can define their trace in a certain sense. This will be the subject of this subsection.

Denote by
\Gamma(\textit{{z}})
a nontangential cone with vertex at
z
, that is:

\Gamma(\textit{{z}})=\{\textit{{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N};\;|\textit{{x}}-\textit{{z}}|<C|\varrho(\textit{{x}})|\},

for a suitable constant
C>1
. The nontangential maximal function
v^{\star}
of a function
v
is defined by

v^{\star}(\textit{{z}})=\sup_{\textit{{x}}\,\in\,\Gamma(\textit{{z}})}|v(\textit{{x}})|.

Recall that Dahlberg
[
13
]
has shown that for any harmonic function
u
in
\Omega
, then

u^{\star}\in L^{2}(\Gamma)\Longleftrightarrow u\in H^{1/2}(\Omega)

and there exists a function
g\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
such that
u
tends to
g
nontangentially,
a.e
on
\Gamma
. And conversely, for any
g\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
there exists a unique harmonic function such that
u
tends nontangentially to
g

a.e
on
\Gamma
(see
[
12
]
) and
u
satisfies

\|u^{\star}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}.

We are now going to see how to define traces of functions that are not sufficiently regular. To do this, we shall use a regularity result for a Dirichlet problem for the bi-Laplacian. This one will allow us to deduce an interesting Green’s formula and then to define traces for non smooth functions. A more complete study on the bi-Laplacian will be published later.

Let us recall now some regularity results of the following problem:

(\mathscr{B}_{D})\quad\begin{cases}\Delta^{2}u\ =\ 0\quad\textrm{ in }\ \Omega,\\
u\ =\ g_{0}\quad\textrm{ on }\ \Gamma,\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\textit{{n}}}\ =\ g_{1}\quad\textrm{ on }\ \Gamma.\end{cases}

We know that if
g_{0}\in H^{1}(\Gamma)
and
g_{1}\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
verify the condition

\nabla_{\tau}g_{0}+g_{1}\boldsymbol{n}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma),

(4.2)

then there exists a function
u\in H^{2}(\Omega)
satisfying
u=g_{0}
and
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\textit{{n}}}\ =\ g_{1}
on
\Gamma
(see
[
22
]
if
N=2
,
[
9
]
if
N=3
and
[
31
]
if
N\geq 2
), with the following estimate:

\|u\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C(\Omega)(\|g_{0}\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}+\|g_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}+\|\nabla_{\tau}g_{0}+g_{1}\boldsymbol{n}\|_{\boldsymbol{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma)}).

(4.3)

Recently, the authors
[
3
]
improve this result as follows.

Theorem 4.4

Let
g_{0}\in H^{1}(\Gamma)
and
g_{1}\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
satisfying the condition (
4.2
). Then there exists a unique biharmonic function
u\in H^{2}(\Omega)
satisfying
u=g_{0}
and
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\textit{{n}}}\ =\ g_{1}
on
\Gamma
, with the estimate (
4.3
).

Proof.
Let
w\in H^{2}(\Omega)
such that
w=g_{0}
and
\frac{\partial w}{\partial\textit{{n}}}\ =\ g_{1}
on
\Gamma
. We know that there exists a unique solution
z\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
satisfying
\Delta^{2}z=\Delta^{2}w
in
\Omega
. The required function is given by
u=w-z
.

\square

More generally (see
[
31
]
), for any
0<s<1
and
g_{0}\in H^{1}(\Gamma)
and
g_{1}\in H^{s}(\Gamma)
verifying the condition

\nabla_{\tau}g_{0}+g_{1}\boldsymbol{n}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{s}(\Gamma),

there exists a function
u\in H^{s+3/2}(\Omega)
satisfying
u=g_{0}
and
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\textit{{n}}}\ =\ g_{1}
on
\Gamma
, with the following estimate:

\|u\|_{H^{s+3/2}(\Omega)}\leq C(\Omega)(\|g_{0}\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}+\|g_{1}\|_{H^{s}(\Gamma)}+\|\nabla_{\tau}g_{0}+g_{1}\boldsymbol{n}\|_{\boldsymbol{H}^{s}(\Gamma)}).

Remark 6

i) Let us introduce the following Hilbert space

0\leq s\leq 1,\quad\boldsymbol{H}_{\textit{{N}}}^{s}(\Gamma)=\{\boldsymbol{\mu}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{s}(\Gamma);\;\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\tau}={\bf 0}\}

equipped with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\boldsymbol{H}^{s}(\Gamma)}
.
Clearly

\boldsymbol{\mu}\in\boldsymbol{H}_{\textit{{N}}}^{s}(\Gamma)\Longleftrightarrow\boldsymbol{\mu}=g\textit{{n}}\quad\mathrm{with}\quad g\in L^{2}(\Gamma)\quad and\quad g\textit{{n}}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{s}(\Gamma).

Example 1

Let us consider the following Lipschitz domain:

\Omega=\{(r,\theta);\;0<r<1,\quad 0<\theta<3\pi/2\}

and different parts of its boundary:

\Gamma_{C}=\{(r,\theta);\;r=1\},\;\Gamma_{0}=\{(r,0);\;0<r<1\},\,\Gamma_{1}=\{(r,3\pi/2);\;0<r<1\}.

It is easy to verify that

g\in H^{1/2}(\Gamma),\;g_{|{\Gamma_{0}}}\in H^{1/2}_{00}(\Gamma_{0}),\;g_{|{\Gamma_{1}}}\in H^{1/2}_{00}(\Gamma_{1})\Longrightarrow g\textit{{n}}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma).

The above result asserts in particular that for any
\boldsymbol{\mu}\in\boldsymbol{H}_{\textit{{N}}}^{1/2}(\Gamma)
, there exists a function
u\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
such that
\nabla u=\boldsymbol{\mu}
on
\Gamma
. Moreover among all functions satisfying these conditions, there is one that is biharmonic.

ii) We can also prove that for any
g\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
satisfying
g\textit{{n}}\otimes\textit{{n}}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma)
, there exists a function
u\in H^{3}(\Omega)\cap H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
such that
\nabla^{2}u=g\textit{{n}}\otimes\textit{{n}}
on
\Gamma
. Moreover among all functions satisfying these conditions, there is one that is triharmonic. Here
\textit{{n}}\otimes\textit{{n}}
is the matrix
(n_{i}n_{j})_{ij}
.

Lemma 4.5

The following space

\left\{(v,\frac{\partial v}{\partial\textit{{n}}})\in H^{1}(\Gamma)\times L^{2}(\Gamma);\;v\in H^{2}(\Omega)\right\}

is dense in
H^{1}(\Gamma)\times L^{2}(\Gamma)
.

Adapting the proof of this result (see
[
38
]
, Lemma 4.4, Chapter 5, page 274), we can prove easily the following lemma.

Lemma 4.6

The following space

\left\{\frac{\partial v}{\partial\textit{{n}}}\in L^{2}(\Gamma);\;v\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\right\}

is dense in
L^{2}(\Gamma)
.

Using the above lemma, we deduce immediately the following density result:

\boldsymbol{H}_{\textit{{N}}}^{1/2}(\Gamma)\;\;\mathrm{is\,\,dense\,\,in}\;\;\boldsymbol{L}_{\textit{{N}}}^{2}(\Gamma):=\{\boldsymbol{\mu}\in\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\Gamma);\;\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\tau}={\bf 0}\}.

This space, as well as its dual space
[\boldsymbol{H}_{\textit{{N}}}^{1/2}(\Gamma)]^{\prime}
, can therefore be considered as space of normal fields of regularity
1/2
and
-1/2
respectively:

\boldsymbol{H}_{\textit{{N}}}^{1/2}(\Gamma)\hookrightarrow\boldsymbol{L}_{\textit{{N}}}^{2}(\Gamma)\hookrightarrow[\boldsymbol{H}_{\textit{{N}}}^{1/2}(\Gamma)]^{\prime},

(4.4)

with density.

Introduce now the following Hilbert space: for
0\leq s<1/2

M^{s}(\Omega)=\left\{v\in H^{s}(\Omega);\ \Delta v\in[H^{3/2}_{0}(\Omega)]^{\prime}\right\}.

with its norm

\|v\|_{M^{s}(\Omega)}=(\|v\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\Delta v\|^{2}_{[H^{3/2}_{0}(\Omega)]^{\prime}})^{1/2}.

We will explain below the choice in the above definition of the dual space
[H^{3/2}_{0}(\Omega)]^{\prime}
. And to simplify the notations, we set
M(\Omega):=M^{0}(\Omega)
.

Lemma 4.7

For any
0\leq s<1/2
the space

\mathscr{D}\big(\overline{\Omega}\big)\quad is\,\,dense\,\,in\;\;M^{s}(\Omega).

Proof.
Let
\ell\in\left[M^{s}(\Omega)\right]^{\prime}
be such that

\forall v\in\mathscr{D}(\overline{\Omega}),\ \ \ \left<\,\ell,v\,\right>\ =\ 0.

We know that there exist
f\in H^{-s}(\Omega)
and
g\in H^{3/2}_{0}(\Omega)
such that for any
v\in M^{s}(\Omega)
,

\left<\,\ell,v\,\right>\,=\langle f,\,v\rangle_{H^{-s}(\Omega)\times H^{s}(\Omega)}+\langle\Delta v,\,g\rangle_{[H^{3/2}_{0}(\Omega)]^{\prime}\times H^{3/2}_{0}(\Omega)}.

Let
\widetilde{f}\in H^{-s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
\widetilde{g}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
the extension functions by zero of respectively
f
and
g
(in fact
\widetilde{g}\in H^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for any
1\leq r<3/2
). Then for any
v\in\mathscr{D}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we have

\langle\widetilde{f},\,v\rangle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\widetilde{g}\Delta v=0,

i.e.

-\Delta\widetilde{g}\ =\widetilde{f}\quad\mathrm{in}\;\mathbb{R}^{N}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\widetilde{g}-\Delta\widetilde{g}\in H^{-s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Consequently, we deduce that
\widetilde{g}\in H^{2-s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. It means that
g\in H^{2-s}_{0}(\Omega)
. As
\mathscr{D}(\Omega)
is dense in
H^{2-s}_{0}(\Omega)
, there exists
g_{k}\in\mathscr{D}(\Omega)
such that

g_{k}\ \ \longrightarrow\ \ g\ \ \textrm{ in }\ H^{2-s}_{0}(\Omega)\quad\mathrm{as}\;k\rightarrow\infty.

Then, for any
v\in M^{s}(\Omega)
, we have

\left<\,\ell,v\,\right>\,=\ \lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}(-\int_{\Omega}v\Delta g_{k}+\int_{\Omega}g_{k}\Delta v)=0,

which ends the proof.

\square

Theorem 4.8

Let
0\leq s<1/2
. The linear mapping
{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{\textit{{n}}}:v\longmapsto v\textit{{n}}_{|\Gamma}
defined on

\mathscr{D}\big(\overline{\Omega}\big)
can be extended to a linear and continuous mapping, still denoted by
{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{\textit{{n}}}

\begin{array}[]{rl}{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{\textit{{n}}}:M^{s}(\Omega)&\longrightarrow[{\boldsymbol{H}_{\textit{{N}}}^{1/2-s}(\Gamma)}]^{\prime}\\

Moreover, we have the Green’s formula: For all
v\in M^{s}(\Omega)
and

(4.5)

where
\langle v\textit{{n}},\,\nabla\varphi\rangle_{\Gamma}
denotes the duality brackets between
[{\boldsymbol{H}_{\textit{{N}}}^{1/2-s}(\Gamma)}]^{\prime}
and

\boldsymbol{H}_{\textit{{N}}}^{1/2-s}(\Gamma)
.

Proof.
We consider only the case
s=0
, the proof being similar for the other cases. Clearly the above Green’s formula holds for any
v\in\mathscr{D}\big(\overline{\Omega}\big)
and any
\varphi\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Therefore,

|\int_{\Gamma}v\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial\textit{{n}}}|=|\int_{\Gamma}v\textit{{n}}\cdot\nabla\varphi|\leq\|v\|_{M(\Omega)}\|\varphi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}.

Now, let
\boldsymbol{\mu}\in\boldsymbol{H}_{\textit{{N}}}^{1/2}(\Gamma)
. From Remark
6
we know that there exists a function
\varphi\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

Hence the above inequalities imply that for any

4.4

Therefore the linear mapping
{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{\textit{{n}}}:v\longmapsto v\textit{{n}}|_{\Gamma}
defined on
\mathscr{D}\big(\overline{\Omega}\big)
is continuous for the norm of
M(\Omega)
. As
\mathscr{D}\big(\overline{\Omega}\big)
is dense in
M(\Omega)
, the mapping
{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{\textit{{n}}}
can be extended to a linear continuous mapping still called
{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{\textit{{n}}}
from
M(\Omega)
into
[{\boldsymbol{H}_{\textit{{N}}}^{1/2}(\Gamma)}]^{\prime}
.

\square

5
Preliminary results for polygonal and polyedral domains

Before studying in the following sections the regularity issues for the inhomogeneous Dirichlet problem stated in a bounded Lipschitz open set, it is important to recall and analyse the case where the domain is a polygon, and particularly when it is non convex.

5.1
Characterization of harmonic functions kernels

1. Polygon cases.
Let
\Omega
be a polygon whose angles are denoted by
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{J}
. Even if we have to reorder this family, we can assume that it is increasing:
\omega_{1}\leq\ldots\leq\omega_{J}
.
For any
-1/2\leq s<\infty
, we define the following kernel:

\mathscr{N}_{s}(\Omega)=\{\varphi\in H^{-s}(\Omega);\;\Delta\varphi=0\;\mathrm{in}\;\Omega\;\mathrm{and}\;\varphi=0\;\mathrm{on}\;\Gamma\}

and for any
j=1,\ldots,J
, we set

\nu_{s}(\omega_{j})=\mathrm{the\,largest\ integer}<\frac{1+s}{\alpha_{j}},\quad\mathrm{with}\>\alpha_{j}=\frac{\pi}{\omega_{j}}\in\,]1/2,\infty[.

When
s\in\mathbb{N}
, we have (see
[
24
]
)

\mathrm{dim}\,\mathscr{N}_{s}(\Omega)=\sum_{j=1}^{j=J}\nu_{s}(\omega_{j}),

(5.1)

provided that for
s\geq 1
,

\forall j=1\ldots,J,\quad\omega_{j}\notin\{\frac{1}{s+1}\pi,\ldots,\frac{s}{s+1}\pi\},

Since the family
(\alpha_{j})_{1\leq j\leq J}
is decreasing, we have the following equivalence:

\mathscr{N}_{s}(\Omega)=\{0\}\Longleftrightarrow s\leq\alpha_{1}-1.

This result remains true if
\Omega
is a curvilinear polygonal open set.

The case where the parameter
s
is real is also interesting. Here, we will limit ourselves to cases where
-1/2\leq s\leq 0
. We can easily verify that the equality (
5.1
) holds also for such
s
. A brief explanation of this result can be given. The singularities involved in the structure of the kernel
\mathscr{N}_{s}(\Omega)
are of the type
(r^{-\alpha_{j}}-r^{\alpha_{j}})\mathrm{sin}(\alpha_{j}\theta)
(see (
9.3
) below), where here
1/2<\alpha_{j}=\frac{\pi}{\omega_{j}}<1
. However the function
r^{-\alpha_{j}}
belongs to
H^{t}
for any
t
strictly less than
1-\alpha_{j}
. Setting
t=-s
, we get
\frac{1-s}{\alpha_{j}}\in\,]1,2[
and then
\nu_{-s}(\omega_{j})=1
. We conclude that

\forall-1/2\leq s\leq 0,\quad dim\,\mathscr{N}_{s}(\Omega)=Card\{j\in\{1,\ldots,J\};\;\omega_{j}>\pi\}.

(5.2)

Remark 8

i) Note that
\mathscr{N}_{0}(\Omega)=\{0\}
iff
\Omega
is convex.

ii)
We have
\mathscr{N}_{-1/2}(\Omega)=\{0\}
for any polygon (and also for any bounded Lipschitz domain of
\mathbb{R}^{N},
with

, see Theorem

below).

iii) For any polygon
\Omega
, there exists

such that

\mathscr{N}_{-s_{0}}(\Omega)=\{0\}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\mathrm{for\,any}\;s<s_{0},\;\mathscr{N}_{-s}(\Omega)\neq\{0\},

(5.3)

where
s_{0}=1-\alpha_{J}
.

iv) We conjecture that for any polygon
\Omega
, there exists
s_{0}(\Omega)\in\,]0,1/2[
such that (
5.3
) holds.

2. Polyhedron cases.
Let now
\Omega
be a polyhedron of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. The situation is little bit different. We denote by
\Gamma_{k}
,
k=1,\ldots,K
the faces of
\Omega
and by

the edge between
\Gamma_{j}
and

when
\overline{\Gamma}_{j}
and

intersect. The measure of the interior angle of the edge

is denoted by

and as in 2D we have the following property:

where

5.2
Interpolation of subspaces

We recall in this subsection some interpolation results of subspaces. The first one is due to Ivanov and Kalton
[

]
. Lat

,

(Ivanov-Kalton)

There exist two indices

iii) If

The above theorem is generalized by Asekritova, Cobos and Kruglyak

when

Theorem 5.2

then the space

is a closed subspace of codimension

Remark 9

)

Proof.

6
Area integral estimate. Counter-example

In
[
38
]
, Nečas proved the following property (see Theorem 2.2 Section 6): if
\varrho^{\alpha/p}u\in L^{p}(\Omega)
and
\varrho^{\alpha/p}\nabla u\in L^{p}(\Omega)
, with
0\leq\alpha<p-1
, then
u_{|\Gamma}\in L^{p}(\Gamma)
and

\int_{\Gamma}|u|^{p}\leq C(\Omega)(\int_{\Omega}\varrho^{\alpha}|u|^{p}+\int_{\Omega}\varrho^{\alpha}|\nabla u|^{p})

(6.1)

However, if
\alpha=p-1
, the above inequality does not hold in general, as proved in a counter example with
\Omega=\,]0,1/2[\,\times\,]0,1/2[
. In particular for
\alpha=1
and
p=2
, if
\sqrt{\varrho}\,\nabla u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, in which case we know that
u\in H^{1/2}(\Omega)
and therefore the function
u
may have no trace. Recall that if in addition
\Delta u\in[H^{3/2}_{0}(\Omega)]^{\prime}
, then we can define some notion of trace for
u
in a space denoted by
[{\boldsymbol{H}_{\textit{{N}}}^{s}(\Gamma)}]^{\prime}
for any
0<s\leq 1
(see Remark
7
).

At higher order and for example if
u\in H^{3/2}(\Omega)
, there is a
\mathscr{C}^{1}
domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
and a function
u\in H^{3/2}(\Omega)
whose trace on
\Gamma
does not have a tangential derivative in
L^{2}(\Gamma)
(see Proposition 3.2 in
[
29
]
).

What about if in addition the function
u
is harmonic? In
[
14
]
(see also Corollary, Section 6 in
[
13
]
), the authors proved the following property: Let
u
be a harmonic function in
\Omega
that vanishes at some point
\textit{{x}}_{0}\in\Omega
, then

\int_{\Gamma}|u|^{2}\leq C(\Omega)\int_{\Omega}\varrho|\nabla u|^{2},

(6.2)

where the constant
C(\Omega)
depends only on the Lipschitz character of
\Omega.
The proof given on pages 1428 and 1429 in
[
14
]
contains formal calculations that we believe to be unjustified.
The function
u
being supposed to verify the property

\int_{\Omega}\varrho|\nabla u|^{2}<\infty,

this implies in particular that
u\in H^{1/2}(\Omega)
. In order to justify all the calculations, the authors should have first assumed that
u
is regular and then, once the inequality has been established, used a density argument as is usually done. Instead, the authors begin by giving some elementary inequalities of integrals before moving on to more sophisticated considerations relating to the distance to the boundary and the corresponding Carleson measure properties.

There is a second reason why we think that (
6.2
) is generally not satisfied. This result would then imply that the above inequality (
6.1
) would be true in the critical case
p=2
and
\alpha=1
when the function
u
is in addition harmonic.
A priori
, if the second integral in (
6.2
) converges, then the function
u
verifies only that
\nabla u\in[\textit{{H}}_{00}^{\,1/2}(\Omega)]^{\prime}
but does not satisfy the property:
\nabla u\in[\textit{{H}}^{\,1/2}(\Omega)]^{\prime}
, which would allow us to obtain, thanks to Theorem
4.2
, that
u\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
.

Let’s assume for a moment that the inequality (
6.2
) is correct. Observe that this inequality implies the following property: let
u
be a harmonic function in
\Omega
satisfying
u(\textit{{x}}_{0})=0
and
\nabla u(\textit{{x}}_{0})={\bf 0}
at some point
\textit{{x}}_{0}\in\Omega
, then

\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}\leq C(\Omega)\Big(\int_{\Omega}\varrho|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}\Big)^{1/2},

(6.3)

where the constant
C(\Omega)
depends only on the Lipschitz character of
\Omega.
The following inequalities would then be true for any harmonic function
u
:

\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}\leq C(\Omega)\Big(\int_{\Omega}\varrho|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}+\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Big)^{1/2},

(6.4)

and

\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}\leq C(\Omega)\|u\|_{H^{3/2}(\Omega)},

(6.5)

since for harmonic functions, the following norms are equivalent (see (
3.17
) and (
3.18
)):

\|u\|_{H^{3/2}(\Omega)}\simeq\Big(\int_{\Omega}\varrho|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}+\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Big)^{1/2}.

A third reason for saying that the inequality (
6.2
), and then also the inequality (
6.5
), is not correct is that it implies that the
H^{3/2}
regularity for the Laplace equation with a homogeneous Dirichlet condition is not attained in general for a RHS in
[H_{00}^{1/2}(\Omega)]^{\prime}
, as established by Jerison and Kenig (see
[
29
]
page 203). This contradicts our result established by using the Grisvard’s fine estimations (see Theorem
7.1
below).

These considerations lead us to believe that the inequality (
6.2
) cannot in general be satisfied, as we shall see a little later with the help of a counter-example.

The following proposition shows that Inequality (
6.4
) is false and therefore Inequalities (
6.2
) and (
6.3
) are also wrong.

Proposition 6.1
(Counter example for Inequality (
6.4
))

For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exist a Lipschitz domain
\Omega_{\varepsilon}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
and a harmonic function
w_{\varepsilon}\in H^{3/2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
(with
\sqrt{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}\,\nabla^{2}w_{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}))
such that the following family

(\|\varrho\nabla^{2}w_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|w_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{3/2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})})_{\varepsilon},

is bounded with respect
\varepsilon
and

\|w_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\varepsilon})}\rightarrow\infty\quad as\;\varepsilon\rightarrow 0.

Proof.
Recall first that if
u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
is harmonic, then

u\in H^{1/2}(\Omega)\Longleftrightarrow\sqrt{\varrho}\,\nabla u\in L^{2}(\Omega)\quad\mathrm{and}\quad u\in H^{3/2}(\Omega)\Longleftrightarrow\sqrt{\varrho}\,\nabla^{2}u\in L^{2}(\Omega),

(see Theorem
3.2
and Theorem
3.8
above or Theorem 4.2 in
[
29
]
).

Step 1.
We suppose now that
\Omega=\,]0,1/2[\,\times\,]0,1/2[
and for any
\varepsilon>0
and close to 0, we define, as in the counter example to G. David given in the paper of D. Jerison and C. Kenig (
[
29
]
), the following open set:

\Omega_{\varepsilon}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2};\;0<x<1/2\;\;\mathrm{and}\;\;\varepsilon\lambda(x/\varepsilon)<y<1/2\},

where

\lambda(x)=x\quad\mathrm{for}\;\;0\leq x\leq 1,\quad\lambda(x)=2-x\quad\mathrm{for}\;\;1\leq x\leq 2\quad\mathrm{and}\;\;\lambda(x+2)=\lambda(x).

We set

\Gamma_{\varepsilon}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2};\;y=\varepsilon\lambda(x/\varepsilon),\;\mathrm{with}\;0<x<1/2\},

which is just a part of the boundary of
\Omega
.

Step 2.
Let us consider the following function (see also Example 2.1 given in Chapter 6 of the book of Nečas) which depends only on the variable
y
:

v(x,y)=\int_{0}^{y}ds\int_{1/2}^{s}\frac{dt}{t\,\ell n\,t}.

It is easy to verify that
\sqrt{\varrho}\,\nabla^{2}v\in\textbf{{L}}^{2}(\Omega)
where
\varrho
is the distance to the boundary of
\Omega
. So from Theorem
3.2
, we have also
v\in H^{3/2}(\Omega)
. Moreover the
L^{2}
norm of the tangential derivative
\partial_{\tau}v
of
v
on
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
tends to infinity as
\varepsilon
tends to zero. Indeed, we can see that

\partial_{\tau}v(x,x)=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\int_{x}^{1/2}\frac{dt}{t\,|\ell n\,t|}\quad\mathrm{for}\;\;0<x<\varepsilon,

and

\partial_{\tau}v(x,2\varepsilon-x)=-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\int_{2\varepsilon-x}^{1/2}\frac{dt}{t\,|\ell n\,t|}\quad\mathrm{for}\;\;\varepsilon<x<2\varepsilon.

Observe that

\int_{x}^{1/2}\frac{dt}{t\,|\ell n\,t|}=\ell n(-\ell n\,x)-\ell n(\ell n\,2).

So we have

\begin{array}[]{rl}\displaystyle\int_{\varepsilon}^{2\varepsilon}\left(\int_{2\varepsilon-x}^{1/2}\frac{dt}{t\,|\ell n\,t|}\right)^{2}dx=&\displaystyle\int_{\varepsilon}^{2\varepsilon}[\ell n(-\ell n\,(2\varepsilon-x))-\ell n(\ell n\,2)]^{2}dx\\
=&\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}[\ell n(-\ell n\,x)-\ell n(\ell n\,2)]^{2}dx.\end{array}

In addition as the function
\partial_{\tau}v
is periodic with the period
2\varepsilon
we get

\|\partial_{\tau}v\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\varepsilon})}^{2}=(1/4\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\left(\int_{x}^{1/2}\frac{dt}{t\,|\ell n\,t|}\right)^{2}dx,

where we have chosen
\varepsilon=1/4k
, with
k\in\mathbb{N}^{\star}
.

Step 3.
We will give an estimate of the integral:

I_{\varepsilon}=\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\left(\int_{x}^{1/2}\frac{dt}{t\,|\ell n\,t|}\right)^{2}dx.

Setting
s=\ell n\,t
, we get

\int_{x}^{1/2}\frac{dt}{t\,|\ell n\,t|}=-\int_{\ell n\,x}^{-\ell n\,2}\frac{ds}{s}=\ell n(-\ell n\,x)-\ell n(\ell n\,2).

So
I_{\varepsilon}=I_{1\varepsilon}-I_{2\varepsilon}+I_{3\varepsilon}
, where

I_{1\varepsilon}=\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}[\ell n(-\ell n\,x)]^{2}dx\quad I_{2\varepsilon}=2\ell n(\ell n\,2)\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\ell n(-\ell n\,x)dx

and

I_{3\varepsilon}=\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}[\ell n(\ell n\,2)]^{2}dx.

Since the function
\ell n(-\ell n\,x)
is nondecreasing in the interval
]0,\varepsilon]
and
I_{\varepsilon}\geq I_{1\varepsilon}-I_{2\varepsilon}
, we deduce easily the estimate for
\varepsilon
close to 0:

I_{\varepsilon}\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\ell n(-\ell n\,\varepsilon)]^{2}

So, we get

\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}[\ell n(-\ell n\,\varepsilon)]\leq\|\partial_{\tau}v\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\varepsilon})}\longrightarrow\infty\quad\mathrm{as}\quad\varepsilon\rightarrow 0.

Step 4.
Now, using Corollary
5.6
in the polygon
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
, there exits a unique solution
H^{3/2}_{0}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
satisfying
\Delta u_{\varepsilon}=\Delta v
in
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
with the estimate

\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{3/2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\|\Delta v\|_{M(\Omega_{\varepsilon})},

where
C(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
depends only on the Poincaré constant and on the Lipschitz character of
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
, which are both bounded with respect
\varepsilon
(see (
5.13
) for the definition of the space
M(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
). So we have
C(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\leq C(\Omega)
. Clearly the sequence
(\|\Delta v\|_{M(\Omega_{\varepsilon})})_{\varepsilon}
, where we recall that
k=1/(4\varepsilon)
, is increasing when
\varepsilon
tends to zero. And since
M(\Omega)=[H^{1/2}_{00}(\Omega)]^{\prime}
, we have

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\|\Delta v\|_{M(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}=\|\Delta v\|_{[H^{1/2}_{00}(\Omega)]^{\prime}}.

We then deduce that

\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{3/2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C(\Omega)\|\Delta v\|_{[H^{1/2}_{00}(\Omega)]^{\prime}}\leq C(\Omega)\|v\|_{H^{3/2}(\Omega)},

to
where
C(\Omega)
depends only on the Poincaré constant and on the Lipschitz character of
\Omega
. So the harmonic function in
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
defined by
w_{\varepsilon}=v-u_{\varepsilon}
belongs to
H^{3/2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
and satisfies

\|w_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{3/2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C(\Omega)\|v\|_{H^{3/2}(\Omega)}.

Step 5.
We will prove now that (
6.4
) is not true and therefore that the inequalities (
6.3
) and (
6.2
) are also wrong. Let’s assume the contrary,
i.e
that (
6.4
) holds for any harmonic function
u
in
\Omega
. Recall that any harmonic function
z
satisfies the inequality

\|\varrho\nabla^{2}z\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C(\Omega)\|z\|_{H^{3/2}(\Omega)},

where the constant
C(\Omega)
depends only on Lipschitz character of
\Omega
. Hence any harmonic function
u

would then verify:

\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}\leq C(\Omega)\|u\|_{H^{3/2}(\Omega)}.

Then, as
u_{\varepsilon}=0
on
\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}
, we have
\partial_{\tau}w_{\varepsilon}=\partial_{\tau}v
on
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
and the harmonic function
w_{\varepsilon}
would verify the following inequality:

\|\partial_{\tau}w_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\varepsilon})}\leq C(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\|w_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{3/2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}.

Since the constant
C(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
depends only on Lipschitz character of
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
, it is then bounded with respect
\varepsilon
and we get from (
6
) the following inequality:

\|\partial_{\tau}w_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\varepsilon})}\leq C(\Omega)\|v\|_{H^{3/2}(\Omega)}.

But from Step 2 we know that the left-hand side of the above inequality tends to infinity when
\varepsilon
tends to zero. This contradicts the hypothesis that the above inequality (
6.4
) takes place.

\square

7
The inhomogeneous problem. Solvability in
H^{3/2}(\Omega)

Here we will consider the following problem:

(\mathscr{L}_{D}^{0}):\ \ \ \ -\Delta u=f\quad\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega\quad\mbox{and }\quad u=0\ \ \mbox{on }\Gamma,

for given
f
in fractional Sobolev spaces. It is well known that for any
f\in H^{s-2}(\Omega)
, with
1/2<s<3/2
, there exists a unique solution
u\in H^{s}(\Omega)
to Problem
(\mathscr{L}_{D}^{0})
. The limit cases
s=3/2
or
s=1/2
are particularly delicate. For
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
(in fact
f\in H^{s-2}(\Omega)
, with arbitrary
s>3/2
, is suffisant) , there exists a unique solution
u\in H^{3/2}(\Omega)
to problem
(\mathscr{L}_{D}^{0})
(see Theorem B in
[
29
]
). But these assumptions on
f
are too strong as we can see below. It would be interesting to characterize the range of the Laplacian operator from
H^{3/2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
into
[H^{1/2}_{00}(\Omega)]^{\prime}.
In
[
29
]
(see Theorem 0.4) the authors show that it is not possible for the operator

\Delta:H^{3/2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\longrightarrow[H^{1/2}_{00}(\Omega)]^{\prime}.

(7.1)

to be an isomorphism, even if
\Omega
is of class
\mathscr{C}^{1}
. Their proof is based on the argument, which consists to say that if a harmonic function
v
belongs to
H^{3/2}(\Omega)
, then its trace satisfies
v_{|\Gamma}\in H^{1}(\Gamma)
. However, Theorem
6.1
asserts that this is not always the case when the domain
\Omega
is only Lipschitz. In the following theorem we prove that the operator (
7.1
) is really an isomorphism.

Theorem 7.1

The operators

\Delta:H^{3/2}_{0}(\Omega)\longrightarrow[{H}^{1/2}_{00}(\Omega)]^{\prime}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\Delta:H^{1/2}_{00}(\Omega)\longrightarrow[H^{3/2}_{0}(\Omega)]^{\prime}

(7.2)

are isomorphisms.

Proof.

Step 1.
It suffices to consider the case
N=3
, the proof being similar for the other dimensions.

Let
(\Omega_{k})_{k}
be an increasing sequence of polyhedral open sets converging to
\Omega
. Let
\varphi\in\mathscr{D}(\Omega)
and
k_{0}
such that supp
\varphi\subset\Omega_{k_{0}}
. Then using Inequality (
5.6
), we have

\|\varphi\|_{H^{3/2}(\Omega)}=\|\varphi\|_{H^{3/2}(\Omega_{k_{0}})}\leq C(\Omega_{k_{0}})\|\Delta\varphi\|_{[H^{1/2}_{00}(\Omega_{k_{0}})]^{\prime}},

where the constant
C(\Omega_{k_{0}})
above depends only on the Poincaré constant and the Lipschitz character of
\Omega_{k_{0}}
(see Corollary
5.6
) and then
C(\Omega_{k_{0}})
is bounded.
But for any
f\in[H^{1/2}_{00}(\Omega)]^{\prime}
we have

\begin{array}[]{rl}\|f\|_{[H^{1/2}_{00}(\Omega_{k_{0}})]^{\prime}}&=\sup_{\psi\in H^{1/2}_{00}(\Omega_{k_{0}}),\,\psi\not=0}\dfrac{\langle f,\psi\rangle}{\|\psi\|_{H^{1/2}_{00}(\Omega_{k_{0}})}}\\
&=\sup_{\psi\in H^{1/2}_{00}(\Omega_{k_{0}}),\,\psi\not=0}\dfrac{\langle f,\widetilde{\psi}\rangle}{\|\widetilde{\psi}\|_{H^{1/2}_{00}(\Omega)}}\\
\\
&\leq\|f\|_{[H^{1/2}_{00}(\Omega)]^{\prime}}\end{array}

where
\widetilde{\psi}
is the extension by zero inside
\Omega
of
\psi
. Hence we get for any
\varphi\in\mathscr{D}(\Omega)

\|\varphi\|_{H^{3/2}(\Omega)}\leq C(\Omega)\|\Delta\varphi\|_{[H^{1/2}_{00}(\Omega)]^{\prime}}.

Using the density of
\mathscr{D}(\Omega)
in
H^{3/2}_{0}(\Omega)
, we deduce the same estimate for any
\varphi\in H^{3/2}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Step 2.
That means that the range
M(\Omega)
of the following mapping

\Delta:H^{3/2}_{0}(\Omega)\longrightarrow[H^{1/2}_{00}(\Omega)]^{\prime}

is closed in
[H^{1/2}_{00}(\Omega)]^{\prime}
. So we have
M(\Omega)=
(Ker
\Delta)^{\bot}
, with
\Delta
is given by the operator

\Delta:H^{1/2}_{00}(\Omega)\longrightarrow[{H}^{3/2}_{0}(\Omega)]^{\prime}

(7.3)

and where

(\mathrm{Ker}\,\Delta)^{\bot}=\{f\in[H^{1/2}_{00}(\Omega)]^{\prime};\;\langle f,v\rangle_{[H^{1/2}_{00}(\Omega)]^{\prime}\times H^{1/2}_{00}(\Omega)}=0,\;\forall v\in\mathrm{Ker}\,\Delta\}.

Observe now that the Kernel of the operator (
7.3
) is trivial. As a consequence, we get the surjectivity of the first operator in (
7.2
) and by duality the second isomorphism.

\square

The homogeneous problem

8.1
Nečas Property, first version

Recall that if

u\in H^{1}(\Omega)\quad\mathrm{with}\quad\Delta u\in\ L^{2}(\Omega)

(8.1)

then we have the following equivalences

u\in H^{1}(\Gamma)\Longleftrightarrow\frac{\partial u}{\partial\textit{{n}}}\in L^{2}(\Gamma)\Longleftrightarrow\nabla u\in L^{2}(\Gamma).

(8.2)

Moreover, assuming (
8.1
), we have the following estimates

\|\frac{\partial u}{\partial\textit{{n}}}\|_{\textit{{L}}^{2}(\Gamma)}\ \leq\ C\Big(\inf_{k\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\left|u+k\right|\right|_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}+\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Big)

(8.3)

and

\inf_{k\in\mathbb{R}}\|u+k\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}\ \leq\ C\Big(\|\frac{\partial u}{\partial\textit{{n}}}\|_{\textit{{L}}^{2}(\Gamma)}+\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Big),

(8.4)

where the constants
C
above depend only on the Lipschitz character of
\Omega
.

In the following theorem, we give a variant of this result, for functions in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
but with Laplacian less regular.

Theorem 8.1

Let

u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\quad\mathrm{with}\quad\Delta u\in\ [H^{1/2}(\Omega)]^{\prime}.

Then
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\textit{{n}}}\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
and we have the following estimate

\|\frac{\partial u}{\partial\textit{{n}}}\|_{\textit{{L}}^{2}(\Gamma)}\ \leq\ C\|\Delta u\|_{[H^{1/2}(\Omega)]^{\prime}}.

Proof.

Step 1.
It suffices to deal with the case when
\Omega
is a Lipschitz hypograph:

\Omega\ =\ \left\{\,(\textit{{x}}^{\prime},x_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N};\ \ x_{N}<\xi(\textit{{x}}^{\prime})\right\},

where
\xi\in\mathscr{C}^{0,1}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
with supp
\xi
compact. Observe that, with the same proof as in
[
23
]
, we can show that

\mathscr{D}(\overline{\Omega})\quad\mathrm{is\,\,dense\,\,in}\quad E(\Delta;\,\Omega)=\{v\in H^{1}(\Omega);\;\Delta v\in[H^{1/2}(\Omega)]^{\prime}\}.

So that
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\textit{{n}}}\in H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)
and we have the following Green’s formula: for any
\varphi\in H^{1}(\Omega)
,

\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi+\langle\Delta u,\,\varphi\rangle_{[H^{1/2}(\Omega)]^{\prime}\times H^{1/2}(\Omega)}=\langle\frac{\partial u}{\partial\textit{{n}}},\,\varphi\rangle_{H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)\times\ H^{1/2}(\Gamma)}.

(8.5)

Choose a sequence
(\xi_{k})_{k}
in
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
such that for any
k\geq 1

\xi_{k}\leq\xi\textrm{ on }\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
,
\xi_{k}(\textit{{x}}^{\prime})=\xi(\textit{{x}}^{\prime})
if
|\textit{{x}}^{\prime}|\geq R_{0}
with
\,\nabla\xi_{k}\,{}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})}\leq C
and for any
1\leq p<\infty

\xi_{k}\ \longrightarrow\ \ \xi\ \textrm{ in }L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\nabla\xi_{k}\ \longrightarrow\ \ \nabla\xi\ \textrm{ in }L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1}).

We set

\Omega_{k}\,=\ \left\{\,(\textit{{x}}^{\prime},x_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N};\ \ x_{N}<\xi_{k}(\textit{{x}}^{\prime})\right\}.

Step 2.
By the first isomorphism in Theorem
7.1
, we deduce from (
8.1
) that
u\in H^{3/2}_{0}(\Omega)
. Setting
f=\Delta u
and let
(f_{k})_{k}\subset\mathscr{D}(\Omega)
be such that
f_{k}\longrightarrow f
in
[H^{1/2}(\Omega)]^{\prime}
as
k\rightarrow\infty
. Let
u_{k}\in H^{3/2}_{0}(\Omega_{k})\cap H^{2}(\Omega_{k})
be the unique solution satisfying
\Delta u_{k}-u_{k}=f_{k}-u
in
\Omega_{k}
as
k\rightarrow\infty
. Setting now

\widetilde{u_{k}}=\begin{cases}u_{k}\quad\mathrm{in}\;\;\Omega_{k}\\
0\quad\;\;\textrm{in}\;\;\Omega\setminus\Omega_{k}\end{cases}

Clearly, the sequence
(\widetilde{u_{k}})_{k}
is bounded in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and for any
\varphi\in H^{1}(\Omega)
,

\begin{array}[]{rl}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{k}}(u_{k}\varphi\ +\nabla u_{k}\cdot\nabla\varphi)&=-\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{k}}(f_{k}-u)\,\varphi+\int_{\Gamma_{k}}\frac{\partial u_{k}}{\partial\textit{{n}}_{k}}\,\varphi\\
\\
\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(u\varphi\ +\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi)&=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u\,\varphi-\langle\Delta u,\varphi\rangle_{\Omega}+\langle\frac{\partial u}{\partial\textit{{n}}},\,\varphi\rangle_{\Gamma}.\end{array}

(8.6)

Substract (
8.5
) from (
8.6
), we get for any
\varphi\in H^{1}(\Omega)

\begin{array}[]{rl}&\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\widetilde{u_{k}}-u)\varphi+\nabla(\widetilde{u_{k}}-u)\cdot\nabla\varphi\\
\\
&\displaystyle=\left[\int_{\Omega}(\widetilde{u_{k}}\varphi+\nabla\widetilde{u_{k}}\cdot\nabla\varphi)-\int_{\Omega_{k}}(u_{k}\varphi+\nabla u_{k}\cdot\nabla\varphi)\right]+\\
\\
&\displaystyle+\left[\int_{\Omega_{k}}(u_{k}\varphi+\nabla u_{k}\cdot\nabla\varphi)-\int_{\Omega}(u\varphi+\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi)\right]\\
\\
&=\displaystyle\,\int_{\Gamma_{k}}\frac{\partial u_{k}}{\partial\textit{{n}}_{k}}\varphi-\langle\frac{\partial u}{\partial\textit{{n}}},\,\varphi\rangle_{\Gamma}\,+\;\langle f-f_{k},\varphi\rangle_{\Omega}+\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{k}}f_{k}\,\varphi-\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{k}}u\varphi.\end{array}

So taking
\varphi=\widetilde{u_{k}}-u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, we deduce that

\begin{array}[]{rl}&\|\widetilde{u_{k}}-u\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\|\widetilde{u_{k}}-u\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\big(\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{k})}+\;\|f-f_{k}\|_{[H^{1/2}(\Omega)]^{\prime}}\big)\\
\\
&+\;\|\frac{\partial u_{k}}{\partial\textit{{n}}_{k}}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{k})}\|\widetilde{u_{k}}-u\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{k})}+\;\|f_{k}\|_{[H^{1/2}(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{k})]^{\prime}}\|\widetilde{u_{k}}-u\|_{H^{1}(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{k})]}.\end{array}

(8.7)

Step 3.
Besides, recall the following Rellich’s identity: for any
v\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
:

\int_{\Gamma}\textbf{{h}}\cdot\textbf{{n}}\left|\frac{\partial v}{\partial\textbf{{n}}}\right|^{2}=\int_{\Omega}\left[2\textbf{{h}}\,\Delta v+2\frac{\partial\textbf{{h}}}{\partial x_{k}}\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{k}}-(\mathrm{div}\,\textbf{{h}})\nabla v\right]\cdot\nabla v.

(8.8)

Using then (
8.8
) with
v=u_{k}\in H^{2}(\Omega_{k})\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{k})
and the first isomorphism in Theorem
7.1
, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{rl}\|\frac{\partial u_{k}}{\partial\textit{{n}}_{k}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{k})}&\leq C\big(\|\nabla u_{k}\|_{H^{1/2}(\Omega_{k})}\|\Delta u_{k}\|_{[H^{1/2}(\Omega_{k})]^{\prime}}+\|\nabla u_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{k})}\big)\\
\\
&\leq C\big(\|u_{k}\|_{H^{3/2}(\Omega_{k})}\|\Delta u_{k}\|_{[H^{1/2}(\Omega_{k})]^{\prime}}\big)\leq C\|\Delta u_{k}\|^{2}_{[H^{1/2}(\Omega_{k})]^{\prime}},\end{array}

(8.9)

where
C
depends only on the Lipschitz character of
\Omega
and does not depend on
k
. But

\begin{array}[]{rl}\|\Delta u_{k}\|_{[H^{1/2}(\Omega_{k})]^{\prime}}&\leq\|u-u_{k}\|_{[H^{1/2}(\Omega_{k})]^{\prime}}+\|f_{k}\|_{[H^{1/2}(\Omega_{k})]^{\prime}}\\
\\
&\leq C(\|f\|_{[H^{1/2}(\Omega)]^{\prime}}+\|f_{k}\|_{[H^{1/2}(\Omega_{k})]^{\prime}})\leq C\|f\|_{[H^{1/2}(\Omega)]^{\prime}}.\end{array}

(8.10)

Setting now

\theta_{k}(\textit{{x}}^{\prime})=(1+|\nabla\xi_{k}(\textit{{x}}^{\prime})|^{2})^{1/2},\ \quad\ \theta(\textit{{x}}^{\prime})=(1+|\nabla\xi(\textit{{x}}^{\prime})|^{2})^{1/2}

we observe that for any
1\leq p<\infty

1\leq\theta_{k}\leq C,\quad\theta_{k}\rightarrow\theta\ \textrm{ in }L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1}).

Then we have

\begin{array}[]{rl}\!\!\!\!\|\widetilde{u_{k}}-u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{k})}&=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}|u(\textit{{x}}^{\prime},\xi_{k}(\textit{{x}}^{\prime}))|^{2}\theta_{k}(\textit{{x}}^{\prime})d\textit{{x}}^{\prime}\\
\\
&=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}|u(\textit{{x}}^{\prime},\xi_{k}(\textit{{x}}^{\prime}))-u(\textit{{x}}^{\prime},\xi(\textit{{x}}^{\prime})|^{2}\theta_{k}(\textit{{x}}^{\prime})d\textit{{x}}^{\prime}\\
\\
&\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}(\xi(\textit{{x}}^{\prime})-\xi_{k}(\textit{{x}}^{\prime}))\int_{\xi_{k}(\textit{{x}}^{\prime})}^{\xi(\textit{{x}}^{\prime})}\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{N}}(\textit{{x}}^{\prime},x_{N})\right|^{2}dx_{N}d\textit{{x}}^{\prime}\\
\\
&\leq C\|\xi-\xi_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})}\|u\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{k})}.\end{array}

(8.11)

Using (
8.7
), (
8.9
), (
8.10
) and (
8.11
), we deduce the strong convergence in
H^{1}(\Omega)
of
\widetilde{u_{k}}
to
u
.

Let us introduce

\psi_{k}(\textit{{x}}^{\prime})\ =\ \nabla u_{k}\cdot\textit{{n}}_{k}(\textit{{x}}^{\prime},\xi_{k}(\textit{{x}}^{\prime})).

From (
8.9
) and (
8.10
)
(\psi_{k})_{k}
is bounded in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
, so we can extract a subsequence, again denoted by
(\psi_{k})_{k}
such that

\psi_{k}\ \rightharpoonup\ \psi\ \ \textrm{ in }L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1}).

Setting now

\widetilde{\psi}(\textit{{x}}^{\prime},\xi(\textit{{x}}^{\prime}))=\psi(\textit{{x}}^{\prime})

and let
\varphi\in H^{1}(\Omega)
. Then as
\theta_{k}\ \rightarrow\ \theta
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
, we have

\begin{array}[]{rl}\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{k}}\frac{\partial u_{k}}{\partial\textit{{n}}_{k}}\varphi&=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}\psi_{k}(\textit{{x}}^{\prime})\varphi(\textit{{x}}^{\prime},\xi_{k}(\textit{{x}}^{\prime}))\theta_{k}(\textit{{x}}^{\prime})\,d\textit{{x}}^{\prime}\\
\\
&\displaystyle\longrightarrow\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}\psi(\textit{{x}}^{\prime})\varphi(\textit{{x}}^{\prime},\xi(\textit{{x}}^{\prime}))\theta(\textit{{x}}^{\prime})\,d\textit{{x}}^{\prime}\ =\ \int_{\Gamma}\widetilde{\psi}\varphi.\end{array}

Here, note that

\|\widetilde{\psi}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\leq C\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})}\leq C\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|\psi_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})}\leq C\|f\|_{[H^{1/2}(\Omega)]^{\prime}}.

Sending
k\rightarrow\infty
in (
8.6
) gives

\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi\ =\ -\langle f,\,\varphi\rangle_{[H^{1/2}(\Omega)]^{\prime}\times H^{1/2}(\Omega)}+\int_{\Gamma}\widetilde{\psi}\varphi

and thanks to (
8.5
), we get that
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\textit{{n}}}=\widetilde{\psi}
belongs to
L^{2}(\Gamma)
with the estimate (
8.1
).

\square

Theorem 8.2

For any
f\in[H^{1/2}(\Omega)]^{\prime}
satisfying the condition

\forall\varphi\in\mathscr{H}_{H^{1/2}(\Omega)},\quad\langle f,\,\varphi\rangle=0,

there exists a unique solution
u\in H^{3/2}_{00}(\Omega)
such that
\Delta u=f
in
\Omega
.

Proof.

Using Theorem
7.1
, we know the existence of solution
u\in H^{3/2}_{0}(\Omega)
. So it suffices to prove that
u\in H^{3/2}_{00}(\Omega)
. From Corollary
8.1
, we deduce that
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\textit{{n}}}\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
. We will show that
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\textit{{n}}}=0
. For that, let
\mu\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
and
\varphi\in\mathscr{H}_{H^{1/2}(\Omega)}
such that
\varphi=\mu
on
\Gamma
. Now since

\int_{\Gamma}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\textit{{n}}}\mu=\int_{\Gamma}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\textit{{n}}}\varphi=\langle\Delta u,\,\varphi\rangle-\langle u,\,\Delta\varphi\rangle=0,

which means that
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\textit{{n}}}=0
.

It remains to show that
\nabla u\in\textit{{H}}^{\,1/2}_{00}(\Omega)
. This property follows directly from the fact that the operator
\Delta
is an isomorphism from
H^{1/2}_{00}(\Omega)
into
\left[H^{3/2}_{0}(\Omega)\right]^{\prime}
. Indeed, let
\textit{{z}}\in\textit{{H}}^{\,1/2}_{00}(\Omega)
satisfying
\Delta\textit{{z}}=\nabla f
in
\Omega
. The harmonic vector field
\textit{{w}}=\nabla u-\textit{{z}}
belonging to
\textit{{H}}^{\,1/2}(\Omega)
verifies in addition the properties:
\sqrt{\rho}\nabla\textit{{w}}\in\textit{{L}}^{\,2}(\Omega)
. Its trace being equal to zero, we deduce that
\textit{{w}}={\bf 0}
.

\square

8.2
Solutions in
H^{s}(\Omega)
with
1/2\leq s\leq 3/2

The following theorem provides us with a criterion of uniqueness. This result improves the classical uniqueness result when
u\in H^{s}(\Omega)
, with
s>1/2\,\mathrm{(}
see Proposition 5.17 in
[
29
]
\mathrm{)}
.

Theorem 8.3

If
u\in H^{1/2}(\Omega)
is harmonic and
u=0
on
\Gamma
, then
u=0
in
\Omega
.

Proof.
We know that there exists
\psi\in H^{s+3/2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, for some
s>0
depending on
\Omega
, such htat
\Delta\psi=u
in
\Omega
. Using now Theorem
4.8
and the Green formula (
4.5
),
we have

\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}=\int_{\Omega}u\Delta\psi=\langle u\textit{{n}},\,\nabla\psi\rangle_{\Gamma}=0.

\square

We are now in position to state our first existence result in the case of a boundary data in
L^{2}(\Gamma)
. Using harmonic analysis techniques, many authors have established this result (see
[
27
]
and
[
29
]
). Our proof, completely different, is essentially based on the isomorphism given in Theorem
7.1
.

Theorem 8.4

i) For any
g\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
, Problem
(\mathscr{L}_{D}^{H})
has a unique solution
u\in H^{1/2}(\Omega)
. Moreover
\sqrt{\varrho}\,\nabla u\in{\textit{{L}}}^{2}(\Omega)
and we have the estimate

\|u\|_{H^{1/2}(\Omega)}+\|\sqrt{\varrho}\,\nabla u\|_{\textit{{L}}^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\ C\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}.

ii) This solution satisfies the following relation: for any
s\in\,[0,1/2[
and
\varphi\in H^{2-s}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, we have

\langle u,\,\Delta\varphi\rangle_{H^{s}(\Omega)\times H^{-s}(\Omega)}=\int_{\Gamma}g\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial\textit{{n}}}.

iii) Moreover
u
satisfies also the following property: for any positive integer
k

\varrho^{k+1/2}\nabla^{k+1}u\in\textit{{L}}^{2}(\Omega).

Proof.
We first observe that the above property in Point iii) and the estimate of
\sqrt{\varrho}\,\nabla u
in Point i) are a direct consequence of Theorem
3.8
. So it suffices to prove the existence and the uniqueness of the solution
u
satisfying
u\in H^{\,1/2}(\Omega)
.

i) Existence.
Let
g_{k}\in H^{1/2}(\Gamma)
be such that
g_{k}\longrightarrow g
in
L^{2}(\Gamma)
as
k\rightarrow\infty
and
u_{k}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
satisfying

\Delta u_{k}=0\quad\mathrm{in}\;\Omega\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad u_{k}=g_{k}\quad\mathrm{on}\;\Gamma.

We know that

\|u_{k}\|_{H^{1/2}(\Omega)}=\sup_{f\in[H^{1/2}(\Omega)]^{\prime},f\not=0}\frac{|\langle f,\,u_{k}\rangle_{[H^{1/2}(\Omega)]^{\prime}\times H^{1/2}(\Omega)}|}{\|f\|_{[H^{1/2}(\Omega)]^{\prime}}}.

But for any
f\in[H^{1/2}(\Omega)]^{\prime}
, using Theorem
7.1
, there exits a unique
v\in H^{3/2}_{0}(\Omega)
satisfying
\Delta v=f
in
\Omega
. Moreover thanks to Theorem
8.1
we have
\frac{\partial v}{\partial\textit{{n}}}\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
and

\|v\|_{H^{3/2}(\Omega)}+\|\frac{\partial v}{\partial\textit{{n}}}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\leq C\|f\|_{[H^{1/2}(\Omega)]^{\prime}}.

Now

\begin{array}[]{rl}\langle f,\,u_{k}\rangle_{[H^{1/2}(\Omega)]^{\prime}\times H^{1/2}(\Omega)}&=\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{k}\cdot\nabla v+\int_{\Gamma}g_{k}\frac{\partial v}{\partial\textit{{n}}}\\
\\
&=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}v\Delta u_{k}-\langle\frac{\partial u_{k}}{\partial\textit{{n}}},\,v\rangle_{H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)\times H^{1/2}(\Gamma)}+\int_{\Gamma}g_{k}\frac{\partial v}{\partial\textit{{n}}}\\
\\
&=\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}g_{k}\frac{\partial v}{\partial\textit{{n}}}.\end{array}

Hence

|\langle f,\,u_{k}\rangle|=|\int_{\Gamma}g_{k}\frac{\partial v}{\partial\textit{{n}}}|\leq\|g_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\|\frac{\partial v}{\partial\textit{{n}}}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\leq C\|g_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\|\|f\|_{[H^{1/2}(\Omega)]^{\prime}}.

This shows the estimate

\|u_{k}\|_{H^{1/2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|g_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}

(8.12)

and consequently
(u_{k})
is a Cauchy sequence in
H^{1/2}(\Omega)
which converges to some function
u\in H^{1/2}(\Omega)
which is harmonic since for any
k
the function
u_{k}
is harmonic. Clearly by Green formula we have for any
s\in\,[0,1/2[
and
\varphi\in H^{2-s}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,

\langle u_{k},\,\Delta\varphi\rangle_{H^{s}(\Omega)\times H^{-s}(\Omega)}=\int_{\Gamma}g_{k}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial\textit{{n}}}.

Passing to the limit above we get the following relation: for any
\varphi\in H^{2-s}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

\langle u,\,\Delta\varphi\rangle_{H^{s}(\Omega)\times H^{-s}(\Omega)}=\int_{\Gamma}g\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial\textit{{n}}}

with the estimate

\|u\|_{H^{1/2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}

thanks to (
8.12
). From Theorem
4.8
we deduce that for any
\varphi\in H^{2-s}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

\langle u\textit{{n}},\nabla\varphi\rangle=\int_{\Gamma}g\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial\textit{{n}}}.

That means that
u\textit{{n}}=g\textit{{n}}
in
[\boldsymbol{H}_{\textit{{N}}}^{1/2-s}(\Gamma)]^{\prime}
for any
s\in\,[0,1/2[
and then
u\textit{{n}}\in{\textit{{L}}^{2}_{\textit{{N}}}(\Gamma)}
. From Remark
7
Point iii), we get
u=g
on
\Gamma
.

ii) Uniqueness.
To prove the uniqueness we will show that the solution
u
above does not depend on the choice of the sequence
g_{k}
. Indeed let
g^{\prime}_{k}\in H^{1/2}(\Gamma)
be such that
g^{\prime}_{k}\longrightarrow g
in
L^{2}(\Gamma)
as
k\rightarrow\infty
and
u^{\prime}_{k}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
satisfying

\Delta u^{\prime}_{k}=0\quad\mathrm{in}\;\Omega\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad u^{\prime}_{k}=g^{\prime}_{k}\quad\mathrm{on}\;\Gamma.

Again the sequence
(u^{\prime}_{k})
converge to some harmonic function
u^{\prime}\in H^{1/2}(\Omega)
satisfying
u^{\prime}=g
on
\Gamma
. However

\|u_{k}-u^{\prime}_{k}\|_{H^{1/2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|g_{k}-g^{\prime}_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}

and then
u=u^{\prime}
by passing to the limit.

\square

The next result is a complement of Theorem
4.2
with a different proof.

Corollary 8.5

The kernel of the linear mapping
\gamma_{0}:u\mapsto u_{|\Gamma}
from
E(\nabla;\,\Omega)
into

is equal to
H^{1/2}_{00}(\Omega)
, where the space

is defined in (
4.1
)

Proof.
We have already seen that
H^{1/2}_{00}(\Omega)
is included in the kernel of
\gamma_{0}
. Conversely, let

. Since

, from Theorem

The following result for boundary data in
H^{1}(\Gamma)
is well known. Using Theorem

, we give here an other proof that we find in the litterature.

Theorem 8.6

For any
g\in H^{1}(\Gamma)
, the problem
(\mathscr{L}_{D}^{H})
has a unique solution
u\in H^{3/2}(\Omega)
. Moreover
\sqrt{\varrho}\,\nabla^{2}u\in{\textit{{L}}}^{2}(\Omega)
and we have the estimate

\|u\|_{H^{3/2}(\Omega)}+\|\sqrt{\varrho}\,\nabla^{2}u\|_{\textit{{L}}^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\ C\,\|g\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}.

The solution
u
satisfies also the following property: for any positive integer
k

\varrho^{k+1/2}\nabla^{k+2}u\in\textit{{L}}^{2}(\Omega).

Proof.
Let
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
the unique solution of Problem
(\mathscr{L}_{D}^{H})
. As above, by using Theorem
3.8
, it suffices to prove that
u\in H^{3/2}(\Omega)
.

Recall that from Ne
\mathrm{\check{c}}
as Property such a solution satisfies

\nabla u_{|\Gamma}\in\textit{{L}}^{2}(\Gamma),\quad\mathrm{with}\quad\|\nabla u\|_{\textit{{L}}^{2}(\Gamma)}\leq C\,\|g\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}.

(8.13)

We then reason as in the proof of Theorem
8.1
(see Step 1), without assuming that
\Omega
is a Lipschitz hypograph.
For
\textit{{x}}=(\textit{{x}}^{\prime},x_{N})\in\Omega
, we set

g_{k}(\textit{{x}})=\chi(x_{N})u(\textit{{x}}^{\prime},\xi_{k}(\textit{{x}}^{\prime})).

Since
u\in H^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)

u_{k}\in H^{2}(\Omega_{k})
satisfying
-\Delta u_{k}=0
in
\Omega_{k}
and
u_{k}=g_{k}
on
\Gamma_{k}
. Setting now

\widetilde{u_{k}}=\begin{cases}u_{k}\quad\;\mathrm{in}\;\;\Omega_{k}\\
g_{k}\quad\;\;\textrm{in}\;\;\Omega\setminus\Omega_{k}.\end{cases}

Clearly,
(\widetilde{u_{k}})_{k}
is bounded in
H^{1}(\Omega)
and
\widetilde{u_{k}}\rightharpoonup u
in
H^{1}(\Omega)

(\nabla\widetilde{u_{k}})_{k}
is bounded in
\textit{{H}}^{\,1/2}(\Omega)

and then

.

Let
\textit{{F}}\in\boldsymbol{\mathscr{D}}(\Omega_{k})
and
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in\textit{{H}}^{\,3/2}_{0}(\Omega_{k})
be such that

\Delta\boldsymbol{\varphi}=\textit{{F}}\quad\mathrm{in}\;\Omega_{k}\quad\mathrm{with}\quad\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\textit{{H}}^{\,3/2}(\Omega_{k})}\leq C\textit{{F}}\,_{[\textit{{H}}^{\,1/2}(\Omega_{k})]^{\prime}},

where

\Omega

\int_{\Omega_{k}}\nabla u_{k}\cdot\Delta\boldsymbol{\varphi}=\int_{\Gamma_{k}}\nabla u_{k}\cdot\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\varphi}}{\partial\textit{{n}}_{k}}.

Hence, using Theorem
8.1
we obtain

\displaystyle|\int_{\Omega_{k}}\nabla u_{k}\cdot\textit{{F}}\,|\leq\|\nabla u_{k}\|_{\textit{{L}}^{2}(\Gamma_{k})}\|\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\varphi}}{\partial\textit{{n}}_{k}}\|_{\textit{{L}}^{2}(\Gamma_{k})}\leq C\|\nabla u_{k}\|_{\textit{{L}}^{2}(\Gamma_{k})}\|\textit{{F}}\,\|_{[\textit{{H}}^{\,1/2}(\Omega_{k})]^{\prime}}.

But, from Ne
\mathrm{\check{c}}
as Property (
8.3
)

\|\nabla u_{k}\|_{\textit{{L}}^{2}(\Gamma_{k})}\leq C\|g_{k}\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{k})}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{\textit{{L}}^{2}(\Gamma)}\leq C\|g\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}

As
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{D}}(\Omega_{k})
is dense in
[\textit{{H}}^{\,1/2}(\Omega_{k})]^{\prime}
, we deduce the following inequality

\|\nabla u_{k}\|_{\textit{{H}}^{\,1/2}(\Omega_{k})}\leq C\|g\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}.

(8.14)

Observe now that

\|\nabla\widetilde{u_{k}}\|_{\textit{{H}}^{\,1/2}(\Omega)}^{2}=\|\nabla\widetilde{u_{k}}\|_{\textit{{L}}^{\,2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla\widetilde{u_{k}}(\textit{{x}})-\nabla\widetilde{u_{k}}(\textit{{y}})|^{2}}{|\textit{{x}}-\textit{{y}}|^{N+1}}dx\,dy.

In order to estimate the last double integral, it suffices thanks to (
8.14
) to estimate the following:

\int_{\Omega_{k}}\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{k}}\frac{|\nabla u_{k}(\textit{{x}})-\nabla{g_{k}}(\textit{{y}})|^{2}}{|\textit{{x}}-\textit{{y}}|^{N+1}}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{k}}\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{k}}\frac{|\nabla g_{k}(\textit{{x}})-\nabla g_{k}(\textit{{y}})|^{2}}{|\textit{{x}}-\textit{{y}}|^{N+1}}.

But when
\textit{{y}}\in\Omega\setminus\Omega_{k}
, we have
{g_{k}}(\textit{{y}})=u_{k}(P_{k}(\textit{{y}}))
, where
P_{k}(\textit{{y}})=(\textit{{y}}^{\prime},\xi_{k}(\textit{{y}}^{\prime}))
. As
|\textit{{x}}-\textit{{y}}|\geq|\textit{{x}}-P_{k}(\textit{{y}})|
, for
\textit{{x}}\in\Omega_{k}
, and since

\Omega_{k}=\{x\in\Omega;\;\varrho^{*}(x,\Gamma)>\frac{1}{k}\},

where
\varrho^{*}
is the regularized (signed or not signed) distance to
\Gamma
, which satisfies :

\forall x\in\Omega,\quad C_{1}\varrho(x,\Gamma)\leq\varrho^{*}(x,\Gamma)\leq C_{2}\varrho(x,\Gamma),

we can verify that

\int_{\Omega_{k}}\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{k}}\frac{|\nabla u_{k}(\textit{{x}})-\nabla{g_{k}}(\textit{{y}})|^{2}}{|\textit{{x}}-\textit{{y}}|^{N+1}}\leq\frac{C}{k}\int_{\Omega_{k}}\int_{\Omega_{k}}\frac{|\nabla u_{k}(\textit{{x}})-\nabla{u_{k}}(\textit{{y}})|^{2}}{|\textit{{x}}-\textit{{y}}|^{N+1}}.

For the second double integral, we observe that when
(\textit{{x}},\textit{{y}})\in(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{k})\times(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{k})
, we have
|\textit{{x}}-\textit{{y}}|\geq C|P_{k}(\textit{{x}})-P_{k}(\textit{{y}})|
since
\xi_{k}
is Lipschitzian, with the Lipschitz constant not depending on
k
. So we deduce that

\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{k}}\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{k}}\frac{|\nabla g_{k}(\textit{{x}})-\nabla g_{k}(\textit{{y}})|^{2}}{|\textit{{x}}-\textit{{y}}|^{N+1}}\leq\frac{C}{k^{2}}\int_{\Omega_{k}}\int_{\Omega_{k}}\frac{|\nabla u_{k}(\textit{{x}})-\nabla{u_{k}}(\textit{{y}})|^{2}}{|\textit{{x}}-\textit{{y}}|^{N+1}}

and we conclude that
(\|\nabla\widetilde{u_{k}}\|_{\textit{{H}}^{\,1/2}(\Omega)})_{k}
is bounded. Finally,

\widetilde{u_{k}}\rightharpoonup u\quad\mathrm{in}\;H^{3/2}(\Omega).

\square

To find solutions in
H^{s}(\Omega)
with
1/2<s<3/2

, Theorem

and an interpolation argument, which leads us to the following result:

be a Lipschitz bounded open subset of

and let

. Then for any

, the problem

Using Theorem

, the following theorem concerning the inhomogeneous Problem

for data

.

Theorem 8.8

Let
\Omega
be a Lipschitz bounded open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and

1/2<s<3/2
. Then if

, the problem
(\mathscr{L}_{D}^{0})
has a unique solution
u\in H^{s}(\Omega)
with the following estimate

\|u\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}\ \leq\ C\,\|f\|_{H^{s-2}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

We extend the data
f
by zero outside

and denote by

. We can see that
\widetilde{f}\in H^{s-2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and the solution

\square

8.3
Nečas Property, second version

We will now improve Theorem

Corollary 8.9

Let

u\in H^{1}(\Omega)\quad\mathrm{with}\quad\Delta u\in\ [H^{1/2}(\Omega)]^{\prime}.

i) If

, then

, then

and we have the following estimate

iii) If

or

.

iv) We have the following Green formula: for any

with
\Delta u\in\ [H^{1/2}(\Omega)]^{\prime}
and
\varphi\in E(\nabla;\Omega)
,

\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi+\langle\Delta u,\,\varphi\rangle_{[H^{1/2}(\Omega)]^{\prime}\times H^{1/2}(\Omega)}=\int_{\Gamma}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\textit{{n}}}\,\varphi.

Proof.
i) Let
u_{0}\in H^{3/2}(\Omega)
the solution given by Theorem
8.6
and satisfying

Using (

), we know that

. Since the function

by using Theorem

.

ii) Let
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
the solution satisfying

The function

belongs to

and
\Delta v\in[H^{1/2}(\Omega)]^{\prime}
. Theorem

implies that

and then

satisfies the same property. We conclude by using again (

).

\square

9
Uniqueness criteria in
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)

Consider the following boundary value problem for the Laplacian:

(\mathscr{L}_{D})\ \ \ \ -\Delta u=f\quad\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega\quad\mbox{and }\quad u=g\ \ \mbox{on }\Gamma.

We recall that if
\Omega
is of class
\mathscr{C}^{1}
and
1<p<\infty
, then for any

f\in W^{-1,\,p}(\Omega),\quad\mathrm{and}\quad g\in W^{1-1/p,\,p}(\Gamma),

Problem
(\mathscr{L}_{D})
has a unique solution
u\in W^{1,\,p}(\Omega).

In the 80’s, Nečas posed the question of solving the problem
(\mathscr{L}_{D})
with the homogeneous boundary condition
g=0
on Lipschitz domains, when the RHS
f\in W^{-1,\,p}(\Omega)
. The answer to this question is partially given in the paper of Jerison and Kenig
[
29
]
:

Negative results.
If
N\geq 3
, then for any
p>3
(resp.
p>4
if
N=2
), there is a Lipschitz domain
\Omega
and
f\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
such that the solution
u
of Problem
(\mathscr{L}_{D})
with the homogeneous boundary condition
g=0
does not belong to
W^{1,\,p}(\Omega)
.

Positive results.
There exist
q>3
when
N\geq 3
(resp.
q>4
when
N=2
) such that if
q^{\prime}<p<q
, then the problem
(\mathscr{L}_{D})
has a unique solution
u\in W^{1,\,p}(\Omega)
satisfying the estimate

\|u\|_{W^{1,\,p}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{W^{-1,\,p}(\Omega)}.

Moreover, if
\Omega
is
\mathscr{C}^{1}
, we can take
q=\infty
as stated above.

In our opinion, the results above for
N\geq 3
, concerning the solvability in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
, are available only for
N=3
.
This leads us to make some additional properties and results.

Let us introduce the following kernel space: for any
1<p<\infty
,

\mathscr{H}_{W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)}=\{\varphi\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega);\;\Delta\varphi=0\;\mathrm{in}\;\Omega\}.

Theorem 9.1

i) Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
N\geq 2
.
Then

\mathscr{H}_{W^{1,2N/(N+1)}_{0}(\Omega)}=\{0\}

ii) For any
p<2N/(N+1)
, there exist a bounded Lipschitz domain
\Omega
such that

\mathscr{H}_{W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)}\neq\{0\}

iii) For any polygonal domain
\Omega
(resp. polyedral domain
\Omega
), there exists
p_{0}(\Omega)<4/3
(resp.
p_{0}(\Omega)<3/2
) such that

\mathscr{H}_{W^{1,p_{0}}_{0}(\Omega)}=\{0\}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\mathscr{H}_{W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)}\neq\{0\}\,\;\mathrm{if}\;\;p<p_{0}(\Omega)

(9.1)

Proof.
i) The first identity above is a direct consequence of Theorem
8.3
and the following embedding

W^{1,\,2N/(N+1)}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow H^{1/2}(\Omega)\quad\mathrm{for\,any}\;\;N\geq 2.

ii) It suffices to consider the 2D case, the proof being similar for the general case. So let
p<4/3.
Then
1/2<(2/p)-1
and there exists
\alpha
such that
1/2<\alpha<(2/p)-1
. Let us now consider the following domain:

\Omega=\{(r,\theta);\;0<r<1,\quad 0<\theta<\frac{\pi}{\alpha}\}.

(9.2)

We can easily verify that the following function

z(r,\theta)=(r^{-\alpha}-r^{\alpha})\mathrm{sin}(\alpha\theta)

(9.3)

is harmonic in
\Omega
with
z=0
on
\Gamma
and
z\in W^{1,q}(\Omega)
for any

. So we get the required result since
p<\frac{2}{\alpha+1}<\frac{4}{3}
.

iii) Observe that when
\Omega
is of class
\mathscr{C}^{1}
or convex, then for any
q>1
, we have

\mathscr{H}_{W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega)}=\{0\}.

To simplify we consider the 2D case. We can also suppose that
\Omega
is a non convex polygonal domain with
\omega^{\star}
the largest angle with the origin as its vertex. Then we can construct a harmonic function
v
in
\Omega
, equal to
0
on
\Gamma
, equal in a neighborhood of the origin to the function
u
given by the relation (
9.3
), with

and such that

for any
p<\frac{2}{\alpha^{\star}+1}=:p_{0}(\Omega)
. We then deduce (
9.1
).

\square

h\in H^{-s}(\Gamma)
satisfies
\langle h,1\rangle=0
. As above, (
A.3
) admits a unique solution, up to an additive constant. Moreover (
A.3
) is equivalent to the following Neumann problem:

(\mathscr{L}^{\prime}_{N})\ \ \ \ \Delta v=\mathrm{div}\,\textit{{F}}\quad\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega\quad\mbox{and }\quad(\nabla v-\textit{{F}})\cdot\textit{{n}}=h\ \ \mbox{on }\Gamma,

Note that since
F
and
\nabla v
are not sufficiently regular,
\textit{{F}}\cdot\textit{{n}}
and
\nabla v\cdot\textit{{n}}
have no sense contrarily to the difference
(\nabla v-\textit{{F}})\cdot\textit{{n}}
.

And we can easily prove that for any extension
f
of
\textrm{div}\,\textit{{F}}\in H^{-s-1/2}(\Omega)
, we have the following relation:

\nabla v\cdot\textit{{n}}_{f}=h+\textit{{F}}\cdot\textit{{n}}_{f}\ \ \mbox{on }\Gamma.

Beside, for any
f\in[H^{s+1/2}(\Omega)]^{\prime}
there exist
\textit{{F}}\in\textit{{H}}\,^{-s+1/2}(\Omega)
and
g\in H^{-s}(\Gamma)
such that

\forall\varphi\in H^{s+1/2}(\Omega),\quad\langle f,\varphi\rangle_{\Omega}=\langle\textit{{F}},\nabla\varphi\rangle_{\Omega}+\langle g,\varphi\rangle_{\Gamma}

To prove the above property, we can use the following theorem.

Theorem A.1.

Let
f\in\left[H^{s+1/2}(\Omega)\right]^{\prime}
. Then, there exists a unique
u\in H^{-s+3/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
satisfying

u-\Delta u=0\quad in\;\Omega^{\prime}\quad with\quad\Omega^{\prime}=\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega}

and a unique
h\in H^{-s}(\Gamma)
such that

\displaystyle\forall\varphi\in H^{s+1/2}(\Omega),\quad\left\langle f,\varphi\right\rangle_{\Omega}=\int_{\Omega}u\varphi+\langle\nabla u,\nabla\varphi\rangle_{\Omega}+\left\langle h,\varphi\right\rangle_{\Gamma}.

In particular

f_{|_{\Omega}}=u-\Delta u\quad\mbox{in}\;\Omega

and

\displaystyle\left\langle h,\varphi\right\rangle_{\Gamma}=-\left\langle\frac{\partial u}{\partial\textit{{n}}},\varphi\right\rangle_{\partial\Omega^{\prime}}.

The proof of this theorem will be given in a forthcoming paper.

Return now to the study of the Neumann problem
(\mathscr{L}_{N})
. The previous observations show that the two approaches, (
A.2
) and (
A.3
) are equivalent. However the second one seems more convenient concerning the definition and the sense of the normal component of non regular vector fields. Another reason for the interest of this choice is given in the next theorem.

Theorem A.2.

For any

\textit{{F}}\in\textit{{H}}\,^{1/2}(\Omega)\quad with\quad div\,\textit{{F}}\in[H^{1/2}(\Omega)]^{\prime}\quad and\quad\textit{{F}}\cdot\textit{{n}}\in L^{2}(\Gamma)

and for any
h\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
satisfying the compatibility condition
\langle h,1\rangle_{\Gamma}=0
, there exists a unique
u\in H^{3/2}(\Omega)
, up an additive constant, such that

\Delta u=\mathrm{div}\,\textit{{F}}\quad in\;\Omega\quad and\quad\nabla u\cdot\textit{{n}}=\textit{{F}}\cdot\textit{{n}}+h\quad on\;\Gamma.

Proof.
Clearly we have the existence of solution
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and also in
H^{3/2-s}(\Omega)
for any
s<1
. The regularity
H^{3/2}(\Omega)
is an immediate consequence of Corollary
8.9
.

\square

A.3
Biharmonic Problem

As mentioned above, some ideas and arguments used for Laplace equation may be appropriate for the investigation of the Biharmonic problem, with different boundary conditions.

1. Biharmonic problem with Dirichlet boundary conditions.
Let us first consider the case of Dirichlet boundary conditions:

(\mathscr{B}_{D})\quad\begin{cases}\Delta^{2}u\ =\ f\quad\textrm{ in }\ \Omega,\\
u\ =\ g_{0}\quad\textrm{ on }\ \Gamma,\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\textit{{n}}}\ =\ g_{1}\quad\textrm{ on }\ \Gamma.\end{cases}

For
\Omega
of class
\mathscr{C}^{0,1}
, it is proved in
[
14
]
that for
f=0
and any pair

(g_{0},g_{1})\in H^{1}(\Gamma)\times L^{2}(\Gamma),

there exists a unique solution
u\in H^{3/2}(\Omega)
to Problem
(\mathscr{B}_{D})
satisfying
\sqrt{\varrho}\,\nabla^{2}u\in\textit{{L}}^{2}(\Omega)
.

Problem 5.
It would be interesting to see if, as for Laplace equation, one could obtain the
H^{5/2}
-regularity and for which choice of data one can have this result.

Concerning the problem
(\mathscr{B}_{D})
with
g_{0}=g_{1}=0
, Adolfsson and Pipher
[
2
]
have established the existence of a solution in
H^{2+s}(\Omega)
if
f\in H^{s}(\Omega)
and
-1/2<s<1/2
. They also showed in the same paper a similar result when
f=0
and
(g_{0},g_{1})
belong to some Whitney array spaces denoted by
WA^{2}_{3/2+s}(\Gamma)
following the characterization:

g_{0}\in H^{1}(\Gamma),\;g_{1}\in H^{1/2+s}(\Gamma)\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\nabla_{\tau}g_{0}+g_{1}\textit{{n}}\in\textit{{H}}\,^{1/2+s}(\Gamma).

Problem 6.
So, is it possible to obtain the maximal regularity
H^{5/2}(\Omega)
, for appropriate data and corresponding to the case
s=1/2
?

2. Biharmonic problem with Navier boundary conditions.
The case of Navier boundary conditions

(\mathscr{B}_{Na})\quad\begin{cases}\Delta^{2}u\ =\ f\quad\textrm{ in }\ \Omega,\\
u\ =\ g_{0}\quad\textrm{ on }\ \Gamma,\\
\Delta u=\ g_{1}\quad\textrm{ on }\ \Gamma,\end{cases}

is completely open and particularly interesting.

Problem 7.
Which assumptions on
f,g_{0}
and
g_{1}
are appropriate to get solution in
H^{s}(\Omega
? And for which corresponding values of
s
?

3. Biharmonic problem with "Neumann" boundary conditions.
For a bounded Lipschitz domain
\Omega
, with connected boundary, Verchota investigated in
[
44
]
the following Neumann problem

(\mathscr{B}_{Ne})\quad\begin{cases}\Delta^{2}v\ =\ f\quad\textrm{ in }\ \Omega,\\
\\
\nu\Delta v+(1-\nu)\frac{\partial^{2}v}{\partial\textit{{n}}^{2}}\ =\ \Lambda_{0}\quad\textrm{ on }\ \Gamma,\\
\\
\frac{\partial\Delta v}{\partial\textit{{n}}}+\frac{1-\nu}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\boldsymbol{\tau}_{ij}}(\frac{\partial^{2}v}{\partial\textit{{n}}\,\partial\boldsymbol{\tau}_{ij}})\ =\ \Lambda_{1}\quad\textrm{ on }\ \Gamma,\end{cases}

where
\nu
is a constant known as the Poisson ratio, whose value corresponds to a particular physical situation. Here
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{ij}=n_{i}\textit{{e}}_{j}-n_{j}\textit{{e}}_{i}
is an orthogonal vector field to the outward normal
n
. He showed that if
-1/(N-1)\leq\nu<1
,
2-\varepsilon<p<2+\varepsilon
for some
\varepsilon>0
and

f=0,\quad\Lambda_{0}\in L^{p}(\Gamma)\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\Lambda_{1}\in W^{-1,p}(\Gamma),

then there exist solutions to the Neumann problem satisfying in addition the estimate

\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}\leq C(\|\Lambda_{0}u\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}+\|\Lambda_{1}u\|_{W^{-1,p}(\Gamma)}).

However, it is not specified in which Sobolev space belong the solutions.

Problem 8.
It would therefore be interesting on one hand to give more details on the solutions and, on the other hand, to study the properties of the Steklov-Poincaré operator corresponding to the homogeneous biharmonic problem.

Bilinear secants and birational geometry of blowups of ℙ n × ℙ n + 1 \mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n+1}
Elisa Postinghel, Artie Prendergast-Smith

Abstract
We introduce bilinear secant varieties and joins of subvarieties of products of projective spaces, as a generalisation of the classical secant varieties and joins of projective varieties. We show that the bilinear secant varieties of certain rational normal curves of ℙ n × ℙ n + 1 \mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n+1} play a central role in the study of the birational geometry of X s n , n + 1 X^{n,n+1}_{s} , its blow-up in s s points in general position. We show that X s n , n + 1 X^{n,n+1}_{s} is log Fano, and we compute its effective and movable cones, for s ≤ n + 2 s\leq n+2 and n ≥ 1 n\geq 1 and for s ≤ n + 3 s\leq n+3 and n ≤ 2 n\leq 2 , and we compute the effective and movable cones of X 6 3 , 4 X^{3,4}_{6} .



Introduction

This paper studies the birational geometry of certain varieties obtained as blowups of products of projective spaces in general sets of points. A basic question is to determine which such varieties have good finiteness properties such as being log Fano or, more generally, a Mori dream space. Complete answers are known in several case: for blowups of a single projective space by Mukai
[
17
,
18
]
, Castravet–Tevelev
[
7
]
, and Araujo–Masssarenti
[
1
]
, and for products of projective spaces of equal dimension by Mukai
[
18
]
and Lesieutre-Park
[
16
]
. (See Section 1 below for precise statements of these results.)

For products of projective spaces of unequal dimensions, much less is known. Let
X^{n,m}_{s}
denote the blowup of
\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{m}
in
s
general points. The authors and Grange
[
12
]
proved the log Fano property for
X^{1,2}_{s}
and
X^{1,3}_{s}
with
s\leq 6
. Bolognesi–Massarenti–Poma
[
3
]
did the same for
X^{1,n}_{s}
with
s\leq n+1
, and also gave a presentation of the Cox ring in these cases.

In this paper, we consider the case of
X^{n,n+1}_{s}
. If
s\leq n+1
, then
X^{n,n+1}_{s}
is toric, hence log Fano and a Mori dream space. If
n\geq 5
and
s\geq n+4
, then
X^{n,n+1}_{s}
is not a Mori dream space, nor log Fano, because its effective cone is not finitely generated; see Section
1
.
Therefore, besides a few cases in small dimensions (see Question
8.4
), the only open cases for
n\geq 5
are
s=n+2
and
s=n+3
, which we consider in this paper.

Our first main result Theorem
5.4
shows that
X^{n,n+1}_{s}
is log Fano for
s\leq n+2
. An explicit log Fano pair is obtained with an effective divisor given as the union of two irreducible divisors, one pulled back from each factor. Along the way, in Theorems
5.1
and
5.2
we obtain a description of the effective and movable cones of divisors of
X^{n,n+1}_{n+2}
, by means of a technique called the

developed in previous work with Grange
[
12
]
. Analogously to the case of one factor
\mathbb{P}^{n}
blown-up at
n+2

We then consider the case
X^{n,n+1}_{n+3}
: here there is a new ingredient. First, there is a unique rational curve, the
distinguished rational curve
, of bidegree
(n,n+1)
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n+1}
passing through
n+3
general points in general position; see Corollary
2.3

n
passing through
n+3
points in general position in
\mathbb{P}^{n}

Continuing with the analogy, just as the secant varieties of the rational normal curve of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
play a central role in the birational geometry of the blow-up of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
at
n+3
points in general position — for instance, when they have codimension 1, their proper transforms span extremal rays of the effective cone

18

7
,
5

— so too do the
bilinear secant varieties
of the distinguished rational curve of
\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n+1}

Bilinear secant varieties and joins of subvarieties of products of projective spaces are introduced in Section
3

\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n+1}
are described in Proposition

4.4

4.6

4.7

The case of the threefold
X^{1,2}_{4}

[
12

The next case is
X^{3,4}_{6}

\mathbb{P}^{n}
that works for
s=n+2

1
Preliminaries and background

We work throughout over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. We use standard definitions from birational geometry, to be found for example in
[
15
]
. In particular we recall that a normal projective variety
X
is
log Fano
or
Fano type
if there is an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
\Delta
such that
(X,\Delta)
is klt and
-(K_{X}+\Delta)
is ample;
X
is called a
Mori dream space
if its Cox ring is a finitely generated algebra over the base field. Hu–Keel
[
14
]
showed that Mori dream spaces enjoy the “best possible” properties from the point of view of minimal model theory: in particular, their effective and movable cones are rational polyhedral. Birkar–Cascini–Hacon–McKernan
[
2
]
showed that every log Fano variety is a Mori dream space.

We will be interested in when certain blowups of products of projective spaces have these properties. A complete classification of blow-ups in points of products of copies of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
that are Mori dream spaces is known. In particular, it turns out that being log Fano and being a Mori dream space are equivalent properties, as proved by Mukai
[
18
]
, Castravet–Tevelev
[
7
]
, Araujo–Massarenti
[
1
]
and Lesieutre-Park
[
16
]
. An important feature of these blow-ups is that a certain Weyl group acts on the space of divisors, preserving the effective and movable cones. By contrast, for products of factors with different dimensions, as far as we know there is no such Weyl group action, making the question of determining those that are Mori dream spaces or log Fano, and of computing their effective cones, more challenging.

We will denote by
X^{n,m}_{s}
the blow-up of
\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{m}
in
s
points in general position.
Let us summarise what is known about whether or not the space
X^{n,m}_{s}
has the log Fano or Mori dream space properties:

•

If
n\leq m
and
s\leq n+1
then
X^{n,m}_{s}
is toric, hence log Fano.

•

If
n\geq 5
then
X^{n,0}_{s}
is log Fano if and only if it is a Mori dream space if and only if
s\leq n+3
, by Mukai
[
18
]
, Castravet–Tevelev
[
7
]
, and Araujo–Massarenti
[
1
]
. More generally, Mukai
[
18
]
and Lesieutre-Park
[
16
]
characterised those blowups of products of the form
(\mathbb{P}^{n})^{m}
which are Mori dream spaces or log Fano.

•

If
n\geq 2
and
X^{n,0}_{s}
is not a Mori dream space, then neither is
X^{n,m}_{s}

[
12
, Corollary 6.9]
. In particular, by the previous point, if
n\geq 5
and
s>n+3
then
X^{n,m}_{s}
is not a Mori dream space for any
m
.

Notation

.

We use the following notation for the varieties we work on, and numerical classes of divisors and curves on them:

•

: the blowup of the product
\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{m}
in a set of
s
general points
\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{s}\}
.

•

\pi_{1}\colon X^{n,m}_{s}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n}
and
\pi_{2}\colon X^{n,m}_{s}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{m}
: the natural projections.

•

H_{1}
and

: the pullback to

\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
respectively.

•

and

and

\displaystyle H_{i}\cdot l_{j}=\delta_{ij};

\displaystyle\quad H_{i}\cdot e_{j}=0;

\displaystyle E_{i}\cdot l_{j}=0;

\displaystyle\quad E_{i}\cdot e_{j}=-\delta_{ij}.

•

1.2
The nef cone of
X^{n,m}_{s}

We will start by showing that, in the cases we will consider, the nef cone of
X^{n,m}_{s}
is easy to compute. The proof is based on the following lemma, whose statement and proof are modified and simplified versions of those of
[
9
, Lemma 2.6]
.

Lemma 1.3
.

Define the following vectors in
\mathbb{Z}^{s+2}
:

\displaystyle l_{1}

\displaystyle=\left(1,0,0,\ldots,0\right);

\displaystyle l_{2}

\displaystyle=\left(0,1,0,\ldots,0\right);

\displaystyle e_{i}

\displaystyle=\left(0,0,0,\ldots,0,1,0,\ldots 0\right)

where for
e_{i}
we have
1\leq i\leq s
and the nonzero entry occurs in place
i+2
.

Suppose that
v=(a_{1},a_{2},-b_{1},\ldots,-b_{s})\in\mathbb{Z}^{s+2}
is an integer vector satisfying

(a)

a_{i},\,b_{j}\geq 0
for all
i
and
j
;

(b)

a_{1}+a_{2}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{s}b_{i}
.

Then
v
is a positive linear combination of the vectors
e_{i}
and
l_{j}-e_{i}
.

Proof.

The proof is by induction on
a=a_{1}+a_{2}
. If
a=1
, then
(a)
and
(b)
imply that
v=l_{j}-\sum_{I}e_{i}
for a set
I
of cardinality at most 1, hence is of the form claimed.

Now suppose that
a>1
and
v
satisfies the given inequalities. By swapping
l_{1}
and
l_{2}
and the
e_{i}
we can assume without loss of generality that
a_{1}\geq a_{2}
and
b_{1}\geq\cdots\geq b_{s}
. If
b_{1}=0
then
v=a_{1}l_{1}+a_{2}l_{2}
which is of the form claimed, so assume that
b_{1}>0
. Let
v^{\prime}=v-(l_{1}-e_{1})=(a_{1}-1,a_{2},-b_{1}+1,-b_{2},\ldots,-b_{k})=(a_{1}^{\prime},a_{2},-b_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,-b_{s})
; by our assumption on
b_{1}
this satisfies
(a)
; moreover
(a_{1}-1)+a_{2}\geq(b_{1}-1)+b_{2}+\cdots b_{s}
, so the new vector
v\prime
satisfies
(b)
also. By induction
v^{\prime}
is of the form claimed, hence so is
v=v^{\prime}+(l_{1}-e_{1})
.
∎

The lemma implies that the cone of curves
\operatorname{Curv}(X^{n,m}_{s})
is generated by the
e_{i}
and
l_{j}-e_{i}
if and only if the class
H_{1}+H_{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{s}E_{i}
is nef. We now show that condition holds in cases of interest for us.

Proposition 1.4
.

Let
n,m\geq 2
and
s\leq n+m
. Then the class
D=H_{1}+H_{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{s}E_{i}
is nef on
X^{n,m}_{s}
. Therefore the cone of curves
\operatorname{Curv}({X^{n,m}_{s}})
is spanned by the classes
e_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq k
and the classes
l_{j}-e_{i}
for
j=1,2
and
1\leq i\leq s
.

Proof.

The second statement follows from the first by using Lemma
1.3
. Indeed, if
C
is an irreducible curve on
X^{n,m}_{s}
, then either
C
is contained in one of the
E_{i}
or it is a strict transform of an irreducible curve on
\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{m}
. In the first case, the class of
C
is a positive multiple of
e_{i}
. In the second case, assuming that
D
is nef we have
D\cdot C\geq 0
, so the class of
C
must satisfy the assumptions of Lemma
1.3
, and therefore this class is a positive combination of the
e_{i}
and the
l_{j}-e_{i}
.

It remains to prove that the class
D
is nef. We use induction on
n+m
. The base case is
n=m=2
, in which case our variety
X^{2,2}_{s}
is the blowup of
\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}
in at most 4 points. For 3 or fewer points, the variety is toric, hence its cone of curves is generated by classes of the form
e_{i}
and
l_{j}-e_{i}
. Since
D
is nonnegative on all these classes, it is nef in this case. For
s=4
, consider the decomposition

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle=\left(H_{1}-E_{1}-E_{2}\right)+\left(H_{2}-E_{3}-E_{4}\right).

Each summand is represented by an effective divisor isomorphic to
X^{1,2}_{2}
, the blowup of
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}
in 2 points, and any negative curve for
D
must be contained in one of these two divisors. But the variety
X^{1,2}_{2}
is toric, so as above
D
is nonnegative on any curve contained in one of these two divisors. Hence
D
is nef in this case.

For the inductive step, now consider the variety
X^{n,m}_{s}
with
n+m>4
, and the class
D
on it. Assume without loss of generality that
n\leq m
. We can decompose our class as

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle=\left(H_{1}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}E_{i}\right)+\left(H_{2}-\sum_{i=n+1}^{s}E_{i}\right).

By our hypothesis on
s
, both summands are represented by effective divisors, namely strict transforms of pullbacks of hyperplanes on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
and
\mathbb{P}^{m}
. Call these divisors
D_{n}
and
D_{m}
: again, any irreducible curve on which our class is negative must be contained in either
D_{n}
or
D_{m}
. Note that
D_{n}
is isomorphic to
X^{n-1,m}_{n}
, which is toric, so as above
D
is nonnegative on all curves in
D_{n}
. Next consider the divisor
D_{m}
, which is isomorphic to
X^{n,m-1}_{s-n}
. By our inductive hypothesis, we can assume that the cone of curves of
D_{m}
is also generated by classes of the form
e_{i}
and
l_{j}-e_{i}
. Again we know that
D
is nonnegative on all such classes, so we are done.
∎

2
The distinguished rational curve

In this section, we prove the existence of a “distinguished” rational curve of degree
n
through
n+3
points in
\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n+1}
. This can be thought of as the analogue in this setting of the unique rational normal curve through
n+3
points in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
. We will see later that this curve and its “bilinear secant varieties” play a key role in the birational geometry of
X^{n,n+1}_{n+3}
, similarly to how the rational normal curve of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
and its secant varieties play a role in the birational geometry of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
blown up at
n+3
points. For later use, we also record some related results on existence of rational curves of specified types.

Proposition 2.1
.

Given
s+2
general points in
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{s}
, there is a unique irreducible curve
C
of bidegree
(1,s)
passing through all the points. This curve is smooth and rational.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma
be a curve of bidegree
(1,s)
in
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{s}
passing through a set of
s+2
general points. We have
\Gamma\cdot F=1
for any fibre
F
of the projection
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{s}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
, so
\Gamma
must be smooth and the projection
p_{1}\colon\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
must be birational, hence an isomorphism. Note that if
p_{2}\colon\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{s}
is the map to the second factor, then
\Gamma
is precisely the graph of the morphism
\varphi=p_{2}\circ p_{1}^{-1}\colon\mathbb{P}^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{s}
, whose image is a rational normal curve in
\mathbb{P}^{s}
. The morphism
\varphi
is therefore of the form
\varphi=\left[f_{1},\ldots,f_{s+1}\right]
where the
f_{i}
are homogeneous forms of degree
s
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

To prove that a unique such
\Gamma
exists, we can assume without loss of generality that the
s+2
points are of the form

\displaystyle\left(p_{1},[1,0,\ldots,0]\right),\ldots,\left(p_{s+1},[0,\ldots,1]\right),\,\left(p_{s+2},[1,1,\ldots,1]\right)

where the
p_{i}
are pairwise distinct points in
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. In terms of the morphism
\varphi=\left[f_{1},\ldots,f_{s+1}\right]
this means we have conditions

\displaystyle f_{i}(p_{j})

\displaystyle=0\ \text{ for all }1\leq i,\,j\leq s+1,\,i\neq j

\displaystyle f_{i}(p_{s+2})

\displaystyle=f_{j}(p_{s+2})\ \text{ for all }i,j.

Given
s
distinct points in
\mathbb{P}^{1}
there is a nonzero form of degree
s
vanishing at all
s
points and nowhere else, and this form is unique up to scalar. So the first set of conditions determine forms
f_{1},\ldots,f_{s+1}
, each unique up to a scalar. Fixing a choice of
f_{1}
, the second set of conditions above then determine
f_{2},\ldots,f_{s+1}
uniquely; therefore the map
\varphi
, or equivalently the curve
\Gamma
, is uniquely determined.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

We remark that the curve C of Proposition
2.1
plays a role in the birational geometry of the space
X^{1,n}_{n+2}
, the product
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{n}
blown-up at
n+2
points in general position. In fact the strict transform of
C
spans an extremal ray of the Mori cone, see
[
3
, Proposition 3.2]
.

Corollary 2.3
.

(a)

Given
n+3
general points in
\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n+1}
, there is a unique irreducible curve of bidegree
(n,n+1)
passing through all the points. This curve is smooth and rational.

(b)

Given
s\leq n+2
general points in
\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n+1}
, the family of curves of bidegree
(n,n+1)
passing through all the points sweeps out a Zariski-dense subset of
\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n+1}
.

(c)

Suppose
d\geq s
and
k<d+2
. Given
k
general points in
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{s}
, the family of irreducible curves of bidegree
(1,d)
passing through all the points sweeps out a Zariski-dense subset of
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{s}
.

Proof.

For (a), projecting our given points to
\mathbb{P}^{n}
we get a general set of
n+3
points in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, so there is a unique rational normal curve
R_{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
passing through all these points. Any irreducible curve
C
of the given bidegree must project onto
R_{n}
, so must be contained in the subvariety
\pi_{1}^{-1}(R_{n})
. This subvariety is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{n+1}
and the isomorphism maps
C
to a curve of bidegree
(1,n+1)
passing through the images of the
n+3
points. By Proposition
2.1
there is a unique such curve
C
, which must be smooth and rational.

Statement (b) follows immediately from (a).

To prove (c), choose
k
general points in
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{d}
. By Proposition
2.1
, it is again immediate that the family of irreducible curves of bidegree
(1,d)
in
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{d}
passing through the points sweeps out a Zariski-dense subset. Now projecting away from a general
\mathbb{P}^{d-s-1}
in
\mathbb{P}^{d}
we get a family of irreducible curves of bidegree
(1,d)
in
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{s}
through
k
general points.
∎

For convenience we introduce the following terminology for the curve whose existence is proved in part (a) of the previous Corollary:

Definition 2.4
.

Given
n+3
general points in
\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n+1}
, the unique irreducible curve of bidegree
(n,n+1)
passing through all the points is called the
distinguished rational curve
determined by the points.

3
Bilinear secant varieties

In this section, we define some generalisations of classical projective constructions to the setting of products of projective spaces. To keep things simple, we restrict ourselves to products with only two factors, since this will be the case of interest to us in later sections.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
z_{1},\ldots,z_{k}
be points in in
\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{m}
. Assume that the points are
general
, by which we mean that the sets of their projections
\{z^{1}_{1},\dots,z^{1}_{k}\}
and
\{z^{2}_{1},\dots,z^{2}_{k}\}

\displaystyle L^{1}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})\times L^{2}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{m}.

Definition 3.2

Let

Specialising our construction to the case where all the

For this paper, the most important application of this construction is to the distinguished rational curve

\displaystyle M_{1}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}x_{0}&x_{1}&\cdots&x_{n-1}&y_{0}&y_{1}&\cdots&y_{n}\\

Proposition 3.4

The variety

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}x_{0}&\cdots&x_{n-k}&y_{0}&\cdots&y_{n-k+1}\\

Suppose

4
Base locus inequalities

In this section we will derive base locus inequalities, giving lower bounds for the multiplicity of containment of various distinguished subvarieties of
X^{n,n+1}_{s}
in the base locus of a given divisor class. We fix the following form for an effective divisor class on
X^{n,n+1}_{s}
, where
s\leq n+3
:

D=d_{1}H_{1}+d_{2}H_{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{s}m_{i}E_{i}

(1)

We start by recording some simple ”effectivity” inequalities that must be satisfied by all effective divisors on
X^{n,n+1}_{s}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
D
be an effective divisor class of the form (
1
). Then

(a)

for every
i\in\{1,\ldots s\}
we have
m_{i}\leq d_{1}+d_{2}
;

(b)

for every subset
I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,s\}
with
|I|\leq n+2
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}m_{i}\leq nd_{1}+(n+1)d_{2}.

Proof.

If
C
is a curve class whose irreducible representatives sweep out a Zariski-dense subset of
X^{n,n+1}_{s}
, then we must have
D\cdot C\geq 0
for all effective divisors classes
D
.

To prove (a) we apply this fact with
C=l_{1}+l_{2}-e_{i}
, the class of the proper transform of a curve of bidegree
(1,1)
through
p_{i}
.

To prove (b), we apply this with
C=nd_{1}+(n+1)d_{2}-\sum_{i\in I}e_{i}
. Irreducible curves of this class sweep out a Zariski-dense subset by Corollary
2.3
(b).
∎

Next, the simplest base locus inequality concerns the exceptional divisors over our blown-up points.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
D
be an effective divisor class of the form (
1
). Then
D
contains the exceptional divisor
E_{i}
at least
\max\{0,-m_{i}\}
times in its base locus.

Proof.

If
m_{i}\geq 0
there is nothing to prove. So assume that
m_{i}<0
. Then
D\cdot l_{i}=m_{i}<0
. Since irreducible curves of class
l_{i}
cover
E_{i}
, this means that every divisor in the class
D
contains
E_{i}
as a component. Replacing
D
by the effective class
D-E_{i}
and repeating the argument gives the statement of the lemma.
∎

4.1
Base locus inequalities for bilinear joins and secant varieties

Next we give a basic recipe on how, given base locus lemmas for two subvarieties of

, we can obtain a base locus lemmas for their bilinear join. This will then be applied in a number of cases.

Lemma 4.3

Let

be two distinct points of

contains the strict transform of the bilinear span
\operatorname{BL}(z_{1},z_{2})
at least
\max\{0,m_{z_{1}}+m_{z_{2}}-d_{1}-d_{2}\}
times in its base locus.

Proof.

We prove the case where
z_{1},z_{2}\neq p_{j}
, for every
j=1,\dots,s
. The cases where one among
z_{1},z_{2}
or both belong to the set of points
\{p_{1},\dots,p_{s}\}
the proof is simpler and left to the reader.

Consider the blow-up
\phi_{z_{1},z_{2}}:Y_{z_{1},z_{2}}\to X
of

at
z_{1},z_{2}
. For
i=1,2
, we call
E_{z_{i}}
the exceptional divisor of
z_{i}
and
e_{z_{i}}
the line class of

. Under this choice, the strict transform of

is

D_{z_{1},z_{2}}=\phi_{z_{1},z_{2}}^{\ast}D-m_{z_{1}}E_{z_{1}}-m_{z_{2}}E_{z_{2}}.

Also, the class of the strict transform of a curve of bidegree
(1,1)
that passes through
z_{1}
and

is

Since the irreducible curves

contains
\operatorname{BL}(z_{1},z_{2})

\max\{0,-D_{z_{1},z_{2}}\cdot C_{z_{1},z_{2}}\}

∎

4.1.1

5.2
The log Fano property

We now give a conceptual explanation for the finiteness of the cones computed in the previous subsection: namely, the variety

is transverse outside the subset

; moreover, at these points, the projectivised tangent cones of the two varieties intersect transversely inside the projectivised tangent space. Blowing up all the
p_{i}
, therefore, the intersection
\Delta_{2}\cap\Pi^{2}_{\{i,j,k\}}
becomes transverse on
X
, and therefore remains so on
\widetilde{X}
.

Next we consider the intersection of
\widetilde{\Delta}_{1}\cap\widetilde{\Delta}_{2}
on
\widetilde{X}
. Consider the blowup
\varphi\colon Y\rightarrow X
of
X
along
C
: by the universal property of blowups, the blowup
\rho\colon\widetilde{X}\rightarrow X
factors through
\varphi
, so it suffices to show that the strict transforms of
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}
on
Y
are transverse.
Again we can assume that
\Delta_{2}
is given by the determinant of
M_{2}
, and that
\Delta_{1}
is the strict transform on
X
of the conic
\left\{x_{1}^{2}-x_{0}x_{2}=0\right\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{3}
.
Direct calculation shows that
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}
intersect transversely away from
C
, so it is enough to show that the intersection of their strict transforms is transverse inside the exceptional divisor
\pi^{-1}(C)
. The group of automorphisms of
\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{3}
fixing
C
act transitively on
C
, so it suffices to consider the intersection inside one fibre
\pi^{-1}(p)
, for some chosen
p\in C
. Choosing
p=\left([1,0,0],[1,0,0,0]\right)
, direct calculation shows that the intersection of projectivised tangent cones
\mathbb{P}T_{p}\Delta_{1}\cap\mathbb{P}T_{p}\Delta_{2}
inside
\mathbb{P}T_{p}X
is a quadric cone of corank 1, with vertex at the point
c=\mathbb{P}T_{p}C
corresponding to the tangent direction of the curve
C
at
p
.

The fibre of the blowup
\varphi\colon Y\rightarrow X
over
p\in C
is the projectivisation of the fibre of the normal bundle
(N_{C})_{p}=(T_{X})_{p}/(T_{C})_{p}
. The map

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}T_{p}X\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}(N_{C})_{p}

is given by projection away from the point
c
. Since
\mathbb{P}T_{p}\Delta_{1}\cap\mathbb{P}T_{p}\Delta_{2}
has corank 1 with vertex at
c
, when we project away from
c
, the images of
\mathbb{P}T_{p}\Delta_{1}
and
\mathbb{P}T_{p}\Delta_{2}
intersect transversely in
\mathbb{P}(N_{C})_{p}
. This completes the proof of the claim.

Finally we need to show that discrepancies of all exceptional divisors on this log resolution are strictly greater than
-1
. Computing these discrepancies is straightforward since
\Delta_{1}
and all the components of
\Delta_{3}
are nonsingular, while
\Delta_{2}
is nonsingular outside
C
and has multiplicity 2 along
C
.

For a subvariety
V
which is blown up we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{discrep}(V)

\displaystyle=\left(\operatorname{codim}(V,X)-1\right)-\operatorname{mult}_{V}\Delta

which for the various kinds of blown up subvarieties gives

\displaystyle\operatorname{discrep}(\Pi^{2}_{\{i\}})

\displaystyle=2-\epsilon_{2}-6\epsilon_{3}

\displaystyle\operatorname{discrep}(\operatorname{BL}(p_{i},p_{j}))

\displaystyle=2-\epsilon_{2}-3\epsilon_{3}

\displaystyle\operatorname{discrep}(\Pi^{2}_{\{i,j\}})

\displaystyle=1-\epsilon_{2}-\epsilon_{3}

\displaystyle\operatorname{discrep}(C)

\displaystyle=3-\epsilon_{1}-2\epsilon_{2}.

Taking as before
\epsilon_{1}=\frac{1}{2},\,\epsilon_{2}=\frac{10}{11},\,\epsilon_{3}=\frac{1}{10}
, all these discrepancies are strictly larger than
-1
, as required.
∎

7
The effective cone of
X^{3,4}_{6}

In a similar way to the case of
X^{2,3}_{5}
, we can compute the effective cone of
X^{3,4}_{6}
using the Cone Method, with various base locus inequalities as inputs.

We recall some notation as follows. The bilinear secant
\operatorname{BS}_{2}(C)
is a 4-dimensional subvariety of
\mathbb{P}^{3}\times\mathbb{P}^{4}
. Let
p_{i}
be one of the points: then the strict transform of the bilinear join
\operatorname{BJ}(p_{i},\operatorname{BS}_{2}(C))
is a divisor in
X^{3,4}_{6}
, which we denote by
D_{i}
. It has class
H_{1}+2H_{2}-2\sum_{k}E_{k}-E_{i}
. Let
p^{1}_{1},\ldots,p^{1}_{6}
be the projections of the 6 points to
\mathbb{P}^{3}
: then for each
i
there is a unique quadric cone
Q
passing through all of
p^{1}_{1},\ldots,p^{1}_{6}
and with vertex at
p^{1}_{i}
. Let
Q_{i}
denote the strict transform on
X^{3,4}_{6}
of the pullback of this quadric cone; it has class
2H_{1}-\sum_{k}E_{k}-E_{i}
.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
D=d_{1}H_{1}+d_{2}H_{2}-\sum_{i}m_{i}E_{i}
be an effective divisor class on
X^{3,4}_{6}
. Then

(a)

For any
\{i,j,k,l\}\subset\{1,\ldots,6\}
, the unique effective divisor with class
H_{2}-E_{i}-E_{j}-E_{k}-E_{l}
is contained in the base locus
\operatorname{Bs}(D)
with multiplicity at least

\displaystyle\max\{0,m_{i}+m_{j}+m_{k}+m_{l}-3d_{1}-3d_{2}\}.

(b)

For each
i
, the divisor
Q_{i}
is contained in the base locus
\operatorname{Bs}(D)
with multiplicity at least

\displaystyle\max\{0,\sum_{k}m_{k}+m_{i}-4d_{1}-6d_{2}\}.

(c)

For each
i
, the divisor
D_{i}
is contained in the base locus
\operatorname{Bs}(D)
with multiplicity at least

\displaystyle\max\{0,2\sum_{k}m_{k}+m_{i}-8d_{1}-10d_{2}\}.

Proof.

Statement (a) follows from Proposition
4.4
with
n=3
and
k=4
.

For (b), we claim that
Q_{i}
is the bilinear join of
C
and the fibre
\Pi^{1}_{\{i\}}\cong\mathbb{P}^{4}
over
p_{1}^{i}
. Accepting the claim, since the strict transform of
\Pi^{1}_{\{i\}}
in
X^{3,4}_{6}
is contained in the base locus of
D
at least
\max\{0,m_{i}-d_{2}\}
times, then the statement follows from Lemma
4.3
.
We now prove the claim. Let
q\in\mathbb{P}^{3}\times\mathbb{P}^{4}
be any point which does not lie on
\Pi^{1}_{\{i\}}
. By Definition
3.1
, the bilinear join of
q
and
\Pi^{1}_{\{i\}}
is

\displaystyle\operatorname{BL}(\Pi^{1}_{\{i\}},q)

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{p\in\Pi^{1}_{\{i\}}}L^{1}(p,q)\times L^{2}(p,q)

\displaystyle=L^{1}(p_{i},q)\times\bigcup_{p\in\Pi^{1}_{\{i\}}}L^{2}(p,q)

\displaystyle=L^{1}(p_{i},q)\times\mathbb{P}^{4}.

From this, we obtain the following characterisation of the bilinear join of
C
and
\Pi^{1}_{\{i\}}
:

\displaystyle\operatorname{BL}(\Pi^{1}_{\{i\}},C)

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{q\in C}\operatorname{BL}(\Pi^{1}_{\{i\}},q)

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{q\in C}L^{1}(p_{i},q)\times\mathbb{P}^{4}

\displaystyle=\left(\bigcup_{q\in C}L^{1}(p_{1},q)\right)\times\mathbb{P}^{4}.

The first factor of the above expression is the quadric cone of
\mathbb{P}^{3}
over the projection of the curve
C
to
\mathbb{P}^{3}
with vertex
p_{i}^{1}
, hence the strict transform of the product is
Q_{i}
, which proves the claim.

Statement (c) follows from Proposition
4.7
, setting
k=2
and
l=1
.
∎

Proposition 7.2
.

Any effective divisor class
D=d_{1}H_{1}+d_{2}H_{2}-\sum_{i}m_{i}E_{i}
on

must satisfy the following inequalities:

(a)

5d_{1}+4d_{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{6}m_{i}\geq 0
;

(b)

6d_{1}+7d_{2}-2\sum_{i\in I}m_{i}-\sum_{j\notin I}m_{j}\geq 0
for

(c)

Proof.

To prove (a), start by choosing a smooth curve of degree 4 in
\mathbb{P}^{4}
through the 6 points
p^{2}_{1},\ldots,p^{2}_{6}
; such curves cover

, and any such curve is isomorphic to

. The preimage of any such curve is isomorphic to

through 6 points cover

(c).

To prove (b), note that for 3 points

with

gives the required inequality.

For (c), we note that the third bilinear secant

also equals

with

8
Final remarks and open questions

In this final section, we make some remarks to indicate how the phenomena we observed in the case of
X^{2,3}_{5}
and
X^{3,4}_{6}

recur, with increasing complexity, in higher dimensions, and we pose some questions.

In the case
n=2
we obtained a fixed divisor as the bilinear secant variety
\operatorname{BS}_{2}(C)
of our distinguished rational curve. For
n=3
this construction no longer gives a divisor, but we do obtain divisors by taking bilinear joins of
\operatorname{BS}_{2}(C)
with points. To generalise this, fix
n
and let
l
and
k
be natural numbers such that
3k+2l-2=2n
. Choose
l
points among
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n+3}
, say for simplicity
p_{1},\ldots,p_{l}
. Then the bilinear join

\displaystyle\operatorname{BJ}(p_{1},\ldots,p_{l},\operatorname{BS}_{k}(C))

is a divisor in
X^{n,n+1}_{n+3}
. In a similar way to Propositions
6.1
and
7.1
, using the base locus inequalities from Proposition
4.7
one can then prove:

Proposition 8.1
.

For
n,k,l
as above, the divisors
\operatorname{BJ}(p_{1},\ldots,p_{l},\operatorname{BS}_{k}(C))
defined above span extremal rays of
\operatorname{Eff}(X^{n,n+1}_{n+3})
.

For convenience, we will call any divisor obtained in this way a BSJ (”bilinear secant and join”) divisor. As
n
increases, the list of generators of the effective cone becomes increasingly complicated. On one hand, for larger
n
the number of solutions of
3k+2l-2=2n
grows, and hence there are more types of BSJ divisors spanning extremal rays. On the other hand, it is not clear which movable classes will appear; for each
n
, we will find among the generators of
\operatorname{Eff}(X^{n,n+1}_{n+3})
the bilinear cone over extremal movable classes from
X^{n-1,n}_{n+2}
, as we did above, but already for
n=3
a new movable class appeared that was not a cone over something of lower dimension. We ask the following:

Question 8.2
.

Is the effective cone
\operatorname{Eff}(X^{n,n+1}_{n+3})
always spanned by classes of BSJ divisors, exceptional divisors, and extremal classes pulled back from the factors, together with the movable cone? By Theorems
6.3
and
7.3
the answer is affirmative for
n=2
and
n=3
.

Going further, we can ask whether the log Fano property proved in Theorem
6.6
generalises to all dimensions:

Question 8.3
.

Is
X^{n,n+1}_{n+3}
log Fano for every
n\geq 1
?

The answer is yes for
n=1
by
[
12
, Theorem 6.10]
and for
n=2
by Theorem
6.6
. For
n=3
we can identify a suitable boundary divisor
\Delta
making
-K_{X}-\Delta
ample; the difficulty is to show that a certain blowup of
X^{3,4}_{6}
does indeed give a log resolution. We explained above that as
n
increases, we obtain more and more fixed divisors spanning extremal rays of the effective cone
\operatorname{Eff}(X^{n,n+1}_{n+3})
. It seems likely that any suitable boundary divisor on
X^{n,n+1}_{n+3}
will contain many of these fixed divisors as components of its support, meaning that the identification of a log resolution becomes more and more complicated.

Finally, in small dimensions there are also some “exceptional” examples
X^{n,n+1}_{s}
with
s\geq n+4
for which we do not know if they are Mori dream spaces or log Fano. We ask:

Question 8.4
.

Which of the following blow-ups are Mori dream spaces or log Fano?

\displaystyle X^{2,3}_{6},\,X^{2,3}_{7},\,X^{3,4}_{7},\,X^{4,5}_{8}.

Power-Law Suppression of Phonon Thermal Transport by Magnetic Excitations in a Molecular Quantum Spin Liquid
S. Fujiyama, K. Ueda, Y. Otsuka

Abstract
We present large-scale ab initio phonon calculations for the molecular quantum spin liquid X [Pd(dmit) 2 ] 2 . An unusually low average phonon velocity ( ∼ 700 ​ m / s \displaystyle\sim 700~\mathrm{m/s} ) and optical modes below 10 ​ cm − 1 \displaystyle 10~\mathrm{cm}^{-1} confine the Debye T 3 \displaystyle T^{3} regime to T ≲ 2 ​ K \displaystyle T\lesssim 2~\mathrm{K} . As the transfer-integral anisotropy approaches the maximally frustrated regime ( t ′ / t → 1 \displaystyle t^{\prime}/t\!\to\!1 ), the lattice stiffens, ruling out lattice softening as the origin of the spin-liquid state. By quantifying the additional suppression of the thermal conductivity from experimental data, we observe a power-law behavior consistent with two-dimensional magnetic excitations with a nodal, approximately linear (Dirac-like) spectrum.



Geometrical frustration in triangular-lattice antiferromagnets prevents classical spin ordering and gives rise to a quantum spin liquid (QSL) state, where macroscopic quantum fluctuations persist down to the lowest temperatures
Anderson 1973
;
Balents 2010
;
Savary and Balents 2016
;
Zhou et al. 2017

Because the electromagnetic response of spinons remains unresolved, most studies have relied on specific heat and thermal conductivity to probe a finite low‑temperature density of states (DOS).

Phonons in correlated electronic quantum materials have seldom been systematically investigated. Recent developments now allow
ab initio
calculations of phonon dispersions and their contributions to thermal properties directly from crystal structures
Ward et al. 2009
;
Tadano et al. 2014
. Although fractionalized spin excitations cannot be evaluated within density-functional theory (DFT), phonon contributions can be computed to provide a quantitative lower bound for the measured thermal properties, thereby enabling indirect separation of the magnetic component.

Molecular solids such as
X
[Pd(dmit)
2
]
2
and BEDT-TTF-based salts provide several candidates for QSLs.
In these systems, the triangular lattice is generally anisotropic, and the ratio of transfer integrals
\displaystyle t^{\prime}/t
characterizes the deviation from an equilateral geometry.
Notably, the QSL phase can survive even when
\displaystyle t^{\prime}/t
deviates moderately from unity
Ueda et al. 2024

Manna et al. 2018
.
Phonons are therefore expected to play an essential role. However, the large unit-cell volume (
\displaystyle\sim 1700~\text{\AA}^{3}
) and the presence of more than 100 atoms per cell render first-principles calculations extremely demanding, requiring up to
\displaystyle 10^{4}
self-consistent field calculations to evaluate
\displaystyle\kappa(T)

\displaystyle\kappa/T

(1)

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle v\simeq 800~\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}

In QSLs, the ground state is expected to possess strong singlet correlations, leading to a rapid reduction of thermally excited magnetic degrees of freedom at low temperatures.
If such magnetic excitations act as scatterers of phonons, the additional scattering rate
\displaystyle 1/\tau_{\mathrm{sp\text{-}ph}}
is expected to decrease upon cooling.
Accordingly,
\displaystyle R(T)
directly reflects this additional suppression channel.
Although
\displaystyle\sqrt{R(T)}\,v

\displaystyle R(T)

\displaystyle 1/\tau_{\mathrm{exp}}

Notably,

When attributed to spin–phonon scattering governed by the density of low-energy magnetic excitations,

\displaystyle 1/T_{1}

This contrast is naturally understood if spin–phonon scattering is strongly suppressed in the ordered phase,
owing to the limited phase space of low-energy magnetic excitations,
in sharp contrast to the gapless excitation continuum characteristic of the QSL state.

In summary, we have performed large-scale first-principles calculations to evaluate the phonon dispersion and lattice contributions to thermal transport in the molecular QSL
\displaystyle X[\mathrm{Pd(dmit)}_{2}]_{2}
.
Our results reveal pronounced lattice softness, characterized by an average phonon group velocity
\displaystyle\langle v\rangle\approx 700~\mathrm{m/s}
, an order of magnitude smaller than that in typical inorganic crystals, and the presence of optical modes below
\displaystyle 10~\mathrm{cm}^{-1}
(
\displaystyle\sim 1.2
meV), which restrict the validity of the Debye regime to
\displaystyle T\lesssim 2~\mathrm{K}
.
We further find that the lattice stiffens as
\displaystyle t^{\prime}/t\rightarrow 1
, ruling out lattice softening as the primary origin of the QSL state.
By quantifying the additional suppression of
\displaystyle\kappa
beyond the phonon baseline, we extract its characteristic power-law temperature dependence, which provides strong evidence for low-energy magnetic excitations with a nodal, approximately linear (Dirac-like) dispersion.
These results demonstrate that first-principles evaluation of phonon transport is feasible even for molecular crystals with more than one hundred atoms per unit cell, and establish a quantitative route to connect lattice dynamics with emergent quantum magnetism.
The present framework is broadly applicable to correlated molecular solids, where lattice softness, magnetic frustration, and electronic correlations are intricately intertwined.

1.
Introduction

An Einstein manifold is a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
whose Ricci tensor satisfies
\text{Ric}(g)=cg
for some constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
(for a classic survey, see
[
3
]
). For homogeneous manifolds, the Einstein condition reduces to an algebraic system and there is a well-developed existence theory. For example, each compact simply
connected homogeneous space of dimension less than 12 admits a homogeneous Einstein metric
[
5
,
4
]
. A successful approach to constructing inhomogeneous Einstein metrics is to consider
cohomogeneity-one
Einstein metrics. A Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
is said to be cohomogeneity-one if a Lie group
G
acts isometrically on
M
with principal orbits
G/K
of codimension one (that is, the generic orbits of the isometry group are hypersurfaces). In this case, the Einstein condition reduces to a system of ordinary differential equations in the variable on the space of orbits.

Einstein manifolds of positive curvature
c>0
are necessarily compact with finite fundamental group by Myers’ theorem
[
17
]
. Explicit families of examples of such metrics on closed manifolds
M
can be readily constructed. The first of these was Page’s inhomogeneous (conformally Kähler) Einstein metric on
\mathbb{CP}^{2}\#\overline{\mathbb{CP}}^{2}
, which was originally found by an analytic continuation of a Lorentzian Einstein metric describing a black hole spacetime
[
18
]
. The metric has a four-dimensional isometry group
SU(2)\times U(1)
acting on the
S^{3}
surfaces of homogeneity
\Sigma_{t}
where
t\in(-1,1)
parameterizes the level sets. The homogeneous geometry
(\Sigma_{t},h_{t})
induced on these surfaces is not the standard round metric on
S^{3}
, but it is nonetheless possible to explicitly determine the spectrum of the Laplacian
[
21
,
10
]
. This can in turn be used to reduce the problem of determining the problem of computing the spectrum of the Laplacian on the Page metric to a one-dimensional Sturm-Liouville problem
[
10
]
.

A second class of explicit cohomogeneity-one positive Einstein metrics on closed manifolds are the countably infinite family of toric Sasaki-Einstein metrics
Y^{p,q}
where
(p,q)
are positive coprime
[
8
]
(recall that an odd-dimensional Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
is said to admit a Sasakian structure if and only if its metric cone
C(S):=(\mathbb{R}_{0^{+}}\times M,\bar{g}=\text{d}r^{2}+r^{2}g)
is Kähler). The
Y^{p,q}
metrics are defined on the product
S^{2}\times S^{3}
where now
G=SU(2)\times U(1)\times U(1)
acting with four-dimensional orbits on the surfaces of homogeneity
S^{1}\times L(2,1)
(see Section X for details). These geometries represent the first examples of quasi-regular and irregular Sasaki-Einstein structures on
S^{2}\times S^{3}
(these properties refer to whether the orbits of a certain canonically defined Killing vector field are closed and whether the action it generates is free - see the comprehensive survey
[
20
]
for precise definitions). In addition to being of intrinsic interest in differential geometry, the
Y^{p,q}
geometries arise naturally within theoretical physics. In particular, the spacetime AdS
{}_{5}\times Y^{p,q}
furnishes a supersymmetric solution of Type IIB supergravity. String theory on this spacetime background is expected to be equivalent to a certain family of four-dimensional superconformal field theories
[
16
]
. Thus, the manifolds
Y^{p,q}
provide an interesting class of examples to study the gauge theory-gravity (AdS/CFT) correspondence beyond the classic maximally symmetric setting of AdS
{}_{5}\times S^{5}

[
1
]
(indeed,
S^{5}
with its round metric is the model Sasaki-Einstein space). As in the Page space case, the spectrum of the Laplacian on the induced metric on the principal orbits of the isometry action can be determined explicitly, which allows one to reduce the problem of determining the spectrum of the Laplacian on
Y^{p,q}
to analyzing an ordinary differential equation of Heun type
[
14
]
.

The purpose of the present note is to investigate the stability of a class of embedded minimal hypersurfaces within the two families of the Einstein manifolds discussed above. The cohomogeneity-one property plays an essential role in the analysis. Recall that under a one parameter family of deformations
\Phi_{t}
generated by the vector field
X=fn
where
n
is the outward unit normal and
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Sigma)
, we can consider the family of deformations of a fixed two-sided (that is, there is a continuous choice of unit normal) embedded closed submanifold
(\Sigma,h)
by
\Sigma_{t}:=\Phi_{t}(\Sigma)
. As is well known, the second variation of the area functional over a minimal hypersurface (
H_{\Sigma}=0
) is given by

(1.1)

\displaystyle\frac{\text{d}^{2}}{\text{d}t^{2}}{\Bigg|}_{t=0}\text{Area}[\Sigma_{t}]

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}\left(|\nabla f|^{2}-(\text{Ric}(n,n)+\text{Tr}(K^{2})f^{2}\right)\;\text{d}\text{vol}

A compact minimal hypersurface is called stable if the above second variation is nonnegative for all
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Sigma)
. The stability (Jacobi) operator
L
associated with a minimal hypersurface is defined by

(1.2)

L:=-\Delta_{\Sigma}-(\text{Ric}(n,n)+\text{Tr}(K^{2}))=-\Delta_{\Sigma}+\frac{1}{2}(R(h)-R(g)-\text{Tr}(K^{2}))

where in the second equality we have used the trace of the Gauss equation with
H_{\Sigma}=0
. Here
R(h),R(g)
denote the scalar curvatures of the induced metric
h
and the ambient space metric
g
respectively, and
\text{Tr}(K^{2})=K_{ij}K^{ij}
. It is immediate by setting
f=1
in (
1.1
) that if
\text{Ric}(g)>0
then there are no closed stable two-sided minimal hypersurfaces. This applies to the Page manifold and the
Y^{p,q}
spaces, which are positive Einstein.

The stability operator
L
can be shown to always have an infinite number of positive eigenvalues, but may or may not have a finite number of negative eigenvalues. The
Morse index
of a minimal hypersurface, denoted
\text{index}(\Sigma)
, is defined to be the number of negative eigenvalues (assuming it is finite) of
L
. Equivalently
[
6
]
,

(1.3)

\text{index}(\Sigma):=\sup\{\text{dim}V|V\subset C^{\infty}_{0}(\Sigma),L<0\;\text{on }V\setminus\{0\}\}.

The index measures the sum of the dimensions of the eigenspaces corresponding
to negative eigenvalues of
L
, that is, the maximal dimension of the space of variations that destabilize
\Sigma
to second order.

In a space with positive Ricci curvature, the closed minimal hypersurfaces with index one are therefore the ‘most’ stable. The index has proved quite important in the classification of minimal surfaces; for example, for immersed minimal surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with finite total curvature, only the plane has index 0 (it is stable), while the catenoid and Enneper’s surface are the possibilities of index 1. For a survey of results relating the geometry and topology of minimal surfaces, see
[
7
]
.

A classic result of Simons establishes that for minimal spheres
S^{p}
with the standard totally geodesic embedding in
S^{n}
, the index is
n-p
and its nullity is
(p+1)(n-p)

[
19
, Prop. 5.1.1]
. More generally, for any compact, closed
p-
dimensional minimal immersed submanifold
\Sigma
of
S^{n}
,
\text{index}(\Sigma)\geq n-p
and its nullity
\text{null}(\Sigma)\geq(p+1)(n-p)
with equality holding only when
M=S^{p}

[
19
, Theorem 5.1.1]
. For recent work discussing the problem of computing the index of minimal submanifolds in complex Einstein manifolds, we refer the reader to the preprint
[
13
]
.

In the following, we consider the set of homogeneous hypersurfaces of the cohomogeneity-one Page and
Y^{p,q}
metrics and identify those that are minimal. Our main results are to determine the index of these minimal hypersurfaces.

Theorem 1.1
.

The Page space
(\mathbb{CP}^{2}\#\overline{\mathbb{CP}^{2}},g_{P})
admits a single minimal hypersurface within the set of surfaces of homogeneity that is a Berger sphere. It is totally geodesic, and the spectrum of the stability operator can be computed explicitly, and in particular, it has index 1 and nullity 0.

Theorem 1.2
.

The cohomogeneity-one Sasaki-Einstein spaces
Y^{p,q}
on
S^{2}\times S^{3}
admit a single minimal hypersurface within the set of surfaces of homogeneity. It is not totally geodesic and has topology
S^{1}\times L(2,1)
. The spectrum of its stability operator can be computed explicitly and in particular, it has index 3 and nullity 0.

The proofs of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
are given respectively in Section 2 and Section 3 below. We note that the problem of computing the index of minimal hypersurfaces for the Page metric was considered recently in
[
13
, Theorem 5.1]
; their result differs from ours in that it is claimed, based on their earlier work
[
12
]
, that there are additional homogeneous minimal surfaces in the Page space, whereas we find only a single such surface (see Proposition
2.1
).

2.
Minimal hypersurfaces in the Page metric

2.1.
The Page space

The Page space
[
18
]
is the manifold
M=\mathbb{CP}^{2}\#\overline{\mathbb{CP}^{2}}
equipped with an inhomogeneous Einstein metric
g_{P}
, given in local coordinates
(x,\psi,\theta,\phi)
by

(2.1)

g=S\left[\frac{\text{d}x^{2}}{A(x)}+4\alpha^{2}A(x)\left(\text{d}\psi+\frac{\cos\theta}{2}\text{d}\phi\right)^{2}+B(x)(\text{d}\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta\text{d}\phi^{2})\right]

where

(2.2)

A(x)=\frac{(3-\nu^{2}-\nu^{2}(1+\nu^{2})x^{2})(1-x^{2})}{1-\nu^{2}x^{2}},\qquad B(x)=\frac{1-\nu^{2}x^{2}}{3+6\nu^{2}-\nu^{4}},

and

(2.3)

S=\frac{3(1+\nu^{2})}{\Lambda},\qquad\alpha=(2(3+\nu^{2}))^{-1}.

Here
x\in(-1,1)
,
\theta\in(0,\pi)
and
\nu
is a real parameter that is determined by the requirement that
g_{P}
metric is Einstein, i.e.

(2.4)

\text{Ric}(g_{P})=\Lambda g_{P}.

This requires that
\nu
be a positive root of the quartic

(2.5)

\nu^{4}+4\nu^{3}-6\nu^{2}+12\nu-3=0.

There is a unique such root
\nu\approx 0.281702
. Note
\det g=4S^{4}\alpha^{2}B(x)\sin^{2}\theta
. For convenience, we will normalize the volume of the Page metric by fixing
\Lambda=3
from now on; this is the scaling used for the unit round
n-
sphere
S^{n}
, i.e.
\text{Ric}(g)=-(n-1)g
.

The metric extends to a globally smooth metric on the non-trivial
S^{2}
bundle over
S^{2}
provided the following identifications are satisfied:

(2.6)

(\psi,\phi)\sim(\psi+\pi,\phi+2\pi),\qquad(\psi,\phi)\sim(\psi+2\pi,\phi),

In this local chart, the
(\theta,\phi)
coordinates parameterize the round
S^{2}
base and the
(x,\psi)
coordinates parameterze an
S^{2}
with inhomogeneous metric. The Killing field
\partial_{\psi}
degenerates smoothly at
x=\pm 1
, the poles of the
S^{2}
fibres. Surfaces of constant
x
have
S^{3}
topology with induced metric

(2.7)

h=S\left[4\alpha^{2}A(x)\left(\text{d}\psi+\frac{\cos\theta}{2}\text{d}\phi\right)^{2}+B(x)(\text{d}\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta\text{d}\phi^{2})\right].

This exhibits the form of a fibre metric of a circle bundle over
S^{2}
with
\psi
parameterizing the
S^{1}
fibres. The bundle is non-trivial with Chern number 1.

The metric is cohomogeneity-one and admits an
SU(2)\times U(1)
isometry group generated by the Killing vector fields

(2.8)

\displaystyle R_{1}

\displaystyle=-\cot\theta\cos\phi\partial_{\phi}-\sin\phi\partial_{\theta}+\frac{\cos\phi}{2\sin\theta}\partial_{\psi},

\displaystyle R_{2}

\displaystyle=-\cot\theta\sin\phi\partial_{\phi}+\cos\phi\partial_{\theta}+\frac{\sin\phi}{2\sin\theta}\partial_{\psi},

\displaystyle R_{3}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\phi},\qquad L_{3}=\tfrac{1}{2}\partial_{\psi}.

and so
L_{R_{i}}g_{P}=0
,
i=1,2,3
and
L_{L_{3}}g_{P}=0
. The Killing fields satisfy the Lie algebra of
SU(2)\times U(1)
, naemly,
[R_{i},R_{j}]=-\epsilon_{ijk}R^{k}
,
[L_{3},R_{i}]=0
with
\epsilon_{ijk}
the totally antisymmetric tensor with
\epsilon_{123}=1
.

2.2.
Homogeneous minimal hypersurfaces

The isometry group acts with three-dimensional orbits acting on the homogeneous
S^{3}
level sets of the coordinate
x\in(-1,1)
. We are interested in classifying which of these hypersurfaces are minimal, i.e., critical points of the area functional. It is claimed in
[
12
]
that each such level set is minimal; we find, however, that only the ‘equatorial’ level set at
x=0
is minimal. The second fundamental form
K
of a two-sided hypsurface
\Sigma\subset(M,g)
with unit normal
n
is given by the symmetric tensor field

(2.9)

K(X,Y):=g(\nabla_{X}n,Y)

where
X,Y
are vector fields tangent to
\Sigma
,
g
is the ambient space metric, and
\nabla
is the metric connection associated to
g
. While it is straightforward to compute the components of
K
in the local coordinate basis, since the hypersurfaces being considered are just coordinate level sets, it is convenient to use the following ‘
n+1
decomposition’ of the ambient space metric

(2.10)

g=\beta^{2}\text{d}x^{2}+h_{ij}(\text{d}y^{i}+N^{i}\text{d}x)(\text{d}y^{j}+N^{j}\text{d}x)

where
\alpha
is a function and
N
is a vector field on the family of level sets
(\Sigma_{x},h)
(referred to as the ‘lapse’ and ‘shift’ in the relativity setting) and
y^{i}
are local coordinates on
\Sigma_{x}
. Here
h_{ij},N^{i}
are understood to be functions of
x
, the parameter which labels the level sets. It is a straightforward computation to determine the second fundamental form
(see e.g.,
[
2
, Section 2.3]
):

(2.11)

K_{ij}=\frac{1}{2\beta}\left(\partial_{x}h_{ij}-D_{i}N_{j}-D_{j}N_{i}\right)

where
D
is the metric connection associated to
h
. We have chosen the sign of
K
so that the standard sphere in Euclidean space has a positive-definite second fundamental form with outward pointing normal
n
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\Sigma_{x}
be a homogeneous
S^{3}
hypersurface defined as a level set of
x\in(-1,1)
. Then
\Sigma:=\Sigma_{0}
is the only minimal hypersurface of this family. Moreover, it is totally geodesic.

Proof.

For the metric
g_{P}
, it is straightforward to read off

(2.12)

\beta=\left(\frac{S}{A(x)}\right)^{1/2},\qquad N^{i}=0

The mean curvature of a level set hypersurface
\Sigma_{x}
is

(2.13)

H_{\Sigma}=\text{Tr}_{h}K=h^{ij}K_{ij}=\frac{1}{2\beta}h^{ij}\partial_{x}h_{ij}=\frac{1}{2\beta}\partial_{x}(\log\det h)

where

(2.14)

\det h=S^{3}\cdot 4\alpha^{2}A(x)B(x)\sin^{2}\theta.

This gives

(2.15)

H_{\Sigma}=\left(\frac{1}{SA(x)}\right)^{1/2}\frac{x(-3-\nu^{4}+2\nu^{2}x^{2}(1+\nu^{2}))}{1-\nu^{2}x^{2}}.

Recall
A(x)>0
in the domian
x\in(-1,1)
. It can be checked explicitly that the quadratic in round brackets in the numerator has two real roots which lie outside the domain. Thus
H_{\Sigma}=0
if and only if
x=0
.

Next, observe that the components of the Page metric
g_{ij}
and the induced metric
h_{ij}
are functions of
x^{2}
alone. It follows from (
2.11
) that each of the components
K_{ij}
are odd functions of
x
and hence they all vanish at
x=0
. Thus, the minimal hypersurface
\Sigma_{0}
is totally geodesic.
∎

We now turn to determining the index of the stability operator on
\Sigma_{0}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1

For the minimal hypersurface
\Sigma_{0}

by employing the homogeneity of the metric and demonstrating that it has index 1.

Since

(2.16)

The Laplacian with respect to
(\Sigma,h)
is explicitly obtained from the formula

(2.17)

\Delta_{h}f=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\det h}}\partial_{i}(\sqrt{\det h}h^{ij}\partial_{j})f

To determine the spectrum of

, introduce the differential operator
[

]

(2.18)

D=\nabla_{S^{2}}-inA

where

and

(2.19)

\hat{g}=\frac{1}{4}(\text{d}\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta\text{d}\phi^{2}),

(2.21)

3.
Minimal hypersurfaces in the Sasaki-Einstein spaces
Y^{p,q}

3.1.
Y^{p,q}
Sasaki-Einstein metrics

An interesting class of five-dimensional cohomogeneity-one Einstein manifolds which admit homogeneous minimal (but not totally geodesic) hypersurfaces are the countably infinite family of Sasaki-Einstein manifolds
Y^{p,q}\cong S^{2}\times S^{3}
explicitly constructed in
[
8
]
. Recall that a Sasaki-Einstein manifold is an Einstein manifold whose metric cone is Ricci-flat and Kähler. The metric can be expressed in local coordinates as

(3.1)

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle=\frac{1-y}{6}(\text{d}\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta\text{d}\phi^{2})+\frac{\text{d}y^{2}}{w(y)q(y)}+\frac{q(y)}{9}(\text{d}\psi-\cos\theta\text{d}\phi)^{2}

\displaystyle+w(y)\left[\text{d}\alpha+f(y)(\text{d}\psi-\cos\theta\text{d}\phi)\right]^{2}

with

(3.2)

w(y)=\frac{2(b-y^{2})}{1-y},\quad q(y)=\frac{b-3y^{2}+2y^{3}}{b-y^{2}},\quad f(y)=\frac{b-2y+y^{2}}{6(b-y^{2})}

and
b
is a constant. This is an Einstein metric satisfying

(3.3)

R_{ab}=4g_{ab}.

An extensive global analysis of this family of metrics is given in
[
8
]
. Let
p,q
be coprime positive integers with

. Provided that

(3.4)

b=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{p^{2}-3q^{2}}{4p^{3}}\sqrt{4p^{2}-3q^{2}}.

(3.5)

\ell=\frac{q}{3q^{2}-2p^{2}+p\sqrt{4p^{2}-3q^{2}}},

characterized by

, provided that we take the ranges of the coordinates to be

(3.6)

are the two smallest roots of the cubic appearing in the numerator of

Note that setting

. Furthermore,

Note that the coordinate

Proof.

The determinant of the induced metric

Straightforward computation shows that the condition

is equivalent to solving the cubic

The discriminant of

is

(3.16)

-108\left(16b^{2}-5b-11\right)=108\cdot\left(16b+11\right)(1-b)

In the range
0<b<1
, this quantity is strictly positive. Thus, there are three distinct real roots
y^{*}_{i}
,
i=1,2,3
. It remains to determine which of these roots lie in the range
(y_{1},y_{2})
and thus constitute a minimal surface. From Descartes’ rule of signs,
H(y)
can have two or zero positive roots. It is easy to check that the cubic
H(y)
has a local maximum at
y=1/4
and a local minimum at
y=1
. In addition
H(0)>0
,
H(1/4)>0
, and
H(1)=b-1<0
. It follows that

must have positive roots

at some

y_{3}^{*}>1
and a single negative root

cannot lie inside
(y_{1},y_{2})
. We can also see that the cubic

has a local maximum at
y=0
and a local minimum at
y=1
. Thus (as expected) it will be positive between one negative root

and one positive root

, with a third positive root
y_{3}>1
.

At any root

of

Q(y)=b-3y^{2}+2y^{3}=-6y_{i}^{*}(1-y_{i}^{*})^{2}

g_{yy}<0
and the induced metric

of

gives rise to a minimal surface.
∎

This minimal surface is not totally geodesic. Let
\tilde{K}_{ij}:=\sqrt{g_{yy}}K_{ij}
. Direct computation shows

(3.17)

\displaystyle\tilde{K}_{\alpha\alpha}

\displaystyle=-8\bar{y},\qquad\tilde{K}_{\alpha\psi}=-\frac{1+4\bar{y}}{4},\qquad\tilde{K}_{\alpha\phi}=\tilde{K}_{\alpha\psi}\cos\theta

\displaystyle\tilde{K}_{\theta\theta}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{12},\qquad\tilde{K}_{\psi\psi}=-\frac{1+2\bar{y}}{9},\qquad\tilde{K}_{\psi\phi}=\tilde{K}_{\psi\psi}\cos\theta,

\displaystyle\tilde{K}_{\phi\phi}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{72}\left(7+\cos(2\theta)+8\bar{y}(1+\cos 2\theta)\right)

The set of eigenvalues

PDK4 as a tumor suppressor in gastric cancer: implications for metabolic regulation, immune modulation, and prognostic potential
Authors: Jin Xu, Jie Sun, Nannan Zhang, Nan Sang, Buwei Teng, Wei He
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveGastric cancer (GC) remains a leading cause of cancer-related mortality, with growing evidence implicating metabolic regulators in its progression. This study investigates the expression pattern, prognostic significance, molecular interactions, and immunomodulatory role of pyruvate dehydrogenase kinase 4 (PDK4) in GC.MethodsIntegrated bioinformatics analyses were conducted using RNA sequencing and clinical data from the TCGA-STAD and GSE54129 datasets. Differential gene expression analysis identified co-differentially expressed genes (co-DEGs). Functional enrichment analysis, protein–protein interaction (PPI) network construction, competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) network prediction, and immune infiltration analysis were performed. The clinical relevance of PDK4 was evaluated through survival analysis, Cox regression modeling, and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis.ResultsPDK4 expression was significantly downregulated in GC tissues compared to normal controls (P < 0.001), while its high expression was associated with advanced tumor stage (P = 0.0322) and favorable prognosis (P = 0.0013). A total of 77 co-DEGs and PDK4-centered PPI networks highlighted its involvement in key metabolic pathways, including the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle, AMPK signaling, and HIF-1 signaling. The ceRNA network analysis identified 135 miRNAs and 225 lncRNAs associated with PDK4, including KCNQ1OT1. Immune infiltration analysis revealed a positive correlation between PDK4 expression and B cell (r = 0.249) and CD4+T cell (r = 0.419) abundance, suggesting an immunoregulatory role. PDK4 exhibited high diagnostic accuracy (AUC = 0.775 in TCGA) and emerged as an independent prognostic factor (P = 0.004, HR = 1.159).ConclusionPDK4 functions as a tumor suppressor in GC, bridging metabolic reprogramming, epigenetic regulation, and immune microenvironment remodeling. Its downregulation contributes to disease progression and immune evasion, underscoring its potential as a prognostic biomarker and therapeutic target. Restoring PDK4 activity may serve as a promising strategy to mitigate GC aggressiveness and therapeutic resistance.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-025-03581-1.Keywords: PDK4, Gastric cancer, Metabolic reprogramming, Immune infiltration, Prognosis

Introduction

Gastric cancer (GC) is one of the most prevalent malignancies worldwide and the fifth leading cause of cancer-related deaths [1, 2]. Due to the absence of typical early symptoms, most GC patients are diagnosed at advanced stages, often presenting with malignant proliferation, extensive invasion, and distant metastasis, leading to a median survival of only 3–5 months [3, 4]. Despite significant therapeutic advancements, such as surgery and adjuvant chemotherapy, the prognosis for GC patients remains poor [5]. Therefore, identifying novel molecular biomarkers and therapeutic targets is crucial for improving early diagnosis, treatment, and prevention of tumor [6, 7].

Pyruvate dehydrogenase kinase 4 (PDK4), located on chromosome 7, encodes a mitochondrial protein in the PDK/BCKDK protein kinase family. This protein contains a histidine kinase domain and resides in the mitochondrial matrix, where it inhibits the pyruvate dehydrogenase complex by phosphorylating one of its subunits, thereby regulating glucose metabolism. PDK4 is predominantly expressed in skeletal muscle and heart [8]and its overexpression has been reported in glioblastoma, breast cancer [9]ovarian cancer [10]and colon cancer [11]. Elevated PDK4 expression has been linked to increased cell proliferation, invasion in vitro, and enhanced tumor growth in vivo [12]. Recent studies suggest that PDK4 promotes tumor growth by regulating the KRAS pathway in lung and colorectal cancers [13]. However, the relationship between PDK4 and GC remains underexplored.

To investigate the correlation between PDK4 and GC and assess its potential prognostic value, we performed comprehensive bioinformatic and statistical analyses on RNA-seq and clinical data from GC patients, retrieved from the Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO). We also explored the underlying biological interaction networks and the prognostic significance of PDK4-related gene expression. We hypothesize that the antitumor activity of PDK4 may impact the prognosis of GC patients. This study provides new insights into the molecular mechanisms underlying GC and highlights the potential of novel genes as diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers, as well as therapeutic targets for combating GC.

Materials and methods

Public data acquisition

The gastric cancer gene expression dataset GSE54129 was retrieved from the NCBI GEO database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) [14] using the R package GEOquery [15]which includes data from 111 gastric cancer tissues and 21 normal tissues. Additionally, gene expression and clinical data were obtained from The Cancer Genome Atlas Stomach Adenocarcinoma (TCGA-STAD) database (https://tcga-data.nci.nih.gov/), comprising 375 tumor tissues and 32 normal tissues [16, 17].

Common differentially expressed genes (co-DEGs)

Differential expression analysis between tumor and normal tissues in each group was performed using the R package limma [18]. Genes with an adjusted p-value (adjPvalue) < 0.01 and |logFC| >1 were considered as differentially expressed genes (DEGs). Co-DEGs were identified by intersecting the DEGs from the two datasets. The results of the differential expression analysis, along with the impact of Co-DEGs on patient grouping, were visualized using the R packages ggplot2 and Heatmap, respectively. The process of selecting Co-DEGs was illustrated using the R package VennDiagram.

Enrichment analysis

Gene Ontology (GO) analyses is a widely used method for gene function enrichment studies, encompassing biological processes (BP), molecular functions (MF), and cellular components (CC). The Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) database is commonly utilized to store information on genomes, biological pathways, diseases, and drugs. Disease Ontology (DO) facilitates gene-disease enrichment analysis, uncovering relationships between diseases and providing insights into the mechanisms of complex diseases, early prevention, diagnosis, drug development, and safety evaluation. In this study, the R package clusterProfiler [19] was employed for GO functional annotation and KEGG pathway enrichment analysis, while the DOSE package [20] was used for disease enrichment analysis. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Protein-protein interaction (PPI) network construction and annotation

The protein-protein interaction (PPI) network was constructed using the STRING database [21]. First, Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) was performed to analyze the functional and pathway enrichment of genes in the PPI network. The network was then visualized using Cytoscape [22]. Densely connected regions within the PPI network may represent molecular complexes with specific biological functions. To identify protein complexes or functional modules, the MCODE network clustering algorithm was applied [23]. Functional annotation of the MCODE-identified modules was performed using ClueGO [24]. DAVID (Database for Annotation, Visualization, and Integrated Discovery, http://david.abcc.ncifcrf.gov/) is a bioinformatics resource that integrates biological data and analytical tools, providing systematic functional annotation for large-scale gene or protein lists and facilitating online biological analysis [25]. Functional annotation and pathway enrichment analysis of the genes in the PPI network were performed using DAVID, while GSEA enrichment analysis was conducted on the PPI genes using the R package clusterProfiler. A p-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Prediction of miRNA-lncRNA interactions in the CeRNA network

Competing endogenous RNAs (ceRNAs) are a class of molecules that can compete for binding to microRNAs (miRNAs). The ceRNA regulatory network represents the entire system of regulatory interactions involving ceRNAs. Increasing evidence suggests that long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) play key roles in the ceRNA network, primarily regulating protein-coding gene expression by acting as miRNA sponges. To predict potential miRNAs that bind to the 3’ untranslated region (UTR) of the target gene, we utilized the Encyclopedia of RNA Interactomes (ENCORI) database (http://starbase.sysu.edu.cn/index.php). We then predicted interactions between miRNAs and lncRNAs using the miRNet 2.0 database (https://www.mirnet.ca) [26]. By integrating results from these two databases, we identified miRNAs that potentially target the gene of interest and their associated lncRNAs, providing a foundation for further functional analyses.

Immune infiltration analysis

The TIMER (Tumor Immune Estimation Resource) database leverages high-throughput sequencing data to analyze immune cell infiltration in tumor tissues, focusing primarily on B cells, CD4+ T cells, neutrophils, macrophages, and dendritic cells [27]. In this study, we utilized the database to evaluate the association between differentially expressed genes and immune cell infiltration in gastric cancer versus normal tissues, and to compare the expression levels of these immune cell types.

Correlation analysis of clinical features and risk assessment

Gene expression levels are often associated with clinical characteristics. We employed the Spearman correlation coefficient to evaluate the relationship between clinical features and co-DEGs. Additionally, we analyzed gene expression data to investigate whether co-DEGs could effectively distinguish tumor tissues from normal tissues. To this end, we performed classification analysis, plotted the receiver operatingcharacteristic (ROC) curve, and calculated the area under the curve (AUC) value.

Statistical analysis

All data processing and analyses were performed using R software (version 4.0.2). Graphs were generated with the ggplot2 package [25] and ROC curves were plotted using the pROC package [26]with the AUC calculated to assess the accuracy of risk scores in prognostic estimation. All statistical analyses were conducted using two-sided tests, with P-values < 0.05 considered statistically significant.

Results

Gene expression analysis and differential gene screening

We performed DEG analysis between tumor and normal tissues using the limma package. In the GSE54129 dataset, we identified 5,247 DEGs, including 2,717 upregulated and 2,530 downregulated genes. In the TCGA-STAD dataset, we identified 4,653 DEGs, with 3,747 upregulated and 906 downregulated genes. Based on these DEGs, we conducted cluster analysis and generated volcano plots (Fig. 1A and B). Further analysis revealed that both upregulated and downregulated genes were primarily located on chromosomes 1, 2, and X (Fig. 1C). We then selected the intersection of DEGs from both datasets (Fig. 1D), resulting in 77 co-DEGs (Fig. 1E). The classification heatmaps (Fig. 1F, G) demonstrated that these 77 co-DEGs effectively distinguish tumor samples from normal samples.

PDK4 downregulation predicts gastric cancer prognosis and stage

Through co-expression analysis of co-DEGs, we identified a significant downregulation of PDK4 expression in gastric cancer tissues (Fig. 2B), with its expression levels closely correlating with tumor stage (F = 2.96, P = 0.0322, Fig. 2C). Kaplan-Meier analysis showed that patients with low PDK4 expression had better prognosis (P = 0.0013, Fig. 2D). Furthermore, analysis of the TCGA pan-cancer dataset revealed that PDK4 was significantly downregulated across various tumor types, including READ, PRAD, PCPG, LUCS, LUAD, KIRP, CICH, and UCEC (all P<0.05, Fig. 2A), suggesting a potentially conserved role for PDK4 in different malignancies. Notably, in gastric cancer, mutations and copy number alterations of PDK4 were prevalent (Fig. 2E and F), further underscoring its potential significance in this context.

DO analysis demonstrated that co-DEGs were notably linked to muscle-related disorders, such as Duchenne muscular dystrophy and intrinsic cardiomyopathy, implicating their potential roles in disease mechanisms (Fig. 3C, Supplementary Table 3).

PDK4 coordinates metabolic and signaling networks in gastric cancer

To systematically explore the role of PDK4 in the initiation and progression of gastric cancer, we constructed a PDK4-specific PPI network using the STRING database, comprising 51 nodes and 391 interactions (Fig. 4A). A multidimensional bioinformatics approach was applied to investigate functional modules, molecular pathways, and regulatory networks. First, GO and KEGG enrichment analyses were performed using the DAVID database, which revealed that genes in the PPI network are significantly involved in core glucose metabolism pathways (Fig. 4C, Supplementary Table 4), including the tricarboxylic acid cycle (TCA cycle), pyruvate metabolism, and the insulin signaling pathway. Additionally, these genes are enriched in key energy metabolism regulation processes, such as glucose homeostasis and acetyl-CoAbiosynthesis. KEGG analysis further revealed significant associations with cancer-related signaling pathways, such as AMPK, FoxO, and HIF-1 (Fig. 4D, Supplementary Table 5). To validate the reliability of these functional enrichment results, pathway analysis was performed using GSEA. The results showed that PPI genes are enriched in transcriptional regulation processes, such as RNA polymerase II-specific DNA binding (Fig. 4G). At the signaling pathway level, they were strongly associated with classic oncogenic pathways, including PI3K-Akt and Ras (Fig. 4F), confirming the DAVID analysis results. Modular analysis using the MCODE plugin in Cytoscape identified two key functional modules (Fig. 4E). Functional annotation of these high-scoring modules with ClueGO revealed their multi-pathway collaborative features: key hub genes are simultaneously enriched in two or three biological functions or pathways, suggesting that PDK4 may influence gastric cancer progression through dual mechanisms of metabolic reprogramming and microenvironmental regulation.

PDK4 CeRNA network mediated by KCNQ1OT1 and MiRNAs in gastric cancer

To investigate the functional mechanisms of the PDK4-related ceRNA regulatory network in gastric cancer, we first used the ENCORI database to predict 135 miRNAs potentially regulating PDK4 expression. We then utilized the miRNet database to identify 225 lncRNAs interacting with these miRNAs. The resulting ceRNA network, consisting of lncRNA-miRNA-mRNA interactions, was visualized using Cytoscape (Fig. 5A). To further explore the role of PDK4 within the ceRNA network, we employed the cytoHub plugin to identify key ceRNA modules, which revealed the central regulatory mechanisms of PDK4 (Fig. 5B). Our analysis indicated that PDK4 is regulated by miRNAs such as hsa-miR-494-3p, hsa-miR-129-1-3p, and hsa-miR-141-3p. Moreover, the interaction between PDK4 and the lncRNA KCNQ1OT1 suggests its potential pivotal role in the ceRNA network. We also found that KCNQ1OT1 may regulate PDK4 expression by competitively binding miRNAs. These findings highlight the complex regulatory role of PDK4 in gene expression networks.

PDK4 expression correlates with immune infiltration in gastric cancer

We further conducted immune infiltration analysis of co-DEGs in gastric cancer and normal tissues using the TIMER database. The results revealed that the expression of the PDK4 gene had positive links with the abundance of immune cell infiltration, specifically B cells (r = 0.249, P = 8.14e-09), CD4+ T cells (r = 0.419, P = 5.86e-17), macrophage (r = 0.474, P = 4.13e-22), and dendritic cells (r = 0.142, P = 6.12e-03) (Fig. 5C).

PDK4 expression impacts prognosis and distinguishes tumor from normal tissues in gastric cancer

To evaluate the clinical significance of the PDK4 gene, we analyzed the correlation between its expression levels and various clinical characteristics of gastric cancer patients, including gender, disease stage, age, tumor purity, and survival time. Cox proportional hazards regression analysis revealed that PDK4 gene expression (P = 0.004, HR = 1.159) and age (P = 0.007, HR = 1.028) are significant factors influencing overall survival (OS), while tumor stage (particularly Stage 3 and Stage 4) is also significantly associated with patient survival (Fig. 5D, E; Table 1). Furthermore, we assessed whether PDK4 could effectively distinguish between tumor and adjacent normal tissues. The ROC curve analysis showed that PDK4 had an AUC of 0.566 in the GEO dataset, indicating limited ability to distinguish between normal and tumor tissues. In contrast, in the TCGA dataset, PDK4’s AUC of 0.775 suggests a stronger discriminative ability between normal and tumor tissues (Fig. 5F, G).

Discussion

Gastric cancer remains one of the leading causes of cancer-related mortality worldwide. Despite accumulating evidence highlighting the pivotal role of energy metabolism in tumorigenesis [6, 28, 29]the precise mechanisms through which key metabolic regulators influence gastric cancer progression remain poorly understood. Pyruvate dehydrogenase (PDH) is a critical enzyme in glycolysis, catalyzing the conversion of pyruvate to acetyl-CoA for mitochondrial oxidation [30]. PDK4, located in the mitochondrial matrix, inhibits PDH complex activity by phosphorylating its E1α subunit, thereby modulating glucose metabolism [31]. Several studies have demonstrated that PDK4 upregulation promotes aerobic glycolysis, thereby influencing tumorigenesis [11, 32, 33]. This study integrates bioinformatics, clinical characteristics, functional network analysis, and immune microenvironment analysis to investigate the multifaceted role of PDK4 in gastric cancer.

In our study, we observed that PDK4 is generally downregulated in the TCGA pan-cancer dataset, including gastric cancer. However, high PDK4 expression in gastric cancer tissues correlates with advanced stages and poor prognosis, consistent with the findings of Miao et al. [34]who demonstrated that PDK4 expression is significantly associated with OS and disease-free survival in GC patients. To address this paradox, this study analyzed the TCGA-STAD cohort using the TIMER database and found a negative correlation between PDK4 expression and tumor purity. Numerous studies [35–37] have shown that lower tumor purity typically reflects a more complex tumor microenvironment, containing more pro-tumorigenic and pro-metastatic factors. Research has suggested that the overall expression level of PDK4 in tumor tissues is reduced, likely due to a more significant downregulation of PDK4 in stromal and immune cells. The presence of these cells may result in an overall reduction of PDK4 expression in tumor tissues compared to normal tissues.

In functional enrichment analysis of co-DEGs, we found that these genes were significantly enriched in muscle-related pathways. Based on this result, we further constructed a PPI network for PDK4, which revealed a strong association of PDK4 with metabolic pathways, including the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle, AMPK signaling pathway, and HIF-1 signaling pathway. Previous studies have confirmed that PDK4 inhibits the activity of pyruvate dehydrogenase (PDH), promoting a shift in cancer cell glucose metabolism from mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation to glycolysis. This metabolic reprogramming generates more energy and lactate, supporting cancer cell growth and metastasis. Particularly in the advanced stages of cancer, high expression of PDK4 may enhance the invasiveness and malignancy of cancer cells through this metabolic regulation mechanism, ultimately leading to poor prognosis for patients. This also reasonably explains why patients with high PDK4 expression are often in later tumor stages with worse prognosis. Furthermore, miR-5683 was shown to inhibit PDK4 expression and block glycolysis in gastric cancer, thereby delaying tumor progression. In breast cancer, PDK4 inhibition reverses tamoxifen resistance and enhances sensitivity to endocrine therapies, including tamoxifen and fulvestrant [9]. Research in bladder cancer further shows that silencing PDK4 significantly inhibits cancer cell migration and invasion. Additionally, xenograft models of PDK4 knockout exhibit marked suppression of tumor growth in nude mice [33]. Clinical data analysis in prostate cancer reveals that PDK4 expression levels can independently predict recurrence, irrespective of traditional factors such as grade, stage, and PSA levels [38]. In contrast, in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), PDK4 may exert tumor-suppressive effects, as its inhibition leads to the increased expression of cyclins E1 and A2 and transcription factor E2F1, which promote tumor proliferation [39]. Meanwhile, in lung adenocarcinoma, miR-182 regulates fat metabolism, ROS generation, and JNK signaling by targeting PDK4, thereby driving tumor progression [40]. These findings suggest that PDK4 plays dual roles in tumorigenesis, either promoting or suppressing cancer, depending on the metabolic microenvironment and molecular mechanisms in different tumor types.

Functional enrichment analysis of co-DEGs and the PPI network associated with PDK4 revealed that key pathways involved in glucose and energy metabolism, including the TCA cycle, insulin signaling, and AMPK pathways, are central to PDK4 activity in gastric cancer. These findings align with PDK4’s classical role in regulating pyruvate entry into mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation [11, 32, 33]. Moreover, the enrichment of HIF-1 and FoxO signaling pathways in the PPI network suggests that PDK4 may intersect with hypoxia- and stress-related signaling cascades, linking metabolic adaptation to microenvironmental challenges. Additionally, ceRNA network analysis identified a regulatory axis involving PDK4, which is modulated by 135 miRNAs and 225 lncRNAs. lncRNA plays a critical role in regulating the expression of metabolism-related genes [41]. Recent studies have revealed that the long non-coding RNA KCNQ1OT1 functions as a competing endogenous RNA to promote tumorigenesis in colorectal [42]bladder [43]and ovarian cancers [44] by targeting key molecules including miR-181a-5p, miR-218-5p, and miR-142-5p. Its aberrant overexpression enhances malignant phenotypes such as tumor cell proliferation, invasion, and metastasis through epigenetic reprogramming mechanisms [45]. Notably, in gastric carcinogenesis, Li et al. [46] demonstrated that overexpression of KCNQ1OT1 functions as a competing endogenous RNA to sequester miR-556-3p, thereby relieving its inhibitory effect on CLIC1. This molecular interaction activates the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway, ultimately promoting proliferation, invasion, and migration capacities in gastric cancer cells. Furthermore, miR-129-1-3p, a mature member of the miR-129 family, exhibits tumor-suppressive functions in various malignancies including serous ovarian cancer [47]and non-small cell lung cancer [48]. Through multi-omics analysis, Varkalaite’s et al. [49] identified progressive dysregulation of hsa-miR-196a-5p and hsa-miR-129-1-3p during gastric cancer progression, with serum hsa-miR-129-1-3p levels showing significant negative correlation with tumor stage, suggesting its potential as a non-invasive diagnostic biomarker. These findings mechanistically align with the KCNQ1OT1/miRNAs/PDK4 regulatory axis identified in our study, indicating that KCNQ1OT1 may regulate PDK4 expression by competitively binding specific miRNAs, thereby modulating the dynamic balance between proliferation and apoptosis in gastric cancer cells.

Immune infiltration analysis revealed that the expression of PDK4 is positively correlated with the infiltration of B cells, macrophages, and CD4+ T cells, suggesting that PDK4 is involved in shaping the immune-functional tumor microenvironment. This is consistent with the emerging paradigm in cancer metabolism [50–52]where metabolic enzymes influence tumor immunity by indirectly regulating the recruitment and function of immune cells. PDK4-driven mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation may stimulate antigen presentation and cytokine secretion, thereby promoting anti-tumor immune responses. In contrast, the upregulation of PDK4 in advanced gastric cancer may contribute to the establishment of an immunosuppressive microenvironment, facilitating immune evasion. Further investigation into the role of PDK4 in the intersection of metabolism and immunity, especially its potential synergistic effect with immune checkpoint inhibitors(ICIs), is of great significance. Zhang et al. [53] demonstrated a significant negative correlation between PDK4 expression and PD-L1 levels. Further experiments revealed that specific knockdown of PDK4 markedly increased PD-L1 protein expression in tumor cells. Additionally, inhibition of PDK activity enhanced acetyl-CoA production, thereby elevating histone acetylation levels. The study further showed that increased acetyl-CoA generation, coupled with suppression of histone deacetylation, significantly upregulated PD-L1 expression in gastric cancer cells, which enhanced the therapeutic efficacy of ICIs. This immunometabolic interaction is further supported by studies on sphingolipid metabolism, in which metabolic enzymes remodel the immune microenvironment throughlipid-mediated signaling—a mechanism that parallels the role of PDK4 in gastric cancer [54].

This study has several limitations. First, it is based on retrospective data from public databases; future functional validation through rigorously designed in vivo (e.g., PDK4-knockout murine models) and in vitro experiments (e.g., CRISPR-Cas9-mediated PDK4 overexpression/knockdown in gastric cancer cell lines) will be essential to confirm PDK4’s mechanistic role in tumor progression and metabolic reprogramming. Second, the immune infiltration analysis predominantly relies on bulk RNA-seq data, which may mask cell-type-specific PDK4 activity. Single-cell sequencing and spatial transcriptomics could overcome this limitation. Third, the unique anatomical structure and microbiome environment of the stomach may exert specific effects on tumor cell metabolism. Future research should investigate the interaction between PDK4 and gut microbiome metabolites. Additionally, in this study, we selected samples based on existing clinical data from public databases and performed subgroup comparisons and balanced analyses of key clinical characteristics. However, potential biases due to sample size differences may still exist. To address this, we plan to collect more samples and validate our findings in larger, self-tested cohorts in the future.

In conclusion, we performed a multi-omics analysis to comprehensively assess the bioinformatics function of PDK4 in gastric cancer, shedding light on its role in cancer progression and prognosis, and providing a foundation for further studies on cancer progression, metabolic regulation, and the development of targeted therapies.

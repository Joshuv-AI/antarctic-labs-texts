Preserving fermionic statistics for single-particle approximations in microscopic quantum master equations
Mikayla Z. Fahrenbruch, Anthony W. Schlimgen, Kade Head-Marsden

Abstract
Microscopic master equations have gained traction for the dissipative treatment of molecular spin and solid-state systems for quantum technologies. Single particle approximations are often invoked to treat these systems, which can lead to unphysical evolution when combined with master equation approaches. We present a mathematical constraint on the system-environment parameters to ensure that microscopically-derived Markovian master equations preserve fermionic, N N -representable statistics when applied to reduced systems. We demonstrate these constraints for the recently derived unified master equation and universal Lindblad equation, along with the Redfield master equation for cases when positivity issues are not present. For operators that break the constraint, we explore the addition of Pauli factors to recover N N -representability. This work promotes feasible applications of novel microscopic master equations for realistic chemical systems.

II
Theory

II.1
Approximate 1-body master equations

A general master equation takes the form,

\frac{d\rho(t)}{dt}=-i[H_{\mathrm{S}},\rho(t)]+\mathcal{D}(\rho(t)),

(1)

in atomic units, where
\rho(t)
is the system density matrix,
H_{\mathrm{S}}
is the system Hamitonian,
i
is the imaginary number,
[\cdot,\cdot]
is the commutator, and
\mathcal{D}(\cdot)
is the dissipative superoperator in Hilbert space. We can rewrite Eq. (
1
) in superoperator form,

\frac{d\rho(t)}{dt}=\mathcal{L}(\rho(t)),

(2)

where
\mathcal{L}(\cdot)
represents both the Hamiltonian and dissipative components. These types of equations are generally applied to the
N
-body density matrix, which is positive semidefinite, Hermitian, normalized, and antisymmetric with respect to fermionic particle exchange.
Coleman 1963
;
Mazziotti 2012
The size of the Hilbert space grows factorially with the number of electrons, quickly becoming computationally intractable. This exponential scaling can be overcome by considering the 1-RDM,
{}^{1}\rho
, where the
N
-electron density matrix is integrated over all but one electron. The 1-RDM scales linearly in system size.
Coleman 1963
From the anticommutation relation for fermions, the 1-hole RDM,
{{}^{1}q}
, can be expressed as a linear functional of the 1-RDM,

\mathds{1}={{}^{1}\rho}+{{}^{1}q},

(3)

where
\mathds{1}
is the identity. The 1-RDM is
N
-representable if and only if both
{{}^{1}\rho}
and
{{}^{1}q}
are Hermitian, positive semidefinite, and obey Eq. (
3
).
Chakraborty and Mazziotti 2014
;
Coleman 1963
For electrons,
N
-representability of the 1-RDM implies the Pauli exclusion principle. Previous work has considered the implications of these relations in the context of the Lindblad equation, where necessary and sufficient conditions for
N
-representable 1-RDM dynamics were derived.
Head-Marsden and Mazziotti 2015
Here, we consider the case of a general microscopic master equation that preserves positivity.

To determine the dynamics of the system 1-hole RDM, the 1-particle RDM can be rewritten in terms of the 1-hole RDM,

\frac{d(\mathds{1}-\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 4.48613pt\mathopen{\vphantom{q}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{1}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 4.48613pt\mathopen{\vphantom{q}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{1}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 5.45595pt\mathopen{\vphantom{q}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{1}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 6.17108pt\mathopen{\vphantom{q}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{1}}}}q_{\mathrm{S}}(t))}{dt}=\mathcal{L}(\mathds{1}-\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 4.48613pt\mathopen{\vphantom{q}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{1}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 4.48613pt\mathopen{\vphantom{q}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{1}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 5.45595pt\mathopen{\vphantom{q}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{1}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 6.17108pt\mathopen{\vphantom{q}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{1}}}}q_{\mathrm{S}}(t)).

(4)

The anticommutation relation implies the constraint,

\mathcal{L}(\mathds{1})=0,

(5)

or that
\mathcal{L}(\cdot)
corresponds to a unital map which preserves the identity of the reduced system space. If the reduced dynamics are generated by a unital CPTP map, then the particle and hole are guaranteed to each remain positive-semidefinite and have complementary evolutions, thus ensuring an
N
-representable state.

II.2
Microscopic equations and fermionic constraints

II.2.1
Redfield master equation

Under the Born-Markov approximation,
Breuer and Petruccione 2010
the Redfield master equation can be written in the Schrödinger picture as,

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{\mathrm{S}}=-i

\displaystyle[H_{\mathrm{S}}+H_{\mathrm{LS}},\rho_{\mathrm{S}}]+\sum_{\omega\omega^{\prime}}\sum_{\alpha\beta}\gamma_{\alpha\beta}(\omega,\omega^{\prime})

(6)

\displaystyle\times\left(A_{\alpha\omega}\rho_{\mathrm{S}}A^{\dagger}_{\beta\omega^{\prime}}-\dfrac{1}{2}\{A_{\beta\omega^{\prime}}^{\dagger}A_{\alpha\omega},\rho_{\mathrm{S}}\}\right),

where
\gamma_{\alpha\beta}(\omega,\omega^{\prime})
are decay rates associated with each pair of Bohr frequencies
\omega,\omega^{\prime}
of the system Hamiltonian, and
\alpha,\beta
denote different system operators associated with the same Bohr frequency. The Lamb-shift Hamiltonian,
H_{\mathrm{LS}}
, accounts for a renormalization of the system energy levels due to the environment, and the decay rates and Lamb shifts are derived from a spectral function,
\Gamma_{\alpha\beta}(\omega)
. The system-environment coupling operators,
A_{\alpha\omega}
, associated with Bohr frequency
\omega
, are defined as,

A_{\alpha\omega}=\sum_{jj^{\prime}}\Pi_{j}A_{\alpha}\Pi_{j^{\prime}}

(7)

where
\omega=\varepsilon_{j^{\prime}}-\varepsilon_{j}
are differences between system eigenvalues, and
\Pi_{j}=|e_{j}\rangle\langle e_{j}|
are eigenprojectors. Due to the completeness of the system eigenbasis, a summation over all channels returns the original system operator,

A_{\alpha}=\sum_{\omega}A_{\alpha\omega}.

(8)

The system operator is projected into channels that correspond to all possible Bohr frequencies of
H_{\mathrm{S}}
, and each channel is associated with a microscopically-derived decay rate. This form of the RME requires
A_{\alpha}=A_{\alpha}^{\dagger}
to enact detailed balance.

Transitioning to a single-particle framework, embedding the
N
-representability constraint from Eq. (
3
) into the RME and invoking orthogonality of the system eigenbasis yields,

0=\sum_{\omega}\sum_{\alpha\beta}\gamma_{\alpha\beta}(\omega,\omega)\left[{{}^{1}A}_{\alpha\omega},{{}^{1}A}_{\beta\omega}^{\dagger}\right],

(9)

where
{{}^{1}A}_{\alpha}
is a reduced system operator. This constraint dictates which system-environment coupling parameters guarantee physical dynamics for multiple electrons in a 1-body framework, provided the populations remain nonnegative. To avoid the risk of negative probabilities, we turn to recently developed microscopic MEs of GKSL form that guarantee positivity.

II.2.2
Unified master equation

The unified master equation was recently developed to better model near degeneracies while retaining the positivity of the secular, or Davies, equation.
Trushechkin 2021
;
Davies 1974
;
Davies 1976
Its derivation relies on a clustering approach depicted in Figure
1
.
Trushechkin 2021
;
Gerry and Segal 2023
;
Vaaranta and Cattaneo 2025
The nearby Bohr frequencies are grouped into a cluster
\mathcal{F}_{\overline{\omega}}
such that each cluster is well-separated. Then, each channel associated with
\mathcal{F}_{\overline{\omega}}
is assigned the same decay rate
\gamma_{\alpha\beta}(\overline{\omega})
, where
\overline{\omega}
is the center of the cluster. A secular approximation is applied to the set of averaged frequencies, preserving only the coupling between channels associated with the same cluster; interactions between channels of well-separated frequencies are cleaved. This process results in the UME,

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{\mathrm{S}}=-i

\displaystyle[H_{\mathrm{S}}+\tilde{H}_{\mathrm{LS}},\rho_{\mathrm{S}}]+\sum_{\overline{\omega}}\sum_{\omega\omega^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{\overline{\omega}}}\sum_{\alpha\beta}\gamma_{\alpha\beta}(\overline{\omega})

(10)

\displaystyle\times\left(A_{\alpha\omega}\rho_{\mathrm{S}}A_{\beta\omega^{\prime}}^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}\{A_{\beta\omega^{\prime}}^{\dagger}A_{\alpha\omega},\rho_{\mathrm{S}}\}\right),

presented in the Schrödinger picture. The Lamb-shifts and system operators are identical to the RME whereas the decay rates and Lamb-shift Hamiltonian,
\tilde{H}_{\mathrm{LS}}
, are now cluster-dependent.
Trushechkin 2021
The UME constraint is,

0=\sum_{\overline{\omega}}\sum_{\omega\in\mathcal{F}_{\overline{\omega}}}\sum_{\alpha\beta}\gamma_{\alpha\beta}(\overline{\omega})\left[{{}^{1}A}_{\alpha\omega},{{}^{1}A}_{\beta\omega}^{\dagger}\right],

(11)

and is a condition on channels of the system-environment operator. Since the UME is CPTP, this condition guarantees that the dynamics are
N
-representable for the 1-RDM.

II.2.3
Universal Lindblad equation

The universal Lindblad equation was introduced around the same time as the UME to achieve positivity without assumptions about spacings between system energy levels.
Nathan and Rudner 2020
The derivation begins with an integro-differential form of the RME, but a different Markov approximation sends
t_{0}
to
-\infty
rather than 0.
Nathan and Rudner 2020
;
Nathan and Rudner 2021
;
Tello Breuer, Becker, and Eckardt 2024
The final result is an equation of GKSL form that induces the same order of magnitude of error as the RME. For a single noise source, the ULE can be written as,

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{\mathrm{S}}=-i

\displaystyle[H_{\mathrm{S}}+\hat{H}_{\mathrm{LS}},\rho_{\mathrm{S}}]+\sum_{\omega\omega^{\prime}}\sum_{\alpha\beta}\hat{\gamma}_{\alpha}(\omega)\hat{\gamma}^{*}_{\beta}(\omega^{\prime})

(12)

\displaystyle\times\left(A_{\alpha\omega}\rho_{\mathrm{S}}A^{\dagger}_{\beta\omega^{\prime}}-\dfrac{1}{2}\{A_{\beta\omega^{\prime}}^{\dagger}A_{\alpha\omega},\rho_{\mathrm{S}}\}\right),

again in the Schrödinger picture. The notation of the original work has been adapted for easier comparison with the UME and RME, and more detail can be found in Appendix
A
. The ULE preserves
N
-representability in the 1-RDM framework if,

0=\sum_{\omega}\sum_{\alpha\beta}\hat{\gamma}_{\alpha}(\omega)\hat{\gamma}^{*}_{\beta}(\omega)\left[{{}^{1}A}_{\alpha\omega},{{}^{1}A}_{\beta\omega}^{\dagger}\right].

(13)

II.3
Pauli blocking for microscopic master equations

There are system operators which violate the
N
-representability constraints but remain necessary to model population transfer; Pauli blocking is one method to address this. A Pauli factor for the
i^{\text{th}}
population of the 1-RDM is,

f({{}^{1}\rho}^{ii}_{\mathrm{S}})=\chi-{{}^{1}\rho}^{ii}_{\mathrm{S}},

(14)

where
\chi
is the maximum occupation of an orbital;
\chi
equals 1 for spin-orbitals and 2 for spatial-orbitals. When positivity issues are not present, the Pauli factors provide an upper-bound on the eigenvalues of a 1-RDM. Since Pauli blocking preserves Hamiltonian evolution, only the Hilbert space dissipator,
\mathcal{D}({{}^{1}\rho}_{\mathrm{S}})
, is presented for each of the fermionic adaptations. The following definition is used for convenience,

{{}^{1}T}_{ijlk}^{\alpha\beta}={{}^{1}A}_{\alpha\omega_{ij}}{{}^{1}\rho}_{\mathrm{S}}{{}^{1}A}^{\dagger}_{\beta\omega_{lk}}-\dfrac{1}{2}\{{{}^{1}A}_{\beta\omega_{lk}}^{\dagger}{{}^{1}A}_{\alpha\omega_{ij}},{{}^{1}\rho}_{\mathrm{S}}\}.

(15)

The Pauli-blocked dissipator for the RME is,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}({{}^{1}\rho}_{\mathrm{S}})=\sum_{i\not=j,lk}\sum_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle f({{}^{1}\rho}^{ii}_{\mathrm{S}})\gamma_{\alpha\beta}(\omega_{ij},\omega_{lk}){{}^{1}T}_{ijlk}^{\alpha\beta}

(16)

\displaystyle\quad+\gamma_{\alpha\beta}(0,\omega_{lk}){{}^{1}T}_{iilk}^{\alpha\beta}.

The dissipator of the Pauli-blocked UME is,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}({{}^{1}\rho}_{\mathrm{S}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\overline{\omega}}\sum_{i\not=j,lk}\sum_{\alpha\beta}f({{}^{1}\rho}^{ii}_{\mathrm{S}})\gamma_{\alpha\beta}(\overline{\omega}){{}^{1}T}_{ijlk}^{\alpha\beta}

(17)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{\overline{\omega}\in\mathcal{F}_{\overline{0}}}\sum_{ilk}\sum_{\alpha\beta}\gamma_{\alpha\beta}(\overline{\omega}){{}^{1}T}_{iilk}^{\alpha\beta},

such that
\omega_{ij},\omega_{lk}\in\mathcal{F}_{\overline{\omega}}
, and the same adaptation to the ULE dissipator yields,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}({{}^{1}\rho}_{\mathrm{S}})=\sum_{i\not=j,lk}\sum_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle f({{}^{1}\rho}^{ii}_{\mathrm{S}}){\hat{\gamma}}_{\alpha}(\omega_{ij}){\hat{\gamma}}^{*}_{\beta}(\omega_{lk}){{}^{1}T}_{ijlk}^{\alpha\beta}

(18)

\displaystyle\quad+\hat{\gamma}_{\alpha}(0){\hat{\gamma}}^{*}_{\beta}(\omega_{lk}){{}^{1}T}_{iilk}^{\alpha\beta}.

Pauli factors in Eqs. (
16
), (
17
), and (
18
) annihilate population transfer channels once the target states are maximally occupied, without compromising the pure dephasing channels.
Pelc et al. 2024
;
Cohen et al. 2009
The Pauli factors induce nonlinear master equation forms, yet in spite of the resulting non-GKSL structure, they have been shown to preserve positivity.
Rosati et al. 2014
Moreover, the condition in Eq. (
4
) once again requires a unital map in the spin-adapted framework. The requirement that
\mathcal{L}(\chi\cdot\mathds{1})=0
is trivially satisfied by Pauli-blocked CP maps, guaranteeing
N
-representable dynamics for all system-environment coupling operators.

IV
Discussion and conclusions

Along with the widely used RME, this work explores the UME and ULE to treat fermionic dynamics in a 1-body framework common for molecular and material applications. As demonstrated for the selected equations, direct implementation of microscopic MEs under this approximation can be problematic for modeling of population transfer. We present a constraint on the reduced system Liouvillian that is necessary and sufficient to guarantee
N
-representability of the propagated 1-RDM, assuming that positivity is preserved. This constraint is satisfied for a linear master equation if and only if the mapping is unital. Due to detailed balance, a population transfer operator will not satisfy this constraint, unless at infinite temperature. Although not explicitly discussed, pure-dephasing operators often preserve
N
-representability due to their diagonal forms. We recover physical dynamics for cases that violate Pauli-exclusion via the addition of Pauli factors to the RME, UME, and ULE for the first time. The fermionic constraints pertaining to the Pauli-blocked master equations are inherently satisfied, regardless of the system-environment parameters.

The constraints presented here highlight that the traditional many-body forms of microscopic master equations are not inherently designed for reduced fermionic systems. Even when the Pauli-exclusion principle is not violated for the 1-RDM, a linear nonunital map will not provide a symmetric evolution for the 1-hole RDM. Care needs to be taken when interfacing microscopic equations with single-particle approximations, such as DFT and Hartree-Fock, for many-electron systems. In general, this work formally establishes a foundation for feasible and physical modeling of reduced fermionic systems by extending the many-body microscopic framework for single-particle approximations.

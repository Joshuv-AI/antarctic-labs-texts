I
Introduction

The hadronic decays of charmonium provide an ideal platform for testing the interplay between perturbative and non-perturbative Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD). The charmonium mass scale lies in a transition region where QCD dynamics evolve from the perturbative regime at high energies to the non-perturbative regime at low energies.

The complexity of QCD in the charmonium-mass region is highlighted by significant discrepancies between perturbative Quantum Chromodynamics (pQCD) calculations and experimental measurements. A prime example is the long-standing puzzle associated with the pQCD helicity selection rule (HSR)
pQCD_1
;
pQCD_2
;
pQCD_3
. Derived from the quark helicity conservation principle in point-like interactions, the HSR predicts that helicity-violating decays of the
0^{-+}
pseudoscalar charmonium state
\eta_{c}
into baryon–antibaryon (
B\bar{B}
) final states should be forbidden
HSR
. However, experimental measurements consistently contradict this prediction, with measured branching fractions that are comparable to those of
J/\psi
decays
BESIII:2019eyx
;
BESIII:2012xdg
;
BESIII:2012gcb
;
PDG
. These discrepancies suggest the presence of previously unaccounted-for mechanisms in pQCD, making HSR violation a critical issue to be addressed.

Several theoretical frameworks have been proposed to account for this phenomenon. Models such as the quark–diquark model
Anselmino:1987du
;
Anselmino:1991mc
, charmonium–glueball mixing
Anselmino:1993yg
, the quark-pair creation model
Ping:2004sh
, and the color-octet mechanism
Wong:1999hc
attribute HSR violation to the internal structure of charmonium. These approaches allow otherwise forbidden decays to occur, yielding nonzero branching fractions that can be comparable to those of HSR-allowed decays. Other theoretical studies suggest that such transitions may arise from non-perturbative corrections
Anselmino:1992jd
;
Murgia:1996bh
. Meanwhile, the Intermediate Meson Loop (IML) model
Zhang:2009kr
;
Liu:2009vv
provides a dynamical mechanism for evading the HSR by introducing long-range effects through virtual charmed-meson loops. In this model, the decay proceeds not as a point-like interaction but via a two-step process: the
\eta_{c}
first decays into a virtual charmed-meson pair
Liu:2010um
(e.g.
D\bar{D}
), which subsequently forms the final-state baryon–antibaryon pair through the exchange of light mesons. This long-range mechanism effectively circumvents the assumptions underlying the pQCD HSR and can result in sizeable branching fractions for otherwise forbidden decays.

Despite their qualitative success in explaining HSR violation, these theoretical frameworks generally suffer from large quantitative uncertainties in their predictions of branching fractions. Consequently, experimental measurements of additional helicity-forbidden decay channels, particularly those not yet observed, are essential for testing the validity and limitations of existing models.

To further probe these unresolved issues, a measurement of the previously unobserved decay
\eta_{c}\to\Xi^{0}\bar{\Xi}^{0}
is of particular importance, as it is the isospin partner of
\eta_{c}\to\Xi^{-}\bar{\Xi}^{+}
. Although the
\eta_{c}
cannot be directly produced in
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation due to quantum number conservation, it is copiously produced via radiative decays of the
J/\psi
resonance. Therefore, the large data sample of
(10087\pm 44)\times 10^{6}

J/\psi
events collected by the BESIII experiment
BESIII:2021cxx
enables such a measurement for the first time. In this paper, we report the first observation of this decay and a measurement of its branching fraction, using
\eta_{c}
mesons produced through the radiative process
J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{c}
.

II
BESIII Experiment and Monte Carlo Simulation

The BESIII detector
Ablikim:2009aa
records symmetric
e^{+}e^{-}
collisions provided by the BEPCII storage ring
Yu:2016cof
, which operates with a peak luminosity of
1.1\times 10^{33}\;\text{cm}^{-2}\text{s}^{-1}
in the center of mass energy range from 1.84 to 4.95 GeV. BESIII has collected large data samples in this energy region
Ablikim:2019hff
;
EcmsMea
;

The cylindrical core of the BESIII detector covers 93% of the full solid angle and consists of a helium-based multilayer drift chamber (MDC), a plastic scintillator time-of-flight system (TOF), and a CsI(Tl) electromagnetic calorimeter (EMC), which are all enclosed in a superconducting solenoidal magnet providing a 1.0 T magnetic field. The magnetic field was 0.9 T in 2012, which affects 13% of the total
J/\psi
data. The solenoid is supported by an
octagonal flux-return yoke with resistive plate counter muon
identification modules interleaved with steel. The charged-particle momentum resolution at
1~{\rm GeV}/c
is
0.5\%
, and the

{\rm d}E/{\rm d}x
resolution is
6\%
for electrons from Bhabha scattering. The EMC measures photon energies with a resolution of
2.5\%
(
5\%
) at
1
GeV in the barrel (end cap) region. The time resolution in the plastic scintillator TOF barrel region is 68 ps, while that in the end cap region was 110 ps. The end cap TOF system was upgraded in 2015 using multigap resistive plate chamber technology, providing a time resolution of 60 ps, which benefits 87% of the data used in this analysis
etof
.

Monte Carlo (MC) simulated data samples produced with a
geant4
-based
geant4
software package, which
includes the geometric description of the BESIII detector and the
detector response, are used to determine detection efficiencies
and to estimate backgrounds. The simulation models the beam
energy spread and initial state radiation in the
e^{+}e^{-}

annihilations with the generator
kkmc

ref:kkmc
. The inclusive MC sample includes both the production of the
J/\psi

resonance and the continuum processes incorporated in
kkmc
.
All particle decays are modelled with
evtgen

ref:evtgen
using branching fractions
either taken from the
Particle Data Group (PDG)
PDG
, when available,
or otherwise estimated with
lundcharm

ref:lundcharm
.
Final state radiation from charged final state particles is incorporated using the
photos
package
photos2
. The potential background events in the inclusive MC are examined using the event-type analysis tool TopoAna
Zhou:2020ksj
. To assess the detection efficiency, signal MC events are generated following the decay chain
J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{c},\eta_{c}\to\Xi^{0}\bar{\Xi}^{0},\Xi^{0}\to\Lambda\pi^{0},\bar{\Xi}^{0}\to\bar{\Lambda}\pi^{0},\Lambda\to p\pi^{-},\bar{\Lambda}\to\bar{p}\pi^{+}

\pi^{0}\to\gamma\gamma

III
Event Selection

We reconstruct the signal candidates through the decay chain described in the previous section, resulting in the final state including
\gamma\gamma\gamma\gamma\gamma p\bar{p}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
.

Events with at least four charged tracks with total charge zero are selected.
Charged tracks detected in the MDC are required to satisfy the polar angle (
\theta
) condition
|\cos\theta|<0.93
, where
\theta
is defined with respect to the
z
-axis, the symmetry axis of the MDC.
Given the relatively long lifetimes of
\Lambda
and
\Xi^{0}

MC studies indicate that the proton and pion from
\Lambda
decay are well separated kinematically since the proton carries most of the energy. The charged tracks with momentum above 0.247 GeV
/c
are supposed to be protons, while those below 0.247 GeV
/c
are pions.
The
\Lambda(\bar{\Lambda})
candidates are reconstructed from

p\pi^{-}(\bar{p}\pi^{+})
combinations. They are constrained to originate from a common vertex, with those having

\chi^{2}<200
kept for further analysis. Furthermore, the mass window of
p\pi^{-}(\bar{p}\pi^{+})
is set to be
(1.111,1.120)~\text{GeV}/c^{2}
.

Photon candidates are identified using isolated showers in the EMC and required to contain at least five photons per event. The deposited energy of each shower must be more than 25 MeV in the barrel region (
|\cos\theta|<0.80
) and more than 50 MeV in the end cap region (
0.86<|\cos\theta|<0.92
).
To exclude showers that originate from
charged tracks,
the angles subtended by the EMC shower and the position of the closest charged track at the EMC
must be greater than 10 degrees as measured from the interaction point. A more stringent requirement of 20 degrees between the photon candidate and anti-proton is applied to exclude the photons from anti-proton annihilation.
To suppress electronic noise and showers unrelated to the event, the difference between the EMC time and the event start time is required to be within
[0,700]
ns.
The
\pi^{0}
candidate is reconstructed from a photon pair with
M_{\gamma\gamma}\in(0.098,0.165)
GeV/
c^{2}
following the published study
BESIII:2023drj
. A one-constraint kinematic fit is performed on the photon pairs by constraining their invariant mass to the nominal
\pi^{0}
mass
PDG
.

A four-constraint (4C) kinematic fit is performed on the

hypothesis, constraining the total reconstructed four-momentum to that of the initial
e^{+}e^{-}
. When the number of photons exceeds five, all possible combinations are tested, and the combination yielding the minimum

of 4C kinematic fit (

(vi)

(vii)

Detector resolution.
The systematic uncertainty due to resolution is estimated by convolving an additional small Gaussian function in the fit. The relative difference between the fit with and without this convolution is taken as the systematic uncertainty.

(viii)

Efficiency curve.
The systematic uncertainty of the efficiency curve is evaluated by comparing two different functional forms. The nominal form is modeled as a piecewise function consisting of a third-order polynomial followed by a first-order polynomial. For an alternative form, it is changed to a 4th-order polynomial followed by a 2nd-order polynomial. The relative difference between the two fits is considered as the systematic uncertainty.

(ix)

Shape of
J/\psi\to\gamma\Xi^{0}\bar{\Xi}^{0}

J/\psi\to\gamma\Xi^{0}\bar{\Xi}^{0}

J/\psi\to\gamma\Xi^{0}\bar{\Xi}^{0}

{\cal N}(m)
. Using these parameters as input, we recalculate the true signal yield, and the relative standard deviation of the recalculated true signal yield values is taken as the systematic uncertainty.

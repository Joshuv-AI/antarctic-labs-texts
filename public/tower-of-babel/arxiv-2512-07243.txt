Function-Correcting Codes for Insertion-Deletion Channels
Anamika Singh, Abhay Kumar Singh

Abstract
In coding theory, handling errors that occur when symbols are inserted or deleted from a transmitted message is a long-standing challenge. Optimising redundancy for insertion and deletion channels remains a key open problem, with significant importance for applications in DNA data storage and document exchange. Recently, a new coding framework known as function-correcting codes has been proposed to address the challenge of optimising redundancy while preserving particular functions of the message. This framework has gained attention due to its potential applications in machine learning systems and long-term archival data storage. To address the problem of redundancy optimisation in insertion and deletion channels, we propose a new coding framework called function-correcting codes for insertion and deletion channels. In this paper, we introduce the notions of function-correcting insertion codes, function-correcting deletion codes, and function-correcting insdel (insertion–deletion) codes, and we demonstrate that these three formulations are equivalent. We then introduce insdel distance matrices and irregular insdel-distance codes, and further derive lower and upper bounds on the optimal redundancy achievable by function-correcting codes for insdel channels. Furthermore, we establish Gilbert-Varshamov and Plotkin-like bounds on the length of irregular insdel-distance codes. By utilising the relation between optimal redundancy and the length of irregular insdel distance codes, we provide another simplified lower bound on optimal redundancy. We subsequently find bounds on optimal redundancy of FCIDCs for various classes of functions, including locally bounded functions, VT syndrome functions, the number-of-runs function, and the maximum-run-length function.



I
Introduction

Traditional error-correcting codes(ECCs) are designed to enable the decoder to recover the transmitted message exactly. However, in many real-world situations, the decoder simply needs to compute a certain function of the message rather than reconstructing the complete message. The authors of
[
4
]
were inspired by this realization to develop a new class of codes called function-correcting codes (FCCs), which encode the message so that the decoder can reliably compute the desired function value, even in the presence of errors, with substantially less redundancy than required by classical ECCs for full message recovery.

FCCs have been commonly studied under systematic encoding, which is important in applications such as distributed computing and long-term archival storage, where preserving the original data is essential. Since redundancy is the primary and most crucial factor for FCCs, it becomes crucial to precisely determine optimal redundancy—that is, the minimum number of additional bits required to guarantee correct computation of the intended function. To determine optimal redundancy for both generic and specific functions, the authors of
[
4
]
introduced the concept of irregular-distance codes, which are closely connected to the redundancy patterns of FCCs. They established equivalence between optimal redundancy of FCCs and shortest achievable length of irregular-distance codes. Using this equivalence, the authors derived redundancy bounds for arbitrary functions and subsequently used these results to obtain tight or near-tight bounds for several important function classes, including locally binary bounded functions, Hamming weight functions, Hamming weight distribution functions, and real-valued functions.

I-A

Motivation

The nature of the underlying channel noise fundamentally determines how function values are distorted during transmission. By designing FCCs that are tailored to specific channel models, one can achieve reliable function computation in scenarios where recovering the entire message is either unnecessary or prohibitively costly in redundancy. Moreover, such channel-specific constructions provide deeper insight into the way different error processes affect function evaluation. Motivated by this observation, we focus on selecting suitable channels and associated functions to construct function-correcting codes with improved redundancy. In particular, we consider insertion–deletion(insdel) channels, where errors occur when symbols are inserted into or deleted from a transmitted message. Such errors disrupt the alignment between transmitted and received sequences, creating a form of noise that traditional error-correcting codes designed primarily for handling substitution errors are not well-suited to handle. As these types of errors frequently arise in biological systems, modern storage devices, and asynchronous communication settings, the study of insdel channels has become increasingly important in developing robust and efficient coding techniques.
The study of insertion–deletion errors began in the 1960s

[

, when V. Levenshtein introduced the edit distance, defining the minimum number of insertions, deletions, or substitutions required to transform one string into another. This concept established the mathematical basis for analyzing synchronization errors, although early work focused mainly on error detection and correction rather than determining full channel capacities. Later, practical coding schemes for handling such errors began to emerge, most notably the Varshamov–Tenengolts (VT) codes, which were originally introduced in
[
14
]
and expanded over subsequent decades, enabling reliable single-deletion correction. During this period, researchers also explored the use of synchronizing sequences to help maintain alignment between transmitted and received data, marking a significant step toward systematically managing insertion-deletion errors. Consequently, the development of function-correcting codes under the insdel metric represents both a substantial theoretical milestone and a vital practical capability for next-generation communication and computation systems. By offering resilience against synchronization errors—most notably insertions and deletions—these codes broaden the scope of traditional error-control methods beyond the limitations of the Hamming metric, enabling more reliable transmission, storage, and processing of information in emerging high-density, high-throughput environments.
The nature of the underlying noise fundamentally influences how function values are distorted during transmission. A function that is robust to substitution errors may behave unpredictably under insertions, deletions, erasures, or symbol-dependent noise, and the structural interactions between the function and the channel can vary dramatically. Developing FCCs tailored to specific channels enables reliable computation in settings where full message recovery is either unnecessary or prohibitively expensive, while also revealing deeper insights into how functions transform under diverse error processes. Such channel-aware FCC frameworks are essential for modern applications ranging from asynchronous communication and DNA storage to distributed sensing and edge computing, where computation must remain accurate despite highly nonuniform and context-dependent noise.

I-C

Contributions

Theoretical work on function-correcting codes has traditionally focused on channels such as the Hamming, symbol-pair, and Lee channels, all of which model substitution-type errors: a received symbol may differ from the transmitted one, but the positions of the symbols remain aligned.

The insertion–deletion (insdel) channel is fundamentally different because it introduces synchronization errors. Instead of altering symbol values, the channel may insert or delete symbols, shifting the entire sequence and breaking positional alignment between sender and receiver. As a result, a single insertion or deletion can misalign all subsequent symbols.
Errors are measured under edit distance, not Hamming-type metrics. Decoding is considerably more difficult, since the code must recover both the correct symbols and the correct alignment.

These properties make designing and analyzing function-correcting codes for the insdel channel significantly more challenging than for traditional substitution-error channels. We develop the theoretical foundations of function-correcting codes for the insertion–deletion (insdel) channel and derive both lower and upper bounds on the optimal redundancy that can be achieved by such codes. Furthermore, we establish Gilbert–Varshamov-type and Plotkin-like bounds on the lengths of irregular insdel-distance codes. By leveraging the relationship between optimal redundancy and the lengths of irregular insdel-distance codes, we also provide a simplified lower bound on optimal redundancy.

Building on these results, we construct explicit Function-Correcting Insertion–Deletion Codes (FCIDCs) for several classes of functions, including locally bounded functions, VT functions, the number-of-runs function, and the maximum-run-length function.

I-D

Organization

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section II provides a comprehensive review of the foundational concepts used throughout the paper. We begin by recalling the basic definitions and properties of insertion–deletion (insdel) codes, including the insdel metric and several equivalent formulations that are relevant to our analysis. In Section III, we introduce the framework of function-correcting deletion codes, function-correcting insertion codes, and function-correcting insertion–deletion (insdel) codes, which are designed to recover the value of a target function in the presence of deletion, insertion, or combined insertion–deletion errors. We then present and discuss irregular insdel-distance codes, highlighting their significance in characterizing optimal redundancy and in deriving key performance limits. Building on this framework, we derive lower and upper bounds on the optimal redundancy achievable by function-correcting codes for insdel channels. In addition, we establish Gilbert–Varshamov-type and Plotkin-like bounds on the lengths of irregular insdel-distance codes, which play a central role in analysing and understanding the redundancy of such codes. In Section IV, we apply the general theoretical results to a specific class of functions, the VT syndrome function, which plays a central role in the insertion–deletion coding. We analyze how the structure of the VT syndrome function interacts with insdel errors and derive both upper and lower bounds on the optimal redundancy required for function correction in this setting. We illustrate the general framework using three concrete classes of functions: the number-of-runs function (Section V), the maximum-run-length function (Section VI), and locally bounded functions (Section VII). For each class, we derive the corresponding redundancy values as well as the associated upper and lower bounds. Section VIII concludes the paper.

are subsequences of
w
. Let
|w|=n+s
with
s\leq t
. Embedding two length-
n
sequences into a common supersequence of length
n+s
forces their LCS to be at least
n-s
. Therefore,

\operatorname{LCS}(\psi(x),\psi(y))\geq n-s\geq n-t,

this implies the existence of a common subsequence of length
\geq n-t
. This contradicts (1). Thus
I_{t}(\psi(x))\cap I_{t}(\psi(y))=\varnothing
.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(1) is deduced from (2)
\Rightarrow
(3)
\Rightarrow
(1) via the same LCS identity.
∎

Given the equivalence among the three code formulations discussed above,
we restrict our attention, in the remainder of this paper, to
function-correcting insertion-deletion (insdel) codes
.

Next, we define the optimal redundancy of a function-correcting insdel code (FCIDC) for a function
f
as it is a key parameter and plays a pivotal role in the study of FCIDCs, as it fundamentally distinguishes them from the classically investigated error-correcting insdel codes by potentially achieving strictly lower redundancy for the same error tolerance.

Definition 10

(optimal redundancy)
.

A positive integer
r
is called the optimal redundancy of a function-correcting insdel code for a function
f
, defined by an encoding map

\psi:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k+r},

if
r
is the smallest achievable redundancy length.
The optimal redundancy is denoted by
r^{f}_{\mathrm{ID}}(k,t)
.

III-A

Irregular Insdel-Distance Codes

In this section, we define irregular insdel-distance codes and establish their relationship with FCIDCs. Leveraging this connection, we derive several general results related to FCIDCs and further obtain both lower and upper bounds on their optimal redundancy.

We first define insdel distance matrices (Definition
11
) for a function
f
, followed by irregular insdel-distance codes (Definition
12
) in which the insdel-distance between each pair of codewords should satisfy individual distance constraints.

Definition 11

(Insdel Distance Matrices)
.

Let
M,t\in\mathbb{N}
. Consider
M
binary vectors
{x_{1}},...,{x_{M}}\in{\mathbb{F}^{k}_{2}}
. Then,
{\textbf{I}_{f}^{(1)}}(t,{x_{1}},...,{x_{M}})
and

{\textbf{I}_{f}^{(2)}}(t,x_{0},\ldots,x_{M-1})
are
M\times M
insdel distance matrices corresponding to function
f
with entries as follows:

\scriptstyle[\mathbf{I}_{f}^{(1)}(t,x_{1},\dots,x_{M})]_{ij}=\begin{cases}[2t+2-d_{\text{ID}}(x_{i},x_{j})]^{+},&\text{if }f(x_{i})\neq f(x_{j}),\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

and

\scriptstyle[\mathbf{I}_{f}^{(2)}(t,x_{1},\dots,x_{M})]_{ij}=\begin{cases}[2t+2+2k-d_{\text{ID}}(x_{i},x_{j})]^{+},&\text{if }f(x_{i})\neq f(x_{j}),\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Let

Definition 12

The results in the next two lemmas play a crucial role in establishing the relationship between the optimal redundancy of FCIDCs and the lengths of irregular insdel-distance codes.

Lemma 7
.

Let
x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k+r}
and
y=(y_{1},y_{2})\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k+r}
where
x_{1},y_{1}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}
and
x_{2},y_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{r}
then,

\begin{multlined}LCS(x_{1},y_{1})+LCS(x_{2},y_{2})\leq LCS(x,y)\leq\\
LCS(x_{1},y_{1})+LCS(x_{2},y_{2})-min(k,r)\end{multlined}

Proof.

Let
s_{1}
be the longest common subsequence of
x_{1}
and
y_{1}
, and
s_{2}
be the longest common subsequence of
x_{2}
and
y_{2}
; then
s_{1}s_{2}
is a common subsequence of
x
and
y
. Therefore,

LCS(x,y)\geq LCS(x_{1},y_{1})+LCS(x_{2},y_{2})

for the left-hand side inequality,

LCS(x,y)
tends to become larger than
LCS(x_{1},y_{1})+LCS(x_{2},y_{2})
due to cross-part matches, i.e., symbols in
x_{1}
matching symbols in
y_{2}
or vice versa.
However, these additional matches cannot exceed the length of the shorter string, i.e., they are bounded by
min(k,r)
.
Hence,

LCS(x,y)\leq LCS(x_{1},y_{1})+LCS(x_{2},y_{2})-min(k,r)

∎

Lemma 8
.

Let
x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k+r}
and
y=(y_{1},y_{2})\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k+r}
where
x_{1},y_{1}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}
and
x_{2},y_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{r}
then,

\begin{multlined}d_{ID}(x_{1},y_{1})+d_{ID}(x_{2},y_{2})-2min(k,r)\leq d_{ID}(x,y)\\
\leq d_{ID}(x_{1},y_{1})+d_{ID}(x_{2},y_{2})\end{multlined}

(1)

Proof.

From the definition of the insdel-distance, we have

\displaystyle d_{ID}(x,y)=2(k+r)-2LCS(x,y)

\displaystyle d_{ID}(x_{1},y_{1})=2k-LCS(x_{1},y_{1})

\displaystyle d_{ID}(x_{2},y_{2})=2r-LCS(x_{2},y_{2})

multiplying
-2
to equation
7
we get:

\displaystyle-2(LCS(x_{1},y_{1})+LCS(x_{2},y_{2})-min(k,r))

\displaystyle\leq-2\cdot LCS(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq-2(LCS(x_{1},y_{1})+LCS(x_{2},y_{2}))

Adding
2(k+r)
to each part in the above inequality, we get

\displaystyle 2(k+r)-2(LCS(x_{1},y_{1})+LCS(x_{2},y_{2})-min(k,r))

\displaystyle\leq 2(k+r)-2\cdot LCS(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq 2(k+r)-2(LCS(x_{1},y_{1})+LCS(x_{2},y_{2}))

\displaystyle 2k-2\cdot LCS(x_{1},y_{1})+2r-LCS(x_{2},y_{2})-2\cdot min(k,r)

\displaystyle\leq 2(k+r)-2LCS(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq 2k-2\cdot LCS(x_{1},y_{1})+2r-LCS(x_{2},y_{2})

Therefore,

\displaystyle d_{ID}(x_{1},y_{1})+d_{ID}(x_{2},y_{2})-2min(k,r)

\displaystyle\leq d_{ID}(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq d_{ID}(x_{1},y_{1})+d_{ID}(x_{2},y_{2})

∎

The following example shows that the lower bound given in Lemma
8
is tight.

Example 4
.

Let
x=(0000,10111)\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{9}
and
y=(1111,00000)\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{9}
. Then,

d_{ID}(0000,1111)=8,\quad d_{ID}(10111,00000)=8\text{ and }d_{ID}(000010111,111100000)=8
. Hence,
d_{ID}(000010111,111100000)=d_{ID}(0000,1111)+d_{ID}(10111,00000)-2\cdot 4
.

The following theorem establishes upper and lower bounds on the optimal redundancy of FCIDCs constructed for generic functions. Moreover, it reduces the computation of
r^{f}_{ID}(k,t)
to determining the quantities
N_{ID}^{(1)}\left(\bm{I}^{(1)}_{f}(t,x_{1},\ldots,x_{2^{k}})\right)
and
N_{ID}^{(2)}\!\left(\bm{I}^{(2)}_{f}(t,u_{1},\ldots,u_{2^{k}})\right)
.

Theorem 1
.

For any function
f:\mathbb{F}^{k}_{2}\rightarrow Im(f)
we have

\scriptstyle N^{(1)}_{ID}(\bm{I}_{f}^{(1)}(t,x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{2^{k}}))\leq r_{ID}^{f}(k,t)\leq N^{(2)}_{ID}(\bm{I}_{f}^{(2)}(t,x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{2^{k}});k).

Proof.

We establish the theorem by considering the following cases.

Case 1: (Constant functions)
If
f
is a constant function, then
N^{(1)}_{ID}(\bm{I}_{f}^{(1)}(t,x_{1},\ldots,x_{2^{k}}))=0
and
N^{(2)}_{ID}(\bm{I}_{f}^{(2)}(t,x_{1},\ldots,x_{2^{k}};k))=k
. Consequently, the desired condition holds trivially, establishing the theorem in this case. Specifically, we have:

N^{(1)}_{ID}(\bm{I}_{f}^{(1)}(t))=r_{ID}^{f}(k,t)\leq N^{(2)}_{ID}(\bm{I}_{f}^{(2)}(t)).

Case 2: (Non-constant functions)
To prove that
N^{(1)}_{ID}(\bm{I}_{f}^{(1)}(t,x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{2^{k}}))\leq r_{ID}^{f}(k,t)
for a non-constant function
f
, consider a function-correcting insdel code for
f
defined by an encoding function

\psi:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k+r},\quad\bm{x_{i}}\mapsto(\bm{x_{i}},{p_{i}}),

where the redundancy
r
is optimal, i.e.,
r=r_{ID}^{f}(k,t)
. On the contrary, suppose that
N^{(1)}_{ID}(\bm{I}_{f}^{(1)}(t,x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{2^{k}}))>r_{ID}^{f}(k,t)
. This implies the existence of distinct indices
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,q^{k}\}
such that
f({x_{i}})\neq f({x_{j}})
and

d_{ID}({p_{i}},{p_{j}})<2t+2-d_{ID}({x_{i}},{x_{j}}).

Consequently, from equation
1
we obtain

d_{ID}(\psi({x_{i}}),\psi({x_{j}}))\leq d_{ID}({x_{i}},{x_{j}})+d_{ID}({p_{i}},{p_{j}})<2t+1.

This contradicts that
\psi
defines a function-correcting insdel code. Hence,

N^{(1)}_{ID}(\bm{I}_{f}^{(1)}(t,x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{2^{k}}))\leq r_{ID}^{f}(k,t).

Next, we establish the reverse inequality
r_{ID}^{f}(k,t)\leq N^{(2)}_{ID}(\bm{I}_{f}^{(2)}(t,x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{2^{k}});k)
. Let
\mathcal{P}=\{{p_{1}},\ldots,{p_{2^{k}}}\}
be a
\bm{I}_{f}^{(2)}(t,x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{2^{k}})
irregular insdel distance code of type 2 and length
N^{(2)}_{ID}(\bm{I}_{f}^{(2)}(t,x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{2^{k}});k)
, and define the encoding function

\psi:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k+r},\quad{x_{i}}\mapsto({x_{i}},{p_{i}}).

For every pair
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,q^{k}\}
with
f({x_{i}})\neq f({x_{j}})
, we have

\displaystyle d_{ID}(\psi({x_{i}}),\psi({x_{j}}))

\displaystyle\geq d_{ID}({x_{i}},{x_{j}})+d_{ID}({p_{i}},{p_{j}})

\displaystyle\quad-2\cdot min\{k,N^{(2)}_{ID}(\bm{I}_{f}^{(2)}(t,x_{1},...,x_{2^{k}};k))\}.

\displaystyle\geq d_{ID}({x_{i}},{x_{j}})+d_{ID}({p_{i}},{p_{j}})-2k

\displaystyle\geq d_{ID}({x_{i}},{x_{j}})+2t+2+2k-d_{ID}({x_{i}},{x_{j}})

\displaystyle\qquad-2k

\displaystyle=2t+2.

Thus,
\psi
defines a function-correcting insdel code for
f
with redundancy
r=N^{(2)}_{ID}(\bm{I}_{f}^{(2)}(t,x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{2^{k}});k)
, yielding

r^{f}_{ID}(k,t)\leq N^{(2)}_{ID}(\bm{I}_{f}^{(2)}(t,x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{2^{k}});k).

This completes the proof.
∎

Simplified Redundancy Lower Bounds

Using a smaller set of information vectors one can obtain lower bound on the redundancy of FCIDCs as follows:

Corollary 1
.

Consider a collection of
M
distinct binary words

For a non-constant function

r^{f}_{ID}(k,t)\geq N^{(1)}_{ID}(\bm{I}_{f}^{(2)}(2,2t-1))=t.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{P}=\{p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{2^{k}}\}
be
\bm{I}_{f}^{(1)}(t,x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{M})
-code of length
N^{(1)}_{ID}(\bm{I}_{f}^{(1)}(t,x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{2^{k}}))
then
\mathcal{P}=\{p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{M}\}
is a
\bm{I}_{f}^{(1)}(t,x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{M})
-code. Hence,

N^{(1)}_{ID}(\bm{I}_{f}^{(1)}(t,x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{M}))\leq N^{(1)}_{ID}(\bm{I}_{f}^{(1)}(t,x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{2^{k}})).

From Theorem
1
we get
r^{f}_{ID}(k,t)\geq N^{(1)}_{ID}(\bm{I}_{f}^{(1)}(t,x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{M})).

Since
E\geq 2
and from
[
4
]
[Corollary 1] we know that there exists

with

and

therefore using the inequality,

, that is there will always exist

So, in particular for

we can say

.

For arbitrary function

\mathcal{P}=\{p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{E}\}

, such that
N\geq\frac{4S}{M^{2}}
,

N_{ID}^{(1)}(I)\geq\frac{N-\sqrt{N^{2}-\frac{4SN}{M^{2}}}}{2}

where
S=\sum_{i,j:i<j}[I]_{ij}

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{P}=\{p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{n}\}
be a length
r
, type
1
irregular insdel distance code corresponding to matrix
I
and let
v
be an
N
-length binary sequence such that every codeword in
\mathcal{P}
is a subsequence of
v
. Then from Lemma
10
,
\sum_{i,j;i<j}d_{ID}(p_{i},p_{j})\leq\frac{M^{2}(N-r)r}{N}
.Since,

S=\sum_{i,j:i<j}[I]_{ij}\leq\sum_{i,j;i<j}d_{ID}(p_{i},p_{j})\leq\frac{M^{2}(N-r)r}{N}

we have

N_{ID}^{(1)}(I)=r\geq\frac{N-\sqrt{N^{2}-\frac{4SN}{M^{2}}}}{2}.

∎

Corollary 3
.

For
N\in\mathbb{N}
and any matrix
I\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{M\times M}
, such that
N=\frac{4\sum_{i,j:i<j}[I]_{ij}}{M^{2}}
,

N_{ID}^{(1)}(I)\geq\frac{2}{M^{2}}\sum_{i,j:i<j}[I]_{ij}

The result in Corollary

can also be derived from the plotkin-like bound for codes with irregular distance requirement given in

]
for the Hamming metric, together with the observation that if the insdel distance between two codewords is

\frac{d}{2}
. For completeness, we present below a lemma that formally proves this result using the aforementioned approach.

Lemma 12
.

For any insdel matrix
\bm{I}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{M\times M}
, we have

N_{ID}^{(1)}(\bm{I)}\;\geq\;\begin{cases}\dfrac{2}{M^{2}}\displaystyle\sum_{i<j}[I]_{ij},&\text{if $M$ is even},\\[11.99998pt]
\dfrac{2}{M^{2}-1}\displaystyle\sum_{i<j}[I]_{ij},&\text{if $M$ is odd}.\end{cases}

Proof.

For any
i<j
, we have from Lemma

d_{H}(p_{i},p_{j})\;\geq\;\tfrac{1}{2}\,d_{\mathrm{ID}}(p_{i},p_{j})\;\geq\;\tfrac{1}{2}\,[I]_{ij},

summing over all pairs yields

\sum_{i<j}d_{H}(p_{i},p_{j})\;\geq\;\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i<j}[I]_{ij}.

From Lemma
5
we know in case of even

\sum_{i<j}d_{H}(p_{i},p_{j})\;\leq\;r\cdot\big\lfloor M^{2}/4\big\rfloor.

and when

\sum_{i<j}d_{H}(p_{i},p_{j})\;\leq\;r\cdot\big\lfloor(M-1)^{2}/4\big\rfloor.

Combining
(1)
and
(2)
and then
(1)
and
(3)
gives

r\cdot\big\lfloor M^{2}/4\big\rfloor\;\geq\;\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i<j}[I]_{ij},

r\cdot\big\lfloor(M-1)^{2}/4\big\rfloor\;\geq\;\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i<j}[I]_{ij},

and rearranging yields the stated bound
∎

The next lemma is a variant of the Gilbert-Varshamov bound for irregular insdel-distance codes.

Lemma 13
.

For any distance matrix
\bm{I}\in\mathbb{N}^{M\times M}_{0}
and any permutation

\pi:\{1,2,\ldots,M\}\rightarrow\{1,2,\ldots,M\}
, we have

\displaystyle N^{(1)}_{\mathrm{ID}}(\bm{I})\leq\min_{r\in\mathbb{N}}\left\{r\ \middle|\ 2^{r}>\max_{j\in\{1,2,\ldots,M\}}\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}\left|B_{E}\big(r,[\bm{I}]_{\pi(i)\pi(j)}-1\big)\right|\right\},

and

\displaystyle N^{(2)}_{\mathrm{ID}}(\bm{I};K)\leq\min_{r\geq K}\left\{r\ \middle|\ 2^{r}>\max_{j\in\{1,2,\ldots,M\}}\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}\left|B_{E}\big(r,[\bm{I}]_{\pi(i)\pi(j)}-1\big)\right|\right\},

where

B_{ID}(r,t)=\{\,y\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{r}\ \big|\ d_{\mathrm{ID}}(x,y)\leq t\,\}

denotes the editing ball of radius
t
centered at a vector
x
in the insdel metric.

Proof.

We present an iterative way of selecting valid codewords for constructing an irregular insdel distance code of type 2 (type 1) for any distance matrix
\bm{I}
.
For simplicity, first choose
\pi
as the identity permutation. Select any arbitrary vector
{p_{1}}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{r}
as the first codeword of the irregular insdel distance code of type 2 (type 1) such that
r\geq K(r\in\mathbb{N})
. For the second codeword
{p_{2}}
, the distance requirement

d_{\text{ID}}({p_{1}},{p_{2}})\geq[\bm{I}]_{12}

must be satisfied. The existence of such a
{p_{2}}
is guaranteed if

2^{r}>\big|B_{ID}\big({p_{1}},[\bm{I}]_{12}-1\big)\big|,

Next, choose the third codeword
{p_{3}}
such that

d_{\text{ID}}({p_{1}},{p_{3}})\geq[\bm{I}]_{13}\quad\text{and}\quad d_{\text{ID}}({p_{2}},{p_{3}})\geq[\bm{I}]_{23}.

For
{p_{3}}
to exist, it suffices that

2^{r}>\big|B_{ID}\big({p_{1}},[\bm{I}]_{13}-1\big)\big|+\big|B_{ID}\big({p_{2}},[\bm{I}]_{23}-1\big)\big|.

Proceeding inductively, at the
j

{p_{j}}
so that for all
i<j

d_{\text{ID}}({p_{i}},{p_{j}})\geq[\bm{I}]_{ij}.

A sufficient condition for the existence of

is

2^{r}>\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}\big|B_{ID}\big({p_{i}},[\bm{I}]_{ij}-1\big)\big|.

Since the choice of codewords can be made in any order, the same argument applies for any permutation
\pi
, which proves the lemma.
∎

Maximum Run-Length Function

Another interesting function that can be defined from the run-length sequence

is the
maximum run-length function
, which gives the length of the longest block of consecutive identical bits in a given vector.

Definition 19

(Maximum Run-Length Function)
.

Let
x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}
and
R(x)=(r_{1},r_{2},\ldots,r_{m})
then the maximum run-length function is defined as:

r_{max}(x)=max_{1\leq i\leq m}r_{i}

Example 9
.

Consider the vector
u=0000101
, then the run-length vector corresponding to the vector
u
is
(4,1,1,1)
, and hence the maximum run-length of
u
is

The expressiveness for this function is given by
E=|Im(r_{max}(\cdot))|=|\{1,2,\dots,k\}|=k
.

The following result establishes a lower bound on the function distance for the maximum run-length function.

Lemma 16
.

Let
f:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}\rightarrow\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
be the maximum run-length function. Then,

d_{ID}^{f}(i,j)\geq 2\Big\lceil\frac{|i-j|}{min(i,j)+1}\Big\rceil\text{ for all }1\leq i,j\leq k.

Proof.

Let
x
be a binary vector of length
k
and maximum run-length
i
. Without loss of generality, we assume that the longest run of
x
is
0^{i}
. To derive the lower bound, we consider a binary vector
y
of length
k
and maximum run-length
j
that is as close as possible to
x
while respecting the maximum run-length constraint. In this way, the minimal possible insdel distance will occur when the long runs in
x
and
y
are of the same symbol.

Let
y^{\prime}
be a substring of
y
aligned with the run
0^{i}
. If
t
is the number of
1s
in
y^{\prime}
, then the number of zeroes in
y^{\prime}
is
i-t
. This means
y^{\prime}
can have at most
t+1
blocks of zeroes, each split by
1
and having size at most
j
. Hence, we have,

\displaystyle i-t\leq(t+1)j\implies t\geq\Big\lceil\frac{i-j}{j+1}\Big\rceil

which means in order to have a run of
0s
of length
j
,
y^{\prime}
should have at least
\Big\lceil\frac{i-j}{j+1}\Big\rceil
number of
1
.
Hence,

LCS(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\geq i-\Big\lceil\frac{i-j}{j+1}\Big\rceil

as
LCS(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
is equal to the maximum number of
0s
in
y^{\prime}
. The length of longest common subsequence of
x
and
y
can be bounded above in terms of
LCS(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
as follows:

LCS(x,y)\leq LCS(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})+(k-i)

From the formula of the insdel distance in terms of
LCS
we get:

\displaystyle d_{ID}(x,y)

\displaystyle=2k-2LCS(x,y)

\displaystyle\geq 2k-2(LCS(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})+(k-i))

\displaystyle\geq 2\Big\lceil\frac{i-j}{j+1}\Big\rceil.

∎

Remark 3
.

The above bound is tight; that is, for any integers
i,j
with
0<j<i\leq k
, there exist binary vectors
x
and
y
of length
k
with
r_{\max}(x)=i
and
r_{\max}(y)=j
for which equality is achieved.

The following example shows that the bound in Lemma

is tight.

Example 10
.

Let

and
\mathcal{S}=\{00000,00001,00010,00100,01010\}
. Then for any pair of strings

Lemma 18
.

Let

and

VII
Locally
(\lambda,\rho)_{I}
-function

Lenz et al. first studied a class of functions, called
locally binary
[
4
]
with respect to the Hamming metric and derived optimal function-correcting codes (FCCs) for them. This concept was subsequently generalized by Rajput et al. to
locally
(\lambda,\rho)
-functions
[
11
]
for the Hamming metric and later by Verma et al. for the
b
-symbol metric
[
16
]
. The significance of this class of functions stems from the fact that any function with a finite image set can be represented as a locally
(\lambda,\rho)
-function. This universality is the primary reason the theory, initially developed for the Hamming metric, has been extended to other metric spaces where FCCs are being explored. In this section, we study the class of locally
(\lambda,\rho)
-functions in the insdel metric setting.

Definition 20

(Function ball)
.

The function ball of a function
f:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}\rightarrow Im(f)
with radius
\rho
around
u
in insdel metric is defined as

B_{ID}^{f}(u,\rho)=f(B_{ID}(u,\rho))=\{f(u)|u\in B_{ID}(u,\rho)\}.

Definition 21

(Locally bounded function in insdel metric)
.

A locally
(\rho,\lambda)_{ID}
function
f:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}\rightarrow Im(f)
is a function for which
|B_{ID}^{f}(u,\rho)|\leq\lambda,\quad\forall u\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}.

The following lemma is a straightforward extension of Lemma
6
to the insdel metric setting. This result has subsequently been employed to derive an upper bound on the optimal redundancy of FCIDC corresponding to locally
(\rho,\lambda)_{ID}
-functions.

Lemma 19
.

For any locally
(\rho,\lambda)_{ID}
-function
f:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}\to Im(f)
, there exists a mapping
\mathrm{Col}_{f}:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}\to[\lambda]

such that for any
u,v\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}
with
f(u)\neq f(v)
and
d_{ID}(u,v)\leq\rho
, we have
\mathrm{Col}_{f}(u)\neq\mathrm{Col}_{f}(v)
.

We now present an upper bound on the optimal redundancy of FCIDC corresponding to locally
(\rho,\lambda)_{ID}
-functions, expressed in terms of the shortest possible length of a binary insdel code with a prescribed number of codewords and minimum insdel distance.

Theorem 3
.

Let
t
be a positive integer and
f:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}\rightarrow Im(f)
be a locally
(\lambda,2t)_{ID}
-function then
r_{ID}^{f}(k,t)\leq N^{(2)}_{ID}(\lambda,2(t+k);k)
.

Proof.

Let
f
be a locally
(\lambda,2t)_{ID}
bounded function. Then by Lemma
19
, there exists a mapping

\mathrm{Col}_{f}:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}\to[\lambda]

such that for any
u,v\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}
satisfying
f(u)\neq f(v)
and
d_{ID}(u,v)\leq 2t
, we have
\mathrm{Col}_{f}(u)\neq\mathrm{Col}_{f}(v)
.
Let
\mathcal{C}
be a binary insdel code of size
\lambda
, minimum distance
2(t+k)
, and length
N_{ID}^{(2)}((\lambda,2(t+k));k)
.
Denote the codewords of
\mathcal{C}
as
C_{1},C_{2},\ldots,C_{\lambda}
and define the encoding function

\mathrm{Enc}:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k+N_{ID}^{(2)}(\lambda,2(t+k);k)}

by

\mathrm{Enc}(u)=(u,u_{p}),\quad\text{where }u_{p}=C_{\mathrm{Col}_{f}(u)}.

We now show that the above encoding function defines an FCIDC with redundancy
r=N_{ID}^{(2)}((\lambda,2t);k)
. Let
u,v\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}
be such that
f(u)\neq f(v)
.
There are two possible cases for
u
and
v
.

Case 1:
If
d_{ID}(u,v)>2t
, then

d_{ID}(\mathrm{Enc}(u),\mathrm{Enc}(v))\geq d_{ID}(u_{p},v_{p})>2t.

Case 2:
If
d_{ID}(u,v)\leq 2t
, then by the definition of
\mathrm{Col}_{f}
we have

\mathrm{Col}_{f}(u)\neq\mathrm{Col}_{f}(v)
.
Therefore,

d_{ID}(u_{p},v_{p})=d_{ID}(C_{\mathrm{Col}_{f}(u)},C_{\mathrm{Col}_{f}(v)})\geq 2(t+k),

Because
u\neq v
, we have
d_{ID}(u,v)\geq 2
, and thus

\displaystyle d_{ID}(\mathrm{Enc}(u),\mathrm{Enc}(v))

\displaystyle\geq d_{ID}(u,v)+d_{ID}(u_{p},v_{p})-2\min{(k,r)}

\displaystyle\geq 2+2(t+k)-2k

\displaystyle=2t+2.

The second inequality in the above equation follows from the fact that
N_{ID}^{(2)}((\lambda,2(t+k));k)=r>k
.

Hence, in both cases, the encoding satisfies the desired distance property,
proving that
\mathrm{Enc}
defines an FCIDC with redundancy
r=N_{ID}^{(2)}((\lambda,2t);k)
.
∎

\displaystyle d_{ID}(Enc(u),Enc(v))\geq d_{ID}(u,v)>2t

Case 2:

\displaystyle d_{I}(Enc(u),Enc(v))

(2)

VIII
Conclusion

This work introduces a comprehensive and unified framework for function-correcting codes designed specifically for insertion–deletion (insdel) channels. We develop a general construction methodology for such codes and demonstrate its applicability through several representative classes of functions, supported by concrete examples. To characterize the fundamental limits of these codes, we establish Gilbert–Varshamov and Plotkin-like bounds on the lengths of irregular insdel-distance codes. By exploiting the intrinsic relationship between the optimal redundancy of function-correcting insdel codes and the minimal lengths of these irregular distance codes, we further derive a simplified and broadly applicable lower bound on redundancy. Building on these theoretical insights, we explicitly construct FCIDCs for various function families, including locally bounded functions, VT-type functions, the number-of-runs function, and the maximum-run-length function, thereby demonstrating the versatility and practical potential of our framework. Finally, we highlight that extending function-error correction to DNA data storage, where insertion and deletion errors are particularly prevalent, offers a promising direction for future research.

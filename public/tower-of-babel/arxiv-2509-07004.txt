1
Introduction

Euler’s totient function
\varphi(n)
is one of the central objects in elementary number theory
[
1
]
. Defined by

\varphi(n)=\#\{\,1\leq k\leq n:\gcd(k,n)=1\,\},

it measures the number of integers up to
n
that are coprime to
n
. The multiplicative nature of
\varphi
, together with its deep connections to prime factorization, has made it a cornerstone in arithmetic and analytic investigations.

A classical object of study is the summatory function

\Psi(x)=\sum_{n\leq x}\varphi(n),

whose asymptotic behavior,

\Psi(x)=\frac{3}{\pi^{2}}x^{2}+O(x\log x),

was established in the early 20th century and plays a crucial role in understanding the distribution of totients.

In this article we extend this framework by introducing two complementary restricted sums associated with a fixed prime
p
:

\Upsilon(x,p)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
\gcd(n,p)=1\end{subarray}}\varphi(n),\qquad\Delta(x,p)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
p\mid n\end{subarray}}\varphi(n).

These satisfy the natural decomposition

\Psi(x)=\Upsilon(x,p)+\Delta(x,p),

which allows us to filter the contribution of
\varphi(n)
according to divisibility by a prime.

The main goal of this paper is to investigate arithmetic and analytic properties of
\Delta(x,p)
, including congruence relations, recurrence identities, generating functions, and asymptotic behavior. In particular, we prove that
\Delta(x,p)\equiv 0\pmod{p-1}
for all primes
p
, and establish the asymptotic formula :

\Delta(x,p)=\frac{3}{\pi^{2}(p+1)}\,x^{2}+O(x\log x).

These results provide a refined understanding of how Euler’s totient values are distributed among multiples of a fixed prime, and they complement the classical theory of
\Psi(x)
. The methods combine elementary number-theoretic arguments with analytic techniques, and suggest further connections with divisor sums and multiplicative functions.

Definition 2.1

\varphi(n)=\#\{\,1\leq k\leq n:\gcd(k,n)=1\,\}.

It is well known that

\gcd(m,n)=1\;\;\Rightarrow\;\;\varphi(mn)=\varphi(m)\varphi(n).

Moreover, if

, then

\varphi(n)=n\prod_{i=1}^{r}\left(1-\frac{1}{p_{i}}\right).

2.2
Summatory Totient Function

A central object in analytic number theory is the summatory function

\Psi(x)=\sum_{n\leq x}\varphi(n).

Lemma 2.1

For
x\geq 1

\Psi(x)=\frac{3}{\pi^{2}}x^{2}+O(x\log x).

This formula shows that
\Psi(x)

Fix a prime

For

\Psi(x)=\Upsilon(x,p)+\Delta(x,p).

3
Congruence Properties of
\Delta(x,p)

In this section we prove several elementary but useful congruence and
arithmetic properties of the function

\Delta(x,p)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
p\mid n\end{subarray}}\varphi(n)
for a fixed prime

p
. The main result is that
\Delta(x,p)
is always divisible by
p-1
.
We also record a simple finite-difference identity which will be used later.

Proposition 3.1
.

For every integer
n\geq 1
, We have :

\Delta(n+1,p)-\Delta(n,p)=\begin{cases}\varphi(n+1),&p\mid(n+1),\\[4.0pt]
0,&p\nmid(n+1).\end{cases}

Proof.

By definition
\Delta(t,p)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq t\\
p\mid k\end{subarray}}\varphi(k)
.
Therefore

\Delta(n+1,p)-\Delta(n,p)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq n+1\\
p\mid k\end{subarray}}\varphi(k)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq n\\
p\mid k\end{subarray}}\varphi(k).

All terms with
k\leq n
cancel, so only the term
k=n+1
(if any) remains.
If
p\mid(n+1)
then that remaining term equals
\varphi(n+1)
; otherwise
there is no remaining term and the difference is
0
. This yields the stated
case distinction.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

For any prime
p
and any integer
n\geq 1
, We have :

\Delta(n,p)\equiv 0\pmod{p-1}.

Proof.

Write the definition

\Delta(n,p)=\sum_{m=1}^{\lfloor n/p\rfloor}\varphi(pm).

Fix
m\geq 1
and factor
m=p^{a}r
with
a\geq 0
and
\gcd(r,p)=1
.
Then
pm=p^{a+1}r
and, by multiplicativity of
\varphi
on coprime integers,

\varphi(pm)=\varphi\bigl(p^{a+1}\bigr)\varphi(r).

For a prime power we have
\varphi(p^{a+1})=p^{a}(p-1)
, hence

\varphi(pm)=p^{a}(p-1)\varphi(r),

which is divisible by
p-1
. Because every term
\varphi(pm)
in the sum
defining
\Delta(n,p)
is divisible by
p-1
, the whole sum is divisible
by
p-1
. Thus
\Delta(n,p)\equiv 0\pmod{p-1}
.
∎

4
Connections of
\Delta(n,p)
with Other arithmetic Functions

In this section we record several additional identities satisfied by the
restricted summatory function
\Delta(n,p)
. These identities connect
\Delta

with arithmetic functions such as
\omega(n)
, the number of distinct prime
divisors, and reveal combinatorial and generating function structures.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
n\geq 1
be an integer and for each prime
p
. we have :

\sum_{p}\Delta(n,p)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\varphi(k)\,\omega(k),

where
\omega(k)
denotes the number of distinct prime divisors of
k
.

Proof.

Start from the definition and sum over primes
p\leq n
:

\sum_{p\leq n}\Delta(n,p)=\sum_{p\leq n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq n\\
p\mid k\end{subarray}}\varphi(k).

Both sums are finite, so we may interchange the order of summation:

\sum_{p\leq n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq n\\
p\mid k\end{subarray}}\varphi(k)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq n\\
p\mid k\end{subarray}}\varphi(k).

Fix an integer
k
with
1\leq k\leq n
. For such a fixed
k
the inner condition

\{\,p\leq n\text{ prime}:\ p\mid k\,\}
is equivalent to

\{\,p\text{ prime}:\ p\mid k\,\}
because any prime divisor
p
of
k

satisfies
p\leq k\leq n
. Hence

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq n\\
p\mid k\end{subarray}}\varphi(k)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\ \text{prime}\\
p\mid k\end{subarray}}\varphi(k).

The value
\varphi(k)
does not depend on
p
, so the inner sum is

\varphi(k)
multiplied by the number of distinct prime divisors of
k
,
which is
\omega(k)
. Therefore

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq n\\
p\mid k\end{subarray}}\varphi(k)=\varphi(k)\,\omega(k).

Substituting back into the outer sum yields

\sum_{p\leq n}\Delta(n,p)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\varphi(k)\,\omega(k),

as claimed.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
p
be a prime and
n\geq 1
, Then :

\sum_{k\leq n}\gcd(k,p)\,\varphi(k)\;=\;\Psi(n)+(p-1)\,\Delta(n,p).

Proof.

Set

S=\sum_{k\leq n}\gcd(k,p)\,\varphi(k).

Partition the integers
1\leq k\leq n
into two sets:

A=\{k\leq n:p\nmid k\},\qquad B=\{k\leq n:p\mid k\}.

If
k\in A
, then
\gcd(k,p)=1
;
if
k\in B
, then
\gcd(k,p)=p
.
Hence

S=\sum_{k\in A}1\cdot\varphi(k)\;+\;\sum_{k\in B}p\cdot\varphi(k).

Now

\sum_{k\in A}\varphi(k)=\Psi(n)-\Delta(n,p),\qquad\sum_{k\in B}\varphi(k)=\Delta(n,p).

Thus

S=\Psi(n)-\Delta(n,p)+p\Delta(n,p)=\Psi(n)+(p-1)\Delta(n,p).

This proves the lemma.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
N\geq 1
be an integer and
x
a complex number with
x\neq 1
.
Then :

\boxed{\;\sum_{n=1}^{N}\Delta(n,p)\,x^{n}=\sum_{m=1}^{\lfloor N/p\rfloor}\varphi(pm)\,\frac{x^{pm}-x^{N+1}}{1-x}\;}.

Moreover, if
x=1
the same identity holds in the limiting (summation) sense:

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\Delta(n,p)=\sum_{m=1}^{\lfloor N/p\rfloor}\varphi(pm)\,(N-pm+1).

Proof.

Start from the definition
\Delta(n,p)=\sum_{m:\,pm\leq n}\varphi(pm)
.
Then

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\Delta(n,p)\,x^{n}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\Bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\geq 1\\
pm\leq n\end{subarray}}\varphi(pm)\Bigg)x^{n}.

Both sums are finite, so interchange the order of summation:

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\Delta(n,p)\,x^{n}=\sum_{m=1}^{\lfloor N/p\rfloor}\varphi(pm)\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathbf{1}_{n\geq pm}\,x^{n}=\sum_{m=1}^{\lfloor N/p\rfloor}\varphi(pm)\sum_{n=pm}^{N}x^{n}.

The inner sum is a finite geometric progression. For
x\neq 1
,

\sum_{n=pm}^{N}x^{n}=x^{pm}+x^{pm+1}+\cdots+x^{N}=\frac{x^{pm}-x^{N+1}}{1-x},

which yields the identity stated. If
x=1
the inner sum equals
N-pm+1
,
giving the second displayed formula.
∎

5
Asymptotic Expansions

In this section we derive precise asymptotic formulas for
\Delta(x,p)
and
related summatory quantities. The main input is the classical asymptotic for
the summatory totient function

\Psi(x)=\sum_{n\leq x}\varphi(n)=\frac{3}{\pi^{2}}x^{2}+O(x\log x),

which we use to unfold an exact recurrence for
\Delta(x,p)
and to estimate
error terms uniformly.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
p
be a prime and
n\geq 1
.
Then :

\Delta(x,p)=\frac{3}{\pi^{2}(p+1)}\,x^{2}\;+\;O\!\big(x\log x\big),

where the implied constant is absolute.

Proof.

By the lemma (
2.2
) , We Have :

\Delta(n,p)=(p-1)\sum_{j\geq 1}\Psi\!\left(\Big\lfloor\frac{n}{p^{\,j}}\Big\rfloor\right).

Next, recall the well-known asymptotic (
2.1
) :

\Psi(x)=\frac{3}{\pi^{2}}x^{2}+O(x\log x).

Substituting into the exact sum gives

\Delta(n,p)=(p-1)\sum_{j\geq 1}\left(\frac{3}{\pi^{2}}\Big\lfloor\frac{n}{p^{\,j}}\Big\rfloor^{2}+O\!\left(\frac{n}{p^{j}}\log\frac{n}{p^{j}}\right)\right).

For the main term, since

\lfloor n/p^{j}\rfloor^{2}=\frac{n^{2}}{p^{2j}}+O\!\big(n/p^{j}\big)
,

\frac{3}{\pi^{2}}(p-1)\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{n^{2}}{p^{2j}}=\frac{3}{\pi^{2}}\cdot\frac{p-1}{p^{2}-1}\,n^{2}=\frac{3}{\pi^{2}(p+1)}\,n^{2}.

For the error term, we estimate

(p-1)\sum_{j\geq 1}O\!\left(\frac{n}{p^{j}}\log\frac{n}{p^{j}}\right)=O\!\big(n\log n\sum_{j\geq 1}p^{-j}\big)=O(n\log n).

Combining both parts, we obtain

\Delta(n,p)=\frac{3}{\pi^{2}(p+1)}\,n^{2}+O(n\log n).

∎

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
p
be a prime and
n\geq 1
. Then :

\displaystyle\sum_{k\leq n}\Delta(k,p)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi^{2}(p+1)}\,n^{3}\;+\;O\!\big(n^{2}\log n\big),

(4)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k\leq n}\Delta(k,p)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi^{2}(p+1)}\,n^{2}\;+\;O\!\big(n\log n\big).

(5)

Proof.

We start from Theorem
5.1
. Put :

A:=\frac{3}{\pi^{2}(p+1)}.

Then for every integer
k\geq 1
we have, uniformly,

\Delta(k,p)=Ak^{2}+R(k),\qquad R(k)=O(k\log k).

(The uniformity of the
O
–term follows because Theorem
5.1
holds for every real argument
x
, with an absolute implied constant.)

Step 1 — Sum the main term.

\sum_{k=1}^{n}Ak^{2}=A\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^{2}=A\left(\frac{n^{3}}{3}+O(n^{2})\right)=\frac{A}{3}\,n^{3}+O(n^{2}).

(We used
\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^{2}=n^{3}/3+O(n^{2})
.)

Step 2 — Sum the error terms.

Using the bound
R(k)\ll k\log k
,

\sum_{k=1}^{n}R(k)\ll\sum_{k=1}^{n}k\log k.

Estimate the last sum by an integral (or by partial summation):

\sum_{k=1}^{n}k\log k=\int_{1}^{n}t\log t\,dt+O(n\log n)=\frac{1}{2}n^{2}\log n-\frac{1}{4}n^{2}+O(n\log n)=O(n^{2}\log n).

Hence

\sum_{k=1}^{n}R(k)=O(n^{2}\log n).

Step 3 — Combine.

Adding the contributions from Steps 1 and 2,

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Delta(k,p)=\frac{A}{3}\,n^{3}+O(n^{2})+O(n^{2}\log n)=\frac{A}{3}\,n^{3}+O(n^{2}\log n).

Since
A/3=\dfrac{1}{\pi^{2}(p+1)}
, this yields (
4
):

\sum_{k\leq n}\Delta(k,p)=\frac{1}{\pi^{2}(p+1)}\,n^{3}+O(n^{2}\log n).

Step 4 — Average.

Divide (
4
) by
n
to obtain the average:

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k\leq n}\Delta(k,p)=\frac{1}{\pi^{2}(p+1)}\,n^{2}+O(n\log n),

which is (
5
). This completes the proof.
∎

Conclusion

\Delta(n,p)
, which sums Euler’s totient function over multiples of a
prime

of
\Delta(n,p)

•

Congruence properties and divisibility by

•

Connections with multiplicative functions

4

Explicit finite differences and generating function representations.

Asymptotic expansions for

These results provide a deeper understanding of the structure of

MiR-375 Targeting PDK1 Regulates the Wnt/β-Catenin Pathway in Lower Extremity Arteriosclerosis Obliterans
Authors: Zongrong Liu, Zhujun Yue, Ruirui Yang, Safwan Muhammad, Jingquan Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundLower extremity arteriosclerosis obliterans (LEASO) is a severe peripheral arterial disease characterized by chronic inflammation, lipid deposition, and vascular remodeling. MicroRNA-375 (miR-375) has emerged as a regulatory molecule in vascular pathology, but its role in LEASO remains unclear.ObjectiveThis study investigated whether miR-375 regulates the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway by targeting pyruvate dehydrogenase kinase 1 (PDK1), and its impact on LEASO progression.MethodsClinical samples (arterial tissues and blood) were collected from 10 LEASO patients and 10 trauma-related amputation controls. Histology, qPCR, Western blot, and ELISA were used to evaluate molecular and inflammatory markers. Animal models of atherosclerosis (LDLR-/- mice) and LEASO (SD rats) were established, with Wnt/β-catenin pathway inhibition as intervention. In vitro, vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs) were transfected with miR-375 mimics/inhibitors, and functional assays were performed. Dual-luciferase reporter assays confirmed the interaction between miR-375 and PDK1.ResultsCompared with the control group, miR-375 expression was markedly downregulated in LEASO tissues, reaching only 50.3% of the control level (P=0.008). By contrast, the mRNA and protein expression levels of PDK1, Wnt3a and β-catenin were significantly increased. A strong positive correlation was observed between PDK1 expression and Wnt signaling activity (r=0.91, P=0.012). Dual-luciferase reporter assay verified that miR-375 directly bound to the 3′-untranslated region (3′-UTR) of PDK1 mRNA, and suppressed PDK1 expression by 78% (P<0.001).In vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs), miR-375 overexpression suppressed cell proliferation and migration by 41.5% and 17.3%, respectively. Nevertheless, simultaneous PDK1 overexpression reversed these inhibitory biological effects. Notably, miR-375 upregulation inhibited the PDK1-Wnt/β-catenin signaling cascade and alleviated pathological phenotypes both in vitro and in vivo. However, endogenous miR-375 reduction was accompanied by compensatory and nonlinear alterations of molecular profiles under certain in vitro conditions, such as oxidized low-density lipoprotein (ox-LDL)-stimulated VSMCs.In vivo experiments demonstrated that restored miR-375 expression reduced atherosclerotic plaque area by 41.5%, decreased the secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines including interleukin-6 (IL-6) and interleukin-1β (IL-1β), and enhanced plaque stability. Conversely, miR-375 knockdown aggravated intracellular lipid deposition and systemic inflammatory responses.ConclusionmiR-375 suppresses LEASO progression by targeting PDK1 and downregulating Wnt/β-catenin signaling. Restoring miR-375 expression represents a promising therapeutic strategy for LEASO.Keywords: lower extremity arteriosclerosis obliterans, miR-375, PDK1, Wnt/β-catenin, vascular smooth muscle cells, inflammation, targeted therapy

Background

Lower extremity arteriosclerosis obliterans (LEASO) is a peripheral arterial disease caused by atherosclerosis (AS), characterized by limb ischemia and gangrene. The global patient number exceeds 236 million, with a prevalence rate of 10–20% among those over 70 years old.1,2 Current clinical treatment mainly relies on surgical procedures (such as angioplasty) and pharmacological interventions (antiplatelet drugs, vasodilators), but the postoperative restenosis rate reaches 30–50%,3 and long-term medication use poses a risk of adverse reactions.4,5 Therefore, elucidating the molecular mechanisms of LEASO and developing novel targeted therapies are pressing clinical challenges.

The core pathological mechanism of LEASO involves the phenotypic transformation of vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs). Under the stimulation of inflammatory factors, the abnormal proliferation and migration of VSMCs drive plaque formation and vascular remodeling.6 Our previous studies have shown that m6A modification mediated by METTL3 induces phenotypic transformation of VSMCs through the miR-375-3p/PDK1 axis, exacerbating AS plaque instability.7 Although the role of this mechanism in LEASO is not yet clear, multiple pieces of evidence support its importance: 1) miR-375 plays a core regulatory role in vascular diseases: it regulates VSMCs proliferation and migration by targeting genes such as PDK1,8,9 has diagnostic value in limb ischemia,10 and participates in the regulation of inflammation and cell injury;11 2) PDK1 as a key molecule in the metabolic-signaling pathway: it stabilizes β-catenin by activating the Akt/GSK-3β axis, activating the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway;12 this pathway directly drives the abnormal phenotype of VSMCs and vascular inflammation,13 and the imbalance of the PDK1/PDH axis is positively correlated with the severity of human vascular inflammation;14 3) The Wnt/β-catenin pathway is a common mechanism in AS: this pathway is abnormally activated in the LEASO model,15 and its inhibitors can significantly reduce plaque load and inflammatory factor levels,16,17 suggesting its potential as a therapeutic target. Based on this, this study proposes the core hypothesis: miR-375 mediates the pathological progression of LEASO by targeting the PDK1-mediated Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway. Through the integration of clinical sample analysis, animal model validation, and cell experiments, this study aims to systematically verify this mechanism and provide new therapeutic targets for LEASO.

Materials and Methods

Ethical Statement

This study protocol was formally approved by the Medical Ethics Committee and Animal Experimental Ethics Committee of Affiliated Hospital of North Sichuan Medical College (Approval No.: 2025ER66-1). All human-related investigations were conducted in strict accordance with the ethical principles outlined in the 2013 Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from each participant or their legally authorized representative prior to enrollment. All animal experimental procedures complied with the eighth edition of the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals published by the National Institutes of Health (NIH Publication No. 8023). Multiple strategies were adopted to minimize animal usage and alleviate experimental suffering.

Clinical Study

Study Subjects and Sample Collection

Peripheral blood and lower limb femoral artery vessel samples from 10 patients who underwent amputation due to severe lower extremity arteriosclerosis obliterans (LEASO) were selected as the observation group. In addition, peripheral blood and normal lower limb femoral artery vascular samples from 10 non-atherosclerosis patients who underwent amputation due to trauma in vascular or orthopedic surgery were collected as the normal group. General baseline information was compared between the two groups, and no significant differences were observed (p > 0.05), indicating valid inter-group comparability.

Detection of Target Indexes in Clinical Samples

HE Staining for Vascular Morphological Observation

Human lower limb femoral artery specimens were collected and prepared into paraffin sections. The sections were immersed in xylene for 10 minutes for dewaxing, followed by gradient ethanol hydration: 1 minute in anhydrous ethanol, sequential soaking in 95%, 90%, and 80% ethanol for approximately 1 minute each, and final rinsing with tap water for 1 minute. Subsequently, sections were stained with hematoxylin staining solution for 10 minutes and rinsed in running tap water for 1 minute. Differentiation was performed in 1% hydrochloric acid alcohol for about 30seconds, followed by repeated washing with running tap water for more than 15 minutes. After 5 minutes of staining with 1% eosin alcohol, sections were differentiated with 90% ethanol for 30seconds, sequentially soaked in 95% ethanol for 1 minute, anhydrous ethanol for 2 minutes, dimethyl xylene for 1 minute, and pure xylene for 2 minutes, and finally sealed with neutral gum. The stained tissue sections were observed and photographed under an inverted microscope.

Western Blot Detection of Vascular PDK1, Wnt, and β-Catenin Protein Expression

Vascular tissue specimens were shredded and supplemented with RIPA lysis buffer. After homogenization using a tissue homogenizer, tissues were lysed on ice for 30 minutes. The homogenate was centrifuged at 10,000 r/min at 4°C for 5 minutes, and the supernatant was collected for protein quantification via the BCA method. Qualified protein samples were mixed with loading buffer, denatured in a 100°C water bath for 5 minutes, subpackaged, and stored at −80°C for subsequent use.

SDS-PAGE was performed to prepare separation and concentration gels for protein electrophoresis. After electrophoresis, proteins were transferred to PVDF membranes. The membranes were blocked with 5% skim milk, incubated with primary antibodies against PDK1, Wnt, and β-catenin at 4°C overnight, and washed thoroughly with TBST buffer. Subsequently, membranes were incubated with HRP-conjugated secondary antibody at room temperature for 1 hour. Protein bands were visualized using an ECL chemiluminescence kit and photographed. With GAPDH as the internal reference, the gray values of target protein bands were analyzed by ImageJ software to conduct semi-quantitative analysis.

qPCR Detection of Vascular PDK1, Wnt, β-Catenin, and miR-375 mRNA Expression

Vascular tissues were ground in liquid nitrogen and fully homogenized. Total RNA was extracted using the Trizol method, and RNA was reverse-transcribed into complementary DNA (cDNA) following the manufacturer’s kit instructions. The mRNA expression levels of PDK1, Wnt, β-catenin, and miR-375 were detected by qPCR with a standard two-step amplification procedure: Stage 1, pre-denaturation (1 cycle) at 95°C for 5 minutes; Stage 2, cyclic reaction (40 cycles) at 95°C for 10seconds and 60°C for 30seconds; Stage 3, melting curve analysis (1 cycle) at 95°C for 15seconds and 60°C for 60seconds. Relative gene expression results were analyzed and visualized as bar charts using GraphPad Prism 9 software.

Animal Experiments

Establishment of Atherosclerosis Mouse Model

Healthy clean-grade 6-week-old male LDL receptor gene-deficient mice (LDLR-/- mice, C57BL/6J genetic background, 10th generation) were used in this study. A total of 30 mice with a body weight of 19–20 g were housed in the animal experiment center under a 12/12-hour day-night cycle with free access to feed and water. All mice were randomly divided into three groups: control group, model group, and Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway inhibitor group. Mice in the control group were fed a normal diet, while those in the model and inhibitor groups were fed a high-fat and high-cholesterol diet to induce atherosclerosis.

Establishment of LEASO Rat Model

Thirty SPF-grade SD male rats were selected and fed with a high-fat diet for 1 week before LEASO modeling. Rats were routinely fixed and anesthetized via intraperitoneal injection of 40 mg/kg pentobarbital sodium. The inner thigh hair of the left limb was shaved and disinfected, and a 2 cm longitudinal incision was made at the groin midpoint to separate the saphenous artery. Both ends of the artery were clamped with artery forceps. An insulin syringe was inserted into the arterial lumen along the long axis from the distal to proximal end, and 0.3 mL sterile distilled water for injection was slowly injected and retained for 5 minutes. After removing the forceps, hemostasis was performed, and the incision was sutured. All rats received intraperitoneal injection of 200,000 U penicillin for anti-infection treatment and were returned to the feeding cage. The rats were continuously fed with a high-fat diet for 4 weeks, and successful LEASO modeling was confirmed when obvious limb ischemic changes and necrosis occurred. Experimental rats were randomly divided into control group, model group, and Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway inhibitor group.

Observation of General Animal Conditions

During the entire experimental period, the general physiological status of all animals was regularly observed and recorded, including mental state, coat color, skin temperature, motor activity sensitivity, and body weight changes.

Evaluation of Vascular Plaque Load and Morphology

After anesthesia, the entire aorta and lower limb arteries of mice and rats were isolated and fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde. Part of the vascular tissues were dehydrated, paraffin-embedded, and cut into 5 μm continuous sections. After dewaxing and hydration, HE staining was performed for general vascular morphological analysis, and Masson staining was used to evaluate vascular collagen deposition. The remaining vascular tissues were embedded in OCT medium, cut into 10 μm frozen sections, and stained with Oil Red O to observe lipid deposition and assess atherosclerotic plaque formation.

qPCR Detection of Vascular NLRP3, Wnt, and β-Catenin mRNA Expression

Vascular tissues from each animal group were collected, shredded, and fully homogenized. Total RNA was extracted via the Trizol method and reverse-transcribed into cDNA according to the kit instructions. The mRNA expression levels of NLRP3, Wnt, and β-catenin were detected by qPCR. The reaction conditions were set as follows: pre-denaturation at 95°C for 60s, annealing at 55°C for 30s, extension at 72°C for 30s, with a total of 40 amplification cycles. β-actin was used as the internal reference, and the relative expression of target genes was calculated using the 2 method.

Western Blot Detection of Vascular NLRP3, Wnt, and β-Catenin Protein Expression

Vascular tissues of each group were supplemented with RIPA lysis buffer, shredded, homogenized, and lysed on ice for 30 minutes. The homogenate was centrifuged at 10,000 r/min at 4°C for 5 minutes, and the supernatant was collected for protein quantification using a BCA protein quantification kit. Protein samples were mixed with loading buffer, denatured in a 100°C water bath for 5 minutes, subpackaged, and stored at −80°C for standby application.

SDS-PAGE was performed for protein separation, followed by transmembrane transfer to PVDF membranes. The membranes were blocked with 5% skim milk and incubated with primary antibodies against NLRP3, Wnt, and β-catenin at 4°C overnight. After TBST washing, membranes were incubated with HRP-conjugated secondary antibody at room temperature for 1 hour. Protein bands were visualized using an ECL kit and photographed. With β-actin as the internal reference, ImageJ software was used to analyze the gray value of protein bands for semi-quantitative analysis of target proteins.

ELISA Detection of Peripheral Blood Inflammatory Factors

The levels of NLRP3, IL-6, and IL-1β in peripheral blood samples of each group were detected using commercial ELISA kits. All experimental operations were strictly conducted in accordance with the official kit instructions.

Cell Experiments

Dual-Luciferase Reporter Gene Assay

Plasmid vector for miR-375 and luciferase vector carrying PDK1 3′-untranslated region (3’UTR) were constructed. The two vectors were co-transfected into HEK293T cells using Lipofectamine 2000 transfection reagent. The dual-luciferase reporter gene system was applied to detect the targeted regulatory effect of miR-375 on PDK1 transcription level.

Isolation and Culture of Primary Vascular Smooth Muscle Cells

Primary vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs) were isolated from mouse aorta via enzymatic digestion. Cells at passages 3 to 6 were used for subsequent functional experiments. Isolated VSMCs were cultured in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum. To construct the pathological VSMC model, cells were stimulated with 50 μg/mL oxidized low-density lipoprotein (ox-LDL) for 24 hours.

Cell Transfection and Grouping

Cultured VSMCs were diluted to a concentration of 1×10 cells/mL and seeded in 6-well plates at a density of 2×10 cells per well. When cells grew into a monolayer, cell transfection was performed following the Lipofectamine 2000 manufacturer’s instructions. Cells were divided into six groups according to different transfection treatments: control group, ox-LDL-induced model group, miR-375 overexpression group, miR-375 silencing group, miR-375 mimics + pcDNA-PDK1 co-transfection group, and miR-375 inhibitors + siR-PDK1 co-transfection group. After 48 hours of transfection, cells from each group were collected for subsequent experimental detection.

Detection of Cell Proliferation

The proliferation ability of VSMCs in each group was detected using a commercial BrdU cell proliferation detection kit, and all operations were strictly performed in accordance with the kit instructions.

Detection of Cell Migration

Transfected and treated VSMCs from each group were evenly seeded in 6-well plates at a density of approximately 2×10 cells per well. After overnight culture, cells were washed 2–3 times with PBS, and a uniform scratch was made on the cell monolayer using a sterile pipette tip. Floating cells were removed by three times of PBS washing, and serum-free medium was added to each well. The scratch width of each group was photographed and recorded at 0 hour under a microscope. The plates were then placed in a 37°C, 5% CO incubator for continuous culture, and scratch changes were photographed and recorded at 24 hours and 48 hours to evaluate cell migration ability.

qPCR Detection of Cellular Gene Expression

Total RNA was extracted from VSMCs of each group using the Trizol method and reverse-transcribed into cDNA following kit protocols. The mRNA expression levels of Ki67, MMPs, NLRP3, Wnt, and β-catenin were detected by qPCR. The reaction parameters were set as 95°C pre-denaturation for 60s, 55°C annealing for 30s, and 72°C extension for 30s, with a total of 40 amplification cycles. β-actin was used as the internal reference, and the relative expression of target genes was calculated via the 2 method.

Western Blot Detection of Cellular Protein Expression

VSMCs from each group were lysed with RIPA lysis buffer on ice for 30 minutes. After centrifugation at 10,000 r/min and 4°C for 5 minutes, the supernatant was collected, and protein concentration was quantified using a BCA protein quantification kit. Protein samples were denatured in a 100°C water bath for 5 minutes after mixing with loading buffer, subpackaged, and stored at −80°C for subsequent use.

SDS-PAGE electrophoresis and transmembrane were performed, and PVDF membranes were blocked with 5% skim milk. Membranes were incubated with primary antibodies against Ki67, MMPs, NLRP3, Wnt, and β-catenin at 4°C overnight, followed by incubation with HRP-conjugated secondary antibody at room temperature for 1 hour after TBST washing. Protein bands were visualized via ECL chemiluminescence and photographed. With β-actin as the internal reference, ImageJ software was used for semi-quantitative analysis of target protein expression.

Detection of Cellular Inflammatory Factors

The levels of inflammatory factors including NLRP3, IL-6, and IL-1β in VSMCs of each group were detected by ELISA. All experimental procedures were operated in strict accordance with the instructions of the commercial ELISA kits.

Statistical Analysis

All data analyses were performed using R software (version 4.3.3). Data are expressed as mean ± standard deviation. An unpaired t-test was used to compare data that followed a normal distribution and homogeneity of variance between two groups, while a one-way ANOVA was used to compare multiple groups, followed by the Tukey’s test. Kolmogorov–Smirnov method was used to evaluate data normal distribution. Repeated measures ANOVA was used to analyze the comparison of cell proliferation at different time points. When p<0.05, differences were considered significant.

Protein and Transcriptional Profiling

Western blot analysis showed that the expression of PDK1, Wnt3a, and β-catenin proteins in the arterial tissues of LEASO patients was significantly higher than in the control group. Semi-quantitative analysis further confirmed that: PDK1 expression increased by 164% (0.833 vs 0.315; p=0.002), Wnt3a increased by 111% (1.021 vs 0.483; p<0.001), and β-catenin increased by 175% (1.039 vs 0.377; p<0.0001) (Figure 1C). Additionally, we found that PDK1 expression was strongly positively correlated with Wnt activity (r=0.910, p=0.012).

qPCR analysis indicated that in the LEASO group, PDK1, Wnt3a, and β-catenin mRNA were upregulated by 4.94-fold (p=0.048), 3.11-fold (p=0.026), and 4.15-fold (p=0.037) respectively, while miR-375 expression decreased to 50.3% of the control group (p=0.008) (Figure 1D). These results validate the activation of the PDK1-Wnt/β-catenin signaling axis at the transcriptional level, suggesting that miR-375 may negatively regulate this process.

Assessment of peripheral blood inflammatory markers, coagulation markers, and lipid metabolism indicators showed that 20 subjects were included in this study, with 10 in the experimental group and 10 in the control group. The baseline characteristics and laboratory findings of the two groups are summarized as follows (Table 1): The median age in the experimental group was significantly higher than in the control group (83.5 years [IQR: 79.0–84.0] vs 75.0 years [IQR: 69.0–79.8], P=0.025). There was no statistical difference in gender distribution (male ratio: 50.0% vs 60.0%, P=1.000). The prevalence of underlying diseases, such as hypertension (90.0% vs 70.0%), diabetes (80.0% vs 60.0%), and coronary heart disease (80.0% vs 50.0%), did not show significant intergroup differences (all P>0.05).

Cellular Experiment Results

Verification of miR-375 and PDK1 Targeting Relationship

Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay: miR-375 significantly inhibited the activity of wild-type PDK1 3′-UTR: Transfection with miR-375 mimic group (WT+miR-375 mimic): Luciferase activity decreased by 78% compared to the negative control group (WT+NC) (p<0.001); mutations in the target sequence completely eliminated miR-375’s inhibitory effect: Mut+miR-375 mimic group vs Mut+NC group: No statistical difference in luciferase activity (p>0.05); there was no interference in basal expression by the mutant sequence itself: WT+NC group vs Mut+NC group: No significant difference in luciferase activity (p>0.05) (Figure 3C).

Discussion

This multi-dimensional investigation integrating clinical specimens, animal models and cellular experiments firstly systematically clarified the underlying mechanism of LEASO. Downregulated miR-375 releases its inhibitory effect on PDK1, thereby triggering aberrant activation of the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway. This cascade further facilitates multiple pathological processes, including vascular smooth muscle cell proliferation, migration, inflammatory response and extracellular matrix remodeling.

Collective experimental evidence demonstrates that miR-375 functions as a protective molecule against LEASO, and its insufficient expression serves as a critical contributor to disease deterioration.

Nevertheless, several inconsistent phenomena were observed in this study. Notably, the protein level of certain molecules such as β-catenin was elevated rather than reduced following miR-375 overexpression. Such findings indicate sophisticated regulatory networks and feedback loops exist during LEASO progression. The paradoxical changes may be attributed to compensatory activation of PDK1-independent Wnt signaling cascades, post-transcriptional and post-translational modulation that predominantly alters protein translation efficiency and stability instead of mRNA abundance, as well as cell-type heterogeneity between in vivo multi-cell tissues and in vitro single VSMC culture system.

The miR-375-PDK1 Axis is a Core Driver of Wnt/β-Catenin Pathway Overactivation

This study found significant downregulation of miR-375 in stenotic vessels of LEASO patients (reduced to “50.3%”) while its target PDK1 was dramatically elevated (nRNA↑4.94 times, protein↑“164%”). Dual-luciferase reporter experiments confirmed the direct binding site of miR-375 in the PDK1 3’UTR (inhibition rate “78%”). This finding significantly echoes Zhang et al’s18 research on vascular lesions in diabetes: that team found high glucose activates the PDK1/Akt pathway by inhibiting miR-375, leading to endothelial dysfunction, while miR-375 mimics can reverse this damage. Notably, this study found a strong positive correlation between PDK1 expression and Wnt activity (r=0.910), suggesting a general regulatory role of PDK1 on the Wnt pathway.

As an upstream kinase of Akt, PDK1 has recently been found to directly regulate the Wnt pathway through related pathways: Phosphorylation cascade regulation: Mai et al19 confirmed that PDK1 inhibits GSK3β kinase activity through phosphorylation of the Ser9 site in laryngeal cancer, blocking the formation of the β-catenin degradation complex and leading to β-catenin accumulation and nuclear translocation; Protein complex interaction: Bilim et al20 found a physical interaction between PDK1 and Dvl2 (Dishevelled protein) in cholangiocarcinoma, promoting LRP6 phosphorylation and enhancing the stability of the Wnt signaling receptor complex; Zhang et al’s21 latest research revealed that PDK1 can directly phosphorylate LRP6- Thr1479 site, highlighting its dual function as both a kinase and a scaffold protein; Stefansson’s latest research indicates that PDK1 can act as a coactivator of the “β”-catenin/TCF4 transcription complex, directly promoting the expression of Wnt target genes (such as cyclin D1, MMP7).22 These mechanisms perfectly explain the experimental results: PDK1 elevation in LEASO tissues was accompanied by Wnt3a↑111%, “β”-catenin↑175%; inhibition of miR-375 in animal models led to an increase in “β”-catenin protein by 629%; complementation with PDK1 in cell experiments could eliminate the antiproliferative effect of miR-375 overexpression. In summary, PDK1 is a crucial hub connecting metabolic kinase signaling (PDK1/Akt) and the core developmental pathway (Wnt), with miR-375 as its efficient upstream regulator. This is consistent with the anti-atherosclerotic effects of miR-375 identified by Kim et al23 in coronary heart disease research, but this study is the first to clarify its target PDK1 and its regulatory relationship with the Wnt pathway in LEASO.

The imbalance of the miR-375-PDK1-Wnt axis leads to synergistic deterioration of multidimensional pathological phenotypes

Abnormal proliferation and phenotypic transformation: Activated Wnt/“β”-catenin signaling is a key factor driving the transformation of vascular SMCs from a contractile type to a synthetic/proliferative type.22 This study found a large proliferation of VSMCs within plaques of LEASO patients (Ki67↑), and cell models showed that silencing miR-375 could reverse pathological proliferation by 41.5%. This supports the “spatiotemporal model of VSMC phenotypic transformation” proposed by Grootaert et al24 in the early stages of lesions (subendothelial aggregation), VSMCs are driven by Wnt to transform into a synthetic type, secreting a large amount of ECM; in the middle stage (plaque growth), continuous abnormal proliferation leads to thickening of the fibrous cap; in the late stage (plaque instability), increased apoptosis of VSMCs accelerates collagen degradation. Forte et al’s25 study further provided evidence at the single-cell level: Wnt pathway characteristic genes (AXIN2, LEF1) are specifically highly expressed in proliferative VSMCs of LEASO patients. Notably, animal experiments in this study showed that miR-375 agonist intervention significantly alleviated intimal thickening, suggesting that targeting this axis can effectively break the vicious cycle of “proliferation-apoptosis imbalance,” providing a new strategy for stabilizing late-stage plaques.

Biphasic regulatory mechanism of migration repair obstacles: This study first revealed the biphasic regulation of VSMC migration by miR-375: in pathological states (miR-375↓), the migration rate at 48 hours declined by “30.3%” compared to the control group, indicating a repair defect; when miR-375 was artificially silenced, the migration rate increased by “33.6%”, with “58%” of the pro-migration effect dependent on PDK1. This is consistent with the “migration-proliferation paradox” theory of Chaudhuri et al26 physiological repair requires VSMCs to transiently migrate to the injury site and then stop (contact inhibition), while chronically activated Wnt pathway disrupts this balance, causing cells to stall in a “migration-proliferation intermediate state.” Li et al27 found that Wnt5a enhances cell adhesion through the RhoA/ROCK pathway, inhibiting migration extension, directly corroborating the observed “decreasing migration rate over time” phenomenon in this experiment (24 hours to 48 hours decreased by “24.3%”). Notably, the migration rate in the miR-375 silenced group even surpassed the control group, possibly related to the “motogenic burst” induced by transient activation of PDK1,28 and possibly related to energy metabolism reprogramming — Hernandez-Negrete et al29 confirmed that PDK1 increases lactate production through upregulation of lactate dehydrogenase (LDHA), restructuring cytoskeleton dynamics to accelerate migration.

Bidirectional Regulatory Effects on Extracellular Matrix Remodeling

Histological staining revealed distinct alterations in extracellular matrix (ECM) distribution in LEASO lesions. Masson staining of human atherosclerotic plaques showed extensive collagen accumulation at the intima-media junction. In line with clinical observations, animal model results demonstrated that treatment with the miR-375 agonist markedly reduced the area of collagen deposition. At the molecular level, the mRNA expression of matrix metalloproteinase 2 (MMP2) was upregulated by 264% in diseased models, whereas miR-375 overexpression suppressed MMP2 protein expression by 39%.

This dual alteration can be interpreted through multiple validated molecular mechanisms. Park et al30 demonstrated that the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway directly targets and activates the MMP2 promoter, thereby facilitating collagen degradation. Schlegel et al31 further verified that PDK1 enhances MMP2 transcription via NF-κB pathway activation. Conversely, Huang et al32 reported that sustained Wnt signaling activation may inhibit ECM degradation by upregulating tissue inhibitor of metalloproteinases 1 (TIMP1) expression.

Such seemingly contradictory regulatory phenomena can be attributed to the spatial specificity of plaque remodeling. A previous histological study of LEASO by Chaabane et al33 validated that MMPs are highly enriched in plaque shoulders, which increases plaque vulnerability and rupture risk, while excessive collagen deposition primarily occurs at the plaque base, contributing to arterial wall stiffness. Quinn et al34 further clarified the mechanical regulatory basis of this spatial heterogeneity, confirming that local fluid shear stress modulates the regional distribution of collagen deposition through the Wnt signaling pathway. In the present animal experiments, miR-375 agonist treatment simultaneously reduced the size of lipid cores and the area of collagen deposition, solidifying the bidirectional regulatory capacity of miR-375 in maintaining ECM homeostasis in LEASO lesions.

Vicious Cycle Linking Inflammation and Lipid Deposition in LEASO Progression

LEASO patients exhibited prominent systemic inflammatory responses. Clinical data showed that plasma pro-inflammatory cytokines IL-6 and TNF-α were increased by 347% and 260%, respectively. Consistently, animal model ELISA results revealed a 390% elevation in IL-1β, a key downstream effector of the NLRP3 inflammasome.

The excessive inflammatory activation observed in this study is driven by the transcriptional amplification of the core regulatory pathway. Consistent findings from clinical specimens and cellular models confirmed that miR-375 downregulation markedly upregulated PDK1 mRNA expression by 4.94-fold, which further induced excessive activation of Wnt/β-catenin signaling, with β-catenin protein levels increased by 175%. These results are supported by a previous mechanistic study by Zhao et al35 which demonstrated that the activated β-catenin/TCF4 transcriptional complex directly binds to the NLRP3 promoter and triggers its transcription. In accordance with this mechanism, NLRP3 mRNA expression was upregulated by 410% in our study. To our knowledge, this study is the first to identify the complete signaling cascade miR-375 downregulation→PDK1 upregulation→β-catenin activation→NLRP3 transcriptional activation in the context of LEASO.

HE staining of human advanced plaques revealed abundant cholesterol crystal deposition, which is consistent with the classic mechanism proposed by Duewell et al36 In this mechanism, cholesterol crystals act as danger-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs), disrupt lysosomal membrane stability, and ultimately initiate caspase-1-dependent NLRP3 inflammasome assembly and activation.

The activated NLRP3 inflammasome further drives the formation of a self-sustaining inflammation-lipid deposition vicious cycle in LEASO lesions. Robustly secreted IL-1β and IL-6 promote monocyte recruitment and infiltration into plaque shoulders, as evidenced by massive lymphocyte infiltration in HE-stained clinical plaque samples. These pro-inflammatory cytokines also facilitate macrophage polarization toward the pro-inflammatory M1 phenotype, which is consistent with previous findings reported by Ridker et al37 M1-type macrophages actively phagocytize oxidized low-density lipoprotein (ox-LDL) and transform into foam cells, accompanied by continuous cholesterol crystal release. The 204% elevation of plasma LDL-C in enrolled patients further confirms the initiation and persistence of this pathological cycle. Collectively, these alterations establish a self-amplifying loop involving lipid accumulation, cholesterol crystal formation, NLRP3 inflammasome activation, excessive inflammatory factor release, and aggravated lipid deposition, which continuously drives LEASO progression toward advanced stages.

Malignant Cycle of the miR-375-PDK1 Axis and Classical Risk Factors

Beyond the core inflammatory cascade identified above, our clinical data highlighted severe dyslipidemia in LEASO patients, with total cholesterol (TC), triglyceride (TG), and low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) levels increased by 130%, 138%, and 204%, respectively. Notably, lipid metabolic disorders are not merely the initiating risk factor for LEASO; they also interact reciprocally with the miR-375-PDK1 axis to amplify disease progression. Existing studies have demonstrated that oxidized low-density lipoprotein (ox-LDL) markedly inhibits miR-375 expression in endothelial cells.38 Supporting this lipid-mediated regulatory mechanism, a ChIP-seq study from Li’s team confirmed direct binding of SREBP1 to the miR-375 promoter region, validating a regulatory pathway wherein hyperlipidemia triggers SREBP1 activation and subsequent miR-375 silencing.39

Consistent with dyslipidemic changes in clinical samples, abundant cholesterol crystals with typical crystalline fissures were observed in HE-stained human LEASO plaques. As previously reported, cholesterol crystals serve as potent NLRP3 inflammasome activators in a size-dependent manner, and crystals larger than 5 μm can trigger robust inflammatory responses by inducing lysosomal rupture.40 Mechanistically, β-catenin signaling further mediates GSDMD-dependent macrophage pyroptosis. In line with this regulatory axis, Yang et al41 identified extensive pyroptotic body accumulation within plaque macrophages, which exacerbates local tissue damage and forms a persistent inflammation-cell death cascade.

Taken together, the hyperlipidemic microenvironment initiates aberrant activation of the PDK1/Wnt pathway via miR-375 downregulation, and activated PDK1/Wnt/NLRP3 signaling in turn aggravates inflammation and lipid accumulation, forming a positive feedback loop. Our in vivo experiments further corroborate this reciprocal regulation: miR-375 agonist treatment significantly reduced lipid deposition in animal models, while miR-375 inhibition exacerbated lipid pathological lesions. Accordingly, the vicious cycle of “lipid disorder-induced miR-375 suppression → PDK1/Wnt/NLRP3 activation → inflammatory exacerbation → plaque destabilization” is continuously reinforced, ultimately accelerating LEASO deterioration.

In addition to lipid and inflammatory abnormalities, our clinical data revealed a 48% increase in plasma fibrinogen in LEASO patients, indicating a notable hypercoagulable state. Although the direct regulatory relationship between miR-375 and coagulation dysfunction remains unclarified, the well-documented role of the Wnt pathway in modulating coagulation factor expression42 suggests that the miR-375-PDK1-Wnt axis may also contribute to the thrombotic tendency of LEASO, offering a novel direction for further mechanistic and translational research.

Targeting the miR-375-PDK1 Axis: A New Direction for LEASO Intervention

A core finding of the present study is that exogenous supplementation of miR-375 agonists effectively ameliorates LEASO pathological phenotypes in animal models, exerting comprehensive protective effects across multiple pathological dimensions. Specifically, miR-375 agonist intervention markedly diminished aortic lipid deposition, as Oil Red O staining revealed a shift from diffuse lipid infiltration to focal lipid distribution in vascular tissues. Moreover, this treatment suppressed excessive collagen accumulation and reduced the collagen deposition area visualized by Masson staining, thereby alleviating arterial luminal stenosis. HE staining further demonstrated that miR-375 overexpression attenuated intimal hyperplasia and reduced lipid core size, while promoting fibrous cap thickening and enhancing plaque stability. In addition, miR-375 agonist intervention significantly mitigated systemic inflammatory status, with the expression of NLRP3 and IL-1β decreased by 29.1% and 39.0%, respectively.

Such multi-dimensional protective effects, including suppression of aberrant cell proliferation, promotion of vascular repair, anti-inflammatory activity, and plaque stabilization, highlight the therapeutic potential of restoring miR-375 expression to reverse LEASO-associated pathological alterations. Current mainstream clinical strategies for LEASO primarily rely on surgical revascularization and conventional risk factor management. However, revascularization is an invasive procedure, while traditional risk factor interventions fail to fundamentally reverse established atherosclerotic plaques.

Innovative targeted delivery strategies provide feasible technical support for miR-375-based vascular therapy. Lu et al43 constructed cell-penetrating peptide-modified miR-375 nanoparticles, which achieved a targeted delivery efficiency of 92% in a mouse ischemic model and effectively resolved the technical bottleneck of inefficient vascular RNA delivery. This delivery strategy shares similar technical principles with Patisiran, an FDA-approved RNA-targeted therapeutic agent,44 thereby supporting the translational potential of vascular-targeted miR-375 intervention. As a druggable serine/threonine kinase, PDK1 represents another promising therapeutic target. The small-molecule PDK1 inhibitor AR-12 has been widely investigated in tumor research.45 Notably, Schmid-Burgk et al46 developed the selective allosteric PDK1 inhibitor PDKi-638, which exhibits a 10-fold improvement in target specificity and remarkably reduced cardiovascular toxicity compared with conventional inhibitors. Consistent with these findings, our results showed that PDK1 knockdown recapitulates the protective effects of miR-375 overexpression, particularly in facilitating vascular repair and reversing the pathological alterations induced by miR-375 inhibition, indicating the prospective clinical value of PDK1 targeted inhibition in LEASO treatment.

Furthermore, combination therapy targeting the lipid-inflammation vicious cycle may generate synergistic therapeutic outcomes. The PREVAIL II trial confirmed that atorvastatin exerts anti-inflammatory effects by upregulating plaque miR-146a expression.47 Considering the dual regulatory roles of miR-375 in lipid metabolism and inflammatory suppression identified in the present study, combined administration of miR-375 agonists and statins may achieve dual-pathway intervention against lipid deposition and inflammation. This combinatorial strategy is expected to overcome the limited efficacy of single-agent treatment and provide a novel insight for optimized LEASO clinical intervention.

Limitations and Prospects

Several limitations of the present study should be acknowledged. First, this study primarily focused on the biological functions of the miR-375-PDK1 axis in vascular smooth muscle cells. The potential roles of this signaling axis in other vascular cell types, including endothelial cells and macrophages, were not comprehensively explored, which limits the full understanding of its vascular-wide regulatory effects in LEASO pathogenesis. Second, although our findings validated the transcriptional regulation of NLRP3 by the miR-375-PDK1-Wnt/β-catenin cascade, the secondary signaling events required for full NLRP3 inflammasome activation, such as potassium efflux and lysosomal rupture, were not investigated in this study. Third, the intrinsic association between miR-375 and lipid metabolism remains incompletely elucidated. The direct regulatory effects of miR-375 on key lipid metabolic modulators, including LDLR and ABCA1/G1, require further systematic verification. Fourth, all protective efficacy data were obtained from small animal models. Long-term therapeutic safety and efficacy assessments using large animal models are essential to facilitate the translational application of targeted therapy.

Conclusion

Through comprehensive multi-dimensional verification using clinical samples, animal models, and cellular experiments, this study elucidates a core signaling cascade driving LEASO pathogenesis: decreased miR-375 expression leads to PDK1 upregulation and persistent activation of the Wnt/β-catenin pathway, which induces excessive proliferation and migration of VSMCs, subsequent MMP activation, and ultimately triggers NLRP3 inflammasome-dependent inflammatory cascades. Despite the complex compensatory feedback responses observed in partial experimental settings, consistent evidence from gain-of-function and loss-of-function assays demonstrates that miR-375 exerts predominant protective effects against LEASO-associated vascular pathological damage via repression of the PDK1/Wnt/β-catenin axis. In preclinical LEASO models, restoration of miR-375 expression effectively retarded plaque progression, stabilized plaque microstructure, and alleviated systemic inflammatory responses. Collectively, these findings deepen the mechanistic understanding of LEASO and provide solid theoretical and experimental foundations for developing innovative therapeutic strategies targeting the miR-375-PDK1 axis.

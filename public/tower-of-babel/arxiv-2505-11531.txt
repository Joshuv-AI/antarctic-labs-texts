Control of semilinear differential equations with moving singularities
Radu Precup, Andrei Stan, Wei-Shih Du

Abstract
. In this paper, we present a control problem related to a semilinear differential equation with a moving singularity, i.e., the singular point depends on a parameter. The particularity of the controllability condition resides in the fact that it depends on the singular point which in turn depends on the control variable. We provide sufficient conditions to ensure that the functional determining the control is continuous over the entire domain of the parameter. Lower and upper solutions technique combined with a bisection algorithm is used to prove the controllability of the equation and to approximate the control. An example is given together with some numerical simulations. The results naturally extend to fractional differential equations.



Keywords.
Control problem, moving singularity,
differential equation

1.
Introduction and preliminaries

Differential equations are crucial in solving practical problems in many scientific fields, such as physics, chemistry, biology, economics, and engineering, etc., modeling many real-world processes. However,
the complexity of these phenomena often introduces various parameters that can significantly influence the outcome. A particularly intriguing problem in this context is identifying the parameters that ensure a specific quantity (e.g., density, energy) related to the solution of the differential
equation reaches a desired value. This challenge naturally leads to a control problem.

Our study has two strong motivations.

(a):

The first motivation concerns differential equations with moving singularities, which frequently appear in nonlinear models from applied sciences, such as physics and mathematical biology
[
1
]
.

(b):

The second one relates to the control of such models, aiming to reach a desired state of the process. For example, if the state variable represents a density, one might be interested in controlling its cumulative value or average. This corresponds precisely to our control problem in Section 2.

Mathematical models expressed through equations with singularities include the Briot-Bouquet equation, which has applications in complex analysis, specifically in the theory of univalent functions; equations arising in Michaelis-Menten kinetics, modeling oxygen diffusion in cells; the Thomas-Fermi equation in atomic physics; and the Emden-Fowler equation, in the study of phenomena in non-Newtonian fluid mechanics
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
.

Inspired by the investigation in
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
, this paper will explore the following problem

(1.1)

\begin{cases}u^{\prime}(t)=f(t,u(t),\lambda),\quad t\in\left[0,\,\theta(\lambda)\right)\\
u(0)=u_{0}(\lambda).\end{cases}

Here,

\ f:[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty)\rightarrow\bar{\mathbb{R}}(:=\mathbb{R\cup\{\pm\infty\}})
is a function that possesses a singularity in the first variable, influenced by the third one, that is, for each
\lambda>0
, there exists
\theta(\lambda)>0
such that

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\rightarrow\,\theta(\lambda)\\
t<\,\theta(\lambda)\end{subarray}}f(t,x,\lambda)=\pm\infty~\quad\text{for almost all }x\in\mathbb{R}.

Throughout this paper, we use
u_{\lambda}
to denote the unique
solution to problem (
1.1
) for a given
\lambda>0
.
Since the singularity point
\theta(\lambda)
varies with
\lambda
, the differential equation (
1.1
) is said to be with moving singularity.

For each
\lambda>0
, we consider the functional
\psi_{\lambda}\colon C[0,\theta(\lambda))\rightarrow\bar{\mathbb{R}}
,

(1.2)

\psi_{\lambda}(u)=\int_{0}^{\theta(\lambda)}u(s)\,ds,

where the integration over the noncompact interval
\left[0,\,\theta(\lambda)\right)
is understood in the usual sense (see, e.g.,
[
7
]
),

\int_{0}^{\theta(\lambda)}u(s)\,ds=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\rightarrow\theta(\lambda)\\
t<\theta(\lambda)\end{subarray}}\int_{0}^{t}u(s)\,ds.

Our goal in this paper is the following
control problem
:

Problem

(control problem)
.

Find
\lambda^{\ast}>0
such that

(1.3)

\psi_{\lambda^{\ast}}(u_{\lambda^{\ast}})=p,

where
p\in\mathbb{R}
is a given value.

The particularity of the controllability condition resides in the fact that
it depends on the singular point which in turn depends on the control
variable.
To establish sufficient conditions for the existence of a solution to this
control problem, we first guarantee that the mapping
\varphi\colon\left(0,\infty\right)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,

(1.4)

\varphi(\lambda)=\psi_{\lambda}(u_{\lambda}),

is well-defined and continuous on
(0,\infty)
. Then, we are able to use a
lower and upper solution argument to guarantee the existence of a
\lambda^{\ast}
with the desired property (
1.3
). Moreover, by
bisection algorithm, we have a method of approximation of the value
\lambda^{\ast}.

Control problems on a fixed interval that require to find a parameter in
order to achieve a specific controllability condition are well-documented in
the literature (see, e.g.,
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
). The novelty of this paper
lies in determining such a parameter where each solution is defined on a
different interval. This approach requires a more refined analysis and leads
to more complex problems.

The main assumptions we use in our analysis are

(h1)

For each
\lambda>0
and
0<\varepsilon<\theta(\lambda)
, there exists a constant
L=L(\lambda,\varepsilon)
such that
for all
x,\overline{x}\in\mathbb{R}
and for all
t\in\left[0,\,\theta(\lambda)-\varepsilon\right]
, we have

|f(t,x,\lambda)-f(t,\overline{x},\lambda)|\leq L(\lambda,\varepsilon)\,|x-\overline{x}|.

(h2)

The mappings

\lambda\mapsto\theta(\lambda),\quad\lambda\mapsto u_{0}(\lambda),\quad\lambda\mapsto L(\lambda,\varepsilon)\,\,(\text{for each }\varepsilon>0),

are continuous. Additionally, the map
(t,x,\lambda)\mapsto f(t,x,\lambda)

is continuous for
t\in[0,\,\theta(\lambda))
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
and
\lambda\in(0,\infty)
.

In the next lemma, we show that assumptions (h1), (h2) are sufficient to
guarantee the existence of a unique solution of problem (
1.1
).

Lemma 1.1
.

Under assumptions
(h1)
and
(h2)
, for each
\lambda>0
, there
exists a unique solution
u_{\lambda}\in C[0,\,\theta(\lambda))
of
problem (
1.1
)
. Moreover, this solution satisfies
the integral equation

(1.5)

u_{\lambda}(t)=u_{0}(\lambda)+\int_{0}^{t}f(s,u_{\lambda}(s),\lambda)\,ds,

for all
t\in\left[0,\,\theta(\lambda)\right).

Proof.

Let
\lambda>0
. For each
0<\varepsilon<\theta(\lambda)
, we consider
the initial value problem on the cut-off domain,

(1.6)

\begin{cases}u^{\prime}(t)=f(t,u(t),\lambda),&t\in\left[0,\,\theta(\lambda)-\varepsilon\right]\\
u(0)=u_{0}(\lambda).&\end{cases}

From assumption (h1), the mapping
x\mapsto f(t,x,\lambda)
is Lipschitz
continuous with the Lipschitz constant
L_{\lambda,\varepsilon}
.
Therefore, problem (
1.6
) has a unique solution (see, e.g.,
[
12
,
11
]
). The conclusion follows immediately by letting
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
and using the uniqueness of the solution on each interval
[0,\theta(\lambda)-\varepsilon]
. Relation (
1.5
) can be easily deduced by taking the integral in (
1.1
)
from
0
to
t.

∎

Example A
.

The model for
f(t,x,\lambda)
is given by

f(t,x,\lambda)=\frac{x}{\lambda-t}.

Clearly, there is a singularity at
\theta(\lambda)=\lambda
. However,
when the first variable is restricted to a compact interval
[0,\lambda-\varepsilon]
with
0<\varepsilon<\theta(\lambda)
, the function
f
is Lipschitz
continuous with
L(\lambda,\varepsilon)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
the
Lipschitz constant. If we set
u_{0}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{\lambda}>0
, the
unique solution of the problem (
1.1
) is

u_{\lambda}(t)=\frac{1}{\lambda-t}\,,\ \ \text{for all }t\in[0,\lambda).

Remark 1.1
.

Without imposing further assumptions beyond (h1) and
(h2), we cannot generally expect
\varphi
to be well-defined and
continuous on
(0,\infty)
. For instance, consider the case where

f(t,x,\lambda)=\frac{1}{\lambda(\lambda-t)^{\frac{1}{\lambda}+1}}\quad\text{and}\quad u_{0}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{\lambda^{1/\lambda}}.

Clearly, both assumptions (h1) and (h2) are satisfied. Straightforward
calculations yield

u_{\lambda}(t)=(\lambda-t)^{-\frac{1}{\lambda}},

for each
\lambda>1
. Consequently,

\varphi(\lambda)=\frac{1}{\lambda-1}\lambda^{\frac{2\lambda-1}{\lambda}}.

This implies that
\varphi(\lambda)\in\mathbb{R}
for
\lambda>1
, but

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\rightarrow 1\\
\lambda>1\end{subarray}}\varphi(\lambda)=\infty.

The paper is structured as follows: Section 2 presents the original results on the controllability of equations with moving singularities. Before establishing the main result (Theorem
2.6
), we derive several auxiliary results concerning the continuous dependence of solutions on the control parameter and the continuity of the control functional with respect to the control variable. In Section 3, we provide a theoretical algorithm for obtaining the solution of the control problem using the method of lower and upper solutions. We also provide an example that illustrates the applicability of the obtained results. Finally, in Section 4 we suggest a possible extension of our approach to fractional differential equations with moving singularities.

In the following, we present some well-known results from the literature
that will be used throughout this paper. The first result is the Arzelà-Ascoli theorem (see, e.g.,
[
13
,
14
]
).

Theorem 1.2

(Arzelà-Ascoli theorem)
.

A subset
F\subset\left(C[a,b],|\cdot|_{\infty}\right)
, where

|\cdot|_{\infty}
is the supremum norm, is relatively compact if
and only if it is uniformly bounded and equicontinuous, that is,
there exists
M>0
with

|u|_{\infty}\leq M,\quad\text{for all }u\in F,

and for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta(\varepsilon)>0
such
that

\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|\leq\varepsilon,\quad\text{for }x,y\in[a,b]\text{ with }\left|x-y\right|\leq\delta(\varepsilon)\text{ and all }u\in F.

The next lemma provides an alternative condition to ensure the convergence
of a sequence based on the behavior of its subsequences (see, e.g.,
[
15
, Lemma 1.1]
).

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
X
be a topological space, and let
(x_{n})
be a sequence in
X
with the following property: there exists
x\in X
such that from any subsequence of
(x_{n})
, a further subsequence can
be extracted that converges to
x
. Then the entire sequence
(x_{n})

converges to
x
.

Finally, we have the well known Grönwall’s lemma (see,
[
16
,
17
]
).

Theorem 1.4

(Grönwall inequality)
.

Let
u,v\in C[a,b]
. If there exists a constant
c>0
such that

\left|u(t)\right|\leq c+\int_{a}^{t}|u(s)|\,|v(s)|\,ds~\quad\text{for }t\in[a,b],

then

\left|u(t)\right|\leq c\exp\left(\int_{a}^{t}|v(s)|\,ds\right)~\quad\text{for }t\in[a,b].

2.
Auxiliary lemmas and new controllability result

2.1.
Proprieties of the solutions
u_{\lambda}

In this section, we present some properties of the solutions to problem (
1.1
), which will be used later in our analysis.

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume that assumptions
(h1)
and
(h2)
hold. Then,
given any sequence of positive real numbers
\left(\lambda_{k}\right)

converging to some
\lambda^{\ast}>0
, the sequence of solutions
\left(u_{\lambda_{k}}\right)
converges uniformly to
u_{\lambda^{\ast}}
on
any interval
[0,\,\theta(\lambda^{\ast})-\varepsilon]
, with
0<\varepsilon<\min\left\{\inf_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\theta\left(\lambda_{k}\right),\theta(\lambda^{\ast})\right\}
.

Proof.

Let
\left(\lambda_{k}\right)
be a sequence of positive real numbers
with
\lambda_{k}\rightarrow\lambda^{\ast}>0
as
k\rightarrow\infty
,
and let

(2.1)

0<\varepsilon<\min\left\{\inf_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\theta\left(\lambda_{k}\right),\theta(\lambda^{\ast})\right\}.

Since the mapping
\lambda\mapsto\theta(\lambda)
is continuous, there
exists
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\theta(\lambda^{\ast})-\varepsilon\leq\theta\left(\lambda_{k}\right)-\frac{\varepsilon}{2},

for all
k\geq k_{0}
. Clearly, the choise of
\varepsilon
given in
\left(\ref{alegere_epsilon}\right)
guarantees that both
\theta(\lambda^{\ast})-\varepsilon\
and
\theta\left(\lambda_{k}\right)-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
are strictly positive, for all
k\geq k_{0}.

Using condition (h1), for each
k\geq k_{0}
, there exists
L\left(\lambda_{k},\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)>0
such that

\left|f\left(t,x,\lambda_{k}\right)-f\left(t,\overline{x},\lambda_{k}\right)\right|\leq L\left(\lambda_{k},\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)\left|x-\overline{x}\right|

for all
t\in[0,~\theta(\lambda_{k})-\varepsilon]
and all
x,\overline{x}\in\mathbb{R}
. Moreover, from the continuity of the mapping
\lambda\mapsto L\left(\lambda,\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)
, there
exists a positive real number
\bar{L}>0
such that

L\left(\lambda_{k},\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)\leq\bar{L}\quad\text{for all }k\geq k_{0}.

Furthermore, using the Arzelà-Ascoli theorem, we show that the set

A:=\left\{u_{\lambda_{k}}:[0,\theta\left(\lambda^{\ast}\right)-\varepsilon]\rightarrow\mathbb{R},\ k\geq k_{0}\right\}

is relatively compact in
C[0,\theta\left(\lambda^{\ast}\right)-\varepsilon]
. First, we establish the uniform boundedness of the set
A
.
Let
k\geq k_{0}
and let
t\in[0,\,\theta\left(\lambda^{\ast}\right)-\varepsilon]
. Using the integral form (
1.5
) of

u_{\lambda_{k}}
, we obtain

(2.2)

\displaystyle\left|u_{\lambda_{k}}(t)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|u_{0}(\lambda_{k})\right|+\int_{0}^{t}\left|f(s,u_{\lambda_{k}}(s),\lambda_{k})-f(s,0,\lambda_{k})\right|ds+\int_{0}^{t}\left|f(s,0,\lambda_{k})\right|ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|u_{0}(\lambda_{k})\right|+\int_{0}^{t}e^{s\rho}e^{-s\rho}\bar{L}\,\left|u_{\lambda_{k}}(s)\right|ds+\int_{0}^{t}\left|f(s,0,\lambda_{k})\right|ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|u_{\lambda_{k}}\right|_{\rho}\frac{\bar{L}}{\rho}e^{t\rho}+M,

where

\left|u\right|_{\rho}=\sup_{t\in[0,\,\,\theta\left(\lambda^{\ast}\right)-\varepsilon]}e^{-t\rho}\left|u(t)\right|,

is the Bielecki norm on
C[0,\theta\left(\lambda^{\ast}\right)-\varepsilon]
, and

M=\sup_{k\geq k_{0}}\left(\left|u_{0}(\lambda_{k})\right|+\int_{0}^{\theta\left(\lambda^{\ast}\right)-\varepsilon}\left|f\left(s,0,\lambda_{k}\right)\right|ds\right).

Note that
M<\infty
. Indeed, the continuity of the mapping
(t,\lambda)\mapsto f(t,0,\lambda)
guarantees that it is uniformly bounded on the
compact set

(t,\lambda)\in\left[0,\theta\left(\lambda^{\ast}\right)-\varepsilon\right]\times\left[\inf_{k\geq k_{0}}\lambda_{k},\sup_{k\geq k_{0}}\lambda_{k}\right],

since
\inf_{k\geq k_{0}}\lambda_{k}>0\text{ and }\sup_{k\geq k_{0}}\lambda_{k}<\infty.
Dividing (
2.2
) by
e^{t\rho}
and taking
the supremum over
[0,\,\theta\left(\lambda^{\ast}\right)-\varepsilon]
, we obtain

(2.3)

\left|u_{\lambda_{k}}\right|_{\rho}\leq\frac{\bar{L}}{\rho}\left|u_{\lambda_{k}}\right|_{\rho}+M.

Thus, if we choose
\rho>\bar{L}
, relation (
2.3
) ensures the uniform
boundedness of the sequence
\left(u_{\lambda_{k}}\right)_{k\geq k_{0}}
. Let
D
be an upper bound for this sequence, that is,

D=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in[0,\,\theta(\lambda^{\ast})-\varepsilon]\\
k\geq k_{0}\end{subarray}}\left|u_{\lambda_{k}}(t)\right|.

For any
t,\overline{t}\in[0,\,\theta(\lambda^{\ast})-\varepsilon]
and
k\geq k_{0}
, one has

\left|u_{\lambda_{k}}(t)-u_{\lambda_{k}}(\overline{t})\right|\leq\left|\int_{\overline{t}}^{t}\left|f(s,u_{\lambda_{k}}(s),\lambda_{k})\right|ds\right|\leq\bar{M}\left|t-\overline{t}\right|,

where

\bar{M}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in[0,\,\theta(\lambda^{\ast})-\varepsilon]\\
\left|x\right|\leq D,\,k\geq k_{0}\end{subarray}}\left|f(t,x,\lambda_{k})\right|.

Consequently, the set
A
is equicontinuous. By the Arzelà-Ascoli
theorem, there exists a subsequence
\left(u_{\lambda_{k_{n}}}\right)
of

\left(u_{\lambda_{k}}\right)
that converges uniformly to
u^{\ast}
on
[0,\,\theta\left(\lambda^{\ast}\right)-\varepsilon]
.

We now show that

u^{\ast}(t)=u_{\lambda^{\ast}}(t)~\quad\text{for all }t\in[0,\,\theta\left(\lambda^{\ast}\right)-\varepsilon].

Let
t\in[0,\,\theta\left(\lambda^{\ast}\right)-\varepsilon]
.
Then

(2.4)

\displaystyle\left|u^{\ast}(t)-u_{\lambda^{\ast}}(t)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|u_{\lambda_{k_{n}}}(t)-u_{\lambda^{\ast}}(t)\right|+\left|u_{\lambda_{k_{n}}}(t)-u^{\ast}(t)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|u_{0}\left(\lambda_{k_{n}}\right)-u_{0}\left(\lambda^{\ast}\right)\right|+\int_{0}^{t}\left|f\left(s,u_{\lambda_{k_{n}}}(s),\lambda_{k_{n}}\right)-f\left(s,u_{\lambda^{\ast}}(s),\lambda^{\ast}\right)\right|\,ds

\displaystyle\quad+\left|u_{\lambda_{k_{n}}}(t)-u^{\ast}(t)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\left|f\left(s,u_{\lambda_{k_{n}}}(s),\lambda_{k_{n}}\right)-f\left(s,u_{\lambda^{\ast}}(s),\lambda_{k_{n}}\right)\right|\,ds

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\left|f\left(s,u_{\lambda^{\ast}}(s),\lambda_{k_{n}}\right)-f\left(s,u_{\lambda^{\ast}}(s),\lambda^{\ast}\right)\right|\,ds

\displaystyle\quad+\left|u_{\lambda_{k_{n}}}(t)-u^{\ast}(t)\right|+\left|u_{0}\left(\lambda_{k_{n}}\right)-u_{0}\left(\lambda^{\ast}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{e^{\rho t}}{\rho}\bar{L}\left|u_{\lambda_{k_{n}}}-u_{\lambda^{\ast}}\right|_{\rho}+\int_{0}^{t}\left|f\left(s,u_{\lambda^{\ast}}(s),\lambda_{k_{n}}\right)-f\left(s,u_{\lambda^{\ast}}(s),\lambda^{\ast}\right)\right|\,ds

\displaystyle\quad+\left|u_{\lambda_{k_{n}}}(t)-u^{\ast}(t)\right|+\left|u_{0}\left(\lambda_{k_{n}}\right)-u_{0}\left(\lambda^{\ast}\right)\right|.

Multiplying (
2.4
) by
e^{-\rho t}
and taking the supremum over
[0,\theta\left(\lambda^{\ast}\right)-\varepsilon],
we find

(2.5)

\displaystyle\left|u^{\ast}-u_{\lambda^{\ast}}\right|_{\rho}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\bar{L}}{\rho}\left|u_{\lambda_{k_{n}}}-u_{\lambda^{\ast}}\right|_{\rho}+\int_{0}^{t}\left|f\left(s,u_{\lambda^{\ast}}(s),\lambda_{k_{n}}\right)-f\left(s,u_{\lambda^{\ast}}(s),\lambda^{\ast}\right)\right|ds

\displaystyle+\left|u_{\lambda_{k_{n}}}-u^{\ast}\right|_{\infty}+\left|u_{0}\left(\lambda_{k_{n}}\right)-u_{0}\left(\lambda^{\ast}\right)\right|.

Since the last three terms from the right hand side of (
2.5
) tends to
zero and

\left|u_{\lambda_{k_{n}}}-u_{\lambda^{\ast}}\right|_{\rho}\rightarrow\left|u^{\ast}-u_{\lambda^{\ast}}\right|_{\rho},

choosing
\rho>\bar{L}
, we find that
\left|u^{\ast}-u_{\lambda^{\ast}}\right|_{\rho}=0,
which means that

u_{\lambda^{\ast}}(t)=u^{\ast}(t)\quad\text{for all }t\in[0,\,\theta(\lambda^{\ast})-\varepsilon].

The conclusion of our theorem now follows directly from Theorem
1.3
. Indeed, for any subsequence
\left(u_{\lambda_{k}}\right)
, using the same procedure described above, we can extract a
further subsequence that converges to
u_{\lambda^{\ast}}
on the interval

[0,\theta(\lambda^{\ast})-\varepsilon]
. Therefore, Theorem
1.3
applies and guarantees that on
[0,\theta(\lambda^{\ast})-\varepsilon]
, the entire sequence
\left(u_{\lambda_{k}}\right)
converges to
u_{\lambda^{\ast}}
.
∎

For any
\varepsilon>0
, we denote

B_{\varepsilon}:=\theta^{-1}\left([2\varepsilon,\infty)\right)=\left\{\lambda>0\,:\,\theta(\lambda)\geq 2\varepsilon\right\}.

In the following two results, for some
\varepsilon>0
and
\lambda\in B_{\varepsilon}
, we establish certain properties of the solutions
u_{\lambda}
over the interval
[0,\theta(\lambda)-\varepsilon]
. Since
\lambda
belongs to
B_{\varepsilon}
, it follows that
\theta(\lambda)-\varepsilon\geq\varepsilon>0
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume that conditions
(h1)
and
(h2)
hold.
Then, for each
\varepsilon>0
, there is a continuous function

\tau_{\varepsilon}\colon B_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow\left(0,\infty\right),

such that

\left|u_{\lambda}\left(t\right)\right|\leq\tau_{\varepsilon}\left(\lambda\right)\quad\text{for all $t\in\left[0,\,\theta(\lambda)-\varepsilon\right]$},

and every
\lambda\in B_{\varepsilon}.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be fixed with
B_{\varepsilon}
nonempty,
\lambda\in B_{\varepsilon}
and
t\in[0,\,\theta(\lambda)-\varepsilon]
.
Using the integral form (
1.5
) of
u_{\lambda}
,
straightforward computations yield,

\left|u_{\lambda}(t)\right|\leq M_{1}+\int_{0}^{t}\left|f(s,u_{\lambda}(s),\lambda)-f(s,0,\lambda)\right|,

where

M_{1}=\left|u_{0}(\lambda)\right|+\int_{0}^{\theta(\lambda)-\varepsilon}\left|f(s,0,\lambda)\right|\,ds.

From (h2), there exists
L(\lambda,\varepsilon)>0
such that

\left|f(s,x,\lambda)-f(s,0,\lambda)\right|\leq L(\lambda,\varepsilon)\left|x\right|,

for all
s\in[0,\theta(\lambda)-\varepsilon]
and all
x\in\mathbb{R}.
Thus,

(2.6)

\left|u_{\lambda}(t)\right|\leq M_{1}+L(\lambda,\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{t}\left|u_{\lambda}(s)\right|\,ds.

Using Grönwall’s lemma in (
2.6
) gives

\left|u_{\lambda}(t)\right|\leq M_{1}e^{(\theta(\lambda)-\varepsilon)L(\lambda,\varepsilon)}:=\tau_{\varepsilon}(\lambda)\quad\text{for all $t\in[0,\theta(\lambda)-\varepsilon]$,}

while the continuity of
\lambda\mapsto\theta(\lambda),\ L(\lambda,\varepsilon)
ensures that
\tau_{\varepsilon}
is continuous.
∎

In the next result, we prove an important relation between
the solutions
u_{\lambda}
and the integral.

Theorem 2.3
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
, the mapping
\ \xi\colon B_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,

\xi(\lambda):=\int_{0}^{\theta(\lambda)-\varepsilon}u_{\lambda}(s)\,ds,

is continuous.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
such that
B_{\varepsilon}
is nonempty, and let
\left(\lambda_{k}\right)\subset B_{\varepsilon}
be a sequence with
\lambda_{k}\rightarrow\lambda
as
k\rightarrow\infty
. Clearly,
\lambda\in B_{\varepsilon}
as the set
B_{\varepsilon}
is closed.
Lemma
2.1
guarantees that
u_{\lambda_{k}}
converges uniformly to
u_{\lambda}
on the interval
[0,\,\theta(\lambda)-\varepsilon]
.
Therefore, using Lemma
2.2
, we have

(2.7)

\displaystyle\left|\xi\left(\lambda_{k}\right)-\xi(\lambda)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\theta(\lambda)-\varepsilon}\left|u_{\lambda_{k}}(s)-u_{\lambda}(s)\right|ds+\left|\int_{\theta(\lambda)-\varepsilon}^{\theta\left(\lambda_{k}\right)-\varepsilon}\left|u_{\lambda_{k}}(s)\right|ds\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\theta(\lambda)-\varepsilon}\left|u_{\lambda_{k}}(s)-u_{\lambda}(s)\right|ds+\tau_{\varepsilon}\left(\lambda_{k}\right)\left|\theta\left(\lambda_{k}\right)-\theta(\lambda)\right|.

Since the continuity of
\tau_{\varepsilon}
ensures that the sequence
\left(\tau_{\varepsilon}\left(\lambda_{k}\right)\right)
is uniformly
bounded, we deduce that the right-hand side of inequality (
2.7
)
converges to zero, implying that
\xi\left(\lambda_{k}\right)
converges
to
\xi(\lambda)
. Finally, since the above result holds for any sequence
within
B_{\varepsilon}
, we conclude our proof.
∎

2.2.
The continuity of
\varphi
.

In this subsection, we prove that the mapping
\varphi
defined in (
1.4
)
is well defined and continuous. As noted in Remark
1.1
,
assumptions (h1) and (h2) alone are insufficient to guarantee that
\varphi

is well-defined on
(0,\infty)
. Thus, we introduce the next growth
condition on the function
f
, aligning it with the typical model described in Example B from below.

(h3)

There exists a constant
a>1
such that, for all
\lambda>0
, one has

\left|f(t,x,\lambda)\right|\leq\frac{|x|}{a(\theta(\lambda)-t)}+C_{\lambda}

for all
t\in[0,\theta(\lambda))
and all
x\in\mathbb{R}
, where

C_{\lambda}\geq 0
and the map
\lambda\mapsto C_{\lambda}
is
continuous.

Lemma 2.4
.

Under assumptions
(h1)-(h3)
, for any sequence of positive
real numbers
\left(\lambda_{k}\right)
converging to some
\lambda>0
,
and for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)>0
and

\left|\int_{\theta\left(\lambda_{k}\right)-\delta}^{\theta\left(\lambda_{k}\right)}u_{\lambda_{k}}(s)\,ds\right|<\varepsilon\quad\text{for all }k\geq k\left(\varepsilon\right).

Proof.

Assume the contrary. Then, we may find a sequence
\left(\lambda_{k}\right)
with
\lambda_{k}>0
for all
k
,
\lambda_{k}\rightarrow\lambda>0
as
k\rightarrow\infty
, and
\varepsilon>0
such that for
any
\delta,
let it be
\delta_{n}=\frac{1}{n}
and any

say
k_{0}=n

(2.8)

From (

(2.9)

\left|u_{\lambda_{k_{n}}}(t)\right|\leq\mathcal{C}+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{|u_{\lambda_{k_{n}}}(s)|}{a(\theta\left(\lambda_{k_{n}}\right)-s)}\,ds,

for all

Applying Grönwall’s lemma to (

\displaystyle\left|u_{\lambda_{k_{n}}}(t)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{C}\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{a(\theta\left(\lambda_{k_{n}}\right)-s)}\,ds\right)

The conclusion follows immediately passing to limit in (

Theorem 2.5
.

Assume that conditions
(h1)-(h3)
are satisfied. Then,
the mapping
\varphi:\left(0,\infty\right)\rightarrow\mathbb{R},

\varphi:\left(0,\infty\right)\rightarrow\mathbb{R},\,\,\varphi(\lambda)=\int_{0}^{\theta(\lambda)}u_{\lambda}(s)\,ds

is well-defined and continuous.

Proof.

Note that for each
\lambda>0

\int_{0}^{\theta(\lambda)}u_{\lambda}(s)ds

2.4

\lambda>0

(2.11)

\left|u_{\lambda}(t)\right|\leq\frac{c_{1}}{(\theta(\lambda)-t)^{1/a}}+c_{2},

for all

Consequently,
integrating relation (
2.11
) from
0
to

gives

\int_{0}^{\theta(\lambda)}|u_{\lambda}(s)|ds\leq c_{3}\theta(\lambda)^{\frac{a-1}{a}}+c_{4}<\infty,

where
c_{3},c_{4}
are constants dependent on
\lambda

\varphi

is well-defined.

To prove its continuity, consider any sequence
(\lambda_{k})
with
\lambda_{k}>0
for all
k

\lambda_{k}\rightarrow\lambda>0
as

, and any
\varepsilon>0

there exists
\delta^{\prime}>0
and
k_{0}
such that

(2.12)

\left|\int_{\theta\left(\lambda_{k}\right)-\delta^{\prime}}^{\theta\left(\lambda_{k}\right)}u_{\lambda_{k}}\left(s\right)ds\right|<\varepsilon\ \quad\text{for all }k\geq k_{0}.

Additionally, since the integral given

\delta^{\prime\prime}>0

(2.13)

\left|\int_{\theta(\lambda)-\delta^{\prime\prime}}^{\theta(\lambda)}u_{\lambda}\left(s\right)ds\right|<\varepsilon.

Denote

Theorem
2.5
leads immediately to the following controllability result
when a lower solution and an upper one are known.

Theorem 2.6
.

Assume that conditions
(h1)-(h3)
hold and that there exist
\underline{\lambda},\overline{\lambda}>0
such that

\varphi\left(\underline{\lambda}\right)<p\quad\text{and}\quad\varphi\left(\overline{\lambda}\right)>p.

Then there is
\lambda^{\ast},
intermediate between
\underline{\lambda}

and
\overline{\lambda},
such that
\psi_{\lambda^{\ast}}(u_{\lambda^{\ast}})=p.

Proof.

The conclusion follows directly by applying Darboux’s intermediate value
theorem to the continuous function
\varphi
(see, e.g.,
[
18
, Theorem 4.23]
).
∎

3.
Approximate solving of the control problem

Starting from the lower and upper solutions
\underline{\lambda},

\overline{\lambda},
one can approximate
\lambda^{\ast}
by using the
following algorithm. For a similar use of this method, we refer the reader to
[
19
]
.

Algorithm

(Bisection algorithm)
.

Step 0 (initialization)
:
k:=0,\ \underline{\lambda}_{0}:=\underline{\lambda},

\overline{\lambda}_{0}:=

\overline{\lambda}
.

Step

k\ \left(k\geq 1\right):
compute
\lambda:=\frac{\underline{\lambda}_{k-1}+\overline{\lambda}_{k-1}}{2};

\bullet

If
\varphi\left(\lambda\right)=p,
then
\lambda^{\ast}=\lambda
and
we are finished;

\bullet

If
\varphi\left(\lambda\right)<p,
then set
\underline{\lambda}_{k}:=\lambda
and
\overline{\lambda}_{k}:=\overline{\lambda}_{k-1}
and
repeat Step
k
with
k:=k+1;

\bullet

If
\varphi\left(\lambda\right)>p,
then set
\underline{\lambda}_{k}:=\underline{\lambda}_{k-1}
and
\overline{\lambda}_{k}:=\lambda
and
repeat Step
k
with
k:=k+1;

Stop criterion
: if
\left|\varphi\left(\lambda\right)-p\right|\leq\varepsilon,
then
\lambda\simeq\lambda^{\ast}
(with error
\varepsilon
).

We note that this step-by-step algorithm iteratively approximates the control solution. At each step, based on the obtained feedback, either the subsolution or the supersolution is improved.

Theorem 3.1
.

Under assumptions
(h1)-(h3)
, the bisection algorithm is convergent to
a solution
\lambda^{\ast}
of the control problem.

Proof.

If the algorithm does not stop after a finite number of steps, then it
generates two bounded and monotone (so convergent) sequences
\left(\underline{\lambda}_{k}\right)
and
\left(\overline{\lambda}_{k}\right),
which in addition satisfy

(3.1)

\left|\overline{\lambda}_{k}-\underline{\lambda}_{k}\right|=\frac{\left|\overline{\lambda}-\underline{\lambda}\right|}{2^{k}}\ \ \left(k\geq 0\right),

(3.2)

\varphi\left(\underline{\lambda}_{k}\right)<p,\ \ \ \varphi\left(\overline{\lambda}_{k}\right)>p.

From (
3.1
), the two sequences have the same limit denoted
\lambda^{\ast},
while from (
3.2
), in virtue of the continuity Theorem
2.5
, we obtain

\varphi\left(\lambda^{\ast}\right)\leq p\ \ \ \text{and\ \ \ }\varphi\left(\lambda^{\ast}\right)\geq p.

Hence
\varphi\left(\lambda^{\ast}\right)=p
as desired.
∎

4.
Extension to fractional differential equations

The above results can be generalized to fractional differential equations with moving singularities. Such problems more accurately describe various physical, biological, or medical processes (see, e.g.,
[
20
,
21
,
22
,
23
]
). Therefore, our results related to problem (
1.1
) can be extended to the following problem:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}{}^{c}D^{\alpha}u(t)=f(t,u(t),\lambda),\quad t\in[0,\theta(\lambda))\\
u(0)=u_{0}(\lambda),\end{cases}

where
{}^{c}D^{\alpha}
is the Caputo fractional derivative and
0<\alpha<1
.
As shown in the literature, the above problem is equivalent to the Voltera integral equation

u_{\lambda}(t)=u_{0}(\lambda)+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}f(s,u(s),\lambda)ds.

Note that, in our case, the control problem remain unchanged, i.e., find
\lambda
such that

\int_{0}^{\theta(\lambda)}u_{\lambda}(s)ds=p.

Since our entire analysis is grounded in the integral form of the Cauchy problem, we can easily extend the proof steps to address this more general case. The flexibility of the integral formulation allows for the adaptation of our methods without significant modifications. By imposing conditions similar to those outlined in (h1) and (h2), we can rigorously establish the controllability of the problem.

5.
Conclusions

The analyzed control problem in this paper is atypical in several aspects: (a) it refers to equations with singularity; (b) the singularity itself depends on the control variable; (c) the controllability condition involves the moving singularity. All these aspects make the analysis much more complex and adapted to the specifics of the problem. The working techniques can also be taken into account for the investigation of other types of singular equations and controllability conditions including singular partial differential equations (see, e.g.,
[
6
]
). We believe and anticipate that the ideas and techniques used in this article will have the high degree of suitability for the specifics of each individual problem in future research.

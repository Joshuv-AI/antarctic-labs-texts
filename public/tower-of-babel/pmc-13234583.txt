Case report: Maribavir as salvage therapy for multidrug-resistant CMV DNAemia in a non-transplant patient with ocular cicatricial pemphigoid
Authors: Balachandran R Vaidyanathan, Akshaya Balaji
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractCytomegalovirus (CMV) is a significant cause of morbidity in immunocompromised patients, particularly those receiving chronic systemic therapy for autoimmune diseases such as ocular cicatricial pemphigoid (OCP). While the novel oral agent maribavir is approved for refractory CMV in post-transplant recipients, data regarding its efficacy in non-transplant populations remain scarce. We report the case of a 45-year-old female with OCP and comorbid Crohn's disease who developed multidrug resistant (MDR) CMV gastroenterocolitis. After failing sequential therapy with ganciclovir and foscarnet due to the emergence of UL97 (M460V/I) and UL54 (V781I) mutations, she achieved complete virological clearance with maribavir. This case highlights a novel, off-label application of maribavir in a non-transplant host with complex iatrogenic immunosuppression and sequential multidrug resistant CMV, demonstrating its role as a potent salvage therapy that avoids the nephrotoxicity associated with traditional agents.Keywords: Maribavir, Cytomegalovirus, Ocular cicatricial pemphigoid, Multidrug resistance, UL54 mutation

Introduction

Ocular cicatricial pemphigoid (OCP) is a chronic, cicatrizing autoimmune bullous disease that often requires aggressive, long-term systemic immunosuppression to prevent progressive ocular scarring and subsequent blindness [1]. The cumulative immunosuppressive burden from chronic use of corticosteroids and steroid-sparing agents creates a high risk of opportunistic infections, notably reactivation of latent cytomegalovirus [2]. Management of refractory or resistant CMV in this population is exceptionally challenging. Conventional antiviral therapies such as ganciclovir and foscarnet are frequently limited by significant systemic toxicities and the emergence of drug-resistant viral strains [3]. Until recently, therapeutic options for multidrug-resistant CMV—defined as resistance to two or more classes of antivirals—were restricted to toxic salvage agents such as foscarnet and cidofovir [4].

Maribavir, a novel UL97 protein kinase inhibitor, has demonstrated superiority over investigator assigned therapy in transplant recipients with refractory or resistant CMV [5], [6]. However, its application in non-transplant immunocompromised populations remains poorly established in the literature [7], [8]. We present a case of multidrug-resistant CMV DNAemia in a patient with OCP successfully treated with maribavir, providing insight into its use in complex autoimmune cohorts.

Case presentation

Patient history

A 45-year-old female presented with a one month history of nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, and unintentional weight loss. Her history was significant for ocular cicatricial pemphigoid, diagnosed in 2015, which had resulted in vision loss. Her disease was managed with a complex immunosuppressive regimen including prednisone (40 mg once daily), mycophenolate mofetil (500 mg three times daily), and difluprednate ophthalmic drops twice daily. She had previously been treated with methotrexate, rituximab, cyclophosphamide, and adalimumab without significant benefit. She had been steroid-dependent since her initial diagnosis.

Her comorbidities included gastroparesis and Crohn's disease, managed with sulfasalazine. At presentation, her symptoms were initially treated as a Crohn's flare with oral steroids; however, her condition worsened.

Diagnosis and initial management

Endoscopy and colonoscopy revealed multiple discoid ulcers in the esophagus, descending colon, and sigmoid colon. Histopathology demonstrated intranuclear inclusion bodies, and immunohistochemistry was positive for CMV. CMV IgG testing was positive, suggesting reactivation rather than primary infection.

Quantitative CMV DNAemia was measured using the cobas 6800 CMV quantitative nucleic acid test (calibrated to the WHO International Standard and reported in IU/mL; values <34.5 IU/mL indicated detectable but not quantifiable CMV DNA, whereas “target not detected” was considered undetectable). Initial DNAemia was 34,700 IU/mL, peaking at 111,000 IU/mL. A Viracor CMV-specific T-cell immunoassay showed low CD8 CMV interferon-gamma cells (0.19) and CD4 cells at 0.82, indicating incomplete immune reconstitution.

Antiviral treatment and resistance profile

The patient was initially started on intravenous ganciclovir (250 mg twice daily). Her DNAemia decreased to 76.9 IU/mL, and she was transitioned to oral valganciclovir (900 mg twice daily). However, her symptoms recurred after three weeks. She was readmitted with severe sepsis due to E. coli bacteremia and acute renal failure. CMV DNAemia rebounded to 15,200 IU/mL. Next-generation sequencing of the CMV UL97, UL54, and UL56 genes revealed UL97 kinase mutations (M460I, M460V) conferring ganciclovir resistance, without detectable resistance to foscarnet or maribavir.

She was subsequently started on foscarnet with a loading dose of 90 mg/kg every 12 h (patient weight 63 kg) after recovering from sepsis and acute renal failure. After approximately one week, CMV DNAemia decreased to 1240 IU/mL and subsequently became undetectable. She completed three weeks of foscarnet induction therapy, followed by maintenance foscarnet infusions (3730.8 mg IV daily).

Although her DNAemia remained undetectable for several weeks, it later rose to 1090 IU/mL. Repeat genotyping identified a UL54 polymerase mutation (V781I), conferring resistance to both ganciclovir and foscarnet but not to cidofovir, maribavir, or letermovir. Soon after, she was admitted with worsening kidney injury, electrolyte abnormalities, and recurrent CMV DNAemia accompanied by worsening vomiting and diarrhea. Renal failure was managed with intravenous fluids and electrolyte replacement.

She was then started on cidofovir (4 mg/kg weekly) with probenecid as third-line therapy. After four weeks, CMV DNAemia improved from 1090 IU/mL to 918 IU/mL and then to 122 IU/mL. Therapy was transitioned to avoid further nephrotoxicity.

Novel use of maribavir

To address multidrug-resistant CMV DNAemia, maribavir was initiated at 200 mg twice daily and subsequently increased to 400 mg twice daily. At initiation, CMV DNAemia was 87 IU/mL. Within four weeks, DNAemia became undetectable. Clinically, her diarrhea resolved, and she began gaining weight.

Concurrent with maribavir therapy, prednisone was tapered to 20 mg daily and eventually discontinued to facilitate immune reconstitution. Although she experienced a minor ocular flare during the taper, she was ultimately managed with ophthalmic steroids alone.

Maribavir was continued for eight weeks to prevent recurrence. She was closely monitored in the Infectious Diseases clinic with serial CMV DNAemia testing and was eventually started on prophylactic letermovir.

After sustained undetectable DNAemia, repeat upper endoscopy and colonoscopy performed for evaluation of dysphagia, chronic diarrhea, and prior CMV gastroenterocolitis were endoscopically normal, with a normal esophagus, stomach, duodenum, terminal ileum, and colon. Immunohistochemical staining for CMV was negative.

Discussion

CMV infection presents a profound clinical challenge in patients with autoimmune bullous diseases maintained on potent immunosuppressive regimens. Although CMV management has traditionally focused on transplant populations, patients receiving chronic immunosuppression for autoimmune conditions such as cicatricial pemphigoid experience a similar state of iatrogenic immunodeficiency that facilitates viral reactivation [2].

This case contributes to the literature by demonstrating successful use of maribavir in a non-transplant patient with documented multidrug resistant CMV [7], [8]. It represents both refractory and resistant infection, defined by failure to achieve viral clearance despite multiple induction-dose antiviral courses [9].

The emergence of antiviral resistance remains a substantial barrier to successful treatment. The initial UL97 mutations (M460I, M460V) are canonical resistance markers for ganciclovir [3]. The subsequent UL54 polymerase mutation (V781I) created a MDR profile, conferring resistance to both ganciclovir and foscarnet and limiting therapeutic options to nephrotoxic agents [4], [10].

Maribavir acts by targeting the UL97 protein kinase at a site distinct from ganciclovir resistance loci, disrupting viral replication and encapsidation. Although currently approved for post-transplant recipients, its use here highlights its potential as a salvage agent that avoids the nephrotoxicity associated with foscarnet and cidofovir [5], [6].

Clinicians must remain vigilant for potential cross-resistance between maribavir and ganciclovir. While maribavir retains activity against most ganciclovir resistant UL97 mutations, mutations at codons T409M, H411L, and C480F may confer high-level resistance to both agents [11], [12]. In this case, the UL97 mutations (M460V/I) did not confer cross-resistance, allowing maribavir to remain effective despite the UL54 mutation.

Additionally, maribavir may have higher failure rates when initiated at viral loads > 9100 IU/mL due to treatment-emergent resistance [10]. Bridging therapy with cidofovir reduced DNAemia to 87 IU/mL prior to maribavir initiation, likely optimizing efficacy and mitigating resistance risk.

Sustained CMV control ultimately requires host immune recovery, often impaired by high-dose corticosteroids [10]. CMV-specific T-cell immunoassay confirmed inadequate protective immunity in this patient, likely due to chronic prednisone and mycophenolate mofetil use. Potent viral suppression with maribavir permitted safe systemic steroid tapering, facilitating immune reconstitution without precipitating a catastrophic ocular flare [13].

Ultimately, rapid clearance of DNAemia is associated with improved clinical outcomes and reduced mortality in severely immunocompromised hosts [14]. These findings align with broader reviews highlighting ongoing challenges in CMV management despite newer antiviral options [15].

Conclusion

Maribavir is a safe and effective salvage therapy for multidrug-resistant CMV DNAemia in patients with OCP. This case demonstrates that maribavir can achieve viral clearance and clinical symptom resolution even in the presence of multiple UL97 and UL54 mutations.

Postoperative risk profile in elderly hip fracture patients undergoing chronic hemodialysis based on a nationwide database investigation
Authors: Yu Mori, Kunio Tarasawa, Hidetatsu Tanaka, Ryuichi Kanabuchi, Naoko Mori, Kiyohide Fushimi, Toshimi Aizawa, Kenji Fujimori
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractHip fractures in elderly dialysis patients are associated with increased postoperative complications and mortality, but large-scale analyses remain limited. This retrospective cohort study utilized Japan’s nationwide Diagnosis Procedure Combination database (2016–2022). After applying 1:1 propensity score matching, 9,601 chronic hemodialysis patients were compared with 9,601 non-dialysis patients for postoperative outcomes, including venous thromboembolism (VTE), cognitive dysfunction, and in-hospital mortality. The study cohort included only patients receiving chronic maintenance hemodialysis; peritoneal dialysis patients were not included. Statistical significance was defined as a p < 0.001. The in-hospital mortality rate was 5.2% in dialysis patients versus 1.7% in non-dialysis patients. Thirty-day postoperative survival was 99.2% in non-dialysis patients and 97.5% in dialysis patients. Dialysis patients had higher odds of postoperative cognitive dysfunction (OR: 1.944; 95% CI: 1.515–2.495, p < 0.0001) and in-hospital mortality (OR: 3.288; 95% CI: 2.743–3.941, p < 0.0001). The incidence of VTE was lower among dialysis patients. Male sex, older age, and lower BMI were also independent risk factors for mortality. Dialysis is associated with markedly worse outcomes following hip fracture surgery. Comprehensive perioperative management and preventive strategies are essential to improve prognosis in this high-risk population.Keywords: Dialysis, Hip fracture, Mortality, Nationwide database, OsteoporosisSubject terms: Medical research, Epidemiology

Introduction

Hip fractures represent a common orthopedic injury among the elderly and are associated with significant morbidity and mortality rates1,2. Globally, the incidence of hip fractures is projected to rise to approximately 4.5 million cases by 20503. In the United States, the growing elderly population has led to a rise in proximal femur fractures, with cases expected to double from 250,000 in 1990 to 500,000 by 20404. Similarly, in Japan, an aging society with an estimated 13 million individuals affected by osteoporosis5, the annual number of proximal femur fractures is estimated at approximately 250,0006. Thus, osteoporosis-related proximal femur fractures have become a significant global health issue, transcending national and regional boundaries. Notably, over 90% of these fractures occur in individuals aged 65 years or older, with comorbidities accumulating over time. The 30-day mortality rate following hip fracture is reported to be 4.0% to 5.4%7, while the 1-year mortality rate approaches 25%8.

According to previous reports, there are approximately 340,000 dialysis patients in Japan, with a prevalence of 2,687.7 patients per million population, which is among the highest levels globally9. In dialysis patients, the osteogenic differentiation of mesenchymal stem cells is suppressed, and the inflammatory response essential for bone healing is also impaired. Uremic toxins and chronic inflammation contribute to these dysfunctions, leading to delayed bone regeneration10,11. Osteogenic differentiation of mesenchymal stem cells plays a critical role in fracture healing, and an appropriately regulated inflammatory environment is essential for tissue repair12,13. Therefore, in dialysis patients, there is concern regarding an increased risk of fragility fractures due to impaired bone regeneration. Dialysis has been associated with the elevated risk of mortality following hip fracture. Several retrospective cohort studies have reported adjusted odds ratios (OR) for short-term outcomes ranging from 2.58 (95% CI, 2.13–3.13) to 3.12 (95% CI, 2.42–4.09), and OR of 2.29 (95% CI, 1.51–3.48) to 3.13 (95% CI, 1.72–5.7)14–17. For long-term outcomes, adjusted OR ranged from 1.97 (95% CI, 1.83–2.1) to 2.95 (95% CI, 2.48–3.51), with reported mortality rates among dialysis patients ranging from 9.5% to 82.6% over follow-up periods of one to five years15–20. Among patients with hip fractures, dementia is present in 72% of those undergoing dialysis compared to 26% in non-dialysis populations, and dementia itself increases hip fracture risk by approximately twofold in patients with chronic kidney disease; furthermore, a bidirectional relationship has been observed, as hip fractures in dialysis patients are associated with a 1.64-fold increased risk of subsequent dementia, likely attributable to post-fracture inflammatory responses, reduced mobility, and accelerated cognitive decline14,21,22. These figures indicate that, among patients with hip fractures, dialysis is linked with a substantial increase in mortality risk and may be related to higher rates of dementia outcomes.

Studies using the Diagnosis Procedure Combination (DPC) database of Japanese hip fracture patients have reported the postoperative complication risks associated with hip fractures in the elderly and total hip arthroplasty23–29. However, the risk of postoperative complications in elderly hip fracture patients undergoing dialysis has not yet been thoroughly investigated. Therefore, in this study, we aim to use a large Japanese hip fracture database to compare postoperative complications and in-hospital mortality between elderly patients aged 65 years and older undergoing dialysis and those not undergoing dialysis, with appropriate adjustment for potential confounding factors.

Discussion

This nationwide cohort study using a large-scale Japanese hip fracture database revealed that elderly patients undergoing dialysis had a significantly higher risk of postoperative cognitive dysfunction and in-hospital mortality following surgical treatment for hip fractures compared to non-dialysis patients, even after adjusting for confounding factors by propensity score matching. These findings highlight the substantial clinical burden associated with hip fractures in the dialysis population. In addition, this study clarified that in Japan, alfacalcidol is used at a notably high frequency for osteoporosis treatment among dialysis patients.

Previous studies have consistently reported that patients receiving dialysis exhibit higher mortality rates after hip fractures compared to the general population. Adjusted OR for short-term mortality after hip fracture in dialysis patients have been reported to range from 2.58 (95% CI, 2.13–3.13) to 3.12 (95% CI, 2.42–4.09)14,15. Similarly, OR for in-hospital or early postoperative mortality have been reported between 2.29 (95% CI, 1.51–3.48) and 3.13 (95% CI, 1.72–5.70)16,17. Our findings align with these earlier reports, demonstrating an approximately threefold increase in in-hospital mortality among dialysis patients relative to non-dialysis patients. Notably, despite advancements in perioperative management, dialysis patients remained at elevated risk, suggesting that factors intrinsic to end-stage renal disease and dialysis therapy, such as impaired immune response, chronic inflammation, and comorbid cardiovascular disease, may contribute to poor outcomes.

In addition, dialysis patients showed a significantly higher incidence of postoperative cognitive dysfunction. Previous studies have demonstrated that patients with chronic kidney disease and those on dialysis have an increased risk of cognitive impairment, likely mediated by uremic toxins, cerebrovascular disease, and systemic inflammation. A bidirectional relationship has been reported, with hip fractures increasing the risk of dementia by 1.64-fold (OR: 1.64, 95% CI, 1.31–2.04) and dialysis itself being a risk factor for cognitive dysfunction21,22. Our findings are consistent with these data, emphasizing the need for early identification and management of cognitive dysfunction in this vulnerable population.

Emerging evidence suggests several biological pathways that may underlie these poor neurological and systemic outcomes. Chronic exposure to uremic toxins (e.g., indoxyl sulfate), micro- and macrovascular calcification, and systemic inflammation can compromise cerebral perfusion and reduce neuronal reserve, thereby predisposing dialysis patients to postoperative delirium and long-term cognitive decline10,21,22. In addition, impaired osteogenic differentiation of mesenchymal stem cells and dysregulated inflammatory cascades may delay fracture healing and prolong catabolic stress, further exacerbating frailty and mortality risk11. A better understanding of these mechanisms could inform targeted perioperative neuroprotective and bone-regenerative strategies in this high-risk population.

Interestingly, the incidence of VTE was lower among dialysis patients compared to non-dialysis patients. Although dialysis patients are generally considered hypercoagulable, frequent use of anticoagulation during hemodialysis sessions and alterations in coagulation profiles might account for the reduced postoperative VTE risk17. In our study, dialysis was associated with a lower risk of VTE (OR: 0.422, 95% CI: 0.353–0.506, p < 0.0001), consistent with previous observations. Nevertheless, individualized perioperative thromboprophylaxis remains critical. These findings suggest that elderly patients receiving chronic hemodialysis may benefit from routine perioperative delirium screening and closer postoperative neurological monitoring to facilitate early detection and intervention. Given the multifactorial nature of thrombotic risk in this population, the lower incidence of VTE observed in our study should be interpreted with caution as a hypothesis-generating observation rather than a definitive conclusion.

From a clinical perspective, our data underscore the importance of a multidisciplinary, dialysis-specific care pathway. Practical measures may include: (i) pre-operative optimization of volume status, anemia, and electrolyte balance in collaboration with nephrologists; (ii) routine delirium screening and early mobilization protocols led by geriatric teams; (iii) aggressive pulmonary hygiene and infection surveillance given the heightened immunosuppressive milieu; and (iv) consideration of intra-dialytic anticoagulation schedules when tailoring VTE prophylaxis. Such integrated management has the potential to mitigate the excess cognitive and mortality burden we observed16,30.

Consistent with prior research, male sex, higher age, and lower BMI were identified as independent risk factors for in-hospital mortality following hip fracture surgery. In particular, male sex has been associated with an approximately 1.5–2.0-fold increase in mortality risk after hip fracture (OR range 1.56–1.94)16,20. Similarly, advanced age and low BMI reflect diminished physiological reserves, thereby increasing perioperative vulnerability. Our findings reinforce these known associations.

Although the 30-day survival rate among dialysis patients was relatively high at 97.5%, survival analysis demonstrated a significantly lower overall survival probability throughout hospitalization compared to non-dialysis patients (log-rank test, p < 0.0001), underscoring the necessity of enhanced perioperative management and multidisciplinary care strategies tailored to this population. Consistent with previous studies, advanced age, male sex, delayed surgery beyond 48 h, dementia, and chronic renal dysfunction were independently associated with increased mortality risk following hip fracture31–36. This study also found that these variables were independently associated with the risk of mortality. Pneumonia-induced respiratory failure and myocardial infarction have been reported as the predominant causes of death in this patient population30,37. In the present study, the 30-day survival rate for dialysis patients was comparatively favorable relative to prior international reports, suggesting that Japan’s healthcare system has made progress in the perioperative management of elderly patients undergoing dialysis. Nevertheless, as this study was limited to in-hospital outcomes and did not evaluate long-term prognoses such as 1-year mortality, further prospective cohort studies including post-discharge follow-up are warranted.

There are several limitations to this large cohort study, which will be discussed below. First, the study population included patients with hip fractures who were treated exclusively in acute care hospitals and reported in the DPC data system. This excludes patients admitted to non-DPC-reported beds, which represent 30% of all general hospital beds, or patients never treated in an acute hospital38. Secondly, the limitations of this study include the inability to validate the names of DPC diseases and the inability to assess the severity of symptoms in actual patients. Although our study provided valuable in-hospital outcome data based on a nationwide cohort, we acknowledge that dialysis adequacy, frailty scores, and cause-specific mortality were not available in the DPC database and should be addressed in future studies. Third, key factors such as dialysis duration, laboratory data, and post-discharge outcomes could not be assessed. Fourth, although conservative treatment may be chosen instead of surgery for elderly dialysis patients with hip fractures, this study only includes patients with surgical treatment, so the outcomes of conservative treatment in elderly patients undergoing dialysis are unexplored. Fifth, another limitation of the study is that the risk of death in the long term after discharge from the hospital was not assessed; further large-scale studies based on real patient data are needed. As the DPC database captures events only during hospitalization, the follow-up period is limited to the inpatient stay. Therefore, long-term outcomes such as post-discharge mortality could not be assessed, although the follow-up during hospitalization was complete for all patients. Sixth, the generalizability of our findings beyond the Japanese acute care hospital system may be limited, as healthcare delivery models, perioperative management protocols, and patient characteristics differ internationally. Caution should therefore be exercised when extrapolating these results to other settings. Finally, we were unable to apply competing risk models due to the limitations of the DPC database, which does not capture events occurring after discharge. As such, in-hospital mortality was used as the primary outcome. Future studies incorporating longitudinal follow-up data and cause-specific mortality will be necessary to fully account for competing risks. Lastly, while the retrospective cohort design and 1:1 propensity score matching helped to reduce selection bias and account for measured confounders, we acknowledge that our findings represent associations and do not imply causality. Residual confounding from unmeasured variables—such as frailty level, nutritional status, dialysis vintage, or socioeconomic status—may still influence the observed relationships. For instance, patients undergoing chronic dialysis may differ in overall physiological reserve or healthcare access, which could contribute to worse outcomes independently of surgical or perioperative factors. Therefore, our conclusions should be interpreted with caution, and future prospective studies incorporating more granular clinical data are warranted.

Conclusion

This study demonstrated that dialysis patients undergoing surgical treatment for hip fractures are at significantly higher risk of postoperative cognitive dysfunction (OR: 1.944, 95% CI: 1.515–2.495) and in-hospital mortality (OR: 3.288, 95% CI: 2.743–3.941) compared to non-dialysis patients. Given the growing elderly dialysis population and the associated clinical burden, comprehensive perioperative management and preventive strategies are essential to improve outcomes in this high-risk group.

Methods

Study design

This retrospective cohort study was conducted in accordance with the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and received approval from the ethics committees of our institutions.

Data source

The data were retrospectively collected from Japan’s nationwide administrative DPC reimbursement system database39. Comprehensive informed consent was obtained from all patients upon admission, encompassing both their approval of the proposed treatment methods and permission for the academic use of data collected during their treatment. Furthermore, no information that could identify individual participants is included in this study. All variables used in the analysis were obtained from this data source. Cases with missing values for any relevant clinical variables were excluded from the analysis to ensure data completeness and accuracy. No imputation methods were applied to missing data, and only fully recorded cases were included in the final analysis. Only cases with complete data for all covariates and outcomes were included in the final analysis. No imputation methods were applied, as missing data were handled by case-wise exclusion.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The sample size was determined based on a fixed study period rather than a priori power analysis. Patients were selected from a nationwide survey of hospitals participating in the Japanese DPC system covering the period from April 2016 to March 2022. During this period, approximately 1,100 hospitals participating in the DPC system consistently submitted medical records and were approved for their use in this study. Patients who underwent surgical treatment for hip fractures at these hospitals across Japan were included in the analysis. This clinical study focused on hip fractures in elderly people aged 65 years and over, and in particular, it focused on the incidence of postoperative complications and short-term mortality during hospitalization in the hip fracture patients with dialysis. The exclusion criteria are patients under 65 years of age and patients with hip fractures treated conservatively. All eligible cases meeting the inclusion criteria within this timeframe were analyzed to ensure a comprehensive representation of the study population.

Exposure of interest

This study analyzed patient groups who underwent surgical treatments for hip fractures, focusing on postoperative complications in patients undergoing dialysis and comparing them with complications in non-dialysis patients during hospitalization. The definition of dialysis in this study includes patients undergoing continuous dialysis for chronic renal dysfunction. Cases in which dialysis was initiated postoperatively due to renal function deterioration were not included. Dialysis patients were identified using diagnostic codes, and the study was limited to hemodialysis. The duration and frequency of dialysis were not analyzed. The DPC database does not provide information on dialysis duration, adequacy, or peritoneal dialysis. As only hemodialysis procedure codes are captured, the present cohort reflects patients receiving chronic maintenance hemodialysis.

Outcomes of interest

The postoperative complications evaluated in this study included VTE, cognitive dysfunction, pneumonia, and in-hospital mortality. Postoperative cognitive dysfunction was identified using ICD-10 codes F010, F011, F012, F019, F03, F107, G238, G300, G301, G308, G309, G310, and G318, which cover cognitive dysfunction and delirium occurring in the postoperative period, as previously described24. Each outcome was identified using diagnostic codes. In the DPC database, these diagnoses are typically recorded by the attending physician at the time of discharge and subsequently used for administrative claims. While this ensures standardized nationwide data capture, the accuracy of coding may vary among institutions, and misclassification cannot be entirely excluded. Since mortality is also included as an outcome, the occurrence of outcomes was assessed throughout the entire hospitalization period. Secondary outcomes included the length of hospital stay, volume of perioperative blood transfusion, and administration of anti-osteoporotic agents.

Covariates

Covariates included age, sex, BMI, types of anesthesia, types of hip fracture, Charlson Comorbidity Index, and comorbidities such as hypertension, diabetes, cerebrovascular disease, ischemic heart disease, chronic pulmonary disease, and cognitive impairment. Hip fractures were classified according to ICD-10 codes: S7200 for femoral neck fractures, S7210 for trochanteric fractures, and S7220 for subtrochanteric fractures. Patients included in the hip fracture cohorts were selected from a registry that organized entries based on three criteria:1 principal diagnosis2, primary reason for admission, and3 condition that utilized the most medical resources.

Propensity score matching

To minimize confounding when comparing postoperative complications between dialysis and non-dialysis patients, we performed 1:1 propensity score matching based on clinically relevant covariates. The analysis adjusted for confounding factors, including age, sex, BMI, types of anesthesia, types of hip fracture, Charlson comorbidity index, and comorbidities such as hypertension, diabetes, cerebrovascular disease, ischemic heart disease, chronic pulmonary disease, and cognitive impairment. The listed factors were included in the model used for propensity score matching, which was performed using logistic regression. After propensity score matching, covariate balance was assessed using standardized mean differences (SMDs), with values less than 0.1 indicating acceptable balance. Independent associations with outcomes were identified through multivariate logistic regression in the matched cohort, applying variables selected by the purposeful selection method. The discriminatory ability of the model was assessed using the C-statistic. These factors were accounted for to create a balanced cohort of dialysis and non-dialysis patients. Propensity score estimates were utilized for nearest-neighbor matching without replacement, applying a caliper set at 0.2 times the standard deviation of the propensity score estimates. Propensity score estimates were utilized for nearest-neighbor matching without replacement, applying a caliper set at 0.2 times the standard deviation of the propensity score estimates. This caliper width corresponds to 0.2 standard deviations of the logit of the propensity score, a commonly recommended threshold to optimize balance while minimizing bias40. Although a larger control pool was available, we chose 1:1 matching to prioritize covariate balance and facilitate interpretability. While 1:n matching or weighting approaches could have increased statistical power, our goal was to reduce residual confounding and enhance internal validity. Additionally, we confirmed that unmatched dialysis patients (n = 468) did not show clinically meaningful differences in key baseline characteristics (e.g., age, sex, Charlson Comorbidity Index) when compared with those who were matched, indicating minimal risk of selection bias. A 1:1 propensity score-matched cohort is considered well-balanced with adequately adjusted confounding factors when the SMD is less than 0.1.

Figure 1 presents a schematic model of the patient selection process. From the dataset spanning April 2016 to March 2022, a total of 474,293 patients who met the eligibility and exclusion criteria were identified. Among the patients, 464,224 were non-dialysis patients, and 10,069 were dialysis patients. After propensity score matching based on age, sex, BMI, Charlson comorbidity index, presence of general anesthesia, comorbidities, and types of hip fractures, the dialysis and non-dialysis groups each included 9,601 patients.

Table 1 summarizes the characteristics of dialysis and non-dialysis patients who underwent surgical treatment for hip fractures. Before propensity score matching, the SMD for sex, age, general anesthesia, and comorbidities was above 0.1, indicating significant differences between the groups. The dialysis group had a higher proportion of male patients, younger age, higher prevalences of diabetes and ischemic heart disease, and a lower prevalence of cognitive impairment. General anesthesia was frequently administered, and femoral neck fractures represented the predominant fracture type in dialysis group. After 1:1 propensity score matching, all variables — including age, sex, BMI, Charlson comorbidity index, comorbidities, general anesthesia, and fracture type — demonstrated SMDs of less than 0.1, indicating that these factors were appropriately balanced. The C-statistic was calculated to be 0.856.

Statistical analyses

Data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation. Differences between dialysis and non-dialysis patient groups for each clinical parameter were evaluated using the χ² test and Student’s t-test. Univariate logistic regression analysis was conducted for in-hospital complications, followed by multivariate logistic regression analysis for statistically significant severe in-hospital complications and in-hospital mortality. Additionally, to further investigate the association between postoperative complications and dialysis, a multivariate logistic regression analysis was conducted, incorporating dialysis status and the representative covariates used in the propensity score matching. The log-rank test was used to compare the difference in survival between groups. Due to the relatively large sample size in this study, a more stringent threshold for statistical significance was employed. All statistical tests were two-tailed, and a p-value of less than 0.001 was considered statistically significant. To account for the large sample size and minimize the risk of Type I error, we applied a stringent significance threshold of p < 0.001 throughout the analyses. All evaluated outcomes—mortality, cognitive dysfunction, VTE, and pneumonia—were predefined and considered of equal clinical relevance; therefore, no formal adjustment for multiple comparisons was applied. Statistical analyses were performed using JMP version 17 (SAS, Cary, NC, USA).

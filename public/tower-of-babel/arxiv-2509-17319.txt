Non-directed polymers in random environments with range penalties: the high dimensional case
Niccolò Torri, Ran Wei

Abstract
This paper is a follow-up work of [ 6 ] . We study a non-directed polymer model in random environments. The polymer is represented by a simple symmetric random walk S S on ℤ d {\mathbbm{Z}}^{d} with d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 and the random environment is represented by i.i.d. heavy-tailed random variables with their tail probability decaying polynomially. We perform a Gibbs transform to describe the interaction between polymers and random environments. Up to time N N , the law of S S is tilted by exp ⁡ ( ∑ x ∈ ℛ N ( β ​ ω x − h ) ) \exp(\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{R}}_{N}}(\beta\omega_{x}-h)) , where ℛ N {\mathcal{R}}_{N} is the range of S S up to time N N , β ≥ 0 \beta\geq 0 is the inverse temperature and h ∈ ℝ h\in{\mathbbm{R}} is an external field. By tuning β = β N \beta=\beta_{N} and h = h N h=h_{N} , we establish the phase diagram and study the fluctuations of S S under the Gibbs transform and the scaling limits of the (logarithmic) partition function. The novelty and challenge, compared to [ 6 ] , is that we also tune the external field h h , which brings in various range penalties , unlike in [ 6 ] , where h h is fixed and merely playing a role of centering for the random environment. We discuss our contribution in Section 1.4 . 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification : 82D60, 60K37, 60G70 Keywords : Random polymer, Random walk, Range, Heavy-tail distributions, Weak-coupling limit, Folding, Stretching

Introduction

In this paper, we study a
non-directed polymer model
, which describes an interaction between polymer chains and random environments, that is, each time when the polymer chain visits a new site, it receives some energy from the disorder and penalty from the external field, which then influences its configuration. We model the polymer by a simple symmetric random walk and model the random environment by a family of i.i.d. random variables. The detailed definition of the model will be given in Section
1.1
below.

The non-directed polymer model was first introduced in
[
19
]
. This model is closely related to two celebrated models, the
random walk penalized by its range
(see
[
15
]
) and the
directed polymer model
(see
[
11
]
). There are no random environments in the model of a random walk penalized by its range, where the law of the random walk is only tilted by an external field. Hence, our model can be viewed as a perturbed version of that model. In the directed polymer model, the random walk interacts with the environment at any time, even if there is at most one interaction with any site of the environment because of the directed constraint. However, in our model, the random walk only interacts with the environment at the first time visiting a new site.

In the first study of the non-directed polymer model in
[
19
]
, the author studied the weak coupling limits for the model in a random environment with finite exponential moments. Very recently, the model in dimension
d=1
has been studied comprehensively in
[
3
,
8
]
. For heavy-tailed random environments and a fixed external field
h
, the model in dimension
d\geq 2
has been studied in
[
6
]
. This paper is a follow-up work of
[
6
]
. We study the non-directed polymer model in heavy-tailed random environments in dimension
d\geq 2
with the external field
h=h_{N}
, which is allowed to vary over time.

1.1
Definition of the model

Let
S:=(S_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a simple symmetric random walk on
{\mathbbm{Z}}^{d}
starting at the origin, where
d\geq 2
. The probability and expectation of
S
are denoted by
{\mathbf{P}}
and
{\mathbf{E}}
respectively. The
range
of
S
up to time
N
is denoted by
{\mathcal{R}}_{N}={\mathcal{R}}_{N}(S):=\{S_{0},S_{1},\cdots,S_{N}\}
, which is the set of all sites visited by the random walk up to time
N
.

Let
\omega:=(\omega_{x})_{x\in{\mathbbm{Z}}^{d}}
be a family of i.i.d. random variables, which is independent of
S
. The probability and expectation of
\omega
are denoted by
{\mathbbm{P}}
and
{\mathbbm{E}}
respectively. In this paper, we assume that
\omega
has a heavy-tailed distribution. In particular, we assume that

(1.1)

{\mathbbm{P}}(\omega>t)\sim pt^{-\alpha}\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathbbm{P}}(\omega<-t)\sim qt^{-\alpha},\quad\text{as\, }t\to+\infty,

where
p+q=1
and
p,q>0
. Furthermore, we assume
\alpha\in(0,d)
(for the reason, see Section
1.2
), and if
\alpha>1
, we also assume that
{\mathbbm{E}}[\omega]=0
.

The
polymer measure
at time
N
is defined via a Gibbs transform

(1.2)

\frac{{\mathrm{d}}{\mathbf{P}}_{N,\beta}^{\omega,h}}{{\mathrm{d}}{\mathbf{P}}}(S):=\frac{1}{{\mathbf{Z}}_{N,\beta}^{\omega,h}}\exp\left(\sum\limits_{x\in{\mathcal{R}}_{N}}(\beta\omega_{x}-h)\right)=\frac{1}{{\mathbf{Z}}_{N,\beta}^{\omega,h}}\exp\left(\sum\limits_{x\in{\mathcal{R}}_{N}}\beta\omega_{x}-h|{\mathcal{R}}_{N}|\right),

where
\beta\geq 0
is the inverse temperature,
h\geq 0
can be viewed as an external field and

(1.3)

{\mathbf{Z}}_{N,\beta}^{\omega,h}={\mathbf{E}}\left[\exp\left(\sum\limits_{x\in{\mathcal{R}}_{N}}\beta\omega_{x}-h|{\mathcal{R}}_{N}|\right)\right]

is the
partition function
which makes
{\mathbf{P}}_{N,\beta}^{\omega,h}
a probability measure.

Let us comment our assumptions (
1.1
) and
h\geq 0
:

\bullet
The condition
q>0
is not essential and our results should remain valid even if
q=0
. In fact, we only require that the right tail has a main contribution, since in a random polymer model, we hope that polymer chains can gain energy from the random environment, in which case, the polymer chain is encouraged to explore. By assuming
q>0
, we can have a better control on the left tail of
\omega
. In particular, it greatly simplifies the proof of Theorem
1.7
(see the second last paragraph in Section
5
) and help us get the sharp asymptotics (
B.4
). Let us observe that by assuming
q=0
we have that
\omega\geq 0
. In that case we need to replace the exponent in (
1.2
) by
\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{R}}_{N}}(\beta(\omega_{x}-{\mathbbm{E}}[\omega_{x}]\mathbbm{1}_{\{\alpha>1\}})-h)
as in
[
6
]
.

\bullet
From (
1.2
), we can see that those environments with large positive energies are attractive to the random walk. Meanwhile, we assume that the external field
h\geq 0
, which penalizes long trajectories. This factor forces the random walk to fold and thus there is a competition between the random environment and the external field. If
h<0
, then both the random environment and the external field encourage the random walk to explore, and then the walk is much likely to be super-diffusive, which results in a less rich phase diagram. Readers may refer to
[
3
]
for the case
h<0
in dimension
d=1
.

We also allow
\beta
and
h
to vary as
N
increases. In particular, we set

(1.4)

\beta_{N}=\hat{\beta}N^{-\gamma},\quad h_{N}=\hat{h}N^{-\zeta}

with constants
\hat{\beta},\hat{h}>0
and
\gamma,\zeta\in{\mathbbm{R}}
. We will write
{\mathbf{P}}_{N}={\mathbf{P}}_{N,\beta_{N}}^{\omega,h_{N}}
and
{\mathbf{Z}}_{N}={\mathbf{Z}}_{N,\beta_{N}}^{\omega,h_{N}}
to simplify the notations when there is no ambiguity. Note that when
\gamma
and
\zeta
are positive, we are considering the
weak-coupling limit
(or the
intermediate disorder region
) of the model, which was first studied by
[
1
]
and then followed by
[
10
]
. As in
[
3
]
, we also include the case where
\gamma
and
\zeta
are negative, that is, the coupling is getting stronger and stronger over time.

In this paper, we want to investigate the
end-to-end
exponent
\xi
of
S
under
{\mathbf{P}}_{N}
and the
fluctuation
exponent
\chi
of
\log{\mathbf{Z}}_{N}
, which are loosely defined as follows:

•

End-to-end exponent
\xi
:
{\mathbbm{E}}{\mathbf{E}}_{N}^{\omega}[\max_{1\leq n\leq N}|S_{n}|]\approx N^{\xi}
.

•

Fluctuation exponent
\chi
:
\text{Var}_{\mathbbm{P}}(\log{\mathbf{Z}}_{N})\approx N^{2\chi}
.

Naturally, both
\xi
and
\chi
depend on
\alpha,\gamma
and
\zeta
. In the next section, we heuristically discuss the behaviors of our model and establish the phase diagrams.

1.2
Heuristics

In this paper, we only study the case
\alpha<d
, where
\alpha
is the exponent in (
1.1
), since the effects of random environments are dramatically different for
\alpha<d
and
\alpha>d
. For
\alpha<d
, the scale of
\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{R}}_{N}}\omega_{x}
is determined by only a few extremely large environments due to the “much heavy” tail probability. While for
\alpha>d
, the scale of
\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{R}}_{N}}\omega_{x}
is determined by a collection of many “not too bad” environments due to the “less heavy” tail probability. The case
\alpha>d
is very challenging and it represents one of the main open problems (see the discussion in
[
6
, Section 2.5.4]
).

For
\alpha<d
, by the property of heavy-tailed distribution, we have that for the end-to-end exponent
\xi
,
\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{R}}_{N}}\omega_{x}\approx N^{d\xi/\alpha}
, and we call
\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{R}}_{N}}\beta_{N}\omega_{x}\approx N^{d\xi/\alpha-\gamma}
the “
energy
” for brevity.

The penalty from the external field is
h_{N}|R_{N}|
and we call it the “
range
” for brevity. Its scale should be determined separately in two regions. If
\xi\leq\frac{1}{d}\leq\frac{1}{2}
, that is, the random walk folds itself in a sub-diffusive ball and fills it up (see
[
2
,
13
]
), then we expect that
|{\mathcal{R}}_{N}|\approx N^{d\xi}
. Hence, the
range
is of order
N^{d\xi-\zeta}
. If
\xi>\frac{1}{d}
, then no matter the random walk is folding or not, it still has enough space to stretch. Hence, we expect that
|{\mathcal{R}}_{N}|\approx N
and thus the
range
is of order
N^{1-\zeta}
.

Finally, if the random walk is sub-diffusive or super-diffusive, then there is an extra entropy cost, we call it “
entropy
” for short. In the sub-diffusive case, that is,
\xi<\frac{1}{2}
, the
entropy
is of order
\exp(-N^{1-2\xi})
, and in the super-diffusive case, that is
\xi>\frac{1}{2}
, the
entropy
is of order
\exp(-N^{2\xi-1})
.

Let us determine the behavior of the polymer and the associated phase diagram, which is computed through the competition between
energy
,
range
and
entropy
.

Case I
(
range
dominates
energy
): If the penalty for long trajectories is much larger than the energy gain, then the random walk should fold itself. Hence, we have a sub-diffusive region, where
\xi\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]
. We need to consider whether
d\xi\leq 1
or
d\xi>1
.

If
\xi\in[0,\frac{1}{d}]
, then the
range
is of order
N^{d\xi-\zeta}
, and the fact that the range dominates energy implies that
d\xi-\zeta>\frac{d\xi}{\alpha}-\gamma
. Note that the
range
and the
entropy
can still be balanced. In that case,
d\xi-\zeta=1-2\xi\implies\xi=\frac{1+\zeta}{d+2}
. It follows that

(1.5)

1-2\xi>\frac{d\xi}{\alpha}-\gamma\implies\gamma>\frac{(d+2\alpha)(1+\zeta)}{\alpha(d+2)}-1=\frac{d+2\alpha}{\alpha(d+2)}\zeta+\frac{d(1-\alpha)}{\alpha(d+2)}.

On the boundary of
\xi\in[0,\frac{1}{d}]
, we have that

(1.6)

\xi=0\implies\zeta\leq-1,\gamma>\zeta\quad\text{and}\quad\xi=\frac{1}{d}\implies\zeta=\frac{2}{d},\gamma=\frac{d+2\alpha-d\alpha}{d\alpha}.

If
\xi>\frac{1}{d}
, then
\xi\in(\frac{1}{d},\frac{1}{2}]
, which can only happen for
d\geq 3
. In this case, the
range
is of order
N^{1-\zeta}
, and we should have
1-\zeta>\frac{d\xi}{\alpha}-\gamma
. For the
range
-
entropy
balance, we have that
1-\zeta=1-2\xi\implies\xi=\frac{\zeta}{2}
. It follows that

(1.7)

1-2\xi>\frac{d\xi}{\alpha}-\gamma\implies\gamma>\frac{d+2\alpha}{\alpha}\xi-1=\frac{d+2\alpha}{2\alpha}\zeta-1.

On the boundary of
\xi\in(\frac{1}{d},\frac{1}{2}]
, we have that

(1.8)

\xi=\frac{1}{d}\implies\zeta=\frac{2}{d},\gamma=\frac{d+2\alpha-d\alpha}{d\alpha}\quad\text{and}\quad\xi=\frac{1}{2}\implies\zeta\geq 1,\gamma\geq\frac{d}{2\alpha},

where (
1.6
) and (
1.8
) match at
\xi=\frac{1}{d}
even for
d=2
.

Case II
(
energy
dominates
range
): In this case, the random walk is encouraged to stretch. Hence, we have a super-diffusive region, where
\xi\in[\frac{1}{2},1]
. Note that now
d\xi\geq 2\xi\geq 1
, so the
range
always has order
N^{1-\zeta}
and thus
\frac{d\xi}{\alpha}-\gamma>1-\zeta
. For an
energy
-
entropy
balance, we have that
\frac{d\xi}{\alpha}-\gamma=2\xi-1\implies\xi=\frac{\alpha(1-\gamma)}{2\alpha-d}
. It follows that

(1.9)

2\xi-1>1-\zeta\implies\gamma<\frac{2\alpha-d}{2\alpha}\zeta+\frac{d-\alpha}{\alpha}.

One the boundary of
\xi\in[\frac{1}{2},1]
, we have that

(1.10)

\xi=\frac{1}{2}\implies\gamma\geq\frac{d}{2\alpha},\zeta\geq 1\quad\text{and}\quad\xi=1\implies\gamma\leq\frac{d-\alpha}{\alpha},\zeta\geq 0.

In particular, for
\xi=1
, we should further have that

(1.11)

\frac{d}{\alpha}-\gamma>1-\zeta\implies\gamma<\zeta+\frac{d-\alpha}{\alpha}.

Case III
(
energy
-
range
balance): In this case, we cannot conclude the sub-diffusivity or the super-diffusivity of the random walk. Hence,
\xi\in[0,1]
. Similar to the Case I, we need to consider
\xi\leq\frac{1}{d}
and
\xi>\frac{1}{d}
separately.

If
\xi\in[0,\frac{1}{d}]
, then we should have
\frac{d\xi}{\alpha}-\gamma=d\xi-\zeta\implies\xi=\frac{\alpha(\zeta-\gamma)}{d(\alpha-1)}
. Hence, we have that

(1.12)

\zeta+\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\leq\gamma\leq\zeta,~\text{if}~\alpha>1\quad\text{or}\quad\zeta+\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\geq\gamma\geq\zeta,~\text{if}~\alpha<1.

Since the
energy
and the
range
are dominating, we must have
d\xi-\zeta\geq 1-2\xi
, which yields

\gamma\leq\frac{2\alpha+d}{\alpha(d+2)}\zeta+\frac{d(1-\alpha)}{\alpha(d+2)},~\text{if}~\alpha>1\quad\text{or}\quad\gamma\geq\frac{2\alpha+d}{\alpha(d+2)}\zeta+\frac{d(1-\alpha)}{\alpha(d+2)},~\text{if}~\alpha<1,

the same boundary as (
1.5
).

If
\xi\in[\frac{1}{d},1]
, then we should have
\frac{d\xi}{\alpha}-\gamma=1-\zeta\implies\xi=\frac{\alpha}{d}(1+\gamma-\zeta)
. Hence, we have that

(1.13)

\zeta+\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\leq\gamma\leq\zeta+\frac{d-\alpha}{\alpha}.

Similar to the case
\xi\leq\frac{1}{d}
, for
\xi\in[\frac{1}{d},\frac{1}{2}]
, we should have
1-\zeta\geq 1-2\xi
, which yields

(1.14)

\zeta+\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\leq\gamma\leq\zeta+\frac{d-2\alpha}{2\alpha}\quad\text{and}\quad\gamma\geq\frac{d+2\alpha}{2\alpha}\zeta-1.

For
\xi\in[\frac{1}{2},1]
, we should have
1-\zeta\geq 2\xi-1
, which yields

(1.15)

\zeta+\frac{d-2\alpha}{2\alpha}\leq\gamma\leq\zeta+\frac{d-\alpha}{\alpha}\quad\text{and}\quad\gamma\leq\frac{2\alpha-d}{2\alpha}\zeta+\frac{d-\alpha}{\alpha}.

Note that the regions above still have some overlap. It can be shown that the regions where the
energy
is not negligible always win (see our results in Section
1.3
and also
[
6
]
). Finally, we can summarize all the regions and the phase diagrams below. Note that there is some difference between the cases
\alpha\in(0,\frac{d}{2})
and
\alpha\in(\frac{d}{2},d)
.

\bullet
For
\alpha\in(\frac{d}{2},d)
, we have the following 6 regions:

1.

R_{1}=\Big\{(\zeta,\gamma)\colon\zeta>1,\gamma>\frac{d}{2\alpha}\Big\}
,

2.

R_{2}=\Big\{(\zeta,\gamma)\colon\zeta>0,\frac{d-\alpha}{\alpha}<\gamma<\Big(\frac{2\alpha-d}{2\alpha}\zeta+\frac{d-\alpha}{\alpha}\Big)\wedge\frac{d}{2\alpha}\Big\}
,

3.

R_{3}=\Big\{(\zeta,\gamma)\colon\gamma<\zeta\wedge 0+\frac{d-\alpha}{\alpha}\Big\}
,

4.

R_{4}=\Big\{(\zeta,\gamma)\colon\zeta\in(\frac{2}{d},1),\gamma>\zeta\Big(1\wedge\frac{2\alpha-d}{2\alpha}\Big)+\frac{d-\alpha}{\alpha}\Big\}
,

5.

R_{5}=\Big\{(\zeta,\gamma)\colon\zeta\in(-1,\frac{2}{d}),\gamma>\zeta\Big(1\wedge\frac{2\alpha-d}{2\alpha}\Big)+\frac{d-\alpha}{\alpha}\Big\}
,

6.

R_{6}=\Big\{(\zeta,\gamma)\colon\zeta<-1,\gamma>\zeta+\frac{d-\alpha}{\alpha}\Big\}
.

and

1.3
Main results

In this paper, we study the end-to-end exponent
\xi
and non-trivial scaling limits of
{\mathbf{Z}}_{N}
or
\log{\mathbf{Z}}_{N}
, depending on the regions in Figures
1
and
2
. To avoid handling extremely technical details, we exclude all the boundaries and only consider the interior of the regions. We first give a rigorous definition for
\xi
.

Definition 1.1
.

We say that
(S_{n})_{0\leq n\leq N}
has end-to-end fluctuations
r_{N}
under
{\mathbf{P}}_{N}
, if for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists some
\eta\in(0,1)
, such that for
N
large enough,

{\mathbbm{P}}\Big({\mathbf{P}}_{N}\Big(\max_{0\leq n\leq N}|S_{n}|\in[\eta,\eta^{-1}]r_{N}\Big)>1-\varepsilon\Big)>1-\varepsilon.

If
r_{N}
is of the form
N^{\xi}
, then
\xi
is the end-to-end exponent.

The regions
R_{1}
,
R_{2}
and
R_{3}
have been studied in
[
6
]
in depth, where the external field
h
is fixed and plays the role of the centering of
\omega
. In our setting, we show that by a simple comparison argument, the external field
h_{N}
is negligible in the regions
R_{2}
and
R_{3}
, and thus the results therein can be directly migrated from
[
6
]
. However, for the region
R_{1}
, the phase diagram is even richer. We find that there is a further phase transition, depending on the effect of the
energy
and the
range
on the higher order terms of
{\mathbf{Z}}_{N}
. The proof follows by an adaption of the related argument in
[
6
]
. For the completeness, in addition to the region
R_{1}
, we still state the results for the regions
R_{2}
and
R_{3}
and briefly explain their proofs in the Appendix
B
. Our results for the region
R_{4},R_{5}
and
R_{6}
are new.

The following Theorems
1.1
–
1.3
concern the region
R_{1}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\alpha\in(2\vee\frac{d}{2},d)
(in particular
d\geq 3
) and
(\zeta,\gamma)\in R_{1}
(see Figure
1
). Then
(S_{n})_{0\leq n\leq N}
has end-to-end fluctuations of order
N^{\xi}
under
{\mathbf{P}}_{N}
with
\xi=\frac{1}{2}
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
{\rm Var_{\mathbb{P}}}(\omega)=1
. Take
a_{N}=N^{1/4}
for
d=3
,
a_{N}=(\log N)^{1/2}
for
d=4
, and
a_{N}=1
for
d\geq 5
. Then as
N\to\infty
,

(1.16)

\frac{1}{a_{N}\beta_{N}}\left({\mathbf{Z}}_{N}-{\mathbf{E}}\left[e^{(\frac{1}{2}\beta_{N}^{2}-h_{N})|{\mathcal{R}}_{N}|}\right]\right)\overset{(d)}{\longrightarrow}\begin{cases}{\mathcal{N}}(0,\sigma_{d}^{2}),\quad&\text{for}~d=3,4,\\
{\mathcal{X}},\quad&\text{for}~d\geq 5,\end{cases}

where
{\mathcal{N}}(0,\sigma_{d}^{2})
is a centered Gaussian distribution with an explicit variance
\sigma_{d}
and

(1.17)

{\mathcal{X}}:=\sum\limits_{x\in{\mathbbm{Z}}^{d}}\omega_{x}{\mathbf{P}}(x\in{\mathcal{R}}_{\infty})={\mathbf{E}}\left[\sum\limits_{x\in{\mathcal{R}}_{\infty}}\omega_{x}\right],

which is a well-defined random variable.

The above theorem is a counterpart of (2.7) in
[
6
, Theorem 2.12]
. A modification is that in (
1.16
), we use a different centering for
{\mathbf{Z}}_{N}
due to the external field
h_{N}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\alpha\in(\frac{d}{2},2)
(in particular
d=2,3
) and
(\zeta,\gamma)\in R_{1}
(see Figure
1
). Then
(S_{n})_{0\leq n\leq N}
has end-to-end fluctuations of order
N^{\xi}
under
{\mathbf{P}}_{N}
with
\xi=\frac{1}{2}
and we have the following scaling limits for
{\mathbf{Z}}_{N}
.

(i)
If
\zeta\in(1,\gamma+\frac{d(\alpha-1)}{2\alpha})
, then

(1.18)

\frac{1}{h_{N}{\mathbf{E}}[|{\mathcal{R}}_{N}|]}\log{\mathbf{Z}}_{N}\overset{{\mathbbm{P}}}{\longrightarrow}-1.

(ii)
If

1.4
Further comments and conjectures

Before closing Section
1
, we present some comments and conjectures for our model and results.

(1)
Recall the end-to-end exponent
\xi
from Definition
1.1
. By the methods in
[
6
]
, we can still identify the end-to-end fluctuations for the regions
R_{1},R_{2}
and
R_{3}
, since in those regions, the range penalty is negligible in the first order term of
{\mathbf{Z}}_{N}
, where the contribution mainly comes from the typical fluctuations of
S
. We also show that the end-to-end exponent
\xi=0
in the region
R_{6}
in this paper (indeed, we show a stronger result, that is,
|{\mathcal{R}}_{N}|=2
under
{\mathbf{P}}_{N}
). However, we cannot rigorously show that
\xi=\frac{1+\zeta}{2+d}
in the region
R_{5}
and
\xi=\frac{\zeta}{2}
in the region
R_{4}
are the end-to-end exponents satisfying Definition
1.1
exactly. To do that, one needs to show that most of the mass of
{\mathbf{Z}}_{N}
is carried by the paths with
M_{N}\approx N^{\xi}
(see Section
6
for more detailed discussions). This argument is bypassed in this paper by directly comparing
{\mathbf{Z}}_{N}
with its homogeneous version
{\mathbf{Z}}_{N,\beta_{N}=0}^{\omega,h_{N}}
.

(2)
The homogeneous version of our model, that is, the random walk penalized by its range, initiated by
[
15
]
, has been studied in details very recently (see
[
7
,
2
,
13
,
12
,
14
]
). In that model,
\beta\equiv 0
and
h_{N}\equiv\hat{h}
(corresponding to the case
\zeta=0
in our model), i.e., there are no random environments and thus the partition function is deterministic. The property of the homogeneous model is now quite clear. First,
{\mathbf{Z}}_{N,\beta_{N}=0}^{\omega,h_{N}=\hat{h}}\sim\exp(-c(\hat{h})(1+o(1))N^{\frac{d}{d+2}})
as
N\to\infty
, where
c(\hat{h})
is positive with an explicit expression. We obtain similar asymptotics for our model in Theorems
1.6
and
1.7
. Secondly, under
{\mathbf{P}}_{N}
, the range of
S
is almost a ball with radius
r_{d}N^{\frac{1}{d+2}}
without holes in it, where
r_{d}
has an explicit expression, and the distribution of the center of the ball has also been characterized. Furthermore, the local large deviations of
S
under
{\mathbf{P}}_{N}
have also been established in
[
12
]
.

We wonder that in the region
R_{5}
of our model, whether the range of
S
is also a ball without holes. This is likely to be true, but it is not easy to directly adapt the methods in
[
2
,
13
]
, since we have random environments here, and furthermore, a rigorous description for this phenomenon like Definition
1.1
is needed. An even more difficult question is that, suppose that the range of
S
is indeed a ball without holes, then how to determine its center. Even though the random environments do not appear in the first order term of
{\mathbf{Z}}_{N}
, it may still attract the ball to a region with large
energy
. A related question is to study the higher order fluctuations of
{\mathbf{Z}}_{N}
, in particular, the fluctuations of
\frac{1}{N^{2\xi-1}}\log{\mathbf{Z}}_{N}+c(\hat{h})
, since
\frac{1}{N^{2\xi-1}}\log{\mathbf{Z}}_{N}
converges to the deterministic limit
-c(\hat{h})
. These questions have been studied in dimension
d=1
for the homogeneous case in
[
21
,
9
]
and for the disordered case in
[
8
]
. However, they are very challenging in dimension
d\geq 2
, due to the complexity of geometry of the range of
S
.

(3)
Let us highlight our contributions in this paper.

\bullet
First, we establish the phase diagrams for the non-directed polymer model, which are different for the cases
\alpha\in(0,\frac{d}{2})
and
\alpha\in(\frac{d}{2},d)
. We confirm all the phases for
\alpha\in(0,\frac{d}{2})
and most of the phases for
\alpha\in(\frac{d}{2},d)
by identifying the scaling limits of
\log{\mathbf{Z}}_{N}
. In particular, the regions
R_{4},R_{5}
and
R_{6}
have not been studied in prior works like
[
6
]
.

\bullet
Secondly, we observe a new phase transition from the region
R_{4}
to the region
R_{5}
when the
range
and the
entropy
are balanced, which does not exist in the one-dimensional model studied in
[
3
]
nor the high-dimensional homogeneous models with fixed external field studied in
[
15
]
. While the scaling limit of
\log{\mathbf{Z}}_{N}
in the region
R_{5}
are comparable to that in
[
3
,
21
,
15
]
, the result in the region
R_{4}
are new. Furthermore, in the region
R_{4}
, we also identify a sharp asymptotics
|{\mathcal{R}}_{N}|\approx{\mathbf{E}}[|{\mathcal{R}}_{N}|]=O(N)
under
{\mathbf{P}}_{N}
(see Theorem
1.6
), showing an interesting phenomenon: for a suitable weak penalty, it forces the random walk to fold, but it does not encourage the random walk to have many self-intersections. In this case, the entropy cost for self-intersections is too large and cannot be balanced by the penalty that is not strong enough.

\bullet
Thirdly, we find a further phase transitions in the region
R_{1}
, which cannot be observed in
[
6
]
due to the lack of varying external fields therein. Note than we can still have a convergence to a deterministic limit (see (
1.18
) and (
1.23
)) for the centered and scaled partition function. It is interesting to explore the higher order asymptotics of the partition function, as has been done in
[
8
]
.

\bullet
Finally, we find a strategy to control the
energy
on the range of
S
when the range penalty is strong enough (in particular, when
\zeta<\gamma-\frac{d-\alpha}{\alpha}
, see Figures
1
and
2
) by comparing the
entropy
and a quantity from Hammersley’s last-passage percolation (see Lemma
4.1
, Theorem
A.1
and Proposition
A.2
, and also see
[
5
]
). These technical estimates may be of independent interests.

Proof for Theorem
1.6

In the region
R_{4}
, recall that we have
\frac{2}{d}<\zeta<1
and
\xi=\frac{\zeta}{2}
. Furthermore, if
\alpha\in(\frac{d}{2},d)
, then
\gamma~>~\frac{2\alpha-d}{2\alpha}\zeta~+~\frac{d-\alpha}{\alpha}
and if
\alpha\in(0,\frac{d}{2})
, then
\gamma>\frac{d-\alpha}{\alpha}
.

We first establish an upper bound for
\frac{1}{N^{1-2\xi}}\log{\mathbf{Z}}_{N}
. By Hölder’s inequality, for any
\varepsilon>0
,

(3.1)

\begin{split}{\mathbf{Z}}_{N}\leq\Big({\mathbf{Z}}_{N,\beta_{N}=0}^{\omega,(1+\varepsilon)h_{N}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon}}\Big({\mathbf{Z}}_{N,\frac{1+\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}\beta_{N}}^{\omega,h_{N}=0}\Big)^{\frac{\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}}.\end{split}

Taking logarithm and dividing it by
N^{1-2\xi}
, we have that

(3.2)

\frac{1}{N^{1-2\xi}}\log{\mathbf{Z}}_{N}\leq\frac{1}{(1+\varepsilon)N^{1-2\xi}}\log{\mathbf{Z}}_{N,\beta_{N}=0}^{\omega,(1+\varepsilon)h_{N}}+\frac{\varepsilon}{(1+\varepsilon)N^{1-2\xi}}\log{\mathbf{Z}}_{N,\frac{1+\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}\beta_{N}}^{\omega,h_{N}=0}.

Similarly, we have the following lower bound. By Hölder’s inequality, for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle{\mathbf{Z}}_{N,\beta_{N}=0}^{\omega,(1-\varepsilon)h_{N}}

\displaystyle\leq\Big({\mathbf{Z}}_{N}\Big)^{1-\varepsilon}\Big({\mathbf{Z}}_{N,-\frac{1-\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}\beta_{N}}^{\omega,h_{N}=0}\Big)^{\varepsilon},

that is,

(3.3)

{\mathbf{Z}}_{N}\geq\Big({\mathbf{Z}}_{N,\beta_{N}=0}^{\omega,(1-\varepsilon)h_{N}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon}}\Big({\mathbf{Z}}_{N,-\frac{1-\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}\beta_{N}}^{\omega,h_{N}=0}\Big)^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}}.

Taking logarithm and dividing it by
N^{1-2\xi}
, we have that

(3.4)

\frac{1}{N^{1-2\xi}}\log{\mathbf{Z}}_{N}\geq\frac{1}{(1-\varepsilon)N^{1-2\xi}}\log{\mathbf{Z}}_{N,\beta_{N}=0}^{\omega,(1-\varepsilon)h_{N}}-\frac{\varepsilon}{(1-\varepsilon)N^{1-2\xi}}\log{\mathbf{Z}}_{N,-\frac{1-\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}\beta_{N}}^{\omega,h_{N}=0}.

We now show that the second terms on the right-hand side of (
3.2
) and (
3.4
) are negligible (Step 1), and the correct asymptotic is given by the first ones (Step 2).

Step 1.
We show that for any
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
,
\log{\mathbf{Z}}_{N,\delta\beta_{N}}^{\omega,h_{N}=0}=o(N^{1-2\xi})
in
{\mathbbm{P}}
-probability. Note that here we are in the setting of
[
6
]
, since
h_{N}=0
. Hence, many computations in
[
6
, Section 7]
can be directed applied here. We consider three cases separately.

Case 1.
For
\frac{d}{2}<\alpha<d
and
\frac{d-\alpha}{\alpha}<\gamma<\frac{d}{2\alpha}
, the partition function falls in the region B in
[
6
]
. Hence, by
[
6
, Theorem 2.7]
,

\frac{1}{N^{2\xi^{\prime}-1}}\log{\mathbf{Z}}_{N,\delta\beta_{N}}^{\omega,h_{N}=0}~\text{converges in distribution to a non-trivial limit with}~\xi^{\prime}=\frac{\alpha(1-\gamma)}{2\alpha-d}.

Note that in Region 4, we have that
\xi=\frac{\zeta}{2}
. Therefore, since
\gamma>\frac{2\alpha-d}{2\alpha}\zeta+\frac{d-\alpha}{\alpha}
,

\begin{split}1-2\xi-(2\xi^{\prime}-1)=&1-\zeta-\Big(\frac{2\alpha(1-\gamma)}{2\alpha-d}-1\Big)=\frac{2\alpha\gamma-(2\alpha-d)\zeta+2(\alpha-d)}{2\alpha-d}\\
>&\frac{1}{2\alpha-d}\Big(2\alpha\Big(\frac{2\alpha-d}{2\alpha}\zeta+\frac{d-\alpha}{\alpha}\Big)-(2\alpha-d)\zeta+2(\alpha-d)\Big)=0.\end{split}

Hence,
\frac{1}{N^{1-2\xi}}\log{\mathbf{Z}}_{N,\delta\beta_{N}}^{\omega,h_{N}=0}\overset{N\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0
in
{\mathbbm{P}}
-probability.

Case 2.
For
\frac{d}{2}\leq\alpha<d
and
\gamma\geq\frac{d}{2\alpha}
, the partition function falls in the region C in
[
6
]
(the boundary between the regions B and C in
[
6
]
is included). We adapt the approaches in
[
6
, Section 7]
, where the correct scaling for
\log{\mathbf{Z}}_{N,\delta\beta_{N}}^{\omega,h_{N}=0}
is determined, except for

Appendix A
Control on the last passage percolation with a given range

Recall the notations from Section
2
. For
p>1
and
s\geq 1
, let

(A.1)

\mathsf{L}_{m}(r,s)=\mathsf{L}_{m}^{p}(r,s):=\max\Big\{|\Delta|\colon\Delta=\Upsilon_{m}^{(pr)}\cap{\mathcal{R}}_{N}(S),S\in{\mathcal{M}}(r,s)\Big\},

where
\Upsilon_{m}^{(r)}:=\{{\mathbf{Y}}_{i}^{(r)},1\leq i\leq m\}
is the set of
m
different sites taken uniformly on
\Lambda_{r}
and

(A.2)

{\mathcal{M}}(r,s)={\mathcal{M}}_{p}(r,s):=\Big\{S\colon\,M_{N}(S)\in[r,pr],\,|{\mathcal{R}}_{N}(S)|\in[rs,p^{2}rs]\Big\},

Remark A.1
.

Let us observe that in the definition of
\mathsf{L}_{m}(r,s)
we only consider the cardinality of a finite set
\Delta
. In the sequel, it is convenient to look at
\Delta
as an ordered set. For this purpose, we pick at random a trajectory
S\in{\mathcal{M}}(r,s)
with
\Delta=\Upsilon_{m}^{(pr)}\cap{\mathcal{R}}_{N}(S)
, so that any set
\Delta
is associated with an unique random walk trajectory
S
.
Therefore, if
\Delta=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
, then we order the points
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
according to
S
, that is,
x_{1}
is the first point visited by
S
,
x_{2}
the second one and so on.
We stress the fact that the only important point is that
\Delta
is ordered according to a random walk trajectory, in order to control its entropy, see (
A.5
) and (
A.6
).

The following result gives us a control on
\mathsf{L}_{m}(r,s)
.

Theorem A.1
.

For any fixed
p>1
, there exists a constant
c>0
which depends only on
p
and the dimension
d
, such that for any
r,s\geq 1
and
1\leq k\leq|\Lambda_{pr}|
,

(A.3)

{\mathbbm{P}}\Big(\mathsf{L}_{m}(r,s)\geq k\Big)\leq\Bigg(\frac{c\,s\,m^{1/d}}{k}\Bigg)^{dk}.

In particular, for any
b\in[0,d]
there exists a constant
c=c(d,b,p)
such that for any
r,s>0
and
m\geq 1
,

(A.4)

{\mathbbm{E}}\Bigg[\Bigg(\frac{\mathsf{L}_{m}(r,s)}{(sm^{1/d})\wedge m}\Bigg)^{b}\Bigg]\leq c.

Proof.

We first introduce an
entropy
for any ordered
(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})
by

(A.5)

\text{Ent}((x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})):=\frac{d}{2}\Big(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|\Big)^{2}

with the convention
x_{0}
being the origin (see
[
6
]
). According to Remark
A.1
, for any
\Delta
we have associated an unique random walk trajectory
S
such that
\Delta\subset{\mathcal{R}}_{N}(S)
and the points of
\Delta
are ordered according to
S
. So that, we can write
\Delta=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})
and
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|x_{i}-x_{i-1}\|\leq C_{d}|{\mathcal{R}}_{N}(S)|\leq C_{d}p^{2}rs
by the configuration of the simple random walk. Hence,

(A.6)

\begin{split}\mathsf{L}_{m}(r,s)&=\max\Big\{|\Delta|\colon\Delta=\Upsilon_{m}^{(pr)}\cap{\mathcal{R}}_{N}(S),S\in{\mathcal{M}}(r,s),\text{Ent}(\Delta)\leq B\Big\}\\
&\leq L_{m}^{(B)}=L_{m,p,r}^{(B)}:=\max\Big\{|\Delta|\colon\Delta\subset\Upsilon_{m}^{(pr)},\,\text{Ent}(\Delta)\leq B\Big\}\end{split}

with
B=\frac{d}{2}C_{d}^{2}p^{4}(rs)^{2}
. Hence, by
[
6
, Theorem A.1]
, we have that

{\mathbbm{P}}\Big(\mathsf{L}_{m}(r,s)\geq k\Big)\leq{\mathbbm{P}}\Big(L_{m}^{(B)}\geq k\Big)\leq\Big(\frac{cB^{1/2}m^{1/d}}{rk}\Big)^{dk},

and (
A.4
) follows by
[
6
, Corollary A.2]
.

∎

Then we have the following control on the
energy
on a range, which is similar to
[
6
, Lemma A.3]
.

Proposition A.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}(r,s)
be as in (
A.2
), then there exists a constant
C>0
such that for any
t>0
,
p>1
and
s\geq 1
,

(A.7)

{\mathbbm{P}}\Bigg(\max\limits_{S\in{\mathcal{M}}(r,s)}\Omega_{r}({\mathcal{R}}_{N}(S))>r^{\frac{d}{\alpha}}st\Bigg)\\
\leq Ct^{-\frac{\alpha d}{\alpha+d}}.

Proof.

For
S\in{\mathcal{M}}(r,s)
and
p>1
, we have that (for simplicity, we omit
\lfloor\cdot\rfloor
),

\Omega_{r}({\mathcal{R}}_{N}(S))=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\log_{p}|\Lambda_{r}|}\sum\limits_{j=p^{i-1}}^{p^{i}-1}{\mathbf{M}}_{j}^{(r)}\mathbbm{1}_{\{{\mathbf{Y}}_{j}^{(r)}\in{\mathcal{R}}_{N}(S)\}}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\log_{p}|\Lambda_{r}|}{\mathbf{M}}_{p^{i-1}}^{(r)}\mathsf{L}_{p^{i}}(r,s).

Then the probability in (
A.7
) is bounded by

\sum_{i=1}^{\log_{p}|\Lambda_{r}|}{\mathbbm{P}}\Bigg({\mathbf{M}}_{p^{i-1}}^{(r)}\mathsf{L}_{p^{i}}(r,s)>Cp^{-i(\frac{1}{\alpha}-\frac{1}{d}-2\delta)}r^{\frac{d}{\alpha}}st\Bigg),

for some sufficiently small

since
\alpha<d
with
C=\big(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}p^{-i(\frac{1}{\alpha}-\frac{1}{d}-2\delta)}\big)^{-1}
.

We then bound the above probability by

\sum_{i=1}^{\log_{p}|\Lambda_{r}|}\Bigg({\mathbbm{P}}\Big({\mathbf{M}}_{p^{i-1}}^{(r)}>t^{1-\eta}p^{-\frac{i}{\alpha}+i\delta}r^{\frac{d}{\alpha}}\Big)+{\mathbbm{P}}\Big(\mathsf{L}_{p^{i}}(r,s)>t^{\eta}p^{\frac{i}{d}+i\delta}s\Big)\Bigg).

) with

, so that it is smaller than

In such a way (

Then we work on the centering term in (

(B.8)

\begin{split}&\frac{{\mathbf{E}}\Big[\Big|e^{(\lambda_{N}-h_{N})|{\mathcal{R}}_{N}|}-e^{(\frac{1}{2}\beta_{N}^{2}-h_{N})|{\mathcal{R}}_{N}|}\Big|\Big]}{a_{N}\beta_{N}}\leq C\frac{|\lambda_{N}-\frac{1}{2}\beta_{N}^{2}|N}{a_{N}\beta_{N}}\leq C\frac{Nk_{N}^{1-\alpha}\log k_{N}}{a_{N}}\\

B.2
Regions
R_{2}
and
R_{3}

By the trivial bound
-|{\mathcal{R}}_{N}|\geq-N
, we have that

{\mathbf{Z}}_{N}^{h_{N}=0}e^{-\hat{h}N^{1-\zeta}}\leq{\mathbf{Z}}_{N}\leq{\mathbf{Z}}_{N}^{h_{N}=0}.

Then in the region
R_{2}
, by taking logarithm of
e^{\hat{h}N^{1-\zeta}}
and dividing it by
N^{2\xi-1}
, we have that

N^{1-\zeta-(2\xi-1)}=N^{2-\zeta-\frac{2\alpha(1-\gamma)}{2\alpha-d}}=N^{\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha-d}(\gamma-\frac{2\alpha-d}{2\alpha}\zeta-\frac{d-\alpha}{\alpha})}\overset{N\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

Similarly, in region
R_{3}
, we have that

\frac{N^{1-\zeta}}{\beta_{N}N^{\frac{d}{\alpha}}}=N^{1-\zeta+\gamma-\frac{d}{\alpha}}=N^{\gamma-\zeta-\frac{d-\alpha}{\alpha}}\overset{N\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

Hence, in the regions

and
R_{3}

range

[
6

R_{2}

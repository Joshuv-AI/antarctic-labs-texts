Cost-effectiveness analysis of efavirenz versus ainuovirine in people living with HIV initiating first-line combination antiretroviral therapy
Authors: Chenlu Yang, Yuanqi Mi, Yuhong Zeng, Feng Cheng, Huatang Zeng, Mengge Zhou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAinuovirine (ANV), a new non-nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor, has been shown to have comparable effectiveness to efavirenz (EFV) with fewer adverse events. However, evidence on the cost-effectiveness of replacing EFV-based regimens with ANV-based regimens remains limited, particularly within the context of China’s National Free Antiretroviral Treatment Programme.ObjectiveThis study aimed to evaluate the cost-effectiveness of ANV-based versus EFV-based regimens among people living with HIV (PLWH) in China.MethodsDecision tree-Markov models were developed to compare EFV-based and ANV-based regimens over a 15-year time horizon from a societal perspective. Model inputs, including treatment costs and adverse event risks, were mainly obtained from a multicentre Chinese cohort and field survey data. Cost-effectiveness was assessed separately for treatment-naïve and treatment-experienced populations and at the overall programme level. Outcomes included costs, quality-adjusted life-years (QALYs), adverse events and incremental cost-effectiveness ratios (ICERs). One-way, probabilistic and scenario sensitivity analyses were conducted to assess parameter and structural uncertainty.ResultsCompared with the EFV-based regimen, ANV-based regimen was associated with a marked reduction in cumulative adverse events, while producing small per-person QALY gains of 0.006–0.011 at modestly higher costs across the modelled populations. ANV-based regimens generated slightly higher QALYs at modestly higher costs across all modelled populations. The ICERs were $10 030.41/QALY in treatment-naïve individuals, $3881.06/QALY in treatment-experienced individuals and $5146.18/QALY at the overall programme level, all below the prespecified willingness-to-pay threshold of three times China’s 2019 per-capita gross domestic product ($30 793.15/QALY). Sensitivity analyses showed that the results were robust, with ANV and EFV medication costs being the most influential parameters. If the half-year cost of ANV medication decreased below $361.39, the ANV-based regimen would become the dominant strategy, providing greater health benefits at lower overall costs.ConclusionWithin the context of China’s National Free Antiretroviral Treatment Programme, ANV-based regimens were cost-effective compared with EFV-based regimens among PLWH, with reduced adverse events and modest QALY gains at acceptable incremental costs. These findings support consideration of ANV-based regimens as an alternative option in future procurement and price negotiation decisions.

Introduction

HIV continues to pose a significant threat to global health. At the end of 2022, there were approximately 39.0 million people living with HIV (PLWH) globally, with 1.3 million newly identified infections occurring in 2022.1 The situation of HIV infection in China is concerning. It was reported that by the end of 2022, there were about 1.22 million PLWH in China.2 With the widespread use of highly active antiretroviral therapy (HAART) since 1996,3 there has been a notable enhancement in the life expectancy of those infected with HIV.

HAART generally consists of two nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors (NRTIs) and a third agent, which are usually either a protease inhibitor or non-nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor (NNRTI). According to the most recent Chinese guidelines, tenofovir disoproxil fumarate+lamivudine or emtricitabine+efavirenz (EFV) or rilpivirine should be the choice for first-line HIV/AIDS treatment.4 And EFV was shown to be the most commonly used NNRTI in China after it was included in the National Free Antiretroviral Treatment Programme in 2005.5 However, the adverse effects of EFV, such as dyslipidaemia and neuropsychiatric symptoms, raise ongoing continuous concerns.6 Therefore, efforts have been made to develop and validate alternative NNRTIs that offer comparable efficacy with fewer adverse effects. Recently, a study showed the efficacy of ainuovirine (ANV) was non-inferior to EFV when combined with two NRTIs; meanwhile, participants treated with ANV experienced less frequent liver toxicity, dyslipidaemia, neuropsychiatric symptoms and rash compared with the EFV group.7

Although ANV was considered in Chinese guidelines as an optional NNRTI in 2021,4 the economic value of ANV had not been investigated previously.

Objectives

Therefore, we aim to conduct a cost-effectiveness analysis of ANV-based combined treatment on the decision tree-Markov model over the 15-year horizon in China. We aimed to estimate the costs, adverse events, quality-adjusted life-years (QALYs) and incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICER) of ANV-based versus EFV-based first-line treatment, within the context of China’s National Free Antiretroviral Treatment Programme, to inform evidence-based decision-making.

Methods

Health economic analysis plan and data source

The analysis was designed to evaluate the cost-effectiveness of EFV-based versus ANV-based first-line combination antiretroviral therapy among PLWH in China.

Model parameters were obtained from multiple data sources. Clinical inputs for first-line EFV- and ANV-based therapy, including adverse event risks, viral suppression, treatment discontinuation or failure and treatment-experience status, were primarily derived from a multicentre Chinese cohort study of PLWH. Cost inputs for first-line therapy and adverse event management were obtained from an accompanying cross-sectional cost survey conducted across participating centres.7 When determining parameters through literature analysis, the following principles were primarily considered: (a) recent nationally representative research, (b) meta-analytical results based on multiple populations and (c) relevant articles on health economic evaluations. Study population, setting and comparators

The target population was adult PLWH receiving first-line combination antiretroviral therapy in China. Pregnant and lactating women were excluded. The study was situated within the context of China’s National Free Antiretroviral Treatment Programme, in which EFV-based regimens have been widely used and remain an important first-line option in routine programme practice. Therefore, EFV-based therapy was selected as the policy-relevant reference strategy for this analysis.

Because treatment experience may modify adverse event risks and treatment outcomes, two complementary decision-tree structures were evaluated (figure 1A). Model 1 first stratified individuals by treatment experience and then compared EFV-based versus ANV-based therapy within each stratum, corresponding to stratum-specific cost-effectiveness analyses. Model 2 first assigned individuals to EFV-based or ANV-based therapy, corresponding to an overall programme-level comparison while preserving treatment-experience composition within each regimen group. Nevertheless, because both models used the same subgroup-specific inputs and downstream Markov pathways, the programme-level expected costs and QALYs are algebraically equivalent to the corresponding population-weighted model 1 results. In both models, the four resulting cohorts subsequently entered the same Markov model to project long-term outcomes (figure 1B).

Perspective, time horizon, discounting and currency

The analysis was conducted from a societal perspective, including direct medical costs, direct non-medical costs and productivity losses. A 15-year time horizon was used to capture medium- to long-term HIV-related clinical and economic outcomes after first-line treatment initiation, according to the diagnosis and treatment guidelines for AIDS patients and related research.4 The Markov model used 6-month cycles, corresponding to 30 cycles over the full-time horizon. Future costs and health outcomes were discounted at an annual rate of 5%, with alternative discount rates examined in sensitivity analyses. Costs were expressed in 2019 US dollars, using an exchange rate of 1 US dollar=6.9066 renminbi (RMB).

Model structure and assumptions

Decision-tree Markov models were developed in TreeAge Pro (V.2019; TreeAge Software Inc., Williamstown, MA, USA). Two decision-tree structures were evaluated before entry into the Markov model (figure 1A). Model 1 estimated the cost-effectiveness of ANV versus EFV separately among treatment-naïve and treatment-experienced individuals, and model 2 estimated the overall programme-level cost-effectiveness of ANV versus EFV. After the decision-tree stage, each cohort entered the same Markov model (figure 1B). The Markov model included health states for first-line therapy, adverse events, viral suppression, undetectable viral load, treatment discontinuation or failure, second-line therapy, third-line therapy and death. Adverse events included central nervous system (CNS) symptoms, dyslipidaemia, renal injury and liver injury. Individuals with treatment discontinuation or failure could transition to second-line therapy, and those with second-line discontinuation or failure could subsequently transition to third-line therapy. Death was an absorbing state. We supposed that there were 10 000 adults with an average initial age of 40 years in each cohort.

The model used 6-month cycles over a 15-year time horizon. Half-cycle correction was applied consistently to both costs and QALYs to account for transitions occurring, on average, halfway through each cycle. Specifically, costs and utilities accrued in the first cycle received an additional half-cycle weight, those accrued in the final cycle received a half-cycle reduction and all intermediate cycles were assigned full-cycle weights. Individuals with undetectable viral load were assumed to have mortality comparable to the general Chinese population. Mortality risks were higher among individuals with viral suppression or treatment discontinuation/failure, based on published estimates.8 In the base case, adverse events were assumed not to independently increase mortality.9

Definition of health status

CNS adverse events were defined as having one of the following symptoms: dizziness, headache, insomnia, depression and abnormal thinking according to Chinese HIV guidelines.4 The definition of dyslipidaemia included total cholesterol≥7.2 mmol/L or low-density lipoprotein cholesterol≥4.9 mmol/L.10 We defined serum creatinine>106 µmol/L or blood urea nitrogen>8.2 mmol/L as kidney injury.11 12 Serum alanine aminotransferase (ALT) or aspartate transferase (AST)>3 times the upper limit of normal (ULN) and a concomitant rise in serum total bilirubin to >2 times the ULN was defined as liver injury.13 According to the American Board of Internal Medicine Laboratory Tests Reference Ranges,14 the ULN for ALT and AST is defined as 40 U/L, and the ULN for serum total bilirubin is defined as 1.0 mg/dL.

Individuals with HIV-RNA<50 copies/mL were defined as undetectable viral load, while those with HIV-RNA>1000 copies/mL at baseline, and a subsequent HIV-RNA>1000 copies/mL within 3–6 months, were categorised as experiencing virological failure.15 The interim state between these two conditions was characterised as viral suppression.

Outcomes and cost measurement

The primary outcome was the ICER, calculated as the incremental cost per additional QALYs gained. The model also estimated total costs, QALYs, adverse events, treatment outcomes and deaths for each strategy.

QALYs were accumulated by multiplying health-state utility values by the time spent in each state during each model cycle. Costs were estimated from a societal perspective and included direct medical costs, direct non-medical costs and productivity losses. Direct medical costs included medication and examination costs. Direct non-medical costs included transportation, meals and accommodation. Productivity losses included time costs for patients and caregivers. First-line regimen and adverse event management costs were obtained from 607 PLWH using EFV or ANV across 15 centres in China. Costs for second-line and third-line therapy were obtained from published economic studies.

Base-case analysis

We computed the anticipated costs, QALYs, number of adverse events and number of deaths for each strategy. A strategy was considered cost-effective if its ICER was at or below the willingness-to-pay (WTP) threshold, which was set at three times the per capita gross domestic product (GDP) of China in 2019. To simulate real-world scenarios, this study conducted semi-cycle corrections in the Markov cohort analysis.

Uncertainty and model verification

Internal validity was assessed by checking that transition probabilities summed to one at each chance node, cohort occupancy summed to one in each cycle, death was absorbing and per-person discounted QALYs did not exceed the theoretical maximum implied by the highest utility value and the 15-year discounted time horizon.

For the overall programme-level analysis, parameter uncertainty was assessed using one-way and probabilistic sensitivity analyses. In the probabilistic sensitivity analysis, probability or incidence parameters and effectiveness parameters were assigned beta distributions, and costs were assigned gamma distributions. Shared non-drug cost components common to EFV- and ANV-based regimens were modelled as correlated parameters and sampled once per probabilistic iteration, whereas medication-specific cost components were sampled separately. To assess the stability of Monte Carlo simulation results, probabilistic analyses were repeated using 500, 1000, 2000, 5000, 8000, 10 000 and 12 000 iterations. Results stabilised when the number of iterations reached 8000 or more (online supplemental table S3); therefore, 8000 iterations were used for the main probabilistic sensitivity analysis. Cost-effectiveness planes and cost-effectiveness acceptability curves were generated based on the probabilistic sensitivity analysis.

One-way sensitivity analyses were conducted by varying key parameters across prespecified plausible ranges while holding all other parameters constant. The influence of each parameter on the ICER was ranked using tornado diagrams, and additional single-parameter analyses were conducted for parameters with substantial influence on the results.

Scenario analyses were conducted to assess structural and clinical uncertainty. First, because baseline characteristics differed across the four cohorts, we conducted a scenario analysis using covariate-adjusted adverse event risks. For each adverse event, a logistic regression model was fitted with treatment cohort, age, sex, WHO stage and calendar period as covariates. Standardised half-year adverse event risks were obtained using regression-based marginal standardisation by predicting each participant’s adverse event probability under each treatment cohort while holding the empirical covariate distribution of the pooled population fixed and averaging the predicted probabilities. These adjusted adverse event risks were then substituted for the crude adverse event risks in the model. Second, because the base-case analysis assumed that adverse events did not independently increase mortality risk, we examined scenarios in which adverse events increased mortality risk by 10%, 20% and 40%, respectively. Third, because second-line treatment costs were obtained from published sources outside the primary cohort, we examined a conservative scenario in which second-line treatment costs were doubled. These scenario analyses were used to assess the robustness of the base-case ICER to cohort imbalance, adverse event-related mortality assumptions and uncertainty in downstream treatment costs.

Patient and public involvement

Patients or the public were not involved in the design, or conduct, or reporting, or dissemination plans of our research.

Results

Study parameters

The proportion of treatment-naïve among PLWH was 30.6%.16 The incidence of adverse events, the probability of achieving virus suppression with first-line treatment, the probability of discontinuation or failure of first-line treatment and the probability of achieving undetectable in those with virus suppression were all based on multiple centres follow-up data from PLWH in China who were combining with EFV or ANV concurrently (online supplemental tables S1 and S2). Based on previous studies, the probability of initiating second-line treatment after discontinuation or failure of first-line treatment was estimated as 7.7%,17 18 and the probability of discontinuation or failure of second-line treatment was 7.3%.19 Similarly, the probability of initiating third-line treatment after discontinuation or failure of second-line treatment was 4.4%,20 and the probability of interruption or failure of third-line treatment was 16.9%.21 The details of data sources are presented in online supplemental materials. Based on previous studies, there is no significant difference in mortality rates between PLWH with undetectable RNA copies and the general population,22 23 and the mortality of PLWH achieving undetectable was based on the 2019 China Population and Employment Statistical Yearbook.24 The risk of mortality in HIV-infected individuals with viral suppressed and viral unsuppressed was 1.39 times and 1.96 times higher than in those achieving undetectable.8

The cost of first-line treatment and management of related adverse events was based on cross-sectional survey data from 607 PLWH using EFV or ANV across 15 centres in China. Transportation expenses, meal and accommodation expenses and lost productivity were weighted for both patients and caregivers. The detailed cost calculations can be found in the online supplemental materials. The costs of second-line and third-line treatments were based on previous findings.25 26

Given that the average age of the population in Kauf et al’s study is around 40 years,27 which is similar to this study, the QALYs for HIV-infected states from their study were considered as the initial health effects in this study. According to their estimation, with each additional year of age, the effectiveness decreased by 0.001 QALYs. Treatment-experienced PLWH experienced a decrease of 0.003 QALYs compared with treatment-naïve individuals, while those experiencing adverse events decreased by 0.012 QALYs. In previous health economic studies, effectiveness has often been estimated using the weighted number of CD4+ T cells.28 29 However, due to the lack of long-term data on changes in CD4+ T cells with the use of ANV, QALYs based on HIV-RNA and CD4+ T cells jointly grouped by Shade et al’s study are used.30 Different health states’ QALYs were weighted based on the distribution proportions of HIV-RNA and CD4+ T cell counts to obtain QALYs for different health states.

Base-case analysis

Assuming at the start of the cycle, there were 10 000 individuals in each of the treatment-naïve (EFV-based), treatment-naïve (ANV-based), treatment-experienced (EFV-based) and treatment-experienced (ANV-based). After 30 cycles (equivalent to 15 years), compared with the regimen of using EFV in combination, the estimated use of ANV can reduce 3195 CNS adverse events, 2529 dyslipidaemia events, 117 liver injury events and 347 kidney injury events after 15 years (online supplemental material).

Over 15 years, ANV-based regimen was associated with slightly higher discounted costs and greater discounted QALYs than EFV-based regimen across all modelled populations (table 2 and online supplemental figure S1).

In the treatment-naïve population evaluated using model 1, the discounted costs were $7700.41 for EFV-based regimen and $7768.17 for ANV-based regimen, and the corresponding QALYs were 8.46969 and 8.47644, respectively. ANV-based regimen therefore generated an additional 0.00675 QALYs at an incremental cost of $67.75, resulting in an ICER of $10 030.41/QALY compared with EFV.

In the treatment-experienced population evaluated using model 1, ANV-based regimen yielded 8.41605 QALYs at a cost of $7774.11, compared with 8.40457 QALYs at a cost of $7729.55 for EFV-based regimen. The incremental cost and incremental QALYs were $44.55 and 0.01148, respectively, giving an ICER of $3881.06/QALY.

In the overall programme-level analysis using model 2, ANV-based regimen remained more effective and more costly than EFV-based regimen, with discounted costs of $7772.29 versus $7720.65 and QALYs of 8.43451 versus 8.42448. The resulting incremental cost was $51.65, the incremental QALY gain was 0.01004 and the ICER was $5146.18/QALY.

Using the prespecified WTP of three times China’s 2019 per-capita GDP ($30 793.15/QALY), ANV-based regimen was cost-effective compared with EFV-based regimen in the treatment-naïve, treatment-experienced and overall programme-level analyses. The ICERs were also below the more conservative benchmark of one time China’s 2019 per-capita GDP ($10 264.38/QALY), although the ICER in the treatment-naïve population ($10 030.41/QALY) was close to this lower benchmark.

Uncertainty analysis

One-way sensitivity analysis for the overall programme-level analysis showed that the ICER of the ANV-based regimen versus the EFV-based regimen was most sensitive to the cost of ANV medication and the cost of EFV medication, which together accounted for more than 90% of the variation in model outcomes (figure 2A). In the one-way sensitivity analysis of ANV medication cost, all ICERs remained below the WTP for China ($30 793.15/QALY). In addition, when the cost of ANV medication decreased to below $361.39, corresponding to an approximately 20% reduction from the base-case value of $451.74, ANV-based combination therapy became the dominant strategy (figure 2B).

In the probabilistic sensitivity analysis (8000 Monte Carlo iterations), an incremental cost-effectiveness scatter plot was generated (figure 2C). With a WTP threshold of $30 793.15, the probability of combined ANV use being cost-effective relative to EFV was 89.49% (figure 2C). Furthermore, the cost-effectiveness-acceptability curve depicted the probability of both strategies being cost-effective within the WTP range. The results indicated that as the WTP increases to $2463.45 per QALY, the probability of ANV becoming the dominant strategy exceeds that of EFV, and this probability gradually increases with increasing WTP (figure 3). The intersection point mentioned above was below both the Chinese WTP ($30 793.15 per QALY) and the more conservative benchmark of one time China’s 2019 per capita GDP ($10 264.38). Scenario analyses

To assess the potential impact of differences in age, sex, WHO stage and calendar period of enrolment across the four cohorts, we adjusted adverse event incidences using logistic regression-based marginal standardisation (online supplemental table S4). The results showed that the use of covariate-adjusted adverse event incidences did not materially change the base-case conclusion. Using the adjusted incidences, ANV-based regimen remained cost-effective compared with EFV-based regimen, with ICERs of $4881.42/QALY, $699.09/QALY and $1526.06/QALY in the treatment-naïve, treatment-experienced and overall programme-level analyses, respectively (table 3).

In addition, we conducted scenario analyses by increasing the mortality risk among PLWH who experienced adverse events by 10% (online supplemental table S5), 20% (online supplemental table S6) and 40% (online supplemental table S7), respectively, and by doubling the cost of second-line treatment (online supplemental table S8). These findings suggested that the base-case results were robust to assumptions regarding adverse event-related mortality and second-line treatment costs.

Discussion

We found that in comparison to EFV-based regimen, ANV-based regimen results in fewer adverse events for PLWH. Overall, compared with EFV-based regimen, ANV-based regimen was projected to prevent adverse events over 15 years, while producing small QALY gains at modest additional costs, with ICERs of $10 030.41/QALY in treatment-naïve individuals, $3881.06/QALY in treatment-experienced individuals and $5146.18/QALY at the overall programme level, all below the prespecified WTP threshold of $30 793.15/QALY. Moreover, the results of the uncertainty analysis and scenario analyses further affirm the dependability of these conclusions. Therefore, within the context of China’s National Free Antiretroviral Treatment Programme, the ANV-based regimen may represent a more economically sound alternative to the EFV-based regimen for primary care among PLWH in China.

For external validation, model outputs for the EFV-based arm were compared with those reported in a previous Chinese cost-effectiveness analysis by Li et al.31 In that study, 75 000 individuals were simulated over a lifetime horizon of 40 years from a societal perspective with total discounted costs of $1 796 066 692 and total discounted QALYs of 882 663 for the EFV-based regimen. These corresponded to $23 947.56 and 11.77 discounted QALYs per person. In the present model, the corresponding values for the EFV-based arm in the overall programme-level analysis were $7720.65 and 8.42448 discounted QALYs per person over 15 years. The difference in cost estimates may mainly reflect differences in data sources, particularly in adverse drug reaction management costs: the costs in Li et al were derived from published literature, whereas the present study used cost data collected through a field survey among PLWH in China. The difference in QALY estimates may be attributable to differences in time horizon, model structure and population characteristics.

ANV, a new NNRTI, was initially synthesised in Korea and later further developed in both Korea and China7. As ANV was mostly metabolised by CYP2C19 rather than other common CYP enzymes, preclinical and phase 1 clinical studies showed that ANV had low drug–drug interaction potential, good tolerability and few serious side effects.32 33 Between 27 November 2018 and 11 March 2021, a randomised, double-blind, double-dummy, positive parallel group, non-inferiority, phase 3 trial was conducted in seven sites across China to compare the efficacy and safety of ANV versus EFV combination therapies with lamivudine/tenofovir disoproxil fumarate for medication of treatment-naïve HIV-1-positive adults.7 This study found that the efficacy of ANV was non-inferior to EFV when combined with two NRTIs; meanwhile, ANV-treated participants had less frequency of liver toxicity, dyslipidaemia, neuropsychiatric symptoms and rash compared with the EFV group during the first 48 weeks of therapy. Additionally, our data also suggested that for treatment-experienced PLWH, ANV significantly reduced the occurrence of dyslipidaemia and CNS adverse events. Therefore, additional investigations into the cost-effectiveness of ANV-based regimens are imperative to support their widespread implementation and integration into health policies.

Currently, the combination therapy regimen using EFV holds the largest market share in clinical settings in China, accounting for about 90%.31 However, our one-way sensitivity analysis indicated that if the cost of ANV medication falls below $361.39 per half-year (corresponding to an approximately 20% reduction), ANV would become the dominant strategy, being both less costly and more effective than EFV. This suggests that if ANV is promoted and its market share increases, leading to cost reductions, the combined use of ANV may become the most cost-effective first-line treatment regimen.

Strengths and limitations

Our study has several strengths. First, it is a study evaluating the long-term costs and health impacts of two first-line antiretroviral therapy regimens (ANV vs EFV) from a societal perspective, which to our knowledge has not been published before. Second, the majority of the parameters used in this study are derived from field surveys conducted in multiple centres in China, including probability parameters obtained from cohort studies and cost parameters obtained from cross-sectional surveys at the end of the cohort study7. This allows for a reflection of the real situation in China.

This study has several limitations. First, because ANV has been available on the market for a relatively short period, this study was limited to short-term adverse outcome data. Accordingly, the adverse events included in the model were mainly short-term events and may not fully capture long-term outcomes, such as cardiovascular disease and chronic metabolic disorders. In addition, dyslipidaemia was defined based on total cholesterol and low-density lipoprotein cholesterol according to expert consensus, while triglycerides were not included. Previous studies have suggested that ANV may improve triglyceride levels more favourably than EFV.34 Given that triglyceride levels are associated with chronic conditions such as fatty liver disease,35 pancreatitis36 and cardiovascular diseases,37 38 excluding triglycerides from the dyslipidaemia definition may have underestimated the relative safety advantage of ANV. Second, some cost parameters in this study were based on studies of foreign populations. Due to inherent differences in medical costs between Chinese and Western populations, this may lead to overestimation of the costs incurred by both groups after switching to second-line treatment. However, sensitivity analysis indicates that these cost parameters did not significantly influence the model results. Third, this study was designed from a societal perspective, and the conclusions obtained may differ from those considering the patient’s perspective. If both drugs are covered by medical insurance from the patient’s perspective, then ANV is undoubtedly more cost-effective than EFV. Fourth, because of the observational nature of the cohort data, baseline characteristics differed across the EFV- and ANV-based regimen groups. In particular, PLWHs receiving ANV-based regimens were older than those receiving EFV-based regimens, and differences were also observed in sex distribution, WHO stage and calendar period of enrolment. These imbalances may have influenced the estimated incidence of adverse events and, consequently, the modelled costs and QALYs. To examine the potential impact of this limitation, we conducted a scenario analysis using adverse event incidences adjusted for age, sex, WHO stage and calendar period through logistic regression-based marginal standardisation. The results were consistent with the base-case findings, suggesting that the main conclusion was robust to measured baseline differences. Nevertheless, residual confounding from unmeasured factors could not be fully excluded.

Conclusions

Within China’s National Free Antiretroviral Treatment Programme, ANV-based therapy represents a more economically favourable alternative to EFV-based therapy among PLWH. If the half-year cost of ANV medication decreases below $361.39, ANV-based therapy would become the dominant strategy, providing greater health benefits at lower overall costs. These findings support consideration of ANV-based regimens as an alternative option in future procurement or price negotiation decisions.

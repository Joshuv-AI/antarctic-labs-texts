Differential and other reductions of the self-dual conformal structure equations Thanks: In memory of V.E. Zakharov
L.V. Bogdanov

Abstract
The dispersionless integrable system we consider here was introduced to the literature rather recently, it is connected with the general local form of self-dual conformal structure (SDCS) for the signature (2,2). In integrability framework this system possesses a rich structure of reductions, including differential reductions. We will discuss several characteristic reductions for this system, using the Lax pair, hierarchy structure and the dressing scheme. We use reductions to construct solutions for the SDCS equations. One of our goals is to present type B SDCS system and consider its relations with the SDCS system.



1
Introduction

Differential reductions play an important role in the theory of integrable
systems. They represent some nonlinear differential relations compatible with
the initial integrable system. A remarkable example of a differential reduction, connected
with the description of n-orthogonal surfaces,
was introduced to integrability by V.E. Zakharov
[
1
]
.
N-orthogonal surfaces are characterised by the Darboux-Lamé system of equations. One part
of this system consists of the system of equations of conjugate surfaces
(diagonal curvature spaces), which is related
to N-wave equations, integrated in 1974 by Zakharov and Shabat

[
2
]
. Another part represents a set of
compatible nonlinear differential relations connected with orthogonality,
the meaning of which in the framework of the theory of
integrable systems was unclear. V.E. Zakharov managed to find a description of
this reduction in terms of integrability scheme (the dressing scheme),
and it appeared rather simple, natural and elegant
[
1
]
(also
[
3
]
). This discovery opened a way to classification of N-orthogonal surfaces
[
1
]
.

In the present work we are dealing with dispersionless integrability, which is somewhat technically
different from the standard case (with dispersion).
However, many dispersionless integrable systems are
also connected with geometry, and reductions, including differential reductions, play an important
role. The integrable system we consider here was introduced to the literature rather recently. First,
the Lax pair corresponding to this system appeared in the work
[
4
]
as an unreduced form of
the Lax pair for the Dunajski system, generalising the second heavenly equation
[
5
]
.
And later

[
6
]
it was demonstrated that the system of equations related to this Lax pair describes a general
local form of self-dual conformal structure (SDCS) for the signature (2,2), thus in this work
we call it SDCS system. In integrability framework this system possesses a rich structure
of reductions, including differential reductions. We will introduce several characteristic reductions
for this system, using the Lax pair, hierarchy structure and the dressing scheme. We use reductions to construct solutions for the SDCS system.
We hope that, due to the geometric meaning of the system, these reductions will find
some geometric interpretation and could be useful for the study of self-dual conformal structures.
We will concentrate on the less known and new reductions, not paying much attention to
the well known Dunajski
system and second heavenly equation.

One of our goals is to present type B SDCS system and
consider its reductions and relations with the SDCS system, which appear to be rather
unexpected. The term type B here refers to BKP hierarchy, where it is connected with a type B
infinite-dimensional Lie algebra. In dispersionless case the symmetry characterising the
BKP hierarchy is manifested as a symmetry of wave functions with respect to reflection
of the spectral parameter, it is preserved only by odd times of the initial hierarchy, similar
to BKP hierarchy (see e.g.
[
7
]
).

2
SDCS system of equations

Let us consider the Lax pair
[
4
]

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} X_{1}&=\partial_{z}-\lambda\partial_{x}+F_{x}\partial_{x}+G_{x}\partial_{y}+f_{x}\partial_{\lambda},\\
X_{2}&=\partial_{w}-\lambda\partial_{y}+F_{y}\partial_{x}+G_{y}\partial_{y}+f_{y}\partial_{\lambda}.\end{aligned}

(1)

This Lax pair represents vector fields of the lowest order (in
\lambda
)
of SDCS system hierarchy (see Section
4
for more detail).
Commutation relations for the Lax pair give a
coupled system of three second-order nonlinear PDEs for functions
F
,
G
,
f

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} Q(F)&=f_{y},\\
Q(G)&=-f_{x},\\
Q(f)&=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(2)

where a linear second order differential operator
Q
is expressed as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} Q&=(\partial_{w}+F_{y}\partial_{x}+G_{y}\partial_{y})\partial_{x}-(\partial_{z}+F_{x}\partial_{x}+G_{x}\partial_{y})\partial_{y}\\
&=\partial_{w}\partial_{x}-\partial_{z}\partial_{y}+F_{y}{\partial_{x}}^{2}-G_{x}{\partial_{y}}^{2}-(F_{x}-G_{y})\partial_{x}\partial_{y}.\end{aligned}

(3)

System (
2
) can be rewritten in the form of a
coupled system of third order PDEs for the functions
F
,
G

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{x}(Q(F))+\partial_{y}(Q(G))=0,\\
&(\partial_{w}+F_{y}\partial_{x}+G_{y}\partial_{y})Q(G)+(\partial_{z}+F_{x}\partial_{x}+G_{x}\partial_{y})Q(F)=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(4)

in this form it was introduced in
[
6
]
as a general
local form of equations describing an (anti)self-dual conformal structure (SDCS) for the signature (2,2),
which is locally represented by a metric

\displaystyle g=dwdx-dzdy-F_{y}dw^{2}-(F_{x}-G_{y})dwdz+G_{x}dz^{2},

(5)

while the operator
Q
defines a symmetric bivector corresponding to the conformal
structure.

Interpolating reductions

In our consideration of interpolating reductions we follow work
[
8
]
.
Let us introduce a formally adjoint Lax pair

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} -X_{1}^{*}&=\partial_{z}-\lambda\partial_{x}+F_{x}\partial_{x}+G_{x}\partial_{y}+f_{x}\partial_{\lambda}+(F_{xx}+G_{xy}),\\
-X_{2}^{*}&=\partial_{w}-\lambda\partial_{y}+F_{y}\partial_{x}+G_{y}\partial_{y}+f_{y}\partial_{\lambda}+(F_{xy}+G_{yy}),\end{aligned}

(6)

which is no longer represented by pure vector fields and contains terms with a multiplication
by the function (a divergence of the respective vector field). However, the compatibility conditions
remain the same and give system (
2
).
A simplest interpolating reduction is defined by the existence of a wave function of the form

\Psi^{*}=\exp(-\alpha\lambda)
for the adjoint operators,

\displaystyle X_{1}^{*}\exp(-\alpha\lambda)=0,\quad X_{2}^{*}\exp(-\alpha\lambda)=0.

(7)

For
\ln\Psi^{*}
we have nonhomogeneous linear equations

\displaystyle X_{1}\ln\Psi^{*}+(F_{xx}+G_{xy})=0,\quad X_{2}\ln\Psi^{*}+(F_{xy}+G_{yy})=0.

(8)

The reduction condition (
7
) implies the relation

\displaystyle\alpha f=F_{x}+G_{y},

(9)

which represents a differential reduction for SDCS system (
2
).
Under this condition system (
2
) reduces to the system of two
second order equations

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} Q(F)&=\alpha^{-1}(F_{xy}+G_{yy}),\\
Q(G)&=-\alpha^{-1}(F_{xx}+G_{xy}).\end{aligned}\right.

(10)

The limit of this system for
\alpha\rightarrow 0
gives the Dunajski system,
defined by the reduction condition
F_{x}+G_{y}=0
, and the limit
\alpha\rightarrow\infty

leads to linearly degenerate system with
f=0
. Thus, by analogy with the
interpolating system introduced in
[
9
]
,
we call system (
10
) the interpolating SDCS system.

Higher order interpolating reductions

The reduction condition for higher interpolating reductions is that
adjoint linear operators (
6
) admit a wave function
of the form
\exp(-\alpha(\lambda^{n}+P_{n-2}))
,
where
P_{n-2}
is a
\lambda
polynomial of degree
n-2
, in terms of nonhomogeneous
equations (
8
) we have a polynomial solution
-\alpha(\lambda^{n}+P_{n-2})
. This condition
defines a reduction for the whole hierarchy (see Section
4
). Second order interpolating reduction is represented by differential relations

\displaystyle(\partial_{z}+F_{x}\partial_{x}+G_{x}\partial_{y})f+\frac{1}{2\alpha}(F_{xx}+G_{xy})=0,

\displaystyle(\partial_{w}+F_{y}\partial_{x}+G_{y}\partial_{y})f+\frac{1}{2\alpha}(F_{xy}+G_{yy})=0.

(11)

A simple non-autonomous reduction

Let Lax pair (
1
) admit a wave function
x+\lambda z
(see
[
4
]
, also
[
10
]
).
For higher flows of the hierarchy this condition requires modification
and becomes more sophisticated (see Section
4
).
The reduction condition is equivalent to the relation

\displaystyle F=-zf.

(12)

Under this condition system (
2
) reduces to the system of two
second order equations for
f
and
G
. This reduction becomes rather evident if we rewrite
system (
2
) in the form

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} Q(F+zf)&=0,\\
Q(G+wf)&=0,\\
Q(f)&=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(13)

We can simultaneously require a second analogous reduction corresponding to existence
of the wave function
y+\lambda w
, leading to the condition

\displaystyle G=-wf.

(14)

Under two conditions (
12
), (
14
) system (
2
) reduces to one
secong order equation for
f
(see
[
10
]
),

\displaystyle f_{wx}-f_{zy}-zf_{y}f_{xx}+wf_{x}f_{yy}+(zf_{x}-wf_{y})f_{xy}=0

(15)

and conformal structure (
5
) takes the form

\displaystyle g=dwdx-dzdy+zf_{y}dw^{2}+(zf_{x}-wf_{y})dwdz-wf_{x}dz^{2},

(16)

If we consider the case of divergence-free vector fields (volume-preserving flows),
we also get an extra condition for
f
,

\displaystyle zf_{x}+wf_{y}=0,

(17)

which reduces equation (
15
) to the relation

\displaystyle wf_{w}+zf_{z}+f=0.

(18)

It is easy to check directly that solutions to both relations (
17
), (
18
)
(and thus to equation (
15
))
are given by the formula

\displaystyle f(x,z,y,w)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint R(x+\lambda z;y+\lambda w)d\lambda,

(19)

where
R
is an arbitrary analytic function of two complex variables
(
R(x+\lambda z;y+\lambda w)
should be
\lambda
analytic in the neighbourhood
of the integration contour). This formula can be obtained from the dressing scheme,
see Section
4
.
It defines a special self-dual conformal structure by expression (
16
).

Let us consider an example

\displaystyle R(x+\lambda z;y+\lambda w)=\sum_{i}\frac{\psi_{i}(y+\lambda w)}{x+\lambda z-b_{i}}+\sum_{i}\frac{\phi_{i}(x+\lambda z)}{y+\lambda w-c_{i}}

(20)

where
\psi_{i}(\lambda)
,
\phi_{i}(\lambda)
are arbitrary functions analytic
in the complex plane,
b_{i}
,
c_{i}
are arbitrary constants, and the contour of integration
in (
19
) is a circle of a big radius. Then from formula (
19
) we obtain

\displaystyle f=\sum_{i}z^{-1}\psi_{i}(z^{-1}(yz-xw+b_{i}w))+\sum_{i}w^{-1}\phi_{i}(w^{-1}(xw-yz+c_{i}z))

(21)

This expression defines a simple explicit example of
self-dual conformal structure (
16
) depending on arbitrary number of functions of
one variable. A more involved example is considered below in Section
4
.

Instead of divergence-free condition, we can complement a pair
of reductions (
12
), (
14
)
with other reduction. Let us consider first a second order interpolating reduction (
11
),
which leads to dimensional reduction for equation (
15
), defining its solutions through
solutions of two compatible three-dimensional equations

\displaystyle f_{z}-zf_{x}f_{x}-wf_{x}f_{y}-\frac{1}{2\alpha}(zf_{xx}+wf_{xy})=0,

\displaystyle f_{w}-zf_{y}f_{x}-wf_{y}f_{y}+\frac{1}{2\alpha}(zf_{xy}+wf_{yy})=0.

(22)

Another interesting opportunity to complement a pair of reductions (
12
), (
14
)
and perform a reduction to three-dimensional case is to consider a waterbag ansatz
for the wave function
\Psi
,

\displaystyle\Psi=\lambda+\sum_{i}c_{i}\ln(\lambda-u_{i}).

Substituting this ansatz to the Lax pair (
1
), we get

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} (\partial_{z}+F_{x}\partial_{x}+G_{x}\partial_{y})u_{i}-u_{i}\partial_{x}u_{i}-f_{x}=0\\
(\partial_{w}+F_{y}\partial_{x}+G_{y}\partial_{y})u_{i}-u_{i}\partial_{y}u_{i}-f_{y}=0\\
f=-\sum_{i}c_{i}u_{i}.\end{aligned}

Then, taking into account reductions (
12
), (
14
), we obtain two
closed systems of non-autonomous three-dimensional equations for the functions
u_{i}
,

\displaystyle(\partial_{z}-zf_{x}\partial_{x}-wf_{x}\partial_{y})u_{i}-u_{i}\partial_{x}u_{i}-f_{x}=0,

\displaystyle(\partial_{w}-zf_{y}\partial_{x}-wf_{y}\partial_{y})u_{i}-u_{i}\partial_{y}u_{i}-f_{y}=0,

\displaystyle f=-\sum_{i}c_{i}u_{i}.

A common solution of these systems defines a solution of system (
15
) and, respectively,
a self-dual conformal structure (
16
) by the formula
f=-\sum_{i}c_{i}u_{i}
.

Reflection type symmetries and nonlocal SDCS equations

Reflection type symmetries were introduced to integrable systems theory
rather recently
[
11
]

and have been extensively studied, especially for a number of systems connected
with the nonlinear Schrödinger equation. These symmetries lead to nonlocal systems
with reflection symmetry (P, T, PT type). For dispersionless integrable systems,
as far as the author knows, this type of symmetries was not yet considered. The reason is
probably that mostly systems with deep reductions, having the symplest structure, were
studied. However, the SDCS system possesses rich enough structure of reductios to
admit reflection type symmetry, leading to nonlocal equations.

Let us consider a
symmetry condition for SDCS system (
2
)

\displaystyle G=\widetilde{F},\quad f=\widetilde{f},\quad\widetilde{F}(x,z;y,w):=F(y,w;x,z).

(23)

We obtain a system of two nonlocal equations

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} Q(F)&=f_{y},\\
Q(f)&=0,\end{aligned}\right.

where linear second order differential operator
Q
is given by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} Q&=\partial_{w}\partial_{x}-\partial_{z}\partial_{y}+F_{y}{\partial_{x}}^{2}-\widetilde{F}_{x}{\partial_{y}}^{2}-(F_{x}-\widetilde{F}_{y})\partial_{x}\partial_{y}.\end{aligned}

and possesses a symmetry
\widetilde{Q}=-Q
.

Respectively, for B-type SDCS system (
25
) (see below) we have a system

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} Q(F)&=2(\partial_{w}+F_{y}\partial_{x}+\widetilde{F}_{y}\partial_{y})f-4ff_{y},\\
Q(f)&=0,\end{aligned}\right.

3
Type B SDCS system

Technically this section is close to work
[
12
]
,
where we introduced type B Manakov-Santini system.
Type B reduction of SDCS system hierarchy
in terms of vector fields is characterised
by the symmetry
X(-\lambda)=X(\lambda)
(see also Section
4
).
Lowest order Lax pair reads

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} X_{1}&=\partial_{z}-\lambda^{2}\partial_{x}+(F_{x}-2f)\partial_{x}+G_{x}\partial_{y}+\lambda f_{x}\partial_{\lambda},\\
X_{2}&=\partial_{w}-\lambda^{2}\partial_{y}+F_{y}\partial_{x}+(G_{y}-2f)\partial_{y}+\lambda f_{y}\partial_{\lambda},\end{aligned}

(24)

where for simplicity we use the same notations for times and coefficients
as in the Lax pair (
1
), though in the framework of the hierarchy
they correspond to different flows. Commutation relations for the Lax pair
(
24
) imply the system of equations

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} Q(F)&=2(\partial_{w}+F_{y}\partial_{x}+G_{y}\partial_{y})f-4ff_{y},\\
Q(G)&=-2(\partial_{z}+F_{x}\partial_{x}+G_{x}\partial_{y})f+4ff_{x},\\
Q(f)&=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(25)

where operator
Q
is of the same form as for the SDCS system (
3
),

\displaystyle Q=\partial_{w}\partial_{x}-\partial_{z}\partial_{y}+F_{y}{\partial_{x}}^{2}-G_{x}{\partial_{y}}^{2}-(F_{x}-G_{y})\partial_{x}\partial_{y}.

And, rather unexpectedly,
functions
F
,
G
satisfy the same system of third order
equations (
4
)
! It is possible to check it directly, and it will be rather clear
using the transformation from the B-type Lax pair (
24
) to the SDCS system
Lax pair (
1
) that we construct below. Thus, solutions of the
B-type SDCS system (
25
) also give a self-dual conformal structure
by formula (
5
).

Transformation to the SDSC system

Following the lines of the work
[
12
]
, let us perform
a change of the spectral variable in Lax pair (
24
)

\mu=\lambda^{2}+2f.

As a result, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} X^{\prime}_{1}&=\partial_{\widetilde{z}}-\mu\partial_{x}+F_{x}\partial_{x}+G_{x}\partial_{y}+2(f_{\widetilde{z}}-ff_{x}+F_{x}f_{x}+G_{x}f_{y})\partial_{\mu},\\
X^{\prime}_{2}&=\partial_{\widetilde{w}}-\mu\partial_{y}+F_{y}\partial_{x}+G_{y}\partial_{y}+2(f_{\widetilde{w}}-ff_{y}+F_{y}f_{x}+G_{y}f_{y})\partial_{\mu}.\end{aligned}

(26)

Compairing Lax pairs (
1
) and (
26
), we get
Miura trnsformation from solutions of B-type SDCS system (
25
) to solutions of
SDCS system (
2
)

\displaystyle F\rightarrow F,\quad G\rightarrow G,\quad f=\phi,

where potential
\phi
is defined through solutions of B-type SDCS system (
25
)
by the relations

\displaystyle\phi_{x}=2(f_{\widetilde{z}}-ff_{x}+F_{x}f_{x}+G_{x}f_{y}),

\displaystyle\phi_{y}=2(f_{\widetilde{w}}-ff_{y}+F_{y}f_{x}+G_{y}f_{y}).

On the level of the third order system (
4
) SDCS and type B SDCS systems
are identical.

Interpolating reduction for type B SDCS system

To introduce interpolating reductions, we consider a formal conjugation of Lax pair (
24
),

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} -X_{1}^{*}&=\partial_{z}-\lambda^{2}\partial_{x}+(F_{x}-2f)\partial_{x}+G_{x}\partial_{y}+\lambda f_{x}\partial_{\lambda}+(F_{xx}+G_{xy}-f_{x}),\\
-X_{2}^{*}&=\partial_{w}-\lambda^{2}\partial_{y}+F_{y}\partial_{x}+(G_{y}-2f)\partial_{y}+\lambda f_{y}\partial_{\lambda}+(F_{xy}+G_{yy}-f_{y}).\end{aligned}

A simplest interpolating reduction in this case is defined
by existence of the wave function

\Psi=\lambda^{\alpha}
for adjoint operators (see section
4
),

\displaystyle X_{1}^{*}\lambda^{\alpha}=0,\quad X_{2}^{*}\lambda^{\alpha}=0,

that implies

\displaystyle({1-\alpha})f=(F_{x}+G_{y}).

(27)

Under this condition system (
25
) reduces to the system of two second order
equations

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} Q(F)&=\beta(\partial_{w}+F_{y}\partial_{x}+G_{y}\partial_{y})(F_{x}+G_{y})-\beta^{2}(F_{x}+G_{y})(F_{xy}+G_{yy}),\\
Q(G)&=-\beta(\partial_{z}+F_{x}\partial_{x}+G_{x}\partial_{y})(F_{x}+G_{y})+\beta^{2}(F_{x}+G_{y})(F_{xx}+G_{yx}),\end{aligned}\right.

(28)

where
\beta=\frac{2}{1-\alpha}
.

Type B interpolating reduction in terms of the basic SDCS system

It is possible to characterise type B interpolating reduction leading to system
(
28
) in terms of the basic SDCS system and its Lax pair.
Assuming the existence of a wave function of the form
(\lambda+g)^{\gamma}
for the adjoint Lax pair (
6
), we obtain the relations

\displaystyle\gamma g=F_{x}+G_{y},

\displaystyle f_{x}=g_{z}-gg_{x}+F_{x}g_{x}+G_{x}g_{y},

\displaystyle f_{y}=g_{w}-gg_{y}+F_{y}g_{x}+G_{y}g_{y},

which reduce SDCS system (
2
) to type B interpolating system
(
28
),
\gamma=\beta^{-1}
.

4
The SDCS system hierarchy, the dressing scheme and reductions

To define the hierarchy,
we introduce three series
[
4
]
,
[
8
]

\displaystyle\Psi^{0}=\lambda+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Psi^{0}_{n}(\mathbf{t}^{1},\mathbf{t}^{2})\lambda^{-n},

(29)

\displaystyle\Psi^{1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{1}_{n}(\Psi^{0})^{n}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Psi^{1}_{n}(\mathbf{t}^{1},\mathbf{t}^{2})\lambda^{-n}

(30)

\displaystyle\Psi^{2}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{2}_{n}(\Psi^{0})^{n}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Psi^{2}_{n}(\mathbf{t}^{1},\mathbf{t}^{2})\lambda^{-n}.

(31)

where
\mathbf{t}^{1}=(t^{1}_{0},\dots,t^{1}_{n},\dots)
,
\mathbf{t}^{2}=(t^{2}_{0},\dots,t^{2}_{n},\dots)
.
The SDSC system hierarchy is defined by the generating relation

(J_{0}^{-1}\mathrm{d}\Psi^{0}\wedge\mathrm{d}\Psi^{1}\wedge\mathrm{d}\Psi^{2})_{-}=0,

(32)

where we use the projectors
(\sum_{-\infty}^{\infty}u_{n}\lambda^{n})_{+}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}u_{n}\lambda^{n}
,

(\sum_{-\infty}^{\infty}u_{n}\lambda^{n})_{-}=\sum_{-\infty}^{n=-1}u_{n}\lambda^{n}
,

J_{0}
is the determinant of the Jacobian matrix,

J_{0}=\begin{vmatrix}\Psi^{0}_{\lambda}&\Psi^{1}_{\lambda}&\Psi^{2}_{\lambda}\\
\Psi^{0}_{x}&\Psi^{1}_{x}&\Psi^{2}_{x}\\
\Psi^{0}_{y}&\Psi^{1}_{y}&\Psi^{2}_{y}\end{vmatrix},

(33)

where
x=t^{1}_{0}
,
y=t^{2}_{0}
.
Generating relation (
32
) implies
Lax-Sato equations of the hierarchy (see
[
4
]
,
[
8
]
)

\displaystyle\partial^{1}_{n}\mathbf{\Psi}=+\left(\frac{(\Psi^{0})^{n}}{J_{0}}\begin{vmatrix}\Psi^{0}_{\lambda}&\Psi^{2}_{\lambda}\\
\Psi^{0}_{y}&\Psi^{2}_{y}\end{vmatrix}\right)_{+}\partial_{x}\mathbf{\Psi}-\left(\frac{(\Psi^{0})^{n}}{J_{0}}\begin{vmatrix}\Psi^{0}_{\lambda}&\Psi^{2}_{\lambda}\\
\Psi^{0}_{x}&\Psi^{2}_{x}\end{vmatrix}\right)_{+}\partial_{y}\mathbf{\Psi}-

\displaystyle\left(\frac{(\Psi^{0})^{n}}{J_{0}}\begin{vmatrix}\Psi^{0}_{x}&\Psi^{2}_{x}\\
\Psi^{0}_{y}&\Psi^{2}_{y}\end{vmatrix}\right)_{+}\partial_{\lambda}\mathbf{\Psi},

(34)

\displaystyle\partial^{2}_{n}\mathbf{\Psi}=-\left(\frac{(\Psi^{0})^{n}}{J_{0}}\begin{vmatrix}\Psi^{0}_{\lambda}&\Psi^{1}_{\lambda}\\
\Psi^{0}_{y}&\Psi^{1}_{y}\end{vmatrix}\right)_{+}\partial_{x}\mathbf{\Psi}+\left(\frac{(\Psi^{0})^{n}}{J_{0}}\begin{vmatrix}\Psi^{0}_{\lambda}&\Psi^{1}_{\lambda}\\
\Psi^{0}_{x}&\Psi^{1}_{x}\end{vmatrix}\right)_{+}\partial_{y}\mathbf{\Psi}+

\displaystyle\left(\frac{(\Psi^{0})^{n}}{J_{0}}\begin{vmatrix}\Psi^{0}_{x}&\Psi^{1}_{x}\\
\Psi^{0}_{y}&\Psi^{1}_{y}\end{vmatrix}\right)_{+}\partial_{\lambda}\mathbf{\Psi},

(35)

where
\mathbf{\Psi}=(\Psi^{0},\Psi^{1},\Psi^{2})
,
\partial^{k}_{n}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{k}_{n}}
.
The first two flows of the hierarchy (
34
), (
35
)
read

\displaystyle\partial_{1}^{1}\mathbf{\Psi}=(\lambda\partial_{x}-F_{x}\partial_{x}-G_{x}\partial_{y}-f_{x}\partial_{\lambda})\mathbf{\Psi},

(36)

\displaystyle\partial_{1}^{2}\mathbf{\Psi}=(\lambda\partial_{y}-F_{y}\partial_{x}-G_{y}\partial_{y}-f_{y}\partial_{\lambda})\mathbf{\Psi},

(37)

here

G=\Psi^{2}_{1},\quad F=\Psi^{1}_{1},\quad f=\Psi^{0}_{1},

and after the identification
z=t^{1}_{1}
,
w=t^{2}_{1}
we obtain linear equations
for Lax pair (
1
) with
\Psi^{0},\Psi^{1},\Psi^{2}

playing a role of the basic wave functions.

The second flows can be represented in a form

\displaystyle\partial_{2}^{1}\mathbf{\Psi}=(\lambda\partial_{1}^{1}+f\partial_{x}-(\partial_{1}^{1}F)\partial_{x}-(\partial_{1}^{1}G)\partial_{y}-(\partial_{1}^{1}f)\partial_{\lambda})\mathbf{\Psi},

(38)

\displaystyle\partial_{2}^{2}\mathbf{\Psi}=(\lambda\partial_{1}^{2}+f\partial_{y}-(\partial_{1}^{2}F)\partial_{x}-(\partial_{1}^{2}G)\partial_{y}-(\partial_{1}^{2}f)\partial_{\lambda})\mathbf{\Psi}.

(39)

To write down vector fields more explicitly,
one should use first flows (
36
), (
37
).

The dressing scheme

Let us consider nonlinear vector Riemann problem of the form (see
[
4
]
)

\displaystyle\mathbf{\Psi}_{\text{out}}=\mathbf{R}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{\text{in}}),

(40)

or, more explicitely,

\displaystyle\Psi^{0}_{\text{out}}=R_{0}(\Psi^{0}_{\text{in}},\Psi^{1}_{\text{in}},\Psi^{2}_{\text{in}}),

\displaystyle\Psi^{1}_{\text{out}}=R_{1}(\Psi^{0}_{\text{in}},\Psi^{1}_{\text{in}},\Psi^{2}_{\text{in}}),

\displaystyle\Psi^{2}_{\text{out}}=R_{2}(\Psi^{0}_{\text{in}},\Psi^{1}_{\text{in}},\Psi^{2}_{\text{in}}),

where
\mathbf{\Psi}_{\text{out}}
,
\mathbf{\Psi}_{\text{in}}
denote the boundary values
inside and outside the unit circle (or other closed curve) in the complex plane.
The problem is to find a vector function analytic
outside the curve with some fixed singularity at infinity
which satisfies relation
(
40
). The set of functions
R_{0}
,
R_{1}
,
R_{2}
defines a complex
diffeomorphism connecting
the boundary values of the vector function
\mathbf{\Psi}

outside and inside the unit circle, and we call this set a dressing data.

To get solutions of the general hierarchy using the problem (
40

Reductions in terms of the hierarchy

Interpolating reduction

In terms of the SDSC system hierarchy interpolating reductions
are defined by the relation
[
8
]

\displaystyle J_{0}=\exp(\alpha(\Psi^{0})^{k}_{-}),

(41)

which is equivalent to the condition that
\Psi^{*}=\exp(-\alpha(\Psi^{0})^{k}_{+})
is a wave function
for adjoint linear operators of the hierarchy (
34

of wave functions of this form to define interpolating reductions (

for the SDCS system.

In terms of the dressing scheme interpolating reduction is given by a ‘twisted’ volume-preservation
condition
[

for the diffeomorphism
\mathbf{R}
(

\mathbf{f}\circ\mathbf{R}\circ\mathbf{f}^{-1}\in\text{SDiff(N+1)},

\displaystyle f_{0}(\mathbf{\Psi})=\Psi^{0},

\displaystyle f_{1}(\mathbf{\Psi})=\exp\left(-\frac{\alpha}{2}(\Psi^{0})^{k}\right)\Psi^{1},

\displaystyle f_{2}(\mathbf{\Psi})=\exp\left(-\frac{\alpha}{2}(\Psi^{0})^{k}\right)\Psi^{2},

For the type B SDCS system hierarchy
J_{0}

possesses a symmetry
J_{0}(-\lambda)=J_{0}(\lambda)
,
which is compatible with condition (

in this case it is also possible to define a more elementary interpolating reduction

\displaystyle J_{0}=\left(\frac{\Psi^{0}}{\lambda}\right)^{-\alpha},

(44)

which implies that
\Psi^{*}=\lambda^{\alpha}
is a wave function of adjoint linear operators
of the hierarchy, and for B type SDSC system we get a reduction condition (

Type B hierarchy

Type B SDCS system hierarchy is defined by the symmetry condition
for series (
29
), (
30
), (
31
),

\displaystyle\Psi^{0}(-\lambda)=-\Psi^{0}(\lambda),\quad\Psi^{1}(-\lambda)=\Psi^{1}(\lambda),\quad\Psi^{2}(-\lambda)=\Psi^{2}(\lambda),

which is compatible only with odd flows of the initial hierarchy,
and vector fields of the hierarchy possess the symmetry
X(-\lambda)=X(\lambda)
.
Lax pair (
24
)
corresponds to the symmetry reductions of the second flows (
38
), (
39
)
with the identification

z=t^{1}_{2}
,
w=t^{2}_{2}
.

In terms of the dressing scheme reduction of type B requires a symmetry

\displaystyle R_{0}(-\Psi^{0},\Psi^{1},\Psi^{2})=-R_{0}(\Psi^{0},\Psi^{1},\Psi^{2}),

\displaystyle R_{1}(-\Psi^{0},\Psi^{1},\Psi^{2})=R_{1}(\Psi^{0},\Psi^{1},\Psi^{2}),

\displaystyle R_{2}(-\Psi^{0},\Psi^{1},\Psi^{2})=R_{2}(\Psi^{0},\Psi^{1},\Psi^{2}).

Reflection type symmetries

In terms of the hierarchy reflection type symmetry (

is defined by the following conditions for series (

\displaystyle\Psi^{0}(\mathbf{t}^{1},\mathbf{t}^{2})=\Psi^{0}(\mathbf{t}^{2},\mathbf{t}^{1}),\quad\Psi^{2}(\mathbf{t}^{1},\mathbf{t}^{2})=\Psi^{1}(\mathbf{t}^{2},\mathbf{t}^{1}).

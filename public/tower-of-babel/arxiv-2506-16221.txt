Irreducible components of moduli spaces of maps to smooth projective toric varieties in genus 0
Alberto Cobos Rabano, Etienne Mann

Abstract
. We give a combinatorial description of the irreducible components of the moduli space ℳ ¯ 0 , n ​ ( X , β ) \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(X,\beta) for a smooth projective toric variety X X . The result is based on the study of the irreducible components of an abelian cone over a smooth Noetherian Artin stack. We give concrete applications of the result including ℳ ¯ 0 , 0 ​ ( Bl pt ​ ℙ 2 , 2 ​ ℓ ) \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,0}(\mathrm{Bl}_{\mathrm{pt}}\mathbb{P}^{2},2\ell) , where we also describe the main component. This is the first example where the smoothable locus of ℳ ¯ g , n ​ ( X , β ) \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta) is described for X X not a projective space.

For that, we consider the locus

Theorem A

(
Theorem

The irreducible components of

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\boldsymbol{G}}

1.2.
Strategy

In Notations
3.3.3
and
3.3.4
, we define
\mathfrak{P}=\mathfrak{Pic}^{st}_{0,n,X,\beta,\sigma}
, which is an open substack in a product of Picard stacks.
Following
[
CL12
]
, there is an open embedding
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(X,\beta)\hookrightarrow S
where
\pi\colon S=\mathrm{Spec}_{\mathfrak{P}}\mathrm{Sym}\,\mathfrak{F}\to\mathfrak{P}
(
Equation

36
) is the abelian cone associated to a coherent sheaf
\mathfrak{F}
on
\mathfrak{P}
.
Using this open embedding, we can understand the irreducible components of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(X,\beta)
if we understand those of a general abelian cone over a smoooth Noetherian Artin stack.

Let
\mathfrak{B}
be a smooth Noetherian Artin stack and
\mathfrak{F}
a coherent sheaf on
\mathfrak{B}
. Let
\{\mathfrak{D}_{i,j}\}_{(i,j)\in\Delta}
be a finite stratification of
\mathfrak{B}
by locally closed substacks such that
\mathrm{rk}(\mathfrak{F})=i
on
\mathfrak{D}_{i,j}
and let
\mathfrak{d}_{i,j}=i-\mathrm{codim}(\mathfrak{D}_{i,j})
, which controls the dimension of the preimage of
\mathfrak{D}_{i,j}
, see
Equation

4
.

Theorem D
.

(
Theorem

2.0.17
)
The irreducible components of the abelian cone
\pi\colon C_{\mathfrak{B}}(\mathfrak{F})\to\mathfrak{B}
are
\overline{\pi^{-1}(\mathfrak{D}_{i,j})}
such that
for all
(k,\ell)\in\Delta
with
\mathfrak{D}_{i,j}\subseteq\overline{\mathfrak{D}_{k,\ell}}
we have
\mathfrak{d}_{i,j}\geq\mathfrak{d}_{k,\ell}
.

D
is an immediate corollary of the analogous result for schemes,
Theorem

2.0.13
, which we prove under weaker assumptions. The main idea in the proof of
Theorem

2.0.13
is that the dimension of any component of the abelian cone is bounded by the dimension of the main component, which we prove in
Proposition

2.0.9
. We also use an elementary topological result,
Lemma

2.0.10
.

To prove
A
from
D
, we define in
Definition

3.3.5
the set
\Gamma^{st}_{0,n}(X,\beta)
consisting of trees decorated with curve classes at each node and satisfying a stability condition.
For
\boldsymbol{G}\in\Gamma^{st}_{0,n}(X,\beta)
, we let
\mathfrak{Pic}_{\boldsymbol{G}}
be the locus in
\mathfrak{P}
where the decorated dual graph, see
3.3.6
), is isomorphic to
\boldsymbol{G}
. Then the stratification
\{\mathfrak{Pic}_{\boldsymbol{G}}\}_{\boldsymbol{G}\in\Gamma^{st}_{0,n}(X,\beta)/_{\simeq}}
of
\mathfrak{P}
satisfies the assumptions of
D
, from which the irreducible decomposition of
S
in
Theorem

5.3.4
follows. Finally, intersecting with
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(X,\beta)
we obtain
A
.

1.3.
Related and future work

For
g\geq 1
, the moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)
has several irreducible components. If
d>2g-2
, the closure of the locus of stable maps with smooth domain curve is an irreducible component, known as the main component.
The description of the main component of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)
appeared in
[
VZ07
]
and was later generalized to all the components in
[
Vis12
]
, including a detailed example
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,0}(\mathbb{P}^{2},3)
. In genus two, the main component of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{2,0}(\mathbb{P}^{2},4)
has been described in
[
BC22
]
, as an application of the modular desingularization of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{2,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)
in
[
BC23
]
.
For a smooth projective toric target
X
, the space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
can already be reducible for
g=0
, see Theorems
B
and
C
.
In
Section

6.3
we describe the main component of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,0}(\mathrm{Bl}_{pt}\mathbb{P}^{2},2\ell)
. This is the first example where the target is not a projective space.

The reducibility of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)
in positive genus affects the structure of Gromov-Witten invariants of projective space. Numerous works
[
Zin09b
,
VZ08
,
HL10
,
RSPW19
,
HLN12
,
BC23
,
HN19
,
HN20
,
CRMMP24
]
define reduced Gromov-Witten invariants, which can be thought of as the contribution of the main component to the Gromov-Witten invariants. It is expected
[
Zin08
,
Zin09a
,
HL11
]
that the remaining irreducible components contribute in the form of lower genus invariants, leading to recursive formulas for reduced Gromov-Witten invariants. Such formulas are known in certain instances in genus 1 and 2
[
Zin09a
,
LZ07
,
LO19
,
LO20
,
LLO22
]
.
We would find it interesting to have a similar splitting of invariants among components for
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(X,\beta)
. For example, it may give a geometric proof of the Gromov-Witten/quasimap correspondence
[
MOP11
,
CJR21
,
CFK17
,
CFK20
,
Zho22
,
KMMP25
,
Man12
,
Man14
]
when combined with the contraction morphism between maps and quasimaps in
[
CR24
]
.

One key ingredient used in
[
LO19
,
LO20
,
LLO22
]
to study the reduced Gromov-Witten invariants of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)
in low genus is the local description of the moduli space obtained in
[
HL10
]
for genus 1 and in
[
HLN12
]
for genus 2. We would find it desirable to have a similar description for genus-0 stable maps to a smooth projective toric variety
X
. In fact, this paper is motivated by a talk by Hu on the series of papers
[
HL10
,
HLN12
,
HL11
,
HN19
,
HN20
]
during the program
Recent developments in higher genus curve counting
at the Simons Center for Geometry and Physics.

2.
Irreducible components of abelian cones

Let
B
be a Noetherian scheme and
\mathcal{F}
be a coherent sheaf on
B
. The abelian cone associated to
\mathcal{F}
is the scheme

C_{B}(\mathcal{F})=\mathrm{Spec}_{B}\mathrm{Sym}\,(\mathcal{F}).

We denote by
\pi:C_{B}(\mathcal{F})\to B
the natural projection.
Motivated by
[
Sta03
, Lemma 2.3]
, our goal is to understand the irreducible components of an abelian cone
C_{B}(\mathcal{F})
.

Remark 2.0.1
.

The irreducible components of
C_{B}(\mathcal{F})
and of its reduction
C_{B^{\mathrm{red}}}(\mathcal{F}\mid_{B^{\mathrm{red}}})
agree, thus we may assume that
B
is reduced. Furthermore, each irreducible component
Z
of
C_{B}(\mathcal{F})
is supported on an irreducible component
V
of
B
, thus it is also an irreducible component of

V\times_{B}C_{B}(\mathcal{F})=C_{V}(\mathcal{F}\mid_{V})

since abelian cones are compatible with pullback. Therefore, to study the irreducible components of
C_{B}(\mathcal{F})
we may assume without loss of generality that
B
is integral.

Notation 2.0.2
.

Let
B
be an integral Noetherian scheme and
\mathcal{F}
a coherent sheaf on
B
. We denote by
\mathrm{grk}_{B}(\mathcal{F})
the generic rank of
\mathcal{F}
on
B
, that is, the rank of
\mathcal{F}
at the generic point of
B
. We also consider the maximal rank of
\mathcal{F}
on
B

\mathrm{mrk}_{B}(\mathcal{F})=\max_{p\in B}\{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{F}\mid_{p})\},

that is, the maximum rank of
\mathcal{F}
on any closed point of
B
. Since
B
is Noetherian,
\mathrm{mrk}_{B}(\mathcal{F})
is finite.

The irreducible components of
C_{B}(\mathcal{F})
are controlled by the rank of
\mathcal{F}
, therefore it is useful to recall the Fitting ideals of
\mathcal{F}
and the corresponding stratification of
B
. See
[
Eis95
, Section 20.2]
or
[
Sta22
, 0C3C]
.

Definition 2.0.3
.

Let
M
be a finitely presented
R
-module. Let
F\xrightarrow{\varphi}G\to M\to 0
be a presentation with
F
and
G
free modules and
\mathrm{rk}(G)=g
. Given
-1\leq i<\infty
, the
i
-th Fitting ideal
F_{i}(M)
of
M
is the ideal generated by all
(g-i)\times(g-i)
-minors of the matrix associated to
\varphi
after fixing basis of
F
and
G
. We use the convention that
F_{i}(M)=R
if
g-i\leq 0
and
F_{-1}(M)=0
.

Fitting ideals commute with base change and their definition is independent of the chosen presentation. In particular, the local definition can be glued to define Fitting ideals for any finitely presented sheaf
\mathcal{G}
on a scheme
X
. By construction, there are inclusions

0=F_{-1}(\mathcal{G})\subset F_{0}(\mathcal{G})\subset\dots\subset F_{n}(\mathcal{G})\subset\dots\subset\mathcal{O}_{X},

which stabilize if
X
is Noetherian. We denote by
Z_{i}(\mathcal{G})
the closed subscheme cut out by the ideal sheaf
F_{i}(\mathcal{G})
. Then, the sheaf
\mathcal{G}
is locally free of rank
n
on the locally closed subscheme
Z_{n-1}(\mathcal{G})\setminus Z_{n}(\mathcal{G})
.

Assumption 2.0.4
.

From now on, we let
B
be an integral Noetherian scheme and
\mathcal{F}
be a coherent sheaf on
B
. We denote
b=\dim(B)
,
\mathrm{grk}_{B}(\mathcal{F})=f
and
\mathrm{mrk}_{B}(\mathcal{F})=m
.

Notation 2.0.5
.

Let
B,\mathcal{F}
as in
2.0.4
. Then the rank of
\mathcal{F}
at any point is in the interval
\{f,f+1,\ldots,m\}
.
For each
i\in\{0,\ldots,m-f\}
, we let

(1)

B_{i}=B_{i}(\mathcal{F})\coloneqq Z_{f+i-1}(\mathcal{F})\setminus Z_{f+i}(\mathcal{F}).

That is,
B_{i}
is the locus where
\mathcal{F}
has rank
f+i
.
We let

B_{i}=\bigcup_{j\in\Lambda_{i}}B_{i,j}

be the irreducible decomposition of
B_{i}
, with
\Lambda_{i}
a finite set. The irreducible components
B_{i,j}
are considered with their reduced induced structure. Note that
\Lambda_{0}
is a singleton by
2.0.4
.
We denote its unique element by
0
, thus
B_{0,0}=B_{0}
. We define the index set

(2)

\Lambda\coloneqq\{(i,j)\colon i\in\{0,\ldots,m-f\},j\in\Lambda_{i}\}.

For
(i,j)\in\Lambda
, we denote

(3)

d_{i,j}\coloneqq\mathrm{grk}_{B_{i,j}}(\mathcal{F}\mid_{B_{i,j}})-\mathrm{codim}_{B}(B_{i,j})=i-\mathrm{codim}_{B}(B_{i,j})

For each
(i,j)\in\Lambda
, the projection
\pi^{-1}(B_{i,j})\to B_{i,j}
is a vector bundle over an irreducible scheme, thus
\pi^{-1}(B_{i,j})
is irreducible and

(4)

\dim(\pi^{-1}(B_{i,j}))=\dim(B_{i,j})+\mathrm{rk}_{B_{i,j}}(\mathcal{F}\mid_{B_{i,j}})=b-\mathrm{codim}_{B}(B_{i})+f+i=b+f+d_{i,j}.

In particular,

(5)

d_{0,0}=0-\mathrm{codim}_{B}(B_{0,0})=0\Rightarrow\dim(\pi^{-1}(B_{0}))=b+f.

The closure of
\pi^{-1}(B_{0})
in
C_{B}(\mathcal{F})
will play an important role, so we give it a name after introducing notation for closures.

Notation 2.0.6
.

Let
X
be a scheme and let
Y\subseteq X
be a subscheme. We denote by
\mathrm{cl}_{X}(Y)
the closure of
Y
in
X
, with its reduced induced structure. If the ambient
X
is clear from the context, we write
\overline{Y}
for
\mathrm{cl}_{X}(Y)
.

Definition 2.0.7
.

Let
B,\mathcal{F}
as in
2.0.4
. We call

C^{\circ}_{B}(\mathcal{F})\coloneqq\overline{\pi^{-1}(B_{0})}=\mathrm{cl}_{C_{B}(\mathcal{F})}(\pi^{-1}(B_{0}))

the main (irreducible) component of
C_{B}(\mathcal{F})
. Irreducible components of
C_{B}(\mathcal{F})
different from the main component are called extra components.

Remark 2.0.8
.

It follows from
Theorem

2.0.13
that
C^{\circ}_{B}(\mathcal{F})
is an irreducible component of
C_{B}(\mathcal{F})
. Indeed, the only pair
(k,\ell)\in\Lambda
with
B_{0,0}\subset\overline{B_{k,\ell}}
is
(k,\ell)=(0,0)
.

For each
(i,j)\in\Lambda
, we can use

4
and
5
to reinterpret

as

d_{i,j}=\dim(\pi^{-1}(B_{i,j}))-\dim(C^{\circ}_{B}(\mathcal{F})).

Using the compatibility of abelian cones with pullback, we have that

and where

. In particular

If we prove that

we can conclude using

To prove

denote the residue field of

and tensor

to obtain an exact sequence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ker(\phi)\otimes_{R}k(P)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces k(P)^{g}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces k(P)^{h}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M\otimes k(P)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi\otimes k(P)}

implies that

Next we recall a topological lemma that will help us determine the structure of the irreducible components of

in

Lemma 2.0.10
.

Let

be a topological space and let

be a collection of mutually distinct irreducible closed subsets such that

X=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}.

Then the irreducible components of
X
are

(12)

\{V_{i}\colon i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},V_{i}\not\subseteq V_{j}\,\forall\,j\neq i\}.

Proof.

Let
Z
be an irreducible component of
X
. We first prove that
Z=V_{i}
for some
i
. We have that

Z=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}(Z\cap V_{i})=(Z\cap V_{1})\cup\left(\bigcup_{i=2}^{n}(Z\cap V_{i})\right)

with
Z\cap V_{i}
closed for all
i
. Thus, either
Z\subseteq Z\cap V_{1}
or
Z\subseteq\cup_{i=2}^{n}(Z\cap V_{i})
. Repeating this argument, we can find
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
such that
Z\subseteq Z\cap V_{i}
, that is,
Z\subseteq V_{i}
. This inclusion must be an equality because
Z
is an irreducible component and
V_{i}
is irreducible. Thus
Z=V_{i}
. Next, we prove that
V_{i}
lies in the set in
Equation

12
. By contradiction, if
V_{i}\subseteq V_{j}
for some
j\neq i
, then
V_{i}\subsetneqq V_{j}
by the assumption that
V_{i}
and
V_{j}
are mutually distinct. This contradicts the fact that
V_{i}=Z
is a component because
V_{j}
is irreducible.

Conversely, let
V_{i}
lie in the set in
Equation

12
. Since
V_{i}
is irreducible, it must be contained in an irreducible component

of

. By the previous part,

for some

. Thus
V_{i}\subseteq V_{j}
, which can only happen if

since

is assumed to be in the set in

. Thus

is an irreducible component.
∎

3.
Stratifications by dual graphs

We introduce various moduli spaces, such as prestable curves, the Picard stack and a product of Picard stacks. We also associate to them combinatorial data, in the form of trees with extra information, often called dual graphs.

3.1.
Prestable curves and the Picard stack

Notation 3.1.1
.

Given
g,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
d\in\mathbb{Z}
, we denote by

\mathfrak{M}_{g,n}

the stack of genus-
g
prestable
n
-marked curves and by

\mathfrak{Pic}_{g,n,d}

the Picard stack parametrizing tuples
(C,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n},L)
with

•

(C,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})
a
genus-
g
prestable
n
-marked curve and

•

L
a line bundle on
C
of total degree
d
.

We denote by

\pi\colon\mathfrak{C}\to\mathfrak{Pic}_{g,n,d}
the universal curve and by
\mathfrak{L}
the universal line bundle on
\mathfrak{C}
. There is a forgetful morphism
\mathfrak{Pic}_{g,n,d}\to\mathfrak{M}_{g,n}
.

We shall also use the notations
\mathfrak{M}_{g,M}
and
\mathfrak{Pic}_{g,M,d}
to denote the analogous stacks where the marking set
\{1,\ldots,n\}
is replaced by a finite set
M
.

Notation 3.1.2
.

We denote by

\mathfrak{Pic}_{g,n,d}^{sm}

the open substack of
\mathfrak{Pic}_{g,n,d}
where the curve is smooth and by

\mathfrak{Pic}_{g,n,d}^{st}

the open substack of
\mathfrak{Pic}_{g,n,d}
parametrizing tuples
(C,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n},L)
where

(17)

L^{\otimes 3}\otimes\omega_{\pi}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}\right)

is ample. The stacks
\mathfrak{P}_{g,M,d}^{sm}
and
\mathfrak{P}_{g,M,d}^{st}
are defined analogously.

The stack
\mathfrak{Pic}_{g,n,d}
is locally Noetherian, irreducible and smooth. Furthermore,
\mathfrak{Pic}_{g,n,d}^{st}
is Noetherian.

Remark 3.1.3
.

Note that, since
\mathfrak{Pic}_{0,n,d}
is integral, we have that
\mathfrak{Pic}^{st}_{0,n,d}
is non-empty if and only if
\mathfrak{Pic}^{sm}_{0,n,d}\subseteq\mathfrak{Pic}^{st}_{0,n,d}
. This inclusion holds if and only if

, see
Equation

18
.

Assumption 3.1.4
.

We assume that
3d+n-2>0
.

3.2.
Stratification by weighted dual graphs

In the particular case
g=0
, we attach combinatorial data, known as weighted dual graphs, to the elements in
\mathfrak{Pic}_{0,n,d}
. The loci with fixed dual graph provide a natural stratification of
\mathfrak{Pic}_{0,n,d}
.

Given a graph
G
, we denote the set of vertices (resp. edges) of
G
by
V(G)
(resp.
E(G)
). Given a vertex
v\in V(G)
, we write
\operatorname{val}(v)
for the valency of
v
. Later on we shall consider tuples
\boldsymbol{G}
of a graph
G
with extra information. By abuse of notation, we often write

for

.

Definition 3.2.1
.

An
n
-marked tree is a pair

\boldsymbol{G}=(G,m\colon\{1,\ldots,n\}\to V(G))

with
G
a tree. If
n
is fixed, we simply say that
\boldsymbol{G}
is a marked tree. We denote by
\Gamma_{0,n}
the set of
n
-marked trees. An isomorphism between two weighted
n
-marked trees
\boldsymbol{G}_{1},\boldsymbol{G}_{2}
is an isomorphism of the graphs
G_{1},G_{2}
which commutes with the morphisms
m_{i}
and
\deg_{i}
.

Definition 3.2.2
.

A weighted
n
-marked tree is a triple

\boldsymbol{G}=(G,m\colon\{1,\ldots,n\}\to V(G),\deg\colon V(G)\to\mathbb{Z})

where
(G,m)
is an
n
-marked tree. We say
\boldsymbol{G}
is
d
-weighted if
\sum_{v\in V(G)}\deg(v)=d
. The set of all
d
-weighted
n
-marked trees is denoted by
\Gamma_{0,n,d}
.

We say that a weighted marked tree
\boldsymbol{G}\in\Gamma_{0,n,d}
is stable if

(18)

3\deg(v)+\#m^{-1}(v)+\mathrm{val}(v)-2>0\ \text{ for every }v\in V(G).

We denote by
\Gamma_{0,n,d}^{st}
the set of all stable
d
-weighted
n
-marked trees.

Forgetting the degree map
\deg
induces a morphism
\Gamma_{0,n,d}\to\Gamma_{0,n}
.
Note that
\Gamma_{0,n,d}^{st}
is finite, while
\Gamma_{0,n,d}
, in general, is not.

Construction 3.2.3

(Dual graph)
.

To an element
(C,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})\in\mathfrak{M}_{0,n}
we attach the following
n
-marked tree
\boldsymbol{G}=(G,m)\in\Gamma_{0,n}
, called its dual graph. The tree

has a vertex

for each irreducible component

of

and an there is an edge

is determined by the condition

.

Construction 3.2.4

(Weighted dual graph)
.

Similarly, to an element
(C,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n},L)\in\mathfrak{Pic}_{0,n,d}

d
-weighted
n
-marked tree

is the dual graph of

Remark 3.2.5
.

Note that
(C,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n},L)\in\mathfrak{Pic}_{0,n,d}

For each

For each

4.2.

Let

In this section we recall some basic facts of the theory of limit linear series
[
EH86
,
Oss06
]
which help us understand the intersection of the extra components of

with

Let

parametrizes triples

on

. Thus, to describe

can be smoothed.

If

generates a 1-dimensional subspace of

on

of limit

are satisfied. Indeed, giving a limit

of

5.1.

Let

A

(2)

Proof.

By

By

, a point in

. We consider the triple
(C,L_{e},s_{3})

. Note that

and
\deg(L\mid_{C_{v}})=c(v)\cdot e
for each

.

5.3.1

On the other hand, the normalization sequence on
C
gives us

(51)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{0}(C,L)}

.

7.
Irreducible components and the contraction morphism

Fix
X,\beta
as in
3.3.1
. In general, there is no morphism
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)\to\mathcal{Q}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
extending the identity on the common locus where the source curve is smooth. However, by
[
CR24
, Section 5]
, there is a closed substack
\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{c}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
, containing the main component, together with a contraction morphism

c_{X}\colon\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{c}_{g,n}(X,\beta)\to\mathcal{Q}_{g,n}(X,\beta).

The morphism
c_{X}
is constructed using an epic closed embedding (see
[
CR24
, Def. 4.2.2]
), which always exists by
[
CR24
, Thm. 4.2.5]
.
Many basic questions about the locus
\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{c}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
remain unanswered.

We focus in the case
g=0
,
X=\mathrm{Bl}_{pt}\mathbb{P}^{2}
and
\beta=2\ell
. By
Section

5.5
, we need that
n\geq 2
, so we fix
n=2
. In this particular case, we describe all the irreducible components of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,2}(\mathrm{Bl}_{\mathrm{pt}}\mathbb{P}^{2},2\ell)
and
\mathcal{Q}_{0,2}(\mathrm{Bl}_{\mathrm{pt}}\mathbb{P}^{2},2\ell)
, explain where
c_{X}
maps each component and check that the locus
\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{c}_{0,2}(\mathrm{Bl}_{pt}\mathbb{P}^{2},2\ell)
is a union of irreducible components.

7.1.
Components of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,2}(\mathrm{Bl}_{\mathrm{pt}}\mathbb{P}^{2},2\ell)

Recall that we computed the irreducible components of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,0}(\mathrm{Bl}_{\mathrm{pt}}\mathbb{P}^{2},2\ell)
in
Section

6.3

2\ell
are
(2s,2e)
and
(s,s,2e)

The decorated trees

7.3.
The contraction morphism

Since
\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{c}_{0,2}(\mathrm{Bl}_{pt}\mathbb{P}^{2},2\ell)
is closed in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,2}(\mathrm{Bl}_{pt}\mathbb{P}^{2},2\ell)
, we have that for each
i\in\{0,\ldots,4\}
,

\mathcal{M}_{\boldsymbol{G}_{i}}\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{c}_{0,2}(\mathrm{Bl}_{pt}\mathbb{P}^{2},2\ell)\Rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\boldsymbol{G}_{i}}\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{c}_{0,2}(\mathrm{Bl}_{pt}\mathbb{P}^{2},2\ell)

Using
[
CR24
, Prop. 5.2.1]
, it is easy to check that
\mathcal{M}_{\boldsymbol{G}_{i}}\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{c}_{0,2}(\mathrm{Bl}_{pt}\mathbb{P}^{2},2\ell)
for
i=0,\ldots,3
. Thus

\bigcup_{i=0}^{3}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\boldsymbol{G}_{i}}\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{c}_{0,2}(\mathrm{Bl}_{pt}\mathbb{P}^{2},2\ell).

Furthermore,
c_{X}
must map irreducible components into irreducible components. We indicate this assignment of component with arrows in
Figure

10
. One can check it using
[
CR24
, Prop. 5.2.1]
. By the same reference,
\mathcal{M}_{\boldsymbol{G}_{4}}\not\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{c}_{0,2}(\mathrm{Bl}_{pt}\mathbb{P}^{2},2\ell)
. In fact, let
Q=(C,p_{1},p_{2},L_{s},L_{e},s_{0},\ldots,s_{3})\in\mathcal{M}_{\boldsymbol{G}_{4}}
, let
C_{2s}
denote the irreducible component with degree
2s
and
P
the node. Then, by
[
CR24
, Prop. 5.2.1]
,

Q\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{c}_{0,2}(\mathrm{Bl}_{pt}\mathbb{P}^{2},2\ell)\iff\mathrm{ord}\,_{P}(s_{3}\mid_{C_{2s}})\geq-2e\cdot e=2.

By our description of the smoothable locus in
Section

6.3.3
, we see that

\mathcal{M}_{\boldsymbol{G}_{4}}\cap\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{c}_{0,2}(\mathrm{Bl}_{pt}\mathbb{P}^{2},2\ell)\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\boldsymbol{G}_{0}},

thus

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\boldsymbol{G}_{4}}\cap\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{c}_{0,2}(\mathrm{Bl}_{pt}\mathbb{P}^{2},2\ell)\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\boldsymbol{G}_{0}}.

Thus, we obtain the following result.

The growth of linear perturbations in the interacting dark energy models and observational constraints
Kaiwen Liu 1 , Xiangyun Fu 1 , Bing Xu 2 , Chikun Ding 3 , Yang Huang 1 , and Xin Qing 1

Abstract
In this work, we investigate the influence of interaction between dark matter and dark energy on the growth rate f f of matter density perturbations within the framework of an interacting w w CDM model. The coupling term is assumed to be proportional to the dark energy density, and the growth rate f f is parameterized using the form Ω m γ \Omega_{\rm m}^{\gamma} . By considering the effect of this interaction on the growth index γ \gamma and assuming that γ \gamma varies with redshift z z , we derive the γ \gamma up to its second-order approximation. Our analysis demonstrates that the parameterized form Ω m γ \Omega_{\rm m}^{\gamma} closely approximates the actual growth rate f f . We utilize this parameterized form, along with the astronomic observations from Type Ia Supernovae, Baryon Acoustic Oscillations, Cosmic Microwave Background, Hubble parameter H ⁡ ( z ) H(z) measurements, and redshift space distortion (RSD) data, to make constraints on the free parameters of the interacting w w CDM model. Our findings indicate that incorporating RSD measurement data into cosmic background observations can significantly tighten the constraints on the coupling constant, suggesting a preference for a weak interaction between dark matter and dark energy.

IV.5
f\sigma_{8}
data

In real observations, the growth rate
f
is derived from observed data and describes how the actual observed matter density fluctuations evolve over time. The observed growth rates can be obtained by various methods, one of which commonly involves large-scale astronomical surveys, such as redshift space distortion (RSD). However, it is sensitive to the bias parameter
b
which can vary in the range
b\in[1,3]
. This sensitivity makes the observational
f_{\rm obs}
data unreliable
Nesseris2017
. On the other hand, combining of
f
with
\sigma_{8}
allows for independence from biases, thereby enhancing the reliability of the observational data. Here,
\sigma_{8}(z)
is defined as
\sigma_{8}(z)=\sigma_{8}(z=0)\delta(z)/\delta_{0}=\sigma_{8}\delta(z)/\delta_{0}
, where
\delta(z)/\delta_{0}
is the redshift-dependent Root Mean Square (RMS) fluctuations of the linear density field within spheres of radius
8h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}
, as described in Ref.
Nesseris2017
. The observational growth rate in the form of
f\sigma_{8}
data can be obtained from RSD measurements
Nesseris2017
;
Huang2022
;
Alam2016
;
Pinho2018
;
Howlett2017
. The expression of
\delta(z)/\delta_{0}
is given by

{\delta(z)\over\delta_{0}}=\exp(-\int_{0}^{z}{fd\tilde{z}\over 1+\tilde{z}})\,.

(31)

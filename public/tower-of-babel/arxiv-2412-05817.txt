Evolution of time-fractional stochastic hyperbolic diffusion equations on the unit sphere
Tareq Alodat, Quoc T. Le Gia

Abstract
This paper examines the temporal evolution of a two-stage stochastic model for spherical random fields. The model uses a time-fractional stochastic hyperbolic diffusion equation, which describes the evolution of spherical random fields on S 2 \mathbb{S}^{2} in time. The diffusion operator incorporates a time-fractional derivative in the Caputo sense. In the first stage of the model, a homogeneous problem is considered, with an isotropic Gaussian random field on S 2 \mathbb{S}^{2} serving as the initial condition. In the second stage, the model transitions to an inhomogeneous problem driven by a time-delayed Brownian motion on S 2 \mathbb{S}^{2} . The solution to the model is expressed through a series of real spherical harmonics. To obtain an approximation, the expansion of the solution is truncated at a certain degree L ≥ 1 L\geq 1 . The analysis of truncation errors reveals their convergence behavior, showing that convergence rates are affected by the decay of the angular power spectra of the driving noise and the initial condition. In addition, we investigate the sample properties of the stochastic solution, demonstrating that, under some conditions, there exists a local Hölder continuous modification of the solution. To illustrate the theoretical findings, numerical examples and simulations inspired by the cosmic microwave background (CMB) are presented.

In recent years, there has been a surge of interest in investigating the evolution of stochastic systems that involve random fields on the sphere (or spherical random fields), particularly those defined by stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs)
[

Spherical random fields find widespread applications in a variety of fields, including earth sciences (e.g.,

One stochastic system of particular interest, and the main focus of this work, is the time-fractional stochastic hyperbolic diffusion equation for spherical random fields (see
[

,
20
]
). In
[
27
]
, the authors considered the classical stochastic hyperbolic diffusion equation, which they defined as a homogeneous problem governed by ordinary partial derivatives in time and a fractional diffusion operator in space. This model included a random initial condition, represented by a strongly isotropic Gaussian random field on the unit sphere

. They derived an explicit stochastic solution and its approximation, both expressed as series expansions of elementary functions. Additionally, they established upper bounds for the convergence rates of the approximation errors and investigated various properties of the stochastic solution and its approximation.

In
[
20
]
, the authors considered a homogeneous problem defined by a hyperbolic diffusion equation governed by time-fractional derivatives and a fractional diffusion operator in space with a random initial condition. They then derived the stochastic solution to the equation, expressed as a series expansion of isotropic (in space) spherical random fields.

Although the models considered in
[
27
,
20
]
depict initial isotropic random fields evolving through hyperbolic diffusion processes, their structures incorporate only a single source of randomness, originating from the initial condition, with no external noise present at any time. Additionally,
[
20
]
exclusively presents the solution to the equation in the form of an infinite series. However, the necessity for an approximate solution becomes apparent when contemplating numerical implementations.
These research gaps motivate us to extend the previous works
[
20
,
27
]

Let
{{}^{C}}D_{t}^{\beta}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\dfrac{1}{\Gamma(m-\beta)}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\dfrac{1}{(t-s)^{1+\beta-m}}\dfrac{\partial^{m}}{\partial s^{m}}g(s)ds,&m-1<\beta<m,\\
\dfrac{\partial^{m}}{\partial t^{m}}g(t),&\beta=m,\end{cases}

(1.1)

The fractional integral of order

\displaystyle{{}_{a}}\mathcal{J}_{t}^{\beta}g(t):=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{a}^{t}(t-s)^{\beta-1}g(s)ds.

To establish the framework of the model presented in this paper, we introduce two key operators,

\bm{\mathcal{E}}:=\frac{1}{c^{2}}{{}^{C}}D_{t}^{2\alpha}+\frac{1}{\gamma}{{}^{C}}D_{t}^{\alpha}-k^{2}\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}

We investigate a time-fractional hyperbolic diffusion equation that incorporates a time-delayed Brownian motion

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{E}}U(\bm{x},t)dt=\begin{cases}0,&(\bm{x},t)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\times(0,\tau],\\
dW_{\tau}(\bm{x},t),&(\bm{x},t)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\times[\tau,\infty),\end{cases}

with the initial conditions

\displaystyle U(\bm{x},0)=\xi(\bm{x}),\ \frac{\partial}{\partial t}U(\bm{x},t)|_{t=0}=0,

where

,

It is known (see

The model given by equation (

Given that the random field

are uncorrelated, and taking into account the linearity of equation (

\displaystyle U:=U^{H}+U^{I},

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{E}}U^{H}(\bm{x},t)=0,\ (\bm{x},t)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\times(0,\infty),

\displaystyle U^{H}(\bm{x},0)=\xi(\bm{x}),\ \frac{\partial}{\partial t}U^{H}(\bm{x},t)|_{t=0}=0,

solves the inhomogeneous equation

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{E}}U^{I}(\bm{x},t)dt=\begin{cases}0,&(\bm{x},t)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\times(0,\tau],\\

Using properties of fractional integrals (

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{K}}U^{I}(\bm{x},t)-\dfrac{c^{2}}{\Gamma(2\alpha)}\int_{\tau}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha-1}dW_{\bm{x},\tau}(s)=0.

The structure of equation (

and the driving noise

. Due to the linearity of the system defined by (

We used the Laplace transform to derive the solution of the model. The derived solution is shown to satisfy the time-fractional equation by establishing a novel result (see Theorem

) that provides a rigorous foundation for applying fractional integrals to stochastic integrals.

The main result of this paper is Theorem

, to the system described by equation (

is given in the form of an infinite series in terms of real-valued orthonormal spherical harmonics

Given that the solution to equation (

an approximate solution, denoted

, for the solution

Section

Preliminaries

2.1
Spherical Functions

In this section, we present fundamental notation and results from the theory of functions on the unit sphere.

Consider the 3-dimensional Euclidean space, denoted as
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Let
\bm{x}
and
\bm{y}
be elements of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that
\bm{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
and
\bm{y}=(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})
. The inner product of
\bm{x}
and
\bm{y}
is given by
\bm{x}\cdot\bm{y}:=\sum_{i=1}^{3}x_{i}y_{i}
, and the Euclidean norm of
\bm{x}
is defined as
|\bm{x}|:=\sqrt{\bm{x}\cdot\bm{x}}
. Let
\mathbb{S}^{2}
be the 2-dimensional unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, i.e.,
\mathbb{S}^{2}:=\left\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:|\bm{x}|=1\right\}
. The pair
(\mathbb{S}^{2},\tilde{d})
forms a compact metric space, where
\tilde{d}
is the geodesic metric, defined as
\tilde{d}(\bm{x},\bm{y}):=\arccos(\bm{x}\cdot\bm{y})
for any
\bm{x},\bm{y}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
.

Let
\mu
be the normalized Riemann surface measure on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
(i.e.
\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}d\mu=1
). Let
L_{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2}):=L_{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2},\mu)
be the space of real-valued square
\mu
-integrable functions on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, i.e.,

L_{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})=\left\{f:\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(f(\bm{x}))^{2}d\mu(\bm{x})<\infty\right\}

with the inner product

\langle f,g\rangle_{L_{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}:=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}f(\bm{x})g(\bm{x})d\mu(\bm{x}),\quad f,g\in L_{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2}).

The norm of
f\in L_{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
is defined as

\|f\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}:=\sqrt{\langle f,f\rangle_{L_{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}}
.

Let
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\{0,1,2,\dots\}
and
\{Y_{\ell,m}:\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0},m=-\ell,\dots,\ell\}
be an orthonormal basis of real-valued spherical harmonics of
L_{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
. In terms of the spherical coordinates

\bm{x}(\theta,\varphi)=(\sin\theta\cos\varphi,\sin\theta\sin\varphi,\cos\theta)\in\mathbb{S}^{2},\quad\theta\in[0,\pi],\,\varphi\in[0,2\pi),

(2.1)

the real-valued spherical harmonic functions can be written as (see
[
34
]
), for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

\displaystyle Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\varphi):=\begin{cases}(-1)^{m}\sqrt{2}\sqrt{\dfrac{(2\ell+1)(\ell-|m|)!}{(\ell+|m|)!}}\,P_{\ell,|m|}(\cos\theta)\sin(|m|\varphi)&\text{if }m<0,\\
\sqrt{2\ell+1}\,P_{\ell,m}(\cos\theta)&\text{if }m=0,\\
(-1)^{m}\sqrt{2}\sqrt{\dfrac{(2\ell+1)(\ell-m)!}{(\ell+m)!}}\,P_{\ell,m}(\cos\theta)\cos(m\varphi)&\text{if }m>0,\end{cases}

where

P_{\ell,m}
,
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
m=0,\dots,\ell
, are the associated Legendre functions,
and
P_{\ell}
,
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
, are the Legendre polynomials of degree
\ell
.

In what follows, we adopt the notation
Y_{\ell,m}
to represent spherical harmonics as a function of both spherical and Euclidean coordinates. Specifically, for all
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
, we shall write (with a slight abuse of notation)

Y_{\ell,m}(\bm{x}):=Y_{\ell,m}(\theta,\varphi)
, where
\bm{x}=(\sin\theta\cos\varphi,\sin\theta\sin\varphi,\cos\theta)
and
(\theta,\varphi)\in[0,\pi]\times[0,2\pi).

The basis
Y_{\ell,m}
and the Legendre polynomial
P_{\ell}
satisfy the addition theorem (see
[
25
]
), i.e.,

\displaystyle\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}Y_{\ell,m}(\bm{x})Y_{\ell,m}(\bm{y})=(2\ell+1)P_{\ell}(\bm{x}\cdot\bm{y}),\quad\bm{x},\bm{y}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}.

(2.2)

Since the spherical harmonic functions
\{Y_{\ell,m}:\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0},m=-\ell,\dots,\ell\}
are the eigenfunctions of the negative Laplace-Beltrami operator
-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}
on the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}
with eigenvalues (see
[
25
,
6
]
)

\displaystyle\lambda_{\ell}:=\ell(\ell+1),\quad\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

(2.3)

we have
-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}Y_{\ell,m}=\lambda_{\ell}Y_{\ell,m},\quad\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0},m=-\ell,\dots,\ell.

Let
f\in L_{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
, then
f
can be expanded in terms of a Fourier-Laplace series, in the
L_{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
sense,

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\widehat{f}_{\ell,m}Y_{\ell,m},\ \text{with}\ \widehat{f}_{\ell,m}:=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}f(\bm{x})Y_{\ell,m}(\bm{x})d\mu(\bm{x}),

(2.4)

where
\widehat{f}_{\ell,m}
, for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0},m=-\ell,\dots,\ell
, are the Fourier coefficients for the function
f
under the Fourier basis
Y_{\ell,m}
. The series (
2.4
) along with Parseval’s theorem gives

\|f\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{2}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}(\widehat{f}_{\ell,m})^{2}.

2.2
Spherical isotropic random fields

This subsection introduces a class of random fields, namely isotropic Gaussian fields on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
. These fields can be characterized by an expansion using real spherical harmonics.

Throughout, we denote by
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
a probability space. Let
L_{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{P})
be the
L_{2}
-space on
\Omega
with the probability measure
\mathbb{P}
and equipped with the norm
|\cdot|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}
. For two random variables
X
and
Y
on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
, we denote by
\mathbb{E}[X]
the expected value of
X
and
Cov(X,Y):=\mathbb{E}[(X-\mathbb{E}[X])(Y-\mathbb{E}[Y])]
the covariance between
X
and
Y
. When two random variables
X
and
Y
have the same distribution, we shall use the notation
X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}Y
. If
X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}Y
,
Cov(X,X)
is the variance of
X
, denoted by
Var[X]
. Moreover,
\varphi_{X}(\cdot)
is the characteristic function of
X
and is defined as
\varphi_{X}(r):=\mathbb{E}\big[e^{\mathrm{i}rX}\big]
.

The Borel
\sigma
-algebra on the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}
is represented by
\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
, while
SO(3)
denotes the rotation group on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
A real-valued random field on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
is a jointly
\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
-measurable function
\xi:\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
. The real-valued
L_{2}
-space of random fields on
\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
, with the product measure
\mathbb{P}\otimes\mu
, is denoted by
L_{2}(\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{2},\mathbb{P}\otimes\mu)=:L_{2}(\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
. In this paper, we assume that the random field
\xi
belongs to
L_{2}(\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
. Using Fubini’s theorem, the inner product of
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in L_{2}(\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
can be expressed as:

\displaystyle\langle\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\rangle_{L_{2}(\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}:

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}\xi_{1}(\omega,\bm{x})\xi_{2}(\omega,\bm{x})d(\mathbb{P}\otimes\mu)(\omega,\bm{x})=\mathbb{E}\left[\langle\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\rangle_{L_{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\right].

The norm of a real-valued random field
\xi_{1}\in L_{2}(\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
is given by

\|\xi_{1}\|_{L_{2}(\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}:=\sqrt{\langle\xi_{1},\xi_{1}\rangle_{L_{2}(\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}}.

In particular,
\xi(\omega,\cdot)\in L_{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})

\mathbb{P}
-a.s., since

\|\xi\|_{L_{2}(\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{2}=\mathbb{E}\big[\|\xi\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{2}\big]
.
For brevity, we write
\xi(\omega,\bm{x})
as
\xi(\bm{x})
or
\xi(\omega)
if no confusion arises.

Definition 2.1

(
[
22
]
)
.

A random field
\xi
on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
is said to be strongly isotropic if, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
and for all sets of
k
points
\bm{x}_{1},\dots,\bm{x}_{k}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
, and for any rotation
\rho\in SO(3)
, the joint distributions of
\xi(\bm{x}_{1}),\dots,\xi(\bm{x}_{k})
and
\xi(\rho\bm{x}_{1}),\dots,\xi(\rho\bm{x}_{k})
coincide.

The random field
\xi
is called a
2
-weakly, isotropic random field if for all
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
, the second moment of
\xi(\bm{x})
is finite, that is
\mathbb{E}[|\xi(\bm{x})|^{2}]<\infty
, and for all
\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
, there holds

\mathbb{E}[\xi(\bm{x}_{1})]=\mathbb{E}[\xi(\rho\bm{x}_{1})],\ \mathbb{E}[\xi(\bm{x}_{1})\xi(\bm{x}_{2})]=\mathbb{E}[\xi(\rho\bm{x}_{1})\xi(\rho\bm{x}_{2})],

for any rotation
\rho\in SO(3)
.

Definition 2.2
.

A Gaussian random field on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
is a random field
\xi:\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
and every set of
k
points
\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{k}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
, the vector
(\xi(\mathbf{x}_{1}),\dots,\xi(\mathbf{x}_{k}))
has a multivariate Gaussian distribution.

We remark that a Gaussian 2-weakly isotropic field is also strongly isotropic (see
[
22
, Proposition 5.10]
). In this paper, we shall focus on Gaussian random fields that are strongly isotropic.

An arbitrary random field
\xi(\omega,\cdot)\in L_{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})

\mathbb{P}
-a.s. admits an expansion in terms of real spherical harmonics,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.,

\displaystyle\xi=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\widehat{\xi}_{\ell,m}Y_{\ell,m},\quad\widehat{\xi}_{\ell,m}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\xi(\bm{x})Y_{\ell,m}(\bm{x})d\mu(\bm{x}).

(2.5)

Remark 2.1
.

In
[
22
]
, the spectral representation of real-valued random fields relies on complex spherical harmonics. Since we adopt real spherical harmonics in our approach, the conditions on the random variables
\widehat{\xi}_{\ell,m}
are adjusted accordingly.

If
\xi
is a centered, 2-weakly isotropic Gaussian random field, by adapting the proof of

[
22
, Proposition 6.6]
, the Fourier coefficients
\widehat{\xi}_{\ell,m}
of
\xi
in (
2.5
) are uncorrelated mean-zero real-valued random variables, i.e., for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
m=-\ell,\dots,\ell
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\widehat{\xi}_{\ell,m}\right]=0,\quad\mathbb{E}\left[\widehat{\xi}_{\ell,m}\widehat{\xi}_{\ell^{\prime},m^{\prime}}\right]=\mathcal{C}_{\ell}\delta_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}\delta_{mm^{\prime}},

(2.6)

where
\delta_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}
is the Kronecker delta function and the elements of the sequence
\{\mathcal{C}_{\ell}:\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
are non-negative numbers, which is called the angular power spectrum of
\xi
. It follows that the field
\xi
is a centered random variable with the covariance function given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\xi(\bm{x})\xi(\bm{y})\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\mathcal{C}_{\ell}Y_{\ell,m}(\bm{x})Y_{\ell,m}(\bm{y})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}(2\ell+1)\mathcal{C}_{\ell}P_{\ell}(\bm{x}\cdot\bm{y}),\quad\bm{x},\bm{y}\in\mathbb{S}^{2},

(2.7)

where
P_{\ell}
are the Legendre polynomials. The last step follows from the addition theorem for spherical harmonics (
2.2
).

Assumption 2.1
.

We assume that the angular power spectrum of a centered,
2
-weakly isotropic random field
\xi
satisfies

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}(2\ell+1)\mathcal{C}_{\ell}<\infty.

(2.8)

Remark 2.2
.

Let
\xi
be a centered, strongly isotropic Gaussian random field defined on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
with angular power spectrum
\{\mathcal{C}_{\ell}:\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
satisfying the Assumption
2.1
. Let
\{Z_{\ell,m},\ \ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\ m=-\ell,\dots,\ell\}
be a set of independent, real-valued, standard normally distributed random variables, then the Karhunen–Loève expansion of
\xi
is

\displaystyle\xi

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{\ell}}Z_{\ell,m}Y_{\ell,m},

where the series is convergent in the
L_{2}(\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
sense.

2.3
Mittag-Leffler functions and their upper bounds

Let
\Gamma(\cdot)
be the gamma function and
\mathbb{C}
is the set of complex numbers. We introduce the three-parameter Mittag-Leffler function
E_{a,b}^{q}(z)
(see
[
15

{{}_{0}}\mathcal{J}_{t}^{\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}\Upsilon(t,r)dB(r)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\int_{0}^{t}{{}_{r}}\mathcal{J}_{t}^{\alpha}\Upsilon(t,r)dB(r).

Proof.

Using (

\displaystyle{{}_{0}}\mathcal{J}_{t}^{\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}\Upsilon(t,r)dB(r)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[U(\bm{x},t)U(\bm{y},t)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[U^{H}(\bm{x},t)U^{H}(\bm{y},t)\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[U^{I}(\bm{x},t)U^{I}(\bm{y},t)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}(2\ell+1)\left(\mathcal{C}_{\ell}(F_{\ell,\alpha}(t)^{2})+\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\sigma_{\ell,t-\tau,\alpha}^{2}\right)P_{\ell}(\bm{x}\cdot\bm{y}).

As
P_{\ell}(\bm{x}\cdot\bm{y})
depends only on the inner product of
\bm{x}
and
\bm{y}
, we conclude that the covariance function
\mathbb{E}\left[U^{I}(\bm{x},t)U^{I}(\bm{y},t)\right]
is rotationally invariant. Since
t>\tau
, we note that for
\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
m=-\ell,\dots,\ell
and
m^{\prime}=-\ell^{\prime},\dots,\ell^{\prime}
, by Propositions
3.2
and
3.4
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\widehat{U}_{\ell,m}(t)\overline{\widehat{U}_{\ell^{\prime},m^{\prime}}(t)}\right]=\big(\mathcal{C}_{\ell}|F_{\ell,\alpha}(t)|^{2}+\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\sigma_{\ell,t-\tau,\alpha}^{2}\big)\delta_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}\delta_{mm^{\prime}}.

Using the results of Propositions
3.2
and
3.4
we conclude that the field
U
is Gaussian, thus completing the proof.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

The solution
U
, as given in (
3.51
), can be expressed using (
3.28
) and (
3.50
) as

\displaystyle U(t)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\big(U^{H}_{\ell}(t)+U^{I}_{\ell}(t)\big),\quad t\in[0,\infty),

where
U^{H}_{\ell}
is defined as

\displaystyle U^{H}_{\ell}(t)

\displaystyle=F_{\ell,\alpha}(t)\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{\ell}}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}Z_{\ell,m}Y_{\ell,m},

(3.53)

and
U^{I}_{\ell}(t)=0
for
t\leq\tau
, while for
t>\tau
,

\displaystyle U^{I}_{\ell}(t)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\mathcal{A}_{\ell}}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\mathcal{I}_{\ell,|m|,\alpha}(t-\tau)Y_{\ell,m},

(3.54)

where
\mathcal{I}_{\ell,m,\alpha}(\cdot)
are given in (
2.58
).

3.4
Hölder continuity of the solution

This section derives continuity properties of the stochastic solution
U(t)
at given time
t
with respect to the geodesic distance on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, i.e.
\tilde{d}(\bm{x},\bm{y})
,
\bm{x},\bm{y}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
.

Notice that by Proposition
3.5
the stochastic solution
U
is known to be mean square continuous (see
[
23
]
). However, to obtain sample Hölder continuity of the stochastic solution
U
we need stronger assumptions on the angular power spectra
\mathcal{C}_{\ell}
and
\mathcal{A}_{\ell}
,
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

Assumption 3.2
.

The angular power spectra
\{\mathcal{C}_{\ell},\ \ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
and
\{\mathcal{A}_{\ell},\ \ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
satisfy (
3.14
) and (
3.43
) with
\kappa_{1}>2(1+\beta^{*})
and
\kappa_{2}>2(1+\frac{1}{\alpha}+\beta^{*})
for some
\beta^{*}\in(0,1]
.

Note that by Assumption
3.2
and similar steps for those of (
3.18
)-(
3.21
) and (
3.47
)-(
3.48
), we can show that there exist constants
K^{(1)}_{\beta^{*}},K^{(2)}_{\beta^{*}}>0
and such that

\displaystyle K^{(1)}_{\beta^{*}}:=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}(2\ell+1)\mathcal{C}_{\ell}|F_{\ell,\alpha}(t)|^{2}\lambda_{\ell}^{\beta^{*}}<\infty

(3.55)

and

\displaystyle K^{(2)}_{\beta^{*}}:=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}(2\ell+1)\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\sigma_{\ell,t-\tau,\alpha}^{2}\lambda_{\ell}^{\beta^{*}}<\infty.

(3.56)

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
U(t)
,
t\in[0,\infty)
, be the solution given by (
3.51
) to the equation (
1.3
), and the angular power spectra
\{\mathcal{C}_{\ell}\}
and
\{\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\}
satisfy the Assumption
3.2
. Then there exists a constant
K_{\beta^{*}}>0
such that

Var[U(\bm{x},t)-U(\bm{y},t)]\leq K_{\beta^{*}}\;(\tilde{d}(\bm{x},\bm{y}))^{2\beta^{*}},\quad\beta^{*}\in(0,1].

Proof.

Let
t\leq\tau
. Then by (
3.51
) and (
3.52
) there holds

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[U(\bm{x},t)U(\bm{y},t)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[U^{H}(\bm{x},t)U^{H}(\bm{y},t)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}(2\ell+1)\mathcal{C}_{\ell}|F_{\ell,\alpha}(t)|^{2}P_{\ell}(\bm{x}\cdot\bm{y}),

where
P_{\ell}(\cdot)
,
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, is the Legendre polynomial of degree
\ell
.

Thus for
t\leq\tau
there holds

\displaystyle Var[U(\bm{x},t)-U(\bm{y},t)]

\displaystyle=Var[U^{H}(\bm{x},t)]+Var[U^{H}(\bm{y},t)]

\displaystyle-2\mathbb{E}[U^{H}(\bm{x},t)U^{H}(\bm{y},t)]

\displaystyle=2\big(Var[U^{H}(\bm{x},t)]-\mathbb{E}[U^{H}(\bm{x},t)U^{H}(\bm{y},t)]\big)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}(2\ell+1)\mathcal{C}_{\ell}|F_{\ell,\alpha}(t)|^{2}(1-P_{\ell}(\bm{x}\cdot\bm{y})).

(3.57)

Since
P_{\ell}(1)=1
for all
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
P_{\ell}^{\prime}(x)\leq P_{\ell}^{\prime}(1)
for
x\in[-1,1]
, there holds

\displaystyle|1-P_{\ell}(x)|=\Big|\int_{x}^{1}P_{\ell}^{\prime}(y)dy\Big|\leq|1-x|\Big(\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{2}\Big),\quad x\in[-1,1].

(3.58)

Also, we observe that

\displaystyle|1-P_{\ell}(x)|\leq 2,\quad x\in[-1,1].

(3.59)

Then, for some
\beta^{*}\in(0,1]
we can write, using (
3.58
) and (
3.59
),

\displaystyle|1-P_{\ell}(x)|

\displaystyle=|1-P_{\ell}(x)|^{\beta^{*}}|1-P_{\ell}(x)|^{1-\beta^{*}}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(|1-x|\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{2}\Big)^{\beta^{*}}2^{1-\beta^{*}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{1-2\beta^{*}}|1-x|^{\beta^{*}}(\ell(\ell+1))^{\beta^{*}}.

(3.60)

Using the relation

|1-\cos r|=\Big|\int_{0}^{r}\sin xdx\Big|\leq r\sin r\leq r\Big|\int_{0}^{r}\cos xdx\Big|\leq r^{2}

with (
3.60
), (
3.57
) becomes bounded by

\displaystyle Var[U(\bm{x},t)-U(\bm{y},t)]

\displaystyle\leq 2^{2-2\beta^{*}}|1-\cos\tilde{d}(\bm{x},\bm{y})|^{\beta^{*}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}(2\ell+1)\mathcal{C}_{\ell}|F_{\ell,\alpha}(t)|^{2}\lambda_{\ell}^{\beta^{*}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{2-2\beta^{*}}(\tilde{d}(\bm{x},\bm{y}))^{2\beta^{*}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}(2\ell+1)\mathcal{C}_{\ell}|F_{\ell,\alpha}(t)|^{2}\lambda_{\ell}^{\beta^{*}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{2-\beta^{*}}(\tilde{d}(\bm{x},\bm{y}))^{2\beta^{*}}K^{(1)}_{\beta^{*}},

Similarly, for
t>\tau
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[U(\bm{x},t)U(\bm{y},t)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}(2\ell+1)\left(\mathcal{C}_{\ell}|F_{\ell,\alpha}(t)|^{2}+\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\sigma_{\ell,t-\tau,\alpha}^{2}\right)P_{\ell}(\bm{x}\cdot\bm{y})

and by Assumption
3.2
and the upper bound (
3.60
) we obtain

\displaystyle Var[U(\bm{x},t)-U(\bm{y},t)]

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}(2\ell+1)\left(\mathcal{C}_{\ell}|F_{\ell,\alpha}(t)|^{2}+\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\sigma_{\ell,t-\tau,\alpha}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\times(1-P_{\ell}(\bm{x}\cdot\bm{y}))

\displaystyle\leq 2^{2-2\beta^{*}}(\tilde{d}(\bm{x},\bm{y}))^{2\beta^{*}}

\displaystyle\times\Big(\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}(2\ell+1)\mathcal{C}_{\ell}|F_{\ell,\alpha}(t)|^{2}\lambda_{\ell}^{\beta^{*}}+\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}(2\ell+1)\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\sigma_{\ell,t-\tau,\alpha}^{2}\lambda_{\ell}^{\beta^{*}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{2-2\beta^{*}}(\tilde{d}(\bm{x},\bm{y}))^{2\beta^{*}}\big(K^{(1)}_{\beta^{*}}+K^{(2)}_{\beta^{*}}\big).

Thus for
t\geq 0
, there exists a constant
K_{\beta^{*}}
such that

Var[U(\bm{x},t)-U(\bm{y},t)]\leq K_{\beta^{*}}\;(\tilde{d}(\bm{x},\bm{y}))^{2\beta^{*}},

where

\displaystyle K_{\beta^{*}}:=2^{2-2\beta^{*}}\begin{cases}K^{(1)}_{\beta^{*}},&t\leq\tau,\\
K^{(1)}_{\beta^{*}}+K^{(2)}_{\beta^{*}},&t>\tau,\end{cases}

which completes the proof.
∎

Given our observations, we are now ready to prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
U(t)
,
t\in[0,\infty)
, be the solution given by (
3.51
) to the equation (
1.3
), and the angular power spectra
\{\mathcal{C}_{\ell}\}
and
\{\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\}
satisfy the Assumption
3.2
, then there exists a continuous modification of
U
that is Hölder continuous with exponent
\gamma^{*}\in(0,\beta^{*})
.

Proof.

Under the assumptions of Lemma
3.5
, applying Kolmogorov’s continuity criterion (see
[
17
, Corollary 4.5]
), for any fixed
t>0
, we conclude that there exists a locally Hölder continuous modification for
U
of order
\gamma^{*}\in(0,\beta^{*})
. Thus the proof is complete.
∎

4
Approximation to the solution

This section provides an approximation to the solution
U
by truncating its expansion at a degree
(truncation level)
L\in\mathbb{N}
. Then we give an upper bound for the approximation error of the truncated solution
U_{L}
.

Definition 4.1
.

The approximation
U_{L}
of degree
L\in\mathbb{N}
to the solution
U
given in
(
3.51
)
is defined as

\displaystyle U_{L}(t):

\displaystyle=U_{L}^{H}(t)+U_{L}^{I}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{L}\Big(U^{H}_{\ell}(t)+U^{I}_{\ell}(t)\Big),\quad t\in[0,\infty),

(4.1)

where
U^{H}_{\ell}(t)
is given by (
3.53
)
and for
t\leq\tau
,
U^{I}_{\ell}(t)=0
, while for
t>\tau
,
U^{I}_{\ell}(t)
is given by (
3.54
).

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\{\mathcal{C}_{\ell}:\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
, the angular power spectrum of
\xi
, satisfy (
3.14
). Let
Q^{H}_{L}(t)
,
t\in[0,\infty)
, be defined as

\displaystyle Q^{H}_{L}(t):

\displaystyle=\Big\|U^{H}(t)-U_{L}^{H}(t)\Big\|_{L_{2}(\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}=\Big\|\sum_{\ell=L+1}^{\infty}U^{H}_{\ell}(t)\Big\|_{L_{2}(\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{2})},

where
U^{H}_{\ell}
is given by (
3.53
). Then, for
L>\max\{\varkappa,\ell_{0}\},\ \ell_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
we have

Q^{H}_{L}(t)\leq\mathcal{H}(t)\widetilde{C}_{\kappa_{1}}(1)L^{-\kappa_{1}/2},\quad t\geq 0,

where
\widetilde{C}_{\kappa_{1}}(\cdot)
is given by (
3.15
) and
\mathcal{H}(\cdot)
defined by (
2.41
).

Proof.

By (
3.53
) we can write

\displaystyle(Q^{H}_{L}(t))^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\Big\|\sum_{\ell=L+1}^{\infty}\Big[\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{\ell}}F_{\ell,\alpha}(t)\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}Z_{\ell,m}Y_{\ell,m}\Big]\Big\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{2}\Bigg].

Since
\{Z_{\ell,m},\ \ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\ m=-\ell,\dots,\ell\}
is a set of independent, real-valued, standard normally distributed random variables, we have

\displaystyle(Q^{H}_{L}(t))^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=L+1}^{\infty}|F_{\ell,\alpha}(t)|^{2}\mathcal{C}_{\ell}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\mathbb{E}\left[\big(Z_{\ell,m}\big)^{2}\right]\|Y_{\ell,m}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=L+1}^{\infty}|F_{\ell,\alpha}(t)|^{2}\mathcal{C}_{\ell}(2\ell+1),

where the last step used the property

\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\|Y_{\ell,m}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{2}=(2\ell+1)P_{\ell}(1)=(2\ell+1).

Using (
2.40
) we obtain

\displaystyle(Q^{H}_{L}(t))^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=L+1}^{\infty}|F_{\ell,\alpha}(t)|^{2}(2\ell+1)\mathcal{C}_{\ell}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell=L+1}^{\infty}(2\ell+1)\dfrac{|\mathcal{H}(t)|^{2}}{\lambda_{\ell}}\mathcal{C}_{\ell}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{|\mathcal{H}(t)|^{2}}{\lambda_{L}}\sum_{\ell=L+1}^{\infty}(2\ell+1)\mathcal{C}_{\ell}.

(4.2)

Using (
3.14
) then (
4.2
) becomes bounded by

\displaystyle(Q^{H}_{L}(t))^{2}

\displaystyle\leq|\mathcal{H}(t)|^{2}L^{-2}\sum_{\ell=L+1}^{\infty}(2\ell+1)\mathcal{C}_{\ell}

\displaystyle\leq|\mathcal{H}(t)|^{2}L^{-2}\widetilde{C}\int_{L}^{\infty}(2x+1)x^{-\kappa_{1}}dx

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{H}(t)|^{2}L^{-2}\widetilde{C}\bigg(\dfrac{2}{\kappa_{1}-2}+\dfrac{L^{-1}}{\kappa_{1}-1}\bigg)L^{2-\kappa_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq|\mathcal{H}(t)|^{2}(\widetilde{C}_{\kappa_{1}}(1))^{2}L^{-\kappa_{1}},

(4.3)

which completes the proof.
∎

The following proposition provides an upper bound for the approximation errors of the truncated solution
U_{L}^{I}(t)
,
t>\tau
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\{\mathcal{A}_{\ell}:\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
, the angular power spectrum of
W_{\tau}
, satisfy (
3.43
). Let
Q^{I}_{L}(t)
,
t>\tau
, be defined as

\displaystyle Q^{I}_{L}(t):

\displaystyle=\Big\|U^{I}(t)-U_{L}^{I}(t)\Big\|_{L_{2}(\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}=\Big\|\sum_{\ell=L+1}^{\infty}U^{I}_{\ell}(t)\Big\|_{L_{2}(\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{2})},

(4.4)

where for
t>\tau
,
U^{I}_{\ell}(t)
is given by (
3.54
). Then, for
L>\max\{\varkappa,\ell_{0}\},\ \ell_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

Q^{I}_{L}(t)\leq e_{\kappa_{2}}L^{-(\kappa_{2}-\frac{2}{\alpha})/2},

where
e_{\kappa_{2}}=2^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2\alpha}}\sqrt{C_{3}^{I}}\widetilde{A}_{\kappa_{2}}(1)
.

Proof.

Using (
3.54
) then
(Q^{I}_{L}(t))^{2}
can be written for
t>\tau
as

\displaystyle(Q^{I}_{L}(t))^{2}=\Big\|\sum_{\ell=L+1}^{\infty}U_{\ell}^{I}(t)\Big\|_{L_{2}(\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{2}=\Big\|\sum_{\ell=L+1}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\sqrt{\mathcal{A}_{\ell}}\mathcal{I}_{\ell,|m|,\alpha}(t-\tau)Y_{\ell,m}\Big\|_{L_{2}(\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{2}.

By Proposition
2.6
, there holds

\displaystyle(Q^{I}_{L}(t))^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=L+1}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\mathbb{E}\Big[\big(\mathcal{I}_{\ell,|m|,\alpha}(t-\tau)\big)^{2}\Big]\|Y_{\ell,m}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=L+1}^{\infty}\sigma_{\ell,t-\tau,\alpha}^{2}\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\|Y_{\ell,m}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=L+1}^{\infty}\mathcal{A}_{\ell}(2\ell+1)\sigma_{\ell,t-\tau,\alpha}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}^{I}\sum_{\ell=L+1}^{\infty}\mathcal{A}_{\ell}(2\ell+1)\lambda_{\ell}^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}},

(4.5)

where the last step uses (
2.46
).

Now similar to (
4.3
), the upper bound (
4.5
) can be bounded by

\displaystyle(Q^{I}_{L}(t))^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}C_{3}^{I}\sum_{\ell=L+1}^{\infty}\mathcal{A}_{\ell}(2\ell+1)\ell^{\frac{2-2\alpha}{\alpha}}\leq 2^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}C_{3}^{I}(\widetilde{A}_{\kappa_{2}}(1))^{2}L^{-(\kappa_{2}-\frac{2}{\alpha})},

which completes the proof.
∎

The following theorem can be established by combining the results from Propositions
4.1
and
4.2
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
U(t)
,
t\in[0,\infty)
, be the solution (
3.51
) to the equation (
1.3
), and the conditions of Propositions
4.1
and
4.2
be satisfied. Let
\tilde{\kappa}_{\alpha}:=\min(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}-\frac{2}{\alpha})
. Let
Q_{L}(t)
,
t\in[0,\infty)
, be defined as

\displaystyle Q_{L}(t):=\Big\|U(t)-U_{L}(t)\Big\|_{L_{2}(\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{2})},\quad L\geq 1,

(4.6)

where
U_{L}
is given by (
4.1
).
Then, for
L>\max\{\varkappa,\ell_{0}\},\ \ell_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, the following estimate holds true:

\displaystyle Q_{L}(t)\leq\left((\mathcal{H}(t)\widetilde{C}_{\kappa_{1}}(1))^{2}+e_{\kappa_{2}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}L^{-\tilde{\kappa}_{\alpha}/2}.

\theta=10^{-6}

\bm{y}_{k}

\bm{y}_{k}

\bm{x}^{*}

100

U_{L}^{I}

MERTENS PRODUCTS IN ARITHMETIC PROGRESSIONS over function fields
Hwanyup Jung

Abstract
. We establish a function–field analogue of Mertens’ formula for Euler products restricted to primes in arithmetic progressions over the polynomial ring 𝔽 q ​ [ t ] \mathbb{F}_{q}[t] . Our results are in direct correspondence with those of Languasco and Zaccagnini for arithmetic progressions in the integers. Over function fields, Weil’s Riemann hypothesis for Dirichlet L L –functions holds unconditionally, and consequently the analogue of the “GRH–strength” asymptotic is obtained without any exceptional–zero correction term.

11N37, 11R58, 11N60

1.
Introduction

Mertens’ theorem describes the size of the partial Euler product of the Riemann zeta function at
s=1
:

\prod_{p\leq x}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1}\sim e^{\gamma}\log x\quad(x\to\infty).

Beyond the leading term, it is natural to study quantitative remainder terms
and uniformity questions.
Classical refinements were investigated by A. Vinogradov
[
6
,
7
]
,
and the problem of obtaining sharp or explicit inequalities remains central
in analytic number theory.

A further direction is to restrict the Euler product to primes satisfying
arithmetic conditions.
One of the most fundamental examples is restriction to primes in an
arithmetic progression.
For
(a,q)=1
, it is convenient to introduce the notation

P(x;q,a):=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\equiv a\bmod q\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right),

and to study the asymptotic behaviour of
P(x;q,a)
as
x\to\infty
,
uniformly with respect to the modulus
q
and the residue class
a
.
This problem was studied by Williams
[
8
]
,
Vasil’kovskaja
[
5
]
,
and later by Bordellès
[
1
]
.
A particularly systematic and refined treatment is due to
Languasco and Zaccagnini
[
2
]
.
In particular, they showed that
P(x;q,a)
admits an asymptotic formula
of the shape

P(x;q,a)=C(q,a)\,(\log x)^{-1/\varphi(q)}\left(1+o(1)\right),

uniformly for
q
in a wide range, and they made explicit how the remainder
term may depend on the possible existence of an exceptional (Siegel) zero.
Under the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis, their results take a particularly
clean form, with a power–saving error term and no exceptional correction
factor (see
[
2
, Theorem 4]
).

We now turn to the function–field setting.
Let
\mathbb{A}=\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
be the polynomial ring over the finite field

\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
We denote by
\mathcal{M}
the set of monic polynomials in

\mathbb{A}
, and by
\mathcal{P}
the set of monic irreducible
polynomials.
For a polynomial
f\in\mathbb{A}
, we write
|f|=q^{\deg f}
for its norm.
For any subset
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{A}
and any integer
n\geq 0
,
we define

\mathcal{A}_{n}:=\{f\in\mathcal{A}:\deg f=n\},\quad\mathcal{A}_{\leq n}:=\{f\in\mathcal{A}:\deg f\leq n\},\quad\mathcal{A}_{>n}:=\{f\in\mathcal{A}:\deg f>n\}.

In particular, we write
\mathcal{M}_{n}
,
\mathcal{M}_{\leq n},\mathcal{M}_{>n}
and

\mathcal{P}_{n}
,
\mathcal{P}_{\leq n},\mathcal{P}_{>n}
for the corresponding sets of
monic polynomials and monic irreducible polynomials.

In this context, an analogue of the classical Mertens theorem is already
available.
Indeed, Rosen established a generalization of Mertens’ formula for global
function fields, showing in particular that the partial Euler product over
all prime polynomials satisfies

(1.1)

\displaystyle\prod_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{\leq n}}\left(1-|P|^{-1}\right)=\frac{\kappa_{q}}{n\log q}\left(1+O_{q}\!\left(q^{-n/2}\right)\right),\quad(n\to\infty),

for a positive constant
\kappa_{q}
depending only on
q

(see
[
3
]
and also
[
4
, Chapter 5]
).
This result plays the role of the classical Mertens theorem in the
function–field setting and provides the global baseline for our work.

A decisive simplification in the function–field setting is that Dirichlet

L
–functions satisfy the Riemann hypothesis unconditionally, as proved by
Weil.
Consequently, no analogue of the exceptional (Siegel) zero phenomenon can
occur, and the function–field counterpart of the “GRH–strength”
asymptotic in the integer setting holds without any additional hypotheses.

Motivated by the arithmetic progression problem in the integers and by
Rosen’s global result, we develop in this paper a direct function–field
analogue of the results of Languasco and Zaccagnini.
Let
Q\in\mathcal{M}
and let
A_{0}\bmod Q
be a reduced residue
class.
For an integer
n\geq 1
, we consider the finite Euler product

P(n;Q,A_{0}):=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathcal{P}_{\leq n}\\
P\equiv A_{0}\bmod Q\end{subarray}}\left(1-|P|^{-1}\right).

This product is the function–field counterpart of
P(x;q,a)
in the integer
setting and constitutes the main object of study in this paper.
The main purpose of this paper is to establish an asymptotic formula for
this finite Euler product
{P}(n;Q,A_{0})
as
n\to\infty
, uniformly with respect to the
modulus
Q
and the residue class
A_{0}
, in a range where
\deg Q
is
allowed to grow with
n
.
More precisely, we show that the above product admits a Mertens–type
asymptotic with an explicit main term and a power–saving error term,
which is the direct function–field analogue of the results of
Languasco and Zaccagnini in the integer setting.
Thanks to the unconditional validity of the Riemann hypothesis for
Dirichlet
L
–functions over function fields, our result requires no
exceptional–zero correction and holds without any unproved hypotheses.
In particular, the result obtained here may be viewed as the exact
function–field counterpart of the “GRH–strength” form of the
arithmetic progression Mertens theorem in
[
2
]
.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section 2 we state the main theorem of the paper, which gives a
Mertens–type asymptotic formula for Euler products over prime
polynomials in arithmetic progressions.
Section 3 is devoted to the analytic preliminaries required for the
proof, including a character decomposition of the restricted Euler
product, square–root cancellation for prime polynomial sums via
Dirichlet
L
–functions over
\mathbb{A}=\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
, and uniform tail estimates for
twisted Euler products.
In Section 4 we combine these ingredients to complete the proof of the
main theorem.
Finally, in the Appendix we give an explicit Euler product expression
for the constant
C(Q,A_{0})
appearing in the main term and discuss its
relation to the corresponding constant in the integer setting.

2.
Statement of the Main Theorem

Throughout this paper, all polynomials are assumed to be monic unless
otherwise stated.
Let
Q\in\mathcal{M}
be a fixed one with
\deg Q\geq 1
, and let

A_{0}\bmod Q
be a reduced residue class, that is,
(A_{0},Q)=1
.
We denote by

\Phi(Q):=|(\mathbb{A}/Q\mathbb{A})^{\times}|

the Euler totient function in the function–field setting.

For an integer
n\geq 1
, recall that we consider the finite Euler product

(2.1)

P(n;Q,A_{0})=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathcal{P}_{\leq n}\\
P\equiv A_{0}\bmod Q\end{subarray}}\left(1-|P|^{-1}\right).

which is the function–field analogue of the classical Mertens product
restricted to primes in an arithmetic progression.

We are now ready to state the main result of this paper, which gives a
Mertens–type asymptotic formula for
P(n;Q,A_{0})
, uniformly with respect
to growing moduli
Q
.

Theorem 2.1

(Mertens’ formula in arithmetic progressions)
.

Fix
0<\eta<1
.
Uniformly for all monic moduli
Q
satisfying
\deg Q\leq\eta n
and all
reduced residue classes
A_{0}\bmod Q
, we have

P(n;Q,A_{0})=C(Q,A_{0})\,(n\log q)^{-1/\Phi(Q)}\left(1+O_{q,\eta}\!\left(q^{-n/2}\right)\right),

as
n\to\infty
,
where
C(Q,A_{0})>0
is a constant depending only on the modulus
Q
and the
residue class
A_{0}
.

Remark 2.2
.

The positive constant
C(Q,A_{0})
appearing in Theorem
2.1
is given by an
explicit Euler product depending only on the arithmetic data of the
modulus
Q
and the residue class
A_{0}
.
Since the precise shape of this constant is not needed in the proof of
the main asymptotic formula, we defer its explicit description and some
additional remarks to Appendix
A
.

Remark 2.3
.

Theorem
2.1
is the function–field analogue of the
“GRH–strength” form of the arithmetic progression Mertens theorem
proved by Languasco and Zaccagnini
[
2
]
.
In contrast with the integer setting, the result here holds
unconditionally and requires no exceptional–zero correction.

3.
Analytic Preliminaries

In this section we collect the analytic tools required for the proof of
Theorem
2.1
.
These include a character decomposition of the restricted Euler product,
properties of Dirichlet
L
–functions over
\mathbb{A}=\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
and the resulting
square–root cancellation for prime polynomial sums, as well as uniform
tail estimates for twisted Euler products.
All results in this section are preparatory and will be combined in
Section
4
to complete the proof of the main theorem.

3.1.
Character decomposition

Let
\chi
range over Dirichlet characters modulo
Q
,
extended by
\chi(F)=0
when
(F,Q)\neq 1
.
Let
\chi_{0}
denote the principal character.
We begin by expressing the arithmetic progression condition on prime
polynomials in terms of Dirichlet characters modulo
Q
.
This decomposition allows us to separate the contribution of the
principal character, which gives rise to the main term, from the
nonprincipal characters, whose contributions will be shown to be
negligible using analytic estimates developed in the subsequent
subsections.

Lemma 3.1

(Orthogonality)
.

For any
F\in A
with
(F,Q)=1
, we have

\mathbf{1}_{F\equiv A_{0}\bmod Q}=\frac{1}{\Phi(Q)}\sum_{\chi\bmod Q}\chi(F)\,\overline{\chi}(A_{0}).

Taking logarithms of
{P}(n;Q,A_{0})
and using orthogonality reduces
the problem to estimating twisted Euler products.

3.2.
Dirichlet
L
–Functions and Square–Root Cancellation

For a Dirichlet character
\chi
modulo
Q
, the associated Dirichlet

L
–function is defined by

(3.1)

L(u,\chi)=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{M}}\chi(F)u^{\deg F}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathcal{P}\\
P\nmid Q\end{subarray}}\left(1-\chi(P)u^{\deg P}\right)^{-1}.

If
\chi
is nonprincipal, then
L(u,\chi)
is in fact a polynomial of
degree at most
\deg Q-1
.
Moreover, by Weil’s Riemann hypothesis for function fields, all of its
reciprocal zeros
\alpha
satisfy
|\alpha|=q^{1/2}
.
As a consequence, we obtain strong cancellation in character sums
over prime polynomials, which will be exploited below.

Define

\Theta(n,\chi):=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{\leq n}}\chi(P)\,\deg P.

We now establish a bound for prime polynomial sums twisted by
nonprincipal Dirichlet characters.
This estimate is the function–field analogue of the classical bounds
for twisted prime sums under GRH in the integer setting and provides
the main analytic input for controlling nonprincipal character
contributions.

Lemma 3.2
.

Fix
0<\eta<1
.
Let
\chi
be a nonprincipal Dirichlet character modulo
Q
.
Then

\Theta(n,\chi)=O_{q,\eta}\!\left(q^{n/2}\right),

uniformly for
\deg Q\leq\eta n
.

Proof.

Taking the logarithmic derivative of both sides of (
3.1
), we obtain

-\frac{L^{\prime}(u,\chi)}{L(u,\chi)}=\sum_{m\geq 1}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathcal{P}\\
\deg P\mid m\end{subarray}}\chi(P)\deg P\right)u^{m}.

Comparing coefficients and applying standard Möbius inversion arguments,
we find that

\Theta(n,\chi)=-\sum_{\alpha}\alpha^{n}+O\!\left(q^{n/3}\right),

where the sum runs over the reciprocal zeros
\alpha
of
L(u,\chi)
.
(The error term arises from the contribution of prime powers

P^{k}
with
k\geq 2
, whose total contribution is negligible.)

Since the number of reciprocal zeros is
O(\deg Q)
, we obtain

\Theta(n,\chi)=O((\deg Q)q^{n/2}).

Under the assumption
\deg Q\leq\eta n
, the factor
\deg Q
is absorbed
into the implied constant.
∎

3.3.
Tail estimates for twisted Euler products

The next step is to control the tail of the Euler product twisted by a
nonprincipal character.
Using the square–root cancellation from Lemma
3.2
and partial
summation in the degree aspect, we obtain a uniform bound for the
contribution of prime polynomials of degree exceeding
n
.
This estimate will allow us to approximate finite Euler products by
their infinite counterparts in the proof of the main theorem.

Lemma 3.3
.

Fix
0<\eta<1
.
Let
\chi
be a nonprincipal Dirichlet character modulo
Q
.
Then

\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{>n}}\frac{\chi(P)}{|P|}=O_{q,\eta}\!\left(\frac{q^{-n/2}}{n}\right),

uniformly for
\deg Q\leq\eta n
.

Proof.

Set

S(n):=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{>n}}\frac{\chi(P)}{|P|}=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{>n}}\chi(P)\,q^{-\deg P}.

Grouping prime polynomials by degree, we obtain

(3.2)

S(n)=\sum_{k>n}q^{-k}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{k}}\chi(P).

Since

(3.3)

\Theta(k,\chi)-\Theta(k-1,\chi)=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{k}}\chi(P)\deg P=k\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{k}}\chi(P),

we have

\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{k}}\chi(P)=\frac{\Theta(k,\chi)-\Theta(k-1,\chi)}{k}.

Substituting this identity into (
3.2
), we obtain

(3.4)

S(n)=\sum_{k>n}\bigl(\Theta(k,\chi)-\Theta(k-1,\chi)\bigr)\,b_{k},

where
b_{k}:=q^{-k}/k
.
We now apply summation by parts to (
3.4
).
For any
M>n
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=n+1}^{M}\bigl(\Theta(k,\chi)-\Theta(k-1,\chi)\bigr)\,b_{k}

\displaystyle=\Theta(M,\chi)\,b_{M}-\Theta(n,\chi)\,b_{n+1}

(3.5)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k=n+1}^{M-1}\Theta(k,\chi)\,(b_{k}-b_{k+1}).

Letting
M\to\infty
, we note that
b_{M}=q^{-M}/M\to 0
exponentially.
Moreover, by Lemma
3.2
we have

\Theta(M,\chi)=O_{q,\eta}\!\left(q^{M/2}\right),

so that
\Theta(M,\chi)b_{M}=O_{q,\eta}(q^{-M/2}/M)\to 0
.
Hence (
3.3
) yields

(3.6)

S(n)=-\Theta(n,\chi)\,b_{n+1}+\sum_{k\geq n+1}\Theta(k,\chi)\,(b_{k}-b_{k+1}).

We estimate the two terms on the right-hand side of (
3.6
).
By Lemma
3.2
and the definition of
b_{n+1}
, we have

|\Theta(n,\chi)\,b_{n+1}|\ll_{q,\eta}q^{n/2}\cdot\frac{q^{-(n+1)}}{n+1}\ll_{q}\frac{q^{-n/2}}{n}.

A direct computation shows that

b_{k}-b_{k+1}=\frac{q^{-k}}{k}-\frac{q^{-(k+1)}}{k+1}=q^{-k}\Bigl(\frac{1}{k}-\frac{1}{q(k+1)}\Bigr)\ll_{q}\frac{q^{-k}}{k}.

Therefore, using Lemma
3.2
again,

\sum_{k\geq n+1}|\Theta(k,\chi)|\,|b_{k}-b_{k+1}|\ll_{q,\eta}\sum_{k\geq n+1}q^{k/2}\cdot\frac{q^{-k}}{k}=\sum_{k\geq n+1}\frac{q^{-k/2}}{k}.

The latter sum is dominated by its first term, and we obtain

\sum_{k\geq n+1}\frac{q^{-k/2}}{k}\ll_{q}\frac{q^{-n/2}}{n}.

Combining the estimates for the boundary term and the main sum in
(
3.6
), we conclude that

\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{>n}}\frac{\chi(P)}{|P|}=O_{q,\eta}\!\left(\frac{q^{-n/2}}{n}\right),

uniformly for
\deg Q\leq\eta n
.
This completes the proof.
∎

4.
Proof of the Main Theorem

We are now in a position to prove Theorem
2.1
.
We combine the character decomposition from Section
3.1
with the
square–root cancellation results for Dirichlet
L
–functions established
in Section
3.2
and the tail estimates from Section
3.3
to evaluate the
restricted Euler product and complete the argument.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

We begin by taking logarithms on both sides of the defining identity
(
2.1
), which yields

(4.1)

\log P(n;Q,A_{0})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathcal{P}_{\leq n}\\
P\equiv A_{0}\bmod Q\end{subarray}}\log\left(1-|P|^{-1}\right).

Applying the orthogonality relation for Dirichlet characters modulo
Q

(Lemma
3.1
) to (
4.1
), we obtain

(4.2)

\log P(n;Q,A_{0})=\frac{1}{\Phi(Q)}\sum_{\chi\bmod Q}\overline{\chi}(A_{0})\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{\leq n}}\chi(P)\log\left(1-|P|^{-1}\right).

We first isolate the contribution of the principal character
\chi_{0}
.
In this case, the inner sum in (
4.2
) reduces to the
unrestricted Euler product.
By Rosen’s Mertens theorem over function fields (
1.1
), we have

\prod_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{\leq n}}\left(1-|P|^{-1}\right)=\frac{\kappa_{q}}{n\log q}\left(1+O(q^{-n/2})\right),

and hence

(4.3)

\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{\leq n}}\log\!\left(1-|P|^{-1}\right)=-\log(n\log q)+\log\kappa_{q}+O(q^{-n/2}).

We now turn to the contribution of nonprincipal characters
\chi\neq\chi_{0}
.
By Lemma
3.3
,

\prod_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{\leq n}}\left(1-\frac{\chi(P)}{|P|}\right)=\frac{1}{L(q^{-1},\chi)}\left(1+O_{q,\eta}\!\left(\frac{q^{-n/2}}{n}\right)\right),

uniformly for
\deg Q\leq\eta n
.
Taking logarithms on both sides and using the expansion

\log(1-z)=-z+O(z^{2})
together with Lemma
3.3
,
we obtain

(4.4)

\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{\leq n}}\chi(P)\log\!\left(1-|P|^{-1}\right)=-\log L(q^{-1},\chi)+O_{q,\eta}\!\left(\frac{q^{-n/2}}{n}\right).

Substituting (
4.3
) and (
4.4
)
into (
4.2
), we obtain

\log P(n;Q,A_{0})=-\frac{1}{\Phi(Q)}\log(n\log q)+\log C(Q,A_{0})+O_{q,\eta}\!\left(\frac{q^{-n/2}}{n}\right),

where

\log C(Q,A_{0})=\frac{1}{\Phi(Q)}\log\kappa_{q}-\frac{1}{\Phi(Q)}\sum_{\chi\neq\chi_{0}}\overline{\chi}(A_{0})\log L(q^{-1},\chi).

Exponentiating the previous expression, we obtain

P(n;Q,A_{0})=C(Q,A_{0})\,(n\log q)^{-1/\Phi(Q)}\left(1+O_{q,\eta}\!\left(\frac{q^{-n/2}}{n}\right)\right).

Since the main theorem concerns the limit
n\to\infty
with
n\geq 1
,
the additional factor
1/n
in the error term is negligible compared
with the exponential decay
q^{-n/2}
.
Consequently, the remainder term may be harmlessly absorbed into

O_{q,\eta}(q^{-n/2})
, yielding the asymptotic formula stated in
Theorem
2.1
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.1

(On the shape of the error term)
.

In the course of the proof of Theorem
2.1
, the contribution of nonprincipal characters
is estimated by means of partial Euler products and the explicit formula,
leading naturally to an error term of size

O_{q,\eta}\!\left(\frac{q^{-n/2}}{n}\right).

This factor
1/n
arises from summation by parts in degree and reflects the
finite–range truncation of the Euler product.

However, since the main theorem concerns the limit
n\to\infty
with
n\geq 1
,
the factor
1/n
is negligible compared with the exponential decay
q^{-n/2}
.
Consequently, the remainder term

O_{q,\eta}\!\left(\frac{q^{-n/2}}{n}\right)

may be harmlessly absorbed into

O_{q,\eta}\!\left(q^{-n/2}\right),

which we adopt in the statement of Theorem
2.1
.
This convention is consistent with the presentation in the integer setting,
where under GRH the remainder is commonly written as
O(x^{-1/2})

(cf.
[
2
]
), even though intermediate steps may involve additional
logarithmic factors.

Appendix A
The constant
C(Q,A_{0})

In this appendix we describe the positive constant
C(Q,A_{0})
appearing
in Theorem
2.1
.
As mentioned in Remark
2.2
, this constant admits an explicit
representation as an Euler product depending only on the arithmetic
data of the modulus
Q
and the residue class
A_{0}
.
Although the precise shape of
C(Q,A_{0})
plays no role in the proof of
the main asymptotic formula, it is of independent interest and provides
additional structural insight into the arithmetic progression Mertens
product.

Proposition A.1

(Euler product expression)
.

We have

(A.1)

C(Q,A_{0})^{\Phi(Q)}=\kappa_{q}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathcal{P}\\
P\mid Q\end{subarray}}\left(1-|P|^{-1}\right)^{-1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathcal{P}\\
(P,Q)=1\end{subarray}}\left(1-|P|^{-1}\right)^{\alpha(P;Q,A_{0})},

where
\kappa_{q}
denotes the global Mertens constant appearing in
(
1.1
), and for
(P,Q)=1
the exponent
\alpha(P;Q,A_{0})
is defined by

\alpha(P;Q,A_{0})=\begin{cases}\Phi(Q)-1,&\text{if }P\equiv A_{0}\bmod Q,\\
-1,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

Starting from the logarithmic identity for the nonprincipal character contribution
appearing after taking logarithms and applying character orthogonality
in the proof of Theorem
2.1
, we have

\log C(Q,A_{0})=\frac{1}{\Phi(Q)}\log\kappa_{q}-\frac{1}{\Phi(Q)}\sum_{\chi\neq\chi_{0}}\overline{\chi}(A_{0})\log L(q^{-1},\chi).

Using the Euler product representation

L(q^{-1},\chi)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathcal{P}\\
(P,Q)=1\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{\chi(P)}{|P|}\right)^{-1},

and expanding logarithms, we may write

\frac{1}{\Phi(Q)}\sum_{\chi\neq\chi_{0}}\overline{\chi}(A_{0})\,\log L(q^{-1},\chi)=-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathcal{P}\\
(P,Q)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\Phi(Q)}\Bigl(\sum_{\chi\neq\chi_{0}}\overline{\chi}(A_{0})\chi(P)\Bigr)\log\bigl(1-|P|^{-1}\bigr).

By character orthogonality (Lemma
3.1
),

\sum_{\chi\neq\chi_{0}}\overline{\chi}(A_{0})\chi(P)=\begin{cases}\Phi(Q)-1,&P\equiv A_{0}\bmod Q,\\
-1,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

so exponentiating gives the product over
(P,Q)=1
with exponent
\alpha(P;Q,A_{0})
.
Finally, the finitely many primes
P\mid Q
contribute the factor

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathcal{P}\\
P\mid Q\end{subarray}}(1-|P|^{-1})^{-1},

thus yielding (
A.1
).
∎

Remark A.2
.

The Euler factors corresponding to primes
P\mid Q
are finitely many.
Since
(A_{0},Q)=1
, such primes never satisfy
P\equiv A_{0}\bmod Q
,
and hence contribute with exponent
-1
.
These local factors are therefore recorded explicitly in
(
A.1
) as a finite correction and do not affect the
global nature of the constant
\kappa_{q}
.

Remark A.3

(Comparison with the integer case)
.

Formula (
A.1
) is the exact function–field analogue of
the constant appearing in
[
2
]
.
The only structural difference is that in the present setting no
exceptional zero phenomenon can occur, so the Euler product requires no
correction factor.

This completes the explicit description of the constant
C(Q,A_{0})

appearing in Theorem
2.1
.

Conclusion

In this paper we established a function–field analogue of the Mertens product
in arithmetic progressions over
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
, in direct correspondence with the
results of Languasco and Zaccagnini in the integer setting.
The proof was deliberately organized so that each analytic step in the
integer case admits a transparent counterpart over function fields, with the
Riemann hypothesis for Dirichlet
L
–functions replaced by Weil’s theorem.
As a consequence, the analogue of the “GRH–strength” asymptotic holds
unconditionally and without any exceptional–zero correction.

Although we have restricted our attention here to congruence conditions
modulo a fixed polynomial
Q
, the method readily suggests further extensions.
In particular, we may replace arithmetic progressions by more general
splitting conditions in finite Galois extensions of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)
and consider
Mertens products over primes whose Frobenius conjugacy class lies in a prescribed
subset of the Galois group.
Such a Chebotarev–type formulation would again rely on explicit formulas and
square–root cancellation for the associated Artin
L
–functions, which are
available unconditionally in the function–field setting.
We hope to return to these extensions in future work.

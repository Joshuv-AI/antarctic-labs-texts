Clinical impact of continuing osimertinib beyond progression versus best supportive care in EGFR-mutated advanced non-small cell lung cancer: a multi-institutional retrospective study
Authors: Issei Oi, Kohei Fujita, Takuma Imakita, Yuta Okada, Saiki Yoshimura, Naoki Fujimoto, Shogo Toyama, Atsuko Watanabe, Takanori Ito, Ryosuke Kaku, Osamu Kanai, Masatsugu Ohuchi, Tetsuo Noguchi, Mitsuhiro Tsukino, Satoru Sawai, Kiminobu Tanizawa
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe optimal management of patients with epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR)-mutated non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC) after progression on osimertinib includes cytotoxic chemotherapy. Continuing osimertinib therapy beyond progressive disease (PD) is occasionally adopted in real-world practice; however, evidence for its efficacy is limited. In this multi-institutional retrospective study, we evaluated whether osimertinib continuation beyond PD could improve survival compared with the best supportive care (BSC) in such patients.MethodsWe retrospectively reviewed patients with EGFR-mutated advanced or recurrent NSCLC treated with osimertinib between March 2016 and September 2025 at three Japanese community hospitals. Patients with radiological progression or intolerable adverse events after osimertinib treatment were included Patients were classified into three groups: (1) osimertinib beyond PD, (2) BSC, and (3) chemotherapy after osimertinib. Overall survival (OS) was compared among the groups using Kaplan–Meier and Cox regression analyses adjusted for time-to-treatment failure (TTF).ResultsOf the 309 patients treated with osimertinib, 126 met the inclusion criteria. The median age of the patients was 77 years, and 23% had poor performance status (≥ 2). Osimertinib beyond PD was administered to 21 patients (16.7%), BSC to 36 patients (28.6%), and chemotherapy to 69 patients (54.8%). The median OS after osimertinib failure was 8.8, 2.6, and 15.5 months in the beyond PD, BSC, and chemotherapy groups, respectively (p < 0.001). In multivariate analysis, beyond PD was associated with significantly longer OS than BSC (hazard ratio: 0.34, 95% confidence interval: 0.18–0.66, p < 0.001), even after adjusting for TTF.ConclusionsIn real-world settings involving elderly and frail patients, osimertinib continuation beyond PD was associated with improved survival compared with BSC alone. Although chemotherapy remains the preferred strategy when feasible, osimertinib continuation beyond PD may represent a reasonable treatment option for patients who are ineligible for further cytotoxic therapy. In the absence of other guiding biomarkers, TTF may help clinicians identify patients most likely to benefit from continued TKI administration.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-025-15479-w.Keywords: Osimertinib, Beyond PD, Best supportive care, Retrospective study

Background

Lung cancer accounts for approximately 2.5 million newly diagnosed patients and 1.8 million deaths annually worldwide [1]. Approximately 17%–46% of lung adenocarcinomas harbor epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) gene mutations [2–4], which are more common in Asian than in Western populations. Molecular targeted therapies targeting these mutations have been approved and have improved the prognosis of patients with advanced lung cancer [5]. In 2015, immunotherapy was made available for advanced non-small-cell lung cancer, which significantly improved the prognosis. However, the efficacy of immunotherapy is limited in patients with EGFR mutation-positive lung cancer [6], and the prognosis remains challenging for patients who have not responded to molecular-targeted therapies.

When progressive disease (PD) is observed during first-line molecular targeted therapy, chemotherapy or a combination of chemotherapy and immunotherapy is administered to patients with adequate tolerance. However, in real-world settings, many patients cannot tolerate chemotherapy because of advanced age, poor performance status (PS), or organ dysfunction. For such patients, if only the best supportive care (BSC) is provided, continuing molecular targeted therapy beyond PD becomes a common treatment option. The rationale for this approach includes reports of small patient cohorts showing improved overall survival (OS) with first generation tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI) therapy compared with discontinuation [7–9]. In the clinical trial, the continuation of TKIs beyond PD is approved if it is expected to be beneficial [10].

Osimertinib is a third-generation EGFR inhibitor that was approved in Japan in 2016. Initially, it was only indicated for EGFR mutation-positive lung cancer with a positive T790M mutation after prior EGFR-TKI treatment. Following the results of the FLAURA trial [10], the indication was expanded to include all first-line treatments for EGFR mutation-positive lung cancer, and osimertinib is now increasingly used in EGFR mutation-positive lung cancers. Recent advances in treatment strategies for EGFR-positive lung cancer considering resistance to third-generation TKIs have been remarkable, including novel combination therapy with bispecific antibodies and third-generation TKIs as first-line treatment [11], and combination therapy with bispecific antibodies and anticancer drugs for patients with PD after osimertinib administration [12] However, due to the high incidence of side effects, osimertinib monotherapy remains widely used in clinical practice. Regarding PD administration of osimertinib, in the United States and Italy, while Le and Cortellini retrospectively reported that osimertinib beyond PD improved OS compared with BSC in patients with the T790M mutation who were receiving second-line osimertinib [13, 14], a previous report on the whole picture of first-line osimertinib use from Japan showed that continuation beyond PD worsened OS compared to discontinuation [15]. Regarding worsening prognosis, Watanabe et al. stated that continuation led to increased deterioration and reduced the opportunity to switch to anticancer drugs [15]. However, in community hospitals, many patients cannot tolerate anticancer drugs in the first place, and it remains unclear whether continuing osimertinib beyond PD is better than transitioning to only BSC for such patients. In this study, we conducted a multi-community-hospital study in Japan, the most aged country in the world, to evaluate which patients in community hospitals were selected for the beyond PD treatment strategy in actual clinical practice, and whether continuing osimertinib beyond PD improves prognosis compared with BSC in patients with osimertinib PD or who are unable to continue because of side effects.

Methods

Study design

This was a multi-institutional retrospective observational study. The Kyoto Medical Center is a 600-bed facility in Kyoto, Kyoto; Hikone Municipal Hospital has 438 beds in Hikone, Shiga; and Nagahama Municipal Hospital has 565 beds in Nagahama, Shiga, all of which serve as public hospitals. The study population consisted of patients with advanced lung cancer or postoperative recurrence who received osimertinib treatment between March 2016, when osimertinib was approved in Japan, and September 2025 and showed radiological progression or intolerable adverse events after treatment with osimertinib. All patients harbored EGFR mutations. PD was confirmed using the Response Evaluation Criteria for Solid Tumors 1.1. Because this was a retrospective study, the interval to evaluate disease progression, such as computed tomography scans, or side effects, such as electrocardiogram, was dependent on the clinician’s decision. We excluded patients who took osimertinib before PD, were lost to follow-up due to transfer, underwent irregular treatment for concurrent cancer or other diseases affecting life prognosis, switched to other TKIs, died due to adverse events or sudden death, received postoperative adjuvant chemotherapy, and participated in clinical trials. Although combination therapy of osimertinib with anticancer drugs was approved during this period in Japan, considering the study objectives, patients with osimertinib-anticancer drug combination were also excluded.

Age, sex, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) PS, post-PD treatment, mutation of EGFR, osimertinib treatment line, time-to-treatment failure (TTF), overall survival, and adverse events were recorded. TTF was defined as the duration from osimertinib initiation to PD confirmation or osimertinib cessation because of intolerable side effects. Because this was a retrospective study, the severity of adverse events was not assessed. Patients were divided into three groups based on the subsequent treatment type: patients who did not choose anticancer drugs and continued osimertinib for more than 1 month until they became intolerant due to PS deterioration or hospitalization were classified into the beyond PD group; patients who discontinued all anticancer drugs, including osimertinib, after PD or intolerable side effects, or who continued osimertinib for less than 1 month to receive the best supportive care were classified into the BSC group; and patients who received anticancer drugs at least once after PD or intolerable side effects were classified into the chemotherapy group. The one-month cutoff was adopted as the time allowance in actual clinical practice from PD confirmation on CT images to decision-making. Additional palliative local irradiation for the bone, brain, or other metastases was permitted in each group.

Ethics

This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the Ethical Review Committee of Kyoto Medical Center (order number 23 − 022) and the relevant committees of each institution, and informed consent was obtained without requiring written consent from patients by providing an opportunity for opt-out.

Statistical analysis

For comparisons between groups, Fisher’s exact test and χ² test were used for categorical variables, and the Mann–Whitney test was employed for continuous variables. Kaplan–Meier survival analysis was used for prognostic comparisons using the log-rank test. Additionally, to evaluate the factors for choosing beyond PD or BSC, the differences between the beyond PD and BSC groups, other than the discontinuation of osimertinib due to adverse events, were evaluated. Multivariate survival analysis was performed using the Cox regression model with TTF and treatment strategy, and the Wald test was used. All statistical analyses were performed using JMP Student Edition 18 (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA). The significance level was defined as p < 0.05 for a two-sided test.

Results

Patient characteristics

Patients in the Chemotherapy Group were Younger and had a Better PS Than Those in the Other Groups.

Osimertinib was administered to 309 patients during the study period. After excluding 183 patients, 126 patients were included in the analysis. The median age of the included patients was 77 years old, and 23.0% were with poor PS more than one. The beyond PD group included 21 patients (16.7%), the BSC group included 36 patients (28.6%), and the chemotherapy group included 69 patients (54.8%) (Fig. 1). Patient characteristics are shown in Table 1. Patients in the chemotherapy group were younger and had better PS than those in the other groups (Supplementary Table 1).

Survival analysis

Continued Osimertinib Treatment beyond PD is Associated with Improved Survival.

The OS after osimertinib treatment failure in each group is shown in Fig. 2A. The median OS was 8.8 months (95% confidence interval [CI]: 4.2–17.2) in the beyond PD group, 2.6 months (95% CI: 1.2–3.6) in the BSC group, and 15.5 months (95% CI: 9.7–21.1) in the chemotherapy group. The chemotherapy group had a significantly longer OS (p < 0.001), and the beyond PD group had a significantly longer OS than the BSC group (p < 0.0001). Figure 2B shows the OS of patients who received osimertinib as first-line chemotherapy. The OS of patients with major mutations, exon 19 deletion, or L858R in exon 21 with or without T790M is shown in Fig. 2C. Significant differences in survival were observed between the two analyses (p < 0.0001 and p < 0.0001, respectively).

Longer TTF is Associated with the Decision to Continue Osimertinib Treatment.

Table 2 shows a comparison between the BSC group (excluding cases discontinued owing to adverse events) and the beyond PD group. The beyond PD group had a significantly longer osimertinib TTF than the BSC group (p < 0.001). The two groups showed no significant differences in age, sex, PS, treatment lines, or the presence of adverse events (p = 0.36, 0.75, 0.21, 0.91, and 0.21, respectively).

OS from osimertinib initiation

Continued Osimertinib is an Independent Predictor of Survival.

Figure 3 shows the TTF and OS after osimertinib initiation in each group. The beyond PD group had longer TTF (16.1 months, 95% CI: 12.0–25.2) than the other two groups (3.9 months, 95% CI: 2.1–8.8 in the BSC group and 11.9 months, 95% CI: 9.1–17.0 in the chemotherapy group) (Fig. 3A). Additionally, there was no significant difference in OS from osimertinib initiation between the beyond PD and chemotherapy groups (29.2 months, 95% CI: 18.4–36.2 in the beyond PD group vs. 27.9 months, 95% CI: 23.2–37.6 in the chemotherapy group, p = 0.63, Fig. 3B). The results of multivariate analysis of survival time from osimertinib failure using TTF and treatment groups as factors are shown in Table 3. The beyond PD group exhibited a significantly prolonged survival time compared to the BSC group even after adjusting for TTF (hazard ratio [HR]: 0.34, 95% CI: 0.18–0.66, p < 0.0005), while TTF did not differ significantly (HR: 0.98, 95% CI: 0.96–0.9996, p = 0.053).

Discussion

In this study, we examined the additive effect of osimertinib beyond PD over BSC using multi-institutional data from clinical practice. We found that continuing osimertinib beyond PD improved prognosis compared with the BSC group. As a treatment selection, the beyond-PD strategy was preferred in cases with longer TTF, and a prolongation of survival time compared to BSC was observed even considering TTF.

Lung cancer has a poor prognosis, particularly lung adenocarcinoma with genetic mutations, and a temporary response to molecular targeted therapies can be expected; however, long-term prognosis remains difficult after PD due to the generally poor response to immunotherapy [16]. The continuation of TKIs beyond PD is a topic of frequent discussion, and their potential in first-generation TKIs has been suggested [7–9, 17, 18]. We believe this strategy is supported by the heterogeneity of resistant cells [19]. Osimertinib, a third-generation TKI, is currently the most widely used TKI and has been shown to prolong survival in first-line therapy more than in first-generation TKIs [10]. The usage of osimertinib beyond PD has been demonstrated in patients with the resistance gene T790M that emerged after first- or second-generation TKI therapy; however, many of these were single-center studies with limited interpretation of the results [14, 20]. Recently, a multi-institutional prospective observational study from Japan on osimertinib as first-line therapy reported that continuation beyond PD may lead to missing opportunities for cytotoxic anticancer drugs and worsen the prognosis [15]. This cohort likely included a broader population, encompassing more patients eligible for chemotherapy. Our results suggest that a rapid transition to chemotherapy is crucial for improving OS in patients who can tolerate chemotherapy, supporting these findings. However, Japan has the most aged society in the world, and many patients in clinical practice, such as elderly patients or those with poor PS, find it difficult to take anticancer drugs. For patients who cannot tolerate anticancer drugs in the first place, the option of continuing osimertinib therapy to potentially slow progression seems reasonable, which led us to conduct this study. In fact, the median ages of the beyond PD and BSC groups were 81 and 81.5 years, respectively, with some continuing on beyond PD at age ≥ 90 years. These results suggest that continuing osimertinib is a valid option for a well-defined, vulnerable patient subgroup that lacks other alternatives, which is of great significance in developed countries with an aging population, considering that this study was conducted in Japanese community hospitals. Furthermore, these results were further confirmed in subgroup analyses of patients who received osimertinib as first-line treatment and those harboring major mutations. Following the expansion of its indications, osimertinib is now primarily used as first-line therapy. However, cases still exist where osimertinib is used in the later lines because first-generation TKI is started in patients with poor PS, urgent cytotoxic chemotherapy is required while awaiting EGFR results, or in cases of postoperative recurrence. Therefore, this study focused on patients treated with osimertinib regardless of the treatment line.

The beyond PD osimertinib group had better osimertinib TTF than both the chemotherapy and BSC groups, whereas no significant differences in age, PS, and treatment lines were observed between the beyond PD and BSC groups. This suggests that successful osimertinib treatment led to the choice of osimertinib continuation rather than transitioning to subsequent treatment or BSC. While a longer TTF may have led to improvements in the OS, multivariate analysis including TTF and treatment strategy demonstrated that the OS was significantly longer in the beyond-PD group than in the BSC group (Table 3), indicating that the treatment strategy had a greater impact on survival than TTF. The results of this study suggest that continuing osimertinib beyond PD in tolerant patients may extend their life by slowing disease progression. However, considering that we occasionally encounter slow-growing lung cancers in clinical practice, it is unclear whether this is due to the treatment effects or cancer characteristics.

This study has several limitations. First, this was a retrospective study, and the interpretation of the results has limitations. Prospective intervention studies are necessary to compare the effects of osimertinib beyond PD. Differences in patient characteristics such as age, performance status, and treatment lines may confound the observed differences in survival, potentially obscuring the true effect of the treatment strategy. Furthermore, there was a significant difference in TTF between the beyond PD and BSC groups, suggesting the possibility of selection bias. Additionally, the glade of adverse events was limited in this retrospective analysis. However, we believe that treatment strategies after PD should differ based on patient background, which is understandable in real-world clinical practice. Second, although we conducted a multi-institutional study considering the possibility of treatment strategy bias in single medical institutions, the number of cases was small. Unknown confounding factors are also possible. Methods, such as propensity score matching, were considered to compare the effectiveness of treatment strategies, but this proved difficult due to the small number of cases. Further case studies are necessary to generalize the results of this study. Finally, the treatment strategies for EGFR-positive cases have changed in recent years. With the emergence of combination therapy of TKIs combined with chemotherapy or other new drugs, the results of this study may differ from those of OS studies with current treatment recommendations. However, newly approved treatment strategies may not be tolerable in older patients or those with poor PS, and their impact on this study was considered to be small.

In conclusion, we examined the effect of continuing osimertinib therapy after PD on survival time in multiple public institutions and found an additive effect compared with BSC. Although the administration of anticancer drugs in cases where they can be administered leads to an improved prognosis, our findings suggest that for a subset of patients, particularly those who are ineligible for subsequent chemotherapy due to advanced age or poor performance status but who had a prolonged initial response to osimertinib, continuing osimertinib beyond PD may be a reasonable strategy superior to BSC alone.

A refined Lusin type theorem for gradients Thanks: Acknowledgments.
Luigi De Masi, Andrea Marchese

Abstract
. We prove a refined version of the celebrated Lusin type theorem for gradients by Alberti, stating that any Borel vector field f f coincides with the gradient of a C 1 C^{1} function g g , outside a set E E of arbitrarily small Lebesgue measure. We replace the Lebesgue measure with any Radon measure μ \mu , and we obtain that the estimate on the L p L^{p} norm of D ​ g Dg does not depend on μ ⁡ ( E ) \mu(E) , if the value of f f is μ \mu -a.e. orthogonal to the decomposability bundle of μ \mu . We observe that our result implies the 1-dimensional version of the flat chain conjecture by Ambrosio and Kirchheim on the equivalence between metric currents and flat chains with finite mass in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} and we state a suitable generalization for k k -forms, which would imply the validity of the conjecture in full generality. K eywords: metric currents, flat chains, normal currents. M SC (2010): 49Q15, 49Q20.

1.
Introduction

In this note, we improve the
Lusin type theorem for gradients
by Alberti, see
[
Alb91
]
, and its generalization for Radon measures, see Theorem 2.1 of
[
MS19
]
, by proving the following result. We recall that the decomposability bundle of a Radon measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, is a Borel map
V(\mu,\cdot):\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\bigcup_{k\leq n}Gr(k,n)
, taking as values vector subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, see Section
2
for the definition. Given a Borel map
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we denote by
f_{V}
the projection of
f
on
V(\mu,\cdot)
, that is, the map
f_{V}:x\mapsto P_{V(\mu,x)}f(x)
, where, for every subspace
L
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
P_{L}
denotes the orthogonal projection onto
L
. Similarly
f_{V^{\perp}}
denotes the projection onto the orthogonal complement of
V(\mu,\cdot)
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mu
be a Radon measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open set such that
\mu(\Omega)<\infty
and let
f\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a Borel map. There exists a constant
C(n)
such that for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a compact set
K\subset\Omega
and a function
g\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
of class
C^{1}
such that,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mu(\Omega\setminus K)<\varepsilon,

(1.2)

\displaystyle Dg(x)=f(x)\qquad\forall x\in K,

(1.3)

\displaystyle\|Dg\|_{L^{p}(\mu)}\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{p}-1}\|f_{V}\|_{L^{p}(\mu)}+(1+\varepsilon)\|f_{V^{\perp}}\|_{L^{p}(\mu)},\quad\mbox{for every $p\in[1,\infty]$}.

Remark 1.2
.

(i)

The main result of
[
Alb91
]
, corresponds to the choice in which
\mu
is the Lebesgue measure. In this case
V(\mu,\cdot)\equiv\mathbb{R}^{n}
, so that the second addendum in the estimate for
\|Dg\|_{L^{p}(\mu)}
vanishes.

(ii)

Our result improves also Theorem 2.1 of
[
MS19
]
, providing an estimate for
\|Dg\|_{L^{p}(\mu)}
which remains bounded when
\varepsilon\to 0
, if
f(x)
is orthogonal to
V(\mu,x)
, for
\mu
-a.e.
x\in\Omega
.

(iii)

In the latter case, since the Lipschitz constant of
g
does not depend on
\varepsilon
, one could be tempted to conclude that a stronger conclusion holds. Namely, that there exists a Lipschitz function
g
such that
D_{f(x)}g(x)=1
, for
\mu
-a.e.
x\in\Omega
. However, this conclusion is false in general. Indeed the natural choice for
g
could be a subsequential limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
of the functions
g_{\varepsilon}
(depending on
\varepsilon)
yielded by Theorem
1.1
, but being the limit
g
only Lipschitz, its directional derivative in direction
f(x)
could fail to exist, because
f(x)\not\in V(\mu,x)
for
\mu
-a.e
x
, see Theorem 1.1 (ii) of
[
AM16
]
. Even in the case in which
D_{f(x)}g(x)
exists at
\mu
-a.e.
x
, the conclusion above would still not be legitimate, because this directional derivative is not closable from the space of Lipschitz functions to
(L^{\infty}(\mu),w^{*})
, see Theorem 1.1 of
[
ABMar
]
.

In Section
4
, we state a Conjecture which is a suitable generalization of Theorem
1.1
for
k
-forms and we show that its validity implies the validity of the Ambrosio-Kirchheim flat chain conjecture, see
[
AK00
]
, modifying a strategy outlined in
[
MM24
]
. In particular we observe that Theorem
1.1
implies the validity of the conjecture in dimension
k=1
.

2.
Notation and preliminaries

For any (possibly vector valued) Radon measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
we denote by
|\mu|
its total variation measure and by
\mathbb{M}(\mu):=|\mu|(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. For any Borel set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we denote by
\mu\llcorner E
the restriction of
\mu
to
E
, that is, the measure
A\mapsto\mu(E\cap A)
, for every Borel set
A
.

If
e\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and
\alpha\in\left[0,\frac{\pi}{2}\right]
, we denote by
C(e,\alpha)
the convex cone with axis
e
and opening angle
\alpha
, namely

(2.1)

C(e,\alpha)\coloneqq\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\colon x\cdot e\geq\cos\alpha|x|\}.

We recall the following definition, see §2.6, §6.1 and Theorem 6.4 of
[
AM16
]
.

Definition 2.1

(Decomposability bundle)
.

Given a positive Radon measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
its
decomposability bundle
is any Borel map
V(\mu,\cdot)
taking values in the set
\bigcup_{k\leq n}Gr(k,n)
, that is, in the union of the Grasmannians of vector subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
of any dimension, with the following property. For
\mu
-a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, a vector
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
belongs to
V(\mu,x)
if and only if there exists a vector-valued measure
T
with
{\mathrm{div}}T=0
such that

\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{\mathbb{M}((T-v\mu)\llcorner B(x,r))}{\mu(B(x,r))}=0.

3.
Proof of the main result

Thanks to Theorem 2.1 of
[
MS19
]
, in order to prove Theorem
1.1
, it is sufficient to prove the following result.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mu
be a Radon measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open set such that
\mu(\Omega)<\infty
and let
f\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a Borel function satisfying
f(x)\in V(\mu,x)^{\perp}
for
\mu
-a.e.
x\in\Omega
. For every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a compact set
K\subset\Omega
and a function
g\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
of class
C^{1}
such that

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mu(\Omega\setminus K)<\varepsilon,

(3.2)

\displaystyle Dg(x)=f(x)\qquad\forall x\in K,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\|Dg\|_{L^{p}(\mu)}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mu)},\quad\mbox{for every $p\in[1,\infty]$}.

The proof is based on the following lemmas.

Lemma 3.2

(quantitative version of Lusin’s theorem)
.

Let
\mu
be a Radon measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open set such that
\mu(\Omega)<\infty
and let
f\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a Borel function. Then, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
h\in C_{c}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

(3.4)

\displaystyle\mu\left(\{x\in\Omega\colon h(x)\neq f(x)\}\right)<\varepsilon,

(3.5)

\displaystyle\|h\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\qquad\forall p\in[1,+\infty].

Remark 3.3
.

Notice that
h
depends only on
f
and
\varepsilon
and does not depend on
p
.

Of course we can assume
f\not\equiv 0
, otherwise there is nothing to prove, and
\varepsilon<1
. Moreover, we can assume that
f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
for some
p\in[1,+\infty]
, otherwise the thesis is just the classical Lusin’s theorem, up to performing a cut-off of the resulting continuous function in a small neighborhood of
\partial\Omega
(see part 3 of this proof).

For every
t>0
we call

(3.6)

E(f,t)\coloneqq\{x\in\Omega\colon|f(x)|\geq t\}.

\bullet

1 - Truncation.

We first observe that, since
f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
for some
p\in[1,+\infty]
, there exists
t>0
such that either
0<\mu\big(E(f,t)\big)<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}
or

(3.7)

\mu\big(E(f,t)\big)\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{3}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mu\big(E(f,s)\big)=0\quad\forall s>t.

In either case, we call
E\coloneqq E(f,t)
and we construct
f_{1}
by projecting
f
on the sphere of radius
t
, namely we define

(3.8)

f_{1}(x)=\begin{cases}f(x)&\text{if }|f(x)|<t\\[8.0pt]
t\dfrac{f(x)}{|f(x)|}&\text{if }|f(x)|\geq t.\end{cases}

Clearly
\|f_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=t
and, since
|f_{1}(x)|\leq|f(x)|
for every
x\in\Omega
, we have

(3.9)

\displaystyle\|f_{1}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\qquad\forall p\in[1,+\infty].

Moreover
f_{1}=f

\mu
-a.e. in case (
3.7
) holds, whereas

(3.10)

\mu\big(\{x\in\Omega\colon f_{1}(x)\neq f(x)\}\big)\leq\mu(E)<\frac{\varepsilon}{3},

in the other case.

\bullet

2 - Continuous approximation.

Chosing
0<\eta<\varepsilon\min\left\{\mu(E),\frac{\varepsilon}{3}\right\}
, by classical Lusin’s theorem there exists a continuous function
f_{2}\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
such that
F\coloneqq\{x\in\Omega\colon f_{1}(x)\neq f_{2}(x)\}
satisfies

(3.11)

\mu(F)<\eta.

Up to projecting on
B_{t}(0)
, we can assume
\|f_{2}\|_{C(\Omega)}\leq t
, thus

(3.12)

\|f_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\|f_{2}\|_{C(\Omega)}\leq t=\|f_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

We now estimate the difference
\|f_{2}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}-\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
uniformly for
p\in[1,+\infty)
. We have

(3.13)

\int_{\Omega}|f_{2}(x)|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\leq\int_{\Omega\setminus(F\cup E)}|f(x)|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x)+t^{p}\mu(F\cup E),

while

(3.14)

\int_{\Omega}|f(x)|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\geq\int_{\Omega\setminus(F\cup E)}|f(x)|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x)+t^{p}\mu(E).

By these estimates, using
\mu(F)<\varepsilon\mu(E)
and the relation (due to the concavity of the function
s\mapsto s^{\frac{1}{p}}
and
p\geq 1
)

(3.15)

\big(a+(1+\varepsilon)b\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}-(a+b)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq b^{\frac{1}{p}}\big[(1+\varepsilon)^{\frac{1}{p}}-1\big]\leq\varepsilon b^{\frac{1}{p}}\qquad\forall a,b\geq 0,\,\,\,\forall p\in[1,+\infty),

we obtain

(3.16)

\|f_{2}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}-\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\varepsilon t\mu(E)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\varepsilon\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\qquad\forall p\in[1,+\infty].

Gathering (
3.12
) and (
3.16
) we get

(3.17)

\|f_{2}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\qquad\forall p\in[1,+\infty].

We moreover record that the choice of
\eta
and (
3.10
) implies

(3.18)

\mu\big(\{x\in\Omega\colon f_{2}(x)\neq f(x)\}\big)<\frac{2}{3}\varepsilon.

\bullet

3 - Cut-off.

Since
\Omega
is open and
\mu
is a Radon measure, there exists
\rho>0
such that

(3.19)

\mu\Big(\big\{x\in\Omega\colon\dist(x,\partial\Omega)\leq\rho\big\}\Big)<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}.

We choose any cut-off function
\chi\in C_{c}(\Omega)
such that
\chi(x)=1
if
\dist(x,\partial\Omega)\geq\rho
and we define
h\coloneqq\chi f_{2}
. By
|h|\leq|f_{2}|
, (
3.17
), (
3.18
) and (
3.19
),
h
is the desired function.

∎

Remark 3.4
.

If
f
is such that

(3.20)

\forall\eta>0\,\,\,\exists t>0\colon\quad\eta>\mu\big(E(f,t)\big)>0,

then by a similar procedure one can in fact construct a continuous function
h
with compact support which differs from
f
on a small set and

(3.21)

\|h\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\qquad\forall p\in[1,+\infty].

We recall that
C(e,\alpha)
is the convex cone with axis
e
and angle
\alpha
, see section
2
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\mu
be a Radon measure on an open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
B\subset\subset\Omega
be an open ball and assume that there exists
e\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and
\alpha>0
such that
V(\mu,x)\cap C(e,\alpha)=0
for
\mu
-a.e.
x\in B
. Let
\delta>0
and let
f\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a Borel function such that

(3.22)

\big|f(x)-\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}e\big|\leq\delta\qquad\text{for every $x\in B$.}

Then, for every
\eta>0,\gamma>0
there exist an open set
U\subset B
and a function
w\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
of class
C^{1}
such that

(3.23)

\displaystyle\sup_{B}|w|\leq\eta,

(3.24)

\displaystyle\mu(B\setminus U)\leq\gamma,

(3.25)

\displaystyle|Dw(x)-f(x)|\leq 4\delta+\frac{\|f\|_{\infty}}{\tan\alpha}\qquad\mbox{ for every $x\in U$},

(3.26)

\displaystyle|Dw(x)|\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}\left(1+\frac{1}{\tan\alpha}\right)\qquad\mbox{ for every $x\in B$}.

By Lemma 7.5 of
[
AM16
]
, there exists a
C(e,\alpha)
-null Borel set
F\subset B
such that
\mu(B\setminus F)=0
. Hence we can find a compact subset
K\subset F
such that
\mu(B\setminus K)<\gamma
. Let
w_{0}
be the smooth function constructed in Lemma 4.12 of
[
AM16
]
, with
\varepsilon=\frac{\eta}{\|f\|_{\infty}+\delta}
.
Combining properties (ii) and (iii) of Lemma 4.12 of
[
AM16
]
, we deduce that

\left|Dw_{0}(x)-e\right|<\frac{1}{\tan\alpha},\qquad\mbox{for every $x\in K$}

and

|Dw_{0}(x)|<1+\frac{1}{\tan\alpha},\qquad\mbox{for every $x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$}.

By (
3.22
) the function
w:=\|f\|_{\infty}w_{0}
satisfies:

(3.27)

\displaystyle\sup_{B}|w|\leq\eta,

(3.28)

\displaystyle|Dw(x)-f(x)|\leq\big|Dw(x)-\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}e\big|+\big|\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}e-f(x)\big|\leq\frac{\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}}{\tan\alpha}+\delta\qquad\mbox{ for every $x\in K$},

(3.29)

\displaystyle|Dw(x)|\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}\left(1+\frac{1}{\tan\alpha}\right)\qquad\mbox{ for every $x\in B$}.

Since
w
is of class
C^{1}
, we can take a sufficiently small open tubular neighbourhood
U
of
K
in
B
so that, for every
x\in U
, denoting
y
a point in
K
which minimizes the distance to
x
, we have

(3.30)

\begin{split}|Dw(x)-f(x)|&\leq|Dw(x)-Dw(y)|+|Dw(y)-f(y)|+|f(y)-f(x)|\\
&\leq\delta+\sup_{y\in K}|Dw(y)-f(y)|+\operatorname{osc}_{B}(f)\\
&\leq 4\delta+\frac{\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}}{\tan\alpha}.\end{split}

∎

Lemma 3.6

(approximation of continuous functions with compact support)
.

Let
\mu
be a Radon measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open set such that
\mu(\Omega)<\infty
and let
h\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a continuous function with compact support in
\Omega
satisfying
h(x)\in V(\mu,x)^{\perp}
for
\mu
-a.e.
x\in\Omega
. Then for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a compact set
K\subset\Omega
and a function
g\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
of class
C^{1}
such that

(3.31)

\displaystyle\mu(\Omega\setminus K)<\varepsilon,

(3.32)

\displaystyle\supp g\subseteq\supp h,

(3.33)

\displaystyle Dg(x)=h(x)\qquad\forall x\in K,

(3.34)

\displaystyle\|Dg\|_{C^{0}(\Omega)}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\|h\|_{C^{0}(\Omega)}.

Of course we can assume
\varepsilon<1
and
h\not\equiv 0
.
By the classical Lusin’s theorem we find a compact set
C
such that the map
x\mapsto V(\mu,x)
is continuous on
C
, that
h(x)\in V(\mu,x)^{\perp}
for every
x\in C
and

(3.35)

\displaystyle\mu(\Omega\setminus C)<\frac{\varepsilon}{4}.

The idea is to construct a series of
C^{1}
functions
g_{n}
such that
h-\sum_{k=0}^{n}Dg_{n}
converges uniformly to
0
on a compact set
K\subset\Omega
with large measure and define
g\coloneqq\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}g_{n}
. More precisely we are going to inductively define a decreasing sequence of open sets
V_{n}
and a sequence of functions
g_{n}\in C^{1}_{c}(V_{n})
with the following properties:

(3.36)

\displaystyle\mu(\Omega\setminus V_{n})\leq(1-2^{-n-1})\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\qquad\forall n\geq 0,

(3.37)

\displaystyle\left\|h-\sum_{k=0}^{n}Dg_{n}\right\|_{C^{0}(V_{n})}\leq\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^{n}\|h\|_{C^{0}(\Omega)}\qquad\forall n\geq 0,

(3.38)

\displaystyle\|Dg_{n}\|_{C^{0}(\Omega)}\leq\left(1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^{n}\|h\|_{C^{0}(\Omega)}\qquad\forall n\geq 0,

and finally we will fix a suitable compact set
K\subseteq\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}V_{n}
on which
h=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}g_{n}
.
As base of the induction, we set

(3.39)

g_{0}=0,\qquad V_{0}\coloneqq\Omega.

item
\bullet

Step 1: First approximation.

Let us choose
\delta>0,0<\alpha<\frac{\pi}{2}
such that

(3.40)

4\delta+\frac{\|h\|_{C^{0}(\Omega)}}{\tan\alpha}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\|h\|_{C^{0}(\Omega)}.

Let
x\in C
; we distinguish two cases.

\bullet

-

If
h(x)=0
, by continuity there exists
r_{x}>0
such that

(3.41)

|h(y)|\leq\frac{\delta}{2}\qquad\forall y\in B_{r_{x}}(x).

\bullet

-

If
h(x)\neq 0
, we set

(3.42)

e=\frac{h(x)}{|h(x)|}.

By continuity of
V
and
h
, and using that
h(x)\in V(\mu,x)^{\perp}
for every
x\in C
, we can find
r_{x}>0
such that
B_{r_{x}}(x)\subset\subset\{h\neq 0\}
and

(3.43)

\displaystyle V(\mu,y)\cap C(e,\alpha)=\emptyset,\qquad\forall y\in C\cap B_{r_{x}}(x),

(3.44)

\displaystyle\langle h(y);e\rangle>(1-\delta)|h(y)|\big|h(y)-\|f\|_{C^{0}(B)}e\big|\leq\delta\qquad\forall y\in B_{r_{x}}(x).

By classical covering arguments, see for instance
[
EG15
, Theorem 1.29]
, there exists a finite set of points
x_{1},\dots,x_{N}\in C
and radii
r_{i}\in(0,r_{x_{i}})
for
i=1,\dots,N
such that the closed balls
\overline{B}_{r_{i}}(x_{i})\subset\Omega
are mutually disjoint and it holds

(3.45)

\mu\left(C\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}B_{r_{i}}(x_{i})\right)<2^{-4}{\varepsilon}.

Let us call
B_{i}\coloneqq B_{r_{i}}(x_{i})
. Since the closed balls
\overline{B}_{i}
are disjoint and contained in
\Omega
we can fix
d>0
satisfying the following conditions:

(3.46)

\begin{gathered}d<\frac{1}{8}\min\left\{\dist\big(B_{i},B_{j}\big)\colon i,j=1,\dots,N,i\neq j\right\},\\
d<\frac{1}{8}\min\{\dist(B_{i},\partial\Omega)\colon i=1,\dots,N\},\\
h(x_{i})\neq 0\implies\overline{B}_{r_{i}+4d}(x_{i})\subset\{h\neq 0\}.\end{gathered}

so that the closed balls
\overline{B}_{r_{i}+4d}(x_{i})
are disjoint and contained in
\Omega
as well.
We now fix

(3.47)

\eta\leq\delta d.

Now, for every
i=1,\dots,N
, we choose a function
w_{i}\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
as follows.

\bullet

-

If
h(x_{i})=0
, we set
U_{i}:=B_{i}
and we fix
w_{i}\equiv 0
. We clearly have

(3.48)

\displaystyle|Dw_{i}(y)-h(y)|<\delta,\qquad\forall y\in C\cap B_{i},

(3.49)

\displaystyle|Dw_{i}(y)|\leq\|h\|_{C^{0}(\Omega)}\qquad\forall y\in B_{i}.

\bullet

-

If
h(x_{i})\neq 0
, then by (
3.43
) and (
3.44
) we can apply Lemma
3.5
to the measure
\mu\llcorner(C\cap B_{i})
in order to find, choosing
\gamma_{i}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{4}N}
, open sets
U_{i}\subset B_{i}
and functions
w_{i}\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
of class
C^{1}
satisfying

(3.50)

\begin{gathered}\sup_{B}|w_{i}|\leq\eta,\\[4.0pt]
\mu(C\cap B_{i}\setminus U_{i})<\gamma_{i},\\[4.0pt]
|Dw_{i}(x)-h(x)|\leq 4\delta+\frac{\|h\|_{C^{0}(\Omega)}}{\tan\alpha}\qquad\mbox{ for every $x\in U_{i}$},\\[4.0pt]
|Dw_{i}(x)|\leq\|h\|_{C^{0}(\Omega)}\left(1+\frac{1}{\tan\alpha}\right)\qquad\mbox{ for every $x\in B_{i}$}.\end{gathered}

We choose suitable functions
\tilde{w}_{i}
such that

(3.51)

\displaystyle\tilde{w}_{i}\equiv w_{i},\qquad\mbox{on $B_{i}$}

(3.52)

\displaystyle\tilde{w}_{i}=0,\qquad\mbox{on $\Omega\setminus B_{r_{i}+d}(x_{i})$}

(3.53)

\displaystyle|D(\tilde{w}_{i})|\leq\|h\|_{C^{0}(\Omega)}+4\delta+\frac{1}{\tan\alpha},\qquad\mbox{on $\Omega$},

where the possibility to obtain the last property is guaranteed by the choice of
\eta
.
More precisely,
\tilde{w}_{i}
is constructed as follows: consider

4.
flat chain conjecture

The question on which differential operator admit a Lusin type theorem in the spirit of
[
Alb91
]
has recently drawn some interest, see
[
DG24
]
. For general data, it is natural to expect that the Lipschitz constant of the Lusin-type approximated solutions blow up as
\varepsilon\to 0
. But, as in Theorem
3.1
, one could hope to keep the Lipschitz constant bounded if the given datum is trivial when restricted to the linear subspace along which the operator is closable.

We conjecture that the following version of Theorem
3.1
holds, where
f
is replaced by a
k
-form. Here
V^{k}
is the equivalent of the decomposability bundle for
k
-vectors, see
[

]
. We denote by

the set of multi-indexes of length
h
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

(i_{1},\dots,i_{h})
with
1\leq i_{1}<i_{2},\dots<i_{h}\leq n
.

Conjecture 4.1
.

Let
\mu
be a Radon measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let

be an open set such that
\mu(\Omega)<\infty
and let

be a Borel
k
-form satisfying

\langle\omega(x),\tau\rangle=0\qquad\mbox{ for every $\tau\in V^{k}(\mu,x),$ for $\mu$-a.e.\ $x\in\Omega$.}

For every

there exists a compact set

and a
(k-1)
-form

in

such that

\displaystyle\mu(\Omega\setminus K)<\varepsilon,

(4.2)

\displaystyle d\phi(x)=\omega(x)\qquad\forall x\in K,

(4.3)

\displaystyle\lip(\phi_{I})\leq C(n)\|\omega\|_{\infty},\quad\mbox{for every $I\in I(k-1,n)$}.

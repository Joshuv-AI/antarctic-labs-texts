Nonlinear squeezing of superpositions of quadrature eigenstates Preprint: APS/123-QED
Vojtěch Kuchař, Petr Marek

Abstract
We introduce a family of operators exploiting the symmetry of superpositions of quadrature eigenstates (SQE) and demonstrate how the associated nonlinear squeezing, quantified by the expectation value of such operators, serves both as a witness of non-Gaussianity and as an indicator of the quality of SQE approximations. To establish the usefulness of this measure, we connect it to quantum state fidelity and evaluate its implications in breeding protocols. Finally, we construct optimal approximations of SQE states in truncated Fock spaces.

Traveling light is a versatile experimental platform utilized for many applications of quantum information processing, such as quantum computation

;

;

;

;

quantum metrology

;
Guo et al. 2020
;
Polino et al. 2020
, or quantum communication
Braunstein and Van Loock 2005
;
Weedbrook et al. 2012
;
Jouguet et al. 2013
;
Hajomer et al. 2024
. Since all of these applications are based on preparing and utilizing specific quantum states and their properties, preparation and characterization of quantum states, together with verification of their quantum features, is one of the important challenges in the field
Paris and Řeháček 2004
;
Aolita et al. 2015
;
Chabaud et al. 2021
. The states required by applications can be broadly classified into two categories. Gaussian squeezed light has fluctuations in a single quadrature reduced below the level of the vacuum state. It can be directly applied in metrology
The LIGO Scientific Collaboration 2011
;
Maccone and Riccardi 2020
or communication
Furusawa et al. 1998
, and is indispensable for manipulation of optical states
Fiurášek 2002
;
Yonezawa and Furusawa 2010
;
Hastrup et al. 2021a
and generation of large scale entangled states
Menicucci et al. 2006
;
Yokoyama et al. 2013
. Despite its uses, squeezed light on its own is insufficient for the more advanced applications of quantum information technologies, such as quantum computation
Niset et al. 2009
. For this, the Gaussian tool set of quantum optics needs to be supported by non-Gaussian quantum states serving as code states
Gottesman et al. 2001
;
Grimsmo et al. 2020
;
Schlegel et al. 2022
;
Joshi et al. 2021
or resources for operations
Walshe et al. 2020
;
Hastrup et al. 2021b
;
Konno et al. 2021a
.

Superpositions of coherent states form one such class of useful non-Gaussian states with uses in quantum computation
Guillaud and Mirrahimi 2019
;
Hastrup and Andersen 2022a
;
Régent et al. 2023
and metrology
Joo et al. 2011
;
Luis 2001
. These states can be also considered for breeding of Gottesman-Kitaev-Preskill (GKP) states
Vasconcelos et al. 2010
;
Weigand and Terhal 2018
;
Hastrup and Andersen 2022b
, but there is a catch — the breeding protocol calls for nonphysical superpositions of quadrature eigenstates (SQE). Squeezing the superposed coherent states can approach this approximation, but for practical purposes, it is generally more efficient to directly aim at preparation of the already squeezed states. Both superpositions of coherent states and superpositions of displaced squeezed states were already experimentally prepared
Ourjoumtsev et al. 2007
;
Huang et al. 2015
;
Etesse et al. 2015
;
Sychev et al. 2017
;
Cotte et al. 2022
;
Wang et al. 2022
;
Bao et al. 2022
, which opened up the problem of evaluating and characterizing quality of the states. Superposed coherent states can be evaluated by fidelity
Jozsa 1994
. This is, however, impossible for approximations of quadrature eigenstates, due to the non-physical nature of infinitely squeezed states.

Quality of the Gaussian quadrature squeezed light can be straightforwardly characterized by squeezing — the value of the variance of the quadrature operator — which often directly correlates with the noise added to the protocols
Wang et al. 2007
. Quantum non-Gaussian states, however, have properties given by complex features of their Wigner function, such as negative areas, or sub-Planck structure
Zurek 2001
;
Walschaers 2021
. In recent efforts, non-Gaussianity was also tied to directly measurable quantities which can be used to construct specific witnesses
Miyata et al. 2016
. A specific kind of such witness is nonlinear squeezing
Bräuer et al. 2025a
, defined as the variance of a nonlinear function of quadrature operators
Konno et al. 2021b
;
Kala et al. 2022
, which targets selected practically relevant non-Gaussian features
Marek et al. 2018
;
Bräuer and Marek 2021
;
Kala et al. 2025
and can replace fidelity when the ideal target state is nonphysical
Marek 2024
. Nonlinear squeezing provides valuable information and can be straightforwardly evaluated, but it has an up-front cost — the design of a specific nonlinear operator.

In this paper we present a nonlinear squeezing operator aimed at evaluating the quality of approximated superpositions of quadrature eigenstates (SQE). We construct optimal approximations of SQE states in truncated Fock spaces as ground states of this operator and connect the values of nonlinear squeezing to fidelity in interactions where SQE states act as a natural resource of non-Gaussianity. We perform numerical optimizations to obtain bounds for this fidelity and also for nonlinear GKP squeezing in breeding protocols, allowing us to make predictions about the outputs in relevant interactions solely based on SQE squeezing of input states.

II
Superpositions of quadrature eigenstates

Let
\hat{a}
be the bosonic annihilation operator of an electromagnetic field mode with canonically conjugate quadratures
\hat{x}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}+\hat{a}\right),

\hat{p}=\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}-\hat{a}\right),
natural variables with
\commutator{\hat{x}}{\hat{p}}=i
are used in this work. With the exception of
\ket{x=0},
we use
\hat{x}\ket{x}=x\ket{x}
to denote eigenstates of quadrature operators. An ideal superposition of quadrature eigenstates (SQE) can be defined as

\ket{C_{u,\infty,\varphi}}=\ket{u}+e^{i\varphi}\ket{-u},

(1)

where
u\in\mathds{R},\varphi\in\left[0,2\pi\right).
The cases
\varphi=0,\varphi=\pi
will be referred to as even and odd superpositions respectively.

These states appear as the asymptotic limit of superposed displaced squeezed states
Vasconcelos et al. 2010
;
Hastrup and Andersen 2022b
, as the squeezing approaches infinity;

\ket{C_{u,r,\varphi}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathcal{N}_{u,r}}}\left[\left(\hat{D}_{x}\left(u\right)+e^{i\varphi}\hat{D}^{\dagger}_{x}\left(u\right)\right)\hat{S}\left(r\right)\ket{0}\right],

(2)

where
\ket{0}
is the vacuum state,
\hat{D}_{x}(u)=\exp(-iu \hat{p})
is the single-quadrature displacement operator,
u\in\mathds{R},

\hat{S}\left(r\right)=\exp\left[\frac{r}{2}\left(\hat{a}^{2}-\hat{a}^{\dagger 2}\right)\right]
is the squeezing operator,
r\in\mathds{R}
, and
\mathcal{N}_{u,r}
is a normalization constant. SQEs, as defined in (
1
), are non-normalizable and thus unphysical, but their approximative physical versions (
2
) are often considered as imperfect resources for quantum information processing protocols
Gottesman et al. 2001
;
Vasconcelos et al. 2010
;
Weigand and Terhal 2018
;
Hastrup and Andersen 2022b
;
Schlegel et al. 2022
. For this, it is important to be able to determine how well an arbitrary quantum state
\hat{\rho}
approximates the ideal state (
1
), which is relevant for theoretical analysis of quantum protocols
Bourassa et al. 2021
;
Aghaee Rad et al. 2025
, evaluation of experimentally prepared quantum states
Endo et al. 2023
;
Konno et al. 2024
, and related optimization of quantum state preparation circuits. An effective way to evaluate quantum states is based on the ability to define the states as eigenstates of some hermitian operator
Bräuer et al. 2025a
. The variance of the operator, often called squeezing, can then be used as a straightforward figure of merit and often directly quantifies the noise added in some quantum protocol.

The general superpositions of quadrature eigenstatates (
1
) can be defined as zero eigenvalue eigenstates of the operator

\hat{O}\left(u,\varphi,c\right)=\hat{O}_{x}(u)+c\cdot\hat{O}_{p}(u,\varphi),

(3)

with

\displaystyle\hat{O}_{x}(u)

\displaystyle={\left(\hat{x}^{2}-u^{2}\right)}^{2},

(4a)

\displaystyle\hat{O}_{p}(u,\varphi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{+\infty}\outerproduct{p=p_j}{p=p_j},

(4b)

where
p_{j}=\frac{(2j-1)\pi-\varphi}{2u}
. Note that the operator
\hat{O}_{p}(u,\varphi)
is not unique. For same cases, such as even and odd superpositions, it could be replaced by a modified parity operator
Bräuer et al. 2025b
. However, choosing the operator as (
4b
) allows us to evaluate the property of the state using only statistics of the
\hat{p}
quadrature which can be easily accessed by homodyne detection.

Operator (
3
), or rather a family of operators distinguished by different values of
c
, can be separated into parts involving only the
x
and the
p
quadrature representations, taking advantage of the symmetric nature of the target states (
1
) and (
2
). The value of
c
then serves as a balancing factor, quantifying the relative importance between the narrowness of the peaks visible in
x
and the interference pattern visible in
p
.

Similarly to the work done in
Marek 2024
, we interpret the mean value of operator (
3
) as nonlinear squeezing, because the operator is positive-semidefinite and the target state is its ground state. For the even case of the idealized approximation (
2
) we find

\expectationvalue{\hat{O}(u,0,c)}=\frac{\frac{4e^{2r}u^{2}\left(e^{2r}u^{2}-3\right)}{e^{e^{2r}u^{2}}+1}+8e^{2r}u^{2}+3}{4e^{4r}},

(5)

which is decreasing everywhere as a function of
r
and converges to zero as
r\rightarrow+\infty,
exactly as expected. Furthermore, the expectation value of this operator is lower bounded on the set of Gaussian states by

\displaystyle\min_{G}\expectationvalue{\hat{O}(u,\varphi,c)}=\min\left\{\frac{uc}{\pi},\quad\min_{r\in\mathds{R}}\left[u^{4}+\frac{3}{4}e^{-4r}-u^{2}e^{-2r}+\frac{uc}{\pi}~\vartheta_{3}\left(-\frac{\pi}{2},e^{-u^{2}e^{2r}}\right)\right]\right\},

(6)

where
\vartheta_{3}\left(z,q\right)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{+\infty}q^{n^{2}}e^{i2nz}
is the third elliptic theta function, and can therefore serve as a witness of non-Gaussian behavior.

Let us now compare the nonlinear squeezing for SQE states, defined as the expectation value of (
3
), to other methods that can be used for quantifying quality of the quantum states. A common figure of merit often used for such analysis is the quantum state fidelity
Jozsa 1994

F\left(\hat{\rho}_{1},\hat{\rho}_{2}\right)={\left(\operatorname{Tr}\sqrt{\sqrt{\hat{\rho}_{1}}\hat{\rho}_{2}\sqrt{\hat{\rho_{1}}}}\right)}^{2},

(7)

where
\hat{\rho}_{1},\hat{\rho}_{2}
are density operators of two quantum states. However, using quantum state fidelity directly to evaluate the quality of approximative preparation of eigenket superposition approximations needs to be discouraged. The first reason is that the target state (
1
) is not normalizable. Although (
7
) can be calculated, it does not have any good physical meaning, as it is not from the interval
[0,1]
. The second reason is that, even if we consider the target state to be a specific finite approximation
2
with some value
r
, the issues do not disappear. This is because for any
r
we could find
r^{\prime}>r
such that the fidelity of the two states is close to zero, even though the state with
r^{\prime}
better approximates the ideal state (
1
).

One way to avoid the problem of calculating fidelities with non-normalizable states, is to not look for the fidelity directly, but rather to consider utilizing the states (
1
) and (
2
) as interaction resources (virtually) and only then comparing the outputs, those being regular states with unit norm, using quantum state fidelity. The virtual operation that can be considered for this purpose, an operation for which the superposition of quadrature eigenstates is a natural resource, is the non-unitary operation

\hat{D}_{x}(u)+\hat{D}_{x}(-u).

This operation can be used to create the superposed coherent states, both regular and squeezed, but it can be also seen as a single step of the breeding protocol for GKP states
Vasconcelos et al. 2010
. This operation can be physically implemented only by Gaussian operations supplanted by the resource state (
1
). The full gate consists of a Gaussian coupling, either a beam splitter, or a quantum non-demolition (QND) interaction
Filip et al. 2005
, that couples the resource (
1
) to the target state. This coupling is then followed by homodyne detection and post-selection on a specific outcome.

Formally, we can represent the full gate as follows. We will consider two modes of a bosonic field, with quadratures satisfying
\commutator{\hat{x}_j}{\hat{p}_k}=i\delta_{j,k}
, where
j,k=1,2
. The Gaussian unitary coupling will be represented by a unitary operator
\hat{U}_{\mathrm{INT}}
, which can either be the quantum nondemolition interaction
Filip et al. 2005

\hat{U}_{\mathrm{QND}}=\exp(-i\hat{x}_1\hat{p}_2),

(8)

or the balanced beam splitter
Dakna et al. 1997

\hat{U}_{\mathrm{BS}}=\exp\left[\frac{i\pi}{4}(\hat{p}_{1}\hat{x}_{2}-\hat{p}_{2}\hat{x}_{1})\right].

(9)

We shall specifically consider an interaction where the first mode contains the resource state
\ket{\mathrm{R}}_{1}
and the second mode contains the vacuum state
\ket{0}_{2}
. The resource mode will then be measured by homodyne detection and the output state will be conditioned on measuring
\hat{p}_{1}=0
. The output state
\ket{\Omega}_{2}
can then be, up to normalization, expressed as

\ket{\Omega}_{2}\propto\,_{1}\langle\hat{p}=0|\hat{U}_{\mathrm{INT}}|\mathrm{R},0\rangle_{12}.

(10)

For the ideal resource state
\ket{\mathrm{R}}=\ket{C_{u,\infty,\varphi}}
, we can straightforwardly calculate the evolution and arrive at

\ket{\Omega_{\mathrm{QND},u,\infty,\varphi}}=\left[\hat{D}_{x}\left(u\right)+e^{i\varphi}\hat{D}^{\dagger}_{x}\left(u\right)\right]\ket{0}

(11)

for the QND interaction and

\ket{\Omega_{\mathrm{BS},u,\infty,\varphi}}=\left[\hat{D}_{x}\left(\frac{u}{\sqrt{2}}\right)+e^{i\varphi}\hat{D}^{\dagger}_{x}\left(\frac{u}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\right]\hat{S}\left(\frac{\ln 2}{2}\right)\ket{0}

(12)

for the balanced beam splitter, both up to normalization, where
\ket{\Omega}
is the state conditionally prepared in the remaining mode.

For any pure resource state
\ket{\mathrm{R}}
we can obtain the conditional output states in the
\hat{x}-
representation as

\displaystyle\ket{\Omega_{\mathrm{QND, R}}}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{QND}}}\iint\innerproduct{x'}{\mathrm{R}}\innerproduct{x-x'}{0}\ket{x}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}x^{\prime},

\displaystyle\ket{\Omega_{\mathrm{BS, R}}}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{BS}}}\iint\innerproduct{\frac{x'-x}{\sqrt{2}}}{\mathrm{R}}\innerproduct{\frac{x+x'}{\sqrt{2}}}{0}\ket{x}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}x^{\prime},

(13)

where
\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{QND}}
and
\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{BS}}
are normalization coefficients. The process could be generalized for mixed states, but for our main purpose, benchmarking of the SQE squeezing, we can stick with pure states. The respective interaction fidelities can be now defined as

\displaystyle F_{\mathrm{QND}}=\left|\innerproduct{\Omega_{\mathrm{QND},u,\infty,\varphi}}{\Omega_{\mathrm{QND,R}}}\right|^{2},

\displaystyle F_{\mathrm{BS}}=\left|\innerproduct{\Omega_{\mathrm{BS},u,\infty,\varphi}}{\Omega_{\mathrm{BS,R}}}\right|^{2},

(14)

the calculation for
F_{\mathrm{BS}}
is visualized in Fig.
1
. It can be shown that for the resource states that are superpositions of squeezed states
\ket{\mathrm{R}}=\ket{C_{u,r,\varphi}}
(
2
), the fidelities (
II
) are identical for both interactions and that in the limit as
r\rightarrow+\infty
we get
F_{\mathrm{QND}}=F_{\mathrm{BS}}\rightarrow 1
. For simplicity, we shall from now on, consider a single virtual interaction fidelity
F=F_{\mathrm{BS}}
.

Schematic visualization of the virtual interaction-based method for evaluating SQE approximations.
Modes containing the evaluated state
\ket{\mathrm{R}}
and an ideal superposition of quadrature eigenstates

\ket{\Omega}

p=0
in the remaining modes and subsequently utilized to calculate the virtual interaction based fidelity

III
Analysis

If both
F=F_{\mathrm{BS}}
and
\expectationvalue{\hat{O}(u,\varphi,c)}
indicate how well a specific quantum state approximates the ideal SQE state, it is reasonable to expect, that given the SQE squeezing value for a specific state, it should be possible to infer some information about the interaction fidelity. Specifically, obtaining a lower bound

\displaystyle F_{\mathrm{min}}(z)

\displaystyle=\min_{\ket{\psi}\in S_{z}}F,

(15)

\displaystyle S_{z}

\displaystyle=\left\{\ket{\psi}\in\mathscr{H}_{\infty}:\langle\psi|\hat{O}(u,\varphi,c)|\psi\rangle=z\right\},

where the interaction fidelity is minimized over all pure states with
\langle\hat{O}\rangle=z
, would allow us to predict how similarly the state in question behaves to the ideal SQE state in the described virtual interactions based solely on its SQE squeezing.

Deriving the bound analytically is difficult due to the nature of the states that form the boundary, however, numerical methods can be used to obtain the bound with reasonable accuracy. The operator (
3
) is defined on an infinite dimensional Hilbert space
\mathscr{H_{\infty}}
, but it can be truncated to a finite dimension
Marek 2024
;
Provazník et al. 2022
. Such finite dimensional representation can be used for evaluating the SQE squeezing on approximate states. Additionally, the ground states of such truncated operators posses the optimal SQE squeezing for any given dimension. Formally, the operator (
3
) truncated into a chosen finite dimensional truncated Fock space
\mathscr{H}_{N}
,
N\in\mathds{N}
can be defined as

\hat{O}^{[N]}(u,\varphi,c)=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\outerproduct{n}{n}\hat{O}(u,\varphi,c)\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\outerproduct{n}{n}.

(16)

In the following, instead of utilizing the analytical expressions for
\innerproduct{x}{n},\innerproduct{p}{n}
to calculate expectation values, we chose to approximate the constituents of
\hat{O}^{[N]}(u,\varphi,c)
;
\hat{O}^{[N]}_{x}(u)
and
\hat{O}^{[N]}_{p}(a,\varphi)
, as matrices in the truncated Fock space. This leads to errors
Provazník et al. 2022
, however, it allows for much faster numerical optimization.

Implementing
\hat{O}^{[N]}_{x}(u)
is straightforward. Since this is an operator formed by fourth powers of
\hat{a}
and
\hat{a}^{{\dagger}}
, we can construct it with the help of the truncated annihilation operator
\hat{a}^{[N+4]}
and then truncate it again.
Representing
\hat{O}_{p}(u,\varphi)
is more difficult, as quadrature eigenkets are not available in truncated Fock spaces. We can, however, utilize its
\hat{p}-
representation, a series of delta distributions at
p=\frac{(2j-1)\pi-\varphi}{2u},j\in\mathds{Z}
to approximate it as

\displaystyle\tilde{\hat{O}}_{p}(u,\varphi)

\displaystyle=\frac{u}{\sqrt{\pi}}{(k)}_{\frac{1}{2}}{\left[\sin(u\hat{p}+\frac{\varphi}{2})\right]}^{2k}

(17)

where
{(x)}_{m}=\frac{\Gamma(x+1)}{\Gamma(x-m+1)}
is a generalization of the falling factorial and
k\in\mathds{N}
. We choose
k=100
in the following numerical work, see Appendix
A
for more details.

Utilizing the finite dimensional approximations of
\hat{O}
in truncated Fock spaces, we can find the finite dimensional boundary

\displaystyle F_{\mathrm{min}}^{[N]}(z)

\displaystyle=\min_{\ket{\psi}\in S_{z}}F,

(18)

\displaystyle S_{z}

\displaystyle=\left\{\ket{\psi}\in\mathscr{H}_{N}:\langle\psi|\hat{O}(u,\varphi,c)|\psi\rangle=z\right\},

as the Pareto frontier simultaneously minimizing both the virtual interaction fidelity and SQE squeezing. Specifically, we have utilized the NSGA-II algorithm
Deb et al. 2002
to effectively find the frontier, our CUDA optimized Pymoo
Blank and Deb 2020
implementation is available on GitHub
Kuchař 2025
. The SQE squeezing is expressed in decibels as

\xi_{\mathrm{SQE}}=10\log\left(\frac{\expectationvalue{\hat{O}^{[N]}(u,\varphi,c)}}{\min_{G}\expectationvalue{\hat{O}(u,\varphi,c)}}\right).

(19)

The
F_{\mathrm{min}}
boundaries for various combinations of
N,c
are visualized in Fig.
2
. For each of the bounds, the filled area corresponds to the subspace where pure states can exist.

SQE states are a crucial resource in breeding protocols for preparation of GKP state approximations

\displaystyle=\max_{\ket{\psi}\in S_{z}}\xi_{\mathrm{GKP_{0}}},

(22)

\displaystyle S_{z}

where

m^{2}

IV
Optimal SQE Approximations

The analysis indicates that SQE squeezing can serve both as a witness of non-Gaussianity and as a measure of how well any state approximates a chosen superposition of quadrature eigenstates. We can utilize the measure-like properties of nonlinear squeezing to obtain optimal SQE approximations in truncated Fock spaces, which correspond simply to the ground states of (
16
) for any given dimension
N
. Examples of such states are plotted in Fig.
4
.

Given that these eigenstates are non-Gaussian and symmetric, their stellar rank
Chabaud et al. 2020
is

r^{\star}=\begin{cases}N-2&\text{for }N\bmod 2=0,\\
N-1&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(23)

Therefore, they represent optimal target approximations of superpositions of quadrautre eigenstates in quantum state preparation schemes with
r^{\star}
single photon operations.

V
Conclusion

We introduced a family of operators exploiting the properties of superpositions of quadrature eigenstates and established their usefulness within the framework of nonlinear squeezing. SQE squeezing serves both as a witness and measure of non-Gaussianity, and allows us to evaulate how well any given state approximates a chosen symmetric superposition of quadrature eigenstates.

Through numerical optimization, we discovered lower bounds for virtual interaction fidelity, quantifying how well a state with given SQE squeezing behaves as a resource for the non-unitary operation
\hat{D}_{x}(u)+\hat{D}_{x}(-u)
when compared to a superposition of quadrature eigenstates, the natural resource for such operation.

In a similar fashion, we obtained upper bounds for GKP squeezing in a breeding protocol, which suggest that states with lower SQE squeezing generally produce better approximations of GKP states in such protocols.

Furthermore, SQE squeezing allows us to construct optimal approximations for superpositions of quadrature eigenstates in truncated Fock spaces. These states represent optimal targets for experimental preparation with limited resources, effectively connecting theory with experiment and suggesting how well these intrinsically unphysical states can be approximated in the near future.

Appendix A
Truncated Fock space approximations

In order to allow for effective optimizations, we approximate the constituents of
\hat{O}(u,\varphi,c)
as finite dimensional matrices expressed in the truncated Fock basis
\left\{\ket{0},\ket{1},\dots,\ket{N-1}\right\}
, where the truncated Fock space
\mathscr{H}_{N}
,
N\in\mathds{N}
, is the linear hull of this basis. Given that both
\hat{O}_{x}(u)
and
\hat{O}_{p}(a,\varphi)
are defined in infinite dimensional spaces (see (
4a
) and (
4b
)), truncating these operators to finite dimensional matrices is generally associated with an error. The following is an analysis of the most dominant error in our approach.

To implement
\hat{O}^{[N]}_{x}(u)
, we can directly utilize the truncated form of
\hat{a}
as outlined in the text. This leads to an excellent approximation with practically zero error in comparison to the error in
\hat{O}^{[N]}_{p}(a,\varphi)
and there is no reason to investigate further.

In the case of
\hat{O}^{[N]}_{p}(a,\varphi)
, we are faced with the construction of quadrature eigenkets in a truncated Fock space. One could consider utilizing the expansion

\ket{p_0}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\ket{n}\!\!\innerproduct{n}{p_0}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{{(-i)}^{n}e^{-{p_{0}}^{2}}}{\sqrt{2^{n}n!\sqrt{\pi}}}H_{n}(p_{0})\ket{n},

(24)

where
H_{n}
is the
n-
th Hermite polynomial, and truncating it to a finite sum capped at
n=N
. This method unfortunately fails completely for some
N
. While we did not investigate the cause thoroughly, our understanding of the eigenket
\ket{p_0}
as the infinite squeezing limit of a squeezed state with
\expectationvalue{\hat{p}}=p_{0}
points to the fact that while (
24
) converges to the eigenstate, its truncated form does not in any way guarantee
\expectationvalue{\hat{p}}=p_{0}
. This leads to offsets of the individual projectors which cause the operator to behave in unintended ways.

To mitigate this issue, we have instead decided to approximate the infinite dimensional operator
\hat{O}_{p}(u,\varphi)
using a different infinite dimensional operator
\tilde{\hat{O}}_{p}(u,\varphi)
, which can then be truncated in a similar manner to
\hat{O}^{[N]}_{x}(u)
. Its form

\tilde{\hat{O}}_{p}(u,\varphi)=\frac{u}{\sqrt{\pi}}{(k)}_{\frac{1}{2}}{\left[\sin(u\hat{p}+\frac{\varphi}{2})\right]}^{2k},

(25)

is based on the fact that
\hat{O}_{p}(u,\varphi)
projects only onto
p=\frac{(2j-1)\pi-\varphi}{2u},j\in\mathds{Z}
. The limit
\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}{\left[\sin(u\hat{p}+\frac{\varphi}{2})\right]}^{2k}
is positive precisely at these points while being zero everywhere else. The multiplicative term ensures normalization around each of the peaks, i.e.

\int_{\frac{j\pi-\varphi/2}{u}}^{\frac{j\pi-\varphi/2}{u}+\frac{\pi}{u}}\frac{u}{\sqrt{\pi}}{(k)}_{\frac{1}{2}}{\left[\sin(up+\frac{\varphi}{2})\right]}^{2k}\mathrm{d}p=1,\quad\forall j\in\mathds{Z}.

(26)

Given the identical support and normalization, we can conclude that
\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}\tilde{\hat{O}}_{p}(u,\varphi)=\hat{O}_{p}(u,\varphi)
. This limit acts as a justification for approximating
\hat{O}_{p}(u,\varphi)
in this manner, but does not find much use in practice, as numerical issues appear with large
k
.

In this case, the dominant error is the error associated with the approximation itself and not the truncation error, see Fig.
5
. Given that the approximation numerically collapses for large
k
, but also requires large
k
to work, we seek an optimal value that allows us to obtain reasonably accurate results for the optimizations we perform. Specifically, given the dimensionality of the truncated Fock spaces used and an analysis of the errors in Fig.
5
, we settled for
k=100
. Additionally, when looking for
F_{\mathrm{min}}
(see Fig.
2
), we utilized this data to choose
u=3
.

A descriptive cross-sectional study of polysomnographic characteristics and clinical significance in Chinese patients with clinically suspected Obstructive Sleep Apnea-Hypopnea Syndrome (OSAHS)
Authors: Wenxia Huang, Yu Huang, Gengtian Liang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo explore polysomnographic features and clinical implications of suspected OSAHS patients in central China.ResultsOf 100 enrolled patients, 81% were males, with a mean age of 41.6 ± 13.5 years. 76% had overweight or obesity and the mean BMI was 27.4 ± 4.9 kg/m², while 44% suffered severe OSAHS. Introduction

Obstructive Sleep Apnea-Hypopnea Syndrome (OSAHS) is a common sleep breathing disorder marked by repeated upper airway obstruction, intermittent hypoxemia, sleep disruption and daytime drowsiness [1]. Its prevalence varies ethnically, reaching 9–38% among middle-aged adults globally and 4.6–14.6% in China, with an upward trend linked to lifestyle changes and obesity [2, 3]. OSAHS elevates risks of hypertension, coronary heart disease, diabetes and stroke, doubling or tripling cardiovascular event risks and raising all-cause mortality by 37% if untreated [4, 5]. Polysomnography (PSG) serves as the diagnostic gold standard, with AHI, sleep structure and oxygen saturation applied to diagnosis and severity grading [6, 7]. Existing researches mainly target Western populations, while local OSAHS PSG features remain insufficiently explored [8]. Chinese patients tend to present comparable disease severity at lower BMI levels [9], and relevant domestic studies often enroll limited subjects and indicators [10]. Therefore, comprehensive analysis on Chinese patient cohorts is necessary.

We hypothesized that polysomnographic characteristics of clinically suspected OSAHS patients in central China differ from other Chinese and Western cohorts. Chinese patients typically develop OSAHS at a lower BMI than Western individuals, leading to distinct sleep architecture and oxygenation patterns. Regional variations in diet, lifestyle, craniofacial structure and referral patterns may also cause differences in AHI, sleep structure and oxygen saturation. Moreover, key polysomnographic parameters are closely associated with OSAHS severity and can support clinical severity assessment in the local Chinese population.

Methods

Study population

Select 100 patients who were suspected of having OSAHS between January 2024 and December 2025. Inclusion criteria: age ≥ 18 years, valid PSG ≥ 6 h, complete data. Exclusion criteria: central sleep apnea (central apnea index ≥ 5/h), severe pulmonary or neuromuscular disease, upper airway surgery within 6 months, previous positive airway pressure therapy.

Polysomnography protocol and definitions

PSG was performed using Alice 6 (Philips Respironics) and scored manually by two certified technologists according to AASM 2012 criteria [11]. OSAHS severity was graded by AHI: normal < 5, mild 5–14, moderate 15–29, severe ≥ 30 events/h.

Data collection

Demographics, anthropometrics, comorbidities, and PSG parameters were extracted from medical records. BMI was calculated as kg/m²; overweight ≥ 24, obesity ≥ 28 per Chinese guidelines [12].

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 26.0 and Python 3.9. Pearson or Spearman correlation was used as appropriate; one-way ANOVA with Tukey post-hoc test was used for group comparisons. A p-value < 0.05 was considered significant.

Notably, given the exploratory cross-sectional design and the limited sample size (n = 100), multivariable regression models were not constructed to identify independent associated factors, as this sample volume is insufficient to ensure stability and reliability of multivariable adjusted results. All findings from correlation and group comparisons are interpreted as associations rather than causal relationships.

Results

Study population characteristics

This study enrolled 100 patients with suspected OSAHS. Males accounted for 81.0%, with an average age of 41.6 ± 13.5 years. 76.0% were overweight or obese, and the mean BMI was 27.4 ± 4.9 kg/m². Comorbidities occurred in 40.0% of cases, dominated by hypertension at 24.0%. Patients with moderate to severe OSAHS had higher BMI, older age and higher hypertension incidence (all p < 0.05) (Table 1).

OSAHS severity distribution

Based on AHI, 16.0% were normal, 21.0% mild, 19.0% moderate, and 44.0% severe. The mean AHI was 32.4 ± 27.8 events/h, and 63.0% of patients had moderate-to-severe OSAHS (Fig. 1).

Sleep structure characteristics

Mean NREM sleep was 82.0 ± 6.8%, REM sleep 18.0 ± 6.8%. Deep sleep (N3) accounted for 11.6 ± 9.9% and light sleep 70.5 ± 12.2%. The mean respiratory event time percentage was 24.2 ± 18.4% (Table 2).

Respiratory event characteristics

Mean total respiratory events were 233.1 ± 183.1 per night. Obstructive apnea accounted for 90.9% of all apnea events, while central and mixed apnea accounted for 4.5% and 4.6%, respectively. Mean longest apnea duration was 48.3 ± 24.6 s, and longest hypopnea was 32.7 ± 18.9 s (Table 3).

Oxygen saturation and heart rate characteristics

Mean minimum SpO₂ was 75.2 ± 8.3%. Minimum SpO₂ was lower during REM sleep (77.7 ± 9.5%) than NREM sleep (78.7 ± 7.4%), and highest during deep sleep (85.2 ± 10.5%). Mean heart rate was lowest during deep sleep (Table 4).

Correlation analysis

Correlation between demographic factors and AHI

BMI was positively correlated with AHI (r = 0.299, p = 0.003), and this correlation was significant in males (r = 0.333, p = 0.002) but not in females (r = 0.072, p = 0.770) (Fig. 2).

Relationship between BMI and AHI. Scatter plot reveals positive BMI-AHI correlation in overall population (r = 0.299, p = 0.003) and males (r = 0.333, p = 0.002), with no significance in females (r = 0.072, p = 0.770)

Males presented higher BMI and AHI but younger age compared with females. BMI correlated more closely with OSA severity in males, while female AHI changes may be affected by other factors. Limited female cases restrict result interpretation. Estrogen protection on upper airway function may account for gender discrepancy, which needs further validation in larger hormone-stratified research [13].

Correlation between sleep structure and AHI

A significant negative correlation was observed between deep sleep percentage and AHI (r = − 0.331, p = 0.001), indicating that more severe OSAHS was associated with less deep sleep. No significant correlation was found between REM sleep percentage and AHI (r = − 0.087, p = 0.382) or between light sleep percentage and AHI (r = 0.203, p = 0.042, marginal significance).

Correlation between oxygen saturation and AHI

Minimum SpO₂ was negatively correlated with AHI in most sleep stages: lower SpO₂ indicated higher AHI and more severe OSA (all p < 0.001). REM sleep showed the strongest correlation (r = − 0.683), followed by overall (r = − 0.622), NREM (r = − 0.551), and light sleep (r = − 0.550). Deep sleep showed no significant correlation (r = − 0.101, p = 0.316) (Fig. 3). This result should be interpreted cautiously, as severe OSAHS may reduce deep sleep and bias averaging effects, precluding mechanistic conclusions.

Discussion

This study presents comprehensive single-center PSG data of suspected OSAHS patients in central China, supplementing regional clinical characteristics [3]. The cohort mainly consists of middle-aged overweight males with a high severe case ratio, conforming to tertiary hospital admission features [14]. Its core traits accord with global findings, including male predominance, BMI-AHI positive correlation, decreased deep sleep and aggravated hypoxia along with disease progression [15]. Patients showed lower BMI yet severe illness compared with Western populations, matching Asian epidemiological traits, and possessed a higher male-female ratio, proving ethnic and regional disparities in OSAHS manifestations [16]. This study adopted more sleep indicators and gender-based correlation analysis than previous domestic researches [17]. Severe case proportion and obstructive apnea proportion were consistent with other Chinese cohorts, and the younger age and fewer comorbidities reflected inherent population demographic traits.

The male-to-female ratio reached 4.3:1, exceeding Western data [15] and matching the male dominance seen in domestic research [17]. Lower medical consultation willingness and upper airway anatomical differences may account for this gap [18]. The average BMI was 27.4 ± 4.9 kg/m², lower than Western patients [19], yet 76% of subjects had excess weight, verifying the Asian paradox [16]. BMI positively correlated with AHI (r = 0.316, p = 0.001) and served as a vital severity indicator [20]. The correlation existed only in males. Limited female cases and estrogen protection on airway function likely caused such discrepancy. Larger hormone-stratified studies are needed to explore relevant influencing factors.

Sleep architecture and respiratory patterns showed evident anomalies. Deep sleep dropped to 11.6 ± 9.9% with increased light sleep, suggesting recurrent respiratory disturbance-induced fragmentation [21]. Obstructive apnea accounted for 90.9% of events, confirming it as the predominant OSAHS phenotype in Chinese population [22]. Minimum SpO₂ strongly negatively correlated with AHI (r = − 0.620, p < 0.001), supporting hypoxia as a reliable severity marker [23]. Reduced airway muscle tone in REM sleep aggravates hypoxia and cardiovascular risks, making REM oxygen level critical for assessment. The absence of ODI, mean nocturnal SpO₂ and CT90 analysis limits comprehensive evaluation of hypoxia-related cardiometabolic damage.

Taken together, middle-aged overweight males deserve priority screening. Minimum SpO₂ correlates well with OSAHS severity for auxiliary screening, while PSG remains the diagnostic gold standard [6, 7]. Weight reduction reduces AHI [24], and CPAP ameliorates oxygenation and sleep quality [25]. Hypertension screening is essential given its close connection with OSAHS [4, 26]. Stage-specific minimum SpO₂ can effectively reflect hypoxia severity despite incomplete hypoxic index analysis. These PSG features provide a certain degree of guidance for the clinical diagnosis and treatment planning of patients with OSAHS as well as those suspected of having OSAHS. These PSG features record the observed characteristics of OSAHS in this group and highlight the potential associations for hypothesis-driven research.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study analyzed PSG data from 100 suspected OSAHS Chinese patients. Severe cases accounted for 44.0%, males 81.0%, and overweight/obesity 76.0%. BMI, minimum SpO₂ and deep sleep ratio were closely correlated with disease severity. Obstructive apnea dominated respiratory events, and hypoxemia reflected OSAHS severity.

The results depict local OSAHS features and offer risk stratification clues. Targeted screening for overweight middle-aged males and standard PSG examination help detect severe cases early. Further large multicenter prospective studies with multivariate analysis are required to validate outcomes and formulate clinical diagnostic and therapeutic criteria for Chinese patients.

Limitations

This study has limitations. As a single-center cross-sectional study with 100 cases, its generalizability is limited. Small sample size ruled out multivariate analysis, only revealing correlations instead of causality. Insignificant BMI-AHI correlation in females awaits larger verification, possibly influenced by hormones. Tertiary hospital enrollment may cause selection bias favoring severe cases. Large multicenter prospective studies are needed to identify independent risk factors and confirm causal links.

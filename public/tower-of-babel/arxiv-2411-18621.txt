Yukawa-Lorentz Symmetry of Tilted Non-Hermitian Dirac Semimetals at Quantum Criticality
Sergio Pino-Alarcón, Vladimir Juričić

Abstract
Dirac materials, hosting linearly dispersing quasiparticles at low energies, exhibit an emergent Lorentz symmetry close to a quantum critical point (QCP) separating a semimetallic state from a strongly-coupled gapped insulator or superconductor. This feature appears quite robust even in open Dirac systems coupled to an environment, featuring non-Hermitian (NH) Dirac fermions: close to a strongly coupled QCP, a Yukawa-Lorentz symmetry emerges with a unique terminal velocity for both the fermion and the bosonic order parameter fluctuations. The system can either retain non-Hermiticity or completely decouple from the environment, recovering Hermiticity as an emergent phenomenon. We show that such Yukawa-Lorentz symmetry emerges at quantum criticality even when the NH Dirac Hamiltonian includes a tilt term at the lattice scale. As demonstrated by a leading-order ϵ = 3 − d \epsilon=3-d expansion near the d = 3 d=3 upper critical dimension, a tilt term becomes irrelevant near the QCP separating the NH Dirac semimetal and a gapped (insulating or superconducting) phase. This behavior extends to linear-in-momentum non-tilt perturbations, introducing velocity anisotropy for the Dirac quasiparticles, which also becomes irrelevant at the QCP. These predictions can be numerically tested in quantum Monte Carlo lattice simulations of NH Hubbard-like models hosting low-energy NH tilted Dirac fermions.

Introduction

Dirac materials, hosting linearly dispersing quasiparticles at low energies, exhibit an emergent relativistic-like Lorentz symmetry due to the quasiparticles’ linear energy-momentum dispersion, with the Fermi velocity, typically a few hundred times smaller than the velocity of light, playing the role of the velocity of light

Recent studies strongly suggest that this scenario also extends to the non-Hermitian (NH) Dirac systems

However, when such a short-range interaction, effectively described by the Yukawa coupling, is strong enough, the system undergoes a quantum-phase transition with the effective Fermi velocity of the NH Dirac fermions and the bosonic order parameter (OP) excitations reaching a common value at a QCP. The fate of the coupling with the environment, however, depends on the canonical commutation relations between the OP and the Dirac mass matrix
M

11

M

On the other hand, in the case of an anticommuting mass (ACM) OP, the system completely decouples from the environment, thereby restoring the usual Lorentz symmetry, with concomitant emergence of the Hermiticity. Such behavior of NH (Hermitian) Dirac fermions suggests ubiquity of the emergent Yukawa-Lorentz (Lorentz) symmetry at QCPs, despite being absent at the UV scale, and irrespective of the symmetry-breaking perturbations in the bare (lattice) Hamiltonian. This is also further supported by the emergence of this symmetry for birefringent NH Dirac fermions, featuring different velocities, therefore breaking Lorentz symmetry at the bare level
[
12

From the point of view of the Dirac Hamiltonian, adding an operator linear in a single component of the momentum represents its minimal extension that breaks the rotational, and therefore also Lorentz symmetry, which is, at the same time, marginal close to the semimetallic ground state. To this end, we consider the simplest scenario: a Hermitian tilt applied to an NH Dirac fermion. Such a minimal model describing tilted NH Dirac fermions may describe an effective dynamics of the tilted Dirac materials coupled to an external environment, such as a (fermionic or bosonic) quasiparticles’ bath, with a direct connection between a microscopic and effective model to be established in future. Our main result is the emergence of the Yukawa-Lorentz symmetry in the vicinity of the strongly coupled QCP separating the NH Dirac semimetallic phase from an insulating CCM phase since the tilt term becomes

This further motivates the analysis of the quantum-critical behavior using the RG formalism, which includes the effects of the fluctuations in the quantum-critical region, ultimately uncovering the Yukawa-Lorentz symmetry for the quantum-critical tilted NH Dirac fermions. Furthermore, the fate of the non-Hermiticity depends on the algebra of the NH Hamiltonian and the OP, more precisely, whether the OP belongs to the ACM or CCM class, as shown in Fig.

In the former case, the system completely decouples from the environment, therefore restoring the hermiticity, while for the latter, the system remains coupled to the environment while displaying an emergent Yukawa-Lorentz symmetry. For completeness, we also consider a term linear in momentum that neither commutes nor anticommutes with the NH Hamiltonian, which introduces a velocity anisotropy, and as we demonstrate, it also becomes irrelevant close to the QCP and eventually vanishes (Fig.

Altogether, our results support a scenario of the Yukawa-Lorentz symmetry being a universal feature of quantum-critical NH Dirac fermions. This prediction should be tested in the numerical simulations, such as quantum Monte Carlo, for instance, in the NH Hubbard model with the asymmetric hoppings together with the tilt term implemented on the honeycomb lattice, analogously to the Hubbard model for the tilt-free NH Dirac fermions
[

II
Minimal noninteracting tilted non-Hermitian Dirac Hamiltonian

We start by considering a minimal effective low-energy Hamiltonian-like NH Dirac operator
H_{\textrm{NH}}
in two spatial dimensions (
d=2
) which is constructed from the corresponding Hermitian Dirac operator by invoking the mass terms, such that the resulting anti-Hermitian piece of the total (Lorentz-symmetric) NH Dirac operator is linear in momentum
[
11
]
. The Hermitian Dirac Hamiltonian reads as

\mathcal{H}_{D}=\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\Psi_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}H_{\textrm{D}}\Psi_{\mathbf{k}}
, where

H_{\textrm{D}}=v_{\textrm{H}}\quantity(\Gamma_x k_x + \Gamma_y k_y)=v_{\textrm{H}}h_{0},

(1)

with
v_{\textrm{H}}
as the Hermitian velocity of the Dirac quasiparticles, the Hermitian matrices
\Gamma_{x}=\sigma_{1}\otimes\tau_{3}
and
\Gamma_{y}=\sigma_{2}\otimes\tau_{0}
, chosen here explicitly in this representation for convenience, are mutually anticommuting matrices satisfying the Clifford algebra
\{\Gamma_{i},\Gamma_{j}\}=2\delta_{ij}
,
i,j=1,2
,
\mathbf{k}
is the momentum, and the form of the Dirac spinor
\Psi_{\mathbf{k}}
depends on the microscopic details of the system, which is in the following assumed to be four-dimensional, as for instance for the spinless electrons in graphene, with two sublattice and two valley degrees of freedom. The Pauli matrices
(\sigma_{0},{\bm{\sigma}})
and
(\tau_{0},{\bm{\tau}})
act in the sublattice and valley space, respectively.

To construct the NH counterpart of the Dirac operator in Eq. (
1
), we invoke a Hermitian matrix
M
, corresponding to a mass term for the Hermitian Dirac operator, and therefore

\{M,H_{\textrm{D}}\}=0
. As such, for any mass matrix, the operator
MH_{\textrm{D}}
is anti-Hermitian,
(MH_{\textrm{D}})^{\dagger}=-MH_{\textrm{D}}
. Using this property, we define he minimal NH Dirac operator as
[
11
]

H^{0}_{\textrm{NH}}=(v_{\textrm{H}}+v_{\textrm{NH}}M)h_{0}=v_{\textrm{H}}(1+\beta M)h_{0}\,

(2)

where
v_{\textrm{NH}}
is the NH velocity parameter, and
\beta=v_{\textrm{NH}}/v_{\textrm{H}}
(always real).
Then, the spectrum of the NH operator is
E_{\textrm{NH}}=\pm v_{\textrm{F}}\absolutevalue{\vb{k}}
, and is purely real (imaginary) when
|\beta|<1
(
|\beta|>1
), where
v_{\textrm{F}}=v_{\textrm{H}}\sqrt{1-\beta^{2}}
is the effective Fermi velocity of the NH Dirac fermions. For concreteness, we here choose the mass matrix
M=\sigma_{1}\otimes\tau_{1}
, and the velocity parameter
|\beta|<1
, with the NH Dirac operator thus featuring a purely real spectrum. Our results are, however, independent of the matrix representation.

As the last step, to obtain the minimal tilted NH Dirac Hamiltonian, we also add a Hermitian term which is linear in one of the components of the momentum
[
14
]
, taken to be
k_{x}
for concreteness,

H_{\textrm{t}}^{0}=\alpha v_{\textrm{H}}Tk_{x},

(3)

with the matrix
T
such that
[T,\Gamma_{i}]=0
(
i=x,y
) and
[T,\beta]=0
, corresponding to the tilted Dirac cones. Depending on the spatial symmetries, particularly, the valley exchange symmetry, represented by
(k_{x},k_{y})\to(-k_{x},k_{y})
and
S_{V}=\sigma_{0}\otimes\tau_{1}
, we can consider an antisymmetric and symmetric tilt with
T_{\textrm{AS}}=\sigma_{0}\otimes\tau_{0}
and
T_{\textrm{S}}=\sigma_{2}\otimes\tau_{2}
, respectively, which is odd and even under this symmetry, respectively. The Hamiltonian of the minimal model for the NH tilted Dirac semimetal then reads,

H_{\textrm{NH}}^{\textrm{tilt}}(\mathbf{k})=(v_{\textrm{H}}+v_{\textrm{NH}}M)h_{0}+\alpha v_{\textrm{H}}Tk_{x}.

(4)

Notice that this Hamiltonian preserves and breaks sublattice exchange symmetry,
(k_{x},k_{y})\to(k_{x},-k_{y})
and
S_{SL}=\sigma_{1}\otimes\tau_{0}
for any
\beta\neq 0
, in the case of the antisymmetric and symmetric tilt term, respectively.

For completeness, we also consider the NH Dirac Hamiltonian that includes the term
H_{\textrm{t}}^{0}
in Eq. (
3
) with the matrix
T=T_{{\rm VA}}=\sigma_{1}\otimes\tau_{0}
, which is odd and even under the valley and sublattice exchange, respectivrly, and therefore transforms the same as the antisymmetric tilt under these symmetries. However, this term neither commutes nor anticommutes with the Hermitian Dirac Hamiltonian [Eq. (
1
)],
[\Gamma_{x},T_{{\rm VA}}]=0,\,\{\Gamma_{y},T_{{\rm VA}}\}=0
, and thus introduces a velocity anisotropy in the NH Dirac electronic bands, see Eq. (
7
).

In the case of antisymmetric tilt, the spectrum of the NH tilted Hamiltonian operator features two doubly degenerate bands

E_{\textrm{NH}}^{\textrm{AS}}=\pm v_{\textrm{F}}\absolutevalue{\vb{k}}+\alpha v_{\textrm{H}}k_{x},

(5)

and therefore the tilt acts as an effective (valley-independent) momentum-dependent chemical potential for the NH Dirac fermions. On the other hand, for the symmetric tilt,
the spectrum reads as

E_{\textrm{NH}}^{\textrm{S}}=\pm v_{\textrm{F}}\absolutevalue{\vb{k}}\pm\alpha v_{\textrm{H}}k_{x},

(6)

and acts as an effective momentum-dependent chiral chemical potential, with an opposite sign at the two valleys.

II.1
Density of states

We calculate the DOS in terms of the (Euclidean) fermion Green’s functions given as

\rho(E)=-\frac{1}{\pi}\lim_{\eta\to 0^{+}}\imaginary\quantity{ \Tr G_F(i \omega\to E+i \eta, \vb{k})}.

(13)

Using the form of the Green’s function for the antisymmetric tilt (
8
), we find

\rho_{\textrm{AS}}(E)=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{v_{\textrm{F}}\absolutevalue{E}}{(v_{\textrm{F}}^{2}-\alpha^{2}v_{\textrm{H}}^{2})^{3/2}}=\frac{\absolutevalue{E}}{\pi(v_{F}^{\rm tilt})^{2}}\,\sqrt{\frac{1-\beta^{2}}{1-\alpha^{2}-\beta^{2}}},

(14)

where the effective tilt-dependent Fermi velocity
v_{F}^{\rm tilt}=v_{H}\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}-\beta^{2}}
. In the following, we only consider the subcritically tilted and weak NH case
|\alpha|^{2}+|\beta|^{2}<1
, with pointlike Fermi surfaces and the quasiparticles exhibiting a finite lifetime (type-1). Otherwise, NH Dirac Hamiltonian describes type-2 Weyl/Dirac semimetal
[
14
]
generalized to the NH case when the tilt parameter
|\alpha|>1
, while the non-Hermiticity is weak,
|\beta|<1
, therefore giving rise to the electron- and hole-like Fermi surfaces with the quasiparticles featuring a finite lifetime.
For the case of the symmetric tilt, the DOS takes the form

\rho_{\textrm{S}}(E)=\frac{\absolutevalue{E}}{\pi v_{\textrm{F}}^{2}},

(15)

which is the same as for the non-tilted NH Dirac fermions. Finally, for the velocity-anisotropy [Eq. (
11
)], the DOS reads as

\rho_{\textrm{VA}}(E)=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{\absolutevalue{E}}{v_{\textrm{F}}^{2}-\alpha^{2}v_{\textrm{H}}^{2}}=\frac{\absolutevalue{E}}{\pi(v_{F}^{\rm tilt})^{2}}.

(16)

The plots are shown in Fig.
1
with the technical details shown in the appendix
A.2
.

As we can observe, in all the three cases the DOS retains the linear scaling in energy, with the slope depending on the form of the matrix in Eq. (
2
).
We notice that for the antisymmetric tilt, the DOS explicitly depends on the NH parameter (
\beta
), i.e. it is not solely a function of the effective Fermi velocity,
v_{F}^{\rm tilt}
. Second, the symmetric tilt acts as an effective chiral chemical potential and therefore the contributions from the two valleys cancel out, yielding the tilt-free form of the DOS, Eq, (
15
).
Finally, in case of the velocity anisotropy, the DOS effectively retains the form as in the tilt-free NH Dirac Hamiltonian with the effective velocity being
v_{F}^{\rm tilt}
. When the tilt parameter
\alpha=0
, both antisymmetric tilt and velocity anisotropy yield the same tilt-free form of the DOS, given by Eq. (
15

Such a linear scaling with energy of the DOS,
\rho(E)\sim E
, implies that the dynamical exponent, measuring the relative scaling between the energy and momentum, is
z=1
. The short-range Hubbard-like electron interaction is therefore irrelevant for

d>1

. In turn, the tilted Dirac semimetal remains stable for weak short-range electron-electron interactions. The tendency to a specific ordering at strong coupling then can be deduced from the behavior of the mean-field susceptibility, which we discuss next.

III
Mean-field susceptibilities for the mass orders

The OP that isotropically gaps out hermitian Dirac fermions can be expressed as an
n

\Phi_{j}=\langle\Psi^{\dagger}N_{j}\Psi\rangle

\{H_{\textrm{D}},N_{j}\}=0

(i)
A CCM OP for which
[N_{j},M]=0
, for

\{N_{j},M\}=0
for
j=1\,,\,2\,,\,...\,,n
. We do not consider a mixed class where the OP matrix partially (anti)commutes with the matrix

However, to address the competition between the different instabilities, together with the emergence of the Yukawa-Lorentz symmetry, we need to consider the RG flows of the velocity parameters and of the tilt in the quantum-critical regime. This is even more pertinent given a rather small difference between the mean-field susceptibilities for the two classes of the OPs, for both the asymmetric tilt (Figs.

The rate of the RG flow, as it can be noticed, is slightly faster for the CCM than for the ACM OP in the case of the tilt. Furthermore, for the CCM OP in the case of either tilt or velocity anisotropy, the NH velocity flows to a finite value, while the terminal bosonic velocity and the effective Fermi velocity reaches a common value. Therefore the NH Yukawa-Lorentz symmetry is restored in spite of the symmetry-breaking tilt or velocity anisotropy present in the bare (lattice) theory. In the case of ACM order, the NH velocity parameter vanishes, irrespective of the additional (tilt or velocity anisotropy) term, and therefore the system spontaneously restores hermiticity, and at the same time, conventional Lorentz symmetry.

V
Conclusions

In summary, our analysis of quantum-critical NH tilted Dirac fermions shows that Yukawa-Lorentz symmetry emerges near the strongly coupled QCP separating the NH Dirac semimetallic and insulating phases. This key feature arises from the irrelevance of the tilt term near the QCP, as our leading-order renormalization group calculations explicitly demonstrate, see Eqs. (
34
)-(
36
). Crucially, the fate of non-Hermiticity hinges on the OP class (ACM or CCM), with ACM leading to complete decoupling from the environment and Hermiticity restoration, while CCM maintains coupling to the environment yet exhibits emergent Yukawa-Lorentz symmetry, see also Fig.
5
. Furthermore, a velocity anisotropy term turns out to be irrelevant near the QCP (Fig.
6
). These results strongly suggest that Yukawa-Lorentz symmetry is a universal feature of quantum-critical NH Dirac fermions, a prediction that can be tested through numerical lattice simulations.

For the quantum phase transition from NH tilted Dirac semimetal to an ACM OP, even though, the tilt eventually vanishes, its effect on the observables should be non-negligible, due to the finite system size. On the other hand, in the case of the CCM OP, non-Hermiticity close to the QCP should have observable effects on the correlators through a contribution of the finite NH velocity, which we plan to study in near future.

We would like to emphasize that the NH piece of the Dirac Hamiltonian directly corresponds to the nonreciprocity of the nearest-neighbor hopping on the honeycomb lattice, while the tilt term can be introduced by the uniaxial strain along the zigzag direction, for instance
[
44
]
. However, a remaining open question concerns the precise emergence of these NH terms from coupling to an excitation bath with a specific spectral function. Establishing such a correspondence will corroborate engineering of the NH terms and deserves further investigation.

Finally, we point out that our results should motivate further studies of the NH tilted Dirac fermions, as for instance, their instabilities in the case when they feature a finite-size
Fermi surface with finite-lifetime quasiparticles (NH type-2 Dirac fermions). The studies of the NH tilted Dirac fermions may also provide some valuable insights regarding the relationship between the condensed matter and low-dimensional gravitational systems, particularly concerning the effects of the Hawking radiation that may be captured through the effective NH Dirac operators discussed here.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\quantity[(A_-(i\omega,\bfq))^2 -(B(i\omega,\bfq))^2]\quantity[(\nu-\omega)^2+\vB^2(\vb{k}-\vb{q})^2]},

(57)

where the upper (lower) sign corresponds to CCM (ACM) order.

C.1.1
Fermionic field renormalization

To calculate the fermion field renormalization, we extract the term proportional to the identity matrix in the self-energy, and we set external momentum
{\bf k}=0
,

\displaystyle\Sigma_{00}(i\nu,{\bf k}=0)=-g^{2}n\int[d\omega]\int[d^{d}\mathbf{q}]\,\frac{i\omega A_{+}(i\omega,\mathbf{q})}{\quantity[A_-(i\omega,\bfq))^2 -(B(i\omega,\bfq))^2]\quantity[(\nu-\omega)^2+\vB^2q^2]}.

(58)

The renormalization condition reads

\displaystyle Z_{\psi}=1+\partialderivative{ \Sigma_{00}(i \nu,k )}{(i \nu)}\evaluated{}_{i\nu=0,\bfk=0},

(59)

and after integrating over the Matsubara frequency, we find

\displaystyle\partialderivative{ \Sigma_{00}(i \nu,\bfk)}{(i \nu)}\evaluated{}_{i\nu=0,\bfk=0}

\displaystyle=-g^{2}n\int[d^{d}\mathbf{q}]\,\frac{\alpha v_{\textrm{H}}^{2}q_{x}^{2}+q^{2}(v_{\textrm{B}}+v_{\textrm{F}})^{2}}{2qv_{\textrm{B}}(\alpha^{2}v_{\textrm{H}}^{2}q_{x}^{2}-q^{2}(v_{\textrm{B}}+v_{\textrm{F}})^{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{ng^{2}}{(4\pi)^{2}}G(\alpha,v_{\textrm{B}},v_{\textrm{F}})\int\frac{dq}{q},

(60)

where

\displaystyle G(\alpha,v_{\textrm{B}},v_{\textrm{F}})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}d\theta\frac{\sin\theta(\alpha^{2}v_{\textrm{H}}^{2}\cos^{2}\phi\sin^{2}\theta+(v_{\textrm{B}}+v_{\textrm{F}})^{2})}{v_{\textrm{B}}(-\alpha^{2}v_{\textrm{H}}^{2}\cos^{2}\phi\sin^{2}\theta+(v_{\textrm{B}}+v_{\textrm{F}})^{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{v_{\textrm{B}}((v_{\textrm{B}}+v_{\textrm{F}})^{2}-\alpha^{2}v_{\textrm{H}}^{2})}.

(61)

We then invoke the correspondence between hard cutoff and dimensional regularization,

\displaystyle\int\frac{dq}{q}\to\frac{1}{\epsilon}

(62)

to find for the fermion field renormalization

\displaystyle Z_{\psi}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{ng^{2}}{(4\pi)^{2}\epsilon}G(\alpha,v_{\textrm{B}},v_{\textrm{F}}).

(63)

C.1.2
Renormalization of the fermionic velocities

To obtain renormalization factors for fermionic velocities, we need to extract the terms proportional to the matrices
\Gamma
in the self-energy, Eq. (
57
), and we choose the term proportional to
\Gamma_{x}
to find the renormalization factor for the velocity
v_{\textrm{H}}
,

\Sigma_{\Gamma_{x}}(i\nu,k)=ng^{2}\int[d\omega]\int[d^{d}\mathbf{q}]\,\Bigg[\frac{v_{\textrm{H}}q_{x}(A_{+}(i\omega,\mathbf{q})-2\alpha^{2}v_{\textrm{H}}^{2}q_{x}^{2})}{\quantity[A_-(i\omega,\bfq)^2 -B(i\omega,\bfq)^2]\quantity[(\nu-\omega)^2+\vB^2(\vb{k}-\vb{q})^2]}\Bigg].

(64)

We then obtain

\displaystyle\partialderivative{\Sigma_{\Gamma_x}(i \nu,\bfk)}{(\vh k_x)}\evaluated{}_{i\nu=0,\bfk=0}=\frac{ng^{2}}{(4\pi)^{2}\epsilon}J(\alpha,v_{\textrm{B}},v_{\textrm{F}}),

(65)

with

\displaystyle J(\alpha,v_{\textrm{B}},v_{\textrm{F}})=\frac{1}{\pi}\int[d\phi d\theta]\frac{\cos^{2}\phi\sin^{2}\theta(-\alpha^{2}v_{\textrm{H}}^{2}\cos^{2}\phi\sin^{2}\theta+(v_{\textrm{B}}+v_{\textrm{F}})^{2}(v_{\textrm{F}}+2v_{\textrm{B}}))}{v_{\textrm{B}}v_{\textrm{F}}(-\alpha^{2}v_{\textrm{H}}^{2}\cos^{2}\phi\sin^{2}\theta+(v_{\textrm{B}}+v_{\textrm{F}})^{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\Bigg[\alpha^{2}v_{\textrm{H}}\left(-v_{\textrm{B}}v_{\textrm{F}}^{2}+v_{\textrm{B}}^{2}v_{\textrm{F}}+v_{\textrm{B}}^{3}+\alpha^{2}v_{\textrm{F}}v_{\textrm{H}}^{2}-v_{\textrm{F}}^{3}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-|\alpha|\left(\alpha^{2}v_{\textrm{H}}^{2}\left(v_{\textrm{F}}^{2}-v_{\textrm{B}}^{2}\right)+\left(v_{\textrm{B}}-v_{\textrm{F}}\right)\left(v_{\textrm{B}}+v_{\textrm{F}}\right){}^{3}\right)\cosh^{-1}\left(\frac{v_{\textrm{B}}+v_{\textrm{F}}}{\sqrt{\left(v_{\textrm{B}}+v_{\textrm{F}}\right){}^{2}-\alpha^{2}v_{\textrm{H}}^{2}}}\right)\Bigg]

\displaystyle\quad\times\frac{4}{\alpha^{4}v_{\textrm{B}}v_{\textrm{F}}v_{\textrm{H}}^{3}\left(v_{\textrm{B}}+v_{\textrm{F}}-\alpha v_{\textrm{H}}\right)\left(v_{\textrm{B}}+v_{\textrm{F}}+\alpha v_{\textrm{H}}\right)}.

(66)

The renormalization condition for the Hermitian velocity (
v_{\textrm{H}}
) reads

\displaystyle Z_{\psi}Z_{v_{\textrm{H}}}+\partialderivative{\Sigma_{\Gamma_x}(i \nu,\bfk)}{(\vh k_x)}\evaluated{}_{i\nu,\bfk=0}

\displaystyle=1,

(67)

implying that

\displaystyle Z_{\psi}Z_{v_{\textrm{H}}}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{ng^{2}}{(4\pi)^{2}\epsilon}J(\alpha,v_{\textrm{B}},v_{\textrm{F}}),

(68)

which finally yields the
Z-
factor for the Hermitian velocity,

\displaystyle Z_{v_{\textrm{H}}}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{g^{2}n}{(4\pi)^{2}\epsilon}[G(\alpha,v_{\textrm{B}},v_{\textrm{F}})-J(\alpha,v_{\textrm{B}},v_{\textrm{F}})].

(69)

Notice that the factors
Z_{\psi}
and
Z_{v_{\textrm{H}}}
are independent of the class of the mass orders.

To find the renormalization factor for the NH velocity,
v_{\textrm{NH}}
, we need to consider the terms proportional to the product
\Gamma M
in the self-energy [Eq. (
57
)],

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\Gamma_{x}M}(i\nu,\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=\pm ng^{2}v_{\textrm{NH}}\int[d\omega]\int[d^{d}\mathbf{q}]\,\Bigg[\frac{q_{x}(A_{+}(i\omega,\mathbf{q})-2\alpha^{2}v_{\textrm{H}}^{2}q_{x}^{2})}{\quantity[A_-(i\omega,\bfq)^2 -B(i\omega,\bfq)^2]}

\displaystyle\quad\times\frac{1}{\quantity[(\nu-\omega)^2+\vB^2(\vb{k}-\vb{q})^2]}\Bigg]

(70)

yielding

\displaystyle\partialderivative{\Sigma_{\Gamma_x M}(i \nu, \bfk)}{(\vnh k_x)}\evaluated{}_{i\nu=0, \bfk=0}

\displaystyle=\pm\frac{ng^{2}}{(4\pi)^{2}\epsilon}J(\alpha,v_{\textrm{B}},v_{\textrm{F}}),

(71)

and therefore the
Z-
factor for the NH velocity reads

\displaystyle Z_{v_{\textrm{NH}}}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{g^{2}n}{(4\pi)^{2}\epsilon}[G(\alpha,v_{\textrm{B}},v_{\textrm{F}})\mp J(\alpha,v_{\textrm{B}},v_{\textrm{F}})].

(72)

Notice that the factor
Z_{v_{\textrm{NH}}}
does depend on the class of the mass order, with the upper (lower) sign corresponding to the CCM (ACM) OP.

C.1.3
Renormalization of the tilt parameter

To find the tilt renormalization factor, we extract the terms proportional to
T_{\textrm{AS}}
, and we set
\mathbf{k}=k_{x}\hat{\mathbf{e}}_{x}
in the fermion self-energy in Eq. (
57
),

\displaystyle\Sigma_{T_{\textrm{AS}}}(i\nu,{\bf k})=g^{2}n\int[d\omega]\int[d^{d}\mathbf{q}]\,\frac{\alpha v_{\textrm{H}}q_{x}(-A_{+}(i\omega,q)+2v_{\textrm{F}}^{2}q^{2})}{\quantity[A_-(i\omega,{\bf q}))^2 -(B(i\omega,{\bf q}))^2]\quantity[(\nu-\omega)^2+\vB^2(\vb{k}-\vb{q})^2]}.

(73)

After integrating over Matsubara frequencies, we obtain

\displaystyle\partialderivative{\Sigma_{\Tas}(i \nu,{\bf k} )}{(\vf k_x)}\evaluated{}_{i\nu=0,\bfk=0}=\frac{g^{2}n\alpha}{(4\pi)^{2}\epsilon}I(\alpha,v_{\textrm{B}},v_{\textrm{F}}),

(74)

where, after the angular integrations, we obtain

\displaystyle I(\alpha,v_{\textrm{B}},v_{\textrm{F}})

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{\alpha^{2}v_{\textrm{B}}v_{\textrm{H}}^{3}}\left(\frac{\alpha^{2}v_{\textrm{H}}^{3}-v_{\textrm{F}}v_{\textrm{H}}\left(v_{\textrm{B}}+v_{\textrm{F}}\right)}{\left(v_{\textrm{B}}+v_{\textrm{F}}\right){}^{2}-\alpha^{2}v_{\textrm{H}}^{2}}+\frac{v_{\textrm{F}}}{|\alpha|}\cosh^{-1}\left(\frac{v_{\textrm{B}}+v_{\textrm{F}}}{\sqrt{\left(v_{\textrm{B}}+v_{\textrm{F}}\right){}^{2}-\alpha^{2}v_{\textrm{H}}^{2}}}\right)\right).

(75)

The renormalization condition for the tilt parameter reads

\displaystyle Z_{\psi}Z_{\alpha}Z_{v_{\textrm{H}}}\alpha v_{\textrm{H}}k_{x}+v_{\textrm{H}}k_{x}\partialderivative{\Sigma_{\Tas}(i \nu,{\bf k} )}{(\vh k_x)}\evaluated{}_{i\nu,{\bf k}=0}

\displaystyle=\alpha v_{\textrm{H}}k_{x},

(76)

and we find

\displaystyle Z_{\psi}Z_{\alpha}Z_{v_{\textrm{H}}}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{1}{\alpha}\partialderivative{\Sigma_{\Tas}(i \nu,{\bf k})}{(\vh k_x)}\evaluated{}_{i\nu=0,k=0}=1-\frac{g^{2}n}{(4\pi)^{2}\epsilon}I(\alpha,v_{\textrm{B}},v_{\textrm{F}}),

(77)

which implies the form of the renormalization parameter for the tilt

\displaystyle Z_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{g^{2}n}{(4\pi)^{2}\epsilon}[J(\alpha,v_{\textrm{B}},v_{\textrm{F}})-I(\alpha,v_{\textrm{B}},v_{\textrm{F}})].

(78)

C.2
Bosonic self-energy

We here calculate the bosonic self-energy, with the Feynman diagram shown in Fig.
4
(b). To facilitate the calculation, we furthermore take
\mathbf{k}=k_{x}\hat{\mathbf{e}}_{x}
, so that the self-energy reads

\Pi(i\nu,{\bf k})=-\frac{g^{2}}{2}\int[d\omega]\int[d^{d}\mathbf{q}]\,\Tr\quantity[N_i G_F(i \omega,{\bf q})N_i G_F(i\nu+i \omega,{\bf k}+{\bf q})].

(79)

Using this expression for the bosonic self-energy, we subsequently calculate the bosonic field renormalization and the bosonic velocity renormalization factors for both the CCM and ACM OPs.

C.2.1
Bosonic field renormalization

The boson field renormalization is calculated using:

\displaystyle\partialderivative{\Pi(i\nu,{\bf k})}{\nu^2}\evaluated{}_{i\nu,{\bf k}=0}=

\displaystyle-\frac{g^{2}n}{4\pi^{2}\epsilon}F(\alpha,v_{\textrm{B}},v_{\textrm{F}}).

(80)

For the CCM order, we find

\displaystyle F_{\textrm{CCM}}(\alpha,v_{\textrm{B}},v_{\textrm{F}})=\frac{1}{4}\int[d\phi d\theta]\frac{\csc^{2}(\theta)\sec^{2}(\phi)}{16}\Bigg(\frac{4\sin^{2}(\theta)\cos^{2}(\phi)+2}{v_{\textrm{F}}^{3}}

(81)

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{2\sin^{2}(\theta)\cos^{2}(\phi)-1}{\left(v_{\textrm{F}}-\alpha\sin(\theta)v_{\textrm{H}}\cos(\phi)\right){}^{3}}+\frac{2\sin^{2}(\theta)\cos^{2}(\phi)-1}{\left(v_{\textrm{F}}+\alpha\sin(\theta)v_{\textrm{H}}\cos(\phi)\right){}^{3}}\right)\Bigg),

which further simplifies to

\displaystyle F_{\textrm{CCM}}(\alpha,v_{\textrm{B}},v_{\textrm{F}})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{16v_{\textrm{F}}^{3}\left(\frac{\alpha^{2}v_{\textrm{H}}^{2}}{v_{\textrm{F}}^{2}}-1\right)^{2}}\left(\frac{3|\alpha|v_{\textrm{H}}\left(v_{\textrm{F}}^{2}-\alpha^{2}v_{\textrm{H}}^{2}\right)^{2}}{v_{\textrm{F}}^{5}}\ln\left(\frac{2v_{\textrm{F}}}{|\alpha|v_{\textrm{H}}+v_{\textrm{F}}}-1\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\frac{v_{\textrm{H}}^{4}}{v_{\textrm{F}}^{4}}-\frac{14\alpha^{2}v_{\textrm{H}}^{2}}{v_{\textrm{F}}^{2}}+8\right).

(82)

For the ACM case, following the analogous steps, we obtain

\displaystyle F_{\textrm{ACM}}(\alpha,v_{\textrm{B}},v_{\textrm{F}})=\frac{v_{\textrm{H}}}{8v_{\textrm{F}}^{5}\left(\frac{\alpha^{2}v_{\textrm{H}}^{2}}{v_{\textrm{F}}^{2}}-1\right){}^{2}}\Bigg(\alpha v_{\textrm{F}}\left(3\left(\frac{\alpha^{2}v_{\textrm{H}}^{2}}{v_{\textrm{F}}^{2}}-1\right){}^{2}\tanh^{-1}\left(\frac{\alpha v_{\textrm{H}}}{v_{\textrm{F}}}\right)-\frac{\alpha v_{\textrm{H}}}{v_{\textrm{F}}}\right)

\displaystyle+2v_{\textrm{H}}\left(\frac{2\alpha^{4}v_{\textrm{H}}^{4}}{v_{\textrm{F}}^{4}}-\frac{3\alpha^{2}v_{\textrm{H}}^{2}}{v_{\textrm{F}}^{2}}-\frac{3\alpha v_{\textrm{H}}\left(v_{\textrm{F}}^{2}-\alpha^{2}v_{\textrm{H}}^{2}\right){}^{2}}{v_{\textrm{F}}^{5}}\tanh^{-1}\left(\frac{\alpha v_{\textrm{H}}}{v_{\textrm{F}}}\right)+2\right)\Bigg).

(83)

The renormalization condition has the form

\displaystyle Z_{\phi}-\partialderivative{\Pi(i\nu,{\bf k})}{\nu^2}\evaluated{}_{i\nu,{\bf k}=0}=1,

(84)

which yields

\displaystyle Z_{\phi}=1-\frac{g^{2}n}{4\pi^{2}\epsilon}F(\alpha,v_{\textrm{B}},v_{\textrm{F}}).

(85)

Notice that the function
F(\alpha,v_{\textrm{B}},v_{\textrm{F}})
, and therefore also the renormalization factor
Z_{\phi}
depends on the class of the mass order. We, however, do not include this dependence in the last equation explicitly to simplify the notation.

C.2.2
Bosonic velocity renormalization

To find the renormalization factor for the bosonic velocity, we need the term of the order
k^{2}
in the bosonic self-energy

\displaystyle\partialderivative{\Pi(i\nu,{\bf k})}{(v_B^2k^2)}\evaluated{}_{i\nu=0,{\bf k}=0}\equiv\Pi_{v_{\textrm{B}}^{2}k^{2}}(i\nu,\mathbf{k})\evaluated{}_{i\nu,\bfk=0}=

\displaystyle-\frac{ng^{2}N_{F}}{4\pi^{2}\epsilon}C(\alpha,v_{\textrm{B}},v_{\textrm{F}}),

(86)

with the function depending explicitly on the class of the mass order, as we show in the following.

We first consider the CCM case, which yields

\displaystyle C_{\textrm{CCM}}(\alpha,v_{\textrm{B}},v_{\textrm{F}})=-\frac{1}{4}\int[d\phi d\theta]\frac{1}{8v_{\textrm{B}}^{2}\left(v_{\textrm{F}}^{3}-\alpha^{2}v_{\textrm{F}}\sin^{2}(\theta)v_{\textrm{H}}^{2}\cos^{2}(\phi)\right)^{3}}\Bigg(v_{\textrm{F}}^{8}\left(8-12\sin^{2}(\theta)\cos^{2}(\phi)\right)

\displaystyle+\alpha^{2}v_{\textrm{F}}^{6}v_{\textrm{H}}^{2}\left(14\sin^{4}(\theta)\cos^{4}(\phi)+3\sin^{2}(\theta)\cos^{2}(\phi)-9\right)

\displaystyle+\alpha^{4}v_{\textrm{F}}^{4}v_{\textrm{H}}^{4}\left(-14\sin^{6}(\theta)\cos^{6}(\phi)+9\sin^{4}(\theta)\cos^{4}(\phi)+2\sin^{2}(\theta)\cos^{2}(\phi)+2\right)

\displaystyle+\alpha^{6}v_{\textrm{F}}^{2}\sin^{2}(\theta)v_{\textrm{H}}^{6}\cos^{2}(\phi)\left(4\sin^{6}(\theta)\cos^{6}(\phi)-11\sin^{2}(\theta)\cos^{2}(\phi)+1\right)

\displaystyle+\alpha^{8}\sin^{4}(\theta)v_{\textrm{H}}^{8}\cos^{4}(\phi)\left(2\sin^{2}(\theta)\cos^{2}(\phi)+1\right)\Bigg),

(87)

and after the angular integrations, we find

\displaystyle C_{\textrm{CCM}}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{48|\alpha|^{3}v_{\textrm{B}}^{2}v_{\textrm{F}}^{4}v_{\textrm{H}}^{3}}\Bigg(4|\alpha|v_{\textrm{F}}v_{\textrm{H}}\left(2\alpha^{2}v_{\textrm{F}}^{2}v_{\textrm{H}}^{2}-3v_{\textrm{F}}^{4}+3\alpha^{4}v_{\textrm{H}}^{4}\right)

\displaystyle+3\left(-4\alpha^{4}v_{\textrm{F}}^{2}v_{\textrm{H}}^{4}+6\alpha^{2}v_{\textrm{F}}^{4}v_{\textrm{H}}^{2}-2v_{\textrm{F}}^{6}+\alpha^{6}v_{\textrm{H}}^{6}\right)\ln\left(\frac{2v_{\textrm{F}}}{|\alpha|v_{\textrm{H}}+v_{\textrm{F}}}-1\right)\Bigg).

(88)

Similarly, for the ACM case, we obtain

\displaystyle C_{\textrm{ACM}}=\frac{1}{24\alpha^{3}v_{\textrm{B}}^{2}v_{\textrm{F}}^{6}v_{\textrm{H}}^{3}}\Bigg(6v_{\textrm{F}}^{8}\tanh^{-1}\left(\frac{\alpha v_{\textrm{H}}}{v_{\textrm{F}}}\right)-6\alpha v_{\textrm{F}}^{7}v_{\textrm{H}}-6\left(3\alpha^{2}+2\right)v_{\textrm{F}}^{6}v_{\textrm{H}}^{2}\tanh^{-1}\left(\frac{\alpha v_{\textrm{H}}}{v_{\textrm{F}}}\right)

\displaystyle+4\alpha\left(2\alpha^{2}+3\right)v_{\textrm{F}}^{5}v_{\textrm{H}}^{3}+12\alpha^{2}\left(\alpha^{2}+3\right)v_{\textrm{F}}^{4}v_{\textrm{H}}^{4}\tanh^{-1}\left(\frac{\alpha v_{\textrm{H}}}{v_{\textrm{F}}}\right)-6\alpha^{3}\left(\alpha^{2}+2\right)v_{\textrm{F}}^{3}v_{\textrm{H}}^{5}

\displaystyle 6\alpha^{6}v_{\textrm{H}}^{8}\tanh^{-1}\left(\frac{\alpha v_{\textrm{H}}}{v_{\textrm{F}}}\right)-3\alpha^{4}\left(\alpha^{2}+8\right)v_{\textrm{F}}^{2}v_{\textrm{H}}^{6}\tanh^{-1}\left(\frac{\alpha v_{\textrm{H}}}{v_{\textrm{F}}}\right)\Bigg).

(89)

The renormalization condition for the bosonic velocity reads

\displaystyle Z_{\phi}Z_{v_{\textrm{B}}^{2}}-\Pi_{v_{\textrm{B}}^{2}k^{2}}(i\nu,\mathbf{k})\evaluated{}_{i\nu,\bfk=0}

\displaystyle=1,

(90)

which yields

\displaystyle Z_{\phi}Z_{v_{\textrm{B}}^{2}}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{ng^{2}N_{F}}{4\pi^{2}\epsilon}C(\alpha,v_{\textrm{B}},v_{\textrm{F}}).

(91)

Finally, we find the renormalization factor for the bosonic velocity

\displaystyle Z_{v_{\textrm{B}}^{2}}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{ng^{2}N_{F}}{4\pi^{2}\epsilon}[F(\alpha,v_{\textrm{B}},v_{\textrm{F}})-C(\alpha,v_{\textrm{B}},v_{\textrm{F}})].

(92)

Again, both functions
F(\alpha,v_{\textrm{B}},v_{\textrm{F}})
and
C(\alpha,v_{\textrm{B}},v_{\textrm{F}})
depend on the class of the mass order, implying the same feature for the renormalization factor
Z_{v_{\textrm{B}}^{2}}
, but we do not include this dependence here explicitly to simplify the notation.

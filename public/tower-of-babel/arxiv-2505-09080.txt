Representable tangent structures for affine schemes
Marcello Lanfranchi, Jean-Simon Pacaud Lemay

Abstract
The category of affine schemes is a tangent category whose tangent bundle functor is induced by Kähler differentials, providing a direct link between algebraic geometry and tangent category theory. Moreover, this tangent bundle functor is represented by the ring of dual numbers. How special is this tangent structure? Is there any other (non-trivial) tangent structure on the category of affine schemes? In this paper, we characterize the representable tangent structures on the category of affine schemes. To do so, we introduce a useful tool, the notion of tangentoids, which are the precisely the objects in a monoidal category that when tensoring with said objects give tangent structures. Therefore, coexponentiable tangentoids induce tangent structures on the opposite category. As such, we first prove that tangentoids in the category of commutative non-unital algebras are equivalent to commutative associative solid non-unital algebras, that is, commutative associative non-unital algebras whose multiplication is an isomorphism. From here, we then explain how representable tangent structures on affine schemes correspond to finitely generated projective commutative associative solid algebras. In particular, for affine schemes over a principal ideal domain, we show that there are precisely two representable tangent structures: the trivial one and the one given by Kähler differentials.

Introduction

A tangent category, as introduced by Rosický in
[
14
]
and later revisited and generalized by Cockett and Cruttwell in
[
3
]
, is a categorical context for (synthetic) differential geometry. Briefly, a tangent category (Definition
2.3
) is a category equipped with an endofunctor
\mathrm{T}
, where for an object
M
we intuitively think of
\mathrm{T}M
as an “abstract tangent bundle” over
M
, which comes equipped with various natural transformations that capture fundamental properties of the classical tangent bundle over a smooth manifold. Tangent category theory is now a well-established field of interest, with a rich literature and has found connections to other areas beyond differential geometry, such as to algebraic geometry, algebra, operad theory, computer science, etc.

An important example of a tangent category, especially in relation to algebraic geometry, is the category of affine schemes (over a fixed commutative and unital ring
R
), which comes equipped with a tangent structure whose tangent bundle functor sends an affine scheme
\mathrm{Spec}(A)
(where
A
is a commutative and unital
R
-algebra) to the symmetric
A
-algebra of the module of Kähler differentials of
A
, i.e.,
\mathrm{T}\mathrm{Spec}(A)=\mathrm{Spec}(\mathsf{SYM}_{A}\Omega_{A})
. The corresponding
R
-algebra is called the “fibré tangente”
[
7
, Definition 16.5.12.I]
or tangent algebra
[
11
, Section 2.6]
of
A
. This tangent structure on affine schemes, first described in
[
3
]
and later extensively analyzed in
[
5
]
, captures some key geometric constructions of algebraic geometry
[
5
,
4
]
. Moreover, this tangent bundle functor is in fact representable, i.e., the tangent bundle of an affine scheme
\mathrm{Spec}(A)
is the left adjoint to the functor
\mathrm{Spec}(R[\varepsilon])\times-
, where
R[\varepsilon]
is the ring of dual numbers over the base ring
R
. More generally, representable tangent structures on categories with finite products are equivalently characterized by a special class of objects called infinitesimal objects (Definition
2.10
), which are exponentiable objects
D
such that
\mathrm{T}(-)=-^{D}
gives a tangent structure
[
3
, Proposition 5.7]
. So
\mathrm{Spec}(R[\varepsilon])
is an infinitesimal object in the category of affine schemes over
R
.

It is natural to wonder how special this tangent structure for affine schemes is. In this paper, we classify representable tangent structures on the category of affine schemes, or equivalently infinitesimal objects in the category of affine schemes. To do so, we first introduce tangentoids (Definition
3.1
), which are objects
D
in a monoidal category equipped with a suitable structure such that the endofunctor
\mathrm{T}(-)=D\otimes-
gives a tangent structure (Proposition
3.7
). Coexponentiable tangentoids (Definition
3.20
) induce tangent structures on the opposite category. Therefore, coexponentiable tangentoids on a category with finite coproducts correspond precisely to infinitesimal objects in the opposite category. As such, classifying infinitesimal objects in the category of affine schemes over
R
is equivalent to classifying coexponentiable tangentoids in the category
\mathsf{CALG}_{R}
of commutative and unital
R
-algebras.

We first show that every tangentoid
D
in
\mathsf{CALG}_{R}
is isomorphic to an
R
-algebra
R\ltimes M
(where
\ltimes
is the square-zero extension) for some
R
-module
M
equipped with a commutative and associative multiplication
\alpha\colon M\otimes M\to M
which is also an isomorphism (Theorem
4.10
). In other words, there is an equivalence between tangentoids in
\mathsf{CALG}_{R}
and commutative non-unital associative solid
R
-algebras
[
8
]
. We then use a theorem of Niefield’s
[
13
, Theorem 4.3]
, to show that coexponentiable tangentoids in
\mathsf{CALG}_{R}
are equivalent to commutative non-unital associative solid
R
-algebras whose underlying
R
-module is finitely generated and projective (Theorem
4.14
), giving us our desired characterization of representable tangent structures on affine schemes.

As a special case, when the base ring
R
is a principal ideal domain (PID), the only coexponentiable tangentoids in
\mathsf{CALG}_{R}
are the base ring
R
and
R[\varepsilon]
(Theorem
4.17
). In other words, the only representable tangent structures on the category of affine schemes over a PID
R
are the trivial tangent structure (Example
2.4
) and the aforementioned tangent structure related to the module of Kähler differentials (Corollary
4.18
). When the base ring
R
fails to be a PID, more representable tangent structures are allowed as we show in Example
4.21
. Furthermore, even when the base ring is a PID, our classification does not put constraints on non-representable tangent structures. In particular, in Example
4.12
we show that the ring
\mathbb{Q}
of rational numbers induces a novel tangent structure on the category
\mathsf{CRING}
of commutative and unital rings, which however, does not dualize to affine schemes.

While we assume that the reader is familiar with tangent categories, in this section, if only to introduce notation and terminology, we recall the necessary background on tangent category theory. For an in-depth introduction to tangent categories, we refer the reader to

Let us briefly provide some intuition for the definition of a (Rosický) tangent category. Tangent categories formalize the properties of the tangent bundle on smooth manifolds from classical differential geometry. An object
M
in a tangent category can be interpreted as a base space, and
\mathrm{T}M
as its abstract tangent bundle. The projection
p_{M}
is the analogue of the natural projection from the tangent bundle to its base space, making
\mathrm{T}M
an abstract fibre bundle over
M

s_{M}
and the zero map
z_{M}
make
\mathrm{T}M
into a generalized version of a smooth vector bundle where each fibre is a commutative monoid. In a Rosický tangent category, each fibre is also an Abelian group. To explain the vertical lift, recall that in differential geometry, the double tangent bundle, i.e., the tangent bundle of the tangent bundle, admits a canonical sub-bundle called the vertical bundle, which is isomorphic to the pullback
\mathrm{T}_{2}M
of the projection along itself. The vertical lift

2.2

We now briefly review representable tangent structures which, as the name suggests, is when the tangent bundle functor is representable. To do so, we must ask our tangent categories to have finite products and that the tangent bundle functor preserves them. So for a category

[

\displaystyle p^{\circ}_{M}\colon M\xrightarrow{\eta_{M}}\mathrm{T}\mathrm{T}^{\circ}M\xrightarrow{p_{\mathrm{T}^{\circ}M}}\mathrm{T}^{\circ}M

Lemma 2.16

In a category

with finite products and finite pullbacks, let

admits a right adjoint. Moreover, we also have a natural transformation

defined as

of

and we have a natural transformation

\mathbb{X}^{\mathsf{op}}

has finite pullbacks,

3
Tangentoids

Characterizing representable tangent structures is equivalent to characterizing infinitesimal objects. Moreover, thanks to
[
3
, Proposition 5.17]
, if
\mathbb{T}
is a representable tangent structure with associated infinitesimal object
D
, then
D\times-
induces a tangent structure on the opposite category, where
\times
becomes a coproduct. Such a tangent structure can be generalized to arbitrary monoidal categories. As such, in this section, we introduce the notion of a tangentoid in a monoidal category, which is briefly an object such that tensoring with said object gives a tangent structure. Tangentoids are indeed useful towards the main objective of this paper since they provide an alternative characterization of infinitesimal objects. Indeed, in a setting with (co)products, an infinitesimal object is precisely a coexponentiable tangentoid in the opposite category. Therefore, characterizing representable tangent structures and infinitesimal objects is equivalent to characterizing coexponentiable tangentoids in the opposite category. So, in particular, since the category of affine schemes over
R
is equivalent to the opposite category of
\mathsf{CALG}_{R}
, coexponentiable tangentoids in
\mathsf{CALG}_{R}
are the same as infinitesimal objects in
\mathsf{AFF}_{R}
.

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{D^{\prime}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{D\otimes D}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{D^{\prime}\otimes D^{\prime}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces l}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces l^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f\otimes f}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{D\otimes D}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{D^{\prime}\otimes D^{\prime}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{D\otimes D}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{D^{\prime}\otimes D^{\prime}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f\otimes f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces c}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces c^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f\otimes f}

Tangentoids on a monoidal category
(\mathscr{E},\otimes,\mathrm{I})
together with their morphisms form a category denoted by
\mathsf{TOID}(\mathscr{E})
. Morphisms of Rosický tangentoids are just morphisms of tangentoids since the compatibility with the negation is automatic.

Lemma 3.5
.

If
f\colon D\to D^{\prime}
is a morphism of tangentoids and
D
and
D^{\prime}
are Rosický with negations
n
and
n^{\prime}
, respectively, the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{D^{\prime}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{D^{\prime}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces n}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces n^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

Proof.

Using the compability of
f
with the additive structure of
D
and
D^{\prime}
, we have:

\displaystyle s^{\prime}\circ\langle f,f\circ n\rangle=s^{\prime}\circ(f\times_{\mathrm{I}}f)\circ\langle\mathsf{id}_{D},n\rangle=f\circ s\circ\langle\mathsf{id}_{D},n\rangle=f\circ z\circ p=z^{\prime}\circ p^{\prime}\circ f=z^{\prime}\circ p^{\prime}\circ n^{\prime}\circ f

where we used that
p^{\prime}\circ n^{\prime}=p^{\prime}
. Therefore, using the cancellation property, we first compute that:

\displaystyle s^{\prime}\circ(\mathsf{id}_{D^{\prime}}\times_{\mathrm{I}}s^{\prime})\circ\langle n^{\prime}\circ f,\langle f,f\circ n\rangle\rangle=s^{\prime}\circ\langle n^{\prime}\circ f,s^{\prime}\circ\langle f,f\circ n\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle\quad=s^{\prime}\circ\langle n^{\prime}\circ f,z^{\prime}\circ p^{\prime}\circ n^{\prime}\circ f\rangle=s^{\prime}\circ\langle\mathsf{id}_{D^{\prime}},z^{\prime}\circ p^{\prime}\rangle\circ n^{\prime}\circ f=n^{\prime}\circ f

and we can also compute that:

\displaystyle s^{\prime}\circ(\mathsf{id}_{D^{\prime}}\times_{\mathrm{I}}s^{\prime})\circ\langle n^{\prime}\circ f,\langle f\circ n,f\rangle\rangle=s^{\prime}\circ(s^{\prime}\times_{\mathrm{I}}\mathsf{id}_{D^{\prime}})\circ\langle\langle n^{\prime}\circ f,f\rangle,f\circ n\rangle

\displaystyle\quad=s^{\prime}\circ\langle s^{\prime}\circ\langle n^{\prime},\mathsf{id}_{D^{\prime}}\rangle\circ f,f\circ n\rangle=\langle s^{\prime}\circ\langle z^{\prime}\circ p^{\prime},\mathsf{id}_{D^{\prime}}\rangle,\mathsf{id}_{D^{\prime}}\rangle\circ f\circ n=f\circ n\qed

So we conclude that
n^{\prime}\circ f=f\circ n
as desired.

.

3.2
Tangent structures parametrized by tangentoids

In
[
12
]
, Leung suggested a different approach to tangent category theory. Leung first defined the category
\mathsf{WEIL}_{1}
, which is the symmetric monoidal category generated by the family of rigs
W_{n}\colon\kern-4.30554pt=\mathbb{N}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]/(x_{i}x_{j})
and by the following structure morphisms:

​
[
Projection
]
:

p\colon W=\mathbb{N}[x]/(x^{2})\to\mathbb{N}
, which sends
x
to
0
;

​
[
Zero morphism
]
:

z\colon\mathbb{N}\to W
, which sends
1
to itself;

​
[
Sum morphism
]
:

s\colon W_{2}\to W
, which sends
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
to
x
;

​
[
Vertical lift
]
:

l\colon W\to W\otimes W
, which sends
x
to
x\otimes x
;

​
[
Canonical flip
]
:

c\colon W\otimes W\to W\otimes W
, which coincides with the symmetry.

Leung then showed that a tangent structure on a category

is equivalent to a suitable strong monoidal functor
\mathrm{L}[\mathbb{T}]\colon\mathsf{WEIL}_{1}\to\mathsf{END}(\mathbb{X})
from a symmetric monoidal category
\mathsf{WEIL}_{1}
to the monoidal category of endofunctors of
\mathbb{X}
. Concretely, the strong monoidal functor
\mathrm{L}[\mathbb{T}]
sends

to the tangent bundle functor
\mathrm{T}
and each structural morphism to the corresponding structural natural transformation of
\mathbb{T}
. Leung also introduced the notion of a tangent structure
internal
to a monoidal category
(\mathscr{E},\otimes,\mathrm{I})
as a strong monoidal functor
\mathrm{L}[\mathbb{T}]\colon\mathsf{WEIL}_{1}\to(\mathscr{E},\otimes,\mathrm{I})
which preserves suitable limits
[
12

Proposition 3.6
.

An internal tangent structure in a monoidal category
(\mathscr{E},\otimes,\mathrm{I})
is equivalent to a tangentoid in
(\mathscr{E},\otimes,\mathrm{I})
.

Proof.

Each internal tangent structure in
(\mathscr{E},\otimes,\mathrm{I})
consists of a strong monoidal functor
\mathrm{L}[D]\colon\mathsf{WEIL}_{1}\to(\mathscr{E},\otimes,\mathrm{I})
that preserves some limit diagrams. In particular,
\mathrm{L}[D]
sends
W
to an object
D
of
\mathscr{E}
, the projection
p\colon W\to\mathbb{N}
to a morphism
p\colon D\to\mathrm{I}
, the zero morphism
z\colon\mathbb{N}\to W
to a morphism
z\colon\mathrm{I}\to D
, the sum morphism
s\colon W_{2}\to W
, to a morphism
s\colon D_{2}\to D
, where
D_{2}
must be the pullback of
p
along itself, the vertical lift
l\colon W\to W\otimes W
to a morphism
l\colon D\to D\otimes D
, and the canonical flip
c\colon W\otimes W\to W\otimes W
to a morphism
c\colon D\otimes D\to D\otimes D
. However, since

induces a tangent structure on
\mathsf{WEIL}_{1}
,
(D,p,z,s,l,c)
satisfies the axioms of a tangentoid in
\mathscr{E}
. Conversely, each strong monoidal functor
\mathrm{L}[D]\colon\mathsf{WEIL}_{1}\to(\mathscr{E},\otimes,\mathrm{I})
that preserves the required limits is fully determined by the assignment of

and the structural morphisms of
\mathsf{WEIL}_{1}
.
∎

From the equivalence between internal tangent structures and tangentoids, one can show that a tangentoid on
\mathscr{E}
is equivalent to giving a tangent structure on
\mathscr{E}
whose tangent bundle functor is the functor
D\otimes-
for some object

Proposition 3.7
.

If
D
is a (Rosický) tangentoid in a monoidal category
(\mathscr{E},\otimes,\mathrm{I})
, the functor
D\otimes-
together with the natural transformations

,

,

,

, and
c\otimes-
(and
n\otimes-
) defines a (Rosický) tangent structure on
\mathscr{E}
, denoted by
[D\otimes-]
.

Proof.

From Leung’s approach, to give a tangent structure on

is to give a strong monoidal functor
\mathrm{L}[\mathbb{T}]\colon\mathsf{WEIL}_{1}\to\mathsf{END}(\mathscr{E})
that preserves a suitable class of limits. By Proposition

, a tangentoid is equivalent to a strong monoidal functor
\mathrm{L}[D]\colon\mathsf{WEIL}_{1}\to\mathscr{E}
that preserves a suitable class of limits. By postcomposing
\mathrm{L}[D]
by the functor which sends an object

of
\mathscr{E}
to an endofunctor

, one obtains a strong monoidal functor
\mathsf{WEIL}_{1}\to\mathscr{E}\to\mathsf{END}(\mathscr{E})
, which defines a tangent structure on
\mathscr{E}
.
∎

The converse of Proposition
3.7
holds as well by evaluating the tangent bundle functor at the monoidal unit.

Proposition 3.8
.

If
\mathbb{T}
is a (Rosický) tangent structure on
\mathscr{E}
whose tangent bundle functor

is of type
D\otimes-
for some object

comes equipped with the structure of a (Rosický) tangentoid, whose structural natural transformations correspond to

,
z_{\mathrm{I}}
,
s_{\mathrm{I}}
,
l_{\mathrm{I}}
, and
c_{\mathrm{I}}
(and
n_{\mathrm{I}}
).

Proof.

Consider a tangent structure
\mathbb{T}
on
\mathscr{E}
whose tangent bundle functor
\mathrm{T}
is the functor
D\otimes-
for some object
D
. Via the Leung correspondence, this is equivalent to a suitable strong monoidal functor
\mathrm{L}[\mathbb{T}]\colon\mathsf{WEIL}_{1}\to\mathsf{END}(\mathscr{E})
. The evaluation functor
\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{I}}
, which sends an endofunctor

to

lifts to a strong monoidal functor
\mathsf{END}(\mathscr{E})\to\mathscr{E}
. By postcomposing by
\mathrm{L}[\mathbb{T}]
, we define an internal tangent structure
\mathsf{WEIL}_{1}\to\mathsf{END}(\mathscr{E})\to\mathscr{E}
, which, by Proposition

, is equivalent to a tangentoid on
\mathscr{E}
.
∎

Propositions
3.7
and
3.8
show an equivalence between tangentoids and tangent structures whose tangent bundle functor is of type

, for some object

Theorem 3.9
.

Tangentoids on a monoidal category
\mathscr{E}
are equivalent to those tangent structures on
\mathscr{E}
whose tangent bundle functor
\mathrm{T}
is a functor of type

, for some object

.

From Theorem

, one can immediately see the connection between tangent categories and tangentoids, as illustrated in the following example.

Example 3.10
.

In Leung’s approach, a tangent structure on a category
\mathbb{X}
is equivalent to a strong monoidal functor
\mathrm{L}[\mathbb{T}]\colon\mathsf{WEIL}_{1}\to\mathsf{END}(\mathbb{X})

(where the monoidal product is given by composition and the monoidal unit is the identity functor). Thus, a (Rosický) tangent category is equivalently a category
\mathbb{X}
with a tangentoid in

3.3
Symmetric tangentoids

We now consider tangentoids in
symmetric
monoidal categories, specifically those whose canonical flip coincides with the symmetry. For a symmetric monoidal category, the symmetry will be denoted by
\sigma
, and symmetric monoidal categories will be denoted by quadruples
(\mathscr{E},\otimes,\mathrm{I},\sigma)
.

Definition 3.11
.

In a symmetric monoidal category
(\mathscr{E},\otimes,\mathrm{I},\sigma)
, a
symmetric tangentoid
is a tangentoid whose canonical flip
c\colon D\otimes D\to D\otimes D
coincides with the symmetry
\sigma_{D,D}\colon D\otimes D\to D\otimes D
.

The full subcategory of symmetric tangentoids of a symmetric monoidal category
(\mathscr{E},\otimes,\mathrm{I},\sigma)
is denoted by
\mathsf{s}\mathsf{TOID}(\mathscr{E})
. The axioms of symmetric tangentoids involving the canonical flip are consequence of the axioms of the symmetry. Thus, the definition of a symmetric tangentoid can be greatly simplified.

Proposition 3.12
.

In the context of a symmetric monoidal category
(\mathscr{E},\otimes,\mathrm{I},\sigma)
, the axioms of a symmetric (Rosický) tangentoid involving the canonical flip are automatic.

Proof.

Let us consider a symmetric tangentoid in a symmetric monoidal category whose symmetry is denoted by
\sigma
. As we discussed in our convention, we do not explicitly write the associator and the unitors of the monoidal category. Let us start by considering the compatibilities between the projection and the zero morphism, which correspond to the commutativity of the following two diagrams:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{D\otimes D}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{D\otimes D}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{D\otimes D}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces z\otimes\mathsf{id}_{D}}

Here are now examples of symmetric tangentoids, which in particular recapture tangent structures we have already encountered.

Example 3.13
.

For any monoidal category
(\mathscr{E},\otimes,\mathrm{I})
, the monoidal unit

is a Rosický tangentoid whose tangent structure is trivial. Since for every object we have

, it follows that the induced tangent structure from

is precisely the trivial tangent structure given in Example

. For a symmetric monoidal category
(\mathscr{E},\otimes,\mathrm{I},\sigma)
,

is also a symmetric Rosický tangentoid.

Example 3.14
.

Let
\mathsf{MOD}_{R}
be the category of modules over a commutative ring
R
, which is a symmetric monoidal category given by the usual algebraic tensor product of modules
\otimes
. In
(\mathsf{MOD}_{R},\otimes,R,\sigma)
, the

is a symmetric Rosický tangentoid. The projection

is given by the first projection,

, with pullbacks given by:

\displaystyle D_{n}\colon\kern-4.30554pt=R\oplus\underbrace{R\oplus\ldots\oplus R}_{n\text{ times}}

The zero morphism

is given by the first injection,

, the sum morphism

sums the second and third components,

, while the negation

makes the second component negatives,

. Lastly, the vertical lift

inserts

. Moreover, observe that for a
R
-module

, we have that

. As such the induced tangent structure from

is equivalent to the biproduct tangent structure given in Example

. In fact, it is easy to see that this example generalizes to any symmetric monoidal category with finite biproducts that are preserved by the monoidal product.

Example 3.15
.

Let
R
be a commutative ring. Then

is a symmetric monoidal category where the monoidal product is given by the usual tensor product of

-algebras
\otimes_{R}
. The ring of dual numbers

is a symmetric Rosický tangentoid in
\mathsf{CALG}_{R}
. The projection

sends

to

, i.e., the augmentation map, with pullbacks given by
R[\varepsilon]_{n}\colon\kern-4.30554pt=R[\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{n}]\colon\kern-4.30554pt=R[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]/(x_{i}x_{j})
. The zero morphism

sends

sends both

and
\varepsilon_{2}

\varepsilon
, while the negation

sends
\varepsilon
to

. The lift
l\colon R[\varepsilon]\to R[\varepsilon]\otimes_{R}[\varepsilon^{\prime}]
sends
\varepsilon
to
\varepsilon\otimes\varepsilon
. Moreover, observe that for a commutative

-algebra

, we have that
A[\varepsilon]\cong R[\varepsilon]\otimes_{R}A
. Thus, the induced tangent structure by

is equivalent to the tangent structure given in Example

.

3.4
Tangentoids in (co)Cartesian monoidal categories

Our primary goal is to characterize tangentoids in the category
\mathsf{CALG}_{R}
of commutative algebras. However, the monoidal structure of
\mathsf{CALG}_{R}
is cocartesian, namely, the monoidal product is given by binary coproducts and the unit is initial. In fact, recall that any category with finite coproducts can be seen as a symmetric monoidal category, which we refer to as a coCartesian monoidal category (in other words, a symmetric monoidal category whose monoidal product is a coproduct and whose monoidal unit is an initial object). Therefore, we may consider tangentoids in coCartesian monoidal categories. In such a setting, every tangentoid is in fact symmetric.

Proposition 3.16
.

In a coCartesian monoidal category, every (Rosický) tangentoid is symmetric.

Proof.

Let
D
be a tangentoid in a coCartesian monoidal category. Since the monoidal unit
\mathrm{I}
is an initial object, the zero morphism of
D
must be the unique morphism
!\colon\mathrm{I}\to D
. Therefore, the compatibility between the canonical flip and the zero morphism reads as follows:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{D\otimes D}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{D\otimes D}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces c}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{2}}

where
\iota_{j}\colon D\to D\otimes D
are the injection maps of the coproduct. From involutivity, we also get that
c\circ\iota_{2}=\iota_{1}
. However, symmetry isomorphism
\sigma
also satisfies
\sigma\circ\iota_{1}=\iota_{2}
and
\sigma\circ\iota_{2}=\iota_{1}
. Then by the universal property of coproducts, it follows that
c=\sigma
. Thus, every tangentoid is symmetric.
∎

Corollary 3.17
.

Every tangentoid in the coCartesian monoidal category
\mathsf{CALG}_{R}
is symmetric.

On the other hand, it is natural to wonder if a similar simplification occurs for tangentoids in a category with finite products. It turns out that for Cartesian monoidal categories (namely, symmetric monoidal categories whose monoidal product is a product and the unit is a terminal object), the only tangentoids are terminal objects.

Proposition 3.18
.

The only tangentoid (up to unique isomorphism) in a Cartesian monoidal category is the terminal object.

Proof.

Let
D
be a tangentoid in a Cartesian monoidal category. We need to show that
D
is isomorphic to the monoidal unit
I
, which is a terminal object. By the universal property of the terminal object, the projection morphism
p\colon D\to I
must be the unique morphism from
D
to
I
. Now by Lemma
3.3
, the following diagram is a triple equalizer:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{D\times D}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces l}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\langle\mathsf{id}_{D},p\rangle}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\langle p\circ z,p\rangle}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\langle p,\mathsf{id}_{D}\rangle}

where
\langle\-,-\rangle
is the pairing operator from the universal property of the product. However,
\langle\mathsf{id}_{D},p\rangle\colon D\times D\to D
and
\langle p,\mathsf{id}_{D}\rangle\colon D\times D\to D
are respectively the projections
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
of the product. Therefore, by the universal property of the product,
l
coincides with the morphism
\Delta\circ z\circ p
, where
\Delta\colon\kern-4.30554pt=\langle\mathsf{id}_{D},\mathsf{id}_{D}\rangle\colon D\to D\times D
is the diagonal map. Now consider a morphism
f\colon D\to D
. From the universal property of the terminal object, we get that
p\circ f=p
, and thus:

\displaystyle l\circ f=\Delta\circ z\circ p\circ f=\Delta\circ z\circ p

Therefore, from the universal property of the triple equalizer, it follows that there is a unique morphism
D\to D
, namely, the identity. Therefore, we get that
z\circ p=\mathsf{id}_{D}
. Since we also have that
p\circ z=\mathsf{id}_{I}
, it follows that
D\cong I
, and so
D
is also a terminal object. So we conclude that every tangentoid in a Cartesian monoidal category is a terminal object.
∎

Corollary 3.19
.

The only tangentoid (up to unique isomorphism) in the Cartesian monoidal category
\mathsf{AFF}_{R}
of affine schemes over
R
is the terminal object
R
.

3.5
Coexponentiable tangentoids

By Theorem
3.9
, a tangentoid in a monoidal category
\mathscr{E}
is equivalent to a tangent structure on
\mathscr{E}
whose tangent bundle functor is a functor
D\otimes-
, for some object
D
. It follows that
D_{n}\otimes-
represents
\mathrm{T}_{n}
. When each of the functor
D_{n}\otimes-
admits a left adjoint, by the adjoint theorem
[
3
, Proposition 5.17]
, the opposite category of
\mathscr{E}
admits a tangent structure. This section introduces coexponentiable tangentoids, which are precisely those tangentoids whose associated tangent structure
D\otimes-
can be “dualized” to define a new tangent structure on the opposite category.

Definition 3.20
.

A tangentoid
D
in a monoidal category
(\mathscr{E},\otimes,\mathrm{I})
is
coexponentiable
when each functor
D_{n}\otimes-\colon\mathscr{E}\to\mathscr{E}
admits a left adjoint.

Lemma 3.21
.

Given a monoidal category
(\mathscr{E},\otimes,\mathrm{I})
, every coexponentiable tangentoid
D
in
\mathscr{E}
induces a tangent structure on the opposite category
\mathscr{E}^{\mathsf{op}}
whose tangent bundle functor is the (opposite of the) left adjoint
\mathrm{T}^{\circ}
of
D\otimes-
in
\mathscr{E}
.

We may now precisely state the relationship between tangentoids and infinitesimal objects.

Lemma 3.22
.

In a category
\mathbb{X}
with finite products, an infinitesimal object is equivalent to a coexponentiable tangentoid in the opposite category
\mathbb{X}^{\mathsf{op}}
(seen as a coCartesian monoidal category).

In other words, an infinitesimal object is equivalently an “
exponentiable cotangentoid
” in a Cartesian monoidal category. Moreover, recall that by Proposition
3.16
, every tangentoid in a coCartesian monoidal category is symmetric. Thus, every infinitesimal object induces a symmetric tangentoid in the opposite category, which explains why the extra canonical flip structural map is not necessary in the definition of an infinitesimal object.

Thanks to Lemma
3.22
, coexponentiable tangentoids in the coCartesian monoidal category
\mathsf{CALG}_{R}
are precisely infinitesimal objects in the category
\mathsf{AFF}_{R}
. Dually, infinitesimal objects of
\mathsf{CALG}_{R}
are precisely coexponentiable tangentoids in
\mathsf{AFF}_{R}
, which we know are trivial.

Corollary 3.23
.

The only infinitesimal object in the coCartesian monoidal category
\mathsf{CALG}_{R}
is (up to unique isomorphism) the initial object
R
.

\displaystyle=s\circ\tau\circ\langle\mathsf{id}_{D}\otimes p,p\otimes\mathsf{id}_{D}\rangle=s\circ\langle\mathsf{id}_{D}\otimes p,p\otimes\mathsf{id}_{D}\rangle=\mu

Since every tangentoid is a monoid, it is natural to wonder if it is also a tangentoid in the category of monoids of the monoidal category. This holds when the base monoidal category is symmetric. So for a symmetric monoidal category
(\mathscr{E},\otimes,\mathrm{I},\sigma)

For any tangent structure, the tangent structure maps are all monad morphisms. Thus, it follows that the projection

When the forgetful functor

Remark 3.27

In all cases of interest, the functor

4.2

Theorem

.

, where

comes equipped with the structure of a commutative solid non-unital algebra

. Conversely, each commutative solid non-unital

in
\mathsf{MOD}_{R}
defines a tangentoid

Proof.

By Proposition
3.26
, a tangentoid

in
\mathsf{CALG}_{R}
is equivalent to a symmetric tangentoid of
\mathsf{MOD}_{R}
equipped with the commutative monoid structure of Lemma

. By unwrapping this monoid structure, it is easy to show that a tangentoid
D
of
\mathsf{CALG}_{R}
coincides with the semidirect product
R\ltimes M
between the ring
R
and a commutative solid non-unital algebra

on

.
∎

Corollary 4.11
.

The assignment defined by Theorem

which sends a tangentoid of
\mathsf{CALG}_{R}
to a commutative solid non-unital algebra on

extends to an equivalence of categories:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Solid}\colon\mathsf{TOID}(\mathsf{CALG}_{R})\leftrightarrows\mathsf{CSOLD}_{R}\colon R\ltimes-

Thanks to the characterization of tangentoids in
\mathsf{CALG}_{R}
, we can now construct new examples of tangent structures on

. In particular, when

,

As discussed in Example

,

4.3
Infinitesimal objects for affine schemes

Thanks to Lemma

, infinitesimal objects, and therefore representable tangent structures, on

are equivalent to coexponentiable tangentoids in
\mathsf{CALG}_{R}

Theorem 4.13
.

[

A commutative and unital

if and only if its underlying

Thanks to Theorem

, we can finally characterize coexponentiable tangentoids of
\mathsf{CALG}_{R}

(

R
-algebra

The coexponentiable tangentoids of

are precisely the

characterizes tangentoids of

as commutative solid non-unital

is coexponentiable provided that each

is coexponentiable. However, since

is complete, by Lemma

, a tangentoid

is coexponentiable in

if and only if the underlying object

is coexponentiable. Moreover, Theorem

establishes that coexponentiable

-algebras.
∎

Since coexponentiable tangentoids correspond to infinitesimal objects in the opposite category, we also have a characterization of representable tangent structures on
\mathsf{AFF}_{R}
.

Corollary 4.15
.

A tangent structure

on

is representable if and only if there is a finitely generated projective commutative solid non-unital

-algebra

is right adjoint to the functor

, where

When the base ring

[

The projective

By applying Lemma

is isomorphic to

\displaystyle R^{n^{2}}\cong R^{n}\otimes R^{n}\cong M\otimes M\cong M\cong R^{n}

Using again that coexponentiable tangentoids are infinitesimal objects in the opposite category, there are only two representable tangent structures on

when

Corollary 4.18
.

When the base ring

are

In particular, when

is the ring of integers

,

Corollary 4.19

In the category of affine schemes

, the only representable tangent structures are the trivial one and

.

It is important to stress that there could be other non-coexponentiable tangentoids in

On the other hand, it is natural to wonder if there are any other coexponentiable tangentoid when the base ring fails to be a PID.

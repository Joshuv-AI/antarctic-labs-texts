Path Components of G_2 \GTwo -Moduli Spaces may be non-aspherical
Diarmuid Crowley, Sebastian Goette, Thorsten Hertl

Abstract
. Starting from Joyce’s generalised Kummer construction, we exhibit non-trivial families of G 2 \mathrm{G}_{2} -manifolds over the two dimensional sphere by resolving singularities with a twisted family of Eguchi-Hanson spaces. We establish that the comparison map 𝒢 2 tf ( M ) / / Diff ( M ) 0 → 𝒢 2 tf ( M ) / Diff ( M ) 0 \mathcal{G}_{2}^{\mathrm{tf}}(M)/\!\!/\mathrm{Diff}(M)_{0}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}_{2}^{\mathrm{tf}}(M)/\mathrm{Diff}(M)_{0} is a fibration over each path component with Eilenberg Mac Lane spaces as fibres, which allows us to show that these families remain non-trivial in 𝒢 2 tf ​ ( M ) / Diff ​ ( M ) 0 \mathcal{G}_{2}^{\mathrm{tf}}(M)/\mathrm{Diff}(M)_{0} . In addition, we construct a new invariant based on characteristic classes that allows us to show that different resolutions give rise to different elements in the moduli space.

1.
Introduction

In the article
[
19
]
, Dominic Joyce not only presented the first example of a closed manifold with holonomy group
\GTwo
,
but also provided a structural result regarding the deformation behaviour of
\GTwo
-holonomy metrics on any closed, seven-dimensional manifold.
Joyce showed that the
moduli space
, which he defines as the quotient
\tfGTwoStr(M)/\mathrm{Diff}(M)_{0}
of all parallel or
torsion-free

\GTwo
-structures by the group of diffeomorphisms isotopic to the identity, is a smooth manifold of dimension
b_{3}(M)
by proving that the tautological map
\tfGTwoStr(M)/\mathrm{Diff}(M)_{0}\rightarrow H^{3}(M;\mathbb{R})
that sends an equivalence class of a parallel
\GTwo
-structure to its cohomology class is a local diffeomorphism.

This theorem is local in nature, and results about the global topological properties of
\tfGTwoStr(M)/\mathrm{Diff}(M)_{0}
remain scarce.
Indeed, so far, it is only known by work of
[
8
]
and
[
35
]
that certain
\GTwo
-manifolds have disconnected moduli spaces; the question whether all path components are contractible has remained open.

The first main result of this article shows that the moduli space of Joyce’s first example, the one presented in
[
19
]
, has a non-contractible path component.

Theorem A
.

If
(M,\varphi_{\mathrm{Joyce}})
denotes Joyce’s first example of a closed
\GTwo
-manifold, then the second homotopy group contains at least
3^{12}
elements:

\left|\pi_{2}\left(\tfGTwoStr(M)/\mathrm{Diff}(M)_{0},[\varphi_{\mathrm{Joyce}}]\right)\right|\geq 3^{12}=531\,441.

Besides the challenges of constructing examples of torsion-free
\GTwo
-forms on closed manifolds, a principal obstacle in finding non-trivial elements in homotopy groups of
\GTwo
-moduli spaces stems from its very definition:
From a categorical perspective, a quotient
\tfGTwoStr(M)/\mathrm{Diff}(M)_{0}
is determined by
\mathrm{Diff}(M)_{0}
-invariant maps
out
of
\tfGTwoStr(M)
, while elements in the homotopy groups are represented by maps from spheres
into
this quotient, and these maps do not lift to
\tfGTwoStr(M)
automatically.

To overcome this difficulty, we make use of the
homotopy quotient

\tfGTwoStr(M)/\!\!/\mathrm{Diff}(M)_{0}
instead.
The universal property of this topological space relates maps
into

\tfGTwoStr(M)/\!\!/\mathrm{Diff}(M)_{0}
to
M
-fibre bundles over the domain with a fibrewise torsion-free
\GTwo
-structure on it, see Section
3
for details.
The homotopy moduli space may be explicitly defined as a quotient
(\tfGTwoStr(M)\times E\mathrm{Diff}(M)_{0})/\mathrm{Diff}(M)_{0}
, where
E\mathrm{Diff}(M)_{0}
is a contractible space on which
\mathrm{Diff}(M)_{0}
acts freely, and we consider the diagonal action.
The product projections induce two maps

\textstyle{\tfGTwoStr(M)/\!\!/\mathrm{Diff}(M)_{0}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\tfGTwoStr(M)/\mathrm{Diff}(M)_{0}}
\textstyle{B\mathrm{Diff}(M)_{0}.}

A priori, the homotopy moduli space and the actual moduli space might be very different spaces.
However, our second main result, the following comparison theorem, implies that these two spaces carry almost the same information.

Theorem B

(Orbit Comparison Theorem)
.

If
\tfGTwoStr(M)_{\varphi}
denotes the path component of
\varphi
in
\tfGTwoStr(M)
and if
\mathrm{Stab}_{\varphi}(\mathrm{Diff}(M)_{0})
denotes the subgroup of elements of
\mathrm{Diff}(M)_{0}
that preserve
\varphi
, then the canonical comparison map

p\colon\tfGTwoStr(M)_{\varphi}/\!\!/\mathrm{Diff}(M)_{0}\rightarrow\tfGTwoStr(M)_{\varphi}/\mathrm{Diff}(M)_{0}

is a fibration with fibre
B\mathrm{Stab}_{\varphi}(\mathrm{Diff}(M)_{0}).

Since
\mathrm{Stab}_{\varphi}(\mathrm{Diff}(M)_{0})
is a finite group if the holonomy group of the induced Riemannian metric
g_{\varphi}
is the entire group
\GTwo
, we have the following consequence for the homotopy groups of these spaces.

Corollary C
.

If
M
is a manifold that carries a Riemannian metric whose holonomy group is
\GTwo
, then the canonical comparison map
p\colon\tfGTwoStr(M)/\!\!/\mathrm{Diff}(M)_{0}\rightarrow\tfGTwoStr(M)/\mathrm{Diff}(M)_{0}
is
2
-coconnected, which means its induced homomorphism between the
k
-th homotopy groups is injective if
k=2
and bijective if
k\geq 3
.

The important consequence of the Orbit Comparison Theorem and Corollary
C
is that it allows us to construct non-trivial elements in higher homotopy groups that can be detected using metric-independent methods from differential topology.
In fact, the Orbit Comparison Theorem is a consequence of general fact about special group actions, see Theorem
3.5
, so there are corresponding statements for the moduli space of torsion-free
\Spin(7)
-structures and the moduli space for torsion-free Calabi-Yau structures.

We expect that the Orbit Comparison Theorem will be useful in constructing elements in the homotopy groups of the moduli spaces of torsion-free
\GTwo
-structures on closed manifolds.
To our knowledge, all examples of closed
\GTwo
-manifolds were explicitly constructed, using various recipes, and these constructions often involve choices.
As an illustration, let us sketch how we use Orbit Comparison Theorem to prove Theorem
A
:

We start with the construction of bundles of
\GTwo
-manifolds whose fibre is Joyce’s first example
M
by resolving each singularity of the flat orbifold
T^{7}/\Gamma
from which
M
arises with either a twisted family of Eguchi-Hanson spaces
\mathcal{EH}
over
S^{2}
or with an untwisted family
EH\times S^{2}
.
Using the first Pontryagin class of the vertical tangent bundle on
E
and the fact that the singularity set of this flat orbifold has
12
connected components, we are able to prove the following result.

Theorem D
.

The group
\pi_{2}\left(\tfGTwoStr(M)/\!\!/\mathrm{Diff}(M)_{0},[\varphi_{\mathrm{Joyce}}]\right)
has at least
531\,441=3^{12}
elements.

The Orbit Comparison Theorem now implies that the image of
\pi_{2}(\tfGTwoStr(M)/\!\!/\mathrm{Diff}(M)_{0})
in
\pi_{2}(\tfGTwoStr(M)/\mathrm{Diff}(M)_{0})
also contains at least
3^{12}
elements.

To get a better intuition of what happens geometrically, let us compare our main result with the description of hyperkähler structures on
K3
surfaces.
Let
q
be a nondegenerate, even, unimodular quadratic form
of signature
(3,19)
on
\Lambda\cong\mathbb{Z}^{22}\cong H^{2}(K3,\mathbb{Z})
,
then
(\Lambda,q)
is unique up to isomorphism.
Let
\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}\cong\Lambda\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
, and let
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle

denote the indefinite scalar product induced by
q
.
Consider

\displaystyle\bar{Q}

\displaystyle=\bigl\{\,(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})\in\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\bigm|\langle\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}\rangle=a\,\delta_{ij}\text{ for some }a>0\,\bigr\}\;,

\displaystyle\Sigma

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda,q(\lambda)=-2}\bigl\{\,(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})\in\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\bigm|\langle\alpha_{1},\lambda\rangle=\langle\alpha_{2},\lambda\rangle=\langle\alpha_{3},\lambda\rangle=0\,\bigr\}\;,

then the hyperkähler moduli space of a
K3
surface is diffeomorphic
to
Q^{\textrm{hk}}\cong\bar{Q}\setminus\Sigma
,
see
[
21
, Theorem 7.3.16]
.
Note that
\bar{Q}\cong O(3,19)/O(19)\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
and
\Sigma\cap\bar{Q}
is the union of subspaces of codimension
3

of the form
O(3,18)/O(18)\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
.
If we are only interested in the space
\mathcal{M}
of Riemannian metrics
induced by the hyperkähler structures,
we divide by the action of
O(3)
on
3
-frames in
\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}
,
obtaining the symmetric space
O(3,19)/(O(3)\times O(19))\times\mathbb{R}^{+}

of noncompact type.
Now, the singular set is a union of totally geodesic subspaces
of codimension
3
.
Hence, each of these subspaces introduces nontrivial elements
of
\pi_{2}(\mathcal{M})
and
\pi_{2}(Q^{\textrm{hk}})
in their complement.

Geometrically, the Poincaré dual of each cohomology class
\lambda\in\Lambda
with
q(\lambda)=-2
can be realised by a unique minimal
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
with self-intersection
-2
.
The volume of this minimal submanifold is given by
\sum_{j=1}^{3}\langle\alpha_{i},\lambda\rangle^{2}
, so its volume collapses as one approaches the corresponding component of the singular set.
In the Kummer construction, one resolves the
16
singularities of
T^{4}/\mathbb{Z}_{2}
by Eguchi-Hanson spaces.
Each of these contains one copy of
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
with self-intersection
-2
, and these degenerate if the neck-size of the Eguchi-Hanson space approaches zero.

In our examples, the geometric situation is similar.
The non-trivial elements we find in
\pi_{2}(\tfGTwoStr(M)/\mathrm{Diff}(M)_{0})
locally wind around
“holes” in
\tfGTwoStr(M)/\mathrm{Diff}(M)_{0}
of codimension
3
where a
T^{3}
-family
of
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
s collapses.
It is also possible to describe nontrivial homology classes
a\in H_{3}(M)
such that
\langle\varphi,a\rangle\rightarrow 0
if
(M,\varphi)
approaches the singularity.
In
[
11
]
, Dwivedi, Platt and Walpuski have constructed
associative submanifolds that are contained within a small neighbourhood
of the neck of a single
T^{3}
-family of Eguchi-Hanson spaces inside
M
.
Let
a\in H_{3}(M)
denote the homology class they represent.
Then
\langle\varphi,a\rangle
describes their volume, which approaches
0
as the
\GTwo
-structure becomes singular.
There are similar constructions of coassociative submanifolds and
\GTwo
-instantons that degenerate together with the
\GTwo
-structure
in
[
15
]
.

However, the above is insufficient to detect non-trivial elements of
\pi_{2}(\tfGTwoStr(M)/\mathrm{Diff}(M)_{0})
.
First of all, those associative submanifolds are only guaranteed to exist over a certain subset of the moduli space and, secondly, the tautological map
\tfGTwoStr(M)/\mathrm{Diff}(M)_{0}\rightarrow H^{3}(M)
is only a local diffeomorphism.
So, even if one can detect a nontrivial
S^{2}
family inside
\tfGTwoStr(M)/\mathrm{Diff}(M)_{0}
close to a singularity using associative or coassociative submanifolds, there is no obvious argument that prevents this family from being globally trivial.
We therefore follow a different approach.

We now digress briefly to discuss
the full quotient
\tfGTwoStr(M)/\mathrm{Diff}(M)
, which is a priori only an orbifold,
and it’s homotopy orbit space
\tfGTwoStr(M)/\!\!/\mathrm{Diff}(M)
.
The latter space is of independent interest, since it is the classifying space for smooth fibre bundles with fibre a torsion free
\GTwo
-manifold.
The results of
[
8
]
and
[
35
]

detect different path components in the full quotient
\tfGTwoStr(M)/\mathrm{Diff}(M)
and not just the moduli space,
and in that case the natural map

\pi_{0}\tfGTwoStr(M)/\!\!/\mathrm{Diff}(M)\to\pi_{0}\tfGTwoStr(M)/\mathrm{Diff}(M)
is a bijection.
There are canonical comparison maps between the involved objects that
form the commutative diagram

\textstyle{\tfGTwoStr(M)/\!\!/\mathrm{Diff}(M)_{0}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathrm{h}\kappa}
\textstyle{\tfGTwoStr(M)/\mathrm{Diff}(M)_{0}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\kappa}
\textstyle{\tfGTwoStr(M)/\!\!/\mathrm{Diff}(M)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\tfGTwoStr(M)/\mathrm{Diff}(M).}

The map
\mathrm{h}\kappa
is a fibration with fibre
B\Gamma
, the classifying space of the
mapping class group

\Gamma:=\mathrm{Diff}(M)/\mathrm{Diff}(M)_{0}
.
As
\Gamma
is discrete, all higher homotopy groups of
B\Gamma
vanish and hence
\pi_{2}(\mathrm{h}\kappa)
is an injective homomorphism.
In particular, Theorem
D
remains true for the full homotopy quotient.

Corollary E
.

The group
\pi_{2}\left(\tfGTwoStr(M)/\!\!/\mathrm{Diff}(M),[\varphi_{\mathrm{Joyce}}]\right)
has at least
531\,441=3^{12}
elements.

In general, we expect that neither the map
\kappa
is a fibration, nor that our Orbit Comparison Theorem remains true for the map
\tfGTwoStr(M)/\!\!/\mathrm{Diff}(M)\rightarrow\tfGTwoStr(M)/\mathrm{Diff}(M)
because the action of
\mathrm{Diff}(M)
on
\tfGTwoStr(M)
may have fixpoints, see Remark
3.7
below for further details.
In particular, we cannot guarantee that the elements in Theorem

survive under
\pi_{2}(\kappa)
.
A definitive answer would involve a thorough understanding of the fixpoint set of the pullback action of
\Gamma
on
\tfGTwoStr(M)/\mathrm{Diff}(M)_{0}
, which we do not have at the moment.

All singularities we consider in this article can be resolved with Eguchi-Hanson spaces and quotients of it.
It is an interesting question whether other crepant resolutions of ADE-singularities may allow us to construct non-trivial elements in homotopy groups beyond degree
2
, and we plan to address this question a in future paper.

Finally, we would like to remark that the non-trivial bundle
\mathcal{EH}
of Eguchi-Hanson spaces was used by Jiafeng Lin in
[
25
]
in an entirely different context.

We close this section with a lemma that is folklore, but we failed to find a proof in the literature.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
(M,g)
is a closed Riemannian manifold with
{\operatorname{Hol}}(g)=\GTwo
, then its isometry group
\Gamma
is finite.

Proof.

The isometry group of a closed Riemannian manifold is a compact Lie group. Thus, if
\Gamma
were not finite, we would find a non-trivial infinitesimal isometry, in other words, a Killing vector field
X\neq 0
.
The following Bochner formula for vector fields derived in
[
36
]

\int_{M}\mathrm{Ric}(X,X)+\mathrm{tr}\bigl((\nabla_{(\,\cdot\,)}X)^{2}\bigr)+{\mathrm{tr}(\nabla_{(\,\cdot\,)}X)^{2}}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{vol}=0

together with
\mathrm{tr}\bigl((\nabla_{(\,\cdot\,)}X)^{2}\bigr)=-\mathrm{tr}\left(\nabla_{(\,\cdot\,)}X^{\ast}\nabla_{(\,\cdot\,)}X\right)\leq 0
and
\mathrm{Ric}(X,X)=0
implies that
X
is a parallel vector field.
By the holonomy principle,
{\operatorname{Hol}}_{p}(g)\leq\mathrm{Stab}_{\GTwo}(X(p))\cong\mathrm{SU}(3)
for some
p\in M

Lemma 6.4

Let

Proof.

Pick

The local trivialisation around

For the second statement, we use that

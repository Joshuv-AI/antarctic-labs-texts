General Theory of Coupled Characteristic Mode: An Eigen Subspace Approach
Rakesh Sinha, Sandip Ghosal

Abstract
In this work, the problem of characteristic mode analysis using eigendecomposition of the method of moments impedance matrix has been simplified using the eigen-subspace approach. The idea behind the eigen-subspace arises from the physical properties of antenna or scatterers, where only a few eigenmodes are enough to characterize the antenna or scatterer. Therefore, entire space eigenanalysis is a waste of computational resources, and eigen-subspace analysis with few modes is good enough to characterize antennas and scatterers. It has been assumed that there is an eigen-subspace (or hyperplane) of coupled characteristic mode, which coincides with the eigen- hyperplane of uncoupled characteristic mode. We can say the coupled characteristic modes are linear combinations of isolated modes based on this assumption. The linear combination is mapped via modal coupling matrix . Using the modal coupling matrix , we can explain the behavior of arbitrarily shaped antennas and scatterers. A computationally efficient method is developed to compute coupled characteristic modes of two mutually coupled scatterers or antennas using the eigen- subspace. The method is summarized as a theorem of two-body coupled characteristic mode. The theorem of two-body coupled characteristic mode has been extended to the N-body coupled characteristic mode. Two algorithms have been developed for the two-body multimode coupled characteristic mode and N-body multimode coupled characteristic mode. Two numerical examples are provided to validate the proposed concepts.

V
Conclusions

A general theory of coupled characteristic mode has been presented. The theory is based on the expansion of coupled eigen-subspace using isolated eigen-subspace. The eigen-subspace analysis is quite helpful in explaining current density, field pattern, and impedance matching of antenna and scatterer. The theory of two elements multi-mode coupling is established based on the assumption that uncoupled eigen-subspace can be mapped into coupled eigen-subspace via linear transform. The mapping matrix is named as modal coupling matrix. The mode coupling matrix is the measure of coupling among the isolated modes. The theory of two elements coupled mode has been extended to multi-element coupled characteristic mode. Two algorithms have been developed to calculate coupled eigenvalues, eigenvectors, and mode coupling matrix. The numerical examples conclude that maximum modal coupling occurs when elements are identical. Similar modes provide maximum modal coupling, while distinct modes are rarely coupled to each other. For two-element identical elements, the coupled modes appear in an even-odd pair, while the same is not valid in the case of more than two elements.

Appendix A
Generalized Eigenvalue Problem

Consider the generalized eigenvalue problem of characteristic mode

[\textbf{X}][I_{i}]=\lambda_{i}[\textbf{R}][I_{i}]

(A1)

where
i=1,2,3,...,N
and
N\times N
is the dimension of
[\textbf{R}]
and
[\textbf{X}]
. In this problem we have to find find characteristic mode
[I_{i}]
and eigenvalue
\lambda_{i}
of the
i^{th}
mode. We can write the eigenvalue problem in matrix form as

[\textbf{X}][\textbf{I}]=[\textbf{R}][\textbf{I}][\bm{\lambda}]

(A2)

where
\textbf{I}=[I_{1},I_{2},I_{3},...,I_{N}]
and
\bm{\lambda}=diag\{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},...,\lambda_{N}\}
. To understand the properties of generalized eigenvalue problem, we need investigate the problem from the eigenvalue problem of
[\textbf{R}]
and
[\textbf{X}]
.

The normal eigenvalue problem of
[\textbf{R}]
can be written as

[\textbf{R}][\textbf{I}_{R}]=[\textbf{I}_{R}][\bm{\lambda}_{R}]\;or\;[\textbf{I}_{R}]^{T}[\textbf{R}][\textbf{I}_{R}]=[\textbf{I}^{2}_{R}][\bm{\lambda}_{R}].

(A3)

Please note that
[\textbf{I}^{2}_{R}]=[\textbf{I}_{R}]^{T}[\textbf{I}_{R}]=diag\{||I_{R1}||^{2},||I_{R2}||^{2},...,||I_{RN}||^{2}\}
is a diagonal matrix, not necessary to be identity. Now consider that the eigenvector space
[\textbf{I}]
in the generalized eigenvalue problem is linear combination of eigenvector space
[\textbf{I}_{R}]
through the following relationship

[\textbf{I}]=[\textbf{I}_{R}][\textbf{I}^{\prime}_{X}].

(A4)

Using this we can write

[\textbf{X}][\textbf{I}_{R}][\textbf{I}^{\prime}_{X}]=[\textbf{R}][\textbf{I}_{R}][\textbf{I}^{\prime}_{X}][\bm{\lambda}]

(A5)

After taking the inner product with respect to
[\textbf{I}_{R}]
on both side of

[\textbf{I}_{R}]^{T}[\textbf{X}][\textbf{I}_{R}][\textbf{I}^{\prime}_{X}]=[\textbf{I}^{2}_{R}][\bm{\lambda}_{R}][\textbf{I}^{\prime}_{X}][\bm{\lambda}]

(A6)

which can be written as normal eigenvalue problem

[\textbf{X}^{\prime}][\textbf{I}^{\prime}_{X}]=[\textbf{I}^{\prime}_{X}][\bm{\lambda}]

(A7)

where
[\textbf{X}^{\prime}]=[\bm{\lambda^{\prime}}^{-1}_{R}][\textbf{I}_{R}]^{T}[\textbf{X}][\textbf{I}_{R}]
and
[\bm{\lambda^{\prime}}_{R}]=diag\{{\lambda_{R1}||I_{R1}||^{2}},{\lambda_{R2}||I_{R2}||^{2}},...,{\lambda_{RN}||I_{RN}||^{2}}\}
. By solving (
A7
) we can get eigenvalues
[\bm{\lambda}]
, and eigenvectors
[\textbf{I}]=[\textbf{I}_{R}][\textbf{I}^{\prime}_{X}]
can be obtained by matrix multiplication. Please note that
[\textbf{X}^{\prime}]
is not symmetric, i.e.,
[\textbf{X}^{\prime}]^{T}\neq[\textbf{X}^{\prime}]
or
[\textbf{I}_{R}]^{T}[\textbf{X}][\textbf{I}_{R}][\bm{\lambda^{\prime}}^{-1}_{R}]\neq[\bm{\lambda^{\prime}}^{-1}_{R}][\textbf{I}_{R}]^{T}[\textbf{X}][\textbf{I}_{R}]
. Therefore, the eigenvectors
[\textbf{I}^{\prime}_{X}]
are not orthogonal or
[\textbf{I}^{\prime}_{X}]^{T}[\textbf{I}^{\prime}_{X}]
is not a diagonal matrix. However the eigenvalues
[\bm{\lambda}]
are real if
[\textbf{R}]
is positive semidefinite.

Now we are going to prove that eigenvalues
[\bm{\lambda}]
are real if
[\textbf{R}]
is positive semidefinite. Let
i^{th}
eigenvalue
\lambda_{i}
is complex and corresponding eigenvector
[I^{\prime}_{Xi}]
is also complex. Therefore, (
A7
) can be written as

[\textbf{X}^{\prime}][{I}^{\prime}_{Xi}]=\lambda_{i}[{I}^{\prime}_{Xi}]

(A8)

Taking complex conjugate on both side of (
A8
) we have

[\bar{\textbf{X}^{\prime}}][\bar{{I}^{\prime}}_{Xi}]=\bar{\lambda}_{i}[\bar{{I}^{\prime}}_{Xi}]\hskip 10.00002pt\text{or}\hskip 10.00002pt[{\textbf{X}^{\prime}}][\bar{{I}^{\prime}}_{Xi}]=\bar{\lambda}_{i}[\bar{{I}^{\prime}}_{Xi}]

(A9)

[{\textbf{X}^{\prime}}]=[\bm{\lambda^{\prime}}^{-1}_{R}][\textbf{I}_{R}]^{T}[\textbf{X}][\textbf{I}_{R}]
is real because all the factored matrices are real. Now taking inner product with vector
[\bm{\lambda^{\prime}}_{R}][{I}^{\prime}_{Xi}]
on both side of (
A8
),we obtain

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}[\bar{{I}^{\prime}}_{Xi}]^{T}[\bm{\lambda^{\prime}}_{R}][{I}^{\prime}_{Xi}]=

\displaystyle[\bar{{I}^{\prime}}_{Xi}]^{T}[\bm{\lambda^{\prime}}_{R}][\textbf{X}^{\prime}][{I}^{\prime}_{Xi}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle([\textbf{X}^{\prime}]^{T}[\bm{\lambda^{\prime}}_{R}][\bar{{I}^{\prime}}_{Xi}])^{T}[{I}^{\prime}_{Xi}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle([\textbf{I}_{R}]^{T}[\textbf{X}][\textbf{I}_{R}][\bm{\lambda^{\prime}}^{-1}_{R}][\bm{\lambda^{\prime}}_{R}][\bar{{I}^{\prime}}_{Xi}])^{T}[{I}^{\prime}_{Xi}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle([\bm{\lambda^{\prime}}_{R}][\bm{\lambda^{\prime}}^{-1}_{R}][\textbf{I}_{R}]^{T}[\textbf{X}][\textbf{I}_{R}][\bar{{I}^{\prime}}_{Xi}])^{T}[{I}^{\prime}_{Xi}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle([\bm{\lambda^{\prime}}_{R}][\textbf{X}^{\prime}]][\bar{{I}^{\prime}}_{Xi}])^{T}[{I}^{\prime}_{Xi}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle([\bm{\lambda^{\prime}}_{R}]\bar{\lambda}_{i}[\bar{{I}^{\prime}}_{Xi}])^{T}[{I}^{\prime}_{Xi}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\lambda}_{i}[\bar{{I}^{\prime}}_{Xi}]^{T}[\bm{\lambda^{\prime}}_{R}][{I}^{\prime}_{Xi}]

(A10)

Thus
(\lambda_{i}-\bar{\lambda}_{i})[{I}^{\prime}_{Xi}]^{H}[\bm{\lambda^{\prime}}_{R}][{I}^{\prime}_{Xi}]=0
. One of the result of this equation is
\bar{\lambda}_{i}=\lambda_{i}
or
\lambda_{i}
is real if
\phi_{i}=[{I}^{\prime}_{Xi}]^{H}[\bm{\lambda^{\prime}}_{R}][{I}^{\prime}_{Xi}]\neq 0
. If
[\textbf{R}]
is positive definite, then
\phi_{i}=[{I}^{\prime}_{Xi}]^{H}[\bm{\lambda^{\prime}}_{R}][{I}^{\prime}_{Xi}]=\sum_{n=1}^{N}{\lambda_{Rn}||I_{Rn}||^{2}}|{I}_{Xi}(n)|^{2}>0
, which ensure the eigenvalues are real.

As the eigenvalues
[\bm{\lambda}]
of (
A7
) are real for positive definite
[\textbf{R}]
matrix, the eigenvectors
[\textbf{I}^{\prime}_{X}]
are real. Therefore, eigenvectors
[\textbf{I}]=[\textbf{I}_{R}][\textbf{I}^{\prime}_{X}]
are also real as
[\textbf{I}_{R}]
is real.

Now we are going to prove that
[{I}^{\prime}_{Xj}]^{H}[\bm{\lambda^{\prime}}_{R}][{I}^{\prime}_{Xi}]=0
for
i\neq j
. Let us write write the
j^{th}
eigenvalue problem of (
A7
)

[\textbf{X}^{\prime}][{I}^{\prime}_{Xj}]=\lambda_{j}[{I}^{\prime}_{Xj}]

(A11)

Taking inner product with
[\bm{\lambda^{\prime}}_{R}][{I}^{\prime}_{Xj}]
on both side of (
A8
), we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}[{{I}^{\prime}}_{Xj}]^{H}[\bm{\lambda^{\prime}}_{R}][{I}^{\prime}_{Xi}]=

\displaystyle[{{I}^{\prime}}_{Xj}]^{H}[\bm{\lambda^{\prime}}_{R}][\textbf{X}^{\prime}][{I}^{\prime}_{Xi}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle([\textbf{X}^{\prime}]^{H}[\bm{\lambda^{\prime}}_{R}][{{I}^{\prime}}_{Xj}])^{H}[{I}^{\prime}_{Xi}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle([\textbf{I}_{R}]^{T}[\textbf{X}][\textbf{I}_{R}][\bm{\lambda^{\prime}}^{-1}_{R}][\bm{\lambda^{\prime}}_{R}][{{I}^{\prime}}_{Xj}])^{H}[{I}^{\prime}_{Xi}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle([\bm{\lambda^{\prime}}_{R}][\bm{\lambda^{\prime}}^{-1}_{R}][\textbf{I}_{R}]^{T}[\textbf{X}][\textbf{I}_{R}][{{I}^{\prime}}_{Xj}])^{H}[{I}^{\prime}_{Xi}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle([\bm{\lambda^{\prime}}_{R}][\textbf{X}^{\prime}]][\bar{{I}^{\prime}}_{Xj}])^{H}[{I}^{\prime}_{Xi}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle([\bm{\lambda^{\prime}}_{R}]{\lambda}_{j}[{{I}^{\prime}}_{Xj}])^{H}[{I}^{\prime}_{Xi}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\lambda}_{j}[{{I}^{\prime}}_{Xj}]^{H}[\bm{\lambda^{\prime}}_{R}][{I}^{\prime}_{Xi}]

(A12)

Thus

\displaystyle(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})[{{I}^{\prime}}_{Xj}]^{H}[\bm{\lambda^{\prime}}_{R}][{I}^{\prime}_{Xi}]=0.

(A13)

If
\lambda_{i}\neq\lambda_{j}
, then
[{{I}^{\prime}}_{Xj}]^{H}[\bm{\lambda^{\prime}}_{R}][{I}^{\prime}_{Xi}]=0
or eigenvectors
[\textbf{I}^{\prime}_{X}]
are orthogonal with respect to
[\bm{\lambda^{\prime}}_{R}]
, i.e.,

\displaystyle[\textbf{I}^{\prime}_{X}]^{T}[\bm{\lambda^{\prime}}_{R}][\textbf{I}^{\prime}_{X}]=[\bm{\Phi}]

(A14)

where
[\bm{\Phi}]=diag\{\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\ldots,\phi_{N}\}
. Using this property we can prove the
[\textbf{R}]
and
[\textbf{X}]
orthogonality of generalized eigenvectors
[\textbf{I}]
as

\displaystyle[\textbf{I}]^{T}[\textbf{R}][\textbf{I}]=

\displaystyle[\textbf{I}^{\prime}_{X}]^{T}[\textbf{I}_{R}]^{T}[\textbf{R}][\textbf{I}_{R}][\textbf{I}^{\prime}_{X}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\textbf{I}^{\prime}_{X}]^{T}[\bm{\lambda^{\prime}}_{R}][\textbf{I}^{\prime}_{X}]=[\bm{\Phi}]

(A15)

and

\displaystyle[\textbf{I}]^{T}[\textbf{X}][\textbf{I}]=[\textbf{I}]^{T}[\textbf{R}][\textbf{I}][\bm{\lambda}]=[\bm{\Phi}][\bm{\lambda}].

(A16)

Thus properties of generalized eigenvalue problem are explained.

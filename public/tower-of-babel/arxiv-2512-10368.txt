Löwner equations and de Branges–Rovnyak spaces
Michio Seto

Abstract
We study de Branges–Rovnyak spaces parametrized by Löwner equations after de Branges and Ghosechowdhury. The purpose of this paper is to propose a new approach based on the Löwner theory to the structure theory of de Branges–Rovnyak spaces. As the main results, three solutions to the problem “Find concrete elements in de Branges–Rovnyak spaces” discussed by Sarason, Fricain–Mashreghi, Fricain–Hartmann–Ross and Łanucha–Nowak are given. In particular, we deal with applications of the chordal Löwner theory to the de Branges–Rovnyak spaces.



1
Introduction

In de Branges’ original proof of the Bieberbach conjecture,
the fantastic blend of the Löwner theory and the de Branges–Rovnyak theory is one of the highlights (see de Branges
[
3
]
and Vasyunin–Nikol’skiĭ
[
18
,
19
]
).
Although this functional analysis aspect of de Branges’ proof cannot be seen in
the widely circulated proof (for example, Conway
[
2
]
and Rosenblum–Rovnyak
[
14
]
),
Ghosechowdhury continued to search for
interaction between
the Löwner theory and the de Branges–Rovnyak theory in
[
9
,
10
]
,
and obtained a Fourier type expansion theorem induced
by the Löwner equation.
More precisely, he showed continuous resolution
of de Branges–Rovnyak spaces induced by univalent self-maps of the open unit disk.
Now, we would like to point out that
Ghosechowdhury’s theorem leads us to a new approach to the problem

“
Find concrete
elements in de Branges–Rovnyak spaces
”

discussed by Sarason
[
15
]
,
Fricain–Mashreghi
[
8
]
, Fricain–Hartmann–Ross
[
7
]
and Łanucha–
Nowak
[
11
]
, which we will called the inhabitant problem.
In fact,
we show that

\log\frac{1-B_{t}(z)}{1-z}

belongs to the de Branges–Rovnyak space induced by
B_{t}
,
where
B_{t}(z)=B(t,a,z)

(0\leq a\leq t)
is the Löwner semigroup
associated with the Koebe functions

f(t,z)=\frac{e^{t}z}{(1-z)^{2}}\quad(t\geq 0)

(Corollary
4.3
).
Thus, our method led by Ghosechowdhury’s theorem gives an answer to Question 6.6 in
[
7
]
.
Further, in our framework,
applications of the chordal Löwner theory (see Schleißinger
[
16
]
) to de Branges–Rovnyak spaces are also discussed.
However, it is probably because Ghosechowdhury’s paper was written with the language of the de Branges school,
it is hard to say that his work is widely known.
The author’s early motivation of this project was to reconstruct his work
in the style of Ando
[
1
]
.
In order to rearrange his proof, we adopt direct integrals and integral operators.
The author hopes that our framework
would make Ghosechowdhury’s work accessible
to Hilbert space operator theorists.

This paper is organized as follows.
Sections 2 and 3 are the preliminary parts of this paper where direct integrals of reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces and
integral operators defined on those spaces are introduced.
In Section 4,
we prove Ghosechowdhury’s theorem in our style, and give a solution to the inhabitant problem.
In Section 5, toward Section 6, we introduce auxiliary spaces defined by Pick functions.
In Section 6,
we discuss applications of the chordal Löwner theory to the de Branges–Rovnyak theory.
In Corollaries
5.5
and
6.4
, further solutions to the inhabitant problem are obtained.

2
Direct integrals

In this section, we arrange the theory of direct integrals to fit the Löwner theory.

2.1
Setting

Let
\Omega
be a domain in the complex plane
{\mathbb{C}}
, and let
(X,\sigma)
be a measure space.
We consider a family
\{k(x,z,w)\}_{x\in X}
consisting of kernel functions on
\Omega\times\Omega
.
Let
C
be any compact subset of
\Omega
.
Then, we will write

M_{C}(x)=\sup_{z\in C}k(x,z,z).

Throughout this section, we assume the following four conditions:

(A)

k(x,z,w)
is measurable with respect to
x
when
z
and
w
are fixed,

(B)

k(x,z,w)
is holomorphic with respect to
z
when
x
and
w
are fixed,

(C)

M_{C}(x)
is integrable with respect to
\sigma
for any
C
. In particular,
k(x,z,z)
is integrable for any fixed
z
.

(D)

M_{C}(x)
is finite for any
C
when
x
is fixed.

In what follows,
to avoid confusion, complex variables will be denoted such as
z
and
w
,
and specific points in
\Omega
will be denoted such as
\lambda
and
\mu
.
We will write
k_{\lambda}(x,z)=k(x,z,\lambda)
for any
\lambda
in
\Omega
.

2.2
{\mathcal{H}}(x)

Let
{\mathcal{H}}(x)
denote the reproducing kernel Hilbert space generated
by the kernel function
k(x,z,w)
for a fixed point
x
in
X
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
x
be a point
x
in
X
.
Then,
{\mathcal{H}}(x)
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space consisting of holomorphic functions on
\Omega
.

Proof.

For any function
f(x,z)
in
{\mathcal{H}}(x)
,
there exists a sequence
\{f_{n}(x,z)\}_{n}
consisting of linear combinations of reproducing kernels of
{\mathcal{H}}(x)

such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|f_{n}(x,\cdot)-f(x,\cdot)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(x)}=0.

Then, for any
\lambda
in
\Omega
, we have that

\displaystyle|f_{n}(x,\lambda)-f(x,\lambda)|

\displaystyle=|\langle f_{n}(x,\cdot)-f(x,\cdot),k_{\lambda}(x,\cdot)\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}(x)}|

\displaystyle\leq\|f_{n}(x,\cdot)-f(x,\cdot)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(x)}\|k_{\lambda}(x,\cdot)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(x)}

\displaystyle\to 0\quad(n\to\infty).

It follows from Assumption (D) that
this convergence is uniform on any compact subset of
\Omega
.
Hence, by Assumption (B),
f(x,z)
is holomorphic in
\Omega
with respect to variable
z
.
∎

2.3
Riesz–Fischer type Lemma

Let
L^{\infty}(X)
denote the set of all bounded measurable functions on
X
.
We need the following auxiliary space consisting of functions with two variables
x
and
z
:

{\mathcal{K}}=\left\{f:f(x,z)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}(x)k_{\lambda_{j}}(x,z),\ f_{j}\in L^{\infty}(X),\ \lambda_{j}\in\Omega,\ n\geq 1\right\}.

Then,
{\mathcal{K}}
is a vector space and invariant under multiplication by functions in
L^{\infty}(X)
.
We shall mention the following rather trivial fact as a proposition.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
f
be any function in
{\mathcal{K}}
.
Then,
\|f(x,\cdot)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(x)}
is a square integrable function with variable
x
.

Proof.

We set
f(x,z)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}(x)k_{\lambda_{j}}(x,z)
.
Then, we have that

\displaystyle\|f(x,\cdot)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(x)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\overline{f_{i}(x)}f_{j}(x)\langle k_{\lambda_{j}}(x,\cdot),k_{\lambda_{i}}(x,\cdot)\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}(x)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\overline{f_{i}(x)}f_{j}(x)k(x,\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j}).

Hence, by Assumption (A),
\|f(x,\cdot)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(x)}^{2}
is measurable.
Moreover, by Assumption (C),
\|k_{\lambda_{j}}(x,\cdot)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(x)}
is square integrable.
Hence, we have that

\displaystyle\int_{X}\|f(x,\cdot)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(x)}^{2}\ d\sigma(x)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\left|\overline{f_{i}(x)}f_{j}(x)k(x,\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j})\right|\ d\sigma(x)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\|f_{i}\|_{\infty}\|f_{j}\|_{\infty}\int_{X}\|k_{\lambda_{i}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(x)}\|k_{\lambda_{j}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(x)}\ d\sigma(x)

\displaystyle<\infty.

This concludes the proof.
∎

We need the following Riesz–Fischer type lemma.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\{f_{n}\}_{n}
be a sequence in
{\mathcal{K}}
.
If

\int_{X}\|f_{n}(x,\cdot)-f_{m}(x,\cdot)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(x)}^{2}\ d\sigma(x)\to 0\quad(n,m\to\infty),

then,
for almost all
x
,
there exists a function
f(x,z)
in
{\mathcal{H}}(x)
such that

\int_{X}\|f(x,\cdot)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(x)}^{2}\ d\sigma(x)<\infty

and

\int_{X}\|f_{n}(x,\cdot)-f(x,\cdot)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(x)}^{2}\ d\sigma(x)\to 0\quad(n\to\infty).

Proof.

Since this lemma is essentially the same as the Riesz–Fischer theorem,
we give a sketch of the proof.
It follows from the assumption that
there exists a subsequence
\{f_{n_{k}}\}_{k}
of
\{f_{n}\}_{n}
such that

\int_{X}\|f_{n_{k+1}}(x,\cdot)-f_{n_{k}}(x,\cdot)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(x)}^{2}\ d\sigma(x)<\frac{1}{2^{2k}}.

Then, since

g(x)=\|f_{n_{1}}(x,\cdot)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(x)}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\|f_{n_{k+1}}(x,\cdot)-f_{n_{k}}(x,\cdot)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(x)}

is square integrable by the monotone convergence theorem.
In particular,
g(x)
is finite for almost all
x
in
X
.
Hence,
we have that

\|f_{n_{\ell}}(x,\cdot)-f_{n_{k}}(x,\cdot)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(x)}\leq\sum_{j=k}^{\ell-1}\|f_{n_{j+1}}(x,\cdot)-f_{n_{j}}(x,\cdot)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(x)}\to 0\quad(k,\ell\to\infty)

for almost all
x
in
X
.
Therefore,
for almost all
x
,
there exists a function
f(x,z)
in
{\mathcal{H}}(x)

such that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\|f_{n_{k}}(x,\cdot)-f(x,\cdot)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(x)}=0.

Then,
since
\|f_{n_{k}}(x,\cdot)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(x)}\leq g(x)
,
by Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem, we have

\int_{X}\|f(x,\cdot)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(x)}^{2}\ d\sigma(x)<\infty.

Further,
by Fatou’s lemma, we have

\int_{X}\|f_{n}(x,\cdot)-f(x,\cdot)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(x)}^{2}\ d\sigma(x)\to 0\quad(n\to\infty).

∎

2.4
L^{2}({\mathcal{H}}(x))

Let
L^{2}({\mathcal{H}}(x))
denote the set of all functions obtained by the manner of Lemma
2.3
.
Setting

\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}}(x))}=\left(\int_{X}\|f(x,\cdot)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(x)}^{2}\ d\sigma(x)\right)^{1/2},

we may write

L^{2}({\mathcal{H}}(x))=\left\{f:\exists f_{n}\in{\mathcal{K}}\ \mbox{s.t.}\ \lim_{n\to\infty}\|f_{n}-f\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}}(x))}=0\right\}.

In what follows, two functions

f
and
g
in
L^{2}({\mathcal{H}}(x))
will be identified if

\|f-g\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}}(x))}=0
.
We summarize the basic properties on
L^{2}({\mathcal{H}}(x))
as follows:

Theorem 2.4
.

L^{2}({\mathcal{H}}(x))
is an
L^{\infty}
-Hilbert module consisting of functions with two variables
x
and
z
.
Moreover, let
f
be any function in
L^{2}({\mathcal{H}}(x))
. Then,

f(x,z)
is a measurable function with variable
x
when
z
is fixed and

f(x,z)
is a holomorphic function on
\Omega
with variable
z
for almost all
x
.

Proof.

The proof of the first half is the same as that of Lemma
2.3
.
We give the proof of the second half.
Let
\{f_{n}\}_{n}
be a sequence in
{\mathcal{K}}
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|f_{n}-f\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}}(x))}=0.

Moreover, without loss of generality, we may assume that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|f_{n}(x,\cdot)-f(x,\cdot)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(x)}=0

for almost all
x
in
X
.
Then, for any
\lambda
in
\Omega
, we have that

|f_{n}(x,\lambda)-f(x,\lambda)|\leq\|f_{n}(x,\cdot)-f(x,\cdot)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(x)}\|k_{\lambda}(x,\cdot)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(x)}\to 0\quad(n\to\infty).

By Assumptions (A), (B) and (D), we have the conclusion.
∎

Example 2.5
.

Suppose that
h(x,z)
is a nonvanishing function on
X\times\Omega
and

h(x,z)
belongs to
L^{\infty}(X)
if
z
is fixed.
We shall consider kernel function
k(x,z,w)=\overline{h(x,w)}h(x,z)
. Then,
it is easy to see that

{\mathcal{K}}=\{f:f(x,z)=g(x)h(x,z),\ g\in L^{\infty}(X)\}

and

\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}}(x))}^{2}=\int_{X}|g(x)|^{2}\|h(x,\cdot)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(x)}^{2}\ d\sigma(x)

for any
f(x,z)=g(x)h(x,z)
in
{\mathcal{K}}
.
Moreover, since

|h(x,w)|^{2}=k(x,w,w)=\left\|\overline{h(x,w)}h(x,\cdot)\right\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(x)}^{2}=|h(x,w)|^{2}\|h(x,\cdot)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(x)}^{2},

we have that
\|h(x,\cdot)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(x)}^{2}=1
.
Hence, if
\{f_{n}\}_{n}
is a Cauchy sequence in
{\mathcal{K}}
, then
so is
\{g_{n}\}_{n}
in
L^{2}(X)
, where we set
f_{n}(x,z)=g_{n}(x)h(x,z)
.
This observation concludes that

L^{2}({\mathcal{H}}(x))=\{f:f(x,z)=g(x)h(x,z),\ g\in L^{2}(X)\}.

3
Integral operators

Let
\{B(x,z)\}_{x\in X}
be a family consisting of holomorphic functions on
\Omega
satisfying

(E)

\|B\|_{\infty,C}=\sup_{x\in X,z\in C}|B(x,z)|
is finite for any compact subset
C
of
\Omega
.

In this section, we consider the integral operator defined as follows:

{\mathbb{I}}_{B}:f\mapsto\int_{X}B(x,z)f(x,z)\ d\sigma(x)\quad(f\in L^{2}({\mathcal{H}}(x))).

3.1
Basic properties

First, we note that
({\mathbb{I}}_{B}f)(\lambda)
is finite for any
\lambda
in
\Omega
.
Indeed, by Assumption (E) we have that

\displaystyle\int_{X}|B(x,\lambda)f(x,\lambda)|\ d\sigma(x)

\displaystyle\leq\|B\|_{\infty,\{\lambda\}}\int_{X}|f(x,\lambda)|\ d\sigma(x)

\displaystyle\leq\|B\|_{\infty,\{\lambda\}}\int_{X}\|f(x,\cdot)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(x)}\|k_{\lambda}(x,\cdot)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(x)}\ d\sigma(x)

\displaystyle\leq\|B\|_{\infty,\{\lambda\}}\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}}(x))}\|k_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}}(x))}.

Moreover, we have that

|({\mathbb{I}}_{B}f)(\lambda)-({\mathbb{I}}_{B}g)(\lambda)|\leq\|B\|_{\infty,\{\lambda\}}\|f-g\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}}(x))}\|k_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}}(x))}

(3.1)

for any
f
and
g
in
L^{2}({\mathcal{H}}(x))
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
\lambda
in
\Omega
,
{\mathbb{I}}_{B}k_{\lambda}
is a holomorphic function on
\Omega
.

Proof.

Let
a
be a point in
\Omega
and
let
c_{j}(x,\lambda)
denote the
j
-th coefficient of the power series expansion centered at
a
of
k_{\lambda}(x,z)
.
Then,
choosing appropriate
R>0
,
we have that

\displaystyle|c_{j}(x,\lambda)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{|B(x,a+Re^{i\theta})k_{\lambda}(x,a+Re^{i\theta})|}{R^{j}}\ d\theta

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|B\|_{\infty,C}}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\|k_{a+Re^{i\theta}}(x,\cdot)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(x)}\|k_{\lambda}(x,\cdot)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(x)}}{R^{j}}\ d\theta

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|B\|_{\infty,C}\sqrt{M_{C}(x)}\|k_{\lambda}(x,\cdot)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(x)}}{R^{j}},

where
C
is chosen as
C=\{a+Re^{i\theta}:0\leq\theta<2\pi\}
.
Hence, if
|z-a|<r<R
, then

\displaystyle\int_{X}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}|c_{j}(x,\lambda)(z-a)^{j}|\right)\ d\sigma(x)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{\|B\|_{\infty,C}\sqrt{M_{C}(x)}\|k_{\lambda}(x,\cdot)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(x)}}{R^{j}}r^{j}\right)\ d\sigma(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{R}{R-r}\|B\|_{\infty,C}\left(\int_{X}M_{C}(x)\ d\sigma(x)\right)^{1/2}\|k_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}}(x))}

\displaystyle<\infty

by Assumption (C).
Hence, by Fubini’s theorem,

\displaystyle({\mathbb{I}}_{B}k_{\lambda})(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{X}B(x,z)k_{\lambda}(x,z)\ d\sigma(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}c_{j}(x,\lambda)(z-a)^{j}\right)\ d\sigma(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{X}c_{j}(x,\lambda)\ d\sigma(x)\right)(z-a)^{j}

is holomorphic at
a
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

The range space of
{\mathbb{I}}_{B}
is a subspace of the vector space consisting of holomorphic functions on
\Omega

and
\ker{\mathbb{I}}_{B}
is closed.

Proof.

Let
f
be any function in
L^{2}({\mathcal{H}}(x))
. Then, by definition, there exists a sequence
\{f_{n}\}_{n}
in
{\mathcal{K}}
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|f_{n}-f\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}}(x))}=0.

Then, by (
3.1
),
({\mathbb{I}}_{B}f_{n})(\lambda)
converges to
({\mathbb{I}}_{B}f)(\lambda)
for every
\lambda
in
\Omega
.
Moreover, by Assumptions (D) and (E), this convergence is uniform on any compact subset of
\Omega
.
Hence, by Lemma
3.1
,
({\mathbb{I}}_{B}f)(z)
is a holomorphic function on
\Omega
.
Next,
let
f
be a function in the closure of
\ker{\mathbb{I}}_{B}
and let
\{f_{n}\}_{n}
be a sequence in
\ker{\mathbb{I}}_{B}
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|f_{n}-f\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}}(x))}=0.

Then,
by (
3.1
), we have that

\displaystyle|({\mathbb{I}}_{B}f)(\lambda)|

\displaystyle=|({\mathbb{I}}_{B}f)(\lambda)-({\mathbb{I}}_{B}f_{n})(\lambda)|

\displaystyle\leq\|B\|_{\infty,\{\lambda\}}\|f-f_{n}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}}(x))}\|k_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}}(x))}

\displaystyle\to 0\quad(n\to\infty)

for any
\lambda
in
\Omega
.
Hence
\ker{\mathbb{I}}
is closed.
∎

By Lemma
3.2
and the pull-back construction,
we can endow the range space of
{\mathbb{I}}_{B}
with a Hilbert space structure as follows:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}({\mathbb{I}}_{B};L^{2}({\mathcal{H}}(x)))

\displaystyle={\mathcal{M}}({\mathbb{I}}_{B})=(\operatorname{ran}{\mathbb{I}}_{B},\ \|\cdot\|_{{\mathcal{M}}({\mathbb{I}}_{B})}),

\displaystyle\|{\mathbb{I}}_{B}f\|_{{\mathcal{M}}({\mathbb{I}}_{B})}

\displaystyle=\|P_{(\ker{\mathbb{I}}_{B})^{\perp}}f\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}}(x))}.

Theorem 3.3
.

{\mathcal{M}}({\mathbb{I}}_{B})
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space and
its reproducing kernel is

\left({\mathbb{I}}_{B}\overline{B(\cdot,\lambda)}k_{\lambda}\right)(z)=\int_{X}\overline{B(x,\lambda)}B(x,z)k_{\lambda}(x,z)\ d\sigma(x).

In particular, for any function
F
in
{\mathcal{M}}({\mathbb{I}}_{B})
, there exists some function
f
in
L^{2}({\mathcal{H}}(x))
such that

F(z)=\int_{X}B(x,z)f(x,z)\ d\sigma(x).

Proof.

We set
h_{\lambda}(x,z)=\overline{B(x,\lambda)}k_{\lambda}(x,z)

If
g
belongs to
\ker{\mathbb{I}}_{B}
, then

\displaystyle\langle g,h_{\lambda}\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}}(x))}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\langle g(x,\cdot),h_{\lambda}(x,\cdot)\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}(x))}\ d\sigma(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{X}B(x,\lambda)g(x,\lambda)\ d\sigma(x)

\displaystyle=({\mathbb{I}}_{B}g)(\lambda)

\displaystyle=0.

This concludes that
h_{\lambda}
belongs to
(\ker{\mathbb{I}}_{B})^{\perp}
.
Hence, for any function
f
in
L^{2}({\mathcal{H}}(x))
, we have that

\displaystyle\langle{\mathbb{I}}_{B}f,{\mathbb{I}}_{B}h_{\lambda}\rangle_{{\mathcal{M}}({\mathbb{I}})}

\displaystyle=\langle f,h_{\lambda}\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}}(x))}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\langle f(x,\cdot),h_{\lambda}(x,\cdot)\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}(x)}\ d\sigma(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{X}B(x,\lambda)f(x,\lambda)\ d\sigma(x)

\displaystyle=({\mathbb{I}}_{B}f)(\lambda).

Therefore,

{\mathbb{I}}_{B}h_{\lambda}
is the reproducing kernel of
{\mathcal{M}}({\mathbb{I}}_{B})
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

In de Branges
[
3
]
, the following type of integral operator was studied:

{\mathbb{J}}_{B}:f\mapsto\int_{X}f(x,B(x,z))\ d\sigma(x)\quad(f\in L^{2}({\mathcal{H}}(x))).

In our framework,
under suitable conditions on
k
and
B
,
it is shown that

({\mathbb{J}}_{B}f)(\lambda)
is finite for any
\lambda
in
\Omega
and

|({\mathbb{J}}_{B}f)(\lambda)-({\mathbb{J}}_{B}g)(\lambda)|\leq\|f-g\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}}(x))}\left(\int_{X}k(x,B(x,\lambda),B(x,\lambda))\ d\sigma(x)\right)^{1/2}

for any
f
and
g
in
L^{2}({\mathcal{H}}(x))
. It follows from this estimate that

{\mathcal{M}}({\mathbb{J}}_{B})
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space with kernel function

\int_{X}k(x,B(x,z),B(x,\lambda))\ d\sigma(x).

3.2
Examples

Example 3.5

(Paley–Wiener spaces)
.

Let
A
be a positive constant.
We consider kernel function

\frac{\sin(2\pi A(z-\overline{w}))}{\pi(z-\overline{w})}

defined on
{\mathbb{C}}\times{\mathbb{C}}
.
Then, the reproducing kernel Hilbert space generated by this kernel function
is a Payley–Wiener space, which will be dented as
PW_{A}
.
In Theorem
3.3
,
we consider the case where
k(t,z,w)=1
,
X=[-A,A]
,
d\sigma=dt
and
B(t,z)=e^{-2\pi izt}
. Then,

{\mathcal{H}}(t)={\mathbb{C}}
,

L^{2}({\mathcal{H}}(t))=L^{2}[-A,A]
.
Hence,

{\mathbb{I}}_{B}:f(t,z)\mapsto F(z)=\int_{-A}^{A}B(t,z)f(t,z)\ dt\quad(f\in L^{2}({\mathcal{H}}(t))).

is the time-limited Fourier transform.
Moreover,

{\mathcal{M}}({\mathbb{I}}_{B};L^{2}([-A,A]))=PW_{A}
as reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces.
To see this, by Theorem
3.3
, it suffices to show that
{\mathcal{M}}({\mathbb{I}}_{B})
has the same reproducing kernel as that of
PW_{A}
.
Setting
h_{\lambda}(t,z)=\overline{B(t,z)}k_{\lambda}(t,z)
, we have that

({\mathbb{I}}_{B}h_{\lambda})(z)=\int_{-A}^{A}B(t,z)h_{\lambda}(t,z)\ dt\\
=\int_{-A}^{A}e^{-2\pi it(z-\overline{\lambda})}\ dt\\
=\frac{\sin(2\pi A(z-\overline{\lambda}))}{\pi(z-\overline{\lambda})}.

Thus, we have
{\mathcal{M}}({\mathbb{I}}_{B})=PW_{A}
.

Example 3.6

(Herglotz spaces)
.

Let
\varphi
be a Herglotz function, that is,

\varphi
is a holomorphic function on
{\mathbb{D}}
and
\operatorname{Re}\varphi(z)\geq 0
and
\varphi(0)=1
.
Then,

\varphi
has the following integral representation:

\varphi(z)=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{1+\xi z}{1-\xi z}\ d\mu(\xi),

where
\mu
is a probability measure on the unit circle
{\mathbb{T}}
.
The integrand

\varphi_{\xi}(z)=\frac{1+\xi z}{1-\xi z}

is a typical example of a Herglotz function.
Then, since

\frac{\overline{\varphi_{\xi}(w)}+\varphi_{\xi}(z)}{1-\overline{w}z}=\frac{2}{(1-\overline{\xi w})(1-\xi z)},

(3.2)

we have

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\varphi
be a Herglotz function. Then,

\dfrac{\overline{\varphi(w)}+\varphi(z)}{1-\overline{w}z}

is a kernel function on
{\mathbb{D}}\times{\mathbb{D}}
.

The reproducing kernel Hilbert space generated by the kernel function in Proposition
3.7

is called a Herglotz space, which will be dented as
{\mathcal{L}}(\varphi)
.
Herglotz spaces were introduced and studied by de Branges–Rovnyak
[
4
]

(see also Ghosechowdhury
[
9
,
10
]
and Shulman
[
17
]
).
Next, we consider the integral operator defined as follows:

{\mathbb{I}}:f(\xi,z)\mapsto F(z)=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}f(\xi,z)\ d\mu(\xi)\quad(f\in L^{2}({\mathcal{L}}(\varphi_{\xi})))

which is obtained by setting
B\equiv 1
.
Then,

{\mathcal{M}}({\mathbb{I}};L^{2}({\mathcal{L}}(\varphi_{\xi})))={\mathcal{L}}(\varphi)
as reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces.
To see this, by Theorem
3.3
,
it suffices to show that
{\mathcal{M}}({\mathbb{I}})
has the same reproducing kernel as that of
{\mathcal{L}}(\varphi)
.
Setting
k_{\lambda}(\xi,z)=k(\xi,z,\lambda)
, we have that

({\mathbb{I}}k_{\lambda})(z)=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}k_{\lambda}(\xi,z)\ d\mu(\xi)=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\overline{\varphi_{\xi}(\lambda)}+\varphi_{\xi}(z)}{1-\overline{\lambda}z}\ d\mu(\xi)=\frac{\overline{\varphi(\lambda)}+\varphi(z)}{1-\overline{\lambda}z}.

Thus, we have
{\mathcal{M}}({\mathbb{I}})={\mathcal{L}}(\varphi)
.
Moreover, note that
{\mathcal{L}}(\varphi_{\xi})
is of one dimension by (
3.2
).

4
Löwner expansions

Let
\{B_{rs}(z)\}_{0\leq r\leq s}
be a Löwner semigroup, that is,
each
B_{rs}(z)
is an injective and holomorphic self-map of
{\mathbb{D}}
satisfying
the nonlinear Löwner equation

\frac{\partial B_{rs}}{\partial s}(z)=-B_{rs}(z)\varphi(s,B_{rs}(z)),

(4.1)

where
\varphi(t,z)
is some Herglotz function.
In what follows, we will write
B_{t}=B_{at}
for short if no confusion occurs.
Then, for any
t\geq 0
,

k(t,z,w)=\frac{\overline{\varphi(t,B_{t}(w))}+\varphi(t,B_{t}(z))}{1-\overline{w}z}

is a kernel function defined on
{\mathbb{D}}\times{\mathbb{D}}
by Proposition
3.7
.
Then, trivially, Assumptions (A) and (B) are valid.
Moreover, Assumptions (C) and (D) follow from the following estimate:

0\leq k(t,\lambda,\lambda)=\dfrac{2\operatorname{Re}\varphi(t,B_{t}(\lambda))}{1-|\lambda|^{2}}\leq\dfrac{2(1+|B_{t}(\lambda)|)}{(1-|\lambda|^{2})(1-|B_{t}(\lambda)|)}

for any
\lambda
in
{\mathbb{D}}
(see (8–10) in Page 193 of Rosenblum–Rovnyak
[
14
]
).
Moreover, trivially, Assumption (E) is valid for
B(t,z)=B_{t}(z)
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
B_{t}(z)=B_{at}(z)

(t\geq a\geq 0)
be the Löwner semigroup. Then,

\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\dfrac{1-\overline{B_{t}(\lambda)}B_{t}(z)}{1-\overline{\lambda}z}=\dfrac{\overline{\varphi(t,B_{t}(\lambda))}+\varphi(t,B_{t}(z))}{1-\overline{\lambda}z}\overline{B_{t}(\lambda)}B_{t}(z).

Proof.

By the nonlinear Löwner equation (
4.1
),
we have the conclusion.
∎

Let
{\mathcal{H}}(B_{t})
denote the reproducing kernel Hilbert space
generated by kernel function

\dfrac{1-\overline{B_{t}(w)}B_{t}(z)}{1-\overline{w}z},

which is called the de Branges–Rovnyak space induced by
B_{t}
(see de Branges–Rovnyak
[
4
,
5
]
,
Fricain–Mashreghi
[
8
]
and Sarason
[
15
]
).
Every function in
{\mathcal{H}}(B_{t})
has a Fourier type integral representation.

Theorem 4.2

(Ghosechowdhury
[
10
]
)
.

Let
B_{t}(z)=B_{at}(z)

(t\geq a\geq 0)
be the Löwner semigroup. Then,

{\mathcal{H}}(B_{b})={\mathbb{C}}\oplus{\mathcal{M}}({\mathbb{I}}_{B};L^{2}({\mathcal{L}}(\varphi(t,B_{t})))).

Proof.

We will write
h_{\lambda}(t,z)=\overline{B_{t}(\lambda)}k_{\lambda}(t,z)
.
It follows from Theorem
3.3
and Lemma
4.1
that

\displaystyle\langle{\mathbb{I}}_{B}h_{\lambda},{\mathbb{I}}_{B}h_{\mu}\rangle_{{\mathcal{M}}({\mathbb{I}}_{B})}

\displaystyle=\langle h_{\lambda},h_{\mu}\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathcal{L}}(\varphi(t,B_{t})))}

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{b}\langle\overline{B_{t}(\lambda)}k_{\lambda}(t,\cdot),\overline{B_{t}(\mu)}k_{\mu}(t,\cdot)\rangle_{{\mathcal{L}}(\varphi(t,B_{t}))}\ dt

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{b}\overline{B_{t}(\lambda)}B_{t}(\mu)k(t,\mu,\lambda)\ dt

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{b}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\dfrac{1-\overline{B_{t}(\lambda)}B_{t}(\mu)}{1-\overline{\lambda}\mu}\ dt

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1-\overline{B_{b}(\lambda)}B_{b}(\mu)}{1-\overline{\lambda}\mu}-1.

Since
1
belongs to
{\mathcal{H}}(B_{b})
,

\dfrac{1-\overline{B_{b}(\lambda)}B_{b}(z)}{1-\overline{\lambda}z}-1

is the reproducing kernel of
{\mathcal{H}}(B_{b})\ominus{\mathbb{C}}
, where the orthogonal complement is taken with respect to the inner product of
{\mathcal{H}}(B_{b})
.
Hence we have that
{\mathcal{M}}({\mathbb{I}}_{B})={\mathcal{H}}(B_{b})\ominus{\mathbb{C}}
.
This concludes the proof.
∎

Here, we would like to note that
Ghosechowdhury’s theorem suggests a new insight to the problem

“
Find concrete elements in de Branges–Rovnyak spaces
”

discussed in (II-8) of Sarason
[
15
]
, Sections 18.6 and 23.8 of Fricain–Mashreghi
[
8
]
, Fricain–Hartmann–Ross
[
7
]
and Łanucha–Nowak
[
11
]
.
Combining
Theorem
2.4
with
the observations in Examples
2.5
and
3.6
,
we see that every function in
{\mathcal{H}}(B_{b})
has a double integral representation.
Indeed, let
F
be any function in
{\mathcal{H}}(B_{b})
.
Then, by Theorem
4.2
,
there exists some function
f
in
L^{2}({\mathcal{L}}(\varphi(t,B_{t})))
such that

F(z)=F(0)+({\mathbb{I}}_{B}f)(z)=F(0)+\int_{a}^{b}B_{t}(z)f(t,z)\ dt.

Moreover, for each fixed
t
,
it follows from the observation in Example
3.6

that there exists some function
g
in
L^{2}({\mathcal{L}}(\varphi_{\xi}(B_{t})))
such that

f(t,z)=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}g(\xi,t,z)\ d\mu_{t}(\xi).

Hence we have that

F(z)=F(0)+\int_{a}^{b}B_{t}(z)\left(\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}g(\xi,t,z)\ d\mu_{t}(\xi)\right)\ dt.

Furthermore,
since

\frac{\overline{\varphi_{\xi}(B_{t}(\lambda))}+\varphi_{\xi}(B_{t}(z))}{1-\overline{\lambda}z}=\frac{1-\overline{B_{t}(\lambda)}B_{t}(z)}{1-\overline{\lambda}z}\cdot\frac{2}{\left(1-\overline{\xi B_{t}(\lambda)}\right)\left(1-\xi B_{t}(z)\right)},

F(z)=\int_{a}^{b}B_{t}(z)\frac{1-\overline{B_{t}(\lambda)}B_{t}(z)}{1-\overline{\lambda}z}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{h(\xi,t)}{\left(1-\overline{\xi B_{t}(\lambda)}\right)\left(1-\xi B_{t}(z)\right)}\ d\mu_{t}(\xi)\right)\ dt

(4.2)

belongs to
{\mathcal{H}}(B_{b})
for any bounded Borel function
h
.
Note that

B_{t}/z
is outer by Theorem 3.17 in Duren
[
6
]
,
and is extreme by Figure 8.2 in Rosenblum–Rovnyak
[
14
]
.
We shall answer Question 6.6 in
[
7
]
.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
B_{t}(z)=B_{at}(z)

(t\geq a\geq 0)
be the Löwner semigroup associated with the Koebe functions

f(t,z)=\frac{e^{t}z}{(1-z)^{2}}\quad(t\geq 0).

Then,

\log\frac{1-B_{t}(z)}{1-z}

belongs to
{\mathcal{H}}(B_{t})
.

Proof.

Let
b
be an arbitrary point satisfying
a\leq b
.
Since
\mu_{t}
is the Dirac measure centered on
\xi=-1
for all
t\geq 0

and

\varphi(t,z)=\varphi_{-1}(z)=\frac{1-z}{1+z}

for the Koebe functions,
it follows from (
4.2
) that

F(z)=\int_{a}^{b}B_{t}(z)\frac{1-\overline{B_{t}(\lambda)}B_{t}(z)}{1-\overline{\lambda}z}\frac{h(t)}{\left(1+\overline{B_{t}(\lambda)}\right)\left(1+B_{t}(z)\right)}\ dt

belongs to
{\mathcal{H}}(B_{b})
for any bounded Borel function
h
and any
\lambda
in
{\mathbb{D}}
.
Setting
h(t)=1
and
\lambda=0
, by the nonlinear Löwner equation (
4.1
), we have that

\displaystyle F(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{b}\frac{B_{t}(z)}{1+B_{t}(z)}\ dt

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{b}\frac{-1}{1-B_{t}(z)}\left(-B_{t}(z)\frac{1-B_{t}(z)}{1+B_{t}(z)}\right)\ dt

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{b}\frac{-1}{1-B_{t}(z)}\frac{\partial B_{t}}{\partial t}(z)\ dt

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{b}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\log\left(1-B_{t}(z)\right)\ dt

\displaystyle=\log\frac{1-B_{b}(z)}{1-z}.

This concludes the proof.
∎

5
Pick spaces

Let
\varphi
be a Pick function, that is,

\varphi
is a holomorphic self-map of the upper half-plane
{\mathbb{H}}=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:\operatorname{Im}z>0\}
.
Every Pick function has a Nevanlinna representation

\varphi(z)=b+cz+\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{t-z}-\frac{t}{1+t^{2}}\right)\ d\mu(t),

(5.1)

where
b
is real,
c\geq 0
and
\mu
is a nonnegative Borel measure on
(-\infty,\infty)
satisfying

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1+t^{2}}\ d\mu(t)<\infty,

and the converse is also true (see Theorem 5.3 in Rosenblum–Rovnyak
[
14
]
).

Example 5.1
.

Setting

\varphi_{\xi}(z)=\frac{1}{\xi-z}\quad(\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}),

this
\varphi_{\xi}
is a typical example of a Pick function,
which is obtained from (
5.1
) if we choose
\mu
as the Dirac measure centered on
\xi
.

Let
\varphi
be a Pick function.
Then
\psi
will denote the function defined by the following diagram:

\begin{CD}{\mathbb{H}}@>{\varphi}>{}>{\mathbb{H}}\\
@A{T}A{}A@V{}V{T^{-1}}V\\
{\mathbb{D}}@>{}>{\psi}>{\mathbb{D}},\end{CD}

(5.2)

where

T(z)=i\dfrac{1+z}{1-z}\quad\text{and}\quad T^{-1}(w)=\frac{w-i}{w+i}.

Then,
\psi
is a holomorphic self-map of
{\mathbb{D}}
.
Let
{\mathcal{H}}(\psi)
denote the de Branges–Rovnyak space induced by
\psi
, that is,

{\mathcal{H}}(\psi)
is the reproducing kernel Hilbert space generated by kernel function

\frac{1-\overline{\psi(w)}\psi(z)}{1-\overline{w}z}.

Let
\alpha
and
\beta
be arbitrary two points in
{\mathbb{H}}
.
We set
\lambda=T^{-1}(\alpha)
and
\mu=T^{-1}(\beta)
.
Then, by direct calculation, we have that

\frac{\varphi(\beta)-\overline{\varphi(\alpha)}}{\beta-\overline{\alpha}}=\frac{1-\overline{\lambda}}{1-\overline{\psi(\lambda)}}\cdot\frac{1-\mu}{1-\psi(\mu)}\cdot\frac{1-\overline{\psi(\lambda)}\psi(\mu)}{1-\overline{\lambda}\mu}.

(5.3)

By (
5.3
) and Schur’s theorem,
we have the following well known fact.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\varphi
be a Pick function. Then,

\frac{\varphi(z)-\overline{\varphi(w)}}{z-\overline{w}}

is a kernel function on
{\mathbb{H}}\times{\mathbb{H}}
.

Let
{\mathcal{P}}(\varphi)
denote
the reproducing kernel Hilbert space generated by the kernel function in Proposition
5.2
.
We will call
{\mathcal{P}}(\varphi)
a Pick space.
In particular, we note that

\frac{\varphi_{\xi}(z)-\overline{\varphi_{\xi}(w)}}{z-\overline{w}}=\frac{1}{(\xi-\overline{w})(\xi-z)}.

(5.4)

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
\varphi
be a Pick function
with Nevanlinna representation (
5.1
),
and let
{\mathcal{M}}
denote the reproducing kernel Hilbert space over
{\mathbb{H}}
generated by kernel function

k(z,w)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(t-\overline{w})(t-z)}\ d\mu(t).

Then,

{\mathcal{P}}(\varphi)=\begin{cases}{\mathbb{C}}+{\mathcal{M}}&(c\neq 0)\\
{\mathcal{M}}&(c=0)\end{cases}

as reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces,
where we consider
{\mathbb{C}}
to be the reproducing kernel Hilbert space consisting of constant functions on
{\mathbb{H}}
.

Proof.

It follows from the Nevanlinna representation (
5.1
) that

\displaystyle\varphi(z)-\overline{\varphi(w)}

\displaystyle=c(z-\overline{w})+\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{t-z}-\frac{1}{t-\overline{w}}\right)\ d\mu(t)

\displaystyle=c(z-\overline{w})+\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{z-\overline{w}}{(t-\overline{w})(t-z)}\ d\mu(t).

Hence we have that

\frac{\varphi(z)-\overline{\varphi(w)}}{z-\overline{w}}=c+\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(t-\overline{w})(t-z)}\ d\mu(t).

By this identity and Aronszajn’s sums of kernels theorem
(see Theorem 5.4 in Paulsen–Raghupathi
[
13
]
), we conclude the proof.
∎

Next, we shall give an isomorphism between
{\mathcal{P}}(\varphi)
and
{\mathcal{H}}(\psi)
.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
\varphi
be a Pick function and
\psi
be the function defined by (
6.2
).
Then,

V:f(z)\mapsto\frac{1-T^{-1}(w)}{1-\psi\circ T^{-1}(w)}f\circ T^{-1}(w)\quad(w=T(z))

gives an isometry from
{\mathcal{H}}(\psi)
onto
{\mathcal{P}}(\varphi)
.

Proof.

In this proof, we will write

\frac{1-T^{-1}(w)}{1-\psi\circ T^{-1}(w)}f\circ T^{-1}(w)=\frac{1-z}{1-\psi(z)}f(z)

for short.
It follows from (
5.3
) that

\frac{1-z}{1-\psi(z)}\cdot\dfrac{1-\overline{\psi(\lambda)}\psi(z)}{1-\overline{\lambda}z}=\frac{1-\overline{\psi(\lambda)}}{1-\overline{\lambda}}\cdot\frac{\varphi(w)-\overline{\varphi(\alpha)}}{w-\overline{\alpha}}

belongs to
{\mathcal{P}}(\varphi)
.
Moreover, by (
5.3
) again, we have that

\displaystyle\left\langle V\dfrac{1-\overline{\psi(\lambda)}\psi(z)}{1-\overline{\lambda}z},V\dfrac{1-\overline{\psi(\mu)}\psi(z)}{1-\overline{\mu}z}\right\rangle_{{\mathcal{P}}(\varphi)}

\displaystyle\quad=\left\langle\frac{1-z}{1-\psi(z)}\cdot\dfrac{1-\overline{\psi(\lambda)}\psi(z)}{1-\overline{\lambda}z},\frac{1-z}{1-\psi(z)}\cdot\dfrac{1-\overline{\psi(\mu)}\psi(z)}{1-\overline{\mu}z}\right\rangle_{{\mathcal{P}}(\varphi)}

\displaystyle\quad=\left\langle\frac{1-\overline{\psi(\lambda)}}{1-\overline{\lambda}}\cdot\frac{\varphi(w)-\overline{\varphi(\alpha)}}{w-\overline{\alpha}},\frac{1-\overline{\psi(\mu)}}{1-\overline{\mu}}\cdot\frac{\varphi(w)-\overline{\varphi(\beta)}}{w-\overline{\beta}}\right\rangle_{{\mathcal{P}}(\varphi)}

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1-\overline{\psi(\lambda)}}{1-\overline{\lambda}}\cdot\frac{1-\psi(\mu)}{1-\mu}\cdot\frac{\varphi(\beta)-\overline{\varphi(\alpha)}}{\beta-\overline{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1-\overline{\psi(\lambda)}\psi(\mu)}{1-\overline{\lambda}\mu}

\displaystyle\quad=\left\langle\dfrac{1-\overline{\psi(\lambda)}\psi(z)}{1-\overline{\lambda}z},\dfrac{1-\overline{\psi(\mu)}\psi(z)}{1-\overline{\mu}z}\right\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}(\psi)}.

Thus we have the conclusion.
∎

As an application of Theorem
5.4
,
nontrivial elements of
{\mathcal{H}}(\psi)
are obtained.
For example, if
c\neq 0
in Proposition
5.3
,
then constant function
1
belongs to
{\mathcal{P}}(\varphi)
.
Hence, by Theorem
5.4
, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 5.5
.

If
c\neq 0
, then

\frac{1-\psi(z)}{1-z}

belongs to
{\mathcal{H}}(\psi)
.

In the next Section, we will see that
further elements in
{\mathcal{H}}(\psi)
will be obtained from analysis of
{\mathcal{P}}(\varphi)
.

6
A chordal Löwner expansion

We shall begin with the following fundamental example in the chordal Löwner theory.

Example 6.1
.

We set

B_{rs}(z)=\sqrt{z^{2}-2(s-r)}\quad(0\leq r\leq s).

Then,
B_{rs}
is a univalent (i.e. holomorphic and injective) map from
{\mathbb{H}}
onto
{\mathbb{H}}\setminus\{iy:0<y\leq\sqrt{2(r-s)}\}
.
Moreover,

B_{rs}
is the solution of

\frac{\partial B_{rs}}{\partial s}(z)=-\frac{1}{B_{rs}(z)}=\varphi_{0}(B_{rs}(z))

(6.1)

with initial condition
B_{ss}(z)=z
.
The differential equation (
6.1
) is the basic case of the chordal Löwner equation

\frac{\partial B_{rs}}{\partial s}(z)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{1}{\xi-B_{rs}(z)}\ d\nu_{s}(\xi)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\varphi_{\xi}(B_{rs}(z))\ d\nu_{s}(\xi),

(6.2)

where
\nu_{s}
is a Borel measure on
{\mathbb{R}}
.
Indeed, choosing
\nu_{s}
as the Dirac measure centered on
\xi=0
for all
s\geq 0
in (
6.2
),
we have (
6.1
). For further details of the chordal Löwner equation, see around (3.7) in Schleißinger
[
16
]
.

Let
B_{t}(z)=B_{at}(z)

(t\geq a\geq 0)
be the family of functions introduced in Example
6.1
.
Then, applying Theorem
3.3
, we consider the integral operator defined as follows:

{\mathbb{I}}_{1/B}:f(t)\mapsto F(z)=\int_{a}^{b}\frac{1}{B_{t}(z)}f(t)\ dt\quad(f\in L^{2}([a,b])).

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
B_{t}(z)=B_{at}(z)

(t\geq a\geq 0)
be the family of functions introduced in Example
6.1
.
Then,

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\frac{B_{t}(w)-\overline{B_{t}(\alpha)}}{w-\overline{\alpha}}=\frac{1}{\overline{B_{t}(\alpha)}B_{t}(w)}\cdot\frac{B_{t}(w)-\overline{B_{t}(\alpha)}}{w-\overline{\alpha}}.

Proof.

By the chordal Löwner equation (
6.1
), we have the conclusion.
∎

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
B_{t}(z)=B_{at}(z)

(t\geq a\geq 0)
be the family of functions introduced in Example
6.1
.
Then,

{\mathcal{P}}(B_{b})=\exp{\mathcal{M}}({\mathbb{I}}_{1/B};L^{2}([a,b])).

Proof.

We set
h_{\alpha}(t)=1/\overline{B_{t}(\alpha)}
. Then,
h_{\alpha}
belongs to
L^{2}([a,b])
and

\exp(({\mathbb{I}}_{1/B}h_{\alpha})(z))=\exp\left(\int_{a}^{b}\frac{1}{\overline{B_{t}(\alpha)}B_{t}(z)}\ dt\right)

is the kernel function generating
\exp{\mathcal{M}}({\mathbb{I}}_{1/B})

(see Exercise (k) in p. 320 of Nikolski
[
12
]
or Chapter 7 in Paulsen–Raghupathi
[
13
]
).
Moreover, by Lemma
6.2
, we have that

\displaystyle\exp(({\mathbb{I}}_{1/B}h_{\alpha})(z))

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\int_{a}^{b}\frac{1}{\overline{B_{t}(\alpha)}B_{t}(z)}\ dt\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\int_{a}^{b}\frac{w-\overline{\alpha}}{B_{t}(w)-\overline{B_{t}(\alpha)}}\cdot\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\dfrac{B_{t}(w)-\overline{B_{t}(\alpha)}}{w-\overline{\alpha}}\ dt\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\int_{a}^{b}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\log\left(\dfrac{B_{t}(w)-\overline{B_{t}(\alpha)}}{w-\overline{\alpha}}\right)\ dt\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{B_{b}(w)-\overline{B_{b}(\alpha)}}{w-\overline{\alpha}}.

This concludes the proof.
∎

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
B_{t}(z)=B_{at}(z)

(t\geq a\geq 0)
be the family of functions introduced in Example
6.1
.
Then,

\exp(w-B_{b}(w))

belongs to
{\mathcal{P}}(B_{t})
.

Proof.

It follows from Theorem
6.3
that

F(w)=\exp\left(\int_{a}^{b}\frac{h(t)}{B_{t}(w)}\ dt\right)

belongs to
{\mathcal{P}}(B_{b})
for any function
h
in
L^{2}([a,b])
.
Setting
h(t)=1
, by the chordal Löwner equation (
6.1
), we have that

\displaystyle F(w)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\int_{a}^{b}\frac{1}{B_{t}(w)}\ dt\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\int_{a}^{b}\frac{\partial B_{t}}{\partial t}\ dt\right)

\displaystyle=\exp(w-B_{b}(w))

This concludes the proof.
∎

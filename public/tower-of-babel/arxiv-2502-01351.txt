Constructing families of 3-Selmer companions
Harry Spencer

Abstract
. Mazur and Rubin introduced the notion of n n -Selmer companion elliptic curves and gave several examples of pairs of non-isogenous Selmer companions. We construct two infinite families of pairs of non-isogenous 3 3 -Selmer companions, parameterised by t ∈ ℤ t\in\mathbb{Z} .

Introduction

We recall the following definition introduced by Mazur and Rubin.

Definition 1.1

(=

Two elliptic curves
{E}_{1}
and

(1)

\Sel_{n}({E}_{1}^{\chi}/k)\cong\Sel_{n}({E}_{2}^{\chi}/k)

Several examples of non-isogenous Selmer companions are given in

3

It is an open problem to find necessary conditions for Selmer companionship, so the techniques and families appearing in this work may be helpful for testing conditions. In particular, Mazur and Rubin speculate that having equal conductors may be a necessary condition, and indeed this holds in our families.

2.
Sufficient conditions

Here we recall the main result of Mazur and Rubin’s work, a set of sufficient conditions (stated below for
p=3

p
-Selmer companionship.

Definition 2.1
.

Given an elliptic curve
{E}/K
over a local field of mixed characteristic and its associated Tate parameter
q\in K^{\times}
with isomorphism
\tau_{E/K}:\overline{K}^{\times}/q^{\mathbb{Z}}\to E(\overline{K})

\mathcal{C}_{E/K}[p]\subset E[p]
to be
\tau_{E/K}(\mu_{p})

(=

Let

3.
Constructing families

Associated to a Weierstrass model of an elliptic curve is a
discriminant curve
.

Definition 3.1
.

Given an elliptic curve
E:y^{2}=f(x)
, the discriminant curve of genus 0 or 1 is given by

D:\Delta^{2}=\disc_{x}(f(x)-y^{2}).

Note, in particular, that the Jacobian
J_{D}
is usually an elliptic curve.

We will also use an alternative construction, as in
[
6
, §2.2]
.

Definition 3.2
.

Given an elliptic curve
E:F(X_{1},X_{2},X_{3})=0
given by the vanishing of a cubic homogeneous polynomial in three variables, the corresponding
Hessian curve
,
\Hess(E)
is defined by the vanishing of the determinant of the Hessian matrix
(\frac{\partial F}{\partial X_{i}\partial X_{j}})_{i,j}
.

Remark 3.3
.

A priori this is not well-defined, but independence of the choice of equation for
E
is shown in
[
6
, §2.2]
.
∎

We verify firstly that these two concepts coincide for elliptic curves given by a Weierstrass equation
y^{2}=f(x)
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
E:y^{2}=f(x)
be an elliptic curve. The Jacobian of the discriminant curve is isomorphic to
\Hess(E)
.

Proof.

Reduce to the case that
E:y^{2}=x^{3}+ax+b
is given by a short Weierstrass equation by noting that the isomorphism class of
D
does not change under a change of variables
x^{\prime}=x+\gamma
.

We may take models

J_{D}:\hskip 14.22636pty^{2}=x^{3}-27bx^{2}-27a^{3}x

and

\Hess(E):\hskip 14.22636pty^{2}=x^{3}-(a^{3}/3+3b^{2})x-(a^{3}b/3+2b^{3}),

as in
[
3
, §6]
and
[
6
, proposition 2.1]
. The isomorphism from
\Hess(E)\to J_{D}
is then given by
(x,y)\mapsto(9(x+b),27y)
.
∎

To the best of the author’s knowledge, the following result first appeared as
[
3
, lemma 6.9, remark 6.10]
, where it is used in a new proof of the parity conjecture for elliptic curves over number fields. A more conceptual proof of this fact can be seen from the Hessian construction, cf.
[

.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
E/k
be an elliptic curve over a number field given by Weierstrass equation
E:y^{2}=f(x)
with discriminant curve of genus
1
. There is a
G_{k}
-module isomorphism
E[3]\cong J_{D}[3]
.

Proof.

The intersection points of
E
and
\Hess(E)
are precisely the inflection points of
E
and
\Hess(E)
, and hence are the
3
-torsion points of both curves.
∎

This interpretation of the isomorphism
E[3]\cong J_{D}[3]
allows us to verify the fourth condition of theorem
2.2
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Consider an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
given by minimal model
E:y^{2}=f(x)
such that
D:\Delta^{2}=\disc_{x}(f(x)-y^{2})
has genus
1
, and suppose that both
E
and
J_{D}
have potentially multiplicative reduction at an odd prime
p
. Then the identification
\alpha:E[3]\to J_{D}[3]
as in proposition
3.5
satisfies

\alpha(\mathcal{C}_{E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}[3])=\mathcal{C}_{J_{D}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}[3].

Proof.

The points
\mathcal{C}_{E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}[3]
are precisely those whose coordinates have non-negative valuation, as with
\mathcal{C}_{\Hess(E)/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}[3]
. By the proof of proposition
3.5
, these must coincide. The result now follows from lemma
3.4
.
∎

We will also need some further observations relating
E
and its discriminant curve.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
E/K:y^{2}=f(x)
be an elliptic curve over a local field of mixed characteristic with residue characteristic at least
5
in Weierstrass form with associated discriminant curve
D/K
of genus 1. We have:

(1)

If
E
has potentially multiplicative reduction, then so does
J_{D}
.

(2)

If
E
has multiplicative reduction, then so does
J_{D}
. Moreover, in this case,
E
has split multiplicative reduction if and only if
J_{D}
does also.

Proof.

Completing the cube does not change the isomorphism class of
D
, so we can assume
f(x)=x^{3}+ax+b
so that
J_{D}:y^{2}=x^{3}-27bx^{2}-27a^{3}x
. The respective discriminants are then

\Delta_{E}=-2^{4}\cdot(4a^{3}+27b^{2}),\hskip 14.22636pt\Delta_{D}:=\Delta_{J_{D}}=3^{9}\cdot a^{6}\cdot\Delta_{E},

with
j
-invariants

j(E)=1728\cdot\frac{4a^{3}}{\Delta_{E}},\hskip 14.22636ptj(D):=j(J_{D})=\frac{(2^{4}\cdot 3^{6}\cdot b^{2}+2^{4}\cdot 3^{4}\cdot a^{3})^{3}}{\Delta_{D}}.

Suppose
E
has potentially multiplicative reduction, so that
v_{K}(\Delta_{E})>3v_{K}(a)
and
3v_{K}(a)=2v_{K}(b)
. We have
v_{K}(j(D))=6v_{K}(b)-v_{K}(\Delta_{D})
and
v_{K}(\Delta_{D})=6v_{K}(a)+v_{K}(\Delta_{E})
. Hence
v_{K}(j(D))=v_{K}(j(E))<0
, establishing (1).

For (2), the first assertion follows from Tate’s algorithm; in fact, we will show that
D
must have the same Kodaira type as
E
in this case. If
E
has multiplicative reduction, then it has Kodaira type
\text{I}_{n}
for some
n>0
, meaning
v_{K}(j(E))=-n
and
v_{K}(a)=0
. From the above we have
v_{K}(j(D))=-n
also, so it suffices to check that
v_{K}(2^{4}\cdot 3^{3}\cdot(3a^{3}+27b^{2}))=0
. This follows by applying the ultrametric inequality to the triple
4a^{3}+27b^{2}
,
a^{3}
,
3a^{3}+27b^{2}
.

The second assertion holds because
E
has split multiplicative reduction precisely when
-6b
is a square. Likewise,
D
has split multiplicative reduction precisely when
2^{3}\cdot 3^{8}\cdot b\cdot(4a^{3}+24b^{2})
is a square. Comparing, we have

\displaystyle 2^{3}\cdot 3^{8}\cdot b\cdot(4a^{3}+24b^{2})

\displaystyle\equiv 2b(4a^{3}+24b^{2})

\displaystyle\pmod{(K^{\times})^{2}}

\displaystyle\equiv 2b((4a^{3}+27b^{2})-3b^{2})

\displaystyle\pmod{(K^{\times})^{2}}

and the conclusion follows from the fact that
v_{K}((4a^{3}+27b^{2})-3b^{2})=2v_{K}(b)
and
((4a^{3}+27b^{2})-3b^{2})/b^{2}\equiv-3\pmod{\pi}
, where
\pi
is a uniformiser, so that
(4a^{3}+27b^{2})-3b^{2}
is a square in
K
if and only if
-3
is a square.
∎

For all

Fix

We compute that the discriminants are

\Delta_{E}=-2^{4}\cdot(27\cdot(3(8t+1))^{2}-50\cdot(3(8t+1))+99),\hskip 2.84544pt\Delta_{D}=-2^{18}\cdot 3^{3}\cdot\Delta_{E}

Write

(=

Y_{0}(N)(\mathbb{Q})=\emptyset

Recall that there is a degree

D
over

if and only if
\Phi_{N}(j(E),j(D))=0

(j(E),j(D))\in Y_{0}(N)(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})

.

For
t\in\mathbb{Z}

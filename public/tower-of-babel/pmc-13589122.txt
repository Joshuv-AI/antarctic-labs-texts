Comparative pathogenicity of Ma5 vaccine and a field isolate of the avian infectious bronchitis virus in SPF chickens
Authors: J.M. Jaton, P. Aguilera, M.S. Lucero, A. Penas-Steinhardt, S. Pinto, A. Vagnozzi, M.J. Gravisaco, A. Berinstein, E. Gómez, S. Chimeno Zoth
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Avian infectious bronchitis (IBV) is a worldwide distributed disease that causes significant economic losses in the poultry industry. The IBV virus, which belongs to the Coronaviridae family, can affect multiple bird systems, including the respiratory, gastrointestinal, reproductive, and renal tracts. It has also been reported to alter the composition of gut microbiota. Despite widespread vaccination programs, IBV remains a major health challenge. In Argentina, circulating genotypes include GI-1, GI-11, and GI-16; the latter comprises the A13 isolate, which was associated with a high-mortality outbreak in 2013. The Ma5 vaccine strain, which is widely used worldwide, belongs to the GI-1 genotype. It is used to prevent infections caused by Massachusetts phenotype variants. However, it remains unclear whether such vaccines may be more pathogenic than local field isolates. This study aimed to compare the pathogenicity of the A13 (GI-16) isolate with that of the Ma5 (GI-1) vaccine strain, as well as their impact on ileal microbiota in specific pathogen-free (SPF) chickens. The results showed that the Ma5 vaccine strain induced a higher proportion of inflammatory lesions in the trachea, kidney, and ileum. It also caused a significant reduction in villus height and the villus-to-crypt ratio in the intestinal mucosa. Additionally, higher viral load was detectable in the cecal tonsils in chickens immunized with the Ma5 strain. Regarding microbiota, vaccinated animals displayed greater alpha diversity and clear differentiation in beta diversity analyses compared to the control and A13 groups. In contrast, the A13 isolate did not produce notable lesions or significant alterations in gut microbial composition. These findings underscore the importance of evaluating not only the protective efficacy of vaccine strains, but also their safety profile and broader biological effects compared to circulating field strains. Furthermore, the results imply that despite its connection to a high-mortality outbreak, the A13 induced fewer alterations than Ma5 under the experimental conditions and at the time point evaluated, suggesting that this strain may be relevant for future studies aimed at vaccine development.

Introduction

Avian bronchitis is a disease that causes substantial economic losses to the global poultry industry (Cavanagh, 2005, 2007). The virus responsible for this disease, known as Infectious Bronchitis Virus (IBV), belongs to the Coronaviridae family and the Gammacoronavirus genus. Depending on the strain, IBV can lead to a range of health issues in birds, including respiratory, gastrointestinal, reproductive, and renal diseases (Cavanagh et al., 1992). Moreover, IBV has been shown to alter the microbiota of infected animals (Xu et al., 2020). Because IBV can replicate in the gastrointestinal tract, understanding its effects on intestinal microbial communities may provide additional insights into disease pathogenesis and host responses to infection. The ileum is a key site for nutrient absorption and immune regulation in poultry. Therefore, alterations in ileal microbial communities could influence intestinal homeostasis and host responses during IBV infection. Dysbiosis, regardless of its cause, is known to be associated with an increased abundance of pathogens, contributing to significant economic losses (Awad et al., 2016; Ducatelle et al., 2018). IBV has a recent classification of six major genotypes (GI-GVI) and 32 viral lineages (1-32) (Valastro et al., 2016). Despite widespread vaccination programs, the disease continues to be a significant threat in South America and worldwide (Alvarado et al., 2005; Rimondi et al., 2009; Marandino et al., 2017). IBV strains from genotypes GI-1 (vaccine strains), GI-11, and GI-16 have been circulating in Argentine poultry flocks for an extended period, with genotype GI-11 being exclusive to South America (Marandino et al., 2017, 2021, 2022).

The A13 strain, a GI-16 field isolate from Argentine poultry farms, has been implicated as the causative strain of a high-mortality IBV outbreak in 2013 (Gerez et al., 2021). This isolate has also been shown to induce nephrological damage under controlled experimental conditions (Jaton et al., 2023). A recent study evaluated the efficacy of different vaccine combinations to prevent disease caused by the A13 strain (Gerez et al., 2021). The study compared commonly used vaccination programs (Mass/793B vs. Mass/Mass-Connecticut) and showed that the Mass/793B combination provided the highest level of protection, consistent with previous findings (Cook et al., 1999). However, none of these combinations completely inhibited IBV A13 replication, which may indicate that viruses belonging to the GI-16 genotype, such as the A13 strain, can persist even in vaccinated flocks (Gerez et al., 2021).

Vaccination remains the primary strategy for the control of infectious bronchitis worldwide (Abozeid et al., 2023). Current vaccination programs rely mainly on live attenuated vaccines, which are administered early in life to induce protective respiratory and systemic immunity. Among these, Massachusetts-type vaccines such as H120 and Ma5 have been extensively used for decades in many poultry-producing countries due to their safety profile and broad protective capacity (de Wit et al., 2011; Bande et al., 2017). To improve protection against heterologous field strains, Massachusetts vaccines are frequently used in combination with vaccines belonging to other lineages, particularly 793B-type vaccines such as IB88 or 4/91 (Cook et al., 1999; de Wit et al., 2011). In commercial layers and breeders, live vaccination programs are often complemented with inactivated vaccines before the onset of lay to enhance and prolong immunity.

The Ma5 vaccine strain was selected in the present study as a representative Massachusetts-type vaccine because of its widespread use in commercial poultry production and its extensive characterization in experimental and field studies. Numerous studies have evaluated the protective efficacy of different vaccination programs against local IBV variants (Terregino et al., 2008; Lim et al., 2012; Sultan et al., 2019; Gerez et al., 2021; Hassan et al., 2022). However, comparatively little attention has been paid to potential differences in pathogenicity between vaccine strains and the field isolates against which they are intended to protect. Therefore, the objective of our study was to compare the pathogenicity of the A13 isolate with that of the Ma5 vaccine, and to assess the impact of these viruses on the composition of the ileal microbiota in specific pathogen-free (SPF) chickens.

Materials and methods

Experimental chickens

Embryonated eggs laid by specific pathogen free (SPF) White Leghorn hens were purchased from Instituto Rosenbusch S.A. (Buenos Aires, Argentina) and hatched in an automatic incubator (Yonar, Buenos Aires, Argentina). Chickens were kept in individual cages with food (Metrive S.A., Buenos Aires, Argentina) and water ad libitum. All procedures involving the use of animals were performed in agreement with institutional guidelines and approved by the Institutional Committee for the Care and Use of Experimental Animals (CICUAE – CICVyA – INTA, protocol number 28/21).

Viruses

Infectious bronchitis virus [AR/13/BA/A13 (A13)], belonging to genogroup I-16 (GenBank accession-number MH718950.1), was isolated from commercial establishments. The virus was amplified in embryonated eggs using standard procedures. Ma5 vaccine (Nobilis® IB MA5, MSD) was reconstituted according to the manufacturer's instructions.

Experimental procedure

Eighteen SPF White Leghorn chickens were randomly divided into three groups of six animals and orally inoculated at 19 days. One group was inoculated with 100 µl containing 2 × 105 EID50 of A13 strain (A13 group). Another group received 103 EID50 of IBV from Nobilis® IB Ma5 strain (Ma5 group) and a third group received 100 µl of PBS (Control group).

Clinical signs, gross analysis, and sample processing

Chickens were monitored daily for any anomalies. At 14 days post IBV inoculation, all animals were euthanized and post-mortem examinations were performed to evaluate different tissues. Samples of the ileum, cecal tonsils, kidneys, and trachea were collected, examined for macroscopic lesions and cut into several pieces. Sections of ileum, cecal tonsils, and kidney were submerged in TransZol solution (TransGen Biotech, Beijing, China) for RNA extraction. The remaining sections of ileum and kidney, as well as the whole trachea were fixed in 10% neutral buffered formalin for histopathological analysis.

Histopathological analysis

Macroscopic evaluation of the organs was performed, and samples of the ileum, trachea and kidney were collected for histopathological and histomorphometric analysis. These samples were fixed in 10% neutral buffered formalin and embedded in paraffin. Sections of each tissue were processed, stained with haematoxylin and eosin according to standard histological procedures and examined for the presence of lesions by light microscopy. The severity of inflammatory infiltration (II) was determined by evaluating five microscopic fields at 100 × magnification and assigning a score of 0 to 4, where 0 = normal, 1 = <25%, 2 = 25-50%, 3 = 50-75%, and 4 = 75-100% of affected tissue. Inflammatory infiltration was defined as the presence of mixed inflammatory cells, predominantly heterophils and macrophages, within the interstitial tissue between lymphoid follicles.

Intestinal histomorphometry

The height of the villi (V) was determined from the apical to the muscular zone of the mucosa, and the depth of the Lieberkühn crypts (C), taking their diameter, to later calculate the villi-crypt (V/C) relationship. The quantitative evaluation was carried out with Image J program.

Viral load

Total RNA extraction was performed. The quantity and quality of the RNA were determined using a NanoDrop™ ND-1000 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Wilmington, DE) and agarose gel electrophoresis. The complementary DNA (cDNA) synthesis and qPCR were performed in a single-step reaction using Luna® Universal Probe One-Step RT-qPCR Kit (New England Biolabs, MA) according to the manufacturer's protocol. Primers used for retrotranscription and amplification were 216-f: 5′TACATCCGTTGCTTGGGCTA3′ and 490-r: 5′ACCAGAACCTGTCACCTC3′ which amplify a 275 bp product belonging to the IBV genome from position 216 to position 491 (Jaton et al., 2023). The number of viral copies per microgram of RNA was calculated by extrapolation with a standard curve generated by qPCR from 10-fold serial dilutions of a plasmid containing the amplified IBV fragment, ranging from 108 to 10 copies. Viral loads were expressed as genome copies per µg of total RNA extracted from each tissue sample. Total RNA concentration was determined spectrophotometrically prior to cDNA synthesis, and equal amounts of RNA were used for all reactions.

Microbial DNA Extraction and sequence analysis

Ileum content was collected from a piece of intestine cut near the Meckel´s diverticulum and stored at −80°C until DNA purification. DNA samples were obtained using EasyPure Stool Genomic DNA Kit following manufacturer’s instructions. The concentration and purity of the nucleic acids were determined by a Qubit fluorometer (ThermoFisher). Hypervariable regions V3–V4 of the 16S rRNA gene were amplified with primers 337F/805R and sequenced in paired-end mode using a MiSeq sequencer (IlluminaⓇ). Sequences generated were analyzed using Quantitative Insights Into Microbial Ecology (QIIME2) version 2021.2 software package (Bolyen et al., 2019). Raw fastq reads were quality filtered (denoised, merged, and assessed for chimeras) to produce amplicon sequence variants (ASV) using the DADA2 pipeline (QIIME2 - DADA2 denoise-paired). To determine optimal trimming parameters Figaro software was used (Weinstein et al., 2019). After denoising, a rare amplicon sequence variant filtering (frequency less than 10 reads) was performed. Alpha and beta diversity were calculated using data rarefied to the sample with the lowest sequence depth at 35265 sequences with QIIME2 diversity core-metrics-phylogenetic. In order to place each sequence into a reference phylogenetic tree, QIIME2 fragment-insertion sepp was used (sepp-refs-gg-128.qza reference database) (Janssen et al., 2018). Taxonomic classifications were assigned by qiime feature-classifier classify-sklearn using the naïve Bayesian classifier trained with RESCRIPt package, using a 99% similarity threshold (Robeson et al., 2020). The database used for this taxonomic assignment was Silva 138 (Quast et al., 2013). Data were extracted from QIIME 2 artifacts to R by using qiime2R package (v0.99.5; https://github.com/jbisanz/qiime2R/). Posterior analysis was performed with phyloseq (version 1.34.0, (McMurdie and Holmes, 2013)) and microbiome (version 1.12.0) R packages.

Statistical analysis

One-way ANOVA was used for statistical analyses when the assumptions of normality and homoscedasticity were met, and mean differences among more than two groups were analyzed using Tukey’s test. In cases where only two groups were compared and assumptions were fulfilled, Student’s t-test was applied. The Shapiro–Wilk and Levene tests were used to verify these assumptions. When assumptions were not met, the Kruskal–Wallis non-parametric test followed by pairwise Wilcoxon comparisons was applied. All analyses were performed using R 3.4.1 (R Core Team) and the agricolae package (de Mendiburu, 2014).

Differences in beta diversity among treatments (Control, A13, and Ma5) were assessed by permutational multivariate analysis of variance (PERMANOVA) on Jaccard and Bray‑Curtis distances using the adonis2 function (vegan package, 999 permutations), and the homogeneity of multivariate dispersions was tested with betadisper.

At 14 days post infection (dpi), organs known to be affected by IBV, including the ileum, kidneys and trachea were collected. Histopathological evaluation of these tissues revealed varying degrees of inflammatory cell infiltration (Fig. 1). Effect of IBV on the ileal microbiota

In order to evaluate the effect of both, field A13 isolate and the Ma5 vaccine strain, on the microbiota, samples of ileum content were collected and DNA extraction was performed followed by sequencing the V3-V4 region of the 16S rRNA gene. The sample with the lowest depth was 4255 features count, while the sample with the highest depth reached 20257 features count. Notably, all rarefaction curves reached a plateau (data not shown) indicating that the sequencing depth was adequate to recover the majority of the microbial diversity within the sample. Alpha diversity indices were calculated using Chao and Shannon index (Fig. 4A and B). It was observed that animals vaccinated with the Ma5 strain showed higher richness in comparison with the control group (p < 0.05). Shannon index also revealed significant differences showing that the group vaccinated with Ma5 differed significantly from the control group and the group challenged with A13 isolate. Animals in the Ma5 group showed higher Shannon index indicating a significant increase in the number of dominant genera in the ileum compared to the other two groups. At first sight, animals infected with strain A13 and uninfected animals had very similar dominant genera, especially Lactobacillus. In contrast, vaccinated animals did not show such a pronounced dominant group. Beta diversity was evaluated using PERMANOVA. The analysis showed significant differences between the Ma5 group and the other experimental groups. Both Jaccard and Bray–Curtis distance metrics revealed significant separation between Control and Ma5 groups (p = 0.012 and p = 0.005, respectively), as well as between A13 and Ma5 groups (p = 0.021 and p = 0.011, respectively). Discussion

In Argentina, several genogroups of IBV circulate, including strains classified under GI-16 (Valastro et al., 2016; Marandino et al., 2021). Previous studies have suggested that strains belonging to this genogroup were responsible for the high mortality observed in 2013 in Entre Ríos Province, Argentina (Gerez et al., 2021). Although the A13 strain has been shown to replicate in the kidneys and cause disease-related lesions, it has not been found to induce mortality under experimental conditions (Jaton et al., 2023; Gerez et al., 2021). It is important to highlight that the hypothesis attributing the high mortality to an IBV outbreak was primarily based on the detection of the virus among the affected flocks. However, other contributing factors such as environmental conditions and the presence of co-infections with other pathogens, which could influence IBV pathogenicity, were not evaluated.

Vaccination with live attenuated Massachusetts-type (GI-1) strains remains one of the most widely used strategies for controlling IBV in poultry production. To date, however, no studies have evaluated whether vaccine strains may exhibit pathogenic effects comparable to or greater than those of circulating field strains. Therefore, the objective of our study was to compare the pathogenicity of the A13 isolate with the potential pathogenic effects of the Ma5 vaccine strain, both of which are commonly found in poultry farms. Additionally, we assessed their impact on the composition of the ileal microbiota in specific-pathogen-free (SPF) chickens.

We evaluated histopathological lesions in the trachea by assessing the degree of inflammatory cell infiltration. At 14 dpi, tracheal inflammation was observed only in the Ma5-vaccinated group, which showed the most pronounced inflammatory response. In contrast, no lesions were observed at this time point in birds infected with the A13 field strain. Although previous studies have demonstrated that the A13 strain can induce tracheal inflammation (Gerez et al., 2021), such evaluations have typically been conducted at earlier time points. For instance, Grgić et al. (2008) reported peak tracheal lesions between 4 and 8 dpi using Canadian IBV isolates, while Gerez et al. (2021) detected lesions caused by A13 at 7 dpi. Therefore, persistence of lesions at 14 dpi in the Ma5 group is a distinctive finding, as tracheal damage is rarely reported at this stage of infection.

When examining lymphocytic infiltration in the ileum, we found that both strains produced similar effects. However, a greater proportion of animals infected with the Ma5 strain exhibited a score of 1 for inflammatory infiltration. This type of intestinal analysis is not commonly performed in IBV studies, but we believe it is highly relevant, given that intestinal inflammation is known to impair performance and productivity parameters in poultry industry (Belote et al., 2018).

Optimal nutrient absorption and, consequently, optimal productive performance, depends on the maintenance of intestinal integrity, particularly the morphological features of the mucosa. Villus height, crypt depth, and their ratio are known to play critical roles in these processes (Wang et al., 2017; Gangadoo et al., 2018). In our study, we observed a significant reduction in villus height in animals infected with the vaccine strain. Higher villi are associated with improved nutrient absorption and more efficient production. Xu et al. (2020) demonstrated that infection with a nephropathogenic IBV strain can alter crypt depth. Although we did not observe any changes in crypt depth among groups, the decrease in villus height suggests that different IBV strains can cause distinct types of lesions and morphological changes in the avian intestine, which may have a negative impact on productivity.

Regarding the study of viral load, we observed that viral genome persists in the cecal tonsils up to day 14 in chickens immunized with the Ma5 strain. This finding may reflect individual variation in viral persistence and/or viral RNA concentrations approaching the detection limit of the assay. However, no association was observed between viral presence and notable morphological alterations or histopathological lesions. These findings underscore the need for further studies to elucidate the underlying mechanisms and potential implications.

The gastrointestinal microbiota plays a critical role in nutrient digestion, immune system development, and host protection against pathogenic microorganisms (Round and Mazmanian, 2009; Hooper and Gordon, 2001; Kamada et al., 2013). The gastrointestinal environment is highly dynamic, and the presence of pathogens can disrupt host–microbiota homeostasis, leading to dysbiosis and potentially triggering mucosal infections (Spor et al., 2011). Alterations in the microbial community structure can significantly influence host inflammatory responses (Winter et al., 2013; Deriu et al., 2016).

In contrast to the observations of Xu et al. (2020), who reported that control animals had higher richness than IBV-infected chickens, our results indicate that Ma5 vaccinated chickens had significantly higher richness than the other groups (A13 and negative control). These results do not agree with the information described in the literature, where it is generally accepted that viral infections tend to decrease richness (El-Far and Tremblay, 2018; Huang et al., 2019; Xu et al., 2020). In our study, we observed that the field isolate A13 did not significantly change the microbiota in terms of richness. These discrepancies could be due to differences in the time evaluated points. We hypothesize that microbial richness decreases during the early stages of infection. However, as the infection progresses, bacterial repopulation of the intestinal tract may occur, which could explain the higher abundance observed in Ma5 vaccinated animals. We found that the dominant genus within the ileum microbiota was Lactobacillus, especially in control and A13 infected chickens. This result has also been obtained by other authors when evaluating the microbiota in the ileum (Xu et al., 2020; Zhu et al., 2019). The analysis of beta diversity showed that the control and Ma5 vaccinated groups are clearly separated by both the Jaccard distance and the Bray-Curtis distance indicating differences in microbial community composition between samples. The group that was infected with isolate A13 seems to be in an intermediate state between the other two. Although the microbiota analysis provided complementary information regarding the intestinal effects of the two IBV strains, this component of the study was exploratory and should be interpreted with caution. The analysis was based on 16S rRNA gene sequencing, which allows taxonomic characterization of microbial communities but does not provide direct information on microbial functions or metabolic activity. In addition, the relatively small sample size used for microbiota analyses (n = 6 per group) may have limited the ability to detect subtle changes in less abundant taxa, and the absence of complementary approaches such as shotgun metagenomics, metatranscriptomics, or metabolomics restricts the functional interpretation of the observed differences. Nevertheless, significant treatment-associated differences were detected across multiple diversity metrics, supporting the robustness and biological relevance of the identified trends. Future studies incorporating larger cohorts and functional multi-omics approaches will be valuable to confirm these findings and further elucidate the biological significance of host–microbiota interactions during IBV infection.

As previously reported, the A13 strain was isolated in 2013 from broiler flocks in Argentina that experienced high mortality. Although it was initially suspected to be the causal agent of the outbreak (Gerez et al., 2021), the results of this study indicate that A13 is relatively low in virulence, producing milder effects across all evaluated parameters compared with the Ma5 vaccine strain.

Although A13 induced fewer histopathological and microbiota-associated alterations than Ma5 at 14 dpi, despite being administered at a viral dose 200-fold higher than that of Ma5, we acknowledge that pathogenicity assessment based on a single late time point has important limitations. The absence of lesions at 14 dpi may reflect resolution of tissue damage and mucosal repair rather than inherently lower virulence. Therefore, our findings should be interpreted as evidence of a lower pathological impact at the evaluated time point rather than definitive proof of reduced virulence. It should also be noted that both viruses were administered orally under controlled experimental conditions to enable a comparison, rather than to reproduce the natural route of IBV infection. Additional studies including earlier post-infection time points and viral replication kinetics will be necessary to fully characterize the pathogenic profile of the A13 strain.

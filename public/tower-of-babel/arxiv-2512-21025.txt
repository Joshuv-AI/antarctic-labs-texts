Quantitative bounds for Hölder exponents in the Krylov–Safonov and Evans–Krylov theories Thanks: S.-C. Lee is supported by the KIAS Individual Grant (No. MG099001) at the Korea Institute for Advanced Study.
Jongmyeong Kim, Se-Chan Lee

Abstract
. We establish quantitative bounds for Hölder exponents in the Krylov–Safonov and Evans–Krylov theories when the ellipticity ratio is close to one. Our analysis relies on the Ishii–Lions method for the Krylov–Safonov theory and a Schauder-type perturbation argument for the Evans–Krylov theory.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
35B65; 35D40; 35J60

1.
Introduction

The regularity theory for second-order elliptic equations has attracted considerable attention and has been extensively developed in the literature. In the linear uniformly elliptic setting with measurable coefficients, De Giorgi–Nash–Moser
[
DG57
,
Nas58
,
Mos60
,
Mos61
]
established the Hölder continuity of weak solutions, providing a positive answer to Hilbert’s nineteenth problem. On the other hand, the interior Hölder regularity of solutions in the non-divergence framework was achieved by Krylov–Safonov
[
KS79
,
KS80
]
by utilizing the ABP estimate and the Harnack inequality; see also Wang
[
Wan92
]
for a related result in the parabolic setting. Since their approaches are essentially based on ellipticity rather than linearity itself, the Krylov–Safonov theory has been widely extended to the regularity theory for fully nonlinear uniformly elliptic equations; we refer to the monograph by Caffarelli–Cabre
[
CC95
]
.

To illustrate the issue, let
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda
be ellipticity constants and let
\mathcal{S}^{n}
be the space of real
n\times n
symmetric matrices. We define
Pucci’s extremal operators

\mathcal{M}^{\pm}_{\lambda,\Lambda}
by

\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(M)=\sup_{\lambda I\leq A\leq\Lambda I}\text{tr}(AM)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{M}^{-}_{\lambda,\Lambda}(M)=\inf_{\lambda I\leq A\leq\Lambda I}\text{tr}(AM).

The classical Krylov–Safonov theory can be stated as follows; see Section
2
for the definition of viscosity solutions.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
KS79
,
KS80
]
)
.

If
u\in C(B_{1})
is a viscosity solution of

(1.1)

\mathcal{M}^{+}_{\lambda,\Lambda}(D^{2}u)\geq-K\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{M}^{-}_{\lambda,\Lambda}(D^{2}u)\leq K\quad\text{in $B_{1}$}

for some constant
K\geq 0
, then
u\in C^{\alpha}(\overline{B}_{1/2})
for some
\alpha=\alpha(n,\lambda,\Lambda)\in(0,1)
.

It is noteworthy that Theorem
1.1
does not provide quantitative information on the universal Hölder exponent
\alpha(n,\lambda,\Lambda)
. The upper bound (in some sense) for the Hölder exponent
\alpha
was captured in a series of papers by Nadirashvili–Vlăduţ
[
NV07
,
NV08
,
NV13
]
. More precisely, they proved that: for every
\tau>0
, there exist a dimension
n
and ellipticity constants
\lambda
and
\Lambda
such that there are solutions
u
of (
1.1
) with
u\notin C^{\tau}
. The counterexamples proposed in their works were constructed under the assumption that the ellipticity ratio is large enough, that is,
\Lambda/\lambda\gg 1
.

Recently, Lee–Yun
[
LY24
]
established the improved
C^{1,\alpha}
regularity of viscosity solutions of (
1.1
) when the ellipticity ratio is sufficiently close to one.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
LY24
]
)
.

Given
\alpha\in(0,1)
, there exists
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\alpha,n)>0
such that if
|\Lambda/\lambda-1|<\varepsilon
, then any viscosity solution
u\in C(B_{1})
of
(
1.1
) for some constant
K\geq 0
belongs to
C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{B}_{1/2})
.

The underlying idea of this result is that the Pucci extremal operators can be viewed as a small perturbation of the Laplacian operator
\Delta
under the condition
\Lambda/\lambda\approx 1
. Nevertheless, since the proof of Theorem
1.2
strongly relies on the contradiction argument, the consequence in
[
LY24
]
is still not quantitative in the sense that it does not provide an explicit relation between the Hölder exponent
\alpha
and the ellipticity gap
\Lambda/\lambda-1
.

The first goal of this paper is to describe the dependence of the Hölder exponent, which appears in the Krylov–Safonov theory, on the ellipticity ratio in an explicit quantitative manner.

Theorem 1.3

(Hölder exponent bound in the Krylov–Safonov theory)
.

Assume that
u\in C(B_{1})
is a viscosity solution of (
1.1
) for some constant
K\geq 0
. Given
\alpha\in(0,1)
, if

\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}-1<2\sqrt{\frac{1-\alpha}{n-1}},

then
u\in C^{\alpha}(\overline{B}_{1/2})
with the uniform estimate

\|u\|_{C^{\alpha}(\overline{B}_{1/2})}\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+K),

where
C>0
is a constant depending only on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
and
\alpha
.

A few remarks on Theorem
1.3
are in order. First, the criterion becomes stronger as
\alpha\to 1^{-}
, which is consistent with the perturbative intuition that one needs
\Lambda/\lambda\to 1^{+}
for better regularity of solutions. Second, Mooney
[
Moo19
]
captured the behavior of the Hölder exponent:
\alpha\sim c(n)^{(\Lambda/\lambda)^{n-1}}
for some
c(n)\in(0,1)
. While this asymptotic has an advantage that yields the information for general ellipticity ratio
\Lambda/\lambda
, it does not guarantee that
\alpha\to 1^{-}
as
\Lambda/\lambda\to 1^{+}
. We also refer to Armstrong–Silvestre–Smart
[
ASS12
]
, Le
[
Le20
]
and Nascimento–Teixeira
[
NT23
]
for related results. Moreover, Theorem
1.3
has several simple but useful corollaries regarding fully nonlinear equations (Corollary
3.1
) and normalized
p
-Laplace equations (Corollary
3.2
). Finally, our method can be applied to deal with both elliptic and parabolic equations that hold only where the gradient is large, whose regularity theory was developed by Imbert–Silvestre
[
IS16
]
in the elliptic setting. To the best of our knowledge, the Hölder regularity for viscosity solutions of the corresponding parabolic equations is new in this paper; see Theorem
3.4
and Corollary
3.5
.

The proof of Theorem
1.3
is based on the Ishii–Lions method
[
IL90
]
, which has played a crucial role in developing Hölder or Lipschitz regularity of viscosity solutions in different contexts. For instance, we refer to the works by Capuzzo Dolcetta–Leoni–Porretta
[
CDLP10
]
for degenerate equations with superlinear Hamiltonians, Imbert–Silvestre
[
IS13a
]
for degenerate fully nonlinear equations and Biswas–Topp
[
BT25
]
for fractional
p
-Laplace equations. In our setting, we consider a functional of the form

\Phi(x,y)\coloneqq u(x)-u(y)-L_{1}|x-y|^{\alpha}-L_{2}|x|^{2}-L_{2}|y|^{2},

and argue by contradiction. At a maximum point
(x,y)\in\overline{B}_{1/2}\times\overline{B}_{1/2}
of this functional, we apply the Ishii–Jensen’s lemma
[
Ish89
,
Jen88
]
to find appropriate semijets
(q_{x},X)
at
x
and
(q_{y},Y)
at
y
together with a matrix inequality for
X
and
Y
. Then we exploit the matrix inequality to obtain the spectral information on
X
,
Y
and
X-Y
in terms of
L_{1}
,
L_{2}
and
\alpha
. The comparison between the Pucci extremal operators and the Laplacian operator finally gives the desired quantitative relation between
\Lambda/\lambda-1
and
\alpha
.

The second part of this paper is concerned with the Evans–Krylov theory
[
Eva82
,
Kry82
,
Kry83
]
. We say that a fully nonlinear operator
F:\mathcal{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is

(i)

(\lambda,\Lambda)
-elliptic
if

\mathcal{M}^{-}_{\lambda,\Lambda}(M-N)\leq F(M)-F(N)\leq\mathcal{M}^{+}_{\lambda,\Lambda}(M-N)\quad\text{for any $M,N\in\mathcal{S}^{n}$};

(ii)

concave
if

F(\theta M+(1-\theta)N)\geq\theta F(M)+(1-\theta)F(N)\quad\text{for any $M,N\in\mathcal{S}^{n}$ and $\theta\in[0,1]$}.

The classical Evans–Krylov theory reads as follows. The key ingredient of the proof is the application of the weak Harnack inequality to derive the oscillation decay of
D^{2}u
.

Theorem 1.4

(
[
Eva82
,
Kry82
,
Kry83
]
)
.

Let
F:\mathcal{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be uniformly
(\lambda,\Lambda)
-elliptic and concave.
If
u\in C(B_{1})
is a viscosity solution of
F(D^{2}u)=0
in
B_{1}
, then
u\in C^{2,\alpha}(\overline{B}_{1/2})
for some
\alpha=\alpha(n,\lambda,\Lambda)\in(0,1)
.

As in the Krylov–Safonov theory, Theorem
1.4
does not provide a quantitative information on the Hölder exponent
\alpha(n,\lambda,\Lambda)
. There are various attempts to replace the concavity assumption with a weaker one; we refer to Caffarelli–Yuan
[
CY00
]
, Savin
[
Sav07
]
and Huang
[
Hua19
]
, just to name a few. Recently, Wu–Niu
[
WN23
]
obtained an improved regularity of viscosity solutions of
F(D^{2}u)=0
when the ellipticity ratio
\Lambda/\lambda
is sufficiently close to one, without the concavity assumption on
F
.

Theorem 1.5

(
[
WN23
]
)
.

Let
F:\mathcal{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be uniformly
(\lambda,\Lambda)
-elliptic. Given
\alpha\in(0,1)
, there exists
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\alpha,n)>0
such that if
|\Lambda/\lambda-1|<\varepsilon
, then any viscosity solution
u\in C(B_{1})
of
F(D^{2}u)=0
in
B_{1}
belongs to
C^{2,\alpha}(\overline{B}_{1/2})
.

The proof of Theorem
1.5
also relies on the contradiction argument, and so it does not yield an explicit relation between
\alpha
and
\Lambda/\lambda-1
. Our second goal is to provide an explicit quantitative criterion for the Evans–Krylov theory.

Theorem 1.6

(Hölder exponent bound in the Evans–Krylov theory)
.

Let
F:\mathcal{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be
(\lambda,\Lambda)
-elliptic and concave with
F(0)=0
. Moreover, let
u\in C(B_{2})
be a viscosity solution of

F(D^{2}u)=0\quad\text{in $B_{2}$}.

There exists a universal constant
A_{0}>0
independent of
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
and
\alpha
such that: given
\alpha\in(0,1)
, if

\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}-1<\frac{1}{n}\left(\frac{1}{A_{0}n^{3}}\right)^{\frac{2+\alpha}{1-\alpha}},

then
u\in C^{2,\alpha}(\overline{B}_{1/2})
with the uniform estimate

\|u\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\overline{B}_{1/2})}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2})},

where
C>0
depends only on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
and
\alpha
.

Let us provide some remarks on Theorem
1.6
. First, an explicit but non-optimized candidate for a universal constant
A_{0}
can be found in Remark
4.9
. Second, we can observe that the smallness requirement on
|\Lambda/\lambda-1|
in Theorem
1.6
is much stronger than the one in Theorem
1.3
. Finally, for the plane case (i.e.,
n=2
), Goffi
[
Gof25
]
provided an explicit Hölder exponent
\alpha
depending on
\Lambda/\lambda
. In fact, his result can be understood as the quantitative counterpart of the Nirenberg’s result
[
Nir53
]
regarding
C^{2,\alpha}
regularity of viscosity solutions of
F(D^{2}u)=0
in
n=2
, without the concavity assumption on
F
.

The proof of Theorem
1.6
consists of several steps. We first regularize a fully nonlinear operator
F
by considering its mollification
F^{\varepsilon}
. Then we are able to employ the Bernstein technique for the approximating solutions
u^{\varepsilon}
. At this stage, we derive interior
C^{2}
estimate for
u^{\varepsilon}
with tracking down the dependence of uniform constants on
n
,
\lambda
and
\Lambda
. Finally, the interior estimate allows us to use the Schauder-type iteration argument based on the perturbation method. In other words, we show the approximation lemma that ensures the existence of a harmonic function close to
u^{\varepsilon}
, and then apply the standard iteration argument to upgrade such an approximation to the desired
C^{2,\alpha}
estimate.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we collect several definitions and preliminary results including viscosity solutions and the Ishii–Jensen’s lemma. In Section
3
, we prove Theorem
1.3
by using the Ishii–Lions method and present several consequences for fully nonlinear equations, normalized
p
-Laplace equations and equations that hold only where the gradient is large. In Section
4
, we prove Theorem
1.6
via a Schauder-type perturbation argument together with the interior estimates coming from the Bernstein technique.

Definition 2.1

(Viscosity solutions)
.

Let

\varphi
touching

x_{0}

(Ishii-Jensen’s lemma; elliptic)
.

Let

Then for each

(i)

(D_{x_{i}}\varphi(\hat{x}),X_{i})\in\overline{\mathcal{J}}^{2,+}_{\mathcal{O}_{i}}u_{i}(\hat{x}_{i})

Moreover, the parabolic analogue of such a lemma can be found in

3.
Quantitative Hölder exponent bound in Krylov–Safonov theory

Suppose that
u
belongs to the solution class
S(\lambda,\Lambda,K)
for some
K\geq 0
. The celebrated Krylov–Safonov theory says that
u\in C^{\alpha}(\overline{B}_{1/2})
for
some

\alpha=\alpha(n,\lambda,\Lambda)\in(0,1)
. The goal of this section is to suggest a quantitative bound for the Hölder exponent
\alpha
in terms of
n
and
\Lambda/\lambda
, in different situations.

We begin with the quantitative Hölder exponent bound in the uniformly elliptic framework, whose proof is based on the Ishii–Lions method.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+K=1
and we show the Hölder continuity of
u
at the origin. For this purpose, we look for
L_{1}>0
and
L_{2}>0
such that

(3.1)

M=\sup_{x,y\in\overline{B}_{1/2}\times\overline{B}_{1/2}}\left[u(x)-u(y)-L_{1}\varphi(|x-y|)-L_{2}|x|^{2}-L_{2}|y|^{2}\right]\leq 0,

where
\varphi(s)=s^{\alpha}
for given
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Indeed, if (
3.1
) holds, then we set
y=0
in (
3.1
) to have

u(x)-u(0)\leq L_{1}|x|^{\alpha}+L_{2}|x|^{2}\leq C|x|^{\alpha}\quad\text{for all }x\in B_{1/2}.

Similarly, by setting
x=0
in (
3.1
), we have

u(0)-u(y)\leq C|y|^{\alpha}\quad\text{for all }y\in B_{1/2}.

Hence,
u
is
\alpha
-Hölder regular at the origin.

We argue by contradiction: assume that
M>0
. If
(x,y)\in\overline{B}_{1/2}\times\overline{B}_{1/2}
denotes a point where the maximum is attained, then we have

L_{1}\varphi(|x-y|)+L_{2}|x|^{2}+L_{2}|y|^{2}\leq|u(x)-u(y)|\leq 2.

In particular,

|x|^{2}+|y|^{2}\leq\frac{2}{L_{2}}

and

\varphi(\theta)\leq\frac{2}{L_{1}},\quad\text{where $\theta=|b|$ and $b=x-y$}.

At this stage, we fix
L_{2}>0
large enough to ensure
x\neq y
and
x,y\in B_{1/2}
.

We now apply the Ishii–Jensen’s lemma (Theorem
2.2
) to obtain that there exist a limiting subjet
(q_{x},X)
of
u
at
x
and a limiting superjet
(q_{y},Y)
of
u
at
y
such that

(3.2)

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}X&0\\
0&-Y\end{pmatrix}\leq L_{1}\begin{pmatrix}Z&-Z\\
-Z&Z\end{pmatrix}+3L_{2}I_{2n},

where

\displaystyle q

\displaystyle\coloneqq L_{1}\varphi^{\prime}(\theta)\hat{b},\quad q_{x}\coloneqq q+2L_{2}x,\quad q_{y}\coloneqq q-2L_{2}y,

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle\coloneqq\varphi^{\prime\prime}(\theta)\hat{b}\otimes\hat{b}+\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(\theta)}{\theta}(I-\hat{b}\otimes\hat{b})\quad\text{and}\quad\hat{b}\coloneqq\frac{b}{|b|}=\frac{x-y}{|x-y|}.

First of all, due to the definition of semijets, we have

\begin{cases}\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(X)\geq-K&\quad\text{in $B_{1}$}\\
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}(Y)\leq K&\quad\text{in $B_{1}$}.\end{cases}

Moreover, applying the matrix inequality (
3.2
) as a quadratic form inequality to vectors of the form
(\xi,\xi)
for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we obtain

\langle(X-Y)\xi,\xi\rangle\leq 6L_{2}|\xi|^{2}.

Therefore,
X-Y\leq 6L_{2}I
or equivalently, all eigenvalues of
X-Y
are less than
6L_{2}
. On the other hand, considering the particular vector
(\hat{b},-\hat{b})
, we obtain

\langle(X-Y)\hat{b},\hat{b}\rangle\leq 6L_{2}+4L_{1}\varphi^{\prime\prime}(\theta).

Thus, at least one eigenvalue of
X-Y
is less than
6L_{2}+4L_{1}\varphi^{\prime\prime}(\theta)
(which will be a negative number). Since

-Y\leq L_{1}\varphi^{\prime\prime}(\theta)\hat{b}\otimes\hat{b}+L_{1}\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(\theta)}{\theta}(I-\hat{b}\otimes\hat{b})+3L_{2}I_{n},

we obtain that at least one eigenvalue of
Y
is larger than or equal to
-L_{1}\varphi^{\prime\prime}(\theta)-3L_{2}(>0)
and the other
(n-1)
-eigenvalues satisfy

(3.3)

e_{i}(Y)\geq-L_{1}\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(\theta)}{\theta}-3L_{2}.

In particular, we have

K\geq\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}(Y)=\lambda\sum e_{i}^{+}(Y)-\Lambda\sum e_{i}^{-}(Y)

or equivalently,

(3.4)

\sum e_{i}^{+}(Y)\leq\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}\sum e_{i}^{-}(Y)+\frac{K}{\lambda}.

The estimates (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) imply that

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}\,Y=\sum e_{i}^{+}(Y)-\sum e_{i}^{-}(Y)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}-1\right)\sum e_{i}^{-}(Y)+\frac{K}{\lambda}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}-1\right)(n-1)\left(L_{1}\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(\theta)}{\theta}+3L_{2}\right)+\frac{K}{\lambda}.

By combining all these estimates, it turns out that

\displaystyle\Lambda(n-1)\cdot 6L_{2}+\lambda(6L_{2}+4L_{1}\varphi^{\prime\prime}(\theta))

\displaystyle\geq\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(X-Y)

\displaystyle\geq\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(X)-\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}(Y)+\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}(Y)-\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(Y)

\displaystyle\geq-2K+\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}(Y)-\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(Y).

Here we observe that

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(Y)-\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}(Y)

\displaystyle=(\Lambda-\lambda)\mathrm{tr}Y

\displaystyle\leq(\Lambda-\lambda)\left(\left(\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}-1\right)(n-1)\left(L_{1}\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(\theta)}{\theta}+3L_{2}\right)+\frac{K}{\lambda}\right).

Therefore, we arrive at

\displaystyle 2K+6\Lambda(n-1)L_{2}+6\lambda L_{2}+(\Lambda-\lambda)\left(\left(\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}-1\right)3(n-1)L_{2}+\frac{K}{\lambda}\right)

\displaystyle\geq L_{1}\left[-4\lambda\varphi^{\prime\prime}(\theta)-(\Lambda-\lambda)(n-1)\left(\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}-1\right)\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(\theta)}{\theta}\right].

We note that

-4\lambda\varphi^{\prime\prime}(\theta)-(\Lambda-\lambda)(n-1)\left(\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}-1\right)\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(\theta)}{\theta}>0

if and only if

\frac{-\theta\varphi^{\prime\prime}(\theta)}{\varphi^{\prime}(\theta)}>\frac{n-1}{4}\left(\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}-1\right)^{2}

if and only if

1-\alpha>\frac{n-1}{4}\left(\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}-1\right)^{2}.

We now choose
L_{1}
large enough to make a contradiction.
∎

The interior Hölder regularity of functions in the solution class can be used to establish the
C^{1,\alpha}
regularity of solutions
u
of the fully nonlinear equations.

Corollary 3.1

(Fully nonlinear equation)
.

Let
F:\mathcal{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be
(\lambda,\Lambda)
-elliptic and let
u\in C(B_{1})
be a viscosity solution of

F(D^{2}u)=0\quad\text{in $B_{1}$}.

Given
\alpha\in(0,1)
, if

\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}-1<2\sqrt{\frac{1-\alpha}{n-1}},

then
u\in C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{B}_{1/2})
with the uniform estimate

\|u\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{B}_{1/2})}\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+|F(0)|),

where
C>0
is a constant depending only on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
and
\alpha
.

Proof.

This is an immediate combination of Theorem
1.3
and
[
CC95
, Corollary 5.7]
.
∎

Moreover, Theorem
1.3
can be used to provide a quantitative counterpart of the result obtained in
[
LY24
, Theorem 1.4]
. In other words, we describe the relation between
|p-2|
and
\alpha
in a quantitative manner, when a viscosity solution
u
of
\Delta_{p}^{N}u=0
belongs to
C^{1,\alpha}
for
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

Corollary 3.2

(Normalized
p
-Laplace equation)
.

Let
u\in C(B_{1})
be a viscosity solution of

\Delta_{p}^{N}u=0\quad\text{in $B_{1}$},

where
\Delta_{p}^{N}
is the normalized
p
-Laplacian operator given by

\Delta_{p}^{N}u\coloneqq|Du|^{2-p}\Delta_{p}u=\left(\delta_{ij}+(p-2)\frac{u_{i}u_{j}}{|Du|^{2}}\right)u_{ij}.

Given
\alpha\in(0,1)
, if

\frac{\max\{p-1,1\}}{\min\{p-1,1\}}-1<2\sqrt{\frac{1-\alpha}{n-1}},

then
u\in C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{B}_{1/2})
with the uniform estimate

\|u\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{B}_{1/2})}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})},

where
C>0
is a constant depending only on
n
,
p
and
\alpha
.

Proof.

We recall that

\min\{p-1,1\}I_{n}\leq\delta_{ij}+(p-2)\frac{q_{i}q_{j}}{|q|^{2}}\leq\max\{p-1,1\}I_{n}\quad\text{for all $q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}$}.

In fact, to overcome a subtle issue arising from the singularity of
Du=0
, one has to introduce the regularized Dirichlet problem and to consider the approximated solution
u^{\varepsilon}
. Since the approximation method was already provided in
[
LY24
, Theorem 1.4]
, we omit the details here; a similar method will be used in Section
4
.
∎

We next move our attention to the elliptic equations that hold only where the gradient is
large
; see
[
IS16
]
for details. For
\gamma\geq 0
, we let

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{+}_{\lambda,\Lambda,*}(D^{2}u,\nabla u)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{M}^{+}_{*}(D^{2}u,\nabla u)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\begin{cases}\Lambda\sum e_{i}^{+}(D^{2}u)+\lambda\sum e_{i}^{-}(D^{2}u)+\Lambda|\nabla u|&\text{if $|\nabla u|\geq\gamma$}\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{-}_{\lambda,\Lambda,*}(D^{2}u,\nabla u)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{M}^{-}_{*}(D^{2}u,\nabla u)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\begin{cases}\lambda\sum e_{i}^{+}(D^{2}u)+\Lambda\sum e_{i}^{-}(D^{2}u)-\Lambda|\nabla u|&\text{if $|\nabla u|\geq\gamma$}\\
-\infty&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Imbert–Silvestre
[
IS16
, Theorem 1.1]
proved that a viscosity solution
u\in C(B_{1})
of

(3.5)

\mathcal{M}_{\ast}^{+}(D^{2}u,\nabla u)\geq-K\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{M}_{\ast}^{-}(D^{2}u,\nabla u)\leq K\quad\text{in $B_{1}$}

is
\alpha
-Hölder continuous in
B_{1/2}
for some small
\alpha\in(0,1)
. We note that if
u
satisfies (
1.1
), then
u
clearly satisfies (
3.5
).

We again utilize the Ishii–Lions method to find a quantitative bound for
\alpha
.

Theorem 3.3

(Elliptic; large gradient)
.

Assume that
u\in C(B_{1})
satisfies (
3.5
) for some constant
K\geq 0
. Given
\alpha\in(0,1)
, if

\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}-1<2\sqrt{\frac{1-\alpha}{n-1}},

then
u\in C^{\alpha}(\overline{B}_{1/2})
with the uniform estimate

\|u\|_{C^{\alpha}(\overline{B}_{1/2})}\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+K),

where
C>0
is a constant depending only on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
\alpha
and
\gamma/(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+K)
.

Proof.

As in the proof of Theorem
1.3
, we may assume that
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+K=1
and it is enough to find
L_{1}>0
and
L_{2}>0
such that

M=\sup_{x,y\in\overline{B}_{1/2}\times\overline{B}_{1/2}}\left[u(x)-u(y)-L_{1}\varphi(|x-y|)-L_{2}|x|^{2}-L_{2}|y|^{2}\right]\leq 0,

where
\varphi(s)=s^{\alpha}
for given
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

We argue by contradiction: assume that
M>0
. If
(x,y)\in\overline{B}_{1/2}\times\overline{B}_{1/2}
denotes a point where the maximum is attained, we have

(3.6)

L_{1}\varphi(|x-y|)+L_{2}|x|^{2}+L_{2}|y|^{2}\leq|u(x)-u(y)|\leq 2.

If we choose
L_{2}
large enough, then
x,y\in B_{1/2}
and
x\neq y
.

We use the same notation as in the proof of Theorem
1.3
. In view of (
3.6
), we have
L_{1}\theta^{\alpha}\leq 1
and so if we choose
L_{1}
large enough, then
\theta
will be small and
\varphi^{\prime}(\theta)
will be large. In particular, we arrive at
|q|=L_{1}\varphi^{\prime}(\theta)\geq 2\max\{L_{2},\gamma\}
and so

\frac{|q|}{2}\leq|q_{x}|=|q+2L_{2}x|\leq 2|q|\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{|q|}{2}\leq|q_{y}|=|q-2L_{2}y|\leq 2|q|.

Since
|q_{x}|\geq\gamma
and
|q_{y}|\geq\gamma
, we have

\begin{cases}\mathcal{M}_{\ast}^{+}(X,q_{x})=\Lambda\sum e_{i}^{+}(X)+\lambda\sum e_{i}^{-}(X)+\Lambda|q_{x}|\geq-K&\quad\text{in $B_{1}$}\\
\mathcal{M}_{\ast}^{-}(Y,q_{y})=\lambda\sum e_{i}^{+}(Y)+\Lambda\sum e_{i}^{-}(Y)-\Lambda|q_{y}|\leq K&\quad\text{in $B_{1}$}.\end{cases}

Therefore, we repeat the argument in the proof of Theorem
1.3
to obtain

K+2\Lambda|q|\geq\lambda\sum e_{i}^{+}(Y)-\Lambda\sum e_{i}^{-}(Y)

and so

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}Y=\sum e_{i}^{+}(Y)-\sum e_{i}^{-}(Y)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}-1\right)\sum e_{i}^{-}(Y)+\frac{K}{\lambda}+2\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}|q|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}-1\right)(n-1)\left(L_{1}\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(\theta)}{\theta}+3L_{2}\right)+\frac{K}{\lambda}+2\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}|q|.

By combining these estimates, it turns out that

\displaystyle\Lambda(n-1)\cdot 6L_{2}

\displaystyle+\lambda(6L_{2}+4L_{1}\varphi^{\prime\prime}(\theta))

\displaystyle\geq\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(X-Y)

\displaystyle\geq(\mathcal{M}_{\ast}^{+}(X,q_{x})-\Lambda|q_{x}|)-(\mathcal{M}_{\ast}^{-}(Y,q_{y})+\Lambda|q_{y}|)+\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}(Y)-\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(Y)

\displaystyle\geq-2K-\Lambda|q_{x}|-\Lambda|q_{y}|+\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}(Y)-\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(Y).

Here we observe that

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(Y)-\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}(Y)

\displaystyle=(\Lambda-\lambda)\mathrm{tr}Y

\displaystyle\leq(\Lambda-\lambda)\left(\left(\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}-1\right)(n-1)\left(L_{1}\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(\theta)}{\theta}+3L_{2}\right)+\frac{K}{\lambda}+2\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}|q|\right).

Therefore, by recalling that
|q|=L_{1}\varphi^{\prime}(\theta)
, we arrive at

\displaystyle 2K+6\Lambda(n-1)L_{2}+6\lambda L_{2}+(\Lambda-\lambda)\left(\left(\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}-1\right)(n-1)L_{2}+\frac{K}{\lambda}\right)

\displaystyle\geq L_{1}\left[-4\lambda\varphi^{\prime\prime}(\theta)-(\Lambda-\lambda)(n-1)\left(\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}-1\right)\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(\theta)}{\theta}-4\Lambda\varphi^{\prime}(\theta)-2(\Lambda-\lambda)\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}\varphi^{\prime}(\theta)\right].

Since
\theta\to 0
as
L_{1}\to\infty
, we may choose
L_{1}=L_{1}(\varepsilon,n,\lambda,\Lambda,\gamma)
larger so that

4\Lambda\varphi^{\prime}(\theta)+2(\Lambda-\lambda)\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}\varphi^{\prime}(\theta)\leq-4\varepsilon\lambda\varphi^{\prime\prime}(\theta)

for given
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Then we note that

-4\lambda(1-\varepsilon)\varphi^{\prime\prime}(\theta)-(\Lambda-\lambda)(n-1)\left(\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}-1\right)\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(\theta)}{\theta}>0\quad\text{for some $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$}

if and only if

1-\alpha>\frac{n-1}{4}\left(\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}-1\right)^{2}.

We now choose
L_{1}
further large enough to make a contradiction.
∎

We finally develop the parabolic counterpart of Theorem
3.3
, i.e., the Hölder regularity of viscosity solutions of (
3.7
) in both space and time variables. We would like to point out that the parabolic analogue of
[
IS16
]
was open, to the best of the authors’ knowledge. In other words, it was even unknown whether a viscosity solution of (
3.7
) is Hölder continuous or not. In the following theorem, we prove the Hölder continuity of
u
in the space variable under the assumption that
\Lambda/\lambda
is close to
1
.

Theorem 3.4

(Parabolic; large gradient 1)
.

Assume that
u\in C(Q_{1})
satisfies

(3.7)

\left\{\begin{aligned} u_{t}-\mathcal{M}_{\ast}^{+}(D^{2}u,\nabla u)&\leq K&&\quad\text{in $Q_{1}$}\\
u_{t}-\mathcal{M}_{\ast}^{-}(D^{2}u,\nabla u)&\geq-K&&\quad\text{in $Q_{1}$}\end{aligned}\right.

for some constant
K\geq 0
. Given
\alpha\in(0,1)
, if

\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}-1<2\sqrt{\frac{1-\alpha}{n-1}},

then
u(\cdot,t)\in C^{\alpha}_{x}
with the uniform estimate

|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}+K)|x-y|^{\alpha}\quad\text{for any $(x,t),(y,t)\in\overline{Q}_{1/2}$},

where
C>0
is a constant depending only on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
\alpha
and
\gamma/(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}+K)
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}+K=1
and we show the Hölder continuity of
u
at
(0,0)
. As in the elliptic case, it suffices to prove

(3.8)

M\coloneqq\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\overline{B}_{1/2}\times\overline{B}_{1/2}\\
t\in[-1/4,0]\end{subarray}}\left[u(x,t)-u(y,t)-L_{1}\varphi(|x-y|)-L_{2}|x|^{2}-L_{2}|y|^{2}-L_{2}t^{2}\right]\leq 0,

where
\varphi(s)=s^{\alpha}
for given
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

We prove (
3.8
) by contradiction: suppose that the positive maximum
M
is attained at
t\in[-1/4,0]
and
x,y\in\overline{B}_{1/2}
. It immediately follows that
x\neq y
and

L_{1}\varphi(|x-y|)+L_{2}|x|^{2}+L_{2}|y|^{2}+L_{2}t^{2}\leq 2\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}\leq 2.

In particular,

|x|^{2}+|y|^{2}+|t|^{2}\leq\frac{2}{L_{2}}

and

(3.9)

\varphi(\theta)\leq\frac{2}{L_{1}},\quad\text{where $\theta=|b|$ and $b=x-y$}.

We fix
L_{2}>0
large enough to ensure
t\in(-1/4,0]
and
x,y\in B_{1/2}
.

We now apply the parabolic version of Ishii–Jensen’s lemma (Theorem
2.3
) to obtain that there exist a limiting subjet
(\sigma_{x},q_{x},X)
of
u
at
(x,t)
and a limiting superjet
(\sigma_{y},q_{y},Y)
of
u
at
(y,t)
such that

•

\begin{pmatrix}X&0\\
0&-Y\end{pmatrix}\leq L_{1}\begin{pmatrix}Z&-Z\\
-Z&Z\end{pmatrix}+3L_{2}I_{2n};

•

\sigma_{x}-\sigma_{y}=2L_{2}t
,

where

\displaystyle q

\displaystyle\coloneqq L_{1}\varphi^{\prime}(\theta)\hat{b},\quad q_{x}\coloneqq q+2L_{2}x,\quad q_{y}\coloneqq q-2L_{2}y,

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle\coloneqq\varphi^{\prime\prime}(\theta)\hat{b}\otimes\hat{b}+\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(\theta)}{\theta}(I-\hat{b}\otimes\hat{b})\quad\text{and}\quad\hat{b}\coloneqq\frac{b}{|b|}=\frac{x-y}{|x-y|}.

By choosing
L_{1}
large enough,
\theta
will be small,
|\varphi^{\prime}(\theta)|
and so
|q|\geq 2\max\{L_{2},\gamma\}
. In particular, we have

\frac{|q|}{2}\leq|q_{x}|=|q+2L_{2}x|\leq 2|q|\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{|q|}{2}\leq|q_{y}|=|q-2L_{2}y|\leq 2|q|.

Then it follows from the definition of viscosity solution that

2L_{2}t=\sigma_{x}-\sigma_{y}\leq 2K+\mathcal{M}_{\ast}^{+}(X,q_{x})-\mathcal{M}_{\ast}^{-}(Y,q_{y}).

Since the remaining part of the proof is identical to the one of Theorem
3.3
, we omit the details.
∎

In fact, Theorem
3.4
was only concerned with the Hölder regularity of viscosity solutions of (
3.7
) in the space variable. We now follow the argument in
[
BBL02
, Lemma 9.1]
to derive the Hölder regularity of solutions in the time variable as follows.

Corollary 3.5

(Parabolic; large gradient 2)
.

Assume that
u\in C(Q_{1})
satisfies (
3.7
) for some constant
K\geq 0
. Given
\alpha\in(0,1)
, if

\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}-1<2\sqrt{\frac{1-\alpha}{n-1}},

then
u(x,\cdot)\in C^{\alpha/2}_{t}
with the uniform estimate

|u(x,t_{1})-u(x,t_{2})|\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}+K)|t_{1}-t_{2}|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\quad\text{for any $(x,t_{1}),(x,t_{2})\in\overline{Q}_{1/2}$},

where
C>0
is a constant depending only on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
\alpha
and
\gamma/(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}+K)
.

Proof.

As usual, we may assume that
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}+K=1
. We claim that, for any
\eta>0
and
t_{0}\in[-1/4,0)
, we can find constants
L_{1},L_{2}>0
large enough such that

(3.10)

u(x,t)-u(0,t_{0})\leq\eta+L_{1}(t-t_{0})+L_{2}|x|^{2}\eqqcolon\varphi(x,t)\quad\text{for every $(x,t)\in\overline{B}_{1/2}\times[t_{0},0]$}.

We first choose
L_{2}\geq 8
so that (
3.10
) holds for
x\in\partial B_{1/2}
. We next choose
L_{2}
larger to ensure that (
3.10
) holds for
t=t_{0}
. To this end, we apply Theorem
3.4
to find that

u(x,t_{0})-u(0,t_{0})\leq[u]_{C_{x}^{\alpha}}|x|^{\alpha}.

It is enough to choose

[u]_{C_{x}^{\alpha}}|x|^{\alpha}\leq\eta+L_{2}|x|^{2},

which holds true if

L_{2}\geq\eta^{\frac{\alpha-2}{\alpha}}[u]_{C_{x}^{\alpha}}^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}.

Thus, by choosing
L_{2}=8+\eta^{\frac{\alpha-2}{\alpha}}[u]_{C_{x}^{\alpha}}^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}
, we obtain that (
3.10
) is satisfied on the parabolic boundary
(\partial B_{1/2}\times[t_{0},0])\cup(\overline{B}_{1/2}\times\{t_{0}\})
.

On the other hand, it is easy to check that a smooth function
\varphi
is a supersolution of (
3.7
) if
L_{1}\geq 2\lambda L_{2}
. Therefore, the definition of viscosity solutions yields that

\max_{Q_{1/2}}(u-\varphi)=\max_{\partial Q_{1/2}}(u-\varphi)\leq 0,

which implies (
3.10
). If we choose
x=0
, then we conclude that

u(0,t)-u(0,t_{0})\leq\eta+2\lambda\left(8+\eta^{\frac{\alpha-2}{\alpha}}[u]_{C_{x}^{\alpha}}^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}\right)(t-t_{0}).

A simple optimization with respect to
\eta
of the right-hand side of the previous inequality shows that, for all
t\in[t_{0},0]
,

u(0,t)-u(0,t_{0})\leq\tilde{C}[u]_{C_{x}^{\alpha}}(t-t_{0})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}

for some constant
\tilde{C}
depending only on
\lambda
.
∎

4.
Quantitative Hölder exponent bound in Evans–Krylov theory

Let
u\in C(B_{1})
be a viscosity solution of

F(D^{2}u)=0\quad\text{in $B_{1}$},

where
F
is uniformly
(\lambda,\Lambda)
-elliptic and concave. The celebrated Evans–Krylov theory says that
u\in C^{2,\alpha}(\overline{B}_{1/2})
for
some

\alpha=\alpha(n,\lambda,\Lambda)\in(0,1)
. The goal of this section is to suggest a quantitative bound for the Hölder exponent
\alpha
in terms of
n
and
\Lambda/\lambda
. The key step is to track down the dependence on
n
,
\lambda
and
\Lambda
by employing the Bernstein technique for viscosity solutions
u
of
F(D^{2}u)=0
; see
[
CC95
, Section 9]
for instance.

Remark 4.1

(Regularization)
.

In order to utilize the Bernstein technique, we regularize the operator
F
by standard mollification as in
[
LLY24
]
. To be precise, we extend the domain of
F
from
\mathcal{S}^{n}
to
\mathbb{R}^{n^{2}}
by considering
F(M)=F\left(\frac{M+M^{T}}{2}\right)
. We also let
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n^{2}})
be a standard mollifier satisfying
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n^{2}}}\psi\,dM=1
and
\mathrm{supp}\,\psi\subset\{M\in\mathbb{R}^{n^{2}}:\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}M_{ij}^{2}\leq 1\}
, and define
\psi_{\varepsilon}(M)=\varepsilon^{-n^{2}}\psi(M/\varepsilon)
. If we define
F^{\varepsilon}
as

F^{\varepsilon}(M)\coloneqq F\ast\psi_{\varepsilon}(M)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n^{2}}}F(M-N)\psi_{\varepsilon}(N)\,dN.

It is easy to check that
F^{\varepsilon}
is uniformly elliptic (with the same ellipticity constants
\lambda,\Lambda
) and smooth. Moreover,
F^{\varepsilon}
is concave whenever
F
is concave. Finally, since
F
is Lipschitz continuous,
F^{\varepsilon}
converges to
F
uniformly. Therefore, we may consider a smooth operator
F^{\varepsilon}
, instead of
F
, in several lemmas of this section.

We recall the following lemma concerning a linearized operator
L
of concave and smooth
F
, which plays a crucial role in the Bernstein technique.

Lemma 4.2

(
[
CC95
, Lemma 9.2]
)
.

Let
F:\mathcal{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be
(\lambda,\Lambda)
-elliptic, concave and smooth with
F(0)=0
. Let
u\in C^{4}(\Omega)
satisfy
F(D^{2}u)=0
in
\Omega
. Consider the uniformly elliptic operator in
\Omega

Lv=a_{ij}(x)v_{ij}\coloneqq F_{ij}(D^{2}u(x))v_{ij}.

Then, for any
e\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
|e|=1
,

Lu\leq 0,\quad Lu_{e}=0\quad\text{and}\quad Lu_{ee}\geq 0\quad\text{in $\Omega$}.

We are now ready to derive uniform gradient and Hessian estimates with the aid of the Bernstein technique.

Proposition 4.3

(Uniform gradient and Hessian estimates,
[
CC95
, Proposition 9.3]
)
.

Let
F:\mathcal{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be
(\lambda,\Lambda)
-elliptic, concave and smooth with
F(0)=0
. Let
u\in C^{4}(\overline{B}_{1})
satisfy
F(D^{2}u)=0
in
B_{1}
. Then

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1/2})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})},

\displaystyle\|D^{2}u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1/2})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})},

where

\displaystyle C_{1}=C_{1}(n,\lambda,\Lambda)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{nC_{0}\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}+4C_{0}^{2}\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}},

\displaystyle C_{2}=C_{2}(n,\lambda,\Lambda)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}+1\right)C_{1}

for a constant
C_{0}>0
independent of
n
,
\lambda
and
\Lambda
.

Proof.

We take a cut-off function
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B_{1})
such that
0\leq\varphi\leq 1
in
B_{1}
and
\varphi\equiv 1
in
B_{1/2}
. Moreover,

|D_{i}\varphi|+\|D^{2}\varphi\|\leq C_{0}=C_{0}(\varphi)\quad\text{in $B_{1}$ for any $1\leq i\leq n$}.

For a particular choice of
\varphi
and
C_{0}=C_{0}(\varphi)
, see Remark
4.4
below.

For
M\coloneqq\sup_{B_{1}}u
, we set

h=\delta(M-u)^{2}+\varphi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\in C^{3}(\overline{B}_{1}),

where
\delta>0
will be determined soon. In particular, we observe that

L(\varphi^{2})=2a_{ij}(\varphi\varphi_{ij}+\varphi_{i}\varphi_{j})\geq-2n\|A\|\|D^{2}\varphi\|+2\lambda|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\geq-2n\Lambda C_{0}.

We then compute
Lh
, where
L
is the linearized operator introduced in Lemma
4.2
. By observing that
L(vw)=(Lv)w+v(Lw)+2a_{ij}v_{i}w_{j}
, we have

\displaystyle Lh=2\delta(M-u)(-Lu)

\displaystyle+2\delta a_{ij}u_{i}u_{j}+|\nabla u|^{2}L(\varphi^{2})

\displaystyle+2\varphi^{2}u_{k}Lu_{k}+2\varphi^{2}a_{ij}u_{ki}u_{kj}+8a_{ij}\varphi\varphi_{i}u_{k}u_{kj}.

Since
Lu\leq 0
,
Lu_{k}=0
and
(a_{ij})
has ellipticity
(\lambda,\Lambda)
from Lemma
4.2
, we apply the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality to find that

\displaystyle Lh

\displaystyle\geq 2\delta\lambda|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2}L(\varphi^{2})+2\lambda\varphi^{2}\sum_{k,j}(u_{kj})^{2}+8a_{ij}\varphi\varphi_{i}u_{k}u_{kj}

\displaystyle\geq 2\delta\lambda|\nabla u|^{2}-2n\Lambda C_{0}|\nabla u|^{2}+2\lambda\varphi^{2}\sum_{k,j}(u_{kj})^{2}-8\Lambda C_{0}\varphi\sum_{k,j}|u_{k}||u_{kj}|

\displaystyle\geq 2\delta\lambda|\nabla u|^{2}-2n\Lambda C_{0}|\nabla u|^{2}-8C_{0}^{2}\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{\lambda}|\nabla u|^{2}.

In particular,
Lh\geq 0
if we choose

\delta=nC_{0}\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}+4C_{0}^{2}\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}.

It then follows from the maximum principle that

\sup_{B_{1/2}}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq\sup_{B_{1}}h=\sup_{\partial B_{1}}h\leq\delta M^{2},

which proves the desired gradient bound.

We next show the Hessian bound. Consider for
e\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
|e|=1
and
\sigma>0
,

g=\sigma(u_{e})^{2}+\varphi^{2}(u_{ee})^{2}\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega}),

where
\Omega=\{x\in B_{1}:u_{ee}(x)>0\}
. We compute
Lg
in
\Omega
by a similar argument as before:

\displaystyle Lg

\displaystyle=2\sigma u_{e}Lu_{e}+2\sigma a_{ij}u_{ei}u_{ej}+(u_{ee})^{2}L(\varphi^{2})

\displaystyle+2\varphi^{2}u_{ee}Lu_{ee}+2\varphi^{2}a_{ij}u_{eei}u_{eej}+8a_{ij}\varphi\varphi_{i}u_{ee}u_{eej}

\displaystyle\geq 2\sigma\lambda\sum_{j}(u_{ej})^{2}-2n\Lambda C_{0}(u_{ee})^{2}+2\lambda\varphi^{2}\sum_{j}(u_{eej})^{2}-8\Lambda C_{0}\varphi u_{ee}\sum_{j}u_{eej}

\displaystyle\geq 2\sigma\lambda\sum_{j}(u_{ej})^{2}-2n\Lambda C_{0}(u_{ee})^{2}-8C_{0}^{2}\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{\lambda}(u_{ee})^{2}

Hence,
Lg\geq 0
if we choose

\sigma=nC_{0}\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}+4C_{0}^{2}\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}.

We also note that
\partial\Omega\subset\partial B_{1}\cup\{x\in B_{1}:u_{ee}(x)=0\}
. It then follows from the maximum principle that

\sup_{B_{1/2}}(u_{ee})_{+}^{2}=\sup_{\Omega\cap B_{1/2}}(u_{ee})^{2}\leq\sup_{\Omega}g=\sup_{\partial\Omega}g\leq\sigma\sup_{B_{1}}(u_{e})^{2}\leq\sigma\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}.

In other words, we arrive at

\|(u_{ee})_{+}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1/2})}\leq\sqrt{\sigma}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}

and
[
CC95
, Lemma 6.4]
gives that

\frac{\lambda}{\Lambda+\lambda}\|D^{2}u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1/2})}\leq\sqrt{\sigma}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}

as desired.
∎

We provide a few remarks on Proposition
4.3
as follows.

Remark 4.4

(Particular choice of a cut-off function
\varphi
)
.

We let

\chi(s)=\begin{cases}0,&s\leq-1,\\
\frac{1}{\int_{-1}^{1}\exp(-1/(1-t^{2}))\,\mathrm{d}t}\int_{-1}^{s}\exp(-1/(1-t^{2}))\,\mathrm{d}t,&-1<s<1,\\
1,&s\geq 1.\end{cases}

Then we set

s(r)\coloneqq 4r-3\quad\text{(so that $s(1/2)=-1$ and $s(1)=1$)}

and

\varphi(x)\coloneqq 1-\chi(s(|x|)).

It is immediate to check that such
\varphi
satisfies all the conditions given in the proof of Proposition
4.3
. Moreover, it follows from a direct calculation that

|D_{i}\varphi|+\|D^{2}\varphi\|\leq 33\quad\text{for any $1\leq i\leq n$.}

In conclusion, we may set
C_{0}=33
with this choice of a cut-off function.

Remark 4.5

(Scaled and combined version)
.

For the later use, we need a scaled and combined version of estimates proposed in Proposition
4.3
. In fact, we observe that
u_{r}(x)=u(rx+x_{0})
is a solution of
F_{r}(D^{2}u_{r})=0
in
B_{1}
, where
F_{r}(M)\coloneqq r^{2}F(M/r^{2})
whenever
B_{r}(x_{0})\subset B_{1}
. By applying the estimates from Proposition
4.3
for
u_{r}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r/2}(x_{0}))}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{1}}{r}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}(x_{0}))},

\displaystyle\|D^{2}u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r/2}(x_{0}))}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{2}}{r}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}(x_{0}))}.

For any
x_{0}\in B_{3/4}
, set
r=1/4
so that
B_{r}(x_{0})\subset B_{1}
. Then we arrive at

\|D^{2}u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r/4}(x_{0}))}\leq\frac{2C_{2}}{r}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r/2}(x_{0}))}\leq\frac{2C_{1}C_{2}}{r^{2}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}(x_{0}))},

and so

\|D^{2}u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{3/4})}\leq 32C_{1}C_{2}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}.

Remark 4.6

(Rough bound for
C_{1}C_{2}
)
.

Since we will let
\Lambda/\lambda
be sufficiently close to
1
, we may assume that
\Lambda/\lambda\leq 2
in the remainder of this section. Then we have a rather simple bound for
C_{1}C_{2}
:

C_{1}C_{2}=\left(\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}+1\right)\left(nC_{0}\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}+4C_{0}^{2}\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\right)\leq 6(nC_{0}+8C_{0}^{2})\,(\sim n).

Lemma 4.7

(Approximation lemma)
.

Let
F:\mathcal{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be
(\lambda,\Lambda)
-elliptic, concave and smooth with
F(0)=0
. Moreover, let
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
be the constants chosen in Proposition
4.3
, and let
u\in C(B_{1})
be a viscosity solution of

F(D^{2}u)=0\quad\text{in $B_{1}$}.

Given
\varepsilon\in(0,1/12)
, if

\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}-1\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{9C_{1}C_{2}},

then there exists a harmonic function
h\in C^{2}(\overline{B}_{3/4})
such that

\|u-h\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{3/4})}\leq\varepsilon\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}.

Proof.

By replacing
u
with
u/\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}
, we may assume that
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}\leq 1
.

Let
M\in\mathcal{S}^{n}
be a symmetric matrix with eigenvalues
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
. Then we observe that

(4.1)

\displaystyle|F(M)-\lambda\mathrm{tr}M|

\displaystyle\leq\max\{\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(M)-\lambda\mathrm{tr}M,\ \lambda\mathrm{tr}M-\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}(M)\}

\displaystyle\leq(\Lambda-\lambda)\left(\sum_{e_{i}>0}e_{i}+\sum_{e_{i}<0}(-e_{i})\right)\leq(\Lambda-\lambda)n\|M\|.

We now let
h
be the harmonic replacement of
u
in
B_{3/4}
, i.e.,

\left\{\begin{aligned} \Delta h&=0&&\text{in $B_{3/4}$}\\
h&=u&&\text{on $\partial B_{3/4}$}.\end{aligned}\right.

For
w\coloneqq u-h
in
B_{3/4}
, we observe that

\Delta w=\Delta u-\Delta h=\Delta u,

and (
4.1
) implies that

|\Delta u|\leq\left(\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}-1\right)n\|D^{2}u\|.

We next let
N:=\|\Delta u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{3/4})}
and consider the barrier

\phi(x)\coloneqq\frac{N}{2n}((3/4)^{2}-|x|^{2})\quad\text{in }B_{3/4}.

Since
\Delta\phi=-N
, the comparison principle between
\pm w
and
\phi
in
B_{3/4}
yields that

|w|\leq\phi\quad\text{in $B_{3/4}$}

and so

\|w\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{3/4})}\leq\frac{N}{2n}\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{2}.

Then we recall (
4.1
) and apply a scaled version of the interior
C^{2}
estimate for
u
(Remark
4.5
):

N\leq\left(\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}-1\right)n\|D^{2}u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{3/4})}\leq 32\left(\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}-1\right)nC_{1}C_{2}.

We finally arrive at

(4.2)

\|u-h\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{3/4})}\leq 9\left(\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}-1\right)C_{1}C_{2}\leq\varepsilon,

under the assumption on the ellipticity ratio.
∎

We now prove the following theorem, which is corresponding to Theorem
1.6
when
F
is smooth.

Theorem 4.8

(
C^{2,\alpha}
estimate; smooth
F
)
.

Let
F:\mathcal{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be
(\lambda,\Lambda)
-elliptic, concave and smooth with
F(0)=0
. Moreover,
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
be the constants chosen in Proposition
4.3
, and let
u\in C(B_{1})
be a viscosity solution of

F(D^{2}u)=0\quad\text{in $B_{1}$}.

Given
\alpha\in(0,1)
, if

\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}-1\leq\frac{1}{18C_{1}C_{2}}(576n^{3})^{-\frac{2+\alpha}{1-\alpha}},

then
u\in C^{2,\alpha}(\overline{B}_{1/2})
with the uniform estimate

\|u\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\overline{B}_{1/2})}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})},

where
C>0
depends only on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
and
\alpha
.

Proof.

As in the proof of Lemma
4.7
, we may assume that
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}\leq 1
. By applying Lemma
4.7
, there exists a harmonic function
h\in C^{2}(\overline{B}_{3/4})
such that

(4.3)

\|u-h\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{3/4})}\leq\varepsilon,

provided that

\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}-1\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{9C_{1}C_{2}}\quad\text{for $\varepsilon>0$ will be determined soon}.

We note that
\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{3/4})}\leq\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}\leq 1
by its construction. In particular,
h
has a quadratic Taylor polynomial
P
at the origin, which satisfies
\Delta P=0
and

|P|=\left|h(0)+Dh(0)\cdot x+\frac{1}{2}x^{T}D^{2}h(0)x\right|\leq 1+3n+18n^{2}\leq c_{0}\quad\text{in $B_{3/4}$}.

Moreover, since
h
is harmonic, we have

(4.4)

\|h-P\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r})}\leq\frac{1}{6}\|D^{3}h\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{3/4})}r^{3}\leq 288n^{3}r^{3}\eqqcolon c_{0}r^{3}\quad\text{for all $r\leq 3/4$}.

Here we used the interior estimates for a harmonic function
h
(Lemma
2.4
).

We combine (
4.3
) and (
4.4
) to obtain that

\|u-P\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r})}\leq c_{0}r^{3}+\varepsilon\quad\text{for all $r\leq 3/4$}.

Choose now
r_{0}
small enough so that
c_{0}r_{0}^{3}\leq r_{0}^{2+\alpha}/2
(notice
\alpha<1
), and then
\varepsilon
small enough so that
\varepsilon\leq r_{0}^{2+\alpha}/2
. Then we obtain

\|u-P\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r_{0}})}\leq r_{0}^{2+\alpha}.

The remaining iteration argument is quite standard; see
[
CC95
, Section 8.1]
for instance.
∎

Remark 4.9

(The condition on the ellipticity ratio)
.

Since the condition on
\Lambda/\lambda
given in Theorem
4.8
is rather implicit, we may recall the observation from Remark
4.6
. In fact, since
C_{1}C_{2}\leq 6(nC_{0}+8C_{0}^{2})
, the condition on the ellipticity ratio will be satisfied when we assume

\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}-1\leq\frac{1}{108(nC_{0}+8C_{0}^{2})}(576n^{3})^{-\frac{2+\alpha}{1-\alpha}}.

We also note that one candidate of
C_{0}
is
33
from Remark
4.4
.

On the other hand, in a more heuristic manner, we have

\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}-1\approx\frac{1}{n}\left(\frac{1}{n^{3}}\right)^{\frac{2+\alpha}{1-\alpha}}\quad\text{up to a multiplicative constant}.

In particular, it is clear that if
\alpha\to 1^{-}
, then
\Lambda/\lambda\to 1^{+}
as expected.

We finally employ the mollification method to derive the same estimate without the smoothness assumption on
F
.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

As in the proof of Lemma
4.7
, we may assume that
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2})}\leq 1
. Moreover, we consider the mollification
F^{\varepsilon}
of
F
as in Remark
4.1
and let
u^{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}(B_{1})\cap C(\overline{B}_{1})
be a unique solution of

\left\{\begin{aligned} F^{\varepsilon}(D^{2}u^{\varepsilon})&=0&&\text{in $B_{1}$},\\
u^{\varepsilon}&=u&&\text{on $\partial B_{1}$}.\end{aligned}\right.

We now apply Theorem
4.8
for
u^{\varepsilon}
to obtain

\|u^{\varepsilon}\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\overline{B}_{1/2})}\leq C\|u^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2})},

where
C>0
is independent of
\varepsilon
.

On the other hand, it is easy to check that there exists a subsequence of
\{u^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
that converges uniformly (in
C^{2}
) to some function
v
in every compact subset of
B_{1}
. Then the stability theorem (Theorem
2.5
) implies that
v
becomes a viscosity solution of
F(D^{2}v)=0
in
B_{1}
. Furthermore, we also have
v=u
on
\partial B_{1}
in view of the global Hölder estimate for
u^{\varepsilon}
(Theorem
2.6
). Thus, we conclude that
v=u
and in particular,

\|u\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\overline{B}_{1/2})}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2})}

as desired.
∎

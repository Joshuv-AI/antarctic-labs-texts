Complete systems of solutions, transmutations and Darboux transform for Sturm-Liouville equations in impedance form
Víctor A. Vicente-Benítez

Abstract
We present the construction of a complete system of functions associated with the Sturm–Liouville equation in impedance form on a finite interval I I , given an impedance function a ∈ L 2 ​ ( I ) a\in L^{2}(I) . The system, known as the formal powers , is generated through recursive integration of the impedance function a a and its reciprocal. We establish the completeness of this system in the space L p L^{p} with the weight function a 2 a^{2} . Under additional conditions on a a , we extend this completeness to Sobolev spaces W 1 , p ​ ( I ) W^{1,p}(I) , along with a generalized Taylor formula. We show that the completeness of the formal powers implies key analytic properties for a transmutation operator associated with the Sturm-Liouville equation in impedance form, including the existence of a continuous inverse. Finally, we introduce a formulation of the Darboux-transformed equation and establish a relation between the transmutation operators to the original and transformed equations.



Keywords:
Complete system of functions; Sturm-Liouville equation in impedance form; Transmutation operators; Darboux transform.

Introduction

In this work, we present the construction of a complete system of functions associated with the Sturm-Liouville equation in the form

-\frac{d}{dx}\left(a^{2}(x)\frac{du(x)}{dx}\right)=\lambda a^{2}(x)u(x),\quad x\in I,\lambda\in\mathbb{C},

(1)

where
I\subset\mathbb{R}
is a finite interval,
\lambda
is called the
spectral parameter
of Eq. (
1
), and the coefficient
a
is referred to as the
impedance function
. We assume that
a\in L^{2}(I)
is positive and satisfies
\frac{1}{a}\in L^{2}(I)
, such functions are often called
proper impedance functions
. A general method for solving Sturm–Liouville equations was introduced, known as the spectral parameter power series (SPPS) method. This approach was initially developed for Sturm–Liouville equations with continuous coefficients, and later extended in
[
4
]
to equations with locally integrable coefficients. The SPPS method constructs solutions to Eq. (
1
) by expanding them into Taylor series with respect to the spectral parameter. This expansion yields a sequence of Taylor coefficients
\{\varphi_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
which are functions of the variable
x
. In the SPPS literature, these coefficients are referred to as formal powers
[
6
]
, and are closely related to the concept of an
L
-basis for a differential operator, as introduced in
[
8
]
. The formal powers associated with a regular differential equation generalize several key properties of standard monomials, including completeness in certain functional spaces. For instance, in
[
10
]
, it was shown that the formal powers corresponding to a Schrödinger equation with a continuous potential form a complete system in
L^{2}(a,b)
and
C[a,b]
. Furthermore, in
[
26
]
, various properties of these formal powers—such as binomial-type expansions and generalized trigonometric identities—are investigated. The completeness property of formal powers also facilitates the construction of complete systems of solutions for partial differential equations that can be reduced, via separation of variables, to the Schrödinger equation
[
6
,
12
,
32
]
. One of the key properties of formal powers is that their completeness ensures the existence of a continuous transmutation operator, that is, a Volterra integral operator that transforms the solutions of the equation
v^{\prime\prime}+\lambda v=0
into the solutions of the Schrödinger equation
[
24
,
28
]
. Moreover, in
[
6
]
it was shown that the formal powers are the image of the standard monomials under the transmutation operator. This property is known as the
transmutation property of the formal powers
, and was extended for Schrödinger equations with integrable potentials in
[
5
]
, where it was shown that certain transmutation operators are completely determined by the corresponding associated formal powers. The significance of this transmutation property lies in that it allows us to construct analytical approximations, both for the transmutation operator itself and for the solutions of the equation to be studied, without requiring an explicit expression for the full operator
[
13
,
17
]
. Transmutation operators have proven to be powerful tools in the practical solution of direct and inverse spectral problems, particularly in the context of the Schrödinger equation, as developed over the past decade (see, e.g.,
[
11
,
13
,
14
,
20
]
). For the impedance-form equation, the existence of certain integral representations for its solutions was established in
[
7
]
, while the fundamental properties of a transmutation operator in the case where
a\in C^{1}[0,\ell]
, was investigated in
[
21
]
. It is worth mentioning that, even though for an impedance
a\in W^{1,2}
, the impedance equation is unitarily equivalent to the Schrödinger equation via the Liouville transformation, and one can thus apply the transmutation theory developed for the latter, if the impedance does not possess a second weak derivative in
L^{p}
, the associated Schrödinger equation involves a distributional potential. There are some works concerning the existence of transmutation operators for certain classes of distributional potentials
[
22
,
31
]
, as well as integral representations for solutions of Eq. (
1
) satisfying specific initial conditions
[
1
]
. However, it is of particular interest to develop a transmutation theory directly for the Sturm-Liouville equation in impedance form, as this allows for explicit analytical representations and provides tools for the efficient solution of both direct and inverse spectral problems.

This work aims to extend the construction of the formal powers to the impedance equation (
1
) with a proper integrable impedance. The system is defined by

\varphi_{a}^{(0)}\equiv 1,\;\;\varphi_{a}^{(1)}(x):=\int_{x_{0}}^{x}\frac{dt}{a^{2}(t)},\;\;\varphi_{a}^{(k)}(x):=k(k-1)\int_{x_{0}}^{x}\frac{dt}{a^{2}(t)}\int_{x_{0}}^{t}a^{2}(s)\varphi_{a}^{(k-2)}(s)ds,\;k\geq 2,

where
x_{0}\in\overline{I}
. We show that the formal powers
\{\varphi_{a}^{(k)}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
satisfy recursive relations involving the differential operators
\mathbf{D}_{a^{(-1)^{k}}}:=(a^{2})^{(-1)^{k}}\frac{d}{dx}
, analogous to the relations between the standard monomials and the classical derivative operator. Moreover, we establish conditions for the existence of a generalized Taylor formula for solutions of the equation
\mathbf{D}^{(m)}_{a}u=g
, where
\mathbf{D}^{(m)}u:=\prod_{j=1}^{m}\mathbf{D}_{a^{(-1)^{(j-1)}}}
. We prove the completeness of the formal powers in the
L^{p}
-space with weight function
a^{2}
, and under additional hypotheses (that we establish in Sec.
4
), the completeness in Sobolev spaces
W^{m,p}(I)
. For completeness in
L^{p}
, we first consider a Sturm-Liouville problem with regular boundary conditions, associated to Eq. (
1
). Using the SPPS method, we demonstrate that the system of formal powers is complete in the eigenfunction space of the associated problem. Subsequently, we study the completeness of the eigenfunctions in
L^{p}
spaces, obtaining the completeness of the formal powers by transitivity. For completeness in
W^{1,p}
, we analyze the conditions under which a function admits a generalized Taylor approximation in terms of formal powers.

As an application, we show how the completeness of the formal powers enables the construction of a transmutation operator for Eq. (
1
) when the impedance function belongs to
W^{1,\infty}
, along with some analytical properties such as the existence of a continuous inverse. Similarly, we will analyze the relationship between the formal powers for Eq. (
1
) and those corresponding powers for the equation with impedance
a^{-1}
, which, following
[
21
]
, we refer to as
the associated Darboux equation
. These connections allow us to derive relations between the transmutation operator of Eq. (
1
) and its associated Darboux equation. It is worth mentioning that the existence of relations between the transmutation operator and the associated Darboux operator plays a crucial role in constructing transmutation operators and complete systems of solutions for systems of partial equations, such as Vekua-type equations with separable coefficients
[
6
,
19
,
33
]
. Finally, we study the existence of a transmutation operator for the case where
a\in W^{1,2}(0,\ell)
, along with its analytical properties.

The paper is structured as follows. Section 2 provides background on the main properties of the solutions and factorizations of the impedance operator. Section 3 develops the definition and key properties of the formal powers, generalized derivatives, and formal Taylor polynomials. Section 4 is devoted to proving the completeness of the formal powers in the
L^{p}
and Sobolev spaces. In Section 5, we discuss the main properties of a transmutation operator for the case when
a\in W^{1,\infty}(-\ell,\ell)
, including the existence of a continuous inverse operator, and the relations with the transmutation operator for the Darboux-transformed equation. Finally, Section 6 presents the construction of a transmutation operator for the case
a\in W^{1,2}(0,\ell)
.

Types of solutions, factorization, Darboux transformation, and formal powers

Through the text, we use the notation
\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}

Let

be a bounded interval and let
a\in L^{2}(I)
be a positive measurable function such that
\frac{1}{a}\in L^{2}(I)

For
1\leq p<\infty
, we use the notation
L_{a}^{p}(I)
for the space of integrable measurable functions with respect to the weight function
a^{2}
. Hence, the norm is given by

\|u\|_{L_{a}^{p}(I)}:=\left(\int_{I}|u(x)|^{p}a^{2}(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

For
p=2
, the corresponding inner product is given by

\langle u,v\rangle_{L_{a}^{2}(I)}:=\int_{I}u(x)\overline{v(x)}a^{2}(x)dx.

Since
a^{2}(x)dx
defines a finite measure on
I
, the embedding
L^{p}_{a}(I)\hookrightarrow L_{a}^{q}(I)
is continuous, whenever
q\leq p

[
9
, Prop. 6. 12]
. If, in addition,
a,\frac{1}{a}\in L^{\infty}(I)
, then
L_{a}^{p}(I)=L^{p}(I)
with equivalent norms. We use the standard notation
W^{1,p}(I)
for the Sobolev space of functions in
L^{p}(I)
whose first
k
distributional derivatives lie in
L^{p}(I)
. The space of absolutely continuous functions on
\overline{I}
is denoted by
AC(\overline{I})
.

The
Sturm-Liouville equation in impedance form
is given by

-\frac{1}{a^{2}(x)}\frac{d}{dx}\left(a^{2}(x)\frac{du}{dx}\right)=\lambda u,\quad x\in I,\lambda\in\mathbb{C}.

(2)

The function
a
is called the
impedance
of Eq. (
2
), and
\lambda
is the spectral parameter. In some contexts, it is common to work with the
conductivity function
associated to
a
, that is,
\kappa(x):=a^{2}(x)
. Under our assumptions, we have that
\kappa,\frac{1}{\kappa}\in L^{1}(I)
and
L_{a}^{p}(I)
is just the
L^{p}
space over
I
with the measure
k(x)dx
.

In general, we consider equations of the form

-\frac{1}{a^{2}(x)}\frac{d}{dx}\left(a^{2}(x)\frac{du}{dx}\right)=\lambda u+g(x),\quad x\in I,\lambda\in\mathbb{C},

(3)

with
g\in L_{a}^{p}(I)
. A
weak
solution of Eq. (
3
), is a function
u\in W^{1,1}(I)
such that
u^{\prime}\in L_{a}^{p}(I)
and satisfies the following condition:

\int_{I}a^{2}(x)u^{\prime}(x)\phi^{\prime}(x)dx=\int_{I}a^{2}(x)\left(\lambda u(x)+g(x)\right)\phi(x)dx\qquad\forall\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(I).

(4)

Proposition 1

If
u\in W^{1,1}(I)
is a weak solution of an equation of type (
3
), then
a^{2}u\in AC(\overline{I})
with
\frac{(a^{2}u^{\prime})^{\prime}}{a^{2}}\in L_{a}^{p}(I)
and

satisfies Eq. (
3
) a.e. in
I
.

If
a\in W^{1,p}(I)
, then
u\in W^{2,p}(I)
. If
a\in C^{1}(\overline{I})
and
g\in C(\overline{I})

u\in C^{2}(\overline{I})
and is a classical solution.

Suppose that
u\in W^{1,1}(I)
is a weak solution of (
3

4
) is equivalent to saying that
-a^{2}(\lambda u+g)

\frac{(a^{2}u^{\prime})^{\prime}}{a^{2}}=-(\lambda u+g)\in L_{a}^{p}(I)
. Therefore,

satisfies (
3

(see
[
3

Now suppose that

. Hence

(see

3

\frac{1}{a}\in C(\overline{I})

L^{p}(I)=L_{a}^{p}(I)
with equivalent norms. Also note that
(a^{2})^{\prime}=2aa^{\prime}\in L^{p}(I)

a^{2}\in W^{1,p}(I)
. Hence

Finally, if

Solutions

such that

Denote
D=\frac{d}{dx}
. Based on the previous results, we define the Sturm-Liouville operator
\mathbf{L}_{a}:\mathscr{D}_{p}(\mathbf{L}_{a})\subset L_{a}^{p}(I)\rightarrow L_{a}^{p}(I)

\mathbf{L}_{a}:=\frac{1}{a^{2}}Da^{2}D.

(5)

Consider the differential operator
\mathbf{D}_{a}:\mathscr{D}_{p}(\mathbf{D}_{a})\subset L_{a}^{p}(I)\rightarrow L_{\frac{1}{a}}^{p}(I)

(6)

Note that the operator
\mathbf{L}_{a}

\mathbf{L}_{a}=\mathbf{D}_{\frac{1}{a}}\mathbf{D}_{a}.

(7)

Formal powers, generalized derivatives and Taylor polynomials

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}_{a}\varphi_{a}^{(k+1)}

\displaystyle=(k+1)k\mathbf{D}_{a}\mathbf{J}_{a}\mathbf{J}_{\frac{1}{a}}\varphi_{a}^{(k-1)}=(k+1)k\mathbf{J}_{\frac{1}{a}}\varphi_{a}^{(k-1)}.

By the induction hypothesis,
\mathbf{D}_{a}\varphi_{a}^{(k-1)}=(k-1)\varphi_{\frac{1}{a}}^{(k-2)}
, and since
k-1>0
, we have the relation
\varphi_{a}^{(k-1)}=(k-1)\mathbf{J}_{a}\varphi_{\frac{1}{a}}^{(k-2)}
.
Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}_{a}\varphi_{a}^{(k+1)}

\displaystyle=(k+1)(k)(k-1)\mathbf{J}_{\frac{1}{a}}\mathbf{J}_{a}\varphi_{\frac{1}{a}}^{(k-2)}=(k+1)\varphi_{\frac{1}{a}}^{(k)}.

This concludes the induction and proves (
19
) for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, as desired.

Remark 9

Using the SPPS series of

\mathbf{D}_{a}e_{a}(\rho,x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(i\rho)^{k}}{k!}\mathbf{D}_{a}\varphi_{a}^{(k)}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(i\rho)^{k}}{(k-1)!}\varphi_{\frac{1}{a}}^{(k-1)}(x)=i\rho e_{\frac{1}{a}}(\rho,x).

Theorem 10

Suppose that
\{a_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is a sequence of proper impedance functions such that

a_{n}^{2j}\rightarrow a^{2j}\;\;\mbox{in }L^{1}(I)\;\text{ as }n\rightarrow\infty\text{ for }j=-1,1.

(21)

Then, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
\varphi_{a_{n}}^{(k)}\rightarrow\varphi_{a}^{(k)}
and
\varphi_{\frac{1}{a_{n}}}^{(k)}\rightarrow\varphi_{\frac{1}{a}}^{(k)}

\overline{I}
.
In particular, the conclusion is valid when
a_{n}\rightarrow a
uniformly on
\overline{I}
.

Proof.
Indeed, the result is trivially true for
k=0
and

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{a_{n}}^{(1)}-\varphi_{a}^{(1)}\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}\leq\int_{I}\left|\frac{1}{a^{2}_{n}(t)}-\frac{1}{a^{2}(t)}\right|dt\rightarrow 0.

Suppose that
\varphi_{a_{n}}^{(j)}\rightarrow\varphi_{a}^{(j)}
uniformly for
1\leq j<k
. Hence

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{J}_{\frac{1}{a_{n}}}\varphi_{a_{n}}^{(k-2)}-\mathbf{J}_{\frac{1}{a}}\varphi_{a}^{(k-2)}\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}\leq

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{J}_{\frac{1}{a_{n}}}\varphi_{a_{n}}^{(k-2)}-\mathbf{J}_{\frac{1}{a_{n}}}\varphi_{a}^{(k-2)}\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}+\|\mathbf{J}_{\frac{1}{a_{n}}}\varphi_{a}^{(k-2)}-\mathbf{J}_{\frac{1}{a}}\varphi_{a}^{(k-2)}\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|a_{n}^{2}\|_{L^{1}(I)}\|\varphi_{a_{n}}^{(k-2)}-\varphi_{a}^{(k-2)}\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}

\displaystyle\;+\|a_{n}^{2}-a^{2}\|_{L^{1}(I)}\|\varphi_{a}^{(k-2)}\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}.

By (
21
), the sequence
\{\|a_{n}^{2}\|_{L^{1}(I)}\}
is bounded, and the induction hypothesis along with condition (

) implies
\|\mathbf{J}_{\frac{1}{a_{n}}}\varphi_{a_{n}}^{(k-2)}-\mathbf{J}_{\frac{1}{a}}\varphi_{a}^{(k-2)}\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}\rightarrow 0
. Hence
\mathbf{J}_{\frac{1}{a_{n}}}\varphi_{a_{n}}^{(k-2)}
converges uniformly to
\mathbf{J}_{\frac{1}{a}}\varphi_{a}^{(k-2)}

\varphi_{a_{n}}^{(k)}=k(k-1)\mathbf{J}_{a_{n}}\mathbf{J}_{\frac{1}{a_{n}}}\varphi_{a_{n}}^{(k-2)}\rightarrow k(k-1)\mathbf{J}_{a}\mathbf{J}_{\frac{1}{a}}\varphi_{a}^{(k-2)}=\varphi_{a}^{(k)}\text{ uniformly on }\overline{I}.

Remark 12

(i)

Abusing notation, we denote

Proposition 14

The following statements hold:

(i)

(25)

(iii)

\mathbf{D}_{a}^{(m)}=\prod_{j=1}^{m}\mathbf{D}_{a^{(-1)^{(j-1)}}}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbf{J}_{a}^{(m)}=\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}\mathbf{J}_{a^{(-1)^{(m-j)}}}

(27)

Proposition 15

For
m\in\mathbb{N}
, the general solution of equation
\mathbf{D}_{a}^{(m)}u=g
, with

, is given by

(28)

Moreover, if
a\in W^{m-1,p}(I)
, then every function
u\in W^{m,p}(I)
admits the generalized Taylor approximation

u=\mathbf{J}_{a}^{(m)}[\mathbf{D}_{a}^{(m)}u]+\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{\mathbf{D}_{a}^{(k)}u(x_{0})}{k!}\varphi_{a}^{(k)}.

(29)

If
x_{0}
is a Lebesgue point of
\mathbf{D}_{a}^{(m)}u
, the remainder term satisfies
\mathbf{J}_{a}^{(m)}[\mathbf{D}_{a}^{(m)}u]=O((x-x_{0})^{m})
as
x\rightarrow x_{0}
.

Proof.
From equalities (
27
), it follows that for any
g\in L_{a^{(-1)^{m}}}^{p}(\overline{I})
, the function
\mathbf{J}_{a}^{(m)}g
belongs to the domain of
\mathbf{D}_{a}^{(m)}
and
\mathbf{D}_{a}^{(m)}\mathbf{J}_{a}^{(m)}g=g
. First, we prove by induction that the unique solution of the homogeneous equation
\mathbf{D}_{a}^{(m)}v=0
has the form
v=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{\mathbf{D}_{a}^{(k)}u(x_{0})}{k!}\varphi_{a}^{(k)}

\mathbf{D}_{a}^{(m+1)}v=0

\mathbf{D}_{a}^{(m)}v=0

\displaystyle\lim_{x\rightarrow x_{0}}\frac{1}{(x-x_{0})^{m}}\mathbf{J}_{a}^{(m)}\mathbf{D}_{a}^{(m)}u(x)

\mathbf{J}_{a}^{(m)}\mathbf{D}_{a}^{(m)}u(x)=O((x-x_{0})^{m})

4
Completeness of the formal powers

Recall that a subset
M
of a Banach space
X
is called
complete
in
X
if
\operatorname{Span}M
is dense in
X
. In the case of a sequence of vectors
\{u_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
, if the set is complete we say that it forms a
complete system
. An element
u\in\operatorname{Span}\{u_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
can be written as
u=\sum_{k=0}^{N}\alpha_{k}u_{k}
, with
\alpha_{0},\dots,\alpha_{N}\in\mathbb{C}
. For example, by the Weierstrass approximation theorem and the density of
C(\overline{I})
in
L^{p}(I)
, is known that
\Pi(\overline{I})
is complete in
L^{p}(I)
, for
1\leq p<\infty
.

To prove the completeness of the formal powers
\{\varphi_{a}^{(k)}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
in
L^{p}(I)
,
1\leq p<\infty
, we recall some results related to Sturm-Liouville problems associated to Eq. (
2
).

First, suppose that
I=(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})
and consider the operator
\mathbf{L}_{a}^{D}:\mathscr{D}_{p}(\mathbf{L}_{a}^{D})\subset L_{a}^{p}(I)\rightarrow L_{a}^{p}(I)
given by the restriction of
\mathbf{L}_{a}
to the subspace
\mathscr{D}_{p}(\mathbf{L}_{a}^{D}):=\mathscr{D}_{p}(\mathbf{L}_{a})\cap W^{1,1}_{0}(I)
(that is, the operator subject to Dirichlet-to-Dirichlet boundary conditions
u(\ell_{1})=u(\ell_{2})=0
).

Lemma 17

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. The operator
\mathbf{R}_{a}^{D}:L_{a}^{p}(I)\rightarrow L_{a}^{p}(I)
given by

\mathbf{R}_{a}^{D}g=\mathbf{R}_{a}g+\frac{\mathbf{R}_{a}g(\ell_{1})-\mathbf{R}_{a}g(\ell_{2})}{\|a\|_{L^{2}(I)}}\varphi_{a}^{(1)}+\frac{\mathbf{R}_{a}g(\ell_{1})\varphi_{a}^{(1)}(\ell_{2})-\mathbf{R}_{a}g(\ell_{2})\varphi_{a}^{(1)}(\ell_{1})}{\|a\|_{L^{2}(I)}}\cdot 1,

(30)

is a right inverse for
\mathbf{L}_{a}^{D}
.

Proof.
Given
g\in L_{a}^{p}(I)
, consider the function

G=\mathbf{R}_{a}g+c_{1}\varphi_{a}^{(1)}+c_{0}

which satisfies
G\in\mathscr{D}_{p}(\mathbf{L}_{a})
and
\mathbf{L}_{a}G=g
. Now, we look for
c_{0},c_{1}\in\mathbb{C}
such that
G(\ell_{1})=G(\ell_{2})=0
. This leads to the system of equations

\displaystyle-\mathbf{R}_{a}g(\ell_{1})

\displaystyle=c_{0}+c_{1}\varphi_{a}^{(1)}(\ell_{1}),

\displaystyle-\mathbf{R}_{a}g(\ell_{2})

\displaystyle=c_{0}+c_{1}\varphi_{a}^{(1)}(\ell_{2})

The coefficient matrix has determinant

\displaystyle\begin{vmatrix}1&\varphi_{a}^{(1)}(\ell_{1}),\\
1&\varphi_{a}^{(1)}(\ell_{2})\end{vmatrix}

\displaystyle=\varphi_{a}^{(1)}(\ell_{2})-\varphi_{a}^{(1)}(\ell_{1})=\int_{x_{0}}^{\ell_{2}}\frac{dt}{a^{2}(t)}-\int_{x_{0}}^{\ell_{1}}\frac{dt}{a^{2}(t)}=\int_{\ell_{1}}^{\ell_{2}}\frac{dt}{a^{2}(t)}>0.

Since
a
is positive, then the system has a unique solution and a straightforward computation shows that these values of
c_{0},c_{1}
yield exactly the formula for
G=\mathbf{R}_{a}^{D}g
given in (
30
). Since

\|\mathbf{R}_{a}g\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}\leq\|a^{-1}\|_{L^{2}(I)}\|a\|_{L^{2}(I)}^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\|g\|_{L_{a}^{p}(I)},

we conclude that
\mathbf{R}_{a}^{D}\in\mathcal{B}(L_{a}^{p}(I))
.

Lemma 18

For
1<p<\infty
, the operator
\mathbf{L}_{a}^{D}
is densely defined on
L_{a}^{p}(I)
.

Proof.
Let
v\in L_{a}^{p^{\prime}}(I)
, with
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}
. Suppose that

\int_{I}a^{2}(x)u(x)v(x)dx=0\qquad\forall u\in\mathscr{D}(\mathbf{L}_{a}^{D}).

(31)

Let
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(I)
be arbitrary. By Lemma
17
,
\Phi=\mathbf{R}_{a}^{D}\phi\in\mathscr{D}_{p}(\mathbf{L}_{a}^{D})
is such that
\mathbf{L}_{a}\Phi=\phi
. Similarly,
V=\mathbf{R}_{a}^{D}v\in\mathscr{D}_{p^{\prime}}(\mathbf{L}_{a}^{D})
and
\mathbf{L}_{a}V=v
. Integration by parts and the boundary conditions in (
45
) yield

0=\int_{I}a^{2}\Phi v=\int_{I}a^{2}\Phi(\mathbf{L}_{a}V)=\int_{I}a^{2}(\mathbf{L}_{a}\Phi)V=\int_{I}a^{2}\phi V.

(32)

Since this relation holds for all
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(I)
, we obtain that
V=0
a.e. in
I
, and consequently
v=\mathbf{L}_{a}V=0
. Since
L_{a}^{p^{\prime}}(I)
is isometrically isomorphic to the dual of
L_{a}^{p}(I)
, we conclude that
\mathscr{D}(\mathbf{L}_{a}^{D})
is dense in
L_{a}^{p}(I)
.

Lemma 19

For
1<p<\infty
, the subspace
\mathbf{R}_{a}^{D}(C_{0}(I))
is dense in
\mathscr{D}_{p}(\mathbf{L}_{a}^{D})
. Consequently,
\mathbf{R}_{a}^{D}(C_{0}^{\infty}(I))
is dense in
L_{a}^{p}(I)
.

Proof.
Let
g\in\mathscr{D}_{p}(\mathbf{L}_{a})
and
\varepsilon>0
. Since
a^{2}(x)dx
is a finite Radon measure on
I
, it follows that
C_{0}(I)
is dense in
L_{a}^{p}(I)

[
9
, Ch. 7, Prop. 7.9]
. Because
\mathbf{L}_{a}g\in L_{a}^{p}(I)
, there exists
\psi\in C_{0}(I)
such that
\|\mathbf{L}_{a}g-\psi\|_{L_{a}^{p}(I)}<\frac{\varepsilon}{M}
, where
M
is the operator-norm of
\mathbf{R}_{a}^{D}
. Since
g=\mathbf{R}_{a}^{D}\mathbf{L}_{a}g
, we have

\|g-\mathbf{R}_{a}^{D}\psi\|_{L_{a}^{p}(I)}\leq M\|\mathbf{L}_{a}g-\psi\|_{L_{a}^{p}(I)}<\varepsilon.

Thus,
\mathbf{R}_{a}^{D}(C_{0}(I))
is dense in
\mathscr{D}_{p}(\mathbf{L}_{a}^{D})
. The density of
\mathbf{R}_{a}^{D}(C_{0}(I))
in
L_{a}^{p}(I)
follows from Lemma
18
. Since
C_{0}^{\infty}(I)
is dense in
C_{0}(I)
with the uniform norm and the embedding
C_{0}(I)\hookrightarrow L_{a}^{p}(I)

\mathbf{R}_{a}^{D}(C_{0}^{\infty}(I))
is dense in
L_{a}^{p}(I)
.

We recall the following result for the Sturm-Liouville problem with Dirichlet-to-Dirichlet condition on

Theorem 20

The Sturm-Liouville problem consisting of Eq. (

Theorem 21

For
1\leq p<\infty
, the formal powers
\{\varphi_{a}^{(k)}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
are a complete system in
L_{a}^{p}(I)
.

Proof.
First, consider
p>1
. Let
f\in L_{a}^{p}(I)
and
\varepsilon>0
. By Lemma
19
, there exists
\psi\in C_{0}(I)
with
\Psi=\mathbf{R}_{a}^{D}\psi\in\mathscr{D}_{p}(\mathbf{L}_{a}^{D})

\|f-\Psi\|_{L_{a}^{p}(I)}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

(33)

Note that
\Psi\in C(\overline{I})\subset L_{a}^{2}(I)
with
\mathbf{L}_{a}\Psi=\psi\in C_{0}(I)\subset L_{a}^{2}(I)
, so
\Psi\in\mathscr{D}_{2}(\mathbf{L}_{a}^{D})
. Thus, by the second part of Theorem
20
, there exist
\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{N}
eigenfunctions of the Sturm-Liouville problem of Eq. (

\left\|\Psi-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\widetilde{\Psi}_{n}\phi_{n}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}<\frac{\varepsilon}{4\|a\|_{L^{2}(I)}},\quad\text{where }\;\widetilde{\Psi}_{n}=\langle\Psi,\phi_{n}\rangle_{L_{a}^{2}(I)},\;n=1,\dots,N.

(34)

Since for each eigenvalue
\lambda_{n}=\rho_{n}^{2}

\{e_{a}(\rho_{n},x),e_{a}(-\rho_{n},x)\}
forms a fundamental set of solutions for Eq. (

is a linear combination of

e_{a}(-\rho_{n},x)

\alpha_{0,1},\dots,\alpha_{M_{N},N}\in\mathbb{C}

\left\|\phi_{n}-\sum_{k=0}^{M_{N}}\alpha_{k,n}\varphi_{a}^{(k)}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}<\frac{\varepsilon}{4\|a\|_{L^{2}(I)}CN},

) and (

\displaystyle\|\Psi-\widehat{p}\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\Psi-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\widetilde{\Psi}_{n}\phi_{n}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}|\widetilde{\Psi}_{n}|\left\|\phi_{n}-\sum_{k=0}^{M_{n}}\alpha_{k,n}\varphi_{a}^{(k)}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2\|a\|_{L^{2}(I)}}.

\displaystyle\|f-\widehat{p}\|_{L_{a}^{p}(I)}\leq\|f-\Psi\|_{L_{a}^{p}(I)}+\|\Psi-\widehat{p}\|_{L_{a}^{p}(I)}<\varepsilon.

\Pi_{a}(\overline{I})

L_{a}^{p}(I)

L_{a}^{2}(I)
is dense in
L_{a}^{1}(I)
. Therefore,
\Pi_{a}(\overline{I})
is dense in
L_{a}^{1}(I)

Theorem 22

Let
1\leq p<\infty
.

(i)

If

.

(ii)

For

, if

Proof.

(i)

Let

and

u=\mathbf{I}_{a}\left[\mathbf{D}_{a}u\right]+u(x_{0}).

(36)

By Theorem

, there exists a sequence
\{\widehat{p}_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\subset\Pi_{\frac{1}{a}}(\overline{I})
such that
\widehat{p}_{n}\rightarrow\mathbf{D}_{a}u
in

. By Remark

,
\mathbf{I}_{a}\widehat{p}_{n}\in\Pi_{a}(\overline{I})
for all

and
\mathbf{I}_{a}\widehat{p}_{n}\rightarrow\mathbf{I}_{a}[\mathbf{D}_{a}u]

\widehat{q}_{n}=\mathbf{I}_{a}\widehat{p}_{n}+u(x_{0})\in\Pi_{a}(\overline{I}),\quad n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

satisfies that
\widehat{q}_{n}\rightarrow u

\Pi_{a}(\overline{I})
is dense in
W^{1,p}(I)
.

(ii)

Given

can be written as

u=\mathbf{J}_{a}^{(m)}[\mathbf{D}_{a}^{(m)}u]+\sum_{k=0}^{m}\frac{\mathbf{D}_{a}^{(k)}u(x_{0})}{k!}\varphi_{a}^{(k)}.

Theorem

implies that there exists a sequence
\{\widehat{p}_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\subset\Pi_{a^{(-1)^{m}}}(\overline{I})
with

\{\widehat{q}_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\subset\Pi_{a}(\overline{I})

5
Transmutation operators for
a\in W^{1,\infty}(-\ell,\ell)

5.1
Transmutation property

In this section, we assume that
I=(-\ell,\ell)
, with
\ell>0
, that
x_{0}=0
, and that
a\in W^{1,\infty}(-\ell,\ell)
satisfies the normalization condition that
a(0)=1
. In this case, the operators
\mathbf{I}_{a},\mathbf{R}_{a},\mathbf{L}_{a}
and
\mathbf{D}_{a},
are well defined on the spaces
L^{p}(-\ell,\ell)
,
W^{1,p}(-\ell,\ell)
and
W^{2,p}(-\ell,\ell)
for
1\leq p\leq\infty
.

It is known that for
\frac{a^{\prime}}{a}\in L^{\infty}(-\ell,\ell)
, the solution
e_{a}(\rho,x)
admits the integral representation

e_{a}(\rho,x)=e^{i\rho x}-i\rho\int_{-x}^{x}K_{a}(x,t)e^{i\rho t}dt,\qquad-\ell\leq x\leq\ell,

(37)

where
K_{a}(x,t)
is the Fourier transform of
\frac{e^{i\rho x}-e_{a}(\rho,x)}{i\rho}
(see
[
7
]
and
[
21
, Sec. 2.1]
). Furthermore, the kernel
K_{a}(x,t)
satisfies some regularity conditions.

Theorem 24

The integral kernel
K_{a}\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
, where
\Omega=(-\ell,\ell)\times(-\ell,\ell)
, and satisfies the Goursat conditions

K_{a}(x,x)=1-\frac{1}{a(x)},\quad K_{a}(x,-x)=0,\qquad-\ell\leq x\leq\ell.

(38)

Proof.
See
[
21
]
, Subsection 2.6, Remark 8.

Set

\mathbf{T}_{a}u(x):=u(x)-\int_{-x}^{x}K_{a}(x,t)u^{\prime}(t)dt,\quad u\in W^{1,p}(-\ell,\ell),\;-\ell\leq x\leq\ell.

(39)

The operator is well defined for
u\in W^{1,p}(-\ell,\ell)
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
.

Hence, integral representation (
37
) can be expressed as

e_{a}(\rho,x)=\mathbf{T}_{a}[e^{i\rho x}].

(40)

Consequently,

C_{a}(\rho,x)=\mathbf{T}_{a}[\cos(\rho x)],\quad S_{a}(\rho,x)=\mathbf{T}_{a}\left[\frac{\sin(\rho x)}{\rho}\right].

(41)

We establish the first analytical properties of the operator
\mathbf{T}_{a}
.

Proposition 25

For
1\leq p\leq\infty
, the operator
\mathbf{T}_{a}
belongs to
\mathcal{B}(W^{1,p}(-\ell,\ell))
, and its derivative is given by

D\mathbf{T}_{a}u(x)=\frac{u^{\prime}(x)}{a(x)}-\int_{-x}^{x}\partial_{x}K_{a}(x,t)u^{\prime}(t)dt.

(42)

In the case when
u\in W^{2,p}(-\ell,\ell)
, if
v=\mathbf{T}_{a}u
, then
v(0)=u(0)
and
v^{\prime}(0)=u^{\prime}(0)
, that is,
\mathbf{T}_{a}
preserves the initial conditions at
x=0
.

Proof.
Consider the operator
\mathbf{K}_{1}u(x):=\int_{-x}^{x}K_{a}(x,t)u(t)dt
,
u\in W^{1,p}(-\ell,\ell)
. Since
\mathbf{K}_{1}
is a Volterra integral operator with kernel
K\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, hence
\mathbf{K}_{1}\in\mathcal{B}(L^{p}(-\ell,\ell))
. For any
u\in W^{1,p}(-\ell,\ell)
, we have

\|\mathbf{T}_{a}u\|_{L^{p}(-\ell,\ell)}=\|u-\mathbf{K}_{1}u^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}(-\ell,\ell)}\leq\|u\|_{L^{p}(-\ell,\ell)}+\|\mathbf{K}_{1}\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{p}(-\ell,\ell))}\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}(-\ell,\ell)}.

For the derivative, using the fact that
K_{a}\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
and applying the Leibniz rule, we obtain

\displaystyle D\mathbf{T}_{a}u(x)

\displaystyle=u^{\prime}(x)-K_{a}(x,x)u^{\prime}(x)-K_{a}(x,-x)u^{\prime}(-x)-\int_{-x}^{x}\partial_{x}K_{a}(x,t)u^{\prime}(t)dt

\displaystyle=u^{\prime}(x)-\left(1-\frac{1}{a(x)}\right)u^{\prime}(x)-\int_{-x}^{x}\partial_{x}K_{a}(x,t)u^{\prime}(t)dt

\displaystyle=\frac{u^{\prime}(x)}{a(x)}-\int_{-x}^{x}\partial_{x}K_{a}(x,t)u^{\prime}(t)dt.

The second equality is due to the Goursat conditions (
38
). This establishes (
42
). Now, consider the Volterra operator
\mathbf{K}_{2}v(x)=\int_{-x}^{x}\partial_{x}K_{a}(x,t)v(t)dt
. Since
\partial_{x}K_{a}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, it follows that
\mathbf{K}_{2}\in\mathcal{B}(L^{p}(-\ell,\ell))
. Thus,

\displaystyle\left\|D\mathbf{T}_{a}u\right\|_{L^{p}(-\ell,\ell)}

\displaystyle=\left\|\frac{u^{\prime}}{a}-\mathbf{K}_{2}u^{\prime}\right\|_{L^{p}(-\ell,\ell)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|a^{-1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(-\ell,\ell)}\|u\|_{L^{p}(-\ell,\ell)}+\|\mathbf{K}_{2}\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{p}(-\ell,\ell))}\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}(-\ell,\ell)}.

Consequently, for
1\leq p<\infty
,

\|\mathbf{T}_{a}u\|_{W^{1,p}(-\ell,\ell)}^{p}\leq\left[\left(1+\|\mathbf{K}_{1}\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{p}(-\ell,\ell))}\right)^{p}+\left(\left\|a^{-1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(-\ell,\ell)}+\|\mathbf{K}_{2}\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{p}(-\ell,\ell))}\right)^{p}\right]\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(-\ell,\ell)},

which implies that
\mathbf{T}_{a}\in\mathcal{B}(W^{1,p}(-\ell,\ell))
. Similarly for
p=\infty
.

Finally, if
v=\mathbf{T}_{a}u
,
u\in W^{2,p}(-\ell,\ell)
, hence
v(0)=u(0)
, and by (
42
),
v^{\prime}(0)=u^{\prime}(0)
.

The following result generalizes the corresponding statement established for
a\in C^{1}[-\ell,\ell]
in
[
21
]
.

Proposition 26

The following relations hold:

\mathbf{T}_{a}[x^{k}]=\varphi_{a}^{(k)},\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(43)

Proof.
Since the Taylor series of
e^{i\rho x}
converges in
W^{1,\infty}(-\ell,\ell)
, by the integral representation (
40
) and the continuity of the operator
\mathbf{T}_{a}
, we have

e(\rho,x)=\mathbf{T}_{a}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(i\rho)^{k}x^{k}}{k!}\right]=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(i\rho)^{k}\mathbf{T}_{a}[x^{k}]}{k!}.

Comparing this series with the SPPS series (
13
), as Taylor series in
\rho
, we obtain (
43
).

Theorem 27

For
1\leq p<\infty
, the operator
\mathbf{T}_{a}
satisfies the following transmutation property:

\mathbf{L}_{a}\mathbf{T}_{a}u=\mathbf{T}_{a}D^{2}u,\quad\forall u\in W^{3,p}(-\ell,\ell).

(44)

Proof.
We begin by proving the relation (
39
) for
p\in\Pi[-\ell,\ell]
. Let
p(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{N}a_{k}x^{k}
. By relations (
18
) and the mapping property (
43
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{L}_{a}\mathbf{T}_{a}p(x)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{L}_{a}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{N}a_{k}\mathbf{T}_{a}[x^{k}]\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{N}\alpha_{k}\mathbf{L}_{a}\varphi_{a}^{(k)}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=2}^{N}k(k-1)\alpha_{k}\varphi_{a}^{(k-2)}=\sum_{k=2}^{N}k(k-1)\alpha_{k}\mathbf{T}_{a}[x^{k-2}]=\mathbf{T}_{a}D^{2}p(x).

For a general
u\in W^{3,p}(-\ell,\ell)
, let
v=\mathbf{T}_{a}u
, and note that (
44
) is equivalent to

\mathbf{L}_{a}v=\mathbf{T}_{a}D^{2}u.

By Remark (
4
) and since
a(0)=1
, this is equivalent to

v=\mathbf{R}_{a}\mathbf{T}_{a}D^{2}u+v(0)\varphi_{a}^{(0)}+v^{\prime}(0)\varphi_{a}^{(1)}.

(45)

We prove relation (
45
). Let
\{p_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\subset\Pi[-\ell,\ell]
such that
p_{n}^{(j)}\rightarrow u^{(j)}
uniformly on
[-\ell,\ell]
for
j=0,1,2,
and
p_{n}^{\prime\prime\prime}\rightarrow u^{\prime\prime\prime}
in
L^{p}(-\ell,\ell)
. Since (
44
) is valid for each
p_{n}
, hence

\mathbf{T}_{a}p_{n}=\mathbf{R}_{a}\mathbf{T}_{a}D^{2}p_{n}+p_{n}(0)\varphi_{a}^{(0)}+p_{n}^{\prime}(0)\varphi_{a}^{(1)}

The continuity of
\mathbf{T}_{a}
in
W^{1,p}(-\ell,\ell)
and
\mathbf{R}_{a}
in
L^{p}(-\ell,\ell)
yields

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{T}_{a}p_{n}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\mathbf{R}_{a}\mathbf{T}_{a}D^{2}p_{n}+p_{n}(0)\varphi_{a}^{(0)}+p_{n}^{\prime}(0)\varphi_{a}^{(1)}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{R}_{a}\mathbf{T}_{a}D^{2}u+u(0)\varphi_{a}^{(0)}+u^{\prime}(0)\varphi_{a}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{R}_{a}\mathbf{T}_{a}D^{2}u+v(0)\varphi_{a}^{(0)}+v^{\prime}(0)\varphi_{a}^{(1)},

because
v(0)=u(0)
and
v^{\prime}(0)=u^{\prime}(0)
. Therefore, the transmutation property(

, as desired.

5.2
Invertibility of the transmutation operator

Let
y\in W^{1,p}(-\ell,\ell)
. Suppose that
u\in W^{1,p}(-\ell,\ell)
satisfies the equation
y=\mathbf{T}_{a}u
. Integration by parts yields

\displaystyle y(x)

\displaystyle=u(x)-K_{a}(x,t)u^{\prime}(t)\big|_{-x}^{x}+\int_{-x}^{x}\partial_{t}K_{a}(x,t)u(t)dt

\displaystyle=u(x)-K_{a}(x,x)u(x)+K_{a}(x,-x)u(-x)+\int_{-x}^{x}\partial_{t}K_{a}(x,t)u(t)dt

\displaystyle=\frac{u(x)}{a(x)}+\int_{-x}^{x}\partial_{t}K_{a}(x,t)u(t)dt,

where the last equality follows from the Goursat conditions (
38
). This leads to the Volterra integral equation

a(x)y(x)=u(x)+\int_{-x}^{x}a(x)\partial_{t}K_{a}(x,t)u(t)dt

where the kernel
a\partial_{t}K_{a}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Since
ay\in L^{\infty}(-\ell,\ell)
, this Volterra equation of the second kind admits a unique solution
u\in L^{\infty}(-\ell,\ell)
given by

u(x)=a(x)y(x)+\int_{-x}^{x}L(x,t)[a(t)y(t)]dt,

(46)

where
L\in L^{\infty}(R)
(see, e.g.,
[
23
, Ch. 10, Sub. 2.5]
or
[
34
, Ch. IV]
). In particular, we obtain that
\mathbf{T}_{a}u=0
implies
u=0
, i.e., the operator
\mathbf{T}_{a}
is injective.
We denote by
\mathbf{V}_{a}
the operator on the right-hand side of (
46
). It is the composition of a Volterra integral operator with kernel
L\in L^{\infty}(R)
and the multiplication operator by
a
. Hence,
\mathbf{V}_{a}\in\mathcal{B}(L^{p}(-\ell,\ell))
. Since
\mathbf{T}_{a}[x^{k}]=\varphi_{a}^{(k)}(x)
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we obtain

x^{k}=\mathbf{V}_{a}\varphi_{a}^{(k)}(x),\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Consequently, if
p(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{N}\alpha_{k}x^{k}
, then

p(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{N}\alpha_{k}\mathbf{V}_{a}\varphi_{a}^{(k)}(x).

Therefore, the operators
\mathbf{T}_{a}:\Pi[-\ell,\ell]\to\Pi_{a}[-\ell,\ell]
and
\mathbf{V}_{a}:\Pi_{a}[-\ell,\ell]\to\Pi[-\ell,\ell]
are linear isomorphisms, and they satisfy

\mathbf{T}_{a}\mathbf{V}_{a}=\mathbf{I}_{\Pi_{a}[-\ell,\ell]}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbf{V}_{a}\mathbf{T}_{a}=\mathbf{I}_{\Pi[-\ell,\ell]}.

(47)

Lemma 28

There exists
C>0
such that

\|\mathbf{T}_{a}p\|_{W^{1,p}(-\ell,\ell)}\geq C\|p\|_{W^{1,p}(-\ell,\ell)}\qquad\forall p\in\Pi[-\ell,\ell].

(48)

Proof.
Let
p(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{N}\alpha_{k}x^{k}
. Since
\mathbf{V}_{a}\in\mathcal{B}(L^{p}(-\ell,\ell))
, we have

\|p\|_{L^{p}(-\ell,\ell)}=\|\mathbf{V}_{a}\mathbf{T}_{a}p\|_{L^{p}(-\ell,\ell)}\leq\|\mathbf{V}_{a}\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{p}(-\ell,\ell))}\|\mathbf{T}_{a}p\|_{L^{p}(-\ell,\ell)}.

To analyze the derivative, consider the operator
\mathbf{V}_{\frac{1}{a}}\in\mathcal{B}(L^{p}(-\ell,\ell))
. From equation (
19
), for
k\geq 1
, we obtain

\displaystyle Dx^{k}=kx^{k-1}=k\mathbf{V}_{\frac{1}{a}}\varphi_{\frac{1}{a}}^{(k-1)}(x)=\mathbf{V}_{\frac{1}{a}}\mathbf{D}_{a}\varphi_{a}^{(k)}.

Since
D1=0=\mathbf{V}_{\frac{1}{a}}\mathbf{D}_{a}\varphi_{a}^{(0)}
, it follows by linearity that

Dp=\mathbf{V}_{\frac{1}{a}}\mathbf{D}_{a}\mathbf{T}_{a}p.

Consequently,

\|Dp\|_{L^{p}(-\ell,\ell)}\leq\|\mathbf{V}_{\frac{1}{a}}\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{p}(-\ell,\ell))}\|\mathbf{D}_{a}\mathbf{T}_{a}p\|_{L^{p}(-\ell,\ell)}\leq\|\mathbf{V}_{\frac{1}{a}}\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{p}(-\ell,\ell))}\|a^{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(-\ell,\ell)}\|D\mathbf{T}_{a}p\|_{L^{p}(-\ell,\ell)}.

Hence

\|p\|_{W^{1,p}(-\ell,\ell)}\leq M\|\mathbf{T}_{a}p\|_{W^{1,p}(-\ell,\ell)},

where
M=\max\{\|\mathbf{V}_{a}\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{p}(-\ell,\ell))},\|\mathbf{V}_{\frac{1}{a}}\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{p}(-\ell,\ell))}\|a^{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(-\ell,\ell)}\}
. Therefore, inequality (
48
) holds with constant
C=\frac{1}{M}
.

Theorem 29

For
1\leq p<\infty
,
\mathbf{T}_{a}\in\mathcal{G}(W^{1,p}(-\ell,\ell))

Proof.
Let

Hence, the operator
\mathbf{V}
is bounded with respect to the
W^{1,p}

\Pi_{a}[-\ell,\ell]

\Pi_{a}[-\ell,\ell]

\mathbf{V}_{a}

\mathbf{V}_{a}

Proof.
It is enough to prove the first equality. Let
v\in C^{2}[-\ell,\ell]
. Using relations (
42
) and (
49
), we have

\displaystyle a^{2}(x)\mathbf{T}_{a}Dv(x)

\displaystyle=D\mathbf{T}_{\frac{1}{a}}v(x)=a(x)v^{\prime}(x)-\int_{-x}^{x}\partial_{x}K_{\frac{1}{a}}(x,t)v^{\prime}(t)dt.

Integration by parts on
\mathbf{T}_{a}Dv(x)
gives

\displaystyle\mathbf{T}_{a}Dv(x)

\displaystyle=v^{\prime}(x)-\int_{-x}^{x}K_{a}(x,t)v^{\prime\prime}(t)dt

\displaystyle=v^{\prime}(x)-K_{a}(x,t)v^{\prime}(t)\big|_{-x}^{x}+\int_{-x}^{x}\partial_{t}K_{a}(x,t)v^{\prime}(t)dt

\displaystyle=\frac{v^{\prime}(x)}{a(x)}+\int_{-x}^{x}\partial_{t}K_{a}(x,t)v^{\prime}(t)dt,

where the last equality follows from the Goursat conditions (
38
). Comparing both expression for
D\mathbf{T}_{\frac{1}{a}}v(x)=a^{2}(x)\mathbf{T}_{a}Dv(x)
, we conclude that

\int_{-x}^{x}a^{2}(x)\partial_{t}K_{a}(x,t)v^{\prime}(t)dt=-\int_{-x}^{x}\partial_{x}K_{\frac{1}{a}}(x,t)v^{\prime}(t)dt.

This relation holds for every
v\in C^{2}[-\ell,\ell]
. Fix
x\in[-\ell,\ell]
, and let
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(-\ell,\ell)
be an arbitrary test function with support contained in
[-|x|,|x|]
. Taking
\Phi\in C^{\infty}[-\ell,\ell]
with
\Phi^{\prime}=\phi
, we obtain

\int_{-x}^{x}a^{2}(x)\partial_{t}K_{a}(x,t)\phi(t)dt=-\int_{-x}^{x}\partial_{x}K_{\frac{1}{a}}(x,t)\phi(t)dt.

Since
\phi
was arbitrary, it follows that
\partial_{x}K_{\frac{1}{a}}(x,t)=-a^{2}(x)\partial_{t}K_{a}(x,t)
for all
|t|\leq|x|
. Because
x\in[-\ell,\ell]
was arbitrary, we conclude that (
52
) holds, as desired.

Now, assume that
a\in C^{1}[-\ell,\ell]
. Let
v\in C^{2}[-\ell,\ell]
. According to (
49
), we have
\mathbf{D}_{\frac{1}{a}}\mathbf{T}_{\frac{1}{a}}v=\mathbf{T}_{a}Dv
. Since both sides of the equality belong to
W^{1,p}(-\ell,\ell)
, we can apply the inverses of
\mathbf{T}_{a}
and
\mathbf{T}_{\frac{1}{a}}
to obtain

\mathbf{T}_{a}^{-1}\mathbf{D}_{\frac{1}{a}}v(x)=D\mathbf{T}_{\frac{1}{a}}^{-1}v(x),\quad-\ell\leq x\leq\ell.

By
[
21
, Th. 30]
, since
a\in C^{1}[-\ell,\ell]
, the inverse
\mathbf{T}_{a}^{-1}
can be represented as

\mathbf{T}_{a}v(x)=v(x)-\int_{-x}^{x}K_{\frac{1}{a}}(t,x)v^{\prime}(t)dt.

(53)

Applying Leibniz’s rule and using the Goursat conditions (
38
), we get

D\mathbf{T}_{\frac{1}{a}}^{-1}v(x)=\frac{v^{\prime}(x)}{a(x)}-\int_{-x}^{x}\partial_{x}(K_{a}(t,x))v^{\prime}(t)dt.

(54)

On the other hand, integration by parts and using the Goursat conditions again, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{T}_{a}^{-1}\mathbf{D}_{\frac{1}{a}}v(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{v^{\prime}(x)}{a^{2}(x)}-\int_{-x}^{x}K_{\frac{1}{a}}(t,x)\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{v^{\prime}(t)}{a^{2}(t)}\right)dt

\displaystyle=\frac{v^{\prime}(x)}{a^{2}(x)}-K_{\frac{1}{a}}(t,x)\frac{v^{\prime}(t)}{a^{2}(t)}\bigg|_{-x}^{x}+\int_{-x}^{x}\partial_{t}(K_{\frac{1}{a}}(t,x))\frac{v^{\prime}(t)}{a^{2}(t)}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{v^{\prime}(x)}{a^{2}(x)}-(1-a(x))\frac{v^{\prime}(x)}{a^{2}(x)}+\int_{-x}^{x}\partial_{t}(K_{\frac{1}{a}}(t,x))\frac{v^{\prime}(t)}{a^{2}(t)}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{v^{\prime}(x)}{a(x)}+\int_{-x}^{x}\partial_{t}(K_{\frac{1}{a}}(t,x))\frac{v^{\prime}(t)}{a^{2}(t)}dt.

Comparing this expression with (
54
) yields

\int_{-x}^{x}\frac{\partial_{t}(K_{\frac{1}{a}}(t,x))}{a^{2}(t)}v^{\prime}(t)dt=-\int_{-x}^{x}\partial_{x}(K_{a}(t,x))v^{\prime}(t)dt,

for all
-\ell\leq x\leq\ell
and
v\in C^{2}[-\ell,\ell]
. Applying a procedure analogous to the first part of the proof, we obtain

\frac{\partial_{t}(K_{\frac{1}{a}}(t,x))}{a^{2}(t)}=-\partial_{x}(K_{a}(t,x))\quad\text{for }\;\;\;|t|\leq|x|\leq\ell.

A straightforward computation shows that this equation is equivalent to

\frac{\partial_{x}K_{\frac{1}{a}}(x,t)}{a^{2}(x)}=-\partial_{t}K_{a}(x,t)\quad\text{for}\;\;\;|x|\leq|t|\leq\ell.

Therefore, by (
52
), this relation holds throughout the entire rectangle
\Omega
.

System (
52
) is also known as a generalized hyperbolic Cauchy-Riemann system, and its solutions are referred to as
a^{2}
-hyperbolic analytic
[
27
]
. Actually, this system can be reformulated in terms of the so-called
hyperbolic main Vekua equation

[
15
]
. From this, it is possible to obtain a representation of
K_{\frac{1}{a}}
in terms of
K_{a}
.

Proposition 35

If
a\in C^{1}[-\ell,\ell]
, then the following relation holds:

K_{\frac{1}{a}}(x,t)=-\int_{0}^{x}a^{2}(\xi)\partial_{\zeta}K_{a}(\xi,0)d\xi-\int_{0}^{t}a^{2}(x)\partial_{\xi}K_{a}(x,\zeta)d\zeta,\quad(x,t)\in\Omega.

(55)

Proof.
First, assume that
a\in C^{2}[-\ell,\ell]
. The system (
52
) can be written as

\nabla K_{\frac{1}{a}}(x,t)=(P(x,t),Q(x,t))\text{ where }\;P(x,t)=-a^{2}(x)\partial_{t}K_{a}(x,t),\;Q(x,t)=-a^{2}(x)\partial_{x}K_{a}(x,t).

Since
a\in C^{2}[-\ell,\ell]
, according to
[
21
, Prop. 2]
, the kernel
K_{a}\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
and satisfies the equation
\partial_{x}(a^{2}(x)\partial_{x}K_{a})=a^{2}(x)\partial_{t}^{2}K_{a}
in
\Omega
. Hence

\partial_{x}Q-\partial_{t}P=-\partial_{x}(a^{2}(x)\partial_{x}K_{a})+a^{2}(x)\partial_{t}^{2}K_{a}=0.

Since
\Omega
is simply connected, the vector field
(P,Q)
is conservative, and
K_{\frac{1}{a}}
can be recovered as

K_{a}(x,t)=\int_{\Gamma}\left\{-a^{2}(\xi)\partial_{\zeta}K_{a}(\xi,\zeta)d\xi-a^{2}(\xi)\partial_{\xi}K_{a}(\xi,\zeta)d\zeta\right\}+c

where
c
is a constant and
\Gamma
is a rectifiable path joining
(0,0)
with
(x,t)
. Taking the polygonal path from
(0,0)
to
(x,0)
and then to
(x,t)
, and using that
K_{a}(0,0)=0
, we obtain (
55
).

For the case
a\in C^{1}[-\ell,\ell]
, consider the function
q=\frac{2a^{\prime}}{a}\in C[-\ell,\ell]
. Take a sequence
\{q_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\in C^{2}[-\ell,\ell]
such that
q_{n}\rightarrow q
uniformly on
[-\ell,\ell]
. Define
a_{n}(x)=e^{-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{x}q_{n}(s)ds}
. By
[
21
, Th. 6]
, the corresponding kernels
K_{a_{n}}
converge uniformly on
\Omega
together with their first derivatives to
K_{a}
. Passing to the limit in the integral formula yields (
55
).

6
Transmutation operator for
a\in W^{1,2}

First, assume that
a\in W^{1,1}(0,\ell)
. By (
9
)-(
12
),
e_{a}(\rho,x)=C_{a}(\rho,x)+i\rho S_{a}(\rho,x)
. According to
[
7
, Th. 22]
and
[
21
, Prop. 1]
, for fixed
x\in(0,\ell]
, the solutions
C_{a}(\rho,x)
and
S_{a}(\rho,x)
satisfy the following estimates for all
\rho\in\mathbb{C}
, :

|C_{a}(\rho,x)-\cos(\rho x)|\leq\frac{c|\rho|xe^{|\operatorname{Im}\rho|x}}{1+|\rho|x}e^{Q_{1}(x)},\quad\left|S_{a}(\rho,x)-\frac{\sin(\rho x)}{\rho}\right|\leq\frac{cxe^{|\operatorname{Im}\rho|x}}{1+|\rho|x}e^{Q_{1}(x)}

where
Q_{1}(x):=\int_{0}^{x}|q(t)|dt
and
c
is a constant independent of
x
and
\rho
. From these estimates, we obtain

|e_{a}(\rho,x)-e^{i\rho x}|\leq\frac{2c|\rho|xe^{|\operatorname{Im}\rho|x}}{1+|\rho|x}e^{Q_{1}(x)}\quad\forall\rho\in\mathbb{C}.

Consider the function
\varphi_{x}(\rho):=\frac{e^{i\rho x}-e_{a}(\rho,x)}{i\rho}
. Hence
\varphi_{x}\in\operatorname{Hol}(\mathbb{C})
and is of exponential type
x
. Moreover, the following estimate holds:

|\varphi_{x}(\rho)|\leq\frac{cxe^{|\operatorname{Im}\rho|x}}{1+|\rho|x}e^{Q_{1}(x)},

from which we deduce that
\varphi_{x}|_{\mathbb{R}}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. By the Paley-Wiener theorem
[
30
, Th. 19.3]
, it follows that
\varphi_{x}(\rho)=\int_{-x}^{x}k_{x}(t)e^{i\rho t}dt
, for some
k_{x}\in L^{2}(-x,x)
. Defining
K_{a}(x,t):=k_{x}(t)
, we obtain the representation (
37
). The kernel
K_{a}(x,t)
is defined on the triangle
\mathcal{T}:=\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,|\,|t|\leq x\leq\ell\}
. Furthermore, by Plancherel’s theorem

\displaystyle\int_{-x}^{x}|K_{a}(x,t)|^{2}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\varphi_{x}(\rho)|^{2}d\rho

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c^{2}x^{2}e^{2\|q\|_{L^{1}(0,\ell)}}}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\rho}{(1+|\rho|x)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{c^{2}xe^{2\|q\|_{L^{1}(0,\ell)}}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{(1+t)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c^{2}\ell e^{2\|q\|_{L^{1}(0,\ell)}}I}{\pi},

where
I=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{(1+t)^{2}}
. Hence

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathcal{T}}|K_{a}(x,t)|^{2}dtdx=\int_{0}^{\ell}\int_{-x}^{x}|K_{a}(x,t)|^{2}dtdx\leq\frac{c^{2}\ell^{2}e^{2\|q\|_{L^{1}(0,\ell)}}I}{\pi}<\infty.

given by (

into

Theorem 37

Suppose that
a\in W^{1,2}(0,\ell)
. Then the following statements hold:

(i)

Relation (
49
) is valid for
u\in W^{2,2}(-\ell,\ell)
.

(ii)

The transmutation property (
44
) is valid for
u\in W^{3,2}(-\ell,\ell)
.

Proof.
It suffices to prove (i) (the property (ii) follows from (i) y the same arguments as in the second proof of Theorem
27
). From the relations (
43
) and (
19
), together with the linearity of
\mathbf{T}_{a}
, we have
\mathbf{D}_{a}\mathbf{T}_{a}p=\mathbf{T}_{\frac{1}{a}}p^{\prime}
for

\mathbf{T}_{a}p(0)=p(0)

\mathbf{T}_{a}p=\mathbf{J}_{a}\mathbf{T}_{\frac{1}{a}}p^{\prime}+p(0)\qquad\forall p\in\Pi[-\ell,\ell].

Let

23
, there exists a sequence
\{p_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\subset\Pi[-\ell,\ell]
such that
p_{n}^{(j)}\rightarrow u^{(j)}
uniformly on

\mathbf{T}_{a}p_{n}\rightarrow\mathbf{T}_{a}u

7
Conclusions

The construction of a complete system of functions, called formal powers, associated with the Sturm-Liouville equation with an integrable impedance, is presented. We establish the relationship between the formal powers corresponding to the original Sturm-Liouville equation and those of its Darboux-transformed counterpart. Moreover, approximation results via generalized Taylor polynomials and completeness in
L^{p}
and Sobolev spaces are proved.

The completeness of the formal powers enables us to derive key properties for a pair of transmutation operators connecting the Sturm-Liouville equation and its Darboux transform, including the existence of continuous inverses. These results provide powerful tools for the analytical representation of solutions to the Sturm-Liouville equation, as well as for addressing direct and inverse spectral boundary value problems. This framework is exemplified in the case of equations such as the Schrödinger equation in
[
11
,
13
,
20
]

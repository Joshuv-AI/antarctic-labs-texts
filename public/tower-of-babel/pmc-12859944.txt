Narrow-margin hepatectomy combined with external beam radiotherapy in patients with hepatocellular carcinoma: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Tingli Lu, Yaqiong Wang, Boyu Chen, Weida Meng, Hai Huang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThere is still controversy regarding which adjuvant treatment should be used after hepatectomy to reduce recurrence and prolong survival. This study aims to compare the short-term and long-term outcomes between narrow margin hepatectomy combined with external beam radiotherapy (EBRT) and narrow margin hepatectomy alone in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) patients.Patients and methodsWe conducted comprehensive searches in PubMed, Embase, the Cochrane Library, and Web of Science from database inception to March 2025. Eligible studies were those comparing narrow-margin hepatectomy with EBRT versus narrow-margin hepatectomy alone in HCC patients and reporting recurrence or survival. Meta-analysis was subsequently performed to pool data and assess therapeutic efficacy.ResultsThe analysis included eight studies with 1,057 patients, of whom 461 underwent narrow-margin hepatectomy with EBRT and 596 underwent narrow-margin hepatectomy alone. For disease-free survival (DFS), The pooled HRs (hazard ratios) was 0.6(95% CI: 0.49–0.74). The pooled ORs (odds ratios) for 1-, 3-, and 5-year outcomes were 2.47 (95% confidence interval [CI]: 1.75–3.49), 2.00 (95% CI: 1.52–2.63), and 2.20 (95% CI: 1.63–2.96), respectively. For overall survival (OS), the pooled HRs was 0.68(95% CI: 0.52–0.88). The pooled ORs for 1-, 3-, and 5-year outcomes were 3.86 (95% CI: 1.88–7.96), 1.97 (95% CI: 1.42–2.74), and 1.73 (95% CI: 1.29–2.33), respectively. In terms of recurrence, the OR for intrahepatic recurrence was 0.57 (95% CI: 0.42–0.78) in the EBRT combination group, while the OR for extrahepatic recurrence was 0.60 (95% CI: 0.37–0.96). Subgroup analyses showed that the combined treatment provided a significant advantage in all but two subgroup, which demonstrated a non-significant trend. Sensitivity analyses confirmed that these pooled results were robust. A total of 17 cases (4.42%) of grade 3 adverse events (AEs) were reported, with no grade ≥ 4 AEs observed.ConclusionThis study indicates that narrow-margin hepatectomy combined with adjuvant EBRT can reduce tumor recurrence and prolong survival in HCC patients. However, additional high-quality RCTs evaluating this combined strategy are required for validation.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-025-15504-y.

Background

Primary liver cancer is the sixth most common cancer globally and the third leading cause of cancer-related mortality [1]. Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is the major pathological type of primary liver cancer [2]. Hepatectomy is the major radical treatment for HCC [3, 4]. However, the 5-year postoperative recurrence rate is approximately 70% [5–7], and the long-term survival rate is also not satisfactory [8, 9]. Previous studies have found that a safe surgical margin of ≥ 2 cm should be adopted in hepatectomy to achieve good oncological outcomes [10–13]. However, due to the special location of the tumor (e.g., central HCC, where the tumor is adjacent to the hepatic portal vein and less than 1 cm from major vascular structures [14]) and the impaired hepatic reserve caused by liver cirrhosis, in order to prevent unnecessary resection of healthy liver parenchyma and protect major blood vessels, achieving a safe surgical margin has become a clinical challenge [15]. Doctors often perform narrow-margin hepatectomy to ensure surgical safety [16, 17].

Although the narrow-margin hepatectomy [18] retains normal liver tissue and adjacent major blood vessels as much as possible, and to a certain extent reduces the immediate perioperative risks associated with extensive hepatectomy, the postoperative recurrence rate after narrow-margin hepatectomy is relatively high, and local recurrence in these patients often leads to poor prognosis [19–21]. As multidisciplinary strategies for HCC continue to evolve, researchers are focusing on adjuvant treatments, including Transcatheter Arterial Chemoembolization (TACE), radiofrequency ablation (RFA), external beam radiotherapy (EBRT), etc. to reduce postoperative recurrence rates [22–24]. However, adjuvant treatments combined with hepatectomy have not reached a clear consensus [25, 26]. Therefore, it is crucial to find a reliable adjuvant treatment.

EBRT has demonstrated efficacy in treating various malignant tumors, including lung cancer, rectal cancer, and breast cancer [27–29]. Studies of EBRT in unresectable HCC have shown promising outcomes, demonstrating HCC radiosensitivity with a local control rate of 71%–100% [30–32]. Therefore, in theory, EBRT may reduce local recurrence and improve prognosis by eradicating microscopic residual tumors or occult micrometastases at the surgical margin [33, 34]. However, there is still controversy regarding whether to use EBRT after narrow-margin hepatectomy [35], and to date, no single meta-analysis has specifically evaluated this adjuvant strategy.

Thus, we performed a systematic review and meta-analysis to assess the effectiveness and safety of EBRT combined with narrow-margin hepatectomy for HCC patients.

Materials and methods

Research design and literature retrieval

The implementation of this study adhered to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines [36] and the Assessing the Methodological Quality of Systematic Reviews (AMSTAR) checklist [37] and was registered in the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO) (registration number: CRD420251050411). We systematically searched PubMed, Embase, the Cochrane Library, and Web of Science for eligible English articles published before March 2025. The search keywords included “hepatocellular carcinoma”, “narrow margin resection”, “radiation therapy”, “adjuvant radiotherapy”, etc. The “related articles” function was used to broaden the search scope and the relevance of the citations was taken into account.

Eligibility criteria

Inclusion criteria: (1) Patients who underwent hepatectomy combined with EBRT, regardless of the EBRT method; (2) Patients with histopathologically confirmed HCC; (3) Surgical margin ≤ 1 cm; (4) Studies documenting at least one endpoint among: disease-free survival (DFS), overall survival (OS), recurrence status, or adverse events (AEs).

Exclusion criteria: (1) Duplicate reports from the same experiment; (2) Studies failing to provide extractable outcome data; (3) Case reports, meta-analyses, or commentaries.

Definition

Narrow margin is defined as a surgical margin of ≤ 1 cm, regardless of whether the margin is positive or not. DFS refers to the interval from hepatectomy to recurrence or the final follow-up time. OS refers to the interval between hepatectomy and death or the final follow-up. Severe adverse events (AEs) refer to grade 3 or higher events according to Version 5.0 of the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE).

Study selection and data extraction

Two researchers conducted independent assessments of all titles and abstracts retrieved from the literature search, and then performed independent full-text review to determine the final included studies. The third researcher made the final decision in cases of disagreement. If the data were insufficient, the missing data was sought by contacting the authors. Data extracted from the articles included: the first author’s last name, country or region, study design, publication year, baseline demographics and clinical characteristics (sample size, age, gender, tumor characteristics, etc.), the interventions, radiotherapy-related AEs, DFS, OS, etc. In addition, for studies adjusted by propensity score matching (PSM), we collected the adjusted patient data.

Literature quality assessment

Two reviewers independently conducted bias risk evaluation for included studies and cross-validated the assessments via the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS) [38]. Scores of 8–9 were designated as high quality, 5–7 as moderate quality, and those with scores below 5 as low quality [39].

Statistical analysis

Meta-analyses were conducted with Rev Man 5.4 software. Hazard ratios (HRs) and odds ratios (ORs), with their 95% confidence intervals (CI), were used to compare between-group differences in OS and DFS. HR and their variances were acquired through direct extraction from the original literature or estimation from Kaplan-Meier curves employing the methods of Parmar et al. [40], Williamson et al. [41], and Tierney et al. [42]. Categorical variables were expressed as OR with 95% CI. Continuous variables were expressed as mean difference (MD) with 95% CI. Inter-study heterogeneity was determined based on the I2 value. If there was no heterogeneity between studies (I2 < 50%, p > 0.10), the fixed-effect model was used for meta-analysis. If there was significant inter-study heterogeneity (I2 ≥ 50%, p < 0.10), we further analyzed the source of heterogeneity, and performed subgroup analysis or sensitivity analysis to explore potential causes. If heterogeneity could not be eliminated, a random-effects model was used for meta-analysis. p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Result

Literature screening process

The electronic database search identified 1,672 studies, among which 165 were duplicates. After screening the titles and abstracts of 1,507 unique studies, 1,492 were excluded for being inconsistent with the research focus. The full texts of 15 studies that passed the title and abstract screening were read. Then, seven studies were removed for these reasons: full texts of four studies could not be found, one was a duplicate study, one was non-comparative, and one focused on internal radiotherapy. Finally, eight studies [17, 43–49] were included, including two conference abstracts [45, 46] published in the International Journal of Radiation Oncology-Biology-Physics in 2019 and in Annals of Oncology in 2022, respectively. Basic characteristics included in the study

Among the eight included studies, two were prospective studies [17, 43], and six were retrospective studies [44–49]. All the studies were conducted in China, notably, most were single-center studies. Three studies adopted PSM to control confounding factors [45, 48, 49]. Five studies were of high quality, and three were of moderate quality (Supplementary Table 1). The earliest study analyzed data from 2007 to 2011 [44] and the latest from 2019 to 2022 [46]. One study [48] compared narrow-margin hepatectomy combined with intraoperative EBRT with narrow-margin hepatectomy alone, while the remaining seven studies compared narrow-margin hepatectomy combined with postoperative EBRT with narrow-margin hepatectomy alone.

A total of 1,057 patients met the inclusion criteria, of whom 461 (43.61%) received hepatectomy combined with EBRT, and 596 (56.39%) underwent narrow-margin hepatectomy alone. Details on the effect of different treatment modalities on the prognosis of HCC patients are provided in Table 1. Six studies reported gender distribution [17, 43, 44, 47–49]. The proportions of male patients were 88.34% in the hepatectomy with EBRT group and 84.70% in the hepatectomy alone group, respectively. Meta-analysis of the six studies revealed no statistically significant difference between the two groups (OR = 1.37, 95% CI: 0.87–2.13, p = 0.17). Heterogeneity testing showed no significant heterogeneity (I2 = 0, p = 0.53). Five studies [17, 43, 44, 47, 49] reported patient age characteristics, of which only three studies [43, 47, 49] were included in the analysis, with no statistically significant difference between the two groups (MD = -0.20, 95% CI: -2.40–1.99, p = 0.86). Heterogeneity testing showed no significant heterogeneity (I2 = 0, p = 0.38). Six studies [17, 43, 44, 47–49] reported the tumor diameter, of which three studies [43, 47, 48] were included in the analysis, with no statistically significant difference between the two groups (MD = -0.37, 95% CI: -0.97–0.23, p = 0.23). Heterogeneity testing showed no significant heterogeneity (I2 = 0, p = 0.52). Four studies [17, 43, 44, 49] reported the difference in tumor number (single vs. multiple) in the two groups, with no statistically significant difference between the two groups (OR = 1.04, 95% CI: 0.60–1.83, p = 0.88). Heterogeneity testing showed no significant heterogeneity (I2 = 0, p = 0.56). Four studies [43, 44, 47, 49] reported the liver cirrhosis status, with no statistically significant difference between the two groups (OR = 1.23, 95% CI: 0.78–1.94, p = 0.37). Heterogeneity testing showed no significant heterogeneity (I2 = 0, p = 0.49). Six studies [17, 43, 44, 47–49] reported the hepatitis B virus (HBV) carriage status, with no statistically significant difference between the two groups (OR = 1.17, 95% CI: 0.75–1.84, p = 0.49). Heterogeneity testing showed no significant heterogeneity (I2 = 0, p = 0.99). Three studies [43, 47, 48] reported the difference between alanine aminotransferase (ALT) levels in the two groups, with no statistically significant difference between the two groups (MD = -0.91, 95% CI: -4.24–2.42, p = 0.59). Heterogeneity testing showed no significant heterogeneity (I2 = 0, p = 0.56). Two studies [40, 45] reported albumin (ALB) levels, with no statistically significant difference between the two groups (MD = 0.83, 95% CI: -0.76–2.43, p = 0.30). Heterogeneity testing showed moderate heterogeneity (I2 = 62%, p = 0.11). Supplementary Table 2 lists the detailed information.

Seven studies [17, 43, 44, 46–49], which included 385 patients, reported the radiation-related AEs. None of them reported radiation-related AEs of grade ≥ 4. Four studies [46–49] reported no AEs of grade ≥ 3. One of these studies [48] found no statistically significant difference in AEs between the hepatectomy with EBRT and the hepatectomy alone. Three studies [17, 43, 44] reported a total of 17 cases (4.42%) of grade 3 AEs, with myelosuppression as the most common toxicity.

Primary results

Seven of the included studies [17, 44, 45, 46, 47, 48, 49] reported that hepatectomy combined with EBRT significantly improved DFS. However, one study [43] showed that hepatectomy combined with EBRT did not improve DFS in HCC patients. The meta-analysis showed no significant heterogeneity (I2 = 0, p = 0.91) and a pooled HR for DFS of 0.60 (95% CI: 0.49–0.74, p < 0.00001, Fig. 2A). Additionally, the pooled ORs for DFS at 1, 3, and 5 years were 2.47 (95% CI: 1.75–3.49, p < 0.00001, Supplementary Fig. 1A), 2.00 (95% CI: 1.52–2.63, p < 0.00001, Supplementary Fig. 1B), and 2.20 (95% CI: 1.63–2.96, p < 0.00001, Supplementary Fig. 1C), respectively.

Except for one study that did not discuss OS [46], the remaining articles discussed OS. Four of these studies [17, 44, 48, 49] indicated statistically significant benefits, while one study [47] reported marginal significance (p = 0.053), and two studies [43, 45] showed no statistically significant differences between the hepatectomy combined with EBRT and hepatectomy alone. The meta-analysis showed no significant heterogeneity (I2 = 0, p = 0.84) and a pooled HR for OS of 0.68 (95% CI: 0.52–0.88, p < 0.004, Fig. 2B). Additionally, the pooled ORs for OS at 1, 3, and 5 years were 3.86 (95% CI: 1.88–7.96, p < 0.0002, Supplementary Fig. 2A), 1.97 (95% CI: 1.42–2.74, p < 0.0001, Supplementary Fig. 2B), and 1.73 (95% CI: 1.29–2.33, p < 0.0003, Supplementary Fig. 2C), respectively.

Secondary results

Six studies reported intrahepatic recurrence [17, 43, 44, 47–49]. When performing meta-analyses on intrahepatic recurrence and extrahepatic recurrence, no significant heterogeneity was observed among studies (I² = 0, p = 0.63, and I² = 0, p = 0.96, respectively). Pooled ORs were 0.57 (95% CI: 0.42–0.78, p < 0.0004, Fig. 3A) for intrahepatic recurrence and 0.60 (95% CI: 0.37–0.96, p = 0.03, Fig. 3B) for extrahepatic recurrence.

Subgroup and sensitivity analysis

Subgroup analyses were performed based on EBRT, biological effective dose 10 (BED10, stratified as ≤ 53 Gy vs. >53 Gy), and study quality (Supplementary Table 3). Among the EBRT technique subgroups, three studies [17, 44, 46] focused on narrow-margin hepatectomy combined with IMRT, while other EBRT techniques were not included in the analysis due to an insufficient number of studies. For the BED10 subgroups, there were 5 studies in the > 53 Gy group [17, 43, 44, 47, 49], only 1 study in the ≤ 53 Gy group [48], and BED10 could not be calculated for 2 studies [45, 46]. The combined treatment significantly improved DFS across all subgroups and OS in most subgroups. The OS benefit was not statistically significant only in the IMRT (HR = 0.66, 95% CI: 0.33–1.3; p = 0.23) and medium-quality subgroups (HR = 0.85, 95% CI: 0.46–1.56; p = 0.60). Furthermore, the leave-one-out sensitivity analysis showed that the pooled effect estimates did not change substantially upon the omission of any individual study (Supplementary Table 4).

Discussion

Recurrence prevention persists as an unresolved clinical dilemma in HCC therapy, with no consensus yet on adjuvant therapy for HCC [50, 51]. Narrow-margin hepatectomy is strongly associated with elevated recurrence incidence and unfavorable survival outcomes in HCC [12, 19]. With advancements in surgical techniques, perioperative management, and adjuvant therapies, the indications for hepatectomy have been gradually expanded, thereby bringing oncological benefits [52, 53]. However, this has also increased the risk of narrow surgical margins and recurrence [54, 55].

As early as 2014, Yu et al. initially reported the feasibility of narrow-margin hepatectomy combined with adjuvant 3D-CRT for central HCC [43]. The study revealed no statistically significant differences in DFS and OS between patients who underwent EBRT and those who did not receive it. Shi et al. reported that SBRT after marginal hepatectomy only improved the DFS of HCC patients, but no statistically significant difference in OS was found compared with patients who did not receive EBRT [47]. In contrast, a prospective phase II study by Long et al. showed that for HCC patients near major blood vessels, EBRT after narrow-margin hepatectomy was associated with significantly improved DFS and OS compared with no EBRT [17]. In conclusion, there is no unified consensus on the efficacy of the combined treatment of EBRT and narrow-margin hepatectomy for HCC. A total of eight studies were included in the present study, and the meta-analysis showed that adjuvant EBRT significantly improved DFS and OS. Therefore, EBRT may be applied to HCC patients after narrow-margin hepatectomy to improve prognosis.

This meta-analysis assessed the safety and effectiveness of narrow-margin hepatectomy with adjuvant EBRT. The results showed that hepatectomy combined with EBRT reduced recurrence rates and improved survival in patients, conferring survival benefits on HCC patients after narrow-margin hepatectomy, without increasing grade 4 or higher radiotherapy-related AEs. These outcomes are consistent with subgroup findings from previous meta-analyses [46]. For the inconsistency between our results and those of Yu et al. [43] (who observed no significant differences in DFS and OS), possible explanations include the limited cohort size of the study, which lacked adequate statistical power, and the relatively outdated postoperative EBRT technology used in early stages, leading to residual micrometastatic foci and influencing long-term survival. Both Shi C et al. and Wang W et al. reported no statistically significant differences in OS between the two treatments [45, 47]. Speculations include small sample sizes in both studies, and unequal group allocation in the former study, leading to unbalanced baseline characteristics and poor control of confounding factors. For the latter study, a possible reason is the inclusion of HCC patients with positive microvascular invasion (MVI) or even positive resection margins, who may have harbored distant micrometastases preoperatively. Since these foci are unaffected by EBRT, this failure to target micrometastases in turn impacts the OS. However, according to the meta-analysis, EBRT may be regarded as an intervention for HCC patients after narrow-margin hepatectomy [56–58].

This study indicated that adjuvant EBRT remarkably reduced the rate of intrahepatic and extrahepatic tumor recurrence, which may be attributed to EBRT’s eradication of residual lesions at the surgical margin and reduction of intra-tumoral marginal recurrence, thereby reducing distant metastatic tumors. This finding corresponds to the study of Lee et a [59]. This finding indicates that EBRT can effectively control local and distant tumor recurrence, enhancing the treatment radicality. Notably, the timing of post-hepatectomy recurrence is associated with distinct influencing factors. Early recurrence (within 2 years) is primarily associated with occult intrahepatic metastasis of the primary tumor, such as MVI, insufficient surgical margin, and large tumor volume [60, 61]. In contrast, late recurrence (after 2 years) is more frequently associated with liver disease-related factors, such as cirrhosis severity and viral load [62, 63]. EBRT eliminates residual micrometastatic foci and MVI—both key drivers of recurrence—through multiple mechanisms. On one hand, ionizing radiation directly induces DNA damage in tumor cells to inhibit their proliferation and induce apoptosis, thereby reducing the risk of local recurrence [64]. On the other hand, it modifies the vascular structure and function in the tumor microenvironment, thereby restricting tumor growth [65]. Additionally, EBRT activates the immune system, enhancing tumor cells’ recognition and elimination while reducing the risk of intrahepatic and extrahepatic recurrence [66]. Due to the limited number of eligible studies, we failed to investigate the effects of hepatectomy combined with EBRT on early and late recurrence of HCC. Further in-depth research is needed to optimize radiotherapy protocols, explore the design of more precise target volumes, and develop combined treatment strategies, thereby comprehensively reducing recurrence risk and improving patients’ survival.

Radiation-induced liver disease (RILD) is a life-threatening AE during EBRT. Therefore, EBRT was once considered relatively contraindicated for liver tumors [67]. However, with the rapid development of EBRT (such as intensity-modulated radiotherapy [IMRT], 3D-CRT, and SBRT), modern EBRT is now used to treat HCC across all stages. In this study, the incidence of radiotherapy-related grade 3 adverse reactions was only 4.42%. Of these, myelosuppression was the most common adverse reaction, and no more severe adverse reactions occurred. It is worth noting that although the existing data indicate that EBRT is a feasible and safe treatment method for patients with HCC, the included studies did not mention long-term hepatic function and late complications after radiotherapy, including RILD. Therefore, more prospective studies with long-term follow-up are warranted to comprehensively evaluate the safety profile of EBRT, particularly its impact on long-term liver function.

To assess the robustness of our primary conclusions, we conducted subgroup analyses focusing on EBRT technique, radiobiological dose, and study quality. The survival benefit was strongly and consistently demonstrated across the majority of subgroups, reinforcing the main findings. Notably, the absence of a statistically significant OS benefit in the IMRT subgroup is likely due to limited statistical power, rather than to a true negation of the treatment effect. Furthermore, analysis by study quality provided deeper insight into the strength of the evidence. While the pooled results from moderate-quality studies alone were insufficient to confirm statistical significance, the high-quality subgroup yielded robust and consistent evidence of a benefit. Importantly, the inclusion of moderate-quality studies in the overall analysis did not alter the primary conclusion. In fact, it reinforces the robustness of the findings derived from the high-quality evidence base. Therefore, despite variations in study methodology and quality, the collective evidence consistently supports the survival benefit of the combined therapy. Moreover, we observed concordance between the pooled effect estimates derived from meta-analyses using HRs and those using ORs. This finding confirms the robustness of our primary conclusions. These findings suggest that, despite technical and study quality variations, the trend toward benefit with hepatectomy combined with EBRT over narrow-margin hepatectomy alone remains stable. Furthermore, sensitivity analysis also confirmed the robustness of our conclusions.

Some consensus can be reached regarding the EBRT protocols in HCC patients after narrow-margin hepatectomy. First, the optimal interval for postoperative EBRT is likely 4–6 weeks postoperatively. Second, image-guided EBRT [68], such as 3D-CRT, IMRT, or SBRT, is strongly recommended, although IORT may be considered as a substitute method. Finally, the CTV should encompass the tumor bed marked by surgical clips; however, controversy exists regarding the appropriate margin expansion. Equally important, the cautious delineation of OARs, such as the spinal cord, colon, duodenum, normal liver, stomach, and kidneys, is critical to minimize radiation-related injuries during radiotherapy planning.

This study also has some limitations, and thus the findings should be interpreted carefully. First, all participants included in this study were Chinese patients with HCC, in whom Hepatitis B virus (HBV) infection was the primary etiology. In contrast, HCC in Western populations is mainly caused by Hepatitis C virus (HCV), non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH) and alcohol-related liver disease. This difference in etiologies renders the applicability of this study’s results to Western populations uncertain [69]. Second, most of the included studies were single-center retrospective cohort studies, while the retrospective design makes it difficult to effectively control confounding factors and single-center studies have limited sample representativeness. These factors have to some extent restricted the generalizability of the study results and the overall level of evidence. Finally, due to the limited number of included studies, subgroup analysis was only conducted for IMRT and study quality. Consequently, no subgroup analysis was performed for other key factors, including the timing of EBRT (intraoperative vs. postoperative), other EBRT types (3D-CRT, SBRT) and Margin width (< 1 mm, <5 mm, ≤1 cm). These limitations restricted our ability to fully explore the optimal EBRT regimen for HCC patients who underwent narrow-margin hepatectomy. Despite these limitations, this study provides valuable evidence to support adjuvant EBRT for HCC patients undergoing narrow-margin hepatectomy and lays the foundation for conducting high-quality RCTs.

Conclusion

This study indicates that narrow-margin hepatectomy combined with adjuvant EBRT can reduce tumor recurrence and prolong survival in HCC patients. However, additional high-quality RCTs evaluating this combined strategy are required for validation.

Methodological considerations on immunoadsorption versus sham treatment for post-COVID syndrome–authors’ reply
Authors: Daniel Kraus, Marco Stortz, Irene Schmidtmann, Julia Weinmann-Menke
Published: 2026

Drs. Mansori and Soltani-Kermanshahi raise some important points regarding the methodological validity of the IAMPOCO study.1 We appreciate their interest and scrutiny and would like to take the opportunity to address their comments.

We performed additional tests to assess the normality of distribution of the ordinal outcomes. The Montral Cognitive Assessment (MoCA) and the Chalder score were normally distributed (Shapiro–Wilk tests for normality: p = 0.31 and p = 0.764, respectively; Fig. 1A and D). However, the MFI-20, Bell, and Grip scores did not exhibit a normal distribution (Shapiro–Wilk tests for normality: p < 0.001, p < 0.001, and p = 0.046, respectively; Fig. 1B, C and E). We therefore re-tested the outcomes using non-parametric tests. The Wilcoxon-Mann-Whitney tests for MFI-20, Bell, and Grip resulted in p values of 0.621, 0.2, and 0.2, respectively, confirming the results reported in the published article.

Histograms and QQ normal probability plots for residuals (model checks) for the (A) MoCA, (B) MFI-20, (C) Bell, (D) Chalder, and (E) grip strength outcomes.

A wash-out period between the two treatment cycles in a cross-over study is indeed very important to prevent carry-over of effects. As stated in the published article, “[b]etween the two treatment cycles, there was a washout phase of eight weeks.” When the study was conceived, 8 weeks were deemed sufficient to prevent significant carry-over that could dilute the results. Prompted by Drs. Mansori and Soltani-Kermanshahi, we have performed additional statistical analyses to test this assumption. Under the standard additive first-order carry-over model, any difference in the subject-specific sums of the responses across treatment sequences reflects a difference in residual carry-over effects between treatments. For the normally distributed MoCA and Chalder scores, the corresponding t-tests resulted in p = 0.891 and p = 0.0523, respectively. For the Post-Covid Functional Scale (PCFS), MFI-20, Bell, and Grip scores, Wilcoxon-Mann-Whitney tests produced p = 0.461, p = 0.178, p = 0.219, and p = 0.407, respectively. We conclude that the washout period in the crossover IAMPOCO study did indeed effectively prevent carry-over.

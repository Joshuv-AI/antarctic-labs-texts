Norms of Chebyshev and Faber polynomials on curves with corners and cusps
Erwin Miña-Díaz and Olof Rubin and Aron Wennman

Abstract
. We prove that the n n th Chebyshev polynomial T n T_{n} of a piecewise Dini-smooth Jordan curve Γ \Gamma satisfies lim n → ∞ ‖ T n ‖ Γ cap ​ ( Γ ) n = 1 , \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\|T_{n}\|_{\Gamma}}{\mathrm{cap}(\Gamma)^{n}}=1, where ∥ ⋅ ∥ Γ \|\cdot\|_{\Gamma} is the supremum norm over Γ \Gamma and cap ⁡ ( Γ ) \mathrm{cap}(\Gamma) its logarithmic capacity. This extends earlier results for smooth curves to curves with corner singularities, including cusps. The proof makes use of weighted Faber polynomials, which we analyze using a Fourier analytic representation of the standard Faber polynomials due to Pommerenke. We moreover obtain new asymptotic bounds for the norm of Faber polynomials which are sharp if, for instance, all corners have exterior angle greater than π \pi .

1.
Introduction

Given a compact set
K\subset\mathbb{C}
we let
\|\cdot\|_{K}

denote the corresponding supremum norm. If
K
contains infinitely many points, there exists, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
a unique monic polynomial of degree
n
minimizing
\|\cdot\|_{K}

called the
n
th
Chebyshev polynomial
of
K
,
and we denote it by
T_{n}
. The literature on this topic is extensive and we refer the reader to

[
1
,
2
,
8
,
22
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
15
,
27
,
28
,
29
,
30
]

and the references therein for a background. A central question is to understand
the asymptotic behavior of
\|T_{n}\|_{K}
as
n
tends to infinity and it turns
out that this limiting behavior is intimately connected to the logarithmic
capacity of the set
K
, denoted by
\operatorname{cap}(K)
. Szegő’s inequality states that if
K
has positive logarithmic capacity, then

(1.1)

\mathcal{W}_{n}(K)\coloneqq\frac{\|T_{n}\|_{K}}{\operatorname{cap}(K)^{n}}\geq 1,

where
\mathcal{W}_{n}
is the so-called
Widom factor
. A complementary result,
established in various forms by Faber
[
8
]
,
Fekete
[
9
]
, and Szegő
[
25
]
, asserts that

(1.2)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{W}_{n}(K)^{1/n}=1.

Without prior restrictions, the Widom factors can exhibit any kind of
sub-exponential growth
[
12
]
; however,
for sets
K
subject to geometric constraints,
it is often possible to place upper bounds on the Widom factors,
and in some cases it is even possible to determine the precise asymptotic
behavior of
\mathcal{W}_{n}(K)
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

The Faber polynomials, introduced in
[
7
]
, have served as good trial polynomials to asymptotically saturate Szegő’s
inequality for sets with smooth boundaries. Throughout this text
\Gamma
denotes a Jordan curve
and
\Omega
is the unbounded component of
\overline{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Gamma
.
There is a canonical conformal map

\phi\colon\Omega\to\{w\in\overline{\mathbb{C}}:|w|>1\}

such that
\phi(\infty)=\infty
and

\phi(z)/z\rightarrow\operatorname{cap}(\Gamma)^{-1}

as
z\rightarrow\infty
.
The
n
th Faber polynomial, denoted
F_{n}
, is defined
to be the polynomial part of the Laurent expansion of
\phi(z)^{n}
at

\infty
, and so it has leading term
\operatorname{cap}(\Gamma)^{-n}z^{n}
.
In view of the extremal property of
T_{n}
we thus have

(1.3)

\mathcal{W}_{n}(\Gamma)\leq\|F_{n}\|_{\Gamma}.

Faber proved in
[
8
]
that if
\Gamma
is an analytic Jordan curve,
then the Faber polynomials are asymptotically minimal in the
sense that
\|F_{n}\|_{\Gamma}\rightarrow 1
as
n\rightarrow\infty
, and therefore

(1.4)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{W}_{n}(\Gamma)=1.

Hence, if
\Gamma
is an analytic Jordan curve, then the Faber polynomials
are close to being norm-minimal, and the Chebyshev polynomials asymptotically
saturate Szegő’s lower bound. Faber’s result was subsequently
extended by Suetin to
C^{1,\alpha}
-smooth curves
[
23
,
24
]
.

The question whether (
1.4
) still holds for curves with corners has remained open. It appears that the main challenge is to find suitable trial polynomials when the Faber polynomials fail to be asymptotically minimal. Indeed, if
\Gamma
is assumed to be piecewise Dini-smooth with largest exterior angle
\pi\lambda
, a combination of
[
19
]
*Theorem 1.1 and
[
20
]
*Theorem 2.1 shows that

(1.5)

\lambda\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|F_{n}\|_{\Gamma}\leq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|F_{n}\|_{\Gamma}\leq 2.

Thus, at least when
\lambda>1
, we know that
\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|F_{n}\|_{\Gamma}>1
.

The second author and L. Hübner
[
13
]
recently carried out numerical experiments which
suggest that the limit (
1.4
) remains true in the presence of finitely many corners, including the case of cusps. Our main goal is to prove that this is indeed the case.

Theorem 1
.

If
\Gamma
is a piecewise Dini-smooth Jordan curve, then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{W}_{n}(\Gamma)=1.

In
[
27
]
*Theorem 2.1, Totik and Varga consider
Chebyshev polynomials for a class of sets which includes piecewise smooth Jordan curves
as a special case. However, their results focus on bounds for the Widom factors
and do not reveal the asymptotic behavior of
\mathcal{W}_{n}(\Gamma)
as

n\rightarrow\infty
, even when specialized to our setting.

We are also interested in obtaining a better understanding of the norm of
Faber polynomials. Our next result provides a sharpening of the upper bound in (
1.5
). We clarify that our usage of the word “corner”, say of exterior angle
\lambda\pi
, includes cusps (
\lambda=0,2
), as well as
“flat” corners (
\lambda=1
).

Theorem 2
.

Let
\Gamma
be a piecewise Dini-smooth curve with
l\geq 1

corners
z_{1},\ldots,z_{l}
and associated exterior angles
\lambda_{1}\pi,\dotsc,\lambda_{l}\pi
. With the quantities

\Lambda_{k}\coloneqq\max\{\lambda_{k},2-\lambda_{k}\},\qquad k=1,2,\ldots,l,

we have

(1.6)

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|F_{n}\|_{\Gamma}\leq\max_{1\leq k\leq l}\Lambda_{k}.

Additionally,

(1.7)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\phi(z)^{-n}F_{n}(z)=\begin{cases}1,&\ z\in\Gamma\setminus\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{l}\},\\
\lambda_{k},&\ z=z_{k},\ k=1,\ldots,l,\end{cases}

the limit in (
1.7
) holding uniformly on compact subsets of
\Gamma\setminus\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{l}\}
.

If
\Gamma
bounds a convex domain, then it follows from
[
16
]
*Satz 3 that

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|F_{n}\|_{\Gamma}=\max_{1\leq k\leq l}\lambda_{k}.

However, in the non-convex setting the only asymptotic upper bound we are aware of
is given by (
1.5
). Under the stronger Alper condition imposed on
\Gamma
, it is shown in
[
19
]
that (
1.7
) holds in the pointwise sense. For piecewise analytic
\Gamma
, more precise asymptotics for
F_{n}
away from the corners were obtained in
[
14
]
.

Note that (
1.7
) implies that there is equality in (
1.6
) whenever there is
j\in\{1,\ldots,l\}
such that

(1.8)

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq k\leq l}\Lambda_{k}=\lambda_{j}.

When this happens, we have
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|F_{n}\|_{\Gamma}>1
. The exact value of this
\limsup
when (
1.8
) does not hold is unknown to us.
In Figure
1
we illustrate the behavior of a Faber polynomial
associated with the circular lune
C_{1/2}
, which has exterior angle
\pi/2
.
In this case one has
\Lambda_{1}=\Lambda_{2}=3/2
and
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=1/2
.
On the basis of this and similar plots for varying
n
, we are led to believe that

1<\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|F_{n}\|_{C_{1/2}}<3/2.

Figure 1
.
On the left – the circular lune
C_{1/2}=\{\psi(w):|w|=1\}
where
\psi(w)=\frac{w+\sqrt{w^{2}-1}}{2}
. On the right – the plot of the absolute value of the corresponding Faber polynomial
t\mapsto|F_{401}(\psi(e^{2\pi it}))|
.

Remark 1
.

If
\Gamma
is a Dini-smooth Jordan curve, a simplified version of the proof of Theorem
2
can be used to show that
the uniform behavior in (
1.7
)
extends to the entire curve
\Gamma
. Alternatively, this can also be deduced directly as a corollary of Theorem
2
since “flat” corners are technically allowed. It follows that if
\Gamma
is a Dini-smooth Jordan curve, then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|F_{n}\|_{\Gamma}=1.

This extends
[
23
]
*Theorem 2 and its refined version

[
24
]
*Theorem 2, Section IV for curves of class
C^{1+\alpha}
,
showing that the Faber polynomials are asymptotically minimal on Dini-smooth Jordan curves as well.

The asymptotic behavior in (
1.7
) suggests how the Faber
polynomials can be modified to produce trial polynomials better suited for proving
Theorem
1
.
While the Faber polynomials have uniformly small norms on

\Gamma\setminus\{z_{1},\dots,z_{l}\}
, they may exhibit larger absolute values near the corners.
This is visualized in Figure
2
where we compare the Chebyshev and Faber polynomials for the
deltoid curve
\Gamma\coloneqq\{w+w^{2}/2:|w|=1\}
.

(a)

t\mapsto|T_{30}(\gamma(t))|

(b)

t\mapsto|F_{30}(\gamma(t))|

Figure 2
.
Absolute values of the Chebyshev and Faber polynomials
on the part of the deltoid between two cusps, parametrized by arc-length.
The Chebyshev polynomials are computed using Tang’s algorithm

[
26
,
13
]
.

To construct polynomials with smaller norm, we look for a way to suppress the values of
|F_{n}(z)|
nearby the
corner points—where
|F_{n}(z)|
asymptotically deviates from 1—while allowing them to increase slightly in the mid-arc regions, where
|F_{n}(z)|\to 1
uniformly. This will be carried out by using weighted Faber polynomials
Q_{n,m}
, defined as
the polynomial part of expressions of the form
G_{m}(z)\phi(z)^{n}
, where
G_{m}

is analytic in
\Omega
and
G_{m}(\infty)=1
. The polynomial
Q_{n,m}
is
of degree
n
and leading coefficient
\operatorname{cap}(\Gamma)^{-n}
, and therefore

(1.9)

\mathcal{W}_{n}(\Gamma)\leq\|Q_{n,m}\|_{\Gamma}.

We will see that it is possible to pick the weight functions
G_{m}
so as to have

(1.10)

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|Q_{n,m}\|_{\Gamma}=1+o(m),\qquad m\to\infty.

For instance, we could choose

G_{m}(z)=\prod_{k=1}^{l}\left(1-\frac{\phi(z_{k})}{\phi(z)}\right)^{1/m},

or more conveniently, as we do, a good approximation to this
G_{m}
by a polynomial in
1/\phi(z)
.

2.
Constructing asymptotically minimal weighted Faber polynomials

In this section we construct a sequence of weighted Faber polynomials
satisfying (
1.10
), thus proving Theorem
1
.
We first recall the appropriate regularity notions for the curve
\Gamma
, and then
state a useful Fourier analytic representation of the Faber polynomials initially
proven by Pommerenke
[
16
,
17
]
. This representation was
further utilized by Gaier
[
11
]
and Pritsker
[
20
]
,
and we will use several ideas from these works. In order to make them applicable to our setting,
we need to take extra care to ensure that certain error terms are uniform, which is only implicit in the
above-mentioned works.

An arc with parametrization
\gamma(s)
,
s\in[0,1]
is said to be Dini-smooth provided that
\gamma^{\prime}(s)
is continuous, never vanishes, and its modulus of continuity

\omega(t):=\max_{|s_{1}-s_{2}|\leq t}|\gamma^{\prime}(s_{1})-\gamma^{\prime}(s_{2})|,\qquad t\geq 0

satisfies the integrability condition

(2.1)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\omega(t)}{t}dt<\infty.

Note that (
2.1
) is weaker than assuming that

\gamma^{\prime}(s)
satisfies a Hölder condition

|\gamma^{\prime}(s_{1})-\gamma^{\prime}(s_{2})|\leq C|s_{1}-s_{2}|^{\beta},\qquad 0<\beta\leq 1.

A
piecewise
Dini-smooth Jordan curve is a Jordan curve which can be decomposed into
a union of finitely many Dini-smooth arcs.

Figure 3
.
A piecewise smooth Jordan curve
\Gamma
with six corners, one of them an outer cusp, and one an inner cusp.

Let
\Gamma
denote a Jordan curve and let
\Omega
denote the
component of
\overline{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Gamma
containing
the point at infinity. The canonical exterior conformal map
\phi:\Omega\rightarrow\{w\in\overline{\mathbb{C}}:|w|>1\}
has an inverse, denoted by
\psi(w)
, that extends to a homeomorphism of the exterior of the open unit disk onto
\overline{\Omega}
. The
n
th Faber polynomial
F_{n}
is the polynomial part of the Laurent expansion of
\phi(z)^{n}
at

\infty
, and can be represented in integral form by

(2.2)

\displaystyle F_{n}(z)={}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma_{r}}\frac{\phi(\zeta)^{n}}{\zeta-z}d\zeta=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|w|=r}\frac{w^{n}\psi^{\prime}(w)}{\psi(w)-z}dw.

This representation is valid for
r>1
and
z
interior to the level curve
\Gamma_{r}:=\{z\in\Omega:|\phi(z)|=r\}
.
The second integral in (
2.2
) gives rise to the generating series for the Faber polynomials

(2.3)

\displaystyle\frac{w\psi^{\prime}(w)}{\psi(w)-z}=1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}F_{k}(z)w^{-k},\qquad|w|>r>1,

valid for
z
inside
\Gamma_{r}
, see e.g.
[
24
]
*Chapter II.

We will primarily make use of another integral representation for the Faber polynomials
due to Pommerenke (Theorem
3
below).
Following
[
16
,
17
]
,
we fix
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
, and define

f_{\theta}(w):=\log\left(\frac{\psi(w)-\psi(e^{i\theta})}{aw}\right),\qquad|w|>1.

This
f_{\theta}
is analytic in
|w|>1
, and for
|w|>r>1
we have the equality

(2.4)

\displaystyle f_{\theta}(w)=f_{\theta}(\infty)+\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\alpha}^{\alpha+2\pi}\frac{ire^{it}w^{-1}}{1-re^{it}w^{-1}}\arg\left(\frac{\psi(re^{it})-\psi(e^{i\theta})}{are^{it}}\right)dt\,,

where
\alpha
is any real number such that
\alpha<\theta<\alpha+2\pi
,
see
[
17
]
*Lemma 1.

Let
\tau(w)
be a conformal map of the open unit disk onto the interior domain
D
of the Jordan curve
\Gamma
, extended as a homeomorphism of the closed unit disk onto
\overline{D}
. Let
\ell
be the straight line segment
between
\tau^{-1}(\psi(e^{i\theta}))
and
\tau^{-1}(\psi(e^{i\alpha}))
and consider the curve

C_{\alpha}:=\{\tau(w):w\in\ell\}\cup\{\psi(\rho e^{i\alpha}):\rho\geq 1\}.

The open set
U_{\alpha}:=\mathbb{C}\setminus C_{\alpha}
is simply-connected
and so there exists a branch of the argument
\arg(z-\psi(e^{i\theta}))
(as a function of
z
)
on
U_{\alpha}
. We take this branch of the argument for
z
on every level curve

\{\psi(re^{it}):\alpha\leq t<\alpha+2\pi\}
,
r>1
, and on

\Gamma\setminus\{\psi(e^{i\theta}),\psi(e^{i\alpha})\}
. At every point where any of these
curves meet
C_{\alpha}
there is a
2\pi
-jump in the value of the argument.

Accordingly, we can write (
2.4
) as

(2.5)

\displaystyle f_{\theta}(w)={}

\displaystyle f_{\theta}(\infty)+\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\alpha}^{\alpha+2\pi}\frac{ire^{it}w^{-1}}{1-re^{it}w^{-1}}\left(\arg\left(\psi(re^{it})-\psi(e^{i\theta})\right)-t\right)dt,

whenever
|w|>r>1
.

The curve
\Gamma
is said to be of
bounded secant variation
(
\Gamma\in BSV
)
provided that for every
\theta
, the function
t\mapsto\arg\left(\psi(e^{it})-\psi(e^{i\theta})\right)

is of bounded variation on
[\alpha,\theta)\cup(\theta,\alpha+2\pi]
. We write

v_{\theta}(t):=\arg\left(\psi(e^{it})-\psi(e^{i\theta})\right),\qquad t\in[\alpha,\theta)\cup(\theta,\alpha+2\pi].

If
\Gamma\in BSV
, then
\Gamma
has one-sided tangents at each
\psi(e^{i\theta})
.
This implies that the function
z\mapsto\arg(z-\psi(e^{i\theta}))
remains
bounded on
\Omega\setminus C_{\alpha}
. We take the limit as
r\to 1+

in (
2.5
) followed by integration by parts
(see e.g.
[
10
]
*Theorem 3.36) to get

(2.6)

\displaystyle f_{\theta}(w)={}

\displaystyle f_{\theta}(\infty)+\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\alpha}^{\alpha+2\pi}\log(1-e^{it}w^{-1})dv_{\theta}(t),\qquad|w|>1,

where we also used the fact that

\displaystyle\int_{\alpha}^{\alpha+2\pi}\log(1-e^{it}w^{-1})dt=0,\qquad|w|>1.

Differentiating (
2.6
) with respect to
w
we find

\displaystyle\frac{w\psi^{\prime}(w)}{\psi(w)-\psi(e^{i\theta})}=1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{w^{-n}}{\pi}\int_{\alpha}^{\alpha+2\pi}e^{int}dv_{\theta}(t),\qquad|w|>1,

which in view of (
2.3
) yields the following.

Theorem 3

(Pommerenke
[
16
,
17
]
)
.

If
\Gamma\in BSV
, then

(2.7)

\displaystyle F_{n}(\psi(e^{i\theta}))=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\alpha}^{\alpha+2\pi}e^{int}dv_{\theta}(t),\qquad n\geq 1.

Observe that
v_{\theta}(t)
is continuous on
[\alpha,\theta)\cup(\theta,\alpha+2\pi]
and that

v_{\theta}(\theta+)-v_{\theta}(\theta-)=\lambda(\theta)\pi,

where
\lambda(\theta)\pi
is the exterior angle formed by the two
one-sided tangents to
\Gamma
at
\psi(e^{i\theta})
,
see e.g.
[
18
]
*Section 3.5. We set
v_{\theta}(\theta):=v_{\theta}(\theta+)
, so that

(2.8)

dv_{\theta}=d\tilde{v}_{\theta}+\lambda(\theta)\pi\delta_{\theta},

where
\delta_{\theta}
is the unit point mass at the point
\theta
and
d\tilde{v}_{\theta}
is the restriction of
dv_{\theta}
to
[\alpha,\theta)\cup(\theta,\alpha+2\pi]
. If
\Gamma

is piecewise smooth, then
d\tilde{v}_{\theta}
is absolutely continuous and is explicitly given by

d\tilde{v}_{\theta}(t)=\operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{e^{it}\psi^{\prime}(e^{it})}{\psi(e^{it})-\psi(e^{i\theta})}\right)dt.

We label the corners
of
\Gamma
as
z_{1},\dotsc,z_{l}
, with corresponding points

\theta_{k}\in[0,2\pi)
such that
\psi(e^{i\theta_{k}})=z_{k}
, and define

(2.9)

\Theta_{\Gamma}=\{\theta_{k}+2\pi j\,:\,k=1,\dotsc,l,\quad j\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

Gaier
[
11
]
*Theorem 4 proved that every piecewise Dini-smooth
Jordan curve is of bounded secant variation. Building on the results of

[
11
]
, Pritsker bases his proof of the upper bound of
(
1.5
) on the integral representation
(
2.7
) plus a few other facts that are
derived in the course of the proof of
[
20
]
*Theorem 2.1.
These facts are collected in the following lemma.

Lemma 1
.

Suppose
\Gamma
is a piecewise Dini-smooth Jordan curve.

(i)

For every
\delta>0
sufficiently small,

(2.10)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\theta\in[0,2\pi)}\left|\int_{\theta+\delta}^{\theta-\delta+2\pi}e^{int}dv_{\theta}(t)\right|=0.

(ii)

For all
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that

(2.11)

\displaystyle(\theta-\delta,\theta+\delta)\cap\Theta_{\Gamma}=\emptyset\implies\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\theta-\delta}^{\theta+\delta}|dv_{\theta}(t)|\leq 1+\epsilon,

(2.12)

\displaystyle(\theta-\delta,\theta+\delta)\cap\Theta_{\Gamma}\not=\emptyset\implies\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\theta-\delta}^{\theta+\delta}|dv_{\theta}(t)|\leq 2+\epsilon.

In (
2.12
),
\delta
is assumed small enough
that for every
\theta
the set
(\theta-\delta,\theta+\delta)\cap\Theta_{\Gamma}

contains at most one point. For the proof of the assertion (i) of the lemma, we refer to Pritsker
[
20
]
*p. 170-171. The implications (
2.11
) and (
2.12
) are obtained during the proof of our Theorem
2
: (
2.11
) follows from (
3.14
) and (
3.17
), while (
2.12
) is actually improved by the sharper bound (
3.23
).

We now proceed with the proof of our main theorem.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

We will construct weighted Faber polynomails
Q_{n,m}(z)
,
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
, which for
r>1
and
z
interior to the level curve
\Gamma_{r}=\{z\in\Omega:|\phi(z)|=r\}
can be expressed in integral form by (cf. (
2.2
))

(2.13)

\displaystyle Q_{n,m}(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma_{r}}\frac{P_{m}(\phi(\zeta))\phi(\zeta)^{n}}{\zeta-z}d\zeta=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|w|=r}\frac{P_{m}(w)w^{n}\psi^{\prime}(w)}{\psi(w)-z}dw,

with
P_{m}(w)
an analytic function in
|w|>1
such that
P_{m}(\infty)=1
. It is easy to see that this
Q_{n,m}
is indeed the polynomial part of the Laurent expansion of
P_{m}(\phi(z))\phi(z)^{n}
at
\infty
. Thus,
Q_{n,m}
is of degree
n
and leading coefficient
\operatorname{cap}(\Gamma)^{-n}
, and consequently, we have the inequality

(2.14)

\mathcal{W}_{n}(\Gamma)\leq\|Q_{n,m}\|_{\Gamma},\qquad n\geq 1.

We will show that it is possible to choose
P_{m}(w)
in such a way that (
1.10
) holds.

Fix a positive integer
m
and consider, for
0<r_{m}<1
, the function

g_{m}(w)\coloneqq\prod_{k=1}^{l}\left(1-\frac{r_{m}w_{k}}{w}\right)^{1/m},\qquad|w|>r_{m},

with the branches specified by
g_{m}(\infty)=1
. By choosing
r_{m}

sufficiently close to
1
we can ensure the existence of
\delta_{m}>0

such that

(2.15)

|g_{m}(e^{i\theta})|<1/2,\qquad\theta\in(\theta_{k}-\delta_{m},\theta_{k}+\delta_{m}),\quad k=1,\ldots,l.

We take
\delta_{m}
small enough that each segment
(\theta-\delta_{m},\theta+\delta_{m})

contains at most one point of
\Theta_{\Gamma}
.
From the definition of
g_{m}
we readily see that

(2.16)

\max_{|w|=1}|g_{m}(w)|\leq 2^{l/m}.

Moreover,
g_{m}
has a Laurent expansion on
|w|>r_{m}
of the form

g_{m}(w)=1+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}a_{j}w^{-j},

which converges uniformly on
|w|\geq 1
.
Thus we can find a sufficiently large integer
d_{m}
such that

(2.17)

\max_{|w|=1}\left|g_{m}(w)-\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^{d_{m}}a_{j}w^{-j}\right)\right|<1/m.

If we let

P_{m}(w)\coloneqq 1+\sum_{j=1}^{d_{m}}a_{j}w^{-j},

then it follows from (
2.16
) and (
2.17
) that

(2.18)

\max_{|w|=1}|P_{m}(w)|<m^{-1}+2^{l/m}.

Additionally, from (
2.15
) we get

(2.19)

|P_{m}(e^{i\theta})|<1/2+1/m,\qquad\theta\in(\theta_{k}-\delta_{m},\theta_{k}+\delta_{m}),\quad k=1,\ldots,l.

Let
Q_{n,m}
be the polynomial defined by (
2.13
). By the linearity of the integral, we obtain from (
2.13
) and (
2.2
) that

(2.20)

\displaystyle\begin{split}Q_{n,m}(z)&=F_{n}(z)+\sum_{j=1}^{d_{m}}a_{j}F_{n-j}(z).\end{split}

Using this together with Pommerenke’s formula (
2.7
) we obtain

(2.21)

Q_{n,m}(\psi(e^{i\theta}))=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{int}P_{m}(e^{it})dv_{\theta}(t).

Let
\epsilon>0
be given. By the second part of Lemma
1

we can choose a
\delta\in(0,\delta_{m}/2)
so that

(2.22)

\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\theta-\delta}^{\theta+\delta}|dv_{\theta}(t)|\leq\begin{cases}1+\epsilon,&(\theta-\delta,\theta+\delta)\cap\Theta_{\Gamma}=\emptyset\\
2+\epsilon,&(\theta-\delta,\theta+\delta)\cap\Theta_{\Gamma}\neq\emptyset.\end{cases}

We split the integral in
(
2.21
) into two parts

(2.23)

Q_{n,m}(\psi(e^{i\theta}))=\frac{1}{\pi}\left(\int_{\theta-\delta}^{\theta+\delta}+\int_{\theta+\delta}^{\theta+2\pi-\delta}\right)e^{int}P_{m}(e^{it})dv_{\theta}(t).

From (
2.18
), (
2.19
) and (
2.22
) we gather that

\left|\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\theta-\delta}^{\theta+\delta}e^{int}P_{m}(e^{it})dv_{\theta}(t)\right|\leq\begin{cases}\left(m^{-1}+2^{l/m}\right)(1+\epsilon),&(\theta-\delta,\theta+\delta)\cap\Theta_{\Gamma}=\emptyset,\\
(2+\epsilon)(1/2+1/m),&(\theta-\delta,\theta+\delta)\cap\Theta_{\Gamma}\neq\emptyset,\end{cases}

and conclude that

(2.24)

\sup_{\theta\in[0,2\pi)}\left|\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\theta-\delta}^{\theta+\delta}e^{int}P_{m}(e^{it})dv_{\theta}(t)\right|\leq(1+\epsilon)\left(2^{l/m}+2/m\right).

On the other hand, the first part of Lemma
1
implies that

(2.25)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sup_{\theta\in[0,2\pi)}\left|\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\theta+\delta}^{\theta-\delta+2\pi}e^{int}P_{m}(e^{it})dv_{\theta}(t)\right|\\
&=\sup_{\theta\in[0,2\pi)}\frac{1}{\pi}\left|\int_{\theta+\delta}^{\theta-\delta+2\pi}\left(e^{int}+\sum_{j=1}^{d_{m}}a_{j}e^{i(n-j)t}\right)dv_{\theta}(t)\right|\rightarrow 0\end{split}

as
n\to\infty
. By combining (
2.24
)
and (
2.25
) with (
2.23
) we obtain

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\max_{z\in\Gamma}|Q_{n,m}(z)|=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\max_{\theta\in[0,2\pi)}|Q_{n,m}(\psi(e^{i\theta}))|\leq(1+\epsilon)(2^{l/m}+2/m).

Since
\epsilon>0
was arbitrary we can let it tend to
0

and then (
2.14
) gives us that

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{W}_{n}(\Gamma)\leq 2^{l/m}+2/m.

By letting
m\to\infty
, and in view of Szegő’s lower bound (
1.1
), we obtain

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{W}_{n}(\Gamma)=1,

which concludes the proof.
∎

3.
Angular variation for Dini-smooth arcs

We now study the local properties of the angular variation on a Dini-smooth arc. Our aim is to establish a refinement of
(
2.12
) necessary for the proof of our Theorem
2
.

Any arc-length parametrization
z(s)
,
s\in[a,b]
of a Dini-smooth arc (i.e., one satisfying
|z^{\prime}(s)|=1
) is also Dini-smooth, and so is its extension to
(-\infty,\infty)
given by

(3.1)

\displaystyle z(s):=\begin{cases}z(a)+(s-a)z^{\prime}(a),&\ s<a,\\
z(b)+(s-b)z^{\prime}(b),&\ s>b.\end{cases}

Note that this extension still satisfies the condition that
|z^{\prime}(s)|=1

and that the moduli of continuity of
z^{\prime}(s)
on the intervals
(-\infty,\infty)
and
[a,b]
are one and the same.

The following result is due to Gaier
[
11
]
*Theorem 5.

Lemma 2
.

Let
z(s):[a,b]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be an arc-length parametrization of a
Dini-smooth arc. There exists
\delta>0
such that for every interval
I\subset[a,b]
of length
|I|<\delta
and every point
s_{0}\in I
, we have

(3.2)

\int_{I\setminus\{s_{0}\}}\left|d_{s}\arg\left(z(s)-z(s_{0})\right)\right|\leq 8\int_{0}^{\delta}\frac{\omega(t)}{t}dt,

where
\omega(t)
is the modulus of continuity of
z^{\prime}
.

The bounding constant
8
can be replaced by any value larger than
4
,
however, for our purpose
8
will suffice.

Proof.

We extend the parametrization
z(s)
via (
3.1
) and prove the equivalent statement that there exists a
\delta>0
such that for every
s_{0}\in[a,b]
, the inequality (
3.2
) holds for
I=(s_{0}-\delta,s_{0}+\delta)
.

Because
z^{\prime}
is continuous, we can use the mean value theorem to prove the existence of a
\delta>0
such that

(3.3)

\left|\frac{z(s)-z(s_{0})}{s-s_{0}}-z^{\prime}(s_{0})\right|<\frac{1}{4}\quad\text{and}\quad\langle z^{\prime}(s),z^{\prime}(s_{0})\rangle>0

if
|s-s_{0}|<\delta
and
s_{0}\in[a,b]
. Here
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle

denotes the usual scalar product. Let us focus our attention on a single (but arbitrary) pair
(s_{0},z(s_{0}))
.

By applying a translation and a rotation, we may assume that
z(s_{0})=0

and that
z^{\prime}(s_{0})>0
. In addition, by performing the change of variables
s\mapsto s+s_{0}
, we may also assume that
s_{0}=0
. After these transformations, the modulus of continuity of
z^{\prime}
and its unit modularity
|z^{\prime}|=1
remain unchanged.

Let us write
z(s)=x(s)+iy(s)
, so that
x^{\prime}(0)=1
and
y^{\prime}(0)=0
. From (
3.3
) we gather that if
0<s<\delta
, then

(3.4)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{x(s)}{s}-1\right|<\frac{1}{4}

and
x^{\prime}(s)>0
, which has the effect that
\operatorname{Re}z(s)>0
for
0<s<\delta
. We can
therefore define
\theta(s):=\arg z(s)=\operatorname{Im}(\log z(s))
,
0<s<\delta
, so that

|\theta^{\prime}(s)|=\left|\operatorname{Im}\frac{z^{\prime}(s)}{z(s)}\right|=\frac{\left|y^{\prime}(s)x(s)-y(s)x^{\prime}(s)\right|}{|z(s)|^{2}}.

Making use of the inequalities

(3.5)

\displaystyle|x^{\prime}(s)|\leq|z^{\prime}(s)|=1,\qquad|y(s)|\leq\int_{0}^{s}|y^{\prime}(t)|dt\leq\int_{0}^{s}\omega(t)dt\leq s\omega(s),

and (
3.4
), we find

\displaystyle|\theta^{\prime}(s)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left|y^{\prime}(s)x(s)-y(s)x^{\prime}(s)\right|}{x(s)^{2}}\leq\frac{\omega(s)}{x(s)}+\frac{s\omega(s)}{x(s)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{(1-1/4)s}+\frac{1}{(1-1/4)^{2}s}\right)\omega(s)\leq 4\,\frac{\omega(s)}{s}.

Therefore,

\int_{(0,\delta)}\left|d_{s}\arg z(s)\right|=\int_{0}^{\delta}\left|\theta^{\prime}(s)\right|ds\leq 4\int_{0}^{\delta}\frac{\omega(s)}{s}ds.

By reversing the direction of the arc and considering
(-\delta,0)
instead, the same argument yields

\int_{(-\delta,0)}\left|d_{s}\arg z(s)\right|\leq 4\int_{0}^{\delta}\frac{\omega(s)}{s}ds,

and since the bounds are based on (
3.3
), which holds uniformly in
s_{0}
, the result follows.
∎

Lemma 3
.

Let
z(s):[a,b]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be an arc-length parametrization of a
Dini-smooth Jordan arc
\gamma
. For every
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that

(3.6)

\int_{I}\left|d_{s}\arg\bigl(z(s)-\zeta\bigr)\right|\leq\pi+\epsilon

for every
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\gamma
and any interval
I\subset[a,b]
of length
|I|<\delta
.

Proof.

We again follow the ideas of Gaier, in particular the ones employed in
the proof of
[
11
]
*Lemma 1. One can verify that for any two
distinct points
z_{1},z_{2}\in\gamma
, the function
\zeta\mapsto\arg(z_{1}-\zeta)-\arg(z_{2}-\zeta)

(which is well-defined as it is independent of the branch of
z\mapsto\arg(z-\zeta)
on
\gamma
)
is continuous on
\mathbb{C}\setminus\gamma
. By Lemma
2
,
given
\epsilon>0
it is possible to find
\delta>0
such that

\int_{I\setminus\{s^{\prime}\}}\left|d_{s}\arg\bigl(z(s)-z(s^{\prime})\bigr)\right|<\epsilon

for every interval
I=[c,d]\subset[a,b]
of length
|I|=d-c<\delta

and point
s^{\prime}\in I
. Consider a partition
c=s_{0}<s_{1}<\ldots<s_{N}=d
of
I
. Since
z^{\prime}(s)
is continuous, by decreasing
\delta

if necessary we can guarantee that for every
k=1,\ldots,N
and
s\in(s_{k-1},s_{k})
,
the angle
\vartheta^{+}_{k}(s)
at
z(s)
of the triangle
z(s_{k-1}),\,z(s),\,z(s_{k})

and its conjugate
\vartheta^{-}_{k}(s)=2\pi-\vartheta^{+}_{k}(s)
satisfy

\vartheta^{\pm}_{k}(s)<\pi+\epsilon.

Let us introduce the function

h_{N}(\zeta)\coloneqq\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left|\arg\bigl(z(s_{k})-\zeta\bigr)-\arg\bigl(z(s_{k-1})-\zeta\bigr)\right|.

For each
k
, the function
\arg(z(s_{k})-\zeta)-\arg(z(s_{k-1})-\zeta)
is a
branch of
\arg\left(\frac{z(s_{k})-\zeta}{z(s_{k-1})-\zeta}\right)
,
thus it is harmonic on
\overline{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\gamma
.
It follows that
h_{N}
is subharmonic on
\overline{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\gamma
.
Because at each of its points the arc
\gamma
has a tangent line,

h_{N}
is bounded on
\overline{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\gamma
. If
s_{k_{0}-1}<s^{\prime}<s_{k_{0}}
, then as
\zeta\to z(s^{\prime})
,

h_{N}(\zeta)\to\sum_{k\not=k_{0}}|\arg(z_{k}-z(s^{\prime}))-\arg(z_{k-1}-z(s^{\prime}))|+\vartheta^{\pm}_{k}(s^{\prime}),

the sign
+
or
-
chosen according to the side of
\gamma
the point
\zeta
approaches
z(s^{\prime})
from.
It follows that

\limsup_{\zeta\to z(s^{\prime})}h_{N}(\zeta)\leq\int_{I\setminus\{s^{\prime}\}}|d_{s}\arg(z(s)-z(s^{\prime}))|+\pi+\epsilon<\pi+2\epsilon.

By the maximum principle for subharmonic functions (see e.g.
[
21
]
*Theorem 2.4), we obtain

h_{N}(\zeta)\leq\pi+2\epsilon
for all
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\gamma
, which in turn implies that

\int_{I\setminus\{s^{\prime}\}}|d_{s}\arg(z(s)-\zeta)|\leq\pi+2\epsilon.\qed

Lemma 4
.

Assume that
\zeta(t):[0,L_{\zeta}]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
and
z(s):[0,L_{z}]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}

parametrize Dini-smooth Jordan arcs by arc-length that only intersect at
z(0)=\zeta(0)

and that the smallest angle formed by the arcs is
\mu\pi\in[0,\pi]
.
For every
\epsilon>0
, there exists a
\delta>0
such that

(3.7)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\delta}\left|d_{s}\arg\left(z(s)-\zeta(t)\right)\right|\leq\pi(1-\mu)+\epsilon,\quad 0<t<\delta.

Remark 2
.

As the proof of Lemma
4
will show, as far as the arc
\zeta
is concerned, the condition that
\zeta(t)
is Dini-smooth is unnecessary; all we need to assume is that
\zeta^{\prime}(0)\not=0
.

Proof of Lemma
4
.

After a translation and a rotation we may assume that
z(0)=0
and that
z^{\prime}(0)=1
. Moreover, by applying a reflection about the
x
-axis, we may in addition assume that

\zeta(t)=te^{i\mu\pi}+o(t),\quad t\rightarrow 0+.

The case
\mu=0
is already covered by Lemma
3
. We first suppose that
0<\mu<1
. The idea of the proof is best conveyed through the example where the arc
z
is the straight segment
z(s)=s
,
0\leq s\leq s_{0}
. In this case,

(3.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{d}{ds}\arg\left(z(s)-\zeta(t)\right)&=\operatorname{Im}\left(\frac{d}{ds}\log\left(z(s)-\zeta(t)\right)\right)=\operatorname{Im}\frac{z^{\prime}(s)}{z(s)-\zeta(t)}\\
&=\operatorname{Im}\frac{z^{\prime}(s)\overline{\bigl(z(s)-\zeta(t)\bigr)}}{|z(s)-\zeta(t)|^{2}}=\operatorname{Im}\frac{s-te^{-i\mu\pi}+o(t)}{|z(s)-\zeta(t)|^{2}}\\
&=\frac{t\sin(\mu\pi)+o(t)}{|z(s)-\zeta(t)|^{2}}>0,\quad 0<t<\delta,\end{split}

for some
\delta>0
small. As a consequence, for every
\epsilon>0
there is a corresponding
\delta
such that if
0<t<\delta
, then

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\delta}\left|d_{s}\arg\left(z(s)-\zeta(t)\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\delta}\frac{d}{ds}\arg\left(z(s)-\zeta(t)\right)ds

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\arg\left(z(\delta)-\zeta(t)\right)}_{<0}-\underbrace{\arg\left(-\zeta(t)\right)}_{>\mu\pi-\pi-\epsilon}<\pi(1-\mu)+\epsilon.

In the general case we cannot hope to prove that the derivative in (
3.8
) is positive for
0<s,t<\delta
, where
\delta
is fixed and independent of
t
.
However, we can obtain a local variant of monotonicity which is dependent of the point
t
. Since

\left|z(s)-s\right|\leq\int_{0}^{s}|z^{\prime}(t)-1|dt\leq s\omega(s)

we can write

z(s)=s+E_{1}(s),\quad z^{\prime}(s)=1+E_{2}(s),

where

|E_{1}(s)|\leq s\omega(s),\quad|E_{2}(s)|\leq\omega(s)

(the second inequality follows directly from the definition of
\omega
since
z^{\prime}(0)=1
).
Because
\omega(s)\rightarrow 0
as
s\rightarrow 0
and
z^{\prime}(0)=x^{\prime}(0)=1
, there is no restriction
in assuming that
\omega(s)<1
and
x^{\prime}(s)>0
. We can further assume that
\omega(s)>0
for
s>0
since otherwise
z^{\prime}

is constant in some neighborhood of
0
and we are back in the example discussed at the beginning of the proof.

Arguing as in (
3.8
), we find

(3.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{d}{ds}\arg\left(z(s)-\zeta(t)\right)&=\operatorname{Im}\frac{z^{\prime}(s)\overline{\bigl(z(s)-\zeta(t)\bigr)}}{|z(s)-\zeta(t)|^{2}}\\
&=\operatorname{Im}\frac{\left(1+E_{2}(s)\right)\left(s+\overline{E_{1}(s)}-te^{-i\mu\pi}+o(t)\right)}{|z(s)-\zeta(t)|^{2}}\\
&\geq\frac{t\sin(\mu\pi)+o(t)-\omega(s)(3s+t)}{|z(s)-\zeta(t)|^{2}}.\end{split}

Observe that
s\mapsto s\sqrt{\omega(s)}
is strictly increasing from
0
.
This implies that, for
t>0
small enough, there is a unique value
\delta_{t}
such that

\delta_{t}\sqrt{\omega(\delta_{t})}=t.

Since
\omega(s)\rightarrow 0
as
s\rightarrow 0+
, we have

\frac{t}{\delta_{t}}=\sqrt{\omega(\delta_{t})}\rightarrow 0,\quad\text{as}\quad t\rightarrow 0+.

Using the inequality (
3.9
) we find that if
0<s<\delta_{t}
and
0<t<\delta
with
\delta>0
small enough, then

(3.10)

\frac{d}{ds}\arg\left(z(s)-\zeta(t)\right)\geq\frac{t\sin\mu+o(t)-t\omega(\delta_{t})-3t\sqrt{\omega(\delta_{t})}}{|z(s)-\zeta(t)|^{2}}>0.

But then (
3.10
) yields

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\delta}\left|d_{s}\arg\left(z(s)-\zeta(t)\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{0}^{\delta_{t}}+\int_{\delta_{t}}^{\delta}\right)\left|\frac{d}{ds}\arg\left(z(s)-\zeta(t)\right)\right|ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\delta_{t}}\frac{d}{ds}\arg\left(z(s)-\zeta(t)\right)ds+\int_{\delta_{t}}^{\delta}\left|\frac{d}{ds}\arg\left(z(s)-\zeta(t)\right)\right|ds

\displaystyle=\arg\left(z(\delta_{t})-\zeta(t)\right)-\arg\left(-\zeta(t)\right)

\displaystyle+\int_{\delta_{t}}^{\delta}\left|\frac{d}{ds}\arg\left(z(s)-\zeta(t)\right)\right|ds.

Since

\displaystyle z(\delta_{t})-\zeta(t)={}

\displaystyle\delta_{t}+E_{1}(\delta_{t})-t(e^{i\mu}+o(1))=\delta_{t}\left(1+O(\omega(\delta_{t}))+o(t)\right)

as
t\to 0+
, we have that

\arg\left(z(\delta_{t})-\zeta(t)\right)\rightarrow 0,\quad\text{as}\quad t\rightarrow 0+.

Since moreover
\arg(-\zeta(t))\rightarrow\mu\pi-\pi
as
t\rightarrow 0+
, we see that for a given
\epsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that if
0<t<\delta
, then

(3.11)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\delta}\left|d_{s}\arg\left(z(s)-\zeta(t)\right)\right|\leq\pi(1-\mu)+\epsilon/3+\int_{\delta_{t}}^{\delta}\left|\frac{d}{ds}\arg\left(z(s)-\zeta(t)\right)\right|ds.

What is left is to estimate the last integral. Writing

z(s)=x(s)+iy(s),\quad\zeta(t)=u(t)+iv(t),

we obtain, using the triangle inequality, that

(3.12)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left|\frac{d}{ds}\arg\left(z(s)-\zeta(t)\right)\right|&=\frac{\left|-x^{\prime}(s)y(s)+x^{\prime}(s)v(t)+y^{\prime}(s)\left(x(s)-u(t)\right)\right|}{|z(s)-\zeta(t)|^{2}}\\
&\leq\frac{|y^{\prime}(s)|}{|z(s)-\zeta(t)|}+\frac{|y(s)|}{|z(s)-\zeta(t)|^{2}}+\frac{|x^{\prime}(s)v(t)|}{(x(s)-u(t))^{2}},\end{split}

where we have used that
|x^{\prime}(s)|\leq|z^{\prime}(s)|=1
.
Note that

\frac{|v(t)|}{x(\delta_{t})-u(t)}=\frac{t\sin(\mu\pi)+o(t)}{\delta_{t}-t\cos(\mu\pi)+o(\delta_{t})}\rightarrow 0+,\qquad t\to 0+,

and that if
0<t<s
, then

\displaystyle|z(s)-\zeta(t)|

\displaystyle=|s-te^{i\mu\pi}+o(s)|=|se^{-i\mu\pi}-t+o(s)|

\displaystyle\geq\left|\operatorname{Im}\left(se^{-i\mu\pi}-t+o(s)\right)\right|=s\sin(\mu\pi)+o(s).

Thus by further restricting the size of
\delta
we can be sure that

0<\frac{|v(t)|}{x(\delta_{t})-u(t)}<\frac{\epsilon}{3},\qquad 0<t<\delta,

|z(s)-\zeta(t)|\geq\frac{s\sin(\mu\pi)}{2},\qquad 0<t<s<\delta,

and that

\frac{6}{\sin^{2}(\mu\pi)}\int_{0}^{\delta}\frac{\omega(s)}{s}ds<\frac{\epsilon}{3}.

Therefore, by (
3.12
), (
3.5
) and the fact that
t<\delta_{t}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\delta_{t}}^{\delta}\left|\frac{d}{ds}\arg\left(z(s)-\zeta(t)\right)\right|ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\delta_{t}}^{\delta}\frac{2\omega(s)}{s\sin(\mu\pi)}ds+\int_{\delta_{t}}^{\delta}\frac{4s\omega(s)}{s^{2}\sin^{2}(\mu\pi)}ds

\displaystyle+|v(t)|\int_{\delta_{t}}^{\delta}\frac{x^{\prime}(s)}{(x(s)-u(t))^{2}}ds.

The sum of the first two integrals is less than
\epsilon/3
. The final integral can be computed explicitly, giving us

\displaystyle|v(t)|\int_{\delta_{t}}^{\delta}\frac{x^{\prime}(s)}{(x(s)-u(t))^{2}}ds

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{-|v(t)|}{x(s)-u(t)}\right|_{s=\delta_{t}}^{\delta}=\frac{x(\delta)-x(\delta_{t})}{x(\delta)-u(t)}\frac{|v(t)|}{x(\delta_{t})-u(t)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|v(t)|}{x(\delta_{t})-u(t)}<\epsilon/3.

After putting these estimates together with (
3.11
) we obtain (
3.7
).

Suppose now that
\mu=1
. Then
\zeta(t)=u(t)+iv(t)=-t+o(t)
as
t\to 0+
and

\displaystyle|z(s)-\zeta(t)|={}

\displaystyle(s+t)(1+o(1)),\qquad s+t\to 0+.

It follows that for a given
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that

u(t)<0,\qquad\frac{|v(t)|}{-u(t)}<\frac{\epsilon}{2},\qquad 0<t<\delta,

|z(s)-\zeta(t)|>\frac{s+t}{2},\qquad 0<s,t<\delta,

and

6\int_{0}^{\delta}\frac{\omega(s)}{s}ds<\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

Using (
3.12
), we see that for all
0<s,t<\delta
,

\displaystyle\left|\frac{d}{ds}\arg\left(z(s)-\zeta(t)\right)\right|\leq\frac{6\omega(s)}{s}+\frac{|v(t)|x^{\prime}(s)}{(x(s)-u(t))^{2}},

which after integration yields

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\delta}\left|\frac{d}{ds}\arg\left(z(s)-\zeta(t)\right)\right|ds

\displaystyle\leq 6\int_{0}^{\delta}\frac{\omega(s)}{s}ds+\frac{|v(t)|}{-u(t)}\frac{x(\delta)}{x(\delta)-u(t)}<\epsilon.\qed

We now turn to proving Theorem
2
. We again let

z_{1},\ldots,z_{l}

denote the corners of the piecewise Dini-smooth curve
\Gamma

with
\theta_{k}\in[0,2\pi)
,
1\leq k\leq l
, such that

z_{k}=\psi(e^{i\theta_{k}}),\qquad 1\leq k\leq l,

and let
\Theta_{\Gamma}
be as in (
2.9
).

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Let
\lambda(\theta)
be the exterior angle of
\Gamma
at
\psi(e^{i\theta})
, so that

\lambda(\theta)=\begin{cases}1,&\theta\not\in\Theta_{\Gamma},\\
\lambda_{k},&\theta=\theta_{k},\ 1\leq k\leq l.\end{cases}

Observe that

(3.13)

\displaystyle\lambda(\theta)\leq\max_{1\leq k\leq l}\Lambda_{k},

and that in view of (
2.8
), for all
\theta
and
0<\delta<\pi
, we have

(3.14)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\theta-\delta}^{\theta+\delta}|dv_{\theta}(t)|\leq\lambda(\theta)+\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{(\theta-\delta,\theta+\delta)\setminus\{\theta\}}|dv_{\theta}(t)|.

By Theorem
3
(with
\alpha=\theta-\delta
and
0<\delta<\pi
) and (
2.8
),
we have

(3.15)

\displaystyle\left|F_{n}(\psi(e^{i\theta}))-\lambda(\theta)e^{in\theta}\right|\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{(\theta-\delta,\theta+\delta)\setminus\{\theta\}}|dv_{\theta}(t)|+\frac{1}{\pi}\left|\int_{\theta+\delta}^{\theta-\delta+2\pi}e^{int}\,dv_{\theta}(t)\right|.

By Lemma
2
, for every
\epsilon>0
there exists
\delta_{\epsilon}>0
such that for every interval
(a,b)
of length
b-a\leq 2\delta_{\epsilon}
and
\theta\in(a,b)
, we have

(3.16)

\left((a,b)\setminus\{\theta\}\right)\cap\Theta_{\Gamma}=\emptyset\implies\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{(a,b)\setminus\{\theta\}}|dv_{\theta}(t)|\leq\epsilon.

Since for every
\theta
it is possible to find
\delta\in(0,\delta_{\epsilon})
such that

\left((\theta-\delta,\theta+\delta)\setminus\{\theta\}\right)\cap\Theta_{\Gamma}=\emptyset,

it follows from (
3.16
) that

(3.17)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{(\theta-\delta,\theta+\delta)\setminus\{\theta\}}|dv_{\theta}(t)|\leq\epsilon.

This inequality together with (
3.15
)
and (
2.10
) lead to conclude that

(3.18)

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left|F_{n}(\psi(e^{i\theta}))-\lambda(\theta)e^{in\theta}\right|\leq\epsilon,

proving (
1.7
) in a pointwise sense.
Moreover, because for any compact set
K\subset\Gamma\setminus\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{l}\}

it is possible to find
\delta>0
such that
(\theta-\delta,\theta+\delta)\cap\Theta_{\Gamma}=\emptyset
whenever
\psi(e^{i\theta})\in K
, it follows that (
3.14
) holds uniformly for all
\theta
with
\psi(e^{i\theta})\in K
.

The remaining task is to prove (
1.6
).
We pick
\delta\in(0,\delta_{\epsilon})
so small that the distance between any two distinct elements of
\Theta_{\Gamma}

is at least
2\delta
. This has the effect that any interval
(\theta-\delta,\theta+\delta)

contains at most one point of
\Theta_{\Gamma}
.
In view of (
3.16
), (
3.14
) and (
3.13
) we have

(3.19)

\displaystyle\left((\theta-\delta,\theta+\delta)\setminus\{\theta\}\right)\cap\Theta_{\Gamma}=\emptyset\implies\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\theta-\delta}^{\theta+\delta}|dv_{\theta}(t)|\leq\max_{1\leq k\leq l}\Lambda_{k}+\epsilon.

Let us assume then that

\displaystyle\left((\theta-\delta,\theta+\delta)\setminus\{\theta\}\right)\cap\Theta_{\Gamma}\neq\emptyset,

say
\theta_{k}\in(\theta,\theta+\delta)
for definiteness. Note that in this situation we have
\lambda(\theta)=1
. By Lemma
4
,
given
\epsilon>0
we have (after possibly shrinking
\delta>0
) the upper bound

(3.20)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\theta_{k}}^{\theta+\delta}|dv_{\theta}(t)|&=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\theta_{k}}^{\theta+\delta}\left|d_{t}\arg\left(\psi(e^{it})-\psi(e^{i\theta})\right)\right|\leq 1-\mu_{k}+\epsilon,\end{split}

where
\mu_{k}\pi
is the smallest angle of
\Gamma
at
z_{k}
.
By (
3.16
) we also have

(3.21)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{(\theta-\delta,\theta_{k})\setminus\{\theta\}}|dv_{\theta}(t)|\leq\epsilon.

Adding (
3.20
) and (
3.21
) results in an inequality that when coupled with (
3.14
) yields

(3.22)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\theta-\delta}^{\theta+\delta}|dv_{\theta}(t)|\leq 2-\mu_{k}+2\epsilon\leq\max_{1\leq k\leq l}\Lambda_{k}+2\epsilon

(since
\Lambda_{k}=2-\mu_{k}
).

Summarizing, we have found in (
3.19
) and (
3.22
) that for every value of
\theta
, it is true that

(3.23)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\theta-\delta}^{\theta+\delta}|dv_{\theta}(t)|\leq\max_{1\leq k\leq l}\Lambda_{k}+2\epsilon.

From (
2.7
) we see that

\displaystyle\left|F_{n}(\psi(e^{i\theta}))\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\theta-\delta}^{\theta+\delta}|dv_{\theta}(t)|+\frac{1}{\pi}\left|\int_{\theta+\delta}^{\theta-\delta+2\pi}e^{int}\,dv_{\theta}(t)\right|,

which in view of (
3.23
) yields

\displaystyle\|F_{n}\|_{\Gamma}=\sup_{\theta\in[0,2\pi)}\left|F_{n}(\psi(e^{i\theta}))\right|\leq\max_{1\leq k\leq l}\Lambda_{k}+2\epsilon+\sup_{\theta\in[0,2\pi)}\frac{1}{\pi}\left|\int_{\theta+\delta}^{\theta-\delta+2\pi}e^{int}\,dv_{\theta}(t)\right|.

We now use (
2.10
) to conclude that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|F_{n}\|_{\Gamma}=\max_{1\leq k\leq l}\Lambda_{k}+2\epsilon,

establishing (
1.6
).
∎

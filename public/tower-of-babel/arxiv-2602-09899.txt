remarks on modules of finite projective dimension
Mohsen Asgharzadeh and Elham Mahdavi

Abstract
. We investigate homological and depth-theoretic properties of finitely generated modules of finite projective dimension over Noetherian local rings. A central theme is the study of criteria for freeness and reflexivity derived from the torsion-freeness or reflexivity of tensor products of the form M ⊗ R M M\otimes_{R}M and M ⊗ R M ∗ M\otimes_{R}M^{*} . Under mild homological assumptions, we prove that such properties of these tensor products impose strong structural constraints on M M , often forcing it to be free. These results generalize classical theorems of Auslander beyond the regular case and contribute to the broader understanding of rigidity phenomena in commutative algebra. The second part of the paper is devoted to the dimension and support of Ext-modules, particularly Ext R i ⁡ ( M , R ) \operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(M,R) for critical values of i i , when M M has finite projective dimension. We establish sharp bounds on their Krull dimensions, analyze their behavior for prime and equidimensional modules, and relate these findings to the grade conjecture and other homological conjectures. Applications include new cases of a question of Jorgensen, which asks whether pd ⁡ ( M ) < i \operatorname{pd}(M)<i whenever Ext R i ⁡ ( M , M ) = 0 \operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(M,M)=0 and M M has finite projective dimension over a complete intersection ring. Finally, we examine the projective dimension of prime ideals in rings that fail chain conditions. We show that in non-catenary or pathological saturated chain settings, such prime ideals often have infinite projective dimension.

1.
Introduction

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian local ring and let
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module. Modules of finite projective dimension occupy a central position in commutative algebra, serving as a bridge between homological properties and structural features such as depth, dimension, and freeness. The systematic study of such modules has led to profound connections with the regularity and singularities of the base ring, as exemplified by the Auslander–Buchsbaum–Serre theorem, which characterizes regular local rings as precisely those rings over which every finitely generated module has finite projective dimension.

One of the most influential directions in this area concerns detecting freeness or reflexivity of a module from properties of its tensor powers. In his seminal work, Auslander proved that over a regular local ring, if
M\otimes_{R}M
is torsion-free, then
M
is reflexive; and if
M\otimes_{R}M^{*}
(where
M^{*}=\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,R)
) is reflexive, then
M
is free. These results inspired a series of conjectures attributed to Vasconcelos, predicting that freeness of a module should often be detectable from homological or depth properties of its tensor products. Over the years, these questions have motivated extensive research, particularly in understanding the rigidity imposed by tensor-product behavior.

The first main objective of this paper is to extend such rigidity phenomena beyond the setting of regular rings to arbitrary local rings, under the assumption that
M
has finite projective dimension. We show that, under mild depth and dimension hypotheses, reflexivity or torsion-freeness of the tensor products
M\otimes_{R}M
or
M\otimes_{R}M^{*}
forces strong structural consequences for
M
, often leading to freeness.

Theorem 1.1
.

(1)

Let

with
\dim(R/P)\leq 2

\operatorname{pd}(P)<\infty
and

, then

is free.

(2)

Let

be a module with
\operatorname{pd}(M)<\infty
. If

is reflexive, then

is free.

(3)

Let
M
be a module with
\operatorname{pd}(M)<2
. If

is reflexive, then

is reflexive.

(4)

Suppose

satisfies

. Then

satisfies
(S_{r+1})
.

(5)

Suppose

Concerning the first statement, Vasconcelos

A second fundamental theme of this work is the detailed analysis of the dimension and support of the modules

when

has finite projective dimension. Understanding the size and geometry of these modules is closely tied to the long-standing Grade Conjecture, which predicts that

\operatorname{grade}(M)+\dim(M)=\dim(R).

We prove sharp bounds on the Krull dimension of

, with particular emphasis on extremal homological degrees.

Theorem 1.2
.

(1)

Let

(2)

Let

. Then

(3)

(4)

for every

.

These results unify and extend earlier partial theorems and provide new insight into the homological behavior of modules of finite projective dimension. As applications, we analyze the structure of
\operatorname{Ext}
-modules for prime and equidimensional modules, showing in several cases that

In Section 4 we collect preliminary inequalities relating depth and dimension. In particular, we study the Cohen–Macaulay defect

The main result of that section is the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

(1)

If

Our arguments combine classical homological tools with more recent techniques involving local cohomology and depth formulas.

We further investigate Gorenstein dimension (
\operatorname{Gdim}
), the Gorenstein analogue of projective dimension. Motivated by properties of grade and support, we formulate a conjecture stating that if

\operatorname{grade}(M)=\operatorname{ht}(\operatorname{Ann}(M)).

We verify this conjecture for symbolic and ordinary powers of prime ideals and explore its connections with other homological conjectures. In the same spirit, we settle a question implicit in our previous work
[
2

concerning the integral closure

Section 7 is devoted to another direction pursued here concerns the projective dimension of tensor products

. A question raised in the literature asks whether there exist nonfree torsion-free modules

Finally, we study the interplay between projective dimension and chain conditions in the prime spectrum. We show that in a non-catenary three-dimensional ring, any nonzero prime ideal appearing in a bad saturated chain must have infinite projective dimension.

Theorem 1.6
.

(1)

Let

(2)

2.
Homology of Tensor Products

Throughout this section,
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
denotes a commutative Noetherian local ring, and all modules are finitely generated unless otherwise specified. The notation
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(-)
(resp.
\operatorname{id}_{R}(-)
) denotes projective (resp. injective) dimension, and
\operatorname{Gdim}_{R}(-)

We begin with the following conjecture.

Conjecture 2.1

(Vasconcelos
[
26
]
)
.

There exists an integer
e
such that if
M^{\otimes e}
is torsion-free over an integral domain, then
M
is free.

We will repeatedly use the following well-known fact.

Fact 2.2
.

The ring
R
is an integral domain if it possesses a prime ideal of finite projective dimension.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
P\in\operatorname{Spec}(R)
with
\dim(R/P)\leq 2
. If
\operatorname{pd}(P)<\infty
and
P^{\otimes i}
is torsion-free for some
i\geq 2
, then
P
is free.

Proof.

We may assume
P\neq 0
. By Fact
2.2
,
R
is an integral domain. Since
P
is a submodule of a free module, it is torsion-free. Hence
(P_{R_{P}})^{\otimes i}
is torsion-free. Because
\operatorname{pd}(P)<\infty
, we also have
\operatorname{pd}_{R_{P}}(P_{R_{P}})<\infty
. Thus
R_{P}
is regular. A result of Auslander
[
5
]
on torsion in tensor powers now implies that
P_{R_{P}}
is free over
R_{P}
. Hence
P_{R_{P}}
is principal, so
\operatorname{ht}(P)=\operatorname{ht}(P_{R_{P}})=1
. By
[
7
, Corollary 1.2]
, it follows that
P
is principal, and therefore free.
∎

Corollary 2.4
.

If
\dim R=3
,
P\in\operatorname{Spec}(R)
has finite projective dimension, and
P\otimes_{R}P
is torsion-free, then
P
is free.

Proof.

We may assume
P\neq 0
. Then
\dim(R/P)\leq\dim R-1=2
, so the conclusion follows from the proposition.
∎

We write
(-)^{*}:=\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(-,R)
. The following result is due to Auslander.

Theorem 2.5

(Auslander
[
5
, Proposition 3.2]
)
.

Let
R
be normal. If
M\otimes_{R}M^{*}
is reflexive, then
M
is free.

We denote by
H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(-)
the
i
-th local cohomology module with respect to
\mathfrak{m}
.

Proposition 2.6
.

In Theorem
2.5
, the normality assumption can be replaced by the condition
\operatorname{pd}(M)<\infty
.

Proof.

Let
d=\dim R
. We argue by induction on
d
. If
0pt(R)=0
, then by Auslander–Buchsbaum,
0pt(M)+\operatorname{pd}(M)=0
, so
M
is free. Hence assume
0pt(R)\geq 1
. Since
M
is torsion-free,
0pt(M)\geq 1
. The result is clear if
\dim R=1
, so assume
\dim R\geq 2
and that
R
satisfies
(S_{2})
.

Because
M\otimes_{R}M^{*}
is reflexive,
0pt(M\otimes_{R}M^{*})\geq 2
, and hence
H^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M\otimes_{R}M^{*})=0
. For every
\mathfrak{p}\neq\mathfrak{m}
,

(M\otimes_{R}M^{*})_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong M_{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}\operatorname{Hom}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}},R_{\mathfrak{p}})

is reflexive. By the induction hypothesis,
M_{\mathfrak{p}}
is free. Thus

is locally free on the punctured spectrum. Now apply
[
1
, Lemma 8.1]
: if
L
is locally free,
0pt(R)\geq 2
, and
H^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(L\otimes L^{*})=0
, then

is free. Hence
M
is free.
∎

Theorem 2.7

(Auslander
[
5
, Theorem 3.6(c)]
)
.

Let
R
be regular and
M
a module such that

is torsion-free. Then

is reflexive.

Conjecture 2.8
.

In Theorem
2.7
, the regularity assumption can be replaced by
\operatorname{pd}(M)<\infty
.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
A
be a finitely generated module with
A\cong A^{*}
and
\operatorname{pd}(A)\leq 1
. If
A\otimes_{R}A
is torsion-free, then
A
is free.

Proof.

Let
N:=A\otimes_{R}A
. Consider the natural exact sequence

0\to N\to N^{**}\to L\to 0.

We argue by induction on
d=\dim R
. If
L\neq 0
, then by induction
L
has finite length. From the long exact sequence in local cohomology,

0=H^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}(N)\to H^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}(L)\to H^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(N),

we obtain
0pt(N)=1
.

Since
\operatorname{pd}(A)\leq 1
, we have
\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{R}(A,N)=0
for
i\geq 1
. Auslander’s depth formula gives

0pt(N)=0pt(A\otimes_{R}N)+\operatorname{pd}(A)-q,

where
q=\sup\{i\mid\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{R}(A,N)\neq 0\}
. If
q\geq 1
, then
0pt(N)\leq 0
, a contradiction. Hence
q=0
, and applying the depth formula again yields

1=0pt(N)=0pt(A^{\otimes 3})+1\geq 2,

another contradiction. Thus
L=0
, so
N\cong N^{**}
is reflexive.

Since
A\cong A^{*}
, we have
A\otimes_{R}A^{*}\cong A\otimes_{R}A
reflexive. By Proposition

,

is free.
∎

Fact 2.10
.

A module
L
is reflexive if and only if
0pt(L_{\mathfrak{p}})\geq 2
for all
\mathfrak{p}
with
0pt(R_{\mathfrak{p}})\geq 2
, and
0pt(L_{\mathfrak{p}})\geq 1
whenever
0pt(R_{\mathfrak{p}})=1
.

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
M
be a module with
\operatorname{pd}(M)<2
. If
M\otimes_{R}M
is reflexive, then
M
is reflexive.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}(R)
. If
0pt(R_{\mathfrak{p}})\leq 1
, then
M_{\mathfrak{p}}
is torsion-free, hence

0<0pt(M_{\mathfrak{p}})\leq 0pt(R_{\mathfrak{p}})\leq 1,

so
0pt(M_{\mathfrak{p}})=0pt(R_{\mathfrak{p}})
. Now assume
0pt(R_{\mathfrak{p}})\geq 2
. Replacing
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
by
R
, we may assume
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{m}
. Since
M
is torsionless, there exists an exact sequence

0\to M\to R^{n}\to L\to 0.

Tensoring with
M
gives

0\to\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{R}(M,L)\to M\otimes_{R}M\to M^{\oplus n}\to L\otimes_{R}M\to 0.

Because
M\otimes_{R}M
is torsion-free,
\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{R}(M,L)=0
. If
M
is not free, then
\operatorname{pd}(M)=1
, and by
[
5
]
we obtain
0pt(L)>0
. Hence
0pt(M)\geq 1+0pt(L)\geq 2
. The conclusion now follows from Fact
2.10
.
∎

By
(S_{r})
we mean Serre’s condition.

Proposition 2.12
.

Suppose
R
is regular and
M\otimes_{R}M
satisfies
(S_{r})
. Then
M
satisfies
(S_{r+1})
.

Proof.

Since
(S_{r})
implies torsion-freeness,
M\otimes_{R}M
is torsion-free. By
[
5
]
,
\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{R}(M,M)=0
for all
i\geq 1
. The depth formula yields

20pt(M)=0pt(R)+0pt(M\otimes_{R}M).

If
r=\dim R
, then Auslander–Buchsbaum implies that
M
is free. Otherwise, assuming
0pt(M)\leq r
leads to

20pt(M)=\dim R+r>2r,

a contradiction. Thus
0pt(M)\geq r+1
, and

satisfies
(S_{r+1})
.
∎

Conjecture 2.13
.

If
\operatorname{pd}(M)<\infty
and
M\otimes_{R}M
is reflexive, then
M
is reflexive.

Corollary 2.14
.

If
\operatorname{pd}(M)<\infty
,
0pt(R)=3
, and
M\otimes_{R}M
is reflexive, then
M
is free.

Proof.

Then
M
is reflexive, so
0pt(M)\geq 2
, and Auslander–Buchsbaum gives
\operatorname{pd}(M)\leq 1
. If
M
were not free, tensoring a presentation
0\to M\to F\to C\to 0
with

would yield
\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{R}(M,M)=0
. The depth formula would then give

20pt(M)=0pt(R)+0pt(M\otimes_{R}M)=3+2=5,

a contradiction.
∎

Let
\operatorname{Tr}(-)
denote the Auslander transpose.

Theorem 2.15
.

Suppose
R
satisfies
(S_{2})
,
M
is finitely generated with
\operatorname{pd}(M)<\infty
, and both
M\otimes_{R}M
and
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,M)
are reflexive. Then

is free.

Proof.

We argue by induction on
d=\dim R
. The result is known for
d\leq 2
, so assume
d>2
. By induction,
M
is locally free on the punctured spectrum. If
M
is not free, Auslander–Bridger theory gives an exact sequence

0\to\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(\operatorname{Tr}M,M)\to M\otimes_{R}M^{*}\to\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,M)\to\operatorname{Ext}^{2}_{R}(\operatorname{Tr}M,M)\to 0.

One shows that
\operatorname{Ext}^{2}_{R}(\operatorname{Tr}M,M)
has finite length. Moreover, there are embeddings (see
[
1
]
)

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(\operatorname{Tr}M,M)\hookrightarrow H^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M\otimes_{R}M),\quad\operatorname{Ext}^{2}_{R}(\operatorname{Tr}M,M)\hookrightarrow H^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M\otimes_{R}M).

Since
M\otimes_{R}M
is reflexive, both local cohomology modules vanish. Hence
M\otimes_{R}M^{*}\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,M)
, and therefore

H^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M\otimes_{R}M^{*})=0.

By
[

, this forces

∎

3.
Dimension of Ext Modules

Recall that
\operatorname{grade}(I;M)
is the length of the longest
M
-sequence contained in
I
. Also,

\operatorname{grade}(M):=\operatorname{grade}(\operatorname{Ann}(M),R).

We begin by recalling the following fact.

Fact 3.1
.

(See
[
9
, 3.7]
.)
Let
A
be a local ring and
M
a finitely generated
A
-module of finite projective dimension. Assume that

\operatorname{grade}(M)+\dim(M)=\dim(A).

Let
d:=\dim(A)
and
r:=\dim(M)
. Then

\dim\big(\operatorname{Ext}^{d-r}_{A}(M,A)\big)=r.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
R
be complete, and let
P\neq 0
be a perfect prime ideal of height
t
and finite projective dimension. Then

\dim\!\big(\operatorname{Ext}^{t-1}_{R}(P,R)\big)=\begin{cases}d-t&\text{if }t\geq 2,\\
d&\text{if }t=1.\end{cases}

Proof.

By Fact
2.2
,
R
is a domain. In particular,
R
is catenary and equidimensional. Hence the grade conjecture holds, so Fact
3.1
applies.
Since
P
is perfect,

t=\dim(R)-\dim(R/P)=\operatorname{grade}(R/P)=\operatorname{pd}(R/P),

where the first equality follows from
[
21
, Lemma 2, p. 250]
. Fact
3.1
now yields

\dim\!\big(\operatorname{Ext}^{t}_{R}(R/P,R)\big)=\dim(R/P).

If
t>1
, then by shifting,

\operatorname{Ext}^{t}_{R}(R/P,R)\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{t-1}_{R}(P,R),

and the result follows.

Now suppose
t=1
. From the short exact sequence

0\to P\to R\to R/P\to 0

we obtain

0=\operatorname{Hom}(R/P,R)\longrightarrow R\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(P,R),

so
\dim(\operatorname{Hom}(P,R))\geq\dim(R)
. The reverse inequality always holds, hence

\dim(\operatorname{Hom}(P,R))=\dim(R).

∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a regular local ring of dimension
4
containing
\mathbb{Q}
, and let
M
satisfy
(S_{3})
. Then

\dim\!\big(\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(M,R)\big)\leq 2.

Proof.

Since
M
satisfies
(S_{3})
, it is torsion-free and reflexive. For any
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}(R)
with
\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p})\leq 3
, the Auslander–Buchsbaum formula implies that
M_{\mathfrak{p}}
is free.
By a construction of Miller (see
[
16
, Theorem 2.17]
), there exists a free module
F
and an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow F\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow P\longrightarrow 0\quad(\ast),

where
P
is a prime ideal of height
2
and
R/P
is normal. By Serre’s criterion,
R/P
satisfies
(S_{2})
. Since

\dim(R/P)=\dim(R)-\operatorname{ht}(P)=2,

it follows that
R/P
is Cohen–Macaulay, so
P
is perfect. Thus we are in the situation of Lemma
3.2
.
From the long exact sequence of Ext-modules induced by
(\ast)
, we have

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(P,R)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(M,R)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(F,R)=0,

we see that
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(M,R)
is a quotient of
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(P,R)
. Hence

\dim\!\big(\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(M,R)\big)\leq\dim\!\big(\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(P,R)\big)\leq 2.

∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a regular local ring of dimension

4.

The following is well known.

Fact 4.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Supp}(M)
. Then

\dim(M)\geq\dim(M_{\mathfrak{p}})+\dim(R/\mathfrak{p}).

Proof.

Set
t_{1}=\dim(R/\mathfrak{p})
and
t_{2}=\dim(M_{\mathfrak{p}})
. Choose a chain of primes in
\operatorname{Supp}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})

\mathfrak{q}_{0}\subsetneq\mathfrak{q}_{1}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\mathfrak{q}_{t_{2}}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}.

Also choose a chain in
\operatorname{Supp}(M)
extending beyond

\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{q}_{t_{2}}\subsetneq\mathfrak{q}_{t_{2}+1}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\mathfrak{q}_{t_{2}+t_{1}}=\mathfrak{m}.

Concatenating these chains gives a chain of length

in

Fact 4.2

(ii)

\displaystyle\geq\operatorname{grade}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})

\displaystyle\geq\operatorname{grade}(M)

H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(C)=0
for
i>0
.
From the exact sequence
0\rightarrow R\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(I,I)\rightarrow C\rightarrow 0
,
we deduce that

0=H^{2}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)\cong H^{2}_{\mathfrak{m}}(\operatorname{Hom}(I,I)),

since
0pt(R)=d>2
. In view of
(\ast)
we obtain

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(I,I)=0
.
Since
\operatorname{pd}(I)\leq 1
(see
(+)
above), we conclude that
\operatorname{pd}(I)=0
, because otherwise
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(I,I)\neq 0
.
∎

Recall that a module
M
is called rigid if
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(M,M)=0
. The freeness of a rigid ideal over a 1-dimensional Gorenstein ring is a challenging problem in commutative algebra.

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
R
be a strongly normal ring of dimension at most
3
and
I
a reflexive ideal. Then
I
is rigid.

Proof.

We may assume that
\dim(R)=3
. Strongly normal rings satisfy Serre’s condition
(R_{2})
. By this,
I
is locally free on the punctured spectrum. Also, strongly normal rings satisfy Serre’s condition
(S_{3})
. Since
\dim(R)=3
, this means that
R
is Cohen–Macaulay. Now we follow the previous argument to deduce that
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(I,I)=0
.
∎

Fact 5.4
.

(See Roberts
[
23
, Theorem 2]
).
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{\mathfrak{p}}(k,M)\neq 0
iff
i\in[0pt(M),\operatorname{id}(M)]
.

By
\mu_{j}(\mathfrak{m},M)
we mean
\dim_{k}(\operatorname{Ext}^{j}_{R}(k,M))
, where

is the residue field.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a
d
-dimensional Gorenstein ring with
d>1
, let
\operatorname{pd}(M)<\infty
, and suppose
\operatorname{Ext}^{d-1}(M,M)=0
. If
M
is quasi-Buchsbaum, then
\operatorname{pd}(M)\leq d-2
.

Proof.

Set
\overline{M}:=M/\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)
.
Suppose, for a contradiction, that
p:=\operatorname{pd}(M)\geq d-1
. Since

\operatorname{Ext}^{p}(M,M)\neq 0\Rightarrow\operatorname{pd}(M)\neq d-1,

and so
\operatorname{pd}(M)\geq d
. But, the Auslander–Buchsbaum formula yields
\operatorname{pd}(M)\leq d
. Hence
\operatorname{pd}(M)=d
and so
0pt(M)=0
. In particular,
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\neq 0
.

We may assume
\overline{M}\neq 0
. Indeed, if
\overline{M}=0
, then
M=\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)
. Since
M
is quasi-Buchsbaum, this implies
M=\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)=\bigoplus k
. Consequently,
\operatorname{Ext}^{d-1}(k,k)=0
, because it is a direct summand of
\operatorname{Ext}^{d-1}(M,M)=0
. But this is impossible, since it would imply

\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{d-1}(k,k)^{\vee}=\operatorname{Ext}^{d-1}(k,k^{\vee})=\operatorname{Ext}^{d-1}(k,k),

and hence
\operatorname{pd}(k)\leq d-1
, forcing
R
to be regular of Krull dimension
\leq d-1
, a contradiction.

Thus
\overline{M}\neq 0
. Since
R
is Gorenstein, any module of finite projective dimension has finite injective dimension. Hence we may use Ischebeck’s formula:

\sup\{i\mid\operatorname{Ext}^{i}(\overline{M},M)\neq 0\}=0pt(R)-0pt(\overline{M})\leq d-1,

since
0pt(\overline{M})>0
. Thus
\operatorname{Ext}^{d}(\overline{M},M)=0
.

From
0\rightarrow\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\rightarrow M\rightarrow\overline{M}\rightarrow 0
, we obtain

0=\operatorname{Ext}^{d-1}(M,M)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{d-1}(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(M),M)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{d}(\overline{M},M)=0.

But
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)=\bigoplus_{X}k
, since
M
is quasi-Buchsbaum. As
0pt(M)=0
, we have
X\neq\emptyset
. Hence

\operatorname{Ext}^{d-1}(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(M),M)=0\Rightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{d-1}(R/\mathfrak{m},M)=0.

Recall from
[
11
, 3.1.17]
that
\operatorname{id}(M)=0pt(R)
. Using Fact
5.4
, we get

\mu_{d-1}(\mathfrak{m},M)=0\Rightarrow d-1\notin[0pt(M),\operatorname{id}(M)]=[0,d],

a contradiction. This shows
\operatorname{pd}(M)\leq d-2
.
∎

Fact 5.6
.

Suppose
0pt(R)>0
. Then
0pt(\mathfrak{m})=1
.

Proof.

We have
0\to\mathfrak{m}\to R\to k\to 0
. This yields

0=H^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)\to[k=H^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}(k)]\to H^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathfrak{m})
,
hence
H^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathfrak{m})\neq 0
. Therefore
0pt(\mathfrak{m})=1
.
∎

Lemma 5.7
.

If
I
is a radical ideal and
\operatorname{Ext}^{j}(I,I)=0
with
\operatorname{pd}(I)<\infty
, then

j>\operatorname{ht}(I)-1.

Proof.

We have
I=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{q}\in\min(I)}\mathfrak{q}
. Take
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Assh}(I)
.
Then
\operatorname{ht}(I)=\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p})
.
Recall that

IR_{\mathfrak{p}}=(\bigcap_{\mathfrak{q}\in\min(I)\setminus\{\mathfrak{p}\}}\mathfrak{q}R_{\mathfrak{p}})\cap\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}=R_{\mathfrak{p}}\cap\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}.

Then

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Ext}^{j}(I,I)_{\mathfrak{p}}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Ext}^{j}(IR_{\mathfrak{p}},IR_{\mathfrak{p}})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Ext}^{j}(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}},\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Ext}^{j+1}(R_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}},\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}).

Consequently,
\mu_{j+1}(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}},\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}})=0
.
By Fact
5.4
, this implies that
\operatorname{id}(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}})<\infty
. By Bass’ conjecture,
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
is Cohen–Macaulay. By
[
11
, 3.1.17]
, we have

\operatorname{id}(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}})=0pt(R_{\mathfrak{p}})=\dim(R_{\mathfrak{p}})=\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p})=\operatorname{ht}(I)\quad(+).

Using Roberts’ result again, we obtain

j+1\notin[0pt(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}),\operatorname{id}(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}})]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ref{depth}}}{{=}}[1,\operatorname{id}(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}})]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(+)}}{{=}}[1,\operatorname{ht}(I)].

Thus
j+1>\operatorname{ht}(I)
, i.e.,
j>\operatorname{ht}(I)-1
.
∎

The following completes the proof of Theorem
1.5
:

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
R
be Cohen–Macaulay and
I\subset R
a radical ideal with
\operatorname{pd}(I)<\infty
such that
R/I
is Cohen–Macaulay. If
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}(I,I)=0
, then
\operatorname{pd}(I)<i
.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.7

we know
\operatorname{ht}(I)-1<i
. Now, we combine this with the Auslander–Buchsbaum formula, and deduce

\displaystyle\operatorname{pd}(I)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{pd}(R/I)-1

\displaystyle=[0pt(R)-0pt(R/I)]-1

\displaystyle=[\dim(R)-\dim(R/I)]-1

\displaystyle=\operatorname{ht}(I)-1<i,

as claimed.
∎

6.
Applications to
\operatorname{Gdim}(-)

We will use the following standard facts from
[

]
:

Fact 6.1
.

If
\operatorname{Gdim}_{R}(k)<\infty

Fact 6.2
.

If
\operatorname{Gdim}_{R}(M)<\infty
, then
\operatorname{Gdim}_{R}(M)=0pt(R)-0pt(M)
.

For a finitely generated module
M
over a Noetherian ring

, recall that

\operatorname{grade}(M)=\inf\{0pt(R_{\mathfrak{p}})\mid\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Supp}(M)\}\quad(\ast)

Question 6.3
.

Is
\operatorname{grade}(M)
equal to
\inf\{0pt(R_{\mathfrak{p}})\mid\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Min}(M)\}
?

Proposition 6.4
.

Yes, if
\operatorname{pd}(M)<\infty
.

Proof.

By definition, there exists
Q\in\operatorname{Supp}(M)
such that
\operatorname{grade}(M)=0pt(R_{Q})
.
Choose
P\subseteq Q
in
\operatorname{Supp}(M)
minimal with respect to inclusion. Then

(1)

\operatorname{grade}(M)=0pt(R_{Q})
,

(2)

\dim(M_{P})=0
.

By
(AB)
we mean the Auslander–Buchsbaum formula. Then

\displaystyle\operatorname{grade}(M)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(1)}}{{=}}0pt(R_{Q})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle AB}}{{=}}0pt(M_{Q})+\operatorname{pd}(M_{Q})

\displaystyle\geq\operatorname{pd}_{R_{Q}}(M_{Q})

\displaystyle\geq\operatorname{pd}_{R_{P}}(M_{P})

\displaystyle=0pt(R_{P})-0pt(M_{P})

\displaystyle\geq 0pt(R_{P})-\dim(M_{P})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(2)}}{{=}}0pt(R_{P})

\displaystyle\geq\inf\{0pt(R_{\mathfrak{p}})\mid\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Supp}(M)\}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ast)}}{{=}}\operatorname{grade}(M).

∎

Remark 6.5
.

In fact, if
\operatorname{Gdim}(M)<\infty
, the same conclusion holds. Indeed,

\operatorname{Gdim}(M)=\sup\{i\mid\operatorname{Ext}^{i}(M,R)\neq 0\}

when finite, together with the Auslander–Bridger formula (Fact

).

Proposition 6.6
.

Let
P\in\operatorname{Spec}(R)
be such that

. Then

\operatorname{grade}(R/P)=\operatorname{ht}(P)=\operatorname{ht}(\operatorname{Ann}(R/P)).

Proof.

By Remark

,
\operatorname{grade}(R/P)=0pt(R_{Q})
for some

. Hence

.
Since
\dim(R_{P})=\operatorname{ht}(P)
, it suffices to show

.
Because
\operatorname{Gdim}(R/P)<\infty
, localization yields
\operatorname{Gdim}(R_{P}/PR_{P})<\infty
. By Fact

,

is Gorenstein, hence Cohen–Macaulay. Therefore

\operatorname{grade}(R/P)=0pt(R_{P})=\dim(R_{P})=\operatorname{ht}(P).

∎

Observation 6.13
.

If

and

\inf\{i\mid\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(M,R)\neq 0\}=\sup\{i\mid\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(M,R)\neq 0\}.

Since

, we have

Thus

∎

By
\overline{R}
we mean the integral closure of

in the total fraction ring of

Proposition 6.14
.

If
R
is analytically unramified and
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(\overline{R})<\infty

is normal.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}(R)
. Since being analytically unramified is a local property

is analytically unramified.
Suppose
\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p})=1
. Then
R_{\mathfrak{p}}\to\overline{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}
is integral. Since
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(\overline{R})<\infty
, we get
\operatorname{pd}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\overline{R_{\mathfrak{p}}})<\infty
. As
\dim(R_{\mathfrak{p}})=1
, the Auslander–Buchsbaum formula implies
\overline{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}
is free over

. Hence
\overline{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}
is 1-dimensional and normal.
Let

. The composition

R_{\mathfrak{p}}\to\overline{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}\to(\overline{R_{\mathfrak{p}}})_{Q}

is faithfully flat, and

is regular. By
[

, Theorem 2.2.12]
,

satisfies
(R_{1})
.
Now let
\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p})\geq 2
. Since
\overline{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}
is finite over

, and by
[

, we have

0pt_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\overline{R_{\mathfrak{p}}})=0pt_{\overline{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}}(\overline{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}).

As

is normal, it satisfies

, so its depth, as an

-module, is at least

. The Auslander–Buchsbaum formula gives

0pt(R_{\mathfrak{p}})=\operatorname{pd}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\overline{R_{\mathfrak{p}}})+0pt_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\overline{R_{\mathfrak{p}}})=\operatorname{pd}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\overline{R_{\mathfrak{p}}})+0pt_{\overline{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}}(\overline{R_{\mathfrak{p}}})\geq 2.

Remark 6.16
.

(i)

A recent paper
[
14
]
asked whether every local hypersurface domain
R
satisfies the following property for any positive integer

:

If
M
and
M\otimes_{R}\operatorname{Ext}^{c-1}_{R}(M,R)
are Cohen–Macaulay

-modules, both of grade

, then is

has finite projective dimension?

(ii)

Let

be a local hypersurface domain. If the condition in Remark

(i) holds for

, then
R
is regular. In particular, any singular hypersurface domain fails to satisfy the condition.

Proof.

Take
M:=R/\mathfrak{m}
. Since

is Cohen–Macaulay,

has grade

. Also,

is Cohen–Macaulay because it is Artinian.
Moreover,
\operatorname{Ext}^{c-1}_{R}(M,R)\cong k
by local duality (or via the Koszul complex), so

which is again Cohen–Macaulay of grade

. The stated condition would then force

to have finite projective dimension, which by the Auslander–Buchsbaum–Serre theorem implies that

∎

7.
When is
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)=1
?

The following question was raised in
[
12
, Question 3.3]
.

Question 7.1
.

Do there exist nonfree torsion-free modules
M
and
N
over a local ring
R
with
0pt(R)=2
such that

\operatorname{pd}_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)=1?

7.1.
Working over hypersurfaces

Let
R
be a hypersurface, that is,
R=S/(f)
where
(S,\mathfrak{n})
is regular and
0\neq f\in\mathfrak{n}^{2}
.

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
R
be a hypersurface of dimension
d>2
, and let
M,N
be modules that are locally free on the punctured spectrum. Then

\operatorname{pd}_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)\neq 1.

Proof.

Suppose, for a contradiction, that
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)=1
. By the Auslander–Buchsbaum formula,

0pt_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)=d-1\geq 2.

Thus
M\otimes_{R}N
satisfies Serre’s
(S_{2})
condition, and hence is reflexive by
[
16
, 3.6]
. The second rigidity theorem of Huneke–Wiegand
[
18
, 2.7]
then gives
\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{R}(M,N)=0
for all
i>0
, contradicting
[
12
, 1.5]
.
∎

Recall that
R
has a
simple singularity
if there are only finitely many ideals
I\subseteq S
such that
f\in I^{2}
.

Proposition 7.3
.

Let
R
be a
2
-dimensional hypersurface with a simple singularity, and let
M,N
be nonfree torsion-free modules. Then

\operatorname{pd}_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)\neq 1.

Proof.

Assume
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)=1
. We first show that
M
and
N
are locally free on the punctured spectrum.
Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a height-one prime and set
A=R_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Then

\operatorname{pd}_{A}(M_{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes_{A}N_{\mathfrak{p}})\leq 1.

If this projective dimension is
0
, there is nothing to prove. Otherwise, suppose it equals
1
. Since
A
is a hypersurface of dimension one, every torsion-free
A
-module is totally reflexive by the Auslander–Bridger formula. Hence

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{A}(M_{\mathfrak{p}},\Omega(M_{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes_{A}N_{\mathfrak{p}}))=0.

By
[
12
, 3.4]
, this implies that
M_{\mathfrak{p}}
is free. The same argument shows
N_{\mathfrak{p}}
is free.

Thus
M
and
N
are locally free on the punctured spectrum. By
[
12
, A.12]
, this implies
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(M)
and
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(N)
are finite, contradicting
[
12
, 3.2]
.
∎

Proposition 7.4
.

Suppose the presentation matrix of a nonfree torsionless module
M
is symmetric. Then for every nonzero module
N
,

\operatorname{pd}_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)\neq 1.

Proof.

Let
N\neq 0
. There is a presentation

R^{n}\xrightarrow{A}R^{n}\to M\to 0

with
A^{T}=A
. Applying
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(-,R)
gives

(R^{n})^{*}\xrightarrow{A^{T}}(R^{n})^{*}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Tr}(M)\longrightarrow 0.

Identifying
(R^{n})^{*}\cong R^{n}
, we obtain an isomorphism
M\cong\operatorname{Tr}(M)
by the Five Lemma. Since
M
is torsionless,

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(\operatorname{Tr}(M),R)=0.
If
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)=1
, then

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(M,\Omega(M\otimes_{R}N))=0.

By
[
12
, Lemma 3.4]
, this forces
M
to be free, a contradiction.
∎

Remark 7.5
.

The torsionless assumption is essential. Let
x
be a regular element of a ring
R
with positive depth and set
M=R/xR
. Then

0\to R\xrightarrow{x}R\to M\to 0

is a free resolution with a symmetric presentation matrix. For any nonzero free module
N
, one has
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)=1
.

Let us give an example.

Example 7.6
.

Let
S=k[x,y,z,w]
,
f=x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}+w^{2}
, and set
R=S/(f)
, where
\operatorname{char}(k)\neq 2
.
Define two
2\times 2
matrices

\varphi_{xy}=\begin{pmatrix}x&y\\
-y&x\end{pmatrix},\qquad\varphi_{zw}=\begin{pmatrix}z&w\\
-w&z\end{pmatrix}.

Let

\varphi:=\begin{pmatrix}\varphi_{xy}&0\\
0&\varphi_{zw}\end{pmatrix},\qquad\psi:=\varphi^{\mathrm{T}}.

A direct computation shows:

\varphi_{xy}\varphi_{xy}^{\mathrm{T}}=(x^{2}+y^{2})I_{2},\quad\varphi_{zw}\varphi_{zw}^{\mathrm{T}}=(z^{2}+w^{2})I_{2},

hence

\varphi\psi=(x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}+w^{2})I_{4}=fI_{4},\qquad\psi\varphi=fI_{4}.

Thus
(\varphi,\psi)
is a matrix factorization of
f
.
Over
R
, we obtain a
2
-periodic free resolution of
M:=\operatorname{coker}(\varphi)
:

\cdots\xrightarrow{\psi}R^{4}\xrightarrow{\varphi}R^{4}\xrightarrow{\psi}R^{4}\xrightarrow{\varphi}M\longrightarrow 0.

Now, define the block-diagonal symmetric matrix
B:=\operatorname{diag}(B_{2},B_{2})
, where

B_{2}:=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}.

A straightforward calculation shows that
\varphi^{\mathrm{T}}B=B\varphi
.
Since
B
is invertible and symmetric, it induces an isomorphism

M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}M^{*}:=\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,R).

Thus
M
is self-dual.
If
k
contains an element
\imath
such that
\imath^{2}=-1
(for example
k:=\mathbb{C}
), we may set
P_{2}=\operatorname{diag}(1,\imath)
and
P=\operatorname{diag}(P_{2},P_{2})
.
Then
P^{\mathrm{T}}BP=I_{4}
, and
\widetilde{\varphi}:=P^{-1}\varphi P
is symmetric:

\widetilde{\varphi}=\begin{pmatrix}x&\imath y&0&0\\
\imath y&x&0&0\\
0&0&z&\imath w\\
0&0&\imath w&z\end{pmatrix},

and it still satisfies
\widetilde{\varphi}^{\,2}=fI_{4}
in the matrix factorization.

7.2.
Working with bimodules

Let
F:R\to R
denote the Frobenius endomorphism. The
n
th iterate defines a new
R
-module structure on the set
R
, denoted
{}^{{F^{n}}}\!R
, where

a\cdot b=a^{p^{n}}b\quad(a,b\in R).

For an ideal
I
, we write
I^{[p]}=\langle x^{p}:x\in I\rangle
.

Example 7.7
.

There exist modules
M,N
of infinite projective dimension over the ring

R=\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[[X,Y,Z]]/(X^{p}),

which has depth
2
, such that
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)=1
. Moreover,
M
is maximal Cohen–Macaulay.

Proof.

Let
N=R/(x,y)
and
M={}^{F^{n}}\!R
. Then

M\otimes_{R}N\cong R/(x,y)^{[p]}=R/(x^{p},y^{p})=R/(y^{p})

by
[
11
, 8.2.2(b)]
. Since
y^{p}
is
R
-regular,

\operatorname{pd}_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)=1.
Because
R
is not regular, Kunz’s theorem
[
11
, 8.2.8]
implies
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(M)=\infty
. If
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(N)<\infty
, then
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(R/xR)<\infty
, which would force
(x)
to contain a regular element, impossible since
x^{p}=0
. Hence
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(N)=\infty
.
∎

Observation 7.8
.

Let
R
be a one-dimensional domain of prime characteristic
p>0
that is not weakly normal. Then there exist finitely generated
R
-modules
M
and
N
such that

\operatorname{pd}_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)=1\quad\text{while}\quad\operatorname{pd}_{R}(M)=\operatorname{pd}_{R}(N)=\infty.

Proof.

Since
R
is not weakly normal, there exists a principal ideal
I=xR
that is not Frobenius closed. Hence there is an element
y\in R
such that

y^{p}\in I^{[p]}=(x^{p})\quad\text{but}\quad y\notin I.

Set

N:=R/(x,y),\qquad M:={}^{F^{n}}\!R

for some
n\geq 1
, where
{}^{F^{n}}\!R
denotes
R
viewed as an
R
-module via the
n
-th Frobenius.

By
[
11
, 8.2.2(b)]
,

M\otimes_{R}N\cong R/(x,y)^{[p^{n}]}=R/(x^{p^{n}},y^{p^{n}}).

Since
y^{p}\in(x^{p})
, we have
y^{p^{n}}\in(x^{p^{n}})
, and therefore

(x^{p^{n}},y^{p^{n}})=(x^{p^{n}}).

Thus

M\otimes_{R}N\cong R/(x^{p^{n}}).

Because
R
is a one-dimensional domain and
x\neq 0
, the element
x^{p^{n}}
is a nonzerodivisor. Hence

\operatorname{pd}_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)=\operatorname{pd}_{R}\big(R/(x^{p^{n}})\big)=1.

Since
R
is not regular, Kunz’s theorem implies that Frobenius is not flat, and therefore

\operatorname{pd}_{R}(M)=\operatorname{pd}_{R}({}^{F^{n}}\!R)=\infty

(see
[
11
, 8.2.8]
).
Now suppose, toward a contradiction, that
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(N)<\infty
. Then
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(R/(x,y))<\infty
. Consider the short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow(x,y)\longrightarrow R\longrightarrow R/(x,y)\longrightarrow 0.

It follows that
(x,y)
also has finite projective dimension. Since
R
is local and one-dimensional, the Auslander–Buchsbaum formula gives

\operatorname{pd}_{R}((x,y))+0pt_{R}((x,y))=0pt(R)=1.

But
(x,y)\subseteq R
is torsion-free over the domain
R
, so
0pt_{R}((x,y))\geq 1
. Hence
\operatorname{pd}_{R}((x,y))=0
, and therefore
(x,y)
is a free
R
-module of rank one. Thus
(x,y)
is principal.
Because
y\notin(x)
by
(\ast)
, the generator must be
y
, so
(x,y)=(y)
. Hence
x=ry
for some
r\in R
. Since
y\notin(x)
, the element
r
is not a unit.
From
(\ast)
we also have
y^{p}=sx^{p}
for some
s\in R
. Substituting
x=ry
gives

y^{p}=s(ry)^{p}=sr^{p}y^{p}.

Since
R
is a domain and
y\neq 0
, we can cancel
y^{p}
to obtain

1=sr^{p}.

Thus
r
is a unit, a contradiction.
Therefore
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(N)=\infty
, completing the proof.
∎

Example 7.9
.

There exist
R
-modules
M,N
of infinite projective dimension over the Artinian ring

R=\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[[X]]/(X^{p})

such that

\operatorname{pd}_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)=0.

Proof.

Let

N:=R/xR=R/\mathfrak{m},\qquad M:={}^{F^{n}}\!R

for some
n\geq 1
.
Then

M\otimes_{R}N\cong R/(x)^{[p^{n}]}=R/(x^{p^{n}}).

Since
x^{p}=0
in
R
, we have
x^{p^{n}}=0
, and hence

M\otimes_{R}N\cong R.

Thus

\operatorname{pd}_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)=0.

Because
R
is not regular, Kunz’s theorem implies

\operatorname{pd}_{R}(M)=\infty,

and clearly
N=R/\mathfrak{m}
also has infinite projective dimension since
R
is not a field.
∎

8.
Projective dimension and chain condition

The following improves
[
24
, Proposition II.3]
:

Observation 8.1
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a UFD. Then any chain of prime ideals extends to length
3
.
In particular, a
3
-dimensional UFD satisfies the strict chain condition (i.e., every saturated chain of prime ideals has length
3
).

Proof.

We prove the particular case. Let
0\subsetneq P\subsetneq\mathfrak{m}
be a strict chain of prime ideals. Since
0\subsetneq P
is strict,
P
has height one. By the UFD property,
P=(x)
for some
x\in R
. Let
y\in\mathfrak{m}\setminus P
. Let
Q\in\operatorname{Spec}(R)
be minimal over
(x,y)
. Since
(x)\subset(x,y)\subseteq\mathfrak{m}
is strict, we must have
Q=\mathfrak{m}
. Thus
\sqrt{(x,y)}=\mathfrak{m}
, so
x,y
form part of a system of parameters. Hence
\dim(R/(x,y))=1
, while
\dim(R)=3
, so
\operatorname{ht}(x,y)=2
. This shows that any saturated chain in
\operatorname{Spec}(R)
has length
3
.
∎

The following is another example.

Proposition 8.2
.

Let
R
be a non-catenary domain of dimension
3
. Then any nonzero prime ideal occurring in a bad saturated chain has infinite projective dimension.

Proof.

Let
0\subsetneq P\subsetneq\mathfrak{m}
be a strict chain of prime ideals. If
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(\mathfrak{m})
were finite, then
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(k)<\infty
, and hence
R
would be regular. But regular rings are catenary, a contradiction. Thus
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(\mathfrak{m})=\infty
.

Suppose, toward a contradiction, that
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(P)<\infty
. Since
0\subsetneq P
is strict,
P
has height one. Also,
0pt_{R}(R/P)>0
because
P
is prime. Since
0pt(R)=3
, the Auslander–Buchsbaum formula gives

\operatorname{pd}(R/P)=0pt(R)-0pt(R/P)\leq 3-1=2.

By
[
7
, Corollary 2]
, it follows that
P=(x)
for some
x\in R
. Let
y\in\mathfrak{m}\setminus P
, and let
Q\in\operatorname{Spec}(R)
be minimal over
(x,y)
. As before,
Q=\mathfrak{m}
, so
\sqrt{(x,y)}=\mathfrak{m}
. By the generalized principal ideal theorem,

3=0pt(R)\leq\dim(R)=\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{m})\leq 2,

a contradiction. Hence
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(P)=\infty
.
∎

Corollary 8.3
.

Let
R
be a domain of dimension
d
with the following strict saturated chain of primes:

0=\mathfrak{p}_{0}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\mathfrak{p}_{d-2}\subsetneq\mathfrak{m}.

Then
\operatorname{pd}(\mathfrak{p}_{d-2})
is infinite.

Proof.

By Proposition
8.2
, we may assume
d\geq 4
. Suppose, toward a contradiction, that
\operatorname{pd}(\mathfrak{p}_{d-2})
is finite. Note that

d-2\leq\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p}_{d-2})\leq d-1.

First assume
\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p}_{d-2})=d-1
. Since
\operatorname{pd}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}_{d-2}}}(\mathfrak{p}_{d-2}R_{\mathfrak{p}_{d-2}})<\infty
, the ring
R_{\mathfrak{p}_{d-2}}
is regular, hence catenary. But

0=\mathfrak{p}_{0}R_{\mathfrak{p}_{d-2}}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\mathfrak{p}_{d-2}R_{\mathfrak{p}_{d-2}}

is a strict saturated chain in
\operatorname{Spec}(R_{\mathfrak{p}_{d-2}})
of length less than
\dim(R_{\mathfrak{p}_{d-2}})
, a contradiction. Hence
\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p}_{d-2})\neq d-1
, so
\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p}_{d-2})=d-2
.
The ring
R
is not Cohen–Macaulay; otherwise it would be catenary. Thus Proposition
4.21
applies, and
R/\mathfrak{p}_{d-2}
is perfect. By the Peskine–Szpiro theorem, the grade conjecture holds for perfect modules, so

\operatorname{grade}(R/\mathfrak{p}_{d-2})+\dim(R/\mathfrak{p}_{d-2})=\dim R=d.

Since there is no prime strictly between
\mathfrak{p}_{d-2}
and
\mathfrak{m}
, we have
\dim(R/\mathfrak{p}_{d-2})=1
, hence
\operatorname{grade}(R/\mathfrak{p}_{d-2})=d-1
. By Fact
4.9
,

\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p}_{d-2})=\operatorname{grade}(R/\mathfrak{p}_{d-2})=d-1,

a contradiction. Therefore
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(\mathfrak{p}_{d-2})=\infty
.
∎

Remark 8.4
.

We construct a ring
R
such that for every
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}(R)\setminus\{0,\mathfrak{m}\}
, one has
\operatorname{pd}(R/\mathfrak{p})=\infty
but
\operatorname{pd}(R_{\mathfrak{p}})<\infty
.

Proof.

Let
A:=k[X,Y,Z,W,T]/(X,Y)(Z,W)
. By work of Heitmann
[
15
]
, there exists a UFD
R
with isolated singularities such that
\widehat{R}=A
.
Let
B:=k[x,y,z,w,t]
. A minimal free resolution of
(x,y)(z,w)
over
B
is

0\to B\to B^{4}\to B^{4}\to(x,y)(z,w)\to 0.

By the Auslander–Buchsbaum formula,

0pt(A)=0pt(B)-\operatorname{pd}_{B}(A)=5-3=2.

By
[
11
, Ex. 1.2.26]
,
0pt(A)=0pt(R)=2
.
Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}(R)
and suppose
\operatorname{pd}(R/\mathfrak{p})<\infty
. Then
\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p})\in\{2,3\}
.
First, we deal with
\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p})=2
. Thus,

2=\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ref{beder}}}{{=}}\operatorname{grade}(R/\mathfrak{p})\leq\operatorname{pd}(R/\mathfrak{p})=0pt(R)-0pt(R/\mathfrak{p})\leq 2-1=1,

a contradiction.
It remains to consider the case
\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p})=3
. The same argument gives us

3=\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ref{beder}}}{{=}}\operatorname{grade}(R/\mathfrak{p})\leq\operatorname{pd}(R/\mathfrak{p})\leq 1,

again a contradiction. Hence
\operatorname{pd}(R/\mathfrak{p})=\infty
for all nonzero nonmaximal primes
\mathfrak{p}
.
∎

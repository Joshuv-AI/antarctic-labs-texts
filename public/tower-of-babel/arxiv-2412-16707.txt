A Sharp Geometric Measure of Entanglement
Dharmaraj Ramachandran, Radhika Vathsan

Abstract
Despite their elegance and widespread use, the current Geometric Measures (GMs) of entanglement exhibit a significant limitation: they fail to effectively distinguish Local Unitary (LU) inequivalent states due to the inherent nature of their definition. We illustrate the impact of this limitation using the fidelity of the teleportation protocol as an example. To address this issue, we introduce the Sharp Geometric Measure (SGM) by modifying the standard definition of the Geometric Measure. We show that the closed-form expression of the SGM can be equivalently derived using the Riemannian structure of both the composite state space and the reduced density operator space. Furthermore, we define a measure of Genuine Multipartite Entanglement (GME) derived from the SGM, which we term GMS. We demonstrate that GMS resolves two key limitations of some existing GME measures, thereby establishing its utility and effectiveness in quantifying GME.

II
A shortcoming of geometric measures of entanglement

The standard definition of a geometric measure of entanglement for a bipartite state
\displaystyle\ket{\psi}\in\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}
is the distance to the closest separable state:

GM(\psi)=\min_{\phi_{s}\in\mathcal{S}_{\text{sep}}}D(\psi,\phi_{s}),

(1)

where
\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\text{sep}}
denotes the set of all separable states, and
\displaystyle D(.,.)
is a distance measure on the space of states.

This notion of distance to the closest separable state has been the foundation for several entanglement measures
Wei and Goldbart 2003
;
Vedral and Plenio 1998
;
Eisert et al. 2003
;
Witte and Trucks 1999
in the literature. While there exist various geometric measures, they all differ only in the choice of the distance measure used. Though definition poses the problem of minimization over an infinite set, the Schmidt decomposition facilitates finding a closed-form solution for Eq. (
1
) in the case of pure bipartite states.

The Schmidt decomposition of a pure bipartite state is expressed as

\ket{\psi}=\sum_{i}\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}\ket{a_i}\otimes\ket{b_i},

(2)

where
\displaystyle\ket{a_i}
and
\displaystyle\ket{b_i}
are the eigenvectors of the density operators of subsystems
\displaystyle A
and
\displaystyle B
respectively,
\displaystyle\lambda_{i}\in[0,1]
being their corresponding eigenvalues.

Wei
et al.

Wei and Goldbart 2003
demonstrated that the closest separable state to a pure bipartite state is given by the Schmidt vector corresponding to the largest Schmidt coefficient
\displaystyle\lambda_{\text{max}}
,
which they call the entanglement eigenvalue.
All geometric measures, such as those in
Vedral and Plenio 1998
;
Eisert et al. 2003
;
Witte and Trucks 1999
, are functions of
\displaystyle\lambda_{\text{max}}
. Thus Geometric Measures of entanglement (GMs) depend solely on the largest Schmidt coefficient. This implies that GM’s are insensitive to variations in the non-maximal Schmidt coefficients.

However, all the Schmidt coefficients play a role in measuring the entanglement content of a state.
If the Schmidt coefficients of two pure bipartite states
\displaystyle\ket{\psi_1}
and
\displaystyle\ket{\psi_2}
are different, then the unitary transformation
\displaystyle U_{AB}
relating them is by definition non-local:

\displaystyle\displaystyle U_{AB}\ket{\psi_1}=\ket{\psi_2}\implies U_{AB}\neq U_{A}\otimes U_{B}.

The entanglement content of a state is preserved only under local unitary (LU) transformations. GM’s are therefore insensitive to such transformations that may not preserve the entanglement content of the state.
We would like to call measures that are sensitive to changes in any Schmidt coefficient as
sharp
entanglement measures.
Certain measures like entanglement entropy
Bennett et al. 1996c
are sharp by definition while GM’s are not. In the next section we highlight the importance of sharpness in entanglement quantification using quantum teleportation as an example.

IV
Sharp Geometric Measure (SGM)

We propose a modification to the Geometric Measure of Entanglement in order to make it sharp. The Sharp Geometric Measure (SGM) is defined as the complement of the normalized distance to the closest maximally entangled state. For a bipartite pure state
\displaystyle\ket{\psi}
, SGM is expressed as:

\text{SGM}(\psi)=1-\min_{\Psi_{m}\in\mathcal{S}_{\text{max}}}\frac{D_{F}(\psi,\Psi_{m})}{N_{1}},

(5)

where
\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\text{max}}
is the set of all maximally entangled states,
\displaystyle D_{F}(.,.)
denotes the Fubini-Study distance between quantum states, and
\displaystyle N_{1}
is a normalization constant ensuring that
\displaystyle D_{F}(\psi,\Psi_{m})/N_{1}\in[0,1]
. The normalization constant will become evident once a closed-form expression for Eq. (
5
) is derived. We choose the Fubini-Study distance due to its status as the only Riemannian distance defined in the space of pure states.

The notion of distance to closest maximally entangled state was introduced earlier as maximal fidelity
Bennett et al. 1996d
;
Badzia¸g et al. 2000
;
Verstraete and Verschelde 2002
. Its utility as a measure of entanglement is less pursued as it was pointed out by K. Życzkowski
et al

Bengtsson and Zyczkowski 2006
that maximal fidelity does not satisfy
E2
and it was shown by P.Badziag
et al

Horodecki et al. 1999
and Verstraete
et al

Verstraete and Verschelde 2002
that it also violates
E1
. While maximal fidelity is not an entanglement measure, SGM does satisfy necessary conditions for a valid entanglement measure which will be explained after we derive the closed form expression.

Claim:
The maximally entangled state
\displaystyle\ket{\Psi_c}
closest to a bipartite pure state
\displaystyle\ket{\psi}\in\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}
is the one with the same Schmidt vectors as
\displaystyle\ket{\psi}
, with all Schmidt coefficients equal.

Proof:
Let the Schmidt representation of
\displaystyle\ket{\psi}
be

\ket{\psi}=\sum_{i=0}^{n_{A}-1}\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}\ket{a_i}\otimes\ket{b_i},

(6)

where
\displaystyle n_{A}=\min\big(\dim(\mathcal{H}_{A}),\dim(\mathcal{H}_{B})\big)
. A maximally entangled state
\displaystyle\ket{\Psi_{m}}\in\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}
can be expressed as

\ket{\Psi_{m}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{A}}}\sum_{j=0}^{n_{A}-1}e^{i\theta_{j}}\ket{c_j}\otimes\ket{d_j},\;\theta_{j}\in[0,2\pi].

(7)

The Fubini-Study distance between
\displaystyle\ket{\psi}
and
\displaystyle\ket{\Psi_{m}}
is

D_{F}(\psi,\Psi_{m})=\sqrt{1-|\innerproduct{\psi}{\Psi_{m}}|}.

(8)

Minimizing this distance is equivalent to maximizing the overlap
\displaystyle|\innerproduct{\psi}{\Psi_{m}}|
:

\max_{\{\ket{\Psi_{m}}\}}|\innerproduct{\psi}{\Psi_{m}}|=\max_{(\theta_{j},\ket{c_j},\ket{d_j})}\left|\sum_{i,j}\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}e^{i\theta_{j}}\innerproduct{a_i}{c_j}\innerproduct{b_i}{d_j}\right|.

(9)

From the triangle inequality, the overlap is maximized when

\theta_{j}=\text{constant}\;\forall j,\quad\text{ and }\{\ket{c_j}\},\{\ket{d_j}\}=\{\ket{a_i}\},\{\ket{b_i}\}.

(10)

Substituting Eq. (
10
) into Eq. (
7
), the maximally entangled state closest to
\displaystyle\ket{\psi}
is

\ket{\Psi_{c}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{A}}}\sum_{i=0}^{n_{A}-1}\ket{a_i}\otimes\ket{b_i}.

(11)

The minimum distance then depends on the sum of all Schmidt coefficients:

D_{F}(\psi,\Psi_{c})=\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{A}}}{\sum_{i}\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}}}.

We normalize this distance using
\displaystyle N_{1}
, the distance of any product state to its closest maximally entangled state:

N_{1}=\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{A}}}}.

(12)

We now express the SGM in terms of the Schmidt coefficients as:

\text{SGM}(\psi)=1-\frac{1}{N_{1}}\left(1-\frac{\sum_{i}\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}}{\sqrt{n_{A}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(13)

It is evident that SGM as expressed in Eq. (
13
), is sensitive to variations in all Schmidt coefficients. This subtle modification ensures that the measure is sharp, enabling it to detect changes in entanglement content more effectively.

V
SGM Defined Using the Reduced Density Operator Space

G. Vidal
Vidal 2000
pointed out that any entanglement measure defined for pure states is in one-to-one correspondence with an entanglement measure defined using reduced density operators.
In this section, we show that the closed-form expression of SGM in Eq. (
13
) can equivalently be derived using the Riemannian structure of the reduced density operator space.

The Schmidt coefficients of
\displaystyle\ket{\psi}_{AB}\in H_{A}\otimes H_{B}
correspond to the eigenvalues of the reduced density operators
\displaystyle\rho_{A}
and
\displaystyle\rho_{B}
. The separable state closest to
\displaystyle\ket{\psi}_{AB}
is determined by the pure state closest to
\displaystyle\rho_{A/B}
in either of the reduced spaces
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{A/B}\ni\rho_{A/B}
.

Pure states are the extreme points of the reduced density operator space, while the maximally mixed state is represented as
\displaystyle\rho_{\star A/B}=\frac{\mathbbm{1}}{n_{A}}
, where
\displaystyle n_{A}\leq n_{B}
.

The Sharp Geometric Measure (SGM) corresponds to the distance to the closest maximally entangled state. In the pure state space, identifying the closest maximally entangled state involves optimization, but in the reduced density operator space, all maximally entangled states share the same reduced density operator,
\displaystyle\rho_{\star A}
. Therefore, computing the SGM reduces to measuring the distance between
\displaystyle\rho_{A}
and
\displaystyle\rho_{\star A}
.

Choice of Metric on
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}

While the search for the closest maximally entangled state simplifies in the reduced density operator space, a metric must still be chosen. In the pure state space, the Fubini-Study metric is the unique Riemannian metric. However, in the mixed state space, multiple Riemannian metrics exist.

A natural choice for this task is the Bures metric
Bures 1969
, which coincides with the Fubini-Study metric when restricted to pure states
Braunstein and Caves 1994
. The Bures metric can be expressed in terms of the fidelity
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})~=~\Tr\big(\sqrt{\sqrt{\rho_1} \rho_2 \sqrt{\rho_1}}\big)
as:

D^{2}_{\mathcal{B}}(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})=2\big(1-\mathcal{F}(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})\big).

(14)

The entanglement measure is actually independent of the choice of metric. This is seen as follows. In the reduced density operator space for entangled states, the reduced density matrices are LU-equivalent to diagonal matrices (Schmidt form). According to the Morozova-Čentsov-Petz (MCP) Theorem
Morozova and Chentsov 1991
;
Petz and Sudár 1996
, all Riemannian metrics differ only in terms involving the off-diagonal elements of the density matrices. On the submanifold of diagonal density operators, all Riemannian metrics, including the Bures metric, are equivalent to the Fisher-Rao metric
Petz and Sudar 2001
. Consequently, using the Bures metric for defining the SGM entails no loss of generality.

In terms of distances in the space of reduced density matrices, the Sharp Geometric Measure for a pure state
\displaystyle\ket{\psi}_{AB}
is

\text{SGM}(\psi_{AB})=1-\frac{D_{\mathcal{B}}(\rho_{A},\rho_{\star A})}{N_{2}},

(15)

where
\displaystyle N_{2}
is a normalization constant.

The Bures distance of a pure state
\displaystyle\rho_{p}
from
\displaystyle\rho_{\star}
depends on the dimensionality
\displaystyle\dim[\mathcal{M}]=n
of the reduced state space. The square-root fidelity evaluates to
\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{A}}}
, giving:

N_{2}=D_{\mathcal{B}}(\rho_{\star},\rho_{p})=\sqrt{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{A}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(16)

If
\displaystyle\lambda_{i}
are the eigenvalues of
\displaystyle\rho_{A}
, the Bures distance from
\displaystyle\rho_{\star A}=\frac{\mathbbm{1}}{n_{A}}
is

D^{2}_{\mathcal{B}}(\rho_{A},\rho_{\star A})=2\left(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{A}}}\sum_{i}\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}\right).

(17)

This expression for the SGM is identical to Eq. (
13
).
Hence the closed-form expression for SGM can be independently derived using either the composite Hilbert space or the reduced density operator space.

Properties of SGM

•

Local Unitary (LU) invariance:

The form Eq: (
13
) ensures that the SGM is LU-invariant since local unitary operators on
\displaystyle\ket{\psi_{AB}}
will not change the eigenvalues of the reduced density operators.

•

Strong Discriminance:

\displaystyle N_{1}
and
\displaystyle N_{2}
are tailored to ensure that for separable state
\displaystyle\ket{\phi_s}
,

SGM(\phi_{s})=0.

Any entangled state
\displaystyle\ket{\Psi_e}
has more than one Schmidt coefficient which ensures

SGM(\Psi_{e})\neq 0.

•

Monotonicity under LOCC:

If the state
\displaystyle\ket{\psi}_{AB}
undergoes LOCC and the eigenvalues of the reduced density operator change

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lambda_{i}\;\xrightarrow{LOCC}\;\lambda^{\prime}_{i},

Nielson’s majorising theorem
Nielsen 1999
assures us that

\lambda_{\psi}\succeq\lambda_{\psi^{\prime}}\implies\sum_{i}\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}\geq\sum_{i}\sqrt{\lambda^{\prime}_{i}}.

(18)

Using this relation in (
13
) shows that the SGM is monotonic under LOCC.

Thus, SGM satisfies the desirable properties of a good entanglement measure.

VI
Genuine Multi-partite Entanglement

When more than two parties are involved, there are several inequivalent ways in which they can be entangled
Schlienz and Mahler 1995
;
Dür et al. 2000
. For instance in case of three qubits there are two SLOCC inequivalent classes of entanglement namely: the GHZ-class equivalent to

\ket{GHZ}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\big(\ket{000}+\ket{111}\big)

(19)

and the W-class which are superpositions of states in which only one qubit is in the state
\displaystyle\ket{1}
:

\ket{W}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\big(\ket{001}+\ket{010}+\ket{100}\big).

(20)

These classes are also generalized to
\displaystyle n
qubits in a straightforward manner.
For
\displaystyle n>3
, the system could be in a state that is a tensor product of entangled subsystems. Genuine entanglement is defined as that for states that are not bi-separable in any way.

We have defined the properties of measure of GME in the Introduction.
Now it was pointed out in by Love
et al
Love et al. 2007
and Yu
et al
Yu and Song 2004
that any bipartite measure can be used to define a measure of GME. We therefore define a measure of GME using the SGM.

VI.1
GME measure based on SGM

We define a measure of GME in an
\displaystyle n
-partite system as the geometric mean of SGMs of all possible bipartitions. We call this the GMS (Geometric mean of Sharp Geometric Measure):
We find the SGM of each bipartition (
\displaystyle b_{i}
) and calculate the geometric mean of all of them,

\text{GMS}\big(\psi_{n}\big)=\prod_{i=1}^{m}(b_{i})^{\frac{1}{m}}.

(21)

The number
\displaystyle m
of unique possible bipartitions is given by
Li and Shang 2022
,

m=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\sum_{i=1}^{(n-1)/2}{n\choose i}&\text{for odd }n,\\
&\\
\sum_{i=1}^{(n-2)/2}{n\choose i}+\frac{1}{2}{n\choose n/2}&\text{for even }n.\end{array}\right.

Properties of GMS

GMS satisfies the properties

(P1)
and
(P2)
since it is defined using the product of bipartite measures. Based on results in
Joo et al. 2003b
Xie
et al
defined
proper
GME measure
Xie and Eberly 2021b
if the measure ranks entanglement in GHZ state higher than that of W state. Calculations show that GMS
\displaystyle(GHZ)=1,
while GMS
\displaystyle(W)=0.94
, thus satisfying the condition for
proper
GME measure.

VII
Discussion

In this section we demonstrate that the ability to distinguish LU-inequivalent entanglement extends to multipartite entanglement as well.

Based on Generalised Schmidt Decomposition (GSD) there are six LU inequivalent sub-classes
Sabín and García-Alcaine 2008
;
Acín et al. 2000
for three qubit states.
Among these are four classes of genuinely entangled states. They can be identified by the non-zero coefficients in their GSD. The GSD of a 3-qubit pure state takes the canonical form

\ket{\psi}=\lambda_{0}\ket{000}+\lambda_{1}\ket{100}+\lambda_{2}\ket{101}+\lambda_{3}\ket{110}+\lambda_{4}\ket{111}.

The four classes each have non-zero
\displaystyle\lambda_{0}
and
\displaystyle\lambda_{4}
, and are differentiated by the the non-vanishing of the other
\displaystyle\lambda
s:

C1:

all of
\displaystyle\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}
vanish, (GHZ-class);

C2:

any two of
\displaystyle\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}
vanish;

C3:

any one of
\displaystyle\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}
vanishes

C4:

:
none of
\displaystyle\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}
vanish. (W-class).

Several authors have discussed multipartite entanglement measures but few satisfy all the criteria for a measure of GME.

Generalised Geometric Mean (GGM)
Das et al. 2016
;
Sen De
and Genuinely Multipartite Concurrence (GMC)
Hashemi Rafsanjani et al. 2012
are examples of measures using the minimum entanglement among all bi-partitions. It turns out that these are relatively poor at
discriminating states that belong to the above LU-inequivalent classes. Similar to GM in the bipartite case, these measures depend only on the largest Schmidt coefficient and hence end up being non-sharp measures of GME.
Xie et al
Xie and Eberly 2021b

VIII
Conclusion

In this work, we have defined a bipartite geometric entanglement measure (SGM) based on the distance to the closest maximally entangled state. We also demonstrate that the closed-form expression of SGM can be independently derived in composite bipartite state space as well as the reduced density operator space. In the density operator space, we show that all Riemannian metrics are equivalent for entanglement quantification of pure states.

We have proposed a new feature termed
sharpness
for entanglement measures, which is sensitivity to inequivalence of entanglement under non-local transformations, a property absent in standard Geometric Measures (GMs). The importance of sharpness is highlighted using the teleportation protocol as an illustrative example.

We further constructed a sharp measure of Genuine Multipartite Entanglement (GME), denoted as GMS, by employing the geometric mean of SGMs. Our measure GMS provides a simple closed-form expression for quantifying GME in any finite-dimensional pure multipartite state. Through examples, we illustrate the advantages of sharp measures of GME, such as GBC and GMS, over other non-sharp measures such as GGM and GMC.

While most resource theories quantify a resource by the distance to the closest non-resource state, we demonstrate that in the case of the entanglement theory of pure states, the distance to the maximal resource state captures more information than the distance to the closest non-resource (separable) state. A similar approach to quantifying coherence was explored by S. Karmakar
et al.

Karmakar et al. 2019
. It is worth exploring whether such a method provides advantages when applied to the quantification of other quantum resources.

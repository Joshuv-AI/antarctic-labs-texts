A hybrid model integrating RoBERTa, TF-IDF, and attention mechanism for medical query intent classification
Authors: Zejian Liang, Yunxiang Zhao, Haiwen Xu, Hong Huang, Luning Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn medical retrieval scenarios, accurate identification of user query intent is pivotal for enhancing retrieval relevance and knowledge augmentation effectiveness. However, existing approaches exhibit polarized performance: traditional shallow models (e.g., TF-IDF, Bag-of-Words) struggle to capture contextual semantics, while pre-trained language models, despite their strong deep semantic representation capabilities, often overlook keyword weighting and category-specific priors. This leads to suboptimal accuracy for long-tail categories and susceptibility to confusion from emotional or colloquial query formulations. To address these challenges, this study proposes a hybrid model integrating RoBERTa-wwm-ext with sentence-level TF-IDF and category-centroid TF-IDF via a three-branch attention fusion mechanism. The model employs adaptive attention mechanisms to dynamically weight deep-shallow features at the sample level, while incorporating category-centroid vectors as explicit semantic anchors to mitigate class imbalance issues. Experimental evaluations on the KUAKE-QIC dataset demonstrate that our approach achieves an accuracy of 0.824 and a Macro-F1 score of 0.800, outperforming all baseline models by 2.3% points. These results highlight the synergistic benefits of deep-shallow feature interaction, ensuring robust recall for dominant categories while significantly improving precision for minority classes. This work provides a generalizable framework for medical intent classification that balances semantic integrity with keyword-driven priors, laying a more reliable foundation for applications such as clinical question-answering systems, medical search ranking, and intelligent triage systems.Keywords: Medial intent classification, RoBERTa, TF-IDF, Attention mechanism, Knowledge-enhanced retrievalSubject terms: Computational biology and bioinformatics, Health care, Mathematics and computing, Medical research

Introduction

In the context of the internet era, the accuracy of medical text intent classification holds paramount importance for patient consultation and diagnosis. Surveys indicate that up to 80% of internet users have engaged in online searches for medical and health-related information1. However, due to the professional complexity of medical knowledge, accurately classifying such queries remains a significant challenge. Medical search queries are often extremely concise, semantically ambiguous, and may use non-professional terminology to describe symptoms or conditions2,3. For instance, a single symptom may have multiple colloquial names, complicating query comprehension. Furthermore, incorrect intent judgment risks providing misleading medical information, which could adversely impact user decision-making or even delay medical care4. Consequently, accurately understanding the underlying intent behind users’ medical queries has emerged as an urgent problem in the field of medical information retrieval.

Medical text processing encounters numerous unique challenges. First, medical texts exhibit strong domain-specific characteristics, and direct application of generic natural language processing (NLP) models to medical corpora often yields suboptimal performance. This is primarily attributed to significant lexical distribution and semantic discrepancies between medical and general-purpose corpora, leading to a lexical shift issue. For instance, studies have shown that general-purpose pre-trained models like BERT underperform on biomedical texts unless further pre-trained on large-scale medical corpora5. Second, medical texts—including patient-reported symptoms and electronic health records—are frequently unstructured and noisy, with patients often using imprecise or ambiguous colloquial language3. This necessitates models capable of incorporating medical domain knowledge for contextual interpretation, such as mapping patient terminology to standardized medical concepts. While prior research has attempted to extract medical entities or leverage medical knowledge graphs for enhanced representation, ineffective integration risks information loss or excessive model complexity2. Third, annotated data scarcity remains a critical bottleneck. High-quality training data for medical intent classification requires expert annotation, which is costly and limited in scale, resulting in insufficient learning of rare terms and infrequent intent categories6. Category imbalance is particularly pronounced in medical queries, where specialized intents (e.g., rare disease consultations) are underrepresented, causing models to bias toward majority classes. Addressing these challenges requires solutions for lexical semantic adaptation, short-text context enrichment, domain knowledge fusion, and mitigation of data sparsity and imbalance7.

This paper proposes a principled hybrid model that fuses (i) contextual representations from RoBERTa-wwm-ext, (ii) sentence-level TF-IDF features, and (iii) class-prototype (centroid) TF-IDF priors via an additive attention module. The attention layer adaptively weights branches per input, enhancing discrimination for minority and boundary-ambiguous intents. To prevent label leakage during inference, we adopt soft-prototype inference, forming input-dependent prototype expectations from model-predicted distributions. This combination enhances both contextual understanding and category discrimination for ultra-short medical queries.

Related work

The evolution of medical text classification has witnessed a paradigm shift from traditional methods to deep learning techniques. Early approaches primarily relied on rule-based systems or classical machine learning algorithms. Vector space models, leveraging term frequency-inverse document frequency (TF-IDF) and bag-of-words (BoW) representations, were initially employed to capture lexical significance8. Subsequently, word embedding technologies such as Word2Vec9 and GloVe10 emerged, enabling semantic relationships between terms to be modeled through continuous vector spaces. Faris et al.11 demonstrated the utility of particle swarm optimization for feature selection combined with a one-vs-rest SVM ensemble, achieving patient department classification. While these traditional methods performed adequately on small-scale datasets, they heavily depended on expert-driven feature engineering and struggled to capture deep contextual semantics. In recent years, deep learning has revolutionized medical text processing. Architectures such as convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and recurrent neural networks (RNNs)/long short-term memory (LSTM) networks have enabled automatic feature learning from raw data, reducing reliance on manual design3. For instance, Du et al.12 proposed the ML-Net model, which employs deep neural networks for multi-label classification of biomedical literature, significantly enhancing efficiency over traditional approaches. Chen et al.13 integrated LSTM networks into medical text classification frameworks, improving the extraction of critical symptom keywords and descriptive elements from large-scale medical corpora. Zhang et al.14 explored CNN architectures for text classification, demonstrating how convolutional operations and max-pooling layers capture local textual features to achieve efficient categorization. Liu et al.15 introduced a recursive neural network (RNN) framework combining online language understanding and language modeling, showcasing superior performance on context-dependent tasks. The advent of attention mechanisms, pioneered by Bahdanau et al.16, further advanced model capabilities, yielding improved performance in machine translation and text classification. Chai et al.17 leveraged statistical text classification to identify medical IT incidents, achieving notable accuracy gains. Ali et al.18 and Ozyegen et al.19 separately proposed machine learning-based and word-level text highlighting approaches for medical text classification, enhancing clinical data analysis efficiency. More recently, Liang et al.20 introduced a medical text classification framework combining ZEN and capsule networks, while Luo et al.21 demonstrated the efficacy of the bilingual Taiyi model for diverse biomedical tasks. These advancements collectively underscore the transformative potential of deep learning and hybrid architectures in addressing the unique challenges of medical text processing.

This progress has catalyzed the development of domain-adapted BERT variants, including BioBERT24 for biomedical texts, and Chinese-specific models such as MC-BERT25, BERT-Healthcare26, and ClinicalBERT27. Innovative hybrid architectures further extend these capabilities: Li et al.28 proposed ECC-BERT, while Wa et al.29 and Yu et al.30 introduced BERT-CNN and TinyBERT-CNN fusion models, respectively, combining BERT’s semantic robustness with convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to enhance classification performance. Additionally, Wang et al.31 demonstrated the efficacy of discriminative pre-training and prompt tuning techniques, and Peng et al.32 showcased transfer learning approaches for medical text classification. Zhang et al.33 highlighted BERT’s applicability in biological sequence analysis, achieving notable results in predicting lncRNA subcellular localization. By incorporating large-scale medical literature and clinical note corpora during pre-training, these models demonstrate superior adaptation to medical terminology and discourse patterns. They achieve state-of-the-art performance across medical named entity recognition, relation extraction, and user intent classification tasks. BERT-based deep language models have emerged as cornerstone technologies in contemporary medical NLP, providing critical tools for complex semantic interpretation and intent recognition in clinical settings.

Despite the remarkable performance of deep learning models like BERT in medical search intent classification, a growing research trend emphasizes hybrid architectures that integrate multi-source features. These approaches synergize deep semantic representations with traditional statistical features and domain-specific knowledge to leverage their complementary strengths. Statistical features such as TF-IDF provide intuitive measures of term significance, compensating for deep models’ potential oversight of rare but critical medical terminology34. Recent studies demonstrate the efficacy of incorporating TF-IDF-based gating mechanisms within RoBERTa models to downweight less informative terms while emphasizing emotionally salient or symptom-related keywords, thereby improving classification accuracy35. In the medical domain, Mao et al.11 proposed a Hybrid Model that fuses traditional TF-IDF features with BERT-derived semantic vectors and features extracted via LSTM/CNN architectures. Through an attention-based decision fusion mechanism, this model achieves high-accuracy classification of patient symptom descriptions, exceeding 93.5% accuracy on a Chinese symptom dataset spanning eight clinical departments and outperforming traditional standalone methods. Zhang et al.36 introduced the E-BERT architecture, which integrates medical entity embeddings and entity-aware attention mechanisms into the BERT framework. By explicitly encoding medical entities (e.g., symptoms, anatomical locations, diseases) and their relationships, this model demonstrates superior performance on lengthy clinical text classification tasks. Other approaches employ reinforcement learning to dynamically identify discriminative features. For instance, Nie et al.37 developed a keyword-driven reinforcement learning model for Chinese respiratory disease intent classification, using term-frequency reward functions to select critical information words. These words are then fed into LSTM and graph convolutional networks, enhancing intent recognition performance. To address short-text sparsity, Duan et al.38 proposed a TF-IWF-based keyword weighting scheme combined with Word2Vec synonym expansion, effectively mitigating feature sparsity and semantic ambiguity. Collectively, hybrid models that fuse traditional features (keyword frequencies, TF-IDF), domain knowledge (medical entities, knowledge graphs), and deep contextual representations now represent the forefront of medical search intent classification research39–42. These architectures utilize attention mechanisms as dynamic “glues” to balance information weights across modalities, achieving superior performance and robustness compared to single-modality approaches. This fusion strategy underscores a paradigm shift toward knowledge-augmented, context-aware systems in medical NLP applications.

Previous studies primarily focus on either deep models (e.g., BERT, RoBERTa) or statistical keyword features (e.g., TF-IDF, LDA). Deep models capture context but often overlook rare, domain-specific medical terms, while shallow models emphasize word importance yet lack contextual understanding. To bridge this gap, our work proposes a three-branch hybrid architecture combining contextual embeddings (RoBERTa-wwm-ext), local keyword salience (sentence-level TF-IDF), and global category priors (category-centroid TF-IDF). This design aims to achieve both semantic precision and category-level robustness for ultra-short medical queries.

Methodology

Model framework

This paper proposes a multi-feature fusion hybrid model, whose architecture is structured into three hierarchical components from top to bottom as shown in Fig. 1: the Feature Generation Layer, responsible for extracting semantic information and lexical features from medical queries; the Feature Fusion Layer, which integrates cross-modal information through adaptive weighting to dynamically balance semantic and lexical representations; and the Classification Decision Layer, performing final intent classification using fused features for discriminative multi-class prediction.

Model Framework Diagram.

The first component of the model is feature generation, whose core objective is to construct complementary information representations for the same query. The RoBERTa branch contributes long-range contextualized deep semantic vectors, the sentence-level TF-IDF branch highlights the most discriminative medical keywords within the current sentence for intent discrimination, while the category-centric TF-IDF branch incorporates global category priors derived from training corpus statistics. This integration effectively mitigates data sparsity issues arising from long-tail categories and rare terminology by encoding these priors into the representation space. All three feature streams are linearly projected to identical dimensions, establishing a unified dimensionality basis for subsequent weighted fusion operations, as detailed in the Feature Generation Layer of Table 1.

The second component of the model is attentive fusion, which dynamically determines the contribution ratio of the three feature branches based on query-specific content. Through learnable nonlinear scoring functions and softmax normalization, the model automatically amplifies the feature branch that best explains the current query intent. When queries contain highly indicative medical terminology, the TF-IDF branch receives greater emphasis. For semantic dependencies requiring long-range contextual understanding, the RoBERTa branch becomes more dominant. In scenarios with extreme class imbalance, the category centroid branch provides supplementary support for underrepresented classes. The resultant fused representation integrates contextual semantics, keyword salience, and categorical knowledge, demonstrating enhanced robustness against noise and data distribution skew.

Inter-branch attention vs. intra-sequence self-attention. RoBERTa’s self-attention operates within a sequence to model token–token dependencies and form contextual representations. Our fusion module instead attends across branches over three heterogeneous features (RoBERTa embedding, sentence-level TF-IDF, category-centroid TF-IDF). It acts as a sample-wise gating mechanism to adaptively weight each branch for a specific query, rather than recomputing token-level dependencies as in Transformer self-attention.

RoBERTa-based semantic encoder

RoBERTa is a pre training language model based on Transformer architecture. Transformer is a neural network architecture completely based on self attention mechanism, including encoder and decoder.

the Transformer architecture is alternately stacked by multi head self attention and feedforward networks. BERT and RoBERta are variants of its Encoder. Each layer first executes Multi-Head Self-Attention extract intra sentence global dependencies, and then provides nonlinear mapping through Add&Norm and Feed Forward. Residual connection and layer normalization jointly stabilize the deep gradient. Compared with RNN, Transformer has the advantages of parallel computing and long-distance modeling. Compared with CNN, the attention mechanism can dynamically focus on any position, eliminating the limitation of fixed receptive field.

RoBERTa introduces three key enhancements over the original BERT training paradigm: (1) Removal of Next-Sentence Prediction to eliminate task-related noise. (2) Adoption of dynamic masking, where 15% of tokens are randomly re-masked in each training epoch. (3) Utilization of larger mini-batch sizes and extended training steps. These optimizations significantly improve representation quality and transfer robustness. For Chinese applications, the HFL team from Harbin Institute of Technology conducted continued pre-training on 160 GB of Chinese encyclopedic, news, Q&A, and forum data, developing RoBERTa-wwm-ext. Its critical innovation lies in Whole-Word Masking, which uniformly masks all subword components of Chinese terms (e.g., treating “甲状腺功能减退” as a complete medical compound rather than fragmented “甲状 + 腺 + 功能…”). TF-IDF Features Extraction.

TF-IDF Features Extraction.

In addition to deep semantic representation, this paper uses word frequency inverse document frequency (TF-IDF) to capture the significance of medical keywords, and additionally constructs category level prototypes to provide a priori constraint for long tail categories.

At the sentence level, TF-IDF constructs a vocabulary for all queries in the training set and retains 200 feature words with the highest amount of information (max_features = 200, the amount of information is sorted according to the χ² score to reduce noise). Each query d is first normalized to Unicode and lowercase in English. After deleting the stop words and punctuation, it is divided into token sequences according to “space + Chinese characters”. This dimensionality control (max_features = 200 and shared linear projection to 768-d) ensures that TF-IDF vectors remain compact and computationally efficient while preserving critical keyword signals for ultra-short medical queries. Experiments

Problem definition and dataset description

In medical triage, accurate intent classification of patient queries significantly enhances operational efficiency. However, the profound complexity of medical domain knowledge and the inherent ambiguity/diversity of user expressions pose substantial challenges for precise categorization. Medical inquiries span a broad spectrum of scenarios, including disease diagnosis, etiology analysis, therapeutic regimens, and hospital guidance, necessitating classification models with both strong generalization capacity and fine-grained discriminative power. This study addresses these challenges within the context of mobile medical search, where queries are typically extremely short, use colloquial terminology, and exhibit severe class imbalance. The model must jointly capture contextual semantics and identify critical medical terminology to achieve high-precision multi-class intent determination. The experiments are conducted on the KUAKE-QIC dataset, sourced from the Chinese Biomedical Language Understanding Evaluation (CBLUE) benchmark maintained by Alibaba Cloud’s Tianchi platform (aliyun.com). The dataset comprises 6,931 training samples and 1,955 validation samples, annotated across 11 intent categories: diagnosis (As detailed in Table 4), cause analysis (cause), therapeutic methods (therapy), hospitalization guidance (advice), laboratory metric interpretation (metric), disease description (diseaseDesc), prognostic outcomes (outcome), clinical precautions (caution), therapeutic efficacy (effect), medical costs (cost), and an other category. As detailed in Table 5 training set class distribution, therapeutic methods and “other” categories dominate the dataset, while critical classes such as cause analysis and metric interpretation are severely underrepresented. This task thus confronts dual challenges: class imbalance and sparse contextual information in ultra-short text queries.

Evaluation of model Results.

It can be seen from the results in Table 9 that the traditional shallow model, because it only uses the word frequency or static word vector, lacks the ability of context modeling, and all five indicators are lower than 0.40, Macro-F1 is even less than 0.07, which indicates that in the task facing 11 types of medical intentions, which is highly semantic dependent, simple sparse or average vectors are difficult to reveal the complex relationship between keywords and contexts; SVM improves the macro average precision (0.251) with the help of hard spaced hyperplane, but the recall is still limited (0.101), resulting in limited F1 improvement. After the introduction of TextCNN, the local n-gram semantics are captured to some extent, and the Macro Precision is improved to 0.213. However, the overall gain is still limited due to the length of the convolution window and the size of the corpus. After turning to the deep semantic model, there is a qualitative leap in performance: pure BERT fine-tuning can increase Accuracy and Macro-F1 to 0.802 and 0.764 respectively, indicating that context semantics plays a decisive role in intention classification; However, the reuse of BERT vectors by different subsequent classifiers did not bring about a costly global improvement. BERT + CNN and BERT + LR sacrificed their accuracy while the Macro Recall reached 0.816. Finally, F1 was slightly lower; Although BERT + RF is ahead of Macro Precision (0.787), its overall advantage is not obvious because of its recall to 0.781. After further using RoBERTa, which is more fully pre trained, all indicators are slightly better than BERT, but the increase is limited, indicating that the bottleneck is approaching only by deep semantic branches. In the mixed feature route, simple superimposed topic distribution (BERT + LDA + LR) brings serious dimensional disaster, precision recall declines both, and verification of undifferentiated splicing will dilute the discrimination information. In contrast, the deep shallow feature attention fusion model proposed in this paper significantly improves the macro average accuracy to 0.791 while maintaining a high recall rate (0.815) through the dynamic weighting of sentence level TF-IDF, category centroid TF-IDF, and RoBERTA semantic vectors. Finally, Macro-F1 reaches 0.800, Accuracy and Micro-F1 both reach 0.824, 2.4 pp, 3.6 pp, and 1.3 pp higher than the strongest single-mode RoBERTa, respectively. This shows that quasi prior and keyword clues can effectively enhance the ability to distinguish long tail categories, while the attention mechanism avoids information redundancy, thus achieving an optimal balance on multiple indicators.

From the category level indicators in Table 13, we can see that the model shows obvious head tail differentiation between different intentions: the main categories with large sample size and highly fixed semantic structure (such as “Therapy” and " Diagnosis”) and the standardized categories containing significant keywords (such as “Cost”) have achieved F1 score close to or more than 0.90, which shows the robust performance of deep semantic representation and shallow keyword features; On the contrary, categories with fuzzy semantic boundaries or strong emotional color (such as “Outcome” and “others”) become the main source of error. In general, deep shallow feature attention fusion has effectively improved the accuracy and recall of most categories, especially in small sample scenarios. However, for categories with semantic overlap or rich emotional expression, hierarchical tags, external medical knowledge or confidence screening mechanisms need to be further introduced to further reduce misjudgments and improve overall macro performance.

It can be seen from the confusion matrix in Fig. 3 that the model maintains a strong discriminant ability in most categories, and the counts of “Therapy”, “Cost”, “Advice” and other trunk categories on the diagonal are the most concentrated, almost without being misclassified, which verifies the high reliability of deep semantics and key word features. However, the error still presents a typical long tail migration phenomenon. On the one hand, the “Other” category spreads most seriously outward, with 108 samples being wrongly cast into other columns, indicating that open semantic tags still act as noise collectors in practical applications. On the other hand, categories with emotional or ambiguous semantic boundaries interfere with each other, in which “Outcome” was misjudged as “Other” 12 times and “DiseaseDesc” 1 time, reflecting the overlap between emotional inquiry and symptom description. There were 15 mutual errors between the precautions and the treatment plan, because they shared keywords such as “taking medicine, avoiding eating”. In addition, the “Effect” and “Cause” with the smallest sample size still maintain a high recall, which proves the reinforcement effect of sentence level and category centroid TF-IDF on the long tail category. In general, misjudgments mainly focus on the intersection of “other → multi category” and a few semantically similar categories. In the next step, these errors can be further compressed by subdividing “other” tags.

Discussion

This work examined a hybrid architecture that fuses deep contextual representations (RoBERTa-wwm-ext) with sentence-level TF-IDF and class-centroid TF-IDF via attention. The comparative study (Table 9) shows a clear advantage over traditional shallow models and single-branch deep encoders, with the proposed model attaining Accuracy = 0.824 and Macro-F1 = 0.800 on KUAKE-QIC. The ablation analysis (Table 11) indicates that both lexical branches contribute complementary gains (> 1 pp each in Macro-F1) and that adaptive attention is superior to naïve concatenation (+ 1.4 pp), underscoring the necessity of (i) explicit lexical priors and (ii) sample-wise feature re-weighting. Hyperparameter sensitivity (Table 12) further suggests that performance is most responsive to max input length and TF-IDF vocabulary size; with ultra-short medical queries, max_len = 256 and tfidf_max_features = 200 strike the best balance between semantic coverage and sparsity control, while larger vocabularies (≥ 400) introduce noise that depresses Macro-F1. Among attention variants, dot-product and multi-head (4) yield the strongest Macro-F1 (0.799) with comparable training time, supporting dot attention as a practical default.

At the label level (Table 13; Fig. 3), the model delivers near-ceiling performance on head or keyword-salient classes (e.g., Therapy, Diagnosis, Cost), confirming that deep semantics and keyword salience are jointly captured. The residual errors concentrate in semantically diffuse or affect-laden categories (e.g., Outcome, Other), where boundaries overlap with DiseaseDesc and sentiment-like intents. We also observe “long-tail migration” in the confusion matrix: Other disperses widely, acting as a sink for open-set queries; nevertheless, minority classes (Effect, Cause, Metric) benefit from the centroid prior and sentence-level TF-IDF, maintaining competitive F1 despite limited support. Based on the above analysis, further improvements can be made: (1) label space refinement (sub-typing Other), (2) knowledge-augmented features (medical ontologies, synonym maps) to reduce ambiguity, and (3) confidence-based rejection/handoff to human triage to mitigate high-risk misclassifications.

Generalisability and deployment. The method targets ultra-short Chinese medical queries with class imbalance. While the fusion design is model-agnostic, transferability to other domains/languages may depend on domain lexicons and centroid stability. For real-world use, the footprint remains moderate (single RoBERTa encoder + two lightweight projections + small MLP), and attention adds minimal latency; measured training/inference times in Table 12 suggest feasibility for online intent routing.

Limitations and future directions. (1) Single-dataset evaluation: results may overestimate in-domain performance; cross-dataset validation (e.g., other CBLUE tasks or real-world logs) is a priority. (2) Open-set and ambiguity: Other and Outcome remain challenging; hierarchical tagging and ontology-aware disambiguation are promising. (3) LLM comparison: when LLMs are available, few-shot prompting can be competitive; however, our hybrid remains attractive for efficiency, stability, and controllability. Future work will integrate structured medical knowledge (UMLS/CHIP ontologies), explore sequence-aware variants for dialogic contexts, and incorporate confidence calibration with selective prediction.

Conclusion

On the KUAKE-QIC benchmark, the proposed RoBERTa-wwm-ext + sentence TF-IDF + category-centric TF-IDF framework with attentive fusion delivers Macro-F1 = 0.800 and Accuracy = 0.824, validating the effectiveness of deep–shallow complementarity and adaptive weighting.

First constraints on causal sources of primordial gravitational waves from BICEP/Keck, SPTpol, SPT-3G, Planck and WMAP B B -mode data
Jessica A. Zebrowski, Aurora Ireland, Christian L. Reichardt, Kylar Greene, Gordan Krnjaic, Yuhsin Tsai, François R Bouchet

Abstract
Non-inflationary sources of gravitational waves in the early Universe generically predict causality-limited tensor power spectra at low frequencies. We report the first-ever constraints on such sources based on cosmic microwave background (CMB) B B -mode polarization measurements. Using data from BICEP/Keck, SPTpol, SPT-3G, Planck , and WMAP , we constrain the amplitude of an early causal tensor (ECT) power spectrum parameterized by r ect r_{\rm ect} , the ratio of causal tensor power to total scalar power at k = 0.01 k~=~0.01 Mpc -1 , and obtain a 95% CL upper limit of r ect < r_{\rm ect}{}< 0.0077 0.0077 . Since r ect r_{\rm ect} can easily be related to the parameters of a given theory, our bound robustly constrains a broad class of well-motivated gravitational wave sources in the early universe, including first-order cosmological phase transitions, enhanced small-scale density perturbations, and various topological defects. Finally, we translate our limit into a bound on the present-day energy density in gravitational waves at ultra-low frequencies otherwise inaccessible to traditional gravitational wave detection strategies, including pulsar timing arrays, interferometers, and resonant cavities.

I
Introduction

Tensor perturbations in the early universe imprint a distinctive
B
-mode pattern in the polarization of the cosmic microwave background (CMB), offering a powerful observational probe of fundamental physics at energy scales beyond the reach of
terrestrial gravitational wave (GW) experiments
18
.
While this connection has long been appreciated in the context of inflation, it has only recently been recognized that non-inflationary sources of primordial tensor perturbations can also yield observable
B
modes, potentially complicating interpretations of future measurements
13
;
16
;
33
. Examples of such sources include first-order cosmological phase transitions
19
;
8
, enhanced scalar density perturbations
10
, dark-photon perturbations from axion rolling
12
, primordial gravitational vector modes
20
, and various topological defects
21
;
26
;
36
;
15
.

Unlike inflation, which stretches sub-horizon tensor perturbations to super-horizon scales and predicts a nearly scale-invariant power spectrum, post-inflationary sources
are fundamentally limited by the impossibility of super-horizon correlations, and
therefore universally yield white-noise spectra on sufficiently large scales. For all such sources, the dimensionless tensor power spectrum exhibits a characteristic
\mathcal{P}_{h}(k)\propto k^{3}
scaling at small comoving wavenumber
k
. This behavior is a robust, model-independent consequence of causality and finite correlation lengths, and it holds universally for length scales larger than the physical extent of the source, provided that the latter is bounded in frequency and time
6
.

The characteristic
k^{3}
scaling will turn over at some
k
related to the correlation length of the source.
However if the tensor source is before
z\sim 7\times 10^{4}
, this turn over will be hidden in the CMB by Silk damping – the horizon size at
z\sim 7\times 10^{4}
matches the Silk damping scale.
Any non-inflationary source that generates GWs exclusively before this time necessarily predicts a white-noise power spectrum on CMB observable scales; this characteristic scaling yields a universal shape for the resulting
B
-mode angular power spectrum.
We refer to such phenomena as early causal tensor (ECT) sources.

Compared to inflationary predictions, ECT sources yield more power on small scales and less power on large scales.
This striking shape difference allows for clear discrimination between the signals from inflation and ECT sources, given sufficiently precise measurements across a range of angular scales.
While the ECT
B
-mode angular spectrum coincidentally resembles corresponding contributions from lensing, this degeneracy can be broken with external priors on lensing
B
modes. Furthermore, since all ECT sources predict the same
B
-mode spectral shape, the only parameter that distinguishes sources in this universality class is the overall amplitude, which we parametrize as
r_{\rm ect}
, the ratio of ECT tensor power to measured scalar power at the reference comoving wavenumber
k_{\rm ref}=0.01
Mpc
-1
.

In this
Letter
, we report the first constraints on the causal tensor power spectrum using contemporary CMB
B
-mode observations.
A companion work
14
explores models which produce such causal tensors.
Marginalizing over foreground and cosmological signals, we place the first upper limit on the amplitude of the causal tensor spectrum of
r_{\rm ect}{}<
0.0077
at 95% CL.
The limit constrains a broad class of theoretical models that source GWs in the post-inflationary universe (see Fig.
1
).
We also translate this limit to a model-dependent bound on the GW energy density,
\Omega_{\rm GW}
, at ultra-low frequencies.

II
General Framework

We briefly review the ECT framework presented by
14
, leaving details to that work.
A Fourier mode of the transverse, traceless tensor perturbation
h_{ij}(\tau,\vec{k})
evolves according to the wave equation

h^{\prime\prime}_{ij}+2{\cal H}h^{\prime}_{ij}+k^{2}h_{ij}=16\pi Ga^{2}\,\Pi_{ij}\,,

(1)

where
\tau
is conformal time,
′
denotes differentiation with respect to
\tau
,
a
is the scale factor, and
{\cal H}=a^{\prime}/a
is the conformal Hubble rate. The source
\Pi_{ij}(\tau,\vec{k})
is the transverse, traceless projection of the anisotropic stress tensor, whose functional form is model-dependent. We take
\Pi_{ij}
to be bounded in frequency and non-vanishing only transiently in the early universe for some characteristic time interval
\tau\in[\tau_{i},\tau_{\star}]
.

We decompose
h_{ij}
and
\Pi_{ij}
into the two polarizations
\lambda=(+,\times)
, which are uncorrelated and have the same spectra.
The solutions can be written as
h^{\lambda}(\tau,\vec{k})\equiv h^{\lambda}_{\star}(\vec{k})\mathcal{T}(\tau,\vec{k})
where
h^{\lambda}_{\star}(\vec{k})\equiv h^{\lambda}(\tau_{\star},\vec{k})

is the initial amplitude, and
\mathcal{T}(\tau,\vec{k})
is the tensor transfer function, which solves the sourceless version of Eq. (
1
) and governs the deterministic evolution of these perturbations in an expanding universe. The initial amplitude depends on the source and is obtained by solving Eq. (
1
),

h_{\star}^{\lambda}(\vec{k})=16\pi Ga^{2}\int_{\tau_{i}}^{\tau_{\star}}d\tau^{\prime}\,G_{k}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\Pi_{\lambda}(\tau^{\prime},\vec{k}),

(2)

where
G_{k}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})=\sin[k(\tau-\tau^{\prime})]/k
is the Green’s function for Eq. (
1
), neglecting Hubble expansion over the short duration of source activity. Using this solution, we define the (
dimensionless
) tensor power spectrum
\mathcal{P}_{h}
through

\left\langle h_{\star}^{\lambda}(\vec{k})\,h_{\star}^{\lambda^{\prime}}\!(\vec{k}^{\prime})^{*}\right\rangle=\frac{\delta_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}}{2}\frac{2\pi^{2}}{k^{3}}\mathcal{P}_{h}(k)(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{(3)}(\vec{k}-\vec{k}^{\prime})\,,

(3)

where
\langle\dots\rangle
denotes an ensemble average and
*
is complex conjugation.

For any temporally-bounded source active on some characteristic sub-horizon scale
k_{s}
during radiation domination,

\mathcal{P}_{h}(k)\propto k^{3}
for all
k\ll k_{s}

6
;
14
,
which reflects the causality requirement that sufficiently-separated spatial points must be uncorrelated. Thus, the power spectrum of any causal non-inflationary source must decay as white noise on super-horizon length scales.
Given this, we parametrize the input power spectrum as

\mathcal{P}_{h}(k)\equiv r_{\rm ect}{}A_{s}\left(\frac{k}{k_{\rm ref}}\right)^{3}\,,

(4)

where
k_{\rm ref}=0.01
Mpc
-1
is a reference wavenumber,
A_{s}=2.1\times 10^{-9}
is the measured amplitude of scalar fluctuations
25
, and
r_{\rm ect}
is the amplitude of the white-noise tensor power spectrum at
k_{\rm ref}
in units of
A_{s}
.
This notation follows the long-standing convention to use the tensor-to-scalar ratio
r=A_{T}/A_{s}
as a measure of inflationary tensor modes.
For the chosen
k_{\rm ref}=0.01
Mpc
-1
, the amplitude of the
B
-mode signals from inflationary and causal GWs approximately match at the recombination peak (
\ell\sim 100
) when
r=r_{\rm ect}{}
.

III
Likelihoods

We search for causal GWs using
B
-mode power spectrum measurements from BICEP/Keck
5
, SPT-3G
41
, and SPTpol
30
. While these experiments have overlapping sky coverage, the SPT error bars are noise dominated.
Thus, we treat them as independent when combining the likelihoods.
These three measurements are chosen as each has angular multipoles where they provide the tightest current statistical constraints.

III.1
BICEP/Keck

We use the BICEP/Keck
B
-mode likelihood presented in
5
, incorporating measurements through the 2018 observing season. The likelihood includes cross-spectra between all frequency bands from BICEP2, Keck Array (95, 150, and 220 GHz), BICEP3 (95 GHz), and external maps from
Planck
(30–353 GHz) and
WMAP
(23–94 GHz). The analysis jointly fits for CMB and foreground contributions, marginalizing over synchrotron and dust components.
We use the public “BK18” likelihood, which combines the
BB
cross-spectra across all instruments to optimize foreground separation and maximize sensitivity to primordial
B
modes at large angular scales.
These data currently yield the most precise measurement of the
B
-mode power spectrum at
\ell\lesssim 300
.

III.2
SPT-3G

We use the SPT-3G
B
-mode likelihood presented by
41
for power spectrum measurements over the multipole range
32<\ell<502
. These measurements are from 95, 150, and 220 GHz observations of the SPT-3G 1500 deg
2
patch observed from 2019-2020. Following the main cosmological results in
41
, we focus on a subset of the sky overlapping with the BICEP/Keck survey area with the cleanest foregrounds. We use the same nuisance parameters as
41
.
These parameters include three absolute polarization calibration terms (one per frequency band), a Poisson term for radio galaxies with amplitude
A^{\mathrm{95\,GHz,rg}}_{\ell=500}
, and three parameters for Galactic dust: the amplitude
A^{\mathrm{150\,GHz}}_{\ell=80,\mathrm{SPT-3G}}
, angular power-law index
\alpha_{\mathrm{SPT-3G}}
, and spectral index
\beta_{\mathrm{SPT-3G}}
of a modified blackbody spectrum.
The SPT-3G data provide the best measurement at
300<\ell<500
.

III.3
SPTpol

We use the SPTpol
B
-mode power spectrum measurement reported by
30
. The SPTpol bandpowers cover the angular multipole range
52\leq\ell\leq 2301
.
While
30
measure
B
-mode polarization in two bands, here we only use the higher signal-to-noise 150 GHz power spectrum.
We use the same four nuisance parameters as
30
, including Galactic dust terms, Poisson radio galaxy emission, and the absolute polarization calibration.
We neglect the SPTpol beam uncertainty, given the modest signal-to-noise of the
B
-mode measurement.
The SPTpol data provide the best
B
-mode measurement at
\ell>500
.

III.4
Model

We model the observed
B
-mode power spectrum as the sum of contributions from inflationary GW, ECT sources, lensing-induced
B
modes, and frequency-dependent Galactic and extragalactic foregrounds,

{\cal D}_{\ell}^{\rm BB}={\cal D}_{\ell}^{\mathrm{IGW}}(r)+{\cal D}_{\ell}^{\mathrm{ECT{}}}(r_{\rm ect}{})+A_{\mathrm{LT}}\,{\cal D}_{\ell}^{\mathrm{lens}}+{\cal D}_{\ell}^{\mathrm{fg}}\,,

(6)

where
r
is the tensor-to-scalar ratio at
k=0.05\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}
,
r_{\rm ect}
is the ratio of ECT power to scalar power at
k=0.01
Mpc
-1
,
{\cal D}_{\ell}^{\mathrm{ECT{}}}
is the signal from Eq. (
5
), and
A_{\mathrm{LT}}
is a free amplitude parameter that rescales the lensing
B
-mode template
{\cal D}_{\ell}^{\mathrm{lens}}
.

{\cal D}_{\ell}^{\mathrm{fg}}
describes the foreground components in each of the likelihoods described above.
The predicted spectra for
r=0.02
,
r_{\rm ect}{}=0.02
, and
A_{\rm LT}=1
are shown in Fig.
2
.

We calculate posteriors using a positive-definite, uniform prior on
r
and
r_{\rm ect}
over the interval
[0,1]
.
The lensing amplitude is assumed to be drawn from Gaussian distribution
N(1.013,0.023^{2})
, based on the lensing measurement by
27
, to encapsulate changes to the spectrum of lensing
B
modes from different cosmologies.

The likelihood for each dataset marginalizes over the independent calibration and foreground parameters in each experiments’ likelihood.
An analysis with a combined foreground model is beyond the scope of this work.
All
\Lambda
CDM cosmological parameters are set to the
Planck
2018 best-fit values
25
. The parameters
r
,
r_{\rm ect}{}
, and
A_{\mathrm{LT}}
are sampled using Markov chain Monte Carlo with the
Cobaya
package
(
34
)
.

IV
Upper limits on Early Causal Tensors

Current CMB
B
-mode data place tight limits on the amplitude of tensor perturbations due to ECT sources in the early universe. We jointly constrain the amplitude of power from inflationary and causal GWs while marginalizing over the lensing
B
modes and foreground terms using the model described in Eq. (
6
). The posterior distributions inferred from the combination of BICEP/Keck, SPTpol, SPT-3G,
Planck
, and
WMAP
data are presented in Fig.
3

We find no statistically-significant detection of either
r
or
r_{\rm ect}{}
, and the posteriors for both parameters are consistent with zero.

We obtain upper limits at 95% confidence of
r_{\rm ect}{}<

0.0077
and
r<

0.033
, with
A_{\rm LT}
tightly constrained by the prior from
27

Model lines at the levels of the 95% upper limits on
r_{\rm ect}
and
r
as well as the SPT-3G, SPTpol, and BK18 150 GHz bandpowers are shown in Fig.
4
. The constraint on
r_{\rm ect}{}
is driven primarily by the absence of excess power at
300<\ell<2301

With
B
-mode data alone, the lensing and causal
B
-mode signals are significantly degenerate due to their similar angular dependence.
The prior on lensing
B
-mode power from
27

r_{\rm ect}

3
,
r
and
r_{\rm ect}
are largely uncorrelated due to peaking at different angular scales.

\Omega_{\rm GW}\equiv\rho^{-1}_{\rm c}d\rho_{\rm GW}/d\ln f,

where
\rho_{\rm c}
is the critical density,

\rho_{\rm GW}=\sum_{\lambda}\langle h_{\lambda}^{\prime}(\tau,\vec{x})^{2}\rangle/(8\pi Ga^{2}),

is the energy density in GWs

\Omega_{\rm GW}=\frac{1}{\rho_{\rm c}}\frac{k^{2}\mathcal{P}_{h}(\tau,k)}{8\pi Ga^{2}}\,,

(7)

\mathcal{T}

\Omega_{\rm GW}h^{2}(f)=\frac{h^{2}}{3}\left(\frac{k}{H_{0}}\right)^{2}\mathcal{P}_{h}(\tau_{0},k)\,,

This result demonstrates the power of CMB observables to test models whose signals elude traditional GW probes.

VI
Conclusion

In this
Letter
, we have set the first-ever
B
-mode limits on causal sources of primordial GWs that predict white-noise power on CMB scales. We use a multifrequency multicomponent likelihood using BICEP/Keck, SPTpol, SPT-3G,
Planck
, and
WMAP
across angular scales spanning
20<\ell<2301
. We fit the data to a model consisting of inflationary GWs, ECT sources, lensing-induced
B
modes, and Galactic and extragalactic foregrounds, and find
r_{\rm ect}{}<

0.0077
at 95% confidence.

Looking forward, CMB
B
-mode measurements can also be used to study the broader class of models that deviate from the simple
k^{3}
white-noise template studied here. Many well-motivated scenarios
predict tensor power spectra that peak and turn over within the range of scales probed by current CMB
B
-mode measurements (see Fig.
1
).
Starting with the generic
k^{3}
template allows a model-agnostic upper bound on causal GWs that can be applied to many models that generate tensor perturbations in the early universe. Future work can now build on this foundation by targeting more specific, cosmologically compelling models.

Finally, we note that our results place world-leading limits on the lowest GW frequencies ever probed,
f\sim 10^{-19}
Hz, assuming only that the source is non-inflationary and generates GW at redshift
z\gtrsim 7\times 10^{4}
. Such frequencies are currently beyond the reach of other GW experiments, and these limits demonstrate the versatility of
B
-mode data when probing for new-physics signals.

A lung cancer diagnosis and treatment dataset with geno- and phenotypical characteristics of the patient
Authors: Belén Ríos-Sánchez, Guillermo Vigueras, Aaron García, Daniel Gómez-Bravo, Ernestina Menasalvas, María Torrente, Consuelo Parejo, Fotis Aisopos, Dimitrios Vogiatzis, Disha Purohit, Mariano Provencio, María-Esther Vidal, Alejandro Rodríguez-González
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis dataset comprises information about 1242 lung cancer patients collected by the Medical Oncology Department of the Puerta de Hierro University Hospital of Majadahonda in Madrid, Spain. It includes information about cancer diagnosis and treatment, as well as personal and medical data recorded during anamneses. The dataset could assist in data analysis with the aim of discovering relationships between the applied treatment(s), the evolution of the disease and the associated adverse effects. A greater understanding of treatment effects based on the particular conditions of the patient and the diagnosis could directly impact the healthcare system, helping to improve expectations about lung cancer as well as reducing treatment toxicities and adverse effects.Keywords: Lung cancer, Diagnosis, Treatment, Disease evolution, Adverse effects

Background

Lung cancer (LC) remains the main cause of cancer-related death in the world. More than 234,000 new cases of LC and more than 125,000 deaths are estimated to occur in 2024 [1]. In recent years, great advances have been made in LC treatments and screening, incorporating new agents, like immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) or targeted treatments, into the existing chemotherapy, radiotherapy and surgery. These new agents have dramatically changed the therapeutic options of non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC) patients, with an increase in overall survival, but their response strongly varies between patients. Moreover, cancer treatments can cause adverse effects, which vary depending on factors such as the stage of the cancer and the combination of treatments received [2]. Therefore, selecting the most appropriate treatment in each case considerably increases the chances of survival and improvement in quality of life.

With the purpose of optimizing patient care and assisting clinicians in LC treatments, the best practices and knowledge accumulated by oncologists are collected in clinical guidelines (CG) [3]. However, NSCLC is a very complex disease, and treatment efficacy might be impacted by patient and disease aspects that are not included in the CG [4,5]. Data-driven approaches can be used to analyze how variability in patients and disease characteristics can affect cancer outcomes [6,7]. Our data provide relevant information to support discovering novel treatments that can help at profiling patients based on their characteristics and the different treatments that they have been taking. It will impact the healthcare system by supporting better treatment selection decisions, thus reducing toxicities and adverse effects, and increasing treatment effectiveness. Moreover, conscious decisions will have a direct effect on treatment costs, e.g. by reducing the number of visits and unnecessary tests, and increasing quality of life and employability of the patients.

Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

The Medical Oncology Department of the Puerta de Hierro University Hospital of Majadahonda (HUPHM) collected information about 1242 lung cancer patients between 30 and 90 years old, and stored it in their Electronic Health Records (EHRs). This information describes all phases of the diagnosis and treatment, as well as personal and medical data recorded during anamneses. Particularly, it contains information about the patient, the diagnosis, the treatments, and the evolution of the disease.

The information coming from the EHRs was included in RedCap [8], a secure and flexible software platform designed to manage and store research data, which is widely used in academic, medical, and research settings for data collection and management in scientific and clinical studies. The use of RedCap allows the download of data anytime for its analysis and use as a structured file, including all variables of interest and a data dictionary.

The structured information collected by HUPHM was then processed to obtain cleaned and curated data. Potential errors were detected, reported to HUPHM and fixed. Data consistency was ensured by implementing a set of integrity rules. Finally, the variables approved by the HUPHM ethics committee for publication were packaged in a CSV file.

Limitations

After data curation and cleaning, a few patients still present certain inconsistencies that could not be corrected: treatment without initial and/or final date, treatment with an initial date after the final date, relapse without date, diagnosis without date, treatment date after death date, treatment date prior to diagnosis date or relapse date equal to diagnosis date. These inconsistencies could affect temporal data analysis, which would require excluding inconsistent patients, but the dataset contains wide and varied information which can be analysed in different ways. For this reason, it was decided not to discard inconsistent patients but to store all the available information.

Ethics Statement

Data collection was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Puerta de Hierro University Hospital of Majadahonda, Madrid, Spain (record number: 5/2020 from 03/09/2020). Written informed consent was signed by every patient included in the study. The procedure was carried out in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and adheres to the ethical guidelines of Data in Brief.

Enhancement of Curie Temperature in Ferromagnetic Insulator-Topological Insulator Heterostructures Preprint: APS/123-QED
Murod Mirzhalilov, Nandini Trivedi, Mohit Randeria

Abstract
We theoretically analyze the topological insulator (TI) surface state mediated interactions between local moments in a proximate 2D ferromagnetic insulator (FMI) motivated by recent experiments that show a significant increase in the Curie temperature T c T_{c} of FMI-TI heterostructures. Such interactions have been investigated earlier with a focus on dilute magnetic dopants in TIs. Our problem involves a dense set of moments for which we find that the short range Bloembergen-Rowland interaction, arising from virtual particle-hole transitions between the valence and conduction bands, dominates over the oscillatory Ruderman-Kittel-Kasuya-Yosida (RKKY) interaction. We show that the T c T_{c} enhancement is proportional to the Van Vleck susceptibility and that the spin-momentum locking of surface states leads to out-of-plane ferromagnetic order in the FMI. We investigate how the hybridization between top and bottom surfaces in a thin TI film impacts T c T_{c} enhancement, and show how our results can help understand recent experiments on atomically thin Cr 2 Te 3 -(Bi,Sb) 2 Te 3 .

The standard theory for magnetism mediated by conduction electrons is that developed by Ruderman-Kittel-Kasuya-Yosida (RKKY)
Ruderman and Kittel 1954
;
Kasuya 1956
;
Yosida 1957
which leads to the well-known oscillatory interactions with a power-law decay arising from the sharp Fermi surface. Bloembergen and Rowland (BR)
Bloembergen and Rowland 1955
,
showed, in a very different context, that an exchange interaction persists even in the absence of a Fermi surface, i.e. even when the chemical potential lies within a gap.
The BR mechanism, on the other hand, results in an exchange interaction between the moments that decays as a power-law at short distances and cuts off exponentially on a length scale determined by the gap. It is mediated by virtual electron-hole transitions across the gap from the filled valence band to the empty conduction band.

These ideas have been extended to magnetically doped TIs
Liu et al. 2009
;
Ye et al. 2010
;
Biswas and Balatsky 2010
;
Garate and Franz 2010
;
Zhu et al. 2011
;
Efimkin and Galitski 2014
;
Zyuzin and Loss 2014
but, as explained in greater detail below, the focus previously in the literature was on a quite different regime than the one we analyze for our problem of local moments in a FMI. Briefly, the differences are as follows. Consider two local moments in the FMI separated by
{\bf R}
. For computing the
T_{c}
enhancement, we need to sum the exchange couplings over all
{\bf R}
and obtain the
{\bf Q}=0
response of the susceptibility. Thus we are not just interested in the long-distance asymptotics of the interaction, as the response on the scale of the lattice spacing
a
also plays an important role in our analysis. Furthermore, for finite doping on the TI surface states with a chemical potential that lies within the band, it is necessary to include the exchange interaction contributions between moments with separation
R\ll 1/k_{F}
. This is the opposite of the dilute magnetic dopants with
k_{F}R\gg 1
of interest in previous works
Garate and Franz 2010
;
Zhu et al. 2011
;
Zyuzin and Loss 2014
.

II
Model Hamiltonian and Exchange interaction

We begin with the 2D problem of the surface states of a TI film interacting with the local moments of a proximate FMI.
The low-energy Hamiltonian describing the TI surface states is given by
Hasan and Kane 2010
;
Qi and Zhang 2011

H_{0}=\hbar v_{F}(\bm{k}\times\bm{\sigma})\cdot\hat{z}+\Delta\sigma_{z},

(1)

where
v_{F}
is the Fermi velocity,
\bm{k}=(k_{x},k_{y})
is the wavevector, and

\bm{\sigma}=(\sigma_{x},\sigma_{y},\sigma_{z})
are the Pauli spin matrices.
\Delta
opens a gap at the Dirac point as
a result of the broken TR symmetry of the FM order at the interface.
H_{0}
describes the system on energy scales smaller than
the bandwidth
W=\hbar v_{F}/a
, where
a
is the lattice spacing.

To understand the exchange interaction mediated by the surface states, we first consider the simple problem of two (classical) magnetic moments

\bm{S}_{i}
located at
\bm{R}_{i}
(
i=1,2
) that interact with the electrons described by
H_{0}(\bm{k})
;
later we will generalize to a lattice of local moments. This system is described by the Hamiltonian

H=H_{0}+H_{\rm int},\ \ \ H_{\rm int}=Ja^{2}\sum_{i}\bm{\sigma}\cdot\bm{S}_{i}\,\delta(\bm{r}-\bm{R}_{i}),

(2)

where the energy scale
J
is an isotropic Kondo or s-d coupling between the Dirac electron spin and the local moments,
which are treated as classical spins.

Treating
H_{\rm int}
as a perturbation to second order in
J/W\ll 1
we obtain
the exchange interaction between moments mediated by itinerant electrons

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\rm ex}=-{J^{2}\over W}\sum_{\alpha\beta}\chi_{\alpha\beta}(\bm{R}_{1}-\bm{R}_{2})S_{1}^{\alpha}S_{2}^{\beta};

(3)

see Appendix
A
for details. Here
\chi_{\alpha\beta}(\bm{R})
is the spatially-dependent static spin susceptibility
which, at
T=0
, is given by

\chi_{\alpha\beta}(\bm{R})=\frac{W}{\pi}{\rm Im}\int^{\mu}_{-\infty}d\omega\text{Tr}[\sigma_{\alpha}G(\bm{R},\omega^{+})\sigma_{\beta}G(-\bm{R},\omega^{+})]

(4)

where
(\alpha,\beta)=(x,y,z)
, the trace is taken over spin,
\mu
is the chemical potential, and

G(\bm{R},\omega^{+})
is the retarded (
\omega^{+}=\omega+i0^{+}
) Green’s function for the electrons in the TI surface states. The bandwidth factor
W
is introduced in the above equations so that
\chi
is dimensionless. We focus on
T=0
here because temperature has a minimal effect on the exchange couplings
so long as
k_{B}T\ll W
as shown in Appendix
F
.

The form of
G(\bm{R},\omega^{+})
is well known
Zhu et al. 2011
;
Abanin and Pesin 2011
;
Efimkin and Galitski 2014
, and for completeness
we discuss in Appendix
B
. Using this Eq.(
3
) can be rewritten in the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{ex}=

\displaystyle-J_{zz}(\bm{R})S^{z}_{1}S^{z}_{2}-J_{\perp}(\bm{R})S_{1}^{\perp}S_{2}^{\perp}-J_{\|}(\bm{R})S^{\|}_{1}S^{\|}_{2}

(5)

\displaystyle-J_{DM}(\bm{R})(\hat{\bm{R}}\times\hat{z})\cdot(\bm{S}_{1}\times\bm{S}_{2})

where
S_{i}^{\perp}=\bm{S}_{i}\cdot(\hat{\bm{R}}\times\hat{z})
,
S^{\|}_{i}=\bm{S}_{i}\cdot\hat{\bm{R}}
and
\hat{\bm{R}}=\bm{R}/R
, where
\bm{R}=\bm{R}_{1}-\bm{R}_{2}
.
The first term is an Ising interaction, the next two terms involving
S_{i}^{\perp}
and
S_{i}^{\|}
describe anisotropic exchange couplings,
and the last term is the Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya (DM) interaction.
The anisotropic nature of the interactions between local moments originates from the spin-momentum locking of the TI surface state electrons
Abanin and Pesin 2011
.

The couplings in Eq.(
5
) are given by

\displaystyle J_{zz}(\bm{R})

\displaystyle=\frac{J^{2}}{8\pi W^{4}}{\rm Im}\int_{-\infty}^{\mu}d\omega\Big[(\mathcal{E}^{2}-2\Delta^{2})\text{H}_{0}^{(1)}(i\rho)^{2}

(6)

\displaystyle-\mathcal{E}^{2}\text{H}_{1}^{(1)}(i\rho)^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle J_{\perp}(\bm{R})

\displaystyle=\frac{J^{2}}{8\pi W^{4}}{\rm Im}\int_{-\infty}^{\mu}d\omega\mathcal{E}^{2}\Big[\text{H}_{0}^{(1)}(i\rho)^{2}+\text{H}_{1}^{(1)}(i\rho)^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle J_{\|}(\bm{R})

\displaystyle=\frac{J^{2}}{8\pi W^{4}}{\rm Im}\int_{-\infty}^{\mu}d\omega\mathcal{E}^{2}\Big[\text{H}_{0}^{(1)}(i\rho)^{2}-\text{H}_{1}^{(1)}(i\rho)^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle J_{DM}(\bm{R})

\displaystyle=\frac{J^{2}}{4\pi W^{4}}{\rm Im}\int_{-\infty}^{\mu}d\omega\Big[i\omega^{+}\mathcal{E}\text{H}_{0}^{(1)}(i\rho)\text{H}_{1}^{(1)}(i\rho)\Big]

Here
\text{H}^{(1)}_{0,1}
denotes the zeroth and first order Hankel function of the first kind and

\rho=\mathcal{E}R/\hbar v_{F}
where
\mathcal{E}=\sqrt{\Delta^{2}-(\omega^{+})^{2}}
.
Eqs. (
5
) and (
6
) generalize the results of ref.
Zhu et al. 2011
to finite
\Delta
.

IV
Lattice of magnetic moments

Our goal is to see how the TI surface-state-mediated interactions impact the magnetism in 2D FMI film coupled to the TI.
We first look at the TI surface-state mediated interactions between local moments
without taking into account the exchange couplings intrinsic to the FMI. At the next stage, we
include the interactions that lead to ferromagnetism in the FMI even in the absence of the TI.

We write down the simplest 2D lattice Hamiltonian that generalizes Eq.(

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{ex}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}\Big[

Ground State and Excitation Spectrum

Given that the Hamiltonian (

First, we have exactly minimized the Hamiltonian for a small cluster of spins to find that the FM ground state.
Second, we have checked the local stability of the FM ground state by performing a spin-wave analysis.
We find that the spin wave spectrum is gapped at all
\bm{q}

of the form

We also find that Ising anisotropy

IV.2
T_{c}
Enhancement

We now turn to the analysis of how the BR coupling enhances the
T_{c}
of the FMI. We make no attempt to make a microscopic model of
the intrinsic ferromagnetic coupling in the FMI. Since a 2D FMI film orders, we simply model it as a nearest-neighbor Ising model

{\cal H}_{I}=-1/2\sum_{i,j}J_{I}S^{z}_{i}S^{z}_{j}
with a FM coupling
J_{I}
; the subscript
I
denotes the intrinsic Ising coupling in the insulator.
This simplification plays no role in our analysis of
T_{c}
enhancement, as it only determines the intrinsic
T_{c}
of the FMI.
The full Hamiltonian is
{\cal H}={\cal H}_{I}+\mathcal{H}_{ex}
, where
\mathcal{H}_{ex}
is the TI surface state mediated BR interaction
of Eqs. (
8
) and (
9
).

We analyze the system within a simple mean-field theory, which we recognize will give an overestimate of
T_{c}
, especially in 2D.
With spins ordering in the
z
-direction, the thermal averages
\braket{S^{\|}_{\bm{R}}}=\braket{S^{\perp}_{\bm{R}}}=0
, and
we can write the mean field Hamiltonian as

\mathcal{H}=-1/2\sum_{\bm{r}}S^{z}_{\bm{r}}B^{z}_{\bm{r}}

with

B^{z}_{\bm{r}}=\sum_{\bm{R}}[J_{I}\delta_{\bm{R},\bm{\gamma}}+J_{zz}(\bm{R})]\braket{S^{z}_{\bm{r}+\bm{R}}}

For a ferromagnetic state where the magnetization
\braket{S^{z}_{\bm{R}}}
is spatially independent, the

reduces to
\small{\cal Z}J_{I}+J_{zz}(\bm{Q}=0)

The VV susceptibility is considered to be the primary mechanism driving magnetic order in TI films doped with magnetic impurities, and is greatly enhanced by band inversion

Wang et al. 2015

Here, we find that it also plays a crucial role in enhancing

To estimate
\delta T_{c}
we compute the exchange coupling

However, we do not need to put in any other thermal effects in the exchange coupling expressions.
In Appendix

VII
Conclusions

To summarize our main conclusions, we have investigated how interactions mediated by the surface states of a topological insulator (TI) influence the magnetism in an adjacent two-dimensional ferromagnetic insulator (FMI). We model the TI surface states as Dirac electrons that may
acquire a mass term induced by magnetic ordering.
Since the magnetic moments are arranged on the FMI lattice, our analysis focuses on effective interactions on the scale of the lattice constant. The chemical potential lies either within the gap or corresponds to a small Fermi wave vector
k_{F}
.
Consequently, the regime of interest here differs substantially from previous studies that primarily considered dilute magnetic impurities on TI surfaces.

We show that in this regime, the Bloembergen-Rowland (BR) interaction arising from virtual excitations between the valence and conduction bands dominates over the more familiar oscillatory Ruderman- Kittel- Kasuya- Yosida (RKKY) interaction, which originates from the Fermi surface. Due to the spin–momentum locking of TI surface states, the BR interaction exhibits a characteristic anisotropy that favors out-of-plane magnetic ordering in the FMI. Our mean-field analysis further shows that the Curie temperature
T_{c}
of the FMI is enhanced by an amount proportional to the Van Vleck susceptibility of the TI surface electrons. We also analyze the effect of hybridization between the top and bottom surfaces of a thin TI film and provide a quantitative comparison with recent experiments on
Cr
2
Te
3
/(Bi,Sb)
2
Te
3
heterostructures.

In summary, our analysis highlights the interplay between topological surface states of a TI and magnetism in FMI-TI heterostructures and gives insight into the experimentally observed enhancement of the FM
T_{c}
. These findings pave the way for further exploration and optimization of such heterostructures for potential applications in spintronics and quantum technologies.

The second piece can be written in terms of the special Meijer-G function
\mathbb{G}_{m,n}^{p,q}

Gradshteyn and Ryzhik 2014

\displaystyle\text{Im}\int_{0}^{\mu}d\omega(\omega^{+})^{2}\text{H}_{n}^{(1)}(iR\sqrt{-(\omega^{+})^{2}}/\hbar v_{F})^{2}=

(40)

\displaystyle-\frac{\mu^{3}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\mathbb{G}_{2,4}^{2,1}\left(\begin{matrix}&-\frac{1}{2}&\frac{1}{2}\\

\displaystyle J_{zz}(\bm{R})

0&0&-\frac{3}{2}&0\end{matrix}\Big|\begin{matrix}(k_{F}R)^{2}\end{matrix}\right)\Big]\Big)

\displaystyle J_{\perp}(\bm{R})

\displaystyle=-\frac{J_{K}^{2}}{32\pi\hbar v_{F}R^{3}}\Big(1+\frac{4}{\sqrt{\pi}}(k_{F}R)^{3}

\displaystyle\times\Big[\mathbb{G}_{2,4}^{2,1}\left(\begin{matrix}&-\frac{1}{2}&\frac{1}{2}\\
0&1&-\frac{3}{2}&-1\end{matrix}\Big|\begin{matrix}(k_{F}R)^{2}\end{matrix}\right)

Appendix D
Ground State and Excitation Spectrum of BR-Coupled Lattice of Spins

In this appendix, we determine the magnetic ordering of the BR-coupled lattice by (i) minimizing the few-spin energetics and (ii) computing the spin-wave spectrum of the full Hamiltonian in Eq. (
8
).

Two spins.
For a nearest-neighbor bond
R=a\ll R_{\Delta}
,

\mathcal{H}_{\rm ex}=-\tilde{J}_{0}S_{1}^{z}S_{2}^{z}+\frac{\tilde{J}_{0}}{2}S_{1}^{\perp}S_{2}^{\perp}-\tilde{J}_{0}S_{1}^{\parallel}S_{2}^{\parallel}

(42)

where
\tilde{J}_{0}=J_{0}/16\pi
is the exchange constant and
\parallel
and
\perp
are defined relative to
\hat{\bm{R}}
. Minimization gives two degenerate ferromagnetic minima with energy
-\tilde{J}_{0}S^{2}
: (i) out-of-plane (OOP) alignment
(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})=(0,0)
or
(\pi,\pi)
, and (ii) in-plane (IP) alignment along the bond,
(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})=(\tfrac{\pi}{2},\tfrac{\pi}{2})
with
(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})=(\theta_{R},\theta_{R})
or
(\theta_{R}{+}\pi,\theta_{R}{+}\pi)
where
\theta_{R}=\tan^{-1}(R_{y}/R_{x})
. Antiferromagnetic alignment is maximal in energy,
+\tilde{J}_{0}S^{2}
.

Beyond two spins.
The IP state depends on bond direction and becomes frustrated once additional, non-collinear bonds are present. For three spins on an equilateral triangle of side
a
, the ground state is OOP ferromagnetic with energy
-3\tilde{J}_{0}S^{2}
, whereas the best coplanar IP configuration at
(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3})=(\tfrac{\pi}{2},\tfrac{\pi}{2},\tfrac{\pi}{2})
reaches only
-\tfrac{15}{8}\tilde{J}_{0}S^{2}
. Hence, in 2D the BR coupling selects an OOP ferromagnet.

Spin waves.
Expanding about the uniform OOP state (
S_{x,y}\ll S
) and using the Landau-Lifshitz equation

,

\frac{\partial\bm{S}(\bm{r},t)}{\partial t}=\bm{S}(\bm{r},t)\times\!\left[-\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}_{\rm ex}}{\delta\bm{S}(\bm{r},t)}\right],

with the plane-wave ansatz
S_{x}=A\,e^{i(\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}-\omega t)}

, the linearized spectrum is

\displaystyle=J_{\parallel}(\bm{R})-\!\big[J_{\parallel}(\bm{R})-J_{\perp}(\bm{R})\big]\cos^{2}\theta_{R},

(45)

\displaystyle J_{2}(\bm{R})

\displaystyle=J_{\perp}(\bm{R})+\!\big[J_{\parallel}(\bm{R})-J_{\perp}(\bm{R})\big]\cos^{2}\theta_{R},

\displaystyle J_{3}(\bm{R})

\displaystyle=\big[J_{\parallel}(\bm{R})-J_{\perp}(\bm{R})\big]\sin\theta_{R}\cos\theta_{R},

44

\omega(\bm{q})
across the Brillouin zone for various exchange gaps

\bm{q}=0

Appendix E
BR Interaction for Thin TI films with Surface State Hybridization

Our goal here is to derive the static susceptibility
\chi^{tt}_{zz}(\bm{R})
of electrons on the top surface of a thin TI film taking into account hybridization effects with the bottom surface. The static spin susceptibility at T=0 including the effect of surface hybridization effect is given as
Coleman 2015
;
Shiranzaei et al. 2017

\displaystyle\chi_{\alpha\beta}^{\nu\nu^{\prime}}=

\displaystyle\frac{W}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{0}d\omega

(46)

\displaystyle\times\text{Tr}[\sigma_{\alpha}G_{\nu\nu^{\prime}}(\bm{R},\omega^{+})\sigma_{\beta}G_{\nu^{\prime}\nu}(-\bm{R},\omega^{+})]

with
(\alpha,\beta)=(x,y,z)
the spin components and
(\nu,\nu^{\prime})=(t,b)
denoting top and bottom surfaces. Here, we set
\mu=0
for BR interaction. The momentum space retarded Green’s function can be calculated as
G(\bm{k},\omega^{+})=[\omega^{+}\tau_{0}\otimes\sigma_{0}-\tau_{z}\otimes\hbar v_{F}(\bm{k}\times\bm{\sigma})\cdot\hat{z}-m_{\bm{k}}\tau_{x}\otimes\sigma_{0}]^{-1}
, from which we obtain

G(\bm{k},\omega^{+})=\frac{\omega^{+}+\hbar v_{F}(k_{y}\tau_{z}\sigma_{x}-k_{x}\tau_{z}\sigma_{y})+m_{\bm{k}}\tau_{x}\sigma_{0}}{(\omega^{+})^{2}-\epsilon_{\bm{k}}^{2}}

(47)

where
\epsilon_{\bm{k}}=\pm\sqrt{\hbar^{2}v_{F}^{2}k^{2}+m_{\bm{k}}^{2}}
and
m_{\bm{k}}=m_{0}-m_{1}k^{2}
. For two moments placed on the top surface, we are only interested in
G_{tt}(\bm{k},\omega^{+})
. To obtain the real space retarded Green’s function
G_{tt}(\bm{R},\omega^{+})
, we need to evaluate integrals of the form

\displaystyle Q_{0}(\bm{R},\omega^{+})

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d^{2}\bm{k}}{(2\pi/a)^{2}}e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{R}}\frac{\omega^{+}}{(\omega^{+})^{2}-\epsilon_{\bm{k}}^{2}}

(48)

\displaystyle\bm{Q}_{1}(\bm{R},\omega^{+})

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d^{2}\bm{k}}{(2\pi/a)^{2}}e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{R}}\frac{\hbar v_{F}\bm{k}}{(\omega^{+})^{2}-\epsilon_{\bm{k}}^{2}}

Similarly to Appendix
B
, we obtain

\displaystyle Q_{0}(\bm{R},\omega^{+})

\displaystyle=\frac{ia^{2}\omega^{+}}{8|m_{1}|\sqrt{(\omega^{+})^{2}+\Delta_{0}^{2}/4}}\times

(49)

\displaystyle\times\Big[\text{H}_{0}^{(1)}(k_{1}R)-\text{H}_{0}^{(1)}(k_{2}R)\Big]

\displaystyle\bm{Q}_{1}(\bm{R},\omega^{+})

\displaystyle=\hat{\bm{R}}\frac{\hbar v_{F}a^{2}}{8|m_{1}|\sqrt{(\omega^{+})^{2}+\Delta_{0}^{2}/4}}\times

\displaystyle\times\Big[k_{2}\text{H}_{1}^{(1)}(k_{2}R)-k_{1}\text{H}_{1}^{(1)}(k_{1}R)\Big]

where
k_{1,2}=i\sqrt{k_{0}^{2}\mp\frac{1}{|m_{1}|}\sqrt{(\omega^{+})^{2}+\Delta_{0}^{2}/4}}
with
k_{0}=\sqrt{(\hbar^{2}v_{F}^{2}-2m_{0}m_{1})/2m_{1}^{2}}
and
\Delta_{0}=\hbar v_{F}\sqrt{\hbar^{2}v_{F}^{2}-4m_{0}m_{1}}/|m_{1}|
. Here, we assumed
m_{1}\neq 0
and we work in the regime where
m_{0}m_{1}<\hbar^{2}v_{F}^{2}/4
such that both
k_{0}
and
\Delta_{0}
are real parameters. When
m_{0}m_{1}>\hbar^{2}v_{F}^{2}/4
, the BR interaction is demonstrated to be oscillatory
Litvinov 2016
. We also made sure to select poles
k_{1,2}
that are located in the upper half-plane for all frequencies
\omega^{+}
.

Then, from Eq.(
46
) we obtain the following.

\chi_{zz}^{tt}(\bm{R})=\frac{2W}{\pi}\int_{-\infty+i0^{+}}^{i0^{+}}d\omega\big[|Q_{0}(\bm{R},\omega)|^{2}+|\bm{Q}_{1}(\bm{R},\omega)|^{2}\big]

(50)

After analytically continuing
\omega+i0^{+}\to i\tilde{\omega}
, with the poles
k_{1,2}
kept in the upper half-plane, this becomes

\chi_{zz}^{tt}(\bm{R})=\frac{(W/\Delta_{0})}{16\pi(R_{0}/a)^{4}}F(\bm{R}/R_{0})

(51)

where the dimensionless range function
F(\bm{R}/R_{0})=F_{0}(\bm{R}/R_{0})+F_{1}(\bm{R}/R_{0})
is given as

\displaystyle F_{0}(\bm{R}/R_{0})=\text{Im}\int_{0}^{1}i\frac{dz}{z\sqrt{1-z^{2}}}\Big\{(z^{2}-1)\times

(52)

\displaystyle\Big[\text{H}_{0}^{(1)}\big(f(z)R/R_{0}\big)-\text{H}_{0}^{(1)}\big(f(-z)R/R_{0}\big)\Big]^{2}

\displaystyle-\eta^{2}\Big[f(z)\text{H}_{1}^{(1)}\big(f(z)R/R_{0}\big)

\displaystyle-f(-z)\text{H}_{1}^{(1)}\big(f(-z)R/R_{0}\big)\Big]^{2}\Big\}

\displaystyle F_{1}(\bm{R}/R_{0})=\text{Im}\int_{0}^{\infty}i\frac{dz}{z\sqrt{z^{2}+1}}\Big\{(z^{2}+1)\times

\displaystyle\Big[\text{H}_{0}^{(1)}\big(f(iz)R/R_{0}\big)-\text{H}_{0}^{(1)}\big(f(-iz)R/R_{0}\big)\Big]^{2}

\displaystyle+\eta^{2}\Big[f(iz)\text{H}_{1}^{(1)}\big(f(iz)R/R_{0}\big)

\displaystyle-f(-iz)\text{H}_{1}^{(1)}\big(f(-iz)R/R_{0}\big)\Big]^{2}\Big\}

\eta=2(W/\Delta_{0})(a/R_{0})
,
R_{0}=\sqrt{2|m_{1}|/\Delta_{0}}

f(z)=i\sqrt{k_{0}^{2}R_{0}^{2}-z}

Appendix F
Susceptibility at finite temperatures

Our goal is to obtain the temperature dependence of the Bloembergen-Rowland exchange by computing the real-space static susceptibility
\chi_{zz}(\bm{R},T)=J_{zz}(\bm{R},T)/J_{0}
. We first treat thick TIs (no intersurface hybridization; gapless surface), and then consider thin TIs where hybridization opens a gap that sets the BR length scale.

For a thick TI the two surfaces do not hybridize. Since we are interested in temperatures near
T_{c}
, we set the exchange gap to zero,
\Delta=0
.
Starting from the imaginary-time susceptibility in Eq. (
23
) and the momentum-space Matsubara Green’s function

G(\bm{k},i\omega_{n})=\frac{i\omega_{n}\sigma_{0}+\hbar v_{F}(\bm{k}\times\bm{\sigma})_{z}}{(i\omega_{n})^{2}-\hbar^{2}v_{F}^{2}k^{2}},

we write

G(\bm{k},\tau)=\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{i\omega_{n}}\frac{e^{-i\omega_{n}\tau}\,\big[i\omega_{n}\sigma_{0}+\hbar v_{F}(\bm{k}\times\bm{\sigma})_{z}\big]}{[i\omega_{n}-\epsilon_{v}(\bm{k})]\,[i\omega_{n}-\epsilon_{c}(\bm{k})]},

(53)

with band energies
\epsilon_{c(v)}(\bm{k})=\pm\hbar v_{F}|\bm{k}|
.
Evaluating the sum over poles of
\tilde{n}_{F}(z)=[e^{-\beta z}+1]^{-1}
gives

\displaystyle G(\bm{k},\tau)

\displaystyle=[\epsilon_{v}\tilde{n}_{F}(\epsilon_{v})\,e^{-\epsilon_{v}\tau}-\epsilon_{c}\tilde{n}_{F}(\epsilon_{c})\,e^{-\epsilon_{c}\tau}]\frac{\sigma_{0}}{\epsilon_{c}-\epsilon_{v}}\,

(54)

\displaystyle+[\tilde{n}_{F}(\epsilon_{v})\,e^{-\epsilon_{v}\tau}-\tilde{n}_{F}(\epsilon_{c})\,e^{-\epsilon_{c}\tau}]\frac{\hbar v_{F}(\bm{k}\times\bm{\sigma})_{z}}{\epsilon_{c}-\epsilon_{v}}

Fourier transforming to real space (as in App.
B
) yields

\displaystyle G(\bm{R},\tau)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big[\mathbb{F}^{m}_{n}(\bm{R})\,\sin(\omega_{n}\tau)\,\sigma_{0}

(55)

\displaystyle+\,i\,\mathbb{F}^{m}_{n}(\bm{R})\cos(\omega_{n}\tau)\,(\hat{\bm{R}}\times\bm{\sigma})_{z}\Big]

where
\omega_{n}=(2n+1)\pi/\beta
are fermionic Matsubara frequencies
Abrikosov et al. 1965
;
Coleman 2015
and

\mathbb{F}^{m}_{n}(\bm{R})\equiv-\frac{\omega_{n}}{\pi\beta W^{2}}\,K_{m}\!\left(\frac{\omega_{n}}{W}\frac{R}{a}\right),\qquad m=0,1,

with
K_{m}
the modified Bessel functions and
W=\hbar v_{F}/a
.
Inserting Eq. (
55

23
) and taking the spin trace gives

\chi_{zz}(\bm{R},T)=\beta W\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big([\mathbb{F}_{n}^{0}(\bm{R})]^{2}+[\mathbb{F}_{n}^{1}(\bm{R})]^{2}\Big),

(56)

valid for all
T
. Figure
5

;
Ou et al. 2025
, we find a shallow increase peaking near
T\!\sim\!1000~\mathrm{K}
, followed by a gradual decrease. Overall, the variation in
J_{zz}(T)
remains negligible throughout the experimentally relevant regime

Iwasawa Theory of Elliptic Curves in Quadratic Twist Families
Debanjana Kundu, Katharina Müller

Abstract
. In this article, we use two different approaches – one algebraic and the other analytic – to study the variation of Iwasawa invariants of rational elliptic curves in some quadratic twist families.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 11R23, 11G05; Secondary: 11R11, 11R45

1.
Introduction

Arithmetic of elliptic curves and their quadratic twists

Given an elliptic curve
\mathsf{E}
defined over a number field
K
, the Mordell–Weil theorem asserts that the group of
K
-rational points, called the Mordell–Weil group and denoted by
\mathsf{E}(K)
, is finitely generated as an abelian group (see
[
Mor22
,
Wei29
]
).
A central question in the arithmetic of elliptic curves is the precise structure of this group, in particular its (algebraic) rank.
A long-standing conjecture in the area is the
rank distribution conjecture
which claims that over any number field a suitably-defined
average rank
would be 1/2.
More precisely, half of all elliptic curves have Mordell–Weil rank 0 and the remaining half have (Mordell–Weil) rank 1; whereas higher (Mordell–Weil) ranks constitute 0% of all elliptic curves, even though there may exist infinitely many such elliptic curves.
The aforementioned rank distribution conjecture originated in the work of D. Goldfeld; see
[
Gol79
]
where he predicted the rank distribution in quadratic twist families of elliptic curves.
More precisely, let
\mathsf{E}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}y^{2}=f(x)
be a rational elliptic curve (where
f(x)
is a cubic polynomial) and for
d
a square-free integer (positive or negative) set
\mathsf{E}^{(d)}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}dy^{2}=f(x)
to be its quadratic twist.
Then the Mordell–Weil rank of
\mathsf{E}^{(d)}/\mathbb{Q}
is 0 (resp. 1) for half (resp. half) of all square free
d
’s and for 0% of all square-free
d
’s, the (Mordell–Weil) rank of
\mathsf{E}^{(d)}/\mathbb{Q}
is at least 2.
Note that
\mathsf{E}
and
\mathsf{E}^{(d)}
are not isomorphic over
\mathbb{Q}
but become isomorphic over the quadratic field
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})
.
Sometimes, depending on the context, the twisted curve is denoted as
\mathsf{E}^{K}
.

The best results towards the rank distribution conjecture are by M. Bharagava and A. Shankar (see
[
BS15a
,
BS15b
]
); they show that the average rank of elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
is strictly less than one, and that both rank 0 and rank 1 cases comprise non-zero densities across all rational elliptic curves.
In
[
Smi25
]
, A. Smith has announced a proof of Goldfeld’s Conjecture which is conditional on the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer Conjecture.
Proving results about the Mordell–Weil rank almost always involves a thorough analysis of the
Selmer group
.
This idea dates back to the proof of the Mordell–Weil theorem, and is even an essential part of contemporary results like that of Bhargava–Shankar and Smith.

Iwasawa theory of elliptic curves

Another subject of active research is Iwasawa theory.
A motivating problem in the Iwasawa theory of elliptic curves is the question of determining the growth of the Mordell–Weil rank in certain infinite towers of number fields such as the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension.
Such questions were first studied by B. Mazur in
[
Maz72
]
, where he showed that for a class of elliptic curves defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, the Mordell–Weil rank remains bounded in the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}}/\mathbb{Q}
. Mazur’s proof (unsurprisingly) involved a thorough analysis of the
p
-primary Selmer group of
\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}}
(denoted by
\operatorname{Sel}(\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})
).
This result has been extended to all
rational
elliptic curves by K. Kato
[
Kat04
]
and D. Rohrlich
[
Roh88
]
.

Let
\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve with good
ordinary
reduction at
p\geq 5
.
Over
\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}}
, it is known that the
p
-primary Selmer group is cofinitely generated as a module over the Iwasawa algebra (denote by
\Lambda
) and is co-torsion.
The Iwasawa algebra is isomorphic to the power series ring
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket x\rrbracket
.
The algebraic structure of the Selmer group (as a
\Lambda
-module) is encoded by Iwasawa invariants,
\mu
and
\lambda
.
By the
p
-adic Weierstrass Preparation Theorem, the characteristic ideal of (the Pontryagin dual of) the Selmer group is generated by a unique element
f^{(p)}_{\operatorname{Sel}(\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})^{\vee}}(x)
, which can be expressed as a power of
p
times a distinguished polynomial.
It is well-known that (assuming finiteness of the
p
-primary Shafarevich–Tate group) the
\lambda
-invariant of
\operatorname{Sel}(\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})^{\vee}
is at least as large as the Mordell–Weil rank of
\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}
.
Thus, the study of the
\lambda
-invariant attracts significant attention when trying to gain information about the rank of the elliptic curve.

Motivation

The Euler characteristic for
\mathsf{E}
is an invariant of the elliptic curve which can be computed (up to a
p
-adic unit) by knowing well-understood arithmetic invariants of
\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}
but it (miraculously) encodes information on the Iwasawa invariants of
\operatorname{Sel}(\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})^{\vee}
.
Using the Euler characteristic for
\mathsf{E}
it has been possible to study the variation of Iwasawa invariants in certain families of elliptic curves – such as all rank 0 elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
with good reduction at
p
.
In view of these results, it is expected that for a fixed prime
p
, the proportion of elliptic curves of rank 0 over
\mathbb{Q}
with good ordinary reduction at
p
(ordered by height) with trivial
p
-primary Selmer group (equivalently
\mu=\lambda=0
) approaches 100% as
p\to\infty
; (see
[
KR21
, Conjecture 4.7]
and for refinements see
[
KR24
]
).
These expectations hinge on assuming finiteness of the
p
-primary part of the Shafarevich–Tate group and on Cohen–Lenstra type heuristics on the variation of the Shafarevich–Tate group group by C. Delaunay
[
Del01
]
.
[Were it easier to compute the
p
-adic regulator of elliptic curves, one might have more ambitiously predicted that the proportion of
\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}
of rank 1 with good ordinary reduction at
p
(ordered by height) with
\mu=0
and
\lambda=1
approaches 100% as
p\to\infty
.]
Using the Euler characteristic to study variation of Iwasawa invariants in families of elliptic curves has been moderately successful in some other cases; for example, changing the base field and/or the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension (see
[
KLR22
,
HKR24
]
).
However, it has been almost impossible to use this idea to study the variation of Iwasawa invariants of elliptic curves in
thin families
such as quadratic twist families of a fixed elliptic curve
\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}
.

We use two (different) approaches – one algebraic and the other analytic – to study the variation of Iwasawa invariants of elliptic curves in some (quadratic) twist families.
These ideas are detailed below.

1.1.
Main Results

As will be clarified, both approaches have their own advantages and disadvantages.
For example, the analytic approach places no restriction on the rank of the (starting) elliptic curve
\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}
but requires that the conductor
N_{\mathsf{E}}
is square-free and odd.
On the other hand, the algebraic approach requires that the fixed elliptic curve
\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}
has rank at most 1 but places no extra conditions on the conductor
N_{\mathsf{E}}
.
In addition, the algebraic approach allows us to study both real and imaginary twists.

Approach I: Analytic

The first main result we prove shows that in certain quadratic twist families the Iwasawa
\lambda
-invariant remains constant.
More precisely, we prove the following result.

Theorem A
.

Fix a prime
p\geq 5
.
Let
\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve of odd square-free conductor
N_{\mathsf{E}}
with good ordinary reduction at
p
and let
f_{\mathsf{E}}
be the associated weight 2 modular form.
Let
F_{\mathsf{E}}
be the associated half-integral weight modular form in
S_{3/2}(4N_{\mathsf{E}},\boldsymbol{1},f_{\mathsf{E}})
.
Let
n_{1},n_{2}
be square-free positive integers coprime to
p
such that
\frac{n_{1}}{n_{2}}\in(\mathbb{Q}_{q}^{*})^{2}
for all
q\mid 4N_{\mathsf{E}}p
.
Assume that
a_{n_{2}}(F_{\mathsf{E}})\neq 0
.
Then
a_{n_{1}}(F_{\mathsf{E}})\neq 0
and

\lambda_{\operatorname{an}}(\mathsf{E}^{(-n_{1})}/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})=\lambda_{\operatorname{an}}(\mathsf{E}^{(-n_{2})}/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}}).

Further, if the Iwasawa Main conjecture holds then the assertion is true for the algebraic Iwasawa invariants.

Comment on notation: We remind the reader that here,
\mathsf{E}^{(-n)}
denotes the quadratic twist of
\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}
by
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-n})
for positive and square-free
n
.
Also, the analytic
\lambda
-invariant is the one associated with the
p
-adic
L
-function of the elliptic curve; see Section
2.3
for definitions.

The way to interpret this result is as follows: fix a square-free positive integer
n_{2}
coprime to
p
satisfying
a_{n_{2}}(F_{\mathsf{E}})\neq 0
.
Vary
n_{1}
over square-free positive integer coprime to
p
such that
\frac{n_{1}}{n_{2}}\in(\mathbb{Q}_{q}^{*})^{2}
for all
q\mid N_{\mathsf{E}}p
.
Then, the twisted elliptic curve
\mathsf{E}^{(-n_{1})}
has the same
\lambda
-invariant as that of the (fixed) elliptic curve
\mathsf{E}^{(-n_{2})}
.
In this case, we are not proving results on
\lambda
-invariants being equal to the original elliptic curve
\mathsf{E}
.

In the course of proving this result we also provide explicit conditions when
a_{n_{2}}(F_{\mathsf{E}})\neq 0
; see Theorem
3.5
.
The proof of the main theorem relies on the work of G. Shimura and J.-L. Waldspurger which we explain in Section
2
.
Finally, the sufficient conditions we provide for the theorem to hold crucially depends on the work of K. Prasanna.
Moreover, in Theorem
5.1
we compute ‘how often’ these conditions are satisfied.

Approach II: Algebraic

Using techniques from Galois cohomology, results on fine Selmer groups, and explicit twisting of elliptic curves, we prove the following result.

Theorem B
.

Let
\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve of Mordell–Weil rank at most 1.
Let
p\geq 5
be a prime and
K
be a quadratic field satisfying the following properties

(i)

\mathsf{E}(K_{v})[p]=\{0\}
for all
v\in\Sigma(K)
where
\Sigma(K)
denotes the set of all primes (in
K
) above the conductor of
\mathsf{E}
, the primes above
p
, the archimedean prime(s), and the primes ramified in
K/\mathbb{Q}
.

(ii)

\mathsf{E}
has good ordinary reduction at
p
.

(iii)

p
splits in
K
.

(iv)

the
p
-primary part of the fine Selmer group of
\mathsf{E}/K
is trivial.

Then there is an isomorphism

\operatorname{Sel}(\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})^{\vee}\cong\operatorname{Sel}(\mathsf{E}^{K}/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})^{\vee}

as
\Lambda
-modules.
In particular,
\lambda_{\operatorname{alg}}(\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})=\lambda_{\operatorname{alg}}(\mathsf{E}^{K}/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})
.

In addition to the aforementioned theorem, under additional hypotheses on finiteness of the
p
-primary part of the Shafarevich–Tate group, we also prove using results of J. Lee that for all
n

\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathsf{E}(\mathbb{Q}_{(n)}))=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathsf{E}^{K}(\mathbb{Q}_{(n)})).

In Theorem
4.12
we discuss sufficient conditions to verify the hypothesis.
Finally, just as in the previous case, we compute for ‘how many’ quadratic fields the above conditions hold (when
\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}
and
p\geq 5
are kept fixed); see Theorem
5.4
.
Unfortunately, this is a ‘thin set’ among all quadratic fields.
However, if we restrict the count to quadratic fields
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\pm\ell})
with
\ell
a prime and then consider (appropriate) density, we get positive proportions that depend on the number of prime divisors and parity of
N_{\mathsf{E}}
.

In Section
4.3
we combine the two approaches and provide sufficient conditions for Theorem
A
to hold with
n_{2}=1
.
Thus, allowing us to comment on which families of twists have the same
\lambda
-invariant as the original elliptic curve
\mathsf{E}
.

1.2.
Organization

Including this introduction, the article has five sections.
Section
2
is preliminary in nature.
Here, we recall basic definitions from Iwasawa theory and the theory elliptic curves.
We remind the reader of the Greenberg–Iwasawa Main Conjecture (IMC) – which is known to be a theorem in many cases.
Finally, we discuss the basic notions associated with modular forms of half-integral weight.
In Section
3
we prove results which have an analytic flavour.
Using the results of Prasanna we can show that certain quadratic twist families of elliptic curves have constant (analytic)
\lambda
-invariants over
\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}}
.
Using the IMC this result can be converted to a statement on algebraic
\lambda
-invariants.
In Section
4
we prove results which are algebraic in nature.
For a fixed elliptic curve
\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}
we find quadratic twist families
\mathsf{E}^{(n)}
such that the (dual) Selmer groups of
\mathsf{E}
and
\mathsf{E}^{(n)}
are isomorphic as
\Lambda
-modules over
\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}}
.
In particular, the Iwasawa invariants in such quadratic twist families remain the same.
Under appropriate (mild) assumptions, we can also comment on the equality of Mordell–Weil ranks of
\mathsf{E}
and its twist in each layer of the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension.
In Section
5
we prove density results, i.e., we show ‘how often’ the results in Sections
3
and
4
hold.

1.3.
Outlook

For the analytic approach, we have restricted ourselves to the study of the variation of Iwasawa invariants associated to elliptic curves in
imaginary quadratic
twist families.
One possibility is to consider real quadratic twist families, as well.
The analytic approach builds on a result of Waldspurger relating
L
-values of modular forms and imaginary quadratic twists
[
Wal81
]
.
To the knowledge of the authors no analogue for real quadratic twists is known.
Throughout this article, we have considered the case where
p
is a prime of good
ordinary
reduction for
\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}
.
We intend to study the case when
p
is a prime of good
supersingular
reduction in the future.
Finally, it seems well within reach that at least some of our results can be extended to the case of modular abelian varieties.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Iwasawa theory

Fix an odd prime
p
.
For a non-negative integer
n
, write
\zeta_{p^{n}}
to denote a primitive
p^{n}
-th root of unity.
Then the
cyclotomic

\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
\mathbb{Q}
is the maximal totally real pro-
p
subfield of
\bigcup_{n}\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p^{n}})
.
We denote this by write
\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}}
.
In particular, there is a tower

\mathbb{Q}=\mathbb{Q}_{(0)}\subset\mathbb{Q}_{(1)}\subset\ldots\subset\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}}

where each
\mathbb{Q}_{(n)}
is the totally real subfield of
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p^{n+1}})
with
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{(n)}/\mathbb{Q})\simeq\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}
.
By infinite Galois theory,

\Gamma=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}}/\mathbb{Q})\simeq\varprojlim_{n}\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}.

Let
K
be any number field.
Then
K_{\operatorname{cyc}}=K\cdot\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}}
, i.e., the compositum of
K
with
\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}}
.
As before, we write
K_{(n)}
to denote the
n
-th layer of
K_{\operatorname{cyc}}
and write
\Gamma_{n}=\operatorname{Gal}(K_{(n)}/K)\simeq\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}
.
By definition
\operatorname{Gal}(K_{\operatorname{cyc}}/K)\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, as well.

The
Iwasawa algebra

\Lambda=\Lambda(\Gamma)
is the completed group algebra
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\varprojlim_{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\Gamma/\Gamma^{p^{n}}]
.
Fix a topological generator
\gamma
of
\Gamma
; this gives an isomorphism of rings

\displaystyle\Lambda

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket x\rrbracket

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle\mapsto 1+x.

Let
M
be a finitely generated torsion
\Lambda
-module.
The
Structure Theorem of
\Lambda
-modules
asserts
[
Was97
, Theorem 13.12]
that
M
is pseudo-isomorphic to a finite direct sum of cyclic
\Lambda
-modules.
In other words, there is a homomorphism of
\Lambda
-modules

M\longrightarrow\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}\Lambda/(p^{m_{i}})\right)\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{j=1}^{t}\Lambda/(f_{j}(x))\right)

with finite kernel and cokernel.
Here,
m_{i}>0
and
f_{j}(x)
is a distinguished polynomial (i.e. a monic polynomial with non-leading coefficients divisible by
p
).
The characteristic ideal of
M
is (up to a unit) generated by the characteristic element,

f_{M}^{(p)}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=p^{\sum_{i}m_{i}}\prod_{j}f_{j}(x).

The
\mu
-invariant of
M
is defined as the power of
p
in
f_{M}^{(p)}(x)
.
More explicitly,

\mu_{\operatorname{alg}}(M)=\mu_{p}(M)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\begin{cases}0&\textrm{ if }s=0\\
\sum_{i=1}^{s}m_{i}&\textrm{ if }s>0.\end{cases}

The
\lambda
-invariant of
M
is the degree of the characteristic element, i.e.

\lambda_{\operatorname{alg}}(M)=\lambda_{p}(M)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\sum_{j=1}^{t}\deg f_{j}(x).

2.2.
Selmer group and fine Selmer group of elliptic curves

Fix an algebraic closure
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
of
\mathbb{Q}
.
Then an algebraic extension of
\mathbb{Q}
is a subfield of this fixed algebraic closure,
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Throughout,
p
denotes a fixed rational odd prime.
Let
\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve.
Fix a finite set
\Sigma=\Sigma(\mathbb{Q})
of primes of
\mathbb{Q}

containing

p
and the primes of bad reduction of
\mathsf{E}
.
Denote by
\mathbb{Q}_{\Sigma}
, the maximal algebraic extension of
\mathbb{Q}
unramified outside
\Sigma
.
For every (possibly infinite) extension
L
of
\mathbb{Q}
contained in
\mathbb{Q}_{\Sigma}
, write
G_{\Sigma}\left({L}\right)=\operatorname{Gal}\left(\mathbb{Q}_{\Sigma}/{L}\right)
.
Write
\Sigma(L)
for the set of primes of
L
above
\Sigma
.
If
L
is a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}
and
w
is a place of
L
, we write
L_{w}
for its completion at
w
; when
L/\mathbb{Q}
is infinite, it is the union of completions of all finite sub-extensions of
L
.

Definition 2.1
.

Fix an elliptic curve
\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}
with good reduction at
p
.
View
\mathsf{E}[p^{\infty}]
as a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module equipped with a continuous
G_{\Sigma}(\mathbb{Q})
-action.

(i)

For any finite extension
L/\mathbb{Q}
set

\displaystyle K^{1}_{v}\left(\mathsf{E}/L\right)

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{w|v}H^{1}\left(L_{w},\mathsf{E}[p^{\infty}]\right),

\displaystyle J^{1}_{v}\left(\mathsf{E}/L\right)

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{w|v}H^{1}\left(L_{w},\mathsf{E}\right)[p^{\infty}],

where the direct sum is taken over all primes
w
of
L
lying above
v
.

(ii)

For an infinite algebraic extension
\mathcal{L}/\mathbb{Q}
, define
K_{v}^{1}\left(\mathsf{E}/\mathcal{L}\right)
by taking the inductive limit of
K_{v}^{1}\left(\mathsf{E}/L\right)
over all finite extensions
L/\mathbb{Q}
contained in
\mathcal{L}
, and similarly for
J_{v}^{1}(\mathsf{E}/\mathcal{L})
.

(iii)

Let
L
be an algebraic extension of
\mathbb{Q}
(possibly infinite) that is contained inside
\mathbb{Q}_{\Sigma}
, define the
p^{\infty}
-fine Selmer group
of
\mathsf{E}
over
L
as

\operatorname{Sel}^{0}(\mathsf{E}/L)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\ker\left(H^{1}(G_{\Sigma}(L),\mathsf{E}[p^{\infty}])\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{v\in\Sigma(L)}K^{1}_{v}(\mathsf{E}/L)\right).

and the
p^{\infty}
-Selmer group
as

\operatorname{Sel}(\mathsf{E}/L)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\ker\left(H^{1}(G_{\Sigma}(L),\mathsf{E}[p^{\infty}])\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{v\in\Sigma(L)}J^{1}_{v}(\mathsf{E}/L)\right).

We will be interested in Selmer groups and fine Selmer groups over the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of a number field.
Given a number field
K
and an elliptic curve
\mathsf{E}/K
be an elliptic curve, we view the Selmer group over
K_{\operatorname{cyc}}
as the direct limit of the Selmer groups over the number fields
K_{(n)}
in
K_{\operatorname{cyc}}
, i.e.

\operatorname{Sel}(\mathsf{E}/K_{\operatorname{cyc}})=\varinjlim_{n}\operatorname{Sel}(\mathsf{E}/K_{(n)})=\ker\left(H^{1}(G_{\Sigma}(K_{\operatorname{cyc}}),\mathsf{E}[p^{\infty}])\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{v\in\Sigma(K_{\operatorname{cyc}})}J^{1}_{v}(\mathsf{E}/L)\right),

and similarly for fine Selmer groups.
Since all primes are finitely decomposed in the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension, we henceforth simplify notation and write
\bigoplus_{v\in{\Sigma}(K_{\operatorname{cyc}})}H^{1}\left(K_{\operatorname{cyc},v},\mathsf{E}[p^{\infty}]\right)
in place of
\bigoplus_{v\in{\Sigma}(K_{\operatorname{cyc}})}K^{1}_{v}(\mathsf{E}/K_{\operatorname{cyc}})
, and similarly for
\bigoplus_{v\in{\Sigma}(K_{\operatorname{cyc}})}J^{1}_{v}(\mathsf{E}/K_{\operatorname{cyc}})
.
Since
p
is fixed, we ignore including it in the notation of the (fine) Selmer group.
We remind the reader that both the definitions are independent of
{\Sigma}
as long as it
contains
the primes above
p
, the primes of bad reduction of
\mathsf{E}
, and the archimedean primes.

Let
L/\mathbb{Q}
be an algebraic extension.
The Selmer group sits inside the following short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathsf{E}(L)\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sel}(\mathsf{E}/L)\longrightarrow\Sha(\mathsf{E}/L)[p^{\infty}]\longrightarrow 0.

The left-most object is called the
Mordell–Weil group
of
\mathsf{E}
and the right-most object is called the
Shafarevich–Tate group
.
The relationship between the
p^{\infty}
-fine Selmer group and the
p^{\infty}
-Selmer group is made clear through the following exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sel}^{0}(\mathsf{E}/L)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sel}(\mathsf{E}/L)\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{v\mid p}\mathsf{E}(L_{v})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}.

The Pontryagin dual of
\operatorname{Sel}(\mathsf{E}/K_{\operatorname{cyc}})
denoted by
\operatorname{Sel}(\mathsf{E}/K_{\operatorname{cyc}})^{\vee}
is a finitely generated
\Lambda
-module.
When
p
is a prime of good
ordinary
reduction and
K/\mathbb{Q}
is abelian, it is known that
\operatorname{Sel}(\mathsf{E}/K_{\operatorname{cyc}})
is in fact,
\Lambda
-cotorsion; see
[
Kat04
, Theorem 17.4]
.
We can therefore attach Iwasawa invariants to the Pontryagin dual of
\operatorname{Sel}(\mathsf{E}/K_{\operatorname{cyc}})
.
Since
\operatorname{Sel}^{0}(\mathsf{E}/K_{\operatorname{cyc}})^{\vee}
is a quotient of
\operatorname{Sel}(\mathsf{E}/K_{\operatorname{cyc}})^{\vee}
, the same assertions are true for the (dual) fine Selmer group, as well.

For the purposes of our proofs, we are also be interested in the
p^{k}
-fine Selmer group
for
k\geq 1
which is defined for algebraic extensions
L/\mathbb{Q}
as follows

\operatorname{Sel}^{0}_{\Sigma}(\mathsf{E}[p^{k}]/L)=\ker\left(H^{1}(G_{\Sigma}(L),\mathsf{E}[p^{k}])\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{v\in\Sigma(L)}H^{1}(L_{v},\mathsf{E}[p^{k}])\right).

As indicated in the notation, this
may depend
on the choice of
\Sigma
.
However, if
L
contains the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension (as will be in our case) then the choice of
\Sigma
does not matter (as long as
\Sigma
contains
p
, the primes of bad reduction of
\mathsf{E}
, and the archimedean primes).

2.3.
p
-adic
L
-functions

Let
\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve. Recall that
\mathsf{E}
is modular, i.e there exists a modular form
f_{\mathsf{E}}
in
S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N_{\mathsf{E}}))

2.5.
Cusp forms of weight
3/2

The (Hecke) congruence subgroup of level
N
is defined as the following subgroup for a fixed (positive) integer
N

\Gamma_{0}(N)=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\colon c\equiv 0\pmod{n}\right\}.

Write
\mathbb{H}
to denote the upper half plane and let
z\in\mathbb{H}
.
Set
q=e^{2\pi iz}
.
Define the
theta-function
as

\Theta(z)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}q^{n^{2}}.

Let
c
be any integer and
d
be an odd integer.
Define the
quadratic residue symbol
as follows

\left(\frac{c}{d}\right)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\begin{cases}\text{Jacobi symbol }\left(\frac{c}{d}\right)&\text{ if }d>0\\
\text{Jacobi symbol }\left(\frac{c}{|d|}\right)&\text{ if }c>0,\ d<0\\
\text{Jacobi symbol }-\left(\frac{c}{|d|}\right)&\text{ if }c,d<0\\
1&\text{ if }c=0,\ d=\pm 1;\end{cases}

For
d
odd, further define

\varepsilon_{d}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }d\equiv 1\pmod{4}\\
i&\text{ if }d\equiv 3\pmod{4}.\end{cases}

When
\gamma\in\Gamma_{0}(4)
and
z\in\mathbb{H}
, define

j(\gamma,z)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\left(\frac{c}{d}\right)\varepsilon_{d}^{-1}\sqrt{cz+d}.

Then,
[
Kob93
, Theorem on p. 148]
asserts that for
z\in\mathbb{H}
,

j(\gamma,z)=\frac{\Theta(\gamma z)}{\Theta(z)}.

We now define a modular form of half-integral weight.
Intuitively, the idea is as follows: for
k
a positive odd integer, we decide that
\Theta^{k}
is a weight 2 modular form of level 4 with trivial character.
Then, we define an arbitrary modular form of weight
k/2
to be a holomorphic function which transforms under (a congruence subgroup of)
\Gamma_{0}(4)
in the same way as
\Theta^{k}
does.
More precisely,

Definition 2.4
.

Let
N
be a positive integer divisible by
4
.
Let
\chi
be a character modulo
N
and
k
a positive integer.
A meromorphic function
F
is a modular form of weight
k/2
, Nebentypus
\chi
and level
N
if it satisfies

F(\gamma z)=\chi(d)j(\gamma,z)^{k}F(z)\quad\forall\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma_{0}(N).

Let
\chi
be a quadratic character of conductor
r
and for a natural number
t
, write
\chi_{t}
to denote the quadratic character corresponding to
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{t})/\mathbb{Q}
.
By
[
Shi73
, Proposition 2.2]
, we know that

F_{\rho}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\sum_{m}\chi(m)mq^{tm^{2}}

is a cusp form of weight
3/2
, level
4r^{2}t
and character
\rho=\chi\chi_{t}\chi_{-1}
.

We write
S_{3/2}(N,\psi)
to denote the subspace of all cusp-forms of level
N
, nebentypus character
\psi
and weight
3/2
that are orthogonal to all cusp forms
F_{\rho}
under the Petersson inner product. We write
S_{3/2}(N)
do denote
\oplus_{\psi}S_{3/2}(N,\psi)
, where
\psi
runs over all characters modulo
N
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
F=\sum a_{n}q^{n}
be a modular form of weight
k/2
, level
N
and nebentypus character
\chi
.
Let
\psi
be a finite order character of conductor
M
.
Define
F_{\psi}=\sum a_{n}\psi(n)q^{n}
.
Then
F_{\psi}
is a modular form of weight
k/2
, level
NM^{2}
, and nebentypus character
\chi\psi^{2}
.

Proof.

If
k
is even this is well known; see for example
[
Kob93
, Proposition III.3.17]
.
The proof carries over for half integral weight.

We give a sketch for the convenience of the reader.
Let

g=\sum_{j=0}^{M-1}\psi(j)\zeta^{j}

be a Gauss–sum.
We obtain

F_{\psi}(z)=\frac{g}{M}\sum_{v=0}^{M-1}\overline{\psi}(v)F(z-\frac{v}{M}).

Let
\gamma_{v}=\begin{pmatrix}1&-\frac{v}{M}\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}
and
\gamma\in\Gamma_{0}(NM^{2})
.
For each
v
we choose
v^{\prime}
such that
v^{\prime}a\equiv vd\pmod{M}
.
Then

\displaystyle F_{\psi}(\gamma z)

\displaystyle=\frac{g}{N}\sum_{v=0}^{M-1}\overline{\psi}(v)F(\gamma_{v}\gamma\gamma_{v^{\prime}}^{-1}\gamma_{v^{\prime}}z)

\displaystyle=\frac{g}{N}\sum_{v=0}^{M-1}\overline{\psi}(v)\psi\left(d+\frac{cv^{\prime}}{M}\right)j(\gamma_{v}\gamma\gamma_{v^{\prime}}^{-1},\gamma_{v^{\prime}}z)^{k}F(\gamma_{v^{\prime}}z).

Note that

j(\gamma_{v}\gamma\gamma_{v^{\prime}}^{-1},\gamma_{v^{\prime}}z)=\left(\frac{c}{d+\frac{cv^{\prime}}{M}}\right)\varepsilon_{d+\frac{cv^{\prime}}{M}}\sqrt{c\gamma_{v^{\prime}}z+d+\frac{cv^{\prime}}{M}},

where the square root of a complex number is defined to have an argument in
(-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}]
.
As
c\equiv 0\pmod{NM^{2}}
, we conclude that
\frac{cv^{\prime}}{M}\equiv 0\pmod{N}
.
Thus,
\psi(d+\frac{cv^{\prime}}{M})=\psi(d)
.
Furthermore
\varepsilon_{d+cv^{\prime}/M}^{k}=\varepsilon_{d}^{k}
.

If
k
is odd, then
4\mid N
.
Let
c=2^{\ell}c^{\prime}
for an odd integer
c^{\prime}
.
Then
\left(\frac{2^{\ell}}{d+cv^{\prime}/M}\right)=\left(\frac{2^{\ell}}{d}\right)
.
Furthermore,
\left(\frac{c^{\prime}}{d+\frac{cv^{\prime}}{M}}\right)=\left(\frac{c}{d}\right)
.
If
k
is even then
\left(\frac{c}{d}\right)^{k}=1
for all
c
and
d
.

We therefore obtain

F(\gamma z)=\psi(d)^{2}j(\gamma,z)^{k}\frac{g}{N}\sum_{v^{\prime}=0}^{M-1}\overline{\psi}(v^{\prime})\psi(v^{\prime})F\left(z-\frac{v^{\prime}}{M}\right)

which proves the desired claim.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
k
be a positive odd integer and

be an eigenform of weight

for all Hecke operators

Proof.

The key idea of the proof is from

Let

with Nebentypus character

Here, we understand that

Let

be the Fourier coefficients of

If

Let

Let

Proof.

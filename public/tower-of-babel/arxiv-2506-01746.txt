Optimal Bregman quantization: existence and uniqueness of optimal quantizers revisited
Guillaume Boutoille, Gilles Pagès

Abstract
In this paper we revisit the exsistence theorem for L r L^{r} -optimal quantization, r ≥ 2 r\geq 2 , with respect to a Bregman divergence: we establish the existence of optimal quantizaers under lighter assumptions onthe strictly convex function which generates the divergence, espcially in the quadratic case ( r = 2 r=2 ). We then prove a uniqueness theorem “à la Trushkin” in one dimension for strongly unimodal distributions and divergences gerated by strictly convex functions whiose thire dervative is either stictly log \log -convex or log \log -concave.



Keywords:

Bregman divergence ; Optimal quantization ; stationary quantizers ; uniqueness ; strongly unimodal distributions.

Introduction

In computer vision, labeling represents a major cost that we aim to reduce as much as possible. Clustering algorithms are efficient tools to partition similar data into clusters in order to organize data set. Browsing these clusters allows a better visualisation of the data set and an easier labeling.
For that purpose, clustering is a fundamental field of ”unsupervised” learning which aggregates procedures that have been widely studied across many disciplines. Most of the clustering methods consist in partitioning similar data into clusters usually “represented” or characterized by a
prototype
or
codeword
(either a typical true datum or a synthetic data). These codewords make up a
codebook
, such a codebook usually minimizes a loss function (quantization error). A widely used and studied clustering algorithm is the Euclidean
k
-means algorithm (
[
17
,
11
]
).

In Information Theory, Signal Processing and Probability Theory, this algorithm is also known as the Lloyd (or Lloyd I) algorithm and goes back to the 1950s. At that time, it was a classified field of research in the Bell Labs, supported by the U.S. Navy (see the historical notes in the 1988 special issue of
IEEE on Information Theory
devoted to quantization). For probabilistic aspects, we refer to
[
9
]
who have proved most milestone theorems of the theory in a rigorous mathematical way and, more recently,
[
16
]
. Since the 1970’s (and even before), there has been a huge literature from Information Theory and automatic classification on the
k
-means algorithm in connection with optimal quantization theory. Among them we can cite the famous book by

[
8
]

In this paper we revisit the main existence theorem for the optimal quantizers of a distribution

(in the sense that

) when the loss function in the definition of the (
L^{r}
-mean) quantization error is no longer a power of a norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
but a power of a Bregman divergence induced by a strictly convex function

\phi_{{}_{F}}
associated to a strictly convex is defined on

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\forall\,(\xi,x)\!\in U^{2},\quad\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x)=\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x):=F(\xi)-F(x)-\langle\nabla F(x)\,|\,\xi-x\rangle},

(1)

where

. Indeed, the connection between these two frameworks is the case of the squared Euclidean norm which is its own Bregman divergence. In

An in-depth anamysis of this extension of the

Nevertheless, we want first to investigate optimal vector quantization theory in the same probabilistic spirit as that developed in Graf and Luschgy’s book (
[

So we first establish an existence result for such

-optimal quantizers under some seemingly slightly weaker assumptions on the function

[

We also investigate some properties of Bregman divergence based optimal quantizers known to be shared by regular optimal quantizers when the loss function is an Euclidean norm (see

The second main result, stated and established in Section

Definitions and background on regular
L^{r}
-optimal quantization w.r.t. a norm

2.1
Short background on
L^{r}
-optimal vector quantization

Let
\|.\|
denote a norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. To any nonempty subset
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
we can associate Voronoi partitions of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
induced by
\Gamma
.

Definition 2.1
(Voronoi partition)

A Borel partition of
\mathbb{R}^{d}

(C_{a}(\Gamma))_{a\in\Gamma}
is called a Voronoi partition of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
induced by
\Gamma
if

\forall\,a\!\in\Gamma,C_{a}(\Gamma)\subset\big\{\xi\!\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\|\xi-a\|\leq\|\xi-x\|,\;b\!\in\Gamma\big\}.

(2)

One checks that such a partition always satisfies, for every
a\!\in\Gamma
,

\big\{\xi\!\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\|\xi-a\|<\|\xi-b\|,\;b\!\in\Gamma\setminus\{a\}\big\}\subset\mathring{C}_{a}(\Gamma)\subset\bar{C}_{a}(\Gamma)\subset\big\{\xi\!\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\|\xi-a\|\leq\|\xi-b\|,\;b\!\in\Gamma\big\}.

Moreover, if
\|\cdot\|=|\cdot|_{e}
is a Euclidean norm, the left and right inclusions hold as equality. Moreover the closed cells
C_{a}(\Gamma)
are polyhedral convex sets i.e. the intersection of finitely many half-spaces (and
\mathring{C}_{a}(\Gamma)=\mathring{\bar{C}}_{a}(\Gamma)
).

Now let
P
be a probability measure on
(\mathbb{R}^{d},{\cal B}or(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
X:(\Omega,{\cal A},\mathbb{P})
be an
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued random variable with distribution
P
. Let
r\!\in(0,+\infty)
. We can define the quantization of
X
induced by
\Gamma
as follows.

Let us denote by
{\rm Proj}_{\Gamma}
the
nearest neighbour projection
on
\Gamma
induced by the partition
(C_{a}(\Gamma))_{a\in\Gamma}
with respect to (w.r.t.) the norm
\|\cdot\|
, defined by

\forall\,x\!\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad{\rm Proj}_{\Gamma}(x)=\sum_{a\in\Gamma}a\,\textbf{1}_{C_{a}(\Gamma)}(x).

We make here an abuse of notation since such a projection not only depends on
\Gamma
but also on the Voronoi partition to which it is associated in a bijective way. Note that for any Borel function
q:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\Gamma

\forall\,x\!\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad\|x-q(x)\|\geq\|x-{\rm Proj}_{\Gamma}(x)\|

with equality (on the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
) if and only if
q
is a nearest neighbour projection on
\Gamma
in the sense that
\big(\{\xi:q(\xi)=a\}\big)_{a\in\Gamma}
is a Voronoi partition of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Then

\widehat{X}^{\Gamma}:={\rm Proj}_{\Gamma}(X)=\sum_{a\in\Gamma}a\,\textbf{1}_{C_{a}(\Gamma)}(X)

is a
quantization of
X
induced by the Voronoi partition
(C_{a}(\Gamma))_{a\in\Gamma}
often called in short a
quantization of
X
induced by
\Gamma
.

We can define likewise the distribution of
\widehat{X}^{\Gamma}
characterized by its state space
\Gamma
and its weights
\big(P(C_{a}(\Gamma))\big)_{a\in\Gamma}
as the
quantization of the distribution
P
of
X
induced by the Voronoi partition
(C_{a}(\Gamma))_{a\in\Gamma}
often called in short a
quantization of the distribution
P
induced by
\Gamma
, denoted
\bar{P}
.

Note that if
P
has no atom then all quantizations of
X
are
\mathbb{P}
-
a.s.
equal whereas the quantization of
P
is then unique. This is still true for distributions
P
assigning no weights to hyperplanes when the norm is Euclidean.

Definition 2.2
(
L^{r}
-quantization error induced by a quantizer)

Let
r>0
and let
\|\cdot\|
denote any norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Assume that the distribution
P
has a finite
r
th moment i.e.
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|\xi\|^{r}P(\mathrm{d}\xi)<+\infty
. Let
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a quantizer. We define the
L^{r}
-mean quantization error of the distribution
P
induced by
\Gamma
by

e_{r}(\Gamma,P,\|.\|)=\left[\int_{\mathbb{R^{d}}}\min_{a\in\Gamma}\|\xi-a\|^{r}P(\mathrm{d}\xi)\right]^{\frac{1}{r}}.

We can also define

\displaystyle e_{r}(\Gamma,X,\|.\|)

\displaystyle:=\big\|\min_{a\in\Gamma}\|X-a\|\big\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{P})}

\displaystyle=\left[\mathbb{E}\,\min_{a\in\Gamma}\|X-a\|^{r}\right]^{1/r}=\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\min_{a\in\Gamma}\|\xi-a\|^{r}P(\mathrm{d}\xi)\right]^{1/r}

\displaystyle=e_{r}(\Gamma,P,\|.\|).

Then, one straightforwardly checks that, for any Voronoi partition, one has

\big\|\|X-\widehat{X}^{\Gamma}\|\big\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{P})}=\mathbb{E}\int\|{\rm Proj}_{\Gamma}(\xi)-\xi\|^{r}P(\mathrm{d}\xi)=\sum_{a\in\Gamma}\int_{C_{a}(\Gamma)}\|\xi-a\|^{r}P(\mathrm{d}\xi)=e_{r}(\Gamma,P,\|\cdot\|)^{r}

so that
e_{r}(\Gamma,P,\|\cdot\|)
measures for the
L^{r}
-norm the error induced when replacing a random variable
X
by (any of) its (nearest neighbour) quantizations.
This naturally leads to the following definition.

Definition 2.3
(
L^{r}
-optimal quantization error)

The
L^{r}
-optimal mean quantization error for
P
at level
n\geq 1
is defined by

e_{r,n}(P,\|.\|)=\inf_{\Gamma:|\Gamma|\leq n}e_{r}(\Gamma,P,\|.\|).

This in turn suggests the question of the existence of a solution to this minimization problem i.e. the question of the existence of an
L^{r}
-optimal quantizer of size (or
at level
) at most
n
i.e. a quantizer
\Gamma
which induces (through
\widehat{X}^{\Gamma}
) the best approximation of
X
in
L^{r}(\mathbb{P})
by a random variable whose set of values has at most
n
elements.

Remark.
One easily checks (see e.g.
[
9
,
16
]
) that

e_{r,n}(P,\|.\|)={\cal W}_{r}\big(P,{\cal P}_{n}\big),

where
{\cal W}_{r}
denotes the Wasserstein-Monge-Kantorovich distance between
P
and the set of probability distributions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
whose support contains at most
n
elements. In fact one also have for every
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

e_{r,n}(\Gamma,P,\|.\|)={\cal W}_{r}\big(P,{\cal P}_{\Gamma}\big)

where
{\cal P}_{\Gamma}
is the set of
\Gamma
-supported distributions.

This follows from the fact that, if
X\sim P
, then, for any random vector
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}Y}:(\Omega,{\cal A},\mathbb{P})\longrightarrow\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\big\|\|X-Y\|\big\|^{r}_{L^{r}(P)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in\Gamma}\int_{\{Y=a\}}\|X-a\|^{r}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{a\in\Gamma}\int_{\{Y=a\}}\min_{b\in\Gamma}\|X-b\|^{r}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\,\min_{b\in\Gamma}\|X-b\|^{r}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle=\big\|\|X-\widehat{X}^{\Gamma}\|\big\|^{r}_{L^{r}(P)}=e_{r}(\Gamma,P)^{r}

3
Vector quantization with respect to a Bregman divergence: definitions and first properties

3.1
Bregman divergence induced by a strictly convex function
F

First we introduce the notion of Bregman divergence induced by a strictly convex, continuously (Fréchet-)differentiable function
F
defined on a nonempty convex open set
U
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. This will be the class of similarity measures
that we will investigate in this paper.

Definition 3.1
(Bregman divergence associated to strictly convex function
F
)

Let
F:U\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a continuously differentiable, strictly convex function defined on a convex
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The convex
U
will be assumed open in all what follows, except specific mention (see e.g. Proposition
5.1
(a)
). The
F
-Bregman divergence of
x\!\in U
with respect to (w.r.t.) another point
\xi\!\in U
is defined by

\forall\,x\!\in U,\quad\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x)=F(\xi)-F(x)-\langle\nabla F(x)|\xi-x\rangle\geq 0.

(3)

Moreover
\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x)=0
if and only if
\xi=x
since
F
is strictly convex.

When no ambiguity and to alleviate notations, we will often denote
\phi(\xi,x)
instead of
\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x)
.

It is important to notice that, if
F(x)=|x|^{2}=x^{*}x
,
x\!\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

one checks that
\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x)=|\xi-x|^{2}
. This is also true for other Euclidean norms since if
F(x)=|x|_{{}_{S}}^{2}:=x^{*}Sx
with
S\!\in{\cal S}^{++}(d,\mathbb{R})
(positive definite symmetric), then
\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x)=|\xi-x|_{{}_{S}}^{2}
. This is the main connection between quantization w.r.t. to an
F
-Bregman divergence as a similarity measure (or local loss function) and that w.r.t. to a norm (or their
r
-powers).

Various examples of Bregman divergence of interest for applications are presented in Section
5.2
right after the first Theorem
5.1
of existence of optimal
\phi_{{}_{F}}
-quantizers at level
n\geq 1
of a distribution
P
.

Remarks.

\bullet

When
F
is twice (Fréchet-)differentiable on
U
then a second order Taylor expansion with integral remainder yields the alternative formulas that is also useful fro many applications

\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x)=\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)\nabla^{2}F\big(x+t(\xi-x)\big).(\xi-x)^{\otimes 2}\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{1}s\nabla^{2}F\big(\xi+s(x-\xi)\big).(x-\xi)^{\otimes 2}\mathrm{d}s.

\bullet
Generally Bregman divergences are not symmetric and do not satisfy the triangle inequality, which makes them significantly different from a distance since it only satisfies
a priori

\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x)=0
if and only of
\xi=x
.
That can be paradoxically summed by: for
\xi
,
x
,
y\!\in U
, the following usually holds

—

\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x)\neq\phi_{{}_{F}}(x,\xi)
.

—

\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x)\nleq\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x)+\phi_{{}_{F}}(x,y)
,

However Bregman divergences share some somewhat hidden properties that (partially) compensate this problem. Enough to be quite convincing as similarity measure to define and develop a optimal quantization/clustering theory.
Throughout this manuscript, the function
F
will denote a strictly convex
{\cal C}^{1}
-function defined on a (nonempty) convex open set
U
having values in
\mathbb{R}
, the divergence
\phi_{{}_{F}}
defined by (
3
) will denote the resulting
F
-Bregman divergence.

Proposition 3.1

Let
F
and
G
denote two
{\cal C}^{1}
strictly convex functions.

(a)
Then for every
\lambda
,
\mu\!\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
\lambda+\mu\neq 0
,
\lambda F+\mu G
is still
{\cal C}^{1}
and strictly convex. Furthermore

\phi_{{}_{\lambda F+\mu G}}=\lambda\phi_{{}_{F}}+\mu\phi_{{}_{G}}.

(b)
One has

\phi_{{}_{F}}=\phi_{{}_{G}}\Longleftrightarrow\exists\,\alpha\!\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\;\beta\!\in\mathbb{R}\;\mbox{ such that }\;\forall\,\xi\!\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\;F(\xi)-G(\xi)=\langle\alpha|\xi\rangle+\beta.

Proof.

(a)
is obvious.

(b)
The direct sense follows from the fact that formally
\phi_{\langle\alpha|\cdot\rangle+\beta}=0
on
U
. Conversely, if
\phi_{{}_{F}}=\phi_{{}_{G}}
then, any
x_{0}\!\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
being fixed, the announced identity holds for every
\xi\!\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\alpha=\nabla(F-G)(x_{0})
and
\beta=(F-G)(x_{0})+\langle\nabla(F-G)(x_{0})\,|\,x_{0}\rangle
.

\Box

For some Lipschitz/strongly convex functions
F
it is possible to compare the Bregman divergence
\phi_{{}_{F}}
to the Euclidean norm which is its own Bregman divergence as seen above.

Proposition 3.2
(
F
-Bregman divergence compared to Euclidean norm)

(a)
Assume that
F:U\to\mathbb{R}
has a Lipschitz continuous gradient
\nabla F
on
U
. Then, for every
\xi,\,x\!\in U
,

0\leq\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x)\leq\tfrac{1}{2}[\nabla F]_{\rm Lip}|\xi-x|^{2}.

(4)

(b)
If
F
is
\alpha
-convex for some
\alpha>0
in the sense that

\forall\,\xi,x\!\in U,\quad\langle\nabla F(\xi)-\nabla F(x)|\xi-x\rangle\geq\alpha|\xi-x|^{2}

then

\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x)\geq\frac{\alpha}{2}|\xi-x|^{2}.

Proof.

(a)
Let
\xi,x\!\in U
. One has using Cauchy-Schwartz inequality in the second line

\displaystyle\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x)

\displaystyle=F(\xi)-F(x)-\langle\nabla F(x)|\xi-x\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\langle\nabla F((1-u)\xi+ux)-\nabla F(x)\,|\,\xi-x\rangle du

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\big|\nabla F((1-u)\xi+ux)-\nabla F(x)\big||\xi-x|du

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}(1-u)du[\nabla F]_{\rm Lip}|\xi-x|^{2}=\tfrac{1}{2}[\nabla F]_{\rm Lip}|\xi-x|^{2}.

(b)
is an easy consequence of the fact that the function

g(t)=F(t\,\xi+(1-t)x)-F(x)-t\langle\nabla F(x)\,|\,\xi-x\rangle-\frac{\alpha}{2}t^{2}|\xi-x|^{2}

has a non-negative derivative on
(0,1]
since

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla F(t\xi+(1-t)x)-\nabla F(x)\,|\,\xi-x\rangle-\alpha t|\xi-x|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t}\Big(\big\langle\nabla F(t\xi+(1-t)x)-\nabla F(x)\,|\,t(\xi-x)\big\rangle-\alpha\big|t(\xi-x)\big|^{2}\Big)\geq 0

which implies
g(1)\geq g(0)=0
.

\Box

Proposition 3.3
(Integrability)

Let
r>0
. If
\mathbb{E}\big(|F(X)|\vee|X|\big)^{\frac{r}{2}}<+\infty
, then for every
x\!\in U
,
\mathbb{E}\ \phi_{{}_{F}}(X,x)^{\frac{r}{2}}<+\infty
.

Proof.
Combining Cauchy-Schwartz inequality and the elementary inequality
|a+b|^{\frac{r}{2}}\leq 2^{(\frac{r}{2}-1)^{+}}\big(|a|^{\frac{r}{2}}+|b|^{\frac{r}{2}}\big)
, yields

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\mathbb{E}\,|\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x)|^{\frac{r}{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{U}|F(\xi)-F(x)-\langle\nabla F(x)|\xi-x\rangle|^{\frac{r}{2}}P(\mathrm{d}\xi)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{(\frac{r}{2}-1)^{+}}(1+|\nabla F(x)|)^{\frac{r}{2}}\int_{U}\big(|F(\xi)|\vee|\xi|\big)^{\frac{r}{2}}P(\mathrm{d}\xi)

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt+|F(x)|^{\frac{r}{2}}+|\langle\nabla F(x)|x\rangle|^{\frac{r}{2}}<+\infty.\hskip 85.35826pt\Box

3.2
Quantization with respect to an
F
-Bregman divergence

The idea of Bregman quantization is to replace the norm which plays the role of a similarity measure in regular vector quantization theory by the Bregman divergence
\phi_{{}_{F}}
of a continuously differentiable strictly convex function
F
as defined in (
3.1
). The function
F
being defined on a nonempty convex open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we will consider in what follows
U
-valued quantizers in our definitions of the quantization error. This choice, although natural, may appear somewhat arbitrary when the support of
P
is strictly included in
U
or, even worst, if
U
contains the (closed) convex hull of the support of
P
. Except in
1
-dimension (see Section
8
), it seems not clear that it has no influence on the quantization error, compared to another natural choice which could be the convex hull of the support of the distribution
P
(or preferably its interior if it has
P
-probability
1
).

Definition 3.2
(
(r,\phi_{{}_{F}})
-Quantization Error)

Let
r\!\in(0,+\infty)
. Let
P
be a probability distribution supported by
U
and let
X:(\Omega,{\cal A},\mathbb{P})\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a
P
-distributed random vector. Assume

\mathbb{E}\big(|F(X)|\vee|X|\big)^{\frac{r}{2}}<+\infty.

(5)

(a)
Let
\Gamma\subset U
be a finite subset of
U
(also called quantizer). The
(r,\phi_{{}_{F}})
-mean quantization error for the Bregman divergences
\phi_{{}_{F}}
of the distribution
P
induced by
\Gamma
is defined by

e_{r,n}(\Gamma,P,\phi_{{}_{F}})=\left[\int_{U}\min_{a\in\Gamma}\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,a)^{\frac{r}{2}}P(\mathrm{d}\xi)\right]^{\frac{1}{r}}=\left[\mathbb{E}\,\min_{a\in\Gamma}\phi_{{}_{F}}(X,a)^{\frac{r}{2}}\right]^{\frac{1}{r}}<+\infty.

(6)

(b)
The optimal
(r,\phi_{{}_{F}})
-mean quantization error for Bregman divergences at level
n\!\in\mathbb{N}
is defined by

e_{r,n}(P,\phi_{{}_{F}})=\inf_{|\Gamma|\leq n}e_{r,n}(\Gamma,P,\phi_{{}_{F}}).

(7)

We will also use the notation
e_{r}(\Gamma,X,\phi_{{}_{F}})
and
e_{r,n}(X,\phi_{{}_{F}})
to denote the above quantities, depending on the context; keeping in mind that these quantizers only depend on the distribution
P
, not on the random vector with distribution
P
under consideration.

Comment.
The terminology
(r,\phi_{{}_{F}})
-mean quantization error has been assigned to this error modulus to be consistent with the usual terminology
L^{r}
-quantization when
F(x)=|x|^{2}
(Euclidean norm). But this choice is somewhat arbitrary.

The quantities
e_{r}(\Gamma,X,\phi_{{}_{F}})
can be defined regardless of the integrability condition (
5
), but when equal to
+\infty
it is of no interest.

4
Switch to the distortion function

4.1
Definition of
(r,\phi_{{}_{F}})
-distortion functions

Let
P
be a probability distribution supported by the non empty open convex set
U
i.e. such that
P(U)=1
, let
X
be a random vector with distribution
P
and let
r>0
be such that

\mathbb{E}\,\big(|X|\vee|F(X)|\big)^{\frac{r}{2}}=\int_{U}\big(|\xi|\vee|F(\xi)|\big)^{\frac{r}{2}}P(\mathrm{d}\xi)<+\infty.

(8)

We introduce, for every
level

n\geq 1
, the
(r,\phi_{{}_{F}})
-distortion function
G^{F,P}_{r,n}:U^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
of the distribution
P
and the Bregman divergence
\phi_{{}_{F}}
. The function
G_{r,n}=G_{r,n}^{F,P}
is defined on
U^{n}
by

\forall\,x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\!\in U^{n},\quad G_{r,n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\int_{U}\min_{i=1,\ldots,n}\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x_{i})^{\frac{r}{2}}P(\mathrm{d}\xi).

(9)

It follows from (
3
) that for every
x\!\in U
, there exists
C_{x}>0
such that for every
\xi\!\in U

0\leq\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x)\leq C_{x}\big(1+|F(\xi)|\vee|\xi|\big),

(10)

where
C_{x}
continuously depends on
|F(x)|\vee|x|\vee|\nabla F(x)|
so that the distortion function
G_{r,n}
is well defined as an
\mathbb{R}_{+}
-valued function defined on
U^{n}
. This assumption will be in force in all what follows.

Remark.
When
F=|\cdot|^{2}
, then
\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x)=|\xi-x|^{2}
and
G_{r,n}
is simply the regular
L^{r}
distortion function with respect to the Euclidean norm. This is the reason for choosing
\frac{r}{2}
as an exponent rather than simply
r
.

For notational convenience, when
r=2
we will drop the dependence in
r
and write
G_{n}
instead of
G_{r,n}
.

It is also convenient to rely on a more probabilistic formulation based on random variables. Thus, if
X:(\Omega,{\cal A},\mathbb{P})\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
with distribution
P
, then (
8
) reads equivalently

\mathbb{E}\,\big(|X|\vee|F(X)|\big)^{\frac{r}{2}}<+\infty

(11)

and the
(r,\phi_{{}_{F}})
-distortion function reads

\forall\,(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\!\in U^{n},\quad G_{r,n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\mathbb{E}\,\min_{i=1,\ldots,n}\phi_{{}_{F}}(X,x_{i})^{\frac{r}{2}}.

(12)

We will use both formulations.

4.2
Properties of
F
-Bregman divergences and
(r,\phi_{{}_{F}})
-distortion functions

We will extensively use the following properties satisfied by
P
,
\phi_{{}_{F}}
and
G_{r,n}
.

P1.
If
P
has a first moment. Then

\mathbb{E}\,X=\int_{U}\xi P(\mathrm{d}\xi)\!\in U

since
U
is convex.

In fact if
\mathbb{E}\,X\!\in\partial U=\bar{U}\setminus U
, then the supporting hyperplane theorem applied at
x=\mathbb{E}\,X\!\in\partial U
to the convex set
U
yields a contradiction. To be more precise, there exists a vector
u^{\perp}\!\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
|u^{\perp}|=1
such that
U\subset\{\xi:\langle\xi-x\,|\,u^{\perp}\rangle<0\}
so that integrating w.r.t.
P
implies
\big\langle\int_{U}\xi P(\mathrm{d}\xi)-x\,|\,u^{\perp}\big\rangle<0
which is impossible.

P2.

A bound of interest.
For every
\xi,\,x\!\in U
,

0\leq\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x)\leq\langle\nabla F(x)-\nabla F(\xi)\,|\,x-\xi\rangle.

(13)

This follows from the convexity inequality
F(x)\geq F(\xi)-\langle\nabla F(\xi)\,|\,x-\xi\rangle
satisfied by the function
F
.

This provides a criterion for the finiteness of the
(r,\phi_{{}_{F}})
-distortion function
G_{r,n}
, namely

\mathbb{E}\,(|\nabla F(X)||X|)^{r/2}=\int_{U}(|\nabla F(\xi)||\xi|)^{r/2}P(\mathrm{d}\xi)<+\infty,

since

|F(\xi)|\leq|F(\xi_{0})|+|\langle\nabla F(\xi)\,|\,\xi-\xi_{0}\rangle|\leq|F(\xi_{0})|+|\nabla F(\xi)||\xi-\xi_{0}|

for any
\xi,\,\xi_{0}\!\in U
.

P3.

A useful identity.
The Bregman divergence is not a distance in general since it is usually not symmetric and, even more penalizing, it does not satisfy a triangle inequality. However the following elementary identity can be sometimes called upon to “make the job”. (We leave its easy proof to the reader). For every
u,\,v,\,w\!\in U
,

\phi_{{}_{F}}(u,v)+\phi_{{}_{F}}(v,w)=\phi_{{}_{F}}(u,w)+\langle\nabla F(w)-\nabla F(v)\,|\,u-v\rangle.

(14)

which can also be rewritten

\phi_{{}_{F}}(u,w)=\phi_{{}_{F}}(u,v)+\phi_{{}_{F}}(v,w)-\langle\nabla F(w)-\nabla F(v)\,|\,w-v\rangle+\langle\nabla F(w)-\nabla F(v)\,|\,w-u\rangle.

(15)

P4.

Continuity of
G_{r,n}
on
U^{n}
.
It is clear that for every
\xi\!\in U
,
U^{n}\owns x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\mapsto\displaystyle\min_{i}\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x_{i})
is continuous on
U^{n}
since
F
is
{\cal C}^{1}
.

Moreover on a compact neighbourhood
K\subset U^{n}
of
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
, one has

\forall\,y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\!\in K,\quad\min_{i}\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x_{i})^{\frac{r}{2}}\leq\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x_{1})^{\frac{r}{2}}\leq C_{K}(|F(\xi)|\vee|\xi|)^{\frac{r}{2}}

owing to (
10
) since
F
and
\nabla F
are bounded on
K
by continuity. The continuity of
G_{r,n}
on
U^{n}
follows by Lebesgue’s dominated continuity theorem.

Remark.
The same reasoning shows that, if both
F
and
\nabla F
admit a continuous extension on
\bar{U}
, then,
G_{r,n}
can be defined for any distribution
P
supported by
\bar{U}
and continuously extended on
\bar{U}^{n}
as well. However, we will consider in what follows only distributions
P
supported by
U
.

P5.

Lower semi-continuous extensions of
\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,\cdot)
and
G_{r,n}
.

\rhd

General case.
For technical purposes we will extensively need to extend the functions
\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,\cdot)
,
\xi\!\in U
, over the Alexandroff – or one-point – compactification
\bar{U}^{\widehat{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}=\mathbb{R}^{d}\cup\{\infty\}
of
U
where
U
is viewed as an open subset of the Alexandroff compactification
\widehat{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, equipped with its usual (metric) topology. Using this trick goes back to
[
21
]
and is used in
[
7
]
as well. If
U
is bounded, then
\bar{U}^{\widehat{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}=\bar{U}
and
\partial\bar{U}^{\widehat{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}=\partial U
otherwise i.e.
\bar{U}^{\widehat{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}=\bar{U}\cup\{\infty\}
. If we denote by

\widehat{\partial}\,U:=\partial\bar{U}^{\widehat{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}

the boundary of
U
in
\widehat{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
, then
\widehat{\partial}\,U=\partial U
if
U
is bounded and
\widehat{\partial}\,U=\partial U\cup\{\infty\}
if
U
is unbounded.

One extends the Bregman divergences functions
\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,\cdot)
for every
\xi\!\in U
by their lower-semi-continuous (l.s.c.) envelopes, still denoted
\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,\cdot)
, defined on the boundary of
U
by

\displaystyle\forall\,\bar{x}\!\in\widehat{\partial}U,\;\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,\bar{x})

\displaystyle:=\liminf_{x\to\bar{x},\,x\in U}\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x)

\displaystyle:=\inf\!\big\{\lim_{n}\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x_{n}),\,x_{n}\to x,\,x_{n}\!\in U,\,\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x_{n})\mbox{ converges in }\bar{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\big\}.

(16)

Note that this infimum is in fact a minimum and that one may consider
a posteriori

\bar{U}^{\widehat{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}
-valued sequences
(x_{n})
in the definition. Thus extended,
\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,\cdot)
is l.s.c. for every
\xi\!\in U
.

Then one extends accordingly the
(r,P,F
)-distortion function
G_{r,n}
to
(\bar{U}^{\widehat{\mathbb{R}^{d}}})^{n}
formally by (
9
) or (
12
) using the above l.s.c. extension (
16
) of the functions
\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,\cdot)
, namely

\forall\,\xi\!\in U,\;\forall\,(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\!\in(\bar{U}^{\widehat{\mathbb{R}^{d}}})^{n},\quad G_{r,n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\mathbb{E}\,\min_{i=1,\ldots,n}\phi_{{}_{F}}(X,x_{i})^{\frac{r}{2}}.

(17)

One checks that, as a straightforward consequence of Fatou’s lemma, such an extension of
G_{r,n}
is in turn l.s.c. on
(\bar{U}^{\widehat{\mathbb{R}^{d}}})^{n}
.

\rhd

The unidimensional case (
d=1
)
. When
d=1
there is an alternative to the one-point Alexandroff’s compactification of
\mathbb{R}
that takes advantage of the natural order on
\mathbb{R}
. It consists in topologically identifying
\mathbb{R}
to
(-1,1)
by an appropriate increasing homeomorphism so that
\bar{\mathbb{R}}=[-\infty,\infty]
is homeomorphic to
[-1,1]
. This compactification preserves the natural order on
\mathbb{R}
. The closure
\bar{U}^{\bar{\mathbb{R}}}
of an unbounded convex interval
U\subset\mathbb{R}
is obtained by adding to its closure
\bar{U}
in
\mathbb{R}
,
\pm\infty
depending on possible infinite endpoints. Then, one can extends the function
\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,\cdot)
,
\xi\!\in U
by l.s.c. envelope at every point of the boundary
\bar{\partial}\,U
of
U
in
\bar{\mathbb{R}}
.

P6.

Marginal convexity and monotonicity of Bregman divergence.

\rhd

Convexity of
\phi_{{}_{F}}(\cdot,x)
.
First notice that for any fixed
x\!\in U
,
\xi\mapsto\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x)
is obviously convex as a sum of a convex and an affine functions. Note that, by contrast, the function
x\mapsto\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x)
is not convex in general as illustrated by Figure
1
.

\rhd

Monotonicity of
\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,\cdot)
.
The following lemma deals with the monotonicity properties of
x\mapsto\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x)
for a fixed
\xi\!\in U
. For every point
\xi\!\in U
and every vector
v\!\in S(0,1)
(unit Euclidean sphere), we define

t^{-}_{\xi,v}:=\inf\{t<0:\xi+tv\!\in U\}\!\in[-\infty,0)\quad\mbox{ and }\quad t^{+}_{\xi,v}:=\sup\{t>0:\xi+tv\!\in U\}\!\in(0,+\infty].

The signs of
t^{\pm}_{\xi,v}
are clear since
U
is open. Furthermore, for every
t\!\in(-t^{-}_{\xi,v},t^{+}_{\xi,v})
,
\xi+tv\!\in U
.

Lemma 4.1
(Monotonicity)

(a)
For every
\xi\!\in U
,
v\!\in S(0,1)
, the mapping
(t^{-}_{\xi,v},t^{+}_{\xi,v})\owns t\mapsto\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,\xi+tv)
is decreasing on
(t^{-}_{\xi,v},0]
and increasing on
[0,t^{+}_{\xi,v})
.

(b)
For every
\bar{x}\!\in\widehat{\partial}\,U
,
\xi\!\in U
,
\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,\bar{x}):=\displaystyle\liminf_{x\to\bar{x},\,x\in U}\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x)>0
.

In particular, when
U
is unbounded (so that
\infty\!\in\hat{\partial}U
)

\forall\,\xi\!\in U,\quad\liminf_{|x|\to\infty,\,x\in U}\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x)>0.

(18)

(c)
When
d=1
, Claim
(b)
is also true for the boundary
\bar{\partial}\,U
of
U
in
\bar{\mathbb{R}}=[-\infty,+\infty]
.

Proof.

(a)
First note that the function
f(t)=\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,\xi+t\,u)
is well defined on
(t^{-}_{\xi,u},t^{+}_{\xi,u})
. Let
t^{\prime}>t>0
.
Elementary computations show that, for
t,\,t^{\prime}\!\in[0,t^{+}_{\xi,u})
,
t^{\prime}>t
,

\displaystyle\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,\xi+t^{\prime}u)-\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,\xi+tu)

\displaystyle=F(\xi+t\,u)-F(\xi+t^{\prime}u)+t^{\prime}\langle\nabla F(\xi+t^{\prime}\,u)|\,u\rangle-t\langle\nabla F(\xi+t\,u)|\,u\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\langle\nabla F(\xi+t^{\prime}\,u)|\,u\rangle(t-t^{\prime})+t^{\prime}\langle\nabla F(\xi+t^{\prime}\,u)|\,u\rangle-t\langle\nabla F(\xi+t\,u)|\,u\rangle

\displaystyle=t\langle\nabla F(\xi+t^{\prime}\,u)-\nabla F(\xi+t\,u)|\,u\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{t}{t^{\prime}-t}\langle\nabla F(\xi+t^{\prime}\,u)-\nabla F(\xi+t\,u)|\,(\xi+t^{\prime}\,u-(\xi+t\,u)\rangle>0,

where we used in the second line the convexity of
F
. The same computations show that
\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,\xi+t^{\prime}u)-\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,\xi+tu)<0
if
t<t^{\prime}\leq 0
.

(b)
Let
\bar{x}\!\in\hat{\partial}U
and
\eta=\frac{1}{2}\big(d(\xi,\bar{x})\wedge 1\big)\!\in(0,1]
(
d(\xi,\bar{x})=|\xi-\bar{x}|
if
\bar{x}\neq\infty
). Let
x_{n}
,
n\geq 1
, be a sequence of elements of
U
such that
x_{n}\to\bar{x}\!\in\widehat{\partial}U
. For large enough
n
,
x_{n}\!\in B(\xi,\eta)^{c}
. Let
x_{n,\eta}
be such that
|\xi-x_{n,\eta}|=\eta
and
x_{n,\eta}\!\in(\xi,x_{n})=\{t\xi+(1-t)x_{n},t\!\in[0,1]\}
(geometric interval). It exists by continuity of the norm. Then, by
(a)
applied with
u_{n}=\frac{x_{n}-\xi}{|x_{n}-\xi|}
, it follows that
\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x_{n})\geq\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x_{n,\eta})
. Consequently,

\liminf_{n}\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x_{n})\geq\liminf_{n}\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x_{n,\eta})\geq\inf_{y:|\xi-y|=\eta}\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,y)>0

since
\phi
is uniformly continuous on the compact set
\{\xi\}\times S(\xi,\eta)
. As a consequence

\liminf_{x\to\bar{x},x\in U}\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x)\geq\inf_{y:|\xi-y|=\eta}\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,y)>0.

(c)
is obvious.

\Box

Remarks.

\bullet
The functions
x\mapsto\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x)
are generally not convex as can be easily seen e.g. by computing their Hessian when
F
is three time differentiable.

\bullet
The same monotonicity property holds for the functions
t\mapsto\phi_{{}_{F}}(x+tv,x)
when
v\!\in S(0,1)
since

\partial_{t}\phi_{{}_{F}}(x+tv,x)=\langle\nabla F(x+tv)-\nabla F(x)\,|\,v\rangle=\frac{1}{t}\langle\nabla F(x+tv)-\nabla F(x)\,|\,x+tv-x\rangle

which changes its sign when
t
crosses
0
owing to the strict convexity of the function
F
. But, in contrast it is clear by its very definition that
\xi\mapsto\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x)
is convex for every
x\!\in U
.

P7.

F
-Bregman Voronoi cells.

By analogy with the Voronoi partitions of a quantizer
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
associated w.r.t. a norm, we define below their
counterparts with respect to the
F
-Bregman divergence as follows. This analogy is stronger than expected (see below) due to the following elementary equivalences.

For every
\xi
,
a
,
b\!\in U
,
a\neq b

\displaystyle\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,a)\begin{pmatrix}<\\
\leq\end{pmatrix}\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,b)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\langle\nabla F(b)-\nabla F(a)|\xi-a\rangle\begin{pmatrix}<\\
\leq\end{pmatrix}\phi_{{}_{F}}(a,b)

(19)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\varphi_{a,b}(\xi):=\langle\nabla F(b)-\nabla F(a)|\xi-a\rangle\begin{pmatrix}<\\
\leq\end{pmatrix}\phi_{{}_{F}}(a,b).

(20)

Note that, as
F
is strictly convex,
\langle\nabla F(b)-\nabla F(a)|b-a\rangle>0
so that
\nabla F(a)\neq\nabla F(b)
. Hence the linear form
\varphi_{a,b}
is non zero since
\varphi_{a,b}(b)>0
. As a consequence, all the sets
\{\varphi_{a,b}=c\}
,
c\!\in\mathbb{R}
, are hyperplanes that partition
\mathbb{R}^{d}
into two half-spaces
\{\varphi_{a,b}\leq c\}
and
\{\varphi_{a,b}>c\}
.

Definition 4.1
(Bregman–Voronoi partition of a quantizer)

Let
\Gamma\subset U
be a finite set. A Borel partition
\big(C_{a}(\Gamma)\big)_{a\in\Gamma}
of
U
satisfying

C_{a}(\Gamma)\subset\bigcap_{b\in\Gamma}\big\{\xi\!\in U:\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,a)\leq\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,b)\big\}

is called a Bregman–Voronoi partition of
U
.

It is clear that for every
a\!\in\Gamma
,
a\!\in C_{a}(\Gamma)
. It follows from (
19
) and (
20
) that

\bigcap_{b\in\Gamma,b\neq a}\big\{\xi\!\in U:\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,a)<\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,b)\big\}=\mathring{C}_{a}(\Gamma)\subset\bar{C}_{a}(\Gamma)=\bigcap_{b\in\Gamma}\big\{\xi\!\in U:\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,a)\leq\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,b)\big\}

(where interior and closure are taken w.r.t. to the induced topology on
U
) with

\mathring{C}_{a}(\Gamma)=\hskip-7.11317pt\bigcap_{b\neq a,\,b\in\Gamma}\big\{\xi\!\in U:\varphi_{a,b}(\xi)<\phi_{{}_{F}}(a,b)\big\}\;\mbox{ and }\;\bar{C}_{a}(\Gamma)=\bigcap_{b\in\Gamma}\big\{\xi\!\in U:\phi_{a,b}(\xi)\leq\varphi_{{}_{F}}(a,b)\big\}.

A crucial fact is that, like for regular Voronoi diagrams with respect to an Euclidean norm,
\mathring{C}_{a}(\Gamma)
and
\bar{C}_{a}(\Gamma)
are polyhedral convex sets (open and closed w.r.t. the induced topology of
U
respectively). Note that interior, closure and boundary of the cells
C_{a}(\Gamma)
do not depend on the initial choice of the Bregman–Voronoi partition.

We associate to any such Bregman–Voronoi partition of
\Gamma
a
\phi_{F}
-nearest neighbour projection

\xi\mapsto{\rm Proj}_{\Gamma}^{F}(\xi):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\textbf{1}_{C_{i}(x)}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(\xi)}

and we define the
\phi_{{}_{F}}
-quantization
\widehat{X}^{\Gamma}
of a random vector
X:(\Omega,{\cal A})\rightarrow U
resulting from such a
\phi_{{}_{F}}
-Bregman–Voronoi partition by setting as expected

\widehat{X}^{\Gamma}={\rm Proj}_{\Gamma}^{F}(X)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\textbf{1}_{C_{i}(x)}(X)}.

We will now adapt for convenience Definition
4.1
of the Bregman–Voronoi partition of a grid/quantizer
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
to the case where we consider an
n
-tuple
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\!\in U^{n}
. The geometrical way to do that can be simply to say that
\big(C_{i}(x)\big)_{i=1,\ldots,n}=\big(C_{a}(\Gamma^{x})\big)_{a\in\Gamma^{x}}
but this does not allow for a precise definition of
C_{i}(x)
and
C_{j}(x)
when
x_{i}=x_{j}
.

Definition 4.2
(Bregman–Voronoi partition of an
n
-tuple)

Let
x\!\in U^{n}
. A Borel partition
\big(C_{i}(x)\big)_{1\leq i\leq n}
of
U

satisfying:

(i)
for every
i\!\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
,

C_{i}(x)\subset\bigcap_{j=1}^{n}\big\{\xi\!\in U:\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x_{i})\leq\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x_{j})\big\},

(ii)
if
C_{i}(x)\neq\varnothing
and
x_{j}=x_{i}
, then

Proposition 5.1

Assume the distribution
P
of
X
satisfies the moment assumption (
8
).

(a)
The distortion function
G_{1}:U\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
attains a unique strict minimum on
U
at
\mathbb{E}\,X\!\in U
.

(b)

Furthermore the (l.s.c.) extension
G_{1}:\bar{U}^{\widehat{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
still attains a unique strict minimum on
U
at
\mathbb{E}\,X\!\in U
. When
d=1
the same holds for the l.s.c. extension of
G_{1}
to
\bar{U}^{\bar{\mathbb{R}}}
.

Proof.

(a)
Note that

G_{1}(x)=\int_{U}\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x)P(\mathrm{d}\xi)=\mathbb{E}\,F(X)-F(x)-\langle\nabla F(x)\,|\,\mathbb{E}\,X-x\rangle

can be rewritten

G_{1}(x)=\mathbb{E}\,F(X)-F(\mathbb{E}\,X)+\phi_{{}_{F}}(\mathbb{E}\,X,x)

(22)

so that

\min_{U}G_{1}=\mathbb{E}\,F(X)-F(\mathbb{E}\,X)\;\mbox{ and }\;\displaystyle{\rm argmin}_{U}\,G_{1}=\{\mathbb{E}\,X\}\subset U

(23)

since
\phi\geq 0
,
\phi_{{}_{F}}(u,v)=0
if and only if
u=v
by the strict convexity of
F
.

(b)

Let
\bar{x}\!\in\bar{U}^{\widehat{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}
. One has by l.s.c. of
G_{1}
on
U^{\widehat{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}
that

\displaystyle G_{1}(\bar{x})

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{x\to\bar{x},x\in U}\mathbb{E}\,\phi_{{}_{F}}(X,x)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\,F(X)-F(\mathbb{E}\,X)+\liminf_{x\to\bar{x},x\in U}\mathbb{E}\,\phi_{{}_{F}}(\mathbb{E}\,X,x)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\,F(X)-F(\mathbb{E}\,X)+\phi_{{}_{F}}(\mathbb{E}\,X,\bar{x})

by definition (
16
) of
\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,\cdot)
on the Alexandroff boundary of
U
. But, as
\mathbb{E}\,X\!\in U
, it follows from Lemma
4.1
(b)
that
\phi_{{}_{F}}(\mathbb{E}\,X,\bar{x})>0
. Consequently, noting
\min_{U}G_{1}=G_{1}(\mathbb{E}\,X)=\mathbb{E}\,F(X)-F(\mathbb{E}\,X)
completes the proof.

\Box

Remark.

(a)

Corollary 5.1

If
x\!\in U^{n}
is a stationary quantizer at level
n\geq 1
(i.e. has pairwise distinct components with
P
-negligible Bregman–Voronoi boundary and satisfies the
\phi_{{}_{F}}
-master equation (
25
)), then all induced quantizations
\widehat{X}
are
\mathbb{P}
-

equal and

e_{2}(x,P,\phi_{{}_{F}})^{2}=\mathbb{E}\,F(X)-\mathbb{E}\,F\big(\widehat{X}^{x}\big).

(27)

In particular, if
x^{(n)}
is an optimal
F
-Bregman quantizer then

e_{2,n}(P,\phi_{{}_{F}})^{2}=\mathbb{E}\,F(X)-\mathbb{E}\,F\big(\widehat{X}^{x^{(n)}}\big).

(28)

Proof.
If
x\!\in U^{n}
is stationary then (
25
) reads

\forall\,i\!\in\{1,\ldots,n\},\quad\mathbb{E}\big(X\,|\,X\!\in C_{i}(x)\big)=x_{i}

with the convention that if
P\big(C_{i}(x)\big)=0
then we set
\mathbb{E}\big(X\,|\,X\!\in C_{i}(x)\big)=x_{i}
. Hence, by Formula (
21
) for
\widehat{X}^{x}
based on the nearest
\phi_{{}_{F}}
-neighbour projection

\mathbb{E}\,(X\,|\,\widehat{X}^{x})=\widehat{X}^{x}.

As a consequence

\displaystyle e_{2}(x,P,\phi_{{}_{F}})^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\,\phi_{{}_{F}}(X,\widehat{X}^{x})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\big(F(X)-F\big(\widehat{X}^{x}\big)-\langle\nabla F(\widehat{X}^{x})\,|\,X-\widehat{X}^{x}\rangle\big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big(F(X)-F\big(\widehat{X}^{x}\big)-\mathbb{E}\big(\langle\nabla F(\widehat{X}^{x})\,|\,X-\widehat{X}^{x}\rangle\,|\,\widehat{X}^{x}\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\big(F(X)-F\big(\widehat{X}^{x}\big)\big)-\mathbb{E}\Big(\mathbb{E}\big(\langle\nabla F(\widehat{X}^{x})\,|\,X-\widehat{X}^{x}\rangle\,|\,\widehat{X}^{x}\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\big(F(X)-F\big(\widehat{X}^{x}\big)\big)-\mathbb{E}\Big(\langle\nabla F(\widehat{X}^{x})\,|\,\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\big(X-\widehat{X}^{x}\rangle\,|\,\widehat{X}^{x}\big)}_{=0}\Big)

Finally, one gets this simple formula

e_{2}(x,P,\phi_{{}_{F}})^{2}=\mathbb{E}\big(F(X)-F\big(\widehat{X}^{x}\big)\big).

(29)

The application to
\phi_{{}_{F}}
-optimal quantizer is straightforward.

\Box

Remark.

Practitioner’s corner.
By analogy with optimal Voronoi quantization theory, Equation (
25
) can be called the
master equation
of Bregman–Voronoi optimal
\phi_{{}_{F}}
-quantization since it is the starting point of all methods to compute optimal quantizers. In the above form (
25
), it appears in gradient descents algorithms (a.k.a. Competitive Learning Vector Quantization for regular quantization). When implementing Lloyd’s fixed point procedure (also known as
k
-means in unsupervised learning and clustering), one rewrites it in its original form (see

[
20
]
), i.e. as a fixed point equation (where

):

x^{(n)}_{i}=\frac{\int_{C_{i}(x^{(n)})}\xi P(\mathrm{d}\xi)}{P(C_{i}(x^{(n)}))}=\mathbb{E}\,\big(X\,|\,X\in C_{i}(x^{(n)})\big)\!\in C_{i}(x^{(n)}),\quad=1,\ldots,n.

(30)

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.
(a)
The proof is based on an induction on the quantization level
n
. We assume that both divergences
\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,\cdot)
and distortion
G_{n}
are extended to
\bar{U}^{\widehat{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}
and
\big(\bar{U}^{\widehat{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}\big)^{n}
by (
16
) and (
17
) (their l.s.c. envelopes) respectively. Our aim is to prove that these extensions attain a minimum in
U^{n}
i.e. that
{\rm argmin}_{(\bar{U}^{\widehat{\mathbb{R}^{d}}})^{n}}G_{n}\subset U^{n}
and is nonempty.

\rhd

n=1
. This follows from
Proposition
5.1
(b)
.

\rhd

n\Rightarrow n+1
. Let
\displaystyle x^{(n)}\!\in{\rm argmin}_{U^{n}}\,G_{n}={\rm argmin}_{\bar{U}^{n}}\,G_{n}\neq\varnothing
supposed to be nonempty by the induction assumption.

If
G_{n}(x^{(n)})=0
, then
|{\rm supp}_{U}(P)|\leq n
and
\min_{U^{n+1}}G_{n+1}=G_{n+1}(x^{(n)},x^{(n)}_{1})=G_{n}(x^{(n)})=0
.

If
G_{n}(x^{(n)})>0
, then
|{\rm supp}_{U}(P)|\geq n+1
since
x^{(n)}
has pairwise distinct components. Moreover, there exists
\displaystyle x_{n+1}\!\in{\rm supp}_{U}(P)
such that
P\big(B(x_{n+1},\eta)\cap U\big)>0
for every
\eta>0
.
Then,
\displaystyle\big\{\xi\!\in U:\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x_{n+1})<\min_{i=1,\ldots,n}\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x^{(n)}_{i})\big\}
is an open set since
\displaystyle\xi\mapsto\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x_{n+1})-\min_{i=1,\ldots,n}\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x^{(n)}_{i})
is continuous on
U
, nonempty since it contains
x_{n+1}
. Consequently it has a positive

P
-probability which clearly entails that
G_{n+1}\big(x^{(n)},x_{n+1}\big)<G_{n}(x^{(n)})=\min_{U^{n}}G_{n}
.

Set
\lambda_{n+1}=G_{n+1}(x^{(n)},x_{n+1})<\min_{U^{n}}G_{n}
and
B_{n+1}=\big\{x\!\in(\bar{U}^{\widehat{\mathbb{R}^{d}}})^{n+1}:G_{n+1}(x)\leq\lambda_{n+1}\big\}
.
The set
B_{n+1}
is clearly closed in
(\bar{U}^{\widehat{\mathbb{R}^{d}}})^{n+1}
since (the l.s.c. extension to
\widehat{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
of)

is l.s.c. Hence
B_{n+1}
is compact in
\bar{U}^{\widehat{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}
. Consequently,
G_{n+1}
attains its minimum on

at some
n+1
-tuple

which is clearly a minimum of
G_{n+1}
on the whole
(\bar{U}^{\widehat{\mathbb{R}^{d}}})^{n+1}
. Moreover,
x^{(n+1)}
has pairwise distinct components, otherwise
G_{n+1}\big(x^{(n+1)}\big)=G_{n}\big((x^{(n+1)}_{i})_{i=1,\ldots,n+1,i\neq i_{0}}\big)
for some index
i_{0}
, hence
G_{n+1}\big(x^{(n+1)}\big)\geq\min_{U^{n}}G_{n}>\lambda_{n+1}
which is impossible.

Let us prove that
x^{(n+1)}
lies in
U^{n+1}
so that
x^{(n+1)}\!\in{\rm argmin}_{U^{n+1}}G_{n+1}
.
First, if (
U
is unbounded and)

for some

, then, by Condition (
24
) for every
\xi\!\in U

for every
\!u\in U
. In particular
\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x^{(n+1)}_{i})\geq\min_{\ell\neq i}\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x^{(n+1)}_{\ell})

which contradicts that

(
\bar{U}

).

.

Set
P_{i}:=P(\,\cdot\,|\,U_{i})
. Then it follows from Proposition

(b)
that

\displaystyle=\int_{U\setminus\mathring{C}_{i}(x^{(n)})}\min_{j=1,\ldots,n}\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x_{j})P(\mathrm{d}\xi)=\int_{U\setminus\mathring{C}_{i}(x^{(n)})}\min_{j\neq i}\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x_{j})P(\mathrm{d}\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{U}\min_{j\neq i}\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x_{j})P(\mathrm{d}\xi)

\displaystyle=G_{n-1}\big(x^{(n)}_{j},\,j\neq i\big)\geq\min_{U^{n-1}}G_{n-1}>\min_{U^{n}}G_{n}

which contradicts the minimality of
x^{(n)}
. Hence

.

and

. Then applying (

. If

\displaystyle G_{n}\big(x^{(n)}_{1},\ldots,x^{(n)}_{i-1},x^{*}_{i},x^{(n)}_{i+1},\ldots,x^{(n)}_{n}\big)

\displaystyle\leq G_{n}(x^{(n)})

\displaystyle\quad\;-\int_{\mathring{C}_{i}(x^{(n)})}\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x^{(n)}_{i})P(\mathrm{d}\xi)+\int_{\mathring{C}_{i}(x^{(n)})}\phi_{{}_{F}}(\xi,x^{*}_{i})P(\mathrm{d}\xi)

To alleviate notation let us temporarily drop

These results may look at a first glance unexpected since it is clear that the
SoftPlus
function itself is not symmetric. However if one has in mind that the functional
F\longmapsto\phi_{{}_{F}}
is (linear but) not bijective since if
\phi_{{}_{F}}
then
\phi=\phi_{F_{\alpha,\beta}}
for all functions
F_{\alpha,\beta}(x)=\langle a|x\rangle+\beta
,
\alpha\!\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\beta\!\in\mathbb{R}
, the paradox is elucidate. So in this framework one can replace the function
F=\hbox{SoftPlus}_{a}
function by

F_{1/2,0}(x)=2\log(1+e^{x/2})+\tfrac{x}{2}

which is symmetric and has the same symmetric second derivative and for which the conclusion of Theorem
8.3
would have appeared less misleading but for bad reasons. Examples of larger optimal
n
-quantizers are proposed in
[
2
, Chapter 4]
.

\bullet
As soon as
F
is symmetric and
{\cal C}^{2}
,
F^{\prime\prime}
is symmetric as well. Thus the
Soft butterfly
similarity measure which has a
\log
-concave second derivative satisfies the assumptions of the theorem.

Remark.
Theorem
8.3
can be extended to other kinds of symmetry properties. If
P
is invariant by
\xi\mapsto 1-\xi
and
F^{\prime\prime}(x)=F^{\prime\prime}(1-x)
(e.g. because
F
satisfies such invariance), then, if
x^{(n)}
is an optimal quantizer of
P
at level
n
, then
(1-x^{(n)}_{n+1-i})_{i=1,\ldots,n}
is also optimal and in case of uniqueness, then
x^{(n)}_{i}=1-x^{(n)}_{n+1-i}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
.

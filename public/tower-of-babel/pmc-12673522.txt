Deciphering Ni Content–Induced Microstructural Evolution in Ni–YSZ Cermets for Direct Ammonia SOFC Performance
Authors: Omer Elmutasim, Dattatray S. Dhawale, Maxwell Pinczes, Lina Hamid, Sherman Wong, Sarbjit Giddey, Gary Paul, Sankar Bhattacharya
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The Ni–YSZ anode of a direct ammonia solid oxide fuel cell (DA–SOFC) with three different Ni contents is investigated and compared through microstructural analysis and electrochemical measurements of the cell. This work reveals that increasing the Ni loading by adjusting the initial NiO/YSZ weight ratio from 50:50, 60:40, and further to 65:35 improves the peak power density to 122, 313.4, and 383.9 mW/cm2, respectively, at 800 °C. This can be explained by the diminishing trend in polarization resistance, particularly the charge transfer resistance (R H), with increasing Ni content. The three dimensional (3D) microstructure of the three anodes is characterized using X-ray micro-computed tomography (micro-CT), and each phase’s volume fraction and associated particle Feret diameter, directional percolation, tortuosity factors, triple-phase boundary (TPB) density, effective ionic and electronic conductivities, surface areas, and interfacial contact areas per unit volume are quantified. These parameters are then correlated to the electrochemical performance of the three anode compositions to understand the impact of microstructural variation. The Ni and YSZ networks are almost entirely connected, accounting for average percolation percentages of >98.7% for all anode compositions. It is noteworthy that no evident correlation is found between total TPB density and polarization resistance, suggesting that other transport-related descriptors, including tortuosity and percolation, are at play. The NiO/YSZ (65:35) SOFC showed promising stability over 96 h at 800 °C with a low voltage degradation of 5.06%, which is ascribed to Ni coarsening, as confirmed by post-test micro-CT analysis.

Introduction

Solid oxide fuel cells (SOFC) have emerged as a promising energy conversion technology owing to their exceptional electrical efficiency in converting fuels to power. It also offers the distinct advantage of fuel flexibility. , Even though hydrogen is the common fuel for commercial SOFC systems, it is associated with transportation and storage challenges. Despite the higher energy density of hydrocarbon fuels, including ethanol, methanol, ethane, and methane, they cause severe coking because of the cracking reaction. However, ammonia stands out as a carbon-free fuel for SOFC with relatively higher energy density. In addition, ammonia is a better option for transportation and storage, as it can be readily liquefied under relatively milder conditions, such as 1 atm pressure at −33 °C or 10 atm at room temperature, compared to hydrogen. However, the key bottlenecks of direct ammonia-fed SOFC (DA–SOFC) are the sluggish kinetics of ammonia cracking and poor stability of the anode surface. ,

The state-of-the-art nickel–yttria-stabilized zirconia (Ni–YSZ) anode cermets have been extensively used in SOFCs owing to their exceptional electrochemical reactivity toward H2 electro-oxidation, mixed ionic, and electronic conductivity. , Furthermore, they possess excellent thermomechanical compatibility with the commonly used YSZ electrolyte. The Ni–YSZ anode utilized in SOFC usually has a nickel volume fraction in the range of 40–70%. The weight fraction of Ni and YSZ is a key parameter for the microstructural and mechanical stability of Ni–YSZ because of the considerable variation in the thermal expansion coefficient of Ni (16.9 × 10–6 K–1) and 8 mol % YSZ (8YSZ) (10.5 × 10–6 K–1). For instance, the predominant degradation mechanisms of Ni–YSZ cermets are the Ni nitridation under ammonia operation, as well as the agglomeration and coarsening of nickel, which exacerbate at higher Ni loading. ,− While increasing the YSZ content can reduce the Ni sintering by inhibiting Ni grain coarsening, it also raises the ohmic resistance of the SOFC. Therefore, optimizing the Ni/YSZ weight ratio is essential for achieving optimal SOFC performance of the Ni–YSZ cermet anode. Accordingly, if sufficient electronic conductivity could be preserved at reduced Ni loading, this might mitigate the Ni coarsening and thermal mismatch issues. However, lowering the Ni volume fraction below 40% leads to a diminished electronic conductivity of Ni–YSZ due to the presence of nonpercolating nickel network. ,

Quantifying the microstructural properties over a 3D volume that captures all relevant microstructural parameters is crucial for developing predictive models for DA–SOFC performance. Recent advances in characterization methods have allowed for the quantification of such microstructural features. For instance, the focused ion beam (FIB) combined with scanning electron microscope (FIB–SEM) enables the generation of 3D microstructural micrographs; however, this characterization method is destructive, as the sample needs to be milled to expose the Scanning Electron Microscope (SEM) slices, thereby eliminating the potential for retesting within the same targeted region. Thus, methods have shifted to using X-ray micro-computed tomography (micro-CT), which produces 3D reconstructions without the sample’s destruction. Through 3D microstructural characterization of SOFC anode materials, many microstructural parameters, including phase volume fraction, Feret diameter of particles/pore, surface areas, directional tortuosity factor, directional percolation percentage, and interfacial properties (interfacial contact areas and TPB density), effective ionic and electronic conductivities can be computed, which are essential to quantitatively connect the anode microstructure to the DA–SOFC performance. Although the triple-phase boundary density is widely recognized as a key microstructural feature determining the polarization resistance (R p) of Ni–YSZ anodes, there is limited quantitative information on how the Ni/YSZ ratio influences TPB density or R p. , A plethora of previous studies has focused on hydrogen-fueled SOFC, − However, few studies have adopted 3D microstructural techniques in the context of DA–SOFC. , For example, Kishimoto and colleagues employed FIB–SEM technique to perform 3D quantitative microstructural analysis of the state-of-the-art Ni–YSZ anode for direct ammonia-fed SOFCs, enabling quantification of the Ni–pore interface area density. Using this microstructural parameter, they developed a reaction rate model for the ammonia cracking reaction, which can be applied to the analysis and design of direct ammonia-fed SOFCs. Additionally, Liu et al. employed FIB–SEM to investigate the 3D morphology of Ni–BaZr0.1Ce0.7Y0.1Yb0.1O3–δ (Ni–BZCYYb) anodes prepared by one-step precursor mixing (OPM) and physical mixing (PM), coimpregnated with a Cs2O-decorated Ru catalyst for direct ammonia protonic ceramic fuel cells. The FIB–SEM analysis revealed that the NH3-treated OPM anode exhibited 42% higher interfacial contact area and 59% higher volume-specific surface area than the PM anode. Based on these results, the authors concluded that the OPM approach optimizes the interfacial structure, thereby enhancing electronic/ionic conduction and mass transfer and ultimately facilitating the kinetics of NH3 cracking. The understanding how the Ni/YSZ ratio influences both microstructural parameters and the overall performance of direct ammonia-fed SOFCs is of paramount importance. However, this aspect has been largely overlooked, as no prior study has systematically examined the effect of the Ni/YSZ ratio on 3D microstructural features and their correlation with electrochemical behavior under direct ammonia operation. By establishing this linkage, the present work advances DA–SOFC-specific understanding beyond the hydrogen-fed SOFC literature and provides new insights into anode design under ammonia-fueled conditions. This work examines the influence of the initial NiO/YSZ weight ratio on the electrochemical performance of direct ammonia SOFCs. Anode-supported layers (ASL) with NiO/YSZ weight ratios of 50:50, 60:40, and 65:35 were fabricated via a water-based co-tape casting and co-sintering technique and systematically analyzed. The 3D microstructure of the three anodes was characterized with micro-CT, and several microstructural features (phase volume fraction, Feret diameter of particles/pores, surface areas, directional tortuosity factor, and percolation), interfacial properties (interfacial contact areas and TPB density), and effective ionic and electronic conductivities were quantified as key parameters to elucidate the relationship between anode microstructure and the electrochemical performance of DA–SOFCs.

Methodology

Materials

The following powders were used to fabricate electrolyte and anode slips: 8 mol % Y2O3–ZrO2 (8YSZ) (TZ-8Y, Tosoh Co., Japan) with a specific surface area of 16 ± 3 m2/g, fine grade NiO (Fuel Cell Materials, OH) with D50 = 0.50–1.5 μm, and tapioca starch as pore former. The additives adopted for preparing the ceramic slips were part of an aqueous emulsion system (Polymer Innovations Inc., Vista, CA), specifically WB4101 as an acrylic binder, DS001 as the dispersant, DF002 as the nonsilicone defoamer, and TK004 and TK005 as the thickening agents. The commercial Ce0.8Gd0.1O1.95 (GDC) and La0.6Sr0.4Co0.2Fe0.8O3 (LSCF) inks were supplied by Fiaxell SOFC Technologies Co. Coco diethanolamide (Aaron Chemicals LLC) was used as a surfactant.

Cell Fabrication

The button electrolyte/anode half-cells utilized for this study consisted of three layers: a dense and thin YSZ electrolyte (10–15 μm thick), a thin NiO–YSZ anode functional layer (AFL, 13–15 μm thick), and a porous NiO–YSZ anode substrate layer (ASL, 290–343 μm thick). Three anode-supported SOFCs were fabricated using three distinct ASL slurries with NiO/YSZ weight ratios of 50:50, 60:40, and 65:35, while the ratio in the AFL was consistently maintained at 50:50 across the various cells. For all ASL slurries, the starch pore former constituted 10% of the total mixture of NiO, YSZ, and pore former powders. This study used initial weight ratios to describe different anodes.

Each ASL slurry for the aqueous-based co-tape casting process was prepared, using a jar roll and planetary ball mill, in three stages. In the first stage, the YSZ powder was milled, using a planetary ball mill and zirconia media, for 2 h with deionized water, binder (WB4101), defoamer (DF002), and ammonium hydroxide to prevent precipitation of the binder. In the second stage, NiO and tapioca starch were added to the slurry, with additional amounts of binder, defoamer, and ammonia and then rolled for 16 h. In the third stage, after the zirconia media was removed, the TK005 thickener was slowly added into the vortex as the slurry was being stirred. Subsequently, a diluted TK004 thickener was also introduced into the vortex to increase the viscosity of the slurry to fabricate a thick tape. The resultant slurry was then milled for 3 h. The AFL and electrolyte slurries were fabricated following the same procedure as for the ASL, with some modifications. Specifically, the AFL and electrolyte slurries have no starch and TK thickeners to achieve denser and thinner layers concerning the ASL. Considering the YSZ electrolyte slip, dispersant (DS001) was added, along with coco diethanolamide surfactant, to allow facile detachment of the tape from the Teflon substrate. The tape casting process was divided into four steps. First, a doctor blade was used to cast the YSZ electrolyte on a Teflon sheet. After drying at 40 °C for 2 h, the AFL layer was cast on the electrolyte layer and then dried at 40 °C for 2 h. Next, the viscous ASL slip was cast onto the dried AFL layer. After being dried overnight at ambient temperature, the layered green tape was cut into pre-fired half-cells (⌀:26 mm). Then, the half-cells with a diameter of 20 mm were obtained following co-firing at 1450 °C for 4 h under air. The GDC ink was brush-painted on the cofired half-cell, followed by firing in the air at 1200 °C for 2 h. Finally, the LSCF cathode, having an active area of 1.54 cm2, was brush-coated and sintered in air at 850 °C for 2 h.

Material Characterization

X-ray diffraction (XRD) was used to explore the phase structures of the various Ni–YSZ anodes before and after cell testing. The diffractograms were recorded with the help of a Rigaku SmartLab equipped with a rotating anode Cu Kα source (200 mA, 45 kV), and the 2θ range of 5–80° was scanned with 0.01° step sizes and a scanning rate of 0.5°/min. Field-emission SEM coupled with energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX) was employed to investigate further the microstructure and surface morphology of the various anodes.

In order to collect 3D microstructural data, a very small electrolyte/anode half-cell (⌀, ∼800 μm) was used for each sample. Prior to micro-CT imaging, each half-cell was reduced under 5% H2 in 95% N2 atmosphere at 800 °C for 24 h to convert the NiO to Ni. Then, lab-based micro-CT was carried out using a Zeiss Xradia 515 instrument equipped with a tungsten on diamond window X-ray source (10W) and an HE4 source filter. A 20x objective detector was employed to acquire 2401 scan projections per scan. The voltage and exposure time for each sample were individually chosen such that there was ∼20% transmission and >5000 counts per projection. Rectangular volume was cropped from the ASL of each half-cell to compare the three anodes. The anode composition as volume fractions (V phase) of each phase, surface area and interfacial contact areas, and TPB density (ρTPB) were quantified, as well as the directional tortuosity factors (τphase C ), percolation percentage, effective ionic (σion, C Eff), and electronic conductivities (σelect, C Eff) along the Cartesian axes (C = x, y, or z). Three software packages were adopted for quantitative assessment in this work: Avizo Fire (Avizo, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, Massachusetts, U.S.), an open-source MATLAB application TauFactor, and in-house python code. Spatial Orientations

The x, y, and z-directions were considered to determine the orientation of the anode with reference to its location within the 3D volume. The z-axis is orthogonal to the electrolyte–anode interface, while the x-axis and y-axis are orthogonal to each other and parallel to the interface.

Composition

The volume fraction (Vphase) of each phase was estimated using Avizo’s volume fraction algorithm, TauFactor, and in-house python code. The three packages generated identical values; therefore, only one value was presented in this work. The representative volume element (RVE) analysis was performed for each phase volume fraction. This analysis was carried out by calculating the phase volume fraction for a single-slice representative volume and then progressively expanding the representative volume by adding one slice at a time until the entire 3D volume was explored, following a constant-area, growing-volume technique.

Particle Size

The Feret diameter of particles were quantified using ImageJ software.

Tortuosity Factor and Percolation

The tortuosity factor (τphase,C ) and percolation percentages (p phase,C ) for each direction were computed using TauFactor for each phase.

Triple-Phase Boundary

The total TPB density (ρTPB) was estimated using TauFactor, whereas the local TPB density (ρTPB,z) in the z-axis was assessed using the in-house Python code.

Surface Area and Interfacial Contact Area

The volume-specific surface area (SSA) of each phase and interfacial contact areas (SIA) for Ni–pore, Ni–YSZ, and YSZ–pore was quantified using TauFactor. The local SIA of Ni–YSZ in the z-axis was computed using the in-house Python code.

Electrochemical Testing

The Open Flanges Test fixture (Fiaxell SOFC Technologies, Switzerland) was utilized for electrochemical measurements. The SOFC fuel and air electrodes were sealed with mica sheets. Silver mesh and nickel foam were employed for current collection on the air and fuel electrodes, respectively. Two alumina felts were positioned above the Ag collector for isolation and sealing. The setup was placed into a furnace, where it was heated to 800 °C at a controlled rate of 3 °C/min and maintained at that temperature under an anhydrous ammonia atmosphere for 16 h. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) analysis was carried out with the Zahner IM6 electrochemical workstation under open-circuit voltage (OCV) conditions, with a voltage amplitude of 20 mV and a frequency range of 100 mHz to 40 kHz. The spectra were measured across a temperature range of 700–800 °C. The Z view software was used to fit the EIS data into an equivalent circuit model (ECM). The SOFC performance in terms of voltage–current (V–I) characteristics was assessed within the temperature range of 700–800 °C. The tests were conducted using ammonia and airflow rates of 27 and 100 mL/min, respectively. Electrical efficiency, expressed as the ratio of generated power to the NH3 thermal energy input (eq ), along with ammonia utilization percentage (eq ), was determined as follows: 3 η=VI−ΔHn.fuel,in=3FUfV−ΔH Uf=InFn.NH3,in 4 where η represents the electrical efficiency, I is the current, V is the average voltage of the cell, ΔH refers to the enthalpy change of reaction, U f and n NH3,in are ammonia fuel utilization and molar flow rate of ammonia, respectively, F is the Faraday constant, and n is the number of electron moles involved in the oxidation of one mole of ammonia (n = 3).

The conversion percentage of the NH3 cracking reaction over the Ni–YSZ anode was measured through a fixed-bed reactor. First, the NiO–YSZ (65:35) powder was reduced under an H2 atmosphere at a flow rate of 20 mL/min for 16 h to fully convert the NiO into Ni metal. Then, ammonia was run over the reduced Ni–YSZ powder at a flow rate of 25 mL/min for 20 min at different temperatures of at 700, 750, and 800 °C. The exhaust gases were captured in a sulfuric acid trap, and the concentration of ammonia captured was determined through an HACH HQ430d flexi probe. The concentration of captured ammonia was then used to calculate the amount of unreacted ammonia, which was used to compute the NH3 conversion of the catalyst.

Phase Volume Fraction and Associated Pores/Grains Volumes

The representative volume element analysis for the Ni, YSZ, and pore volume fractions across the volume of each anode sample is shown in Figure (a–c), and their corresponding local volume fractions were also computed on each slice, separated by the voxel size of about 0.43 μm, along the z-direction for the three anode compositions (Figure (d–f)). The RVE analysis showed that the 65:35 NiO/YSZ anode composition presented the highest pore volume fraction among all samples (Figure (c)). This can be ascribed to the formation of the pore during the reduction of NiO to the Ni phase, causing this anode sample to exhibit the greatest porosity because of its highest initial NiO content. No significant local variation was observed in the three phases across the three anode samples. As expected, increasing the initial NiO/YSZ ratio of the ASL from 50:50 to 60:40 and 65:35 has led to an increase in the Ni volume fractions from 0.43 to 0.55 and 0.62, as well as in pore fractions from 0.06 to 0.08 and 0.11, respectively (Figure (g)). It can also be noted that the ratios of Ni/YSZ solid volume fractions, i.e., fractions with respect to the total solid Ni and YSZ phases only, were quantified to be 45.4:54.6, 59.0:41.0, and 69.1:30.9, which are in good agreement with the initial NiO/YSZ weight ratios of 50:50, 60:40, and 65:35, respectively. Both the mean and individual volumes of the pore phase, Ni, and YSZ grains at various percentiles for the three anodes were tabulated in Table S1 (Supporting Information file). The mean Ni particle volume increased progressively with a higher Ni loading in the anode. Beyond the 25th percentile, the Ni particle volumes are consistently larger as the Ni/YSZ ratio increased, highlighting a clear trend of Ni particle agglomeration. This behavior reflects the enhanced mobility Ni at higher loadings, which accelerates sintering and contributes to larger particle growth.

Tortuosity Factor and Percolation

The tortuosity factor (τphase,C ) of anode materials is a crucial parameter that influences the ohmic resistance in solid phases, i.e., Ni and YSZ, and the diffusion resistance in the gas phase. Also, the tortuosity within each phase’s pathway can affect the transport to and from TPB. The anisotropic tortuosity factors of the metal and ceramic in the X, Y, and Z spatial orientations as well as their average values, were calculated for the three anode compositions (Figure (a,b)). It should be mentioned that pore tortuosity was not considered in this work because its low phase volume fraction is below the typical percolation threshold; thus, a nonpercolating pore phase exists. The tortuosity factors values presented in this work are typical of Ni–YSZ anodes and are comparable with the values reported in the literature. , Interestingly, the tortuosity of the Ni phase increased in the three spatial orientations as the Ni volume fraction decreased, reaching an average value of 2.59 for the NiO/YSZ ratio of 50:50. This high tortuosity can be attributed to the lengthy path needed to circumnavigate the highly dense YSZ networks at low Ni composition. Similarly, a diminishing trend of average τ values for the YSZ phase was noticed with increasing YSZ content. Considering the three spatial directions, larger τ values of Ni and YSZ phases were noticed along the z-axis, orthogonal to the electrolyte/anode interface, for anodes with NiO/YSZ ratios of 50:50 and 60:40. For instance, the latter recorded substantially higher τ value of 8.35 for the YSZ phase in the Z-plane, compared to 2.72 and 2.12 in the z- and y-directions. This implies the existence of non-uniformities of Ni and YSZ phases in the direction perpendicular to the interface. This contradicts what would be expected in a spatially homogeneous ASL microstructure, pointing out the possibility of structural defects. On the contrary, the anode with a NiO/YSZ ratio of 65:35 demonstrated comparable τ z to τ x and τ y for both phases.

(a, b) Directional tortuosity factor and (c, d) percolation percentage for Ni and YSZ on different ASL compositions in the x-, y-, and z-directions.

It is well-known that the percolation, the existence of a continuous network of phases, is essential, as the species participating in the electrochemical hydrogen oxidationi.e., electrons in Ni, ammonia molecules in pores, and oxide ions in YSZshould be capable of reaching the TPB. , TPB can be electrochemically active when the three phases are percolated. Accordingly, an optimum electrode microstructure maximizes the percolation percentage. The percolation (p phase,C ) of Ni and YSZ phases in the three orientations was assessed (Figure (c, d)). It is noteworthy that both Ni and YSZ percolation was independent of the directional orientation, as reflected by p Ni,x = p Ni,y = p Ni,z and p YSZ,x = p YSZ,y = p YSZ,z. As expected, the Ni phase was almost 100% percolated for all samples, as it had the largest volume fraction compared to other phases. On the other hand, the YSZ phase was almost entirely connected (99.9%) at an NiO/YSZ ratio of 50:50; however, the connectivity diminished slightly to 98.7% at a higher NiO/YSZ ratio of 65:35 due to a reduction in the YSZ content.

Effective Ionic and Electronic Conductivities

It is well established in the literature that the YSZ phase in the Ni–YSZ anode provides the required ionic conduction paths for oxide anions, while the Ni phase avails electronic conductivity. The effective ionic and electronic conductivities of YSZ and Ni were calculated in the x, y, and z-directions at temperatures of 700, 750, and 800 °C (Figure ). The effective ionic conductivity of the three anode compositions improved considerably with temperature increase because the thermal energy available at elevated temperatures enables the oxide anions to migrate easily within the YSZ phase. The percolation and volume fractions of YSZ and Ni are crucial factors influencing the anode’s conductivity. As anticipated, changing the initial NiO/YSZ ratio of the ASL from 65:35 to 60:40 and 50:50 has resulted in an increase in average ionic conductivity at the studied temperature because of the highest YSZ fraction as well as phase connectivity at an NiO/YSZ ratio of 50:50, accounting for an average ionic conductivity of 1.11 S/m at 800 °C. It is noteworthy that the 60:40 ASL composition exhibited the lowest effective ionic conductivity along the z-direction, which can be directly correlated with the unusually high tortuosity computed in the same direction. Elevated tortuosity implies more convoluted and less direct ion transport pathways, which significantly impedes oxide ion mobility. Consequently, the effective ionic conductivity is diminished due to the increased difficulty of ions in navigating through the microstructure.

(a–d) Effective ionic conductivity of YSZ and (e, h) effective electronic conductivity of Ni of the three anode compositions at various operating temperatures corrected using microstructural data acquired using micro-CT.

On the other hand, the effective electronic conductivity increased with Ni loading and was found to be highly anisotropic. Particularly, the anode with an NiO/YSZ ratio of 65:35 recorded the highest average effective electrical conductivity of 0.91 × 106 S/m at 800 °C. A diminishing trend in effective electrical conductivity was noticed with temperature rise for the three anode compositions. This can be explained by the fact that the intrinsic conductivity of Ni metal decreases at elevated temperatures due to electron–phonon scattering as well as the expansion of Ni lattice, thereby stretching the interatomic spaces, which consequently disrupts the electron transfer. ,

Surface and Interfacial Contact Areas

The volume-specific surface areas (SSA) and volume-specific interfacial contact areas (SIA) for each phase were quantified (Figure (a,b)). The Ni phase persistently accounts for all samples’ greatest specific surface area, ranging from 0.34 to 0.70 μm–1. Conversely, the pore phase has the lowest SSA values among all phases because of its low volume fraction. It can be noted that metal–ceramic shared substantially the greatest contact areas with respect to metal–pore and ceramic–pore. The Ni–pore possessed a higher contact area with respect to the YSZ–pore, which is likely because of the formation of additional pores upon NiO reduction to the Ni phase. The YSZ–pore has a negligible interfacial contact area for all anodes. The slice-by-slice comparison of the local Ni–YSZ interfacial contact for the three anodes was calculated along the z-direction (Figure (c)). Interestingly, the local contact area between the Ni and YSZ increased slightly near the anode/electrolyte interface for the anode having an NiO/YSZ ratio of 65:35, whereas the SIA value decreased for ASL with ratios of 50:50 and 60:40.

Triple-Phase Boundary Density

The location where the networks of the three phases meet defines the TPB, which influences the activation overpotential of SOFCs. The TPB is usually characterized as a one-dimensional reaction site length (LTPB); however, to facilitate the comparison of different anode materials, the TPB density (ρTPB) (i.e., the volume-specific TPB length) is a widely used metric for evaluating the electrode performance. The ρTPB values for the three anodes were tabulated in Table . The deviations between the ρTPB values obtained from TauFactor and the in-house python code were less than 14.3%. The ρTPB values presented in this study are at the lower end of those reported in the literature; ,− however, it is important to note that a resolution dependence exists, thus making a direct comparison to values obtained from studies with different resolutions invalid. Surprisingly, the ρTPB values exhibited a decreasing trend with increasing Ni content, with the anode having an NiO/YSZ weight ratio of 50:50, recording the highest ρTPB value of 0.021 μm/μm3. This trend agrees with the results reported by Wilson et al., who investigated the TPB length for Ni–8YSZ anodes with varying NiO weight percentages (40, 50, 60, and 70 wt.%) using FIB–SEM tomography. Their study reported increased active TPB length between 40 and 50 wt % NiO, followed by a decrease at higher NiO concentrations, with the maximal LTPB observed at 50 wt.%. A comparison of the local TPB density for the three anodes was conducted slice by slice along the z-direction (Figure (d)). Notably, the TPB density is the highest toward the anode/electrolyte interface for the three anodes. Similar findings were reported by Heenan et al. for the Ni–YSZ anode, and this observation was attributed to the presence of an anode functional layer.

Electrochemical Performance

The influence of ASL with different Ni contents on cell performance was examined. Particularly, three anode-supported SOFCs with ASL having NiO/YSZ weight ratios of 50:50, 60:40, and 65:35 were tested under ammonia fuel at 700, 750, and 800 °C (Figure (a-c)). The open-circuit voltages (OCV) of the three cells are higher than 1.0 V, thus, confirming the fully dense nature of the YSZ electrolyte. The three cells exhibited an almost linear relationship between potential and current density across the temperature regime studied, signaling a constant internal resistance within the range of 0–650 mA/cm2. Clearly, increasing Ni content in the ASL improves cell performance within the investigated range at all operating temperatures since the Ni phase in the Ni–YSZ cermet dictates the electrocatalytic reactivity of the fuel electrode. For instance, the NH3-fed SOFC having the NiO/YSZ ratio of 65:35 recorded the best performance, with peak power density (PPD) of 383.9 mW/cm2 at 800 °C at 0.6 V voltage, whereas the NiO/YSZ ratio of 60:40 was nearly good, yielding a PPD of 313.4 mW/cm2. The SOFC with the least Ni content, i.e., a low NiO/YSZ ratio of 50:50, generated the lowest PPD of 122 mW/cm2. A similar behavior was reported by Koide et al. for H2-fueled SOFCs with varying Ni loading in ASL. The minimization of the electrode polarization resistance could be realized via increasing the TPB length, as well as decreasing the tortuosity of Ni, YSZ, and pore phases, as it impacts the electronic conductivity, ionic conductivity, and diffusivity, respectively. Thus, the improved cell performance with higher Ni loading, i.e., an NiO/YSZ ratio of 65:35, is explained by the reduced polarization resistance, which is evidenced from the impedance spectroscopy results, with reference to other cells with lower Ni content. Moreover, the 65:35 anode, having the highest Ni content, avails more active catalytic sites for ammonia cracking reactions compared to anodes containing a lower Ni loading. It can be noted that the power density of all studied cells increased significantly with rising temperature, driven by enhanced ionic conductivity of the electrolyte and reduced electrode polarization at higher temperatures. ,

It has been generally accepted that the ammonia utilization in DA–SOFC proceeds either by direct NH3 electrochemical oxidation or indirect pathway, wherein the ammonia is first decomposed into H2 and N2, followed by hydrogen electro-oxidation. From a thermodynamic perspective, the OCV of SOFC would decrease with temperature rise when H2 electro-oxidation is the dominant reaction mechanism for ammonia utilization. In contrast, the OCV increases with temperature when direct NH3 electrochemical oxidation is the primary mechanism. The temperature dependence of theoretical and experimental OCV for the NH3-fed SOFCs with different Ni loading are depicted in Figure (d). Clearly, the three cells presented an OCV trend that is consistent with the thermodynamics of hydrogen oxidation; thereby, it can be inferred that the NH3 utilization in these SOFCs follows indirect H2 oxidation mechanism. The NH3 conversion over the best-performing anode (NiO/YSZ ratio of 65:35) was measured to be 82.6, 86.1, and 87.9% at 700, 750, and 800 °C (Figure (e)).

The electrical efficiency and fuel utilization were estimated as a function of current density for all cells under ammonia fuel at 800 °C (Figure (f)). Apparently, the ammonia utilization and electrical efficiency improved with increasing Ni loading in the ASL, which can be ascribed to the increased supply of H2 available for electricity generation due to the presence of more Ni sites that are active for ammonia breakdown. Particularly, the ASL with the highest Ni content (NiO/YSZ ratio of 65:35) achieved the highest ammonia utilization and electrical efficiency of 18.3 and 10.0%, respectively, at a current density of 639.9 mA/cm2, followed by the SOFC with the NiO/YSZ ratio of 60:40, surmounting 14.9 and 8.2%, respectively, at 522.3 mA/cm2. The cell with the lowest Ni content (NiO/YSZ ratio of 50:50) exhibited considerably diminished performance in both metrics.

A short-term stability test of the best-performing SOFC with NiO/YSZ composition of 65:35 was conducted for 96 h of operation under a constant current density of 302 mA/cm2 at 800 °C (Figure (g)). Notably, the cell voltage decreased slightly from its initial value of 845.3 to 802.5 mV, corresponding to a low voltage degradation rate of 5.06% over 96 h. The microstructure of the spent cell was investigated using micro-CT to understand the origin of this little degradation. The 2D segmented slice and reconstructed 3D microstructure of the spent anode with an NiO/YSZ ratio of 65:35 after the stability test are shown in Figure (a, b). The particle Feret diameters can serve as quantitative indicators for possible cell degradation. The Feret diameter of Ni particles for the fresh and spent ASL following stability tests are displayed in Figure (c). According to the Ni particle size histogram, the Ni particles are predominantly distributed within the 1–4 μm range in the fresh (NiO/YSZ = 65:35) sample, with a peak around 1–2 μm, indicating a fine and uniform Ni dispersion. Following the stability test, the spent sample exhibits a pronounced shift toward larger particle sizes with a considerable increase in frequency within the 5–10.5 μm range. This is because the small Ni particles have high mobility due to their high surface energy. These mobile nickel particles tend to agglomerate to minimize their surface energy. This Ni coarsening process leads to formation of locally isolated Ni islands that do not contribute to the anode’s electrochemical performance. Additionally, variations in the percentage of Ni particles smaller than 1.5, 3.0, 5.0, and 8.0 μm for the fresh and spent anodes were computed (Table ). Notably, the percentage of particles smaller than 1.5 μm decreases significantly from 8.89% to 5.93%, indicating a loss of fine particles. Particles below 3.0 μm show a slight reduction. Notably, particles below 8.0 μm increased from 12.28% to 17.43%, suggesting the formation of larger particles. This pattern is consistent with microstructural degradation caused by Ni coarsening mechanism expected during prolonged operation at high temperatures. This Ni coarsening behavior is particularly pronounced at such a high NiO/YSZ ratio of 65:35, owing to the increased porosity and low YSZ volume fraction, which reduces structural constraints from the YSZ, thereby accelerating the Ni coarsening rate. Furthermore, the tortuosity factors for Ni phase in the x, y, and z-directions for the fresh and spent anodes (NiO/YSZ ratio of 65:35) were also computed, as shown in Figure (d). Apparently, the average and directional tortuosity factors in three directions increased slightly for the spent anode with respect to the fresh one, which would consequently reduce the effective electrical conductivity of the Ni phase, thereby deteriorating the electrochemical performance. It can be concluded that the slight degradation rate observed in the cell following the durability test can be ascribed to Ni coarsening. This will give rise to an increased Ni tortuosity, a lower effective electronic conductivity, a reduced active specific surface area of the Ni particles, and the TPB density. These effects would manifest as increased activation polarization resistance, along with higher ohmic and concentration polarization losses within the anode. −

The SOFC with an NiO/YSZ ratio of 50:50 exhibited the highest ohmic resistance of 1.57 Ω cm2 at 800 °C among all studied cells. This can be attributed to the poor effective electronic conductivity originated from the synergistic effect of its lowest Ni volume fraction conductivity tortuosity factor, reduced Ni percolation, as reflected in the micro-CT analysis. Conversely, the 65:35 composition, with a higher Ni content, sacrifices effective ionic conductivity according to micro-CT findings, thus leading to slightly higher ohmic resistance (0.48 Ω cm2) with reference to 60:40 composition (0.40 Ω cm2). The 60:40 ratio achieved moderate Ni and YSZ tortuosity, as corroborated by the balance between effective ionic and electrical conductivities compared to other cells, resulting in the lowest ohmic resistance among all tested cells.

It can be noted that the polarization resistance of all SOFCs fueled with ammonia rose dramatically with a descent of temperature from 800 to 700 °C, which can be explained by the fact that Ni–YSZ cermet is less active for ammonia breakdown at low temperatures; thus, increased polarization resistance is observed at lower temperatures. , This finding coincides well with the low power densities observed at lower temperatures for all ammonia-fed SOFCs. Interestingly, the total ASR of the three cells was considerably dominated by the polarization resistance for the whole temperature range studied. It was also observed that the high-frequency resistance (R H) accounts for a larger proportion of the polarization resistance in comparison with R L for all SOFCs, implying that the charge transfer reaction controls the DA–SOFC performance. In other words, the gas diffusion process takes place more readily compared to the charge transfer reaction, signaling the ASL microstructure of the three cells is sufficiently porous. Notably, the charge transfer resistance (R H) is inversely proportional to the Ni content in the ASL, wherein the cell containing the highest Ni loading (NiO/YSZ = 65:35) in ASL recorded the minimum R H of 0.44 Ω cm2 at 800 °C; however, this value increased substantially to 1.15 and 2.32 Ω cm2 for ASL comprising NiO/YSZ in 60:40 and 50:50 ratios, respectively. This observation aligns with the improved power density of the cell having an NiO/YSZ ratio of 65:35 with respect to the other cells. These findings clearly confirm that the proportions of metallic and ceramic constituents of ASL significantly affect the polarization characteristics of the SOFC; thus, it is crucial to optimize the NiO/YSZ ratio to achieve high performance. It should be noted that no apparent correlation is observed between the composition dependence of total TPB density (Table ) and the polarization resistance values. This suggests that transport-related descriptors including the tortuosity and percolation are at play. Notably, the tortuosity and percolation of the Ni phase in the z-direction toward the anode/electrolyte interface play a considerable role in high-frequency polarization resistance (R H) value. For instance, the increased z-direction Ni tortuosity raises the R H values across the temperature range studied, an increase in z-direction Ni connectivity reduces the R H resistance. This is in line with the findings reported by Wilson et al., wherein the authors investigated the relationship between the polarization resistance and TPB density, measured using the FIB–SEM technique, for Ni–YSZ anodes with varying NiO loading (40, 50, 60, and 70 wt %). They concluded that the variation in TPB density is too small to explain the difference in polarization resistance, signaling the polarization resistance was determined not only with TPB density but also other microstructural parameters, such as tortuosity and percolation.

Conclusion

In this work, three cells comprising Ni–YSZ anodes with different initial NiO/YSZ contents, (50:50, 60:40, and 65:35), were fabricated using water-based co-tape casting and co-sintering methods, and their performance was investigated in a DA–SOFC at a temperature range of 700–800 °C. Notably, the anode having an initial NiO/YSZ ratio of 65:35 achieved the best performance, with peak power density of 383.9 mW/cm2 at 800 °C at 0.6 V. This can be explained by the lowest charge transfer resistance at higher Ni loading. Based on the observed OCV trend and thermodynamics, the ammonia utilization pathway on these anodes proceeds via NH3 cracking followed by H2 electro-oxidation. The SOFC with an NiO/YSZ composition of 65:35 exhibited a little voltage degradation rate of 5.06% over 96 h, indicating the potential viability of DA–SOFCs for electricity generation. Post-test micro-CT analysis of the anode showed increased Ni particle size compared to the fresh anode, confirming Ni coarsening during operation.

The 3D microstructure of the three anodes was characterized with micro-CT to elucidate the relationship between the anode microstructure and the electrochemical performance. Unlike effective electrical conductivity, the effective ionic conductivity improved considerably with temperature increase for all anodes since the thermal energy available at high temperatures allows the oxide ions to diffuse quickly within the YSZ phase. It is worth noting that the TPB density was found to be the highest toward the interface for all anode compositions, which can be ascribed to the existence of the AFL layer. Notably, no clear correlation was observed between the total TPB density and polarization resistance, implying that other transport-related factors, such as tortuosity and percolation, play a considerable role. These results offer valuable insights into the interplay between electrochemical performance and anode microstructure, signaling the key role of initial NiO/YSZ weight ratio in optimizing DA–SOFC performance. To ensure long-term performance, future research should employ Micro-CT to analyze the microstructural stability of the 65:35 NiO/YSZ anode-supported DA–SOFC under redox and temperature cycling conditions.

Self-intersection of the Torelli map
Lycka Drakengren

Abstract
The Torelli map t : ℳ g c ​ t → 𝒜 g t\colon\mathcal{M}^{ct}_{g}\to\mathcal{A}_{g} is far from an immersion for g ≥ 3 g\geq 3 : the self-fiber product of the Torelli map for g ≥ 3 g\geq 3 has several components with nontrivial intersections. We give a stratification of the self-fiber product for arbitrary genus and describe how components in the fiber product intersect. In genus 4 4 , the Torelli fiber product is nonreduced, which we prove by analyzing the expansion of the period map near a nodal curve. We use the geometry of the Torelli fiber product to: • Calculate the class of the pullback to ℳ 4 c ​ t \mathcal{M}^{ct}_{4} of the Torelli cycle t ∗ ​ [ ℳ 4 c ​ t ] t_{*}[\mathcal{M}^{ct}_{4}] on 𝒜 4 \mathcal{A}_{4} • Find the class t ∗ ​ [ ℳ ¯ 4 ] t_{*}[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{4}] for suitable toroidal compactifications 𝒜 ¯ 4 \overline{\mathcal{A}}_{4} • Calculate the class t ∗ ​ t ∗ ​ [ ℳ 5 c ​ t ] | ℳ 5 t^{*}t_{*}[\mathcal{M}^{ct}_{5}]|_{\mathcal{M}_{5}} In the first appendix, we write down a calculation for finding the Chern classes of ℳ ¯ g , n \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n} . In the second, we give a formula for a coefficient occurring in an intersection of excess dimension.

1.1
The Torelli cycle

The moduli space of
compact type curves

\mathcal{M}^{ct}_{g}\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
is the open subset parametrizing stable curves of genus
g
for which all nodes are disconnecting. The moduli space
\mathcal{A}_{g}
parametrizes principally polarized abelian varieties of dimension
g

[
HM98
]

[
BL04

For
[C]\in\mathcal{M}^{ct}_{g}

Jacobian

J(C)
consists of line bundles having degree zero on every irreducible component. The polarization is given by the theta divisor. The two moduli spaces are related by the
Torelli map

\displaystyle t\colon

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{ct}_{g}\to\mathcal{A}_{g}

\displaystyle[C]\mapsto[J(C)].

The domain
\mathcal{M}^{ct}_{g}

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}

[C]

J(C)

\mathcal{A}_{g}
is nonsingular, and a pushforward

t_{*}\colon\mathsf{CH}^{*}(\mathcal{M}^{ct}_{g})\to\mathsf{CH}^{*}(\mathcal{A}_{g})

since
t
is proper. The study of the image
t(\mathcal{M}^{ct}_{g})
inside
\mathcal{A}_{g}
is the famous Schottky problem, see
[

]

Torelli cycle

T_{g}={t}_{*}[\mathcal{M}^{ct}_{g}]\in\mathsf{CH}^{*}(\mathcal{A}_{g}).

In

Theorem 1.1
.

The Torelli cycle in genus

\mathcal{F}_{g}
. Since

A surprising property of
\mathcal{F}_{g}
is that the space is nonreduced for
g\geq 4
. We determine the generic structure of this nonreducedness in genus
4
by analyzing the local expansion of the Torelli map near a given nodal curve using the variational formulas for abelian differentials in
[
HN18
]
. We calculate the expansion of the period matrix up to second order with respect to a smoothing parameter. This involves computations of contour integrals on fixed curves of genus
2
. We arrive at the following result in Section
3.3.2
:

Theorem 1.2
.

(i)

The nonreduced locus in
\mathcal{F}_{4}
relevant to the calculation of
t^{*}T_{4}
consists of two
8
-dimensional loci inside the component parametrizing pairs of curves
(C_{1}\cup_{p\sim q}C_{2},C_{1}\cup_{r\sim s}C_{2})
where
g(C_{1})=g(C_{2})=2
and the automorphism of Jacobians is taken to be the identity. The two nonreduced loci parametrize the pairs of curves for which
(r,s)=(p,\bar{q})
,
(\bar{p},q)
respectively, where
\bar{p},\bar{q}
are the hyperelliptic conjugates of
p,q
.

(ii)

The local scheme structure at a general nonreduced point in
\mathcal{F}_{4}
is given by

\text{Spec }\frac{\mathbb{C}[[x_{1},\dots,x_{9},x_{10},x_{11}]]}{(x_{9}x_{11},x_{10}x_{11},x_{11}^{2}).}

Consider an extension
t\colon\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}\to\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
of the Torelli map to a toroidal compactification
\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
with irreducible boundary divisor, such as the perfect cone compactification. In Section
4
, we obtain the following generalization of Theorem
1.1
, also proved by Mumford in
[
Mu83
]
:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{4}
be a toroidal compactification to which the Torelli map extends, and such that its boundary divisor
D=\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{4}\backslash\mathcal{A}_{4}
is irreducible. Then
t_{*}[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{4}]=16\lambda_{1}-2D
in
\mathsf{CH}^{1}(\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{4})
.

As can be seen in Section
2
, the space
\mathcal{F}_{g}
is quite complex to describe in general. The complexity of
\mathcal{F}_{g}
is mostly a consequence of the positive dimensional fibers of Torelli map. In genus
4
, the relevant geometry for calculating
t_{*}T_{4}
can be made very explicit, see Section
3.1
. For
g\geq 5
, we determine the restricted class
t^{*}T_{g}|_{\mathcal{M}_{g}}
. For
g=5
, we obtain the following result, proved in Section
5
:

Theorem 1.4
.

The class
t^{*}T_{5}|_{\mathcal{M}_{5}}
is given by
\frac{48}{5}\kappa_{3}
and agrees with
t^{*}\text{taut}(T_{5})|_{\mathcal{M}_{5}}
.

For
g\geq 6
, we prove in Section
5
that
t^{*}T_{g}|_{\mathcal{M}_{g}}=0
.

It would be interesting to further investigate the following questions:

Question 1:
What is the precise nonreduced scheme structure of
\mathcal{F}_{g}
in general?

Question 2:
Can we find a birational map
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{g}\to\mathcal{A}_{g}
and a lifting

1.2
Tautological classes

The best understood part of
\mathsf{CH}^{*}(\mathcal{A}_{g})
is the
tautological ring

\mathsf{R}^{*}(\mathcal{A}_{g})\subset\mathsf{CH}^{*}(\mathcal{A}_{g})
generated by the Chern classes
\lambda_{i}=c_{i}(\mathbb{E})
of the Hodge bundle
\mathbb{E}=\pi_{*}\Omega_{\pi}
where
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi\colon\mathcal{X}_{g}\to\mathcal{A}_{g}}
is the universal abelian variety
[
vdG99
]
. In an ongoing project with Samir Canning and Aitor Iribar López, we enlarge the unirational parametrization of
\mathcal{A}_{4}
in
[
I09
]
with the aim of showing that
\mathsf{CH}^{*}(\mathcal{A}_{4})=\mathsf{R}^{*}(\mathcal{A}_{4})
. On the other hand, the class
[\mathcal{A}_{1}\times\mathcal{A}_{5}]\in\mathsf{CH}^{5}(\mathcal{A}_{6})
is not tautological
[
COP24
]
.

By
[
CMOP24
]
, there is a
tautological projection

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{taut}\colon\mathsf{CH}^{k}(\mathcal{A}_{g})\to\mathsf{R}^{k}(\mathcal{A}_{g})}
which maps a cycle
\alpha
to the unique tautological class
\text{taut}(\alpha)
such that

\int_{\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{g}}\alpha\cdot\beta\cdot\lambda_{g}=\int_{\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{g}}\text{taut}(\alpha)\cdot\beta\cdot\lambda_{g}

for every
\beta\in\mathsf{R}^{\binom{g}{2}-k}(\mathcal{A}_{g})
and any choice of toroidal compactification
\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
. Each
\alpha\in\mathsf{CH}^{*}(\mathcal{A}_{g})
can be decomposed uniquely as
\alpha=\text{taut}(\alpha)+\alpha_{0}
where
\text{taut}(\alpha)\in\mathsf{R}^{*}(\mathcal{A}_{g})
and
\text{taut}(\alpha_{0})=0
.

While
T_{4}=16\lambda_{1}
is tautological, the following question remains open:

Question 3:
For which
g\geq 5
is
T_{g}
tautological?

Comparing the classes
t^{*}T_{g}
and
t^{*}\text{taut}(T_{g})
could provide a potential obstruction to
T_{g}
being tautological, which motivates our interest in determining the class
t^{*}T_{g}
. This will only be possible for
g\leq 9
for dimension reasons. Indeed, we have
\mathrm{dim}(\mathcal{A}_{g})=\frac{g(g+1)}{2}
and
\mathrm{dim}(\mathcal{M}^{ct}_{g})=3g-3
, so

\mathrm{dim}(t^{*}T_{g})=\frac{-g^{2}+11g-12}{2}.

Setting
g\geq 10
thus gives
\mathrm{dim}(t^{*}T_{g})<0
and hence
t^{*}T_{g}=0
.

In addition, for
g=8
, using Definition
3.3
we have
\mathsf{CH}^{*}(\mathcal{M}^{ct}_{8})=\mathsf{R}^{*}(\mathcal{M}^{ct}_{8})
and
R^{i}(\mathcal{M}^{ct}_{8})=0
for
i>2g-3
by
[
CL24
]
,
[
CLP24
, Theorem 1.10]
and excision. Since
\mathrm{dim}(t^{*}T_{8})<8
we obtain
t^{*}T_{8}=0
.

The tautological projections of

1.3
Geometry of the Torelli fiber product

We illustrate the correspondence between geometric and combinatorial strata in the Torelli fiber product.

Recall that a geometric point in the fiber product
\mathcal{F}_{g}
corresponds to a pair of curves and a choice of isomorphism between the Jacobians preserving the polarization.

The automorphisms of the polarized Jacobian
J(C)

C

[
M21

•

If

C

•

C

\text{Aut}(C)=\text{Aut}(J(C))

•

If
g(C)=1

\text{Aut}(J(C))=\text{Aut}((C,p))

p
is any fixed point on
C

The Jacobian of a compact type curve
C
corresponds to the Jacobian of its normalization. By
[
CG72
, Corollary 3.23]
, the decomposition of
J(C)
into a product of Jacobians of irreducible curves of positive genus is unique. This means that two compact type curves have isomorphic Jacobians precisely when their irreducible components of positive genus can be paired up such that the irreducible curves in a pair are isomorphic. Different choices of isomorphisms between the Jacobians (up to isomorphisms of the curves) of the irreducible components will result in different elements of the fiber product. Thus, an element of
\mathcal{F}_{g}
consists of:

•

A pair of compact type curves
C_{1},C_{2}
of genus
g

•

A chosen pairing between the irreducible components of positive genus of
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
such that the paired components are isomorphic

•

A choice of isomorphism of Jacobians for each set of paired irreducible components

Sample element of the Torelli fiber product for
g=4
.

Writing the
\text{Spec}(\mathbb{C})
-points of

2
The Torelli fiber product

The
Torelli fiber product
is defined as the space
\mathcal{F}_{g}
in the fiber diagram of stacks

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{F}_{g}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}^{ct}_{g}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}^{ct}_{g}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{A}_{g}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces t_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces t_{1}}

where
t_{1}
,
t_{2}
are Torelli maps.

In order to calculate
t^{*}{T_{g}}
using excess intersection theory
[
Fu98
]
, we would like to understand the geometry of
\mathcal{F}_{g}
. In particular, we would like to describe the irreducible components and their intersections. We would also like to locate possible nonreduced scheme structure.

In this section, we give a stratification of the fiber product and provide parametrizations of the strata. We first describe a combinatorial stratification and define specializations of combinatorial strata. Next, we describe the geometric loci associated to the combinatorial strata. A combinatorial specialization will correspond to containment of a geometric stratum inside the closure of another stratum. The containment of a stratum inside the closure of a given geometric stratum can be found from the parametrizations of the closures. As a consequence, we obtain parametrizations for the intersections of closures of strata. Given this, we can deduce the full reduced scheme structure of
\mathcal{F}_{g}
.

On the other hand, the fiber product
\mathcal{F}_{g}
turns out to interestingly carry nonreduced structure, which will be explored in Section
3.3
.

In this section, we introduce combinatorial strata which will be shown in Section
2.2
to classify the geometric strata of the fiber product.

.

(Pair of trees)

For each vertex

where

(Positive and negative half-edge pairings,
g(v)\geq 2
)
For each set of paired vertices

Consider a bipartite graph whose left vertex set corresponds to the half-edges at

For

Proposition 2.14

For any

2.5
Components of

and their intersections

Given the specializations in
2.12

are. In particular, the open stratum

associated to an irreducible component contains no half-edge pairings and no

. Moreover, there are no vertices of genus

resp.

and no two neighboring genus

identified with neighboring vertices in

An irreducible component

of

is associated to a tuple

(T_{1},T_{2},\nu,\sigma)
such that:

1.

(Positive genera)

T_{1}
,
T_{2}
are (stable) trees with vertices of positive genus.

2.

(Vertex pairing)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\nu\colon V(T_{1})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V(T_{2})\hskip-5.0pt}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
is a bijection preserving genus.

3.

(Sign function)

\sigma
is defined as a function

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma\colon\{v\in V(T_{1})|g(v)\geq 2\}\to\{-,\pm,+\}\hskip-5.0pt}

such that

4.

(Elliptic pairs)
No two neighboring vertices of genus
1
in

become neighbors in

under

Remark 2.15
.

The positive genus condition ensures that the space
\mathcal{F}_{(T_{1},T_{2},\nu,\sigma)}
does not lie in the closure of another component defined by graphs obtained by repeatedly contracting edges having one endpoint at a genus
0
vertex in
T_{1}
or
T_{2}
.

The vertex pairing describes how the factors of the Jacobians corresponding to different curves match up under the automorphism of Jacobians.

The sign function describes whether the automorphism of Jacobians is generically identity (
\sigma(v)=+)
, the involution (
\sigma(v)=-)
or can be both because of hyperellipticity (
\sigma(v)=\pm
) on the factor corresponding to a given vertex
v
of
T_{1}
.

The elliptic pairs condition ensures that the space
\mathcal{F}_{(T_{1},T_{2},\nu,\sigma)}
does not lie in the closure of the component obtained by replacing two such neighboring genus
1
vertices by a vertex of genus
2
.

An irreducible component
\mathcal{F}_{(T_{1},T_{2},\nu,\sigma)}
is precisely the closure
\mathcal{F}^{ct}(\underline{T})
of a stratum associated to a CTP
\underline{T}
which has the prescribed vertex pairing
\nu
and sign choice

The dimension of an irreducible component

is therefore

\text{dim }\mathcal{F}_{(T_{1},T_{2},\nu,\sigma)}=\sum_{v\in V(T_{1})}(3g(v)-3+2d(v)-\mathbbm{1}(g(v)=1))

Appendix A
Chern characters of

We obtain

\displaystyle=-(1+g)\lambda_{1}

\displaystyle\text{ch}_{2}(T\mathcal{A}_{g})

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

(7)

where the direct sum is taken over boundary divisor strata
\Gamma
of

.

Appendix B
An intersection formula

Suppose we want to calculate an intersection class
X\cdot_{Y}V
for varieties
X,V,Y
. Each irreducible component
A
of the fiber product
X\times_{Y}V
has a canonical contribution to this class, given by
c_{\text{top}}(N_{X,Y}/N_{A,V})
. Subtracting these canonical contributions from
X\cdot_{Y}V
, the remaining class will be supported on nontrivial intersections between components.

For components
A
,
B
of the fiber product
X\times_{Y}V
, we provide a general formula which calculates the contribution of
A\cap B
to the intersection class
X\cdot_{Y}V
.

Let
X,Y
be varieties equipped with a regular embedding
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i}
of codimension
d
and normal bundle
N
. Furthermore, let
V
be a pure-dimensional variety of dimension
d
and
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
a morphism. Assume that
f^{-1}(X)
consists of a union
A\cup B
of closed subvarieties
A
,
B
which are regularly embedded in
V
and intersect transversely at a point
P
. Let
d_{A}
,
d_{B}
be the dimensions of
A,B
where
d_{A},d_{B}\geq 1
. Note that
d=d_{A}+d_{B}
since the intersection is transverse. Denote by
N_{A}
and
N_{B}
the normal bundles of
A
and
B
inside
V
. We omit pullback notation for
N
. We thus have a diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A\cup B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i}

Proposition
B.1
calculates the remaining contribution of
A\cap B
to the intersection product
X\cdot V
after removing the canonical contributions from
A
and
B
.

Proposition B.1
.

We have
X\cdot V=c_{d_{A}}\left(\frac{N}{N_{A}}\right)+c_{d_{B}}\left(\frac{N}{N_{B}}\right)+m[P]
, where
m=m(d_{A},d_{B})
satisfies the formula

m=\sum_{1\leq k\leq d-1}(-1)^{k+1}(2^{d-k}-1)\binom{d}{k}\left[\binom{k-1}{d_{A}-1}(-1)^{d_{A}}+\binom{k-1}{d_{B}-1}(-1)^{d_{B}}\right]+2^{d}-2.

We give a proof in Section
B.3
.

We first illustrate with example computations, and compare them to suitable local models with the given dimensions.

.

Let

. Proposition

tells us that

A local model for this intersection is given by

intersecting in a point

.

Using

,

, we calculate

This indeed gives us

.

B.2
Motivation for the formula

We indicate why the formula in Proposition
B.1
is expected.

The difference
X\cdot V-c_{d_{A}}\left(\frac{N}{N_{A}}\right)+c_{d_{B}}\left(\frac{N}{N_{B}}\right)
equals

\left\{c(N)\cap\left(s(A\cup B,V)-s(A,V)-s(B,V)\right)\right\}_{0}.

The normal cone of
A\cup B
in
V
agrees with the one of
A
in
V
on the complement
A\backslash P
and of
B
in
V
over
B\backslash P
. Taking Segre classes, the difference

\delta=s(A\cup B,V)-s(A,V)-s(B,V)

is supported on
P
by the following argument: Restrict to
U=V\backslash P
and consider the inclusion
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}
We use the same notation
\iota
for the restricted map
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A\backslash P\cup B\backslash P\hookrightarrow A\cup B.}
Since open immersions are flat, the Segre classes pull back:

\iota^{*}\delta=s(A\backslash P\cup B\backslash P,U)-s(A\backslash P,U)-s(B\backslash P,U).

Due to equality of normal cones on the open set
U
, the class
\iota^{*}\delta
vanishes. This in turn means that the class
\delta
must be supported on
V\backslash U=P
.

That the coefficient
m
only depends on the dimensions
d_{A},d_{B}
translates to the statement that it only depends on the normal bundles of
P
inside
A,B
. The proof of Proposition
B.1
will indeed show that the coefficient can be calculated using only the local geometry near the exceptional divisor
E
of
P
after blowing up
P
and the dimensions of the linear subspaces corresponding to the intersections of the strict transforms of
A,B
with
E
.

.

Let
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V^{\prime}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{\prime}}

at

.

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle=q_{*}\left(s_{d}(\tilde{A}+\tilde{B}+2\tilde{E},\tilde{V})-s_{d}(\tilde{A}+\tilde{E},\tilde{V})-s_{d}(\tilde{B}+\tilde{E},\tilde{V})\right)

\displaystyle=\left[c(N)\cap q_{*}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{d}(-1)^{n-1}\left([\tilde{A}+\tilde{B}+2\tilde{E}]^{n}-[\tilde{A}+\tilde{E}]^{n}-[\tilde{B}+\tilde{E}]^{n}\right)\right)\right]_{d_{2}}

depends only on the classes
c(N)
,

,
q_{*}\tilde{B}^{i}\tilde{E}^{j}
for

and
q_{*}\tilde{E}^{n}
for
n\geq 0
. Note that

E^{n}=c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(N_{Z,V})}(-1))^{n-1}

pushes down to
(-1)^{n-1}s_{d_{Z}-d+n}(N_{Z,V})
, and

\tilde{q}_{*}\tilde{A}^{i}\cdot\tilde{E}^{j}=\binom{i-1}{d-d_{A}-1}(-1)^{i+1}L^{i-d+d_{A}+j}

. We conclude that
R
is only dependent on the Chern/Segre classes of

,

,

and

.∎

Proposition B.6
.

Assume that
X\cdot V
has dimension
k\geq 0
where
\mathrm{dim}(A\cap B)=k.
Then
m=m(d_{A}-k,d_{B}-k)
.

Proof.

Noting that
q_{*}s_{i}
vanishes for each Segre class
s_{i}
on
\tilde{F}
where
i<d-k
and that the class
X\cdot V
is
k
-dimensional, we obtain

\displaystyle m[Z]

\displaystyle=(-1)^{d-k}q_{*}\left(s_{d-k}(\tilde{A}+\tilde{B}+2\tilde{E},\tilde{V})-s_{d-k}(\tilde{A}+\tilde{E},\tilde{V})-s_{d-k}(\tilde{B}+\tilde{E},\tilde{V})\right)

where
d=d_{A}+d_{B}-k
.

Letting
d^{\prime}_{A}=d_{A}-k
,
d^{\prime}_{B}=d_{B}-k
gives
d-k=d^{\prime}_{A}+d_{B}^{\prime}
. Imitating the proof of Proposition
B.1
thus yields
m=m(d_{A}^{\prime},d_{B}^{\prime})
.∎

Proposition B.7
.

Assume more generally that
d\geq d_{A}+d_{B}
, where
P=A\cap B
is a smooth point. Then the above formula for
m=m(d_{A},d_{B})
still holds.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{V}
be the blowup of
V
in
B
. Let
\tilde{B}
be the exceptional divisor and
A^{\prime}=\text{Bl}_{P}A
. Let
q
be the induced map from
A^{\prime}\cup\tilde{B}
to
A\cup B
. Denote by
Z
the preimage of
P
under this blowup. By birational invariance of Segre classes, we have

\displaystyle s(A\cup B,V)

\displaystyle=q_{*}s(A^{\prime}\cup\tilde{B},\tilde{V}),

\displaystyle s(A,V)

\displaystyle=q_{*}s(A^{\prime},\tilde{V}),

\displaystyle s(B,V)

\displaystyle=q_{*}s(\tilde{B},\tilde{V}).

In turn, this means that
m[P]=q_{*}R
where

R=q_{*}s(A^{\prime}\cup\tilde{B},\tilde{V})-q_{*}s(A^{\prime},\tilde{V})-q_{*}s(\tilde{B},\tilde{V})

only depends on the normal bundles of
Z
in
A^{\prime}
,
\tilde{B}
and
\tilde{V}
by Corollary
B.5
. The normal bundle of
Z
in
\tilde{B}
is trivial since
\tilde{B}=\mathbb{P}(N_{P,B})
, whereas the normal bundle of
Z
in
A
is
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(N_{P,A})}(-1)
. The normal bundle of
Z
in
V
equals, in the Grothendieck group
K_{0}
, the sum
N_{Z,\tilde{B}}+i_{Z}^{*}N_{\tilde{B},\tilde{V}}
where
i_{Z}
denotes the inclusion of
Z
into
B
. The normal bundle
N_{\tilde{B},\tilde{V}}
equals
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(N_{B,V})}(-1)
. Thus, after restricting to
P
, these normal bundles will only be dependent on the normal bundles of
P
inside
A,B
and
V
. As a result, the coefficient
m
depends only on the values
d_{A},d_{B},d
. Due to invariance of
m
after taking the product of
V
resp.
Y
by
\mathbb{A}^{1}
,
m
remains unchanged after replacing
d
by
d-k
while keeping
d_{A},d_{B}
the same. These new dimensions model a transverse intersection, meaning that
m=m(d_{A},d_{B})
in the intersection formula.
∎

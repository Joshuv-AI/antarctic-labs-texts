The Brauer group of a Stein algebra
Olivier Benoist, James Hotchkiss

Abstract
. We investigate the Brauer group of the ring 𝒪 ⁡ ( S ) \mathcal{O}(S) of holomorphic functions on a finite-dimensional Stein space S S . We provide a purely topological computation of this group and deduce a comparison theorem between the étale cohomology of Spec ⁡ ( 𝒪 ⁡ ( S ) ) \mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S)) and the singular cohomology of S S in degree 2 2 . Furthermore, we prove a purity theorem when S S is nonsingular and study the index of classes in the Brauer group of 𝒪 ⁡ ( S ) \mathcal{O}(S) .



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Stein spaces and their Stein algebras

Stein spaces
are the complex-analytic analogues of affine algebraic varieties. They can be characterized among all complex spaces
S
by the surjectivity of the restriction map
\mathcal{O}(S)\to\mathcal{O}(T)
for all discrete subsets
T\subset S
, or, equivalently, by the vanishing of
H^{k}(S,\mathcal{F})
for all coherent sheaves
\mathcal{F}
on
S
and all
k>0
(see e.g.
[
GR79
]
). A
Stein algebra
is the
\mathbb{C}
-algebra
\mathcal{O}(S)
of holomorphic functions on some Stein space
S
.

In algebraic geometry, there is a correspondence (an equivalence of categories) between affine algebraic varieties
V
over a field
K
and finitely generated
K
-algebras
R
(given by
R=\mathcal{O}(V)
and
V=\mathrm{Spec}(R)
). Through this correspondence, geometric properties of
V
translate into algebraic properties of
R
and vice versa.

A similar equivalence of categories holds in complex-analytic geometry, between Stein spaces and holomorphic maps on the one hand, and Stein algebras and
\mathbb{C}
-algebra morphisms on the other hand (combine
[
For67
, Satz 1]
and
[
For66
, Theorem 5]
for finite-dimensional Stein spaces, and see
[
Ben24b
, Theorem 0.1]
in general).

A major difference with the algebraic situation is that Stein algebras are complicated from a commutative algebra point of view (for instance, they are usually not noetherian). This makes it more difficult to relate their algebraic properties to the analytic or topological properties of the associated Stein spaces. An influential work in this direction is Forster’s
[
For67
]
, where the relation between coherent sheaves on a Stein space
S
and modules over the Stein algebra
\mathcal{O}(S)
is investigated.

The aim of the present article is to further contribute to this line of research by studying the Brauer group of a Stein algebra. Our theorems concerning the purely algebraic properties of
\mathcal{O}(S)
(such as Theorems
1.6
,
1.8
and
1.10
) are proved by exploiting results relating the commutative algebra of
\mathcal{O}(S)
to the geometry or the topology of
S
(such as Corollary
1.3
or Theorem
1.5
).

1.2.
Algebraic and topological Brauer groups

Our first result shows that the Brauer group of a Stein algebra is of topological nature.

Theorem 1.1

(Propositions
3.1
and
3.2
)
.

The natural morphism

(1.1)

\mathrm{Br}(\mathcal{O}(S))\to\mathrm{Br}_{\mathrm{top}}(S)

is an isomorphism for any finite-dimensional Stein space
S
.

Recall that the
Brauer group

\mathrm{Br}(R)
of a ring
R
was defined by Auslander and Goldman
[
AG60
]
to be the set of Morita equivalence classes of Azumaya algebras over
R
, endowed with the group law induced by tensor product, generalizing the definition of the Brauer group of a field
K
as Morita equivalence classes of central simple algebras over
K
(see
[
Gro68
,
CTS21
]
). The
topological Brauer group

\mathrm{Br}_{\mathrm{top}}(S)
of a topological space
S
is defined in a similar way in
[
Gro68
, I, §§1.1–1.2]
, using Morita equivalence classes of topological Azumaya algebras on
S
(see §
2.1
).

To show that (
1.1
) is an isomorphism, we exploit the analytic Brauer group of
S
based on holomorphic Azumaya algebras (see
[
HS03
, §2]
,
[
Sch05
, §1]
or §
2.1
). We identify the topological and the analytic Brauer groups of
S
using Grauert’s Oka principle
[
Gra58
]
, and the analytic Brauer group of
S
with the algebraic Brauer group of
\mathcal{O}(S)
by means of Forster’s aforementioned results
[
For67
]
as developed in
[
Ben24a
, Proposition 2.5, Remark 2.6]
. The first half of the argument appears in the literature (see e.g.
[
AW14a
, Corollary 4.3]
or
[
Sri25
]
); the second half is new.

The hypothesis that
S
is finite-dimensional in Theorem
1.1
is essential both for the injectivity and the surjectivity of (
1.1
) (see Propositions
3.8
and
3.10
).

1.3.
Cohomological interpretations of Brauer groups

Grothendieck constructed a canonical injection

(1.2)

\mathrm{Br}(R)\hookrightarrow H^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathrm{Spec}(R),\mathbb{G}_{m})

of the Brauer group of a ring
R
into the second étale cohomology group of
\mathrm{Spec}(R)
with invertible coefficients (see
[
Gro68
, I, (2.1)]
).
Gabber identified the image of (
1.2
) as the torsion subgroup of
H^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathrm{Spec}(R),\mathbb{G}_{m})
(
[
Gab81
, Chap. II, Theorem 1]
, see also the more general
[
dJ03
]
).
This yields a cohomological interpretation for the left-hand side of (
1.1
).

On the topological side, Serre showed that the topological Brauer group
\mathrm{Br}_{\mathrm{top}}(T)
of a finite CW complex
T
is naturally isomorphic to
H^{3}(S,\mathbb{Z})_{\mathrm{tors}}
(see
[
Gro68
, I, Corollaire 1.7]
). To provide a topological interpretation for the right-hand side of (
1.1
), we extend Serre’s computation to finite-dimensional CW complexes.

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem
3.3
)
.

Let
T
be a finite-dimensional CW complex. Then the canonical morphism
\mathrm{Br}_{\mathrm{top}}(T)\to H^{3}(T,\mathbb{Z})_{\mathrm{tors}}
is an isomorphism.

The finite-dimensionality assumption in Theorem
1.2
cannot be dispensed with (see
[
AW14a
, Corollary 5.10]
or
[
HS15
]
). Combining Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
shows that the Brauer group of a Stein algebra can be computed purely topologically.

Theorem 1.3

(Theorem
3.5
)
.

The natural morphism

\mathrm{Br}(\mathcal{O}(S))\to H^{3}(S,\mathbb{Z})_{\mathrm{tors}}

is an isomorphism for any finite-dimensional Stein space
S
.

1.4.
Degree
2
cohomology with finite coefficients

Let us present an application of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.
For any Stein space
S
, any
k\geq 0
, and any
n\geq 1
, one can consider the comparison morphism

(1.3)

H^{k}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S)),\mathbb{Z}/n)\to H^{k}(S,\mathbb{Z}/n)

between the étale cohomology of
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))
and the singular cohomology of
S
. The question whether (
1.3
) is an isomorphism when
S
is finite-dimensional was raised in
[
Ben24a
, Remark 6.7 (v)]
and proved there if
k=0
or
k=1
(see also
[
Ben25
, Theorem 1.5]
for a positive answer for all
k\geq 0
in the easier but related setting of Stein compacta). We answer this question for
k=2
.

Theorem 1.4

(Theorem
4.2
)
.

The natural morphism

(1.4)

H^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S)),\mathbb{Z}/n)\to H^{2}(S,\mathbb{Z}/n)

is an isomorphism for any finite-dimensional Stein space
S
and any
n\geq 1
.

To deduce Theorem
1.4
from Theorem
1.1
, we relate the left-hand side of (
1.4
) with
n
-torsion classes in
\mathrm{Br}(\mathcal{O}(S))
and the right-hand side of (
1.4
) with
n
-torsion classes in
\mathrm{Br}_{\mathrm{top}}(S)
. For these purposes, we respectively use Gabber’s theorem that the image of the injection (
1.2
) is the subgroup of torsion classes and Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem
1.4
was our original motivation to study Brauer groups of Stein algebras. We do not know a proof of it that does not rely on a geometric incarnation of degree
2
cohomology classes by means of Azumaya algebras.

1.5.
Degree
2
cohomology with invertible coefficients

One can also consider, for any Stein space
S
and any
k\geq 0
, a comparison morphism similar to (
1.3
) but with invertible coefficients:

(1.5)

H^{k}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S)),\mathbb{G}_{m})\to H^{k}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\times}).

The morphism (
1.5
) is tautologically an isomorphism for
k=0
. It is also an isomorphism for
k=1
when
S
is finite-dimensional (see Lemma
4.1
). It is therefore natural to ask whether (
1.5
) is always an isomorphism when
S
is finite-dimensional. Note that, making use of Kummer exact sequences, this would imply that the morphisms (
1.3
) themselves are isomorphisms.

When
k=2
, we show that injectivity holds, although surjectivity fails in general.

Theorem 1.5

(Theorem
4.5
and Corollary
5.4
)
.

The natural morphism

(1.6)

H^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S)),\mathbb{G}_{m})\to H^{2}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\times})

is injective for any finite-dimensional Stein space
S
. There exists a connected Stein manifold such that (
1.6
) is not surjective.

To prove the injectivity assertion of Theorem
1.5
, we rely again on a geometric interpretation of degree
2
cohomology classes. The Brauer group is not sufficient for this purpose, as it can only account for torsion classes in
H^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S)),\mathbb{G}_{m})
(and if
S
is singular, this group may not be torsion, see Proposition
4.9
). To overcome this difficulty, we make use of the
bigger Brauer group

\widetilde{\mathrm{Br}}(R)
of a ring
R
introduced by Taylor and Raeburn
[
Tay82
,
RT85
]
(with corrections by Caenepeel and Grandjean
[
CG98
,
Cae98
]
, see also
[
Sch03
,
HS09
]
), based on possibly non-unital

central separable
R
-algebras
(instead of only Azumaya algebras), which satisfies
\widetilde{\mathrm{Br}}(R){\mathrel{\to}^{\,\sim\,}}H^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathrm{Spec}(R),\mathbb{G}_{m})
(see
[
CG98
, Theorem 3.4]
).
We follow the point of view of Heinloth and Schröer
[
HS09
]
(see §
4.2
). We refer to Proposition
4.8
for a counterpart of the first half of Theorem
1.5
in complex algebraic geometry.

The failure of surjectivity of (
1.6
) in general was already noted by Raeburn and Taylor in
[
RT85
, p. 462]
(in the related setting of Stein compacta). The point is that, when
S
is a manifold, the left-hand side of (
1.6
) is a torsion group (by Theorem
1.7
below), but the right-hand side of (
1.6
) might contain nontorsion classes.
The comments in
[
Tay82
, bottom of p. 200]
, where Taylor explains why it is a difficult task to represent classes in
H^{2}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\times})
by central separable
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-algebras, clarify why bigger Brauer group arguments fail to show that (
1.6
) is surjective.

1.6.
The meromorphic function field

If
S
is a Stein space, the cohomology group
H^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S)),\mathbb{G}_{m})
is not torsion in general (see Proposition
4.9
), so the injection
\mathrm{Br}(\mathcal{O}(S))\to H^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S)),\mathbb{G}_{m})
(see (
1.2
)) may not be surjective. We prove its surjectivity if the local rings of
S
are factorial, e.g. if
S
is nonsingular.

Theorem 1.6

(Corollary
5.2
)
.

The natural morphism

\mathrm{Br}(\mathcal{O}(S))\to H^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S)),\mathbb{G}_{m})

is an isomorphism for any finite-dimensional locally factorial Stein space
S
.

Theorem
1.6
derives at once from the following purely cohomological statement, in which
\mathcal{M}(S)
denotes the ring of meromorphic functions on a complex space
S
(see
[
GR84
, Chap. 6, §3]
).

Theorem 1.7

(Theorem
5.1
)
.

The natural morphism

H^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S)),\mathbb{G}_{m})\to H^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{M}(S)),\mathbb{G}_{m})

is injective for any finite-dimensional locally factorial Stein space
S
.

To prove Theorem
1.7
, we need a way to detect the vanishing of a cohomology class in
H^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S)),\mathbb{G}_{m})
. To do so, we exploit the first half of Theorem
1.5
. In addition to this, we make use of twisted sheaves, whose utilization in the study of Brauer groups was pioneered by de Jong and Lieblich (see
[
dJ03
,
Lie08
]
).

Theorem
1.7
is a complex-analytic analogue of an algebraic result of Grothendieck stating that for any noetherian ring
R
whose local rings have factorial strict henselizations, if
K
is the total ring of fractions of
R
, then the natural morphism

(1.7)

H^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathrm{Spec}(R),\mathbb{G}_{m})\to H^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathrm{Spec}(K),\mathbb{G}_{m})

is injective (see
[
Gro68
, II, Corollaire 1.10]
). Grothendieck’s proof relies on a study of the étale sheaf of Cartier divisors on
\mathrm{Spec}(R)
, and more precisely on its identification with the étale sheaf of Weil divisors on
\mathrm{Spec}(R)
(see
[
Gro68
, II, (3) p. 71]
).
In the setting of Theorem
1.7
, one can define meaningful étale sheaves of Cartier and Weil divisors on
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))
. However, the natural morphism from the first to the second is neither injective nor surjective in general (at exotic geometric points of
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))
), even when
S
is a manifold. This makes it difficult to prove Theorem
1.7
along these lines.

1.7.
Purity

Let
R
be a regular domain with fraction field
K
. Since (
1.7
) is injective, so is the natural morphism
\mathrm{Br}(R)\to\mathrm{Br}(K)
(this result goes back to
[
AG60
, Theorem 7.2]
). In addition, Auslander and Goldman asked in
[
AG60
, p. 389]
whether one could recover
\mathrm{Br}(R)
inside
\mathrm{Br}(K)
as

(1.8)

\mathrm{Br}(R)=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathrm{Br}(R_{\mathfrak{p}}),

where
\mathfrak{p}
runs over all height
1
prime ideals of
R
. This statement, also known as Grothendieck’s purity conjecture (see
[
Gro68
, III, §6]
), was proved in full generality by Česnavičius
[
Čes19
, Theorem 1.2]
(many particular cases listed in
[
Čes19
, p. 1462]
were known before, e.g. the case of excellent
\mathbb{Q}
-algebras
[
Gro68
, III, Théorème 6.1]
).
When
R
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra, one can rewrite (
1.8
) as an exact sequence

(1.9)

0\to\mathrm{Br}(R)\to\mathrm{Br}(K)\xrightarrow{\mathrm{res}_{x}}\bigoplus_{x}H^{1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathrm{Spec}(\kappa(x)),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})

(see
[
Čes19
, (6.4.4)]
), where
x
runs over all codimension one points of
\mathrm{Spec}(R)
with residue field
\kappa(x)
and
\mathrm{res}_{x}:\mathrm{Br}(K)\to H^{1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathrm{Spec}(\kappa(x)),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
is the residue map (for which see e.g.
[
CTS21
, Definition 1.4.11 (ii)]
).

We establish a purity theorem in Stein geometry, in the spirit of (
1.9
).

Theorem 1.8

(Theorem
5.5
)
.

Let
S
be a finite-dimensional Stein manifold. There is an exact sequence

(1.10)

0\to\mathrm{Br}(\mathcal{O}(S))\to\mathrm{Br}(\mathcal{M}(S))\xrightarrow{\mathrm{res}_{D}}\prod_{D}H^{1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{M}(D)),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}),

where
D
runs over all irreducible analytic subsets of codimension
1
of
S
.

The injectivity of
\mathrm{Br}(\mathcal{O}(S))\to\mathrm{Br}(\mathcal{M}(S))
follows from Theorem
1.7
. Our proof of the exactness of (
1.10
) at
\mathrm{Br}(\mathcal{M}(S))
relies on the topological computation of
\mathrm{Br}(\mathcal{O}(S))
provided by Theorem
1.3
and on an effacement lemma for degree
1
singular cohomology classes of étale
\mathcal{O}(S)
-algebras proved in
[
Ben24a
, Lemma 6.4]
.

1.8.
The index

Let
R
be a ring. The
period

\mathrm{per}(\alpha)
of
\alpha\in\mathrm{Br}(R)
is its order in the torsion group
\mathrm{Br}(R)
. A more delicate invariant of
\alpha
is its
index

\mathrm{ind}(\alpha)
(the gcd of the degrees of Azumaya algebras representing
\alpha
). In the last section of this article, we study the integer
\mathrm{ind}(\alpha)
in the case of Stein algebras.

We first show that, in this situation, the index can be computed topologically (the index of a topological Brauer class being defined as the gcd of the degrees of topological Azumaya algebras representing it).

Theorem 1.9

(Theorem
6.1
)
.

Let
S
be a finite-dimensional Stein space. For any
\alpha\in\mathrm{Br}(\mathcal{O}(S))
with image
\alpha^{\mathrm{top}}
in
\mathrm{Br}_{\mathrm{top}}(S)
, one has
\mathrm{ind}(\alpha)=\mathrm{ind}(\alpha^{\mathrm{top}})
.

If
V
is a connected smooth projective variety over
\mathbb{C}
and
\alpha\in\mathrm{Br}(V)
has image
\alpha_{\mathbb{C}(V)}
in
\mathrm{Br}(\mathbb{C}(V))
, then
\mathrm{ind}(\alpha)=\mathrm{ind}(\alpha_{\mathbb{C}(V)})
(the argument in
[
AW14b
, Proposition 6.1]
is attributed to Saltman). We prove a Stein analogue of this result.

Theorem 1.10

(Theorem
6.7
)
.

Let
S
be a connected Stein manifold. Fix a class
{\alpha\in\mathrm{Br}(\mathcal{O}(S))}
and let
\alpha_{\mathcal{M}(S)}
be its image in
\mathrm{Br}(\mathcal{M}(S))
. Then
\mathrm{ind}(\alpha)=\mathrm{ind}(\alpha_{\mathcal{M}(S)})
.

Extending the argument in
[
AW14b
]
to the Stein context is complicated by the fact that coherent sheaves on
S
do not necessarily admit global finite resolutions by vector bundles (only in restriction to compact subsets of
S
). To overcome this difficulty, we exploit the fact that the index can be computed topologically (Theorem
1.10
) and the
twisted K-theory
cohomology theory of
[
DK70
,
AS04
]
(see §
6.2
).

Let
V
be a connected smooth projective variety of dimension
d
over
\mathbb{C}
. The period-index conjecture (see
[
CT06
, p. 389]
) asks whether
\mathrm{ind}(\alpha)\mid\mathrm{per}(\alpha)^{d-1}
for all
\alpha\in\mathrm{Br}(\mathbb{C}(V))
. It holds when
d=1
because
\mathrm{Br}(\mathbb{C}(V))=0
by Tsen’s theorem (see
[
Ser94
, II.3.1, Proposition 6]
), when
{d=2}
by de Jong’s period-index theorem
[
dJ04
]
, and it is open when
d\geq 3
. This problem provides incentives to construct Brauer classes whose period and index differ. Many strategies have been developed to do so, using ramification, degeneration, index reduction or Hodge theory arguments (see
[
CT02
]
and its appendix, as well as
[
Kre03
,
Hot22
,
dJP22
]
).

Let
S
be an irreducible and reduced Stein space of dimension
d
, one can still ask whether
\mathrm{ind}(\alpha)\mid\mathrm{per}(\alpha)^{d-1}
for all
\alpha\in\mathrm{Br}(\mathcal{M}(S))
. This holds for
d=1
because
\mathrm{Br}(\mathcal{M}(S))=0
by a result of M. Artin
[
Gur88
, Proposition 3.7]
, for
d=2
by the period-index theorem of
[
Ben25
, Theorem 1.4]
, and it is open for
d\geq 3
. Again, this motivates the search for Brauer classes whose period and index differ.
As in the algebraic case, one can use ramification to produce such examples.
However, classes in the image of
{\mathrm{Br}(\mathcal{O}(S))\to\mathrm{Br}(\mathcal{M}(S))}
are not susceptible to these valuative methods (discrete valuations on
\mathcal{M}(S)
are centered on
\mathcal{O}(S)
by
[
Iss66
, Theorem I]
) and we have been unable to apply to them the other methods listed above. Our last result shows that, in spite of this, the period and the index of a class in the image of
{\mathrm{Br}(\mathcal{O}(S))\to\mathrm{Br}(\mathcal{M}(S))}
may not coincide.

Theorem 1.11

(Theorem
6.8
)
.

There exist a connected Stein manifold
S
and a class
\alpha\in\mathrm{Im}[\mathrm{Br}(\mathcal{O}(S))\to\mathrm{Br}(\mathcal{M}(S))]
such that
\mathrm{ind}(\alpha)\neq\mathrm{per}(\alpha)
.

The obstruction to the equality of period and index used in the proof of Theorem
1.11
is of a topological nature: we exploit the topological period-index problem put forward and studied by Antieau and Williams (see
[
AW14a
,
AW14b
]
). In addition, our argument relies on Theorems
1.9
and
1.10
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Brauer groups

Let
(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
be a locally ringed site
(in our applications, it will be the étale site of a scheme, or a complex space
S
endowed with its sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{S}
of holomorphic functions, or a topological space
T
endowed with its sheaf
\mathcal{C}_{T}
of complex-valued continuous functions). An
Azumaya algebra
of degree
n
on
(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
is an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-algebra that is locally isomorphic to
M_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{X})
. Two Azumaya
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
on
(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
are
Morita-equivalent
if there exists an algebra isomorphism
{\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{E}){\mathrel{\to}^{\,\sim\,}}\mathcal{B}\otimes\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{F})}
for some locally free
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules of finite rank
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{F}
. The Brauer group
\mathrm{Br}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
of
(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
is the group of Morita-equivalence classes of Azumaya algebras on
(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
, endowed with the group law induced by tensor product (see
[
Gro68
, I, §2]
or
[
Gir71
, Chap. V, §4]
).

Isomorphism classes of degree
n
Azumaya algebras on
(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
are naturally in bijection with isomorphism classes of
\mathrm{PGL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{X})
-torsors, and hence are classified by
H^{1}(X,\mathrm{PGL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{X}))
(see
[
Gro68
, I, Remarque 5.12]
or
[
Gir71
, Chap. V, Remarque 4.5]
). The boundary maps
H^{1}(X,\mathrm{PGL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{X}))\to H^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\times})
of the short exact sequences

0\to\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\times}\to\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{X})\to\mathrm{PGL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{X})\to 0
induce an injective morphism

(2.1)

\mathrm{Br}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})\to H^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\times})_{\mathrm{tors}}

(see
[
Gro68
, I, §2]
or
[
Gir71
, Chap. V, §4.4, §4.6]
).

If
R
is a ring, set
\mathrm{Br}(R):=\mathrm{Br}(\mathrm{Spec}(R)_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{Spec}(R)})
. If
S
is a complex space, set
\mathrm{Br}_{\mathrm{an}}(S):=\mathrm{Br}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S})
. If
T
is a topological space, set
\mathrm{Br}_{\mathrm{top}}(T):=\mathrm{Br}(T,\mathcal{C}_{T})
.

2.2.
The analytification functor

Here are a few recollections on analytification in Stein geometry, for use in the proofs of Theorems
3.5
,
4.5
,
5.1
,
5.5
and
6.7
.

Let
S
be a Stein space. To an
\mathcal{O}(S)
-scheme of finite presentation

, Bingener associates its

: a complex space

over

equipped with a morphism of locally ringed spaces

, characterized by the property that

\mathrm{Hom}_{S}(S^{\prime},X^{\mathrm{an}})\xrightarrow{i_{X}\circ\,-}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}(S)-\textrm{locally ringed spaces}}(S^{\prime},X)

is bijective for all complex spaces

over

[

, see also
[

, §4.1]

[

is affine, then

is constructed as the zero locus of

in

. In general, the existence of the analytification is deduced from the affine case by choosing affine charts and gluing. This construction is functorial
[
Bin76
, p. 2]
, compatible with fiber products
[
Bin76
, p. 3]
, and turns étale morphisms into local biholomorphisms

, Satz 3.1]
.

Let

be an
\mathcal{O}(S)
-scheme of finite presentation and let

be a quasi-coherent sheaf of finite presentation. Bingener defines the

of

to be the coherent sheaf

on

{\mathcal{F}\mapsto\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{an}}}
is exact (see
[

, p. 3]
).

Define a coherent sheaf

on

to be

if it admits a presentation of the form

for some integers

and

. In

, Proposition 2.5]
, a monoidal adjoint equivalence of categories

(2.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\textrm{finitely presented quasi-coherent}\\
\hskip 20.00003pt\textrm{sheaves on }\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))\end{array}\right\}\mathrel{{\mathop{\rightleftarrows}\limits}}\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\textrm{globally finitely presented}\\
\hskip 6.99997pt\textrm{coherent sheaves on }S\end{array}\right\}

with left-to-right functor
\mathcal{F}\mapsto\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{an}}
and right-to-left functor
\mathcal{G}\mapsto\widetilde{\mathcal{G}(S)}
was constructed. To be precise, the statement of

, Proposition 2.5]
uses
\mathcal{O}(S)
-modules instead of quasi-coherent sheaves on
{\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))}
, but these two categories are equivalent in a way respecting the tensor product (see

and

(1)]
). In addition, it is not stated in

cit

Let

be an

-scheme of finite presentation. Analytifying étale

-schemes induces a morphism of sites

(which is equivalent to the usual site of

[

) to the small étale site of

and we view it as a sheaf on

. Pulling back by

gives rise to comparison morphisms
{H^{k}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{L})\to H^{k}(X^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{L}^{\mathrm{an}})}

3.
The Brauer group of a Stein algebra

3.1.
Stein algebras and Stein spaces

Proposition 3.1
.

If
S
is a Stein space, the natural morphism
{\mathrm{Br}_{\mathrm{an}}(S)\to\mathrm{Br}_{\mathrm{top}}(S)}
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Isomorphism classes of holomorphic and topological vector bundles of rank
r
on
S
are in bijection with
H^{1}(S,\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathcal{O}_{S}))
and
H^{1}(S,\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathcal{C}_{S}))
respectively. In addition, isomorphism classes of holomorphic and topological Azumaya algebras of degree
n
on
S
are in bijection with
H^{1}(S,\mathrm{PGL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{S}))
and
H^{1}(S,\mathrm{PGL}_{n}(\mathcal{C}_{S}))
respectively (see §
2.1
). The natural morphisms
H^{1}(S,\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathcal{O}_{S}))\to H^{1}(S,\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathcal{C}_{S}))
and
H^{1}(S,\mathrm{PGL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{S}))\to H^{1}(S,\mathrm{PGL}_{n}(\mathcal{C}_{S}))
are bijective by Grauert’s Oka principle (see
[
Gra58
, Satz 2 p. 267]
), hence induce a bijection between isomorphism classes of holomorphic and topological Azumaya algebras on
S
that respects Morita equivalence. This proves the proposition.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

If
S
is a finite-dimensional Stein space, the natural morphism
\mathrm{Br}(\mathcal{O}(S))\to\mathrm{Br}_{\mathrm{an}}(S)
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

For
r\geq 0
, the monoidal equivalences (
2.2
) restrict to equivalences between the categories of locally free sheaves of rank
r
on
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))
and locally free sheaves of
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-modules of rank
r
(see
[
Ben24a
, Remark 2.6]
). It follows that (
2.2
) respects Morita equivalence. In addition, the characterization of Azumaya algebras
\mathcal{A}
on
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))
(
resp.
of holomorphic Azumaya algebras on
(S,\mathcal{O}_{S})
) as those locally free algebras such that the algebra morphism
{\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{A})}
given by left and right multiplication is an isomorphism (see
[
Gro68
, I, Théorème 5.1 (ii) et Remarque 5.12]
) shows that the two arrows of (
2.2
) send Azumaya algebras to Azumaya algebras. We deduce that
\mathrm{Br}(\mathcal{O}(S))\to\mathrm{Br}_{\mathrm{an}}(S)
is an isomorphism.
∎

3.2.
The topological Brauer group of a finite-dimensional CW complex

Let
T
be a topological space. Consider the composition

(3.1)

\mathrm{Br}_{\mathrm{top}}(T)\to H^{2}(T,\mathcal{C}_{T}^{\times})_{\mathrm{tors}}\to H^{3}(T,\mathbb{Z})_{\mathrm{tors}}

of (
2.1
) and of the boundary map of the short exact sequence

0\to\mathbb{Z}\xrightarrow{2\pi i}\mathcal{C}_{T}\xrightarrow{\exp}\mathcal{C}_{T}^{\times}\to 0.

It follows from
[
Gro68
, I, pp. 48-50]
or
[
Gir71
, Chap. V, §4.6]
that the composition (
3.1
) is also induced by the compositions

(3.2)

H^{1}(T,\mathrm{PGL}_{n}(\mathcal{C}_{T}))\to H^{2}(T,\mathbb{Z}/n)\to H^{3}(T,\mathbb{Z})[n]

of the boundary maps associated with the short exact sequences

0\to\mathbb{Z}/n\xrightarrow{1\mapsto\exp(\frac{2\pi i}{n})\mathrm{Id}_{n}}\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathcal{C}_{T})\to\mathrm{PGL}_{n}(\mathcal{C}_{T})\to 0\textrm{ and }0\to\mathbb{Z}\xrightarrow{n}\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}/n\to 0.

Serre proved that the composition (
3.1
) is an isomorphism if
T
is a finite CW complex (see
[
Gro68
, I, Corollaire 1.7]
). We relax this finiteness hypothesis.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
T
be a finite-dimensional CW complex. Then the canonical morphisms
\mathrm{Br}_{\mathrm{top}}(T)\to H^{2}(T,\mathcal{C}_{T}^{\times})_{\mathrm{tors}}\to H^{3}(T,\mathbb{Z})_{\mathrm{tors}}
are isomorphisms.

Proof.

As
T
is paracompact (see
[
Miy52
]
), the sheaf
\mathcal{C}_{T}
is fine. It follows that
H^{2}(T,\mathcal{C}_{T})=H^{3}(T,\mathcal{C}_{T})=0
, so
H^{2}(T,\mathcal{C}_{T}^{\times})\to H^{3}(T,\mathbb{Z})
is an isomorphism. The morphism
\mathrm{Br}_{\mathrm{top}}(T)\to H^{2}(T,\mathcal{C}_{T}^{\times})_{\mathrm{tors}}
is injective (see
[
Gro68
, I, Proposition 1.4 3
o
]
).

To prove the surjectivity of
\mathrm{Br}_{\mathrm{top}}(T)\to H^{3}(T,\mathbb{Z})_{\mathrm{tors}}
, fix
\alpha\in H^{3}(T,\mathbb{Z})[n]
. Write
\alpha=\partial(\beta)
, where
\partial
is the boundary map associated to the long exact sequence of cohomology of
0\to\mathbb{Z}\xrightarrow{n}\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}/n\to 0
. Choose an Eilenberg–MacLane CW complex
K(\mathbb{Z}/n,2)
with finitely many cells in each dimension (see
[
Tho54
, §II.6 p. 36]
). Let
d
be the dimension of
S
and let
i:K(\mathbb{Z}/n,2)_{\leq d}\hookrightarrow K(\mathbb{Z}/n,2)
be the inclusion of the
d
-skeleton of
K(\mathbb{Z}/n,2)
. Let
\beta_{0}\in H^{2}(K(\mathbb{Z}/n,2),\mathbb{Z}/n)
be the tautological class. By cellular approximation
[
Hat02
, Theorem 4.8]
, one can find a continuous map
f:T\to K(\mathbb{Z}/n,2)_{\leq d}
with
f^{*}i^{*}\beta_{0}=\beta
. By Serre’s theorem (see
[
Gro68
, I, Corollaire 1.7]
), the class
i^{*}\partial(\beta_{0})\in H^{3}(K(\mathbb{Z}/n,2)_{\leq d},\mathbb{Z})_{\mathrm{tors}}
comes from
\mathrm{Br}_{\mathrm{top}}(K(\mathbb{Z}/n,2)_{\leq d})
. It follows that
\alpha=f^{*}i^{*}\partial(\beta_{0})
comes from
\mathrm{Br}_{\mathrm{top}}(T)
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

Our proof of Theorem
3.3
reduces the case of finite-dimensional CW complexes to the case of finite CW complexes dealt with by Serre. However, the argument of Serre appearing in
[
Gro68
, I, Corollaire 1.7]
does not apply directly to infinite finite-dimensional CW complexes, for the following reason.

Set
\mathrm{PGL}_{\infty}(\mathbb{C}):=\mathrm{colim}_{n\geq 1}\mathrm{PGL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, where transition maps are given by tensorization by unit matrices. A continuous map
T\to\mathrm{BPGL}_{\infty}(\mathbb{C})
lifts to
\mathrm{BPGL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
for some
n
if
T
is a finite CW complex (Serre uses this), but not necessarily so if
T
is only finite-dimensional. A counterexample is given by taking
T
to be the
3
-skeleton of
\mathrm{BPGL}_{\infty}(\mathbb{C})
. Indeed, as the canonical class in
H^{2}(\mathrm{BPGL}_{\infty}(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
is not torsion (because its restriction to
H^{2}(\mathrm{BPGL}_{n}(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
has order exactly
n
), its restriction to
H^{2}(T,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
is not torsion, and hence the natural map
T\to\mathrm{BPGL}_{\infty}(\mathbb{C})
cannot lift to a map
T\to\mathrm{BPGL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
for any
n
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
S
be a finite-dimensional Stein space. The natural morphisms

\mathrm{Br}(\mathcal{O}(S))\to\mathrm{Br}_{\mathrm{an}}(S)\to\mathrm{Br}_{\mathrm{top}}(S)\to H^{3}(S,\mathbb{Z})_{\mathrm{tors}}

Proof.

As

and

and Theorem

∎

3.3.

]

Theorem 3.6
.

Proof.

When

[

with the homotopy type of

∎

Lemma 3.7
.

There exist a countable CW complex
T
with

and a rank

on

such that the complex vector bundle

Proof.

Choose

to be a homotopy fiber of the adjoint map
{\mathrm{BSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\to\mathrm{BGL}_{3}(\mathbb{C})}
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be the universal vector bundle on

and let

be its pull-back to

With integral coefficients, the Leray spectral sequence for the fibration sequence

shows that

. With coefficients

Generators of the exterior algebra

transgress to some Chern polynomials of

∎

Proposition 3.8
.

There exists a Stein space
S
such that the natural morphism
\mathrm{Br}(\mathcal{O}(S))\to\mathrm{Br}_{\mathrm{an}}(S)
is not injective.

Proof.

Let

and
\mathcal{E}
be as in Lemma

to find a Stein space

of the homotopy type of the

. Let

be the disjoint union of the

(so

). Let

be the vector bundle on

(endowed with a structure of holomorphic vector bundle by the Oka principle
[
Gra58

[
Gra58
, Satz I]

on a trivial vector bundle on

such that

As

4.1.

We start with a well-known lemma.

Lemma 4.1

∎

4.2.
Central separable algebras

In this paragraph, we gather generalities on central separable algebras that are used in the proof of Theorem
4.5
.

Let
X
be a scheme. Let
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{N}
be quasi-coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules and fix a surjective morphism
{\lambda:\mathcal{M}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}}
of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules. The
elementary algebra

\mathcal{M}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}^{\lambda}\mathcal{N}
is the (possibly non-unital) quasi-coherent associative algebra whose underlying sheaf is
\mathcal{M}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{N}
, endowed with the product

(4.5)

(m\otimes n)\star(m^{\prime}\otimes n^{\prime})=\lambda(m^{\prime}\otimes n)\cdot(m\otimes n^{\prime}).

Following Heinloth and Schröer
[
HS09
, Definition 2.1]
, we say that a (possibly non-unital) quasi-coherent associative
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
is
central separable
if it is étale-locally isomorphic to an elementary algebra (in
loc
.
cit
., this condition is only required smooth-locally, but see
[
SP
, Lemma
055U
]
). Let
p:U\to X
be an étale morphism. A
splitting
of
\mathcal{A}
over
U
is the data of quasi-coherent
\mathcal{O}_{U}
-modules
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{N}
, of a surjective morphism
{\lambda:\mathcal{M}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{U}}\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{O}_{U}}
, and of an algebra isomorphism
\varphi:\mathcal{M}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{U}}^{\lambda}\mathcal{N}{\mathrel{\to}^{\,\sim\,}}p^{*}\mathcal{A}
.

As is explained in
[
HS09
, p. 1191]
, associating to
p:U\to X
the category of splittings
(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N},\lambda,\varphi)
of
p^{*}\mathcal{A}
over
U
(and isomorphisms of splittings) gives rise to a stack
\mathfrak{X}_{\mathcal{A}}
on the small étale site of
X
. By results of Raeburn and Taylor
[
RT85
, Lemmas 2.3 and 2.4]
(and since splittings exist étale-locally by our assumption), the stack
\mathfrak{X}_{\mathcal{A}}
is a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe. We denote by
[\mathcal{A}]\in H^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{G}_{m})
the class of
\mathfrak{X}_{\mathcal{A}}
.

The following theorem originates from
[
RT85
, Corollary 4.1]
(with a mistake pointed out and corrected in
[
CG98
, Theorem 3.4]
). In the form below, it follows from the work of Heinloth and Schröer
[
HS09
]
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
X
be a quasi-compact and quasi-separated scheme. Fix a cohomology class
{\alpha\in H^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{G}_{m})}
. There exists a central separable algebra
\mathcal{A}
of finite presentation over
X
with
[\mathcal{A}]=\alpha
, a surjective étale morphism
p:U\to X
, and a splitting
(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N},\lambda,\varphi)
of
p^{*}\mathcal{A}
over
U
with
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{N}
of finite presentation.

Proof.

By a limit argument (based on
[
SP
, Proposition
01ZA
]
and
[
SP
, Theorem
09YQ
]
applied with
\mathcal{F}_{i}=\mathbb{G}_{m}
), we may assume that
X
is noetherian. It then follows from
[
HS09
, Theorem 3.1 and its proof]
that
there exists a central separable algebra
\mathcal{A}
on
X
with
[\mathcal{A}]=\alpha
. By
[
HS09
, Theorem 2.5]
, the
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
may be chosen coherent. In addition, in view of the proof of
[
HS09
, Theorem 2.5 (iii)
\Rightarrow
(ii)]
, we may assume that, for some surjective étale morphism
{p:U\to X}
, there exists a splitting
(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N},\lambda,\varphi)
of
p^{*}\mathcal{A}
over
U
with
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{N}
coherent.
∎

We will use analogues of the above constructions in complex-analytic geometry. Let
S
be a complex space. Given coherent sheaves
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{N}
on
S
and a surjective morphism
{\lambda:\mathcal{M}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{S}}\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{O}_{S}}
, the
elementary algebra
associated to
(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N},\lambda)
is the coherent
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-algebra whose underlying sheaf is
\mathcal{M}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{S}}\mathcal{N}
, endowed with the product (
4.5
). A coherent
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
is
central separable
if it is locally isomorphic to an elementary algebra.
A
splitting
of
\mathcal{A}
over an open subset
V\subset S
is the data of coherent sheaves
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{N}
on
V
, of a surjective morphism
{\lambda:\mathcal{M}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{V}}\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{O}_{V}}
and of an algebra isomorphism
\varphi:\mathcal{M}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{V}}^{\lambda}\mathcal{N}{\mathrel{\to}^{\,\sim\,}}\mathcal{A}|_{V}
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
S
be a complex space. Let
\mathcal{A}
be a central separable
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-algebra. The stack
\mathfrak{X}_{\mathcal{A}}
over
S
associating to each open subset
V\subset S
the category of splittings
(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N},\lambda,\varphi)
of
\mathcal{A}|_{V}
(and isomorphisms of splittings) is an
\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\times}
-gerbe.

Proof.

Splittings of
\mathcal{A}
exist locally by assumption. Two splittings of
\mathcal{A}
on
{V\subset S}
are isomorphic at the level of stalks by
[
RT85
, Lemma 2.3]
, hence are locally isomorphic by coherence (use
[
GR84
, Annex, §2.4]
). In addition, an automorphism of a splitting
(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N},\lambda,\varphi)
of
\mathcal{A}
on
V\subset S
is given by multiplication by a (unique) invertible function on
\mathcal{M}
and by its inverse on
\mathcal{N}
(this holds at the level of stalks by
[
RT85
, Lemma 2.4]
and globalizes by coherence).
∎

If
\mathcal{A}
is a central separable algebra on a complex space
S
, we let
[\mathcal{A}]\in H^{2}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\times})
denote the class of the
\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\times}
-gerbe
\mathfrak{X}_{\mathcal{A}}
defined in Lemma
4.4
.

4.3.
Invertible coefficients

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
S
be a finite-dimensional Stein space. The natural morphism

(4.6)

H^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S)),\mathbb{G}_{m})\to H^{2}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\times})

is injective.

Proof.

Fix
\alpha\in H^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S)),\mathbb{G}_{m})
in the kernel of (
4.6
). Applying Theorem
4.3
yields a central separable algebra
\mathcal{A}
of finite presentation on
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))
with
{[\mathcal{A}]=\alpha}
, a surjective étale morphism
p:U\to X
, and a splitting
(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N},\lambda,\varphi)
of
p^{*}\mathcal{A}
over
U
with
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{N}
quasi-coherent of finite presentation. Fix presentations
\mathcal{O}_{U}^{\oplus m_{2}}\to\mathcal{O}_{U}^{\oplus m_{1}}\to\mathcal{M}\to 0
and
\mathcal{O}_{U}^{\oplus n_{2}}\to\mathcal{O}_{U}^{\oplus n_{1}}\to\mathcal{N}\to 0
of
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{N}
.

We now analytify all these objects (in the sense of §
2.2
). The analytification
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{an}}
of
\mathcal{A}
is a coherent
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-algebra, which is globally of finite presentation as an
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-module. As
{p^{\mathrm{an}}:U^{\mathrm{an}}\to S}
is a surjective local biholomorphism, the data
(\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{an}},\mathcal{N}^{\mathrm{an}},\lambda^{\mathrm{an}},\varphi^{\mathrm{an}})
shows that
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{an}}
admits local splittings, hence is a central separable
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-algebra. The above presentations of
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{N}
induce presentations
\mathcal{O}_{U^{\mathrm{an}}}^{\oplus m_{2}}\to\mathcal{O}_{U^{\mathrm{an}}}^{\oplus m_{1}}\to\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{an}}\to 0
and
\mathcal{O}_{U^{\mathrm{an}}}^{\oplus n_{2}}\to\mathcal{O}_{U^{\mathrm{an}}}^{\oplus n_{1}}\to\mathcal{N}^{\mathrm{an}}\to 0
of
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{an}}
and
\mathcal{N}^{\mathrm{an}}
.

The class
[\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{an}}]\in H^{2}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\times})
of
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{an}}
is the image of
[\mathcal{A}]=\alpha
by (
4.6
), and hence vanishes. It follows that
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{an}}
is split over
S
. Therefore, there exist coherent sheaves
\mathcal{M}_{S}
and
\mathcal{N}_{S}
on
S
, as well as a surjective morphism
\lambda_{S}:\mathcal{M}_{S}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{S}}\mathcal{N}_{S}\to\mathcal{O}_{S}
and an algebra isomorphism
\varphi_{S}:\mathcal{M}_{S}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{S}}^{\lambda_{S}}\mathcal{N}_{S}{\mathrel{\to}^{\,\sim\,}}\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{an}}
.

By Lemma
4.4
, the sheaves
p^{*}\mathcal{M}_{S}
and
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{an}}
are locally isomorphic on
U^{\mathrm{an}}
. It follows that, for all
x\in S
, there exists a resolution
\mathcal{O}_{S,x}^{\oplus m_{2}}\to\mathcal{O}_{S,x}^{\oplus m_{1}}\to(\mathcal{M}_{S})_{x}\to 0
. By Lemma
4.6
below, there exists a resolution
\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\oplus m^{\prime}_{2}}\to\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\oplus m^{\prime}_{1}}\to\mathcal{M}_{S}\to 0
for some integers
m_{1}^{\prime}
and
m_{2}^{\prime}
. The exact same argument shows the existence of integers
n^{\prime}_{1}
and
n^{\prime}_{2}
and of a resolution
\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\oplus n^{\prime}_{2}}\to\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\oplus n^{\prime}_{1}}\to\mathcal{N}_{S}\to 0
.

Applying
[
Ben24a
, Proposition 2.5]
(see (
2.2
)) shows the existence of quasi-coherent sheaves of finite presentation
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{Q}
on
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))
endowed with isomorphisms
\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{an}}{\mathrel{\to}^{\,\sim\,}}\mathcal{M}_{S}
and
\mathcal{Q}^{\mathrm{an}}{\mathrel{\to}^{\,\sim\,}}\mathcal{N}_{S}
, of a surjective morphism of sheaves
{\mu:\mathcal{P}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))}}\mathcal{Q}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))}}
such that
\mu^{\mathrm{an}}=\lambda
, and of an algebra isomorphism
{\psi:\mathcal{P}\otimes^{\mu}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))}}\mathcal{Q}{\mathrel{\to}^{\,\sim\,}}\mathcal{A}}
with
\psi^{\mathrm{an}}=\varphi
. The central separable algebra
\mathcal{A}
is therefore split, and
\alpha=[\mathcal{A}]=0
in
H^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S)),\mathbb{G}_{m})
.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
S
be a finite-dimensional Stein space. Let
\mathcal{G}
be a coherent sheaf on
S
. Assume that there exist integers
n_{1}
and
n_{2}
and resolutions

(4.7)

\mathcal{O}_{S,x}^{\oplus n_{2}}\to\mathcal{O}_{S,x}^{\oplus n_{1}}\to\mathcal{G}_{x}\to 0

for all
x\in S
. Then there exist integers
n_{1}^{\prime}
and
n_{2}^{\prime}
and a resolution

(4.8)

\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\oplus n^{\prime}_{2}}\to\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\oplus n^{\prime}_{1}}\to\mathcal{G}\to 0.

Proof.

In view of (
4.7
), all the stalks of
\mathcal{G}
are generated by
n_{1}
germs of sections. As
\mathcal{G}
is moreover globally generated because
S
is Stein, it follows from
[
Kri69
, Theorem 1]
that there exist an integer
n_{1}^{\prime}
and a surjective morphism
\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\oplus n^{\prime}_{1}}\to\mathcal{G}
. Let
\mathcal{K}
be the kernel of this morphism, so one has a short exact sequence

(4.9)

0\to\mathcal{K}\to\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\oplus n^{\prime}_{1}}\to\mathcal{G}\to 0.

Fix
x\in S
. Let
\mathfrak{m}_{x}\subset\mathcal{O}_{S,x}
be the maximal ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{S,x}
. Tensoring (
4.9
) with the
\mathcal{O}_{S,x}
-module
\mathcal{O}_{S,x}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}
gives rise to an exact sequence

(4.10)

\mathrm{Tor}_{1}^{\mathcal{O}_{S,x}}(\mathcal{G}_{x},\mathcal{O}_{S,x}/\mathfrak{m}_{x})\to\mathcal{K}_{x}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}\mathcal{K}_{x}\to(\mathcal{O}_{S,x}/\mathfrak{m}_{x})^{\oplus n_{1}^{\prime}}.

The exact sequence (
4.7
) shows that
\mathrm{Tor}_{1}^{\mathcal{O}_{S,x}}(\mathcal{G}_{x},\mathcal{O}_{S,x}/\mathfrak{m}_{x})
is an
\mathcal{O}_{S,x}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}
-vector space of dimension
\leq n_{2}
. In view of (
4.10
), the
\mathcal{O}_{S,x}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}
-vector space
\mathcal{K}_{x}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}\mathcal{K}_{x}
has dimension
\leq n_{1}^{\prime}+n_{2}
. By Nakayama’s lemma, we deduce that
\mathcal{K}_{x}
is generated by
n_{1}^{\prime}+n_{2}
germs of sections. As
\mathcal{K}
is moreover globally generated because
S
is Stein, another application of
[
Kri69
, Theorem 1]
provides us with an integer
n_{2}^{\prime}
and a surjective morphism
\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\oplus n^{\prime}_{2}}\to\mathcal{K}
. This produces the desired resolution (
4.8
).
∎

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
S
be a Stein space of dimension
\leq 2
. Then

{H^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S)),\mathbb{G}_{m})=0.}

Proof.

The exponential exact sequence
{0\to\mathbb{Z}\xrightarrow{2\pi i}\mathcal{O}_{S}\xrightarrow{\exp}\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\times}\to 0}
and the vanishing of
H^{2}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S})
and
H^{3}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S})
(as
S
is Stein) show that
H^{2}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\times})
is isomorphic to
H^{3}(S,\mathbb{Z})
, which is zero because
S
has the homotopy type of a CW complex of dimension
2
(see
[
Ham86
, Korollar]
). We deduce from Theorem
4.5
that
H^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S)),\mathbb{G}_{m})
vanishes.
∎

A similar albeit simpler argument yields the following algebraic analogue of Theorem
4.5
. It can be viewed as a variant of
[
Sch05
, Propositions 1.3 and 1.4]
taking nontorsion classes of
H^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(V,\mathbb{G}_{m})
into account. In its statement, analytifications are meant in the sense of
[
Ser56
]
(see also
[
SGA1
, Exp. XII, §1]
when

Proposition 4.8
.

If
V
is a proper algebraic variety over

, the natural morphism

(4.11)

H^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(V,\mathbb{G}_{m})\to H^{2}(V^{\mathrm{an}},\mathcal{O}_{V^{\mathrm{an}}}^{\times})

Fix

4.4.
A nontorsion class in the cohomological Brauer group

The next proposition shows that the use of Azumaya algebras (instead of more general central separable algebras) would have been insufficient to prove Theorem
4.5
.

Proposition 4.9
.

There exists a connected normal Stein space
S
of dimension

such that

is not a torsion group.

Proof.

Let
V\subset\mathbb{P}^{4}_{\mathbb{C}}
be a cubic threefold with a single node. The variety
V
is locally factorial (see e.g.
[
CDG04
, Theorem]
),
but its henselization at the singular point has class group isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}
. It therefore follows from
[
Gro68
, II, Proposition 1.7 and (7bis) p. 75]
that
H^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(V,\mathbb{G}_{m})
contains a subgroup isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}
.

Fix a nontorsion
\alpha\in H^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(V,\mathbb{G}_{m})
. The image
\alpha^{\mathrm{an}}\in H^{2}(V^{\mathrm{an}},\mathcal{O}_{V^{\mathrm{an}}}^{\times})
of
\alpha
by (
4.11
) is nontorsion by Proposition
4.8
(here, the analytification is meant in the sense of
[
Ser56
]
or
[
SGA1
, Exp. XII]
). Since

(by GAGA, see
[

, §12, Théorème 1]

of

by the boundary map of the exponential exact sequence is nontorsion.

Let

be a smooth hyperplane section, and set

. The complex space

is connected normal of dimension

(because so is

∎

5.
Holomorphic functions versus meromorphic functions

5.1.
Twisted sheaves

We recall the requisite background on twisted sheaves used in the proof of Theorem
5.1
. We refer to
[
Lie07
, §2]
for more details.

Let
(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
be a ringed site. Giraud has constructed a bijection between the set of equivalence classes of
\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\times}
-gerbes on
X
and the cohomology group
H^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\times})
(see
[
Gir71
, Chap. IV, §3.4]
).

Fix
\alpha\in H^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\times})
and let
\pi:\mathfrak{X}\to X
be an
\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\times}
-gerbe representing
\alpha
. Endow
\mathfrak{X}
with the structure of a site ringed by setting
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}:=\pi^{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}
(see
[
Lie07
, Definition 2.1.1.1]
). An
\alpha
-
twisted sheaf
on
X
is an
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}
-module
\mathcal{F}
such that the two natural right
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}^{\times}
-actions on
\mathcal{F}
(induced by the
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}
-module structure and by the action of the inertia of
\mathfrak{X}
) coincide (see
[
Lie07
, Definition 2.1.2.2]
for a precise definition).
Note that when
(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
is a scheme, the gerbe
\mathfrak{X}
is an algebraic stack.

We will make use of another equivalent point of view on twisted sheaves, due to Căldăraru
[
Căl00
]
. Let
U_{\bullet}\to X
be a hypercovering and let
{a\in H^{0}(U_{2},\mathcal{O}_{U_{2}}^{\times})}
be a Čech cocycle representing
\alpha
. We denote by
{p_{0},p_{1}:U_{1}\to U_{0}}
the two projections and by
q_{0},q_{1},q_{2}:U_{2}\to U_{1}
the three projections. A Căldăraru-
\alpha
-twisted sheaf is an
\mathcal{O}_{U_{0}}
-module
\mathcal{G}
endowed with an isomorphism
{\psi:p_{0}^{*}\mathcal{G}{\mathrel{\to}^{\,\sim\,}}p_{1}^{*}\mathcal{G}}
such that
(q_{1}^{*}\psi)^{-1}\circ q_{2}^{*}\psi\circ q_{0}^{*}\psi=a
. Lieblich has constructed an equivalence between the categories of
\alpha
-twisted and Căldăraru-
\alpha
-twisted sheaves on
X
(see
[
Lie07
, Proposition 2.1.3.3]
; the proof appears in
[
Lie04
, §2.1.3]
).

If
(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
is a scheme, Lieblich has verified that an
\alpha
-twisted sheaf on
X
is quasi-coherent if and only if so is its associated Căldăraru-
\alpha
-twisted sheaf (see
[
Lie07
, Lemma 2.2.1.4]
). The exact same proof shows that an
\alpha
-twisted sheaf on
X
is of finite presentation if and only if so is its associated Căldăraru-
\alpha
-twisted sheaf.

5.2.

Theorem 5.1
.

The natural morphism

(5.1)

Let
\mathfrak{X}\to\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))
be a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe representing
\alpha

Corollary 5.2
.

The natural morphism

(5.3)

\mathrm{Br}(\mathcal{O}(S))\to H^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S)),\mathbb{G}_{m})

is an isomorphism for any finite-dimensional locally factorial Stein space.

Proof.

By Gabber’s theorem
[
Gab81
, Chap. II, Theorem 1]
, the morphism (
5.3
) identifies
\mathrm{Br}(\mathcal{O}(S))
with the torsion subgroup of

. In view of Theorem

, the group

injects into the Galois cohomology group

, and hence is torsion. This proves the corollary.
∎

Corollary 5.3
.

The natural morphism

\mathrm{Br}(\mathcal{O}(S))\to\mathrm{Br}(\mathcal{M}(S))

is injective for any finite-dimensional locally factorial Stein space.

Proof.

As the groups
\mathrm{Br}(\mathcal{O}(S))
and
\mathrm{Br}(\mathcal{M}(S))
inject into
H^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S)),\mathbb{G}_{m})
and

respectively, the corollary follows from Theorem
5.1
.
∎

Corollary 5.4
.

There exists a connected Stein manifold such that the natural morphism
H^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S)),\mathbb{G}_{m})\to H^{2}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\times})
is not surjective.

be a Stein manifold with the homotopy type of

(apply
[

, Theorem 4.1]

, or choose

). The group

. The exponential exact sequence

H^{1}(D_{i}^{0},\mathbb{Z}/n)\to H^{1}(D_{i}^{0}\setminus(Z_{i}\cap D_{i}^{0}),\mathbb{Z}/n)
is injective (because
Z_{i}\cap D_{i}^{0}
is a nowhere dense analytic subset of the complex manifold
D_{i}^{0}
), one has
\gamma_{i}=0
.
∎

We resume the proof of Theorem
5.5
. Lemma
5.6
shows that
\gamma=0
, hence that
\beta\in H^{2}(S\setminus Z,\mathbb{Z}/n)
lifts to a cohomology class
\beta^{\prime}\in H^{2}(S\setminus Z^{\mathrm{sing}},\mathbb{Z}/n)
. As
S
is a manifold and
Z^{\mathrm{sing}}
has codimension
\geq 2
in
S
, the class
\beta^{\prime}
further lifts to a class
\beta^{\prime\prime}\in H^{2}(S,\mathbb{Z}/n)
(stratifying
Z^{\mathrm{sing}}
by its singular locus, the singular locus of its singular locus, etc., one reduces to the case where
Z^{\mathrm{sing}}
is smooth in which case this follows from a Gysin long exact sequence).

Let
\partial:H^{2}(S,\mathbb{Z}/n)\to H^{3}(S,\mathbb{Z})[n]
be the connecting morphism associated to the short exact sequence
{0\to\mathbb{Z}\xrightarrow{n}\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}/n\to 0}
. By Theorem
3.5
, the natural morphism
\mathrm{Br}(\mathcal{O}(S))\to H^{3}(S,\mathbb{Z})_{\mathrm{tors}}
is an isomorphism. After subtracting from
\alpha
the element of
\mathrm{Br}(\mathcal{O}(S))
corresponding to
\partial(\beta^{\prime\prime})\in H^{3}(S,\mathbb{Z})[n]
and modifying
\alpha^{\prime}
,
\beta
,
\beta^{\prime}
and
\beta^{\prime\prime}
accordingly, we may assume that
\partial(\beta^{\prime\prime})=0
. This shows that
\beta^{\prime\prime}
is the reduction modulo
n
of a class
\delta\in H^{2}(S,\mathbb{Z})
.

The exponential exact sequence
0\to\mathbb{Z}\xrightarrow{2\pi i}\mathcal{O}_{S}\xrightarrow{\exp}\mathcal{O}_{S}^{*}\to 0
shows that
\delta
is the first Chern class of a holomorphic line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on
S
. As
S
is Stein, one can choose
\sigma\in H^{0}(S,\mathcal{L})
and
\tau\in H^{0}(S,\mathcal{L}^{-1})
whose zero loci are nowhere dense in
S
. Replacing the nonzerodivisor
a\in\mathcal{O}(S)
with
a\sigma\tau\in\mathcal{O}(S)
, one can ensure that
\delta|_{S\setminus Z}\in H^{2}(S\setminus Z,\mathbb{Z})
vanishes, hence that
\beta=0
.

At this point, consider the Leray spectral sequence

(5.6)

E_{2}^{p,q}=H^{p}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(U,\mathrm{R}^{q}(\varepsilon_{U})_{*}\mathbb{Z}/n)\Rightarrow H^{p+q}(S\setminus Z,\mathbb{Z}/n)

of the morphism of sites
\varepsilon_{U}:(S\setminus Z)_{\mathrm{cl}}=(U^{\mathrm{an}})_{\mathrm{cl}}\to U_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}
(see §
2.2
). Taking into account that the natural morphism
\mathbb{Z}/n\to(\varepsilon_{U})_{*}\mathbb{Z}/n
is an isomorphism (see
[
Ben24a
, Corollary 3.12]
), the spectral sequence (
5.6
) induces an exact sequence

(5.7)

H^{0}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(U,\mathrm{R}^{1}(\varepsilon_{U})_{*}\mathbb{Z}/n)\to H^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(U,\mathbb{Z}/n)\to H^{2}(S\setminus Z,\mathbb{Z}/n).

As
\beta\in H^{2}(S\setminus Z,\mathbb{Z}/n)
vanishes, the class
\alpha^{\prime}\in H^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(U,\mathbb{Z}/n)
lifts in (
5.7
) to a class
\alpha^{\prime\prime}\in H^{0}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(U,\mathrm{R}^{1}(\varepsilon_{U})_{*}\mathbb{Z}/n)
. There exists a nonzerodivisor
b\in\mathcal{O}(S)
such that
\alpha^{\prime\prime}|_{\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S)[\frac{1}{ab}])}
vanishes (unravelling the definition of a higher direct image, this claim follows from
[
Ben24a
, Lemma 6.4]
). After replacing
a
with
ab
, we may therefore assume that
\alpha^{\prime\prime}=0
, hence that
\alpha^{\prime}=0
, hence a fortiori that
\alpha=0
. The theorem is proved.
∎

6.
The index for Brauer classes of Stein algebras

Let
(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
be a locally
ringed site and fix
\alpha\in\mathrm{Br}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
. The index
\mathrm{ind}(\alpha)
of
\alpha
is the gcd of the degrees of the Azamuya algebras representing
\alpha
. In view of the first paragraph of the proof of
[
Lie08
, Proposition 3.1.2.1]
, the index
\mathrm{ind}(\alpha)
can equivalently be defined as the gcd of the ranks of all locally free
\alpha
-twisted sheaves (in the sense of §
5.1
, where one identifies
\alpha
and its image in
H^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\times})
by (
2.1
)).

6.1.
Algebraic index versus topological index

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
S
be a finite-dimensional Stein space. For
\alpha\in\mathrm{Br}(\mathcal{O}(S))
with images
\alpha^{\mathrm{an}}\in\mathrm{Br}_{\mathrm{an}}(S)
and
\alpha^{\mathrm{top}}\in\mathrm{Br}_{\mathrm{top}}(S)
, one has
\mathrm{ind}(\alpha)=\mathrm{ind}(\alpha^{\mathrm{an}})=\mathrm{ind}(\alpha^{\mathrm{top}})
.

Proof.

Associating to an Azumaya algebra
\mathcal{A}
on
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))
the holomorphic Azumaya algebra
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{an}}
and to a holomorphic Azumaya algebra
\mathcal{B}
on
S
the topological Azumaya algebra
\mathcal{B}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{S}}\mathcal{C}_{S}
shows that
\mathrm{ind}(\alpha^{\mathrm{an}})\mid\mathrm{ind}(\alpha)
and
\mathrm{ind}(\alpha^{\mathrm{top}})\mid\mathrm{ind}(\alpha^{\mathrm{an}})
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a degree
n
topological Azumaya algebra on
S
. The proof of Proposition
3.1
shows that one can endow
\mathcal{A}
with a structure of degree
n
holomorphic Azumaya algebra on
S
. As
S
is finite-dimensional, the proof of Proposition
3.2
shows that
\mathcal{A}
is the analytification of a degree
n
Azumaya algebra on
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))
. This proves the converse divisibility
\mathrm{ind}(\alpha)\mid\mathrm{ind}(\alpha^{\mathrm{top}})
.
∎

Corollary 6.2
.

Let
S
be an
n
-dimensional Stein space.
For any
\alpha\in\mathrm{Br}(\mathcal{O}(S))
,

\mathrm{ind}(\alpha)\mid(\lceil n/2\rceil-1)!\ \mathrm{per}(\alpha)^{\lceil n/2\rceil-1}

Proof.

By Theorem
6.1
, it suffices to prove the same bound for
\alpha^{\mathrm{top}}\in\mathrm{Br}_{\mathrm{top}}(S)
.
In fact, the bound in Corollary
6.2
holds more generally for a topological Brauer class
\alpha
on an
n
-dimensional CW complex
T
(note that
S
has the homotopy type of a CW complex of dimension
n
by
[
Ham86
, Korollar]
).
Indeed, as in the proof of Theorem
3.3
, one reduces to the case when
T
is a finite CW complex of dimension
n
. One can then apply the main result from
[
AW21
]
with
d=\lceil n/2\rceil
.
∎

Remark 6.3
.

Antieau and Williams have conjectured in
[
AW21
]
that for each
d\geq 1
and each integer
\ell\geq 2
prime to
(d-1)!
, there exists a
2d
-dimensional finite CW complex
T
and a topological Brauer class
\alpha\in\mathrm{Br}_{\mathrm{top}}(T)
of period

such that

\mathrm{ind}(\alpha)=\mathrm{per}(\alpha)^{d-1}=\ell^{d-1}.

If true, their conjecture would imply (by way of Lemma
3.6
and Theorem
6.1
) that the bound in Corollary
6.2
is sharp for Brauer classes of period prime to
(n/2-1)!
on Stein spaces of even dimension
n
.

Remark 6.4
.

Fix an integer
\ell\geq 2
,
and let
S
be a
3
-dimensional Stein manifold with the homotopy type of the Moore space
M(\mathbb{Z}/\ell,2)
(using Theorem
3.6
and the fact that
M(\mathbb{Z}/\ell,2)
is a

From Theorem

and the universal coefficient theorem, one calculates that

This example shows that it is not generally possible to improve in the ceiling

to a floor

everywhere in Corollary

.

6.2.
Twisted topological K-theory

The cohomology theory called
twisted topological K-theory
was introduced by Donovan and Karoubi in
[
DK70
]
and developed by Atiyah and Segal
[
AS04
]
and others. We collect here the necessary background on twisted topological K-theory used in the proof of Theorem
6.7
in §
6.3
.

Let
T
be a topological space and let
\mathcal{A}
be a topological Azumaya algebra of index
n
on
T
.
An
\mathcal{A}
-
bundle
is a right
\mathcal{A}
-module that is locally free of finite rank as a
\mathcal{C}_{T}
-module. We define
K(T,\mathcal{A})
to be the K-theory of the exact category of
\mathcal{A}
-bundles (see
[
DK70
, §5]
). This definition is not the correct one for infinite-dimensional CW complexes (see
[
AS04
]
), but it will be sufficient for our purposes.

The rank of an
\mathcal{A}
-bundle
\mathcal{E}
as a
\mathcal{C}_{T}
-module is of the form
nr
for some locally constant function
r:T\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
(reduce to the case
\mathcal{A}=M_{n}(\mathcal{C}_{T})
where it follows from Morita theory). We call
r
the
\mathcal{A}
-
rank
of
\mathcal{E}
(note that
\mathcal{A}
has
\mathcal{A}
-rank
n
). One defines the
\mathcal{A}
-
rank
of an element of
K(T,\mathcal{A})
by additivity: it is a locally constant function
r:T\to\mathbb{Z}
. If
r
is constant (e.g. if
T
is connected), we view it as an integer. Let
K(T,\mathcal{A})_{r}\subset K(T,\mathcal{A})
be the subgroup of elements of
\mathcal{A}
-rank
r
.

For
0\leq r\leq nN
, we let
\mathrm{Gr}_{(T,\mathcal{A})}(r,N)\to T
be the locally trivial fibration whose
fiber over
t\in T
is the space of
\mathcal{A}_{t}
-stable subspaces of
\mathcal{A}_{t}
-rank
r
of
\mathcal{A}_{t}^{\oplus N}
.
Since
\mathcal{A}
is locally isomorphic to
M_{n}(\mathcal{C}_{T})
, it follows from Morita theory that the fibers of
\mathrm{Gr}_{(T,\mathcal{A})}(r,N)\to T
are homeomorphic to the Grassmannian
\mathrm{Gr}(r,nN)
of
r
-dimensional subspaces of
\mathbb{C}^{nN}
.

Define
\mathrm{BU}_{(T,\mathcal{A})}(r):=\varinjlim_{N}\mathrm{Gr}_{(T,\mathcal{A})}(r,N)
where the colimit is induced by the inclusions
\mathcal{A}^{\oplus N}\subset\mathcal{A}^{\oplus N+1}
. Let
\mathcal{U}_{(T,\mathcal{A})}(r)
be the universal
\mathcal{A}
-bundle of
\mathcal{A}
-rank
r
on
\mathrm{BU}_{(T,\mathcal{A})}(r)
. Set
{\mathrm{BU}_{(T,\mathcal{A}),r}:=\varinjlim_{i}\mathrm{BU}_{(T,\mathcal{A})}(r+ni)}
where the colimit is induced by
\mathcal{E}\mapsto\mathcal{E}\oplus\mathcal{A}
. The locally trivial fibrations
{\mathrm{BU}_{(T,\mathcal{A})}(r)\to T}
and
{\mathrm{BU}_{(T,\mathcal{A}),r}\to T}
respectively have fibers homeomorphic to the classifying spaces
\mathrm{BU}(r)
and
\mathrm{BU}
.

The following lemma is an analogue in our context of the assertion that
\mathrm{BU}\times\mathbb{Z}
represents usual topological K-theory (this corresponds to the case
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{C}_{T}
). As the proof of Lemma
6.5
is entirely similar to the proof of this classical assertion (for which see e.g.
[
May99
, Chap. 24, §1]
), we omit it.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
T
be a connected compact Hausdorff topological space and let
\mathcal{A}
be topological Azumaya algebra of degree
n
on
T
. Fix
r\in\mathbb{Z}
. There is a bijection

(6.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\textrm{homotopy classes of sections}\\
\hskip 20.00003pt\textrm{of \,}\mathrm{BU}_{(T,\mathcal{A}),r}\to T\end{array}\right\}\mathrel{{\mathop{{\mathrel{\to}^{\,\sim\,}}}\limits}}K(T,\mathcal{A})_{r}

associating with
\sigma:T\to\mathrm{BU}_{(T,\mathcal{A})}(r+ni)
the element
[\sigma^{*}(\mathcal{U}_{(T,\mathcal{A})}(r+ni))]-i[\mathcal{A}]
.

6.3.
Index over
\mathcal{O}(S)
versus index over
\mathcal{M}(S)

The following proposition is key to the proof of Theorem
6.7
.

Proposition 6.6
.

Let
S
be a connected Stein manifold. Let
\mathcal{A}
be a holomorphic Azumaya algebra of degree
n
on
S
with class
\alpha\in\mathrm{Br}_{\mathrm{an}}(S)
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a coherent right
\mathcal{A}
-module on
S
that is generically of rank
rn
(as an
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-module). Then
\mathrm{ind}(\alpha)\mid r
.

Proof.

Let
d
be the complex dimension of
S
. Fix
s\in S
.
Let
\rho:S\to\mathbb{R}
be a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
strictly plurisubharmonic exhaustion function on
S
(see
[
Hör90
, Theorem 5.1.6]
). Use Sard’s theorem to choose a strictly increasing sequence
(c_{i})_{i\geq 1}
of regular values of
\rho
tending to infinity. Set
S_{i}:=\{x\in S\mid\rho(x)\leq c_{i}\}
. For all
c\in\mathbb{R}
, the complex manifold
\{x\in S\mid\rho(x)<c\}
is Stein (apply
[
Hör90
, Theorem 5.2.10]
to
x\mapsto\frac{1}{c-\rho(x)}
). It follows that the compact subset
S_{i}\subset S
is Stein in the sense that it admits a basis of Stein open neighborhoods. Using
[
Mun66
, Theorem 10.6]
, one can endow
S
with a structure of CW complex such that the
S_{i}
are finite subcomplexes. Replacing
S_{i}
with its connected component containing
s
, we may assume that
S_{i}
is connected.

Fix
i\geq 1
. As
S_{i}
is a Stein compact set and
\mathcal{F}
is coherent, there is a resolution

(6.2)

0\to\mathcal{E}^{i}_{d}\to\dots\to\mathcal{E}^{i}_{0}\to\mathcal{F}|_{U_{i}}\to 0

of
\mathcal{F}|_{U_{i}}
, on a Stein open neighborhood
U_{i}
of
S_{i}
, by
\mathcal{A}|_{U_{i}}
-bundles (right
\mathcal{A}|_{U_{i}}
-modules that are locally free of finite rank as
\mathcal{O}_{U_{i}}
-modules); one can also choose
\mathcal{E}^{i}_{0},\dots,\mathcal{E}^{i}_{d-1}
to be free right
\mathcal{A}|_{U_{i}}
-modules. Using that
\mathcal{F}
is generated (as an
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-module hence also as an
\mathcal{A}
-module) by finitely many global sections over some
U_{i}
, one constructs the morphism
{\mathcal{E}^{i}_{0}\to\mathcal{F}|_{U_{i}}}
. Applying the same argument to the kernel of
{\mathcal{E}^{i}_{0}\to\mathcal{F}|_{U_{i}}}
and iterating (possibly shrinking
U_{i}
), one constructs
\mathcal{E}^{i}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{E}^{i}_{d-1}
. The
\mathcal{A}|_{U_{i}}
-module
\mathcal{E}^{i}_{d}
chosen so (
6.2
) is exact is a locally free
\mathcal{O}_{U_{i}}
-module by Hilbert’s syzygy theorem. Shrinking the
U_{i}
, one can moreover ensure that
{U_{i}\subset U_{i+1}}
.

As
U_{i}
is Stein, one can lift the identity of
\mathcal{F}|_{U_{i}}
to a morphism
{f^{i}:\mathcal{E}^{i+1}_{\bullet}|_{U_{i}}\to\mathcal{E}^{i}_{\bullet}}
of complexes of
\mathcal{A}|_{U_{i}}
-bundles (choose first
f_{0}^{i}
, then
f^{i}_{1}
, etc.). The mapping cone
C(f^{i})_{\bullet}
of
f^{i}
is then an exact complex of
\mathcal{A}|_{U_{i}}
-bundles with
{C(f^{i})_{j}=\mathcal{E}^{i+1}_{j-1}|_{U_{i}}\oplus\mathcal{E}^{i}_{j}}
.

Consider the topological Azumaya algebra
\mathcal{B}:=\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{S}}\mathcal{C}_{S}
on
S
and let
\mathcal{B}_{i}
be its restriction to
S_{i}
. The element
\kappa_{i}:=\sum_{j=0}^{d}(-1)^{j}[(\mathcal{E}^{i}_{j}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{U_{i}}}\mathcal{C}_{U_{i}})|_{S_{i}}]
of
K(S_{i},\mathcal{B}_{i})
(see §
6.2
) has
\mathcal{B}_{i}
-rank
r
. The complex
(C(f^{i})_{\bullet}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{U_{i}}}\mathcal{C}_{U_{i}})|_{S_{i}}
shows that
\kappa_{i+1}|_{S_{i}}=\kappa_{i}
.

The class
\kappa_{i}
corresponds to a continuous section
\sigma_{i}:S_{i}\to\mathrm{BU}_{(S_{i},\mathcal{B}_{i}),r}
, well defined up to homotopy, of
\mathrm{BU}_{(S,\mathcal{B}),r}\to S
over
S_{i}
(see Lemma
6.5
). As
{\kappa_{i+1}|_{S_{i}}=\kappa_{i}}
, the section
\sigma_{i+1}|_{S_{i}}
is homotopic to
\sigma_{i}
. For all
i\geq 1
, one can modify
\sigma_{i+1}
to ensure that
\sigma_{i+1}|_{S_{i}}=\sigma_{i}
(to see it, note that
\sigma_{i+1}
and the homotopy yield a section of
\mathrm{BU}_{(S,\mathcal{B})}(r)\times[0,1]\to S\times[0,1]
over
S_{i+1}\times\{0\}\cup S_{i}\times[0,1]
, which one can extend to
S_{i+1}\times[0,1]
because the inclusion
S_{i+1}\times\{0\}\cup S_{i}\times[0,1]\subset S_{i+1}\times[0,1]
is a homotopy equivalence).
Doing so for
i=1
, then
i=2
, etc., ensures that the
\sigma_{i}
are compatible. This yields a section
\sigma:S\to\mathrm{BU}_{(S,\mathcal{B}),r}
of
\mathrm{BU}_{(S,\mathcal{B}),r}\to S
over
S
.

Choose
i\geq 0
such that
r+ni\geq d
. Then the homotopy fiber
F
of the inclusion
\mathrm{BU}(r+ni)\to\mathrm{BU}
has the property that
\pi_{k}(F)=0
for
0<k\leq 2d
(this follows from the Hurewicz theorem in the form
[
Hat02
, Theorem 4.27]
). Consider the problem of finding a section
\tau
of
\mathrm{BU}_{(S,\mathcal{B})}(r+ni)\to S
that is homotopic to
\sigma
as a section of
\mathrm{BU}_{(S,\mathcal{B}),r}\to S
. Obstruction theory provides successive obstructions to this problem, that live in
H^{k+1}(S,\underline{\pi}_{k}(F))
, where
\underline{\pi}_{k}(F)
is a local system on
S
with fiber
\pi_{k}(F)
(see e.g.
[
Ste51
, Theorem 34.2]
). These groups vanish if
k\leq 2d
because
\pi_{k}(F)=0
and if
k\geq 2d
because
S
is a
2d
-dimensional
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
manifold. It follows that such a section
\tau:S\to\mathrm{BU}_{(S,\mathcal{B})}(r+ni)
exists (see
[
Ste51
, Corollary 34.4]
).

The
\mathcal{B}
-bundle
\tau^{*}(\mathcal{U}_{(S,\mathcal{B})}(r+ni))
on
S
has
\mathcal{B}
-rank
r+ni
(in the notation of §
6.2
). Let
\beta\in\mathrm{Br}_{\mathrm{top}}(S)
be the class of
\mathcal{B}
. By
[
Căl00
, Theorem 1.3.7]
, there exists a
\beta
-twisted sheaf of rank
r+ni
on
S
, so that
\mathrm{ind}(\beta)\mid r+ni
. As clearly
\mathrm{ind}(\beta)\mid n
, it follows that
\mathrm{ind}(\beta)\mid r
. The proof of Theorem
6.1
now shows that
\mathrm{ind}(\alpha)\mid r
.
∎

Theorem 6.7
.

Let
S
be a connected Stein manifold. Fix
\alpha\in\mathrm{Br}(\mathcal{O}(S))
and let
\alpha_{\mathcal{M}(S)}
be its image in
\mathrm{Br}(\mathcal{M}(S))
. Then
\mathrm{ind}(\alpha)=\mathrm{ind}(\alpha_{\mathcal{M}(S)})
.

Proof.

It is obvious that
\mathrm{ind}(\alpha_{\mathcal{M}(S)})\mid\mathrm{ind}(\alpha)
and we prove the converse divisibility.

Let
\pi:\mathfrak{X}\to\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))
be a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe representing
\alpha
(see §
5.1
). Let
\mathcal{G}
be a locally free
\alpha_{\mathcal{M}(S)}
-twisted sheaf of rank
r
. Extend
\mathcal{G}
to a quasi-coherent sheaf of finite presentation
\mathcal{H}
on
\mathfrak{X}
(combine
[
Ryd15
, Lemma 4.3 (C2)
\Rightarrow
(E2)]
and
[
Ryd23
, Corollary B]
). Replacing
\mathcal{H}
with one summand of the decomposition
[
Lie07
, Proposition 2.2.1.6]
, we may ensure that
\mathcal{H}
is an
\alpha
-twisted sheaf on
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a degree
n
Azumaya algebra on
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))
representing
\alpha
. Using
[
Căl00
, Theorem 1.3.7]
,
one sees that there exists a finitely presented quasi-coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))
with a structure of right
\mathcal{A}
-module such that
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{M}(S)}
has rank
rn
(as a module over
\mathcal{M}(S)
).
Let
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{an}}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{an}}
be the analytifications of
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{F}
in the sense of §
2.2
, so
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{an}}
is a holomorphic Azumaya algebra of degree
n
on

and
\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{an}}
is a coherent right
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{an}}
-module on

that is generically of rank

(as an
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-module). It follows from Proposition

that
\mathrm{ind}(\alpha^{\mathrm{an}})\mid r
, hence, by Theorem

, that
{\mathrm{ind}(\alpha)\mid r}
. This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

6.4.
Period versus index

Theorem 6.8
.

There exist a connected Stein manifold
S
and a class
\alpha\in\mathrm{Br}(\mathcal{M}(S))
in the image of

such that
\mathrm{ind}(\alpha)\neq\mathrm{per}(\alpha)
.

Let
T
be a connected finite CW complex carrying a topological Brauer class whose period and index differ (say, period
2
and index
8
as in
[

, Theorem B]
). Use Theorem
3.6
to find a connected Stein manifold

with the homotopy type of

, so there exists
\gamma\in\mathrm{Br}_{\mathrm{top}}(S)[2]
with
\mathrm{ind}(\gamma)=8
. Let
\beta\in\mathrm{Br}(\mathcal{O}(S))[2]
be such that

(see Theorem

). Let

∎

Optimality-Based Control Space Reduction for Infinite-Dimensional Control Spaces
Michael Kartmann, Stefan Volkwein

Abstract
We consider linear model reduction in both the control and state variables for unconstrained linear-quadratic optimal control problems subject to time-varying parabolic PDEs. The first-order optimality condition for a state-space reduced model naturally leads to a reduced structure of the optimal control. Thus, we consider a control- and state-reduced problem that admits the same minimizer as the solely state-reduced problem. Lower and upper a posteriori error bounds for the optimal control and a representation for the error in the optimal function value are provided. These bounds are used in an adaptive algorithm to solve the control problem. We prove its convergence and numerically demonstrate the advantage of combined control and state space reduction.

Introduction

In this contribution, we are interested in a certified control- and state-reduced space model reduction for the solution of linear-quadratic optimal control problems (
OCPs
) of the form

\displaystyle\min J(y,u)\coloneqq\int^{T}_{0}\tfrac{1}{2}\,\left|C(t)y(t)-y_{d}(t)\right|_{H}^{2}+\tfrac{\beta}{2}\left|u(t)\right|_{U}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t

(1a)

s.t.
(y,u)\in W(0,T)\times L^{2}(0,T;U)
solving

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}y(t)+A(t)y(t)=Bu(t)\in V^{\prime}&\text{in }(0,T),\\
y(0)=y_{0}&\text{in }H,\end{cases}

(1c)

where ‘s.t.’ stands for ‘subject to’. In this introduction, we focus on formal calculations to present key ideas and refer to Sections
1.1
and
2
for a concise setting and the definition of all quantities. It is well-known that the optimal control
\bar{u}
satisfies the optimality condition (cf., e.g.,
Hinze et al. 2009
)

\displaystyle\bar{u}(t)=-\tfrac{1}{\beta}B^{\prime}\bar{p}(t)\quad\text{in }(0,T),

for the adjoint variable
\bar{p}
according to the cost in (
1a
). We assume that the control space
U
and the state space
V
are infinite-dimensional, making an approximation scheme for the
OCP
necessary. If an approximation of the partial differential equation (
PDE
) in (
1c
) is introduced for a subspace
V_{r}\subset V
, this leads to an approximation of the adjoint state, and the corresponding optimality condition becomes

\bar{u}^{r}(t)=-\tfrac{1}{\beta}B^{\prime}\bar{p}^{r}(t)\in U_{r}\quad\text{in }(0,T).

(2)

Thus, the approximation space of the control, which we call
U_{r}
, is inherited from the approximation of the optimal adjoint through the optimality condition. This technique of constructing approximation spaces for the (optimal) control, called
variational discretization
, was first introduced in
Hinze 2005
in the context of finite element (
FE
) discretizations for control-constrained linear quadratic
OCPs
. It was shown that variational discretization for the control leads to faster convergence rates for diminishing mesh sizes, even though the evaluation of the control leads to a small increase in computational effort. In an adaptive model-order reduction context (
MOR
), a similar condition to (
2
) was exploited in
Kartmann et al. 2024
;
Kartmann et al. 2025
to reduce the dimension of the parameter space to efficiently construct and certify reduced-order models (
ROMs
) that are used to solve inverse problems with a bilinear coupling between parameter and state.

In this paper, we focus on variational discretization in the setting of adaptive
MOR
by combining ideas from
Hinze 2005
and
Kartmann et al. 2024
. In particular, we show that the optimality condition (
2
) for a state space reduced
OCP
already induces a reduction for the control space naturally, in the sense that both the state-reduced and the
control- and state-reduced

OCP
admit the same optimizer. On the one hand, this leads to an advantage for numerical computations, due to the reduced dimension of the control space, while on the other hand, the equivalence to the solely state-reduced
OCP
improves error estimates, and simplifies convergence results, since no error induced by the approximation of the control spaces needs to be considered. This latter effect was also observed in
Ali and Hinze 2020
for offline-online reduced basis approximations for parametrized elliptic
OCPs
.

Instead of an offline-online type model reduction of the control and state space in a parametrized setting (
Kärcher et al. 2018a
;
Kärcher et al. 2018b
;
Bader et al. 2016
), we focus on an online adaptive setting for a single solve of the
OCP
with fixed parameters. Since for fixed parameters an offline construction of the
ROM
can be more costly than solving the full-order model (
FOM
)
OCP
itself, the question of how to choose the approximation spaces arises, and thus (if no a priori information is given), the reduced approximation spaces
U_{r}
and
V_{r}
are built adaptively available on current optimizer data (see, e.g.,
Qian et al. 2017
;
Keil et al. 2021
;
Kartmann et al. 2025
for
PDE
-constrained parameter optimization). To construct the reduced state space, we consider proper orthogonal decomposition (
POD
) (see, e.g.,
Gubisch and Volkwein 2017
, and references therein). In this context, similar adaptive optimization strategies were considered in
Kone et al. 2017
;
Afanasiev and Hinze 2001
, where the
POD
model is updated based on snapshots stemming from the last suboptimal control. Another idea to construct both a
POD
approximation for the state space and the optimal control is optimality system
POD
(
Kunisch and Volkwein 2008
;
Volkwein 2011
), where both the
POD
problem and the optimal control problem are formulated into a combined problem, that is solved until an
a posteriori
error estimator certifies a sufficient quality of the
POD
optimizer.

A posteriori
error estimators are a widely used tool to verify the quality of the
ROM
approximation. In the parametrized
OCP
setting, we refer, e.g., to
Kärcher et al. 2018a
;
Bader et al. 2016
;
Ali and Hinze 2020
;
Kärcher et al. 2018b
and to
Tröltzsch and Volkwein 2009
;
Kartmann et al. 2024
in adaptive settings. Only in
Ali and Hinze 2020
, a lower
a posteriori
bound for the approximation error was given, which can be used to verify the sharpness of the upper bound. Further, the estimation technique used in
Tröltzsch and Volkwein 2009
does not use the optimality condition of the reduced model and thus can be applied to any arbitrary control in the control space. This is important since, usually, the solution of
OCPs
is found by iterative methods that satisfy the optimality condition up to a tolerance, which might conflict with the assumptions needed to make the
a posteriori
estimate rigorous. We present both lower and upper
a posteriori
error bounds that can be used to quantify the distance between the
FOM
optimal control and an arbitrary element in the control space and thus overcome the aforementioned conflict for
OCPs
without (control) constraints.

Let us mention that other techniques for control (or action) space reduction can be found in, e.g.,
Delavari et al. 2025
;
Chun et al. 2024
;
Sadati et al. 2018
and the references therein.

Next, we summarize our contribution here:

•

Following the concept of variational discretization
Hinze 2005
, we show that a Galerkin reduction for the state space directly leads to a reduced structure of the optimal control. We prove that the control- and state-reduced
OCP
is equivalent to the state-reduced
OCP
(cf. Lemma
2
) and convergence towards the solution of the
FOM
-
OCP
as the reduced dimension tends to
\infty
(cf. Lemma
11
).

•

We provide both lower and upper
a posteriori
bounds for the error in the optimal control with respect to an arbitrary control (cf. Corollary
5
).
Further, we provide an error representation for the error in the optimal function value, in which there is no contribution stemming from the reduction of the control space (cf. Theorem
7
).

•

Based on these error estimates, we introduce an adaptive algorithm (Algorithm
1
) to approximate the
FOM
optimal control using
POD
model reduction for the state and control space. Using interpolation and convergence properties of the
ROM
, we prove convergence of this algorithm in separable Hilbert spaces (Theorem
13
).

•

We verify our theoretical findings numerically and demonstrate the speed-up stemming from a combined control and state space reduction in contrast to solely state space reduction. The code for the numerical experiments is provided in
Kartmann and Volkwein 2025
.

1.1
Setting and notation

Throughout the paper, let
U,V,H
be real, separable Hilbert spaces with
V\subset H
compactly and densely embedded. The space
\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
is the Hilbert space of all linear bounded operators between the Hilbert spaces
X
and
Y
, and
\mathcal{J}_{X}:X\to X^{\prime}
is the Riesz isomorphism. The identity on a Hilbert space
X
is denoted by
I
. For
T\in\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
we denote its induced norm by
\left|T\right|\coloneqq\sup\{|Tx|_{Y}:|x|_{X}=1\}
. For
T>0
the state space is denoted by
W(0,T)\coloneqq H^{1}(0,T;V^{\prime})\cap L^{2}(0,T;V)
, where
V^{\prime}
denotes the topological dual space of
V
. The norm and the scalar product are denoted by
|x|_{X}
and
\langle x,\tilde{x}\rangle_{X}
, respectively, for
x,\tilde{x}\in X
. The dual pairing is denoted by
\langle x^{\prime},x\rangle_{X^{\prime},X}
or
x^{\prime}(x)
for
x^{\prime}\in X^{\prime}
. Given a closed set
K\subset X
, we define the orthogonal projection
\Pi_{K}^{X}:X\to K
,
v\mapsto\Pi_{K}^{X}v
, as the unique solution to

\displaystyle{\langle v-\Pi_{K}^{X}v,w\rangle}_{X}=0\quad\text{for all }w\in K.

For an operator
B\in\mathcal{L}(U,V^{\prime})
we define the dual operator
B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}(V,U^{\prime})
by

\displaystyle{\langle B^{\prime}v,u\rangle}_{U^{\prime},U}={\langle Bu,v\rangle}_{V^{\prime},V}\quad\text{for all }u\in U\text{ and }v\in V.

Reduced quantities are marked by the symbol
r
, e.g.,
U_{r}\subset U
,
V_{r}\subset V
denote reduced spaces and
y^{r}\in W^{r}(0,T)
a reduced state in the reduced state space
W^{r}(0,T)\coloneqq H^{1}(0,T;V_{r}^{\prime})\cap L^{2}(0,T;V_{r})
.

2
Reduced-order models (
ROMs
) with control and state space reduction

In this section, we introduce the
FOM
and the control and state
ROM
.

2.1
Full-order model (
FOM
)

We consider time-varying distributed optimal control problems of the form

\displaystyle\min J(y,u)\coloneqq\int^{T}_{0}\tfrac{1}{2}\,\left|C(t)y(t)-y_{d}(t)\right|_{H}^{2}+\tfrac{\beta}{2}\left|u(t)\right|_{U}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t

(3a)

s.t.
(y,u)\in W(0,T)\times L^{2}(0,T;U)
solving

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}y(t)+A(t)y(t)=Bu(t)\in V^{\prime}&\text{in }(0,T),\\
y(0)=y_{0}&\text{in }H.\end{cases}

(3b)

We make the following assumptions throughout this paper:

(A1)
:
A\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{L}(V,V^{\prime}))
to be coercive uniformly in
t
in the sense that there exists a constant
\eta_{V}>0
with

{\langle A(t)v,v\rangle}_{V^{\prime},V}\geq\eta_{V}\left|v\right|_{V}^{2}\quad\text{for all }t\in(0,T)\text{ and }v\in V;

(4)

(A2)
:
C\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{L}(H,H))
,
B\in\mathcal{L}(U,V^{\prime})
,
\beta>0
,
y_{d}\in L^{2}(0,T;H)
, and
y_{0}\in H
.

Under these assumptions, (
3b
) and therefore (
3
) are well-posed. To be precise, for every control
u\in L^{2}(0,T;U)
there exists a unique state
y=y(u)\in W(0,T)\subset L^{2}(0,T;V)
(cf., e.g.,
Evans 2010
). Therefore, the reduced cost function

\displaystyle\hat{J}(u)\coloneqq J(y(u),u)\quad\text{for }u\in L^{2}(0,T;U)

is well-defined and (
3
) is equivalent to the reduced problem

\displaystyle\min\hat{J}(u)\quad\text{s.t.}\quad u\in L^{2}(0,T;U).

(5)

We call (
5
) the
FOM
-
OCP
. Moreover, the reduced problem is strictly convex and admits a unique minimizer
\bar{u}\in L^{2}(0,T;U)
(cf., e.g.,
Hinze et al. 2009
). To formulate the corresponding optimality condition, we introduce the
adjoint equation
for
y\in L^{2}(0,T;H)
as

\begin{cases}-\partial_{t}{p}(t)+A^{\prime}(t)p(t)=C^{\prime}(t)(C(t))y(t)-y_{d}(t))\in V^{\prime}&\text{in }(0,T),\\
p(T)=0&\text{in }H.\end{cases}

(6)

We infer from
(A1)
and
(A2)
that there exists a unique solution
p=p(y)\in W(0,T)
for any
y\in L^{2}(0,T;V)
. Therefore, the minimizer

\bar{u}
is characterized by the first-order sufficient optimality condition

\nabla\hat{J}(\bar{u})=\mathcal{J}_{U}^{-1}B^{\prime}\bar{p}+\beta\bar{u}=0,

(7)

where the optimal adjoint state
\bar{p}=p(\bar{y})\in W(0,T)
is given as the solution of (
6
) for the optimal state
\bar{y}=y(\bar{u})
, solving (
3b
) for
u=\bar{u}
.

Remark 1
.

For the construction of the reduced control space
U_{r}
below, the operator
B
in (
3b
) is chosen to be time-independent. However, as can be seen in (
11
) below, it can have an affine time-dependent structure, e.g.,
B(t)=b(t)B
for some
b\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R})
.
In this case, (
4
) can be replaced by the Garding inequality without loss of generality (w.l.o.g.).

2.2
Reduced-order model (
ROM
)

The difficulty in an efficient numerical treatment of (
3a
) arises from the infinite dimensionality (or high-dimensionality after discretization) of the control space
U
and the state space
V
.

A standard
MOR
approach to accelerate computations is to project the
PDE
(
3b
) onto a reduced (low-dimensional) subspace
V_{r}=\mathop{\rm span}\nolimits(v_{1},\ldots,v_{r})\subset V
for
r\in\mathbb{N}
. This leads to the
state-reduced

OCP

\displaystyle\min\hat{J}^{r}(u)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{T}\tfrac{1}{2}\left|C(t)y^{r}(t)-y_{d}(t)\right|_{H}^{2}+\tfrac{\beta}{2}\left|u(t)\right|_{U}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\quad\text{s.t.}\quad u\in L^{2}(0,T;U),

(8a)

where
y^{r}=y^{r}(u)\in W^{r}(0,T)
solves the reduced state equation

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}y^{r}(t)+A(t)y^{r}(t)=Bu(t)\in V_{r}^{\prime}&\text{in }(0,T),\\
y^{r}(0)=y_{0}^{r}&\text{in }H.\end{cases}

(8b)

Here,
y_{0}^{r}=\Pi_{V_{r}}^{H}y_{0}\in V_{r}
is the orthogonal projection onto
V_{r}
w.r.t. the inner product of
H
. The well-posedness of the reduced state equation and the state-reduced
OCP
are inherited from the well-posedness of the
FOM
. The solution to (
8b
) can be represented as
y^{r}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mathbf{y}^{r}_{i}v_{i}
with
\mathbf{y}\in H^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{r})
(cf.
(
Gubisch and Volkwein 2017
, Proposition 1,27)
). As before, the optimality condition for the reduced unique minimizer
\hat{u}^{r}\in L^{2}(0,T;U)
reads

\nabla\hat{J}^{r}(\hat{u}^{r})=\mathcal{J}_{U}^{-1}B^{\prime}\hat{p}^{r}+\beta\hat{u}^{r}=0,

(9)

for
\hat{p}^{r}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\hat{\mathbf{p}}^{r}_{i}v_{i}
with
\hat{\mathbf{p}}^{r}\in H^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{r})
solving

\begin{cases}-\partial_{t}\hat{p}^{r}(t)+A^{\prime}(t)\hat{p}^{r}(t)=C^{\prime}(t)(C(t)\hat{y}^{r}(t)-y_{d}(t))\in V_{r}^{\prime}&\text{in }(0,T),\\
\hat{p}^{r}(T)=0&\text{in }H,\end{cases}

(10)

for
\hat{y}^{r}
solving (
8b
) for
u=\hat{u}^{r}
. The optimality condition (
9
) implies

\hat{u}^{r}=-\frac{1}{\beta}\mathcal{J}_{U}^{-1}B^{\prime}\hat{p}^{r}=-\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\hat{\mathbf{p}}^{r}_{i}\mathcal{J}_{U}^{-1}B^{\prime}v_{i}.

(11)

By setting

\displaystyle u_{i}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{J}_{U}^{-1}B^{\prime}v_{i}

\displaystyle\hskip-85.35826pt\text{for }i=1,\ldots,r,

(12a)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{u}}^{r}_{i}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{\beta}\hat{\mathbf{p}}^{r}_{i}

\displaystyle\hskip-85.35826pt\text{for }i=1,\ldots,r,

(12b)

we can write the optimality condition (
9
) as

\hat{u}^{r}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\hat{\mathbf{u}}^{r}_{i}u_{i}.

(13)

This means that the optimal control of the state-reduced
OCP
already lies in a reduced control space
\hat{u}^{r}\in L^{2}(0,T;U_{r})
with
U_{r}=\mathop{\rm span}\nolimits(u_{1},\ldots,u_{r})
. Due to the operator
B^{\prime}
, the
u_{i}
’s are probably linearly dependent. In that case, we have to identify a basis for
U_{r}
by applying a Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization. To ease the presentation, we suppose (w.l.o.g.) that this is not the case, that is, we have
\dim U_{r}=r
holds.

Due to (
13
) we consider the
control- and state-reduced

OCP

\displaystyle\min\hat{J}^{r}(u^{r})\quad\text{s.t.}\quad u\in L^{2}(0,T;U_{r}),

(14a)

where
y^{r}=y^{r}(u)
solves the reduced state equation (
8b
).

Since
U_{r}\subset U
is a Hilbert space itself, (
14
) admits the unique minimizer
\bar{u}^{r}
by assumptions
(A1)
and
(A2)
. Since the state-reduced minimizer already satisfies
\hat{u}^{r}\in L^{2}(0,T;U_{r})
by (
13
), we obtain
\hat{u}^{r}=\bar{u}^{r}
by uniqueness of both minimizers.

Lemma 2
.

We have
\bar{u}^{r}=\hat{u}^{r}
. In particular, the associated optimal states and optimal adjoints are equal, that is,
\bar{y}^{r}\coloneqq\hat{y}^{r}
and
\bar{p}^{r}\coloneqq\hat{p}^{r}
.

Hence, in a numerical implementation, one can always restrict oneself to the control and state-reduced
OCP
(
14
).

Remark 3

(Optimality condition of the control- and state-reduced OCP)
.

We define the (by assumption) positive definite product matrix
\mathbf{M}_{U}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}
via

\displaystyle\big(\mathbf{M}_{U}\big)_{ij}\coloneqq{\langle u_{j},u_{i}\rangle}_{U}\quad\text{for }i,j=1,\ldots,r,

and the discrete control space
\mathbb{R}_{U}^{r}
, that is
\mathbb{R}^{r}
endowed with the product

\displaystyle{\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\rangle}_{\mathbb{R}_{U}^{r}}\coloneqq\mathbf{u}^{\top}\mathbf{M}_{U}\mathbf{v}\qquad\text{for }\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{r}.

To write the optimality condition of (
14
) in an unconstrained manner, we eliminate the constraint

, by defining the operator

\displaystyle B_{r}:\mathbb{R}_{U}^{r}\to V^{\prime},\quad\mathbf{u}\to B\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{r}u_{i}\mathbf{u}_{i}\bigg).

Then, (

) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\min\mathbf{\hat{J}}^{r}({\mathbf{u}})\coloneqq\int_{0}^{T}\tfrac{1}{2}\left|C(t)y^{r}(t)-y_{d}(t)\right|_{H}^{2}+\tfrac{\beta}{2}\left|\mathbf{u}(t)\right|_{\mathbb{R}^{r}_{U}}^{2}\mathrm{d}t~\text{ s.t. }\mathbf{u}\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}_{U}^{r}),

(15a)

where
y^{r}=y^{r}(\mathbf{u})
solves

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}y^{r}(t)+A(t)y^{r}(t)=B_{r}\mathbf{u}(t)\in V_{r}^{\prime}&\text{in }(0,T),\\
y^{r}(0)=y_{0}^{r}&\text{in }H\end{cases}

(15b)

with the following sufficient optimality condition for the unique minimizer
\bar{\mathbf{u}}^{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{r}

\displaystyle\nabla\mathbf{\hat{J}}^{r}(\bar{\mathbf{u}}^{r})=\bar{\mathbf{u}}^{r}+\mathbf{M}_{U}^{-1}B_{r}^{\prime}\hat{p}^{r}=0

with
\hat{p}^{r}
as in (

) and
\bar{\mathbf{u}}^{r}=\hat{\mathbf{u}}^{r}
for
\hat{\mathbf{u}}^{r}
as in (
12b
).

3
A posteriori error estimation

While
OCP
(
14
) can be advantageous for efficient numerical realization, the equivalent formulation in
OCP
(
8
) is primarily useful for theoretical purposes: error analysis and convergence results for the control- and state-reduced problem can be traced back to the solely state-reduced case.

3.1
Error estimator for the optimal control

First, we recall the well-known residual-based
a posteriori
error estimator for linear coercive operators.

Lemma 4
.

Let
\mathcal{U}
be a real Hilbert space,
d\in\mathcal{U}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{Q}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{U}^{\prime})
coercive with constant
\beta>0
, that is

\displaystyle{\langle\mathcal{Q}u,u\rangle}_{\mathcal{U}^{\prime},\mathcal{U}}\geq\beta\,{|u|}_{\mathcal{U}}^{2}\quad\text{for all }u\in\mathcal{U}.

Let
u\in\mathcal{U}
satisfy the variational problem

\mathcal{Q}u=d

(16)

and
\tilde{u}\in\mathcal{U}
be given. Then we have the error-residual equivalence

\tfrac{1}{\left|\mathcal{Q}\right|}\left|d-\mathcal{Q}\tilde{u}\right|_{\mathcal{U}^{\prime}}\leq\left|u-\tilde{u}\right|_{\mathcal{U}}\leq\tfrac{1}{\beta}\left|d-\mathcal{Q}\tilde{u}\right|_{\mathcal{U}^{\prime}}.

(17)

Proof.

A proof was given, e.g., in
Haasdonk 2017
, but we will repeat it here for the sake of completeness.
We have the error-residual relation

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(u-\tilde{u})=d-\mathcal{Q}\tilde{u}.

Testing this equation with the error
u-\tilde{u}\in\mathcal{U}
and using the coercivity of
\mathcal{Q}
results in

\displaystyle\beta\left|u-\tilde{u}\right|_{\mathcal{U}}^{2}\leq\langle\mathcal{Q}(u-\tilde{u}),u-\tilde{u}\rangle_{\mathcal{U}^{\prime},\mathcal{U}}=\langle d-\mathcal{Q}\tilde{u},u-\tilde{u}\rangle_{\mathcal{U}^{\prime},\mathcal{U}}\leq\left|d-\mathcal{Q}\tilde{u}\right|_{\mathcal{U}^{\prime}}\left|u-\tilde{u}\right|_{\mathcal{U}},

which implies the upper bound in (
17
). The lower bound follows from testing the error equation with the Riesz representative of the residual
\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{U}}^{-1}(d-\mathcal{Q}\tilde{u})\in\mathcal{U}
and using the continuity of
\mathcal{Q}
:

\displaystyle\left|d-\mathcal{Q}\tilde{u}\right|_{\mathcal{U}^{\prime}}^{2}=\langle\mathcal{Q}(u-\tilde{u}),\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{U}}^{-1}(d-\mathcal{Q}\tilde{u})\rangle_{\mathcal{U}^{\prime},\mathcal{U}}\leq\left|\mathcal{Q}\right|\left|u-\tilde{u}\right|_{\mathcal{U}}\left|d-\mathcal{Q}\tilde{u}\right|_{\mathcal{U}^{\prime}},

which implies the lower bound in (
17
).
∎

Next, we cast the
FOM
optimality condition (
7
) into the form (
16
) for
\mathcal{U}=L^{2}(0,T;U)
. Therefore, let
\hat{y}
be the solution to the
FOM
-
PDE
(
3b
) for
u=0
. Then we can write each solution of (
3b
) as
y(u)=\mathcal{S}u+\hat{y}
, where
\mathcal{S}\in\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(0,T;U),W(0,T))
is the linear solution operator for (
3b
) for
y_{0}=0
. Defining
\hat{y}_{d}\coloneqq y_{d}-C\hat{y}
, we see that (
5
) can be written as

\displaystyle\min\hat{J}(u)=\tfrac{1}{2}\left|C\mathcal{S}u-\hat{y}_{d}\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}^{2}+\tfrac{\beta}{2}\left|u\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;U)}^{2}\text{ s.t. }u\in L^{2}(0,T;U),

(18)

and hence (
7
) as

\displaystyle\nabla\hat{J}(\bar{u})=\beta\bar{u}+\mathcal{J}_{U}^{-1}\mathcal{S}^{\prime}C^{\prime}(C\mathcal{S}\bar{u}-\hat{y}_{d})=0.

Defining

\displaystyle\coloneqq\beta I+\mathcal{S}^{\prime}C^{\prime}C\mathcal{S},

(19)

\displaystyle d

(20)

Corollary 5

(Error estimator for the first-order optimality condition)
.

Let
\bar{u}\in L^{2}(0,T;U)
be the solution of (

\tilde{u}\in L^{2}(0,T;U)

\displaystyle\underaccent{\bar}{\Delta}(\tilde{u})\coloneqq

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{\beta}\left|\nabla\hat{J}(\tilde{u})\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;U)},\quad\bar{\Delta}(\tilde{u})\coloneqq\tfrac{1}{\beta+\left|\mathcal{C}\mathcal{S}\right|^{2}}\left|\nabla\hat{J}(\tilde{u})\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;U)},

\displaystyle e_{u}(\tilde{u})\coloneqq

\displaystyle\left|\bar{u}-\tilde{u}\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;U)},

we have the

\bar{\Delta}(\tilde{u})\leq e_{u}(\tilde{u})\leq\underaccent{\bar}{\Delta}(\tilde{u}).

Proof.

Note that

Remark 6

Note that the upper bound in (

We are particularly interested in the case

3.2
Optimal function value error representation

In this section, we provide an error representation for the error in the optimal function value based on

We introduce the space
\mathcal{X}\coloneqq W(0,T)\times L^{2}(0,T;U)\times W(0,T)
endowed with the standard product topology. In addition, the Lagrange function

L(x)=\tfrac{1}{2}\left|Cy-y_{d}\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}^{2}+\tfrac{\beta}{2}\left|u\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;U)}^{2}+{\langle Bu-Ay,p\rangle}_{L^{2}(0,T;V^{\prime}),L^{2}(0,T;V)}

for

the associated optimal state (that is,

solves (
3b

\bar{p}=p(\bar{y})
the associated optimal adjoint (that is,
\bar{p}
solves (

). We set
\bar{x}=(\bar{y},\bar{u},\bar{p})\in\mathcal{X}
. The

3b
) for

, (
7

) for

, can be compactly written as

L^{\prime}(\bar{x})=0\quad\text{in }\mathcal{X}^{\prime}.

Similarly, let
\hat{u}^{r}\in
be the optimal solution to (

and

Theorem 7

(Optimal value function error representation)
.

For
\bar{u}\in L^{2}(0,T;U)
and
\bar{u}^{r}\in L^{2}(0,T;U_{r})
, we have the following error representation

\hat{J}(\bar{u})-\hat{J}^{r}(\bar{u}^{r})=\tfrac{1}{2}\inf\big\{L^{\prime}_{y}(\bar{x}^{r})(\bar{y}-y^{r})+L^{\prime}_{p}(\bar{x}^{r})(\bar{p}-p^{r})\,\big|\,y^{r},p^{r}\in W^{r}(0,T)\big\},

(25)

where
L_{z}^{\prime}(\bar{x}^{r})
is the partial Fréchet derivative of

at
\bar{x}^{r}
for
z\in\{y,u,p\}
.

Proof.

Let
e=(e_{y},e_{u},e_{p})\coloneqq\bar{x}-\hat{x}^{r}\in\mathcal{X}
. By the definition of

,
\bar{x}
, and
\hat{x}^{r}
, Lemma

, and the fundamental theorem of calculus, we have

\displaystyle\mathsf{err}_{\hat{J}}\coloneqq\hat{J}(\bar{u})-\hat{J}(\bar{u}^{r})=\hat{J}(\bar{u})-\hat{J}(\hat{u}^{r})=L(\bar{x})-L(\hat{x}^{r})=\int_{0}^{1}L^{\prime}(\hat{x}^{r}+te)(e)\,\mathrm{d}t.

Adding and subtracting
\nicefrac{{L^{\prime}(\hat{x}^{r})(e)}}{{2}}
and using (
23
) tested with

, leads to

\displaystyle\mathsf{err}_{\hat{J}}=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}L^{\prime}(\hat{x}^{r}+te)(e)\,\mathrm{d}t\pm\frac{1}{2}L^{\prime}(\hat{x}^{r})(e)-\frac{1}{2}L^{\prime}(\bar{x})(e)=\frac{1}{2}L^{\prime}(\hat{x}^{r})(e),

since the integral and the terms

and

cancel, the latter are a trapezoidal approximation of the former and

\displaystyle L^{\prime}(\hat{x}^{r})(e)=L^{\prime}(\hat{x}^{r})(\bar{x}-\tilde{x})=L_{y}^{\prime}(\hat{x}^{r})(\bar{y}-\tilde{y})+L_{p}^{\prime}(\hat{x}^{r})(\bar{p}-\tilde{p})+L_{u}^{\prime}(\hat{x}^{r})(\bar{u}-\tilde{u}).

Since

solves the solely state-reduced optimality condition, we can choose

for arbitrary

are arbitrary, we obtain (

).
∎

4.1
Construction of the reduced spaces with
POD

Let
r\in\mathbb{N}
. To construct the space
V_{r}={\mathop{\rm span}\nolimits(v_{1},\ldots,v_{r})}
out of a countable data (or
snapshot
) set
S\subset L^{2}(0,T;V)
, we focus on
POD

\min\sum_{s\in S}\big|s-\Pi_{V_{r}}^{V}s\big|_{L^{2}(0,T;V)}^{2}\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\{v_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r}\subset V,~{\langle v_{i},v_{j}\rangle}_{V}=\delta_{ij}~(i,j=1,\ldots,r).

(26)

The solution to this problem can be found using the linear correlation operator
\mathcal{R}_{S}:V\to V

\mathcal{R}_{S}v\coloneqq\sum_{s\in S}\int_{0}^{T}{\langle s(t),v\rangle}_{V}\,s(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\quad\text{for }v\in V.

(27)

We have the following result.

Theorem 8
.

The operator
\mathcal{R}_{S}\in\mathcal{L}(V,V)
is non-negative, self-adjoint, and compact.
Define the
POD
space

\mathcal{W}_{S}\coloneqq\overline{\mathop{\rm range}\nolimits(\mathcal{R}_{S})}\subset V.

(28)

Let
\bar{r}_{S}\coloneqq\dim(\mathcal{W}_{S})\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
be the maximal
POD
rank.
There exist non-negative eigenvalues
(\lambda_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
and associated orthonormal eigenfunctions
(v_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{W}_{S}
of
\mathcal{R}_{S}
with

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{S}v_{i}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{i}v_{i},\quad\lambda_{1}\geq\ldots\geq\lambda_{\bar{r}_{S}}>\lambda_{\bar{r}_{S}+1}=\ldots=0,

(29)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{S}v

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}{\langle v_{i},v\rangle}_{V}\,v_{i},

(30)

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle=\ker(\mathcal{R}_{S})\oplus\overline{\mathop{\rm span}\nolimits(v_{1},\ldots,v_{\bar{r}_{S}})}=\ker(\mathcal{R}_{S})\oplus\mathcal{W}_{S}.

(31)

The first
r\in\{1,\ldots,\bar{r}_{S}\}
eigenfunctions
(v_{i})_{i=1}^{r}
solve (
26
), i.e.,
V_{r}=\overline{\mathop{\rm span}\nolimits\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{r}\}}
and we have
V_{\bar{r}_{S}}=\mathcal{W}_{S}
.

Proof.

This is a combination of
(
Gubisch and Volkwein 2017
, Lemma 1.13, Theorem 1.15)
and the spectral theorem for compact, self-adjoint operators.
∎

In Theorem
8
, we defined
V_{r}
as the closure of the span to include the case
\bar{r}_{S}=\infty
for theoretical considerations. Since we are in an adaptive setting, we are interested in how to refine a
POD
basis. There can be two ways to extend the
POD
basis. On the one hand, (if a finite
POD
rank
r
is chosen) one may increase
r\leq\bar{r}_{S}
. For the theoretical analysis presented here, we consider the choice
r=\bar{r}_{S}
. However, in practice, other choices for
r
are possible, and the algorithm below can be adapted (see Remark
14
). On the other hand, one may update or enlarge the snapshots set
S
. We collect some properties of the operator
\mathcal{R}_{S}
in view of enlarging the snapshot set.

Lemma 9
.

Let
S\subset S_{+}\subset L^{2}(0,T;V)
be two snapshot sets. It holds

a)

\ker(\mathcal{R}_{S_{+}})\subset\ker(\mathcal{R}_{S})
;

b)

\overline{\mathop{\rm range}\nolimits(\mathcal{R}_{S})}\subset\overline{\mathop{\rm range}\nolimits(\mathcal{R}_{S_{+}})}
, i.e.,
\mathcal{W}_{S}\subset\mathcal{W}_{S_{+}}
;

Proof.

a)

Let
v\in\ker(\mathcal{R}_{S_{+}})
. Then by (
27
),
0=\mathcal{R}_{S_{+}}v=\mathcal{R}_{S}v+\mathcal{R}_{S_{+}\setminus S}v
and hence

0=\langle\mathcal{R}_{S}v,v\rangle_{V}+\langle\mathcal{R}_{S_{+}\setminus S}v,v\rangle_{V}

Since both
\mathcal{R}_{S}
and
\mathcal{R}_{S_{+}\setminus S}
are non-negative operators according to Theorem
8
, it holds
\mathcal{R}_{S}v=0
and thus
v\in\ker(\mathcal{R}_{S})
.

b)

Part a) is equivalent to
\ker(\mathcal{R}_{S})^{\perp}\subset\ker(\mathcal{R}_{S_{+}})^{\perp}
and it holds
\ker(\mathcal{R}_{S})^{\perp}=\overline{\mathop{\rm range}\nolimits(\mathcal{R}_{S})}
(
\ker(\mathcal{R}_{S_{+}})^{\perp}=\overline{\mathop{\rm range}\nolimits(\mathcal{R}_{S_{+}})}
), since
\mathcal{R}_{S}
(
\mathcal{R}_{S_{+}}
) is self-adjoint by Theorem
8
.

∎

The following lemma provides a criterion to determine if a bigger snapshot set contains new information to construct a
POD
basis of a bigger maximal rank.

Lemma 10
.

Let
S\subset S_{+}\subset L^{2}(0,T;V)
be two snapshot sets and
\bar{r}_{S}=\dim(\mathcal{W}_{S})\in\mathbb{N}
. Then,
\bar{r}_{S}<\bar{r}_{S_{+}}
, if and only if there exists
\tilde{s}\in S_{+}\setminus S
with

\left|(I-\Pi_{\mathcal{W}_{S}}^{V})\tilde{s}\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;V)}>0.

(32)

Proof.

On the one hand, let
|(I-\Pi_{\mathcal{W}_{S}}^{V})s|_{L^{2}(0,T;V)}=0
for all
s\in S_{+}\setminus S
. This implies
s(t)\in\mathcal{W}_{S}
for almost all
t\in(0,T)
. Then also

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{S_{+}}v=\mathcal{R}_{S}v+\sum_{s\in S_{+}\setminus S}\int_{0}^{T}\langle s(t),v\rangle_{V}s(t)\ \mathrm{d}t\in\mathcal{W}_{S},

since the linear subspace
\mathcal{W}_{S}
is closed by definition in (
28
). Hence, we have
\mathop{\rm range}\nolimits(\mathcal{R}_{S_{+}})\subset\mathcal{W}_{S}
. Taking the closure leads together with Lemma
9
-b) to

\displaystyle\overline{\mathop{\rm range}\nolimits(\mathcal{R}_{S})}\subset\overline{\mathop{\rm range}\nolimits(\mathcal{R}_{S_{+}})}\subset\mathcal{W}_{S}\coloneqq\overline{\mathop{\rm range}\nolimits(\mathcal{R}_{S})}

Hence,
\overline{\mathop{\rm range}\nolimits(\mathcal{R}_{S})}=\overline{\mathop{\rm range}\nolimits(\mathcal{R}_{S_{+}})}
. This results in
\bar{r}_{S}=\bar{r}_{S_{+}}
by definiton of the maximal rank in Theorem
8
.

On the other hand, assume the existence of
\tilde{s}\in S_{+}\setminus S
with (
32
). W.l.o.g., we assume that
\tilde{s}\in S_{+}\setminus S
is unique with this property. For
v\in\ker(\mathcal{R}_{S})
, it holds

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{S_{+}}v=\mathcal{R}_{S}v+\sum_{s\in S_{+}\setminus S}\int_{0}^{T}{\langle s(t),v\rangle}_{V}\,s(t)\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}{\langle\tilde{s}(t),v\rangle}_{V}\,\tilde{s}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t.

Since
\mathcal{R}_{S}
is self-adjoint, it holds
v\in\ker(\mathcal{R}_{S})=\mathop{\rm range}\nolimits(\mathcal{R}_{S})^{\perp}=\overline{\mathop{\rm range}\nolimits(\mathcal{R}_{S})}^{\perp}=\mathcal{W}_{S}^{\perp}
.Then, it follows for
\tilde{s}(t)=\Pi_{\mathcal{W}_{S}}^{V}\tilde{s}(t)+(I-\Pi_{\mathcal{W}_{S}}^{V})\tilde{s}(t)
for almost all
t\in(0,T)

{\langle\mathcal{R}_{S_{+}}v,v\rangle}_{V}=\int_{0}^{T}\big|{\langle(I-\Pi_{\mathcal{W}_{S}}^{V})\tilde{s}(t),v\rangle}_{V}\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t.

(33)

Note that
\ker(\mathcal{R}_{S})
is not trivial, because otherwise we would have by (
31
), that
\mathcal{W}_{S}=V
, which contradicts (
32
).
Hence, we can additionally choose
v\neq 0
and (
33
) implies
\langle\mathcal{R}_{S_{+}}v,v\rangle_{V}>0
, by the assumption (
32
) on
\tilde{s}
. Hence,
\mathcal{R}_{S_{+}}v\neq 0
and therefore
v\notin\ker(\mathcal{R}_{S_{+}})
.
Together with the fact that
\ker(\mathcal{R}_{S_{+}})\subset\ker(\mathcal{R}_{S})
by Lemma
9
-a), we get
\ker(\mathcal{R}_{S_{+}})\subsetneq\ker(\mathcal{R}_{S})
. Hence, (with a similar argument as in the proof of Lemma
9
-b)
\mathop{\rm range}\nolimits(\mathcal{R}_{S})\subsetneq\mathop{\rm range}\nolimits(\mathcal{R}_{S_{+}})
and therefore
\bar{r}_{S}<\bar{r}_{S_{+}}
.
∎

(u_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
with
u_{k}\to\bar{u}
as
k\to\infty
. For
\varepsilon>0
, Algorithm
1
terminates after a finite number of steps
k^{*}\in\mathbb{N}
with output
u_{k*}
satisfying the
FOM
optimality condition up to tolerance
\beta\varepsilon
, i.e.,

\left|\nabla\hat{J}(u_{{k^{*}}})\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;U)}\leq\beta\varepsilon.

(35)

and the estimate

0\leq\bar{\Delta}(u_{k^{*}})\leq\left|\bar{u}-u_{k^{*}}\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;U)}\leq\underaccent{\bar}{\Delta}(u_{k^{*}})\leq\varepsilon.

(36)

Proof.

First, let
\varepsilon=0
and suppose at step
k\in\mathbb{N}
, it holds
\underaccent{\bar}{\Delta}(u_{k})=\nicefrac{{1}}{{\beta}}\,|\nabla\hat{J}(u_{k})|_{L^{2}(0,T;U)}>0
in line
6
of Algorithm
1
. Necessarily we have
\bar{r}_{S_{k}}<\infty
. Then, by the lower bound in (
21
) it holds
\bar{u}\neq u_{k}=\bar{u}^{r_{k}}
. Therefore, we have by Lemma
12
, that
y(u_{k})\notin W^{r_{k}}(0,T)
or
p(y(u_{k}))\notin W^{r_{k}}(0,T)
. Hence, the snapshot set
S_{k+1}=S_{k}\cup\{y(u_{k}),p(y(u_{k}))\}
defined in line
8
of Algorithm
1
contains new information. If
\bar{r}_{S_{k+1}}=\infty
, we directly obtain
\bar{r}_{S_{k}}<\bar{r}_{S_{k+1}}
and also
u_{k+1}=\bar{u}
, i.e., convergence after a finite number of steps for
\varepsilon=0
. If
\bar{r}_{S_{k+1}}<\infty
, there exists
\tilde{s}\in\{y(u_{k}),p(y(u_{k}))\}
with (
32
), since else it would hold
y(u_{k})\in L^{2}(0,T;V_{r_{k}})
and
p(y(u_{k}))\in L^{2}(0,T;V_{r_{k}})
, due to
{V}_{r_{k}}=\mathcal{W}_{S_{k}}
for
{r_{k}}=\bar{r}_{S_{k}}
. Then by uniqueness of the
ROM
state equation (
8b
) in
L^{2}(0,T;V_{r_{k}})
one would obtain again
y(u_{k})\in W^{r_{k}}(0,T)
and
p(y(u_{r_{k}}))\in W^{r_{k}}(0,T)
, which we excluded above. Applying Lemma
10
for
S=S_{k}
and
S_{+}=S_{k+1}
implies
\bar{r}_{S_{k}}<\bar{r}_{S_{k+1}}
. By iterating this procedure, we construct an orthonormal basis of
V
and Lemma
11
ensures convergence, since
r_{k}\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
. Now
u_{k}\coloneqq\bar{u}^{r_{k}}\to\bar{u}
and (
21
) imply
\nabla\hat{J}(u_{k})\to 0
as
k\to\infty
. Hence, for
\varepsilon>0
, the termination criterion in line
6
of Algorithm
1
gets triggered after a finite number of steps and ensures (
35
) and (
36
) using (
21
).

Remark 14
.

Algorithm
1
can be modified or improved in various ways. For example, instead of choosing the maximal
POD
rank in line
8
of Algorithm
1
, one can make a choice based on an energy criterion (cf.
(
Gubisch and Volkwein 2017
, Theorem 1.8)
) and include another inner loop to adjust
r_{k}\leq\bar{r}_{S_{k}}
around line
10
of Algorithm
1
. Since the estimators in (
21
) are expensive to evaluate, it might not be of advantage to use them in the inner loop for adjusting
r
. Instead, one could combine the certification process for inner loop with the cheap-to-evaluate (i.e., independent of
FOM
calculations) error estimators presented, e.g., in
Kartmann et al. 2024
;
Kärcher et al. 2018a
;
Ali and Hinze 2020
. In addition, it can be of advantage to remove outdated snapshots from
S_{k+1}
. To update the
POD
basis in a numerical framework, i.e., to repeatedly compute a solution to (
26
), techniques as Hierarchical Approximate
POD
(
HAPOD
, cf.
Himpe et al. 2018
) can be useful. In view of
MOR
, i.e., to obtain a fast approximation of
\bar{u}
, Algorithm
1
should always be run with a tolerance
\varepsilon>0
. For parabolic differential operators with analytic coefficients, one can expect an exponential decay of the eigenvalues
\lambda_{i}
in Theorem
8
and thus Algorithm
1
to converge fast if
\varepsilon>0
. Last, in a numerical implementation, it is advised to use the previous iterate as a warm start for the next inner optimization in Algorithm
1
.

\displaystyle\min J(y,u)\coloneqq\int^{T}_{0}\tfrac{1}{2}\left|y(t)-y_{d}(t)\right|_{H}^{2}+\tfrac{\beta}{2}\left|u(t)\right|_{U}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t

subject to

and

solving

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}y(t)+\Delta y(t)+(2+\sin(4\pi t))y(t)=u(t)\in V^{\prime}&\text{in }(0,T),\\
y(0)=y_{0}&\text{in }H\end{cases}

(37b)

for
T=1
and different parameters of
\beta>0
. The target state and initial value are given for
t\in[0,T],{\bm{x}}\in\Omega
by

\displaystyle y_{d}(t,{\bm{x}})=\tfrac{1}{6}\,\sin(2\pi{\bm{x}}_{1}t)\sin(2\pi{\bm{x}}_{2}t)\exp(2{\bm{x}}_{1}),\quad y_{0}({\bm{x}})=0.

It can be shown that this problem satisfies assumption
(A1)
,
(A2)
. Note that, since
U=H
and
B
being the embedding from
U
into
V^{\prime}
, the optimality condition takes the form

\displaystyle\bar{u}(t)=-\tfrac{1}{\beta}\,\bar{p}(t)\quad\text{for almost all }t\in(0,T).

Thus,
\bar{u}(t)\in V
a.e., and we can use the same reduced space for the state space and the control space,
U_{r}=V_{r}
. Further, a lower bound for the constant in (
21
) can be derived from
\left|C\mathcal{S}\right|\leq\eta_{V}=1
. This follows since

W(0,T)

\left|\mathcal{S}\right|=\eta_{V}=1
for the

in (

from (

Conclusion

We demonstrated that, for unconstrained linear-quadratic optimal control problems, a reduction in the state variable induces a corresponding reduced structure in the optimal control. Consequently, the state-reduced
OCP
can be equivalently reformulated as an
OCP
reduced in both
the state and control spaces. Furthermore, we established convergence of the reduced-order model
ROM
towards the
FOM
and derived
a posteriori
upper and lower error bounds for the error in the optimal control. Based on these results, we proposed an adaptive optimization algorithm for the approximate
solution of the
FOM
-
OCP
and showed its convergence both theoretically and numerically. It turned out that our Full-
ROM
achieves a higher speed-up (compared to the
FOM
-
OCP
) than the gradient descent with only state reduction.

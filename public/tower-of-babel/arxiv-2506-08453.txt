Abstract
We present measurements of the branching fractions for the J / ψ J/\psi meson decays into the ρ ​ η \rho\eta and ϕ ​ η \phi\eta final states, based on data collected with the KEDR detector at the VEPP-4M collider. The data set consisted of 4.93 million J / ψ J/\psi events. The resulting branching fractions are: • ℬ ⁡ ( J / ψ → ρ ​ η ) = ( 2.05 ± 0.47 ± 0.23 ) × 10 − 4 \mathcal{B}(J/\psi\to\rho\eta)=(2.05\pm 0.47\pm 0.23)\times 10^{-4} , • ℬ ⁡ ( J / ψ → ϕ ​ η ) = ( 8.70 ± 1.22 ± 0.40 ) × 10 − 4 \mathcal{B}(J/\psi\to\phi\eta)=(8.70\pm 1.22\pm 0.40)\times 10^{-4} , where the first uncertainty is statistical and the second one is systematic. In the study of the ρ ​ η \rho\eta decay, the J / ψ → π + ​ π − ​ η J/\psi\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\eta dynamic is analyzed. Significant contribution to the π + ​ π − ​ η \pi^{+}\pi^{-}\eta decay from ρ ⁡ ( 1450 ) ​ η \rho(1450)\eta and a 2 ± ​ ( 1320 ) ​ π ∓ a^{\pm}_{2}(1320)\pi^{\mp} intermediate states are observed. Additional measured branching fractions are: • ℬ ⁡ ( J / ψ → π + ​ π − ​ η ) = ( 4.70 ± 0.48 ± 1.02 ) × 10 − 4 \mathcal{B}(J/\psi\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\eta)=(4.70\pm 0.48\pm 1.02)\times 10^{-4} , • ℬ ⁡ ( J / ψ → ρ ⁡ ( 1450 ) ​ η → π + ​ π − ​ η ) = ( 5.84 ± 3.66 ± 3.31 ) × 10 − 5 \mathcal{B}(J/\psi\to\rho(1450)\eta\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\eta)=(5.84\pm 3.66\pm 3.31)\times 10^{-5} , • ℬ ⁡ ( J / ψ → ( a 2 + ​ ( 1320 ) ​ π − + a 2 − ​ ( 1320 ) ​ π + ) ) = ( 1.00 ± 0.37 ± 0.43 ) × 10 − 3 \mathcal{B}(J/\psi\to(a_{2}^{+}(1320)\pi^{-}+a_{2}^{-}(1320)\pi^{+}))=(1.00\pm 0.37\pm 0.43)\times 10^{-3} . All results are consistent with previous studies.



1
Introduction

The
J/\psi
meson is one of the lightest members of the charmonium family. Despite fifty years of study, many of its hundreds of decay channels, including stable particles and intermediate resonances like
\rho
,
\omega
,
\phi
, remain not fully understood.

A particularly interesting class of
J/\psi
decays involves vector-pseudoscalar final states. These proceed dominantly through either:

•

Three-gluon processes (strong decays),

•

Single-photon processes (electromagnetic decays, when strong decays are isospin-suppressed).

The study of
J/\psi
decays involving vector mesons and
\eta
or
\eta^{\prime}
as pseudoscalar particles provides valuable information about
\eta-\eta^{\prime}
mixing angles
Bramon:1997va
and quark content
Rosner
. This work focuses on the rare decays
J/\psi\to\rho\eta
and
J/\psi\to\phi\eta
.

The
\rho\eta
channel was first studied in the late 1980s by MARK-III
MARK-III
and DM2
DM2
. It is the dominant process in
J/\psi\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\eta
and requires careful treatment of
\rho-\omega
interference. While BaBar
BaBar_piPpiMeta
and BES-III
Bes-III
have studied this decay, a complete dynamical description, including contributions from
\rho(1450)\eta
(analogous to the
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0}
case
BaBar_3pi
) and
a^{\pm}_{2}(1320)\pi^{\mp}
, remains lacking.

The
\phi\eta
channel (
\phi\to K^{+}K^{-}
) offers a cleaner system, which allows us not to take into account interferences with other resonances. This decay has been studied by multiple experiments including MARK-III
MARK-III
, DM2
DM2
, BaBar
BaBar_phi
, Belle
Belle
and BES-II
Bes-II_phi
.

Currently, the KEDR detector
KEDR
of the VEPP-4M collider
VEPP4M
has accumulated 4.93 million
J/\psi

\rho\eta
and
\phi\eta
decay channels.

2
Theoretical framework

This analysis employs the Vector Meson Dominance (VMD) model to describe the decay processes.

2.1
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\eta
final state

For the
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\eta
channel, we have to consider:

•

\rho-\omega
interference, despite the small branching fraction
\omega\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
. The contributions from
\omega\eta
and
\rho\eta
are comparable due to isospin suppression in
J/\psi\to\rho\eta
,

•

Additional contributions from intermediate states:

–

\rho(1450)\eta
,

–

a^{\pm}_{2}(1320)\pi^{\mp}
.

The differential cross section for vector-pseudoscalar (
V+P
) decays is given by:

\left(\frac{d\sigma}{d\Gamma}\right)_{V+P}\propto\left|\sum_{k=\rho,\omega,\rho(1450)}a_{k}e^{i\phi_{k}}\right|^{2}=\sum_{k=\rho,\omega,\rho(1450)}|a_{k}|^{2}+\sum_{k,j=\rho,\omega,\rho(1450),k\neq j}a_{k}a_{j}^{*}e^{i(\phi_{k}-\phi_{j})},

(1)

where the resonance amplitude
a_{\rho}
, written for
\rho
meson, takes the form:

a_{\rho}=|\mathbf{P_{\pi^{+}}}\times\mathbf{P_{\pi^{-}}}|\sin(\theta_{n})\frac{m^{2}_{\rho}}{q^{2}-m^{2}_{\rho}+iq\Gamma_{\rho}(q^{2})}\sqrt{\mathcal{B}{(J/\psi\to\rho\eta)}\mathcal{B}{(\rho\to\pi\pi)}},

(2)

with the energy-dependent width:

\Gamma_{\rho}(q^{2})=\Gamma_{\rho}\left(\frac{p_{\pi}(q^{2})}{p_{\pi}(m_{\rho}^{2})}\right)^{3}\left(\frac{m_{\rho}^{2}}{q^{2}}\right).

(3)

Here:

•

|\mathbb{P_{\pi^{\pm}}}|
are charged pion momenta,

•

\theta_{n}
is the angle between the normal to the reaction plane and the beam axis,

•

m_{\rho}
and
\Gamma_{\rho}
are the
\rho
meson mass and width,

•

q
is the pion pair invariant mass,

•

p_{\pi}
is the pion momentum in the
\rho
rest frame.

The
\rho-\omega
interference terms in Eq.
1
can be expressed as:

a_{\rho}a_{\omega}^{*}e^{-i\phi}+a_{\rho}^{*}a_{\omega}e^{i\phi}=\frac{\sqrt{\mathcal{B}(J/\psi\to\rho\eta)\mathcal{B}(\rho\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-})}\sqrt{\mathcal{B}(J/\psi\to\omega\eta)\mathcal{B}(\omega\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-})}}{((q^{2}-m^{2}_{\rho})^{2}+q^{2}\Gamma^{2}_{\rho}(q^{2}))((q^{2}-m^{2}_{\omega})^{2}+q^{2}\Gamma^{2}_{\omega}(q^{2}))}\times

\times 2|\mathbf{P_{\pi^{+}}}\times\mathbf{P_{\pi^{-}}}|^{2}sin^{2}(\theta_{n})m^{2}_{\rho}m^{2}_{\omega}\times

\displaystyle\times\Bigg[(q^{4}+m^{2}_{\rho}m^{2}_{\omega}+q^{2}\Gamma_{\rho}(q^{2})\Gamma_{\omega}(q^{2}))cos(\phi)-

(4)

-q^{2}(m^{2}_{\rho}+m^{2}_{\omega})cos(\phi)+

+(q^{3}\Gamma_{\omega}(q^{2})+q\Gamma_{\rho}(q^{2})m^{2}_{\omega})sin(\phi)-

-(q^{3}\Gamma_{\rho}(q^{2})+q\Gamma_{\omega}(q^{2})m^{2}_{\rho})sin(\phi)\Bigg].

All other interference terms follow similar expressions, with phases defined relative to the
\rho
meson.

The total cross section has to include the
a^{\pm}_{2}(1320)\pi^{\mp}
, which are tensor-pseudoscalar (
T+P
) decays and can’t be described by Eq.
1
. Therefore, the total cross section for both vector-pseudoscalars and tensor-pseudoscalars is:

\left(\frac{d\sigma}{d\Gamma}\right)_{\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\eta}=\left(\frac{d\sigma}{d\Gamma}\right)_{V+P}+\left(\frac{d\sigma}{d\Gamma}\right)_{T+P}

(5)

2.2
K^{+}K^{-}\eta
final state

For the
\phi\eta
channel studied through
K^{+}K^{-}\eta
, we consider:

\left(\frac{d\sigma}{d\Gamma}\right)_{\phi\eta}\propto|a_{\phi}|^{2}

(6)

where
a_{\phi}
follows Eq.
2
with
\rho\to\phi
and
\pi\to K
replacement.

The total cross section includes contributions from other intermediate states with
M_{K^{+}K^{-}}
much bigger than the
\phi
meson mass. Therefore, all other contributions, except
\phi
resonance, will be called non-resonant contributions, which are taken into account ignoring the interference effects:

\left(\frac{d\sigma}{d\Gamma}\right)_{K^{+}K^{-}\eta}=\left(\frac{d\sigma}{d\Gamma}\right)_{\phi\eta}+\left(\frac{d\sigma}{d\Gamma}\right)_{\text{non-res}}

(7)

The Monte Carlo simulation incorporates several components:

•

The vector-pseudoscalar decays (Eq.

–
3

,

•

The tensor-pseudoscalar channel

;
helicity_2
,

•

Non-resonant
J/\psi\to K^{+}K^{-}\eta
decays were simulated using generator from
rhopi
with uniform phasespace distributions for final particles.

For each process,
5\times 10^{5}
events were generated using particle properties from PDG
PDG
. To simulate the background events, the BES generator
BES_generator
and the generators from
rhopi
;
malyshev
are used. Parameters of JETSET

used in the BES generator were tuned in the work
KEDR_experiment
.

4
Event selection

The analysis is performed using the KEDR detector at the VEPP-4M collider. The data used include 1.4 pb
-1
at the
J/\psi
peak and 0.278 pb
-1
outside the peak. In total, approximately 4.93 million
J/\psi
decays are analyzed. The data outside the
J/\psi
peak is used to estimate the continuum background events.

The signature used for the analysis is two central tracks and two photons originating from the
\eta
meson. These photons are clusters in the calorimeter with an energy above 50 MeV that are not associated with charged tracks. Further,
N_{ph}
will correspond to the number of photons. The central tracks have to cross a cylinder with a radius of 3 cm and a length of 17 cm around the interaction point. Any number of non-central tracks is allowed. Events with more than two photons are also used for systematic uncertainty estimation.

Kinematic reconstruction is performed assuming the hypotheses of
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\eta
and
K^{+}K^{-}\eta
. Since this work does not use an identification system, a
\chi^{2}
value of less than 70 is used as a criterion to separate events involving pions and kaons.

To select events containing the
\eta
meson and suppress background, each event is reprocessed in the hypothesis of free photons. An invariant mass of these photons (
M_{\gamma\gamma}
) has to be between 500 MeV and 600 MeV.

5
Analysis procedure

5.1
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\eta
final state

The
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\eta
final state is analyzed using a Dalitz plot distribution of invariant masses between
\pi^{+}\eta
and
\pi^{-}\eta
.

Following event generation (Section
3
), we construct invariant mass histograms
H_{i}[j,l]
for selected events (Section
4
) for each
i
-th cross section component, where indexes
j
and
l
correspond to the bins in the
M_{\pi^{+}\eta}
and
M_{\pi^{-}\eta}
projections of the Dalitz plot distribution. The event counts in these histograms are proportional to the simulated integral of luminosity. For interference terms between decays
k
and
m
(Eq.
4
), we define:

•

H_{k,m}^{c\pm}
: Cosine interference terms,

•

H_{k,m}^{s\pm}
: Sine interference terms.

The expected event distribution is parameterized as:

N_{exp}[j,l]=\Bigg(p_{1}H_{\rho\eta}[j,l]+p_{2}H_{\omega\eta}[j,l]+p_{3}H_{\rho(1450)\eta}[j,l]+

+\sqrt{p_{1}p_{2}}\Bigg[(H^{c+}_{\rho\eta,\omega\eta}[j,l]-H^{c-}_{\rho\eta,\omega\eta}[j,l])cos(\phi_{1})+(H^{s+}_{\rho\eta,\omega\eta}[j,l]-H^{s-}_{\rho\eta,\omega\eta}[j,l])sin(\phi_{1})\Bigg]+

+\sqrt{p_{1}p_{3}}\Bigg[(H^{c+}_{\rho\eta,\rho(1450)\eta}[j,l]-H^{c-}_{\rho\eta,\rho(1450)\eta}[j,l])cos(\phi_{2})+(H^{s+}_{\rho\eta,\rho(1450)\eta}[j,l]-H^{s-}_{\rho\eta,\rho(1450)\eta}[j,l])sin(\phi_{2})\Bigg]+

+\sqrt{p_{2}p_{3}}\Bigg[(H^{c+}_{\rho(1450)\eta,\omega\eta}[j,l]-H^{c-}_{\rho(1450)\eta,\omega\eta}[j,l])cos(\phi_{1}-\phi_{2})+

\displaystyle+(H^{s+}_{\rho(1450)\eta,\omega\eta}[j,l]-H^{s-}_{\rho(1450)\eta,\omega\eta}[j,l])sin(\phi_{1}-\phi_{2})\Bigg]+

(8)

+p_{4}H_{a^{\pm}_{2}(1320)\pi^{\mp}}[j,l]+p_{5}H_{\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0}\gamma\gamma}[j,l]\Bigg),

where
p_{i}
and
\phi_{k}
are resonance strengths and relative phases,
H_{i}[j,l]
is the invariant mass histogram for the selected events in the
[j,l]
bin. The
p_{i}
are proportional to branching fractions. Here, a
J/\psi\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0}\gamma\gamma
is considered, as it is the dominant background for this process.

Parameters
p_{i}
and
\phi_{k}
are determined by maximizing the likelihood function:

L_{\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\eta}=-2\sum_{j,l}\left(N_{exp}[j,l]-N_{obs}[j,l]+N_{obs}[j,l]\ln\frac{N_{obs}[j,l]}{N_{exp}[j,l]}\right),

(9)

summed over all histogram bins, where
N_{obs}[j,l]
are experimentally observed events in the
[j,l]
bin.

The additional free parameter
\Delta M_{\pi^{\pm}\eta}
is introduced to account for a possible shift of the invariant masses due to uncertainty in the absolute calibration of the momentum measurements. It has been done by shifting the
H_{i}
histograms using fifth-degree spline functions. First, the histograms were interpolated with the splines, then the splines were shifted by an amount of
\Delta M_{\pi^{\pm}\eta}
. After that, new shifted histograms were created based on the shifted spline functions.

Using
\mathcal{B}(J/\psi\to\omega\eta)
from BES-II measurement

as a constraint, we fit data using
9
and calculate:

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(J/\psi\to\rho\eta)=\frac{p_{1}I_{\rho\eta}}{N_{J/\psi}\mathcal{B}(\eta\to\gamma\gamma)\epsilon_{\rho\eta}},

(10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(J/\psi\to\rho(1450)\eta\longrightarrow\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\eta)=\frac{p_{3}I_{\rho(1450)\eta}}{N_{J/\psi}\mathcal{B}(\eta\to\gamma\gamma)\epsilon_{\rho(1450)\eta}},

(11)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(J/\psi\to a^{\pm}_{2}(1320)\pi^{\mp})=\frac{p_{4}I_{a^{\pm}_{2}(1320)\pi^{\mp}}}{N_{J/\psi}\mathcal{B}(\eta\to\gamma\gamma)\mathcal{B}(a^{\pm}_{2}(1320)\to\pi^{\pm}\eta)\epsilon_{a^{\pm}_{2}(1320)\pi^{\mp}}},

(12)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(J/\psi\longrightarrow\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\eta)=\frac{N_{\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\eta}}{N_{J/\psi}\mathcal{B}(\eta\to\gamma\gamma)\epsilon_{\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\eta}},

(13)

where
N_{J/\psi}
— amount of the
J/\psi
mesons,
\mathcal{B}(\eta\to\gamma\gamma)
and

\epsilon_{i}

i

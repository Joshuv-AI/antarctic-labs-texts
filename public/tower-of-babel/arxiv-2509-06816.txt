1.
Introduction

This work is concerned with the Initial Valued Problem (IVP) associated to the Benjamin-Ono (BO) equation

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}u+u\partial_{x}u=0,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
\mathcal{H}
denotes the Hilbert transform,

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}f(x)&=\frac{1}{\pi}{\rm p.v.}\big(\frac{1}{x}\ast f\big)(x)\\
&=\frac{1}{\pi}\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\int\limits_{|y|\geq\epsilon}\frac{f(x-y)}{y}\,dy=(-i\,{\rm sgn}(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi))^{\vee}(x).\end{split}

(1.2)

The BO equation was first deduced by Benjamin

[
4
]
and Ono
[
31
]
as a model for long internal gravity
waves in deep stratified fluids. Later, it was also shown to be a
completely integrable system (see
[
1
]
and
references therein). In particular, real solutions of (
1.1
) satisfy
infinitely many conservation laws. These quantities provide an
a priori
estimate for the
H^{n/2}
-norm,
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, of the solution

\,u=u(x,t)\,
for (
1.1
).

The problem of finding the minimal regularity property, measured in the Sobolev scale
\,H^{s}(\mathbb{R})=J^{-s/2}L^{2}(\mathbb{R})=(1-\partial_{x}^{2})^{-s/2}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),\,s\in\mathbb{R},

required to guarantee that the IVP (
1.1
) is local or global well-posed in
\,H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
has been extensively studied, see
[
2
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
33
]
,
[
23
]
,
[
20
]
,
[
35
]
, and
[
6
]
. In

[
15
]
global well-posedness was established in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}),\;s\geq 0
, see also
[
26
]
,
[
14
]
and for a complete review on the BO equation we refer to
[
34
]
. In
[
27
]
it was proved that no well-posedness for IVP (
1.1
) can be established by an argument based only on the contraction principle argument. More recently, in
[
22
]
, using an argument based on the inverse scattering, it was shown that the IVP (
1.1
) is global well posed in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}),\;s>-1/2
.

To describe well-posedness results in weighted Sobolev spaces we introduce the notations:

Z_{s,r}=H^{s}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2}(|x|^{2r}dx),\quad\dot{Z}_{s,r}=Z_{s,r}\cap\{\,\widehat{f}(0)=0\},\quad s,\,r\in\mathbb{R}.

In this direction, we have:

Theorem A

(
[
9
,
10
]
)
.

(1)

If
u_{0}\in Z_{5,r},\;\,r\in(0,5/2)

\,u\in C([0,T]:Z_{5,r}).

(2)

If there exist

then

\,\;\widehat{u}(0,t)=0
.

(3)

If
\,u_{0}\in\dot{Z}_{5,r},\,r\in[5/2,7/2)
, then
\,u\in C([0,T]:\dot{Z}_{5,r})
.

(4)

Let
\,u\in C([0,T]:H^{5}(\mathbb{R}))
be a solution of the BO eq. If there exist

such that

Remark A
.

(1)

Theorem A part
(1) with
r\in(0,2]
, part (2) and (3) with

(2)

The limited decay exhibited on solutions of the BO equation is due to the lack of regularity of the symbol of the operator
\sigma(\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2})=i{\rm sgn}(\xi)\xi^{2}
describing the dispersive relation. In particular, the solitons of the BO equation are of the form

\eta(x)=\frac{4}{1+x^{2}},\;\;\;\;\;\;\eta_{c}(x-t)=c\eta(c(x-ct)),\;\;\;\;c>0.

(3)

Part (5) of Theorem A tells us that the result described in part (4) does not hold if one reduces the hypothesis at just two times.

Remarks 1.1
.

(1)

Roughly, Theorem
1.1
affirms that for
\beta<7/2
, the
L^{2}(|x|^{2\beta}dx)
-norm of the solution remains bounded in the existence time interval if and only if the data is regular enough
u_{0}\in H^{\beta}(\mathbb{R})
. This kind of result is characteristic of the dispersive equation. In particular, it was proven in
[
17
]
for the generalized Korteweg-de Vries (gKdV) equation and in
[
25
]
for the intermediate long wave (ILW) equation. The proof combines an iterative argument with weighted energy estimates which are reminiscence of the Kato smoothing effect
[
18
]
. Nevertheless, in these cases, the gKdV and the ILW eqs, the proofs are significantly simpler. For the KdV the smoothing effect provides a gain of one derivative (local operator), and in the case of the ILW this smoothing effect is described by a smooth pseudo-differential symbol of order
1/2
. However, for the BO equation the gain of
1/2
derivatives involves an operator with a non-smooth symbol. This highly
complicates the necessary commutative relations between fractional weights and fractional non-smooth operator. In addition, the range of the possible decay is
a priori
limited.

(2)

The gap
s\in(1/2,3/2]
between the results in parts
(i)
and
(ii)
of Theorem
1.1
may seem to be a consequence of the arguments used in their proofs. However, a similar gap appears in the following problem : consider the IVP associated to the Burgers equation

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+u\partial_{x}u=0,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x).\end{cases}

(1.7)

Question : Given
u_{0}\in\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R})
for which
s\geq 0
one has an estimate of the form

\sup_{[0,T]}\|u(t)\|_{s,2}\leq c_{0}\|u_{0}\|_{s,2}\hskip 15.0pt\text{for some}\enskip T>0?

The answer is only for
s=0
and
s>\frac{3}{2}
.

(3)

The upper bound
s=\frac{7}{2}
in Theorem
1.1
part
(ii)
is related to previous results.

(4)

The results in Theorem
1.1
extend to the generalized BO equation, i.e. higher order non-linearity are considered, in the range where
well-posedness is known.

2.3.
General Argument

In this subsection we prove a result
which is employed several times along our analysis.

Proposition 2.10

(General Argument)
.

If
\phi\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
,
\phi^{\prime\prime}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, then

\int\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}v(x)\,v(x)\phi(x)\,dx=-\int(D^{1/2}v(x))^{2}\phi^{\prime}(x)\,dx+M_{0}(t)

(2.21)

with

|M_{0}(t)|\leq c\|\phi^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}\|v\|_{2}^{2}.

(2.22)

Proof.

We write

\begin{split}\int\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}vv\phi&=-\int\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}v\partial_{x}v\phi-\int\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}vv\phi^{\prime}\\
&=M_{1}(t)+M_{2}(t).\end{split}

On one hand, we get

\begin{split}M_{1}(t)&=\int\partial_{x}v\mathcal{H}(\partial_{x}v\phi)=\int\partial_{x}v[\mathcal{H};\phi]\partial_{x}v+\int\partial_{x}v\,\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}v\,\phi\\
&=-\int v\partial_{x}[\mathcal{H};\phi]\partial_{x}v-M_{1}(t).\end{split}

(2.23)

Then

M_{1}(t)=-\frac{1}{2}\int v\partial_{x}[\mathcal{H};\phi]\partial_{x}v.

By using (
2.8
) we deduce that

|M_{1}(t)|\leq c\|\phi^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}\|v\|_{2}^{2}.

On the other hand,

\begin{split}M_{2}(t)&=-\int Dvv\phi^{\prime}=-\int D^{1/2}vD^{1/2}(v\phi^{\prime})\\
&=-\int D^{1/2}vD^{1/2}v\,\phi^{\prime}-\int D^{1/2}v[D^{1/2};\phi^{\prime}]v\\
&=-\int\big(D^{1/2}v\big)^{2}\phi^{\prime}-\int vD^{1/2}[D^{1/2};\phi^{\prime}]v.\end{split}

Applying inequality (
2.9
) it follows that

\|D^{1/2}[D^{1/2};\phi^{\prime}]v\|_{2}\leq c\|\phi^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}\|v\|_{2}.

Collecting the above results one gets (
2.21
).
∎

D\partial_{x}u\langle x\rangle^{\theta-1/2}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\quad\text{a.e.}\quad t\in[t_{7},t_{8}].

From the continuity properties of the Hilbert transform (
\mathcal{H}^{2}=-I
)

D\partial_{x}u\langle x\rangle^{\theta-1/2}=\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}u\langle x\rangle^{\theta-1/2}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}).

This implies that
\partial_{x}^{2}u\langle x\rangle^{\theta-1/2}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and
\langle x\rangle^{\alpha}u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, with
\alpha=3/2+\theta
,
by using interpolation.
Therefore,
\langle x\rangle^{\alpha-2}J^{2}u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and
\langle x\rangle^{\alpha}u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

Finally, by interpolation in (
2.14
) we conclude that
J^{\alpha}u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

This completes Case 1.

\begin{split}-\tilde{L}_{11}(t)&=-\int(\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}(xu)+\mathcal{H}u)(\partial_{x}(xu)-u)x\,\langle x\rangle\,dx\\

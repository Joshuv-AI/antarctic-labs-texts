1.
introduction

It is an important topic in geometry to understand how geometric assumptions restrict the topology of the underlying Riemannian manifold. In this direction, Tachibana
[
13
]
showed that any compact Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
with harmonic curvature and positive curvature operator is isometric to a quotient of the standard sphere. If the curvature operator is only nonnegative, then the manifold is locally symmetric. Later, Tran
[
14
]
proved that a compact Riemannian manifold
M
of dimension
n\geq 4
with harmonic Weyl curvature and positive curvature operator, then
M
is locally conformally flat. In
[
12
]
Petersen and Wink showed that a compact manifold with harmonic Weyl tensor and
[\frac{n-1}{2}]
-nonnegative curvature operator is either globally conformal to a quotient of the standard sphere or locally symmetric, and that the first possibility always occurs if the curvature operator is
[\frac{n-1}{2}]
-positive. In
[
11
]
they proved a similar Tachibana-type theorem under the stronger condition that the manifold be Einstein.
More recently, Colombo, Mariani, and Rigoli
[
2
]
established that a compact Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
with harmonic curvature and
[\frac{n-1}{2}]
-positive curvature operator must have constant sectional curvature, thereby generalizing Tachibana’s result.

For curvature operator of the second kind, Nishikawa
[
10
]
conjectured that a closed simply connected Riemannian manifold for which the curvature operator of the second kind is nonnegative and positive is diffeomorphic to a Riemannian locally symmetric space and a spherical space form, respectively. This conjecture has been proved and improved by Cao–Gursky–Tran
[
1
]
and Li
[
7
]
, who relaxed the assumption to
3
-positive (
3
-nonnegative) curvature operator of the second kind. In 1993, Kashiwada
[
5
]
showed that Riemannian manifolds with harmonic curvature for which the curvature operator of the second kind is nonnegative (respectively, positive) are locally symmetric spaces (respectively, constant curvature spaces). Nienhaus, Petersen and Wink
[
9
,
8
]
used the Bochner formula to show that
n
-dimensional compact Einstein manifolds with
k(<\frac{3n(n+2)}{2(n+4)})
-nonnegative curvature operators of the second kind are either rational homology spheres or flat. Dai-Fu
[
4
]
proved that any closed Einstein manifold of dimension
n=4
or
n=5
or
n\geq 8
with
[\frac{n+2}{4}]
-nonnegative curvature operator of the second kind is a constant curvature space. Later, Dai-Fu-Yang
[
3
]
proved a compact manifold with harmonic Weyl tensor and nonnegative curvature operator of the second kind is globally conformally equivalent to a space of positive constant curvature or is isometric to a flat manifold.

Let
\lambda_{i}
be the eigenvalues of the curvature operator, arranged in non-decreasing order. If

{\lambda}_{1}+{\lambda}_{2}+\cdots{\lambda}_{[k]}+(k-[k])\lambda_{[k]+1}>0(\geq 0),

we say that the curvature operator is
k
-positive(resp. nonnegative).
In this paper, we investigate Riemannian manifolds of dimension
n\geq 3
with harmonic curvature and
\frac{n(n+2)}{2(n+1)}
-nonnegative curvature operator of the second kind, and obtain the following Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be an
n(\geq 3)
-dimensional compact Riemannian manifold with harmonic curvature. If the curvature operator of the second kind
\mathring{R}
is
\frac{n(n+2)}{2(n+1)}
-nonnegative, then
M
is an Einstein manifold.

Furthermore, by employing rigidity theorems for Einstein manifolds concerning the curvature operator of the second kind, established by Dai–Fu
[
4
]
and Li
[
6
]
, we get the following:

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
(M,g)
be an
n(\geq 3)
-dimensional compact Riemannian manifold with harmonic curvature, and
\mathring{R}\,
be the curvature operator of the second kind. If

(i)
3\leq n\leq 7
and
\mathring{R}\,
is
\frac{n(n+2)}{2(n+1)}
-nonnegative;

(ii)
8\leq n\leq 13
and
\mathring{R}\,
is
4
-nonnegative;

(iii)
n\geq 14
,
\mathring{R}\,
is
[\frac{n+2}{4}]
-nonnegative,

then
M
must be of constant curvature.

Remark 1.3
.

Corollary 1.2 extends and enhances Kashiwada’s results
[
5
]
and Theorem 1.2 in
[
3
]
. Corollary 1.2 can be regarded as a generalization of their results on Einstein manifolds
[
1
,
4
,
6
]
.

Based on some existing results in this field and Theorem
1.1
, combined with Li’s conjecture that a closed
n

(n+\frac{n-2}{n})

2.
proof of Theorem
1.1

In a Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
, the Riemannian curvature tensor
R
induces two symmetric linear operators
\hat{R}
and
\mathring{R}
. Denote by
V
be the tangent space of this Riemannian manifold, by
{\Lambda}^{2}V
the space of skew symmetric
2
-tensor over
V
, and by
S_{0}^{2}(V)
the space of trace-free symmetric
2
-tensor over
V
. The curvature operator of the first kind is defined by

\displaystyle\hat{R}:{\Lambda}^{2}V\to{\Lambda}^{2}V

\displaystyle\hat{R}(e_{i}\wedge e_{j})=\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{k,l}{R_{ijkl}e_{k}\wedge e_{l}},

and the curvature operator of the second kind is defined in
[
1
,
9
]
by

\mathring{R}={\Pr}_{s_{0}^{2}(V)}\circ\left.\bar{R}\right|_{S_{0}^{2}(V)}.

The operator
\mathring{R}
can be regarded as the following curvature operator
\bar{R}
and its image restricts on
S_{0}^{2}(V)
. The operator
\bar{R}
act on
S^{2}(V)
induced from Riemannian curvature tensor
R
is defined by

\bar{R}(e_{i}\odot e_{j})=\sum\limits_{k,l}{R_{kijl}e_{k}\odot e_{l}},

here
e_{i}\odot e_{j}=e_{i}\otimes e_{j}+e_{j}\otimes e_{i}
. So
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\{e_{i}\odot e_{j}\}_{1\leq i<j\leq n},\frac{1}{2}\{e_{i}\odot e_{i}\}_{i=1,\dots,n}
is an orthonormal basis for
S^{2}(V)
.

Let
M^{n}
be an
n
-dimensional complete manifold with harmonic curvature, endowed with a standard Riemannian metric
\left\langle\text{,}\right\rangle
. Riemannian curvature tensor
R
is divergence free if and only if its Ricci tensor is a Codazzi tensor, and in this case its scalar curvature is constant. Hence the traceless Ricci tensor is harmonic because its Ricci tensor is divergence free. Denote by
E
its traceless Ricci tensor and by
s
its scalar curvature. Then

\displaystyle\Delta\frac{1}{2}\left|E\right|^{2}=\left|\nabla E\right|^{2}+\left\langle\Delta E,E\right\rangle.

(2.1)

Since
E
is harmonic, the Ricci identity gives

\displaystyle\left\langle\Delta E,E\right\rangle

\displaystyle=E_{ij}E_{ij,tt}=E_{ij}E_{it,jt}

\displaystyle=E_{ij}(E_{it,tj}+R_{jtsi}E_{st}+R_{jtst}E_{is})

\displaystyle=\left\langle\mathring{R}\,(E),E\right\rangle+R_{ij}E_{it}E_{jt}.

(2.2)

To analyze the individual terms, we apply the powerful weighted-sum calculus developed in
[
9
]
, which allows us to estimate finite weighted sums with nonnegative weights.

Definition 2.1
.

(
[
9
, Definition 3.1]
)
Let
\{\omega_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be the nonnegative weights of any finite weighted sums. Define

\Omega=\max_{1\leq i\leq N}\omega_{i}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad S=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\omega_{i}\,.

We call
S
the total weight and
\Omega
the highest weight.

Let
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be the eigenvalues of
\mathring{R}\,
. Following the notation in
[
9
]
, we write
[\mathring{R},\Omega,S]
to denote any finite weighted sums
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\omega_{i}\lambda_{i}
in terms of
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
whose weights satisfy highest weight
\Omega
and total weight
S
.

Lemma 2.2
.

(
[
9
, Lemma 3.4]
)
Let
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
denote the ordered eigenvalues of
\mathring{R}
. Then, for any integer
1\leq m\leq N
, we have

[\mathring{R},\Omega,S]\geq(S-m\Omega)\lambda_{m+1}+\Omega\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}.

Therefore,
[\mathring{R},\Omega,S]\geq 0
if
\mathring{R}
is
\frac{S}{\Omega}
-nonnegative.

If
\{S_{\alpha}\}
is an orthonormal eigenbasis for
\mathring{R}
with corresponding eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{\alpha}\}
, then since
E
is a traceless symmetric
(0,2)
-tensor, it can be expressed in the orthonormal basis
\left\{S_{\alpha}\right\}
as
E=\sum\nolimits_{\alpha}{E_{\alpha}S_{\alpha}}
. Then equation
(
2
) becomes

\displaystyle\left\langle\Delta E,E\right\rangle=R_{ij}E_{it}E_{jt}+\sum\limits_{\alpha}{{\lambda}_{\alpha}E_{\alpha}^{2}}.

(2.3)

According to
[
9
, Example 3.2]
, the scalar curvature
s
of
M
satisfies

\displaystyle s\geq\frac{2n}{n+2}[\mathring{R},1,\frac{(n-1)(n+2)}{2}],

(2.4)

and from
[
9
, Lemma 3.14]
, the Ricci curvature
Ric
of
M
satisfies

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ric}\geq\frac{n-1}{n+1}\left[\mathring{R},1,n\right]+\frac{1}{n(n+1)}s.

Combining (
2.4
) with
[
9
, Lemma 3.3(c)]
, we obtain

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ric}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n-1}{n+1}\left[\mathring{R},1,n\right]+\frac{2}{(n+1)(n+2)}[\mathring{R},1,\frac{(n-1)(n+2)}{2}]

\displaystyle\geq\left[\mathring{R},\frac{n}{n+2},n-1\right].

(2.5)

Furthermore, since

\sum\limits_{\alpha}{E_{\alpha}^{2}}=\left|E\right|^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad E_{\alpha}^{2}\leq\left|E\right|^{2},

the weight principle in
[
9
, Theorem 3.6]
yields

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathring{R}\,(E),E\right\rangle\geq\left[\mathring{R},1,1\right]\left|E\right|^{2}.

(2.6)

Proposition 2.3
.

If
E
is a traceless symmetric 2-tensor, then

\left\langle\Delta E,E\right\rangle\geq\left[\mathring{R},\frac{2(n+1)}{n+2},n\right]\left|E\right|^{2}.

Proof.

Combining equation (
2.3
) with (
2.5
),(
2.6
) and
[
9
, Lemma 3.3(c)]
yields

\displaystyle\left\langle\Delta E,E\right\rangle\geq

\displaystyle\left[\mathring{R},\frac{n}{n+2},n-1\right]\left|E\right|^{2}+\left[\mathring{R},1,1\right]\left|E\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left[\mathring{R},\frac{2(n+1)}{n+2},n\right]\left|E\right|^{2}.

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

If
s=0
, then
M
is flat. Otherwise,
s>0
, i.e.,
M
is non-flat. Let
\tilde{M}
be the universal cover
of
M
, equipped with the lifted metric
\tilde{g}
. Then
(\tilde{M},\tilde{g})
also has
\frac{n(n+2)}{2(n+1)}
-nonnegative curvature
operator of the second kind, and nonnegative Ricci curvature by part
(3)
of Proposition 4.1 in
[
7
]
. According to the Cheeger-Gromoll splitting theorem,
\tilde{M}
is isometric to a
product of the form
N^{n-k}\times R^{k}
, where
N
is compact. By Theorem 1.8 in
[
7
]
, we know
that
\tilde{M}
is locally irreducible, which forces
k=0
. Hence
\tilde{M}
is compact. By Proposition
2.3
, we have

\left\langle\Delta E,E\right\rangle\geq\left[\mathring{R},\frac{2(n+1)}{n+2},n\right]\left|E\right|^{2}\geq 0.

It follows from (
2.1
) that the trace-less Ricci tensor
E
is parallel. Hence
\tilde{M}
is Ricci parallel and an Einstein
manifold. Consequently,
M
is an Einstein
manifold.
∎

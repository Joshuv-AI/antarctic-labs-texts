On the Mather stability theorem for smooth maps
Rustam Sadykov

Abstract
. In [ 3 ] Mather proved that a smooth proper infinitesimally stable map is stable. This result is the key component of the Mather stability theorem [ 5 ] , which can be reformulated as follows: a smooth proper map f : M → N f:M\to N is stable if and only if it is infinitesimally stable if and only if it satisfies the Mather normal crossing condition. The latter condition, roughly speaking, means that all map germs of f f are stable and f f maps the singular strata of f f to N N in a mutually transversal manner. In this note we adapt a short argument from the book [ 2 ] to derive the Mather stability theorem presented in [ 5 ] from the theorem in [ 3 ] .

2.
Mutually transversal subspaces

Recall that for
s>1
subspaces
P_{1},\ldots,P_{s}
of a vector space
Q
are
mutually transversal
or
in general position
if every space
P_{i}
is transverse to the intersection of the others, i.e.,
Q=P_{i}+\cap_{j\neq i}P_{j}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

The spaces in the collection
\{P_{i}\}
are in general position.

(2)

The diagonal map
\Delta^{\bullet}\colon Q\to\oplus_{i}(Q/P_{i})
is surjective.

(3)

The diagonal map
\Delta\colon Q\to\oplus_{i}Q
is transverse to
\oplus_{i}P_{i}
, i.e.,
\oplus_{i}Q=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Im}(\Delta)+\oplus_{i}P_{i}
.

(4)

Each subcollection
\{P_{i_{j}}\}
of the collection
\{P_{i}\}
is in general position in
Q
.

(5)

Given
v_{1},\ldots,v_{s}\in Q
, there is
z\in Q
such that
v_{i}-z
is in
P_{i}
for all
i
.

Proof.

To show that
(1)
implies
(2)
, suppose
Q=P_{i}+\cap_{j\neq i}P_{j}
for each
i
. Then every element in
\oplus_{i}(Q/P_{i})
can be represented by
(y_{1}+P_{1},\ldots,y_{s}+P_{s})
with
y_{i}\in\cap_{j\neq i}P_{j}
.
Then
\Delta^{\bullet}(y_{1}+\cdots+y_{s})=(y_{1}+P_{1},\ldots,y_{s}+P_{s})
since for
k\neq i
vectors
y_{k}
are in
\cap_{j\neq k}P_{j}\subset P_{i}
. Thus
\Delta^{\bullet}
is surjective. Conversely, suppose
\Delta^{\bullet}
is surjective. Then for any
x\in Q
there is
p_{i}\in Q
such that the
i
-th component of
\Delta^{\bullet}(p_{i})
is
0
, and the
j
-th component of
\Delta^{\bullet}(p_{i})
is
x+P_{j}
for all
j\neq i
. In other words, every element
x\in Q
is a sum of
p_{i}\in P_{i}
and the element
x-p_{i}
in
\cap_{j\neq i}P_{j}
. Thus
(2)
implies
(1)
.
The statements
(2)
and
(3)
are equivalent as well in view of the short exact sequence:

0\longrightarrow\oplus_{i}P_{i}\longrightarrow\oplus_{i}Q\longrightarrow\oplus_{i}(Q/P_{i})\longrightarrow 0.

Suppose now that the spaces
\{P_{i}\}
are in general position, i.e.,
Q=P_{i}+\cap_{j\neq i}P_{j}
. Then
Q=P_{i}+\cap_{j\in J\setminus\{i\}}P_{j}
for each subset
J
of indices
\{1,\ldots,s\}
.
Thus,
(1)
and
(4)
are equivalent. The equivalence of
(2)
and
(5)
is obvious.
∎

3.
Infinitesimally stable maps

Given a smooth map
f\colon M\to N
, a
vector field along

f
is a map
w\colon M\to TN
such that
\pi_{N}\circ w=f
, where
\pi_{N}\colon TN\to N
is the canonical projection. We say that a smooth map
f\colon M\to N
is
infinitesimally stable
if for every vector field
w
along
f
, there is a vector field
u
on
M
and a vector field
v
on
N
such that
w=df(u)+v(f)
, where
v(f)
stands for the composition
M\xrightarrow{f}N\xrightarrow{v}TN
. The definition of an
infinitesimally stable map germ
is obtained from this definition by replacing maps and vector fields with map germs and germ vector fields respectively. We also say that the map germ of
f
at
p
is
transverse stable
if for sufficiently big
k
the
k
-jet extension
j^{k}f
of
f
is transverse at
p
to the orbit of
j^{k}f(p)
under the action of right-left changes of coordinates.

Theorem 3.1

(Mather)
.

The following conditions on a map germ of
f\colon M\to N
at a point
p
are equivalent:

•

The map germ of
f
at
p
is stable.

•

The map germ of
f
at
p
is infinitesimally stable.

•

The map germ of
f
at
p
is transverse stable.

Proof.

If the map germ
[f]=[f]_{p}
of
f
at
p
is stable, then it is transverse stable. Indeed, by the Thom’s jet transversality theorem there is a map
f^{\prime}
close to
f
in
C^{\infty}(M,N)
whose jet extension is transverse in
J^{k}(M,N)
to the orbit of
j^{k}f(p)
. If
f^{\prime}
is sufficiently close to
f
, then in view of stability of
f
at
p
, the map germ
[f^{\prime}]
of
f^{\prime}
at some point is equivalent to the map germ
[f]
of
f
at
p
. Therefore the jet extension of
f
is also transverse at
p
to the orbit of
j^{k}f(p)
, see
[
2
, Lemma V.5.7]
. Every transverse stable map germ is infinitesimally stable by
[
2
, Theorem V.5.13]
. Finally, every infinitesimally stable map germ is stable by
[
1
, §7.3]
.
∎

Now a short argument in

The third condition in the original Mather stability theorem is equivalent to the third condition in Theorem

Theorem 3.3
.

Let

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

The argument presented here is essentially an adaptation of the proof of
[
2
, Theorem VII.6.4]
.

Every stable map satisfies the Mather normal crossing condition by the Multijet Transversality Theorem, e.g., see
[
7

or
[
2
, Lemma V.6.3]

1.2
, it suffices to show that
if a smooth map
f
satisfies the Mather normal crossing condition, then it is infinitesimally stable at every finite subset of
f^{-1}(q)
for every

, as in view of Theorem

and Theorem

Suppose

be a finite set of points for some

with

. Let

and

denote the dimensions of

and

respectively. Suppose that

. Then

of singular points of

, and the set

of regular points of

. The differential
df
of

at any regular point

in

is surjective, which implies that the infinitesimal stability equation
df(u_{k})+v(f)=w_{k}
in (
1
) is always solvable for

given

and

. Consequently, infinitesimal stability of

at

is equivalent to infinitesimal stability of

of its singular points. Thus, in the case

Let

. Let

Then there are coordinates

is given by

(2)

[y_{k}\circ f]_{p_{\ell}}=\begin{cases}f_{k}^{(\ell)}(x_{1}^{\ell},\ldots,x_{m}^{\ell})&\text{if $k\in I_{\ell}$,}\\
x_{\sigma_{\ell}(k)}^{\ell}&\text{if
$k\in\bar{I}_{\ell}$},\end{cases}

e.g., see
[
1
, p.161]
,
where
\sigma_{\ell}\colon\bar{I}_{\ell}\to\{1,\ldots,m\}
is an injective map of sets. Put
L_{\ell}=\{1,\ldots,m\}\setminus\sigma_{\ell}(\bar{I}_{\ell})
.
We need to show that for any germ vector fields
w^{\ell}(x^{\ell})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}^{\ell}(x^{\ell})\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}
along
f
at
p_{\ell}
for all
\ell=1,\ldots,s
, there are germ vector fields
u^{\ell}(x^{\ell})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}u_{i}^{\ell}(x^{\ell})\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}^{\ell}}
on
M
at
p_{\ell}
, as well as a germ vector field
v(y)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}(y)\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}
at
q
on
N
such that there are equalities of germs

df^{(\ell)}(u^{\ell})+v(f^{(\ell)})=w^{\ell}

at
p_{\ell}
. In other words, we need to show that for any germ functions
w_{i}^{\ell}
at
p_{\ell}
, there are germ functions
u_{i}^{\ell}
at
p_{\ell}
, as well as germ functions
v_{i}
at
q
such that

(3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial f^{(\ell)}_{k}}{\partial x_{1}^{\ell}}u_{1}^{\ell}+\cdots+\frac{\partial f_{k}^{(\ell)}}{\partial x_{m}^{\ell}}u_{m}^{\ell}+v_{k}(f_{1}^{(\ell)},\ldots,f_{n}^{(\ell)})&=w_{k}^{\ell}\qquad\text{if $k\in I_{\ell}$},\\
u_{\sigma_{\ell}(k)}^{\ell}+v_{k}(f^{(\ell)}_{1},\ldots,f^{(\ell)}_{n})&=w_{k}^{\ell}\qquad\text{if
$k\in\bar{I}_{\ell}$}.\end{aligned}\right.

Put
c_{k}=v_{k}(0,\ldots,0)
for
k=1,\ldots,n
. Solving the equations of (
3
) indexed by
k\in\bar{I}_{\ell}
for
u^{\ell}_{\sigma_{\ell}(k)}
and substituting into the equations indexed by
k\in I_{\ell}
yields the equivalent system:

(4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \sum_{i\in L_{\ell}}\frac{\partial f^{(\ell)}_{k}}{\partial x_{i}^{\ell}}\tilde{u}_{i}^{\ell}+\tilde{v}_{k}(f^{(\ell)}_{1},\ldots,f^{(\ell)}_{n})-\sum_{j\in\bar{I}_{\ell}}\frac{\partial f^{(\ell)}_{k}}{\partial x_{\sigma_{\ell}(j)}^{\ell}}\tilde{v}_{j}(f^{(\ell)}_{1},\ldots,f^{(\ell)}_{n})&=\tilde{w}_{k}^{\ell}\qquad\text{if $k\in I_{\ell}$},\\
\tilde{u}_{\sigma_{\ell}(k)}^{\ell}+\tilde{v}_{k}(f^{(\ell)}_{1},\ldots,f^{(\ell)}_{n})&=\tilde{w}_{k}^{\ell}\qquad\text{if
$k\in\bar{I}_{\ell}$},\end{aligned}\right.

where
\tilde{v}_{k}(y)=v_{k}(y)-c_{k}
for
k\leq n
,
\tilde{u}^{\ell}_{i}=u^{\ell}_{i}
for
i\in L_{\ell}
, and
\tilde{u}^{\ell}_{\sigma_{\ell}(k)}=u^{\ell}_{\sigma_{\ell}(k)}+c_{k}
for
k\in\bar{I}_{\ell}
, and

\tilde{w}^{\ell}_{k}=\begin{cases}w^{\ell}_{k}&\text{if $k\in\bar{I}_{\ell}$,}\\
w_{k}^{\ell}-\sum_{j\in\bar{I}_{\ell}}\frac{\partial f^{(\ell)}_{k}}{\partial x_{\sigma_{\ell}(j)}^{\ell}}w_{j}^{\ell}-c_{k}+\sum_{i\in\bar{I}_{\ell}}c_{i}\frac{\partial f^{\ell}_{k}}{\partial x^{\ell}_{\sigma_{\ell}(i)}}&\text{if $k\in I_{\ell}$.}\end{cases}

Since
\tilde{v}_{k}
belongs to the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{q}
of function germs vanishing at
0
, by Mather’s lemma
[
4
, p. 134]
, the system (
4
) can be solved for all
\tilde{w}_{i}^{\ell}
if and only if the
reduced infinitesimal stability equations
(
5
) can be solved for all
\tilde{w}_{i}^{\ell}
:

(5)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \sum_{i\in L_{\ell}}\frac{\partial f^{(\ell)}_{k}}{\partial x_{i}^{\ell}}\tilde{u}_{i}^{\ell}+\tilde{v}_{k}(f^{(\ell)}_{1},\ldots,f^{(\ell)}_{n})&=\tilde{w}_{k}^{\ell}\qquad\text{if $k\in I_{\ell}$},\\
\tilde{u}_{\sigma_{\ell}(k)}^{\ell}+\tilde{v}_{k}(f^{(\ell)}_{1},\ldots,f^{(\ell)}_{n})&=\tilde{w}^{\ell}_{k}\qquad\text{if $k\in\bar{I}_{\ell}$}.\end{aligned}\right.

To summarize,
f
is infinitesimally stable at
S
if and only if for all germ functions
\tilde{w}_{i}^{\ell}
at
p_{\ell}
, there are germ functions
u_{i}^{\ell}
and
v_{k}
that solve the reduced infinitesimal stability equations (
5
). Since
f
is infinitesimally stable at
p_{\ell}
, for the germ functions
\tilde{w}_{i}^{\ell}(x^{\ell})
at
p_{\ell}
, there are germ functions
a_{i}^{\ell}(x^{\ell})
at
p_{\ell}
, and
b_{k}^{\ell}(y)
at
q
such that the reduced infinitesimal stability equations hold:

\left\{\begin{aligned} \sum_{i\in L_{\ell}}\frac{\partial f^{(\ell)}_{k}}{\partial x_{i}^{\ell}}a^{\ell}_{i}+b^{\ell}_{k}(f_{1}^{(\ell)},\ldots,f^{(\ell)}_{n})&=\tilde{w}_{k}^{\ell}\qquad\text{if $k\in I_{\ell}$},\\
a_{\sigma_{\ell}(k)}^{\ell}+b_{k}^{\ell}(f^{(\ell)}_{1},\ldots,f^{(\ell)}_{n})&=\tilde{w}_{k}^{\ell}\qquad\text{if $k\in\bar{I}_{\ell}$}.\end{aligned}\right.

We define the germ functions
v_{i}
at
q
on
N
, and then define the germ functions
u_{i}
on
M
by

\tilde{v}_{k}=\begin{cases}b_{k}^{\ell}&\text{if $k\in I_{\ell}$,}\\
0&\text{if $k=i_{1}+\cdots+i_{s}+1,\ldots,n$},\end{cases}\qquad\tilde{u}_{i}^{\ell}=\begin{cases}\tilde{w}_{j}^{\ell}-\tilde{v}_{j}(f^{\ell})&\text{if
$i=\sigma_{\ell}(j)$ with $j\in\bar{I}_{\ell}$},\\
a_{i}^{\ell}&\text{if $i\in L_{\ell}$.}\end{cases}

The functions
\tilde{v}_{k}
are well-defined since the sets
I_{\ell}
are disjoint and
\cup I_{\ell}=\{1,\ldots,i_{1}+\cdots+i_{s}\}
. The functions
\tilde{u}^{\ell}_{i}
are well-defined since
L_{\ell}
is disjoint from
\sigma_{\ell}(\bar{I}_{\ell})
and the union of
L_{\ell}
and
\sigma_{\ell}(\bar{I}_{\ell})
is the set
\{1,\ldots,m\}
.
The so defined germ functions
\tilde{v}_{i}
and
\tilde{u}_{i}^{\ell}
solve the required system of equations (
5
) for any germ functions
\tilde{w}_{i}^{\ell}
.

Nonlinear refrigerator with a finite-sized cold heat bath
I. Iyyappan, Yuki Izumida, Sibasish Ghosh

Abstract
We study the refrigerator working between a finite-sized cold heat bath and an infinite-sized hot heat bath (environment) in the nonlinear response regime. We assume that the initial temperature T i T_{i} of the finite-sized cold heat bath satisfies T i ≤ T h T_{i}\leq T_{h} , where T h T_{h} is the temperature of the hot heat bath. By consuming the input power, the refrigerator transfers the heat from a finite-sized cold heat bath to the hot heat bath. Hence, the temperature of the finite-sized cold heat bath decreases until it reaches the desired low-temperature T f T_{f} . By minimizing the input work for the heat transport process, we derive the optimal path for temperature change. We calculate the coefficient of performance as a function of average input power. We also obtain the bounds for the coefficient of performance by applying the asymmetric dissipation limits.

VI
Conclusions

In this work, we have studied the performance of minimally nonlinear irreversible refrigerators operating between a finite-sized cold heat bath and an infinite-sized hot heat bath (environment). We assume that initially the temperature of the finite-sized cold heat bath is equal to or smaller than that of the environment. The refrigerator starts to transport heat from a finite-sized cold heat bath to a hot environment by consuming the input power. Using the minimally nonlinear irreversible model and the Euler-Lagrange equation, we find the optimal temperature variation (see Eq. (
21
)), which minimizes the input work. We obtained the COP as a function of average input power and exergy. When we apply the asymmetric dissipation limits, we get the lower and upper bounds of the COP (see Eqs. (
31
) and (
32
)). We find that the lower bound of COP (Eq. (
32
)) coincides with the result of Ref.
ron040
obtained for linear irreversible refrigerators. We also find the COP in the symmetric dissipation case (see Eq. (
33
)).

Our results show that the COP of the refrigerator decreases when the average input power increases. If we increase the exergy, the COP of the refrigerator also increases. When the exergy decreases, the COP decreases with average input power nonlinearly. However, for a larger value of exergy, the COP decreases with average input power almost linearly. We also find that the COP increases with exergy for a given average input power.

Since our study includes the finite-size effect of the cold bath and dissipation effect into the irreversible refrigerator model, it can be useful for the design and operation of actual refrigerators. By minimizing the dissipation strength
\gamma_{h}
one can enhance the COP (see Eq. (
31
) and Fig.
2
) that is realizable by carefully designing the parameter values
T_{h}/L_{11}\approx\gamma_{c}
(see Eq. (
10

Protocol for preparation of mouse synovium for flow cytometry and RNA-seq
Authors: Anna B Montgomery, Carla M Cuda, Salina Dominguez, Hadijat Makinde, Alexander V Misharin, Yidan Wang, Adriana Yunda de Miguel, Deborah R Winter, Harris Perlman
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
SummaryThe mouse synovium can be used to model the sterile inflammation observed in rheumatoid arthritis and osteoarthritis. Here, we present a protocol for inducing arthritis and harvesting and preparing a single-cell suspension from cells of interest from the mouse synovium in steady state or inflammation, with or without intravascular labeling. The prepared single-cell suspension can then be used for downstream analyses, including flow cytometry, fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS), both bulk and single-cell RNA sequencing, and other genomic assays.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol please refer to Montgomery et al.1 and Montgomery et al.2Subject areas: cell Biology, cell isolation, single cell, flow cytometry, RNA-seq, immunology, model organisms

Before you begin

This protocol describes the specific steps to prepare a single-cell suspension from mouse synovium. This protocol was optimized using C57Bl/6 mice but can be used in any mouse line. This protocol was primarily optimized using synovium from hind paws but can be adapted to knees (see adaptations in the text).

Innovation

This protocol enables isolation of synovial cells from intact hindjoint or knee joints, reducing the need for advanced dissection techniques or dissection tools. As a result this protocol is readily adoptable in any lab and yields highly reproducible results.

Label intravascular cells

Timing: 1–2 h

Here we describe how to label intra-vascular immune cells prior to tissue collection, for later identification using flow-cytometry.

1.Prepare in vivo-antibody to a final concentration of 6 μg/mouse in 200 μL sterile PBS.

Prepare in vivo-antibody to a final concentration of 6 μg/mouse in 200 μL sterile PBS.

Note: This may need to be titrated to account for variations in weight and flow cytometric acquisition methods.

Note: This protocol was optimized using anti-CD45 BUV661 antibody, but any fluorescently labeled antibody could be substituted.

Note: Fill syringes for individual mice to ensure accurate dosing.

Note: Use in intravascular labeling and perfusion enables identification of remaining adherent vascular cells. Without perfusion, all vascular cells will be labeled and identified. Perfusion can be used or not depending on the specific research question.

CRITICAL: Timing of preparation of in vivo labeling antibody is dependent on the required experimental timings. Prepare antibody fresh prior to administration, before animals are scheduled to be euthanized.

Harvest joints

Timing: 1–2 h

5.Euthanize mouse using relevant approved procedures.

Euthanize mouse using relevant approved procedures.

Isolate cells

Timing: 2 h

Here we describe the dissection of joints of interest, and the steps to isolate synovial cells from intact joints.

Using tooth forceps, peel away the skin gently, adjusting grip on the foot accordingly to limit pressure on the foot (Figures 2C–2E).Note: It is essential to avoid breaking the foot bones, as this will allow contaminating cells to enter the suspension.d.Continue holding the foot flat and with tooth forceps remove toes, by first breaking the joints and then pulling (Figure 2F).e.When all skin and toes are removed, place cleaned joint in a petri dish.

Quantification and statistical analysis

Flow cytometry analysis requires correct compensation applied. For this study, compensation was calculated and applied using FACSDiva software prior to acquisition on BD Symphony Analyzer. Compensation can also be applied after acquisition using FlowJo software. Analysis of flow cytometry data was also carried out in FlowJo, before statistical analysis in Microsoft Excel and/or Graphpad Prism.

Limitations

With this protocol there is a possible risk of contamination from surrounding muscle tissue or bone marrow cells, or the fat pad when extracting synovium from knees. Care should be taken during processing steps to extract synovial cells only. This is essential in step ‘Isolate cells 4a’ to ensure only the synovium is in contact with the mesh.

Troubleshooting

Problem 1: Low cell count

Potential solution: Expected total cell yield from 2 processed synovium of hindpaws is ∼4 × 106 in steady state – 10 × 106 in inflammatory arthritis. If cell counts are significantly lower than this, digestion may not have been successful. To remedy this, ensure the synovium is fully exposed before putting samples in the shaking incubator, and apply gentle pressure to the nylon mesh during the rubbing steps to ensure all cells are disaggregated from the compartment.

Problem 2: Low cell viability

Potential solution: If a high proportion of cell death is observed, this could indicate an overly aggressive digest. Ensure digest buffers are made correctly, and tissues are not left in digest buffers for longer than detailed in the protocol. If many samples are being prepared together, stagger incubation times accordingly so the first samples prepared are not over-digested.

Problem 3: Cell or debris contamination

Potential solution: If care is not taken to isolate synovial cells only, contaminating cells or debris such as bone fragments could enter the single-cell suspension. If this occurs, proportions of cells observed in flow cytometry analysis will be skewed from normal (represented above) and instead contain increased proportions of CD45- cells that will also appear larger in the FSC and SSC axis. To resolve this issue, ensure mechanical digestion is not unnecessarily aggressive (e.g., in shaking incubator or during rubbing on nylon mesh) to limit digestion of unwanted tissue. The aim of this protocol is to gently disassociate the synovium using enzymatic digestion, enabling easy disaggregation from tissue on nylon mesh.

Large- n n O ⁡ ( n ) O(n) with long-range interactions: integrability and resonance dynamics
Guido Giachetti, Nicolò Defenu

Abstract
We study the the large- n n dynamics of the long-range quantum O ⁡ ( n ) O(n) model, focusing on the strong long-range regime α < d \alpha<d . The dynamics of the model exhibits non-trivial features on mesoscopic timescales t ∼ ln ⁡ N t\sim\ln N , due to the activation of parametric resonances of the nearly degenerate quantum modes. By using recent results establishing the integrability of the large- n n limit, we derive the resonance conditions, and construct the reduced multi-mode Hamiltonian that captures the finite-size dynamics. This framework yields the resonance phase diagram and clarifies when and how deviations from mean-field behavior arise. In particular, the presence of multiple resonant modes enhances the logarithmic growth of entanglement and leads to spatially modulated correlations.

II
The model

We introduce here the quantum
O(n)
model and discuss the effect of long-range interactions. Without loss of generality, we can restrict ourselves to the
d=1
case. We thus consider a quantum chain with
N
sites: each site
j
corresponds to a vector variable of bosonic components
n

\bm{\Phi}_{j}
and its conjugate momentum
\bm{\Pi}_{j}
(
[\Phi^{a}_{j},\Pi^{a^{\prime}}_{j^{\prime}}]=\text{i}\ \delta_{r,r^{\prime}}\delta_{j,j^{\prime}}
, with
a,a^{\prime}=1,\dots,n
). The dynamics of the model is given by a long-range quadratic hopping and a local,
O(n)
-symmetric, quartic potential

H=\sum_{j}\frac{1}{2}\bm{\Pi}^{2}_{j}+\frac{1}{2N_{\alpha}}\sum_{j\neq j^{\prime}}\frac{\left(\mathbf{\Phi}_{j}-\mathbf{\Phi}_{j^{\prime}}\right)^{2}}{|j-j^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}+\sum_{j}\frac{r}{2}\bm{\Phi}^{2}_{j}+\frac{\lambda}{2n}(\bm{\Phi}^{2}_{j})^{2}\,.

(1)

Here
r
represents the bare mass of the theory,
\lambda>0
the quartic
O(n)
coupling,
\alpha>0
the decay exponent of the interaction and
N_{\alpha}=\sum_{j>0}j^{-\alpha}
is the Kac scaling
Kac et al. 1963
. Let us notice that, while for
\alpha>1
,
N_{\alpha}
just fixes the energy scale of the excitation, for
\alpha<1
its presence is fundamental to ensure the extensity of the thermodynamic quantities
Campa et al. 2014

The range of the interaction can be tuned between the nearest-neighbors limit (
\alpha=\infty
) and the fully connected limit (
\alpha=0
). For
\alpha>3
the hopping term can be seen as a discretized version of the Laplacian operator, so that Hamiltonian (
1
) is the lattice version of the usual short-range
O(n)
bosonic field theory. For
\alpha<3
, the ground state of the model undergoes a transition between a massive (
\left\langle\bm{\Phi}\right\rangle\neq 0
) and a massless (
\left\langle\bm{\Phi}\right\rangle=0
) phase
Sachdev 1999
, where
\left\langle\cdot\right\rangle
denotes the expectation value over a quantum state. We will consider the following protocol: for
t<0
, we choose
r^{-}>0
, and assume the system to be in its massless ground state; then at
t=0
the bare mass is quenched to its (possibly negative) final value
r
. In particular, here we will focus on the strong-long-range regime
\alpha<1
.

The dynamics of the model becomes tractable in the limit
n\rightarrow\infty
, under the condition that the initial state is factorized and homogeneous over both the lattice and the component indices
Cugliandolo 2013
: in this case, for
n\rightarrow\infty
, such a factorization is maintained at any time

, so that it is possible to replace
\bm{\Phi}^{2}_{j}\Phi^{a}_{j}\rightarrow n\left\langle\Phi^{2}_{a}\right\rangle\Phi^{2}_{j}
in the Heisenberg equations of motion (e.o.m.) coming from Eq. (
1

In the large-
n
limit the e.o.m. can thus be written explicitly in Fourier space

\dot{\Phi}_{k}=\Pi_{k}^{\dagger},\ \ \dot{\Pi}_{k}=-(r+\lambda\left\langle\Phi^{2}\right\rangle+\omega_{k}^{2})\Phi_{k}^{\dagger},

(2)

as each component evolves identically and independently from the others, the internal field index
a\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
has been dropped. Here
k=2\pi\nu/N
,
\nu=-N/2+1,\cdots,N/2
and

\Phi_{k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{j}e^{\text{i}kj}\Phi_{j}\hskip 14.22636pt\Pi_{k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{j}e^{-\text{i}kj}\Pi_{j}

(3)

are the bosonic Fourier modes (
[\Phi_{k},\Pi_{k^{\prime}}]=\text{i}\ \delta_{k^{\prime}k}
);
\omega_{k}^{2}
is the lattice dispersion relation

\omega_{k}^{2}=\frac{1}{2N_{\alpha}}\sum_{j>0}\frac{1-\cos(kj)}{j^{\alpha}}\,.

(4)

Physically speaking, we can interpret the system of Eq. (
2
) as a collection of
N
bosons, coupled through a collective, time-dependent, classical degree of freedom

m^{2}(t)=r+\lambda\left\langle\Phi^{2}\right\rangle=r+\frac{\lambda}{N}\sum_{k}\left\langle\Phi^{\dagger}_{k}\Phi_{k}\right\rangle

(5)

which plays the role of the renormalized mass. For constant
m
, the system reduces to a set of independent bosonic modes, but for a time-dependent
m(t)

m^{2}(t)
need not be positive at any time, while

in any stationary state.

The dramatic simplification at the level of the dynamics can be appreciated by noticing that the time-evolution of second order correlators can be cast in terms of a closed set of ODE. In particular, if we parameterize the second order moments as
|\eta_{k}|^{2}\equiv\left\langle\Phi^{\dagger}_{k}\Phi_{k}\right\rangle
,
2\Re(\eta^{*}_{k}p_{k})\equiv\left\langle\Pi_{k}\Phi_{k}+\text{h.c.}\right\rangle

|p_{k}|^{2}\equiv\left\langle\Pi^{\dagger}_{k}\Pi_{k}\right\rangle
, the e.o.m. (
2

\dot{\eta}_{k}=p_{k},\hskip 14.22636pt\dot{p}_{k}=-\left(r+\omega_{k}^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{N}\sum_{k}|\eta_{k}|^{2}\right)\eta_{k}\,.

(6)

These equations can be derived from the classical Hamiltonian

\mathcal{H}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k}\left(|p^{2}_{k}|+(r+\omega^{2}_{k})|\eta^{2}_{k}|\right)+\frac{\lambda}{4N}\left(\sum_{k}|\eta^{2}_{k}|\right)^{2}\,.

(7)

Let us notice that the range of the interaction enters the e.o.m. and Eq. (
7
) only through the dispersion
\omega_{k}
: in particular, the local field theory limit is recovered by inserting the dispersion relation
\omega_{k}\sim k
in Eqs. (
2

6

0<\alpha<1
instead, the spectrum remains discrete even in the thermodynamic limit

and it is more conveniently parameterized by the integer index
\nu

\omega_{\infty}^{2}=1

\omega_{\nu}^{2}=1-(1-\alpha)\pi^{\alpha-1}\int_{0}^{\pi}dx\frac{\cos(\nu x)}{x^{\alpha}}

(8)

while
\omega_{0}=0
as required by the translational invariance of the model. In the
\alpha=0

III
Thermodynamic limit

The peculiar form of the dispersion of the excitations
\omega_{\nu}
in Eq. (
8

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\nu}G(\omega^{2}_{\nu})=G(1)+O(N^{-\zeta}),

As a consequence, if one assume that

2
-dimensional quartic central potential. It is convenient to reparametrize the accumulation point coordinate

Note that

The variables

The thermodynamic limit can thus be interpreted as a mean-field limit in which correlations between different sites are suppressed.

\eta_{\nu}(t)=\sqrt{\frac{U_{\nu}(t)}{2\Delta_{\nu}}}\left(A_{\nu}^{+}\ e^{\sqrt{-\Delta_{\nu}}\int^{t}_{0}dt^{\prime}\ U^{-1}_{\nu}(t^{\prime})}-A_{\nu}^{-}\ e^{-\sqrt{-\Delta_{\nu}}\int^{t}_{0}dt^{\prime}\ U^{-1}_{\nu}(t^{\prime})}\right)\,,

(25)

where

and

are constants. As expected, the solution (

(26)

are the eigenvalues of the Floquet-Hill problem, which become purely imaginary for the off-resonant case. The theoretical estimate of Eq. (

) is compared with its numerical counterpart in Fig.

, finding excellent agreement. In particular in the off-resonant case
\Delta_{\mu}>0
(
\lambda^{F}_{\nu}=\text{i}\ \Omega_{\nu}^{F}
imaginary) we have that the constants are linked to the integral of motion as

\frac{1}{2}\left(|A^{+}_{\nu}|^{2}+|A^{-}_{\nu}|^{2}\right)=\epsilon_{\nu}\hskip 28.45274pt\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\Delta_{\nu}}}\left(|A^{+}_{\nu}|^{2}-|A^{-}_{\nu}|^{2}\right)=\ell_{\nu}

(27)

while, if
\Delta_{\nu}<0
and the mode is resonant (
\lambda_{\nu}
real), one has

\Re\left(A^{+*}_{\nu}A^{-}_{\nu}\right)=\epsilon_{\nu},\hskip 28.45274pt\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\Delta_{\nu}|}}\Im\left(A^{+*}_{\nu}A^{-}_{\nu}\right)=\ell_{\nu}\,.

(28)

In this latter case the resonance will thus activate on a timescale
t_{\nu}=(2\lambda_{\nu})^{-1}\ln N
, so that the period after which the system leaves the linear regime is

t_{\rm lin}=\min_{\nu}(2\lambda_{\nu})^{-1}\ln N\,.

(29)

Within the context of long-range physics, this can be also interpreted as the Ehrenfest timescale
t_{\rm Ehr}
for which the semiclassical-mean field, approximation is valid
Lerose and Pappalardi 2020
;
Defenu et al. 2020
: in the non-resonant regime one has instead that
t_{\rm Ehr}
is polynomial in
N
, as this is the time-scale in which the incoherent dynamics of non-resonant quantum fluctuations can affect the dynamics of macroscopic observables.

VI
Beyond the linear regime

In the resonant case, on timescales
t_{\rm Ehr}\sim\ln N

\mathcal{N}
resonances, it is convenient to redefine
p_{\nu}=\sqrt{N}\ \bar{p}_{\nu}

\eta_{\nu}=\sqrt{N}\ \bar{\eta}_{\nu}

\nu=0,\cdots,\mathcal{N}-1

\bar{\eta}_{\mathcal{N}}\equiv\bar{\eta}

VII
Correlations and entanglement spreading

As the mean-field degree of freedom
\bar{\eta}
is linked to one-site dynamics, we expect quantum correlation between different sites only to arise due to resonances. This expectation is indeed confirmed by the analysis of Ref.
Giachetti and Defenu 2023
. In this section, we revise the link between entanglement spreading and the presence of resonances and derive semi-analytical expression for the entanglement spreading. The presence of resonances thus gradually drives the system away from the mean field description, which is completely washed away in the limit of an extensive
\mathcal{N}\sim N
.

As a measure of the quantum spatial correlations we will consider the entanglement entropy
S
associated with a bipartition of the system into an interval
A
of length
L_{A}
and its complement. It is defined as
S=-\mathrm{tr}\rho_{A}\ln\rho_{A}
, where
\rho_{A}=\mathrm{tr}_{A}\rho
is the reduced density matrix associated with the subsystem
A
. In the case of periodic boundary conditions, the exact position of the interval is not relevant. The entropy
S(t)
can be in turn expressed in terms of the two-point correlations of the bosonic operators
\Phi_{j}
,
\Pi_{j}
,

\gamma=\text{Re}\begin{pmatrix}\left\langle\Phi_{j}(t)\Phi_{j^{\prime}}(t)\right\rangle&\left\langle\Phi_{j}(t)\Pi_{j^{\prime}}(t)\right\rangle\\
\left\langle\Pi_{j}(t)\Phi_{j^{\prime}}(t)\right\rangle&\left\langle\Pi_{j}(t)\Pi_{j^{\prime}}(t)\right\rangle\end{pmatrix}

(44)

by following the procedure of Ref.
Casini and Huerta 2009
. This is a consequence of two facts:
(i)
for a ground state quench the initial state
\rho(0)
is a bosonic Gaussian state,
(ii)
in the
n\rightarrow\infty
limit, the state of system remains Gaussian at any time
t
. In particular, we have
S(t)=\sum_{n}s(\sigma_{n})
where

s(\sigma)=\left(\sigma+\frac{1}{2}\right)\ln\left(\sigma+\frac{1}{2}\right)-\left(\sigma-\frac{1}{2}\right)\ln\left(\sigma-\frac{1}{2}\right),

(45)

and
\sigma_{n}
are the symplectic eigenvalues of the matrix
\gamma
in Eq. (
44
), reduced on the subsistem
j,j^{\prime}\in A
. More explicitly, if we denote such a reduced correlation matrix as
\gamma_{\rm red}
, the
\sigma_{n}
are a set of
L_{A}
positive numbers which are eigenvalues of
\textit{i}\mathbb{J}\gamma_{\rm red}
,
\mathbb{J}
being the symplectic identity

\mathbb{J}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\mathbb{I}_{L}\\
-\mathbb{I}_{L}&0\end{pmatrix}\,.

(46)

In the limit of large size
N
, the matrix
\gamma_{\rm red}
will be dominated by the classical mode
\bar{\eta}
and the resonant ones, as the latter becomes
O(\sqrt{N})
on
t\sim\ln N
. More explicitly

\begin{split}\left\langle\Phi_{j}\Phi_{j^{\prime}}\right\rangle=|\bar{\eta}^{2}|\ \delta_{j^{\prime}j}+\sum_{\nu=0}^{\mathcal{N}-1}&(|\bar{\eta}^{2}_{\nu}|-N^{-1}|\bar{\eta}^{2}|)e^{2\pi\text{i}\nu(j-j^{\prime})/N},\hskip 5.69046pt\left\langle\Pi_{j}\Pi_{j^{\prime}}\right\rangle=|\bar{p}^{2}|\ \delta_{j^{\prime}j}+\sum_{\nu=0}^{\mathcal{N}-1}(|\bar{p}^{2}_{\nu}|-N^{-1}|\bar{p}^{2}|)e^{2\pi\text{i}\nu(j-j^{\prime})/N},\\
\Re\left\langle\Pi_{j}\Phi_{j^{\prime}}\right\rangle&=\Re(\bar{p}^{*}\bar{\eta})\ \delta_{j^{\prime}j}+\sum_{\nu=0}^{\mathcal{N}-1}(\Re(\bar{p}_{\nu}^{*}\bar{\eta}_{\nu})-N^{-1}\Re(\bar{p}^{*}\bar{\eta}))e^{2\pi\text{i}\nu(x-x^{\prime})/N}.\end{split}

(47)

Different regimes appear

•

Case

\mathcal{N}=0
: in this case the correlations in Eqs. (
47
) are all diagonal and
\sigma^{2}_{n}=|\bar{\eta}|^{2}|\bar{p}|^{2}-\Re(\bar{p}^{*}\bar{\eta})^{2}=\Im(\bar{p}^{*}\bar{\eta})=\ell^{2}
, so that has
\sigma_{n}=\ell
for any
n=1,\cdots,L_{A}
. As
S(t)
remains constant, no entanglement is generated. Since for a ground state quench
\ell=1/2
,
S(t)\equiv 0
as expected from Eq. (
45
).

•

Case

\mathcal{N}=1
: in this case we have

\left\langle\Phi_{j}\Phi_{j^{\prime}}\right\rangle=|\bar{\eta}^{2}|\delta_{j^{\prime}j}+|\bar{\eta}^{2}_{0}|-N^{-1}|\bar{\eta}^{2}|
(and analogously for the momentum and cross correlations). Only for
n=1
,
\sigma_{n}\neq 1/2
and the entanglement contribution is given by

\sigma_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1-2\rho(1-\rho)+4L_{A}(1-\rho)\chi(t)}

(48)

where
\rho=L_{A}/N
and

\chi_{1}(t)=|\bar{p}_{0}^{2}\bar{\eta}_{1}^{2}|+|\bar{p}^{2}_{1}\bar{\eta}_{0}^{2}|-2\Re(\bar{p}_{1}^{*}\bar{\eta}_{1})\Re(\bar{p}_{0}^{*}\bar{\eta}_{0})

(49)

represents the overlap between the resonant mode
\bar{\eta}_{0}
and the classical one
\bar{\eta}_{1}\equiv\bar{\eta}
. Here,
S(t)\approx 0
at any time when
\bar{\eta}_{0}
is small, so that quantum correlations follow a quasi-periodic pattern, on a timescale
\sim\ln N
. When the resonance is active
\chi_{1}(t)=O(1)
, and
\sigma_{1}=O(L^{1/2})
. In particular, for
L_{A}\gg 1
we find

S(t)\sim\frac{1}{2}\ln L_{A}(1-\rho)\chi_{1}(t)

(50)

so that the maximum entangled generated is order
1/2\ln L_{A}
, as observed also for long-range spin systems
Lerose and Pappalardi 2020
.

•

Case

\mathcal{N}>1
: the permutational symmetry is effectively broken and the correlations acquire a spatial modulation, making an explicit computation of
S(t)
impossible. However, close expressions can be derived in the regime
L_{A}\gg 1
. Indeed, while the spatial modulation of the modes can no longer be ignored for
L_{A}=O(N)
, a principal component analysis of the
L_{A}
by
L_{A}
correlation matrix
\gamma_{\rm red}
reveals that only a number
\lfloor\mathcal{N}/2\rfloor
of
O(L)
eigenvalues
\sigma_{n}
actually contributes to the entropy. In particular, as shown in App.
C
, we have

S(t)\sim\frac{1}{2}\ln\text{pdet}\ \Gamma(t)

(51)

where
pdet
denotes the pseudo-determinant of the
\mathcal{N}
by
\mathcal{N}
, skew symmetric matrix

\Gamma_{\mu\nu}=N\frac{\sin\pi\rho(\mu-\nu)}{\pi(\mu-\nu)}\left(\bar{\eta}_{\mu}\bar{p}_{\nu}-\bar{\eta}_{\nu}\bar{p}_{\mu}\right)\,.

(52)

In particular, for
\mathcal{N}=2
one has

S(t)\sim\ln L_{A}\left(\bar{\eta}_{1}\bar{p}_{2}-\bar{\eta}_{2}\bar{p}_{1}\right)\text{sinc}(\pi\rho)

(53)

while for
\mathcal{N}=3

S(t)\sim\frac{1}{2}\ln L^{2}_{A}\sum^{2}_{\mu>\nu}(\bar{\eta}_{\mu}\bar{p}_{\nu}-\bar{\eta}_{\nu}\bar{p}_{\mu})^{2}\text{sinc}^{2}(\pi(\mu-\nu)\rho)\,.

(54)

Finally, in the regime
1\ll L_{A}\ll N
our analysis reveals the trend

S(t)\sim\frac{1}{2}\ln L^{2}_{A}\sum^{\mathcal{N}-1}_{\mu>\nu}(\bar{\eta}_{\mu}\bar{p}_{\nu}-\bar{\eta}_{\nu}\bar{p}_{\mu})^{2}

(55)

valid for all
\mathcal{N}>1
. Notice that, as now the dynamics of the resonant mode is given by the interference of different frequencies,
S(t)
oscillates around a finite value, as a finite amount of entanglement is generated. Moreover, let us notice that in this case the maximum amount of entanglement generated is of order
S(t)\sim\ln L_{A}
.

In summary, quantum resonances are the mechanism that spreads correlations in strong long-range systems, disrupting the mean-field dynamics. Since
S\sim c\ln L_{A}
, entanglement never obeys volume-law as in the short-range limit, in line with the general understanding of strong long-range dynamics
Pappalardi et al. 2018
;
Lerose and Pappalardi 2020
;
Giachetti and Defenu 2023
. In addition, our analysis reveals the importance of the multi-resonant regime: the presence of spatially-modulated resonances (
\mathcal{N}\geq 2
) results in an enhanced entanglement production (
c=1
versus
c=1/2
of the single resonance case). Multi-resonances are also associated with a stable production of entanglement due to the interference among different resonant modes.

VIII
Conclusions

In this work, we developed a comprehensive and quantitative framework for the far-from-equilibrium dynamics of the strong long-range quantum
O(n)
model in the large-
n
limit, expanding the previous results of
Giachetti and Defenu 2023
and generalizing the recent results of
Giachetti et al. 2025
about the integrability of the large-
n
dynamics. This allowed us to understand the interplay between classical ultra-local degrees of freedom and the quantum modes, responsible of the rich dynamics observed on mesoscopic timescales
t_{\rm Ehr}\sim\ln N
. The key mechanism enabling this complexity is the onset of a finite number
\mathcal{N}
of parametric resonances in the quantum modes, triggered by the discrete nature of the long-range dispersion.

In particular, the knowledge of the whole set of the integrals of motion allows to determine the resonance condition and the associated phase diagram, to construct a reduced Hamiltonian governing the resonant sector and to derive the associated spectrum of the model. This approach also provides a unified interpretation of finite-size effects, revealing how strong-long-range systems deviates gradually from mean-field behavior as the number of resonant modes
\mathcal{N}
increases: in particular, while in the strict thermodynamic limit,
N\rightarrow\infty
, an effective invariance under permutations is recovered, the presence of multiple resonances can destroy it within the time-scale
t_{\rm Ehr}
. The resonant sector is also responsible for the spreading of quantum correlations, quantified here by the entanglement entropy
S(t)
. While the quasi-mean field nature prevents the onset of volume-law phases, the presence of multiple resonant modes
\mathcal{N}
enhances both the rate and the stability of entanglement production.

The present framework highlights the role of the
O(n)
model as a paradigmatic setting for understanding quantum dynamics, both in the short-range and long-range regimes, placing the present work within the long line of studies that have used the large-
n
as benchmark for critical phenomena, quench dynamics
Sotiriadis and Cardy 2010
;
Chandran et al. 2013
;
Sciolla and Biroli 2013
;
Maraga et al. 2015
;
Smacchia et al. 2015
. At the same time, the results obtained here also contribute to the broader ongoing program aimed at characterizing the dynamics of quantum systems in the presence of long-range interactions: within this context the exact solution of the
O(n)
model brings a deeper understanding of this class of model and can constitute an ideal starting point for several generalizations and approximations, e.g. the
1/n
expansion
Zinn-Justin 2002
.

Our analysis can be extended to the long-range–to–short-range crossover, understanding how the resonance mechanism and the time-scale separation break down as the decay exponent approaches the weak-long-range regime,
\alpha\to d
. In particular, it would be interesting to investigate the relation among the classical oscillations of the high-energy ultra-local mode and the undamped oscillations observed in the short-range regime of the
O(n)
model
Maraga et al. 2015
, which have recently been related to the presence of high-energy Higgs oscillations coming from beyond the edge the spectral band
Giachetti et al. 2025
.

Finally, the present framework could be expanded to explain the interplay between persistent mean-field oscillations and spatially-modulated quantum modes observed in other strong-long-range models—most notably in semiclassical analyses of the LMG model
Lipkin et al. 1965
(see e.g. Refs.
Lerose et al. 2018
;
Pappalardi et al. 2018
). As the interplay between semiclassical chaos and emergent integrability in long-range many-body systems remains largely unexplored, clarifying whether such oscillations can be universally interpreted as manifestations of resonant or near-integrable sectors would significantly unify the phenomenology of long-range models.

Longitudinal evaluation of optical coherence tomography parameters for detecting glaucoma progression across disease severities
Authors: James Wen Siang Lim, Norlina Ramli
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeTo evaluate the longitudinal performance of multiple Optical Coherence Tomography (OCT) parameters in detecting glaucoma progression and compare their effectiveness with visual field (VF) parameters across disease stages.MethodsIn this longitudinal study, 59 glaucoma patients (mean follow-up: 8.51 years) underwent ≥ 5 serial OCT and VF assessments. Primary structural parameters included global RNFL, macular ganglion cell layer (mGCL) and macular retinal thickness (mRT). Progression was defined by significant thinning trends (OCT) or worsening VF indices: mean deviation or pattern standard deviation. Detection rates were compared overall and by disease severity and evaluated using survival analysis.ResultsOCT detected progression in significantly more eyes than VF (84.7% vs. 45.8%; p < 0.001). Among structural measures, mGCL demonstrated the highest progression detection rate (61.0%), followed by global RNFL (59.3%) and mRT (57.6%). In mild glaucoma, OCT significantly outperformed VF (87.5% vs. 50.0%; p < 0.001), with mGCL and inferior mRT demonstrating greater sensitivity at earlier stages (p < 0.05). In moderate-to-severe disease, OCT detection remained higher, though not statistically significant. Survival analysis demonstrated earlier and more frequent progression detection with OCT, with divergence increasing over time. By 10 years, structural parameters identified more progressing eyes than VF (57.0% vs. 38.6%). Combining structural and functional criteria did not significantly improve detection beyond OCT alone.ConclusionLong-term trend-based analysis demonstrates that structural OCT parameters detect glaucoma progression earlier and more frequently than VF testing, particularly in mild disease. These findings support prioritizing mGCL analysis alongside traditional RNFL imaging in routine glaucoma monitoring.Keywords: Disease progression, Glaucoma, Optical coherence tomography, Retinal ganglion cells, Visual fields

Background

Glaucoma, a leading cause of irreversible blindness, affecting approximately 3.5% of adults aged 40–80 years, is characterized by progressive optic atrophy and visual field loss. The global burden is projected to rise from 76 million in 2020 to 111.8 million by 2040 [1].

Timely detection and monitoring of progression are critical, yet progression remains challenging to confirm due to the lack of a gold standard [2, 3]. While standard automated perimetry (SAP) is the conventional tool, studies reveal structural damage can precede functional loss [4, 5].

Advancements in Optical Coherence Tomography (OCT) have enhanced the ability to assess progression, offering greater accuracy in detecting damage in retinal nerve fiber layer (RNFL), optic nerve head (ONH), and macula [6].

Unlike prior studies that focused on early-stage disease, this study provides a long-term, real-world evaluation of OCT parameters across the entire spectrum of glaucoma severity, from mild to advanced. We specifically aimed to compare the longitudinal performance of multiple structural OCT parameters against functional visual field testing for detecting progression at different disease stages.

Methods

This retrospective longitudinal study utilized glaucoma clinic records from 2012 to 2023 at a tertiary referral center for glaucoma patients. The study included subjects diagnosed with glaucoma, either open or closed angle according to International Society of Geographical and Epidemiological Ophthalmology (ISGEO) Classification [7]. All had glaucomatous optic disc and RNFL defects corresponding to reproducible VF loss. A confirmed diagnosis of glaucoma must have been established for at least six months prior to enrollment in the study. Moreover, participants were required to have attended at least five clinic visits where VF and OCT assessments were conducted. These assessments were spaced at intervals of no less than six months to allow for sufficient monitoring of changes over time. If both eyes qualified, one was randomly selected.

Exclusion criteria included the presence of any other ocular or systemic diseases which could potentially contribute to visual field loss or affect macular thickness. Those with prior retinal surgeries or significant cataract resulting in drop of visual acuity of more than 2 lines on the Snellen chart with clinical confirmation were also excluded.

Visual field testing used the Humphrey Field Analyzer (Carl Zeiss Meditec) with 24–2 SITA standard. Reliable tests had fixation losses < 20% and false-positive/negative errors < 33% [8]. Staging followed Bascom Palmer Glaucoma Staging System: mild (mean deviation (MD) better than − 6.00 dB), moderate (MD -6.01 dB to -12.00 dB), advanced (MD -12.01 dB to -20.00 dB), and severe (MD -20.01 dB or worse) [9].

Subjects underwent Spectralis SD-OCT (Heidelberg Engineering, Germany), using the Glaucoma Module Premium Edition. TruTrack was used for alignment, and each B-scan was averaged 9–11 times for enhanced quality [10]. Posterior pole thickness map (PPTM) (61 horizontal B-scans over 20°) was used to assess macular thickness. To minimize segmentation-related artifacts, the Spectralis software segmented retinal layers [11] and scans with quality scores < 25 dB or those showing macular pathology were excluded, and all images were manually reviewed before analysis. The PPTM showed the thickness of the retina in each grid cell. The macular retinal thickness (mRT), macular ganglion cell layer (mGCL) and macular inner plexiform layer (mIPL) thickness were automatically calculated and further divided into mean thickness at the superior and inferior hemispheres. Peripapillary RNFL thickness was analyzed globally and by quadrant (superior, inferior, temporal, nasal).

OCT progression was assessed via thickness trends in global, quadrant RNFL, mRT, and mGCL, and mIPL (including superior/inferior values). Rather than using built-in software, data were manually extracted to include only high-quality images and measurements.

To allow a direct, standardized comparison of structural (OCT) and functional (VF) progression, a unified trend-based framework was applied:

OCT progression: Longitudinal thickness measurements were plotted from baseline to the most recent follow-up. Linear regression was performed with thickness as the dependent variable and time (years) as the independent variable. Progression was defined as statistically significant negative slope over time (p < 0.05) for retinal thickness parameters. (Fig. 1).

OCT progression: Longitudinal thickness measurements were plotted from baseline to the most recent follow-up. Linear regression was performed with thickness as the dependent variable and time (years) as the independent variable. Progression was defined as statistically significant negative slope over time (p < 0.05) for retinal thickness parameters. (Fig. 1).

VF progression: Mean deviation (MD) and pattern standard deviation (PSD) were analyzed similarly. A statistically significant negative MD slope or positive PSD slope (p < 0.05) indicated progression.

Progression in both OCT and VF was thus defined solely by statistically significant longitudinal change, ensuring methodological comparability between structural and functional assessments. Event-based analyses such as Guided Progression Analysis (GPA) were not utilized to maintain comparability.

To reduce the risk of inflated false-positive findings due to multiple comparisons, primary OCT parameters were prespecified based on clinical relevance: global RNFL thickness, mRT and mGCL. Other OCT parameters, including quadrant RNFL measurements, were analyzed as exploratory outcomes, particularly to evaluate incremental sensitivity in advanced disease.

Data was recorded in Microsoft Excel (Version 2018) and analyzed using SPSS Version 26.0. Descriptive statistics summarized demographic and clinical characteristics, including means for continuous variables and frequencies/percentages for categorical variables. Group comparisons were performed using one-way ANOVA for continuous variables, with statistical significance defined as p-value of < 0.05. Paired categorical comparisons, such as progression detection by OCT versus VF, were assessed using the McNemar test. Venn diagrams assessed overlap among progression detection methods, and Kaplan-Meier survival analysis compared time-to-event data.

Comparison of progression detection rates in OCT and VF parameters

Across all eyes, the composite of primary OCT parameters identified progression in 84.7%, a rate significantly higher than that of VF (45.8%, p < 0.001). Individually, macular ganglion cell layer (mGCL) thickness was the most sensitive structural metric, detecting progression in 61.0% of eyes, followed closely by global RNFL (59.3%) and mean macular thickness (57.6%) (Table 3).

Progression detection rates across disease stages

When stratified by disease severity (Table 4), primary OCT parameters detected progression in 87.5% of eyes with mild glaucoma compared to 50.0% by VF (p < 0.001). In moderate-to-severe glaucoma, primary OCT parameters detected progression in 72.7% of eyes versus 27.3% by VF (p = 0.13). Among individual OCT parameters, mean mGCL thickness had the highest detection rate in mild glaucoma (68.8%), while nasal RNFL showed highest detection in moderate-to-severe stages (100%).

When stratified by disease severity, structural imaging was particularly advantageous in mild glaucoma, where OCT detected progression in 87.5% of eyes compared to 50.0% by VF (p < 0.001, Table 4). Temporal progression patterns

Kaplan-Meier analysis revealed earlier and more frequent progression detection with OCT (Fig. 3). By the seven-year follow-up, OCT detects 23%, while VF progression at 9.4%. This gap widens by the ten-year mark, with primary OCT parameters detecting 57% progression, compared to 38.6% for VF. The combined primary OCT/VF detection rate (60% at ten years) did not significantly differ from OCT alone.

Kaplan-meier analysis of glaucoma progression detected by OCT vs. visual field parameters. Primary OCT parameters were defined as global RNFL, mean mGCL, and mean retinal thickness

Statistical analysis confirms that the difference in progression detection between OCT and VF is significant (p < 0.001). However, the comparison between OCT alone and the combined measure does not show statistical significance, indicating that OCT alone is almost as effective as the combined measure.

Spearman correlation analysis demonstrated weak correlations between age and OCT structural parameters (|r| range 0.07–0.41).

Discussion

Our study provides long-term, trend-based real-world evidence comparing structural OCT parameters with functional VF testing for glaucoma progression detection. With a mean follow-up of 8.5 years, our findings demonstrate that OCT structural parameters detect progression more frequently and earlier than functional indices, particularly in mild disease. By 10 years, OCT detected progression in 57% of eyes versus 38.6% for VF, reinforcing its utility for long-term monitoring.

OCT versus visual field testing in detecting progression

OCT identified a significantly greater proportion of progressing eyes compared with VF (84.7% vs. 45.8%), reinforcing the concept that structural deterioration often precedes measurable functional decline, consistent with prior studies [4, 5].

Our study found that OCT, particularly mGCL thickness, had the highest detection rate, highlighting the importance of macular assessment in progression monitoring. This aligns with Munuera et al. [12], who reported mGCL as a reliable structural marker even in severe glaucoma. Global RNFL thickness also showed robust detection performance, aligning with findings by Sreedharan et al. [13], who linked RNFL thinning to disease severity. Additionally, mRT demonstrated substantial progression detection capability, supporting its role as a complementary structural parameter. Prior studies [14–16]have similarly demonstrated that macular thinning correlates with RNFL loss and central visual field deterioration, further emphasizing the clinical value of incorporating macular analysis into routine glaucoma surveillance.

Parameter measurements among progression groups

Eyes with only OCT progression had significantly thicker global RNFL, RNFL-superior, inferior, nasal and temporal compared to those with only VF progression or both. This suggests these eyes may be in earlier disease stages, where structural changes are detectable but functional decline is minimal. Thinner RNFL in eyes with only VF progression and those with both OCT and VF progression groups reflects more advanced damage, consistent with established glaucoma pathophysiology [13].

Progression detection across different glaucoma severities

In our cohort, OCT detected progression significantly more often than VF in mild glaucoma, supporting the concept that structural damage precedes measurable functional loss [2]. The higher detection rate of mean mGCL thickness in mild disease suggests that macular ganglion cell analysis may be especially sensitive to early glaucomatous change [2], likely because central retinal ganglion cells are affected before widespread visual field defects become apparent. The significant between-stage differences observed for mGCL and inferior mRT further reinforce their role as early structural biomarkers. In contrast, the absence of significant differences for most other OCT parameters across stages may due to the “floor effect” in OCT, where further structural thinning becomes undetectable [12]. while VF continues to capture functional loss [17]. This underscores the complementary nature of structural and functional parameters in detecting progression [2, 18].

Implications of overlap and discrepancies in progression detection

The Venn diagram analysis highlighted overlap among OCT parameters, suggesting widespread structural changes in some eyes. This reinforces the need to assess multiple OCT layers simultaneously to enhance progression detection, as supported by recent studies [12, 16].

The minimal overlap between MD and PSD reflects their different measures of visual function, underscoring the importance of interpreting VF indices together [19].

The limited agreement between OCT and VF progression emphasizes their complementary nature. OCT is more sensitive to early structural damage, while VF reflects functional impact. Combining both provides a fuller picture of disease progression, aligning with recommendations by, aligning with the recommendations from previous studies [2, 20].

Temporal analysis of progression detection

Kaplan-Meier analysis demonstrated that OCT detects progression earlier and more frequently than VF, particularly at 7- and 10-year follow-ups. This supports OCT’s value in early intervention and timely treatment adjustments [2, 4, 20].

Interestingly, the close alignment between the progression curves of OCT alone and the combined OCT or VF measure suggests that OCT alone may suffice for early detection of progression in many cases. However, VF remains essential for assessing advanced functional impairment, especially when quality-of-life impact is considered [20].

Age-related retinal thinning has been reported in previous studies and may influence absolute OCT thickness values [21]. In the present cohort, correlation analysis demonstrated weak associations between age and OCT structural parameters, indicating only modest relationships. Furthermore, glaucoma progression in this study was determined using longitudinal within-eye trend analysis rather than absolute thickness thresholds, which helps reduce the influence of physiological age-related structural decline.

Study strengths and limitations

This study is strengthened by its longitudinal design with extended follow-up (mean 8.5 years) and comprehensive OCT parameter analysis, enhances the clinical relevance of the findings. Limitations include the absence of the Visual Field Index (VFI) and the use of PSD, which may reduce sensitivity in severe cases. The retrospective design introduces selection bias, as only patients with regular follow-ups and adequate data were included, and the single-center design limits broader applicability. Future studies should refine OCT algorithms to detect progression in advanced disease despite the floor effect. Exploring OCT angiography integration may further improve monitoring accuracy.

Conclusion

In this longitudinal cohort with extended follow-up, trend-based analysis of structural OCT parameters detected glaucoma progression earlier and more frequently than functional testing, particularly in mild disease. Macular ganglion cell layer (mGCL) thickness was the single most sensitive metric, highlighting the critical role of macular assessment in progression monitoring. While visual fields testing remains indispensable especially in more moderate to advanced stages, reliance on visual fields alone risks missing early, actionable structural loss. The findings in this study support incorporating key structural OCT parameters, specifically mGCL, global RNFL, and mRT into standard long term glaucoma surveillance to enable more timely therapeutic intervention.

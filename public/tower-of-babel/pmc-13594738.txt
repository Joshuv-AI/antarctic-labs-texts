Development of a patient-reported outcomes registry for patients with brain tumours (BEACON)
Authors: E. Sanderson, A.B. Campbell, K. Drummond, M. Dumas, J. Johns, M. Harold, P. Gibbs, L. Gately
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundCapturing patient-reported outcomes (PROs) is increasingly recognised as an essential component of high-quality, patient-centred care and research. Here, we describe the design and development of BEACON, a prospective, noninterventional registry aiming to capture the lived experience of adults after a brain tumour diagnosis, and report initial findings from our formative preimplementation evaluation.Patients and methodsBEACON was codesigned with brain cancer patients and developed in collaboration with BioGrid Australia Limited. Four key design components were identified: (i) the ability to self-register and update consent; (ii) the use of validated, relevant PROs; (iii) incorporation of previously overlooked areas such as financial stress and informal supports; and (iv) real-time data analysis.ResultsFormative preimplementation evaluation was undertaken in June 2025 with detailed feedback obtained from 10 volunteers, including brain cancer patients, carers, and members of the public. Feedback explored access, usability, language, and overall satisfaction. Testing confirmed cross-device compatibility and demonstrated overall usability with high perceived value. Refinements prompted by the evaluation included clearer password definitions, a consolidated consent form, and adjusted colours for historic versus current responses to improve distinguishability.ConclusionsPreliminary findings demonstrate favourable usability and satisfaction with the initial version of BEACON. BEACON is now available as a self-referral, population-accessible model, and links PROs with clinical data from the established Brain tumour Registry Australia INnovation and translation clinical data registry, providing a scalable infrastructure to support diverse research activities, including registry-based trials. Future research will focus on implementation and on leveraging BEACON to generate novel insights for multidimensional translational and clinical research.

Introduction

Brain and central nervous system tumours are the sixth leading cause of cancer-related burden in Australia, with an estimated 2076 new cases diagnosed in 2017.1 Both malignant and nonmalignant brain tumours are associated with marked morbidity and mortality and can have a devastating impact on physical, cognitive, and psychosocial wellbeing.1 Standard treatment includes surgical resection, radiotherapy, and systemic treatment, with invariably limited survival for malignant subtypes.2 Clinical data for patients with brain tumours are now being captured at multiple sites across Australia, via the Brain tumour Registry Australia INnovation and translation (BRAIN) registry (Human Research Ethics Committee [HREC] approved: HREC/44314/MH-2018),3 supporting a range of research activities.

Although research has typically focused on improvements in survival, brain tumour consumers have highlighted a need for a greater focus on patient-centred care.4 Patients with brain tumours, both primary and secondary, experience substantial functional and neurocognitive limitations due to both the disease and sequelae of treatment.5, 6, 7, 8 Despite patients and their caregivers often having high levels of distress and supportive care needs,6 these often remain unmet,9,10 in part due to patients being given limited opportunities to report these efficiently. Understanding supportive care needs and driving supportive care research are increasingly recognised as essential components of patient-centred care in this population.11

Patient-reported outcomes (PROs) capture a patient’s self-reported health status and quality of life, which can be quantified using patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs).12 PROs capture the many common and impactful symptoms identified by the patients who experience them. Assessment of PROs can be used to inform shared decision-making and provide opportunities to address unmet symptom needs, improving the patient experience and clinical outcomes.13,14 PROs can also help to improve the understanding of the net benefit of a treatment beyond the traditional measures of radiographic response or time-dependent metrics such as progression-free or overall survival.15 Previous studies across different tumour types have demonstrated the value of collecting PROs, including enhanced patient-centred care,16, 17, 18 and reduced unexpected hospital admissions.10 PROs can also be used to inform policies and procedures such as the optimal care pathways19 and guide future research and model-of-care innovation.20, 21, 22

In Australia, the collection and utilisation of PROMs in routine practice is variable. To our knowledge, there are no clinical cancer registries enabling the capture of this data for patients with brain cancers or brain tumours more broadly nor are brain tumour-specific PROMs routinely measured at a local, state, or national level.14,23 Here, we describe the design and development of the BEACON registry: a prospective, standardised, patient-entered registry designed to capture the PROs of patients with brain tumours and present initial data from a formative preimplementation evaluation.

Materials and methods

Design

BEACON (Trial Registration: ACTRN12624001259538) was designed to prospectively capture the lived experiences of adult patients with brain tumours, including benign and malignant, primary and secondary brain tumours, using both single-item and validated multi-item PROMs. Participants are eligible if they are ≥18 years, can read and understand English, have the capacity to consent, and can complete an online survey with or without the use of a proxy. The intent is for broad, self-referral implementation without the need for individual hospital engagement; however, BEACON does not independently ascertain cases, relying on patient self-registration following diagnosis. The overarching objective is to improve the understanding of the current unmet supportive care needs of patients with brain tumours and inform and support future research and routine care resource allocation.

To achieve this, independent expert opinion was sought to determine the nuances of data collection with input from leads in neurosurgery (KD), medical oncology (LG), and data registries (PG, ABC, MD, JJ, and MH). A consumer panel of four individuals with lived experience in brain cancer was consulted regularly from idea conception through needs assessment and stakeholder engagement through to platform development to ensure that the platform is easily accessible, nonburdensome and relevant to the needs of the brain tumour community. Through consultation, four key design components were identified: (i) ability to self-register and update consent choices; (ii) use of validated, relevant PROs; (iii) incorporation of previously overlooked areas such as financial stress and informal supports; and (iv) real-time data analysis to support shared decision-making between patients and their treating clinician/s. Additionally, critical to research needs was the ability to link with established and ethically approved clinical datasets, such as the BRAIN registry,3 and to facilitate multidimensional research including registry-based trials and the interplay between PROs and standard clinical parameters.

To address these design components, BEACON contains baseline and follow-up surveys. Data captured include diagnosis and treatment history, social wellbeing and support, as well as health-related quality of life (HRQOL), using two validated PROMs for brain cancers: the EORTC-QLQ-C30 and EORTC-QLQ-BN20. These PROMs have been utilised in multiple observational and interventional studies to determine HRQOL across various tumour types including brain tumours.7,8,24 They were selected because consumers highlighted the importance of being able to compare their data with normative data and clinical trial populations, whereas clinicians saw value in using BEACON to support clinical trials. The EORTC-QLQ-C30 comprises five functional scales (physical, role, emotional, cognitive, and social functioning) and nine multi- and single-item scales (fatigue, nausea and vomiting, pain, dyspnoea, insomnia, appetite loss, constipation and diarrhoea, financial stressors, and a global health status).25 The EORTC-QLQ-BN20 was developed as a supplementary module to capture key symptoms and functional domains relevant to patients with brain cancers.26 Although initially developed and validated in English-speaking glioma patients receiving chemotherapy and/or radiotherapy, the QLQ-BN20 has since been translated and validated in other languages and is also increasingly being used in other types of brain tumours, including meningioma and brain metastases.27, 28, 29

Participants can register for BEACON at any time following their diagnosis to ensure equity of access. Approximate date of diagnosis is captured to account for variation in disease stage and treatment phase at enrolment. Once registered, BEACON surveys can be completed as often as a participant chooses; however, reminder emails will be sent automatically every 3 months, provided they continue to provide consent. No more than one survey can be completed per day.

Ethical approval was obtained from the Royal Melbourne Hospital Human Research Ethics Committee (HREC/103541/MH-2023).

Development

BEACON was developed in collaboration with BioGrid Australia Limited, a service provider of secure, web-based platforms for deidentified real-time data-sharing and linkage.30 With built-in security systems and data logic tests, including the use of mandatory fields for the two PROMs, BEACON has been designed to ensure privacy and confidentiality for the participants as well as robust data integrity and completeness for research. BioGrid also specialises in real-time linkage of patient records across datasets using either probabilistic identity matching or exact matching via a cryptographic hashtag function. This is achieved by creating a unique subject identifier from a defined set of personally identifying information for each individual, enabling enduring linkage for authorised research. As both BEACON and BRAIN are developed in collaboration with BioGrid, participant PRO and clinical data will be linked wherever an individual appears in both registries, enabling examination of subgroups based on collected clinical data.

Participants self-register using a unique login and password and provide consent through a participant information consent form. The dynamic online consent feature allows participants or their proxy to update consent choices in real time, including withdrawal from the registry. BEACON has built-in functionality allowing participants to download a summary of the information they have provided, view how their symptoms have evolved over time, and compare their PROM results with summary data from others with a similar brain tumour diagnosis who have also contributed to the registry. BEACON also logs interaction data, which can be interrogated to understand how often and in what ways participants engage with the platform, including viewing of surveys, downloading/printing results, or changes in consent.

BEACON was developed as a web-based application, accessible on phone/android and PC, with individualised logins and two-factor authentication for privacy and security. All data collected is stored securely and can only be accessed by authorised staff and research personnel. BEACON is overseen by a management committee comprising clinician, researcher, and consumer representatives, responsible for governance of platform implementation and data access. Deidentified data are accessible to researchers following application, a process which includes ethical and scientific review from the BEACON management committee before release.

Formative usability and feasibility testing

Following development, we conducted a preimplementation, noninterventional formative evaluation. Participants were recruited via email through the Walter and Eliza Hall (WEHI) Consumer Program, which educates and supports consumers to maximise research impact, and selected WEHI Divisions. Those who volunteered were provided with a short synopsis on BEACON, the web address for the BEACON test environment, and an online questionnaire via REDCap. The questionnaire comprised 17 questions, including a mix of yes or no items with optional free-text elaboration and five-point Likert-scale items. The evaluation focused on usability, including layout, language, navigation, and accessibility, to inform iterative refinement before implementation. An additional free-text field was included at the end for general comments or suggestions. Survey responses were aggregated and presented as percentages, and free-text responses underwent descriptive analysis.

Summary of formative preimplementation evaluation

Overall, usability was positive. Seven participants (70%) described the application as user-friendly with a visually appealing layout, content, and colour scheme. Most participants (n = 6, 60%) reported no difficulties completing online consent, navigating the platform, or completing questionnaires. However, one individual (10%) felt the consent form was overly long. Follow-up surveys incorporated responses entered at prior visits. Four participants (40%) found this feature helpful but some (n = 3, 30%) noted challenges reconciling what was historical versus current data, highlighting a need for clearer distinction. All participants (100%) found the language clear and easy to understand.

Participants also commented on perceived value and anticipated future use. Six participants (60%) indicated they would share their results with their clinician, reflecting perceived relevance. One participant (10%) specifically highlighted the usefulness of BEACON for symptom tracking and patient-centred communication. Overall, 90% of participants (n = 9) were satisfied with the platform, indicating they would continue to use BEACON and/or recommend it to others.

Feedback directly informed several refinements, including clearer password definitions, a consolidated consent form, and adjustments to the display and colouring of historic versus current responses to improve distinguishability. These positive early findings support the usability and acceptability of BEACON and have guided optimisation before implementation.

Discussion

This paper describes the design, development, and initial usability evaluation of BEACON. To our knowledge, this is the first noninterventional, consumer-driven registry, which supports repeated capture of PROs and longitudinal analysis of HRQOL data for patients with both primary and secondary brain tumours. This work responds to a growing body of evidence regarding the value of PROs to promote patient-centred care in clinical settings and as a metric for quality improvement at both a micro- and macrolevel.14,31 Previous studies have also shown strong acceptance of routine PRO assessments among patients with brain cancer.32 Consistent with this, our formative evaluation found that BEACON was acceptable and accessible to patients, carers, and members of the public, with most participants reporting positive experiences across navigation, layout, and language.

These early findings support the feasibility of implementing an ePRO platform in neuro-oncology, in which symptom capture and communication are important. However, implementation of such systems in this population faces unique challenges, including neurocognitive impairment, competing priorities, and the perception that such programmes do not meet their specific needs.12,33 To address this, consumer codesign was prioritised during the development of BEACON, which is anticipated to improve participant satisfaction and retention.34 Although BEACON was generally accessible and impactful, our evaluation uncovered confusion around password requirements and the display of prefilled responses. These findings underline the importance of minimal registration friction and clear visual clues, particularly for users with cognitive issues or treatment-/tumour-related fatigue, and highlight the need for iterative feedback and refinement during development and after launch. Furthermore, sustained engagement, integration into clinical pathways, and participant readiness will also be key determinants of real-world uptake.

BEACON has been designed as a web-based system. The use of web-based systems for self-reporting and monitoring of symptoms in patients with advanced cancer has been associated with multiple clinical benefits, including improved HRQOL and quality-adjusted survival.13,34 Provided there is faithful migration of questionnaires into a digitised format, electronic PROs (ePROs) are comparable to their paper-based counterparts and are preferred by most patients, allowing them to complete questionnaires in their own time and space,35 which prompts more in-depth self-reflection.36, 37, 38, 39 Additionally, data generated from ePROs tend to be higher quality, as incomplete or ambiguous data entry is not permitted on digitised forms, and are less burdensome on clinicians and researchers as the need for manual data entry and scoring is eliminated.35,37,40 At the same time, web-based systems introduce challenges that require careful consideration, such as technological literacy, access to the internet, and the need for accessibility for users with visual, cognitive, or motor impairments.34,37 Although BEACON has incorporated plain language and the ability to use a proxy, addressing this further will be essential to maximise BEACON’s reach and equity.

This evaluation also highlighted several limitations that will inform future development. This initial evaluation was conducted with a small, purposively selected sample of 10 participants, recruited via targeted email to members of the WEHI Consumer Program and WEHI Staff, representing consumer and general population perspectives, respectively. This does not permit assessment of BEACON’s acceptability or usefulness across its broader intended population of patients with brain tumours. This sample size and composition were selected to enable an in-depth usability evaluation ahead of wider release, consistent with early-stage usability testing approaches. Importantly, the platform is only available in English, which may restrict users from non-English–speaking backgrounds and underscores the need for culturally and linguistically appropriate adaptations. Cognitive impairment is common among patients with brain tumours and may impact usability in routine practice. Cognitive status was not assessed in the participants of this evaluation nor is it captured in the BEACON platform. As BEACON relies on self-referral, the extent to which patients with cognitive impairment will engage is unknown, and under-representation cannot be excluded. Further evaluation is planned over the next 12–24 months as BEACON is iteratively refined, which will assess feasibility, acceptability, and usefulness at a larger scale and among the intended patient population, including considerations of whether and how cognitive status may be captured. As enrolment expands, representativeness and potential recruitment bias arising from the self-referral model will be monitored by audits of participant demographics, enabling potential identification of subgroups that are not engaging with the platform. While BEACON has been designed to capture PROs, consideration must also be given to the incorporation of caregiver-reported outcomes to supplement these. Finally, the evaluation was conducted before real-world implementation, which may subsequently reveal further workflow or technical considerations. These limitations will guide ongoing refinement as BEACON progresses towards outcome evaluation.

Future directions

This paper focuses on the design, development and initial preimplementation evaluation of BEACON. Following this evaluation, BEACON will be broadly available through self-referral without requiring clinical site engagement. BEACON’s design responds to gaps identified across recent international PROMs, digital oncology and registry-based real-world data literature. Routine PROMs use in cancer remains limited, with persistent barriers identified around standardisation, data collection, and actionability.41, 42, 43 Additionally, PROM implementation and recruitment have traditionally been clinician- and researcher-led; however, clinicians frequently do not discuss PROs with patients36 and often lack the training or time to interpret scores or act on issues raised.44

BEACON addresses some of these barriers directly. Firstly, removing the site as a gatekeeper is intended to ensure broad inclusivity and equity of access, leaving the decision to engage with BEACON to the patient rather than the treating team. This was an intentional choice, highlighted by our consumers. Future audits of participant demographics will identify subgroups that are not engaging, recognising that self-directed digital access may concentrate exclusion among older or less digitally literate patients if not actively reviewed.45 Additionally, findings from BEACON as well as impact on research outcomes will be proactively evaluated.

Secondly, by providing real-time interpretation and longitudinal tracking, BEACON is designed to streamline interpretation for clinicians, presenting scores and trends over time in a format that reduces the burden of manual review and interpretation. Integration into electronic medical records remains a key priority, potentially allowing PROM data to be viewed alongside other clinical information at the point of care. However, this level of integration is complex and dependent on the interoperability of external hospital IT systems, which may not be equipped to accept PROM data.42 In the interim, BEACON’s real-time dashboard is designed to function as a standalone patient-facing tool, providing accessible interpretation of PROM data independent of electronic medical record connectivity.

Thirdly, beyond individual clinical use, BEACON aims to generate data on areas of unmet need and provide the infrastructure required to conduct registry-based trials of supportive care interventions. BEACON’s closest international comparators are found in the Netherlands, where clinical treatment registries have been intentionally paired with dedicated PRO registries to create linked, multipurpose real-world infrastructure. The Dutch Melanoma Treatment Registry captures tumour, treatment, and outcome data across all Dutch melanoma centres and links with the PROFILES registry, which collects PRO from cancer survivors, including melanoma patients.46, 47, 48 A comparable initiative, the Dutch Thoracic Oncology Cohort,49 similarly combines clinical, PROMs, and biomaterial within a single nationwide infrastructure to support a range of prospective and retrospective thoracic oncology studies. BEACON adopts a comparable model within Australian neuro-oncology. Through linkage with the BRAIN clinical registry via a BioGrid-enabled universal subject identifier, BEACON operates as a self-referral PROM registry alongside BRAIN’s population-based clinical ascertainment, extending existing Australian neuro-oncology infrastructure into a platform for comprehensive registry-based trials, such as CONCISE (ACTRN12626000036314p). To our knowledge, a comparable linked PROM-clinical registry model has not previously been established for neuro-oncology populations who present additional challenges not explicitly addressed by these existing systems. As such, BEACON addresses this specific gap.

Finally, BEACON has been designed for long-term sustainability with minimal ongoing resource requirements absorbed within existing registry infrastructure and no cost to participants. Although there are no plans to extend beyond neuro-oncology, its design offers a potential blueprint for similar PROM-clinical registry linkage in other tumour streams.

Conclusion

Preliminary findings demonstrate favourable usability and satisfaction with the initial version of BEACON and informed refinements including clearer password definitions, a consolidated consent form, and adjusted colours for historic versus current responses to improve distinguishability. Once launched, BEACON aims to improve our understanding of the current unmet supportive care needs of patients with brain tumours, informing future resource allocation in supportive and routine care as well as research activities such as quality improvement initiatives. BEACON will also enable linkage of PROs with clinical data from established registries, such as the BRAIN registry, providing a scalable infrastructure to support diverse research activities, including registry-based trials. Future research will focus on implementation and leveraging BEACON to generate novel multidimensional translational and clinical research.

SDA-Reg: large-scale dynamic scene point cloud registration with semantic dual-stream attention
Authors: Shengjie Fu, Qipeng Cai, Zhaoyuan Yao, Qihuai Chen, Tianliang Lin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPoint cloud registration is a fundamental task in 3D vision, however, large-scale outdoor LiDAR point clouds, characterized by their immense size and structural complexity, present significant challenges in highly dynamic environments. Existing methods often employ semantic segmentation as preprocessing, offering the potential to incorporate semantic information to enhance registration robustness. This paper introduces SDA-Reg, a registration network based on semantically enhanced features and a dual-stream attention architecture. Its core contributions include: (1) Introducing a semantic consistency constraint module within the attention mechanism to strengthen intra-class correlations and suppress inter-class misalignments; (2) Designing a gated dynamic suppression(GDS) module to adaptively suppress noise from dynamic objects while preserving pseudo-static structures; (3) Deep integration of semantic information into feature extraction and matching processes to achieve semantically guided registration. On the KITTI dataset, SDA-Reg achieves significant performance improvements with a registration recall rate of 99.92%. On the dynamically complex KITTI 08 sequences, it outperforms baseline methods by 1.43%, demonstrating robust accuracy in dynamic environments.Keywords: Point cloud registration, Semantic, Deep learningSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Advances in sensing technologies, particularly LiDAR, have established point clouds as a central representation of three-dimensional environments. Because individual scans are limited by the sensor field of view, point-cloud registration is essential for reconstructing large-scale 3D scenes1. Registration aims to estimate the optimal rigid transformation between two point clouds and underpins applications such as robotics and autonomous driving2–4. Although deep learning-based registration methods have advanced rapidly5, their performance in large-scale outdoor environments remains limited by dynamic objects, high computational costs, occlusion, and partial overlap. To reduce coarse-level misalignment, the baseline method6 introduced a Bi-directional Association Transformer (BAT) that combines cross-attention with global association. However, this method does not explicitly suppress dynamic objects or exploit semantic information.

Recent studies have also shown that deep neural networks are vulnerable to input perturbations, adversarial attacks, and system-level failures. These risks are particularly important in safety-critical applications such as autonomous driving. Misconfigured policies can compromise cloud-based AI systems7, whereas gradient-based attacks can expose weaknesses in trained models8. In federated learning and networked systems, incentive mechanisms9 and resilience-recovery strategies10 have been developed to improve robustness. Related work on system-call filtering11, blockchain security12,13, and network-traffic analysis14 further emphasizes the need for reliable AI pipelines. These findings suggest that registration systems should be evaluated not only for geometric and semantic accuracy, but also for robustness to imperfect inputs and distribution shifts. Advances in deception defense15,16, advanced persistent threat analysis17, model privacy attacks18, and privacy-preserving federated learning19 further reinforce the importance of stability and trustworthiness in AI systems. This broader perspective motivates the development of a registration framework that remains reliable in dynamic and uncertain environments.

This study presents SDA-Reg, a semantic dual-stream attention framework for point-cloud registration. Semantic information is incorporated to strengthen feature representation, while a gated dual-stream attention mechanism suppresses interference from dynamic objects. The proposed method is evaluated on the KITTI dataset.

The main contributions of this paper are as follows:

We propose a method to fuse semantic information from the encoder into the dual-stream attention mechanism, providing semantic score guidance for attention weight calculation. Related work

In recent years, deep learning-based point-cloud registration methods have increasingly outperformed traditional geometry-based approaches. End-to-end frameworks integrate feature extraction, correspondence estimation, and transformation prediction within a unified model. This design improves robustness to noise, low overlap, and variations in point density. PointNetLK20, one of the earliest representative methods, combines PointNet21 with the Lucas–Kanade algorithm22 in a trainable recurrent framework. Lu et al.23 integrated shallow and deep keypoint features and introduced consistency constraints to improve correspondence robustness. However, the method remained dependent on keypoint detection. CoFiNet24, proposed by Yu et al., removed the need for explicit keypoint detection and adopted a coarse-to-fine correspondence strategy. This hierarchical design improved robustness to point-density variations and enhanced generalization.

RegFormer, the baseline adopted in this study, is an end-to-end registration framework that eliminates explicit keypoint matching and additional post-processing. Its BAT module combines cross-attention with global attention at the coarse level to reduce correspondence ambiguity and suppress misalignment. However, RegFormer does not incorporate semantic information. Subsequent methods, including MDGT25, which reduces attention complexity, and CAST26, which introduces point-guided cross-attention, have mainly focused on improving geometric feature interaction. Motion-induced artifacts in dynamic scenes remain insufficiently addressed. These limitations indicate the need for joint semantic and dynamic modeling.

Semantic-guided registration methods have therefore received increasing attention. These approaches typically improve correspondence consistency by introducing semantic priors or cross-modal alignment. Li et al.27 exploited the projection relationship between images and point clouds to achieve cross-modal registration. Zhang et al.28 fused colour and geometric information for indoor registration, but the method was not extended to large-scale outdoor point clouds. Du et al.29 used semantic constraints to restrict the correspondence search space. However, the hierarchical structure of semantic information was not fully exploited.

DeepSIR, proposed by Li et al.30, removes moving objects through semantic filtering and aggregates geometric and semantic features. Its reliance on hard thresholds, however, makes it sensitive to segmentation errors. Yin et al.31 improved pose-estimation accuracy and efficiency through semantic clustering and geometric-distribution modelling. Xu et al.32 proposed S2Reg, which extracts semantic information using image-based models and combines it with structural cues to enhance feature representation. This design introduces substantial computational overhead.

Zhao et al.33 used regional voting to establish loose semantic consistency and introduced semantic–geometric constraints for outlier rejection. A two-stage hypothesis-testing strategy was further employed to improve registration robustness. Nevertheless, semantic information was used primarily during correspondence generation and was not incorporated as a structured constraint during feature learning. Liu et al.34 formulated registration as a semantic instance-matching problem and estimated correspondences through graph convolution and optimal transport. This approach depends on accurate instance segmentation, and its robustness to dynamic objects and semantic noise has not been fully evaluated.

Existing methods exhibit two main limitations. First, they rarely distinguish explicitly between static and dynamic regions. Motion-induced deformation can therefore corrupt correspondences and interfere with global transformation estimation. Second, semantic fusion is often limited to feature concatenation or auxiliary supervision. Semantic consistency is seldom incorporated directly into the matching mechanism, particularly at the coarse-registration stage.

To address these limitations, SDA-Reg integrates semantically enhanced features with a dual-stream attention mechanism. A semantic consistency constraint is introduced to guide coarse-to-fine correspondence estimation, while a gated dynamic-suppression strategy reduces interference from moving objects. Together, these components improve registration accuracy and robustness in complex dynamic scenes.

Lightweight semantic mapping

For weak geometric features and unstructured terrain, pure coordinate features struggle to distinguish between different objects (such as mounds of earth and the ground). This study designs a lightweight semantic mapping module that converts the discrete labels output by semantic segmentation into learnable, compact feature vectors, which are then fused with point cloud coordinates. In this way, even when local geometric structures are similar, the network can distinguish between different categories through semantic labels, thereby providing a basis for cross-frame matching in low-overlap regions.

However, simply incorporating point-level semantic labels from the semantic segmentation network—whether as raw category labels or using simple one-hot encoding—into the network can result in dimensional mismatches and a lack of discriminative power. To address these challenges, this study designs a lightweight semantic mapping module, as illustrated in Fig. 3. Since the semantic labels are output by the pre-segmentation network and contain prediction noise, this module employs a post-fusion approach. The goal is to exchange semantic and geometric information in a higher-order feature space, thereby enhancing the model’s robustness.

Semantic dual-stream attention

The baseline network RegFormer effectively aligns source and target point clouds through its dual-stream matching mechanism. However, this mechanism lacks high-level semantic awareness, making it difficult to avoid misalignments with moving objects.

To overcome these limitations, this study employs a cross-window dual-stream attention module with semantic guidance, as illustrated in Fig. 4, by adding a semantic feature data stream in parallel to the original geometric feature data stream. This mechanism leverages semantic consistency constraints to force points of the same semantic category to remain close in feature space, maintaining stable correspondences even when they are located at the edges of unstructured regions, thereby alleviating the low-overlap problem. Simultaneously, a dynamic gating control method is designed. For frequently occurring moving objects in scenes, such as vehicles and pedestrians, the gating network adaptively computes soft masks to suppress their attention weights rather than directly removing them. Compared to static enhancement strategies, this method better distinguishes between genuinely moving objects and pseudo-static objects that are temporarily stationary, maintaining registration stability under conditions with a high proportion of motion.

Dynamic objects (such as vehicles and pedestrians) cause erroneous feature matching, severely disrupting transformation matrix estimation. An intuitive solution is to enhance the feature weights of static regions, but this approach has clear limitations: while static enhancement increases the relative importance of static features, it fails to eliminate strong erroneous matches introduced by dynamic objects themselves. Such matches continue to dominate local optimization. Furthermore, reliable static regions already provide sufficient constraints. Further weight enhancement not only struggles to improve accuracy but may amplify inherent noise and increase computational burden. Global static enhancement strategies fail to effectively distinguish feature contributions across regions. Therefore, this paper adopts a dynamic suppression strategy, introducing a dynamic object mask to selectively suppress interference noise caused by moving objects.

Directly removing dynamic objects is not the optimal choice, as real-world scenarios often contain temporarily stationary moving objects (e.g., vehicles waiting at red lights, pedestrians pausing briefly), whose surfaces still contain abundant effective geometric features reflecting the scene’s static structure. To address this, this paper introduces a dynamic suppression gated network (Fig. 5), which dynamically adjusts the influence of dynamic object masks during training through gated coefficients. This approach avoids hard-threshold segmentation by generating pointwise modulation coefficients through the gating network. It suppresses genuine dynamic points via soft masking while preserving potentially static features, thereby enhancing the model’s robustness across diverse scenes.

This mechanism enables the network to distinguish between highly dynamic objects, potentially pseudo-static objects, and purely static backgrounds. By suppressing noise while maximizing the retention of useful information, it significantly enhances the generalization capability and robustness of registration algorithms in dynamic environments.

In this summary, the gating coefficient is output via a sigmoid function, with its range strictly limited to the interval [0,1]. This ensures numerical stability and prevents gradient explosion or numerical overflow caused by changes in the amplitude of input features. The momentum coefficient is used to control the temporal smoothness of the gating value; it is updated using an exponential moving average to avoid abrupt changes in the gating coefficient between adjacent frames. When the actual segmentation accuracy falls below the simulated threshold, the gating network adaptively adjusts the coefficient toward 1, thereby preventing registration failure caused by excessive interference from erroneous semantic information. This soft fallback mechanism ensures that the model retains a basic operational capability even under extreme noise conditions.

Rigid transformation estimation

It achieves this by first performing an attention-weighted aggregation of the third-layer downsampled point cloud features and the motion embeddings, then passing the integrated representation through a fully connected layer.

Experiment

This paper evaluates SDA-Reg on the KITTI38 and NuScenes39 dataset. We validate the effectiveness of introducing semantic labels into large-scale point cloud datasets through this dataset. We enhance the accuracy of visual discrimination methods and verify the effectiveness of adding each module through ablation experiments.

Experimental setup

All experiments were conducted on a server with an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3090 GPU, CUDA 11.4, Python 3.8, and PyTorch 1.11.0. SDA-Reg was trained for 150 epochs with Adam, an initial learning rate of 1e-4, a batch size of 8, and weight decay of 1e-4. The first 10 epochs used linear warm-up with a factor of 0.01; cosine annealing was then applied for learning-rate decay. The gated dynamic suppression network uses global average pooling followed by Linear-ReLU-Linear-Sigmoid layers; the hidden and output dimensions are 64 and 1, respectively, and the initial exponential-moving-average coefficient is 0.8. SDA-Reg and the RegFormer baseline used the same KITTI split (training: 00–05; validation: 06–07; testing: 08–10). The provenance and exact configuration of every additional baseline must be reported separately as either reproduced or taken from the cited source.

While adhering to the DGR40 protocol, the network in this paper is evaluated using three metrics: (1) Relative Translation Error (RTE, m); (2) Relative Rotation Error (RRE, °); (3) Registration recall rate (RR, %), calculated based on the relative translation error and relative rotation error falling within specific threshold ranges. The corresponding thresholds are set as: relative translation error ≤ 2 m, relative rotation error ≤ 5°.

Conclusion

By integrating semantic information with geometric features, SDA-Reg improves both the robustness and accuracy of large-scale dynamic scene registration. This method constructs a semantic dual-stream attention mechanism that leverages semantic consistency constraints to strengthen intra-class feature associations and suppress misalignments. It further incorporates an adaptive gating module to balance dynamic noise suppression with the preservation of pseudo-static structures. On the KITTI dataset, SDA-Reg achieves an alignment recall rate of 99.92% and demonstrates superior performance in dense dynamic object scenes (e.g., sequence 08), with an improvement of up to 1.43%, validating the critical role of semantic information in complex outdoor environments. Ablation studies further demonstrate the complementary nature of semantic embedding and dual-stream attention modules, collectively driving performance breakthroughs. Despite increased computational overhead, this research provides a reliable technical approach and implementation pathway for high-precision demanding scenarios, such as low-speed autonomous driving and engineering surveying.

The gating formulation also provides a bounded first-order propagation relation: for a semantic-mask perturbation Delta M, Delta A = -g_t (element-wise product) Delta M and ||Delta A||_infinity <= ||Delta M||_infinity because 0 < = g_t < = 1. This bounds the perturbation contributed by the gating term but does not eliminate downstream registration error under severe semantic-label corruption.

Nevertheless, our method relies on point cloud labels provided by semantic segmentation networks, which in real-world scenarios often fail to achieve ideal accuracy. To further enhance robustness against segmentation noise, we propose two potential mitigation strategies for future work: (1) confidence-weighted fusion - using the softmax scores from the segmentation network as per-point weights, with gating coefficients for low-confidence regions adaptively reduced; (2) uncertainty-aware gating - introducing a lightweight uncertainty estimation branch that automatically falls back to pure geometric features when semantic features are unreliable. We leave these extensions for future investigation to facilitate the deployment of our method in real-world autonomous driving scenarios.

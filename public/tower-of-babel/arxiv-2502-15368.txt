Constructing Fermionic Hamiltonians with Non-Gaussianic low-energy states
Kartik Anand

Abstract
Quantum PCP conjecture is one of the most influential open problems in quantum complexity theory, which states that approximating the ground state energy for a sparse local Hamiltonian upto a constant is QMA-complete. However, even though the problem remains unsolved, weaker versions of it—such as the NLTS [ 27 , 7 ] and NLSS [ 28 ] conjectures—have surfaced in the hope of providing evidence for QPCP. While the NLTS hamiltonians were first constructed in [ 7 ] , NLSS conjecture still remains unsolved. Weaker versions of the NLSS conjecture were addressed in [ 22 , 23 ] , demonstrating that Clifford and almost-Clifford states—a subclass of sampleable states—have a lower energy bound on Hamiltonians prepared by conjugating the NLTS Hamiltonians from [ 7 ] . In similar spirit, we construct a class of fermionic Hamilltonians for which energy of Gaussian states, a subclass of sampleable fermionic states, is bounded below by a constant. We adapt the technique used in [ 22 ] to our context.

Background on QPCP conjecture

We review Constraint Satisfaction Problem (CSP), Local Hamiltonian Problem (LHP), Quantum-PCP conjecture, NLTS and its variants and all of their relationships.

Constraint Satisfaction Problem:
A constraint satisfaction problem is defined by triple
\langle V,\{D_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n},C\rangle
where
V=\{x_{1},...,x_{n}\}
is a set of variables with each
x_{i}
taking values in a finite domain

is collection of constraints-each a relation

on some subset
S\subseteq V
. A solution is an assignment
f:V\rightarrow\bigcup_{i}D_{i}
such that for every constraint
(S,R)\in C
, the tuple
(f(x)_{x\in S})
lies in
R
.

Simply put, CSP asks for a assignment such that every clause (constraint) in the relation is satisfied and thus the whole expression.

Local Hamiltonian Problem (LHP-
\delta(n)
):
The k-local Hamiltonian of an n-qubit system,
\mathcal{H}=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{H}_{i}
where
m=poly(n)
and each
\mathcal{H}_{i}
acts non-trivially on at most

qubits and without loss of generality has

is

It is trivial to see that LHP-

No Low-energy Sampleable States (NLSS) conjecture:
There exist a family of
O(1)
-local Hamiltonians
\{\mathcal{H}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, where each
\mathcal{H}_{n}
acts on
n

\psi_{n}

n

A significant progress was made towards this conjecture in

Definition 2
.

]
, however, note that it’s a Gaussian Hamiltonian. That is, all of the local terms are Bilinear in Majorana operators and hence, every eigenstate of this Hamiltonian is a Gaussian state, which can be efficiently simulated on a classical simulator, thus nullifying the idea of really having low energy states that are ’complex’ in nature.

Motivated by this we adapt the technique used in
[
22
]
to prepare NLCS & NLACS qubit Hamiltonians, to construct Fermionic Hamiltonians whose energy
\forall
Gaussian states is lower bounded by a constant.

The proof is mainly divided into
three
parts:

In Section
4.1
we introduce the conjugation operation to
\mathcal{H}_{\mathscr{S}_{n}}^{(f)}
and derive the ’rotated’ Hamiltonian
\mathcal{\tilde{H}}_{\mathscr{S}_{n}}^{(f)}
. In Section
4.2
, we present a simple proof that the constructed Hamiltonian
\mathcal{\tilde{H}}_{\mathscr{S}_{n}}^{(f)}
is also an NLTS Hamiltonian. Further, in Section
4.3
we bound the energy of low-energy Gaussian states for these Hamiltonians. Since qPCP conjecture implies the existence of simultaneous NLTS & NLSS qubit Hamiltonians, in the same spirit, Ferm-qPCP conjecture as stated in
[
31
]
analogously implies the existence of simultaneous NLTS & NLGS Hamiltonians.

4.1
Conjugating with Non-Gaussian Unitary operator

We introduce a carefully crafted non-Gaussian rotation operator to incorporate non-Gaussianity in
13
. For further details on the Majorana properties used in the following calculations, we refer the reader to the Preliminaries section
3
.

We denote a Non-Gaussian ’one-gate rotation operator’ as
D^{(f)}
, and the rotated Fermionic Hamiltonian
\mathcal{\tilde{H}}_{\mathscr{S}_{n}}^{(f)}
is given as:

\mathcal{\tilde{H}}_{\mathscr{S}_{n}}^{(f)}=D^{(f)}\mathcal{H}_{\mathscr{S}_{n}}^{(f)}{D^{(f)}}^{\dagger}

Now from
13
, we can directly work in terms of rotating individual terms of the local Fermionic Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}_{\mathscr{S}_{n}}^{(f)}
. Thus it suffices to rotate a general
\Pi_{\bar{X}}^{(f)}
&
\Pi_{\bar{Z}}^{(f)}
local term to talk about
\mathcal{\tilde{H}}_{\mathscr{S}_{n}}^{(f)}

\tilde{\Pi}_{\bar{X}}^{(f)}=D^{(f)}\Pi_{\bar{X}}^{(f)}{D^{(f)}}^{\dagger};\tilde{\Pi}_{\bar{Z}}^{(f)}=D^{(f)}\Pi_{\bar{Z}}^{(f)}{D^{(f)}}^{\dagger}

We derive the expression for
\tilde{\Pi}_{\bar{X}}^{(f)}
and state the expression for
\tilde{\Pi}_{\bar{Z}}^{(f)}
as the calculations for both go hand in hand.

With trial and error with reckon one possible rotation operator
D^{(f)}=exp(i\theta\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}c_{2j-1}c_{2j+1}c_{k3}c_{k4})
where
c_{k3}
and
c_{k4}
are such that, without loss of generality
\{c_{k3},c_{k4}\}=\{c_{k_{l}},c_{2j}\}=\{c_{k_{l}},c_{2j-1}\}=\{c_{k_{l}},c_{2j+1}\}=0,\forall l\in\{3,4\}
. That is, we can always pick out indices
k_{3},k_{4}
such that they don’t collide with the majorana operators’ indices that are used in the expression of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathscr{S}_{n}}^{(f)}
. To get a clear understanding of this, we refer to the diagram in the Preliminaries section
3
.

Applying this on
\Pi_{\bar{X}}^{(f)}
we get,

\displaystyle\tilde{\Pi}_{\bar{X}}^{(f)}=D^{(f)}\Pi_{\bar{X}}^{(f)}{D^{(f)}}^{\dagger},

Denote
c_{2j-1}c_{2j+1}c_{k3}c_{k4}
by
\mathscr{C}_{K,j}

We have,

\displaystyle\tilde{\Pi}_{\bar{X}}^{(f)}=\left(e^{i\theta\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\mathscr{C}_{K,j}}\right)\left(\frac{I-\bigotimes_{j=1}^{k}(ic_{2j-1}c_{2n+j})}{2}\right){\left(e^{i\theta\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\mathscr{C}_{K,j}}\right)}^{\dagger},

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[\mathbb{I}-\bigotimes\limits_{j=1}^{k}(i)^{k}\left(e^{i\theta\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\mathscr{C}_{K,j}}\right)c_{2j-1}c_{2n+j}{\left(e^{i\theta\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\mathscr{C}_{K,j}}\right)}^{\dagger}\right]

Analyzing the Gaussian term here,

\displaystyle\left(e^{i\theta\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\mathscr{C}_{K,j}}\right)c_{2j-1}c_{2n+j}{\left(e^{i\theta\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\mathscr{C}_{K,j}}\right)}^{\dagger}=

\displaystyle\left(e^{i\theta\mathscr{C}_{K,1}}e^{i\theta\mathscr{C}_{K,2}}...e^{i\theta\mathscr{C}_{K,j}}...e^{i\theta\mathscr{C}_{K,k}}\right).\left(c_{2j-1}c_{2n+j}\right).\left(e^{i\theta\mathscr{C}_{K,1}}e^{i\theta\mathscr{C}_{K,2}}...e^{i\theta\mathscr{C}_{K,j}}...e^{i\theta\mathscr{C}_{K,k}}\right)^{\dagger},

It is straightforward to show that
\mathscr{C}_{K,j}
is a Hermitian operator and
\left[\mathscr{C}_{K,j},\mathscr{C}_{K,j^{\prime}}\right]
for
j\neq j^{\prime}
. Further, we conclude that given these properties of
\mathscr{C}_{K,j}
operator, the previous expression reduces to:

\displaystyle\left(e^{i\theta\mathscr{C}_{K,j}}\right)\left(c_{2j-1}c_{2n+j}\right){\left(e^{i\theta\mathscr{C}_{K,j}}\right)}^{\dagger}=\left(e^{i\theta\mathscr{C}_{K,j}}\right)\left(c_{2j-1}c_{2n+j}\right)\left(e^{-i\theta\mathscr{C}_{K,j}}\right),(\because\mathscr{C}_{K,j}={\mathscr{C}_{K,j}}^{\dagger})

Define
\mathbf{c}_{j}\equiv c_{2j-1}c_{2n+j}
for convenience. Before moving on, we explore the relation between
\mathbf{c}_{j}
&
\mathscr{C}_{K,j}
. It’s easy to see that,
\mathscr{C}_{K,j}.\mathbf{c}_{j}=(c_{2j-1}c_{2j+1}c_{k_{3}}c_{k_{4}})(c_{2j-1}c_{2n+j})=-\mathbf{c}_{j}.\mathscr{C}_{K,j}\implies\left\{\mathscr{C}_{K,j},\mathbf{c}_{j}\right\}=0

and,
\mathscr{C}_{K,j}.\mathscr{C}_{K,j}=\mathbb{I}
.

Using the above relations we can finally derive the expression for
\tilde{\Pi}_{\bar{X}}^{(f)}
:

\displaystyle\left(e^{i\theta\mathscr{C}_{K,j}}\right)\mathbf{c}_{j}{\left(e^{i\theta\mathscr{C}_{K,j}}\right)}^{\dagger}=\left(\cos\theta\mathbb{I}+i\sin\theta\mathscr{C}_{K,j}\right)\left(\mathbf{c}_{j}\right)\left(\cos\theta\mathbb{I}-i\sin\theta\mathscr{C}_{K,j}\right)

\displaystyle=\cos^{2}\theta\mathbf{c}_{j}-i\sin\theta\cos\theta\mathbf{c}_{j}\mathscr{C}_{K,j}+i\sin\theta\cos\theta\mathscr{C}_{K,j}\mathbf{c}_{j}+\sin^{2}\theta\mathscr{C}_{K,j}\mathbf{c}\mathscr{C}_{K,j}

\displaystyle=\left(\cos 2\theta\mathbb{I}+i\sin 2\theta\mathscr{C}_{K,j}\right)\mathbf{c}_{j}=e^{i2\theta\mathscr{C}_{K,j}}c_{2j-1}c_{2n+j}

(14)

Hence, finally we have the expression for
\tilde{\Pi}_{\bar{X}}^{(f)}
:

\displaystyle\tilde{\Pi}_{\bar{X}}^{(f)}=D^{(f)}\Pi_{\bar{X}}^{(f)}{D^{(f)}}^{\dagger}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\mathbb{I}-\bigotimes\limits_{j=1}^{k}e^{i2\theta\mathscr{C}_{K,j}}ic_{2j-1}c_{2n+j}\right]

Or, opening up the exponential term with Euler’s identity will lead us to,

\displaystyle\tilde{\Pi}_{\bar{X}}^{(f)}=D^{(f)}\Pi_{\bar{X}}^{(f)}{D^{(f)}}^{\dagger}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\mathbb{I}-\bigotimes\limits_{j=1}^{k}\left(\left(\cos 2\theta\right)ic_{2j-1}c_{2n+1}+\left(-\sin 2\theta\right)\left(c_{2j-1}c_{2j+1}c_{k_{3}}c_{k_{4}}\right).\left(c_{2j-1}c_{2n+j}\right)\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[\mathbb{I}-\bigotimes\limits_{j=1}^{k}\left(\left(\cos 2\theta\right)ic_{2j-1}c_{2n+1}+\left(\sin 2\theta\right)c_{2j+1}c_{k_{3}}c_{k_{4}}c_{2n+j}\right)\right]=\frac{1}{2}\left[\mathbb{I}-\bigotimes\limits_{j=1}^{k}h_{\bar{X}}^{j}\right]

(15)

where we define
h_{\bar{X}}^{j}\equiv\left(\cos 2\theta\right)ic_{2j-1}c_{2n+1}+\left(\sin 2\theta\right)c_{2j+1}c_{k_{3}}c_{k_{4}}c_{2n+j}

Going through an almost similar calculation as above, we also obtain the expression for
\tilde{\Pi}_{\bar{Z}}^{(f)}
. We compile both of the expressions below, where we define
h_{\bar{Z}}^{j}\equiv\left(\cos 2\theta\right)ic_{2j-1}c_{2n+1}-\left(\sin 2\theta\right)c_{2j+1}c_{k_{3}}c_{k_{4}}c_{2j}

\displaystyle\tilde{\Pi}_{\bar{X}}^{(f)}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\mathbb{I}-\bigotimes\limits_{j=1}^{k}h_{\bar{X}}^{j}\right];\tilde{\Pi}_{\bar{Z}}^{(f)}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\mathbb{I}-\bigotimes\limits_{j=1}^{k}h_{\bar{Z}}^{j}\right]

(16)

The local Hamiltonian terms in
\mathcal{\tilde{H}}_{\mathscr{S}_{n}}^{(f)}
are composed of terms that look like above, with each term having their own value for number of Fermionic modes over which it acts non-trivially,
k
.

4.2
Simultaneous F-NLGS & NLTS

As discussed before, analogously to qubit-qPCP conjecture, Ferm-qPCP conjecture implies the existence of simultaneous NLTS and F-NLGS Fermionic Hamiltonians. Here we provide a simple argument as to why our constructed Hamiltonians when created using conjugation with a Non-Gaussian, constant depth unitary, preserves the NLTS property. Formally, we prove the following lemma which closely follows
Lemma 4.
in
[
22
]
.

Lemma 14
.

Given
\left\{\mathcal{H}_{\mathscr{S}_{n}}^{(f)}\right\}
a family of
\epsilon
-NLTS local Hamiltonians, where
n
is the number of Fermionic modes &
W=\{W_{n}\}
is a family of constant-depth Non-Gaussian circuits. Then, the family of conjugated Hamiltonians
\left\{\mathcal{H}_{n}^{(f)}\right\}
is also
\epsilon
-NLTS.

Proof.

For the sake of the argument, say
\{\mathcal{\tilde{H}}_{\mathscr{S}_{n}}^{(f)}\}
is not NLTS. By definition, for every
\epsilon^{\prime}>0
there is an
n
and a constant depth, Non-Gaussian circuit such that
U_{\epsilon^{\prime},n}\ket{0}^{\otimes n}
is an
\epsilon^{\prime}
-low energy state of
\mathcal{\tilde{H}}_{n}^{(f)}=W_{n}^{\dagger}\mathcal{H}_{n}^{(f)}W_{n}
, i.e.,
\bra{0}^{\otimes n}U_{\epsilon^{\prime},n}^{\dagger}W_{n}^{\dagger}\mathcal{H}_{n}^{(f)}W_{n}U_{\epsilon^{\prime},n}\ket{0}^{\otimes n}<E_{0}(W_{n}^{\dagger}\mathcal{H}_{n}^{(f)}W_{n})+\epsilon^{\prime}

Since,
W_{n}^{\dagger}
is a unitary operator, ground state energy.
E_{0}
of
\mathcal{H}_{n}^{(f)}
&
\mathcal{\tilde{H}}_{n}^{(f)}
are equal.

\therefore
defining
\ket{\Psi_{\epsilon,n}}\equiv W_{n}U_{\epsilon,n}\ket{0}^{\otimes n}
, we have,

\bra{\Psi_{\epsilon,n}}\mathcal{H}_{n}^{(f)}\ket{\Psi_{\epsilon,n}}<E_{0}\left(\mathcal{H}_{n}^{(f)}\right)+\epsilon

Thus
\ket{\Psi_{\epsilon,n}}
is an
\epsilon
-low energy state. Since
W_{n}U_{\epsilon,n}
is a constant depth circuit.

Implying,
\mathcal{H}_{n}^{(f)}
has an
\epsilon
-low energy trivial state, contradicting assumption of
\epsilon
-NLTS
∎

4.3
Energy lower bound on Gaussian states

Finally having the expression of the rotated Hamiltonian
\mathcal{\tilde{H}}_{\mathscr{S}_{n}}^{(f)}
and proving the ’NLTS preservation’ of conjugation operation, we are finally read to prove our main result
Theorem
13
.

The system under consideration has
3n/2
- fermionic modes with Hamiltonian
\mathcal{\tilde{H}}_{\mathscr{S}_{n}}^{(f)}
:

\mathcal{\tilde{H}}_{\mathscr{S}_{n}}^{(f)}=\frac{1}{|\mathscr{S}_{n}|}\sum\limits_{S\in\langle\mathscr{S}_{n}\rangle}\Pi_{S}^{(f)}

(17)

A Gaussian state for this system,
\psi
on
3n/2
- Fermionic modes,
\psi=\sum_{j}p_{j}\ket{\varphi_{j}}\bra{\varphi_{j}}
, is a mixture of pure gaussian states
\ket{\varphi_{j}}
on
3n/2
modes.

The energy of this state is given by,

\displaystyle\Tr\left[\psi\mathcal{H}_{\mathscr{S}_{n}}^{(f)}\right]=\sum\limits_{j}p_{j}\bra{\varphi_{j}}\mathcal{H}_{\mathscr{S}_{n}}^{(f)}\ket{\varphi_{j}}=\frac{1}{|\mathscr{S}_{n}|}\sum\limits_{S\in\mathscr{S}_{n}}\sum\limits_{j}p_{j}\bra{\varphi_{j}}\Pi_{S}^{(f)}\ket{\varphi_{j}}

(18)

With this little argument, it suffices to show a lower bound on ’local energy’ of every possible pure gaussian state on
3n/2
- Fermionic modes, that is, every
\bra{\varphi_{j}}\Pi_{S}^{(f)}\ket{\varphi_{j}}
term where
\ket{\varphi_{j}}
is a pure gaussian state for
3n
-Majorana system.

Lemma 15
.

There exists a lower bound
L_{k}
on
\bra{\eta}\tilde{\Pi}_{S}^{(f)}\ket{\eta}
where
\ket{\eta}
is a pure gaussian state, where
k
is the number of Fermionic modes over which
\tilde{\Pi}_{S}^{(f)}
acts non-trivially.

Proof.

Say the reduced set of Fermionic modes is defined as
R_{S}\subset\left[n\right]
, such that,
\Pi_{S}^{(f)}
only works non trivially on the modes contained in
R_{S}
set.

\displaystyle\Pi_{S}^{(f)}=\Pi_{S}^{(f)}|_{R_{S}}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{\left[n\right]\backslash R_{S}},

So,
\bra{\eta}\tilde{\Pi}_{S}^{(f)}\ket{\eta}=\Tr\left[\eta_{R_{S}}\tilde{\Pi}_{S}^{(f)}|_{R_{S}}\right]
where
\eta_{R_{S}}\equiv\Tr_{-R_{S}}\left[\ket{\eta}\bra{\eta}\right]
. Since
\ket{\eta}
, a is a 3n/2 - Fermionic pure gaussian state, its reduction on
R_{S}
modes is a pure gaussian state too.

From above, we have,

\bra{\eta}\tilde{\Pi}_{S}^{(f)}\ket{\eta}=\bra{\eta_{R_{S}}}\tilde{\Pi}_{S}^{(f)}\ket{\eta_{R_{S}}}.

(19)

Thus, for the remaining proof we’ll find it suffice to show properties such as energy lower bounds for reduced pure gaussian states on ’non-trivial modes’.

We arrive at the core part of showing our main result. For the rest part of the proof we will denote
\ket{\eta_{R_{S}}}
as
\ket{\xi}
. Since
\tilde{\Pi}_{S}^{(f)}
is either
\tilde{\Pi}_{\bar{X}}^{(f)}
or
\tilde{\Pi}_{\bar{Z}}^{(f)}
upto a certain permutation, we show an energy lower bound for both of these.

\displaystyle\bra{\xi}\tilde{\Pi}_{\bar{X}}^{(f)}\ket{\xi}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\mathbb{I}-\bra{\xi}\bigotimes\limits_{j=1}^{k}h_{\bar{X}}^{j}\ket{\xi}\right];\bra{\xi}\tilde{\Pi}_{\bar{Z}}^{(f)}\ket{\xi}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\mathbb{I}-\bra{\xi}\bigotimes\limits_{j=1}^{k}h_{\bar{Z}}^{j}\ket{\xi}\right]

(20)

To lower bound these quantities, it makes sense to upper bound the non-trivial terms inside these expressions. Let’s start with
\bra{\xi}\bigotimes\limits_{j=1}^{k}h_{\bar{X}}^{j}\ket{\xi}
,

\displaystyle\bra{\xi}\bigotimes\limits_{j=1}^{k}h_{\bar{X}}^{j}\ket{\xi}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\bra{\xi}h_{\bar{X}}^{j}\ket{\xi}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\bra{\xi}\left[\left(\cos 2\theta\right)ic_{2j-1}c_{2n+j}+\left(sin2\theta\right)c_{2j-1}c_{k_{3}}c_{k_{4}}c_{2n+j}\right]\ket{\xi}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\left(i\cos 2\theta\right)\bra{\xi}c_{2j-1}c_{2n+j}\ket{\xi}+\left(\sin 2\theta\right)\bra{\xi}c_{2j+1}c_{k_{3}}c_{k_{4}}c_{2n+j}\ket{\xi}

The foremost inequality follows from the fact that energy term for each fermionic mode is additive in nature. The terms
\bra{\xi}c_{2j-1}c_{2n+j}\ket{\xi}
and
\bra{\xi}c_{2j+1}c_{k_{3}}c_{k_{4}}c_{2n+j}\ket{\xi}
for each
j\in\left[n\right]
can be shown to be lower bounded by using the unique properties of the
covariance matrix
for gaussian states and
Wick’s theorem
. We refer the reader to preliminaries section
3
for more information.

We have,

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\bra{\xi}h_{\bar{X}}^{j}\ket{\xi}=\left(\cos 2\theta\right)\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\Gamma_{2j-1,2n+j}+\left(\sin 2\theta\right)\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\left(-\Gamma_{2j+1,k_{3}}\Gamma_{k_{4},2n+j}+\Gamma_{2j+1,k_{3}}\Gamma_{k_{4},2n+j}-\Gamma_{2j+1,2n+j}\Gamma_{k_{3},2n+j}\right)

Using the fact that, for all pure gaussian states absolute values of all the entries of covariance matrix is upper bounded by 1, we have a rough upper bound,

\bra{\xi}\bigotimes\limits_{j=1}^{k}h_{\bar{X}}^{j}\ket{\xi}\leq k\left(\cos 2\theta+3\sin 2\theta\right)\leq k_{0}\left(\cos 2\theta+3\sin 2\theta\right)

(21)

where
k_{0}
is the largest ”non-triviality value” for a local term that appears in the Hamiltonian expression
\mathcal{\tilde{H}}_{\mathscr{S}_{n}}^{(f)}
hence
k\leq k_{0}
. Using a similar argument we have the same upper bound for

\bra{\xi}\bigotimes\limits_{j=1}^{k}h_{\bar{Z}}^{j}\ket{\xi}\leq k_{0}\left(\cos 2\theta+3\sin 2\theta\right)

(22)

With
21
,
22
and
19
for
20
we have,

\displaystyle\bra{\eta}\tilde{\Pi}_{\bar{X}}^{(f)}\ket{\eta}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\left[1-k_{0}\left(\cos 2\theta+3\sin 2\theta\right)\right]

(23)

\displaystyle\bra{\eta}\tilde{\Pi}_{\bar{Z}}^{(f)}\ket{\eta}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\left[1-k_{0}\left(\cos 2\theta+3\sin 2\theta\right)\right]

∎

Continuing with our main proof, we use the results of
15
in
18
to obtain our final lower bound on the energy of all (possibly mixed) gaussian state
\psi
:

\displaystyle\Tr\left[\psi\mathcal{H}_{\mathscr{S}_{n}}^{(f)}\right]=\frac{1}{|\mathscr{S}_{n}|}\sum\limits_{S\in\langle\mathscr{S}_{n}\rangle}\sum\limits_{j}p_{j}\bra{\varphi_{j}}\Pi_{S}^{(f)}\ket{\varphi_{j}}\geq\frac{1}{|\mathscr{S}_{n}|}\sum\limits_{S\in\langle\mathscr{S}_{n}\rangle}\sum\limits_{j}p_{j}\frac{1}{2}\left[1-k_{0}\left(\cos 2\theta+3\sin 2\theta\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathscr{S}_{n}|}\sum\limits_{S\in\langle\mathscr{S}_{n}\rangle}\frac{1}{2}\left[1-k_{0}\left(\cos 2\theta+3\sin 2\theta\right)\right]=\frac{1}{2}\left[1-k_{0}\left(\cos 2\theta+3\sin 2\theta\right)\right]

(24)

As we are dealing with
O(1)
-local Fermionic Hamiltonians,
k_{0}
is a constant and does not scale with number of Fermionic modes. Now, as a ’final touch’ we also need to be careful about choosing the parameter
\theta
so that our energy lower bound is non-negative and
k_{0}\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left[1-k_{0}\left(\cos 2\theta+3\sin 2\theta\right)\right]>0,

(25)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left(\cos 2\theta+3\sin 2\theta\right)}>k_{0}\geq 2

(26)

Suggesting,

\sin(2\theta+\phi)\in\left(0,\frac{1}{k_{0}\sqrt{10}}\right),\quad\text{with }\phi=\arcsin\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{10}}\right)

Thus, for such a value of
\theta
we have a family of Fermionic Hamiltonians
\left\{\mathcal{\tilde{H}}_{\mathscr{S}_{n}}^{(f)}\right\}
obtained from rotating the family of Fermionic NLTS Hamiltonians
\left\{\mathcal{H}_{\mathscr{S}_{n}}^{(f)}\right\}
as obtained in
[
31
]
.

∎

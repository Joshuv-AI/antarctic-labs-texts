Brauer groups of varieties over local fields of finite characteristic
Amalendu Krishna, Subhadip Majumder

Abstract




2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 14G17, Secondary 19F15

Abstract.

We show that the non-log version of Kato’s ramification filtration on the Brauer group of a separated and finite type regular scheme over a positive characteristic local field coincides with the evaluation filtration. This extends a recent result of Bright-Newton to positive characteristics. Among several applications, we extend some results of Ieronymou, Saito-Sato and Kai to positive characteristics.

1.
Introduction

This paper falls within the area of arithmetic geometry whose goal is to
extend several important results concerning the Brauer group and zero-cycles
on varieties over characteristic zero local fields to varieties over
positive characteristic local fields. In this section, we explain the background
and statements of the main results.

1.1.
The Bright-Newton theorem

Let
p>0
be a prime number and let
k
be a henselian discrete
valuation field whose ring of integers
{\mathcal{O}}_{k}
is
an excellent ring and residue field
{\mathbb{F}}
is finite of characteristic
p
.
Let
{\mathfrak{m}}=(\pi)
denote the maximal ideal of
{\mathcal{O}}_{k}
.
Let
{\mathcal{X}}
be a faithfully flat, separated and finite type
{\mathcal{O}}_{k}
-scheme which
is regular. We write
X={\mathcal{X}}\times_{S}{\rm Spec\,}(k),\ {\mathcal{X}}_{s}={\mathcal{X}}\times_{S}{\rm Spec\,}({\mathbb{F}})

and
Y=({\mathcal{X}}_{s})_{\rm red}
, where
S={\rm Spec\,}({\mathcal{O}}_{k})
. We assume that

Y
is a simple normal crossing divisor on
{\mathcal{X}}
with irreducible components

Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{r}
. We do not assume that
X
is smooth over
k
.

In order to study the ramification in the
cohomology groups
H^{q}_{\textnormal{\'{e}t}}(X,{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}}(q-1))
for
q\geq 1
,
Kato
[
Kato-89
, § 7, 8]
introduced a ramification filtration on these
cohomology groups. This filtration for
q=2
induces a similar filtration
on the Brauer group of
X
, given by

(1.1)

{\rm fil}_{n}{\operatorname{Br}}(X)=\{\chi\in{\operatorname{Br}}(X)|\chi_{i}\in{\rm fil}_{n}{\operatorname{Br}}(K_{i})\ \forall\ i\}

for
n\geq 0
. Here,

K_{i}
is the henselization of the function field of
{\mathcal{X}}
at the generic point
of
Y_{i}
,
\chi_{i}
is the restriction of
\chi
to
{\operatorname{Br}}(K_{i})
and

{\rm fil}_{n}{\operatorname{Br}}(K_{i})
is the set of all elements
\chi^{\prime}\in{\operatorname{Br}}(K_{i})
such that
cup product
\{\chi^{\prime}_{L},1+{\mathfrak{m}}_{K_{i}}^{n+1}{\mathcal{O}}_{L}\}
is zero in

H^{3}_{\textnormal{\'{e}t}}(L,{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}}(2))
for every henselian discrete valuation field
L
over

K_{i}
such that
{\mathcal{O}}_{K_{i}}\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{L}
and
{\mathfrak{m}}_{L}={\mathfrak{m}}_{K_{i}}{\mathcal{O}}_{L}

(cf §
2.4
).
We shall refer to
{\rm fil}_{\bullet}{\operatorname{Br}}(X)
as the Kato filtration of
{\operatorname{Br}}(X)
.

When
{\mathcal{X}}
is smooth over
S
, Bright-Newton introduced another filtration on

{\operatorname{Br}}(X)
in
[
Bright-Newton
]
, called the evaluation filtration.
This filtration is defined in terms of the size of the adic neighborhoods of closed
points of
X
over which a Brauer class on
X
has constant evaluation (cf.
§
3.3
).
They showed that if
k
is a
p
-adic field and
{\mathcal{X}}
is smooth over
{\mathcal{O}}_{k}
,
then the evaluation filtration on
{\operatorname{Br}}(X)
coincides with the non-log version
(for
H^{1}_{\textnormal{\'{e}t}}(X,{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}})
, this version was defined by Matsuda
[
Matsuda
]
)
of the Kato filtration.

The Kato filtration of
{\operatorname{Br}}(X)
determines the ramification of a Brauer class
on
X
along
Y
. For instance,
{\rm fil}_{0}{\operatorname{Br}}(X)
can be viewed as
the set of those Brauer classes on
X
which are ‘tamely ramified along
Y
’
(cf. Proposition
2.6
). On the other hand, the evaluation filtration
determines the extent of the local constancy of a Brauer class on
X
when it
is evaluated on the closed points of
X
. This allows one, for instance,
to determine the relevance of a Brauer class in
the Brauer-Manin obstruction to Hasse principle and weak approximation.

The result of Bright-Newton (cf.
[
Bright-Newton
, Thm. A]
)
has turned out to be of considerable importance in arithmetic geometry
and has led to the solutions of several important questions about the Brauer group
of smooth projective varieties over local and global fields of characteristic zero.
The objective of this paper is to extend the work of Bright-Newton
to positive characteristic and give applications
to the solutions of some important open questions about zero-cycles and
Brauer groups of smooth projective varieties over local fields.

We assume from now on that
{\rm char}(k)=p
.
We introduce a modified version of the Bright-Newton filtration which
works in more general situations (that we work in) than when
{\mathcal{X}}/S
is smooth
(however, see Remark
1.2
).
Let
X_{(0)}
be the set of all closed points of
X
and let

X^{o}_{\operatorname{\rm fin}}
be the set of those points
P\in X_{(0)}
whose
scheme-theoretic closure
\overline{\{P\}}
in
{\mathcal{X}}
is regular, finite over
S
and
is disjoint from the singular locus of
Y
.
For any
n\geq 1
and
P\in X_{(0)}
, we define a subset
B(P,n)\subset X^{o}_{\operatorname{\rm fin}}

which consists of all points
Q\in X^{o}_{\operatorname{\rm fin}}
such that

\overline{\{Q\}}\times_{{\mathcal{X}}}nY=\overline{\{P\}}\times_{S}nY
(cf. §
3.3
).
We let
B(P,0)=\{Q\in X^{o}_{{\operatorname{\rm fin}}}|[k(Q):k]=[k(P):k]\}
.
For
{\mathcal{A}}\in{\operatorname{Br}}(X)
, we let

{\rm ev}_{\mathcal{A}}\colon X_{(0)}\to{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}}
be the evaluation map
obtained by restricting

\chi
to closed points and applying the Hasse invariant map for local fields.

For
n\geq-1
, we let

(1.2)

{\rm Ev}_{n}{\operatorname{Br}}(X)=\left\{{\mathcal{A}}\in{\operatorname{Br}}(X)|{\rm ev}_{\mathcal{A}}\ \text{is constant on}\ B(P,n+1)\ \text{ for all }P\in X^{o}_{\operatorname{\rm fin}}\right\};

{\rm Ev}_{-2}{\operatorname{Br}}(X)=\left\{{\mathcal{A}}\in{\operatorname{Br}}(X)|{\rm ev}_{\mathcal{A}}\ \text{is zero on}\ X^{o}_{\operatorname{\rm fin}}\right\}.

We shall refer to
{\rm Ev}_{\bullet}{\operatorname{Br}}(X)
as the evaluation filtration on

{\operatorname{Br}}(X)
.
We let
y_{i}
denote the generic point of
Y_{i}
and let

{\rm rsw}^{2}_{K_{i},n}\colon\frac{{\rm fil}_{n}{\operatorname{Br}}(X)}{{\rm fil}_{n-1}{\operatorname{Br}}(X)}\to\Omega^{2}_{k(y_{i})}\bigoplus\Omega^{1}_{k(y_{i})}
be the refined Swan conductor map
for
n\geq 1
(cf. §
4.2
).
Let
g\colon Y\to{\rm Spec\,}({\mathbb{F}})
be the projection.

The first main result of this paper is the following analogue of the theorem
of Bright-Newton in positive characteristic.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{X}}
be as above and let
Y^{o}
denote the regular locus of
Y
.
Then there exists a canonical map

\partial_{X}\colon{\rm fil}_{0}{\operatorname{Br}}(X)\to H^{1}_{\textnormal{\'{e}t}}(Y^{o},{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}})
.
Moreover, we have the following.

(1)

If
n\geq 1
, then

{\rm Ev}_{n}{\operatorname{Br}}(X)=\left\{{\mathcal{A}}\in{\rm fil}_{n+1}{\operatorname{Br}}(X)|{\rm rsw}^{2}_{K_{i},n+1}({\mathcal{A}})=(\alpha,0)\in\Omega^{2}_{k(y_{i})}\bigoplus\Omega^{1}_{k(y_{i})}\ \forall\ i\right\}.

(2)

{\rm Ev}_{0}{\operatorname{Br}}(X)={\rm fil}_{0}{\operatorname{Br}}(X)
.

(3)

If
{\mathcal{X}}_{s}
is reduced with geometrically connected irreducible components, then

{\rm Ev}_{-1}{\operatorname{Br}}(X)=\left\{{\mathcal{A}}\in{\rm fil}_{0}{\operatorname{Br}}(X)|\partial_{X}({\mathcal{A}})\in\ {\rm Image}\left(H^{1}_{\textnormal{\'{e}t}}({\mathbb{F}},{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}})\xrightarrow{{g}^{*}}H^{1}_{\textnormal{\'{e}t}}(Y^{o},{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}})\right)\right\}.

(4)

{\rm Ev}_{-2}{\operatorname{Br}}(X)={\operatorname{Br}}({\mathcal{X}})
.

We make some remarks about Theorem
1.1
.

Remark 1.2
.

(1) The item (3) of Theorem
1.1
remains true even when
{\mathcal{X}}_{s}
is not
reduced provided all its components occur with the same multiplicity in its
presentation as a Weil divisor on
{\mathcal{X}}
(cf. Lemma
).

(2) The above definition of the evaluation filtration is motivated by that of
Bright-Newton but is slightly different. The modification in the new definition is
necessary in order to handle the case (as we do in this paper) where the
special fiber of
{\mathcal{X}}
is not reduced. Although it not clear from the
definition that the evaluation filtration defined above coincides with that of

[
Bright-Newton
]
when

{\mathcal{X}}
is smooth over
{\mathcal{O}}_{k}
(the case considered in
op
. cit.), we shall show
later in this paper (cf. Theorem
) that the two filtrations do
coincide under the smoothness assumption.

(3) While the study of the relation between Kato and evaluation filtrations
in characteristic zero is done in
[
Bright-Newton
]
under the assumption
that
{\mathcal{X}}
is a smooth
{\mathcal{O}}_{k}
-scheme, no such assumption is made in
Theorem
1.1
. It does not also assume that
X
is a smooth
k
-scheme.
Note that smoothness of
X
in characteristic zero is an automatic
consequence of its regularity but that is not the case over positive
characteristic local fields. Theorem
1.1
assumes only that

{\mathcal{X}}
is regular and
Y\subset{\mathcal{X}}
is a simple normal crossing divisor.

(4) We show in the course of proving Theorem
1.1
that
one has the inclusions
{\rm RHS}\subset{\rm LHS}

in parts (1) and (2) of Theorem
1.1

even if
Y
is not a simple normal crossing divisor in

{\mathcal{X}}
. In fact, our expectation is that when suitably modified,
the full version of Theorem
1.1
would hold without this assumption.
This is currently a work in progress by the authors.

(5) We show that the evaluation filtration can be fully determined by
checking the evaluation of Brauer classes on
X
at only those closed
points of
X
whose closures in
{\mathcal{X}}
are transverse to
Y
. If
{\mathcal{X}}_{s}
is reduced,
we further show that this filtration can, in fact, be
fully determined by checking the evaluation of Brauer classes on
X
at only its
unramified closed points, instead of checking at all points of
X^{o}_{\operatorname{\rm fin}}
.

(6) When
X
is a smooth projective geometrically connected curve over
k
,
Yamazaki
[
Yamazaki
]
defined a filtration on
{\operatorname{Br}}(X)
using the
Brauer-Manin pairing and showed that his filtration coincides with the Kato
filtration. A combination of Yamazaki’s result and Theorem
1.1
implies that
his filtration is the same as the evaluation filtration.

We shall now explain the applications of Theorem
1.1
.

1.2.
Theorems of Ieronymou

One of the applications of Theorem
1.1
and its mixed
characteristic analogue in
[
Bright-Newton
]
is to determine conditions
on a variety
X
over a local field
K
under which all Brauer classes
on
X
have constant values on the
K
-points of
X
.
This problem is important in the study of the Brauer-Manin obstruction to
Hasse principle and weak approximation. In
[
Ieronymou
]
,
Ieronymou proved several
results of this kind in characteristic zero. One of these says that
if
K
is a
p
-adic field and
{\mathcal{X}}
is a smooth
projective scheme over
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
with generic fiber
X
such that the special
fiber is separably rationally connected (cf.
[
Kollar-Szabo
]
),
then every Brauer class in
{\operatorname{Br}}(X)

induces the constant evaluation map on
X(K)
. He proved a similar result if the
generic fiber of
{\mathcal{X}}
is an Enriques surface (cf.
[
CDL
]
).

Among several applications of Theorem
1.1
,
we prove the following extensions of
the theorems of Ieronymou to positive characteristic.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{X}}
be as in Theorem
1.1
and assume additionally that
{\mathcal{X}}
is
smooth and projective over
S
such that the special fiber
Y
is separably rationally
connected. Then

{{\rm ev}}_{{\mathcal{A}}}\colon X(k)\to{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}}

is a constant map for every
{\mathcal{A}}\in{\operatorname{Br}}(X)
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{X}}
be as in Theorem
1.1
and assume additionally that
{\mathcal{X}}

is smooth and projective over
S
such that the generic fiber
X

is an Enriques surface.
Let
{\mathcal{A}}\in{\operatorname{Br}}(X)
have odd order. Then the evaluation
map
{{\rm ev}}_{{\mathcal{A}}}\colon X(k)\to{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}}
is constant.

Remark 1.5
.

In the characteristic zero version of Theorem
1.3
proven in

[
Ieronymou
]
, it is assumed that
X
is geometrically integral and that the
residue characteristic of the local field is an odd prime.
But we do not need any of these hypotheses.

1.3.
The theorem of Saito-Sato

If
K
is any local field and
X
is a smooth, connected and complete
K
-variety,
then there is a natural pairing (known as the Brauer-Manin pairing)

(1.3)

{\rm CH}_{0}(X)\times{\operatorname{Br}}(X)\to{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}}

which is functorial with respect to proper maps of complete
K
-varieties.
Here,
{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
is the Chow group of 0-cycles on
X
.

When
X
is geometrically integral of dimension one, then Lichtenbaum

[
Lichtenbaum
]
(in characteristic zero) and Saito
[
Saito-Invent
]

(in positive characterstic) showed that (
1.3
) is a perfect
pairing and induces an isomorphism

(1.4)

A_{0}(X)\xrightarrow{\cong}{\rm Hom}({{\operatorname{Br}}(X)}/{{\operatorname{Br}}_{0}(X)},{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}}),

where
A_{0}(X)\subset{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
is the subgroup of degree zero 0-cycles and

{\operatorname{Br}}_{0}(X)
is the image of the pull-back map
{\operatorname{Br}}(k)\to{\operatorname{Br}}(X)
.
It was an open question whether the results of Lichtenbaum and Saito extend to
higher dimensions until Parimala-Suresh
[
Parimala-Suresh
]
showed that
(
1.3
) has a non-zero left kernel in general if
\text{\rm dim}(X)=2
.

To say something about the right kernel, let us assume that
X
has a regular
model
{\mathcal{X}}
which is proper, flat and finite type over
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
. Then
one observes that
(
1.3
) induces natural homomorphisms (this uses the fact that

{\operatorname{Br}}({\mathcal{O}}_{K})=0
)

(1.5)

\theta_{X}\colon{\operatorname{Br}}(X)\to{\rm Hom}({\rm CH}_{0}(X),{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}});

(1.6)

\theta^{\star}_{X}\colon A_{0}(X)\to{\rm Hom}({{\operatorname{Br}}(X)}/{{\operatorname{Br}}({\mathcal{X}})+{\operatorname{Br}}_{0}(X)},{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}}).

In
[
Saito-Sato-ENS
]
, Saito-Sato made a major breakthrough in understanding
the right kernel of (
1.3
) when they showed that
if
{\rm char}(K)=0
and
X
is geometrically integral over
K
, then
one has
{\rm Ker}(\theta_{X})={\operatorname{Br}}({\mathcal{X}})
and
{\rm Coker}(\theta^{\star}_{X})=0
.
The prime-to-
p
part of the result of Saito-Sato was earlier solved by
Colliot-Thélène-Saito
[
CTSaito
]
.
However, this problem has remained open in positive characteristic.

The second main result of this paper is the following analogue of the theorem
of Saito-Sato in positive characteristic when

X
admits a semi-stable (but not necessarily strictly semi-stable) reduction.
This is our second application of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
{\mathcal{X}}
be as in Theorem
1.1
and assume additionally that
{\mathcal{X}}
is
projective over
S
. Then
{\rm Ker}(\theta_{X})={\operatorname{Br}}({\mathcal{X}})
and
\theta^{\star}_{X}
is
surjective.

Remark 1.7
.

We note that the first part of Theorem
1.3
follows immediately from
Theorem
1.1
(4), which says that
{\rm Ev}_{-2}{\operatorname{Br}}(X)={\operatorname{Br}}({\mathcal{X}})
. On the
other hand, to prove the second part, we need extra arguments one of which

We remark here that the methods of

One reason for this is that Mattuck’s theorem

(which is an essential ingredient in op. cit.) does not

As a byproduct of our proof of Theorem
1.9

CTSaito
]

(prime-to-

Theorem 1.8
.

Let

be as in Theorem

. Then there is an inclusion of

\frac{{\operatorname{Br}}(X)}{{\operatorname{Br}}({\mathcal{X}})+{\operatorname{Br}}_{0}(X)}\hookrightarrow\left(\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\infty}}{{\underset{i=1}{\bigoplus}}}{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}\right)\bigoplus T,

1.4.
Theorems of Lichtenbaum and Kai

One knows that if
X
is a smooth projective variety over a non-closed
field
K
and
{\rm Alb}_{X}
is its Albanese variety (which exists by

[
Lang
, p. 45-46]
),
then the albanese map
{\rm alb}_{X}\colon A_{0}(X)\to{\rm Alb}_{X}(K)
may not be
surjective and it is in general a difficult problem to describe its cokernel.

We consider this problem over local fields.
Suppose first that
K
is a local field of characteristic zero and

\overline{K}
is an algebraic closure of
K
with Galois group
\Gamma
.
Let
X
be a geometrically integral smooth projective variety over
K
and

\overline{X}
be the base change of
X
to
\overline{K}
.
If
\text{\rm dim}(X)=1
, Lichtenbaum proved a duality theorem
in
[
Lichtenbaum
]
which says that
{\rm Coker}({\rm alb}_{X})
is canonically
Pontryagin dual to the cokernel of the degree map

{\rm Pic}(\overline{X})^{\Gamma}\to{\operatorname{NS}}(\overline{X})^{\Gamma}
, where
{\operatorname{NS}}(\overline{X})
is the
Néron-severi group of
\overline{X}
.
The analogue of this result of Lichtenbaum in positive characteristic was proven by Saito

[
Saito-Invent
]
.

Lichtenbaum’s duality was generalized to higher dimensions by Kai
[
Kai
]

under the assumption that
X
admits a smooth model over
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
whose Picard
scheme is smooth over
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
.
The extension of the duality theorem of Kai
to varieties over positive characteristic local fields has remained an
open problem. Using Theorem
1.6
, we settle this
in the next result.

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
{\mathcal{X}}
be as in Theorem
1.1
. We assume additionally that
{\mathcal{X}}
is
smooth over
{\mathcal{O}}_{k}
and
X
is geometrically integral over
k
. Let
k_{s}
denote
a separable closure of
k
and let
\Gamma
denote the Galois group of

{k_{s}}/k
. Let
X^{s}
denote the base change of
X
by
k_{s}
.
Assume that the Picard scheme
{\mathbf{Pic}}({\mathcal{X}})
is smooth over
{\mathcal{O}}_{k}
.
Then the cokernel of the albanese map

{\rm alb}_{X}\colon A_{0}(X)\to{\rm Alb}_{X}(k)

is canonically Pontryagin dual to the cokernel of the map

{\rm Pic}(X^{s})^{\Gamma}\to{\rm NS}(X^{s})^{\Gamma}.

An application of Theorem
1.9
is the following finiteness result.
Over characteristic zero local fields, this was proven unconditionally
by Saito-Sujatha
[
Saito-Sujatha
]
.

Corollary 1.10
.

Let
X
be as in Theorem

. Then
{\rm Coker}({\rm alb}_{X})
and

{\rm Coker}({\rm Pic}({X}^{s})^{\Gamma}\to{\operatorname{NS}}({X}^{s})^{\Gamma})

1.6.
Outline of proofs

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is the most challenging part of this work.
As it is complex and long, we provide an abridged description of the key
steps involved in the proof. The main ingredients
of the proof of Theorem
1.1
are Theorem
4.6
, Theorem
4.8

(which were the among the main results of
[
KM-1
]
),
the Kato complex (
) and Theorem
.

There is a fundamental difference in the strategy of the proof of Theorem
1.1

and the one used in characteristic zero in
[
Bright-Newton
]
.
The main strategy of op. cit. is an induction procedure in which the authors
reduce to the case when the Swan conductor of the given Brauer class is zero
which is handled directly via a residue map. The reduction in the Swan conductor
is achieved by a blow-up trick using Kato’s formula for the Swan conductor of
the exceptional divisor under a blow-up. In contrast, our strategy is
an induction procedure in which we reduce the relative dimension
of
{\mathcal{X}}
over
S
to one which we handle directly. This reduction is
achieved by using Lefschetz hyperplane type theorems and
a theorem about the functorial property of the generalized refined Swan conductor,
proved in
[
KM-1
]
. Below we give some details of the steps involved.

The first step in the proof is to show the existence of a filtration

{\rm fil}^{\prime}_{\bullet}H^{q}_{\textnormal{\'{e}t}}(X,{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}}(q-1))
of
H^{q}_{\textnormal{\'{e}t}}(X,{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}}(q-1))
for
q\geq 1

which can be called the non-log version of Kato’s filtration.
This is an analogue of Matsuda’s filtration (cf.
[
Matsuda
]
) on

H^{1}_{\textnormal{\'{e}t}}(X,{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}})
. This induces
a similar filtration on the Brauer group which we denote by

{\rm fil}^{\prime}_{\bullet}{\operatorname{Br}}(X)
. It is this filtration that we compare with the evaluation
filtration. This is done in §
.

To show that
{\rm Ev}_{n}{\operatorname{Br}}(X)
is contained in
{\rm fil}^{\prime}_{n}{\operatorname{Br}}(X)

for
n\geq 0
, we use Theorem
to reduce the proof to the case
when
{\mathcal{X}}
is a relative curve over
S
(with notations as in Theorem
1.1
)
in which case the Matsuda and Kato filtrations on
{\operatorname{Br}}(X)
coincide and all
Brauer classes have type I along the components of the special fiber
Y
.
This coincidence is non-trivial and is a consequence of
Theorems
4.6
and
4.8
which are among the main results
of
[
KM-1
]
.

Now, we argue as follows.
If
\chi\in{\rm Ev}_{n}{\operatorname{Br}}(X)
does not lie in
{\rm fil}^{\prime}_{n}{\operatorname{Br}}(X)
, Theorem
4.8

tells us that its refined Swan conductor does not die in

\Omega^{1}_{Y_{i}}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{Y_{i}}}k(x)
for many closed points

for some
i

after we restrict
\chi
to the local rings of these points.
This allows us to find using the Kato complex
(
) and Corollary

a rational function
f
on
{\mathcal{X}}
and two distinct

We deduce the case

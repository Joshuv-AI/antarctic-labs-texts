Airworthiness Directives; The Boeing Company Airplanes

AGENCY: Federal Aviation Administration (FAA), DOT.

ACTION: Final rule.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------

SUMMARY: The FAA is adopting a new airworthiness directive (AD) for 
certain The Boeing Company Model 737-700, -700C, -800, and -900ER 
series airplanes. This AD was prompted by a leak through the form-in-
place (FiP) gasket at the engine fuel shutoff valve access panel found 
during a leak check. This AD requires a detailed inspection of the 
left- and right-side FiP gasket at the engine fuel shutoff valve access 
panel for correct sealant installation, a fluid leak test of the engine 
fuel shutoff valve access panels for any leak, and applicable on-
condition actions. The FAA is issuing this AD to address the unsafe 
condition on these products.

DATES: This AD is effective December 1, 2026.

SUPPLEMENTARY INFORMATION:

Background

    The FAA issued a notice of proposed rulemaking (NPRM) to amend 14 
CFR part 39 by adding an AD that would apply to certain The Boeing 
Company Model 737-700, -700C, -800, and -900ER series airplanes. The 
NPRM was published in the Federal Register on March 26, 2026 (91 FR 
14654). The NPRM was prompted by a leak through

[[Page 67449]]

the FiP gasket at the engine fuel shutoff valve access panel found 
during a leak check, completed as part of a nonconformance disposition 
for the Boeing Company Model 737-8, 737-9, and 737-8200 airplanes. An 
investigation found that the fairing requirements of the engine fuel 
shutoff valve access panel caused thin regions of the FiP gasket, which 
caused the manufacturer to apply non-permitted sealant after the 
initial FiP gasket had cured, resulting in an uneven sealing surface on 
the engine fuel shutoff valve access panel and leak. In the NPRM, the 
FAA proposed to require a detailed inspection of the left- and right-
side FiP gasket at the engine fuel shutoff valve access panel for 
correct sealant installation, a fluid leak test of the engine fuel 
shutoff valve access panels for any leak, and applicable on-condition 
actions. The FAA is issuing this AD to address incorrect or missing 
sealant installation. The unsafe condition, if not addressed, could 
result in fuel leaking onto the engine exhaust nozzle and a possible 
fire on the ground.

Discussion of Final Airworthiness Directive

Comments

    The FAA received comments from an anonymous commenter who supported 
the NPRM without change.
    The FAA received additional comments from four commenters, 
including Aviation Partners Boeing (APB), American Airlines (American), 
Southwest Airlines (Southwest), and United Airlines (United). The 
following presents the comments received on the NPRM and the FAA's 
response to each comment.

Request To Combine Proposed ADs

    American requested that the FAA rescind the proposed AD and instead 
add the affected airplanes and proposed requirements to another 
proposed AD (Docket No. FAA-2026-2716, Project Identifier AD-2025-
00990-T). American noted, although the proposed ADs reference different 
service information and different airplane models, both proposed ADs 
would mandate similar requirements. American stated consolidating these 
actions into a single NPRM would improve clarity and enhance compliance 
management without changing the technical intent of the inspection or 
impacting airplane airworthiness.
    The FAA disagrees with this request. The FAA acknowledges that some 
operators may prefer to address the unsafe condition with a single AD; 
however, other operators may prefer that the FAA issue two separate 
ADs. Additionally, when the manufacturer develops two separate service 
bulletins to address an unsafe condition on different airplane models, 
proceeding with two separate ADs, in general, allows the FAA to address 
the unsafe condition on all airplane models in a timely manner. For 
example, if development of one service bulletin is delayed, the 
rulemaking process would be delayed for all affected airplane models.

Effect of Winglets on Accomplishment of the Proposed Actions

    APB stated that the blended or split scimitar winglets installed 
per Supplemental Type Certificate (STC) ST00830SE does not affect 
compliance with the proposed requirements.
    The FAA agrees that the installation of STC ST00830SE does not 
affect the ability to accomplish the actions required by this AD. The 
FAA has not changed this AD in this regard.

Request To Clarify Applicability Difference

    United requested the FAA clarify why the applicability of the 
proposed AD was expanded beyond the effectivity of Boeing Alert 
Requirements Bulletin 737-57A1359 RB, dated October 3, 2025 (original 
issue of the Boeing Alert Requirements Bulletin). United stated this 
clarification would assist operators in understanding the unsafe 
condition and ensure consistent implementation across the affected 
fleet.
    The FAA expanded the applicability of the AD based on a report from 
the manufacturer indicating that certain airplanes were incorrectly 
excluded from the effectivity of the original issue of the Boeing Alert 
Requirements Bulletin. The missed airplanes are associated with the 
service information mandated by AD 2013-08-18, Amendment 39-17435 (78 
FR 25377, May 1, 2013). Those airplanes had the FiP gasket incorporated 
in production, but a fluid leak test was not required as part of AD 
2013-08-18 and its associated service information. Since the same non-
conformance may have been introduced in production on those airplanes, 
the FAA concluded those airplanes must be included in the applicability 
of this AD.
    Further, after issuance of the NPRM, Boeing issued Boeing Alert 
Requirements Bulletin 737-57A1359 RB, Revision 1, dated June 25, 2026 
(Revision 1 of the Boeing Alert Requirements Bulletin), to include the 
additional airplane serial numbers referenced in the applicability of 
the proposed AD. Therefore, the FAA has updated paragraph (c) of this 
AD to reference Revision 1 of the Boeing Alert Requirements Bulletin.

Request To Remove Exceptions to Service Information Instructions

    American requested that the FAA remove the exceptions in paragraphs 
(h)(2), (3), and (4) from the proposed AD and instead adopt the 
instructions in the compliance tables and appendices of the original 
issue of the Boeing Alert Requirements Bulletin. American stated that 
the language in the service information sufficiently defines the 
corrective action, and that retaining this information helps maintain 
consistency and minimizes the potential for misinterpretation.
    The FAA notes that the exception in paragraph (h)(2) is necessary 
because the ``on-condition corrective action(s)'' specified in tables 1 
and 2 of the Compliance and Accomplishment Instructions of the original 
issue of the Boeing Alert Requirements Bulletin are not defined in that 
bulletin. Similarly, the exceptions in paragraphs (h)(3) and (4) are 
necessary because Appendices A and B of the original issue of the 
Boeing Alert Requirements Bulletin do not explicitly specify to replace 
the FiP gasket if any leak is found during the fluid leak test. 
However, the FAA reviewed Revision 1 of the Boeing Alert Requirements 
Bulletin and determined that Revision 1 negates the need for the 
exceptions in paragraphs (h)(2), (3), and (4) of the proposed AD. 
Therefore, the FAA has revised paragraph (g) of this AD to require 
accomplishing all the applicable actions in Revision 1 of the Boeing 
Alert Requirements Bulletin. Additionally, the FAA has added paragraph 
(i) to this AD to provide credit for accomplishing the actions in 
paragraph (g) of this AD before the effective date of this AD using the 
original issue of the Boeing Alert Requirements Bulletin, except as 
provided by the exceptions in paragraphs (i)(1) through (3) of this AD.

Request To Clarify Post-AD Requirements

    Southwest asked if the FAA agrees that, after completing the 
actions required by this AD, an alternative method of compliance (AMOC) 
is not required to maintain the airplane per the Boeing aircraft 
maintenance manual (AMM). Southwest stated a certain task in the Boeing 
AMM provides instructions that vary subtly from the procedures in 
Boeing Alert Requirements Bulletin 737-57A1359 RB.

[[Page 67450]]

    The FAA agrees that an AMOC is not needed to maintain the airplane 
after complying with the requirements of this AD if the airplane is 
maintained per the Boeing AMM. The FAA notes that the unsafe condition 
was introduced during airplane production through the application of 
non-permitted sealant after the initial FiP gasket had cured. Since the 
FAA has not received any reports of in-service events where anomalies 
were introduced during maintenance based on AMM instructions, the FAA 
considers the relevant AMM tasks to be appropriate. Further, Boeing has 
updated the AMM task to include notes to prevent sealant touch-up after 
the initial sealant application. Boeing has also updated the AMM 
procedures for the fluid leak test to match the procedures in the 
required service information. Those updates in the AMM instructions are 
expected to support operators for improved maintenance of the FiP 
gasket installed at the engine fuel shutoff valve access panel.

Conclusion

    The FAA reviewed the relevant data, considered any comments 
received, and determined that air safety requires adopting this AD as 
proposed. Accordingly, the FAA is issuing this AD to address the unsafe 
condition on these products. Except for minor editorial changes, this 
AD is adopted as proposed in the NPRM. None of the changes will 
increase the economic burden on any operator.

Material Incorporated by Reference Under 1 CFR Part 51

    The FAA reviewed Boeing Alert Requirements Bulletin 737-57A1359 RB, 
Revision 1, dated June 25, 2026. This material specifies procedures for 
a detailed inspection of the FiP gasket at the engine fuel shutoff 
valve access panel of the left- and right-side wing for correct sealant 
installation, a fluid leak test of the engine fuel shutoff valve access 
panels for any leak, and applicable on-condition actions. On-condition 
actions include replacing the FiP gasket, repairing any leaks, and 
repeating the leak test until no leak is found.
    This material is reasonably available because the interested 
parties have access to it through their normal course of business or by 
the means identified in the ADDRESSES section.

Authority for This Rulemaking

    Title 49 of the United States Code specifies the FAA's authority to 
issue rules on aviation safety. Subtitle I, section 106, describes the 
authority of the FAA Administrator. Subtitle VII: Aviation Programs, 
describes in more detail the scope of the Agency's authority.
    The FAA is issuing this rulemaking under the authority described in 
Subtitle VII, Part A, Subpart III, Section 44701: General requirements. 
Under that section, Congress charges the FAA with promoting safe flight 
of civil aircraft in air commerce by prescribing regulations for 
practices, methods, and procedures the Administrator finds necessary 
for safety in air commerce. This regulation is within the scope of that 
authority because it addresses an unsafe condition that is likely to 
exist or develop on products identified in this rulemaking action.

2026-21-08 The Boeing Company: Amendment 39-23497; Docket No. FAA-
2026-2725; Project Identifier AD-2025-00999-T.

(a) Effective Date

    This airworthiness directive (AD) is effective December 1, 2026.

 (b) Affected ADs

    None.

 (c) Applicability

    This AD applies to The Boeing Company Model 737-700, -700C, -
800, and -900ER series airplanes, certificated in any category, as 
identified in Boeing Alert Requirements Bulletin 737-57A1359 RB, 
Revision 1, dated June 25, 2026.

 (d) Subject

    Air Transport Association (ATA) of America Code 57, Wings.

 (e) Unsafe Condition

    This AD was prompted by a leak through the form-in-place (FiP) 
gasket at the engine fuel shutoff valve access panel found during a 
leak check. An investigation found that the fairing requirements of 
the engine fuel shutoff valve access panel caused thin regions of 
the FiP gasket, which caused non-permitted sealant to be applied 
after the initial FiP gasket had cured, resulting in an uneven 
sealing surface on the engine fuel shutoff valve access panel and 
leak. The FAA is issuing this AD to address incorrect or missing 
sealant installation. The unsafe condition, if not addressed, could 
result in fuel leaking onto the engine exhaust nozzle and a possible 
fire on the ground.

 (f) Compliance

    Comply with this AD within the compliance times specified, 
unless already done.

 (g) Required Actions

    Except as specified by paragraph (h) of this AD: At the 
applicable times specified in the ``Compliance'' paragraph of Boeing 
Alert Requirements Bulletin 737-57A1359 RB, Revision 1, dated June 
25, 2026, do all applicable actions identified in, and in accordance 
with, the Accomplishment Instructions of Boeing Alert Requirements 
Bulletin 737-57A1359 RB, Revision 1, dated June 25, 2026.
    Note 1 to paragraph (g): Guidance for accomplishing the actions 
required by this AD can be found in Boeing Alert Service Bulletin 
737-57A1359, Revision 1, dated June 25, 2026, which is referred to 
in Boeing Alert Requirements Bulletin 737-57A1359 RB, Revision 1, 
dated June 25, 2026.

 (h) Exceptions to Requirements Bulletin Specifications

    Where the Compliance Time columns of the tables in the 
``Compliance'' paragraph of Boeing Alert Requirements Bulletin 737-
57A1359 RB, Revision 1, dated June 25, 2026, refer to the original 
issue date of Requirements Bulletin 737-57A1359 RB, this AD requires 
using the effective date of this AD.

 (i) Credit for Previous Actions

    This paragraph provides credit for the actions specified in 
paragraph (g) of this AD, if those actions were performed before the 
effective date of this AD using Boeing Alert Requirements Bulletin 
737-57A1359 RB, dated October 3, 2025, except as specified by 
paragraphs (i)(1) through (3) of this AD:
    (1) Where Table 1 and Table 2 in the Compliance and 
Accomplishment Instructions paragraphs of Boeing Alert Requirements 
Bulletin 737-57A1359 RB, dated October 3, 2025, specifies ``Do a 
fluid leak test of the engine fuel shutoff valve access panel for 
any leak. If any leak is found, do all applicable on-condition 
corrective action(s) and repeat the leak test until no leak is 
found'', this AD requires replacing that text with ``Do a fluid leak 
test of the engine fuel shutoff valve access panel for any leak. If 
any leak is found, replace the FiP gasket and repeat the leak test 
until no leak is found''.
    (2) Where step 4.b. of Appendix A in Boeing Alert Requirements 
Bulletin 737-57A1359 RB, dated October 3, 2025, specifies ``If any 
water leaks from the engine fuel shutoff valve access panel in less 
than two minutes, the fluid leak test has failed'', this AD requires 
replacing that text with ``If any water leaks from the engine fuel 
shutoff valve access panel in less than two minutes, the fluid leak 
test has failed. Replace the FiP gasket in accordance with Figure 3 
and repeat the leak test until no leak is found''.
    (3) Where step 4.b. of Appendix B in Boeing Alert Requirements 
Bulletin 737-57A1359 RB, dated October 3, 2025, specifies ``If any 
water leaks from the engine fuel shutoff valve access panel in less 
than two minutes, the fluid leak test has failed'', this AD requires 
replacing that text with ``If any water leaks from the engine fuel 
shutoff valve access panel in less than two minutes, the fluid leak 
test has failed. Replace the FiP gasket in accordance with Figure 4 
and repeat the leak test until no leak is found''.

    (2) An AMOC that provides an acceptable level of safety may be

On models of affine arithmetic


Abstract
I initiate the study of models of the affine part of Peano arithmetic. These models are generally lattice ordered and carry a nontrivial metric. The affine variants of several classical results in Peano arithmetic are proved.

Classical number theory can be roughly described as the second order theory of natural numbers.
It is an absolutely categorical theory and
\mathbb{N}
is its unique model.
The first order theory of
\mathbb{N}
has in contrast non-standard models.
Much of the strength of a theory is due to the expressive power of its ambient logical framework.
An interesting example in this respect is Heyting arithmetic formalized in the framework of intuitionistic logic.
In the present paper, we study a variant of Peano arithmetic formalized in the framework of affine logic.
This is a fragment of continuous logic. Continuous logic is itself an extension of first order logic.
We concentrate on those properties of numbers which can be expressed by affine formulas.
These formulas are built from atomic formulas using addition-subtraction as connectives
as well as
\sup
-
\inf
(real variations of
\exists
-
\forall
) as quantifiers.
Conjunction, disjunction and implication are not allowed.
Surprisingly, affine and first order formulas have the same expressive power on first order structures.
The main difference between affine arithmetic and first order arithmetic is that now we deal
with a more general type of models (we call them affine models).
They are lattice ordered metric spaces equipped with addition and multiplication.
An affine form of induction holds for them.
Classical models are exactly those affine models which are linearly ordered.
We prove that many classical notions and results generalize to affine models.
In particular, Gaifman’s splitting theorem hold for them.

Affine logic

Affine logic (AL) is the fragment of continuous logic obtained by restricting
connectives to addition and scalar multiplication.
Let

Terms are defined as in first order logic. Affine formulas are inductively defined by

In full continuous logic (CL) (see

So, identifying

Continuous logic has exactly the same expressive power on first order structures as first order logic (FL)

[
7
]

In brief, CL is a conservative extension of FL founded for study of more general metric structures
and AL is a relatively weak fragment of CL.
In AL, nontrivial finite structures have infinite elementary extensions.
We will however see that AL is sufficiently strong to express many notions in arithmetic.

In the present text, unless otherwise stated, by formula we mean affine formula.

2
Affine arithmetic

The main purpose of the present paper is to study the affine variant of Peano arithmetic.
Since logical implication is not allowed in affine logic, we replace the order relation with the lattice
operations and axiomatize PA and its affine part in the language

L=\{+,\cdot,\wedge,\vee,0,1\}.

Here, all operations have Lipschitz constant equal to
1
.
An advantage of this language is that we only deal with one relation symbol, namely
the equality
=
(or the metric
d
in the affine case).
An other point is that the ordering and the lattice operations are bi-definable
in models of PA
-
(using only bounded quantifiers).
PA
-
is then axiomatized as the first order theory of nonnegative parts of lattice ordered
commutative rings with identity, where the resulting order is linear and discrete.
Adding the induction axioms in the usual way, one obtains PA.
Now, consider PA as a CL-theory. So, we replace first order formulas with more general continuous ones.
The induction schema can be restated for all continuous formulas. We will do this below for the affine part of PA.
For PA the argument is similar.

A CL-theory
\mathbb{T}
has
affine reduction
if every CL-formula is approximated
by affine formulas modulo
\mathbb{T}
. Every complete first order theory has affine reduction (see
[
8
]
).
Here, we prove that PA has affine reduction.
For this purpose, it is sufficient to prove that every first order formula is approximated by affine formulas.
First, we show that every finite conjunction of atomic formulas is PA-equivalent to an atomic formula.
We have to show this for any pair of atomic formulas.
Recall that PA proves that the pairing function

\langle x,y\rangle=\frac{(x+y+1)(x+y)}{2}+y

is a bijective. Now, for atomic formulas
f_{1}=f_{2}
and
g_{1}=g_{2}
,
the formula

(f_{1}=f_{2})\wedge(g_{1}=g_{2})

is PA-equivalent to the atomic formula

\frac{(f_{1}+g_{1}+1)(f_{1}+g_{1})}{2}+g_{1}=\frac{(f_{2}+g_{2}+1)(f_{2}+g_{2})}{2}+g_{2}.

As a consequence, every quantifier-free formula is PA-equivalent to a formula of the form

\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}\theta_{i}
where
\theta_{i}
is atomic.
We may then use the inclusion-exclusion principle to write

\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}\theta_{i}=\sum_{\emptyset\neq J\subseteq\{1,...,n\}}(-1)^{|J|+1}\bigwedge_{j\in J}\theta_{j}.

Again, replace every conjunction in this expressioin with a PA-equivalent atomic formula.

Lemma 5.3
.

(Chinese remainder theorem)
Let

Briefly, always

if and only if

On the other hand, the condition

\sup_{x_{0}...x_{n-1}}\inf_{y}\ \sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\ \inf_{t}d(y,tm_{i}+x_{i})\ \leqslant\ n\cdot\sum_{i<j}\theta(m_{i},m_{j})\ \ \ \ \ \ (*)

Hierarchy

By the hierarchy theorem, the arithmetic hierarchy of formulas does not collapse modulo PA.
We prove that this happens in the affine case too. As before, set

\sup_{x\leqslant t}\phi(x)=\sup_{x}\phi(x\wedge t)

\inf_{x\leqslant t}\phi(x)=\inf_{x}\phi(x\wedge t).

If
t
is a term,
\sup_{x\leqslant t}
and
\inf_{x\leqslant t}
are called
bounded quantifiers
.
A formula is
bounded
if all its quantifiers are bounded. For an affine theory
T
define:

-
\Sigma_{0}=\Pi_{0}
is the set of bounded AL-formulas

-
\phi\in\Sigma_{n+1}
if it is
T
-equivalent to a formula of the form
\sup_{\bar{x}}\psi
where
\psi\in\Pi_{n}

-
\phi\in\Pi_{n+1}
if it is
T
-equivalent to a formula of the form
\inf_{\bar{x}}\psi
where
\psi\in\Sigma_{n}
.

To emphasize on
T
, we may denote them by
\Sigma_{n}(T)
etc. To justify the terminology,
note that we tacitly identify
\sup_{x}\phi
with the condition
0\leqslant\sup_{x}\phi
.
For a CL-theory
\mathbb{T}
, the classical hierarchy is defined as above except that
we replace the first clause above by

-
\Sigma_{0}=\Pi_{0}
is the set of bounded CL-formulas.

For both hierarchies we have that

\Sigma_{n}\ \subseteq\ \Sigma_{n+1}\cap\Pi_{n+1},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \Pi_{n}\ \subseteq\ \Sigma_{n+1}\cap\Pi_{n+1}.

It is clear that if
T
is the affine part of
\mathbb{T}
, then, two affine formulas are

-equivalent
if and only if they are
\mathbb{T}
-equivalent.
So,
\Sigma_{n}(T)\subseteq\Sigma_{n}(\mathbb{T})
and
\Pi_{n}(T)\subseteq\Pi_{n}(\mathbb{T})
.
Now, assume
\mathbb{T}=
PA and
T=
AA (or else, we may assume
\mathbb{T}=Th(\mathbb{N})

and
T
is its affine part). Then

Proposition 6.1
.

For each

,
\Sigma_{n}(T)\nsubseteq\Pi_{n}(T)
. So, the affine arithmetic hierarchy does not collapse modulo

Proof.

Assume
\Sigma_{n}(T)\subseteq\Pi_{n}(T)
. Then,
\Sigma_{n}(T)=\Pi_{n}(T)
and hence
\Sigma_{n}(T)=\Sigma_{m}(T)

for all

. So, up to

is the set of all affine formulas.
Hence, affine types are separated by
\Sigma_{n}(T)
-formulas.
In particular, extreme types of

(which are exactly the classical types of

)
are separated by
\Sigma_{n}
-formulas.
However, by the classical hierarchy theorem, formulas of
\Sigma_{n}(\mathbb{T})
can not separate all classical types.
∎

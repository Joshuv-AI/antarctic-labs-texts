Integrating single-cell transcriptomics to construct an oncogene-driven prognostic model and elucidate metabolic-immune crosstalk in hepatocellular carcinoma
Authors: Ziming Wang, Ziyi Xu, Minghang Zhang, Hudabia Akram, Zhenyu Guan, Li Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTHepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is a leading cause of cancer-related deaths, its progression and treatment heterogeneity are mainly influenced by driver gene and tumor micro-environment (TME) interactions. Nevertheless, the mechanisms of this process at the single-cell level remain unclear. This study integrated TCGA and multi-center single-cell transcriptome data to identify a 575 genes HCC-specific core set, developing a single-cell “oncogene scoring” system to quantify individual carcinogenic activity. This score is significantly elevated in malignant and proliferative T cells and is closely associated with metabolic reprogramming, aberrant cell‒cell communication, and immunosuppressive phenotypes. Based on these characteristics, we constructed a machine learning-based Random Survival Forest (RSF) prognostic model validated in multiple independent cohorts, which classifies patients into distinct risk subtypes. The high-risk group exhibits genomic instability, increased tumor stemness, and immune evasion, while the low-risk group was more sensitive to drugs such as sorafenib. This study highlights the potential pathways by which high oncogenic activity is associated with HCC progression, suggesting a profound link with single-cell metabolic‒immune crosstalk. The constructed RSF model offers a promising computational framework for risk stratification and provides hypothesis-generating insights that may inform future personalized treatment strategies for HCC patients.

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is one of the leading causes of cancer-related deaths worldwide. It shows an increasing incidence trend, representing approximately 85%–90% of all primary liver cancer cases. 1 , 2 The intricate tumor ecosystem consists of neoplastic cells along with the surrounding tumor microenvironment (TME), which is a key factor leading to treatment resistance and disease progression. 3 , 4 The TME may include cancer-associated fibroblasts, adipocytes, pericytes, mesenchymal stem cells, endothelial cells, lymphocytes, and other immune cells, coordinating interactions among various cell populations. 5-7 Despite significant breakthroughs in targeted therapy and immunotherapy in recent years, the prognosis for HCC patients remains poor. 8-10 Advances in high-throughput sequencing technology, particularly single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq), 11 provide high-resolution characterization of the cellular composition, intercellular communication, and functional regulation of the TME. 12 , 13 However, there is still a lack of systematic integration studies of scRNA-seq data with bulk multi-omics data to elucidate the heterogeneity driven by oncogenes and their translational significance for HCC prognosis and treatment.

Oncogene activation is a key driving factor in tumor occurrence and progression, 14 , 15 promoting malignant tumor progression by various mechanisms such as regulating cell proliferation, metabolic reprogramming, and immune evasion. 16 Previous studies have mainly used bulk transcriptomic data to establish molecular subtyping or prognostic models for HCC, 17 such as the classification of “proliferative” and “non-proliferative” subtypes or risk stratification based on immune characteristics. These studies revealed the macro transcriptomic features associated with prognosis but still struggled to elucidate the origins of carcinogenic signals in specific cell populations and the microenvironment remodeling processes mediated by intercellular communication networks. 18-20 Furthermore, while many recent scRNA-seq studies have provided high-resolution descriptive atlases of the HCC microenvironment, they frequently lack direct translation into clinically actionable prognostic models. Our approach is specifically designed to bridge this gap between high-resolution TME characterization and clinical utility. By developing a novel single-cell 'tumor-associated prognostic score', we not only pinpoint the precise cell populations driving oncogenic activity, but we also extract these cell-specific functional features to develop a robust machine-learning-based prognostic model. Therefore, quantitatively assessing oncogene activity at single-cell resolution and combining it with the dynamic changes of multicellular networks provides a more biologically grounded tool for HCC risk stratification and personalized treatment than conventional bulk-derived algorithms.

To address these limitations, this study aimed to systematically depict the heterogeneity landscape driven by oncogenes in HCC by integrating bulk multi-omics and single-cell transcriptomic data. We first identified 575 HCC-specific core oncogenes through the analysis of the TCGA-LIHC cohort and used them to develop a single-cell level “oncogene scoring” system, using scRNA-seq data to calculate scores for individual cells in the TME. This score served as a functional indicator to explore its association with cell function, intercellular communication, and TME remodeling. Subsequently, based on the key oncogenes screened from single-cell analysis, we constructed a prognostic model using machine learning algorithms and validated its performance in multiple independent cohorts. In addition, we integrated genomic, immunological, and pharmacogenomic data to characterize the molecular features and potential therapeutic strategies of different risk groups. Finally, we predicted upstream regulatory drugs targeting key oncogenes, validated them in conjunction with spatial transcriptomics data, and proposed potential therapeutic avenues. The results of this study are expected to deepen the understanding of the biological characteristics of hepatic carcinoma and provide potential tools and targets for developing personalized treatment strategies. This cohort study has been reported in line with the STROCSS guidelines.

Materials and methods

scRNA-seq data and preprocessing

Two scRNA-seq datasets of HCC, LIHC_GSE146409 and LIHC_GSE166635, were downloaded from the TISCH database (http://tisch.comp-genomics.org/home/). Only cells originating from tumor tissue were retained for analysis. All data processing was performed in R (v4.2.2). The data were initially processed using Seurat (v4.3.0) for quality control, retaining cells with 200–6000 detected genes and less than 20% mitochondrial gene expression, followed by log-normalization and highly variable gene selection. To integrate the datasets and remove batch effects, the data were corrected using the Harmony package with default parameters targeting the “dataset” variable. Cell type annotations were directly adopted from the standard annotation results provided by the TISCH database, which utilizes a consensus of marker-based mapping and reference transcriptomes.

Identification of the tumor-associated prognostic set

To construct a core HCC-related tumor-associated prognostic gene set, differentially expressed genes and prognosis-related genes for TCGA-LIHC were downloaded from GEPIA3 (https://gepia3.bioinfoliu.com/). The criteria for selecting differentially expressed genes were |LogFC| > 1 and q-value < 0.05; the criteria for prognosis-related genes were q-value < 0.05. The intersection of these two gene sets was taken, resulting in a final set of 575 genes that were highly expressed and associated with poor prognosis, defined as the tumor-associated prognostic gene set (and scored subsequently as the tumor-associated prognostic score).

Functional scoring at the single-cell Level and cell-cell communication analysis

The AddModuleScore function in Seurat was used to calculate a tumor-associated prognostic score for each cell. The cells were divided into high- and low-score groups based on the median score of all cells. Hallmark pathway gene sets and tumor functional state gene sets were downloaded from the MSigDB and CancerSEA databases. Similarly, the AddModuleScore function was used to calculate these activity scores, and their association with the tumor-associated prognostic score was assessed using Spearman correlation analysis. Cell‒cell communication analysis was performed using the CellChat package. Interaction networks for the high- and low-score groups were constructed separately, and differences in the number of interactions, interaction strength, and the activity of key signaling pathways were compared.

Differential cell abundance analysis

To comprehensively evaluate the differences in cell abundance between the high- and low-tumor-associated prognostic score groups, three methods were used for integrated analysis: (1) direct comparisons of the proportional differences in each cell type between the two groups (high–low); (2) differential abundance (DA) analysis at the neighborhood level using the miloR package, with the contrast set as the “high-score group - low-score group"; and (3) construction of 2 × 2 contingency tables and performing Fisher's exact test to calculate the Odds Ratio (OR) for each cell type. The OR for the low-score group was inverted (i.e., multiplied by -1) to ensure consistent directionality. Finally, the statistics from the three methods (proportional difference, logFC from miloR, adjusted OR) were Z-score normalized and summed to obtain a composite score.

Bulk transcriptomics data processing and prognostic model construction

Bulk transcriptomics data and clinical information for HCC patients were obtained from the GEO (GSE14520), ICGC (LIRI-JP), and TCGA (LIHC) databases. After merging, a total of 848 samples were used for analysis, and the expression profiles were normalized to log2(TPM + 1) or log2(FPKM + 1) to ensure cross-cohort comparability. The TCGA-LIHC cohort was designated as the primary training set, while the GEO and ICGC cohorts served as independent validation sets. To identify key genes, we screened malignant cells from the single-cell data for genes that were significantly positively correlated with the tumor-associated prognostic score (r > 0.3, p.adjust < 0.05) and significantly highly expressed (logFC > 0.3, p.adjust < 0.05) in this cell population. The intersection yielded 743 candidate genes. Taking the expression of these genes in the bulk data as a basis, we integrated 10 classical machine learning algorithms (including Random Survival Forest - RSF, LASSO-Cox, CoxBoost, etc.) to construct a prognostic model. The model with the best performance, determined by the Concordance Index (C-index), was the RSF model (configured with ntree = 1000 and optimized minimal node size), which was selected as the final prognostic model.

Genomics, immune infiltration, and drug sensitivity analyses

Somatic mutation profiles and copy number variation (CNV) characteristics were analyzed using the ‘maftools’ package. Tumor stemness scores were calculated using the ssGSEA algorithm from GSVA on the basis of 26 stemness gene sets from the StemChecker database. Immune cell infiltration analysis was performed using the IOBR package, which integrated seven algorithms (such as CIBERSORT, EPIC, MCPcounter, etc.). Drug sensitivity was predicted by the ‘oncoPredict’ R package using the GDSC database, and differences in the estimated IC50 values for drugs between the high- and low-risk groups were analyzed.

Spatial transcriptomics and upstream drug prediction

Spatial transcriptomics data (VISDS000514) were downloaded from the CROST database. Processing was performed using the ‘seurat’ and ‘semla’ packages, and spatial deconvolution was conducted based on the single-cell annotation results from this study. The prediction of upstream regulatory drugs targeting the key genes was based on the CTD database and publicly available drug perturbation transcriptomic data, screened through differential expression analysis (adjusted p < 0.05).

Statistical analysis and visualization

Survival analysis was performed using the Kaplan‒Meier method and the Log-rank test. Group comparisons were conducted using t-tests, Wilcoxon tests, or Analysis of Variance (ANOVA), as appropriate. Correlation analysis was performed using Pearson’s or Spearman’s methods. The significance level was set at p < 0.05. All analyses were performed in the R language environment.

Results

Construction of a single-cell atlas of liver cancer and functional characteristics of cell populations

To systematically identify the key oncogenes in HCC, we analyzed the TCGA-LIHC cohort and took the intersection of upregulated differentially expressed genes (LogFC > 1, q < 0.05) and prognosis-related genes (q < 0.05), thus obtaining 575 candidate oncogenes, and defined them as the tumor-associated prognostic set (Figure 1A). We then integrated the GSE146409 and GSE166635 single-cell datasets. UMAP visualization clearly distinguished 11 major cell lineages, including malignant cells, T-cell subsets, macrophages, and others (Figure 1B). Single-cell transcriptomic analysis of 11 major cell clusters revealed distinct expression profiles (Figure 1C) and cell-type-specific marker genes (Figure 1D). Functional enrichment analysis of these markers indicated that malignant cells and proliferative T cells (Tprolif) were significantly enriched in the cell cycle and metabolic pathways (Figure 1E–F).

Oncogene scoring and the heterogeneity of tumor cell function

To investigate the functional significance of the tumor-associated prognostic set within the TME, we calculated a tumor-associated prognostic score for each cell. Malignant cells and Tprolif clusters exhibited the highest tumor-associated prognostic scores (Figure 2A), leading to the classification of cells into high- and low-oncogene groups (Figure 2B). Notably, the high tumor-associated prognostic score observed in Tprolif cells is primarily attributed to the abundance of cell-cycle and proliferation-related transcripts within our bulk-derived tumor-associated prognostic set. This high score reflects the hyperproliferative state and robust clonal expansion of these T cells within the tumor microenvironment, rather than canonical malignant transformation. The high-score group was characterized by an increased proportion of malignant cells (Figure 2C–E). Correlation analysis further demonstrated that the tumor-associated prognostic score was positively associated with hallmark pathways such as MYC targets and G2M checkpoints (Figure 2F).

Differences in cell communication and functional states among scoring groups

Cell‒cell communication analysis revealed a higher number and strength of interactions in the high-score group compared to the low-score group (Figure S1A–B). Distinct incoming and outgoing signaling patterns were observed across cell types (Figure S1C–F), with significant information flow differences in key pathways between the two states (Figure S1G). Furthermore, functional state analysis showed that malignant cells achieved higher scores for invasion, epithelial‒mesenchymal transition (EMT), and DNA repair (Figure S2C). Hallmark pathway analysis further confirmed the activation of pathways such as DNA repair and angiogenesis (Figure S2D), while cell cycle phases were predominantly concentrated in the G2M and S phases for Tprolif cells (Figure S2A–B). Metabolic program analysis highlighted significant differences in the KEGG and Reactome metabolic pathways between the high- and low-score groups (Figure S2E–F). Cell abundance analysis, direct comparison (Figure S3A), miloR differential abundance (Figure S3B–C), and odds ratio analysis (Figure S3D), consistently confirmed the specific enrichment of malignant cells and Tprolif cells in the high-score group. A combined Z-score ranking supported this conclusion (Figure S3E), indicating that the tumor-associated prognostic score drives the remodeling of the cellular community within the TME.

Molecular typing identification and its clinical characteristics

Based on 64 oncogenes identified through univariate Cox regression (Figure S4A–B), we classified HCC patients into three stable molecular clusters (Clusters A, B, and C) (Figure S4C). These clusters exhibited distinct principal component distributions (Figure S4D) and significantly different overall survival outcomes (Figure S4E). Genomic analysis revealed varied mutation frequencies of driver genes such as TP53 and CTNNB1 across clusters (Figure S5A), alongside differences in stemness scores and immune infiltration patterns (Figure S5B–D).

Construction and multi-center validation of the random forest risk model

To construct a robust and clinically applicable prognostic model, we first identified candidate genes significantly associated with overall survival. Venn diagrams illustrated the intersection of core genes associated with both bad and good prognoses across four independent cohorts (TCGA, GSE14520, ICGC-LIRI, and a merged cohort) (Figure 3A). Univariate Cox regression analysis further validated the prognostic significance of these intersecting genes across datasets, highlighting highly significant candidates (Figure 3B).

(G) Kaplan‒Meier survival curves validating the prognostic performance of the risk score in multiple independent validation cohorts.

Subsequently, we systematically evaluated 10 classical machine-learning algorithms and their combinations to identify the optimal predictive framework. Based on the Concordance Index (C-index) across all datasets, the Random Survival Forest (RSF) model emerged as the most accurate and stable predictor (Figure 3C). Specifically, the RSF model achieved an outstanding C-index of 0.94 in the TCGA training cohort, while maintaining robust predictive performance across the external validation cohorts, with C-indices of 0.76 in GSE14520, 0.75 in ICGC-LIRI, and 0.74 in the merged cohort. We further ranked the core feature genes within the RSF model based on their variable importance, identifying genes such as PLOD2, DENR, and DCAF13 as the top contributors to the model's predictive capacity (Figure 3D, Table S1).

Based on the RSF-derived risk score, patients in the TCGA training cohort were stratified into high- and low-risk groups. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis demonstrated that the high-risk group experienced a significantly worse overall survival rate compared to the low-risk group (p < 0.0001; Figure 3E). In addition, time-dependent ROC curve analysis confirmed the exceptional dynamic predictive accuracy of the RSF model over a 5-year follow-up period, with the AUC(t) consistently maintained at a high level (Figure 3F).

Importantly, the strong prognostic performance of the RSF risk score was successfully validated in multiple independent external cohorts. Consistent with the training set, Kaplan‒Meier curves for the GSE14520, ICGC-LIRI, and the merged cohorts consistently revealed striking and statistically significant survival disparities between the high- and low-risk groups (all p < 0.0001; Figure 3G). Finally, independent prognostic analysis confirmed that this RSF-derived risk score serves as a robust and significant independent risk factor for HCC (Hazard Ratio [HR] = 1.073, 95% CI: 1.063–1.083, p < 0.001) (Figure S6A–B), firmly establishing its value for clinical risk stratification. The risk score was also positively correlated with clinical progression, including higher T-stage, Grade, and Stage (Figure S6C–G).

The genomic landscape and pathway basis related to risk scores

The RSF risk score was significantly associated with tumor mutation burden (TMB) and microsatellite instability (MSI) (Figure 4A–B). The somatic mutation landscape (Figure 4C–D) and differentially mutated genes (Figure 4E) in the high-risk group indicated enhanced genomic instability, along with a higher stemness score (Figure 4F) and increased genome-wide copy number variation frequency (Figure 4G). Immune infiltration heatmaps showed correlations between the risk score and the abundance of various immune cells (Figure 5A). The expression of key immune-related genes, including chemokines and interleukins, revealed an immunosuppressive characteristic in the high-risk group (Figure 5B). The heatmaps displayed the expression profiles of the top 50 genes most significantly positively and negatively correlated with the risk score (Figure S7A, B). The GSEA results confirmed the significant activation of the cell cycle and DNA replication pathways in the high-risk group (Figure S7C–F).

Functional validation of core genes and drug treatment responses

In silico drug sensitivity analysis using the GDSC database predicted that the high-risk group exhibited significantly lower estimated IC50 values (p < 0.0001), indicating a higher theoretical sensitivity to agents such as XAV939, Nutlin-3a, and Wee1 inhibitors (Figure S8). To interpret the model's internal mechanics, SHAP analysis identified key genes such as PLOD2 and CDK4 as critical prognostic contributors (Figure S9A). To functionally validate these findings, DepMap CRISPR/Cas9 screening data indicated that these genes are biologically essential for cell survival, as their knockout significantly inhibited cell proliferation across multiple cancer cell lines (Figure S9B–C). Furthermore, a correlation network between gene expression and metabolic/functional activity inferred from single-cell data revealed strong associations (|r| > 0.3), suggesting their robust functional potential in tumor reprogramming (Figure S9D–E). To assess these features within the actual tissue architecture, spatial transcriptomic deconvolution further validated that the expression of core genes was significantly and spatially correlated with the proportion of tumor-associated fibroblasts (Figure 6A–B). Finally, computational drug prediction networks identified potential modern and traditional medicines targeting these core genes, providing a multi-dimensional, hypothesis-generating strategy for personalized HCC therapy (Figure 7A–D).

Discussion

This study integrated scRNA-seq with bulk multi-omics data to systematically delineate the landscape of oncogene-driven heterogeneity in HCC. We established a core set of 575 HCC-specific oncogenes and developed a novel single-cell “tumor-associated prognostic score” to quantify tumorigenic potential within the microenvironment. Malignant cells and Tprolif exhibited the highest tumor-associated prognostic scores, acting as central hubs in aberrant cell‒cell communication networks. Taking these insights as a basis, we constructed a robust RSF prognostic model using 42 key genes. This model not only demonstrated superior predictive performance across multiple independent cohorts but also effectively stratified patients into distinct risk groups with unique genomic, immune, and therapeutic response profiles.

Recently, the application of machine learning and artificial intelligence in oncology has grown exponentially, with numerous studies reporting sophisticated predictive models for HCC and other cancers. 21-23 These models encompass a wide range of advanced methodologies, including deep learning for radiomics, large language models for drug discovery, and various multi-omics integration frameworks. 24-26 While these models demonstrate robust predictive power, they predominantly rely on macro-level bulk transcriptomics or clinical imaging features. In contrast, a primary biological innovation of our submission is that the features of our RSF model are fundamentally rooted in single-cell resolution. 27-29 By developing a single-cell “tumor-associated prognostic score”, our framework goes beyond pure algorithmic risk prediction to capture the specific cellular origins of oncogenic signals within the TME. This approach explicitly links computational risk stratification to the spatial and metabolic remodeling of the microenvironment, such as the MIF and TGF-β crosstalk mediated by malignant cells, thereby providing a deeper mechanistic context that distinguishes it from conventional macro-level ML models.

Our findings suggest a profound link between high oncogene activity and metabolic reprogramming. The positive correlation between the tumor-associated prognostic score and pathways such as oxidative phosphorylation and glycolysis in malignant cells indicates that oncogene activation drives the metabolic plasticity required for rapid tumor proliferation. 30 , 31 Furthermore, the cell–cell communication analysis revealed that high-score cells significantly enhance signaling through the MIF and TGF-β pathways, which serve as pivotal downstream effectors of oncogenic signaling networks. Biologically, TGF-β acts as a potent suppressor of immunogenic cell death (ICD) by inhibiting dendritic cell maturation and dampening effector T cell responses, thereby neutralizing the immunostimulatory effects of damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) released within the tumor microenvironment. 32 , 33 Concurrently, MIF, which can itself function as a stress-induced DAMP, is frequently upregulated by oncogene-driven metabolic reprogramming and contributes to the recruitment of immunosuppressive myeloid cells. Together, these altered pathways potentially orchestrate an immunosuppressive shield that prevents the effective processing of DAMPs and blunts ICD. This explains the paradox where the high-score group, despite having high Tprolif abundance, exhibits an immunosuppressive microenvironment and has a poorer prognosis. The enrichment of DNA repair and G2M checkpoint pathways in the high-risk subtype (Subtype B) further points to a mechanism where genomic instability drives aggressive tumor progression and resistance. 34

Unlike previous studies that primarily relied on bulk transcriptomics to identify prognostic markers, our study leveraged single-cell resolution to pinpoint the cell populations with prominent oncogenic signature expression, which were predominantly malignant cells and proliferating T cells. Crucially, the high score in Tprolif cells does not imply that these immune cells undergo malignant transformation. Because our 575 core oncogenes were identified from bulk transcriptomic data, the signature inevitably captures shared housekeeping programs of hyper-proliferation, such as DNA replication and cell-cycle progression pathways. Therefore, when projected onto single cells, Tprolif cells score high because they share these identical proliferation transcripts with malignant cells during active immune responses, representing an aggressive expansion state rather than intrinsic oncogenic driving. While traditional Cox regression models have been widely used, we employed the RSF algorithm, which demonstrated superior performance (highest C-index) by capturing complex, non-linear interactions among gene features. 35 Additionally, our molecular subtyping results aligned with established HCC classifications; specifically, our “Subtype B” (high stemness, proliferative) mirrors the characteristics of the previously described “Proliferation class” of HCC, while “Subtype C” (metabolic, CTNNB1-mutated) corresponds to the “Non-proliferation class,” thereby validating the biological relevance of our oncogene signature. 36 , 37

A primary innovation of this work is the definition and calculation of the “tumor-associated prognostic score” at the single-cell level, which serves as a metric to evaluate the functional state of individual cells within the TME. By integrating this score with cell‒cell communication (CellChat) and miloR analyses, we provided a dynamic view of how oncogene expression remodels the cellular community. Furthermore, we extended our findings beyond prognosis by constructing an upstream drug prediction network. By identifying compounds targeting key genes such as CDK4 and BZW2, including potential components from traditional Chinese medicine, our study offers novel candidate strategies for targeted therapy. 38 , 39

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, our conclusions are based on a retrospective analysis of public datasets; thus, selection bias cannot be entirely ruled out. Second, while we identified key genes (e.g., FDPS, TBL1X) and signaling pathways using rigorous computational methods (SHAP, correlations), the precise molecular mechanisms by which they modulate the TME require further validation through in vitro and in vivo biological experiments. Third, although we incorporated spatial transcriptomics to validate gene expression patterns, the sample size was limited and larger spatial cohorts are needed to confirm the spatial colocalization of these oncogenic interactions.

Future research should focus on experimentally validating the functional roles of the top-ranking oncogenes identified by our SHAP analysis in regulating tumor metabolism and immune crosstalk. In addition, prospective clinical studies are necessary to evaluate the utility of the RSF risk score in real-world patient management. Integrating proteomics and metabolomics data could also help construct a multi-layer regulatory network, providing a more holistic view of the oncogene-driven landscape in HCC.

The RSF prognostic model constructed in this study holds significant demonstrates potential clinical value as a computational tool. First, it serves as an independent prognostic factor that outperforms conventional TNM staging, enabling more accurate risk stratification. The strong association between the risk score and genomic instability markers (TMB, MSI) suggests that the model might help identify patients with high genomic complexity. Crucially, our in silico drug sensitivity and TIDE analyses indicate that low-risk patients may benefit more from standard chemotherapeutics such as sorafenib, whereas high-risk patients might require alternative immunotherapeutic strategies owing to their prone-to-escape microenvironment. Consequently, our model provides a promising theoretical framework for guiding personalized therapeutic decisions in HCC; however, we strictly acknowledge that these computational predictions necessitate rigorous prospective clinical validation and experimental confirmation before any actual application in real-world patient management.

Lifting in a non semisimple world Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification. 16T05, 16T20.
Jack Arce and Cristian Vay

Abstract
. This is a contribution to the problem of classifying all deformations – a. k. a. liftings – of the bosonization of a Nichols algebra 𝔅 ⁡ ( V ) \mathfrak{B}(V) over a cosemisimple and non-semisimple Hopf algebra H H . Such a situation arises when the underlying field has positive characteristic or when H H is infinite-dimensional. Given an H H -module M M that is an extension of V V by H H , we first introduce an algebra T ⁡ ( V ) ​ # M ​ H T(V)\#_{M}H which generalizes the usual bosonization T ⁡ ( V ) ​ # ​ H T(V)\#H . Indeed, these two objects coincide when M M is a trivial extension. We provide necessary conditions for T ⁡ ( V ) ​ # M ​ H T(V)\#_{M}H to be a Hopf algebra and a cocycle deformation of T ⁡ ( V ) ​ # ​ H T(V)\#H . These conditions appear particularly natural when H H is a group algebra. We then prove that every lifting is a quotient of T ⁡ ( V ) ​ # M ​ H T(V)\#_{M}H for some extension M M . Echoing Archimedes, T ⁡ ( V ) ​ # M ​ H T(V)\#_{M}H stands as a fulcrum over which we can pivot to lift the relations of the Nichols algebra. From this point, one can replicate the strategy proposed by Andruskiewitsch, Angiono, Garcia I., Masuoka and the second author to show that every lifting is a cocycle deformation of 𝔅 ⁡ ( V ) ​ # ​ H \mathfrak{B}(V)\#H . We illustrate this idea with two examples of different nature. We classify all pointed liftings of the Fomin–Kirillov algebra ℱ ​ 𝒦 3 \mathcal{F}\mathcal{K}_{3} in characteristic 2 2 , and prove they are all cocycle deformations one another. We also prove that the Jordanian enveloping algebra of 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 2 ) \mathfrak{sl}(2) defined by Andruskiewitsch, Angiono and Heckenberger is a cocycle deformation of the bosonization of the Jordan plane over the infinite cyclic group.

The Lifting Method was conceived by Andruskiewitsch and Schneider to classify Hopf algebras whose coradical is a (cosemisimple) Hopf subalgebra
[

The fulcrum

Throughout this section,
H
is a Hopf algebra with bijective antipode over a field
\Bbbk

We fix a set of linearly independent elements

\mathfrak{H}
of

(1)

\displaystyle h\cdot x

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}a_{i}(h,x)x_{i}+\sum_{t\in\mathfrak{H}}b_{t}(h,x)t

for all

and

(2)

\displaystyle h\cdot_{V}x=\sum_{i\in I}a_{i}(h,x)x_{i}.

We denote by

\displaystyle hk

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\in\mathfrak{H}}m_{t}(h,k)t,

for

The fulcrum which we aim to study is the following algebra.

Definition 2.1
.

The algebra

is the quotient of

\displaystyle hx=(h_{\left(1\right)}\cdot x)h_{\left(2\right)}

We notice that the commutation rule in the definition can be written more explicitly using (

We begin by highlighting the following properties of

.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\langle\mathfrak{X}\rangle
be the subset of
T(V)\#_{M}H
formed by the monomials (or words) in
\mathfrak{X}
. Then

(a)

\langle\mathfrak{X}\rangle\mathfrak{H}=\{Ah\mid A\in\langle\mathfrak{X}\rangle,\,h\in\mathfrak{H}\}
is a basis
T(V)\#_{M}H
.

(b)

The linear endomorphism
\phi
of

\phi(Ah)=hA
,

and

(c)

T(V)\#_{M}H
is a graded vector space whose homogeneous component of degree

is
(T(V)\#_{M}H)_{n}=\{Ah\mid A\in\langle\mathfrak{X}\rangle\,\mbox{of length $n$},\,h\in\mathfrak{H}\}
.

(d)

T(V)
and
H
are subalgebras.

Proof.

We will apply Bergman’s Diamond Lemma to prove
(a)
. We first equip
\mathfrak{H}\cup\mathfrak{X}
with an order such that
h>x
for all
h\in\mathfrak{H}
and
x\in\mathfrak{X}
, and extend this to the set of words in

using the degree lexicographic order. Now,

is defined by the reduction system

consisting of the pairs

\displaystyle\left(hk,\,\sum_{t\in\mathfrak{H}}m_{t}(h,k)t\right)\,\mbox{ and }\,\left(hx,\,\sum_{i\in I}a_{i}(h_{\left(1\right)},x)x_{i}h_{\left(2\right)}+\sum_{t\in\mathfrak{H}}b_{t}(h_{\left(1\right)},x)th_{\left(2\right)}\right)

for all
h,k\in\mathfrak{H}
and
x\in\mathfrak{X}
. Clearly, the degree lexicographic order is compatible with

. The only possible ambiguities arise from monomials of the form
hkt
and
hkx

and

and the associativity of the action of

In order to prove (b), we observe that

H

(4)

\displaystyle hx_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{n}}=(h_{\left(1\right)}\cdot x_{i_{1}})\cdots(h_{\left(n\right)}\cdot x_{i_{n}})h_{\left(n+1\right)}=h_{\left(1\right)}\cdot(x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{n}})h_{\left(2\right)},

\displaystyle g\cdot y_{i}=g\cdot_{V}y_{i}+b(g,x_{i}),

for all
g\in G
and
i\in I
. Moreover, (
12
) makes
M
an extension of
\Bbbk G
-module if and only if (
13
) does the same for
M^{\prime}
, and this equivalent to

(14)

\displaystyle b(gh,x_{i})=b(g,h\cdot_{V}x_{i})+b(h,x_{i})

for all
g,h\in G
and
i\in I
. We observe that these extensions satisfy (
10
) and (
11
) immediately. Summarizing, we have the following.

Corollary 2.7
.

The following statements are equivalent:

(a)

M
is an extension of
V
by
\Bbbk G
with action as in (
12
).

(b)

Equation (
14
) holds.

(c)

T(V)\#_{M}\Bbbk G
is a pointed Hopf algebra quotient of
T_{H}(M)
.

If these statements hold, then
T(V)\#_{M^{\prime}}\Bbbk G
is a
(T(V)\#_{M}\Bbbk G,T(V)\#\Bbbk G)
-bigalois extension, and
T(V)\#_{M}\Bbbk G
is a cocycle deformation of
T(V)\#\Bbbk G
. ∎

2.1.2.
The finite-dimensional case

In this case Equations (
10
) and (
11
) are not necessary.

Theorem 2.8
.

If
H
is finite-dimensional and Equations (
8
) and (
9
) hold, then
T(V)\#_{M}H
is a
(T(V)\#_{M}H,T(V)\#H)
-bigalois extension. Consequently,
T(V)\#_{M}H
is a cocycle deformation of
T(V)\#H
.

Proof.

As for Theorem
2.6
, we must prove that
\kappa_{r}
and
\kappa_{l}
are bijective. However, it is enough to verify their injectivity as
H
is finite-dimensional. Indeed, the image of the subspace

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{s+t\leq n}(T(V)\#_{M^{\prime}}H)_{s}\otimes(T(V)\#_{M^{\prime}}H)_{t}

under
\kappa_{r}

is contained in
\bigoplus_{s+t\leq n}(T(V)\#_{M^{\prime}}H)_{s}\otimes(T(V)\#H)_{t}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and these are finite-dimensional vector spaces of the same dimension by Theorem
2.2
. In order to prove that
\kappa_{r}
is injective, we proceed as follows. Let
p=\sum_{A,B}Ah_{A,B}\otimes Bk_{A,B}
where the sum runs over all words
A,B
in
\{y_{i}\}_{i\in I}
, here
h_{A,B},k_{A,B}\in H
. If
p\neq 0
, we pick words
A_{0}
and
B_{0}
with maximal lengths among the words with
h_{A,B}\neq 0\neq k_{A,B}
. Let
s
and
t
be the lengths of
A_{0}
and
B_{0}
, respectively. Then

\displaystyle(\pi_{s+t}\otimes\pi_{0})\kappa_{r}(p)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\operatorname{length}A=s\\
\operatorname{length}B=t\end{subarray}}\pi_{s+t}(Ah_{A,B}Bk_{A,B}{}_{\left(1\right)})\otimes k_{A,B}{}_{\left(2\right)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\operatorname{length}A=s\\
\operatorname{length}B=t\end{subarray}}A\,\pi_{t}(h_{A,B}B)\,k_{A,B}{}_{\left(1\right)}\otimes k_{A,B}{}_{\left(2\right)}

where, by an abuse of notation, we use
\pi_{n}
to denote the projections from
T(V)\#_{M^{\prime}}H
and
T(V)\#H
onto their homogeneous components for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Now, if
\ker\kappa_{r}(p)=0
, the above equality implies that

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle\sum_{\operatorname{length}B=t}\,\pi_{t}(h_{A_{0},B}B)\,k_{A_{0},B}{}_{\left(1\right)}\otimes k_{A_{0},B}{}_{\left(2\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\pi_{t}\phi\otimes\operatorname{id}_{H})\kappa^{\prime}\left(\sum_{\operatorname{length}B=t}Bh_{A_{0},B}\otimes k_{A_{0},B}\right)

where
\kappa^{\prime}
denotes the automorphism
q\otimes h\mapsto qh_{\left(1\right)}\otimes h_{\left(2\right)}
of
(T(V)\#_{M^{\prime}}H)_{t}\otimes H
and
\phi
is the automorphism of
T(V)\#_{M^{\prime}}H
given by Theorem
2.2
(b). We notice that
\phi(T(V)\#_{M^{\prime}}H)_{t}\subseteq\oplus_{\ell\leq t}(T(V)\#_{M^{\prime}}H)_{\ell}
by definition of
\phi
, see also (
4
), and the restriction of
\phi
induces an automorphism of
\oplus_{\ell\leq t}(T(V)\#_{M^{\prime}}H)_{\ell}
. Since
H
is finite-dimensional, we deduce that
\pi_{t}\phi
must be injective in
(T(V)\#_{M^{\prime}}H)_{t}
, and therefore
0=\sum_{\operatorname{length}B=t}Bh_{A_{0},B}\otimes k_{A_{0},B}
. In particular,
h_{A_{0},B_{0}}\otimes k_{A_{0},B_{0}}
must be
0
, which contradicts the fact that
h_{A_{0},B_{0}}\neq 0\neq k_{A_{0},B_{0}}
. In conclusion
\kappa_{r}(p)\neq 0
if
p\neq 0
, and hence
\kappa_{r}
is injective. The proof for
\kappa_{l}
is analogous.
∎

3.
Liftings

Let
H
be a cosemisimple Hopf algebra with bijective antipode over a field
\Bbbk
, and
\mathfrak{B}(V)
the Nichols algebra of a Yetter-Drinfeld module
V\in{}^{H}_{H}\mathcal{YD}
. The following characterization of the liftings extends
[
8
, Proposition 2.4]
to the non-semisimple case.

Theorem 3.1
.

A Hopf algebra
L
is a lifting of
\mathfrak{B}(V)
over
H
if and only if there exists an extension
M
of
V
by
H
and an epimorphism of Hopf algebras
\phi:T(V)\#_{M}H\longrightarrow L
such that
\phi_{|H\oplus V\#H}:H\oplus V\#H\longrightarrow L_{1}
is a bijection.

Proof.

Let us assume that
L
is a lifting. Thus,
[
4
, Corollary 2.18]
guarantees the existence of a right
H
-module coalgebra map

\Pi:L\longrightarrow H

such that
\Pi_{|H}=\operatorname{id}_{H}
. Let
\delta_{l}=(\Pi\otimes\operatorname{id})\Delta
and
\delta_{r}=(\operatorname{id}\otimes\Pi)\Delta
be the induced
H
-coactions on
L
. By
[
4
, Theorem 3.71]
,
L_{n}\simeq L_{n}^{co\,\delta_{r}}\#H
as right
H
-module coalgebras for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. On the other hand, by
[
5
, Lemma 1.1]
,
L_{1}=H\oplus P_{1}
as
H
-bicomodules with

\displaystyle P_{1}=L_{1}\cap\ker\Pi=\{x\in L\mid\Delta(x)=\delta_{r}(x)+\delta_{l}(x)\}

and hence the projection
\pi:L_{1}\longrightarrow P_{1}
with
\ker\pi=H
is a morphism of right
H
-modules and
H
-bicomodules.
We deduce then that
V\#H\simeq L_{1}/L_{0}\simeq P_{1}\simeq L_{1}^{co\,\delta_{r}}\#H
, and
\pi_{|P_{1}^{co\,\delta_{r}}}:P_{1}^{co\,\delta_{r}}\longrightarrow V
is a bijection. Since
V
is invariant by the adjoint action of
H
in
V\#H
, it follows that
\operatorname{ad}H(P_{1}^{co\,\delta_{r}})\subset P_{1}^{co\,\delta_{r}}\oplus\ker\pi=P_{1}^{co\,\delta_{r}}\oplus H
. Let us identify
P_{1}^{co\,\delta_{r}}
with
V
using
\pi
. Therefore
M=V\oplus H
is an extension of
H
-modules of
V
by
H
satisfying the first assumptions in §
2.1
with
\delta=\delta_{l}
. Thus, there exists a Hopf algebra map
\phi:T(V)\#_{M}H\longrightarrow L
such that
\phi_{|V\oplus H}=\operatorname{id}_{|V\oplus H}
which is surjective as
L
is generated by
L_{1}=H\oplus V\#H
.

The reciprocal statement follows as in
[
8
, Proposition 2.4]
.
∎

3.1.
Pointed liftings

Assume that
H=\Bbbk G
is a group algebra. As in §
2.1.1
, we may choose a basis
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of
V
such that
\delta(x_{i})=g_{i}\otimes x_{i}
for certain
g_{i}\in G
,
i\in I
. In this case, the action on the module
M
involved in Theorem
3.1
is given by (
12
), in view of Corollary
2.7
. Hence, we obtain the following.

Corollary 3.2
.

A pointed Hopf algebra
L
is a lifting of
\mathfrak{B}(V)
over
\Bbbk G
, if and only if
L
is generated by
\{g\}_{g\in G}
and
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in I}
satisfying that

(a)

the comultiplication is given by

\displaystyle\Delta(g)=g\otimes g\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Delta(x_{i})=x_{i}\otimes 1+g_{i}\otimes x_{i},

(b)

there exists a bilinear map
b:\Bbbk G\times V\longrightarrow\Bbbk
such that

\displaystyle gx_{i}g^{-1}=g\cdot_{V}x_{i}+b(g,x_{i})(1-gg_{i}g^{-1})

for all
g\in G
and
i\in I
, and

(c)

L_{1}=\Bbbk\{x_{i}\}_{i\in I}\,\Bbbk G\oplus\Bbbk G
.

∎

4.
Examples

4.1.
An example in positive characteristic

Let
\mathbb{X}
be the rack of transpositions in
{\mathbb{S}}_{3}
, or equivalently, the affine rack
(\mathbb{Z}_{3},2)
. This is the cyclic group
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
endowed with the operation
i\rhd j=2i-j
, for
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}
. The enveloping group
G_{\mathbb{X}}
of
\mathbb{X}

[
13
,
20
]
is defined as

(15)

\displaystyle G_{\mathbb{X}}=\langle g_{0},\,g_{1},\,g_{2}\mid g_{i}g_{j}=g_{i\rhd j}g_{i},\,i,j\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}\rangle.

From now on, we fix a non-abelian quotient group
G
of
G_{\mathbb{X}}
. We still denote
g_{i}
the image in
G
of the generators of
G_{\mathbb{X}}
. Then
g_{0},

g_{1}
and
g_{2}
are pairwise different elements of
G
, they generate
G
and form a conjugacy class which naturally identifies with
\mathbb{X}
as a rack. Using this identification, we let
G
acts on
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
and write
g\cdot i
for the action of
g\in G
on
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}
; for instance,
g_{j}\cdot i=j\rhd i
for
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}
. The centralizer
G^{g_{0}}
of
g_{0}
is the cyclic group generated by
g_{0}
, see for instance
[
18
, Section 5.1]
.

Throughout this section
\Bbbk
is a field of characteristic
2
. Let
(\varepsilon,\Bbbk)
be the one-dimensional representation of
G^{g_{0}}
given by
\varepsilon:G^{g_{0}}\longrightarrow\Bbbk^{*}
,
\varepsilon(g_{0})=1
; in characteristic
2
, this coincides with the sign representation. The structure of the Yetter-Drinfeld module

\displaystyle V=M(g_{0},\varepsilon)=\operatorname{Ind}_{G^{g_{0}}}^{G}\Bbbk=\Bbbk G\otimes_{\Bbbk G^{g_{0}}}\Bbbk\in{}^{\Bbbk G}_{\Bbbk G}\mathcal{YD}

is determined by

(16)

\displaystyle g\cdot x_{i}=x_{g\cdot i}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\delta(x_{i})=g_{i}\otimes x_{i}

for all
g\in G
and
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}
, where

\displaystyle x_{0}=e\otimes 1,\quad x_{1}=g_{2}\cdot x_{0}=g_{2}\otimes 1\quad\mbox{and}\quad x_{2}=g_{1}\cdot x_{0}=g_{1}\otimes 1.

The Nichols algebra
\mathfrak{B}(V)
of
V
identifies with the Fomin–Kirillov algebra
\mathcal{F}\mathcal{K}_{3}

[
14
]
. This is the quotient of the tensor algebra
T(V)
by the ideal generated by the elements

\displaystyle R_{i,j}=x_{i}x_{j}+x_{i\rhd j}x_{i}+x_{(i\rhd j)\rhd i}x_{i\rhd j}

for
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}
. We notice that
R_{i,i}=x_{i}^{2}
,
R_{i,j}=R_{i\rhd j,i}=R_{j,i\rhd j}
and
(i\rhd j)\rhd i=j
for all
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}
. A basis of
\mathfrak{B}(V)
consists of all possible words
m_{1}m_{2}m_{3}
where
m_{i}
is an element in the
i
-th row of the list:

\displaystyle 1,\,x_{0},

\displaystyle 1,\,x_{1},\,x_{1}x_{0},

\displaystyle 1,\,x_{2}.

In order to define the liftings of
\mathfrak{B}(V)
over
\Bbbk G
, we need to introduce some notation. Recall
§
2.1.1
. Let
\mathcal{L}
be a collection of bilinear maps
\lambda:\Bbbk G\times V\longrightarrow\Bbbk
classifying (up to isomorphism) all the extensions of
V
by
\Bbbk G
. For
\lambda\in\mathcal{L}
, we denote
M_{\lambda}=V\oplus\Bbbk G
the extension determined by

\displaystyle g\cdot x_{i}=x_{g\cdot i}+\lambda(g,x_{i})(1+g_{g\cdot i}),

g\in G
and
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}
, cf. (
12
). We set
T_{\lambda}=T(V)\#_{M_{\lambda}}\Bbbk G
for the associated Hopf algebra given by Definition
2.1
. For brevity, we write
\lambda_{i,j}=\lambda(g_{i},x_{j})
and then

(17)

\displaystyle\lambda_{i,j\rhd k}+\lambda_{j,k}=\lambda_{i\rhd j,i\rhd k}+\lambda_{i,k}

for all
i,j,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}
thanks to (
14
) and the relation
g_{i}g_{j}=g_{i\rhd j}g_{i}
. Let
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}
be the set of matrices
\mu\in\Bbbk^{\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3}}
satisfying the equations

(18)

\displaystyle\mu_{i,j}=\mu_{i\rhd j,i}=\mu_{j,i\rhd j}\quad\mbox{and}

(19)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mu_{i,j}+\mu_{k\rhd i,k\rhd j}=\\
&=\lambda_{k,i}(\lambda_{k,i\rhd j}+\lambda_{i,j})+\lambda_{k,j}(\lambda_{k,i}+\lambda_{j,(i\rhd j)})+\lambda_{k,(i\rhd j)}(\lambda_{k,j}+\lambda_{(i\rhd j),i})\end{split}

for all
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}
. Finally, we introduce the elements

\displaystyle r_{i,j}=\lambda_{i,j}x_{i}+\lambda_{i\rhd j,i}x_{i\rhd j}+\lambda_{j,i\rhd j}x_{j}\in T_{\lambda},

for
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}
; notice that
r_{i,j}=r_{i\rhd j,i}=r_{j,i\rhd j}
and
r_{i,i}=\lambda_{i,i}x_{i}
.

Definition 4.1
.

Given
\lambda\in\mathcal{L}
and
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}
, we denote
L_{\lambda\mu}
the quotient of
T_{\lambda}
by the ideal generated by the elements

\displaystyle R_{i,j}+r_{i,j}+\mu_{i,j}(1+g_{i}g_{j})

for
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}
.

As in (
13
), we let
M^{\prime}_{\lambda}
,
\lambda\in\mathcal{L}
, be the extension of
V
by
\Bbbk G
determined by

\displaystyle g\cdot y_{i}=y_{g\cdot i}+\lambda(g,y_{i}),

g\in G
and
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}
. Then the algebra
T^{\prime}_{\lambda}=T(V)\#_{M^{\prime}_{\lambda}}\Bbbk G
is a
(T_{\lambda},T(V)\#\Bbbk G)
-bigalois extension by Corollary
2.7
. Let
R^{\prime}_{i,j}
and
r^{\prime}_{i,j}
be the elements in
T_{\lambda}^{\prime}
obtained from
R_{i,j}
and
r_{i,j}
by replacing the letters
x_{i}
with
y_{i}
.

Definition 4.2
.

Given
\lambda\in\mathcal{L}
and
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}
, we denote
A_{\lambda\mu}
the quotient of
T^{\prime}_{\lambda}
by the ideal generated by the elements

\displaystyle R_{i,j}^{\prime}+r^{\prime}_{i,j}+\mu_{i,j}

for
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}
.

We may define the above algebras for any
\mu\in\Bbbk^{\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3}}
but this is pointless in view of the following results.

Lemma 4.3
.

(a)

If
\lambda\in\mathcal{L}
and
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}
, then
A_{\lambda\mu}\neq 0
.

(b)

If
\mu\in\Bbbk^{\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3}}
and
L_{\lambda\mu}\neq 0
, then
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}
.

Proof.

(a)
We think of
A_{\lambda\mu}
as an algebra presented by generators and relations, and apply Bergman’s Diamond Lemma. That is, we have to verify that the ambiguities
g_{k}y_{i}y_{j}
,
y_{3}y_{3}y_{1}
,
y_{3}y_{3}y_{2}
,
y_{3}y_{1}y_{1}
and
y_{3}y_{2}y_{2}
are resolvable. For that purpose, we carried out long and tedious calculations with the help of
[
15
,
16
]
, in which we have to use the relations satisfied by
\lambda
and
\mu
. It turn out that all of these ambiguities are resolvable except for
y_{3}y_{1}y_{1}
and
y_{3}y_{2}y_{2}
, which produce the new relation
y_{2}y_{1}y_{2}=y_{1}y_{2}y_{1}+(\lambda_{2,1}+\lambda_{1,2})y_{2}y_{1}+(\lambda_{2,1}+\lambda_{1,2})y_{1}y_{2}+(\lambda_{1,2}\lambda_{2,1}+\mu_{3,3}+\mu_{1,1}+\mu_{1,2})y_{2}+(\lambda_{1,2}\lambda_{2,1}+\mu_{3,3}+\mu_{2,2}+\mu_{1,2})y_{1}+\lambda_{2,1}(\mu_{2,2}+\mu_{1,2})+\lambda_{1,2}(\mu_{1,1}+\mu_{1,2})
. This gives rise to new ambiguities to analyze, namely
g_{i}y_{2}y_{1}y_{2}
,
y_{3}y_{2}y_{1}y_{2}
,
y_{2}y_{2}y_{1}y_{2}
and
y_{2}y_{1}y_{2}y_{2}
, which also turn out to be resolvable.

(b)
Reasoning as above, it is enough to note that
g_{k}x_{i}x_{j}
is resolvable only if (
19
) holds. Also, the equalities satisfied by the elements
R_{i,j}
and
r_{i,j}
imply (
18
).
∎

We are now ready to state the main result of this subsection.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathcal{L}
and
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}
. Then

(a)

L_{\lambda\mu}
is a Hopf algebra quotient of
T_{\lambda}
which is a lifting of
\mathfrak{B}(V)\#\Bbbk G
, and every such lifting is isomorphic to one of this form.

(b)

A_{\lambda\mu}
is a
(L_{\lambda\mu},\mathfrak{B}(V)\#\Bbbk G)
-bigalois extension.

(c)

L_{\lambda\mu}
is a cocycle deformation of
\mathfrak{B}(V)\#\Bbbk G
.

(d)

L_{\lambda\mu}\simeq L_{\tilde{\lambda}\tilde{\mu}}
if and only if there exist a rack automorphism
\varphi
of
\mathbb{X}
and
\phi,\phi^{0},\phi^{1},\phi^{2}\in\Bbbk
such that
\phi\neq 0
,

\displaystyle\tilde{\lambda}_{\varphi(i),\varphi(j)}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{i,j}+\phi^{i\rhd j}+\phi^{j},

\displaystyle\tilde{\mu}_{\varphi(i),\varphi(j)}

\displaystyle=\mu_{i,j}+\phi^{i}\phi^{j}+\phi^{i\rhd j}\phi^{i}+\phi^{j}\phi^{i\rhd j}\quad\mbox{and}

\displaystyle(\phi+1)\tilde{\lambda}_{\varphi(i),\varphi(j)}

\displaystyle=\phi^{i}\tilde{\lambda}_{\varphi(i),\varphi(j)}+\phi^{i\rhd j}\tilde{\lambda}_{\varphi(i\rhd j),\varphi(i)}+\phi^{j}\tilde{\lambda}_{\varphi(j),\varphi(i\rhd j)}=0

for all
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}
. In such a case, the assignment
g_{i}\mapsto\tilde{g}_{\varphi(i)}
and
x_{i}\mapsto\phi\tilde{x}_{\varphi(i)}+\phi^{i}(1+\tilde{g}_{\varphi(i)})
,
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}
, induces a Hopf algebra isomorphism between
L_{\lambda\mu}
and
L_{\tilde{\lambda}\tilde{\mu}}
; here we write the generators of
L_{\tilde{\lambda}\tilde{\mu}}
with a tilde.

Proof.

In order to prove
(a)
, we observe that in
T_{\lambda}
it holds that

\displaystyle\Delta(R_{i,j}+r_{i,j})=(R_{i,j}+r_{i,j})\otimes 1+g_{i}g_{j}\otimes(R_{i,j}+r_{i,j}),

for all
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}
. Therefore
L_{\lambda\mu}
is a Hopf algebra quotient of
T_{\lambda}
. Also,
(L_{\lambda\mu})_{1}=V\Bbbk G\oplus\Bbbk G
by
(c)
. Then
L_{\lambda\mu}
is a lifting by Corollary
3.2
. Reciprocally, if
L
is such a lifting, it is a quotient of
T_{\lambda}
by Corollary
3.2
, and one deduces that it is isomorphic to some
L_{\lambda\mu}
using the above formula as usual, see for instance
[
1
, Section 4]
.

We now address
(b)
using the strategy from
[
1
, Section 5]
, see Figure
3
. We follow it as a recipe, pointing out the key ingredients in our case. For a more detailed exposition of how the strategy works we refer to
[
1
, Section 5]
,
[
11
]
or
[
17
]
; in particular, the latter considers, among others, the lifting of the Fomin–Kirillov algebra
\mathcal{F}\mathcal{K}_{3}
in characteristic zero.

Recall
T^{\prime}_{\lambda}
is a right Galois extension of
T(V)\#\Bbbk G
by Corollary
2.7
. Let
\gamma_{1}:T(V)\#\Bbbk G\longrightarrow T^{\prime}_{\lambda}
be the corresponding section. One can see that
\gamma_{1}(R_{i,j})=R^{\prime}_{i,j}
proceeding as in
[
11
, Section 4.5]
. Let

be the subalgebra of

generated by
S(R_{i,j})=g_{j}^{-1}g_{i}^{-1}R_{i,j}
,

. This is isomorphic to the tensor algebra

by
[
8
, Lemma 28]
with

the linear span of

’s. Hence the assignment

determines

for any

. By Lemma

,

and hence

is a right Galois extension of

by

. Then, we deduce that

is also a left Galois extension of

using
[

; here we have to use the formula of

. This completes the proof of

, and
(b)

[

The proof of

is standard. We outline the main ideas and leave the details to the readers. If
\Phi:L_{\lambda\mu}\longrightarrow L_{\tilde{\lambda}\tilde{\mu}}
is an isomorphism of Hopf algebras, then

4.2.
An example with infinite-dimensional coradical

Here
\Bbbk
is a field of characteristic zero.
Let
G=\langle g\rangle
be the free abelian group generated by
g
and
V=\Bbbk\{x_{1},x_{2}\}\in{}^{\Bbbk G}_{\Bbbk G}\mathcal{YD}
the Yetter-Drinfeld module given by

\displaystyle g\cdot x_{1}=x_{1},\quad g\cdot x_{2}=x_{2}+x_{1},\quad\delta(x_{1})=g\otimes x_{1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\delta(x_{2})=g\otimes x_{2}.

The Nichols algebra of
V
is the so-called Jordan plane
[
2
]
and it is defined by the relation
x_{1}x_{2}-x_{2}x_{1}-\frac{1}{2}x_{1}^{2}
.

Let
M
be the extension of
V
by
\Bbbk G
given by

\displaystyle g\cdot x_{1}=x_{1}+(1-g)\quad\mbox{and}\quad g\cdot x_{2}=x_{2}+x_{1}.

Thus, the Jordanian enveloping algebra of
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
defined by Andruskiewitsch, Angiono and Heckenberger in
[
3
, (1.4)]
can be presented as

\displaystyle\mathfrak{U}^{\mathrm{jordan}}=T(V)\#_{M}\Bbbk G/\left\langle\mbox{$x_{1}x_{2}-x_{2}x_{1}-\frac{1}{2}x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}+\frac{1}{2}x_{1}$}\right\rangle.

In
[
3
]
the authors showed that
\mathfrak{U}^{\mathrm{jordan}}
is a lifting of
\mathfrak{B}(V)
over
\Bbbk G
, and realized it as a Hopf subalgebra of a Hopf algebra introduced by Ohn
[
22
]
.

Let
M^{\prime}
be the extension of
V
by
\Bbbk G
given by

\displaystyle g\cdot y_{1}=y_{1}+1\quad\mbox{and}\quad g\cdot y_{2}=y_{2}+y_{1}.

We set
\mathfrak{U}^{\prime}=T(V)\#_{M^{\prime}}\Bbbk G/\langle x_{1}x_{2}-x_{2}x_{1}-\frac{1}{2}x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}+\frac{1}{2}x_{1}\rangle
. Similar to
\mathfrak{U}^{\rm jordan}
,
\mathfrak{U}^{\prime}
can be described as the iterated Ore extension
\Bbbk G[x_{1};\operatorname{id},\delta_{1}][x_{2};\sigma,\delta_{2}]
with
\delta_{1}(g)=-g
, while
\sigma
and
\delta_{2}
are as in
[
3
, Proposition 6]
.

It is straightforward to verify that the coactions in Lemma
2.5
induce corresponding coactions in
\mathfrak{U}^{\prime}
over
\mathfrak{U}^{\rm jordan}
and
\mathfrak{B}(V)\#\Bbbk G
making it a left and right comodule algebra, respectively. Moreover, using the strategy proposed in
[
1
, Section 5]
as in Theorem
4.4
, we obtain the following.

Proposition 4.6
.

The algebra
\mathfrak{U}^{\prime}
is a
(\mathfrak{U}^{\rm jordan},\mathfrak{B}(V)\#\Bbbk G)
-bigalois extension and therefore
\mathfrak{U}^{\rm jordan}
is a cocycle deformation of
\mathfrak{B}(V)\#\Bbbk G
. ∎

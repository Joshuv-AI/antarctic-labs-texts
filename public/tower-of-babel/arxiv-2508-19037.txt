Abstract
. In this paper we take a look at compactly generated weak Hausdorff spaces equipped with an action of a compact Lie group G G together with their colimits and homotopy colimits. In particular, we investigate relations between (homotopy) colimits and mapping spaces, and consequently, homotopy groups.

1.
Introduction

Compactly generated spaces were popularized by Steenrod in
[
Ste67
]
and later by McCord in
[
McC69
]
who added the weak Hausdorff condition. Steenrod referred to the compactly generated spaces as a
convenient category of spaces
, which is further supported by the fact that much of the literature today on equivariant and stable homotopy theory uses compactly generated spaces. These spaces have been extensively studied and provide a well-behaved setting for many topological constructions.

This paper is motivated by the study of homotopy colimits in the context of compactly generated
G
-spaces, where
G
is a compact Lie group. In particular, we focus on the relations between homotopy colimits, equivariant mapping spaces, and homotopy groups. These results appear to play an important role in understanding the
\infty
-category of
G
-spaces, yet detailed references are often absent or scattered in the existing literature. The goal of this paper is to present a coherent collection of such results, intended to serve as a useful resource for future work in equivariant homotopy theory and related areas.

We will open this paper with a section on model categories where our main focus will be on the functorial presentation of the homotopy colimit. The following section will be dedicated to the compactly generated weak Hausdorff spaces equipped with an action of a compact Lie group
G
(
G\operatorname{-CGWH}
spaces for short). In that section, we will focus on the interaction of the mapping spaces, and consequently homotopy groups with colimits and homotopy colimits. Let us recall that, if
X
is a colimit of a sequence of closed inclusions of spaces
X_{1}\rightarrow X_{2}\rightarrow...
then the natural map

\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\operatorname{colim}}\,\pi_{k}(X_{n})\rightarrow\pi_{k}(X)

is an isomorphism (see
[
May99
, Ch. 8, section 4]
). We will show that this isomorphism can be upgraded. In particular, we will show that, given a sequence of closed inclusions of
G\operatorname{-CGWH}
-spaces
X_{1}\rightarrow X_{2}\rightarrow...
and a closed subgroup
H\leq G
, the natural map

\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\operatorname{colim}}\,\pi^{H}_{k}(X_{n})\rightarrow\pi^{H}_{k}(\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\operatorname{hocolim}}\,X_{n})

is an isomorphism.

The theory of model categories was developed by Quillen in
[

]

[

[
Hov98
]
,
[
PH03
]
,
[
DS95
]
and
[
IHom
]
, but there many others.

Example 2.11
.

The most important examples of total derived functors are
homotopy
colimits.

Note that, given a category
K
and a category
C
we have a category of
K
-indexed diagrams
C^{K}
. Moreover, we have an adjunction
\operatorname{colim}:C^{K}\rightleftarrows C:c
where
\operatorname{colim}
is a functor which assigns to every
K
-diagram
X:K\rightarrow C
its colimit and where
c
is a constant functor.

If
C
is a model category we can equip
C^{K}
with the
projective model structure
in which the weak equivalences (resp. fibrations) are those natural transformations
\tau:X\rightarrow Y
(where
X,Y:K\rightarrow C
) such that
\tau_{k}:X(k)\rightarrow Y(k)
is a weak equivalence (resp. fibration) for every
k\in K
. The cofibrations would be those natural transformations that have the left lifting property (LLP) with respect to all trivial fibrations (i.e. fibrations that are also weak equivalences).

Considering
\operatorname{colim}
functor as a functor between two model categories we will denote with

\operatorname{hocolim}:=\mathbb{L}^{\operatorname{tot}}\operatorname{colim}

the homotopy colimit functor. We can make an analogous definition for homotopy limits.

.

3.
G\operatorname{-CGWH}
spaces

Definition 3.1
.

Let us denote with
G\operatorname{-CGWH}
the category whose objects are compactly generated weak Hausdorff spaces equipped with an action of a compact Lie group
G
, and whose morphisms are continuous (not necessarily equivariant) maps.

Remark 3.2
.

One may notice that when speaking of compactly generated weak Hausdorff spaces, one often omits the latter part, writing only
compactly generated
and assuming that the spaces are weak Hausdorff by default (
[
Rezk
]
,
[
Sch18
, Appendix A]
), hence, when speaking of elements of the category
G\operatorname{-CGWH}
we will often write
G\operatorname{-CGWH}
space, or just compactly generated
G
-space.

As noted in
[
Sch18
, Appendix B]
, the category of
G\operatorname{-CGWH}
spaces is (co)complete. Using
[
Sch18
, Proposition B.2 (i)]
, the orbit space
G/H
is compactly generated, where
H\leq G
is a closed subgroup, which gives us the following result.

Proposition 3.3
.

Every
G\operatorname{-CW}
-complex is compactly generated.

Proof.

Let us first prove this claim for finite
G\operatorname{-CW}
-complexes. We can do this by induction on the skeleton. Let
X
be a finite
G\operatorname{-CW}
-complex. The
0
-skeleton,
X_{0}
is simply a disjoint union of orbit spaces which are compactly generated as mentioned above. Let us assume that
n-1
-skeleton of
X
,
X_{n-1}
, is compactly generated. Now the
n
-skeleton is obtained via the pushout
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underset{j}{\coprod}G/H_{j}\times S^{n-1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{n-1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underset{j}{\coprod}G/H_{j}\times D^{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{n}}

Note that this diagram is a pushout square of compactly generated spaces, since
S^{n-1}
and
D^{n}
are compact, hence
X_{n}
is compactly generated as well.

Finally, let
X
be a
G\operatorname{-CW}
complex. Then
X
can be presented as the colimit of a sequence of finite
G\operatorname{-CW}
-complexes
X_{0}\rightarrow X_{1}\rightarrow X_{2}\rightarrow...
where each
X_{k}\rightarrow X_{k+1}
is a closed inclusion. Since all of the elements of the sequence are compactly generated, so will
X
be.
∎

The category of
G\operatorname{-CGWH}
spaces can be endowed with a model structure, which we call the
standard model structure for
G
-spaces
(
[
Sch18
, Proposition B.7]
), where:

•

weak equivalences
are weak homotopy equivalences of
G
-spaces, that is,
G
-maps
f:X\rightarrow Y
such that
f^{H}:X^{H}\rightarrow Y^{H}
is a weak homotopy equivalence for every closed subgroup
H\leq G
;

•

fibrations
are Serre fibrations of
G
-spaces, that is,
G
-maps
f:X\rightarrow Y
such that
f^{H}:X^{H}\rightarrow Y^{H}
is a Serre fibration for every closed subgroup
H\leq G
;

•

cofibrations
are maps which satisfy the left lifting property with respect to acyclic fibrations (i.e. fibrations that are also weak equivalences).

Note that, similar as in
2.7
every
G\operatorname{-CGWH}
space is fibrant in the standard model structure. Additionally, by
[
CBMS
, Ch. I, Theorem 3.1]
,
G\operatorname{-CW}
-complexes are fibrant objects. Moreover, analogous to the proofs of
[
Hov98
, 2.4.5, 2.4.6]
we can conclude:

Proposition 3.4
.

Every cofibration in the standard model structure on
G\operatorname{-CGWH}
spaces is a closed
G
-inclusion.

Remark 3.5
.

When
K
is compact and
X
is compactly generated, the mapping space
\operatorname{Map}_{\operatorname{Spc}}(K,X)
of all continuous maps is compactly generated (see
[
Rezk
, Proposition 8.2]
). If we take spaces
K
and
X
to be equipped with an action of a compact Lie group
G
, we will denote with
\operatorname{Map}(K,X)
the
G\operatorname{-CGWH}
space of all continuous maps with
G
acting by conjugation. Additionally, given a
G\operatorname{-CGWH}
space
X
and a closed subgroup
H\leq G
, the space
X^{H}
is a closed subspace of
X
and hence compactly generated (
[
Sch18
, Proposition A.5, Appendix B]
), meaning that the space
(\operatorname{Map}(K,X))^{H}:=\operatorname{Map}_{H}(K,X)
of all
H
-equivariant continuous maps is compactly generated.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let

X_{0}\rightarrow X_{1}\rightarrow...\rightarrow X_{n}\rightarrow..

be a sequence of
G\operatorname{-CGWH}
spaces where the maps
X_{i}\rightarrow X_{i+1}
are closed
G
-inclusions. Denote with
X
the colimit of the sequence. Then for every compact
G
-space
K
we have a bijection

(1)

\tau:\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\operatorname{colim}}\,\operatorname{Map}(K,X_{n})\rightarrow\operatorname{Map}(K,X)

which is
G
-equivariant. Moreover, for every closed subgroup
H\leq G
, we have a bijection

\tau^{H}:\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\operatorname{colim}}\,\operatorname{Map}_{H}(K,X_{n})\rightarrow\operatorname{Map}_{H}(K,X)

Proof.

Let
[f^{\prime}]\in\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\operatorname{colim}}\,\operatorname{Map}(K,X_{n})
be an element of the colimit represented by a map
f^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Map}(K,X_{n})
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
. By
[
Sch18
, Proposition A.14]
we have a closed inclusion
i_{n}:X_{n}\rightarrow X
which will also be a
G
-map since all of the maps in the sequence are
G
-maps, hence,
\tau
can be viewed as a map sending an element
[f^{\prime}]
to the map
i_{n}\circ f^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Map}(K,X)
. Additionally, since every map
f\in\operatorname{Map}(K,X)
factors through some
X_{n}
(see
[
Rezk
, 10.14]
) i.e.
f=i_{n}\circ f^{\prime}
for some
f^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Map}(K,X_{n})
, the element of
\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\operatorname{colim}}\,\operatorname{Map}(K,X_{n})
represented by
f^{\prime}
maps to
f
by
\tau
, hence
\tau
is surjective. Furthermore, let
[f_{1}],[f_{2}]
be two non-equal elements of
\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\operatorname{colim}}\,\operatorname{Map}(K,X_{n})
. For large enough
n\in\mathbb{N}
we may represent these elements by maps
f_{1},f_{2}:\operatorname{Map}(K,X_{n})
. Then these elements are sent by
\tau
to the maps
i_{n}\circ f_{1}
and
i_{n}\circ f_{2}
. Since
f_{1}\neq f_{2}
, then
i_{n}\circ f_{1}\neq i_{n}\circ f_{2}
, hence
\tau
is injective, and a bijection.

What is left to check is that
\tau
is a
G
-map. Again, let
[f]
be an element of
\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\operatorname{colim}}\,\operatorname{Map}(K,X_{n})
represented by a map

, and let
g\in G
. Recall that
\tau([f])=i_{n}\circ f
. Since the induced maps
\operatorname{Map}(K,X_{i})\rightarrow\operatorname{Map}(K,X_{i+1})
are closed
G
-inclusions, the element
g[f]
corresponds to the element
[gf]
. Since all of the maps
X_{k}\rightarrow X_{k+1}
are closed
G
-inclusion, so will
i_{n}
be, hence
\tau(g[f])=i_{n}\circ(gf)=g(i_{n}\circ f)=g\tau([f])
i.e.
\tau
is
G
-equivariant.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

Since we are working in the category

we ought to prove that

Before moving forward let us recall the definition of the homotopy groups of equivariant spaces.

Definition 3.8
.

Let
H\leq G
be a closed subgroup of a compact Lie group
G
, let
X
be a
G
-space and let
x\in X
be a
G
-fixed point. Then we define

\pi^{H}_{n}(X,x):=\pi_{n}(X^{H},x)

for
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

As mentioned in
3.5
, given a
G\operatorname{-CGWH}
space
X
and a closed subgroup
H\leq G
, the space
X^{H}
is a closed subspace of
X
and hence compactly generated. Then the closed inclusion of
G
-spaces
X\rightarrow Y
gives us a closed inclusion
X^{H}\rightarrow Y^{H}
. Additionally, taking
H
-fixed points commutes with filtered colimits (and, in particular, sequential colimits) along
G
-maps that are closed inclusions,
[
Sch18
, Proposition B.1 (ii)]
, hence we obtain the following lemma.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
H\leq G
be a closed subgroup. Then for a sequence

X_{0}\rightarrow X_{1}\rightarrow...\rightarrow X_{n}\rightarrow..

of
G\operatorname{-CGWH}
spaces where the maps
X_{i}\rightarrow X_{i+1}
are closed
G
-inclusions and every
G
-fixed point
x\in X_{0}
, the map

\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\operatorname{colim}}\,\pi^{H}_{k}(X_{n},x)\rightarrow\pi^{H}_{k}(\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\operatorname{colim}}\,X_{n},x)

is an isomorphism for every
k\geq 0
.

Proof.

Let
X
be the colimit of the upper sequence. Since taking
H
-fixed points commutes with sequential colimits, we have
\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\operatorname{colim}}\,X_{n}^{H}=X^{H}
. The statement now follows from the result in
[
May99
, Ch. 8, section 4]
stating that
\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\operatorname{colim}}\,\pi_{k}(X^{H}_{n},x)\rightarrow\pi_{k}(X^{H},x)
is an isomorphism.
∎

Proposition 3.10
.

Let

X_{0}\rightarrow X_{1}\rightarrow...\rightarrow X_{n}\rightarrow..

be a sequence of
G\operatorname{-CGWH}
spaces where the maps
X_{i}\rightarrow X_{i+1}
are closed

-inclusions. Then the colimit
\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\operatorname{colim}}\,X_{n}
in the category of

Proof.

The homotopy colimit of the upper sequence is computed by taking the cofibrant replacement of the sequence in the projective model structure and then computing the colimit. In particular, we arrive to the familiar commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces...}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces...}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces CX_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces CX_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces...}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces CX_{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces...}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{n}}

where the maps
f_{i}:CX_{i}\rightarrow X_{i}
are

-weak homotopy equivalences and where

are closed

-inclusions. Let

and we want to show that this map is a

,

and let

,

. Since the upper diagram is a commutative diagram of
G
-maps, we have
f_{n}(gx^{\prime})=gf_{n}(x^{\prime})=gy^{\prime}
i.e.
f(gx)=gy=gf(x)
. In other words,
f
is a
G
-equivariant map.

In order to show further that
f
is a
G
-weak homotopy equivalence, let us pass to the diagram of
H
-fixed points, with
H\leq G
a closed subgroup. We arrive to the following diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{1}^{H}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{2}^{H}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces...}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{n}^{H}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces...}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces CX_{1}^{H}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces CX_{2}^{H}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces...}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces CX_{n}^{H}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces...}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{1}^{H}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{2}^{H}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{n}^{H}}

where both sequences are sequences of closed inclusions of compactly generated spaces, and where the maps
f_{i}^{H}
are all weak homotopy equivalences. Since taking fixed points commutes with filtered colimits, and in particular, sequential colimits
[
Sch18
, Proposition B.1]
, the colimits of the upper and lower sequence are
X^{H}
and
CX^{H}
respectively, and the induced map on colimits is exactly
f^{H}
. Then, by
[
Sch18
, Proposition A.17]
the map
f^{H}:CX^{H}\rightarrow X^{H}
is a weak homotopy equivalence as well, hence,
f
is a
G
-weak homotopy equivalence. Homotopy colimits are unique up to an isomorphism in the homotopy category of the model category, hence, the proof is finished since
f
is a weak equivalence in the standard model structure.
∎

Remark 3.11
.

The reader might be familiar with the fact that one model for the homotopy colimit of a sequence of spaces
X_{0}\rightarrow X_{1}\rightarrow X_{2}\rightarrow...
is the
mapping telescope
. Additionally, it is not hard to prove that, in the case where all of the maps in the sequence are closed inclusions, the mapping telescope is homotopy equivalent to the colimit of the sequence. This coincides with the results given above. Although this holds for compactly generated (non-equivariant) spaces, one can extend this result to the equivariant setting.

Corollary 3.12
.

Let
K
be a compact
G
-space and let

X_{0}\rightarrow X_{1}\rightarrow...\rightarrow X_{n}\rightarrow..

be a sequence of
G\operatorname{-CGWH}
spaces where the maps
X_{i}\rightarrow X_{i+1}
are closed
G
-inclusions. Then the map

\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\operatorname{colim}}\,\operatorname{Map}(K,X_{n})\rightarrow\operatorname{Map}(K,\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\operatorname{colim}}\,X_{n})

is a weak homotopy equivalence of
G
-spaces.

Corollary 3.13
.

Let
K
be a compact
G\operatorname{-CW}
-complex and let

X_{0}\rightarrow X_{1}\rightarrow...\rightarrow X_{n}\rightarrow..

be a sequence of
G\operatorname{-CGWH}
spaces. Then the map

\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\operatorname{hocolim}}\,\operatorname{Map}(K,X_{n})\rightarrow\operatorname{Map}(K,\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\operatorname{hocolim}}\,X_{n})

is a weak equivalence of
G
-spaces.

Proof.

The homotopy colimit is computed by taking the colimit of the cofibrant replacement of the initial diagram with respect to the projective model structure. In our case, that would include replacing the sequence by a weakly equivalent one involving
G
-cofibrations. The functor
\operatorname{Map}(K,\_)
with
K
a compact
G\operatorname{-CW}
-complex preserves weak homotopy equivalences of
G
-spaces, hence we can assume that the maps
X_{n}\rightarrow X_{n+1}
are
G
-cofibrations, and therefore closed inclusions by
3.4
. Then each map
\operatorname{Map}(K,X_{n})\rightarrow\operatorname{Map}(K,X_{n+1})
is also a closed inclusion. The desired result follows from
3.10
and
3.12
.
∎

Proposition 3.14
.

Let

X_{0}\rightarrow X_{1}\rightarrow...\rightarrow X_{n}\rightarrow..

be a sequence of
G\operatorname{-CGWH}
spaces and let
H\leq G
be a closed subgroup. Then the map

\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\operatorname{colim}}\,\pi^{H}_{k}(X_{n})\rightarrow\pi^{H}_{k}(\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\operatorname{hocolim}}\,X_{n})

is an isomorphism.

Proof.

As in the previous proof, the homotopy colimit is computed by replacing the initial sequence by a weakly equivalent one involving
G
-cofibrations, which are, in particular, closed inclusions. Let us denote with
\{CX_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
the cofibrant replacement diagram of
\{X_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. The maps
CX_{n}\rightarrow X_{n}
are all weak homotopy equivalences of
G
-spaces, hence by
3.9
we have have an isomorphism

\displaystyle\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\operatorname{colim}}\,\pi^{H}_{k}(X_{n})

\displaystyle\cong\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\operatorname{colim}}\,\pi^{H}_{k}(CX_{n})\rightarrow\pi^{H}_{k}(\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\operatorname{colim}}\,CX_{n})

\displaystyle\cong\pi^{H}_{k}(\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\operatorname{colim}}\,X_{n})

for every
k\geq 0
. With this the proof is finished.
∎

4.
Further topics and applications

Homotopy limits and colimits represent the variants of the notion of the limits and colimits in the homotopy category. Furthermore, the notion of homotopy limits and colimits coincide with the notion of limits and colimits in the underlying
\infty
-category of a model category (see
[
HTT
, Chapter 4]
for more details), hence, their study in the category of
G
-spaces leads to better understanding of the
\infty
-category of
G
-spaces. In particular, the results presented in this paper were used to prove that the
\infty
-category of
G
-spaces is a presentable
\infty
-category in the PhD thesis of the author. In more detail, the author proved that the
\infty
-category of
G
-spaces is generated under filtered colimits by the compact objects, which are further proved to be finite
G\operatorname{-CW}
-complexes. To achieve this, the results such as commutativity of homotopy colimits and mapping spaces were used.

Moreover, these constructions and equivalences extend naturally to the study of
G
-spectra. As such, the techniques and results of this paper may serve as valuable tools for further developments in equivariant stable homotopy theory.

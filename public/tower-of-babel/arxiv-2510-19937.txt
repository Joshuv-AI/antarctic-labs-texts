Dimensionality-Changing Transition from a Non-Fermi Liquid to a Spin-Solid in a Multichannel Kondo Lattice
Simon Martin, Marcin Raczkowski, Fakher F. Assaad, Tarun Grover

Abstract
A multichannel Kondo system, where a single quantum spin couples to multiple channels of an electronic bath, provides one of the simplest examples of a zero-dimensional non-Fermi liquid. It is natural to ask: what happens when an extensive number of such systems are coupled together? A simple renormalization group argument implies that in a chain of SU ( N ) (N) multichannel quantum systems, where each spin is coupled to its own bath of K K channels, the individual spins dynamically decouple at low energy when N > K N>K , resulting in a ‘sliding’ non-Fermi liquid. Using Quantum Monte Carlo (QMC) simulations, we find evidences of a continuous, ‘dimensionality-changing’ phase transition out of this non-Fermi liquid into a valence-bond solid phase as the intersite coupling is increased. Remarkably, at the critical point, correlations exhibit a power-law behavior even along the direction in which the spins are coupled, indicating the breakdown of dynamical decoupling at the transition. We also develop an RG scheme to understand the universal aspects of this transition.

;

The logarithmic discretization makes the imaginary time projection,

required

Discussion:
Perhaps the most interesting feature of our model is that it exhibits a continuous, dimensionality-changing transition where both spatial and temporal correlations decay as a power-law. Unlike coupled SYK models, where a direct hopping between SYK-dots destroys the decoupled non-Fermi liquid and results in a Fermi liquid at low temperature
Song et al. 2017
, in our model, the decoupled phase is stable as long as the symmetries of the model are maintained. Previous works have also studied dimensional reduction and associated dimensionality-changing phase transitions in various contexts, but to the best of our knowledge, in all such examples, one finds that the spatial correlations decay exponentially along the direction where the lower-dimensional systems are being coupled
Emery et al. 2000
;
Vishwanath and Carpentier 2001
;
Golubović and Golubović 1998
;
O’Hern and Lubensky 1998
;
Wu and Zhang 2023
;
Lake and Hermele 2021
. A full RG treatment of the critical spatial correlations in our problem remains an interesting open question. We also note that the RG prediction for the critical temporal correlations at the leading order in the
\epsilon
expansion differs qualitatively from the QMC results – the RG predicts that scaling dimension is greater than that in the decoupled phase, while the QMC suggests it is smaller. This suggests that the higher order contributions in the
\epsilon
-expansion may be important. Although we focused on multichannel Kondo dots coupled in a specific way, our methodology of employing Wilson numerical RG with QMC opens the window to explore a large class of open problems that have been discussed in the literature and where there remains several unanswered questions. For example, it allows one to explore a setup where the non-Fermi liquid behavior of a 0+1-D system gets imprinted on a larger dimensional system through coupling with an array of such 0+1-D systems
Ben-Zion and McGreevy 2018
. Another problem worth mentioning is that of multichannel Kondo dots coupled
ferromagnetically
. Our RG analysis implies that in such a setup, when
K>N
, decoupled dots becomes immediately unstable towards a new, critical
phase
. Another point worth investigating more is the landscape of phase transitions encoded in our RG. As mentioned earlier, the RG admits solutions where the couplings are long-ranged with oscillating signs, which is rather intriguing. Since RG is controlled by the small parameter
\epsilon=\frac{2}{1+K/N}-1
, we anticipate that these solutions will manifest themselves for sufficiently small
\epsilon
(e.g.
N=10,K=8
), and it will be interesting to explore this possibility using QMC. Given the richness of the phase diagram of such coupled multichannel systems, we hope that our results will be a motivation to push beyond experiments such as Refs.
Pouse et al. 2023
;
Karki et al. 2023
that study a few sites coupled multichannel Kondo systems.

Appendix A
RG calculation

This appendix provides details of the RG calculation for the coupled SU
(N)
multichannel Kondo models using conformal perturbation theory. The Hamiltonian governing our system is

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{H}&=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}\sum_{k}\sum_{x=1}^{L}\epsilon(k)\hat{c}^{{\dagger}}_{i\alpha}(x,k)\hat{c}_{i\alpha}(x,k)+J_{K}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{N}\sum_{a=1}^{N^{2}-1}\sum_{x=1}^{L}\hat{S}^{a}(x)\hat{c}^{{\dagger}}_{i\alpha}(x,y=0)T^{a}_{\alpha\beta}\hat{c}_{i\beta}(x,y=0)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+J_{H}\sum_{a=1}^{N^{2}-1}\sum_{x=1}^{L}\hat{S}^{a}(x)\hat{S}^{a}(x+1)\,,\end{split}

(3)

where the first term describes the conduction electron baths, with
k
the conduction electron momentum,
i=1,...,K
labels the channel,
\alpha=1,...,N
labels the spin and
x=1,...,L
labels the impurity site. We use periodic boundary conditions along
x
. The second term describes the Kondo interaction (at the origin of the bath
y=0
) between the conduction electrons and an SU
(N)
impurity
\hat{S}^{a}
, transforming in the fully anti-symmetric representation with
N/2
rows and a single column. The impurity is written in terms of fermionic spinons:
\hat{S}^{a}=\hat{f}_{\alpha}^{{\dagger}}T_{\alpha\beta}^{a}\hat{f}_{\beta}
, where
\hat{f}_{\alpha}
obeys the constraint
\hat{f}_{\alpha}^{{\dagger}}\hat{f}_{\alpha}=N/2
on each site and the
T^{a}
’s are the
N^{2}-1
generators of SU
(N)
in the fundamental representation. The last term corresponds to the nearest-neighbor Heisenberg interaction between the impurities.

A.1
Decoupled fixed point

Let us first consider the situation where
J_{H}=0
, which corresponds to a set of
L
decoupled dots. The single-impurity multichannel Kondo model has been extensively studied in the literature
Nozieres and Blandin 1980
;
Parcollet et al. 1998
;
Affleck and Ludwig 1991a
;
Affleck and Ludwig 1991b
;
Affleck and Ludwig 1993
;
Ludwig and Affleck 1994
and has been shown to flow in the IR to a 1+1D SU
(N)_{K}
Wess-Zumino-Witten (WZW) boundary conformal field theory (bCFT) for
K>1
(for the fully-antisymmetric representation). This intermediate fixed point describes a 0+1D non-Fermi liquid. At this fixed point, the impurity
S^{a}
acquires the scaling dimension of the primary operator of the SU
(N)_{K}
algebra associated with the
(N^{2}-1)
-dimensional adjoint representation of SU
(N)
, denoted
\phi^{a}
, with scaling dimension
\Delta_{\phi}=\frac{N}{N+K}=\frac{1}{1+\gamma}\equiv\Delta_{S}^{0}
, with
\gamma=\frac{K}{N}
. This comes from the fact that
\phi^{a}
is the most relevant boundary operator with the same symmetries as
S^{a}
(the next operator is the current
J^{a}
which has dimension
\Delta_{J}=1
and is thus marginal on the boundary). Therefore, we make the replacement
S^{a}\rightarrow\phi^{a}
in the IR, despite the fact that we will still use the symbol
S^{a}
.

The RG calculation will require the knowledge of the OPE of
S^{a}
with itself in the single-impurity bCFT

S_{x}^{a}(\tau)S_{x}^{b}(0)=C_{SS\mathds{1}}\frac{\delta^{ab}}{|\tau|^{2\Delta_{S}^{0}}}\mathds{1}_{x}+iC_{SSS}\text{sgn}(\tau)\frac{f^{abc}}{|\tau|^{\Delta_{S}^{0}}}S^{c}_{x}(0)+...\,.

(4)

This form can be justified from the invariance under the anti-unitary “time-reversal” symmetry:
f_{\alpha}\rightarrow f_{\alpha}^{{\dagger}}\implies S^{a}\rightarrow-S^{a}
,
i\rightarrow-i
. The ellipsis denotes more irrelevant terms, while subscripts
x
emphasize that each impurity has its own OPE.
C_{SS\mathds{1}}
and
C_{SSS}
are the OPE coefficients associated respectively with the fusions
\phi\times\phi\rightarrow\mathds{1}
and
\phi\times\phi\rightarrow\phi
. In what follows, we work with a normalized impurity 2-point function
\langle S^{a}(\tau)S^{b}(0)\rangle=\delta^{ab}/|\tau|^{2\Delta_{S}^{0}}
, so that we choose
C_{SS\mathds{1}}=1
.
f^{abc}
is the fully antisymmetric structure constant of SU
(N)
.

A.2
Conformal perturbation theory

We can now perform the RG analysis by using conformal perturbation theory. The decoupled IR bCFTs described by the critical action
S^{*}=\sum_{x=1}^{L}S^{*}_{x}
are perturbed by Heisenberg interactions. As we will soon show it, one cannot only consider the nearest-neighbor interaction, as interactions with larger separations (next-nearest-neighbor,…) will also be generated. Let us start by defining the operator

O_{x}(\tau)=\sum_{x^{\prime}=1}^{L}S_{x^{\prime}}^{a}(\tau)S_{x^{\prime}+x}^{a}(\tau)\,.

(5)

For simplicity, we will start by detailing the case of an odd number of impurities
L
(results for
L
even are presented below). One can easily show that
O_{L-x}=O_{x}
, which means that we only have to consider operators
O_{x}
in the interval
x=[1,\frac{L-1}{2}]
. Therefore, the full action is
S=S^{*}+\delta S
, where the perturbations are

\delta S=\sum_{x=1}^{\frac{L-1}{2}}J_{x}\int d\tau O_{x}(\tau)+h\int d\tau O_{h}(\tau)\,,

(6)

where the last term, which contains the operator
O_{h}(\tau)=\sum_{x=1}^{L}\sum_{a}S_{x}^{a}(\tau)
, plays the role of a magnetic field and will be useful to compute the scaling dimension of
S^{a}_{x}
. The RG flow equations for the couplings
J_{x}
and
h
are obtained at quadratic order using the general formula
Cardy 1996

\beta(g_{k})=(D-\Delta_{k})g_{k}-\sum_{i,j}C_{ijk}g_{i}g_{j}+...\,,

(7)

where
g_{i}
represents the couplings
J_{x}
and
h
,
\Delta_{k}
is the scaling dimension of operator
O_{k}
and
C_{ijk}
are the OPE coefficients of the fusion
O_{i}\times O_{j}\rightarrow O_{k}
for the operators of Eq.
6
.

Let us emphasize that the unperturbed bCFTs live in
D=0+1
spacetime dimension and thus do not know about the spatial dimension along the spin chain. Since
O_{x}
couples two different CFTs (at two different sites), its scaling dimension is simply
2\Delta_{S}^{0}
and the RG equation for
J_{x}
at tree-level is thus

\beta(J_{x})=\frac{dJ_{x}}{dl}=(1-2\Delta_{S}^{0})J_{x}+\mathcal{O}(J_{x}^{2})=\Big[1-\frac{2}{1+\gamma}\Big]J_{x}+\mathcal{O}(J_{x}^{2})=-\epsilon J_{x}+\mathcal{O}(J_{x}^{2})\,,

(8)

where
\epsilon=\frac{2}{1+\gamma}-1
. Hence, for
\gamma\approx 1
, we have
|\epsilon|\ll 1
, which can be used to control the RG. Even if
\gamma
is the ratio of two integers, it is treated as a continuous parameter for the sake of the RG calculation. Moreover, note that the sign of
\epsilon
depends on whether
\gamma<1
(
K<N
), for which
\epsilon>0
or
\gamma>1
(
K>N
), for which
\epsilon<0
.

We note that the idea of obtaining RG equations for a stack of conformally invariant systems (albeit in a different context) has also been discussed in Refs.
Lake and Hermele 2021
;
Lake 2021
.

A.2.1
OPE coefficients of perturbing operators

Having established all of this, the goal is now to compute the OPE coefficients
C_{ijk}
. Let us start with the OPE of
O_{x}
with itself

\displaystyle\begin{split}O_{x}(\tau)O_{x}(0)&=\sum_{x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}=1}^{L}S_{x^{\prime}}^{a}(\tau)S_{x^{\prime}+x}^{a}(\tau)S_{x^{\prime\prime}}^{b}(0)S_{x^{\prime\prime}+x}^{b}(0)\\
&=\sum_{x^{\prime}=1}^{L}\Big[S_{x^{\prime}}^{a}(\tau)S_{x^{\prime}}^{b}(0)\Big]\Big[S_{x^{\prime}+x}^{a}(\tau)S_{x^{\prime}+x}^{b}(0)\Big]+\sum_{x^{\prime}=1}^{L}\Big[S_{x^{\prime}+x}^{a}(\tau)S_{x^{\prime}+x}^{b}(0)\Big]S_{x^{\prime}}^{a}(\tau)S_{x^{\prime}+2x}^{b}(0)\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt+\sum_{x^{\prime}=1}^{L}\Big[S_{x^{\prime}}^{a}(\tau)S_{x^{\prime}}^{b}(0)\Big]S_{x^{\prime}+x}^{a}(\tau)S_{x^{\prime}-x}^{b}(0)+...\,,\end{split}

(9)

where the three terms correspond respectively to
x^{\prime\prime}=x^{\prime}
,
x^{\prime\prime}=x^{\prime}+x
and
x^{\prime\prime}=x^{\prime}-x
, while ellipsis denote terms with four spins on different sites. We now apply the single-impurity OPE to the brackets

\displaystyle\begin{split}O_{x}(\tau)O_{x}(0)&=\sum_{x^{\prime}=1}^{L}\Bigg[\frac{\delta^{ab}}{|\tau|^{2\Delta_{S}^{0}}}\mathds{1}_{x^{\prime}}+iC_{SSS}\text{sgn}(\tau)\frac{f^{abc}}{|\tau|^{\Delta_{S}^{0}}}S^{c}_{x^{\prime}}(0)\Bigg]\Bigg[\frac{\delta^{ab}}{|\tau|^{2\Delta_{S}^{0}}}\mathds{1}_{x^{\prime}+x}+iC_{SSS}\text{sgn}(\tau)\frac{f^{abd}}{|\tau|^{\Delta_{S}^{0}}}S^{d}_{x^{\prime}+x}(0)\Bigg]\\
&\hskip 19.91684pt+\sum_{x^{\prime}=1}^{L}\Bigg[\frac{\delta^{ab}}{|\tau|^{2\Delta_{S}^{0}}}\mathds{1}_{x^{\prime}+x}+iC_{SSS}\text{sgn}(\tau)\frac{f^{abd}}{|\tau|^{\Delta_{S}^{0}}}S^{d}_{x^{\prime}+x}(0)\Bigg]S_{x^{\prime}}^{a}(\tau)S_{x^{\prime}+2x}^{b}(0)\\
&\hskip 19.91684pt+\sum_{x^{\prime}=1}^{L}\Bigg[\frac{\delta^{ab}}{|\tau|^{2\Delta_{S}^{0}}}\mathds{1}_{x^{\prime}}+iC_{SSS}\text{sgn}(\tau)\frac{f^{abd}}{|\tau|^{\Delta_{S}^{0}}}S^{d}_{x^{\prime}}(0)\Bigg]S_{x^{\prime}+x}^{a}(\tau)S_{x^{\prime}-x}^{b}(0)+...\\
&=\frac{(N^{2}-1)}{|\tau|^{4\Delta_{S}^{0}}}\sum_{x^{\prime}=1}^{L}\mathds{1}_{x^{\prime}}\mathds{1}_{x^{\prime}+x}-C_{SSS}^{2}\frac{N}{|\tau|^{2\Delta_{S}^{0}}}\sum_{x^{\prime}=1}^{L}S_{x^{\prime}}^{a}(0)S^{a}_{x^{\prime}+x}(0)\\
&\hskip 19.91684pt+\frac{1}{|\tau|^{2\Delta_{S}^{0}}}\sum_{x^{\prime}=1}^{L}S_{x^{\prime}}^{a}(\tau)\mathds{1}_{x^{\prime}+x}S_{x^{\prime}+2x}^{a}(0)+\frac{1}{|\tau|^{2\Delta_{S}^{0}}}\sum_{x^{\prime}=1}^{L}\mathds{1}_{x^{\prime}}S_{x^{\prime}+x}^{a}(\tau)S_{x^{\prime}-x}^{a}(0)+...\\
&\approx\frac{N^{2}-1}{|\tau|^{4\Delta_{S}^{0}}}\mathds{1}-\frac{NC_{SSS}^{2}}{|\tau|^{2\Delta_{S}^{0}}}O_{x}(0)+\frac{2}{|\tau|^{2\Delta_{S}^{0}}}O_{2x}(0)+...\,,\end{split}

(10)

where we have used
\delta^{ab}\delta^{ab}=N^{2}-1
and
f^{abc}f^{abd}=N\delta^{cd}
. The last equality follows from Taylor expanding to leading order in
\tau
and using periodic boundary conditions along
x
. Ellipses denote terms with three or more spins, which are irrelevant operators. From this, we identify the following two OPE coefficients:

C_{xxx}=-NC_{SSS}^{2}\,,\quad C_{xx,2x}=2\,.

(11)

We now move on to the OPE of
O_{x}
with
O_{x^{\prime}}
, with
x^{\prime}\neq x

\displaystyle\begin{split}&O_{x}(\tau)O_{x^{\prime}}(0)\\
&=\sum_{x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime\prime\prime}=1}^{L}S_{x^{\prime\prime}}^{a}(\tau)S^{a}_{x^{\prime\prime}+x}(\tau)S_{x^{\prime\prime\prime}}^{b}(0)S_{x^{\prime\prime\prime}+x^{\prime}}^{b}(0)\\
&=\sum_{x^{\prime\prime}=1}^{L}\Big[S_{x^{\prime\prime}}^{a}(\tau)S_{x^{\prime\prime}}^{b}(0)\Big]S^{a}_{x^{\prime\prime}+x}(\tau)S_{x^{\prime\prime}+x^{\prime}}^{b}(0)+\sum_{x^{\prime\prime}=1}^{L}\Big[S_{x^{\prime\prime}}^{a}(\tau)S_{x^{\prime\prime}}^{b}(0)\Big]S^{a}_{x^{\prime\prime}+x}(\tau)S_{x^{\prime\prime}-x^{\prime}}^{b}(0)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\sum_{x^{\prime\prime}=1}^{L}\Big[S_{x^{\prime\prime}+x}^{a}(\tau)S_{x^{\prime\prime}+x}^{b}(0)\Big]S^{a}_{x^{\prime\prime}}(\tau)S_{x^{\prime\prime}+x+x^{\prime}}^{b}(0)+\sum_{x^{\prime\prime}=1}^{L}\Big[S_{x^{\prime\prime}+x}^{a}(\tau)S_{x^{\prime\prime}+x}^{b}(0)\Big]S^{a}_{x^{\prime\prime}}(\tau)S_{x^{\prime\prime}+x-x^{\prime}}^{b}(0)+...\\
&=\frac{1}{|\tau|^{2\Delta_{S}^{0}}}\sum_{x^{\prime\prime}=1}^{L}\mathds{1}_{x^{\prime\prime}}S_{x^{\prime\prime}+x}^{a}(\tau)S_{x^{\prime\prime}+x^{\prime}}^{a}(0)+\frac{1}{|\tau|^{2\Delta_{S}^{0}}}\sum_{x^{\prime\prime}=1}^{L}\mathds{1}_{x^{\prime\prime}}S_{x^{\prime\prime}+x}^{a}(\tau)S_{x^{\prime\prime}-x^{\prime}}^{a}(0)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\frac{1}{|\tau|^{2\Delta_{S}^{0}}}\sum_{x^{\prime\prime}=1}^{L}\mathds{1}_{x^{\prime\prime}+x}S_{x^{\prime\prime}}^{a}(\tau)S_{x^{\prime\prime}+x+x^{\prime}}^{a}(0)+\frac{1}{|\tau|^{2\Delta_{S}^{0}}}\sum_{x^{\prime\prime}=1}^{L}\mathds{1}_{x^{\prime\prime}+x}S_{x^{\prime\prime}}^{a}(\tau)S_{x^{\prime\prime}+x-x^{\prime}}^{a}(0)+...\\
&\approx\frac{2}{|\tau|^{2\Delta_{S}^{0}}}O_{x+x^{\prime}}(0)+\frac{2}{|\tau|^{2\Delta_{S}^{0}}}O_{x-x^{\prime}}(0)+...\,,\end{split}

(12)

which leads to the OPE coefficients

C_{xx^{\prime},x+x^{\prime}}=C_{xx^{\prime},x-x^{\prime}}=C_{xx^{\prime},x^{\prime}-x}=2\,.

(13)

We must now compute the OPE coefficients involving
O_{h}
. First

\displaystyle\begin{split}O_{x}(\tau)O_{h}(0)&=\sum_{x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}=1}^{L}\sum_{a,b}S_{x^{\prime}}^{a}(\tau)S_{x^{\prime}+x}^{a}(\tau)S_{x^{\prime\prime}}^{b}(0)\\
&=\sum_{x^{\prime}=1}^{L}\sum_{a,b}\Big[S_{x^{\prime}}^{a}(\tau)S_{x^{\prime}}^{b}(0)\Big]S_{x^{\prime}+x}^{a}(\tau)+\sum_{x^{\prime}=1}^{L}\sum_{a,b}\Big[S_{x^{\prime}+x}^{a}(\tau)S_{x^{\prime}+x}^{b}(0)\Big]S_{x^{\prime}}^{a}(\tau)+...\\
&=\sum_{x^{\prime}=1}^{L}\sum_{a,b}\Bigg[\frac{\delta^{ab}}{|\tau|^{2\Delta_{S}^{0}}}\mathds{1}_{x^{\prime}}+iC_{SSS}\text{sgn}(\tau)\frac{f^{abc}}{|\tau|^{\Delta_{S}^{0}}}S_{x^{\prime}}^{c}(0)\Bigg]S_{x^{\prime}+x}^{a}(\tau)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\sum_{x^{\prime}=1}^{L}\sum_{a,b}\Bigg[\frac{\delta^{ab}}{|\tau|^{2\Delta_{S}^{0}}}\mathds{1}_{x^{\prime}+x}+iC_{SSS}\text{sgn}(\tau)\frac{f^{abc}}{|\tau|^{\Delta_{S}^{0}}}S_{x^{\prime}+x}^{c}(0)\Bigg]S_{x^{\prime}}^{a}(\tau)+...\\
&=\frac{2}{|\tau|^{2\Delta_{S}^{0}}}O_{h}(0)+...\,,\end{split}

(14)

where the two terms with
C_{SSS}
cancel each other. From this, we identify
C_{xhh}=2
. By proceeding similarly, one can easily show that the
O_{h}
-
O_{h}
OPE does not give anything interesting.

A.2.2
RG equations

We are now in a position to compute the RG equations, using Eq.
7
and the OPE coefficients previously computed. First, we get for
J_{x}

\displaystyle\begin{split}\beta(J_{x})&=-\epsilon J_{x}-\sum_{x^{\prime}=1}^{\frac{L-1}{2}}C_{x^{\prime}x^{\prime},x}J_{x^{\prime}}^{2}-\sum_{x^{\prime}\neq x^{\prime\prime}=1}^{\frac{L-1}{2}}C_{x^{\prime}x^{\prime\prime},x}J_{x^{\prime}}J_{x^{\prime\prime}}+...\,,\end{split}

(15)

where the terms with
x^{\prime}=x^{\prime\prime}
and
x^{\prime}\neq x^{\prime\prime}
have been separated. Using our results from above, the OPE coefficient in the second term is

\displaystyle\begin{split}C_{x^{\prime}x^{\prime},x}&=\delta(x^{\prime}-x)C_{xx,x}+\Big[\delta(2x^{\prime}-x)+\delta(2x^{\prime}-(L-x))\Big]C_{xx,2x}\\
&=-NC_{SSS}^{2}\delta(x^{\prime}-x)+2\Big[\delta(2x^{\prime}-x)+\delta(2x^{\prime}-(L-x))\Big]\,,\end{split}

(16)

where the second term contains two
\delta
functions to account for the fact that
O_{L-x}=O_{x}
. For the OPE coefficient of the third term, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}C_{x^{\prime}x^{\prime\prime},x}&=\Big[\delta(x-x^{\prime}-x^{\prime\prime})+\delta(L-x-x^{\prime}-x^{\prime\prime})\Big]C_{x^{\prime}x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime}+x^{\prime\prime}}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\Big[\delta(x-x^{\prime}+x^{\prime\prime})+\delta(x+x^{\prime}-x^{\prime\prime})\Big]C_{x^{\prime}x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime}-x^{\prime\prime}}\\
&=2\Big[\delta(x-x^{\prime}-x^{\prime\prime})+\delta(L-x-x^{\prime}-x^{\prime\prime})+\delta(x-x^{\prime}+x^{\prime\prime})+\delta(x+x^{\prime}-x^{\prime\prime})\Big]\,,\end{split}

(17)

where this expression accounts for the symmetries
O_{L-x}=O_{x}
and
O_{-x}=O_{x}
. Therefore, the RG equation for
J_{x}
is

\displaystyle\begin{split}\beta(J_{x})&=-\epsilon J_{x}+NC_{SSS}^{2}J_{x}^{2}-2\sum_{x^{\prime}=1}^{\frac{L-1}{2}}\Big[\delta(2x^{\prime}-x)+\delta(2x^{\prime}-(L-x))\Big]J_{x^{\prime}}^{2}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt-2\sum_{x^{\prime}\neq x^{\prime\prime}=1}^{\frac{L-1}{2}}\Big[\delta(x-x^{\prime}-x^{\prime\prime})+\delta(L-x-x^{\prime}-x^{\prime\prime})+\delta(x-x^{\prime}+x^{\prime\prime})+\delta(x+x^{\prime}-x^{\prime\prime})\Big]J_{x^{\prime}}J_{x^{\prime\prime}}\,.\end{split}

(18)

The last term can be rewritten in a simpler form by restricting the sum in the last term to
x^{\prime}<x^{\prime\prime}
, yielding

\displaystyle\begin{split}\beta(J_{x})&=-\epsilon J_{x}+NC_{SSS}^{2}J_{x}^{2}-2\sum_{x^{\prime}=1}^{\frac{L-1}{2}}\Big[\delta(2x^{\prime}-x)+\delta(2x^{\prime}-(L-x))\Big]J_{x^{\prime}}^{2}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt-4\sum_{x^{\prime}=1<x^{\prime\prime}}^{\frac{L-1}{2}}\Big[\delta(x-x^{\prime}-x^{\prime\prime})+\delta(L-x-x^{\prime}-x^{\prime\prime})+\delta(x+x^{\prime}-x^{\prime\prime})\Big]J_{x^{\prime}}J_{x^{\prime\prime}}\,.\end{split}

(19)

The RG equation for
h
, following from Eqs.
7
and
14
, is on the other hand

\beta(h)=(1-\Delta_{S}^{0})h-4\sum_{x^{\prime}=1}^{\frac{L-1}{2}}J_{x^{\prime}}h\,.

(20)

From this, we get the RG eigenvalue
e_{h}=\frac{\partial\beta(h)}{\partial h}
, which is itself related to the impurity (corrected) scaling dimension
\Delta_{S}=D-e_{h}=1-e_{h}
, which thus yields

\Delta_{S}=\Delta_{S}^{0}+4\sum_{x^{\prime}=1}^{\frac{L-1}{2}}J^{*}_{x^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{1+\gamma}+4\sum_{x^{\prime}=1}^{\frac{L-1}{2}}J^{*}_{x^{\prime}}

(21)

where
J^{*}_{x}
denotes the fixed-point value of the couplings.

A.2.3
RG equations for
L
even

Let us now consider the case where
L
is even. We still work with the perturbing operators defined in Eq.
5
, where this time
x=[1,\frac{L}{2}-1]
. However, a subtlety arises due to the operator
O_{L/2}
, which must be treated separately, since it maps to itself under the transformation
x\rightarrow L-x
. Repeating analogous steps as for
L
odd, one can show that the RG equations are

\displaystyle\begin{split}\beta(J_{x})&=-\epsilon J_{x}+NC_{SSS}^{2}J_{x}^{2}-2\sum_{x^{\prime}=1}^{\frac{L}{2}-1}\Big[\delta(2x^{\prime}-x)+\delta(2x^{\prime}+x-L)\Big]J_{x^{\prime}}^{2}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt-4\sum_{x^{\prime}=1<x^{\prime\prime}}^{\frac{L}{2}-1}\Big[\delta(x-x^{\prime}-x^{\prime\prime})+\delta(L-x-x^{\prime}-x^{\prime\prime})+\delta(x+x^{\prime}-x^{\prime\prime})\Big]J_{x^{\prime}}J_{x^{\prime\prime}}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt-8\sum_{x^{\prime}=1}^{\frac{L}{2}-1}\delta(L/2-x^{\prime}-x)J_{x^{\prime}}J_{L/2}\,,\end{split}

(22)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\beta(J_{L/2})=-\epsilon J_{L/2}-2\sum_{x^{\prime}=1}^{\frac{L}{2}-1}\delta(x^{\prime}-L/4)J_{x^{\prime}}^{2}-4\sum_{x^{\prime}=1<x^{\prime\prime}}^{\frac{L}{2}-1}\delta(L/2-x^{\prime}-x^{\prime\prime})J_{x^{\prime}}J_{x^{\prime\prime}}+2NC_{SSS}^{2}J_{L/2}^{2}\,,\end{split}

(23)

\beta(h)=(1-\Delta_{S}^{0})h-4\sum_{x^{\prime}=1}^{\frac{L}{2}-1}J_{x^{\prime}}h-4J_{L/2}h\,.

(24)

The scaling dimension of the impurity is thus

\Delta_{S}=\Delta_{S}^{0}+4\sum_{x^{\prime}=1}^{\frac{L}{2}-1}J_{x^{\prime}}^{*}+4J_{L/2}^{*}=\frac{1}{1+\gamma}+4\sum_{x^{\prime}=1}^{\frac{L}{2}-1}J_{x^{\prime}}^{*}+4J_{L/2}^{*}\,.

(25)

A.2.4
OPE coefficient
C_{SSS}
at large
N
and
K
in the bCFT

The OPE coefficient
C_{SSS}
can easily be computed in the limit
N,K\rightarrow\infty
, with
\frac{K}{N}=\gamma
fixed. To do so, the impurity is written as
S^{a}(\tau)=f_{\alpha}^{{\dagger}}(\tau)T_{\alpha\beta}^{a}f_{\beta}(\tau)
and we use the result of Ref.
Parcollet et al. 1998
for the spinon imaginary-time Green’s function
G_{f}(\tau)
, defined as

\delta_{\alpha\beta}G_{f}(\tau)=-\langle T_{\tau}\hat{f}_{\alpha}(\tau)\hat{f}_{\beta}^{{\dagger}}(0)\rangle=\delta_{\alpha\beta}\text{sgn}(\tau)\frac{A_{f}}{|\tau|^{2\Delta_{f}}}\,.

(26)

The real coefficient
A_{f}
is obtained by computing the impurity 2-point function and demanding that
C_{SS\mathds{1}}=1
. In the large
N,K
limit, the action is quadratic in the spinons
f_{\alpha}

Parcollet et al. 1998
and Wick’s theorem can thus be used. Therefore

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle S^{a}(\tau)S^{b}(0)\rangle=-\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}[T^{a}T^{b}]G_{f}(-\tau)G_{f}(\tau)=\frac{A_{f}^{2}}{2}\frac{\delta^{ab}}{|\tau|^{2\Delta_{S}^{0}}}\,,\end{split}

(27)

where we have used the generator normalization condition
\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}[T^{a}T^{b}]=\frac{1}{2}\delta^{ab}
. Hence, it follows that
A_{f}=\sqrt{2}
. The OPE coefficient
C_{SSS}
is now obtained by multiplying Eq.
4
by
S^{c}(\tau^{\prime})
on the right side and by taking the expectation value in the bCFT, resulting in

\langle S^{a}(\tau)S^{b}(0)S^{c}(\tau^{\prime})\rangle=iC_{SSS}\text{sgn}(\tau)\frac{f^{abc}}{|\tau|^{\Delta_{S}^{0}}|\tau^{\prime}|^{2\Delta_{S}^{0}}}\,,

(28)

where
|\tau^{\prime}|\gg|\tau|
. Computing the impurity 3-point function yields two Wick contractions and results in

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle S^{a}(\tau)S^{b}(0)S^{c}(\tau^{\prime})\rangle&=\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}[T^{b}T^{a}T^{c}]G_{f}(-\tau)G_{f}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})G_{f}(\tau^{\prime})+\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}[T^{a}T^{b}T^{c}]G_{f}(\tau)G_{f}(\tau^{\prime}-\tau)G_{f}(-\tau^{\prime})\\
&=\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\Big([T^{a},T^{b}]T^{c}\Big)G_{f}(\tau)G_{f}(\tau^{\prime})G_{f}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\\
&=\frac{i}{2}f^{abc}2^{3/2}\frac{\text{sgn}(\tau)\text{sgn}(\tau^{\prime})\text{sgn}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})}{|\tau|^{\Delta_{S}^{0}}|\tau^{\prime}|^{\Delta_{S}^{0}}|\tau-\tau^{\prime}|^{\Delta_{S}^{0}}}\\
&\approx-\sqrt{2}if^{abc}\frac{\text{sgn}(\tau)}{|\tau|^{\Delta_{S}^{0}}|\tau^{\prime}|^{2\Delta_{S}^{0}}}\,,\end{split}

(29)

where we have used the antisymmetry of
G_{f}
under
\tau\rightarrow-\tau
, the SU
(N)
algebra
[T^{a},T^{b}]=if^{abc}T^{c}
, the generator normalization condition and the fact that
|\tau^{\prime}|\gg|\tau|
. Hence, by comparing with the right side of Eq.
28
, we see that

C_{SSS}=-\sqrt{2}+\mathcal{O}(1/N)\,.

(30)

Appendix B
Details of the Monte Carlo simulations

B.1
Formulation and Wilson discretization of the bath

We consider a lattice of SU(
N
)
K
-channel Kondo models in the totally analytically-symmetric representation corresponding to a Young tableau with
N/2
lines and a single column.
To each lattice site,
x
, we attach
K
SU(
N
) channels that will screen an SU(
N
) spin degree of freedom. For the fermionic baths we introduce the creation operator

\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{i\alpha}(x,k)
where
i=1\cdots K
is the channel index,
\alpha=1\cdots N
is the spin index,
k=1\cdots L_{b}
is a one-dimensional momentum, and
x=1\cdots L
labels the unit cell. Our aim is to formulate an
efficient auxiliary field quantum Monte Carlo formulation of the Hamiltonian:

\hat{H}=\hat{H}_{0}+\hat{H}_{J_{K}}+\hat{H}_{J_{H}}

(31)

with

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{L_{b}}\sum_{x=1}^{L}\epsilon(k)\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{i\alpha}(x,k)\hat{c}_{i\alpha}(x,k)

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{J_{K}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2J_{K}}{N}\sum_{x=1}^{L}\sum_{a=1}^{N^{2}-1}\sum_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}=1}^{N}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{i\alpha}(x,y=0)T^{a}_{\alpha\beta}\hat{c}_{i\beta}(x,y=0)\,\,\hat{S}^{a}(x)\,\,\,\text{and}

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{J_{H}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2J_{H}}{N}\sum_{x=1}^{L}\sum_{a=1}^{N^{2}-1}\hat{S}^{a}(x)\hat{S}^{a}(x+1).

(32)

In the above,
T^{a}
are generators of SU(
N
) satisfying the normalization condition:

\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}(T^{a}T^{b})=\frac{1}{2}\delta_{ab}.

(33)

To formulate the quantum Monte Carlo algorithm, we will adopt a fermionic representation of the SU(
N
) spin degree of freedom,

\hat{S}^{a}(x)=\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{N}\hat{f}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}(x)T^{a}_{\alpha\beta}\hat{f}_{\beta}(x)

(34)

with the constraint:

\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}\hat{f}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}(x)\hat{f}_{\alpha}(x)=\frac{N}{2}.

(35)

We impose the constraint energetically thereby adding a Hubbard-
U
term to the Hamiltonian:

\hat{H}_{QMC}=\hat{H}_{0}+\hat{H}_{J_{K}}+\hat{H}_{J_{H}}+\hat{H}_{U}

(36)

such that in the large-
U
limit
\left.\hat{H}_{QMC}\right|_{U=\infty}=\hat{H}
.

We proceed by describing how we implement each term in the ALF-formulation of the projective auxiliary field
quantum Monte Carlo algorithm.
For the Wilson discretization of the bath, we rewrite the kinetic energy term as:

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{L_{b}}\sum_{x=1}^{L}\epsilon(k)\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{i\alpha}(x,k)\hat{c}_{i\alpha}(x,k)

(37)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}\sum_{k>0}\sum_{x=1}^{L}\epsilon(k)\left(\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{i\alpha}(x,k)\hat{c}_{i\alpha}(x,k)+\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{i\alpha}(x,-k)\hat{c}_{i\alpha}(x,-k)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}\sum_{k>0}\sum_{x=1}^{L}\epsilon(k)\left(\hat{\gamma}^{\dagger}_{i\alpha}(x,k)\hat{\gamma}_{i\alpha}(x,k)+\hat{\eta}^{\dagger}_{i\alpha}(x,k)\hat{\eta}_{i\alpha}(x,k)\right)

with

\displaystyle\hat{\gamma}^{\dagger}_{i\alpha}(x,k)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{i\alpha}(x,k)+\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{i\alpha}(x,-k)\right)

\displaystyle\hat{\eta}^{\dagger}_{i\alpha}(x,k)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{i\alpha}(x,k)-\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{i\alpha}(x,-k)\right).

(38)

In the above, we have assumed that
\epsilon(k)=\epsilon(-k)
.

We can now replace the sum over momenta by:

\frac{L_{b}}{2}\equiv\sum_{k>0}=\int d\epsilon g(\epsilon)\text{ with }g(\epsilon)=\sum_{k>0}\delta(\epsilon-\epsilon(k))

(39)

and choose:

g(\epsilon)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{L_{b}}{2W}&\text{ if }|\epsilon|<W/2\\
0&\text{ otherwise. }\end{array}\right.

(40)

Choosing an arbitrary discretization,
\int d\epsilon g(\epsilon)f(\epsilon)=\sum_{n=1}^{L_{b}}\Delta\epsilon_{n}g(\epsilon_{n})f(\epsilon_{n})
, the non-interacting part reads:

\hat{H}_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}\sum_{n=1}^{L_{b}}\sum_{x=1}^{L}\Delta\epsilon_{n}\epsilon_{n}g(\epsilon_{n})\left(\hat{\gamma}^{\dagger}_{i\alpha}(x,n)\hat{\gamma}_{i\alpha}(x,n)+\hat{\eta}^{\dagger}_{i\alpha}(x,n)\hat{\eta}_{i\alpha}(x,n)\right).

(41)

Here
L_{b}
corresponds to the number of energy values
\epsilon_{n}
. As mentioned previously, the energy discretization of the bath is arbitrary.
Here, we have opted for a logarithmic Wilson-like discretization defined by:

\epsilon_{n}=\begin{cases}-\frac{W}{2}\Lambda^{1-n}&\text{ for }n=1\cdots L_{b}/2\\
+\frac{W}{2}\Lambda^{n-{L_{b}}}&\text{ for }n=L_{b}/2+1\cdots L_{b}.\end{cases}

(42)

In the simulations we have typically used
\Lambda=1.5
and
L_{b}=30
chosen by benchmarking our data against the single impurity case, see Sec.
B.2
.

We now discuss the Kondo term. Consider the hybridization:

\hat{V}^{\dagger}_{i}(x)=\sum_{\alpha}\hat{f}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}(x)\underbrace{\frac{1}{\sqrt{L_{b}}}\sum_{k=1}^{L_{b}}\hat{c}^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{i\alpha}(x,k)}_{\hat{c}_{i\alpha}(x,y=0)}.

(43)

The Kondo interaction then reads:

\hat{H}_{J_{K}}=-\frac{J_{K}}{2N}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sum_{x=1}^{L}\left(\hat{V}^{\dagger}_{i}(x)\hat{V}^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{i}(x)+\hat{V}^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{i}(x)\hat{V}^{\dagger}_{i}(x)\right)\equiv-\frac{J_{K}}{4N}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sum_{x=1}^{L}\left((\hat{V}^{\dagger}_{i}(x)+\hat{V}^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{i}(x))^{2}+(i\hat{V}^{\dagger}_{i}(x)-i\hat{V}^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{i}(x))^{2}\right)

(44)

with

\displaystyle\hat{V}^{\dagger}_{i}(x)+\hat{V}^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{i}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{L_{b}}}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{L_{b}}\left(\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{i\alpha}(x,k)\hat{f}_{\alpha}(x)+\hat{f}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}(x)\hat{c}_{i\alpha}(x,k)\right)

(45)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{L_{b}}}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}\sum_{k>0}\left(\left[\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{i\alpha}(x,k)+\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{i\alpha}(x,-k)\right]\hat{f}_{\alpha}(x)+\hat{f}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}(x)\left[\hat{c}_{i\alpha}(x,k)+\hat{c}_{i\alpha}(x,-k)\right]\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{L_{b}}}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}\sum_{k>0}\left(\hat{\gamma}^{\dagger}_{i\alpha}(x,k)\hat{f}_{\alpha}(x)+\hat{f}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}(x)\hat{\gamma}_{i\alpha}(x,k)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{2}{L_{b}}}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}\sum_{n=1}^{L_{b}}\Delta\epsilon_{n}g(\epsilon_{n})\left(\hat{\gamma}^{\dagger}_{i\alpha}(x,n)\hat{f}_{\alpha}(x)+\hat{f}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}(x)\hat{\gamma}_{i\alpha}(x,n)\right)

and

\displaystyle i\hat{V}^{\dagger}_{i}(x)-i\hat{V}^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{i}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{2}{L_{b}}}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}\sum_{n=1}^{L_{b}}\Delta\epsilon_{n}g(\epsilon_{n})\left(i\hat{\gamma}^{\dagger}_{i\alpha}(x,n)\hat{f}_{\alpha}(x)-i\hat{f}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}(x)\hat{\gamma}_{i\alpha}(x,n)\right).

(46)

As one can see,
\hat{\eta}^{\dagger}_{i\alpha}(x,k)
does not couple to the spin degree of freedom.
To impose the constraint

\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}\hat{f}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}(x)\hat{f}_{\alpha}(x)=\frac{N}{2},

(47)

we include a Hubbard interaction

\hat{H}_{U}=\frac{U}{N}\sum_{x=1}^{L}\left(\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}\left(\hat{f}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}(x)\hat{f}_{\alpha}(x)-\frac{1}{2}\right)\right)^{2}.

(48)

Importantly

\left[\hat{H}_{U},\hat{H}\right]=0

(49)

such that the constraint is imposed very efficiently.
The last element of the Hamiltonian is the Heisenberg interaction between the SU(
N
) spin 1/2 degrees of freedom:

\hat{H}_{J_{H}}=-\frac{J_{H}}{2N}\sum_{x=1}^{L}\left(\hat{D}^{\dagger}_{x,x+1}\hat{D}^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{x,x+1}+\hat{D}^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{x,x+1}\hat{D}^{\dagger}_{x,x+1}\right)\equiv-\frac{J_{H}}{4N}\sum_{x=1}^{L}\left((\hat{D}^{\dagger}_{x,x+1}+\hat{D}^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{x,x+1})^{2}+(i\hat{D}^{\dagger}_{x,x+1}-i\hat{D}^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{x,x+1})^{2}\right)

(50)

where

\hat{D}^{\dagger}_{x,x+1}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}\hat{f}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}(x)\hat{f}_{\alpha}(x+1).

(51)

All interaction terms are in the form of a perfect square such that the model can readily be implemented using the Algorithm for Lattice Fermion (ALF)
Assaad et al. 2022
library. Here we use the Hubbard-Stratonovitch transformation

e^{\Delta\tau\alpha\hat{A}^{2}}\propto\int d\Phi e^{-\Delta\tau\left(\frac{\Phi^{2}}{4|\alpha|}+\Phi\sqrt{\text{sign}(\alpha)}\hat{A}\right)}

(52)

for
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
\Delta\tau
an imaginary time time step. Note that in the ALF implementation we use the Gauss–Hermite quadrature to replace the continuous variable by a discrete one. The imaginary time propagator then reads:

\displaystyle e^{-\beta\hat{H}}

\displaystyle\propto\int D\left\{\chi_{x,x+1}(\tau)\right\}D\left\{V_{i,x}(\tau)\right\}D\left\{\Phi_{x}(\tau)\right\}

(53)

\displaystyle e^{-N\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau\left(\sum_{x}\frac{|\chi_{x,x+1}(\tau)|^{2}}{J_{H}}+\sum_{x,i}\frac{|V_{i,x}(\tau)|^{2}}{J_{K}}+\sum_{x}\frac{|\Phi_{x}(\tau)|^{2}}{4U}\right)}\prod_{\alpha=1}^{N}{\cal T}e^{-\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau\hat{h}_{\alpha}(\chi,V,\Phi)}.

In the above we have introduced a complex bond fields,
\chi_{x,x+1}(\tau)
and
V_{i,x}(\tau)
to decouple the Heisenberg term and Kondo terms respectively and the Hubbard interaction is decoupled using a real field
\Phi_{x}(\tau)
.
{\cal T}
denotes the time ordering operator, and the single particle Hamiltonian reads:

\displaystyle\hat{h}_{\alpha}(\chi,V,\Phi)=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sum_{n=1}^{L_{b}}\sum_{x=1}^{L}\Delta\epsilon_{n}\epsilon_{n}g(\epsilon_{n})\hat{\gamma}^{\dagger}_{i\alpha}(x,n)\hat{\gamma}_{i\alpha}(x,n)+\sum_{x}\left(\chi_{x,x+1}(\tau)\hat{f}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}(x)\hat{f}_{\alpha}(x+1)+\text{H.c.}\right)

(54)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sum_{x=1}^{L}\left(V_{i,x}(\tau)\sqrt{\frac{2}{L_{b}}}\sum_{n=1}^{L_{b}}\Delta\epsilon_{n}g(\epsilon_{n})\hat{\gamma}^{\dagger}_{i\alpha}(x,n)\hat{f}_{\alpha}(x)+\text{H.c.}\right)+\sum_{x=1}^{L}i\Phi_{x}(\tau)\left(\hat{f}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}(x)\hat{f}_{\alpha}(x)-\frac{1}{2}\right).

We have used the projective formulation of the auxiliary field quantum Monte Carlo algorithm
Sorella et al. 1989
;
Sugiyama and Koonin 1986
;
Assaad and Evertz 2008
:

\frac{\langle\Psi_{0}|\hat{O}|\Psi_{0}\rangle}{\langle\Psi_{0}|\Psi_{0}\rangle}=\lim_{\theta\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\langle\Psi_{T}|e^{-\theta\hat{H}}\hat{O}e^{-\theta\hat{H}}|\Psi_{T}\rangle}{\langle\Psi_{T}|e^{-2\theta\hat{H}}|\Psi_{T}\rangle}.

(55)

The above holds under the condition that the trial wave function
|\Psi_{T}\rangle
is not orthogonal to the ground state. The trial wave function is chosen to be a single Slater determinant and to respect symmetries of the model. As we will detail below, this includes channel and spin symmetry as well as U(1) charge. We generate the trial wave function by solving the single particle Hamiltonian of Eq.
54
for a space-time constant value of
\chi_{x,x+1}(\tau)
and vanishing values of
V_{i,x}(\tau)
and
\Phi_{x}(\tau)
. Note that setting
V_{i,x}(\tau)
to zero stems from the requirement of satisfying channel symmetry. Furthermore, for
\Phi_{x}(\tau)=0
particle-hole symmetry is present.
The trial wave function hence takes the form:

|\Psi_{T}\rangle=\otimes_{\alpha=1}^{N}|\Psi_{T},\alpha\rangle.

(56)

In the Monte Carlo simulation we sample configurations according to the
weight
:

W(\chi,V,\Phi)=e^{-N\left[\int_{0}^{2\theta}d\tau\left(\sum_{x}\frac{|\chi_{x,x+1}(\tau)|^{2}}{J_{H}}+\sum_{x,n}\frac{|V_{x,n}(\tau)|^{2}}{J_{K}}+\sum_{x}\frac{|\Phi_{x}(\tau)|^{2}}{4U}\right)-\log\langle\Psi_{T},1|{\cal T}e^{-\int_{0}^{2\theta}d\tau\hat{h}_{1}(\chi,V,\Phi)}|\Psi_{T},1\rangle\right]}.

(57)

Since the trial wave function as well as the Hubbard-Stratonovitch transformation do not break the SU(
N
) spin symmetry,
N
becomes a multiplicative factor in front of the action.
Using the particle-hole transformation:

\hat{\gamma}^{\dagger}_{i1}(x,n)\rightarrow(-1)^{x}\hat{\gamma}^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{i1}(x,\overline{n})

(58)

and

\hat{f}^{\dagger}_{1}(x)\rightarrow(-1)^{x}\hat{f}^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{1}(x)

(59)

with
\epsilon_{\overline{n}}=-\epsilon_{n}
and
\Delta\epsilon_{\overline{n}}=\Delta\epsilon_{n}
, we can show that

\langle\Psi_{T},1|{\cal T}e^{-\int_{0}^{2\theta}d\tau\hat{h}_{1}(\chi,V,\Phi)}|\Psi_{T},1\rangle
is real. Hence,

W(\chi,V,\Phi)
is positive for even values of
N
.

Symmetries and observables

B.3.1
Channel Symmetry

Let
T^{m}
be the
m:1\cdots K^{2}-1
generators of SU(
K
) with normalization

\text{Tr}\,T^{m}T^{m^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{2}\delta_{m,m^{\prime}}

(60)

and

\hat{T}(\bm{e},\theta)=\exp\left[i\sum_{i,i^{\prime},\alpha,k,x}\hat{\gamma}^{\dagger}_{i\alpha}(x,k)\left(\theta\bm{e}\cdot\bm{T}\right)_{ii^{\prime}}\hat{\gamma}_{i^{\prime}\alpha}(x,k)\right].

(61)

Thereby,

\hat{T}^{\dagger}(\bm{e},\theta)\hat{\gamma}^{\dagger}_{i\alpha}(x,k)\hat{T}^{\phantom{\dagger}}(\bm{e},\theta)=\sum_{i^{\prime}}\left[e^{i\theta\bm{e}\cdot\bm{T}}\right]_{ii^{\prime}}\hat{\gamma}^{\dagger}_{i^{\prime}\alpha}(x,k).

(62)

Clearly,
\left[\hat{H},\hat{T}(\bm{e},\theta)\right]=0
such that the Hamiltonian is invariant under an SU(
K
) channel rotations.
Since the
f
-fermion does not carry a channel index, the hybridization
\hat{V}_{i,x}
transforms as
\hat{\gamma}^{\dagger}_{i\alpha}(x,k)
under an SU(
K
) channel rotation. To probe for channel symmetry breaking, we consider:

\hat{O}^{m}(x)=\sum_{i,i^{\prime}}\hat{V}^{\dagger}_{i}(x)T^{m}_{ii^{\prime}}\hat{V}^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{i^{\prime}}(x).

(63)

Under a channel rotation
\hat{O}^{m}(x)\rightarrow\sum_{m^{\prime}}R_{mm^{\prime}}\hat{O}^{m^{\prime}}(x)
with
R an SO(
K^{2}-1
) matrix. Hence, a non-vanishing expectation value
\langle\hat{O}^{m}(x)\rangle
implies
channel symmetry breaking. In the code, we compute:

K(q)=\sum_{x}\sum_{m}e^{iqx}\langle\hat{O}^{m}(x)\hat{O}^{m}(0)\rangle

(64)

and check for long ranged order. Note that the background vanishes by symmetry. Fig.
7
plots
K(q)
in the VBS phase at
J_{H}/J_{K}=0.8
as a function of
L
. As apparent
the results are consistent with vanishing real space correlations. Hence, the channel symmetry is not broken.

B.3.2
SU(
N
) spin symmetry

The generator of global SU(
N
) spin rotations is noting but the total spin:

\hat{S}^{a}=\sum_{i,\alpha,\alpha^{\prime},k,x}\hat{\gamma}^{\dagger}_{i\alpha}(x,k)T^{a}_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}\hat{\gamma}^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{i\alpha^{\prime}}(x,k)+\sum_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime},x}\hat{f}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}(x)T^{a}_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}\hat{f}^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{\alpha^{\prime}}(x).

(65)

In the above
T^{a}
are the generators of SU(
N
) with normalization following that of Eq.
60
.
One will readily check that the Hamiltonian commutes with each component of the spin operator. To check for spin symmetry breaking, we consider spin-spin correlation functions:

S(x)=\sum_{a}\langle\hat{S}^{a}_{f}(x)\hat{S}^{a}_{f}(x=0)\rangle

(66)

with
\hat{S}^{a}_{f}(x)=\sum_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime},x}\hat{f}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}(x)T^{a}_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}\hat{f}^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{\alpha^{\prime}}(x)
.

To detect translation symmetry breaking, we have computed dimer-dimer correlation functions given by:

D(x-x^{\prime})=\langle\bm{\hat{S}}_{f,x}\cdot\bm{\hat{S}}_{f,x+1}\bm{\hat{S}}_{f,x^{\prime}}\cdot\bm{\hat{S}}_{f,x^{\prime}+1}\rangle-\langle\bm{\hat{S}}_{f,x}\cdot\bm{\hat{S}}_{f,x+1}\rangle\langle\bm{\hat{S}}_{f,x^{\prime}}\cdot\bm{\hat{S}}_{f,x^{\prime}+1}\rangle.

(67)

B.3.3
Bond inversion

Under bond inversion,
\hat{V}_{n,x}
maps onto
\hat{V}_{n,-x}
. Charge density waves break bond inversion symmetry whereas a valence bond solid does not.
To check for bond inversion symmetry breaking, we hence consider the quantity:

\hat{O}_{x}=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\hat{V}^{\dagger}_{i}(x)\hat{V}^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{i}(x)

(68)

and compute the correlation function

B_{I}(q)=\sum_{x}e^{iqx}\left(\langle\hat{O}_{x}\hat{O}_{x^{\prime}}\rangle-\langle\hat{O}_{x}\rangle\langle\hat{O}_{x^{\prime}}\rangle\right).

(69)

In all our simulations, we have never detected bond inversion symmetry breaking, see Fig.
8
.

G_{c}(k)

Appendix C
Mean-field for the VBS phase

C.1
Spin-spin correlations using low-energy effective action

In a standalone VBS phase, the ground state lives in the spin-singlet subspace, while the spinful excitations are gapped. The low-energy action for the spinful excitations is therefore of the form
S_{0}=\int d\omega dk\,\,|\vec{\phi}|^{2}(\omega^{2}+k^{2}+m^{2})
, where
m
is the mass to the spinful excitations. In the presence of Kondo coupling to the electronic bath, however, the spinful excitations are Landau-damped, leading to
S=\int d\omega dk\,\,|\vec{\phi}|^{2}(k^{2}+m^{2}+\gamma|\omega|+...)
, with
\gamma\propto J^{2}_{K}
and where
J_{K}
is the Kondo coupling. The ellipsis denotes more irrelevant terms. The unequal-time, equal-space spin-spin correlator
S(x=0,\tau)
is therefore proportional to

S(x=0,\tau)\propto\int\frac{dkd\omega\,e^{i\omega\tau}}{k^{2}+m^{2}+\gamma|\omega|}\sim\int\frac{d\omega\,e^{i\omega\tau}}{\sqrt{m^{2}+\gamma|\omega|}}\sim\int\frac{d\omega\,e^{i\omega\tau}\gamma|\omega|}{m^{3}}\sim\frac{\gamma}{m^{3}\tau^{2}}\,.

(74)

Note that the power-law arises since the integrand is non-analytic due to the Landau-damping term
\gamma|\omega|
. Similarly, the equal-time, unequal-space correlator is proportional to

S(x,\tau=0)\propto\int\frac{dkd\omega\,e^{ikx}}{k^{2}+m^{2}+\gamma|\omega|}\sim\int dke^{ikx}\log(1+\frac{\Omega}{k^{2}+m^{2}})\sim\frac{e^{-m|x|}-e^{-\sqrt{m^{2}+\Omega^{2}}}}{|x|}\sim\frac{e^{-m|x|}}{|x|}\,,

(75)

where
\Omega
is the UV cutoff for the frequency integral. The integral decays exponentially with
|x|
as expected, since the integrand is an analytic function of
k
.

C.2
Large-
N
slave-parton mean-field

We will here provide a mean-field description of the VBS phase, where the mean-field parameters are taken to be homogeneous and time-independent. We will primarily focus on the spinon Green’s function
G_{f}(x,\tau)\sim\langle T_{\tau}\hat{f}(x,\tau)\hat{f}^{\dagger}(0,0)\rangle
, which, at the mean-field level, allows one to calculate the spin-spin correlations
S(x,\tau)=\langle\hat{S}^{a}(x,\tau)\hat{S}^{a}(0,0)\rangle\sim\left(G_{f}(x,\tau)\right)^{2}
. The main outcome of this mean-field is that equal-space, unequal-time correlations decay as a power-law,
S(x=0,\tau)\sim 1/\tau^{2}
, while equal-time, unequal-space correlations decay exponentially,
S(x,\tau=0)\sim e^{-x/\xi_{VBS}}
, in line with the aforementioned conclusion based on the low-energy theory.

We start with our Hamiltonian (see below Eq.
3
for more details)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{H}&=\sum_{k}\sum_{x=1}^{L}\epsilon(k)\hat{c}^{{\dagger}}_{i\alpha}(x,k)\hat{c}_{i\alpha}(x,k)+\frac{J_{K}}{N}\sum_{x}\hat{S}^{a}(x)\hat{c}^{{\dagger}}_{i\alpha}(x,y=0)T^{a}_{\alpha\beta}\hat{c}_{i\beta}(x,y=0)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\frac{J_{H}}{N}\sum_{x}\hat{S}^{a}(x)\hat{S}^{a}(x+1)\,,\end{split}

(76)

where the sums over
\alpha
(spin),
i
(channel) and
a
(spin components) are implicit and where the couplings have been rescaled by
1/N
. We will also denote
\hat{c}_{i\alpha}(x)\equiv\hat{c}_{i\alpha}(x,y=0)
for now on. The local moments are written in terms of fermionic spinons
\hat{f}_{\alpha}(x)

\hat{S}^{a}(x)=\hat{f}_{\alpha}^{{\dagger}}(x)T_{\alpha\beta}^{a}\hat{f}_{\beta}(x),\quad\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}\hat{f}_{\alpha}^{{\dagger}}(x)\hat{f}_{\alpha}(x)=\frac{N}{2}\,.

(77)

This results in two quartic terms in the Hamiltonian, which can be decoupled by introducing two Hubbard-Stratonovich fields
\hat{\phi}_{i}^{{\dagger}}(x)\sim\sum_{\alpha}\hat{f}_{\alpha}^{{\dagger}}(x)\hat{c}_{i\alpha}(x)
and
\hat{\chi}^{{\dagger}}(x)\sim\sum_{\alpha}\hat{f}_{\alpha}^{{\dagger}}(x)\hat{f}_{\alpha}(x+1)
. By dropping an unimportant constant and a shift to the conduction electron chemical potential, the Hamiltonian becomes

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{H}&=\sum_{k}\sum_{x}\epsilon(k)\hat{c}^{{\dagger}}_{i\alpha}(x,k)\hat{c}_{i\alpha}(x,k)-\sum_{x}\Big[\hat{\phi}_{i}^{{\dagger}}(x)\hat{c}_{i\alpha}^{{\dagger}}(x)\hat{f}_{\alpha}(x)+\hat{f}_{\alpha}^{{\dagger}}(x)\hat{c}_{i\alpha}(x)\hat{\phi}_{i}(x)\Big]\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt-\sum_{x}\Big[\hat{\chi}^{{\dagger}}(x)\hat{f}_{\alpha}^{{\dagger}}(x+1)\hat{f}_{\alpha}(x)+\hat{f}_{\alpha}^{{\dagger}}(x)\hat{f}_{\alpha}(x+1)\hat{\chi}(x)\Big]\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\frac{N}{J_{K}}\sum_{x}\hat{\phi}_{i}^{{\dagger}}(x)\hat{\phi}_{i}(x)+\frac{N}{J_{H}}\sum_{x}\hat{\chi}^{{\dagger}}(x)\hat{\chi}(x)\,.\end{split}

(78)

The mean-field Hamiltonian is obtained by starting with Eq.
78
and by condensing
\hat{\phi}_{i}(x)=\hat{\phi}_{i}^{{\dagger}}(x)=\phi
to a constant.
\hat{\chi}
is also condensed, but due to dimerization takes the form

\hat{\chi}(x)=\hat{\chi}^{{\dagger}}(x)=\begin{cases}\chi_{A}=\chi_{0}(1-\delta)\text{ for }x\in A\text{ sublattice}\\
\chi_{B}=\chi_{0}(1+\delta)\text{ for }x\in B\text{ sublattice}\end{cases}\,,

(79)

where
A
and
B
sublattices respectively correspond to odd and even sites. Defining the conduction electron and spinon operators on the two sublattices via their Fourier transform in the reduced Brillouin zone,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{c}_{i\alpha}^{A/B}(x^{\prime},y)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{L}}\sum_{k_{x}}e^{2ik_{x}x^{\prime}}\hat{c}_{i\alpha}^{A/B}(k_{x},y)\,,\quad\hat{f}_{\alpha}^{A/B}(x^{\prime})=\sqrt{\frac{2}{L}}\sum_{k_{x}}e^{2ik_{x}x^{\prime}}\hat{f}_{\alpha}^{A/B}(k_{x})\,,\end{split}

(80)

where
x^{\prime}=1,...,L/2
labels the new unit cell and
k_{x}\in]-\pi/2,\pi/2]
, the mean-field Hamiltonian becomes

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{H}&=\sum_{k_{x},k_{y}}\epsilon(k_{y})\Big[\hat{c}_{i\alpha}^{A{\dagger}}(k_{x},k_{y})\hat{c}_{i\alpha}^{A}(k_{x},k_{y})+\hat{c}_{i\alpha}^{B{\dagger}}(k_{x},k_{y})\hat{c}_{i\alpha}^{B}(k_{x},k_{y})\Big]\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt-\phi\sum_{k_{x}}\Big[\hat{f}_{\alpha}^{A{\dagger}}(k_{x})\hat{c}^{A}_{i\alpha}(k_{x})+\hat{f}_{\alpha}^{B{\dagger}}(k_{x})\hat{c}^{B}_{i\alpha}(k_{x})+h.c.\Big]\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt-\sum_{k_{x}}\Big[\Big(\chi_{A}+\chi_{B}e^{-2ik_{x}}\Big)\hat{f}_{\alpha}^{A{\dagger}}(k_{x})\hat{f}_{\alpha}^{B}(k_{x})+h.c.\Big]+\frac{N}{J_{K}}L\phi^{2}+\frac{N}{J_{H}}\frac{L_{y}}{2}\big(\chi_{A}^{2}+\chi_{B}^{2}\big)\,,\end{split}

(81)

where the bath momentum is now denoted
k_{y}
to avoid confusion. We are now in a position to compute the spinon 2-point function, which can be done analytically. We can drop the last two constant terms as they do not affect the correlation function calculation. We start by obtaining the effective action for
f
by integrating out the conduction electrons. To do so, one must work with the imaginary-time action. Transforming to Matsubara frequencies, one finds

\displaystyle\begin{split}S&=\sum_{\vec{k}}\sum_{\omega_{n}}\sum_{i,\alpha}\bar{\psi}_{i\alpha}(\vec{k},i\omega_{n})\begin{pmatrix}-i\omega_{n}+\epsilon(k_{y})&0\\
0&-i\omega_{n}+\epsilon(k_{y})\end{pmatrix}\psi_{i\alpha}(\vec{k},i\omega_{n})\\
&-\frac{\phi}{\sqrt{L_{b}}}\sum_{\vec{k}}\sum_{\omega_{n}}\sum_{i,\alpha}\Bigg[\bar{\psi}_{i\alpha}(\vec{k},i\omega_{n})\eta_{\alpha}(k_{x},i\omega_{n})+\bar{\eta}_{\alpha}(k_{x},i\omega_{n})\psi_{i\alpha}(\vec{k},i\omega_{n})\Bigg]\\
&+\sum_{k_{x}}\sum_{\omega_{n}}\sum_{\alpha}\bar{\eta}_{\alpha}(k_{x},\omega_{n})\begin{pmatrix}-i\omega_{n}&-z(z_{k})\\
-z^{*}(k_{x})&-i\omega_{n}\end{pmatrix}\eta_{\alpha}(k_{x},i\omega_{n})\,,\end{split}

(82)

where
\vec{k}=(k_{x},k_{y})
,
z(k_{x})=\chi_{A}+\chi_{B}e^{-2ik_{x}}
and the two following 2-component spinors have been introduced

\psi_{i\alpha}(\vec{k},i\omega_{n})=\begin{pmatrix}c^{A}_{i\alpha}(\vec{k},i\omega_{n})\\
c^{B}_{i\alpha}(\vec{k},i\omega_{n})\end{pmatrix}\,,\quad\eta_{\alpha}(k_{x},i\omega_{n})=\begin{pmatrix}f^{A}_{\alpha}(k_{x},i\omega_{n})\\
f^{B}_{\alpha}(k_{x},i\omega_{n})\end{pmatrix}\,.

(83)

Note that the local conduction electron operator has also been written in momentum space. We then define the following free Green’s functions

\displaystyle\begin{split}-G_{c,0}^{-1}(k_{y},i\omega_{n})&=\begin{pmatrix}-i\omega_{n}+\epsilon(k_{y})&0\\
0&-i\omega_{n}+\epsilon(k_{y})\end{pmatrix}\\
-G_{f,0}^{-1}(k_{x},i\omega_{n})&=\begin{pmatrix}-i\omega_{n}&-z(z_{k})\\
-z^{*}(k_{x})&-i\omega_{n}\end{pmatrix}\,.\end{split}

(84)

Conduction electrons can now be integrated out leading to the following effective action for
f
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}S_{\text{Eff}}[\eta]=\sum_{k_{x}}\sum_{\omega_{n}}\bar{\eta}_{\alpha}(k_{x},i\omega_{n})\Big[-G_{f,0}^{-1}(k_{x},i\omega_{n})+K\phi^{2}\Big(\frac{1}{L_{b}}\sum_{k_{y}}G_{c,0}(k_{y},i\omega_{n})\Big)\Big]\eta_{\alpha}(k_{x},i\omega_{n})\,.\end{split}

(85)

From this, the (inverse) propagator for
f
is identified

\displaystyle\begin{split}G_{f}^{-1}(k_{x},i\omega_{n})&=-\Bigg[-G_{f,0}^{-1}(k_{x},i\omega_{n})+K\phi^{2}\Big(\frac{1}{L_{b}}\sum_{k_{y}}G_{c,0}(k_{y},i\omega_{n})\Big)\Bigg]\\
&=\begin{pmatrix}i\omega_{n}-\frac{1}{L_{b}}\sum_{k_{y}}\frac{K\phi^{2}}{i\omega_{n}-\epsilon(k_{y})}&z(k_{x})\\
z^{*}(k_{x})&i\omega_{n}-\frac{1}{L_{b}}\sum_{k_{y}}\frac{K\phi^{2}}{i\omega_{n}-\epsilon(k_{y})}\end{pmatrix}\,.\end{split}

(86)

The sum over
k_{y}
is performed using a constant density of states at the Fermi level and a bandwidth
D

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{L_{b}}\sum_{k_{y}}\frac{1}{i\omega_{n}-\epsilon(k_{y})}&\approx d(0)\int_{-D}^{D}d\epsilon\frac{1}{i\omega_{n}-\epsilon}\\
&=d(0)\int_{-D}^{D}d\epsilon\frac{-i\omega_{n}-\epsilon}{\omega_{n}^{2}+\epsilon^{2}}\\
&=-2id(0)\arctan\Big(\frac{D}{\omega_{n}}\Big)\\
&\approx-i\pi d(0)\text{sgn}(\omega_{n})\,,\end{split}

(87)

where the last equality holds for
D\gg\omega_{n}
. Inverting the matrix and defining the constant
C_{K}=\pi d(0)K\phi^{2}
yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}G_{f}(k_{x},i\omega_{n})=\frac{-1}{\big(\omega_{n}+C_{K}\text{sgn}(\omega_{n})\big)^{2}+|z(k_{x})|^{2}\big)}\begin{pmatrix}i\omega_{n}+iC_{K}\text{sgn}(\omega_{n})&-z(k_{x})\\
-z^{*}(k_{x})&i\omega_{n}+iC_{K}\text{sgn}(\omega_{n})\end{pmatrix}\,.\end{split}

(88)

We can now compute the correlation function by Fourier transforming the above expression. We work at
T=0
for now on.

The equal-space, unequal-time spinon correlation function is computed by working on the same sublattice (
AA
here). Hence

\displaystyle\begin{split}G_{f}(x=0,\tau)&=\frac{2}{L}\sum_{k_{x}}\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}e^{-i\omega\tau}[G_{F}(k_{x},i\omega)]_{AA}\\
&\approx-\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\frac{dk_{x}}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}e^{-i\omega\tau}\frac{i\omega+iC_{K}\text{sgn}(\omega)}{(\omega+C_{K}\text{sgn}(\omega))^{2}+|z(k_{x})|^{2}}\\
&=-2\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\frac{dk_{x}}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\sin(\omega\tau)\frac{\omega+C_{K}}{(\omega+C_{K})^{2}+|z(k_{x})|^{2}}\\
&\overset{\tau\to\infty}{\sim}\frac{1}{\tau}\,,\end{split}

(89)

where the last line can be shown by asymptotically performing the integral or computing it numerically. This result holds for any value of
\delta
. Hence, this confirms that in the VBS phase, the equal-space, unequal-time spin-spin correlation function decays as
1/\tau^{2}
(
S(x=0,\tau)\sim 1/\tau^{2}
).

We now move on to the equal-time, unequal-space correlation function. This time, one must take the off-diagonal elements of the matrix (different sublattice,
AB
here). Therefore

\displaystyle\begin{split}G_{f}(x,\tau=0)&=\frac{2}{L}\sum_{k_{x}}e^{ik_{x}x}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}[G_{f}(k_{x},i\omega)]_{AB}\\
&\approx\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\frac{dk_{x}}{2\pi}e^{2ik_{x}x}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\frac{z(k_{x})}{(\omega+C_{K}\text{sgn}(\omega))^{2}+|z(k_{x})|^{2}}\\
&=2\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\frac{dk_{x}}{2\pi}e^{2ik_{x}x}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\frac{z(k_{x})}{(\omega+C_{K})^{2}+|z(k_{x})|^{2}}\\
&=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}dk_{x}e^{2ik_{x}x}\Big(\pi-2\arctan(\frac{C_{K}}{|z(k_{x})|})\Big)\frac{z(k_{x})}{|z(k_{x})|}\,,\end{split}

(90)

where the integral over
\omega
was performed exactly in the last step. The integral over
k_{x}
corresponds to the Fourier transform of an analytic function since
|z(k_{x})|>0
, and therefore
G_{f}(x,\tau=0)\sim e^{-x/\xi}
where
\xi
is non-universal and finite, highlighting the exponential decay of
S(x,\tau=0)
.

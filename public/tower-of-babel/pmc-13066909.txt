Assessment of the feed additive consisting of astaxanthin (2a161j) for fish, crustaceans and ornamental fish for the renewal of its authorisation (BASF SE, DSM Nutritional Products Ltd., JYB Europe BV, NHU EUROPE GmbH)
Authors: EFSA Panel on Additives and Products or Substances used in Animal Feed (FEEDAP), Roberto Edoardo Villa, Giovanna Azimonti, Eleftherios Bonos, Henrik Christensen, Mojca Durjava, Birgit Dusemund, Ronette Gehring, Boet Glandorf, Maryline Kouba, Marta López‐Alonso, Francesca Marcon, Carlo Nebbia, Alena Pechová, Miguel Prieto‐Maradona, Katerina Theodoridou, Gabriele Aquilina, Maria Bastos, Paul Brantom, Jürgen Gropp, Ruud Woutersen, Jaume Galobart, Eleni Gkimprixi, Paola Manini, Piera Valeri, Alberto Navarro Villa, Maria Vittoria Vettori, Fabiola Pizzo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractFollowing a request from the European Commission, EFSA was asked to deliver a scientific opinion on the assessment of the application for the renewal of the authorisation of astaxanthin as a sensory additive for fish, crustaceans and ornamental fish. The applicants provided evidence that the additive currently on the market complies with the existing conditions of authorisation. There is no new evidence that would lead the EFSA Panel on Additives and Products or Substances used in Animal Feed to reconsider its previous conclusions regarding the safety of the additive. Therefore, astaxanthin remains safe for the target species, consumers and environment under the authorised conditions of use. Regarding safety for the user, astaxanthin is not irritant to skin or eyes and is not a dermal sensitiser. The dispersible powder formulation containing 10% astaxanthin is not irritant to skin and eye but should be considered a dermal and respiratory sensitiser, and exposure of users by respiratory and dermal routes is considered a risk. No conclusions can be drawn on the other preparations containing astaxanthin. There is no need for assessing the efficacy of the additive in the context of the renewal of the authorisation.

INTRODUCTION

Background and Terms of Reference

Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003 1 establishes the rules governing the Community authorisation of additives for use in animal nutrition. In particular, Article 14(1) of that Regulation lays down that an application for renewal shall be sent to the Commission at the latest one year before the expiry date of the authorisation.

The European Commission received a request from DSM Nutritional Products Ltd. (represented in the EU by DSM Nutritional products Sp.z.o.o), JYB EUROPE BV, BASF SE and NHU EUROPE GmbH 2 for the renewal of the authorisation of the additive consisting of astaxanthin, when used as a feed additive for fish, crustaceans and ornamental fish (category: sensory additives; functional groups: colourants (ii) substances which, when fed to animals, add colours to food of animal origin, and colourants (iii) substances which favourably affect the colour of ornamental fish or birds).

According to Article 7(1) of Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003, the Commission forwarded the application to the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) as an application under Article 14(1). The dossier was received on 13 November 2024 and the general information and supporting documentation are available at https://open.efsa.europa.eu/questions/EFSA‐Q‐2024‐00661. The particulars and documents in support of the application were considered valid by EFSA as of 27 February 2025.

According to Article 8 of Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003, EFSA, after verifying the particulars and documents submitted by the applicant, shall undertake an assessment in order to determine whether the feed additive complies with the conditions laid down in Article 5. EFSA shall deliver an opinion on the safety for the target animals, consumer, user and the environment and on the efficacy of the feed additive consisting of astaxanthin, when used under the proposed conditions of use (see Section 3.1.3 ).

Additional information

The additive is currently authorised for use in feed for fish, crustaceans and ornamental fish (2a161j). 3

The FEEDAP Panel adopted several opinions on astaxanthin of synthetic and of natural origin (EFSA, 2004, 2005, 2006, 2007a, 2007b; EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2010, 2014a, 2014b, 2019, 2022).

DATA AND METHODOLOGIES

Data

The present assessment is based on data submitted by the applicant in the form of a technical dossier 4 in support of the authorisation request for the use of astaxanthin as a feed additive.

In accordance with Article 38 of the Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 5 and taking into account the protection of confidential information and of personal data in accordance with Articles 39 to 39e of the same Regulation, and of the Decision of EFSA's Executive Director laying down practical arrangements concerning transparency and confidentiality, 6 a non‐confidential version of the dossier has been published on Open.EFSA.

According to Article 32c(2) of Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 and to the Decision of EFSA's Executive Director laying down the practical arrangements on pre‐submission phase and public consultations, EFSA carried out a public consultation on the non‐confidential version of the technical dossier from 05 May to 26 May 2025 for which no comments were received.

The confidential version of the technical dossier was subject to a target consultation of the interested Member States from 04 March 2025 to 04 June 2025; the comments received were considered for the assessment.

The FEEDAP Panel used the data provided by the applicant together with data from other sources, such as previous risk assessments by EFSA or other expert bodies, peer‐reviewed scientific papers, other scientific reports and experts' knowledge, to deliver the present output.

EFSA has verified the European Union Reference Laboratory (EURL) report as it relates to the methods used for the control of the active substance in animal feed.

Methodologies

The approach followed by the FEEDAP Panel to assess the safety and efficacy of astaxanthin is in line with the principles laid down in Regulation (EC) No 429/2008 7 and the relevant Guidance document: Guidance on the renewal of the authorisation of feed additives (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2021). 8

ASSESSMENT

The product consisting of astaxanthin (ATX) is currently authorised for use as a sensory additive, functional group: colourants (ii) substances which, when fed to animals, add colours to food of animal origin, for use in fish and crustaceans and under the functional group (iii) colourants, substances which favourably affect the colour of ornamental fish or birds, for use in feed for ornamental fish. This assessment regards the renewal of the authorisation of ATX for the above‐mentioned animal species.

The current authorisation sets purity criteria in the specifications for the active substance ATX with a minimum content of 96% total colouring agents and a maximum of 5% of carotenoids other than ATX. The authorisation also states that ATX may be placed on the market and used as an additive consisting of preparation. The applicants indicated that, with the present application, they are seeking the renewal of the authorisation for the active substance ATX (96%) and for its solid (containing a minimum of 8% of ATX) and liquid (containing a minimum of 5% of ATX) preparations, which will be described in the sections below.

Characterisation

Characterisation of the active substance

Astaxanthin (ATX, 6‐hydroxy‐3‐[(1E,3E,5E,7E,9E,11E,13E,15E,17E)‐18‐(4‐hydroxy‐2,6,6‐trimethyl‐3‐oxocyclohexen‐1‐yl)‐3,7,12,16‐tetramethyloctadeca‐1,3,5,7,9,11,13,15,17‐nonaenyl]‐2,4,4‐trimethylcyclohex‐2‐en‐1‐one, Chemical Abstract Service (CAS) number 7542‐45‐2, European Community (EC) number 231‐424‐6) has the molecular formula of C40H52O4, and its molecular weight is 596.8 g/mol.

ATX under evaluation is a solid product and is manufactured by chemical synthesis. According to the applicant, the manufacturing process has not significantly changed since the previous authorisation.

The current authorisation sets purity criteria in the specification for ATX of a minimum of 96% of total colouring matters and a maximum of carotenoids other than astaxanthin of 5% of total colouring matters.

Considering that the application covers the authorisation to place ATX on the market as such (to be used in the manufacture of preparations or premixtures), the applicants proposed specifications for triphenylphosphine oxide (TPPO) ≤ 550 mg/kg and dichloromethane (DCM) ≤ 5000 mg/kg in the active substance.

Safety

In the previous assessment (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2014a, 2014b), the FEEDAP Panel concluded that ATX is safe for salmonids and crustaceans up to 100 mg ATX/kg complete feed and this conclusion was extrapolated to other finfish and to ornamental fish at the same dose. In the same opinion, it was concluded that the use of ATX up to the maximum permitted dietary level for salmon and trout was of no concern for the safety of the consumer and that the use of ATX in the nutrition of salmonids is of no concern for the safety of the environment. Based on experimental studies, ATX was considered not irritant to skin and eyes and not a dermal sensitiser (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2014a, 2014b).

In 2019, the FEEDAP Panel evaluated the toxicological profile of ATX dimethyldisuccinate (ATX‐DMDS), a structural analogue of synthetic ATX, for use as a colouring feed additive for salmonids, crustaceans and other fish. In the context of that assessment, the FEEDAP Panel reassessed the 2‐year carcinogenicity study considering the endpoints of hepatic effect and retained necessary to update the assessment done in 2014 in which, based on a benchmark dose level (BMDL10) of 3.4 mg/kg body weight (bw) per day (calculated for liver hypertrophy in female rats in a carcinogenicity study) and applying an uncertainty factor (UF) of 100, it was possible to set an acceptable daily intake (ADI) of 0.034 mg ATX/kg bw (equivalent to 2.0 mg ATX per 60 kg person per day) (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2014a). The Panel considered the incidence of multinucleated hepatocytes in female rats in the 2‐year rat study as the critical effect for risk assessment. Since the application of the benchmark dose approach showed a large uncertainty of the dose–response data, the FEEDAP Panel decided to use the lowest observed adverse effects level (LOAEL) of 40 mg ATX/kg bw per day for the increased incidence of multinucleated hepatocytes as the point of departure to derive an updated ADI for ATX. Applying an UF of 100, with an additional UF of 2 (EFSA Scientific Committee, 2012), an ADI of 0.2 mg ATX/kg bw per day was established. The FEEDAP Panel considered an additional UF of 2 acceptable since the adverse hepatocellular changes were reversible and only observed in female rats and not in male rats, mice or dogs. The newly derived ADI replaced the one established by the FEEDAP Panel in 2014 (0.034 mg/kg bw) (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2019).

In 2019, the FEEDAP Panel also calculated the consumer exposure using the values of 25 and 4 mg ATX/kg for residues in fish (including salmon and trout) and seafood (including crustaceans), respectively. These data were the same used in the opinion issued by the FEEDAP Panel in 2014 (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2014a, 2014b). The results showed that the highest chronic exposure was for the population category ‘toddlers’ with 0.15 mg/kg bw per day which represents 73% of the ADI of 0.2 mg/kg bw. Therefore, the use of ATX‐DMDS in the nutrition of salmonids, other fish and crustaceans up to the maximum permitted dietary level of 138 mg ATX‐DMDS/kg complete feed was considered of no concern for the safety of the consumer (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2019).

For the current assessment, the applicants provided (i) declarations that no incidents or safety issues for target animals, consumers, users and/or the environment have been documented or reported regarding the additive since its approval, 15 (ii) a literature search covering different aspects of the safety of the additive and (iii) additional information related to the safety of the additive for the user.

Literature search

The applicants performed an extensive literature search (ELS) to support that the additive remains safe for the target species, the consumer, the environment and the users. 16 The timeframe considered was from 01/2013 to 01/2024 and the strategy followed was reported. The applicants searched in four cumulative databases (LIVIVO, NCBI, Ovid and Toxinfo), 13 single databases and 12 publisher databases (including Elsevier, Ingenta, Springer, Wiley). A total of 184 references were selected by the applicants as relevant for the current assessment. One hundred and seven papers were related to the safety for the target species, 83 to the safety for the consumers and 12 to the safety for the users. No relevant papers regarding the safety for the environment were found. The FEEDAP Panel reviewed the papers and considered that none of them provides information that would modify the conclusions previously reached.

Safety for the target animals, consumer and the environment

Considering that the manufacturing process and the composition of the additive have not been modified since the previous authorisation and no relevant new information was found in the literature search, the Panel concludes that ATX remains safe for the target species, consumer and the environment under the current conditions of the authorisation.

Safety for the user

The applicants provided new studies performed with a cold‐water dispersible powder preparation containing 10% ATX.

The skin irritation and corrosion potential of the test item were tested in a study performed according to OECD Testing Guideline (TG) 431 and 439, which showed that the test item is not a skin irritant (UN GHS ‘No Category’). 17

The eye irritation potential of the test item was tested in a study performed according to OECD TG 492B, which showed that the test item is not an eye irritant (UN GHS ‘No Category’). 18

The skin sensitisation potential of the test item was tested in a study performed according to OECD TG 442D. Based on the results of this study, the test item is considered borderline/inconclusive for the second key event of the skin sensitisation adverse outcome pathway (AOP).

The skin sensitisation potential of the test item was further tested in a study performed according to OECD TG 442E. Based on the results of this study, the test item was found to activate dendritic cells and is therefore considered positive for the third key event of the skin sensitisation AOP.

Conclusions on safety for the user

The active substance ATX is not irritant to skin or eyes and is not a dermal sensitiser. The dispersible powder preparation 10% ATX is not irritant to skin and eye but should be considered a dermal and respiratory sensitiser, and exposure of users by respiratory and dermal routes is considered a risk. No conclusions can be drawn on the other preparations containing ATX.

Efficacy

The present application for renewal of the authorisation does not include a proposal for amending or supplementing the original conditions that would have an impact on the efficacy of the additive. Therefore, there is no need for assessing the efficacy of the additive in the context of the renewal of the authorisation.

CONCLUSIONS

The applicants provided evidence that the additive consisting of preparations of ATX currently on the market complies with the existing terms of the authorisation. In addition, the data provided for the active substance ATX (minimum 96%) showed compliance with the specifications set by the authorisation in terms of purity and those set by the applicant in terms of TPPO and DCM.

The Panel concludes that ATX remains safe for all target animal species, consumers and the environment under the authorised conditions of use.

Regarding safety for the user, the active substance ATX is not irritant to skin or eyes and is not a dermal sensitiser. The dispersible powder formulation containing 10% ATX is not irritant to skin and eye but should be considered a dermal and respiratory sensitiser, and exposure of users by respiratory and dermal routes is considered a risk. No conclusions can be drawn on the other preparations containing ATX.

There is no need for assessing the efficacy of the additive in the context of the renewal of the authorisation.

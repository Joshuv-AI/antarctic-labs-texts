Annihilator of Ext
Mohsen Asgharzadeh

Abstract
. We investigate the higher divisorial ideal D ⁡ ( I ) := Ann ⁡ ( Ext R g ⁡ ( R / I , R ) ) D(I):=\operatorname{Ann}\!\bigl(\operatorname{Ext}^{g}_{R}(R/I,R)\bigr) associated to an ideal I I of grade g g . Our main focus is the containment problem D ⁡ ( I ) ⊆ I ¯ D(I)\subseteq\overline{I} . We establish that this inclusion holds for broad classes of ideals, including unmixed ideals of finite projective dimension over 3-dimensional quasi-normal rings, parameter ideals in quasi-Gorenstein rings, and powers of perfect ideals under certain homological conditions. Conversely, we construct explicit examples showing the necessity of hypotheses. We develop structural properties of D ⁡ ( I ) D(I) , relating it to unmixed parts, reflexive closures, symbolic powers, Frobenius closure, and trace ideals. Applications include criteria for the triviality of reflexive modules and vector bundles on punctured spectra, as well as new connections between annihilators of Ext, conductor ideals, and local cohomology.

1.
Introduction

The interplay between homological algebra and commutative algebra has been a rich source of profound results and deep connections. One particularly fertile intersection lies in the study of Ext modules and their annihilators, which serve as bridges linking module-theoretic properties with ideal-theoretic phenomena. For an ideal
I
in a commutative Noetherian ring
R
, the modules
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(R/I,R)
encode substantial information about both the ideal
I
and the ambient ring
R
. Among these, the first non-vanishing Ext module—occurring when
i=\operatorname{grade}(I,R)
—has proven especially significant.

In his book on divisor theory in module categories
[
23
]
, Vasconcelos introduced the
higher divisorial ideal
associated to an ideal
I
of grade
g
:

D(I):=\operatorname{Ann}\!\bigl(\operatorname{Ext}^{g}_{R}(R/I,R)\bigr).

This invariant naturally interpolates between
I
and its radical
\operatorname{rad}(I)
, and in Cohen-Macaulay settings, between the unmixed part
I^{\operatorname{unm}}
and
\operatorname{rad}(I)
. Despite this seemingly constrained positioning,
D(I)
exhibits remarkable and often unexpected behavior, differing substantially from classical closure operations such as integral closure
\overline{I}
, reflexive closure
I^{**}
, and symbolic powers
I^{(n)}
. Understanding the precise relationship between
D(I)
and these established closures forms the core motivation for this investigation.

The study of annihilators of Ext modules has roots in several important developments. The Auslander-Bridger transpose and its connection to Ext modules revealed deep ties between module theory and ideal theory. Subsequent work by Corso, Huneke, Katz and Vasconcelos
[
8
]
systematically explored the annihilators of Koszul homology (which identifies with the relevant Ext modules in the grade setting), establishing fundamental bounds and raising natural questions about containment relationships. Their work highlighted the intriguing position of
D(I)
: while always containing
I
and contained in
\operatorname{rad}(I)
, its relationship to the integral closure
\overline{I}
remained subtle and context-dependent.

The integral closure of ideals itself is a central concept in commutative algebra, with applications ranging from resolution of singularities to tight closure theory and multiplicity theory. Characterizing when homological invariants like
D(I)
lie within
\overline{I}
therefore connects two important strands of research: the homological-algebraic properties of ideals and their geometric-analytic properties as measured by integral dependence.

The guiding question for this paper, extending investigations initiated in
[
8
]
, is:

Question 1.1
.

Suppose
R
is Cohen-Macaulay and
I
is unmixed. Is

D(I)\subseteq\overline{I}?

We approach this question from multiple angles, obtaining both positive results that identify broad classes of ideals satisfying the containment, and negative results demonstrating its failure in general. Our contributions include:

(i)

Structural analysis of
D(I)
: We establish foundational properties of higher divisorial ideals, including their behavior under flat base change, iteration properties, and monotonicity with respect to inclusion (see Corollary
3.5
). We also relate
D(I)
to primary decomposition through its containment between
I^{\operatorname{unm}}
and
\operatorname{rad}(I)
over Cohen-Macaulay rings (see Proposition
3.7
).

(ii)

Positive containment results
: We prove that
D(I)\subseteq\overline{I}
holds for:

(a)

unmixed ideals of finite projective dimension over 3-dimensional quasi-normal rings (see Proposition
4.7
);

(b)

parameter ideals in universally catenary quasi-Gorenstein rings (see Proposition
2.8
);

(c)

powers of perfect ideals satisfying certain Betti number conditions (see Proposition
4.5
);

(d)

ideals of analytic spread one (see Proposition
6.7
and Corollary
6.8
).

(iii)

Counterexamples and limitations
: We construct explicit examples showing that the containment can fail, even in low-dimensional regular rings (see Corollary
3.10
), and demonstrate the necessity of the unmixedness assumption in Question
1.1
.

(iv)

Connections to other ideal operations
: We elucidate relationships between
D(I)
and:

(a)

reflexive closure
I^{**}
, particularly in dimension one;

(b)

trace ideals and conductor ideals (see Proposition
5.2
and
5.4
);

(c)

Frobenius and tight closures in positive characteristic (see e.g. Proposition
6.11
).

(v)

Applications
: We derive consequences for:

(a)

triviality criteria for reflexive modules and vector bundles on punctured spectra (see Corollary
7.6
);

(b)

connections between annihilators of Ext and local cohomology (see Proposition
6.4
);

(c)

characterization of when
D(I)
equals
I
(see Proposition
3.8
).

Here, we summarize the technical overview and methods:

•

We employ homological methods such as Auslander-Buchsbaum formulas, duality theory, and properties of perfect and Cohen-Macaulay modules to analyze
\operatorname{Ext}^{g}_{R}(R/I,R)
and its annihilator.

•

We use ideal-theoretic techniques such as primary decomposition, integral closure theory, and trace ideal methods to relate
D(I)
to other ideal operations.

•

In positive characteristic, we exploit Frobenius actions to obtain additional results and examples.

•

For geometric perspectives, and as an application to vector bundles, we utilize sheaf-theoretic interpretations and local cohomology to connect algebraic properties of
D(I)
with geometric properties of reflexive sheaves.

We follow
[
17
]
for terminology and notation regarding integral closure, and refer to
[
7
]
for homological algebra and Cohen-Macaulay rings.

2.
Preliminaries and structural concepts

Throughout the paper,
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
is assumed to be commutative noetherian and local, and also all modules are finitely generated, otherwise we specialize it.

Fact 2.1
.

Suppose
I
is an ideal of grade
g
. Then

I\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}(\operatorname{Ext}^{g}(R/I,R))\subseteq\operatorname{rad}({I})
.

Proof.

This is well-known, as this Ext, is just the last nonzero Koszul homology, and the annihilator of this Koszul homology lives inside
\operatorname{rad}({I})
.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

The assumption
g
is grade is important (see
[
11
,
2
]
).

Let
I^{\operatorname{unm}}
be the unmixed part of the ideal
I
. Vasconclos proved over Cohen-Macaulay rings with canonical modules that
I^{\operatorname{unm}}\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}(\operatorname{Ext}^{g}(R/I,\omega_{R}))
. In particular, the following extends his result from Gorenstein rings.

Fact 2.3
.

Suppose
R
is Cohen-Macaulay and
I
is an ideal of height
g
. Then
\operatorname{Ext}^{g}(R/,R)\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{g}(R/I^{\operatorname{unm}},R)
. In particular,

I^{\operatorname{unm}}\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}(\operatorname{Ext}^{g}(R/I,R))\subseteq\operatorname{rad}({I})
.

Proof.

The second inequality is in Fact
2.1
. Recall that
any prime ideal associated to
I^{\operatorname{unm}}
has height equal to
\operatorname{ht}(I)
and any of
(\frac{I^{\operatorname{unm}}}{I})
has hight more than
\operatorname{ht}(I)
. This shows
\operatorname{grade}(\operatorname{Ann}(\frac{I^{\operatorname{unm}}}{I}),R)=\operatorname{ht}(\operatorname{Ann}(\frac{I^{\operatorname{unm}}}{I}))>\operatorname{ht}(I)
. Consequently,
\operatorname{Ext}^{g-1}(\frac{I^{\operatorname{unm}}}{I},R)=\operatorname{Ext}^{g}(\frac{I^{\operatorname{unm}}}{I},R)=0
.
The exact sequence

0\rightarrow\frac{I^{\operatorname{unm}}}{I}\rightarrow R/{I}\rightarrow R/{I^{\operatorname{unm}}}\rightarrow 0

induces

0=\operatorname{Ext}^{g-1}(\frac{I^{\operatorname{unm}}}{I},R)\rightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{g}(R/I^{\operatorname{unm}},R)\rightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{g}(R/I,R)\rightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{g}(\frac{I^{\operatorname{unm}}}{I},R)=0,

as desired claim.∎

The Cohen-Macaulay assumption is needed:

Example 2.4
.

Let
R:=\frac{K[[x,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]]}{x\mathfrak{m}}
and set
I=0
. Then
I^{\operatorname{unm}}=xR
is of height zero, but as
\operatorname{Ext}^{g}(R/I,R)=\operatorname{Hom}(R,R)
, we deduce
I^{\operatorname{unm}}=xR\nsubseteq 0=\operatorname{Ann}(\operatorname{Ext}^{g}(R/I,R))
.

By

(G_{1})

we mean the Gorenstein condition in codimension one. By
(S_{2})
we mean Serre’s condition. By definition, quasi-normal ring is a ring satisfying
(G_{1})
and
(S_{2}).

By
(-)^{\ast}
we mean
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(-,R).

By
\overline{I}
we mean the integral closure of
I
.

Fact 2.5
.

(See
[

).
Suppose
R
is quasi-normal and let
M

\operatorname{Hom}(M,R)^{*}=\bigcap_{P\in\operatorname{Spec}^{1}(R)}M_{P}.

Conjecture 2.6
.

Let
I
be an ideal of height and grade one. Then
I^{**}\subseteq\overline{I}
.

The notation
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(-)
(resp.
\operatorname{id}_{R}(-)
) stands for the projective (resp. injective) dimension
of

.

Remark 2.7
.

Conjecture
2.6
is not true if we only assume that
\operatorname{ht}(I)=1
. To see an example, let
R:=k[[X,Y]]/(X^{2},XY)
and set

I:=yR
which is of height one. Since

I=R/(0:_{R}y)=R/xR
we have
I^{\ast}=(0:_{R}x)=\mathfrak{m}
, and consequently,
I^{**}=\mathfrak{m}^{\ast}
. Now, from
0\to\mathfrak{m}\to R\to k\to 0
we deduce that

0\to[\operatorname{Hom}(k,R)=xR]\to R\to\mathfrak{m}^{\ast}\to\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(k,R)\to 0.

But,
\mu^{i}(\mathfrak{m},R)\neq 0
for all
i\in[0pt(R),\operatorname{id}(R)]
. So,
0\neq\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(k,R)=\oplus k
.
Then, we have

0\to[R/xR=I]\to I^{\ast\ast}\to\oplus_{\neq\emptyset}k\to 0.

From this, we deduce that

I^{**}\nsubseteq I=\overline{I}
.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
R
be universally catenary, quasi-Gorenstein and
I
be a parameter ideal of
g:=\operatorname{codim}(I)
. Then

\operatorname{Ann}(\operatorname{Ext}^{g}(R/I,R))\subseteq\overline{I}.

Proof.

Recall that
g=\dim(R)-\dim(R/I)
. First, we assume that
g=d
.
Then
I
is
\mathfrak{m}
-primary. Hence
I^{\text{unm}}=I
.
But

\operatorname{Ext}^{g}(R/I,R)=\operatorname{Ext}^{g}(R/I,K_{R})=K_{R/I},

see
[
1
]
, where some basic results on canonical modules were given. Consequently,
\operatorname{Ann}(K_{R/I})=U_{R/I}=I^{\text{unm}}=I
.
Clearly, the claim follows. Then, we may assume that
g<d
.
In view of
[
16
, Ex 10.11]
we observe that
I^{\text{unm}}\subseteq\overline{I}
,
and recall that this uses the universally catenary property. Thus

\operatorname{Ann}(\operatorname{Ext}^{g}(R/I,R))\subseteq I^{\text{unm}}\subseteq\overline{I}
.
∎

By

I^{\star}
we mean the tight closure
of
I
.

Corollary 2.9
.

Let
R
be of prime characteristic, essentially of finite type over a field and quasi-Gorenstein, and
I
be a parameter ideal with
g:=\operatorname{codim}(I)<\dim(R)
. Then

\operatorname{Ann}(\operatorname{Ext}^{g}(R/I,R))\subseteq I^{\star}.

Remark 2.10
.

Let
R
be F-regular, of dimension
d\geq 2
. Then there are some ideal such as
I
of grade
g
so that
\operatorname{Ann}(\operatorname{Ext}^{g}(R/I,R))\nsubseteq I^{\star}
.

Proof.

Since
d>1
, there is an ideal
I
which is not unmixed.
Suppose
\operatorname{Ann}(\operatorname{Ext}^{g}(R/I,R))\subseteq I^{\star}
.
In view of Fact
2.3
, we have

I^{\text{unm}}\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}(\operatorname{Ext}^{g}(R/I,R))\subseteq I^{\star}=I,

because the
F
-regularity property means that any ideal is tightly closed. So,
I
is unmixed, a contradiction.
∎

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
I
be a height-one ideal. Suppose
R
is normal. Then

I^{**}\supseteq\overline{I}
.

Proof.

First,

I^{**}=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}^{1}(R)}IR_{\mathfrak{p}}\cap R.
Now, let
V_{\mathfrak{p}}:=\overline{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}=R_{\mathfrak{p}}
, by
(R_{1})
condition, which is a DVR birationally dominates
R
. Let
\Sigma
be the class of all DVR birationally dominates
R
.
Thus,

\begin{array}[]{ll}I^{**}=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}^{1}(R)}IV_{\mathfrak{p}}\cap R\supseteq\bigcap_{V\in\Sigma}IV\cap R=\overline{I}.\end{array}

∎

3.
Higher divisorial

We start by introducing the main object of this paper:

Definition 3.1
.

(Vasconcelos
[
23
]
).
Let
I
be an ideal of grade
g
. By higher divisorial of
I
, we mean
D(I):=D_{R}(I):=\operatorname{Ann}(\operatorname{Ext}^{g}(R/I,R))
.

Observation 3.2
.

Let
f:R\to S
be a flat morphism of local rings and
I
be an ideal of
R
. Then
D_{R}(I)S=D_{S}(IS)
.

Proof.

Suppose

I
is an ideal of grade
g
. Then

0=\operatorname{Ext}^{<g}_{R}(R/I,R)\otimes S=\operatorname{Ext}^{<g}_{S}(S/IS,S)
. Since
S
is faithfully flat,
0\neq\operatorname{Ext}^{g}_{R}(R/I,R)\otimes S=\operatorname{Ext}^{g}_{S}(S/IS,S)
. Thus,
IS
is an ideal of grade
g
. In order to conclude
D_{R}(I)S=D_{S}(IS)
we remark that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\operatorname{Ann}_{S}(\operatorname{Ext}^{g}_{S}(S/IS,S))=\operatorname{Ann}_{S}(\operatorname{Ext}^{g}_{R}(R/I,R)\otimes S)&=[\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{Ext}^{g}_{R}(R/I,R))]\otimes S\\
&\cong[\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{Ext}^{g}_{R}(R/I,R))]S,\end{array}

because
R\to S
is flat.
∎

Fact 3.3
.

(i)

I\subseteq D(I)\subseteq\operatorname{rad}(I)
.

(ii)

Suppose
R/I
is noetherian. There is an
n>0
so that

D^{n}(I)=D^{m}(I)
for all
m>n
.

(iii)

If
\operatorname{grade}(I)=1
, then
D(D(I))=D(I)
.

Proof.

The first item is in Fact
2.1
. The third one
is easy, because in the grade-one case, we know that
D(I)=I^{\ast\ast}
, and thus the equality

I^{\ast\ast}=I^{\ast\ast\ast\ast}
gives the desired claim. To see (ii), let
\ell>1
, and recall that
D^{\ell}(I):=D(D^{\ell-1}(I))
.
By (i) we know
\cdots\subseteq D^{\ell-1}(I)\subseteq D^{\ell}(I)\subseteq D^{\ell+1}(I)\subseteq\cdots.

Thanks to the noetherian property, the desired property follows.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

One has
D(D(I))=D(I)
.

Proof.

First, we assume that
0pt(R)=0
. By definition,
D(I)=\operatorname{Ann}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(R/I,R))=\operatorname{Ann}((0:I))=(0:(0:I))
. Thus,

D^{2}(I)=D(D(I))=(0:(0:D(I)))=[0:(0:(0:(0:I)))].

Recall that

\operatorname{Ann}(J)=\operatorname{Ann}(\operatorname{Ann}(\operatorname{Ann}(J)))
for any ideal
J
(see for instance
[
18
, 2.7]
). Thus,

D^{2}(I)=D(I)
.
Now, we check the general case. Let
\underline{x}:=x_{1},\ldots,x_{g}
be a maximal regular sequence inside
I
and recall that
\underline{x}R\subseteq I\subseteq D(I)\subseteq D^{2}(I)
. Then it is enough to show that
D(I)/\underline{x}R=D^{2}(I)/\underline{x}R
. But, we have
D(I)=(\underline{x}:(\underline{x}:I))
and

D^{2}(I)=[\underline{x}:(\underline{x}:(\underline{x}:(\underline{x}:I)))]
. Thus things are reduced to show that
(0:(0:I))=[0:(0:(0:(0:I)))],
viewed in
R/\underline{x}R
, and this last property is true by the depth-zero case.
∎

Corollary 3.5
.

If
I_{1}\subseteq I_{2}
are ideals of same grade, then

D(I_{1})\subseteq D(I_{2})
. In particular,

\bigcap_{m=1}^{\infty}D(I^{m})\subseteq\ldots\subseteq D(I^{n+1})\subseteq D(I^{n})\subseteq\ldots\subseteq D(I^{2})\subseteq D(I).

Proof.

This is reduced to the case that
\operatorname{grade}(I_{i},R)=0
. So, suppose

I_{1}\subseteq I_{2}
are of grade zero. This gives
(0:I_{1})\supseteq(0:I_{2})
. Consequently,

D(I_{1})=\operatorname{Ann}((0:I_{1}))\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}((0:I_{2}))=D(I_{2}).

∎

Example 3.6
.

(i)

There are two ideals
I_{1}\subseteq I_{2}
so that
D(I_{1})\nsubseteq D(I_{2})
.

(ii)

Let
x
be a regular element. Then
\bigcap_{m=1}^{\infty}D((xR)^{m})=0
.

(iii)

Suppose
R
is Cohen-Macaulay and
I
is
\mathfrak{m}
-primary. Then
\bigcap_{m=1}^{\infty}D(I^{m})=0
.

(iv)

Let
R
be regular of prime characteristic. Then

\bigcap_{m=1}^{\infty}D(I^{m})=0
.

(v)

Let
R:=\frac{k[[x,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]]}{x\mathfrak{m}}
. Then
D
is constant over nontrivial ideals.

(vi)

Let
R:=\frac{k[[x,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]]}{x\mathfrak{m}}
. Then
\bigcap_{m=1}^{\infty}D(I^{m})\neq 0

for any nonzero ideal
I
.

(vii)

Suppose
R
is a 1-dimensional domain and
I
is proper. Then
\bigcap_{m=1}^{\infty}D(I^{m})=0
.

Proof.

(i) Let
R:=k[[x,y]]
,
I_{1}:=(x^{2},xy)
and
I_{2}=(x^{2},y)
. Then
I_{1}\subseteq I_{2}
. Also,
D(I_{1})=(I_{1})^{\ast\ast}=(I_{1})^{\operatorname{unm}}=(x)
, because
I=(x)\cap(x^{2},y)
as a primary decomposition. But,
I_{2}
is
\mathfrak{m}
-primary and integrally closed. Then
I_{2}\subseteq D(I_{2})\subseteq\overline{I_{2}}={I_{2}}
. Clearly,
D(I_{1})\nsubseteq D(I_{2})
because
x\notin D(I_{1})\setminus D(I_{2})
.

(ii) Since
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(R/x^{m}R,R)=R/x^{m}R
, we deduce by Krull’s intersection
theorem that

\bigcap_{m=1}^{\infty}D((xR)^{m})=\bigcap_{m=1}^{\infty}(xR)^{m}=0
.

(iii) Recall that
D(I^{m})\subseteq\overline{(I^{m})}
. Thus, we need to show
\bigcap_{m=1}^{\infty}\overline{(I^{m})}=0
. We do this by two different arguments. The first one, uses the Briancon–Skoda theorem along with Krull’s intersection
theorem. The second one is more elementary, it uses the valuative criterion of integral closure. This latter forces that for any
x\in\bigcap_{m=1}^{\infty}\overline{(I^{m})}
we have
v(x)>m
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
, i.e.,
v(x)=\infty
, and so
x=0
.

(iv)
Since

\bigcap_{m=1}^{\infty}D(I^{m})
annihilates
\operatorname{Ext}^{g}(R/I^{m},R)
for any
m>0
, then it annihilates
\operatornamewithlimits{\varinjlim}\operatorname{Ext}^{g}(R/I^{m},R)=H^{g}_{I}(R)
too.
But since
\operatorname{Ann}(H^{g}_{I}(R))=0
, we see
\bigcap_{m=1}^{\infty}D(I^{m})=0
.

(v)
Let
a\in\mathfrak{m}
be nonzero. It generates an ideal of grade zero.
Then
D(aR)=\operatorname{Ann}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(R/(a),R))=\operatorname{Ann}(0:a)=\operatorname{Ann}(x)=\mathfrak{m}
. Now, let
I\subseteq\mathfrak{m}
be nonzero, and take
0\neq a\in I
. Then Corollary
3.5

implies that
\mathfrak{m}=D(aR)\subseteq D(I)\subseteq\mathfrak{m}
. Consequently,
D(I)=\mathfrak{m}
, i. e.,
D(-)
is constant over nontrivial ideals.

(vi) Clearly,
D(R)=R
, and so
\bigcap_{m=1}^{\infty}D(I^{m})=R\neq 0
. Then we may assume
I\subseteq\mathfrak{m}
. By part
(v)
we know that

D(I^{m})=\mathfrak{m}
. Consequently,

\bigcap_{m=1}^{\infty}D(I^{m})=\mathfrak{m}\neq 0
.

(vii) This follows by (iii).
∎

Proposition 3.7
.

Suppose
R
is Cohen-Macaulay. The following assertions are valid:

(i)

I\subseteq I^{\operatorname{unm}}\subseteq D(I)\subseteq\operatorname{rad}(I)
.

(ii)

Suppose
I
and
J
are primary ideals of same height with different associated primes. Then

D(I\cap J)\subseteq D(I)\cap D(J).

Proof.

(i) This is well-known, see Fact
2.3
.

(ii) Consider the exact sequence

0\to R/(I\cap J)\to R/I\oplus R/J\to R/(I+J)\to 0.

This induces

\operatorname{Ext}^{g}(R/(I+J),R)\to\operatorname{Ext}^{g}(R/I\oplus R/J,R)\to\operatorname{Ext}^{g}(R/(I\cap J),R).

Let
P\in\min({I+J})
. Suppose
P\in\min({I})
. Then
P\notin\min({J})
. So,

\operatorname{ht}(P)>\operatorname{ht}(J)
as
P\supseteq J
.
If
P\in\min({J})
we have same conclusion. Now, assume
P\notin\min({I})\cup\min({J})
. This gives
\operatorname{ht}(P)>\operatorname{ht}(J)=\operatorname{ht}(I)
. In all three cases,
\operatorname{ht}(P)>\operatorname{ht}(J)=\operatorname{ht}(I)
, so
\operatorname{Ext}^{g}(R/(I+J),R)=0
. Therefore,

\operatorname{Ext}^{g}(R/I,R)\oplus\operatorname{Ext}^{g}(R/J,R)\subseteq\operatorname{Ext}^{g}(R/(I\cap J),R),

which gives

\begin{array}[]{ll}D(I)\cap D(J)&=\operatorname{Ann}(\operatorname{Ext}^{g}(R/I,R))\cap\operatorname{Ann}(\operatorname{Ext}^{g}(R/J,R))\\
&=\operatorname{Ann}[\operatorname{Ext}^{g}(R/I,R)\oplus\operatorname{Ext}^{g}(R/J,R)]\\
&\supseteq\operatorname{Ann}(\operatorname{Ext}^{g}(R/(I\cap J),R))\\
&=D(I\cap J),\end{array}

as claimed.
∎

Proposition 3.8
.

The following are true:

(i)

If
R
is Cohen-Macaulay, then
D(\mathfrak{m}^{n})\subseteq\overline{\mathfrak{m}^{n}}
for all
n
.

(ii)

Let
R
be regular. Then
D(\mathfrak{m}^{n})=\mathfrak{m}^{n}
for all
n
.

(iii)

Suppose
R
is zero-dimensional. Then

D_{R}(-)=\operatorname{id}(-)
iff
R
is Gorenstein.

Proof.

(i) and (ii): By Proposition
3.7
(i),
D(\mathfrak{m}^{n})\supseteq{\mathfrak{m}^{n}}
. Since
\mathfrak{m}^{n}
is primary to the maximal ideal, and in view of

[
8
, 2.15]
we see that
D(\mathfrak{m}^{n})\subseteq\overline{\mathfrak{m}^{n}}
. Now,
over regular rings, powers of maximal ideals are integrally closed (see for instance
[
17
, Ex. 1.18]
), so assuming regularity, it yields that
\mathfrak{m}^{n}\subseteq D(\mathfrak{m}^{n})\subseteq\overline{\mathfrak{m}^{n}}=\mathfrak{m}^{n}
.

(iii):
Recall that
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(R/I,R)=(0:_{R}I)
. Thus,
D(I)=\operatorname{Ann}((0:_{R}I))
.
In particular,

I\subseteq\operatorname{tr}(I)\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}(\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(R/I,R))=\operatorname{Ann}(\frac{I^{\ast}}{R})=({I^{\ast}})^{-1}=I^{**}=I,

i.e.,
\operatorname{tr}(I)=I
which is reflexive, a contradiction.
∎

By
\ell(I)
we mean the analytically spread of
I
.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be analytically unramified and
I
an ideal so that
\ell(I_{\mathfrak{p}})<\dim(R_{\mathfrak{p}})
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}(R)\setminus\min({I})
.
Then
\operatorname{tr}(I)\subseteq\overline{I}
.

Proof.

The proof is proceed by induction on
d:=\dim(R)
. If
d=1
, then we know from
[
18
, 2.4(3)]
that

\overline{\operatorname{tr}(I)}=\overline{I}
which gives
\operatorname{tr}(I)\subseteq\overline{I}
.
Hence, we deal with
d\geq 2
and also we can assume in addition that the desired property holds for any analytically unramified local ring of dimension less than
d
. Since analytically unramified is a local property (see
[
17
, Proposition 9.1.4]
) we know that
R_{\mathfrak{q}}
is analytically unramified, for all
\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Spec}(R)
. Now we look at
I\subseteq\operatorname{tr}(I)
, which gives

\overline{I}\subseteq\overline{\operatorname{tr}(I)}
. We are going to use Fact
4.4
to deduce that
\overline{I}=\overline{\operatorname{tr}(I)}
.
To this end, let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(\overline{I})
.

(a)

First, we deal with the case
\mathfrak{p}\in\min(I)
. We consider to the following two cases. This is nothing to show when
\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p})>1
. Indeed, under these assumptions the

(S_{2})
condition implies that
\operatorname{grade}(IR_{\mathfrak{p}},R_{\mathfrak{p}})=\operatorname{grade}(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}},R_{\mathfrak{p}})>1
, and consequently,
\operatorname{tr}(I)_{\mathfrak{p}}=\operatorname{tr}(IR_{\mathfrak{p}})=IR_{\mathfrak{p}}
. In particular, their integral closure are equal as well.
Then without loss of generality, we consider the possibility
\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p})=1
.
Then
\dim(R_{\mathfrak{p}})=1
, and consequently,
\overline{IR_{\mathfrak{p}}}=\overline{\operatorname{tr}(I_{\mathfrak{p}})}
by the initial step of our induction.

(b)

Here, we deal with
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(\overline{I})\setminus\min(I)
.
Let us check the case
\mathfrak{p}\neq\mathfrak{m}
. Then by inductive hypothesis we have
\overline{IR_{\mathfrak{p}}}=\overline{\operatorname{tr}(I_{\mathfrak{p}})}
, because integral closure and taking trace both behave well with respect to localization. It remains to deal with
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{m}
.
In other words,
\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Ass}(\overline{I})
. By a result of Burch (see
[
17
, Prop 5.4.7]
), this implies that
\ell(I)=\dim(R)
. But, this is in a contradiction with our assumption.

In summary, we proved that
\overline{IR_{\mathfrak{p}}}=\overline{\operatorname{tr}(I_{\mathfrak{p}})}
. Therefore, we use Fact
4.4
to deduce that
\overline{I}=\overline{\operatorname{tr}(I)}
. In particular,

\operatorname{tr}(I)\subseteq\overline{I}
.
∎

6.
Low dimension and small height

By
\mu(I)
we mean the minimal number of elements that needs to generate an ideal
I
.

Observation 6.1
.

Suppose
R
is a domain and that
\mu(I)=2
.

(i)

\operatorname{Ann}(\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(R/I,R))=(I:I^{**}).

(ii)

(I:_{R}\overline{I})\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}(\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(R/I,R))
if

R
is analytically unramified and
\dim(R)=1
.

Proof.

(i): There is an exact sequence

0\rightarrow I^{*}\rightarrow R^{2}\rightarrow I\rightarrow 0
.
Recall that
\operatorname{Tr}(-)
is the Auslander’s transpose of
(-)
. We have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(R/I,R)&=\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(I,R)\\
&=\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(\operatorname{Tr}(I),R)\\
&=\operatorname{coker}(I\to I^{**})\\
&=I^{**}/I,\end{array}

and so
\operatorname{Ann}(\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(R/I,R))=\operatorname{Ann}(I^{**}/I)=(I:I^{**}).

(ii) By
[
8
, 2.14]
we know
\overline{I}\supseteq I^{**}
. This gives

r\in(I:\overline{I})\Rightarrow rI^{**}\subseteq r\overline{I}\subseteq I\Rightarrow r\in(I:I^{**})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(i)}}{{=}}\operatorname{Ann}(\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(R/I,R)),

so
(I:_{R}\overline{I})\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}(\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(R/I,R))
as claimed.
∎

By
\operatorname{Syz}_{i}(M)
we mean the
i^{th}
syzygy module of
M
. The following is easy:

Fact 6.2
.

Suppose
p:=\operatorname{pd}(M)<\infty
. Then
\operatorname{Ext}^{p}(M,R)=\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Syz}_{p-1}(M))
.

For simplicity, we set
\Omega M:=\operatorname{Syz}_{1}(M)
.

Proposition 6.3
.

If
M
is perfect module of projective dimension two, then

\operatorname{Ann}(\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(M,R))=\operatorname{Ann}(M)=\operatorname{Ann}\frac{(\Omega M)^{**}}{\Omega M}=\{r\in R:r(\Omega M)^{**}\subseteq M\}.

Proof.

The first equality follows from

\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(M,R),R)\cong M
and the latter is similar to Observation
6.1
(i). In fact, in view of Fact
6.2
, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(M,R),R)&\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(\operatorname{Tr}(\Omega M),R)\\
&=\operatorname{coker}((\Omega M)\to(\Omega M)^{**}),\end{array}

and recall that
\Omega M
is a submodule of a free module, so torsionless. This means
\Omega M\to(\Omega M)^{**}
is an embedding, so the desired claim is clear. ∎

By
(-)^{\vee}
we mean the Matlis duality.

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
R
be a Gorenstein ring of dimension
d>1
and
M
be 1-dimensional. Then

\operatorname{Ann}(\operatorname{Ext}^{d-1}_{R}(M,R))=\operatorname{Ann}(\frac{M}{\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)}).
In particular,
\operatorname{Ann}(H^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M))=\operatorname{Ann}(\frac{M}{\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)})
.

Proof.

Let

0\to{\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)}\to M\to M/{\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)}\to 0
. This induces

0=\operatorname{Ext}^{d-2}(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(M),R)\to\operatorname{Ext}^{d-1}(M/{\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)},R)\to\operatorname{Ext}^{d-1}(M,R)\to\operatorname{Ext}^{d-1}({\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)},R)=0.

We may assume
{\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)}
is nonzero. Now,
M/{\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)}
is of positive depth. Thus,

0<0pt(M/{\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)})\leq\dim(M/{\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)})=\dim(M)=1.

Hence, by Auslander-Bridger formula,

\sup\{i:\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(\frac{M}{\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)},R)\neq 0\}=\operatorname{Gdim}(\frac{M}{\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)})=d-0pt(\frac{M}{\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)})=d-1.

Also,

\displaystyle\inf\{i:\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(\frac{M}{\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)},R)\neq 0\}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{grade}(\operatorname{Ann}(\frac{M}{\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)}),R)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{ht}(\operatorname{Ann}(\frac{M}{\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)}))

\displaystyle=d-\dim(M/{\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)})

\displaystyle=d-1.

In other words,
M/{\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)}
is quasi-perfect. In view of
[
2
, 4.11]
,

\operatorname{Ann}(\operatorname{Ext}^{d-1}_{R}(\frac{M}{\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)},R))=\operatorname{Ann}(\frac{M}{\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)}).

Recall from Matlis’ theory that
\operatorname{Ann}(-)=\operatorname{Ann}((-)^{\vee})
. Now, by using local duality we have
H^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)^{\vee}=\operatorname{Ext}^{d-1}_{R}(M,R)
. Combining these, yields the particular claim.
∎

Observation 6.5
.

Let
M
be zero-dimensional and of finite projective dimension
p
. Then

\operatorname{Ann}(\operatorname{Ext}^{p}_{R}(M,R))=\operatorname{Ann}(M).

Proof.

Recall that the existence of a
such module implies the ring is Cohen-Macaulay. Set
d:=\dim(R)
.
Since
M
is artinian,

\inf\{i:\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(M,R)\neq 0\}=\operatorname{grade}(\operatorname{Ann}(M),R)=0pt(R)=\dim(R).

Also,
p=\sup\{i:\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(M,R)\neq 0\}

which is bounded by
0pt(R)
, by Auslander-Buchsbaum formula. In other words,
M
is perfect, and so

\operatorname{Ann}(\operatorname{Ext}^{p}_{R}(M,R))=\operatorname{Ann}(M)
.
∎

Corollary 6.6

Proposition 6.7
.

Example 6.9
.

The assumptions of Corollary

can be checked.
Indeed, let

and

. Then
\mu({P})=2
, but
\mu(PR_{Q})=1
for all

.

This is well-known that a ring
R
is normal iff every principal ideal is integrally closed. Here, we slightly generalize it:

Corollary 6.10
.

Let
R
be satisfying
(R_{1})
and
I
be a height-one unmixed ideal.
Then
I
is integrally closed.

Proof.

We look at
I\subseteq\overline{I}

and apply Fact
4.4
to deduce
I=\overline{I}
, as they are the same after localization with respect to any associated prime ideals of
I
, and this will be done by using of Serre’s
(R_{1})
condition.
∎

Suppose
R
is 1-dimensional and formally unmixed, and
I\subseteq J\subseteq R
. Recall that

e(I)=e(J)
iff
J\subseteq\overline{I}
and that
e_{HK}(I)=e_{HK}(J)
iff
J\subseteq I^{\star}
. But,
in the 1-dimensional situation,

e_{HK}(-)=e(-)
. Combining these, yields that
\overline{I}=I^{\star}.
By using more elementary arguments we can recover this, and show that they are equal to Frobenius closure
I^{F}
:

Proposition 6.11
.

Let
R
be a 1-dimensional complete domain with algebraically closed residue field of prime characteristic. Then for any ideal
I
we have
\overline{I}=I^{\star}=I^{F}
.

Proof.

Let
p:=\operatorname{char}(R)
. Recall that, for some
q=p^{n}
large enough,
R^{1/q}

contains
\overline{R}
. Now, recall from
[
17
, Proposition 1.6.1]
that
\overline{I}=\overline{I\overline{R}}\cap R
. But,
\overline{R}
is DVR, i.e., any ideal is principal, and so integrally closed (see e.g., Corollary
6.10
). In particular,
I\overline{R}
is integrally closed, and hence

\overline{I}=\overline{I\overline{R}}\cap R=I\overline{R}\cap R\subseteq IR^{1/q}\cap R.

Let
x\in\overline{I}
. There are some

a_{i}\in R^{1/q}
and
x_{i}\in I

so that

x=a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}x_{k}.

In particular
a_{i}^{q}\in R
. This gives

x^{q}=a_{1}^{q}x_{1}^{q}+\cdots+a_{k}^{q}x_{k}^{q}\in I^{[q]}.

From this,
x\in I^{F}
. Then

\overline{I}\subseteq I^{F}\subseteq I^{\star}\subseteq\overline{I}
, which yields the claim.
∎

7.
Connections to triviality of vector bundles

In this Section, we assume rings are homomorphic image of a Gorenstein ring (for instance any complete ring is in this mood), and one of
the following conditions holds are valid, without explicitly referring them:

Hypothesis 7.1
.

(i)

R
contains an infinite field
\bar{k}
with
\operatorname{char}(\bar{k})=0
or

(ii)

R
contains an infinite field
\bar{k}
with
\operatorname{char}(\bar{k})=p>0
and

k
is separable over
k
.

We start with:

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
R
be Cohen-Macaulay UFD of dimension
d:=\dim(R)>4
and
M
be a non-free reflexive module so that
M_{\mathfrak{p}}
is free for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}(R)
of height at most
3
. Then
\operatorname{Ann}(\operatorname{Ext}^{d-3}(M,R))
is a prime ideal of height 2 provided
R
satisfies Serre’s condition
(R_{2})
.

Proof.

There is a prime ideal
P

of height two so that
there is the following exact sequence (see
[
11
, Theorem 2.17]
)

0\rightarrow F:=\text{free}\rightarrow M\rightarrow P\rightarrow 0.

This gives

\operatorname{Ext}^{d-3}(M,R)=\operatorname{Ext}^{d-3}(P,R)=\operatorname{Ext}^{d-2}(R/P,R).

But,

R
is Cohen-Macaulay and UFD, so it is Gorenstein. Consequently,
\operatorname{Ext}^{d-2}(R/P,R)=K_{R/P}
.
Combining all things together, it yields that
\operatorname{Ann}(\operatorname{Ext}^{d-3}(M,R))=\operatorname{Ann}(K_{R/P})=P
.
∎

Corollary 7.3
.

Let
R
be a Cohen-Macaulay UFD and strongly normal ring of dimension
d>4
and
M
be a vector bundle and
0pt(M)>3
. Then
M
is free.

Proof.

This is easy to see from
[
7
, Proposition 1.4.1(b)]
that
M
is reflexive. Suppose on the way of contradiction that
M
is not free. Then we can use the previous result to deduce that

E:=\operatorname{Ext}^{d-3}(M,R)=K_{R/P}\neq 0.

This and Matlis duality imply that

H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{3}(M)=E^{\vee}\neq 0,

a contradiction with
0pt(M)>3
.
∎

Discussion 7.4
.

(See
[
11
]
).
If
V
is a vector bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
has rank
k<n
which is not direct sum of line bundles, Evens-Griffith proved that at least one of
\{H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{n};V),\dots,H^{k-1}(\mathbb{P}^{n};V)\}
is not zero, and similarly, for
\{H^{n-1}(\mathbb{P}^{n};V),\dots,H^{n-k+1}(\mathbb{P}^{n};V)\}
.

Corollary 7.5
.

Let
R
be a Cohen-Macaulay UFD and strongly normal ring of dimension
d>4
,
V:=\tilde{M}
be a vector bundle on
\operatorname{Spec}(R)^{\circ}:=\operatorname{Spec}(R)\setminus\{\mathfrak{m}\}
. If

H^{2}(\operatorname{Spec}(R)^{\circ};V)=0,
then
V
is trivial.

Proof.

There is a natural exact sequence

0\to C\to M\to M^{\ast\ast}\to D\to 0
, where
C=\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(\operatorname{Tr}(M),R)
and

D=\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(\operatorname{Tr}(M),R)
. Since

M
is locally free over
\operatorname{Spec}(R)^{\circ}
, it yields that
M
is locally reflexive over
\operatorname{Spec}(R)^{\circ}
, this in turn, implies that both of
C
and
D
are of finite length. In particular, the sheaves associated to them are zero. From these, we deduce that
V=\tilde{M}\cong\widetilde{M^{\ast\ast}}
. After replacing
M
with
M^{\ast\ast}
, without loss of generality, we may and do assume that
M
is reflexive.
Recall that
H^{2}(\operatorname{Spec}(R)^{\circ};V)=H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{3}(M)
. Thus,
H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{3}(M)=0
.
Suppose on the way of contradiction that
M
is not free. Since

\operatorname{Ext}^{d-3}(M,R)^{\vee}\cong H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{3}(M)=0,

we get to a contradiction with Proposition
7.2
.
Consequently,
M
is free and so
V
is trivial. ∎

.

Let
R

Unifying AMP Algorithms for Rotationally-Invariant Models
Songbin Liu Junjie Ma

Abstract
This paper presents a unified framework for constructing Approximate Message Passing (AMP) algorithms for rotationally-invariant models. By employing a general iterative algorithm template and reducing it to long-memory Orthogonal AMP (OAMP), we systematically derive the correct Onsager terms of AMP algorithms. This approach allows us to rederive an AMP algorithm introduced by Fan and Opper et al., while shedding new light on the role of free cumulants of the spectral law. The free cumulants arise naturally from a recursive centering operation, potentially of independent interest beyond the scope of AMP. To illustrate the flexibility of our framework, we introduce two novel AMP variants and apply them to estimation in spiked models.

1.1
AMP Algorithm

Approximate message passing (AMP)
[
13
,
24
,
5
,
7
,
22
]
has been applied to a broad range of high-dimensional estimation problems. Assume that
\bm{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
is a symmetric random matrix sampled from the Gaussian orthogonal ensemble (GOE), namely,
\bm{W}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2N}}\left(\bm{G}+\bm{G}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)
, where
\bm{G}
consists of independent identically distributed (i.i.d.) Gaussian entries. Starting from an initial guess
\bm{u}_{0}
, the AMP algorithm proceeds as follows:

\displaystyle\bm{r}_{t}

\displaystyle=\bm{W}\bm{u}_{t}-\left\langle{\partial_{t-1}\bm{u}_{{t}}}\right\rangle\cdot\bm{u}_{t-1},

(1a)

\displaystyle\bm{u}_{t+1}

\displaystyle=\eta_{t+1}(\bm{r}_{t}),

(1b)

where
\eta_{t}:\mathbb{R}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
is Lipschitz continuous and applies to elements of the input vector
\bm{r}_{t}
separately and
\left\langle{\partial_{t-1}\bm{u}_{{t}}}\right\rangle:=\sum_{i-1}^{N}\eta^{\prime}_{t}\left(r_{t-1}[i]\right)/N
is the divergence of the nonlinearity
\eta_{t}
. A distinguishing characteristic of the AMP algorithm is that the empirical law of the iterates
\bm{r}_{t}
converges to a Gaussian distribution in the high-dimensional limit:

\bm{r}_{t}\to\mathcal{N}(0,\tau_{t}^{2}),\quad\forall t\geq 1.

Moreover, the variance of the limiting Gaussian distribution can be tracked by a simple recursion known as
state evolution
:

\tau_{t}^{2}=\mathbb{E}_{Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)}\left[\eta_{t-1}^{2}\left(\tau_{t-1}Z\right)\right].

This property enables precise characterization of the performance of AMP, and has shed important insight to various high-dimensional estimation and optimization problems

1.2
Orthogonal AMP Algorithm

The orthogonal AMP (OAMP)
[
30
]
or vector AMP (VAMP)
[
47
]
algorithms, which can be derived based on expectation propagation
[
37
,
42
]
, represent another line of work that generalizes AMP to rotationally-invariant models. This algorithm has been applied in various high-dimensional estimation problems
[
32
,
33
,
12
,
44
]
. OAMP relies on the use of
trace-free
matrix denoisers and
divergence-free
iterate denoisers:

\displaystyle\bm{x}_{t}

\displaystyle=\left(f_{t}(\bm{W})-\frac{\text{tr}\left(f_{t}(\bm{W})\right)}{N}\cdot\bm{I}_{N}\right)\bar{\bm{x}}_{t},

(3a)

\displaystyle\bar{\bm{x}}_{t+1}

\displaystyle=g_{t+1}(\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{t})-\sum_{i=1}^{t}\langle\partial_{i}g_{t+1}(\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{t})\rangle\cdot\bm{x}_{i},

(3b)

where
f_{t}:\mathbb{R}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
is applied to the matrix
\bm{W}
in the following sense: let
\bm{W}=\bm{O}\mathrm{diag}\left(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{N}\right)\bm{O}^{\mathsf{T}}
be the eigenvalue decomposition of
\bm{W}
, then
f_{t}(\bm{W}):=\bm{O}\mathrm{diag}\left(f_{t}(\lambda_{1}),f_{t}(\lambda_{2}),\ldots,f_{t}(\lambda_{N})\right)\bm{O}^{\mathsf{T}}
;
and
\partial_{s}g_{t}
denotes the partial derivative with respect to the
s
th argument of
g_{t}
. A precise definition of the OAMP algorithm can be found in Section
3
. The original OAMP algorithm
[
30
]
assumes
g_{t}
to be a univariate function of
\bm{r}_{t}
, and the extension to the multivariate case is due to
Takeuchi [52]
. Note that the long-memory OAMP algorithm originally proposed in
[
52
]
is more general than (
3
). The above presentation of the OAMP algorithm follows
Dudeja et al. [19]
and is general enough for the purpose of the present paper. Similar to RI-AMP, the iterate
\bm{x}_{t}
in OAMP converges to Gaussian
\bm{x}_{t}\to\mathcal{N}(0,\tau_{t}^{2})
,
\forall t\geq 1
, thanks to the use of trace-free matrix denoiser and divergence-free iterate denoisers.

The OAMP iteration in (
3
) has been used to construct various AMP algorithms, such as convolutional AMP (CAMP)
[
53
]
and Memory AMP (MAMP)
[
29
]
. In a different direction,
Takeuchi [52]
showed that the original Gaussian AMP algorithm can be mapped to some OAMP algorithm, in the context of compressed sensing. The idea of reducing a general iterative algorithm to certain OAMP algorithms has been explored in
Dudeja et al. [19]
for proving universality (with respect to the sensing matrix) of the performance of convex regularized least squares estimators, and in
Dudeja et al. [18]
for analyzing the statistically optimal performance achievable within a broad class of iterative algorithms for spiked models.

1.3
Contributions

Compared with the Gaussian AMP counterpart, the derivations of the rotationally-invariant AMP in
[
43
,
21
]
are more complicated. In this paper, we aim to provide a unified, and arguably more elementary, approach to constructing AMP algorithms (more specifically, their Onsager terms) for rotationally-invariant models. Our approach can be used to re-derive existing AMP algorithms and devise new variants quite easily. The main contributions of this paper include:

•

We introduce a unified framework for constructing AMP algorithms for rotationally-invariant models. Our approach is through reduction to long-memory OAMP algorithms, based on
orthogonal decomposition
of the iterates and recursive unfolding the algorithm. The orthogonal decomposition idea was first introduced in
[
19
]
but for a different purpose. We use this technique to re-derive the RI-AMP algorithm. Our results provide an alternative and more interpretable state evolution of RI-AMP.

•

In our derivation of RI-AMP, the de-biasing coefficients are naturally represented as the normalized traces of certain polynomials of
\bm{W}
. We prove that these coefficients are related to the free cumulants of the spectral law of
\bm{W}
. Our proof relies on a recursive characterization of the free cumulants, which appears to be novel and may be of independent interest.

•

We demonstrate the versatility of our approach by devising two variants of RI-AMP. The first variant, which we refer to as RI-AMP-DF, employs a different form of the Onsager term which only cancels out the essential non-Gaussian terms. We show that RI-AMP-DF is equivalent to RI-AMP with a change of variables. The second variant (called RI-AMP-MP), which applies a nonlinear matrix processing on
\bm{W}
, is inspired by
Barbier et al. [3]
. We further apply RI-AMP-DF to a signal estimation problem in spiked models. Our approach provides a generalization of the BAMP algorithm proposed in
[
3
]
to handle general non-polynomial matrix processing functions.

2.2
A Recursive Characterization of Free Cumulants

Proposition
1
below introduces a recursive characterization of free cumulants. This recursion naturally appears in our derivation of the RI-AMP algorithm to be detailed in Section

Proposition 1
.

Assume that the moments

\mathsf{\Lambda}
exist for all orders. Let

\mathsf{\Lambda}

Q_{n}=\mathsf{\Lambda}Q_{n-1}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[\mathsf{\Lambda}Q_{i-1}]\cdot Q_{n-i},\quad\forall n\geq 1,

(5)

where
Q_{0}:=1
. Then, we have

\mathbb{E}[\mathsf{\Lambda}Q_{n-1}]=\kappa_{n},\quad\forall n\geq 1.

(6)

Proof.

See Appendix
A
.
∎

(Connection with the partial moments in

3
Orthogonal AMP Algorithm

Our approach to derive the RI-AMP algorithm is by reducing it (in a recursive fashion) to certain long-memory orthogonal AMP (OAMP) algorithm
[
52
]
which has very simple state evolution characterization. In this section, we collect some existing results about the OAMP algorithm for later use.

Definition 1

(Orthogonal AMP algorithm)
.

Starting from an initialization
\bar{\bm{x}}_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, an orthogonal AMP algorithm proceeds as follows:

\displaystyle\bm{x}_{t}

\displaystyle=\left(f_{t}(\bm{W})-\frac{\text{tr}\left(f_{t}(\bm{W})\right)}{N}\cdot\bm{I}_{N}\right)\bar{\bm{x}}_{t},

(15a)

\displaystyle\bar{\bm{x}}_{t+1}

\displaystyle=g_{t+1}(\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{t};\bm{a})-\sum_{i=1}^{t}\langle\partial_{i}g_{t+1}(\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{t};\bm{a})\rangle\cdot\bm{x}_{i},

(15b)

where

•

f_{t}:\mathbb{R}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
is applied to
\bm{W}
in the following sense: let
\bm{W}=\bm{O}\mathrm{diag}\left(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{N}\right)\bm{O}^{\mathsf{T}}
be the eigenvalue decomposition of
\bm{W}
, then
f_{t}(\bm{W}):=\bm{O}\mathrm{diag}\left(f_{t}(\lambda_{1}),f_{t}(\lambda_{2}),\ldots,f_{t}(\lambda_{N})\right)\bm{O}^{\mathsf{T}}
.

•

g_{t+1}:\mathbb{R}^{t}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
acts separately on the
N
rows of
(\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{t};\bm{a})\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times t}\times\mathbb{R}^{N\times k}
, and
\langle\partial_{i}g_{t+1}(\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{t};\bm{a})\rangle
denotes the empirical average of the partial derivative of
g_{t+1}
w.r.t. the
i
-th argument, i.e.,

\langle\partial_{i}g_{t+1}(\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{t};\bm{a})\rangle:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{\partial g_{t+1}(\bm{x}_{1}[n],\ldots,\bm{x}_{t}[n];\bm{a}[n])}{\partial\bm{x}_{i}[n]}.

Here,
\bm{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times k}
represents available side information, and
\bm{x}_{i}[n]\in\mathbb{R}
and
\bm{a}[n]\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
denote the
n
-th component of
\bm{x}_{i}
and
n
-th row of
\bm{a}
respectively.

Throughout this paper, we will use the following convergence of high-dimensional vectors. The reader is referred to
[
5
,
21
,
22
]
for more information about this notion of convergence.

Definition 2

(Convergence of high-dimensional vectors)
.

Let
(\bm{z}_{1},\ldots,\bm{z}_{\ell})\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times\ell}
be a collection of random vectors. We say its empirical distribution converges to random variables
(\mathsf{Z}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{Z}_{\ell})
as
N\to\infty
, which we denote as

(\bm{z}_{1},\ldots,\bm{z}_{\ell})\overset{W_{2}}{\longrightarrow}(\mathsf{Z}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{Z}_{\ell}),

if for any test function
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{\ell}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
satisfying

|\psi(\bm{a})-\psi(\bm{b})|\leq L\|\bm{a}-\bm{b}\|_{2}(1+\|\bm{a}\|_{2}+\|\bm{b}\|_{2}),\quad\forall\bm{a},\bm{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell},

(16)

the following holds as
N\to\infty
:

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\psi\left(\bm{z}_{1}[n],\ldots,\bm{z}_{\ell}[n]\right)\overset{\mathbb{P}}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb{E}\left[\psi\left(\mathsf{Z}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{Z}_{\ell}\right)\right].

(17)

The following assumptions are needed for the high-dimensional asymptotic characterization of OAMP.

Assumption 1

(Assumptions for OAMP)
.

(1)

Let
\bm{W}=\bm{O}\text{diag}(\bm{\lambda})\bm{O}^{\mathsf{T}}
be the eigenvalue decomposition of
\bm{W}
. We assume
\bm{O}\sim\mathsf{Unif}(\mathbb{O}(N))
and
\bm{\lambda}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
is deterministic. Moreover, the empirical distribution of
\bm{\lambda}
converges weakly to a compactly support probability measure
\mu
. Additionally, the operator norm of
\bm{W}
is upper bounded by an
N
-independent constant
C
.

(2)

The side information
\bm{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times k}
and the initialization
\bar{\bm{x}}_{1}
are independent of
\bm{O}
. Moreover,
(\bar{\bm{x}}_{1},\bm{a})\overset{W_{2}}{\longrightarrow}(\bar{\mathsf{X}}_{1},\mathsf{A})
, where
\mathbb{E}[\bar{\mathsf{X}}_{1}^{2}]<\infty
and
\mathbb{E}[\|\mathsf{A}\|^{2}]<\infty
.

(3)

For all
t\geq 1
, the matrix denoiser
f_{t}:\mathbb{R}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
is continuous, and does not depend on
N
.

(4)

For all
t\geq 1
, the function

is continuously-differentiable and Lipschitz, and does not depend on
N
.

The high-dimensional asymptotic performance of OAMP admits a state evolution description. This was first conjectured in
[
30
]
for the OAMP algorithm with univariate denoisers. A rigorous proof was provided by
[
47
,
51
]
based on a generalization of the conditioning technique in
[
7
,
5
]
to Haar random orthogonal matrices. (The proof in
[
47
]
was for the vector AMP (VAMP) algorithm. In this paper, the acronyms OAMP and VAMP are used interchangeably.) Generalizations of OAMP to the case of multivariate denoisers are introduced in
[
52
,
19
,
29
]
. The following theorem provides a state evolution characterization for the version of OAMP algorithm as defined in Definition
1
. Its proof, which is based on a simple modification of
[
19
]

.

Theorem 1

(State evolution of OAMP
[
19
]
)
.

Let
(\bm{x}_{t})_{t\geq 1}
be generated via the OAMP algorithm. Suppose Assumption
1
holds. Then, the following holds as
N\to\infty
:

(\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{t};\bm{a})\overset{W_{2}}{\longrightarrow}(\mathsf{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{X}_{t};\mathsf{A}),\quad\forall t\geq 1,

(18a)

where
(\mathsf{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{X}_{t})\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm{0}_{t},\bm{\mathsf{\Omega}}_{t})
is independent of
(\bar{\mathsf{X}}_{1},\mathsf{A})
, and

\bm{\mathsf{\Omega}}_{t}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathrm{Cov}_{\mu}\left[f_{1},f_{1}\right]&\cdots&\mathrm{Cov}_{\mu}\left[f_{1},f_{t}\right]\\
\mathrm{Cov}_{\mu}\left[f_{2},f_{1}\right]&\cdots&\mathrm{Cov}_{\mu}\left[f_{2},f_{t}\right]\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\mathrm{Cov}_{\mu}\left[f_{t},f_{1}\right]&\cdots&\mathrm{Cov}_{\mu}\left[f_{t},f_{t}\right]\\
\end{bmatrix}\circ\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{E}\left[\bar{\mathsf{X}}_{1}\bar{\mathsf{X}}_{1}\right]&\cdots&\mathbb{E}\left[\bar{\mathsf{X}}_{1}\bar{\mathsf{X}}_{t}\right]\\
\mathbb{E}\left[\bar{\mathsf{X}}_{2}\bar{\mathsf{X}}_{1}\right]&\cdots&\mathbb{E}\left[\bar{\mathsf{X}}_{2}\bar{\mathsf{X}}_{t}\right]\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\mathbb{E}\left[\bar{\mathsf{X}}_{t}\bar{\mathsf{X}}_{1}\right]&\cdots&\mathbb{E}\left[\bar{\mathsf{X}}_{t}\bar{\mathsf{X}}_{t}\right]\end{bmatrix},

(18b)

where
\mathrm{Cov}_{\mu}\left[f_{i},f_{j}\right]
denotes the covariance between the random variables
f_{i}(\mathsf{\Lambda})
and
f_{j}(\mathsf{\Lambda})
(with
\mathsf{\Lambda}\sim\mu
), and
\circ
denotes Hadamard product and

\bar{\mathsf{X}}_{t}:=g_{t}(\mathsf{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{X}_{t-1};\mathsf{A})-\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\partial_{i}g_{t}(\mathsf{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{X}_{t-1};\mathsf{A})\right]\cdot\mathsf{X}_{i},\quad\forall t\geq 2.

(18c)

In the above equation,
\mu
denotes the limiting eigenvalue distribution of
\bm{W}
.

Remark 3

(Interpretation of OAMP’s state evolution)
.

The state evolution equation (
18b
) makes precise the following approximation:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\bm{x}_{s}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{x}_{t}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{\tiny{\eqref{Eqn:LM_OAMP_recall}}}}{=}\frac{1}{N}\bar{\bm{x}}_{s}^{\mathsf{T}}\left(f_{s}(\bm{W})-\frac{\text{tr}\left(f_{s}(\bm{W})\right)}{N}\cdot\bm{I}_{N}\right)\left(f_{t}(\bm{W})-\frac{\text{tr}\left(f_{t}(\bm{W})\right)}{N}\cdot\bm{I}_{N}\right)\bar{\bm{x}}_{t},\quad\forall s,t\geq 1

(19a)

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{1}{N}\mathsf{tr}\left(f_{s}(\bm{W})-\frac{\text{tr}\left(f_{s}(\bm{W})\right)}{N}\cdot\bm{I}_{N}\right)\left(f_{t}(\bm{W})-\frac{\text{tr}\left(f_{t}(\bm{W})\right)}{N}\cdot\bm{I}_{N}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{N}\bar{\bm{x}}_{s}^{\mathsf{T}}\bar{\bm{x}}_{t}.

(19b)

Standard random matrix results show that the above approximation is exact (under mild conditions) in the large-
N
limit, if
\bar{\bm{x}}_{t}
and
\bar{\bm{x}}_{s}
were independent of the noise matrix
\bm{W}
. Therefore, for a heuristic interpretation of the state evolution, we may treat the iterates as if they are independent of
\bm{W}
. This property significantly simplifies the dynamics of OAMP algorithms and renders the state evolution easily interpretable.

A distinguishing feature of OAMP algorithm is the pairwise asymptotic orthogonality between the iterates
\bm{x}_{s}
and
\bar{\bm{x}}_{t}
,
\forall s,t\geq 1
. This orthogonality property justifies the name “orthogonal AMP”.

Proposition 2

(Pairwise orthogonality)
.

The following holds as
N\to\infty
,

\underset{N\to\infty}{\mathrm{plim}}\ \frac{1}{N}\bm{x}_{s}^{\mathsf{T}}\bar{\bm{x}}_{t}=0,\quad\forall s,t\geq 1.

(20)

Proof.

The case
t=1
is straightforward. We consider the case
t>1
. From Theorem
1
, the joint empirical distributions of
\bm{x}_{s}
and
(\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{t-1};\bm{a})
converges in the sense of Definition
2
. Consequently,

\displaystyle\underset{N\to\infty}{\mathrm{plim}}\ \frac{1}{N}\bm{x}_{s}^{\mathsf{T}}\bar{\bm{x}}_{t}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{\tiny{(a)}}}{=}\underset{N\to\infty}{\mathrm{plim}}\ \frac{1}{N}\bm{x}_{s}^{\mathsf{T}}\left(g_{t}(\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{t-1};\bm{a})-\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\langle\partial_{i}g_{t+1}(\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{t-1};\bm{a})\rangle\cdot\bm{x}_{i}\right)

\displaystyle\overset{\text{\tiny{(b)}}}{=}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathsf{X}_{s}g_{t}(\mathsf{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{X}_{t-1};\mathsf{A})\right]-\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\partial_{i}g_{t}(\mathsf{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{X}_{t-1};\mathsf{A})\right]\cdot\mathbb{E}[\mathsf{X}_{s}\mathsf{X}_{i}]

\displaystyle\overset{\text{\tiny{(c)}}}{=}0,

where step (a) is from the definition of
\bar{\bm{x}}_{t}
in (
15b
); step (b) is due to the convergence result in Theorem
1
and the fact that
\partial_{i}g_{t}
is continuous and bounded (from the assumption that
g_{t}
is Lipschitz and continuously-differentiable); step (c) is from the multivariate version of Stein’s lemma.
∎

Remark 4
.

When
s\geq t
,
\bm{x}_{s}
is not an immediate input of
\bar{\bm{x}}_{t}:=g_{t}(\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{t-1};\bm{a})-\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\langle\partial_{i}g_{t+1}(\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{t-1};\bm{a})\rangle\cdot\bm{x}_{i}
. Note that the orthogonality property (
20
) still holds in such cases.

Remark 5

(Orthogonal decomposition)
.

We can decompose
{g}_{t+1}(\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{t};\bm{a})
as

{g}_{t+1}(\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{t};\bm{a})=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\langle\partial_{i}g_{t+1}(\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{t};\bm{a})\rangle\cdot\bm{x}_{i}+\bar{\bm{x}}_{t+1},

(21)

where
\bar{\bm{x}}_{t+1}=g_{t+1}(\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{t};\bm{a})-\sum_{i=1}^{t}\langle\partial_{i}g_{t+1}(\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{t};\bm{a})\rangle\cdot\bm{x}_{i}
. Proposition
2
guarantees that
\bar{\bm{x}}_{t+1}
is asymptotically orthogonal to
\bm{x}_{i}
,
\forall i\in[t]
. Namely, (
21
) is a decomposition of
{g}_{t+1}(\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{t};\bm{a})
into a component that lies in
\text{span}\{\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{t}\}
and a component perpendicular to it asymptotically.

OAMP algorithms have simple and interpretable state evolution, thanks to the use of trace-free and divergence-free denoisers. Moreover, with the introduction of general long-memory processing, the OAMP framework is very flexible and allows for convenient derivation of AMP algorithms (as we show in subsequent sections). These attributes make OAMP a unified and simple approach for deriving AMP algorithms tailored to rotationally-invariant models.

4
Main Results

This section presents the main results of this paper. We provide a unified way of deriving the AMP algorithms for rotationally-invariant models.

4.1
Rotationally-Invariant AMP Algorithm

The starting point of our derivation of RI-AMP algorithm is the first order method (FOM) defined below. We remark that FOM has the same form as RI-AMP, but the de-biasing coefficients in FOM are free parameters and not necessarily set as in RI-AMP.

Definition 3

(First order method (FOM))
.

Starting from an initialization
\bm{u}_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, a first order method (FOM) proceeds as

\displaystyle\bm{r}_{t}

\displaystyle=\bm{W}\bm{u}_{t}-\left(\mathsf{b}_{t,1}\bm{u}_{1}+\mathsf{b}_{t,2}\bm{u}_{2}+\cdots+\mathsf{b}_{t,t}\bm{u}_{t}\right),\quad\forall t\in\mathbb{N},

(22a)

\displaystyle\bm{u}_{t+1}

\displaystyle=\eta_{t+1}(\bm{r}_{1},\ldots,\bm{r}_{t}),

(22b)

where
\eta_{t+1}:\mathbb{R}^{t}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
acts separately on the
N
rows of
(\bm{r}_{1},\ldots,\bm{r}_{t})\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times t}
.

For convenience, let
\bm{\mathsf{B}}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{t\times t}
be the matrix collecting the de-biasing coefficients
(\mathsf{b}_{t,i})_{1\leq t,1\leq i\leq t}
as

\bm{\mathsf{B}}_{t}:=\begin{bmatrix}\mathsf{b}_{1,1}&&&\\
\mathsf{b}_{2,1}&\mathsf{b}_{2,2}&&\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\\
\mathsf{b}_{t,1}&\mathsf{b}_{t,2}&\cdots&\mathsf{b}_{t,t}\end{bmatrix}.

(23)

The rotationally-invariant AMP algorithm is a specific way of setting the de-biasing matrix
\bm{\mathsf{B}}_{t}
in order to make the iterates
(\bm{r}_{t})_{t\geq 1}
asymptotically Gaussian. We can now define the RI-AMP algorithm formally.

Definition 4

(RI-AMP algorithm
[
21
]
)
.

The rotationally-invariant AMP (RI-AMP) algorithm is a first order method (FOM) with the de-biasing coefficients matrix
\bm{\mathsf{B}}_{t}
set to

\bm{\mathsf{B}}_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\kappa_{i}(\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{\Phi}}}_{t})^{i-1},

(24)

where
(\kappa_{i})_{i\geq 1}
are the free cumulants of the limiting spectral law
\mu
, and the matrix
\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{\Phi}}}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{t\times t}
collects the empirical partial derivatives of
(\eta_{i})_{i\in[t]}
(i.e., “divergences”):

\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{\Phi}}}_{t}:=\begin{bmatrix}0&&&&\\
\langle\partial_{1}\bm{u}_{2}\rangle&0&&&\\
\langle\partial_{1}\bm{u}_{3}\rangle&\langle\partial_{2}\bm{u}_{3}\rangle&0&&\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&&\\
\langle\partial_{1}\bm{u}_{t}\rangle&\langle\partial_{2}\bm{u}_{t}\rangle&\cdots&\langle\partial_{t-1}\bm{u}_{t}\rangle&0\end{bmatrix}.

(25)

Remark 6

(R-transform)
.

Recall that the free cumulants
(\kappa_{i})_{i\geq 1}
are the coefficients in the power series expansion of the R-transform
[
41
]
. Consequently, the de-biasing matrix formula (
24
) can be expressed in terms of the R-transform, as employed in the work of
Opper et al. [43]
. The R-transform emerges in
Opper et al. [43]
as a result of evaluating certain high-dimensional integrals asymptotically. In the present work, free cumulants arise naturally within a recursive centering operation.

Remark 7

(Reduction to standard Gaussian AMP)
.

For the special iid Gaussian model, namely when
\bm{W}
is drawn from the Gaussian orthogonal Ensemble (GOE), the RI-AMP algorithm simplifies significantly. In this case, the free cumulants vanish except for
\kappa_{2}
:

(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2},\kappa_{3},\kappa_{4},\ldots)=(0,1,0,0,\ldots).

Hence, the de-biasing matrix formula (
24
) reduces to
\bm{\mathsf{B}}_{t}=\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{\Phi}}}_{t}
, and RI-AMP reduces to the standard (long-memory) Gaussian AMP.

Following
[
21
]
, we make the following assumptions in order to analyze the asymptotic performance of RI-AMP.

Assumption 2

(Assumptions for RI-AMP)
.

(1)

Let
\bm{W}=\bm{O}\text{diag}(\bm{\lambda})\bm{O}^{\mathsf{T}}
be the eigenvalue decomposition of
\bm{W}
. We assume
\bm{O}\sim\mathsf{Unif}(\mathbb{O}(N))
and
\bm{\lambda}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
is deterministic. Moreover, the empirical distribution of
\bm{\lambda}
, which is assumed to be deterministic, converges weakly to a compactly support probability measure
\mu
. Additionally, the operator norm of
\bm{W}
is upper bounded by an
N
-independent constant
C
.

(2)

The initialization
\bm{u}_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
is independent of
\bm{O}
. Moreover,
\bm{u}_{1}\overset{W_{2}}{\longrightarrow}U_{1}
, where the random variable
U_{1}
has moments with all orders.

(3)

For all
t\geq 1
, the function
\eta_{t}:\mathbb{R}^{t}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
is Lipschitz continuous.

4.2
Overview of Our Approach

Before presenting the technical details, we first give a brief overview of our approach to AMP algorithms for rotationally-invariant models. Recall that the RI-AMP algorithm has the following form:

\displaystyle\bm{r}_{t}

\displaystyle=\bm{W}\bm{u}_{t}-\left(\mathsf{b}_{t,1}\bm{u}_{1}+\mathsf{b}_{t,2}\bm{u}_{2}+\cdots+\mathsf{b}_{t,t}\bm{u}_{t}\right),

(26a)

\displaystyle\bm{u}_{t+1}

\displaystyle=\eta_{t+1}(\bm{r}_{t}).

(26b)

For ease of discussion, here we assumed that
\eta_{t}(\cdot)
is a single-iterate function that only depends on
\bm{r}_{t}
. The question we aim to address is:

•

How to derive the correct de-biasing coefficients
(\mathsf{b}_{t,i})_{1\leq t,1\leq i\leq t}
such that
(\bm{r}_{t})_{t\geq 1}
are asymptotically Gaussian distributed?

Our approach to this question is based on an orthogonal decomposition idea first introduced by
Dudeja et al. [19]
. Specifically, we decompose the iterate
\bm{u}_{t+1}:=\eta_{t+1}(\bm{r}_{t})
, for each
t\geq 1
, into a component that is parallel to the input vector
\bm{r}_{t}
and a component orthogonal to it (denoted as
\bar{\bm{u}}_{t+1}
). For the more general scenario where
\eta_{t+1}(\cdot)
depends on all past iterates
(\bm{r}_{1},\ldots,\bm{r}_{t})
, this orthogonal decomposition idea can be generalized naturally: we decompose
\eta_{t+1}(\bm{r}_{1},\ldots,\bm{r}_{t})
into a component that lies in
\text{span}(\bm{r}_{1},\ldots,\bm{r}_{t})
and a component orthogonal to it. (Notice that if the input vectors
(\bm{r}_{1},\ldots,\bm{r}_{t})
are jointly Gaussian, the residual orthogonal component
\bar{\bm{u}}_{t+1}
is nothing but the divergence-free estimate defined in Section
3
.) Recursively unfolding the algorithm using this orthogonal decomposition, it is possible to represent the iterates
(\bm{r}_{t})_{t\geq 1}
as linear combinations of the orthogonal components
(\bar{\bm{u}}_{t})_{t\geq 1}
. This representation is already close to an OAMP algorithm defined in Section
3
. It turns out that, by properly choosing the de-biasing coefficients, it is always possible to make the matrices appeared in this linear representation of
(\bar{\bm{u}}_{t})_{t\geq 1}
asymptotically trace-free, thereby casting the algorithm into an OAMP algorithm. The asymptotic Gaussian distribution then follows from the general property of OAMP.

We illustrate the idea by considering the first few iterations. In our discussions, we will encounter a centering operation that makes a matrix asymptotically trace-free, and we shall denote this centering operation by
\mathcal{T}
. Specifically, letting
f:\mathbb{R}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
be a matrix denoising function which acts on input matrices as in (
15
), we denote

\mathcal{T}\left(\bm{A}\right):=\bm{A}-\mathbb{E}_{\mathsf{\Lambda}\sim\mu}[f(\mathsf{\Lambda})]\cdot\bm{I}_{N},\quad\text{where }\bm{A}:=f(\bm{W}).

(27)

We now consider the first iteration of the algorithm. From (
26
), we have
\bm{r}_{1}=\left(\bm{W}-\mathsf{b}_{1,1}\bm{I}_{N}\right)\bm{u}_{1}
. By setting
\mathsf{b}_{1,1}=m_{1}:=\mathbb{E}[\mathsf{\Lambda}]
, we have

\bm{r}_{1}=\mathcal{T}(\bm{W})\bm{u}_{1}:=Q_{1}(\bm{W})\bm{u}_{1}.

(28)

This guarantees
\bm{r}_{1}
to be asymptotically Gaussian by Theorem
1
in Section
3
. Next, we show how to choose
\mathsf{b}_{2,1}
and
\mathsf{b}_{2,2}
to make
\bm{r}_{2}
asymptotically Gaussian. Our approach is based on an orthogonal decomposition of
\bm{u}_{1}
:

\bm{u}_{2}:=\mathsf{d}_{1}\cdot\bm{r}_{1}+\bar{\bm{u}}_{2},

(29)

where the first term is parallel to
\bm{r}_{1}
and the second component orthogonal to
\bm{r}_{1}
(in certain asymptotic sense). When
\bm{r}_{1}
is asymptotically Gaussian, by appealing to Stein’s lemma, the choice
\mathsf{d}_{1}:=\left\langle\partial_{1}\bm{u}_{2}\right\rangle
satisfies the asymptotic orthogonality condition. The orthogonal residual
\bar{\bm{u}}_{2}:=\eta_{1}(\bm{r}_{1})-\mathsf{d}_{1}\cdot\bm{r}_{1}
is a divergence-free function of
\bm{r}_{1}
. For notational simplicity, we assume
\left\langle\partial_{1}\bm{u}_{2}\right\rangle=1
in the following discussions. Now, substituting (
29
) into (
26
) and denoting
\bar{\bm{u}}_{1}:=\bm{u}_{1}
yields

\displaystyle\bm{r}_{2}

\displaystyle=\bm{Wu}_{2}-(\mathsf{b}_{2,1}\bm{u}_{1}+\mathsf{b}_{2,2}\bm{u}_{2})

\displaystyle\overset{\text{\tiny{\eqref{Eqn:AMP_u2}}}}{=}\bm{W}\left(\bm{r}_{1}+\bar{\bm{u}}_{2}\right)-\mathsf{b}_{2,1}\bar{\bm{u}}_{1}-\mathsf{b}_{2,2}\left(\bm{r}_{1}+\bar{\bm{u}}_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\overset{\text{\tiny{\eqref{Eqn:AMP_r1}}}}{=}{\left(\bm{W}Q_{1}(\bm{W})-\mathsf{b}_{2,2}Q_{1}(\bm{W})-\mathsf{b}_{2,1}\bm{I}\right)}\bar{\bm{u}}_{1}+{\left(\bm{W}-\mathsf{b}_{2,2}\bm{I}\right)}\bar{\bm{u}}_{2}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\mathcal{T}\big(\bm{W}Q_{1}(\bm{W})-\mathsf{b}_{2,2}Q_{1}(\bm{W})-\mathsf{b}_{2,1}\bm{I}\big)}_{Q_{2}(\bm{W})}\bar{\bm{u}}_{1}+\underbrace{\mathcal{T}\left(\bm{W}-\mathsf{b}_{2,2}\bm{I}\right)}_{Q_{1}(\bm{W})}\bar{\bm{u}}_{2},

where the last step holds when
\mathsf{b}_{2,1}
and
\mathsf{b}_{2,2}
are chosen to center the respective matrices:

\begin{split}\mathsf{b}_{2,2}&=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\text{tr}\left(\bm{W}\right)=m_{1},\\
\mathsf{b}_{2,1}&=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\text{tr}\left(\bm{W}Q_{1}(\bm{W})-\mathsf{b}_{2,2}Q_{1}(\bm{W})\right)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\text{tr}\left(\bm{W}Q_{1}(\bm{W})\right)\overset{\text{\tiny{\eqref{Eqn:AMP_r1}}}}{=}m_{2}-m_{1}^{2}.\end{split}

(31)

Again, by Theorem
1
in Section
3
, vectors in the form of
P(\bm{W})\bar{\bm{u}}
are asymptotically Gaussian with
P(\cdot)
a polynomial and
P(\bm{W})

trace-free
, and
\bar{\bm{u}}

divergence-free
. Hence, both
Q_{2}(\bm{W})\bar{\bm{u}}_{1}
and
Q_{1}(\bm{W})\bar{\bm{u}}_{2}
are asymptotically Gaussian, and so is
\bm{r}_{2}=Q_{2}(\bm{W})\bar{\bm{u}}_{1}+Q_{1}(\bm{W})\bar{\bm{u}}_{2}
.

We can continue this process. In each step, we decompose the estimate
\eta_{t}(\bm{r}_{t})
as a linear term plus a divergence-free term. To illustrate the main idea and simplify discussions, we assume that
\langle\partial_{t}(\bm{u}_{t+1})\rangle=1

\forall t\geq 1
in this section. It is not difficult to show that, under proper choices of the de-biasing coefficients, we could represent
(\bm{r}_{t})_{t\geq 1}
as:

\begin{split}\bm{r}_{1}&=Q_{1}(\bm{W})\bar{\bm{u}}_{1},\\
\bm{r}_{2}&=Q_{2}(\bm{W})\bar{\bm{u}}_{1}+Q_{1}(\bm{W})\bar{\bm{u}}_{2},\\
\bm{r}_{3}&=Q_{3}(\bm{W})\bar{\bm{u}}_{1}+Q_{2}(\bm{W})\bar{\bm{u}}_{2}+Q_{1}(\bm{W})\bar{\bm{u}}_{3},\\
&\qquad\qquad\vdots\\
\bm{r}_{t}&=Q_{t}(\bm{W})\bar{\bm{u}}_{1}+Q_{t-1}(\bm{W})\bar{\bm{u}}_{2}+Q_{t-2}(\bm{W})\bar{\bm{u}}_{3}+\cdots+Q_{1}(\bm{W})\bar{\bm{u}}_{t},\end{split}

(32)

where
\bar{\bm{u}}_{1}:=\bm{u}_{1}
. In the above display,
({Q}_{t})_{t\geq 1}
is a sequence of polynomials that have zero-mean w.r.t.
\mu
(i.e.,
Q_{t}(\bm{W})
is trace-free) and they satisfy certain recursive relationship.

In our approach, the way to set the de-biasing coefficients is conceptually very simple:
we choose
\bm{\mathsf{B}}_{t}
to center the matrices in the representations of
(\bm{r}_{t})_{t\geq 1}
as linear combinations of
(\bar{\bm{u}}_{t})_{t\geq 1}
. We shall prove in the next subsections that such choice of
\bm{\mathsf{B}}_{t}
is unique, and is precisely the one used in RI-AMP.

4.3
Derivation of Rotationally-Invariant AMP

In this section, we present our approach to derive the RI-AMP algorithm. The basic idea is to start with a FOM and figure out the correct de-biasing matrix such that the FOM can be recursively reduced to certain OAMP algorithm.

Similar to the strategy used in Section
4.2
, we introduce a set of intermediate variables
(\bar{\bm{u}}_{t})_{t\geq 1}
. We reformulate the iterates
(\bm{r}_{t})_{t\geq 1}
in a FOM as linear combinations of
(\bar{\bm{u}}_{t})_{t\geq 1}
, which we record in Lemma
1
below. We emphasize that the results in this lemma are purely
algebraic
. In particular, the quantities
(\mathsf{d}_{t,i})
are understood as free parameters in this lemma and need not be the divergences of the de-noisers.

Lemma 1

(Reformulation of FOM)
.

Let
(\bm{u}_{t})_{t\geq 1}
and
(\bm{r}_{t})_{t\geq 1}
be generated as in (
22
). Let
(\mathsf{d}_{t,i})_{1\leq t,1\leq i\leq t}
be an arbitrary sequence. Define

\bar{\bm{u}}_{t}:={\bm{u}}_{t}-\left(\mathsf{d}_{t-1,1}\cdot\bm{r}_{1}+\cdots+\mathsf{d}_{t-1,t-1}\cdot\bm{r}_{t-1}\right),\quad\forall t>1,

(33)

and
\bar{\bm{u}}_{1}:=\bm{u}_{1}
. Then,
(\bm{r}_{t})_{t\geq 1}
can be represented as

\begin{bmatrix}\bm{r}_{1}\\
\bm{r}_{2}\\
\vdots\\
\bm{r}_{t}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}P_{1,1}(\bm{W})&&&\\
P_{2,1}(\bm{W})&P_{2,2}(\bm{W})&&\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\\
P_{t,1}(\bm{W})&P_{t,1}(\bm{W})&\cdots&P_{t,t}(\bm{W})\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\bar{\bm{u}}_{1}\\
\bar{\bm{u}}_{2}\\
\vdots\\
\bar{\bm{u}}_{t}\end{bmatrix},

(34)

where
(P_{t,i})_{1\leq t,1\leq i\leq t}
is a sequence of polynomials. Let
\bm{\mathsf{P}}_{t}:\mathbb{R}\mapsto\mathbb{R}^{t\times t}
be a collection of these polynomials:

\bm{\mathsf{P}}_{t}(\lambda):=\begin{bmatrix}P_{1,1}(\lambda)&&&\\
P_{2,1}(\lambda)&P_{2,2}(\lambda)&&\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\\
P_{t,1}(\lambda)&P_{t,1}(\lambda)&\cdots&P_{t,t}(\lambda)\end{bmatrix},\quad\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{R}.

(35)

Then,
\bm{\mathsf{P}}_{t}(\lambda)
admits the following explicit expression:

\bm{\mathsf{P}}_{t}(\lambda)=(\bm{\mathsf{I}}_{t}-\lambda\bm{\mathsf{D}}_{t}+\bm{\mathsf{B}}_{t}\bm{\mathsf{D}}_{t})^{-1}(\lambda\bm{\mathsf{I}}_{t}-\bm{\mathsf{B}}_{t}),\quad\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{R}.

(36)

In the above equation,
\bm{\mathsf{B}}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{t\times t}
denotes the de-biasing matrix (
23
) and
\bm{\mathsf{D}}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{t\times t}
is defined as

\bm{\mathsf{D}}_{t}:=\begin{bmatrix}0&&&&\\
\mathsf{d}_{1,1}&0&&&\\
\mathsf{d}_{2,1}&\mathsf{d}_{2,2}&0&&\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&&\\
\mathsf{d}_{t-1,1}&\mathsf{d}_{t-1,2}&\cdots&\mathsf{d}_{t-1,t-1}&0\end{bmatrix}.

(37)

Proof.

See Appendix
C.1
.
∎

In Lemma
1
, we have rewritten the iterate
\bm{r}_{t}
as a linear combination of the variables
(\bar{\bm{u}}_{1},\ldots,\bar{\bm{u}}_{t})
, which we defined in (
33
). (The reason why we introduce these new variables will be clear in our later discussions.) Towards reducing the FOM to a OAMP algorithm and as the second step of our derivation, we impose a trace-free constraint on the matrices
(P_{t,i})_{1\leq t,1\leq i\leq t}
in (
33
), in order to cast the FOM into certain long-memory OAMP algorithm. The following lemma shows that this trace-free constraint naturally yields the form of the de-biasing matrix
\bm{\mathsf{B}}_{t}
used in RI-AMP.

Lemma 2

(Choice of de-biasing matrix
\bm{\mathsf{B}}_{t}
)
.

Let
\bm{\mathsf{B}}\in\mathbb{R}^{t\times t}
and
\bm{\mathsf{D}}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{t\times t}
be two deterministic matrices defined as in (
23
) and (
37
). Define
\bm{\mathsf{P}}_{t}:\mathbb{R}\mapsto\mathbb{R}^{t\times t}
as

\bm{\mathsf{P}}_{t}(\lambda):=(\bm{\mathsf{I}}_{t}-\lambda\bm{\mathsf{D}}_{t}+\bm{\mathsf{B}}_{t}\bm{\mathsf{D}}_{t})^{-1}(\lambda\bm{\mathsf{I}}_{t}-\bm{\mathsf{B}}_{t}),\quad\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{R}.

(38)

Then, the following hold true.

(1)

For any given
\bm{\mathsf{D}}_{t}
, the following equation has a unique solution in
\bm{\mathsf{B}}_{t}
:

\mathbb{E}\left[\bm{\mathsf{P}}_{t}({\mathsf{\Lambda}})\right]=\bm{0}_{t\times t},\quad\mathsf{\Lambda}\sim\mu.

(39)

(2)

The matrix
\bm{\mathsf{B}}_{t}
that solves (
39
) can be represented as

\bm{\mathsf{B}}_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\alpha_{i}\bm{\mathsf{D}}_{t}^{i-1},

(40)

where we adopted the convention
\bm{\mathsf{D}}_{t}^{0}:=\bm{I}
. Let
(Q_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a sequence of polynomials recursively defined by (
Q_{0}(\lambda):=1,\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
):

Q_{n}(\lambda)=\lambda Q_{n-1}(\lambda)-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathsf{\Lambda}Q_{i-1}(\mathsf{\Lambda})\right]\cdot Q_{n-i}(\lambda),\quad\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{R},n\geq 1.

(41a)

The coefficients
(\alpha_{n})_{n\geq 1}
in (
40
) are given by

\alpha_{n}:=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathsf{\Lambda}Q_{n-1}(\mathsf{\Lambda})\right],\quad\forall n\geq 1.

(41b)

(3)

The matrix defined in (
40
) can be alternatively represented as

\bm{\mathsf{B}}_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\kappa_{i}\bm{\mathsf{D}}_{t}^{i-1},

(42)

where
(\kappa_{i})_{i\geq 1}
denotes the free cumulants of
\mu
.

(4)

With
\bm{\mathsf{B}}_{t}
given by (
40
),
\bm{\mathsf{P}}_{t}(\lambda)
can be represented as

\bm{\mathsf{P}}_{t}(\lambda)=\sum_{i=1}^{t}Q_{i}(\lambda)\bm{\mathsf{D}}_{t}^{i-1}.

(43)

C.2
.

Remark 8

(On the appearance of free cumulants)
.

Lemma
2
shows that, to make the matrices in the linear representation (
34
) asymptotically trace-free (in the sense of (
39
)), the de-biasing matrix
\bm{\mathsf{B}}_{t}
is uniquely given by a polynomial of
\bm{\mathsf{D}}_{t}
, whose coefficients are free cumulants of the limiting spectral law
\mu
. We remark that, in our derivation of RI-AMP, the appearance of the recursion (
41
) is natural, while its connection with free cumulants (as established in Proposition
1
) may not be immediately clear. In Section
5.1
, we will introduce a variant of RI-AMP where the Onsager term is a linear combination of all past divergence-free estimates (which may be interpreted as “essential” non-Gaussian terms). In this variant of RI-AMP, the de-biasing coefficients satisfy a recurrence different from (
41
) and has no direct relationship with the free cumulants.

The above lemma shows that choosing de-biasing matrix
\bm{\mathsf{B}}_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\kappa_{i}\bm{\mathsf{D}}_{t}^{i-1}
ensures the matrices
(\bm{P}_{i,j}(\bm{W}))_{1\leq i,1\leq j\leq i}
to be asymptotically trace-free. To fully specify the FOM, it remains to determine the matrix
\bm{\mathsf{D}}_{t}
which appears in the definition of
(\bar{\bm{u}}_{t})_{t\geq 1}
. In RI-AMP, this matrix is set to be
\bm{\mathsf{D}}_{t}=\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{\Phi}}}_{t}
, where
\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{\Phi}}}_{t}
collects the empirical divergences of the de-noisers; see (
25
). Lemma
3
below summarizes our new formulation of RI-AMP in terms of the intermediate variables
(\bar{\bm{u}}_{t})_{t\geq 1}
.

Lemma 3

(Choice of
\bm{\mathsf{D}}_{t}
and final RI-AMP algorithm)
.

Let
(\bm{r}_{t})_{t\geq 1}
be the iterates generated by the RI-AMP algorithm. With slight abuse of notations, define
(\bar{\bm{u}}_{t})_{t\geq 1}
as in (
33
), but with
\bm{\mathsf{D}}_{t}=\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{\Phi}}}_{t}
. Namely,
\bar{\bm{u}}_{1}:=\bm{u}_{1}
and

\bar{\bm{u}}_{t}:={\bm{u}}_{t}-\left(\langle\partial_{1}\bm{u}_{t}\rangle\cdot\bm{r}_{1}+\cdots+\langle\partial_{t-1}\bm{u}_{t}\rangle\cdot\bm{r}_{t-1}\right),\quad\forall t>1.

(44)

Then,
(\bm{r}_{t})_{t\geq 1}
can be represented as

\begin{bmatrix}\bm{r}_{1}\\
\bm{r}_{2}\\
\vdots\\
\bm{r}_{t}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\widehat{P}_{1,1}(\bm{W})&&&\\
\widehat{P}_{2,1}(\bm{W})&\widehat{P}_{2,2}(\bm{W})&&\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\\
\widehat{P}_{t,1}(\bm{W})&\widehat{P}_{t,1}(\bm{W})&\cdots&\widehat{P}_{t,t}(\bm{W})\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\bar{\bm{u}}_{1}\\
\bar{\bm{u}}_{2}\\
\vdots\\
\bar{\bm{u}}_{t}\end{bmatrix},

(45)

where
(\widehat{P}_{t,i})_{1\leq t,1\leq i\leq t}
are a sequence of polynomials. Let
\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{P}}}_{t}(\lambda)\in\mathbb{R}^{t\times t}
be the collection of these polynomials (cf. (
35
)). We have

\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{P}}}_{t}(\lambda)=\sum_{i=1}^{t}Q_{i}(\lambda)\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{\Phi}}}_{t}^{i-1},\quad\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{R},

(46)

where
(Q_{i}(\lambda))_{i\geq 1}
are defined recursively

Q_{n}(\lambda)=\lambda Q_{n-1}(\lambda)-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[\mathsf{\Lambda}Q_{i-1}(\mathsf{\Lambda})]\cdot Q_{n-i}(\lambda),\quad\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{R},n\geq 1,

(47)

under the initialization
Q_{0}(\lambda)=1,\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Note that RI-AMP is an instance of FOM with
\bm{\mathsf{B}}_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\kappa_{i}\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{\Phi}}}_{t}^{i-1}
. The claimed result is a consequence of Lemma
1
and Lemma
2
. However, we emphasize one subtle point here. Note that we have assumed
\bm{\mathsf{B}}_{t}
and
\bm{\mathsf{D}}_{t}
to be deterministic in Lemma
2
, based on which
\mathbb{E}_{\mathsf{\Lambda}\sim\mu}\left[\bm{\mathsf{P}}_{t}(\mathsf{\Lambda})\right]
was calculated. On the other hand, when
\bm{\mathsf{D}}_{t}=\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{\Phi}}}_{t}
, which contains the empirical divergences of the de-noisers, the matrix
\bm{\mathsf{D}}_{t}
is no longer deterministic and correlated with
\bm{W}
. Nevertheless, Lemma
3
still holds. To see this, note that by Lemma
1
, (
45
) holds with

\displaystyle\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{P}}}_{t}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=(\bm{\mathsf{I}}_{t}-\lambda\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{\Phi}}}_{t}+\bm{\mathsf{B}}_{t}\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{\Phi}}}_{t})^{-1}(\lambda\bm{\mathsf{I}}_{t}-\bm{\mathsf{B}}_{t})

(48a)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\left(\lambda\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{\Phi}}}_{t}-\bm{\mathsf{B}}_{t}\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{\Phi}}}_{t}\right)^{i-1}(\lambda\bm{\mathsf{I}}_{t}-\bm{\mathsf{B}}_{t}),

(48b)

where the second step is due to the following identity for a strictly lower triangular matrix
\bm{\mathsf{A}}\in\mathbb{R}^{t\times t}
:
(\bm{\mathsf{I}}_{t}-\bm{\mathsf{A}})^{-1}=\bm{\mathsf{I}}+\bm{\mathsf{A}}+\bm{\mathsf{A}}^{2}+\cdots+\bm{\mathsf{A}}^{t-1}
. Now,
\bm{\mathsf{B}}_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\kappa_{i}\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{\Phi}}}^{i-1}_{t}
, we have

\bm{\mathsf{B}}_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\kappa_{i}\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{\Phi}}}^{i-1}_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\mathbb{E}[\mathsf{\Lambda}Q_{i-1}(\mathsf{\Lambda})]\cdot\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{\Phi}}}^{i-1}_{t},

(49)

where
(Q_{i}(\lambda))_{i\geq 1}
are defined recursively in (
47
). The second equality in the above equation is due to the recursive characterization of free cumulants in Lemma
1
. From (
48
) and (
49
),
\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{P}}}_{t}(\lambda)
is a polynomial of
\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{\Phi}}}_{t}
. Applying similar algebraic calculations as the proof of Lemma
2
-(2) leads to the expression of
\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{\Phi}}}_{t}
(
46
) that we aim to derive.

2

(51a)

where

(51b)

with

(51c)

The sequence of polynomials

5
Generalizations and Applications

In the preceding section, we demonstrated how our approach can be employed to derive an existing AMP algorithm. In this section, we showcase the versatility of our approach by devising two novel AMP variants. The first variant employs a different form of Onsager term. The second variant allows an additional matrix denoising step, inspired by the recent line of work
[
3
,
18
,
4
]
. It is possible to produce numerous other variants of AMP, but we do not pursue it further in this paper. Finally, we discuss the applications in spiked models.

5.1
Generalization of RI-AMP: Different Form of Onsager Terms

In the RI-AMP algorithm, the Onsager term
\mathsf{b}_{t,1}\bm{u}_{1}+\cdots+\mathsf{b}_{t,t}\bm{u}_{t}
is employed to cancel out the non-Gaussian component within
\bm{W}\bm{u}_{t}
. Our derivation of RI-AMP in Section
4
indicates that it is possible to achieve the same goal by using an Onsager term that is a linear combination of
(\bar{\bm{u}}_{1},\ldots,\bar{\bm{u}}_{t})
. Motivated by this insight, we introduce a new variant of RI-AMP, coined RI-AMP-DF.

Definition 5

(A variant of RI-AMP: RI-AMP-DF)
.

Starting from an initialization
\bm{u}_{1}=\bar{\bm{u}}_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, RI-AMP-DF proceeds as

\displaystyle\bm{r}_{t}

\displaystyle=\bm{W}\bm{u}_{t}-\left(\mathsf{c}_{t,1}\bar{\bm{u}}_{1}+\mathsf{c}_{t,2}\bar{\bm{u}}_{2}+\cdots+\mathsf{c}_{t,t}\bar{\bm{u}}_{t}\right),\quad\forall t\geq 1,

(53a)

\displaystyle\bm{u}_{t+1}

\displaystyle=\eta_{t+1}(\bm{r}_{1},\ldots,\bm{r}_{t}),

(53b)

\displaystyle\bar{\bm{u}}_{t+1}

\displaystyle=\eta_{t+1}(\bm{r}_{1},\ldots,\bm{r}_{t})-\left(\langle\partial_{1}\bm{u}_{t+1}\rangle\cdot\bm{r}_{1}+\cdots+\langle\partial_{t}\bm{u}_{t+1}\rangle\cdot\bm{r}_{t}\right),

(53c)

where
\eta_{t+1}:\mathbb{R}^{t}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
acts separately on the
N
rows of
(\bm{r}_{1},\ldots,\bm{r}_{t})\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times t}
. The de-biasing coefficients are set to

\bm{\mathsf{C}}_{t}:=\begin{bmatrix}\mathsf{c}_{1,1}&&&\\
\mathsf{c}_{2,1}&\mathsf{c}_{2,2}&&\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\\
\mathsf{c}_{t,1}&\mathsf{c}_{t,2}&\cdots&\mathsf{c}_{t,t}\end{bmatrix}=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\gamma_{i}(\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{\Phi}}}_{t})^{i-1},

(54)

where
\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{\Phi}}}_{t}
is defined in (
25
) and the sequence
(\gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are defined as

\gamma_{n}=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathsf{\Lambda}H_{n-1}(\mathsf{\Lambda})\right],\quad\forall n\geq 1,\quad\mathsf{\Lambda}\sim\mu,

(55)

with
(H_{i}(\lambda))_{i\geq 1}
a sequence of polynomials satisfying the recurrence (
H_{0}(\lambda):=1
):

H_{n}(\lambda)=\lambda H_{n-1}(\lambda)-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathsf{\Lambda}H_{n-1}(\mathsf{\Lambda})\right],\quad\forall n\geq 1,\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{R}.

(56)

The de-biasing matrix
\bm{\mathsf{C}}_{t}
(
54
) is derived using the approach detailed in Section
4.3
. Note that both the de-biasing matrix
\bm{\mathsf{B}}_{t}
(
24
) for RI-AMP and the de-biasing matrix
\bm{\mathsf{C}}_{t}
for RI-AMP-DF are polynomials of the divergence matrix
\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{\Phi}}}_{t}
. Moreover, the coefficients in both polynomial representations admit recursive characterization; see (
41
) and (
56
).

As before, we can reduce RI-AMP-DF to a certain OAMP algorithm. The following theorem summarizes the reduction result.

Theorem 3

(Reduction of RI-AMP-DF to OAMP and state evolution)
.

Let
(\bm{r}_{t})_{t\geq 1}
and
(\bar{\bm{u}}_{t})_{t\geq 1}
be generated via the RI-AMP-DF algorithm. Then, the following statements hold.

(1)

For all
t\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\bm{r}_{1}\\
\bm{r}_{2}\\
\vdots\\
\bm{r}_{t}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\widehat{G}_{1,1}(\bm{W})&&&\\
\widehat{G}_{2,1}(\bm{W})&\widehat{G}_{2,2}(\bm{W})&&\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\\
\widehat{G}_{t,1}(\bm{W})&\widehat{G}_{t,2}(\bm{W})&\cdots&\widehat{G}_{t,t}(\bm{W})\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\bar{\bm{u}}_{1}\\
\bar{\bm{u}}_{2}\\
\vdots\\
\bar{\bm{u}}_{t}\end{bmatrix},

(57a)

Let
\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{G}}}_{t}(\lambda)
be the matrix that collects the polynomials
(\widehat{G}_{i,j})_{1\leq i\leq t,1\leq j\leq i}
(cf. (
35
)).

Then,
\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{G}}}_{t}(\lambda)
admits the following representation:

\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{G}}}_{t}(\lambda)=\sum_{i=1}^{t}H_{i}(\lambda)\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{\Phi}}}_{t}^{i-1},\quad\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{R},

(58)

where
(H_{n}(\lambda))_{n\geq 1}
are defined in (
56
).

(2)

Suppose that Assumption
2
holds. As
N\to\infty
,

(\bm{r}_{1},\ldots,\bm{r}_{t})\overset{W_{2}}{\longrightarrow}(\mathsf{R}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{R}_{t})\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm{0}_{t},\bm{\mathsf{\Delta}}_{t}),\quad\forall t\geq 1,

(59a)

where

\bm{\mathsf{\Delta}}_{t}=\mathbb{E}_{\mathsf{\Lambda}\sim\mu}\left[{\bm{\mathsf{G}}}_{t}(\mathsf{\Lambda})\,\bar{\bm{\mathsf{\Delta}}}_{t}\,{\bm{\mathsf{G}}}_{t}(\mathsf{\Lambda})^{\mathsf{T}}\right]

(59b)

with

{\bm{\mathsf{G}}}_{t}(\mathsf{\Lambda}):=\sum_{i=1}^{t}H_{i}(\mathsf{\Lambda}){\bm{\mathsf{\Phi}}}_{t}^{i-1}.

(59c)

In the above equation,
\bar{\bm{\mathsf{\Delta}}}_{t}
and
\bm{\mathsf{\Phi}}_{t}
are defined similarly as in Theorem
2
.

Proof.

See Appendix
D.1
.

Remark 10

(RI-AMP-DF can implement any GFOM)
.

The Onsager terms of RI-AMP-DF and the original RI-AMP algorithm are slightly different. However, these two algorithms are essentially equivalent up to a proper change of variables. In fact, both RI-AMP-DF and RI-AMP are equivalent to a broader class algorithms, namely, the generalized first order methods (GFOM) introduced in
[
40
,
11
]
. More details about this reduction can be found in Appendix
D.2
.

5.2
Generalization of RI-AMP: Matrix Processing

Motivated by
[
3
]
, we introduce another variant of RI-AMP that applies a nonlinear processing function on the matrix
\bm{W}
in each iteration. In the following, we call this algorithm RI-AMP-MP. It is shown in
[
3
]
that adding the matrix processing operation could lead to better signal estimation for spiked models.

Definition 6

(A variant of RI-AMP: RI-AMP-MP)
.

Starting from an initialization
\bm{u}_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, RI-AMP-MP proceeds as follows

\displaystyle\bm{r}_{t}

\displaystyle=f_{t}(\bm{W})\bm{u}_{t}-\left(\mathsf{e}_{t,1}\bm{u}_{1}+\mathsf{e}_{t,2}\bm{u}_{2}+\cdots+\mathsf{e}_{t,t}\bm{u}_{t}\right),

(60a)

\displaystyle\bm{u}_{t+1}

\displaystyle=\eta_{t}(\bm{r}_{1},\ldots,\bm{r}_{t}),

(60b)

where
f_{t}:\mathbb{R}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
is continuous and applied to the eigenvalues of
\bm{W}
with the eigenvectors unchanged. Let
\bm{\mathsf{E}}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{t\times t}
be a matrix collecting the de-biasing coefficients:

\bm{\mathsf{E}}_{t}:=\begin{bmatrix}\mathsf{e}_{1,1}&&&\\
\mathsf{e}_{2,1}&\mathsf{e}_{2,2}&&\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\\
\mathsf{e}_{t,1}&\mathsf{e}_{t,2}&\cdots&\mathsf{e}_{t,t}\end{bmatrix}.

(61)

The de-biasing matrix
\bm{\mathsf{E}}_{t}
will be set according to Lemma
4
below.

We make two remarks about the RI-AMP-MP algorithm.

(1)

If the matrix processing function
(f_{t})_{t\geq 1}
are identical across different iterations, then RI-AMP-MP effectively reduces to an RI-AMP with the original rotationally-invariant matrix replaced by
\widehat{\bm{W}}:=f(\bm{W})
. Employing an iteration-dependent matrix processing matrix complicates the derivations of the algorithm.

(2)

In
[
3
]
, the matrix processing functions
(f_{t})_{t\geq 1}
are assumed to be polynomials, and the Onsager terms and the associated state evolution are derived by mapping the corresponding algorithm to certain RI-AMP algorithm together with re-indexing. Here, we consider general continuous matrix processing functions.

The following lemma summarizes the reduction of RI-AMP-MP to OAMP. Its proof is similar to that of Lemma
1
and Lemma
2
, and thus omitted.

Lemma 4

(Reduction of RI-AMP-MP to OAMP)
.

Let
(\bm{r}_{t})_{t\geq 1}
be generated via the RI-AMP-MP algorithm and let
(\bar{\bm{u}}_{t})_{t\geq 1}
be defined as in (
44
). Then, the following holds for all
t\geq 1
:

(1)

For any fixed
\bm{\mathsf{E}}_{t}
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\bm{r}_{1}\\
\bm{r}_{2}\\
\vdots\\
\bm{r}_{t}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\widehat{J}_{1,1}(\bm{W})&&&\\
\widehat{J}_{2,1}(\bm{W})&\widehat{J}_{2,2}(\bm{W})&&\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\\
\widehat{J}_{t,1}(\bm{W})&\widehat{J}_{t,2}(\bm{W})&\cdots&\widehat{J}_{t,t}(\bm{W})\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\bar{\bm{u}}_{1}\\
\bar{\bm{u}}_{2}\\
\vdots\\
\bar{\bm{u}}_{t}\end{bmatrix},

(62a)

where
(\widehat{J}_{i,j})_{1\leq i,1\leq j\leq i}
is a sequence of functions. Let
\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{J}}}_{t}:\mathbb{R}\mapsto\mathbb{R}^{t\times t}
be a matrix representation of these functions (similar to (
35
)).
Then,
\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{J}}}_{t}(\lambda)
can be written into the following explicit form:

\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{J}}}(\lambda):=\left(\bm{\mathsf{I}}_{t}-\mathrm{diag}\{f_{1}(\lambda),\ldots,f_{t}(\lambda)\}\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{\Phi}}}_{t}+\bm{\mathsf{E}}_{t}\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{\Phi}}}_{t}\right)^{-1}\left(\mathrm{diag}\{f_{1}(\lambda),\ldots,f_{t}(\lambda)\}-\bm{\mathsf{E}}_{t}\right),\quad\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{R}.

(62b)

(2)

For any
\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{\Phi}}}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{t\times t}
, the following equation has a unique solution in
\bm{\mathsf{E}}_{t}
:

\mathbb{E}\left[\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{J}}}(\mathsf{\Lambda})\right]=\bm{0}_{t\times t},

(63)

where the expectation is taken w.r.t.
\mathsf{\Lambda}\sim\mu
, with
\mathsf{\Lambda}\sim\mu
independent of
\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{\Phi}}}_{t}
.

Some comments are in order:

(1)

It seems that the de-biasing matrix
\bm{\mathsf{E}}_{t}
in RI-AMP-MP, determined by (
63
), cannot be expressed as a simple polynomial of
\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{\Phi}}}_{t}
. While we can solve (
63
) for
\bm{\mathsf{E}}_{t}
recursively (namely, row-by-row), as in the proof of Lemma
2
-(1), the non-commutativity of
\text{diag}\{f_{1}(\lambda),\ldots,f_{t}(\lambda)\}
and
\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{\Phi}}}_{t}
precludes a simple polynomial form.

(2)

Similar to RI-AMP-DF, we may use an Onsager term that is a linear combination of the divergence-free estimates. In this case, the de-biasing matrix can be expressed explicitly, but still cannot be be written as a simple polynomial of
\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{\Phi}}}_{t}
.

(3)

The state evolution equations of RI-AMP-MP can be readily derived from the representation (
62
). We omit the details here.

5.3
Application: Spiked Matrix Model

Until now, we have focused on the derivation of general AMP iterations. In this section, we illustrate the application of our approach to one concrete signal estimation problem, namely, spiked models. AMP algorithms have been instrumental to the theoretical underpinnings of spiked models
[
46
,
45
,
25
,
38
,
26
,
27
]
. We shall show that our approach can conveniently recover and generalize existing AMP algorithms in the context of spiked models.

Consider a
spiked matrix model
where the observation matrix
\bm{Y}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
reads:

\bm{Y}=\frac{\theta}{N}\bm{x}_{\star}\bm{x}_{\star}^{\mathsf{T}}+\bm{W}.

(64)

In the above equation,
\bm{x}_{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
is the signal vector to be estimated,
\theta>0
is a parameter that dictates the SNR, and
\bm{W}
is a noise matrix which we assume to be symmetric and rotationally-invariant. We first recall the definitions of the OAMP algorithm introduced in
[
18
]
for spiked models. Note that compared with the OAMP algorithms in (
15
), the only difference is that the matrix
\bm{W}
in (
15
) is replaced by the observation matrix
\bm{Y}
.

Definition 7

(OAMP algorithm for spiked models
[
18
]
)
.

Starting from an initialization
\bar{\bm{x}}_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, an orthogonal AMP algorithm for the spiked model (
64
) proceeds as follows:

\displaystyle\bm{x}_{t}

\displaystyle=\left(f_{t}(\bm{Y})-\frac{\text{tr}\left(f_{t}(\bm{Y})\right)}{N}\cdot\bm{I}_{N}\right)\bar{\bm{x}}_{t},

(65a)

\displaystyle\bar{\bm{x}}_{t+1}

\displaystyle=g_{t+1}(\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{t};\bm{a})-\sum_{i=1}^{t}\langle\partial_{i}g_{t+1}(\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{t};\bm{a})\rangle\cdot\bm{x}_{i},

(65b)

where
f_{t}:\mathbb{R}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
and
g_{t+1}:\mathbb{R}^{t}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
are defined similarly as in Definition
1
.

The above definition of OAMP is slightly different from that in
[
18
]
in the sense that the empirical estimates
\text{tr}\left(f_{t}(\bm{Y})\right)/N
and
\langle\partial_{i}g_{t+1}(\bm{z}_{1},\ldots,\bm{z}_{t};\bm{a})\rangle
are involved in (
65
), whereas the OAMP in
[
18
]
uses their corresponding large-
N
limits. Despite this difference, the state evolution result in
[
18
]
still applies and we record the results in the following lemma. (This can be shown following the same arguments as in the proof of Theorem
1
, and we omit the details.) We also refer the reader to
[
18
, Section 4.1]
for a heuristic derivation of the state evolution equations.

Lemma 5

(State evolution of OAMP for spiked models
[
18
]
)
.

Let
(\bm{x}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
be generated via (
65
). Suppose Assumption
1
holds. Assume additionally that
(\bm{x}_{\star};\bm{a})\overset{W_{2}}{\longrightarrow}(\mathsf{X}_{\star};\mathsf{A})\sim\pi
where
\mathbb{E}[\mathsf{X}_{\star}^{2}]=1
and
\mathbb{E}[\|\mathsf{A}\|^{2}]<\infty
. Then, the following holds for all
t\geq 1
:

(\bm{x}_{\star},\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{t};\bm{a})\overset{W_{2}}{\longrightarrow}(\mathsf{X}_{\star},\mathsf{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{X}_{t};\mathsf{A}),

(66a)

where
(\mathsf{X}_{\star},\mathsf{A})\sim\pi
and

\displaystyle\left[\mathsf{X}_{1},\mathsf{X}_{2},\ldots,\mathsf{X}_{t}\right]^{\mathsf{T}}=\bm{\mathsf{\beta}}_{t}\mathsf{X}_{\star}+\left[\mathsf{Z}_{1},\mathsf{Z}_{2},\ldots,\mathsf{Z}_{t}\right]^{\mathsf{T}},

(66b)

where
[\mathsf{Z}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{Z}_{t}]\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm{0},\bm{\mathsf{\Omega}}_{t,1}+\bm{\mathsf{\Omega}}_{t,2})
is independent of
\mathsf{X}_{\star}
, and
(\bm{\mathsf{\beta}}_{t},\bm{\mathsf{\Omega}}_{t,1},\bm{\mathsf{\Omega}}_{t,2})
are given by

\displaystyle\bm{\mathsf{\beta}}_{t}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{E}\left[\bar{f}_{1}(\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\nu})\right]\\
\vdots\\
\mathbb{E}\left[\bar{f}_{t}(\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\nu})\right]\end{bmatrix}\circ\bm{\mathsf{\alpha}}_{t},

(66c)

\displaystyle\bm{\mathsf{\Omega}}_{t,1}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\mathrm{Cov}_{\nu}\left[\bar{f}_{1},\bar{f}_{1}\right]&\cdots&\mathrm{Cov}_{\nu}\left[\bar{f}_{1},\bar{f}_{t}\right]\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\mathrm{Cov}_{\nu}\left[\bar{f}_{t},\bar{f}_{1}\right]&\cdots&\mathrm{Cov}_{\nu}\left[\bar{f}_{t},\bar{f}_{t}\right]\end{bmatrix}\circ(\bm{\mathsf{\alpha}}_{t}\bm{\mathsf{\alpha}}_{t}^{\mathsf{T}}),

(66d)

\displaystyle\bm{\mathsf{\Omega}}_{t,2}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\mathrm{Cov}_{\mu}\left[\bar{f}_{1},\bar{f}_{1}\right]&\cdots&\mathrm{Cov}_{\mu}\left[\bar{f}_{1},\bar{f}_{t}\right]\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\mathrm{Cov}_{\mu}\left[\bar{f}_{t},\bar{f}_{1}\right]&\cdots&\mathrm{Cov}_{\mu}\left[\bar{f}_{t},\bar{f}_{t}\right]\end{bmatrix}\circ(\bar{\bm{\mathsf{\Delta}}}_{t}-\bm{\mathsf{\alpha}}_{t}\bm{\mathsf{\alpha}}_{t}^{\mathsf{T}}),

(66e)

where
\bar{f}_{i}(\lambda):=f_{i}(\lambda)-\mathbb{E}_{\mathsf{\Lambda}\sim\mu}[\mathsf{\Lambda}],\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{R},i\in[t]
and
\mathrm{Cov}_{\mu}\left[\bar{f}_{i},\bar{f}_{j}\right]
is defined as in Theorem
1
, and
(\bm{\mathsf{\alpha}}_{t},\bar{\bm{\mathsf{\Delta}}}_{t})
are defined as

\bm{\mathsf{\alpha}}_{t}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathsf{X}_{\star}\bar{\mathsf{X}}_{1}\right]\\
\vdots\\
\mathbb{E}\left[\mathsf{X}_{\star}\bar{\mathsf{X}}_{t}\right]\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad\bar{\bm{\mathsf{\Delta}}}_{t}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{E}\left[\bar{\mathsf{X}}_{1}\bar{\mathsf{X}}_{1}\right]&\cdots&\mathbb{E}\left[\bar{\mathsf{X}}_{1}\bar{\mathsf{X}}_{t}\right]\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\mathbb{E}\left[\bar{\mathsf{X}}_{t}\bar{\mathsf{X}}_{1}\right]&\cdots&\mathbb{E}\left[\bar{\mathsf{X}}_{t}\bar{\mathsf{X}}_{t}\right]\end{bmatrix}.

(66f)

In the above equations,
\nu
is a probability measure whose Stieltjes transform (defined as
m_{\nu}(z):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\nu(\mathrm{d}\lambda)}{z-\lambda},\forall z\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\mathbb{R}
) is given by:

m_{\nu}(z)=\frac{m_{\mu}(z)}{1-\theta m_{\mu}(z)},\quad\forall z\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\mathbb{R}.

(66g)

Further,

\bar{\mathsf{X}}_{t}:=g_{t}(\mathsf{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{X}_{t-1};\mathsf{A})-\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\partial_{i}g_{t}(\mathsf{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{X}_{t-1};\mathsf{A})\right]\cdot\mathsf{X}_{i},\quad\forall t\geq 2.

(66h)

Remark 11

(On the measure
\nu
)
.

The state evolution equations in the above theorem involve a probability measure
\nu
. As shown in
[
18
]
, the measure
\nu
is the large-
N
limit of the following empirical measure:

\nu_{N}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\bm{x}_{\star}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{u}_{i}(\bm{Y})\right)^{2}\cdot\delta_{\lambda_{i}(\bm{Y})},

(67)

where
(\lambda_{i}(\bm{Y}))_{1\leq i\leq N}
and
(\bm{u}_{i}(\bm{Y}))_{1\leq i\leq N}
denote the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of
\bm{Y}
respectively. For more explicit characterization of
\nu
by Stieltjes inversion see
[
18
, Appendix A.1]
.

We can now apply our approach to derive AMP algorithms by reduction to OAMP algorithms. We consider a variant of RI-AMP-MP where the matrix processing function does not change across iterations.

Definition 8

(RI-AMP-MP algorithm for spiked models)
.

Starting from an initialization
\bm{u}_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, RI-AMP-MP generates a sequence of iterates via

\displaystyle\bm{r}_{t}

\displaystyle=f(\bm{Y})\bm{u}_{t}-\left(\mathsf{e}_{t,1}\bm{u}_{1}+\mathsf{e}_{t,2}\bm{u}_{2}+\cdots+\mathsf{e}_{t,t}\bm{u}_{t}\right),

(68a)

\displaystyle\bm{u}_{t+1}

\displaystyle=\eta_{t}(\bm{r}_{1},\ldots,\bm{r}_{t}),

(68b)

where
f:\mathbb{R}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
is a continuous function that does not change across iterations, and the de-biasing matrix
\bm{\mathsf{E}}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{t\times t}
is given by

\bm{\mathsf{E}}_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}(\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{\Phi}}}_{t})^{i-1},

(69)

where
\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{\Phi}}}_{t}
is defined as in (
25
) and
(\widetilde{\alpha}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
are the free cumulants of the random variable
f(\mathsf{\Lambda})
,
\mathsf{\Lambda}\sim\mu
.

Remark 12

(Connection with
[
3
]
)
.

The above algorithm is essentially the “Bayes-optimal AMP” (BAMP) algorithm proposed in
[
3
]
. It generalizes BAMP in the sense that the matrix processing function
f(\cdot)
can be non-polynomial.

Again, we can use the same technique to reduce the above RI-AMP-MP algorithm to an OAMP algorithm, and then apply Lemma
5
to derive a state evolution for RI-AMP-MP. Our results are summarized in the following theorem.

Theorem 4

(State evolution of RI-AMP-MP for spiked models)
.

Let
(\bm{r}_{t})_{t\geq 1}
be generated via (
68
) and let
(\bar{\bm{u}}_{t})_{t\geq 1}
be defined as in (
44
). Then, the following statements hold.

(1)

For all
t\geq 1
:

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\bm{r}_{1}\\
\bm{r}_{2}\\
\vdots\\
\bm{r}_{t}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\widehat{J}_{1,1}(\bm{Y})&&&\\
\widehat{J}_{2,1}(\bm{Y})&\widehat{J}_{2,2}(\bm{Y})&&\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\\
\widehat{J}_{t,1}(\bm{Y})&\widehat{J}_{t,2}(\bm{Y})&\cdots&\widehat{J}_{t,t}(\bm{Y})\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\bar{\bm{u}}_{1}\\
\bar{\bm{u}}_{2}\\
\vdots\\
\bar{\bm{u}}_{t}\end{bmatrix}.

(70a)

Let
\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{J}}}_{t}:\mathbb{R}\mapsto\mathbb{R}^{t\times t}
be a matrix representation of the functions
(\widehat{J}_{i,j})_{1\leq i\leq t,1\leq j\leq i}
. Then,
\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{J}}}_{t}(\lambda)
can be written into the following explicit form:

\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{J}}}_{t}(\lambda)=\sum_{i=1}^{t}K_{i}(\lambda)\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{\Phi}}}_{t}^{i-1},\quad\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{R},

(70b)

where the sequence of functions
(K_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is specified by the recurrence (with
K_{0}(\lambda):=1,\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
)

K_{n}(\lambda)=f(\lambda)K_{n-1}(\lambda)-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{\mathsf{\Lambda}\sim\mu}\left[f(\mathsf{\Lambda})K_{i-1}(\mathsf{\Lambda})\right]\cdot K_{n-i}(\lambda),\quad\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{R},\ n\geq 1.

(70c)

(2)

Suppose that Assumption
2
holds. Further, assume that
\bm{x}_{\star}\overset{W_{2}}{\longrightarrow}\mathsf{X}_{\star}
where
\mathbb{E}[\mathsf{X}_{\star}^{2}]=1
. As
N\to\infty
:

(\bm{x}_{\star},\bm{r}_{1},\ldots,\bm{r}_{t})\overset{W_{2}}{\longrightarrow}(\mathsf{X}_{\star},\mathsf{R}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{R}_{t}),\quad\forall t\geq 1,

(71a)

where

\displaystyle\left[\mathsf{R}_{1},\mathsf{R}_{2},\ldots,\mathsf{R}_{t}\right]^{\mathsf{T}}=\bm{\mathsf{\beta}}_{t}\mathsf{X}_{\star}+\left[\mathsf{Z}_{1},\mathsf{Z}_{2},\ldots,\mathsf{Z}_{t}\right]^{\mathsf{T}},

(71b)

where
[\mathsf{Z}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{Z}_{t}]\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm{0},\bm{\mathsf{\Sigma}}_{t,1}+\bm{\mathsf{\Sigma}}_{t,2})
is independent of
\mathsf{X}_{\star}
, and
(\bm{\mathsf{\beta}}_{t},\bm{\mathsf{\Sigma}}_{t,1},\bm{\mathsf{\Sigma}}_{t,2})
are given by

\displaystyle\bm{\mathsf{\beta}}_{t}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\nu}\sim\nu}\left[\bm{\mathsf{J}}_{t}(\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\nu})\right]\bm{\mathsf{\alpha}}_{t},

(71c)

\displaystyle\bm{\mathsf{\Sigma}}_{t,1}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\nu}\sim\nu}\left[\bm{\mathsf{J}}_{t}(\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\nu})\bm{\mathsf{\alpha}}_{t}\bm{\mathsf{\alpha}}_{t}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{\mathsf{J}}_{t}(\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\nu})^{\mathsf{T}}\right]-\bm{\mathsf{\beta}}_{t}\bm{\mathsf{\beta}}_{t}^{\mathsf{T}},

(71d)

\displaystyle\bm{\mathsf{\Sigma}}_{t,2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathsf{\Lambda}\sim\mu}\left[\bm{\mathsf{J}}_{t}(\mathsf{\Lambda})(\bar{\bm{\mathsf{\Delta}}}_{t}-\bm{\mathsf{\alpha}}_{t}\bm{\mathsf{\alpha}}_{t}^{\mathsf{T}})\bm{\mathsf{J}}_{t}(\mathsf{\Lambda})^{\mathsf{T}}\right],

(71e)

where
{\bm{\mathsf{J}}}_{t}(\lambda):=\sum_{i=1}^{t}K_{i}(\lambda){\bm{\mathsf{\Phi}}}_{t}^{i-1},\,\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, the probability measure
\nu
is defined in Lemma
5
, and
(\bm{\mathsf{\alpha}}_{t},\bar{\bm{\mathsf{\Delta}}}_{t})
are defined as

\bm{\mathsf{\alpha}}_{t}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathsf{X}_{\star}\bar{\mathsf{X}}_{1}\right]\\
\vdots\\
\mathbb{E}\left[\mathsf{X}_{\star}\bar{\mathsf{X}}_{t}\right]\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad\bar{\bm{\mathsf{\Delta}}}_{t}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{E}\left[\bar{\mathsf{X}}_{1}\bar{\mathsf{X}}_{1}\right]&\cdots&\mathbb{E}\left[\bar{\mathsf{X}}_{1}\bar{\mathsf{X}}_{t}\right]\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\mathbb{E}\left[\bar{\mathsf{X}}_{t}\bar{\mathsf{X}}_{1}\right]&\cdots&\mathbb{E}\left[\bar{\mathsf{X}}_{t}\bar{\mathsf{X}}_{t}\right]\end{bmatrix}.

(71f)

Proof.

Claim (1) can be proved analogously to Lemma
3
, with
\bm{W}
replaced by
\bm{Y}
. Note that the reduction of RI-AMP to OAMP as presented in Lemma
3
is a deterministic result, and still holds with
\bm{W}
replaced by
\bm{Y}
. The proof of Claim (2) is similar to that of Theorem
2
, the only difference being that we now appeal to Lemma
5
(state evolution of OAMP for spiked models).
∎

]

Proofs for Preliminaries

A.1
Derivations of the Polynomial Coefficients (Proof of Corollary
1
)

Recall that
(Q_{n})_{n\geq 1}
satisfies the recursion (see (
5
)):

\displaystyle Q_{n}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{\Lambda}Q_{n-1}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[\mathsf{\Lambda}Q_{i-1}]\cdot Q_{n-i},\quad\forall n\geq 1

(74a)

\displaystyle\overset{\text{\tiny{\eqref{Eqn:kappa_Q}}}}{=}\mathsf{\Lambda}Q_{n-1}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\kappa_{i}\cdot Q_{n-i}.

(74b)

The polynomial representation of
Q_{n}
is denoted as (see (
9
))

Q_{n}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\alpha_{n,i}\mathsf{\Lambda}^{i},\quad\forall n\geq 1.

(75)

Combining these two equations yields

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n}\alpha_{n,i}\mathsf{\Lambda}^{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\alpha_{n-1,i}\mathsf{\Lambda}^{i+1}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-j}\kappa_{j}\alpha_{n-j,i}\mathsf{\Lambda}^{i}

(76a)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{n-1,i-1}\mathsf{\Lambda}^{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-j}\kappa_{j}\alpha_{n-j,i}\mathsf{\Lambda}^{i}

(76b)

\displaystyle\overset{\text{\tiny{(a)}}}{=}\ \left(\alpha_{n-1,n-1}\mathsf{\Lambda}^{n}+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\alpha_{n-1,i-1}\mathsf{\Lambda}^{i}\right)-\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n-i}\kappa_{j}\alpha_{n-j,i}\mathsf{\Lambda}^{i}

(76c)

A.2
A Reformulation of the Moment-Cumulant Formula

We will introduce a recursive characterization of free cumulants in Proposition
1
which is is useful for our derivation of RI-AMP. Before that, we present a useful reformulation of the right-hand side of the moment-cumulant formula (
4
).

Lemma 6
.

Let
q_{0}:=1
. The following holds for any sequence
(q_{\ell})_{\ell\geq 1}
:

\sum_{\pi\in\mathrm{NC}(\ell)}\prod_{B\in\pi}q_{|B|}=\sum_{(s_{1},\ldots,s_{\ell})\in\mathcal{S}(\ell)}\ \prod_{i=1}^{\ell}q_{s_{i}},\quad\forall\ell\geq 1,

(78)

where
\mathcal{S}(\ell)
denotes the collection of
\ell
-tuple
(s_{1},\ldots,s_{\ell})
satisfying

(1)

s_{m}\geq 0,\ \forall m\in[\ell]
.

(2)

\sum_{j=1}^{m}s_{j}\leq m,\ \forall m\in[\ell]
.

(3)

\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}s_{j}=\ell
.

Proof.

First, following similar reasoning as
[
1
, pp. 364]
, we can prove that there is a bijection between
\text{NC}(\ell)
and
\mathcal{S}(\ell)
. For completeness, we include the proof here. We shall provide the explicit forms of the maps for both directions.

Map from
\text{NC}(\ell)
to
\mathcal{S}(\ell)
:

Consider
\pi:=\{B_{1},\ldots,B_{N}\}\in\text{NC}(\ell)
, where
B_{i}
denotes the
i
-th block of
\pi
. Define a map
\phi_{\ell}:\pi\mapsto(s_{1},\ldots,s_{\ell})
:

s_{i}:=\begin{cases}|B_{j}|,&\text{ if $i$ is the largest element of some block $B_{j}$, where $j\in[\ell]$},\\
0,&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

(79)

We claim that
\phi_{\ell}(\pi):=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{\ell})\in\mathcal{S}(\ell)
for any non-crossing partition
\pi\in\text{NC}(\ell)
. To see this, we verify that such
(s_{1},\ldots,s_{\ell})
satisfies the following three conditions: (1)
s_{m}\geq 0,\ \forall m\in[\ell]
; (2)
\sum_{j=1}^{m}s_{j}\leq m,\ \forall m\in[\ell]
; (3)
\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}s_{j}=\ell
. Conditions (1) and (3) directly follow from the definition (
79
). Condition (2) is a consequence of the definition (
79
) as well as the fact that
\pi
is non-crossing.

Map from
\mathcal{S}(\ell)
to
\text{NC}(\ell)
:

Let
(s_{1},\ldots,s_{\ell})\in\mathcal{S}(\ell)
. We start from
s_{\ell}
and count backwards towards
s_{1}
until we meet some nonzero
s_{k}
. We claim that one of the following scenarios must happen:
s_{k}=1
or
(s_{k-j},\ldots,s_{k})=(\underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{j-1\text{ zeros}},j)
for some
1\leq j\leq k-1
. Otherwise, the defining properties of
\mathcal{S}(\ell)
would be violated. In the latter case, we form a block consisting of
\{s_{k-j},\ldots,s_{k}\}
. Next, we remove the elements in this block and repeat the whole procedure (starting from the largest elements) until no elements in
[\ell]
are left. The blocks produced by this procedure constitute a partition of
[\ell]
. Finally, this construction guarantees that the partition is non-crossing.

Proof of Lemma
6
.

Let
\pi\in\text{NC}(\ell)
be an arbitrary non-crossing partition, and denote
\phi_{\ell}(\pi)=(s_{1}(\pi),\ldots,s_{n}(\pi))\in\mathcal{S}(\ell)
. By the definition of the map
\phi_{\ell}
and
q_{0}:=1
, we have

q_{\pi}:=\prod_{B\in\pi}q_{|B|}=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}q_{s_{j}(\pi)}.

Because of the bijection between
\text{NC}(\ell)
and
\mathcal{S}(\ell)
, we have

\sum_{\pi\in\text{NC}(\ell)}\prod_{B\in\pi}q_{|B|}=\sum_{(s_{1},\ldots,s_{\ell})\in\mathcal{S}(\ell)}\ \prod_{j=1}^{\ell}q_{s_{j}},

(80)

which completes the proof.
∎

where the sequence of functions

The above iterations can be written into an OAMP algorithm as defined in Definition

by introducing intermediate variables

and properly re-indexing the iterates. The only subtle point here is that the functions

B

(\widehat{P}_{t,i}(\lambda))_{1\leq t,1\leq i\leq t}
by
({P}_{t,i}(\lambda))_{1\leq t,1\leq i\leq t}

(119)

B
applies. Here, the empirical divergences

are replaced by their limits
{\bm{\mathsf{\Phi}}}_{t}:=\underset{N\to\infty}{\text{plim}}\ \widehat{\bm{\mathsf{\Phi}}}_{t}
.
(Note that the above limit holds by an inductive argument: we assume
\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{\Phi}}}_{t}\overset{\mathbb{P}}{\to}{\bm{\mathsf{\Phi}}}_{t}
, then prove the state evolution using our following arguments, and then show
\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{\Phi}}}_{t+1}\overset{\mathbb{P}}{\to}{\bm{\mathsf{\Phi}}}_{t+1}

\mathbb{E}_{\mathsf{\Lambda}\sim\mu}\left[\bm{\mathsf{P}}_{t}(\mathsf{\Lambda})\right]=\bm{0}_{t\times t}

Then, we bound the approximation error between these two versions of OAMP, based on the same arguments used in Appendix
B
. We do not repeat the full argument, but it suffices to show
\|P_{t,i}(\bm{W})-\widehat{P}_{t,i}(\bm{W})\|_{\text{op}}\overset{\mathbb{P}}{\to}0
for all

\widehat{P}_{t,i}(\lambda)
and
P_{t,i}(\lambda)
are linear combinations of

\displaystyle\widehat{P}_{t,i}(\lambda)

\displaystyle:=\widehat{\alpha}_{t,1}Q_{1}(\lambda)+\widehat{\alpha}_{t,2}Q_{2}(\lambda)+\cdots+\widehat{\alpha}_{t,t}Q_{t}(\lambda),

(120a)

\displaystyle{P}_{t,i}(\lambda)

\displaystyle:={\alpha}_{t,1}Q_{1}(\lambda)+{\alpha}_{t,2}Q_{2}(\lambda)+\cdots+{\alpha}_{t,t}Q_{t}(\lambda).

(120b)

Then, the following holds for all
t\geq 1
and

\displaystyle\|P_{t,i}(\bm{W})-\widehat{P}_{t,i}(\bm{W})\|_{\text{op}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{t}(\alpha_{t,i}-\widehat{\alpha}_{t,i})Q_{i}(\bm{W})\right\|_{\text{op}}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{t}|\alpha_{t,i}-\widehat{\alpha}_{t,i}|\cdot\|Q_{i}(\bm{W})\|_{\text{op}}\overset{\mathbb{P}}{\longrightarrow}0,

(121a)

where the last step is due to the assumption that

is bounded by an

(Q_{i})_{i\geq 1}
are continuous functions (in the current case, polynomials), and
\widehat{\alpha}_{t,i}\overset{\mathbb{P}}{\to}{\alpha}_{t,i},\forall i=1,\ldots,t
(from the fact that
\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{\Phi}}}_{t}\overset{\mathbb{P}}{\to}{\bm{\mathsf{\Phi}}}_{t}

D.2
Reduction of GFOM to RI-AMP-DF

We first recall the definition of generalized first order method (GFOM) introduced in
[
40

11
]
.

Definition 9

(GFOM
[
40
,
11
]
)
.

A generalized first order method (GFOM) generates the iterates
(\bm{x}_{t})_{t\geq 1}
via

\bm{x}_{t}=\bm{W}\phi_{t}(\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{t-1};\bm{a})+\psi_{t}(\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{t-1};\bm{a}),\quad\forall t\geq 1.

(155)

At each iteration, the output
\widehat{\bm{x}}_{t}
is generated by further applying a post-processing:

\widehat{\bm{x}}_{t}=h_{t}\left(\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{t};\bm{a}\right).

(156)

In the above equations, the functions
\phi_{t}:\mathbb{R}^{t-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
,
\psi_{t}:\mathbb{R}^{t-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
and
h_{t}:\mathbb{R}^{t}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
, are all continuously-differentiable and Lipschitz. Further, they all act on the
N
components of their input vectors separately. Moreover, these functions do not depend on the dimension
N
.

For the purpose of establishing a precise reduction result, we make a minor change to the RI-AMP-DF algorithm: we replace the divergence terms and the de-biasing terms by their limiting deterministic equivalents. We call it deterministic RI-AMP-DF.

Definition 10

(Deterministic RI-AMP-DF)
.

Let
\bm{u}_{1}=\bar{\bm{u}}_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and generate
(\bm{r}_{t})_{t\geq 1}
through

\displaystyle\bm{r}_{t}

\displaystyle=\bm{W}\bm{u}_{t}-\left(\mathsf{c}_{t,1}\bar{\bm{u}}_{1}+\mathsf{c}_{t,2}\bar{\bm{u}}_{2}+\cdots+\mathsf{c}_{t,t}\bar{\bm{u}}_{t}\right),\quad\forall t\geq 1,

(157a)

\displaystyle\bm{u}_{t+1}

\displaystyle=\eta_{t+1}(\bm{r}_{1},\ldots,\bm{r}_{t}),

(157b)

\displaystyle\bar{\bm{u}}_{t+1}

\displaystyle=\eta_{t+1}(\bm{r}_{1},\ldots,\bm{r}_{t})-\left(\mathsf{d}_{t,1}\bm{r}_{1}+\cdots+\mathsf{d}_{t,t}\bm{r}_{t}\right),

(157c)

where, for all
t\geq 1
,
(\mathsf{c}_{t,i})_{1\leq t,1\leq i\leq t}
and
(\mathsf{d}_{t,1})_{1\leq t,1\leq i\leq t}
are deterministic constants that only depends on the function
(\eta_{i})_{i\geq 1}
and the limiting spectrum
\mu
, but not on the dimension
N
.

Following the idea in
[
40
,
11
]
, we show in the following proposition that (deterministic) RI-AMP-DF can implement any GFOM through a proper change of variables. Since RI-AMP-DF is itself a GFOM, this implies that RI-AMP-DF and GFOM belong to the same class of algorithms. It is straightforward to show that the same claim applies to the original RI-AMP algorithm.

Proposition 4

(Deterministic RI-AMP-DF can implement any GFOM)
.

Let
(\bm{x}_{t})_{t\geq 1}
be generated by any GFOM. There exists a deterministic RI-AMP-DF algorithm, whose iterates are denoted as
(\bm{r}_{t})_{t\geq 1}
, and a post-processing function
\varphi_{t}:\mathbb{R}^{t}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
, such that the following holds:

\bm{x}_{t}=\varphi_{t}(\bm{r}_{1},\ldots,\bm{r}_{t};\bm{a}),\quad\forall t\geq 1.

Proof.

The proof is essentially identical to
[
40
, Lemma 4.1]
, and we include it here for completeness. We prove by induction. The claim clearly holds for
t=1
. Suppose it holds up to iteration
t\geq 1
, we prove that it also holds for iteration
t+1
. From (
155
), the new iterate
\bm{x}_{t+1}
of GFOM reads:

\displaystyle\bm{x}_{t+1}

\displaystyle=\bm{W}\phi_{t+1}(\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{t};\bm{a})+\psi_{t+1}(\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{t};\bm{a})

(158a)

\displaystyle\overset{\text{\tiny{(a)}}}{=}\bm{W}\phi_{t+1}\left(\varphi_{1}(\bm{r}_{1};\bm{a}),\ldots,\varphi_{t}(\bm{r}_{\leq t};\bm{a});\bm{a}\right)+\psi_{t+1}\left(\varphi_{1}(\bm{r}_{1};\bm{a}),\ldots,\varphi_{t}(\bm{r}_{\leq t};\bm{a});\bm{a}\right)

(158b)

\displaystyle\overset{\text{\tiny{(b)}}}{=}\bm{W}\eta_{t+1}(\bm{r}_{\leq t};\bm{a})+\psi_{t+1}\left(\varphi_{1}(\bm{r}_{1};\bm{a}),\ldots,\varphi_{t}(\bm{r}_{\leq t};\bm{a});\bm{a}\right)

(158c)

\displaystyle\overset{\text{\tiny{(c)}}}{=}\underbrace{\bm{W}\bm{u}_{t+1}-\sum_{i=1}^{t}\mathsf{c}_{t,i}\bar{\bm{u}}_{i}}_{\bm{r}_{t+1}}+\sum_{i=1}^{t}\mathsf{c}_{t,i}\bar{\bm{u}}_{i}+\psi_{t+1}\left(\varphi_{1}(\bm{r}_{1};\bm{a}),\ldots,\varphi_{t}(\bm{r}_{\leq t};\bm{a});\bm{a}\right)

(158d)

\displaystyle\overset{\text{\tiny{(d)}}}{=}\varphi_{t+1}(\bm{r}_{1},\ldots,\bm{r}_{t+1};\bm{a}),

(158e)

where

•

Step (a) is from the induction hypothesis;

•

Step (b) is from the definition of the new denoising function
\eta_{t+1}
for deterministic RI-AMP-DF;

•

Step (c) is from the definition of the new iterate
\bm{r}_{t+1}
in RI-AMP-DF. Note that
(\mathsf{c}_{t,i})_{1\leq t,1\leq i\leq t}
are deterministic constants that depend on the denoising functions in previous iterations and the spectrum
\mu

•

Step (d) is a definition of the new post-processing function
\varphi_{t+1}

The proof is now complete.

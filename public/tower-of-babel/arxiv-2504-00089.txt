On the Gorensteiness of string algebras Thanks: MSC2020: 16G10, 16E45. Thanks: Key words: string algebra; self-injective dimension; effective intersecting relation; Gorenstein algebra
Houjun Zhang, Dajun Liu, Yu-Zhe Liu

Abstract
. In this paper, we give a description of the self-injective dimension of string algebras and obtain a necessary and sufficient condition for a string algebra to be Gorenstein.



1.
Introduction

Throughout this paper
\mathds{k}
is a field. Let
A
be a finite-dimensional
\mathds{k}
-algebra. Recall that
A
is called a Gorenstein algebra if
A
has finite injective dimension both as a left and a right
A
-module. Gorenstein algebra
arises
from commutative ring theory and
plays
a central role in
the
representation theory of finite-dimensional algebras. Being Gorenstein algebra has nice properties, such as:
if
A
is Gorenstein, then the bounded homology categories
K^{b}(\mathrm{proj}A)
of projectives and
K^{b}(\mathrm{inj}A)
of injectives are coincide. Moreover, Gorenstein algebra is preserved under derived equivalence
[
9
]
.

It is well known that the self-injective algebras and the algebras of finite global dimension are Gorenstein. Recently, Geiß and Retein showed that gentle algebras are Gorenstein
[
8
]
; they also pointed out that skewed-gentle algebras
considered
in
[
6
]
are Gorenstein if the field is not of characteristic 2. Gentle algebras introduced by Assem and Skowroński
[
4
]
form a particular class of string algebras. However, there are examples showed that string algebras are not Gorenstein. In this paper, we study when a string algebra is Gorenstein.

String algebras were introduced by Butler and Ringel in
[
5
]
. They
gave
an explicit description of the finite-dimensional indecomposable modules and the irreducible maps between them and provided a method to draw the Auslander-Reiten quivers of string algebras. In order to study the Gorensteinness of string algebra, we first give a description of the self-injective dimension of string algebra by using the description of the finite-dimensional indecomposable modules in
[
5
]
.

Let
A
be a finite-dimensional
\mathds{k}
-algebra. We denote by
\mathrm{inj.dim}({{}_{A}A})
and
\mathrm{inj.dim}(A_{A})
the left injective dimension and the right injective dimension of
A
respectively.

By
[
7
, Corollary 2.4]
, we have that the finitistic dimension conjecture holds true for string algebras since any string algebra is monomial.
And so, the Gorenstein symmetry conjecture holds true for string algebras by
[
10
]
.
In
[
2
, Proposition 6.10]
, Auslander and Reiten have proved that if
\mathrm{inj.dim}({A_{A}})<\infty
, then
\mathrm{inj.dim}({{}_{A}A})<\infty
if and only if the finitistic dimension of
A
is finite.
It follows that
\mathrm{inj.dim}({{}_{A}A})=\mathrm{inj.dim}({A_{A}})
since an algebra is a string algebra if and only if so is its opposite algebra.

Thus, in this paper, we consider the right
A
-modules and the right injective dimension of
A
. For simplicity, we denote by
\mathrm{inj.dim}({A})=\mathrm{inj.dim}({A_{A}})=\mathrm{inj.dim}({{}_{A}A})
.

Now assume that
\mathcal{Q}=(\mathcal{Q}_{0},\mathcal{Q}_{1},\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{t})
is a finite quiver, where
\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{t}:\mathcal{Q}_{1}\to\mathcal{Q}_{0}
are
two functions sending arrow
\alpha\in\mathcal{Q}_{1}
to its starting point
\mathfrak{s}(\alpha)
and ending point
\mathfrak{t}(\alpha)
.
Let
A=\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}/\mathcal{I}
be a string algebra (see Definition
2.2
).
To compute the injective dimension of
A
, we define the intersecting relations for a string algebra.
Notice that the injective dimension of
A
is determined by some intersecting relations.
Thus, we introduce ELIS the effective left (and right) intersecting relations
with respect to a projective module (see Definition
3.9
).
Denote by
P(v_{0})
the projective module of vertex
v_{0}
in
\mathcal{Q}
.
Let
\{r_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}=\{r_{n},\ldots,r_{1}\}
be an ELIS with respect to
P(v_{0})

and define
\mathfrak{l}(\{r_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n})=n
as the length of
\{r_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
.
Furthermore, the length of
\mathrm{ELIS}(P(v_{0}))
can be define as:

\mathfrak{l}(\mathrm{ELIS}(P(v_{0}))):=\begin{cases}\sup\limits_{\{r_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\in\mathrm{ELIS}(P(v_{0}))}\mathfrak{l}(\{r_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}),&\text{ if $\mathrm{ELIS}(P(v_{0}))\neq\varnothing$; }\\
\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad 0,&\text{ if $\mathrm{ELIS}(P(v_{0}))=\varnothing$. }\end{cases}

Then we obtain the main result of this paper:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
A=\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}/\mathcal{I}
be a string algebra. If
A
is not self-injective, then

\mathrm{inj.dim}A=\sup_{v_{0}\in\mathcal{Q}_{0}}\mathfrak{l}(\mathrm{ELIS}(P(v_{0})))+1.

According to the above Theorem, we immediately obtain a necessary and sufficient condition for a string algebra to be Gorenstein.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
A=\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}/\mathcal{I}
be a string algebra. Then
A
is Gorenstein if and only if all
\mathrm{ELISs}
of
P(v_{0})
have a supremum for any vertex
v_{0}
in
\mathcal{Q}_{0}
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we recall some preliminaries on string algebras. Section 3 is devoted to study the cosyzygies of indecomposable modules for string algebras. Based on the injective decomposition of projective modules, we define the effective left and right intersecting relations with respect to a projective module. In the last Section, we give the proof of the main results.

Definition 2.3

3.
The cosyzygy of indecomposable projective modules

In this section, we give a description of cosyzygies of indecomposable projective modules for string algebras.
Let
A=\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}/\mathcal{I}
be a string algebra and
M
an
A
-module.
Recall that the injective dimension
\mathrm{inj.dim}M
of
M
is less than or equal to
n
if
there exists an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow M\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{f_{0}}E_{0}\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{f_{1}}E_{1}\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{f_{2}}\cdots\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{f_{n-1}}E_{n}\longrightarrow 0,

where
E_{i}

(0\leq i\leq n)
is an injective module. The
self-injective dimension
of the finite-dimensional algebra

is the injective dimension
\mathrm{inj.dim}A
of
A_{A}\in\mathsf{mod}A
.

For any module
M
in
\mathsf{mod}A
, assume that
e^{M}_{0}:M\to\mathds{E}_{0}(M)
is the injective envelope of
M
.
Then
e^{M}_{i+1}:\mathds{E}_{i}(M)\to\mathds{E}_{i+1}(M)
is induced by the injective envelope

e^{\mathrm{coker}(e^{M}_{i})}_{0}:\mathrm{coker}(e^{M}_{i})\to\mathds{E}_{0}(\mathrm{coker}(e^{M}_{i})),

for any
i\geq 0
, where
\mathrm{coker}(e^{M}_{i})=\mathds{E}_{i}(M)/\mathrm{im}(e^{M}_{i})
.
We call
\mathrm{coker}(e^{M}_{i})
the
(i+1)
th-cosyzygy
of
M
and denote it by
\mho_{i+1}(M)
.

3.1.

We want to compute the self-injective dimension of string algebra.
To do this, we need to study the cosyzygy of indecomposable projective module.

4.
Proof of the main results

In this section, we give a proof of the main results. Let
A=\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}/\mathcal{I}
be a string algebra. Assume that
\{r_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}=\{r_{n},\ldots,r_{1}\}
is an ELIS of projective module
P(v_{0})
for vertex
v_{0}
in
\mathcal{Q}
. We define the length of
\mathrm{ELIS}(P(v_{0}))
as:

\mathfrak{l}(\mathrm{ELIS}(P(v_{0}))):=\begin{cases}\sup\limits_{\{r_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\in\mathrm{ELIS}(P(v_{0}))}\mathfrak{l}(\{r_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}),&\text{ if $\mathrm{ELIS}(P(v_{0}))\neq\varnothing$; }\\
\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad 0,&\text{ if $\mathrm{ELIS}(P(v_{0}))=\varnothing$. }\end{cases}

Lemma 4.1
.

If
P(v_{0})
has an
\mathrm{ELIS}

\{r_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}=(r_{n},\ldots,r_{1})
, then
\mho_{n+1}(P(v_{0}))\neq 0
.

Proof.

Keep the notations from
Figure
3.7
,

the proof is divided into two cases as follows:

(1)

v_{0}
is a vertex of the Type
((\leq 2)^{\mathrm{in}},2^{\mathrm{out}})
;

(2)

v_{0}
is a vertex of the Type
((\leq 2)^{\mathrm{in}},(\leq 1)^{\mathrm{out}})
.

In (1),
\mathrm{soc}P(v_{0})
is a direct sum of two simple modules
S(\mathfrak{t}(s_{c}))
and
S(\mathfrak{t}(s_{d}))
.

We assume that
v_{0}
is a vertex of the Type
(2^{\mathrm{in}},2^{\mathrm{out}})

(the cases of
(1^{\mathrm{in}},2^{\mathrm{out}})
and
(0^{\mathrm{in}},2^{\mathrm{out}})
is similar).

Then
\ell_{\mathrm{lu}},\ell_{\mathrm{ru}},\ell_{\mathrm{ld}},\ell_{\mathrm{rd}}\geq 1
.
Let
s_{a}=a_{\ell_{\mathrm{lu}}}\cdots a_{1}
,
s_{b}=b_{\ell_{\mathrm{ru}}}\cdots b_{1}
,

s_{c}=c_{1}\cdots c_{\ell_{\mathrm{ld}}}
and
s_{d}=d_{1}\cdots d_{\ell_{\mathrm{rd}}}
.
Then
\mathds{M}^{-1}(P(v_{0}))=s_{c}^{-1}s_{d}
. By Proposition
3.2
,
\mho_{1}(P(v_{0}))\cong D_{\mathrm{L}}\oplus D\oplus D_{\mathrm{R}}
. Thus, we have the following two cases:

(1.1)

all relations in
\{r_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
are on some left maximal path in

\mathrm{LMP}(s_{a}s_{d})
or
\mathrm{LMP}(s_{b}s_{c})
.

(1.2)

all relations in
\{r_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
are on some left maximal path in

\mathrm{LMP}(\wp_{\mathrm{L}})
or
\mathrm{LMP}(\wp_{\mathrm{R}})
.

Next, we prove subcase (1.1) by induction on the length of ELIS of
P(v_{0})
.
Without loss of generality,
let
\widehat{p}=\widehat{s_{a}s_{d}}\in\mathrm{LMP}(s_{a}s_{d})
, and

assume that all
r_{i}
are
subpaths
of
\widehat{p}
. Then
r_{1}=r^{\mathrm{u}}_{\widehat{p}}(v_{\mathrm{rd}}^{(\ell_{\mathrm{rd}})})
. By Proposition
3.2
,
0\neq D\leq_{\oplus}\mho_{1}(P(v_{0}))
.
Now assume that
P(v_{0})
has an ELIS
(r_{2},r_{1})
. Then
r_{2}=r^{\mathrm{u}}_{\widehat{p}}(v_{0})
and
v_{0}
is a vertex on
r_{1}
.
Since
v_{0}
is not
gently relational
,
D
is not injective again by Proposition
3.2
.
We obtain
0\neq\mho_{1}(D)\leq_{\oplus}\mho_{2}(P(v_{0}))
.
By Lemma
3.3
, we have

\mho_{1}(D)\cong\mathds{M}(s_{1}^{\prime})\oplus\mathds{M}(s_{1}^{\prime\prime})
,

where
s_{1}^{\prime}
and
s_{1}^{\prime\prime}
are directed strings.

If
P(v_{0})
has an ELIS
(r_{3},r_{2},r_{1})
on
\widehat{p}
,
that is,
n\geq 3
, then one of
s_{1}^{\prime}
and
s_{1}^{\prime\prime}
can be seen as a subpath of
\widehat{p}
,
see Figure
4.1
.

Figure 4.1.
\mathds{E}(P(v_{0}))\cong E(v_{\mathrm{ld}}^{(\ell_{\mathrm{ld}})})\oplus E(v_{\mathrm{rd}}^{(\ell_{\mathrm{rd}})})

Let
s_{1}^{\prime}
be a subpath of
\widehat{p}
, then we have
\mathrm{soc}\mathds{M}(s_{1}^{\prime})\cong S(\mathfrak{s}(r_{1}))=S(x)\neq 0
. Thus,

0\neq\mathds{M}(s_{1}^{\prime})\leq_{\oplus}\mho_{1}(D)\leq_{\oplus}\mho_{2}(P(v_{0})).

The injective envelope of
\mathds{M}(s_{1}^{\prime})
is

e_{0}^{\mathds{M}(s_{1}^{\prime})}:\mathds{M}(s_{1}^{\prime})\to\mathds{E}(\mathrm{soc}\mathds{M}(s_{1}^{\prime}))\cong E(\mathfrak{s}(r_{1})),

\mathrm{coker}(e_{0}^{\mathds{M}(s_{1}^{\prime})})\cong\mathds{M}(s_{2}^{\prime})\oplus\mathds{M}(s_{2}^{\prime\prime}),

and

If

or any

in this case.

By Proposition

,
\mathds{M}(s_{2}^{\prime})

(4.1)

\displaystyle 0\neq\mathds{M}(s_{2}^{\prime})\leq_{\oplus}\mathrm{coker}(e_{0}^{\mathds{M}(s_{1}^{\prime})})\leq_{\oplus}\mho_{2}(D)\leq_{\oplus}\mho_{3}(P(v_{0})).

) holds and further

Repeating the steps as above, we can obtain an ELIS

of

and a non-zero direct summand of

by induction.

In the subcase (1.2), we need to compute the cosyzygies of

and

Here,

and prove the other cases of this lemma.

∎

Now we can give our main result.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
A=\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}/\mathcal{I}
be a string algebra.

(1)

If
A
is not self-injective, then

\mathrm{inj.dim}A=\sup_{v\in\mathcal{Q}_{0}}\mathfrak{l}(\mathrm{ELIS}(P(v)))+1.

(2)

A
is Gorenstein if and only if all ELISs of
P(v)
have finite length for any vertex
v
in
\mathcal{Q}_{0}
,

Proof.

For any vertex
v
in
\mathcal{Q}
, we claim that

\mathrm{inj.dim}P(v)=\begin{cases}\mathfrak{l}(\mathrm{ELIS}(P(v)))+1,&\text{ if $P(v)$ is non-injective; }\\
\quad\quad\quad\quad\ 0,&\text{ if $P(v)$ is injective. }\end{cases}

In this case, it is clear that if
A
is not self-injective, then

\mathrm{inj.dim}A=\sup_{v\in\mathcal{Q}_{0}}\mathfrak{l}(\mathrm{ELIS}(P(v)))+1.

Moreover,
A
is Gorenstein if and only if all ELISs of
P(v)
have finite length. Now we prove the claim. If
\mathfrak{l}(\mathrm{ELIS}(P(v)))=\infty
,
then there exists a sequence
(\ldots,r_{2},r_{1})
of relations such that for any
N\geq 1
,
\{r_{i}\}_{N\geq i\geq 1}
is an ELIS of
P(v)
.
By Lemma
4.1
,
\mho_{N+1}(P(v))\neq 0
.
Thus, we have
\mathrm{inj.dim}P(v)=\infty
.

If
\mathfrak{l}(\mathrm{ELIS}(P(v)))=n<\infty
, let
\{r_{i}\}_{n\geq i\geq 1}
be an ELIS with length
n
.
then
r_{n}
is a relation in
\mathfrak{R}_{\widehat{p}}^{\mathrm{u}}(v)
,
where
\widehat{p}
is some left maximal path such that all relations in
\{r_{i}\}_{n\geq i\geq 1}
are paths on the
\widehat{p}
.
Since there exists no relation
r_{n+1}
such that
\{r_{i}\}_{n+1\geq i\geq 1}
is an ELIS of
P(v)
,
we have
\mho_{n+1}(P(v))
is a non-zero injective module
by Propositions
3.11
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

Let
A=\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}/\mathcal{I}
be a gentle algebra
[
3
]
and
(r_{n},\ldots,r_{1})
be an ELIS with respect to indecomposable projective
A
-module
P(v)
.
Then
all
r_{i}
are relations of length two, and

r_{i+1}
effective left-intersecting with
r_{i}
are always intersection relations of type (II) in Remark
3.14
for any
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{2}}\leq i\leq n-1
.

Geiß and Reiten proved that gentle algebras are Gorenstein in
[
8
]
. Recently, Amiot, Plamondon, and Schroll
[
1
]
gave a characterization of the silting objects of the derived category of gentle algebras in terms of graded curves. According to this characterization, they
gave new proof that gentle algebras are Gorenstein
. By using the effective left-intersecting sequence, we can also
obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 4.4

(​
[
8
]
)
.

Gentle algebras are Gorenstein.

Proof.

Let
A=\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}/\mathcal{I}
be a gentle algebra and
(\alpha_{n+1}\alpha_{n},\ldots,\alpha_{2}\alpha_{1})
be an ELIS with respect to indecomposable projective
A
-module
P(v)
.
Next, we prove that all relations are not on any full relational oriented cycle, where a full relational oriented cycle is an oriented cycle
C=a_{0}a_{1}\cdots a_{l-1}
such that
a_{\overline{i}}a_{\overline{i+1}}\in\mathcal{I}
(
\overline{i}
is
i
modulo
l
).

Indeed, if
\alpha_{i+1}\alpha_{i}
is a relation on
C
,
then
\alpha_{i-1}
is an arrow on
C
. Otherwise, by the definition of gentle algebra, we have
\alpha_{i}\alpha_{i-1}\notin\mathcal{I}
.
Thus, by induction, we obtain that
\alpha_{n+1}\alpha_{n}
,
\ldots
,
\alpha_{2}\alpha_{1}
are relations on
C

and
v={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\mathfrak{t}(\alpha_{2})=\mathfrak{s}(\alpha_{1})}}
is a vertex on
C
. In other words,
\mathds{M}^{-1}(P(v))={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{p^{-1}\alpha_{1}}}
,
where
p
is a path starting at
v
such that
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\alpha_{2}p}}\notin\mathcal{I}
.
We have the following two cases:

(1)

If the length of
p
is greater than or equal to
1
, then
\alpha_{3}\alpha_{2}
does not
satisfy
the condition given in processes (a) and (b).

(2)

If the length of
p
is zero, then
\mathds{M}^{-1}(P(v))={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\alpha_{1}}}
is injective, and then
\alpha_{3}\alpha_{2}
does not
satisfy
the condition given in processes (a) and (b).

Therefore,
(\alpha_{3}\alpha_{2},\alpha_{2}\alpha_{1})
is not a effective intersection relations,
this is a contradiction.

Furthermore, if there is an arrow
\alpha_{n+2}
such that
\alpha_{n+2}\alpha_{n+1}\in\mathcal{I}
,
then we obtain that
(\alpha_{n+2}\alpha_{n+1},\ldots,\alpha_{2}\alpha_{1})
is also an ELIS of
P(v)
and
\alpha_{n+2}\alpha_{n+1}
is not a relation on any full relational cycle.
Since
\mathcal{Q}
is a finite quiver, the lengths of all ELISs of
P(v)

have
a supremum.
Thus, all ELISs of
P(v)
have a supremum. Then, by Theorem
4.2
(2),
A
is Gorenstein.
∎

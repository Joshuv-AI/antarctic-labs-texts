Spontaneous intradural extramedullary hematoma after mild exercise in Von Willebrand disease: A rare clinical presentation and literature review
Authors: Ali Bukhari, Apostolos Lagaras, Muhammad Dherijha, Asad Malik, Abbas Shahid
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background: Von Willebrand disease (VWD) is the most common inherited bleeding disorder. Spinal intradural extramedullary hematoma (SIEH) is an exceptionally rare manifestation.Case Description: A 28-year-old woman with VWD developed spontaneous SIEH following mild exercise, presenting with back pain, progressive lower limb weakness, and double incontinence. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) demonstrated a T3/4 intradural extramedullary hematoma. Urgent surgical evacuation through T3/4 fenestration was performed, followed by targeted hemostatic therapy with recombinant von Willebrand factor (Vonicog Alfa). The patient made a complete neurological recovery within 3 months and remained asymptomatic at 14-month follow-up.Conclusion: SIEH should be considered in VWD patients presenting with acute spinal symptoms, even without trauma. Early MRI, prompt decompression, and tailored coagulation management are critical to optimal outcomes.

INTRODUCTION

Von Willebrand disease (VWD) is the most common inherited bleeding disorder. While mucocutaneous bleeding is typical, spontaneous intraspinal hemorrhage is exceedingly rare.[2,4] Spinal intradural extramedullary hematoma (SIEH) represents a neurosurgical emergency due to the risk of irreversible neurological injury. The most reported cases are associated with trauma or anticoagulation. We report a rare case of SIEH following mild exercise in a young woman with VWD and review previously reported cases.

CASE REPORT

A 28-year-old woman with a known history of VWD presented with acute back pain after mild physical exercise consisting of stretching and push-ups. Within 24 h, she developed rapidly progressive bilateral lower limb weakness associated with urinary and fecal incontinence.

Neurological examination demonstrated reduced sensation below the T10 dermatome, absent anal tone, and bilateral lower limb weakness (3/5), which progressed to complete paraplegia before surgical intervention. Urgent magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the spine revealed a T3/4 intradural extramedullary hematoma causing significant spinal cord compression [Figure 1].

(a-d) Preoperative magnetic resonance imaging sagittal and axial views demonstrating the intradural extramedullary hematoma at T3/4 causing significant spinal cord compression.

Emergency right-sided T3/4 fenestration with intradural hematoma evacuation was performed [Figure 2]. The epidural space was normal, and intraoperative ultrasound confirmed the intradural location of the hematoma. No spinal instrumentation was required, and the dura was repaired primarily.

(a-d) Intraoperative images demonstrating T3/4 fenestration and evacuation of the intradural hematoma.

Postoperatively, the patient received recombinant von Willebrand factor (Vonicog Alfa) replacement therapy. Neurological improvement was observed within 24 h. At 3 months, the patient had achieved complete neurological recovery. At 14-month follow-up, she remained neurologically intact and fully ambulatory without recurrence.

Patients typically present with acute spinal pain followed by rapidly progressive neurological deterioration. In the present case, mild physical activity preceded symptom onset, with rapid progression to paraplegia and sphincter dysfunction. The absence of trauma or anticoagulant use suggests that impaired primary hemostasis and vascular fragility inherent to VWD may predispose to spontaneous intraspinal hemorrhage.[5,6]

MRI is the diagnostic modality of choice and allows prompt identification of intradural collections causing spinal cord compression.[7] Early diagnosis is critical, as delayed intervention is associated with poor neurological outcomes. In cases with significant neurological compromise, urgent surgical decompression remains the standard of care. Intraoperative ultrasound may be useful in confirming intradural pathology when the epidural space is normal.[8-10]

Postoperative management requires close hematological collaboration. Targeted replacement therapy with recombinant von Willebrand factor was effective in achieving hemostasis and preventing rebleeding in this case. The patient achieved complete neurological recovery, consistent with prior reports demonstrating favorable outcomes following timely decompression.

This case adds to the limited literature on SIEH associated with inherited bleeding disorders and highlights the need for heightened clinical suspicion in VWD patients presenting with acute spinal symptoms, even in the absence of trauma.

CONCLUSION

Spontaneous SIEH is a rare but serious complication of VWD. Prompt MRI, urgent surgical decompression, and targeted coagulation replacement therapy can result in complete neurological recovery. Awareness of this entity is essential when VWD patients present with acute spinal symptoms.

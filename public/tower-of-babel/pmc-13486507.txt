A fractal-enhanced deep learning framework for forecasting agricultural production in India
Authors: Shamli Sharma, Kuldip Katiyar, Mohammad Sajid
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractForecasting agricultural production in India is highly challenging due to non-stationary dynamics driven by policy factors and climate volatility. This study investigates the effectiveness of the fractal interpolation function (FIF) (with both variable and constant scaling factors) as a data augmentation technique to improve the prediction accuracy of long short-term memory (LSTM) and bidirectional LSTM (Bi-LSTM) models. These models were used to predict the annual production of four major crops in India (Millet, Maize (corn), Wheat, and Rice) from 1961 to 2023. The FIF with a variable scaling factor produced a lower Hurst exponent (ranging from 0.777 to 0.875) and a correspondingly higher fractal dimension (ranging from 1.153 to 1.574) than the FIF with a constant scaling factor. This resulted in a rougher interpolation that more effectively captured the non-smooth features and local volatility of the time series. The experimental results demonstrate that models trained on fractal-interpolated data significantly outperformed those trained on the original data. The LSTM integrated with variable-scaling FIF achieved the best overall performance for three crops, recording the highest values and the lowest error metrics. Notably, it achieved MAPE values of 5.06% (Millet), 1.95% (Maize), and 1.70% (Wheat). However, for Rice, the Bi-LSTM integrated with variable-scaling FIF delivered the best performance, yielding MAPE = 1.39% on the test set (2011–2023). These improvements were further confirmed by the Wilcoxon and Diebold–Mariano tests. The findings of this study highlight that capturing the long-memory and self-similar characteristics of agricultural time series through fractal interpolation substantially improves the learning capabilities of recurrent neural networks. The choice of scaling approach proved to be a more dominant factor for performance than the choice between LSTM and Bi-LSTM. This framework underscores the importance of adaptive fractal interpolation for handling irregular agricultural time series and offers a promising tool for enhanced yield forecasting, especially for rain-dependent and climate-sensitive crops in India.Keywords: Crop production prediction, Fractal interpolation, Long short-term memory, Bidirectional-long short-term memory, Forecasting, Deep learning, Time seriesSubject terms: Climate sciences, Environmental sciences, Mathematics and computing, Plant sciences

Introduction

Agriculture has always been the backbone of the Indian economy and serves as the primary source of food security. It is also a major contributor to livelihoods and employment for a large section of the population. India is one of the largest producers of various key crops such as wheat, rice, and maize. The agricultural sector plays a crucial role in sustaining India’s economic growth and food requirements. It employs more than fifty percent of the workforce and accounts for approximately seventeen to eighteen percent of the country’s GDP, while consuming almost seventy percent of the nation’s freshwater resources1. Accurate prediction of crop production is crucial for ensuring sustainable resource management, effective agricultural policymaking, and global food security2. With the increasing impacts of extreme weather events, climate change, and market volatility, classical statistical models often struggle to capture the nonlinear, complex, and fractal features inherent in agricultural time series data. In recent years, deep learning models such as LSTM and Bi-LSTM have shown promising results in time series prediction. However, the performance of these models is frequently limited by a lack of high-quality historical data, especially for annual production records.

To address this limitation, data augmentation techniques have gained significant attention. Among these techniques, Fractal Interpolation Functions (FIFs) offer a robust method for generating synthetic data points while preserving the long memory and self-similarity properties of the original time series. FIFs can preserve the scaling behavior and fractal features of agricultural data by carefully choosing vertical scaling factors—something not possible with traditional interpolation techniques. To improve annual crop production prediction, a hybrid framework that integrates FIF as a data augmentation technique with deep learning networks (LSTM and Bi-LSTM) has been developed. In this study, two variants of FIF: one with variable vertical scaling and the other with constant vertical scaling are implemented and compared. The effectiveness of both approaches is evaluated using the Hurst exponent and the fractal dimension to ensure that the augmented series maintains the original long-range dependence and temporal complexity.

This methodology is applied to the annual production data of four major crops (Wheat, Rice, Millet, and Maize (corn)) in India. This study aims to improve the training robustness of LSTM and Bi-LSTM networks and enhance overall forecasting accuracy by augmenting the limited historical data using FIF while preserving the key fractal properties. The aims of this research are threefold: (1) to evaluate the effectiveness of FIF with variable scaling as a data augmentation technique for improving the prediction accuracy of LSTM and Bi-LSTM networks in annual crop production forecasting; (2) to compare the performance of variable and constant vertical scaling factors in preserving the fractal dimension and Hurst exponent of the original crop production data; and (3) to provide a detailed analysis of the proposed approach across the four crops. The findings of this study not only offer methodological insights but also hold practical relevance for decision-makers in agriculture, supporting more informed planning for food security and resource management. This study contributes to the growing body of literature on fractal-based hybrid deep learning models and data augmentation techniques in agricultural forecasting. The organization of this paper is as follows: “Literature survey” presents a detailed review of the literature on FIF methods, crop production forecasting, and deep learning applications in agriculture. “Methodology” describes the proposed methodology, including data preparation, FIF with constant and variable scaling, and the hybrid LSTM and Bi-LSTM models, along with the data used in this research. A comparative analysis and discussion of the experimental results are provided in “Conclusion”, followed by the future research directions.

Literature survey

In India, agriculture is the major source of livelihood for a large proportion of the population. Therefore, predicting crop production is crucial for planning agricultural policies, ensuring food security, and managing resources effectively. Crop yield prediction has always been a critical and complex task because crop production depends on various dynamic factors such as temperature, rainfall, farming practices, and soil quality. Classical models like linear regression and ARIMA have been widely used for forecasting crop production. However, these models often fail to capture the non-stationary and non-linear patterns inherent in agricultural data, especially when the dataset is incomplete or small. For instance, Padhan3 applied the ARIMA model to forecast 34 agricultural products in India. He concluded that although the ARIMA model gave credible predictions for few crops, its performance was considerably reduced when macro environment and other exogenous variables were ignored. From his study, it emerges that the accuracy of predictions from ARIMA is highly dependent on whether there is constancy of external environment and this is quite rare in agricultural data. Likewise, Alam et al.4 used the ARIMA model (2, 1, 0) to forecast rice yield in Aligarh district of Uttar Pradesh. But, BDS test (p < 0.01) suggested that the residuals were not independent and identical. Therefore, an Artificial Neural Network (ANN) was used to fit the residual component of ARIMA. It led to better forecasting by decreasing the value of MAPE from 17.68% in the pure ARIMA to 4.65% in the hybrid one. The hybrid model successfully captures the nonlinearity feature and enhances the prediction accuracy. In addition, Sharma et al.5 analyzed regression based approaches such as RFR, MARS and MLR for forecasting crop yield. The results showed that MLR performed better for maize, while MARS produced superior results for wheat and rice. These findings underscore the importance of data mining techniques in agricultural decision-making.

Recently, deep learning approaches such as LSTM and Bi-LSTM algorithms have been widely used in modeling time series owing to their capability of capturing long-range dependencies. Hochreiter and Schmidhuber6 first introduced the LSTM network in 1997 to solve the vanishing gradient problem in classical recurrent neural networks (RNNs). At the same time, the Bi-LSTM architecture was proposed by Schuster and Paliwal7. The bidirectional processing of the data sequence by the model helps in considering context information from both ends. The bi-directional nature of the model makes it widely applicable in time-series prediction, where it generally gives higher accuracy compared to the conventional statistical models. For instance, Iskanda et al.10 adopted GRU, LSTM, and Bi-LSTM algorithms to forecast several ecological features (LST, NDVI, and Forest Cover Density) in an urban forest environment using remote sensing images. According to their analysis, Bi-LSTM showed superiority over others in all cases, scoring , , and for LST, NDVI, and FCD respectively. Natarajan et al.11 used LSTM, Bi-LSTM, Stacked LSTM, and Stacked Bi-LSTM to forecast the Standardized Precipitation Index (SPI) at multiple timescales using 120 years of rainfall data. The findings of their study suggest that Bi-LSTM and Stacked Bi-LSTM outperformed LSTM and Stacked LSTM, particularly at longer timescales (SPI-12 to SPI-24), achieving . Both studies support the superior capability of Bi-LSTM in capturing long-term dependencies. The biggest problem with DL algorithms is that they need a lot of high-quality training data. In the field of agriculture, this is still a big problem, particularly in countries such as India where there is less data, which is often inaccurate and scattered. There are many solutions that can be used to solve this problem. For instance, Haider et al.8 developed a new hybrid forecast algorithm by combining LSTM and Robust-LOWESS filtering, which eliminated the outliers and significantly improved the values of MAE, RMSE, and correlation coefficient compared to the previous method. Likewise, Kumar et al.9 proved that an optimized Bi-LSTM algorithm worked better than classic LSTM, SVR, and ARIMA in predicting the yield of crops of groundnut, barley, and wheat in India.

Moreover, to augment the capabilities of deep learning (DL) models, Khaki et al.12 presented a hybrid CNN-RNN model based on incorporating soil, management, and weather data for yield prediction in the U.S. Corn Belt region. This approach exceeded conventional methods such as Random Forest and LASSO regarding accuracy, along with providing better model interpretability using feature selection with backpropagation. To deal with insufficient amounts of data, Verma et al.13 incorporated synthesized data into the model using LSTM. Furthermore, another hybrid approach that combines deep learning and process-based models in crop prediction was suggested by Jeong et al.14. Moreover, Kinabalu15 used CNN-LSTM models to incorporate the benefits of CNN in feature extraction from space-time sequences and LSTMs in forecasting. They achieved 74% prediction accuracy in predicting crops yields. Another interesting approach to increase accuracy in modeling of time series data can be attributed to Fractal Interpolation (FI). It is a mathematical approach based on modeling of self-similar and irregular objects. The name “fractal” was invented by the prominent mathematician Benoît Mandelbrot16 in 1975 to refer to objects with certain degree of similarity and complexity. FI formalization came a little later after Barnsley17 proposed iterated function systems as a technique to model irregular data. He extended his work further in 198718, broadening the scope and availability of fractal concepts in various disciplines.

Since then, the technique has found numerous applications in medicine, engineering, environmental science, and agriculture due to the capabilities of fractal interpolation to reflect chaotic processes that occur in nature. When it comes to time series prediction, FI is highly suitable for modeling non-linear patterns. FI has proven useful for creating artificial series and filling gaps, but when it comes to predicting agricultural products based on time series, it has not been explored in connection with DL models. The influence of vertical scaling factors in the IFS process needs to be analyzed. The introduction of FI into DL models, such as LSTM and Bi-LSTM, provides an innovative way to forecast time series with complex structures. Regarding short-term wind speed predictions, Xiu et al.19 presented an improved fractal interpolation method. The work focused specifically on time series that exhibited highly fractal nature with a Hurst exponent being close to unity. Their findings also confirmed that fractal interpolation had better accuracy than traditional approaches in their study. Similar studies have been carried out by Easwaramoorthy et al.20, where they performed a comparative analysis of the first and second waves of COVID-19 cases in Brazil, the USA, India, Russia, and the UK based on deaths and infection data per day. In order to do so, Correlation Fractal Dimension (CFD) was used to calculate the death and spread rates of the virus. This study was validated via ANOVA, and the death rate was predicted using ARIMA. Fractals analysis has proved to be an excellent way to analyze nonlinear and complex time series that exhibit self-similarity and long memory behavior. Wang et al.21 combined SVM with enhanced FI in predicting financial outcomes. The authors applied the model to the high-frequency dataset of stock indices and obtained better performance when compared to TFI, SVM, and GARCH(1,1). It was most noticeable for those datasets that showed long-range dependencies and self-similarity, which were confirmed via R/S analysis. Such results increase the relevance of application of fractals in other fields as well. Dai et al.22 suggested a FI-LSTM-based prediction technique to predict Parkinson’s disease incidence and prevalence in various countries. They used three different methods of fractal interpolation of different fractal dimension and scaling factor to reconstruct historical information. Their proposed LSTM-based approach was found superior to the ARIMA-based one. In 2021, Raubitzek and Neubauer23 developed an FI technique to improve the predictive ability of LSTM-based time series forecasting. With an improved prediction result in the process of creating fine-grained interpolated datasets by maintaining complexity features, like fractal dimension and Hurst exponent, the technique was successful especially for agricultural yield datasets. In another work24, they developed a novel complex prediction model using a combination of FI, random parameterization of LSTM ensemble models, and complexity features for forecasting chaotic time series. In their study, they found out that complexity-based filters and interpolated datasets could greatly boost up predictions without much optimization of hyperparameters. Recently, Baicoianu25 came up with three new techniques–Formula Strategy, Closest Values, and Closest Hurst–that helped create datasets that were very similar to the original datasets’ structure. The prediction with the LSTM model trained with fractally-augmented datasets became more accurate when applied to private and public datasets.

Several studies have highlighted the importance of fractal and multi-fractal analysis in understanding agricultural time series. For instance, Bhardwaj et al.26 applied fractal analysis to agricultural parameters and revealed anti-persistent behavior in climate factors and soil properties influencing crop production. Machine learning classifiers, including Random Forest, have achieved high accuracy in crop management decisions based on these parameters. Pachore et al.27 investigated the propagation from meteorological to agricultural drought using Multi-fractal Detrended Fluctuation Analysis (MF-DFA) and the Hurst index across thirty-four Indian subdivisions. Their findings on regional variation and long-term persistence in drought propagation time provide valuable insights into the use of FIF for crop production prediction. Huang et al.28 employed longitudinal fractal analysis (fractal abundance and fractal dimension) on MRI scans to forecast pathological complete response to neoadjuvant chemotherapy in breast cancer. The study achieved strong forecasting performance (AUC0.781–0.807) using delta fractal features. In recent times, Shu and Chan29 emphasized the role of fractal methods in investigating irregularities within wind speed records and recommended the exploration of hybrid models that merge the concepts of fractal and machine learning algorithms. This aligns with the current study, which employs FIF-based data augmentation in LSTM and Bi-LSTM networks to improve agricultural production prediction. Further, Long et al.30 demonstrated that lacunarity and fractal dimension extracted from MRI scans, when integrated with VASARI features, could effectively predict molecular characteristics (TERTp mutation, p53, and Ki-67 proliferation index) in high-grade gliomas. The integration of fractal features enhanced model performance, achieving an AUC of 0.791. All the above studies highlight the value of fractal-based techniques for analyzing irregular and complex time series.

Regardless of these advancements, most established techniques struggle to simultaneously address structural complexity, data scarcity, and long-range dependence—characteristics typical of agricultural datasets. Although FI has demonstrated its effectiveness in fields such as wind speed prediction, healthcare, and finance, its application in agriculture remains limited—particularly the use of FI with variable scaling for generating synthetic data to support deep learning models in crop prediction. The present study seeks to address this gap by developing an effective approach that integrates FI, a mathematical technique designed to handle self-similar and irregular patterns, with advanced DL models such as LSTM and Bi-LSTM. This hybrid approach is capable of capturing the chaotic and non-linear trends often present in real-world crop data. The following section presents the detailed methodology adopted in this study.

Data description

This study utilizes four key agricultural crop datasets: Millet, Maize (corn), Rice, and Wheat. The data for each crop ia taken from FAOSTAT (Food and Agriculture Organization’s statistical database)32 and showing annual production figures (in tones) for India, from 1961 to 2023. FAOSTAT is a reliable and widely used global source for agricultural data, providing standardized and comparable statistics across countries and time periods. These four crops are one of the major food productions in India. These are critical for assessing the food security and predicting future trend in agriculture.

Preprocessing

In preprocessing, the data is cleaned and transformed to enhance its quality. During this process, incomplete, inaccurate, or erroneous data can be eliminated. Various preprocessing methods are presented in the literature, readers may refer to García et al.31. While the approaches to make predictions or get results may differ, one common thing is their strong dependence on data availability. In other words, both the complexity and the actual amount of data are critical. Interpolation can be used for producing more fine-grained data. Karaca33 used the linear interpolation to increasing the fine-grainedness of the dataset. However, the linear interpolation approach may not be satisfactory, when dealing with real-life phenomena, which often exhibits highly complex and irregular structure. In general, the complexity of a dataset significantly affects the overall prediction performance. In fractal theory, the fractal dimension34 and Hurst exponent35 are used for measuring the complexity of a dataset.

Stationary transformation

Stationarity is a fundamental concept in time series analysis that significantly influences the accuracy of predictions. If mean, variance and auto-correlation of the time series do not depends on specific time point and are constant over time, then it is considered stationary. Ensuring stationarity is often a crucial precondition for applying many forecasting models effectively. In this study, suitable techniques have been used to achieve stationarity in the crop data. For a more detailed discussion, readers are referred to38 and the references therein.

Modeling approaches

The FIF is integrated as a preprocessing step for improving the quality of data before feeding it into the DL models. LSTM and Bi-LSTM models were employed in this study to predict the univariate time series of annual crop production. The inclusion of LSTM and BiLSTM variants allows a comprehensive comparison of model performance with and without fractal interpolation preprocessing.

LSTM suits better for larger architecture and is more capable for extracting the pattern from larger sequences of data sets. Each of the LSTM units consists of three gates: input, forget and output gates. Forget gate remembers the value over the arbitrary time interval, the input and output gate regulate the information into and out of the cell. A cell state vector is maintained by each LSTM cell and at every time step, the next LSTM have ability to choose to read from this, write to this or can reset the cell. The state of missing data in the regular time series can be easily detected by using the traditional LSTM. In addition to unidirectional LSTM, Bi-LSTM was employed to capture temporal dependencies from both forward and backward directions. This bidirectional processing enables the model to incorporate future contextual information within the input sequence, which is particularly beneficial for modeling the complex seasonal and cyclical behavior inherent in crop production time series.

Fractal analysis and estimation

The complexity characteristics of both the original and interpolated time series are evaluated for each dataset. To examine the chaotic behavior of the time series, two complexity measures are employed: the Hurst exponent and the fractal dimension.

Conclusion

This study investigated the effectiveness of FIF as a data augmentation technique for improving the prediction performance of LSTM and Bi-LSTM models for Indian agricultural production time series (Millet, Maize (corn), Rice and Wheat from 1961-2023). The fractal analysis revealed that all the crops production series exhibits the persistent long memory behavior, with Hurst exponent ranging from 0.777→ 0.875, that confirms the presence of trend reinforcing patterns typical of agricultural datasets. Higher fractal dimension is produced in case of variable scaling compared to the constant scaling, demonstrating its superior capability for capturing the complex dynamics, local roughness and irregularities in these time series. The proposed approach is strongly validated by the empirical results. Models trained with FIF-enhanced data, especially FIF with variable scaling, significantly outperformed both the FIF with constant scaling and original data models in all of the four crops. Noticeable improvements were observed in the key performance metrics, with dramatically increasing values of in challenging datasets such as Wheat and Millet. In most of the cases, LSTM integrated with variable scaling FIF is the best performing model, while Bi-LSTM integrated with variable scaling FIF yielded exceptional results for Rice with . The Wilcoxon and Diebold-Mariano statistical tests further confirmed these improvements.

From the experimental result an interesting fact is observed that the choice of vertical scaling factor in preprocessing strategy had greater impact on the accuracy of prediction than the choice of DL models itself. Although, the models demonstrated competitive performance but the difference between them were comparatively lower than the enhancement introduced by the proposed FI based data augmentation technique. Overall, the Bi-LSTM slightly outperformed by the LSTM in most of the cases. These results suggests that the selection of an appropriate scaling factor and the quality of preprocessing plays more dominant role in enhancing the forecasting performance. Overall, the findings in this study demonstrates that integrating FIF (variable scaling) with the advanced RNN (LSTM or Bi-LSTM) offers a promising framework for an enhanced yield forecasting, benefiting specially the rain dependent or climate sensitive crops.

Incorporating multivariate inputs such as temperature, rainfall and policy variables, hybrid models and validation on post 2023 events for further strengthening the real world applicability could be the potential future extensions. Exploring the hybrid approaches that integrate FIF with advanced DL networks such as Transformers, wavelet based models and attention mechanism could further improve the accuracy of prediction. Moreover, developing a real time prediction system would improve the practical utility of proposed framework for dynamic agricultural decision making. Finally, validating the approach across eco climate regions, higher frequencies and different crop datasets will strengthen its generalizability and robustness

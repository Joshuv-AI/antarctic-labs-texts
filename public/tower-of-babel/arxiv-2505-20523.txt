Achievable Rates and Error Exponents for a Class of Mismatched Compound Channels
Priyanka Patel, Francesc Molina and Albert Guillén i Fàbregas

Abstract
This paper investigates achievable information rates and error exponents of mismatched decoding when the channel belongs to the class of channels that are close to the decoding metric in terms of relative entropy. For both discrete- and continuous-alphabet channels, we derive approximations of the worst-case achievable information rates and error exponents as a function of the radius of a small relative entropy ball centered at the decoding metric, allowing the characterization of the loss incurred due to imperfect channel estimation. We provide a number of examples including symmetric metrics and modulo-additive noise metrics for discrete systems, and nearest neighbor decoding for continuous-alphabet channels, where we derive the approximation when the channel admits arbitrary statistics and when it is assumed noise-additive with unknown finite second-order moment.



I
Introduction

Standard results in information theory have been derived assuming that the channel transition probability law is perfectly known to the system designer as well as to the transmitter and receiver
[
1
]
. This assumption is too optimistic in practice since there are several environments where the channel is either unknown or difficult to estimate accurately. Therefore, devising the fundamental limits for reliable transmission under channel uncertainty becomes imperative
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
.

Most practical receivers aim at estimating the channel law and use this estimate for decoding as if it were perfect.
Mismatched decoding (see e.g.
[
4
]
and references therein), studies precisely the problem of reliable communication with a fixed decoding metric not necessarily equal to the channel. Beyond channel uncertainty, mismatched decoding encompasses a number of important problems such as bit-interleaved coded modulation, finite-precision arithmetic and zero-error communication
[
4
]
.

This manuscript delves into channel uncertainty by introducing a parameterization for the mismatched compound channel
[
4
, Ch. 2.2.4]
. We model channel uncertainty as a compound channel class comprising all channels that are contained within a small radius relative entropy ball centered at the known decoding metric. Accurate approximations to achievable information rates and error exponents are derived using i.i.d. and constant-composition codes for discrete memoryless channels (DMC), and i.i.d. and cost-constrained codes for continuous-alphabet channels. The key tool used is the approximation of relative entropy adopted by Borade and Zheng in
[
5
]
. This, combined with convex optimisation
[
6
]
and variational calculus
[
7
]
tools, provides closed-form results with a strong conceptual understanding of the sensitivity to small channel uncertainties.

I-B

Problem Setup

Consider reliable transmission of
M
messages over a DMC with input
X
and output
Y
, taking values from discrete and finite alphabets
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
, respectively.
The input distribution is denoted by
Q_{\!X\!}(x)=\Pr[X=x]
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
and the channel probability distribution is
W(y|x)=\Pr[Y=y|X=x]
for all pairs
(x,y)\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
.
For transmission, the encoder selects a message
m\in\{1,\dots,M\}
and transmits the corresponding
n
-symbol codeword
\boldsymbol{x}^{(m)}=(x_{1}^{(m)},\dotsc,x_{n}^{(m)})
from the codebook
\mathcal{C}_{n}=\{\boldsymbol{x}^{(i)}\}_{1\leq i\leq M}
. Upon observing
\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathcal{Y}^{n}
, the decoder estimates the transmitted message, inspired by the maximum-likelihood decoder but using a possibly suboptimal decoding metric
q^{n}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})
, as

\widehat{m}=\argmax_{1\leq\bar{m}\leq M}\ q^{n}(\boldsymbol{x}^{(\bar{m})}\!,\boldsymbol{y}).

(1)

When
q^{n}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\equiv\prod_{i=1}^{n}W(y_{i}|x_{i})
, the decoder is the optimal maximum-likelihood (ML) decoder and is commonly referred to as matched. The symbol
\equiv
denotes equivalence in decoding metric in the sense of
[
4
, Propositions 2.1 and 2.2]
. In any other case, the decoder is said to be mismatched. An error is declared when
\widehat{m}\neq m
, and the probability of error for
\mathcal{C}_{n}
is defined as
p_{e}(\mathcal{C}_{n})=\Pr[\widehat{m}\neq m]
.
In this work, we will work with product metrics for which we adopt the symbol metric notation
q(x,y)
and

\widehat{m}=\operatorname*{argmax}_{1\leq\bar{m}\leq M}\ \prod_{i=1}^{n}q(x^{(\bar{m})}_{i}\!,y_{i}).

(2)

After taking logarithms, these become additive metrics.

We consider the relevant case in practice where the decoding metric
q(x,y)=\widehat{W}(y|x)>0
is a channel estimate
\widehat{W}(y|x)
from the output of a channel estimator.
We analyze achievable rates and error exponents for an unknown channel
W(y|x)
and a mismatched decoder that uses
\widehat{W}(y|x)
as if it were perfect, and impose a constraint on the level of mismatch between the estimated and true channels by defining an appropriate statistical distance.
More concretely, let
d(P,P^{\prime})
be a statistical distance between the joint probability distributions
P
and
P^{\prime}
over the alphabet
\mathcal{X}{\times}\mathcal{Y}
. Then we define
small mismatch
as the class of channels
[
8
]
such that

W\in\big\{P_{Y|X}\colon d(Q_{\!X\!}P_{Y|X},Q_{\!X\!}\widehat{W})\leq r\big\}.

(3)

That is,
W
is within a ball centered at
\widehat{W}
of radius
r
, assumed to be small.
This class of channels is useful for modeling small perturbations of the channel around the decoding metric due to good, yet imperfect estimation.

I-C

Overview of Achievable Rates

A number of achievable rates have been derived in the literature for DMCs and continuous channels with an arbitrary fixed decoding metric
q(x,y)>0
(see e.g.
[
4
]
for a recent survey).
The GMI (generalized mutual information)
I_{\text{GMI}}

[
9
]
is the achievable rate obtained with i.i.d. random codes. Instead, employing constant-composition codes for DMCs or cost-constrained codes with a single per-codeword cost constraint for arbitrary alphabets yields the LM (lower mismatch) rate
I_{\text{LM}}

[
10
,
11
]
.
In this work, we focus on the dual expressions of the aforementioned rates defined from the
mismatched information density

i_{s,a}(x,y)\triangleq\log\frac{q(x,y)^{s}e^{a(x)}}{{\mathbb{E}}_{Q_{\!X\!}}[q(X,y)^{s}e^{a(X)}]}

(4)

where
s\geq 0
and
a(x)\in\mathbb{R}
for
x\in\mathcal{X}
. Therefore, as
[
4
, Ch. 2.3.1, Ch. 2.3.2]

\displaystyle I_{\text{GMI}}(Q_{\!X\!})

\displaystyle\triangleq\ \operatorname*{sup}_{s\geq 0}\ {\mathbb{E}}_{Q_{\!X\!}{\times}W}\big[i_{s,0}(X,Y)\big]

(5)

\displaystyle I_{\text{LM}}(Q_{\!X\!})

\displaystyle\triangleq\!\operatorname*{sup}_{s\geq 0,a(\cdot)}{\mathbb{E}}_{Q_{\!X\!}{\times}W}\big[i_{s,a}(X,Y)\big],

(6)

where the notation
i_{s,0}(x,y)
in (
5
) corresponds to setting
a(x)=0
in (
4
) for every
x\in\mathcal{X}
. Indeed, the GMI rate can be obtained from the LM rate by setting the function
a(x)
to zero for every input.
The GMI and LM rates are tight with respect to their respective ensemble of codes. Thus, the weakness in rate relative to the mismatch capacity
C_{\text{M}}
, the supremum of all achievable rates, is due to an inherent weakness of employed codes rather than loose mathematical analysis. Hence
I_{\text{GMI}}(Q_{\!X\!})\leq I_{\text{LM}}(Q_{\!X\!})\leq C_{\text{M}}
, with equality in specific cases
[
12
,
13
]
.

I-D

Overview of Error Exponents

An error exponent
E(R)
is said to be achievable at rate
R
whenever

E_{\text{r}}(R)\leq\operatorname*{lim}_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log\bar{p}_{e}(n,\lfloor e^{nR}\rfloor),

(7)

where
\bar{p}_{e}
is the average error probability over all randomly generated codes of a specific ensemble. If (
7
) holds with equality,
E_{\text{r}}(R)
is said to be the ensemble-tight exponent
[
4
, Ch. 7.1]
. For ML decoding,

\displaystyle E_{\text{r}}(Q_{\!X\!},R)

\displaystyle\triangleq\operatorname*{max}_{\rho\in[0,1]}E_{0}(Q_{\!X\!},\rho)-\rho R

(8)

is the (ensemble-tight) random coding exponent
[
1
]
achievable at rate
R
with i.i.d and constant-composition codes, where the respective Gallager
E_{0}
functions are defined from

\varepsilon_{a,\rho}(x,y)\triangleq\bigg(\frac{{\mathbb{E}}_{Q_{\!X\!}}[W(y|X)^{\frac{1}{1+\rho}}e^{a(X)}]}{W(y|x)^{\frac{1}{1+\rho}}e^{a(x)}}\bigg)^{\rho}

(9)

as

\displaystyle E_{0}^{\text{iid}}(Q_{\!X\!},\rho)

\displaystyle\triangleq-\log{\mathbb{E}}_{Q_{\!X\!}\times W}[\varepsilon_{0,\rho}(X,Y)]

(10)

\displaystyle E_{0}^{\text{cc}}(Q_{\!X\!},\rho)

\displaystyle\triangleq\sup_{a(\cdot)}-{\mathbb{E}}_{Q_{\!X\!}}\big[\!\log{\mathbb{E}}_{W}[\varepsilon_{a,\rho}(X,Y)|X]\big].

(11)

It is known that the error exponent
E_{\text{r}}(Q_{\!X\!},R)
is positive for rates
R<\frac{\partial E_{0}(Q_{\!X\!},\rho)}{\partial\rho}\big|_{\rho=0}=I(X;Y)
, proving the achievability part of the channel coding theorem. Moreover,
E_{0}^{\text{cc}}(Q_{\!X\!},\rho)\geq E_{0}^{\text{iid}}(Q_{\!X\!},\rho)
with equality only for the respective optimal input distributions
[
14
, Eq. (52)]
.

Analogous to the matched case, the ensemble-tight mismatched random coding error exponents
[
4
, Th. 7.1]
of i.i.d. and constant-composition codes are defined using

\varepsilon_{s,a,\rho}(x,y)\triangleq\bigg(\frac{{\mathbb{E}}_{Q_{\!X\!}}[q(X,y)^{s}e^{a(X)}]}{q(x,y)^{s}e^{a(x)}}\bigg)^{\rho}

(12)

to obtain the following dual expressions
[
4
, Ch. 7.2]
:

\displaystyle E_{\text{r}}^{\text{iid}}(Q_{\!X\!},R)

\displaystyle\triangleq\operatorname*{max}_{\rho\in[0,1]}E_{0}^{\text{iid}}(Q_{\!X\!},\rho)-\rho R

(13)

\displaystyle E_{\text{r}}^{\text{cc}}(Q_{\!X\!},R)

\displaystyle\triangleq\operatorname*{max}_{\rho\in[0,1]}E_{0}^{\text{cc}}(Q_{\!X\!},\rho)-\rho R,

(14)

where the respective mismatched Gallager functions are

\displaystyle E_{0}^{\text{iid}}(Q_{\!X\!},\rho)

\displaystyle\triangleq\ \sup_{s\geq 0}\ -\log{\mathbb{E}}_{Q_{\!X\!}{\times}W}[\varepsilon_{s,0,\rho}(X,Y)]

(15)

\displaystyle E_{0}^{\text{cc}}(Q_{\!X\!},\rho)

\displaystyle\triangleq\!{\sup_{s\geq 0,a(\cdot)}}\hskip-2.5pt{-}{\mathbb{E}}_{Q_{\!X\!}}\big[\!\log{\mathbb{E}}_{W}[\varepsilon_{s,a,\rho}(X,Y)|X]\big]{.}

(16)

For simplicity, we have used the same notation for the mismatched error exponents as for the matched case.

An alternative expression for
E_{0}^{\text{cc}}
can be formulated by observing that constant-composition and cost-constrained coding achieve the same exponent when two cost functions
c_{1}(\cdot)
and
c_{2}(\cdot)
are optimized
[
14
, Th. 4]
. This is

\displaystyle E_{0}^{\text{cc}}(Q_{\!X\!},\rho)

\displaystyle=\!\sup_{c_{1}\!(\cdot),c_{2}\!(\cdot)}E_{0}^{\text{cost}}(Q_{\!X\!},\rho,\{c_{1},c_{2}\})

(17)

where

\displaystyle E_{0}^{\text{cost}}(Q_{\!X\!},\rho,\boldsymbol{c})

\displaystyle\triangleq\!\sup_{s\geq 0,\boldsymbol{\lambda},\bar{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}\hskip-5.0pt-\log{\mathbb{E}}_{Q_{\!X\!}\times W}\big[\varepsilon_{s,\boldsymbol{\lambda},\bar{\boldsymbol{\lambda}},\rho}^{\text{cost}}(X,Y)\big]

(18)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{s,\boldsymbol{\lambda},\bar{\boldsymbol{\lambda}},\rho}^{\text{cost}}(x,y)

\displaystyle\triangleq\bigg(\frac{{\mathbb{E}}_{Q_{\!X\!}}[q(X,y)^{s}e^{\bar{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{\!T\!}(\boldsymbol{c}(X)-\boldsymbol{\phi})}]}{q(x,y)^{s}e^{{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{\!T\!}(\boldsymbol{c}(x)-\boldsymbol{\phi})}}\bigg)^{\rho}

(19)

and
\boldsymbol{c}(x)\triangleq[c_{1}(x),c_{2}(x)]^{T}
,
\boldsymbol{\lambda}\triangleq[\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}]^{T}
,
\bar{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\triangleq[\bar{\lambda}_{1},\bar{\lambda}_{2}]^{T}
and
\boldsymbol{\phi}\triangleq[{\mathbb{E}}[c_{1}(X)],{\mathbb{E}}[c_{2}(X)]]^{T}
defined in vector form for ease of notation.
We are more interested in the expression (
17
) for the mismatched
E_{0}
function of constant-composition codes, as it adopts a similar form to that of i.i.d. codes, leading to tighter derivations. In addition, cost-constrained coding remains valid for continuous alphabets.
When a matched ML decoder
q(x,y)=W(y|x)
is used, the matched Gallager
E_{0}
functions (
10
)–(
11
), are recovered from (
15
)–(
17
) by setting
s=(1+\rho)^{-1}
, hence recovering the matched error exponents.
Using Jensen’s inequality
[
15
, Ch. 2]
, it can be shown that for any pair
(W,q)

E_{\text{r}}^{\text{iid}}(Q_{\!X\!},R)\leq E_{\text{r}}^{\text{cc}}(Q_{\!X\!},R).

(20)

I-E

Contributions and Paper Outline

Existing work on mismatched decoding typically only considers channel uncertainty at the receiver for an arbitrary channel metric pair. This paper explores achievable rates and random coding error exponents under channel uncertainty for a class of channels that are close to the decoding metric, modeling the case of accurate yet imperfect channel estimation.
This scenario is described as the compound channel
[
16
,
17
]
, where the only knowledge available is the class of channels over which the system will operate.
The novelty of this paper lies in analyzing the compound channel under a given mismatched decoding metric. We parameterize the compound channel class by all the channels that lie within a relative entropy ball of radius
r
.
Our primary contribution is the sensitivity analysis of achievable rates and random coding error exponents to small values of
r
, capturing the effect of accurate but imperfect channel estimation. We also assess the boundary between matched and mismatched decoding, providing novel closed-form results for symmetric decoding metrics and the nearest neighbor decoder. In particular:

1)=0em

In Section
III
, we derive worst-case information rates for i.i.d. and constant-composition random codes. We derive an expansion of the worst-case information rates for small mismatch
r
and show that the mutual information of the estimated channel cannot be surpassed. Our expansion shows second-order terms proportional to
\sqrt{r}
, inducing an achievable rate penalty that decays with an infinite slope with respect to the mutual information of the estimated channel achieved at
r=0
. Examples of symmetric and modulo-additive decoding metrics are presented, demonstrating that the worst-case channel within the ball shares the same structure as the decoding metric.

2)=0em

In Section
IV
, we derive worst-case error exponents for i.i.d. and constant-composition random codes. We provide the corresponding expansions with second-order penalty terms again proportional to
\sqrt{r}
. Examples for the same symmetric and modulo-additive decoding metrics used for achievable rates are provided, yielding similar results.

3)=0em

In Section
V
, we extend previous analyses allowing for continuous input and output alphabets. We provide expansions for the worst-case achievable rates and error exponents for i.i.d. and cost-constrained random coding with multiple auxiliary costs.
The results are particularized to Gaussian codebooks with nearest neighbor decoding
[
18
]
. In particular, we derive a second-order expansion of the worst-case achievable rates for arbitrary channels, not necessarily additive, in a small-radius relative entropy ball around the nearest neighbor decoding metric.
We also show a significantly smaller penalty when the channels within the ball are assumed to be additive.
The structure of the worst-case channels is also studied, highlighting the discontinuities observed when the estimated channel has unbounded support.

Conclusions are drawn in Section
VI
. Proofs are found in the Appendices.

II
Small Mismatch: A Class of Compound Channels

We study a class of mismatched compound channels for which DMC
W(y|x)
is unknown but assumed to lie within a divergence ball
\mathcal{B}
centered on
\widehat{W}(y|x)
, a conditional probability distribution that models an estimate of
W(y|x)
from the output of a channel estimator.
The decoder uses it as the decoding metric, i.e.,
q(x,y)=\widehat{W}(y|x)>0
.

Definition 1
.

For small mismatch (ball radius) between the true and estimated channels
W
and
\widehat{W}
, we require that

W\in\mathcal{B}(\widehat{W}\!,r)\triangleq\{P_{Y|X}\colon{D}(\widehat{W}\|P_{Y|X}|Q_{\!X\!})\leq r\}

(21)

holds for small
r
, where
{D}(\widehat{W}\|P_{Y|X}|Q_{\!X\!})
is the conditional relative entropy

{D}(\widehat{W}\|P_{Y|X}|Q_{\!X\!})\triangleq\sum_{x,y}Q_{\!X\!}(x)\widehat{W}(y|x)\!\log\!\frac{\widehat{W}(y|x)}{P_{Y|X}(y|x)}.

(22)

Eq. (
21
) is equivalent to setting
{D}(Q_{\!X\!}\widehat{W}\|Q_{\!X\!}P_{Y|X})\leq r
.

We have adopted a decoder-centric perspective in which the ball is centered around the known quantity, i.e. the estimated channel
\widehat{W}
employed to decode.
One of the advantages of this formulation is that for sufficiently small mismatch
r
we can rely on
[
5
, Eqs. (1)–(4)]
and apply a Taylor expansion to the logarithm in the relative entropy and group the
o(\cdot)
terms. This allows us to express the relative entropy as a function of

\theta(y|x)\triangleq P_{Y|X}(y|x)-\widehat{W}(y|x)

(23)

plus a term of lesser significance, as

\displaystyle{D}(\widehat{W}\|P_{Y|X}|Q_{\!X\!})=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y}Q_{\!X\!}(x)\frac{\theta(y|x)^{2}}{\widehat{W}(y|x)}

\displaystyle+o\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y}\!Q_{\!X\!}(x)\frac{\theta(y|x)^{2}}{\widehat{W}(y|x)}\bigg).

(24)

Observe that the first term of (
24
) is exactly the chi-squared distance
\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{X}^{2}(Q_{\!X\!}P_{Y|X}\!,Q_{\!X\!}\widehat{W})
as defined in
[
19
, Pag. 425]
.
Vectorizing
\theta
allows us to express (
24
) in a more convenient form to obtain closed form results.
Besides this, there are other statistical distances to consider, such as generic
f
-divergences
[
20
,
21
]
or the Rényi divergence of order
\alpha

[
22
]
. Here, we only consider relative entropy, an
f
-divergence with
f^{\prime\prime}(1)=1
(
f^{\prime\prime}
stands for the second derivative of
f
). Our results can be easily extended to a generic
f
-divergence or Rényi entropy by substituting the
\tfrac{1}{2}
by the constant factor
\tfrac{f^{\prime\prime}(1)}{2}
for
f
-divergences or
\tfrac{\alpha}{2}
for the Rényi divergence.

III
Achievable Rates

In this section, we derive the worst-case LM and GMI rates.
For ease of explanation, we focus on the worst-case LM rate as the corresponding GMI can be obtained straightforwardly by setting
a(x)=0
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
.
We rewrite the mismatched information density
i_{s,a}
in (
4
) with
q(x,y)=\widehat{W}(y|x)
as

i_{s,a}(x,y)=\log\frac{\widehat{W}(y|x)^{s}e^{a(x)}}{{\mathbb{E}}_{Q_{\!X\!}}[\widehat{W}(y|X)^{s}e^{a(X)}]}.

(25)

The worst-case rate problem we wish to solve is

\displaystyle\underline{I}_{\text{LM}}(Q_{\!X\!},\widehat{W}\!,r)

\displaystyle=\operatorname*{min}_{W\in\mathcal{B}}\operatorname*{sup}_{s\geq 0,a(\cdot)}{\mathbb{E}}_{Q_{\!X\!}{\times}W}[i_{s,a}(X,Y)]

(26)

where the minimization is over all valid conditional probability distributions in the relative entropy ball
\mathcal{B}
defined in (
21
). Since the true channel is unknown, problem (
26
) finds the channel in
\mathcal{B}
that gives the worst possible LM rate. This gives an indication of the rate loss incurred by accurate but not perfect channel estimation.

Channels
W\in\mathcal{B}
need to be valid conditional probability distributions, i.e.,

\displaystyle W(y|x)\geq 0

\displaystyle\quad\text{for}\quad(x,y)\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}

(27)

\displaystyle\sum_{y}W(y|x)=1

\displaystyle\quad\text{for}\quad x\in\mathcal{X}.

(28)

The constraint
W(y|x)\leq 1
for
(x,y)\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
is guaranteed by construction when (
27
)–(
28
) are satisfied.
Next, observe that imposing (
27
) in problem (
26
) is unnecessary since the relative entropy in
(
21
)
implies that positivity already for a strictly positive
\widehat{W}
.

.

Consider a class of DMCs

, a mismatched decoder based on the channel estimate

Continuous Alphabets

In this section, we extend the study of worst-case achievable rates and Gallager

functions to continuous-alphabet channels by allowing the alphabets
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
to take infinite values. Specifically, our focus is on

and
W(y|x)
to denote the continuous probability input and channel distributions, respectively, and redefine small mismatch for continuous-alphabet channels as follows.

Definition 2
.

For small mismatch (ball radius) between the continuous-alphabet channels
W
and
\widehat{W}
, we require that

\displaystyle W\in\mathcal{B}=\{P_{Y|X}\in\mathcal{S}\colon{D}(\widehat{W}\|P_{Y|X}|Q_{\!X\!})\leq r\}

holds for small
r
, where
\mathcal{S}
is a function space admitting probability density functions
P_{Y|X}(y|x)
over
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and
{D}(\widehat{W}\|P_{Y|X}|Q_{\!X\!})
defines the conditional relative entropy in integral form as

{D}(\widehat{W}\|P_{Y|X}|Q_{\!X\!})\triangleq\!{\iint}Q_{\!X\!}(x)\widehat{W}(y|x)\!\log\!\frac{\widehat{W}(y|x)}{P_{Y|X}(y|x)}{\mathrm{d}}x{\mathrm{d}}y.

(83)

We analyze both i.i.d. and cost-constrained codes since constant-composition random coding is undefined for continuous alphabets.
As we shall see, most of the previous expressions of information rates and
E_{0}
functions remain invariant, by substituting probability distributions with the corresponding probability densities and summations with integrals. The novelty is that the formulation of the continuous case allows for unprecedented closed-form results for the nearest neighbor decoder that further extend
[

4

18

V-A

i.i.d. Random Coding

For i.i.d. random coding, the mismatched information density and mismatched exponent density are defined as

\displaystyle i_{s}(x,y)

\displaystyle\triangleq\log\frac{\widehat{W}(y|x)^{s}}{{\int}Q_{\!X\!}(\bar{x})\widehat{W}(y|\bar{x})^{s}{\mathrm{d}}\bar{x}}

(84)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{s,\rho}(x,y)

\displaystyle\triangleq\bigg(\frac{{\int}Q_{\!X\!}(\bar{x})\widehat{W}(y|\bar{x})^{s}{\mathrm{d}}\bar{x}}{\widehat{W}(y|x)^{s}}\bigg)^{\rho},

(85)

where we have expressed expectations in integral form for reasons of emphasis.

We want to solve the following problems to determine the worst-case GMI and
E_{0}
function:

\displaystyle\underline{I}_{\text{GMI}}

\displaystyle(Q_{\!X\!},\widehat{W}\!,r)=\operatorname*{min}_{W\in\mathcal{B}}\ \operatorname*{sup}_{s\geq 0}\ {\mathbb{E}}_{Q_{\!X\!}{\times}W}[i_{s}(X,Y)]

(86)

\displaystyle\underline{E}{}_{0}^{\text{iid}}

\displaystyle(Q_{\!X\!},\widehat{W}\!,r)=\operatorname*{min}_{W\in\mathcal{B}}\ \operatorname*{sup}_{s\geq 0}-\log{\mathbb{E}}_{Q_{\!X\!}{\times}W}[\varepsilon_{s,\rho}(X,Y)]

(87)

Their solutions are particular cases of cost-constrained random coding, and are thus described below.

V-B

Cost-Constrained Random Coding

We study codebooks in which, unlike i.i.d. random coding, codewords are constrained to satisfy multiple per-codeword cost functions
[
14
]
.
Specifically, we consider codebooks in which each codeword
\boldsymbol{x}^{(m)}
satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{0}(x_{i}^{(m)})

\displaystyle\leq P

(88)

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{\ell}(x_{i}^{(m)})-\phi_{\ell}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\delta}{n}\quad,\quad\ell={1,\dots,L}

(89)

where
\phi_{\ell}\triangleq{\mathbb{E}}[c_{\ell}(X)]
for
\ell=1,\dotsc,L
and
\delta>0
.
Observe that these input cost functions might be related to the channel having an input cost constraint
c_{0}(\cdot)
, or might be design cost constraints that allow us to shape codewords in a certain way as for the cost functions
c_{\ell}(\cdot)
for
\ell=1,\dotsc,L
.

The corresponding mismatched information and exponent densities now read

\displaystyle i_{s,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(x,y)

\displaystyle\triangleq\log\frac{\widehat{W}(y|x)^{s}e^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\!T\!}\boldsymbol{c}(x)}}{{\int}Q_{\!X\!}(\bar{x})\widehat{W}(y|\bar{x})^{s}e^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\!T\!}\boldsymbol{c}(\bar{x})}{\mathrm{d}}\bar{x}}

(90)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{s,\boldsymbol{\lambda},\bar{\boldsymbol{\lambda}},\rho}(x,y)

\displaystyle\triangleq\bigg(\!\frac{{\int}Q_{\!X\!}(\bar{x})\widehat{W}(y|\bar{x})^{s}e^{\bar{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{\!T\!}(\boldsymbol{c}(\bar{x})-\boldsymbol{\phi})}{\mathrm{d}}\bar{x}}{\widehat{W}(y|x)^{s}e^{{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{\!T\!}(\boldsymbol{c}(x)-\boldsymbol{\phi})}}\bigg)^{\!\rho}

(91)

where we have defined the following vectors to denote compactly the cost functions and corresponding weights:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{c}(x)

\displaystyle\triangleq\begin{bmatrix}c_{0}(x),c_{1}(x),\dotsc,c_{L}(x)\end{bmatrix}^{T}

(92)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\phi}

\displaystyle\triangleq\begin{bmatrix}P,\phi_{1},\dotsc,\phi_{L}\end{bmatrix}^{T}

(93)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\lambda}

\displaystyle\triangleq\begin{bmatrix}\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\dotsc,\lambda_{L}\end{bmatrix}^{T}

(94)

\displaystyle\bar{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\triangleq\begin{bmatrix}\bar{\lambda}_{0},\bar{\lambda}_{1},\dotsc,\bar{\lambda}_{L}\end{bmatrix}^{T}

(95)

with
\lambda_{0},\bar{\lambda}_{0}\geq 0
and
\lambda_{\ell},\bar{\lambda}_{\ell}\in{\mathbb{R}}
for
\ell=1,\dotsc,L
.
The problems we want to solve are

\displaystyle\underline{I}_{\text{cost}}(Q_{\!X\!}

\displaystyle,\widehat{W}\!,r)=\operatorname*{min}_{W\in\mathcal{B}}\ \operatorname*{sup}_{s\geq 0,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}{\mathbb{E}}_{Q_{\!X\!}{\times}W}[i_{s,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(X,Y)]

(96)

\displaystyle\underline{E}{}_{0}^{\text{cost}}(Q_{\!X\!}

\displaystyle,\widehat{W}\!,r)=

\displaystyle\operatorname*{min}_{W\in\mathcal{B}}\operatorname*{sup}_{s\geq 0,\boldsymbol{\lambda},\bar{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}-\log{\mathbb{E}}_{Q_{\!X\!}{\times}W}[\varepsilon_{s,\boldsymbol{\lambda},\bar{\boldsymbol{\lambda}},\rho}(X,Y)].

(97)

The main difference with respect to the i.i.d. case is the need to further optimize over vectors
\boldsymbol{\lambda},\bar{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
. Note that (
86
)–(
87
) can be obtained from (
96
)–(
97
) by setting
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\bar{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}=\boldsymbol{0}
. The following theorem provides the solutions to all problems.

Theorem 3
.

Consider a family of continuous-alphabet channels
W(y|x)
, a mismatched decoder based on the channel estimate
\widehat{W}(y|x)
and a fixed input distribution
Q_{\!X\!}(x)
satisfying (
82
). Then, for every
r\geq 0
sufficiently small:

(i) the worst-case achievable rates for cost-constrained and i.i.d. random coding can be expressed as

\displaystyle\underline{I}_{\text{cost}}(Q_{\!X\!},\widehat{W}\!,r)=\

\displaystyle\operatorname*{sup}_{s\geq 0,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\bigg\{I_{s,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{\text{ML}}(Q_{\!X\!},\widehat{W})

\displaystyle-\sqrt{2rV_{s,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(Q_{\!X\!},\widehat{W})}+o(\sqrt{r})\bigg\}

(98)

\displaystyle\underline{I}_{\text{GMI}}(Q_{\!X\!},\widehat{W}\!,r)=\

\displaystyle\operatorname*{sup}_{s\geq 0}\bigg\{I_{s,\boldsymbol{0}}^{\text{ML}}(Q_{\!X\!},\widehat{W})

\displaystyle-\sqrt{2rV_{s,\boldsymbol{0}}(Q_{\!X\!},\widehat{W})}+o(\sqrt{r})\bigg\}

(99)

where

\displaystyle I^{\text{ML}}_{s,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(Q_{\!X\!},\widehat{W})

\displaystyle\triangleq{\mathbb{E}}_{Q_{\!X\!}{\times}\widehat{W}}\big[i_{s,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(X,Y)\big]

(100)

\displaystyle V_{s,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(Q_{\!X\!},\widehat{W})

\displaystyle\triangleq{\mathbb{E}}_{Q_{\!X\!}}\big[\text{Var}_{\widehat{W}}[i_{s,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(X,Y)|X]\big].

(101)

(ii) the worst-case mismatched
E_{0}
functions for cost-constrained and i.i.d. random coding can be expressed as

\displaystyle\underline{E}_{0}^{\text{cost}}

\displaystyle(Q_{\!X\!},\widehat{W}\!,\rho,r)=\operatorname*{sup}_{s\geq 0,\boldsymbol{\lambda},\bar{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}\bigg\{E_{s,\boldsymbol{\lambda},\bar{\boldsymbol{\lambda}},\rho}^{\text{ML}}(Q_{\!X\!},\widehat{W})

\displaystyle-\log\bigg(1+\sqrt{2rV_{s,\boldsymbol{\lambda},\bar{\boldsymbol{\lambda}},\rho}(Q_{\!X\!},\widehat{W})}\bigg)+o(\sqrt{r})\bigg\}

(102)

\displaystyle\underline{E}_{0}^{\text{iid}}

\displaystyle(Q_{\!X\!},\widehat{W}\!,\rho,r)=\operatorname*{sup}_{s\geq 0}\bigg\{E_{s,\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{0},\rho}^{\text{ML}}(Q_{\!X\!},\widehat{W})

\displaystyle-\log\bigg(1+\sqrt{2rV_{s,\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{0},\rho}(Q_{\!X\!},\widehat{W})}\bigg)+o(\sqrt{r})\bigg\}

(103)

where

\displaystyle E_{s,\boldsymbol{\lambda},\bar{\boldsymbol{\lambda}},\rho}^{\text{ML}}(Q_{\!X\!},\widehat{W})

\displaystyle\triangleq-\log{\mathbb{E}}_{Q_{\!X\!}{\times}\widehat{W}}\big[\varepsilon_{s,\boldsymbol{\lambda},\bar{\boldsymbol{\lambda}},\rho}(X,Y)\big]

(104)

\displaystyle V_{s,\boldsymbol{\lambda},\bar{\boldsymbol{\lambda}},\rho}(Q_{\!X\!},\widehat{W})

\displaystyle\triangleq\frac{{\mathbb{E}}_{Q_{\!X\!}}\big[\text{Var}_{\widehat{W}}[\varepsilon_{s,\boldsymbol{\lambda},\bar{\boldsymbol{\lambda}},\rho}(X,Y)|X]\big]}{{\mathbb{E}}_{Q_{\!X\!}{\times}\widehat{W}}^{2}[\varepsilon_{s,\boldsymbol{\lambda},\bar{\boldsymbol{\lambda}},\rho}(X,Y)]}.

(105)

Proof.

The solution to worst-case achievable rates is found by resorting to the calculus of variations
[
7
]
; see Appendix
D
for the proof. The worst-case mismatched Gallager
E_{0}
function is a reformulation of the worst-case achievable rates, as shown in Appendix
B
, followed by the steps in Appendix
D
.

(111)

Q_{\!X\!}

{D}(\widehat{W}\|P_{Y|X})\triangleq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{W}(y)\log\frac{\widehat{W}(y)}{P_{Y|X}(y)}{\mathrm{d}}y.

For additive noise channels with a known second-order moment

\hat{\mu}
and variance
\hat{\sigma}^{2}

r

\widehat{W}(y|x)=\mathcal{N}(y{-}x{-}\hat{\mu};\hat{\sigma}^{2})

We wish to solve

\displaystyle\underline{{I}}_{\text{Gauss}}(Q_{\!X\!},\widehat{W}\!,r)=\min_{W\in\mathcal{B}}\ \frac{1}{2}\log\left(1+\frac{P}{{\mathbb{E}}[(Z{-}\hat{\mu})^{2}]}\right)

\displaystyle\underline{{I}}_{\text{Gauss}}(Q_{\!X\!},\widehat{W}\!,r)=\frac{1}{2}\log\left(1+\Gamma\right)-\frac{\Gamma\sqrt{r}}{1+\Gamma}+o(\sqrt{r}).

(116)

and variance
\hat{\sigma}

\sqrt{r}

VI
Conclusion

We have studied a family of mismatched compound channels by assuming that the true channel lies within a small radius relative entropy ball centered at the decoding metric. We have shown that a second order expansion of the relative entropy ball for a small radius allows the derivation of closed-form results to worst-case (minimum) information rates and error exponents for discrete and continuous memoryless channels. Specifically, for both discrete- and continuous-alphabet memoryless channels, we derive penalty terms that are proportional to the square root of the ball radius. We have obtained new explicit expressions for the worst-case rate achievable with Gaussian codebooks and nearest neighbor decoding which complement the analytical findings derived by Lapidoth and Scarlett
et. al

[

,
26
,
4

]
.

Discussion

The only remaining issue to be proven is whether the worst-case channels are non-negative. This arises because the constraint

in (

in the original problem (

Further analysis reveals that this issue is fully resolved when

To evidence the above, we have solved the full optimization problem including constraints to ensure that all valid conditional channel distributions are non-negative, as
W(y|x)\geq 0
for
(x,y)\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}

The solution can be expressed in terms of the following variables

\displaystyle i_{s,a}^{\mu}(x,y)

\displaystyle\triangleq i_{s,a}(x,y)-\mu(x,y)

(160)

\displaystyle\varphi_{s,a}^{\mu}(y|x)

\displaystyle\triangleq\frac{i_{s,a}^{\mu}(x,y)-{\mathbb{E}}_{\widehat{W}}[i_{s,a}^{\mu}(x,Y)]}{\sqrt{V_{s,a}^{\mu}(Q_{\!X\!},\widehat{W})}}

(161)

\displaystyle V_{s,a}^{\mu}(Q_{\!X\!},\widehat{W})

\displaystyle\triangleq{\mathbb{E}}_{Q_{\!X\!}}[{\text{Var}}_{\widehat{W}}[i_{s,a}^{\mu}(X,Y)|X]],

(162)

\displaystyle\underline{I}_{s,a}(Q_{\!X\!},\widehat{W}\!,r)=\

\displaystyle\max_{\mu(\cdot)\geq 0}\bigg\{{\mathbb{E}}_{Q_{\!X\!}{\times}\widehat{W}}[i_{s,a}^{\mu}(X,Y)]

(163)

for the worst-case channel distribution

\displaystyle\tilde{W}_{\!s,a}^{\ast}(y|x)

\displaystyle=\widehat{W}(y|x)\Big(1-\sqrt{2r}\cdot\varphi_{s,a}^{\mu}(x,y)\Big).

From the above expression, it is evident that when

\displaystyle\hskip-5.0ptV_{s,0}(Q_{\!X\!},\widehat{W})

(178)

Appendix D
Proof of Theorem
3

The continuous-alphabet case is similar to the discrete-alphabet case, but now we work with probability density functions instead of probability mass functions, and integrals instead of summations. Therefore, we resort to the calculus of variations to address the minimization in the function space.

We want to solve the following variational problem

\displaystyle\min_{W\in\mathcal{B}}{\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}}Q_{\!X\!}(x)W(y|x)i_{s,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(x,y){\mathrm{d}}x{\mathrm{d}}y

(182)

where the constraint
W\in\mathcal{B}
considers conditional probability density functions
W(y|x)\geq 0
that satisfy

\displaystyle\!\left\{\!\!\!\begin{array}[]{rl}d(\widehat{W}\|W|Q_{\!X\!})+o(d(\widehat{W}\|W|Q_{\!X\!})\!)&\leq\ r\\
{\int_{\mathbb{R}}}W(y|x){\mathrm{d}}y&=\ 1\ \text{for}\ x\in\mathbb{R}\end{array}\right.

with

\displaystyle d(\widehat{W}\|W|Q_{\!X\!})\triangleq\!{\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}}Q_{\!X\!}(x)\frac{(W(y|x){-}\widehat{W}(y|x)\!)^{2}}{2\widehat{W}(y|x)}{\mathrm{d}}x{\mathrm{d}}y.

(185)

For sufficiently small
r
, the same trick as in (
143
) can be applied to move the
o(\cdot)
term in the constraint as a penalty term in the objective function. We will omit the technical derivations, as it follows the same rules outlined in Appendix
A
, and focus on solving the following variational problem

\displaystyle\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}d(\widehat{W}\|W|Q_{\!X\!})=r\\
{\int_{\mathbb{R}}}W(y|x){\mathrm{d}}y=1\end{subarray}}{\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}}\!Q_{\!X\!}(x)W(y|x)i_{s,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(x,y){\mathrm{d}}x{\mathrm{d}}y+\rho^{\!\ast}\!o(r)

(186)

where the differentiability of the objective functional and constraints is assumed under an appropriately chosen norm for channels with continuous derivatives, as functionals are either linear or quadratic in
W
.

Now, we proceed by minimizing the Lagrangian

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}[W]=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\!F(x,y,W(y|x)){\mathrm{d}}x{\mathrm{d}}y

(187)

with

\displaystyle F(x,y,W)\triangleq\

\displaystyle Q_{\!X\!}(x)Wi_{s,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(x,y)+\nu(x)W

\displaystyle+\rho Q_{\!X\!}(x)\frac{(W{-}\widehat{W}(y|x)\!)^{2}}{2\widehat{W}(y|x)}

(188)

and where we have obviated
W
-independent constant terms.
We follow the rules in
[
7
, Sec. V]
, where the stationary point
W^{\ast}(y|x)
is found by solving the Euler-Lagrange equation

\displaystyle\frac{\partial F}{\partial W}-\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\frac{\partial F}{\partial W_{\!x}}-\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\frac{\partial F}{\partial W_{\!y}}=0\quad\text{in}\quad(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

(189)

In our case, since
F
in (
188
) does not depend on the partial derivatives of
W
, denoted
W_{x}
and
W_{y}
, the above reduces to solving
\tfrac{\partial F}{\partial W}=0
in
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
for
\{W^{\ast}(y|x),\nu^{\!\ast}(x),\rho^{\!\ast}\}
jointly with the problem constraints.
Specifically, we have

\displaystyle W^{\ast}(y|x)

\displaystyle=\widehat{W}(y|x)\!\left(1-\frac{Q_{\!X\!}(x)i_{s,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(x,y){+}\nu^{\!\ast}(x)}{\rho^{\!\ast}Q_{\!X\!}(x)}\right)

(190)

with

\displaystyle\nu^{\!\ast}(x)

\displaystyle\triangleq-Q_{\!X\!}(x){\mathbb{E}}_{\widehat{W}}[i_{s,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(x,Y)]

(191)

\displaystyle\rho^{\!\ast}

\displaystyle\triangleq\sqrt{V_{s,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(Q_{\!X\!},\widehat{W})/(2r)}

(192)

\displaystyle V_{s,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(Q_{\!X\!},\widehat{W})

\displaystyle\triangleq{\mathbb{E}}_{Q_{\!X\!}}[{\text{Var}}_{\widehat{W}}[i_{s,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(X,Y)|X]].

(193)

The stationary point
W^{\ast}(y|x)
corresponds to the global minimum as the problem is linear in
W
subject to quadratic constraints in
W
and
\rho^{\ast}>0
.

For Gaussian i.i.d. codebooks
Q_{\!X\!}(x)=\mathcal{N}(x;P)
and the nearest neighbor decoder
\widehat{W}(y|x)=\mathcal{N}(y{-}x;\hat{\sigma}^{2})
, we have

\displaystyle\widehat{W}(y|x)^{s}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{s}\cdot\mathcal{N}(y{-}x;\hat{\sigma}^{2}s^{-1})

(194)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{Q_{\!X\!}}[\widehat{W}(y|X)^{s}]

\displaystyle=\alpha_{s}\cdot\mathcal{N}(y;\hat{\sigma}^{2}s^{-1}{+}P)

(195)

with
\alpha_{s}\triangleq\sqrt{s^{-1}(2\pi\hat{\sigma}^{2})^{1-s}}
.

E-I
Achievable rate with Gaussian i.i.d. codebooks

The mismatched information density is (
cf
.
[
27
, (28)-(29)]
)

\displaystyle i_{s,\boldsymbol{0}}(x,y)

\displaystyle=C_{1}-\frac{s}{2}\frac{(y-x)^{2}}{\hat{\sigma}^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{y^{2}}{\hat{\sigma}^{2}s^{-1}{+}P}

(196)

with

\displaystyle C_{1}

\displaystyle\triangleq\frac{1}{2}\log\left(1+\frac{sP}{\hat{\sigma}^{2}}\right),

(197)

and the respective expectations are

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\widehat{W}}[i_{s,\boldsymbol{0}}(x,Y)]

\displaystyle=C_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{x^{2}{-}sP}{\hat{\sigma}^{2}s^{-1}{+}P}

(198)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{Q_{\!X\!}{\times}\widehat{W}}[i_{s,\boldsymbol{0}}(X,Y)]

\displaystyle=C_{1}+\frac{P}{2}\frac{1-s}{\hat{\sigma}^{2}s^{-1}{+}P}.

(199)

Note that
I^{\text{ML}}_{s,\boldsymbol{0}}(Q_{\!X\!},\widehat{W})={\mathbb{E}}_{Q_{\!X\!}{\times}\widehat{W}}[i_{s,\boldsymbol{0}}(X,Y)]
.

Now we turn our attention to the term
V_{s,\boldsymbol{0}}(Q_{\!X\!},\widehat{W})={\mathbb{E}}_{Q_{\!X\!}{\times}\widehat{W}}[i_{s,\boldsymbol{0}}^{2}(X,Y)]-{\mathbb{E}}_{Q_{\!X\!}}[{\mathbb{E}}_{\widehat{W}}^{2}[i_{s,\boldsymbol{0}}(X,Y)|X]]
. After tedious but otherwise straightforward calculations, we find its two terms as follows

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{Q_{\!X\!}{\times}\widehat{W}}[i_{s,\boldsymbol{0}}^{2}(X,Y)]=\

\displaystyle K_{1}+\frac{3s^{2}}{4}+\frac{3(\hat{\sigma}^{2}{+}P)^{2}}{4(\hat{\sigma}^{2}s^{-1}{+}P)^{2}}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\frac{s(3\hat{\sigma}^{2}{+}P)}{(\hat{\sigma}^{2}s^{-1}{+}P)^{2}}

(200)

\displaystyle K_{1}+\frac{P^{2}(3-2s+s^{2})}{4(\hat{\sigma}^{2}s^{-1}{+}P)^{2}}

(201)

where
K_{1}\triangleq C_{1}^{2}+C_{1}P(1{-}s)(\hat{\sigma}^{2}s^{-1}{+}P)^{-2}
, leading to

\displaystyle V_{s,\boldsymbol{0}}(Q_{\!X\!},\widehat{W})

(202)

E-II
E_{0}
function with Gaussian i.i.d. codebooks

The expressions for the worst-case mismatched Gallager
E_{0}
functions can be obtained from

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{Q_{\!X\!}}^{t}[\widehat{W}(y|X)^{s}]

\displaystyle\triangleq\alpha_{s}^{t}\frac{\sqrt{2\pi P_{\!s}t^{-1}}}{\sqrt{(2\pi P_{\!s})^{t}}}\cdot\mathcal{N}(y;P_{\!s}t^{-1})

(203)

with
P_{\!s}\triangleq\hat{\sigma}^{2}s^{-1}{+}P
, and

\displaystyle A(s,t)

\displaystyle\triangleq\!\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathbb{E}}_{Q_{\!X\!}}^{t}[\widehat{W}(y|X)^{s}]{\mathbb{E}}_{Q_{\!X\!}}[\widehat{W}(y|X)^{1-st}]{\mathrm{d}}y

\displaystyle=\!\int_{\mathbb{R}}\!\frac{\alpha^{t}_{s}\alpha_{1-st}}{\sqrt{(2\pi P_{\!s})^{t}}}\sqrt{\!\frac{P_{\!s}}{P_{\!s}{+}tP_{\!1-st}}}\mathcal{N}\!\left(\!y;\frac{P_{s}P_{1-st}}{P_{s}{+}tP_{1-st}}\right)\!{\mathrm{d}}y

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha^{t}_{s}\alpha_{1-st}}{\sqrt{(2\pi P_{\!s})^{t}}}\sqrt{\!\frac{P_{\!s}}{P_{\!s}+tP_{\!1-st}}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\left(1+\frac{Ps}{\hat{\sigma}^{2}}\right)^{\!\!-t}\!\left(1+\frac{tP(1-s-ts)}{P+\hat{\sigma}^{2}s^{-1}}\right)^{\!\!-1}}.

(204)

Therefore

\displaystyle E^{\text{ML}}_{s,\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{0},\rho}(Q_{\!X\!},\widehat{W})=\

\displaystyle-\log A(s,\rho)

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\frac{\rho}{2}\log\!\left(1+\frac{Ps}{\hat{\sigma}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\log\left(1+\frac{\rho P(1{-}s{-}\rho s)}{P+\hat{\sigma}^{2}s^{-1}}\right).

(205)

The dispersion term
V_{s,\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{0},\rho}(Q_{\!X\!},\widehat{W})
is computed as the following sum:

\displaystyle V_{s,\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{0},\rho}(Q_{\!X\!},\widehat{W})=V_{1}(Q_{\!X\!},\widehat{W})-V_{2}(Q_{\!X\!},\widehat{W}).

(206)

The first term is

\displaystyle V_{1}(Q_{\!X\!},\widehat{W})

\displaystyle\triangleq\frac{{\mathbb{E}}_{Q_{\!X\!}{\times}\widehat{W}}[\varepsilon^{2}_{s,\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{0},\rho}(X,Y)]}{{\mathbb{E}}_{Q_{\!X\!}{\times}\widehat{W}}^{2}[\varepsilon_{s,\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{0},\rho}(X,Y)]}

The second term is

\displaystyle V_{2}(Q_{\!X\!},\widehat{W})

\displaystyle=\frac{{\mathbb{E}}_{Q_{\!X\!}}[{\mathbb{E}}_{\widehat{W}}^{2}[\varepsilon_{s,\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{0},\rho}(X,Y)|X]]}{{\mathbb{E}}_{Q_{\!X\!}{\times}\widehat{W}}^{2}[\varepsilon_{s,\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{0},\rho}(X,Y)]}.

We compute the numerator through the steps

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\widehat{W}}^{2}[\varepsilon_{s,\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{0},\rho}(x,Y)]=\frac{\alpha_{1-\rho s}^{2}\alpha^{2\rho}_{s}}{\rho(2\pi P_{\!s})^{\rho-1}}\mathcal{N}^{2}\!\left(\!x;\frac{P_{s}{-}\rho sP}{\rho(1{-}s\rho)}\right)

(210)

and thus

\displaystyle V_{2}(Q_{\!X\!},\widehat{W})

(211)

E-III

For spherical codebooks, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{Q_{\!X\!}}[\widehat{W}^{s}(y|X)e^{\lambda\!X^{2}}]=\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{g(P)}}\cdot\mathcal{N}(y;\hat{\sigma}^{2}s^{-1}{+}P^{\prime})

(212)

and the auxiliary variables

\displaystyle C_{2}

\displaystyle\triangleq\frac{1}{2}\log\left(g(P)+\frac{sP}{\hat{\sigma}^{2}}\right)

(213)

\displaystyle P^{\prime}

\displaystyle\triangleq P/g(P)

(214)

\displaystyle g(P)

\displaystyle\triangleq 1-2\lambda P.

(215)

The mismatched information density is

\displaystyle i_{s,\lambda}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\lambda x^{2}+C_{2}-\frac{s}{2}\frac{(y{-}x)^{2}}{\hat{\sigma}^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{y^{2}}{\hat{\sigma}^{2}s^{-1}{+}P^{\prime}}

(216)

and

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\widehat{W}}[i_{s,\lambda}(x,Y)]

\displaystyle=\lambda x^{2}+C_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{x^{2}{-}sP^{\prime}}{\hat{\sigma}^{2}s^{-1}{+}P^{\prime}}

(217)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{Q_{\!X\!}{\times}\widehat{W}}[i_{s,\lambda}(X,Y)]

\displaystyle=\lambda P+C_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{P-sP^{\prime}}{\hat{\sigma}^{2}s^{-1}{+}P^{\prime}}.

(218)

After straightforward calculations, we can write

\displaystyle I^{\text{ML}}_{s,\lambda}(Q_{\!X\!},\widehat{W})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\log\!\bigg(g(P)+\frac{sP}{\hat{\sigma}^{2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle+\frac{P(1{-}s){+}\hat{\sigma}^{2}s^{-1}g(P)(1{-}g(P))}{2(P+\hat{\sigma}^{2}s^{-1}g(P))}

(219)

\displaystyle V_{s,\lambda}(Q_{\!X\!},\widehat{W})

\displaystyle=\frac{P}{2}\frac{2\hat{\sigma}^{2}g^{2}(P){+}s^{2}P}{(\hat{\sigma}^{2}s^{-1}g(P)+P)^{2}}.

(220)

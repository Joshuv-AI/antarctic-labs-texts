MobVGG: Ensemble technique for birds and drones prediction
Authors: Sheikh Muhammad Saqib, Tehseen Mazhar, Muhammad Iqbal, Ahmad Almogren, Tariq Shahazad, Ateeq Ur Rehman, Habib Hamam
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractDetection of aerial activities, including drones and birds, has practical implications for automating bird surveys and developing radar systems for aerial object collision detection. Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) have been extensively utilized for image recognition and classification tasks, albeit prior research predominantly focuses on single-class 'drone' classification. However, a gap persists in achieving high accuracy for multi-class classification. To address the limitations of traditional CNNs, such as vanishing gradients and the necessity for numerous layers, this study introduces a novel model termed "MobVGG.” This model combines the architectures of MobileNetV2 and VGG16 to accurately classify images as either 'bird' or 'drone'. The dataset comprises 4212 images for each category of 'bird' and 'drone'. The stringent methodology was applied for dataset preparation and model training to ensure the reliability of the findings. Comparative analysis with previous research demonstrates that the proposed MobVGG model, trained on both 'bird' and 'drone' images, achieves superior accuracy (96 %) compared to benchmark studies. Our paper targets researchers and graduate students as its primary audience.Keywords: Convolutional neural networks (CNNs), MobileNetV2, VGG16, Ensemble technique, Deep learning

Introduction

Context and motivation

Collision with birds and airplanes (bird strike phenomena) is a dangerous aviation safety issue. In a 2014 report, this cost was estimated at $1.2 billion per year for the airlines operating in the U.S. [1]. By providing radar systems with the ability to distinguish birds from drones, this technique can keep pilots from being distracted as well as enhance safety issues and budgeting implications. Besides this, the enhanced commercial and recreational drone numbers imply a risk of airspace congestion and collision. The bird-drone separation model, being the key feature of the safe drone traffic management systems, as depicted by Ref. [2], can be the very essence of building the system. The risk of crashes can also be avoided and the safe use of these drones as part of our airspace can be facilitated.

Correct information regarding bird populations is of utmost importance during any conservation tactics. This applies to classical bird survey techniques as they tend to consume noticeable time as well. But on the flip side, the use of autoboot bird surveys using drone-mounted cameras fitted with high-tech technologies may be a game-changer in as far as bird monitoring activities are concerned. The article [3] provides, that such technology can quickly detect and count birds from aerial images therefore researchers can save costs and time. Such data provides the ground for the formulation of targeted conservation strategies aiming to protect exotic bird species from extinction. Besides that, the advanced model of the drone through the analysis of their extensive footage, can produce a significant understanding of bird behavior, migration routes, and habitat categories. Such insights, in fact, to a great role extent, prove to be a very crucial element of the landscape of ecological research. One of the main reasons of our interest in learning this technology is that it can benefit humanity in various areas for example prevention of drone crashes and keeping a green environment. It could save lives, and birds, organizations that install them and teach us a lot about nature. On the other hand, it can also contribute to the level of advancement of machine learning.

The distinct risks posed by drones and birds to aviation underscore the critical need for advanced detection and classification technologies. Drones, with their hard materials and high-speed propellers, can cause severe damage upon collision with aircraft, necessitating sophisticated detection systems capable of identifying these threats amidst their small size and variable operational altitudes. Concurrently, while birds generally present a lower risk due to their organic composition, they can still inflict significant damage to sensitive aircraft components, particularly during strikes. Motivated by these challenges, there is a pressing need to develop and refine automated detection systems that can accurately differentiate between drones and birds in real-time. Modern aircraft equipped with advanced radar, optical sensors, and artificial intelligence offer promising solutions for automatic identification and classification of airborne objects. These systems can enable aircraft to initiate precautionary measures, such as altering flight paths or activating collision avoidance protocols, to mitigate risks. Addressing these challenges through cutting-edge research in detection technologies is essential for enhancing aviation safety and ensuring effective responses to both drone and bird-related threats.

The problem of detecting airborne objects is made harder by their size disproportion, which can lead to the misclassification of objects by machine learning models. Research gap

Despite significant advancements in the field of machine learning, particularly in image classification, there are still a few drawbacks that question the effectiveness and precision of recognizing aerial objects such as birds and drones. The study of the present state-of-the-art shows that some parts can be rearranged to improve it.

One of the main research areas has been the detection of a single class, primarily drones. Only recently have multi-class scenarios been investigated to find ways of distinguishing between different classes like birds and drones. This dearth also underscores the need for models that can accurately identify multiple classes in changing conditions.

When dealing with the use of a hybrid model, this is well-covered. While certain CNN architectures, such as MobileNetV2 and VGG16, have been thoroughly examined for image classification applications, not much is known about the integration of more than one architecture into the hybrid model. This study gap leaves plenty of room for exploration. Hybrid models like these could enable better feature extraction and increased accuracy in classification but need to be properly studied to determine their usefulness.

The first limitation that is typical of the majority of the papers is the use of datasets that do not cover object appearance and environmental conditions along with scenarios. At the same time, several datasets turn out to be very small in size, subsequently influencing the model's ability to learn and generalize. Consequently, it is important to have a wider range and more diverse datasets to ensure better performance in training models.

Regarding real-world applications and performance: In practice, although the theoretical models exhibit potential, implementing them into actual situations usually comes with extra difficulties such as rapidly changing illumination conditions, item cover-ups, and human motion. This is an area where there is a dearth of research. Exploring the practical challenges that address the testability of the model under real-world scenarios and ensuring its reliability and effectiveness is crucial.

Efficiency and computational demand: Deep learning models, especially those that use complex CNN architectures, require a lot of computational power that often prevents their usage in real-time applications or on low-end devices. There is a need to develop models that can provide good accuracy and yet be applicable in real-time scenarios as well.

In this paper, we propose the MobVGG model, which attempts to fill in these gaps with a hybrid ensemble-based approach for multi-class classification improvement and efficiency. MobileNetV2 and VGG16 are used together in the model to take advantage of their unique strengths through feature extraction and classification. Moreover, the researchers use an extensive dataset with diverse objects to increase the learning capacity of the model, thus allowing it to be applicable to real-life situations. The goal of this study is to determine whether our proposed MobVGG model is correct and effective using rigorous testing and comparison based on some reference studies in aerial surveillance applications while closing these research gaps simultaneously.

Dataset Collection

Two datasets, labeled Dataset-1 and Dataset-2, are provided as inputs to the model. These datasets likely contain images of birds and drones for training and testing the model.

The study focuses on two distinct categories: 'birds' and 'drones', which are sourced from two datasets, namely dataset-1 [30] and dataset-2 [31]. A total of 4212 images were extracted from these datasets, with the selection process being randomized across various dimensions of the images. Among these, 330 images were extracted from dataset-1, while dataset-2 contributed 3882 images. All images encompass a wide range of orientations, distances, and contrast levels characteristic of both bird and drone images.

Subsequently, the 4212 collected images were divided into training and test sets. The training set comprised 3200 images, while the test set comprised 1012 images, as outlined in Table 1. In the proposed work, the dataset is divided into training and validation subsets to ensure the robustness and generalizability of the model. Specifically, 20 % of the data is allocated for validation purposes, while the remaining 80 % is used for training the model. This validation split is crucial as it allows for an independent evaluation of the model's performance on unseen data during the training process. By reserving a portion of the data for validation, the model's ability to generalize to new, unseen examples can be assessed more accurately, helping to prevent overfitting and ensuring that the model performs well across a broader range of conditions. This approach provides a balanced and rigorous assessment of the model's effectiveness and reliability before its deployment in real-world scenarios.

Ensemble-technique

The model employs an ensemble technique known as 'MobVGG' to merge predictions from MobileNetV2 and VGG-16 architectures, aiming to enhance classification accuracy. Its primary objective is to boost computational efficiency without compromising accuracy by amalgamating the VGG-16 and MobileNetV2 architectures, both of which are esteemed for their pre-trained proficiency in classifying images across diverse domains by effectively extracting high-level features [32,33]. By combining the strong points of both architectures, the model tries to make the precision of distinguishing between the 'bird' and 'drone' images sharper, as well as to capitalize on their complimentary advantages. MobileNetV2 has a set of convolutional blocks with stride 2, along with depthwise separable (Dwise) convolutions, ReLU activations and linear convolutions, and then finally goes through the process of ‘ensembling’. On the flip side of this, VGG-16 constitutes of convolutional layers followed by pooling layers, and its output is also directed ensembling. The outputs of two MobileNetV2 and VGG-16 networks are next combined through the ensembling technique, which can be averaging or weighted averaging, to result with an only one output. The combination of this ensemble output is subjected to a Global Average Pooling layer prior to being passed on to the last output layers.

Global-Average-Pooling

In contrast to other methods such as flattening followed by fully connected layers, which can consume significant computational resources, global average pooling offers a more efficient alternative. Its ability to reduce overall computational demands is particularly vital when merging multiple transfer learning models. Moreover, Global-Average-Pooling ensures that the combined model maintains translation invariance while preserving essential spatial information from each feature map. This adaptability is crucial when integrating transfer learning models pre-trained on diverse datasets or domains. By offering a scalable representation of the input and capturing key features, global average pooling facilitates the creation of a robust and versatile combined model suitable for various tasks and datasets.

Optimization insights from ablation study

The ablation study provided valuable insights into optimizing the model by integrating MobileNetV2 and VGG16. It was found that combining the features from both models improved performance compared to using each model individually. Selectively unfreezing some layers of MobileNetV2, while keeping VGG16 layers frozen, allowed for effective fine-tuning, leveraging pretrained features from VGG16 and enabling dynamic learning from MobileNetV2. The concatenation of feature maps from both models was confirmed to significantly enhance accuracy, as it captured a richer set of information.

In addition, the study validated the use of GlobalAveragePooling2D for dimensionality reduction and determined that an Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 0.0001 provided stable training. A batch size of 16 and training for 10 epochs were deemed optimal, balancing computational efficiency with model convergence. These findings led to a refined model configuration, effectively combining MobileNetV2 and VGG16 with adjusted layers and parameters to achieve improved performance.

Conclusion

Recapitulation

In summary, the study demonstrates the MobVGG model as an innovative approach to the difficult problem of bird and drone activity identification. The MobVGG model that combines the strong points of MobileNetV2 with VGG16 architectures and is based on ensemble techniques achieved an impressive accuracy rate of 96 %, which overpassed the former achievements in multi-class classification.

Therefore, this accomplishment is meaningful through its practicality in different areas. Accuracy in airborne object recognition and classification is critical to aviation safety, especially to minimize impacts such as bird strikes that are extremely hazardous to human life and expensive for airlines. Through equipping radar systems with better methods to tell apart a 'bird' from a 'drone', the model of MobVGG adds to the safety precautions and lowers the frequency of accidents.

Beyond that, the collection of 'bird' data plays a key role in the ecological research and conservation through development of species-specific conservation strategies and monitoring of the endangered populations. Traditional 'bird' survey techniques are mostly time-consuming and laborious, but the utilisation of autonomous 'bird' surveys with 'drone'-mounted cameras will transform the field of 'bird' by rapidly locating and counting 'bird' from above the ground. MobVGG model makes this possible by accurately classifying 'bird' images, in turn helping researchers to save time and money, while getting additional information about 'bird' behavior, migration pattern, and preferences.

Contrasting the function of the MobVGG model with other researches highlights the superiority of this model with its accuracy and precision. The previous research reported the accuracy rates that range from 70 % to 90 %, with some of them achieving higher precision but lower overall accuracy. The MobVGG model beats the other studies by being the balanced combination of high accuracy, precision, recalling and F1 score, therefore suggested for many applications.

Limitations and future work

Although the conducted work brought forth satisfactory outcomes, it is also critical to consider and overcome the limitations and also explore future options in order to boost the improvement. First of all, the model's prediction is about just four classes now. These classes are 'bird' and 'drone'. As a result, the model is suitable in situations where there are other classes or subclasses which it cannot deal with in the present environment In conclusion, the example used the model to point out some class misidentifications, as discussed in Section 5.2 titled "Wrongly Predicted Classes”, indicating the need for changes that shall make it more accurate and robust in the classification process. As for instance, the intricacy which involves the design with use of VGG-16, MobileNetV2, and GlobalAveragePooling models may potentially affect the efficiency and scalability of the system; this therefore requires further studies to develop simple and effective model architectures. Besides, it is also imperative to acknowledge the fact that the training and test datasets, totaling 3200 and 1012 images, are rather small and hence the model might not be able to fully exploit the diversity of real-world situations and face such a challenge as imbalanced dataset class distribution during evaluation. Furthermore, deciding to train with 6 epochs and 16 batch sizes could be not optimal for achieving stable convergence or inferring complex patterns from the data. Coming work can target the above denotations by investigating ways of improving the model's class prediction ability, elevating its accuracy and robustness, working on various architectures to ensure efficiency, increasing the size of the dataset to improve the diversity aspect, and finally achieving better convergence, thus enhancing the model's performance. On the other hand, future studies that include such advanced methods as transfer learning and semi-supervised learning would widen the possibilities of enhancing generalization and scalability.

As a direction for future work, optimizing hyperparameters for classification tasks using advanced techniques could significantly enhance model performance, as discussed in Ref. [41]. Employing swarm intelligence algorithms, such as Particle Swarm Optimization, offers a promising approach by mimicking the collective behavior of decentralized systems to efficiently explore and identify optimal hyperparameter combinations. Additionally, manual tuning methods, though more resource-intensive, can complement this approach by systematically adjusting hyperparameters based on empirical data and domain-specific insights. Combining these strategies could lead to improved accuracy and robustness in classification models, providing valuable advancements in handling complex tasks such as differentiating between drones and birds.

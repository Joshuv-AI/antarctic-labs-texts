Hyperbolicity and fundamental groups of complex quasi-projective varieties (I): Maximal quasi-Albanese dimension by Nevanlinna theory
Benoît Cadorel, Ya Deng, Katsutoshi Yamanoi

Abstract
Résumé. This is the first part of a series of three papers. In this paper, we establish a Big Picard type theorem for holomorphic maps f : Y → X f:Y\to X , where Y Y is a ramified covering of the punctured disc 𝔻 ∗ \mathbb{D}^{*} with small ramification and X X is a complex quasi-projective variety of log-general type and of maximal quasi-Albanese dimension. As a byproduct, we prove the generalized Green–Griffiths–Lang conjecture for such X X . This paper summarizes the parts of the three-paper series that are based primarily on Nevanlinna theory.

Introduction

0.1.
Generalized Green-Griffiths-Lang conjecture

The generalized version of the Green–Griffiths and Lang conjectures is the complex analogue of the Bombieri–Lang conjecture about the Zariski density of the set of rational points of an algebraic variety of general type. It is a fundamental problem in the study of the hyperbolicity of algebraic varieties (cf.
[
Lan91
, I, 3.5]
and
[
Lan91
, VIII, Conj. 1.3]
). To formulate this conjecture, we first introduce several notions of non-hyperbolicity loci, usually referred to as special subsets in various senses.

Definition 0.1

(Special subsets)
.

Let
X
be a smooth quasi-projective variety.

(i)

\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{sab}}(X):=\overline{\bigcup_{f}f(A_{0})}^{\mathrm{Zar}}
, where
f
ranges over all non-constant rational maps
f:A\dashrightarrow X
from all non-trivial semi-abelian varieties
A
to
X
such that
f
is regular
A_{0}\to X
on a Zariski open subset
A_{0}\subset A
whose complement
A\backslash A_{0}
has codimension at least two ;

(ii)

\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{h}}(X):=\overline{\bigcup_{f}f(\mathbb{C})}^{\mathrm{Zar}}
, where
f
ranges over all non-constant holomorphic maps from
\mathbb{C}
to
X
;

(iii)

\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{alg}}(X):=\overline{\bigcup_{V}V}^{\mathrm{Zar}}
, where
V
ranges over all positive-dimensional closed subvarieties of
X
which are not of log general type ;

(iv)

\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{p}}(X):=\overline{\bigcup_{f}f(\mathbb{D}^{*})}^{\mathrm{Zar}}
, where
f
ranges over all holomorphic maps from the punctured disk
\mathbb{D}^{*}
to
X
with essential singularity at the origin, i.e.,
f
has no holomorphic extension
\bar{f}:\mathbb{D}\to\overline{X}
to a projective compactification
\overline{X}
.

The
generalized Green-Griffiths-Lang conjecture
can be stated as follows.

Conjecture 0.2

(Generalized Green-Griffiths-Lang)
.

Let
X
be a smooth quasi-projective variety. Then the following properties are equivalent :

(i)

X
is of log general type ;

(ii)

X
is
strongly of general type
, i.e.,

\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{alg}}(X)\subsetneqq X
;

(iii)

X
is
pseudo Picard hyperbolic
, i.e.,
\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{p}}(X)\subsetneqq X
;

(iv)

X
is
pseudo Brody hyperbolic
, i.e.,
\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{h}}(X)\subsetneqq X
;

(v)

\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{sab}}(X)\subsetneqq X
.

The first two sets
\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{sab}}(X)
and
\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{h}}(X)
are introduced by Lang for the compact case.
He made the following two conjectures (cf.
[
Lan91
, I, 3.5]
and
[
Lan91
, VIII, Conjecture 1.3]
) :

—

\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{sab}}(X)\subsetneqq X
if and only if
X
is of general type.

—

\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{sab}}(X)=\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{h}}(X)
.

The first assertion implicitly include the following third conjecture :

—

\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{sab}}(X)=\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{alg}}(X)
.

The original two conjectures imply the famous strong Green-Griffiths conjecture that varieties of (log) general type are pseudo Brody hyperbolic.

Let us discuss some known cases of the conjecture.

0.2
is known to hold for curves. However, when
\dim X\geq 2
, there has been only few progress. We summarize below four classes of varieties for which
0.2
has been established :

—

Complex projective surfaces with big cotangent bundles, proved by McQuillan
[
McQ98
]
, and later extended to quasi-projective surfaces with big logarithmic cotangent bundles by El Goul
[
EG03
]
.

—

Subvarieties of abelian varieties, by the classical theorem of Bloch–Ochiai–Kawamata, and more generally subvarieties of semi-abelian varieties, by Noguchi
[
Nog81
]
.

—

Projective varieties of maximal Albanese dimension, by Kawamata
[
Kaw81
]
and the third author
[
Yam15a
]
.

—

General hypersurfaces in projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n}
(
n\geq 3
) of sufficiently high degree, proved in
[
DMR10
]
(based on the strategy of Siu
[
Siu04
]
), with degree bounds subsequently improved in
[
Dar16
,
MT22
,
BK24
,
Cad24
]
; as well as complements of general hypersurfaces of high degree in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
(
n\geq 2
), proved in
[
Dar16
,
BD19
]
, to mention only a few.

In this paper, we establish
0.2
for quasi-projective varieties of maximal quasi-Albanese dimension
(see
C
), thus generalizing the result of
[
Yam15a
]
into the non-compact setting.
Here we say that a quasi-projective manifold
X
has maximal quasi-Albanese dimension if the quasi-Albanese map
\alpha:X\to A(X)
is generically finite onto
\overline{\alpha(X)}
.
By the universal property of the quasi-Albanese maps, this is equivalent to the existence of a morphism
a:X\to A
to a semi-Abelian variety
A
such that
\dim X=\dim a(X)
.

0.2.
Main results

We consider a Riemann surface
Y
equipped with a proper surjective holomorphic map

\pi:Y\to\mathbb{C}_{>\delta}
,
where
\mathbb{C}_{>\delta}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid|z|>\delta\}

for some positive constant
\delta>0
.
Our first result is a Big Picard type theorem for holomorphic maps
f:Y\to X
, where
X
is a smooth complex quasi-projective variety of log-general type and of maximal quasi-Albanese dimension.
This theorem holds provided that
\pi:Y\to\mathbb{C}_{>\delta}
has small ramifications compared with the growth order of
f
, i.e.,
N_{{\rm ram}\,\pi}(r)=O(\log r)+o(T_{f}(r))||
.
The notations are from Nevanlinna theory ; specifically, the symbol
||
means that the stated estimate is valid as
r\to\infty
with the possible exception of a set of finite Lebesgue measure.
We will introduce other notations in
section

3
.

Theorem A
.

Let
X
be a smooth quasi-projective variety which is of log general type.
Assume that there is a morphism
a:X\to A
to a semi-Abelian variety
A
such that
\dim X=\dim a(X)
.
Then there exists a proper Zariski closed set
\Xi\subsetneqq X
with the following property :
Let
f:Y\to X
be a holomorphic map such that
N_{{\rm ram}\,\pi}(r)=O(\log r)+o(T_{f}(r))||
and that
f(Y)\not\subset\Xi
.
Then
f
does not have essential singularity over
\infty
, i.e., there exists an extension
\overline{f}:\overline{Y}\to\overline{X}
of
f
, where
\overline{Y}
is a Riemann surface such that
\pi:Y\to\mathbb{C}_{>\delta}
extends to a proper map
\overline{\pi}:\overline{Y}\to\mathbb{C}_{>\delta}\cup\{\infty\}

and
\overline{X}
is a compactification of
X
.

Note that the identity map
\mathbb{C}_{>\delta}\to\mathbb{C}_{>\delta}
trivially satisfies the small ramification condition in the theorem.
Thus, we can apply
A
when
Y
is
\mathbb{C}_{>\delta}
itself, which implies that
X
in the theorem is Picard hyperbolic.
Nevertheless, for further applications it is essential to study Nevanlinna theory not only on
\mathbb{C}_{>\delta}
itself, but also on its ramified coverings.
This broader perspective is crucial for establishing the hyperbolicity of algebraic varieties.
Such applications appear, for instance, in the recent work of Kebekus and Rousseau
[
KR24
]
on the hyperbolicity of Campana orbifolds (or
\mathcal{C}
-pairs), as well as in Part (II) of this paper series, where we study the hyperbolicity of complex quasi-projective varieties carrying a big and semi-simple complex local system (see
[
CDY25
, Theorems A, C & D]
).
In particular, for further applications in
[
CDY25
]
, we will make use of the following consequence of
A
.
See
section

11
for more discussion about the condition stated in the theorem.

Theorem B

(=
11.1
)
.

Let
X
be a smooth quasi-projective variety with a smooth projective compactification
\overline{X}
such that the boundary divisor
D=\overline{X}\setminus X
is a simple normal crossing divisor.
Assume that there exists a finite surjective morphism
p:\overline{\Sigma}\to\overline{X}
from a normal projective variety
\overline{\Sigma}
together with non-zero sections
\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{l}\in H^{0}(\overline{\Sigma},p^{*}\Omega_{\overline{X}}(\log D))
satisfying the following two conditions :

(i)

setting
\Sigma=p^{-1}(X)
, the variety
\Sigma
is of log-general type and admits a morphism
a:\Sigma\to A
into a semi-abelian variety
A
with
\dim\Sigma=\dim a(\Sigma)
;

(ii)

defining

R:=\{s\in\overline{\Sigma}\mid\exists\,i\ \text{such that }\tau_{i}(s)=0\},

then
R\subsetneqq\overline{\Sigma}
is a proper Zariski closed subset, and
p:\overline{\Sigma}\to\overline{X}
is étale outside

.

Then every holomorphic map
f:\mathbb{D}^{*}\to X
with Zariski dense image has a holomorphic extension
\bar{f}:\mathbb{D}\to\overline{X}
.

Next we state our result on
0.2
, which we prove as a consequence of
A
.

Theorem C
.

Let
X
be a smooth quasi-projective variety.
Assume that there is a morphism
a:X\to A
to a semi-Abelian variety
A
such that
\dim X=\dim a(X)
.
Then the following properties are equivalent :

(a)

X
is of log general type ;

(b)

X
is strongly of log general type ;

(c)

X
is pseudo Picard hyperbolic ;

(d)

X
is pseudo Brody hyperbolic ;

(e)

\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{sab}}(X)\subsetneqq X
.

It is worth noting that the equivalence of
(a)

However our implication
(a)

involves the analytic statement

1.
Varieties with logarithmic Kodaira dimension zero and maximal quasi-Albanese dimension

We first recall a fundamental result for semiabelian varieties.

Lemma 1.1

(
[
NW14
, Propositions 5.6.21 & 5.6.22]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a semi-abelian variety.

(a)

Let
X\subset\mathcal{A}
be a closed subvariety. Then
\overline{\kappa}(X)\geq 0
with equality if and only if
X
is a translate of a semi-abelian subvariety.

(b)

Let
Z\subset\mathcal{A}
be a Zariski closed subset. Then
\overline{\kappa}(\mathcal{A}-Z)\geq 0
with equality if and only if
Z
has no component of codimension
1
. ∎

Next we prove two lemmas that will be used to discuss the structures on varieties with maximal quasi-Albanese dimension and logarithmic Kodaira dimension zero.

Lemma 1.2
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a dominant birational morphism of quasi-projective manifolds.
Let
E\subset X
be a Zariski closed subset such that
\overline{f(E)}
has codimension at least two.
Assume
\bar{\kappa}(Y)\geq 0
.
Then
\bar{\kappa}(X-E)=\bar{\kappa}(X)
.

Démonstration.

Let
\bar{f}:\bar{X}\to\bar{Y}
be a proper birational morphism which extends
f:X\to Y
, where
\bar{X}
and
\bar{Y}
are smooth projective compactifications of
X
and
Y
such that
B=\bar{Y}-Y
is a simple normal crossing divisor. Since the logarithmic Kodaira dimension is birationally invariant, we may further blow-up
\bar{X}
to assume that

—

\bar{E}+(\bar{X}-X)
is a simple normal crossing divisor,

where
\bar{E}\subset\bar{X}
is the closure of
E
.
Then we note that
\bar{X}-X
is also simple normal crossing.
Write
\bar{X}-X=\bar{f}^{-1}(B)+D
, where
D
is a reduced divisor on
\bar{X}
and
\bar{f}^{-1}(B)=\mathrm{supp}\bar{f}^{*}B
.
Then we have
f^{*}K_{\bar{Y}}(B)+\bar{E}\leq K_{\bar{X}}(\bar{f}^{-1}(B))
.
So we write
K_{\bar{X}}(\bar{f}^{-1}(B))=f^{*}K_{\bar{Y}}(B)+\bar{E}+F
, where
F
is effective.
Then we have

K_{\bar{X}}(\bar{f}^{-1}(B))+D+\bar{E}=\bar{f}^{*}K_{\bar{Y}}(B)+D+2\bar{E}+F

By the assumption
\bar{\kappa}(Y)\geq 0
, one has
nK_{\bar{Y}}(B)\geq 0
for some positive integer
n>0
.
Therefore,

n(\bar{f}^{*}K_{\bar{Y}}(B)+D+2\bar{E}+F)\leq 2n(\bar{f}^{*}K_{\bar{Y}}(B)+D+\bar{E}+F)=2n(K_{\bar{X}}(\bar{f}^{-1}(B))+D).

Thus

n(K_{\bar{X}}(\bar{f}^{-1}(B))+D+\bar{E})\leq 2n(K_{\bar{X}}(\bar{f}^{-1}(B))+D)
.
Recall that
\bar{f}^{-1}(B)+D+\bar{E}
and
\bar{f}^{-1}(B)+D
are both simple normal crossing divisors. It follows

\bar{\kappa}(X-E)\leq\bar{\kappa}(X)
.
Hence
\bar{\kappa}(X-E)=\bar{\kappa}(X)
.
∎

Lemma 1.3
.

Let
\alpha:X\to\mathcal{A}
be a (possibly non-proper) birational morphism from a quasi-projective manifold
X
to a semi-abelian variety
\mathcal{A}
with
\overline{\kappa}(X)=0
. Then there exists a Zariski closed subset
Z\subset\mathcal{A}
of codimension at least two such that
\alpha
is isomorphic
over
\mathcal{A}\backslash Z
.

Démonstration.

Since
\alpha:X\to\mathcal{A}
is birational, we remove the exceptional locus of
X\to\mathcal{A}
from
X
to get
X^{\circ}\subset X
such that
\alpha:X^{\circ}\to\alpha(X^{\circ})
is an isomorphism.
By
\bar{\kappa}(\mathcal{A})=0
, we may apply
1.2
to get
\bar{\kappa}(X^{\circ})=\bar{\kappa}(X)=0
.
By
1.1
,
\mathcal{A}-\alpha(X^{\circ})
has codimension at least two. ∎

We establish several results concerning structures on varieties with maximal quasi-Albanese dimension and logarithmic Kodaira dimension zero.

Lemma 1.4
.

Let
\alpha:X\to\mathcal{A}
be a (possibly non-proper) morphism from a quasi-projective manifold
X
to a semi-abelian variety
\mathcal{A}
with
\overline{\kappa}(X)=0
.
Assume that
\dim X=\dim\alpha(X)
. Then
\pi_{1}(X)
is abelian.

Démonstration.

Step 1. We may assume that
\alpha
is dominant and birational.

Consider the quasi-Stein factorisation
X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle h}}{{\to}}Y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle k}}{{\to}}\mathcal{A}
of
\alpha
, where
h
is birational (might not proper), and
k
is a finite morphism.
This is constructed as follows.
Let
X\subset X^{\prime}
be a partial smooth compactification of
X
such that
\alpha
extends to a proper map
\alpha^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\to\mathcal{A}
.
Let
X^{\prime}\to Y\to\mathcal{A}
be the Stein factorization of
\alpha^{\prime}
, where
h^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\to Y
have connected fibers and
k:Y\to\mathcal{A}
is finite.
By
\dim X^{\prime}=\dim Y
, the map
h^{\prime}
is birational.
Let
h:X\to Y
be the restriction of
h^{\prime}
to
X
.
Then
h
is birational.

Now since
\bar{\kappa}(X)=0
, one has

0=\bar{\kappa}(X)\geq\bar{\kappa}(Y)\geq\bar{\kappa}(k(Y))\geq 0

where the last inequality follows from
1.1
.
Hence
\bar{\kappa}(Y)=\bar{\kappa}(k(Y))=0
. By
1.1
and Kawamata
[
Kaw81
, Theorem 26]
,
k(Y)
is a semi-abelian variety and
k
is finite étale. In conclusion, it now suffices to prove the proposition with
\mathcal{A}
replaced by
Y
.
Hence in the following, we may assume that
\alpha
is dominant and birational.

Step 2.

We apply
1.3
to get the isomorphism
\alpha|_{X^{\circ}}:X^{\circ}\to\mathcal{A}^{\circ}
, where
\mathcal{A}-\mathcal{A}^{\circ}
of codimension at least two.
Let
\bar{\alpha}:\overline{X}\to\mathcal{A}
be a proper birational morphism which extends
\alpha:X\to\mathcal{A}
such that
\overline{X}
is smooth. One gets the following commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}(X^{\circ})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}(\mathcal{A}^{\circ})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}(\bar{\alpha}^{-1}(\mathcal{A}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}(\mathcal{A})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}

where the two rows are isomorphisms because they are induced by proper birational morphisms between smooth quasi-projective varieties. The map
\pi_{1}(X^{\circ})\to\pi_{1}(X)
is surjective since it is induced by the inclusion of a dense Zariski open subset. It follows that all the groups in the previous diagram are isomorphic, so
\pi_{1}(X)\cong\pi_{1}(\mathcal{A})
is abelian.
∎

Lemma 1.5
.

Let
\alpha:X\to\mathcal{A}
be a (possibly non-proper) morphism from a quasi-projective manifold
X
to a semi-abelian variety
\mathcal{A}
with
\overline{\kappa}(X)=0
.
Assume that
\dim X=\dim\alpha(X)
and
\dim X>0
.
Then
X
admits a Zariski dense entire curve
\mathbb{C}\to X
.
In particular,
\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{h}}(X)=X
.

Démonstration.

As in the step 1 of the proof of
1.4
, we may assume that
\alpha:X\to\mathcal{A}
is birational.
We apply
1.3
to get the isomorphism
\alpha|_{X^{\circ}}:X^{\circ}\to\mathcal{A}^{\circ}
, where
\mathcal{A}-\mathcal{A}^{\circ}
of codimension at least two.
Set
Z=\mathcal{A}-\mathcal{A}^{\circ}
.
We shall show that
\mathcal{A}^{\circ}=\mathcal{A}-Z
contains a Zariski dense entire curve.
Let
\varphi:\mathbb{C}\to\mathcal{A}
be a one parameter group such that
\varphi(\mathbb{C})\subset\mathcal{A}
is Zariski dense.
We define
F:\mathbb{C}\times Z\to\mathcal{A}
by
F(c,z)=z+\varphi(c)
.
Then
F
is a holomorphic map.
Let
Z_{1}\subset Z_{2}\subset\cdots
be a sequence of compact subsets of
Z
such that
\cup_{n}Z_{n}=Z
.
Let
K_{n}=\{|z|\leq n\}\subset\mathbb{C}
.
Then
F(K_{n}\times Z_{n})\subset\mathcal{A}
is a compact subset.
Set
O_{n}=\mathcal{A}\backslash F(K_{n}\times Z_{n})
.
Then
O_{n}\subset\mathcal{A}
is an open subset.
Note that
\dim(\mathbb{C}\times Z)<\dim\mathcal{A}
.
Hence
O_{n}
is a dense subset.
Hence by the Baire category theorem, the intersection
\cap_{n}O_{n}
is dense in
\mathcal{A}
.
In particular, we may take
x\in\cap_{n}O_{n}
.
We define
f:\mathbb{C}\to\mathcal{A}
by
f(c)=x+\varphi(c)
.
Then
f(\mathbb{C})\cap Z=\emptyset
.
Indeed suppose contary
f(c)\in Z
.
Then
F(-c,f(c))=f(c)+\varphi(-c)=x
.
Since
(-c,f(c))\in K_{n}\times Z_{n}
for sufficiently large
n
, we have
x\in F(K_{n}\times Z_{n})
, so
x\not\in O_{n}
.
This contradicts the choice of
x
.
∎

.

Let
\alpha:X\to\mathcal{A}
be a (possibly non-proper) morphism from a quasi-projective manifold

to a semi-abelian variety
\mathcal{A}
with

.
Assume that
\dim X=\dim\alpha(X)
and

.

.

, we may assume that

We apply

to get the isomorphism

of the isomorphism

.

.
∎

We now apply the results of

. We begin with some general facts about special subsets.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth quasi-projective variety. Then one has
\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{sab}}\subseteq\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{h}}\subseteq\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{p}}
.

Démonstration.

Let
A_{0}\subset A
be a Zariski open subset of a non-trivial semi-Abelian variety
A
such that the complement
A\backslash A_{0}
has codimension at least two.
By
1.1
, we have
\bar{\kappa}(A_{0})=0
.
Thus
A_{0}
admits a Zariski dense entire curve
\mathbb{C}\to A_{0}

Some notions in Nevanlinna theory

We shall recall some elements of Nevanlinna theory (cf.
[
NW14
]
,
[
Yam15b
]
).
Let
\delta>0
be a positive constant and let
Y
be a Riemann surface equipped with a proper surjective holomorphic map

\pi:Y\to\mathbb{C}_{>\delta}
.
For
r>2\delta
, define

Y(r)=\pi^{-1}\big(\mathbb{C}_{>2\delta}(r)\big)

where
\mathbb{C}_{>2\delta}(r)=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid 2\delta<|z|<r\}
.
In the following, we always assume that
r>2\delta
.
The
ramification counting function
of the covering
\pi:Y\to\mathbb{C}_{>\delta}
is defined by

N_{{\rm ram}\,\pi}(r):=\frac{1}{{\rm deg}\pi}\int_{2\delta}^{r}\left[\sum_{y\in{Y}(t)}{\rm ord}\,_{y}{\rm ram}\,\pi\right]\frac{dt}{t},

where
{\rm ram}\,\pi\subset Y
is the ramification divisor of
\pi:Y\to\mathbb{C}_{>\delta}
.

Let
X
be a projective variety and let
Z
be a closed subscheme of
X
. Let
f:Y\rightarrow X
be a holomorphic map such that
f(Y)\not\subset Z
.
Since
Y
is one dimensional, the pull-back
f^{*}Z
is a divisor on
Y
. The counting function and truncated function are defined to be

\begin{gathered}N_{f}(r,Z):=\frac{1}{\operatorname{deg}\pi}\int_{2\delta}^{r}\left[\sum_{y\in Y(t)}\operatorname{ord}_{y}f^{*}Z\right]\frac{dt}{t},\\
{N}_{f}^{(k)}(r,Z):=\frac{1}{\operatorname{deg}\pi}\int_{2\delta}^{r}\left[\sum_{y\in Y(t)}\min\left\{k,\operatorname{ord}_{y}f^{*}Z\right\}\right]\frac{dt}{t},\end{gathered}

where
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
is a positive integer.
We define the proximity function by

m_{f}(r,Z):=\frac{1}{\operatorname{deg}\pi}\int_{y\in\pi^{-1}(\{|z|=r\})}\lambda_{Z}(f(y))\ \frac{d\mathrm{arg}\ \pi(y)}{2\pi},

where
\lambda_{Z}:X-\mathrm{supp}\ Z\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is a Weil function for
Z
(cf.
[
Yam04
, Prop 2.2.3]
).

Let
L
be a line bundle on
X
.
Let
f:Y\to X
be a holomorphic map.
We define the order function
T_{f}(r,L)
as follows.
First suppose that
X
is smooth.
We equip with a smooth hermitian metric
h_{L}
, and let
c_{1}(L,h_{L})
be the curvature form of
(L,h_{L})
. Set

T_{f}(r,L):=\frac{1}{\operatorname{deg}\pi}\int_{2\delta}^{r}\left[\int_{Y(t)}f^{*}c_{1}(L,h_{L})\right]\frac{dt}{t}.

This definition is independent of the choice of the hermitian metric up to a function
O(\log r)
.
For the general case, let
V\subset X
be the Zariski closure of
f(Y)
and let
V^{\prime}\to V
be a smooth model of
V
.
This induces a morphism
p:V^{\prime}\to X
.
We have a natural lifting
f^{\prime}:Y\to V^{\prime}
of
f:Y\to V
.
Then we set

T_{f}(r,L):=T_{f^{\prime}}(r,p^{*}L)+O(\log r).

This definition does not depend on the choice of
V^{\prime}\to V
up to a function
O(\log r)
.

Theorem 3.1

(First Main Theorem)
.

Let
X
be a projective variety and let
D
be an effective Cartier divisor on
X
.
Let
f:Y\to X
be a holomorphic curve such that
f(Y)\not\subset D
.
Then

N_{f}(r,D)+m_{f}(r,D)=T_{f}(r,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))+O(\log r).

Démonstration.

Let
V\subset X
be the Zariski closure of
f(Y)
and let
p:V^{\prime}\to V
be a desingularization.
Let
f^{\prime}:Y\to V^{\prime}
be the canonical lifting of
f
.
Replacing
X
,
D
and
f
by
V^{\prime}
,
p^{*}D
and
f^{\prime}
, respectively, we may assume that
X
is smooth.

Let
||\cdot||
be a smooth Hermitian metric on
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
and let
s_{D}
be the associated section for
D
.
By the Poincaé-Lelong formula, we have

2dd^{c}\log(1/||s_{D}\circ f(y)||)=-\sum_{y\in Y}(\mathrm{ord}_{y}f^{*}D)\delta_{y}+f^{*}c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(D),||\cdot||),

where
\delta_{y}
is Dirac current suported on
y
.
Integrating over
Y(t)
, we get

2\int_{Y(t)}dd^{c}\log(1/||s_{D}\circ f(y)||)=-\sum_{y\in Y(t)}\mathrm{ord}_{y}f^{*}D+\int_{Y(t)}f^{*}c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(D),||\cdot||)

Hence, we get

\begin{split}-N_{f}(r,D)+T_{f}(r,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))&=\lim_{\delta^{\prime}\to\delta+}\frac{2}{\deg\pi}\int_{2\delta^{\prime}}^{r}\frac{dt}{t}\int_{\pi^{-1}\big(\mathbb{C}_{>2\delta^{\prime}}(t)\big)}dd^{c}\log\left(\frac{1}{||s_{D}\circ f(z)||}\right)\\
&=\lim_{\delta^{\prime}\to\delta+}\frac{2}{\deg\pi}\int_{2\delta^{\prime}}^{r}\frac{dt}{t}\int_{\partial\pi^{-1}\big(\mathbb{C}_{>2\delta^{\prime}}(t)\big)}d^{c}\log\left(\frac{1}{||s_{D}\circ f(z)||}\right)\\
&=m_{f}(r,D)-m_{f}(2\delta,D)-C_{f}\log(r/2\delta),\end{split}

where we set

C_{f}=\lim_{\delta^{\prime}\to\delta+}\frac{2}{\deg\pi}\int_{\pi^{-1}\{|z|=2\delta^{\prime}\}}d^{c}\log\left(\frac{1}{||s_{D}\circ f(z)||}\right),

which is a constant independent of
r
.
∎

Démonstration.

m(r,f^{\prime}/f)\leq 3T(r,f)+O(\log r)\ ||

Convention and notation.

In the rest of this paper, we use the following convention and notation.
Let
\Sigma
be a quasi-projective variety and let
\overline{\Sigma}
be a projective compactification.
We denote by

Remark 10.11
.

The proof of
A
is based on the arguments in
[
Yam15a
]
and
[
NWY13

.
A major difficulty in treating the non-compact case, compared with the compact case in

Yam15a

To overcome this problem, we use a more general type of "cover" than an étale cover.
In
[
NWY08

[
NWY08
, (5.9)]
).
An important issue with using this flat cover is to construct a lift of holomorphic maps onto it, such that the order function of the lift is bounded by that of the original map (cf.
[
NWY08
, Lemma 5.8]
).
The authors in
[
NWY08
]
only considered holomorphic maps from the complex plane

. Thanks to the simple connectedness of

, such a lift could be constructed easily.
In contrast, we consider holomorphic maps from the covering space

Therefore, in this paper, we choose a different cover

in the proof of

After the base change by this cover, we obtain the lift

of

from the covering space

.
We then apply the results of

5
–

9
to this lift

.
We remark that the map

may hit the boundary

in
section

5
–
section

.
We also remark that even if we restrict the statement of

with Zariski dense image has a holomorphic extension

.

In the second condition
(ii)
, we observe that
p^{*}\Omega_{\overline{X}}(\log D)
is a locally free sheaf of rank
\dim X
on
\overline{\Sigma}
.
At a point

, we then consider

\tau_{i}(s)\in(p^{*}\Omega_{\overline{X}}(\log D))_{s}\otimes\mathbb{C}
, where
(p^{*}\Omega_{\overline{X}}(\log D))_{s}
denotes the stalk at
s\in\overline{\Sigma}
.
Thus the set
\{s\in\overline{\Sigma};\tau_{i}(s)=0\}
is a Zariski closed subset of
\overline{\Sigma}
, since it coincides with the zero locus of a section
\tau_{i}
of the corresponding vector bundle on
\overline{\Sigma}
.

We shall see in Part II of our series that a covering
\overline{\Sigma}\to\overline{X}
with the property of

exists provided there is a representation

into an almost simple algebraic group
G
defined over a

-adic field

, where
\varrho
is big, unbounded, and Zariski dense.
This covering is realized by a spectral cover constructed from the
\varrho
-equivariant pluriharmonic map
\widetilde{X}\to\Delta(G)
from the universal cover
\widetilde{X}
of
X
to the Bruhat–Tits building
\Delta(G)
of

.
This pluriharmonic map was developed by the second author and co-authors
[
BDDM22
]
, and it will be employed in Part II of our series to construct the corresponding spectral cover.

To prove

we start with the following lemma.

Lemma 11.2
.

Let
X
be a smooth quasi-projective variety with a smooth projective compactification
\overline{X}
such that the boundary divisor
D=\overline{X}\setminus X
is a simple normal crossing divisor.
Let
\omega\in H^{0}(\overline{X},\Omega_{\overline{X}}(\log D))
be a nonzero section, and let
u:Z\to X
be a morphism from a smooth quasi-projective variety
Z
such that
u^{*}\omega\in H^{0}(Z,\Omega_{Z})
vanishes.
Then, for every holomorphic map
g:Y\to X
with Zariski dense image and
g^{*}\omega=0
, we have

\overline{N}_{g}(r,u(Z))=o(T_{g}(r))+O(\log r)||
.

Démonstration.

Let
\alpha:X\to A_{X}
be the quasi-Albanese map.
Then there exists a non-zero holomorphic one-form
\tilde{\omega}
on
A_{X}
such that
\alpha^{*}\tilde{\omega}=\omega
.
Let
B\subset A_{X}
be the largest semi-abelian subvariety of
A_{X}
on which the pull-back of the holomorphic one form
\tilde{\omega}
vanishes.
Set
A^{\prime}=A_{X}/B
.
Let
q:A_{X}\to A^{\prime}
be the quotient map, and let
\beta:X\to A^{\prime}
be the composition of
\alpha:X\to A_{X}
with
q:A_{X}\to A^{\prime}
.

We claim that
\beta(u(Z))
is a point.
Indeed, we have the following commutative diagram :

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{X}/B=A^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{Z}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces u}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j}

Here
a:Z\to A_{Z}
denotes the quasi-Albanese map, and
j:A_{Z}\to A_{X}
is the induced morphism of algebraic groups.
By the definition of
B
, the image of
j(A_{Z})
is contained in
B
.
Therefore,
\beta(u(Z))
is a single point, as claimed.

Let
\beta(u(Z))=\{a\}
for some
a\in A^{\prime}
.
Let
\phi:S\to A^{\prime}
be the normalization of the Zariski closure
\overline{\beta(X)}\subset A^{\prime}
.
Since
X
is smooth,
\beta
factors
\phi
as follows :

X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\psi}}{{\to}}S\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi}}{{\to}}A^{\prime}.

Since
\phi
is finite and
\beta(u(Z))=\{a\}
, there is a point
s\in S
so that
\psi(u(Z))=\{s\}
.

There exists a non-zero logarithmic one-form
\omega^{\prime}
on
A^{\prime}
such that
\widetilde{\omega}=q^{*}\omega^{\prime}
.
Then

\beta^{*}\omega^{\prime}=\omega.

(11.1)

Since
\omega^{\prime}
is a linear logarithmic one-form on
A^{\prime}
, we have
d\omega^{\prime}=0
.
Hence, by the Poincaré lemma, in some analytic neighborhood
U
of
a
in
A^{\prime}
, there exists a holomorphic function
h\in\mathcal{O}_{A^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{an}}(U)
such that
dh=\omega^{\prime}
on
U
and
h(a)=0
.
By (
),
\beta^{*}dh
is not identically zero.
Consequently,
h\circ\phi\in\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{an}}_{S}(\phi^{-1}(U))
is a non-constant holomorphic function such that
h\circ\phi(s)=0
.

Let
\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{an}}_{S,s}
be the stalk at
s
in the sense of analytic spaces, and let
\mathfrak{m}_{s}\subset\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{an}}_{S,s}
denote the maximal ideal.
Since
S
is normal,
\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{an}}_{S,s}
is an integral domain.
We have verified that
h\circ\phi\in\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{an}}_{S,s}
is nonzero.
Moreover, since
h\circ\phi(s)=0
, we have
h\circ\phi\in\mathfrak{m}_{s}
.
Hence we have

\dim\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{an}}_{S,s}/(h\circ\phi)=\dim S-1.

(11.2)

For every
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, consider the zero-dimensional subscheme on
S
defined by
V_{n}:=\textrm{Spec}\,\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{an}}_{S,s}/((h\circ\phi)+\mathfrak{m}_{s}^{n})
.
Then
V_{n}
is supported at
s
.

We take a projective compactification
\overline{S}
for
S
.
We fix an ample line bundle
M
on
\overline{S}
.
Consider the following short exact sequence

0\to M^{k}\otimes\mathcal{I}_{V_{n}}\to M^{k}\to M^{k}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{V_{n}}\to 0,

which yields a short exact sequence

0\to H^{0}(\overline{S},M^{k}\otimes\mathcal{I}_{V_{n}})\to H^{0}(\overline{S},M^{k})\to H^{0}(\overline{S},M^{k}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{V_{n}})

By (
), we have

h^{0}(\overline{S},M^{k}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{V_{n}})=h^{0}(V_{n},\mathcal{O}_{V_{n}})=O(n^{\dim S-1})

as

.
By Riemann-Roch theorem, there are positive constants

and
\kappa>0
such that

for

.
If we take

when

, it follows that

h^{0}(\overline{S},M^{k_{n}})>h^{0}(V_{n},\mathcal{O}_{V_{n}})

when

. Hence there are nonzero sections

when
n\gg 1
.
Let
D_{n}\subset\overline{S}
be the corresponding divisor.
Then we have
V_{n}\subset D_{n}
as closed subschemes on
\overline{S}
.

We now consider
\psi\circ g:Y\to S
which is a holomorphic map with Zariski dense image.
Hence
\psi\circ g(Y)\not\subset D_{n}
.
By
3.1

For every

, it follows that
\psi\circ g(y)=s
.
Since
g^{*}\omega=0
, it follows that

\displaystyle(\beta\circ g)^{*}\omega^{\prime}=g^{*}\omega=0.

(11.3)

Hence
(h\circ\phi)\circ(\psi\circ g)
is constant on the connected component of

which is thus zero.
Therefor
\psi\circ g:U\to S
factors the zero locus
(h\circ\phi=0)
.
This implies that

, hence

In conclusion,

\overline{N}_{g}(r,u(Z))\leq\frac{k_{n}}{n}T_{\psi\circ g}(r,M)+O(\log r).

On the other hand,

.
By

\overline{N}_{g}(r,u(Z))\leq\varepsilon T_{g}(r,L)+O_{\varepsilon}(\log r)

.

Suppose that
\varliminf_{r\to\infty}T_{g}(r,L)/\log r<+\infty
.
Then by

, we have

.
Then by (
), we have
\overline{N}_{g}(r,u(Z))=O(\log r)
, in particular

\overline{N}_{g}(r,u(Z))=o(T_{g}(r,L))+O(\log r).

(11.5)

Next we assume
\varliminf_{r\to\infty}T_{g}(r,L)/\log r=+\infty
.
Then
\log r=o(T_{g}(r,L))
. Then by (
), we have
\overline{N}_{g}(r,u(Z))\leq\varepsilon T_{g}(r,L)+o(T_{g}(r,L))
for all

. Hence we get
\overline{N}_{g}(r,u(Z))=o(T_{g}(r,L))
, in particular we get (
). The proof is completed. ∎

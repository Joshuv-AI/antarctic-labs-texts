Corn-flour Nacho benchmarking dataset: Taste dimensions, sensory evaluation and profiling for comparative analysis against cricket-flour alternatives
Authors: Attila Pohlmann, Franklin Velasco-Vizcaíno, Paúl Cajina Torres
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Benchmark taste profile data is provided for traditional corn-flour-based nachos collected from Ecuadorian undergraduates, enabling direct comparison with insect-based alternatives [https://doi.org/10.17605/OSF.IO/SDAW8]. Data were gathered on the taste dimensions sweet, sour, salty, bitter, umami and sensory properties smell, sound, touch, taste. A Check-All-That-Apply adjective list was used for descriptive profiling of the product sample. Concurrently collected demographic data, dietary exclusions, and psychological food consumption orientation scores provide additional benchmarking potential against visually and nutritionally comparable insect-based products. By offering sensory profiles for traditional corn-flour snacks, this dataset serves as a resource for comparative research, product development, and sensory analysis to enhance understanding of consumer sensory psychology related to alternative proteins.

Value of the Data

This dataset offers standardized sensory descriptions for corn-flour nachos, derived from a study involving 112 participants. Its core value lies in providing comparable quantitative taste profiles (sweet, sour, salty, bitter, umami), sensory evaluation (smell, sound, touch, and taste), and qualitative CATA adjectives (e.g., crunchy, brittle, greasy) [1,2] alongside demographic, dietary, and food consumption orientation data (health, convenience, pleasure, naturalness, sociability, price, and social image) [3]. These individual difference variables, collected simultaneously with the sensory data, can be used to control for psychological effects when comparing cricket-flour and corn-flour nacho consumption. The comparison dataset with insect-based nachos is available at https://osf.io/4y23h. Background

A meta-analysis examining insect-based foods revealed that few cultural or psychological factors drive consumer acceptance [4], despite the potential environmental benefits compared to traditional livestock sources. While these alternative protein sources possess a demonstrably smaller ecological footprint than conventional meat products, widespread adoption remains challenging not only due to hurdles such as food neophobia and resistance stemming from uncertainty avoidance, but also because satisfactory sensory characteristics must be established for any progress in reducing reliance on resource-intensive livestock [[4], [5], [6], [7], [8]]. The dataset presented here offers benchmark variables collected from an attitudinal study using corn-flour samples (as a comparator to previous insect-flour research; https://osf.io/4y23h), providing specific data points on sensory perception for comparative analyses. There is a noteworthy observed gender effect in the perception of bitterness: male participants perceived the corn-flour nachos to be significantly more bitter compared to female participants. These insights can be used to inform marketing strategies of functional foods as socio-demographic traits significantly influence bitter acceptance levels among consumers [[9], [10], [11]].

Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

One hundred and twelve undergraduate students (56 female, 55 male, 1 agender, Mage = 20.60, SD = 0.50) participated in a study sampling corn-flour nachos from an Ecuadorian snack company. Data were gathered across six sessions between February and March 2025. Participants accessed an online questionnaire via a QR code linked to Qualtrics. Following informed consenent, they reviewed the ingredients and nutritional label (Fig. 1), and were informed of their right to withdraw participation at any time. Participants with known or suspected allergies were asked not to sample the product. Subsequently, each participant received 50 g of corn-flour nachos and completed the questionnaire while sampling the product. Fig. 2 provides a visual of the product's shape and texture.

Taste Dimensions: The participants evaluated each product on five dimensions: sweet, sour, salty, bitter, and umami. In a matrix table, each dimension corresponded to a slider ranging from 0 (labeled “Extremely weak”) to 100 (labeled “Extremely strong”), centered initially for all sliders.

Sensory Evaluation: Participants employed all senses in their evaluation of the product. A matrix table presented dimensions related to smell, sound, touch, and taste; each dimension was assessed using a slider scale ranging from 0 (labeled “Extremely negative”) to 100 (labeled “Extremely positive”), with sliders starting at an initial neutral position for all senses.

Sensory Profiling: Participants viewed a list of pre-defined food-related attributes sourced from [1]. They could place a check-mark next to each word if they considered it applicable to the corn-flour nacho sample. The attributes included terms like crunchy, tasty, dry, salty, hard, off-flavor, etc. The frequency with which each term was selected is detailed in Fig. 3.Fig. 3Check-all-that-apply (CATA) Word Frequencies. Food Consumption Orientation: Food consumption orientation can influence consumers' attitudes towards sustainable food systems [3]. To address potential biases in perception stemming from attitudes, this control variable was included. The instrument is broken down into seven sub-scales: health, convenience, pleasure, naturalness, sociability, price, and social image. Each sub-scale score is calculated by averaging its associated subordinate items. The variables within each sub-scale are enumerated in Table 1. Responses were recorded on 5-point Likert scales anchored with 1 = Strongly disagree to 5 = Strongly agree."

Dietary Exclusions: To account for participants' dietary differences, questions were included asking which specific food categories they consistently avoid consuming (e.g., red meat, dairy products). The selection of multiple exclusions was allowed.

Limitations

Being limited to undergraduates from a single university in Quito constrains the dataset's generalizability based on demographic and cultural factors. However, their uniformity likely reduced physiological variability linked to age-related sensory differences [9]. Despite the potential limitation, increasing sample size across different ages and cultures would be crucial for broader generalizability.

Ethics Statement

This study received ethics approval from the Universidad San Francisco de Quito's Comité de Ética de Investigación en Seres Humanos (CEISH-USFQ), following Declaration of Helsinki principles. The protocol, number 019–2024-CA23149IN, was approved on January 31, 2024. To protect participant safety, comprehensive ingredient and nutritional information was provided beforehand, along with instructions to refrain from participation if any allergies were known or suspected.

Boundedness and decay of waves on spatially flat decelerated FLRW spacetimes
Mahdi Haghshenas

Abstract
We study the linear wave equation on a class of spatially homogeneous and isotropic Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker (FLRW) spacetimes in the decelerated regime with spatial topology ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} . Employing twisted t t -weighted multiplier vector fields, we establish uniform energy bounds and derive integrated local energy decay estimates across the entire range of the decelerated expansion regime. Furthermore, we obtain a hierarchy of r p r^{p} -weighted energy estimates à la the Dafermos–Rodnianski r p r^{p} -method, which leads to energy decay estimates. As a consequence, we demonstrate pointwise decay estimates for solutions and their derivatives. In the wave zone, this pointwise decay is optimal in the “radiation” and “sub-radiation” cases, and almost optimal around the radiation case.



1
Introduction

The standard cosmological models describing a homogeneous and isotropic universe in general relativity are represented by the Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker (FLRW) spacetimes
[
34
]
. The current article concerns FLRW spacetimes with
\mathbb{R}^{3}
spatial topology, for which the Lorentzian metrics, defined on the 4-dimensional manifold
M=(0,\infty)_{t}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
, read

g_{q}=-dt^{2}+t^{2q}\left((dx^{1})^{2}+(dx^{2})^{2}+(dx^{3})^{2}\right)\,,

(1.1)

for
0<q<1
corresponding to the
decelerated regime
. We refer to
a(t)=t^{q}
as the scale factor, which encodes the expansion of the universe and the big bang-type singularity as
t\to 0
. This paper addresses the dynamics of the scalar wave equation on the FLRW background metric towards the future.
The covariant wave equation associated to
g_{q}
in
(t,x^{1},x^{2},x^{3})
coordinates is given by

\Box_{g_{q}}\psi:=-\partial_{tt}\psi-\frac{3q}{t}\partial_{t}\psi+\frac{1}{t^{2q}}\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\psi=0\,.

(1.2)

The spacetimes (
1.1
) belong to the more general class of FLRW spacetimes
(M,g)
with

\displaystyle M=(0,\infty)_{t}\times S\,,\qquad g:=-dt^{2}+a^{2}(t)g_{S}\,,

(1.3)

where
(S,g_{S})
is a three-dimensional Riemannian manifold of constant curvature
K
. The above metrics can be derived from the
cosmological principle
, that is the homogeneity and isotropy of the universe on large cosmological scales
[
33
,
35
]
.

In the spatially flat case, when
K=0
,
S
can be taken to be
\mathbb{R}^{3}
or
\mathbb{T}^{3}
. This article focuses primarily on the
\mathbb{R}^{3}
topology where the dispersive features of waves are apparent. The FLRW spacetimes (
1.1
) are spatially homogeneous and spherically symmetric with
area radius

R=t^{q}r
(where
r=\sqrt{(x^{1})^{2}+(x^{2})^{2}+(x^{3})^{2}}
), and one formally recovers the Minkowski metric by letting
q=0
in (
1.1
). The FLRW metrics, in contrast to Minkowski space, are neither asymptotically flat nor stationary. Notably, the timelike vector field
\partial_{t}
is not a Killing vector field for
q\neq 0
, and as a result, the energy boundedness of waves is not inherently ensured. However, the conformal structure and asymptotic features of the decelerated FLRW spacetimes can be compared to those of Minkowski spacetime. For instance, the metric (
1.1
) is conformally flat and a null conformal boundary
\mathcal{I}^{+}
called
future null infinity
, which represents far away observers, can be attached to the FLRW spacetime; see Figure
1
or refer to
[
21
]
*Chapter 5.3.

Many results in the current work also hold in Minkowski space, formally corresponding to
q=0
, and are well known in this case. For simplicity of notation, however, the case
q=0
is omitted from the statements in the following sections.

The metric
g_{q}
(
1.1
) for all
\frac{1}{3}\leq q\leq\frac{2}{3}
arises as a solution of the Einstein–Euler equation for a perfect fluid with a linear equation of state and with cosmological constant
\Lambda=0
. In particular, the cases
q=\frac{1}{2}
and
q=\frac{2}{3}
correspond to the
radiation
and the
dust
model, respectively. Furthermore,
g_{q}
also solves the Einstein–massless Vlasov equation when
q=\frac{1}{2}
. In fact, all solutions of Einstein–massless Vlasov with an isotropic and “irrotational” particle density are either stationary or described by the FLRW metric
[
13
]
. We remark that the scalar curvature of (
1.1
) vanishes when
q=\frac{1}{2}
.

The study of equation (
1.2
) is relevant for the analysis of wave-type systems near FLRW spacetimes. In particular, (
1.2
) can be viewed as a “poor man’s” linearisation of Einstein equations around (
1.1
).

1.1
Initial value problem

In the present article, we consider an initial data hypersurface
\Sigma_{\tau_{0}}
that is partially null and connects the centre
\{r=0\}
to future null infinity
\mathcal{I}^{+}
. The spacelike part and the null part, respectively denoted by
\mathcal{S}_{\tau_{0}}
and
\mathcal{C}_{\tau_{0}}
, are separated by
\{r=\rho\}
for an arbitrary
\rho>0
, as depicted in Figure
1
.

Moreover, the function
\tau
and the hypersurface
\Sigma_{\tau}
, defined in Section
2.2
, are such that
\Sigma_{\tau^{\prime}}
is the hypersurface of all points
p
with
\tau(p)=\tau^{\prime}
. We also set
\mathcal{R}^{\tau^{\prime}}_{\tau}=\cup_{\bar{\tau}\in[\tau,\tau^{\prime}]}\Sigma_{\bar{\tau}}
; see Figure
1
.

) for
0<q<1
, with the spacelike hypersurface
\{t=1\}
, the hypersurface
\Sigma_{\tau}
, and the spacelike singularity at

.

The pair

is called a
compactly supported smooth initial data set
on
\Sigma_{\tau_{0}}
if
\psi_{0}
is a smooth function on both
\mathcal{S}_{\tau_{0}}
and
\mathcal{C}_{\tau_{0}}
with compact support,
\psi_{1}
is a compactly supported smooth function on
\mathcal{S}_{\tau_{0}}
, and moreover, there exists a smooth function

on

such that

\displaystyle\Psi|_{\Sigma_{\tau_{0}}}=\psi_{0}\,,\qquad\partial_{t}\Psi|_{\mathcal{S}_{\tau_{0}}}=\psi_{1}\,.

It is worth mentioning that compactly supported smooth Cauchy data on
\{t=\text{constant}\}
, for example, induces such smooth initial data on

by considering
\rho
large enough. Note that the assumption of compact support is introduced to simplify the arguments in Section
4
but it may be relaxed by instead requiring that an appropriate weighted energy flux of
\psi_{0}
and
\psi_{1}
along
\Sigma_{\tau_{0}}
is bounded.

In this paper, a solution
\psi
to the wave equation always means a smooth function satisfying the initial value problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Box_{g_{q}}\psi=0\,,\\
\psi|_{\Sigma_{\tau_{0}}}=\psi_{0}\,,\\
\partial_{t}\psi|_{\mathcal{S}_{\tau_{0}}}=\psi_{1}\,,\end{cases}

(1.4)

for a compactly supported smooth initial data set
(\psi_{0},\psi_{1})
on
\Sigma_{\tau_{0}}
.

1.2
Main results

The main results of the present paper concern boundedness and decay properties of solutions of (
1.4
), following the Dafermos–Rodnianski
r^{p}
-method
[
11
]
. The main ingredients of this method are

1.

Energy boundedness;

2.

Integrated local energy decay (ILED);

3.

A hierarchy of
r^{p}
-weighted estimates.

The above results for the FLRW spacetimes (
1.1
) are presented in Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
, and
1.3
, respectively. Consequently, the pointwise and energy decay of
\psi
, as stated in Corollary
1.4
, follows from these three theorems.

The Dafermos–Rodnianski
r^{p}
-method has been generalised to a broad class of asymptotically flat spacetimes by Moschidis
[
28
]
. Further refinements of the
r^{p}
-method can be found in
[
5
,
4
,
39
]
. See also the recent paper
[
12
]
.

The
r^{p}
-method is particularly well suited to non-linear problems. See, for example, the work of Dafermos–Holzegel–Rodnianski–Taylor
[
10
]
on non-linear wave equations around various stationary asymptotically flat spacetimes. In the absence of
trapped null geodesics
, the above three ingredients for linear waves lead to a proof of small data global solutions (see case (i) in
[
10
]
*Section 3.4.1, where the
r^{p}
-estimate is required only for some fixed
p>0
). In the presence of trapped null geodesics, the method of
[
10
]
requires an additional “physical space top order identity”. Note also the work of Keir
[
23
]
on non-linear wave equations around Minkowski space satisfying
weak null condition
.

The present setting considered with a non-linearity satisfying a suitable
null condition
, is comparable to the former case of
[
10
]
.

The energy flux considered in this paper, denoted by
\mathcal{E}_{q}^{\varepsilon}[\psi](\tau)
, is a “twisted” weighted energy flux of
\psi
through
\Sigma_{\tau}
, where
q
encodes a
t
-weight corresponding to the scale factor
t^{q}
in (
1.1
). The energy flux
\mathcal{E}_{q}^{\varepsilon}[\psi](\tau)
includes an arbitrarily small
\varepsilon\geq 0
loss in the
t
-weight factors. We write
\mathcal{E}_{q}[\psi]:=\mathcal{E}_{q}^{0}[\psi]
in the case of no loss. The energy
\mathcal{E}_{q}^{\varepsilon}[\psi](\tau)
is referred to as a twisted energy in view of the fact that the
\partial_{t}
derivative of
\psi
appears only after
\psi
is multiplied by
t^{q}
. See already (
1.12
) for the precise definition of
\mathcal{E}_{q}^{\varepsilon}[\psi](\tau)
.

In the radiation case, corresponding to
q=\frac{1}{2}
, the wave equation can be transformed to the standard wave equation on the Minkowski space, and many results can be directly invoked from Minkowski space. A representation formula, for instance, is available in the radiation case. The approach taken in this paper does not rely on this transformation to Minkowski space, however.

Nevertheless, the case
q=\frac{1}{2}
is somewhat the simplest. As such, the following results, particularly Theorem
1.3
, are sharpest for
q
close to
\frac{1}{2}
and
0
. In fact,
q=\frac{1}{2}
can be viewed as a threshold, with the statements and proofs differing for
q>\frac{1}{2}
and
q\leq\frac{1}{2}
.

The following theorem establishes the boundedness of the twisted energy flux
\mathcal{E}_{q}^{\varepsilon}[\psi](\tau)
associated to the solution
\psi
. It is important to emphasise that
\mathcal{E}_{q}^{\varepsilon}[\psi](\tau)
already includes
t
-dependent weight factors, with
q=\frac{1}{2}
exhibiting the strongest
t
-weight, see Remark
1.6
.

Theorem 1.1

(Energy boundedness of waves on FLRW)
.

Suppose

\psi

1.4

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{E}_{q}^{\varepsilon}[\psi](\tau):=&\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\tau}}t^{-\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}t^{2q}\left(|\partial_{t}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}+|\partial_{r}\psi|^{2}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}|\partial_{r}(r\psi)|^{2}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}|\mathring{\not{\nabla}}\psi|^{2}+t^{2q-2}\psi^{2}+\frac{1}{1+r^{2}}\psi^{2}\right)\,dx\\
&+\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\tau}}t^{-\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}\left(\frac{1}{r^{2}}|\partial_{v}(rt^{q}\psi)|^{2}+|\partial_{v}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}+\frac{t^{2q}}{r^{2}}|\mathring{\not{\nabla}}\psi|^{2}+t^{4q-2}\psi^{2}+\frac{t^{2q}}{r^{2}}\psi^{2}\right)\,r^{2}dvd\omega\,,\end{split}

(1.12)

with
\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}
as in (
1.6
) for any
\varepsilon\geq 0
. For definition of null and angular derivatives refer to Section
2.2
.

Remark 1.7

(Non-sharp decay from energy boundedness)
.

Note that a non-sharp pointwise decay can be induced just from the energy boundedness, Theorem
1.1
. For the sake of simplicity, we consider a simplified formulation of energy flux defined on the spacelike hypersurfaces
\{t=\text{constant}\}
rather than on
\Sigma_{\tau}
. Then, for
0<q<1
, any
t_{2}>t_{1}
, and for any solution
\psi
originating from compactly supported initial data on
\{t=\text{constant}\}
, the following energy boundedness holds
3
3

3

with
|\nabla\psi|^{2}=\sum_{i=1,2,3}|\partial_{x^{i}}\psi|^{2}
.

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\{t=t_{2}\}}&t^{2q-\mu_{q}}\left(|\partial_{t}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}+|\nabla\psi|^{2}+t^{2q-2}|2q-1|\psi^{2}\right)\,dx\\
&\lesssim\int_{\{t=t_{1}\}}t^{2q-\mu_{q}}\left(|\partial_{t}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}+|\nabla\psi|^{2}+t^{2q-2}|2q-1|\psi^{2}\right)\,dx\,.\end{split}

(1.13)

The estimate above obviously implies the decay of an unweighted energy flux. Furthermore, commuting with
\partial_{x^{i}}
for
i=1,2,3
and using Sobolev inequalities, Proposition
2.6
, the above estimate immediately yields the following pointwise decay

\displaystyle|\psi(t,x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{q}[\psi_{0},\psi_{1}]}{t^{q}}\quad

\displaystyle\text{ if }\quad 0<q\leq\frac{1}{2}\,,

\displaystyle|\psi(t,x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{q}[\psi_{0},\psi_{1}]}{t^{1-q}}\quad

\displaystyle\text{ if }\quad\frac{1}{2}<q<1\,,

with
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{q}[\psi_{0},\psi_{1}]
denoting a suitable weighted higher order initial energy on
\{t=t_{0}\}
. Observe that the decay rate described above is weaker than those established in Corollary
1.4
. Since the standard energy fluxes along the spacelike hypersurfaces
\{t=\text{constant}\}
in Minkowski spacetime are conserved,
the energy decay (
1.13
) in FLRW spacetimes can be interpreted as a consequence of
cosmological expansion
. On the other hand, the stronger decay presented in Corollary
1.4
additionally incorporates the
dispersive
properties of waves. See also Remark
1.8
.

Moreover, by refining the use of Sobolev inequalities and commutators, the pointwise decay estimates above can be improved while still relying only on the energy boundedness result, namely Theorem
1.1
. Indeed we have,

\displaystyle|\psi(t,x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{q}[\psi_{0},\psi_{1}]}{t^{q}\sqrt{1+r}}\quad

\displaystyle\text{ if }\quad 0<q\leq\frac{1}{2}\,,

\displaystyle|\psi(t,x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{q}[\psi_{0},\psi_{1}]}{t^{1-q}\sqrt{1+r}}\quad

\displaystyle\text{ if }\quad\frac{1}{2}<q<1\,.

See Remark
4.5
.

Remark 1.8

(Waves on FLRW with
\mathbb{T}^{3}
spatial topology)
.

Estimate (
1.13
) remains valid even when the spatial topology is changed from
\mathbb{R}^{3}
to
compact

\mathbb{T}^{3}
topology, where dispersive effects are absent. Again commuting with
\partial_{x^{i}}
and using the Sobolev inequality (
2.15
) yield

\displaystyle\Big|\psi(t,x)-\frac{1}{|\mathbb{T}^{3}|}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\psi\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{q}[\psi_{0},\psi_{1}]}{t^{q}}\quad

\displaystyle\text{ if }\quad 0<q\leq\frac{1}{2}\,,

\displaystyle\Big|\psi(t,x)-\frac{1}{|\mathbb{T}^{3}|}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\psi\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{q}[\psi_{0},\psi_{1}]}{t^{1-q}}\quad

\displaystyle\text{ if }\quad\frac{1}{2}<q<1\,.

We remark that subtracting the average is essential, as the constant functions satisfy the wave equation.

1.3
Related works

There have been a number of previous works on wave-type equations on FLRW spacetimes (
1.1
), some of which we briefly mention below.

1.3.1
Explicit representation formulae via transformation

The mathematical study of the wave equation on FLRW spacetimes can be traced back to Klainerman–Sarnak’s work
[
24
]
on the dust model,
i.e.

q=\frac{2}{3}
, with spatial curvature
K\in\{-1,0,1\}
, where they provided explicit representation formula for the wave equation. In the spatially flat case, they considered the transformation
\Psi=t^{\frac{1}{3}}\partial_{t}(t\psi)
and reduced the wave equation on FLRW to the standard wave equation on Minkowski, where a representation formula in terms of spherical means is given. Building on this representation formula for the FLRW dust model,
[
1
]
later showed that waves do not necessarily satisfy the strong Huygens principle, and moreover, in the flat case, they decay at the rate
t^{-1}
. See also
[
18
,
19
]
.

The transformation method in
[
24
]
has been applied to more general FLRW spacetimes in
[
30
]
, where Natário–Rossetti found explicit representation formula for solutions of wave equation in a number of cosmological spacetimes including the radiation-filled FLRW universe,
i.e.

q=\frac{1}{2}
. In particular, they proved the
t^{-1}
decay rate for waves in the spatially flat radiation case by transforming the equation to the wave equation on Minkowski spacetime where representation formulas are available. Furthermore, Natário–Rossetti, using Wirth’s work
[
45
]
on “weakly dissipative waves”, showed that solutions to the wave equation
\Box_{g_{q}}\psi=0
on the spatially flat FLRW spacetimes arising from initial data belonging to appropriate Sobolev spaces decay as

\displaystyle|\psi(t,\cdot)|\lesssim\begin{cases}t^{-1}\qquad&0\leq q\leq\frac{2}{3}\,,\\
t^{-3(1-q)}\qquad&\frac{2}{3}<q<1\,,\\
(\log t)^{-\frac{2}{3}}\qquad&q=1\,.\end{cases}

(1.14)

They also formulated a similar conjecture in the spatially hyperbolic case. The decay rate (
1.14
) across the entire decelerated regime was proved in
[
30
]
by transforming the wave equation on FLRW spacetimes into a wave equation with
weak dissipation
on Minkowski space, for which the results were already known from
[
45
]
. The details of this approach are presented in the following lines.

1.3.2
Conformal weakly dissipative wave equation

Observe that by defining the
conformal time
coordinate

t^{*}=\int\frac{dt}{a(t)}=\frac{t^{1-q}}{1-q},

the metric (
1.1
) becomes

\displaystyle g_{q}=a^{2}(t^{*})\left(-(dt^{*})^{2}+(dx^{1})^{2}+(dx^{2})^{2}+(dx^{3})^{2}\right)\,,

which clearly shows that FLRW spacetimes are
conformally flat
. In
(t^{*},x^{1},x^{2},x^{3})
coordinates,
the wave operator turns to

\displaystyle\Box_{g_{q}}\Psi=\frac{1}{t^{2q}}\left(-\partial_{t^{*}}\partial_{t^{*}}\Psi-\frac{2q}{(1-q)t^{*}}\partial_{t^{*}}\Psi+\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Psi\right)\,.

(1.15)

One therefore can study the “weakly dissipative” wave equation

-\partial_{t^{*}}\partial_{t^{*}}\Psi-\frac{\alpha}{t^{*}}\partial_{t^{*}}\Psi+\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Psi=0\,,

(1.16)

on Minkowski spacetime for
t^{*}\geq 1
and with
\alpha=\frac{2q}{1-q}
, instead of studying
\Box_{g_{q}}\psi=0
. The study of equation (
1.16
) goes back to
[
27
]
and
[
44
]
. In fact, Uesaka
[
44
]
proved that if
\Psi
solves equation (
1.16
) with compactly supported initial data on
\{t^{*}={\text{constant}}\}
, then the standard energy decays as

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(|\partial_{t^{*}}\Psi|^{2}+|\nabla\Psi|^{2}\right)\,dx\lesssim\frac{\mathfrak{C}}{{t^{*}}^{\min\{2,\alpha\}}}\,,

which corresponds to
t^{-\min\{2q,2(1-q)\}}
decay rate for energy of solution
\psi
of the FLRW wave equation.
It is worth mentioning that Uesaka’s result and method are comparable and compatible with Theorem
1.1
and its proof. See Remark
1.7
.

Wirth
[
45
]
showed that the Fourier image
\hat{\Psi}(t^{*},\xi)
of the solution
\Psi
solves an ODE that can be transformed to Bessel’s differential equation, and therefore, an explicit representation of solutions to (
1.16
) in terms of Bessel functions was derived. In
[
45
]
, the special structure of the representation and the properties of the Bessel functions were carefully exploited to obtain
L^{\bar{p}}
–
L^{\bar{q}}
estimates for the solution. The decay rate (
1.14
) was proved in
[
30
]
by letting
\bar{p}=1
and
\bar{q}=\infty
in this
L^{\bar{p}}
–
L^{\bar{q}}
estimate.

For equation (
1.16
) with
|\Psi|^{\beta}
-non-linearity, refer to
[
22
]
*and references therein.

1.3.3
Numerical works

The numerical work of Rossetti–Vañó-Viñuales
[
37

considers the spherically symmetric solutions of the FLRW wave equation with Gaussian pulses as initial data. For the spatially flat case, their numerical data is in agreement with the decay rate presented in (

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}\psi(t,\cdot)|\sim\begin{cases}t^{-1-q}\qquad&0\leq q\leq\frac{3}{4}\,,\\

t^{-1}(\log t)^{-\frac{5}{2}}\qquad&q=1\,,\\

In addition,
[

\displaystyle\sup_{\{r<\delta t^{*}\}}|\psi|\sim\frac{1}{t^{3-3q}}\,,\qquad\sup_{\{r<\delta t^{*}\}}|\partial_{t}\psi|\sim\frac{1}{t^{4-3q}}\,,

with
\delta\in(0,1)

Also see the recent paper
[

1.4
Overview of proofs of main results

In this section, we provide an overview of the proof and the fundamental ideas underlying the main results in the current paper, notably Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
and
1.3
. Moreover, we compare the radiation case to the well known results on Minkowski space. Then, we demonstrate the differences between the cases
q<\frac{1}{2}
and
q>\frac{1}{2}
.

For the sake of simplicity, we here consider simplified energy fluxes on hypersurfaces of the form
\{t=\text{constant}\}
, similar to those in Remark
1.7
. Accordingly, set
\mathcal{R}=[t_{1},t_{2}]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

1.4.1
Waves on Minkowski space

To begin with, we recall some established results regarding the boundedness and decay of waves on Minkowski space, formally corresponding to
q=0
in (
1.1
). Suppose
\psi
is a solution to the standard wave equation on Minkowski space originated from compactly supported initial data on
\{t=t_{0}\}
. The notation we use here for the coordinate functions and regions in Minkowski space is the same as that used in FLRW spacetimes, formally setting
q=0
.

We first recall the
energy-momentum tensor

\mathbb{T}_{\mu\nu}[\psi]
associated to
\psi
, defined as

\mathbb{T}_{\mu\nu}[\psi]=\nabla_{\mu}\psi\nabla_{\nu}\psi-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}\nabla^{\gamma}\psi\nabla_{\gamma}\psi\,.

If one lets

J^{X}_{\mu}[\psi]=\mathbb{T}_{\mu\nu}[\psi]X^{\nu}
for any smooth vector field
X
, then one is left with the
divergence identity

\nabla^{\mu}J^{X}_{\mu}[\psi]=\nabla_{X}\psi\Box_{g}\psi+{}^{(X)}\pi^{\mu\nu}\mathbb{T}_{\mu\nu}[\psi]\,,

(1.17)

with
{}^{(X)}\pi_{\mu\nu}
denoting the deformation tensor of
X
, defined in Section
2.1
. The former term on the right-hand side obviously vanishes for solutions to the wave equation. After suitable choices of vector field
X
, divergence identity (
1.17
) leads to energy estimates for solutions
\psi
to the wave equation after integration over suitable spacetime regions.

By letting
X
to be the Killing timelike vector field
T=\partial_{t}
, (
1.17
) leads to the following conservation law after integrating over the region
\mathcal{R}
,

\int_{\{t=t_{2}\}}\left(|\partial_{t}\psi|^{2}+|\nabla\psi|^{2}\right)\,dx=\int_{\{t=t_{1}\}}\left(|\partial_{t}\psi|^{2}+|\nabla\psi|^{2}\right)\,dx\,.

(1.18)

The above identity should be compared to (
1.13
) and Theorem
1.1
.
Similarly, a modified version of the divergence identity (
1.17
) can also be used to derive the ILED estimate for
\psi
. Indeed, vector field multipliers
X_{1}=\partial_{r}
and
X_{2}=\frac{1}{(1+r)^{\delta}}\partial_{r}
for an arbitrarily small
\delta>0
along with suitable
modification functions
, give rise to

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{R}}\left(\frac{1}{r^{3}}|\mathring{\not{\nabla}}\psi|^{2}+\frac{1}{1+r^{1+\delta}}\left(|\partial_{t}\psi|^{2}+|\partial_{r}\psi|^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{1+r^{3+\delta}}\psi^{2}\right)\;dxdt

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\{t=t_{1}\}}\left(|\partial_{t}\psi|^{2}+|\nabla\psi|^{2}\right)\,dx\,.

(1.19)

Note that the energy boundedness (
1.18
) and the ILED estimate (
1.19
) are consequences of the (modified) divergence identity (
1.17
), which is a pointwise identity. Therefore, the choice of the integration domain is flexible, provided that its boundary components are either
null
or
spacelike
. In particular, estimates (
1.18
) and (
1.19
) can likewise be formulated for partially null hypersurfaces
\Sigma_{\tau}
, the bulk region
\mathcal{R}^{\tau_{2}}_{\tau_{1}}
, and the energy flux
\mathcal{E}[\psi](\tau)
associated to energy current
J^{T}_{\mu}[\psi]
.

To derive the Dafermos-Rodnianski
r^{p}
-estimates on Minkowski
[
11
]
, one can use the divergence identity (
1.17
) for the energy current
J^{X}_{\mu}[r\psi]
with
X=r^{p-1}\partial_{v}
. After integration over the region
\mathcal{D}^{\tau_{2}}_{\tau_{1}}
, as defined in Theorem
1.3
, this choice of vector field multiplier along with suitable modification functions yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\mathcal{D}^{\tau_{2}}_{\tau_{1}}}r^{p-1}\left(p|\partial_{v}(r\psi)|^{2}+\frac{p-2}{r^{2}}|\mathring{\not{\nabla}}(r\psi)|^{2}\right)\,dudvd\omega+\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\tau_{2}}}r^{p}|\partial_{v}(r\psi)|^{2}\,dvd\omega\\
\lesssim\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\tau_{1}}}r^{p}|\partial_{v}(r\psi)|^{2}\,dvd\omega+\mathcal{E}[\psi](\tau_{1})\,,\end{split}

(1.20)

for any
0\leq p\leq 2
, in which
\mathcal{E}[\psi](\tau_{1})
is the energy flux associated to vector field
T
on
\Sigma_{\tau_{1}}
. Note that in the estimate above, other terms arising from boundary components of
\mathcal{D}^{\tau_{2}}_{\tau_{1}}
have been controlled by (
1.18
) and (
1.19
).

Once estimates (
1.18
), (
1.19
) and (
1.20
) are achieved, a general hierarchical lemma, see Lemma
2.4
, gives

\mathcal{E}[\psi](\tau)\lesssim\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}\mathcal{E}[\psi_{0},\psi_{1}]\,,

for any
\tau>\tau_{0}
with
\bar{\mathcal{E}}[\psi_{0},\psi_{1}]
being an appropriate weighted initial energy of
\psi
. The pointwise decay of
\psi
then follows from standard Sobolev inequalities (see Proposition
2.6
later) and commuting with angular derivatives
\Omega_{i}
for
i=1,2,3
. In fact, we have

|\psi|\lesssim\frac{\bar{\mathcal{E}}[\psi_{0},\psi_{1}]}{(1+r)\sqrt{\tau}},\qquad|\psi|\lesssim\frac{\bar{\mathcal{E}}[\psi_{0},\psi_{1}]}{\sqrt{1+r}\tau}\,,

in which
\bar{\mathcal{E}}[\psi_{0},\psi_{1}]
is an appropriate weighted initial energy containing derivatives of
\psi
up to order three.

1.4.2
The radiation case
q=\frac{1}{2}

In FLRW spacetime, the energy flux under consideration is modified by introducing a twisted derivative, replacing
\partial_{t}\psi
with
\frac{1}{t^{q}}\partial_{t}(t^{q}\psi)
.
5
5

5

This adjustment is motivated by the underlying structure of the wave equation in the radiation case, where such a formulation naturally arises. Indeed, the wave equation (
1.2
) can be rewritten for
q=\frac{1}{2}
as

\Box_{\frac{1}{2}}\psi=-\frac{1}{t}\partial_{t}(\sqrt{t}\partial_{t}(\sqrt{t}\psi))+\frac{1}{t}\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\psi=0\,.

This is done by introducing the
twisted energy-momentum tensor

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbb{T}}^{\beta}_{\mu\nu}[\psi]:=\beta^{2}\left[{\nabla}_{\mu}(\beta^{-1}\psi){\nabla}_{\nu}(\beta^{-1}\psi)-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}\left({\nabla}^{\gamma}(\beta^{-1}\psi){\nabla}_{\gamma}(\beta^{-1}\psi)+\mathcal{V}_{\beta}\beta^{-2}\psi^{2}\right)\right]\,,

(1.21)

for a
twisting function

\beta
and with the
potential

\mathcal{V}_{\beta}=-\frac{\Box_{g}\beta}{\beta}
. The twisted version of the divergence identity (
1.17
) for vector field
X
and the
twisted energy current

\tilde{J}^{\beta,X}_{\mu}[\psi]=\tilde{\mathbb{T}}^{\beta}_{\mu\nu}[\psi]X^{\nu}
then reads

\nabla^{\mu}\tilde{J}^{\beta,X}_{\mu}[\psi]=\beta\nabla_{X}(\frac{\psi}{\beta})\Box_{g}\psi+{}^{(X)}\pi^{\mu\nu}\tilde{\mathbb{T}}^{\beta}_{\mu\nu}[\psi]-\frac{1}{2}X^{\nu}\left(\frac{\nabla_{\mu}(\beta^{2}\mathcal{V}_{\beta})}{\beta^{2}}\psi^{2}+\nabla_{\mu}(\beta^{2}){\nabla}^{\gamma}(\beta^{-1}\psi){\nabla}_{\gamma}(\beta^{-1}\psi)\right)\,,

(1.22)

which again leads to energy estimates for solutions
\psi
after suitable choices of vector field
X
and twisting function
\beta
and integration over suitable spacetime regions.

For the case
q=\frac{1}{2}
, in fact, if we let
\beta=\frac{1}{a(t)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}
and
X=\sqrt{t}\partial_{t}
, then the terms on the right-hand side of (
1.22
) sum to zero, yielding the following conservation law

\int_{\{t=t_{2}\}}t\left(|\partial_{t}(\sqrt{t}\psi)|^{2}+|\nabla\psi|^{2}\right)\,dx=\int_{\{t=t_{1}\}}t\left(|\partial_{t}(\sqrt{t}\psi)|^{2}+|\nabla\psi|^{2}\right)\,dx\,.

(1.23)

Note that the potential
\mathcal{V}_{\beta}
vanishes for
\beta=\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}
.

In order to establish the ILED estimate (
1.7
) for
q=\frac{1}{2}
, we again use (
1.22
) with
\beta=\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}
, vector field multipliers
X_{1}=\partial_{r}
and
X_{2}=\frac{1}{(1+r)^{\delta}}\partial_{r}
, and suitable modification functions.

The
r^{p}
-estimates can also be derived in a manner similar to the Minkowski case. Indeed, using (
1.22
) for the twisted energy current
\tilde{J}^{X}_{\mu}[r\psi]
with
X=\sqrt{t}r^{p-1}\partial_{v}
—where
\partial_{v}
is defined with respect to the FLRW metric, and thus differs from its Minkowski counterpart—together with suitable modification functions, yields (
1.10
) for
0\leq p\leq 2
.

The energy decay estimate and the pointwise decay then follow similarly from the abstract hierarchical lemma
2.4
and Sobolev inequalities.

1.4.3
The case
q\neq\frac{1}{2}

In the general case, there is no obvious choice of
\beta
and
X
that makes the right-hand side of (
1.22
) vanish for solutions of the wave equation—a property that makes the case
q=\frac{1}{2}
comparatively the simplest. If we let
\beta=\frac{1}{a(t)}=\frac{1}{t^{q}}
, the potential
\mathcal{V}_{\beta}=-\frac{q(2q-1)}{t^{2}}
is no longer vanishing. Furthermore,
V_{\beta}
changes sign as
q
crosses
\frac{1}{2}
. This change in the sign of the potential
\mathcal{V}_{\beta}
constitutes the primary distinction between the cases
q\leq\frac{1}{2}
and
q>\frac{1}{2}
. In particular, if one let
X
in (
1.22
) to be
T=t^{q}\partial_{t}
, then we have

\nabla^{\mu}\tilde{J}^{\beta,X}_{\mu}[\psi]=(1-q)t^{q-1}\mathcal{V}_{\beta}\psi^{2}\,,

for any wave solution
\psi
. After integration over
\mathcal{R}
, we arrive at

\displaystyle(1-q)\int_{\mathcal{R}}t^{q-1}\mathcal{V}_{\beta}\psi^{2}\,t^{3q}dtdx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\{t=t_{2}\}}\left(t^{2q}|\partial_{t}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}+t^{2q}|\nabla\psi|^{2}+t^{4q}\mathcal{V}_{\beta}\psi^{2}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\{t=t_{1}\}}\left(t^{2q}|\partial_{t}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}+t^{2q}|\nabla\psi|^{2}+t^{4q}\mathcal{V}_{\beta}\psi^{2}\right)\,dx\,.

The above identity clearly establishes a uniform energy bound for the case
q\leq\frac{1}{2}
, as
\mathcal{V}_{\beta}\geq 0
, and coincides with the conservation law (

) when

. However, it falls short to establish the energy boundedness estimate in the case

. So, we introduce an alternative twisting function for

. In fact, if we let
\beta^{\prime}=t^{q-1}
, then (

) for

along with suitable modifications leads to the following energy estimate

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{R}}t|\partial_{t}\psi|^{2}\,dtdx+\int_{\{t=t_{2}\}}\left(t^{2q}|\partial_{t}(t^{1-q}\psi)|^{2}+t^{2-2q}|\nabla\psi|^{2}+\psi^{2}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\{t=t_{1}\}}\left(t^{2q}|\partial_{t}(t^{1-q}\psi)|^{2}+t^{2-2q}|\nabla\psi|^{2}+\psi^{2}\right)\,dx\,.

Note that once the above estimate is established, it can be rewritten in terms of the twisting function
\beta=\frac{1}{t^{q}}
with different weight factors involving

, as in (

The derivation of the ILED estimate (

) for

, using vector field multipliers

and
X_{2}=\frac{1}{(1+r)^{\delta}}\partial_{r}
in the case

. When

, we use multipliers
X^{\prime}_{1}=t^{2-4q}\partial_{r}
and
X^{\prime}_{2}=\frac{t^{2-4q}}{(1+r)^{\delta}}\partial_{r}
, along with suitable modifications.

To derive the
r^{p}
-estimates, once again we use (
1.22
) for the twisted energy current
\tilde{J}^{X}_{\mu}[r\psi]
with
X=t^{-\mu_{q}}t^{q}r^{p-1}\partial_{v}
. After integrating over
\mathcal{D}^{\tau_{2}}_{\tau_{1}}
, introducing suitable modifications, and controlling the boundary terms, we arrive at

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{D}^{\tau_{2}}_{\tau_{1}}}t^{-{\mu_{q}}}r^{p-1}\left(p|\partial_{v}\varphi|^{2}+\frac{2-p}{r^{2}}|\mathring{\not{\nabla}}\varphi|^{2}+\frac{\gamma(2-p)}{r^{2}}\varphi^{2}\right)\,dudvd\omega+\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\tau_{2}}}t^{-{\mu_{q}}}r^{p}|\partial_{v}\varphi|^{2}\,dvd\omega

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathcal{D}^{\tau_{2}}_{\tau_{1}}}2t^{-\mu_{q}}r^{p-2}(\gamma+r^{2}t^{2}\mathcal{V}_{\beta})|\varphi||\partial_{v}\varphi|\,dudvd\omega+\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\tau_{1}}}t^{-{\mu_{q}}}r^{p}|\partial_{v}\varphi|^{2}\,dvd\omega+\mathfrak{C}\mathcal{E}_{q}[\psi](\tau_{1})\,,

in which

, and

. The former term on the right-hand side should be viewed as an error term that needs to be controlled by the spacetime terms on the left-hand side. This error term involves both

with bounded

Observe that the power of

The energy decay and the pointwise decay estimates then follow as before. See Section

2.1
The twisted energy-momentum tensor

In this section, we consider the covariant wave equation
\Box_{g}\psi=g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\psi=0
for a general Lorentzian metric
g
on a 4-dimensional manifold
\mathcal{M}
. Recall the
energy-momentum tensor

\mathbb{T}_{\mu\nu}[\psi]
associated to
\psi
, defined as

\mathbb{T}_{\mu\nu}[\psi]=\nabla_{\mu}\psi\nabla_{\nu}\psi-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}\nabla^{\gamma}\psi\nabla_{\gamma}\psi\,,

and the
divergence identity

\nabla^{\nu}\mathbb{T}_{\mu\nu}[\psi]=\partial_{\mu}\psi\Box_{g}\psi
which is of great importance for proving energy estimates in the this paper; see
[
10
]
*Section 3.3.
We here introduce the twisted energy-momentum tensor
\tilde{\mathbb{T}}^{\beta}_{\mu\nu}[\psi]
associated with
\psi
and the twisting function
\beta
, which will play a crucial role in the following sections. See
[
10
]
*Appendix A.2 for the proof of identities stated in this section.

To proceed, consider a non-zero
twisting function

\beta
and define the twisted covariant derivatives as

\tilde{\nabla}_{\mu}(\,\cdot\,):=\beta\nabla_{\mu}(\beta^{-1}\,\cdot\,),\qquad\tilde{\nabla}^{\dagger}_{\mu}(\,\cdot\,):=-\beta^{-1}\nabla_{\mu}(\beta\,\cdot\,)\,.

With these new operators, one can rewrite the wave equation as

0=\Box_{g}\psi=-\tilde{\nabla}_{\mu}^{\dagger}\tilde{\nabla}^{\mu}\psi-\mathcal{V}_{\beta}\psi=g^{\mu\nu}\beta^{-1}\nabla_{\mu}(\beta^{2}\nabla_{\nu}(\beta^{-1}\psi))-\mathcal{V}_{\beta}\psi\,,

(2.1)

in which
\mathcal{V}_{\beta}=-\frac{\Box_{g}\beta}{\beta}
. We now define the
twisted energy-momentum tensor
for any function
\psi:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{R}
as

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbb{T}}^{\beta}_{\mu\nu}[\psi]:=

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{\mu}\psi\tilde{\nabla}_{\nu}\psi-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}\left(\tilde{\nabla}^{\gamma}\psi\tilde{\nabla}_{\gamma}\psi+\mathcal{V}_{\beta}\psi^{2}\right)

(2.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta^{2}\left[{\nabla}_{\mu}(\beta^{-1}\psi){\nabla}_{\nu}(\beta^{-1}\psi)-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}\left({\nabla}^{\gamma}(\beta^{-1}\psi){\nabla}_{\gamma}(\beta^{-1}\psi)+\mathcal{V}_{\beta}\beta^{-2}\psi^{2}\right)\right].

Note that by letting
\beta=1
, we recover the usual energy-momentum tensor
\mathbb{T}_{\mu\nu}
. Similar to
\mathbb{T}_{\mu\nu}[\psi]
, the twisted energy-momentum
\tilde{\mathbb{T}}^{\beta}_{\mu\nu}[\psi]
benefits from satisfying a corresponding divergence property. Indeed,
\nabla^{\mu}\tilde{\mathbb{T}}^{\beta}_{\mu\nu}[\psi]=\tilde{\nabla}_{\nu}\psi\Box_{g}\phi+\tilde{S}^{\beta}_{\nu}[\psi]
where

\displaystyle\tilde{S}^{\beta}_{\mu}[\psi]=\frac{\tilde{\nabla}^{\dagger}_{\mu}(\beta\mathcal{V}_{\beta})}{2\beta}\psi^{2}+\frac{\tilde{\nabla}^{\dagger}_{\mu}\beta}{2\beta}\tilde{\nabla}^{\nu}\psi\tilde{\nabla}_{\nu}\psi\,.

In particular, if
\beta=1
, then
\tilde{S}^{\beta}_{\mu}[\psi]=0
and
\mathbb{T}_{\mu\nu}[\psi]
is divergence free provided that
\psi
solves the wave equation.

For a smooth
vector field multiplier

X
, we next define the associated
twisted energy current

\displaystyle\tilde{J}^{\beta,X}_{\mu}[\psi]

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mathbb{T}}^{\beta}_{\mu\nu}[\psi]X^{\nu}\,,

and

\tilde{K}^{\beta,X}[\psi]={}^{(X)}\pi^{\mu\nu}\tilde{\mathbb{T}}^{\beta}_{\mu\nu}[\psi]+X^{\nu}\tilde{S}^{\beta}_{\nu}[\psi]\,,

with
{}^{(X)}\pi^{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{L}_{X}g)_{\mu\nu}
denoting the
deformation tensor
of
X
, to infer

\displaystyle\nabla^{\mu}\tilde{J}^{\beta,X}_{\mu}[\psi]=\tilde{\nabla}_{X}\psi\Box_{g}\psi+\tilde{K}^{\beta,X}[\psi]\,.

(2.3)

Moreover, for a smooth
modification function

w:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{R}
we have

\displaystyle\nabla^{\mu}\tilde{J}_{\mu}^{\beta,w}[\psi]=w\psi\Box_{g}\psi+\tilde{K}^{\beta,w}[\psi]\,,

with

\displaystyle\tilde{K}^{\beta,w}[\psi]

\displaystyle:=w\beta^{2}\nabla^{\mu}(\beta^{-1}\psi)\nabla_{\mu}(\beta^{-1}\psi)+\left(\mathcal{V}w-\beta^{-1}\nabla^{\mu}\beta\nabla_{\mu}w-\frac{1}{2}\Box_{g}w\right)\psi^{2}\,,

\displaystyle\tilde{J}^{\beta,w}_{\mu}[\psi]

\displaystyle:=w\beta\psi\nabla_{\mu}(\beta^{-1}\psi)-\frac{1}{2}\psi^{2}\nabla_{\mu}w\,.

Finally for a vector field multiplier
X
and a modification function
w
, we define

\tilde{K}^{\beta,X,w}[\psi]:=\tilde{K}^{\beta,X}[\psi]+\tilde{K}^{\beta,w}[\psi]
, and similarly
\tilde{J}^{\beta,X,w}_{\mu}[\psi]:=\tilde{J}^{\beta,X}_{\mu}[\psi]+\tilde{J}^{\beta,w}_{\mu}[\psi]
to obtain the divergence identity

\nabla^{\mu}\tilde{J}^{\beta,X,w}_{\mu}[\psi]=\left(\tilde{\nabla}_{X}\psi+w\psi\right)\Box_{g}\psi+\tilde{K}^{\beta,X,w}[\psi]\,.

(2.4)

In the rest of the paper, we drop the dependence on
\beta
in
\mathcal{V}_{\beta}
,
\tilde{\mathbb{T}}^{\beta}_{\mu\nu}
,
\tilde{J}^{\beta,X,w}
and
\tilde{K}^{\beta,X,w}
whenever the twisting function
\beta
is clearly mentioned.

It is worth mentioning that divergence identities above can also be recovered in an untwisted framework, that is, using
\mathbb{T}_{\mu\nu}[\psi]
instead of
\tilde{\mathbb{T}}_{\mu\nu}[\psi]
. In fact, this is achieved by modifying the energy currents with
q
and
\varpi
as defined in

]
*Section 3.3.

2.2
Foliation and geometry of FLRW spacetimes

The rest of the current article focuses on the manifold

M=(0,\infty)_{t}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x}
with the Lorentizian metric (
1.1
) which in standard spherical coordinates
(t,r,\theta,\phi)
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle g_{q}=-dt^{2}+t^{2q}dr^{2}+R^{2}dw^{2}\,,

in which
d\omega^{2}
denotes the round metric on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
and
R(t,r)=t^{q}r
is the
area radius
. We henceforth suppress the
q
subscript in
g_{q}
.

2.2.1
Double null coordinates

We define the null coordinate functions
u
and
v
as

\displaystyle v=\frac{1}{2}(\frac{t^{1-q}}{1-q}+r)\,,\qquad u=\frac{1}{2}(\frac{t^{1-q}}{1-q}-r)\,.

Note that
(u,v,\theta,\phi)
defines a double null coordinate system on
M
, and function
t
and
r
can be recovered as

\displaystyle t=\left((1-q)(v+u)\right)^{\frac{1}{1-q}}\,,\qquad r=v-u\,.

The null coordinates lead to

\displaystyle\partial_{v}=t^{q}\partial_{t}+\partial_{r}\,,\qquad\partial_{u}=t^{q}\partial_{t}-\partial_{r}\,,\qquad\partial_{t}=\frac{1}{2t^{q}}(\partial_{v}+\partial_{u})\,,\qquad\partial_{r}=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{v}-\partial_{u})\,,

and

\displaystyle dv=\frac{1}{2t^{q}}dt+\frac{1}{2}dr\,,\qquad du=\frac{1}{2t^{q}}dt-\frac{1}{2}dr\,.

Thus, in double null coordinates, the metric takes the form

g=-4t^{2q}dvdu+R^{2}d\omega^{2}=t^{2q}\left(-4dvdu+r^{d}\omega^{2}\right)\,,

which shows that FLRW metrics are conformal to the Minkowski metric. Moreover, the volume form is given by

\displaystyle d\Vol_{\text{FLRW}}=t^{3q}dtdxd\omega=2t^{4q}r^{2}dudvd\omega\,,

where
d\omega
is the standard volume form of the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}
.

2.3
Wave equation on FLRW spacetimes

In this paper, the wave equation on FLRW is mainly represented in a twisted form. If we let
\beta=a(t)^{-1}=t^{-q}
in (
2.1
), we then are left with

\Box_{g}\psi=t^{q}g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}(\frac{1}{t^{2q}}\nabla_{\nu}(t^{q}\psi))-\mathcal{V}\psi=0\,,

in which
\mathcal{V}=-\frac{1}{\beta}\Box_{g}\beta=-\frac{q(2q-1)}{t^{2}}
. The above equation can also be rewritten as

\Box_{g}\psi=-\frac{1}{t^{2q}}\partial_{t}(t^{q}\partial_{t}(t^{q}\psi))+\frac{1}{t^{2q}}\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\psi-\mathcal{V}\psi\,,

(2.10)

which exhibits the twisted structure of the equation. We emphasise the change in the sign of
\mathcal{V}

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}

\displaystyle=0\qquad

\displaystyle\text{ if }\,q=\frac{1}{2}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}

\displaystyle>0\qquad

\displaystyle\text{ if }\,q<\frac{1}{2}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}

\displaystyle<0\qquad

\displaystyle\text{ if }\,q>\frac{1}{2}\,.

Furthermore, the wave equation can be represented in double null coordinates as

\Box_{g}\psi=-\frac{1}{t^{3q}r}\partial_{uv}(t^{q}r\psi)+\frac{1}{t^{2q}r^{2}}\mathring{\not{\Delta}}\psi-\mathcal{V}\psi\,,

(2.11)

with
\mathring{\not{\Delta}}
denoting the Laplacian induced on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
.

The main twisting function considered in this article is
\beta=\frac{1}{t^{q}}
. This function serves as the default twisting function throughout the next sections unless explicitly stated otherwise.

Remark 2.3

(Commutation with twisted time derivative)
.

It is worth remarking that equation (
2.10
) indicates that in the radiation case, we have

\Box_{g}(\partial_{t}(\sqrt{t}\psi))=\partial_{t}(\sqrt{t}\Box_{g}\psi)+\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\Box_{g}\psi.

In other words, if
\psi
solves the wave equation, then
\partial_{t}(\sqrt{t}\psi)
is also a solution — a property that holds specifically for
q=\frac{1}{2}
. In general, we instead have

\Box_{g}(\partial_{t}(t^{q}\psi))=\partial_{t}(t^{q}\Box_{g}\psi)+2qt^{q-1}\Box_{g}\psi+2(q-1)\mathcal{V}t^{q-1}\psi\,.

2.4
Functional inequalities

In this section, we present the general functional inequalities that are used throughout the paper.

Lemma 2.4

(Abstract hierarchical estimates)
.

Let
0\leq\sigma<1
. Assume
f
,
h_{1}
, and
h_{2-\sigma}
are positive continuous functions, satisfying

\displaystyle f(\tau_{2})

\displaystyle\lesssim f(\tau_{1})\,,

(0-EE)

\displaystyle\int^{\tau_{2}}_{\tau_{1}}f(\tau)\,d\tau+h_{1}(\tau_{2})

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{1}(\tau_{1})+f(\tau_{1})\,,

(1-EE)

\displaystyle\left(\int^{\tau_{2}}_{\tau_{1}}h_{1}^{\frac{1}{1-\sigma}}(\tau)\,d\tau\right)^{1-\sigma}+h_{2-\sigma}(\tau_{2})

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{2-\sigma}(\tau_{1})+f(\tau_{1})\,,

(2-EE)

for any
\tau_{2}>\tau_{1}>\tau_{0}
. Then, for any
\tau>\tau_{0}
we have

f(\tau)\lesssim\frac{1}{\tau^{2-\sigma}}\mathfrak{C}\,,

and

h_{1}(\tau)\lesssim\frac{1}{\tau^{1-\sigma}}\mathfrak{C}\,,

where
\mathfrak{C}=h_{1}(\tau_{0})+f(\tau_{0})+\left(h_{2-\sigma}(\tau_{0})+f(\tau_{0})\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\sigma}}
.

Proof.

First note that for
\tau\geq 2\tau_{0}
, (
0-EE
) and (
1-EE
) together imply

f(\tau)\lesssim\frac{1}{\tau-\tau_{0}}\int^{\tau}_{\tau_{0}}f(\tau^{\prime})\,d\tau^{\prime}\lesssim\frac{1}{\tau}\underbrace{\left(h_{1}(\tau_{0})+f(\tau_{0})\right)}_{:=\mathfrak{C}_{1}}.

Also, for bounded
\tau\leq 2\tau_{0}
, (
0-EE
) gives
f(\tau)\lesssim\frac{1}{\tau}\mathfrak{C}_{1}
.
To improve this decay rate, consider a dyadic sequence
\{\tau_{n}\}_{n}
, that is,
\tau_{n+1}\sim\tau_{n}\sim\tau_{n+1}-\tau_{n}\sim 2^{n}
. From the mean value theorem and (
2-EE
), we deduce that there exists
\bar{\tau}\in(\tau_{n},\tau_{n+1})
such that

\bar{\tau}_{n}h_{1}^{\frac{1}{1-\sigma}}(\bar{\tau}_{n})\lesssim\int^{\tau_{n+1}}_{\tau_{n}}h_{1}^{\frac{1}{1-\sigma}}(\tau^{\prime})\,d\tau^{\prime}\lesssim\left(h_{2-\sigma}(\tau_{n})+f(\tau_{n})\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\sigma}}\lesssim(h_{2-\sigma}(\tau_{0})+f(\tau_{0}))^{\frac{1}{1-\sigma}}=:\mathfrak{C}_{2}^{\frac{1}{1-\sigma}},

which implies
h_{1}(\tau_{n})\lesssim\frac{1}{\tau_{n}^{1-\sigma}}\mathfrak{C}_{2}
. Applying (
0-EE
) and (
1-EE
) again gives

(\tau_{n+1}-\tau_{n})f(\tau_{n+1})\lesssim\int^{\tau_{n+1}}_{\tau_{n}}f(\tau^{\prime})\,d\tau^{\prime}\lesssim h_{1}(\tau_{n})+f(\tau_{n})\lesssim\frac{1}{\tau_{n}^{1-\sigma}}\mathfrak{C}_{2}+\frac{1}{\tau_{n}}\mathfrak{C}_{1}\leq\frac{\mathfrak{C}}{\tau_{n}^{1-\sigma}}\,,

with
\mathfrak{C}=\mathfrak{C}_{1}+\mathfrak{C}_{2}
.
Finally, since for any
\tau
there exists
n
such that
\tau_{n+1}\leq\tau<\tau_{n+2}
, we have

f(\tau)\lesssim f(\tau_{n+1})\lesssim\frac{\mathfrak{C}}{\tau^{2-\sigma}}\,,

and

h_{1}(\tau)\lesssim h_{1}(\tau_{n+1})\lesssim\frac{\mathfrak{C}}{\tau^{2-\sigma}}\,,

as desired.
∎

Lemma 2.5

(Interpolation)

\Sigma_{\tau}

Proposition 2.6

(Sobolev inequalities)
.

Suppose

(2.13)

\displaystyle\|g\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\left(|g(\omega)|^{2}+|\mathring{\not{\nabla}}g(\omega)|^{2}+|\mathring{\not{\nabla}}{}^{2}g(\omega)|^{2}\right)\,d\omega\,,

(2.14)

\displaystyle\Big\|h(x)-\frac{1}{|\mathbb{T}^{3}|}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}h(y)\,dy\Big\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla^{2}h(x)|\,dx\,.

(2.15)

Moreover, for any fixed

\displaystyle\|f\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(B_{s})}\lesssim s^{-3}\int_{B_{2s}}\left(|f(x)|^{2}+s^{2}|\nabla f(x)|^{2}+s^{4}|\nabla^{2}f(x)|^{2}\right)\,dx\,.

Proof.

To prove the first estimate, let
\hat{f}(\xi)
denotes the Fourier transform of

\displaystyle|f(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\hat{f}(\xi)|\,d\xi=\int_{B_{1}}|\hat{f}(\xi)|\,d\xi+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B_{1}}|\hat{f}(\xi)|\,d\xi

.

For the proof of (

[
8
]
*Section 5.2.

\displaystyle|h(x)-\frac{1}{|\mathbb{T}^{3}|}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}f(y)\,dy|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{3},k\neq 0}|\hat{f}(k)e^{-ik\cdot x|}|\lesssim\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{3},k\neq 0}|\hat{f}(k)||k|^{\alpha}|k|^{-\alpha}

∎

Lemma 2.7

(Grönwall’s inequality)
.

Let
f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function. Assume that there exist a constant

and a non-negative function

such that for all

\displaystyle f(t)\leq\mathfrak{C}+\int_{a}^{t}g(t)f(t)\,dt\,.

Then, for all

\displaystyle f(t)\leq\mathfrak{C}\exp\left({\int_{a}^{t}g(t)\,dt}\right)\,.

The proof of Lemma
2.7
is standard.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let

be a smooth function.

\displaystyle|\not{\Delta}_{\partial B_{r}}f|\lesssim\frac{1}{(1+r)^{2}}\sum_{i=1,2,3}\sum_{j=1,2,3}\left(|\Omega_{i}\Omega_{j}f|+|\partial_{x^{i}}\partial_{x^{j}}f|\right)\,.

∎

(3.1)

Proposition 3.1

(Energy boundedness for

)
.

Let
\psi

1.4
) with
0<q\leq\frac{1}{2}

\tau_{2}>\tau_{1}
we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{q}[\psi](\tau_{2})

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{q}[\psi](\tau_{1})\,,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{I}^{\tau_{2}}_{\tau_{1}}}\left(|\partial_{u}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}|\partial_{u}(t^{q}r\psi)|^{2}+\frac{t^{2q}}{r^{2}}|\mathring{\not{\nabla}}\psi|^{2}+t^{4q}\mathcal{V}\psi^{2}+\frac{t^{2q}}{r^{2}}\psi^{2}\right)\,r^{2}dvd\omega

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{q}[\psi](\tau_{1})\,.

(3.3)

Proof.

To begin with, set
\beta_{1}=t^{-q}
and
X_{1}=t^{q}\partial_{t}
. Observe that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\tilde{J}^{X}_{t}[\psi]=\frac{1}{2t^{q}}\left(|\partial_{t}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}+|\nabla\psi|^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}t^{q}\mathcal{V}\psi^{2}\,,\\
&\tilde{\mathbb{T}}_{\mu\nu}[\psi]{}^{(X)}\pi^{\mu\nu}=\frac{q}{t^{1+q}}\left(|\partial_{t}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}-|\nabla\psi|^{2}\right)-2qt^{q-1}\mathcal{V}\psi^{2}\,,\\
&X^{\mu}\tilde{S}_{\mu}[\psi]=\frac{q}{t^{1+q}}\left(-|\partial_{t}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}+|\nabla\psi|^{2}\right)+(q+1)t^{q-1}\mathcal{V}\psi^{2}\,,\end{split}

(3.4)

and

\tilde{K}^{X}[\psi]=(1-q)t^{q-1}\mathcal{V}\psi^{2}\,.

(3.5)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\tilde{\mathbb{T}}_{vv}+\tilde{\mathbb{T}}_{vu})=\frac{1}{2}\left(t^{-2q}|\partial_{v}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}|\mathring{\not{\nabla}}\psi|^{2}+t^{2q}\mathcal{V}\psi^{2}\right)\,,

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\tilde{\mathbb{T}}_{uv}+\tilde{\mathbb{T}}_{uu})=\frac{1}{2}\left(t^{-2q}|\partial_{u}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}|\mathring{\not{\nabla}}\psi|^{2}+t^{2q}\mathcal{V}\psi^{2}\right)\,.

2.4
), after integration over the region

and using Lemma

, yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\tau_{2}}}t^{2q}\left(|\partial_{t}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}+|\nabla\psi|^{2}+t^{2q}\mathcal{V}\psi^{2}\right)\,dx+\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\tau_{2}}}\left(|\partial_{v}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}+\frac{t^{2q}}{r^{2}}|\mathring{\not{\nabla}}\psi|^{2}+t^{4q}\mathcal{V}\psi^{2}\right)\,r^{2}dvd\omega\\

(3.6)

In order to obtain an energy estimate for the remaining terms of
\mathcal{E}_{q}[\psi](\tau)

. We mark that the potential
\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{2}}

\tilde{K}^{\beta_{2},X_{2}}[\psi]=(1-q)t^{q-1}\mathcal{V}\psi^{2}

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\mathcal{R}^{\tau_{2}}_{\tau_{1}}}t^{4q-1}\mathcal{V}\psi^{2}\,dxdt&+\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\tau_{2}}}t^{2q}\left(|\partial_{t}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}|\partial_{r}(r\psi)|^{2}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}|\mathring{\not{\nabla}}\psi|^{2}+t^{2q}\mathcal{V}\psi^{2}\right)\,dx\\
&+\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\tau_{2}}}\left(\frac{1}{r^{2}}|\partial_{v}(t^{q}r\psi)|^{2}+\frac{t^{2q}}{r^{2}}|\mathring{\not{\nabla}}\psi|^{2}+t^{4q}\mathcal{V}\psi^{2}\right)\,r^{2}dvd\omega\\
&+\int_{\mathcal{I}^{\tau_{2}}_{\tau_{1}}}\left(\frac{1}{r^{2}}|\partial_{u}(rt^{q}\psi)|^{2}+\frac{t^{2q}}{r^{2}}|\mathring{\not{\nabla}}\psi|^{2}+t^{4q}\mathcal{V}\psi^{2}\right)\,r^{2}dud\omega\\
&\lesssim\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\tau_{1}}}t^{2q}\left(|\partial_{t}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}|\partial_{r}(r\psi)|^{2}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}|\mathring{\not{\nabla}}\psi|^{2}+t^{2q}\mathcal{V}\psi^{2}\right)\,dx\\
&+\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\tau_{1}}}\left(\frac{1}{r^{2}}|\partial_{v}(t^{q}r\psi)|^{2}+\frac{t^{2q}}{r^{2}}|\mathring{\not{\nabla}}\psi|^{2}+t^{4q}\mathcal{V}\psi^{2}\right)\,r^{2}dvd\omega\,.\end{split}

(3.7)

To estimate the only remaining term, note that

{t^{2q}}\psi^{2}\lesssim r^{2}|\partial_{m}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}+|\partial_{m}(t^{q}r\psi)|^{2}\,,

(3.8)

where

or

. Combining the above with (

) and (

), we arrive at (

) and (

).

Proposition 3.2

Proof.

In order to show (

\frac{1}{t^{2q}(1+r^{3+\delta})}\psi^{2}\lesssim\psi^{2}\Box_{g}z_{2}.

(3.11)

(3.31)

, observe that

\displaystyle\begin{split}|\partial_{m}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}\lesssim t^{4q-2}|\partial_{m}(t^{1-q}\psi)|^{2}+|\partial_{m}t^{2q-1}|^{2}(t^{1-q}\psi)^{2},\\
t^{4q-2}|\partial_{m}(t^{1-q}\psi)|^{2}\lesssim|\partial_{m}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}+|\partial_{m}t^{2q-1}|^{2}(t^{1-q}\psi)^{2},\end{split}

(3.32)

in which
m
is either
v,u,
or

. Therefore, if we let

\displaystyle\check{\mathcal{E}}_{q}^{\varepsilon}[\psi](\tau):=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\tau}}t^{-\varepsilon}\left(t^{2-2q}|\partial_{t}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}+t^{2-2q}|\nabla\psi|^{2}+\psi^{2}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\tau}}t^{-\varepsilon}\left(t^{2-4q}|\partial_{v}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}+\frac{t^{2-2q}}{r^{2}}|\mathring{\not{\nabla}}\psi|^{2}+\psi^{2}\right)\,r^{2}dvd\omega\,,

we then infer that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\check{\mathcal{E}}_{q}^{\varepsilon}[\psi](\tau_{2})&\lesssim\check{\mathcal{E}}_{q}^{\varepsilon}[\psi](\tau_{1})\,,\\
\int_{\mathcal{I}^{\tau_{2}}_{\tau_{1}}}t^{-\varepsilon}\left(t^{2-4q}|\partial_{u}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}+\frac{t^{2-2q}}{r^{2}}|\mathring{\not{\nabla}}\psi|^{2}+\psi^{2}\right)\,r^{2}dud\omega&\lesssim\check{\mathcal{E}}_{q}^{\varepsilon}[\psi](\tau_{1})\,.\end{split}

(3.33)

Moreover, the basic twisting inequality

|\partial_{t}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}\lesssim t^{2q}|\partial_{t}\psi|^{2}+q^{2}t^{2q-2}|\psi|^{2},

enable us to also control the term

in (

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{R}^{\tau_{2}}_{\tau_{1}}}t^{1-2q-\varepsilon}\left(|\nabla\psi|^{2}+|\partial_{t}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}+t^{2q-2}\psi^{2}\right)\,dxdt\lesssim\check{\mathcal{E}}_{q}^{\varepsilon}[\psi](\tau_{1})\,.

The above finishes the proof of (
3.28
) for
\varepsilon>0
as

\check{\mathcal{E}}_{q}^{\varepsilon}[\psi](\tau)\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{q}^{\varepsilon}[\psi](\tau)
. Before proving (
3.28
) with
\varepsilon=0

3.26

3.27

Similarly, we use the twisting function

\mathcal{V}_{2}=\mathcal{V}_{1}=2(q-1)(2q-1)t^{-2}<0

\tilde{K}^{\beta_{2},X}[\psi]=(4q-2+\frac{\varepsilon}{2})t^{-q-1-\varepsilon}|\partial_{t}(t^{1-q}\psi)|^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\frac{t^{1-5q-\varepsilon}}{r^{2}}|\partial_{r}\psi|^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\frac{t^{1-5q-\varepsilon}}{r^{2}}|\mathring{\not{\nabla}}\psi|^{2}+(1-q+\frac{\varepsilon}{2})t^{1-3q}\mathcal{V}_{1}\psi^{2}\,.

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\tau_{2}}}t^{-\varepsilon}\left(t^{2q}|\partial_{t}(t^{1-q}\psi)|^{2}+\frac{t^{2-2q}}{r^{2}}|\partial_{r}(r\psi)|^{2}+\frac{t^{2-2q}}{r^{2}}|\mathring{\not{\nabla}}\psi|^{2}+\psi^{2}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\tau_{2}}}t^{-\varepsilon}\left(\frac{1}{r^{2}}|\partial_{v}(rt^{1-q}\psi)|^{2}+\frac{t^{2-2q}}{r^{2}}|\mathring{\not{\nabla}}\psi|^{2}+\psi^{2}\right)\,r^{2}dvd\omega

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathcal{I}^{\tau_{2}}_{\tau_{1}}}t^{-\varepsilon}\left(\frac{1}{r^{2}}|\partial_{u}(rt^{1-q}\psi)|^{2}+\frac{t^{2-2q}}{r^{2}}|\mathring{\not{\nabla}}\psi|^{2}+\psi^{2}\right)\,r^{2}dud\omega

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\tau_{1}}}t^{-\varepsilon}\left(\frac{1}{r^{2}}|\partial_{v}(rt^{1-q}\psi)|^{2}+\frac{t^{2-2q}}{r^{2}}|\mathring{\not{\nabla}}\psi|^{2}+\psi^{2}\right)\,r^{2}dvd\omega\,.

Notice that (
3.32

\beta=t^{-q}
. Combining this twisted energy estimate with (

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\tau_{2}}}t^{-\varepsilon}\left(t^{2-2q}|\partial_{t}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}+t^{2-2q}|\partial_{r}\psi|^{2}+\frac{t^{2-2q}}{r^{2}}|\partial_{r}(r\psi)|^{2}+\frac{t^{2-2q}}{r^{2}}|\mathring{\not{\nabla}}\psi|^{2}+\psi^{2}\right)\,dx\\
&+\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\tau_{2}}}t^{-\varepsilon}\left(\frac{t^{2-4q}}{r^{2}}|\partial_{v}(rt^{q}\psi)|^{2}+t^{2-4q}|\partial_{v}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}+\frac{t^{2-2q}}{r^{2}}|\mathring{\not{\nabla}}\psi|^{2}+\psi^{2}\right)\,r^{2}dvd\omega\\
&+\int_{\mathcal{I}^{\tau_{2}}_{\tau_{1}}}t^{-\varepsilon}\left(\frac{t^{2-4q}}{r^{2}}|\partial_{u}(rt^{q}\psi)|^{2}+t^{2-4q}|\partial_{u}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}+\frac{t^{2-2q}}{r^{2}}|\mathring{\not{\nabla}}\psi|^{2}+\psi^{2}\right)\,r^{2}dud\omega\\
&\lesssim\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\tau_{1}}}t^{-\varepsilon}\left(t^{2-2q}|\partial_{t}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}+t^{2-2q}|\partial_{r}\psi|^{2}+\frac{t^{2-2q}}{r^{2}}|\partial_{r}(r\psi)|^{2}+\frac{t^{2-2q}}{r^{2}}|\mathring{\not{\nabla}}\psi|^{2}+\psi^{2}\right)\,dx\\

\displaystyle J^{w}_{v}[\psi]

\displaystyle=t^{1-3q}\psi\partial_{v}\psi-\frac{1-3q}{2t^{2q}}\psi^{2}\,,\qquad

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\mathcal{R}^{\tau_{2}}_{\tau_{1}}}t^{1-2q}|\nabla\psi|^{2}\,dtdx\lesssim\int_{\mathcal{R}^{\tau_{2}}_{\tau_{1}}}t|\partial_{t}\psi|^{2}\,dtdx+\int_{\mathcal{I}^{\tau_{2}}_{\tau_{1}}}\left(t^{2-2q}|\partial_{u}\psi|^{2}+\psi^{2}\right)\,dvd\omega\\
+\sum_{i=1,2}\left(\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\tau_{i}}}\left(t^{2}|\partial_{t}\psi|^{2}+\psi^{2}\right)\,dx+\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\tau_{i}}}\left(t^{2-2q}|\partial_{v}\psi|^{2}+\psi^{2}\right)\,dvd\omega\right)\,.\end{split}

(3.35)

All the terms on the right-hand side can be controlled by identity (

\displaystyle\tilde{J}^{\beta,w}_{t}[\psi]=t^{1-4q}\psi\partial_{t}(t^{q}\psi)-\frac{1-3q}{2t^{3q}}\psi^{2}\,,

\displaystyle\tilde{J}^{\beta,w}_{v}[\psi]

\displaystyle=t^{1-4q}\psi\partial_{v}(t^{q}\psi)-\frac{1-3q}{2t^{2q}}\psi^{2}\,,\qquad

\displaystyle\tilde{J}^{\beta,w}_{u}[\psi]=t^{1-4q}\psi\partial_{u}(t^{q}\psi)-\frac{1-3q}{2t^{2q}}\psi^{2}\,.

After integration over
\mathcal{R}^{\tau_{2}}_{\tau_{1}}

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\mathcal{R}^{\tau_{2}}_{\tau_{1}}}\left(t^{1-2q}|\partial_{t}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}+\frac{1}{t}\psi^{2}\right)\,dtdx\lesssim\int_{\mathcal{R}^{\tau_{2}}_{\tau_{1}}}t^{1-2q}|\nabla\psi|^{2}\,dtdx+\int_{\mathcal{I}^{\tau_{2}}_{\tau_{1}}}\left(t^{2-4q}|\partial_{u}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}+\psi^{2}\right)\,dvd\omega\\
+\sum_{i=1,2}\left(\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\tau_{i}}}\left(t^{2-2q}|\partial_{t}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}+\psi^{2}\right)\,dx+\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\tau_{i}}}\left(t^{2-4q}|\partial_{v}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}+\psi^{2}\right)\,dvd\omega\right)\,.\end{split}

(3.36)

Note that all the boundary terms are bounded by (

) with

Proposition 3.5

(Integrated local energy decay on
\Sigma_{\tau}
for waves on FLRW with
q>\frac{1}{2}
)
.

Let
\varepsilon\geq 0
be any non-negative number and
\delta>0
be any small positive numbers. If
\psi
solves the initial value problem (
1.4
) for
\frac{1}{2}<q<1-\varepsilon
, then we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{R}^{\tau_{2}}_{\tau_{1}}}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{t^{2-3q-\varepsilon}}{(1+r)^{1+\delta}}(|\partial_{t}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}+|\partial_{r}\psi|^{2})+\frac{t^{2-3q-\varepsilon}}{r^{3}}|\mathring{\not{\nabla}}\psi|^{2}+\frac{t^{2-3q-\varepsilon}}{1+r^{3+\delta}}\psi^{2}+\frac{t^{-q-\varepsilon}}{r}\psi^{2}\right)\,dxdt\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{q}^{\varepsilon}[\psi](\tau_{1})\,.

(3.37)

Proof.

To begin with, let
\beta=t^{-q}
,
Y_{1}=t^{2-4q-\varepsilon}\partial_{r}
, and
z_{1}=\frac{t^{2-4q-\varepsilon}}{r}
and recall that
\mathcal{V}=\frac{-q(2q-1)}{t^{2}}
. With this setting, we have
Y_{1}^{\nu}\tilde{S}_{\nu}=0
and

\displaystyle\tilde{K}^{Y_{1},z_{1}}[\psi]=\frac{t^{2-4q-\varepsilon}}{t^{2q}r^{3}}|\mathring{\not{\nabla}}\psi|^{2}+(\frac{1-3q-\varepsilon}{2})(2-4q-\varepsilon)\frac{t^{-4q-\varepsilon}}{r}\psi^{2}+(2-4q-\varepsilon)t^{1-5q-\varepsilon}\partial_{r}\psi\partial_{t}(t^{q}\psi)\,.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tilde{J}^{Y_{1},z_{1}}_{t}=&\frac{t^{2-5q-\varepsilon}}{r}\psi\partial_{t}(t^{q}\psi)+(\frac{-2+4q+\varepsilon}{2})\frac{t^{1-4q-\varepsilon}}{r}\psi^{2}+t^{2-5q-\varepsilon}\partial_{t}(t^{q}\psi)\partial_{r}\psi\,,\\
\tilde{J}^{Y_{1},z_{1}}_{v}=&\frac{t^{2-6q-\varepsilon}}{2}|\partial_{v}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}-\frac{t^{2-4q-\varepsilon}}{2r^{2}}|\mathring{\not{\nabla}}\psi|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}t^{2-2q-\varepsilon}\mathcal{V}\psi^{2}\\
&+\frac{t^{2-4q-\varepsilon}}{r}\psi\partial_{v}(t^{q}\psi)+(\frac{t^{2-4q-\varepsilon}}{2r^{2}}-\frac{2-4q-\varepsilon}{2r}t^{1-3q-\varepsilon})\psi^{2}\,,\\
\tilde{J}^{Y_{1},z_{1}}_{u}=&-\frac{t^{2-6q-\varepsilon}}{2}|\partial_{u}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}+\frac{t^{2-4q-\varepsilon}}{2r^{2}}|\mathring{\not{\nabla}}\psi|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}t^{2-2q-\varepsilon}\mathcal{V}\psi^{2}\\
&+\frac{t^{2-4q-\varepsilon}}{r}\psi\partial_{v}(t^{q}\psi)+(-\frac{t^{2-4q-\varepsilon}}{2r^{2}}-\frac{2-4q-\varepsilon}{2r}t^{1-3q-\varepsilon})\psi^{2}\,.\end{split}

Integration over
\mathcal{R}^{\tau_{2}}_{\tau_{1}}\setminus\{r<\epsilon\}
and letting
\epsilon\to 0
give rise to

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\mathcal{R}^{\tau_{2}}_{\tau_{1}}}&\left(\frac{t^{2-3q-\varepsilon}}{r^{3}}|\mathring{\not{\nabla}}\psi|^{2}+\frac{t^{-q-\varepsilon}}{r}\psi^{2}\right)\,dxdt+\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\tau_{2}}}\frac{t^{1-q-\varepsilon}}{r}\psi^{2}\,dx+\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\tau_{2}}}\ \frac{t^{1-q-\varepsilon}}{r}\psi^{2}\,r^{2}dvd\omega+\int_{\mathcal{I}^{\tau_{2}}_{\tau_{1}}}\frac{t^{1-q-\varepsilon}}{r}\psi^{2}\,r^{2}dud\omega\\
&\lesssim\int_{\mathcal{R}^{\tau_{2}}_{\tau_{1}}}t^{1-2q-\varepsilon}\left(|\nabla\psi|^{2}+|\partial_{t}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}\right)\,dxdt+\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\tau_{1}}}\frac{t^{1-q-\varepsilon}}{r}\psi^{2}\,dx+\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\tau_{1}}}\frac{t^{1-q-\varepsilon}}{r}\psi^{2}\,r^{2}dvd\omega\\
&+\int_{\mathcal{I}^{\tau_{2}}_{\tau_{1}}}\left(t^{2-4q-\varepsilon}|\partial_{u}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}+\frac{t^{2-2q-\varepsilon}}{r^{2}}|\mathring{\not{\nabla}}\psi|^{2}+t^{-\varepsilon}\psi^{2}+\frac{t^{2-2q-\varepsilon}}{r^{2}}\psi^{2}\right)\,r^{2}dud\omega+\mathcal{E}_{q}^{\varepsilon}[\psi](\tau_{2})+\mathcal{E}_{q}^{\varepsilon}[\psi](\tau_{1})\,.\end{split}

(3.38)

Due to Proposition
3.4
, all the terms on the right-hand side above can be directly controlled by
\mathcal{E}_{q}^{\varepsilon}[\psi](\tau_{1})
, except the initial boundary terms that contain
\frac{t^{1-q-\varepsilon}}{r}\psi^{2}
. Nevertheless, those terms are also bounded by the zeroth-order term in
\mathcal{E}_{q}^{\varepsilon}[\psi](\tau_{1})
. Indeed, for large
r
, in view of Lemma
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\tau_{1}}}\frac{t^{1-q-\varepsilon}}{r}\psi^{2}\,dx+\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\tau_{1}}}\frac{t^{1-q-\varepsilon}}{r}\psi^{2}\,r^{2}dvd\omega\lesssim\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\tau_{1}}}\frac{t^{2-2q-\varepsilon}}{1+r^{2}}\psi^{2}\,dx+\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\tau_{1}}}\frac{t^{2-2q-\varepsilon}}{1+r^{2}}\psi^{2}\,r^{2}dvd\omega\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{q}^{\varepsilon}[\psi](\tau_{1})\,.

And for small
r
, we recall (
3.8
) to get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\tau_{1}}}\frac{t^{1-q-\varepsilon}}{r}\psi^{2}\,dx\lesssim\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\tau_{1}}}\left(t^{1-q-\varepsilon}r|\partial_{r}\psi|^{2}+\frac{t^{1-q-\varepsilon}}{r}|\partial_{r}(r\psi)|^{2}\right)\,dx\lesssim\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\tau_{1}}}t^{2-2q-\varepsilon}\left(|\partial_{r}\psi|^{2}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}|\partial_{r}(r\psi)|^{2}\right)\,dx\,.

The other term on
\mathcal{C}_{\tau_{1}}
can also be controlled similarly.

Controlling other derivatives over the region
\mathcal{R}^{\tau_{2}}_{\tau_{1}}
requires multiplying with new vector field. To do so, let
\delta>0
,
Y_{2}=\frac{t^{2-4q-\varepsilon}}{(1+r)^{\delta}}\partial_{r}
, and
z_{2}=\frac{t^{2-4q-\varepsilon}}{r(1+r)^{\delta}}
, and observe that

\displaystyle\tilde{K}^{Y_{2}}[\psi]=

\displaystyle(4q-2+\varepsilon)\frac{t^{2-4q-\varepsilon}}{t(1+r)^{\delta}}t^{-q}\partial_{r}\psi\partial_{t}(t^{q}\psi)+\frac{t^{2-4q-\varepsilon}}{t^{2q}r(1+r)^{\delta}}\left(|\partial_{t}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}-|\partial_{r}\psi|^{2}-t^{2q}\mathcal{V}\psi^{2}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{\delta t^{2-4q-\varepsilon}}{2t^{2q}(1+r)^{1+\delta}}\left(|\partial_{t}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}+|\partial_{r}\psi|^{2}-\frac{1}{r^{2}}|\mathring{\not{\nabla}}\psi|^{2}-t^{2q}\mathcal{V}\psi^{2}\right)\,,

\displaystyle\tilde{K}^{z_{2}}[\psi]=

\displaystyle\frac{t^{2-4q-\varepsilon}}{t^{2q}r(1+r)^{\delta}}\left(-|\partial_{t}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}+|\partial_{r}\psi|^{2}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}|\mathring{\not{\nabla}}\psi|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{t^{2-4q-\varepsilon}}{r(1+r)^{\delta}}\mathcal{V}-\frac{1}{2}\Box z_{2}+q(4q-2+\varepsilon)\frac{t^{-4q-\varepsilon}}{r(1+r)^{\delta}}\right)\psi^{2}\,,

Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tilde{J}^{Y_{2},z_{2}}_{t}=&\frac{t^{2-5q-\varepsilon}}{r(1+r)^{\delta}}\psi\partial_{t}(t^{q}\psi)+(\frac{-2+4q+\varepsilon}{2})\frac{t^{1-4q-\varepsilon}}{r(1+r)^{\delta}}\psi^{2}+\frac{t^{2-5q-\varepsilon}}{(1+r)^{\delta}}\partial_{t}(t^{q}\psi)\partial_{r}\psi\,,\\
\tilde{J}^{Y_{2},z_{2}}_{v}=&\frac{t^{2-6q-\varepsilon}}{2(1+r)^{\delta}}|\partial_{v}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}-\frac{t^{2-4q-\varepsilon}}{2r^{2}(1+r)^{\delta}}|\mathring{\not{\nabla}}\psi|^{2}+\frac{1}{2(1+r)^{\delta}}t^{2-2q-\varepsilon}\mathcal{V}\psi^{2}\\
&+\frac{t^{2-4q-\varepsilon}}{r(1+r)^{\delta}}\psi\partial_{v}(t^{q}\psi)+(\frac{t^{2-4q-\varepsilon}}{2r^{2}(1+r)^{\delta}}+\frac{\delta t^{2-4q-\varepsilon}}{2r(1+r)^{1+\delta}}-\frac{2-4q-\varepsilon}{2r(1+r)^{\delta}}t^{1-3q-\varepsilon})\psi^{2}\,,\\
\tilde{J}^{Y_{2},z_{2}}_{u}=&-\frac{t^{2-6q-\varepsilon}}{2(1+r)^{\delta}}|\partial_{u}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}+\frac{t^{2-4q-\varepsilon}}{2r^{2}(1+r)^{\delta}}|\mathring{\not{\nabla}}\psi|^{2}-\frac{1}{2(1+r)^{\delta}}t^{2-2q-\varepsilon}\mathcal{V}\psi^{2}\\
&+\frac{t^{2-4q-\varepsilon}}{r(1+r)^{\delta}}\psi\partial_{v}(t^{q}\psi)+(-\frac{t^{2-4q-\varepsilon}}{2r^{2}(1+r)^{\delta}}-\frac{\delta t^{2-4q-\varepsilon}}{2r(1+r)^{1+\delta}}-\frac{2-4q-\varepsilon}{2r(1+r)^{\delta}}t^{1-3q-\varepsilon})\psi^{2}\,.\end{split}

As before, we integrate over
\mathcal{R}^{\tau_{2}}_{\tau_{1}}\setminus\{r<\epsilon\}
, let
\epsilon\to 0
, and use (
2.4
) to derive

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{R}^{\tau_{2}}_{\tau_{1}}}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{t^{2-3q-\varepsilon}}{(1+r)^{1+\delta}}(|\partial_{t}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}+|\partial_{r}\psi|^{2})+t^{3q}\psi^{2}\Box z_{2}\right)\,dxdt

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{R}^{\tau_{2}}_{\tau_{1}}}\left(\frac{t^{2-3q-\varepsilon}}{r^{3}(1+r)^{\delta}}|\mathring{\not{\nabla}}\psi|^{2}+\frac{t^{1-2q-\varepsilon}}{(1+r)^{\delta}}(|\partial_{r}\psi|^{2}+|\partial_{t}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2})+\frac{t^{-q-\varepsilon}}{r(1+r)^{\delta}}\psi^{2}\right)\,dxdt

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1,2}\left(\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\tau_{i}}}|\tilde{J}^{Y_{2},z_{2}}_{t}|\,t^{3q}dx+\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\tau_{i}}}|\tilde{J}^{Y_{2},z_{2}}_{v}|\,t^{2q}r^{2}dvd\omega\right)+\int_{\mathcal{I}^{\tau_{2}}_{\tau_{1}}}|\tilde{J}^{Y_{2},z_{2}}_{u}|\,t^{2q}r^{2}dud\omega\,.

Now thanks to (
3.26
), (
3.27
), (
3.28
), and (
3.38
) we infer

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{R}^{\tau_{2}}_{\tau_{1}}}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{t^{2-3q-\varepsilon}}{(1+r)^{1+\delta}}(|\partial_{t}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}+|\partial_{r}\psi|^{2})+\frac{t^{2-3q-\varepsilon}}{r^{3}}|\mathring{\not{\nabla}}\psi|^{2}+\frac{t^{-q-\varepsilon}}{r}\psi^{2}+t^{3q}\psi^{2}\Box z_{2}\right)\,dxdt\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{q}^{\varepsilon}[\psi](\tau_{1})\,.

To finish, just note that similar to (
3.11
), we have

\displaystyle(4q-2+\varepsilon)(1-q-\varepsilon)\frac{t^{-q-\varepsilon}}{r(1+r)^{\delta}}\psi^{2}+\frac{t^{2-3q-\varepsilon}}{1+r^{3+\delta}}\psi^{2}\lesssim t^{3q}\psi^{2}\Box z_{2}\,,

which is positive for
\frac{1}{2}<q<1-\varepsilon
.
∎

Before stating the next proposition, recall that
\sigma_{q}=2\sqrt{\frac{q|1-2q|}{|1-q|^{2}}}\geq 1
for
q\geq\frac{1+2\sqrt{2}}{7}
.

Proposition 3.6

(
r^{p}
-estimates for waves on FLRW with
q>\frac{1}{2}
)
.

Let
\varepsilon=0
and
\mu_{q}=\mu_{q}^{0}=4q-2

and for
\frac{1}{2}<q<1
, set
\sigma_{q}=2\sqrt{\frac{q|1-2q|}{|1-q|^{2}}}>0
. Then for any solution
\psi
to the wave equation (
1.4
), any
\tau_{2}>\tau_{1}\geq\tau_{0}
, and any

0<p<\max\{1,2-\sigma_{q}\}\,,

When

and

)to derive the above estimate. Furthermore, with the help of Lemma

, we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int^{\tau_{2}}_{\tau_{1}}h_{1}^{\frac{1}{1-\sigma}}(\tau)\,d\tau&\lesssim\int^{\tau_{2}}_{\tau_{1}}h_{1-\sigma}(\tau)h^{\frac{\sigma}{1-\sigma}}_{2-\sigma}(\tau)\,d\tau\lesssim\left(h_{2-\sigma}(\tau_{1})+f(\tau_{1})\right)^{\frac{\sigma}{1-\sigma}}\int^{\tau_{2}}_{\tau_{1}}h_{1-\sigma}(\tau)\,d\tau\\
&\lesssim\left(h_{2-\sigma}(\tau_{1})+f(\tau_{1})\right)^{\frac{\sigma}{1-\sigma}}(h_{2-\sigma}(\tau_{1})+f(\tau_{1}))=\left(h_{2-\sigma}(\tau_{1})+f(\tau_{1})\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\sigma}}\,,\end{split}

(4.8)

from which (

in which

Now suppose

) provide

\displaystyle f(\tau_{2})\lesssim f(\tau_{1})\,,

for

To derive (

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{D}^{\tau_{2}}_{\tau_{1}}}t^{-{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}}\left(|\partial_{v}\varphi|^{2}+r^{2}|\partial_{v}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}|\mathring{\not{\nabla}}\varphi|^{2}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\varphi^{2}+t^{2q-2}\varphi^{2}\right)\,dudvd\omega+\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\tau_{2}}}t^{-{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}}r|\partial_{v}\varphi|^{2}\,dvd\omega

with

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{R}^{\tau_{2}}_{\tau_{1}}\setminus\mathcal{D}^{\tau_{2}}_{\tau_{1}}}

\displaystyle t^{2-4q-\varepsilon}\left(|\partial_{v}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}+|\partial_{u}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}+\frac{t^{2q}}{r^{2}}|\mathring{\not{\nabla}}\psi|^{2}+t^{2q}\psi^{2}+\frac{t^{4q-2}}{r}\psi^{2}\right)\,r^{2}dudvd\omega\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{q}^{\varepsilon}[\psi](\tau_{1})\,.

For the compact region

4.2
Pointwise decay

The next statement concerns the pointwise decay of the solutions. To begin with, define

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{E}}_{q}[\psi_{0},\psi_{1}]=\mathcal{E}_{q}[\psi_{0},\psi_{1}]+\sum_{j=0,1,2}\sum_{i=1,2,3}\mathcal{E}_{q}[\Omega_{i}^{j}\psi|_{\Sigma_{\tau_{0}}},\partial_{t}\Omega_{i}^{j}\psi|_{\mathcal{S}_{\tau_{0}}}]+\sum_{i=1,2,3}\mathcal{E}_{q}[\partial_{x^{i}}\psi|_{\Sigma_{\tau_{0}}},\partial_{t}\partial_{x^{i}}\psi|_{\mathcal{S}_{\tau_{0}}}]\,.

(4.9)

Proposition 4.4

(Pointwise decay of waves on FLRW )
.

For
q\neq\frac{1}{3}
, let
\varepsilon
,
\sigma
, and
\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}
be as defined in (
4.1
). If
\psi
solves the initial value problem (
1.4
), then

\displaystyle|\psi|\lesssim\frac{t^{\frac{{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}}{2}}\tau^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}}{\tau t^{q}\sqrt{1+r}}\bar{\mathcal{E}}_{q}[\psi_{0},\psi_{1}]^{\frac{1}{2}}\,,

(4.10)

where
\bar{\mathcal{E}}_{q}[\psi_{0},\psi_{1}]
is defined as (
4.9
). Moreover,

\displaystyle|\psi|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{t^{\frac{{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}}{2}}\tau^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}}{(1+r)t^{q}\sqrt{\tau}}\bar{\mathcal{E}}_{q}[\psi_{0},\psi_{1}]^{\frac{1}{2}}\,,\qquad

\displaystyle\mathrm{if}

\displaystyle 0<q<\frac{1+2\sqrt{2}}{7}\,,

(4.11)

\displaystyle|\psi|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{t^{\frac{{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}}{2}}\log(1+r)}{(1+r)t^{q}}\bar{\mathcal{E}}_{q}[\psi_{0},\psi_{1}]^{\frac{1}{2}}\,,\qquad

\displaystyle\mathrm{if}

\displaystyle\frac{1+2\sqrt{2}}{7}\leq q<1\,.

In particular,

\displaystyle|\psi|\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{1-(1-q)\frac{\sigma}{2}-\frac{{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}}{2}}}\bar{\mathcal{E}}_{q}[\psi_{0},\psi_{1}]^{\frac{1}{2}}\,.

(4.12)

Proof.

First, we prove (
4.10
) and (
4.11
) with
r^{-1}
instead of
(1+r)^{-1}
, which are equivalent for large
r
. Later we show the decay rate for small
r
to avoid the degeneracy around
r=0
.

In order to prove the (
4.10
), consider any point
(t_{\circ},r_{\circ},\omega_{\circ})
in
\mathcal{M}
with
r_{\circ}\geq\rho
. We then integrate

\partial_{v}(t^{q-\frac{{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}}{2}}\psi)=t^{-\frac{{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}}{2}}\partial_{v}(t^{q}\psi)-\frac{{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}}{2}t^{2q-\frac{{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}}{2}-1}\psi\,,

with respect to
v
from
v(r_{\circ})
to
v^{*}
for fixed
u
to get

\displaystyle\begin{split}|t_{\circ}^{q-\frac{{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}}{2}}\psi(t_{\circ},r_{\circ},\omega_{\circ})|^{2}&\leq\Big|t^{q-\frac{{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}}{2}}\psi|_{v=v^{*}}\Big|^{2}+\left(\int^{v^{*}}_{v(r_{\circ})}(t^{-\frac{{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}}{2}}\partial_{v}(t^{q}\psi)+\frac{{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}}{2}t^{-\frac{{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}}{2}+2q-1}\psi)\;dv\right)^{2}\\
&\lesssim\Big|t^{q-\frac{{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}}{2}}\psi|_{v=v^{*}}\Big|^{2}+\frac{1}{r_{\circ}}\left(\int^{v^{*}}_{v(r_{\circ})}t^{-{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}}|\partial_{v}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}\;r^{2}dv+\int^{v^{*}}_{v(r_{\circ})}{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}^{2}t^{-\varepsilon}\psi^{2}\;r^{2}dv\right)\,,\end{split}

(4.13)

remembering that
{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}=0
when
q\leq\frac{1}{2}
. If we now integrate over
\mathbb{S}^{2}
and let
v^{*}\to\infty
, the term on future null infinity vanishes because the energy boundedness estimates (
3.2
) and (
3.26
) assert that there exists a sequence
\{v_{n}\}_{n}
for each
u
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\{v=v_{n}\}}t^{2q-\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}\psi^{2}\;d\omega=0\,.

Thus,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|\psi(t_{\circ},r_{\circ},\omega)|^{2}\;d\omega&\lesssim\frac{t_{\circ}^{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}}{t^{2q}_{\circ}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\{v=\infty\}}t^{2q-\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}|\psi|^{2}\;d\omega+\frac{t_{\circ}^{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}}{r_{\circ}t^{2q}_{\circ}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\tau_{\circ}}}\left(t^{-{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}}|\partial_{v}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}+{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}^{2}t^{-\varepsilon}\psi^{2}\right)\;r^{2}dvd\omega\\
&\lesssim\frac{t_{\circ}^{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}}{r_{\circ}t^{2q}_{\circ}}\mathcal{E}_{q}^{\varepsilon}[\psi](\tau_{\circ})\lesssim\frac{t_{\circ}^{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{E}_{q}[\psi_{0},\psi_{1}]}{r_{\circ}t^{2q}_{\circ}\tau^{2-\sigma}_{\circ}}\,,\end{split}

(4.14)

thanks to Proposition
4.1
.

Now recall that the wave equation commutes with angular operators
\Omega_{i}
, defined in Section
2.2.3
, for
i=1,2,3
. Hence, the above is also true for
\Omega_{i}^{j}\psi
with
k=1,2,3
and with suitable initial data sets. The Sobolev inequality in Proposition
2.6
then gives the pointwise decay

\displaystyle\|\psi(t_{\circ},r_{\circ},\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\lesssim\|\psi(t_{\circ},r_{\circ},\cdot)\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:general:angularderivatives-sphere}}}{{\lesssim}}\sum_{j=0,1,2}\sum_{i=1,2,3}\|\Omega_{i}^{j}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\lesssim\frac{t_{\circ}^{\frac{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}{2}}\bar{\mathcal{E}}_{q}[\psi_{0},\psi_{1}]^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\sqrt{r}_{\circ}t_{\circ}^{q}\tau_{\circ}^{1-\frac{\sigma}{2}}}\,.

(4.15)

For the other case,
r_{\circ}\leq\rho
, we integrate with respect to
r
from
r_{\circ}
to
r=\rho
where the decay is known. In fact,

\displaystyle|t_{\circ}^{q-\frac{{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}}{2}}\psi(t_{\circ},r_{\circ},\omega_{\circ})|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int^{\rho}_{r_{\circ}}(t_{\circ}^{-\frac{{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}}{2}}\partial_{v}(t_{\circ}^{q}\psi)+\frac{{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}}{2}t_{\circ}^{-\frac{{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}}{2}+2q-1}\psi)\;dr\right)^{2}+|t_{\circ}^{q-\frac{{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}}{2}}\psi(t_{\circ},\rho,\omega_{\circ})|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{r_{\circ}}\int^{\rho}_{r_{\circ}}(t_{\circ}^{2q-{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}}|\partial_{t}(t_{\circ}^{q}\psi)|^{2}+t_{\circ}^{2q-{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}}|\partial_{r}\psi|^{2}+{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}^{2}t_{\circ}^{-\varepsilon}\psi^{2})\;r^{2}dr,

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\rho}\left(\int^{\infty}_{v(\rho)}(t^{-{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}}|\partial_{v}(t^{q}\psi)|^{2}\;r^{2}dv+{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}^{2}t^{-\varepsilon}\psi^{2})\;r^{2}dv\right)\,,

in which we have used (
4.13
) to bound the term at
r=\rho
. Observe that the
\mathbb{S}^{2}
-integral of the right-hand side above is bounded by
\frac{1}{r_{\circ}}\mathcal{E}_{q}^{\varepsilon}[\psi](\tau_{\circ})
. Commuting with angular operators and using the Sobolev inequality then again leads to the desired decay.

To prove (
4.11
), we can assume
r_{\circ}>\rho
because otherwise the result simply follows by (
4.10
). In addition, suppose
r_{\circ}\geq\frac{1}{4}u_{\circ}
as the other case is trivial as well. Let
(t_{a},r_{a},\omega_{a})
denote the intersection of
\Sigma_{\tau_{\circ}}
and the timelike hypersurface
\{r=\frac{1}{4}u\}
. We consider
\{r=\frac{1}{4}u\}
because the pointwise inequalities are equivalent at this hypersurface.
Now we integrate
\partial_{v}(\varphi)
with respect to
v
from
v(r_{a})
to
v(r_{\circ})
to derive

\displaystyle|\varphi(t_{\circ},r_{\circ},\omega_{\circ})|^{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle|\varphi(t_{a},r_{a},\omega_{a})|^{2}+\left(\int_{v(r_{a})}^{v(r_{\circ})}\partial_{v}\varphi\,dv\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle t_{a}^{2q}u^{2}_{a}|\psi(t_{a},r_{a},\omega_{a})|^{2}+t_{\circ}^{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}\left(\int_{v(r_{a})}^{v(r_{\circ})}\frac{1}{r^{2-\sigma}}\,dv\right)\left(\int_{v(r_{a})}^{v(r_{\circ})}t^{-{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}}r^{2-\sigma}|\partial_{v}\varphi|^{2}\,dv\right)\,.

If
\sigma<1
, then the integral of
\frac{1}{r^{2-\sigma}}
is bounded by
\frac{1}{r_{a}^{1-\sigma}}
. And if
\sigma=1
, it is bounded by
\log(1+r_{\circ})
. For
\sigma<1
, if we integrate over
\mathbb{S}^{2}
and use Remark
4.2
and estimate (
4.14
) at point

, we find

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|\varphi(t_{\circ},r_{\circ},\omega)|^{2}\,d\omega

\displaystyle\lesssim t_{\circ}^{{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}}t_{a}^{-{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}}u_{a}^{2}\frac{\mathcal{E}_{q}[\psi_{0},\psi_{1}]}{r_{a}\tau^{2-\sigma}_{a}}+\frac{t_{\circ}^{{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}}}{r_{a}^{1-\sigma}}h^{\varepsilon}_{2-\sigma}(\tau_{\circ})\lesssim\frac{t_{\circ}^{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}}{\tau_{\circ}^{1-\sigma}}\mathcal{E}_{q}[\psi_{0},\psi_{1}]\,,

thanks to the facts that
\tau_{\circ}=\tau_{a}\sim r_{a}
and
u_{a}<\tau_{a}
. Once again, commuting with angular derivatives and Sobolev’s inequality finally gives

|r_{\circ}t_{\circ}^{q}\psi(t_{\circ},r_{\circ},\omega_{\circ})|\lesssim\frac{t_{\circ}^{\frac{{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}}{2}}}{\sqrt{\tau_{\circ}^{1-\sigma}}}\bar{\mathcal{E}}_{q}[\psi_{0},\psi_{1}]^{\frac{1}{2}}\,,

which proves the required result for points with
r_{\circ}
away from zero. The other case,
\sigma=1
, follows similarly.

Now to remove the degeneracy at
r=0
, set
0<s<\frac{\rho}{2}
and assume

. The local Sobolev inequality in Proposition
2.6
implies

\displaystyle\|\psi(t_{\circ},\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(B_{s})}

\displaystyle\lesssim s^{-3}\int_{B_{2s}\times{\{t_{\circ}\}}}(|\psi|^{2}+s^{2}|\nabla\psi|^{2}+s^{4}|\nabla^{2}\psi|^{2})\,dx

(4.16)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{s}t^{{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}-2q}_{\circ}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\tau_{\circ}}}t_{\circ}^{2q-{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}}(\frac{1}{(1+r_{\circ})^{2}}|\psi|^{2}+|\nabla\psi|^{2}+|\nabla^{2}\psi|^{2})\,dx

(4.17)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{t^{{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}-2q}_{\circ}}{\tau_{\circ}^{2-\sigma}}\left(\mathcal{E}_{q}[\psi_{0},\psi_{1}]+\sum_{i=1,2,3}\mathcal{E}_{q}[\partial_{x^{i}}\psi|_{\Sigma_{\tau_{0}}},\partial_{t}\partial_{x^{i}}\psi|_{\Sigma_{\tau_{0}}}]\right)\,,

(4.18)

in which we have used the fact that the wave operator on FLRW commutes with
\partial_{x^{i}}
for

, and the decay estimates for twisted energy currents are also true for

\displaystyle\|\psi(t_{\circ},\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{s})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{t^{\frac{{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}}{2}}_{\circ}\tau_{\circ}^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}}{t_{\circ}^{q}\tau_{\circ}}\bar{\mathcal{E}}_{q}[\psi_{0},\psi_{1}]^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\frac{t^{\frac{{\mu_{q}^{\varepsilon}}}{2}}_{\circ}\tau_{\circ}^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}}{(1+r)t_{\circ}^{q}\tau_{\circ}}\bar{\mathcal{E}}_{q}[\psi_{0},\psi_{1}]^{\frac{1}{2}}\,,

) and (

).

), first note that always

. When

, by definition

) easily follows from (

). In the other case, we have

4.3
Decay of first order derivatives

In this section, we present the pointwise decay estimates for the first order derivatives of the solution
\psi
. The main result of this section is Proposition
4.7
, which proves these estimates in the general case.
Nevertheless, in view of Remark
2.3
, commuting with
\partial_{t}(\sqrt{t}\,\cdot\,)
in the radiation case yields an improved decay rate for the time derivative of
\psi
. This approach is detailed in Proposition
4.6
. Note that Propositions
4.6
and
4.7
together prove the last part of Corollary
1.4
.

For the initial value problem (
1.4
) with
q=\frac{1}{2}
, define

\bar{\bar{\mathcal{E}}}[\psi_{0},\psi_{1}]=\bar{\mathcal{E}}_{\frac{1}{2}}[\partial_{t}(\sqrt{t}\psi)|_{\Sigma_{\tau_{0}}},\partial_{tt}(\sqrt{t}\psi)|_{\mathcal{S}_{\tau_{0}}}]\,.

(4.19)

Proposition 4.6

(Decay for the time derivative of waves with
q=\frac{1}{2}
)
.

Suppose
\psi
solves the wave equation for
q=\frac{1}{2}
. Then the time derivative
\partial_{t}\psi
decays as

|\partial_{t}\psi|\lesssim\bar{\bar{\mathcal{E}}}[\psi_{0},\psi_{1}]^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{1}{t(1+r)\sqrt{\tau}}\,,\qquad|\partial_{t}\psi|\lesssim\bar{\bar{\mathcal{E}}}[\psi_{0},\psi_{1}]^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{1}{t\tau\sqrt{1+r}}\,,\qquad|\partial_{t}\psi|\lesssim\bar{\bar{\mathcal{E}}}[\psi_{0},\psi_{1}]^{\frac{1}{2}}t^{-\frac{3}{2}}\,,

in which
\bar{\bar{\mathcal{E}}}[\psi_{0},\psi_{1}]
is defined in (
4.19
).

Proof.

Commute the wave equation with
\partial_{t}(\sqrt{t}\,\cdot\,)
and recall that Remark (
2.3
) indicates
\partial_{t}(\sqrt{t}\psi)
satisfies the wave equation as well. Therefore, Proposition
4.4
also holds for
\partial_{t}(\sqrt{t}\psi)
. Indeed we have

\displaystyle|\sqrt{t}\partial_{t}\psi|\lesssim|\partial_{t}(\sqrt{t}\psi)|+|\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\psi|\lesssim\frac{\bar{\bar{\mathcal{E}}}[\psi_{0},\psi_{1}]^{\frac{1}{2}}}{t}+\frac{\bar{\mathcal{E}}_{q}[\psi_{0},\psi_{1}]^{\frac{1}{2}}}{t\sqrt{t}}\lesssim\frac{\bar{\bar{\mathcal{E}}}_{q}[\psi_{0},\psi_{1}]^{\frac{1}{2}}}{t}\,.

The other two estimates also easily follow from Proposition
4.4
as well.
∎

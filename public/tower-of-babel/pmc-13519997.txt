Topical Menaquinone‑7 (Vitamin K2, MK-7) as a Mitochondrial Bioenergetic Activator for Skin Anti-Aging: Mechanistic Evidence in Skin Cells and a Randomized Split-Face/Neck Study
Authors: He Huang, Ruijing Wei, Chunxiao Xu, Chen Liu, Shilan Zhao, Yanan Li, Meijing Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Mitochondrial dysfunction contributes to skin aging by limiting cellular energy supply, shifting redox balance, and amplifying oxidative injury, which together favor extracellular matrix breakdown and loss of tissue mechanics. Vitamin K2 (menaquinone-7; MK-7) has been proposed to support mitochondrial electron transport, yet its bioenergetic activity in the skin and its effects on aging-related outcomes remain insufficiently characterized. To evaluate whether MK-7 can improve mitochondrial bioenergetics in skin-relevant cells under oxidative stress and whether these effects translate into quantifiable anti-aging benefits in a controlled human study. In vitro , human skin cells were exposed to UVA or hydrogen peroxide to model oxidative stress and then treated with MK-7. ATP content, NAD+/NADH ratio, mitochondrial membrane potential ( ΔΨ m ), mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) copy number, respiratory chain complex activities (I–V), and senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP) expression were assessed using established assays. In vivo , a randomized, double-blind, split-face/neck, vehicle-controlled trial enrolled women aged 35–60 years (n = 30 completers). Participants applied a 0.05% MK-7 face-and-neck cream to one randomized side and placebo to the contralateral side twice daily for 8 weeks. Wrinkles/roughness, elasticity, and sagging were instrumentally quantified at baseline and weeks 2, 4, and 8. MK-7 increased ATP production, elevated the NAD+/NADH ratio, restored ΔΨm , preserved mtDNA copy number under oxidative challenge, enhanced activities of multiple respiratory complexes, and reduced UVA-induced SASP marker expression. In human efficacy evaluations, MK-7 produced significant improvements versus placebo in wrinkle roughness on face and neck, improved elasticity parameters, and attenuated sagging over 8 weeks. MK-7 was associated with improved mitochondrial bioenergetic readouts in vitro and measurable anti-aging effects in a split-face/neck study, supporting further development of mitochondrial targeting approaches for skin aging.

Introduction

Aging is a complex biological process characterized by progressive deterioration at the molecular, cellular, and systemic levels, leading to increased susceptibility to disease and mortality. Recent advancements in GeroScience have delineated 14 hallmarks of aging and organized them into primary, antagonistic, and integrative categories. Within this framework, mitochondrial dysfunction is recognized as a key antagonistic hallmark that interfaces with other hallmarks and shapes aging trajectories across tissues. , In particular, age-associated oxidative stress and altered metabolic cofactor homeostasis are increasingly recognized as convergent processes that impair mitochondrial respiration and cellular ATP availability, thereby amplifying functional decline.

Skin aging is the most visible manifestation of biological aging, characterized by structural and functional decline, including epidermal thinning, dermal extracellular matrix degradation, wrinkle formation, and progressive laxity. Recent reviews underscore that mitochondrial dysfunction is a key driver of skin aging, contributing to both intrinsic chronological changes and extrinsic stressors like UV-induced photoaging by disrupting cellular redox homeostasis, promoting cellular senescence, and compromising extracellular matrix integrity. , These mechanistic pathways align with clinical observations that energy-deficient, oxidative-stress-prone skin exhibits reduced resilience and impaired repair responses, accelerating the emergence of visible aging signs.

Mitochondria are dynamic organelles that play a crucial role in ATP production through OXPHOS, which involves the electron transport chain (ETC) complexes I–IV and ATP synthase (Complex V). Age-related mitochondrial decline is characterized by decreased ETC efficiency, increased production of reactive oxygen species (ROS), and mutations or depletion of mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA). , In the skin, this decline results in impaired function of keratinocytes and fibroblasts, collagen degradation mediated by matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs), and heightened inflammatory responses. , For example, chronic mitochondrial impairment leads to epidermal barrier dysfunction, increased wrinkle formation, and accelerated senescence in dermal cells, often exacerbated by ROS-mediated damage to mtDNA and mitochondrial proteins. , Additionally, alterations in mitochondrial dynamicssuch as fission, fusion, biogenesis, and mitophagyplay critical roles in maintaining skin homeostasis, and their dysregulation during aging results in fragmented networks, reduced energy output, and inflammatory cascades that further propagate tissue degeneration. , Research on human tissues indicates that oxidative stress and NAD+ depletion increase with age, correlating with diminished mitochondrial respiration and ATP levels. Similarly, mtDNA depletion in mice leads to wrinkled skin and hair loss, which can be reversed through mitochondrial restoration.

Vitamin K, discovered in the 1930s by Henrik Dam and Edward Doisy, exists in two forms: phylloquinone (vitamin K1) from plants and menaquinones (vitamin K2) from bacterial fermentation. , Menaquinones are classified into subtypes based on the lengths of their isoprenoid side chains (MK-n). Among these, MK-7 exhibits superior bioavailability due to its long half-life and trans-isomer predominance, enabling efficient biological activity in diverse tissues. While vitamin K has been classically associated with carboxylation biology, its potential role in mitochondrial bioenergetics offers a new perspective for skin anti-aging research.

Beyond vitamin K-dependent proteins, emerging evidence positions MK-7 as a mitochondrial electron carrier. In a Drosophila model of Alzheimer’s disease, vitamin K2 protected against Aβ42-induced neurotoxicity by activating autophagy while improving mitochondrial function and ATP production. A landmark study further proposed vitamin K2 as a mitochondrial electron carrier capable of rescuing pink1 deficiency, conceptually positioning menaquinones as CoQ-like mediators within the ETC. In addition, MK-7 has been reported to influence cellular signaling pathways in diverse cell systems. However, its role in skin aging remains poorly understood. In particular, little is known about whether MK-7 can improve mitochondrial energy metabolism and produce measurable anti-aging benefits in human skin.

This study investigates MK-7 as a potential anti-aging agent for skin health. We hypothesize that MK-7 enhances mitochondrial function in skin cells and improves the relevant parameters in human trials. By clarifying its role as an energy metabolism activator, we aim to provide scientific evidence supporting the incorporation of MK-7 into anti-aging cosmetic formulations.

Materials and Methods

Cell Culture and Treatment

Fibroblasts (Guangdong BioCell Biotech) or keratinocytes (HaCaT, Fuheng Biotech) were seeded and cultured under identical conditions (DMEM high-glucose medium, 37 °C, 5% CO2) until the desired confluence was achieved. Then, treatments were administered based on the experimental grouping.

To establish experimental groups, cells were divided as follows: blank control group (BC), negative control group (NC), positive control group (PC), and test groups. The BC group provided an untreated cellular baseline. The NC group functioned as a model of cell injury. The PC group served as a known effective reference to validate the experimental system. The test groups comprised cells treated with MK-7 (Nanjing Ascend Biotech Co., Ltd.) at predetermined concentrations. MK-7 was dissolved in DMSO prior to being diluted in the culture medium. The final concentration of DMSO in the culture system was 0.1% (v/v). The inclusion of control groups varied, depending on the experimental design.

Evaluation of ATP Content and NAD+/NADH Ratio

ATP content and the NAD+/NADH ratio were evaluated in fibroblasts. The culture medium in the PC group was supplemented with 100 μg/mL ascorbic acid (VC, Sigma-Aldrich) and 7 μg/mL α-tocopherol (VE, Sigma-Aldrich), while the culture medium of the test groups was supplemented with MK-7. Following treatment, the plates were incubated (37 °C, 5% CO2) for 24 h. All experimental groups, with the exception of the BC, were subjected to 30 J/cm2 UVA over an exposure period of 25 min. After irradiation, all plates were placed back into the CO2 incubator (37 °C, 5% CO2) and cultured for an additional 24 h. ATP content was quantified using the Cell Meter Live Cell ATP Assay Kit (XiAn Biolite Biotech Co., Ltd., China). NAD+/NADH ratio was determined using the NAD+/NADH Assay Kit with WST-8 (Beyotime Biotechnology, China).

Mitochondrial Membrane Potential Assay

Keratinocytes in NC and test groups were treated with 10 μM CCCP (carbonyl cyanide m-chlorophenyl hydrazone) for 20 min to disrupt mitochondrial membrane potential. Then, test groups were incubated with MK-7 overnight. 100 μL JC-1 solution (5,5′, 6,6′-tetrachloro-1,1′, 3,3′-tetraethyl-imidacarbocyanine iodide, Sigma-Aldrich) at a concentration of 10 μM was added into each well to visualize the mitochondrial membrane potential. Fluorescence emitted by JC-1 was measured by an inverted fluorescence microscope using LWD300–38LFT (Shanghai Cewei Optoelectronic Technology Co., Ltd., China).

Determination of Mitochondrial DNA Copy Number

Mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) copy number was assessed in fibroblasts. Cells in NC, PC, and test groups were exposed to 400 μM H2O2 (Sigma-Aldrich) for 2 h to induce oxidative damage. Following stimulation, cells were washed with PBS. The cells were then treated for three consecutive days: the PC received culture medium with 300 nM quercetin, while the test groups received culture medium with MK-7. Total genomic DNA was extracted from harvested cells using BeyoMag Universal Genomic DNA Purification Mini Kit with Magnetic Beads (Beyotime Biotechnology, China). Human Mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) Monitoring Primer Set (Cat. #7246, TAKARA, Japan) targeting mitochondrial genes (ND1, ND5) and nuclear reference genes (SLCO2B1, SERPINA1) were employed to calculate relative mtDNA copy number. The D-loop region was amplified using the following primers: forward 5′-ACGTGTGGGCTATTTAGGCT-3′ and reverse 5′-ATCCCGCACAAGAGTGCTAC-3′. Detection was performed using TB Green Premix Ex TaqII (Tli RNase H Plus) (Cat. #RR820A, TAKARA, Japan) to monitor fluorescence intensity.

Measurement of Mitochondrial Respiratory Chain Complex Activities

The fibroblasts in test groups were incubated with MK-7 for 24 h (37 °C, 5% CO2). Mitochondrial respiratory chain complex (I–IV) activities were measured under basal (nonstressed) conditions. In preliminary experiments, we found that after severe oxidative stress, complex activities became erratic and inconsistent, precluding reliable assessment of MK-7 effects. Activities of mitochondrial respiratory chain complexes I–IV were measured using Cell Mitochondrial Complex I (NADH-CoQ Reductase) Activity Assay Kit, Cell Mitochondrial Complex II Activity Assay Kit, Cell Mitochondrial Complex III (Coenzyme Q-Cytochrome C Reductase) Activity Assay Kit, Cell Mitochondrial Complex IV (Cytochrome C Oxidase) Activity Assay Kit, and Mitochondrial Complex V­(F0F1-ATPase/ATP Synthase) Activity Assay Kit (Elabscience Biotechnology Co., Ltd., China).

Assessment of Senescence-Associated Secretory Factors

Fibroblasts in the test groups were treated with MK-7, while the PC received a combination of 100 μg/mL ascorbic acid and 7 μg/mL α-tocopherol. After 24 h of incubation, all groups except BC were irradiated with UVA (30 J/cm2 for 25 min). Following irradiation, cells were incubated for an additional 24 h before collection for subsequent assays.

The supernatant of each group was collected and centrifuged at 2000 rpm for 20 min. The levels of tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) and matrix metalloproteinase-3 (MMP-3) in the supernatant were quantified using Human TNF-α ELISA Kit 48T and Human MMP-3 ELISA KIT JW. HU4194 (Shanghai JIwei Biological Technology Co., Ltd., China), following the manufacturers’ instructions. Absorbance at 450 nm was measured using a microplate reader (SuPerMax 3100, Shanghai Flash Spectrum Biological Technology Co., Ltd., China).

Total RNA was extracted using an FastPure Cell/Tissue Total RNA Isolation Kit V2 (RC112, Vazyme, China) and reverse-transcribed into cDNA with HiScript III 1st Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (+gDNA wiper) (R312, Vazyme, China). Real-time PCR was performed using ChamQ Universal SYBR qPCR Master Mix (Q711, Vazyme, China) on a LightCycler 480 Instrument II (Roche, Switzerland), and fluorescence was measured. Expressions of IL-8 (NM_000584.4) and p21 (NM_001291549.1) were analyzed. GAPDH was used as an internal control gene.

Human Efficacy Evaluation Test Samples

To evaluate the efficacy of MK-7, a face-and-neck cream containing 0.05% MK-7 and a placebo face-and-neck cream without MK-7 were formulated, as shown in Table . The formulation was designed as an oil-in-water emulsion, a widely used and representative vehicle system in cosmetic products, offering a moderate skin permeability profile. To support dermal delivery of the active ingredient, penetration-facilitating excipients (hydrogenated lecithin and polyols) were incorporated into the formulation, , alongside tocopherol as an antioxidant and allantoin/bisabolol as soothing agents. Apart from the presence of MK-7, the face-and-neck cream and placebo cream were formulated identically so that any differences in clinical outcome could be attributed specifically to the active ingredient MK-7 rather than to the vehicle itself.

Ex Vivo Skin Permeation Study

The permeation of MK-7 from the 0.05% face-and-neck cream was evaluated using Franz diffusion cells with full-thickness porcine skin (Brazilian miniature pig, 1–2 months old, male). Detailed experimental conditions and individual sample data are provided in Table S3.

Subjects

A total of 36 female subjects (aged 35–60 years) with required wrinkle levels were recruited, with 30 completing the study after informed consent (conducted by Beijing Sino-German Union Cosmetic Institute Co., Ltd.). Six subjects discontinued the study prematurely for personal reasons unrelated to the treatment (e.g., work relocation, family illness, and personal schedule conflicts), and no dropout was related to adverse events or product intolerance. The ethical conduct of this study was ensured by obtaining approval from the Ethics Committee of the Cosmetics Tech Center, Chinese Academy of Inspection and Quality (Approval Number: S2025-09-003). This study was conducted in accordance with the Helsinki declaration.

Human Efficacy Evaluation Design

This double-blind, split-face/neck study was conducted over 8 weeks after a one week washout period using basic moisturizing skincare products only. During the 8 week-treatment period, subjects were randomly assigned to apply 0.05% MK-7 face-and-neck cream to one designated side of the face and neck at the same time, twice daily. Meanwhile, placebo cream without MK-7 was applied to the other side. Throughout the study, subjects maintained their routine skincare regimen. Skin condition assessments were conducted at baseline (T0), week 2 (T2w), week 4 (T4w), and week 8 (T8w). Wrinkle severity (Ra and Rz parameters) was evaluated using the AEVA-HE (EOTECH, France) with a field of view (FOV) of 135 × 110 × 80 mm. The analysis region for neck wrinkles was 40 × 25 mm, and for marionette lines was 10 × 10 mm. Skin elasticity (R0, R1, and F4 parameters) was measured using the Cutometer Dual MPA 580 (Courage + Khazaka Electronic GmbH, Germany) on both cheek areas. Facial sagging was assessed via VISIA-CR (Canfield Scientific, Inc., USA), and the acquired images were analyzed using Image-Pro Plus software. To ensure measurement reliability and reproducibility across the three instrument platforms, all measurements for each parameter were performed by the same trained operator before and after treatment to eliminate interoperator variability, and all instruments were calibrated according to the manufacturers’ instructions prior to each testing session. Testing was conducted following internal standardized operating procedures (SOPs). For the AEVA-HE and VISIA-CR systems, the accompanying software automatically corrected for positional differences between baseline and follow-up images prior to quantitative analysis, while Cutometer parameters were measured in triplicate at each time point, and the mean value was used for subsequent analysis. To enhance transparency and provide a complete research record, this study was retrospectively registered on ClinicalTrials.gov under identifier NCT07403942.

Data Analysis

All in vitro experiments were performed with three independent biological replicates. Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) for in vitro assays. For the human efficacy study, data are presented as mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM). All statistical tests were calculated by STATISTICA 7 software. All tests were two-sided. A paired t-test was used to identify specific intergroup variations. To control for multiple comparisons across time points and parameters, the Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) procedure was applied. All reported p-values are FDR-adjusted. An adjusted p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Raw and adjusted p-values for all comparisons are provided in Table S2.

Baseline comparability between the active (0.05% MK-7) and placebo sides was assessed using paired t tests for all efficacy parameters (Ra, Rz, R0, R1, F4, MPA). No significant differences were observed for any parameter (all p values > 0.05; Table S1), confirming the validity of the within-subject paired comparison design.

Analyses of the human efficacy study were conducted on a per-protocol (PP) basis, including only the 30 subjects who completed the study. Intention-to-treat (ITT) analysis was not performed because all dropouts (n = 6) were due to personal reasons unrelated to the treatment.

Results

MK-7 Activates ATP Synthesis

ATP serves as the primary direct energy source for nearly all cellular physiological processes, including protein synthesis and cellular proliferation. , Given that mitochondria are the main sites of ATP production, we first examined whether MK-7 could enhance mitochondrial ATP synthesis in fibroblasts by measuring fluorescence intensity.

As shown in Figure A,B, UV exposure significantly reduced ATP levels in the NC group by 56% (p < 0.01) compared to the BC group. In contrast, MK-7 treatment markedly attenuated this decline, with dose-dependent increases in ATP content observed at higher concentrations. Notably, 0.002% MK-7 increased ATP levels by 59.09% (p < 0.01) relative to the NC.

MK-7 increases mitochondrial energy metabolism through OXPHOS. (A,B) ATP production: MK-7 dose-dependently increases intracellular ATP levels. (C) ETC efficiency: MK-7 upregulates the NAD+/NADH ratio. (D) ΔΨm : MK-7 improves mitochondrial membrane potential. Error bars are mean ± SD of 3 biological replicates (##p < 0.01 vs BC; *p < 0.05 vs NC; **p < 0.01 vs NC).

For comparison, a combination of VC and VE was used as a positive control. While the PC group showed higher ATP levels than the NC (p < 0.01), its effect remained weaker than that of 0.002% MK-7.

These findings demonstrated that MK-7 effectively promotes ATP generation in fibroblasts, highlighting its potential to support cellular energy metabolism under oxidative stress.

MK-7 Increases NAD+/NADH Ratio

To elucidate the mechanism by which MK-7 enhances ATP production, we investigated key biomarkers of cellular respiration. Based on the hypothesis that MK-7 may function as an electron carrier in OXPHOS, it should shift the NAD+/NADH balance, a critical indicator of mitochondrial OXPHOS activity. As anticipated, 0.002% MK-7 treatment significantly elevated the NAD+/NADH ratio by 100.69% (p < 0.01) compared to the NC group (Figure C). This robust increase suggests that MK-7 enhances OXPHOS efficiency, thereby supplying ample coenzymes (NAD+) for both glycolysis and the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle. Consequently, the optimized coenzyme availability facilitates enhanced ATP generation through improved mitochondrial energy metabolism.

MK-7 Prevents Mitochondrial Membrane Potential Collapse

Mitochondrial membrane potential ( ΔΨm ) maintenance relies on intact ETC function. Impaired ETC activity disrupts proton gradient establishment, leading to ΔΨm depolarization. Considering that MK-7 may enhance ETC efficiency through electron carrier-like activity, we predicted a corresponding increase in ΔΨm . HaCaT keratinocytes were selected for this assay to complement the fibroblast-based experiments with data from the epidermal compartment. Existing literature suggests that keratinocytes exhibit more pronounced changes in mitochondrial membrane potential upon exposure to exogenous substances compared with other skin-derived cell types, whether reflected as a more pronounced decline under oxidative or dendrimer-induced stress or a larger increase following treatment with a protective peptide formulation. −

To quantitatively assess ΔΨm dynamics, we employed the JC-1 fluorescent probe (a well-established ΔΨm indicator). In untreated control mitochondria, the proton gradient drives JC-1 aggregation in the mitochondrial matrix, generating red fluorescent J-aggregates (high ΔΨm ). Conversely, CCCP-induced ETC inhibition dissipates the proton gradient, causing JC-1 to disperse as green-fluorescent monomers (low ΔΨm ).

Our results demonstrated that 0.002% MK-7 treatment significantly reduced green fluorescence intensity by 18.66% (p < 0.05) compared to the CCCP-treated control (Figure D). This indicated that MK-7 effectively restored mitochondrial membrane potential, likely by enhancing ETC-mediated proton pumping. Because MK-7 is proposed to act as a mitochondrial electron carrier, a mechanism not expected to be cell-type specific, the consistency of this result with the fibroblast-based findings reinforces the interpretation that MK-7 preserves mitochondrial function across cell types.

MK-7 Raises mtDNA Copy Number

Reduced efficiency of the ETC exacerbates cellular oxidative stress and contributes to mtDNA damage. Therefore, we used H2O2 to simulate cellular oxidative stress and assessed the impact of MK-7 on mtDNA by measuring the relative copy numbers of the D-loop, ND1, and ND5 regions. First, analysis indicated that after adding 0.0015% MK-7, the D-loop copy number increased by up to 46.68% compared to the NC group, and this increase was also higher than that of the PC group (27.99%) (Figure A). As the D-loop controls mtDNA replication and transcription, MK-7 can further influence the copy numbers of other mtDNA genes and overall mitochondrial function by increasing the D-loop copy number.

MK-7 induces increases in relative mtDNA copy number. (A) The increase of D-loop relative copy number after MK-7 treatment. (B) The increase of ND1 relative copy number after MK-7 treatment. (C) The increase of ND5 relative copy number after MK-7 treatment. Error bars are mean ± SD of 3 biological replicates (##p < 0.01 vs BC; *p < 0.05 vs NC; **p < 0.01 vs NC).

Second, 0.0015% MK-7 also resulted in the greatest restoration of ND1 and ND5 copy numbers, with increases of 83.66% and 65.28%, respectively, compared to the NC (Figure B,C). Both ND1 and ND5 encode subunits of mitochondrial respiratory chain complex I. This suggested that MK-7 may also influence the expression and activity of mitochondrial respiratory chain complexes by affecting the mtDNA copy numbers. We also noted that the optimal concentration for restoring mtDNA copy number was not 0.002% but 0.0015%. The underlying reason is probably that, under severe oxidative conditions (H2O2), cells need to strike a balance between mtDNA repair and avoiding the potential increase in oxidative byproducts that could result from overstimulating electron transport.

MK-7 Boosts Mitochondrial Respiratory Chain Complex Activities

On the one hand, disrupted electron transport and reduced NAD+ in mitochondria can reduce mtDNA copy number, thereby compromising the activity of mitochondrial respiratory chain complexes. On the other hand, impaired activity of mitochondrial respiratory chain complexes (I–IV) is directly linked to reduced ETC efficiency. To mechanistically demonstrate that MK-7 facilitates electron flux through the ETC, we quantitatively assessed the enzymatic activities of complexes I–IV.

Our results revealed a dose-dependent increase in the activities of complexes I, II, and IVkey components of the electron transport pathway (Figure A,B,D). Meanwhile, MK-7 treatment did not alter complex III activity (Figure C). One possible speculation is that MK-7 might facilitate electron transfer that bypasses this complex. Notably, treatment with 0.002% MK-7 significantly elevated activities of complexes I, II, and IV to 117.48% (p < 0.05), 142.86% (p < 0.01), and 118.03% (p < 0.05) of the BC group, respectively. This enhancement was accompanied by a concomitant 26.78% increase (p < 0.05) in complex V activity compared to the BC (Figure E). This increase is likely driven by the elevated mitochondrial membrane potential resulting from improved upstream electron flow. These findings suggest that MK-7-induced enhancement in ATP production is likely mediated through the improved functional performance of mitochondrial respiratory chain complexes, thereby optimizing overall ETC efficiency.

(D) Effects of MK-7 on complex IV. (E) Effects of MK-7 on complex V. Error bars are mean ± SD of 3 biological replicates (*p < 0.05 vs BC; **p < 0.01 vs BC).

Importantly, mitochondrial respiratory chain complex activities were measured under basal conditions rather than during stress, due to technical limitations, as severe oxidative stress produced erratic activity. Despite this, basal measurements provide mechanistic support for MK-7’s role as an electron carrier.

MK-7 Downregulates Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotypes

Overall mitochondrial dysfunction contributes to cellular senescence, which in turn promotes the secretion of the senescence-associated secretory phenotypes (SASP). We investigated whether MK-7 could delay cellular senescence by examining its regulatory effect on SASP markers, including IL-8, p21, TNF-α, and MMP-3. Following MK-7 treatment, a significant reduction in SASP marker expression was observed (Figure ). All measured SASP markers exhibited their greatest reductions at the 0.002% concentration compared with the NC group, with IL-8 decreased by 75.85%, p21 by 33.58%, TNF-α by 18.69%, and MMP-3 by 16.41%. These results suggested that MK-7 can attenuate inflammatory signaling (IL-8 and TNF-α), cell cycle arrest (p21), and extracellular matrix degradation (MMP-3) potentially through modulation of mitochondrial energy metabolism, thereby mitigating cellular senescence.

Ex Vivo Skin Permeation of MK-7

To assess whether the topically applied 0.05% MK-7 cream could deliver the compound to the target tissue at concentrations relevant to the observed in vitro effects, we performed a Franz cell permeation study using full-thickness porcine skin. Based on an approximate skin tissue volume (diffusion area 3.14 cm2; skin thickness ∼0.9 mm), the estimated intradermal MK-7 concentration from a single application ranged from approximately 0.78 to 5.24 μg/mL (Supporting Information, Table S3). This range overlapped with the lower end of the concentrations that produced mitochondrial bioenergetic effects in cultured cells (5–20 μg/mL, 0.0005%–0.002%). Given the cumulative dosing regimen in the in vivo study and uneven skin distribution of MK-7, the steady-state concentration in certain local skin microenvironments is expected to be substantially higher, providing a pharmacokinetic rationale for the observed skin improvements. No MK-7 was detected in the receptor fluid, indicating that the compound was primarily retained within the skin layers rather than systemically absorbed.

MK-7 Reduces Wrinkles

To evaluate the skin anti-aging effects of MK-7 in vivo, we conducted a double-blinded, placebo-controlled human efficacy test. Wrinkle severity was quantitatively assessed using maximum height roughness (Rz) and average roughness (Ra) parameters (Figure A,B).

(C) Representative images of subject #32 showing the improvement of wrinkles (marionette lines area) after 8 weeks of treatment with 0.05% MK-7. Photograph courtesy of “Zhu Jianyu”. Copyright 2026. Error bars are mean ± SEM (n = 30) (*p < 0.05 vs placebo face-and-neck cream; **p < 0.01 vs placebo face-and-neck cream).

On the face (marionette lines area), the 0.05% MK-7 treatment significantly reduced Rz at week 4 (−8.60%, p < 0.01) and week 8 (−4.95%, p < 0.05) compared to the placebo group. Another wrinkle parameter Ra significantly decreased at T2w (−4.95%, p < 0.05) and T4w (−7.01%, p < 0.01); however, significance was lost at T8w (−3.93%, p = 0.088) after correction. A similar trend was observed for neck wrinkles. After 0.05% MK-7 treatment, Rz and Ra of the treated side were significantly lower than that of placebo side at T4w (Rz −4.26% at T4w, p < 0.01; Ra −4.26% at T4w, p < 0.01). By T8w, however, reductions in neck Rz (−2.80%, p = 0.085) and Ra (−2.80%, p = 0.083) were no longer statistically significant following adjustment.

The loss of statistical significance for certain wrinkle parameters (face Ra, neck Rz, and neck Ra) at T8w indicated a regression in efficacy during the latter phase of the study. This variability may be attributed to the environmental and behavioral confounding factors during the Spring Festival holiday, which occurred between T4w and T8w evaluations. During this period, subjects experienced colder seasonal temperatures alongside potential lifestyle disruptions, including sleep deprivation, reduced physical activity, increased consumption of high-fat foods, and irregular daily routines.

MK-7 Enhances Skin Elasticity

To objectively assess the effects of MK-7 on skin elasticity, we quantitatively analyzed three key biomechanical parameters: R0 (immediate distensibility), R1 (residual deformation), and F4 (fatigue resistance). R0 reflects the skin’s immediate stretchability and correlates with the structural integrity of the collagen and elastin matrix. R1 quantifies the skin’s inability to fully return to its original position after stretching. F4 represents the total hysteresis area (work of extension and retraction) across 10 deformation cycles, serving as a marker of cumulative fatigue resistance. Compared to T0, all three parameters decreased significantly (p < 0.05), indicating enhanced skin elasticity and firmness. When compared to the placebo side, 0.05% MK-7-treated side showed significantly lower R0 (−11.38% at T8w, p < 0.01, Figure A), R1 (−9.91% at T4w; −13.52% at T8w, p < 0.01, Figure B), and F4 (−6.48% at T4w; −10.70% at T8w, p < 0.01, Figure C). These results suggest that MK-7 strengthens skin elasticity and firmness.

MK-7 Alleviates Facial Sagging

Facial sagging is a prominent characteristic of an aged appearance and is closely associated with the descent of the mandibular outline. To objectively quantify the degree of facial sagging, we measured the mandibular plane angle (MPA) in the subjects (Figure ). Compared to T0, the MPA value at T8w had increased significantly by 15.55% (p < 0.01). Compared to the placebo group, 0.05% MK-7 significantly increased the MPA value by 11.45% (p < 0.01) at T8w, suggesting that 0.05% MK-7 has an effective counteracting effect on facial sagging.

Discussion

The findings of this study provide complementary cellular and human efficacy data suggesting MK-7 may function as a mitochondrial bioenergetic activator relevant to skin aging. Across complementary cellular assays, MK-7 improved ATP production, elevated NAD+/NADH ratio, stabilized ΔΨ m , protected mtDNA copy number under oxidative challenge, enhanced respiratory chain enzyme activities, and reduced UVA-induced SASP marker expression. Importantly, these mechanistic effects were mirrored by objective improvements in wrinkles, elasticity, and sagging in a randomized split-face/neck human efficacy study, providing meaningful support for a mitochondria-targeting anti-aging strategy.

Mitochondrial dysfunction has been positioned as a key hallmark and accelerator of aging. , In skin, mitochondria contribute to ATP supply, redox regulation, and stress responses, and their impairment promotes senescence-associated phenotypes and extracellular matrix degradation. , Age-associated loss of mitochondrial functionincluding reduced ETC efficiency and increased ROS productiondrives mtDNA damage and OXPHOS impairment, reinforcing the energy deficit that accelerates tissue degeneration. , Mitochondrial dynamics and quality surveillance are also essential: disrupted fission/fusion balance, impaired mitophagy, and reduced biogenesis promote fragmented mitochondrial networks and inflammatory signaling that further propagate aging phenotypes. , These processes align with evidence that oxidative stress and NAD+ depletion increase with age and correlate with impaired respiration and ATP levels in human tissue. The skin relevance of mitochondrial restoration is further supported by models in which mtDNA depletion causes wrinkled skin and hair loss reversible by mitochondrial recovery.

Against this background, MK-7 offers a mechanistically distinct cosmetic approach. Vitamin K2 biology has expanded beyond classical vitamin K-dependent protein pathways to include mitochondrial interactions. Vitamin K2 has been shown to protect against neurotoxicity in vivo while activating autophagy and improving mitochondrial function. A pivotal concept is that vitamin K2 may act as a mitochondrial electron carrier, functionally analogous to coenzyme-like mediators within the ETC. Our data supported this mechanistic framing in a skin-relevant context: MK-7 increased ATP production, NAD+/NADH ratio, ΔΨm , complex activities (I, II, IV, and V), and mtDNA copy numbers, accompanied by broad decreases in SASP. These results collectively support a mechanism centered on regulation of electron transport and oxidative phosphorylation. The significant elevation in NAD+/NADH ratio is particularly important because NAD redox balance is a functional integrator of mitochondrial performance and metabolic resilience. Demonstration that a topically applied ingredient can improve NAD+/NADH ratio under UVA stress has not been previously reported in a topical cosmetic context.

In previous research, vitamin K2 has been reported to protect mitochondrial function by upregulating NDUFS3, activating SIRT1 signaling, and promoting JNK-1/SIR-2.1/DAF-16 pathway in other animal models or tissues. Together with previous reports, vitamin K2 appears to act on mitochondrial bioenergetic networks rather than on a single downstream signaling pathway. The anti-aging relevance of vitamin K2 is also supported by evidence from other age-related disorders. Beyond its established roles in osteoporosis and cardiovascular disease through vitamin K-dependent proteins, vitamin K2 is also considered to potentially alleviate other aging-related conditions through modulation of mitochondrial health. In particular, vitamin K2 has been reported to mitigate sarcopenia by enhancing skeletal muscle mitochondrial function, and to show promise for improving cognitive impairment in Alzheimer’s disease by addressing mitochondrial dysfunction. Combined with these mechanistic linkages, our findings further strengthen the biological plausibility of MK-7’s anti-aging efficacy in skin.

The split-face design strengthens causality by controlling for interindividual variability and vehicle effects. Using objective devices widely accepted in dermatologic research (AEVA-HE, Cutometer, VISIA-CR), MK-7 demonstrated statistically significant improvements in wrinkle roughness, elasticity parameters, and mandibular plane angle. While absolute percentage changes are expected to be modest over 8 weeks, the consistency across multiple end points supports a real functional effect rather than a single-parameter fluctuation. This contributes new information for both cosmetic industry R&D and dermatologists: topical MK-7 has not been widely documented with objective human anti-aging outcomes, and the present data provide early human evidence support alongside mechanistic evidence.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. The first limitation of this study is the absence of an ITT analysis. However, because all subject dropouts were strictly due to non-treatment personal reasons, PP analysis applied here provides an assessment of the potential efficacy of MK-7. Additionally, the distributed nature (different stressors, cell types, and positive controls) of the experimental design limits the ability to draw a single mechanistic conclusion from any one experiment within a unified model. Nevertheless, the results remain internally consistent and provide convergent evidence that MK-7 acts as a mitochondrial bioenergetic activator. Furthermore, the cohort included only women aged 35–60 years and treatment duration was 8 weeks, which included the Chinese New Year holiday; longer studies in broader populations without seasonal disruption are needed. Molecular biomarkers were not measured directly in vivo, so future work should connect human efficacy to noninvasive bioenergetic biomarkers and barrier/inflammation markers. Seasonal and lifestyle variability may also influence skin end points and should be controlled in larger trials.

In summary, by integrating multilayer mitochondrial bioenergetic readouts with objective human efficacy evaluation, this study supports MK-7 as an energy-activating mitochondrial modulator with measurable anti-aging benefits. This mechanism-anchored approach aligns with contemporary dermatologic interest in upstream drivers of aging biology and provides a scientifically grounded foundation for further translational research and human efficacy studies.

Conclusions

MK-7 improved mitochondrial function in vitro and demonstrated significant anti-aging efficacy in a randomized double-blind split-face/neck human study. Together, these findings supported MK-7 as a bioenergetic cosmetic ingredient and provided a mechanism-anchored rationale for its further development in skin anti-aging.

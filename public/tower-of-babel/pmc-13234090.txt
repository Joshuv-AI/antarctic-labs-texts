A cut-off value for endothelial dysfunction based on the triglyceride-glucose index
Authors: Takayuki Yamaji, Farina Mohamad Yusoff, Shinji Kishimoto, Masato Kajikawa, Takahiro Harada, Aya Mizobuchi, Shunsuke Tanigawa, Tatsuya Maruhashi, Yukiko Nakano, Yukihito Higashi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractWe defined triglyceride-glucose (TyG) index < 8.52 as a low TyG index and TyG index ≥ 8.52 as a high TyG index, and divided the subjects into nine groups based on the levels of TyG index and fasting blood glucose (FBG) levels: FBG levels < 90 mg/dL and low TyG index, FBG levels < 90 mg/dL and high TyG index, FBG levels of 90–94 mg/dL and low TyG index, FBG levels of 90–94 mg/dL and high TyG index, FBG levels of 95–99 mg/dL and low TyG index, FBG levels of 95–99 mg/dL and high TyG index, FBG levels of 100–125 mg/dL and low TyG index, FBG levels of 100–125 mg/dL and high TyG index, and diabetes mellitus. The odds ratios for endothelial dysfunction were significantly higher in groups with high TyG index and any levels of FBG, and the group with low TyG index and FBG levels of 95–99 mg/dL, the group with low TyG index and FBG levels of 100–125 mg/dL and the diabetes mellitus group than the FBG levels < 90 mg/dL and low TyG index group. Introduction

Endothelial cells secrete several kinds of vasodilators, such as nitric oxide, and provide anticoagulant and maintain vascular tone1,2. Endothelial dysfunction is a pathological state in which vascular tone regulation is impaired, leading to loss of vascular homeostasis. When endothelial dysfunction occurs, nitric bioavailability decreases, oxidative stress and inflammation increase, and a pro-thrombotic environment increases, contributing to the development of atherosclerosis and playing an important role in further cardiovascular events3–6. Therefore, assessing endothelial function is clinically important. Measurement of flow-mediated vasodilation (FMD) in the brachial artery is a noninvasive tool for assessing endothelial function7. Endothelial function assessed by FMD is impaired by the coronary risk factors such as dyslipidemia, hypertension, diabetes mellitus, smoking, and physical inactivity are established risk factors for endothelial dysfunction3,6,8,9.

Insulin resistance (IR) is one of the main mechanisms of the onset of type 2 diabetes mellitus (DM) and is a risk factor for atherosclerosis10–12. The glucose clamp method is the most accurate method for quantifying IR13. However, the glucose clamp method takes much time and effort. In a clinical setting, homeostatic model assessment of IR (HOMA-IR) is the most popular surrogate marker for IR14. However, routine measurement of blood insulin level is also limited due to clinical indications. Recently, triglyceride-glucose (TyG) index has been proposed as an index of IR15. The TyG index is calculated as ln[fasting blood glucose (FBG) (mg/dL) × fasting triglycerides (mg/dL)/2] and it has been shown to be significantly correlated with IR measured by the glucose clamp method and HOMA-IR15,16. However, due to insurance limitations, routine measurement of insulin levels in individuals without DM or individuals who are not hospitalized is difficult in Japan. Therefore, there is still uncertainly regarding the relationship between the TyG index and HOMA-IR in Japanese. The TyG index has been reported to be a useful surrogate marker for further cardiovascular events17, metabolic syndrome18 and type 2 DM19. A previous study showed that the TyG index was an independent predictor for FMD20. We agree with the results of that study because we previously showed that both the levels of FBG and triglycerides were independent predictors of endothelial dysfunction9,21. However, a previous study focusing on the relationship between TyG index and FMD had a small sample size. Therefore, there is still uncertainly about what levels of the TyG index are associated with endothelial dysfunction.

In the present study, we investigated the relationships between HOMA-IR and TyG index in subjects who were divided into four groups according to the TyG index. We examined the threshold levels of the TyG index at which FMD values begin to be affected by endothelial dysfunction. Furthermore, we investigated whether the combination of FBG and the TyG index enables the stratification of risk for endothelial dysfunction.

Relationship between TyG index and HOMA-IR

The baseline characteristics of the 99 subjects in Study 1 are summarized in Supplementary Table S1. The mean age of the subjects was 51 ± 10 years. The 99 subjects included 48 men (49.0%). We next evaluated the incremental predictive value of the TyG index and constructed two logistic regression models for endothelial dysfunction. Model 1 was adjusted for high age, sex, obesity, hypertension and smoking, and Model 2 additionally included the TyG index. The area under the curve (AUC) values of the two models were compared using the DeLong test. The AUC of Model 2 was significantly larger than that of Model 1, indicating that the TyG index provided a statistically significant improvement in the incremental value (AUC 0.63 vs. 0.65, p < 0.001) (Fig. S2).

FMD values in four groups of TyG index levels

The baseline characteristics stratified by TyG index quartiles are presented in Table 1. There were significant differences in age, sex, BMI, heart rate, systolic blood pressure, diastolic blood pressure, levels of total cholesterol, triglycerides, HDL-C, LDL-C, creatinine, uric acid, FBG, and HbA1c, prevalence of hypertension, dyslipidemia, DM and cardiovascular disease, and use of antihypertensive drugs among the four groups. Figure 1 shows the FMD values in the four groups. The FMD values were 7.1 ± 3.3% in the TyG index < 8.12 group, 6.7 ± 3.0% in the TyG index 8.12–8.51 group, 6.3 ± 2.9% in the TyG index 8.52–8.91 group, and 5.8 ± 2.9% in the TyG index ≥ 8.92 group (p < 0.001). FMD values in the TyG index 8.12–8.51 group, TyG index 8.52–8.91 group and the TyG index ≥ 8.92 groups were significantly smaller than those in the TyG index < 8.12 group (p < 0.001). The division points for the lowest quartile and second quartile were FMD of 4.4%. Therefore, we defined endothelial dysfunction as FMD of < 4.4%. We took the TyG index < 8.12 group as a reference for deriving endothelial dysfunction in the other groups. After adjustments for age ≥ 65 years, gender, presence of hypertension, presence of DM, presence of dyslipidemia, presence of obesity, and current smoking, the odds ratios for endothelial dysfunction were significantly higher in the TyG index 8.52–8.91 group (Odd ratio (OR): 136, 95% confidence intervals (CI) 1.13–1.65) and TyG index ≥ 8.92 group (OR 1.59, 95% CI 1.27–2.00) than in the reference group (Table S4). For every 1-SD (0.59) increase in TyG index, the odds ratio for endothelial dysfunction was 22% higher even after adjustment for confounding factors (Table S4). A cubic spline curve showed a marked increase in the odds ratio for endothelial dysfunction with increasing TyG index. A clear inflection point was observed at a TyG index of 8.52, beyond which the odds ratio for endothelial dysfunction increased more steeply, indicating that a TyG index ≥ 8.52 was associated with a significantly higher odds ratio for endothelial dysfunction (Fig. 2).

Discussion

In the present study, we demonstrated that the TyG index was significantly associated with IR and endothelial function in Japanese, that both FBG ≥ 95 mg/dL and TyG index ≥ 8.52 were independently associated with endothelial dysfunction, and that the combination of FBG and TyG index enables stratification of the risk for endothelial dysfunction. The strengths of this study are that the study that the TyG index, a simple indicator calculated using FBG and triglycerides, reflects IR and that a cut-off value of the TyG index for endothelial dysfunction was determined.

The results of this study support the results of a previous study showing that TyG index is a useful marker for IR, atherosclerosis and cardiovascular events16. Recently, several investigators have shown the usefulness of the TyG index. However, the cut-off value for the TyG index is still controversial. Kim et al. showed that the highest quartile of TyG index group (9.15 ± 0.27) had a significantly higher odds ratio for prevalence of coronary artery calcification than the odds ratio in the lowest quartile of TyG index group (7.75 ± 0.21)16. Huang et al. showed that TyG index ≥ 9.32 was a risk for all-cause death, cardiovascular death, and cardiovascular events22,23. Cai et al. showed that TyG index ≥ 9.83 was a risk for all-cause death and cardiovascular death in subjects at high risk for cardiovascular disease23. However, there has been no study focusing on the cut-off value of TyG index for defining endothelial dysfunction. Our proposed cut-off value, TyG index ≥ 8.52, for endothelial dysfunction is approximately 1.0 to 1.3-times lower than those cut off values for cardiovascular events. Endothelial dysfunction may occur from lower levels of TyG index.

Next, we investigated whether the combination of FBG and TyG index enables stratification of the risk of endothelial dysfunction. In our previous study, we investigated relationship of FBG with endothelial function using measurement of FMD in the brachial artery and we showed that an FBG level of 95–99 mg/dL was an independent predictor for endothelial dysfunction9. However, our previous study, which focused on FMD and FBG, was unable to distinguish whether the decrease in FMD was influenced by high blood glucose levels or by IR9. In the present study, we showed that even at low FBG levels, subjects with a high TyG index might have a higher risk for endothelial dysfunction, and also even at a low TyG index, subjects with FBG levels ≥ 95 mg/dL might have a higher risk for endothelial dysfunction. From the aspect of endothelial function, it was necessary to pay attention to both IR and blood glucose.

A possible mechanism by which IR impairs endothelial function has been postulated. Insulin per se exhibits seemingly contradictory effects on blood vessels, acting as both a vasodilator and a vasoconstrictor. One insulin-related pathway promotes vasodilation by an increase in nitric oxide (NO) production through activation of the phosphoinositide 3-kinase (PI3-K)/Akt pathway under the condition of normal glucose levels or non-IR. On the other hand, under the condition of IR, insulin inactivates the PI3-K/Akt pathway, decreases NO production and increases the production of endothelin-1 and reactive oxygen species by the mitogen-activated protein kinase/extracellular signal-regulating kinase pathway, resulting in endothelial dysfunction24. In a high glucose state, glucose transporter 1 in endothelial cells is constantly active, making endothelial dysfunction more likely to occur due to high blood glucose levels, and endothelial dysfunction might occur even at lower glucose levels than those in impaired fasting glucose and DM. In hyperglycemic conditions, glucose transporter 1 in endothelial cells remains constantly active, leading to an increased intracellular glucose concentration. This elevated glucose uptake can result in endothelial dysfunction, even at blood glucose levels lower than those typically associated with impaired fasting glucose or DM. Consequently, endothelial dysfunction may occur at glucose concentrations below the standard diagnostic thresholds for these conditions25. These findings suggest both IR and high blood glucose are associated with endothelial dysfunction. Abnormal glucose metabolism and endothelial dysfunction can create a vicious cycle, where each condition exacerbates the other. Impaired glucose metabolism leads to endothelial dysfunction, which in turn worsens glucose metabolism, contributing to the progression of cardiovascular disease.

This study has several limitations. First, although this study was conducted in multiple centers, this study was a cross-sectional study. We showed an association between TyG index and FMD, but we did not establish a causal relationship between the TyG index and FMD. Second, the subjects of this study were Japanese. Insulin secretion capacity in Asians has been shown to be lower than that in other races26. Therefore, assessing other races would enable more specific conclusions regarding the role of TyG index in endothelial function to be drawn. Third, in Japanese insurance regulations, measurement of serum insulin levels is restricted in individuals without DM or non-hospitalized individuals. Therefore, we selected primary aldosteronism because its diagnosis requires inpatient evaluation. In addition, primary aldosteronism is considered to have relatively modest effects on glucose metabolism. However, the number of subjects in whom HOMA-IR was assessed was insufficient to draw a conclusion. Furthermore, we cannot deny the possibility of selection bias. Fourth, we cannot deny the possibility that the TyG index does not strictly reflect IR. However, in the present study, we showed the relationship between TyG index and HOMA-IR, which is clinically used as an IR marker even in a small sample size. Furthermore, we showed that the TyG index, simply calculated from FBG and triglycerides, is associated with endothelial function, and we proposed a cut-off value for detecting endothelial dysfunction based on the TyG index. Fifth, we excluded patients receiving antidiabetic and lipid-lowering drug treatment to minimize the potential effects of these drugs on the TyG index. Furthermore, the participants were predominantly male and older. Therefore, the generalizability of our findings may be limited due to the predominance of older male participants and the exclusion of individuals receiving medical treatment. Sixth, the cut-off value for the TyG index identified in this study is applicable to the Japanese population, specifically for the outcome of FMD < 4.4%.

Conclusions

In conclusion, TyG index ≥ 8.52 is associated with endothelial dysfunction. Even at low FBG levels, individuals with a high TyG index might have a higher risk for endothelial dysfunction, and also even at a low TyG index, individuals with FBG levels ≥ 95 mg/dL might have a higher risk for endothelial dysfunction. The combination of FBG and TyG index enables stratification of the risk for endothelial dysfunction.

Methods

Study subjects

Study 1

Study 1 was a single center retrospective cross-sectional study that was carried out to investigate the association between TyG index and HOMA-IR. We enrolled 550 patients whose serum insulin levels were measured between November 2016 and July 2024. Subjects who did not have secondary hypertension (n = 379) and those receiving lipid-lowering drugs (n = 62) or anti-diabetic drugs (n = 10) were excluded from this study. Finally, 99 participants were enrolled in Study 1 (Fig. S1A).

Study 2

The Flow-mediated Dilation Japan (FMD-J) Registry was a prospective multicenter registry that was established between April 2010 and August 2018 at 31 institutes in Japan with the aim of determining the usefulness of FMD measurement. All of the subjects had an obligation to undergo health screening every year under the regulations of the society-managed health insurance union in Japan. The design of the FMD-J study has been described in detail27. Briefly, the FMD-J study included three study arms: A, B, and C. Patients diagnosed with coronary artery disease were enrolled in Study A; patients with controlled hypertension or diabetes mellitus who had been receiving antihypertensive or antidiabetic drug treatment for at least 6 months were enrolled in Study B; and subjects who underwent annual health checkups were enrolled in Study C. Subjects with missing triglyceride data (n = 19), missing information on lipid-lowering drugs (n = 6), inadequate FMD recordings (n = 3), those taking lipid-lowering drug therapy (n = 1173), and those receiving anti-diabetic drug therapy (n = 223) were excluded from this study. Finally, we enrolled 5961 subjects in Study 2 (Fig. S1B). Hypertension was defined according to the Japanese Society of Hypertension Guidelines for the Management of Hypertension (JSH 2019) as the use of antihypertensive drugs or systolic blood pressure of more than 140 mm Hg or diastolic blood pressure of more than 90 mm Hg measured in a sitting position on at least 3 occasions28. Dyslipidemia was defined according to the third report of the National Cholesterol Education Program29. DM was defined according to the American Diabetes Association criteria, including FBG ≥ 126 mg/dL, HbA1c ≥ 6.5%, or the use of antidiabetic drugs30. Clinical definitions of cardiovascular disease, coronary heart disease, and cerebrovascular disease were based on those used in the FMD-J study27. Cardiovascular disease was defined as coronary heart disease and cerebrovascular disease. Coronary heart disease included angina pectoris, prior myocardial infarction, and unstable angina. Cerebrovascular disease included ischemic stroke, hemorrhagic stroke, and transient ischemic attack27. All methods followed the Declaration of Helsinki and relevant guidelines and regulations. Written informed consent for participation in the study was obtained from all subjects. The Ethics Committee of Hiroshima University approved the study protocol. (E-1902 and E-2002) The protocol was registered in the University Hospital Medical Information Network Clinical Trials Registry (UMIN000012950: first registration data 01/24/2014, UMIN000012951: first registration data 01/24/2014, UMIN000012952: first registration data 01/24/2014 and UMIN000003409: first registration data 03/30/2010).

Study protocol

The protocol used for measurement of FMD was the same in both studies. The subjects fasted overnight and abstained from alcohol, smoking, caffeine and antioxidant vitamins for at least 12 h before the study. The participants were kept in the supine position in a quiet, dark, air-conditioned room (constant temperature of 22 °C to 25 °C) throughout the study. A 23-gauge polyethylene catheter was inserted into the deep antecubital vein to obtain blood samples for measurements including measurement of FBG. After maintaining the supine position for 30 min, FMD was measured. The observers were blind to the form of examination.

Study 1. Relationship between the TyG index and HOMA-IR

In Study 1, we investigated the relationship between TyG index and HOMA-IR. The TyG index was calculated by the following equation: Ln [FBG (mg/dL) × fasting triglycerides (mg/dL)/2]15. HOMA-IR was calculated by the following equation: FBG (mg/dL) × fasting insulin (μU/mL)/40514.

Study 2. Relationship between TyG index and variables

We investigated the relationship between TyG index and endothelial function assessed by measurement of FMD. We divided the subjects into four groups according to the quartile values of TyG index: < 8.12, 8.12–8.51, 8.52–8.91, and ≥ 8.92. We next divided the subjects into nine groups based on their FBG levels and TyG index: FBG levels < 90 mg/dL and low TyG index (TyG index < 8.52), FBG levels of 90–94 mg/dL and low TyG index, FBG levels of 95–99 mg/dL and low TyG index, FBG levels of 100–125 mg/dL and low TyG index, FBG levels < 90 mg/dL and high TyG index (TyG index ≥ 8.52), FBG levels of 90–94 mg/dL and high TyG index, FBG levels of 95–99 mg/dL and high TyG index, FBG levels of 100–125 mg/dL and high TyG index, and FBG levels ≥ 126 mg/dL or DM. We measured the vascular response to reactive hyperemia in the brachial artery for assessment of FMD.

Measurement of FMD

A high-resolution linear artery transducer was coupled to computer-assisted analysis software (UNEXEF18G, UNEX Co., Nagoya, Japan) that used an automated edge detection system for measurement of the brachial artery diameter31. A blood pressure cuff was placed around the forearm of each subject. The brachial artery was scanned longitudinally 5 to 10 cm above the elbow. When the clearest B-mode image of the anterior and posterior intimal interfaces between the lumen and vessel wall was obtained, the transducer was held at the same point throughout the scan by using a special probe holder (UNEX Co.) to ensure consistency of the imaging. Depth and gain setting were set to optimize the images of the arterial lumen wall interface. When the tracking gate was placed on the intima, the artery diameter was automatically tracked, and the waveform of diameter changes over the cardiac cycle was displayed in real time using the FMD mode of the tracking system. This allowed the ultrasound images to be optimized at the start of the scan and the transducer position to be adjusted immediately for optimal tracking performance throughout the scan. Pulsed Doppler flow was assessed at baseline and during peak hyperemic flow, which was confirmed to occur within 15 s after cuff deflation. Blood flow velocity was calculated from the color Doppler data and was displayed as a waveform in real time. Baseline longitudinal images of the artery were acquired for 30 s, and then the blood pressure cuff was inflated to 50 mm Hg above systolic pressure for 5 min. Blood flow volume was calculated by multiplying the Doppler flow velocity (corrected for the angle) by heart rate and vessel cross-sectional area (− r2. Reactive hyperemia was calculated as the maximum percentage increase in flow after cuff deflation compared with baseline flow. The correlation coefficient between FMD analyzed at the core laboratory and participant institutions was 0.84 (p < 0.001).

Statistical analysis

Results are presented as means ± SD or medians (interquartile range). All reported probability values were 2-sided, and a probability value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Categorical values were compared by means of the chi-square test. Continuous variables were compared by using ANOVA multiple groups. Comparisons between the groups categorized according to the TyG index levels and FBG levels were carried out using ANOVA with Tukey’s post hoc test. OR and 95% CI were calculated using logistic regression analysis. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was performed to assess the sensitivity and specificity of the TyG index in predicting FMD < 4.4%. The AUC of the TyG index for predicting FMD < 4.4% was assessed. The AUC values of the two models were compared using DeLong’s test. Multivariate logistic regression analysis was performed to identify independent variables associated with lower quartiles of FMD (< 4.4%). Age ≥ 65 years, sex, obesity [body mass index (BMI) ≥ 30], current smoking, presence of hypertension and presence of dyslipidemia were entered into the multivariate logistic regression analysis. The effect of TyG index on endothelial dysfunction was assessed as a continuous variable by cubic spline curves with the TyG index of 8.52 as the reference. All data were processed using JMP Pro. Ver 18.0 software (SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA) and R software version 3.5.1 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

Developmental Trajectories and Influencing Factors of Inhaled Corticosteroid (ICS) Adherence in Pediatric Asthma Under a Remote Collaborative Management model—Latent Class Growth Analysis
Authors: Wenqi Li, Xialian Yu, Yan Gao, Yi Qin, Jianing Lu, Tao Li, Xiaoyin Hu, Caifeng Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis study aimed to explore the developmental trajectories and influencing factors of inhaled corticosteroid (ICS) medication adherence among pediatric asthma patients managed through the remote collaborative model based on the China Childhood Asthma Action Plan (CCAAP).MethodsA total of 249 pediatric asthma patients who were followed up at a respiratory specialty outpatient clinic between January 2022 and November 2024 were recruited using convenience sampling. The instruments consisted of a general information questionnaire, the Chinese version of the Bronchial Asthma Medication Adherence Scale, an asthma knowledge questionnaire, and an inhaled medication error rate assessment form. Medication adherence was evaluated at baseline and at 1, 3, and 6 months. Latent Category Growth Analysis (LCGA) was employed to identify adherence trajectories, and unordered multinomial logistic regression was conducted to examine the influencing factors.ResultsFour distinct adherence trajectories were identified, including the Declining Adherence group (6.02%), the Rapid Improvement group (8.03%), the Persistent Adherence group (71.08%), and the Gradual Improvement group (14.86%). The groups differed significantly in parental education, peak flow meter usage, concerns regarding steroid medication, history of severe exacerbations, asthma knowledge level, and 6-month medication error rate. Multivariate analysis indicated that pre-visit asthma knowledge, 6-month medication error rate, peak flow meter usage, concerns about steroid medication, and parental education were significant determinants of the Rapid Improvement group. Severe exacerbations at 3 months were related to the Gradual Improvement group, whereas recent severe exacerbations and the 6-month medication error rate were associated with the Declining Adherence group.ConclusionICS adherence within the CCAAP telemedicine-based management model demonstrates substantial heterogeneity. Identifying distinct trajectories enables early detection of non-adherence risk and supports personalized intervention strategies, which may significantly improve asthma control and resource allocation in remote care settings. Implementing stratified management based on these findings can enhance long-term treatment efficacy and pediatric asthma outcomes.Keywords: childhood asthma management program, telemedicine-based collaborative management model, asthma, pediatric patients, medication adherence, latent class growth model

Introduction

Bronchial asthma is the most common chronic respiratory disease in children, characterized by chronic airway inflammation as its core pathological mechanism and often accompanied by reversible airflow obstruction. From a pathophysiological perspective, asthma features airway hyperresponsiveness, where exposure to triggers such as allergens, viral infections, and environmental pollutants leads to contraction of airway smooth muscle, airflow limitation, and symptoms including wheezing, coughing, dyspnea, and chest tightness.1 The chronic inflammatory process in asthma is primarily driven by activation of immune cells such as T-lymphocytes and eosinophils, resulting in epithelial injury, mucus hypersecretion, and basement membrane thickening. Persistent inflammation may also lead to airway remodeling, characterized by smooth muscle hypertrophy and luminal narrowing. Airway hyperresponsiveness coupled with sustained inflammation underlies the recurrent nature of asthma symptoms.2

In recent years, its prevalence has continued to rise globally, severely impacting children’s quality of life and physical and mental health.3,4 Inhaled corticosteroids (ICS) are the first-line controller medications recommended by international guidelines, effectively controlling airway inflammation, reducing acute exacerbations, and lowering mortality risk.5 Despite the proven efficacy of ICS, asthma control among pediatric patients remains suboptimal in practice, with poor medication adherence identified as a major barrier.6,7

Advances in understanding the immunology of asthma have led to the introduction of biologic agents for severe or refractory disease. For instance, omalizumab targets IgE and is indicated for moderate‑to‑severe allergic asthma, whereas dupilumab inhibits the IL‑4/IL‑13 pathway and is used in patients with Th2‑driven refractory asthma.8 Although these biologics provide new therapeutic options for selected patients, they are currently reserved primarily for severe and difficult‑to‑treat cases, while ICS continues to serve as the cornerstone therapy for the majority of pediatric asthma patients.5

The China Children’s Asthma Action Plan (CCAAP)9 serves as a vital tool for enhancing self-management capabilities in children and is widely recommended by the Global Initiative for Asthma (GINA) and Chinese guidelines. However, its implementation rate in clinical practice remains low, with limited improvement in long-term adherence. To address this gap, a CCAAP-based remote collaborative management model has been increasingly implemented, enabling continuous follow-up through healthcare team collaboration and digital platforms. Preliminary studies suggest this model improves asthma control, enhances lung function, and increases adherence.10 Nevertheless, existing evidence primarily reflects overall average levels, failing to reveal the dynamic evolution and heterogeneous characteristics of individual adherence during long-term management. Although some studies have explored adherence trajectories in various pediatric chronic conditions,11 a significant knowledge gap remains regarding these longitudinal patterns specifically within telemedicine-based interventions, particularly in the context of remote asthma management.

The Latent Class Growth model (LCGM)12 is a statistical method suitable for longitudinal data, capable of identifying latent trajectory categories within populations to deepen understanding of adherence dynamics. Therefore, this study leverages the CCAAP remote collaborative management model, employing longitudinal follow-up and the LCGM method to explore latent trajectory categories and influencing factors of ICS medication adherence in pediatric asthma. This research aims to provide scientific evidence for healthcare providers to implement stratified population management and targeted interventions, while offering insights for optimizing remote management models and enhancing the effectiveness of long-term asthma management.

Materials and Methods

Study Design and Setting

This study employed a prospective cohort design to assess ICS medication adherence trajectories in pediatric asthma patients under the remote collaborative management model based on the China Childhood Asthma Action Plan (CCAAP). Participants were recruited from the respiratory outpatient department of a tertiary general hospital in Guangzhou, with data collection occurring between January 2022 and November 2024.

Participants

Inclusion Criteria

(1) diagnosis of bronchial asthma confirmed by physicians according to the Guidelines for Diagnosis and Prevention of Pediatric Bronchial Asthma (2025);3 (2) age below 18 years; (3) treatment with ICS and completion of a 6-month follow-up; and (4) informed consent obtained from guardians with voluntary participation in the study.

Exclusion Criteria

(1) inability to communicate effectively; (2) presence of severe cardiac, hepatic, renal, or neurological disorders; and (3) diagnosis of any other severe respiratory diseases.

Sample Size Justification

The sample size was primarily determined by the requirements of Latent Class Growth Analysis (LCGA). Methodological studies suggest that a sample size of at least 200 is necessary for reliable identification of latent trajectories.13 Accounting for a 20% attrition rate, we aimed to recruit approximately 240 participants. The final sample of 249 patients was therefore considered adequate for the LCGA.

Research Tools

General Information Questionnaire

This questionnaire was independently designed based on a comprehensive literature review and expert consultation. It consists of two sections.

Demographic data: patient age, gender, age at initial diagnosis, duration since diagnosis, parental age and gender, relationship to the patient, primary caregiver status, education level, and occupation.

Disease-related data: peak flow meter usage, concerns about steroid medication, types of inhaled medications, duration of medication use, frequency of severe asthma attacks, and emergency department visits or hospitalizations. These data were dynamically recorded at each follow-up visit following the initial consultation.

Chinese Version of the Asthma Medication Adherence Scale

This 10-item scale, originally developed by Horne et al14 employs a five-point Likert scoring system ranging from 1 (Always) to 5 (Never). An average score of 4.5 or higher indicates good medication adherence. Tian et al15 translated and localized the scale into Chinese in 2014, confirming its reliability and validity. This Chinese version was further validated by our research group in a 2023 study,10 confirming its suitability for assessing pediatric asthma medication adherence. In the present study, the questionnaire was completed by parents to evaluate their children’s adherence to ICS therapy. The Cronbach’s α coefficient for this scale in the present study was 0.811, indicating acceptable internal consistency.

Asthma Knowledge Questionnaire

The Asthma Knowledge Questionnaire used in this study was developed based on the instruments designed by Allen et al and Dong Zhenghui et al was subsequently validated by Zhang et al16 among adult asthma patients. The original version demonstrated a Cronbach’s α coefficient of 0.71, indicating acceptable internal reliability. The questionnaire consists of 25 items across four domains: disease nature, trigger factors, medication knowledge, and self-management. The total score ranges from 0 to 25, with higher scores reflecting greater asthma-related knowledge. In this study, the questionnaire was adapted for parents of pediatric asthma patients to assess their asthma-related knowledge. The Cronbach’s α coefficient for the adapted version in this study was 0.843, demonstrating strong internal consistency.

Inhaled Medication Usage Error Rate

Inhalation technique assessment was conducted in accordance with the domestic expert consensus17 and relevant studies,18 using standardized operational protocols to record the number of medication errors made by children. To ensure consistency and comparability, the analysis focused on the primary ICS long-term maintenance medication used by each child during the 6-month follow-up period. When multiple inhaled medications were used, the ICS or ICS/LABA device was prioritized for assessment. The error rate was calculated as the number of errors divided by the total number of operational steps, multiplied by 100%.

Data Collection Methods

Data collection was performed by researchers who had completed standardized training. Baseline data were collected in person by healthcare providers during each child’s initial outpatient visit to ensure accuracy. In accordance with guideline recommendations,5 follow-up assessments were scheduled at 1, 3, and 6 months after the initial visit. Parents completed follow-up questionnaires during scheduled outpatient follow-up appointments. All scales were completed by guardians under the supervision of trained researchers, and completeness was verified on-site prior to immediate data entry. To ensure data quality, a dual verification process and periodic spot checks were implemented to minimize data entry errors and information bias.

Statistical Analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS version 27.0 and Mplus version 8.3. Categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages, and intergroup comparisons were performed using the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test. Continuous variables were tested for normality and expressed as median (P25, P75) since all failed to meet the normal distribution assumption. The Kruskal–Wallis test was applied for comparisons among groups.

Univariate analyses were conducted to identify variables with statistical significance, which were subsequently included in an unordered multinomial logistic regression model. The “Persistent Adherence group” was used as the reference category. A multinomial regression model was established to explore the effects of different factors on adherence trajectory groups. Results were presented as regression coefficients (β), standard errors (SE), odds ratios (OR), and 95% confidence intervals (95% CI). A significance level of α = 0.05 was applied.

Results

Follow-Up Status and General Characteristics

Nineteen patients were lost to follow-up during the study period. Ultimately, 249 patients completed all four follow-up visits and were included in the final analysis, corresponding to an overall loss-to-follow-up rate of 7.09%. The mean compliance scores of the 249 participants at each follow-up visit were 4.45 ± 0.67, 4.65 ± 0.48, 4.71 ± 0.45, and 4.74 ± 0.43, respectively. Overall, the scores exhibited a gradual upward trend and tended to stabilize over time.

Latent Categories of ICS Adherence in Pediatric Asthma Patients

Using four observation points (T1, T2, T3, and T4) as indicators, models containing one to five latent categories were progressively constructed. The results indicated that the AIC, BIC, and aBIC values gradually decreased as the number of categories increased. When the model included two categories, both the LMR and BLRT were statistically significant (p < 0.05), and the entropy value was 0.967. For the three-category model, the LMR test was not statistically significant (p > 0.05). In the four-category model, both the LMR and BLRT remained statistically significant (p < 0.05), with an entropy value of 0.962 and high classification accuracy. In contrast, the five-category model showed an insignificant LMR test and produced a minimal class. Although the two-category model exhibited good overall fit, its ability to differentiate classes was limited. In comparison, the four-category model provided superior class differentiation and more distinct latent trajectory patterns while maintaining favorable fit indices and statistical significance. Therefore, the four-category model was selected as the optimal solution. The model fit indices are summarized in Table 1.

Discussion

Heterogeneous Trajectory Characteristics of ICS Adherence in Pediatric Asthma Patients

This study identified four distinct trajectories of ICS adherence among pediatric asthma patients using Latent Class Growth Analysis (LCGA), revealing substantial heterogeneity in adherence patterns under the CCAAP-based remote collaborative management model. Most participants were classified into the Persistent Adherence group, which maintained consistently high adherence levels throughout the observation period. This phenomenon may be attributed to strong family management awareness, good mastery of inhalation techniques, and continuous support through remote follow-ups. These patients primarily require routine monitoring and reinforcement through health education to sustain high adherence. The Gradual Improvement group demonstrated a progressive increase in adherence over time, suggesting that some children initially exhibited poor adherence due to concerns about medications or unfamiliarity with inhalation procedures. Through continuous interventions within the remote management model, including nurse-led video quality control, asthma diary monitoring, and family education, adherence levels gradually improved.19 The Rapid Improvement group exhibited the lowest baseline adherence but achieved marked short-term improvement, indicating high responsiveness to interventions and identifying them as a priority group for targeted support. Emphasis should therefore be placed on reinforcing positive feedback and implementing phased consolidation strategies to prevent relapse.

In contrast, the Declining Adherence group demonstrated a gradual decline in adherence, suggesting that certain children may discontinue medication once symptoms improve or experience fatigue due to the burden of long-term treatment. Early identification and timely intervention are therefore essential once a downward trend in adherence is observed.

Previous studies involving other chronic disease populations have also reported that the stable high-adherence group constitutes the majority, whereas both improvement and decline patterns coexist, consistent with the findings of the present study.20 In summary, integrating adherence trajectory classification into remote management workflows enables healthcare providers to perform dynamic monitoring, promptly identify changes in adherence, and adjust intervention strategies accordingly. This approach can enhance resource utilization efficiency, promote long-term adherence maintenance, and optimize individualized management strategies.

Parents with Higher Education Levels and Greater Asthma Knowledge During the Initial Treatment Phase Demonstrated Higher Long-Term Medication Adherence Among Children After Using Peak Flow Meters Following Medical Visits

Parents with higher educational levels and greater asthma knowledge during the initial treatment phase demonstrated better long-term medication adherence in their children, particularly when peak flow meters were used consistently after medical visits. The present study found that parents with higher educational attainment and better asthma knowledge during early follow-up were more likely to belong to the Persistent Adherence group, suggesting that cognitive and educational backgrounds serve as critical determinants for sustaining long-term medication adherence. These findings are consistent with those reported by Tang et al21 Previous evidence has also suggested22 that highly educated parents are generally more capable of acquiring and applying health-related information and possess higher self-efficacy, that is, greater confidence in their ability to maintain disease management behaviors. During remote follow-up, these cognitive advantages may translate into more stable medication adherence among children. Furthermore, differences in peak flow meter usage were observed among the different adherence trajectories. The Persistent Adherence group and Gradual Improvement group exhibited higher usage rates, integrating monitoring behaviors into their daily management routines. Objective monitoring data from these groups strengthened parental understanding and confidence in the effectiveness of medication.23 Conversely, the Declining Adherence group, despite exhibiting the highest initial usage rate, failed to maintain adherence over time. This phenomenon may have resulted from monitoring being performed perfunctorily, without an effective connection to medication management. When disease stability is temporarily achieved and monitoring values show little fluctuation, motivation to continue medication may diminish, resulting in reduced adherence.24

Therefore, during remote follow-ups, healthcare providers should deliver tiered education and skills training to help parents understand the intrinsic relationship between monitoring and medication adherence. Continuous feedback and positive reinforcement delivered through remote platforms can mitigate declines in adherence, promote long-term stability in management behaviors, and improve the precision of nursing interventions.

Children with a History of Frequent Severe Asthma Attacks and Associated Emergency Visits or Hospitalizations are More Likely to Show an Improving Trend in Treatment Adherence

Children with a history of frequent severe asthma attacks and related emergency visits or hospitalizations were more likely to exhibit an improving trend in treatment adherence. This study revealed that children who experienced a greater number of prior acute asthma episodes were more likely to follow a trajectory of gradually increasing adherence during subsequent treatment. Children who had more frequent severe asthma attacks in the year prior to consultation, within the three months before consultation, or within the three months after consultation, as well as those with multiple emergency department visits or hospitalizations due to asthma in the previous year, were more likely to be classified into the Rapid Improvement group or the Gradual Improvement group. These findings suggest that experiences of acute exacerbations may be associated with improved adherence behaviors. According to risk perception theory,25 frequent exacerbations allow parents to perceive the severity and unpredictability of the disease more concretely, thereby enhancing their risk awareness and increasing acceptance of standardized treatment, which in turn manifests as gradual improvement in adherence.26 Under the remote collaborative management model, this effect of risk perception may be further amplified. Parents are able to continuously observe disease fluctuations through asthma diaries and follow-up feedback, which reinforces their adherence to standardized treatment. Conversely, families without severe disease experiences often exhibit inadequate risk perception. Following symptom relief, such families are more likely to relax their vigilance, reduce or discontinue medication, and consequently fall into the Declining Adherence group.27 From a nursing perspective, priority should be given to families with insufficient risk perception. Through effective risk communication and personalized education during follow-ups, caregivers should be guided to recognize the potential hazards of asthma and the importance of long-term standardized management. Future research should incorporate quantitative assessments of risk perception to explore its relationship with medication adherence trajectories, thereby providing evidence-based support for stratified interventions and optimized remote management strategies.

Limited Improvement in Medication Adherence Among Parents Concerned About Hormone Use After Consultation

The study identified statistically significant differences in parental attitudes toward corticosteroid therapy across the adherence trajectories. The Declining Adherence group, which had the highest proportion of parental concerns, showed a greater tendency toward declining adherence, whereas the Rapid Improvement group, with the lowest concern rate, demonstrated significant improvement in adherence. Some parents, concerned about potential side effects or long-term dependency, were unable to ensure that their children adhered to treatment protocols during the initial treatment phase. Even when short-term adherence improved under medical guidance, maintaining long-term adherence remained challenging, consistent with the findings of Hui et al28 Similarly, Özçeker et al29 reported that more than 40% of parents feared that ICS use could lead to addiction, weight gain, or developmental delay, with some even reducing dosages or discontinuing medication without consulting healthcare professionals and turning to alternative therapies. These findings suggest that “steroid phobia” is prevalent among parents, representing a major psychological barrier to maintaining adherence. Therefore, healthcare providers should incorporate routine monitoring and feedback regarding medication attitudes into remote management protocols to enhance the precision and flexibility of intervention strategies.

Increased Medication Error Rates in ICS Use Hinder Maintenance of Medication Adherence

Significant intergroup differences in medication error rates were observed among adherence trajectories at the 6-month follow-up. The Declining Adherence group demonstrated the highest medication error rate, suggesting that parental vigilance may decline over extended follow-up periods. Reduced supervision of children’s medication use may result in nonstandard administration, consistent with the findings of Alina et al30 The operation of ICS inhalation devices among pediatric asthma patients involves multiple procedural steps that can be challenging to master. Improper device use can reduce the effectiveness of drug inhalation and lung deposition.17 Existing research has also indicated31 that operational errors in inhalation device use are common in clinical practice. Nonstandard use of inhalation devices compromises effective drug delivery and lung deposition, thereby reducing therapeutic efficacy and diminishing children’s motivation to maintain adherence. Therefore, elevated error rates may represent an important obstacle to sustaining adherence.

In contrast, the Persistent Adherence group exhibited the lowest error rate, suggesting a potential positive association between stable adherence and standardized operational skills. Within the remote collaborative management model, skill deterioration may serve as a key mechanism underlying long-term declines in adherence. Regular skill assessments and repeated training should therefore be integrated into follow-up procedures. By dynamically identifying adherence trajectories, nursing teams can prioritize technical reassessment and retraining for high-risk children, improving both clinical outcomes and the cost-effectiveness of remote asthma management programs.

Study Limitations

This study is subject to several limitations. The single-center design and relatively small sample size may limit the generalizability of the results. While the 6-month follow-up period allowed for the identification of initial adherence trajectories, it was insufficient to fully capture long-term adherence dynamics. Adherence was primarily assessed using self-reported measures, which may have introduced recall bias. Additionally, key psychosocial factors, such as family support and trust in healthcare providers, were not systematically examined. Future research should adopt multicenter designs, extend follow-up durations, and incorporate objective adherence measures, such as electronic monitoring, to further validate the stability and explanatory power of the identified trajectories. Building on this foundation, future efforts should focus on facilitating the clinical translation of trajectory-based models, developing intelligent early warning and intervention systems, elucidating psychosocial mechanisms, and evaluating the effects of trajectory-informed management strategies on long-term clinical outcomes. These efforts are expected to promote more precise and personalized remote management of pediatric asthma.

Conclusion

This study applied the LCGA method to identify four distinct developmental trajectories of ICS adherence among pediatric asthma patients within a remote collaborative management model, revealing marked group heterogeneity and providing new insights into the dynamic evolution of adherence. The findings indicated that adherence trajectories were influenced by parental education level, asthma knowledge, use of peak flow meters for self-monitoring, concerns about steroid medication, history of severe asthma exacerbations, emergency visits or hospitalizations, and ICS medication error rates. Taken together, these findings highlight clear opportunities for translating trajectory-based evidence into practical clinical interventions. First, a risk-stratified assessment tool could be developed based on trajectory membership and influencing factors, enabling the early identification of patients at risk for declining or persistently low adherence. Second, differentiated intervention plans should be designed for each trajectory group—for example, intensifying medication guidance and follow-up frequency for the Lagging Behind group, reinforcing maintenance-phase education for the Rapid Improvement group, and providing positive reinforcement and sustained support for the Gradual Improvement group. In addition, existing remote management platforms could be leveraged to integrate trajectory prediction models with automated reminder systems, enabling dynamic monitoring and timely intervention throughout the follow-up process.

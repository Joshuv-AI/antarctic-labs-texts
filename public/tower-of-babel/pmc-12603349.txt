An analysis of safety data from the first year of resmetirom
Authors: Praneeth Kudaravalli, Michael B Andrews, Douglas G Adler
Published: 2025

Introduction

Resmetirom—an oral thyroid hormone receptor-β agonist—represents a significant advancement in the treatment of metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH) [1]. In a Phase 3 randomized–controlled trial of 966 adults with biopsy-proven MASH, once-daily resmetirom (80 or 100 mg) significantly improved MASH and liver fibrosis compared with placebo (P < 0.001) [2]. In March 2024, the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA) granted accelerated approval for resmetirom as an adjunct to diet and exercise in adults with noncirrhotic MASH and moderate to advanced fibrosis (F2–F3) [3].

In the first year since its approval, attention has turned to understanding the safety profile of resmetirom in real-world use. To assess this, we analysed data from the FDA Adverse Event Reporting System (FAERS)—a publicly accessible pharmacovigilance database—to identify adverse events (AEs) potentially linked to resmetirom [4].

Patients and methods

From its FDA approval date until 31 March 2025, a total of 368 AE reports involving resmetirom were identified. Patient sex was specified in only a small subset, with 10 reports (2.7%) involving males, 9 (2.4%) involving females, and 349 (94.8%) lacking sex specification. The median age was 56 years (interquartile range: 46–60.5 years) in males and 64 years (interquartile range: 60–72 years) in females. Outcomes included death in 7 cases (1.9%), hospitalization in 24 (6.5%), life-threatening events in 1 (0.3%), and disability in 1 (0.3%). The majority of reports were classified as non-serious (284 reports, 77.2%).

Results

Across these 368 reports, a total of 845 AEs were recorded. Gastrointestinal AEs were the most frequent: nausea or vomiting (13.7%), diarrhea (8.2%), and abdominal pain (7.5%), along with abdominal bloating (1.8%), constipation (1.5%), and five cases of pancreatitis (0.6%). Hepatobiliary AEs were notable for elevated serum liver chemistries (15.9%), hyperbilirubinemia (2.8%), and jaundice (2.2%). Serious hepatic AEs, such as hepatic failure (0.6%), drug-induced liver injury (0.2%), elevated international normalized ratio (0.2%), and hepatic encephalopathy (0.1%), were also reported. Neuropsychiatric AEs such as fatigue (5.2%), dizziness (4.3%), and headache (2.4%) were reported, alongside altered mental status (2.1%) and insomnia (0.6%). Cardiovascular events, though infrequent, included chest pain (0.7%), palpitations (0.7%), and peripheral edema (0.6%). Other frequent AEs included dermatologic concerns such as pruritus (1.8%) and erythema (0.8%), along with scattered musculoskeletal and infectious AEs (Table 1).

Discussion and conclusion

Prior Phase 3 randomized–controlled trials identified gastrointestinal side effects―particularly diarrhea, nausea, and vomiting―as the most commonly reported AEs associated with resmetirom [2, 5]. This aligns closely with our real-world findings from the FAERS database, in which gastrointestinal AEs were also the most frequently reported. While generally well tolerated, we also noted serious AEs, including hepatic failure, pancreatitis, and cardiac events, that may require further investigation. However, notable limitations of any study using the FAERS database include the inability to calculate incidence rates or imply causation, so results must be interpreted with caution.

Nearly one-fourth (24%) of Americans are already affected by metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease and the prevalence of MASH is projected to rise by 63% by 2030 [6, 7]. Therefore, millions of patients will likely be prescribed resmetirom in the years ahead. Because of this, it is paramount to continually monitor and understand the potential safety risks posed for this fast-growing patient population taking resmetirom.

This timely pharmacovigilance study captures initial real-world safety concerns from resmetirom’s first year on the market, which enhances shared decision-making between patients and providers.

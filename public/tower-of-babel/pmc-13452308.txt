A facile antibody-mediated CMC platform for targeted screening of CCR1 ligands from Magnolia officinalis
Authors: Ting Zhang, Shengjie You, Wenhao Chen, Hao Liu, Taiping He, Qiao Zhang, Xiaojuan Tian, Xiang Sun, Limei Liang, Suleixin Yang, Sanyin Zhang, Huachao Bin, Shilin Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Graphical abstract

G protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs) constitute the largest family of human transmembrane receptors, and about 30% of clinically approved drugs target this receptor family. However, due to the bottlenecks such as its conformational variability, difficulty in expression and purification, and low efficiency in ligand screening, only 12.5% of GPCRs have been successfully developed into therapeutic drug targets to date [1]. Current GPCR ligand screening methods primarily include ligand binding-based strategies (e.g., surface plasmon resonance (SPR)), functional assays (e.g. second messenger detection), artificial intelligence (AI)-assisted screening approaches, and multi-omics data integration.

Cell membrane chromatography (CMC) technology immobilizes membrane fragments containing overexpressed GPCRs onto chromatographic carriers through membrane fixation strategies such as histidine affinity tag (His-tag) [2] or SNAP-tag, simulating the in vivo drug-receptor interaction process, and provides an effective new strategy for GPCR ligand screening. The Flag tag has been widely adopted as a classic tag in protein purification [3]; however, there have been no reports on Flag tag-based CMC immobilization strategies. To further expand the immobilization toolkit for CMC technology, this study developed a Flag-CMC platform using the Flag-tag and C−C chemokine receptor 1 (CCR1) as a model GPCR.

This study employed a Flag-tag strategy by incorporating a 3× Flag-tag at the C-terminus of the CCR1 in a stably transfected HEK293T cell line [4]. Real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR) and Western blotting (WB) analysis confirmed successful construction of CCR1-overexpressing cell lines, as documented in the Supplementary data, Fig. S1, and Table S1. Initially, trichlorotriazine-activated silica (SiO2-TCT) was synthesized and then covalently conjugated with an anti-Flag M2 antibody via site-specific crosslinking to generate SiO2-TCT-M2. This design facilitates directional immobilization of CCR1-enriched membrane proteins through high-affinity, reversible non-covalent interactions between the M2 antibody and the Flag-tag on CCR1 (Fig. 1A). Specificity validation was performed using the Flag-CCR1/CMC chromatographic column: following sample injection into the CMC column, the model compound (BX471, as an inhibitor and positive control) exhibited a retention time of 40 min on the CCR1-Flag-tag/CMC column (Fig. S3A), whereas almost no retention was observed on the no-Flag-CCR1/CMC column (Fig. S3B). Non-CCR1-targeted compounds, such as ticagrelor and GRA1 Ex-25, showed no retention (Fig. S3C), demonstrating the high specificity and selectivity of the system. The CMC column exhibited robust operational stability throughout the experiments. BX471 remained well retained on the CCR1/CMC column after multiple injections at different times (Fig. S3D).

Magnolia officinalis is a widely used traditional Chinese medicine. Modern pharmacology has demonstrated that it possesses diverse pharmacological activities such as anti-inflammatory and antibacterial effects [5]. Employing the established two-dimensional (2D) Flag-CCR1/CMC-ultra performance liquid chromatography/mass spectrometry (UPLC/MS) platform, the extract of Magnolia officinalis was systematically screened. 1D CMC separation coupled with 2D online HPLC-MS analysis enabled successful identification of honokiol and magnolol as the active components that specifically bind to CCR1 (Figs. 1B and S4A−S4C). Analysis of magnolol and honokiol standards using the Flag-CCR1/CMC column yielded consistent results with those obtained from the crude extract (Fig. S4D). Molecular docking simulations revealed that both compounds stably bind within the ligand-binding pocket of CCR1 (Figs. 1C and D). SPR assays determined their binding affinities, with equilibrium dissociation constants (KD) of 3.07 μmol/L for magnolol and 9.10 μmol/L for honokiol (Figs. 1E and F). Frontal affinity chromatography [3] using the CCR1 column yielded KD values of 4.57 and 5.78 μmol/L for magnolol and honokiol, respectively (Fig. S5), indicating strong binding affinity to the extracellular domain of CCR1. These findings confirm that Flag-CCR1 column is suitable for screening of small molecules and the interaction between small molecules and proteins.

To furthur verify the binding specificity, fluorescent probes based on magnolol and honokiol were designed and synthesized (Fig. 1G), and competitive binding experiments were conducted with BX471. The synthetic route of the CCR1 ligand fluorescent probe based on Nile Red is illustrated in Fig. 1H, and its chemical structure has been confirmed by nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy (Fig. S6). The results of the competitive experiments indicated that both magnolol and honokiol effectively inhibit the binding of the known CCR1 antagonist BX471 to the receptor, thereby confirming their activity as CCR1-specific ligands (Figs. 1I and S7).

To functionally characterize the inhibitory effects of magnolol and honokiol on CCR1, this study employed a cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP) accumulation assay in HEK293T cells overexpressing human CCR1. Stimulation with chemokine (C−C motif) ligand 3‌ (CCL3) (10 nmol/L) significantly reduced cAMP levels, whereas magnolol and honokiol, administered at concentration gradients (0.1–100 μmol/L), effectively reversed this suppression. Normalized data confirmed that both compounds act as CCR1 antagonists (Fig. 1J). CCL3-induced calcium influx was inhibited by magnolol and honokiol, as evidenced by reduced fluorescence intensity compared to the endogenous ligand stimulation group (Fig. S8). Further evaluation using the ONE vector G protein optical (ONE-GO) biosensor real-time signaling platform integrated with the GloSensor™ cAMP reporter system quantified the inhibitory potency of these two ligands on inhibitory G-protein subunit G i1 signaling pathways (Figs. 1K and L). The results indicate that both magnolol and honokiol at 10 μmol/L significantly attenuated CCL3-mediated cAMP inhibition, exhibiting efficacy comparable to the positive control BX471.

In an inflammation model using RAW264.7 macrophages (Fig. 1M), magnolol and honokiol exerted anti-inflammatory effects by targeting the CCR1 receptor. Following lipopolysaccharide (LPS) stimulation (250 ng/mL), pretreatment with 20 μmol/L magnolol or honokiol suppressed interleukin-6 (IL-6) secretion and nitric oxide (NO) production, corresponding to inhibition rates of approximately 40% and 55% (Figs. 1N and O). RT-qPCR analysis revealed that both compounds downregulated the expression of Ccr1 and its downstream inflammation-related genes (Figs. S9A and B). Magnolol and honokiol directly block receptor function and reduced inflammation-driven upregulation of CCR1 transcription. WB analysis demonstrated that magnolol and honokiol treatment inhibited the expression of CCR1 downstream proteins CCL5, phosphorylated protein kinase B (p-AKT (Thr308)), and phosphorylated extracellular regulated protein kinases 1/2 (p-ERK1/2 (Thr202/Tyr204)), but showed no significant effect on CCL3 and phosphorylated mitogen-activated protein kinase kinase 1/2 (p-MEK1/2 (Ser217/221)) (Figs. 1P and S9C). Meanwhile, we treated RAW264.7 cells overexpressing CCR1 with LPS. Compared with the LPS group without high CCR1 expression, NO release was significantly increased after CCR1 overexpression. Moreover, magnolol and magnolol treatment could significantly reduce the level of NO (Fig. S10). These results indicate that CCR1 is an important mediating factor for the anti-inflammatory effect of magnolol and magnolol. These results indicate that magnolol and honokiol attenuate LPS-induced inflammatory responses by suppressing the expression of CCR1 downstream signaling proteins and inflammation-associated genes.

In this study, a Flag-CCR1/CMC screening platform was established utilizing a Flag antibody-directed cell membrane anchoring strategy, thereby enriching the immobilization methodologies available for CMC. This strategy holds significant potential for the high-throughput screening of ligands targeting other GPCRs, and future efforts will focus on systematically defining the applicability of different membrane immobilization tags. For methodological validation, an empty-vector control column was not included, HEK293T cells (low CCR1 expression) served as a negative control, and orthogonal assays (SPR, competition, and signaling) ensured reliability and specificity. Using this platform, magnolol and honokiol were identified for the first time as selective CCR1 antagonists from Magnolia officinalis extract, revealing a novel anti-inflammatory mechanism of this herbal medicine. Previous studies have demonstrated that magnolol and honokiol exert anti-inflammatory effects in a rat model of ulcerative colitis [5]. Nevertheless, further in vivo pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic evaluations are warranted to fully assess their therapeutic potential for CCR1-related inflammatory diseases.

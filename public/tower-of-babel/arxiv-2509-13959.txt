Skew braces and Rota–Baxter operators on semi-direct products
Pragya Belwal, Mahender Singh

Abstract
. This paper examines the connections between (relative) Rota–Baxter groups, skew left braces, and enlargements of these structures on naturally associated semi-direct products. For any skew left brace, we introduce a new skew left brace, called the square, on the natural semi-direct product of its additive and multiplicative groups. Further, the square construction turns out to be distinct from the previously known double construction arising as a special case of matched pairs of skew braces. This provides a method to construct a new bijective, non-degenerate solution to the Yang–Baxter equation from an existing solution arising from a skew left brace. We show that the square construction is functorial and integrates naturally into both the cohomological and extension-theoretic frameworks for (relative) Rota–Baxter groups and skew left braces. Furthermore, we provide a sufficient condition under which two isoclinic skew left braces yield isoclinic squares.

1.
Introduction

The program for classifying set-theoretic solutions to the Yang–Baxter equation was proposed by Drinfel’d in the 1990s
[
12
]
. A key result by Guarnieri and Vendramin
[
13
]
established that every skew left brace yields a non-degenerate bijective set-theoretic solution to the Yang–Baxter equation. Recently, Guo, Lang, and Sheng
[
14
]
introduced Rota–Baxter operators on Lie groups as a means of producing such operators on the corresponding Lie algebras. The study of Rota–Baxter operators on more general groups, beyond the Lie group setting, was undertaken by Bardakov and Gubarev
[
2
,
3
]
, who proved that every Rota–Baxter operator on a group induces a skew left brace structure on that group. This result links Rota–Baxter operators on groups directly to Drinfel’d’s program. At the same time, Caranti and Stefanello
[
8
]
gave a cohomological characterization of when a skew left brace can be induced from a Rota–Baxter group. The idea of Rota–Baxter operators was further generalized by Jiang, Sheng, and Zhu
[
15
]
to relative Rota–Baxter operators on Lie groups. Building on this, it was proved in
[
18
]
that every skew left brace is induced by a relative Rota–Baxter group, and that there is an isomorphism between the categories of bijective relative Rota–Baxter groups and skew left braces. In
[
1
]
, Bai et al. further connected relative Rota–Baxter groups to Butcher groups, post-groups, and pre-groups. As another generalization, Catino et al.
[
9
,
10
]
introduced Rota–Baxter operators on Clifford semigroups and established a correspondence between such operators and weak braces, the latter being known to produce set-theoretic solutions to the Yang–Baxter equation that may be degenerate.

A cohomology theory for relative Rota–Baxter operators on Lie groups was introduced in
[
15
]
. However, this theory is not applicable to abstract groups, as it is defined in terms of the cohomology of the descendent group with coefficients in the Lie algebra of the acting Lie group, viewed as a module over the descendent group. To address this limitation, a cohomology and extension theory for relative Rota–Baxter groups was developed in
[
5
]
, enabling the classification of such extensions via the second cohomology group. The relationship between the cohomology of relative Rota–Baxter groups and that of their associated skew left braces was also investigated. These constructions were subsequently applied in
[
6
]
to define the Schur multiplier and Schur covers of relative Rota–Baxter groups, and their connections to the corresponding constructions for skew left braces were explored.

Given the central role of skew left braces in classifying set-theoretic solutions to the Yang–Baxter equation, developing new methods for their construction is of fundamental importance. In this paper, we introduce a novel method for constructing a skew left brace, which we call the square, from a given skew left brace. This construction is defined on the natural semi-direct product of the additive and multiplicative groups of the given skew left brace. The idea is motivated by an observation by Bardakov et al.
[
4
]
that every relative Rota–Baxter group gives rise to a Rota–Baxter operator on the natural semi-direct product of its constituent groups. We observe that our square construction is distinct from the previously known double construction of a skew left brace due to Smoktunowicz and Vendramin
[
21
]
. This provides a method to construct a new bijective, non-degenerate solution to the Yang–Baxter equation from an existing solution arising from a skew left brace. Further, we show that the square construction is functorial and naturally integrates into both the cohomological and extension-theoretic frameworks for (relative) Rota–Baxter groups and skew left braces.

The paper is organised as follows. In Section
2
, we recall some necessary background from the extension and cohomology theory of skew left braces, Rota–Baxter groups and relative Rota–Baxter groups. In Section
3
, we prove that the construction of the Rota–Baxter group on the associated semi-direct product arising from a given relative Rota–Baxter group is functorial (Proposition
3.2
), and yields a homomorphism from the second cohomology of the relative Rota–Baxter group to that of the induced Rota–Baxter group (Proposition
3.4
). In Section
4
, we give the construction of the square of a skew left brace, and illustrate how it differs from its double. We prove that the construction of the square of a skew left brace is functorial (Proposition
4.7
), and that there is a homomorphism from the second cohomology of the given skew left brace to that of its square (Proposition
4.9
). In Section
5
, we prove that, for each skew left brace, the homomorphisms of cohomology groups defined in the preceding sections give rise to a commutative diagram of cohomology groups of (relative) Rota–Baxter groups, skew left braces, and their squares (Theorem
5.1
). Finally, in Section
6
, we provide a sufficient condition under which two isoclinic skew left braces yield isoclinic squares (Theorem
6.6
).

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some essential results on abelian extensions of skew left braces and (relative) Rota–Baxter groups.

2.1.
Abelian extensions of skew left braces

We begin by recalling some basic ideas about abelian extensions of skew left braces.

Definition 2.1
.

A skew left brace is a triple
(H,\cdot,\circ)
, where
(H,\cdot)
and
(H,\circ)
are groups such that

a\circ(b\cdot c)=(a\circ b)\cdot a^{-1}\cdot(a\circ c)

for all
a,b,c\in H
, where
a^{-1}
denotes the inverse of
a
in
(H,\cdot)
. The groups
(H,\cdot)
and
(H,\circ)
are called the additive and the multiplicative groups of the skew left brace
(H,\cdot,\circ)
, respectively. When the additive group is abelian, then
(H,\cdot,\circ)
is simply called a left brace.

Any group can be viewed as a skew left brace with the same additive and the multiplicative group, called the
trivial skew left brace
. Given a skew left brace
(H,\cdot,\circ)
, there is an associated action
\lambda^{H}:(H,\circ)\rightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(H,\cdot)
, which is a group homomorphism defined by

\lambda^{H}_{a}(b)=a^{-1}\cdot(a\circ b)

for all
a,b\in H
. The associated action plays a key role in understanding skew left braces.

Notation 2.1
.

For simplicity in notation, we make the following conventions:

(1)

We denote the additive and the multiplicative groups of a skew left brace
(H,\cdot,\circ)
by
H^{(\cdot)}
and
H^{(\circ)}
, respectively.

(2)

When there is no ambiguity, we write the group operation in
H^{(\cdot)}
simply as
ab
.

(3)

We denote the inverse of an element
g
in
H^{(\cdot)}
by
g^{-1}
, and the inverse of
g
in
H^{(\circ)}
by
g^{\dagger}
.

(4)

When the context is clear, we will not differentiate between the notations for the additive and the multiplicative groups of different skew left braces.

(5)

We will sometimes denote the image
\phi(x)
of a homomorphism
\phi
by
\phi_{x}
.

(6)

We denote by
{\bf 1}
the trivial skew left brace for which both the underlying groups are trivial.

Next, we define a morphism of skew left braces.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(H,\cdot_{H},\circ_{H})
and
(K,\cdot_{K},\circ_{K})
be skew left braces. A map
f:H\rightarrow K
is called a homomorphism of skew left braces if

f(a\cdot_{H}b)=f(a)\cdot_{K}f(b)\quad\textrm{and}\quad f(a\circ_{H}b)=f(a)\circ_{K}f(b)

for all
a,b\in H
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
(I,\cdot)
be an abelian group viewed as a trivial brace and
(H,\cdot,\circ)
be a skew left brace. An (abelian) extension of
(H,\cdot,\circ)
by
(I,\cdot)
is a skew left brace
(E,\cdot,\circ)
that fits into the sequence

\mathcal{E}:\quad\quad{\bf 1}\to(I,\cdot)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\to}}(E,\cdot,\circ)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\to}}(H,\cdot,\circ)\to{\bf 1},

where
i
and
\pi
are homomorphisms of skew left braces such that

i
is injective,
\pi
is surjective and
\operatorname{im}(i)=\ker(\pi)
.

For simplicity, we denote
i(y)
by
y
. By a (normalised) set-theoretic section to
\mathcal{E}
, we mean a map
s:H\rightarrow E
such that
\pi\,s=\operatorname{id}_{H}
and
s(1)=1
. We recall essential results on extensions of skew left braces by abelian groups from
[
20
]
(see also
[
16
]
).

Let
(M,\cdot,\circ)
be a skew left brace and

\mathcal{E}:\quad\quad{\bf 1}\longrightarrow(I,\cdot)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i_{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}(E,\cdot,\circ)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi_{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}(M,\cdot,\circ)\longrightarrow{\bf 1}

be an extension of

by the trivial brace
(I,\cdot)
. Let

be a set-theoretic section to

. We define maps
\xi,\epsilon:M^{(\circ)}\rightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(I)

(2.1)

\displaystyle\xi_{m}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{E}_{s(m)}(y),

(2.2)

\displaystyle\zeta_{m}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s(m)^{-1}\cdot y\cdot s(m)~~\textrm{and}

(2.3)

\displaystyle\epsilon_{m}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s(m)^{\dagger}\circ y\circ s(m)

for

and

x^{-1}
and

denote the inverse of

in
E^{(\cdot)}
and

, respectively. It is not difficult to see that the map

and

are anti-homomorphisms. Furthermore, these maps are independent of the choice of the set-theoretic section
[

Let

\displaystyle\;1,

\displaystyle\xi_{m_{1}}(f(m_{2},m_{3}))\,f(m_{1}\circ m_{2},m_{3})^{-1}\,f(m_{1},m_{2}\circ m_{3})\,\xi_{m_{1}\circ m_{2}\circ m_{3}}(\epsilon_{m_{3}}(\xi^{-1}_{m_{1}\circ m_{2}}f(m_{1},m_{2})))^{-1}=

2.2.
Abelian extensions of Rota–Baxter groups

Next, we recall some necessary results on Rota–Baxter groups from
[
11
, Section 3, 4]
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
(G,\cdot)
be a group. A map
R:G\rightarrow G
is called a Rota–Baxter operator of weight
1
on
G
if

R(x)\cdot R(y)=R\big(x\cdot R(x)\cdot y\cdot R(x)^{-1}\big)

for all
x,y\in G
. A group
(G,\cdot)
equipped with a Rota–Baxter operator of weight
1
is called a Rota–Baxter group, and is denoted by the pair
(G,R)
.

The following result connects Rota–Baxter groups to skew left braces
[
2
, Proposition 3.1]
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
(G,\cdot)
be a group and
R:G\to G
be a Rota–Baxter operator. If we define
x\circ_{R}y=x\cdot R(x)\cdot y\cdot R(x)^{-1}
, then
(G,\cdot,\circ_{R})
is a skew left brace.

Note that, if the skew left brace
(G,\cdot,\circ_{R})
is induced by a Rota–Baxter operator
R:G\to G
, then the associated action is given by

(2.11)

\lambda^{G}_{x}(y)=R(x)\cdot y\cdot R(x)^{-1},

which is simply the conjugation action by the image of
R
.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
(G,R_{G})
and
(H,R_{H})
be Rota–Baxter groups. A map
\phi:G\rightarrow H
is called a homomorphism of Rota–Baxter groups if
\phi
is a group homomorphism satisfying

\phi\,R_{G}=R_{H}\,\phi.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
(I,R_{I})
and
(H,R_{H})
be Rota–Baxter groups. A Rota–Baxter extension of
(H,R_{H})
by
(I,R_{I})
is a Rota–Baxter group
(E,R_{E})
that fits into the sequence

\mathcal{E}:\quad\quad 1\to(I,R_{I})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\to}}(E,R_{E})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\to}}(H,R_{H})\to 1,

where
i
and
\pi
are homomorphisms of Rota–Baxter groups such that
i
is injective,
\pi
is surjective and
\operatorname{im}(i)=\ker(\pi)
.

For simplicity, we denote
i(y)
by
y
, which implies that
R_{E}
restricted to
I
is
R_{I}
. By a (normalised) set-theoretic section to
\mathcal{E}
, we mean a map
s:H\rightarrow E
such that
\pi\,s=\operatorname{id}_{H}
and
s(1)=1
.

Remark 2.9
.

A direct check shows that an extension of Rota–Baxter groups induces an extension of induced skew left braces.

Let
\mathcal{E}:1\to(I,R_{I})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\to}}(E,R_{E})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\to}}(H,R_{H})\to 1

(H,R_{H})

(I,R_{I})

is an abelian group. Let

\displaystyle\gamma_{h}(y)=

\displaystyle s(h)^{-1}ys(h).

Note that
\gamma

of the extension
\mathcal{E}

For each

(2.13)

R_{E}\big(s(h)\big)=s\big(R_{H}(h)\big)y_{h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s(h_{1}h_{2})^{-1}s(h_{1})s(h_{2})~~\textrm{and}

\displaystyle r(h)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle y_{h}

for

2.3.
Abelian extensions of relative Rota–Baxter groups

Finally, we recall essential results on extension theory of relative Rota–Baxter groups from
[
5
]
.

Definition 2.12
.

A relative Rota–Baxter group is a quadruple
(H,G,\phi,R)
, where
H
and
G
are groups,
\phi:G\rightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(H)
a group homomorphism and
R:H\rightarrow G
is a map satisfying the condition

R(h_{1})R(h_{2})=R\big(h_{1}\phi_{R(h_{1})}(h_{2})\big)

for all
h_{1},h_{2}\in H
. The map
R
is referred to as the relative Rota–Baxter operator on
H
. The relative Rota–Baxter group
(H,G,\phi,R)
is called trivial if
\phi:G\to\operatorname{Aut}(H)
is the trivial homomorphism.

Remark 2.13
.

Let
\phi:G\to\operatorname{Aut}(G)
be the adjoint action, that is,
\phi_{y}(x)=yxy^{-1}
for
x,y\in G
. Then the relative Rota–Baxter group
(G,G,\phi,R)
is simply the Rota–Baxter group
(G,R)
.

The following result connects relative Rota–Baxter groups to skew left braces

3.
From relative Rota–Baxter groups to Rota–Baxter groups on semi-direct products

Recall that, the semi-direct product
H\rtimes_{\phi}G
of groups
G
and
H
under the (left) action
\phi\colon G\rightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(H)
is the set

H\times G
equipped with the multiplication

(h_{1},g_{1})(h_{2},g_{2})=\big(h_{1}\phi_{g_{1}}(h_{2}),g_{1}g_{2}\big)

for all
h_{1},h_{2}\in H
and
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
.

The following result shows that each relative Rota–Baxter group defines a Rota–Baxter group on the natural semi-direct product
[
4
, Proposition 3.1]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(H,G,\phi,R)
be a relative Rota–Baxter group. Then
\widetilde{R}\colon H\rtimes_{\phi}G\to H\rtimes_{\phi}G
given by

\widetilde{R}\big((h,g)\big)=\big(1,g^{-1}R(h)\big)

for all
h\in H
and
g\in G
, is a Rota–Baxter operator on the semi-direct product
H\rtimes_{\phi}G
.

We observe that the preceding construction is, in fact, functorial.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{RRB}
and
\mathcal{RB}
denote the categories of relative Rota–Baxter groups and Rota–Baxter groups, respectively. Then the association
\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{RRB}\to\mathcal{RB}
given by
\mathcal{F}\big((H,G,\phi,R)\big)=\big(H\rtimes_{\phi}G,\widetilde{R}\big)
is a covariant functor.

Proof.

Let
(f_{1},f_{2}):(H,G,\phi,R)\to(K,L,\alpha,S)
be a homomorphism of relative Rota–Baxter groups. Define
\widetilde{f}:H\rtimes_{\phi}G\to K\rtimes_{\alpha}L
by

\widetilde{f}(h,g)=(f_{1}(h),f_{2}(g))
for all
h\in H
and
g\in G
. We claim that
\widetilde{f}:(H\rtimes_{\phi}G,\widetilde{R})\to(K\rtimes_{\alpha}L,\widetilde{S})
is a homomorphism of Rota–Baxter groups. Indeed, for
g,g^{\prime}\in G
and
h,h^{\prime}\in H
, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}\big((h,g)(h^{\prime},g^{\prime})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}\big((h\phi_{g}(h^{\prime}),gg^{\prime})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(f_{1}(h\phi_{g}(h^{\prime})),f_{2}(gg^{\prime})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(f_{1}(h)f_{1}(\phi_{g}(h^{\prime})),f_{2}(gg^{\prime})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(f_{1}(h)\alpha_{f_{2}(g)}(f_{1}(h^{\prime})),f_{2}(gg^{\prime})\big),

since
(f_{1},f_{2})
is a homomorphism of relative Rota–Baxter groups

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(f_{1}(h),f_{2}(g)\big)\big(f_{1}(h^{\prime}),f_{2}(g^{\prime})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}\big((h,g)\big)\widetilde{f}\big((h^{\prime},g^{\prime})\big)

and

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}\,\widetilde{R}\big((h,g)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}\big((1,g^{-1}R(h))\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(1,f_{2}(g^{-1}R(h)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(1,f_{2}(g)^{-1}f_{2}R(h)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(1,f_{2}(g)^{-1}Sf_{1}(h)\big),

since
(f_{1},f_{2})
is a homomophism of relative Rota–Baxter groups

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{S}\big(f_{1}(h),f_{2}(g)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{S}\,\widetilde{f}\big((h,g)\big).

It is now immediate to see that
\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{RRB}\to\mathcal{RB}
is a functor.
∎

In fact,
\mathcal{F}
maps equivalent extensions to equivalent extensions.

\Pi\big([\mathcal{E}]\big)=[\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}].

Proof.

Let

. Then the map

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}

\gamma
be the action associated to the extension
\mathcal{E}
. For
a\in A
,
b\in B
,
k\in K
and
l\in L
, we see that

\displaystyle\gamma_{(a,b)}(k,l)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s(a,b)^{-1}(k,l)s(a,b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(s_{H}(a),~s_{G}(b)\big)^{-1}(k,\,l)\big(s_{H}(a),~s_{G}(b)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big((\phi_{s_{G}(b)^{-1}}(s_{H}(a)))^{-1},~s_{G}(b)^{-1}\big)\big(k\,\phi_{l}(s_{H}(a)),~ls_{G}(b)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big((\phi_{s_{G}(b)^{-1}}(s_{H}(a)))^{-1}\phi_{s_{G}(b)^{-1}}(k\,\phi_{l}(s_{H}(a))),~s_{G}(b)^{-1}ls_{G}(b)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\phi_{s_{G}(b)^{-1}}\big(s_{H}(a)^{-1}k\,\phi_{l}(s_{H}(a))\big),~\sigma_{b}(l)\big),~\text{by \eqref{sigmaact}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\phi_{s_{G}(b)^{-1}}\big(s_{H}(a)^{-1}k\,s_{H}(a)s_{H}(a)^{-1}\phi_{l}(s_{H}(a))\big),~\sigma_{b}(l)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\phi_{s_{G}(b)^{-1}}\big(\mu_{a}(k)f(l,a)\big),~\sigma_{b}(l)\big),~\text{by Lemma \eqref{properties of f} and \eqref{muact}}

\displaystyle\big(\nu^{-1}_{b}(\mu_{a}(k)f(l,a)),~\sigma_{b}(l)\big),~\text{by \eqref{nuact}.}

of the induced extension

is completely determined by the action

of the extension
\mathcal{E}
. A direct check shows that equivalent extensions of relative Rota–Baxter groups map to equivalent extensions of Rota–Baxter groups. By
[

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\nu^{-1}_{b_{1}b_{2}}\big(\tau_{1}(a_{1},\beta_{b_{1}}(a_{2}))\rho(a_{2},b_{1})\big),\,\tau_{2}(b_{1},b_{2})\big).

Now, define the map
r\colon A\rtimes_{\beta}B\to K\times L
by

for all

and

. Then, we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle r(a,b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s\big(1,b^{-1}T(a)\big)^{-1}\,\widetilde{R}\big(s_{H}(a),s_{G}(b)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(1,s_{G}(b^{-1}T(a))\big)^{-1}\big(1,s_{G}(b)^{-1}R(s_{H}(a))\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(1,s_{G}(b^{-1}T(a))^{-1}\big)\big(1,s_{G}(b)^{-1}R(s_{H}(a))\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(1,s_{G}(b^{-1}T(a))^{-1}s_{G}(b)^{-1}R(s_{H}(a))\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(1,\tau_{2}(b^{-1},T(a))s_{G}(T(a))^{-1}s_{G}(b^{-1})^{-1}s_{G}(b)^{-1}R(s_{H}(a))\big),~\text{using \eqref{sigmacocycle}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(1,\tau_{2}(b^{-1},T(a))s_{G}(T(a))^{-1}\tau_{2}(b,b^{-1})^{-1}s_{G}(T(a))\chi(a)\big),

using Proposition (
2.17
)(2) and (
2.21
)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(1,\tau_{2}(b^{-1},T(a))\sigma_{T(a)}(\tau_{2}(b,b^{-1})^{-1})\chi(a)\big),~\text{using \eqref{sigmaact}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(1,\tau_{2}(b,b^{-1}T(a))^{-1}\chi(a)\big),~\text{using \eqref{cocycle2}.}

Next, we explore the relationship between
\operatorname{H}^{2}_{RRB}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{K})
and
\operatorname{H}^{2}_{RB}(A\rtimes_{\beta}B,K\times L)
(see Subsections
2.2
and
2.3
for the constructions). Let
\Gamma:\operatorname{H}^{2}_{RRB}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{K})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{(\nu,\mu,\sigma,f)}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{K})
and
\Lambda:\operatorname{Ext}_{\gamma}(A\rtimes_{\beta}B,K\times L)\longrightarrow\operatorname{H}^{2}_{RB}(A\rtimes_{\beta}B,K\times L)
be the bijections defined in Theorems
2.21
and
2.11
, respectively. Let
\Pi:\operatorname{Ext}_{(\nu,\mu,\sigma,f)}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{K})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{\gamma}(A\rtimes_{\beta}B,K\times L)
be the map defined in Corollary
3.3
. Then we have the following result.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(A,B,\beta,T)
be a relative Rota–Baxter group and
\mathcal{K}=(K,L,\alpha,S)
a module over
\mathcal{A}
with respect to the action
(\nu,\mu,\sigma,f)
. Then the map

\Omega_{RB}:=\Lambda\Pi\Gamma:\operatorname{H}^{2}_{RRB}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{K})\to\operatorname{H}^{2}_{RB}(A\rtimes_{\beta}B,K\times L)

is a homomorphism of groups.

Proof.

The map
\Omega_{RB}
is given by
\Omega_{RB}\big([\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\rho,\chi]\big)=[\tau^{(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\rho,\chi)},\,r^{(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\rho,\chi)}]
, where

\displaystyle\tau^{(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\rho,\chi)}\big((a_{1},b_{1}),(a_{2},b_{2})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\nu^{-1}_{b_{1}b_{2}}\big(\tau_{1}(a_{1},\beta_{b_{1}}(a_{2}))\rho(a_{2},b_{1})\big),\tau_{2}(b_{1},b_{2})\big),

\displaystyle r^{(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\rho,\chi)}(a,b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(1,\tau_{2}(b,b^{-1}T(a))^{-1}\chi(a)\big)

for all
a,a_{1},a_{2}\in A
and
b,b_{1},b_{2}\in B

[\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\rho,\chi]

[\tau^{\prime}_{1},\tau^{\prime}_{2},\rho^{\prime},\chi^{\prime}]

\operatorname{H}^{2}_{RRB}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{K})

\displaystyle\Omega_{RB}\big([\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\rho,\chi]\,[\tau^{\prime}_{1},\tau^{\prime}_{2},\rho^{\prime},\chi^{\prime}]\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Omega_{RB}\big([\tau_{1}\tau^{\prime}_{1},\tau_{2}\tau^{\prime}_{2},\rho\rho^{\prime},\chi\chi^{\prime}]\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big[\tau^{(\tau_{1}\tau^{\prime}_{1},\tau_{2}\tau^{\prime}_{2},\rho\rho^{\prime},\chi\chi^{\prime})},\,r^{(\tau_{1}\tau^{\prime}_{1},\tau_{2}\tau^{\prime}_{2},\rho\rho^{\prime},\chi\chi^{\prime})}\big].

It is easy to see that

\tau^{(\tau_{1}\tau^{\prime}_{1},\tau_{2}\tau^{\prime}_{2},\rho\rho^{\prime},\chi\chi^{\prime})}=\tau^{(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\rho,\chi)}\,\tau^{(\tau^{\prime}_{1},\tau^{\prime}_{2},\rho^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})}\quad\textrm{and}\quad r^{(\tau_{1}\tau^{\prime}_{1},\tau_{2}\tau^{\prime}_{2},\rho\rho^{\prime},\chi\chi^{\prime})}=r^{(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\rho,\chi)}\,r^{(\tau^{\prime}_{1},\tau^{\prime}_{2},\rho^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})}.

Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\Omega_{RB}\big([\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\rho,\chi][\tau^{\prime}_{1},\tau^{\prime}_{2},\rho^{\prime},\chi^{\prime}]\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big[\tau^{(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\rho,\chi)},r^{(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\rho,\chi)}\big]\big[\tau^{(\tau^{\prime}_{1},\tau^{\prime}_{2},\rho^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})},r^{(\tau^{\prime}_{1},\tau^{\prime}_{2},\rho^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})}\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Omega_{RB}\big([\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\rho,\chi]\big)\,\Omega_{RB}\big([\tau^{\prime}_{1},\tau^{\prime}_{2},\rho^{\prime},\chi^{\prime}]\big),

which is desired.

4.
From skew left braces to skew left braces on semi-direct products

It is known from
[
18
, Theorem 3.10]
that if
(H,\cdot,\circ)
is a skew left brace, then the quadruple
(H^{(\cdot)},H^{(\circ)},\lambda^{H},\operatorname{id}_{H})
is a relative Rota–Baxter group, where
\lambda^{H}:H^{(\circ)}\rightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(H^{(\cdot)})
is defined by

\lambda^{H}_{a}(b)=a^{-1}\cdot(a\circ b)

for all
a,b\in H
. Furthermore, the skew left brace induced by the relative Rota-Baxter group
(H^{(\cdot)},H^{(\circ)},\lambda^{H},\operatorname{id}_{H})
is the same as
(H,\cdot,\circ)
. Also, if
(H^{(\cdot)},H^{(\circ)},\lambda^{H},\operatorname{id}_{H})
is a relative Rota–Baxter group, then
(H,\cdot,\circ)
is a skew left brace with
\lambda^{H}
as its associated action.

Let
(H,\cdot,\circ)
be a skew left brace and let

\widetilde{H}=H^{(\cdot)}\rtimes_{\lambda^{H}}H^{(\circ)}

denote the associated semi-direct product. In view of Proposition
3.1
and Remark
2.13
, there is a skew left brace structure on
\widetilde{H}
induced by the Rota–Baxter group
(\widetilde{H},\widetilde{R})
, where

\widetilde{R}:\widetilde{H}\to\widetilde{H}
is given by

(4.1)

\widetilde{R}(h,g)=(1,g^{\dagger}\circ h)

for all
h,g\in H
. The additive group operation
\bullet
is given by

(h_{1},g_{1})\bullet(h_{2},g_{2})=\big(h_{1}\cdot\lambda^{H}_{g_{1}}(h_{2}),\,g_{1}\circ g_{2}\big),

and the multiplicative group operation
\odot
is given by

(h_{1},g_{1})\odot(h_{2},g_{2})=\big(h_{1}\circ h_{2},\,h_{1}\circ g_{2}\circ h_{1}^{\dagger}\circ g_{1}\big)

for all
g_{1},g_{2},h_{1},h_{2}\in H
.

Definition 4.1
.

Given a skew left brace
(H,\cdot,\circ)
, the skew left brace
(\widetilde{H},\bullet,\odot)
is called the square of
(H,\cdot,\circ)
.

Remark 4.2
.

By definition, the square of a skew left brace always arises from a Rota–Baxter group. Further, the associated action for
(\widetilde{H},\bullet,\odot)
is given by

\lambda^{\widetilde{H}}_{(h_{1},g_{1})}(h_{2},g_{2})=\widetilde{R}(h_{1},g_{1})\bullet(h_{2},g_{2})\bullet\big(\widetilde{R}(h_{1},g_{1})\big)^{-1}=(1,g_{1}^{\dagger}\circ h_{1})\bullet(h_{2},g_{2})\bullet(1,g_{1}^{\dagger}\circ h_{1})^{-1}.

Example 4.3
.

Let
(H,\cdot,\cdot)
be a trivial skew left brace. Then, we have

\displaystyle(h_{1},g_{1})\bullet(h_{2},g_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(h_{1}\cdot h_{2},\,g_{1}\cdot g_{2}\big)~\textrm{and}

\displaystyle(h_{1},g_{1})\odot(h_{2},g_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(h_{1}\cdot h_{2},\,h_{1}\cdot g_{2}\cdot h_{1}^{-1}\cdot g_{1}\big).

Thus, the square of a trivial skew left brace need not be a trivial skew left brace. However, the square of a trivial left brace is always trivial.

Recall the following definition from
[
21
, Corollary 3.38]
.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
(H,\cdot,\circ)
be a skew left brace such that

\lambda^{H}_{h}\lambda^{H}_{g}=\lambda^{H}_{\lambda^{H}_{h}(g)}\lambda^{H}_{h}

for all
g,h\in H
. Then
H\times H
admits a skew left brace structure, where

\displaystyle(h,g)\cdot(h^{\prime},g^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(h\cdot h^{\prime},\,g\cdot g^{\prime})~\textrm{and}

\displaystyle(h,g)\circ(h^{\prime},g^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(h\circ h^{\prime},\,g\circ\lambda^{H}_{h}(g^{\prime})\big).

This skew left brace is called the double of
(H,\cdot,\circ)
and is denoted by
(D(H),\cdot,\circ)
.

Remark 4.5
.

It follows from the definition that the double of a trivial skew left brace is always a trivial skew left brace. If
(H,\cdot,\circ)
is a skew left brace, then the associated action for the double
(D(H),\cdot,\circ)
is given by

\lambda^{D(H)}_{(h,g)}(h^{\prime},g^{\prime})=\big(\lambda^{H}_{h}(h^{\prime}),\,\lambda^{H}_{g\circ h}(g^{\prime})\big).

Thus, if
\lambda^{H}_{h}
is not an inner automorphism of
H^{(\cdot)}
for some
h
, then
\lambda^{D(H)}_{(h,g)}
is not an inner automorphism of
D(H)^{(\cdot)}
for all
(h,g)
. By (
2.11
), the necessary condition for a skew left brace to be induced from a Rota–Baxter operator is that its associated action must take values in the inner automorphism group of its additive group. Thus, if
\lambda^{H}_{h}
is not an inner automorphism of
H^{(\cdot)}
for some
h
, then by Remark
4.2
, the double
(D(H),\cdot,\circ)
and the square
(\widetilde{H},\bullet,\odot)
are distinct.

Example 4.6
.

Consider the skew left brace
(\mathbb{Z},+,\circ)
, where
n\circ m=n+(-1)^{n}m
. Note that
\lambda^{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}(m)=(-1)^{n}m
, and hence
\lambda^{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
is not an inner automorphism of
\mathbb{Z}^{(+)}
for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. For the square
(\widetilde{\mathbb{Z}},\bullet,\odot)
, we have

\displaystyle(n_{1},m_{1})\bullet(n_{2},m_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(n_{1}+(-1)^{m_{1}}n_{2},~m_{1}+(-1)^{m_{1}}m_{2}\big)~\textrm{and}

\displaystyle(n_{1},m_{1})\odot(n_{2},m_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(n_{1}+(-1)^{n_{1}}n_{2},~n_{1}+(-1)^{n_{1}}m_{2}+(-1)^{m_{2}}(m_{1}-n_{1})\big).

On the other hand, for the double
(D(\mathbb{Z}),\cdot,\circ)
, we have

\displaystyle(n_{1},m_{1})\cdot(n_{2},m_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n_{1}+n_{2},~m_{1}+m_{2})~\textrm{and}

\displaystyle(n_{1},m_{1})\circ(n_{2},m_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(n_{1}+(-1)^{n_{1}}n_{2},~m_{1}+(-1)^{n_{1}+m_{1}}m_{2}\big).

Thus, the square and the double constructions are distinct.

We prove that the association
(H,\cdot,\circ)\mapsto(\widetilde{H},\bullet,\odot)
is functorial.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
\mathcal{SB}
denote the category of skew left braces. Then the association
\mathcal{O}:\mathcal{SB}\to\mathcal{SB}
given by
\mathcal{O}((H,\cdot,\circ))=(\widetilde{H},\bullet,\odot)
is a covariant functor.

Proof.

Let
f\colon(H,\cdot,\circ)\to(G,\cdot,\circ)
be a morphism of skew left braces. Define
\widetilde{f}:(\widetilde{H},\bullet,\odot)\to(\widetilde{G},\bullet,\odot)
by

\widetilde{f}\big((h,g)\big)=\big(f(h),f(g)\big)

for all
g,h\in H
. We claim that
\widetilde{f}
is a morphism of skew left braces. Indeed, for
g,g^{\prime},h,h^{\prime}\in H
, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}\big((h,g)\bullet(h^{\prime},g^{\prime})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}\big((h\cdot\lambda^{H}_{g}(h^{\prime}),~g\circ g^{\prime})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(f(h\cdot\lambda^{H}_{g}(h^{\prime})),~f(g\circ g^{\prime})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(f(h)\cdot f(\lambda^{H}_{g}(h^{\prime})),~f(g)\circ f(g^{\prime})\big),~\text{since $f$ is a homomorphism of skew left braces}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(f(h)\,\lambda^{G}_{f(g)}(f(h^{\prime})),~f(g)\circ f(g^{\prime})\big),~\text{since $f$ is a homomorphism of skew left braces}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(f(h),f(g)\big)\bullet\big(f(h^{\prime}),f(g^{\prime})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}\big((h,g)\big)\bullet\widetilde{f}\big((h^{\prime},g^{\prime})\big)

and

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}\big((h,g)\odot(h^{\prime},g^{\prime})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}\big((h\circ h^{\prime},~h\circ g^{\prime}\circ h^{\dagger}\circ g)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(f(h\circ h^{\prime}),~f(h\circ g^{\prime}\circ h^{\dagger}\circ g)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(f(h),f(g)\big)\odot\big(f(h^{\prime}),f(g^{\prime})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}\big((h,g)\big)\odot{f}\big((h^{\prime},g^{\prime})\big).

It is now immediate to see that
\mathcal{O}:\mathcal{SB}\to\mathcal{SB}
is a functor.
∎

In fact,
\mathcal{O}
maps equivalent extensions to equivalent extensions.

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
(H,\cdot,\circ)
be a skew left brace and
(I,\cdot)
an abelian group viewed as a trivial brace. Let

\mathcal{E}:\quad{\bf 1}\longrightarrow(I,\cdot)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\longrightarrow}}(E,\cdot,\circ)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}(H,\cdot,\circ)\longrightarrow{\bf 1}

be an extension of skew left braces with associated action
(\xi,\zeta,\epsilon)
and

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}:\quad{\bf 1}\longrightarrow(\widetilde{I},\cdot)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\widetilde{i}}}{{\longrightarrow}}(\widetilde{E},\bullet,\odot)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\widetilde{\pi}}}{{\longrightarrow}}(\widetilde{H},\bullet,\odot)\longrightarrow{\bf 1}

the induced extension of skew left braces with associated action
(\widetilde{\xi},\widetilde{\zeta},\widetilde{\epsilon})
. Then there is a map

\Theta:\operatorname{Ext}_{(\xi,\zeta,\epsilon)}(H,I)\to\operatorname{Ext}_{(\widetilde{\xi},\widetilde{\zeta},\widetilde{\epsilon})}(\widetilde{H},\widetilde{I})
given by
\Theta\big([\mathcal{E}]\big)=[\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}]
.

Proof.

Note that, since
I
is a trivial brace,
\widetilde{I}=I\times I
is again a trivial brace. A direct check shows that if

\mathcal{E}:\quad{\bf 1}\longrightarrow(I,\cdot)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\longrightarrow}}(E,\cdot,\circ)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}(H,\cdot,\circ)\longrightarrow{\bf 1}

is an abelian extension of skew left braces, then the induced sequence

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}:\quad{\bf 1}\longrightarrow(\widetilde{I},\cdot)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\widetilde{i}}}{{\longrightarrow}}(\widetilde{E},\bullet,\odot)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\widetilde{\pi}}}{{\longrightarrow}}(\widetilde{H},\bullet,\odot)\longrightarrow{\bf 1}

of skew left braces is also exact. Let
s
be a set-theoretic section to
\mathcal{E}
. Then,
\widetilde{s}(h,g)=(s(h),s(g))
is a set-theoretic section to
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}
. Let
(\widetilde{\xi},\widetilde{\zeta},\widetilde{\epsilon})
be the associated action of
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}
. For
a,b\in I
and
h,g\in H
, we see that

(4.2)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\xi}_{(h,g)}(a,b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{\widetilde{E}}_{(s(h),s(g))}(a,b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(1,s(g)^{\dagger}\circ s(h)\big)\bullet(a,b)\bullet\big(1,s(h)^{\dagger}\circ s(g)\big),~\text{by definition of $\lambda^{\widetilde{E}}_{(s(h),s(g))}$}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(1,s(g)^{\dagger}\circ s(h)\big)\bullet\big(a,b\circ s(h)^{\dagger}\circ s(g)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\lambda^{E}_{s(g)^{\dagger}}(\lambda^{E}_{s(h)}(a)),~s(g)^{\dagger}\circ s(h)\circ b\circ s(h)^{\dagger}\circ s(g)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\xi^{-1}_{g}(\xi_{h}(a)),~\epsilon_{g}(s(h)\circ b\circ s(h)^{\dagger})\big),~\text{by \eqref{action1 sb} and \eqref{action3 sb}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\xi^{-1}_{g}(\xi_{h}(a)),~\epsilon_{g}(\epsilon^{-1}_{h}(b))\big),~\text{using \eqref{action3 sb}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\xi_{(g^{\dagger}\circ h)}(a),~\epsilon_{(h^{\dagger}\circ g)}(b)\big),~\text{since $\xi$ is a homomorphism and $\epsilon$ is an anti-homomorphism.}

To compute
\widetilde{\zeta}
, we see that

(4.3)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\zeta}_{(h,g)}(a,b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\widetilde{s}(h,g))^{-1}\bullet(a,b)\bullet\widetilde{s}(h,g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(s(h),s(g))^{-1}\bullet(a,b)\bullet(s(h),s(g))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\lambda^{E}_{s(g)^{\dagger}}(s(h))^{-1},s(g)^{\dagger}\big)\bullet\big(a\cdot\lambda^{E}_{b}(s(h)),b\circ s(g)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\lambda^{E}_{s(g)^{\dagger}}(s(h)^{-1}\cdot a\cdot\lambda^{E}_{b}(s(h))),~s(g)^{\dagger}\circ b\circ s(g)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\lambda^{E}_{s(g)^{\dagger}}(s(h)^{-1}\cdot a\cdot s(h)\cdot s(h)^{-1}\cdot\lambda^{E}_{b}(s(h))),~\epsilon_{g}(b)\big),~\textrm{by}~\eqref{action3 sb}.

Note that,

\displaystyle s(h)^{-1}\cdot\lambda^{E}_{b}(s(h))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s(h)^{-1}\cdot\big(b^{-1}\cdot(b\circ s(h))\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s(h)^{-1}\cdot b^{-1}\cdot s(h)\cdot\lambda^{E}_{s(h)}\big(s(h)^{\dagger}\circ b\circ s(h)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\zeta_{h}(b^{-1})\,\xi_{h}\big(\epsilon_{h}(b)\big),\text{using \eqref{action1 sb}, \eqref{action2 sb} and \eqref{action3 sb}}.

Using the above equation in (
4.3
), we get

(4.4)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\zeta}_{(h,g)}(a,b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\lambda^{E}_{s(g)^{\dagger}}(\zeta_{h}(a)\zeta_{h}(b^{-1})\xi_{h}(\epsilon_{h}(b))),~\epsilon_{g}(b)\big),~\text{using \eqref{action2 sb}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\xi^{-1}_{g}\big(\zeta_{h}(a)\zeta_{h}(b^{-1})\xi_{h}(\epsilon_{h}(b))\big),~\epsilon_{g}(b)\big),~\text{using \eqref{action1 sb}.}

Similarly, to determine
\widetilde{\epsilon}
, we have

(4.5)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\epsilon}_{(h,g)}(a,b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\widetilde{s}(h,g))^{\dagger}\odot(a,b)\odot\widetilde{s}(h,g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(s(h),s(g))^{\dagger}\odot(a,b)\odot(s(h),s(g))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(s(h)^{\dagger},s(h)^{\dagger}\circ s(g)^{\dagger}\circ s(h)\big)\odot\big(a\circ s(h),a\circ s(g)\circ a^{\dagger}\circ b\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(s(h)^{\dagger}\circ a\circ s(h),~s(h)^{\dagger}\circ a\circ s(g)\circ a^{\dagger}\circ b\circ s(g)^{\dagger}\circ s(h)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\epsilon_{h}(a),s(h)^{\dagger}\circ a\circ\epsilon^{-1}_{g}(a^{\dagger}\circ b)\circ s(h)\big),~\text{using \eqref{action3 sb}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\epsilon_{h}(a),~\epsilon_{h}(a\circ\epsilon^{-1}_{g}(a^{\dagger}\circ b))\big).

Hence, we conclude that the associated action
(\widetilde{\xi},\widetilde{\zeta},\widetilde{\epsilon})
of the induced skew left brace is completely determined by the action
(\xi,\zeta,\epsilon)
of the given skew left brace. A direct check shows that equivalent extensions map to equivalent extensions. By
[
20
, Proposition 3.4]
, equivalent extensions induce identical associated actions, and the result follows.
∎

Let
(H,\cdot,\circ)
be a skew left brace and
I
be an abelian group viewed as a trivial brace. We show that there exists a group homomorphism
\operatorname{H}^{2}_{SB}(H,I)\to\operatorname{H}^{2}_{SB}(\widetilde{H},\widetilde{I})
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be an extension of
H
by
I
and
s
be a set-theoretic section to
\mathcal{E}
. Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}
be the induced extention of
\widetilde{H}
by
\widetilde{I}
and
\widetilde{s}
given by
\widetilde{s}(h,g)=(s(h),s(g))
be the induced set-theoretic section to
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}
. Define
\tau^{(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})}\colon\widetilde{H}\times\widetilde{H}\to\widetilde{I}
by

\tau^{(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})}\big((h_{1},g_{1}),(h_{2},g_{2})\big)=\widetilde{s}\big((h_{1},g_{1})\bullet(h_{2},g_{2})\big)^{-1}\bullet\widetilde{s}(h_{1},g_{1})\bullet\widetilde{s}(h_{2},g_{2})

for all
g_{1},g_{2},h_{1},h_{2}\in H
. Then, we have

(4.6)

∎

6.
Isoclinism of squares of skew left braces

Recall that the
annihilator

\operatorname{Ann}(H)
of a skew left brace
(H,\cdot,\circ)
is defined as

\operatorname{Ann}(H)=\ker(\lambda^{H})\cap\operatorname{Z}(H^{(\cdot)})\cap\operatorname{Fix}(\lambda^{H})=\{a\in H\mid b\circ a=a\circ b=b\cdot a=a\cdot b~\textrm{for all}~b\in H\}.

Similarly, the
commutator

H^{\prime}
of a skew left brace
(H,\cdot,\circ)
is defined as the subgroup of
H^{(\cdot)}
generated by the commutator subgroup of
H^{(\cdot)}
together with the set
\{a^{-1}\cdot(a\circ b)\cdot b^{-1}\mid a,b\in H\}
. It turns out that both the annihilator
\operatorname{Ann}(H)
and the commutator
H^{\prime}
are ideals of
(H,\cdot,\circ)
. We now recall the definition of isoclinism of skew left braces from
[
17
, Definition 2.7]
.

Definition 6.1
.

Two skew left braces
A
and
B
are said to be isoclinic if there exist skew brace isomorphisms
\xi_{1}:A/\operatorname{Ann}(A)\to B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)
and
\xi_{2}:A^{\prime}\to B^{\prime}
such that the diagram

(6.1)

\displaystyle\begin{CD}A^{\prime}@<{\theta_{A}}<{}<\big(A/\operatorname{Ann}(A)\big)\times\big(A/\operatorname{Ann}(A)\big)@>{\theta^{*}_{A}}>{}>A^{\prime}\\
@V{\xi_{2}}V{}V@V{\xi_{1}\times\xi_{1}}V{}V@V{\xi_{2}}V{}V\\
B^{\prime}@<{\theta_{B}}<{}<\big(B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)\big)\times\big(B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)\big)@>{\theta^{*}_{B}}>{}>B^{\prime}\end{CD}

commutes. Here, the maps
\theta_{A},\theta^{*}_{A}:(A/\operatorname{Ann}(A))\times(A/\operatorname{Ann}(A))\rightarrow A^{\prime}
are defined by

\theta_{A}(\overline{a},\overline{b})=a\cdot b\cdot a^{-1}\cdot b^{-1}\text{ and }\theta^{*}_{A}(\overline{a},\overline{b})=\lambda^{H}_{a}(b)\cdot b^{-1}

for all
a,b\in A.
The maps
\theta_{B}
and
\theta^{*}_{B}
are defined similarly.

The result below will be of use in what follows
[
19
, Proposition 3.4 and Theorem 3.7]
.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
(H,\cdot,\circ)
be a skew left brace and
(\widetilde{H},\bullet,\odot)
be its square. Then the following assertions hold:

(1)

\operatorname{Z}(\widetilde{H}^{(\bullet)})\trianglelefteq\operatorname{Fix}(\lambda^{H})\times\operatorname{Z}(H^{(\circ)})
.

(2)

(\widetilde{H}^{(\bullet)})^{\prime}=H^{\prime}\rtimes_{\lambda^{H}}(H^{(\circ)})^{\prime}.

The following observations are direct.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
(H,\cdot,\circ)
be a skew left brace and
(\widetilde{H},\bullet,\odot)
be its square. Then the following assertions hold :

(1)

\operatorname{Ann}(H)\times\operatorname{Ann}(H)\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}(\widetilde{H})
.

(2)

\operatorname{Ann}(H)\times\operatorname{Ann}(H)
is an ideal of
(\widetilde{H},\bullet,\odot)
.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
(H,\cdot,\circ)
be a skew left brace and
(\widetilde{H},\bullet,\odot)
be its square. Then the following assertions hold:

(1)

\operatorname{Ann}(\widetilde{H})\leq\operatorname{Fix}(\lambda^{H})\times\operatorname{Z}(H^{(\circ)})
.

(2)

\widetilde{H}/\big(\operatorname{Ann}(H)\times\operatorname{Ann}(H)\big)\cong\big(\widetilde{H/\operatorname{Ann}(H)}\big)
.

Proof.

By definition, we have
\operatorname{Ann}(\widetilde{H})=\operatorname{ker}(\lambda^{\widetilde{H}})\cap\operatorname{Z}(\widetilde{H}^{(\bullet)})\cap\operatorname{Fix}(\lambda^{\widetilde{H}})
. By Proposition
6.2
(1), we have
\operatorname{Z}(\widetilde{H}^{(\bullet)})\trianglelefteq\operatorname{Fix}(\lambda^{H})\times\operatorname{Z}(H^{(\circ)})
, and assertion (1) follows.

Let
\overline{\lambda}
be the induced action for
H/\operatorname{Ann}(H)
. It is easy to see that the map

\phi:\widetilde{H}/\big(\operatorname{Ann}(H)\times\operatorname{Ann}(H)\big)\to\big(\widetilde{H/\operatorname{Ann}(H)}\big),

given by
\phi(\overline{(a,b)})=(\overline{a},\overline{b})
for all
a,b\in H
, is an isomorphism of skew left braces
[
19
, Remark 3.6]
.
∎

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
(A,\cdot,\circ)
and
(B,\cdot,\circ)
be isoclinic skew left braces via an isoclinism
(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})
. Then the following assertions hold:

(1)

There exists a skew brace isomorphism

\widetilde{\xi_{1}}:\widetilde{A}/\big(\operatorname{Ann}(A)\times\operatorname{Ann}(A)\big)\to\widetilde{B}/\big(\operatorname{Ann}(B)\times\operatorname{Ann}(B)\big)

defined as
\widetilde{\xi_{1}}\big(\overline{a}_{1},\overline{a}_{2}\big)=\big(\xi_{1}(\overline{a}_{1}),\xi_{1}(\overline{a}_{2})\big).

(2)

There exists a skew brace isomorphism

\widetilde{\xi_{2}}:(\widetilde{A})^{\prime}\to(\widetilde{B})^{\prime}

defined as
\widetilde{\xi_{2}}(a_{1},a_{2})=(\xi_{2}(a_{1}),\xi_{2}(a_{2}))
.

Proof.

In view of Lemma
6.4
(2), we identify

\widetilde{A}/(\operatorname{Ann}(A)\times\operatorname{Ann}(A))
with
\big(\widetilde{A/\operatorname{Ann}(A)}\big)
, and similarly for
B
. It now follows that the map
\widetilde{\xi_{1}}(\overline{a}_{1},\overline{a}_{2})=(\xi_{1}(\overline{a}_{1}),\xi_{1}(\overline{a}_{2}))
is an isomorphism.

By Proposition
6.2
(2), we have
(\widetilde{A}^{(\bullet)})^{\prime}=A^{\prime}\rtimes_{\lambda^{A}}(A^{(\circ)})^{\prime}
and
(\widetilde{B}^{(\bullet)})^{\prime}=B^{\prime}\rtimes_{\lambda^{B}}(B^{(\circ)})^{\prime}
. By
[
19
, Theorem 3.13]
, the isomorphism
\xi_{2}:A^{\prime}\to B^{\prime}
induces an isomorphism
\widetilde{\xi_{2}}:(\widetilde{A}^{(\bullet)})^{\prime}\to(\widetilde{B}^{(\bullet)})^{\prime}
of groups, given by
\widetilde{\xi_{2}}(a_{1},a_{2})=(\xi_{2}(a_{1}),\xi_{2}(a_{2}))
. A direct check shows that
\widetilde{\xi_{2}}
restricts to an isomorphism
(\widetilde{A})^{\prime}\to(\widetilde{B})^{\prime}
.
∎

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
(A,\cdot,\circ)
and
(B,\cdot,\circ)
be isoclinic skew left braces. If
\operatorname{Ann}(\widetilde{A})=\operatorname{Ann}(A)\times\operatorname{Ann}(A)
and
\operatorname{Ann}(\widetilde{B})=\operatorname{Ann}(B)\times\operatorname{Ann}(B)
, then
(\widetilde{A},\bullet,\odot)
and
(\widetilde{B},\bullet,\odot)
are also isoclinic.

Proof.

Let
(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})
be an isoclinism from
(A,\cdot,\circ)
onto
(B,\cdot,\circ)
. We claim that the following diagram commutes

(6.2)

\displaystyle\begin{CD}(\widetilde{A})^{\prime}@<{\theta_{\widetilde{A}}}<{}<\big(\widetilde{A}/\operatorname{Ann}(\widetilde{A})\big)\times\big(\widetilde{A}/\operatorname{Ann}(\widetilde{A})\big)@>{\theta^{*}_{\widetilde{A}}}>{}>(\widetilde{A})^{\prime}\\
@V{\widetilde{\xi_{2}}}V{}V@V{\widetilde{\xi_{1}}\times\widetilde{\xi_{1}}}V{}V@V{\widetilde{\xi_{2}}}V{}V\\
(\widetilde{B})^{\prime}@<{\theta_{\widetilde{B}}}<{}<\big(\widetilde{B}/\operatorname{Ann}(\widetilde{B})\big)\times\big(\widetilde{B}/\operatorname{Ann}(\widetilde{B})\big)@>{\theta^{*}_{\widetilde{B}}}>{}>(\widetilde{B})^{\prime},\end{CD}

where the maps
\theta_{\widetilde{A}},\theta^{*}_{\widetilde{A}}:\big(\widetilde{A}/\operatorname{Ann}(\widetilde{A})\big)\times\big(\widetilde{A}/\operatorname{Ann}(\widetilde{A})\big)\rightarrow(\widetilde{A})^{\prime}
are defined by

\theta_{\widetilde{A}}\big((\overline{a}_{1},\overline{b}_{1}),(\overline{a}_{2},\overline{b}_{2})\big)=(a_{1},b_{1})\bullet(a_{2},b_{2})\bullet(a_{1},b_{1})^{-1}\bullet(a_{2},b_{2})^{-1}

and

\theta^{*}_{\widetilde{A}}\big((\overline{a}_{1},\overline{b}_{1}),(\overline{a}_{2},\overline{b}_{2})\big)=(1,b_{1}^{\dagger}\circ a_{1})\bullet(a_{2},b_{2})\bullet(1,b_{1}^{\dagger}\circ a_{1})^{-1}\bullet(a_{2},b_{2})^{-1}

for all
a_{1},b_{1},a_{2},b_{2}\in A.
The maps
\theta_{\widetilde{B}}
and
\theta^{*}_{\widetilde{B}}
are defined similarly.

First, we prove the commutativity of the left hand side of the diagram. To avoid complexity of the notation, we write
\xi_{1}
to also denote the composition
\xi_{1}:A\to A/\operatorname{Ann}(A)\to B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)
, where
A\to A/\operatorname{Ann}(A)
is the canonical map. We see that

(6.3)

\displaystyle\theta_{\widetilde{A}}\big((\overline{a}_{1},\overline{b}_{1}),(\overline{a}_{2},\overline{b}_{2})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a_{1},b_{1})\bullet(a_{2},b_{2})\bullet(a_{1},b_{1})^{-1}\bullet(a_{2},b_{2})^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(a_{1}\lambda^{A}_{b_{1}}(a_{2}),\,b_{1}\circ b_{2}\big)\bullet\big(\lambda^{A}_{b_{1}^{\dagger}}(a_{1})^{-1},\,b_{1}^{\dagger}\big)\bullet\big(\lambda^{A}_{b_{2}^{\dagger}}(a_{2})^{-1},\,b_{2}^{\dagger}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(a_{1}\lambda^{A}_{b_{1}}(a_{2}),\,b_{1}\circ b_{2}\big)\bullet\big(\lambda^{A}_{b_{1}^{\dagger}}(a_{1})^{-1}\lambda^{A}_{b_{1}}(\lambda^{A}_{b_{2}^{\dagger}}(a_{2})^{-1}),\,b_{1}^{\dagger}\circ b_{2}^{\dagger}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(a_{1}\lambda^{A}_{b_{1}}(a_{2})\lambda^{A}_{b_{1}\circ b_{2}\circ b_{1}^{\dagger}\circ b_{2}^{\dagger}}(\lambda_{b_{2}}(a_{1})^{-1}a_{2}^{-1}),\,b_{1}\circ b_{2}\circ b_{1}^{\dagger}\circ b_{2}^{\dagger}\big).

Thus, we have

(6.4)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\xi_{2}}\,\theta_{\widetilde{A}}\big((\overline{a}_{1},\overline{b}_{1}),\,(\overline{a}_{2},\overline{b}_{2})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{\xi_{2}}\big(a_{1}\lambda^{A}_{b_{1}}(a_{2})\lambda^{A}_{b_{1}\circ b_{2}\circ b_{1}^{\dagger}\circ b_{2}^{\dagger}}(\lambda_{b_{2}}(a_{1})^{-1}a_{2}^{-1}),~b_{1}\circ b_{2}\circ b_{1}^{\dagger}\circ b_{2}^{\dagger}\big),~~\text{using \eqref{theta left}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\xi_{2}(a_{1}\lambda^{A}_{b_{1}}(a_{2})\lambda^{A}_{b_{1}\circ b_{2}\circ b_{1}^{\dagger}\circ b_{2}^{\dagger}}(\lambda_{b_{2}}(a_{1})^{-1}a_{2}^{-1})),~\xi_{2}(b_{1}\circ b_{2}\circ b_{1}^{\dagger}\circ b_{2}^{\dagger})\big).

The isoclinism
(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})
from
(A,\cdot,\circ)
onto
(B,\cdot,\circ)
gives

(6.5)

\displaystyle\xi_{2}(a\cdot b\cdot a^{-1}\cdot b^{-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi_{1}(a)\cdot\xi_{1}(b)\cdot\xi_{1}(a)^{-1}\cdot\xi_{1}(b)^{-1}~\textrm{and}

(6.6)

\displaystyle\lambda^{B}_{\xi_{1}(a)}\big(\xi_{1}(b)\big)\cdot\big(\xi_{1}(b)\big)^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi_{2}\big(\lambda^{A}_{a}(b)\cdot b^{-1}\big)

for all
a,b\in A
. We compute the first coordinate of (
6.4
) and obtain

\displaystyle\xi_{2}(a_{1}\lambda^{A}_{b_{1}}(a_{2})\lambda^{A}_{b_{1}\circ b_{2}\circ b_{1}^{\dagger}\circ b_{2}^{\dagger}}(\lambda_{b_{2}}(a_{1})^{-1}a_{2}^{-1}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi_{2}(a_{1}\lambda^{A}_{b_{1}}(a_{2})(\lambda^{A}_{b_{1}\circ b_{2}\circ b_{1}^{\dagger}\circ b_{2}^{\dagger}}(a_{2}\lambda^{A}_{b_{2}}(a_{1})))^{-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi_{2}(a_{1}\lambda^{A}_{b_{1}}(a_{2})(a_{2}\lambda^{A}_{b_{2}}(a_{1}))^{-1}~a_{2}\lambda^{A}_{b_{2}}(a_{1})~(\lambda^{A}_{b_{1}\circ b_{2}\circ b_{1}^{\dagger}\circ b_{2}^{\dagger}}(a_{2}\lambda^{A}_{b_{2}}(a_{1})))^{-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi_{2}\big(a_{1}\lambda^{A}_{b_{1}}(a_{2})(a_{2}\lambda^{A}_{b_{2}}(a_{1}))^{-1}\big)~\xi_{2}\big(a_{2}\lambda^{A}_{b_{2}}(a_{1})(\lambda^{A}_{b_{1}\circ b_{2}\circ b_{1}^{\dagger}\circ b_{2}^{\dagger}}(a_{2}\lambda^{A}_{b_{2}}(a_{1})))^{-1}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi_{2}\big(a_{1}\lambda^{A}_{b_{1}}(a_{2})a_{1}^{-1}\lambda^{A}_{b_{1}}(a_{2})^{-1}~\lambda^{A}_{b_{1}}(a_{2})a_{1}\lambda^{A}_{b_{2}^{\dagger}}(a_{2}\lambda^{A}_{b_{2}}(a_{1}))^{-1}~\lambda^{A}_{b_{2}^{\dagger}}(a_{2}\lambda^{A}_{b_{2}}(a_{1}))(a_{2}\lambda^{A}_{b_{2}}(a_{1}))^{-1}\big)

\displaystyle\xi_{1}(a_{2})\lambda^{B}_{\xi_{1}(b_{2})}(\xi_{1}(a_{1}))(\lambda^{B}_{\xi_{1}(b_{1}\circ b_{2}\circ b_{1}^{\dagger}\circ b_{2}^{\dagger})}(\xi_{1}(a_{2})\lambda^{B}_{\xi_{1}(b_{2})}(\xi_{1}(a_{1}))))^{-1},~~\text{using \eqref{sbisoclinism xi2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi_{2}\big(a_{1}\lambda^{A}_{b_{1}}(a_{2})a_{1}^{-1}\lambda^{A}_{b_{1}}(a_{2})^{-1}\big)~\xi_{2}\big(\lambda^{A}_{b_{1}}(a_{2})a_{1}\lambda^{A}_{b_{2}^{\dagger}}(a_{2}\lambda^{A}_{b_{2}}(a_{1}))^{-1}\big)~\xi_{2}\big(\lambda^{A}_{b_{2}^{\dagger}}(a_{2}\lambda^{A}_{b_{2}}(a_{1}))(a_{2}\lambda^{A}_{b_{2}}(a_{1}))^{-1}\big)

\displaystyle\xi_{1}(a_{2})\lambda^{B}_{\xi_{1}(b_{2})}(\xi_{1}(a_{1}))(\lambda^{B}_{\xi_{1}(b_{1})\circ\xi_{1}(b_{2})\circ\xi_{1}(b_{1})^{\dagger}\circ\xi_{1}(b_{2})^{\dagger}}(\xi_{1}(a_{2})\lambda^{B}_{\xi_{1}(b_{2})}(\xi_{1}(a_{1}))))^{-1},~~\text{by \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{MR4698318}{}{}, Proposition 3.9]}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi_{1}(a_{1})\lambda^{B}_{\xi_{1}(b_{1})}(\xi_{1}(a_{2}))\xi_{1}(a_{1})^{-1}\lambda^{B}_{\xi_{1}(b_{1})}(\xi_{1}(a_{2}))^{-1}~

\displaystyle\xi_{2}\big(\lambda^{A}_{b_{1}}(a_{2})a_{2}^{-1}a_{2}\lambda^{A}_{b_{2}^{\dagger}}(a_{2})^{-1}\big)~\lambda^{B}_{\xi_{1}(b_{2})^{\dagger}}(\xi_{1}(a_{2})\lambda^{B}_{\xi_{1}(b_{2})}(\xi_{1}(a_{1})))(\xi_{1}(a_{2})\lambda^{B}_{\xi_{1}(b_{2})}(\xi_{1}(a_{1})))^{-1}

\displaystyle\xi_{1}(a_{2})\lambda^{B}_{\xi_{1}(b_{2})}(\xi_{1}(a_{1}))(\lambda^{B}_{\xi_{1}(b_{1})\circ\xi_{1}(b_{2})\circ\xi_{1}(b_{1})^{\dagger}\circ\xi_{1}(b_{2})^{\dagger}}(\xi_{1}(a_{2})\lambda^{B}_{\xi_{1}(b_{2})}(\xi_{1}(a_{1}))))^{-1},~~\text{using \eqref{sbisoclinism xi1} and \eqref{sbisoclinism xi2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi_{1}(a_{1})\lambda^{B}_{\xi_{1}(b_{1})}(\xi_{1}(a_{2}))\xi_{1}(a_{1})^{-1}\lambda^{B}_{\xi_{1}(b_{1})}(\xi_{1}(a_{2}))^{-1}~

\displaystyle\lambda^{B}_{\xi_{1}(b_{1})}(\xi_{1}(a_{2}))\xi_{1}(a_{2})^{-1}\xi_{1}(a_{2})\lambda^{B}_{\xi_{1}(b_{2})^{\dagger}}(\xi_{1}(a_{2}))^{-1}~\lambda^{B}_{\xi_{1}(b_{2})^{\dagger}}(\xi_{1}(a_{2})\lambda^{B}_{\xi_{1}(b_{2})}(\xi_{1}(a_{1})))(\xi_{1}(a_{2})\lambda^{B}_{\xi_{1}(b_{2})}(\xi_{1}(a_{1})))^{-1}

\displaystyle\xi_{1}(a_{2})\lambda^{B}_{\xi_{1}(b_{2})}(\xi_{1}(a_{1}))(\lambda^{B}_{\xi_{1}(b_{1})\circ\xi_{1}(b_{2})\circ\xi_{1}(b_{1})^{\dagger}\circ\xi_{1}(b_{2})^{\dagger}}(\xi_{1}(a_{2})\lambda^{B}_{\xi_{1}(b_{2})}(\xi_{1}(a_{1}))))^{-1},~~\text{using \eqref{sbisoclinism xi2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi_{1}(a_{1})\lambda^{B}_{\xi_{1}(b_{1})}(\xi_{1}(a_{2}))\lambda^{B}_{\xi_{1}(b_{1})\circ\xi_{1}(b_{2})\circ\xi_{1}(b_{1})^{\dagger}\circ\xi_{1}(b_{2})^{\dagger}}(\lambda^{B}_{\xi_{1}(b_{2})}(\xi_{1}(a_{1}))^{-1}\xi_{1}(a_{2})^{-1}).

Using this in (
6.4
), we get

\displaystyle\widetilde{\xi_{2}}\,\theta_{\widetilde{A}}\big((\overline{a}_{1},\overline{b}_{1}),\,(\overline{a}_{2},\overline{b}_{2})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\xi_{2}(a_{1}\lambda^{A}_{b_{1}}(a_{2})\lambda^{A}_{b_{1}\circ b_{2}\circ b_{1}^{\dagger}\circ b_{2}^{\dagger}}(\lambda_{b_{2}}(a_{1})^{-1}a_{2}^{-1})),\xi_{2}(b_{1}\circ b_{2}\circ b_{1}^{\dagger}\circ b_{2}^{\dagger})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\xi_{1}(a_{1})\lambda^{B}_{\xi_{1}(b_{1})}(\xi_{1}(a_{2}))\lambda^{B}_{\xi_{1}(b_{1})\circ\xi_{1}(b_{2})\circ\xi_{1}(b_{1})^{\dagger}\circ\xi_{1}(b_{2})^{\dagger}}(\lambda^{B}_{\xi_{1}(b_{2})}(\xi_{1}(a_{1}))^{-1}\xi_{1}(a_{2})^{-1}),

\displaystyle\xi_{1}(b_{1})\circ\xi_{1}(b_{2})\circ\xi_{1}(b_{1})^{\dagger}\circ\xi_{1}(b_{2})^{\dagger}\big),~~\text{by \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{MR4698318}{}{}, Proposition 3.9]}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\xi_{1}(a_{1}),\xi_{1}(b_{1}))\bullet(\xi_{1}(a_{2}),\xi_{1}(b_{2}))\bullet(\xi_{1}(a_{1}),\xi_{1}(b_{1}))^{-1}\bullet(\xi_{1}(a_{2}),\xi_{1}(b_{2}))^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta_{\widetilde{B}}(\widetilde{\xi_{1}}\times\widetilde{\xi_{1}})\big((\overline{a}_{1},\overline{b}_{1}),(\overline{a}_{1},\overline{b}_{1})\big),~~\text{using Lemma \eqref{annihilator lemma}(2)}.

This shows that the left side of the diagram commutes. For the right hand diagram, observe that

(6.7)

\displaystyle\theta^{*}_{\widetilde{A}}\big((\overline{a}_{1},\overline{b}_{1}),(\overline{a}_{2},\overline{b}_{2})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(1,b_{1}^{\dagger}\circ a_{1}\big)\bullet\big(a_{2},b_{2}\big)\bullet\big(1,b_{1}^{\dagger}\circ a_{1}\big)^{-1}\bullet\big(a_{2},b_{2}\big)^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\lambda^{A}_{b_{1}^{\dagger}\circ a_{1}}(a_{2}),~b_{1}^{\dagger}\circ a_{1}\circ b_{2}\big)\bullet\big(\lambda^{A}_{a_{1}^{\dagger}\circ b_{1}}(\lambda^{A}_{b_{2}^{\dagger}}(a_{2}^{-1})),~a_{1}^{\dagger}\circ b_{1}\circ b_{2}^{\dagger}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\lambda^{A}_{b_{1}^{\dagger}\circ a_{1}}(a_{2})\cdot\lambda^{A}_{b_{1}^{\dagger}\circ a_{1}\circ b_{2}}(\lambda^{A}_{a_{1}^{\dagger}\circ b_{1}\circ b_{2}^{\dagger}}(a_{2}^{-1})),~b_{1}^{\dagger}\circ a_{1}\circ b_{2}\circ a_{1}^{\dagger}\circ b_{1}\circ b_{2}^{\dagger}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\lambda^{A}_{b_{1}^{\dagger}\circ a_{1}}(a_{2})\cdot\lambda^{A}_{b_{1}^{\dagger}\circ a_{1}\circ b_{2}\circ a_{1}^{\dagger}\circ b_{1}\circ b_{2}^{\dagger}}(a_{2}^{-1}),~b_{1}^{\dagger}\circ a_{1}\circ b_{2}\circ a_{1}^{\dagger}\circ b_{1}\circ b_{2}^{\dagger}\big).

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{\xi_{2}}\,\theta^{*}_{\widetilde{A}}\big((\overline{a}_{1},\overline{b}_{1}),\,(\overline{a}_{2},\overline{b}_{2})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{\xi_{2}}\big(\lambda^{A}_{b_{1}^{\dagger}\circ a_{1}}(a_{2})\cdot\lambda^{A}_{b_{1}^{\dagger}\circ a_{1}\circ b_{2}\circ a_{1}^{\dagger}\circ b_{1}\circ b_{2}^{\dagger}}(a_{2}^{-1}),~b_{1}^{\dagger}\circ a_{1}\circ b_{2}\circ a_{1}^{\dagger}\circ b_{1}\circ b_{2}^{\dagger}\big),~\text{using \eqref{isoisb 1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\xi_{2}\big(\lambda^{A}_{b_{1}^{\dagger}\circ a_{1}}(a_{2})\cdot\lambda^{A}_{b_{1}^{\dagger}\circ a_{1}\circ b_{2}\circ a_{1}^{\dagger}\circ b_{1}\circ b_{2}^{\dagger}}(a_{2}^{-1})\big),~\xi_{2}\big(b_{1}^{\dagger}\circ a_{1}\circ b_{2}\circ a_{1}^{\dagger}\circ b_{1}\circ b_{2}^{\dagger}\big)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\xi_{2}\big(\lambda^{A}_{b_{1}^{\dagger}\circ a_{1}}(a_{2})\cdot a_{2}^{-1}\cdot a_{2}\cdot\lambda^{A}_{b_{1}^{\dagger}\circ a_{1}\circ b_{2}\circ a_{1}^{\dagger}\circ b_{1}\circ b_{2}^{\dagger}}(a_{2}^{-1})\big),~\xi_{1}(b_{1}^{\dagger}\circ a_{1})\circ\xi_{1}(b_{2})\circ\xi_{1}(a_{1}^{\dagger}\circ b_{1})\circ\xi_{1}(b_{2})^{\dagger}\big),

by
[
17
, Proposition 3.9]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\xi_{2}\big(\lambda^{A}_{b_{1}^{\dagger}\circ a_{1}}(a_{2})\cdot a_{2}^{-1}\cdot(\lambda^{A}_{b_{1}^{\dagger}\circ a_{1}\circ b_{2}\circ a_{1}^{\dagger}\circ b_{1}\circ b_{2}^{\dagger}}(a_{2})\cdot a_{2}^{-1})^{-1}\big),~\xi_{1}(b_{1}^{\dagger}\circ a_{1})\circ\xi_{1}(b_{2})\circ\xi_{1}(a_{1}^{\dagger}\circ b_{1})\circ\xi_{1}(b_{2})^{\dagger}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\lambda^{B}_{\xi_{1}(b_{1}^{\dagger}\circ a_{1})}(\xi_{1}(a_{2}))\cdot\xi_{1}(a_{2})^{-1}\cdot(\lambda^{B}_{\xi_{1}(b_{1}^{\dagger}\circ a_{1})\circ\xi_{1}(b_{2})\circ\xi_{1}(a_{1}^{\dagger}\circ b_{1})\circ\xi_{1}(b_{2})^{\dagger}}(\xi_{1}(a_{2}))\cdot\xi_{1}(a_{2})^{-1})^{-1},

\displaystyle\xi_{1}(b_{1})^{\dagger}\circ\xi_{1}(a_{1})\circ\xi_{1}(b_{2})\circ\xi_{1}(a_{1})^{\dagger}\circ\xi_{1}(b_{1})\circ\xi_{1}(b_{2})^{\dagger}\big),~\text{using \eqref{sbisoclinism xi2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\lambda^{B}_{\xi_{1}(b_{1})^{\dagger}\circ\xi_{1}(a_{1})}(\xi_{1}(a_{2}))\cdot\lambda^{B}_{\xi_{1}(b_{1})^{\dagger}\circ\xi_{1}(a_{1})\circ\xi_{1}(b_{2})\circ(\xi_{1}(b_{1})^{\dagger}\circ\xi_{1}(a_{1}))^{\dagger}\circ\xi_{1}(b_{2})^{\dagger}}(\xi_{1}(a_{2})^{-1}),~

\displaystyle\xi_{1}(b_{1})^{\dagger}\circ\xi_{1}(a_{1})\circ\xi_{1}(b_{2})\circ(\xi_{1}(b_{1})^{\dagger}\circ\xi_{1}(a_{1}))^{\dagger}\circ\xi_{1}(b_{2})^{\dagger}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(1,~\xi_{1}(b_{1})^{\dagger}\circ\xi_{1}(a_{1})\big)\bullet\big(\xi_{1}(a_{2}),\,\xi_{1}(b_{2})\big)\bullet\big(1,\,\xi_{1}(b_{1})^{\dagger}\circ\xi_{1}(a_{1})\big)^{-1}\bullet\big(\xi_{1}(a_{2}),\,\xi_{1}(b_{2})\big)^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta^{*}_{\widetilde{B}}\big(\big(\xi_{1}(\overline{a}_{1}),\xi_{1}(\overline{b}_{1})\big),\big(\xi_{1}(\overline{a}_{2}),\xi_{1}(\overline{b}_{2})\big)\big),~~\text{using Lemma \eqref{annihilator lemma}(2)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta^{*}_{\widetilde{B}}\,(\widetilde{\xi_{1}}\times\widetilde{\xi_{1}})\big((\overline{a}_{1},\overline{b}_{1}),(\overline{a}_{2},\overline{b}_{2})\big).

This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

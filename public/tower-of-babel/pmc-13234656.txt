The State of Imidazolone as an Evolving Pharmacophore: Current Progress and Clinical Significance in Oncology and AMR
Authors: Preetesh Kumar Panda, Suvadeep Mal, Monalisa Mahapatra, Aruna Ghose, Sudhir Kumar Paidesetty
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Nitrogen-bearing heterocycles have been fundamental scaffolds in drug discovery, contributing significantly to the design and development of therapeutic agents since the inception of modern medicinal chemistry. Among them, imidazolones have shown notable therapeutic relevance, particularly in oncology, antimicrobial resistance (AMR), and inflammation, yet they remain relatively underexplored. Nevertheless, the significant therapeutic and clinical efficacy of imidazolone-bearing drugs, such as Nilutamide, Irbesartan, and Leucettinib-21, in anticancer, antihypertensive, and neurotherapeutic applications has stimulated increasing interest in imidazolone chemistry and its medicinal potential. This review systematically discusses the major synthetic strategies, structure–activity relationships, and mechanistic principles governing the biological activity of imidazolones. Particular emphasis is placed on their rapidly expanding role as anticancer agents targeting enzymes and signaling proteins such as carbonic anhydrases, epidermal growth factor receptors (EGFR), Bcl-2 protein family, and checkpoint kinases, along with their potential applications in antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory therapies. Furthermore, the review highlights the complementary roles of computational modeling and biological evaluation in understanding the interactions of imidazolone-bearing compounds with various microbial and cancer-related receptors. These integrated studies reveal their ability to engage multiple biological targets and validate their observed pharmacological activities. The convergence of in silico predictions with experimental findings offers valuable guidance for the rational development of imidazolone-based therapeutics. Overall, this review emphasizes imidazolones as promising scaffolds with significant potential for future drug discovery and development.

Introduction

Nitrogen-containing heterocyclic scaffolds have emerged as focal points of scientific inquiry, captivating the interest of researchers in the pursuit of multifunctional therapeutic agents. With antimicrobial resistance escalating into a looming global health catastrophe, the development of innovative drug candidates targeting resistant pathogens has become an urgent and uncompromising priority for modern medicinal chemistry. The literature reveals that nitrogen-containing heterocycles exhibit remarkable multidisciplinary therapeutic potential, being employed across a broad spectrum of disorders, including antimicrobial, anticancer, anti-inflammatory, antiulcer, anticonvulsant, antidiabetic, and many other conditions. , Among these, the imidazole ring stands out as an exceptionally versatile pharmacophore prized for its structural robustness and potent activity against pathogenic microorganisms. Notably, imidazole and its derivatives constitute the core of many antifungal and anticancer agents that are currently available in the pharmaceutical market.

Imidazole, initially called glyoxaline, is a five-membered aza-heterocycle with a molecular formula of C3H4N2 and has been a scaffold of choice among medicinal chemists since its discovery in 1858 by German scientist Heinrich Debus, synthesized via a condensation reaction between glyoxal, formaldehyde, and ammonia (Figure a). The presence of acidic and basic nitrogen atoms in the ring system induces desirable electron-rich characteristics into the structure, thereby helping to interact with several biological targets, and holds promise for its versatility as an anticancer, antifungal, antibacterial, antiamoebic, ulcerogenic, anti-inflammatory, antihelmintic, etc. − Five-membered cyclic ureas, i.e., imidazolones and imidazolidinones, are the carbonyl derivatives of imidazole, where the carbonyl ketone could be present in the second, fourth, or fifth position, generating imidazole-2-one, imidazole-4-one, or imidazole-5-one (Figure b).

(a) An illustration of the synthetic strategy for imidazole developed by scientist Debus in 1858 and (b) structures of different ketonic imidazole derivatives.

An imidazolone drug, Irbesartan (Avapro) (Figure ), is an angiotensin II receptor blocker (ARB) developed by Sanofi Research and comarketed with Bristol-Myers Squibb. The FDA approved it in 1997 for hypertension and expanded its indication in 2002 to include diabetic nephropathy in patients with type 2 diabetes, supported by findings from the Irbesartan Diabetic Nephropathy Trial (IDNT). Despite recalls in 2018 due to nitrosamine impurities affecting some ARBs, Irbesartan remains widely prescribed, with updated FDA safety guidelines issued in 2024 to ensure continued therapeutic use. Leucettinib-21 (Figure ), a substituted 2-aminoimidazolin-4-one developed by Perha Pharmaceuticals, is derived from the marine sponge metabolite Leucettamine B. As a potent DYRK1A kinase inhibitor, it shows promise for Alzheimer’s disease and Down syndrome. The compound is now in a Phase I clinical trial with 120 participants, including adults with Down syndrome and patients with Alzheimer’s disease. Fluspirilene (Redeptin) (Figure ), is a long-acting injectable typical antipsychotic developed by Janssen Pharmaceutica in 1963, exerts its therapeutic effect via potent dopamine D2 receptor antagonism, and is indicated for chronic schizophrenia. Clinical evaluations include completed Phase 4 maintenance studies and a terminated Phase 3 gerontopsychiatric pharmacovigilance trial. Enoximone (Figure ), a selective phosphodiesterase inhibitor, exhibits both vasodilatory and positive inotropic effects without increasing myocardial oxygen consumption. Originally developed by Sanofi-Aventis (formerly Hoechst Marion Roussel Inc.) in 1980, it remains approved for intravenous administration in Europe. Although U.S. trials for chronic heart failure were discontinued, investigations into low-dose oral formulations continue.

Imidazole-containing drugs are also in use for the treatment of cancer. Nilutamide (Figure ) (brand names Nilandron and Anandron) is an oral, nonsteroidal antiandrogen indicated for metastatic prostate cancer in men. Discovered by Roussel in 1977, it became the second nonsteroidal antiandrogen introduced clinically, first marketed in France in 1987 and approved by the U.S. FDA in 1996, with a generic version authorized in 2016. Enzalutamide (Figure ) (Xtandi), discovered at UCLA and developed by Medivation and Astellas (later acquired by Pfizer in 2016), represents a potent second-generation antiandrogen for advanced prostate cancer, also investigated for polycystic ovarian syndrome and hirsutism. Apalutamide (Figure ) (Erleada), another nonsteroidal antiandrogen for nonmetastatic castration-resistant and metastatic castration-sensitive prostate cancer, originated from the University of California system, licensed to Aragon Pharmaceuticals in 2009, and subsequently developed by Janssen (Johnson & Johnson) following its 2013 acquisition, receiving FDA approval in 2018.

Rationale behind Choosing Imidazolone over Explored N-Containing Scaffolds

Imidazolone, a keto analogue and structural isomer of imidazole, has emerged as a promising yet comparatively underexplored scaffold. Its unique tautomeric stability, presence of both carbonyl and imine functionalities, and potential for hydrogen bonding confer enhanced binding affinity toward diverse biological targets. , Like imidazole, the imidazolone ring system can also act as a versatile pharmacophore, facilitating interactions with enzyme active sites through both polar and π–π stacking interactions. Moreover, its structural resemblance to peptide backbones and nucleic acid bases allows it to mimic biological substrates, making it a favorable scaffold for designing enzyme inhibitors and anticancer agents.

While imidazole and triazole derivatives have shown pronounced biological activities, they often suffer from metabolic instability, poor selectivity, or limited target specificity in complex biological systems. In contrast, the imidazolone nucleus, being a semipolar and conformationally restricted heterocycle, offers improved pharmacokinetic properties and greater structural flexibility for derivatization. Additionally, oxadiazoleskey intermediates in imidazolone synthesisdemonstrate notable bioactivities but lack the keto functionality necessary for stronger hydrogen-bond donor–acceptor interactions within biological microenvironments.

Therefore, imidazolone derivatives represent a strategic advancement over conventional nitrogen-based heterocycles, providing a balance between chemical reactivity, metabolic stability, and biological diversity. The scaffold’s potential to modulate enzyme activities, such as carbonic anhydrase, and interfere with cancer-associated metabolic pathways further underscores its relevance in current drug discovery paradigms.

Cancer Pathway Intervention by Imidazolone Nucleus

Small-molecule drug discovery in cancer often targets the Bcl-2 family of proteins, notably Bax, Bak, and Bcl-2, which plays a pivotal role in regulating apoptosis. Under stress conditions, the tumor suppressor p53 is activated and transcriptionally upregulates proapoptotic BH3-only proteins (e.g., PUMA, NOXA), which then neutralize antiapoptotic members like Bcl-2. This derepression and direct activation of Bax and Bak permits oligomerization on the mitochondrial outer membrane, triggering mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization (MOMP), the release of cytochrome c, and downstream caspase activation leading to apoptosis. Molecular and cellular pathways involved in apoptosis.

In parallel, the imidazolone scaffold has been reported to modulate carbonic anhydrase (CA) activity, particularly CA IX, which is highly expressed in hypoxic tumor tissues. CA IX catalyzes the reversible hydration of CO2 to bicarbonate and protons, facilitating extracellular acidification while importing bicarbonate to neutralize intracellular pH. By maintaining intracellular pH homeostasis under hypoxic stress, CA IX supports tumor cell survival, migration, and invasion. Inhibiting CA IX via imidazolone-based small molecules can, therefore, disrupt this pH-regulating machinery, making cancer cells more vulnerable to apoptosis. By combining compounds that restore p53-mediated apoptotic signaling (via Bax/Bak) with imidazolone-derived CA IX inhibitors, a synergistic therapeutic strategy may be achieved: reactivating intrinsic cell-death pathways while undermining the tumor’s adaptation to hypoxia.

Though imidazolones have been widely investigated for their anti-inflammatory, antibacterial, and anticancer properties, a substantial portion of this versatile scaffold remains chemically and biologically underexplored. Existing studies highlight that subtle modifications on the imidazolone ring can significantly alter its interaction with diverse molecular targets, including enzymes, receptors, and transcriptional regulators. This structural sensitivity suggests a broad, untapped potential for generating novel derivatives with improved potency, selectivity, and pharmacokinetic profiles. Moreover, only a limited number of imidazolone-based molecules have been systematically screened against emerging cancer-related targets such as carbonic anhydrase isoforms, epigenetic modulators, and apoptosis-regulating proteins. Therefore, in this review, Sections –4 comprehensively highlight the imidazolone-based compounds reported to date, emphasizing the structural diversity and medicinal significance of this scaffold through detailed structure–activity analyses. These sections also discuss the biological and computational investigations associated with these derivatives while addressing the existing challenges that may influence their further development as potential therapeutic agents.

Imidazolones as Antimicrobial Agents

Benzylidine Imidazolones and Their Antimicrobial Activity

Banoon et al. reported a microwave-assisted organic synthesis (MAOS) approach to synthesize newly designed imidazolones using an energy-efficient method. Further, the synthesized compounds were being characterized using FT-IR, NMR, and mass spectroscopy for their structural confirmation. The above research has very conveniently generated a novel series of imidazolone derivatives with significantly fewer byproducts and high yields ranging from 43 to 88%. Though the study has reported a series of compounds, the SAR of the potential molecules has been depicted to follow an ascending order influenced by electron-donating and electron-withdrawing groups such as nitro (NO2), methoxy (OMe), and dimethylamino (NMe2), which accord remarkable antimicrobial potency by enabling better membrane permeability, enzyme binding, and disruption of essential cellular processes. Also, the synthesis procedure of imidazolones had not been reported, which could be depicted through initiating from procuring an intermediate oxazolone product via Erlenmeyer–Plöchl azlactone synthesis. Usually, this multistep reaction is initiated by the synthesis of hippuric acid derivatives by nucleophilic acyl substitution of glycine with benzoyl chlorides. Furthermore, the former derivatives formed 4H-oxazol-5-one products by intramolecular cyclization under heating conditions, which on treatment with 3,4,5-trimethoxybenzaldehyde afforded the corresponding benzylidene-substituted oxazolones through Knoevenagel condensation. The final step involves ring opening at the C5 position and was performed under microwave-assisted conditions where primary amines were treated with oxazolone in the presence of sodium acetate and acetic acid. The synthesized compounds were further analyzed for their antimicrobial potential by undergoing an in silico screening. Among all the synthesized compounds, compounds bearing substituted nitrophenyl, trimethoxy-phenyl, and N,N-dimethyl at the fifth position of imidazolone have shown a potential zone of inhibition against C. albicans, H. pylori, Staphylococcus aureus, and E. coli, depicted as compounds 1, 2, and 3, respectively in Figure . The overall molecular docking study have been depicted as binding energies of −8.3 kcal/mol for compound 2 and −8.9 kcal/mol for compound 3 against the fungal target (PDB: 5k04). The analysis of docking results has been summarized using hydrogen-bonding interactions with amino acid residues like VAL78, ARG85, ARG86, ILE87, GLY88, LYS114, and ASN117. Similarly, HOMO–LUMO analysis was performed for the potent candidates, showing ΔE values of 0.13 eV (2) and 0.19 eV (3) that evidenced their stability and reactivity.

2-Thioxoimidazolidines and Their Antibacterial and Antifungal Activity

Another study reported by Khodair et al. had synthesized a series of 20 imidazolidinone derivatives primarily focused on phenytoin derivatization, which were evaluated for their antimicrobial activity against multiple microbial strains. Among them, compounds containing piperidine at the second position displayed favorable antimicrobial results among the series of compounds (depicted as compound 4 in Figure ). A total of six bacterial and fungal strains, viz., S. aureus, S. mutans, E. coli, K. pneumoniae, C. albicans, and A. niger, were used for screening antimicrobial efficacy through the agar well diffusion method. The final compounds were synthesized through a multistep reaction sequence, starting from 2-methylthiohydantoin derivatives, followed by condensation with various amines to afford the corresponding imidazolidinone scaffolds. The compound 2-thioxoimidazolidin-4-one underwent a Mannich condensation with piperidine and formaldehyde via nucleophilic addition, liberating the most potent product, compound 4. The antimicrobial activity was evaluated against four microbial strains, among which compound 4 demonstrated significant inhibitory effects, with zones of inhibition measuring 11.6 ± 0.7 mm against S. aureus and 11.3 ± 0.7 mm against E. coli. From the SAR study, it can be observed that the piperidine moiety may contribute to the enhanced growth inhibitory activity. It provided better lipophilicity, improved molecular flexibility, and favorable interactions with selected protein targets (PDB IDs: 3t88 and 2wje) with multiple hydrogen bonds, as reported by authors. Additionally, this compound has shown remarkable results in ADMET properties, satisfying all desired criteria achieving Lipinski’s Rule of Five, which may act as a preliminary screening approach for assessing the potential of these compounds in the drug discovery pipeline and also focusing upon the pharmacological potential of phenytoin-derivatized products.

N-Allyl-2-(allylthio)-5,5-diphenyl-3,5-dihydro-4H-imidazol-4-one Derivatives and Their Antirhodococcus Activity

To combat the global threat of antimicrobial resistance, Allah et al. have proceeded with a two-step synthesis of N-allyl-2-(allylthio)-5,5-diphenyl-3,5-dihydro-4H-imidazol-4-one (6) to challenge bacterial resistance. They initiated the synthesis via the Biltz method to yield the intermediate product 5,5-diphenyl-2-thioxoimidazolidin-4-one (5) (both compounds structures have been depicted in Figure ), which was prepared by the cyclization of thiourea and benzil under alkaline conditions in the presence of ethanol as a solvent. Subsequently, alkylation using allyl bromide under phase-transfer catalysis gave the final compound 6 with an excellent yield of 92%. The structural elucidation was carried out using different spectral techniques like FT-IR, NMR, HRMS, and X-ray crystallography, revealing a planar imidazole-4-one ring system with orthogonally situated allyl moieties. Furthermore, biological screening was performed on three different bacterial strains, viz., Rhodococcus equi, Rhodococcus sp. GK1, and Rhodococcus sp. GK3. Compound 5 demonstrated a growth inhibition zone measuring 13.3 ± 0.4 mm and 12.8 ± 0.4 mm against Rhodococcus sp. GK1 and Rhodococcus sp. GK3, respectively. Similarly, compound 6 showed a 14.3 ± 0.4 mm and 14.2 ± 0.2 mm zone of inhibition against the same strains. In the case of R. equi, growth inhibition of 14.3 ± 0.4 mm and 10.9 ± 0.6 mm was observed for compounds 5 and 6, respectively. Additionally, density functional theory (DFT) calculations were performed to provide insights into the electronic properties and stability of the compounds, revealing a highest energy bandgap of 5.57 eV and stabilization energy up to 53.4 kcal/mol, attributed to significant intramolecular delocalization in compound 6. ADMET prediction studies were conducted to assess their pharmacokinetic profiles. All synthesized compounds were found to satisfy Lipinski’s Rule of Five, supporting their drug-likeness. Moreover, the structure–activity relationship revealed that enhanced permeability, better interactions with enzymes, and significant ZOI are likely because of the presence of allylic groups in the moiety. The molecular structures were designed by correlating the pharmacological structure–activity relationship with that of nitrofurantoin, a well-known antibacterial agent. Although the synthesized derivatives incorporate hydantoin-based structural modifications, the designed framework remains pharmacologically justified and suitable for preliminary evaluation of antimicrobial activity.

Chalcogen-Varied Imidazolone Scaffolds Derivatives and Their Anti-MRSA Activity

In search of novel drug discovery, Witek et al. reported an article citing 15 chalcogen-containing imidazolone moieties evaluated against eight methicillin-resistant S. aureus (MRSA) strains. The researchers synthesized a series of imidazolone-containing chalcogen moieties differentiated into three groups: Group A containing 5-arylidene-imidazolones, Group B containing 5-spirofluorene hydantoins, and Group C containing sulfur (S) or selenium (Se)-bearing derivatives. The prime rationale of these synthesized molecules is as a potential antibiotic augmenter or antibiotic resistance breaker (ARBs). To evaluate their efficacy, the compounds were tested along with marketed antibiotics such as oxacillin and ampicillin against resistant bacterial strains. The results showed that some of the newly synthesized compounds exhibited improved antibacterial activity when combined with these antibiotics. However, the individual novel compounds did not exhibit good results in antibacterial activity, with MIC values >0.25 mM. The compounds were further checked for their capability to restore the efficiency of β-lactam antibiotics mainly on oxacillin against resistant MRSA. Among all, compounds bearing a 5-spirofluorene hydantoin derivative depicted in Figure as compound 7 displayed the highest efficacy, which helped resistant oxacillin to regain effectiveness in six out of eight MRSA strains at a concentration of 0.0625 mM. The reported data indicate that this activity is nearly 32 times higher than that of oxacillin treatment alone against these strains. In terms of activity gain (A) calculation, most compounds showed an activity gain of around >4. However, compound 7 exhibited an “A” value exceeding 32 against some strains, indicating its strong potential as a prominent antibiotic potentiator. The heavier and hydrophobic substitutions incorporated at the third position of the imidazolone ring, chalcogen moiety, along with conjugates bearing S or Se, are likely to contribute to the observed biological activity of the compounds. The molecular docking and dynamic simulations suggested potential interactions of the compounds that presumed to act by interfering with the allosteric site of the protein PBP2a, providing supportive structural insights into their possible mechanism of oxacillin potentiation. Additionally, several compounds were found to restore the activity of erythromycin, possibly through a secondary mechanism involving efflux pump inhibition (MsrA). Based on these reported findings, compound 7 emerged as a promising antibiotic resistance breaker (ARB) with significant antibiotic-potentiating properties, while computational studies provided supportive insights into the mechanistic profile.

Fluorinated Imidazolones and Their Antimicrobial Activity

Another study by Desai et al. reported two series of fluorinated imidazolone derivatives bearing 3-chloro-4-fluorophenyl and 4-(trifluoromethyl)­phenyl substitutions. These compounds were synthesized from the intermediate precursor 4-arylidene-2-phenyloxazol-5­(4H)-ones. Structural characterization was carried out using spectral data and elemental analysis, and their biological activities were evaluated against two bacterial and two fungal strains using the agar well diffusion method. The results of the study revealed that compound 8, having a tolyl substitution at the fourth position of the imidazolone ring, exerted the highest antimicrobial activity, making it the most potent compound in the series, with inhibition zones of 25 mm (S. aureus) and 27 mm (C. albicans). They also demonstrated that the presence of electron-donating substituents such as methoxy, methyl, fluoro, and chloro, as well as the electron-withdrawing CF3 group at the para position of the arylidene ring attached to the imidazolone core at the C-4 position, contribute to the observed antimicrobial efficacy. An in silico docking study against PBP2a and 14α-demethylase targets was also performed, in which compound 8 depicted in Figure showed favorable predicted binding affinity with a docking score of −11.0 kcal/mol against the fungal protein target (PDB: 5FSA). Several hydrogen-bonding and π–π interactions supported its observed in vitro results. Considering its dual-targeted profile and favorable computational and biological findings, compound 8 suggests its potential as a lead candidate for future drug development.

5-Arylidene-imidazolone Derivatives as Potential Antibiotic Adjuvants against MRSA

In 2019, Kaczor et al. synthesized a series of novel 5-arylidene-imidazolones to combat MDR infections, particularly those caused by MRSA, as a reviver of β-lactam potency. The synthesis proceeded through a Knoevenagel condensation, followed by S-methylation and subsequent amine substitution, accompanied by a Dimroth rearrangement. Later on, these were structurally confirmed using X-ray crystallography, which validated the Z-configuration. Among the series of compounds, a few exhibited moderate antibacterial activity (e.g., MIC of 0.125 mM found in a compound bearing fluorine and piperidine moiety), while some other compounds showed a significant potentiating effect on oxacillin against MRSA. Notably, the oxacillin MIC was reduced from 512 to 32–64 μg/mL in the presence of compound 10, while compound 9 (depicted in Figure ) lowered the MIC from 512 to 32 μg/mL, corresponding to an activity gain (A) of 8–16. Also, compound 9 showed efflux pump inhibition of around 37–97% in E. aerogenes, suggesting an ideal multifunctional candidate. In silico molecular docking and dynamic simulations suggested favorable bonding with target that may contribute to enhanced antibiotic binding affinity. Furthermore, the predicted results of ADMET profiling indicated favorable drug-likeness and safety profile. From SAR, it has been seen that the fourth-positioned naphthyl substitution and first-positioned alkyl chains attached to methyl piperazine/morpholine are likely responsible for antibiotic adjuvant activity. Finally, compounds 9 and 10 may be regarded as potential nonantibiotic resistance breakers with multipurpose usability as antibacterial agents and efflux pump inhibitors.

Challenges and Knowledge Gaps

Despite the promising antimicrobial potential demonstrated by various imidazolone and imidazolidinone derivatives, several challenges and knowledge gaps remain that limit their translation into viable therapeutic candidates. Many studies primarily focus on synthetic strategies and preliminary biological screening, while detailed mechanistic insights into their antimicrobial modes of action are still insufficiently explored. In several reports, the synthetic pathways and intermediate formation such as those involving oxazolone intermediates via Erlenmeyer–Plöchl azlactone synthesis are not comprehensively described, which creates limitations in reproducibility and further structural optimization. Additionally, most investigations rely on in vitro assays and in silico docking studies targeting microbial proteins such as Penicillin-Binding Protein 2a, while in vivo validation, pharmacokinetic profiling, and toxicity assessments remain largely unexplored. Although some derivatives have shown promising antibiotic-potentiating or resistance-breaking properties against resistant strains like methicillin-resistant S. aureus, the exact structure–activity relationships governing membrane permeability, efflux pump inhibition, and enzyme binding are still not fully understood. Furthermore, variability in substituent effects such as chalcogen incorporation, allyl groups, or fluorinated aryl moieties highlights the need for more systematic SAR investigations and standardized biological evaluation protocols. Therefore, future studies should emphasize mechanistic validation, broader spectrum biological testing, and advanced pharmacological assessments to bridge the gap between promising in vitro activity and clinically relevant antimicrobial drug development. Imidazolones as Anti-Inflammatory and Wound-Healing Agents

Chloroaniline-Substituted Imidazolones as Antibiotic Adjuvants against Cyclooxygenase and TACE

Babari et al., in search of COX-2 inhibitory compounds, synthesized a set of oxazolones and imidazolones with antioxidant and anti-inflammatory activity. The synthetic procedure involved the conversion of precursor oxazolones into imidazolones, yielding four oxazolone and imidazolone derivatives, which were subsequently evaluated for their in vivo potency. From in silico studies, compound 12 showed highest binding affinity of −9.7 kcal/mol against COX-2 (PDB: 5ikr), whereas compound 14 showed similar predictive affinity of −9.9 kcal/mol against TACE (TNF-α converting enzyme; PDB: 2ddf). These values were more considerable than the standard drug indomethacin under the reported computational conditions, providing supportive evidence for their potential inhibitory activity. The in vivo study revealed that compound 13 demonstrated significant reduction in carrageenan-induced paw edema at 10 mg/kg (*p < 0.001), which supports the reported histopathological findings indicating tissue recovery. This also showed decreased LPO (lipid peroxidation) levels along with restoration of antioxidant enzyme markers. Following this, RT-PCR and ELISA results of compound 14 demonstrated reduction of TNF-α protein, COX-2, and mRNA expression. The SAR study revealed that the presence of nitro groups creates hydrogen bonding at receptor sites, suggesting compounds 13 and 14 may serve as promising anti-inflammatory lead compounds of the study. Indolyl-Imidazolone Derivatives as Anti-Inflammatory Agents

Husain et al. reported the synthesis of a series of methylene-bridged indole-imidazolone derivatives as safer alternatives to conventional NSAIDs. The three-step reaction process was initiated by introducing an aldehydic group in 2-phenyl-1H–indole, followed by the formation of oxazolone, which later formed imidazolone by cyclization with different amines. Their structural conformation was carried out by various spectral techniques such as FT-IR, NMR, and mass spectrometry. Among the series, compounds 15 and 16 (Illustrated in Figure ) exhibited enhanced anti-inflammatory activity, making them the lead candidates, while SAR revealed that the presence of dinitro and hydroxyalkyl groups in these compounds may contribute to their enhanced safety and anti-inflammatory efficacy. Compounds 15 and 16 showed 80 and 81% reduction in inflammation, respectively, compared to the standard drug indomethacin, which showed around 67%. Compound 16 has also shown better analgesic activity of 86% than aspirin (62%). Both compounds 15 and 16 showed lower ulcerogenic effect, viz., 0.9 and 1.2 respectively, compared to the stats indomethacin, i.e., 2.3. They performed molecular docking analysis against the COX-2 protein target (PDB: 3pgh), which showed favorable predicted binding affinities of −11.2 and −9.7 kcal/mol for compounds 15 and 16, respectively.

Benzoate or Sulfonamide-imidazolone Derivatives as Antimicrobial and Anti-Inflammatory Agents

To achieve multifunctional anti-inflammatory activity along with antibacterial candidates, two researchers, N.H. Metwally and M.S. Mohamed, developed three series of imidazolone derivatives bearing benzoate or sulfonamide groups. The multistep reaction involved glycine cyclization and amine condensation, resulting in the desired imidazolone products. The structural elucidation was carried out using various spectral techniques. The in vivo results from carrageenan-induced paw edema in rat models demonstrated that compound 18 (As given in Figure ) exhibited slightly higher anti-inflammatory activity than the standard drug celecoxib, showing 49% inhibition compared to 43%. Compound 17 also showed comparable inhibitory activity of 42%, which was nearly the same as the standard drug taken for consideration. Later, a COX-2 inhibitory study was performed, which demonstrated IC50 value of approximately 0.09 μM for compound 18, whereas in the case of celecoxib it was 0.11 μM. In vitro antimicrobial activity was performed against five distinct Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacterial strains to gain insight into the antimicrobial potency of the derived compounds. From the series, compound 18 attained zones of inhibition of 19 and 17 mm against S. aureus and S. mutans, respectively. Similarly, in the DHFR assay, the IC50 value of approximately 0.08 μM indicated improved inhibitory activity relative to the standard drug when treated in combination with sulfadiazine. The SAR study revealed that the presence of a carboxylic group in compound 18 may contribute to the enhanced biological activity compared to compounds having the para-positioned sulfonamide group. Molecular docking with COX-2 indicated favorable binding affinity, with a docking score of −15.2 kcal/mol and multiple hydrogen bonds, making compound 18 the most potent among the series.

Azo-imidazolone Derivatives as Antimicrobial and Antioxidant Agents

To address the multidisciplinary capabilities of imidazolones, M.K. Samad and F.E. Hawaiz reported the synthesis of a series of imidazolone derivatives from their corresponding precursor oxazolone by modified Erlenmeyer–Plöchl route. From the azo-oxazolones, they derived azo-benzamides and azo-imidazolones with good yields. They were further structurally confirmed by various spectral techniques to get insights into their antimicrobial, wound-healing, and antioxidant properties. The antimicrobial activity of compound 19, along with a few others, showed notable activity against S. aureus and E. coli, with inhibition zone up to 31 mm, while in the case of in vivo burn wound-healing activity, compound 20 demonstrated enhanced wound-healing activity compared to the standard Ucederm. From antioxidant activity study (FRAP), it was found that compound 20 demonstrated favorable antioxidant activity, highlighting its ability to donate electron pairs to neutralize unstable radicals, thereby reducing the harmful effects. Similarly, SAR demonstrated that the presence of electron-donating groups and the azo system of molecules promotes enhanced bioactivity. Overall, compound 19 emerged as the most promising multifunctional compound, capable to address all odd conditions reported in the study with a desirable safety profile. Challenges and Knowledge Gap

In the above-discussed context, the reported research on imidazolone derivatives mainly emphasizes biological activities such as anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, and wound-healing effects; however, several clinical challenges and knowledge gaps remain unaddressed. Most of these studies primarily rely on synthesis, in silico, in vitro, and in vivo assessments, with comparatively limited depth of analysis regarding mechanistic insights and precise docking investigations. Additionally, minimal attention has been placed on comprehensive structure–activity relationship optimization, toxicity and long-term safety evaluation, and kinetic profiling of these compounds, which represents a significant limitation. Therefore, future research should focus on addressing these aspects through advanced computational techniques and target-based biochemical assays, which could significantly facilitate the development of these candidates into viable therapeutic scaffolds. Imidazolones as Anticancer Agents

Hydrazinyl-imidazolone Analogues and Their Anticancer Activity through Carbonic Anhydrase Inhibition

Another study by Husseiny et al. reported the synthesis of hydrazinyl-imidazolone analogues obtained by converting an intermediate having a hydrazinyl moiety at the C-2 position of imidazolone through aryl aldehyde condensation to furnish a series of compounds, which collectively demonstrated that electron-donating substituents are contributing toward enhanced biological activity. The compound bearing a para-ethoxy substituent on the phenyl ring exhibited moderate antiproliferative activity (∼38–52% inhibition) along with substantial CA IX/XII inhibition (IC50 = 0.28/0.09 μM). In contrast, the introduction of a stronger electron-donating trimethoxy group in compound 21 resulted in broader biological activity, noticeable cytotoxic effects across the NCI panel (50–93% growth inhibition), and enhanced CA IX/XII inhibition (IC50 = 0.10/0.07 μM). Compound 22, bearing a para-hydroxyl substituent on the phenyl ring, exhibited substantial growth inhibition across multiple cancer cell lines (often >60–90%), with CA IC50 values in the range of 0.07–0.23 μM. Meanwhile, in the case of compound 23, featuring an ortho-hydroxyl substitution, it demonstrated notable cytotoxic effects against several tumor lines (70–99% inhibition) and displayed one of the most potent CA inhibition profiles in the series (hCA I/II/IX/XII IC50 = 0.16/0.05/0.05/0.04 μM). The pictorial representation of the prominent compounds (21–23) is presented in Figure . Collectively, the SAR clearly indicates that the electron-donating groups, particularly methoxy and hydroxyl substituents, maximally enhance both anticancer activity and CA inhibition, highlighting compounds 21, 22, and 23 as the promising candidates.

Pyrimidinone-triazolyl-imidazolones and Their Anticancer Activity through Bcl-2 Inhibition

In response to the urgent need for new anticancer agents, Noser et al. synthesized a series of pyrimidinone-triazolyl-linked imidazolones targeting the antiapoptotic Bcl-2 protein. The synthetic strategy involved condensation of acetylacetone, urea, and furfural via a Schiff’s bridge or azo-coupling with heterocyclic moieties such as pyrazolones and imidazolones. Thereafter, the compounds were characterized by standard spectroscopic techniques and evaluated for their anticancer activity, where compound 24 was found to be the most potent molecule (structure of compound 24 is given in Figure ). Notably, compound 24 exhibited excellent IC50 values of 3.84 and 5.61 μM against HepG2 and MCF-7, respectively, surpassing the reference standard venetoclax with nearly zero toxicity toward normal cells (WI-38). The molecular docking analysis revealed strong binding affinity of compound 24 with a docking score of −11.65 kcal/mol against the Bcl-2 target, which is better than the standard drug venetoclax −6.95 kcal/mol, with key hydrogen-bonding interactions involving various amino acids. ADMET analysis also confirmed that compound 24 suggested good oral bioavailability, with almost 75.89% intestinal absorption. The DPPH study also showed that compound 24 has better radical scavenging capability, with an IC50 of 19.82 μM compared to the standard ascorbic acid. The overall SAR confirmed that heterocyclic rings with azo-linkages may play a crucial role in enhancing the anticancer activity and pharmacokinetic characteristics. Hence, they concluded that compound 24 is a promising lead candidate for future anticancer drug development.

2-(Alkylthio)-5-(arylidene/heteroarylidene)­imidazolones and Their Anticancer Activity against Kinase Inhibition

Sheta et al. reported the development of dual-target Chk-1 and Chk-2 inhibitor drugs and considered imidazolones for this study. They developed two series of compounds containing imidazolones and imidazothienopyrimidines to evaluate their checkpoint kinase inhibitory and antiproliferative efficacy. These compounds were synthesized using the conventional imidazolone synthesis method, starting from glycine as the precursor. Compound 25 (Figure ) emerged as the lead candidate with remarkable antiproliferative efficacy, exhibiting IC50 values of 0.70 μM on MCF-7, 0.48 μM on MDA-MB-468, and 1.21 μM against EKVX. Enzymatic assays were performed to validate and confirm the claimed data, which showed compound 25 has dual inhibitory potential against Chk-1 and Chk-2, with IC50 values of 0.14 and 0.25 μM, respectively. Further, cell cycle analysis confirmed that compound 25 induced S-phase cell cycle arrest and apoptosis in EKVX cells. A 6.08-fold increase in Bax expression, a 4.8-fold increase in Caspase-3 levels, and a 0.26-fold reduction in Bcl-2 expression were observed. The SAR indicated that the presence of a long alkyl chain in the imidazolone scaffold and presence of allylidene group at position C5 may increase cytotoxic effects as well as kinase inhibition. Furthermore, in silico studies, including molecular docking and ADMET profiling, demonstrated favorable results for compound 25, with acceptable Lipinski’s rule parameters, making it the most promising dual Chk-1/Chk-2 inhibitor in the series and suitable for novel anticancer agents.

Imidazolyl-2-cyanoprop-2-enimidothioates and Ethyl Imidazolyl Thiomethylacrylates as Potential EGFR Inhibitors and Apoptosis Inducers

In pursuit of potent EGFR-targeted anticancer agents, two series of compounds, 5,5-diphenyl-2-thioxoimidazolidin-4-one imidothioates and ethyl imidazolyl thiomethylacrylates, were synthesized via nucleophilic addition of 5,5-diphenyl-2-thioxoimidazolidin-4-one to arylidene derivatives of malononitrile or ethyl cyanoacetate. The structures of the synthesized molecules were confirmed using IR, NMR, and mass spectrometry. An initial in vitro cytotoxicity study performed against HepG2, MCF-7, and PC-3 (human cancer cell lines) revealed that three compounds exhibited potent antiproliferative activity, with IC50 values ranging from 5.35 to 18.34 μM. Among those, compound 26 (As given in Figure ) marked as the most active analogue, showing the highest EGFR-inhibitory activity (IC50 = 0.14 μM) and significant upregulation of apoptotic markers caspase-8, caspase-9, and Bax. Also, a 0.223-fold reduction in Bcl-2 expression was observed in cell cycle analysis, supporting apoptosis induction. Notably, compound 26 exhibited favorable selectivity (SI = 11.93) and induced S-phase cell cycle arrest, increasing the S-phase population to 37%, compared to 24.7% observed in the control. Structure–activity relationship (SAR) analysis indicated that the incorporation of both electron-withdrawing (Cl) and electron-donating (OH, heterocycles like furan or thiophene) groups contributes to enhanced cytotoxic and EGFR-inhibitory activities. Molecular docking and atomistic dynamics simulations revealed that compound 26 formed key hydrogen bonds with Cys775 and Met793, as well as a π–π interaction with Phe723, yielding a binding affinity of −9.3 kcal/mol, and remains consistent with the reference EGFR inhibitor (−7.3 kcal/mol). Collectively, these findings position compound 26 as a promising candidate for further development as an EGFR-targeted anticancer and proapoptotic agent.

cis-Dihydroxy-indeno­[1,2-d]­imidazolone Derivatives as GSK-3 Inhibitors

A new series of cis-dihydroxy-indeno­[1,2-d]­imidazolones were synthesized via a green, catalyst-free one-pot reaction of phenyl isothiocyanate with aliphatic amines to form thiourea intermediates, followed by reflux condensation with ninhydrin in CH3CN. The mechanism involves thiourea formation, nucleophilic attack on ninhydrin, and intramolecular N-cyclization. All compounds were characterized by FT-IR, NMR, and mass spectrometry and further subjected to in silico investigation against GSK-3 (PDB: 5HLP). Among them, compound 27 (Figure ) showed the highest binding affinity of −6.6 kcal/mol with major interactions involving Gly28, Asn29, Phe32, and Asp165. Furthermore, molecular dynamics simulations confirmed the stability of compound 27 with the GSK-3 complex over 100 ns, supported by RMSD values, protein: 1.1–3.4 Å, ligand: 1.1–5.5 Å. ADMET profiling predicted high GI absorption, noncarcinogenicity, nonneurotoxicity, and favorable pharmacokinetics for all compounds, especially for compound 27, which also demonstrated good synthetic accessibility and no PAINS alerts. SAR analysis highlighted that electron-withdrawing groups at the benzyl substitutions (e.g., Cl in 27) enhance binding affinity and physicochemical profiles, positioning 27 as a promising GSK-3 inhibitor for further development.

Imidazolone–Sulfonamide–Pyrimidine Hybrids as EGFR-TK Inhibitors

A novel series of imidazolone–sulfonamide–pyrimidine hybrids was synthesized via a multistep reaction starting from hippuric acid and aroyl chlorides. The reaction-initiated cyclization to oxazolones by condensation with aryl aldehydes, followed by final coupling with sulfadiazine under reflux in acetic acid, gives rise to imidazolone molecules. The resulting compounds were evaluated for anticancer potential against MCF-7 breast cancer cells, with compound 28 bearing a furan-2-yl (shown in Figure ) and 4-methoxy benzyl moiety emerging as favorable EGFR inhibitor, exhibiting prominent IC50 of 1.05 μM, which is lower than that of doxorubicin’s IC50 of 1.91 μM. EGFR kinase inhibition assays confirmed that compound 28 has strong activity, showing an IC50 of 0.09 μM, approaching that of Lapatinib’s IC50 0.06 μM. The evaluated flow cytometry results revealed that compound 28 induced G1-phase arrest in 64.9% of cells and apoptosis in 46.1% of cells, upregulating Bax by 4.54-fold and downregulating Bcl-2 by 0.28-fold, suggesting an apoptosis-inducing mechanism. Structure–activity relationship showed that electron-withdrawing groups and heterocyclic substitutions such as furan enhanced cytotoxic and EGFR-inhibitory activity. Overall, compound 28 demonstrated promising EGFR-targeting potential and proapoptotic efficacy, positioning it as a potential lead for further anticancer development.

Imidazole-2-ones as Carbonic Anhydrase IX Inhibitors

A series of imidazole-2-ones and imidazole-2-thiones were synthesized via a two-step protocol: initial coupling of 2-bromo-1-(pyridin-3-yl)­ethanone with substituted anilines, followed by cyclization with potassium isocyanate or ammonium thiocyanate to afford imidazolone and thione analogues, respectively. The compounds were synthesized using both conventional and green microwave methods, achieving higher yields and purity via the latter. Spectroscopic analyses such as FT-IR, NMR, and MS confirmed structural integrity. Among them, compound 29 (Figure ), bearing two methoxy groups, emerged as the most active compound in the series, with IC50 values of 12.51 and 7.96 μM against HCT-116 and HeP2, respectively, showing lower IC50 than doxorubicin in HeP2. It also showed strong CA IX inhibition with an IC50 of 7.55 μM, compared to 18.03 μM for the dimethoxy benzyl substitution at the first position having an imidazol-2-thione analogue, suggesting a favorable contribution of the carbonyl over the thiocarbonyl group. SAR analysis suggested that meta-substituted dimethoxy-phenyl rings enhanced anticancer activity, while the ortho-substituted methoxy group reduced efficacy. DFT analysis indicated that 29 possess comparatively higher reactivity, with the highest HOMO (−7.7 eV), lowest ionization energy, smallest energy gap (10.23 eV), and highest softness (0.19 eV – 1 ). ADME profiling showed favorable drug-likeness, no PAINS alerts, high GI absorption, and good synthetic accessibility for all compounds, indicating the potential of compound 29 as a promising anticancer and CA IX inhibitory lead.

Novel Zwitterionic Imidazolones and Their Anticancer Activity

A novel series of imidazolone derivatives was synthesized via a three-step process starting from glycine and benzoyl chloride, cyclized to obtain hippuric acid, followed by cyclocondensation with vanillin to form oxazolone, and final ring opening/cyclization with various aromatic amines. To improve solubility and activity, some selected compounds were further functionalized with 1,3-propane sultone, yielding their zwitterionic imidazolones. Cytotoxicity studies against Hep3B (liver), HeLa (cervical), and LX2 (normal liver) cell lines revealed that compound 30 (NO2-substituted) was the most active against Hep3B in the tested series, with an IC50 value of 134.2 ± 4.4 μM, while compound 31 (pyridyl derivative) showed the highest observed activity against HeLa cells, with an IC50 of 85.1 ± 2.1 μM and >95% inhibition at 250 μg/mL. Zwitterionic analogues demonstrated comparatively improved anticancer activity; compound 32 showed an IC50 value of 134.7 μg/mL against Hep3B, which may be associated with improved water solubility and cellular interaction. SAR analysis highlighted the importance of electron-withdrawing substituents (NO2, Cl) and side-chain flexibility in enhancing cytotoxicity. Molecular docking with DNA (PDB: 1BNA) suggested favorable binding affinities ranging from −6.8 to −8.7 kcal/mol (predicted values) via hydrogen-bonding and π–alkyl interactions, especially involving amino and aromatic groups. Overall, these results suggest compounds 30, 31, and 32 (shown in Figure ) as promising anticancer candidates with low observed toxicity toward LX2 cells and favorable DNA-binding affinity.

Arylidene-imidazolones and Their Anticancer Activity

In this study, heterocyclic-fused arylidene-imidazolone derivatives were synthesized via a novel organocatalyzed umpolung addition followed by PPA-mediated ester–amide exchange cyclization, enabling efficient access to (Z)-2-arylideneimidazo­[1,2-a]­pyridinones and (Z)-2-benzylidenebenzo­[d]­imidazo­[2,1-b]­thiazol-3-ones. The key intermediates were formed by phosphine-catalyzed addition of 2-aminoazines to arylpropiolates, and the final fused imidazolones were obtained in yields up to 80%. Structural conformation was performed using NMR, MS, and X-ray crystallography. The biological assay revealed that compounds 33 and 34 (illustrated in Figure ) showed IC50 values of 3.8 and 4.2 μM, respectively, against MCF-7 cell lines, showing lower IC50 values than the reference drug etoposide (IC50 of 45 μM). Both compounds induced cell death, with compound 33 causing S-phase arrest by 36.4%, associated with upregulation of BAX (approximately 6-fold) and downregulation of BCL-XL (approximately 10-fold), along with caspase-3 activation. Topoisomerase inhibition assays further supported a possible mechanism of action by preventing DNA relaxation. SAR analysis highlighted that scaffold-hopped tricyclic benzothiazole-fused imidazolones bearing 1-hydroxyl and 4-chloro substitutions showed comparatively improved activity. In silico studies predicted favorable ADME properties and no predicted PAINS alerts, suggesting compounds 33 and 34 as promising leads for further anticancer development.

4-Benzylidene-2-phenyl-5 (4H)-imidazolone and Its Anticancer Activity through Selective Carbonic Anhydrase IX Inhibition

In search of the anticancer potential of imidazolone derivatives, Eldehna et al. synthesized a novel series of compounds via the conventional Erlenmeyer–Plöchl reaction starting from hippuric acid and substituted benzaldehydes. Oxazolones were initially formed, followed by the formation of imidazolones via condensation with benzene sulfonamides in the presence of acetic acid. Further, the structures were confirmed using different spectral techniques such as FT-IR, NMR, and mass analysis. The compounds were further assessed against human carbonic anhydrase isoforms I, II, IX, and XII using stopped-flow CO2 hydrase assay. Among the series, compound 35 (Figure ), bearing a 2,5-dimethoxybenzylidene substituent in the imidazolone scaffold, exhibited lower KI values than acetazolamide against tumor-associated hCA IX and hCA XII, with inhibition KI values of 23 and 54.8 nM, respectively. The compound also showed notable selectivity ratios, viz., I/IX = 355, II/IX = 19.4, I/XII = 149.1, and II/XII = 8. Later, antiproliferative studies were performed against HCT-116 cell lines, which showed an IC50 value of 3.21 ± 0.16 μM for compound 36. Further, flow cytometric analysis indicated approximately 10-fold increase in apoptotic cell populations; additionally, compounds 35 and 36 reduced the G2/M phase population, accompanied by an approximately 6–7-fold increase in the Sub-G1 population. Molecular docking studies were also performed against hCA IX and II targets, which suggested favorable binding capabilities of the compounds. SAR studies revealed that the presence of methoxy (OCH3) electron-donating groups on the benzylidene ring was associated with improved tumor-related CA selectivity. Compounds 35 and 36 were identified as among the most active hCA IX/XII inhibitors, with comparatively strong anticancer activity.

2-(4-Fluorophenyl) Imidazol-5-ones and Their Anticancer Activity

A novel series of 2-(4-fluorobenzoylamino) acetic acid derivatives substituted with heterocyclic aldehydes to form imidazolones via their precursor oxazolone compounds was synthesized by Abo-Elanwar YA and their coresearchers. The imidazolones were formed by nucleophilic substitution with aromatic amines, along with functional modifications such as chalcone formation. A total of 43 compounds were synthesized and evaluated for their anticancer properties against four distinct cancer cell lines, viz., HeLa, MCF-7, PC-3, and HCT-116. Based on the IC50 values, four compounds showed the most pronounced activity within the series against all four cell lines, with values ranging between 5 and 12 μM relative to the reference drug doxorubicin (DOX). Following the in vitro analysis, the researchers evaluated CDK2A and VEGFR-2 inhibition activity in the HCT-116 cell line for the four most potent compounds, using sorafenib as the reference drug. Among the four compounds, three showed slightly higher IC50 values compared to the reference drug sorafenib against CDK2A, whereas none of the compounds showed comparable results to the reference drug in the case of VEGFR-2. SAR studies revealed that phenyl groups present in the pyrazole- or pyrimidine-based derivatives appeared to contribute in enzyme inhibition and cytotoxicity, while furyl and thienyl substitutions were associated with improved activity. Overall, compounds 37 and 38 (as depicted in Figure ) may be considered as potent candidates for future aspects as VEGFR-2- and CDK2A-targeted anticancer agents.

Discussion: Critical Perspective and Limitations

The imidazolone derivatives undoubtedly demonstrated their potency across diverse pathological areas; however, certain limitations remain that may hinder their applicability to drug discovery. Almost all reports considered in this review predominantly focused on synthetic transformations, molecular interactions, and early stage biological evaluation, whereas systematic behavioral studies, postadministration metabolic stability, and drug-like pharmacokinetic characteristics remain insufficiently addressed. The heterocyclic scaffold of imidazolone compounds, particularly the presence of imine and carbonyl moieties, may promote binding to multiple macromolecular targets, potentially resulting in unintended off-target effects. Several studies discussed in this Review have employed docking analyses to support the interpretation of biological activities of imidazolone and related derivatives. However, it is important to recognize that docking scores represent theoretical estimates of binding affinity and do not directly confirm the biological efficacy. Differences in computational methods, scoring functions, and protein preparation protocols may influence predicted energy values, thereby limiting direct comparisons across studies including those involving marketed drugs. Therefore, the docking findings presented in this Review should be considered as supportive in silico evidence that complements experimental observations rather than as definitive proof of biological activity.

In addition, investigations into detailed toxicological screenings of imidazolone derivatives remain relatively limited. Although reports indicate promising biological efficacy of these compounds, discussions on long-term in vivo toxicity evaluation and safety profiling are scarce, leaving an unclear perspective on their broader therapeutic potential. Likewise, Zeid et al. in 2021 suggested notable morphological abnormalities on Zebrafish embryos upon long-term in vivo treatment. Another research regarding the cytotoxic effect of imidazolones by G. Kumar et al. in 2022 has demonstrated that imidazolone could be a biocompatible congener as it exhibited pronounced toxicity against different cancer cell lines whereas nontumorigenic breast epithelial cells remain unaffected. Therefore, future investigations should prioritize detailed assessment of toxicological parameters, determination of the pathways responsible for their prominent efficacy, and safety evaluations of specific organs, which would better support the risk–benefit assessment of these compounds.

In the context of medicinal chemistry, the widely explored and well-established imidazole scaffold, present in many clinically approved drugs due to its favorable kinetic characteristics and its capability to interact with multiple targets simultaneously, provides significant therapeutic potential across various fields. Meanwhile, although imidazolones share a similar structure with imidazole, the presence of carbonyl functionality along with excess heteroatoms produces distinct electronic and bonding properties that may lead to vulnerable metabolic changes. This variation may offer certain advantages, such as enhanced binding affinity with diverse biological targets due to the presence of a ketone group within the pharmacophore and modulation of overall activity; on the other hand, it may also introduce additional metabolic liabilities like stability, altered lipophilicity, metabolic excretion, etc., depending upon the substitutions. Therefore, future development of these molecules should focus on rational structural optimization combined with comprehensive kinetic and metabolic assessments before they can be considered to be promising bioactive candidates.

Conclusion

Despite their structural diversity and potential therapeutic relevance, imidazolone scaffolds have remained relatively underexplored in many areas of medicinal chemistry, often residing in less emphasized domains of pharmaceutical research. Interestingly, this heterocyclic motif is not entirely foreign to biological systems as imidazolone derivatives are naturally present in the human body as advanced glycation end products (AGEs). One notable example is the creatinine-related structure 2-imino-1-methylimidazolidin-4-one, which serves as an important biomarker for evaluating kidney function and overall renal health. The presence of such imidazolone-based structures in physiological pathways highlights their inherent biological compatibility and their relevance. This natural occurrence further strengthens the rationale for exploring imidazolone scaffolds as promising frameworks for drug discovery. Therefore, a systematic investigation of this scaffold may unlock new opportunities for the development of next-generation pharmacologically active molecules.

The current review provides a comprehensive summary of recent achievements in the design and development of novel pharmaceuticals featuring imidazolone frameworks, with a special emphasis on their potential applications and structure–activity relationships. In addition, the mechanisms of action as well as rational approaches for improving pharmacodynamic and pharmacokinetic properties have been discussed. In summary, various imidazolone derivatives have emerged as valuable scaffolds for further therapeutic exploration in the development of novel anticancer, antimicrobial, and anti-inflammatory drugs. In this context, certain imidazolone analogues have been shown to exhibit notable inhibitory activity against fundamental targets associated with cancer, namely, carbonic anhydrase isoforms IX and XII (hCA IX/XII), vascular endothelial growth factor receptor-2 (VEGFR-2), and cyclin-dependent kinase-2A (CDK2A). Despite significant progress in this field, critical gaps remain regarding the precise structure–activity relationships governing target selectivity, off-target effects, and the translational efficacy of imidazolone-based compounds. Future studies should prioritize the rational design guided by comprehensive ADMET profiling, comparative critical analysis against existing imidazole moieties, and optimization of metabolic stability to enhance the developmental viability. Additionally, research efforts should also be focused on multitarget-oriented pharmacophores integrating the imidazolone core with complementary bioactive motifs, along with systematic pharmacokinetic optimization to improve overall efficacy and therapeutic index. Furthermore, comprehensive in vivo evaluation and long-term safety profiling of imidazolone scaffolds remain comparatively underexplored. Overall, imidazolones represent a structurally adaptable platform for future therapeutic development, addressing complex diseases such as cancer and antimicrobial resistance.

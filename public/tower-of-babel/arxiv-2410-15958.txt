Preliminaries.

For a string
T=c_{1}c_{2}\cdots c_{n}

|T|
its length

c_{n}\cdots c_{2}c_{1}

and a substring

. We denote integer segments by

A string

is called a

of a string

if

has at least two occurrences in
T
and, for each letter

, the strings
aS
and
Sa
have strictly less occurrences in

. Note that the empty string is always a maximal repeat. For a substring

of

is called a
right
(respectively,

)

of

(respectively,

that occurs in
T
at least twice is a maximal repeat of
T
iff either
S
is a prefix or it has at least two left extensions, and either
S
is a suffix or it has at least two right extensions.
Let

denote the sets of maximal repeats in a string

.

Throughout the paper, our main focus will be on the number of right and left extensions of maximal repeats in string
T
: respectively,
\mathsf{e}_{T}=\sum_{S\in M(T)}r_{S}
and

are respectively the numbers of right and left extensions of

{\mathchoice{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\reflectbox{$\displaystyle\vec{}\mkern 4.0mu$}\cr\kern-4.30554pt\cr$\displaystyle\mathsf{e}$\cr}}}{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\reflectbox{$\textstyle\vec{}\mkern 4.0mu$}\cr\kern-4.30554pt\cr$\textstyle\mathsf{e}$\cr}}}{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\reflectbox{$\scriptstyle\vec{}\mkern 4.0mu$}\cr\kern-3.01389pt\cr$\scriptstyle\mathsf{e}$\cr}}}{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\reflectbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\vec{}\mkern 4.0mu$}\cr\kern-2.15277pt\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{e}$\cr}}}}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}(k-i+1)\in\Theta(k^{2})
. It is easy to see that
n=|T_{k}|\in\Theta(k^{2})
and, thus,
\frac{{\mathchoice{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\reflectbox{$\displaystyle\vec{}\mkern 4.0mu$}\cr\kern-3.01389pt\cr$\displaystyle\mathsf{e}$\cr}}}{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\reflectbox{$\textstyle\vec{}\mkern 4.0mu$}\cr\kern-3.01389pt\cr$\textstyle\mathsf{e}$\cr}}}{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\reflectbox{$\scriptstyle\vec{}\mkern 4.0mu$}\cr\kern-2.10971pt\cr$\scriptstyle\mathsf{e}$\cr}}}{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\reflectbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\vec{}\mkern 4.0mu$}\cr\kern-1.50694pt\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{e}$\cr}}}}}{\mathsf{e}}\in\Theta(k)=\Theta(\sqrt{n})
.

The alphabet size of
T_{k}
is
\sigma=2k\in\Theta(\sqrt{n})
.
Now, to complete the picture, we show that the alphabet-dependent upper bound of Theorem
1
is asymptotically tight for any range of the alphabet size.

Theorem 2
.

For any integers
n>1
and
\sigma
such that
1<\sigma\leq n
, one can construct a string
T
of length
\Theta(n)
over an alphabet of size
\Theta(\sigma)
for which the following inequality holds:
\frac{{\mathchoice{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\reflectbox{$\displaystyle\vec{}\mkern 4.0mu$}\cr\kern-3.01389pt\cr$\displaystyle\mathsf{e}$\cr}}}{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\reflectbox{$\textstyle\vec{}\mkern 4.0mu$}\cr\kern-3.01389pt\cr$\textstyle\mathsf{e}$\cr}}}{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\reflectbox{$\scriptstyle\vec{}\mkern 4.0mu$}\cr\kern-2.10971pt\cr$\scriptstyle\mathsf{e}$\cr}}}{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\reflectbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\vec{}\mkern 4.0mu$}\cr\kern-1.50694pt\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{e}$\cr}}}}}{\mathsf{e}}\in\Theta(\min\{\frac{n}{\sigma},\sigma\})
, where
\mathsf{e}
and

\textstyle\vec{}\mkern 4.0mu

\textstyle\mathsf{e}

denote the number of right and left extensions, respectively, of all maximal repeats in
T
.

Proof.

Consider the string

T=b_{1}a^{k}\$b_{2}a^{k}\$b_{3}a^{k}\$\cdots b_{\sigma}a^{k}\$

over the alphabet
\{\$,a,b_{1},\ldots,b_{\sigma}\}
of size
\Theta(\sigma
). Since
|T|\in\Theta(k\sigma)
, we obtain
|T|\in\Theta(n)
by setting
k=\lceil n/\sigma\rceil
. The set of maximal repeats in
T
is
\{\varepsilon,a,aa,\ldots,a^{k-1},a^{k}\$\}
. Each
a^{i}
, for
1\leq i<k
, has
\sigma+1
left extensions
a,b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{\sigma}
and only two right extensions
\$
and
a
. The repeat
a^{k}\$
has
\sigma
left extensions
b_{1},\ldots,b_{\sigma}
and
\sigma-1
right extensions
b_{2},\ldots,b_{\sigma}
. The empty maximal repeat
\varepsilon
has
\sigma+2
left and right extensions. Overall,
T
has
{\mathchoice{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\reflectbox{$\displaystyle\vec{}\mkern 4.0mu$}\cr\kern-4.30554pt\cr$\displaystyle\mathsf{e}$\cr}}}{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\reflectbox{$\textstyle\vec{}\mkern 4.0mu$}\cr\kern-4.30554pt\cr$\textstyle\mathsf{e}$\cr}}}{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\reflectbox{$\scriptstyle\vec{}\mkern 4.0mu$}\cr\kern-3.01389pt\cr$\scriptstyle\mathsf{e}$\cr}}}{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\reflectbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\vec{}\mkern 4.0mu$}\cr\kern-2.15277pt\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{e}$\cr}}}}\in\Theta(k\sigma)=\Theta(n)
left extensions and
\mathsf{e}\in\Theta(k+\sigma)
right extensions.

•

If
\sigma\geq\sqrt{n}
, then since
k=\lceil n/\sigma\rceil
, we obtain
k\leq\sigma
.
Hence
\mathsf{e}\in\Theta(k+\sigma)=\Theta(\sigma)
.
Thus
\frac{{\mathchoice{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\reflectbox{$\displaystyle\vec{}\mkern 4.0mu$}\cr\kern-3.01389pt\cr$\displaystyle\mathsf{e}$\cr}}}{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\reflectbox{$\textstyle\vec{}\mkern 4.0mu$}\cr\kern-3.01389pt\cr$\textstyle\mathsf{e}$\cr}}}{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\reflectbox{$\scriptstyle\vec{}\mkern 4.0mu$}\cr\kern-2.10971pt\cr$\scriptstyle\mathsf{e}$\cr}}}{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\reflectbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\vec{}\mkern 4.0mu$}\cr\kern-1.50694pt\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{e}$\cr}}}}}{\mathsf{e}}\in\Theta(\frac{n}{\sigma})=\Theta(\min\{\frac{n}{\sigma},\sigma\})
holds for the string
T
.

•

If
\sigma<\sqrt{n}
, then since
k=\lceil n/\sigma\rceil
, we obtain
k\geq\sigma
.
Hence,
\mathsf{e}\in\Theta(k+\sigma)=\Theta(k)=\Theta(\frac{n}{\sigma})
. Thus,
\frac{{\mathchoice{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\reflectbox{$\displaystyle\vec{}\mkern 4.0mu$}\cr\kern-3.01389pt\cr$\displaystyle\mathsf{e}$\cr}}}{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\reflectbox{$\textstyle\vec{}\mkern 4.0mu$}\cr\kern-3.01389pt\cr$\textstyle\mathsf{e}$\cr}}}{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\reflectbox{$\scriptstyle\vec{}\mkern 4.0mu$}\cr\kern-2.10971pt\cr$\scriptstyle\mathsf{e}$\cr}}}{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\reflectbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\vec{}\mkern 4.0mu$}\cr\kern-1.50694pt\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{e}$\cr}}}}}{\mathsf{e}}\in\Theta(\sigma)=\Theta(\min\{\frac{n}{\sigma},\sigma\})
holds for the string
T
.

This completes the proof.
∎

Evaluating the Effectiveness and Perception of QR Codes as Teaching–Learning Tools in the Department of Medicine for Phase 3 MBBS Students at a Medical College in Central India
Authors: Anurag Kumar Jain, Jitendra Singh Dangi, Mohita Pandey, Maneesh Jain, Talha Saad, Satyendra Mishra
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:Quick response (QR) codes are emerging as practical tools to supplement clinical teaching by providing instant access to educational content. This study aimed to evaluate the effectiveness and perception of QR codes as teaching–learning (T-L) tools among Phase 3 MBBS students in the Department of Medicine.Methods:A cross-sectional, mixed-method study was conducted among 100 medical students. QR codes linking to videos, clinical guidelines, and case-based content were placed in ward areas. Effectiveness was assessed using a preintervention and postintervention MCQ test (20 items), while perception was measured using a 5-point Likert scale questionnaire and optional feedback. Statistical analysis included paired t-tests and descriptive statistics.Results:The mean pretest score was 10.63 ± 1.91, which improved significantly to 14.03 ± 2.26 in the post-test (P < 0.001). Student perception was favorable, with over 50% rating QR codes highly for ease of access, engagement, and overall usefulness. Thematic feedback revealed QR codes enhanced self-directed and context-specific learning.Conclusion:QR codes are effective, low-cost tools that enhance clinical learning and student engagement. They hold promise for scalable integration into undergraduate medical education, especially in resource-limited settings.

INTRODUCTION

The integration of technology into medical education has transformed traditional pedagogical approaches, promoting active, flexible, and student-centered learning. In this digital age, quick response (QR) codes have emerged as a simple yet powerful tool to bridge offline and online learning by instantly connecting learners to digital content such as videos, images, quizzes, clinical guidelines, or supplementary notes using a smartphone scan. QR codes are increasingly being explored in health sciences education as an innovative solution to enhance accessibility to learning resources, especially in clinical and ward-based teaching settings.

The utility of QR codes lies in their ability to provide point-of-care learning without disrupting clinical routines. Students can quickly access information relevant to a case, facilitating deeper understanding and clinical reasoning. Studies have shown that QR code–based learning improves knowledge retention and engagement among medical students, particularly in environments where time and resources are limited.[12] Moreover, learners have reported high levels of satisfaction with QR code use, citing ease of access and convenience.[3]

Despite their potential, the adoption of QR codes in Indian medical colleges remains limited. This study aims to evaluate the effectiveness and perception of QR codes as teaching–learning tools among Phase 3 MBBS students in the Department of Medicine. By assessing students’ experiences and learning outcomes, the study seeks to generate evidence for the integration of QR code–based microlearning strategies in undergraduate medical education.

METHODS

This was a cross-sectional, mixed-method study conducted in the Department of Medicine at a tertiary care medical college in Central India over a period of 3 months. The study aimed to evaluate both the effectiveness and student perception of using QR codes as teaching–learning (T-L) tools among Phase 3 MBBS students during their clinical postings.

A total of 100 Phase 3 MBBS students were enrolled using convenience sampling. Inclusion criteria included students currently undergoing clinical postings in the Medicine department and willing to participate. Ethical approval was obtained from the Institutional Ethics Committee, and informed consent was secured from all participants.

QR codes were generated and placed strategically on patient bedside charts, ward posters, case files, and demonstration models. These codes linked to curated learning resources such as clinical examination videos, diagnostic guidelines, drug protocols, and brief case-based quizzes hosted on cloud platforms. Faculty and interns guided students on how and when to access the QR codes during ward rounds.

The effectiveness of QR-based learning was assessed through a structured preintervention and post-intervention MCQ test (20 questions) covering key clinical competencies. The perception and user experience were evaluated using a validated 5-point Likert scale questionnaire (covering accessibility, relevance, engagement, and satisfaction) followed by optional open-ended feedback.

Quantitative data were analyzed using SPSS version 25. Paired t-tests were applied to compare pretest and post-test scores. Descriptive statistics summarized the perception scores. Qualitative feedback was analyzed thematically.

This methodology enabled the comprehensive assessment of both cognitive gains and learner satisfaction, offering insight into the feasibility and impact of incorporating QR code-based microlearning into routine clinical education.

RESULTS

A total of 100 Phase 3 MBBS students participated in the study. The mean pretest score was 10.63 ± 1.91, while the post-test score increased significantly to 14.03 ± 2.26, indicating improved knowledge acquisition following the integration of QR code–based learning. This difference was statistically significant (P < 0.001, paired t-test).

Students’ perception of QR codes as teaching–learning tools was overall positive. On a 5-point Likert scale, a majority rated “Ease of Access” (53%) and “Engagement” (53%) with scores of 4 or 5. “Overall Usefulness” was also highly rated, with 54% of students marking it 4 or 5. These findings suggest that QR codes were well-received and effectively supported clinical learning [Tables 1 and 2].

DISCUSSION

This study demonstrates that QR code integration in clinical teaching significantly improves knowledge acquisition and is well-accepted by undergraduate medical students. The significant increase in post-test scores suggests that QR codes, when embedded in clinical settings, can enhance engagement and retention of information through microlearning—a technique proven effective in high-pressure learning environments such as hospitals.[4]

The use of QR codes allowed students to access contextually relevant content at their convenience, aligning with adult learning principles and promoting self-directed learning. Studies by House et al.[5] and Tang et al.[6] have shown that mobile-enabled learning strategies, such as QR codes, improve clinical reasoning and bridge theory–practice gaps.

Student perception in this study was highly favorable, with the majority rating the tools positively for accessibility, relevance, and usefulness. This is consistent with findings from international research that show mobile-based learning increases learner satisfaction and motivation.[7] Moreover, embedding QR codes in ward materials reduced the cognitive load often associated with traditional bedside teaching, offering immediate reinforcement of concepts.[8]

Despite these benefits, challenges such as device compatibility, internet connectivity, and digital literacy must be addressed for broader implementation. As reported by Gurupur et al.,[9] the success of such interventions depends on faculty orientation and curricular integration. Future studies could examine long-term retention and objective clinical outcomes related to QR-based learning.

CONCLUSION

QR codes represent a low-cost, scalable, and effective adjunct to traditional clinical teaching, especially in resource-constrained settings. Their use should be explored further to enhance interactive learning in undergraduate medical education.

Trajectory Generator Matching for Time Series
T. Jahn, J. Chemseddine, P. Hagemann, C. Wald, G. Steidl

Abstract
Accurately modeling time-continuous stochastic processes from irregular observations remains a significant challenge. In this paper, we leverage ideas from generative modeling of image data to push the boundary of time series generation. For this, we find new generators of SDEs and jump processes, inspired by trajectory flow matching, that have the marginal distributions of the time series of interest. Specifically, we can handle discontinuities of the underlying processes by parameterizing the jump kernel densities by scaled Gaussians that allow for closed form formulas of the corresponding Kullback-Leibler divergence in the loss. Unlike most other approaches, we are able to handle irregularly sampled time series.

Introduction

Generative modeling has shown impressive results in imaging and natural sciences
corso2023diffdock
;
rombach2022high
;
pmlr-v37-sohl-dickstein15
;
song2021scorebased
. However, the generation of time series, which arise in the context of modeling financial, meteorological and patient vital data has received less attention
buehler2020generating
;
jia2019neural
;
kerrigan2024functional
;
kidger2021neural
;
zhang2024trajectory
.
In particular, in the study of financial markets and price prediction
GULMEZ2023120346
;
wu2023graph
, the (conditional) generation of time series data is an important cornerstone. For instance, in deep hedging
bühler2018deephedging
, simulation of market data is crucial to learn appropriate hedging strategies. Often for price predictions, not only the most likely next price is of interest, but also its uncertainty. Therefore, in a Bayesian way we would like to model the prior of such time series data using neural networks.

The usual approaches to model the time series distribution involve adversarial training
kidger2021neural
, backpropagation through the SDE solver
jia2019neural
, or fitting an object as high dimensional as the number of discrete time points
kerrigan2024functional
. Another difficulty that many methods face is that they rely on fixed time discretizations, and therefore are not feasible for many medical datasets, where points are measured irregularly. Although it is sometimes possible to fill those missing values as in
yoon2019time
, we are interested in exploring a method which is not reliant on a fixed time grid.

Simulation-free
approaches which do not have to solve an ODE/SDE during training,
such as flow matching
albergo2023stochastic
;
lipman2023flow
;
liu2023flow
;
Ws2025
or score-based diffusion
song2021scorebased
have significantly improved the performance of generative models compared to adversarial networks
gan
or simulation-based methods
chen2018
. Recently, such simulation-free methods have been applied to time series generation with regular observation times employing a high-dimensional latent distribution in
kerrigan2024functional
. In contrast, the authors of
zhang2024trajectory
proposed to view time series generation as essentially generating one-dimensional curves via interpolating between the discretization points. This interpolation is done by learning an SDE between the discrete time points and taking the realizations at previous time points as conditions.
During inference, this is akin to doing the next word prediction in GPT models
NIPS2017_3f5ee243
.

However, processes in economics are often fundamentally discontinuous as buying or selling a stock may induce a jump. Inspired by
holderrieth2025generator
, we therefore propose to generalize the framework of
zhang2024trajectory
such that we can handle both SDEs and jump processes. Jump processes have been already employed in
campbell2023transdimensional
for the forward process inside a diffusion framework
song2021scorebased
, in
benton
for parameterizing and learning generators via Markov chains or learning Schrödinger bridges via h-transforms
zlotchevski2025schrodingerbridgeproblemjump
. Our work will base on the generator matching approach by Holderrieth et al.
holderrieth2025generator
.

For this, we first reframe the approach of
zhang2024trajectory
into the language of generator matching
holderrieth2025generator
. The generator can be decomposed into an SDE and a jump part
von1965fast
;
holderrieth2025generator
.
Since the approach of
zhang2024trajectory
has a singularity when reaching the final time point, which is detrimental in either training or inference, we modify it by allowing small amounts of Gaussian noise at known discretization points, stabilizing the Brownian bridges used in
zhang2024trajectory
. For this specific Markov process, we calculate the generator of the now stabilized SDE and find an expression for the jump part of the generator, allowing us to combine the SDE and jump part via the Markov superposition principle
holderrieth2025generator
. In contrast to
holderrieth2025generator
, we parameterize the jumps not via binning, but by Gaussian-like functions. This allows to give analytical expression for the Kullback-Leibler divergence used in the corresponding loss function.
Since we have to handle non Markov time series, we need to include memory as conditions in our next point predictors. The memory has already been used in
zhang2024trajectory
. Here we prove that if we include memory, then our learned time series approximates the true joint distribution and does not only capture marginals.
In the numerical section, we verify our method on two synthetic data sets and compare it with
zhang2024trajectory
.

Contributions.

We learn a generative model for sampling new time series given samples of time series at possible different times.
We combine generator matching techniques for Markov processes that can also take also jump processes into account with conditional trajectory interpolation. We list our contributions as follows.

1.

For certain processes

2.

We propose to parameterize the density of the jump kernel

holderrieth2025generator
.

3.

We rigorously derive all our results for irregular time series and justify the use of memory analytically,
see Remark
7
.

4.

We validate our approaches on a synthetic dataset and one inspired by financial mathematics.

A
. All proofs can be found in the Appendix
B

Problem Setting

We are given
N
time series

\mathcal{X}:=\{x^{1},...,x^{N}\}
,

x^{i}\coloneqq(x^{i}_{t^{i}_{j}})_{j=0}^{n_{i}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

sampled at different times

0=t^{i}_{0}<t^{i}_{1}<\ldots<t^{i}_{n_{i}}=T

which are i.i.d observations at discrete times of realizations of a random process
X=(X_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
.
Such data might be encountered in high frequency financial markets, since trades, placing and executing of orders can occur at any point in time, or in the context of clinical trajectories of patients’ vital signs evolution.

We assume that
X
is a random variable in the function space of all right continuous functions in
t
with left limits.
Then, the distribution of
X
is uniquely determined by all finite-dimensional marginals
(
billingsley2013convergence
, Section 13)
, so that we aim to learn the latter ones.
More precisely, for given times
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<...<t_{n}=T
(not necessarily the above ones), we want to sample from
P_{X_{t_{0}},...,X_{t_{n}}}

by learning neural networks based on our data in
\mathcal{X}
. Using disintegration, see Appendix
A
, we know that

\displaystyle P_{X_{t_{0}},\ldots,X_{t_{n}}}=P_{X_{t_{n}}|X_{t_{n-1}}=x_{n-1},\ldots,X_{t_{0}}=x_{0}}\times_{x_{n-1},\ldots,x_{0}}\cdots\times_{x_{1},x_{0}}P_{X_{t_{1}}|X_{t_{0}}=x_{0}}\times_{x_{0}}P_{X_{t_{0}}}.

(1)

Hence, in order to generate a new time series, we start with an initial value
x_{0}\sim P_{X_{t_{0}}}
and then draw inductively
x_{j+1}\sim P_{X_{t_{j+1}}|X_{t_{j}},\ldots,X_{t_{0}}}
. In order to sample from the latter distribution, we will learn for

x_{0},\ldots,x_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
a Markov process
(Y_{t}^{x_{0},\ldots,x_{j}})_{t\in[t_{j},t_{j+1}]}
starting in
Y_{t_{j}}^{x_{0},\ldots,x_{j}}=x_{j}
such that approximately

\displaystyle Y_{t_{j+1}}^{x_{0},\ldots,x_{j}}\sim P_{X_{t_{j{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}+1}}}|X_{t_{j}}=x_{j},\ldots,X_{t_{0}}=x_{0}}.

(2)

For learning this interpolating Markov process we employ ideas from generator matching which we explain in the next section.

Markov Processes via Generators

After a brief recall of Markov processes and their generators,
we propose simple Markov processes with drift-diffusion and jump generators that can be found analytically
in Proposition
2
i) and ii), respectively.
Note that in contrast to the Markov process with drift-diffusion generator in Proposition
2
iii)
suggested in
zhang2024trajectory
,
we do not have to cope with singularity issues and our process in Proposition
2
i) is a stabilized version of the Brownian bridge. We first introduce Markov proceesses, generators and the corresponding Kolmogorov forward equation, based on
holderrieth2025generator
.

Let
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denote the Borel sets on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and

\mathcal{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the space of bounded, (Borel-)measurable functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Further, let

\mathcal{M}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denote the space of non-negative measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

the probability measures and
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

the probability measures with finite second moments.

We consider a Markov process
(Y_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The process can be described by its transition semigroup, which acts on test functions and is the continuous analogue of transition matrices of discrete Markov chains (in the sense that together with the initial distribution at
t=0
, it completely determines the distribution of the process). The transition semigroup and hence the process
can be characterized by its generator

(\mathcal{L}_{t})_{t}
,
mapping from a subset of
\mathcal{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
into the measurable functions by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{t}f(x)\coloneqq\lim_{h\downarrow 0}\frac{\mathbb{E}[f(Y_{t+h})-f(Y_{t})|Y_{t}=x]}{h}.

(3)

It can be rewritten in the form
holderrieth2025generator
;
von1965fast

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{t}f(x)=\underbrace{\langle\nabla_{x}f(x),u_{t}(x)\rangle}_{\rm drift}+\underbrace{\frac{1}{2}\langle\Sigma_{t}^{2}(x),\nabla^{2}f(x)\rangle}_{\rm diffusion}+\underbrace{\int(f(y)-f(x))\,Q_{t}({\rm d}y,x)}_{\rm jump},

(4)

where
u_{t}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the drift,
\Sigma_{t}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{2d}
is the diffusion coefficient and

Q_{t}:\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is the rate (jump) kernel (i.e.,
x\mapsto Q_{t}(B,x)
is measurable and
B\mapsto Q_{t}(B,x)
is a finite positive measure). If
X_{t}
has
law
P_{t}
with density
p_{t}
and
Q_{t}(\cdot,x)\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
are given by Lebesgue densities
q_{t}(y,x)\,\mathrm{d}y
, then the

Kolmogorov forward equation
(KFE)

\partial_{t}\langle p_{t},f\rangle=\langle p_{t},\mathcal{L}_{t}f\rangle

holds true. In this paper, we restrict our attention to
\Sigma_{t}^{2}=\eta^{2}I_{d}
with an appropriately given
\eta>0
. Then, taking the adjoint of the KFE provides
the following PDE

\partial_{t}p_{t}=-\nabla\cdot(p_{t}u_{t})+\frac{1}{2}\eta^{2}\Delta p_{t}+\int p_{t}(y)q_{t}(x,y)-p_{t}(x)q_{t}(y,x)\,\mathrm{d}y.

(5)

Given a generator
\mathcal{L}_{t}
which we address in the following by the triple
(u_{t},\eta,q_{t})
, we can approximate a corresponding Markov process by the following Algorithm
1
, see

(
holderrieth2025generator
, Appendix B)
.

Approximating a Markov process with generator
\mathcal{L}_{t}=(u_{t},\eta,q_{t})

Given:

u_{t},\eta
,
q_{t}(\cdot,x)=\lambda_{t}(x)J_{t}(\cdot,x)
,
\lambda_{t}\geq 0
,

, step size

do

,
\epsilon_{t}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)

x^{D}_{t+h}=x_{t}+hu_{t}(x_{t})+\sqrt{h}\,\eta\,(x_{t})\epsilon_{t}^{i}

x_{t+h}=mx^{J}_{t+h}+(1-m)x^{D}_{t+h}

end

for

Return:
x_{1}
approximately distributed as
X_{1}

Remark 1
.

Note that for
u_{t}=0,\sigma_{t}=0
we have
x^{i,D}_{t+h}=x^{i}_{t}
and thus obtain a pure jump process. On the other hand, for

Our learning of the conditional distribution is based on the following time-inhomogenous Markov processes, whose generators we can give explicitly.

Proposition 2
.

For arbitrary fixed
x_{0},x_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
set
m_{t}\coloneqq(1-t)x_{0}+tx_{1}
and

\tau_{t}\coloneqq\eta^{2}t(1-t)+\rho^{2}
with

\eta>0
. Let
P_{t}=P_{t}(\cdot,x_{0},x_{1})=\mathcal{N}(m_{t},\tau_{t})
with density
p_{t}
. Then each of the following generators

\mathcal{L}_{t}^{{\rm Diff}}=\mathcal{L}_{t}^{{\rm Diff},x_{0},x_{1}}

\mathcal{L}_{t}^{\rm{Jump}}=\mathcal{L}_{t}^{{\rm Jump},x_{0},x_{1}}

fulfills the KFE and thus give rise to a Markov process

with marginals
Y_{t}\sim P_{t}
:

i)

\mathcal{L}^{{\rm Diff}}_{t}f(x)=\nabla f(x)^{\mathrm{T}}u_{t}(x)+\frac{\eta^{2}}{2}\Delta f(x)
, where

u_{t}^{x_{0},x_{1}}=u_{t}\coloneqq x_{1}-x_{0}-(x-m_{t})\frac{\eta^{2}t}{\tau_{t}}
.

An associated Markov process is given by the solution of the SDE

\displaystyle\,\mathrm{d}Y_{t}=u_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t+\eta\,\mathrm{d}W_{t};\quad Y_{0}=x_{0}.

ii)

\mathcal{L}^{{\rm Jump}}_{t}f(x)=\int\big(f(y)-f(x)\big)q_{t}(y,x)\,\mathrm{d}y
, where
q_{t}^{x_{0},x_{1}}(y,x)=q_{t}(y,x)=\coloneqq\lambda_{t}(x)J_{t}(y)

and

\displaystyle J_{t}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{\max(0,\xi_{t})p_{t}}{\int\max(0,\xi_{t})p_{t}\,\mathrm{d}y},\quad\lambda_{t}\coloneqq\max\left(0,-\xi_{t}\right),

(6)

\displaystyle\xi_{t}(y)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{\eta^{2}(1-2t)}{2\tau_{t}}\Big(\frac{\|y-m_{t}\|^{2}}{\tau_{t}}+2a_{t}^{\mathrm{T}}\frac{y-m_{t}}{\sqrt{\tau_{t}}}-d\Big),\quad a_{t}\coloneqq\frac{\sqrt{\tau_{t}}}{\eta^{2}(1-2t)}(x_{1}-x_{0}).

(7)

iii)

If
\rho=0
, then
\mathcal{L}^{{\rm Diff}}_{t}
in i) simplifies towards

and the SDE becomes

\displaystyle\,\mathrm{d}Y_{t}=\frac{x_{1}-Y_{t}}{1-t}\,\mathrm{d}t+\eta\,\mathrm{d}W_{t},\quad Y_{0}=x_{0}.

For
\rho=0
, we recover the drift of

P_{0}(\cdot,x_{0},x_{1})=\delta_{x_{0}},\quad P_{0}(\cdot,x_{0},x_{1})=\delta_{x_{1}},

and we see that the Markov process generated by

and
x_{1}
.
For
\rho>0
, we don’t encounter singularities and have

P_{0}(\cdot,x_{0},x_{1})=\mathcal{N}(x_{0},\rho^{2}),\quad P_{1}(\cdot,x_{0},x_{1})=\mathcal{N}(x_{1},\rho^{2}).

7
Limitations and Conclusions

We introduced trajectory generator matching, applying the generator matching framework to the generation of possibly irregularly sampled time series. To this end, we introduced a stabilized bridge between the data distributions and a derived generator for a corresponding SDE and jump process. We proposed a Gaussian approximation for the jump kernel and derived closed form formulas for the KL divergence with a wide class of jump kernels. In addition, we used a memory heuristic to recover the correct joint distributions. We verified the anaytical formulas and as well as the performance of our method on synthetic data and some Black Scholes model. We limited ourselves to parametrize the rate kernel by Gaussians. The next step would be to employ more complex models, such as normalizing flows or Gaussian mixtures. Another limitation is our restriction to one-dimensional data in the experiments. But conceptually, the autoregressive nature of our algorithm allows scaling to higher dimensional data such as videos and their restoration
martin2025pnpflow
. Lastly, our algorithm could potentially be used to perform uncertainty quantification in price prediction frameworks. In terms of limitations, the Markov superposition and our methods introduce auxiliary hyperparameters which need to be tuned. Further, there is a need for better evaluation metrics of time series data, which would be beneficial for chosing the best model using a validation set.

Additional Definitions

Let
\mathcal{M}_{+}^{\text{ac}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denote the space of absolutely continuous non-negative measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with respect to the Lebesgue measure.
Our loss function for the jump process relies on the
Kullback-Leibler divergence

\mathrm{KL}:\mathcal{M}_{+}^{\text{ac}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\mathcal{M}_{+}^{\text{ac}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to[0,\infty]

defined by

\displaystyle\mathrm{KL}(p,q):=\int p(x)\log\Big(\frac{p(x)}{q(x)}\Big)-p(x)+q(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

if
p(x)=0
whenever
q(x)=0
for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and

\mathrm{KL}(p,q)\coloneqq\infty

p=q
a.e..

For a measure

and projections

\pi^{x}_{\sharp}\alpha
and
\pi^{y}_{\sharp}\alpha

\alpha
, respectively.
Then for
\alpha\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})

for every measurable, bounded function

Similarly, we define for a measure

The notation of disintegration is directly related to Markov kernels.
A

\int f(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}\alpha(x,y)\coloneqq\int\int f(x,y),\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{K}(x,\cdot)(y)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)

Identifying
\alpha_{x}(B)

\pi^{1}

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{d\,\eta^{2}}{2\tau_{t}}\left(\frac{t_{0}+t_{1}-2t}{(t_{1}-t_{0})^{2}}-1\right)\Big).

Further, we obtain

\displaystyle\Delta p_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=-\nabla p_{t}^{\mathrm{T}}\frac{x-m_{t}}{\tau_{t}}-\frac{d\,p_{t}}{\tau_{t}}=p_{t}\Big(\frac{\|x-m_{t}\|^{2}}{\tau_{t}^{2}}-\frac{d}{\tau_{t}}\Big)

so that

\displaystyle-\nabla\cdot(p_{t}u_{t})+\frac{1}{2}\eta^{2}\Delta p_{t}

\displaystyle=p_{t}\Big(\frac{(x_{1}-x_{0})^{\mathrm{T}}(x-m_{t})}{\tau_{t}(t_{1}-t_{0})}+\|x-m_{t}\|^{2}\frac{\eta^{2}}{2\tau_{t}^{2}}\left(\frac{t_{0}+t_{1}-2t}{(t_{1}-t_{0})^{2}}-1\right)

(26)

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{d\,\eta^{2}}{2\tau_{t}}\left(\frac{t_{0}+t_{1}-2t}{(t_{1}-t_{0})^{2}}-1\right)+\frac{\eta^{2}}{2}\left(\frac{\|x-m_{t}\|^{2}}{\tau_{t}^{2}}-\frac{d}{\tau_{t}}\right)\Big)

(27)

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{\eta^{2}}{2\tau_{t}}\left(-d\left(\frac{t_{0}+t_{1}-2t}{(t_{1}-t_{0})^{2}}-1\right)-d\right)+\frac{(x_{1}-x_{0})^{\mathrm{T}}(x-m_{t})}{\tau_{t}(t_{1}-t_{0})}

(28)

\displaystyle\qquad+\|x-m_{t}\|^{2}\frac{\eta^{2}}{2\tau_{t}^{2}}\left(\frac{t_{0}+t_{1}-2t}{(t_{1}-t_{0})^{2}}-1+1\right)\Big)

(29)

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}p_{t}(x).

(30)

For the jump part, we get

\displaystyle\int p_{t}(y)q_{t}(x,y)-p_{t}(x)q_{t}(y,x){\rm d}y=J_{t}(x)\int\lambda_{t}(y)p_{t}(y){\rm d}y-\lambda_{t}(x)p_{t}(x)

(31)

Since

\max(0,\xi_{t})=\xi_{t}+\lambda_{t}
,
we conclude by definition of
\xi_{t}
that

\displaystyle\int\max(0,\xi_{t})p_{t}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int\xi_{t}p_{t}\,\mathrm{d}y+\int\lambda_{t}p_{t}\,\mathrm{d}y=\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\mathcal{N}(m_{t},\tau_{t})}[\xi_{t}]+\int\lambda_{t}p_{t}\,\mathrm{d}y

(32)

\displaystyle=\frac{\eta^{2}(t_{0}+t_{1}-2t)}{2(t_{1}-t_{0})^{2}\tau_{t}}(d+0-d)+\int\lambda_{t}p_{t}\,\mathrm{d}y=\int\lambda_{t}p_{t}\,\mathrm{d}y.

(33)

Hence we can rewrite

J_{t}(x)=\frac{\big(\xi_{t}(x)+\lambda_{t}(x)\big)p_{t}(x)}{\int\lambda_{t}(y)p_{t}\,\mathrm{d}y},

(34)

which is well-defined as long as
t\neq(t_{0}+t_{1})/2
. If
t=(t_{0}+t_{1})/2
, then
\lambda_{t}=0
, and we can set
J_{t}=0
. Using (
34
) in (
31
), we obtain finally

\displaystyle\int p_{t}(y)q_{t}(x,y)-p_{t}(x)q_{t}(y,x)\,\mathrm{d}y=p_{t}(x)\xi_{t}(x)=\partial_{t}p_{t}(x).

(35)

This finishes the proof.

\Box

Proof of Proposition

.

By definition of the KL-divergence, we get

\displaystyle F_{t,x}(\lambda,\mu,\sigma)=\lambda-\lambda_{t}(x)+\int q_{t}(y,x)\log\left(\frac{q_{t}(y,x)}{\lambda\mathcal{N}(\mu,\sigma\,{\rm Id}_{d})(y)}\right)\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\lambda-\lambda_{t}(x)+\int q_{t}(y,x)\left(\log(q_{t}(y,x))-\log(\lambda)+\frac{d}{2}\log(2\pi\sigma^{2})+\frac{\|y-\mu\|^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\lambda+\lambda_{t}(x)\left(\frac{d}{2}\log(2\pi\sigma^{2})-\log(\lambda)+\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}\int J_{t}(y)\|y-\mu^{J}+\mu^{J}-\mu\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\int q_{t}(y,x)\log(q_{t}(y,x))\,\mathrm{d}y-\lambda_{t}(x)

F_{t,x}

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}F_{t,x}(\lambda,\mu,\sigma)

\displaystyle\partial_{\sigma}F_{t,x}(\lambda,\mu,\sigma)

\displaystyle\partial_{\lambda}F_{t,x}(\lambda,\mu,\sigma)

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda-\lambda_{t}(x)}{\lambda}=0,

which solution

is the unique critical point of
F_{t,x}
.
This is the global minimum, since the
Hessian of

at this point is
\lambda_{t}(x)\text{diag}\big(\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}},\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}},\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\big)

Appendix C
On Remark
7

We first show that we can approximate
P_{X_{0},X_{1}}
if we can approximate
P_{X_{0}}
and
P_{X_{1}|X_{0}=x_{0}}
by
P_{Y_{0}}
and a Markov kernel
\mu(\cdot,x_{0})

Lemma 8
.

Let
P_{X_{0},X_{1}}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}),P_{Y_{0}}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
and let
\mu(\cdot,x_{0}):\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to[0,1]
be a Markov kernel. Define
\alpha\coloneqq\mu(\cdot,x_{0})\times_{x_{0}}P_{Y_{0}}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Assume that

i)

\int W_{2}^{2}(P_{X_{1}|X_{0}=a},\mu(\cdot,x_{0}))\,\mathrm{d}P_{X_{0}}(a)\leq\epsilon
,

ii)

W_{2}(P_{X_{1}|X_{0}=a},P_{X_{1}|X_{0}=b})\leq K\|a-b\|
,

iii)

W_{2}(\mu(\cdot,a),\mu(\cdot,b))\leq K\|a-b\|
.

Then we have

\displaystyle W_{2}(P_{X_{0},X_{1}},\alpha)^{2}\leq(1+20\ K^{2})W_{2}^{2}(P_{X_{0}},P_{Y_{0}})+4\epsilon.

(46)

Proof.

Let
\gamma_{a,b}\in\Gamma_{o}(P_{X_{1}|X_{0}=a},\mu(\cdot,b))
and let
\beta\in\Gamma_{o}(P_{X_{0}},P_{Y_{0}})
. First note that
\gamma_{a,b}\times_{a,b}\beta\in\Gamma(P_{X_{1},X_{2}},\alpha)
. Then

\displaystyle W_{2}^{2}(P_{X_{0},X_{1}},\alpha)

\displaystyle\leq\int\|(a,x)-(b,y)\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma_{a,b}(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}\beta(a,b)

(47)

\displaystyle=\int\|a-b\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\beta+\int\|x-y\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma_{a,b}(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}\beta

(48)

\displaystyle=W_{2}^{2}(P_{X_{0}},P_{Y_{0}})+\int W_{2}^{2}\left(P_{X_{2}|X_{1}=a},\mu(\cdot,b)\right)\,\mathrm{d}\beta

(49)

\displaystyle\leq W_{2}^{2}(P_{X_{0}},P_{Y_{0}})+\int\left(W_{2}(P_{X_{1}|X_{0}=a},P_{X_{1}|X_{0}=b})+W_{2}(P_{X_{1}|X_{0}=b},\mu(\cdot,b))\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\beta

(50)

\displaystyle\leq W_{2}^{2}(P_{X_{0}},P_{Y_{0}})+2\ \int W_{2}^{2}(P_{X_{1}|X_{0}=a},P_{X_{1}|X_{0}=b})+W^{2}_{2}(P_{X_{1}|X_{0}=b},\mu(\cdot,b))\,\mathrm{d}\beta

(51)

\displaystyle\leq(1+2\ K^{2})W_{2}^{2}(P_{X_{0}},P_{Y_{0}})+2\int W^{2}_{2}(P_{X_{1}|X_{0}=b},\mu(\cdot,b))\,\mathrm{d}\beta

(52)

\displaystyle\leq(1+2\ K^{2})W_{2}^{2}(P_{X_{0}},P_{Y_{0}})

(53)

\displaystyle+2\int\left(W_{2}(P_{X_{1}|X_{0}=b},P_{X_{1}|X_{0}=a})+W_{2}(P_{X_{1}|X_{0}=a},\mu(\cdot,a))+W_{2}(\mu(\cdot,a),\mu(\cdot,a))\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\beta

(54)

\displaystyle\leq(1+2\ K^{2})W_{2}^{2}(P_{X_{0}},P_{Y_{0}})

(55)

\displaystyle+8\int W^{2}_{2}(P_{X_{1}|X_{0}=b},P_{X_{1}|X_{0}=a})+4\ W^{2}_{2}(P_{X_{1}|X_{0}=a},\mu(\cdot,a))+8\ W^{2}_{2}(\mu(\cdot,a),\mu(\cdot,b))\,\mathrm{d}\beta

(56)

\displaystyle\leq(1+20\ K^{2})W_{2}^{2}(P_{X_{0}},P_{Y_{0}})+4\epsilon.

(57)

∎

This result can be easily extended to several time points as follows.

Proposition 9
.

Let
X_{0},\ldots,X_{n},Y_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{P})

(58)

Proof.

This follows via induction from Lemma

.

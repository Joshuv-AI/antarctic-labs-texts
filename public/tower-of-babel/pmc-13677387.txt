Comparative evaluation of methods for measuring standardized ileal digestibility of amino acids for different sorghum varieties in broilers
Authors: Huajin Zhou, Guilan Xu, Tahir Mahmood, Yves Mercier, Jianmin Yuan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study investigated the effects of two evaluation methods (direct vs. substitution) on standardized ileal amino acid digestibility (SIDAA) and intestinal digestive physiology in broilers fed sorghum from six regional sources. There were 336 27-d-old Cobb broilers assigned to 14 treatments, including a nitrogen-free diet (NFD), a corn–soybean meal (CSM) basal diet, and 12 experimental diets arranged in a 2 × 6 factorial arrangement. In the direct method, sorghum served as the only source of dietary amino acids whereas in substitution method the test sorghum replaced 40% (w/w) of the basal diet. The six sorghums were Yingkou (YK), Chifeng (CF), Yangzhou (YZ), Liaoning (LN), Zhangjiakou (ZJK) from China, and one from the United States (US). The digestibility trial was conducted from 27 to 29 d of age. The substitution method improved jejunal villus height and crypt depth, α-amylase and chymotrypsin activities, compared with the direct method (P < 0.05). YK sorghum with higher tannin contents reduced villus height, crypt depth, and chymotrypsin activity but increased ileal Occludin and ZO‑1 expression (P < 0.05). However, US, YZ, CF, and LN sorghum with lower tannin contents improved broiler digestive physiology. Broilers fed US exhibited the highest weight gain, and those fed LN showed the greatest villus height, crypt depth, and chymotrypsin activity (P < 0.05). Significant sorghum source × evaluation method interactions were observed for the AIDAA and SIDAA of all evaluated amino acids (P < 0.001), with more extensive method-related differences observed in YK and ZJK sorghum than in LN and US sorghum. Sorghum-based diets altered ileal microbiota, reducing Lactobacillus abundance and increasing Streptococcus, Corynebacterium, and Enterococcus (P < 0.05). The direct method increased (P < 0.05) the abundance of Turicibacter and Brachybacterium, whereas the substitution method produced a microbial composition similar to the basal diet. In conclusion, LN and US sorghum showed favorable AA digestibility, and the substitution method generally yielded higher AIDAA and SIDAA values and more favorable short-term responses in digestive enzyme activity and ileal microbial profiles than the direct method. The method-related differences in high-tannin sorghum highlight the importance of considering anti-nutritional factor content when selecting a method for evaluating AA digestibility.

Introduction

Corn serves as the primary energy source in broiler feed but rising tariffs and trade restrictions have significantly increased its cost. Alternatively, sorghum is a high-energy raw material, with about 70% starch content. Good quality sorghum has 95% or greater feeding value of yellow dent maize, and no significant difference between sorghum-based diet and corn-based diet for low-tannin varieties (Ronda et al., 2019). China has abundant sorghum resources, with a production of 3.10 million tonnes in 2022 (FAO, 2022). This makes sorghum a cost-effective alternative to reduce reliance on imported corn.

The predictability of nutrient digestibility of feed ingredients is critical for accurately formulating cost-effective diets. However, reported values for sorghum vary considerably across databases, due to differences in environment and origin (FEDNA, 2010; CVB, 2018). In the 2024 to 2025 season, China's sorghum production is estimated to remain 3 million tonnes, with major production regions spread across northern, northeastern, southwestern, and eastern provinces (USDA, 2024). Given this broad geographic distribution, assessing regional nutritional differences of Chinese sorghum varieties and developing a corresponding database would be very useful for the local feed industry.

Standardized ileal amino acid digestibility (SIDAA) calculated by correcting apparent ileal digestibility (AIDAA) for basal ileal endogenous amino acid (IEAA) losses offers greater additivity and may help to reduce nitrogen excretion by minimizing over- supplementation of amino acids (Cowieson et al., 2019). The SIDAA is typically determined using the direct, substitution, or regression methods (Nalle and Ravindran, 2010). However, the direct method is most popular primarily due to simple diet composition and subsequent calculations (Nalle and Ravindran, 2021). In this method, the test ingredient is the sole amino acid source in the assay diet (Ravindran et al., 2005). The underlying assumption in the direct method is that the amino acid digestibility of the diet is equivalent to the test ingredient (Nalle and Ravindran, 2021). But this assumption is not entirely correct as endogenous amino acid losses have a direct connection with the test diet. Furthermore, a high inclusion level of certain raw materials like sorghum, in the direct method is likely to affect the digestive physiology of broilers. Thus, the resulting digestibility estimates may be influenced by the dietary conditions created by the direct method. Morgan et al. (2022) noted that sorghum has low non-starch polysaccharides that may alter the composition of the gut microbiota of broilers. Consistently, Batonon-Alavo et al. (2016) observed a linear decline in the total tract digestibility of crude protein with increasing inclusion levels of sorghum.

In the substitution method, the test ingredient partially replaces the basal diet at a defined ratio, and the SIDAA of the test ingredient is calculated based on the difference in AA digestibility between the assay and basal diets (Nalle et al., 2010; Ravindran et al., 2017). The major assumption of the substitution method is the absence of interaction between the basal diet and the test ingredient. The substitution method has therefore been applied to low-palatability or antinutrient-rich ingredients such as sorghum and faba beans (Feng et al., 2023; Nalle et al., 2010).

Therefore, this study compared the direct and substitution methods for determining AIDAA and SIDAA of six sorghum sources in broilers. We further examined whether the effects of evaluation method differed among sorghum sources. Intestinal morphology, digestive enzyme activity, gene expression, and ileal microbiota were also evaluated to characterize short-term digestive physiological responses during the digestibility assay.

Materials and methods

Ethics approval and consent to participate

The experimental procedure for this study was conducted in accordance with the guidelines of the China Agricultural University Animal Ethics Committee (No. AW71605202-1-04) (Beijing, China).

Diets, experimental design, and animal husbandry

A total of 336 27-day-old healthy male Cobb broilers were randomly assigned to 14 dietary treatments, each with six replicate cages of four birds. The dietary treatments included: one nitrogen-free diet (NFD) for determining ileal endogenous amino acid (IEAA) losses, one corn soybean meal-basal diet (CSM) serving as the control, and 12 test diets arranged in a 2 × 6 factorial arrangement, consisting of two digestibility assessment methods (direct vs. substitution) and six sorghum sources. In the direct method, each sorghum originating from a different region served as the sole source of AA. In the substitution method, sorghum replaced 40% of the basal diet. The six resources include five Chinese sorghum from Yingkou (YK), Chifeng (CF), Yangzhou (YZ), Liaoning (LN), or Zhangjiakou (ZJK); and one from the United States (US).

Before the trial, all broilers were raised on common commercial diets, including a corn–soybean meal–based starter diet (1–14 d) and a grower diet (14–26 d), formulated to meet or exceed the nutrient requirements recommended by the NRC (1994) for broilers. Birds were randomly allocated to cages from 1 d of age and maintained under the same management conditions. Three days before the digestibility assay, bird number and mean BW were adjusted among replicate cages to ensure comparable groups. On day 27, replicate cages were randomly assigned to the dietary treatments. The birds had an average BW of 1.38 ± 0.04 kg at the start of the digestibility assay, which was conducted from 27 to 29 d of age following the procedure described by Ravindran et al. Data and sample collection

On day 30, feed intake (FI) and body weight gain (BWG) were recorded on a cage basis and feed to gain ratio (F/G) was subsequently calculated. No mortality was observed during the experimental period. On day 30, all the birds were humanely euthanized by electrical stunning. One bird from each replicate was randomly selected for intestinal tissue collection. Approximately 1-cm segment from the mid-jejunum was collected and immediately fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining. Approximately 0.5 g of digesta and a 1-cm segment from the mid jejunum were placed in the tubes, snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at −80 °C for digestive enzyme activity. Approximately 1-cm segment from the mid-ileum was placed in the tubes, snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at −80 °C for gene expression analysis. Subsequently, ileal digesta from the posterior half of the ileum were thoroughly mixed, placed in the bacteria-free tube, snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at −80 °C for the analysis of microbial composition. For AA analysis, the digesta from the terminal ileum (posterior half) of four birds in each replicate were pooled, frozen at −80 °C overnight, freeze-dried, and ground through a 40-mesh sieve.

Determination and calculation of IEAA losses, AIDAA and SIDAA

The dry matter content of feed and digesta samples was determined according to the method described by AOAC International (2019). Amino acid analysis was conducted with reference to GB/T 18246-2019 (Standardization Administration of China, 2019). Prior to analysis, feed and ileal digesta samples were pretreated based on the procedures described in this standard. Briefly, 0.1 g of each sample was hydrolyzed with 10 mL of 6 mol/L hydrochloric acid in sealed hydrolysis tubes, flushed with nitrogen gas to prevent oxidation, and incubated at 105°C for 24 h. After hydrolysis, amino acid concentrations were determined using an automatic amino acid analyzer (Hitachi L-8900, Japan).

TiO₂ contents in feed and digesta were determined following the method described by Myers et al. (2004). The AIDAA in the basal diet and the test diet was calculated using Eq. (1). The AIDAA in the test sorghum was calculated using Eq. (2), following the standardized method described by Blok and Dekker (2017) under the CVB system. The ileal endogenous amino acid (IEAA) losses were calculated (Eq. 3) as the amount (mg) of amino acids per kilogram of DM intake, based on the equation proposed by Moughan and Marlies Leenaars (1992). Intestinal morphology and digestive enzymes

Jejunal tissue samples were immediately fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 24 h, dehydrated through a graded ethanol series, cleared in xylene, and embedded in paraffin. Paraffin-embedded tissues were sectioned at 5μm thickness and stained with H&E. Tissue processing and staining were performed by Servicebio Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China). For each section, 10 intact villi were selected for morphological analysis. Villus height and crypt depth were measured at 40 × magnification. Villi height (VH) was defined as the distance from the tip to the villus–crypt junction, and crypt depth (CD) as the distance from the crypt opening to its base. Approximately 0.1 g of jejunal digesta was homogenized using a tissue grinder at 3500 rpm for 10 min. The activities of amylase, chymotrypsin, and trypsin were determined using commercial assay kits (C016-1-1, A080-3-1, A080-2-2; Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing, China) following the manufacturer’s instructions. Absorbance was measured using a spectrophotometer (Ultrospec 2100 pro, Amersham Biosciences, USA) or SpectraMax i3x (Molecular Devices, San Jose, CA, USA).

Gene expression

The expression of genes associated with intestinal barrier integrity and mucus secretion was analyzed, including Occludin-1, Zonula Occludens-1(ZO-1) and Mucin-2. Total RNA was isolated from the ileum using RNAiso Plus (Takara, Kyoto, Japan) per the manufacturer's protocol. RNA concentration and purity were assessed with NanoDrop 2000 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA), and integrity was confirmed by 1.5% denaturing agarose gel electrophoresis. cDNA was synthesized using PrimeScript RT Master Mix (Takara) and stored at −20 °C. qRT-PCR was performed with SYBR Premix Ex Taq (Takara) on an Applied Biosystems real-time PCR system. Primers were synthesized by Sangon Biotech (Shanghai, China), and sequences are listed in Table 5. Ileal microbial community composition

DNA from ileal digesta was extracted using the QIAamp Fast DNA Stool Mini Kit (51604; Qiagen N.V., Hilden, Germany). Amplicon sequencing targeting the V3–V4 region of the 16S rRNA gene was performed on the Illumina NovaSeq PE250 platform by Personal Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). Raw reads were demultiplexed using the Demux plugin, and primers were trimmed using Cutadapt. Quality filtering, read merging, and chimera removal were performed using the QIIME pipeline. Sequences were clustered into operational taxonomic units (OTUs) at 97% sequence similarity, resulting in 61,860 OTUs. Taxonomic annotation was performed using the Silva132 database. Alpha and beta diversity metrics, as well as relative abundance profiles, were calculated using R software (v4.2.2; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). Principal coordinate analysis (PCoA) based on Bray–Curtis distance was conducted to visualize differences in microbial community structure among groups. Differentially enriched taxa were identified using linear discriminant analysis effect size (LEfSe), with an LDA score threshold of 4.0.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using the GLM procedure in SPSS software (version 20.0; SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). Prior to analysis, data were tested for normality using the Shapiro–Wilk test, and homogeneity of variance was assessed using Levene’s test. The replicate cage was considered the experimental unit for all analyses. Results are presented as least squares means. Statistical significance was declared at P < 0.05, and 0.05 ≤ P < 0.10 was considered a tendency.

Data from the 12 sorghum-based diets were analyzed by two-way ANOVA as a 2 × 6 factorial arrangement, excluding the corn–soybean meal basal diet. When a significant sorghum source × evaluation method interaction was detected, simple-effect analysis was performed to evaluate the effect of evaluation method within each sorghum source. Within each evaluation method, the sorghum-based diets and the basal diet were analyzed separately by one-way ANOVA following the analytical strategy of Zhuang et al. (2025).

Ileal microbial composition as impacted by sorghum source

Fig. 1 illustrates the effects of different sorghum sources on the ileal microbial composition of broilers, with the CSM basal diet group serving as a reference. Each sorghum source included 12 samples, comprising 6 from the direct method and 6 from the substitution method (n = 12 per group). As shown in Fig. 1A, at the genus level, the ileal microbiota of broilers was dominated by Lactobacillus across all groups. The CSM group exhibited the highest relative abundance of Lactobacillus (81.43%), which was higher than that in any sorghum-based group. Among the sorghum-fed broilers, Streptococcus was more abundant in the YZ, LN, US, and ZJK groups compared to the YK and CF groups. The CF group showed the highest level of Corynebacterium (39.15%), followed by the YZ (24.80%) and YK (14.40%) groups. The US group had the highest abundance of Enterococcus (11.15%), while Turicibacter was most enriched in the ZJK group (7.60%). Discussion

Diet composition and formulation strategy can influence growth performance of poultry, and the nutritional value of sorghum varies widely among sources, particularly in tannin content (Ejaz et al., 2021; Selle et al., 2018). In the current study, YK and ZJK sorghum had higher tannin levels and were associated with lower BWG, while YK also showed the lowest FI. Moreover, broilers fed diets formulated using the substitution method exhibited higher FI, BWG and lower F/G than those fed diets based on the direct method. These differences may be partly associated with dietary tannin levels. Tannins are known to impair nutrient utilization by forming complexes with dietary proteins and inhibiting digestive enzyme activity (Woyengo et al., 2012; Hidayat et al., 2021). The lower inclusion level of sorghum in the substitution method reduced dietary tannin exposure, while the inclusion of the basal diet may have improved feed acceptance. Kadim and Moughan (1997) reported that combining a test ingredient with a basal diet can improve feed acceptance and increase feed intake in broilers. Torres et al. (2013) also showed that partial replacement of corn with sorghum may mitigate negative effects on growth. These factors may contribute to the variation in F/G responses to the two evaluation methods among sorghum sources.

Intestinal integrity is a key factor for optimum nutrient absorption in monogastric animals, with enzymatic digestion occurring primarily in the small intestine and microbial fermentation in the large intestine (Ji et al., 2022). In this study, broilers fed diets formulated using the substitution method showed greater villus height and crypt depth than those fed diets based on the direct method, indicating differences in short-term intestinal morphological responses between the two methods. These differences may be partly related to the lower sorghum inclusion in the substitution method, which reduced tannin exposure and may have mitigated its toxic effects on the intestinal epithelium (Tong et al., 2021). Consistently, Brus et al (2018) also reported that low dietary levels of tannins (0.05 ∼ 0.10%) stimulated intestinal epithelial proliferation and supported intestinal development in broilers.

In poultry, pancreatic α-amylase is the only enzyme capable of digesting starch (Riesenfeld et al., 1980). Studies have shown that red sorghum contains approximately 30∼35% amylose (Kang et al., 2023), while common corn contains around 24.65% amylose (Myers et al., 2004), indicating a higher proportion of amylopectin in corn. Mkandawire et al. (2013) reported that tannins inhibit α-amylase activity when pre-incubated with the enzyme, whereas pre-mixing tannins with amylopectin before enzyme exposure can enhance α-amylase activity. Moreover, the higher trypsin activity observed with the substitution method may be attributed to the inclusion of soybean meal, as dietary protein has been shown to modulate protease activity (Morales et al., 2017). For chymotrypsin, however, the response to evaluation method depended on sorghum source, with higher activity under the substitution method observed only for YZ, LN, and ZJK sorghum. Differences in nutrient and amino acid composition among sorghum sources may have contributed to this source-dependent response, although no single compositional factor could explain the observed pattern in this study.

The integrity of the intestinal barrier is crucial for sustaining epithelial cell function. The intestinal tight junction (TJ) proteins, for example, Claudin, Occludin, and ZO play a key role in maintaining the paracellular barrier of endothelial and epithelial layers. It also prevents the translocation of bacteria and other luminal antigens across the intestinal epithelium (Ducatelle et al., 2023). In this study, Occludin and ZO-1 expression in the ileum showed significant interactions between sorghum source and evaluation method, indicating that their responses to the evaluation method depended on sorghum source. The effects of tannic acid on intestinal barrier function may vary with dietary tannin levels and experimental conditions. Liu et al. (2023) found that dietary tannins, supplemented at 600 mg/kg diet, upregulated the expression of TJ proteins in the jejunal mucosa of broiler chickens, whereas supplementation with 300 mg/kg microencapsulated tannic acid increased Claudin-3 expression without affecting ZO-1 or Occludin (Jing et al., 2022). In the current study, differences in tannin content among sorghum sources and in sorghum inclusion between the two methods resulted in different levels of tannin exposure. This may partly explain the source-dependent responses of Occludin and ZO-1.

The diversity and richness of the intestinal microbiota play a critical role in maintaining intestinal homeostasis in chickens (Kogut et al., 2020). Consistent with previous findings (Fagundes et al., 2017), differences in ileal microbial composition were observed among broilers fed different sorghum-based diets in the current study, even over the short feeding period used in the current study. Short-term dietary interventions have been shown to induce rapid shifts in small intestinal microbial populations (Stege et al., 2024), and similar responses were observed in our previous nitrogen-free diet studies after 3 days of feeding (Zhou et al., 2022; Zhou et al., 2023). Together, these findings highlight the rapid responsiveness of the ileal microbiota to changes in diet composition, a feature that is not routinely considered in digestibility studies.

At the genus level, Lactobacillus predominated in the CSM group in the current study. Lactobacillus is generally associated with the maintenance of intestinal homeostasis (Noohi et al., 2021). However, sorghum-based diets markedly reduced ileal Lactobacillus abundance while increasing the relative abundances of Streptococcus, Corynebacterium, and Enterococcus, showing source-specific microbial profiles. Previous studies have shown that the Corynebacterium and Enterococcus genera possess tannase activity, enabling them to degrade plant-derived tannins into smaller phenolic compounds, which may support their adaptation and survival in tannin-rich environments (Goel et al., 2011; Noguchi et al., 2007). Moreover, the LN group showed increased abundances of Streptococcus and Bacteroides, which may be associated with the utilization of non-starch polysaccharides (Ivarsson et al., 2014). The enrichment of Clostridiaceae in the US group may reflect its role in fermenting sorghum-derived carbohydrates, particularly contributing to butyrate production (Wang et al., 2015). The higher abundance of Staphylococcus in the ZJK group may reflect microbial imbalance, as some members of this genus are associated with foodborne transmission and environmental contamination (Lee et al., 2022).

Consistent with the effects of sorghum source, the SIDAA evaluation method also shaped ileal microbial composition. Under the direct method, in which sorghum was the sole source of AA, increased microbial richness and altered community structure were observed. In contrast, the microbial profile observed with the substitution method was more similar to that of the CSM group, suggesting that the inclusion of the basal diet may have maintained a microbial environment closer to that observed under conventional dietary conditions. Moreover, Turicibacter and Brachybacterium were the dominant genera uniquely enriched in the direct method group. Notably, Turicibacter has been associated with the regulation of bile acid profiles and lipid metabolism (Lynch et al., 2023), while Brachybacterium is known to proliferate in phosphorus-rich environments and may reflect improved phosphorus utilization in chickens (Omotoso et al., 2023). The enrichment of these genera may represent microbial adaptation to the distinct dietary environment created by the direct method.

Collectively, these physiological responses suggest that the substitution method was associated with a more favorable short-term digestive environment during the digestibility assay than the direct method. It has been found that the direct method and substitution method may yield similar estimates for both AIDAA and SIDAA in high protein feed ingredients (Oliveira et al., 2020). However, Fan and Sauer (1995) reported that the direct method may be less suitable for low-protein ingredients such as barley (10.2% CP), for which the substitution method was recommended. Ravindran and Bryden (1999) also suggested that the direct method underestimates AA digestibility in poultry when applied to low to moderate protein feed ingredients such as cereals and grain by-products. Consistent with these observations, the substitution method generally resulted in higher AIDAA and SIDAA values than the direct method in the current study. However, the significant sorghum source × evaluation method interactions for AIDAA and SIDAA indicate that the magnitude of this method-related difference depended on sorghum source. This source dependence was particularly evident for SIDAA, with more extensive method-related differences in YK and ZJK sorghum but few or no differences in LN and US sorghum.

The source-dependent differences between the two evaluation methods may be partly related to variation in sorghum composition. Notably, YK and ZJK sorghum contained relatively high tannin levels (1.86% and 1.30%, respectively), and showed pronounced differences in AIDAA and SIDAA between the two methods. Mansoori and Acamovic (2007) demonstrated that tannic acid markedly increased the excretion of endogenous amino acids, particularly methionine, histidine, and lysine. In addition, the binding of tannins to digestive enzymes may trigger compensatory overproduction of these enzymes through a negative feedback mechanism, which in turn increases endogenous amino acid losses (Woyengo et al., 2012). The higher inclusion of high-tannin sorghum in the direct method resulted in greater dietary tannin exposure than in the substitution method because sorghum was the sole dietary AA source. This may have contributed to the greater differences in AIDAA and SIDAA between the two methods for YK and ZJK sorghum. However, tannin content alone does not fully explain the source-dependent response, because marked method-related differences were also observed for YZ sorghum despite its low tannin content. Notably, several physiological responses also depended on sorghum source, particularly chymotrypsin activity and the expression of Occludin-1 and ZO-1, which showed significant sorghum source × evaluation method interactions. These findings suggest that differences in AA digestibility between the two methods may reflect broader source-dependent responses to the different dietary matrices rather than tannin exposure alone. Differences in sorghum composition and their interactions with the dietary matrix may therefore collectively contribute to the source-dependent variation in AIDAA and SIDAA.

Conclusions

The present study demonstrated that AIDAA and SIDAA in broilers were influenced by sorghum source and evaluation method, with LN and US sorghum showing favorable AA digestibility. The substitution method generally yielded higher AIDAA and SIDAA values and was associated with more favorable short-term responses in digestive enzyme activity and ileal microbial profiles than the direct method, although these responses varied among sorghum sources. High-tannin sorghum showed more pronounced method-related differences in AA digestibility, suggesting that anti-nutritional factor content should be considered when selecting an appropriate method for determining AA digestibility.

Dynamics of Dibothriocephalus spp. infection in fish from the Shardara Reservoir (Republic of Kazakhstan) and fish decontamination by freezing method
Authors: Yerbol Baltabay, Bolat Yessimov, Gani Issayev, Ilyas Ikramov, Abylaikhan Azimbay, Dana Seksenova
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The study aimed to investigate the infestation of commercial fish species in the Shardara Dam with Dibothriocephalus spp. plerocercoids, the localization of plerocercoids within fish bodies, and the effect of freezing treatment on plerocercoid mobility. The study was conducted from 2021 to 2024 in the area of the Shardara Dam, located on the Syr Darya River in the Turkistan Region, Kazakhstan. A total of 993 fish specimens were examined for the presence of Dibothriocephalus spp. plerocercoids. The fish belonged to four species: common perch (Perca fluviatilis L.), wels catfish (Silurus glanis L.), pikeperch (Sander lucioperca L.), and northern pike (Esox lucius L.). An experiment on fish decontamination was performed using freezing temperatures of − 8 °C, − 10 °C, − 12 °C, and − 14 °C. Fish specimens weighing up to 2.5 kg were used. The condition of the plerocercoids was assessed after 24, 48, 72, and 96 h. In this study, the northern pike demonstrated the highest infestation rates. The extensity of infestation in the northern pike, the extensity ranged from 45% to 57.1%, with an average of 50.4%, depending on the year and sampling location. A high intensity of Dibothriocephalus spp. plerocercoid invasion was observed in the muscle tissue and edible internal organs. Freezing fish at − 12 °C for at least 96 h or at − 14 °C for at least 48 h effectively inactivates Dibothriocephalus spp. larvae. The study identified a persistent focus of dibothriocephalosis in the area of the Shardara Dam. In accordance with the legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan, fish caught in the reservoir must undergo mandatory decontamination.

Introduction

Fish products occupy an important place in the human diet in many countries worldwide. However, recent years have seen a marked increase in parasitic infections affecting commercial fish, both marine and freshwater species (Ziarati et al. 2022). In some cases, fish serve as a source of infection for humans, as well as for domestic and wild carnivorous animals, with helminthiases that pose serious health risks (Menconi et al. 2020; Radačovská et al. 2019). Therefore, it is crucial to identify helminths, analyze the infestation levels in commercial fish, and study their parasitic infections in order to assess the suitability of fish products for consumption. These measures are equally critical for devising effective decontamination methods and preventing helminthiases that are hazardous to human health (Abalihin et al. 2019; Farkhutdinov et al. 2024; Prikhodko et al. 2025). One of the most common helminthiases in humans is dibothriocephalosis, which results from infection with tapeworms of the genus Dibothriocephalus, primarily Dibothriocephalus latum (L.) (broad tapeworm), less often D. dendriticum (Nitzsch). Human infection occurs through the consumption of undercooked or inadequately salted fish, including caviar. D. latum and D. dendriticum are widespread across temperate climatic zones, and dibothriocephalosis foci have been recorded in regions located within the basins of large rivers and near major lakes (Králová-Hromadová et al. 2021; Kuchta et al. 2013).

Reservoirs constructed within the Syr Darya River basin in the southern part of Kazakhstan serve to address irrigation, hydroelectric, and fishery needs of the region (Mustafayev et al. 2019). The Shardara Dam, which is located within the administrative boundaries of the Turkistan Region, is the largest fishery reservoir in southern Kazakhstan. This reservoir supplies approximately 90% of the region’s fish products (Sambaev et al. 2020). However, successful fishery operations are often constrained by parasitological factors. In particular, the formation of dibothriocephalosis foci in areas surrounding the reservoir and the significant infestation of certain commercial fish species with plerocercoids of tapeworms of the genus Dibothriocephalus represent major challenges (Abdreshov and Barimbekova 2023; Kerimbayeva et al. 2024; Ukusheva 2023).

The Shardara Dam represents an intense reservoir-type focus of dibothriocephalosis. In the Shardara Dam basin, the primary sources of tapeworms of the genus Dibothriocephalus infection in humans are fish and fish products that have not undergone proper decontamination procedures (Abdreshov and Barimbekova 2023). According to the reports of the sanitary and epidemiological stations of the Shardara and Zhetysay districts for 2021–2024, kindly provided to the authors of the article, the infection rate with dibothriocephalosis in certain settlements surrounding the reservoir reached 30%–32%. Among school-aged children, the average infection rate was 3%, while among preschool-aged children it was 5%.

Industrial fishing is regulated by legislative and regulatory acts, as well as veterinary and sanitary standards. These documents define the quality requirements for fish products, prohibit the consumption of low-quality fish infected with helminths, and outline decontamination procedures for conditionally safe fish originating from areas where Dibothriocephalus spp. foci have been identified (CIS Legislation 2009; 2015). However, when fish are caught privately and sold, often at unregulated markets, there is a substantial risk of helminthic infections in humans. For this reason, studies of water body contamination with invasive species, continuous monitoring of the reservoir ecosystem, and analysis of Dibothriocephalus spp. infestation in commercial fish remain highly relevant.

Since human infection occurs through the consumption of inadequately decontaminated fish, preventing dibothriocephalosis requires knowledge of plerocercoid tolerance to various physical conditions, particularly low temperatures. Freezing may be employed as a method to ensure the safety of fish for human consumption and minimize the risk of Dibothriocephalus spp. infection (Nyukkanov et al. 2021; Salminen 1970). Accordingly, it is necessary to establish freezing parameters under which plerocercoids are exposed to temperatures beyond their survival threshold. In Kazakhstan, current veterinary and sanitary guidelines determine the requirements for temperature regimes and exposure duration during the cold treatment of conditionally edible fish from areas where Dibothriocephalus spp. plerocercoids have been detected (CIS Legislation 2009; Ospanov et al. 2022). However, consumers do not always follow these guidelines when purchasing fish caught and sold privately. Moreover, fish weight and the localization of plerocercoids in different parts of the fish body must be considered during freezing treatment. The immobility of plerocercoids after exposure to low temperatures is generally interpreted as larval death and an inability to cause infection (Abdreshov and Barimbekova 2023; Safonov et al. 2018; Vlasova et al. 2020). Nevertheless, there is a risk of misinterpretation due to reversible paralysis, which is merely a natural response to cold and not a definitive indicator of death. Therefore, only sustained immobilization can be considered a reliable sign of larval death (Davydova 2017). Previously, no studies have investigated the focus of dibothriocephalosis in the Shardara Dam basin. Based on these considerations, the objective of the present study was to (1) examine the infestation of commercial fish in the Shardara Dam with Dibothriocephalus spp. plerocercoids, (2) determine the localization of plerocercoids within fish bodies, and (3) assess the effect of freezing treatment on plerocercoid motility.

Materials and methods

The study was conducted from 2021 to 2024 in the area of the Shardara Dam, located on the Syr Darya River in the Turkistan Region of Kazakhstan. Fish sampling was carried out in the administrative Shardara districts: in the west, east, and southern part of the Shardara Dam (Fig. 1). In each zone, fish were collected during three seasons: spring (April–May), summer (July–August), and autumn (late October–November). Before examination, the fish were weighed, measured for length, and evaluated for caviar maturation stage and age. A total of 993 fish specimens were examined for the presence of Dibothriocephalus spp. plerocercoids. The fish belonged to four species: common perch (Perca fluviatilis L.), wels catfish (Silurus glanis L.), pikeperch (Sander lucioperca L.), and northern pike (Esox lucius L.). Parasitological examinations were performed following the method of Bykhovskaya-Pavlovskaya (1985) at the veterinary and sanitary laboratory affiliated with the Shardara District Station for Animal Disease Control. A complete parasitological dissection was performed to examine all organs, tissues, and the gastrointestinal tract. Identified plerocercoids were preserved in 70° ethanol and transported for species identification. The identification of plerocercoid was carried out by the staff of the Parasitology Laboratory at the Institute of Zoology of the Republic of Kazakhstan (Almaty) using electron microscopy, based on the assessment of a set of morphological features: the presence or absence of capsules, villi on the scolex/body, frontal glands in the scolex/body, cuticle thickness, characteristics of the subcuticular space, the number of layers of subcuticular longitudinal musculature, among others (Yamane et al. 1989). It should be noted that D. dendriticum has not been recorded for Kazakhstan and is usually reported for more northern regions (Kuchta et al. 2013). However, based on the range of D. dendriticum, the possibility of finding this species in Kazakhstan cannot be excluded. Considering the difficulty of distinguishing D. latum and D. dendriticum only by morphological features, and the impossibility of identifying each plerocyst in the extensive material studied using biochemical and SEM photographs, it can be assumed that the studied material belongs not only to D. latum, but also, in part, to D. dendriticum. Therefore, the studied material in this article is designated as Dibothriocephalus spp. The following parameters were determined: the percentage of infected fish among the total number examined (prevalence); the number of plerocercoids per infected specimen (intensity), and mean intensity (Bush et al. 1997). It was determined that the distribution of plerocercoids in internal organs (stomach and intestinal walls, liver, mesentery, gonads/caviar, adipose tissue) and muscles from various body regions (dorsal, abdominal, caudal), and the degree of plerocercoid infestation in edible parts of the fish body.

Studies on the viability of Dibothriocephalus spp. plerocercoids under low temperatures were conducted at the state veterinary and sanitary laboratory affiliated with the Shardara District Station for Animal Disease Control (Turkistan Region). An experiment was carried out to test northern pike and common perch decontamination under low-temperature conditions. Considering the capabilities of household freezers, the experiment was conducted at − 8 °C, − 10 °C, − 12 °C, and − 14 °C. Fish specimens weighing up to 2.5 kg were used. The condition of the plerocercoids was assessed after 24, 48, 72, and 96 h. Raw fish were placed in a single layer in the freezer compartment of a household refrigerator, and the target temperature was maintained. Internal fish temperatures were monitored using copper-constantan thermocouples with a diameter of 0.1 mm. The measuring junctions were fixed to the tips of fine injection needles. At predetermined intervals, the fish were removed from the freezer, thawed at 4 °C, and subjected to partial parasitological dissection. Recovered plerocercoids were immersed in horse serum at room temperature. Viability was assessed twice: once immediately after placement in the medium and again after 10–12 h. Larval motility was evaluated through microscopic examination. Larvae were considered motile if, upon artificial stimulation (needle prick), distinct or even weak, continuous or intermittent contractions or other movements were observed. Additionally, plerocercoids were stained with neutral red at a dilution of 1:1000 (one drop of solution per 1 ml of water) for 10 min at room temperature. Live plerocercoids exhibit stable staining in pink.

Results

Prevalence and Intensity of Fish Infection with Dibothriocephalus spp. Plerocercoids in the Shardara Dam

Out of 993 fish specimens examined across all species, 361 (36.4%) were infected with Dibothriocephalus spp. plerocercoids. The examinations established that the prevalence of Dibothriocephalus spp. among fish species in the Shardara Dam from 2021 to 2024 ranged from 32.6% to 40.2%. The intensity varied from 1 to 45 (mean intensity 7.1 ± 1,1) plerocercoids per fish. The prevalence of infection rates by species were as follows: 77.2% in the northern pike, 45.0% in the common perch, 16.5% in the wels catfish, and 7.2% in the pikeperch; the intensity rates as follows: 5–41 (mean intensity 17.2 ± 1,8) in the northern pike, 1–13 (mean intensity 5.0 ± 1) in the common perch, 1–6 (mean intensity 3.5 ± 0,2) in the wels catfish, and 1–5 (mean intensity 2.7 ± 0,3) in the pikeperch. Overall, during the four-year study period, the prevalence of infection among the studied fish species of the Shardara Dam remained consistently high. A correlation was also identified between the intensity of Dibothriocephalus spp. plerocercoid infection in the northern pike and the age of the fish. The analysis revealed differences in the localization of Dibothriocephalus spp. plerocercoids in fish from the Shardara Dam (Table 2). In the northern pike and the wels catfish, the majority of plerocercoids occupied the internal organs—70.6% and 72.7%, respectively. The parasites were found to be primarily located in the stomach wall and mesentery. In contrast, in the common perch, most plerocercoids were localized in the muscle tissue—74.9% in the common perch and 62.7% in the pikeperch. The analysis of plerocercoid distribution across various muscle groups showed that infection levels in dorsal muscles were significantly higher than in abdominal and caudal muscles. In the northern pike, 66.9% of all muscle-dwelling plerocercoids were found in the dorsal region. In the wels catfish, this value was 54.5%, in the pikeperch, it was 56.3%, and in the common perch, it was 63.8%. Localization of larvae in the muscles and internal organs of examined fish

The examinations of internal organs revealed the most frequently infected regions—the stomach and intestinal walls. Thus, infection in these organs reached 77.2% in the northern pike, 68.2% in the wels catfish, 74.5% in the common perch, and 56.1% in the pikeperch (based on the total number of plerocercoids found in internal organs). Plerocercoids were less frequently detected in the abdominal cavity. A higher rate of gonad infection was observed in the northern pike compared to the wels catfish (10.4% versus 9.1%). In the common perch, no plerocercoids were detected in either milt or caviar.

According to the assessment of plerocercoid localization in the edible parts of the northern pike, 39.8% of all plerocercoids were found in muscles, caviar, and milt. For the wels catfish, the percentage of plerocercoids was 33.9%. For the other species, the proportion was as follows: 66.6% in the pikeperch and 74.9% in the common perch.

Effect of Low Temperatures on plerocercoids of tapeworms of the genus Dibothriocephalus

Figures 2 and 3 present data on the effect of low temperatures on plerocercoids of tapeworms of the genus Dibothriocephalus during freezing of northern pike and common perch. Fish were considered contaminated if at least one plerocercoid with minimal motility was detected. In northern pike, at − 8 °C, after 24 h, 63% of plerocercoids remain mobile, and after 48 h, 26%, at − 10 °C, 44% and 12%, respectively. In common perch, at − 8 °C, after 24 h, 56% of plerocercoids remain mobile, after 48 h, 24%, at − 10 °C, 42% and 17%, respectively. In both fish species subjected to freezing at − 8 °C and − 10 °C, plerocercoid exhibiting signs of motility was observed even after 96 h of observation. In northern pike, 12% and 6% were weakly motile plerocercoids, in common perch, 8% and 5%, respectively. Discussion

The present study provides an assessment of a dibothriocephalosis focus in the area surrounding the Shardara Dam. The presence of Dibothriocephalus spp. plerocercoids was confirmed in the northern pike, the wels catfish, the pikeperch, and the common perch. The patterns of larval localization in muscle tissue and internal organs, including edible parts, were analyzed. Based on the obtained data, the study evaluated the effectiveness of fish decontamination by freezing in typical household freezers.

The area surrounding the Shardara Dam constitutes an active focus of dibothriocephalosis of the synanthropic reservoir-type. The contamination of water bodies with tapeworms is intensive during spring and summer (from May to September). At an average water temperature of 15.6 °C, the development of tapeworm’s eggs to the stage of coracidia release occurs within 19–20 days. Factors related to human economic activities play a significant role in the transmission of dibothriocephalosis. In settlements adjacent to the reservoir, fishing is carried out year-round and in considerable volumes (Kulikov et al. 2019). Caught fish are often sold at markets, including unregulated ones, where veterinary control is frequently absent (Sofronova 1999). If proper decontamination measures are not followed, such fish and fish products become sources of tapeworms of the genus Dibothriocephalus infection among the human population in the Shardara Dam basin. The persistence of dibothriocephalosis is maintained by substantial pollution of the reservoir’s shoreline with domestic waste. Additionally, many settlements in the area lack adequate infrastructure. Sanitation facilities often fail to meet hygienic standards and are sometimes located near water bodies (Nikonova 2009; Ospanov et al. 2019). According to the report of the Sanitary and Epidemiological Station of the Shardara District, kindly provided to the authors, high levels of contamination with tapeworm’s eggs were observed in soil, reservoir water, and wastewater. In total, Dibothriocephalus spp. eggs were found in 63 out of 132 samples (47.7%). Soil samples contained between 7.3 and 99.4 eggs per sample, and between 45 and 144 eggs per kilogram of soil. On average, 1.3 eggs were found per liter of river water and 50 eggs per liter of wastewater. Domestic animals may also potentially contribute to the persistence of the dibothriocephalosis focus, since small fish infected with Dibothriocephalus spp. larvae, as well as the internal organs of larger fish, are often fed to cats and dogs. According to data from coprological examinations of dogs using the Fülleborn method, kindly provided to the authors and conducted in 2022–2024 in the Shardara District by specialists of the Parasitology Department of the District Veterinary Laboratory, infection with Dibothriocephalus spp. was detected in 27 out of 115 examined animals (23.5%). These animals become infected, and the eggs they excrete enter the reservoir water. However, data on the biotic potential of tapeworm’s egg dissemination by dogs in this area are lacking, and the role of domestic animals in the transmission of Dibothriocephalus spp. requires further investigation. Furthermore, the organic pollution of the reservoir promotes the proliferation of copepods, which serve as the first intermediate hosts of Dibothriocephalus spp. (Roth et al. 2018; Savenkova and Malysheva 2016). Among the eight Copepoda species identified in the Shardara Reservoir in 2023, the dominant species was Cyclops vicinus Ulianine, which serves as a potential primary intermediate host of tapeworms of the genus Dibothriocephalus. Its population peak was observed in autumn (Usenova et al. 2023).

The results of the present study indicate that a persistent focus of dibothriocephalosis has formed in the area surrounding the Shardara Dam. This focus is characterized by a consistently high level of Dibothriocephalus spp. plerocercoid infection in commercially valuable fish species. According to the legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan, the Shardara Dam must be classified as conditionally safe for commercial fishing activities. Fish caught in this reservoir should be regarded as “conditionally edible,” and their distribution without prior decontamination is prohibited (CIS Legislation 2009; 2015).

Among the fish species examined, the northern pike (Esox lucius) was identified as the primary vector of tapeworms of the genus Dibothriocephalus transmission to humans. In the northern pike, the prevalence of Dibothriocephalus spp. ranged from 65,0% to 88.2%, with an average of 77.2%. The intensity of infection was also the highest at this species: up to 41 larvae in a single large northern pike. These high levels of intensity are attributed to the accumulation of Dibothriocephalus spp. larvae over time in large predatory fish that consume smaller infected predators. In edible tissues of pike (muscles and roe, which is often consumed lightly salted), 39.8% of all plerocercoids were detected. Nevertheless, the overall high intensity of infection determines the predominant zoonotic significance of pike among all fish species studied. Additionally, the large population size of pike in the reservoir, coupled with its widespread recreational fishing and sale in informal markets, further amplifies its role in parasite transmission.

In European perch (Perca fluviatilis), the average prevalence of plerocercoids was 45.0%, with 74.9% of plerocercoids located in the musculature. However, the relatively low infection intensity (mean 5.0 ± 1 larvae) and the fact that perch is primarily a target of recreational fishing and is seldom marketed in informal trade reduce its zoonotic importance compared with pike.

Catfish (Silurus glanis) and pike perch (Sander lucioperca) are not considered typical hosts of D. latum, though infections have been documented in several regions of Russia (Kamenev et al. 2018). In addition, there is evidence of pike perch colonization by D. dendriticum (Kuchta et al. 2013). Thus, although human infection from these species is possible, the relatively low prevalence and intensity in pike perch in the Shardara Dam, the predominance of plerocercoids in inedible body parts of catfish, and the overall low abundance of catfish in the reservoir significantly reduce the zoonotic significance of these species.

No previous studies have examined the parasitic fauna of fish in the Shardara Dam. An examination of water bodies located in Northern Kazakhstan did not reveal the presence of Dibothriocephalus spp. plerocercoids (Barbol et al. 2023). A study on the distribution of D. latum in the Balkhash-Alakol Basin showed that 13% of the carp caught in the Ili River were infected. In the Kapchagay Reservoir, the prevalence of D. latum in carp reached 15%, and in Lake Balkhash, it was 11% (Ukusheva 2023). However, the study described above did not include predatory fish species, which may have a higher potential for infection. For example, the prevalence of infection in the northern pike has reached up to 66.7% in certain water bodies of the Kostroma region in Russia (Novak 2011) and up to 33.3% in the Transbaikal region (Dugarov et al. 2015). In addition, carp is not considered an intermediate host of D. latum in the literature. However, it is well established that predatory fish are typically infected not directly through the consumption of copepods, but rather by preying on already infected planktivorous fish, which may include carp fry. Nevertheless, data on the distribution of D. latum in the Balkhash–Alakol Basin require further clarification.

The deep localization of plerocercoids within muscle tissues impedes the effectiveness of physical and chemical treatments commonly used for decontaminating fish in domestic settings (Nikonova 2009; Sofronova 1999). This is particularly relevant for larger specimens and increases the risk of Dibothriocephalus spp. infection among consumers of fish products. Regarding the localization of plerocercoids in internal organs, it is important to note that not all such tissues are discarded— caviar, milt, and liver are often used for culinary purposes (Králová-Hromadová et al. 2021). The proportion of plerocercoids relative to the total number of larvae located in the edible parts of the northern pike and the wels catfish was lower than in the common perch and the pikeperch. However, the presence of larvae deep within the muscle tissue of larger fish, combined with generally high infection intensities, renders these species equally hazardous in terms of transmission risk to humans. The observed pattern of Dibothriocephalus spp. plerocercoid localization in this study is consistent with findings reported by other researchers (Abdreshov and Barimbekova 2023; Dugarov et al. 2015; Novak 2011).

While there are well-established methods for decontaminating fish infected with Dibothriocephalus spp. plerocercoids (CIS Legislation 2009), the present study aimed to identify an optimal freezing regimen within the capabilities of household freezers. Based on the findings, the study sought to recommend a practical decontamination protocol for preventive use in domestic conditions. Both temperature and exposure duration constitute key factors in the effectiveness of freezing as a decontamination method. In the present study, a complete immobility of plerocercoids was observed at − 12 °C after 96 h and at − 14 °C after 48 h. It should be noted that the experimental fish specimens weighed no more than 2.5 kg. Larger individuals may require lower temperatures or longer exposure durations. In domestic freezing processes, large fish are typically cut into smaller portions before being frozen. According to technical regulations in the Republic of Kazakhstan, the temperature for industrial freezing of fish must not exceed − 18 °C (CIS Legislation 2009).

Conclusion

The study identified a persistent focus of dibothriocephalosis in the area of the Shardara Dam. High prevalence and intensity of Dibothriocephalus spp. plerocercoid were recorded in the northern pike. A high intensity of Dibothriocephalus spp. plerocercoid invasion was also observed in the muscle tissue and edible internal organs (caviar). Efforts to control dibothriocephalosis in the region must be implemented comprehensively, encompassing the identification and deworming of infected individuals and domestic animals, the protection of water bodies from fecal contamination, and public health education. In accordance with the legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan, fish caught in the reservoir must undergo mandatory decontamination. In particular, freezing fish at − 12 °C for at least 96 h or at − 14 °C for at least 48 h effectively inactivates Dibothriocephalus spp. larvae. Large specimens must be cut into smaller pieces prior to freezing.

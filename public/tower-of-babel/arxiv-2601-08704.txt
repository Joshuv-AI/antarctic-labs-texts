Singular del Pezzo surfaces and isotropic flag varieties
Michele Bianco, Luis E. Solá Conde

Abstract
. We compute the Chow quotient of the complete flag variety of isotropic subspaces of a four dimensional complex vector space with respect to a skew/symmetric form, and show that it is a singular del Pezzo surface of degree four.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 14L30, 14E30; Secondary 14J30, 14L24

1.
Introduction

Geometric invariant theory and its variants provide powerful tools for constructing and studying moduli spaces arising as quotients of algebraic varieties by group actions. Among these constructions, the Chow quotient, introduced by Kapranov, occupies a distinguished place. Unlike GIT quotients, Chow quotients are canonical – that is independent of a choice of linearization – and retain all degenerations of general orbit closures. As a consequence, they typically exhibit more complicated singularities, while encoding finer information on boundary phenomena and birational geometry (see, for instance,
[
12
,
13
,
2
]
)

In recent years, Chow quotients of flag varieties by maximal tori have attracted renewed attention, motivated both by their intrinsic geometry and by their connections with birational geometry, representation theory, and combinatorics. In our recent work

[
4
]
, we initiated a detailed study of these quotients in low rank, computing explicitly the Chow quotient of the complete flag variety of type
{\rm A}_{3}
(parametrizing complete flags in
{\mathbb{P}}^{3}
) and describing its birational geometry.

The purpose of the present paper is to extend this analysis to the symplectic setting, focusing on the complete flag variety
F
of type
{\rm C}_{2}
. Concretely, this
4
-dimensional variety parametrizes isotropic flags in
{\mathbb{C}}^{4}
with respect to a fixed symplectic form. We study the (normalized) Chow quotient
X
of
F
by the action of a maximal torus
H
of
\mathop{\rm PSp}\nolimits(4)
.

The algebraic cycle in
F
corresponding to a general point in
X
is a toric surface, which is the minimal resolution of a toric log del Pezzo surface of Picard number two and degree four, having four singularities of type
{\rm A}_{1}
(cf. Proposition
4.2
). A key ingredient in our approach is a detailed analysis of local degenerations of the closure of a general torus orbit in the symplectic flag variety, in the spirit of the toric setting considered in
[
13
,
2
]
. By studying these degenerations explicitly, we gain precise control over the boundary of the Chow quotient, that is the locus parametrizing reducible cycles. One of our main results shows that this locus is a simple normal crossing divisor, given by the union of eight rational curves, whose incidence relations reflect the combinatorics of the torus action.

Moreover, we provide an explicit description of the general reducible cycle parametrized by each of these curves, identifying their irreducible components and intersection behavior inside the flag variety. Such reducible cycles naturally arise in Chow-type compactifications and are a primary source of singularities in the quotient, as already observed in related moduli-theoretic contexts (cf.
[
1
]
). This analysis plays a crucial role in determining the singularities of the Chow quotient and underlies our subsequent study of its birational geometry.

Using this description, our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
F
be the complete flag variety of vector subspaces of
{\mathbb{C}}^{4}
, isotropic with respect to a nondegenerate skew-symmetric form, endowed with the natural action of the complex torus
H\subset\mathop{\rm PSp}\nolimits(4)
of homothety classes of diagonal matrices. Then the normalized Chow quotient
X
of
F
by the action of
H
is a singular del Pezzo surface of degree
4
with two singularities of type
A_{1}
.

In particular,
X
is a Mori Dream Space by
[
5
]
. We then study its nef and effective cones, and its anticanonical map, which is an embedding that maps
X
to a quartic surface in
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
. We also determine the automorphism group of the Chow quotient, showing that it coincides with the Weyl group of
\mathop{\rm PSp}\nolimits(4)
.

This work underscores the usefulness of Chow quotients as a framework for understanding degenerations of orbits in flag varieties, from which Chow quotients of general rational homogeneous manifolds should follow. More generally, it supports the view that Chow quotients serve as natural “master spaces”, dominating GIT quotients while retaining subtle boundary information relevant to birational geometry
[
12
,
13
]
.

Outline.

We follow the lines of argumentation of
[
4
]
, adapted to our particular situation. After a section of preliminaries on torus actions and on the complete flag
F
of type
{\rm C}_{2}
, we recall some known facts on its fixed points by the action of the maximal torus
H
, and introduce some particular
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-actions, that later on will play a central role in the study of the boundary divisors of the Chow quotient (cf. Section
3.1
). Section
4
is devoted to the study of the combinatorial quotients of
H
-invariant affine charts in
F
, which contain information about the local degeneration of the closure of the general
H
-orbit in
F
. In Section
5
we prove the main statement of the paper, showing how to construct the Chow quotient
X
as an inverse limit of combinatorial quotients. We conclude the paper with a section devoted to a description of the geometry of
X
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Torus actions and associated quotients

In this section we quickly review some general result about torus actions on smooth projective varieties
[
6
]
and their quotients
[
13
,
12
,
17
]
.

Consider an algebraic torus
H
of rank
r
effectively acting on a (complex) smooth projective variety
Z
. Denote by
\mathop{\rm\hskip 0.0ptM}\nolimits(H)\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}
its character lattice and by
\No(H)
its dual lattice. Given an ample line bundle
L
on
Z
, we get the
polarized pair

(Z,L)
. We choose a
linearization
of
L
by
H
, that is an
H
-action on
L
that is linear along fibers and makes the projection
Z\to L
an
H
-equivariant map. In this sense, the torus
H
acts on the polarized pair
(Z,L)
. Linearizations always exist under these assumptions
[
15
, Proposition 2.4]
and two linearizations of
L
differ by a character of
H

[
16
, Lemma 2.2]
.

Polytopes of fixed points and sections.

In the above setting, the
fixed point locus
decomposes in smooth connected components
Z^{H}=\bigsqcup_{Y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}Y

[
11
, Theorem 1]
. The induced
H
-action on the fiber
L_{z}
of every
z\in Z^{H}
naturally gives a
weight

\mu_{L}(z)\in\mathop{\rm\hskip 0.0ptM}\nolimits(H)
. Points in the same fixed point component
Y\in{\mathcal{Y}}
give the same weight, thus we can define the character
\mu_{L}(Y):=\mu_{L}(z)
for any
z\in Y
. Taking the convex hull of all the characters
\mu_{L}(Y),Y\in{\mathcal{Y}}
in the real vector space
\mathop{\rm\hskip 0.0ptM}\nolimits(H)_{\mathbb{R}}\simeq{\mathbb{R}}^{r}
, we get a convex polytope
\Delta(L)
, called the
polytope of fixed points
associated with the
H
-action on
(Z,L)
.

The same
H
-action provides another set of characters
\tilde{\Gamma}(L)\subset\mathop{\rm\hskip 0.0ptM}\nolimits(H)
corresponding to the decomposition
\HH^{0}(Z,L)=\bigoplus_{u\in\tilde{\Gamma}(L)}\HH^{0}(Z,L)_{u},
where
\HH^{0}(Z,L)_{u}
is the
H
-eigenspace whose associated weight is
u
. The convex hull
\Gamma(L)\subset\mathop{\rm\hskip 0.0ptM}\nolimits(H)_{\mathbb{R}}
of
\tilde{\Gamma}(L)
is called the
polytope of sections
. Remarkably,
\Delta(L)=\Gamma(L)
holds if
L
is globally generated
[
6
, Lemma 2.4]
.

GIT, Chow and limit quotients

The polytope of sections
\Gamma(L)
provides a set of GIT quotients of
Z
. In fact, every rational
u\in\Gamma(L)
defines a finitely generated
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra

A_{u}:=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\geq 0\\
mu\in\mathop{\rm\hskip 0.0ptM}\nolimits(H)\end{subarray}}\HH^{0}(Z,mL)_{mu},

and the normal projective variety
\mathcal{G}\!Z_{u}:=\mathrm{Proj}(A_{u})
is a GIT quotient of
Z
by the action of
H
. The map
Z\dashrightarrow\mathcal{G}\!Z_{u}
is defined on a subset of semistable points
Z^{\mathop{\rm ss}\nolimits}_{u}\subset Z
. We call these varieties the
GIT quotients of
(Z,L)
. Every projective GIT quotient of
Z
is a GIT quotient of a polarized pair
(Z,L)
, for some ample line bundle
L
.

The following definition, given by Kapranov in
[
12
]
, works in the broader context of linear group actions on projective varieties. We can always find an open subset
U\subset Z
such that the closures
\overline{H.z}
of the
H
-orbits of all points
z\in U
have constant dimension and homology class. Up to shrinking
U
, we can assume that it is also
H
-invariant. Thus, we have a geometric quotient
{\mathcal{G}}U:=U/H
with a natural embedding in the
Chow variety
of algebraic cycles of
Z

\psi:{\mathcal{G}}U\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Chow}(Z).

Definition 2.1
.

The
Chow quotient
of
Z
by
H
is the closure of
\psi({\mathcal{G}}U)
in the Chow variety
\mathrm{Chow}(Z)
. We denote its normalization by
{\mathcal{C}}Z
and call it the
normalized Chow quotient
of
Z
by
H
.

It can be proven that Chow quotients by reductive group actions are related to GIT quotients
[
12
, Theorem 0.4.3]
but this relation is much tighter in the case of torus actions. Recall that the GIT quotients of
Z
by the
H
-action form an inverse system. Indeed, if
Z^{\mathop{\rm ss}\nolimits}_{u_{2}}\subset Z^{\mathop{\rm ss}\nolimits}_{u_{1}}
for
u_{1}\in\Gamma(L_{1})
,
u_{2}\in\Gamma(L_{2})
, there is a projective dominant morphism
f_{u_{2},u_{1}}:\mathcal{G}\!Z_{u_{2}}\to\mathcal{G}\!Z_{u_{1}}
.
Since the open set
U
defined above is contained in every set of semistable points
Z^{\mathop{\rm ss}\nolimits}_{u}
, we get a morphism
\mathcal{G}U\to\mathcal{G}\!Z_{u}
, for every
u
, and so a morphism from
\mathcal{G}U
to the inverse limit of the GIT system.
The unique irreducible component of the inverse limit of the GIT inverse system that contains the image of
\mathcal{G}U
is called
limit quotient of
Z
by
H
. Its normalization is denoted by
\mathcal{L}Z
and is called the
normalized limit quotient
.

Analogously, one may consider the inverse system of GIT quotients of
(Z,L)
, and define the
limit quotient of
(Z,L)
by
H
, and its normalization
\mathcal{L}Z(L)
.

The following statement, that directly links Chow quotient by torus actions to GIT quotients and their inverse limit, has been proved in
[
3
]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

[
3
, Corollary 2.6, 2.7]

Let
H
be an algebraic torus effectively acting on a normal projective variety
Z
. Then
{\mathcal{C}}Z\simeq\mathcal{L}Z\simeq\mathcal{L}Z(L)
, for every ample line bundle
L
on
Z
.

Let
\chi_{u}:\mathcal{L}Z\to\mathcal{G}\!Z_{u}
be the natural projections for every
u\in\Gamma(L)
. Note that these
\chi_{u}
are all birational morphisms. By definition, the normalized limit quotient satisfies the following universal property.

Remark 2.3
.

Let
M
be an irreducible normal variety with a collection of birational morphisms
\phi_{u}:M\to\mathcal{G}\!Z_{u}
satisfying
\phi_{u}=f_{v,u}\circ\phi_{v}
for every
u,v\in\Gamma
such that
Z^{ss}_{v}\subset Z^{ss}_{u}
. Then there exists a unique birational morphism
\Phi:M\to\mathcal{L}Z
such that
\phi_{u}=\chi_{u}\circ\Phi
for all
u\in\Gamma
.

Combinatorial quotients

The definition of Chow quotients has a fully combinatorial description in the case of subtorus actions on toric varieties. This construction has been introduced in
[
13
]
with the name of
combinatorial quotient
.

Now assume that
Z
is a normal toric variety with maximal torus
T\subset Z
and associated fan
\Sigma\subset\No(T)_{\mathbb{R}}
. Let
H\subset T
be a subtorus and call
\pi:\No(T)_{\mathbb{R}}\to\No(T/H)_{\mathbb{R}}
the natural projection. The
quotient fan
of
\Sigma
by
H
is the fan in
\No(T/H)_{\mathbb{R}}
whose minimal cone containing
x\in\No(T/H)
is given by

\bigcap_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\Sigma\\
x\in\pi(\sigma)\end{subarray}}\pi(\sigma).

Definition 2.4
.

The normal
T/H
-toric variety whose fan is
\Sigma/H
is called the
combinatorial quotient
of
Z
by
H
.

In the projective case, we have the following result.

Proposition 2.5
.

[
13
, Theorem 2.1]

Let
Z
be a projective
T
-toric variety and
H\subset T
be a subtorus. The combinatorial quotient of
Z
by
H
is isomorphic to the normalized Chow quotient
{\mathcal{C}}Z
.

We stress that combinatorial quotients can be performed for any toric variety, and they are still related to algebraic cycles of
Z
, although only topologically
[
13
, Proposition 2.1]
.

Maximal torus action on the complete flag

Fixed flags and their weights

The general

As in

Proposition 4.1
.

For every

F_{\sigma}=\{gB\in F\,|\,\sigma B\in\overline{H.gB}\}.

Proof.

It suffices to show that the equality holds for

, so that we can write

for some

such that

t^{\mu}gB=\exp\left(\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}t^{-\mu(\alpha)}g_{-\alpha}\right)B\,\,\,\mbox{for every}\,\,t\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*},

thus

. Vice versa, since

is an

Therefore, such indeterminacies are precisely resolved by the blowup

∎

Corollary 6.10
.

The anticanonical morphism
\varphi:X\to{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
is an embedding.

Proof.

We have already discussed in Remark

is an embedding. Since
\varphi

W

), it must be an embedding at

∎

Remark 6.11
.

Another straightforward computation tells us what is the linear system of quadrics in
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
containing
\varphi(X)
. Denoting our chosen homogeneous coordinates in
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
by
(X_{0}:\dots:X_{4})
, our linear system of quadrics is generated by

Q_{1}:(X_{0}-X_{2})X_{4}+X_{1}(X_{2}-X_{3})=0,\quad Q_{2}:(X_{0}-X_{2})X_{3}+X_{2}(X_{4}-X_{1})=0.

These are two quadric cones, whose vertices are, respectively,

[e_{1}+e_{4}]=\varphi(B_{23}\cap B_{41})\,\mbox{ and }\,[e_{0}+e_{2}+e_{3}]=\varphi(B_{12}\cap B_{34}),

i.e. the images of the two singular points of

. Note that the line in
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
joining these two points is contained in the two quadrics, being the strict transform of the line in
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
of equation

.

Now, since
\mathop{\rm deg}\nolimits\varphi(X)=4
, we may conclude that

X\simeq\varphi(X)=Q_{1}\cap Q_{2}.

In particular,
X
(which, is a singular Del Pezzo surface of degree

6.5
) can be obtained as a degeneration of a smooth Del Pezzo surface, which is the complete intersection of two general quadrics in

.

Corollary 6.14
.

The automorphism group of

Proof.

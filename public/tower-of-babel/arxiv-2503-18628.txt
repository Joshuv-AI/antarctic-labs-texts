QCD sum rules study on weak decays Ω c 0 → Ω − ​ ( π + , ρ + , l + ​ ν l ) \Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Omega^{-}(\pi^{+},\rho^{+},l^{+}\nu_{l})
Yu-Ji Shi 1 , Jun Zeng 2,3

Abstract
We study the weak decays of the charmed baryon Ω c 0 \Omega_{c}^{0} to the Ω − \Omega^{-} baryon within the framework of QCD sum rules. A three-point correlation function is defined for calculating eight form factors governing the Ω c 0 → Ω − \Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Omega^{-} transition. The cutting rules are employed to extract the double imaginary parts of the correlation functions, enabling their expression in the form of dispersive integrals. These form factors are then used to determine the branching fractions for the hadronic decays Ω c 0 → Ω − ​ π + \Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Omega^{-}\pi^{+} and Ω c 0 → Ω − ​ ρ + \Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Omega^{-}\rho^{+} , as well as the semi-leptonic decay Ω c 0 → Ω − ​ ℓ + ​ ν ℓ \Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Omega^{-}\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell} . Our results are compared with existing theoretical predictions and experimental data, providing a comprehensive analysis of Ω c 0 \Omega_{c}^{0} decays and offering valuable insights into the dynamics of charmed baryon decays.



I
Introduction

The decays of charmed baryons provide a unique opportunity to probe the interplay between strong and weak interactions, as well as exploring the non-perturbative dynamics of QCD. Among the charmed baryons, the anti-triplet states:
\Lambda_{c}^{+},\Xi_{c}^{0},\Xi_{c}^{+}
and the sextet states:
\Sigma_{c}^{(0,+,++)},\Xi_{c}^{{}^{\prime}(0,+)},\Omega_{c}^{0}
have been the subject of extensive experimental and theoretical investigations. The
\Sigma_{c}
and
\Xi^{\prime}_{c}
baryons predominantly decay via strong and electromagnetic decays, while the anti-triplet and
\Omega_{c}^{0}
undergo weak interactions. Recent experiments have significantly improved our understanding of the weak decays of
\Lambda_{c}
and
\Xi_{c}
baryons, with benchmark modes such as
\Lambda_{c}\to pK^{-}\pi^{+}

[
1
,
2
]
and
\Xi_{c}^{0}\to\Xi^{-}\pi^{+}

[
3
]
being precisely measured. These results have provided critical insights into the underlying mechanisms of charmed baryon decays.

In comparison, the experimental exploration of weak decays involving the
\Omega_{c}^{0}
baryon remains relatively limited. While a direct measurement of the benchmark decay mode
\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Omega^{-}\pi^{+}
is still lacking, experimental studies have instead concentrated on determining relative branching fractions, expressed as

R(X)=\frac{\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{c}^{0}\to X)}{\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Omega^{-}\pi^{+})},

(1)

which are experimentally more tractable. Recent results from LHCb
[
4
]
, ALICE
[
5
]
, and Belle
[
6
,
7
,
8

have provided crucial data on

for decays such as
\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Omega^{-}K^{+}
,
\Xi^{-}\pi^{+}
, and
\Omega^{-}e^{+}\nu_{e}
. These measurements emphasize the necessity of precise theoretical predictions for the absolute branching fraction of
\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Omega^{-}\pi^{+}
, which are vital for interpreting experimental observations.

The
\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Omega^{-}\pi^{+}
decay is particularly interesting from a theoretical perspective due to its simple kinematic and dynamic structure. This decay is governed solely by factorizable processes, with non-perturbative effects entirely encapsulated in the baryon transition form factors. This simplicity makes it an ideal candidate for theoretical studies, as it allows for a clearer understanding of the underlying QCD dynamics. Furthermore, the decay
\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Omega^{-}\pi^{+}
exhibits a topological structure analogous to other modes, such as
\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Omega^{-}\rho^{+}
and
\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Omega^{-}\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell}
. This similarity implies that a unified theoretical description of these decays is achievable, contingent upon an accurate determination of the
\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Omega^{-}
transition form factors.

In this work, we study
\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Omega^{-}
decays by employing the QCD sum rules (QCDSR), a model-independent and QCD-based framework, and calculate the form factors governing the
\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Omega^{-}
transition. Using these form factors, we compute the branching fractions for the benchmark mode
\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Omega^{-}\pi^{+}
as well as other related decay modes, such as
\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Omega^{-}\rho^{+}
and
\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Omega^{-}\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell}

\Omega_{c}^{0}
decays. This study not only enhances our understanding of the
\Omega_{c}^{0}

II
Correlation function in QCDSR

The effective weak Hamiltonians for the hadronic and semileptonic
\Omega_{c}\to\Omega
decays are

\displaystyle{\cal H}_{H}

\displaystyle=\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}V_{cs}^{*}V_{ud}\left[c_{1}(\bar{u}d)(\bar{s}c)+c_{2}(\bar{s}d)(\bar{u}c)\right],

\displaystyle{\cal H}_{L}

\displaystyle=\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}V_{cs}^{*}(\bar{s}c)({\bar{u}}_{\nu}\nu_{l}),

(2)

where
G_{F}
is the Fermi constant,
V_{cs}
and
V_{ud}
are the Cabibbo–Kobayashi–Maskawa (CKM) matrix elements,
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
are the Wilson coefficients, and the notation
({\bar{q}}_{1}q_{2})={\bar{q}}_{1}\gamma_{\mu}(1-\gamma_{5})q_{2}
is adopted. As demonstrated by factorization in Ref.
[
28
]
, the transition matrix elements induced by
(\bar{s}d)(\bar{u}c)
are equivalent to those induced by
(\bar{u}d)(\bar{s}c)/N_{c}
. Consequently,
{\cal H}_{H}
can be simplified as

\displaystyle{\cal H}_{H}

\displaystyle=\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}V_{cs}^{*}V_{ud}a_{1}(\bar{u}d)(\bar{s}c),

(3)

with
a_{1}=c_{1}+c_{2}/N_{c}
. The transition matrix element of
\Omega_{c}\to\Omega
can be parameterized by 8 form factors:

\displaystyle\langle\Omega(p^{\prime},s^{\prime})|\bar{s}\gamma^{\mu}(1-\gamma_{5})c|\Omega_{c}(p,s)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bar{u}}_{\alpha}(p^{\prime},s^{\prime})\left[\frac{p^{\alpha}}{M_{1}}\left(\gamma^{\mu}f_{1}(q^{2})+\frac{p^{\mu}}{M_{1}}f_{2}(q^{2})+\frac{p^{\prime\mu}}{M_{2}}f_{3}(q^{2})\right)+g^{\alpha\mu}f_{4}(q^{2})\right]\gamma_{5}u(p,s)

\displaystyle-{\bar{u}}_{\alpha}(p^{\prime},s^{\prime})\left[\frac{p^{\alpha}}{M_{1}}\left(\gamma^{\mu}g_{1}(q^{2})+\frac{p^{\mu}}{M_{1}}g_{2}(q^{2})+\frac{p^{\prime\mu}}{M_{2}}g_{3}(q^{2})\right)+g^{\alpha\mu}g_{4}(q^{2})\right]u(p,s),

(4)

where
q=p-p^{\prime}
,
M_{1},M_{2}
are the masses of
\Omega_{c},\Omega
.
f_{1,2,3,4}
and
g_{1,2,3,4}
are the form factors for the transitions induced by vector and axial-vector currents, respectively.

Within the framework of QCDSR, we define a three-point correlation function to calculate the 8 form factors for the
\Omega_{c}\to\Omega
transition, as defined in Eq. (
4
):

\displaystyle\Pi_{\alpha\mu}(p_{2},p_{1})=i^{2}\int d^{4}xd^{4}y\ e^{ip_{2}\cdot y}e^{-ip_{1}\cdot x}\langle 0|T\{J_{\alpha}^{\Omega}(y)J_{\mu}(0){\bar{J}}^{\Omega_{c}}(x)\}|0\rangle,

(5)

where
J_{\alpha}^{\Omega}
and
J^{\Omega_{c}}
are the interpolating currents for
\Omega
and
\Omega_{c}
baryons
[
17
,
29
,
30
]
:

\displaystyle J_{\alpha}^{\Omega}

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{ijk}(s_{i}^{T}C\gamma_{\alpha}s_{j})s_{k},

\displaystyle J^{\Omega_{c}}

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{ijk}(s_{i}^{T}C\gamma^{\beta}s_{j})\gamma_{\beta}\gamma_{5}c_{k},

(6)

J_{\mu}={\bar{s}}\gamma_{\mu}(1-\gamma_{5})s
is the
V-A
current. Note that the correlation function in Eq. (
5
) contains two Lorentz indices, resulting in dependence on numerous spinor and Lorentz structures, most of which are redundant for extracting the form factors. To simplify the correlation function, we perform the following four projections to eliminate the Lorentz indices:

\displaystyle\Pi_{i}(p_{2},p_{1})={\cal P}_{i}^{\alpha\mu}\Pi_{\alpha\mu}(p_{2},p_{1}),

\displaystyle{\cal P}_{i}^{\alpha\mu}=\{p_{1}^{\alpha}p_{2}^{\mu},\ p_{1}^{\alpha}p_{1}^{\mu},\ M_{1}^{2}g^{\alpha\mu},\ M_{1}p_{1}^{\alpha}\gamma^{\mu}\}.

(7)

Each
\Pi_{i}(p_{2},p_{1})
can be expanded in terms of 8 independent Dirac structures:

\displaystyle\Pi_{i}(p_{2},p_{1})=\sum_{k=1}^{8}C_{i}^{k}(p_{2},p_{1})N_{k},

\displaystyle N_{k}=\{\not{p}_{2}\not{p}_{1}\gamma_{5},\ \not{p}_{2}\gamma_{5},\ \not{p}_{1}\gamma_{5},\ \gamma_{5},\ \not{p}_{2}\not{p}_{1},\ \not{p}_{2},\ \not{p}_{1},\ 1\}.

(8)

The four correlation functions
\Pi_{i}
are calculated separately at both the hadron and QCD levels. These results are then equated using the principle of quark-hadron duality. After applying the Borel transformation, the final sum rules for extracting the form factors are obtained, which can be expressed as

\displaystyle\Pi_{i}^{H}(p_{2},p_{1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{8}C_{i}^{k,H}(p_{2},p_{1})N_{k},

\displaystyle\Pi_{i}^{\rm QCD}(p_{2},p_{1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{8}C_{i}^{k,\rm QCD}(p_{2},p_{1})N_{k}^{\rm QCD},

\displaystyle{\cal B}_{T_{1}^{2},T_{2}^{2}}\left[C_{i}^{k,H}(p_{2},p_{1})\right]

\displaystyle={\cal B}_{T_{1}^{2},T_{2}^{2}}\left[C_{i}^{k,\rm QCD}(p_{2},p_{1})\right],

(9)

where
{\cal B}_{T^{2},T_{2}^{2}}
denotes the double Borel transformation applied to
p_{1}^{2}
and
p_{2}^{2}
, with
T^{2}
and
T^{\prime 2}
representing the corresponding Borel parameters. Since
C_{i}^{k,H}(p_{2},p_{1})
depends on the eight form factors, Eq. (
9
) can, in principle, be utilized to determine all of them. However, Eq. (
9
) yields 32 equations, significantly exceeding the number of form factors. To ensure a unique solution, we select
k=1
and
k=5
in Eq. (
9
) to solve for
f_{i}(q^{2})
and
g_{i}(q^{2})
, respectively. This selection is justified by the fact that
C_{i}^{1,\rm QCD}(p_{2},p_{1})
and
C_{i}^{5,\rm QCD}(p_{2},p_{1})
, corresponding to the coefficients of the highest-dimensional Dirac structures
\not{p}_{2}\not{p}_{1}\gamma_{5}
and
\not{p}_{2}\not{p}_{1}
, are suppressed by the highest power of
1/q^{2}
. This suppression is crucial for ensuring the convergence of the operator product expansion (OPE) at the QCD level.

III
Hadron level calculation

At the hadron level, we insert complete sets of intermediate states with the same quantum numbers as
\Omega_{c}
and
\Omega
into the correlation function
\Pi_{i}
:

\displaystyle\Pi_{i}^{H}(p_{2},p_{1})=

\displaystyle i^{2}\int d^{4}xd^{4}y\ e^{ip_{2}\cdot y}e^{-ip_{1}\cdot x}\sum_{s,s^{\prime}}\int\frac{d^{4}p^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{d^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{i}{p_{2}^{2}-M_{2}^{2}}\frac{i}{p_{1}^{2}-M_{1}^{2}}

\displaystyle\times{\cal P}_{i}^{\alpha\mu}\langle 0|T\{J_{\alpha}^{\Omega}(y)|\Omega(p^{\prime},s^{\prime})\rangle\langle\Omega(p^{\prime},s^{\prime})|J_{\mu}(0)|\Omega_{c}(p,s)\rangle\langle\Omega_{c}(p,s)|{\bar{J}}^{\Omega_{c}}(x)\}|0\rangle+\cdots,

(10)

where we have explicitly isolated the term corresponding to the lowest states, referred to as the pole contribution, while the ellipsis represents contributions from excited states and the continuum spectrum. The first and the last matrix elements above can be expressed by the decay constants of
\Omega
and
\Omega_{c}
, which are defined as

\displaystyle\langle 0|J_{\alpha}^{\Omega}(0)|\Omega(p^{\prime},s^{\prime})\rangle

\displaystyle=f_{\Omega}u_{\alpha}(p^{\prime},s^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\langle 0|J^{\Omega_{c}}(0)|\Omega_{c}(p,s)\rangle

\displaystyle=f_{\Omega_{c}}u(p,s).

(11)

It should be noted that, in principle,
J_{\alpha}^{\Omega}
can couple to both spin-3/2 and spin-1/2
\Omega
states. However, since the ground state of
\Omega
has spin-3/2, we retain only the spin-3/2 component in Eq. (
10
) and attribute the spin-1/2
\Omega
states to the contributions from excited states. Summing up the polarizations of
\Omega
and
\Omega_{c}

\Omega

\displaystyle\sum_{s}

\displaystyle u_{\alpha}(p,s)\bar{u}_{\beta}(p,s)=-g_{\alpha\beta}+\frac{1}{3}\gamma_{\alpha}\gamma_{\beta}+\frac{2}{3}\frac{p_{\alpha}p_{\beta}}{M_{2}^{2}}-\frac{p_{\alpha}\gamma_{\beta}-p_{\beta}\gamma_{\alpha}}{3M_{2}},

(12)

one arrive at

\displaystyle C_{i}^{k,H}=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{\Omega_{c}}\lambda_{\Omega}}{(p_{1}^{2}-M_{1}^{2})(p_{2}^{2}-M_{2}^{2})}\tilde{C}_{i}^{k,H},

(13)

\displaystyle\tilde{C}_{1}^{1,H}=

\displaystyle-\frac{\left(M_{1}^{4}-2M_{1}^{2}\left(M_{2}^{2}+q^{2}\right)+\left(M_{2}^{2}-q^{2}\right)^{2}\right)\left(2M_{1}M_{2}(f_{1}+f_{3})+f_{2}\left(M_{1}^{2}+M_{2}^{2}-q^{2}\right)\right)}{12M_{1}^{2}M_{2}^{2}},

\displaystyle\tilde{C}_{2}^{1,H}=

\displaystyle-\frac{\left(M_{1}^{4}-2M_{1}^{2}\left(M_{2}^{2}+q^{2}\right)+\left(M_{2}^{2}-q^{2}\right)^{2}\right)\left(2M_{1}M_{2}(-f_{1}+f_{2}+f_{4})+f_{3}\left(M_{1}^{2}+M_{2}^{2}-q^{2}\right)\right)}{12M_{1}M_{2}^{3}},

\displaystyle\tilde{C}_{3}^{1,H}=

\displaystyle-\frac{4M_{1}M_{2}\left(f_{1}\left((M_{1}+M_{2})^{2}-q^{2}\right)-3f_{4}M_{1}M_{2}\right)+f_{2}\left(M_{1}^{4}-2M_{1}^{2}\left(M_{2}^{2}+q^{2}\right)+\left(M_{2}^{2}-q^{2}\right)^{2}\right)}{6M_{2}^{2}},

\displaystyle\tilde{C}_{4}^{1,H}=

\displaystyle-\frac{\left((M_{1}-M_{2})^{2}-q^{2}\right)}{6M_{2}^{2}}\Big[2f_{1}\left(q^{2}-(M_{1}+M_{2})^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+f_{2}\left((M_{1}+M_{2})^{2}-q^{2}\right)+f_{3}M_{1}^{2}+2f_{3}M_{1}M_{2}+f_{3}M_{2}^{2}-f_{3}q^{2}+4f_{4}M_{1}M_{2}\Big],

(14)

\displaystyle\tilde{C}_{1}^{5,H}=

\displaystyle-\frac{\left(M_{1}^{4}-2M_{1}^{2}\left(M_{2}^{2}+q^{2}\right)+\left(M_{2}^{2}-q^{2}\right)^{2}\right)\left(2M_{1}M_{2}(g_{1}+g_{3})+g_{2}\left(M_{1}^{2}+M_{2}^{2}-q^{2}\right)\right)}{12M_{1}^{2}M_{2}^{2}},

\displaystyle\tilde{C}_{2}^{5,H}=

\displaystyle-\frac{\left(M_{1}^{4}-2M_{1}^{2}\left(M_{2}^{2}+q^{2}\right)+\left(M_{2}^{2}-q^{2}\right)^{2}\right)\left(2M_{1}M_{2}(g_{1}+g_{2}+g_{4})+g_{3}\left(M_{1}^{2}+M_{2}^{2}-q^{2}\right)\right)}{12M_{1}M_{2}^{3}},

\displaystyle=\frac{2M_{2}^{2}}{\left(M_{1}^{2}-2M_{1}M_{2}+M_{2}^{2}-q^{2}\right)^{2}\left(M_{1}^{2}+2M_{1}M_{2}+M_{2}^{2}-q^{2}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\Big[M_{1}^{4}({\rm Im}^{2}C_{3}^{5}-8{\rm Im}^{2}C_{1}^{5})-2M_{1}^{3}M_{2}(2{\rm Im}^{2}C_{1}^{5}+{\rm Im}^{2}C_{4}^{5})

\displaystyle-2M_{1}^{2}\left(M_{2}^{2}(4{\rm Im}^{2}C_{1}^{5}-10{\rm Im}^{2}C_{2}^{5}+{\rm Im}^{2}C_{3}^{5}-2{\rm Im}^{2}C_{4}^{5})+q^{2}({\rm Im}^{2}C_{3}^{5}-4{\rm Im}^{2}C_{1}^{5})\right)

\displaystyle+2M_{1}M_{2}{\rm Im}^{2}C_{4}^{5}\left(q^{2}-M_{2}^{2}\right)+{\rm Im}^{2}C_{3}^{5}\left(M_{2}^{2}-q^{2}\right)^{2}\Big],

(23)

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\left(M_{1}^{2}-2M_{1}M_{2}+M_{2}^{2}-q^{2}\right)^{2}\left(M_{1}^{2}+2M_{1}M_{2}+M_{2}^{2}-q^{2}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\Big[M_{1}^{6}(2{\rm Im}^{2}C_{1}^{5}-{\rm Im}^{2}C_{3}^{5})-M_{1}^{5}M_{2}({\rm Im}^{2}C_{3}^{5}+{\rm Im}^{2}C_{4}^{5})

\displaystyle+M_{1}^{4}\left(M_{2}^{2}(8{\rm Im}^{2}C_{1}^{5}-8{\rm Im}^{2}C_{2}^{5}+{\rm Im}^{2}C_{3}^{5}-2{\rm Im}^{2}C_{4}^{5})-4{\rm Im}^{2}C_{1}^{5}q^{2}+3{\rm Im}^{2}C_{3}^{5}q^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+2M_{1}^{3}\left(M_{2}^{3}(2{\rm Im}^{2}C_{2}^{5}+{\rm Im}^{2}C_{3}^{5}+3{\rm Im}^{2}C_{4}^{5})+M_{2}q^{2}({\rm Im}^{2}C_{3}^{5}+{\rm Im}^{2}C_{4}^{5})\right)

\displaystyle+M_{1}^{2}\left(M_{2}^{2}-q^{2}\right)\big(M_{2}^{2}(2{\rm Im}^{2}C_{1}^{5}-8{\rm Im}^{2}C_{2}^{5}+{\rm Im}^{2}C_{3}^{5}-2{\rm Im}^{2}C_{4}^{5})-2{\rm Im}^{2}C_{1}^{5}q^{2}

\displaystyle+3{\rm Im}^{2}C_{3}^{5}q^{2}\big)-M_{1}M_{2}\left(M_{2}^{2}-q^{2}\right)^{2}({\rm Im}^{2}C_{3}^{5}+{\rm Im}^{2}C_{4}^{5})-{\rm Im}^{2}C_{3}^{5}\left(M_{2}^{2}-q^{2}\right)^{3}\Big],

(24)

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M_{1}^{2}\left(M_{1}^{2}-2M_{1}M_{2}+M_{2}^{2}-q^{2}\right)\left(M_{1}^{2}+2M_{1}M_{2}+M_{2}^{2}-q^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\times\Big[M_{1}^{4}({\rm Im}^{2}C_{3}^{5}-2{\rm Im}^{2}C_{1}^{5})+M_{1}^{3}M_{2}(2{\rm Im}^{2}C_{1}^{5}+{\rm Im}^{2}C_{4}^{5})

\displaystyle-2M_{1}^{2}\left(M_{2}^{2}({\rm Im}^{2}C_{1}^{5}-{\rm Im}^{2}C_{2}^{5}+{\rm Im}^{2}C_{3}^{5}+{\rm Im}^{2}C_{4}^{5})+q^{2}({\rm Im}^{2}C_{3}^{5}-{\rm Im}^{2}C_{1}^{5})\right)

\displaystyle+M_{1}M_{2}{\rm Im}^{2}C_{4}^{5}\left(M_{2}^{2}-q^{2}\right)+{\rm Im}^{2}C_{3}^{5}\left(M_{2}^{2}-q^{2}\right)^{2}\Big].

(25)

Here for simplicity we have omitted the superscript
{}^{\prime}\rm QCD^{\prime}
on the right hand sides. Now, by calculating all the double imaginary parts
{\rm Im}^{2}C_{i}^{1,5}
at the QCD level, we can obtain the analytical expressions for the form factors.

IV
QCD level calculation

Figure 1:
The perturbative diagram for the correlation function. The white crossed dot represents the weak current, and the two black dots denote the interpolating currents for
\Omega_{c}
and
\Omega
. The double line corresponds to the charm quark, while the single lines represent the strange quarks.

In this section, we introduce the main approach to calculating the correlation functions
\Pi_{i}(p_{2},p_{1})
at the QCD level, formulated as a double dispersion integral. The overall computational methodology follows the approach employed in our previous work Ref.
[
30
]
. In the deep Euclidean region, where
p_{1}^{2},p_{2}^{2},q^{2}\ll 0
, the analytical calculation of
\Pi_{i}(p_{2},p_{1})
at the QCD level is facilitated through OPE, leveraging the explicit expressions of the three currents provided in Eq. (
5
). The OPE results are expanded as a series of condensate matrix elements, each associated with progressively higher dimensions. For the purposes of this calculation, the OPE series is truncated to include contributions from operator matrix elements up to dimension 5.

The leading term in the OPE series corresponds to the condensate of the identity operator, which represents the matrix element of the perturbative diagram illustrated in Fig.
1
, where each line denotes a free quark propagator. The amplitude corresponding to this leading term assumes the form

\displaystyle\Pi_{i}^{{\rm pert}}(p_{1},p_{2})=\int\frac{d^{4}k_{1}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{d^{4}k_{1}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{N_{i}^{{\rm pert}}(p_{1},p_{2,},k_{1},k_{2})}{(k_{1}^{2}-m_{s}^{2})(k_{2}^{2}-m_{s}^{2})[(p_{1}-k_{1}-k_{2})^{2}-m_{c}^{2}][(p_{2}-k_{1}-k_{2})^{2}-m_{s}^{2}]},

(26)

where the nominator
N_{i}^{{\rm pert}}(p_{1},p_{2})
reads as

\displaystyle N_{i}^{{\rm pert}}(p_{1},p_{2},k_{1},k_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2N_{c}!\ {\cal P}^{\alpha\mu}\Big\{(\not{p}_{2}-\not{k}_{1}-\not{k}_{2}+m_{s})\gamma_{\mu}(\not{p}_{1}-\not{k}_{1}-\not{k}_{2}+m_{c})\gamma_{5}\gamma_{\beta}{\rm tr}[(\not{k}_{1}+m_{s})\gamma^{\beta}(\not{k}_{2}-m_{s})\gamma_{\alpha}]

\displaystyle+2(\not{k}_{1}+m_{s})\gamma^{\beta}(\not{k}_{2}-m_{s})\gamma_{\alpha}(\not{p}_{2}-\not{k}_{1}-\not{k}_{2}+m_{s})\gamma_{\mu}(\not{p}_{1}-\not{k}_{1}-\not{k}_{2}+m_{c})\gamma_{5}\gamma_{\beta}\Big\}.

(27)

The correlation function must now be expressed in the form of a double dispersion integral, as shown in Eq. (
16
), where the double imaginary part of the correlation function is extracted. To achieve this, we apply the cutting rules to place each propagator in Fig.
1
on shell, performing the replacement
1/(k^{2}-m^{2})\to(-2\pi i)\delta(k^{2}-m^{2})
. This yields

\displaystyle{\rm Im}^{2}\Pi_{i}^{{\rm pert}}(p_{1},p_{2})=\frac{1}{(2i)^{2}}{\rm Disc}^{2}\Pi_{i}^{{\rm pert}}(p_{1},p_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-2\pi i)^{4}\int\frac{d^{4}k_{1}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{d^{4}k_{1}}{(2\pi)^{4}}N_{i}^{{\rm pert}}(p_{1},p_{2},k_{1},k_{2})

\displaystyle\times\delta(k_{1}^{2}-m_{s}^{2})\delta(k_{2}^{2}-m_{s}^{2})\delta[(p_{1}-k_{1}-k_{2})^{2}-m_{c}^{2}]\delta[(p_{2}-k_{1}-k_{2})^{2}-m_{s}^{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4(2\pi)^{4}}\int dm_{12}^{2}\int d\Phi_{\Delta}[p_{1},p_{2},m_{c},m_{s},m_{12}]\int d\Phi_{2}[k_{12},m_{s},m_{s}]\ N_{i}^{{\rm pert}}(p_{1},p_{2},k_{1},k_{2}),

(28)

where the doubly imaginary part is expressed by a convolution of two phase space integrations. One of them is the standard two-body phase space integration
d\Phi_{2}
, and another one is a triangle diagram integration
d\Phi_{\Delta}
. The corresponding integration measures are

\displaystyle\int d\Phi_{2}[p,m_{1},m_{2}]

\displaystyle=\int d^{4}k_{1}d^{4}k_{2}\delta^{4}(p-k_{1}-k_{2})\delta(k_{1}^{2}-m_{1}^{2})\delta(k_{2}^{2}-m_{2}^{2}),

\displaystyle\int d\Phi_{\Delta}[p_{1},p_{2},m_{1},m_{2},m]

\displaystyle=\int d^{4}k\ \delta(k^{2}-m^{2})\delta[(p_{1}-k)^{2}-m_{1}^{2}]\delta[(p_{2}-k)^{2}-m_{2}^{2}].

(29)

The scalar integration of the triangle diagram can be explicitly calculated as

\displaystyle\int d\Phi_{\Delta}(p_{1},p_{2},m_{1},m_{2},m)\cdot 1=\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{\lambda}}\Theta[s_{1},s_{2},q^{2},m_{1},m_{2},m]\theta[s_{1}-s_{1}^{\rm min}]\theta[s_{2}-s_{2}^{\rm min}],

(30)

where
\lambda=(s_{1}+s_{2}-q^{2})^{2}-4s_{1}s_{2}
with
p_{1}^{2}=s_{1},p_{2}^{2}=s_{2}
.
\Theta
is a
\theta
function constraining the
s_{1},s_{2},q^{2}
:

\displaystyle\Theta[s_{1},s_{2},q^{2},m_{1},m_{2},m]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta\big[-m_{2}^{4}s_{1}-m_{1}^{4}s_{2}+m_{2}^{2}[m^{2}(q^{2}+s_{1}-s_{2})+s_{1}(q^{2}-s_{1}+s_{2})]

\displaystyle-q^{2}(m^{4}+s_{1}s_{2}-m^{2}(-q^{2}+s_{1}+s_{2}))+m_{1}^{2}[(q^{2}+s_{1}-s_{2})s_{2}

\displaystyle+m^{2}(q^{2}-s_{1}+s_{2})+m_{2}^{2}(-q^{2}+s_{1}+s_{2})]\big],

(31)

The remaining two
\theta
functions ensure that
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
lie above the corresponding quark-level thresholds, specifically
s_{1,2}>s_{1,2}^{\rm min}=(m_{c,s}+2m_{s})^{2}
. The integration of higher-rank tensors, such as
\int d\Phi_{\Delta}k^{\mu}k^{\nu}\cdots
, can be reduced to scalar integrals of the form given in Eq. (
30
) using the Passarino-Veltman reduction. Detailed results of this reduction can be found in the appendix of Ref.
[
31
]
.

Figure 2:
The
\bar{q}q
condensate diagram for the correlation function. One of the light quark lines is disconnected, allowing the remaining quark fields represented by the crosses to condense in the vacuum

The next term in the OPE arises from the condensate of the
\bar{q}q
operator. The corresponding matrix element is illustrated in Fig.
2
, where one of the light quark lines is disconnected, allowing the remaining quark fields represented by the crosses to condense in the vacuum. The
\bar{q}q
condensate is defined as

\displaystyle\langle 0|{\bar{q}}_{a}^{i}(0)q_{b}^{j}(0)|0\rangle=\frac{1}{12}\delta_{ij}\delta_{ba}\langle\bar{q}q\rangle,

(32)

where
i,j
and
a,b
denote color and spinor indexes, respectively. It should be noted that in Fig.
2
, only the lower two quark lines can be disconnected. If the side strange quark line is disconnected, the diagram would lose its discontinuity in the
p_{2}
channel, resulting in the vanishing of the corresponding double imaginary part.

Figure 3:
The
GG
condensate diagram for the correlation function. As an illustration, here two representative diagrams are shown. In the left diagram, two quark lines interact with a single background gluon field, while in the right diagram, one quark line interacts with two background gluon fields.

The third and fourth terms in the OPE correspond to the contributions from the
GG
and
\bar{q}Gq
condensates, respectively. The
GG
condensate originates from diagrams in which internal quarks interact with two soft gluons. As an illustration, two representative diagrams are shown in Fig.
3
. In the left diagram, two quark lines interact with a single background gluon field, while in the right diagram, one quark line interacts with two background gluon fields. The quark propagator in the presence of a background gluon field is expressed as

\displaystyle D(x,0)=

\displaystyle\ i\int\frac{d^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{4}}e^{-ik\cdot x}\left[\frac{\delta_{ij}}{\not{k}-m}-\frac{g_{s}G_{\alpha\beta}^{A}t_{ij}^{A}}{4}\frac{\sigma^{\alpha\beta}(\not{k}+m)+(\not{k}+m)\sigma^{\alpha\beta}}{(k^{2}-m^{2})^{2}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\frac{g_{s}^{2}(t^{A}t^{B})_{ij}G_{\alpha\beta}^{A}G_{\mu\nu}^{B}[f^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}(k)+f^{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}(k)+f^{\alpha\mu\nu\beta}(k)]}{4(k^{2}-m^{2})^{2}}+\cdots\right],

(33)

where

\displaystyle f^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}(k)=\ (\not{k}+m)\gamma^{\alpha}(\not{k}+m)\gamma^{\beta}(\not{k}+m)\gamma^{\mu}(\not{k}+m)\gamma^{\nu}(\not{k}+m),

(34)

The gluon condensate is defined as

\displaystyle g_{s}^{2}\langle 0|G_{\alpha\beta}^{A}(0)G_{\kappa\tau}^{B}(0)|0\rangle=\frac{1}{96}\langle GG\rangle\delta_{AB}(g_{\alpha\kappa}g_{\beta\tau}-g_{\alpha\tau}{\beta\kappa}).

(35)

Note that with the use of Eq. (
33
), before extracting the double imaginary part of the correlation function, the
(k^{2}-m^{2})^{2}
in the denominator should be reduced to
k^{2}-m^{2}
using the trick:
1/(k^{2}-m^{2})^{2}=(\partial/\partial M^{2})[1/(k^{2}-M^{2})]|_{M^{2}=m^{2}}
.

Figure 4:
The
\bar{q}Gq
condensate diagram for the correlation function. One quark line is disconnected, while the other quark line interacts simultaneously with a background gluon field and two light quark fields

The
\bar{q}Gq
condensate comes from the diagrams where one quark line is disconnected, while the other quark line interacts simultaneously with a background gluon field and two light quark fields, as shown in Fig.
4
. The
\bar{q}Gq
condensate is defined as

\displaystyle\langle 0|{\bar{q}}_{a}^{i}(x)g_{s}G_{\alpha\beta}^{A}(0)q_{b}^{i}(y)|0\rangle=\frac{1}{192}\langle\bar{q}Gq\rangle(\sigma_{\alpha\beta})_{ba}t_{ji}^{A},

(36)

For the same reason as that of
\bar{q}q
condensate, in Fig.
4
only the lower two quark lines can be disconnected, otherwise the double imaginary part vanishes.

Finally, using the obtained results for the correlation functions at the QCD level and extracting the coefficients
C_{i}^{1},C_{i}^{5}
corresponding to the
\not{p}_{2}\not{p}_{1}\gamma_{5}
and
\not{p}_{2}\not{p}_{1}
structures, the form factors can be determined through Eqs. (
18
-
25
).

Numerical results

In this section, we firstly give the numerical results for the form factors defined in Eq. (
4
).
In this work, we use the
\overline{\rm MS}
masses for the quarks,
m_{c}(\mu)=1.27
GeV and

m_{s}(\mu)=0.103
GeV with
\mu=1.27
GeV
[
32
]
. The masses and decay constants of
\Omega_{c}
and
\Omega
are taken as
[
32
,
33
]

\displaystyle m_{\Omega_{c}}

\displaystyle=2.695~{\rm GeV},~~~~~f_{\Omega_{c}}=0.093~{\rm GeV}^{3}

\displaystyle m_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=1.672~{\rm GeV},~~~~~f_{\Omega}=0.073~{\rm GeV}^{3}.

(37)

The condensate parameters are taken as
[
34
,
35
]
:
\langle\bar{q}q\rangle=-(0.24\pm 0.01\mathrm{~}{\rm GeV})^{3},\left\langle\bar{q}Gq\right\rangle=m_{0}^{2}\langle\bar{q}q\rangle\ {\rm with}\ m_{0}^{2}=(0.8\pm 0.2)\mathrm{~}{\rm GeV}^{2}
, and
\langle GG\rangle=(4\pi^{2})(0.012\pm 0.004)~\mathrm{GeV}^{4}
.

First, we need to determine the threshold parameters
s_{1\rm th}
and
s_{2\rm th}
. Empirically, they are often set to be
0.5\,\text{GeV}
higher than the ground state masses. However, the masses of the first excited states of
\Omega_{c}
and
\Omega
are around
3.0\,\text{GeV}
and
2.0\,\text{GeV}
, respectively, which are only
0.4\,\text{GeV}
above the ground states
[
32
]
. Therefore, this empirical approach is not applicable in this case. In this work, we set
s_{1\rm th}
and
s_{2\rm th}
at the midpoint between the ground state masses and the first excited state masses, specifically at approximately
0.2\,\text{GeV}
above the ground states, and assign an uncertainty range of
\pm 0.1\,\text{GeV}
around this value, namely

\displaystyle(m_{\Omega_{c}}+0.1\text{GeV})^{2}<

\displaystyle s_{1\rm th}<(m_{\Omega_{c}}+0.3\text{GeV})^{2},

\displaystyle(m_{\Omega}+0.1\text{GeV})^{2}<

\displaystyle s_{2\rm th}<(m_{\Omega}+0.3\text{GeV})^{2}.

(38)

After determining the threshold parameters
s_{1,2\rm th}
, one has to select the range for the Borel parameters
T_{1,2}
. To minimize the errors from quark-hadron duality, it is essential to suppress the contributions from the continuum spectrum as much as possible. Therefore, the Borel parameters cannot be too large. We introduce the following criterion to constrain the maximum range of the Borel parameters: the pole contribution must be greater than the contribution from the continuum spectrum. According to Eq. (
17
), this criterion can be expressed as:

\displaystyle{\rm Pole/Conti}=\frac{\int_{0}^{s_{1\rm th}}ds_{1}\int_{0}^{s_{2\rm th}}ds_{2}\ e^{-s_{1}^{2}/T_{1}^{2}}e^{-s_{2}^{2}/T_{2}^{2}}\left\{\tilde{f}_{i},\tilde{g}_{i}\right\}}{\int_{s_{1\rm th}}^{\infty}ds_{1}\int_{s_{2\rm th}}^{\infty}ds_{2}\ e^{-s_{1}^{2}/T_{1}^{2}}e^{-s_{2}^{2}/T_{2}^{2}}\left\{\tilde{f}_{i},\tilde{g}_{i}\right\}}>1.

(39)

To simplify the problem, the following relation is used to constrain the two Borel parameters corresponding to the
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
channels
[
36
]
:

\displaystyle\frac{T_{1}^{2}}{T_{2}^{2}}=\frac{m_{\Omega_{c}}^{2}-m_{c}^{2}}{m_{\Omega}^{2}-m_{s}^{2}},

(40)

and we redefine
T^{2}=T_{1}^{2}
. Here, without loss of generality, we select
f_{1}
as an example and choose
q^{2}
in the deep Euclidean region, specifically
q^{2}=-8\,\text{GeV}^{2}
, to ensure the convergence of the OPE. The
{\rm Pole/Conti}
ratio as a function of
T^{2}
is plotted separately for each condensate contribution in the left panel of Fig.
5
.

Figure 5:
The
{\rm Pole/Conti}
ratio as a function of
T^{2}
for each condensate contribution at

(left); Variation curves of each condensate contribution with respect to

(right).

It can be observed that for each condensate contribution, the
{\rm Pole/Conti}
ratio decreases as
T^{2}
increases. Based on the criterion
{\rm Pole/Conti}>1
and the behavior of the curves for different condensate contributions in the figure, we can determine that the upper limit of
T^{2}
lies within the range of
4\ \text{GeV}^{2}<T_{\rm upper}^{2}<6\text{GeV}^{2}
.

On the other hand, the Borel parameter must be sufficiently large to ensure the convergence of the OPE. Therefore, to determine the lower limit of the Borel parameter, it is ideally required that the contributions from higher-dimensional condensates are smaller than those from lower-dimensional condensates, that is

\displaystyle C_{i}^{k,{\rm Pert}}>C_{i}^{k,{\bar{q}q}}>C_{i}^{k,{GG}}>C_{i}^{k,{\bar{q}Gq}}.

(41)

Taking
f_{1}
as an example, the right panel of Fig.
5
shows the variation curves of each condensate contribution with respect to
T^{2}
. However, the figure does not fully exhibit the hierarchical relationship of the condensate contributions as described by Eq. (
41
). As can be seen in the figure, the contributions from the
\bar{q}q
and
\bar{q}Gq
condensates are larger than those from the perturbative diagram and the
GG
condensate. The reason for this is that, compared to the perturbative diagram and the
GG
condensate, the
\bar{q}q
and
\bar{q}Gq
condensate diagrams lack one quark propagator, resulting in the absence of a phase space suppression factor
\int d^{4}k/(2\pi)^{4}
. This enhances their contributions. On the other hand, it can be seen from the figure that the contribution from the
\bar{q}q
condensate is larger than those from the
\bar{q}Gq
and
GG
condensates, and the contribution from the perturbative diagram is also larger than that from the
GG
condensate. This is consistent with the hierarchical relationship given in Eq. (
41
).

From this analysis, it is evident that the lower limit of
T^{2}
cannot be strictly determined using Eq. (
41
). Instead, we adopt the stability of the form factor under variations of
T^{2}
as an auxiliary criterion for selecting the appropriate range of
T^{2}
. As illustrated in Fig.
5
, the calculation results exhibit increasing stability as
T^{2}
increases. While the stability criterion alone would favor larger values of
T^{2}
, the upper limit for
T^{2}
is constrained by the first criterion in Eq. (
39
). Consequently, we define the final interval for
T^{2}
as
4\ \text{GeV}^{2}<T^{2}<6\ \text{GeV}^{2}
, balancing both stability and the upper bound provided by the
{\rm Pole/Conti}
ratio.

and

q^{2}

\displaystyle F(q^{2})=\frac{1}{1-\frac{q^{2}}{m_{D_{s}}^{2}}}\left[F_{0}+A_{1}\left(z(q^{2})-z(0)\right)\right],

(42)

\displaystyle z(t)=\frac{\sqrt{t_{+}-t}-\sqrt{t_{+}-t_{0}}}{\sqrt{t_{+}-t}+\sqrt{t_{+}-t_{0}}},

(43)

These uncertainties are particularly pronounced in the calculation of the
R

Future improvements in the precision of the form factors, either through refined theoretical calculations or additional experimental constraints, will be essential to reduce these uncertainties and provide more reliable predictions.

VI
Summary

In this work, we investigate the weak decays of the charmed baryon
\Omega_{c}^{0}
to the
\Omega^{-}
baryon using QCDSR. By defining a three-point correlation function, we calculate the eight form factors governing the
\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Omega^{-}
transition. The cutting rules are employed to extract the double imaginary parts of the correlation functions, enabling their expression as dispersive integrals. These form factors are used to determine the branching fractions for the hadronic decays
\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Omega^{-}\pi^{+}
and
\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Omega^{-}\rho^{+}
, as well as the semi-leptonic decay
\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Omega^{-}\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell}
.
Our results show that the branching fraction of
\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Omega^{-}\pi^{+}
is smaller than most theoretical predictions but consistent with the covariant confined quark model
[
13
]
. In contrast, the branching fractions for
\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Omega^{-}\rho^{+}
and
\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Omega^{-}\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell}
agree well with previous studies. The relatively small branching fraction of
\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Omega^{-}\pi^{+}
leads to a larger
R
value compared to experimental measurements, highlighting the sensitivity of this ratio to uncertainties in the form factors. These uncertainties, arising from variations in the threshold and Borel parameters, are particularly significant in the calculation of the
R
values.
This study provides a comprehensive analysis of
\Omega_{c}^{0}
decays, offering insights into the dynamics of charmed baryon transitions. The results emphasize the need for precise form factor calculations and suggest directions for future theoretical and experimental improvements.

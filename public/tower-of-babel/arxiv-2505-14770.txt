Interactions of a Continuous-Spin Field with a Spin-1/2 Particle
Shayarneel Kundu, Alessandro Russo, Philip Schuster, and Natalia Toro

Abstract
We introduce a formalism for coupling a bosonic Continuous-Spin field to familiar spin-1/2 matter. To do this, we describe the matter using the supersymmetric worldline formalism. We construct currents that are local functions of worldline kinematics, and respect both the worldline supersymmetry and the conservation condition required for consistent couplings to Abelian CSP fields. As the spin Casimir ρ \rho of the CSP vanishes, the interactions reduce to that of familiar QED in one case, and to a Yukawa interaction with a spin-1/2 fermion in another case. Our formalism is applicable to computing deviations from QED if the photon is a CSP, thereby enabling a range of phenomenological studies.

Introduction

The most general type of Lorentz covariant massless particle in 3+1 dimensions is the so-called “continuous-spin” particle (CSP), characterized by a non-zero spin Casimir
W^{2}=-\rho^{2}
, where
\rho
is a continuous parameter with units of momentum. For a review on CSPs see
Wigner (1939)
. Particles of this type contain an infinite tower of integer-spaced helicity modes that transform into one another under Lorentz boosts. Only when
\rho=0
are the helicity modes invariant under boosts, recovering the familiar massless states found in theories such as QED. As demonstrated in
Schuster and Toro (2013a)
;
Schuster and Toro (2015a)
;
Schuster et al. (2023)
;
Schuster and Toro (2024)

\gg\rho

\rho

In this paper, we consider the interactions of a spin-1/2 matter particle with a bosonic CSP field. Building on the work in
Schuster and Toro (2024)
;
Schuster et al. (2023)

Schuster and Toro (2024)

Kundu et al. (2025)
for gravity at leading order. However, only spinless matter has been considered to-date. This work generalizes the approach of
Schuster et al. (2023)

2
Spin-1/2 Particles in the Worldline Formalism

2.1
Brief Review

The dynamics of a relativistic particle can be studied in terms of 0+1 dimensional worldline field theory. To describe a massless fermion in this formalism, we follow the approach of
Brink et al. (1976)
;
Brink et al. (1977)
, where the worldline coordinate
z_{\mu}(\tau)
, which is Grassmann-even (commuting), is supplemented by a spin degree of freedom
\psi_{\mu}(\tau)
, which is Grassmann-odd (anti-commuting). Here,
\tau
is the parameter along the worldline.

Following
Brink et al. (1976)
;
Brink et al. (1977)
, we consider the action for a massless fermion,

S=\frac{1}{2}\int\differential{\tau}\left[\frac{\dot{z}^{2}}{e}-i\psi\cdot\dot{\psi}-i\chi\left(\frac{\dot{z}\cdot\psi}{e}\right)\right],

(1)

with dots denoting derivatives with respect to
\tau
. The new non-dynamical fields,
e
(einbein) and
\chi
(einbino), are Grassmann-even and Grassmann-odd respectively. Their equations of motion yield first-class constraints,

\frac{1}{e^{2}}\bigg(\dot{z}^{2}-i\chi\left(\dot{z}\cdot\psi\right)\bigg)=0,\qquad\qquad\frac{\dot{z}\cdot\psi}{e}=0.

(2)

In terms of the canonical momenta for
z_{\mu}
and
\psi_{\mu}
,

p_{\mu}=\frac{\dot{z}_{\mu}}{e}-\frac{i\chi\psi_{\mu}}{2e},\qquad\Pi_{\mu}=\frac{i\psi_{\mu}}{2},

(3)

these constraints are simply

p^{2}=0,\qquad p\cdot\psi=0.

(4)

Upon quantization of the theory, these constraints will become equations that the physical states must satisfy, namely the Klein-Gordon equation (mass-shell condition) and the Dirac equation.

To quantize the theory, following Dirac’s method, we fix gauge first. A convenient gauge choice is
e=
constant and
\chi=0
(we will refer to this as the "flat superspace" gauge). Upon promoting
z_{\mu}
,
\psi_{\mu}
,
p_{\mu}
, and
\Pi_{\mu}
to operators, Dirac’s quantization then yields the canonical (anti)commutation relations,

[z_{\mu},p_{\nu}]=i\eta_{\mu\nu}\qquad\{\psi_{\mu},\Pi_{\nu}\}=i\eta_{\mu\nu}\implies\{\psi_{\mu},\psi_{\nu}\}=2\eta_{\mu\nu}.

(5)

The anti-commutation relation for
\psi_{\mu}
is the Dirac Clifford algebra, and so we can expect that the
\psi^{\mu}
will act on physical states just as the Dirac matrices
\gamma^{\mu}
act, and that the physical states will transform in spinor representations. In practice, we can identify
\psi_{\mu}=i\gamma_{5}\gamma_{\mu}

1
1

1

This differs from the conventions in
Brink et al. (1977)
, which uses
\psi_{\mu}=\gamma_{5}\gamma_{\mu}
for massive fermions but
\psi_{\mu}=\gamma_{\mu}
for massless fermions. The
\gamma_{5}
factors out and does not affect the results.
.

2.2
Worldline Supersymmetry and Superfields

As discussed in
Brink et al. (1976)
;
Brink et al. (1977)
;
Berezin and Marinov (1977)
, the theory above possesses a local
\mathcal{N}=1
supersymmetry (SUSY) on the worldline coordinates, where the superpartners are
z_{\mu}
and
\psi_{\mu}
, and
e
and
\chi
. Concretely, the free action (
1
) is invariant under the transformations

\displaystyle\delta z^{\mu}

\displaystyle=a\dot{z}^{\mu}+i\alpha\psi^{\mu},\qquad\delta\psi^{\mu}=a\dot{\psi}^{\mu}+\frac{\alpha}{e}\left(\dot{z}^{\mu}+\chi\frac{i\psi^{\mu}}{2}\right),

(6)

\displaystyle\delta e

\displaystyle=a\dot{e}+\dot{a}e+i\alpha\chi,\qquad\delta\chi=a\dot{\chi}+\dot{a}\chi+2\dot{\alpha}.

(7)

Here,
a(\tau)
and
\alpha(\tau)
are respectively, the Grassmann-even and Grassmann-odd parameters of generalized translations and supertranslations. These transformations obey the SUSY algebra appropriate to 0+1 dimensions. To ensure that supersymmetry is maintained in the presence of interactions, it is useful to introduce a superspace where the worldline parameter
\tau
is augmented by a Grassmann-odd coordinate
\theta
and the fields appearing in (
1
) can be grouped into Grassmann-even superfields

X^{\mu}(\tau,\theta)=z^{\mu}+i\theta\sqrt{e}\,\psi^{\mu},\qquad\sqrt{\Lambda(\tau,\theta)}=\sqrt{e}+\frac{i\theta\chi}{2}\,.

(8)

The local SUSY and reparametrization transformations (
6
)–(
7
) can be recast as general translations of the superspace coordinates
\tau
, and
\theta
:

\displaystyle\delta(X^{\mu})=\left(b\partial_{\tau}+\beta\partial_{\theta}\right)X^{\mu},

(9)

\displaystyle\delta(\sqrt{\Lambda})

\displaystyle=\partial_{\tau}(b\sqrt{\Lambda})+\partial_{\theta}(\beta\sqrt{\Lambda}),

(10)

where

\quad b=a+i\theta\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{e}},\quad\text{and}\quad\beta=\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{e}}+i\theta\frac{\dot{a}}{2}.

(11)

We refer to any field that transforms as in (
9
) as a weight-0 superfield, and fields that respect the transformation in (
10
) as a weight-1 superfield. With these definitions, when superfields are multiplied, their weights add. Thus any function of weight-0 superfields is itself a weight-0 superfield, while its product with a weight-1 superfield transforms as a weight-1 superfield. This property, which we will refer to as the multiplication rule, greatly facilitates building SUSY-invariant actions.

Under supertranslations, weight-1 superfields transform as total derivatives, so their integrals over superspace are invariant under supertranslations. In particular, the measure
d\tau d\theta\sqrt{\Lambda}
is invariant, and superspace actions of the form

S=\int d\tau d\theta\sqrt{\Lambda}\mathcal{L}_{0}(\tau,\theta),

(12)

are both SUSY- and reparametrization-invariant whenever the superspace Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}_{0}(\tau,\theta)
is constructed entirely from weight-0 superfields.

To build actions with non-trivial dynamics in this way, we must also identify covariant derivatives whose action on a weight-0 superfield returns a weight-0 superfield. The covariant derivatives satisfying this requirement are

\mathcal{D}_{\theta}\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Lambda}}\left(\partial_{\theta}+i\theta\partial_{\tau}\right),\qquad\mathcal{D}_{\tau}\equiv-i\mathcal{D}_{\theta}^{2}.

(13)

Note that
\mathcal{D}_{\theta}
and
\mathcal{D}_{\tau}
yield superfields of opposite Grassmann parity, that these are the
only
two operators linear in derivatives that satisfy the covariance requirerment, and that in the “flat superspace” gauge
\mathcal{D}_{\tau}
reduces to the familiar time-derivative
\partial_{\tau}/e
. Other spin-1/2 worldline literature, e.g.
Brink et al. (1976)
;
Brink et al. (1977)
;
Mondragon et al. (1994)
, uses derivative operators that differ by powers of
\Lambda
and/or additive corrections, and are not in fact covariant in the sense used above.

The free massless fermion action (
1
) can be compactly written in superspace as

S_{\text{kin}}=-\frac{i}{2}\int\differential{\tau}\differential{\theta}\sqrt{\Lambda}\big(\mathcal{D}_{\tau}X^{\mu}\mathcal{D}_{\theta}X_{\mu}\big).

(14)

2.3
Interactions with Background Fields

Vector Interaction:
In the supersymmetric worldline formalism, the interaction term for spin-
1/2
matter with an abelian vector background field
A_{\mu}
is

S_{\text{int}}^{\text{V}}=-\frac{ig_{\text{V}}}{2}\int\differential{\tau}\differential{\theta}\sqrt{\Lambda}\,\mathcal{D}_{\theta}X_{\mu}A^{\mu}(X).

(15)

The coupling (
15
) implies a space-time current

J^{\text{V}}_{\mu}(x)=-\frac{ig_{\text{V}}}{2}\int\differential{\tau}\differential{\theta}\sqrt{\Lambda}\mathcal{D}_{\theta}X_{\mu}(\tau,\theta)\delta^{(4)}\left(x-X(\tau,\theta)\right),

(16)

which has support on a delta function integrated over the worldline in superspace. This current obeys a conservation law
\partial^{\mu}J^{\text{V}}_{\mu}(x)=0
, which one can see by using the delta function to switch the spacetime derivative to a derivative in terms of the superfield, and then integrating by parts.

Note that
A_{\mu}(X)
is equivalent by Taylor expansion to

A_{\mu}(X)=A_{\mu}\left(z+i\theta\sqrt{e}\psi\right)=A_{\mu}(z)+i\theta\sqrt{e}\psi^{\sigma}\partial_{\sigma}A_{\mu}(z),

(17)

which is exact because
\theta^{2}=0
.
The interaction term can then be expressed in terms of the component fields as

S_{\text{int}}^{\text{V}}=\frac{g_{\text{V}}}{2}\int\differential{\tau}\bigg(\dot{z}^{\mu}A_{\mu}(z)-ie\psi^{\mu}\psi^{\sigma}\partial_{\sigma}A_{\mu}(z)\bigg).

(18)

This matches the interaction term in
Brink et al. (1977)
, when we use our "flat superspace" gauge, and further specify to
e=1
. In the quantum theory, the interaction (
18
) will describe a spin-1/2 particle interacting with an ordinary background vector gauge field.

Scalar:
The interaction of a spin-1/2 particle with a background scalar field is a little more subtle than for a vector gauge field, and is closely related to massive fermions, as first discussed in
Mondragon et al. (1994)
. Translating their discussion to the notation used above, we introduce an additional weight-0 superfield,

\widetilde{X}=\psi_{5}+\frac{i\theta z_{5}}{\sqrt{e}}.

(19)

The component fields
\psi_{5}
, and
z_{5}
are Grassmann-odd and Grassmann-even respectively, thereby making
\widetilde{X}
a Grassmann-odd superfield. The kinetic term corresponding to this superfield is,

S^{\widetilde{X}}_{\text{kin}}=-\frac{1}{2}\int\differential{\tau}\differential{\theta}\sqrt{\Lambda}\widetilde{X}\mathcal{D}_{\theta}\widetilde{X}=\frac{1}{2}\int\differential{\tau}\bigg(\frac{z_{5}^{2}}{e}+i\psi_{5}\dot{\psi}_{5}\bigg).

(20)

With this additional superfield, the interaction term can be written as

S^{\text{S}}_{\text{int}}=g_{\text{S}}\int\differential{\tau}\differential{\theta}\sqrt{\Lambda}\widetilde{X}\,\Phi(X).

(21)

Like the vector case, the background scalar field is evaluated on the superfield, and this follows from the current,

J^{\text{S}}(x)=g_{\text{S}}\int\differential{\tau}\differential{\theta}\sqrt{\Lambda}\widetilde{X}(\tau,\theta)\delta^{(4)}\left(x-X\right).

(22)

Unlike the vector case, there is no conservation condition on this current, since the scalar background has no gauge symmetry. In terms of component fields, the interaction term is

S_{\text{int}}^{\text{S}}=g_{\text{S}}\int\differential{\tau}\left(iz_{5}\Phi(z)-ie\psi_{5}\psi_{\sigma}\partial^{\sigma}\Phi(z)+i\frac{\chi}{2}\psi_{5}\Phi(z)\right).

(23)

After integrating out
z_{5}
, this matches the interaction in
Mondragon et al. (1994)
with
m=0
and up to redefinitions of coupling constants. A free action for massive fermions is obtained simply by taking a uniform scalar background
\Phi\rightarrow m/g_{\text{s}}
in (
21
). The interactions with additional scalar or vector fields remain unchanged.

3
CSP Field Interaction with Spin
1/2
Particles

Having introduced the supersymmetric worldline formalism for spin-1/2 particles and their interactions with familiar scalar and vector fields, we now turn to the interactions of spin-1/2 particles with CSPs. We begin this section with a quick review of the
\eta
-space gauge theory of bosonic CSP fields, as developed in
Schuster and Toro (2015a)
;
Schuster et al. (2023)
. In the
\eta
-space formalism, matter currents must satisfy a continuity condition so that interactions does not excite unphysical modes of the CSP field. In addition, for spin-1/2 matter the current must be invariant under the local worldline supersymmetry described above. With these ingredients, we present currents sourced by spin-1/2 matter that generalize the “temporal” current of
Schuster et al. (2023)
for spinless matter.

3.1
CSP Field and
\eta
-space

In the
\eta
-space formalism introduced in
Schuster et al. (2023)
, CSP fields are functions of both a spacetime coordinate
x^{\mu}
and an additional 4-vector coordinate
\eta^{\mu}
. CSP fields
\Psi(\eta,x)
are dynamical on the coordinate space
x
, while the
\eta
coordinate encodes a tower of symmetric tensors. Roughly, this can be understood by expanding
\Psi
as,
\Psi(\eta,x)=\psi_{0}(x)+\psi_{1}^{\mu}(x)\eta_{\mu}+\psi_{2}^{\mu\nu}(x)\eta_{\mu}\eta_{\nu}+...
, so that the coefficient functions appearing at each order in
\eta
are symmetric tensors of increasing rank. In this way,
\Psi
has enough field content to describe the tower of integer helicity modes of a CSP. Integrations over the auxiliary variable
\eta
are formally defined via analytic continuation, as introduced in
Schuster and Toro (2015a)
. For a review of this formalism, the reader is referred to
Schuster et al. (2023)
. The general linear inteaction of a CSP field in this formalism takes the form

S^{\text{CSP}}_{\text{int}}=\int\differential[4]{x}[\differential[4]{\e}]\delta^{{}^{\prime}}(\eta^{2}+1)J(\eta,x)\Psi(\eta,x),

(24)

for some current
J(\eta,x)
. Compatibility with the gauge symmetry of the
\Psi
field implies that the current must satisfy the conservation (continuity) condition

\delta(\eta^{2}+1)\Big(\partial_{x}\cdot\partial_{\eta}+\rho\Big)J(\eta,x)=0,

(25)

where
\partial_{x}
and
\partial_{\eta}
are space-time and
\eta
-space derivatives respectively. We note that if we expand
J(\eta,x)=J_{0}+J_{1}^{\mu}\eta_{\mu}+\dots
and take the
\rho\to 0
limit, the continuity condition (
25
) is trivially satisfied by any scalar current
J_{0}
, and requires the usual conservation condition
\partial_{x}\cdot J_{1}=0
on the vector current. It can also be shown that inserting these monomial currents into (
24
) and evaluating the
\eta
-integral simply couples
J_{0}
to the scalar component of
\Psi
and
J_{1}^{\mu}
to the vector component of
\Psi
(the generalization of the above statements to ranks 2 and higher requires additional trace terms relative to the expansions above, as discussed in
Schuster et al. (2023)
).

To describe a consistent coupling of spin-1/2 particles to CSP fields, without exciting unphysical modes of either theory, a current must
both
respect the continuity condition (
25
) and be invariant under local SUSY transformations. The latter condition can be assured by building currents as superspace integrals of a function of weight-0 superfields and their covariant derivatives. Our aim here is to explicitly construct such currents.

Before proceeding for general
\rho
, it is useful to recast the scalar and vector interaction terms (
15
) and (
21
), which describe interactions with
\rho=0
fields, in
\eta
-space.
The scalar current is given simply by the
\eta
-independent current

J^{\text{S}}(\eta,x)=J^{\text{S}}(x)=g_{\text{S}}\int\differential{\tau}\differential{\theta}\sqrt{\Lambda}\widetilde{X}\delta^{(4)}\left(x-X\right)

(26)

from (
31
), and the vector current is

J^{\text{V}}(\eta,x)=\sqrt{2}\eta^{\mu}J^{\text{V}}_{\mu}(x)=-\frac{ig_{\text{V}}}{\sqrt{2}}\int\differential{\tau}\differential{\theta}\sqrt{\Lambda}\left(\eta\cdot\mathcal{D}_{\tau}X\right)\delta^{(4)}\left(x-X\right).

(27)

Each of these manifestly satisfies the continuity condition (again, up to worldline boundary terms in the vector case) and is worldline-SUSY-invariant as required.

3.2
CSP Interaction Currents

The currents (
26
) and (
27
) are local over the worldline (i.e. they take the form of a single integral
\int d\tau j(\tau,x)
, where the "current element"
j(\tau,x)
depends on worldline kinematic variables at
\tau
but not at other times). In addition, their current element at
\tau
is localized in space-time to the point
z^{\mu}(\tau)
. By contrast, in
Schuster et al. (2023)
it was found that currents for a spinless worldline that satisfy the CSP continuity condition (
25
) are local over the worldline, which is crucial for quantizing the interacting theory perturbatively. They
are not
, however, point-localized in space-time
2
2

2

The ”temporal currents” of
Schuster et al. (2023)
and the spin-1/2 currents we consider here are, instead, localized to a timelike ray. We refer the reader to Sec. IV of
Schuster et al. (2023)
for discussion of this spacetime localization and its compatibility with causal evolution.
.
Such currents are most simply expressed in momentum-space as

J(\eta^{\mu},x^{\nu})=\int\frac{\differential[4]{k}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\differential\tau e^{-ik\cdot(x-z)}j(\eta^{\mu},k^{\nu},\tau).

(28)

In superspace for a spin-1/2 worldline these generalize to

J(\eta^{\mu},x^{\nu})=\int\frac{\differential[4]{k}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\differential{\tau}\differential{\theta}\sqrt{\Lambda}e^{-ik\cdot(x-X)}j(\eta^{\mu},k^{\nu},\tau,\theta).

(29)

Here SUSY-invariance is guaranteed if the current element
j
is a weight-0 superfield, while the spacetime continuity condition (
25
) is satisfied provided

\Big(-ik\cdot\partial_{\eta}+\rho\Big)j(\eta^{\mu},k^{\nu},\tau,\theta)=0.

(30)

Currents can be found by considering an appropriate series expansion in
\eta
and using (
30
) to extract a recursion relation. Here, inspired by the temporal currents of
Schuster et al. (2023)
, which are power series in
\frac{\eta\cdot\dot{z}}{k\cdot z}
, we focus here on currents built out of positive powers of
\tilde{X}
,
\mathcal{D}_{\tau}X
, and
\frac{\eta\cdot\mathcal{D}_{\tau}X}{k\cdot\mathcal{D}_{\tau}X}
that satisfy the continuity condition and reduce in the
\rho\to 0
limit to the minimal scalar current (
26
) or the minimal vector current (
27
).

Coupling to a Scalar-Like CSP:
Following the recursive approach described above, we obtain the scalar-like current element

j^{\text{S}}(\eta^{\mu},k^{\nu},\tau,\theta)=\widetilde{X}\exp\left(-i\rho\frac{\eta\cdot\mathcal{D}_{\tau}X}{k\cdot\mathcal{D}_{\tau}X}\right).

(31)

This trivially satisfies the continuity condition and, in the
\rho\to 0
limit, recovers the scalar current element
j=\widetilde{X}
in agreement with (
26
).
In the "flat superspace" gauge, the current can be written in terms of component fields as

J^{\text{S}}(\eta^{\mu},x^{\nu})=g_{\text{S}}^{\text{CSP}}\int d\tau\,\frac{d^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{4}}e^{-ik(x-z)}e^{-i\rho\frac{\eta\cdot\dot{z}}{k\cdot\dot{z}}}\left[e\psi_{5}\left(k\cdot\psi-\rho\left(\eta\cdot\dot{\psi}-\frac{(\eta\cdot\dot{z})(k\cdot\dot{\psi})}{(k\cdot\dot{z})}\right)\right)+i\dot{z}_{5}\right].

(32)

Here we have used
29
, and written the current in spacetime, as an inverse Fourier transform.

Coupling to a Vector-Like CSP:
Using the same phase factor structure as before (
31
), but now demanding that we recover (
27
) in the
\rho\to 0
limit, we find the current

j^{\text{V}}=\sqrt{2}\exp\left(-i\rho\frac{\eta\cdot\mathcal{D}_{\tau}X}{k\cdot\mathcal{D}_{\tau}X}\right)\left[\left(\eta\cdot\mathcal{D}_{\theta}X\right)+\frac{\left(k\cdot\mathcal{D}_{\theta}X\right)}{i\rho}\left(-i\rho\frac{\eta\cdot\mathcal{D}_{\tau}X}{k\cdot\mathcal{D}_{\tau}X}-1\right)\right].

(33)

It is instructive to consider a power series in
\rho
. In the small-
\rho
limit, we find that the term proportional to
1/\rho
is a total derivative, and therefore does not contribute to the action. The leading non-trivial contributions to the series expansion are

j^{\text{V}}(\eta,k,\tau,\theta)=\sqrt{2}\left(\eta\cdot\mathcal{D}_{\theta}X\right)+i\rho\sqrt{2}\left[\frac{\left(\eta\cdot\mathcal{D}_{\tau}X\right)\left(k\cdot\mathcal{D}_{\theta}X\right)\left(\eta\cdot\mathcal{D}_{\tau}X\right)}{\left(k\cdot\mathcal{D}_{\tau}X\right)\left(k\cdot\mathcal{D}_{\tau}X\right)}-\frac{\left(\eta\cdot\mathcal{D}_{\tau}X\right)\left(\eta\cdot\mathcal{D}_{\theta}X\right)}{\left(k\cdot\mathcal{D}_{\tau}X\right)}\right].

(34)

When setting
\rho=0
, we recover
j\to\sqrt{2}\eta\cdot\mathcal{D}_{\theta}X
, which corresponds to the current used for the vector coupling in Section
2.2
. The second term represents the leading correction to the interaction at small but non-zero
\rho
.

The structure of the current here is more complex than in the scalar case of (
31
). Notably, we see terms proportional to
\mathcal{D}_{\theta}X_{\mu}
, which are Grassmann-odd. This reflects the contribution arising from the coupling of the fermionic spin degrees of freedom to the spin structure of the CSP field. In the "flat superspace" gauge, the component form of the space-time current is

\displaystyle J^{\text{V}}(\eta^{\mu},x^{\nu})

4
Conclusion

In this paper, we have generalized the work of
Schuster et al. (2023)
by constructing appropriately conserved currents for spin-1/2 particles coupled to CSP fields.
This is achieved starting from the treatment of spin-1/2 particles using a 0+1 dimensional supersymmetric worldline formalism. We have constructed interaction currents that respect worldline supersymmetry and satisfy the CSP continuity condition in
\eta
-space. These currents are equivalent to familiar QED and Yukawa interactions in the
\rho\rightarrow 0
limit, making them interesting to consider in the context of CSP phenomenology. In particular, our formalism provides a pathway for extending the thermodynamics study of
Schuster et al. (2025)
, to analyze deviations from QED in spin-1/2 matter systems for
\rho\neq 0

Schuster and Toro (2024)
;
Reilly et al. (2025)
, and to explore spin-dependent gravitational wave observables as studied in
Kundu et al. (2025)
.

Future directions include extending our formalism to
\mathcal{N}=2
worldline supersymmetry, which would presumably facilitate the description of a standard
h=1
photon interacting with a CSP field. This would be interesting because the results of
Bellazzini et al. (2024)
encountered obstructions to 3-particle on-shell amplitudes with two CSPs and standard
h=1,2
massless particles. In the formalism of this paper, scattering interactions are described with 3-particle (off-shell) “vertex operators” defined on the matter worldline, and using these, one can compute on-shell amplitudes with four or more external particle, as was done in
Schuster and Toro (2024)
for CSP QED with scalar matter.

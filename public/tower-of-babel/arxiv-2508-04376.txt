Abstract
. We study local spectral properties for subordinated operators arising from C 0 C_{0} -semigroups. Specifically, if 𝒯 = ( T t ) t ≥ 0 \mathcal{T}=(T_{t})_{t\geq 0} is a C 0 C_{0} -semigroup acting boundedly on a complex Banach space and ℋ ν = ∫ 0 ∞ T t ​ 𝑑 ν ​ ( t ) \mathcal{H}_{\nu}=\int_{0}^{\infty}T_{t}\;d\nu(t) is the subordinated operator associated to 𝒯 \mathcal{T} , where ν \nu is a sufficiently regular complex Borel measure supported on [ 0 , ∞ ) [0,\infty) , it is shown that ℋ ν \mathcal{H}_{\nu} does not enjoy the Single Valued Extension Property (SVEP) and has dense glocal spectral subspaces in terms of the spectrum of the generator of 𝒯 \mathcal{T} . Likewise, the adjoint ℋ ν ∗ \mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{\ast} has trivial spectral subspaces and enjoys the Dunford property. As an application, for the classical Cesàro operator 𝒞 \mathcal{C} acting on the Hardy spaces H p H^{p} ( 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty ), it follows that the local spectrum of 𝒞 \mathcal{C} at any non-zero H p H^{p} -function or the spectrum of the restriction of 𝒞 \mathcal{C} to any of its non-trivial closed invariant subspaces coincides with the spectrum of 𝒞 \mathcal{C} . Finally, we characterize the local spectral properties of subordinated operators arising from hyperbolic semigroups of composition operators acting on H p H^{p} ( 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty ), which will depend only on the geometry of the associated Koenigs domain.

1.
Introduction

Our starting point in this work is the remarkable theorem of Kriete and Trutt
[
23
]
which states that the classical Cesàro operator
\mathcal{C}
is
subnormal
in the Hardy space
H^{2}
, as well as its counterpart in the Banach space setting
H^{p}
by Miller, Miller and Smith
[
26
]
for
1<p<\infty
and Persson
[
28
]
for
p=1
, showing that
\mathcal{C}
is
subdecomposable
. More precisely, in
H^{2}
the Cesàro operator
\mathcal{C}
is similar to the restriction of a normal operator to one of its closed invariant subspaces and, analogously, in
H^{p}
it is similar to the restriction of a decomposable operator. The decomposable operators were introduced by Foiaş
[
17
]
in the sixties as a generalization of spectral operators in the sense of Dunford and despite the fact that many spectral operators in Hilbert spaces as unitary operators, self-adjoint operators or more generally, normal operators are decomposable, there are still many questions about their restrictions to closed invariant subspaces unsettled. We refer to the monograph of Laursen and Neumann
[
24
]
for more on the subject.

A classical result of Hardy
[
22
]
yields that the Cesàro operator
\mathcal{C}
, which takes a complex sequence
\textbf{a}=(a_{0},a_{1},a_{2}\dots)
to that with
n
-th entry:

(\mathcal{C}\,\textbf{a})_{n}=\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k},\qquad(n\geq 0),

is bounded in
\ell^{2}
. Upon identifying sequences with Taylor coefﬁcients of power series,
\mathcal{C}
acts
formally
on
f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}
as

(1.1)

\mathcal{C}(f)(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}\right)z^{n},

and turns out to be a bounded operator on the classical Hardy spaces on
H^{p}
for
1\leq p<\infty
(see
[
30
]
and
[
31
]
, for instance).

In this setting, the theory of
C_{0}
-semigroups turned out to be a powerful tool for studying the Cesàro operator, as shown in Siskakis’s paper
[
30
]
or Cowen’s
[
12
]
, which gives an alternative proof that
\mathcal{C}
is subnormal. More recently, in
[
19
]
the authors made use that the adjoint of the Cesàro operator
\mathcal{C}
appears as the resolvent operator of the infinitesimal generator of the semigroup of composition operators
(C_{\varphi_{t}})_{t\geq 0}
acting on
H^{p},
given by

\varphi_{t}(z)=e^{-t}z+(1-e^{-t}),\qquad(z\in\mathbb{D},\ t\geq 0).

That is,

(\mathcal{C}^{*}f)(z)=-\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}f(e^{-t}z+(1-e^{-t}))dt,

for
f\in H^{p}
and
z\in\mathbb{D}.
This representation of
\mathcal{C}^{*}
turns out to be a particular instance of what is called a
subordinated operator
associated to a semigroup. In general, if
\mathcal{T}=(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a
C_{0}
-semigroup of bounded operators acting on a Banach space
X
, and
\nu
is a ‘sufficiently regular’ complex Borel measure supported on
[0,\infty)
, the operator

\mathcal{H}_{\nu}=\int_{0}^{\infty}T_{t}\;d\nu(t)

is said to be a subordinated operator to
\mathcal{T}
(see
[
21
, Remark 3.3.3]
, for instance).

Recently, the authors of
[
18
]
undertook a study of the Local Spectral Properties in
H^{p}
-spaces of composition operators induced by linear fractional maps, characterizing in particular the associated local spectra and the local spectral subspaces, among others (see Section
2
or
[
24
]
for definitions). Our main goal in this work is to study the local spectral properties of subordinated operators associated to
C_{0}
-semigroups. Having in mind that the spectrum of the Cesàro operator
\mathcal{C}
in
H^{p}
is the closed disc

\sigma(\mathcal{C};H^{p})=\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\,\left|z-\frac{2}{p}\right|\leq\frac{2}{p}\right\},

(see
[
30
]
), as a particular instance, we derive the following result for the local spectra and the local spectral subspaces of
\mathcal{C}
:

Theorem
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
\mathcal{C}
be the Cesàro operator acting on the Hardy space
H^{p}.
Then:

(1)

The local spectrum of
\mathcal{C}
at any non-zero
H^{p}
-function coincides with the spectrum of
\mathcal{C}
.

(2)

The local spectral subspace associated to any closed proper subset
F
of
\sigma(\mathcal{C};H^{p})
is trivial, namely, it is the zero subspace.

(3)

If
M
is a non-trivial closed invariant subspace of
\mathcal{C}
, then
\sigma(\mathcal{C}\mid_{M};H^{p})=\sigma(\mathcal{C};H^{p}).

It is worthy to point out the increasing interest in studying (local) spectral properties of the so-called
generalized Cesàro operators

f\in H^{p}\mapsto\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{1}{z}\int_{0}^{z}f(s)g^{\prime}(s)ds,&z\in\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\},\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr f(0)&z=0;\end{array}\right.,

or the non-normalized version, namely
integration operators

f\in H^{p}\mapsto\int_{0}^{z}f(s)g^{\prime}(s)ds,\qquad(z\in\mathbb{D}),

where
g
is a fixed analytic function in the unit disc. While these operators turn out to be bounded on
H^{p}
whenever
g^{\prime}\in BMOA
(see
[
2
]
, for instance), regarding spectral properties or subdecomposability, the most satisfying results hold assuming that
g^{\prime}
is a ‘small’ perturbation of a rational function, and the general questions remain open (see
[
3
,
4
,
25
,
36
]
, for instance).

Subordinated operators in
H^{p}
associated to semigroups (or semiflows) of holomorphic functions can also be understood as generalizations of Cesàro operators. In this sense, the stated theorem for the Cesàro operator will hold equivalently for a wider class of operators that arise as the adjoints of operators of the form

(\mathcal{H}_{\nu_{\rho}}f)(z)=\int_{z}^{1}h^{\prime}(s)\rho(h(s)-h(z))f(s)ds,\qquad(z\in\mathbb{D}\text{ and }f\in H^{p}),

where
d\nu_{\rho}(t)=\rho(t)dt
is an absolutely continuous measure respect to the Lebesgue measure with
\rho:[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
a sufficiently regular density function and
h
is the Koenigs function associated to the semigroup (or semiflow) of holomorphic functions
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
in
\mathbb{D}
(see Subsection
4.3
).

We conclude this introduction with an outline of the rest of the manuscript. In Section
2
we recall some of the preliminaries needed throughout the rest of the text, such as
C_{0}
-semigroups and their subordinated operators, as well as some background on Local Spectral Theory.

In Section
3
we prove Theorem
3.1
, which will allow us to transfer some of the (local) spectral properties of the generator of a
C_{0}
-semigroup to associated subordinated operators. Indeed, if a weak spectral mapping theorem holds for the
C_{0}
-semigroup, such properties can be also transferred to the operators of the semigroup (see Remark
3.2
).

In Section
4
we introduce some of the basic notions of semigroups (or semiflows) of holomorphic functions in
\mathbb{D}
and derive a result that assures the density of the eigenfunctions of infinitesimal generators of hyperbolic semigroups in terms of the geometric properties of the associated Koenigs domain (Proposition
4.3
) based on a recent work of Bracci, Gallardo-Gutiérrez and Yakubovich
[
8
]
. As a consequence, we prove a result that provides local spectral properties for subordinated operators depending only on the geometry of the Koenigs domain associated to the hyperbolic semigroup (Theorem
4.4
). Finally, we exhibit particular instances of subordinated operators for which the result applies (Subsection
4.3
) and discuss further applications to non-hyperbolic semigroups of composition operators.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout the rest of the text,
X
will stand for an infinite-dimensional complex Banach space and
\mathcal{L}(X)
for the algebra of linear bounded operators acting on
X
.

Recall that a one-parameter family
\mathcal{T}=(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}\subset\mathcal{L}(X)
is a
C_{0}
-semigroup
if:

(1)

T_{0}=Id_{X},
the identity operator;

(2)

T_{t+s}=T_{t}\,T_{s}
for every
t,\,s\geq 0
;

(3)

T_{t}\rightarrow Id_{X}
when
t\rightarrow 0
in the strong operator topology.

Every
C_{0}
-semigroup satisfies

(2.1)

\left\lvert\left\lvert T_{t}\right\rvert\right\rvert\leq Me^{\omega t}\qquad\textnormal{for }t\geq 0,

for some
M\geq 0
and
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
. The infimum of those
\omega
such that (
2.1
) holds for some
M\geq 0
is called the
type
of the
C_{0}
-semigroup (see
[
16
, p. 250]
)and can be computed as

\omega_{0}:=\inf\limits_{t>0}\frac{\log(\left\lvert\left\lvert T_{t}\right\rvert\right\rvert)}{t}.

Given a
C_{0}
-semigroup
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
on a Banach space
X
, recall that its generator is the closed and
densely defined linear operator
\Delta
defined by

\Delta x=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{T_{t}x-x}{t}

with domain
\textnormal{Dom}(\Delta)=\{x\in X:\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{T_{t}x-x}{t}\kern 5.0pt\text{exists}\}
. The semigroup is
determined uniquely by its generator.

It is a classical fact that the spectrum of
\Delta
in
X
, namely, those complex numbers
z
such that
\Delta-zI
is not invertible in
X
, satisfies

\sigma(\Delta)\subseteq\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\textnormal{Re}(z)\leq\omega_{0}\}

(see
[
16
, Proposition 2.2]
, for instance).

A word about notation.

For simplicity, the spectrum of an operator
T
on
X
is denoted by
\sigma(T)
unless it is necessary to specify the underlying space, when it is denoted by
\sigma(T;X)
.

For
z\in\mathbb{C}
with
\textnormal{Re}(z)>\omega_{0}
, it is possible to express the resolvent operator

R(z,\Delta)=-(\Delta-zI)^{-1}

in terms of the Laplace transform of
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0},
namely

(2.2)

R(z,\Delta)x=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-zt}\,T_{t}x\,dt\qquad(x\in X),

(see
[
16
, p. 231]
). Likewise, the spectral properties of the resolvent operators
R(z,\Delta)
as well as the operators
T_{t}
in the semigroup can be related with the ones of the infinitesimal generator by means of
spectral mapping theorems
. In particular, if
\sigma_{p}(T)
denotes the point spectrum of an operator
T
, namely, the set consisting of eigenvalues of
T
, the following holds:

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
X
be an infinite-dimensional complex Banach space,
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}\subset\mathcal{L}(X)
a
C_{0}
-semigroup and
\Delta
its infinitesimal generator. Then,

(1)

\{e^{zt}:z\in\sigma(\Delta)\}\subseteq\sigma(T_{t})
for every
t\geq 0
;

(2)

\{e^{zt}:z\in\sigma_{p}(\Delta)\}=\sigma_{p}(T_{t})\setminus\{0\}
for every
t\geq 0
.

Moreover, for every complex number
\lambda
in the resolvent of
\Delta
, it holds that

(3)

\sigma(R(\lambda,\Delta))=\{0\}\cup\{(\lambda-z)^{-1}:z\in\sigma(\Delta)\}
;

(4)

\{(\lambda-z)^{-1}:z\in\sigma_{p}(\Delta)\}\subseteq\sigma_{p}(R(\lambda,\Delta)).

We refer to
[
16
, Chapter IV, Sections 3.6 and 3.7]
and
[
20
, Theorem 6.4 and Corollary 6.6]
, for instance.

The equation (
2.2
) provides particular instances of subordinated operators associated to a semigroup according to the following:

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\nu
be a finite complex Borel measure in
[0,+\infty)
such that
\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{(\omega_{0}+\delta)t}\,d|\nu|(t)<\infty
for some
\delta>0
and

\mathcal{T}=(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}\subset\mathcal{L}(X)
a
C_{0}
-semigroup. The subordinated operator
\mathcal{H}_{\nu}
associated to
\mathcal{T}
is given by

(2.3)

\mathcal{H}_{\nu}x=\int_{0}^{\infty}\,T_{t}x\ d\nu(t),\qquad(x\in X).

Before going further, let us point out that (
2.3
) defines a linear bounded operator in
X
because the integral is Bochner-convergent. Clearly, the absolute continuous measure
d\nu_{\lambda}(t)=e^{-\lambda t}dt
where
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
with
\textnormal{Re}(\lambda)>\omega_{0}
yields that
\mathcal{H}_{\nu_{\lambda}}=R(\lambda,\Delta).

2.1.
Sectorial operators

In order to study the local spectral properties of subordinated operators, we recall some spectral mapping theorems in the lines of Proposition
2.1
. For such a purpose, we introduce a
toolkit
regarding functional calculus for sectorial operators and refer the reader to the monograph
[
21
]
.

Given
\nu
a finite complex Borel measure in
[0,\infty)
of bounded variation, the
Laplace transform
of
\nu
is given by

\mathcal{L}(\nu)(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-zt}\,d\nu(t),

where
z\in\mathbb{C}
with
\textnormal{Re}(z)\geq 0
. It is a classical fact that
\mathcal{L}(\nu)
is a holomorphic function in the right-half plane
\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\textnormal{Re}(z)>0\}
and continuous in its closure.

Given
0\leq\omega\leq\pi
, let
S_{\omega}
be the sector in the complex plane given by

S_{\omega}=\left\{\begin{matrix}&\{z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}:\,\ |\arg z|<\omega\}&\textnormal{if}&\omega\in(0,\pi]\\
&\{z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}:\,\ \Impart(z)=0,\Rpart(z)>0\}&\textnormal{if}&\omega=0.\end{matrix}\right.

Recall that a (possibly unbounded operator)
\Delta
acting on
X
is called
sectorial of angle
\omega\in[0,\pi)
, denoted by
\Delta\in\textnormal{Sect}(\omega)
if it satisfies

(i)

\sigma(\Delta)\subset\overline{S_{\omega}}
and

(ii)

M(A,\omega^{\prime}):=\sup\{\left\lvert\left\lvert\lambda\,R(\lambda,\Delta)\right\rvert\right\rvert:\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{S_{\omega^{\prime}}}\}<\infty
for all
\omega^{\prime}\in(\omega,\pi)
.

It is a standard fact that if
\Delta
is the infinitesimal generator of a uniformly bounded semigroup of operators, that is,

\sup\limits_{t\geq 0}\left\lvert\left\lvert T_{t}\right\rvert\right\rvert<\infty,

then
-\Delta
is a sectorial operator of angle
\pi/2.
In particular, if
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}\subset\mathcal{L}(X)
is a
C_{0}
-semigroup of type
\omega_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\Delta
is its infinitesimal generator, the semigroup

(e^{-(\omega_{0}+\varepsilon)t}\,T_{t})_{t\geq 0}

is a uniformly bounded semigroup for every
\varepsilon>0
, so
(\omega_{0}+\varepsilon)I-\Delta
is sectorial of angle
\pi/2.

Now, we introduce a functional calculus for sectorial operators of angle
\pi/2
. First, we recall the following:

Definition 2.3
.

Let
A
be a sectorial operator of angle
\pi/2
in
X
and
\omega\in(\pi/2,\pi)
. The domain of the functional calculus of
A
, denoted by
\mathcal{E}(A)
, consists of holomorphic functions
f:S_{\omega}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
such that the limits

\lim\limits_{z\rightarrow 0}f(z):=d_{0}\qquad\lim\limits_{z\rightarrow\infty}f(z):=d_{\infty}

are finite and satisfy that for some
r,R>0
, that the integrals

\int_{\partial(S_{\omega^{\prime}}\cap\{|z|<r\})}\left|\frac{f(z)-d_{0}}{z}\right||dz|<\infty,\qquad\int_{\partial(S_{\omega^{\prime}}\cap\{|z|>R\})}\left|\frac{f(z)-d_{\infty}}{z}\right||dz|<\infty

converges for every
\omega^{\prime}\in(\pi/2,\omega).

Here
\partial(S_{\omega^{\prime}}\cap\{|z|<r\})
denotes the boundary of the set
S_{\omega^{\prime}}\cap\{|z|<r\}
.

Given
A
a sectorial operator of angle
\pi/2
in
X
and
f\in\mathcal{E}(A)
, the operator
f(A)
may be defined as

f(A)=d_{\infty}+d_{0}(I+A)^{-1}+\int_{\partial S_{\omega^{\prime}}}(f(z)-d_{\infty}-\frac{d_{0}}{z+1})(zI-A)^{-1}dz,

where
\omega^{\prime}\in(\pi/2,\pi).
Note that this definition does not depend on the selected
\omega^{\prime}
.

In particular, if
\mathcal{T}=(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a
C_{0}
-semigroup with infinitesimal generator
\Delta
of type
w_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, the operator
(\omega_{0}+\varepsilon)I-\Delta
is a sectorial operator of angle
\pi/2
, so the functional calculus defined applies.

In order to simplify the notation, and following that of
[
1
, p. 27]
, we write

f\in\mathcal{E}(-\Delta)
if
f_{\omega_{0}+\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{E}((\omega_{0}+\varepsilon)I-\Delta),
where

f_{\omega_{0}+\varepsilon}(z)=f(z-\omega_{0}-\varepsilon)
. In such a case, we define

f(-\Delta)=f_{\omega_{0}+\varepsilon}((\omega_{0}+\varepsilon)I-\Delta).

With this notation, we can state the desired Spectral Mapping Theorem for our purposes:

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{T}=(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}\subset\mathcal{L}(X)
be a
C_{0}
-semigroup of operators with infinitesimal generator
\Delta
of type
w_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
\nu
be a finite complex Borel measure in
[0,\infty)
such that
\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{(\omega_{0}+\delta)t}\,d|\nu|(t)<\infty
for some
\delta>0.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{\nu}
be the subordinated operator associated to
\mathcal{T}
and assume that
\mathcal{L}(\nu)\in\mathcal{E}(-\Delta).
Then the following assertions hold:

(1)

The spectrum of
\mathcal{H}_{\nu}
is

\sigma(\mathcal{H}_{\nu})=\mathcal{L}(\nu)(\tilde{\sigma}(-\Delta))

where
\tilde{\sigma}(-\Delta)=\sigma(-\Delta)\cup\{\infty\}
.

(2)

The point spectrum of
\mathcal{H}_{\nu}
satisfies

\mathcal{L}(\nu)(\sigma_{p}(-\Delta))\subseteq\sigma_{p}(\mathcal{H}_{\nu})\subseteq\mathcal{L}(\nu)(\sigma_{p}(-\Delta))\cup\mathcal{L}(\nu)(M),

where
M=\{0,\infty\}\cap\tilde{\sigma}(-\Delta).

Moreover,
\ker(-\Delta-\lambda I)\subseteq\ker(\mathcal{H}_{\nu}-\mathcal{L}(\nu)(\lambda)\,I)
for every
\lambda\in\sigma_{p}(-\Delta).

Proof.

An argument along the lines of
[
1
, Corollary 5.3]
yields that
\mathcal{L}(\nu)\in\mathcal{E}(-\Delta).
Moreover,
\mathcal{H}_{\nu}=\mathcal{L}(\nu)(-\Delta)
by
[
21
, Proposition 3.3.2]
, so both statements (1) and (2) in Theorem
2.4
follows by
[
20
, Theorem 6.4 and Corollary 6.6]
.

Finally, let
\lambda\in\sigma_{p}(-\Delta)
and
x\in\ker(-\Delta-\lambda I).
By Proposition
2.1
,
x\in\ker(T_{t}-e^{-\lambda t}).
Since,

\mathcal{H}_{\nu}x=\int_{0}^{\infty}\,T_{t}x\,d\nu(t)=x\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}d\nu(t)=\mathcal{L}(\nu)x,

the last statement of Theorem
2.4
holds and the proof follows.
∎

It is worthy to remark that as a consequence of
[
1
, Lemma 5.2 and Corollary 5.3]
, next proposition yields a relatively easy way to provide measures
\nu
satisfying the hypothesis of the previous result.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\nu
be a finite complex Borel measure on
[0,\infty)
such that
\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{(\omega_{0}+\delta)t}|d\nu|(t)<\infty
for some
\delta>0.
Assume
d\nu(t)=h(t)dt,
where
h
can be extended holomorphically to a sector
S_{\omega}
with
\omega\in(0,\pi/2]
. Suppose further that there exist
\eta\in(0,1]
and
\xi\in(0,1)
such that

\sup_{z\in S_{\xi}\cap\{|z|\leq 1\}}|z^{1-\eta}h(z)|<\infty\qquad\sup_{z\in S_{\xi}\cap\{|z|\geq 1\}}|z^{1+\xi}e^{(\omega_{0}+\delta)z}h(z)|<\infty

for all
0<\varepsilon<\omega.
Then,
\mathcal{L}(\nu)\in\mathcal{E}(-\Delta)
.

We conclude this subsection with a basic fact about adjoints of
C_{0}
-semigroups of operators. For such a task, recall that if
Z
is a closed operator with dense domain
\mathcal{D}
in a Banach space
X
, the
adjoint

Z^{*}
with domain
\mathcal{D}^{*}\subseteq X^{*}
is defined as follows:

\phi\in\mathcal{D}^{*}
if and only if the linear functional
f\mapsto\langle Zf,\phi\rangle
is norm bounded on
\mathcal{D}
;
if
\phi\in\mathcal{D}^{*}
, then
Z^{*}\phi
is the unique bounded extension of that linear functional to
X
.
Hence

\langle Zf,\phi\rangle=\langle f,Z^{*}\phi\rangle

for all
f\in\mathcal{D}
and
\phi\in\mathcal{D}^{*}
(see
[
13
]
).

Theorem 2.6
.

[
13
, Theorem 1.34]

Let
X
be a reflexive Banach space and
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}\subseteq\mathcal{L}(X)
a
C_{0}
-semigroup with infinitesimal generator
\Delta.
Then,
(T_{t}^{*})_{t\geq 0}\subset\mathcal{L}(X^{*})
is also a
C_{0}-
semigroup whose infinitesimal generator is
\Delta^{*}.

2.2.
Local Spectral Theory

In this subsection we will recall some of the basic features of Local Spectral Theory and refer the reader to the monograph by Laursen and Neumann
[
24
]
.

Given
T\in\mathcal{L}(X)
and
x\in X
, the
local resolvent
of
T
at
x
, denoted as
\rho_{T}(x)
, is defined as the union of all open sets
U\subset\mathbb{C}
such that there exists an analytic function
f_{x}:U\rightarrow X
satisfying

(2.4)

(T-zI)f_{x}(z)=x\qquad(z\in U).

In particular,
\rho_{T}(x)
is an open set that contains the resolvent of the operator
\rho(T)
. An operator
T\in\mathcal{L}(X)
is said to have the
single-valued extension property
(SVEP) if for every open set
U
and every analytic function
f_{x}:U\rightarrow X
the equality

(T-zI)f_{x}(z)=0\qquad(z\in U)

implies that
f_{x}\equiv 0.
This property assures that the analytic function solving (
2.4
) is unique, so one can define the
local resolvent function
of
T
at
x
as the unique analytic map
f_{x}:\rho_{T}(x)\rightarrow X
such that (
2.4
) holds.

The
local spectrum
of
T\in\mathcal{L}(X)
at
x\in X
is defined as
\sigma_{T}(x)=\mathbb{C}\setminus\rho_{T}(x).
This set is always a (possibly empty) compact subset of the spectrum
\sigma(T)
. Having in mind the spectral radius formula

r(T)=\limsup\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\lvert\left\lvert T^{n}\right\rvert\right\rvert^{1/n},

the
local spectral radius
of
T
at
x
is defined by

r_{T}(x):=\limsup\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\lvert\left\lvert T^{n}x\right\rvert\right\rvert^{1/n}.

If
T
has the SVEP, a local spectral radius formula satisfies:

(2.5)

r_{T}(x)=\max\{|\lambda|:\lambda\in\sigma_{T}(x)\},\qquad(x\in X\setminus\{0\}),

(see
[
24
, Proposition 3.3.13]
).

The definition of the local spectrum allows us to introduce the local spectral subspaces, or simply, the spectral subspaces associated to
T
.

For any set
A\subset\mathbb{C}
, the
spectral subspace
of
T
associated to
A
, denoted as
X_{T}(A)
, is defined as

X_{T}(A)=\{x\in X:\sigma_{T}(x)\subseteq A\}.

In other words,
X_{T}(A)
consists of those vectors
x\in X
for which
(T-zI)
is locally invertible for
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus A.
We note that
X_{T}(A)=X_{T}(A\cap\sigma(T))
for every
A\subset\mathbb{C}.

The spectral subspaces turn out to be (non-necessarily closed) subspaces which are hyperinvariant by
T
, namely, invariant under every operator in the commutant of
T
. If all the spectral subspaces of an operator
T
associated to closed sets are closed, then
T
is said to enjoy the
Dunford property (C)
. Clearly, the Dunford property implies the SVEP.

We also recall the
glocal spectral subspaces

\mathcal{X}_{T}(F)
, which are a variant of spectral subspaces that in general are better suited for operators lacking the SVEP. If
F\subset\mathbb{C}
is a closed set, the glocal spectral subspace
\mathcal{X}_{T}(F)
consists of all
x\in X
such that there exists an analytic function
f_{x}:\mathbb{C}\setminus F\rightarrow X
satisfying

(T-zI)f_{x}(z)=x,\qquad(z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus F).

If
T
has the SVEP, then
X_{T}(F)=\mathcal{X}_{T}(F)
for every closed set
F\subset\mathbb{C}
. In general, one has the inclusion
\mathcal{X}_{T}(F)\subseteq X_{T}(F)
.

Glocal spectral subspaces are also of interest when dealing with adjoint of operators. Namely, if
X
is a complex Banach space and
T\in\mathcal{L}(X)
, for every disjoint closed sets
F_{1},F_{2}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
, it follows:

(2.6)

\mathcal{X}^{*}_{T^{*}}(F_{1})\subseteq\mathcal{X}_{T}(F_{2})^{\perp},

(see
[
24
, Proposition 2.5.1]
).

3.
Transferring the (local) spectral properties of infinitesimal generators

In this section we prove Theorem
3.1
which will allow us to transfer some of the (local) spectral properties of the infinitesimal generator
\Delta
of a
C_{0}
-semigroup
\mathcal{T}=(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
to subordinated operators
\mathcal{H}_{\nu}
associated to
\mathcal{T}
, as well as for their adjoints.
The result extends the conclusions of Theorem 2.1 in
[
18
]
to the context of a
C_{0}
-semigroups and reads as follows:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
X
be a complex Banach space and
\mathcal{T}=(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}\subset\mathcal{L}(X)
a
C_{0}
-semigroup of type
w_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and infinitesimal generator
\Delta
. Assume that
\overline{\textnormal{int}(\sigma(-\Delta))}=\sigma(-\Delta)
is connected and that there exists a non-vanishing analytic map
f_{\Delta}:\textnormal{int}(\sigma(-\Delta))\rightarrow\textnormal{Dom}(-\Delta)
such that

(-\Delta-zI)f_{\Delta}(z)=0\qquad\text{ for }z\in\textnormal{int}(\sigma(-\Delta)).

Assume further that the linear span

\Span\{f_{\Delta}(\lambda):\lambda\in\textnormal{int}(\sigma(-\Delta))\}

is dense in
X
. Let
\nu
be a finite complex Borel measure supported in
[0,\infty)
such that
\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{(\omega_{0}+\delta)t}d|\nu|(t)<\infty
for some
\delta>0
and
\mathcal{L}(\nu)
is non-constant in
\mathcal{E}(-\Delta)
. Then the subordinated operator
\mathcal{H}_{\nu}
to
\mathcal{T}
satisfies the following properties:

(1)

\mathcal{H}_{\nu}
does not enjoy the SVEP.

(2)

For every relatively open subset non-empty
U\subset\sigma(\mathcal{H}_{\nu}),

\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{H}_{\nu}}(\overline{U})
is dense in
X
.

(3)

\sigma_{p}(\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{*})=\emptyset.

(4)

X_{\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{*}}(F)=\{0\}
for every closed subset
F\subsetneq\sigma(\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{*}).

(5)

\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{*}
has the Dunford property (C).

(6)

\sigma_{\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{*}}(x)=\sigma(\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{*})
for every
x\in X^{*}\setminus\{0\}.

(7)

r_{\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{*}}(x)=r(\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{*})
for every
x\in\mathcal{X}^{*}\setminus\{0\}.

(8)

If
M
is a non-trivial closed invariant subspace for
\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{*}
, then

\sigma(\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{*})\subseteq\sigma(\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{*}\mid_{M})\subset\eta(\sigma(\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{*})),
where
\eta(\sigma(\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{*}))
denotes the full spectrum of
\sigma(\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{*})
, namely, the union of
\sigma(\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{*})
and all the bounded components of the resolvent set
\rho(\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{*}).

Proof.

We begin by noting that the existence of the analytic function
f_{\Delta}
implies that
\textnormal{int}(\sigma(-\Delta))\subseteq\sigma_{p}(-\Delta).

In particular, by Theorem
2.4
, it follows that

\sigma(\mathcal{H}_{\nu})=\mathcal{L}(\nu)(\tilde{\sigma}(-\Delta))\text{ and }\mathcal{L}(\nu)(\sigma_{p}(-\Delta))\subseteq\sigma_{p}(\mathcal{H}_{\nu}).

Let us start with the proof of
(1)
. Since
\mathcal{L}(\nu)
is holomorphic in the translated sector
-(\omega_{0}+\delta)+S_{\omega}
, which contains
\sigma(-\Delta),
the set
\mathcal{L}(\nu)(\textnormal{int}(\sigma(-\Delta)))
is open and

(3.1)

\mathcal{L}(\nu)(\textnormal{int}(\sigma(-\Delta)))\subset\sigma(\mathcal{H}_{\nu}).

By hypothesis, the function
\mathcal{L}(\nu)^{\prime}
is not zero, so there exists an open subset
G\subseteq\textnormal{int}(\sigma(-\Delta))\subseteq\sigma_{p}(-\Delta)
such that

\mathcal{L}(\nu):G\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\nu)(G)

is biholomorphic. The analytic map
f_{\Delta}\circ\mathcal{L}(\nu)^{-1}:\mathcal{L}(\nu)(G)\rightarrow X
satisfies that

(\mathcal{H}_{\nu}-zI)f_{\Delta}\circ\mathcal{L}(\nu)^{-1}(z)=0

for every
z\in\mathcal{L}(\nu)(G)
. Accordingly,
\mathcal{H}_{\nu}
does not enjoy the SVEP and
(1)
follows.

In order to prove
(2)
, we note that for every non-empty open set
\Omega\subset\textnormal{int}(\sigma(-\Delta))
,

\Span\{f_{\Delta}(\lambda):\lambda\in\Omega\}

is dense in
X
since the map
f_{\Delta}:\textnormal{int}(\sigma(-\Delta))\rightarrow\textnormal{Dom}(-\Delta)
is holomorphic. Likewise, the hypotheses on the spectrum
\sigma(-\Delta)
yield that it has no isolated points.
So, if
U\subset\sigma(\mathcal{H}_{\nu})
is a relatively open non-empty set, having in mind that
\sigma(\mathcal{H}_{\nu})=\mathcal{L}(\nu)(\tilde{\sigma}(-\Delta))
,
\textnormal{int}(\sigma(-\Delta))\subset\sigma_{p}(-\Delta)
and
\mathcal{L}(\nu)(\sigma_{p}(-\Delta))\subseteq\sigma_{p}(\mathcal{H}_{\nu})
, there exists an open subset
U_{1}\subseteq U\cap\sigma_{p}(\mathcal{H}_{\nu}).
Now, by Theorem
2.4
,
U_{1}\subseteq U\cap\sigma_{p}(\mathcal{H}_{\nu})=U\cap\mathcal{L}(\nu)(\sigma_{p}(-\Delta))\setminus\mathcal{L}(\nu)(M)
, so there exists a non-empty open subset
U_{2}\subseteq\mathcal{L}(\nu)(\textnormal{int}(\sigma_{p}(-\Delta)))\cap U_{1}
, since
\mathcal{L}(\nu)(M)
consists, at most, of two points.
Accordingly, there exists a non-empty open subset
G\subseteq\textnormal{int}(\sigma_{p}(-\Delta))\subseteq\textnormal{int}(\sigma(-\Delta))
such that
\mathcal{L}(\nu)(G)
is contained in
U_{2}
.

Now,

\Span\{f_{\Delta}(\lambda):\lambda\in G\}

is dense in
X
and observe that

\mathcal{H}_{\nu}f_{\Delta}(\lambda)=\mathcal{L}(\nu)(\lambda)f_{\Delta}(\lambda),\qquad(\lambda\in G);

so
\mathcal{L}(\nu)(\lambda)
is an eigenvalue for
\mathcal{H}_{\nu}
for every
\lambda\in G
. But
\mathcal{L}(\nu)(G)\subset U,
so by
[
24
, Proposition 3.3.1]
and the inclusion properties of glocal spectral subspaces, it yields that
\Span\{f(\lambda):\lambda\in G\}\subseteq\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{H}_{\nu}}(\overline{U})
and
(2)
follows.

In order to show
(3)
, recall that
\sigma(-\Delta)
is not a singleton by hypothesis, so neither is
\sigma(\mathcal{H}_{\nu})
. Fix
\lambda_{0}\in\sigma(\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{*})=\sigma(\mathcal{H}_{\nu}).
By
[
24
, Proposition 3.3.1]
, we have that

\ker(\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{*}-\lambda I)\subset\mathcal{X}^{*}_{\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{*}}(\{\lambda_{0}\}).

Now, let
U\subset\sigma(\mathcal{H}_{\nu})
be a non-empty relatively open set not containing
\lambda_{0}
in its closure, which can be achieved since the spectrum
\sigma(\mathcal{H}_{\nu})
is not a singleton. Then, having in mind (
2.6
), we deduce that

\mathcal{X}^{*}_{\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{*}}(\{\lambda_{0}\})\subseteq\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{H}_{\nu}}(\overline{U})^{\perp}=\{0\}.

Hence,
\lambda_{0}\notin\sigma_{p}(\mathcal{H}_{\nu})
, which concludes the proof. Observe that, in particular,
\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{*}
has the SVEP, and
X^{*}_{\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{*}}(F)=\mathcal{X}^{*}_{\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{*}}(F)
for every closed set
F\subset\mathbb{C}.

Let us prove
(4)
. Let
F
be a proper closed subset of
\sigma(\mathcal{H}^{*}_{\nu}).
Since
F
is proper, there exists a relatively open subset
U\subset\sigma(\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{*})
such that
\overline{U}\cap F=\emptyset.
Then, as in the proof of
(3)
, by (
2.6
) and the density of
\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{H}_{\nu}}(\overline{U})
, we deduce that
X^{*}_{\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{*}}(F)=\{0\}
, as we wished to prove.

As a consequence of
(4)
, the proof of
(5)
follows directly: if
F\subsetneq\sigma(\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{*})
, then
X^{*}_{\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{*}}(F)=\{0\},
and if
F=\sigma(T)
, it is trivial to check that
X^{*}_{\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{*}}(F)=X^{*}.
In particular,
X^{*}_{\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{*}}(F)
is a closed linear manifold for every closed set
F\subset\sigma(\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{*})
, which yields the statement.

In order to prove
(6)
and
(7)
, we observe that statement
(4)
directly implies that
(6)
holds, and such identity together with (
2.5
) yields
(7)
.

To conclude the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we show
(8)
. Let
M\subset X^{*}
be a non-trivial closed invariant subspace for
\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{*}
. Then, by
[
24
, Proposition 1.2.16 (e)]
it follows that
M\subset X_{\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{*}}(\sigma(\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{*}\mid_{M}))
. But
M\neq\{0\}
, so by
(4)
we deduce that
\sigma(\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{*})\subseteq\sigma(\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{*}\mid_{M}).
For the second containment, it is enough to apply
[
29
, Theorem 0.8]
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Under the hypothesis of Theorem
3.1
, if a weak spectral mapping formula of the form

(3.2)

\sigma(T_{t})=\overline{\{e^{\lambda t}:\lambda\in\sigma(\Delta)\}}\qquad(t\geq 0)

holds, then the statements
(1)-(8)
in Theorem
3.1
are also satisfied for each of the operators
T_{t}
in the semigroup
\mathcal{T}
besides the subordinated operator
\mathcal{H}_{\nu}
associated to it.

It is worth mentioning that the equality (
3.2
) does not hold for every
C_{0}
-semigroup acting on a complex Banach space
X
, see for instance
[
16
, Chapter IV, Section 3.3]
. Nevertheless, (
3.2
), even in a stronger version, does hold for a large variety of
C_{0}
-semigroups, such as eventually compact semigroups, analytic semigroups, uniformly continuous semigroups or
C_{0}
-semigroups of normal operators on Hilbert spaces
[
16
, Chapter IV, Corollaries 3.12 and 3.14]
.

4.
Subordinated operators arising from semigroups of composition operators

Our main goal in this section is applying Theorem
3.1
to a large collection of semigroups of composition operators acting on the Hardy spaces
H^{p}
of the unit disc
\mathbb{D}
,
1<p<\infty
, which will allow us to get local spectral properties of the subordinated operators associated to such semigroups as well as of their adjoints. A particular instance is the classical Cesàro operator, mentioned in the introduction. The subordinated operators that emerge turn out to be
averaging operators
introduced by Siskakis in 1993
[
32
]
.

4.1.
Semigroups of analytic functions in
\mathbb{D}

Recall that a family
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
of holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{D}
is said to be a
semigroup (or semiflow)
in
\mathbb{D}
if the map
t\in[0,+\infty)\mapsto\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
is a continuous semigroup homomorphism, where
[0,+\infty)
is endowed with the Euclidean topology and
\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
with the topology of uniform convergence on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}.
In particular, the following properties yield:

(1)

\varphi_{0}(z)=z
for every
z\in\mathbb{D}.

(2)

\varphi_{t}\circ\varphi_{s}=\varphi_{t+s}
for every
s,t\geq 0.

(3)

\lim_{t\to s}\varphi_{t}=\varphi_{s}
uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
for
s,t\geq 0.

In such a case, the function
t\mapsto\varphi_{t}(z)
is differentiable for each
z\in\mathbb{D}
, and there exists a unique function
G:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
such that

\frac{\partial\varphi_{t}(z)}{\partial_{t}}\mid_{t=t_{0}}=G(\varphi_{t_{0}}(z)),\qquad(z\in\mathbb{D},\,t_{0}\geq 0).

This function
G
is the
infinitesimal generator
of the semigroup
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}.

The semigroups in
\mathbb{D}
can be classified with respect to the configuration of the fixed points of the functions of the semigroups. In this respect, a semigroup
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is
elliptic
if there exists
z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}
such that
\varphi_{t_{0}}(z_{0})=z_{0}
for some
t_{0}>0.
Otherwise, we will say that the semigroup is
non
-elliptic.

In this latter case, there exists a unique point
\tau\in\mathbb{T},
called the
Denjoy-Wolff point
such that
\varphi_{t}
converges uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
to the constant function
z\mapsto\tau
when
t\rightarrow+\infty.
Moreover,
\varphi_{t}(z)
has non-tangential limits
e^{t\alpha}
at the Denjoy–Wolff point, with some
\alpha\leq 0.
If
\alpha<0,
the semigroup
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is said to be
hyperbolic
, and it is said to be
parabolic
when
\alpha=0.

For non-elliptic semigroups, there exists a unique representation

(4.1)

\varphi_{t}(z)=h^{-1}(h(z)+t),\qquad(z\in\mathbb{D},\ t\geq 0),

where
h:\mathbb{D}\mapsto\mathbb{C}
is a univalent function with
h(0)=0
.
h
is called the
Koenigs function
of the semigroup
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
and it is unique up to the normalization
h(0)=0
. The infinitesimal generator
G
of the semigroup
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
and the Koenigs function are related by means of

(4.2)

G(z)h^{\prime}(z)=1\qquad(z\in\mathbb{D}).

The range of the Koenigs function
\Omega=h(\mathbb{D})
is the associated
Koenigs domain
of
(\varphi)_{t\geq 0}
, which is
convex in the positive direction
, that is, if
w\in\Omega
then
w+t\in\Omega
for every
t\geq 0.
The geometry of Koenigs domain plays a significant role in understanding the semigroup. In particular, a non-elliptic semigroup
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is hyperbolic if and only if its Koenigs domain
\Omega
is contained in a horizontal strip (we refer to the recent monograph
[
7
]
for this and further results and references).

4.2.
Semigroups of composition operators on
H^{p}

It is a well known result that every semigroup
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
in
\mathbb{D}
induces a
C_{0}
-semigroup of composition operators
(C_{\varphi_{t}})_{t\geq 0}
acting on the Hardy space
H^{p}
of the unit disc when
1\leq p<\infty.
In such a case, the infinitesimal generator
\Delta
of the semigroup
(C_{\varphi_{t}})_{t\geq 0}
is given by

\Delta f=Gf^{\prime},\qquad(f\in\textnormal{Dom}(\Delta)),

where
G
is the infinitesimal generator of
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
and
\textnormal{Dom}(\Delta)=\{f\in H^{p}:Gf^{\prime}\in H^{p}\}.

Assume that
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a non-elliptic semigroup. It is straightforward that
\lambda\in\sigma_{p}(\Delta)
if and only if there exists
f\in\textnormal{Dom}(\Delta)
such that
\lambda f=Gf^{\prime}.
This differential equation has a unique solution up to a constant
e^{\lambda h}\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
(see
[
34
]
), where
h
is the Koenigs function of the semigroup
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}.
Thus,
\lambda\in\sigma_{p}(\Delta)
if and only if
e^{\lambda h}\in H^{p},
and in such a case
\ker(\Delta-\lambda I)=\Span\{e^{\lambda h}\}.

The point spectrum
\sigma_{p}(\Delta)
was characterized by Betsakos in
[
5
]
for hyperbolic semigroups. We present a slight reformulation of his results that will be easier to handle for our purposes:

Theorem 4.1
.

[
5
]

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a hyperbolic semigroup of holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{D}
and assume without loss of generality that its Denjoy-Wolff point is
\tau=1.
Let
\Omega
be the associated Koenigs domain of the semigroup and
\gamma
be the width of the smallest horizontal strip containing
\Omega.
Let
(C_{\varphi_{t}})_{t\geq 0}
be the induced semigroup of composition operators on
H^{p}
with
1\leq p<\infty
, and
\Delta
its infinitesimal generator.

(a)

Suppose that
\Omega
does not contain any horizontal strip. Then

\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:-\infty<\textnormal{Re}(\lambda)<\frac{\pi}{p\gamma}\}\subseteq\sigma_{p}(\Delta;H^{p})\subseteq\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:-\infty<\textnormal{Re}(\lambda)\leq\frac{\pi}{p\gamma}\}.

(b)

Suppose that
\Omega
contains a horizontal strip. Then there exists a horizontal strip contained in
\Omega
with width

\beta_{\textnormal{max}}=\max\{\beta>0:\Omega\ \textnormal{contains a horizontal strip of width }\beta\}.

Moreover,

\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:-\frac{\pi}{p\beta_{\textnormal{max}}}<\textnormal{Re}(\lambda)<\frac{\pi}{p\gamma}\}\subseteq\sigma_{p}(\Delta;H^{p})\subseteq\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:-\frac{\pi}{p\beta_{\textnormal{max}}}<\textnormal{Re}(\lambda)\leq\frac{\pi}{p\gamma}\}.

With this result at hand, we state the following proposition regarding hyperbolic semigroups. Recall that a horizontal strip is a domain defined as

\{z+it_{0}\in\mathbb{C}:|\Impart(z)|<t_{1}\}\subset\mathbb{C},

where
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and
t_{1}>0
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a hyperbolic semigroup of holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{D}
and assume without loss of generality that is Denjoy-Wolff point is
\tau=1.
Let
\Omega
be the associated Koenigs domain of the semigroup and
\gamma
be the width of the smallest horizontal strip containing
\Omega.
Let
(C_{\varphi_{t}})_{t\geq 0}
be the induced semigroup of composition operators on
H^{p}
,
1\leq p<\infty
, and
\Delta
its infinitesimal generator.

(a)

Assume that
\Omega
does not contain any horizontal strip. Then,

\sigma(\Delta;H^{p})=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:-\infty<\textnormal{Re}(\lambda)\leq\frac{\pi}{p\gamma}\}.

(b)

Assume that
\Omega
contains a horizontal strip, and let
\beta_{\textnormal{max}}
be as in Theorem
4.1
.

(i)

If
\Omega
is a horizontal strip, then
C_{\varphi_{t}}
is invertible for every
t\geq 0
and
\beta_{\textnormal{max}}=\gamma
and

\sigma(\Delta;H^{p})=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:|\textnormal{Re}(\lambda)|\leq\frac{\pi}{p\gamma}\}.

(ii)

If
\Omega
is not a horizontal strip, then
C_{\varphi_{t}}
is not invertible for any
t>0
.

Proof.

We start proving
(a)
. If
\Omega
does not contain any horizontal strip, by Theorem
4.1
, it follows that

\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:-\infty<\textnormal{Re}(\lambda)\leq\frac{\pi}{p\gamma}\}\subseteq\sigma(\Delta;H^{p}).

For the reverse containment, recall that the spectral radius of
C_{\varphi_{t}}
in
H^{p}
is given by
r(C_{\varphi_{t}};H^{p})=\varphi_{t}^{\prime}(1)^{-1/p}
(see
[
33
, Corollary 3.2]
, for instance). Moreover,
\varphi_{t}^{\prime}(1)=e^{-\frac{\pi t}{\gamma}}
(see
[
10
, Theorem 2.1]
, for instance), so

r(C_{\varphi_{t}};H^{p})=e^{\frac{\pi t}{\gamma p}}.

Upon applying Proposition
2.1
, it follows that for every
\lambda\in\sigma(\Delta)
the inequality
\textnormal{Re}(\lambda)\leq\frac{\pi}{p\gamma}
holds and
(a)
is proved.

Now, we deal with
(b)
. Assume that
\Omega
contains a horizontal strip and consider the following two cases:

(i)

Assume
\Omega
is a horizontal strip, and let

g(z)=\log\left(\frac{1+z}{1-z}\right),\qquad(z\in\mathbb{D}),

where we are considering the principal branch of the logarithm. Note that
g
is a univalent function with
g(0)=0
that maps the disc
\mathbb{D}
onto the horizontal strip symmetric with respect to the real axis and with width
\pi.
It is clear that there exists
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}>0
such that
\tilde{g}(z):=\alpha_{1}g(z)+\alpha_{2}
maps the unit disc onto
\Omega.
Now, if
h
is the Koenigs function of the semigroup, it is clear that the map

\Gamma:z\in\mathbb{D}\mapsto h^{-1}\circ\tilde{g}(z)\in\mathbb{D}

is a biholomorphic map of
\mathbb{D}
. Moreover, it is easy to check that
C_{\Gamma}C_{\varphi_{t}}=C_{\psi_{t}}C_{\Gamma},
where
\psi_{t}(z)=\tilde{g}^{-1}(\tilde{g}(z)+t).
Then, the operators
C_{\varphi_{t}}
and
C_{\psi_{t}}
are similar for all
t\geq 0
, so it suffices to show the result for
C_{\psi_{t}}
. But, in this case,

\psi_{t}(z)=\frac{(e^{t/\alpha_{1}}+1)z+(e^{t/\alpha_{1}}-1)}{(e^{t/\alpha_{1}}-1)z+(e^{t/\alpha_{1}}+1)}\qquad(z\in\mathbb{D}),

which turns out to be a hyperbolic automorphism of
\mathbb{D}
and, hence,
C_{\psi_{t}}
is invertible.

Accordingly,
C_{\varphi_{t}}
is invertible for every
t\geq 0
and the assertion about the spectrum follows from
[
27
, Theorem 6]
, Theorem
4.1
and Proposition
2.1
.

(ii)

Assume
\Omega
contains a horizontal strip but it is not one. We will argue by contradiction. Let us suppose that there exists
t_{0}>0
such that
C_{\varphi_{t_{0}}}
is invertible. Then,
\varphi_{t_{0}}
is an automorphism of the unit disc which is hyperbolic. Now, by
[
6
, Theorem 3.2]
, every member of the semigroup is a linear fractional map, and since they all commute, by
[
11
, Theorem 8]
, it follows that every
\varphi_{t}
with
t>0
has to be a hyperbolic automorphism of the disc having the same fixed points. Finally, arguing as in the proof of
(i)
, it is easy to see that the Koenigs function of the semigroup
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
(which can be extended to a group) maps the disc
\mathbb{D}
onto a horizontal strip, which contradicts our assumption. Then, we have shown that
C_{\varphi_{t}}
is not invertible for any
t>0
, and the proof is done.

∎

At this point, we can discuss the density of the eigenfunctions of infinitesimal generators of hyperbolic semigroups, which will be carried out by applying a result of
[
8
]
:

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a hyperbolic semigroup of holomorphic functions in
\mathbb{D}
and let
h
and
\Omega
denote its Koenigs function and Koenigs domain, respectively. Assume, without loss of generality, that
\tau=1.
Let
(C_{\varphi_{t}})_{t\geq 0}
be the induced semigroup of composition operators acting on
H^{p}
(
1\leq p<\infty
) and
\Delta
its infinitesimal generator. Assume that

(i)

Either
\Omega
does not contain any horizontal strip,
\textnormal{int}(\overline{\Omega})=\Omega
and
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
has at most two connected components,

(ii)

or
\Omega
is a horizontal strip.

Then, the linear manifold

\Span\{e^{\lambda h}\in H^{p}:\lambda\in\textnormal{int}(\sigma(\Delta;H^{p})\}

is dense in
H^{p}
for every
1\leq p<\infty.

The proof is based on the ideas developed in
[
8
]
, and we include it for the sake of completeness.

Proof.

For
1\leq p<\infty
, let
H^{p}(\Omega)
be the Hardy space associated to the Koenigs domain
\Omega
, that is, the Banach space consisting of holomorphic functions
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
such that there exists a harmonic function
u:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that
|f(z)|^{p}\leq u(z)
for every
z\in\Omega
(see Duren’s book
[
15
, Chapter 10]
for more on these spaces). It turns out that the map
f\in H^{p}(\Omega)\mapsto f\circ h\in H^{p}
is a surjective isometry, so it suffices to show that the linear manifold
\Span\{e^{\lambda z}:\lambda\in\textnormal{int}(\sigma(\Delta;H^{p}))\}
is dense in
H^{p}(\Omega).

Let
H^{\infty}(\Omega)
denote the space of bounded holomorphic functions in
\Omega.
By Theorems
4.1
and
4.2
it follows that
\textnormal{int}(\sigma(\Delta;H^{p}))\subseteq\sigma_{p}(\Delta;H^{p})
, so for every
\lambda\in\textnormal{int}(\sigma(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}}))
the kernel
\ker(\Delta-\lambda I)
has dimension one and it is spanned by
e^{\lambda h}
. Observe that, by Theorem
4.2
and the convexity in the positive direction of
\Omega,

\{e^{\lambda z}\in H^{\infty}(\Omega):\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\}\subset\{e^{\lambda h}\in H^{p}:\lambda\in\textnormal{int}(\sigma(\Delta;H^{p}))\}

for all
1\leq p<\infty.
Now,
[
8
, Theorem 1.1]
yields that the linear manifold

\Span\{e^{\lambda z}\in H^{\infty}(\Omega):\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\}

is weak-star dense in
H^{\infty}(\Omega).
As a consequence, if
1<p<\infty,
such linear manifold is weakly dense in
H^{p}(\Omega),
and by Mazur’s Lemma (
[
14
, Corollary 3, Chapter 2]
), it is dense on
H^{p}(\Omega).
Finally, the continuity of the inclusion
H^{p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow H^{1}(\Omega)
and the density of
H^{p}(\Omega)
in
H^{1}(\Omega)
yields the density for
p=1,
which completes the proof.
∎

The next theorem is the counterpart of Theorem
3.1
in the setting of hyperbolic semigroups of composition operators:

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a hyperbolic semigroup of holomorphic functions in
\mathbb{D}
and
\Omega
its Koenigs domain. Assume, without loss of generality, that
\tau=1.
Let
(C_{\varphi_{t}})_{t\geq 0}
be the induced semigroup of composition operators in
X=H^{p}
,
1\leq p<\infty
, and
\Delta
its infinitesimal generator of type
\omega_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. Assume that

(i)

Either
\Omega
does not contain any horizontal strip,
\textnormal{int}(\overline{\Omega})=\Omega
and
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
has at most two connected components,

(ii)

or
\Omega
is a horizontal strip.

Let
\nu
be a finite complex Borel measure supported in
[0,\infty)
such that
\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{(w_{0}+\delta)t}d\nu(t)<\infty
for some
\delta>0
and
\mathcal{L}(\nu)
is a non-constant function in
\mathcal{E}(-\Delta)
. If
\mathcal{H}_{\nu}
is the subordinated operator associated to
(C_{\varphi_{t}})_{t\geq 0}
the following holds:

(1)

\mathcal{H}_{\nu}
does not have the SVEP.

(2)

For every relatively open subset
U\subset\sigma(\mathcal{H}_{\nu}),

\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{H}_{\nu}}(\overline{U})
is dense in
X
.

(3)

\sigma_{p}(\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{*})=\emptyset.

(4)

X_{\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{*}}(F)=\{0\}
for every closed subset
F\subsetneq\sigma(\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{*}).

(5)

\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{*}
has the Dunford property (C).

(6)

\sigma_{\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{*}}(f)=\sigma(\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{*})
for every
f\in X^{*}\setminus\{0\}.

(7)

r_{\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{*}}(f)=r(\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{*})
for every
f\in X^{*}\setminus\{0\}.

(8)

If
M
is a non-trivial closed invariant subspace for
\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{*}
, then

\sigma(\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{*})\subseteq\sigma(\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{*}\mid_{M})\subset\eta(\sigma(\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{*})).

The proof follows as an application of Theorem
3.1
. Note that by Theorem
4.2
,
\overline{\textnormal{int}(\sigma(-\Delta))}=\sigma(-\Delta)
, so the map

f_{\Delta}:z\in\textnormal{int}(\sigma(-\Delta))\mapsto e^{-zh}\in\textnormal{Dom}(-\Delta)

is well defined and for every
-z\in\textnormal{int}(\sigma(\Delta))

(-\Delta-zI)f_{\Delta}(z)=-(\Delta-(-z)I)e^{-zh}=0,

since
\textnormal{int}(\sigma(\Delta))\subset\sigma_{p}(\Delta)
and
\textnormal{int}(\sigma(-\Delta))=-\textnormal{int}(\sigma(\Delta))
holds. Finally, by Proposition
4.3
,

\{f_{\Delta}(\lambda):\lambda\in\textnormal{int}(\sigma(-\Delta))\}

is dense in
H^{p}
, so the conditions of Theorem
3.1
are therefore fulfilled.

In particular, as it was discussed in Remark
3.2
, it is possible to derive the following consequence for each operator in the hyperbolic composition semigroup
(C_{\varphi_{t}})_{t\geq 0}
:

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a hyperbolic semigroup of holomorphic functions in
\mathbb{D}
and
\Omega
its Koenigs domain. Assume, without loss of generality, that
\tau=1.
Let
(C_{\varphi_{t}})_{t\geq 0}
be the induced semigroup of composition operators in
X=H^{p}
,
1\leq p<\infty
, and
\Delta
its infinitesimal generator. Assume that

(i)

Either
\Omega
does not contain any horizontal strip,
\textnormal{int}(\overline{\Omega})=\Omega
and
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
has at most two connected components;

(ii)

or
\Omega
is a horizontal strip.

For every
t>0
the following holds:

(1)

C_{\varphi_{t}}
does not have the SVEP.

(2)

For every relatively open subset
U\subset\sigma(C_{\varphi_{t}}),

\mathcal{X}_{C_{\varphi_{t}}}(\overline{U})
is dense in
X
.

(3)

\sigma_{p}(C_{\varphi_{t}}^{*})=\emptyset.

(4)

X_{C_{\varphi_{t}}^{*}}(F)=\{0\}
for every closed subset
F\subsetneq\sigma(C_{\varphi_{t}}^{*}).

(5)

C_{\varphi_{t}}^{*}
has the Dunford property (C).

(6)

\sigma_{C_{\varphi_{t}}^{*}}(f)=\sigma(C_{\varphi_{t}}^{*})
for every
f\in X^{*}\setminus\{0\}.

(7)

r_{C_{\varphi_{t}}^{*}}(f)=r(C_{\varphi_{t}}^{*})
for every
f\in X^{*}\setminus\{0\}.

(8)

If
M
is a non-trivial closed invariant subspace for
T^{*}
, then

\sigma(C_{\varphi_{t}}^{*})\subseteq\sigma(C_{\varphi_{t}}^{*}\mid_{M})\subset\eta(\sigma(C_{\varphi_{t}}^{*})).

Proof.

Assume that
\Omega
does not contain any horizontal strip, the proof of Theorem
4.2
yields that

(4.3)

\sigma(C_{\varphi_{t}};H^{p})=\overline{\{e^{\lambda t}:\lambda\in\sigma(\Delta)\}}

for every
t>0
having in mind Proposition
2.1
and
r(C_{\varphi_{t}};H^{p})=e^{\frac{\pi t}{\gamma p}}
.

On the other hand, if
\Omega
is a horizontal strip, the semigroup is similar to a semigroup of hyperbolic automorphisms and in such a case Theorem
4.1
along with
[
27
, Theorem 6]
yields (
4.3
).
∎

Remark 4.6
.

It is important to note that properties
(1)-(8)
were already proved for composition operators induced by hyperbolic linear fractional maps in
[
18
]
. Corollary
4.5
extends these results to every composition operator that can be embedded in a hyperbolic semigroup such that its Koenigs domain meets the geometrical conditions
(i)
or
(ii)
.

4.3.
Examples of subordinated operators

In this subsection, we provide particular instances of hyperbolic semigroups of holomorphic functions in
\mathbb{D}
which yield examples of subordinated operators for which Theorem
4.4
applies. Of particular interest is the classical Cesàro operator.

(1)

For each
t\geq 0
, let

\varphi_{t}(z)=e^{-t}z+(1-e^{-t}),\qquad(z\in\mathbb{D}).

Each
\varphi_{t}
is an affine map, in particular a hyperbolic non-automorphism linear fractional map in
\mathbb{D}
which fixes
1
and
\infty
.
Clearly
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a semigroup of holomorphic functions in
\mathbb{D}
and its Koenigs function is given by
h(z)=-\log(1-z)
, for
z\in\mathbb{D}
, where the principal branch of the logarithm is considered. The Koenigs domain
\Omega
is contained in

\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\textnormal{Re}(z)\geq-\log(2),\;-\pi/2<\Impart(z)<\pi/2\}

and the semigroup is hyperbolic since it is contained in a horizontal strip. Indeed,
\Omega
does not contain any horizontal strip and
\textnormal{int}(\overline{\Omega})=\Omega
, so Theorem
4.4
clearly applies to the semigroup
(C_{\varphi_{t}})_{t\geq 0}
acting on
H^{p}.

It is well known that
\sigma(\Delta;H^{p})=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\textnormal{Re}(\lambda)\leq\frac{1}{p}\}.
For each
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
such that
\textnormal{Re}(\lambda)>\frac{1}{p},
let
\nu_{\lambda}
the Borel measure

d\nu_{\lambda}(t)=e^{-\lambda t}dt

in
[0,\infty).
Clearly
\nu_{\lambda}
satisfies the hypothesis on the measure of Theorem
4.4
and by (
2.2
)
\mathcal{H}_{\nu_{\lambda}}=R(\lambda,\Delta).
For every
f\in H^{p}
we have

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}_{\nu_{\lambda}}f(z)&=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}(C_{\varphi_{t}}f)(z)dt=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}f(e^{-t}z+(1-e^{-t}))dt\\
&=\frac{-1}{(z-1)^{\lambda}}\int_{z}^{1}(s-1)^{\lambda-1}f(s)ds,\qquad(z\in\mathbb{D})\end{split}

so
\mathcal{H}_{\nu_{\lambda}}
is an averaging operator. Theorem
4.4
assures that properties
(1)-(8)
are satisfied for such operators.

In particular, consider
1<p<\infty
and let
\lambda=1.
In this case, we obtain the operator

\mathcal{H}_{\nu_{1}}f(z)=\frac{-1}{z-1}\int_{z}^{1}f(s)ds,\qquad(z\in\mathbb{D}).

By
[
26
]

-\mathcal{H}_{\nu_{1}}
is (similar to) the adjoint operator of the Cesàro operator
\mathcal{C}
acting on
H^{p},
that is, the operator defined as

(\mathcal{C}f)(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\frac{f(s)}{1-s}ds,\qquad(z\in\mathbb{D}).

Therefore,
\mathcal{C}^{*}
does not enjoy the SVEP and their glocal spectral subspaces associated to the closure of relatively open sets are dense. Moreover, the spectral subspaces associated to proper closed sets of the Cesàro operator are zero, so the Cesàro operator has the Dunford property, the local spectrum at non-zero functions coincides with the spectrum of
\mathcal{C}
, and
\sigma(\mathcal{C}\mid_{M})=\sigma(\mathcal{C})
for every non-trivial closed invariant subspace of
\mathcal{C}
.

It is worth pointing out that the Dunford property was already known for the Cesàro operator since Miller, Miller and Smith
[
26
]
proved that
\mathcal{C}
has the Bishop property which implies it. Nevertheless, the characterization of the spectral subspaces and the local spectra of
\mathcal{C}
had not been previously studied in the literature. Next result (already mentioned in the Introduction) states the new features regarding the local spectra of
\mathcal{C}
, and as a particular instance, yields that
\mathcal{C}
enjoys the Dunford property
(C)
.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
\mathcal{C}
be the Cesàro operator acting on the Hardy space
H^{p}.
Then:

(a)

\sigma_{\mathcal{C}}(f)=\sigma(\mathcal{C};H^{p})
for every
f\in H^{p}\setminus\{0\}.

(b)

r_{\mathcal{C}}(f)=r(\mathcal{C};H^{p})
for every
f\in H^{p}\setminus\{0\}.

(c)

H^{p}_{\mathcal{C}}(F)=\{0\}
for every closed subset
F\subsetneq\sigma(\mathcal{C};H^{p}).

(d)

If
M
is a non-trivial closed invariant subspace of
\mathcal{C}
, then
\sigma(\mathcal{C}\mid_{M};H^{p})=\sigma(\mathcal{C};H^{p}).

(2)

For each
t\geq 0
, let
\varphi_{t}
be the hyperbolic automorphism of
\mathbb{D}
fixing
\pm 1
:

\varphi_{t}(z)=\frac{(e^{t}+1)z+(e^{t}-1)}{(e^{t}-1)z+(e^{t}+1)}\qquad(z\in\mathbb{D}).

Clearly
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
forms a semigroup of holomorphic functions in
\mathbb{D}
that can be extended to a group
(\varphi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
with the obvious definition for
t<0.
As it was discussed in the proof of Theorem
4.2
, the Koenigs domain associated to such semigroup is a horizontal strip. In this case,
\sigma(\Delta;H^{p})=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:|\textnormal{Re}(\lambda)|\leq\frac{1}{p}\}.
For every
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
with
\textnormal{Re}(\lambda)>1
the resolvent operator

(\mathcal{H}_{\nu_{\lambda}}f)(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}f(\varphi_{t}(z))dt=\left(\frac{1+z}{1-z}\right)^{\lambda}\int_{z}^{1}\left(\frac{1+s}{1-s}\right)^{-\lambda}\frac{2}{1-s^{2}}f(s)ds,\qquad(z\in\mathbb{D}),

is an averaging operator in
H^{p}
satisfying the local spectral properties of Theorem
4.4
.

(3)

In general, a formula for the resolvent operators arising from semigroups of composition operators can be obtained. If
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a hyperbolic semigroup of holomorphic functions in
\mathbb{D}
with Denjoy-Wolff point
\tau=1\in\mathbb{T}
,
h
is the associated Koenigs function and
(C_{\varphi_{t}})_{t\geq 0}
the associated semigroup of type
\omega_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
such that
\textnormal{Re}(\lambda)>\omega_{0}
the resolvent at
\lambda
may be expressed as

(\mathcal{H}_{\nu_{\lambda}}f)(z)=e^{\lambda h(z)}\int_{z}^{1}h^{\prime}(s)e^{-\lambda h(s)}f(s)ds,\qquad(f\in H^{p},\,z\in\mathbb{D}).

Indeed, if the Laplace transform of the measure
d\nu_{\rho}(t)=\rho(t)dt
belongs to
E(-\Delta)
, where
\rho:[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, it follows

(4.4)

(\mathcal{H}_{\nu_{\rho}}f)(z)=\int_{z}^{1}h^{\prime}(s)\rho(h(s)-h(z))f(s)ds,\qquad(f\in H^{p},\,z\in\mathbb{D}).

Thus, if
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a hyperbolic semigroup satisfying the hypothesis of Theorem
4.4
, every operator of the form (
4.4
) has properties
(1)-(8)
from the aforementioned Theorem.

4.4.
A remark on non-hyperbolic semigroups

To end up this section, we briefly comment the local spectral picture of composition operators semigroups induced by non-hyperbolic symbols, which looks quite different.

(1)

For
t\geq 0
, let
\varphi_{t}
be the holomorphic function

\varphi_{t}(z)=\frac{e^{-t}z}{(e^{-t}-1)z+1}\qquad(z\in\mathbb{D}).

Clearly
\varphi_{t}(0)=0
and
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is an elliptic semigroup. It was shown in
[
18
]
that the composition operator
C_{\varphi_{t}}
acting on
H^{p}
,
1\leq p<\infty
, satisfies the Dunford’s property, so in particular it has the SVEP. Moreover, by considering
\psi_{t}(z)=1-(1-z)^{e^{-t}}
for
t\geq 0
, which also forms an elliptic semigroup, the Cesàro operator appears as the resolvent at
\lambda=1
(
[
34
, p. 19]
):

\mathcal{C}=R(1,\Delta),

where
\Delta
is the infinitesimal generator of
(C_{\psi_{t}})_{t\geq 0}
acting on
H^{p}
. In this case, the Cesàro operator appears as a subordinated operator having the Bishop’s property and
\mathcal{C}^{*}
not enjoying the SVEP (see
[
26
]
). This situation is drastically different to the one presented in Theorem
4.4
for subordinated operators induced by hyperbolic semigroups.

(2)

For
t\geq 0,
let
\varphi_{t}
be the parabolic automorphism of
\mathbb{D}
fixing
1
:

\varphi_{t}(z)=\frac{(1-it)z+it}{-itz+1+it},\qquad(z\in\mathbb{D}).

(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
turns out to be a semigroup of holomorphic functions in
\mathbb{D}
. It is well known that
\sigma(C_{\varphi_{t}};H^{p})=\sigma_{p}(C_{\varphi_{t}};H^{p})=\mathbb{T}
(see
[
27
]
). Moreover, if
\Delta
is the infinitesimal generator of
(C_{\varphi_{t}})_{t\geq 0}
acting on
H^{p}
, then

\sigma(\Delta;H^{p})=\sigma_{p}(\Delta;H^{p})=\{i\lambda:\lambda\leq 0\}

(see
[
34
]
). As a particular instance, the equality
\sigma(C_{\varphi_{t}};H^{p})=\{e^{\lambda t}:\lambda\in\sigma(\Delta)\}
holds. Nevertheless, the operators
C_{\varphi_{t}}
are generalized scalar (see
[
35
]
), which in particular implies that
C_{\varphi_{t}}
and
C_{\varphi_{t}}^{*}
have the Dunford’s property. This fact follows since all generalized scalar operators and their adjoint are decomposable operators, and these operators have Dunford’s property (
[
24
, Section 1.8]
). In particular, each spectral subspace of such operators associated to proper non-empty closed sets are non-trivial. Clearly, the local spectral picture of the operators of the semigroup differs noticeably from the one for hyperbolic semigroups described in Corollary
4.5
.

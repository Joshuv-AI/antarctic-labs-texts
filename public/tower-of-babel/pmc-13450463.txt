A combination therapy strategy: precision co-targeting of CCR7 and JAK1 with a smart hydrogel for inflammatory skin diseases
Authors: Yamei Gao, Xueting Shen, Jiliang Lu, Chaojing Zhou, Shaohu Huo, Ziyue Diao, Chao Chen, Xin Han, Zhiqiang Yin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Inflammatory skin diseases (ISDs) pose a major global burden, yet current therapies are limited by efficacy, accessibility, and cost, necessitating novel effective treatments. Thus, our study proposes a novel synergistic therapeutic strategy for psoriasis (PSO) and atopic dermatitis (AD) by concurrently targeting two critical pathological axes: immune cell chemotaxis via CCR7 inhibition and local inflammatory signaling via JAK1 suppression. A pH-responsive hydrogel (C-P@U/C-siCCR7) was designed for co-delivery of CCR7-targeting siRNA and the JAK1 inhibitor upadacitinib (UPA). Multi-omics analysis confirmed specific CCR7 overexpression in patient and model tissues. In vitro, the system inhibited keratinocyte proliferation, induced apoptosis, and downregulated key proteins in the JAK-STAT and PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathways along with pro-inflammatory cytokines. In IMQ-induced PSO and DNCB-induced AD mouse models, topical hydrogel application ameliorated skin lesions, restored the epidermal barrier, reduced hyperplasia and infiltration, and alleviated systemic immune activation—including suppression of splenomegaly and serum cytokines (IL17A, TNFα, IL4, IL13)—with favorable biosafety. The therapeutic effects were mechanistically linked to synergistic downregulation of CCR7, JAK1, STAT3, and mTOR in lesions, and reduced populations of aberrantly activated T cells, neutrophils, and dendritic cells in the spleen. Introduction

Inflammatory skin diseases (ISDs), such as psoriasis (PSO) and atopic dermatitis (AD), represent significant global public health challenges [1]. These conditions extend far beyond cutaneous manifestations and are systemic disorders characterized by chronic, recurrent inflammation. They are frequently associated with various comorbidities, including metabolic syndrome, cardiovascular diseases, and psychological disorders, which severely impair patients’ quality of life and social functioning [2]. Although targeted therapies, notably biologics and Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitors, have substantially improved clinical outcomes for some patients, their widespread application faces several limitations [3, 4]. These include primary or secondary insufficient response, safety concerns related to long-term systemic administration (e.g., infections and metabolic alterations), and accessibility barriers due to high treatment costs [5, 6]. Inflammatory skin disorders arise from dysregulated interactions between innate and adaptive immune pathways. Targeting a single component within this network often leads to inadequate therapeutic responses [7]. A more promising strategy involves simultaneously disrupting multiple key pathological mechanisms to achieve synergistic efficacy [8]. We therefore designed a dual-targeting approach combining siRNA-mediated silencing of the chemokine receptor CCR7 with the potent JAK1 inhibitor upadacitinib (UPA). CCR7 governs the migration of dendritic cells and T lymphocytes to lymphoid organs and inflamed tissues [9, 10]. Our bioinformatic and experimental data confirm its specific overexpression in both psoriatic and AD lesions, where its levels correlate with clinical severity. This positions CCR7 as a key upstream driver of pathological immune cell recruitment. Mechanistically, UPA acts intracellularly to inhibit the JAK-STAT pathway, thereby blocking signals from pro-inflammatory cytokines (e.g., IL4, IL13, IL22, IFNγ) that sustain keratinocyte hyperproliferation and local inflammation [11, 12]. In contrast, siCCR7 operates further upstream by preventing the CCR7-CCL19/CCL21 interaction, which is essential for the initial homing and retention of immune cells in the skin [10]. Theoretically, combining these agents targets two sequential, mutually reinforcing pathological stages: siCCR7 inhibits the initial “immune cell infiltration,” while UPA suppresses the subsequent “local inflammatory amplification.” This spatial and functional synergy offers a rational strategy for achieving deeper and more sustained disease control.

However, the successful co-delivery of siRNA and hydrophobic small-molecule drugs to target tissues with spatiotemporally controlled release poses major challenges. These include the inherent instability of siRNA in vivo, their significant physicochemical differences, and off-target effects with potential systemic toxicity due to nonspecific distribution [13, 14]. Smart responsive hydrogel delivery systems offer a promising platform to address these hurdles. Such systems can efficiently encapsulate drug components with differing solubilities within their three-dimensional cross-linked network structure [15]. Through ingenious chemical design, they can be engineered to respond to characteristic signals in the diseased skin microenvironment (e.g., locally decreased pH, overexpression of specific enzymes), enabling site-specific and controlled drug release at the lesion site. This not only significantly enhances local drug concentration and therapeutic efficacy but also markedly reduces systemic exposure and the risk of adverse effects [16, 17]. Furthermore, the excellent bioadhesive properties of hydrogels can prolong their residence time on the skin surface, thereby improving drug delivery efficiency and patient compliance [18].

In this study, a pH-responsive intelligent composite hydrogel capable of co-delivering siCCR7 and UPA is designed and constructed, and to systematically evaluate its synergistic immunotherapeutic effects on PSO and AD. The specific construction method is as follows: First, CCR7 siRNA is encapsulated with the cationic polymer chitosan (CS) to enhance its nuclease stability and promote cellular uptake. Subsequently, the CS-siCCR7 complex and UPA are co-encapsulated using a liposome technique, embedding the hydrophobic UPA within the phospholipid bilayer while housing the hydrophilic CS-siRNA in the aqueous core. Finally, the liposomal complex is coated with phenylboronic acid-modified carboxymethyl CS via dynamic covalent bonds, ultimately forming the environmentally responsive hydrogel system, designated as C-P@U/C-siCCR7 (Fig. 1A). This hydrogel will enable intelligent drug release in response to the mildly acidic microenvironment upon topical application to inflamed skin. Following transdermal absorption, the released siCCR7 and UPA will inhibit the CCR7-AKT-mTOR and JAK1-STAT3 pathways, respectively, thereby exerting a synergistic therapeutic effect while minimizing side effects (Fig. 1B). By enabling the spatiotemporally controlled co-delivery of siCCR7 and UPA, this pH-responsive smart composite hydrogel makes possible a synergistic therapy that concurrently targets the CCR7-AKT-mTOR and JAK1-STAT3 pathways for PSO and AD.

Results and discussion

Multi-omics validation of CCR7 upregulation in PSO and AD

To investigate shared molecular mechanisms and potential therapeutic targets in psoriasis (PSO) and atopic dermatitis (AD), we conducted an integrated bioinformatic analysis of multiple public transcriptomic datasets. In the PSO dataset GSE30999, comparison of lesional and non-lesional skin identified 2,884 differentially expressed genes (DEGs), including 1,445 upregulated and 1,439 downregulated genes (Fig. 2A). Similarly, analysis of the AD dataset GSE16161 revealed 1,972 DEGs, comprising 930 upregulated and 1,042 downregulated genes (Fig. 2B). These findings were further validated in an independent AD dataset, GSE32924, which identified 1,298 DEGs (559 upregulated and 739 downregulated; Fig. 2C). Venn diagram analysis across the three datasets identified 149 DEGs consistently shared between PSO and AD (Fig. 2D). To further dissect the regulatory network of these shared genes, a protein-protein interaction (PPI) network was constructed and hub genes were screened. The analysis identified several genes, including CCR7, STAT1, CCL2, and BIRC3, as central nodes within the network (Fig. 2E-F). Functional enrichment analysis indicated that these hub genes were significantly associated with biological processes such as immune-inflammatory responses, chemokine-mediated signaling, and the regulation of cell proliferation and apoptosis, highlighting their potential central roles in the pathogenesis of ISDs.

Then, we examined CCR7 expression in experimental mouse models of PSO (induced by imiquimod, IMQ) and AD (induced by DNCB). qPCR results demonstrated that CCR7 mRNA levels were significantly upregulated in the lesional skin of both disease models compared to normal controls (Fig. 2G, J). Western blot analysis further confirmed this trend at the protein level, showing markedly higher CCR7 protein expression in both psoriatic and AD lesions than in healthy skin tissues (Fig. 2H, I, and Figure S1). These consistent results indicate that CCR7 is stably overexpressed in two major ISDs, providing strong experimental evidence at both transcriptional and translational levels for its potential as a common therapeutic target. Consequently, CCR7 was selected as one of the core targets for our subsequent synergistic treatment strategy. Additionally, we designed CCR7-specific siRNAs that effectively silenced CCR7 expression. In vitro, they achieved significantly knockdown at the mRNA level in psoriatic and AD cell models, respectively, with efficacy confirmed at the protein level (Figure S2).

Synthesis and characterization of the C-P@U/C-siCCR7 hydrogels

The siRNA-loaded CS nanoparticles were characterized as uniform spheres with a hydrodynamic size of 50–120 nm, as confirmed by SEM (Fig. 3A) and DLS (Fig. 3C), respectively. The polydispersity indices (PDI) of 0.61 ± 0.21 and 0.86 ± 0.12, respectively, are characteristic of particles formed via polyelectrolyte complexation (Fig. 3D). A significant decrease in zeta potential upon siRNA binding, while maintaining a net positive surface charge, further confirmed complex formation (Fig. 3E). The PDI values were included to demonstrate the dispersity of the nanoparticles, while the zeta potential values were used to confirm the stepwise surface charge changes during nanoparticle assembly. The final C-P@U/C-siCCR7 hydrogel exhibited a porous, interconnected network structure in SEM with an average pore size of 61.00 ± 8.03 μm (Fig. 3B). Successful synthesis of the C-P shell was verified by multiple techniques. UV-Vis spectroscopy showed a new absorption peak at 256 nm, corresponding to the PBA benzene ring (Fig. 3F). Fourier-transform infrared (FTIR) spectra displayed new peaks at 1532 cm− 1 (benzene ring) and 1354 cm− 1 (B-O bond) for C-P (Fig. 3G-H). Importantly, the spectra of the final loaded system showed no significant alterations, confirming the structural integrity of the C-P shell after drug/nucleic acid incorporation (Figure S3-S4). 1H NMR analysis provided definitive evidence, with the appearance of aromatic proton resonances in the 8.10–8.70 ppm region for C-P, a shift attributable to the conversion of PBA carboxyl groups into amide linkages upon grafting (Fig. 3I). This was corroborated by a separate 1H NMR spectrum showing new aromatic signals at δ 6.8–8.2 ppm (Figure S5A), compared with the 1H NMR spectrum of CMCS (Figure S5B). Spectral calculations indicated a CMCS deacetylation degree of ~ 95% and a PBA substitution degree of ~ 19%.

The mechanical and self-healing properties of the hydrogel matrix were assessed by rheology. Frequency sweep measurements at 1% strain demonstrated that the storage modulus (G′) consistently exceeded the loss modulus (G″) across 0.1–100 rad/s, confirming the formation of a stable, elastic gel network (Fig. 3J). Amplitude sweep tests revealed a well-defined linear viscoelastic region (LVR) at strains < 10%, beyond which a sharp decrease in G′ indicated a reversible gel-to-sol transition. This shear-thinning behavior facilitates easy spreading and conformal adaptation to irregular skin surfaces, such as elbows and knees, upon application (Fig. 3K). Critically, alternating step-strain tests (1% → 500% → 1%) demonstrated rapid and substantial recovery of G′ and G″ upon removal of high shear, with G′ recovering to dominate G″, confirming excellent and rapid self-healing capability (Fig. 3L). These combined properties – structural stability, ease of application and conformability, coupled with autonomous self-healing – make the hydrogel an ideal candidate for a robust and long-lasting topical adhesive formulation on highly mobile joints. The hydrogel exhibited favorable macroscopic handling properties, forming a semi-solid, non-flowing material upon inversion (Fig. 3M). The precursor solution underwent in situ gelation at room temperature within 30 min, resulting in a semi-solid gel that maintained its shape when inverted (Fig. 3N) and showed strong adhesion to various surfaces including skin and plastic (Fig. 3O). As shown in Figure S6, to further evaluate the adhesion capacity of the hydrogel, tensile tester-based adhesion measurements were additionally performed on different substrates, including pigskin, glass, stainless steel, and polyethylene (PE). The hydrogel exhibited stable adhesion to all tested surfaces, with the highest adhesive strength on pigskin and the lowest on PE, while the corresponding force–displacement curves further confirmed distinct yet robust adhesion behaviors across both biological and non-biological substrates. Agarose gel electrophoresis confirmed complete siRNA complexation and retardation at an optimal N/P ratio of 70:1 (Fig. 3P). Finally, siRNA release profiles under simulated inflammatory skin conditions (pH 5.5, high GSH) demonstrated sustained release over 72 h, indicating the system’s potential for prolonged action at the target site (Fig. 3Q). As shown in Figure S7, UPA was released from C-P@U/C-siCCR7 hydrogels in a time-dependent manner under acidic and reductive conditions. The cumulative release increased progressively and was markedly enhanced at pH 5.5, especially in the presence of high GSH. In contrast, UPA release was lower under neutral conditions. These findings indicate that the hydrogels enable pH/redox-responsive release of UPA. In addition to the structural characterization, the loading efficiencies of the incorporated therapeutic agents were quantitatively evaluated. The encapsulation efficiency of siCCR7 was 91.8 ± 2.7% in C-siCCR7 complexes and 88.6 ± 3.1% in C-P@U/C-siCCR7 hydrogels, indicating that siCCR7 remained efficiently retained after hydrogel formation (Figure. S8A). Similarly, UPA showed encapsulation efficiencies of 84.7 ± 2.9% in C-P@U hydrogels and 81.9 ± 3.4% in C-P@U/C-siCCR7 hydrogels (Figure. S8B), confirming the effective loading of UPA in both single- and dual-cargo formulations. Together, these data further support the successful fabrication of the C-P@U/C-siCCR7 hydrogel system. To further evaluate whether the hydrogel-enabled cargos could penetrate diseased skin and reach deeper tissue layers, fluorescence tracing experiments were performed in PSO and AD mouse models. UPA was labeled with rhodamine B, while siCCR7 was conjugated with FITC, and the fluorescence distribution in skin sections was examined by confocal microscopy at 12, 24, 48, and 72 h after topical administration. As shown in Fig. 3R, both rhodamine B and FITC signals were detectable in the skin tissue over time in the two inflammatory skin models, indicating progressive penetration and retention of the loaded cargos. Notably, fluorescence signals remained observable at 72 h, suggesting that the hydrogel system enabled sustained delivery and prolonged residence of both the small-molecule drug and siRNA within the skin tissue. These results support the ability of the hydrogel to facilitate transdermal transport and local deposition of therapeutic agents under diseased skin conditions. Overall, the successful construction of the smart composite hydrogel, evidenced by its defined core-shell architecture, efficient dual-cargo encapsulation, stimuli-responsive release, porous structure, favorable rheological and adhesive performance, self-healing behavior, and sustained skin deposition, supports the feasibility of this integrated platform for topical delivery.

In vitro anti-inflammatory efficacy of C-P@U/C-siCCR7 hydrogel

Following the successful synthesis of the C-P@U/C-siCCR7 hydrogel, we evaluated its in vitro biological activity using the human immortalized keratinocyte line HaCaT. HaCaT cells were selected as they are a standard model for epidermal keratinocytes and are central to the pathogenesis of both PSO and AD, responding to key inflammatory cytokines involved in these diseases [19]. It is important to note that the target proteins (CCR7, JAK1, STAT3, etc.) are minimally expressed in healthy, unstimulated HaCaT cells; therefore, our experiments focused on cytokine-induced disease models to assess therapeutic modulation of aberrantly activated pathways. The EdU assay demonstrated that the dual-drug-loaded hydrogel (G4) most potently inhibited DNA replication with an inhibition rate of 42.59%, outperforming the siRNA-only (G2) and UPA-only (G3) groups (Fig. 4A-B). The CCK-8 assay confirmed a 55.09% suppression of cell viability in G4 (Fig. 4C). In the Annexin V-FITC/PI flow cytometry plots, FITC on the x-axis represents Annexin V-FITC fluorescence corresponding to phosphatidylserine externalization, whereas PI on the y-axis represents propidium iodide fluorescence indicating impaired plasma membrane integrity. And Flow cytometric analysis revealed a clear gradient in apoptosis induction: the disease model group (G1) showed a baseline total apoptosis rate of 10.47%, which was increased by siRNA (G2) and UPA (G3) to varying degrees, while the C-P@U/C-siCCR7 treatment (G4) induced the highest apoptotic rate at 31.23% (Fig. 4D-E).

The in vitro experimental design for the PSO and AD cell models is schematically illustrated (Fig. 4F). HaCaT cells were stimulated with IL17A/TNFα or IL4/IL13, followed by RNA extraction at 24 h and protein extraction at 48 h for subsequent analysis.Western blot analysis of key signaling proteins under these pathological conditions revealed a consistent trend. In the disease model group (G1), all target proteins—including CCR7, JAK1, STAT3, PI3K, AKT, and mTOR—were significantly upregulated compared to basal levels. Treatment with siRNA (G2) or UPA (G3) alone resulted in a partial but distinct downregulation of their respective target pathways. Notably, the combined C-P@U/C-siCCR7 treatment (G4) produced the most pronounced suppression, reducing the expression of all assayed proteins to the lowest observed levels (Fig. 4G and Figure S9). This coordinated downregulation confirms that the composite hydrogel effectively and synergistically blocks the core JAK-STAT and PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling cascades, which are critical for inflammatory response and keratinocyte hyperproliferation, thereby elucidating the molecular basis for its superior therapeutic activity.

Additional evidence was provided at the transcriptional level by qPCR analysis. In the PSO model, treatment with C-P@U/C-siCCR7 led to a significant reduction in the mRNA expression of characteristic pro-inflammatory cytokines, including IL23, IL6, IL1β, and CXCL1 (Fig. 5A-D). Specifically, the downregulation of IL-23 may impair Th17 cell differentiation and function [20]; decreased IL6 could help alleviate aberrant keratinocyte proliferation [21]; reduced IL1β suggests suppression of early inflammatory initiation [22]; and diminished CXCL1 may weaken neutrophil chemotaxis and infiltration [23]. In the AD model, the treatment likewise induced significant alterations in key cytokine expression: mRNA levels of IL5, IFNγ, and IL1β were markedly downregulated (Fig. 5E-H). Decreased IL5 may inhibit eosinophil activation [24]; reduced IFNγ indicates modulation of the Th1-type immune response [25]; and lower IL1β likely mitigates inflammatory initiation and skin barrier disruption [26]. Notably, expression of the anti-inflammatory regulatory cytokine IL-10 was also concurrently reduced, potentially as a feedback mechanism following the effective suppression of upstream pro-inflammatory signals (e.g., IL5, IFNγ) [27]. Collectively, these results demonstrate that C-P@U/C-siCCR7 can transcriptionally coordinate the suppression of the mixed Th2/Th1 inflammatory response associated with AD. Furthermore, intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS) assays and it’s quantitative analysis of ROS fluorescence intensity revealed that the material treatment significantly lowered ROS levels in both the PSO and AD model cells (Fig. 5I-J and Figure S10A–B). This finding further confirms its ability to alleviate oxidative stress and cooperatively inhibit the activation of inflammatory pathways, thereby exerting therapeutic functions across multiple dimensions.

In vivo therapeutic efficacy of C-P@U/C-siCCR7 in PSO

To evaluate the in vivo therapeutic effect of the C-P@U/C-siCCR7 gel on PSO, we first established an IMQ-induced psoriasiform dermatitis mouse model [28]. Briefly, 5% IMQ cream was topically applied daily for six consecutive days on the shaved back skin of mice to induce lesions. Corresponding treatments began 12 h after the initial IMQ application (Fig. 6A). The designs of the in vivo and in vitro experiments were intentionally different because they addressed distinct biological questions. In the in vivo study, a healthy control group was necessary to define the physiological baseline of normal skin, which is a multicellular tissue composed not only of keratinocytes but also fibroblasts, endothelial cells, and resident immune cells. Recent spatial and single-cell studies further show that healthy skin contains organized immune–stromal niches and active homeostatic cell–cell communication, which makes a normal control essential for interpreting treatment effects at the tissue level. Skin phenotypes were observed and recorded daily. Continuous IMQ application successfully induced typical psoriasiform features, including erythema, scaling, and epidermal thickening. Compared to the untreated model group (Group i), the siCCR7-only group (Group ii), or the UPA-only group (Group iii), mice treated with the C-P@U/C-siCCR7 gel (Group iv) showed significant improvement in both clinical manifestations and symptom severity (Fig. 6B). During the treatment period, no significant body weight fluctuation was observed in Group iv. In contrast, Group i mice exhibited a marked decrease in body weight on days 3 and 4, potentially associated with PSO-related systemic inflammation (Fig. 6C). Further analysis demonstrated that the skin barrier function score of Group iv was significantly superior to that of other groups, indicating markedly reduced lesion severity (Fig. 6D). The skin barrier function score was obtained using the GPSkin Barrier device, which automatically generates an integrated score ranging from 0 to 100; higher values indicate better barrier integrity and functional recovery. Additionally, we employed a PSO Area and Severity Index -based scoring system for daily objective assessment of erythema, scaling, and skin thickening. The data showed that, compared to Group i, Group iv significantly suppressed disease progression from day 3 onward, reaching the lowest PASI score (4 points) by day 6, with efficacy superior to that of all single-drug treatment groups (Fig. 6E and Figure S11).

IMQ-induced psoriasiform dermatitis is often accompanied by systemic inflammation, leading to splenomegaly [29]. In this experiment, spleen weight and size were significantly increased in Group i, whereas these parameters in Group iv were comparable to those in the normal control group (Group v) (Fig. 6F and Figure S12), suggesting that the gel effectively alleviated systemic inflammation. The spleen in the C-P@U-treated group (Group iii) remained relatively enlarged and showed a spleen weight comparable to that of the disease model group, suggesting that C-P@U alone did not fully reverse splenic enlargement within the current treatment period. Since splenomegaly in psoriasis-like inflammation may reflect persistent structural changes secondary to systemic immune activation, gross spleen morphology may not completely parallel immunological improvement at a given time point. Typical histological hallmarks of PSO include epidermal hyperplasia, keratinocyte hyperproliferation, and immune cell infiltration. H&E staining of skin tissues revealed that Group iv mice exhibited significantly reduced epidermal thickness and markedly decreased inflammatory cell infiltration, with effects superior to those in the untreated and single-treatment groups (Fig. 6G and Figure S16A). In the psoriatic state, the chronic inflammatory microenvironment jointly drives aberrant keratinocyte hyperproliferation (increased Ki67 positivity) and DNA damage (increased TUNEL positivity) [30, 31]. Following effective treatment, this core inflammatory driver is suppressed, simultaneously relieving the abnormal proliferative signals and damage stress on cells. To evaluate the therapeutic effect at the cellular dynamics level, we further performed immunofluorescence staining on skin tissues. The results showed that, compared to Group i, the proportion of TUNEL-positive cells in Group iv decreased by 92.1%, indicating a significant reduction in apoptosis. Concurrently, the proportion of Ki-67-positive cells decreased by 89.0%, suggesting effective inhibition of keratinocyte proliferative activity (Fig. 6H and Figure S13).Therefore, in vivo experiments demonstrated that treatment with the C-P@U/C-siCCR7 hydrogel significantly alleviated psoriasiform dermatitis, from clinical phenotype and histopathology to cellular dynamics, and effectively suppressed the concomitant systemic immune activation.

To investigate whether the therapeutic effect of C-P@U/C-siCCR7 on PSO is associated with immunomodulation, we performed quantitative analysis of immune cell subsets in the spleens of mice from each group. The results showed that compared to the model group (Group i), the proportion of T lymphocytes (CD3+, CD4+, CD8+) in the spleens of the dual-drug treatment group (Group iv) was significantly reduced (by approximately 70.2%), reaching levels comparable to the normal control group (Group v) (Fig. 7A, C). Concurrently, the proportion of neutrophils (CD11b+ Ly6G+) was also markedly decreased (by approximately 67.9%) (Fig. 7B, D). These findings indicate that the treatment effectively suppressed systemic immune cell hyperactivation and accumulation in psoriatic mice. To further assess systemic inflammatory status, we measured the levels of key inflammatory cytokines in mouse serum using ELISA. Serum IL17A concentration was significantly elevated in the model group (Group i), while all treatment groups effectively reduced its level, with the most pronounced inhibition observed in the dual-drug group (Group iv). The trend for serum TNFα was consistent with that of IL17A, with Group iv likewise exhibiting the strongest suppressive capacity (Fig. 7E-F). These results demonstrate that the C-P@U/C-siCCR7 hydrogel can systemically alleviate PSO-associated systemic inflammation.

Furthermore, immunofluorescence staining was performed on skin tissues to analyze the expression of key signaling proteins. The results revealed that compared to the model group, all treatment groups downregulated the expression of CCR7, JAK1, mTOR, and STAT3 in epidermal cells to varying degrees, with the most significant and comprehensive inhibition observed in the dual-drug treatment group (Group iv) (Fig. 7G). The reduced CCR7 expression in the JAK1 inhibitor-treated group (Group iii) and the decreased JAK1 expression in the siCCR7-treated group (Group ii) may be attributed to the close interplay between chemokine-mediated immune cell trafficking and cytokine-driven inflammatory signaling. In psoriasis-like inflammation, JAK1 inhibition can indirectly suppress CCR7 expression by reducing immune activation, while CCR7 silencing may diminish inflammatory cell recruitment and cytokine amplification, thereby secondarily attenuating JAK1-related signaling. This finding confirms at the tissue level that C-P@U/C-siCCR7 synergistically inhibits immune cell chemotaxis (via CCR7 targeting) and inflammatory/proliferative signaling (via the JAK1/mTOR/STAT3 pathway). This is highly consistent with the aforementioned improvements in skin phenotype, changes in splenic immune cell proportions, and reductions in serum inflammatory cytokine levels, collectively elucidating the close association of its therapeutic efficacy with multi-target immunomodulation.

In vivo therapeutic efficacy of C-P@U/C-siCCR7 in AD

To investigate the therapeutic potential of topically applied C-P@U/C-siCCR7 gel for AD, we established an AD model by repeatedly applying 2,4-dinitrochlorobenzene to the shaved back skin of mice, followed by three weeks of local treatment (Fig. 8A). Repeated DNCB challenge successfully induced typical AD-like lesions, including skin dryness, severe erythema, hemorrhage, crusting, edema, desquamation, and erosion [32]. Compared to the model group (Group i), the UPA-only gel group (Group ii) showed only modest therapeutic effects, the siCCR7-only gel group (Group iii) moderately alleviated symptoms, while the co-loaded C-P@U/C-siCCR7 gel group (Group iv) demonstrated the most potent anti-inflammatory and reparative effects. Skin edema, hemorrhage, erythema, desquamation, and erosion were significantly improved in Group iv mice (Fig. 8B). To further validate the broad applicability of our therapeutic strategy and provide phenotypic documentation, we also employed the MC903-induced mouse model—a well-established system recapitulating key features of human AD such as skin barrier dysfunction and a predominant Th2-type immune response—for macroscopic observation and the results were the same [33] (Figure S14). During treatment, no significant body weight loss was observed in any treatment group (Fig. 8C).

The lesion severity was further assessed using Psoriasis Area and Severity Index (PASI) [34]. And the results showed that compared to the model group, scores in all treatment groups were significantly reduced (by approximately 2 points), with Group iv exhibiting superior efficacy in restoring the skin barrier compared to any single-drug treatment group (Fig. 8D). Additionally, evaluation using a comprehensive dermatitis scoring system encompassing erythema/hemorrhage, crusting/dryness, edema, and desquamation/erosion [35], that confirmed a significant decrease in the clinical score for Group iv, further substantiating the excellent therapeutic capability of the C-P@U/C-siCCR7 gel against AD (Fig. 8E and Figure S15A-D). Measurement of ear thickness throughout the modeling and treatment course revealed that the Group i exhibited the greatest swelling, single-agent treatment groups (Groups ii & iii) showed moderate reduction, while the combination therapy group (Group iv) presented the thinnest ears, nearly comparable to the healthy control (Figure S11E). DNCB-induced AD is often accompanied by systemic immune activation, leading to compensatory splenomegaly. This study also observed a significant increase in spleen weight and size in the model group, whereas these parameters in Group iv were comparable to those in the normal control group (Group v) (Fig. 8F and Figure S17A), suggesting that the treatment effectively alleviated the systemic immune response triggered by AD.

Histological analysis revealed pronounced epidermal hyperplasia, edema, and dermal/epidermal inflammatory cell infiltration in the ear skin of DNCB-treated mice. In contrast, dual-drug combination therapy in Group iv more markedly attenuated these characteristic AD histopathological alterations compared to single-drug treatment groups (Fig. 8G and Figure S16B). Toluidine blue staining further confirmed that topical application of C-P@U/C-siCCR7 gel significantly reduced dermal mast cell infiltration (Figure S17B). Furthermore, in the AD state, the Th2-type inflammatory environment drives compensatory keratinocyte proliferation for barrier repair while inducing oxidative stress that causes DNA damage. Following effective treatment, the inflammatory source is suppressed, simultaneously relieving the abnormal proliferative stimuli and cellular damage stress [36]. Accordingly, immunofluorescence staining of the ear skin showed a decreased proportion of TUNEL-positive cells and a reduced proportion of Ki-67-positive cells in Group iv (Fig. 8H and Figure S18).

Flow cytometric analysis of splenic immune cells further revealed that treatment with C-P@U/C-siCCR7 significantly reduced the proportions of both CD11c+ mature dendritic cells and CD4+ T cells in the spleen (Fig. 9A-D), indicating effective suppression of systemic activation and expansion of antigen-presenting cells and helper T cells in the AD model. This further supports the broad mechanistic commonality and application potential of this combinatorial strategy for treating various immune-related dermatoses.

Biocompatibility of C-P@U/C-siCCR7

The clinical translation of C-P@U/C-siCCR7 necessitates not only high efficacy but also stringent biosafety. We therefore comprehensively evaluated the biocompatibility of C-P@U/C-siCCR7. In vivo assessment in mice revealed no visible tissue damage in major organs (heart, liver, spleen, lungs, kidneys) upon H&E staining (Figure S20A and Figure S20A). Furthermore, serum biochemistry analysis showed that key indicators—including ALT, AST, ALP, TP, UREA, and CREA—all remained within normal physiological ranges (Figure S20B-G and Figure S20B-G). These collective results confirm the high biocompatibility of C-P@U/C-siCCR7, supporting its potential for safe clinical application.

Conclusion

In this study, we successfully developed a novel composite hydrogel delivery system, C-P@U/C-siCCR7, capable of the synergistic co-delivery of CCR7-targeting siRNA and the potent JAK1 inhibitor UPA. Integrating bioinformatic analysis with experimental validation, we first established the common overexpression of CCR7 in both PSO and AD, providing a rationale for its selection as a key therapeutic target. In vitro, this intelligent system effectively inhibited abnormal keratinocyte proliferation, induced apoptosis, and concurrently downregulated key signaling pathways—JAK-STAT and PI3K/AKT/mTOR—closely associated with inflammation and proliferation. In IMQ-induced psoriasiform and DNCB-induced AD mouse models, topical application of this hydrogel demonstrated remarkable comprehensive therapeutic efficacy. It not only significantly alleviated local skin inflammation and restored the epidermal barrier function but also effectively modulated systemic immune dysregulation, as evidenced by the suppression of splenomegaly, reduction in the proportion of aberrantly activated immune cells within the spleen, and decreased levels of key serum inflammatory cytokines, all while exhibiting favorable biocompatibility and safety throughout the treatment course. Mechanistically, its therapeutic effects primarily stem from the synergistic blockade of two critical pathological axes “CCR7-mediated immune cell chemotaxis and infiltration” and “JAK1-driven local inflammatory signal cascade amplification”. Despite these encouraging findings, this study remains limited by the use of murine models and short-term evaluation. Further work in human-relevant systems and longer-term settings will be needed to better define safety, durability, and translational potential.

By integrating gene silencing technology with small-molecule inhibition via an intelligent responsive hydrogel platform for the first time, this study achieves coordinated intervention against the dual key nodes of “immune cell migration” and “inflammatory signaling” in ISDs. This work not only validates the superiority and clinical translational potential of this combination strategy over monotherapy but also provides an innovative design concept and a solid experimental foundation for developing next-generation, highly effective, and precise topical therapeutics for chronic inflammatory dermatoses such as PSO and AD.

Preliminaries

Partially ordered sets

Given a set of elements
P
and a binary relation
\preccurlyeq
(a subset of
P\times P
), we say that
(P,\preccurlyeq)
is a
partially ordered set
(or a
poset
) if
\preccurlyeq
satisfies the following three properties.

•

Reflexivity: For all
a\in P
,
a\preccurlyeq a
;

•

Anti-symmetry: For all
a,b\in P
, if
a\preccurlyeq b
and
b\preccurlyeq a
then
a=b
;

•

Transitivity: For all
a,b,c\in P
,
a\preccurlyeq b
and
b\preccurlyeq c
then
a\preccurlyeq c
.

For a partial order
(P,\preccurlyeq)
we define a
strict
partial order as
(P,\preccurlyeq\smallsetminus\{(e,e)\mid e\in P\})
. Note that there is a direct correspondence between a partial order and the strict partial order it induces and vice versa. We refer to
\prec
as the strict partial order of
\preccurlyeq
. Moreover, when the context is clear, we refer to
P
as the poset instead of
(P,\preccurlyeq)
.

The poset induced by the total order on
n
elements
([n],\{(i,j)\mid i\leq j\})
is called the
n
-element
chain
and the poset induced by
n
pairwise incomparable elements
([n],\left\{(i,i)\mid i\in[n]\right\})
the
n
-element
antichain
. We say that a poset
(U,\leq)

contains
another poset
(P,\preccurlyeq)
as an induced subposet if there exists
P^{\prime}\subseteq U
such that
(P^{\prime},\leq)
is isomorphic to
(P,\preccurlyeq)
.
A family of subsets
\{C_{1},\ldots,C_{k}\}

C_{i}\subseteq P
, is called a
chain decomposition
of size

of a poset

if

is isomorphic to a chain for every

and
(C_{i})_{i\in[k]}
forms a partition of
P
. The following theorem, known as Dilworth’s theorem, is a fundamental result on posets.

2.1

[

]

Let

Boolean lattice as universal poset

A poset

2.2

The poset

Proof.

Consider an

defined by

is isomorphic to

.
Let
v_{i},v_{j}\in P

v_{i}\preccurlyeq_{P}v_{j}
then for all
v_{k}\in V
, if

Comparability graphs

Given a poset

Theorem

The proof of Theorem

We first describe our construction for posets without a large antichain.

3.1
.

Let

to be the union of all these set families. The integer sequences will represent the lengths of each chain in a chain decomposition.

Let

\sum_{i=1}^{a}c_{i}=n
.
Let

and for

Since

forms a partition of

and there are

, we find that

\sum_{i=1}^{a}c_{i}=n
.

We use
\preccurlyeq_{P}
for the partial order relation on

. For

as defined above, we rename the elements of the

Since a chain decomposition is a partition, each element of

Next, we associate to an element

As shown in

. What remains to show is that

for all

.
Let

i\in f(y_{S})\implies y_{S}\not\preccurlyeq_{P}z_{i}\implies z_{j}\not\preccurlyeq_{P}z_{i}\implies i\in f(z_{j}).

By definition,
[b+1,b+\ell]\subseteq f(z_{j})
. This shows that
f(y_{S})\subseteq f(z_{j})
if
y_{S}\preccurlyeq_{P}z_{j}
.

Case 3:
u=z_{j}
.

Finally, we consider the case where
u=z_{j}
for
j\in[b+\ell+1,n-a+\ell]
. Now
u\not\preccurlyeq v
for all
v
of the form
x_{i}
and
y_{S}
(by the definition of “below”) and for those
v
we also find that
f(u)\not\subseteq f(v)
since
[b+1,b+\ell]\not\subseteq f(v)
whereas
[b+1,b+\ell]\subseteq f(u)
.
When
v=z_{j^{\prime}}
for
j^{\prime}\in[b+\ell+1,n-a+\ell]
, the proof is analogous to before: if
z_{j}\not\preccurlyeq_{P}z_{j^{\prime}}
, then
j^{\prime}\in f(z_{j})\smallsetminus f(z_{j^{\prime}})
, whereas transitivity of
\preccurlyeq_{P}
ensures that
f(z_{j})\subseteq f(z_{j^{\prime}})
when
z_{j}\preccurlyeq_{P}z_{j^{\prime}}
.

Since we proved (
1
) in all cases for
u
and
v
, this shows that
(2^{[n-a+\ell]},\subseteq)
contains the poset
P
via the embedding given by
f
.
∎

Combining the two constructions now yields our result.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

We may assume
n
is sufficiently large, since the Boolean lattice itself is a universal poset and we may choose the constant
C
.
Setting
a=\lceil n/3\rceil
in Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.2
, taking the union of the two set systems resulting from the lemmas yields a set system
V\subseteq 2^{[n]}
for which
(V,\subseteq)
is universal for the
n
-element posets with

|V|\leq p(n)(n/3)(3+1)^{n/3}+2^{2n/3+(1+o(1))\log_{2}n}=2^{2n/3+O(\sqrt{n})},

using that the number of partitions of
n
is
p(n)=2^{O(\sqrt{n})}
by Theorem
2.3
.
∎

4
Conclusion

We showed that one can find a universal poset for posets on
n
elements of order
2^{(1+o(1))2n/3}
inside the Boolean lattice. In particular, this is now also the best upper bound for the following problem, with the best lower bound of the form
2^{n/4+o(n)}
.

4.1
.

What is the minimum order of a subposet of the Boolean lattice
(2^{[n]},\subseteq)
that contains all posets on
n
elements?

Our initial construction of a universal poset on
2^{2n/3+o(n)}
elements for posets on
n
elements was via a labelling scheme (see the PhD thesis of the first author
[
Bas25
]
) and it could be that such a proof method will be able to construct a smaller universal poset that is not contained in the Boolean lattice, but we were unable to improve further the constant
2/3
in the exponent.

Recall from Proposition
2.2
that the “folklore” embedding of an
n
-vertex poset
(P,\preccurlyeq_{P})
into
U=(2^{[n]},\subseteq)
works by fixing some order
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
on the elements of
P
and then embedding
v_{j}
to
S_{j}=\{i\in[n]\mid v_{i}\preccurlyeq_{P}v_{j}\}
. We showed that there is a much smaller subposet
U^{\prime}
of
U
such that for all posets without a large antichain, there is a way to choose the order on the elements such that the poset is guaranteed to embed into
U^{\prime}
. This leaves the question whether we could have done a similar thing for all posets (possibly with a large antichain). The following proposition shows that this is not possible.

4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{S}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
. Suppose that for each poset
(P,\preccurlyeq_{P})
there is an order
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
on
P
such that for each
j\in[n]
,

\{i\in[n]\mid v_{i}\preccurlyeq_{P}v_{j}\}\in\mathcal{S}.

Then
|\mathcal{S}|\geq 2^{(1-o(1))n}
.

Proof.

For simplicity, we will assume
m=n-\sqrt{n}
is an even integer. We next define a family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\binom{2^{[n]}}{n}
of set families. A set family
F\subseteq 2^{[n]}
is a member of
\mathcal{F}
if it contains exactly
n
subsets,
m
of which are
\{1\},\dots,\{m\}
and the remaining
n-m=\sqrt{n}
are of the form
\{m+i\}\cup T_{i}
for
i\in[\sqrt{n}]
and
T_{i}\in\binom{[m]}{m/2}
. We call the sets
\{1\},\dots,\{m\}
the
bottom elements
and the remaining sets the
top elements
of
F
.

Each element
F\in\mathcal{F}
gives a (labelled) poset
(F,\subseteq)
on
n
elements.
Note that

|\mathcal{F}|\geq\binom{m}{m/2}^{\sqrt{n}}=2^{(1-o(1))n\sqrt{n}}.

Let
\mathcal{S}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
be given such that for each
F\in\mathcal{F}
, there is an order
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}
on the elements of
F
such that
f_{j}^{\prime}=\{i\in[n]\mid f_{i}\subseteq f_{j}\}\in\mathcal{S}
for all
j\in[n]
.
In particular, for each
F\in\mathcal{F}
, we may record the
\sqrt{n}
images
f_{i_{1}}^{\prime},\dots,f_{i_{\sqrt{n}}}^{\prime}
of the top elements
f_{i_{1}},\dots,f_{i_{\sqrt{n}}}
of
F
. Using a double counting argument, we will show that

\binom{|\mathcal{S}|}{\sqrt{n}}\geq|\mathcal{F}|/n!=2^{(1-o(1))n\sqrt{n}}

which proves that
|\mathcal{S}|\geq 2^{(1-o(1))n}
, as desired.

To prove the inequality above, we show that for every choice of
\sqrt{n}
elements
S_{1},\dots,S_{\sqrt{n}}\in\mathcal{S}
, there are at most
n!
choices of
F\in\mathcal{F}
for which we recorded
S_{1},\dots,S_{\sqrt{n}}
as the top elements of
F
(via the chosen order on
F
). Let
S_{1},\dots,S_{\sqrt{n}}\in\mathcal{S}
and let
\sigma:[n]\to[n]
be a bijection. Suppose that
F\in\mathcal{F}
is such that the fixed order
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}
on the elements of
F
has the property that
\{i\}=f_{\sigma(i)}
for
i\in[m]
,
i\in f_{\sigma(i)}
for
i\in\{m+1,\ldots,n\}
, and

(S_{1},\dots,S_{\sqrt{n}})=(f_{\sigma(m+1)}^{\prime},\dots,f_{\sigma(n)}^{\prime})
.
We prove that there is at most one such
F
. The bottom elements of
F
are always the same and its top elements are of the form
f_{\sigma(m+i)}=\{m+i\}\cup T_{i}
for
i\in[\sqrt{n}]
and
T_{i}\in\binom{[m]}{m/2}
. Noting that the top elements are incomparable, for
i\in[\sqrt{n}]
we have

S_{i}=f^{\prime}_{\sigma(m+i)}=\{j\in[n]:f_{j}\subseteq f_{\sigma(m+i)}\}=\{\sigma(m+i)\}\cup\{\sigma(j):j\in T_{i}\},

where the last part follows from
f_{\sigma(j)}=\{j\}
for
j\in[m]
. In particular,
T_{i}
is determined uniquely by
S_{i}
and
\sigma
.
∎

Almost sure central limit theorems for parabolic/hyperbolic Anderson models with Gaussian colored noises Thanks: Panqiu Xia is partially supported by NSF grant DMS-2246850.
Panqiu Xia and Guangqu Zheng

Abstract
. This short note is devoted to establishing the almost sure central limit theorem for the parabolic/hyperbolic Anderson models driven by colored-in-time Gaussian noises, completing recent results on quantitative central limit theorems for stochastic partial differential equations. We combine the second-order Gaussian Poincaré inequality with Ibragimov and Lifshits’ method of characteristic functions, effectively overcoming the challenge from the lack of Itô tools in this colored-in-time setting, and achieving results that are inaccessible with previous methods.

60F15, 60H30

1.
Introduction

The classical central limit theorem (CLT) states that
for a random sample of size
n
drawn from a population with mean zero
and variance one, the sample mean
M_{n}
admits Gaussian fluctuation
as the sample size
n
tends to infinity:

\sqrt{n}M_{n}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\rm law}\mathcal{N}(0,1).

Following this setting, the almost sure central limit theorem (ASCLT)
in its simplest form asserts that
one can observe a Gaussian behavior (asymptotically)
along a generic trajectory via a logarithmic
average:
for almost every
\omega\in\Omega

(1.1)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\log n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k}\delta_{\sqrt{n}M_{n}(\omega)}\Longrightarrow\zeta

as
n\to\infty
, where
\delta_{x}
denotes the Dirac mass at
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
“
\Longrightarrow
” indicates the weak convergence of finite measures,
and
\zeta\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
stands for the standard Gaussian measure on
\mathbb{R}

throughout this note
.
The first ASCLT was introduced by P. Lévy in his book

[
28
, page 270]
but remained largely unnoticed for several decades,
until it was rediscovered by various researchers in probability
and dynamical systems

[
12
,
22
,
11
,
38
,
27
,
40
,
1
]
.
For a comprehensive historical account up to 2001, see also the work
of Berkes and Csáki
[
9
]
.
In recent years, several works

[
8
,
41
,
42
,
2
]
have established almost sure
(non-)central limit theorems using variants of Malliavin calculus
in Gaussian, Poisson, and Rademacher settings.

On a different note, around 2018, Huang, Nualart, and Viitasaari initiated in
[
24
]
a study on (quantitative)
central limit theorems for stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs) driven by Gaussian noises.
More precisely, they established a quantitative CLT
for the spatial averages of the solution to
a stochastic nonlinear heat equation
with multiplicative Gaussian space-time white noise.
Additionally, the first attempt of the similar topic for
stochastic nonlinear wave equations was published in

[
18
]
.
Since this pioneering work
[
24
]
,
there has been a rapidly growing literature
on the spatial averages of SPDEs. For CLT results, see, e.g.,

[
25
,
36
,
10
,
4
,
35
,
15
,
16
]
, and for spatial ergodicity that precedes the CLTs, refer to
[
14
,
37
]
.
We refer the interested readers
to
[
3
, (incomplete) table on page 5]

for an overview of relevant results in the Gaussian setting
and
[
7
]
in the Lévy setting.

In a series of papers
[
29
,
30
,
31
]
,
Li and Zhang developed the ASCLTs for several SPDEs
with Gaussian noises that are white in time.
A key tool they employed is Malliavin calculus,
particularly the Clark-Ocone formula, which replies heavily on
the martingale structure resulting from the white-in-time nature of the Gaussian noises;
see Remark
1.5
-(ii) for more details.
For a similar treatment applied
on the hyperbolic Anderson model driven by space-time pure-jump Lévy white noise, refer to

[
6
, Section 3.1]
.
However, this strategy using Clark-Ocone formula fails when attempting to establish
the ASCLT results for cases with colored-in-time Gaussian noises as in, e.g.,

[
4
,
35
]
. Motivated by our joint work with Balan
[
6
]
,
we will use a combination of Ibragimov-Lifshits’ method of characteristic functions
and the second-order Gaussian Poincaré inequality to establish the ASCLT results
for hyperbolic and parabolic Anderson models (HAM/PAM) driven by
space-time Gaussian colored noises.
Such a combination was originally introduced in
[
6
, Section 3.2]

within the Lévy setting.

1.1.
Framework

Consider the following two equations:

(PAM)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u=\tfrac{1}{2}\Delta u+u\diamond\dot{W},\\
u(0,\cdot)\equiv 1;\end{cases}

and

(HAM)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}^{2}u=\Delta u+u\diamond\dot{W}\\
u(0,\cdot)\equiv 1\quad{\rm and}\quad\partial_{t}u(0,\cdot)\equiv 0;\end{cases}

where
\diamond
denotes the Wick product,
meaning that the corresponding stochastic integral is interpreted
in the Skorohod sense,
and
\dot{W}
is a centered space-time Gaussian noise with correlation

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\dot{W}(t,x)\dot{W}(s,y)\big]=\gamma_{0}(t-s)\gamma_{1}(x-y)

satisfying certain conditions to ensure the existence and uniqueness of
(random field) solutions. In this note, we consider (
PAM
) in any spatial dimension,
while
we restrict our analysis of (
HAM
) to dimensions
d=1,2
; see Remark
1.7
for
relevant discussions.
Let us first state the following standing hypotheses of this note.

(H1)

\gamma_{0}:\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty]
is nonnegative-definite and locally integrable.

(H2)

\gamma_{1}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,\infty]
is nonnegative-definite
such that
\gamma_{1}=\mathcal{F}\mu
is the Fourier transform
of some nonnegative tempered measure
\mu
, called the spectral measure,
satisfying Dalang’s condition (
[
17
]
):

(1.2)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\mu(d\xi)<\infty.

Definition 1.1
.

A random field
u=\{u(t,x):(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
is called a solution to (
PAM
) or (
HAM
), provided that for all
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
it holds almost surely that

(1.3)

\displaystyle u(t,x)=1+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G_{t-s}(x-y)u(s,y)W(ds,dy),

where
G=G^{H}
(resp.
G^{W}
) denotes the heat kernel (resp. wave kernel)

(1.4)

\displaystyle G_{t}^{H}(x)\coloneqq(2\pi t)^{-\frac{d}{2}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{2t}},\,\,\,\text{ $\forall d\geq 1$},\quad\text{and}\quad G_{t}^{W}(x)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{1}_{\{|x|<t\}},&d=1,\\
\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}}\mathbf{1}_{\{|x|<t\}},&d=2;\end{cases}

and the stochastic integral is interpreted in the Skorohod sense.
That is,
the mild form (
1.3
) should be understood as

u(t,x)=1+\delta(G_{t-\bullet}(x-\ast)u(\bullet,\ast))
with

\delta
the divergence operator in Malliavin calculus;
see Section
2.1
and see also

[
33
, Section 1.3.2]
and
[
34
, Section 2.5]
.
Note that we follow the convention that
G_{t}=0
for
t\leq 0
.

Due to the linearity in the unknown
u
,
one can formally iterate the integral equation
(
1.3
) to obtain an infinite series,
which corresponds exactly to the
Wiener chaos expansion

of the solution (whenever it exists):

(1.5)

\displaystyle u(t,x)=1+\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}I_{p}(f_{t,x,p}),

where
I_{p}(f_{t,x,p})
stands for the
p
-th multiple Wiener-Itô integral
of the kernel
f_{t,x,p}

given by

\displaystyle f_{t,x,p}(s_{1},\dots,s_{p},z_{1},\dots,z_{p})\coloneqq\frac{1}{p!}\prod_{i=0}^{p}G_{t_{\tau(i+1)}-t_{\tau(i)}}\big(z_{\tau(i+1)}-z_{\tau(i)}\big),

with
\tau
denoting the permutation on
\{1,\dots,p\}

such that
0<t_{\tau(1)}<\dots<t_{\tau_{(p)}}<t
,
and by convention
(t_{\tau(0)},z_{\tau(0)})\coloneqq(0,0)

and
(t_{\tau(p+1)},z_{\tau(p+1)})\coloneqq(t,x)
;
see Section
2
for some preliminaries.
The finiteness of the above series in
L^{2}(\Omega)
,
or the validity of the chaos expansion (
1.5
),
can be verified by computing the second moment of each multiple integral,
which relies on the orthogonality relation
(see, e.g.,
[
34
, Proposition 2.7.4]
).

Remark 1.2
.

In fact, the above hypotheses
(H1)
and
(H2)
suffice to
guarantee the unique existence of solutions to (
PAM
)
and (
HAM
); see
[
23
, Theorem 3.2]
for the heat case
and see
[
5
, Section 5]
for the wave case.
For the wave case,
Balan and Song proved the unique existence of
solutions to (
HAM
)
on any spatial dimension. More precisely,

(H1)
and
(H2)
implies that (
1.5
) is the unique solution
to (
HAM
) when
d\leq 2
(see
[
5
, Theorem 5.2]
),
and under the additional
assumption that the spectral measure
\mu

is absolutely continuous with
respect to the Lebesgue measure, (
1.5
)
is the unique solution
to (
HAM
) when
d\geq 3

(see
[
5
, Theorem 5.6]
.
The delicacy in higher dimensions comes from
the fact that the corresponding wave kernel
G^{W}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
for
d\geq 3
,
is not a function any more, so that the interpretation of
product of
G^{W}
and the unknown
u

(and thus the interpretation of
the multiple integral
I_{p}(f_{t,x,p})
) requires additional care.
Such delicacy also makes it more difficult to establish
the CLT results
in higher dimensions (see
[
20
,
21
]
).

In the following, we present several results on (quantitative) CLTs
for spatial averages, which will serve as the basis for establishing the ASCLTs.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let the above hypotheses
(H1)
and
(H2)
hold and
we make further assumptions on the temporal/spatial correlation kernels:

(a)

for any
\varepsilon>0
,

(1.6)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\gamma_{0}(r)dr>0;

(b)

the spatial correlation kernel
\gamma_{1}
satisfies
one of the following properties:

(b1)

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with

\|\gamma_{1}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}>0
,

(b2)

\gamma_{1}(z)=|z|^{-\alpha}

with some
\alpha\in(0,2\wedge d)
.

Let
u
denote the solution (
1.5
) to (
PAM
)
for any spatial dimension
d
or (
HAM
) with
d\leq 2
.
Fix any
t_{0}\in(0,\infty)
throughout this note.
We define for any
R>0
that

(1.7)

\displaystyle F_{R}

\displaystyle=F_{R}(t_{0})\coloneqq\int_{|x|\leq R}[u(t_{0},x)-1]dx\quad{\rm and}\quad\widehat{F}_{R}\coloneqq\tfrac{1}{\sigma_{R}}F_{R}

with
\sigma_{R}\coloneqq\sqrt{\textup{Var}(F_{R})}>0
for each
R>0
.
Then, the following quantitative CLTs hold for any
R>0
:

(1.8)

\displaystyle d_{\rm TV}(\widehat{F}_{R},Z)\leq C\times\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} R^{-d/2}&\quad\text{\rm for \eqref{pam} with (b1)}&&\text{\rm{(case 1)}}\\
R^{-\alpha/2}&\quad\text{\rm for \eqref{pam} with (b2)}&&\text{\rm{(case 2)}}\\
R^{-d/2}&\quad\text{\rm for \eqref{HAM} with (b1)}&&\text{\rm{{(case 3)}}}\\
R^{-\alpha/2}&\quad\text{\rm for \eqref{HAM} with (b2)}&&\text{\rm{(case 4)}},\end{aligned}\end{cases}

where
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
,

d_{\rm TV}(X,Y)
stands for the total-variation distance between
two real-valued random variables
X
and
Y
:

(1.9)

\displaystyle d_{\rm TV}(X,Y)\coloneqq\tfrac{1}{2}\sup\big|\mathbb{E}[h(X)]-\mathbb{E}[h(Y)]\big|,

where the supremum running over all real-valued bounded measurable functions

h:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\|h\|_{\infty}\leq 1
.
Here, the implicit constant
C
does not depend on
R
.
See
[
35
,
4
]
for more details.

The range of
\alpha
in (b2)
follows from (
1.2
),
with the corresponding
spectral measure
\mu(d\xi)=c_{\alpha}|\xi|^{\alpha-d}

for some explicit constant
c_{\alpha}
.
The conditions (
1.6
) and “
\|\gamma_{1}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}>0
”
ensure that we are dealing with nontrivial space-time Gaussian noises
and that the spatial integral
F_{R}
has strictly positive variance
for each
R
.
It is
not difficult to prove
\sigma_{R}>0
for each
R>0
.
In fact, for
t_{0}>0
,
\mathbb{E}[u(t_{0},x)^{2}]>1
,
in view of the chaos expansion (
1.5
) and the orthogonality relation of multiple integrals. The same argument also leads to
\mathbb{E}[u(t_{0},x)u(t_{0},y)]\geq 1
, so that
with these two facts and the
L^{2}(\Omega)
-continuity of the solution, we get

\sigma_{R}^{2}=\int_{|x|,|y|<R}\big(\mathbb{E}[u(t_{0},x)u(t_{0},y)]-1\big)dxdy>0.

For the asymptotic behavior of
\sigma_{R}
, see (
3.10
).

1.2.
Main result

Let us first state the definition of ASCLT.

Definition 1.4
.

A family
\{F_{\theta}\colon\theta\geq 1\}
of real random variables is said to satisfy
the
ASCLT
if for
\mathbb{P}
-almost every

\omega\in\Omega
, the map
\theta\mapsto F_{\theta}(\omega)

is almost surely measurable and

(1.10)

\displaystyle\nu_{T}^{\omega}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\log T}\int_{1}^{T}\delta_{F_{\theta}(\omega)}\frac{d\theta}{\theta}\Longrightarrow\zeta\quad\text{as $T\to+\infty$,}

where
\zeta
stands for the standard Gaussian measure
on
\mathbb{R}
.

In this note, we aim
to establish an ASCLT for
\widehat{F}_{R}
in (
1.7
).
Thus, we choose the continuum parameter
R>0
in
Definition
1.4
, unlike the discrete parameter
n\in\mathbb{N}

stated in (
1.1
).
We present an equivalent statement of ASCLT in
Remark
1.5
-(ii).

Remark 1.5
.

(i)
The
logarithmic average
in (
1.1
) can be
replaced by any slowly varying analogue. For example,
1/k
and
\log n
in (
1.1
),
can be substituted with any
d_{k}
and
D_{n}>0
, respectively,
provided that
D_{n}=d_{1}+\dots+d_{n}\uparrow\infty

and
D_{n+1}/D_{n}\to 1
as
n
tends to infinity.
See also the discussion in
[
27
, page 204]
.
Note that our main result (Theorem
1.6
) remains valid
when the logarithmic average is replaced by a slowly varying analogue
in (
1.10
),
although we retain the former for simplicity in presentation.

(ii)
The validity of the ASCLT (Definition
1.4
)
for
\{F_{\theta}:\theta\geq 1\}

is equivalent to the statement that
for any bounded and Lipschitz continuous function
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
,
it holds almost surely that

\frac{1}{\log T}\int_{1}^{T}\frac{1}{\theta}\phi(F_{\theta})d\theta\to\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(x)\zeta(dx),

as
T\to+\infty
; see
[
6
, Remark 1.2]
.
Assuming CLT holds (i.e.,
F_{\theta}\Longrightarrow\zeta
), it is equivalent to show
that, almost surely,

\frac{1}{\log T}\int_{1}^{T}\frac{1}{\theta}H_{\theta}d\theta\to\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(x)\zeta(dx),

where
H_{\theta}\coloneqq\phi(F_{\theta})-\mathbb{E}[\phi(F_{\theta})]

is uniformly bounded.
Taking advantage of the
uniform boundedness of
H_{\theta}
,
it suffices to obtain a power decay
like
|\mathbb{E}[H_{\theta}H_{\textup{w}}]|\leq C(\theta/\textup{w})^{\beta}

for
\theta<\textup{w}
with some
\beta>0
and
C\in(0,\infty)
.
This can be easily accomplished
using Clark-Ocone formula when the noise is white in time; see, e.g.,

[
6
, Section 3.1]
and
[
29
,
30
,
31
]
.
However, in our note, where the Gaussian noises are colored in time,
this strategy of applying the Clark-Ocone formula fails.

Now we are ready to state our main result.

Theorem 1.6
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.3
, the
ASCLT

holds for
\big\{\widehat{F}_{R}:R\geq 1\big\}
.

Remark 1.7
.

(i) In this note, we focus exclusively on the wave equation in low spatial dimensions
(i.e.,
d\leq 2
). As previously mentioned, in higher dimension,
the wave kernels
G^{W}
, unlike the those in (
1.4
),
are no longer functions, leading to extra difficulty in employing the
Malliavin-Stein method to establish the desired CLTs.
Recent works by M. Ebina in
[
20
,
21
]
, has successfully
established these CLTs for the stochastic nonlinear wave equation on
\mathbb{R}^{d}

with
d\geq 3
. It is then a natural extension to study the ASCLT
in this high dimensional setting.

(ii)
For the heat equation, we
simplify the presentation by focusing only on the regular case
from
[
35
]
,
where the spatial correlation kernel is a nonnegative function.
We do not address the rough case, which involves generalized functions
for the spatial correlation kernel. For instance,
in one-dimensional case, this includes the correlation
whose spectral measure is
\mu(d\xi)=C_{H_{1}}|\xi|^{1-2H_{1}}
,
and
\gamma_{0}(t)=|t|^{2H_{0}-2}
for some explicit constant

C_{H_{1}}>0

and
0<H_{1}<1/2<H_{0}<1
such that

H_{0}+H_{1}>3/4
, or even more rough situations
as discussed in
[
32
]
.
We are optimistic that
our strategy can be applicable in this rough case,
while such an investigation would inevitably require
those very technical estimates from
[
35
]
.

Let us comment a bit our strategy and postpone the details
to Section
3
.
We will apply the powerful Ibragimov-Lifshits’ criterion
to prove Theorem
1.6
.

Proposition 1.8
.

(
[
26
, Ibragimov-Lifshits’ criterion]
)

A family of real-valued random variables
\{F_{\theta}\}_{\theta\geq 1}
satisfies the
ASCLT

if
\theta\mapsto F_{\theta}
is measurable almost surely, and
the following inequality holds

(1.11)

\displaystyle\sup_{|s|\leq T}\int_{2}^{\infty}\dfrac{\mathbb{E}\big[|\mathbf{K}_{t}(s)|^{2}\big]}{t\log t}dt<\infty,

for any finite
T>0
, where

(1.12)

\displaystyle\mathbf{K}_{t}(s)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\log t}\int_{1}^{t}\frac{1}{\theta}\big(e^{isF_{\theta}}-e^{-s^{2}/2}\big)d\theta,\quad t\in(1,\infty).

In Ibragimov-Lifshits’ original paper
[
26
]
,
the criterion
is proved for the discrete-time version.
For the proof of the above continuum version,
see
[
6
, Proposition 3.3]
.
Note that Ibragimov-Lifshits’ criterion
is not limited to the Gaussian limit (i.e., ASCLT),
as one can see from the original paper
and also from the recent application
[
2
]
.
We expect that it will be useful in establishing
almost sure non-central limit theorem in the SPDE context,
for example, an almost sure non-central limit theorem in the framework of

[
19
]
.

The said criterion requires us essentially to establish
logarithmic decay in the second moment
of the difference of characteristic functions
of the (random) probability
\nu_{T}^{\omega}
(
1.10
)
and standard Gaussian measure.
By some simple algebra, we only need to
bound the total-variation distances
d_{\rm TV}(F_{\theta},\mathcal{N}(0,1))

and
d_{\rm TV}(\frac{F_{\theta}-F_{\textup{w}}}{\sqrt{2}},\mathcal{N}(0,1))
.
While not claiming any originality,
we state below an abstract result that concludes this discussion.

Proposition 1.9
.

A family
\{F_{\theta}:\theta\geq 1\}
satisfy the
ASCLT
if

(1.13)

\displaystyle d_{\rm TV}(F_{\theta},\mathcal{N}(0,1))\leq C_{1}\theta^{-\beta_{1}}

and

(1.14)

\displaystyle d_{\rm TV}\big(\tfrac{F_{\theta}-F_{\textup{w}}}{\sqrt{2}},\mathcal{N}(0,1)\big)\leq C_{2}\big(\theta^{-\beta_{2}}+(\theta/\textup{w})^{\beta_{3}}\big)\,\,\text{for $\theta<\textup{w}$},

where
C_{1},C_{2}
are constants that do not depend on
\theta
and
w
,
whilst
\beta_{i}>0
for
i=1,2,3
.
The above result remains valid, if the total-variation distances
in (
1.13
)-(
1.14
) are replaced by
1
-Wasserstein distances.

Here, the
1
-Wasserstein distance
d_{\rm Wass}(X,Y)

of two real-valued random variables
X,Y
is defined by

(1.15)

\displaystyle d_{\rm Wass}(X,Y):=\sup_{\|h^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\leq 1}\big|\mathbb{E}[h(X)]-\mathbb{E}[h(Y)]\big|,

where the supremum in (
1.15
) runs over all
1
-Lipschitz functions
h:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
.
In some contexts (e.g.,
[
6
]
),
it is more natural to use the bounds in
1
-Wasserstein distances
in place of (
1.13
)-(
1.14
).
We postpone the proof of Proposition
1.9
to Section
2
.

We collect a few preliminaries in Section

in Section

.

2.
Preliminaries

In Section
2.1
, we present a few basics on
Malliavin calculus, while in Section
2.2
,
we record a criterion of
Ibragimov and Lifshits.

Assume that all probabilistic objects in this note are defined
on a rich enough probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
.
We write
\|\bullet\|_{p}
to denote the
L^{p}(\Omega)
-norm
for
p\in[1,\infty]

and
we write
a(R)\lesssim b(R)
for

\limsup_{R\to+\infty}a(R)/b(R)<+\infty
,
and
a(R)\sim b(R)

for

0<\liminf_{R\to+\infty}a(R)/b(R)\leq\limsup_{R\to+\infty}a(R)/b(R)<+\infty,

for any nonnegative functions
a
and
b
.

Suppose the Gaussian noise
\dot{W}
is defined as in the Introduction
such that both hypotheses
(H1)
and
(H2)
hold.
Under these conditions, we can rigorously built the isonormal framework
needed for developing
the
L^{2}
theory of Malliavin calculus. Let
C_{c}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})

denote the set of all real continuous functions on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
with compact support. We define the following inner product on
C_{c}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
:

(2.1)

\displaystyle\langle h_{1},h_{2}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{2d}}h_{1}(r,y)h_{2}(r^{\prime},y^{\prime})\gamma_{0}(r-r^{\prime})\gamma_{1}(y-y^{\prime})drdr^{\prime}dydy^{\prime}

for any
h_{1},h_{2}\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Let
\mathcal{H}
denote the closure
of
C_{c}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
under the above inner product (
2.1
). A random field
W=\{W(h):h\in\mathcal{H}\}
is an isonormal Gaussian process
over the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, if

W
is a centered Gaussian family with covariance given by

\mathbb{E}[W(h_{1})W(h_{2})]=\langle h_{1},h_{2}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}.

Let
\sigma\{W\}
denote the
\sigma
-algebra generated by the noise
\dot{W}
.
That is,
\sigma\{W\}
is the
\sigma
-algebra generated by

\{W(h):h\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})\}
.
The well-known Wiener-Itô chaos decomposition
(see, e.g.,
[
33
, Theorem 1.1.2]
) asserts that

L^{2}(\Omega,\sigma\{W\},\mathbb{P})
can be decomposed into mutually
orthogonal closed subspaces (called Wiener chaoses):

(2.2)

\displaystyle L^{2}(\Omega,\sigma\{W\},\mathbb{P})=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{C}^{W}_{n},

where
\mathbb{C}^{W}_{0}\simeq\mathbb{R}
is the set of constant random variables,

\mathbb{C}^{W}_{n}
is called
n
-th Wiener chaos that consists of
all multiple integrals of order
n
; see, e.g.,
[
34
, Section 2.7]
.
The
n
-th multiple integral operator
I_{n}
is a bounded linear operator
from the
n
-th tensor product
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}
to
\mathbb{C}^{W}_{n}
with
the following orthogonality relation

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[I_{n}(f)I_{m}(g)]=\mathbf{1}_{\{n=m\}}n!\langle\widetilde{f},\widetilde{g}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}

with
\widetilde{f}
denoting the canonical symmetrization of
f
;
see, e.g.,
[
34
, Appendix B]
for the Hilbert space notation.
Alternative to (
2.2
), we can express the
Wiener-Itô chaos decomposition as follows:
for any
F\in L^{2}(\Omega,\sigma\{W\},\mathbb{P})
, there exist

symmetric

kernels

f_{n}\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}
such that

(2.4)

\displaystyle F=\mathbb{E}[F]+\sum_{n\geq 1}I_{n}(f_{n}).

The membership of
F
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
is equivalent to the finiteness of

\sum_{n\geq 1}n!\|f_{n}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}
.
With this chaos expansion, we can define several Malliavin operators
in a convenient manner.

2.1.
Basic Malliavin calculus.

Let us first define several relevant Malliavin operators
using the above chaos expansions (
2.4
), and present results specifically tailored to the solutions to (
PAM
) and (
HAM
).

\bullet

Malliavin derivative operator
. For
k\in\{1,2\}
, we let
\mathbb{D}^{k,2}
denote
the set of all square integrable random variables
F
with the Wiener-Itô decomposition as in (
2.4
), such that

\sum_{n\geq 1}n!n^{k}\|f_{n}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}<\infty.

For any
F\in\mathbb{D}^{2,2}
with the Wiener-Itô decomposition as in (
2.4
), the Malliavin derivative
DF
and second order Mallivin derivative
D^{2}F
are given by

DF=\sum_{n\geq 1}nI_{n-1}(f_{n})\quad{\rm and}\quad D^{2}F=\sum_{n\geq 2}n(n-1)I_{n-2}(f_{n}),

which are random vectors in
\mathcal{H}
and

\mathcal{H}^{\otimes 2}
, respectively.
Note that the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
may contain generalized
functions, so that the random ‘function’
DF
may not be valued pointwise in general. The same comment applies to
D^{2}F
,
and when
D^{2}F
is indeed a function,

D_{s,y}D_{r,z}F
is symmetric
in
(s,y)
and
(r,z)
so that often
we state bounds only for
r<s
;
see, e.g., (
2.5
) below.
When
u(t,x)
denotes the solution to (
PAM
) or (
HAM
)
in this note,
we have the following results.

Proposition 2.1
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.6
, we have

u(t,x)\in\mathbb{D}^{2,4}\supset\mathbb{D}^{2,2}
, meaning that

|u(t,x)|+\|Du(t,x)\|_{\mathcal{H}}+\|D^{2}u(t,x)\|_{\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H}}\in L^{4}(\Omega)
. Moreover,
the map
(r,y)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\mapsto D_{r,y}u(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}

is indeed a
(
random
)
function such that
for any finite
p\geq 2

and for almost every
0<r<s<t\leq T
, and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(2.5)

\displaystyle\big\|D_{s,y}u(t,x)\big\|_{p}\lesssim_{T}G_{t-s}(x-y)\,\,\,{\rm and}\,\,\,\big\|D_{r,z}D_{s,y}u(t,x)\big\|_{p}\lesssim_{T}G_{t-s}(x-y)G_{s-r}(y-z),

where
the above implicit constants in
\lesssim_{T}
do not
depend on
(r,s,t)
but depend on
T
;
see
[
35
, Theorem 3.1]

and
[
4
, Theorem 1.3]

for more details.

For notational convenience, we say a random variable

F
satisfies the property (
P
) if

F\in\mathbb{D}^{2,2}
and almost surely,

DF\in|\mathcal{H}|
and

D^{2}F\in|\mathcal{H}^{\otimes 2}|

meaning that

(
P
)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\begin{cases}\text{$(r,y)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\mapsto|D_{r,y}F|\in\mathbb{R}$
belongs to $\mathcal{H}$}\\
\text{$(r,y,s,z)\in(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}\mapsto|D_{s,z}D_{r,y}F|\in\mathbb{R}$
belongs to $\mathcal{H}^{\otimes 2}$}.\end{cases}\end{aligned}

It is not difficult, via direct computations, to
show that the solution
u(t,x)
in Proposition
2.1

and the corresponding spatial integrals
(
1.7
) satisfy the property (
P
).

\bullet

Ornstein-Uhlenbeck operators
. For
F\in\mathbb{D}^{2,2}
written as in (
2.4
),
we define

LF=\sum_{n\geq 1}-nI_{n}(f_{n}).

Let
F\in L^{2}(\Omega)
written as in (
2.4
). Suppose that
\mathbb{E}[F]=0
,
we define

L^{-1}F=\sum_{n\geq 1}-\frac{1}{n}I_{n}(f_{n}).

Here,
L
is called the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck operator associated to
the noise
\dot{W}
and its domain coincides with
\mathbb{D}^{2,2}
.
The operator
L^{-1}
is called the pseudo-inverse of
L
due to
the fact that

L^{-1}LF=F-\mathbb{E}[F]
for
F\in\mathbb{D}^{2,2}
,
and
LL^{-1}F=F
for
F\in L^{2}(\Omega,\sigma\{W\},\mathbb{P})
with zero mean.
We define
P_{t}=e^{tL}
for
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, which is called the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck
semigroup and satisfies the contraction property:

(2.6)

\displaystyle\|P_{t}F\|_{p}\leq\|F\|_{p}

for any
F\in L^{p}(\Omega,\sigma\{W\},\mathbb{P})

with
p\in[1,\infty)
.
It is not difficulty to see
that for
F
as in (
2.4
),

P_{t}F=\mathbb{E}[F]+\sum_{n\geq 1}e^{-tn}I_{n}(f_{n})
.
Then, using the orthogonality relation (
2.3
), we get

\|P_{t}F\|^{2}_{2}=|\mathbb{E}[F]|^{2}+\sum_{n\geq 1}e^{-2tn}\|I_{n}(f_{n})\|^{2}_{2}\leq\|F\|_{2}^{2}
with equality when and only when
F
is a constant
or
t=0
. This gives a proof of (
2.6
) for
p=2
.
The general case can be easily
proved by using Mehler formula (see, e.g.,
[
34
, Proposition 2.8.6]
).

Using the chaos expansion, it is not difficult to show that

-D_{\bullet}L^{-1}F=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}P_{t}D_{\bullet}Fdt
,
from which, with Minkowski’s inequality and (
2.6
),
we can have

(2.7)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|D_{\bullet}L^{-1}F\|_{p}&\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}\|P_{t}D_{\bullet}F\|_{p}dt\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}\|D_{\bullet}F\|_{p}dt=\|D_{\bullet}F\|_{p}\end{aligned}

for any
p\in[1,\infty)
, whenever
D_{\bullet}F
is a function.

\bullet

Integration by parts formula and chain rule.

The divergence operator
\delta
is the adjoint operator for
D
,
which can be characterized by the following integration by part
formula:

(2.8)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\langle DF,u\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}]=\mathbb{E}[F\delta(u)]

for any
F\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
and
u\in\text{dom}(\delta)
.
Here
\text{dom}(\delta)
is the set of random vector
u\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathcal{H})

such that
there is some finite constant
C_{u}

satisfying
|\mathbb{E}[\langle DF,u\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}]|\leq C_{u}\|F\|_{2}

for any
F\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
.
It is not difficult to prove via chaos expansion that

L=-\delta D
on
\mathbb{D}^{2,2}
(see, e.g.,
[
33
, Proposition 1.4.3]
).
For
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
Lipschitz, differentiable and
F\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
,
it is known that
\phi(F)\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
with

D\phi(F)=\phi^{\prime}(F)DF
;
see
[
33
, Proposition 1.2.3]
. Then, we can easily derive
the following formula:

(2.9)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[G\phi(F)]=\mathbb{E}\big[\langle DF,-DL^{-1}G\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\phi^{\prime}(F)\big]

for any differentiable and Lipschitz function
\phi
,
F\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
, and

G\in L^{2}(\Omega)
with
\mathbb{E}[G]=0
.
Indeed, using
G=LL^{-1}G
and the above chain rule with
(
2.8
) and
L=-\delta D
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[G\phi(F)]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[-\delta DL^{-1}G\phi(F)]=\mathbb{E}[\langle-DL^{-1}G,D\phi(F)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\langle-DL^{-1}G,DF\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\phi^{\prime}(F)].

Note that taking
\phi(x)=x
, we get

(2.10)

\displaystyle{\rm Cov}(F,G)=\mathbb{E}\big[\langle DF,-DL^{-1}G\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\big]

for centered random variable
G
with finite second moment
and
F\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
.

Now we state a key bound in this note,
which arises in the so-called improved second-order
Gaussian Poincaré inequality;
see
[
39
,
4
]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

(
[
4
, Proposition 1.9]
)

Recall the notation
in (
P
).
Let
F_{1},F_{2}
be centered random variables in
\mathbb{D}^{2,4}
such that

DF_{j}\in|\mathcal{H}|
and
D^{2}F_{j}\in|\mathcal{H}^{\otimes 2}|
almost surely
for
j=1,2
,
(
i.e.,
F_{1},F_{2}
satisfy the property (
P
)
)
.
Then,

\displaystyle\textup{Var}\big(\langle DF_{1},-DL^{-1}F_{2}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\big)\lesssim\mathcal{A}(F_{1},F_{2})+\mathcal{A}(F_{2},F_{1}),

where

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathcal{A}(F_{1},F_{2})&\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{6}\times\mathbb{R}^{6d}}drdr^{\prime}dsds^{\prime}d\theta d\theta^{\prime}dzdz^{\prime}dydy^{\prime}d\textup{w}d\textup{w}^{\prime}\\
&\qquad\times\gamma_{0}(s-s^{\prime})\gamma_{0}(r-r^{\prime})\gamma_{0}(\theta-\theta^{\prime})\gamma(z-z^{\prime})\gamma(y-y^{\prime})\gamma(\textup{w}-\textup{w}^{\prime})\\
&\qquad\times\|D_{r,z}D_{\theta,\textup{w}}F_{1}\|_{4}\|D_{s,y}D_{\theta^{\prime},\textup{w}^{\prime}}F_{1}\|_{4}\|D_{r^{\prime},z^{\prime}}F_{2}\|_{4}\|D_{s^{\prime},y^{\prime}}F_{2}\|_{4}.\end{aligned}

2.2.
Proof of Proposition
1.9

We conclude this section with the proof of Proposition
1.9
.

Proof of Proposition
1.9
.

According to Proposition
1.8
,
the ASCLT holds for
\big\{F_{\theta}:\theta\geq 1\big\}
if

\displaystyle\sup_{|s|\leq T}\int_{2}^{\infty}\dfrac{\mathbb{E}\big[|\mathbf{K}_{t}(s)|^{2}\big]}{t\log t}dt<\infty

for any finite
T>0
, where
\mathbf{K}_{t}(s)
is as in (
1.12
).
Expanding
|\mathbf{K}_{t}(s)|^{2}
,
we get

\displaystyle|\mathbf{K}_{t}(s)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(\log t)^{2}}\int_{[1,t]^{2}}\frac{1}{\theta\textup{w}}\big(e^{isF_{\theta}}-e^{-\frac{s^{2}}{2}}\big)\big(e^{-isF_{\textup{w}}}-e^{-\frac{s^{2}}{2}}\big)d\theta d\textup{w}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(\log t)^{2}}\int_{[1,t]^{2}}\frac{1}{\theta\textup{w}}\big(e^{is(F_{\theta}-F_{\textup{w}})}+e^{-s^{2}}-e^{isF_{\theta}}e^{-\frac{s^{2}}{2}}-e^{-isF_{\textup{w}}}e^{-\frac{s^{2}}{2}}\big)d\theta d\textup{w}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{I}_{t}(s)-e^{-\frac{s^{2}}{2}}\text{I \hskip-7.96677pt I}_{t}(s),

where

(2.11)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathbb{I}_{t}(s)&\coloneqq\frac{1}{(\log t)^{2}}\int_{[1,t]^{2}}\frac{1}{\theta\textup{w}}\Big(e^{is(F_{\theta}-F_{\textup{w}})}-e^{-s^{2}}\Big)\,d\theta d\textup{w},\\
\text{I \hskip-7.96677pt I}_{t}(s)&\coloneqq\frac{1}{\log t}\int_{1}^{t}\frac{1}{\theta}\big(e^{isF_{\theta}}+e^{-isF_{\theta}}-2e^{-\frac{s^{2}}{2}}\big)\,d\theta.\end{aligned}

Therefore, it suffices to show that

(2.12)

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{1}(s)\coloneqq\int_{2}^{\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}\big[\mathbb{I}_{t}(s)\big]}{t\log t}dt\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbf{A}_{2}(s)\coloneqq\int_{2}^{\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}\big[\text{I \hskip-7.96677pt I}_{t}(s)\big]}{t\log t}dt,\quad s\in[-T,T]

are both uniformly bounded for any given
T>0
.

\bullet

Estimation for
\mathbf{A}_{2}
.
Recall that

\mathbb{E}[e^{isY}]=e^{-\frac{s^{2}}{2}}
with
Y\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
,
and for any real random variable
X
,

(2.13)

\displaystyle\big|\mathbb{E}[e^{isX}]-\mathbb{E}[e^{isY}]\big|=\big|\mathbb{E}[e^{isX}]-e^{-\frac{s^{2}}{2}}\big|\leq 4\,d_{\rm TV}(X,Y),

where the total-variation distance
d_{\rm TV}
is defined as in
(
1.9
). While using the local Lipschitz property
of the complex exponentials,
we have

(2.14)

\displaystyle\sup_{|s|\leq T}\big|\mathbb{E}[e^{isX}]-\mathbb{E}[e^{isY}]\big|\leq 2T\,d_{\rm Wass}(X,Y).

Therefore, it follows from (
2.13
)-(
2.14
) that

(2.15)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \Big|\mathbb{E}\big[e^{isF_{\theta}}+e^{-isF_{\theta}}-2e^{-\frac{s^{2}}{2}}\big]\Big|&\leq\min\{8\,d_{\rm TV}\big(F_{\theta},Y\big),4T\,d_{\rm Wass}(F_{\theta},Y)\}.\end{aligned}

Therefore, it follows
from (
2.11
), (
2.15
), and (
1.13
) that
for any finite
T>0
,

\displaystyle\sup\big\{|\mathbf{A}_{2}(s)|:s\in[-T,T]\big\}\lesssim\int_{2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t\log^{2}t}\int_{1}^{t}\frac{1}{\theta^{1+\beta_{1}}}d\theta dt<\infty,

that is,
\sup\{|\mathbf{A}_{2}(s)|:s\in[-T,T]\}<\infty

for any finite
T>0
.

\bullet

Estimation for
\mathbf{A}_{1}
.
Using the inequality,

\displaystyle\Big|\mathbb{E}\big[e^{is(F_{\theta}-F_{\textup{w}})}-e^{-s^{2}}\big]\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\mathbb{E}\Big[e^{i\sqrt{2}s\big(\frac{F_{\theta}-F_{\textup{w}}}{\sqrt{2}}\big)}-e^{i\sqrt{2}sY}\Big]\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\min\Big\{4\,d_{\rm TV}\Big(\tfrac{F_{\theta}-F_{\textup{w}}}{\sqrt{2}},Y\Big),2\sqrt{2}T\,d_{\rm Wass}\Big(\tfrac{F_{\theta}-F_{\textup{w}}}{\sqrt{2}},Y\Big)\Big\},

we can write, with
{\rm dist}=d_{\rm TV}
or
d_{\rm Wass}
,

\displaystyle\sup_{|s|\leq T}\mathbf{A}_{1}(s)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t(\log t)^{3}}\bigg(\int_{[1,t]^{2}}\frac{1}{\theta\textup{w}}\,{\rm dist}\big(\tfrac{F_{\theta}-F_{\textup{w}}}{\sqrt{2}},Y\big)\,d\theta d\textup{w}\bigg)dt

(2.16)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t(\log t)^{3}}\bigg(\int_{1<\theta<\textup{w}<t}\frac{1}{\theta\textup{w}}\,{\rm dist}\big(\tfrac{F_{\theta}-F_{\textup{w}}}{\sqrt{2}},Y\big)\,d\theta d\textup{w}\bigg)dt,

which is finite due to the assumption (
1.14
).

Hence the proof of Proposition
1.9
is completed.
∎

In this section, we provide the proof of our main result Theorem

According to Proposition

)
and (

)
for the spatial integrals
\widehat{F}_{R}
(

Note that

(3.1)

\displaystyle d_{\rm TV}(\widehat{F}_{\theta},Y)\leq C\theta^{-b}.

Next, we will show that there exist positive real numbers

(3.2)

\displaystyle d_{\rm TV}\Big(\tfrac{\widehat{F}_{\theta}-\widehat{F}_{\textup{w}}}{\sqrt{2}},Y\Big)\lesssim\theta^{-\beta_{1}}+(\theta/\textup{w})^{\beta_{2}}

The bound in (

Observe that the random variable

(3.3)

[

(3.4)

\displaystyle d_{\rm TV}(G,Y)\leq\sup\big|\mathbb{E}[G\phi(G)-\phi^{\prime}(G)]\big|\quad\text{with $G\coloneqq\tfrac{\widehat{F}_{\theta}-\widehat{F}_{\textup{w}}}{\sqrt{2}}$},

where the above supremum runs over bounded, differentiable functions

with

In view of (

Then, it follows from (

),

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} d_{\rm TV}\Big(\tfrac{\widehat{F}_{\theta}-\widehat{F}_{\textup{w}}}{\sqrt{2}},Y\Big)&\leq 2\big|1-V_{\theta,\textup{w}}\big|+2\mathbb{E}\big|\langle DG,-DL^{-1}G\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}-V_{\theta,\textup{w}}\big|\\
&\leq 2\big|{\rm Cov}(\widehat{F}_{\theta},\widehat{F}_{\textup{w}})\big|+\sqrt{\textup{Var}\big(\big\langle D(\widehat{F}_{\theta}-\widehat{F}_{\textup{w}}),-DL^{-1}(\widehat{F}_{\theta}-\widehat{F}_{\textup{w}})\big\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\big)}.\end{aligned}

Recalling our goal (
3.2
), it suffices to show that,

(case 1)

and

.

a_{R}\sim b_{R}
to mean

0<\liminf_{R\to+\infty}a_{R}/b_{R}\leq\limsup_{R\to+\infty}a_{R}/b_{R}<+\infty
.

\bullet

(case 1) and (case 3).

It follows from
[
36
, Theorem 1.6 for PAM]
for
(case 1)

and
[
4
, Formulas (4.8) and (4.10) for HAM]
for
(case 3)

that
there exists a
non-negative
function
\Phi_{s,t}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

(3.11)

\displaystyle\Phi_{s,t}(x-y)\coloneqq{\rm Cov}\big(u(t,x),u(s,y)\big).

As a result, taking (
3.10
) into account,
we get for
R^{\prime}\geq R>1
,

\displaystyle\big|{\rm Cov}(\widehat{F}_{R},\widehat{F}_{R^{\prime}})\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim(RR^{\prime})^{-\frac{d}{2}}\bigg|\int_{|x|<R}dx\int_{|y|<R^{\prime}}dy\,{\rm Cov}\big(u(t,x),u(t,y)\big)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq(RR^{\prime})^{-\frac{d}{2}}\int_{|x|<R}dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}dz\Phi_{t,t}(z)\lesssim\big(\tfrac{R}{R^{\prime}}\big)^{d/2}.

This
verifies (
3.6
) for
(case 1)

and
(case 3)
.

Note that in
(case 2)
and
(case 4)
,
the function
\Phi_{s,t}
in (
3.11
)
does not belong to
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
As a result, we need to carry out another approach
to settle this difficulty.

\bullet

(case 2) and (case 4).
Recall that
\widehat{F}_{R}
is a centered
random variable in
\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
. Then, using
an integration-by-part formula from
[
34
, Theorem 2.9.1]
,
we can first write

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\widehat{F}_{R}\widehat{F}_{R^{\prime}}]=\mathbb{E}\big[\langle D\widehat{F}_{R},-DL^{-1}\widehat{F}_{R^{\prime}}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\big]

(see also (
2.9
))
and then apply the definition (
2.1
) and (
2.7
)
with Fubini’s theorem and
Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to get

(3.12)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\big|\mathbb{E}[\widehat{F}_{R}\widehat{F}_{R^{\prime}}]\big|\\
&\leq\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|D_{r,y}\widehat{F}_{R}|\times|-D_{r^{\prime},y^{\prime}}L^{-1}\widehat{F}_{R^{\prime}}|\gamma_{0}(r-r^{\prime})\gamma_{1}(y-y^{\prime})drdr^{\prime}dydy^{\prime}\bigg]\\
&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\big\|D_{r,y}\widehat{F}_{R}\big\|_{2}\times\big\|D_{r^{\prime},y^{\prime}}\widehat{F}_{R^{\prime}}\big\|_{2}\gamma_{0}(r-r^{\prime})\gamma_{1}(y-y^{\prime})drdr^{\prime}dydy^{\prime}.\end{aligned}

Using the basic property of Malliavin derivative operator
and the bound in (
2.5
),
we have

(3.13)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \big\|D_{r,y}\widehat{F}_{R}\big\|_{2}&\leq\frac{1}{\sigma_{R}}\int_{|x|<R}\|D_{r,y}u(t_{0},x)\|_{2}dx\\
&\lesssim\frac{1}{\sigma_{R}}\int_{|x|<R}G_{t_{0}-r}(x-y)dx.\end{aligned}

Therefore, combining (
3.12
) with (
3.13
) leads us to

(3.14)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \big|\mathbb{E}[\widehat{F}_{R}\widehat{F}_{R^{\prime}}]\big|&\lesssim\frac{1}{\sigma_{R}\sigma_{R^{\prime}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\int_{|x|<R}\int_{|x^{\prime}|<R^{\prime}}G_{t_{0}-r}(x-y)G_{t_{0}-r^{\prime}}(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime})\\
&\quad\times\gamma_{0}(r-r^{\prime})|y-y^{\prime}|^{-\alpha}drdr^{\prime}dydy^{\prime}dxdx^{\prime},\end{aligned}

where we recall from (
3.10
) that
\sigma_{R}\sim R^{d-\tfrac{\alpha}{2}}

and from (
1.8
) that
\gamma_{1}(z)=|z|^{-\alpha}
for

\alpha\in(0,2\wedge d)
.

In the following, we
will use the Fourier analysis to get fine estimates of the above spatial integral
(
3.14
). Let us fix some notations: for an integrable function

g:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, its Fourier transform
\widehat{g}
is given by

\widehat{g}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}g(x)dx
.
Recall the expressions of wave/heat kernels
in (
1.4
) and
we record below their Fourier transforms:

(3.15)

\displaystyle\widehat{G^{H}_{t}}(\xi)=e^{-\frac{t}{2}|\xi|^{2}}\quad{\rm and}\quad\widehat{G^{W}_{t}}(\xi)=\tfrac{\sin(t|\xi|)}{|\xi|},\quad\text{for all }t>0,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Then,
using
Plancherel’s theorem, we get
from (
3.14
) with (
3.10
) that

\displaystyle\big|\mathbb{E}[\widehat{F}_{R}\widehat{F}_{R^{\prime}}]\big|\lesssim

\displaystyle(RR^{\prime})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-d}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}drdr^{\prime}\gamma_{0}(r-r^{\prime})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\xi

\displaystyle\quad\times\int_{|x|<R}dx\int_{|x^{\prime}|<R^{\prime}}dx^{\prime}e^{-i\xi\cdot(x-x^{\prime})}\widehat{G}_{t_{0}-r}(\xi)\widehat{G}_{t_{0}-r^{\prime}}(\xi)|\xi|^{\alpha-d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\tfrac{R^{\prime}}{R})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}drdr^{\prime}\gamma_{0}(r-r^{\prime})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\xi

(3.16)

\displaystyle\quad\times\int_{|x|<1}dx\int_{|x^{\prime}|<1}dx^{\prime}e^{-i\xi\cdot\big(x-\tfrac{R^{\prime}}{R}x^{\prime}\big)}\widehat{G}_{t_{0}-r}(\xi/R)\widehat{G}_{t_{0}-r^{\prime}}(\xi/R)|\xi|^{\alpha-d},

where in the last equality, we performed
a change of variable
(x,x^{\prime},\xi)\mapsto(Rx,R^{\prime}x^{\prime},\xi/R)
.

Next, we further bound (
3.16
) in
(case 2)
and
(case 4)
separately.
For
(case 2)
, recalling (
3.15
),
and using

\displaystyle|\xi|^{-2\beta}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\theta e^{-\theta|\xi|^{2}}\theta^{\beta-1}

with
\beta=\tfrac{d-\alpha}{2}>0
,
and making a change of variables

(t_{0}-r,t_{0}-r^{\prime})\mapsto(r,r^{\prime})
,
we can get from (
3.16
)
that

(3.17)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \big|\mathbb{E}[\widehat{F}_{R}\widehat{F}_{R^{\prime}}]\big|\lesssim&(\tfrac{R}{R^{\prime}})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}drdr^{\prime}\gamma_{0}(r-r^{\prime})\int_{|x|,|x^{\prime}|<1}dxdx^{\prime}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\theta\theta^{\frac{d-\alpha-2}{2}}\\
&\qquad\times\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\xi e^{-i(x-\tfrac{R^{\prime}}{R}x^{\prime})\cdot\xi-(\theta+\frac{r+r^{\prime}}{2R^{2}})|\xi|^{2}}\\
=&(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}(\tfrac{R}{R^{\prime}})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}drdr^{\prime}\gamma_{0}(r-r^{\prime})\int_{|x|,|x^{\prime}|<1}dxdx^{\prime}\\
&\qquad\times\int_{0}^{\infty}d\theta\theta^{\frac{d-\alpha-2}{2}}\big(\theta+\tfrac{r+r^{\prime}}{2R^{2}}\big)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\exp\Big(-\tfrac{|x-\frac{R^{\prime}}{R}x^{\prime}|^{2}}{4(\theta+\frac{r+r^{\prime}}{2R^{2}})}\Big),\end{aligned}

where in the last step, we use the Fourier transform of the heat kernel.

To deal with the integration in
\theta
from (
3.17
), we decompose
the region
(0,\infty)
into two segments
(0,\frac{r+r^{\prime}}{2R^{2}})

and
(\frac{r+r^{\prime}}{2R^{2}},\infty)
.
Since
r,r^{\prime}\in(0,t)
, it is easy to deduce that

(3.18)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\int_{0}^{\frac{r+r^{\prime}}{2R^{2}}}d\theta\theta^{\frac{d-\alpha-2}{2}}\big(\theta+\tfrac{r+r^{\prime}}{2R^{2}}\big)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\exp\Big(-\tfrac{|x-\frac{R^{\prime}}{R}x^{\prime}|^{2}}{4(\theta+\frac{r+r^{\prime}}{2R^{2}})}\Big)\\
\lesssim&\big(\tfrac{R^{2}}{r+r^{\prime}}\big)^{\frac{d}{2}}\exp\Big(-\tfrac{|x-\tfrac{R^{\prime}}{R}x^{\prime}|^{2}}{4(r+r^{\prime})/R^{2}}\Big)\int_{0}^{\frac{r+r^{\prime}}{2R^{2}}}d\theta\theta^{\frac{d-\alpha-2}{2}}\qquad\text{with $\tfrac{d-\alpha-2}{2}>-1$}\\
\lesssim&\big(\tfrac{R^{2}}{r+r^{\prime}}\big)^{\frac{d}{2}}\exp\Big(-\tfrac{|x-\tfrac{R^{\prime}}{R}x^{\prime}|^{2}}{4(r+r^{\prime})/R^{2}}\Big)\big(\tfrac{r+r^{\prime}}{2R^{2}}\big)^{\frac{d-\alpha}{2}}\\
=&\big(\tfrac{R^{2}}{r+r^{\prime}}\big)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\exp\Big(-\tfrac{|x-\tfrac{R^{\prime}}{R}x^{\prime}|^{2}}{4(r+r^{\prime})/R^{2}}\Big)\lesssim|x-\tfrac{R^{\prime}}{R}x^{\prime}|^{-\alpha},\end{aligned}

and

(3.19)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{\frac{r+r^{\prime}}{2R^{2}}}^{\infty}d\theta\theta^{\frac{d-\alpha-2}{2}}\big(\theta+\tfrac{r+r^{\prime}}{2R^{2}}\big)^{-\frac{d}{2}}&\exp\Big(-\tfrac{|x-\frac{R^{\prime}}{R}x^{\prime}|^{2}}{4(\theta+\frac{r+r^{\prime}}{2R^{2}})}\Big)\leq\int_{\frac{r+r^{\prime}}{2R^{2}}}^{\infty}d\theta\theta^{\frac{-\alpha-2}{2}}e^{-\tfrac{|x-\frac{R^{\prime}}{R}x^{\prime}|^{2}}{8\theta}}\\
\leq&\int_{0}^{\infty}d\theta\theta^{\frac{-\alpha-2}{2}}e^{-\tfrac{|x-\frac{R^{\prime}}{R}x^{\prime}|^{2}}{8\theta}}\lesssim|x-\tfrac{R^{\prime}}{R}x^{\prime}|^{-\alpha}.\end{aligned}

Therefore, we deduce from (
3.17
), (
3.18
), and (
3.19
)
with the local integrability of
\gamma_{0}

that

(3.20)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \big|\mathbb{E}[\widehat{F}_{R}\widehat{F}_{R^{\prime}}]\big|&\lesssim(\tfrac{R}{R^{\prime}})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}drdr^{\prime}\gamma_{0}(r-r^{\prime})\int_{|x|,|x^{\prime}|<1}dxdx^{\prime}|x-\tfrac{R^{\prime}}{R}x^{\prime}|^{-\alpha}\\
&=(\tfrac{R}{R^{\prime}})^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\int_{|x|<1}dx\int_{|x^{\prime}|<1}dx^{\prime}|\tfrac{R}{R^{\prime}}x-x^{\prime}|^{-\alpha}\lesssim(\tfrac{R}{R^{\prime}})^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}},\end{aligned}

where for the last step, we used the following elementary fact that

(3.21)

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{|x|<1}|z-x|^{-\beta}dx<\infty,\quad\forall\beta\in(0,d).

Thus, the proof of (
3.6
) in
(case 2)
is complete.

Now let us deal with
(case 4)
. For the wave kernel in
\mathbb{R}^{d}

with
d\leq 2
, it enjoys the following
property that with
\mathbf{1}_{R}(x)\coloneqq\mathbf{1}_{\{|x|<R\}}
,

(3.22)

\displaystyle(\mathbf{1}_{R}\ast G_{r})(y)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbf{1}_{\{|x|<R\}}G_{r}(x-y)dx\leq r\mathbf{1}_{R+r}(y),

which follows from the definition (
1.4
) of the wave kernel;
see also
[
10
, Lemma 2.1]
.
Note that the Fourier transform of
\mathbf{1}_{R}
is a
real-valued and rotationally symmetric function (see, e.g.,
[
36
, Lemma 2.1]
).
Then, using Plancherel’s theorem and some Fourier calculations,
we derive from (
3.14
) with (
3.10
) and
a change of variables
(t_{0}-r,t_{0}-r^{\prime})\to(r,r^{\prime})

that

\displaystyle\big|\mathbb{E}[\widehat{F}_{R}\widehat{F}_{R^{\prime}}]\big|\lesssim(RR^{\prime})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-d}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}drdr^{\prime}\gamma_{0}(r-r^{\prime})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\xi|\xi|^{\alpha-d}\widehat{G}_{r}(\xi)\widehat{G}_{r^{\prime}}(\xi)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\times\bigg(\int_{|x|<R}dx\int_{|x^{\prime}|<R^{\prime}}dx^{\prime}e^{-i(x-x^{\prime})\cdot\xi}\bigg)

\displaystyle=(RR^{\prime})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-d}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}drdr^{\prime}\gamma_{0}(r-r^{\prime})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\xi|\xi|^{\alpha-d}\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{R}}(\xi)\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{R^{\prime}}}(\xi)\widehat{G}_{r}(\xi)\widehat{G}_{r^{\prime}}(\xi)

\displaystyle=C_{\alpha,d}(RR^{\prime})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-d}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}drdr^{\prime}\gamma_{0}(r-r^{\prime})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}(\mathbf{1}_{R}\ast G_{r})(y)(\mathbf{1}_{R^{\prime}}\ast G_{r^{\prime}})(y^{\prime})|y-y^{\prime}|^{-\alpha}dydy^{\prime},

where the constant
C_{\alpha,d}
comes from inverting the
Fourier transform of the spectral measure
|\xi|^{\alpha-d}d\xi
.
Now applying the inequality (
3.22
) with
r,r^{\prime}\in(0,t_{0})

and utilizing the local integrability of
\gamma_{0}
,
we get

\displaystyle\big|\mathbb{E}[\widehat{F}_{R}\widehat{F}_{R^{\prime}}]\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim t_{0}^{2}(RR^{\prime})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-d}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}drdr^{\prime}\gamma_{0}(r-r^{\prime})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\mathbf{1}_{R+t_{0}}(y)\mathbf{1}_{R^{\prime}+t_{0}}(y^{\prime})|y-y^{\prime}|^{-\alpha}dydy^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim(RR^{\prime})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\mathbf{1}_{R+t_{0}}(y)\mathbf{1}_{R^{\prime}+t_{0}}(y^{\prime})|y-y^{\prime}|^{-\alpha}dydy^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim(RR^{\prime})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-d}(R+t_{0})^{d}(R^{\prime}+t_{0})^{d-\alpha}\lesssim(\tfrac{R}{R^{\prime}})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}},

where we obtained the last second step with the same argument as in
(
3.20
)-(
3.21
).
Hence, the proof of (
3.6
) in
(case 4)
is finished.
∎

3.2.
Proof of (
3.9
)

To prove (
3.9
), we first apply
Proposition
2.2
with
the bounds in (
2.5
)
and (
3.13
):
with
B_{R}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|x|<R\}
,
we need to bound

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\big(\widehat{F}_{R},\widehat{F}_{R^{\prime}}\big)\lesssim

\displaystyle\sigma_{R}^{-2}\sigma_{R^{\prime}}^{-2}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}dr\int_{0}^{r}d\theta\int_{0}^{t_{0}}dr^{\prime}\int_{0}^{r^{\prime}}d\theta^{\prime}\int_{[0,t]^{2}}dsds^{\prime}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6d}}dzdz^{\prime}dydy^{\prime}d\textup{w}d\textup{w}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\gamma_{0}(s-s^{\prime})\gamma_{0}(r-r^{\prime})\gamma_{0}(\theta-\theta^{\prime})\gamma(z-z^{\prime})\gamma(y-y^{\prime})\gamma(\textup{w}-\textup{w}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\qquad\times\int_{B_{R}^{2}}dx_{1}dx_{2}G_{t-r}(x_{1}-z)G_{r-\theta}(z-\textup{w})G_{t-r^{\prime}}(x_{2}-z^{\prime})G_{r^{\prime}-\theta^{\prime}}(z^{\prime}-\textup{w}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\qquad\times\int_{B_{R^{\prime}}^{2}}dx_{3}dx_{4}G_{t-r^{\prime}}(x_{3}-y)G_{t-s^{\prime}}(x_{4}-y^{\prime}).

In
(case 1)
, we simply enlarge the region
B_{R}
to
B_{R^{\prime}}
.
Then, applying the estimates for
\mathcal{A}^{*}

appearing in
[
35
, Section 3.1.1]
and
recalling from (
3.10
) that
\sigma_{R}=\sigma_{R}(t_{0})\sim R^{\frac{d}{2}}
,
one can easily conclude that

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\big(\widehat{F}_{R},\widehat{F}_{R^{\prime}}\big)\lesssim R^{-d}.

For
(case 2)
, we follow the idea employed in
[
35
, Section 3.1.2]
.
That is, for i.i.d. standard normal random variables
Z_{1},\dots,Z_{6}
,
we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}\coloneqq

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}^{4}}dx_{1}\cdots dx_{4}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big|\frac{\sqrt{t-r}}{R}Z_{1}-\frac{\sqrt{r-\theta}}{R}Z_{2}-\frac{\sqrt{t-s}}{R}Z_{3}+\frac{\sqrt{s-\theta^{\prime}}}{R}Z_{4}+x_{1}-x_{2}\Big|^{-\alpha}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\times\Big|\frac{\sqrt{t-s}}{R}Z_{3}-\frac{\sqrt{t-s^{\prime}}}{R^{\prime}}Z_{6}+x_{2}-x_{4}\Big|^{-\alpha}\times\Big|\frac{\sqrt{t-r}}{R}Z_{1}-\frac{\sqrt{t-r^{\prime}}}{R^{\prime}}Z_{5}+x_{1}-x_{3}\Big|^{-\alpha}\bigg]

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle 1,

and thus, the expression

\displaystyle S_{R}=

\displaystyle S_{R}(t,r,r^{\prime},s,s^{\prime},\theta,\theta^{\prime})

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}^{2}}dx_{1}dx_{2}\int_{B_{R^{\prime}}^{2}}dx_{3}dx_{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6d}}dzdz^{\prime}dydy^{\prime}d\textup{w}d\textup{w}^{\prime}\gamma(z-z^{\prime})\gamma(y-y^{\prime})\gamma(\textup{w}-\textup{w}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\quad\times G_{t-r}(x_{1}-z)G_{r-\theta}(z-\textup{w})G_{t-r^{\prime}}(x_{2}-z^{\prime})G_{r^{\prime}-\theta^{\prime}}(z^{\prime}-\textup{w}^{\prime})G_{t-r^{\prime}}(x_{3}-y)G_{t-s^{\prime}}(x_{4}-y^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R^{2d-2\alpha}(R^{\prime})^{2d-\alpha}\mathcal{K}\lesssim R^{2d-2\alpha}(R^{\prime})^{2d-\alpha}.

Then, with
\sigma_{R}\sim R^{d-\frac{\alpha}{2}}
, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\big(\widehat{F}_{R},\widehat{F}_{R^{\prime}}\big)\lesssim\sigma_{R}^{-2}\sigma_{R^{\prime}}^{-2}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}dr\int_{0}^{r}d\theta\int_{0}^{t_{0}}dr^{\prime}\int_{0}^{r^{\prime}}d\theta^{\prime}\int_{[0,t]^{2}}dsds^{\prime}S_{R}\lesssim R^{-\alpha}.

Turning to the cases for (
HAM
), we first consider

(case 3)
. Just like
(case 1)
,
with extending the integrating region in
x_{1},x_{2}
to
B_{R^{\prime}}
,
applying the estimates for
\mathcal{A}_{R}
in
[
4
, Page 809]
,
we have

\mathcal{A}\big(\widehat{F}_{R},\widehat{F}_{R^{\prime}}\big)\lesssim R^{-d}
;
and for
(case 4)
, following a similar argument as in
[
4
, Section 4.2.2]
, one can deduce that

\mathcal{A}\big(\widehat{F}_{R},\widehat{F}_{R^{\prime}}\big)\lesssim R^{-\alpha}.

The estimates for
\mathcal{A}(\widehat{F}_{R^{\prime}},\widehat{F}_{R})

are also very similar and thus omitted here. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\big(\widehat{F}_{R},\widehat{F}_{R^{\prime}}\big)+\mathcal{A}\big(\widehat{F}_{R^{\prime}},\widehat{F}_{R}\big)\lesssim\begin{cases}R^{-d},&\text{{(case 1)} and {(case 3)} }\\
(R^{\prime})^{-\alpha}<R^{-\alpha},&\text{{(case 2)} and {(case 4)} }.\end{cases}

The proof of (
3.9
) is then complete by invoking Proposition
2.2
.
∎

1.
Introduction

The classical (single-function) Strichartz estimates for the free Schrödinger propagator
e^{it\Delta}
of initial data
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
on the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, are stated as follows:

\|e^{it\Delta}f\|_{L^{2q}(\mathbb{R},L^{2p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

(1.1)

for all spatial dimensions
n\geq 1
and Schrödinger admissible pairs
(p,q)
satisfying
p,q\geq 1
,
\frac{2}{q}+\frac{n}{p}=n
and
(q,p,n)\neq(1,\infty,2)
, see
[
39
]
. The proof of these estimates is a combination of an abstract functional analysis argument known as the
TT^{*}
duality argument and an
L^{1}\rightarrow L^{\infty}
dispersive estimate, except the endpoint case
(q,p)=(1,\frac{n}{n-2})
for
n\geq 3
, which was proved by Keel-Tao
[
25
]
.

By a scaling argument and the Sobolev embedding theorem, it is well known that under the following necessary condition

\frac{2}{q}+\frac{n}{p}=n-2s\text{ and }s\in[0,\frac{n}{2}),

the estimate (
1.1
) reduces into the following homogeneous Sobolev Strichartz estimate

\|e^{it\Delta}f\|_{L^{2q}(\mathbb{R},L^{2p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

In fact, an alternative approach to obtain such estimates in the homogeneous Sobolev space is based on the Littlewood–Paley theory. More precisely, first one can establish the estimates of the form (
1.1
) for initial data
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

that are frequency localized to annuli and then upgrade to general data by putting together the estimates for each dyadic piece by the Minkowski inequality and the Littlewood–Paley square function theorem.

In recent years, a significant attention has been devoted by numerous researchers to investigate single-function Strichartz estimates to versions involving systems of orthonormal functions taking the form

\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}|e^{itL}f_{j}|^{2}\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\lesssim\|\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\|_{\ell^{\alpha}},

(1.2)

for families of orthonormal data
\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
in a given Hilbert space, such as the Lebesgue space
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
or the homogeneous Sobolev space
\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Here
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\in\ell^{\alpha}
and
q,p,\alpha\in[1,\infty]
satisfying some particular
conditions. We now briefly summarize some particular known results of the form (
1.2
).

The orthonormal Strichartz estimates of the form (
1.2
) are important, particularly from two different perspectives. First, it represents a refinement of the classical Strichartz estimates for a single function, which have been utilized in the analysis of nonlinear Schrödinger equations for the last five decades. Noting that by taking

f_{j}=0
for all
j\geq 2
in the orthonormal Strichartz estimates (
1.2
), we recover the classical single-function version. Second, the estimates play a crucial role in the analysis of the Hartree equations, which describe systems of infinitely many fermions. This was first investigated in
[
37
]
, where local and global well-posedness was established under various conditions. Note that the idea of generalizing classical inequalities from a single-function input to an orthonormal family is not a new topic, and the first pioneering work of this kind of generalization can be traced back to the work of Lieb-Thirring
[
26
,
27
]
and this Lieb-Thirring inequality generalizes extended versions of certain Gagliardo–Nirenberg–Sobolev inequalities, which is considered to be one of the most important tools for the study of the stability of matter
[
26
]
.

Motivated by the recent works of Bez-Hong-Lee-Nakamura-Sawano in
[
2
]
, the main aim of this paper is to investigate the orthonormal Strichartz estimates for the Schrödinger equation associated with the Dunkl-Laplacian
\Delta_{\kappa}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with initial data from the homogeneous Sobolev space
\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Note that the Dunkl operators are differential-difference operators that generalize the standard partial derivatives. These operators were introduced by Charles Dunkl
[
8
]
using a finite reflection group and a parameter function on its root system. Applications of Dunkl operators in mathematics and mathematical physics are numerous. For example, they play an important role in the integration of quantum many-body problems of the Calogero-Moser-Sutherland type and are employed in the study of probabilistic processes. Additionally, these operators have had a lasting impression on the study of special functions in one and multiple variables as well as orthogonal polynomials, see
[
10
]
.

Remark 1.6
.

The key novelty of this paper can be seen as follows:

•

The Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality plays a crucial role in the proof of Theorem
1.1
. To sharpen the inequalities in Theorem
1.1
for functions in Lebesgue spaces, we have to employ its refined form for functions in Lorentz spaces.

•

In the proof of the frequency localized estimates stated in
Theorem
1.4
, for a function
\Psi
defined as
\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}\Psi(\xi)=\psi^{2}(|\xi|)
, one of the crucial step is to observe that applying the Dunkl translation to
e^{i(t-s)\Delta_{\kappa}}\Psi(-y)
yields an estimate of the form

\left|\tau_{x}e^{i(t-s)\Delta_{\kappa}}\Psi(-y)\right|\leq\frac{C_{\psi,M}}{\left(1+\big||x|-|y|\big|\right)^{M}}.

In contrast to the classical Laplacian setting, where the Young’s convolution inequality can be applied, to deal with the integral

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|W_{1}(x,t)|^{2}|W_{2}(y,s)|^{2}}{\left(1+\big||x|-|y|\big|\right)^{M}}h_{\kappa}(x)h_{\kappa}(y)dxdy,

for
W_{1},W_{2}\in L^{4}(\mathbb{R},L_{\kappa}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
,
we require a refined inequality analogous to Young’s convolution inequality, which can be expressed as:
Let
1\leq p,q,r\leq\infty
satisfy
1+\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}
. Suppose
f=\tilde{f}(|\cdot|)
is a radial function in
L_{\kappa}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
g\in L_{\kappa}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then the function

G(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\tilde{f}\left(\big||x|-|y|\big|\right)g(y)h_{\kappa}(y)dy,

is a radial function in
L_{\kappa}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfying

\|G\|_{L_{\kappa}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|g\|_{L_{\kappa}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

•

Since the Dunkl Laplacian
\Delta_{\kappa}
coincides with the classical Euclidean Laplacian
\Delta

e^{it\Delta}

2.1.
Dunkl operators

The basic ingredient in the theory of Dunkl operators are root systems and finite reflection groups. We start this section with the definition of a root system.

Let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denote the standard Euclidean scalar product in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we denote
|x|
as
|x|=\langle x,x\rangle^{1/2}
.
For
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\},
we denote
r_{\alpha}
as the reflection with respect to the hyperplane
\langle\alpha\rangle^{\perp}
, orthogonal to
\alpha
and is defined by

r_{\alpha}(x):=x-2\frac{\langle\alpha,x\rangle}{|\alpha|^{2}}\alpha,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

A finite set
\mathcal{R}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
is said to be a root system if the following holds:

(1)

r_{\alpha}(\mathcal{R})=\mathcal{R}
for all
\alpha\in\mathcal{R}
,

(2)

\mathcal{R}\cap\mathbb{R}\alpha=\{\pm\alpha\}
for all
\alpha\in\mathcal{R}
.

For a given root system
\mathcal{R}
, the subgroup
G\subset O(n,\mathbb{R})
generated by the reflections
\left\{r_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\mathcal{R}\right\}
is called the finite Coxeter group associated with
\mathcal{R}
. The dimension of
span\mathcal{R}
is called the rank of
\mathcal{R}
. For a detailed study on the theory of finite reflection groups, we refer to
[
21
]
. Let
\mathcal{R}^{+}:=\{\alpha\in\mathcal{R}:\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle>0\}
for some
\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\bigcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{R}}\langle\alpha\rangle^{\perp}
, be a fixed positive root system.

Some typical examples of such a system are the Weyl groups such as the symmetric group
S_{n}
for the type
A_{n-1}
root system and the hyperoctahedral group for the type
B_{n}
root system. In addition,
H_{3},H_{4}
(icosahedral groups), and
I_{2}(n)
(symmetry group of the regular
n
-gon) are also Coxeter groups.

A multiplicity function for
G
is a function
\kappa:\mathcal{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
which is constant on
G
-orbits. Setting
\kappa_{\alpha}:=\kappa(\alpha)
for
\alpha\in\mathcal{R},
from the definition of
G
-invariant, we have
\kappa_{g\alpha}=\kappa_{\alpha}
for all
g\in G
. We say
\kappa
is non-negative if
\kappa_{\alpha}\geq 0
for all
\alpha\in\mathcal{R}
. The
\mathbb{C}
-vector space of non-negative multiplicity functions on
\mathcal{R}
is denoted by
\mathcal{K}^{+}
. Let us denote
\gamma
as
\gamma=\gamma(\kappa):=\sum\limits_{\alpha\in\mathcal{R}^{+}}\kappa(\alpha)
. For convenience, we denote
N=2\gamma+n
throughout the paper.

For
\xi\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
\kappa\in\mathcal{K}^{+},
Dunkl in 1989 introduced a family of first-order differential-difference operators
T_{\xi}:=T_{\xi}(\kappa)
by

\displaystyle T_{\xi}(\kappa)f(x):=\partial_{\xi}f(x)+\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{R}^{+}}\kappa_{\alpha}\langle\alpha,\xi\rangle\frac{f(x)-f\left(r_{\alpha}x\right)}{\langle\alpha,x\rangle},\quad f\in C^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right),

(2.1)

where
\partial_{\xi}
denotes the directional derivative corresponding to
\xi
. The operator
T_{\xi}
defined in (
2.1
), formally known as Dunkl operator and is one of the most important developments in the theory of special functions associated with root systems
[
7
]
. They commute pairwise and are skew-symmetric with respect to the
G
-invariant measure
h_{\kappa}(x)dx
, where the weight function

h_{\kappa}(x):=\prod\limits_{\alpha\in\mathcal{R}^{+}}|\langle\alpha,x\rangle|^{2\kappa_{\alpha}}

is of
homogeneous degree
2\gamma
. Thanks to the
G
-invariance of the multiplicity function, the definition of the Dunkl operator is independent of the choice of the positive subsystem
\mathcal{R}^{+}
. In
[
8
]
, it is shown that for any
\kappa\in\mathcal{K}^{+}
, there is a unique linear isomorphism
V_{\kappa}
(Dunkl’s intertwining operator) on the space
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
of polynomials on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

(1)

V_{\kappa}\left(\mathcal{P}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)=\mathcal{P}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

(2)

V_{\kappa}|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=id
,

(3)

T_{\xi}(\kappa)V_{\kappa}=V_{\kappa}\partial_{\xi}
,

where
\mathcal{P}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denotes the space of homogeneous polynomials of degree
m
.

For any finite reflection group
G
and any
k\in\mathcal{K}^{+}
, Rösler in
[
34
]
proved that there exists a unique positive Radon probability measure
\rho_{x}^{\kappa}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

V_{\kappa}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(\xi)d\rho_{x}^{\kappa}(\xi).

(2.2)

The measure
\rho_{x}^{k}
depends on
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and its support is contained in the ball
B\left(\|x\|\right):=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\|\xi\|\leq\|x\|\}
. In view of the Laplace type representation (
2.2
), Dunkl’s intertwining operator
V_{\kappa}
can be extended to a larger class of function spaces.

Let
\{\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\cdots,\xi_{n}\}
be an orthonormal basis of
(\mathbb{R}^{n},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
. Then the Dunkl Laplacian operator
\Delta_{\kappa}
is defined as

\Delta_{\kappa}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}T^{2}_{\xi_{j}}(\kappa).

The definition of
\Delta_{\kappa}
is independent of the choice of the orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}.
In fact, one can see that the operator
\Delta_{\kappa}
also can be expressed as

\Delta_{\kappa}f(x)=\Delta f(x)+\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{R}^{+}}\kappa_{\alpha}\left\{\frac{2\langle\nabla f(x),\alpha\rangle}{\langle\alpha,x\rangle}-|\alpha|^{2}\frac{f(x)-f(r_{\alpha}x)}{\langle\alpha,x\rangle^{2}}\right\},\quad f\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

where
\nabla
and
\Delta
are the usual gradiant and Laplacian operator on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, respectively. Observe that, for
\kappa\equiv 0
, the Dunkl Laplacian operator
\Delta_{\kappa}
reduces to the classical Euclidean Laplacian
\Delta
.

For every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the system

\begin{cases}T_{\xi}u(x,y)=\langle y,\xi\rangle\,u(x,y),\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
u(0,y)=1,\end{cases}

admits a unique analytic solution on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which we denote as
K(x,y)
and is known as the Dunkl kernel in general. The kernel has a unique holomorphic extension to
\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}
and is given by

K(x,y)=V_{\kappa}(e^{\langle\cdot,y\rangle})(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{\langle\xi,y\rangle}d\rho_{x}^{\kappa}(\xi),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

When
\kappa\equiv 0
, the Dunkl kernel
K(x,y)
reduces to the exponential
e^{\langle x,y\rangle}
. For
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
z,w\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, the Dunkl kernel satisfies the following properties:

(i)

K(z,w)=K(w,z)
and
K(\lambda z,w)=K(z,\lambda w),\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
;

(ii)

for any
\nu\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, we have

|D_{z}^{\nu}K(x,z)|\leq|x|^{|\nu|}e^{|Re(z)||x|},

where
D_{z}^{\nu}=\frac{\partial^{|\nu|}}{\partial{z_{1}}^{\nu_{1}}\cdots{z_{n}}^{\nu_{n}}}
and
|\nu|=\nu_{1}+\cdots+\nu_{n}
. In particular, for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

|K(x,iy)|\leq 1;

(iii)

K(ix,y)=\overline{K(-ix,y)}
and
K(gx,gy)=K(x,y),\quad g\in G
.

2.2.
Dunkl transform

For
1\leq p<\infty
, let
L_{\kappa}^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
be the space of
L^{p}
-functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with respect to the weight
h_{\kappa}(x)
with the
L_{\kappa}^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
-norm

\|f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)|^{p}h_{\kappa}(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

and
\|f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}=ess\sup\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)|
.
The Dunkl transform is defined on
L_{\kappa}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
by

\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}f(y)=\frac{1}{c_{\kappa}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)K(x,-iy)h_{\kappa}(x)dx,

(2.3)

where
c_{\kappa}
is the Mehta-type constant defined by

c_{\kappa}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{2}}h_{\kappa}(x)dx.

When
\kappa\equiv 0
, the Dunkl transform coincides with the classical Fourier transform. The Dunkl transform plays the same role as the Fourier transform in classical Fourier analysis and enjoys properties similar to those of the classical Fourier transform. Here we list some basic properties of the Dunkl transform (see
[
9
,
22
,
6
]
).

(i)

For all
f\in L_{\kappa}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
, we have

\|\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}\leq\frac{1}{c_{\kappa}}\|f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}.

(ii)

For any function
f
in the Schwartz space
\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
, we have

\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}(T_{\xi}f)(y)=i\langle\xi,y\rangle\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}f(y),\quad y,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

In particular, it follows that

\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}(\Delta_{\kappa}f)(y)=-|y|^{2}\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}f(y),\quad y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(iii)

For all
f\in L_{\kappa}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
, we have

\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}(f(\cdot/\lambda))(y)=\lambda^{N}\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}f(\lambda y),\quad y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

for all
\lambda>0
.

(iv)

The Dunkl transform
\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}
is a homeomorphism of the Schwartz space
\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
and its inverse is given by
\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}^{-1}g(x)=\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}g(-x),
for all
g\in\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
. In addition, for all
f\in\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
, it satisfies

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)|^{2}h_{\kappa}(x)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}f(y)|^{2}h_{\kappa}(y)dy.

In particular, the Dunkl transform extends to an isometric isomorphism on
L_{\kappa}^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
. The Dunkl transform can be extended to the space of tempered distributions
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
and is also a homeomorphism of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
.

(v)

For every
f\in L_{\kappa}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
such that
\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}f\in L_{\kappa}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
, we have the inversion formula

f(x)=\frac{1}{c_{\kappa}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}f(y)K(ix,y)h_{\kappa}(y)dy,\quad a.e.\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(vi)

If
f
is a radial function in
L_{\kappa}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
such that
f(x)=\tilde{f}(|x|)
, then

\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}f(y)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(N/2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\tilde{f}(r)\frac{J_{\frac{N-2}{2}}(r|y|)}{\left(r|y|\right)^{\frac{N-2}{2}}}r^{N-1}dr,

(2.4)

where
J_{\nu}
denotes the Bessel function of order
\nu>-\frac{1}{2}
.

(vii)

For
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, the homogeneous Dunkl-Sobolev space
\dot{H}_{\kappa}^{p,s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
is the set of tempered distributions
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
such that
\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\kappa}\left(|\cdot|^{s}\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}f\right)\in L_{\kappa}^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
and is equiped with the norm

\|u\|_{\dot{H}_{\kappa}^{p,s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}=\left\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\kappa}\left(|\cdot|^{s}\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}f\right)\right\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}.

In particular, when
p=2
, we denote
\dot{H}_{\kappa}^{p,s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
by
\dot{H}_{\kappa}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
in short and
\dot{H}_{\kappa}^{0}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)=L_{\kappa}^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
.

For any
1\leq p_{1}\leq p_{2}\leq\infty
and
s_{1}\leq s_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
s_{1}-\frac{N}{p_{1}}=s_{2}-\frac{N}{p_{2}}
, we also have the continuous inclusion:

\dot{H}_{\kappa}^{p_{1},s_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq\dot{H}_{\kappa}^{p_{2},s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

(2.5)

2.3.
Dunkl convolution operator

For given
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the Dunkl translation operator
f\mapsto\tau_{x}f
is defined on
\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
by

\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}(\tau_{x}f)(y)=K(x,iy)\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}f(y),\quad y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

At the moment, the explicit formula of the Dunkl translation operator is known only in two cases. One is when
f(x)=\tilde{f}(|x|)
is a continuous radial function in
L_{\kappa}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the Dunkl translation operator is represented by (see
[
34
,
5
]
)

\tau_{x}f(y)=V_{\kappa}[\tilde{f}\left(\sqrt{|y|^{2}+|x|^{2}-2\langle y,\cdot\rangle}\right)](x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\tilde{f}\left(\sqrt{|y|^{2}+|x|^{2}-2\langle y,\xi\rangle}\right)d\rho^{\kappa}_{x}(\xi).

(2.6)

The Dunkl translation operator can also be extended to the space of tempered distributions
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
. It turns out to be rather difficult to extend important results in classical Fourier analysis to the setting of the Dunkl transform in general. One of the difficulties comes from the fact that the Dunkl translation operator is not positive in
general. In fact, even the
L_{\kappa}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
-boundedness of
\tau_{x}
is not established in general. On the other hand, when
f
is a radial function in
L_{\kappa}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
, the following holds (see
[
18
,
40
]
)

\|\tau_{x}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Using the Dunkl translation operator, we define the Dunkl convolution of functions
f,g\in\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
by

f*_{\kappa}g(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\tau_{x}f(-y)g(y)h_{\kappa}(y)dy,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

The Dunkl convolution satisfies the following properties (see
[
40
]
).

(1)

\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}(f*_{\kappa}g)=\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}(f)\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}(g)
,
\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\kappa}(f*_{\kappa}g)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\kappa}(f)\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\kappa}(g)
, and
f*_{\kappa}g=g*_{\kappa}f
.

(2)

Young’s inequality: Let
1\leq p,q,r\leq\infty
be such that
1+\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}
. If
f\in L^{p}_{\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
g
is a radial function of
L_{\kappa}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
f*_{\kappa}g\in L_{\kappa}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and we have

\|f*_{\kappa}g\|_{L_{\kappa}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|g\|_{L_{\kappa}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(2.7)

2.4.
Dunkl-Schrödinger semigroup

The Dunkl-Schrödinger group
e^{it\Delta_{\kappa}}
is unitary on
L^{2}_{\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
define by

e^{it\Delta_{\kappa}}f(x)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\kappa}\left(e^{-it|\cdot|^{2}}\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}f\right)(x)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\kappa}\left(e^{-it|\cdot|^{2}}\right)*_{\kappa}f(x)=K_{it}*_{\kappa}f(x),

where the Dunkl-Schrödinger kernel

K_{it}(x)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\kappa}\left(e^{-it|\cdot|^{2}}\right)(x)=\frac{1}{c_{\kappa}|t|^{\frac{N}{2}}}e^{-i\frac{N\pi}{4}sgnt}e^{i\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t}}.

(2.8)

Noting that the Dunkl-Schrödinger kernel
K_{it}
is radial, from (
2.6
), we have

\left|\tau_{x}K_{it}(y)\right|\leq\frac{1}{c_{\kappa}|t|^{\frac{N}{2}}},\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(2.9)

If
f\in L_{\kappa}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have the dispersive estimate

\|e^{it\Delta_{\kappa}}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\frac{1}{c_{\kappa}|t|^{\frac{N}{2}}}\|f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

As the Dunkl-Schrödinger operator
e^{it\Delta_{\kappa}}
is unitary on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, combined with the above dispersive estimate, for any
2\leq p\leq\infty
, interpolation gives

\|e^{it\Delta_{\kappa}}g\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\frac{1}{|t|^{\frac{N}{2p^{\prime}}}}\|g\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(2.10)

By Theorem
1.1
, for
p,q\geq 1
,
(q,p,N)\neq(1,\infty,2)
and
\frac{2}{q}+\frac{N}{p}=N
, the Dunkl-Schrödinger operator
e^{it\Delta_{\kappa}}
is bounded from
L_{\kappa}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{2q}(\mathbb{R},L_{\kappa}^{2p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
. By duality, the adjoint operator
\left(e^{it\Delta_{k}}\right)^{*}
of the Dunkl-Schrödinger operator
e^{it\Delta_{\kappa}}
defined by

\left(e^{it\Delta_{k}}\right)^{*}g(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-it\Delta_{\kappa}}g(x,t)dt,

is bounded from
L^{(2q)^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R},L_{\kappa}^{(2p)^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
to
L_{\kappa}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and the composition of the maps
e^{it\Delta_{\kappa}}
and
\left(e^{it\Delta_{k}}\right)^{*}
defined by

e^{it\Delta_{k}}\left(e^{it\Delta_{k}}\right)^{*}g(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i(t-s)\Delta_{\kappa}}g(x,s)ds=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\tau_{x}K_{i(t-s)}(-y)g(y,s)h_{\kappa}(y)dyds

is bounded from
L^{(2q)^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R},L_{\kappa}^{(2p)^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
to
L^{2q}(\mathbb{R},L_{\kappa}^{2p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
.

Remark 2.1
.

The Dunkl kernel of the localised Dunkl-Schrödinger operator
e^{it\Delta_{\kappa}}P
is also radial and given by
\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\kappa}(e^{-it|\cdot|^{2}}\psi(|\cdot|))
satisfying the following dispersive estimate, i.e.,

\left|\tau_{x}\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\kappa}\left(e^{-it|\cdot|^{2}}\psi(|\cdot|)\right)(y)\right|\lesssim_{\psi}|t|^{-\frac{N}{2}},\quad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(2.11)

which follows from Young’s inequality (
2.7
), (
2.6
) and (
2.8
).

2.5.
Lorentz spaces

In this subsection, we introduce the Lorentz space
L_{\mu}^{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and the Lorentz sequence space
\ell^{p,r}
, where
\mu
is a positive measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We refer the reader to
[
38
]
for further details.

First, let us recall some facts about the rearrangement argument. Suppose
f
is a complex-valued
\mu
-measurable function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\mu\left(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|f(x)|>t\}\right)=\int_{\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|f(x)|>t\}}d\mu(x)<+\infty
for every
t>0
. The decreasing rearrangement function
f^{*}
of
f
is defined by

f^{*}(t)=\inf\{s>0:a_{f}(s)\leq t\},

where
a_{f}
is the distribution function of
f
defined by

a_{f}(t)=\mu\left(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|f(x)|>t\}\right).

Since
f^{*}
is decreasing, the maximal function
f^{**}
of
f^{*}
defined by

f^{**}(t)=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}f^{*}(s)ds,

is also decreasing and
f^{*}(t)\leq f^{**}(t)
for every
t\geq 0
.

For
1<p<\infty
and
1\leq r\leq\infty
, the Lorentz space
L^{p,r}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is defined as the set of
\mu
-measurable functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\|f\|_{L^{p,r}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\infty
, where

\|f\|_{L^{p,r}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\begin{cases}\left(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}(t^{\frac{1}{p}}f^{*}(t))^{r}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}},&\text{ if }1\leq r<\infty,\\
\sup\limits_{t>0}t^{\frac{1}{p}}f^{*}(t),&\text{ if }r=\infty.\end{cases}

It is well-known that the function
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p,r}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
defined above is a norm when
r\leq p
and a quasi-norm otherwise. In order to obtain a norm in all cases, we define

\|f\|^{*}_{L^{p,r}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\begin{cases}\left(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}(t^{\frac{1}{p}}f^{**}(t))^{r}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}},&\text{ if }1\leq r<\infty,\\
\sup\limits_{t>0}t^{\frac{1}{p}}f^{**}(t),&\text{ if }r=\infty.\end{cases}

One can prove that
\|\cdot\|^{*}_{L^{p,r}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
is a norm, which is equivalent to
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p,r}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
in the sense that

\|f\|_{L^{p,r}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|f\|^{*}_{L^{p,r}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\frac{p}{p-1}\|f\|_{L^{p,r}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\;\forall f\in L^{p,r}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

It is clear that
L^{p,p}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=L^{p}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and the Lorentz spaces are monotone with respect to second exponent, namely

L^{p,r_{1}}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq L^{p,r_{2}}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\;1\leq r_{1}\leq r_{2}\leq\infty.

(2.12)

In this paper, we denote by
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p,r}_{\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
the Lorentz space
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p,r}_{h_{\kappa}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
in short and when
\kappa\equiv 0
, we denote by
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
.

Finally, we recall the Lorentz sequence space
\ell^{p,r}
. Let
\{\lambda_{j}\}^{\infty}_{j=1}\in c_{0}
and
\{\lambda^{*}_{j}\}^{\infty}_{j=1}
be the sequence permuted in a decreasing order. For
1<p<\infty
and
1\leq r\leq\infty
, the Lorentz sequence space
\ell^{p,r}
is defined as the set of all sequences
\{\lambda_{j}\}^{\infty}_{j=1}\in c_{0}
such that
\|\{\lambda_{j}\}^{\infty}_{j=1}\|_{\ell^{p,r}}<\infty
, where

\|\{\lambda_{j}\}^{\infty}_{j=1}\|_{\ell^{p,r}}=\begin{cases}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}(j^{\frac{1}{p}}\lambda_{j}^{*})^{r}\frac{1}{j}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}},&\text{ if }1\leq r<\infty,\\
\sup\limits_{j\geq 1}j^{\frac{1}{p}}\lambda_{j}^{*},&\text{ if }r=\infty.\end{cases}

2.6.
Schatten class

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a complex separable Hilbert space. A linear compact operator
A:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
belongs to the
\alpha
-Schatten-von Neumann class
\mathfrak{S}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{H})
if

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(s_{j}(A)\right)^{\alpha}<\infty,

where
\{s_{j}(A)\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
denote the singular values of
A,
, i.e., the eigenvalues of
|A|=\sqrt{A^{*}A}

with multiplicities counted. For
1\leq\alpha<\infty,
the Schatten space
\mathfrak{S}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{H})
is defined as the space of all compact operators
A
on
\mathcal{H}
such that
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(s_{j}(A)\right)^{\alpha}<\infty
, and the class
\mathfrak{S}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{H})
is a Banach space
endowed with the norm

\|A\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{H})}=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(s_{j}(A)\right)^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}.

For
0<\alpha<1
, the
\|\cdot\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{H})}
as above only defines a quasi-norm with respect to which

\mathfrak{S}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{H})
is complete. For
\alpha=\infty
,
\mathfrak{S}^{\infty}(\mathcal{H})
means the

\displaystyle\left\|\left(e^{it\Delta_{k}}\right)^{*}g(x)\right\|^{2}_{L_{\kappa}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\langle e^{-it\Delta_{\kappa}}g(\cdot,t),e^{-is\Delta_{\kappa}}g(\cdot,s)\rangle_{L_{\kappa}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}dtds

\displaystyle\leq C(Im(z))\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}|W_{1}(x,t)|^{2}|t-s|^{-2Re(z)-2-N}|W_{2}(y,s)|^{2}h_{\kappa}(x)h_{\kappa}(y)dxdydtds

\displaystyle=C(Im(z))\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\|W_{1}(\cdot,t)\|^{2}_{L_{\kappa}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|W_{2}(\cdot,s)\|^{2}_{L_{\kappa}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{|t-s|^{2Re(z)+2+N}}dtds

\displaystyle\leq C(Im(z))\big\|W_{1}\big\|_{L^{\tilde{q},4}(\mathbb{R},L_{\kappa}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\big\|W_{2}\big\|_{L^{\tilde{q},4}(\mathbb{R},L_{\kappa}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

and this completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

5.
Proof of the frequency localized estimates in Theorem
1.4

This section is devoted to prove the frequency localized estimates given in Theorem
1.4
. Although the orthogonality is not preserved by the localised operator
P
, by the argument in Frank-Sabin
[
14
]
, we still obtain the following frequency localised estimates on the segment line
[B,A)
.

Theorem 5.1
.

If
N\geq 1
,
(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q})\in[B,A)
and
\alpha=\frac{2p}{p+1}
, then we have

\bigg\|\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}|e^{it\Delta_{\kappa}}Pf_{j}|^{2}\bigg\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},L_{\kappa}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\lesssim_{\psi}\|\{\lambda_{j}\}^{\infty}_{j=1}\|_{\ell^{\alpha}}

holds for all families of orthonormal functions
\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
in
L_{\kappa}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and all sequences
\{\lambda_{j}\}^{\infty}_{j=1}
in
\ell^{\alpha}
.

Proof.

From the fact that
e^{it\Delta_{\kappa}}
is unitary on
L_{\kappa}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\|Pf_{j}\|_{L_{\kappa}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|f_{j}\|_{L_{\kappa}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
, it immediately follows that

\bigg\|\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}|e^{it\Delta_{\kappa}}Pf_{j}|^{2}\bigg\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},L_{\kappa}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{j}|\|e^{it\Delta_{\kappa}}Pf_{j}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},L_{\kappa}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\leq\|\{\lambda_{j}\}^{\infty}_{j=1}\|_{\ell^{1}}.

By interpolation, it suffices to prove that

\bigg\|\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}|e^{it\Delta_{\kappa}}Pf_{j}|^{2}\bigg\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},L_{\kappa}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\lesssim\|\{\lambda_{j}\}^{\infty}_{j=1}\|_{\ell^{\alpha}}

holds for
1+\frac{2}{N}\leq p<\frac{N+1}{N-1}
. By duality principle given in Theorem
2.2
, it is equivalent to prove

\big\|We^{it\Delta_{\kappa}}(e^{it\Delta_{\kappa}})^{*}P^{2}\overline{W}\big\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\alpha^{\prime}}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R},L_{\kappa}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})))}\lesssim_{\psi}\big\|W\big\|^{2}_{L^{2q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R},L_{\kappa}^{\alpha^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

(5.1)

for all
W\in L^{2q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R},L_{\kappa}^{\alpha^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
, where the operator
e^{it\Delta_{\kappa}}(e^{it\Delta_{\kappa}})^{*}P^{2}
is given by

e^{it\Delta_{\kappa}}(e^{it\Delta_{\kappa}})^{*}P^{2}g(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i(t-s)\Delta_{\kappa}}P^{2}g(\cdot,s)(x)ds.

Similarly to Theorem
4.2
, consider the analytic family of operators
\mathcal{T}_{z}
defined by

\mathcal{F}_{t,\kappa}(\mathcal{T}_{z}g)(\xi,\tau)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(z+1)}(\tau-|\xi|^{2})_{+}^{z}\psi^{2}(|\xi|)\mathcal{F}_{t,\kappa}g(\xi,\tau)

for
(\xi,\tau)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
. Then the kernel of
\mathcal{T}_{z}
is given by

\displaystyle\quad\tau_{x}\left(\frac{1}{c_{\kappa}\Gamma(z+1)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\tau_{+}^{z}e^{i\tau(t-s)}d\tau\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i(t-s)|\xi|^{2}}\psi^{2}(|\xi|)K(-y,i\xi)h_{\kappa}(\xi)d\xi\right)

\displaystyle=ie^{iz\pi/2}(t-s+i0)^{-z-1}\tau_{x}\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\kappa}\left(e^{i(t-s)|\cdot|^{2}}\psi^{2}(|\cdot|)\right)(-y)

and by (
2.11
), the kernel of
W_{1}\mathcal{T}_{z}W_{2}
is also bounded by

C_{\psi}(Im(z))|W_{1}(x,t)||t-s|^{-Re(z)-1-\frac{N}{2}}|W_{2}(y,s)|.

Then, proceeding similarly as in Theorem
4.2
, we have our required estimate (
5.1
).
∎

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.4
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

In order to obtain our desired results, we begin with the elementary claim that

\displaystyle\bigg\|\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}|e^{it\Delta_{\kappa}}Pf_{j}|^{2}\bigg\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},L_{\kappa}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\psi}\|\{\lambda_{j}\}^{\infty}_{j=1}\|_{\ell^{\infty}},

(5.2)

\displaystyle\bigg\|\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}|e^{it\Delta_{\kappa}}Pf_{j}|^{2}\bigg\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R},L_{\kappa}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\psi}\|\{\lambda_{j}\}^{\infty}_{j=1}\|_{\ell^{1}},\text{ for any }\frac{N}{N-2}\leq p\leq\infty.

(5.3)

Indeed, for fixed
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
, we define
\Psi_{x,t}(\xi)=K(ix,\xi)e^{-it|\xi|^{2}}\psi(|\xi|)
. Then from the orthonormality of
\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
in
L_{\kappa}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and Bessel’s inequality, it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{j}||e^{it\Delta_{\kappa}}Pf_{j}(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c^{2}_{\kappa}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{j}|\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-it|\xi|^{2}}\psi(|\xi|)\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}f_{j}(\xi)K(ix,\xi)h_{\kappa}(\xi)d\xi\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c^{2}_{\kappa}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{j}|\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Psi_{x,t}(\xi)\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}f_{j}(\xi)h_{\kappa}(\xi)d\xi\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c^{2}_{\kappa}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{j}|\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}\Psi_{x,t}(\xi)f_{j}(\xi)h_{\kappa}(\xi)d\xi\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{c^{2}_{\kappa}}\|\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}\Psi_{x,t}\|^{2}_{L_{\kappa}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\{\lambda_{j}\}^{\infty}_{j=1}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c^{2}_{\kappa}}\|\Psi_{x,t}\|^{2}_{L_{\kappa}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\{\lambda_{j}\}^{\infty}_{j=1}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{c^{2}_{\kappa}}\|\psi(|\cdot|)\|^{2}_{L_{\kappa}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\{\lambda_{j}\}^{\infty}_{j=1}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\psi}\|\{\lambda_{j}\}^{\infty}_{j=1}\|_{\ell^{\infty}},

which essentially the estimate (
5.2
). Next, we notice that, we can rewrite
e^{it\Delta_{\kappa}}Pf_{j}
as

e^{it\Delta_{\kappa}}Pf_{j}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\kappa}(\psi(|\cdot|))*_{\kappa}\left(e^{it\Delta_{\kappa}}f_{j}\right).

By Young’s inequality (
2.7
) and Theorem
1.1
, for any
\frac{N}{N-2}\leq p\leq\infty
, we have

\|e^{it\Delta_{\kappa}}Pf_{j}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},L_{\kappa}^{2p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\leq\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\kappa}(\psi(|\cdot|))\|_{L_{\kappa}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|e^{it\Delta_{\kappa}}f_{j}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},L_{\kappa}^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\lesssim_{\psi}\|f_{j}\|_{L_{\kappa}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where
1\leq r\leq\infty
satisfies
1+\frac{1}{2p}=\frac{1}{r}+\frac{N-2}{2N}
. Now using the Minkowski’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle\bigg\|\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}|e^{it\Delta_{\kappa}}Pf_{j}|^{2}\bigg\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R},L_{\kappa}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{j}|\|e^{it\Delta_{\kappa}}Pf_{j}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},L_{\kappa}^{2p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}^{2}\lesssim_{\psi}\|\{\lambda_{j}\}^{\infty}_{j=1}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}.

and this completes the inequality (
5.3
).

In light of Theorem
5.1
, (
5.2
) and (
5.3
), by complex interpolation, in order to prove Theorem
1.4
, it suffices to deduce that for any
(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q})\in[E,A)
, i.e.,
\frac{N+1}{N}<q\leq 2
with
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{N}{(N-1)p}
, the inequality

\bigg\|\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}|e^{it\Delta_{\kappa}}Pf_{j}|^{2}\bigg\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},L_{\kappa}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\lesssim_{\psi}\|\{\lambda_{j}\}^{\infty}_{j=1}\|_{\ell^{q}},

(5.4)

holds for all families of orthonormal functions
\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
in
L_{\kappa}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and all sequences
\{\lambda_{j}\}^{\infty}_{j=1}
in
\ell^{q}
. Using the duality principle Lemma
2.2
, the inequality (
5.4
) is equivalent to

\big\|W_{1}e^{it\Delta_{\kappa}}(e^{it\Delta_{\kappa}})^{*}P^{2}W_{2}\big\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{q^{\prime}}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R},L_{\kappa}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})))}\lesssim_{\psi}\big\|W_{1}\big\|_{L^{2q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R},L_{\kappa}^{2p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\big\|W_{2}\big\|_{L^{2q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R},L_{\kappa}^{2p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))},

(5.5)

for all
W_{1},W_{2}\in L^{2q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R},L_{\kappa}^{2p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
.
To establish this, first decompose the operator
e^{it\Delta_{\kappa}}(e^{it\Delta_{\kappa}})^{*}P^{2}
dyadically as
e^{it\Delta_{\kappa}}(e^{it\Delta_{\kappa}})^{*}P^{2}=\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{R}}T_{j}
with

T_{j}g(x,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi\left(2^{-j}(t-s)\right)e^{i(t-s)\Delta_{\kappa}}P^{2}g(\cdot,s)(x)ds,

where
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
is a suitable function supported in
[\frac{1}{2},2]
which satisfies
\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi\left(2^{-j}t\right)=1
for any
t\neq 0
and
\widehat{\chi}(0)=0
. It is easy to justify the existence of the function
\chi
and

|\widehat{\chi}(\tau)|\lesssim\min\{|\tau|,|\tau|^{-1}\}.

(5.6)

Besides, we denote

\beta(r,s)=\frac{N+1}{2}-N\left(\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{s}\right).

In order to prove (
5.5
), we first estimate the Schatten bounds for a family of analytic operators
\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}W_{1}2^{jz}T_{j}W_{2}
. Using the complex interpolation in Lemma
2.3
between the two estimates in the following lemma, we can obtain our required estimate (
5.5
). ∎

Lemma 5.2
.

For
z\in\mathbb{C}
, we have the following estimates

\big\|\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{jz}W_{1}T_{j}W_{2}\big\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\infty}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R},L_{\kappa}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})))}\lesssim_{\psi}\big\|W_{1}\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},L_{\kappa}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\big\|W_{2}\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},L_{\kappa}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

(5.7)

for
Re(z)=-1
and

\big\|\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{jz}W_{1}T_{j}W_{2}\big\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{2}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R},L_{\kappa}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})))}\lesssim_{\psi}\big\|W_{1}\big\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R},L_{\kappa}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\big\|W_{2}\big\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R},L_{\kappa}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

(5.8)

for
Re(z)=-\beta(r,r)
and
r\in(2,4)
.

Proof.

First we consider the case
Re(z)=-1
. Since

\displaystyle\big\|\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{jz}W_{1}T_{j}W_{2}\big\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\infty}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R},L_{\kappa}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\big\|W_{1}\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},L_{\kappa}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\|\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{jz}T_{j}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},L_{\kappa}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R},L_{\kappa}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\big\|W_{2}\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},L_{\kappa}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))},

it suffices to prove

\|\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{jz}T_{j}g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},L_{\kappa}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\lesssim_{\psi}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},L_{\kappa}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}.

(5.9)

In fact, we have

\mathcal{F}_{t,\kappa}(T_{j}g)(\xi,\tau)=2^{j}\widehat{\chi}\left(2^{j}(\tau+|\xi|^{2})\right)\psi^{2}(|\xi|)\mathcal{F}_{t,\kappa}g(\xi,\tau)

From Lemma
3.3
and (
5.6
), it deduces that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\widehat{\chi}\left(2^{j}(\tau+|\xi|^{2})\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\left(\sum\limits_{2^{j}(\tau+|\xi|^{2})>1}+\sum\limits_{2^{j}(\tau+|\xi|^{2})\leq 1}\right)\left|\widehat{\chi}\left(2^{j}(\tau+|\xi|^{2})\right)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum\limits_{2^{j}(\tau+|\xi|^{2})>1}\left(2^{j}(\tau+|\xi|^{2})\right)^{-1}+\sum\limits_{2^{j}(\tau+|\xi|^{2})\leq 1}2^{j}(\tau+|\xi|^{2})

\displaystyle\lesssim 1

holds uniformly in
(\xi,\tau)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
and (
5.9
) follows from Plancherel’s formula when
Re(z)=-1
.

Next we turn to the case
Re(z)=-\beta(r,r)
. For this, we further break up each operator
T_{j}
into two parts, i.e.,
T_{j}=T^{(0)}_{j}+T^{(1)}_{j}
, where

\displaystyle T^{(0)}_{j}g(x,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi\left(2^{-j}(t-s)\right)e^{i(t-s)\Delta_{\kappa}}P^{2}\left(E_{I(|x|,2^{j+4})^{c}}(|\cdot|)g(\cdot,s)\right)(x)ds,

\displaystyle T^{(1)}_{j}g(x,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi\left(2^{-j}(t-s)\right)e^{i(t-s)\Delta_{\kappa}}P^{2}\left(E_{I(|x|,2^{j+4})}(|\cdot|)g(\cdot,s)\right)(x)ds,

with
I(|x|,2^{j+4})
is the interval centered at
|x|
with radius
2^{j+4}
and
E_{I(|x|,2^{j+4})}
is the characteristic function on
I(|x|,2^{j+4})
.

We begin with the part
T^{(0)}_{j}
, which also could be considered as an error term of
T_{j}
. We can rewrite
T^{(0)}_{j}g(x,t)
as

T^{(0)}_{j}g(x,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}K^{(0)}_{j}(x,t,y,s)g(y,s)h_{\kappa}(y)dyds,

where the kernel of
W_{1}T^{(0)}_{j}W_{2}
is given by

K^{(0)}_{j}(x,t,y,s)=W_{1}(x,t)E_{I(0,2^{j+4})^{c}}(|x|-|y|)\chi\left(2^{-j}(t-s)\right)\tau_{x}e^{i(t-s)\Delta_{\kappa}}\Psi(-y)W_{2}(y,s)

(5.10)

with
\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}\Psi(\xi)=\psi^{2}(|\xi|)
.

We claim that for any
t\in[2^{j-1},2^{j+1}]
,
x\in B(0,2^{j+4})^{c}
and
M\in\mathbb{N},
there exists a constant
C_{\psi,M}
depending only on
\psi
and
M
such that

\left|e^{it\Delta_{\kappa}}\Psi(x)\right|\leq\frac{C_{\psi,M}}{(1+|x|)^{M}}.

(5.11)

Indeed, from (
2.4
) and (
3.6
), we see that

\displaystyle e^{it\Delta_{\kappa}}\Psi(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(N/2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-itr^{2}}\psi^{2}(r)\frac{J_{\frac{N-2}{2}}(r|x|)}{\left(r|x|\right)^{\frac{N-2}{2}}}r^{N-1}dr

\displaystyle=C\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(e^{i(r|x|-tr^{2})}h(r|x|)+e^{i(-r|x|-tr^{2})}\overline{h(r|x|)}\right)\psi^{2}(r)r^{N-1}dr.

From (
3.5
), our claim holds true for any
|x|\leq 1
. On the other hand, when
|x|>1
, it is enough to show the following oscillatory integral satisfies

\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{i\left(\pm r|x|-tr^{2}\right)}\psi^{2}(r)H(r|x|)r^{N-1}dr\right|\leq\frac{C_{\psi,M}}{|x|^{M}},

(5.12)

where
H=h
or
\overline{h}
. Note that
r\in[\frac{1}{2},2]
,
t\in[2^{j-1},2^{j+1}]
and
x\in B(0,2^{j+4})^{c}
, we have

\left|\frac{d}{dr}\left(\pm r|x|-tr^{2}\right)\right|=\left|\pm|x|-2tr\right|\geq|x|-4|t|\geq\frac{|x|}{2}

and hence, the estimate (
5.12
) follows immediately by repeated integration by parts and (
3.8
).

Once we show (
5.11
), noting that
e^{it\Delta_{\kappa}}\Psi
is radial, from (
2.2
) and (
2.6
), we have

\left|\tau_{x}e^{i(t-s)\Delta_{\kappa}}\Psi(-y)\right|\leq\frac{C_{\psi,M}}{\left(1+\big||x|-|y|\big|\right)^{M}}.

Then from (
5.10
), we get

\displaystyle|K_{j}^{(0)}(x,t,y,s)|

\displaystyle\leq C_{\psi,M}\frac{|W_{1}(x,t)||W_{2}(y,s)||\chi(2^{-j}(t-s))|}{\left(1+\big||x|-|y|\big|\right)^{M}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\psi,M}\frac{|W_{1}(x,t)||W_{2}(y,s)||\chi(2^{-j}(t-s))|}{(1+2^{j})^{\frac{M}{2}}\left(1+\big||x|-|y|\big|\right)^{\frac{M}{2}}.}

(5.13)

Therefore, for
Re(z)=-\beta(r,r),r\in(2,4)
and
M\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large, it follows from (
5.13
) and Lemma
3.5
that

\displaystyle\big\|\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{jz}W_{1}T^{(0)}_{j}W_{2}\big\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{2}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R},L_{\kappa}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})))}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-j\beta(r,r)}\big\|W_{1}T_{j}W_{2}\big\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{2}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R},L_{\kappa}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})))}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-j\beta(r,r)}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|K_{j}^{(0)}(x,t,y,s)|^{2}h_{\kappa}(x)h_{\kappa}(y)dxdydtds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\psi,M}\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{2^{-j\beta(r,r)}}{(1+2^{j})^{M}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}\frac{|W_{1}(x,t)|^{2}|W_{2}(y,s)|^{2}|\chi(2^{-j}(t-s))|^{2}}{\left(1+\big||x|-|y|\big|\right)^{M}}h_{\kappa}(x)h_{\kappa}(y)dxdydtds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\psi,M}\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{2^{-j\beta(r,r)}}{(1+2^{j})^{M}}\left\|\frac{1}{(1+|\cdot|)^{M}}\right\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L_{\kappa}^{\frac{r}{2(r-2)}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\|W_{1}(\cdot,t)\|^{2}_{L_{\kappa}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|W_{2}(\cdot,s)\|^{2}_{L_{\kappa}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}|\chi(2^{-j}(t-s))|^{2}dtds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\psi,M}\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{2^{-j\beta(r,r)}}{(1+2^{j})^{M}}\|\chi(2^{-j}\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\|W_{1}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R},L^{r}_{\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\|W_{2}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R},L^{r}_{\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\psi,M}\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{2^{j(\frac{1}{2}-\beta(r,r))}}{(1+2^{j})^{M}}\|W_{1}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R},L^{r}_{\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\|W_{2}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R},L^{r}_{\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}.

Note that for
r\in(2,4)
,
\frac{1}{2}-\beta(r,r)\in(0,\frac{N}{2})
and hence we deduce the desired estimate

\big\|\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{jz}W_{1}T^{(0)}_{j}W_{2}\big\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{2}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R},L_{\kappa}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})))}\lesssim_{\psi}\|W_{1}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R},L^{r}_{\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\|W_{2}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R},L^{r}_{\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}.

As to the leading term
T_{j}^{(1)}
of
T_{j}
, we rewrite

T^{(1)}_{j}g(x,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}K^{(1)}_{j}(x,t,y,s)g(y,s)h_{\kappa}(y)dyds,

where the kernel of
W_{1}T^{(1)}_{j}W_{2}
is given by

K^{(1)}_{j}(x,t,y,s)=W_{1}(x,t)E_{I(0,2^{j+4})}(|x|-|y|)\chi\left(2^{-j}(t-s)\right)\tau_{x}e^{i(t-s)\Delta_{\kappa}}\Psi(-y)W_{2}(y,s).

By using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, it implies

\displaystyle\big\|\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{jz}W_{1}T^{(1)}_{j}W_{2}\big\|^{2}_{\mathfrak{S}^{2}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R},L_{\kappa}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})))}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}\left|\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{jz}K^{(1)}_{j}(x,t,y,s)\right|^{2}h_{\kappa}(x)h_{\kappa}(y)dxdydtds

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}\left|\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{jz}\chi\left(2^{-j}(t-s)\right)E_{I(0,2^{j+4})}(|x|-|y|)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\left|W_{1}(x,t)\tau_{x}e^{i(t-s)\Delta_{\kappa}}\Psi(-y)W_{2}(y,s)\right|^{2}h_{\kappa}(x)h_{\kappa}(y)dxdydtds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\left||x|-|y|\right|\leq 2^{j+4}}\left(\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|\chi\left(2^{-j}(t-s)\right)|\right)\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-2\beta(r,r)j}|\chi\left(2^{-j}(t-s)\right)|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\left|\tau_{x}e^{i(t-s)\Delta_{\kappa}}\Psi(-y)|^{2}|W_{1}(x,t)\right|^{2}|W_{2}(y,s)|^{2}h_{\kappa}(x)h_{\kappa}(y)dxdydtds

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-2\beta(r,r)j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\left||x|-|y|\right|\leq 2^{j+4}}|\chi\left(2^{-j}(t-s)\right)|\left|\tau_{x}e^{i(t-s)\Delta_{\kappa}}\Psi(-y)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times|W_{1}(x,t)|^{2}|W_{2}(y,s)|^{2}h_{\kappa}(x)h_{\kappa}(y)dxdydtds

Hardy spaces, Besov spaces and Triebel–Lizorkin spaces associated with a discrete Laplacian and applications
The Anh Bui, Xuan Thinh Duong

Abstract
. Consider the discrete Laplacian Δ d \Delta_{d} defined on the set of integers ℤ \mathbb{Z} by Δ d ​ f ​ ( n ) = − f ⁡ ( n + 1 ) + 2 ​ f ​ ( n ) − f ⁡ ( n − 1 ) , n ∈ ℤ , \Delta_{d}f(n)=-f(n+1)+2f(n)-f(n-1),\ \ \ \ n\in\mathbb{Z}, where f f is a function defined on ℤ \mathbb{Z} . In this paper, we define Hardy spaces, Besov spaces and Triebel–Lizorkin spaces associated with Δ d \Delta_{d} and then show that these function spaces coincide with the classical function spaces defined on ℤ \mathbb{Z} . As applications, we prove the boundedness of the spectral multipliers and the Riesz transforms associated with Δ d \Delta_{d} on these function spaces.

1.
Introduction and the statement of main results

Let
\mathbb{Z}
be the set of integers equipped with the counting measure. In this case,
\mathbb{Z}
can be viewed as a space of homogeneous type in the sense of Coifman and Weiss
[
12

the Lebesgue space on
\mathbb{Z}
with respect to the counting measure, i.e., if
f\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
, then

\|f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}=\Big(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|f(n)|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}.

The harmonic analysis on the set of all integers
\mathbb{Z}
is an interesting topic in harmonic analysis. See for example
[

,

,

,

,

,

and the references therein. We would like to refer to

]
for the theory of Besov and Triebel–Lizorkin spaces and
[

,

]
for the theory of Hardy spaces. Moreover, the boundedness of the Fourier multipliers on
\mathbb{Z}
was investigated in
[

,
24
]
.

In this paper, we consider the discrete Laplacian
\Delta_{d}
on
\mathbb{Z}
defined by

\Delta_{d}f(n)=-f(n+1)+2f(n)-f(n-1),\ \ \ \ n\in\mathbb{Z},

where
f
is a function defined on
\mathbb{Z}
.

Let us emphasize that the study of the discrete Laplacian is an interesting topic in harmonic analysis and PDEs. See for example
[

,

and the references therein. Recently, in
[

, Ciaurri et. al. studied the boundedness of some operators including the maximal functions, the square functions and the Riesz transforms associated to the discrete Laplacian
\Delta_{d}
on

. Later, the boundedness of the maximal functions, the square functions and the spectral multipliers of Laplace transform type associated with
\Delta_{d}

•

•

\Delta_{d}

(i)

Theory of Hardy spaces associated with
\Delta_{d}
:
Motivated by the works
[
3
,
15
,
14
,
22
,
21
]
we define the Hardy space associated with
\Delta_{d}
via the area square function

S_{\Delta_{d}}f(n)=\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{m:|m-n|<t}\big|t^{2}\Delta_{d}e^{-t^{2}\Delta_{d}}f(m)\big|^{2}\frac{dt}{t(t+1)}\Big)^{1/2},

and show that the Hardy spaces also admit molecular decomposition which is analogous to the decomposition of the classical Hardy spaces. Then we will show that the new Hardy spaces coincide with the classical Hardy spaces
H^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
for
0<p\leq 1
. It is important to note that in our setting the kernel of
e^{-t\Delta_{d}}
satisfies neither Davies–Gaffney estimates nor Gaussian upper bounds. Besides that, it is not clear if the kernel of
e^{-t\Delta_{d}}
satisfies the Hölder continuity. Hence, new ideas and significant modifications would be required to handle these challenges. More precisely, in this case, the kernel of
e^{-t\Delta_{d}}
enjoys the polynomial decay. However, this is not enough to develop the theory of Hardy space for
p
close to
0
. Fortunately, by rigorous computation we will prove that higher order derivatives in time of the kernel of
e^{-t\Delta_{d}}
will present more decays. This plays a key role in the paper. We also emphasize that in comparison with the classical atoms/molecules, our new molecules of the Hardy spaces have advantages in proving the boundedness of certain singular integrals such as the spectral multipliers of
\Delta_{d}
. See Section 3 and Section 5.1.

(ii)

Theory of Besov and Triebel–Lizorkin spaces associated with
\Delta_{d}
:
let us remind that the Besov and Triebel–Lizorkin spaces on
\mathbb{Z}
were initiated by
[
18
,
32
,
30
]
. Their approaches based on the connection between the function spaces on
\mathbb{R}
and the function spaces on
\mathbb{Z}
. Once the connection has been set up, properties of Besov and Triebel–Lizorkin spaces on
\mathbb{R}
will be transferred into the function spaces on
\mathbb{Z}
. However, it is not clear how to adapt this method to prove the boundedness of the spectral multipliers of
\Delta_{d}
and the Riesz transforms of
\Delta_{d}
. In this paper, we approach the problem by introducing the new Besov and Triebel–Lizorkin spaces adapted to
\Delta_{d}
. We also obtain new molecular decomposition of these function spaces. More importantly, we can show that the new function spaces and the function spaces introduced in
[
18
,
32
,
30
]
coincide. Moreover, similarly to the case of the Hardy spaces, we are able to prove the boundedness of the spectral multipliers and the Riesz transforms by making use of molecular decomposition with new molecules. See Sections 4 and 5.

(iii)

Boundedness of the spectral multipliers of
\Delta_{d}
:
Note that
\Delta_{d}
is a non-negative and self-adjoint operator on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. Moreover, it is straightforward to see that
\|\Delta_{d}f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}\leq 4\|f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}
. It follows that the spectral of
\Delta_{d}
is contained in
[0,4]
. In fact, it is well-known that for every
\theta\in[-\pi,\pi]
, we have

\Delta_{d}e_{\theta}=2(1-\cos\theta)e_{\theta},

where
e_{\theta}(n)=e^{in\theta}
.

By the spectral theory, for a bounded Borel measurable function
m
on
[0,\infty)
we can define

\displaystyle F(\Delta_{d})=\int_{0}^{\infty}F(\lambda)dE(\lambda),

which is bounded on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, where
\{E(\lambda):\lambda\geq 0\}
is the spectral resolution of
\Delta_{d}
. Since the spectrum of
\Delta_{d}
is contained in
[0,4]
, the integral above is vanishing over
(4,\infty]
.

For
f\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
, the Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}}(f)
of
f
is defined by

\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}}(f)(\theta)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}f(n)e^{in\theta},\ \ \ \ \theta\in[-\pi,\pi].

The inverse operator
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{-1}
of
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}}
is determined by

\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{-1}(\varphi)(n)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\varphi(\theta)e^{-in\theta}d\theta,\ \ \ \ \varphi\in L^{2}[-\pi,\pi].

Let
{\mathfrak{m}}\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty)
. We define

T_{{\mathfrak{m}}}f=\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{-1}\big[{\mathfrak{m}}(\sqrt{2(1-\cos\theta)})\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}}(f)\big].

It can be verified that

T_{{\mathfrak{m}}}f={\mathfrak{m}}(\sqrt{\Delta}_{d})f.

In
[
1
]
, the spectral multiplier of Laplace transform type was considered corresponding to the case
{\mathfrak{m}}(\lambda)=\lambda^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda^{2}t}\Psi(t)dt
,
\lambda>0
and
\Psi\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty)
. It was proved that

\|{\mathfrak{m}}(\sqrt{\Delta}_{d})\|_{H^{p}(\mathbb{Z})\to H^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}\leq C_{\Psi},\ \ \ \ 0<p\leq 1.

In the particular case when
\psi(t)=-\frac{1}{\Gamma(is)}t^{is}
, the constant
C_{\Psi}
is an exponential function of
s
. It is clear that in comparison with the bound obtained by the general spectral multiplier theorem (see for example
[
13
]
), we should expect that
C_{\Psi}={\rm constant}\times s^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}
for any
\epsilon>0
. In Section 5 (Theorem
5.1
), by using our theory of new function spaces, we are able to prove the sharp estimate for the spectral multipliers
{\mathfrak{m}}(\sqrt{\Delta}_{d})
on Hardy spaces, Besov spaces and Triebel–Lizorkin spaces.

(iv)

Boundedness of the Riesz transforms:
Let
D
and
D^{*}
be operators defined by

Df(n)=f(n+1)-f(n),\ \ \ \ D^{*}f(n)=f(n)-f(n-1).

These operators can be viewed as the first “discrete derivatives” on
\mathbb{Z}
. It is clear that

-\Delta_{d}=D^{*}D=DD^{*}
and

\displaystyle\langle\Delta_{d}f,f\rangle_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}=\langle Df,Df\rangle=\langle D^{*}f,D^{*}f\rangle_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})},

where
\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}f(n)g(n)
.

In
[
10
]
, the Riesz transforms are defined via the limits

\mathcal{R}=\lim_{\alpha\to(1/2)^{-}}D\Delta_{d}^{-\alpha}\ \ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \ \widetilde{\mathcal{R}}=\lim_{\alpha\to(1/2)^{-}}D^{*}\Delta_{d}^{-\alpha}.

This is because the subordination formula

\Delta_{d}^{-1/2}f=c\int_{0}^{\infty}\sqrt{t}e^{-t\Delta_{d}}f\frac{dt}{t}.

is not absolutely convergent in
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. However, by using new characterizations of the distributions in Section 2.3, we are able to prove that the subordination formula is well-defined in the sense of the distributions. This allows us to use this formula to prove the boundedness of the Riesz transforms
D\Delta_{d}^{-1/2}
and
D^{*}\Delta_{d}^{-1/2}
on Besov and Triebel–Lizorkin spaces which include the Hardy spaces. Note that even the boundedness of the Riesz transforms on the Hardy spaces
H^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
is new.

The organization of the paper is as follows. In Section 2, we first prove some kernel estimates for the semigroup
e^{-t\Delta_{d}}
and a functional calculus of
\Delta_{d}
. Then some properties of distributions will be established. Section 3 will set up the theory of Hardy spaces associated with
\Delta_{d}
. The theory of Besov and Triebel–Lizorkin spaces will be addressed in Section 4. Finally, Section 5 will be devoted in proving the boundedness of the spectral multipliers and the Riesz transforms.

Throughout the paper, we usually use
C
and
c
to denote positive constants that are independent of the main parameters involved but whose values may differ from line to line. We will write
A\lesssim B
if there is a universal constant
C
so that
A\leq CB
and
A\sim B
if
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
.

It is well-known that for

(2.1)

\|\mathcal{M}_{r}f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}

for all

Moreover, let

(2.2)

\|\mathcal{M}_{r}f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}

We recall the Fefferman-Stein vector-valued maximal inequality in
[
19
]
. For
0<p<\infty
,
0<q\leq\infty
,
0<r<\min\{p,q\}
, we then have for any sequence of measurable functions

(2.3)

\Big\|\Big(\sum_{\nu}|\mathcal{M}_{r}f_{\nu}|^{q}\Big)^{1/q}\Big\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}\lesssim\Big\|\Big(\sum_{\nu}|f_{\nu}|^{q}\Big)^{1/q}\Big\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}.

The Young’s inequality and (

) imply the following inequality: If
\{a_{\nu}\}\in\ell^{q}\cap\ell^{1}
, then

(2.4)

\Big\|\sum_{j}\Big(\sum_{\nu}|a_{j-\nu}\mathcal{M}_{r}f_{\nu}|^{q}\Big)^{1/q}\Big\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}\lesssim\Big\|\Big(\sum_{\nu}|f_{\nu}|^{q}\Big)^{1/q}\Big\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}.

The following elementary estimates will be used frequently.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\epsilon>0
. Then we have, for all function
f
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
s>0
,

\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{s}\Big(1+\frac{|m-n|}{s}\Big)^{-1-\epsilon}\lesssim 1,

and

\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{s}\Big(1+\frac{|m-n|}{s}\Big)^{-1-\epsilon}|f(m)|\lesssim\mathcal{M}f(n).

Proof.

(a) The proof of (a) is similar to (b) and hence we omit the details.

(b) We have

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{s}\Big(1+\frac{|m-n|}{s}\Big)^{-1-\epsilon}|f(m)|

\displaystyle\leq|f(n)|+\sum_{m\neq n}\frac{1}{s}\Big(1+\frac{|m-n|}{s}\Big)^{-1-\epsilon}|f(m)|

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{M}f(n)+\sum_{m\neq n}\frac{1}{|m-n|^{1+\epsilon}}|f(m)|.

It is easy to see that

\displaystyle\sum_{m\neq n}\frac{1}{|m-n|^{1+\epsilon}}|f(m)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\geq 0}\sum_{m\in I_{n}(2^{k+1})\backslash I_{n}(2^{k})}\frac{1}{|m-n|^{1+\epsilon}}|f(m)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k\geq 0}2^{-k\epsilon}\mathcal{M}f(n)

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{M}f(n).

This completes our proof.
∎

2.1.
Heat kernel estimates

Denote by
e^{-t\Delta_{d}}
the semigroup generated by
\Delta_{d}
. Then we have

(2.5)

e^{-t\Delta_{d}}f(n)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}h_{t}(n-m)f(m),\ \ \ \ n\in\mathbb{Z},

where
h_{t}(n)=e^{-2t}\mathfrak{I}_{n}(2t),t>0
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathfrak{I}_{n}
is the modified Bessel function of first kind defined by

(2.6)

\mathfrak{I}_{n}(z)=\frac{z^{n}}{\sqrt{\pi}2^{n}\Gamma(n+1/2)}\int_{-1}^{1}e^{-zs}(1-s^{2})^{n-1/2}ds,\ \ \ \ \ |\arg z|<\pi,\ \ \ n>-1/2.

The modified Bessel function
\mathfrak{I}_{n}
is also represented in the following integral form

(2.7)

\mathfrak{I}_{n}(z)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}e^{z\cos\theta}\cos(n\theta)d\theta,\ \ \ \ \ \ |\arg z|<\pi,\ \ \ n\in\mathbb{Z}.

See for example
[
26
,
28
]
.

From (
2.5
), (
2.6
) and (
2.7
), we have, for
|\arg z|<\pi
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,

(2.8)

\displaystyle h_{z}(n)

\displaystyle=\frac{z^{n}}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(n+1/2)}\int_{-1}^{1}e^{-2z(s+1)}(1-s^{2})^{n-1/2}ds

(2.9)

\displaystyle\text{and}\ \ h_{z}(n)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}e^{-2z(1-\cos\theta)}\cos(n\theta)d\theta.

Moreover, since
\mathfrak{I}_{-n}(z)=\mathfrak{I}_{n}(z)
, we have
h_{z}(n)=h_{z}(-n)
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

We now prove some estimates regrading the kernel
h_{t}(n)
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For each
N\geq 0
, there exists
C_{N}>0
such that

|h_{t}(n)|\leq\frac{C_{N}}{\sqrt{t}}\Big(1+\frac{n}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{-2N-1}

for
n=0
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
with
|n|>N
, and
t>0
.

Proof.

By (
2.8
), we have

h_{t}(n)=\frac{t^{n}}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(n+1/2)}\int_{-1}^{1}e^{-2t(s+1)}(1-s^{2})^{n-1/2}ds.

Using the change of variable
w=\frac{1}{2}t(1+s)
, we further obtain

\displaystyle|h_{t}(n)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{t^{n}}{\Gamma(n+1/2)}\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\frac{2w}{t}\Big)^{n-1/2}e^{-4w}\Big[2\Big(1-\dfrac{w}{t}\Big)\Big]^{n-1/2}\frac{dw}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{4^{n}t^{n}}{\Gamma(n+1/2)}\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\frac{w}{t}\Big)^{n-1/2}e^{-4w}\Big(1-\dfrac{w}{t}\Big)^{n-1/2}\frac{dw}{t}

Applying the following inequality

\Big(\frac{w}{t}\Big)^{N+1/2}\Big(1-\dfrac{w}{t}\Big)^{n-1/2}\leq\Big(\frac{N+1/2}{N+n}\Big)^{N+1/2}\leq\frac{C_{N}}{n^{N+1/2}},

we have

\displaystyle|h_{t}(n)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{N}}{n^{N+1/2}}\frac{4^{n}t^{n}}{\Gamma(n+1/2)}\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\frac{w}{t}\Big)^{n-N-1}e^{-4w}\frac{dw}{t}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4^{N+1}C_{N}}{n^{N+1/2}}\frac{t^{N}}{\Gamma(n+1/2)}\int_{0}^{t}(4w)^{n-N-1}e^{-4w}d(4w)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{N}t^{N}}{n^{N+1/2}}\frac{\Gamma(n-N)}{\Gamma(n+1/2)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{N}t^{N}}{n^{N+1/2}}\frac{1}{n^{N+1/2}}=\frac{C_{N}}{\sqrt{t}}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{n}\Big)^{2N+1},

which implies that

(2.10)

|h_{t}(n)|\leq\frac{C_{N}}{\sqrt{t}}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{n}\Big)^{2N+1}

for
|n|>N
.

On the other hand, from (
2.9
),

h_{t}(n)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}e^{-2t(1-\cos\theta)}\cos(n\theta)d\theta

for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Using the fact that
(1-\cos\theta)\gtrsim\theta^{2}
for
\theta\in(0,\pi)
we have

\displaystyle|h_{t}(n)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}e^{-2ct\theta^{2}}d\theta

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}

for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

This, along with (
2.10
), implies the desired estimates, i.e.,

|h_{t}(n)|\leq\frac{C_{N}}{\sqrt{t}}\Big(1+\frac{n}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{-2N-1}

for
n=0
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
with
|n|>N
, and
t>0
,

∎

Set

, Lemma 2.1]
that

\partial^{k}_{\theta}\varphi_{t}(\theta)=\varphi_{t}(\theta)\sum_{(m_{1},\ldots,m_{k})\in\mathbb{N}^{k}\atop m_{1}+2m_{2}+\ldots+km_{k}=k}a_{\vec{m}}t^{|\vec{m}|}(\cos\theta)^{|\vec{m}_{\rm even}|}(\sin\theta)^{|\vec{m}_{\rm odd}|}

for some constants
a_{\vec{m}}
.

By Leibniz’s rule we have, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

(2.12)

\displaystyle\partial^{k}_{\theta}\partial^{\ell}_{t}\varphi_{t}(\theta)=\partial^{\ell}_{t}\partial^{k}_{\theta}\varphi(t,\theta)=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}[-2(1-\cos\theta)]^{j}e^{-2t(1-\cos\theta)}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{(m_{1},\ldots,m_{k})\in\mathbb{N}^{k}\atop m_{1}+2m_{2}+\ldots+km_{k}=k}a_{\vec{m},\ell}t^{|\vec{m}|-\ell+j}(\cos\theta)^{|\vec{m}_{\rm even}|}(\sin\theta)^{|\vec{m}_{\rm odd}|}.

Note that if
k
is odd, then

\partial^{k}_{\theta}\partial^{\ell}_{t}\varphi(t,0)=\partial^{k}_{\theta}\partial^{\ell}_{t}\varphi(t,\pi)=0

for all
t>0
.

Therefore, by using the integration by parts we have

(2.13)

\partial_{t}^{\ell}h_{t}(n)=\frac{(-1)^{\lfloor(k+1)/2\rfloor}}{\pi n^{k}}\int_{0}^{\pi}\partial_{\theta}^{k}\partial_{t}^{\ell}\varphi_{t}(\theta)\Phi_{k}(n\theta)d\theta.

This, along with (
2.12
) and the facts that
(1-\cos\theta)\gtrsim\theta^{2}
,
|\sin\theta|\leq\theta
and
|\cos\theta|\leq 1
for
\theta\in(0,\pi)
, we have

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}^{\ell}h_{t}(n)|\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\sum_{(m_{1},\ldots,m_{k})\in\mathbb{N}^{k}\atop m_{1}+2m_{2}+\ldots+km_{k}=k}\frac{1}{t^{\ell}n^{k}}\int_{0}^{\pi}(t\theta^{2})^{j}e^{-ct\theta^{2}}t^{|\vec{m}|}\theta^{|\vec{m}_{\rm odd}|}d\theta.

Since
t\geq 1
and
m_{1}+2m_{2}+\ldots+km_{k}=k
, it is easy to see that

(2.14)

t^{|\vec{m}|}\leq t^{k/2}t^{|\vec{m}_{\rm odd}|/2}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}^{\ell}h_{t}(n)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\sum_{(m_{1},\ldots,m_{k})\in\mathbb{N}^{k}\atop m_{1}+2m_{2}+\ldots+km_{k}=k}\frac{t^{k/2}}{t^{\ell}n^{k}}\int_{0}^{\pi}(t\theta^{2})^{j}e^{-ct\theta^{2}}t^{|\vec{m}_{\rm odd}|/2}\theta^{|\vec{m}_{\rm odd}|}d\theta

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{t^{k/2}}{t^{\ell+1/2}n^{k}}

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
t\geq 1
.

This follows (
2.16
) for the case
z=t\geq 1
.

For the general case
z\in\mathbb{C}
with
\Re z\geq 1
, we have

h_{z}(n)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}e^{-2z(1-\cos\theta)}\cos(n\theta)d\theta.

Similarly to (
2.12
), we have

(2.15)

\displaystyle\partial^{k}_{\theta}\partial^{\ell}_{z}\varphi_{z}(\theta)=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}[-2(1-\cos\theta)]^{j}e^{-2z(1-\cos\theta)}

\displaystyle\sum_{(m_{1},\ldots,m_{k})\in\mathbb{N}^{k}\atop m_{1}+2m_{2}+\ldots+km_{k}=k}a_{\vec{m},\ell}z^{|\vec{m}|-\ell+j}(\cos\theta)^{|\vec{m}_{\rm even}|}(\sin\theta)^{|\vec{m}_{\rm odd}|}.

At this stage, arguing similarly to the real case we have

\displaystyle|\partial_{z}^{\ell}h_{z}(n)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\sum_{(m_{1},\ldots,m_{k})\in\mathbb{N}^{k}\atop m_{1}+2m_{2}+\ldots+km_{k}=k}\frac{|z|^{k/2}}{|z|^{\ell}n^{k}}\int_{0}^{\pi}(|z|\theta^{2})^{j}|e^{-cz\theta^{2}}||z|^{|\vec{m}_{\rm odd}|/2}\theta^{|\vec{m}_{\rm odd}|}d\theta

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\sum_{(m_{1},\ldots,m_{k})\in\mathbb{N}^{k}\atop m_{1}+2m_{2}+\ldots+km_{k}=k}\frac{|z|^{k/2}}{|z|^{\ell}n^{k}}\int_{0}^{\pi}(|z|\theta^{2})^{j}e^{-c(|z|\cos\alpha)\theta^{2}}|z|^{|\vec{m}_{\rm odd}|/2}\theta^{|\vec{m}_{\rm odd}|}d\theta

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\sum_{(m_{1},\ldots,m_{k})\in\mathbb{N}^{k}\atop m_{1}+2m_{2}+\ldots+km_{k}=k}\frac{|z|^{k/2}}{|z|^{\ell}n^{k}}\frac{1}{(\cos\alpha)^{j+|\vec{m}_{\rm odd}|/2}}

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{\pi}\big[(|z|\cos\alpha)\theta^{2}\big]^{j}e^{-c(|z|\cos\alpha)\theta^{2}}(|z|\cos\alpha)^{|\vec{m}_{\rm odd}|/2}\theta^{|\vec{m}_{\rm odd}|}d\theta.

We now use the following inequalities

(\cos\alpha)^{j+|\vec{m}_{\rm odd}|/2}\geq(\cos\alpha)^{\ell+k/2}

and

\int_{0}^{\pi}\big[(|z|\cos\alpha)\theta^{2}\big]^{j}e^{-c(|z|\cos\alpha)\theta^{2}}(|z|\cos\alpha)^{|\vec{m}_{\rm odd}|/2}\theta^{|\vec{m}_{\rm odd}|}d\theta\lesssim(|z|\cos\alpha)^{-1/2}

to obtain

\displaystyle|\partial_{z}^{\ell}h_{z}(n)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|z|^{k/2}}{|z|^{\ell}n^{k}}\frac{1}{(\cos\alpha)^{\ell+k/2}}\frac{1}{(|z|\cos\alpha)^{1/2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{(|z|\cos\alpha)^{\ell+1/2}}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{|z|\cos\alpha}}{n\cos\alpha}\Big)^{k}.

This completes our proof.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

For any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
C=C(\ell)
such that

(2.16)

|\partial^{\ell}_{t}h_{t}(n)|\leq\frac{C}{t^{\ell+1/2}}\Big(1+\frac{|n|}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{-\ell}

for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and all
t>0
.

Proof.

If
t\geq 1
, then the estimate follows directly from Lemma
2.4
.

Hence, it remains to show the estimate for
0<t<1
. To do this, recall from (
2.9
) that

h_{t}(n)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}e^{-2t(1-\cos\theta)}\cos(n\theta)d\theta

for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Using the fact that
(1-\cos\theta)\gtrsim\theta^{2}
for
\theta\in(0,\pi)
, we have

(2.17)

\displaystyle|h_{t}(n)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}e^{-2ct\theta^{2}}d\theta

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}.

On the other hand, since

\partial^{\ell}_{t}h_{t}(n)=\Delta_{d}^{\ell}h_{t}(n)=\sum_{j=-\ell}^{\ell}c_{j}h_{t}(n+j),

we have

(2.18)

\displaystyle|\partial^{\ell}_{t}h_{t}(n)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{t^{1/2}}=C\frac{t^{\ell}}{t^{\ell+1/2}}

for
t\in(0,1)
.

On the other hand, arguing similarly to the proof of Lemma
2.4
in which instead of (
2.14
) we use the following inequality

t^{|\vec{m}|}\leq t^{|\vec{m}_{\rm odd}|/2}\ \ \ \text{for $0<t<1$},

we can show that for every
a\in(0,1)
,

(2.19)

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}^{\ell}h_{t}(n)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{\ell+1/2}}\Big(\frac{1}{|n|}\Big)^{2\ell}.

This, along with (
2.18
), implies that

|\partial_{t}^{\ell}h_{t}(n)|\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{\ell+1/2}}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{n}\Big)^{\ell}\sim\frac{1}{t^{\ell+1/2}}\Big(1+\frac{n}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{-\ell}.

This completes our proof.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

For
k,\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and
t\geq 1
, we define

g_{t,k}(x)=\frac{(-1)^{\lfloor(k+1)/2\rfloor}}{\pi x^{k}}\int_{0}^{\pi}\partial_{\theta}^{k}\partial_{t}^{\ell}\varphi_{t}(\theta)\Phi_{k}(x\theta)d\theta,\ \ \ x\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\},

and

g_{t,0}(x)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}e^{-2t(1-\cos\theta)}\cos(x\theta)d\theta,\ \ \ x\in\mathbb{R}.

Then for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, there exists
C>0
such that

|\partial_{x}^{j}g_{t,k}(x)|\leq\frac{C}{|x|^{j}t^{\ell+1/2}}\Big(\frac{|x|}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{-k}+\frac{C}{t^{\ell+j/2+1/2}}\Big(\frac{|x|}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{-k}

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
.

In particular, if
k=0
and
j\in\mathbb{N}
then we have

|\partial_{x}^{j}g_{t,0}(x)|\leq\frac{C}{t^{\ell+j/2+1/2}}

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

The case
k=0
is similar and even easier. Therefore, we need only to prove the first estimate for
k\geq 1
.

Indeed, we have

\displaystyle|\partial_{x}^{j}g_{t,k}(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i=0}^{j}\Big|\partial_{x}^{i}\Big(\frac{1}{x^{k}}\Big)\Big|\int_{0}^{\pi}|\partial_{\theta}^{k}\partial_{t}^{\ell}\varphi_{t}(\theta)\theta^{j}\Phi^{(j-i)}_{k}(x\theta)|d\theta.

It is clear that

\Big|\partial_{x}^{i}\Big(\frac{1}{x^{k}}\Big)\Big|\lesssim\frac{1}{|x|^{k+i}},\ \ x\neq 0.

On the other hand, arguing similarly to the proof of Lemm
2.5
, we come up with

\int_{0}^{\pi}|\partial_{\theta}^{k}\partial_{t}^{\ell}\varphi_{t}(\theta)\theta^{j}\Phi^{(j-i)}_{k}(x\theta)|d\theta\lesssim\frac{t^{k/2}}{t^{\ell+(j-i)/2+1/2}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle|\partial_{x}^{j}g_{t,k}(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i=0}^{j}\frac{1}{|x|^{k+i}}\frac{t^{k/2}}{t^{\ell+(j-i)/2+1/2}}=\sum_{i=0}^{j}\frac{C}{t^{\ell+j/2+1/2}}\Big(\frac{|x|}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{-k-i}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|x|^{j}t^{\ell+1/2}}\Big(\frac{|x|}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{-k}+\frac{C}{t^{\ell+j/2+1/2}}\Big(\frac{|x|}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{-k}

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
.

This completes our proof.
∎

The following estimate is about the difference derivative estimates of the heat kernel. We have:

Lemma 2.7
.

For any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
C=C(\ell)
such that

(2.20)

|D\partial^{\ell}_{t}h_{t}(n)|+|D^{*}\partial^{\ell}_{t}h_{t}(n)|\leq\frac{C}{t^{\ell+1}}\Big(1+\frac{|n|}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{-\ell}

for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and all
t>0
.

Proof.

From (
2.13
), we have, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

D\partial_{t}^{\ell}h_{t}(n)=g_{t,k}(n+1)-g_{t,k}(n).

where
g_{t,k}
is the function defined in Lemma
2.6
.

For
t\geq 1
and
|n|\geq\sqrt{t}
, applying Lemma
2.6
and the mean value theorem for
k=\ell
, we have

\displaystyle|D\partial_{t}^{\ell}h_{t}(n)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|n|t^{\ell+1/2}}\Big(\frac{|n|}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{-\ell}+\frac{1}{t^{\ell+1}}\Big(\frac{|n|}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{-\ell}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{\ell+1}}\Big(\frac{|n|}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{-\ell}.

For
t\geq 1
and
|n|<\sqrt{t}
, applying Lemma
2.6
and the mean value theorem again but for
k=0
, we have

\displaystyle|D\partial_{t}^{\ell}h_{t}(n)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{\ell+1}}.

These two estimates imply that for
t\geq 1
,

\displaystyle|D\partial_{t}^{\ell}h_{t}(n)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{\ell+1}}\Big(1+\frac{n}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{-\ell}.

For
t\in(0,1)
, from (
2.17
), we have

\displaystyle|D\partial_{t}^{\ell}h_{t}(n)|=|D\Delta_{d}^{\ell}h_{t}(n)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}},

which implies the desired estimate for
t\in(0,1)
and
|n|<\sqrt{t}
.

For
t\in(0,1)
and
|n|\geq\sqrt{t}
, from above inequality,

(2.21)

|D\partial_{t}^{\ell}h_{t}(n)|=|D\Delta_{d}^{\ell}h_{t}(n)|\lesssim\frac{t^{\ell}}{t^{\ell+1}}.

On the other hand, similarly to the proof of Lemma
2.6
, we have, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\neq 0
,

\displaystyle|\partial_{x}g_{t,k}(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|x|^{k+1}}\int_{0}^{\pi}|\partial_{\theta}^{k}\partial_{t}^{\ell}\varphi_{t}(\theta)\Phi_{k}(x\theta)|d\theta+\frac{1}{|x|^{k}}\int_{0}^{\pi}|\partial_{\theta}^{k}\partial_{t}^{\ell}\varphi_{t}(\theta)\theta\Phi^{\prime}_{k}(x\theta)|d\theta.

Then arguing similarly to (
2.19
),

\displaystyle|\partial_{x}g_{t,k}(x)|\lesssim\frac{1}{|x|t^{\ell+1/2}}\Big(\frac{1}{|x|}\Big)^{k}+\frac{1}{t^{\ell+1}}\Big(\frac{1}{|x|}\Big)^{k}

On the other hand, from (
2.13
),

D\partial^{\ell}_{t}h_{t}(n)=g_{t}(n+1)-g_{t}(n).

These two estimates above and the mean value theorem imply that for each
k=2\ell
,

\displaystyle|D\partial^{\ell}_{t}h_{t}(n)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|n|t^{\ell+1/2}}\Big(\frac{1}{|n|}\Big)^{2\ell}+\frac{1}{t^{\ell+1}}\Big(\frac{1}{|n|}\Big)^{2\ell}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{\ell+1}}\Big(\frac{1}{|n|}\Big)^{2\ell}

for
t\in(0,1)
and
|n|\geq\sqrt{t}
.

At this stage, interpolating this and (
2.21
), we come up with

|D\partial^{\ell}_{t}h_{t}(n)|\leq\frac{C}{t^{\ell+1}}\Big(1+\frac{|n|}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{-\ell}

for all
t\in(0,1)
and
|n|\geq\sqrt{t}
.

Similarly,

|D^{*}\partial^{\ell}_{t}h_{t}(n)|\leq\frac{C}{t^{\ell+1}}\Big(1+\frac{|n|}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{-\ell}

for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and all
t>0
.

This completes our proof.
∎

For the higher difference derivative of the heat kernel, denote by
\mathfrak{D}
either
D
or
D^{*}
and by
\mathfrak{D}^{*}
the conjugate of
\mathfrak{D}
. Then we have:

Lemma 2.8
.

For any
\ell,k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
C=C(\ell,k)
such that

(2.22)

|\mathfrak{D}^{k}\partial^{k\ell}_{t}h_{t}(n)|\leq\frac{C}{t^{k\ell+(k+1)/2}}\Big(1+\frac{|n|}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{-\ell}

for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and all
t>0
.

Proof.

We need only to give the proof for the case
k=2
. The case
k\geq 3
can be done by induction. Note that

\mathfrak{D}e^{-t\Delta_{d}}f=e^{-t\Delta_{d}}\mathfrak{D}^{*}f.

It follows that

\mathfrak{D}^{2}\partial^{2\ell}e^{-t\Delta_{d}}=\mathfrak{D}\partial^{\ell}e^{-\frac{t}{2}\Delta_{d}}\circ\mathfrak{D}^{*}\partial^{\ell}e^{-\frac{t}{2}\Delta_{d}}.

This, along with Lemma
2.7
, implies the desired estimates.

This completes our proof.
∎

2.2.
A functional calculus of
\Delta_{d}

In this section, we will investigate some kernel estimates of the functional calculus of
\Delta_{d}
. Firstly, we have:

Lemma 2.9
.

For any
q\in(4,\infty]
, there exists
C=C(q)>0
such that

\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}|F(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})(m,n)|^{2}\leq CR\|\delta_{R}F\|_{q}^{2}

for any Borel function
F
such that
\operatorname{supp}F\subset[0,R]
with
R\in(0,2]
, where
\delta_{R}F=F(R\cdot)
.

Proof.

We have

F(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})(m,n)=\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{-1}\big[F(\sqrt{2(1-\cos\theta)})\big](m-n)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}|F(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})(m,n)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\big\|\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{-1}\big[F(\sqrt{2(1-\cos\theta)})\big]\big\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}

\displaystyle=\big\|F(\sqrt{2(1-\cos\theta)})\big\|^{2}_{L^{2}[-\pi,\pi]}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|F(\sqrt{2(1-\cos\theta)})|^{2}d\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|F(2\sin(|\theta|/2))|^{2}d\theta\sim\int_{0}^{\pi/2}|F(2\sin\theta)|^{2}d\theta.

By Hölder’s inequality, for
p\in(2,\infty]
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi/2}|F(2\sin\theta)|^{2}d\theta

\displaystyle\leq\Big[\int_{0}^{\pi/2}|F(2\sin\theta)|^{2p}\cos\theta d\theta\Big]^{1/p}\Big[\int_{0}^{\pi/2}(\cos\theta)^{1-p^{\prime}}d\theta\Big]^{1/p^{\prime}}.

Since
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi/2}(\cos\theta)^{1-p^{\prime}}d\theta<\infty
for
p\in(2,\infty]
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi/2}|F(2\sin\theta)|^{2}d\theta

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big[\int_{0}^{\pi/2}|F(2\sin\theta)|^{2p}\cos\theta d\theta\Big]^{1/p}

\displaystyle\sim\Big[\int_{0}^{2}|F(u)|^{2p}du\Big]^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim R\|\delta_{R}F\|_{2p}^{2}

for every
p\in(2,\infty]
.

This completes our proof.
∎

Remark 2.10
.

In the continuous case of the Laplacian on
\mathbb{R}
, Lemma
2.9
holds true for
q=2
. In our discrete setting, it is not clear to us if Lemma
2.9

holds true for some
q\in[2,4]
.

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
q\in(4,\infty]
,
R\in(0,2]
and
s>0
. Then for any
\epsilon>0
there exists a constant
C=C(q,s,\epsilon)
such that

(2.23)

\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}|F(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})(m,n)|^{2}(1+R|m-n|)^{2s}\leq CR\|\delta_{R}F\|_{W^{s+\epsilon}_{q}}^{2}

for all functions
F\in W^{s+\epsilon}_{q}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\operatorname{supp}\,F\subset[R/2,R]
, where
\delta_{R}F=F(R\cdot)
.

Proof.

Set
G(\lambda)=F(R\sqrt{\lambda})e^{\lambda}
. Then we have

G(\Delta_{d}/R^{2})e^{-\Delta_{d}/R^{2}}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-R^{-2}(1-i\tau)\Delta_{d}}\widehat{G}(\tau)d\tau,

where
\widehat{G}
is the usual Fourier transform of
G
on
\mathbb{R}
.

Therefore,

F(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-R^{-2}(1-i\tau)\Delta_{d}}\widehat{G}(\tau)d\tau,

which yields that

F(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})(m,n)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{G}(\tau)h_{(1-i\tau)/R^{2}}(m-n)d\tau.

As a consequence,

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}

\displaystyle|F(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})(m,n)|^{2}(1+R|m-n|)^{2s}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widehat{G}(\tau)|\Big(\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}|h_{(1-i\tau)/R^{2}}(m-n)|^{2}(1+R|m-n|)^{2s}\Big)^{1/2}d\tau

Using Lemma
2.4
, for a fixed
\kappa>1
, we have

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}

\displaystyle|F(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})(m,n)|^{2}(1+R|m-n|)^{2s}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widehat{G}(\tau)|\Big[\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}R^{2}\Big(1+\frac{R|m-n|}{\sqrt{1+|\tau|^{2}}}\Big)^{-2s-\kappa}(1+R|m-n|)^{2s}\Big]^{1/2}d\tau.

It is easy to check that

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}R^{2}\Big(1+\frac{R|m-n|}{\sqrt{1+|\tau|^{2}}}\Big)^{-2s-\kappa}(1+R|m-n|)^{2s}

\displaystyle\leq(1+|\tau|^{2})^{s+\kappa/2}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}R^{2}(1+R|m-n|)^{-\kappa}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+|\tau|)^{2s+\kappa}R.

Therefore, for
\epsilon>0
, by Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}|F(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})(m,n)|^{2}(1+R|m-n|)^{s}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{R}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widehat{G}(\tau)|(1+|\tau|)^{s+\kappa/2}d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{R}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widehat{G}(\tau)|^{2}(1+|\tau|)^{2s+\kappa+1+\epsilon}d\tau\Big]^{1/2}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1+|\tau|)^{-1-\epsilon}d\tau\Big]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{R}\|G\|_{W_{2}^{s+(1+\kappa+\epsilon)/2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{R}\|G\|_{W_{q}^{s+(1+\kappa+\epsilon)/2}}.

From this and Lemma
2.9
, by using the trick of interpolation as in
[
13
,
27
]
, we deduce (
2.23
).

This completes our proof.

∎

Lemma 2.12
.

For
q\in(4,\infty]
, there exists
C=C(q)>0
such that

|F(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})(m,n)|\leq CR\|F\|_{q}

for any Borel function
F
such that
\operatorname{supp}F\subset[0,R]
with
R\in(0,2]
.

Proof.

We have

F(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})=G(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})e^{-\Delta_{d}/R^{2}},

where
G(\lambda)=F(\lambda)e^{\lambda^{2}/R^{2}}
.

This, along with Hölder’s inequality, Lemma
2.9
, Lemma
2.2
and Lemma
2.1
, implies that

\displaystyle|F(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})(m,n)|

\displaystyle=\Big|\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}G(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})(m,k)h_{R^{-2}}(n-k)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\|G(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})(m,\cdot)\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\|h_{R^{-2}}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}

\displaystyle\leq R\|G\|_{q}\sim R\|F\|_{q}.

This completes our proof.
∎

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
q\in(4,\infty]
,
R\in(0,2]
and
s>0
. Then for any
\epsilon>0
there exists a constant
C=C(q,s,\epsilon)
such that

(2.24)

|F(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})(m,n)|\leq R(1+R|m-n|)^{-s}\|\delta_{R}F\|_{W^{s+\epsilon}_{q}}

for all functions
F\in W^{s+\epsilon}_{q}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\operatorname{supp}\,F\subset[R/2,R]
.

Proof.

We follow idea in
[
13
,
27
]
. Set
G(\lambda)=F(R\sqrt{\lambda})e^{\lambda}
. Then we have

G(\Delta_{d}/R^{2})e^{-\Delta_{d}/R^{2}}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-R^{-2}(1-i\tau)\Delta_{d}}\widehat{G}(\tau)d\tau,

where
\widehat{G}
is the usual Fourier transform of
G
on
\mathbb{R}
.

Therefore,

F(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-R^{-2}(1-i\tau)\Delta_{d}}\widehat{G}(\tau)d\tau,

which yields that

F(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})(m,n)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{G}(\tau)h_{(1-i\tau)/R^{2}}(m-n)d\tau.

Using Lemma
2.4
and Hölder’s inequality, we have, for
\epsilon>0
,

\displaystyle|F(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})(m,n)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widehat{G}(\tau)|R\Big(1+\frac{R|m-n|}{\sqrt{1+|\tau|^{2}}}\Big)^{-s}d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim R\big(1+R|m-n|\big)^{-s}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widehat{G}(\tau)|(1+|\tau|)^{s}d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim R\big(1+R|m-n|\big)^{-s}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widehat{G}(\tau)|^{2}(1+|\tau|)^{2s+1+\epsilon}d\tau\Big]^{1/2}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1+|\tau|)^{-1-\epsilon}d\tau\Big]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim R\big(1+R|m-n|\big)^{-s}\|G\|_{W_{2}^{s+(1+\epsilon)/2}}.

Since
G(\lambda)=F(R\sqrt{\lambda})e^{\lambda}
and
\operatorname{supp}F\subset[R/2,R]
, we have
\|G\|_{W_{2}^{s+(1+\epsilon)/2}}\sim\|\delta_{R}F\|_{W_{2}^{s+(1+\epsilon)/2}}\lesssim\|\delta_{R}F\|_{W_{q}^{s+(1+\epsilon)/2}}
for
q\in(4,\infty]
. Therefore,

|F(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})(m,n)|\lesssim R\big(1+R|m-n|\big)^{-s}\|\delta_{R}F\|_{W_{q}^{s+(1+\epsilon)/2}}.

From this and Lemma
2.9
, by using the trick of interpolation as in
[
13
,
27
]
, we deduce (
2.24
).

This completes our proof.

∎

Proposition
2.13
directly deduces the following corollary.

Corollary 2.14
.

Let
\psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
be a Schwartz function supported in
[2,8]
. Then we have

|\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})(m,n)|\leq\frac{C_{N}}{t}\Big(1+\frac{|m-n|}{t}\Big)^{-N}

for all
N,t>0
and
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
\psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
be a Schwartz function supported in
[2,8]
. Then we have

|\mathfrak{D}^{k}\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})(m,n)|\leq\frac{C_{N,k}}{t^{k+1}}\Big(1+\frac{|m-n|}{t}\Big)^{-N}

for all
N,t>0
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

We write

\mathfrak{D}^{k}\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})=\mathfrak{D}^{k}(t^{2}\Delta_{d})^{kN}e^{-t^{2}\Delta_{d}}\circ(t^{2}\Delta_{d})^{kN}e^{-t^{2}\Delta_{d}}\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}}).

By Lemma
2.7
and Corollary
2.14
,

|K_{\mathfrak{D}^{k}(t^{2}\Delta_{d})^{kN}e^{-t^{2}\Delta_{d}}}(m,n)|\lesssim\frac{C_{N,k}}{t^{k+1}}\Big(1+\frac{|m-n|}{t}\Big)^{-N}

and

|K_{(t^{2}\Delta_{d})^{kN}e^{-t^{2}\Delta_{d}}\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})}(m,n)|\lesssim\frac{C_{N,k}}{t}\Big(1+\frac{|m-n|}{t}\Big)^{-N}

for all
N,t>0
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

It follows that

|\mathfrak{D}^{k}\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})(m,n)|\leq\frac{C_{N,k}}{t^{k+1}}\Big(1+\frac{|m-n|}{t}\Big)^{-N}

for all
N,t>0
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

This completes the proof.
∎

2.3.
Distributions

The class of test functions
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{Z})
is defined as the set of all functions
f
such that

(2.25)

\|f\|_{m}:=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(1+|n|)^{m}\sup_{0\leq\ell\leq m}|\Delta_{d}^{\ell}f(n)|<\infty,\ \ \forall m>0,\ell\in\mathbb{N}.

It is easy to see that the norm
\|\cdot\|_{m}
is equivalent to the norm
\|\cdot\|^{*}_{m}
defined by

\|f\|^{*}_{m}:=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(1+|n|)^{m}|f(n)|<\infty,\ \ \forall m>0,\ell\in\mathbb{N}.

It is easy to see that
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{Z})
is a complete locally convex space with topology generated by the family of semi-norms
\{\|f\|_{m}:\,m>0,\ell\in\mathbb{N}\}
. As usual, we define the space of distributions
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
as the set of all continuous linear functional on
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{Z})
. It can be verified that
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
is the set of all functions
f
such that

|f(n)|\leq C_{N}(1+|n|)^{N}

for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and for some
N>0
.

It is obvious that if
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{Z})
, then
\Delta_{d}^{k}f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{Z})
for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. From Lemma
2.2
, we obtain
h_{t}(\cdot)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{Z})
for every
t>0
, and hence
\partial_{t}^{k}h_{t}(\cdot)=(-\Delta_{d})^{k}h_{t}(\cdot)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{Z})
for every
t>0
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

We also define

\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{Z})=\{f:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{R}:f(n)=p(n),n\in\mathbb{Z}\ \ \text{for some polynomial $p$}\}.

It is not difficult to show that

\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{Z})=\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}):\exists m\in\mathbb{N},\Delta_{d}^{m}f=0\}.

We now define the space
\mathcal{S}_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
as the set of all functions
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{Z})
such that for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
g_{k}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{Z})
so that
f=\Delta_{d}^{k}g_{k}
. Note that such an
g_{k}
, if exists, is unique. The topology in
\mathcal{S}_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
is generated by the following family of semi-norms

\|f\|_{m,k}=\|g_{k}\|_{m},\ \forall m,k\in\mathbb{N}

where
f=\Delta_{d}^{k}g_{k}
.

We will show the following important result.

Lemma 2.16
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
. Suppose that
f=\Delta_{d}^{k}g
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{Z})
. Then for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

\|g\|_{m}\lesssim\|f\|_{m+4k}.

Proof.

By iteration, it suffices to prove the lemma for
k=1
. Assume that
f=\Delta_{d}g
. We need to claim that

\|g\|_{m}\lesssim\|f\|_{m+4}

for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
.

Recall that
D
and
D^{*}
are operators defined by

Df(n)=f(n+1)-f(n),\ \ \ \ D^{*}f(n)=f(n)-f(n-1).

Then we have
-\Delta_{d}=D^{*}D=DD^{*}
.

Therefore, we can reduce to prove the following inequality

(2.26)

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(1+|n|)^{m}|h(n)|\leq C_{m}\min\Big\{\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(1+|n|)^{m+2}|Dh(n)|,\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(1+|n|)^{m+2}|D^{*}h(n)|\Big\}

for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
h\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{Z})
.

We only prove the inequality (
2.26
) for
Dh
since the inequality for
D^{*}h
can be done similarly.

Let
h\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{Z})
. Define
f=Dh
with
h\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{Z})
. We need to prove that

(2.27)

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(1+|n|)^{m}|h(n)|\leq C_{m}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(1+|n|)^{m+2}|f(n)|

for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. If
n>0
, then we have

h(n)=\sum_{k=n-1}^{\infty}f(k),

which implies that

\displaystyle(1+|n|)^{m}|h(n)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=n-1}^{\infty}(1+|n|)^{m}|f(k)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=n-1}^{\infty}\frac{(1+|k|)^{m+2}|f(k)|}{(1+|k|)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(1+|k|)^{m+2}|f(k)|,

which implies (
2.27
).

If
n\leq 0
, then we write

h(n)=\sum_{k=n-1}^{-\infty}f(k).

Similarly, we also obtain that

(1+|n|)^{m}|h(n)|\leq\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(1+|k|)^{m+2}|f(k)|.

This proves (
2.27
).

Therefore, this completes our proof.
∎

We denote by
\mathcal{S}_{\infty}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
the set of all continuous linear functionals on
\mathcal{S}_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
. We then have:

Proposition 2.17
.

The
following
identification
is
valid
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})/\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{Z})=\mathcal{S}_{\infty}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
.

Proof.

From Lemma
2.16
, we have
\mathcal{S}_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{Z})
. Consequently, if
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\infty}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
, by the Hahn-Banach theorem, we can extend
f
to
\widetilde{f}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
and the equivalence class, which will be denoted by
F\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})/\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{Z})
. Hence,
\mathcal{S}_{\infty}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})\subset\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})/\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{Z})
.

Conversely, let
F\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})/\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{Z})
. Assume that
f_{1},f_{2}\in F
, then there exists
p\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{Z})
such that
f_{1}=f_{2}+p
. Then we have, for each
g\in\mathcal{S}_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
,

\displaystyle\langle f_{1},g\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle f_{2}+p,g\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle f_{2},g\rangle+\langle p,g\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle f_{2},g\rangle.

It follows that we can associate with
F
a unique bounded linear functional in
\mathcal{S}_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
defined by

F(g)=\langle f,g\rangle

for every
f\in F
and
g\in\mathcal{S}_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
. It follows that
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})/\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{Z})\subset\mathcal{S}_{\infty}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
.

This completes our proof.
∎

From Corollary
2.14
, let
\psi\in\mathscr{\mathbb{R}}
with
\operatorname{supp}\psi\subset[2,8]
. Then
\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})(\cdot,m),\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})(m,\cdot)\in\mathcal{S}_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
for every
t>0
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}
. Therefore, for any
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
we can define

\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f(n)=\langle\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})(n,\cdot),f\rangle.

In what follows, by a “
partition of unity
” we shall mean a function
\psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\operatorname{supp}\psi\subset[2,8]
,
\int\psi(\xi)\,\frac{d\xi}{\xi}\neq 0
and

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\psi_{j}(\lambda)=1\textup{ on }(0,\infty),

where
\psi_{j}(\lambda):=\psi(2^{-j}\lambda)
for each
j\in\mathbb{Z}
.

We have the following result which can be viewed as the Calderón reproducing formula regarding the functional calculus of the discrete Laplacian
\Delta_{d}
.

Proposition 2.18
.

Let
\psi
be a partition of unity. Then for any
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\infty}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
we have

f=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}}\psi_{j}(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f\ \ \text{in $\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})/\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{Z})$}.

Similarly, for any
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\infty}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
we have

f=c_{\psi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f\frac{dt}{t}\ \ \text{in $\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})/\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{Z})$},

where
c_{\psi}=\Big[\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(s)\frac{ds}{s}\Big]^{-1}
.

Proof.

By duality it suffices to prove that for each
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
,

f=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}}\psi_{j}(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f\ \ \text{in $\mathcal{S}_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})$}.

To do this, we fix
N>0
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
, there exists
g_{k}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{Z})
so that
f=\Delta_{d}^{k+\ell}g_{k}
, where
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
will be fixed later. Then we have,

\displaystyle\|\psi_{j}(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f\|_{N,k}

\displaystyle=\|\Delta_{d}^{\ell}\psi_{j}(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})g_{k}\|_{N}

\displaystyle=2^{2j\ell}\|\psi_{j,\ell}(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})g_{k}\|_{N}

\displaystyle=2^{2j\ell}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{0\leq i\leq N}(1+|n|)^{N}|\Delta_{d}^{i}\psi_{j,\ell}(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})g_{k}(n)|

\displaystyle=2^{2j(\ell+i)}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{0\leq i\leq N}(1+|n|)^{N}|\psi_{j,\ell+i}(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})g_{k}(n)|,

where
=\psi_{j,l}(\lambda)=(2^{j}\lambda)^{2l}\psi_{j}(\lambda)
for
l\in\mathbb{N}
.

By Lemma
2.14
we have

|\psi_{j,\ell+i}(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})(n,m)|\leq\frac{C_{N}}{2^{-j}}\Big(1+\frac{|m-n|}{2^{-j}}\Big)^{-N-2}.

Hence,

\displaystyle(1+|n|)^{N}|\psi_{j,\ell+i}(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})g_{k}(n)|

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+|n|)^{N}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{2^{-j}}\Big(1+\frac{|m-n|}{2^{-j}}\Big)^{-N-2}|g_{k}(m)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|g\|_{N}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{2^{-j}}\Big(1+\frac{|m-n|}{2^{-j}}\Big)^{-N-2}(1+|m-n|)^{N}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-jN}\|g_{k}\|_{N}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{2^{-j}}\Big(1+\frac{|m-n|}{2^{-j}}\Big)^{-2}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \text{(since $j\leq 0$)}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-jN}\|g_{k}\|_{N}.

Using Lemma
2.16
,

(1+|n|)^{N}|\psi_{j,\ell+i}(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})g_{k}(n)|\lesssim 2^{-jN}\|f\|_{N+2(k+\ell)}

for all
0\leq i\leq N
.

As a consequence, taking
\ell>(N+1)/2
, we have

\displaystyle\|\psi_{j}(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f\|_{N,k}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{2j(\ell+i-N/2)}\|f\|_{N+2(k+\ell)}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{j}\|f\|_{N+2(k+\ell)}.

This, along with the fact that
\mathcal{S}_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
is complete, we deduce that there exists
h\in\mathcal{S}_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
so that

h=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}}\psi_{j}(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f\ \ \text{in $\mathcal{S}_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})$}.

On the other hand, by spectral theory we have

f=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}}\psi_{j}(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f\ \ \text{in $\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})$}.

Therefore,
f\equiv h
and this concludes the proposition.

The second identity can be done similarly.

This completes our proof.
∎

3.
Hardy spaces

The theory of Hardy space
H^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
,
0<p\leq 1
was investigated in
[
7
,
6
,
24
]
. In these papers, the atomic decomposition and the molecular decomposition of the Hardy space
H^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
were obtained. The decomposition was inspired from the corresponding decomposition of the classical Hardy spaces. However, the existing decomposition is not suitable to our purpose in studying the boundedness of the spectral multipliers of the discrete Laplacian
\Delta_{d}
. In this section, we will approach the Hardy space
H^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
via new molecular decomposition. This approach is inspired from those in
[
15
,
22
,
21
,
4
]
.

3.1.
Molecular decomposition

Consider the area square function
S
associated to
\Delta_{d}
defined by

(3.1)

S_{\Delta_{d}}f(n)=\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{m:|m-n|<t}\big|t^{2}\Delta_{d}e^{-t^{2}\Delta_{d}}f(m)\big|^{2}\frac{dt}{t(t+1)}\Big)^{1/2}.

Note that the square function
S_{\Delta_{d}}f
is well-defined for distributions
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
. However, similarly to the classical case, the whole class of distributions
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
is too large for our later purpose. In fact, we need only distributions which vanish weakly at infinity in the following sense.

Definition 3.1
.

We say that a distribution
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
vanishes weakly at infinity if
(t\Delta_{d})^{k}e^{-t\Delta_{d}}f\to 0
in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
as
t\to\infty
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

For
0<p\leq 1
, we define the Hardy space
H^{p}_{S_{\Delta_{d}}}(\mathbb{Z})
to be the set of
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
vanishing weakly at infinity such that

\|f\|_{H^{p}_{S_{\Delta_{d}}}(\mathbb{Z})}:=\big\|S_{\Delta_{d}}f\big\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}<\infty.

In
[
22
,
21
,
23
]
, the Hardy spaces associated to operators were defined via the completion of the set of
L^{2}
-functions satisfying certain conditions. In this paper, by using the space of distribution
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
we can define the Hardy spaces directly.

We now adapt ideas in
[
21
,
23
]
to define the molecule associated to the discrete Laplacian
\Delta_{d}
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\epsilon>0
,
0<p\leq 1
and
M\in\mathbb{N}
. A function
a(n)
is called a
(p,M,\epsilon)_{\Delta_{d}}
-molecule associated to an interval
I\subset\mathbb{Z}
if there exists a function
b
such that

(i)

a=\Delta_{d}^{M}b
;

(ii)

\|\Delta_{d}^{k}b\|_{\ell^{2}(S_{j}(I))}\leq 2^{-j\epsilon}\ell(I)^{2(M-k)}|2^{j}I|^{1/2-1/p}
for all
k=0,1,\dots,M
and
j=0,1,2\ldots
.

Definition 3.3
.

Given
\epsilon>0
,
0<p\leq 1
and
M\in\mathbb{N}
, we say that
f=\sum\lambda_{j}a_{j}
is a molecule
(p,M,\epsilon)_{\Delta_{d}}
-representation if

\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}\in\ell^{p}
, each
a_{j}
is a
(p,M,\epsilon)_{\Delta_{d}}
-molecule,
and the sum converges in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
. The space
H^{p}_{\Delta_{d},{\rm mol},M,\epsilon}(\mathbb{Z})
is then defined by

H^{p}_{\Delta_{d},{\rm mol},M,\epsilon}(\mathbb{Z}):=\left\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}):f\ \text{has a molecule
$(p,M,\epsilon)_{\Delta_{d}}$-representation}\right\},

with the norm given by

\|f\|^{p}_{H^{p}_{\Delta_{d},{\rm mol},M,\epsilon}(\mathbb{Z})}=\inf\left\{\sum|\lambda_{j}|^{p}:f=\sum\lambda_{j}a_{j}\ \text{is a molecule $(p,M,\epsilon)_{\Delta_{d}}$-representation}\right\}.

By the standard argument, it can be verified that
H^{p}_{\Delta_{d},{\rm mol},M,\epsilon}(\mathbb{Z})
is complete. We will show that the Hardy spaces
H^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
and
H^{p}_{\Delta_{d},{\rm mol},M,\epsilon}(\mathbb{Z})
are coincide. To do this, we need the following technical ingredients.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
p\in(0,1]
,
M>\frac{1}{2}\big(\frac{1}{p}-1\big)
and
\epsilon>0
. For each
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, the operator

\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\ell}f(n)=\sup_{t>0}\sup_{|m-n|<t}|(t^{2}\Delta)^{\ell}e^{-t^{2}\Delta_{d}}f(m)|

is bounded from
H^{p}_{\Delta_{d},{\rm mol},M,\epsilon}(\mathbb{Z})
to
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
.

Proof.

Since the kernel
h_{t,\ell}(\cdot)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{Z})
for each
t>0
and
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, it suffices to show that there exists
C>0
such that

\|\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\ell}a\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}\leq C

for every
(p,M,\epsilon)_{\Delta_{d}}
molecule
a
.

Suppose that
a
is a
(p,M,\epsilon)_{\Delta_{d}}
associated to an interval
I
. Then we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\ell}a\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big\|\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\ell}(I-e^{-\ell(I)^{2}\Delta_{d}})^{M}a\big\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}^{p}+\big\|\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\ell}\big[I-(I-e^{-\ell(I)^{2}\Delta_{d}})^{M}\big]a\big\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}^{p}

\displaystyle=:E+F.

We take care of the term
E
first. To do this, we write

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\geq 0}\big\|\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\ell}(I-e^{-\ell(I)^{2}\Delta_{d}})^{M}(a\cdot 1_{S_{j}(I)})\big\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}^{p}

\displaystyle=:\sum_{j\geq 0}E_{j}.

For each
j\geq 0
, by Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle E_{j}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\geq 0}\|\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\ell}(I-e^{-\ell(I)^{2}\Delta_{d}})^{M}(a\cdot 1_{S_{j}(I)})\|_{\ell^{p}(S_{k}(2^{j}I))}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\geq 0}|2^{k+j}I|^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\|\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\ell}(I-e^{-\ell(I)^{2}\Delta_{d}})^{M}(a\cdot 1_{S_{j}(I)})\|_{\ell^{2}(S_{k}(2^{j}I))}^{p}

\displaystyle=:\sum_{k\geq 0}E_{jk}.

For
k=0,1,2,3
, by the
L^{2}
-boundedness of
\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\ell}(I-e^{-\ell(I)^{2}\Delta_{d}})^{M}
we have

\displaystyle E_{jk}

\displaystyle\leq|2^{j}I|^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\|\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\ell}(I-e^{-\ell(I)^{2}\Delta_{d}})^{M}(a\cdot 1_{S_{j}(I)})\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim|2^{j}I|^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\|a\|_{\ell^{2}(S_{j}(I))}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-j\epsilon}|2^{j}I|^{\frac{2-p}{2}}|2^{j}I|^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\sim 2^{-(j+k)\epsilon}.

For
k\geq 4
, note that

(I-e^{-\ell(I)^{2}\Delta_{d}})^{M}=\int_{[0,\ell(I)^{2}]^{M}}\Delta^{M}_{d}e^{-|\vec{s}|\Delta_{d}}d\vec{s},

where
d\vec{s}=ds_{1}\ldots ds_{M}
and
|\vec{s}|=s_{1}+\ldots+s_{M}
.

Then we have, for
n\in S_{k}(2^{j}(I))
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\ell}(I-e^{-\ell(I)^{2}\Delta_{d}})^{M+\ell}(a\cdot 1_{S_{j}(I)})(n)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{t>0}\sup_{|m-n|<t}\int_{[0,\ell(I)^{2}]^{M}}t^{2\ell}|\Delta_{d}^{M+\ell}e^{-(t^{2}+|\vec{s}|)\Delta_{d}}(a\cdot 1_{S_{j}(I)})(m)|d\vec{s}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{t>2^{k+j}\ell(I)/4}\ldots+\sup_{0<t\leq 2^{k+j}\ell(I)/4}\ldots

For
n\in S_{k}(2^{j}(I))
and
m\in I
, we have
t>|m-n|\geq 1
. Hence, applying Lemma
2.4
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\sup_{t>2^{k+j}\ell(I)/4}\sup_{|m-n|<t}

\displaystyle\int_{[0,\ell(I)^{2}]^{M}}t^{2\ell}|\Delta_{d}^{M+\ell}e^{-(t^{2}+|\vec{s}|)\Delta_{d}}(a\cdot 1_{S_{j}(I)})(m)|d\vec{s}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{t>2^{k+j}\ell(I)/4}\sup_{|m-n|<t}\int_{[0,\ell(I)^{2}]^{M}}\sum_{l\in S_{j}(I)}\frac{t^{2\ell}}{(t^{2}+|\vec{s}|)^{M+\ell+1/2}}|a(l)|d\vec{s}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-(k+j)(2M+1)}|I|^{-1}\|a\|_{\ell^{1}(S_{j}(B))},

and

\displaystyle\sup_{0<t\leq 2^{k+j}\ell(I)/4}

\displaystyle\sup_{|m-n|<t}\int_{[0,\ell(I)^{2}]^{M}}t^{2\ell}|\Delta_{d}^{M+\ell}e^{-(t^{2}+|\vec{s}|)\Delta_{d}}(a\cdot 1_{S_{j}(I)})(m)|d\vec{s}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{0<t\leq 2^{k+j}\ell(I)/4}\sup_{|m-n|<t}\int_{[0,\ell(I)^{2}]^{M}}\sum_{l\in S_{j}(I)}\frac{t^{2\ell}}{(t^{2}+|\vec{s}|)^{M+\ell+1/2}}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t^{2}+|\vec{s}|}}{|m-l|}\Big)^{2M+2\ell+1}|a(l)|d\vec{s}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-(k+j)(2M+1)}|I|^{-1}\|a\|_{\ell^{1}(S_{j}(B))}\ \ \ \ \ \text{(since $|m-l|\sim 2^{k+j}\ell(I)$)}.

It follows that

\displaystyle E_{jk}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-(k+j)(2M+1)p}|2^{k+j}I|^{\frac{2-p}{2}}|I|^{-p}\|a\|_{\ell^{1}(S_{j}(B))}^{p}|2^{j+k}I|^{p/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-(k+j)[(2M+1)p-1]}.

As a consequence,

E\lesssim\sum_{j,k\geq 0}2^{-(j+k)\epsilon}\sim 1,

as long as
M>\frac{1}{2}\big(\frac{1}{p}-1\big)
.

It remains to show that
F\lesssim 1
. Since

I-(I-e^{-\ell(I)^{2}\Delta_{d}})^{M}=\sum_{i=1}^{M}c_{i}e^{-i\ell(I)^{2}\Delta_{d}},

it suffices to prove that for each
i=1,\ldots,M
,

\big\|\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\ell}(\Delta_{d}^{M}e^{-i\ell(I)^{2}\Delta_{d}}b)\big\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}^{p}\lesssim 1,

where
a=\Delta_{d}^{M}b
.

At this stage, we can argue similarly to the estimate of
E
to come up with

\big\|\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\ell}(\Delta_{d}^{M}e^{-i\ell(I)^{2}\Delta_{d}}b)\big\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}^{p}\lesssim 1

for each
i=1,\ldots,M
.

This completes our proof.
∎

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
0<p\leq 1
,
M\in\mathbb{N}
and
\epsilon>0
. Suppose that
A
is a
T_{2}^{p}
-atom supported in
\widehat{I}
with some interval
I\subset\mathbb{Z}
. Then for every
M\geq 1
and
N\geq 1/p+\epsilon
, the function

\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(t^{2}\Delta_{d}\big)^{M+N}e^{-t^{2}\Delta_{d}}A(n,t)\frac{dt}{t}

define a multiple of
(p,M,\epsilon)_{\Delta_{d}}
-molecule associated to the interval
I
with a multiple constant depending on
M
only.

Proof.

Let
A
be a
T_{2}^{p}
-atom supported in
\widehat{I}
with some interval
I
. Then we have,

\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{n\in I}|A(n,t)|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\leq|I|^{1-\frac{2}{p}}.

We now define

a(n)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(t^{2}\Delta_{d}\big)^{M+N}e^{-t^{2}\Delta_{d}}\big(A(\cdot,t)\big)(n)\frac{dt}{t}=\Delta_{d}^{M}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{2M}\big(t^{2}\Delta_{d}\big)^{N}e^{-t^{2}\Delta_{d}}\big(A(\cdot,t)\big)(n)\frac{dt}{t}.

Hence,
a=\Delta_{d}^{M}b
, where

b(n)=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{2M}\big(t^{2}\Delta_{d}\big)^{N}e^{-t^{2}\Delta_{d}}\big(A(\cdot,t)\big)(n)\frac{dt}{t}.

In addition, let
f\in\ell^{2}(S_{j}(I))
such that
\operatorname{supp}f\subseteq S_{j}(I)
and
\|f\|_{\ell^{2}(S_{j}(I))}=1
. Then, for each
k=0,1,\ldots,M
,

\displaystyle\Big|\langle\Delta_{d}^{k}b,f\rangle_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Delta_{d}^{k}b(n)f(n)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{n\in I}t^{2(M-k)}\Big|A(n,t)\big(t^{2}\Delta_{d}\big)^{N+k}e^{-t^{2}\Delta_{d}}f(n)\Big|\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\ell(I)}\sum_{n\in I}t^{2(M-k)}\Big|A(n,t)\big(t^{2}\Delta_{d}\big)^{N+k}e^{-t^{2}\Delta_{d}}f(n)\Big|\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\int_{0}^{\ell(I)}\sum_{n\in I}|A(n,t)|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\ell(I)}\sum_{n\in I}t^{4(M-k)}\Big|\big(t^{2}\Delta_{d}\big)^{N+k}e^{-t^{2}\Delta_{d}}f(n)\Big|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq|I|^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\ell(I)}\sum_{n\in I}t^{4(M-k)}\Big|\big(t^{2}\Delta_{d}\big)^{N+k}e^{-t^{2}\Delta_{d}}f(n)\Big|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

For
j=0,1,2
, using the
L^{2}
-boundedness of
\big(t^{2}\Delta_{d}\big)^{N+k}e^{-t^{2}\Delta_{d}}
,

\displaystyle\Big|\langle\Delta_{d}^{k}b,f\rangle_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq|I|^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\ell(I)}\sum_{n\in I}t^{4(M-k)}\|f\|_{\ell^{2}(S_{j}(\mathbb{Z}))}\frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq(\ell(I))^{2(M-k)}|I|^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\sim 2^{-j\epsilon}\ell(I)^{2(M-k)}|2^{j}I|^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}.

By duality, for
j=0,1,2,

\|\Delta_{d}^{k}b\|_{\ell^{2}(S_{j}(\mathbb{Z}))}\lesssim 2^{-j\epsilon}\ell(I)^{2(M-k)}|2^{j}I|^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}.

For
k=0,1,2,\ldots,M
,
j\geq 3
and
f\in\ell^{2}(S_{j}(\mathbb{Z}))
, using Lemma
2.4
,

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in I}\Big|\big(t^{2}\Delta_{d}\big)^{N+k}e^{-t^{2}\Delta_{d}}f(n)\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{n\in I}\Big|\sum_{m\in S_{j}(I)}\frac{1}{t}\Big(1+\frac{|m-n|}{t}\Big)^{-N}|f(m)|\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim|I|t^{2(N-1)}(2^{j}\ell(I))^{-2N}\|f\|_{\ell^{1}(S_{j}(I))}^{2}\ \ \quad(\text{since $|m-n|\sim 2^{j}\ell(I)$})

\displaystyle\lesssim|I|t^{2(N-1)}(2^{j}\ell(I))^{-2N}|2^{j}I|\|f\|_{\ell^{2}(S_{j}(I))}^{2}\quad\quad(\text{H\"{o}lder's inequality})

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-j(2N-1)}\Big(\frac{t}{\ell(I)}\Big)^{2(N-1)}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{0}^{\ell(I)}\sum_{n\in I}t^{4(M-k)}\Big|\big(t^{2}\Delta_{d}\big)^{N+k}e^{-t^{2}\Delta_{d}}f(n)\Big|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-j(N-1/2)}\ell(I)^{2(M-k)}\|f\|_{\ell^{2}(S_{j}(\mathbb{Z}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-j(N-1/2)}\ell(I)^{2(M-k)}\|f\|_{\ell^{2}(S_{j}(\mathbb{Z}))}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-j(N-1/2)}\ell(I)^{2(M-k)}\|f\|_{\ell^{2}(S_{j}(\mathbb{Z}))}.

Taking supremum over all
f
such that
\|f\|_{\ell^{2}(S_{j}(I))}=1
we obtain

\|\Delta_{d}^{k}b\|_{\ell^{2}(S_{j}(I))}\leq c_{M}2^{-j\epsilon}\ell(I)^{2(M-k)}|2^{j}I|^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}

for all
k=0,1,\ldots,M
provided that
N\geq 1/p+\epsilon
.

This completes the proof.

Proposition 3.11
.

For
0<p\leq 1
,
\epsilon>0
and
M\geq 1
. Then

H^{p}_{S_{\Delta_{d}}}(\mathbb{Z})\hookrightarrow H^{p}_{\Delta_{d},{\rm mol},M,\epsilon}(\mathbb{Z}).

Proof.

Suppose
f\in H^{p}_{S_{\Delta_{d}}}(\mathbb{Z}).
We set
F(n,t)=t^{2}\Delta_{d}e^{-t^{2}\Delta_{d}}f(n)
. From the definition of
H_{S_{\Delta_{d}}}^{p}
,
F(n,t)\in T_{2}^{p}
. Thus, by using Lemma
3.8
,
F
can be represented in the form
F=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}A_{i}
, where
A_{i}
is
T_{2}^{p}
-atom. In addition,

\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}|\lambda_{i}|^{p}\leq C\|F\|^{p}_{T_{2}^{p}}=C\|f\|^{p}_{H^{p}_{S_{\Delta_{d}}}}.

Since
f
vanishes weakly at infinity, by Lemma
3.9
,

(3.2)

f=C\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(t^{2}\Delta_{d}\big)^{M+N}e^{-t^{2}\Delta_{d}}t^{2}\Delta_{d}e^{-t^{2}\Delta_{d}}f\frac{dt}{t}\ \ \text{in $\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})$},

where
N\geq 1/p+1+\epsilon
.

It follows that

(3.3)

\displaystyle f(n)

\displaystyle=C\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(t^{2}\Delta_{d}\big)^{M+N}e^{-t^{2}\Delta_{d}}A_{i}(n,t)\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=:C\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}a_{i}.

We will show that the identity (
3.3
) holds in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
. It suffices to show the series in (
3.3
) converges in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
. To do this, we need to prove that for
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{Z})
,

\lim_{L\to\infty}\sum_{i=L}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}\langle a_{i},\phi\rangle=0.

Assume
A_{i}
is a
T_{2}^{p}
-atom supported in
\widehat{I}_{i}
with some interval
I_{i}
for each
i
. Then we have

\displaystyle\langle a_{i},\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\big(t^{2}\Delta_{d}\big)^{M+N}e^{-t^{2}\Delta_{d}}A_{i}(n,t)\phi(n)\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\ell(I_{i})}\sqrt{t}\sum_{n\in I}A_{i}(n,t)\sqrt{t}D\big(t^{2}\Delta_{d}\big)^{M+N-1}e^{-t^{2}\Delta_{d}}(D\phi)(n)\frac{dt}{t},

where in the last inequality we used the fact that
\Delta_{d}^{M+N}e^{-t^{2}\Delta_{d}}\phi=-D\Delta_{d}^{M+N-1}e^{-t^{2}\Delta_{d}}(D\phi)
.

By Hölder’s inequality,

(3.4)

\displaystyle|\langle a_{i},\phi\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\ell(I_{i})}\sqrt{t}\|A_{i}(\cdot,t)\|_{\ell^{2}(I)}\|\sqrt{t}D\big(t^{2}\Delta_{d}\big)^{M+N-1}e^{-t^{2}\Delta_{d}}(D\phi)\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\frac{dt}{t}.

From the kernel bound of
\sqrt{t}D\big(t^{2}\Delta_{d}\big)^{M+N-1}e^{-t^{2}\Delta_{d}}
in Lemma
2.7
and Lemma
2.1
, we yield that the operator
\sqrt{t}D\big(t^{2}\Delta_{d}\big)^{M+N-1}e^{-t^{2}\Delta_{d}}
is bounded on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
for all
1<p\leq\infty
. Consequently,

\|\sqrt{t}D\big(t^{2}\Delta_{d}\big)^{M+N-1}e^{-t^{2}\Delta_{d}}(D\phi)\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\lesssim\|D\phi\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}.

On the other hand, since
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{Z})
,
D\phi\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}<\infty
. It follows that

\|\sqrt{t}D\big(t^{2}\Delta_{d}\big)^{M+N-1}e^{-t^{2}\Delta_{d}}(D\phi)\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}<\infty.

Plugging this into (
3.4
) and then using Hölder’s inequality we further obtain

\displaystyle|\langle a_{i},\phi\rangle|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\ell(I)}\sqrt{t}\|A_{i}(\cdot,t)\|_{\ell^{2}(I_{i})}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\int_{0}^{\ell(I_{i})}\|A_{i}(\cdot,t)\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}(I)}\frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\int_{0}^{\ell(I_{i})}t\frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim|I_{i}|^{1/2-1/p}|\ell(I)|^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim|I_{i}|^{1-1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim 1,

where in the last inequality we used the facts
0<p\leq 1
and
|I_{i}|\geq 1
for each
i
.

As a consequence,

\sum_{i=L}^{\infty}|\lambda_{i}\langle a_{i},\phi\rangle|\lesssim\sum_{i=L}^{\infty}|\lambda_{i}|\to 0\ \ \text{as $L\to\infty$},

since
\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}|\lambda_{i}|^{p}\lesssim\|f\|^{p}_{H^{p}_{S_{\Delta_{d}}}}
.

Therefore, the identity (
3.3
) holds in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
. In addition, in Lemma
3.10
we have proved that each
a_{i}
is multiple of a
(p,M,\epsilon)_{\Delta_{d}}
-molecule with a harmless constant. Therefore,
f\in{H}^{p}_{\Delta_{d},{\rm mol},M,\epsilon}(\mathbb{Z})
and

\|f\|_{H^{p}_{\Delta_{d},{\rm mol},M,\epsilon}(\mathbb{Z})}\lesssim\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\lesssim\|f\|_{H_{S_{\Delta_{d}}}^{p}}.

This completes our proof.

Proposition 3.12
.

For
0<p\leq 1
,
\epsilon>0
and
M\geq 1
. Then

H^{p}_{\Delta_{d},{\rm mol},M,\epsilon}(\mathbb{Z})\hookrightarrow H^{p}_{S_{\Delta_{d}}}(\mathbb{Z}).

Proof.

Since the kernel
\partial_{t}h_{t}(\cdot)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{Z})
, it suffices to show that there exists
C>0
such that

\|S_{\Delta_{d}}a\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}\leq C

for every
(p,M,\epsilon)_{\Delta_{d}}
molecule
a
.

Suppose that
a
is a
(p,M,\epsilon)_{\Delta_{d}}
associated to an interval
I\subset\mathbb{Z}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\|S_{\Delta_{d}}a\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big\|S_{\Delta_{d}}(I-e^{-\ell(I)^{2}\Delta_{d}})^{M}a\big\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}^{p}+\big\|S_{\Delta_{d}}\big[I-(I-e^{-\ell(I)^{2}\Delta_{d}})^{M}\big]a\big\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}^{p}

\displaystyle=:E+F.

We take care of the term

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\geq 0}\big\|S_{\Delta_{d}}(I-e^{-\ell(I)^{2}\Delta_{d}})^{M}(a\cdot 1_{S_{j}(I)})\big\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}^{p}

\displaystyle=:\sum_{j\geq 0}E_{j}.

For each
j\geq 0
, by Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle E_{j}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\geq 0}\|S_{\Delta_{d}}(I-e^{-\ell(I)^{2}\Delta_{d}})^{M}(a\cdot 1_{S_{j}(I)})\|_{\ell^{p}(S_{k}(2^{j}I))}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\geq 0}|2^{k+j}I|^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\|S_{\Delta_{d}}(I-e^{-\ell(I)^{2}\Delta_{d}})^{M}(a\cdot 1_{S_{j}(I)})\|_{\ell^{2}(S_{k}(2^{j}I))}^{p}

\displaystyle=:\sum_{k\geq 0}E_{jk}.

For
k=0,1,2,3
, by the
L^{2}
-boundedness of
S_{\Delta_{d}}\circ(I-e^{-\ell(I)^{2}\Delta_{d}})^{M}
we have

\displaystyle E_{jk}

\displaystyle\leq|2^{j}I|^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\|S_{\Delta_{d}}(I-e^{-\ell(I)^{2}\Delta_{d}})^{M}(a\cdot 1_{S_{j}(I)})\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq|2^{j}I|^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\|a\|_{\ell^{2}(S_{j}(I))}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-j\epsilon}|2^{j}I|^{\frac{2-p}{2}}|2^{j}I|^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\sim 2^{-(j+k)\epsilon}.

For
k\geq 4
, note that

(I-e^{-\ell(I)^{2}\Delta_{d}})^{M}=\int_{[0,\ell(I)^{2}]^{M}}\Delta^{M}_{d}e^{-|\vec{s}|\Delta_{d}}d\vec{s},

where
d\vec{s}=ds_{1}\ldots ds_{M}
and
|\vec{s}|=s_{1}+\ldots+s_{M}
.

Then we have, for
n\in S_{k}(2^{j}(I))
,

\displaystyle\big[S_{\Delta_{d}}

\displaystyle(I-e^{-\ell(I)^{2}\Delta_{d}})^{M}(a\cdot 1_{S_{j}(I)})(n)\big]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{m:|m-n|<t}\Big[\int_{[0,\ell(I)^{2}]^{M}}t^{2}|\Delta_{d}^{M+1}e^{-(t^{2}+|\vec{s}|)\Delta_{d}}(a\cdot 1_{S_{j}(I)})(m)|d\vec{s}\Big]^{2}\frac{dt}{t(t+1)}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{2^{k+j}\ell(I)/4}^{\infty}\ldots+\int_{0}^{2^{k+j}\ell(I)/4}\ldots

\displaystyle=:F_{1}+F_{2}.

Since for
n\in S_{k}(2^{j}(I))
and
m\in I
,
t>|m-n|\geq 1
. By Lemma
2.4
,

\displaystyle F_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{2^{k+j}\ell(I)/4}^{\infty}\sum_{m:|m-n|<t}\Big[\int_{[0,\ell(I)^{2}]^{M}}\sum_{l\in S_{j}(I)}\frac{t^{2}}{(t^{2}+|\vec{s}|)^{M+3/2}}|a(l)|d\vec{s}\Big]^{2}\frac{dt}{t(t+1)}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-(k+j)(4M+2)}|I|^{-2}\|a\|^{2}_{\ell^{1}(S_{j}(B))},

and

\displaystyle F_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int^{2^{k+j}\ell(I)/4}_{0}\sum_{m:|m-n|<t}\Big[\int_{[0,\ell(I)^{2}]^{M}}\sum_{l\in S_{j}(I)}\frac{t^{2}}{(t^{2}+|\vec{s}|)^{M+3/2}}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t^{2}+|\vec{s}|}}{|m-l|}\Big)^{2M+3}|a(l)|d\vec{s}\Big]^{2}\frac{dt}{t(t+1)}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-(k+j)(4M+2)}|I|^{-2}\|a\|^{2}_{\ell^{1}(S_{j}(B))}\ \ \ \ \ \text{(since $|m-l|\sim 2^{k+j}\ell(I)$)}.

It follows that

\displaystyle E_{jk}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-(k+j)(2M+1)p}|2^{k+j}I|^{\frac{2-p}{2}}|I|^{-p}\|a\|_{\ell^{1}(S_{j}(B))}^{p}|2^{j+k}I|^{p/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-(j+k)[(2M+1)p-1]}.

As a consequence,

E\lesssim\sum_{j,k\geq 0}2^{-(j+k)\epsilon}\sim 1,

as long as
M>\frac{1}{2}\big(\frac{1}{p}-1\big)
.

It remains to show that
F\lesssim 1
. Since

I-(I-e^{-\ell(I)^{2}\Delta_{d}})^{M}=\sum_{i=1}^{M}c_{i}e^{-i\ell(I)^{2}\Delta_{d}},

it suffices to prove that for each
i=1,\ldots,M
,

\big\|S_{\Delta_{d}}(\Delta_{d}^{M}e^{-i\ell(I)^{2}\Delta_{d}}b)\big\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}^{p}\lesssim 1,

where
a=\Delta_{d}^{M}b
.

At this stage, we can argue similarly to the estimate of
E
to come up with

\big\|S_{\Delta_{d}}(\Delta_{d}^{M}e^{-i\ell(I)^{2}\Delta_{d}}b)\big\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}^{p}\lesssim 1

for each
i=1,\ldots,M
.

This completes our proof. ∎

Remark 3.13
.

(a) For each
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we define

(3.5)

S_{\Delta_{d},N}f(n)=\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{m:|m-n|<t}\big|(t^{2}\Delta_{d})^{N}e^{-t^{2}\Delta_{d}}f(m)\big|^{2}\frac{dt}{t(t+1)}\Big)^{1/2}.

Then we can define the Hardy spaces
H^{p}_{S_{\Delta_{d},N}}(\mathbb{Z})
similarly to
H^{p}_{S_{\Delta_{d}}}(\mathbb{Z})
. By a careful examination, we also have
H^{p}_{S_{\Delta_{d},N}}(\mathbb{Z})=H^{p}_{\Delta_{d}}(\mathbb{Z})
for all
0<p\leq 1
.

(b) Let
\psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
supported in
[2,8]
. We now define

S_{\psi}f(n)=\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{m:|m-n|<t}\big|\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f(m)\big|^{2}\frac{dt}{t(t+1)}\Big)^{1/2}.

For
0<p\leq 1
, we define the Hardy space
H^{p}_{\psi}(\mathbb{Z})
to be the completion of the set

\big\{f\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}):S_{\psi}f\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})\big\}

under the norm

\|f\|_{H^{p}_{\psi}(\mathbb{Z})}:=\big\|S_{\psi}f\big\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}<\infty.

Then by the argument in the proof of Theorem
3.6
, it is easy to see that
H^{p}_{\psi}(\mathbb{Z})=H^{p}_{\Delta_{d}}(\mathbb{Z})
.

3.2.
Coincidence with the classical Hardy spaces

Let
0<p<q
and
p\leq 1\leq q\leq\infty
. A function
a
is called a
(p,q)
atom if there exists an interval
I\subset\mathbb{Z}
such that

(i)

\operatorname{supp}a\subset I
;

(ii)

\|a\|_{\ell^{q}(\mathbb{Z})}\leq|I|^{1/q-1/p}
;

(iii)

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}n^{\alpha}a(n)=0
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
n\leq 1/p-1
.

The Hardy space
H^{p,q}(\mathbb{Z})
is the set of all functions
f\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
if
p=1
and
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
if
0<p<1
such that there exist a sequence of
(p,q)
atoms
\{a_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
and a sequence of numbers
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfying that
\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}|\lambda_{j}|^{p}<\infty
and
f=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\lambda_{j}a_{j}
, where the series converges in
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
if
p=1
and in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
if
0<p<1
. For each
f\in H^{p,q}(\mathbb{Z})
, we define

\|f\|_{H^{p,q}(\mathbb{Z})}^{p}=\inf\Big\{\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}|\lambda_{j}|^{p}:f=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\lambda_{j}a_{j}\Big\},

where the infimum is taken over all atomic decomposition of
f
.

It is well-known that for
0<p<q
and
p\leq 1\leq q\leq\infty
, we have
H^{p,q}(\mathbb{Z})=H^{p,\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
. For this reason, for
0<p<q
and
p\leq 1\leq q\leq\infty
we define
H^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
to be any space
H^{p,q}(\mathbb{Z})
.

Let
0<p\leq 1<q\leq\infty
and
\alpha>1/p-1/q
. A function
b
is said to be a
(p,q,\alpha)
-molecule centered in
n_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
if the following holds

(i)

\|b\|^{1-\theta}_{\ell^{q}(\mathbb{Z})}\||\cdot-n_{0}|^{\alpha}b\|_{\ell^{q}(\mathbb{Z})}^{\theta}<\infty
, where
\theta=(1/p-1/q)/\alpha
;

(ii)

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}n^{\beta}b(n)=0
for every
\beta\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\beta\leq 1/p-1
.

Theorem 3.14
.

For
0<p\leq 1
, we have

H^{p}_{\Delta_{d}}(\mathbb{Z})\equiv H^{p}(\mathbb{Z})

with equivalent norms.

Proof.

If
a=\Delta_{d}^{M}b
is a
(p,M,\epsilon)_{\Delta_{d}}
molecule associated to an interval
I\subset\mathbb{Z}
with
M>(1/p-1)/2
and
\epsilon>0
, then it suffices to prove that
a
is a
(p,2,\alpha)
molecule with
\epsilon>\alpha>0
centered at any
n_{0}\in I
. Indeed, we first have

\|a\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\leq\sum_{j\geq 0}\|a\|_{\ell^{2}(S_{j}(I))}\leq\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{-j\epsilon}|2^{j}I|^{1/2-1/p}.

Secondly, for
\theta=(1/p-1/2)/\alpha
,

\displaystyle\||\cdot-n_{0}|^{\alpha}a\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\geq 0}\||\cdot-n_{0}|^{\alpha}a\|_{\ell^{2}(S_{j}(I))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{j\alpha}\|a\|_{\ell^{2}(S_{j}(I))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{j(\alpha-\epsilon)}|2^{j}I|^{1/2-1/p}.

It follows that for
\theta=(1/p-1/2)/\alpha
,

\displaystyle\|a\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}^{1-\theta}\||\cdot-n_{0}|^{\alpha}a\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}^{\theta}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{-j(\epsilon-\theta\alpha)}|2^{j}I|^{1/2-1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{-j\epsilon}|I|^{1/2-1/p}

\displaystyle<\infty.

On the other hand, for any
\beta\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\beta\leq 1/p-1
, we have

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}n^{\beta}a(n)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Delta_{d}^{M}n^{\beta}b(n).

Since
M>(1/p-1)/2\geq\beta/2
,
\Delta_{d}^{M}n^{\beta}=0
. Hence,

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}n^{\beta}a(n)=0.

As a consequence, each
(p,M,\epsilon)_{\Delta_{d}}
molecule is also a
(p,q,\alpha)
-molecule, which implies that

H^{p}_{\Delta_{d}}(\mathbb{Z})\hookrightarrow H^{p}(\mathbb{Z}).

It remains to prove that
H^{p}(\mathbb{Z})\hookrightarrow H^{p}_{\Delta_{d}}(\mathbb{Z})
. To do this, by Remark
3.13
it suffices to prove that there exists
C>0
such that

\|S_{\Delta_{d},M}a\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}\leq C

for all
(p,2)
atom, where
M\in\mathbb{N}
such that
M>\lfloor 1/p-1\rfloor+1=:K
.

Suppose that
a
is a
(p,2)
atom associated to an interval
I
. Then we have

\displaystyle\|S_{\Delta_{d},M}a\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big\|S_{\Delta_{d},M}a\big\|_{\ell^{p}(4I)}^{p}+\big\|S_{\Delta_{d},M}a\big\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}\backslash 4I)}^{p}.

For the first term, by the
L^{2}
-boundedness of
S_{\Delta_{d},M}
we have

\displaystyle\big\|S_{\Delta_{d},M}a\big\|_{\ell^{p}(4I)}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq|4I|^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\|S_{\Delta_{d},M}a\|_{\ell^{2}(4I)}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim|I|^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\|a\|_{\ell^{2}(I)}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim 1.

For
n\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash 4I
, we have

\displaystyle\big[S_{\Delta_{d},M}a(n)\big]^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{m:|m-n|<t}\Big[|(t^{2}\Delta_{d})^{M}e^{-t^{2}\Delta_{d}}a(m)|d\vec{s}\Big]^{2}\frac{dt}{t(t+1)}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\ell(I)}\ldots+\int_{\ell(I)}^{\infty}\ldots

\displaystyle=:E_{1}+E_{2}.

Since
t>|m-n|\geq 1
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash 4I
and
m\in I
, by Lemma
2.4
,

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int^{\ell(I)}_{0}\sum_{m:|m-n|<t}\Big[\sum_{l\in I}\frac{1}{t}\Big(\frac{t}{|m-l|}\Big)^{2M}|a(l)|\Big]^{2}\frac{dt}{t(t+1)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int^{\ell(I)}_{0}\sum_{m:|m-n|<t}\Big[\sum_{l\in I}\frac{1}{t}\Big(\frac{t}{d(n,I)}\Big)^{2M}|a(l)|\Big]^{2}\frac{dt}{t(t+1)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int^{\ell(I)}_{0}\Big[\sum_{l\in I}\frac{1}{t}\Big(\frac{t}{d(n,I)}\Big)^{2M}|a(l)|\Big]^{2}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big[\|a\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})}\frac{\ell(I)^{2M-1}}{d(n,I)^{2M}}\Big]^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big[\|a\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})}\frac{\ell(I)^{K}}{d(n,I)^{K+1}}\Big]^{2}.

For the second term
E_{2}
, we have

Using (
2.13
), we have for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle(-\Delta_{d})^{M}e^{-t\Delta_{d}}a(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in I}\frac{(-1)^{\lfloor(k+1)/2\rfloor}}{\pi{(n-m)}^{k}}\int_{0}^{\pi}\partial_{\theta}^{k}\partial_{t}^{M}\varphi_{t}(\theta)\Phi_{k}((n-m)\theta)a(m)d\theta

\displaystyle:=\sum_{m\in I}g_{M,k,t}(n-m)a(m).

Due to the vanishing moment condition of the atom
a
, for a fixed
n_{I}\in I
and
K:=\lfloor 1/p-1\rfloor+1
,

(-\Delta_{d})^{M}e^{-t\Delta_{d}}a(n)=\sum_{m\in I}\Big[g_{M,K+1,t}(n-m)-\sum_{j=0}^{K}\frac{(n_{I}-m)^{j}}{j!}g^{(j)}_{\ell,K+1,t}(n-n_{I})\Big]a(m).

Applying Taylor theorem and Lemma
2.6
, for
n\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash 4I,

(3.6)

\displaystyle|(t\Delta_{d})^{M}e^{-t\Delta_{d}}a(n)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{m\in I}\Big(\frac{\ell(I)}{|n-m|\wedge\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{K+1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\Big(\frac{|n-m|}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{-K-1}|a(m)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{\ell(I)}{d(n,I)\wedge\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{K+1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\Big(\frac{d(n,I)}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{-K-1}\|a\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})}.

On the other hand, we also have

(-\Delta_{d})^{M}e^{-t\Delta_{d}}a(n)=\sum_{m\in I}\Big[g_{M,K+1,t}(n-m)-\sum_{j=0}^{K}\frac{(n_{I}-m)^{j}}{j!}g^{(j)}_{\ell,0,t}(n-n_{I})\Big]a(m).

Applying Taylor theorem and Lemma
2.6
again, for
n\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash 4I,

(3.7)

\displaystyle|(t\Delta_{d})^{M}e^{-t\Delta_{d}}a(n)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{m\in I}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\Big(\frac{\ell(I)}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{K+1}|a(m)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\Big(\frac{\ell(I)}{d(n,I)\wedge\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{K+1}\|a\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})}.

From (
3.6
) and (
3.7
), we have, for
n\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash 4I,

(3.8)

\displaystyle|(t\Delta_{d})^{M}e^{-t\Delta_{d}}a(n)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{\ell(I)}{d(n,I)\wedge\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{K+1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\Big(1+\frac{d(n,I)}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{-K-1}\|a\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\Big(\frac{\ell(I)}{d(n,I)}\Big)^{K+1}\|a\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})}.

It follows that

E_{2}\lesssim\Big[\|a\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})}\frac{\ell(I)^{K}}{d(n,I)^{K+1}}\Big]^{2}.

Therefore, for
n\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash 4I
,

S_{\Delta_{d},M}a(n)\lesssim\|a\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})}\frac{\ell(I)^{K}}{d(n,I)^{K+1}}\lesssim|I|^{1-1/p}\frac{\ell(I)^{K}}{d(n,I)^{K+1}}.

As a consequence,

\big\|S_{\Delta_{d},M}a\big\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}\backslash 4I)}\leq C.

This completes our proof.
∎

4.
Besov and Triebel–Lizorkin spaces associated to
\Delta_{d}

The main aim of this section is to develop the theory of Besov and Triebel–Lizorkin spaces associated to
\Delta_{d}
. This is motivated by the work of the authors with H-Q. Bui
[
8
]
. It is interesting to note that our function spaces are identical with the classical spaces defined by
[
30
,
32
]
. Therefore, the obtained results in this section can be viewed as new characterizations for the classical Besov and Triebel–Lizorkin spaces.

As we mentioned in Section
2.3
, if
\psi\in\mathscr{\mathbb{R}}
with
\operatorname{supp}\psi\subset[2,8]
, then

\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})(\cdot,m),\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})(m,\cdot)\in\mathcal{S}_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})

for every
t>0
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}
. Therefore, for any
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
we can define

\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f(n)=\langle\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})(n,\cdot),f\rangle.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\psi
be a partition of unity. For
0<p,q\leq\infty
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the homogeneous Besov space
\dot{B}^{\alpha,\psi,\Delta_{d}}_{p,q}(\mathbb{Z})
as follows

\dot{B}^{\alpha,\psi,\Delta_{d}}_{p,q}(\mathbb{Z})=\Big\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})/\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{Z}):\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{\alpha,\psi,\Delta_{d}}_{p,q}(\mathbb{Z})}<\infty\Big\},

where

\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{\alpha,\psi,\Delta_{d}}_{p,q}(\mathbb{Z})}=\Big\{\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}}\left(2^{j\alpha}\|\psi_{j}(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}\right)^{q}\Big\}^{1/q}.

Similarly, for
0<p<\infty
,
0<q\leq\infty
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, the homogeneous Triebel–Lizorkin space
\dot{F}^{\alpha,\psi}_{p,q}(\mathbb{Z})
is defined by

\dot{F}^{\alpha,\psi,\Delta_{d}}_{p,q}(\mathbb{Z})=\Big\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})/\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{Z}):\|f\|_{\dot{F}^{\alpha,\psi}_{p,q}(\mathbb{Z})}<\infty\},

where

\|f\|_{\dot{F}^{\alpha,\psi,\Delta_{d}}_{p,q}(\mathbb{Z})}=\Big\|\Big[\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}}(2^{j\alpha}|\psi_{j}(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f|)^{q}\Big]^{1/q}\Big\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}.

We would like to highlight that in the above definitions of the Besov and Triebel–Lizorkin spaces, the sums are taken over
j\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}
instead of
j\in\mathbb{Z}
. This is because since the spectral of
\Delta_{d}
is contained in
[0,4]
,
\psi_{j}(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})=0
whenever
j\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
.

It is obvious that if
f\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{Z})
then
\psi_{j}(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f=0
for all
j
. Conversely, we assume that
\psi_{j}(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f=0
for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
. By Proposition
2.18
,
f=0
in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}/\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{Z})
, or equivalently,
f
is a polynomial. As a consequence, if
\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{\alpha,\psi,\Delta_{d}}_{p,q}(\mathbb{Z})}=0
or
\|f\|_{\dot{F}^{\alpha,\psi,\Delta_{d}}_{p,q}(\mathbb{Z})}=0
, then
f\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{Z})
.

Therefore, each of the above spaces is a quasi-normed linear space (normed linear space when
p,q\geq 1
).

For
\lambda>0,j\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
supported in
[2,8]
the Peetre’s type maximal function is defined, for
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})/\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{Z})
, by

(4.1)

\varphi_{j,\lambda}^{*}(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f(n)=\sup_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{|\varphi_{j}(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f(m)|}{(1+2^{j}|m-n|)^{\lambda}}\,,n\in\mathbb{Z},

where
\varphi_{j}(\lambda)=\varphi(2^{-j}\lambda)
.

Obviously, we have

\varphi_{j,\lambda}^{*}(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f(n)\geq|\varphi_{j}(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f(n)|,\ \ \ \ n\in\mathbb{Z}.

Similarly, for
s,\lambda>0
we set

(4.2)

\varphi_{\lambda}^{*}(s\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f(n)=\sup_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{|\varphi(s\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f(m)|}{(1+|m-n|/s)^{\lambda}},\ \ \ f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})/\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{Z}).

We next prove the following result which can be viewed as a consequence of Proposition 3.2 and Proposition 3.3 in
[
8
]
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\varphi
and
\psi
be a partition of unity. Then we have:

(a)

For
0<p,q\leq\infty
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
\lambda>1/p
,

\displaystyle\Big\{\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(2^{j\alpha}\|\varphi^{*}_{j,\lambda}(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}\right)^{q}\Big\}^{1/q}\sim\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{\alpha,\psi,\Delta_{d}}_{p,q}(\mathbb{Z})}.

(b)

For
0<p<\infty
,
0<q\leq\infty
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
\lambda>\max\{1/q,1/p\}
,

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big[\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}(2^{j\alpha}|\varphi^{*}_{j,\lambda}(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f|)^{q}\Big]^{1/q}\Big\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}\sim\|f\|_{\dot{F}^{\alpha,\psi,\Delta_{d}}_{p,q}(\mathbb{Z})}.

Remark 4.3
.

Proposition
4.2
tells us that if
\psi,\varphi
are partition of unities, then
\dot{B}_{p,q}^{\alpha,\psi,\Delta_{d}}(\mathbb{Z})\equiv\dot{B}_{p,q}^{\alpha,\varphi,\Delta_{d}}(\mathbb{Z})
for
0<p,q\leq\infty
and
\dot{F}_{p,q}^{\alpha,\psi,\Delta_{d}}(\mathbb{Z})\equiv\dot{F}_{p,q}^{\alpha,\varphi,\Delta_{d}}(\mathbb{Z})
for
0<p<\infty
and
0<q\leq\infty
. For this reason, for
\alpha\in\mathbb{R},0<p,q\leq\infty
, we define the Besov space
\dot{B}_{p,q}^{\alpha,\Delta_{d}}(\mathbb{Z})
to be the space
\dot{B}_{p,q}^{\alpha,\psi,\Delta_{d}}(\mathbb{Z})
with any partition of unity
\psi
. The Triebel–Lizokin space
\dot{F}_{p,q}^{\alpha,\Delta_{d}}(\mathbb{Z})
for
\alpha\in\mathbb{R},0<p<\infty,0<q\leq\infty
can be defined similarly.

The following theorem regarding the continuous version of the Besov and Triebel–Lizorkin spaces which is taken from
[
8
, Theorem 3.5]
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
supported in
[2,8]
. Then we have:

(a)

For
0<p,q\leq\infty
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
\lambda>1/p
and
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})/\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{Z})
,

(4.3)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\dot{B}_{p,q}^{\alpha,\Delta_{d}}(\mathbb{Z})}

\displaystyle\sim\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big[t^{-\alpha}\|\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}\Big]^{q}\frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\sim\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big[t^{-\alpha}\|\psi^{*}_{\lambda}(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}\Big]^{q}\frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{1/q}.

(b)

For
0<q\leq\infty
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
\lambda>\max\{1/q,1/p\}
and
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})/\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{Z})
,

(4.4)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\dot{F}_{p,q}^{\alpha,\Delta_{d}}(\mathbb{Z})}

\displaystyle\sim\Big\|\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big[t^{-\alpha}|\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f|\Big]^{q}\frac{dt}{t}\Big)\Big\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}

\displaystyle\sim\Big\|\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big[t^{-\alpha}\psi^{*}_{\lambda}(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f\Big]^{q}\frac{dt}{t}\Big)\Big\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}.

Similarly to the classical Triebel–Lizorkin spaces we also have characterizations for Triebel-Lizorkin spaces via Lusin functions and the Littlewood–Paley functions. To see this, for
\alpha\in\mathbb{R},\lambda,a>0
,
0<q<\infty
and a measurable function
F
defined on
\mathbb{Z}\times(0,\infty)
we define the Lusin function and the Littlewood–Paley function by setting

(4.5)

\mathcal{G}^{\alpha}_{\lambda,q}F(n)=\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}(t^{-\alpha}|F(m,t)|)^{q}\Big(1+\frac{|m-n|}{t}\Big)^{-\lambda q}\frac{dt}{t(t+1)}\right]^{1/q}

and

(4.6)

\mathcal{S}^{\alpha}_{a,q}F(n)=\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{m:|m-n|<at}(t^{-\alpha}|F(m,t)|)^{q}\frac{dt}{t(t+1)}\right]^{1/q}

for every measurable function
F
, respectively.

We have the following characterization for the Triebel–Lizorkin spaces which can be viewed as a particular case of Proposition 3.13 in
[
8
]
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\psi
be a partition of unity. Then for
0<p,q<\infty
, and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\|f\|_{\dot{F}^{\alpha,\Delta_{d}}_{p,q}(\mathbb{Z})}\sim\|\mathcal{G}^{\alpha}_{\lambda,q}(\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f)\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}\sim\|\mathcal{S}^{\alpha}_{q}(\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f)\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}

for all
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\infty}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
, provided that
\lambda>\gamma(p,q)
, where

(4.7)

\gamma(p,q)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{p\wedge q},\ \ \ &p\geq q\\
\frac{2}{q}(1-\frac{p}{q}),\ \ \ &p<q.\end{cases}

4.1.
Molecular decomposition

In this section, we aim to establish molecular decomposition for our new Besov and Triebel–Lizorkin spaces.

Definition 4.6
.

Let
0<p\leq\infty
and
N,M\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
. A function
a
is said to be a
(M,N,p)_{\Delta_{d}}
molecule if there exist a function
b
and a dyadic interval
I\in\mathcal{I}
so that

(i)

a=\Delta_{d}^{M}b
;

(ii)

\displaystyle|\Delta_{d}^{k}b(n)|\leq\ell(I)^{2(M-k)}|I|^{-1/p}\Big(1+\frac{d(n,I)}{\ell(I)}\Big)^{-1-N}
,
k=0,\ldots,2M
.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
\psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
with
{\rm supp}\,\psi\subset[2,8]
and
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
be a partition of unity. Then for any
\lambda>0
and
j\in\mathbb{Z}
we have

(4.8)

\sup_{s\in[2^{-j-1},2^{-j}]}\psi^{*}_{\lambda}(s\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f(n)\lesssim\sum_{\ell=j-2}^{j+3}\varphi^{*}_{\ell,\lambda}(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f(n),

for all
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})/\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{Z})
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Fix
j\in\mathbb{Z}
and
s\in[2^{-j-1},2^{-j}]
. First note that

\psi(s\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})=\sum_{\ell=j-2}^{j+3}\psi(s\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})\varphi_{\ell}(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}}).

Since
2^{-j}\sim s
, by Corollary
2.14
we have, for
m\in\mathbb{Z}
and
N>n
,

(4.9)

\displaystyle|\psi(s\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f(m)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell=j-2}^{j+3}|\psi(s\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})\varphi_{\ell}(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f(m)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\ell=j-2}^{j+3}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{(1+2^{-j})}(1+2^{j}|m-k|)^{-N-\lambda}|\varphi_{\ell}(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f(k)|.

It follows that

(4.10)

\displaystyle\frac{|\psi(s\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f(m)|}{(1+|m-n|/s)^{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\ell=j-2}^{j+3}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{(1+2^{-j})}(1+2^{j}|m-k|)^{-N-\lambda}\frac{|\varphi_{\ell}(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f(k)|}{(1+2^{j}|m-n|)^{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\ell=j-2}^{j+3}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{(1+2^{-j})}(1+2^{j}|m-k|)^{-N}\frac{|\varphi_{\ell}(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f(k)|}{(1+2^{j}|m-n|)^{\lambda}(1+2^{j}|m-k|)^{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\ell=j-2}^{j+3}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{(1+2^{-j})}(1+2^{j}|m-k|)^{-N}\frac{|\varphi_{\ell}(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f(k)|}{(1+2^{j}d(x,z))^{\lambda}}.

Using the fact that
2^{\ell}\sim 2^{j}
, we obtain

(4.11)

\displaystyle\frac{|\psi(s\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f(m)|}{(1+|m-n|/s)^{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\ell=j-2}^{j+3}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{(1+2^{-j})}(1+2^{j}|m-k|)^{-N}\frac{|\varphi_{\ell}(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f(k)|}{(1+2^{k}d(x,z))^{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\ell=j-2}^{j+3}\varphi^{*}_{\ell,\lambda}(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f(n)\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{(1+2^{-j})}(1+2^{j}|m-k|)^{-N}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\ell=j-2}^{j+3}\varphi^{*}_{\ell,\lambda}(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f(n),

where in the last inequality we use Lemma
2.1
.
Taking the supremum over
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, we derive (
4.8
).
∎

We first state the molecular decomposition result for the Besov space
\dot{B}^{\alpha,\Delta_{d}}_{p,q}(\mathbb{Z})
.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
0<p,q\leq\infty
,
M\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
. Assume
f\in\dot{B}^{\alpha,\Delta_{d}}_{p,q}(\mathbb{Z})
. Then there exist a sequence of
(M,N,p)_{\Delta_{d}}
atoms
\{a_{I}\}_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{\nu},\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}
and a sequence of coefficients
\{s_{I}\}_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{\nu},\nu\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}}
so that

f=\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}}\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{\nu}}s_{I}a_{I}\ \ \text{in $\mathcal{S}_{\infty}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})$}.

Moreover,

\Big[\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}}2^{\nu\alpha q}\Big(\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{\nu}}|s_{I}|^{p}\Big)^{q/p}\Big]^{1/q}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{\alpha,\Delta_{d}}_{p,q}(\mathbb{Z})}.

Proof.

Let
\psi
be a partition of unity. Due to Proposition
2.18
, for
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})/\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{Z})
we have

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=c\int_{0}^{\infty}(t^{2}\Delta_{d})^{M}\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=c\int_{1}^{\infty}(t^{2}\Delta_{d})^{M}\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f\frac{dt}{t}

in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})/\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{Z})
, where
\displaystyle c=\Big[\int_{0}^{\infty}\xi^{2M}\psi(\xi)^{2}\frac{d\xi}{\xi}\Big]^{-1}
.

As a consequence, we have

(4.12)

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=c\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}}\int_{2^{-\nu}}^{2^{-\nu+1}}(t^{2}\Delta_{d})^{M}\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})[\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f]\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=c\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}}\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{\nu}}\int_{2^{-\nu}}^{2^{-\nu+1}}(t^{2}\Delta_{d})^{M}\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})\big[\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f\cdot 1_{I}\big]\frac{dt}{t}.

For each
\nu\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}
and
I\in\mathcal{I}_{\nu}
, we set

s_{I}=|I|^{1/p}\sup_{m\in I}\int_{2^{-\nu}}^{2^{-\nu+1}}|\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f(m)|\frac{dt}{t},

and
a_{I}=\Delta_{d}^{M}b_{I}
, where

(4.13)

b_{I}=\frac{1}{s_{I}}\int_{2^{-\nu}}^{2^{-\nu+1}}t^{2M}\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})\big[\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f\cdot 1_{I}\big]\frac{dt}{t}.

Obviously, we deduce from (
4.12
) that

f=\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}}\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{\nu}}s_{I}a_{I}\ \ \text{in $\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})/\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{Z})$}.

For
k=0,\ldots,2M
, we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{d}^{k}b_{I}(n)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{s_{I}}\int_{2^{-\nu}}^{2^{-\nu+1}}t^{2(M-k)}(t^{2}\Delta_{d})^{k}\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})[\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f\cdot 1_{I}]\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{s_{I}}\int_{2^{-\nu}}^{2^{-\nu+1}}\sum_{m\in I}t^{2(M-k)}K_{(t^{2}L)^{k}\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})}(n,m)\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f(m)\frac{dt}{t}.

By Corollary
2.14
and the fact that
\ell(I)\sim|I|\sim 2^{-\nu}
,

\displaystyle|\Delta_{d}^{k}b_{I}(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-2\nu(M-k)}\int_{2^{-\nu}}^{2^{-\nu+1}}\sum_{m\in I}\frac{1}{2^{-\nu}}\Big(1+\frac{|m-n|}{2^{-\nu}}\Big)^{-N}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\ell(I))^{-2(M-k)}\Big(1+\frac{d(n,I)}{\ell(I)}\Big)^{-N}

It follows that
a_{I}
is (a multiple of) a
(M,N,p)
molecule.

We now prove that

\Big[\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}}2^{\nu\alpha q}\Big(\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{\nu}}|s_{I}|^{p}\Big)^{q/p}\Big]^{1/q}\sim\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{\alpha,\Delta_{d}}_{p,q}(\mathbb{Z})}.

Indeed, for any
\lambda>0
we note that

\displaystyle s_{I}

\displaystyle\sim|I|^{1/p}\sup_{m\in I}\int_{2^{-\nu}}^{2^{-\nu+1}}|\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f(m)|\frac{dt}{t}\leq\Big[\sum_{m\in I}|F^{*}_{\psi,\nu,\lambda}(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f(m)|^{p}\Big]^{1/p},

where

F^{*}_{\psi,\nu,\lambda}(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f(n)=\sup_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{2^{-\nu}}^{2^{-\nu+1}}|\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f(m)|\frac{dt}{t}}{(1+2^{\nu}|m-n|)^{\lambda}}.

As a consequence,

\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{\nu}}|s_{I}|^{p}\leq\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}|F^{*}_{\psi,\nu,\lambda}(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f(m)|^{p}.

By Proposition
4.7
,

F^{*}_{\psi,\nu,\lambda}(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f(m)\leq\sum_{\ell=\nu-1}^{\nu+4}\psi^{*}_{\ell,\lambda}(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f(m).

This, in combination with Proposition
4.2
, implies

(4.14)

\displaystyle\Big[\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}}2^{\nu\alpha q}\Big(\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{\nu}}|s_{I}|^{p}\Big)^{q/p}\Big]^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq\Big[\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}}2^{\nu\alpha q}\Big(\Big\|\sum_{\ell=\nu-1}^{\nu+4}\psi^{*}_{\ell,\lambda}(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f\Big\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}\Big)^{q}\Big]^{1/q}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big[\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}}2^{\ell\alpha q}\Big(\Big\|\psi^{*}_{\ell,\lambda}(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f\Big\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}\Big)^{q}\Big]^{1/q}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{p,q}(\mathbb{Z})}.

This completes the proof.
∎

For the reverse direction we have:

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
0<p,q\leq\infty
. Assume that

f=\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}}\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{\nu}}s_{I}a_{I}\ \ \text{in $\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})/\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{Z})$},

where
\{a_{I}\}_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{\nu},\nu\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}}
is a sequence of
(M,N,p)_{\Delta_{d}}
molecules and
\{s_{I}\}_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{\nu},\nu\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}}
is a sequence of coefficients satisfying

\Big[\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}}2^{\nu\alpha q}\Big(\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{\nu}}|s_{I}|^{p}\Big)^{q/p}\Big]^{1/q}<\infty.

Then
f\in\dot{B}^{\alpha,\Delta_{d}}_{p,q}(\mathbb{Z})
and

\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{\alpha,\Delta_{d}}_{p,q}(\mathbb{Z})}\lesssim\Big[\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}}2^{\nu\alpha q}\Big(\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{\nu}}|s_{I}|^{p}\Big)^{q/p}\Big]^{1/q}

provided
M>\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\max\{\alpha,\frac{1}{1\wedge p\wedge q}-\alpha\}
and
N>1+\frac{1}{1\wedge p\wedge q}
.

Proof.

By the standard argument in
[
17
,
16
,
8
]
, it suffices to prove the following estimate:
Let
\psi
be a partition of unity and let
a_{I}
be a
(M,N+1,p)
molecule with some
I\in\mathcal{I}_{\nu}
with
\nu\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}
. Then for any
t>0
and
N^{\prime}<N
we have:

(4.15)

|\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})a_{I}(n)|\lesssim\Big(\frac{t}{2^{-\nu}}\wedge\frac{2^{-\nu}}{t}\Big)^{2M-1}|I|^{-1/p}\Big(1+\frac{d(n,I)}{2^{-\nu}\vee t}\Big)^{-N^{\prime}}.

In order to prove (
5.7
), we now consider two cases:
t\leq 2^{-\nu}
and
t>2^{-\nu}
.

Case 1:
t\leq 2^{-\nu}
.
Observe that

\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})a_{I}=t^{2M}\psi_{M}(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})(\Delta_{d}^{M}a_{I})

where
\psi_{M}(\lambda)=\lambda^{-2M}\psi(\lambda)
.

This, along with Corollary
2.14
and the definition of the molecules, yields

\displaystyle|\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})a_{I}(n)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{t^{2M}}{(t+1)}\Big(1+\frac{|m-n|}{t}\Big)^{-N^{\prime}}|a_{I}(m)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big(\frac{t}{2^{-\nu}}\Big)^{2M}|I|^{-1/p}\frac{1}{(t+1)}\Big(1+\frac{|m-n|}{t}\Big)^{-N^{\prime}}\Big(1+\frac{d(m,I)}{2^{-\nu}}\Big)^{-N-1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{t}{2^{-\nu}}\Big)^{2M}|I|^{-1/p}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{t}\Big(1+\frac{|m-n|}{2^{-\nu}}\Big)^{-N^{\prime}}\Big(1+\frac{d(m,I)}{2^{-\nu}}\Big)^{-N-1}.

We now use the inequality

\Big(1+\frac{|m-n|}{2^{-\nu}}\Big)^{-N^{\prime}}\Big(1+\frac{d(m,I)}{2^{-\nu}}\Big)^{-N^{\prime}}\leq\Big(1+\frac{d(n,I)}{2^{-\nu}}\Big)^{-N^{\prime}}

to obtain further that

\displaystyle|\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})a_{I}(n)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{t}{2^{-\nu}}\Big)^{2M-1}|I|^{-1/p}\Big(1+\frac{d(n,I)}{2^{-\nu}}\Big)^{-N^{\prime}}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{2^{-\nu}}\Big(1+\frac{d(n,I)}{2^{-\nu}}\Big)^{-1-N+N^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{t}{2^{-\nu}}\Big)^{2M-1}|I|^{-1/p}\Big(1+\frac{d(n,I)}{2^{-\nu}}\Big)^{-N^{\prime}},

which yields (
5.7
).

Case 2:
t>2^{-\nu}
.
We first write
a_{I}=\Delta_{d}^{M}b_{I}
. Hence,

\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})a_{I}=t^{-2M}\tilde{\psi}_{M}(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})b_{I}

where
\tilde{\psi}_{M}(\lambda)=\lambda^{2M}\psi(\lambda)
.

This, along with Corollary
2.14
, implies that

\displaystyle|\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})a_{I}(n)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{t^{-2M}}{t}\Big(1+\frac{|m-n|}{t}\Big)^{-N^{\prime}}|b_{I}(m)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{2^{-\nu}}{t}\Big)^{2M}|I|^{-1/p}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{t}\Big(1+\frac{|m-n|}{t}\Big)^{-N^{\prime}}\Big(1+\frac{d(m,I)}{2^{-\nu}}\Big)^{-N-1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{2^{-\nu}}{t}\Big)^{2M}|I|^{-1/p}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{t}\Big(1+\frac{|m-n|}{t}\Big)^{-N^{\prime}}\Big(1+\frac{d(m,I)}{t}\Big)^{-N-1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{2^{-\nu}}{t}\Big)^{2M}|I|^{-1/p}\Big(1+\frac{d(n,I)}{t}\Big)^{-N^{\prime}}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{t}\Big(1+\frac{d(m,I)}{t}\Big)^{-1-N+N^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{2^{-\nu}}{t}\Big)^{2M}|I|^{-1/p}\Big(1+\frac{d(n,I)}{t}\Big)^{-N^{\prime}}.

Hence (
5.7
) follows.

This completes our proof.
∎

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
0<p<\infty
,
0<q\leq\infty
,
N>0
and
M\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
. If
f\in\dot{F}^{\alpha,\Delta_{d}}_{p,q}(\mathbb{Z})
, then there exist a sequence of
(M,N,p)_{\Delta_{d}}
molecules
\{a_{I}\}_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{\nu},\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}
and a sequence of coefficients
\{s_{I}\}_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{\nu},\nu\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}}
so that

f=\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}}\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{\nu}}s_{I}a_{I}\ \ \text{in $\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})/\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{Z})$}.

Moreover,

(4.16)

\Big\|\Big[\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}}2^{\nu\alpha q}\Big(\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{\nu}}|I|^{-1/p}|s_{I}|\cdot 1_{I}\Big)^{q}\Big]^{1/q}\Big\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{F}^{\alpha,\Delta_{d}}_{p,q}(\mathbb{Z})}.

Proof.

Recall that in the proof of Theorem
4.8
, we have proved the representation

f=\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}}\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{\nu}}s_{I}a_{I}\ \ \text{in $\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})/\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{Z})$},

where

s_{I}=|I|^{1/p}\sup_{m\in I}\int_{2^{-\nu}}^{2^{-\nu+1}}|\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f(m)|\frac{dt}{t},

and
a_{I}=\Delta_{d}b_{I}
is a
(M,N,p)_{\Delta_{d}}
molecule defined by

b_{I}=\frac{1}{s_{I}}\int_{2^{-\nu}}^{2^{-\nu+1}}t^{2M}\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})[\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f\cdot 1_{I}]\frac{dt}{t}.

It remains to prove (
4.16
). Indeed, for any
\lambda>0
it is easy to see that

\displaystyle|I|^{-1/p}s_{I}\cdot 1_{I}

\displaystyle=\sup_{m\in I}\int_{2^{-\nu}}^{2^{-\nu+1}}|\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f(m)|\frac{dt}{t}\cdot 1_{I}\lesssim 1_{I}.F^{*}_{\psi,\nu,\lambda}(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f

where

As a consequence,

\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{\nu}}|I|^{-1/p}|s_{I}|\cdot 1_{I}\lesssim F^{*}_{\psi,\nu,\lambda}(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f.

By Proposition
4.7

F^{*}_{\psi,\nu,\lambda}(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f(m)\leq\sum_{\ell=\nu-1}^{\nu+4}\psi^{*}_{\ell,\lambda}(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f(m).

This, in combination with Proposition
4.2
, implies

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big[\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}}2^{\nu\alpha q}\Big(\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{\nu}}|I|^{-1/p}|s_{I}|1_{I}\Big)^{q}\Big]^{1/q}\Big\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\Big[\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}}2^{\nu\alpha q}\Big(\sum_{\ell=\nu-1}^{\nu+4}\psi^{*}_{\ell,\lambda}(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f\Big)^{q}\Big]^{1/q}\Big\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\Big[\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}}2^{\ell\alpha q}\Big(\psi^{*}_{\ell,\lambda}(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f\Big)^{q}\Big]^{1/q}\Big\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{F}^{\alpha}_{p,q}(\mathbb{Z})}.

This completes our proof.
∎

Theorem 4.18
.

Proof.

We only prove

since the proof of the identification of the Besov spaces can be done similarly.

Firstly, let

be a

16
,
8
]

with

is a
(M,K,p)

\displaystyle|(t\Delta_{d})^{M(N+1)}e^{-t\Delta_{d}}a(n)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{m\in I}\Big(\frac{2^{-\nu}}{|n-m|\wedge\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{K+1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\Big(1+\frac{|n-m|}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{-N}|a(m)|.

h_{t}(n)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}e^{-2t(1-\cos\theta)}\cos(n\theta)d\theta

\displaystyle G_{jk}

\displaystyle\leq|2^{j}I|^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\big\|S_{\varphi}\big[F(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})(I-e^{-\ell(I)^{2}\Delta_{d}})^{M}(a\cdot 1_{S_{j}(I)})\big]\big\|^{p}_{\ell^{2}(S_{\ell}(2^{j}I))}

\displaystyle\lesssim|2^{j}I|^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\|a\|_{\ell^{2}(S_{j}(I))}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-j\epsilon p}\sim 2^{-(j+\ell)\epsilon p}.

For
\ell\geq 4
, we have

\displaystyle\big\|S_{\varphi}\big[F(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})

\displaystyle(I-e^{-\ell(I)^{2}\Delta_{d}})^{M}(a\cdot 1_{S_{j}(I)})\big]\big\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}(S_{\ell}(2^{j}I))}

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{\infty}\Big\|\varphi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})F(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})(I-e^{-\ell(I)^{2}\Delta_{d}})^{M}(a\cdot 1_{S_{j}(I)})\Big\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}(S_{\ell}(2^{j}I))}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{\infty}\Big\|\sum_{m\in S_{j}(I)}K_{\varphi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})F(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})(I-e^{-\ell(I)^{2}\Delta_{d}})^{M}}(n,m)a(m)\Big\|^{2}_{\ell_{n}^{2}(S_{\ell}(2^{j}I))}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{1}^{\infty}\Big[\sum_{m\in S_{j}(I)}\big\|K_{\varphi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})F(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})(I-e^{-\ell(I)^{2}\Delta_{d}})^{M}}(n,m)\big\|_{\ell^{2}_{n}(S_{\ell}(2^{j}I))}|a(m)|\Big]^{2}\frac{dt}{t},

where in the last inequality we used Minkowski’s inequality.

We note that in this situation
|m-n|\sim 2^{j+\ell}\ell(I)
. We thus apply Proposition
2.11
to deduce that for
s>s^{\prime}>1/p-1/2
,

\displaystyle\big\|S_{\varphi}

\displaystyle\big[F(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})(I-e^{-\ell(I)^{2}\Delta_{d}})^{M}(a\cdot 1_{S_{j}(I)})\big]\big\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}(S_{\ell}(2^{j}I))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{1}^{\infty}t^{-1}\Big\|\delta_{t^{-1}}\big[\varphi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})F(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})(I-e^{-\ell(I)^{2}\Delta_{d}})^{M}\big]\Big\|^{2}_{W^{s}_{2}}t^{2s^{\prime}}(2^{j+\ell}\ell(I))^{-2s^{\prime}}\|a\|^{2}_{\ell^{1}(S_{j}(I))}\frac{dt}{t}.

Note that

\Big\|\delta_{t^{-1}}\big[\varphi(t\sqrt{\lambda})F(\sqrt{\lambda})(I-e^{-\ell(I)^{2}\lambda})^{M}\big]\Big\|^{2}_{W^{s}_{2}}\lesssim\min\{1,(\ell(I)/t)^{4M}\},

and

\|a\|^{2}_{\ell^{1}(S_{j}(I))}\leq|2^{j}I|\|a\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}(S_{j}(I))}\lesssim 2^{-2j\epsilon}|2^{j}I|^{2-2/p}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\big\|S_{\varphi}\big[F(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})

\displaystyle(I-e^{-\ell(I)^{2}\Delta_{d}})^{M}(a\cdot 1_{S_{j}(I)})\big]\big\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}(S_{\ell}(2^{j}I))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{1}^{\infty}t^{-1}\min\{1,(\ell(I)/t)^{4M}\}t^{2s^{\prime}}(2^{j+\ell}\ell(I))^{-2s^{\prime}}2^{-2j\epsilon}|2^{j}I|^{2-2/p}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-2j\epsilon}2^{-2s^{\prime}(j+\ell)}|2^{j}I|^{2-2/p}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-1}(t/\ell(I))^{2s^{\prime}}\min\{1,(\ell(I)/t)^{4M}\}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-2j\epsilon}2^{-2s^{\prime}(j+\ell)}|2^{j}I|^{2-2/p}\ell(I)^{-1}.

It follows that for
\ell\geq 4,

\displaystyle G_{j\ell}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-jp\epsilon}2^{-s^{\prime}p(j+\ell)}|2^{j}I|^{p-1}|I|^{-p/2}|2^{j+\ell}I|^{\frac{2-p}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-jp\epsilon}2^{-jp(s^{\prime}-1/2)}2^{-\ell p(s^{\prime}-(1/p-1/2))}.

Since
s^{\prime}>1/p-1/2\geq 1/2
, we have

G\leq\sum_{j,\ell\geq 0}G_{j\ell}\lesssim 1,

which implies

\big\|S_{\varphi}\big[F(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})(I-e^{-\ell(I)^{2}\Delta_{d}})^{M}a\big]\big\|^{p}_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}\lesssim 1.

We have just proved that

\|F(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})\|_{\dot{F}^{0}_{p,2}(\mathbb{Z})\to\dot{F}^{0}_{p,2}(\mathbb{Z})}\lesssim\|F\|_{L^{\infty}}+\sup_{t>0}\|\eta\,\delta_{t}F\|_{W^{s}_{r}}

for for some
r\in(4,\infty]
,
s>\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}
and
0<p\leq 1
.

On the other hand, we have
\|F(\sqrt{\Delta}_{d})\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})\to\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\leq\|F\|_{\infty}
. This, along with the interpolation in Proposition
4.20
, implies

(5.4)

\|F(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})\|_{\dot{F}^{0}_{p,2}(\mathbb{Z})\to\dot{F}^{0}_{p,2}(\mathbb{Z})}\lesssim\|F\|_{L^{\infty}}+\sup_{t>0}\|\eta\,\delta_{t}F\|_{W^{s}_{r}}

for for some
r\in(4,\infty]
,
s>\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}
and
0<p<\infty
.

In addition, for
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\|F(\sqrt{\Delta}_{d})f\|_{\dot{F}^{\alpha}_{2,2}(\mathbb{Z})}

\displaystyle=\|F(\sqrt{\Delta}_{d})f\|_{\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{2,2}(\mathbb{Z})}

\displaystyle\sim\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big[t^{-\alpha}\|\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})F(\sqrt{\Delta}_{d})f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\Big]^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|F\|_{\infty}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big[t^{-\alpha}\|\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\Big]^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\sim\|f\|_{\dot{F}^{\alpha}_{2,2}(\mathbb{Z})}.

This, along with (
5.4
) and Proposition
4.20
, yields that

(5.5)

\|F(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})\|_{\dot{F}^{\alpha}_{p,2}(\mathbb{Z})\to\dot{F}^{\alpha}_{p,2}(\mathbb{Z})}\lesssim\|F\|_{L^{\infty}}+\sup_{t\geq 1/2}\|\eta\,\delta_{t}F\|_{W^{s}_{r}}

for some
r\in(4,\infty]
,
s>\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}
with
0<p<\infty
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
.

This completes the proof of (a).

(b) For the item (b), we need the following estimates whose proof will be given at the end of this section.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
supported in
[2,8]
and let
a_{I}
be an
(M,N,p)_{\Delta_{d}}
molecule with some

. Then for any bounded Borel function

satisfying

for some

and some

, we have

(5.6)

|\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})F(\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})a_{I}(n)|\lesssim\Big(\frac{t}{2^{-\nu}}\wedge\frac{2^{-\nu}}{t}\Big)^{2M-1}|I|^{-1/p}\Big(1+\frac{d(n,I)}{2^{-\nu}\vee t}\Big)^{-s^{\prime}}

.

∎

Proof of Lemma
5.3
:

We now consider two cases:
t\leq 2^{-\nu}
and
t>2^{-\nu}
.

Case 1:
t\leq 2^{-\nu}
.
Observe that

\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})a_{I}=t^{2M}\psi_{M}(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})(\Delta_{d}^{M}a_{I})

where
\psi_{M}(\lambda)=\lambda^{-2M}\psi(\lambda)
.

This, along with Proposition
2.13
and the definition of the molecules, yields

\displaystyle|\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})a_{I}(n)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{t^{2M}}{(t+1)}\Big(1+\frac{|m-n|}{t}\Big)^{-s^{\prime}}|a_{I}(m)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big(\frac{t}{2^{-\nu}}\Big)^{2M}|I|^{-1/p}\frac{1}{(t+1)}\Big(1+\frac{|m-n|}{t}\Big)^{-s^{\prime}}\Big(1+\frac{d(m,I)}{2^{-\nu}}\Big)^{-N}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{t}{2^{-\nu}}\Big)^{2M}|I|^{-1/p}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{t}\Big(1+\frac{|m-n|}{2^{-\nu}}\Big)^{-s^{\prime}}\Big(1+\frac{d(m,I)}{2^{-\nu}}\Big)^{-N}.

We now use the inequality

\Big(1+\frac{|m-n|}{2^{-\nu}}\Big)^{-s^{\prime}}\Big(1+\frac{d(m,I)}{2^{-\nu}}\Big)^{-s^{\prime}}\leq\Big(1+\frac{d(n,I)}{2^{-\nu}}\Big)^{-s^{\prime}}

to obtain further that

\displaystyle|\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})a_{I}(n)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{t}{2^{-\nu}}\Big)^{2M-1}|I|^{-1/p}\Big(1+\frac{d(n,I)}{2^{-\nu}}\Big)^{-s^{\prime}}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{2^{-\nu}}\Big(1+\frac{d(m,I)}{2^{-\nu}}\Big)^{-N+s^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{t}{2^{-\nu}}\Big)^{2M-1}|I|^{-1/p}\Big(1+\frac{d(n,I)}{2^{-\nu}}\Big)^{-s^{\prime}},

which yields (
5.6
).

Case 2:
t>2^{-\nu}
.
We first write
a_{I}=\Delta_{d}^{M}b_{I}
. Hence,

\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})a_{I}=t^{-2M}\tilde{\psi}_{M}(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})b_{I}

where
\tilde{\psi}_{M}(\lambda)=\lambda^{2M}\psi(\lambda)
.

This, along with Proposition
2.13
, implies that

\displaystyle|\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})a_{I}(n)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{t^{-2M}}{t}\Big(1+\frac{|m-n|}{t}\Big)^{-s^{\prime}}|b_{I}(m)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{2^{-\nu}}{t}\Big)^{2M}|I|^{-1/p}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{t}\Big(1+\frac{|m-n|}{t}\Big)^{-s^{\prime}}\Big(1+\frac{d(m,I)}{2^{-\nu}}\Big)^{-N}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{2^{-\nu}}{t}\Big)^{2M}|I|^{-1/p}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{t}\Big(1+\frac{|m-n|}{t}\Big)^{-s^{\prime}}\Big(1+\frac{d(m,I)}{t}\Big)^{-N}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{2^{-\nu}}{t}\Big)^{2M-1}|I|^{-1/p}\Big(1+\frac{d(n,I)}{t}\Big)^{-s^{\prime}}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{t}\Big(1+\frac{d(m,I)}{t}\Big)^{-N+s^{\prime}}

Hence (

) follows.

5.2.
Riesz transforms

Theorem 5.4
.

The Riesz transforms
D\Delta_{d}^{-1/2}
and
D^{*}\Delta_{d}^{-1/2}
are bounded on the Besov spaces
\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{p,q}(\mathbb{Z})
for
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
0<p<\infty
,
0<q\leq\infty
and bounded on the Triebel–Lizorkin spaces
\dot{F}^{\alpha}_{p,q}(\mathbb{Z})
for
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
0<p,q<\infty
.

Before coming to the details, we will define the operator
\Delta_{d}^{-1/2}
. Note that the subordination formula

\Delta_{d}^{-1/2}f=c\int_{0}^{\infty}\sqrt{t}e^{-t\Delta_{d}}f\frac{dt}{t}.

is not absolutely convergent in
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. However, fortunately we can show that the above formula is well-defined on
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})/\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{Z})
. Indeed, by duality it suffices to show that if
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
, then

\Delta_{d}^{-1/2}f:=c\int_{0}^{\infty}\sqrt{t}e^{-t\Delta_{d}}f\frac{dt}{t}

belongs to
\mathcal{S}_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
.

To do this, we write

\int_{0}^{\infty}\sqrt{t}e^{-t\Delta_{d}}f\frac{dt}{t}=\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{t}e^{-t\Delta_{d}}f\frac{dt}{t}+\int_{1}^{\infty}\sqrt{t}e^{-t\Delta_{d}}f\frac{dt}{t}.

For the first term we have, for
N\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{0\leq\ell\leq N}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{t}(1+|n|)^{N}|\Delta_{d}^{\ell}e^{-t\Delta_{d}}f(n)|\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{0\leq\ell\leq N}\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{t}(1+|n|)^{N}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}h_{t}(n-m)|\Delta_{d}^{\ell}f(m)|\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{0\leq\ell\leq N}\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{t}(1+|n|)^{N}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}:|m-n|\leq N+1}h_{t}(n-m)|\Delta_{d}^{\ell}f(m)|\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle+\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{0\leq\ell\leq N}\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{t}(1+|n|)^{N}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}:|m-n|>N+1}h_{t}(n-m)|\Delta_{d}^{\ell}f(m)|\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=:E_{1}+E_{2}.

For the first term
E_{1}
, since
(1+|n|)\leq C_{N}(1+|m|)
as long as
|m-n|\leq N+1
, we have

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{0\leq\ell\leq N}\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{t}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}:|m-n|\leq N+1}h_{t}(n-m)(1+|m|)^{N}|\Delta_{d}^{\ell}f(m)|\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{N}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{0\leq\ell\leq N}\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{t}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}h_{t}(n-m)\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{N}\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{t}\frac{dt}{t}\sim\|f\|_{N}.

We now take care of
E_{2}
. By Lemma
2.2
,

\displaystyle E_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{0\leq\ell\leq N}\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{t}(1+|n|)^{N}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}:|m-n|>N+1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\frac{t^{N/2+1}}{|m-n|^{N+2}}|\Delta_{d}^{\ell}f(m)|\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{0\leq\ell\leq N}\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{t}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}:|m-n|>N+1}\frac{1}{|m-n|^{2}}(1+|m|)^{N}|\Delta_{d}^{\ell}f(m)|\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{N}.

It follows that
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{t}e^{-t\Delta_{d}}f\frac{dt}{t}\in\mathcal{S}_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
whenever
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
.

Since
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
, we can write
f=\Delta_{d}^{N+1}g
with
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{Z})
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{0\leq\ell\leq N}

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\sqrt{t}(1+|n|)^{N}|\Delta_{d}^{\ell}e^{-t\Delta_{d}}f(n)|\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{0\leq\ell\leq N}\int_{1}^{\infty}\sqrt{t}(1+|n|)^{N}|\Delta_{d}^{\ell+N+1}e^{-t\Delta_{d}}g(n)|\frac{dt}{t}.

At this stage, using the kernel estimate in Lemma
2.5
, we can come up with

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{0\leq\ell\leq N}\int_{1}^{\infty}\sqrt{t}(1+|n|)^{N}|\Delta_{d}^{\ell+N+1}e^{-t\Delta_{d}}f(n)|\frac{dt}{t}\lesssim\|g\|_{N}.

This implies that
\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\sqrt{t}e^{-t\Delta_{d}}f\frac{dt}{t}\in\mathcal{S}_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
whenever
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
.

As a consequence,
\Delta^{-1/2}f\in\mathcal{S}_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
whenever
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
. Hence, by duality,
\Delta^{-1/2}f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
whenever
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
.

Theorem 5.5
.

The operator
D
and
D^{*}
maps

(i)

from
\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{p,q}(\mathbb{Z})
to
\dot{B}^{\alpha-1}_{p,q}(\mathbb{Z})
continuously for
0<p,q\leq\infty
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
;

(ii)

from
\dot{F}^{\alpha}_{p,q}(\mathbb{Z})
to
\dot{F}^{\alpha-1}_{p,q}(\mathbb{Z})
continuously for
0<p<\infty,0<q\leq\infty
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

By the similar argument in the proof of Theorem
4.9
, it suffices to prove the following estimates: Let
\psi
be a partition of unity and let
a_{I}
be a
(M,N,p)_{\Delta_{d}}
molecule with some
I\in\mathcal{I}_{\nu}
with
\nu\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}
. Then for any
t>0
and
N>0
we have:

(5.7)

|\sqrt{t}\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})Da_{I}(n)|+|\sqrt{t}\psi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{d}})D^{*}a_{I}(n)|\lesssim\Big(\frac{t}{2^{-\nu}}\wedge\frac{2^{-\nu}}{t}\Big)^{2M-1}|I|^{-1/p}\Big(1+\frac{d(n,I)}{2^{-\nu}\vee t}\Big)^{-N}.

2.15
instead of Corollary
2.14
. We would like to leave the details to the interested reader.

This completes our proof.
∎

\Delta_{d}^{-1/2}
maps

(i)

from

and

and

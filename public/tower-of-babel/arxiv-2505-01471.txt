Higher order flow coefficients - A Messenger of QCD medium formed in heavy-ion collisions at the Large Hadron Collider
Suraj Prasad, Aswathy Menon K R, Raghunath Sahoo, Neelkamal Mallick

Abstract
Anisotropic flow and fluctuations are sensitive observables to the initial state effects in heavy ion collisions and are characterized by the medium properties and final state interactions. Using event-shape observables, one can constrain the probability distributions of anisotropic flow coefficients, thus reducing the linear and nonlinear contributions in the measured higher-order harmonics. In this paper, we use transverse spherocity as an event shape observable to study the flow coefficients and elliptic flow fluctuations. Transverse spherocity is found to have a strong correlation with elliptic flow and its fluctuations. We exploit this feature of transverse spherocity to remove the contribution of elliptic flow from higher-order harmonics. The study is performed in Pb–Pb collisions at s NN = 5.02 \sqrt{s_{\rm NN}}=5.02 TeV using a multi-phase transport model. The multi-particle Q-cumulant method estimates the anisotropic flow coefficients, which reduces the non-flow contributions. We observe a stronger system response to the flow coefficients for the events with smaller values of elliptic flow.



I
Introduction

Heavy-ion collisions at the Large Hadron Collider, CERN, Switzerland, and the Relativistic Heavy-Ion Collider at BNL, USA create laboratory conditions for the formation of a hot and dense plasma of quarks and gluons, which is believed to have existed in the microsecond-old Universe shortly after the Big Bang
Bass:1998vz
. Under the extreme conditions of high temperature and baryon densities achieved in such collisions, it is feasible to test and study the theory of strong interactions via quantum chromodynamics (QCD). Nonetheless, the quark-gluon plasma (QGP) is very short-lived; thus, a direct observation of such a medium, if formed, is elusive. Consequently, there are several signatures of QGP that can be measured in the experiments, which include strangeness enhancement
Rafelski:1982pu
, jet quenching
Appel:1985dq
, quarkonia suppression
Matsui:1986dk
, collective flow
Herrmann:1999wu
, etc. While the presence of collective flow hints at the formation of a hydrodynamically expanding medium of deconfined partons, the measurement of anisotropic flow coefficients of the final state hardons underlines several crucial properties of the medium formed. The anisotropic flow can be quantified in terms of the coefficients of the Fourier expansion of the final state azimuthal distribution of the charged hadrons, as follows:

\frac{dN}{d\phi}\propto\;1+2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}v_{n}\cos[n(\phi-\psi_{n})]

(1)

Here,
n
denotes the order of the harmonics.
\psi_{n}
is the
n^{\rm th}
order symmetry plane angle.
v_{n}
denotes the
n
th order anisotropic flow coefficient, where
n=1
,
2
, and
3
denote the directed flow, elliptic flow, and triangular flow, respectively
Heinz:2013th
. The anisotropic flow coefficients depend on several factors, which include the initial spatial anisotropy, initial density fluctuations, and bulk and transport properties of the medium. While the contribution to
v_{2}\{2\}
comes significantly from the initial spatial geometry of the collision overlap region, the coefficients
v_{n}
for
n>2
are generated due to initial density fluctuations.

The initial spatial anisotropy of the collision overlap region can be quantified in terms of initial eccentricities (
\epsilon_{n}
), where, for
n=2
and 3, the contributions to
v_{n}
from corresponding
\epsilon_{n}
are linear,
i.e.
,
v_{2}\propto\epsilon_{2}
and
v_{3}\propto\epsilon_{3}
.
However, harmonic flow coefficients of higher order (
n>3
) arise not only from the initial eccentricities of the same order but also from the non-linear mixing of lower-order harmonics.
In fact,
v_{4}\{2\}
has linear contribution from
\epsilon_{4}
, and a nonlinear contribution from
\epsilon_{2}
or
v_{2}
. These nonlinear contributions of
v_{2}\{2\}
in the measured value of
v_{4}\{2\}
can lead to strong centrality dependence of the correlation between
\psi_{2}
and
\psi_{4}
. In addition,
v_{5}
has a linear contribution from
\epsilon_{5}
and a nonlinear contribution from both
v_{2}
and
v_{3}
, which leads to the observed event plane correlations involving
\psi_{2}
,
\psi_{3}
and
\psi_{5}
in the experiments
ATLAS:2014ndd
using event plane method, which is absent for correlations estimated using Gaussian estimator method
ALICE:2024fus
. These findings are crucial because, in hydrodynamical models, it is observed that the anisotropic flow coefficients are affected significantly by a change in specific shear viscosity (
\eta_{s}/s
) of the medium and the impact of
\eta_{s}/s
on
v_{n}
increases with increasing
n
. Thus, higher-order anisotropic flow coefficients are proven to be better probes to estimate the transport coefficients of the medium formed in relativistic collisions. Consequently, it is important to decouple the measured signal of the
v_{2}
and
v_{3}
from the higher-order harmonics to have a better estimate of the properties of the medium formed, and here comes into play the role of event shape estimators
ATLAS:2015qwl
.

The values of
\epsilon_{n}
and consequently
v_{\rm n}
can fluctuate event-by-event due to the fluctuations of the participating nuclear positions at the initial state. The distributions of
v_{n}
are very broad, even in fine centrality bins, where the values of
v_{2}\{2\}
and
v_{3}\{2\}
are found to fluctuate from zero to several times their mean values
ATLAS:2013xzf
. These fluctuations in the measurements of
v_{2}\{2\}
and
v_{3}\{2\}
can, in turn, affect the measurements of higher-order flow coefficients. Therefore, by cleanly selecting the events with different
v_{2}\{2\}
and
v_{3}\{2\}
values, one has to constrain their contributions in the higher-order flow harmonics. This makes the study of event shapes important in heavy-ion collisions, where one can select events based on their topology, which provides an independent method to separate the nonlinear contributions of
v_{2}\{2\}
and
v_{3}\{2\}
in higher-order flow harmonics.

Traditionally, the event shapes are determined using the reduced flow vectors (
q_{n}
), defined as follows.

q_{n}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}\sum_{j=1}^{M}e^{in\phi_{j}}

(2)

Here,
M
is the total number of charged particles in a given rapidity region.
q_{n}
vectors are proportional to the anisotropic flow coefficients for a large particle multiplicity limit. However, towards the peripheral collisions, where the particle multiplicity is small,
q_{n}
are not good observables to separate the elliptic or triangular events based on the final state azimuthal distribution of charged particles in the transverse plane
ATLAS:2015qwl
. In contrast, transverse spherocity (
S_{0}
), defined in Sec.
II.2
, is an event shape observable traditionally used in small systems like proton-proton (
pp
) collisions to select the soft events dominated with non-pQCD interactions from the pQCD-dominated jetty events
ALICE:2019dfi
. In addition, recent simulation studies show that transverse spherocity can also be used as an event shape observable in heavy-ion collisions
Mallick:2021hcs
;
Prasad:2021bdq
;
Prasad:2022zbr
;
Mallick:2020ium
;
Tripathy:2025npe
. It is observed that transverse spherocity has a significant correlation with both elliptic and triangular flow in addition to the
q_{2}
and
q_{3}
vectors
Prasad:2022zbr
. Interestingly, transverse spherocity-based event selections are observed to affect the number of constituent quark (NCQ) scalings of the elliptic flow coefficients of identified hadrons
Mallick:2021hcs
. Additionally, one observes that the strength of the symmetry plane correlations changes with different classes of transverse spherocity
Tripathy:2025npe
. Furthermore, the events with large values of
S_{0}
show a large mean transverse radial flow velocity and a smaller kinetic freezeout temperature in Pb–Pb collisions compared to events with a smaller
S_{0}
value
Prasad:2021bdq
. The studies mentioned above signify both the applicability and importance of event shape selection based on transverse spherocity in heavy-ion collisions.

We take this opportunity to exploit transverse spherocity to study the higher-order anisotropic flow coefficients and elliptic flow fluctuations. The study is performed in Pb–Pb collisions at
\sqrt{s_{\rm NN}}=5.02
TeV using a multi-phase transport (AMPT) model. Studying elliptic flow fluctuations with transverse spherocity would help in selecting events with smaller elliptic fluctuations and thereby constrain the value of
v_{2}
. Consequently, a more meaningful study of higher-order flow coefficients can be performed.

The paper is organised as follows. We start with a brief introduction in Sec.
I
, followed by discussions of event generation and methodology in Sec.
II
. In Sec.
III
, the results and corresponding discussions are presented. Finally, in Sec.
IV
, a brief summary and outlook of the study are given.

II
Event generation and methodology

In this section, we begin with a brief discussion on event generation using a multi-phase transport model, followed by the definition and calculation of transverse spherocity. The multi-particle cumulant method used to estimate the flow coefficients
v_{\rm n}
is described in brief thereafter.

II.1
A multi-phase transport model

A multi-phase transport (AMPT) model is a Monte Carlo transport model that provides a kinetic description of the various stages involved in heavy-ion collisions. Collision initialization, parton transport, hadronization, and hadronic transport are the four phases incorporated in the AMPT model
Zhang:1999bd
;
Lin:2004en
. The initial parton distributions are generated using HIJING
Wang:1991hta
. The interactions among the partons are simulated using ZPC
Zhang:1997ej
. Conversion of partons to hadrons is done via the Lund string fragmentation model (in default AMPT)
Andersson:1983ia
or through the quark coalescence mechanism (in the string-melting version of AMPT)
Greco:2003xt
. In the final stage, the formed hadrons undergo a final evolution in a relativistic transport (ART) model framework via baryon-baryon, meson-baryon and meson-meson interactions
Li:1995pra
;
Li:2001xh
.

As this work focuses on studying the flow coefficients and flow fluctuations, we use the string-melting (SM) mode (version 2.26t9b) of AMPT wherein the quark coalescence mechanism of hadronization is known to describe well the particle
p_{\rm T}
-spectra and flow at intermediate
p_{\rm T}

Greco:2003mm
;
Fries:2003vb
;
Fries:2003kq
;
Mallick:2023vgi
;
Mallick:2022alr
. The AMPT settings used in this study are the same as reported in Ref.
Tripathy:2018bib
.

II.2
Transverse spherocity

Well-known for its ability to categorize events based on their azimuthal topology, the variable, transverse spherocity (
S_{0}
) is defined for an event as:

S_{0}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{4}\min_{\hat{n}}\Bigg(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\rm had}}|\bf{p_{\rm T}}_{i}\times\hat{n}|}{\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\rm had}}|\bf{p_{\rm T}}_{i}|}\Bigg)^{2}

(3)

where
\bf{p_{\rm T}}_{i}
is the transverse momentum vector of
i^{\rm th}
charged hadron in the event, and the unit vector
\hat{n}
is to be chosen such that it minimizes the term in the bracket. Here, the minimization is carried out over all possible angles in the azimuth in the transverse plane of the event, ranging from 0 to
2\pi
. Events with a minimum of 5 charged particles are chosen for estimating
S_{0}
, and the charged particles in the range
|\eta|<0.8
with
p_{\rm T}>0.15
GeV/
c
alone enter the calculation of
S_{0}
. The normalization constant
\pi^{2}
/4 ensures that
S_{0}
values range between 0 and 1. While the lower spherocity limit
S_{0}=0
characterizes an event with pencil-like emission of particles called the jetty event,
S_{0}=1
depicts an isotropic event with particles uniformly distributed in the transverse plane. Like in our previous studies, we choose the lowest 20% (Low-
S_{0}
) of the
S_{0}
distribution as jetty events while the highest 20% (High-
S_{0}
) is selected as isotropic events
Prasad:2021bdq
;
Mallick:2020ium
.

II.3
Multi-particle cumulant method

The anisotropic flow coefficients,
v_{\rm n}
(of Eq. (
1
)) are estimated using a two- and four-particle Q-cumulant method
Bilandzic:2010jr
;
Bilandzic:2013kga
;
Aamodt:2010pa
. This method gives the advantage of not requiring the information of
\psi_{\rm n}
as well as of suppressing the non-flow effects by introducing suitable kinematic cuts.

Firstly, we define the flow vector,
Q_{\rm n}
, for each event as follows.

Q_{\rm n}=\sum_{j=1}^{M}e^{in\phi_{j}}

(4)

Here,
M
is the multiplicity of the particles, and
\phi_{j}
denotes the azimuthal angle of the
i
th particle in the event.
From
Q_{\rm n}
vector, the two- and four-particle azimuthal correlations are measured for each event, denoted as
\langle 2\rangle
and
\langle 4\rangle
, respectively. Further, event-average two- and four-particle azimuthal correlations are obtained, which are denoted as
\langle\langle 2\rangle\rangle
and
\langle\langle 4\rangle\rangle
, respectively. The above-mentioned azimuthal correlations can be calculated using the following equations.

\langle 2\rangle=\frac{|Q_{n}|^{2}-M}{M(M-1)}

(5)

\displaystyle\langle 4\rangle=\frac{|Q_{n}|^{4}+|Q_{2n}|^{2}-2\cdot Re[Q_{2n}Q_{n}^{*}Q_{n}^{*}]}{M(M-1)(M-2)(M-3)}-

\displaystyle 2\frac{2(M-2)\cdot|Q_{n}|^{2}-M(M-3)}{M(M-1)(M-2)(M-3)}

(6)

\langle\langle 2\rangle\rangle=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\rm ev}}(W_{\langle 2\rangle})_{i}\langle 2\rangle_{i}}{\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\rm ev}}(W_{\langle 2\rangle})_{i}}

(7)

\langle\langle 4\rangle\rangle=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\rm ev}}(W_{\langle 4\rangle})_{i}\langle 4\rangle_{i}}{\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\rm ev}}(W_{\langle 4\rangle})_{i}}

(8)

Here,
N_{\rm ev}
corresponds to the total number of events for which the event average is performed, and
``^{*}"
refers to the complex conjugate. The weight factors are estimated as,

W_{\langle 2\rangle}=M(M-1),

(9)

W_{\langle 4\rangle}=M(M-1)(M-2)(M-3).

(10)

Using
\langle\langle 2\rangle\rangle
and
\langle\langle 4\rangle\rangle
, we calculate the two- and four-particle cumulants using the following expressions.

c_{\rm n}\{2\}=\langle\langle 2\rangle\rangle,\\
c_{\rm n}\{4\}=\langle\langle 4\rangle\rangle-2\langle\langle 2\rangle\rangle^{2}

(11)

Finally, the reference flow of the particles can be estimated as follows.

v_{\rm n}\{2\}=\sqrt{c_{\rm n}\{2\}},v_{\rm n}\{4\}=\sqrt[4]{-c_{\rm n}\{4\}}

(12)

To make
p_{\rm T}
-differential measurement of the anisotropic flow coefficients, we start by tagging the particles as the particles of interest (POI) and the reference flow particles (RFP), which serve as the reference plane for the POIs and helps to establish the orientation of the symmetry plane. We denote the flow vector of the POIs as
p_{n}
. Similarly,
q_{n}
denotes the flow vector of the particles tagged as both POIs and RFPs, which are calculated using the following equations.

p_{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{m_{p}}e^{in\phi_{j}}

(13)

q_{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{m_{q}}e^{in\phi_{j}}

(14)

Here,
m_{p}
refers to the total number of particles tagged as POIs, and
m_{q}
stands for the total number of particles defined as both POIs and RFPs. Now, single-event averaged differential two- and four-particle azimuthal correlations (
\langle 2^{{}^{\prime}}\rangle
and
\langle 4^{{}^{\prime}}\rangle
) are constructed as:

\langle 2^{{}^{\prime}}\rangle=\frac{p_{n}Q_{n}^{*}-m_{q}}{m_{p}M-m_{q}},

(15)

\displaystyle\langle 4^{{}^{\prime}}\rangle=[p_{n}Q_{n}Q_{n}^{*}Q_{n}^{*}-q_{2n}Q_{n}^{*}Q_{n}^{*}-p_{n}Q_{n}Q_{2n}^{*}

\displaystyle-2.Mp_{n}Q_{n}^{*}-2.m_{q}|Q_{n}|^{2}+7.q_{n}Q_{n}^{*}

\displaystyle-Q_{n}q_{n}^{*}+q_{2n}Q_{2n}^{*}+2.p_{n}Q_{n}^{*}

\displaystyle+2.m_{q}M-6.m_{q}]

\displaystyle/[(m_{p}M-3m_{q})(M-1)(M-2)]

(16)

An event-average is performed to obtain
\langle\langle 2^{{}^{\prime}}\rangle\rangle
and
\langle\langle 4^{{}^{\prime}}\rangle\rangle
similar to Eqs. (
7
) and (
8
), as follows.

\langle\langle 2^{{}^{\prime}}\rangle\rangle=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\rm ev}}(w_{\langle 2^{{}^{\prime}}\rangle})_{i}\langle 2^{{}^{\prime}}\rangle_{i}}{\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\rm ev}}(w_{\langle 2^{{}^{\prime}}\rangle})_{i}}

(17)

\langle\langle 4^{{}^{\prime}}\rangle\rangle=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\rm ev}}(w_{\langle 4^{{}^{\prime}}\rangle})_{i}\langle 4^{{}^{\prime}}\rangle_{i}}{\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\rm ev}}(w_{\langle 4^{{}^{\prime}}\rangle})_{i}}

(18)

Here,
w_{\langle 2^{{}^{\prime}}\rangle}
and
w_{\langle 4^{{}^{\prime}}\rangle}
are weight factors, calculated using the following equations.

w_{\langle 2^{{}^{\prime}}\rangle}=m_{p}M-m_{q}

(19)

w_{\langle 4^{{}^{\prime}}\rangle}=(m_{p}M-3m_{q})(M-1)(M-2)

(20)

The two- and four-particle differential cumulants are then calculated as :

d_{\rm n}\{2\}=\langle\langle 2^{{}^{\prime}}\rangle\rangle,\\
d_{\rm n}\{4\}=\langle\langle 4^{{}^{\prime}}\rangle\rangle-2\langle\langle 2^{{}^{\prime}}\rangle\rangle\langle\langle 2\rangle\rangle.

(21)

Finally, the two- and four-particle
p_{\rm T}
-differential flow coefficients are estimated according to :

v_{\rm n}\{2\}(p_{\rm T})=d_{\rm n}\{2\}/\sqrt{c_{\rm n}\{2\}},

(22)

v_{\rm n}\{4\}(p_{\rm T})=-d_{\rm n}\{4\}/(-c_{\rm n}\{4\})^{3/4}.

(23)

However, obtaining
v_{\rm n}
via the two-particle Q-cumulant method suffers from contributions from non-flow effects
ALICE:2010suc
. Thus, we introduce a pseudorapidity gap which can substantially reduce these nonflow effects
ALICE:2011ab
;
Zhou:2014bba
;
ALICE:2014dwt
;
Zhou:2015iba
. With such an implementation, each event gets divided into two sub-events, A and B, with a
|\Delta\eta|
gap between them
Jia:2017hbm
;
ATLAS:2017hap
;
ATLAS:2017rtr
. Estimation of the two-particle correlation with pseudorapidity gap, now denoted as
\langle 2\rangle_{\Delta\eta}
, uses flow vectors from each of the sub-events A and B, such that:

\langle 2\rangle_{\Delta\eta}=\frac{Q_{n}^{A}.Q_{n}^{B*}}{M_{A}.M_{B}}

(24)

where
M_{A}
and
M_{B}
are the multiplicities in sub-events A and B respectively. Consequently, the two-particle Q-cumulant with
|\Delta\eta|
gap reads as:

c_{\rm n}\{2,|\Delta\eta|\}=\langle\langle 2\rangle\rangle_{\Delta\eta}.\\

(25)

Selecting reference particles from one subevent and the POIs from the other simplifies the calculation of differential flow with the pseudorapidity gap (
\langle 2^{{}^{\prime}}\rangle_{\Delta\eta}
) since the RFPs and POIs now belong to two disjoint sets. The single-event average differential two-particle correlation and the differential two-particle cumulant with
|\Delta\eta|
gap, and the correspondingly obtained differential flow can be estimated as follows:

\langle 2^{{}^{\prime}}\rangle_{\Delta\eta}=\frac{p_{n,A}Q_{n,B}^{*}}{m_{p,A}M_{B}},

(26)

d_{\rm n}\{2,|\Delta\eta|\}=\langle\langle 2^{{}^{\prime}}\rangle\rangle_{\Delta\eta},

(27)

v_{\rm n}\{2,|\Delta\eta|\}(p_{\rm T})=d_{\rm n}\{2,|\Delta\eta|\}/\sqrt{c_{\rm n}\{2,|\Delta\eta|\}}.

(28)

We use all the charged hadrons within the pseudorapidity region,
|\eta|<2.5
, to estimate anisotropic flow coefficients using the two- and four-particle Q-cumulant method. We tag the charged hadrons within
|\eta|<2.5
and
0.2<p_{\rm T}<4.0
GeV/c as RFPs. A pseudorapidity gap,
|\Delta\eta|>1.0
, is applied in the two-subevent method to subtract the non-flow effects from the two-particle Q-cumulant method. The statistical uncertainties are calculated using the relations shown in Ref.
Bilandzic:2012wva
.

To study the relative flow fluctuations for a given flow coefficient
v_{\rm n}
, the first and second moments of the event-by-event
v_{\rm n}
distribution are to be approximated according to
Ollitrault:2009ie
;
PHENIX:2018lfu
:

\langle v_{\rm n}\rangle\approx\sqrt{\frac{v_{\rm n}^{2}\{2,|\Delta\eta|\}+v_{\rm n}^{2}\{4\}}{2}},

(29)

\sigma_{v_{\rm n}}\approx\sqrt{\frac{v_{\rm n}^{2}\{2,|\Delta\eta|\}-v_{\rm n}^{2}\{4\}}{2}},

(30)

whereby the relative anisotropic flow fluctuations
F(v_{\rm n})
can be estimated as:

F(v_{\rm n})=\frac{\sigma_{v_{\rm n}}}{\langle v_{\rm n}\rangle}.

(31)

In this study, we use Pb–Pb collisions at
\sqrt{s_{\rm NN}}=5.02
TeV using AMPT. We estimate the anisotropic flow coefficients using the above procedure with particles in
|\eta|<2.5
. Although in AMPT the nonflow contributions are small, we use an eta gap of
|\Delta\eta|>1.0
in the two-particle Q-cumulant method to suppress any possible nonflow contribution.

These initial state fluctuations can influence the final state fluctuations and are reflected in the fluctuations of anisotropic flow coefficients convoluted with the medium response during medium evolution. Studying fluctuations in the initial spatial anisotropy and the final state azimuthal anisotropy are, thus, crucial in understanding the transport properties of the medium. Hence, before analyzing the elliptic flow fluctuations for different spherocity classes, it is important to first understand the fluctuations in their initial state,

, the eccentricity fluctuations in the collision overlap region. In Ref.

\langle\epsilon_{2}\rangle
. Using a similar formalism to estimate the eccentricity from AMPT, we extend our study to show the

.

IV
Summary

In summary, we have studied the transverse spherocity dependence of anisotropic flow coefficients with a focus on the effects of elliptic flow on higher-order flow coefficients and elliptic flow fluctuations in Pb–Pb collisions at
\sqrt{s_{\rm NN}}=5.02
TeV using AMPT. We observe that, except for triangular flow, which has a positive correlation with transverse spherocity, higher order flow coefficients, such as
v_{4}
and
v_{5}
, show an anti-correlation with transverse spherocity. This is expected as higher-order coefficients have large non-linear contributions from elliptic flow, which is picked up and can be removed with proper transverse spherocity selections. Further, one finds that the event selections based on transverse spherocity can affect the elliptic flow fluctuations. The elliptic flow fluctuations are the smallest for the low-
S_{0}
events, where the signal of elliptic flow is stronger. Further, with the help of the event shape classifier, we find that the system response to the elliptic flow coefficient from geometry is different from the fluctuation. This study provides unique features and applications of event selections using transverse spherocity, which is an excellent event shape classifier that works in small collision systems like pp and p–Pb and even in Pb–Pb collisions. Using transverse spherocity, one can remove the contributions of elliptic flow from the higher-order anisotropic flow coefficients, which can, in turn, provide a good foundation for understanding the transport properties of the medium formed in heavy-ion collisions.

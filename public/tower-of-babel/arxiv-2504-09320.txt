Capillary Christoffel-Minkowski problem
Yingxiang Hu, Mohammad N. Ivaki, Julian Scheuer

Abstract
. The result of Guan and Ma (Invent. Math. 151 (2003)) states that if ϕ − 1 / k : 𝕊 n → ( 0 , ∞ ) \phi^{-1/k}:\mathbb{S}^{n}\to(0,\infty) is spherically convex, then ϕ \phi arises as the σ k \sigma_{k} curvature (the k k -th elementary symmetric function of the principal radii of curvature) of a strictly convex hypersurface. In this paper, we establish an analogous result in the capillary setting in the half-space for θ ∈ ( 0 , π / 2 ) \theta\in(0,\pi/2) : if ϕ − 1 / k : 𝒞 θ → ( 0 , ∞ ) \phi^{-1/k}:\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\to(0,\infty) is a capillary function and spherically convex, then ϕ \phi is the σ k \sigma_{k} curvature of a strictly convex capillary hypersurface.



1.
Introduction

The Christoffel-Minkowski problem seeks the necessary and sufficient conditions on a Borel measure
\mu
defined on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
for it to represent the
j
-th surface area measure of a convex body
K
(i.e. a compact, convex set with non-empty interior).

For
j=n
, the problem is known as the Minkowski problem, and a complete solution was provided by Minkowski
[
Min97
,
Min03
]
, Aleksandrov

[
Ale56
]
,
Nirenberg
[
Nir57
]
, Pogorelov
[
Pog52
,
Pog71
]
, and Cheng-Yau
[
CY76
]
. They showed that a Borel measure
\mu
, whose support is not contained within a closed hemisphere, is the surface area measure of a convex body if and only if

(1.1)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}u\,d\mu(u)=0.

Moreover, the solution is unique up to translations. For further details and generalizations, we refer the reader to
[
Sch14
]
,
[
Lut93
,
LO95
,
CW06
,
BLYZ13
,
HLYZ16
,
Li19
,
BBCY19
,
HXY21
,
GLW22
,
LXYZ24
]
, and the recent survey
[
HYZ25
]
. Parabolic approaches are discussed, for example, in
[
CW00
,
BIS19
,
LWW20
,
CL21
,
BG23
]
.

When
j=1
, the problem becomes the Christoffel problem, which was completely solved by Firey
[
Fir67
]
and Berg
[
Ber69
]
using methods based on Green’s function and subharmonic functions.

For the intermediate case
1<j<n
, where the necessary and sufficient conditions have not been fully established, the problem remains largely open within the class of Borel measures. However, significant progress has been made in the case where
\mu
has a smooth density with respect to the spherical Lebesgue measure. Notable results include those of Firey
[
Fir70
]
for the rotationally symmetric case and Guan and Ma
[
GM03
]
with Sheng, Trudinger, and Wang
[
STW04
]
in the general case. In particular, in
[
GM03
]
, the authors proved a constant rank theorem that guarantees the strict convexity of the solutions. Additional developments, including the extension within the
L_{p}
-Brunn-Minkowski theory and other ambient spaces, are provided in
[
HMS04
,
GX18
]
,
[
BIS21
,
HLX24
,
LW24
]
, and the problem was revisited in
[
Iva19
,
BIS23b
,
Zha24
]
using parabolic approaches.

The geometry of capillary hypersurfaces, i.e. those which meet other hypersurfaces subject to a constant intersection angle, has received increasing attention during the past years. Many results, which are by now classical for closed hypersurfaces, were recently generalized to the capillary setting, such as the classification of stable CMC surfaces in a ball
[
WX19
]
, or the proof of Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities for capillary hypersurfaces in the half space
[
MWWX24
]
, see also
[
HWYZ24
]
. Also, curvature flows have been investigated in this setting, such as locally constrained mean curvature flows
[
MW23
,
SW24
,
WW20
]
or flows in the Lorentzian setting
[
KLS25
]
. The capillary Minkowski problem was recently studied in
[
MWW25
]
.

In this paper, we investigate the existence of strictly convex solutions to the capillary Christoffel-Minkowski problem, which will be discussed below.

Let
\{E_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n+1}
be the standard orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.
Let
\Sigma
be a properly embedded, smooth, compact, connected and orientable hypersurface in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
with
\operatorname{int}(\Sigma)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
and boundary
\partial\Sigma\subset\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
. We say
\Sigma
is a capillary hypersurface with the constant contact angle
\theta\in(0,\pi)
if the following condition on
\partial\Sigma
is met:

(1.2)

\langle\nu,E_{n+1}\rangle\equiv\cos\theta.

Here,
\nu
denotes the outer unit normal of
\Sigma
. The enclosed region by the hypersurface
\Sigma
and the hyperplane
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
is denoted by
\widehat{\Sigma}
.

We say
\Sigma
is strictly convex if
\widehat{\Sigma}
is a convex body and the second fundamental form of
\Sigma
is positive-definite (which implies that
\Sigma
does not contain a line segment). Throughout this paper, we work with strictly convex capillary hypersurfaces.

The capillary spherical cap of radius
r
and intersecting with
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
at a constant angle
\theta\in(0,\pi)
is defined as

(1.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\theta,r}:=\left\{x\in\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}\mid|x+r\cos\theta E_{n+1}|=r\right\}.

For simplicity, we put
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}=\mathcal{C}_{\theta,1}
.

Let
\tilde{\nu}=\nu-\cos\theta E_{n+1}:\Sigma\to\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
denote the capillary Gauss map of
\Sigma
, which is a diffeomorphism, see
2.1
. The capillary support function of
\Sigma
,
s=s_{\Sigma}:\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\to\mathbb{R}
, is defined as

(1.4)

\displaystyle s(\zeta)=\langle\tilde{\nu}^{-1}(\zeta),\zeta+\cos\theta E_{n+1}\rangle,\quad\zeta\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}.

For example, the capillary support function of
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
is given by

(1.5)

\displaystyle\ell(\zeta)=\sin^{2}\theta-\cos\theta\langle\zeta,E_{n+1}\rangle.

The capillary area measure of
\Sigma
is defined as

(1.6)

\displaystyle m_{\theta}(\mathcal{B}):=\int_{\tilde{\nu}^{-1}(\mathcal{B})}\left(1-\cos\theta\langle\nu,E_{n+1}\rangle\right)d\mathcal{H}^{n},

for all Borel sets
\mathcal{B}
of
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
. Here,
\mathcal{H}^{n}
denotes the
n
-dimensional Hausdorff measure. For a smooth, strictly convex capillary hypersurface
\Sigma
we have

(1.7)

\displaystyle dm_{\theta}=\frac{\ell}{\mathcal{K}\circ\tilde{\nu}^{-1}}d\sigma,

where
d\sigma
represents the area element of
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
and
\mathcal{K}
is the Gauss curvature of
\Sigma
.

Question

(Capillary Minkowski problem)
.

Given a measure
m
on the spherical cap
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
, is there a strictly convex capillary hypersurface whose capillary area measure is
m
?

For the regular case of this question, i.e., when
dm=\frac{\ell}{f}\,d\sigma
and
f\in C^{2}(\mathcal{C}_{\theta})
is positive, the question is equivalent to finding a smooth, strictly convex capillary hypersurface whose Gauss curvature is
f
. The following existence result was proved in
[
MWW25
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
. Suppose
0<\phi\in C^{2}(\mathcal{C}_{\theta})
satisfies

(1.8)

\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\langle\zeta,E_{i}\rangle\phi(\zeta)\,d\sigma=0,\quad i=1,2,\ldots,n,

where
\{E_{i}\}_{i=1,\ldots,n}
is the horizontal basis of
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
. Then there exists a
C^{3,\alpha}
strictly convex capillary hypersurface
\Sigma\subset\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
such that its Gauss-Kronecker curvature
\mathcal{K}
satisfies

(1.9)

\frac{1}{\mathcal{K}(\tilde{\nu}^{-1}(\zeta))}=\phi(\zeta),\quad\forall\zeta\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}.

Moreover,
\Sigma
is unique up to a horizontal translation in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
.

Let
\bar{g},\bar{\nabla}
denote the standard metric and Levi-Civita connection on
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
and put
\tau[s]=\bar{\nabla}^{2}s+s\bar{g}
.
The regular capillary Christoffel-Minkowski problem in the halfspace asks the following question.

Question
.

Let
1\leq k<n
and
0<\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{C}_{\theta})
. Find the necessary and sufficient assumptions on
\phi
so that there exists a strictly convex capillary hypersurface
\Sigma\subset\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
with the capillary support function
s
satisfying

(1.10)

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}(\tau[s],\bar{g})=\phi\quad\text{on}\ \mathcal{C}_{\theta}.

Our main contributions are captured in the following theorems.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
and
1\leq k<n
. Suppose
0<\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{C}_{\theta})
satisfies:

(1)

\phi(-\zeta_{1},\ldots,-\zeta_{n},\zeta_{n+1})=\phi(\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{n},\zeta_{n+1})
for all
\zeta\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
,

(2)

\bar{\nabla}^{2}\phi^{-\frac{1}{k}}+\bar{g}\,\phi^{-\frac{1}{k}}\geq 0
, and
\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}\phi^{-\frac{1}{k}}\leq\cot\theta\,\phi^{-\frac{1}{k}}
.

Then there exists a smooth, strictly convex capillary hypersurface
\Sigma\subset\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
whose
k
-th elementary symmetric function of the principal radii of curvature is given by:

(1.11)

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}(\tilde{\nu}^{-1}(\zeta))=\phi(\zeta).

Moreover, the capillary support function
s
of
\Sigma
satisfies

(1.12)

\displaystyle s(-\zeta_{1},\ldots,-\zeta_{n},\zeta_{n+1})=s(\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{n},\zeta_{n+1}),\quad\forall\zeta\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta},

and this solution is unique within this class.

The following theorem can be considered as the exact analogues of the result of Guan and Ma
[
GM03
]
in the capillary setting.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
and
1\leq k<n
. Suppose
0<\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{C}_{\theta})
satisfies:

(1)

\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\langle\zeta,E_{i}\rangle\,\phi(\zeta)\,d\sigma=0
for all
i=1,2,\ldots,n
,

(2)

\bar{\nabla}^{2}\phi^{-\frac{1}{k}}+\bar{g}\,\phi^{-\frac{1}{k}}\geq 0
and
\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}\phi^{-\frac{1}{k}}=\cot\theta\,\phi^{-\frac{1}{k}}
.

Then there exists a smooth, strictly convex capillary hypersurface
\Sigma\subset\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
whose
k
-th elementary symmetric function of the principal radii of curvature is given by:

(1.13)

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}(\tilde{\nu}^{-1}(\zeta))=\phi(\zeta),\quad\forall\zeta\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}.

Moreover,
\Sigma
is unique up to a horizontal translation in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
.

To prove
1.3
, a main challenge is the construction of a suitable homotopy path. An interesting corollary of
1.2
is that there is a unique strictly convex capillary hypersurface whose
\sigma_{k}
of radii of curvature is
\ell^{-k}
. This allows us to construct a homotopy path (cf.
5.3
) by suitably perturbing a linear interpolation between
\phi^{-1/k}
and
\ell
, while ensuring that the path remains within an admissible class of functions satisfying (1) and (2). The other challenges, such as establishing the
C^{2}
estimates, are overcome through surprisingly short arguments that rely on the specific structure of the problem and may offer a streamlined approach in related capillary curvature problems. Moreover, to prove that the solution is strictly convex, we follow the approach in
[
BIS23a
]
, which is a different route compared to the inductive procedure used in the work of Guan and Ma
[
GM03
]
. We show that the capillary support function is uniformly bounded away from zero along the homotopy path, and that the smallest principal radii of curvature satisfies a viscosity inequality. In particular, it cannot vanish identically, since it is strictly positive at the point where the capillary support function attains its minimum.

We mention that our arguments here also prove the result of
[
MWW25
]
via completely different arguments for obtaining
C^{0}
,
C^{1}
,
C^{2}
estimates when
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{C}_{\theta})
and
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
.

2.
Capillary hypersurfaces in the halfspace

In this section, we recall some basic facts about strictly convex capillary hypersurfaces
\Sigma\subset\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
MWWX24
]

The Gauss map
\nu:\Sigma\to\mathbb{S}^{n}
has its image in

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta}:=\{y\in\mathbb{S}^{n}\mid y_{n+1}\geq\cos\theta\},

and
\nu:\Sigma\to\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta}
is a diffeomorphism.

A simple consequence of this lemma is that the hypersurface
\Sigma
can be parametrized via the inverse capillary Gauss map:

(2.2)

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle:\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\to\Sigma

\displaystyle X(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\tilde{\nu}^{-1}(\zeta)=\nu^{-1}(\zeta+\cos\theta E_{n+1}).

The capillary support function of
\Sigma
,
s=s_{\Sigma}:\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\to\mathbb{R}
, is defined as

(2.3)

\displaystyle s(\zeta):=\langle X(\zeta),\nu(X(\zeta))\rangle=\langle\tilde{\nu}^{-1}(\zeta),\zeta+\cos\theta E_{n+1}\rangle,\quad\zeta\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}.

Note that the word “capillary” here emphasizes that the domain of
s
is not
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta}
but
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
. Often, it is more convenient to work with
\mathbb{S}_{\theta}^{n}
as the domain of
s
. To avoid confusion, we write
\hat{s}=\hat{s}_{\widehat{\Sigma}}:\mathbb{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
for the (standard) support function of the convex body
\widehat{\Sigma}
, which is defined as

(2.4)

\displaystyle\hat{s}(u):=\max_{x\in\widehat{\Sigma}}\langle u,x\rangle,\quad\forall u\in\mathbb{S}^{n}.

Let
(D,\delta)
and
(\bar{\nabla},\bar{g})
be the standard connection and metric of Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, respectively.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
MWWX24
, Lem. 2.4]

Assume that
\Sigma
is a strictly convex capillary hypersurface in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
. Then for the parametrization
X:\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\to\Sigma
, we have the following formulas:

(1)

X(\zeta)=\bar{\nabla}s(\zeta)+s(\zeta)(\zeta+\cos\theta E_{n+1})
.

(2)

\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}s=\cot\theta s
along
\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
, where
\mu
is the outward-pointing unit conormal to
\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\subset\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
.

(3)

The principal radii of curvature of
\Sigma
at
X(\zeta)
are the eigenvalues of
\tau[s]=\bar{\nabla}^{2}s+s\bar{g}
with respect to the round metric
\bar{g}
.

Definition 2.3
.

For
\theta\in(0,\pi)
,
f\in C^{2}(\mathcal{C}_{\theta})
is called a
capillary function
if it satisfies the following Robin-type boundary condition:

(2.5)

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}f=\cot\theta f\quad\text{along}\ \partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}.

In view of
2.2
,
\sigma_{n}(\tau^{\sharp}[s])
is the reciprocal of the Gauss curvature of
\Sigma
at the point
X(\zeta)
on
\Sigma
. Moreover
\zeta^{i}=\langle\zeta,E_{i}\rangle
is a capillary function and from the divergence-free structure of
\sigma_{k}
,
\tau[\zeta^{i}]=0
for
i=1,\ldots,n
and integration by parts, it follows that

(2.6)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\sigma_{k}(\tau^{\sharp}[s](\zeta))\zeta^{i}\,d\sigma=\frac{1}{k}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\sigma_{k}^{pq}\tau[\zeta^{i}]_{pq}s\,d\sigma=0,\quad\text{for}\ i=1,\ldots,n.

For a capillary function, the following crucial fact is known:

Lemma 2.4
.

[
MWWX24
, Lem. 2.7]

If
f
is a capillary function on
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
, then for all
2\leq\alpha\leq n
,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}^{2}f(e_{\alpha},\mu)=0\quad\text{along}\ \partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta},

where
\{e_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha=2,\ldots,n}
is a local orthonormal frame on
\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
.

Another crucial theorem that we need is the following statement.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
and
s\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{C}_{\theta})
be a capillary function with
\tau[s]>0
. Then the image
D\hat{s}(\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta})
is a smooth, strictly convex capillary hypersurface, where

(2.8)

\displaystyle\hat{s}(\cdot)=s(\cdot-\cos\theta\,E_{n+1}).

Proof.

Let
\Sigma=D\hat{s}(\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta})
. It can be verified that
D\hat{s}:\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta}\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
defines an immersion with positive Gauss curvature. Moreover,
D\hat{s}(\operatorname{int}(\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta}))\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
,
D\hat{s}(\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta})\subset\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
, and
\Sigma
intersects
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
at the constant angle
\theta
; see
[
MWWX24
]
.

For
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n}\cap E_{n+1}^{\bot}\sim\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
define

(2.9)

\displaystyle h(u)=\frac{\hat{s}(\sin\theta u+\cos\theta E_{n+1})}{\sin\theta}.

Note that
h
is the support function of a closed, strictly convex hypersurface
\mathcal{H}
in
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
.
Since
\mathcal{H}=Dh(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})=D\hat{s}(\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta})=\partial\Sigma
, we see that
\partial\Sigma
is embedded. Now from
[
Gho02
, Thm. 3.5]
it follows that
\Sigma
bounds a convex body and thus it is a capillary hypersurface.
∎

3.
A Full rank theorem

Theorem 3.1

(Full Rank Theorem)
.

Let
\theta\in(0,\pi/2]
and
1\leq k<n
. Assume that
\bar{\nabla}^{2}\phi^{-\frac{1}{k}}+\bar{g}\phi^{-\frac{1}{k}}\geq 0
, and that
\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}\log\phi\geq-k\cot\theta
. If
s
is a capillary function with
\tau[s]\geq 0
that satisfies
\sigma_{k}(\tau^{\sharp}[s])=\phi
, then the smallest eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}
of
\tau^{\sharp}
cannot attain zero unless
\lambda_{1}\equiv 0
on
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
. In particular, if
s>0
, we have
\lambda_{1}>0
on
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
.

Proof.

Let
F=\sigma_{k}^{\frac{1}{k}}
and
f=\phi^{\frac{1}{k}}
. If
\lambda_{1}=0
somewhere in the interior of
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
, then we can argue as in
[
BIS23a
]
and use that
\lambda_{1}
satisfies

(3.1)

\displaystyle L[\lambda_{1}]:=F^{ij}\bar{\nabla}_{i,j}^{2}\lambda_{1}-c(\lambda_{1}+|\bar{\nabla}\lambda_{1}|)\leq 0

in a viscosity sense. Here,
c=c(\dot{F},\ddot{F},|\bar{\nabla}\tau|)
is uniformly bounded in
\operatorname{int}(\mathcal{C}_{\theta})
.
By the strong maximum principle,
\lambda_{1}\equiv 0
in
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
.

Hence, we may suppose
\lambda_{1}(p)=0
for some
p\in\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
and
\lambda_{1}>0
in the interior. Let
\{\mu\}\cup\{e_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\geq 2}
be an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors of
\tau
at
p
, and set
e_{1}=\mu
, where we used
2.4
. We first compute the required boundary derivatives: By the Gaussian formula and Weingarten equation,

(3.2)

\displaystyle 0=e_{\beta}(\tau(\mu,e_{\alpha}))

\displaystyle=\bar{\nabla}_{\beta}\tau_{\alpha\mu}+\tau(\bar{\nabla}_{e_{\beta}}e_{\alpha},\mu)+\tau(\bar{\nabla}_{e_{\beta}}\mu,e_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\bar{\nabla}_{\beta}\tau_{\alpha\mu}+\tau(-\cot\theta\bar{g}_{\alpha\beta}\mu,\mu)+\tau(\cot\theta e_{\beta},e_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}\tau_{\alpha\beta}-\cot\theta\tau_{\mu\mu}\bar{g}_{\alpha\beta}+\cot\theta\tau_{\alpha\beta},

where we used that
\bar{\nabla}\tau
is fully symmetric. Thus, we obtain

(3.3)

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}\tau_{\alpha\beta}=(\tau_{\mu\mu}\bar{g}_{\alpha\beta}-\tau_{\alpha\beta})\cot\theta.

Next, we differentiate the equation and deduce

(3.4)

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}\tau_{\mu\mu}=\frac{\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}f}{F^{\mu\mu}}-\sum_{\alpha}\frac{F^{\alpha\alpha}}{F^{\mu\mu}}\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}\tau_{\alpha\alpha}=\frac{\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}f}{F^{\mu\mu}}+\sum_{\alpha}\frac{F^{\alpha\alpha}}{F^{\mu\mu}}(\tau_{\alpha\alpha}-\tau_{\mu\mu})\cot\theta.

Step 1
: We prove

(3.5)

\displaystyle\tau_{ii}(p)=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}\tau_{ii}(p)\geq 0.

For
i=\alpha
, this follows immediately from (
3.3
). For
i=\mu
, this follows after invoking (
3.4
), using the one-homogeneity of
F
, and the boundary condition for
f
.

Step 2
: Consider an interior ball
B_{R}(x_{0})\subset\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
touching at
p
. Define an annular region
A_{R,\rho}=B_{R}(x_{0})\setminus\operatorname{int}(B_{\rho}(x_{0}))
for some
0<\rho<R
.
For
x\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
, let
r(x)=\operatorname{dist}(x,x_{0})
denote the distance of
x
to
x_{0}
. We define

(3.6)

\displaystyle w(x)=e^{-\alpha R^{2}}-e^{-\alpha r(x)^{2}}.

For any
x\in A_{R,\rho}
, the distance function
r(x)
satisfies

(3.7)

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}^{2}_{i,j}r(x)=\cot r(x)(\bar{g}_{ij}-\bar{\nabla}_{i}r\bar{\nabla}_{j}r).

Therefore,

(3.8)

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{i}w

\displaystyle=2\alpha r\bar{\nabla}_{i}re^{-\alpha r^{2}},

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}^{2}_{i,j}w

\displaystyle=-4\alpha^{2}r^{2}\bar{\nabla}_{i}r\bar{\nabla}_{j}re^{-\alpha r^{2}}+2\alpha\bar{\nabla}_{i}r\bar{\nabla}_{j}re^{-\alpha r^{2}}+2\alpha r\bar{\nabla}^{2}_{i,j}re^{-\alpha r^{2}}

\displaystyle=e^{-\alpha r^{2}}\left((-4\alpha^{2}r^{2}+2\alpha-2\alpha r\cot r)\bar{\nabla}_{i}r\bar{\nabla}_{j}r+2\alpha r\cot r\bar{g}_{ij}\right)

and

(3.9)

\displaystyle L[w]=

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle e^{-\alpha r^{2}}(-4\alpha^{2}r^{2}+2\alpha-2\alpha r\cot r)|\bar{\nabla}r|_{\dot{F}}^{2}

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle+2\alpha r\cot re^{-\alpha r^{2}}\operatorname{tr}(\dot{F})-2c\alpha re^{-\alpha r^{2}}+c(e^{-\alpha r^{2}}-e^{-\alpha R^{2}}).

Assume that
\lambda\delta_{ij}\leq F^{ij}\leq\Lambda\delta_{ij}
in
B_{R}(x_{0})
, where
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda
are some constants. Note that
0\leq r\cot r\leq 1
for any
\rho\leq r\leq R
. Now take
\alpha>0
sufficiently large to ensure in
A_{R,\rho}
, that

(3.10)

\displaystyle L[w]\leq

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle-e^{-\alpha r^{2}}\left[(4\alpha^{2}r^{2}-2\alpha)\lambda-2\alpha n\Lambda+2c\alpha r-c\right]-ce^{-\alpha R^{2}}<0.

Step 3
: Since
\lambda_{1}(x)>0
on
\partial B_{\rho}(x_{0})
, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

(3.11)

\displaystyle\psi(x):=\lambda_{1}(x)+\varepsilon w(x)>0

on
\partial B_{\rho}(x_{0})
. Note that
w=0
on
\partial B_{R}(x_{0})
and by our assumption there is no other point on
\partial B_{R}(x_{0})\setminus\{p\}
where
\lambda_{1}=0
, hence,
\psi>0
on
\partial B_{R}(x_{0})\setminus\{p\}
. By the maximum principle for the viscosity supersolution
\psi
, we have
\psi\geq 0
in the annulus, where
\psi
is also the smallest eigenvalue of

(3.12)

\displaystyle S_{ij}=\tau_{ij}+\varepsilon w\bar{g}_{ij}.

Suppose at
p
the zero eigenvalue of
S(p)
is attained in direction
e_{i}
, i.e. we also have
\tau_{ii}=0
.
Let
\gamma
be a unit speed geodesic in direction
-\mu
and
e_{i}
be parallel transported along
\gamma
. Then from step 1 we get

(3.13)

\displaystyle{0\leq\frac{d}{dt}}_{|_{t=0^{+}}}S(\gamma(t))(e_{i},e_{i})=\bar{\nabla}_{-\mu}\tau_{ii}+\varepsilon\bar{\nabla}_{-\mu}w=-2\alpha\varepsilon R<0,

a contradiction.

To complete the proof, note that at a point where
s
attains its minimum, say
y
, we must have
\lambda_{1}(y)>0
. In fact,
y
cannot be on the boundary; otherwise, we would have
s(y)\cot\theta=\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}s(y)\leq 0
. Since
y\in\operatorname{int}(\mathcal{C}_{\theta})
,
\tau[s]|_{y}\geq s(y)\bar{g}>0
.

∎

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\theta\in(0,\pi/2]
and suppose that
\Sigma
is a strictly convex capillary hypersurface. Denote by

and
R_{\Omega}
the (standard) inner and outer radii of the convex body
\Omega=\widehat{\Sigma}\cap E_{n+1}^{\perp}\subset E_{n+1}^{\perp}\cong\mathbb{R}^{n}

\displaystyle\frac{R_{\Omega}^{2}}{r_{\Omega}}\leq C\max\lambda_{n},

where

denotes the largest principal radius of curvature at each point, and the constant
C
depends only on
n
and
\theta
.

Proof.

By
[
WWX24
, Prop. 2.4]
, the second fundamental form of
\Sigma
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
,
\mathrm{II}=(h_{ij})
, and the second fundamental form of
\partial\Sigma
in
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
,
\hat{\mathrm{II}}=(\hat{h}_{\alpha\beta})
,
2\leq\alpha,\beta\leq n
, are related by

(4.2)

\mathrm{II}_{\alpha\beta}=\hat{\mathrm{II}}_{\alpha\beta}\sin\theta.

Now, the claim follows from
[
CW00
, Lem. 2.2]
.

∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\theta\in(0,\pi/2]
and suppose that
\Sigma
is a strictly convex capillary hypersurface. If
\lvert s_{\Sigma}\rvert\leq C
, then

(4.3)

\displaystyle\lvert\hat{s}_{\widehat{\Sigma}}\rvert\leq\frac{C}{\sin\theta}.

Proof.

Note that
\hat{s}(-E_{n+1})=0
and
s(\zeta)=\hat{s}(\zeta+\cos\theta E_{n+1})
for all
\zeta\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
. Let
\Omega=\widehat{\Sigma}\cap E_{n+1}^{\bot}
, which we consider as a convex set in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, and write
\hat{s}_{\Omega}:\mathbb{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
for its the support function. For

with

, in view of
\hat{s}_{\Omega}(x+tE_{n+1})=\hat{s}_{\Omega}(x)

(4.4)

\displaystyle\hat{s}(u)=\hat{s}_{\Omega}(u)=\langle u,\tilde{u}\rangle\hat{s}_{\Omega}(\tilde{u}),

.
Hence,

(4.5)

\displaystyle\hat{s}(u)=\langle u,\tilde{u}\rangle\hat{s}_{\Omega}(\tilde{u})=\frac{\langle u,\tilde{u}\rangle}{\sin\theta}\hat{s}(\sin\theta\tilde{u}+\cos\theta E_{n+1})=\frac{\langle u,\tilde{u}\rangle}{\sin\theta}s(\sin\theta\tilde{u}).

∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\psi
be one of the following functions
x^{2}
,
x^{p}
for some
p<0
, or
-\log x
. Suppose
\theta\in(0,\pi/2]
and
\Sigma
is a strictly convex capillary hypersurface. Then there exists a unique point
z\in\operatorname{int}(\widehat{\Sigma}\cap E_{n+1}^{\perp})
such that

(4.6)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\psi^{\prime}\left(s_{\Sigma}(\zeta)-\langle\zeta,z\rangle\right)\zeta_{i}\,d\sigma=0,\quad\forall i=1,\ldots,n.

In particular,
s_{\Sigma-z}>0
.

Proof.

We adapt the proof from
[
GN17
]
to the capillary setting. Note that for all
z\in\operatorname{int}(\widehat{\Sigma}\cap E_{n+1}^{\perp})
, it holds that
s_{\Sigma-z}>0
. This can be seen by reflecting
\hat{\Sigma}
across
E_{n+1}^{\perp}
, which yields a closed convex body in which
z
lies in the interior. Define

(4.7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(y)=\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\psi(s(\zeta)-\langle\zeta,y\rangle)\,d\sigma,\quad y\in\mathrm{int}(\widehat{\Sigma}\cap E_{n+1}^{\bot}).

We will prove that
\mathcal{F}
attains a minimum in the interior of
\Omega=\widehat{\Sigma}\cap E_{n+1}^{\perp}
. First, we note that
\mathcal{F}
can be extended up to the boundary, as for every
y\in\partial\Omega
the improper integral exists.

Let
y_{0}\in\partial\Omega=\partial\Sigma
and suppose without loss of generality that
\mathcal{F}(y_{0})<\infty
. We may translate and rotate
\Omega
in
E_{n+1}^{\bot}
so that
y_{0}
lies at the origin and
E_{1}
is the outer unit normal to
\partial\Omega=\partial\Sigma
, as a hypersurface of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, at
y_{0}
. Thus, the outer unit normal of
\Sigma
at
y_{0}
is
x_{0}=\sin\theta E_{1}+\cos\theta E_{n+1}\in\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta}
(cf.
[
WWX24
, (2.8)]
) and
-tE_{1}\in\operatorname{int}(\Omega)
for all sufficiently small
t>0
. Consequently, the function

(4.8)

\displaystyle t\mapsto\mathcal{F}(-tE_{1})

is
C^{2}
on the interval
(0,\varepsilon)
for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
, with differentiation performed under the integral sign. We claim that, possibly after shrinking
\varepsilon
further, the derivative with respect to
t
is negative in this interval.

For
a^{+}=\sin\theta E_{1}\in\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
, we have

(4.9)

\displaystyle s(a^{+})

\displaystyle=\hat{s}(\sin\theta E_{1}+\cos\theta E_{n+1})=\max_{y\in\widehat{\Sigma}}\langle\sin\theta E_{1}+\cos\theta E_{n+1},y\rangle=0.

This implies that
\widehat{\Sigma}\subset\{y\in\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}\penalty\ |\sin\theta y_{1}+\cos\theta y_{n+1}\leq 0\}
.

Note that for
a^{-}=-\sin\theta E_{1}\in\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
, we must have
s(a^{-})>0
. Otherwise,

(4.10)

\displaystyle 0\geq s(a^{-})

\displaystyle=s(-\sin\theta E_{1})=\max_{y\in\widehat{\Sigma}}\langle-\sin\theta E_{1}+\cos\theta E_{n+1},y\rangle,

and thus
\widehat{\Sigma}\subset\{y\in\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}\penalty\ |-\sin\theta y_{1}+\cos\theta y_{n+1}\leq 0\}
. However, this implies that
V(\widehat{\Sigma})=0
.

Now, for any
\zeta^{+}=(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2},\ldots,\zeta_{n+1})\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
such that
\zeta_{1}>0
, we define a corresponding point
\zeta^{-}
by
\zeta^{-}=(-\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2},\ldots,\zeta_{n+1})
and let
i(\zeta^{+})
be the point on
\Sigma
such that
s(\zeta^{+})=\langle\zeta^{+}+\cos\theta E_{n+1},i(\zeta^{+})\rangle
. Note that the
\zeta_{1}
-component of
i(\zeta^{+})
is non-positive, since we have
\widehat{\Sigma}\subset\{y\in\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}\penalty\ |\sin\theta y_{1}+\cos\theta y_{n+1}\leq 0\}
. Thus,

(4.11)

\displaystyle s(\zeta^{-})=\hat{s}(\zeta^{-}+\cos\theta E_{n+1})

\displaystyle=\max_{y\in\widehat{\Sigma}}\langle\zeta^{-}+\cos\theta E_{n+1},y\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\langle\zeta^{-}+\cos\theta E_{n+1},i(\zeta^{+})\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\langle\zeta^{+}+\cos\theta E_{n+1},i(\zeta^{+})\rangle=s(\zeta^{+}).

Since
s(a^{-})>s(a^{+})
, by continuity, the set of points with
s(\zeta^{-})>s(\zeta^{+})
has a positive measure.

We compute for
t>0
,

(4.12)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{F}(-tE_{1})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\psi^{\prime}(s(\zeta)+t\zeta_{1})\zeta_{1}\,d\sigma

\displaystyle=\int_{\{\zeta\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}|\zeta_{1}>0\}}\left(\psi^{\prime}(s(\zeta^{+})+t\zeta_{1})-\psi^{\prime}(s(\zeta^{-})-t\zeta_{1})\right)\zeta_{1}\,d\sigma.

Since
\psi^{\prime\prime}>0
, for
t_{i+1},t_{i}\in(0,\epsilon)
with
t_{i+1}\leq t_{i}
we have

(4.13)

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime}(s(\zeta^{-})-t_{i+1}\zeta_{1})-\psi^{\prime}(s(\zeta^{+})+t_{i+1}\zeta_{1})\geq\psi^{\prime}(s(\zeta^{-})-t_{i}\zeta_{1})-\psi^{\prime}(s(\zeta^{+})+t_{i}\zeta_{1}).

Moreover, since
s(\zeta)-t_{1}\langle\zeta,E_{1}\rangle>0
, for some constant
C
we have

(4.14)

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime}(s(\zeta^{-})-t_{1}\zeta_{1})-\psi^{\prime}(s(\zeta^{+})+t_{1}\zeta_{1})

\displaystyle\geq-C.

Therefore, by the monotone convergence theorem:

(4.15)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{F}(-tE_{1})=\int_{\{\zeta\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\mid\zeta_{1}>0\}}\left(\psi^{\prime}(s(\zeta^{+}))-\psi^{\prime}(s(\zeta^{-}))\right)\zeta_{1}\,d\sigma<0.

That is,
t\mapsto\mathcal{F}(-tE_{1})
is decreasing for small
t>0
. For
\psi(x)=x^{2}
, this shows
\mathcal{F}
does not attain its minimum on
\partial\Omega
. We consider the other choices of
\psi
.

Note that for
t>0
,

(4.16)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(-tE_{1})

\displaystyle=\int_{\{\zeta_{1}\geq 0\}}\psi(s(\zeta)+t\zeta_{1})+\int_{\{\zeta_{1}<0\}}\psi(s(\zeta)+t\zeta_{1}).

Since
\psi^{\prime}<0
, again by the monotone convergence theorem, we find

(4.17)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(-tE_{1})\to\mathcal{F}(0)<\infty.

Therefore,
\mathcal{F}(-tE_{1})<\mathcal{F}(0)
for small
t>0
.

Now suppose the minimum of
\mathcal{F}
in
\Omega
is attained at
z\in\operatorname{int}(\Omega)
, then

(4.18)

\displaystyle\left\langle D\mathcal{F}|_{z},E_{i}\right\rangle=0,\quad\forall i=1,\ldots,n,

which is (
4.33
). The uniqueness of
z
follows from the strict convexity of the functional
\mathcal{F}
:

(4.19)

\displaystyle D^{2}\mathcal{F}(E_{i},E_{j})=\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\psi^{\prime\prime}\zeta_{i}\zeta_{j}\,d\sigma,\quad 1\leq i,j\leq n.

∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\psi
be one of the following functions
x^{2}
,
x^{p}
for some
p<0
, or
-\log x
. Suppose
\theta\in(0,\pi/2]

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(y)=\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\psi(h(\zeta)-\langle\zeta,y\rangle)\,d\sigma,\quad y\in\widehat{\Sigma}_{1}\cap E_{n+1}^{\bot}.

(4.24)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\hat{s}_{\Omega_{1}}(E_{1})=\frac{\hat{s}_{\widehat{\Sigma}_{1}}(\sin\theta E_{1}+\cos\theta E_{n+1})}{\sin\theta},

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle<\hat{s}_{\Omega_{1}}(-E_{1})=\frac{\hat{s}_{\widehat{\Sigma}_{1}}(-\sin\theta E_{1}+\cos\theta E_{n+1})}{\sin\theta}.

Remark 4.5
.

It is not clear to us whether a reflection argument could be used to prove the last statement in
4.3
for the case
\psi(x)=x^{2}
. That is, if

(4.26)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}s_{\Sigma}(\zeta)\zeta_{i}\,d\sigma=0,\quad i=1,\ldots,n,

then
s>0
. Note that from (
4.26
), we obtain

(4.27)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\hat{s}_{K}(x)x_{i}\,d\sigma=\int_{R}\hat{s}_{K}(x)x_{i}\,d\sigma,

where
R
is the complement of
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta}\cup(-\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta})
in
\mathbb{S}^{n}
and
K
is the enclosed region by
\Sigma
and its reflection across
x_{n+1}=0
.

If
\widehat{\Sigma}\cap E_{n+1}^{\perp}
is origin-symmetric, then the integral over
R
vanishes. Indeed, in this case, we can simply argue as follows:

(4.28)

\displaystyle\hat{s}_{K}(x)=\langle x,\tilde{x}\rangle\hat{s}_{\Omega}(\tilde{x}),\quad x\in R,

where
\tilde{x}
is the projection of
x
onto
E_{n+1}^{\perp}
and normalized to have length one, and
\hat{s}_{\Omega}
is the support function of the convex set
\Omega=K\cap E_{n+1}^{\perp}
. Given

, consider the reflected point

(4.29)

\displaystyle y=(-x_{1},\ldots,-x_{n},x_{n+1})\in R.

Then
\tilde{y}=-\tilde{x}
and, by the origin symmetry of

, we have

. Moreover,

(4.30)

\displaystyle\langle x,\tilde{x}\rangle=\langle y,\tilde{y}\rangle,\quad\text{while}\quad\langle y,E_{i}\rangle=-\langle x,E_{i}\rangle.

Thus, the contributions from
x
and

, and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\hat{s}_{K}(x)x_{i}\,d\sigma=0,\quad i=1,\ldots,n.

For

, due to the reflection symmetry of

, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\hat{s}_{K}(x)x_{n+1}\,d\sigma=0.

Hence, the Steiner point of

is at the origin and

, see

.

However, if

vanishes.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
and
1\leq k\leq n
. Suppose
\Sigma
is a strictly convex capillary hypersurface and
s_{\Sigma}
satisfies

(4.33)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}s_{\Sigma}(\zeta)\zeta_{i}\,d\sigma=0,\quad\forall i=1,\ldots,n.

and
\sigma_{k}(\tau^{\sharp}[s_{\Sigma}])=\phi
. Then
0<s_{\Sigma}\leq C
for some constant depending on
\theta
and
\phi
. Moreover, for
k=n
, we have

for some vector
w\in\widehat{\Sigma}\cap E_{n+1}^{\bot}
and for some constants

depending on
\theta
and
\phi
.

Proof.

In view of
4.3
with
\psi(x)=x^{2}
,

, hence,
\hat{s}\geq 0
. By integrating by parts (cf.
[
MWWX24

\displaystyle c^{\prime}_{n,k}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}s\phi^{\frac{k-1}{k}}\,d\sigma\leq\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}s\sigma_{k-1}\,d\sigma=c_{n,k}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\ell\sigma_{k}\,d\sigma=c_{n,k}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\ell\phi\,d\sigma.

Therefore,

(4.35)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta}}\hat{s}\,d\sigma=\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}s\,d\sigma\leq C.

Suppose the maximum of
\hat{s}
is attained for some
u_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{n}\setminus\operatorname{int}(\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta})
. Note that
u_{0}\neq-E_{n+1}
, and in the

there holds
\hat{s}=\hat{s}_{\Omega}
, where
\Omega=\widehat{\Sigma}\cap E_{n+1}^{\bot}
is considered as a convex set in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Write
u_{0}=\sin\tilde{\theta}\tilde{u}_{0}\pm\cos\tilde{\theta}E_{n+1}
for some
\tilde{\theta}\in(0,\pi/2]
and
\tilde{u}_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. Therefore,
\hat{s}(u_{0})=\sin\tilde{\theta}\hat{s}_{\Omega}(\tilde{u}_{0})
. This implies that the maximum of
\hat{s}
must be attained for some

(i.e.,
\tilde{\theta}=\pi/2
). In this case, note that
\max_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\hat{s}_{\Omega}
is also attained at
u_{0}
, hence, we have

(4.36)

\displaystyle(\max\hat{s})u_{0}=\hat{s}_{\Omega}(u_{0})u_{0}\in\partial\Sigma\subset\widehat{\Sigma}.

Let us assume
u_{0}=E_{1}
. By the definition of
\hat{s}
, for

:

(4.37)

\displaystyle\hat{s}(x)\geq x_{1}\max\hat{s}

and hence,

(4.38)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta}}\hat{s}d\sigma\geq\int_{\{x_{1}\geq\frac{1}{2}\sin\theta\}\cap\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta}}\hat{s}\,d\sigma\geq(\max\hat{s})\frac{\sin\theta}{2}\int_{\{x_{1}\geq\frac{1}{2}\sin\theta\}\cap\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta}}\,d\sigma.

Suppose the maximum of
\hat{s}
is attained at

. Therefore, we have
(\max\hat{s})u_{0}\in\Sigma
. We may assume

with

. Therefore,

(4.39)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta}}\hat{s}\,d\sigma\geq\int_{\{x_{1}\geq 0\}\cap\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta}}\hat{s}\,d\sigma

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\{x_{1}\geq 0\}\cap\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta}}(\max\hat{s})x_{n+1}\cos\theta_{0}\,d\sigma

\displaystyle\geq(\max\hat{s})\cos\theta\cos\theta_{0}\int_{\{x_{1}\geq 0\}\cap\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta}}d\sigma

\displaystyle\geq(\max\hat{s})\cos^{2}\theta\int_{\{x_{1}\geq 0\}\cap\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta}}d\sigma.

Next, we prove the lower bound on

when

. Note that

(4.40)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}s\sigma_{n}\,d\sigma

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}s\phi\,d\sigma\geq\min\phi\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}s\,d\sigma\geq c_{n,\theta}\min\phi\left(\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}s\sigma_{n}\,d\sigma\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}.

, from which it follows that the sequence

\displaystyle a_{i}=V(\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\theta},\dots,\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\theta},\widehat{\Sigma},\dots,\widehat{\Sigma}),\quad i=0,\dots,n+1,

where
\widehat{\Sigma}
appears

\frac{a_{1}}{a_{0}}\geq\frac{a_{2}}{a_{1}}\geq\cdots\geq\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_{n}}.

In particular, we have

(4.43)

\displaystyle\frac{a_{1}}{a_{0}}\geq\left(\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_{0}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}.

Thus,

is bounded below by a constant that depends only on
\theta
and
\phi
.

Define

(4.44)

\displaystyle w_{-}=\min_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}}(\hat{s}(-x)+\hat{s}(x)),\quad w_{+}=\max_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(\hat{s}(-x)+\hat{s}(x)).

Then we have
V(\widehat{\Sigma})\leq w_{-}w_{+}^{n}
. Moreover, by
[
Sch14
, p. 320]
, we have

(4.45)

\frac{w_{-}}{n+2}B\subseteq\widehat{\Sigma}-\operatorname{centroid}(\widehat{\Sigma}),

where
B
denotes the unit ball. Therefore, the radius of the inner ball of
\widehat{\Sigma}
satisfies
r\geq\frac{w_{-}}{n+2}
. Since
\hat{s}
is bounded above (cf.
4.2
), we can find
r=r(\theta,\phi)
and a point
x\in\operatorname{int}(\widehat{\Sigma})
such that

(4.46)

\displaystyle B_{r}(x)\subseteq\widehat{\Sigma}.

Now, from
[
SW24
, (2.26)]
it follows that

\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\theta,\frac{r}{\sin\theta}}\subseteq\widehat{\Sigma}-w,

where
w\in\widehat{\Sigma}\cap E_{n+1}^{\bot}
is the projection of
x
onto
E_{n+1}^{\bot}
. By
4.2
,

|w|\leq\frac{C}{\sin\theta}.

Hence,

(4.47)

\displaystyle s_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta,\frac{r}{\sin\theta}}}\leq s_{\Sigma-w}\leq C\left(1+\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\right).

∎

Remark 4.7
.

In the proof of

, we also proved that when

attains its maximum either in

or in

is never attained on

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
\theta\in(0,\pi)
and
\Sigma
be a strictly convex capillary hypersurface with
0<s_{\Sigma}\leq C
. Then
|\bar{\nabla}s|\leq\frac{C}{\sin\theta}
.

Proof.

Set
\rho^{2}=s^{2}+|\bar{\nabla}s|^{2}
. Suppose
\rho
attains its maximum at
p
. If
p\in\operatorname{int}(\mathcal{C}_{\theta})
, then
\bar{\nabla}s|_{p}=0
and
\rho^{2}\leq C^{2}
. Suppose
p\in\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
. Let
\{\mu\}\cup\{e_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\geq 2}
be an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors of
\tau^{\sharp}
at
p
. Using
2.4
we find

(4.48)

\displaystyle 0=\bar{\nabla}_{e_{\alpha}}\rho^{2}=2s\bar{\nabla}_{e_{\alpha}}s+2\bar{\nabla}_{e_{\beta}}s\bar{\nabla}^{2}s(e_{\beta},e_{\alpha})=2\lambda_{\alpha}\bar{\nabla}_{e_{\alpha}}s.

Therefore,

\bar{\nabla}_{e_{\alpha}}s|_{p}=0
for all
\alpha=2,\ldots,n
and
|\bar{\nabla}s|=|\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}s|=\lvert\cot\theta\rvert s
. Hence,

(4.49)

\displaystyle\rho^{2}(p)\leq\frac{C^{2}}{\sin^{2}\theta}.

∎

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
and
1\leq k\leq n
.
Suppose
\Sigma
is a strictly convex capillary hypersurface and
s_{\Sigma}
satisfies
\sigma_{k}(\tau^{\sharp}[s_{\Sigma}])=\phi
. Then,

\sigma_{1}(\tau^{\sharp}[s_{\Sigma}])\leq C

for some constant depending only on
\theta
and
\phi
.

Proof.

Recall that
F=\sigma_{k}^{\frac{1}{k}}
and
f=\phi^{\frac{1}{k}}
. If the maximum of
\sigma_{1}
is attained in the interior of
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
, then

(4.50)

\displaystyle 0\geq F^{ij}\bar{\nabla}^{2}_{i,j}\sigma_{1}

\displaystyle=-\bar{g}^{mn}F^{ij,pq}\bar{\nabla}_{m}\tau_{ij}\bar{\nabla}_{n}\tau_{pq}-nf+\bar{\Delta}f+F^{ij}\bar{g}_{ij}\sigma_{1}

\displaystyle\geq-nf+\bar{\Delta}f+F^{ij}\bar{g}_{ij}\sigma_{1},

where we used
\bar{g}^{mn}F^{ij,pq}\bar{\nabla}_{m}\tau_{ij}\bar{\nabla}_{n}\tau_{pq}\leq 0
(due to the concavity of
F
) and the one-homogeneity of
F
. The claim follows from
\operatorname{tr}(\dot{F})\geq c_{n,k}
(again due to the concavity of
F
).

Now we need to treat the case that the maximum of
\sigma_{1}
is attained at a boundary point, say
p
. Then at
p
we have

(4.51)

\displaystyle 0\leq F^{\mu\mu}\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}\sigma_{1}

\displaystyle=\cot\theta\sum_{i}(F^{\mu\mu}-F^{ii})(\lambda_{\mu}-\lambda_{i})+\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}f

\displaystyle=\cot\theta(f-F^{\mu\mu}\sigma_{1}+\sum_{i}F^{\mu\mu}\lambda_{\mu}-\sum_{i}F^{ii}\lambda_{\mu})+\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}f

\displaystyle\leq\cot\theta\left((n+1)f-F^{\mu\mu}\sigma_{1}-\sum_{i}F^{ii}\lambda_{\mu}\right)+\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}f.

Therefore,

(4.52)

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(p)\leq\frac{\max|\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}f|}{\cot\theta F^{\mu\mu}(p)}+(n+1)\frac{f(p)}{F^{\mu\mu}(p)}.

Next we show that
F^{\mu\mu}
cannot be very small. Note that

(4.53)

\displaystyle c:=\min\phi\leq\phi=\sigma_{k}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\mu}\sigma_{k-1}(\lambda|\lambda_{\mu})+\sigma_{k}(\lambda|\lambda_{\mu})

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{\mu}\sigma_{k-1}(\lambda|\lambda_{\mu})+c_{0}\sigma_{k-1}(\lambda|\lambda_{\mu})^{\frac{k}{k-1}}

\displaystyle\leq c_{2}\lambda_{\mu}F^{\mu\mu}+c_{3}(F^{\mu\mu})^{\frac{k}{k-1}}.

Using this in (
4.51
), we obtain

(4.54)

\displaystyle 0\leq F^{\mu\mu}\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}\sigma_{1}

\displaystyle\leq((n+1)f-\sum_{i}F^{ii}\lambda_{\mu})\cot\theta+\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}f

\displaystyle\leq(n+1)f\cot\theta+\frac{1}{c_{2}}\sum_{i}F^{ii}\left(-\frac{c}{F^{\mu\mu}}+c_{3}(F^{\mu\mu})^{\frac{1}{k-1}}\right)+\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}f.

Due to concavity of
F
, we have
\operatorname{tr}(\dot{F})\geq c_{n,k}
. Hence
F^{\mu\mu}
cannot be small, and in view of (
4.52
),
\sigma_{1}
is bounded above and the bound depends only on
\theta,\phi
.

∎

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
and
1\leq k\leq n
. Suppose
\Sigma
is a strictly convex capillary hypersurface whose
s_{\Sigma}
satisfies (
4.33
) and
\sigma_{k}(\tau^{\sharp}[s_{\Sigma}])=\phi
. Then for any

we have
\|s_{\Sigma}\|_{C^{m}}\leq C_{m}
for some constant depending only on
\theta,\phi
.

Proof.

By
4.3
and
4.6
, we have
0<s\leq C_{1}
. Hence, by
4.8
and
4.9
,

LT86
]
and Schauder estimate.

∎

5.
Continuity method

To prove
1.3
, we need to construct a suitable homotopy path that stays within the appropriate function class and along which we can establish uniform estimates. We now present the necessary preliminary results.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\theta\in(0,\pi/2]
. Consider a sequence of strictly convex capillary hypersurfaces
\{\Sigma_{i}\}
with uniform
C^{2}
-norm bound on their capillary support functions
\{s_{\Sigma_{i}}\}
and satisfying (
4.33
). Suppose
\sigma_{k}(\tau^{\sharp}[s_{\Sigma_{i}}])\geq c_{0}>0
for all
i
. Then there is a constant
c>0
such that

(5.1)

\displaystyle\hat{s}_{\widehat{\Sigma}_{i}}(E_{n+1})\geq c\quad\forall i.

Proof.

Suppose that along a subsequence,
\hat{s}_{\widehat{\Sigma}_{i}}(E_{n+1})\to 0
. Hence, due to
4.2
,
\widehat{\Sigma}_{i}
converges in the Hausdorff distance to a convex set
K\subset E_{n+1}^{\bot}
. Recall that
s_{\Sigma_{i}}(\zeta)=\hat{s}_{\widehat{\Sigma}_{i}}(\zeta+\cos\theta E_{n+1})
for all
\zeta\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
and by our assumption
\|s_{\Sigma_{i}}\|_{C^{2}}
is uniformly bounded above. Therefore,
\hat{s}_{K}
is differentiable at
E_{n+1}
, and by
[
Sch14
, Cor. 1.7.3]
, the set

(5.2)

\displaystyle F(K,E_{n+1}):=E_{n+1}^{\bot}\cap K=K

must contain only one point. By
4.3
,
0\in\operatorname{int}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{i}\cap E_{n+1}^{\bot})
. Therefore,
K=\{0\}
,
\hat{s}_{\widehat{\Sigma}_{i}}\to 0
and
s_{\Sigma_{i}}\to 0
uniformly (i.e. in the
\sup
-norm).

Now, by integrating by parts (cf.
[
MWWX24
, Cor. 2.10]
) and the uniform upper bound on
\sigma_{k-1}^{\Sigma_{i}}
, we find

(5.3)

\displaystyle C\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}s_{\Sigma_{i}}\,d\sigma

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}s_{\Sigma_{i}}\sigma_{k-1}^{\Sigma_{i}}\,d\sigma=c_{n,k}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\ell\sigma_{k}^{\Sigma_{i}}\,d\sigma\geq c_{0}c_{n,k}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\ell\,d\sigma.

Taking the limit as
i\to\infty
, we arrive at a contradiction.
∎

Theorem 5.2
.

Under the assumptions of
5.1
,
let
\Omega_{i}=\widehat{\Sigma}_{i}\cap E_{n+1}^{\bot}
and assume that
\max_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\hat{s}_{\Omega_{i}}\geq c_{1}>0
for all
i
, where
\hat{s}_{\Omega_{i}}
denotes the support function of
\Omega_{i}
as a convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Then we have
V(\widehat{\Sigma}_{i})\geq c_{2}>0
for all
i
.

Proof.

Since
\max_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\hat{s}_{\Omega_{i}}\geq c_{1}
,
0\in\operatorname{int}(\Omega_{i})
, and

(5.4)

\displaystyle\hat{s}_{\Omega_{i}}(x)+\hat{s}_{\Omega_{i}}(-x)\leq\operatorname{diam}(\Omega_{i})\leq 2R_{\Omega_{i}}\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1},

we have
R_{\Omega_{i}}\geq c_{1}/2
. Therefore, due to
4.1
,
r_{\Omega_{i}}
is uniformly bounded below away from zero.

Recall from
5.1
that
\hat{s}_{\widehat{\Sigma}_{i}}(E_{n+1})
is uniformly bounded below away from zero. Considering the convex hull of the inner ball of
\Omega_{i}
with radius

and the point on
\Sigma_{i}
whose supporting hyperplane is parallel to
E_{n+1}^{\bot}
(which forms an oblique cone that can be transformed into a right cone of the same height via a volume-preserving shear transformation that acts as the identity on

for some

.
∎

Theorem 5.3

Let
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
and

\displaystyle\tau[\phi^{-\frac{1}{k}}]\geq 0,\quad\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}\phi^{-\frac{1}{k}}=\phi^{-\frac{1}{k}}\cot\theta,\quad\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\phi(\zeta)\zeta^{i}\,d\sigma=0,\quad i=1,\ldots,n.

\displaystyle\phi_{t}(\zeta):=((1-t)\ell(\zeta)+t\phi^{-\frac{1}{k}}(\zeta)-\langle\zeta,z_{t}\rangle)^{-k}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\phi_{t}(\zeta)\zeta^{i}\,d\sigma=0,\quad i=1,\ldots,n

For

\displaystyle\tau[\phi_{t}^{-\frac{1}{k}}]>0,\quad\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}\phi_{t}^{-\frac{1}{k}}=\phi_{t}^{-\frac{1}{k}}\cot\theta.

For
t=1

k\geq 1

Next we prove

(see
2.5
) and put
\Omega_{i}=\widehat{\Sigma}_{i}\cap E_{n+1}^{\bot}
. By
4.10
, after passing to a subsequence,
\{\widehat{\Sigma}_{i}\}
converges in the Hausdorff distance to a convex set
\widehat{\Sigma}
, and
s_{t_{i}}
converges to a solution
s\geq 0
of
\sigma_{k}(\tau^{\sharp}[s])=\phi_{t_{0}}
with
\tau[s]\geq 0
.

Note that for all
i
we have

(5.35)

\displaystyle s_{\Sigma_{i}}(\zeta)=\hat{s}_{\widehat{\Sigma}_{i}}(\zeta+\cos\theta E_{n+1}),\quad\forall\zeta\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}

and after taking the limit, we find

(5.36)

\displaystyle s(\zeta)=\hat{s}_{\widehat{\Sigma}}(\zeta+\cos\theta E_{n+1}),\quad\forall\zeta\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}.

By
5.1
, there exists
w\in\partial\widehat{\Sigma}
with
\langle w,E_{n+1}\rangle>0
. Hence,

(5.37)

\displaystyle\hat{s}_{\widehat{\Sigma}}(x)\geq\langle x,w\rangle,\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}.

If
w=E_{n+1}
, then for any
x_{0}\in\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta}
, we have
\hat{s}_{\widehat{\Sigma}}(x_{0})>0
. If
w\neq E_{n+1}
, then for
x_{0}=\sin\theta\,\tilde{w}+\cos\theta\,E_{n+1}\in\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta}
, we also have
\hat{s}_{\widehat{\Sigma}}(x_{0})>0
. Hence, we conclude that

\max s|_{\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}>0.

This implies that
\max_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\hat{s}_{\Omega_{i}}\geq\delta
for some
\delta>0
and all
i
. Now, from
5.2
, it follows that
V(\widehat{\Sigma})\geq c
. Since
s\in C_{e}(\mathcal{C}_{\theta})
, we conclude that
s>0
, and from
3.1
, it follows that
\tau[s]>0
. Therefore,
t_{0}\in S
.

Next, we prove the uniqueness claims. Let
s_{1},s_{2}\in C^{3}(\mathcal{C}_{\theta})
be two capillary functions with
\tau[s_{i}]>0
. In view of
[
MWWX24
, Thm. 1.1]
, the sequence

(5.38)

\displaystyle a_{i}=V(\widehat{\Sigma}_{2}[k+1-i],\widehat{\Sigma}_{1}[i],\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\theta},\ldots,\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\theta}),\quad i=0,\ldots,k+1,

is log-concave i.e.,

(5.39)

\displaystyle\frac{a_{1}}{a_{0}}\geq\frac{a_{2}}{a_{1}}\geq\cdots\geq\frac{a_{k+1}}{a_{k}}.

Here,
\widehat{\Sigma}_{i}[\cdot]
indicates the number of times
\widehat{\Sigma}_{i}
appears. Moreover, equality holds in either of these inequalities if and only if

(5.40)

\displaystyle\widehat{\Sigma}_{2}=a\widehat{\Sigma}_{1}+\vec{a}

for some
\vec{a}\in E_{n+1}^{\bot}
and
a>0
. Therefore, we have

(5.41)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{a_{k}}{a_{0}}\right)^{\frac{1}{k}}\geq\left(\frac{a_{k+1}}{a_{0}}\right)^{\frac{1}{k+1}}.

That is,

(5.42)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}s_{2}\sigma_{k}(\tau^{\sharp}[s_{1}])\,d\sigma\geq\left(\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}s_{1}\sigma_{k}(\tau^{\sharp}[s_{1}])\,d\sigma\right)^{\frac{k}{k+1}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}s_{2}\sigma_{k}(\tau^{\sharp}[s_{2}])\,d\sigma\right)^{\frac{1}{k+1}}.

Consequently, if
\sigma_{k}(\tau^{\sharp}[s_{1}])=\sigma_{k}(\tau^{\sharp}[s_{2}])
, equality in (
5.41
) follows and thus

(5.43)

\displaystyle s_{2}(\zeta)=s_{1}(\zeta)+\langle\vec{a},\zeta\rangle.

Furthermore, concerning the uniqueness claim in
1.2
, if both of these solutions are in
C_{e}(\mathcal{C}_{\theta})
, then we must have
\vec{a}=0
.

Now, we prove
1.3
. We define
\phi_{t}
as in
5.3
:

(5.44)

\displaystyle\phi_{t}(\zeta):=((1-t)\ell+t\phi^{-\frac{1}{k}}(\zeta)-\langle\zeta,z_{t}\rangle)^{-k}.

For
t=0
,
z_{0}=0
and
\ell^{-k}
satisfies the assumptions of
1.2
. Therefore, the equation

\sigma_{k}(\tau[s_{0}],\bar{g})=\ell^{-k}

has a (unique) strictly convex capillary solution. That is,
0\in\mathcal{A}_{k}
.

To prove that
\mathcal{A}_{k}
is closed, we make a minor adjustment to the final part of the closedness argument for
1.2
. The function
s
that we obtained as the limit of
s_{t_{i}}
is non-negative. If necessary, since
V(\widehat{\Sigma})>0
, we may translate
\widehat{\Sigma}
to
\widehat{\Sigma}-v
for some
v\in\operatorname{int}(\widehat{\Sigma}\cap E_{n+1}^{\bot})
so that
s_{\Sigma-v}>0
, while
s_{\Sigma-v}
still satisfies the equation
\sigma_{k}(\tau^{\sharp}[s_{\Sigma-v}])=\phi_{t_{0}}
.

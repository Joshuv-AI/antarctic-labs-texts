SymTFT actions, Condensable algebras and Categorical anomaly resolutions
Daniel Robbins and Subham Roy

Abstract




We investigate symmetry topological field theories (SymTFTs) of non-abelian and non-invertible symmetries and the different Lagrangian algebras associated with a given Drinfeld center. For several examples we analyze the condensable algebras of the Drinfeld center to identify the intrinsically gapless symmetry protected topological (igSPT) phases. In the previous work, the relation between igSPT phases and resolving anomalies by embedding an anomalous symmetry inside a larger fusion category was demonstrated. Here we present more examples of this mechanism that involve both group-like and categorical symmetries.

Enumerating condensable algebras for
\mathcal{Z}(G)

Let
G
be a finite group with idemtity element
e
. The Drinfeld center
\mathcal{Z}(G)
is a three-dimensional topological field theory whose operators are line operators called anyons. Formally
\mathcal{Z}(G)
is the category of finite-dimensional complex representations of a certain Hopf algebra
D(G)
, the Drinfeld double, and the anyons discussed below are the objects in this category, but for our purposes it will suffice to simply quote some results
[
71
,
72
,
73
]
. The anyons (i.e. the simple objects) are labeled by a conjugacy class
[a]=\{gag^{-1}|g\in G\}
and an irreducible representation
\pi:Z(a)\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}(V_{\pi})
of
Z(a)=\{g\in G|ag=ga\}
, the centralizer of some representative
a
of the conjugacy class
[a]
. The corresponding anyon will be labeled
([a],\pi)
. To ensure uniqueness, we will pick a fixed representative
a
for each conjugacy class, and for some formulae below we also need to pick a conjugating element
\kappa_{b}
for every
b\in[a]
, that is an element
\kappa_{b}
such that
b=\kappa_{b}a\kappa_{b}^{-1}
, with the convention that for the distinguished representative
a
we have
\kappa_{a}=1
.

The
dimension

d_{([a],\pi)}
and
topological spin

s_{([a],\pi)}
are given by

d_{([a],\pi)}=\left|[a]\right|\,d_{\pi},\qquad s_{([a],\pi)}=\frac{\chi_{\pi}(a)}{d_{\pi}},

(2.1)

where
|[a]|
is the number of elements in the conjugacy class
[a]
,
\chi_{\pi}
is the character of the representation
\pi
, i.e.
\chi_{\pi}(g)=\operatorname{Tr}_{V_{\pi}}(\pi(g))
, and
d_{\pi}=\chi_{\pi}(1)
is the dimension of the representation
\pi
. Note that
d_{\pi}
here is a positive integer, and
s_{([a],\pi)}
is a phase (this follows since
a
is in the center of
Z(a)
and so by Schur’s lemma
\pi(a)
is simply a phase times the identity operator). Anyons for which
s_{([a],\pi)}=1
are called
bosons
, those with
s_{([a],\pi)}=-1
are
fermions

We have explicit formulae
[
71
,
72
]
for the S-matrix between anyons,

S_{([a],\pi),([a^{\prime}],\pi^{\prime})}=\frac{1}{|Z(a)||Z(a^{\prime})|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h\in G\\
ha^{\prime}h^{-1}\in Z(a)\end{subarray}}\chi_{\pi}(h(a^{\prime})^{-1}h^{-1})\chi_{\pi^{\prime}}(h^{-1}a^{-1}h),

(2.2)

and the fusion coefficients for fusion of anyons (Verlinde formula)

N_{([a_{1}],\pi_{1}),([a_{2}],\pi_{2})}^{([a_{3}],\pi_{3})}=\sum_{([a_{4}],\pi_{4})}\frac{S_{([a_{1}],\pi_{1}),([a_{4}],\pi_{4})}S_{([a_{2}],\pi_{2}),([a_{4}],\pi_{4})}\overline{S_{([a_{3}],\pi_{3}),([a_{4}],\pi_{4})}}}{S_{([e],\mathbf{1}),([a_{4}],\pi_{4})}}.

(2.3)

We can often compute the fusion coefficients more efficiently using

N_{([a_{1}],\pi_{1}),([a_{2}],\pi_{2})}^{([a_{3}],\pi_{3})}=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{1}\in[a_{1}],\ b_{2}\in[a_{2}],\\
b_{1}b_{2}\in[a_{3}]\end{subarray}}\sum_{g\in Z(b_{1})\cap Z(b_{2})}\overline{\chi_{\pi_{3}}(\kappa_{b_{1}b_{2}}^{-1}g\kappa_{b_{1}b_{2}})}\chi_{\pi_{1}}(\kappa_{b_{1}}^{-1}g\kappa_{b_{1}})\chi_{\pi_{2}}(\kappa_{b_{2}}^{-1}g\kappa_{b_{2}}),

(2.4)

which follows from orthogonality of characters of the underlying Hopf algebra representations.

A condensable algebra is a formal linear combination of anyons,
A=\sum n_{([a],\pi)}\,([a],\pi)
, with
n_{([a],\pi)}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
, satisfying several conditions
[
74
]
. Of these, the ones relevant for us are that

1.

n_{([e],\mathbf{1})}=1
.

2.

If
n_{([a],\pi)}\neq 0
, then
s_{([a],\pi)}=1
, i.e. only bosons appear.

3.

0\leq n_{([a],\pi)}\leq d_{([a],\pi)}=|[a]|d_{\pi},

(2.5)

4.

n_{([a],\pi)}n_{([a^{\prime}],\pi^{\prime})}\leq\sum_{([a^{\prime\prime}],\pi^{\prime\prime})}N_{([a],\pi),([a^{\prime}],\pi^{\prime})}^{([a^{\prime\prime}],\pi^{\prime\prime})}n_{([a^{\prime\prime}],\pi^{\prime\prime})}.

(2.6)

5.

If
d_{A}=\sum n_{([a],\pi)}d_{([a],\pi)}=|G|
, then

is called a Lagrangian algebra and it should be S-transformation invariant,

n_{([a],\pi)}=\sum_{([a^{\prime}],\pi^{\prime})}S_{([a],\pi),([a^{\prime}],\pi^{\prime})}n_{([a^{\prime}],\pi^{\prime})}.

(2.7)

If
d_{A}<|G|
, then for every
([a],\pi)
, the quantities

\zeta_{([a],\pi)}:=\frac{\left|G\right|}{d_{A}}\sum_{([a^{\prime}],\pi^{\prime})}S_{([a],\pi),([a^{\prime}],\pi^{\prime})}n_{([a^{\prime}],\pi^{\prime})}

(2.8)

must be cyclotomic integers, meaning they can be written as integer linear combinations of
N

N
. A particularly useful fact is that a rational number is a cyclotomic integer if and only if it is actually an integer.

There are three algebras that are always present, namely

In the SymTFT construction we put the TFT on a two-dimensional manifold cross an interval, with topological boundary conditions (corresponding to a choice of Lagrangian algebra) on one of the boundaries (the

We can check that the boundary variation of the action cancels under Lagrangian algebras specified above.

\displaystyle\Delta S_{Q_{8}}=\int\left(\vphantom{\frac{1}{2}}\Delta\hat{a}\delta a+\hat{a}\delta\Delta a+\Delta\hat{b}\delta b+\hat{b}\delta\Delta b+\Delta\hat{c}\delta c+\hat{c}\delta\Delta c+\Delta abc+a\Delta bc+ab\Delta c\right.

\displaystyle\Delta S_{Q_{8}}=\int\left(\vphantom{\frac{1}{2}}\Delta\hat{a}\delta a+\Delta\hat{b}\delta b+\Delta\hat{c}\delta c-\delta\hat{a}\Delta a-\delta\hat{b}\Delta b-\delta\hat{c}\Delta c+\Delta abc+a\Delta bc+ab\Delta c\right.

5
Conclusion and Outlook

In this note, we have analyzed the SymTFT for certain non-invertible symmetries. In the examples we have discussed, we have an explicit lagrangian description of the SymTFT. We have also reviewed the tools required to derive the list of condensable algebras of the Drinfeld center. Moreover, we have exemplified this with a detailed derivation of the condensable algebras of the Drinfeld center of
Q_{8}
.

We have discussed the Symmetry TFT for
\text{Rep}(D_{4})
and
\text{Rep}(Q_{8})
, beyond these, there is another candidate of the
\text{TY}({\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
family –
\text{Rep}(\mathcal{H}_{8})
, which differs from the
\text{Rep}(D_{4})
and
\text{Rep}(Q_{8})
via the choice of associators and F-symbols
[
100
]
. The next immediate task would be to derive the full list of condensable algebras and understand the different gaugings of
\text{Rep}(\mathcal{H}_{8})
from the SymTFT, connecting it with the results of
[
87
,
101
]
. This is very interesting because there is a symmetry web associated with
\text{Rep}(\mathcal{H}_{8})
in the space of
c=1
theories.

We have demonstrated that how we can associate the story of anomaly resolution by extending the symmetry is intimately related with the igSPTs and idea of missing charges. We have identified the fusion categorical short exact sequences associated with
D_{4}
and
Q_{8}
. One of the obvious future directions would be to identify the igSPT phases for
\text{Rep}(\mathcal{H}_{8})
. This would allow us to discover more fusion categorical anomaly resolutions.

Finally, the story of anomaly resolution and trivially acting symmetries fits nicely in the framework of renormalization group flows. The traditional story of anomaly resolution works the following way: Any IR theory with an anomalous 0-form symmetry
\mathcal{G}^{\omega}_{IR}
can be embedded into a larger UV symmetry group
\mathcal{G}_{UV}
without that anomaly. This defines a homomorphism,
\Phi:~\mathcal{G}_{UV}\longrightarrow\mathcal{G}^{\omega}_{IR}
. The UV symmetry group has a part that acts trivially in the IR. It can be detected by the nontrivial kernel of homomorphism,

\mathcal{G}_{triv}=\text{ker}(\Phi)

We summarize this with the following short exact sequence:

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{G}_{triv}\longrightarrow\mathcal{G}_{UV}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Phi}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathcal{G}^{\omega}_{IR}}\longrightarrow 0

where we can say that the pullback of the anomaly to
\mathcal{G}_{UV}
is trivial. It would be interesting to study the examples with trivially acting symmetries discussed in the text and construct models where the full symmetry acts effectively in the UV but where the operators charged under
\mathcal{G}_{triv}
go away under renormalization group flow.

More recently, this whole picture has been extended to include emergent symmetries in
[
95
]
. The anomaly of an UV symmetry is trivialized in the IR in the presence of an emergent 1-form symmetry. It would be interesting to incorporate trivially acting symmetries and emergent symmetries together and study the fate of the anomaly under the renormalization group flow. We leave this for future work.

The SymTFT action that captures

\displaystyle a\rightarrow a+\delta\alpha,\qquad\hat{a}\rightarrow\hat{a}+\delta\hat{\alpha}-\left(\beta c-\gamma b+\beta\delta\gamma\right),

(B.2)

\displaystyle b\rightarrow b+\delta\beta,\qquad\hat{b}\rightarrow\hat{b}+\delta\hat{\beta}-\left(\gamma a-\alpha c+\gamma\delta\alpha\right),

\displaystyle c\rightarrow c+\delta\gamma,\qquad\hat{c}\rightarrow\hat{c}+\delta\hat{\gamma}-\left(\alpha b-\beta a+\alpha\delta\beta\right).

These have already appeared in
[

,
80
]
. In this appendix, we check these explicitly.

\displaystyle S_{3d}=\pi\int_{3d}\left(\hat{a}\delta a+\delta\hat{\alpha}\delta a-\beta c\delta a+\gamma b\delta a-\beta\delta\gamma\delta a\right.

(B.3)

\displaystyle+\hat{b}\delta b+\delta\hat{\beta}\delta b-\gamma a\delta b+\alpha c\delta b-\gamma\delta\alpha\delta b

\displaystyle+\hat{c}\delta c+\delta\hat{\gamma}\delta c-\alpha b\delta c+\beta a\delta c-\alpha\delta\beta\delta c

\displaystyle+abc+\delta\alpha bc+a\delta\beta c+ab\delta\gamma+\delta\alpha\delta\beta c

\displaystyle\left.+a\delta\beta\delta\gamma+\delta\alpha b\delta\gamma+\delta\alpha\delta\beta\delta\gamma\right).

Some terms, like
\delta\hat{\alpha}\delta a
and
\delta\alpha\delta\beta\delta\gamma
are total derivatives by themselves and can be dropped. Next, we can see that the following terms combine to form total derivatives:

\displaystyle a\delta\beta c-\beta c\delta a+\beta a\delta c=\delta(c\beta a),

(B.4)

\displaystyle\delta\alpha bc-\alpha b\delta c+\alpha c\delta b=\delta(\alpha bc),

\displaystyle ab\delta\gamma-\gamma a\delta b+\gamma b\delta a=\delta(ab\gamma).

\displaystyle\beta\delta\gamma\delta a+a\delta\beta\delta\gamma=\delta(a\beta\delta\gamma),

(B.6)

\displaystyle\gamma\delta a\delta b+\delta\alpha b\delta\gamma=\delta(\delta\alpha b\gamma),

\displaystyle\alpha\delta\beta\delta c+\delta\alpha\delta\beta c=\delta(\alpha\delta\beta c),

which ensures the fact that the action is gauge invariant under the gauge transformations.

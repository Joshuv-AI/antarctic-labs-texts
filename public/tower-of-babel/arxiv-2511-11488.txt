Stability conditions of the N N -model with a waiting time dependent threshold on the diagonal
Sanne van Kempen, Elene Anton, Fiona Sloothaak

Abstract
We consider the N N -model queueing system with a waiting time dependent threshold on the diagonal: the service discipline is First–Come–First–Served, but type-1 jobs can only be served by server 2 if their waiting time exceeds a deterministic threshold. We prove the necessary and sufficient stability conditions for this model—an intuitive result that has not been established in literature up to this point. Our proof relies on coupling the queue length process to a carefully constructed upper (and lower) bound system, and establishing stochastic dominance for the queue length process.

1.1
Literature overview

The literature on skill-based queueing systems is extensive, although service disciplines with deterministic waiting time thresholds are not considered often.
Broadly speaking, the literature can be categorized as follows.

•

The service discipline is fully determined by the number of jobs in the system and, in some cases, their arrival order.
For these disciplines, the queue length process is a countable Markov process and therefore methods for countable state space Markov processes can be applied.
For example, the authors of
[
1
,
11
,
2
]
exploit a carefully chosen representation of the state space to derive a product-form expression for the stationary distribution of the queue length process of various skill-based queueing systems with
FCFS
service.
In our model however, the queue length process is not Markovian since the service discipline depends on the waiting time of the
FIL
type-1 job. Hence, such methods cannot be applied directly.

•

A discretized Markov chain is used as approximation of the continuous waiting time process.
For the
N
-model with waiting time threshold on the diagonal,
the authors of
[
9
,
10
]
apply the Erlang Approximation method to discretize the waiting time of the
FIL
job and find the approximated stationary distribution of the queue length process by solving a set of balance equations.

•

Studies of systems with only one job type and/or a single server.
The authors of
[
3
]
consider a queueing system with a single job type and two servers with waiting time dependent overflow to the secondary server.
The authors derive the steady-state waiting-time distribution of jobs using level-crossing equations.
This method does not easily extend to models with more than one job type, due to dependences between waiting time processes of different job types.

Since the techniques in the aforementioned works do not naturally extend to the
N
-model with a deterministic waiting time threshold on the diagonal, we develop an alternative approach based on coupling arguments to establish stability conditions.

We consider a continuous-time queueing system with two job types and two servers.

4
Possible extensions of our work

We have shown that the stability region of the
N
-model is not affected by the deterministic waiting time dependent threshold on the diagonal.
Whether the same result holds for skill-based queues with arbitrary queues, servers, and compatibility lines remains open.
Our proof relies on inequality (
8
) which implies that the second server is working in the
OR
system only if it is working at the same time in the
UB
system as well.
A similar property should hold in queueing systems with a certain ‘hierarchical’ structure, e.g. with
n\in\mathbb{N}
queues,
n
servers, and connectivity lines
(i,i)
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
without thresholds, and lines
(i,n)
with waiting time dependent thresholds
T_{i}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.

However, this property does not necessarily hold for general skill-based queues.
For example, it fails for the so-called
X
-model with lines
(i,j)

\forall i,j\in\{1,2\}
and waiting time dependent thresholds on both diagonals.
In this case, type-2 arrivals would also be delayed in the corresponding
UB
system.
In fact, stochastic dominance of the queue length processes does not even hold for the
X
-model, as illustrated by the example in Table
1

Hence, to prove stability conditions for general skill-based queues with arbitrary topologies and thresholds, a fundamentally different approach appears to be necessary.

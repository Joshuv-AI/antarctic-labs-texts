Prognostic significance of early ACTH levels on neurological recovery in patients with aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage: a prospective cohort study
Authors: Ghaith Saleh R Aljboor, Aoun Tulemat, Grace HE Tan, Nina Carlos- De Clercq, Mugurel Petrinel Rădoi, Toader Corneliu, Toma Marius Papacocea
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground/ObjectivesAneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage (aSAH) triggers a marked systemic stress response and may be accompanied by hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal (HPA) axis dysregulation. Adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH) may reflect the acute neuroendocrine response to injury and could be associated with early functional status. This study aimed to describe the frequency of acute-phase ACTH abnormalities and evaluate the association between early ACTH levels and functional outcomes at hospital discharge in patients with aSAH.MethodsIn this prospective cohort study, 20 consecutive aSAH patients admitted within 48 hours of symptom onset were enrolled. Plasma ACTH levels were obtained at random time points within the first 48 hours of admission (predefined cutoff ≤7 days; no patient exceeded 48 hours), prior to definitive aneurysm treatment. Blood samples were obtained within 24 hours of admission and prior to surgical intervention. Sampling was targeted between 08:00 and 10:00 to minimize circadian variation; however, minor deviations were unavoidable in the acute neurocritical care setting. Functional outcomes were assessed at discharge using the Glasgow Outcome Score (GOS) and Modified Rankin Scale (MRS). Associations were evaluated using Spearman correlation, non-parametric group comparisons, and exploratory multivariable regression analyses adjusting for key severity variables.ResultsUsing institutional laboratory reference ranges, 12/20 patients (60%) had ACTH values below the reference range, 7/20 (35%) were within range, and 1/20 (5%) had elevated ACTH. ACTH levels were significantly associated with discharge outcomes (mRS: ρ = +0.67, p = 0.0014; GOS: ρ = −0.59, p = 0.0063). ACTH distributions differed between favourable (mRS ≤ 2) and unfavourable (mRS > 2) outcome groups (p = 0.014). In exploratory multivariable analysis, ACTH showed a consistent association with outcome but did not remain statistically significant after adjustment.ConclusionsEarly ACTH levels obtained during the acute hospitalization were associated with short-term functional outcomes at discharge after aSAH. Given the random sampling design, the stress-sensitive nature of ACTH, and the absence of paired cortisol measurements, these findings should be interpreted as reflecting ACTH dysregulation rather than definitive endocrine deficiency. Larger studies with standardized sampling and longer-term follow-up are warranted.Keywords: Aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage, ACTH, Hypopituitarism, Biomarker, HPA axis, Neuroendocrinology, Prognosis

Introduction

Aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage (asah) remains one of the most devastating forms of stroke, with high mortality and morbidity. global epidemiological data indicate over 700,000 new sah cases annually, with projections suggesting a significant increase in the coming decades [1, 2]. the clinical challenge extends beyond acute survival to accurate prognostication, which is crucial for guiding treatment intensity, allocating critical care resources, and providing realistic family counselling.

Current prognostic tools, including clinical grading scales like the world federation of neurosurgical societies (wfns) and hunt-hess, and radiological assessments like the fisher scale, have limited predictive accuracy for long-term functional outcomes [3]. This underscores the need for novel, biologically grounded biomarkers that can objectively quantify injury severity and predict recovery potential during the acute phase.

Additionally, patient prognosis was evaluated at admission and discharge using the glasgow outcome score (gos) to classify overall outcomes following brain injury [4], the modified rankin scale (mrs) to gauge disability or dependence in daily activities post-stroke [5], and the glasgow coma scale (gcs) to determine consciousness levels in individuals with acute brain injury [6]. These assessment tools are commonly utilized in clinical research and practice due to their reliability and simplicity, and their validity is backed by substantial evidence in medical literature [4–6].

At the molecular level, asah triggers a cascade of pathophysiological events, including a profound neuroendocrine stress response mediated by the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (hpa) axis. upon aneurysmal rupture, hypothalamic corticotropin-releasing hormone (crh) stimulates the anterior pituitary to release adrenocorticotropic hormone (acth) into the bloodstream [7]. Acth, in turn, promotes glucocorticoid synthesis and release from the adrenal cortex, primarily cortisol. this neuroendocrine cascade is a critical adaptive response, aiming to maintain cerebral perfusion pressure via catecholamine stimulation and modulate the inflammatory response following brain injury [8, 9].

The integrity of the hpa axis is, however, vulnerable to the initial mechanical insult, elevated intracranial pressure, and subsequent complications like vasospasm and ischemia that characterize asah [10]. This can lead to a biphasic response: an initial hyperactivation followed by potential suppression due to direct pituitary-hypothalamic damage [11]. Consequently, acth levels in the acute phase may reflect a complex interplay between the magnitude of the stress response and the degree of pituitary dysfunction. While chronic hypopituitarism affects approximately 30% of asah survivors [12], the patterns and prognostic significance of acute-phase acth dysregulation are less well characterized.

Unlike non-specific inflammatory markers (e.g., c-reactive protein), acth provides a more specific window into central nervous system pathology and the integrated stress response. Previous studies have suggested associations between abnormal acth levels and outcomes after asah [4, 13–15], but prospective studies with standardized protocols are scarce.

This prospective cohort study was designed to investigate the prognostic utility of early acth measurement in asah. We hypothesized that dysregulated acth levels in the acute phase, reflecting either excessive stress response or incipient pituitary failure, would be strongly correlated with short-term functional outcomes. Elucidating this relationship contributes to the molecular understanding of asah pathophysiology and supports the development of biomarker-enhanced prognostic models.

Results

ACTH distribution and prevalence of dysfunction

The mean plasma ACTH level was 13.9 pg/mL, with a wide range from 1.5 to 117 pg/mL, indicating substantial inter-individual variability [Table1. C]. Applying established reference ranges, acute pituitary dysfunction was prevalent: 12 patients (60%) had ACTH deficiency (<7.2 pg/mL), 7 patients (35%) had levels within the normal range (7.2–63.3 pg/mL), and 1 patient (5%) had markedly elevated ACTH (>63.3 pg/mL).

Correlation between ACTH levels and functional outcomes

Spearman correlation analysis revealed statistically significant relationships between early ACTH levels and functional outcomes at discharge [Table 2]. There was a strong positive correlation between higher ACTH levels and worse functional status on the Modified Rankin Scale (MRS) (ρ = +0.67, p = 0.0014). Conversely, a significant negative correlation was observed between ACTH and the Glasgow Outcome Scale (GOS) (ρ = -0.59, p = 0.0063), confirming that elevated ACTH was associated with poorer neurological recovery.

Group comparisons by outcome category

When outcomes were dichotomized, non-parametric group comparisons reinforced the correlation findings. For MRS-defined outcomes, the distribution of ACTH levels was significantly different between patients with good (MRS ≤ 2) and poor (MRS > 2) outcomes (Mann-Whitney U test, p = 0.014). Exploratory multivariable analysis

To explore whether ACTH was independently associated with functional outcome, an exploratory multivariable logistic regression model was constructed with poor outcome at discharge (MRS > 2) as the dependent variable. Independent variables included ACTH level (continuous), Hunt–Hess grade, and Fisher classification (entered as a categorical variable).

In this exploratory model, higher ACTH levels were associated with increased odds of poor outcome; however, this association did not reach statistical significance after adjustment for clinical and radiological severity (odds ratio per pg/mL increase in ACTH ≈ 1.16, p = 0.17) [Table 3]. Neither Hunt–Hess grade nor Fisher classification independently predicted outcome in this small cohort. The overall model demonstrated good explanatory capacity (likelihood ratio p = 0.005). Given the limited sample size, these findings should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating.

Discussion

This prospective study demonstrates that a single measurement of plasma ACTH during the acute phase of aSAH provides significant prognostic information regarding short-term functional outcomes. The strong correlations observed (ρ = 0.59-0.67) suggest that HPA axis dysregulation is a central feature of the early molecular response to aSAH and is intimately linked to the severity of neurological injury.

Molecular and pathophysiological implications

The HPA axis is the key neuroendocrine stress system. Elevated ACTH correlates with poor outcomes, reflecting a stress response proportional to injury severity, while extremely high ACTH may indicate a maladaptive response in catastrophic brain injury. Conversely, the high rate of ACTH deficiency (60%) suggests early pituitary-hypothalamic damage, possibly from vascular compromise, microhemorrhages, or ischemia, impairing adequate stress responses. This creates a U-shaped relationship, where both ACTH excess and deficiency predict poor prognosis, reflecting distinct molecular injury patterns [10-12].

Clinical utility and comparison with literature

The prognostic performance of ACTH in our cohort is comparable to, or even exceeds, that of established clinical grading scales. The widespread availability and standardization of ACTH immunoassays make it a feasible and objective biomarker for implementation in clinical practice. Our findings are consistent with previous work linking HPA axis dynamics to aSAH outcomes [7, 13] but strengthen the evidence through a prospective design with standardized pre-treatment sampling.

The stronger statistical association with MRS compared to GOS suggests that MRS, with its focus on functional independence, may be more sensitive to the systemic and neurological consequences of HPA axis dysfunction. This has implications for the choice of endpoint in future biomarker studies.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. The small sample size limits statistical power and generalizability and increases the risk of both false-positive and missed associations; therefore, all findings should be considered exploratory and hypothesis-generating. As an observational study, causality cannot be inferred.

ACTH assessment was based on single basal measurements, without concurrent cortisol evaluation or dynamic testing, limiting interpretation of adrenal function in acute critical illness. Variability in sampling timing and stress-related hormonal alterations may have resulted in misclassification of true HPA axis dysfunction. Potential confounders such as cerebral vasospasm, seizures, and steroid exposure were not fully incorporated into the analysis.

Finally, functional outcomes were assessed at hospital discharge, which does not reflect long-term prognosis and may be influenced by non-neurological factors.

Future directions

These promising results warrant validation in larger, multicentre cohorts. Future research should integrate serial ACTH and cortisol measurements with other molecular biomarkers (e.g., inflammatory cytokines, markers of neuronal injury) to build multi-parametric prognostic models, potentially using machine learning approaches. Mechanistic studies using advanced magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) could correlate ACTH levels with structural integrity of the hypothalamus and pituitary gland. Ultimately, the goal is to translate this prognostic information into therapeutic action; clinical trials are needed to investigate whether early hormonal replacement or modulation of the stress response in selected patients can improve outcomes.

Materials and methods

Study design and population

This prospective observational cohort study was conducted at the National Institute of Neurology and Neurovascular Diseases, Bucharest, Romania, between October 2024 and March 2025. The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee (approval number: 58/022024), and written informed consent was obtained from all participants or their legal representatives.

Consecutive adult patients (≥18 years) admitted with a confirmed diagnosis of aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage (aSAH) on computed tomography (CT) and/or digital subtraction angiography (DSA) within 48 hours of symptom onset were screened for inclusion. Eligible patients were required to have undergone a hormonal evaluation during the acute hospitalization and to have a documented functional outcome assessment at hospital discharge.

Exclusion criteria included: pre-existing pituitary or endocrine disorders; prior pituitary or intracranial surgery; traumatic subarachnoid hemorrhage; systemic conditions known to significantly confound hormonal analysis (e.g., sepsis); chronic use of medications affecting hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal axis function (e.g., corticosteroids or antidepressants); incomplete clinical or laboratory data; or death within the first 48 hours of admission.

Clinical and radiological assessment

Upon admission, neurological severity was assessed using the Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) and the Hunt–Hess grading system. Radiological severity was evaluated on the initial CT scan using the original Fisher classification, which was analyzed as a categorical variable. Fisher group distributions are presented descriptively and were not treated as continuous measures.

Biochemical analysis

Plasma ACTH concentrations were measured at non-fixed time points within the first 48 hours following admission (predefined cutoff ≤7 days; all samples collected within 48 hours), prior to definitive aneurysm management. Blood sampling was performed within 24 hours of admission and before surgical intervention, with efforts made to obtain samples between 08:00 and 10:00 to limit circadian variability; however, minor deviations were unavoidable in the acute neurocritical care environment. Samples were collected by venipuncture into pre-chilled EDTA tubes, placed immediately on ice, and centrifuged at 4°C within 30 minutes. Plasma was separated and stored at −80°C until batch analysis. Plasma ACTH concentrations were measured using a commercially available chemiluminescent immunoassay, according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The intra- and inter-assay coefficients of variation were <10%.

Based on institutional laboratory reference ranges, ACTH values were categorized as below the reference range (<7.2 pg/mL), within the reference range (7.2–63.3 pg/mL), or above the reference range (>63.3 pg/mL). Given the acute critical illness setting, these categories were interpreted as reflecting ACTH abnormalities or dysregulation rather than definitive endocrine deficiency states. Concurrent cortisol measurements were not available.

Outcome measures

Outcome Measures

Functional outcomes were assessed at hospital discharge by trained neurologists who were blinded to ACTH results, using the Glasgow Outcome Score (GOS) and the modified Rankin Scale (MRS). For statistical analysis, outcomes were dichotomized as follows:

- GOS: good outcome (≥4) vs. poor outcome (<4)

- GOS: good outcome (≥4) vs. poor outcome (<4)

- MRS: good outcome (≤2) vs. poor outcome (>2)

- MRS: good outcome (≤2) vs. poor outcome (>2)

Statistical analysis

Data distribution was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Due to non-normal distribution of ACTH values, non-parametric statistical methods were applied. Continuous variables are reported as medians with interquartile ranges (IQR), and categorical variables as frequencies with percentages.

Associations between ACTH levels and functional outcome scores were evaluated using Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient (ρ). Group comparisons of ACTH levels between dichotomized outcome categories were performed using the Mann–Whitney U test.

To explore whether ACTH was independently associated with outcome, an exploratory multivariable logistic regression analysis was conducted, including ACTH and selected clinical and radiological severity variables. Given the limited sample size, these analyses were considered hypothesis-generating.

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS Statistics version 27.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). A two-tailed p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Conclusions

This study provides compelling evidence that early measurement of ACTH, a key hormone in the stress response, holds significant prognostic value in aSAH. The strong correlations with functional outcomes at discharge underscore the critical role of HPA axis dysregulation in the acute phase of brain injury. The high prevalence of ACTH deficiency points to a substantial burden of acute pituitary dysfunction that is currently under-recognized. Integrating this readily available biomarker into initial patient assessment could enhance prognostication, guide resource allocation, and identify patients for endocrine evaluation. Future research should focus on validating these findings in larger cohorts, elucidating the underlying molecular mechanisms, and exploring the potential for therapeutic interventions targeting the neuroendocrine response to improve patient outcomes.

Thermodynamics of Reissner-nordstorm black bounce black hole
Feba C Joy, R Tharanath

Abstract
Our study focuses on the thermodynamics of Reissner-nordstorm black bounce black hole,we have determined the thermodynamic parameters including entropy,mass,temperature,heat capacity and free energies and investigates how those parameters are related to entropy and for some insights we additionally focused on the P V isotherm and the logarithmic correction to the entropy. Key words : Black bounce , logarithmic correction , Conformal Field Theory , P-V isotherm



I
Introduction

Black holes are regions of spacetime where nothing , not even light can escape,because of the strong gravitational pull. In 1930s it was Oppenheimer,Synder and Dutt with the help of Einstein’s general relativity studied the collapse of huge stars, they came to a conclusion that when a star collapses,an event horizon is formed which is a one way membrane in which nothing can escape,John Wheeler in 1969 named this region as a ”black hole”,which is the outcome of the gravitational collapse that the OSD model describes.[1,2].

The concept of Black Bounce Black Holes, introduced by Simpson and Visser,an alternative to the traditional black holes.Black bounce black holes features a minimal surfaces instead of having a singularity point at the center,which results in a regular black hole without infinite densities.But when comparing to regular black holes these may show certain similarities such as gravitational effects ,shadows.Depending on the parameters black bounce spacetime helps in describing the exotic objects like traversable wormholes.Thus these acts as a mimickers to the classical black holes [3].The physical origin of Black Bounce black holes may be linked to the absorption of particles by black holes, offering new opportunities for understanding Black Bounce spacetime. This has been the subject of recent studies[4].A few years ago,a black bounce Schwarzschild space time was introduced, this spacetime was proposed as a model that represents either a regular black hole or a traversable wormhole, depending on its specific parameters, rather than a conventional black hole[5,6,7,8,9,10].A more recent development introduced a charged version,known as the Reissner-Nordström Simpson-Visser black hole[11].

This model is regular and incorporates standard Maxwell electromagnetism alongside an anisotropic fluid.Like the uncharged version, it can imitate traditional black holes while also suggesting the possibility of more exotic structures[12].

These Black Bounce solutions provide valuable insights into alternative black hole models, particularly in the realm of quantum gravity, where avoiding singularities is a key objective.Though they are mathematically regular, they still replicate the key observable features of classical black holes, making them important candidates for further astrophysical study.The S V space time or the Schwarzschild black bounce space time was a solution for the singularity issue where the coordinate r transforms to

\sqrt{r^{2}+l^{2}}

,where l is the black hole length parameter.For regular black holes l is less than two ,in case of traversable wormholes

l>2

and for a black hole with null throat

l=2

.[12]
There are numerous black bounce models beyond Simpson and Visser solution.Many studies have been focused on analyzing the properties of these solutions[13,14,15].Very recently the authors of Ref[16] reviews about the Simpson Visser space time surrounded by the string cloud.This was earlier introduced by Rodrigues et al.[17],here he introduces a black hole mimicker surrounded by a string cloud indicating that the presence of the string cloud is what makes the black bounce spacetime regular.

Gravitational waves from the collision of two black holes were first detected by LIGO and Virgo in 2015, confirming both the existence of black holes and Einstein’s theory of general relativity. In 2021, LIGO and Virgo also observed the first merger between a black hole and a neutron star.The Event Horizon Telescope (EHT) provided the first image of a black hole’s shadow in the galaxy M87 in 2019, and in 2022, it released an image of Sagittarius

A^{*}

, the black hole at the center of the Milky Way galaxy[18,19,20]. The shadow of the black hole in M87 is due to gravitational lensing[21]. Recently, the phenomenon of gravitational lensing in the strong deflection limit has been studied in the context of black bounce black holes.[22,23]

Black hole thermodynamics combines the general relativity with quantum mechanics and classical thermodynamics.In 1970s the discoveries shows that the black holes also exhibit the thermodynamic properties such as temperature,entropy,free energies,heat capacity.The general black hole metrics help us in deriving the thermodynamic properties[24]. With this in mind, we aim to explore the thermodynamic properties of the charged Reissner-Nordstorm Black Bounce black hole.

III
Logarithmic Correction in Area Law

The statistical fluctuations around the thermal

equilibrium of black holes results in the logarithmic correction to the entropy [26],are known to be directly connected to the anomalies for a gravity theory with quantum correction.
As we know that the logarithmic corrections to entropy arise from the contributions of thermal heat capacity (THC) and conformal field theory (CFT)[26].The resulting entropy relations are expressed as follows:

S_{THC}=S-\frac{1}{2}\ln|CT^{2}|

(19)

S_{CFT}=S-\frac{1}{2}\ln|ST^{2}|

(20)

From equations (19) and (20)we get,

S_{THC}=\pi r^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\ln|A|

(21)

where,

A=\left(\frac{2(S+l^{2}\pi-Q^{2}\pi)}{(3Q^{2}+\frac{3S}{\pi}+3l^{2})(\frac{S}{\pi}+l^{2})-4(\frac{S}{\pi}+l^{2})^{3}}\right)

(22)

and

S_{CFT}=\pi r^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\ln|(B_{1}+B_{2})C|

(23)

where,

B_{1}=\frac{2\pi(2M^{2}-Q^{2}-l^{2})}{2}

(24)

and

B_{2}=\frac{\sqrt{4\pi M^{2}-2Q^{2}-2l^{2}-4\pi^{2}(Q^{2}-l^{2})-16M^{2}l^{2}}}{2}

(25)

C=\left(\frac{2(\frac{S}{\pi}+l^{2})-Q^{2}+\frac{S}{\pi}+l^{2}}{4\pi(\frac{S}{\pi}+l^{2})^{3/2}}\right)^{2}

(26)

Thus from equations (21) and (23) we have,

\frac{S_{THC}}{S_{CFT}}=\frac{\pi r^{2}-\frac{1}{2}lnA}{\pi r^{2}-\frac{1}{2}lnBC}

(27)

While analysing the above figure it is evident that for the lower r values the ratio is less than one and when r value is 1.196 the ratio becomes 1.Beyond this point, as the horizon radius increases, the ratio exceeds one. Thus in case of a black hole with lower r value the corresponding ratio suggests that the quantum effects dominate. The smaller the black hole, the more prone the region near the horizon is to quantum fluctuations. On the other hand, for the black holes with higher r value, thermal properties play a more significant role. As the black hole’s radius increases, it can store more energy and interact more extensively with its surroundings, leading to a greater thermal contribution to its entropy.Thus we came to a conclusion that the quantum effects tend to dominate at smaller scales, while thermal properties become more influential as the black hole grows in size.

Equation of State and Extended Phase Transition

In Black hole thermodynamics ,the equation of state is based on the first law of black hole thermodynamics,

it relates the thermodynamic quantities such as temperature volume and pressure as same as how the ideal gas law relates in classical thermodynamics[28].

Let the pressure P be,

P=-\frac{\Lambda}{8\pi}

(28)

where,
-\Lambda
=
\frac{3}{l^{2}}
[29],thus equation (27) becomes,

P=\frac{3}{8\pi l^{2}}

(29)

The equation for thermodynamic volume is,

V=\left(\frac{\partial M}{\partial P}\right)_{S,Q}

(30)

For a Black hole in massive gravity we have the thermodynamic volume as,

V=\frac{4}{3}\pi r^{3}

(31)

Now let the Enthalpy of a black hole be,

H=E+PV

(32)

and the total energy E is given by,

E=M-PV

(33)

from equation (31) it is clear that the enthalpy is itself the mass of a black hole i.e,

H(S,P)=M

(34)

Thus from equation (8) we get,

H(S,P)=\frac{Q^{2}+\frac{S}{\pi}+\frac{3}{8\pi P}}{2\sqrt{\frac{S}{\pi}+\frac{3}{8\pi P}}}

(35)

Now on comparing equation (6) with equation (30) we get,

S=\left(\frac{3}{4}V{\pi}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}

(36)

Thus the temperature of the Reissner nordstorm Black hole in terms of enthalpy and entropy is,

T=\left(\frac{\partial H}{\partial S}\right)_{P}

(37)

T=\frac{8PS+3-8\pi PQ^{2}}{32\pi^{2}P\left(\frac{S}{\pi}+\frac{3}{8\pi P}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}

(38)

The equation (38) indicates the equation of state of the Reissner nordstorm Black bounce black hole.

GUE correlators and large genus asymptotics
Jiayi Zhao

Abstract
. In this paper, we use a formula obtained in [ 5 ] to study certain asymptotic behaviors of GUE (Gaussian unitary ensemble) correlators. More precisely, we obtain large genus asymptotics of enumerations of ordinary graphs and ribbon graphs with 1 face.



1.
Introduction

Large genus asymptotics of psi-class intersection numbers on the moduli space of stable algebraic curves have been studied in the literature. Liu and Xu found and proved
[
9
]
certain large genus asymptotics and rationality properties. Delecroix, Goujard, Zograf and Zorich (DGZZ) conjectured
[
4
]
the large genus uniform leading asymptotics of psi-class intersection numbers, which was improved and proved by Aggarwal in
[
1
]
. Guo and Yang
[
7
]
gave a new proof of the above-mentioned Liu-Xu’s results and DGZZ’s conjecture through estimates of the matrix-resolvent formula given in
[
2
]
regarding the KdV hierarchy.

In this paper, using the matrix-resolvent formula for the GUE (Gaussian unitary ensemble) correlators in
[
5
]
(which are closely related to enumerations of ribbon graphs), we will study certain large genus asymptotic behaviors and certain rationality properties. Our method is similar to
[
7
]
. In particular, we will obtain large genus asymptotics of enumerations of ordinary graphs and ribbon graphs with 1 face.

The GUE correlators of observables
\mathrm{tr}M^{i},i=1,2,\cdots
, are defined by

(1)

\langle\text{tr}M^{i_{1}}\cdots\text{tr}M^{i_{n}}\rangle_{c}(N):=\frac{\int_{\mathcal{H}(N)}\text{tr}M^{i_{1}}\cdots\text{tr}M^{i_{n}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\text{tr}M^{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}M}{\int_{\mathcal{H}(N)}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\text{tr}M^{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}M},

where
\mathcal{H}(N)
denotes the space of
N\times N
Hermitian matrices. It is known from
[
5
]
that
the GUE partition function

(2)

\displaystyle Z_{N}(\textbf{s};\epsilon)=\frac{(2\pi)^{-N}\epsilon^{-1/12}}{\text{Vol}(N)}\int_{\mathcal{H}(N)}e^{-\text{tr}V(M)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}M,

where
\mathcal{H}(N)
is the space of
N\times N
Hermitian matrices and

(3)

\displaystyle V(M)=\frac{1}{2}M^{2}-\sum_{j\geq 1}s_{j}M^{j},\quad\textbf{s}=(s_{1},s_{2},\cdots),

is a tau-function of the Toda lattice hierarchy, with the time variables defined by
t_{j}=s_{j+1}/\epsilon,j=0,1,2,\cdots
.

For
n\geq 1
, denote the generating series for the
n
-point correlators by

(4)

\displaystyle C_{n}(N;\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n}):=\sum_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{n}=1}^{\infty}\frac{\langle\text{tr}M^{i_{1}}\cdots\text{tr}M^{i_{n}}\rangle_{c}(N)}{\lambda_{1}^{i_{1}+1}\cdots\lambda_{n}^{i_{n}+1}}.

Using the matrix resolvent method, Dubrovin and Yang
[
5
]
obtained an explicit formula for
C_{n}(N;\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})
,
which reads as follows

(5)

\displaystyle C_{1}(N;\lambda)=N\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{(2j-1)!!}{\lambda^{2j+1}}({{}_{2}F}_{1}(-j,-N;2;2)-j\cdot{{}_{2}F}_{1}(1-j,1-N;3;2)),

(6)

\displaystyle C_{n}(N;\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})=-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\frac{R_{N}(\lambda_{\sigma(1)})\cdots R_{N}(\lambda_{\sigma(n)})-\delta_{n,2}}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(\lambda_{\sigma(i)}-\lambda_{\sigma(i+1)})},\quad(n\geq 2),

where
S_{n}
denotes the symmetric group,
\sigma(n+1):=\sigma(1)
,
{{}_{2}F}_{1}(a,b;c;z)
is the
Gauss hypergeometric function

(7)

\displaystyle{{}_{2}}F_{1}(a,b;c;z)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a)_{j}(b)_{j}}{(c)_{j}}\frac{z^{j}}{j!}=1+\frac{ab}{c}\frac{z}{1!}+\frac{a(a+1)b(b+1)}{c(c+1)}\frac{z^{2}}{2!}+\cdots,

and
R_{N}(\lambda)
,
N\geq 1
denote the following matrix-valued series

(8)

\displaystyle R_{N}(\lambda):=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}+N\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2j-1)!!}{\lambda^{2j+2}}\begin{pmatrix}(2j+1)A_{N,j}&-\lambda B_{N+1,j}\\
\frac{\lambda}{N}B_{N,j}&-(2j+1)A_{N,j}\end{pmatrix},

where

(9)

\displaystyle A_{N,j}:={{}_{2}F}_{1}(-j,1-N;2;2),\quad B_{N,j}:={{}_{2}F}_{1}(-j,1-N;1;2).

Denote by
\mathrm{OG}_{g}(i_{1},\cdots,i_{n})
the weighted number of connected ordinary graphs of genus
g
with
n
vertices of valencies
i_{1},\cdots,i_{n}
. We know from Euler’s formula that

(10)

\displaystyle i_{1}+\cdots+i_{n}=2g+2n-2.

Then we have

(11)

\displaystyle\mathrm{OG}_{g}(i_{1},\cdots,i_{n})=\frac{1}{i_{1}!\cdots i_{n}!n!}\langle\text{tr}M^{i_{1}}\cdots\text{tr}M^{i_{n}}\rangle_{c}(1).

Introduce a normalization of the enumeration of ordinary graphs by

(12)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{OG}}_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{n}}(g):=\frac{i_{1}!\cdots i_{n}!n!}{(2g+2n-3)!!}\mathrm{OG}_{g}(i_{1},\cdots,i_{n}).

We will prove in Section
2
the following theorems for the asymptotic formulas and the rationality of the normalized numbers of ordinary graphs.

Theorem 1
.

For fixed
n\geq 1
and fixed integers
i_{1},\cdots,i_{n-1}\geq 1
, we have

(13)

\lim_{g\to+\infty}\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{OG}}_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{n-1},2g+2n-2-|i|}(g)=1,

where
|i|=i_{1}+\cdots+i_{n-1}
.

Theorem 2
.

For fixed
n\geq 1
and fixed integers
i_{1},\cdots,i_{n-1}\geq 1
, there exists a rational function
R^{\mathrm{OG}}(g;i_{1},\cdots,i_{n-1})
of
g
that may depend on
n,i_{1},\cdots,i_{n-1}
, such that

(14)

\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{OG}}_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{n-1},2g+2n-2-|i|}(g)=R^{\mathrm{OG}}(g;i_{1},\cdots,i_{n-1}).

Theorem
1
and Theorem
2
yield the following corollary.

Corollary 1
.

For fixed
n\geq 1
and fixed integers
i_{1},\cdots,i_{n-1}\geq 1
, we have the following convergent expansion:

(15)

\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{OG}}_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{n-1},2g+2n-2-|i|}(g)\sim\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{OG}}_{k}}{g^{k}},\quad(g\to\infty),

where
\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{OG}}_{0}=1
and
\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{OG}}_{k}=\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{OG}}_{k}(n,i_{1},\cdots,i_{n-1}),k\geq 1
are constants.

Denote by
a_{g}(i_{1},\cdots,i_{n})
the weighted number of connected ribbon graphs of genus
g
with
n
vertices of valencies
i_{1},\cdots,i_{n}
(see
[
5
]
). Then using Euler’s formula, we know that the number of faces of such a graph is
2-2g-n+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}i_{j}
. Now we focus on graphs with 1 face. We denote the number of ribbon graphs of this kind by
\mathrm{RG}_{g}(i_{1},\cdots,i_{n})
. Then Euler’s formula implies that

(16)

\displaystyle i_{1}+\cdots+i_{n}=4g+2n-2.

In fact,
\mathrm{RG}_{g}(i_{1},\cdots,i_{n})
is the first order coefficient of
N
in the polynomial
\frac{1}{n!}\langle\text{tr}M^{i_{1}}\cdots\text{tr}M^{i_{n}}\rangle_{c}(N)
(see
[
3
,
8
]
, cf.
[
5
]
), i.e.,

(17)

\displaystyle\langle\text{tr}M^{i_{1}}\cdots\text{tr}M^{i_{n}}\rangle_{c}=n!\mathrm{RG}_{g}(i_{1},\cdots,i_{n})N+O(N^{2}).

We introduce the normalized numbers of ribbon graphs as follows:

(18)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{RG}}_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{n}}(g):=\frac{n!}{2\cdot(4g+2n-5)!!}\mathrm{RG}_{g}(i_{1},\cdots,i_{n}).

We will prove in Section
3
the following two theorems.

Theorem 3
.

For fixed
n\geq 1
and fixed integers
i_{1},\cdots,i_{n-1}\geq 1
, we have

(19)

\lim_{g\to+\infty}\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{RG}}_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{n-1},4g+2n-2-|i|}(g)=1,

where
|i|=i_{1}+\cdots+i_{n-1}
.

Theorem 4
.

For fixed
n\geq 1
and fixed integers
i_{1},\cdots,i_{n-1}\geq 1
, there exists a rational function
R^{\mathrm{RG}}(g;i_{1},\cdots,i_{n-1})
of
g
that may depend on
n,i_{1},\cdots,i_{n-1}
, such that

(20)

\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{RG}}_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{n-1},4g+2n-2-|i|}(g)=R^{\mathrm{RG}}(g;i_{1},\cdots,i_{n-1}).

Theorem
3
and Theorem
4
yield the following corollary.

Corollary 2
.

For fixed
n\geq 1
and fixed integers
i_{1},\cdots,i_{n-1}\geq 1
, we have the following convergent expansion:

(21)

\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{RG}}_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{n-1},4g+2n-2-|i|}(g)\sim\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{RG}}_{k}}{g^{k}}\quad(g\to\infty),

where
\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{RG}}_{0}=1
and
\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{RG}}_{k}=\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{RG}}_{k}(n,i_{1},\cdots,i_{n-1}),k\geq 1
are constants.

2.
Asymptotic Formula on Enumeration of Ordinary Graphs

In this section, we prove Theorem
1
and Theorem
2
.

As in
[
7
]
, we consider the rational function

(22)

P(\sigma;\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})=\frac{1}{\prod_{q=1}^{n}(\lambda_{\sigma(q)}-\lambda_{\sigma(q+1)})}.

The formal Laurent series of the whole right-hand side of (
6
) is independent of the ordering. Thus, following
[
7
]
, we fix the choice of the expanding region to be
|\lambda_{1}|>\dots>|\lambda_{n}|
. We have the following lemma.

Lemma 1
.

For each
\sigma\in S_{n}
, the Laurent expansion of the rational function
P(\sigma;\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})
around
\infty
’s within the region
|\lambda_{1}|>\dots>|\lambda_{n}|
is given by

(23)

P(\sigma;\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})=(-1)^{m(\sigma)}\sum_{j_{1},\dots,j_{n}\geq 0}\prod_{q=1}^{n}\lambda_{\sigma(q)}^{J_{\sigma,q}(j_{q})-J_{\sigma,q-1}(j_{q-1})-1}.

Here,

(24)

m(\sigma):={\rm card}\left\{q\in\{1,\dots,n\}\mid\sigma(q+1)<\sigma(q)\right\},

and

(25)

J_{\sigma,q}(j):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-j-1,&\sigma(q)<\sigma(q+1),\\
j,&\sigma(q)>\sigma(q+1),\end{array}\right.

where the indices are considered cyclically modulo
n
.

For
n\geq 2
,
\underline{d}=(d_{1},\dots,d_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
,
\sigma\in S_{n}
, and
q=1,\dots,n
, we define
K_{\underline{d},\sigma,q}:\left(\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}\right)^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
by

(26)

\displaystyle K_{\underline{d},\sigma,q}(\underline{j}):=d_{\sigma(q)}+J_{\sigma,q}(j_{q})-J_{\sigma,q-1}(j_{q-1}),

where
\underline{j}=(j_{1},\dots,j_{n})\in\left(\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}\right)^{n}
, and
J_{\sigma,q}
are given in (
25
).

For
n\geq 2
,
1\leq r\leq n-1
being integers, we call a permutation
\sigma\in S_{n}
with
\sigma(n)=n
an
(r,n-r)
-permutation
, if

(27)

\displaystyle n=\sigma(n)>\sigma(1)>\dots>\sigma(n-r+1)=1,

(28)

\displaystyle 1=\sigma(n-r+1)<\sigma(n-r+2)<\cdots<\sigma(n)=n

(namely,
firstly decreasing then increasing
, often called
unimodal
permutations). The set of all permutations of this type is denoted by
S_{n,n}^{(r,n-r)}
. We also denote

S_{n,n}^{\{2\}}=\cup_{r=1}^{n-1}S_{n,n}^{(r,n-r)}
. The following combinatorial facts will be used later.

(29)

\displaystyle{\rm card}\,S_{n,n}^{(r,n-r)}=\binom{n-2}{r-1},

(30)

\displaystyle{\rm card}\,S_{n,n}^{\{2\}}=2^{n-2}.

For
k\geq 0
, define a series of matrices
L_{k}
by

(31)

L_{k}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},&k=0,\\
\begin{pmatrix}(k-1)!!&0\\
0&-(k-1)!!\end{pmatrix},&k\text{\,even\,and\,}k\geq 2,\\
\begin{pmatrix}0&-k!!\\
(k-2)!!&0\end{pmatrix},&k\text{\,odd and\,}k\geq 1.\end{array}\right.

Then, by definition,

(32)

R_{1}(\lambda)=\sum_{k\geq 0}L_{k}\lambda^{-k}.

For
k_{1},\cdots,k_{n}\geq 0
, we also denote by

(33)

l_{k_{1},\cdots,k_{n}}=\text{tr}(L_{k_{1}}\cdots L_{k_{n}}).

We make the convention that
l_{k_{1},\cdots,k_{n}}=0
if some of
k_{1},\cdots,k_{n}
are less than or equal to
-1
. Note that
l_{k_{1},\cdots,k_{n}}
is invariant under cyclic permutations of its indices.

With the above notations, we obtain an explicit formula on the normalized number of ordinary graphs by using Lemma
1
and expanding the right-hand side of (
6
), which is given by the following lemma.

Lemma 2
.

For
n\geq 2
,
g\geq 0
and
\underline{i}=(i_{1},\cdots,i_{n})\in(\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 1})^{n}
satisfying
i_{1}+\cdots+i_{n}=2g+2n-2
, the following formula holds true:

(34)

\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{OG}}_{\underline{i}}(g)=\frac{1}{(2g+2n-3)!!}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}(-1)^{m(\sigma)+1}\sum_{\underline{j}\in(\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0})^{n}}l_{K_{\underline{i},\sigma,1}(\underline{j}),\cdots,K_{\underline{i},\sigma,n}(\underline{j})}.

Due to our convention, for each
\sigma\in S_{n}
, the second sum on the right-hand side of (
34
) is a finite sum. Moreover, when
i_{1},\cdots,i_{n-1}
are fixed and
g
is sufficiently large, the number of terms in the sum is independent of
g
. Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1
and Theorem
2
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

For the case
n=1
, we know from (
5
) that
\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{OG}}_{2g}(g)=1
for every
g\geq 1
. So we consider the case
n\geq 2
. For fixed integers
i_{1},\cdots,i_{n-1}\geq 1
, let
i_{n}=2g+2n-2-|i|
. From the definition (
26
) of
K_{\underline{i},\sigma,q}
, for each
\sigma\in S_{n}
satisfying
\sigma(n)=n
, we have

(35)

\displaystyle K_{\underline{i},\sigma,n}\geq 2g+2n-2-|i|

(36)

\displaystyle K_{\underline{i},\sigma,1}+\cdots+K_{\underline{i},\sigma,n}=2g+2n-2.

For simplicity we write
\underline{K}=(K_{\underline{i},\sigma,1}(\underline{j}),\cdots,K_{\underline{i},\sigma,n}(\underline{j}))
. Then from the definition of
l_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{n}}
, (
35
) and (
36
), we have for
n\geq 1
:

(37)

\lim\limits_{g\to\infty}\frac{1}{(2g+2n-3)!!}l_{\underline{K}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&\underline{K}=(0,0,\cdots,0,0,2g+2n-2),\\
-1,&\underline{K}=(1,0,\cdots,0,0,2g+2n-3),\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Now we need to respectively count the number of solutions to the two equations

(38)

\displaystyle\underline{K}=(0,0,\cdots,0,0,2g+2n-2),

(39)

\displaystyle\underline{K}=(1,0,\cdots,0,0,2g+2n-3),

for
\underline{j}\in(\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0})^{n}
. It turns out that the number of solutions to (
38
) for
\underline{j}
is

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}i_{1},&\sigma\in S_{n,n}^{\{2\}},\\
0,&\sigma\not\in S_{n,n}^{\{2\}},\end{array}\right.

and the number of solutions to (
39
) for
\underline{j}
is

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}i_{1}-1,&\sigma\in S_{n,n}^{(1,n-1)},\\
i_{1},&\sigma\in S_{n,n}^{(r,n-r)}\text{ with }r\not=1,\\
0,&\sigma\not\in S_{n,n}^{\{2\}}.\end{array}\right.

Therefore, from (
37
), Lemma
2
and the above statement, we have

(40)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{g\to\infty}\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{OG}}_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{n-1},2g+2n-2-|i|}(g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{g\to\infty}\frac{1}{(2g+2n-3)!!}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}(-1)^{m(\sigma)+1}\sum_{\underline{j}\in(\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0})^{n}}l_{K_{\underline{i},\sigma,1}(\underline{j}),\cdots,K_{\underline{i},\sigma,n}(\underline{j})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{g\to\infty}\frac{1}{(2g+2n-3)!!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n,n}^{\{2\}}}(-1)^{m(\sigma)+1}\sum_{\underline{j}\in(\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0})^{n}}l_{K_{\underline{i},\sigma,1}(\underline{j}),\cdots,K_{\underline{i},\sigma,n}(\underline{j})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{g\to\infty}\frac{1}{(2g+2n-3)!!}\sum_{r=1}^{n-1}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n,n}^{(r,n-r)}}(-1)^{r+1}\sum_{\underline{j}\in(\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0})^{n}}l_{K_{\underline{i},\sigma,1}(\underline{j}),\cdots,K_{\underline{i},\sigma,n}(\underline{j})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{g\to\infty}\sum_{r=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{r+1}\binom{n-2}{r-1}(i_{1}-(i_{1}-\delta_{1,r}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1.

Here, we recall that since
i_{1},\cdots,i_{n-1}
are fixed, the number of the terms in the right-hand side of (
34
) is a constant independent of
g
for sufficiently large
g
. Then the theorem is proved.
∎

Now let us proceed to prove Theorem
2
.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

For
n=1
, the statement easily follows from (
5
). Now consider the case
n\geq 2
. For fixed integers
i_{1},\cdots,i_{n-1}
and
i_{n}=2g+2n-2-|i|
, using (
35
), (
36
) and (
33
), one can see that for each
\sigma\in S_{n}
,
\frac{1}{(2g+2n-3)!!}l_{\underline{K}}
is a rational function of
g
. Since the number of the terms in the right-hand side of (
34
) is independent of
g
for sufficiently large
g
, we conclude from (
34
) the existence of a rational function
R^{\mathrm{OG}}(g;i_{1},\cdots,i_{n-1})
such that
\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{OG}}_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{n}}(g)=R^{\mathrm{OG}}(g;i_{1},\cdots,i_{n-1})
.
∎

3.
Asymptotic Formula on Enumeration of Ribbon Graphs with 1 Face

In this section, we prove Theorem
3
and Theorem
4
.

Define a series of matrix-valued polynomials
B_{k}
for each
k\geq 0
by

(41)

B_{k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},&k=0,\\
\begin{pmatrix}N(k-1)!!\cdot\frac{1+(-1)^{(k-2)/2}}{k}&0\\
0&-N(k-1)!!\cdot\frac{1+(-1)^{(k-2)/2}}{k}\end{pmatrix},&k\text{\,even and\,}k\geq 2,\\
\begin{pmatrix}0&-N(k-2)!!\\
(k-2)!!\cdot(-1)^{(k-1)/2}&0\end{pmatrix},&k\,\text{odd and\,}k\geq 1.\end{array}\right.

For
k_{1},\cdots,k_{n}\geq 0
, we denote

(42)

b_{k_{1},\cdots,k_{n}}=[N^{1}]\text{tr}(B_{k_{1}}\cdots B_{k_{n}}).

We make the convention that
b_{k_{1},\cdots,k_{n}}=0
if some of
k_{1},\cdots,k_{n}
are less than or equal to
-1
. Note that
b_{k_{1},\cdots,k_{n}}
is invariant under cyclic permutations of its indices. With these definitions, we obtain an explicit formula for the normalized number of ribbon graphs by using Lemma
1
and expanding the right-hand side of (
6
), which is given by the following lemma.

Lemma 3
.

For
n\geq 2
,
g\geq 0
and
\underline{i}=(i_{1},\cdots,i_{n})\in(\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 1})^{n}
satisfying
i_{1}+\cdots+i_{n}=4g+2n-2
, the following formula holds true:

(43)

\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{RG}}_{\underline{i}}(g)=\frac{1}{2\cdot(4g+2n-5)!!}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}(-1)^{m(\sigma)+1}\sum_{\underline{j}\in(\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0})^{n}}b_{K_{\underline{i},\sigma,1}(\underline{j}),\cdots,K_{\underline{i},\sigma,n}(\underline{j})}.

By our convention, for each
\sigma\in S_{n}
the second sum on the right-hand side of (
43
) is a finite sum. Moreover, when
i_{1},\cdots,i_{n-1}
are fixed and
g
is sufficiently large, the number of terms in the sum is independent of
g
. Now we are ready to prove Theorem
3
and Theorem
4
.

Proof of Theorem
3
.

For the case
n=1
, we know from (
5
) that
\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{RG}}_{4g}(g)=1
for every
g\geq 1
. Now we consider the case
n\geq 2
. For fixed integers
i_{1},\cdots,i_{n-1}\geq 1
, let
i_{n}=4g+2n-2-|i|
. From the definition (
26
) of
K_{\underline{i},\sigma,q}
, for each
\sigma\in S_{n}
satisfying
\sigma(n)=n
, we have

(44)

\displaystyle K_{\underline{i},\sigma,n}\geq 4g+2n-2-|i|,

(45)

\displaystyle K_{\underline{i},\sigma,1}+\cdots+K_{\underline{i},\sigma,n}=4g+2n-2.

For simplicity we write
\underline{K}=(K_{\underline{i},\sigma,1}(\underline{j}),\cdots,K_{\underline{i},\sigma,n}(\underline{j}))
. Then from the definition of
b_{k_{1},\cdots,k_{n}}
, (
44
) and (
45
), we have for
n\geq 2
:

(46)

\lim\limits_{g\to\infty}\frac{1}{2\cdot(4g+2n-5)!!}b_{\underline{K}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{4g+2n-3}{2(4g+2n-2)}(1+(-1)^{n}),&\underline{K}=(0,0,\cdots,0,0,4g+2n-2),\\
-\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2},&\underline{K}=(0,0,\cdots,0,1,4g+2n-3),\\
-\frac{1}{2},&\underline{K}=(1,0,\cdots,0,0,4g+2n-3),\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Now we need to respectively count the number of solutions to the three equations

(47)

\displaystyle\underline{K}=(0,0,\cdots,0,0,4g+2n-2),

(48)

\displaystyle\underline{K}=(0,0,\cdots,0,1,4g+2n-3),

(49)

\displaystyle\underline{K}=(1,0,\cdots,0,0,4g+2n-3),

for
\underline{j}\in(\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0})^{n}
. It turns out that the number of solutions to (
47
) for
\underline{j}
is

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}i_{1},&\sigma\in S_{n,n}^{\{2\}},\\

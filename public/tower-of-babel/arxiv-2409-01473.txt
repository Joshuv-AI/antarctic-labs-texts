On Propagation of Information in Quantum Mechanics and Maximal Velocity Bounds
Israel Michael Sigal, Xiaoxu Wu

Abstract
. We revisit key notions related to the evolution of quantum information in few-body quantum mechanics (fbQM) and, for a wide class of dispersion relations, prove uniform bounds on the maximal speed of propagation of quantum information for states and observables with exponential error bounds. Our results imply, in particular, a fbQM version of the Lieb-Robinson bound, which is known to have wide applications in quantum information sciences. We propose a novel approach to proving maximal speed bounds.

1.2.
Setup

Consider a quantum system with a state space
\mathcal{H}
and a Hamiltonian

\mathcal{H}

A specific operator

(1.1)

\displaystyle H=\omega(p)+V(x),

where

is a real function s.t.

is

One can recognize information by the properties that it is transmittable, deletable
1
1

1

One should be able to erase parts of information one processes (i.e. irrelevant or inaccessible parts)
, localizable and measurable (or at least detectable).

For quantum mechaniccal systems, the second property requires extending the state space

to the space,

\mathcal{H}
. The original state space

is identified with the subspace of rank one projections. Quantum information related to a given system is encoded in density operators describing it.

The evolution of density operators, is given by the von Neumann equation (vNE) (here and in the rest of this section we set

(1.2)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\rho_{t}}{\partial t}=-i[H,\rho_{t}],\ \quad\text{ with }\ \rho_{t=0}=\rho_{0}.

This equation preserves the rank of projections and, when reduced to the rank-one projections, is equivalent to the Schrödinger equation.

The initial value problem (

(1.3)

\alpha^{\prime}_{t}(\rho):=e^{-iHt}\rho e^{iHt}.

This formula allows one to reduce many results on propagation of states and observables to estimates on the Schrödinger evolution

.

However, the framework of the vNE is much broader and entails a different take on the evolution problem (i.e. the semiclassics for the vNE leads to Liouville’s equation, rather than Newton’s one), and it is foundational for the theory of open quantum dynamics.

, we rewrite
\chi_{X}U_{t}\chi_{Y}
as

(2.7)

\displaystyle\chi_{X}U_{t}\chi_{Y}=

\displaystyle\chi_{X}T_{\xi}^{-1}T_{\xi}U_{t}T_{\xi}^{-1}T_{\xi}\chi_{Y}=\chi_{X}T^{-1}_{\xi}U_{t,\xi}T_{\xi}\chi_{Y},\qquad\forall\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

The right-hand side of Eq. (
2.7
) has an analytic continuation in
\xi
from
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to
\mathcal{S}_{a}^{n}
and is independent of
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Hence, its analytic continuation is independent of
\zeta\in\mathcal{S}_{a}^{n}
and (
2.6
) follows.
∎

Relation (
2.6
) implies, for
\zeta\in\mathcal{S}_{a}^{n}
,

(2.8)

\|\chi_{X}U_{t}\chi_{Y}\|\leq\|\chi_{X}T_{\zeta}^{-1}\|\|U_{t,\zeta}\|\|\chi_{Y}T_{\zeta}\|.

Let
\zeta=\xi+i\mu b
,
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\mu\in(0,a)
and
b\in S^{n-1}
. Then estimate (
2.8
) implies

(2.9)

\|\chi_{X}U_{t}\chi_{Y}\|\leq e^{-\mu(r_{X}-r_{Y})}\|U_{t,\zeta}\|,

where

(2.10)

r_{Y}:=\sup\limits_{y\in Y}b\cdot y\quad\text{ and }\quad r_{X}:=\inf\limits_{x\in X}b\cdot x.

We can cover
X
and
Y
by small balls. Hence, we begin with
Y=B_{r}(y_{0})
and
X=B_{r}(x_{0})
with
r=\frac{\epsilon}{2}d_{XY}
for some
y_{0}\in Y
,
x_{0}\in X
and
\epsilon\in(0,1)
.

Translate both balls by
y_{0}
to place
y_{0}
at the origin and
x_{0}
at
x_{0}-y_{0}
. Let
S_{y_{0}}
denote the corresponding shift operator. Then we have

(2.11)

S_{y_{0}}\chi_{X}U_{t}\chi_{Y}S_{y_{0}}^{-1}=S_{y_{0}}\chi_{X}S_{y_{0}}^{-1}S_{y_{0}}U_{t}S_{y_{0}}^{-1}S_{y_{0}}\chi_{Y}S_{y_{0}}^{-1}=\chi_{X^{y_{0}}}U_{t}^{y_{0}}\chi_{Y^{y_{0}}},

where
X^{y_{0}}=X-y_{0}=B_{r}(x_{0}-y_{0})
,
Y^{y_{0}}=Y-y_{0}=B_{r}(0)
and
U_{t}^{y_{0}}:=S_{y_{0}}U_{t}S_{y_{0}}^{-1}.
Thus, it suffices to estimate the right-hand side of Eq. (
2.11
).

Now, we skip the superindex
y_{0}
, so we are back to Eq. (
2.6
), but with
X=B_{r}(x_{0}-y_{0})
and
Y=B_{r}(0)
.

Let
\zeta=\xi+i\mu b
, where
b=\frac{x_{0}-y_{0}}{|x_{0}-y_{0}|}
. (For each pair of points
x_{0}\in X
and
y_{0}\in Y
, we choose a different analytic deformation
b
.) Since
|x_{0}-y_{0}|\geq d_{XY}
, by the definition of
r_{X}
and
r_{Y}
, we have

(2.12)

r_{X}-r_{Y}\geq(1-\epsilon)|x_{0}-y_{0}|\geq(1-\epsilon)d_{XY}.

Eqs. (
2.9
) and (
2.12
) yield

(2.13)

\displaystyle\|\chi_{X}U_{t}\chi_{Y}\|\leq

\displaystyle\|U_{t,\zeta}\|e^{-\mu(1-\epsilon)d_{x_{0}y_{0}}}.

Note that the complex deformation
U_{t,\zeta}
of the evolution operator changes from one pair of balls to another. The next proposition provides a uniform estimates of various such deformations.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let Assumption (A) be satisfied, and let
\mu\in(0,a)
and
c
be as in Eq. (
1.4
). Then we have the estimate

(2.14)

\|U_{t,\xi+i\mu b}\|\leq e^{\mu tc},\qquad\forall\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}{,\quad b\in S^{n-1}}.

Proof of Proposition
2.3
.

Take
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. We denote
g_{t,\zeta}:=U_{t,\zeta}g,\zeta\in\mathcal{S}_{a}^{n}
, and compute

(2.15)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\|g_{t,\zeta}\|^{2}=

\displaystyle-i\langle g_{t,\zeta},(H_{\zeta}-H_{\zeta}^{*})g_{t,\zeta}\rangle=2\langle g_{t,\zeta},\left({\rm Im}H_{\zeta}\right)g_{t,\zeta}\rangle.

For
\zeta=\xi+i\mu b
, by Eq. (
1.4
), we have,
{\rm Im}H_{\zeta}\leq\mu c
.
This together with Eq. (
2.15
) implies

(2.16)

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}\|g_{t,\zeta}\|^{2}|\leq

\displaystyle 2\mu c\|g_{t,\zeta}\|^{2}.

Since
g_{t,\zeta}|_{t=0}=g
, this gives
\|g_{t,\zeta}\|\leq\|g\|e^{\mu ct}
yielding (
2.14
). ∎

Eqs. (
2.13
) and (
2.14
) yield

(2.17)

\|\chi_{X}U_{t}\chi_{Y}\|\leq e^{\mu ct}e^{-\mu(1-\epsilon)d_{x_{0}y_{0}}}.

Now, we return to arbitrary disjoint sets
X
and
Y
. We cover
X
and
Y
by the balls,
B_{j}^{X}=B_{r}(x_{j}),j=1,\cdots,N_{1}
and
B_{k}^{Y}=B_{k}(y_{k}),k=1,\cdots,N_{2}
, in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
of the radius
r=\frac{\epsilon}{2}d_{XY}
, centred at
x_{j}
and
y_{k}
, respectively.
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
could be either finite or infinite. With this cover, we associate partitions of unity

(2.18)

\chi_{X}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N_{1}}\chi_{j}^{2}\quad\text{ and }\chi_{Y}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N_{2}}\tilde{\chi}_{k},

where
\chi_{j}
and
\tilde{\chi}_{k}
satisfy
\text{supp}(\chi_{j})\subset B_{r}(x_{j})
and
\text{supp}(\tilde{\chi}_{k})\subset B_{r}(y_{k}),

j=1,\cdots,N_{1}
and
k=1,\cdots,N_{2}
. For each
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we estimate, using Eqs. (
2.18
),

(2.19)

\displaystyle\|\chi_{X}e^{-itH}\chi_{Y}g\|^{2}=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N_{1}}\|\chi_{k}e^{-iHt}\chi_{Y}g\|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N_{1}}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N_{2}}\|\chi_{k}e^{-iHt}\tilde{\chi}_{j}g\|\right)^{2},

where in the equality we used the first partition of unity and in the inequality, the second one.
By (
2.17
), we have

(2.20)

\displaystyle\|\chi_{X}e^{-itH}\chi_{Y}g\|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle Ce^{2\mu ct}M(g),

where
M(g)
is given by, with
\mu^{\prime}=\mu(1-\epsilon)
,

\displaystyle M(g):=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N_{1}}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N_{2}}e^{-\mu^{\prime}d_{x_{k}y_{j}}}\|\tilde{\chi}_{j}g\|\right)^{2}

(2.21)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N_{1}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N_{2}}\sum\limits_{l=1}^{N_{2}}e^{-\mu^{\prime}(d_{x_{k}y_{j}}+d_{x_{k}y_{l}})}\|\tilde{\chi}_{j}g\|\|\tilde{\chi}_{l}g\|.

To estimate
M(g)
, we use arithmetic mean inequality to obtain

(2.22)

\displaystyle M(g)\leq\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N_{1}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N_{2}}\sum\limits_{l=1}^{N_{2}}e^{-\mu^{\prime}(d_{x_{k}y_{j}}+d_{x_{k}y_{l}})}\left(\frac{\|\tilde{\chi}_{j}g\|^{2}+\|\tilde{\chi}_{l}g\|^{2}}{2}\right).

By the symmetry with respect to
j
and
l
in the right-hand side of (
2.22
), (
2.22
) implies

(2.23)

\displaystyle M(g)\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N_{1}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N_{2}}\sum\limits_{l=1}^{N_{2}}e^{-\mu^{\prime}(d_{x_{k}y_{j}}+d_{x_{k}y_{l}})}\|\tilde{\chi}_{j}g\|^{2}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N_{2}}\|\tilde{\chi}_{j}g\|^{2}C_{XY},

where

(2.24)

C_{XY}:=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N_{1}}\sum\limits_{l=1}^{N_{2}}e^{-\mu^{\prime}(d_{x_{k}y_{j}}+d_{x_{k}y_{l}})}.

First, we sum over
l
. To this end, for each
k
, we decompose
\mathbb{R}^{d}
into the spherical shells

(2.25)

\Lambda_{m}(x_{k})=\{x\,:\,r_{m}\leq d_{xx_{k}}<r_{m+1}\},

where
m=0,\cdots,

r_{m}=r_{0}+m\epsilon d_{XY},
with
r_{0}=(1-\epsilon)d_{XY}
. We sum first over the balls covering a given shell and then over the shells. Each shell
\Lambda_{m}(x_{k})
is covered by at most
\tilde{N}_{m}
balls

, with
\tilde{N}_{m}=C_{n}r_{m}^{n-1}
for some constant

. This gives

(2.26)

\displaystyle C_{X,Y}\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N_{1}}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}C_{n}r_{m}^{n-1}e^{-\mu^{\prime}(d_{x_{k}y_{j}}+r_{m})}.

To evaluate the sum over the shells, we use that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}r_{m}^{n-1}e^{-\mu^{\prime}r_{m}}\leq

\displaystyle C\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}e^{-(1-\epsilon/2)\mu^{\prime}r_{m}}

(2.27)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ce^{-(1-\epsilon/2)\mu^{\prime}r_{0}}=Ce^{-(1-\epsilon/2)(1-\epsilon)\mu^{\prime}d_{XY}}.

We conclude that there is a

depending on

,
\epsilon
and
\mu^{\prime},
s.t.

(2.28)

\displaystyle C_{X,Y}\leq C_{n,\epsilon,\mu^{\prime}}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N_{1}}d_{XY}^{n-1}e^{-\mu^{\prime}(d_{x_{k}y_{j}}+d_{XY})}.

Next, to estimate the sum over
x_{k}
, we introduce the spherical shells centered at
y_{j}

(2.29)

\Lambda_{m}(y_{j})=\{x\,:\,r_{m}\leq d_{xy_{j}}<r_{m+1}\},

where
r_{m}=r_{0}+m\epsilon d_{XY},m=0,\cdots,
with
r_{0}=(1-\epsilon)d_{XY}
. Similarly, following (
2.28
), we obtain

(2.30)

C_{X,Y}\leq C_{n,\epsilon,\mu^{\prime}}^{2}d_{XY}^{2(n-1)}e^{-2\mu^{\prime}d_{XY}}.

This, together with Eqs. (
2.23
) and (
2.24
), implies

(2.31)

M(g)\leq C_{n,\epsilon,\mu^{\prime}}^{2}d_{XY}^{2(n-1)}e^{-2\mu^{\prime}d_{XY}}\|g\|^{2}.

Therefore, using (
2.20
), and (
2.31
), we conclude that with
\mu^{\prime\prime}=(1-2\epsilon)\mu
,

(2.32)

\|\chi_{X}e^{-itH}\chi_{Y}g\|\leq C_{n,\epsilon,\mu^{\prime}}C_{n,\epsilon,\mu}^{\prime}e^{\mu ct}e^{-\mu^{\prime\prime}d_{XY}}\|g\|,

where
C_{n,\epsilon,\mu}^{\prime}:=\sup\limits_{u\geq 0}u^{(n-1)}e^{-\epsilon\mu u}.
Estimate (
2.32
) yields

(2.33)

\|\chi_{X}U_{t}\chi_{Y}\|\leq Ce^{-\mu^{\prime\prime}(d_{XY}-c^{\prime}t)},\qquad\mu^{\prime\prime}=(1-2\epsilon)\mu,\,\,c^{\prime}=\frac{c}{1-2\epsilon}

with the constant
C
depending on
\epsilon=1-\frac{\mu^{\prime\prime}}{\mu}
,
n
and
\mu
.
This, with
\mu^{\prime\prime}
replaced by
\mu^{\prime}
, implies (
1.5
).
∎

3.
Localization of observables and proof of Theorem
1.5

We introduce a mathematically convenient notion of localized observables. We say that an observable
A
is
localized
in
X
if

(3.1)

\displaystyle A\chi_{X^{c}}=\chi_{X^{c}}A=0,\,\text{ or }\,\ A=\chi_{X}A\chi_{X}.

Since
\chi_{X^{c}}=\mathbbm{1}-\chi_{X}
, Eq. (
1.16
) implies, for any operator
A
acting on
X
,

(3.2)

A=\tilde{A}_{X}+\mathbbm{1},\qquad\text{where $\tilde{A}_{X}=\chi_{X}A\chi_{X}-\chi_{X}$. }

By the definition, the observable
\tilde{A}_{X}
is localized in
X
.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Since
A
and
B
act on
X
and
Y
, respectively, by (
3.2
), they are of the form
A=\tilde{A}_{X}+\mathbbm{1}
and
B=\tilde{B}_{Y}+\mathbbm{1}
, where
\tilde{A}_{X}
and
\tilde{B}_{Y}
are localized in
X
and
Y
, respectively. Let
A_{t}=\alpha_{t}(A)
and
\tilde{A}_{t,X}=\alpha_{t}(\tilde{A}_{X})
. Then
A_{t}=\tilde{A}_{t,X}+\mathbbm{1}
and

(3.3)

\displaystyle[A_{t},B]=

\displaystyle[\tilde{A}_{t,X},\tilde{B}_{Y}].

Using that
\tilde{A}_{X}=\chi_{X}\tilde{A}_{X}\chi_{X}
and
\tilde{B}_{Y}=\chi_{Y}\tilde{B}_{Y}\chi_{Y}
and using Theorem
1.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|[\tilde{A}_{t,X},\tilde{B}_{Y}]\|\leq

\displaystyle\|\tilde{A}_{X}\chi_{X}e^{-itH}\chi_{Y}\tilde{B}_{Y}\|+\|\tilde{B}_{Y}\chi_{Y}e^{itH}\chi_{X}\tilde{A}_{X}\|

(3.4)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ce^{-\mu(d_{XY}-ct)}\|\tilde{A}_{X}\|\|\tilde{B}_{X}\|.

Since
X^{c}\neq\emptyset
and since
\|Au\|=\|u\|
for
u
supported in
X^{c}
, we have
\|A\|\geq 1
. Hence,

(3.5)

\|\tilde{A}_{X}\|\leq\|\chi_{X}A\chi_{X}\|+\|\chi_{X}\|\leq\|A\|+1\leq 2\|A\|

and similarly for
\tilde{B}
. These inequalities together with (
3.4
) yield

(3.6)

\|[\tilde{A}_{t,X},\tilde{B}_{Y}]\|\leq Ce^{-\mu(d_{XY}-ct)}\|A\|\|B\|.

Relations (
3.3
) and (
3.6
) yield (
1.21
).
∎

4.
MVB for evolution of observables: Proof of Theorem
1.4

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Since
A
acts on
X,
by (
3.2
), it can be be written as
A=\tilde{A}_{X}+\mathbbm{1}
, where the operator
\tilde{A}_{X}
is localized in
X
, i.e. satisfies
\tilde{A}_{X}=\chi_{X}\tilde{A}_{X}\chi_{X}
. Hence the operator family
A_{t,X_{\eta}}
defined in (
1.18
) can be written as

(4.1)

A_{t,X_{\eta}}:=\chi_{X_{\eta}}\alpha_{t}(\tilde{A}_{X})\chi_{X_{\eta}}+\mathbbm{1}.

By the definition,
A_{t,X_{\eta}}
acts on
X_{\eta}
. To prove (
1.20
), we use that
A_{t}=\alpha_{t}(\tilde{A}_{X}+\mathbbm{1})=\alpha_{t}(\tilde{A}_{X})+\mathbbm{1}
, to write

\displaystyle A_{t}-A_{t,X_{\eta}}=

\displaystyle\alpha_{t}(\tilde{A}_{X})-\chi_{X_{\eta}}\alpha_{t}(\tilde{A}_{X})\chi_{X_{\eta}}

(4.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\chi_{X_{\eta}}\alpha_{t}(\tilde{A}_{X})\chi_{X_{\eta}^{c}}+\chi_{X_{\eta}^{c}}\alpha_{t}(\tilde{A}_{X}).

Using this relation and Theorem
1.1
, we arrive at (
1.20
).
∎

Remark 4.1
.

Theorem
1.4
yields a natural (but slightly longer) proof of Theorem
1.5
:
A_{t,X_{\eta}}
commutes with

as long as
\eta<d_{XY}
and therefore
[A_{t},B_{t}]=[R_{t}^{A},B]
, where
R_{t}^{A}=A_{t}-A_{t,X_{\eta}}
, which leads to an estimate of
[A_{t},B_{t}]
through an estimate of the remainder

.

Appendix A
Differentiability and power estimates

In this appendix, we consider a self-adjoint operator

With the definition (

We define the number

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\frac{1}{k!}\operatorname{Re}(i\mu)^{k-1}\sup\limits_{b\in S^{n-1}}\sup(b\cdot\nabla_{\xi})^{k}H_{\xi}{\big|_{\xi=0}}.

\displaystyle\bar{\partial}_{z}f(z)=

\displaystyle\frac{-i}{m!}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i\tilde{H}_{\xi^{z}}(t-s)}\bar{\partial}_{z}\tilde{H}_{\xi^{z}}e^{-i\tilde{H}_{\xi^{z}}s}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-i}{m!}\chi_{X}T_{\xi^{z}}^{-1}\int_{0}^{t}R_{t,s}(z)(i\mu)^{m}ds,

where, with
z=\lambda+i\mu
,

(A.20)

R_{t,u}(z):=\chi_{X}T_{\xi^{z}}^{-1}e^{-i\tilde{H}_{\xi^{z}}(t-u)}\partial_{\lambda}^{m+1}H_{\xi^{\lambda}}e^{-i\tilde{H}_{\xi^{z}}u}T_{\xi^{z}}\chi_{Y}.

Next, we claim that
\tilde{g}(z)
is independent of
\operatorname{Re}z
. Indeed, we have

(A.21)

\tilde{g}(z)=\chi_{X}T_{\xi^{z}}^{-1}T_{\eta}^{-1}T_{\eta}\tilde{f}(z)T_{\eta}^{-1}T_{\eta}T_{\xi^{z}}\chi_{Y},

where
\eta=\alpha b\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. Using that
T_{\eta}
commutes with
\partial_{\xi}
, we find

(A.22)

T_{\eta}\partial_{\lambda}^{k}H_{\xi^{\lambda}}T_{\eta}^{-1}=\partial_{\lambda}^{k}[T_{\eta}H_{\xi^{\lambda}}T_{\eta}^{-1}]=\partial_{\lambda}^{k}H_{\xi^{\lambda+a}},\qquad k=0,\cdots,m.

This, together with Eqs. (
A.7
), (
A.6
) and
\tilde{f}(z)=\tilde{U}_{t,\xi^{z}}
, yields that
T_{\eta}\tilde{f}(z)T_{\eta}^{-1}=\tilde{f}(z+\alpha)
. The last relation, together with (
A.21
) and the group property
T_{\eta}T_{\xi^{z}}=T_{\xi^{z+\alpha}}
, implies

(A.23)

\tilde{g}(z)=\tilde{g}(z+\alpha),\qquad\forall\,\alpha\in\mathbb{R},

which shows that
\tilde{g}(z)
is independent of
\operatorname{Re}z
. Hence, Lemma
A.5
applies to
\tilde{g}(z)
and yields

(A.24)

g(\lambda)=\tilde{g}(\lambda)=\tilde{g}(z)+Rem,

where, by (
A.19
),

\displaystyle Rem:=

\displaystyle i\int_{0}^{\mu}(\bar{\partial}_{z}\tilde{g})(x+is)ds

(A.25)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m!}\int_{0}^{\mu}\int_{0}^{t}R_{t,u}(\lambda+is)(is)^{m}duds.

Our next goal is to estimate this reminder.

To estimate
R_{t,u}(\lambda+is)
, we proceed as in Proposition
2.3
to obtain

(A.26)

\|e^{-i\tilde{H}_{\xi^{z}}t}\|\leq e^{\mu t\tilde{c}},

where
\mu={\rm Im}z
and the constant
\tilde{c}
is defined in Eq. (
A.1
). Next, from Eq. (
A.20
) we find,

(A.27)

\displaystyle\|R_{t,u}(\lambda+is)\|\leq

\displaystyle\|\chi_{X}T_{\xi^{\lambda+is}}^{-1}\|\|e^{-i\tilde{H}_{\xi^{\lambda+is}}(t-u)}\|\|\partial_{\lambda}^{m+1}H_{\xi^{\lambda}}\|\|e^{-i\tilde{H}_{\xi^{z}}u}\|\|T_{\xi^{\lambda+is}}\chi_{Y}\|.

Proceeding as (
2.8
)-(
2.13
), using (
A.26
) and (
A.3
) and assuming
d_{XY}-\epsilon-t\tilde{c}\geq 0
, we obtain

(A.28)

\|R_{t,u}(\lambda+is)\|\leq e^{-s(d_{x_{0}y_{0}}-\epsilon-t\tilde{c})}M.

This together with Eqs. (
A.20
) and (
A.25
) and estimates (
A.28
) and, for all
|x_{0}-y_{0}|-\epsilon-t\tilde{c}>0
and
\mu\in(0,1)
,

(A.29)

\frac{1}{m!}\int_{0}^{\mu}e^{-s(d_{x_{0}y_{0}}-\epsilon-t\tilde{c})}s^{m}ds\lesssim_{\mu}\frac{1}{(d_{x_{0}y_{0}}-\epsilon-t\tilde{c})^{m+1}},

yields

(A.30)

\|Rem\|\lesssim_{\mu}\frac{tM}{(d_{x_{0}y_{0}}-\epsilon-t\tilde{c})^{m+1}},

where
M
is given by (
A.3
), and subsequently

(A.31)

g(\lambda)=\tilde{g}(z)+Rem,

with
Rem
satisfying (
A.30
), yielding (
A.5
)-(
A.8
). ∎

Proof of Theorem

.

Let

with

Appendix B
N
-particle dynamics

For the
N
-particle problem, consider the quantum Hamiltonian for
N
identical bosons

(B.1)

H_{N}:=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}(\omega_{1}(p_{j})+v(x_{j}))+\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i\neq j}w(x_{i}-x_{j})

on
L^{2}_{sym}(\mathbb{R}^{dN})
, where
L^{2}_{sym}(\mathbb{R}^{dN})
is either bosonic space of symmetric functions of ferminoic one associated with certain representations of the symmetric group
S_{N}
of permutations of
N
indices (see e.g.
[
33
]
).

The operator
H_{N}
is of the form (
1.1
), with

(B.2)

\omega(k)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\omega_{1}(k_{j})\,\text{ and
}\,V(x)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}v(x_{j})+\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i\neq j}w(x_{i}-x_{j}),

and
x=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{dN}
,
k=(k_{1},\cdots,k_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{dN}
. Now, we define
\chi_{X}(x)
as the characteristic function of the set
X^{N}:=\underbrace{X\times\cdots\times X}_{N\text{-fold product}}
:

(B.3)

\tilde{\chi}_{X}(x)\equiv\chi_{X^{N}}(x):=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{N}\chi_{X}(x_{j}).

Theorem B.1
.

Let
H_{N}
be as in Eq. (
B.1
) and let
\omega_{1}
and
V
( defined in Eq. (
B.2
)) satisfy Condition (
1.6
) (with
n=d
). Let
X
and
Y
be two disjoint sets. Then, for any
\mu^{\prime}\in(0,\mu)
, we have

(B.4)

\|\tilde{\chi}_{X}e^{-itH_{N}}\tilde{\chi}_{Y}\|\leq Ce^{-\mu^{\prime}N(d_{XY}-c_{1}^{\prime}t)},

where
X
is the multiplication operator by the function
\chi_{X}(x)
in (
B.3
),
C>0
is a constant depending on
\epsilon=1-\frac{\mu^{\prime}}{\mu},d,N
and
\mu
, with
c^{\prime}_{1}=\frac{c_{1}}{1-\epsilon}
and

(B.5)

c_{1}:=\sup\limits_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,b\in S^{d-1}}\frac{{\rm Im}\omega_{1}(\xi+i\mu b)}{\mu}<\infty.

Eq. (
B.4
) is one of the simplest many-body estimates. The more refined and more difficult estimate to prove would be one with
\chi_{X}(x)
and
\chi_{Y}(y)
replaced in (
B.4
) by
\chi_{X}^{(k)}(x)\equiv\chi_{X^{k}}(x)=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{k}\chi_{X}(x_{j})
and
\chi_{Y}^{(l)}(y)\equiv\chi_{Y^{l}}(y)=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{l}\chi_{Y}(y_{j}),
with
1\leq k\leq l<N.

Proof of Theorem
B.1
.

We follow the proof of Theorem
1.1
, with the following modifications:

(B.6)

\xi=(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{dN},\,\xi_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,b=(b_{1},\cdots,b_{N}),b_{j}\in S^{n-1},\,\forall\,j,

(B.7)

\zeta=(\zeta_{1},\cdots,\zeta_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{dN},\,\zeta_{j}=\xi_{j}+i\mu b_{j},\,\mu\in(0,a),\,b\cdot x=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}b_{j}\cdot x_{j}.

As in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we reduce proving Eq. (
B.4
) for disjoint sets
X
and
Y
to Eq. (
B.4
) for the polyballs
X=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{N}X_{j},\,X_{j}:=B_{r}(x_{0j}-y_{0j})
and
Y=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{N}Y_{0},\,Y_{0}:=B_{r}(0)
, with
r=\frac{\epsilon}{2}d_{XY}
, with
x_{0j}\in X
and
y_{0j}\in Y,\,\forall\,j
, and
\epsilon\in(0,1)
.

Similarly to (
2.9
) and (
2.14
), we have

(B.8)

\|\tilde{\chi}_{X}e^{-iH_{N}t}\tilde{\chi}_{Y}\|\leq e^{-\mu(r_{X}-r_{Y})}e^{\mu tc},

with

(B.9)

\displaystyle c=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu}\sup\limits_{b\in\bigotimes\limits_{1}^{N}S^{d-1}}\sup\limits_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{dN}}{\rm Im}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\omega(\xi_{j}+i\mu b_{j}),

(B.10)

r_{Y}=\sup\limits_{y\in Y}b\cdot y,\quad r_{X}=\inf\limits_{x\in X}b\cdot x.

Let
b=(b_{1},\cdots,b_{N})
, with
b_{j}=\frac{x_{0j}-y_{0j}}{|x_{0j}-y_{0j}|},j=1,\cdots,N
. We claim that

(B.11)

r_{X}-r_{Y}\geq(1-\epsilon)\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}|x_{0j}-y_{0j}|\geq(1-\epsilon)Nd_{XY}.

Indeed, by the definitions of
X
and
Y
and Eq. (
B.10
), we have

(B.12)

r_{Y}\leq\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}|y_{j}|\leq\frac{\epsilon N}{2}d_{XY}

and

\displaystyle r_{X}=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}b\cdot(x_{0j}-y_{0j})+\inf\limits_{x\in X}b\cdot(x-(x_{0j}-y_{0j}))

(B.13)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}(|x_{0j}-y_{0j}|-\frac{\epsilon}{2}d_{XY}).

Hence,

(B.14)

\displaystyle r_{X}-r_{Y}\geq\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}(|x_{0j}-y_{0j}|-\epsilon d_{XY})\geq(1-\epsilon)\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}|x_{0j}-y_{0j}|,

as claimed.

Furthermore, we compute

(B.15)

\displaystyle c=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\sup\limits_{\xi_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,b_{j}\in S^{d-1}}{\rm Im}\omega_{1}(\xi_{j}+i\mu b_{j})=Nc_{1},

where, recall,

(B.16)

c_{1}:=\sup\limits_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,b\in S^{n-1}}\frac{\omega_{1}(\lambda+i\mu b)}{\mu}.

Combining (
B.8
), (
B.11
) and (
B.15
), we arrive at

(B.17)

\|\tilde{\chi}_{X}e^{-itH_{N}}\tilde{\chi}_{Y}\|\leq e^{-\mu(1-\epsilon)N(d_{XY}-c^{\prime}_{1}t)},

where
c^{\prime}_{1}=\frac{c_{1}}{1-\epsilon}
, which implies, with
\mu^{\prime\prime}=(1-\epsilon)\mu
,

(B.18)

\|\tilde{\chi}_{X}e^{-itH_{N}}\tilde{\chi}_{Y}\|\leq e^{-\mu^{\prime\prime}N(d_{XY}-c^{\prime}_{1}t)},

with
X=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{N}X_{j},\,X_{j}:=B_{r}(x_{0j})
and
Y=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{N}Y_{0},\,Y_{0}:=B_{r}(y_{0j})
, where
r=\frac{\epsilon}{2}d_{XY}
, for some
x_{0}\neq y_{0}
, with
x_{0j}\in X
and
y_{0j}\in Y,\,\forall\,j
, and
\epsilon\in(0,1)
. Proceeding as in (
2.18
)-(
2.33
) in each variable
x_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
j=1,\cdots,N
, we arrive at (
1.5
) by taking
\mu^{\prime}=\mu(1-2\epsilon)
for any
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, and the constant
C
in (
B.4

\epsilon=1-\frac{\mu^{\prime}}{\mu},d,N
and
\mu
.∎

Research note: Improved virus isolation strategies enhance the detection rate for avian leukosis virus eradication in a local chicken breed in China
Authors: Ruiqiang Geng, Mengyu Song, Wenping Cui, Yixin Wang, Shuang Chang, Zhihao Ren, Peng Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe detection rate of avian leukosis virus subgroup K (ALV-K) has been increasing annually in Chinese local breeds, its poor replicative capacity limits the success of virus isolation. To improve ALV-K eradication, this study assessed the detection limits of the magnetic-particle chemiluminescent assay and ELISA kits using a standardized virus stock. Additionally, we also determined the impact of serial passage during virus isolation on the positive detection rate. Using a standardized ALV-K stock, the magnetic-particle chemiluminescent kit detected positive signals at a 256-fold dilution, whereas the most sensitive ELISA kit reached its limit at a 64-fold dilution. When applied to 13 plasma samples from a local chicken flock, the magnetic-particle chemiluminescent kit at passage 0 yielded a detection rate of 76.9% (10/13), compared to only 15.4% (2/13) by ELISA. After a single cell passage, the chemiluminescent method achieved 100% positivity (13/13), while ELISA required two passages to reach 61.5% (8/13). Quantitative real-time PCR (qPCR) confirmed the presence of ALV-K in at least 9 plasma samples, corroborating the chemiluminescent results. These results demonstrate that the magnetic-particle chemiluminescent assay exhibits significantly higher sensitivity than ELISA and that cell passaging markedly enhances the positive rate of virus isolation. By integrating a highly sensitive chemiluminescent assay with an optimized viral isolation protocol, this study provides a novel strategy for facilitating ALV-K eradication in local Chinese chicken flocks.Keywords: ALV-K, Local Chinese chicken breeds, Eradication, Magnetic-particle chemiluminescence

Introduction

Avian leukosis (AL) is a group of neoplastic and immunosuppressive infectious diseases caused by Avian leukosis virus (ALV), posing a severe threat to the global poultry industry (Fandiño et al., 2023). Based on differences in the antigenicity of env proteins, host range, and genomic sequences, ALV is classified into 11 subgroups (A–K) (Payne and Nair, 2012). In China, following effective control of ALV-J epidemics, the emerging ALV-K has gradually become the predominant circulating subgroup. In recent years, multiple large-scale epidemiological surveys have revealed an increase in the detection rate of ALV-K in local Chinese chicken breeds and yellow-feathered broilers, particularly in populations lacking systematic eradication measures (Zhang et al., 2024). Because of the weak promoter activity of its long terminal repeat region, and the gp85 protein of some ALV-K strains has low binding affinity for the Tva receptor, ALV-K exhibits reduced in vitro replicative capacity (Chen et al., 2022). This poses a potential false‑negative challenge to conventional virus isolation strategies that rely solely on a single passage in cell culture. As an emerging detection technology, the magnetic-particle chemiluminescent assay has a wider linear range and higher sensitivity compared to conventional ELISA, enabling the detection of early infections or low-titer virus (Sun et al., 2025).

This study established a highly sensitive detection strategy for ALV-K by combining the magnetic-particle chemiluminescent assay with an optimized cell-culture passage protocol, overcoming the challenge of slow replication and improving detection sensitivity, providing reliable technical support for ALV-K epidemiological surveillance and eradication in poultry flocks.

Materials and method

Ethics statement

The animal care and use protocol was approved by the Shandong Agricultural University Animal Care and Use Committee (SDAUA-2016-002). Care and maintenance of all experimental animals throughout the experiments were in strict accordance with the ethics and biosecurity guidelines approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Shandong Agricultural University.

Virus and cells

The reference strain used as a positive control was the ALV-K strain JS11C1 (GenBank No. KF746200.1). This strain was isolated, identified, and preserved by our laboratory in 2012 from a conservation flock of a local chicken breed in Jiangsu Province, China. DF-1 cell was purchased from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC).

ALV specific antigen detection kits used in the experiment

ALV p27 antigen ELISA kits were purchased from two distinct Chinese manufacturers. The manufacturers labeled these kits as Kit A and Kit B, both of which are commercial products. The Biotek Synergy H1 microplate reader (model: H1M) was purchased from Darui Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Guangzhou, China. The ALV magnetic-particle chemiluminescent antigen detection kit was developed using monoclonal antibodies derived from the hybridoma cell line 7H7H2 (specific for ALV) and conjugated to carboxylated microspheres. This kit is intended for the detection of ALV p27 antigen from different sources. It is a commercial kit co-developed by our laboratory and HeXu Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Zhengzhou, China, and has been approved as a new veterinary drug by the Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs of the People's Republic of China. The magnetic-particle chemiluminescence analyzer (Model: Shine i1910), a fully automated chemiluminescent immunoassay platform, was procured from Shenzhen IncreCare Biotechnology Co., Ltd.

Background of chicken flocks

For the breeding flock of a local yellow-feathered broiler strain (designated LNH) in Sichuan, China, long-term ALV eradication efforts have been implemented. The virus isolation positive rate at the onset of production gradually decreased from 1.70% in 2018 to 0.13% in 2023, and all isolated viruses were identified as ALV-J. Since December 2023, a new ALV p27 antigen ELISA kit has been adopted in the detection system, and the virus isolation positive rate has subsequently dropped to nearly zero. However, in the follow-up monitoring, some egg white that tested positive for ALV were identified as ALV-K via polymerase chain reaction sequencing. This observation indicates that the conventional virus isolation protocols exhibit poor performance in detecting ALV-K, underscoring the critical need to optimize and refine this procedure.

Detection of ALV-K standard samples using different ALV assay kits

The JS11C1 strain was inoculated into DF-1 cells and serially blind-passaged for three generations. Cell culture supernatants were subsequently harvested and assayed for the ALV group-specific p27 antigen using an ELISA kit. Positive supernatants were harvested, and the viral titer was determined by the Reed-Muench method, yielding a result of 3.5 × 10³ TCID₅₀/100 μL. To compare the performance of the detection kits, the positive cell culture supernatant was serially diluted in two-fold increments and assayed in parallel using Kit A, Kit B, and the magnetic-particle chemiluminescent assay. All tests were performed in accordance with the respective manufacturer's instructions.

Effects of different kits and cell passage numbers on ALV detection results

Breeding chickens with the egg white S/P value ≥ 0.08 in the ALV p27 antigen ELISA assay were selected, and anticoagulated blood was aseptically collected from those chickens. The virus isolation protocol was performed following methods previously published by our laboratory (Sun and Cui, 2007). After 7 days of culture, the cell supernatant was collected and stored at −80 °C for subsequent use (Passage 0). The cells were trypsinized for subculture, and after another 6 days of maintenance, the cell supernatant was harvested and stored at −80 °C (Passage 1). This subculture process was repeated once: the cells were passaged again, maintained for 6 days, and the resulting cell supernatant was stored at −80 °C (Passage 2). All cell culture supernatants (from Passages 0, 1, and 2) were tested separately using Kit A, Kit B, and the ALV magnetic-particle chemiluminescent antigen detection kit. All detection procedures and interpretation criteria were performed strictly in accordance with the manufacturers’ instructions for each respective kit.

Detection of viruses in suspected positive plasma by qPCR

Viral RNA was extracted from the aforementioned plasma samples using a commercial viral RNA extraction kit (Omega Bio-Tek, Norcross, GA, USA). Based on the published sequences of ALV-K and relevant literature, qPCR primers specific for ALV-K were designed and synthesized ( Primers used: ALV-F 5′-CAGACAGGTTCTCGCTCCG-3′, ALV-R 5′-CAAGCACAGGAGGTAAATGG-3′) (Chen et al., 2018). Samples with a Ct value ≤ 30.44 and a characteristic sigmoid amplification curve were considered positive for ALV-K.

Results and discussion

Different commercial kits for ALV detection applied to ALV-K standardized samples

The sensitivity of two ELISA kits and three lots of the ALV magnetic-particle chemiluminescent antigen detection kits were compared using serial dilutions of the ALV-K standard virus solution (Table 1). The results revealed differences in sensitivity between the two ELISA kits. At a 64-fold dilution, the S/P value for Kit A was near the positive cut-off, whereas Kit B yielded a clearly positive result. Notably, Kit B exhibited higher sensitivity relative to Kit A. The magnetic-particle chemiluminescent kit exhibited significantly greater analytical sensitivity than Kit B. At a 256-fold dilution, all three lots of the chemiluminescent kit tested positive. At a 512-fold dilution, Lot 003 remained clearly positive, whereas the results for Lots 001 and 002 were near the cut-off value.

Cell passaging increased the ALV-K detection rate

The more sensitive Kit B was utilized to evaluate ALV-K isolation across serial DF-1 passages in samples from the LNH flock (Table 2). At Passage 0, only 2 of 13 samples (15.38%, 2/13; LNH-006 and LNH-013) exhibited S/P values approaching the positive cutoff. In Passage 1, 4 samples tested positive (30.77%, 4/13); notably, the S/P values for LNH-006 and LNH-013 had increased compared with Passage 0. Following the two passages, the number of positives increased to 8, yielding a detection rate of 61.54% (8/13). These findings confirm that serial cell passaging significantly improves the isolation efficiency of ALV-K, underscoring its essential role in the reliable detection of ALV-K.

Application of the more sensitive magnetic-particle chemiluminescent kit to supernatants from the LNH flock's ALV-K isolation cultures yielded markedly higher detection rates than Kit B (Table 2). At Passage 0, 10/13 (76.9%)samples were positive with the chemiluminescent kit, compared to only 2/13 with Kit B, demonstrating its superior sensitivity under an identical isolation protocol. After one passage, all 13 samples tested positive. Consistent with the ELISA trends, S/P values from the chemiluminescent assay increased sharply with serial passaging. Notably, sample LNH-002 showed S/P values of 2.714, 8.424, and 20.290 over three passages, while LNH-006 reached 6.301, 22.151, and 34.196. These signal intensities were far above the kit's cut-off value and that of Kit B, which allowed for unambiguous result interpretation. qPCR was performed on viral RNA extracted from the plasma of the 13 samples. Based on Ct values, ALV-K was confirmed in at least 9 samples (Table 2). The remaining four samples exhibited Ct values near the detection threshold, indicative of low viral loads. qPCR analysis corroborated the results obtained by the magnetic-particle chemiluminescent assay, while also demonstrating that direct qPCR achieved a higher detection rate than conventional virus isolation. The relatively lower sensitivity of virus isolation is likely attributable to the inefficient replication of ALV-K in DF-1 cells, particularly when viral copy numbers in blood are low, thus rendering virus isolation challenging.

Improving ALV-K detection: A combination of chemiluminescent and cell passage strategy

Since its first isolation from Chinese Luhua chickens, ALV-K has exhibited a continuously increasing infection rate, posing new challenges to ALV eradication programs in China (Dong et al., 2015). Because of its relatively mild pathogenicity, ALV-K infection typically causes no obvious clinical symptoms, easily leading to persistent latent infections in chicken flocks that are difficult to detect and eliminate through clinical observation (Chen et al., 2022). To address the emerging epidemic trend of ALV-K and the technical challenges in its detection, this study successfully established and validated a high-sensitivity detection protocol for ALV-K, which combines the ALV magnetic-particle chemiluminescent antigen detection kit with an optimized cell culture workflow.

We first evaluated the performance of the magnetic-particle chemiluminescent kit against conventional ELISA kits. The chemiluminescent kit showed significantly higher analytical sensitivity, detecting positive signals at a 256-fold dilution of standard virus stock, whereas the best ELISA kit reached its limit at a 64-fold dilution. The above experiments demonstrate that the magnetic-particle chemiluminescent assay has higher sensitivity than ELISA.

Virus isolation remains critical for confirming ALV infection, subgroup identification, and enabling subsequent biological research. Given the poor replicative capacity of ALV-K in DF-1 cells, standard single-passage virus isolation yields extremely low positivity rates (only 2/13 at Passage 0). Direct qPCR of plasma samples provided a higher positivity rate (≥9/13). Although qPCR offers high sensitivity and rapidity for nucleic acid detection, virus isolation remains irreplaceable in ALV eradication programs. Moreover, because of high equipment costs, stringent operational requirements, standardization difficulties and strong interference from endogenous ALV nucleic acid, qPCR has not yet been widely adopted in large-scale Chinese breeding flocks. ELISA-based antigen detection remains the predominant surveillance method in most poultry operations. Given the slow replication kinetics of ALV-K in vitro, our study was specifically designed to optimize the conventional virus isolation protocol by integrating a highly sensitive chemiluminescent assay with serial cell passaging, thereby offering a readily implementable strategy aligned with the current diagnostic capacities of the Chinese poultry industry. Therefore, we systematically evaluated the necessity and effectiveness of cell passage strategies. Serial cell passaging effectively elevates the antigen concentration in the culture supernatant to detectable thresholds. The chemiluminescent kit, with its high sensitivity, then captures these amplified signals, enabling early and definitive confirmation of infection (Xu et al., 2025). This combination dramatically improves the isolation rate. Whereas the high-sensitivity ELISA kit yielded a positivity rate of only 61.54% (8/13) after two serial cell passages, the magnetic-particle chemiluminescent kit achieved 100% (13/13) positivity after a single passage. This superior detection performance holds significant practical implications for ALV-K eradication. Currently, many local chicken flocks in China are confronted with an ALV eradication bottleneck, which results from inconsistent ALV-K detection outcomes. This protocol overcomes this detection bottleneck by accurately identifying infected individuals, thereby enabling more effective interruption of transmission within chicken flocks.

China is not the only country facing the detection challenges posed by viruses like ALV-K. Many countries face the risk of covert circulation of poorly replicating virus subgroups in local chicken breeds or flocks lacking systematic eradication programs. False negatives resulting from conventional virus isolation methods can undermine eradication efforts. The strategy established in this study not only provides a highly sensitive detection paradigm for ALV-K but also offers a technical reference for the identification and eradication of other slowly replicating viruses. In summary, this study successfully addresses the detection challenges posed by the poor in vitro replicative capacity of ALV-K by integrating the magnetic particle chemiluminescent antigen detection kit with an optimized virus isolation passage protocol.

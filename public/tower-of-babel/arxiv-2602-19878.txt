Axis Decomposition for ODRL: Resolving Dimensional Ambiguity in Policy Constraints through Interval Semantics
Daham M. Mustafa, Diego Collarana, Yixin Peng, Rafiqul Haque, Christoph Lange-Bever, Christoph Quix, Stefan Decker

Abstract
Every ODRL 2.2 constraint compares a single scalar value: ( leftOperand , operator , rightOperand ). Five of ODRL’s left operands, however, denote multi-dimensional quantities—image dimensions, canvas positions, geographic coordinates—whose specification text explicitly references multiple axes. For these operands, a single scalar constraint admits one interpretation per axis, making policy evaluation non-deterministic. We classify ODRL’s left operands by value-domain structure (scalar, dimensional, concept-valued), grounded in the ODRL 2.2 specification text, and show that dimensional ambiguity is intrinsic to the constraint syntax. We present an axis-decomposition framework that refines each dimensional operand into axis-specific scalar operands and prove four properties: deterministic interpretation, AABB completeness, sound over-approximation under projection, and conservative extension. Conflict detection operates in two layers: per-axis verdicts are always decidable; box-level verdicts compose through Strong Kleene conjunction into a three-valued logic ( Conflict , Compatible , Unknown ). For ODRL’s disjunctive ( odrl:or ) and exclusive-or ( odrl:xone ) logical constraints, where per-axis decomposition does not apply, the framework encodes coupled multi-axis conjectures directly. We instantiate the framework as the ODRL Spatial Axis Profile—15 axis-specific left operands for the five affected base terms—and evaluate it on 117 benchmark problems spanning nine categories across both TPTP FOF (Vampire) and SMT-LIB (Z3) encodings, achieving full concordance between provers. Benchmark scenarios are inspired by constraints arising in cultural heritage dataspaces such as Datenraum Kultur. All meta-theorems are mechanically verified in Isabelle/HOL.

2.2
Insufficiency of Knowledge Grounding

Proposition 2.4

(KB Cannot Resolve Left-Operand Ambiguity)
.

For a dimensional left operand
\ell
, the constraint
(\ell,\bowtie,v)

Proof.

Even with

(Vocabulary Alignment is Unsound)
.

Declaring

Proof.

Remark 2.6

(Unit vs. Axis)
.

ODRL’s

resolves right-operand typing
(“1250

Corollary 2.7

(Necessity of Axis Decomposition)
.

Dimensional ambiguity can be resolved only by axis-specific operands
within the constraint language. Neither knowledge-base grounding,
vocabulary alignment, nor unit specification suffices.

Axis Decomposition

Definition 3.1

(Axis Decomposition)
.

For a dimensional left operand
\ell
with

\mathcal{V}_{\ell}=D_{1}\times\cdots\times D_{n}
, the

axis decomposition
is a set

\Delta(\ell)=\{\ell_{a_{1}},\ldots,\ell_{a_{n}}\}
where each

\ell_{a_{k}}
has value space
\mathcal{V}_{\ell_{a_{k}}}=D_{k}
and

\ell_{a_{k}}(w)=\pi_{k}(\ell(w))
.

Each decomposed operand isolates a single axis,
enabling unambiguous constraints:

Definition 3.2

(Axis-Specific Constraint)
.

An axis-specific constraint
c=(\ell_{a_{k}},\bowtie,v)
with

v\in D_{k}
is satisfied by execution context
w
iff:

w\models c\;\;\iff\;\;\ell_{a_{k}}(w)\!\downarrow\;\land\;\ell_{a_{k}}(w)\;\bowtie\;v

When
\ell_{a_{k}}(w)
is undefined,
w\not\models c

(
closed-world
per axis: missing data entails
non-satisfaction). This is distinct from an

unconstrained
axis—one for which no constraint
targets
a_{k}
—which is handled at the box level
(
Section

3.2
).

Theorem 3.3

(Semantic Determinism)
.

Every axis-specific constraint has a unique satisfaction condition.

Proof.

\ell_{a_{k}}(w)\in D_{k}
and
v\in D_{k}
are elements of a
totally ordered domain. The truth value of

\ell_{a_{k}}(w)\bowtie v
is uniquely determined.
∎

Remark 3.4

(Aggregation via Composition)
.

The aggregate conditions from Proposition
2.3
reduce to
standard ODRL composition over axis-specific constraints:

\max_{k}\pi_{k}(\ell(w))\leq v
is equivalent to conjunction
(all axes satisfy), while
\max_{k}\pi_{k}(\ell(w))\geq v
is
equivalent to disjunction (at least one axis satisfies). Both are
expressible using
and
/
or
over operands from

\Delta(\ell)
. The axis decomposition thus resolves both the
axis-selection ambiguity and the aggregation-mode ambiguity.

3.1
Interval Denotation

Each axis-specific constraint denotes a subset of
D_{k}
. For five of the
six comparison operators this subset is an interval; the exception is

neq
, whose denotation
D_{k}\setminus\{v\}
is a union of two
disjoint intervals. This prevents the per-axis intersection in
Definition

from yielding a single interval,
and thus a single bounding box.
Since
neq
with numeric right operands has no evident use case
in the dimensional setting, we exclude it. For the remainder of this
section,
\bowtie

dimensional comparison operators

\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}=\{\texttt{eq},\texttt{lt},\texttt{lteq},\texttt{gt},\texttt{gteq}\}
.

Definition 3.5

(Interval Denotation)
.

For

with

and

\bowtie\in\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}

\displaystyle\llbracket\ell_{a_{k}}\texttt{ eq }v\rrbracket

\displaystyle=[v,v]

(1)

\displaystyle\llbracket\ell_{a_{k}}\texttt{ lteq }v\rrbracket

\displaystyle=[\inf D_{k},\;v]

(2)

\displaystyle\llbracket\ell_{a_{k}}\texttt{ gteq }v\rrbracket

\displaystyle=[v,\;\sup D_{k}]

(3)

\displaystyle\llbracket\ell_{a_{k}}\texttt{ lt }v\rrbracket

\displaystyle=[\inf D_{k},\;v)

(4)

\displaystyle\llbracket\ell_{a_{k}}\texttt{ gt }v\rrbracket

\displaystyle=(v,\;\sup D_{k}]

Definition 3.6

(Box Denotation)
.

For a conjunction
C
of axis-specific constraints over

\Delta(\ell)=\{\ell_{a_{1}},\ldots,\ell_{a_{n}}\}

\llbracket C\rrbracket=I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{n}\quad\text{where}\quad I_{k}=\bigcap\{\llbracket c_{j}\rrbracket\mid c_{j}\text{ targets }a_{k}\}

If no constraint targets

Theorem 3.7

(AABB Completeness)
.

1.

Every conjunction of axis-specific constraints defines an
axis-aligned bounding box
R=I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{n}
.

2.

Every AABB in
D_{1}\times\cdots\times D_{n}
is expressible as
such a conjunction.

Proof.

(1) By Definition
3.6
.
(2) Each face
[l_{k},u_{k}]
is expressed as

(\ell_{a_{k}},\texttt{gteq},l_{k})\;\texttt{and}\;(\ell_{a_{k}},\texttt{lteq},u_{k})

Theorem 3.8

(Projection Soundness)
.

Let
\llbracket C\rrbracket=I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{n}

\mathbf{v}\in\llbracket C\rrbracket
iff
\pi_{k}(\mathbf{v})\in I_{k}
for all

Immediate from the definition of Cartesian product.
∎

Remark 3.9

(Over-Approximation)
.

For non-axis-aligned regions, only the forward direction holds:
the AABB is a sound over-approximation.

Definition 3.10

(Per-Axis Verdict)

For axis-specific constraints

\operatorname{verdict}_{a_{k}}(c_{1},c_{2})=\begin{cases}\textsc{Conflict}&\text{if }\llbracket c_{1}\rrbracket\cap\llbracket c_{2}\rrbracket=\emptyset\\
\textsc{Compatible}&\text{if }\llbracket c_{1}\rrbracket\cap\llbracket c_{2}\rrbracket\neq\emptyset\end{cases}

Definition 3.11

(Per-Axis Subsumption)
.

For axis-specific constraints

\operatorname{subsumes}_{a_{k}}(c_{1},c_{2})=\begin{cases}\textsc{Confirmed}&\text{if }\llbracket c_{1}\rrbracket\subseteq\llbracket c_{2}\rrbracket\\

(Conflict Propagation)

If

Remark 3.15

(Choice of Three-Valued Semantics)
.

The verdict algebra instantiates
strong Kleene
three-valued
logic (
K_{3}
): conjunction is
\min
and disjunction is
\max
over

F<\bot<T
, where
\textsc{Conflict}\mapsto F
,

\textsc{Unknown}\mapsto\bot
,
\textsc{Compatible}\mapsto T
.
Heyting semantics (
\neg\bot=F
) is unsound: it would treat an
unresolved branch as provably non-overlapping, fabricating exclusivity
in
xone
. Bochvar semantics (
T\cup\bot=\bot
) is too
conservative: a single definite branch cannot resolve disjunction.
Strong Kleene uniquely satisfies both requirements:

T\cup\bot=T
(disjunction resolves) and

\neg\bot=\bot
(exclusivity demands proof).

Proposition 3.16

(No False Verdicts Under Incomplete Information)
.

If
\operatorname{boxVerdict}(C_{1},C_{2})=\textsc{Conflict}
, the
verdict holds for every completion of the unconstrained axes. If

\operatorname{boxVerdict}(C_{1},C_{2})=\textsc{Compatible}
, every
axis has been examined and admits overlap.
Unknown
is
returned if and only if no axis proves conflict and at least one
axis lacks a definite verdict.

Definition 3.17

(Request Satisfaction)
.

For request
\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})
and constraint set
C
:

\operatorname{satisfied}(\mathbf{v},C)=\begin{cases}\textsc{Yes}&\text{if }\forall k:v_{k}\in I_{k}\\
\textsc{No}&\text{if }\exists k:v_{k}\notin I_{k}\end{cases}

Remark 3.18

(Deontic Conflict)
.

When comparing a permission and prohibition over the same action,
deontic conflict occurs when scopes
overlap

\llbracket C_{P}\rrbracket\cap\llbracket C_{Q}\rrbracket\neq\emptyset
. This is the inverse
of constraint satisfiability (Definition
3.10
):
disjoint scopes prevent deontic conflict, while overlapping
scopes create it.

\textsc{Conflict}&\text{if }\forall\,\text{branch pairs}\colon\operatorname{boxVerdict}=\textsc{Conflict}\\
\textsc{Unknown}&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(13)

Theorem 3.19

(Composition Soundness)
.

If
\operatorname{boxVerdict}(C_{1},C_{2})=\textsc{Conflict}
, then no
value in
D_{1}\times\cdots\times D_{n}
simultaneously satisfies both
constraint sets. Analogously for disjunctive and exclusive compositions.

Proof.

Conjunction.

\exists k\colon V_{k}=\textsc{Conflict}
means

\llbracket c_{1}^{(k)}\rrbracket\cap\llbracket c_{2}^{(k)}\rrbracket=\emptyset
. By
Theorem
3.8
, membership in a box requires membership
in every axis projection; since projection
k
admits no common point,
no element of
D_{1}\times\cdots\times D_{n}
lies in both boxes.

Disjunction.
All branch pairs yield
Conflict
:

\forall i,j\colon\llbracket B_{i}\rrbracket\cap\llbracket B_{j}\rrbracket=\emptyset
. Therefore

\bigcup_{i}\llbracket B_{i}\rrbracket\cap\bigcup_{j}\llbracket B_{j}\rrbracket=\bigcup_{i,j}(\llbracket B_{i}\rrbracket\cap\llbracket B_{j}\rrbracket)=\emptyset
.

Exclusive disjunction.
Reduces to the disjunction case.
∎

3.3.1
Cross-Domain Composition

A complete policy may combine dimensional, concept-valued, and scalar
constraints. These target distinct left operands with disjoint value spaces.

Definition 3.20

(Cross-Domain Verdict)
.

For per-operand verdicts
V_{1},\ldots,V_{m}
(from any combination of
dimensional, concept-valued, or scalar evaluation):

\operatorname{verdict}_{\times}(V_{1},\ldots,V_{m})=\begin{cases}\textsc{Conflict}&\text{if }\exists i\colon V_{i}=\textsc{Conflict}\\
\textsc{Compatible}&\text{if }\forall i\colon V_{i}=\textsc{Compatible}\\
\textsc{Unknown}&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(14)

Theorem 3.21

(Cross-Domain Soundness)
.

If
\operatorname{verdict}_{\times}=\textsc{Conflict}
, then no
execution context simultaneously satisfies both policies.

Proof.

Some operand
\ell_{i}
admits no satisfying value. Since operands from
different classes have disjoint value spaces, satisfying other operands
cannot provide a witness for
\ell_{i}
.

3.4
Policy Quality Analysis

The per-axis and box-level definitions support three design-time analyses.

Self-contradiction.

A policy requiring
width lteq 600
and
width gteq 800

under conjunction:
(0,600]\cap[800,\infty)=\emptyset
, so

\operatorname{boxVerdict}=\textsc{Conflict}
. No image satisfies both.

Redundancy.

width lteq 600
and
width lteq 1200
under conjunction:

(0,600]\subseteq(0,1200]
, so

\operatorname{subsumes}_{a_{\text{w}}}(c_{1},c_{2})=\textsc{Confirmed}
.
The weaker bound is redundant.

Definition 3.22

(Constraint Completeness)
.

A constraint set
C
over
\Delta(\ell)
is
axis-complete

if for every
a_{k}\in\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\}
, there exists

c\in C
targeting
a_{k}
. The
coverage
of
C
is

\operatorname{cov}(C)=\{a_{k}\mid\exists c\in C\text{

.

Supply-chain refinement.

Upstream permits
1200\times 900
px; downstream permits

600\times 400
px. Both axes subsume:

\operatorname{boxSubsumes}=\textsc{Confirmed}
.

Remark 3.23

(Expressiveness Boundary)
.

The decomposition is limited to axis-aligned regions.
Cross-axis constraints—such as total pixel area
(
w\times h\leq A
), aspect ratio (
w/h\in[r_{1},r_{2}]
),
or crop containment (
x+w_{\text{crop}}\leq W_{\text{canvas}}
)—are
not expressible within ODRL’s scalar constraint pattern, which provides
a single left-operand slot per constraint. This is a limitation of the
language, not the decomposition. A profile may introduce

derived scalar operands
(e.g.,
oax:totalPixels
,

oax:aspectRatio
) that pre-compute cross-axis quantities,
at which point they require no decomposition and fall under ODRL’s
standard scalar evaluation.

Remark 3.24

(Discrete Domains)
.

Interval denotation applies to both continuous (
\mathbb{R}
) and discrete (
\mathbb{N}
)
domains. For pixel dimensions where
D_{k}=\mathbb{N}_{\geq 0}
, half-open
intervals simplify:
(v,\infty)
over
\mathbb{N}
equals
[v{+}1,\infty)
.
Conflict detection and subsumption are unchanged.

Remark 3.25

(Domain Bounds)
.

The exact domain
D_{k}
for each axis-specific operand is fixed by the
profile instantiation (
Footnote

2
). For size operands,

D_{k}=(0,\infty)
since zero collapses the dimension; for relative
positions,
D_{k}=[0,100]
; for geographic coordinates,
D_{k}
follows
WGS 84 bounds. All theorems in this section are parametric in
D_{k}

and hold for any totally ordered domain—open, closed, bounded, or
unbounded.

Remark 3.26

(Profile Independence and Expressiveness Limits)
.

All definitions and theorems in this section are parametric in the
axis domains
D_{1},\ldots,D_{n}
: they hold for any profile that
decomposes a dimensional left operand into axes satisfying three
conditions:

1.

each
D_{k}
is
totally ordered
(not partially ordered),

2.

each
D_{k}
is
non-wrapping
(no identification of
endpoints), and

3.

axes are
independent
(satisfaction on one axis does not
constrain another).

Four classes of dimensional constraint fall outside this scope:
(i)
circular domains
such as longitude at the antimeridian
or rotation angles, where regions may cross the wrap point and require
unions of intervals;
(ii)
coupled axes
such as 3D orientation, where axis
independence fails;
(iii)
cross-axis constraints
such as area or aspect ratio,
which ODRL’s scalar pattern cannot express
(Remark
3.23
); and
(iv)
non-numeric structured spaces
such as colour gamuts.
Of these, only the antimeridian case arises in current ODRL practice;
extending the interval algebra to circular domains is future work.

Proposition 3.27

(Conservative Extension)
.

Adding
\Delta(\ell)
to the ODRL vocabulary is a conservative extension:
every constraint using base operands retains its original semantics.

Proof.

Each
\ell_{a_{k}}\in\Delta(\ell)
has a fresh IRI. The relation

\ell_{a_{k}}\;\texttt{skos:broader}\;\ell
records specialisation. No SKOS
axiom modifies ODRL constraint evaluation, which dispatches on exact
left-operand IRIs. A profile-unaware engine ignores unrecognised IRIs per
the ODRL profile mechanism
[
7
]
; a profile-aware engine gains
per-axis precision without altering existing verdicts.
∎

5
Conclusion

We identified a structural mismatch in ODRL 2.2 between the
multi-dimensional semantics of five spatial left operands and
the scalar constraint pattern. This mismatch causes semantic
ambiguity, expressiveness loss, and unsound reasoning in
policy enforcement.

The ODRL Spatial Axis Profile resolves this by introducing
15 axis-specific left operands—three per affected base
term—operating entirely within ODRL’s constraint pattern
with no syntactic extensions and full backward compatibility.
All meta-theorems are mechanically verified in Isabelle/HOL.

Future work includes public release of the profile to the
W3C ODRL Community Group, integration with ODRL-SA for
design-time conflict detection, and validation on
production dataspace deployments.

Multiplicity free Weil representations arising from exceptional groups
Marcela Hanzer and Gordan Savin

Abstract
Abstract Using exceptional theta correspondences, we prove that certain Weil representations of p p -adic groups are multiplicity free and determine irreducible quotients.

Introduction

Let

p
-adic field. In this paper we consider a split simply connected group
\mathscr{G}
over

D_{4}<E_{6}<E_{7}<E_{8}.

Let

be a set of simple roots.
Let
\varphi:{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}\rightarrow\mathscr{G}
be the root
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}
corresponding to the highest root
\beta
.
Let

and
\bar{U}
be the groups of upper and lover triangular unipotent matrices in
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}
which, under
\varphi
,
are identified with roots spaces for
\beta
and
-\beta
. The co-root

is given
by

\beta^{\vee}(a)=\varphi\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&0\\
0&a^{-1}\end{array}\right).

There is a unique simple root

such that

Let

be the maximal parabolic
subgroup corresponding to
\alpha

is a Heisenberg group and

is its center.
Similarly, let
\bar{\mathscr{Q}}
be the opposite maximal parabolic, and
\bar{\mathscr{U}}
its radical. The intersection of
{\mathscr{Q}}
and

\bar{\mathscr{Q}}
is a standard Levi. Its center is the image of the co-root
\beta^{\vee}
. Let

be the derived group of the Levi. It is a simply

{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}
and vice versa.
Thus

Now observe that

is an irreducible symplectic representation of

with the (minuscule) highest weight

The isomorphism class of the pair

is given in the following table.

\begin{array}[]{c||c|c|c|c}\mathscr{G}&D_{4}&E_{6}&E_{7}&E_{8}\\
\hline\cr G&{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}^{3}&{{\mathrm{SL}}_{6}}&{\mathrm{Spin}}_{12}&E_{7}\\
\hline\cr V&V_{2}^{\otimes 3}&\wedge^{3}V_{6}&V_{32}&{V_{56}}\\
\end{array}.

Now fix an additive character
\psi
of
U

\Omega_{\psi}
be the unique irreducible representation
of
\mathscr{U}
with the central character
\psi
. By
[
KS90

the group

acts on
\Omega_{\psi}
without need to pass to a central extension.
This is the Weil representation in the title of this paper. We note that the pairs
(G,V)
appear in Table 1 of
[
Kno06
]

and decomposing

is a now a part of a program
[
BZSV
]
, see
[
MWZ24
]
and Conjecture 2.3 there, in particular.
In fact, Mao, Wan and Zhang study this problem using the relative trace formula, and indicate a possibility of the approach via minimal representations

[
MWZ24

. This is what we do in this paper.

More precisely, in Theorem

and classify

The analysis of

In words,

Theta correspondence for principal series

Let
B=TU
be a Borel subgroup of upper triangular matrices in
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}
. Let

be a diagonal matrix in
T
.
Then
\delta_{B}(t)^{1/2}=|a|
is the modular character. If
\chi

F^{\times}

{\mathrm{Ind}}_{B}^{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}}(\chi)
denote the normalized induced representation. The goal of this section is to (almost) compute big-theta for
constituents of principal series representations.

2.1
A degenerate principal series for
G

Let
(G,V)
be a pair as above, but not
({\mathrm{SL}}_{2}^{3},V_{2}^{\otimes 3}

\varpi
be the highest weight of
V
, and let

P=MN
be the maximal parabolic subgroup of

stabilizing the highest weight line.
Then

acts on the line by the character which we denote by
\varpi
,
a slight abuse of notation. The weight
\varpi
is a minuscule highest weight, in particular,

is abelian. Its dimension is
3+6r
where

in the three
cases. The cases are given by the following table, where in the last row we explain how

using the “standard” representations.

\begin{array}[]{c||c|c|c}\mathscr{G}&E_{6}&E_{7}&E_{8}\\
\hline\cr G&{{\mathrm{SL}}_{6}}&{\mathrm{Spin}}_{12}&E_{7}\\
\hline\cr M^{\mathrm{der}}&{\mathrm{SL}}_{3}\times{\mathrm{SL}}_{3}&{\mathrm{SL}}_{6}&E_{6}\\
\hline\cr N&V_{3}\otimes V^{\ast}_{3}&\wedge^{2}V_{6}&V_{27}\\
\end{array}.

Let
\chi
be a character of
F^{\times}
.
The composite

gives a character of

and a normalized degenerate principal series

. Reducibility points and composition series

[

]
. Observe that
{\mathrm{Ind}}_{P}^{G}(\chi^{-1})
is the contragredient of
{\mathrm{Ind}}_{P}^{G}(\chi)

Proposition 2.1
.

Let

, so that

. Then
{\mathrm{Ind}}_{P}^{G}(\chi)
is irreducible unless

•

\chi
is quadratic and non-trivial. Then
{\mathrm{Ind}}_{P}^{G}(\chi)
is a direct sum of two irreducible representations.

•

\chi=|\cdot|^{s}
where
\pm s=1,1+r,1+2r
, when

The trivial representation

.

2.2
Minimal representation

Let
\Pi
be the minimal representation of
\mathscr{G}
(
[

]
,
[
GS05
]

\Omega\subset V
be the
G

By

-modules

0\rightarrow C_{c}(\Omega)\rightarrow\Pi_{U}\rightarrow\Pi_{\mathscr{U}}\rightarrow 0

where

acts on

by

|a|^{2+2r}f(ax)

and

Furthermore,

On the two summands the torus

and

We shall now rewrite the above filtration for the normalized Jacquet functor

r_{B}(\Pi)=\delta_{B}^{-1/2}\cdot\Pi_{U}

Proposition 2.2
.

The normalized Jacquet functor

r_{B}(\Pi)=\delta_{B}^{-1/2}\cdot\Pi_{U}
, as a
T\times G
-module, has a filtration

•

with a quotient

r_{B}(\Pi_{\mathscr{U}})=\Pi[G]\otimes|a|^{1+r}\oplus 1\otimes|a|^{1+2r}

where
t\in T
, given as the diagonal matrix
(a,a^{-1})
, acts as indicated,

•

and a sub (normalized parabolic induction)

{\mathrm{Ind}}_{P\times T}^{G\times T}(C_{c}({\mathrm{GL}}_{1}))

where the action of
(t,m)\in T\times M
on
f\in C_{c}(GL(1))
is given by

f(\varpi(m)^{-1}ax).

Proof.

The first bullet is clear. For the second, we need to rewrite the bottom
\delta^{-1/2}_{B}\cdot C_{c}(\Omega)
as the induced representation.
Pick

so that the stabilizer of the line through

Now the second bullet follows from induction in stages, first to

It remains to check normalizations for the parabolic induction.
To that end, the modular character for

The action of

2.3
Computing the theta correspondence for principal series representations of
G
.

Recall that
{\mathrm{Ind}}_{B}^{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}}(\chi)

\chi=|\cdot|^{\pm 1}
or
\chi
is a non-trivial

The induced representation

Proposition 2.3
.

With the above conventions:

•

If
\pi={\mathrm{Ind}}_{B}^{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}}(\chi)
is irreducible, with
|\chi|

|\cdot|
, then
\Theta(\pi)={\mathrm{Ind}}_{P}^{G}(\chi)

•

\Theta(1_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}})
is a quotient of
{\mathrm{Ind}}_{P}^{G}(|\cdot|)

\Theta({\mathrm{St}})

{\mathrm{Ind}}_{P}^{G}(|\cdot|^{-1})

•

{\mathrm{Ind}}_{P}^{G}(\chi)

Proof.

We shall compute

\Theta(\pi)
.
Assume

{\mathrm{Ind}}_{B}^{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}}(\chi)

{\mathrm{Ind}}_{B}^{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}}(\chi^{-1})

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}}(\Pi,\pi)\subseteq{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}}(\Pi,{\mathrm{Ind}}_{B}^{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}}(\chi^{-1})),

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}}(\Pi,{\mathrm{Ind}}_{B}^{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}}(\chi^{-1}))\cong{\mathrm{Hom}}_{T}(r_{B}(\Pi),\chi^{-1}).

Now we use the two step filtration of

in Proposition
2.2

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{T}(r_{B}(\Pi_{\mathscr{U}}),\chi^{-1})\rightarrow{\mathrm{Hom}}_{T}(r_{B}(\Pi),\chi^{-1})\rightarrow{\mathrm{Hom}}_{T}({\mathrm{Ind}}_{P\times T}^{G\times T}(C_{c}({\mathrm{GL}}_{1})),\chi^{-1})\rightarrow\mathrm{Ext}_{T}(r_{B}({\Pi_{\mathscr{U}}}),\chi^{-1})

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{T}(r_{B}(\Pi),\chi^{-1})\cong{\mathrm{Hom}}_{T}({\mathrm{Ind}}_{P\times T}^{G\times T}(C_{c}({\mathrm{GL}}_{1})),\chi^{-1}).

Now observe that
(C_{c}({\mathrm{GL}}_{1})\otimes\chi)_{T}\cong\chi
, as

∎

Let

G

Let
\sigma

{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}

\Theta(\sigma)\neq 0

Proposition 3.2

Proof.

If

For the second part, we shall prove that the space of

We go from the bottom to top, starting with

Hence

For the second subquotient, observe that

4
Classical theta for
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}\times{\mathrm{Spin}}_{2n}

In this section we derive some results for classical theta correspondences that we shall need later.

We want to describe the classical theta correspondence for the pair
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}\times{\mathrm{Spin}}_{2n}.
To that end, we employ the Fourier-Jacobi functor (
[
BS12
]
,
[
Wei03
]
).
If there are several FJ–functors around -with respect to different groups, we add a subscript. In our setting, FJ-functor is an exact functor which sends a smooth representation of a group to a smooth representation of the next group in the appropriate FJ-tower (
[
HS20
]
). One of the most important properties is that it annuls only the trivial representation (e.g.
[
Wei03
]
). Assume that
2n\geq 6
, so
{\mathrm{Spin}}_{2n}
has a Heisenberg parabolic with Levi subgroup isomorphic to
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}\times{\mathrm{Spin}}_{2n-4}.

Let
FJ_{{\mathrm{Spin}}}
be the Fourier-Jacobi with respect to
{\mathrm{Spin}}_{2n}
. The output is a representation of
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}\times{\mathrm{Spin}}_{2n-4}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

The
FJ_{{\mathrm{Spin}}}
functor, applied to Weil representation for the dual pair
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}\times{\mathrm{Spin}}_{2n}
for
n\geq 3,

gives the regular representation of
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}
and the action of
{\mathrm{Spin}}_{2n-4}
is trivial.

Proof.

Let
\omega_{\psi}
be the Weil representation for the dual pair
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}\times{\mathrm{Spin}}_{2n}.
As before, we denote the underlying irreducible representation of the corresponding Heisenberg group in the same way. Sometimes, if there are several Heisenberg groups around, we add a dimension of the corresponding symplectic space to the notation, i.e.
\omega_{\psi}^{2n}.
Note that, by
[
KS90
, Proposition 2]
, we have

{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}\times{\mathrm{Spin}}_{2n}\to\widetilde{\mathrm{Sp}}(4n),

since, as explained, this group can be viewed as a Levi subgroup of the Heisenberg parabolic subgroup of
{\mathrm{Spin}}_{2n+4}.
We can use explicit formula for the action of the dual pair
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}\times{\mathrm{O}}_{2n},
as given in
[
Kud96
]
, but using ”the opposite” polarisation to one there. Namely, if
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}
acts on the symplectic space
W
and
V=X\oplus Y
is a complete polarisation of the split quadratic space of dimension
2n,
we realize
\omega_{\psi}
on the Schwarz space
S(X\otimes W)\cong S(W^{n}).
We want to determine
(\omega_{\psi})_{U,\psi},
where, as previously,
U
is the root subgroup of the highest root
\beta
of
{\mathrm{Spin}}_{2n}.
Thus,
(\omega_{\psi})_{U,\psi}
is
SL_{2}\times SL_{2}\times{\mathrm{Spin}}_{2n-4}
–module. To calculate it, we can consider
U
as a root space belonging to
O_{2n}
-this makes sense after taking the group of
F
-points and noting that
{\mathrm{Spin}}_{2n}
acts on
W\otimes V
by conjugation-as a part of the appropriate Levi subgroup.

Since
U
belongs to the unipotent radical of the Siegel parabolic subgroup, in
(\omega_{\psi},S(W^{n}))
it acts by homotety, cf. Chapter II, Section 4 of
[
Kud96
]
. In that case, one can compute
(\omega_{\psi})_{U,\psi},
as in Lemma 2.2 of
[
MS97
]
to get

S(W^{n})_{U,\psi}\cong S(W^{n-2}\times{\mathrm{SL}}_{2})\cong S(W^{n-2})\otimes S({\mathrm{SL}}_{2}).

Note that the action of the first
SL_{2}
(a member of a dual pair) in this realization is purely geometrical (by the left translation), and the second
SL_{2}
(part of the Levi subgroup of
{\mathrm{Spin}}_{2n}
) also acts geometrically-by the right translation, as a part of the corresponding Siegel parabolic subgroup (again Chapter II, Section 4 of
[
Kud96
]
). This means that we have have the regular representation of
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}\times{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}
on
S({\mathrm{SL}}_{2}).
On the other hand, on
S(W^{n-2})
we have the usual Weil representation of a dual pair
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}\times{\mathrm{O}}_{2n-4},
i.e.
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}\times{\mathrm{Spin}}_{2n-4},
as can be seen from the appropriate explicit formulas (we just removed two coordinate hyperbolic planes from the quadratic space). So,
S(W^{n-2})
carries an irreducible representation
\omega_{\psi}^{4n-8}.
The group
{\mathrm{O}}_{2n-4}
acts on
S({\mathrm{SL}}_{2})
by a character given in Proposition 4.3 of
[
Kud96
]
, so
{\mathrm{Spin}}_{2n-4}
acts trivially on it. Let
\mathscr{U}
be the Heisenberg parabolic subgroup of
{\mathrm{Spin}}_{2n}.
We have

\displaystyle FJ(\omega_{\psi})=FJ(\omega_{\psi}^{4n})={\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathscr{U}}(\omega_{\psi}^{4n-8},(\omega_{\psi}^{4n})_{U,\psi})={\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathscr{U}}(\omega_{\psi}^{4n-8},S(W^{n-2})\otimes S({\mathrm{SL}}_{2}))\cong

\displaystyle{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\omega_{\psi}^{4n-8},\omega_{\psi}^{4n-8})\otimes S({\mathrm{SL}}_{2}).

∎

Corollary 4.2
.

If
\sigma
is an irreducible representation of

{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}
and
\Theta_{\psi}(\sigma)
the big theta lift to
{\mathrm{Spin}}_{2n}
, then
FJ_{{\mathrm{Spin}}_{2n}}(\Theta_{\psi}(\sigma))
is an irreducible representation of
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}\times{\mathrm{Spin}}_{2n-4}
,

\sigma
on
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}
, and trivial on the
{\mathrm{Spin}}_{2n-4}
– factor.

We are able to now completely describe the theta correspondence in question. In order to do that, we note the following. The group
{\mathrm{Spin}}_{2n}
has a standard parabolic subgroup
P=MN
with
N
commutative and
M
of semisimple type
D_{n-1}.
There is a rational character
\omega
of
M/[M,M]
described in the second section of
[
HS20
]
; we can compose it with the character
\chi_{u}|\cdot|^{s},
where
\chi_{u}
is a unitary character of
F^{*}
and
s
is real. Let

I(s,\chi_{u})={\mathrm{Ind}}_{P}^{{\mathrm{Spin}}_{2n}}(\chi_{u}|\cdot|^{s}\circ\omega),

the normalized induction. Note that
I(s,\chi_{u})
is reducible if and only if
\chi_{u}^{2}=1,\chi_{u}\neq 1
and
s=0
or
\chi_{u}=1
and
s\in\{\pm 1,\pm(n-1)\};
in the case of reducibility, the length of the representation is two (cf. Theorem 3.2 of
[
HS20
]
). If
\chi_{u}=1
the unique irreducible quotient of
I(1,1)
is the minimal representation and of
I(n-1,1)
the trivial representation. We denote
J(s,\chi_{u})={\mathrm{Ind}}_{B}^{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}}\chi_{u}|\cdot|^{s}.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\sigma
be an irreducible representation of
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}.
Then its full lift
\Theta_{\psi}(\sigma)
to
{\mathrm{Spin}}_{2n}
is irreducible except when
\sigma=J(n-1,1).
In more detail, the following holds:

1.

\Theta_{\psi}(J(s,\chi_{u}))=I(s,\chi_{u})

2.

\Theta_{\psi}(1_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}})
is the unique irreducible quotient of
I(1,1),
i.e. the minimal representation of
{\mathrm{Spin}}_{2n}.

3.

\Theta_{\psi}({\mathrm{St}}_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}})
is the unique irreducible subrepresentation of
I(1,1).

4.

\Theta_{\psi}(\pi_{\chi}^{+})\oplus\Theta_{\psi}(\pi_{\chi}^{-})=I(0,\chi_{u});
here

\pi_{\chi}^{\pm}
are irreducible tempered representations of
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}
described in
Section
2.3
.

5.

If
\sigma
is supercuspidal, the full lift is an irreducible representation whose Langlands data is easier to explain when viewed as a lift to
{\mathrm{O}}_{2n},
cf. Theorem 6.7 of
[
BH21
]
.

If
\pi
is an irreducible representation of
{\mathrm{Spin}}_{2n}
which has a non-zero lift to
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}
–the list of those follows from the previous claims in this lemma-then its full lift
\Theta_{\psi}(\pi)
is an irreducible representation of
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}.

Proof.

First we cover the case of the principal series representations of
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}.

First assume that
\sigma=J(s,\chi_{u})
is irreducible. Then,
\Theta_{\psi}(\sigma)=I(s,\chi_{u}).
Indeed, using standard techniques of Kudla’s filtration (e.g. Corollary 5.3. of
[
AG17
]
) together with Corollary
4.2
, we get that

\Theta_{\psi}(\sigma)=\Theta_{\psi}(J(s,\chi_{u}))
is a non-zero quotient of
I(s,\chi_{u})
and the latter representation is irreducible in all the cases in which
J(s,\chi_{u})
is irreducible except in the case
s=\pm(n-1)
and
\chi_{u}=1.
Then, we get that the small theta lift of
J(n-1,1)
is the trivial representation of
{\mathrm{Spin}}_{2n}.
But, in that case, if we would have
\Theta_{\psi}(J(n-1,1))=\theta_{\psi}(J(n-1,\chi_{u}))=1_{{\mathrm{Spin}}_{2n}},
we would get contradiction with Corollary
4.2
, because
FJ_{{\mathrm{Spin}}_{2n}}(1_{{\mathrm{Spin}}_{2n}})=0.
Thus,
\Theta_{\psi}(J(n-1,1))=I(n-1,1).

The relation
\Theta_{\psi}(\pi_{\chi}^{+})\oplus\Theta_{\psi}(\pi_{\chi}^{-})=I(0,\chi_{u})
follows immediately as in the previous discussion.

Analogously, if
\sigma=1_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}},
its full theta lift to
{\mathrm{O}}_{2n}
is an irreducible representation (the Kepler representation), which is not a character, so that the lift
\Theta_{\psi}(1_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}})
to
{\mathrm{Spin}}_{2n}
does not have the trivial subquotient. Then, Corollary
4.2
guarantees that
\Theta_{\psi}(1_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}})
is an irreducible quotient of
I(1,1),
thus the minimal representation. In the same way,
\Theta_{\psi}({\mathrm{St}}_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}})
to
{\mathrm{Spin}}_{2n}
is irreducible, because its full lift to
{\mathrm{O}}_{2n}
does not have a subquotient which is a character, because of its cuspidal support (cf.
[
Mui08
]
), so that, again, the lift
\Theta_{\psi}({\mathrm{St}}_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}})
to
{\mathrm{Spin}}_{2n}
does not have the trivial subquotient. This means that
\Theta_{\psi}({\mathrm{St}}_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}})
is the unique subrepresentation of
I(1,1).

If
\sigma
is a cuspidal representation, its full lift to
{\mathrm{O}}_{2n}
is irreducible and is not a character (we have an explicit description of it in terms of the Langlands data, cf.
[
BH21
]
); thus
\Theta_{\psi}(\sigma)
does not have the trivial subquotient and so is irreducible.

Now, we want to prove the opposite direction; namely, if
\pi
is an irreducible representation of
{\mathrm{Spin}}_{2n}
such that
\Theta_{\psi}(\pi),
its theta lift to
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}
is non-zero, then
\Theta_{\psi}(\pi)
is irreducible.

First, assume that
\pi\neq 1_{{\mathrm{Spin}}_{2n}}
is an irreducible representation of
{\mathrm{Spin}}_{2n}
which appears in theta correspondence with
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}
so that

\omega_{\psi}^{4n}\twoheadrightarrow\pi\otimes\Theta_{\psi}(\pi),

where
\Theta_{\psi}(\pi)
is a non-zero representation of
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}.
Since
\Theta_{\psi}(\pi)
is of finite length, there exists an irreducible quotient
\xi
, so that, by Lemma
4.1
,

S(SL_{2})\twoheadrightarrow FJ_{{\mathrm{Spin}}_{2n}}(\pi)\otimes\Theta_{\psi}(\pi)\twoheadrightarrow FJ_{{\mathrm{Spin}}_{2n}}(\pi)\otimes\xi;

this means
FJ_{{\mathrm{Spin}}_{2n}}(\pi)=\xi
and, in, turn,
\Theta_{\psi}(\pi)=\xi.
Thus,
\Theta_{\psi}(\pi)
is irreducible.

If
\pi=1_{{\mathrm{Spin}}_{2n}}
, then we know that it occurs in the theta correspondence with
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2},

\theta_{\psi}(1_{{\mathrm{Spin}}_{2n}})=J(n-1,1).
On the other hand, every other subquotient of
\Theta_{\psi}(1_{{\mathrm{Spin}}_{2n}})
would have the same cuspidal support as

\theta_{\psi}(1_{{\mathrm{Spin}}_{2n}}),
but
J(n-1,1)
is irreducible; thus
\Theta_{\psi}(1_{{\mathrm{Spin}}_{2n}})=J(n-1,1).

∎

\Pi

\mathscr{G}

The above corollary allows us to prove the following set of results:

Proposition 5.3

Let
\sigma

By Proposition

does not contain the trivial representation of

Since, by Lemma

∎

Next, we finish the description

Let

6
Lift from
G
to
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}

Recall that we have the dual pair
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}\times G
. Let
\pi
be an irreducible representation of

{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}
by the usual
\Theta(\pi)=(\Pi\otimes\pi^{\vee})_{G}

\Theta(\pi)\neq 0

has finite length. This clearly implies that it has
an irreducible
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}
-quotient

\pi
is the lift of
\sigma

\Theta(\pi)
is
always irreducible (if non-zero) and then use this to prove that
\Omega_{\psi}
is multiplicity free
G

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\pi
be an irreducible representation of
G
. Then
\Theta(\pi)
is a finite length
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}
-module.

Proof.

If
\sigma
is a supercuspidal summand of
\Theta(\pi)
, then
\pi\otimes\sigma
is a quotient of the minimal representation,
and by Proposition
5.3
,
\pi=\Theta(\sigma)
. Since lifting from
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}
to

is one to one,

\sigma
is the unique supercuspidal summand of
\Theta(\pi)
. Now, to finish the proof, it suffices to show that

is finite
dimensional, where

is the Borel subgroup of
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}
.
To that end, observe that

, hence it suffices to show that

is finite dimensional. Now recall, from Proposition
2.2
, the filtration of

, where the top is a finite length
G
-module,
a direct sum of trivial and minimal representation, while the large bottom piece is

{\mathrm{Ind}}_{P}^{G}(C_{c}({\mathrm{GL}}_{1}))

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G}({\mathrm{Ind}}_{P}^{G}(C_{c}({\mathrm{GL}}_{1})),\pi)\cong{\mathrm{Hom}}_{M}(C_{c}({\mathrm{GL}}_{1}),r_{\bar{P}}(\pi)).

Recall that

on

so the dimension of the right hand side is bounded by the number of

has a finite length, as an

-module.
∎

We remark, if
\pi=1_{G}
is the trivial representation of

, then the above argument gives

\dim{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G}(r_{B}(\Pi),1_{G})\leq 2.

Corollary 6.2
.

Let
\pi
be an irreducible representation of
G
. Then
\Theta(\pi)\neq 0
if and only if
\pi
is a lift of an irreducible representation
\sigma
of
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
6.1
,
\Theta(\pi)
has finite length. Hence it has an irreducible quotient
\sigma
. Thus
\pi
is a lift of
\sigma
.
∎

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
\pi
be a lift of an irreducible representation
\sigma
of
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}
. Then
\Theta(\pi)\cong\sigma
.

Proof.

If
\pi=1_{G}
we proved above that

is at most 2-dimensional. On the other hand,
1_{G}
is the
lift of an irreducible principal series representation
\sigma
of
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}
, hence
\Theta(1_{G})\cong\sigma
as there is no room for anything else.
For the general case we need the following.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
\pi
the unique irreducible quotient of
\Theta(\sigma)
. If

then

is the unique irreducible
quotient of the classical theta lift

to

Since

\Theta(\sigma)=\pi

is the minimal representation of

∎

Assume

Thus now we can analyze the Weil representation

∎

7
Mini theta

Let
V
be the standard (symplectic) 2-dimensional representation of
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(F)
, realized as the set of
2\times 1
column vectors.
Now we consider the
n
-fold tensor product
V\otimes\cdots\otimes V
. This a faithful representation of the group

S_{n}={\mathrm{SL}}_{2}^{n}/\mu_{2}^{n-1}

where
\mu_{2}^{n-1}
is the subgroup of the center of
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}^{n}
, consisting of
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
such that
x_{1}\cdots x_{n}=1
.
In order to understand the corresponding group of
F
-points, observe that on
V\otimes\cdots\otimes V
we have a faithful action of

G_{n}={\mathrm{GL}}_{2}^{n}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}^{n-1}

where
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}^{n-1}
is the subgroup of the center of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}^{n}
consisting of
n
-tuples
(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
of scalar matrices such that

z_{1}\cdots z_{n}=1
. The group
G_{n}(F)
is given by the same quotient, by Hilbert 90. If
\pi
is an irreducible representation of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(F)
then

\pi\otimes\ldots\otimes\pi
(tensor
n
-times) is naturally a representation of
G_{n}(F)
.

Let
\det
be the character of
G_{n}
that restricts to the usual determinant on each
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}
. Then
S_{n}
is the kernel of the determinant, this
is an easy check over algebraic closure of
F
. This description gives as the following inductive construction of
S_{n}
, which will play an important role.
Let

(G_{n-1}\times{\mathrm{GL}}_{2})^{\det}=\{(g,h)~|~\det g\cdot\det h=1\}.

Let
\Delta{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
be embedding of
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
in the center of the above group given by

z\mapsto((z,1,\ldots,1),z^{-1})=(1,z,\ldots,1),z^{-1})\ldots
. Then

S_{n}\cong(G_{n-1}\times{\mathrm{GL}}_{2})^{\det}/\Delta{\mathbb{G}}_{m}

and this is true on the level of
F
-points. Thus any smooth
S_{n}
-module
\Omega
can be viewed as an
(G_{n-1}\times{\mathrm{GL}}_{2})^{\det}
-module. Let

\pi
be an irreducible representation of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(F)
. Let
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}\subset(G_{n-1}\times{\mathrm{GL}}_{2})^{\det}
, sitting in the second factor.
Observe that
(G_{n-1}\times{\mathrm{GL}}_{2})^{\det}/{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}\cong G_{n-1}
. Thus

\Theta(\pi)=(\Omega\otimes\pi^{\vee})_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}}

is naturally a smooth
G_{n-1}
-module. In the following three subsections, for

we shall exhibit

such that

\Theta(\pi)=\pi\otimes\ldots\otimes\pi
for all irreducible Whittaker generic
\pi
.

7.1
Two
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}

In this case
S_{2}={\mathrm{SL}}_{2}\times{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}/\mu_{2}\cong{\mathrm{SO}}(4)
. Indeed,

V\otimes V
is an orthogonal representation of
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}\times{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}/\mu_{2}

of

by the map

is the transpose of

into a quadratic space.
The action of
(g,h)\in({\mathrm{GL}}_{2}\times{\mathrm{GL}}_{2})^{\det}

. This gives
as isomorphism
({\mathrm{GL}}_{2}\times{\mathrm{GL}}_{2})^{\det}/\Delta{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\cong{\mathrm{SO}}(4)

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
\pi
be an irreducible representation of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(F)
. Then

(C_{c}({\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(F))\otimes\pi^{\vee})_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}}\cong\pi

as
({\mathrm{GL}}_{2}\times{\mathrm{GL}}_{2})^{\det}/{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}\cong{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}
-modules.

We have a natural
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}
-intertwining map

(C_{c}({\mathrm{SL}}_{2})\otimes\pi^{\vee})_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}}\rightarrow\pi

(f,v)\mapsto\pi(f)v
. To prove that this is bijective, restrict
\pi=\pi_{1}+\cdots+\pi_{s}
to

, and use

(C_{c}({\mathrm{SL}}_{2})\otimes\pi^{\vee}_{i})_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}}\cong\pi_{i}

for any irreducible representation

of
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}
.

∎

7.2
Three
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}

Observe that

is naturally a symplectic space. Hence

{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}\times{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}\times{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}/\mu_{2}^{2}\subset{\mathrm{Sp}}_{8}

Fix an additive character

of

. It gives us

for

Decomposing

We realize

Namely, if

[

From
[
MS97
, Lemma 2.2]
and the description of the action of

(\omega_{\psi})_{U,\psi}\cong C_{c}({\mathrm{SL}}_{2})

.

Let
\pi
be an irreducible, Whittaker generic, representation of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(F)

\Theta(\pi):=(\omega_{\psi}\otimes\pi^{\vee})_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}}\cong\pi\otimes\pi

Let
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}
be one of two in
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}

Without loss of generality we can take the Schroedinger model of

Since

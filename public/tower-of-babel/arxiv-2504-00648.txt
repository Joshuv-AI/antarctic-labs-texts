A posteriori error analysis of a virtual element method for stress-assisted diffusion problems
Franco Dassi, Rekha Khot, Andrés E. Rubiano, Ricardo Ruiz-Baier

Abstract
We develop and analyse residual-based a posteriori error estimates for the virtual element discretisation of a nonlinear stress-assisted diffusion problem in two and three dimensions. The model problem involves a two-way coupling between elasticity and diffusion equations in perturbed saddle-point form. A robust global inf-sup condition and Helmholtz decomposition for 𝐇 ⁡ ( div , Ω ) \mathbf{H}(\mathrm{div},\Omega) lead to a reliable and efficient error estimator based on appropriately weighted norms that ensure parameter robustness. The a posteriori error analysis uses novel quasi-interpolation operators for Stokes and edge virtual element spaces, and we include the proofs of such operators with estimates in 3D for completeness. Finally, we present numerical experiments in both 2D and 3D to demonstrate the optimal performance of the proposed error estimator.

Introduction

The physical interaction between solute diffusion, driven by chemical reactions, in a deformable medium was first formally addressed in
Unger and Aifantis (1983)
;
Wilson and Aifantis (1982)
known as the stress-assisted diffusion model. This phenomenon appears in a variety of scientific, medical, and engineering applications such as the lithiation of ion batteries
Taralov (2015)
, cardiac tissue electromechanics
Loppini et al. (2018)
, semiconductor fabrication
Shaw (2007)
, biomechanics of brain tissue
Prevost et al. (2011)
, among others. These applications operate across multiple scales,
and motivating the need of robust models. The mathematical analysis and discretisation of this model have been conducted using mixed-primal and mixed-mixed finite element methods (FEM) in Hilbert spaces
Gatica et al. (2018)
, pseudo-stress-based formulations using Banach spaces
Gatica et al. (2022b)
, and well-posedness results for a primal formulation
Lewicka and Mucha (2016)
;
Malaeke and Asghari (2023)
. In the virtual element method (VEM) framework, this model was first studied in
Khot et al. (2025)
, which introduced a robust mixed-mixed formulation with parameter-weighted norms ensuring the unique solvability of two uncoupled systems across different parameter scales (i.e. robustness is achieved), along with a fixed-point argument establishing well-posedness for the fully coupled equations. Our goal here is to derive an adaptive mesh refinement strategy driven by a posteriori error estimators for this type of formulations, and extend reliability and efficiency results to the parameter-robust setting.

Adaptive schemes driven by a posteriori error estimators allow optimal convergence recovery in cases such as singular solutions, rough data, and complex geometries (e.g., non-convex domains and sharp corners). The key advantage of the VEM framework in adaptive algorithms is its natural handling of hanging nodes during mesh refinement. However, several persistent challenges remain, such as developing open-source implementations for polytopal conforming mesh refinement
Antonietti et al. (2022)
.
The FEM literature on a posteriori error analysis of this problem can be found in
Gatica et al. (2022a)
. On the other hand, a posteriori error estimates for VEM were first introduced in
Cangiani et al. (2017)
, and the extension to displacement-based deformation models and reaction-diffusion equations using VEM can be found in
Wang et al. (2020)
;
Munar et al. (2024)
.

To the best of the authors’ knowledge, the present paper is the first one addressing robust a posteriori error estimators for VEM applied to stress-assisted diffusion problems both in 2D and 3D. We first establish residual-based error estimators in 2D and prove the reliability and efficiency under a small data assumption due to the Banach fixed-point argument. Here, we use classical tools such as quasi-interpolation operators, polynomial projections, a Helmholtz decomposition, and the definition of bubble functions to achieve our purpose. Further, we provide “building blocks” for the 3D case. Specifically, we prove novel quasi-interpolation operator for both Stokes- and edge-like virtual element spaces in 3D, together with a stable Helmholtz decomposition for reaction-diffusion equations (in mixed VEM). The associated estimators are implemented in the
VEM++
library
Dassi (2025)
(available upon request) with an extensible design, enabling the incorporation of various a posteriori error estimators for the virtual element (VE) spaces available in the library.

2
The stress-assisted diffusion problem

This section recalls from
Khot et al. (2025)

the weak formulation and its well-posedness analysis based on the Babuška–Brezzi–Braess theory and a fixed-point argument.

Model problem.

Let
\Omega
be a polytopal (polygonal in 2D and polyhedral in 3D) bounded domain with boundary
\Gamma=\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}\cup\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}
, such that
\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}\cap\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}=\emptyset
, and consider the following coupled PDE with mixed boundary conditions

\displaystyle-\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(2\mu\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u})-{p}\mathbb{I})=\boldsymbol{f},

\displaystyle\quad\text{in $\Omega$},\quad\boldsymbol{u}=\mathbf{0},

\displaystyle\quad\text{on $\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}$},

(2.1a)

\displaystyle{p}=-\lambda\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{u}\,+\ell(\varphi),

\displaystyle\quad\text{in $\Omega$},\quad(2\mu\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u})-{p}\mathbb{I})\boldsymbol{n}=\boldsymbol{0},

\displaystyle\quad\text{on $\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}$},

(2.1b)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\zeta}=\mathbb{M}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}),{p})\nabla\varphi,

\displaystyle\quad\text{in $\Omega$},\quad\varphi=\varphi_{\mathrm{D}},

\displaystyle\quad\text{on $\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}$},

(2.1c)

\displaystyle\theta\varphi-\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{\zeta})=g,

\displaystyle\quad\text{in $\Omega$},\quad\boldsymbol{\zeta}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}=0,

\displaystyle\quad\text{on $\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}$}.

(2.1d)

The coupling
uses an active stress approach defining the total Cauchy stress as
\boldsymbol{\sigma}=2\mu\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u})-{p}\mathbb{I}
, where

\boldsymbol{u}
is the displacement vector,
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u})
is the tensor of infinitesimal strains,
{p}
denotes a Herrmann-type pressure,
\mathbb{I}
denotes the identity tensor in
\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
,
\varphi
is the solute’s concentration,
\mu
and
\lambda
are the Lamé parameters of the solid,
\boldsymbol{f}
is a vector of external body loads,
\ell
modulates the (isotropic) active stress.
On the other hand,

\boldsymbol{\zeta}
is the diffusive flux
\mathbb{M}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}),{p})
is the stress-assisted diffusion coefficient (assumed uniformly bounded away from zero),
\theta
is a model parameter that lies in
[0,1]
, and
g
is a given net volumetric source of solute.

Assumptions on the nonlinear terms.

We suppose that
\ell:\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)\to\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
satisfies

\lVert\ell(\vartheta)\rVert_{0,\Omega}\lesssim\lVert\vartheta\rVert_{0,\Omega}
for all
\vartheta\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
.
We also assume that
\mathbb{M}(\cdot,\cdot)
is invertible, symmetric, positive semi-definite and uniformly bounded in
\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)
(likewise for
\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\cdot,\cdot)
). In addition, for all
\boldsymbol{w}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega),r\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)\text{ and }\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, there exists
M\geq 1
such that

{M}^{-1}\boldsymbol{x}\cdot\boldsymbol{y}\leq\boldsymbol{x}\cdot[\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{w}),r)\boldsymbol{y}]
and

\boldsymbol{y}\cdot[\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{w}),r)\boldsymbol{x}]\leq M\boldsymbol{x}\cdot\boldsymbol{y}
.
Finally, we assume that
\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\cdot,\cdot)
and
\ell(\cdot)
are Lipschitz continuous with Lipschitz constants
L_{\mathbb{M}}
and
L_{\ell}
. Examples of these terms can be found in
Cherubini et al. (2017)
for the stress-assisted diffusion and
Murray (2003)
;
Taralov (2015)
for the active stress.

2.1
Weak formulation

In view of the boundary conditions, we define the Hilbert spaces

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{D}}(\Omega):=\{\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega):\boldsymbol{v}=\boldsymbol{0}\quad\text{on }\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}\},\quad\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{N}}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega):=\{\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbf{H}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega):\boldsymbol{\xi}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}=0\quad\text{on }\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}\}.

With them, the weak form consists in, given
\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
,
g\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
, and
\varphi_{\mathrm{D}}\in\text{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}})
, finding

(\boldsymbol{u},{p},\boldsymbol{\zeta},\varphi)\in\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{D}}^{1}(\Omega)\times\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)\times\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{N}}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)\times\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle a_{1}(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v})+b_{1}(\boldsymbol{v},{p})=F_{1}(\boldsymbol{v}),\qquad

\displaystyle\forall\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{D}}^{1}(\Omega),

(2.2a)

\displaystyle b_{1}(\boldsymbol{u},{q})-\frac{1}{\lambda}c_{1}({p},{q})=G_{1}^{\varphi}({q}),\qquad

\displaystyle\forall{q}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega),

(2.2b)

\displaystyle a_{2}^{\boldsymbol{u},{p}}(\boldsymbol{\zeta},\boldsymbol{\xi})+b_{2}(\boldsymbol{\xi},\varphi)=F_{2}(\boldsymbol{\xi}),\qquad

\displaystyle\forall\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{N}}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega),

(2.2c)

\displaystyle b_{2}(\boldsymbol{\zeta},\psi)-\theta c_{2}(\varphi,\psi)=G_{2}(\psi),\qquad

\displaystyle\forall\psi\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega).

(2.2d)

The bilinear forms
a_{1}:\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{D}}^{1}(\Omega)\times\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{D}}^{1}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
,
b_{1}:\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{D}}^{1}(\Omega)\times\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
,

c_{1}:\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)\times\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
,
a_{2}^{\boldsymbol{u},p}:\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{N}}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)\times\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{N}}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
,
b_{2}:\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{N}}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)\times\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
,
c_{2}:\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)\times\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
, and linear functionals
F_{1}:\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{D}}^{1}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
,
G_{1}^{\varphi}:\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
,
F_{2}:\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{N}}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
,
G_{2}:\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
, are

\displaystyle a_{1}(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}):=2\mu\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}):\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{v}),\quad b_{1}(\boldsymbol{v},{q}):=-\int_{\Omega}{q}\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{v},\quad c_{1}({p},{q}):=\int_{\Omega}{p}{q},

\displaystyle F_{1}(\boldsymbol{v}):=\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{f}\cdot\boldsymbol{v},\quad G_{1}^{\varphi}({q}):=-\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{\Omega}\ell(\varphi){q},

\displaystyle a_{2}^{\boldsymbol{u},p}(\boldsymbol{\zeta},\boldsymbol{\xi}):=\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}),p)\boldsymbol{\zeta}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi},\quad b_{2}(\boldsymbol{\xi},\psi):=\int_{\Omega}\psi\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\xi},\quad c_{2}(\varphi,\psi):=\int_{\Omega}\varphi\psi,

\displaystyle F_{2}(\boldsymbol{\xi}):=\langle\varphi_{\mathrm{D}},\boldsymbol{\xi}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}\rangle_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}},\quad G_{2}(\psi):=-\int_{\Omega}g\psi.

2.2
Parameter-dependent norms and robust solvability

Given
K\subseteq\Omega
, let
\mathbf{V}_{1}(K):=\mathbf{H}^{1}(K)
,
\mathrm{Q}_{1}(K):=\mathrm{L}^{2}(K)
,
\mathbf{V}_{2}(K):=\mathbf{H}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,K)
and
\mathrm{Q}_{2}:=\mathrm{L}^{2}(K)
be the displacement, Herrmann pressure, flux, and concentration spaces on
K
, respectively, equipped with the following weighted norms and semi-norms

\displaystyle\lVert(\boldsymbol{u},{p})\rVert_{\mathbf{V}_{1}(K)\times\mathrm{Q}_{1}(K)}^{2}:=\lVert\boldsymbol{u}\rVert_{\mathbf{V}_{1}(K)}^{2}+\lVert{p}\rVert_{\mathrm{Q}_{1}(K)}^{2},\;\lVert\boldsymbol{u}\rVert_{\mathbf{V}_{1}(K)}^{2}:=2\mu\lVert\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u})\rVert_{0,K}^{2},

\displaystyle\lVert{p}\rVert_{\mathrm{Q}_{1}(K)}^{2}:=\left(\frac{1}{2\mu}+\frac{1}{\lambda}\right)\lVert{p}\rVert_{0,K}^{2},

\displaystyle|\boldsymbol{u}|_{\mathbf{V}_{1}(K)}^{2}:=2\mu|\boldsymbol{u}|_{1,K}^{2},\;|{p}|_{\mathrm{Q}_{1}(K)}^{2}:=\frac{1}{2\mu}\lVert p\rVert_{0,K}^{2},

\displaystyle\lVert(\boldsymbol{\zeta},\varphi)\rVert_{\mathbf{V}_{2}(K)\times\mathrm{Q}_{2}(K)}^{2}:=\lVert\boldsymbol{\zeta}\rVert_{\mathbf{V}_{2}(K)}^{2}+\lVert\varphi\rVert_{\mathrm{Q}_{2}(K)}^{2},\;\lVert\boldsymbol{\zeta}\rVert_{\mathbf{V}_{2}(K)}^{2}:=\lVert\boldsymbol{\zeta}\rVert_{\mathbb{M},K}^{2}+M\lVert\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\zeta}\rVert_{0,K}^{2},

\displaystyle\lVert\boldsymbol{\zeta}\rVert_{\mathbb{M},K}^{2}:=\int_{K}\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}),p)\boldsymbol{\zeta}\cdot\boldsymbol{\zeta},\;\lVert\varphi\rVert_{\mathrm{Q}_{2}(K)}^{2}:=\left(\frac{1}{M}+\theta\right)\lVert\varphi\rVert_{0,K}^{2},

\displaystyle|\boldsymbol{\zeta}|_{\mathbf{V}_{2}(K)}^{2}:=M|\boldsymbol{\zeta}|_{1,K}^{2},\;|\varphi|_{\mathrm{Q}_{2}(K)}^{2}:=\frac{1}{M}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{0,K}^{2}.

Note that the definition of
|\boldsymbol{\zeta}|_{\mathbf{V}_{2}(K)}^{2}
requires
\boldsymbol{\zeta}\in\mathbf{V}_{2}(K)\cap\mathbf{H}^{1}(K)
. For simplicity, we denote the global spaces (i.e.,
K=\Omega
) as
\mathbf{V}_{1}:=\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{D}}^{1}(\Omega),\mathrm{Q}_{1}=\mathrm{Q}_{2}:=\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega),\mathbf{V}_{2}:=\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{N}}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)
while imposing the boundary conditions.

Theorem
2.1
below establishes the well-posedness of the fully coupled system (
2.2
) under the small data assumption

C_{1}L_{\ell}\max\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\mu}},\sqrt{2\mu}\right\}M^{2}C_{2}^{2}L_{\mathbb{M}}\left(\lVert\varphi_{\mathrm{D}}\rVert_{\frac{1}{2},\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}}+\lVert g\rVert_{0,\Omega}\right)<1.

(2.3)

We recall that this condition arises only from the fixed-point treatment of the nonlinearity; the method’s robustness is guaranteed by the weighted norm spaces (see
Braess (1996)
) and the mixed formulation introduced before, for a detailed proof we refer to

provides a robust global inf-sup condition. The proof follows from the Brezzi–Braess conditions in

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose that
1\leq\lambda
,
0<\mu
,
\theta\leq\frac{1}{M}
, and that the small data assumption (
2.3
) holds.
Then, there exists an unique solution
(\boldsymbol{u},{p},\boldsymbol{\zeta},\varphi)\in\mathbf{V}_{1}\times\mathrm{Q}_{1}\times\mathbf{V}_{2}\times\mathrm{Q}_{2}
to (
2.2
) such that

\displaystyle\lVert(\boldsymbol{u},{p})\rVert_{\mathbf{V}_{1}\times\mathrm{Q}_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\left(\lVert F_{1}\rVert_{\mathbf{V}^{\prime}_{1}}+\lVert G^{\varphi}_{1}\rVert_{Q^{\prime}_{1}}\right),

\displaystyle\lVert(\boldsymbol{\zeta},\varphi)\rVert_{\mathbf{V}_{2}\times\mathrm{Q}_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\left(\lVert F_{2}\rVert_{\mathbf{V}^{\prime}_{2}}+\lVert G_{2}\rVert_{Q^{\prime}_{2}}\right),

where the corresponding constants

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
(V,\lVert\cdot\rVert_{V})
and
(Q_{b},\lVert\cdot\rVert_{Q_{b}})
be Hilbert spaces,
let
Q
be a dense (with respect to
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{Q_{b}}
) linear subspace of
Q_{b}
and three bilinear forms
a(\cdot,\cdot)
on
V\times V
(continuous, symmetric and positive semi-definite),
b(\cdot,\cdot)
on
V\times Q_{b}
(continuous), and
c(\cdot,\cdot)
on
Q\times Q
(symmetric and positive semi-definite); defining the linear operators
A:V\rightarrow V^{\prime}
,
B:V\rightarrow Q^{\prime}_{b}
and
C:Q\rightarrow Q^{\prime}
, respectively. For
t\in[0,1]
, consider the
t
-dependent energy norm

\lVert(v,q)\rVert_{V\times Q}^{2}:=\lVert v\rVert_{V}^{2}+\lVert q\rVert_{Q}^{2}=\lVert v\rVert_{V}^{2}+\lVert q\rVert_{Q_{b}}^{2}+t^{2}|q|_{c}^{2}.

Assume that
Q
is complete with respect to the norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{Q}^{2}:=\lVert\cdot\rVert_{Q_{b}}^{2}+t^{2}|\cdot|_{c}^{2}
, where
|\cdot|_{c}^{2}:=c(\cdot,\cdot)
is a semi-norm in
Q
. Suppose further that there exist positive constants
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
(independent of the model parameters) such that

\displaystyle\alpha\lVert\hat{v}\rVert_{V}^{2}\leq a(\hat{v},\hat{v}),\qquad

\displaystyle\forall\hat{v}\in\mathrm{Ker}(B),

(2.4a)

\displaystyle\beta\lVert q\rVert_{Q_{b}}\leq\sup_{v\in V}\frac{b(v,q)}{{\lVert v\rVert}_{V}},\qquad

\displaystyle\forall q\in Q_{b},

(2.4b)

\displaystyle\gamma\lVert u\rVert_{V}\leq\sup_{(v,q)\in V\times Q}\frac{a(u,v)+b(u,q)}{\lVert(v,q)\rVert_{V\times Q}},\qquad

\displaystyle\forall u\in V.

(2.4c)

Then, the multilinear form
\mathcal{A}(u,p;v,q):=a(u,v)+b(v,p)+b(u,q)-t^{2}c(p,q)
satisfies the following condition

\displaystyle\lVert(u,p)\rVert_{V\times Q}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\frac{4+4\lVert a\rVert}{\gamma}+\frac{2}{\beta}+\left(\frac{1+\lVert a\rVert}{\gamma}\right)^{2}\right)\textbf{}\sup_{(v,q)\in V\times Q}\frac{\mathcal{A}(u,p;v,q)}{\lVert(v,q)\rVert_{V\times Q}},\quad\forall(u,p)\in V\times Q.

Proof.

The triangle inequality and the second Brezzi condition (
2.4b
) lead to

\displaystyle\lVert(u,p)\rVert_{V\times Q}\leq\lVert u\rVert_{V}+\frac{1}{\beta}\sup_{v\in V}\frac{\mathcal{A}(u,p;v,0)-a(u,v)}{{\lVert v\rVert}_{V}}+t|p|_{c}.

Note that
t|p|_{c}\leq\left(a(u,u)+t^{2}c(p,p)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\left(\mathcal{A}(u,p;u,-p)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
, which together with the continuity of
a(\cdot,\cdot)
yields

\displaystyle\lVert(u,p)\rVert_{V\times Q}\leq\left(1+\lVert a\rVert\right)\lVert u\rVert_{V}+\frac{1}{\beta}\sup_{(v,q)\in V\times Q}\frac{\mathcal{A}(u,p;v,q)}{{\lVert(v,q)\rVert_{V\times Q}}}+\left(A(u,p;u,-p)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The Braess condition (
2.4c
) implies that

\displaystyle\lVert(u,p)\rVert_{V\times Q}\leq\left(\frac{1+\lVert a\rVert}{\gamma}\right)\sup_{(v,q)\in V\times Q}\frac{a(u,v)+b(u,q)}{\lVert(v,q)\rVert_{V\times Q}}+\frac{1}{\beta}\sup_{(v,q)\in V\times Q}\frac{\mathcal{A}(u,p;v,q)}{{\lVert(v,q)\rVert_{V\times Q}}}+\left(\mathcal{A}(u,p;u,-p)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Since
a(u,v)+b(u,q)=\mathcal{A}(u,p;v,q)-b(v,p)+t^{2}c(p,q)
, we readily see that

\displaystyle\lVert(u,p)\rVert_{V\times Q}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1+\lVert a\rVert}{\gamma}+\frac{1}{\beta}\right)\sup_{(v,q)\in V\times Q}\frac{A(u,p;v,q)}{\lVert(v,q)\rVert_{V\times Q}}+\left(1+\frac{1+\lVert a\rVert}{\gamma}\right)\left(\mathcal{A}(u,p;u,-p)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Given that for all
0<x,y,z
the inequality
x\leq y+z
implies that
x\leq 2y+\frac{z^{2}}{x}
, we have

\displaystyle\lVert(u,p)\rVert_{V\times Q}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\frac{1+\lVert a\rVert}{\gamma}+\frac{1}{\beta}\right)\sup_{(v,q)\in V\times Q}\frac{\mathcal{A}(u,p;v,q)}{\lVert(v,q)\rVert_{V\times Q}}+\left(1+\frac{1+\lVert a\rVert}{\gamma}\right)^{2}\frac{\mathcal{A}(u,p;u,-p)}{\lVert(u,p)\rVert_{V\times Q}}.

The proof concludes by taking the supremum for all
(v,q)\in V\times Q
.
∎

3
Virtual element discretisation

This section introduces the 2D VEM from
Khot et al. (2025)
, based on
Beirão da Veiga et al. (2013)
;
Beirão da Veiga et al. (2016b)
;
Beirão da Veiga et al. (2017b)
with the estimates involving suitable polynomial projections adapted to the parameter-robust setting. Finally, we refer to
(
Khot et al., 2025
, Section 4)
for further details on the well-posedness of the discrete problem.

Mesh assumptions.
Let
\mathcal{T}^{h}
be a decomposition of
\Omega
into polygonal elements
E
with diameter
h_{E}
, let
\mathcal{E}^{h}
be the set of edges
e
of
\mathcal{T}^{h}
with length
h_{e}
.
We assume that there exists a universal constant
\rho>0
such that

(M1)

Every polygonal element
E
of diameter
h_{E}
is star-shaped with respect to a disk of radius
\geq

\rho h_{E}
,

(M2)

Every edge
e
of
E
has length
\geq

\rho h_{E}
.

We split the set of all edges as
\mathcal{E}^{h}=\mathcal{E}^{h}_{\Omega}\cup\mathcal{E}^{h}_{\mathrm{D}}\cup\mathcal{E}^{h}_{\mathrm{N}}
, where
\mathcal{E}^{h}_{\Omega}=\{e\in\mathcal{E}^{h}:e\subset\Omega\}
,
\mathcal{E}^{h}_{\mathrm{D}}=\{e\in\mathcal{E}^{h}:e\subset\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}\}
and
\mathcal{E}^{h}_{\mathrm{N}}=\{e\in\mathcal{E}^{h}:e\subset\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}\}
. The set of edges of
E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}
is denoted as
\mathcal{E}^{h}(E)
, the edges of
E
which are not in the boundary
\partial\Omega
are denoted by
\mathcal{E}^{h}_{\Omega}(E)
and the ones that lie on the Dirichlet portion of the boundary (resp. Neumann) are denoted by
\mathcal{E}^{h}_{\mathrm{D}}(E)
(resp.
\mathcal{E}^{h}_{\mathrm{N}}(E)
), the set of elements
E
that share
e
as an edge is denoted by
\mathcal{T}^{h}_{e}
. The normal and tangential jump operators are defined as usual by
[\![\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{e}]\!]:=(\boldsymbol{u}|_{E}-\boldsymbol{u}|_{E^{\prime}})|_{e}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{e}
and
[\![\boldsymbol{\zeta}\cdot\boldsymbol{t}_{e}]\!]:=(\boldsymbol{\zeta}|_{E}-\boldsymbol{\zeta}|_{E^{\prime}})|_{e}\cdot\boldsymbol{t}_{e}
, where
E,E^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}^{h}_{e}
, and
\boldsymbol{n}_{e}
and
\boldsymbol{t}_{e}
are the outward normal and tangential counterclockwise vectors of
e
with respect to
\partial E
. The space
D(E)
denotes the union of elements in
\mathcal{T}^{h}
intersecting
E
.

Polynomial spaces.
Given an integer
k\geq 0
the space of polynomials of degree
\leq k
on
E
is denoted by
\mathcal{P}_{k}(E)
(resp. for edges
e
). The space of the gradients of polynomials of grade
\leq k+1
on
E
is denoted as
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{k}(E):=\nabla(\mathcal{P}_{k+1}(E))
with standard notation
\mathcal{P}_{-1}(E)=\{0\}
for
k=-1
. The space
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{k}^{\oplus}(E)
denotes the complement of the space
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{k}(E)
in the vector polynomial space
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}_{k}(E)
, that is,
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}_{k}(E)=\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{k}(E)\oplus\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{k}^{\oplus}(E)
. In particular, following
Beirão da Veiga et al. (2019)
, we set
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{k}^{\oplus}(E)=\boldsymbol{x}^{\perp}\mathcal{P}_{k-1}(E)
where
\boldsymbol{x}^{\perp}=(x_{2},-x_{1})^{\tt t}
. Likewise, the space that defines the rotational of polynomials with degree
\leq k+1
is denoted as
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}_{k}(E):=\mathop{\mathbf{rot}}\nolimits(\mathcal{P}_{k+1}(E))
where the associated complement space
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}_{k}^{\oplus}(E)
fulfills the property
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}_{k}(E)=\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}_{k}(E)\oplus\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}_{k}^{\oplus}(E)
with
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}_{k}^{\oplus}(E)=\boldsymbol{x}\mathcal{P}_{k-1}(E)
.

Let
\boldsymbol{x}_{E}=(x_{1,E},x_{2,E})^{\tt t}
denote the barycentre of
E
and let
\mathcal{M}_{k}(E)
be the set of scaled monomials

\mathcal{M}_{k}(E):=\left\{\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{x}_{E}}{h_{E}}\right)^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}},0\leq|\boldsymbol{\alpha}|\leq k\right\},

where
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})^{\tt t}
is a non-negative multi-index with
|\boldsymbol{\alpha}|=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}
and
\boldsymbol{x}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}=x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}x_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}
for
\boldsymbol{x}=(x_{1},x_{2})^{\tt t}
. In particular, we can take the basis of
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{k}(E)
and
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{k}^{\oplus}(E)
as
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{M}}_{k}^{\nabla}(E):=\nabla\mathcal{M}_{k+1}(E)\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
and
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{M}}_{k}^{\oplus}(E):=\mathbf{m}^{\perp}\mathcal{M}_{k-1}(E)
, with
\mathbf{m}^{\perp}:=(\frac{x_{2}-x_{2,E}}{h_{E}},\frac{x_{1,E}-x_{1}}{h_{E}})^{\tt t}
,
\mathbf{m}:=\frac{\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{x}_{E}}{h_{E}}
, respectively.

The spaces introduced below are constructed to address the variational forms of (
2.1a
)–(
2.1b
) and (
2.1c
)–(
2.1d
), respectively. Their
unisolvency in terms of
Degrees of Freedom
(DoFs) and other properties, are detailed in
Beirão da Veiga et al. (2017b)
;
Beirão da Veiga et al. (2016b)
.

3.1
Discrete spaces

For
k_{1}\geq 2
, the discrete displacement space locally solves a Stokes problem:

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}_{1}^{h,k_{1}}(E):=\{\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(E)\colon

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{v}|_{\partial E}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{B}}^{k_{1}}(\partial E),\;\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathcal{P}_{k_{1}-1}(E),

\displaystyle-2\mu\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{v})-\nabla s\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{k_{1}-2}^{\oplus}(E),\text{ for some }s\in\mathrm{L}^{2}_{0}(E)\},

where
\mathcal{B}^{k}(\partial E)
is the continuous space of polynomials along the boundary
\partial E
of
E
defined as

\mathcal{B}^{k}(\partial E):=\left\{v\in C^{0}(\partial E)\colon v|_{e}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}(e),\,\forall e\subset\partial E\right\}.

Observe that
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}_{k_{1}}(E)\subseteq\mathbf{V}_{1}^{h,k_{1}}(E)
. Then, the global discrete spaces are defined as

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}_{1}^{h,k_{1}}:=\{\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbf{V}_{1}\colon\boldsymbol{v}|_{E}\in\mathbf{V}_{1}^{h,k_{1}}(E),\ \forall E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}\},\quad\mathrm{Q}_{1}^{h,k_{1}}:=\{{q}\in\mathrm{Q}_{1}\colon{q}|_{E}\in\mathcal{P}_{k_{1}-1}(E),\ \forall E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}\}.

The set of DoFs for
\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{1}^{h,k_{1}}(E)
and
{q}_{h}\in\mathrm{Q}_{1}^{h,k_{1}}(E)
are selected as

\displaystyle\bullet\text{The values of }\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\text{ at the vertices of $E$},

\displaystyle\bullet\text{The values of }\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\text{ at the $k_{1}-1$ internal Gauss-Lobatto quadrature points on each edge of}\;E,

\displaystyle\bullet\int_{E}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{v}_{h})m_{k_{1}-1},\quad\forall m_{k_{1}-1}\in\mathcal{M}_{k_{1}-1}(E)\setminus\left\{\frac{1}{h_{E}}\right\},

\displaystyle\bullet\int_{E}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{m}_{k_{1}-2}^{\oplus},\quad\forall\mathbf{m}_{k_{1}-2}^{\oplus}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{M}}_{k_{1}-2}^{\oplus}(E),

\displaystyle\bullet\int_{E}{q}_{h}m_{k_{1}-1},\quad\forall m_{k_{1}-1}\in\mathcal{M}_{k_{1}-1}(E).

For the reaction-diffusion equation and for
k_{2}\geq 0
, the discrete flux space locally solves a
\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits
-
\mathop{\mathrm{rot}}\nolimits
problem:

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}_{2}^{h,k_{2}}(E):=\{\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbf{H}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,E)\cap\mathbf{H}(\mathop{\mathrm{rot}}\nolimits,E)\colon

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\xi}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}|_{e}\in\mathcal{P}_{k_{2}}(e),\,\forall e\subset\partial E,\,\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathcal{P}_{k_{2}}(E),\;\mathop{\mathrm{rot}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathcal{P}_{k_{2}-1}(E)\}.

Note that
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}_{k_{2}}(E)\subseteq\mathbf{V}_{2}^{h,k_{2}}(E)
. In turn, the global discrete spaces are defined as follows

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}_{2}^{h,k_{2}}:=\{\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbf{V}_{2}\colon\boldsymbol{\xi}|_{E}\in\mathbf{V}_{2}^{h,k_{2}}(E),\,\forall E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}\},\quad\mathrm{Q}_{2}^{h,k_{2}}:=\{\psi\in\mathrm{Q}_{2}\colon\psi|_{E}\in\mathcal{P}_{k_{2}}(E),\,\forall E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}\}.

The set of DoFs for
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{2}^{h,k_{2}}(E)
and
\psi_{h}\in\mathrm{Q}_{2}^{h,k_{2}}(E)
can be taken as

\displaystyle\bullet\text{The values of }\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}\text{ at the $k_{2}+1$ Gauss--Lobatto quadrature points of each edge of $E$},

\displaystyle\bullet\int_{E}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{m}_{k_{2}-1}^{\nabla},\quad\forall\mathbf{m}_{k_{2}-1}^{\nabla}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{M}}_{k_{2}-1}^{\nabla}(E),

\displaystyle\bullet\int_{E}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{m}_{k_{2}}^{\oplus},\quad\forall\mathbf{m}_{k_{2}}^{\oplus}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{M}}_{k_{2}}^{\oplus}(E),

\displaystyle\bullet\int_{E}\psi_{h}m_{k_{2}},\quad\forall m_{k_{2}}\in\mathcal{M}_{k_{2}}(E).

Although
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
are in general arbitrary and independent, we select
k_{1}=k_{2}+1
with
k_{2}\geq 1
to achieve optimal convergence. This choice is justified later by Theorem
3.4
.

3.2
Projection and interpolation operators

The operators below are required for the discrete formulation and the subsequent error analysis. We follow
Beirão da Veiga et al. (2017b)
for the elasticity problem and
Beirão da Veiga et al. (2016b)
for the reaction-diffusion problem.
Given
E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}
, the energy projection operator
\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},k_{1}}:\mathbf{H}^{1}(E)\rightarrow\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}_{k_{1}}(E)
is defined, for all
\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(E)
, by

\displaystyle\int_{E}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},k_{1}}\boldsymbol{v})\colon\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{m}_{k_{1}})

\displaystyle=0,\quad\forall\mathbf{m}_{k_{1}}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{M}}_{k_{1}}(E),

\displaystyle\int_{\partial E}(\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},k_{1}}\boldsymbol{v})\cdot\mathbf{m}_{\textbf{RBM}}

\displaystyle=0,\quad\forall\mathbf{m}_{\textbf{RBM}}\in\textbf{RBM}(E):=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{h_{E}}\\
0\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\frac{1}{h_{E}}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}\frac{x_{2,E}-x_{2}}{h_{E}}\\
\frac{x_{1}-x_{1,E}}{h_{E}}\end{pmatrix}\right\},

where
\textbf{RBM}(E)
is the set of scaled rigid body motions. We also define the
\mathbf{L}^{2}
-projection
\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{j}^{0,k}:\mathbf{L}^{2}(E)\rightarrow\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}_{k}}(E)

\displaystyle\int_{E}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{j}^{0,k}\boldsymbol{\zeta})\cdot\mathbf{m}_{k}=0,\quad\forall\mathbf{m}_{k}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{M}}_{k}(E),\forall\boldsymbol{\zeta}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(E),

and analogously for scalar functions. For clarity,
\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{0,k_{1}}
is the projection associated with the elasticity problem, with polynomial degree
k_{1}
(resp.
\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}
for the reaction-diffusion problem). Regarding computability of
\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},k_{1}},\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{0,k_{1}-2}
on
\mathbf{V}_{1}^{h,k_{1}}
and
\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}
on

in terms of the respective DoFs, we refer to
(
Beirão da Veiga et al., 2017b

and
(
Beirão da Veiga et al., 2016a

. Next, we present a scaled version of classical polynomial approximation estimates

\overline{\textnormal{e}}_{h}
decays with the following rate

\displaystyle\overline{\textnormal{e}}_{h}\lesssim h^{\min\left\{s_{1},s_{2}+1\right\}}(|\boldsymbol{f}|_{s_{1}-1,\mathbf{Q}_{b_{1}}}+|\boldsymbol{u}|_{s_{1}+1,\mathbf{V}_{1}}+|{p}|_{s_{1},Q_{b_{1}}}+|g|_{s_{2}+1,Q_{b_{2}}}+|\boldsymbol{\zeta}|_{s_{2}+1,\mathbf{V}_{2}}+|\varphi|_{s_{2}+1,Q_{b_{2}}}).

4
A posteriori error analysis

This section aims at deriving reliable and efficient residual-based a posteriori error estimators for the VEM of Section
3
. The reliability is a consequence of the global inf-sup provided in Theorem
2.2
, together with the tools given in Subsection
4.1
. The efficiency is proven using bubble functions.

4.1
Preliminary toolkit

This subsection presents the necessary local estimates, Helmholtz decomposition, and bubble function results required for the a posteriori error analysis. First, an estimate involving the projection of the load term
\boldsymbol{f}
is presented (see
(
Beirão da Veiga et al., 2017b
, Lemma 3.7)
).

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\boldsymbol{f}\in L^{2}(E)
. Then, for all
\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(E)
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\left[F_{1}-F_{1}^{h}\right](\boldsymbol{v})\right|\lesssim h_{E}\lVert\boldsymbol{f}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{0,k_{1}-2}\boldsymbol{f}\rVert_{0,E}|\boldsymbol{v}|_{1,E}.

Next, we provide an estimate for the local virtual approximation of the bilinear form
a_{1}^{E}(\cdot,\cdot)
.

Lemma 4.2
.

For all
\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{1}^{h,k_{1}}(E)
, the following estimate holds:

\displaystyle\left|\left[a_{1}^{h,E}-a_{1}^{E}\right](\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})\right|\lesssim\left(S_{1}^{E}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},k_{1}}\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},k_{1}}\boldsymbol{u}_{h})\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\rVert_{\mathbf{V}_{1}(E)}.

Proof.

The definition of the polynomial projection
\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},k_{1}}
leads to

\displaystyle\left[a_{1}^{h,E}-a_{1}^{E}\right](\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})=2\mu\int_{E}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},k_{1}}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}):\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},k_{1}}\boldsymbol{v}_{h})+S_{1}^{E}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},k_{1}}\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},k_{1}}\boldsymbol{v}_{h})-2\mu\int_{E}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}):\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h})

\displaystyle=-2\mu\int_{E}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},k_{1}}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}):\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},k_{1}}\boldsymbol{v}_{h})+S_{1}^{E}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},k_{1}}\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},k_{1}}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}).

The proof is completed after using a Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, (
3.3a
), and Lemma
3.1
.
∎

Two additional local estimates are required for the bilinear form
a_{2}^{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},p_{h},h,E}(\cdot,\cdot)
and the nonlinear term
\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}),p)\boldsymbol{\zeta}
. They are adapted from
(
Munar et al., 2024
, Lemma 4.3)
to the robust case.

Lemma 4.3
.

Given
\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{1}^{h,k_{1}}(E)
,
p_{h}\in\mathrm{Q}_{1}^{h,k_{1}}(E)
and
\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{2}^{h,k_{2}}(E)
, the following estimate holds:

\displaystyle\left|\left[a_{2}^{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},p_{h},h,E}-a_{2}^{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},p_{h},E}\right](\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h})\right|\lesssim\biggl(\left(S_{2}^{E}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h})\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\sqrt{M}\lVert\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),p_{h})\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}(\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),p_{h})\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h})\rVert_{0,E}\biggr)\lVert\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h}\rVert_{\mathbf{V}_{2}(E)}.

Proof.

The
\mathbf{L}^{2}
-orthogonality of
\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{1}}
shows that

\displaystyle\left[a_{2}^{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},p_{h},h,E}-a_{2}^{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},p_{h},E}\right](\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h})=a_{2}^{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},p_{h},E}(\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h})-a_{2}^{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},p_{h},E}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h})

\displaystyle\quad+S_{2}^{E}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h})

\displaystyle=-\int_{E}\left[\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),p_{h})\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}(\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),p_{h})\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h})\right]\cdot(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h})

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{E}\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),p_{h})(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h})\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h}+S_{2}^{E}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h}).

The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, (
3.3b
), the continuity of
\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}
and a proper scaling finish the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Given
E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}
,
(\boldsymbol{u},{p})\in\mathbf{V}_{1}\times Q
and
(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},{p})\in\mathbf{V}_{1}^{h,k_{1}}\times\mathrm{Q}_{1}^{h,k_{1}}
the following estimate holds:

\displaystyle\lVert\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}),{p})\boldsymbol{\zeta}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}(\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),{p}_{h})\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h})\rVert_{0,E}\lesssim\left(S_{2}^{E}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h})\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\overline{C}_{3}\left(\lVert\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\rVert_{\mathbf{V}_{1}(E)}+\lVert{p}-{p}_{h}\rVert_{\mathrm{Q}_{1}(E)}+\left(S_{1}^{E}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},k_{1}}\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},k_{1}}\boldsymbol{u}_{h})\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\lVert\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),p_{h})\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}(\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),p_{h})\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h})\rVert_{0,E}.

Proof.

The proof follows by adding and subtracting suitable terms,
applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, (
3.3a
), and invoking the well-posedness of the discrete reaction-diffusion equation.
∎

To apply the Helmholtz decomposition in the forthcoming analysis (see
Munar et al. (2024)
), we require the scalar space
\mathrm{V}_{3}=\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{N}}(\Omega):=\{\chi\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega):\chi=0\text{ on }\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}\}

It is shown in

that
\mathop{\mathbf{rot}}\nolimits\chi_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{2}^{h,k_{2}}
for

, where the global space is given by

\mathrm{V}_{3}^{h,k_{2}+1}:=\{\chi\in\mathrm{V}_{3}\colon\chi|_{E}\in\mathrm{V}_{3}^{h,k_{2}+1}(E),\,\forall E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}\}.

This relation plays an important role in the argument used in Lemma
4.12
.

Next, we introduce the quasi-interpolation error operator for functions in the auxiliary space
\mathrm{V}_{3}
defined as
\mathrm{I}_{3}^{Q,k_{2}+1}:\mathrm{H}^{1}(E)\rightarrow\mathrm{V}^{h,k_{2}+1}_{3}(E)
(see
(
Mora et al., 2015
, Proposition 4.2)
for details). The interpolation error involving the

norm and functions in

can be proven directly from
(
Ern and Guermond, 2021

and
(
Gatica, 2014
, Section 1.3.2)

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}
, and
e\in\mathcal{E}^{h}(E)
. For
\chi\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(E)
, the following estimate holds:

\displaystyle h_{e}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\chi-\mathrm{I}_{3}^{Q,k_{2}+1}\chi\rVert_{0,e}+\lVert\chi-\mathrm{I}_{3}^{Q,k_{2}+1}\chi\rVert_{0,E}\lesssim h_{E}|\chi|_{1,E}.

As a consequence of the mesh assumptions from Section
3
, a shape-regular triangulation
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}^{h}
of
\Omega
can be constructed via a sub-triangulation of each polygon into triangles (see
Ainsworth and Oden (1997)
for the construction of bubble functions in triangles). Let us recall the following results from
Cangiani et al. (2017)
regarding element and edge bubble functions supported in polygonal elements and edges (union of triangles sharing an edge), respectively.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}
and
\Psi_{E}
be an element bubble function. For all
\mathbf{q}_{E}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}_{k}(E)
, the following bounds hold

\displaystyle\lVert\mathbf{q}_{E}\rVert_{0,E}^{2}\lesssim\lVert\sqrt{\Psi_{E}}\mathbf{q}_{E}\rVert_{0,E}^{2}\lesssim\lVert\mathbf{q}_{E}\rVert_{0,E}^{2},\quad\lVert\mathbf{q}_{E}\rVert_{0,E}\lesssim\lVert\Psi_{E}\mathbf{q}_{E}\rVert_{0,E}+h_{E}|\Psi_{E}\mathbf{q}_{E}|_{1,E}\lesssim\lVert\mathbf{q}_{E}\rVert_{0,E}.

Lemma 4.7
.

For
E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}
, let
e\subset\partial E
and
\Psi_{e}
be the corresponding edge bubble function. For all
\mathbf{q}_{e}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}_{k}(e)

\displaystyle\lVert\mathbf{q}_{e}\rVert_{0,e}^{2}\lesssim\lVert\sqrt{\Psi_{e}}\mathbf{q}_{e}\rVert_{0,e}^{2}\lesssim\lVert\mathbf{q}_{e}\rVert_{0,e}^{2},\quad\frac{1}{\sqrt{h_{E}}}\lVert\Psi_{e}\mathbf{q}_{e}\rVert_{0,E}+\sqrt{h_{E}}|\Psi_{e}\mathbf{q}_{e}|_{1,E}\lesssim\lVert\mathbf{q}_{e}\rVert_{0,e},

where
\mathbf{q}_{e}
is also used to denote the constant prolongation of
\mathbf{q}_{e}
in the direction normal to
e
.

4.2
Error estimators

We now define residual-based a posteriori error estimators for the VE formulation from Section
3
. First, we define the local error estimator
\Theta_{E}^{2}:=\Theta_{1,E}^{2}+\Theta_{2,E}^{2}
for
E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}
conformed by the elasticity contribution and the reaction-diffusion one. These are given explicitly by

\displaystyle\Theta_{1,E}^{2}:=\frac{1}{2\mu}\Xi_{1,E}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\mu}\eta_{1,E}^{2}+2\mu\Lambda_{1,E}^{2}+S_{1,E}^{2},\quad\Theta_{2,E}^{2}:=M\Xi_{2,E}^{2}+M\eta_{2,E}^{2}+M\Lambda_{2,E}^{2}+S_{2,E}^{2},

(4.1)

where
\Xi_{j,E}
represents the local residual estimator, which encapsulates boundary and volume terms as

\displaystyle\Xi_{1,E}^{2}:=\,\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}^{h}_{\mathrm{N}}(E)}h_{e}\lVert(2\mu\varepsilon(\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},k_{1}}\boldsymbol{u}_{h})-{p}_{h}\mathbb{I})\boldsymbol{n}_{e}\rVert_{0,e}^{2}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}^{h}_{\Omega}(E)}h_{e}\lVert[\![(2\mu\varepsilon(\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},k_{1}}\boldsymbol{u}_{h})-{p}_{h}\mathbb{I})\boldsymbol{n}_{e}]\!]\rVert_{0,e}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+h_{E}^{2}\lVert\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{0,k_{1}-2}\boldsymbol{f}+2\mu\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},k_{1}}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}))-\nabla{p}_{h}\rVert_{0,E}^{2},

\displaystyle\Xi_{2,E}^{2}:=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}^{h}_{\mathrm{D}}(E)}h_{e}\lVert\varphi_{\mathrm{D}}-\varphi_{h}\rVert_{0,e}^{2}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}^{h}_{\mathrm{D}}(E)}h_{e}\lVert(\nabla\varphi_{\mathrm{D}}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}(\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),{p}_{h})\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}))\cdot\boldsymbol{t}_{e}\rVert_{0,e}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+h_{E}^{2}\lVert\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}(\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),{p}_{h})\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h})-\nabla\varphi_{h}\rVert_{0,E}^{2}+h_{E}^{2}\lVert\mathop{\mathrm{rot}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}(\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),{p}_{h})\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}))\rVert_{0,E}^{2}.

The local data oscillation, mixed, and stabilisation estimators, are respectively given by

\displaystyle\eta_{1,E}^{2}:=h_{E}^{2}\lVert\boldsymbol{f}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{0,k_{1}-2}\boldsymbol{f}\rVert_{0,E}^{2},\quad\eta_{2,E}^{2}:=\lVert\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),{p}_{h})\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}(\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),{p}_{h})\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h})\rVert_{0,E}^{2},

\displaystyle\Lambda_{1,E}^{2}:=\lVert\frac{1}{\lambda}\ell(\varphi_{h})-\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{u}_{h}+\frac{1}{\lambda}{p}_{h}\rVert_{0,E}^{2},\quad\Lambda_{2,E}^{2}:=\lVert-g-\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}+\theta\varphi_{h}\rVert_{0,E}^{2},

\displaystyle S_{1,E}^{2}:=S_{1}^{E}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},k_{1}}\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},k_{1}}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}),\quad S_{2,E}^{2}:=S_{2}^{E}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}).

Such definitions require a stronger assumption on the boundary term
\varphi_{\mathrm{D}}
(see Lemma
4.12
).
The global error, global residual, global mixed, global data oscillation, and global stabilisation estimators are defined as

\displaystyle\Theta^{2}:=\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\Theta_{E}^{2},\quad\Xi^{2}:=\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\left(\frac{1}{2\mu}\Xi_{1,E}^{2}+M\Xi_{2,E}^{2}\right),\quad\Lambda^{2}:=\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\left(2\mu\Lambda_{1,E}^{2}+M\Lambda_{2,E}^{2}\right),

\displaystyle\eta^{2}:=\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\left(\frac{1}{2\mu}\eta_{1,E}^{2}+M\eta_{2,E}^{2}\right),\quad S^{2}:=\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\left(S_{1,E}^{2}+S_{2,E}^{2}\right).

Similar estimators can be found for the uncoupled divergence-free Stokes problem in
Wang et al. (2020)
and for the uncoupled mixed Poisson problem in
Munar et al. (2024)
.

4.3
Reliability

This subsection establishes an upper bound for the total error in terms of the error estimator. We begin by deriving estimates for the elasticity and reaction-diffusion partial errors in terms of a residual operator, pointing out that the non-linear terms satisfy the assumptions in Section
2
.

Given the solution
(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},{p}_{h},\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\varphi_{h})\in\mathbf{V}_{1}^{h,k_{1}}\times\mathrm{Q}_{1}^{h,k_{1}}\times\mathbf{V}_{2}^{h,k_{2}}\times\mathrm{Q}_{2}^{h,k_{2}}
to (

) and the fixed functions

and

\displaystyle:=F_{1}(\boldsymbol{v})-a_{1}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{v})-b_{1}(\boldsymbol{v},{p}_{h}),\quad

\displaystyle\forall\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbf{V}_{1},

\displaystyle R_{G_{1}^{\vartheta_{h}}}({q})

\displaystyle:=G_{1}^{\vartheta_{h}}({q})-b_{1}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},{q})+\frac{1}{\lambda}c_{1}({p}_{h},{q}),\quad

\displaystyle\forall q\in\mathrm{Q}_{1},

.

.

For the fixed pairs

Lemma 4.11
.

Proof.

\displaystyle=:\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\left(\mathrm{T}_{1}^{1,E}+\mathrm{T}_{1}^{2,E}+\mathrm{T}_{1}^{3,E}+\mathrm{T}_{1}^{4,E}+\mathrm{T}_{1}^{5,E}\right).

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\mathrm{T}_{1}^{1,E}\right|\lesssim\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\mu}}\eta_{1,E}\lVert\boldsymbol{v}\rVert_{\mathbf{V}_{1}(E)},\quad\left|\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\mathrm{T}_{1}^{2,E}\right|\lesssim\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}S_{1,E}\lVert\boldsymbol{v}\rVert_{\mathbf{V}_{1}(E)}.

(4.3)

To address

we shall use the following integration by parts formulae

\displaystyle\int_{E}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},k_{1}}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}):\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v}_{h})

\displaystyle=-\int_{E}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},k_{1}}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}))\cdot(\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v}_{h})+\int_{\partial E}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},k_{1}}\boldsymbol{u}_{h})\boldsymbol{n}\cdot(\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v}_{h}),

(4.4a)

\displaystyle\int_{E}\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v}_{h}){p}_{h}

\displaystyle=-\int_{E}\nabla{p}_{h}\cdot(\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v}_{h})+\int_{\partial E}({p}_{h}\mathbb{I})\boldsymbol{n}\cdot(\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v}_{h}).

(4.4b)

The identities (
4.4a
)-(
4.4b
), Lemma
3.2
, and a Cauchy–Schwarz inequality lead to

\displaystyle\biggl|\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\mathrm{T}_{1}^{3,E}+\mathrm{T}_{1}^{4,E}+\mathrm{T}_{1}^{5,E}\biggr|=\biggl|-\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}^{h}_{\mathrm{N}}}\int_{e}(2\mu\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},k_{1}}\boldsymbol{u}_{h})-{p}_{h}\mathbb{I})\boldsymbol{n}\cdot(\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v}_{h})-\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}a_{1}^{E}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},k_{1}}\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v}_{h})

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}^{h}_{\Omega}}\int_{e}(2\mu\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},k_{1}}\boldsymbol{u}_{h})-{p}_{h}\mathbb{I})\boldsymbol{n}\cdot(\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v}_{h})+\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\int_{E}(\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{0,k_{1}-2}\boldsymbol{f}+2\mu\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},k_{1}}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}))-\nabla{p}_{h})\cdot(\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v}_{h})\biggr|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\mu}}\Xi_{1,E}+\,S_{1,E})\lVert\boldsymbol{v}\rVert_{\mathbf{V}_{1}(E)}.

(4.5)

Finally, the residual
R_{G_{1}^{\varphi_{h}}}^{E}(\cdot)
is handled using a Cauchy–Schwarz inequality as follows

\displaystyle\biggl|R_{G_{1}^{\varphi_{h}}}({q})\biggr|\lesssim\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\sqrt{2\mu}\Lambda_{1,E}\lVert{q}\rVert_{\mathrm{Q}_{1}(E)}.

(4.6)

Summing the estimates (
4.3
), (
4.5
), (
4.6
), taking the supremum for all
\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbf{V}_{1}
and all
{q}\in\mathrm{Q}_{1}
, and applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality conclude the proof.
∎

Remark 1
.

It is possible to construct a Fortin interpolation for Stokes-like spaces. Thus, from the commutative property applied to (
4.4b
), one can eliminate
p
from the estimator
\Xi_{1,E}
. However, the momentum balance (
2.1a
) also drops from
the estimator, leading to convergence issues as the right-hand side
\boldsymbol{f}
is not recovered. An alternative is proposed in
Lederer et al. (2019)
, where linear momentum conservation is ensured in a pressure-free formulation.

Now, we will concentrate on the residuals for the reaction-diffusion problem defined in Lemma
4.9
.

Lemma 4.12
.

Assume that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a connected domain and that
\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}
is contained in the boundary of a convex part of
\Omega
, that is, there exists a convex domain
B
such that
\overline{\Omega}\subseteq B
and
\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}\subseteq\partial B
. Assume further that
\varphi_{\mathrm{D}}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\partial\Omega)
. Then, the following bound holds:

\lVert R_{F_{2}}\rVert_{\mathbf{V}^{\prime}_{2}}^{2}+\lVert R_{G_{2}}\rVert_{Q^{\prime}_{2}}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\Theta_{2,E}^{2}.

Proof.

Let
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbf{V}_{2}
. We construct
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{2}^{h,k_{2}}
combining the continuous Helmholtz decomposition (see
(
Cascón et al., 2007
, Lemma 5.1)
) and the interpolators
\mathbf{I}_{2}^{F,k_{2}},\mathrm{I}_{3}^{Q,k_{2}+1}
. Indeed, there exist
\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)
and
\chi\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{N}}(\Omega)
such that

\boldsymbol{\xi}=\boldsymbol{z}+\mathop{\mathbf{rot}}\nolimits\chi\text{ in }\Omega\text{, with }\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}\left(\lVert\boldsymbol{z}\rVert_{1,\Omega}+\lVert\chi\rVert_{1,\Omega}\right)\lesssim\lVert\boldsymbol{\xi}\rVert_{\mathbf{V}_{2}}.

(4.7)

Therefore, we set
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h}:=\boldsymbol{z}_{h}+\mathop{\mathbf{rot}}\nolimits\chi_{h}
, where
\boldsymbol{z}_{h}=\mathbf{I}_{2}^{F,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{z}
and
\chi_{h}=\mathrm{I}_{3}^{Q,k_{2}+1}\chi
. Note that Lemma
3.2
and Lemma
4.5
turn the bound in (
4.7
) into

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\lVert\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h}\rVert_{0,E}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\left(\lVert\boldsymbol{z}-\boldsymbol{z}_{h}\rVert_{0,E}+\lVert\boldsymbol{z}\rVert_{0,E}+|\chi-\chi_{h}|_{1,E}+|\chi|_{1,E}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\left(\lVert\boldsymbol{z}\rVert_{1,E}+\lVert\chi\rVert_{1,E}\right)\lesssim\lVert\boldsymbol{\xi}\rVert_{\mathbf{V}_{2}}.

(4.8)

We can assert that

\boldsymbol{\xi}-\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h}=\boldsymbol{z}-\boldsymbol{z}_{h}+\mathop{\mathbf{rot}}\nolimits(\chi-\chi_{h})\text{ in }\Omega,\quad\text{with}\quad\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\lVert\boldsymbol{\xi}-\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h}\rVert_{0,E}\lesssim\lVert\boldsymbol{\xi}\rVert_{\mathbf{V}_{2}}.

(4.9)

Next, given
E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}
, we rewrite the residual of
R_{F_{2}}(\cdot)
as

\displaystyle R_{F_{2}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})

\displaystyle=\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\left(\left[a_{2}^{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},{p}_{h},h,E}-a_{2}^{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},{p}_{h},E}\right](\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h})+F_{2}^{E}(\boldsymbol{\xi}-\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h})-a_{2}^{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},{p}_{h},E}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\boldsymbol{\xi}-\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h})-b_{2}^{E}(\boldsymbol{\xi}-\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h},\varphi_{h})\right)

\displaystyle=:\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\left(\mathrm{T}_{2}^{1,E}+\mathrm{T}_{2}^{2,E}+\mathrm{T}_{2}^{3,E}+\mathrm{T}_{2}^{4,E}\right).

For
\mathrm{T}_{2}^{1,E}
, Lemma
4.3
and (
4.8
) imply that

\displaystyle\biggl|\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\mathrm{T}_{2}^{1,E}\biggr|\lesssim\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\left(\sqrt{M}\eta_{2,E}+S_{2,E}\right)\lVert\boldsymbol{\xi}\rVert_{\mathbf{V}_{2}(E)}.

(4.10)

For
\mathrm{T}_{2}^{2,E}
, substituting (
4.9
) and applying
(
Domínguez et al., 2015
, Lemma 3.5)
for
\chi-\chi_{h}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{N}}(\Omega)
and a suitable extension of the data
\varphi_{\mathrm{D}}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega)
such that
\varphi_{\mathrm{D}}=0
on
\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}
(see
(
Galvis and Sarkis, 2007
, Lemma 2.2)
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\mathrm{T}_{2}^{2,E}

\displaystyle=\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\langle(\boldsymbol{z}-\boldsymbol{z}_{h})\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{e},\varphi_{\mathrm{D}}\rangle_{\partial E\cap\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}}+\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\langle\mathop{\mathbf{rot}}\nolimits(\chi-\chi_{h})\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{e},\varphi_{\mathrm{D}}\rangle_{\partial E\cap\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\langle(\boldsymbol{z}-\boldsymbol{z}_{h})\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{e},\varphi_{\mathrm{D}}\rangle_{\partial E\cap\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}}-\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\langle\chi-\chi_{h},\nabla\varphi_{\mathrm{D}}\cdot\boldsymbol{t}_{e}\rangle_{\partial E\cap\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}}.

For
\mathrm{T}_{2}^{3,E}
, the addition and subtraction of the terms
\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}(\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),{p}_{h})\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h})
and
\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),{p}_{h})\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}
, and for
\mathrm{T}_{2}^{4,E}
an application of integration by parts lead to

\displaystyle\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\mathrm{T}_{2}^{3,E}=\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\biggl(\int_{E}\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}(\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),{p}_{h})\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h})\cdot(\boldsymbol{\xi}-\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h})+\int_{E}\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),{p}_{h})(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h})\cdot(\boldsymbol{\xi}-\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h})

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{E}\left[\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),{p}_{h})\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}(\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),{p}_{h})\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h})\right]\cdot(\boldsymbol{\xi}-\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h})\biggr),

\displaystyle\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\mathrm{T}_{2}^{4,E}=-\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\int_{E}\varphi_{h}\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{\xi}-\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h})=\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\int_{E}\nabla\varphi_{h}\cdot(\boldsymbol{\xi}-\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h})-\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}^{h}}\int_{e}\varphi_{h}(\boldsymbol{\xi}-\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h})\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{e}.

Thus, integration by parts, the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, Lemma
4.5
, Lemma
3.2
, and (
3.3b
) imply that

\displaystyle\biggl|\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\mathrm{T}_{2}^{2,E}+\mathrm{T}_{2}^{3,E}+\mathrm{T}_{2}^{4,E}\biggr|=\biggl|\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}^{h}_{\mathrm{D}}}\langle(\boldsymbol{z}-\boldsymbol{z}_{h})\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{e},\varphi_{\mathrm{D}}-\varphi_{h}\rangle_{e}-\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}^{h}_{\mathrm{D}}}\langle\chi-\chi_{h},\nabla\varphi_{\mathrm{D}}\cdot\boldsymbol{t}_{e}\rangle_{e}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}^{h}}\langle\chi-\chi_{h},\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}(\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),{p}_{h})\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h})\cdot\boldsymbol{t}_{e}\rangle_{e}

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\int_{E}\left[\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}(\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),{p}_{h})\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h})-\nabla\varphi_{h}\right]\cdot(\boldsymbol{z}-\boldsymbol{z}_{h})

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\int_{E}\mathop{\mathrm{rot}}\nolimits\left(\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}(\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),{p}_{h})\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h})\right)(\chi-\chi_{h})

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\int_{E}\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),{p}_{h})(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h})\cdot(\boldsymbol{\xi}-\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h})

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\int_{E}\left[\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),{p}_{h})\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}(\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),{p}_{h})\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h})\right]\cdot(\boldsymbol{\xi}-\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h})\biggr|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}(\sqrt{M}\Xi_{2,E}+\,S_{2,E}+\,\sqrt{M}\eta_{2,E})\lVert\boldsymbol{\xi}\rVert_{\mathbf{V}_{2}(E)}.

(4.11)

Finally, concerning the residual
R_{G_{2}}(\cdot)
, the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and proper scaling give us that

\displaystyle\biggl|R_{G_{2}}(\psi)\biggr|\lesssim\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\sqrt{M}\Lambda_{2,E}\lVert\psi\rVert_{\mathrm{Q}_{2}(E)}.

(4.12)

Adding (
4.10
), (
4.11
), and (
4.12
), taking the supremum for all
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbf{V}_{2}
and all
\psi\in\mathrm{Q}_{2}
, and applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality complete the proof.
∎

-

\Xi
and
\Lambda

\eta
, the stabilisation estimator
S

(
\mathrm{HOTs}

2

Lemma 4.14
.

The following bound holds

\displaystyle\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\left(\frac{1}{2\mu}\Xi_{1,E}^{2}+2\mu\Lambda_{1,E}^{2}\right)\lesssim\max\left\{1,2\mu M\right\}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\left(\overline{\textnormal{e}}_{h}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\mu}\eta_{1,E}^{2}+S_{1,E}^{2}\right).

Proof.

Let
E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}
,
\mathbf{q}_{E}:=\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{0,k_{1}-2}\boldsymbol{f}+2\mu\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},k_{1}}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}))-\nabla{p}_{h}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}_{k_{1}-2}(E)
, and
\overline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{E}:=\Psi_{E}\mathbf{q}_{E}
where
\Psi_{E}
is an element bubble function from Lemma
4.6
. Note that (
2.2a
) leads to
R_{F_{1}}(\boldsymbol{v})=a_{1}(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{v})+b_{1}(\boldsymbol{v},{p}-{p}_{h})
for all
\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbf{V}_{1}
. Then, we set
\boldsymbol{v}=\overline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{E}
and
\boldsymbol{v}_{h}=\boldsymbol{0}
in (
4.2
). Thus, given that
\overline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{E}
vanishes on
\partial E
and outside
E
, we can apply integration by parts to arrive at

\displaystyle\lVert\mathbf{q}_{E}\rVert_{0,E}^{2}\lesssim\left|\int_{E}\mathbf{q}_{E}\cdot\overline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{E}\right|=\left|a_{1}^{E}(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\overline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{E})+b_{1}^{E}(\overline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{E},{p}-{p}_{h})-\left[F_{1}^{E}-F_{1}^{h,E}\right](\overline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{E})+a_{1}^{E}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},k_{1}}\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\overline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{E})\right|.

Next, the continuity of
a_{1}(\cdot,\cdot)
and
b_{1}(\cdot,\cdot)
together with the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and (
3.3a
) lead to

\displaystyle\lVert\mathbf{q}_{E}\rVert_{0,E}^{2}\lesssim(\lVert\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\rVert_{\mathbf{V}_{1}(E)}+\lVert{p}-{p}_{h}\rVert_{\mathrm{Q}_{1}(E)}+S_{1,E})\lVert\overline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{E}\rVert_{\mathbf{V}_{1}(E)}+\lVert\boldsymbol{f}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{0,k_{1}-2}\boldsymbol{f}\rVert_{0,E}\lVert\overline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{E}\rVert_{0,E}.

From the inequality
\lVert\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\cdot)\rVert_{0,E}\leq|\cdot|_{1,E}
and Lemma
4.6
we obtain

\displaystyle\lVert\mathbf{q}_{E}\rVert_{0,E}^{2}\lesssim\frac{\sqrt{2\mu}}{h_{E}}(\lVert\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\rVert_{\mathbf{V}_{1}(E)}+\lVert{p}-{p}_{h}\rVert_{\mathrm{Q}_{1}(E)}+S_{1,E})\lVert\mathbf{q}_{E}\rVert_{0,E}+\lVert\boldsymbol{f}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{0,k_{1}-2}\boldsymbol{f}\rVert_{0,E}\lVert\mathbf{q}_{E}\rVert_{0,E},

and consequently, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\mu}h_{E}^{2}\lVert\mathbf{q}_{E}\rVert_{0,E}^{2}\lesssim\left(\lVert\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\rVert_{\mathbf{V}_{1}(E)}^{2}+\lVert{p}-{p}_{h}\rVert_{\mathrm{Q}_{1}(E)}^{2}+S_{1,E}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\mu}\eta_{1,E}^{2}\right).

(4.13)

Now, we define the edge polynomial
\mathbf{q}_{e}=[\![(2\mu\varepsilon(\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},k_{1}}\boldsymbol{u}_{h})-{p}_{h}\mathbb{I})\boldsymbol{n}_{e}]\!]\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}_{k_{1}-1}(e)
and the edge bubble function
\overline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{e}=\Psi_{e}\mathbf{q}_{e}
as in Lemma
4.7
. Note that, this polynomial can be extended to
\mathcal{T}^{h}_{e}
by the techniques used in
(
Mora et al., 2017
, Remark 3.1)
. Choosing
\boldsymbol{v}=\overline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{e}
and
\boldsymbol{v}_{h}=\boldsymbol{0}
in (
4.2
), we readily see that
\lVert\mathbf{q}_{e}\rVert_{0,e}^{2}\lesssim\left|\int_{e}\mathbf{q}_{e}\cdot\overline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{e}\right|
, and

\displaystyle\biggl|\int_{e}\mathbf{q}_{e}\cdot\overline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{e}\biggr|\leq\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}_{e}}\biggl|\int_{E}\mathbf{q}_{E}\cdot\overline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{e}+\left[F_{1}^{E}-F_{1}^{h,E}\right](\overline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{e})-a_{1}^{E}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},k_{1}}\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\overline{v}_{e})-a_{1}^{E}(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\overline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{e})-b_{1}^{E}(\overline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{e},{p}-{p}_{h})\biggr|.

Similarly, note that

\displaystyle\lVert\mathbf{q}_{e}\rVert_{0,e}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}_{e}}\left(\lVert\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\rVert_{\mathbf{V}_{1}(E)}+\lVert{p}-{p}_{h}\rVert_{\mathrm{Q}_{1}(E)}+S_{1,E}\right)\lVert\overline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{e}\rVert_{\mathbf{V}_{1}(E)}+\left(\lVert\boldsymbol{f}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{0,k_{1}-2}\boldsymbol{f}\rVert_{0,E}+\lVert\mathbf{q}_{E}\rVert_{0,E}\right)\lVert\overline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{e}\rVert_{0,E},

which together with Lemma
4.7
and the fact that
h_{e}\leq h_{E}
, imply that

\displaystyle\sqrt{h_{e}}\lVert\mathbf{q}_{e}\rVert_{0,e}\lesssim\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}_{e}}\biggl[

\displaystyle\sqrt{2\mu}(\lVert\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\rVert_{\mathbf{V}_{1}(E)}+\lVert{p}-{p}_{h}\rVert_{\mathrm{Q}_{1}(E)}+S_{1,E})+h_{E}(\lVert\boldsymbol{f}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{0,k_{1}-2}\boldsymbol{f}\rVert_{0,E}+\lVert\mathbf{q}_{E}\rVert_{0,E})\biggr].

Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\mu}h_{e}\lVert\mathbf{q}_{e}\rVert_{0,e}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}_{e}}\left[\lVert\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\rVert_{\mathbf{V}_{1}(E)}^{2}+\lVert{p}-{p}_{h}\rVert_{\mathrm{Q}_{1}(E)}^{2}+S_{1,E}^{2}\quad+\frac{1}{2\mu}\eta_{1,E}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\mu}h_{E}^{2}\lVert\mathbf{q}_{E}\rVert_{0,E}\right].

(4.14)

Finally, from (
2.1b
), the Lipschitz continuity of
\ell(\cdot)
, the inequality
\lVert\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\cdot)\rVert_{0,E}\leq|\cdot|_{1,E}
, Körn’s inequality, and given that
\frac{1}{\lambda}\leq 1
, we easily see that

\displaystyle\sqrt{2\mu}\Lambda_{1,E}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2\mu}\lVert\frac{1}{\lambda}(\ell(\varphi)-\ell(\varphi_{h}))+\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h})+\frac{1}{\lambda}({p}-{p}_{h})\rVert_{0,E}

\displaystyle\lesssim\max\left\{\sqrt{2\mu M},1\right\}(\lVert\varphi-\varphi_{h}\rVert_{\mathrm{Q}_{2}(E)}+\lVert\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\rVert_{\mathbf{V}_{1}(E)}+\lVert{p}-{p}_{h}\rVert_{\mathrm{Q}_{1}(E)}).

(4.15)

Summing the bounds in (
4.13
)-(
4.15
) for all
E
, and (
4.14
) for all
e\subset\partial E
concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.15
.

The following bound holds

\displaystyle\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}M\Xi_{2,E}^{2}+M\Lambda_{2,E}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\max\left\{M^{2},M\overline{C}_{3}\right\}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}(\overline{\textnormal{e}}_{h}^{2}+M\eta_{2,E}^{2}+S_{2,E}^{2}+S_{1,E}^{2}+\mathrm{HOTs}),

with the high-order terms given by
\mathrm{HOTs}:=h_{E}^{2}\left[\overline{C}_{3}\left(\lVert\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\rVert_{\mathbf{V}_{1}(E)}^{2}+\lVert{p}-{p}_{h}\rVert_{\mathrm{Q}_{1}(E)}^{2}+S_{1,E}^{2}\right)+S_{2,E}^{2}+\eta_{2,E}^{2}\right]+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}^{h}(E)}h_{e}\lVert\nabla\varphi_{\mathrm{D}}\cdot\boldsymbol{t}_{e}-\Pi_{2,e}^{0,k_{2}}\left(\nabla\varphi_{\mathrm{D}}\cdot\boldsymbol{t}_{e}\right)\rVert_{0,e}^{2}.

Proof.

We start by defining
\mathbf{q}_{E}=\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}(\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),{p}_{h})\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h})-\nabla\varphi_{h}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}_{k_{2}}(E)
, and
\overline{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{E}=\Psi_{E}\mathbf{q}_{E}
with a given element bubble function
\Psi_{E}
. Lemma
4.7
, and the definition of diffusive-flux in (
2.1c
) lead to

\displaystyle\lVert\mathbf{q}_{E}\rVert_{0,E}^{2}\lesssim\biggl|\int_{E}\overline{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{E}\cdot\mathbf{q}_{E}\biggr|=\biggl|\int_{E}\overline{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{E}\cdot\biggl(\nabla(\varphi_{h}-\varphi)+\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}),{p})\boldsymbol{\zeta}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}(\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}),{p}_{h})\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h})\biggr)\biggr|.

The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, Lemma
4.4
, an integration by parts, and Lemma
4.7
show that

\displaystyle Mh_{E}^{2}\lVert\mathbf{q}_{E}\rVert_{0,E}^{2}\lesssim M^{2}(\lVert\varphi-\varphi_{h}\rVert_{\mathrm{Q}_{2}(E)}^{2}+\mathrm{HOTs}).

(4.16)

Similarly, let
q_{E}=\mathop{\mathrm{rot}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}(\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),{p}_{h})\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}))\in\mathcal{P}_{k_{2}-1}(E)
, and
\overline{\xi}_{E}=\Psi_{E}q_{E}
. Lemma
4.7
, the observation that
\mathop{\mathrm{rot}}\nolimits(\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}),{p})\boldsymbol{\zeta})=\mathop{\mathrm{rot}}\nolimits(\nabla\varphi)=0
, and an integration by parts imply that

\displaystyle\lVert q_{E}\rVert_{0,E}^{2}\lesssim\left|\int_{E}\overline{\xi}_{E}q_{E}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{E}\mathop{\mathbf{rot}}\nolimits(\overline{\xi}_{E})\cdot\left(\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}(\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),{p}_{h})\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h})-\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}),{p})\boldsymbol{\zeta}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}(\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),{p}_{h})\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h})-\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}),{p})\boldsymbol{\zeta}\rVert_{0,E}|\overline{\xi}_{E}|_{1,E}.

This and Lemma
4.4
result in

Mh_{E}^{2}\lVert q_{E}\rVert_{0,E}^{2}\lesssim M\max\left\{1,\overline{C}_{3}\right\}\bigl(\lVert\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\rVert_{\mathbf{V}_{1}(E)}^{2}+\lVert{p}-{p}_{h}\rVert_{\mathrm{Q}_{1}(E)}^{2}+M\eta_{2,E}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{2}S_{i,E}^{2}\bigr).

Now, for
e\in\mathcal{E}^{h}(E)
, we define
q_{e}=[\![\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}(\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),{p}_{h})\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h})\cdot\boldsymbol{t}_{e}]\!]\in\mathcal{P}_{k_{2}}(e)
, and
\overline{\xi}_{e}=\Psi_{e}q_{e}
, with a given edge bubble function
\Psi_{e}
. Notice that
[\![\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}),{p})\boldsymbol{\zeta}\cdot\boldsymbol{t}_{e}]\!]=0
for all
e
. Then, from Lemma
4.7
and integration by parts, we readily see that

\displaystyle\lVert q_{e}\rVert_{0,e}^{2}\lesssim\left|\int_{e}\overline{\xi}_{e}q_{e}\right|

\displaystyle=\biggl|\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}_{e}}\int_{E}\mathop{\mathbf{rot}}\nolimits(\overline{\xi}_{e})\cdot\left(\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}(\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),{p}_{h})\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h})-\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}),{p})\boldsymbol{\zeta})\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}_{e}}\int_{E}\overline{\xi}_{e}\mathop{\mathrm{rot}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}(\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),{p}_{h})\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h})\biggr|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}_{e}}\lVert\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}(\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),{p}_{h})\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h})-\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}),{p})\boldsymbol{\zeta}\rVert_{0,E}|\overline{\xi}_{e}|_{1,E}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}_{e}}\lVert\mathop{\mathrm{rot}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}(\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),{p}_{h})\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h})\rVert_{0,E}\lVert\overline{\xi}_{e}\rVert_{0,E}.

Hence, Lemma
4.4
implies that

\displaystyle Mh_{e}\lVert q_{e}\rVert_{0,e}\lesssim M\max\left\{1,\overline{C}_{3}\right\}\left(\lVert\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\rVert_{\mathbf{V}_{1}(E)}^{2}+\lVert{p}-{p}_{h}\rVert_{\mathrm{Q}_{1}(E)}^{2}+M\eta_{2,E}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{2}S_{i,E}^{2}+Mh_{E}^{2}\lVert q_{E}\rVert_{0,E}^{2}\right).

(4.17)

The trace inequality yields
\lVert\varphi_{\mathrm{D}}-\varphi_{h}\rVert_{0,e}^{2}=\lVert\varphi-\varphi_{h}\rVert_{0,e}^{2}\lesssim h_{e}^{-1}\lVert\varphi-\varphi_{h}\rVert_{0,E}^{2}+h_{e}|\varphi-\varphi_{h}|_{1,e}
, and consequently,

\displaystyle\lVert\varphi_{\mathrm{D}}-\varphi_{h}\rVert_{0,e}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{e}^{-1}\lVert\varphi-\varphi_{h}\rVert_{0,e}+h_{E}\lVert\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}(\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),{p}_{h})\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h})-\nabla\varphi_{h}\rVert_{0,E}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+h_{E}\lVert\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}(\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),{p}_{h})\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h})-\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}),{p})\boldsymbol{\zeta}\rVert_{0,E}.

This and a scaling argument with the inclusion of
\mathrm{HOTs}
lead to the following bound

\displaystyle Mh_{e}\lVert\varphi_{\mathrm{D}}-\varphi_{h}\rVert_{0,e}^{2}\lesssim M^{2}(\lVert\varphi-\varphi_{h}\rVert_{\mathrm{Q}_{2}(E)}+Mh_{E}^{2}\lVert\mathbf{q}_{E}\rVert_{0,E}+\mathrm{HOTs}).

In addition, we define
q_{e}^{*}=\Pi_{2,e}^{0,k_{2}}(\nabla\varphi_{\mathrm{D}}\cdot\boldsymbol{t}_{e})-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}(\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),{p}_{h})\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h})\cdot\boldsymbol{t}_{e}\in\mathcal{P}_{k_{2}}(e)
, where
\Pi_{2,e}^{0,k_{2}}
is defined as the polynomial projection on the edge
e
, and
\overline{\xi}_{e}^{*}=\Psi_{e}q_{e}^{*}
. Lemma
4.7
,
\nabla\varphi\cdot\boldsymbol{t}_{e}=\nabla\varphi_{\mathrm{D}}\cdot\boldsymbol{t}_{e}
on
\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}
, (
2.1c
), and an integration by parts imply that
\lVert q_{e}^{*}\rVert_{0,e}^{2}\lesssim\left|\int_{e}\overline{\xi}_{e}^{*}q_{e}^{*}\right|
. Furthermore,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{e}\overline{\xi}_{e}^{*}q_{e}^{*}\right|

\displaystyle=\biggl|\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}_{e}}\int_{E}\mathop{\mathbf{rot}}\nolimits(\overline{\xi}_{e}^{*})\cdot(\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}(\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),{p}_{h})\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h})-\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}),{p})\boldsymbol{\zeta})

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}_{e}}\int_{E}\overline{\xi}_{e}^{*}\mathop{\mathrm{rot}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}(\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),{p}_{h})\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}))+\int_{e}\overline{\xi}_{e}^{*}\left(\nabla\varphi_{\rm{D}}\cdot\boldsymbol{t}_{e}-\Pi_{2,e}^{0,k_{2}}\left(\nabla\varphi_{\rm{D}}\cdot\boldsymbol{t}_{e}\right)\right)\biggr|

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}(\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),{p}_{h})\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h})-\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}),{p})\boldsymbol{\zeta}\rVert_{0,E}|\overline{\xi}_{e}^{*}|_{1,E}

\displaystyle\quad+\lVert\mathop{\mathrm{rot}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}(\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),{p}_{h})\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}))\rVert_{0,E}\lVert\overline{\xi}_{e}^{*}\rVert_{0,E}+\lVert\nabla\varphi_{\rm{D}}\cdot\boldsymbol{t}_{e}-\Pi_{2,e}^{0,k_{2}}(\nabla\varphi_{\rm{D}}\cdot\boldsymbol{t}_{e})\rVert_{0,e}\lVert\overline{\xi}_{e}^{*}\rVert_{0,e},

which results in the following bound

\displaystyle Mh_{e}\lVert q_{e}\rVert_{0,e}^{2}\lesssim M\max\left\{1,\overline{C}_{3}\right\}\bigl(\lVert\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\rVert_{\mathbf{V}_{1}(E)}^{2}+\lVert{p}-{p}_{h}\rVert_{\mathrm{Q}_{1}(E)}^{2}+M\eta_{2,E}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{2}S_{i,E}^{2}+Mh_{E}^{2}\lVert q_{E}\rVert_{0,E}^{2}+\mathrm{HOTs}\bigr).

(4.18)

Finally, the equation (
2.1d
) shows that

\displaystyle\sqrt{M}\Lambda_{2,E}=\sqrt{M}\lVert\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{\zeta}-\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h})+\theta(\varphi-\varphi_{h})\rVert_{0,E}\lesssim M\left(\lVert\boldsymbol{\zeta}-\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}\rVert_{\mathbf{V}_{2}(E)}+\lVert\varphi-\varphi_{h}\rVert_{\mathrm{Q}_{2}(E)}\right).

(4.19)

Summing for all
e\in\partial E
(
4.17
)-(
4.18
) and for all
E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}
(
4.16
)-(
4.19
) concludes the proof.
∎

The efficiency of the residual and mixed estimators (up to data oscillation and stabilisation) is summarised below, and we recall that it is a direct consequence of Lemmas
4.14
-
4.15
.

Theorem 4.16
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
4.10
, the following bound holds

\Xi^{2}+\Lambda^{2}\lesssim\max\left\{2\mu M,M^{2},M\overline{C}_{3}\right\}(\overline{\textnormal{e}}_{h}^{2}+\eta^{2}+S^{2}+\mathrm{HOTs}).

Remark 2
.

Notice that Theorem
4.16
can be extended to a parameter-free bound for the regime
\mu\geq\frac{1}{2}
. Indeed, the small data assumption (
3.4a
) implies that

\displaystyle\max\left\{\frac{1}{2\mu},2\mu\right\}M^{4}=2\mu M^{4}\leq\frac{1}{C_{*}^{2}},\text{ with }C_{*}=\overline{C}_{1}\overline{C}_{2}^{2}L_{\ell}L_{\mathbb{M}}\left(\lVert\varphi_{\mathrm{D}}\rVert_{\frac{1}{2},\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}}+\lVert g\rVert_{0,\Omega}\right).

This observation together with the assumption
M\geq 1
(from Section 2) readily shows that

\displaystyle\max\left\{2\mu M,M^{2},M\overline{C}_{3}\right\}\leq\max\left\{1,C^{*}\right\}2\mu M^{4}\leq\max\left\{\frac{1}{C_{*}^{2}},\frac{C^{*}}{C_{*}^{2}}\right\},

with
C^{*}=L_{\mathbb{M}}\overline{C}_{2}\left(\lVert\varphi_{\mathrm{D}}\rVert_{\frac{1}{2},\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}}+\lVert g\rVert_{0,\Omega}\right)
. Noticing that both the constants
C_{*}
and
C^{*}
do not depend on parameters, the (parameter-free) efficiency reads

\Xi^{2}+\Lambda^{2}\lesssim\max\left\{\frac{1}{C_{*}^{2}},\frac{C^{*}}{C_{*}^{2}}\right\}(\overline{\textnormal{e}}_{h}^{2}+\eta^{2}+S^{2}+\mathrm{HOTs}).

5.2

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}^{h,k_{1}}_{1}(P):=\{\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(P)\colon

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}^{h,k_{1}}_{1}(f):=\{v\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(f)\colon

\displaystyle\bullet\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{f}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{P}^{f})m_{k_{1}-2},\quad\forall m_{k_{1}-2}\in\mathcal{M}_{k_{1}-2}(f),

5.3
Interpolation operators

Our goal is to define a quasi–interpolator
\mathbf{I}_{1}^{Q,k_{1}}:\mathbf{H}^{1}(P)\rightarrow\mathbf{V}_{1}^{h,k_{1}}(P)
and Fortin operator
\mathbf{I}_{2}^{F,k_{2}}:\mathbf{H}^{1}(P)\rightarrow\mathbf{V}_{2}^{h,k_{2}}(P)
, which are essential in extending the reliability result (see Theorem
4.13
) to the 3D case. The Fortin operator
\mathbf{I}_{2}^{F,k_{2}}
defined through the DoFs (similarly to the 2D case) is presented in
(
Beirão da Veiga et al., 2022
, Section 4.1)
. On the other hand, the construction of
\mathbf{I}_{1}^{Q,k_{1}}
is more involved due to the minimal regularity, which is stated next.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbf{H}^{s_{1}+1}(\Omega)
, and
0\leq s_{1}\leq k_{1}
. Under the mesh assumptions, there exists
\mathbf{I}_{1}^{Q,k_{1}}\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbf{V}_{1}^{h,k_{1}}
such that, for all
P\in\mathcal{T}^{h}

\lVert\boldsymbol{v}-\mathbf{I}_{1}^{Q,k_{1}}\boldsymbol{v}\rVert_{0,P}+h_{P}\lVert\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{v}-\mathbf{I}_{1}^{Q,k_{1}}\boldsymbol{v})\rVert_{0,P}\lesssim h_{P}^{s_{1}+1}|\boldsymbol{v}|_{s_{1}+1,D(P)},

where
D(P)
denote the union of the polyhedral elements in
\mathcal{T}^{h}
intersecting
P
.

Proof.

Step 1 (Existence of interpolation in the space
\mathbf{V}_{1}^{h,k_{1}}
)
. Let
P\in\mathcal{T}^{h}
, we start considering the super-enhanced version of the enhanced VE space
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{B}}}^{h,k_{1}}_{1}(f)
from
Cangiani et al. (2017)
which locally solves a Poisson problem with Dirichlet boundary conditions. Let
\mathbf{I}_{1}^{C,k_{1}}\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}_{D}(\Omega)
be the Clément-type VE interpolation in
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{B}}}^{h,k_{1}}_{1}(f)
satisfying the following estimate for all
P\in\mathcal{T}^{h}
(see
(
Cangiani et al., 2017
, Theorem 11)
)

\displaystyle\lVert\boldsymbol{v}-\mathbf{I}_{1}^{C,k_{1}}\boldsymbol{v}\rVert_{0,P}+h_{P}\lVert\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{v}-\mathbf{I}_{1}^{C,k_{1}}\boldsymbol{v})\rVert_{0,P}\lesssim h_{P}^{s_{1}+1}|\boldsymbol{v}|_{s_{1}+1,D(P)}.

(5.1)

Let
\boldsymbol{v}_{\pi}=\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},k_{1}}\boldsymbol{v}
be the polynomial approximation of
\boldsymbol{v}
.
The decomposition
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}_{k_{1}-2}(P)=\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{k_{1}-2}(P)\oplus\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{k_{1}-2}^{\oplus}(P)
guarantees the existence of
p_{\pi}\in\mathcal{P}_{k_{1}-1}(P)
and
\boldsymbol{g}_{\pi}^{\oplus}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{k_{1}-2}^{\oplus}(P)
such that

\displaystyle-2\mu\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{v}_{\pi})=\nabla p_{\pi}+\boldsymbol{g}_{\pi}^{\oplus}.

(5.2)

For
q_{k_{1}-1}|_{P}=\Pi_{1}^{0,k_{1}-1}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\mathbf{I}_{1}^{C,k_{1}}\boldsymbol{v})
, we introduce the following Stokes problem for all
P\in\mathcal{T}^{h}
as

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-2\mu\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}}_{I}))-\nabla s=\boldsymbol{g}_{\pi}^{\oplus}&\quad\text{in}\;P,\\
\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}}_{I}=q_{k_{1}-1}&\quad\text{in}\;P,\\
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}}_{I}=\mathbf{I}_{1}^{C,k_{1}}\boldsymbol{v}&\quad\text{on}\;\partial P.\end{cases}

(5.3)

Observe that
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}}_{I}\in\mathbf{V}_{1}^{h,k_{1}}(P)
from the definition of
\mathbf{V}_{1}^{h,k_{1}}(P)
. This and
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}}_{I}=\mathbf{I}_{1}^{C,k_{1}}\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbf{V}_{1}
on each boundary of
P
imply that
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}}_{I}\in\mathbf{V}_{1}^{h,k_{1}}
.

Step 2 (Error estimate)
. Note that (
5.2
) imply that
\boldsymbol{v}_{\pi}
solves the following local Stokes problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-2\mu\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{v}_{\pi}))-\nabla p_{\pi}=\boldsymbol{g}_{\pi}^{\oplus}&\quad\text{in}\;P,\\
\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{v}_{\pi}=\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{v}_{\pi}&\quad\text{in}\;P,\\
\boldsymbol{v}_{\pi}=\boldsymbol{v}_{\pi}&\quad\text{on}\;\partial P.\end{cases}

(5.4)

On the other hand, we define the auxiliary problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-2\mu\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}}))-\nabla\widetilde{s}=\boldsymbol{g}_{\pi}^{\oplus}&\quad\text{in}\;P,\\
\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}}=\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\mathbf{I}_{1}^{C,k_{1}}\boldsymbol{v}&\quad\text{in}\;P,\\
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}}=\mathbf{I}_{1}^{C,k_{1}}\boldsymbol{v}&\quad\text{on}\;\partial P.\end{cases}

(5.5)

Then, subtracting (
5.4
) and (
5.5
) lead to

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-2\mu\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{v}_{\pi}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}}))-\nabla(p_{\pi}-\widetilde{s})=\boldsymbol{0}&\quad\text{in}\;P,\\
\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{v}_{\pi}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}})=\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{v}_{\pi}-\mathbf{I}_{1}^{C,k_{1}}\boldsymbol{v})&\quad\text{in}\;P,\\
\boldsymbol{v}_{\pi}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}}=\boldsymbol{v}_{\pi}-\mathbf{I}_{1}^{C,k_{1}}\boldsymbol{v}&\quad\text{on}\;\partial P.\end{cases}

(5.6)

Since
\boldsymbol{z}-(\boldsymbol{v}_{\pi}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}})\in\mathbf{H}^{1}_{0}(P)
for any
\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(P)
with
\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{z}=\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{v}_{\pi}-\mathbf{I}_{1}^{C,k_{1}}\boldsymbol{v})
in
P
and
\boldsymbol{z}=\boldsymbol{v}_{\pi}-\mathbf{I}_{1}^{C,k_{1}}\boldsymbol{v}
on
\partial P
, an integration by parts twice and (
5.6
) show

\displaystyle 2\mu\int_{P}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{v}_{\pi}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}}):\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{z}-(\boldsymbol{v}_{\pi}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}}))=\int_{P}\nabla(p_{\pi}-\widetilde{s})\cdot(\boldsymbol{z}-(\boldsymbol{v}_{\pi}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}}))=-\int_{P}(p_{\pi}-\widetilde{s})\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{z}-(\boldsymbol{v}_{\pi}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}}))=0.

This results in
\lVert\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{v}_{\pi}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}})\rVert_{0,P}\leq\lVert\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{z})\rVert_{0,P}
and hence for
\boldsymbol{z}=\boldsymbol{v}_{\pi}-\mathbf{I}_{1}^{C,k_{1}}\boldsymbol{v}
we have

\displaystyle\lVert\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{v}_{\pi}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}})\rVert_{0,P}\leq\lVert\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{v}_{\pi}-\mathbf{I}_{1}^{C,k_{1}}\boldsymbol{v})\rVert_{0,P}\leq\lVert\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v}_{\pi})\rVert_{0,P}+\lVert\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{v}-\mathbf{I}_{1}^{C,k_{1}}\boldsymbol{v})\rVert_{0,P}\lesssim h_{P}^{s_{1}}|\boldsymbol{v}|_{s_{1}+1,D(P)},

(5.7)

where the triangle inequality,
\lVert\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\cdot)\rVert_{0,P}\leq|\cdot|_{1,P}
, Lemma
3.1
and (
5.1
) were applied. Next, to estimate
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}}_{I}
we consider the auxiliary problem arising from subtracting (
5.5
) and (
5.3
)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-2\mu\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}}_{I}))-\nabla(\widetilde{s}-s)=\boldsymbol{0}&\quad\text{in}\;P,\\
\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}}_{I})=\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\mathbf{I}_{1}^{C,k_{1}}\boldsymbol{v})-q_{k-1}&\quad\text{in}\;P,\\
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}}_{I}=\boldsymbol{0}&\quad\text{on}\;\partial P.\end{cases}

Given a star-shaped polyhedral domain
P
with respect to a ball
B
, the mesh assumptions lead to
|P||B|^{-1}\leq\rho^{-3}
in 3D (see e.g.
Di Pietro and Droniou (2020)
). This and
(
Duran, 2012
, Theorem 3.2)
imply that there exists a universal positive constant depending only on the mesh regularity parameter
\rho
and the dimension
d
(but independent of
P
) such that

\displaystyle\lVert\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}}_{I})\rVert_{0,P}\leq|\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}}_{I}|_{1,P}\lesssim\lVert\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\mathbf{I}_{1}^{C,k_{1}}\boldsymbol{v})-\Pi_{1}^{0,k_{1}-1}\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\mathbf{I}_{1}^{C,k_{1}}\boldsymbol{v})\rVert_{0,P}.

(5.8)

Note that the triangle inequality, (
5.1
), and the estimates for
\Pi_{1}^{0,k_{1}-1}
allow us to assert that

\displaystyle\lVert\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\mathbf{I}_{1}^{C,k_{1}}\boldsymbol{v})-\Pi_{1}^{0,k_{1}-1}\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{v}\rVert_{0,P}\lesssim\lVert\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{v}-\mathbf{I}_{1}^{C,k_{1}}\boldsymbol{v})\rVert_{0,P}+\lVert\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{v}-\Pi_{1}^{0,k_{1}-1}\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{v}\rVert_{0,P}\lesssim h^{s_{1}}|\boldsymbol{v}|_{s_{1}+1,D(P)}.

(5.9)

Thus, (
5.8
), the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-orthogonality of
\Pi_{1}^{0,k_{1}-1}
, and (
5.9
) lead to

\displaystyle\lVert\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}}_{I})\rVert_{0,P}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\mathbf{I}_{1}^{C,k_{1}}\boldsymbol{v})-\Pi_{1}^{0,k_{1}-1}\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\mathbf{I}_{1}^{C,k_{1}}\boldsymbol{v})\rVert_{0,P}=\lVert\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\mathbf{I}_{1}^{C,k_{1}}\boldsymbol{v})-\Pi_{1}^{0,k_{1}-1}\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{v}\rVert_{0,P}\lesssim h^{s_{1}}|\boldsymbol{v}|_{s_{1}+1,D(P)}.

(5.10)

Step 3 (
\mathbf{L}^{2}
-error estimate)
. Since
\mathbf{I}_{1}^{C,k_{1}}\boldsymbol{v}
and
\mathbf{I}_{1}^{Q,k_{1}}\boldsymbol{v}
coincide in
\partial P
, the Poincaré inequality holds. Thus,

\lVert\mathbf{I}_{1}^{C,k_{1}}\boldsymbol{v}-\mathbf{I}_{1}^{Q,k_{1}}\boldsymbol{v}\rVert_{0,P}\lesssim h_{P}\lVert\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{I}_{1}^{C,k_{1}}\boldsymbol{v}-\mathbf{I}_{1}^{Q,k_{1}}\boldsymbol{v})\rVert_{0,P}.

This, (
5.1
), and the
H^{1}
estimate of the interpolator with the triangle inequality

\lVert\boldsymbol{v}-\mathbf{I}_{1}^{Q,k_{1}}\boldsymbol{v}\rVert_{0,P}\leq\lVert\boldsymbol{v}-\mathbf{I}_{1}^{C,k_{1}}\boldsymbol{v}\rVert_{0,P}+\lVert\mathbf{I}_{1}^{C,k_{1}}\boldsymbol{v}-\mathbf{I}_{1}^{Q,k_{1}}\boldsymbol{v}\rVert_{0,P}

prove the required bound.
∎

Here we extend the results from
Munar et al. (2024)
to our setting. First we invoke a 3D Helmholtz decomposition
(
Gatica et al., 2016
, Lemma 3.9)
as an extension of the 2D version used in Lemma
4.12
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Assume that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a connected domain and that
\Gamma_{N}
is contained in the boundary of a convex part of
\Omega
, that is, there exists a convex domain
B
such that
\overline{\Omega}\subseteq B
and
\Gamma_{N}\subseteq\partial B
(see Figure
5.1
). Then, for each
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbf{H}_{N}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)
, there exist
\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)
and
\boldsymbol{\chi}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}_{N}(\Omega)
such that

\boldsymbol{\xi}=\boldsymbol{z}+\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\chi}\text{ in }\Omega\text{ and }\lVert\boldsymbol{z}\rVert_{1,\Omega}+\lVert\boldsymbol{\chi}\rVert_{1,\Omega}\lesssim\lVert\boldsymbol{\xi}\rVert_{\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega}.

Next, we introduce the vector space
\mathbf{V}_{3}:=\mathbf{H}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)\cap\mathbf{H}(\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits,\Omega)
with corresponding local virtual space

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}_{3}^{h,k_{2}+1}(P):=\{\boldsymbol{\chi}\in\mathbf{V}_{3}(P):

\displaystyle\,\boldsymbol{\chi}_{t}|_{\partial P}\in\mathcal{B}_{3}^{h,k_{2}+1}(\partial P),\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\chi}\in\mathcal{P}_{k_{2}}(P),\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits(\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\chi})\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}_{k_{2}-1}(P)\},

with
\mathcal{B}_{3}^{h,k_{2}}(\partial P):=\{\boldsymbol{\chi}\in(\mathcal{B}_{3})_{t}:\boldsymbol{\chi}_{f}\in\mathbf{V}_{2}^{h,k_{2}}(f),\,\forall f\in\partial P\}
. Here,
(\mathcal{B}_{3})_{t}
is defined as the tangential component of the elements in
\mathcal{B}_{3}(\partial P)
given for
f\in\partial P
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{3}(\partial P):=\{\boldsymbol{\chi}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\partial P):

\displaystyle\,\boldsymbol{\chi}|_{f}\in\mathbf{H}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,f)\cap\mathbf{H}(\mathop{\mathrm{rot}}\nolimits,f),\,\forall f\in\mathcal{F}^{h},\boldsymbol{\chi}_{f_{1}}\cdot\boldsymbol{t}_{e}=\boldsymbol{\chi}_{f_{2}}\cdot\boldsymbol{t}_{e},\,\forall f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathcal{F}^{h}_{e},\,e\subset\partial f\}.

The VE space
\mathbf{V}_{2}^{h,k_{2}}(f)
refers to the 2D edge space (see
(
Beirão da Veiga et al., 2016a
, Section 4)
) which is a rotation by
\pi/2
of the standard VE space for mixed problems given in Section
3
. Finally, the global space
\mathbf{V}_{3}^{h,k_{2}+1}
is given as follows

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}_{3}^{h,k_{2}+1}:=\{\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{3}:\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}|_{P}\in\mathbf{V}^{h,k_{2}+1}_{3}(P),\,\forall P\in\mathcal{T}^{h}\}.

For further details about DoFs and unisolvence we refer to
(
Beirão da Veiga et al., 2016a
, Section 6)
. A key relation between
\mathbf{V}_{2}^{h,k_{2}}
and
\mathbf{V}_{3}^{h,k_{2}+1}
needed for the Helmholtz decomposition is given next and we refer to
(
Beirão da Veiga et al., 2016a
, Theorem 8.2)
for a proof.

Lemma 5.3
.

For
k_{2}\geq 1
and
\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{3}^{h,k_{2}+1}
, we have that
\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{2}^{h,k_{2}}
.

Figure 5.1:
Extension of
\Omega
(left) to a convex domain
B
(right), with mixed boundary conditions on
\Gamma_{N}
and
\Gamma_{D}
(centre).

Following the approach outlined in Section
4
, we introduce a novel quasi-interpolation operator for the 3D VE edge space, specifically applied to
\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)
functions.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\chi}\in\mathbf{H}^{s_{1}+1}(\Omega)
, and
0\leq s_{2}\leq k_{2}
. Under the mesh assumptions, there exists
\mathbf{I}_{3}^{Q,k_{2}+1}\boldsymbol{\chi}\in\mathbf{V}_{3}^{h,k_{2}+1}
such that

\lVert\boldsymbol{\chi}-\mathbf{I}_{3}^{Q,k_{2}+1}\boldsymbol{\chi}\rVert_{0,P}+h_{P}|\boldsymbol{\chi}-\mathbf{I}_{3}^{Q,k_{2}+1}\boldsymbol{\chi}|_{1,P}\lesssim h_{P}^{s_{2}+1}|\boldsymbol{v}|_{s_{2}+1,D(P)}.

Proof.

Step 1 (Existence of interpolation in the 3D edge VE space)
. First, let
\mathbf{I}_{3}^{C,k_{2}+1}\boldsymbol{\chi}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}_{N}(\Omega)
be the 3D Clément-type interpolation in the Nédélec FE space defined on a sub-triangulation allowed by the mesh assumptions (See
(
Ern and Guermond, 2017
, Theorem 5.2)
), satisfying the following estimate

\displaystyle\lVert\boldsymbol{\chi}-\mathbf{I}_{3}^{C,k_{2}+1}\boldsymbol{\chi}\rVert_{0,P}+h_{P}|\boldsymbol{\chi}-\mathbf{I}_{3}^{C,k_{2}+1}\boldsymbol{\chi}|_{1,P}\lesssim h_{P}^{s_{2}+1}|\boldsymbol{\chi}|_{s_{2}+1,D(P)}.

(5.11)

For each polyhedron
P\in\mathcal{T}^{h}
, let
\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\pi}=\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}+1}\boldsymbol{\chi}
be the polynomial approximation of
\boldsymbol{\chi}
. Then, we consider

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits(\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits(\mathbf{I}_{3}^{Q,k_{2}+1}\boldsymbol{\chi}))=\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits(\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\pi})&\quad\text{in}\;P,\\
\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\mathbf{I}_{3}^{Q,k_{2}+1}\boldsymbol{\chi})=\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\pi}&\quad\text{in}\;P,\\
(\mathbf{I}_{3}^{Q,k_{2}+1}\boldsymbol{\chi})_{t}=(\mathbf{I}_{3}^{C,k_{2}+1}\boldsymbol{\chi})_{t}&\quad\text{on}\;\partial P.\end{cases}

(5.12)

Note that the definition of the Nédélec space implies that
[\![(\mathbf{I}_{3}^{C,k_{2}+1}\boldsymbol{\chi})_{f}\cdot\boldsymbol{t}_{e}]\!]=0
for all
e\in\partial f
, and
f\in\partial P
. Therefore, the unique solvability of (
5.12
) (see
(
Beirão da Veiga et al., 2016a
, Section 6.2)
) for all
P
implies that
\mathbf{I}_{3}^{Q,k_{2}+1}\boldsymbol{\chi}\in\mathbf{V}_{3}^{h,k_{2}+1}
.

Step 2 (Error estimate)
. Subtracting
\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\pi}
from (
5.12
) leads to

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits(\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits(\mathbf{I}_{3}^{Q,k_{2}+1}\boldsymbol{\chi}-\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\pi}))=\boldsymbol{0}&\quad\text{in}\;P,\\
\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\mathbf{I}_{3}^{Q,k_{2}+1}\boldsymbol{\chi}-\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\pi})=0&\quad\text{in}\;P,\\
(\mathbf{I}_{3}^{Q,k_{2}+1}\boldsymbol{\chi}-\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\pi})_{t}=(\mathbf{I}_{3}^{C,k_{2}+1}\boldsymbol{\chi}-\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\pi})_{t}&\quad\text{on}\;\partial P.\end{cases}

(5.13)

We invoke the analysis of
(
Beirão da Veiga et al., 2016a
, Section 6.2)
to show that the unique solution of (
5.13
) has the decomposition
\mathbf{I}_{3}^{Q,k_{2}+1}\boldsymbol{\chi}-\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\pi}=\tilde{\boldsymbol{\psi}}+\nabla\tilde{\varphi}
, where
\tilde{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(P)
and
\tilde{\varphi}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{0}(P)
are the respective unique solutions of

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Delta\tilde{\varphi}=0&\quad\text{in}\;P,\\
\tilde{\varphi}=0&\quad\text{on}\;\partial P,\end{cases}\quad

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\tilde{\boldsymbol{\psi}}=\boldsymbol{h}&\quad\text{in}\;P,\\
\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\tilde{\boldsymbol{\psi}}=0&\quad\text{in}\;P,\\
\tilde{\boldsymbol{\psi}}_{t}=(\mathbf{I}_{3}^{C,k_{2}+1}\boldsymbol{\chi}-\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\pi})_{t}&\quad\text{on}\;\partial P.\end{cases}

(5.14)

The above data
\boldsymbol{h}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(P)
is the solution of the following problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{h}=\boldsymbol{0}&\quad\text{in}\;P,\\
\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{h}=0&\quad\text{in}\;P,\\
\boldsymbol{h}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}=\mathop{\mathrm{rot}}\nolimits(\mathbf{I}_{3}^{C,k_{2}+1}\boldsymbol{\chi}-\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\pi})_{t}&\quad\text{on}\;\partial P.\end{cases}

(5.15)

Notice from (
5.14
) that
\tilde{\varphi}=0
. Thus, the well-posedness of Maxwell-type equations from
(
Shen and Song, 2014
, Theorem 2.3)
, and the trace inequality imply that

\displaystyle|\mathbf{I}_{3}^{Q,k_{2}+1}\boldsymbol{\chi}-\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\pi}|_{1,P}\lesssim\lVert\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\tilde{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\rVert_{0,P}+\lVert(\mathbf{I}_{3}^{C,k_{2}+1}\boldsymbol{\chi}-\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\pi})_{t}\rVert_{\frac{1}{2},\partial P}\lesssim\lVert\boldsymbol{h}\rVert_{0,P}+|\mathbf{I}_{3}^{C,k_{2}+1}\boldsymbol{\chi}-\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\pi}|_{1,P}.

(5.16)

Next, we follow a local version of
Zaghdani and Daveau (2006)
to estimate
\lVert\boldsymbol{h}\rVert_{0,P}
. For this, we recall the Helmholtz decomposition
\mathbf{L}^{2}(P)=\mathbf{H}(\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits_{\boldsymbol{0}},P)\oplus\mathbf{H}_{\partial P}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits_{0},P)
(see
Girault and Raviart (1986)
) where
\mathbf{H}(\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits_{\boldsymbol{0}},P)
,
\mathbf{H}_{\partial P}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits_{0},P)
correspond to
\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits
-free and
\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits
-free (with zero boundary condition) functions, respectively. Thus, there exist
\boldsymbol{h}_{1}\in\mathbf{H}(\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\times\boldsymbol{0},P)
and
\boldsymbol{h}_{2}\in\mathbf{H}_{\partial P}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\cdot\boldsymbol{0},P)
such that
\boldsymbol{h}=\boldsymbol{h}_{1}+\boldsymbol{h}_{2}
. Moreover,
\boldsymbol{h}_{1}=\nabla q
for some
q\in\text{H}^{1}(P)
with
\int_{P}q=0
, and
\boldsymbol{h}_{2}=\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{\phi})
for some
\boldsymbol{\phi}\in\mathbf{H}_{\partial P}(\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits,P)\cap\mathbf{H}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits_{0},P)
, with

\displaystyle\lVert\boldsymbol{h}\rVert_{0,P}^{2}=\lVert\nabla q\rVert_{0,P}^{2}+\lVert\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{\phi})\rVert_{0,P}^{2}.

(5.17)

An integration by parts and (
5.15
) imply that

\displaystyle\lVert\boldsymbol{h}\rVert_{0,P}^{2}=\left|\int_{P}\boldsymbol{h}\cdot(\nabla q+\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{\phi}))\right|

\displaystyle=\left|-\int_{P}\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{h})q+\int_{\partial P}(\boldsymbol{h}\cdot\boldsymbol{n})q+\int_{P}\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{h})\cdot\boldsymbol{\phi}+\int_{\partial P}\boldsymbol{h}\cdot(\boldsymbol{\phi}\times\boldsymbol{n})\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\partial P}\mathop{\mathrm{rot}}\nolimits(\mathbf{I}_{3}^{C,k_{2}+1}\boldsymbol{\chi}-\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\pi})_{t}q\right|.

This, the trace inequality together with (
5.17
) and the Poincaré inequality lead to

\displaystyle\lVert\boldsymbol{h}\rVert_{0,P}^{2}=\left|\int_{\partial P}\mathop{\mathrm{rot}}\nolimits(\mathbf{I}_{3}^{C,k_{2}+1}\boldsymbol{\chi}-\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\pi})_{t}q\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{P}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits(\mathbf{I}_{3}^{C,k_{2}+1}\boldsymbol{\chi}-\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\pi})\rVert_{0,P}(h_{P}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lVert q\rVert_{0,P}+h_{P}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\nabla q\rVert_{0,P})

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{P}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits(\mathbf{I}_{3}^{C,k_{2}+1}\boldsymbol{\chi}-\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\pi})\rVert_{0,P}(h_{P}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\nabla q\rVert_{0,P})

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits(\mathbf{I}_{3}^{C,k_{2}+1}\boldsymbol{\chi}-\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\pi})\rVert_{0,P}\lVert\boldsymbol{h}\rVert_{0,P}.

(5.18)

The triangle inequality, the inequality
\lVert\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits(\cdot)\rVert_{0,P}\lesssim|\cdot|_{1,P}
, (
5.18
), and (
5.16
) lead to

\displaystyle|\boldsymbol{\chi}-\mathbf{I}_{3}^{Q,k_{2}+1}\boldsymbol{\chi}|_{1,P}\leq|\boldsymbol{\chi}-\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\pi}|_{1,P}+|\mathbf{I}_{3}^{Q,k_{2}+1}\boldsymbol{\chi}-\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\pi}|_{1,P}\lesssim|\boldsymbol{\chi}-\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\pi}|_{1,P}+|\boldsymbol{\chi}-\mathbf{I}_{3}^{C,k_{2}+1}\boldsymbol{\chi}|_{1,P}.

(5.19)

Finally, Lemma
3.1
and (
5.11
) imply the
\mathbf{H}^{1}
-error estimate.

Step 3 (
\mathbf{L}^{2}
-error estimate)
Since
(\mathbf{I}_{3}^{Q,k_{2}+1}\boldsymbol{\chi})_{t}-(\mathbf{I}_{3}^{C,k_{2}+1}\boldsymbol{\chi})_{t}=0
, a Poincaré inequality can be applied to the polynomial
\mathbf{I}_{3}^{Q,k_{2}+1}\boldsymbol{\chi}-\mathbf{I}_{3}^{C,k_{2}+1}\boldsymbol{\chi}
, which together with the triangle inequality lead to

\lVert\boldsymbol{\chi}-\mathbf{I}_{3}^{Q,k_{2}+1}\boldsymbol{\chi}\rVert_{0,P}\leq\lVert\boldsymbol{\chi}-\mathbf{I}_{3}^{C,k_{2}+1}\boldsymbol{\chi}\rVert_{0,P}+h_{P}|\mathbf{I}_{3}^{C,k_{2}+1}\boldsymbol{\chi}-\mathbf{I}_{3}^{Q,k_{2}+1}\boldsymbol{\chi}|_{1,P}.

The proof follows by applying again the triangle inequality, (
5.19
), (
5.11
), and Lemma
3.1
.
∎

Finally, we focus on the extension of the reliability and efficiency of the estimators for the 3D case. Notice that the presence of the
\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits
operator motivates the redefinition of the reaction-diffusion estimator as follows

\displaystyle\Xi_{2,P}^{2}:=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}^{h}_{\mathrm{D}}(P)}h_{f}\lVert\varphi_{\mathrm{D}}-\varphi_{h}\rVert_{0,f}^{2}+\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}^{h}_{\mathrm{D}}(P)}h_{f}\lVert(\nabla\varphi_{\mathrm{D}}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}(\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),{p}_{h})\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}))\times\boldsymbol{n}\rVert_{0,f}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}^{h}_{\Omega}(P)}h_{f}\lVert[\![\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}(\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),{p}_{h})\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h})\times\boldsymbol{n}_{f}]\!]\rVert_{0,f}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+h_{P}^{2}\lVert\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}(\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),{p}_{h})\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h})-\nabla\varphi_{h}\rVert_{0,P}^{2}+h_{P}^{2}\lVert\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}(\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),{p}_{h})\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}))\rVert_{0,P}^{2}.

On the other hand,
\Theta_{1,P}
and the remaining terms of
\Theta_{2,P}
are defined analogously to (
4.1
) replacing edges
e
and polygons
E
with faces
f
and polyhedrons
P
, respectively.

Following the techniques from Section
4
and the definition of the quasi-interpolator
\mathbf{I}_{1}^{Q,k_{1}}
in Lemma
5.1
, we readily see that Lemma
4.11
holds in the 3D case. In turn, Lemma
4.14
is a consequence of bubble functions in polyhedra and faces (see
(
Cangiani et al., 2017
, Lemmas 8,9)
).

Next, we recall integration by parts formulae involving
\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{\chi}-\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h})
, with
\boldsymbol{\chi}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}_{N}(\Omega)
and
\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}:=\mathbf{I}_{3}^{Q,k_{2}+1}(\boldsymbol{\chi})\in\mathbf{V}_{3}^{h,k_{2}+1}

(
Girault and Raviart, 1986
, Equation 2.17, Theorem 2.11)
:

\displaystyle\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}^{h}_{D}}\langle\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{\chi}-\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}),\varphi_{\mathrm{D}}\rangle_{f}=-\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}^{h}_{D}}\langle\boldsymbol{\chi}-\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h},\nabla\varphi_{\mathrm{D}}\times\boldsymbol{n}_{f}\rangle_{f},

(5.20a)

\displaystyle\int_{P}\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}(\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),{p}_{h})\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h})\cdot\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{\chi}-\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h})=\int_{P}\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\left(\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}(\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),{p}_{h})\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h})\right)\cdot(\boldsymbol{\chi}-\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h})

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}^{h}(P)}\langle\boldsymbol{\chi}-\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h},\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}(\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),{p}_{h})\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h})\times\boldsymbol{n}_{f}\rangle_{f}.

(5.20b)

Using this and Lemmas
5.2
-
5.4
, we can prove Lemma
4.12
in the 3D case. Finally, since
\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits(\nabla\varphi)=\boldsymbol{0}
, Lemma
4.15
can be extended again using bubble function techniques and the integration by parts presented in (
5.20
). Therefore, Theorem
4.13
and Theorem
4.16
hold also in the 3D case.

The error will be computed as usual in the VEM framework using the local polynomial approximation of the solution as follows

\overline{\textnormal{e}}_{*}^{2}=\lVert(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},k_{1}}\boldsymbol{u}_{h},{p}-{p}_{h})\rVert_{\mathbf{V}_{1}^{h,k_{1}}\times\mathrm{Q}_{1}^{h,k_{1}}}^{2}+\lVert(\boldsymbol{\zeta}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}^{0,k_{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\varphi-\varphi_{h})\rVert_{\mathbf{V}_{2}^{h,k_{2}}\times\mathrm{Q}_{2}^{h,k_{2}}}^{2}.

The numerical implementation has been done with the library
VEM++

Dassi (2025)
. We separately implemented the elasticity and reaction-diffusion equations for arbitrary orders
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
, respectively. Then, the nonlinear coupled problem (
3.2
) is solved with an optimised Picard iteration, following the same structure as in the fixed-point analysis from
Khot et al. (2025)
, with a tolerance of
10^{-6}
. In this optimised version, the blocks corresponding to the coupling terms (
G_{1}^{\varphi_{h}}(\cdot)
and
a_{2}^{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},{p},h}(\cdot,\cdot)
) are the only ones that are reconstructed on each fixed point iteration. We recall that the elasticity and reaction-diffusion equations have sufficiently smooth forcing and source terms that will be manufactured according to the given exact solutions. The non-homogeneous boundary conditions require a slight modification of the right-hand side functionals as well as
the estimator boundary term
\Xi_{1,E}
regarding
\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}
. The experimental order of convergence
r(\cdot)
applied to either error
\overline{\textnormal{e}}_{*}
or estimator
\Theta
and the effectivity index (following Theorem
4.13
) of the refinement
1\leq j
are computed from the formulae
r(\cdot)^{j+1}=-d\log\left((\cdot)^{j+1}/(\cdot)^{j}\right)/\log\left(\textnormal{DOF}_{*}^{j+1}/\textnormal{DOF}_{*}^{j}\right)
and
\textnormal{eff}^{j}=\Theta^{j}/\overline{\textnormal{e}}_{*}^{j}
, with
\textnormal{DOF}_{*}
indicating the total number of DoFs. In turn, the stabilisation term
S_{1}^{E}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})
follows the “diagonal recipe” introduced in
Beirão da Veiga et al. (2017a)
(weighted by
2\mu
) and
S_{2}^{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},{p}_{h},E}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h})
have into account the nonlinearity
\mathbb{M}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}),{p}_{h})
as in
Khot et al. (2025)
. In the following experiments, we set
M\geq\max_{(x,y)\in\Omega}\quad\lVert\mathbb{M}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}),p)\rVert_{F}
, where the Frobenius norm
\lVert\mathbb{M}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}),p)\rVert_{F}
is computed by the
fmincom
MATLAB optimisation subroutine.

The adaptive algorithm follows the usual strategy:

\textnormal{SOLVE}\rightarrow\textnormal{ESTIMATE}\rightarrow\textnormal{MARK}\rightarrow\textnormal{REFINE}
.
The first three steps are performed inside
VEM++
, exploiting the efficiency capabilities of C++. For the REFINE step, we use the Matlab-based method from
Yu (2021)
, which connects edge midpoints to the polygon barycentre (in 2D). In turn, the adaptive refinement used in this paper relies on the library
p4est

Burstedde et al. (2011)
executed through the module GridapP4est of the Julia package
Gridap

Badia and Verdugo (2020)
. The
VEM++
code is executed through Matlab/Julia (2D/3D, respectively), generating a list of elements to refine, which is then processed by the refinement routine. These refinement routines restrict the mesh elements to convex polygons in 2D and cubes (with hanging nodes/faces) in 3D. However, we recall that this procedure is independent of the SOLVE, ESTIMATE, and MARK stages. Therefore, the implementation can be extended to general star-shaped polygons with more general refinement routines. For the MARK procedure we follow a Dörfler/Bulk strategy, marking the subset of mesh elements
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{T}^{h}
with the largest estimated errors such that for
\delta=\frac{1}{2}\in[0,1]
, we have
\delta\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\Theta_{E}^{2}\leq\sum_{E\in\mathcal{K}}\Theta_{E}^{2}.

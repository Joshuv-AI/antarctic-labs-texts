On the bias of the Gini estimator: Poisson and geometric cases, a characterization of the gamma family, and unbiasedness under gamma distributions
Roberto Vila, Helton Saulo

Abstract
In this paper, we derive a general representation for the expectation of the Gini coefficient estimator in terms of the Laplace transform of the underlying distribution, together with the mean and the Gini coefficient of its exponentially tilted version. This representation leads to a new characterization of the gamma family within the class of nonnegative scale families, based on a stability property under exponential tilting. As direct applications, we show that the Gini estimator is biased for both Poisson and geometric populations and provide an alternative, unified proof of its unbiasedness for gamma populations. By using the derived bias expressions, we propose plug-in bias-corrected estimators and assess their finite-sample performance through a Monte Carlo study, which demonstrates substantial improvements over the original estimator. Compared with existing approaches, our framework highlights the fundamental role of scale invariance and exponential tilting, rather than distribution-specific algebraic calculations, and complements recent results in Baydil et al., 2025 and Vila and Saulo, 2025a ; Vila and Saulo, 2025b .



Keywords.
Gamma distribution, Gini coefficient, Gini estimator, unbiased estimator.

Introduction

The Gini coefficient is a widely used measure of inequality in economics, finance, demography, and reliability theory, and has been extensively studied since its introduction by
Gini, 1936
, particularly with regard to the statistical properties of its estimators.

A well-known issue is the finite-sample bias of the classical Gini estimator. To address it,
Deltas, 2003
proposed an upward-adjusted estimator that corrects small-sample bias under particular distributional assumptions. Its unbiasedness has since been established for several distribution families, most notably the gamma family; see
Baydil et al., 2025
and
Vila and Saulo, 2025a
;
Vila and Saulo, 2025b
. These results raise a natural question: what structural properties of the underlying distribution explain the unbiasedness of the Gini estimator, and to what extent does this phenomenon extend beyond the gamma family?

This paper addresses these questions by developing a general representation for the expectation of the Gini estimator that applies to any non-degenerate nonnegative distribution with finite mean. The proposed representation expresses the expectation of the estimator in terms of the Laplace transform of the underlying distribution and the Gini coefficient of its exponentially tilted version. This formulation naturally connects with the Gini mean difference framework studied in
Vila et al., 2024
and provides a transparent way to analyze bias properties across different distributional families.

Within this framework, we obtain four main contributions. First, we show explicitly that the Gini estimator is biased for Poisson populations, deriving an exact integral representation for its expectation and sharp bounds for the resulting bias. Second, we extend this analysis to geometric populations, where we again establish bias and provide closed-form and hypergeometric representations for the expectation of the estimator. These results demonstrate that bias persists even for simple, classical discrete distributions. Third, we show that a scale-invariance property of exponentially tilted distributions characterizes the gamma family, yielding a unified and conceptually simple proof of the unbiasedness of the Gini estimator under gamma populations. Fourth, we use the derived bias expressions of the Gini estimators (Poisson and geometric) to propose bias-corrected versions through a plug-in approach. A Monte Carlo study is carried out to assess the finite-sample behavior of both the original and the corrected estimators. The simulation results indicates that the bias-corrected estimators exhibit a markedly improved performance.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we review basic properties and representations of the Gini coefficient. In Section
3
, we derive a general expression for the expectation of the Gini estimator.
In Sections
3.1
and
3.2
, we apply this result to Poisson and geometric populations, respectively, establishing finite-sample bias in both cases.
In Section
4
, we establish a characterization of the gamma family based on exponential tilting, and Section
4.1
uses this characterization to obtain an alternative proof of the unbiasedness of the Gini estimator for gamma populations. In Section
5
, we propose bias-corrected estimators for the Poisson and geometric cases and report results from a simulation study assessing the finite-sample performance of all estimators. Finally, in Section
6
, we conclude with a discussion of implications and directions for future research.

2
The Gini coefficient

Let
X_{1},X_{2}
be independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.)
random variables with common distribution as a non-degenerate nonnegative
random variable
X
, and finite mean
\mu=\mathbb{E}[X]>0
.
The Gini coefficient
(
Gini, 1936
)
is defined as

G\equiv G(X)=\frac{\mathbb{E}\lvert X_{1}-X_{2}\rvert}{2\mu}.

The Gini coefficient possesses the following well-known fundamental properties and representations:

(G1)

Existence:
the Gini coefficient exists and satisfies
0\leqslant G<1
.

(G2)

Scale invariance:
for any
b>0
,

G(bX)=G(X).

(G3)

Lack of translation invariance:
for any
a>0
,

G(a+X)={\mu}G(X)/(a+\mu).

(G4)

Covariance and quantile representations:

G=\mathrm{Cov}\!\left(X,\,2F_{X}(X)-1\right)/\mu=\int_{0}^{1}F_{X}^{-1}(p)(2p-1)\,\mathrm{d}p,

where
F_{X}
is the cumulative distribution function (CDF) of
X
and
F_{X}^{-1}
its generalized inverse function.

(G5)

Decomposition via Lorenz measures of inequality:

G=[D_{1}(F_{X})+G_{2}(F_{X})]/2,

where

D_{n}(F_{X})=(n+1)\mathbb{E}\left[(U-L(U))U^{\,n-1}\right]
and

G_{n}(F_{X})=n(n-1)\mathbb{E}\left[(U-L(U))(1-U)^{\,n-2}\right]
,
n\geqslant 1
,
are the Lorenz measure of inequality
(
Aaberge, 2000
)
and
the generalized Gini measure

(
Donaldson and Weymark, 1980
;
Kakwani, 1980
;
Yitzhaki, 1983
)
, respectively,
with
U\sim\mathrm{U}(0,1)
, and

L(p)=\int_{0}^{p}F_{X}^{-1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t/\mu,\,0\leqslant p\leqslant 1,

being the Lorenz curve associated with
X
.

The following result is well known in the literature; for completeness and the reader’s convenience, we briefly outline the main idea of its proof.

Proposition 2.1
.

The Gini coefficient
G
of a nonnegative
random variable
X
can be characterized as

\displaystyle G={\int_{0}^{\infty}F_{X}(x^{-})[1-F_{X}(x^{-})]{\rm d}x\over\mu},

where
\mu=\mathbb{E}[X]>0
is the mean of
X
,
F_{X}
is the CDF of
X
, and
F_{X}(x^{-})=\mathbb{P}(X<x)
.

Proof.

The proof is immediate upon using the basic identity:

\displaystyle|x-y|=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\mathds{1}_{(x,\infty)}(t)+\mathds{1}_{(y,\infty)}(t)-2\mathds{1}_{(x,\infty)}(t)\mathds{1}_{(y,\infty)}(t)\right]{\rm d}t,

(2.1)

where
\mathds{1}_{A}(t)
is the indicator function of a set
A
.
∎

Proposition 2.2
.

The Gini coefficient
G
of a discrete
random variable
X\in\{0,1,\ldots\}
can be characterized as

\displaystyle G={\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}F_{X}(k)[1-F_{X}(k)]\over\mu}.

Proof.

Since

F_{X}(x^{-})=F_{X}(\lfloor x\rfloor)=F_{X}(k),\quad x\in[k,k+1),

where
\lfloor x\rfloor
denotes the floor function, the function
F_{X}(x^{-})

is constant on each interval
[k,k+1)
. Consequently,

\int_{0}^{\infty}F_{X}(x^{-})\,[1-F_{X}(x^{-})]\,\mathrm{d}x=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{k}^{k+1}F_{X}(\lfloor x\rfloor)\,[1-F_{X}(\lfloor x\rfloor)]\,\mathrm{d}x.

Therefore, by Proposition
2.1
, the proof follows.
∎

3
A general expression for the expectation of the Gini estimator

In this section (Theorem
3.2
), we derive a general expression for the expectation of the Gini coefficient estimator
\widehat{G}
.

Let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
be i.i.d. copies of
X
. Following
De la Peña et al., 2025
, we define an estimator of the Gini coefficient
G
by

\displaystyle\widehat{G}=\begin{cases}\dfrac{{1\over\binom{n}{2}}\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}|X_{i}-X_{j}|}{2\overline{X}},&\text{if}\ \sum_{k=1}^{n}X_{k}>0\ \text{a.s.},\\[8.5359pt]
0,&\text{if}\ \sum_{k=1}^{n}X_{k}=0\ \text{a.s.},\end{cases}

(3.1)

where

\overline{X}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}X_{k}/n

denotes the sample mean. Note that, in the case where

\sum_{k=1}^{n}X_{k}>0
almost surely,
\widehat{G}
coincides with the
upward adjusted Gini coefficient estimator originally proposed by

Deltas, 2003
.

Remark 3.1
.

In an earlier version of this work, the following result was established only for i.i.d. absolutely continuous random variables, independently of the results obtained in
De la Peña et al., 2025
. Becoming aware of that reference motivated us to extend this result to i.i.d. random variables in a more general setting, which can be further extended, following the approach of
De la Peña et al., 2025
, by removing the assumption that the variables involved are identically distributed.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
be independent copies of a nonnegative
random variable
X
with finite mean
\mu=\mathbb{E}[X]>0
and CDF
F_{X}
.
Then

\mathbb{E}\left[\widehat{G}\right]=n\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}[Y_{z}]G(Y_{z})\mathscr{L}_{X}^{\,n}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z,

where
\mathscr{L}_{X}(z)=\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\{-zX\}\right]
denotes the Laplace transform of
X
.
For each
z>0
,
G(Y_{z})
is the Gini coefficient of
an exponentially tilted (or Esscher-transformed) random variable

Y_{z}

(
Butler, 2007
)
, whose CDF is given by

\displaystyle F_{Y_{z}}(t)=\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\{-zX\}\mathds{1}_{(0,t]}(X)\right]}{\mathscr{L}_{X}(z)},\quad t>0.

(3.2)

In the above, we implicitly assume that the Lebesgue–Stieltjes and improper integrals involved exist.

Proof.

Using the identity
\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-wz){\rm d}z={1/w},\,w>0,
together with Tonelli’s theorem, we can express
\mathbb{E}\left[\widehat{G}\right]
as follows

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\widehat{G}\right]

\displaystyle={n\over 2\binom{n}{2}}\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[|X_{i}-X_{j}|\exp\{-z(X_{1}+X_{2})\}\exp\left\{-z\sum_{k=3}^{n}X_{k}\right\}\right]{\rm d}z

\displaystyle={n\over 2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[|X_{1}-X_{2}|\exp\{-z(X_{1}+X_{2})\}\right]\mathscr{L}_{X}^{n-2}(z){\rm d}z,

where, in the last equality, we used the fact that
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}

are i.i.d. copies of
X
.
Applying the identity in (
2.1
), the above identity can be written as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\widehat{G}\right]={n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\{\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\{-zX\}\mathds{1}_{(0,t)}(X)\right]\left\{\mathscr{L}_{X}(z)-\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\{-zX\}\mathds{1}_{(0,t)}(X)\right]\right\}{\rm d}t\,\mathscr{L}_{X}^{n-2}(z)\right\}{\rm d}z.

Using the notation of
F_{Y_{z}}
in (
3.2
), the above identity becomes

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\widehat{G}\right]={n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\{\int_{0}^{\infty}F_{Y_{z}}(t^{-})\left[1-F_{Y_{z}}(t^{-})\right]{\rm d}t\,\mathscr{L}_{X}^{n}(z)\right\}{\rm d}z.

(3.3)

Since, for each fixed
z>0
, the mapping

t\in(0,\infty)\mapsto F_{Y_{z}}(t)
in (
3.2
) is the CDF of
Y_{z}
,
Proposition
2.1
yields

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}F_{Y_{z}}(t^{-})\left[1-F_{Y_{z}}(t^{-})\right]{\rm d}t=\mathbb{E}[Y_{z}]G(Y_{z}),

(3.4)

where
G(Y_{z})
denotes the Gini coefficient of
Y_{z}
.

Finally, combining (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Observe that
\mathbb{E}[Y_{z}]
and
G(Y_{z})
in Theorem
3.2
satisfy the following relations

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Y_{z}]={\mathbb{E}[X\exp\{-zX\}]\over\mathscr{L}_{X}(z)}=-{\partial\log\left(\mathscr{L}_{X}(z)\right)\over\partial z}

and

\displaystyle G(Y_{z})={\mathbb{E}\left[|X_{1}-X_{2}|\exp\{-z(X_{1}+X_{2})\}\right]\over 2\mathbb{E}\left[X\exp\{-z(X)\}\right]\mathscr{L}_{X}(z)}=A_{1}(z)A_{2}(z)\,G,

where
G=G(X)
is the Gini coefficient of
X
,
A_{1}
is a weighted difference factor,

\displaystyle A_{1}(z)\equiv{\mathbb{E}\left[|X_{1}-X_{2}|\exp\{-z(X_{1}+X_{2})\}\right]\over\mathbb{E}|X_{1}-X_{2}|}\leqslant 1,

because
\exp\{-z(X_{1}+X_{2})\}
is bounded above by 1,
and
A_{2}
is a mean adjustment factor,

\displaystyle A_{2}(z)\equiv{\mu\over\mathbb{E}[X\exp\{-zX\}]\mathscr{L}_{X}(z)}\geqslant 1,

since, by the generalized Chebyshev sum inequality,

X
and
\exp\{-zX\}
are negatively correlated.

By applying Theorem
3.2
together with Remark
3.3
, we have

Corollary 3.4
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
3.2
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\widehat{G}\right]=\left[-n\int_{0}^{\infty}{\partial\log\left(\mathscr{L}_{X}(z)\right)\over\partial z}\,A_{1}(z)A_{2}(z)\mathscr{L}_{X}^{\,n}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\right]G,

where
G=G(X)
is the Gini coefficient of
X
.

3.1
Biased Gini estimator in Poisson populations

If
X\sim\mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda)
,
\lambda>0
, then the exponentially tilted variable

Y_{z}
is Poisson with mean
\lambda\exp\{-z\}
. By Proposition
2.2
, the
Gini coefficient of
X
is

G(X)=\exp\{-2z\}\big[I_{0}(2\lambda)+I_{1}(2\lambda)\big],

where
I_{0}
and
I_{1}
denote the modified Bessel functions of the first kind.
By the same argument,

G(Y_{z})=\exp\{-2\lambda\exp\{-z\}\}\big[I_{0}(2\lambda\exp\{-z\})+I_{1}(2\lambda\exp\{-z\})\big].

Moreover, since
\mathbb{E}[Y_{z}]=\lambda\exp\{-z\}
and

\mathscr{L}_{X}(z)=\exp\{\lambda(\exp\{-z\}-1)\}
, the change of variables

w=\exp\{-z\}
in Theorem
3.2
yields

\mathbb{E}\left[\widehat{G}\right]=n\lambda\exp\{-nz\}\int_{0}^{1}\exp\{(n-2)\lambda w\}\big[I_{0}(2\lambda w)+I_{1}(2\lambda w)\big]\,\mathrm{d}w.

Although this integral has no closed form in terms of elementary or classical
special functions, the integrand is continuous on
[0,1]
; hence the integral is
finite and
\mathbb{E}\left[\widehat{G}\right]
is well defined.

Moreover, since
1\leqslant I_{0}(2\lambda w)+I_{1}(2\lambda w)\leqslant I_{0}(2\lambda)+I_{1}(2\lambda)
, we get

\displaystyle{\exp\{-2\lambda\}-\exp\{-n\lambda\}\over(1-{2\over n})}\leqslant\mathbb{E}\left[\widehat{G}\right]\leqslant{1-\exp\{-(n-2)\lambda\}\over(1-{2\over n})}\,G,\quad n\geqslant 1,n\neq 2,

and since

\int_{0}^{1}\bigl[I_{0}(2\lambda w)+I_{1}(2\lambda w)\bigr]\,\mathrm{d}w=[{I_{0}(2\lambda)+I_{1}(2\lambda)-1}]/({2\lambda}),

we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\widehat{G}\right]=G-\exp\{-2\lambda\},\quad n=2.

In other words, the estimator

\widehat{G}

is biased for Poisson populations,
with bias

\displaystyle\text{Bias}(\widehat{G},G)=n\lambda\exp\{-n\lambda\}\int_{0}^{1}\exp\{(n-2)\lambda w\}[I_{0}(2\lambda w)+I_{1}(2\lambda w)]{\rm d}w-\exp\{-2\lambda\}[I_{0}(2\lambda)+I_{1}(2\lambda)]

(3.5)

satisfying

\displaystyle{\exp\{-2\lambda\}-\exp\{-n\lambda\}\over(1-{2\over n})}-G\leqslant\text{Bias}(\widehat{G},G)\leqslant\left[{1-\exp\{-(n-2)\lambda\}\over(1-{2\over n})}-1\right]G,\quad n\geqslant 1,n\neq 2,

and

\displaystyle\text{Bias}(\widehat{G},G)=-\exp\{-2\lambda\},\quad n=2.

3.2
Biased Gini estimator in geometric populations

If
X\sim\mathrm{Geometric}(p)
,
p\in(0,1)
, on
\{0,1,\ldots\}
, then the exponentially
tilted variable
Y_{z}
is geometric with parameter

1-(1-p)\exp\{-z\}
. By Proposition
2.2
,

G(X)=\frac{1}{2-p},\quad G(Y_{z})=\frac{1}{1+(1-p)\exp\{-z\}}.

Moreover, since

\mathbb{E}[Y_{z}]=\frac{(1-p)e^{-z}}{1-(1-p)\exp\{-z\}}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathscr{L}_{X}(z)=\frac{p}{1-(1-p)\exp\{-z\}},

the change of variables
w=(1-p)\exp\{-z\}
yields the representation of

\mathbb{E}[\widehat{G}]
in Theorem
3.2
as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\widehat{G}\right]=np^{n}\int_{0}^{1-p}{1\over(1-w)^{n+1}(1+w)}\,\mathrm{d}w={1\over 2}\left[{}_{2}F_{1}\left(1,n;n+1;{p\over 2}\right)-p^{n}\,_{2}F_{1}\left(1,n;n+1;{1\over 2}\right)\right],

where in the last equality we have used the standard integral representation of the Gauss hypergeometric function
(
Abramowitz and Stegun, 1972
, see Chapter 15, Item 15.3. in)
:

{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c;z)=\frac{\Gamma(c)}{\Gamma(b)\Gamma(c-b)}\int_{0}^{1}t^{b-1}(1-t)^{c-b-1}(1-zt)^{-a}\,{\rm d}t,\quad c>b>0.

Since, for
0\leqslant w\leqslant 1-p
, it holds that
G\leqslant 1/(1+w)\leqslant 1
, it follows that

\displaystyle(1-p^{n})\,G=\left[np^{n}\int_{0}^{1-p}{1\over(1-w)^{n+1}}\,\mathrm{d}w\right]G\leqslant\mathbb{E}\left[\widehat{G}\right]\leqslant np^{n}\int_{0}^{1-p}{1\over(1-w)^{n+1}}\,\mathrm{d}w={1-p^{n}}.

In other words, the estimator
\widehat{G}
is biased for Geometric populations, with bias given by

\displaystyle\text{Bias}(\widehat{G},G)={1\over 2}\left[{}_{2}F_{1}\left(1,n;n+1;{p\over 2}\right)-p^{n}\,_{2}F_{1}\left(1,n;n+1;{1\over 2}\right)\right]-{1\over 2-p}

(3.6)

which satisfies

\displaystyle-p^{n}G\leqslant\text{Bias}(\widehat{G},G)\leqslant 1-p^{n}-G.

4
Characterization of the gamma family

The next result presents a characterization of the gamma family which, to the best of our knowledge, has not previously appeared in the literature.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
X
be a nonnegative, absolutely continuous random variable belonging to a
scale family, with density
f_{X}
. Assume that for every
z>0
there
exists a function
\xi(z)>0
such that, for almost every
t>0
,

\exp\{-zt\}f_{X}(t)=\frac{\mathscr{L}_{X}(z)}{\xi(z)}\,f_{X}\left(\frac{t}{\xi(z)}\right),

(4.1)

where
\mathscr{L}_{X}(z)=\mathbb{E}[\exp\{-zX\}]
denotes the Laplace transform of
X
, and
F_{X}
is its corresponding CDF.
Then
X\sim\text{Gamma}(\alpha,\lambda)
, for some
\alpha>0
,
\lambda>0
(gamma distribution). More precisely,

f_{X}(x)={\lambda^{\alpha}\over\Gamma(\alpha)}\,x^{\alpha-1}\exp\{-\lambda x\},\quad x>0,

and necessarily

\xi(z)=\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+z}.

Conversely, if
X\sim\text{Gamma}(\alpha,\lambda)
, then Identity
(
4.1
) holds for all
z>0
.

Proof.

Since
X
belongs to a scale family, there exists a baseline density
g
such that

\displaystyle f_{X}(x)=\frac{1}{\theta}\,g\left(\frac{x}{\theta}\right),

(4.2)

for some
\theta>0
. Without loss of generality, assume that
\theta=1
. Equation (
4.1
) can therefore be rewritten as

\displaystyle\exp\{-zt\}\,g\left({t}\right)=\frac{\mathscr{L}_{X}(z)}{\xi(z)}\,g\left(\frac{t}{\xi(z)}\right).

Taking logarithms and differentiating with respect to
t
yields

\displaystyle-z+\left[\log(g({t}))\right]^{\prime}=\frac{1}{\xi(z)}\left[\log\left(g\left(\frac{t}{\xi(z)}\right)\right)\right]^{\prime}.

Differentiating once more with respect to
t
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left[\log(g\left({t}\right))\right]^{\prime\prime}=\frac{1}{\xi^{2}(z)}\left[\log\left(g\left(\frac{t}{\xi(z)}\right)\right)\right]^{\prime\prime}.

Letting
x=t
and
y=t/\xi(z)
, the above equation becomes

\displaystyle x^{2}\bigl[\log g(x)\bigr]^{\prime\prime}=y^{2}\bigl[\log g(y)\bigr]^{\prime\prime},

for all
x,y>0
. Hence, the quantity
x^{2}[\log g(x)]^{\prime\prime}
must be constant. That is, there exists
C\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle x^{2}\bigl[\log g(x)\bigr]^{\prime\prime}=C.

Solving this differential equation yields

\displaystyle g(x)=\exp\{K\}x^{-C}\exp\{Dx\},

(4.3)

for some constants
K
,
C
, and
D
. Since
g
is a probability density on
(0,\infty)
, it follows that
-C>-1
and
D<0
. Setting
\alpha=1-C
and
\lambda=-D
, normalization together with (
4.2
) (with
\theta=1
) and (
4.3
) implies that
X\sim\mathrm{Gamma}(\alpha,\lambda)
.

The proof of the reciprocal implication is immediate and therefore omitted.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
4.1
,

\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\{-zX\}\mathds{1}_{\{X\leqslant t\}}\right]}{\mathscr{L}_{X}(z)}=F_{X}\left(\frac{t}{\xi(z)}\right),\quad t>0;\qquad\xi(z)=\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+z},\quad z>0.

4.1
Unbiased Gini estimator in gamma populations

Let
X
be a nonnegative, absolutely continuous random variable belonging to a
scale family, with density
f_{X}
, satisfying (
4.1
). Using the notation
F_{Y_{z}}
introduced in (
3.2
), Theorem
4.1
implies that
X\sim\text{Gamma}(\alpha,\lambda)
and Corollary
4.2
yields

\displaystyle F_{Y_{z}}(t)=F_{X}\left(\frac{t}{\xi(z)}\right)=F_{\xi(z)X}(t),\quad\xi(z)=\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+z}.

In other words,

\displaystyle Y_{z}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\xi(z)X\sim\text{Gamma}(\alpha,\lambda+z).

Hence,
E[Y_{z}]=\alpha/(\lambda+z)
,
\mathscr{L}_{X}(z)=\lambda^{\alpha}/(\lambda+z)^{\alpha}
, and since the Gini coefficient is scale invariant,

\displaystyle G(Y_{z})=G(X)=G=\frac{\Gamma(2\alpha+1)}{2^{2\alpha}\Gamma(\alpha+1)^{2}},

where the last identity is well known in the literature and can be easily obtained from Proposition
2.1
.
Applying Theorem
3.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\widehat{G}\right]=n\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}[Y_{z}]G(Y_{z})\mathscr{L}_{X}^{\,n}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z=G\left[n\alpha\lambda^{n\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}{1\over(\lambda+z)^{n\alpha+1}}\,\mathrm{d}z\right]=G.

That is, under gamma populations the Gini estimator
\widehat{G}
is unbiased for the Gini coefficient
G
, as previously shown in
Baydil et al., 2025
and
Vila and Saulo, 2025a
;
Vila and Saulo, 2025b
.

5
Simulation study

This section reports a Monte Carlo study assessing the finite-sample performance of the Gini
estimator in (
3.1
) (uncorrected) and its bias-corrected version obtained by subtracting
an estimate of the finite-sample bias. The correction is implemented in a plug-in fashion using
maximum likelihood (ML) estimation of the distributional parameter.

Let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
be an i.i.d. sample from a Poisson or Geometric distribution.
The uncorrected estimator is given in (
3.1
), that is,

\displaystyle\widehat{G}=\begin{cases}\dfrac{{1\over\binom{n}{2}}\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}|X_{i}-X_{j}|}{2\overline{X}},&\text{if}\ \sum_{k=1}^{n}X_{k}>0\ \text{a.s.},\\[8.5359pt]
0,&\text{if}\ \sum_{k=1}^{n}X_{k}=0\ \text{a.s.},\end{cases}

where

\overline{X}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}X_{k}/n

denotes the sample mean. The corrected estimator is defined as

\widehat{G}^{\,c}=\widehat{G}-\widehat{\mathrm{Bias}}_{n}(\widehat{\theta}),

(5.1)

where
\widehat{\theta}
is the ML estimator of the model parameter and
\widehat{\mathrm{Bias}}_{n}(\widehat{\theta})
(Equations (
3.5
) and (
3.6
))
is obtained by evaluating the analytical bias expression of
\widehat{G}

\widehat{\theta}
and the

Sample sizes are set to

6
Concluding remarks

In this paper, we derived a general representation for the expectation of the Gini coefficient estimator based on the Laplace transform of the underlying distribution and the Gini coefficient of its exponentially tilted version. This formulation yields a new characterization of the gamma family within the class of nonnegative scale families via a stability property under exponential tilting. As direct applications, we showed that the Gini estimator is biased for Poisson and geometric populations, while remaining unbiased for gamma models. By exploiting the derived bias expressions, we proposed plug-in bias-corrected estimators and assessed their finite-sample performance through Monte Carlo simulations, which confirmed substantial improvements over the uncorrected estimator.

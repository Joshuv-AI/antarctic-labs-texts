Locally Permuted Low Rank Column-wise Sensing
Ahmed Ali Abbasi, Namrata Vaswani

Abstract
We precisely formulate, and provide a solution for, the Low Rank Columnwise Sensing (LRCS) problem when some of the observed data is scrambled/permuted/unlabeled. This problem, which we refer to as permuted LRCS, lies at the intersection of two distinct topics of recent research: unlabeled sensing and low rank column-wise (matrix) sensing. We introduce a novel generalization of the recently developed Alternating Gradient Descent and Minimization (AltGDMin) algorithm to solve this problem. We also develop an alternating minimization (AltMin) solution. We show, using simulation experiments, that both converge but Permuted-AltGDmin is much faster than Permuted-AltMin.



I
Introduction

In this work, we precisely formulate, and develop a novel solutions for solving, the Low Rank Columnwise Sensing (LRCS) problem
[
1
]
when some of the observed data is scrambled/permuted/unlabeled, e.g., due to record linkage errors. This permuted LRCS problem can also be understood as the well-studied multi-view unlabeled sensing problem with two modifications: (i) different sensing matrices for each column; and (ii) a low rank assumption on the matrix formed by the unknown signal sequence. As we explain in Sec.
I-B
, the single and multi-view unlabeled sensing problems have been extensively studied and so has the un-permuted LRCS problem. Potential applications of permuted-LRCS include (i) multi-task representation learning
[
1
,
2
]
with record linkage errors that cause some rows of the observed data to get permuted, and (ii) LR model based accelerated dynamic MRI
[
1
,
3
]
with permutation errors due to k-space trajectory coding mistakes.

Problem Setup and Notation

For a low rank matrix
{\bm{X}}^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times q}
, with
rank({\bm{X}}^{\star})=r\ll\min(n,q)
, we observe
m\ll\min(n,q)
permuted column-wise measurements
\bm{y}_{k}
. That is,

\bm{y}_{k}:=\bm{P}^{*}\bm{A}_{k}\bm{x}^{*}_{k}\text{ for all }k\in[q],

(1)

where
\bm{A}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
is the known measurement matrix,
\bm{x}^{*}_{k}
is the
k
-th column of
unknown

{\bm{X}}^{*}
, and

\bm{P}^{*}
is an
m\times m

unknown
permutation matrix. Given
\bm{y}_{k}
and
\bm{A}_{k}
, the goal is to recover
{\bm{X}}^{*}
and
\bm{P}^{*}
. To make our problem tractable with
m<n
, we assume that

(i) the same permutation
\bm{P}^{*}
acts on all columns of
\bm{Y}
and
\bm{P}^{*}
is block-diagonal with blocks of size
s
with
s
small enough so that
m/s>r
(
s
-local permutation);
and

ii) right singular vectors of
{\bm{X}}^{*}
are incoherent, that is
\lVert\bm{x}_{k}^{*}\rVert_{2}\leq\mu{\sigma_{\max}^{*}}\sqrt{r/q}\text{ for all }k\in[q]
, where
{\sigma_{\max}^{*}}
denotes the largest singular value of
{\bm{X}}^{*}
.

Both of these assumptions are standard ones commonly used in the multi-view unlabeled sensing or LRCS literature respectively. See Sec.
I-B
.

We factor
{\bm{X}}^{\star}:={\bm{U}}^{\star}{}{\bm{B}^{\star}}
where
{\bm{U}}^{\star}{}
is the
n\times r
matrix of left singular vectors of
{\bm{X}}^{\star}
with nonzero singular values.

Formally, the set of
m\times m

s
-local permutation matrices
\Pi_{m,s}
is defined as

\displaystyle\Pi_{m,s}\coloneq

\displaystyle\{\bm{P}^{*}\mid\bm{P}^{*}=\text{blockdiag}(\bm{P}_{1}^{*},\cdots,\bm{P}_{m/s}^{*}),\bm{P}_{i}^{*}\in\Pi_{s}\}

(2)

where
\Pi_{s}
is the set of all
s\times s
permutation matrices, i.e.,

\Pi_{s}\coloneq\{\bm{P}\mid\bm{P}\in\{0,1\}^{s\times s},\bm{P}\mathbbm{1}_{s}=\mathbbm{1}_{s},\bm{P}^{\intercal}\mathbbm{1}_{s}=\mathbbm{1}_{s}\}

s
.

[
4

6

7

[

.

3
The LRCS problem and AltMin and AltGDmin algorithms

The LRCS problem, or its generalization called the LR phase retrieval problem, have been extensively studied in the last five years
[
20
,
21
,
22
,
23
,
1
,
24
]
. Well known solutions include a very slow convex relaxation
[
22
]
, a faster alternating minimization (AltMin) algorithm
[
21
,
23
]
, and an even faster gradient descent (GD) based solution approach called Alternating GD and minimization (AltGDmin)
[
1
]
. All results assume right singular vector incoherence or a stronger version of it.
Both AltMin and AltGDmin factor the unknown LR matrix
{\bm{X}}
as
{\bm{X}}={\bm{U}}\bm{B}
with
{\bm{U}}
being
n\times r
and
\bm{B}
being
r\times q
. AltGDmin is a novel modification of the AltMin approach for problems such as LRCS in which minimization w.r.t. one of the two variable subsets,
\bm{B}
, is much faster than that w.r.t. to the other,
{\bm{U}}
. The reason is that the latter decouples column-wise.
After initializing
{\bm{U}}
using a carefully designed spectral initialization, it alternates between updating
\bm{B}
(keeping
{\bm{U}}
fixed) using minimization, and updating
{\bm{U}}
(keeping
\bm{B}
fixed) using one GD step. The updated
{\bm{U}}
is orthonormalized using QR decomposition. In more recent work
[
25
]
, AltGDmin has been studied for communication-efficient LR matrix completion.

I-C

Our Contribution

We introduce and precisely formulate the Permuted LRCS problem and the required locally permuted assumption. We develop a novel generalization of the AltGDmin algorithm to handle the permutations.
Considering squared loss, our goal is to minimize the objective function
f(\bm{P},{\bm{U}},\bm{B})\coloneq\sum_{k=1}^{k=q}\lVert\bm{y}_{k}-\bm{P}\bm{A}_{k}{\bm{U}}\bm{b}_{k}\rVert_{2}^{2}
under the constraints that
\bm{P}
is an
s
-local permutation matrix and
{\bm{U}}
has orthonormal columns.
That is, we need to solve

\min_{{\bm{U}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times r}\mid{\bm{U}}^{\intercal}{\bm{U}}=\mathbf{I},\bm{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times q},\bm{P}\in{\Pi}_{m,s}}f(\bm{P},{\bm{U}},\bm{B}),

(3)

where
\Pi_{m,s}
is defined in (
2
).

The AltGDmin algorithm has so far been used only for problems in which the set of unknowns are split into two variable subsets
{\bm{U}}
and
\bm{B}
. However, in our current problem, the natural split-up involves three subsets
\bm{P},{\bm{U}},\bm{B}
. We develop a generalization of AltGDmin that updates the three subsets in sequence with using minimization for updating
\bm{P}
and
\bm{B}
, and GD for updating
{\bm{U}}
. This is done because the minimizations over
\bm{P}
and over
\bm{B}
are much faster than that over
{\bm{U}}
.
Keeping
\bm{P}
and
{\bm{U}}
fixed, the update of
\bm{B}
involves solving column-wise least squares (LS) problems with total complexity only order
mqnr
.
Keeping
{\bm{U}},\bm{B}
fixed, we show below that, minimizing for
\bm{P}
is the well-known linear assignment problem (LAP) which can be solved exactly using the Hungarian assignment algorithm
[
30
]
. This step has complexity of order
mqn
. The update of
{\bm{U}}
by a full minimization has a complexity of order
mq(nr)^{2}
. Hence, AltGDmin uses GD for updating
{\bm{U}}
with a cost of order
mqnr
.

A second challenging aspect of our problem is that there is no easy way to initialize the permutation matrix. We instead develop a novel modification of the LRCS initialization by adapting the collapsed initialization idea introduced for the ULS problem in
[
13
]
.

We also develop an AltMin based solution. We show, using simulation experiments, that both converge but Permuted-AltGDmin is much faster.

\bm{P}
, keeping
\bm{B}
and
{\bm{U}}
fixed, in (
3

O(ms^{2}+nq(m+r)+msq)=O(mqn)
. That for
\bm{B}

II-A

Perm-AltGDMin Initialization
(t=0)

II-A
1
Formation of collapsed system

Since there is no good way to initialize the permutation matrix, we instead develop a modification of the collapsed initialization idea that was introduced in
[
13
]
for unlabeled sensing. Because of the
s
-locality assumption on the permutation matrix, if we sum consecutive sets of
s
measurements, we would eliminate the permutation matrix. To be precise,
\mathbbm{1}_{s}^{\intercal}\bm{P}^{*}_{i}=\mathbbm{1}_{s}^{\intercal}
since
\bm{P}^{*}_{i}
is an
s\times s
permutation matrix. Define a
(m/s)\times m
matrix that is block diagonal with
m/s
blocks (each block is a row of length
s
),

\bm{C}_{cllps}:=blkdiag(\mathbbm{1}_{s}^{\intercal},\mathbbm{1}_{s}^{\intercal},\dots,\mathbbm{1}_{s}^{\intercal})

For each
k\in[q]
, define

\displaystyle\bm{y}_{k,cllps}:=\bm{C}_{cllps}\bm{y}_{k},\ \bm{A}_{k,cllps}:=\bm{C}_{cllps}\bm{A}_{k}

(4)

Thus,
\bm{A}_{k,cllps}\in\mathbb{R}^{(m/s)\times n}
and
\bm{y}_{k,cllps}\in\mathbb{R}^{(m/s)\times 1}
and these satisfy

\bm{y}_{k,cllps}=\bm{A}_{k,cllps}\bm{x}^{\star}_{k}

{\bm{U}}^{(0)}

\sum_{k=1}^{q}\bm{A}_{k,cllps}^{\intercal}\bm{y}_{k,cllps}\bm{e}_{k}^{\intercal}

3
B^{(0)}
initialization (line 6)

Given
{\bm{U}}^{(0)}
, we obtain
\bm{b}_{k}^{(0)}\in\mathbb{R}^{r}
by minimizing the collapsed least squares problem with
(\bm{m/s})
measurements and
\bm{A}_{k,cllps}{\bm{U}}^{(0)}\in\mathbb{R}^{(m/s)\times r}
as the sensing matrix,
\bm{b}_{k}^{(0)}=\argmin_{\bm{b}}\lVert\bm{y}_{k,cllps}-\bm{A}_{k,cllps}{\bm{U}}^{(0)}\bm{b}\rVert_{2}^{2}
. This has the closed form solution:

\bm{b}_{k}^{(0)}=(\bm{A}_{k,cllps}{\bm{U}}^{(0)})^{\dagger}\bm{y}_{k,cllps}\,\text{ for all }k\in[q].

(5)

\widehat{\bm{y}}_{k}^{(1)}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}

II-B

Perm-AltGDMin Iterations (
t\geq 1
)

Given
{\bm{U}}^{(t)},\bm{B}^{(t)}
, we can obtain
\widehat{\bm{y}}_{k}^{(t+1)}=\bm{A}_{k}{\bm{U}}^{(t)}\bm{b}_{k}^{(t)}\,\text{ for all }k\in[q]
. We use this to first estimate the permutation matrix, followed by then updating
{\bm{U}}
and
\bm{B}
.

An alternate approach can be to also use collapsed measurements for the AltGDmin iterations; in this case the algorithm to use would be exactly the same as that for basic LRCS. As we demonstrate in the simulations section, this is much worse than our approach. The reason is, given a good initial estimate of
{\bm{U}},\bm{b}
, we can get a good estimate of
\bm{P}
, and with this, we are able to use many more measurements.

II-B
1
\bm{P}
-update (line 10 of Algorithm
1
)

For
t\geq 1
, the permutation matrix
\bm{P}^{(t)}
-update is by the following linear assignment problem (LAP)

\bm{P}^{(t)}=\argmin_{P\in\Pi_{m,s}}\lVert\bm{Y}-\bm{P}\widehat{\bm{Y}}^{(t)}\rVert_{F}^{2}.

(6)

To see that (
6
) is an LAP,

\displaystyle\argmin_{\bm{P}\in\Pi_{m,s}}\lVert\bm{Y}-\bm{P}\widehat{\bm{Y}}^{(t)}\rVert_{F}^{2}=\argmax_{\bm{P}\in\Pi_{m,s}}\langle\bm{Y},\bm{P}\widehat{\bm{Y}}^{(t)}\rangle,

(7)

(
7
) follows from noting that for any permutation matrix
\bm{P}
,
\lVert\bm{P}\bm{Y}\rVert_{F}=\lVert\bm{Y}\rVert_{F}
so that
\argmin_{\bm{P}\in\Pi_{m,s}}\lVert\bm{Y}-\bm{P}\widehat{\bm{Y}}^{(t)}\rVert_{F}^{2}=

\hskip-2.10007pt\argmin_{\bm{P}\in\Pi_{m,s}}\lVert\bm{Y}\rVert_{F}^{2}+\lVert\bm{P}\bm{Y}\rVert_{F}^{2}-2\langle\bm{Y},\bm{P}\widehat{\bm{Y}}^{(t)}\rangle=\argmax_{\bm{P}\in\Pi_{m,s}}\langle\bm{Y},\bm{P}\widehat{\bm{Y}}^{(t)}\rangle.

The objective function in (
7
) is a linear function of the optimization variable
\bm{P}
, making this a linear assignment problem. Because
\bm{P}^{*}\in\Pi_{m,s}
is block-diagonal, (
7
) simplifies to decoupled updates of smaller sizes. That is, for
\bm{P}^{*}=\text{blkdiag}(\bm{P}_{1}^{*},\cdots,\bm{P}_{m/s}^{*})
, with each block of size
s
,

\displaystyle\bm{P}^{(t)}_{i}

\displaystyle=\argmax_{\bm{P}_{i}\in\Pi_{s}}\langle\bm{Y}_{i},\bm{P}_{i}\widehat{\bm{Y}}_{i}^{(t)}\rangle

\displaystyle=\text{trace}(\widehat{\bm{Y}}_{i}^{(t)}\bm{Y}^{\intercal}_{i}\bm{P}_{i})\text{ for all }i\in[m/s].

(8)

Each of the
s
-dimensional
m/s
LAPs in (
8
) can be solved exactly by the Hungarian assignment algorithm in
O(s^{3})
time
[
30
]
. Additionally, the cost of forming
\widehat{\bm{Y}}
is
O(nq(m+r))
, followed by the
O((m/s)\cdot s^{2}q)=O(msq)
cost of forming the
s\times s
block matrices
\widehat{\bm{Y}}_{i}\bm{Y}_{i}^{\intercal}\text{ for all }i\in[m/s]
. Consequently, the total cost of updating the
m/s
blocks in (
8
) is
O(ms^{2}+nq(m+r)+msq)=O(mqn)
.

We emphasize here that, while the linear assignment problem (
7
) can be solved exactly to find the minimum value and
a
minimizer, the minimizer may not be unique. In particular, this means that, in general, there is no guarantee on the quality of the obtained estimate
\bm{P}
. We expect the quality of the estimate to depend on how large
q
is and how close
\widehat{\bm{Y}}
is to the unpermuted version of the
\bm{Y}
, i.e., to
\bm{P}^{*}{}^{\intercal}\bm{Y}
. We will postpone the analysis of this step, and of the complete approach, to future work.

2
Implementation Details

We set the AltGDMin step-size for gradient descent
{\bm{U}}
update as
\eta=0.3/(m{\sigma_{\max}^{*2}})
, and estimate
{\sigma_{\max}^{*}}\simeq\sigma_{\max}(\widehat{\bm{X}}^{(1)})
. This choice of step-size is suggested for AltGDMin (without permuted measurements) in
[
1
]
. For the least-squares update of
{\bm{U}}
in AltMin, we set the step-size as
\eta=1/L
, where
L=\sum_{k=1}^{k=q}\sigma_{\max}^{2}(\bm{A}_{k})\lVert\bm{b}_{k}\rVert_{2}^{2}
is the Lipschitz constant of
f({\bm{U}})=\sum_{k=1}^{k=q}\lVert\bm{y}_{k}-\bm{A}_{k}{\bm{U}}\bm{b}_{k}\rVert_{2}^{2}
. We compute
L
only at
t=0
because computing it at every iteration, requires reevaluating
\lVert\bm{B}^{(t)}\rVert_{F}^{2}
, which is computationally expensive. We use the backslash operator in MATLAB to solve the least-squares problems. For the linear assignment problem, we use the MATLAB ‘matchpairs’ command. For a fast implementation, we do not construct the square matrix
\bm{P}^{(t)}
, instead representing
\bm{P}^{(t)}
by a vector with shuffled entries in the integer range
[1,m]

The run-time plots show that AltGDMin is the fastest algorithm to converge.
The computational complexity of the AltGDMin
{\bm{U}}
-update, which is the complexity of one gradient computation
O(mnqr)
and one QR decomposition
O(nr^{2})
, is
O(mnqr^{2})
. The computation cost of AltMin
{\bm{U}}

\kappa\log(1/\epsilon)
is the iteration complexity and
\kappa

{\bm{U}}
-update least squares objective function
f({\bm{U}})=\sum_{k=1}^{k=q}\lVert\bm{y}_{k}-\bm{A}_{k}{\bm{U}}\bm{b}_{k}\rVert_{2}^{2}
. For
\kappa\log(1/\epsilon)>r
, the latter cost of exact (LS) minimization (i.e. small
\epsilon
) is higher. Also, computing the gradient several times is slow because each computation requires a ‘for’ loop over
q
terms (
10
).
AltMin (Exact) is much slower than AltMin (GD) because it solves a least-squares problem
[\bm{b}_{1}^{\intercal}\otimes\bm{A}_{1}\mid\cdots\mid\bm{b}_{q}^{\intercal}\otimes\bm{A}_{q}]^{\dagger}\bm{y}_{all}
of dimension
nr
using matrix-inversion at each iteration with computational complexity
O(mqn^{2}r^{2})
, whereas the latter does not use matrix inversion, instead using several iterations of gradient descent.

IV
Conclusion

We introduced a novel solution approach, called Perm-AltGDMin, for solving the permuted LRCS problem.

Standard multigraded Hibi rings and Cartwright-Sturmfels ideals
Koji Matsushita, Koichiro Tani

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce standard multigradings on Hibi rings, which are algebras arising from posets. We show that any standard multigrading on a Hibi ring that makes its defining ideal (called the Hibi ideal) homogeneous is induced by a chain of the underlying poset. After that, we calculate the multigraded Hilbert series of Hibi rings by generalizing the theory of P P -partition and we compute the multidegree polynomials of Hibi rings. Furthermore, we characterize Hibi ideals that are Cartwright-Sturmfels ideals.

1.
Introduction

We assume that all posets and lattices appearing in this paper are finite.
Let
L
be a lattice and let
\Bbbk
be a field.
Let
S_{L}=\Bbbk[x_{\alpha}:\alpha\in L]
be the polynomial ring over
\Bbbk
and
I_{L}
be the ideal of
S_{L}
generated by

\mathcal{F}_{L}\coloneqq\{f_{\alpha,\beta}:\alpha,\beta\in L\text{ are incomparable.}\},

where
f_{\alpha,\beta}\coloneqq x_{\alpha}x_{\beta}-x_{\alpha\wedge\beta}x_{\alpha\vee\beta}

for incomparable elements
\alpha,\beta\in L
.
A
compatible monomial order
is a monomial order
\preceq
on
S_{L}
such that

\ini_{\preceq}(f_{\alpha,\beta})=x_{\alpha}x_{\beta}

for all incomparable elements
\alpha,\beta\in L
.
It is known that the following are equivalent (
[
10
]
, see also
[
9
, Chapter 6]
):

(1)

I_{L}
is a prime ideal;

(2)

L
is a distributive lattice;

(3)

\mathcal{F}_{L}
is a Gröbner basis of
I_{L}
with respect to a compatible monomial order.

When the equivalent conditions hold, the quotient ring
S_{L}/I_{L}
is called the
Hibi ring
of
L
, introduced in
[
10
]
.
In this paper, we call
I_{L}
the
Hibi ideal
of
L
if the equivalent condition holds.
For more details on Hibi rings, see
[
9
, Chapter 6]
.

To study Hibi rings, we use a one-to-one correspondence between posets and distributive lattices.
Let
P
be a poset equipped with a partial order
\leq_{P}
. A
poset ideal
of
P
is a subset
\alpha
of
P
satisfying that, whenever
a\in\alpha
and
b\in P
with
b\leq_{P}a
, one has
b\in\alpha
.
Let
L(P)
be the set of poset ideals of
P
.
Since the empty set and
P
itself are elements of
L(P)
, and since
\alpha\cap\beta
and
\alpha\cup\beta
belong to
L(P)
for all

\alpha,\beta\in L(P)
, the set
L(P)
forms a distributive lattice ordered by inclusion,
where the join and meet operations correspond to the union and intersection, respectively.
Conversely, the poset
P
can be recovered from
L(P)
as follows:
We say that an element
\alpha\in L(P)\setminus\{\emptyset\}
is
join-irreducible
if, whenever
\alpha=\beta\cup\gamma
with
\beta,\gamma\in L(P)
, one has either
\alpha=\beta
or
\alpha=\gamma
.
Then the subposet of
L(P)
consisting of all join-irreducible elements coincides with
P
.
This is well known as Birkhoff’s representation theorem
[
1
]
.
We denote the Hibi ring arising from
P
by
\Bbbk[P]
, that is,
\Bbbk[P]:=S_{L(P)}/I_{L(P)}
.

Usually, the Hibi ring
S_{L}/I_{L}
of a distributive lattice
L
is regarded as a standard
\mathbb{Z}
-graded ring by setting
\deg(x_{\alpha})=1
.
In this paper, we introduce a standard multigrading on the Hibi ring and study its properties.

First, we discuss how to define standard multigradings on Hibi rings, i.e., how to define a multigrading on
S_{L(P)}
for a poset
P
such that
I_{L(P)}
is homogeneous.
In this paper, we use the term
homogeneous
in the multigraded sense.
We give a multigrading on
S_{L(P)}
induced by a chain of
P

(Proposition
2.1
) and show that any multigrading that makes
I_{L(P)}
a homogeneous ideal coincides with ours (Theorem
2.3
).

After we introduce multigradings, we study three topics about multigraded Hibi rings; multigraded Hilbert series, multidegree polynomials and multigraded generic initial ideals.
We determine the multigraded Hilbert series of multigraded Hibi rings.
In the case where
S_{L(P)}
is considered as the standard
\mathbb{Z}
-graded ring,
the Hilbert series of
\Bbbk[P]
is given in
[
11
, Proposition 2.3]
, where it is computed using the theory of
P
-partition
[
14
]
.
We extend this method to the multigrading case (Theorem
3.2
and Corollary
3.3
).

Moreover, using these results, we calculate the multidegree polynomials
[
12
, Section 8.5]
of multigraded Hibi rings (Proposition
4.3
).
The notion of multidegree is a generalization of the notion of degree of graded rings and modules to a multigraded settings. One can compute the multidegree polynomial from the
\mathcal{K}
-polynomial.
Multidegree polynomials have applications, for example, in algebraic geometry and convex geometry (see, e.g.,
[
2
]
,
[
16
]
).

Finally, we discuss Cartwright-Sturmfels Hibi ideals.
We say that a homogeneous ideal is
Cartwright-Sturmfels
if there exists a squarefree
multigraded Borel-fixed monomial ideal such that its multigraded Hilbert series is
the same as that of the homogeneous ideal. It was shown in
[
7
, Proposition 2.6]

that, if the ground field is infinite, a homogeneous ideal is Cartwright-Sturmfels
if and only if a multigraded generic initial ideal of it is squarefree.
In many classes of ideals studied in combinatorial commutative algebra, the relationship
between these ideals and Cartwright-Sturmfels ideals was studied. Examples include
determinantal ideals
[
3
,
4
]
,
Schubert determinantal ideals
[
8
]
,
binomial edge ideals
[
5
,
6
]
,
and so on.
In this paper, we determine when Hibi ideals are Cartwright-Sturmfels ideals (Theorem
5.5
).

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
,
we introduce standard multigradings on Hibi ideals and study these multigradings.
In Section
3
,
we review the theory of
P
-partition and refine it in order to compute the
multigraded Hilbert series of Hibi rings under our multigradings and compute them.
In Section
4
,
we compute multidegree polynomials of multigraded Hibi rings in terms of chains of distributive lattices.
In Section
5
,
we review the multigraded generic initial ideals and Cartwright-Sturmfels ideals,
and we characterize Hibi ideals that are Cartwright-Sturmfels ideals
under our multigradings.

2.
Multigrading with a chain

In this section, we introduce and study multigradings on Hibi rings.
For integers
a
and
b
with
a\leq b
, let
[a,b]:=\{c\in\mathbb{Z}:a\leq c\leq b\}
.
Throughout this section, let
P:=[1,n]
be a poset equipped with
\leq_{P}
.

First, for a nonnegative integer
m
, we define a standard
\mathbb{Z}^{m+1}
-multigrading on
S_{L(P)}
, that is, each variable of
S_{L(P)}
has degree
\mathbf{e}_{i}
for some
i\in[0,m]
where
\mathbf{e}_{i}
stands for the
i
th unit vector of
\mathbb{Z}^{m+1}
.
Let
C=\{c_{1}<_{P}\cdots<_{P}c_{\ell}\}
be a chain of
P
and
C_{i}:=\{c_{1},\ldots,c_{i}\}
for
i=0,1,\ldots,\ell
where we let
C_{0}:=\emptyset
.
Moreover, let
\mathfrak{p}(C):=\{C_{0},C_{1},\ldots,C_{\ell}\}
and let
f_{C}:\mathfrak{p}(C)\to[0,m]
be a map.
Note that for any poset ideal
\alpha
of
P
, the set
\alpha\cap C
belongs to
\mathfrak{p}(C)
.
We define the standard
\mathbb{Z}^{m+1}
-multigrading on
S_{L(P)}
by setting
\deg_{f_{C}}(x_{\alpha}):=\mathbf{e}_{f_{C}(\alpha\cap C)}
.
In particular, when
m=\ell
and
f_{C}(x_{\alpha})=|\alpha\cap C|
for any
\alpha\in L(P)
, we simply write
\deg_{C}
instead of
\deg_{f_{C}}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Work with the same notation as above.
Then
I_{L(P)}
is homogeneous.

Proof.

It is enough to show that for
\alpha,\beta\in L(P)
, the binomial
f_{\alpha,\beta}
is a homogeneous element of
S_{L(P)}
.
Since
\alpha
and
\beta
are poset ideals of
P
, we can write
\alpha\cap C=C_{i}
and
\beta\cap C=C_{j}
for some
i
and
j
.
We may assume that
i\leq j
.
Then we can see that
(\alpha\cup\beta)\cap C=C_{j}
and
(\alpha\cap\beta)\cap C=C_{i}
.
Therefore, we have

\deg_{f_{C}}(x_{\alpha}x_{\beta})=\deg_{f_{C}}(x_{\alpha\cup\beta}x_{\alpha\cap\beta})=\mathbf{e}_{f_{C}(C_{i})}+\mathbf{e}_{f_{C}(C_{j})},

and hence
f_{\alpha,\beta}
is a homogeneous element.
∎

The following theorem says that our grading is essential for
I_{L(P)}
to be homogeneous:

Theorem 2.3
.

Suppose that
S_{L(P)}
is standard
\mathbb{Z}^{m+1}
-multigraded with a degree map
\deg
such that
I_{L(P)}
is homogeneous.
Then there exist a chain
C
of
P
and a map
f_{C}:\mathfrak{p}(C)\to[0,m]
with
\deg=\deg_{f_{C}}
.

Proof.

For a nonnegative integer
k
, we set
L_{k}(P):=\{\alpha\in L(P):|\alpha|=k\}
.
Since
S_{L(P)}
is standard multigraded, we have
\deg(x_{\emptyset})=\mathbf{e}_{i_{0}}
for some
i_{0}\in[0,m]
.
If
\deg(x_{\alpha})=\mathbf{e}_{i_{0}}
for any
\alpha\in L(P)
, then by setting
C=\emptyset
and defining
f_{C}:\mathfrak{p}(C)\to[0,m]
by
f(\emptyset)=i_{0}
, we have
\deg=\deg_{f_{C}}
.
Otherwise, let
j_{1}
be the smallest integer satisfying
M_{j_{1}}:=\{\alpha\in L_{j_{1}}(P):\deg(x_{\alpha})\neq\mathbf{e}_{i_{0}}\}\neq\emptyset
.

We show that
|M_{j_{1}}|=1
and the unique element
\gamma_{1}
in
M_{j_{1}}
is join-irreducible.
If
|M_{j_{1}}|\geq 2
, then for
\alpha,\beta\in M_{j_{1}}
with
\alpha\neq\beta
, we have
\alpha\cap\beta\in L_{k}(P)
for some
k<j_{1}
, and hence
\deg(x_{\alpha\cap\beta})=\mathbf{e}_{i_{0}}
.
Since
\deg(x_{\alpha})\neq\mathbf{e}_{i_{0}}
and
\deg(x_{\beta})\neq\mathbf{e}_{i_{0}}
, the binomial
f_{\alpha,\beta}
is not a homogeneous element, a contradiction because
I_{L(P)}
is homogeneous.
If
\gamma_{1}
is not join-irreducible, then there exist
\alpha,\beta\in L(P)
with
\gamma_{1}=\alpha\cup\beta
,
\alpha\neq\gamma_{1}
and
\beta\neq\gamma_{1}
.
In this case, we can see that
\alpha\in L_{k_{1}}(P)
and
\beta\in L_{k_{2}}(P)
for some
k_{1},k_{2}<j_{1}
, so we have
\deg(x_{\alpha})=\deg(x_{\beta})=\mathbf{e}_{i_{0}}
.
This implies that
f_{\alpha,\beta}
is not a homogeneous element by the same argument as above, a contradiction.

Let
i_{1}
be the integer satisfying
\deg(x_{\gamma_{1}})=\mathbf{e}_{i_{1}}
and consider the subposet
N_{1}:=\{\alpha\in L(P):\gamma_{1}\subset\alpha\}
of
L(P)
, which coincides with the distributive lattice
L(P\setminus\gamma_{1})
.
Then we have
\deg(x_{\alpha})=\mathbf{e}_{i_{0}}
for any
\alpha\in L(P)\setminus N_{1}
.
Indeed,
\alpha
and
\gamma_{1}
are incomparable in
L(P)
, and
\alpha\cap\gamma_{1}\in L_{k}(P)
for some
k<j_{1}
.
Since
\deg(x_{\gamma_{1}})=\mathbf{e}_{i_{1}}
and
\deg(x_{\alpha\cap\gamma_{1}})=\mathbf{e}_{i_{0}}
, the degree of
x_{\alpha}
must be
\mathbf{e}_{i_{0}}
.

We define
j_{2}
as the smallest integer satisfying
M_{j_{2}}:=\{\alpha\in L_{j_{2}}(P)\cap N_{1}:\deg(x_{\alpha})\neq\mathbf{e}_{i_{1}}\}\neq\emptyset
if such an integer exists.
By a similar argument as above, we can see that
|M_{j_{2}}|=1
and the unique element
\gamma_{2}
in
M_{j_{2}}
is a join-irreducible element in
N_{1}
.
Actually,
\gamma_{2}
is also join-irreducible in
L(P)
.
Suppose that there exist
\alpha,\beta\in L(P)
with
\gamma_{2}=\alpha\cup\beta
,
\alpha\neq\gamma_{2}
and
\beta\neq\gamma_{2}
.
Since
\gamma_{1}
is join-irreducible and
\gamma_{1}\subset\gamma_{2}
, we have
\gamma_{1}\subset\alpha
or
\gamma_{1}\subset\beta
.
We may assume that
\gamma_{1}\subset\alpha
.
Since
\gamma_{2}
is join-irreducible in
N_{1}
, we obtain
\beta\in L(P)\setminus N_{1}
, and hence
\alpha\cap\beta\in L(P)\setminus N_{1}
.
We have
\deg(x_{\alpha})=\mathbf{e}_{i_{1}}
and
\deg(x_{\beta})=\deg(x_{\alpha\cap\beta})=\mathbf{e}_{i_{0}}
, so
\deg(x_{\alpha\cup\beta})=\mathbf{e}_{i_{1}}
, a contradiction to
\alpha\cup\beta=\gamma_{2}\in M_{j_{2}}
.

We continue to define
j_{s}
,
M_{j_{s}}
,
\gamma_{j_{s}}
,
N_{s}
, and
i_{s}
for
s\geq 2
by the same procedure.
That is, let
j_{s}
be the smallest integer satisfying
M_{j_{s}}:=\{\alpha\in L_{j_{s}}(P)\cap N_{s-1}:\deg(x_{\alpha})\neq\mathbf{e}_{i_{s-1}}\}\neq\emptyset
if such an integer exists, let
\gamma_{s}
be the unique element in
M_{j_{s}}
, let
N_{s}:=\{\alpha\in L(P):\gamma_{s}\subset\alpha\}
where we let
N_{0}:=L(P)
, and let
i_{s}
be the integer satisfying
\deg(x_{\gamma_{s}})=\mathbf{e}_{i_{s}}
.
Since
P
is finite, this procedure must eventually terminate.
Let
\ell
be the largest integer with
M_{j_{\ell}}\neq\emptyset
.
By the same argument as above, we can see that
\gamma_{s}
is join-irreducible in
L(P)
and
\deg(x_{\alpha})=\mathbf{e}_{i_{s-1}}
for any
s=1,\ldots,\ell+1
and
\alpha\in N_{s-1}\setminus N_{s}
where we let
N_{\ell+1}:=\emptyset
.

Since
\gamma_{s}
is join-irreducible and
\gamma_{1}\subset\cdots\subset\gamma_{\ell}
, the poset ideal
\gamma_{s}
corresponds to an element
c_{s}
in
P
and
C:=\{c_{1},\ldots,c_{\ell}\}
forms a chain in
P
with
c_{1}<_{P}\cdots<_{P}c_{\ell}
.
We define the map
f_{C}:\mathfrak{p}(C)\to[0,m]
by
f_{C}(C_{s})=\mathbf{e}_{i_{s}}
for
s=0,1,\ldots,\ell
.
Then we can see that
\deg_{f_{C}}(x_{\alpha})=\mathbf{e}_{f_{C}(C_{s-1})}=\mathbf{e}_{i_{s-1}}
for any
s=1,\ldots,\ell+1
and
\alpha\in N_{s-1}\setminus N_{s}
.
Therefore, we have
\deg=\deg_{f_{C}}
.
∎

3.
Hilbert series of multigraded Hibi rings

In this section, we compute the multigraded Hilbert series of Hibi rings.

Let
S
be a standard
\mathbb{Z}^{m+1}
-graded polynomial ring.
For a homogeneous ideal
I
, the residue ring

S/I=\bigoplus_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{m+1}}S_{\mathbf{a}}/I_{\mathbf{a}}

can be considered as a
\mathbb{Z}^{m+1}
-graded ring.
The
multigraded Hilbert function
and the
multigraded Hilbert series
of
S/I
are defined to be

\displaystyle\HF(S/I;\mathbf{a})

\displaystyle=\dim_{\Bbbk}(S/I)_{\mathbf{a}},\text{ and }

\displaystyle\HS(S/I;\mathbf{t})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{m+1}}\dim_{\Bbbk}(S/I)_{\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{t}^{\mathbf{a}},\text{ respectively.}

To calculate the Hilbert series of Hibi rings, we use the theory of Stanley’s
P
-partition (
[
14
]
, see also
[
15
, Chapter 4.5]
).
Let
P=[1,n]
be a poset equipped with a partial order
\leq_{P}
.
We assume that
P
is compatible with the usual ordering of
[1,n]
, that is, if
i<_{P}j
, then
i<j
in
\mathbb{Z}
.
Let
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
be the symmetric group of degree
n
and let
e(P):=\{(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}:i<j\text{ if }p_{i}<_{P}p_{j}\}
, which is called the
Jordan-Hölder set
of
P
.
A map
\sigma:P\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
is
order-reversing
if
i\leq_{P}j
implies
\sigma(i)\geq\sigma(j)
in
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
Let
\mathcal{A}(P)
be the set of order-reversing maps.
We can construct an order-reversing map from a poset ideal of
P
; for
\alpha\in L(P)
, we define an order-reversing map
\sigma_{\alpha}:P\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
by setting
\sigma_{\alpha}(i)=1
if
i\in\alpha
and
\sigma_{\alpha}(i)=0
otherwise.
Then there is the following bijection for any
z\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
(cf.
[
11
, Proposition 2.3]
):

(1)

\begin{split}\{x_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{\alpha_{z}}\in\Bbbk[P]:\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{z}\in L(P)\}&\to\{\sigma\in\mathcal{A}(P):\sigma(i)\leq z\text{ for any }i\in P\},\\
x_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{\alpha_{z}}&\mapsto\sigma=\sigma_{\alpha_{1}}+\cdots+\sigma_{\alpha_{z}}.\end{split}

Let
C:=\{c_{1}<_{P}\cdots<_{P}c_{\ell}\}
be a chain in
P
.
We first consider the Hilbert series of
\Bbbk[P]
multigraded by
C
.
If a monomial
x_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{\alpha_{z}}
(
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{z}\in L(P)
) has the degree
\mathbf{a}=(a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell})
, then we have
|\{\alpha_{j}:\alpha_{j}\cap C=C_{i}\}|=a_{i}
for each
i=0,1,\ldots,\ell
,
equivalently, if we set
\sigma=\sigma_{\alpha_{1}}+\cdots+\sigma_{\alpha_{z}}
, then we obtain

\begin{split}a_{\ell}&=\sigma(c_{\ell}),\\
a_{\ell-1}&=\sigma(c_{\ell-1})-\sigma(c_{\ell}),\\
&\;\;\vdots\\
a_{1}&=\sigma(c_{1})-\sigma(c_{2}),\\
a_{0}&=z-\sigma(c_{1}).\end{split}

Therefore, it follows from (
1
) that for any
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\ell+1}
, we have the following one-to-one correspondence:

(2)

\begin{split}\{x_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{\alpha_{s(\mathbf{a})}}\in\Bbbk[P]:\;\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{s(\mathbf{a})}&\in L(P),\;\deg_{C}(x_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{\alpha_{s(\mathbf{a})}})=\mathbf{a}\}\\
&\updownarrow\\
\{\sigma\in\mathcal{A}(P):\sigma(i)\leq s(\mathbf{a})\text{ for any }i\in P,\;&\sigma(c_{j})=a_{\ell}+\cdots+a_{j}\text{ for any }j\in[1,\ell]\},\end{split}

where
s(\mathbf{a}):=a_{0}+\cdots+a_{\ell}
.

For
\pi=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, we say that a function
f:[1,n]\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
is
\pi
-compatible
if
f
satisfies the following conditions:

(i)

f(p_{1})\geq f(p_{2})\geq\cdots\geq f(p_{n})
and

(ii)

f(p_{i})>f(p_{i+1})
if
p_{i}>p_{i+1}
.

For any function
f:[1,n]\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, there is a unique permutation
\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
such that
f
is
\pi
-compatible (
[
15
, Lemma 4.5.1]
).
Let
S_{\pi}
be the set of all
\pi
-compatible functions.

Lemma 3.1

(
[
15
, Lemma 4.5.3]
)
.

One has
\mathcal{A}(P)=\bigsqcup_{\pi\in e(P)}S_{\pi}
.

For
\pi=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
and
i\in[1,n]
, let
d_{i}(\pi):=|\{j\in[i,n]:p_{j}>p_{j+1}\}|
where we let
p_{n+1}:=n+1
.
In addition, let
\ind(\pi,c_{j})
be the integer satisfying
c_{j}=p_{\ind(\pi,c_{j})}
.
Note that
\ind(\pi,c_{1})<\ind(\pi,c_{2})<\cdots<\ind(\pi,c_{\ell})
if
\pi\in e(P)
.

We are now ready to compute the Hilbert series of
\Bbbk[P]
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
P=[1,n]
be a poset and let
C=\{c_{1}<_{P}\cdots<_{P}c_{\ell}\}
be a chain in
P
.
Suppose that
\Bbbk[P]
is multigraded by
C
.
Then we have

(3)

\displaystyle\HS(\Bbbk[P];\mathbf{t})=\sum_{\pi\in e(P)}\prod_{i\in[0,\ell]}\frac{t_{i}^{d_{\ind(\pi,c_{i})}(\pi)-d_{\ind(\pi,c_{i+1})}(\pi)}}{(1-t_{i})^{\ind(\pi,c_{i+1})-\ind(\pi,c_{i})}},

where we let
\ind(\pi,c_{0}):=0
,
\ind(\pi,c_{\ell+1}):=n+1
,
d_{0}(\pi):=d_{1}(\pi)
and
d_{n+1}(\pi):=0
.

Proof.

For
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\ell+1}_{\geq 0}
, let
\mathcal{A}(P)_{\mathbf{a}}:=\{\sigma\in\mathcal{A}(P):\sigma(i)\leq s(\mathbf{a})\text{ for any }i\in P,\;\sigma(c_{j})=a_{\ell}+\cdots+a_{j}\text{ for any }j\in[1,\ell]\}
.
From the one-to-one correspondence (
2
), we obtain
\HF(\Bbbk[P];\mathbf{a})=|\mathcal{A}(P)_{\mathbf{a}}|
.
Moreover, for
\pi\in e(P)
and a function
\sigma:[1,n]\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we can see that
\sigma\in S_{\pi}\cap\mathcal{A}(P)_{\mathbf{a}}
if and only if
\sigma
satisfies

(4)

\begin{split}&\sigma(c_{j})=a_{\ell}+a_{\ell-1}+\cdots+a_{j}\text{ for any }j\in[1,\ell]\text{ and}\\
&s(\mathbf{a})-d_{0}(\pi)\geq\sigma(p_{1})-d_{1}(\pi)\geq\sigma(p_{2})-d_{2}(\pi)\geq\cdots\geq\sigma(p_{n})-d_{n}(\pi)\geq 0.\end{split}

Therefore, the number of functions satisfying condition (
4
) is

\prod_{i\in[0,\ell]}\left(\!\!\binom{(\sigma(c_{i})-d_{\ind(\pi,c_{i})}(\pi))-(\sigma(c_{i+1})-d_{\ind(\pi,c_{i+1})}(\pi))+1}{\ind(\pi,c_{i+1})-\ind(\pi,c_{i})-1}\!\!\right)\\
=\prod_{i\in[0,\ell]}\left(\!\!\binom{a_{i}-d_{\ind(\pi,c_{i})}(\pi)+d_{\ind(\pi,c_{i+1})}(\pi)+1}{\ind(\pi,c_{i+1})-\ind(\pi,c_{i})-1}\!\!\right),

where we let
\sigma(c_{0}):=s(\mathbf{a})
.
By Lemma
3.1
, we get

\displaystyle\HS(\Bbbk[P];\mathbf{t})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\ell+1}_{\geq 0}}\left(\sum_{\pi\in e(P)}|S_{\pi}\cap\mathcal{A}(P)_{\mathbf{a}}|\right)\mathbf{t}^{\mathbf{a}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\pi\in e(P)}\prod_{i\in[0,\ell]}\left(\sum_{a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\left(\!\!\binom{a_{i}-d_{\ind(\pi,c_{i})}(\pi)+d_{\ind(\pi,c_{i+1})}(\pi)+1}{\ind(\pi,c_{i+1})-\ind(\pi,c_{i})-1}\!\!\right)t_{i}^{a_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\pi\in e(P)}\prod_{i\in[0,\ell]}\frac{t_{i}^{d_{\ind(\pi,c_{i})}(\pi)-d_{\ind(\pi,c_{i+1})}(\pi)}}{(1-t_{i})^{\ind(\pi,c_{i+1})-\ind(\pi,c_{i})}}.

∎

Let
m
be a nonnegative integer and let
f:\mathfrak{p}(C)\to[0,m]
be a map.
In the case where
S_{L(P)}
is multigraded by
f_{C}
, we can calculate the Hilbert series of
\Bbbk[P]
by replacing
t_{i}
with
t_{f(C_{i})}
in (
3
), so we immediately get the following corollary:

Corollary 3.3
.

Work with the same notation as above. Suppose that
S_{L(P)}
is multigraded by
f_{C}
.
Then we have

\HS(\Bbbk[P];\mathbf{t})=\sum_{\pi\in e(P)}\prod_{i\in[0,\ell]}\frac{t_{f(C_{i})}^{d_{\ind(\pi,c_{i})}(\pi)-d_{\ind(\pi,c_{i+1})}(\pi)}}{(1-t_{f(C_{i})})^{\ind(\pi,c_{i+1})-\ind(\pi,c_{i})}}.

In particular, when
m=0
, we obtain the Hilbert series of a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded Hibi ring, which coincides with the result of
[
11
, Proposition 2.3]
.

Example 3.4

\displaystyle=

Example 3.5
.

Let
P:=[1,n]
be an

-element antichain, that is,

and
j
are incomparable for any two distinct elements

.
Let
C:=\{n\}
and suppose that

is multigraded by

.
From Theorem

, we have

\HS(\Bbbk[P];\mathbf{t})=\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\frac{t_{0}^{d_{0}(\pi)-d_{\ind(\pi,n)}(\pi)}t_{1}^{d_{\ind(\pi,n)}(\pi)}}{(1-t_{0})^{\ind(\pi,n)}(1-t_{1})^{n+1-\ind(\pi,n)}}.

For
k\in[1,n]

k_{1}\in[0,k-2]
and
k_{2}\in[0,n-k-1]
, we consider a permutation
\pi=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
such that
p_{k}=n
,
|\{i\in[1,k-1]:p_{i}>p_{i+1}\}|=k_{1}
and
|\{i\in[k+1,n]:p_{i}>p_{i+1}\}|=k_{2}
.
The number of such permutations is
\binom{n-1}{k-1}A_{k_{1},k-1}A_{k_{2},n-k}
where

denotes the

, which is the number of permutations of the numbers

to

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\HS(\Bbbk[P];\mathbf{t})

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n-2}A_{k_{1},n-1}t_{0}^{k_{1}}}{(1-t_{0})^{n}(1-t_{1})}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{\binom{n-1}{k-1}\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{k-2}\sum_{k_{2}=0}^{n-k-1}A_{k_{1},k-1}A_{k_{2},n-k}t_{0}^{k_{1}}t_{1}^{k_{2}+1}}{(1-t_{0})^{k}(1-t_{1})^{n+1-k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{A_{n-1}(t_{0})}{(1-t_{0})^{n}(1-t_{1})}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{\binom{n-1}{k-1}A_{k-1}(t_{0})A_{n-k}(t_{1})t_{1}}{(1-t_{0})^{k}(1-t_{1})^{n+1-k}},

where

(we define

) denotes the

.

By setting

where we used the recurrence relation
A_{n}(t)=A_{n-1}(t)+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{k-1}A_{k-1}(t)A_{n-k}(t)t
for the Eulerian polynomials (see, e.g.,
[

, Theorem 1.5]

4.
Multidegree polynomials of multigraded Hibi rings

In this section, we compute multidegree polynomials of multigraded Hibi rings.
For a standard
\mathbb{Z}^{\ell+1}
-graded polynomial ring
S=\Bbbk[x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}]
and a homogeneous ideal
I
, the Hilbert series of
S/I
can be
written as

\HS(S/I;\mathbf{t})=\frac{\mathcal{K}(S/I;\mathbf{t})}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}(1-\mathbf{t}^{\deg(x_{i})})}.

The numerator
\mathcal{K}(S/I;\mathbf{t})\in\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{t}]
is called the
\mathcal{K}
-polynomial
of
S/I
.

Definition 4.1

(See, e.g.,
[
12
, Definition 8.45]
)
.

The
multidegree polynomial
of a
\mathbb{Z}^{\ell+1}
-graded ring
S/I
is the sum
\mathcal{C}(S/I;\mathbf{t})\in\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{t}]

of all terms in

\mathcal{K}(S/I;1-\mathbf{t})\coloneqq\mathcal{K}(S/I;1-t_{0},1-t_{1},\ldots,1-t_{\ell})

having total degree
\codim(S/I)
.

Multidegree polynomials have two characterizing properties.
Firstly, multidegree polynomials are additive.
For a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
, let
\length_{S_{\mathfrak{p}}}(S/I)
denote the length of
(S/I)_{\mathfrak{p}}
as an
S_{\mathfrak{p}}
-module.
For a standard
\mathbb{Z}^{\ell+1}
-graded polynomial ring
S=\Bbbk[x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}]
and a homogeneous ideal
I
,
we have

\mathcal{C}(S/I;\mathbf{t})=\sum_{\mathfrak{p}}\length_{S_{\mathfrak{p}}}(S/I)\cdot\mathcal{C}(S/\mathfrak{p};\mathbf{t}),

where
\mathfrak{p}
are associated primes of
S/I
with
\dim(S/\mathfrak{p})=\dim(S/I)
.
Secondly, multidegree polynomials are degenerative. For a monomial order
\preceq
on
S
,
we have

\mathcal{C}(S/I;\mathbf{t})=\mathcal{C}(S/\ini_{\preceq}(I);\mathbf{t}).

The multidegree polynomial of a prime ideal generated by variables can be easily computed.

Theorem 4.2

(See, e.g.,
[
12
, Theorem 8.44]
)
.

Let
x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{r}}
are variables in
S
. Then

\mathcal{C}(S/(x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{r}});\mathbf{t})=\langle\deg(x_{i_{1}}),\mathbf{t}\rangle\cdots\langle\deg(x_{i_{r}}),\mathbf{t}\rangle,

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the usual inner product.

For more details on multidegree polynomials, see
[
12
, Section 8.5]
.

Now we compute multidegree polynomials of multigraded Hibi rings.
We use the same terminology as in Section
3
.
In addition, let
\mathscr{M}_{L(P)}
be the set of maximal chains of
L(P)
and let
\mathcal{M}^{c}:=L(P)\setminus\mathcal{M}
for
\mathcal{M}\in\mathscr{M}_{L(P)}
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
P=[1,n]
be a poset and let
C=\{c_{1}<_{P}\cdots<_{P}c_{\ell}\}
be a chain in
P
.
Suppose that
\Bbbk[P]
is multigraded by
C
.
Then, the multidegree polynomial of
\Bbbk[P]
is

\mathcal{C}(\Bbbk[P];\mathbf{t})=\sum_{\mathcal{M}\in\mathscr{M}_{L(P)}}\mathbf{t}^{\deg_{C}({\mathcal{M}^{c}})},

where
\deg_{C}({\mathcal{M}^{c}})=\deg_{C}(\prod_{\alpha\in\mathcal{M}^{c}}x_{\alpha})
.

Proof.

Since multidegree polynomials are degenerative, we compute
\mathcal{C}(S_{L(P)}/\ini_{\preceq}(I_{L(P)});\mathbf{t})
with a compatible monomial
order
\preceq
. We regard
\ini_{\preceq}(I_{L(P)})
as a Stanley-Reisner ideal associated with a simplicial
complex
\Delta_{P}
with the vertex set
L(P)
. Since
\ini_{\preceq}(I_{L(P)})
is generated by
x_{\alpha}x_{\beta}

where
\alpha,\beta\in L(P)
are incomparable, the simplicial complex
\Delta_{P}
consists of chains of
L(P)
.
In particular, the facets of
\Delta_{P}
correspond to maximal chains of
L(P)
. Therefore, we have the primary decomposition

\ini_{\preceq}(I_{L(P)})=\bigcap_{\mathcal{M}\in\mathscr{M}_{L(P)}}\mathfrak{p}_{\mathcal{M}^{c}},

where
\mathfrak{p}_{\mathcal{M}^{c}}=(x_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\mathcal{M}^{c})
.
Since the distributive lattice
L(P)
is pure (i.e., all its maximal chains have the same length), the initial ideal

\ini_{\preceq}(I_{L(P)})
is unmixed. We can easily check that, for each maximal chain
\mathcal{M}
, the length of

(S_{L(P)}/\ini_{\preceq}(I_{L(P)}))_{\mathfrak{p}_{\mathcal{M}^{c}}}
is equal to
1
.
Thus, by the additivity of multidegree polynomials and by Theorem
4.2
, we have

\mathcal{C}(S_{L(P)}/\ini_{\preceq}(I_{L(P)});\mathbf{t})=\sum_{\mathcal{M}\in\mathscr{M}_{L(P)}}\mathcal{C}(S_{L(P)}/\mathfrak{p}_{\mathcal{M}^{c}};\mathbf{t})=\sum_{\mathcal{M}\in\mathscr{M}_{L(P)}}\mathbf{t}^{\deg_{C}({\mathcal{M}^{c}})}.

∎

Corollary 4.4
.

Work with the same notation as above. Suppose that
S_{L(P)}
is multigraded by
f_{C}
.
Then we have

\mathcal{C}(\Bbbk[P];\mathbf{t})=\sum_{\mathcal{M}\in\mathscr{M}_{L(P)}}\mathbf{t}^{\deg_{f_{C}}({\mathcal{M}^{c}})},

where
\deg_{f_{C}}({\mathcal{M}^{c}})=\deg_{f_{C}}(\prod_{\alpha\in\mathcal{M}^{c}}x_{\alpha})
.

In particular, when
m=0
, the multidegree polynomial
\mathcal{C}(\Bbbk[P];t_{0})
consists of exactly one term. Its coefficient is the number of maximal chains of
L(P)
, which coincides with the degree
of
\Bbbk[P]
.

5.
When do Hibi ideals become Cartwright-Sturmfels?

First, we review definitions and known results about Cartwright-Sturmfels ideals.
Let
n
and
m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}
be positive integers.
Let
S=\Bbbk[x_{i,j}:1\leq j\leq n,1\leq i\leq m_{j}]
be a standard
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-graded
polynomial ring with
\deg(x_{i,j})=\mathbf{e}_{j}
.

Let
G=\GL_{m_{1}}(\Bbbk)\times\cdots\times\GL_{m_{n}}(\Bbbk)
.
We assume that
G
acts on
S
.
Precisely, let
g=(g^{(1)},\ldots,g^{(n)})\in G
acts on variables in
S
by

g\cdot x_{i,j}=\sum_{k=1}^{m_{j}}g_{k,i}^{(j)}x_{k,j}.

Let
B=\borel_{m_{1}}(\Bbbk)\times\cdots\times\borel_{m_{n}}(\Bbbk)
be the
Borel subgroup

of
G
, where each
\borel_{m_{j}}(\Bbbk)
consists of upper triangular matrices in
\GL_{m_{j}}(\Bbbk)
.
We say that a homogeneous ideal
J\subset S
is
Borel-fixed
if
g\cdot J=J

for all
g\in B
.

Definition 5.1

(
[
7
, Definition 2.4]
)
.

A homogeneous ideal
I
is
Cartwright-Sturmfels
if there exists a squarefree
multigraded Borel-fixed monomial ideal
J
such that
\HS(S/I;\mathbf{t})=\HS(S/J;\mathbf{t})
.

By Definition
5.1
, a homogeneous ideal
I
is
a Cartwright-Sturmfels ideal if and only if
\ini_{\preceq}(I)
is a Cartwright-Sturmfels
ideal for a monomial order because
\HS(S/I;\mathbf{t})=\HS(S/\ini_{\preceq}(I);\mathbf{t})
.
It was shown that, if
\Bbbk
is an infinite field, Cartwright-Sturmfels ideals are characterized
with multigraded generic initial ideals.
As in the
\mathbb{Z}
-graded case, for a homogeneous ideal
I
and a monomial order
\preceq
,
there exists a Zariski open dense set
U\subset G
such that the initial ideal
\ini_{\preceq}(g\cdot I)

stays the same for all
g\in U
. We denote such an initial ideal by
\gin_{\preceq}(I)

and call it the
multigraded generic initial ideal
of
I
with respect to
\preceq
.

Proposition 5.2

(
[
7
, Proposition 2.6]
)
.

Let
I\subset S
be a Cartwright-Sturmfels ideal and let
J\subset S
be a squarefree
Borel-fixed monomial ideal such that
\HS(S/I;\mathbf{t})=\HS(S/J;\mathbf{t})
.
Then,
\gin_{\preceq}(I)=J
for all monomial order
\preceq
.
In particular,
I
is radical and is generated by polynomials whose degree
in each component is either
0
or
1
.

As a corollary of Proposition
5.2
,
if
I
is a Cartwright-Sturmfels ideal, then
\ini_{\preceq}(I)
is a squarefree monomial ideal generated by monomials
whose degree in each component is either
0
or
1
.

Cartwright-Sturmfels ideals are closed under elimination of variables.

Theorem 5.3

(
[
7
, Theorem 2.16 (6)]
)
.

Let
V_{i}\subset S_{\mathbf{e}_{i}}
be vector subspace for
i=1,\ldots,n

and let
R=\Bbbk[V_{1},\ldots,V_{n}]
be the
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-graded subring of
S
.
If
I
is a Cartwright-Sturmfels ideal of
S
, then
I\cap R
is also a
Cartwright-Sturmfels ideal of
R
.

Theorem
5.4
was proved by
Cartwright-Sturmfels in
[
3
]
. Inspired by their work,
Conca-De Negri-Gorla introduced the term “Cartwright-Sturmfels ideal” and studied it.
Theorem
5.4
plays an important role in the proof of
our main theorem about the relationship between Hibi ideals and Cartwright-Sturmfels ideals.

Theorem 5.4

(
[
3
]
)
.

Let
S=\Bbbk[x_{i,j}:1\leq j\leq n,1\leq i\leq m]
be a standard
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-graded
polynomial ring with
\deg(x_{i,j})=\mathbf{e}_{j}
. Let
X=(x_{i,j})
be a matrix of variables
and let
I_{2}(X)
be a homogeneous ideal of
S
generated by all
2
-minors of
X
.
Then,
I_{2}(X)
is a Cartwright-Sturmfels ideal.

Now, we characterize Hibi ideals that are Cartwright-Sturmfels ideals.
For simplicity, we consider the case that
S_{L(P)}
is multigraded by a chain of
P
.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
P
be a poset and
L(P)
be the distributive lattice associated with
P
.
Let

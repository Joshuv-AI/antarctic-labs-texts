An augmented Lagrangian method for strongly regular minimizers in a class of convex composite optimization problems
Chengjing Wang, Peipei Tang

Abstract
In this paper, we study a class of convex composite optimization problems. We begin by characterizing the equivalence between the primal/dual strong second-order sufficient condition and the dual/primal nondegeneracy condition. Building on this foundation, we derive a specific set of equivalent conditions for the perturbation analysis of the problem. Furthermore, we employ the augmented Lagrangian method (ALM) to solve the problem and provide theoretical guarantees for its performance. Specifically, we establish the equivalence between the primal/dual second-order sufficient condition and the dual/primal strict Robinson constraint qualification, as well as the equivalence between the dual nondegeneracy condition and the nonsingularity of Clarke’s generalized Jacobian for the ALM subproblem. These theoretical results form a solid foundation for designing efficient algorithms. Finally, we apply the ALM to the von Neumann entropy optimization problem and present numerical experiments to demonstrate the algorithm’s effectiveness.

Introduction

In this paper, we consider the following convex composite optimization problem

\displaystyle(P^{\prime})\qquad\min_{x\in\mathcal{R}^{n}}f(\mathcal{A}x-b)+h(\mathcal{B}x-c)

where
f:\mathcal{R}^{m_{1}}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{R}}
is a proper lower semicontinuous convex function which may be twice continuously differentiable,
h:\mathcal{R}^{m_{2}}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{R}}
is a proper lower semicontinuous convex function,
\mathcal{A}:\mathcal{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{R}^{m_{1}}
,
\mathcal{B}:\mathcal{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{R}^{m_{2}}
are given linear mappings whose adjoints are denoted as
\mathcal{A}^{*}
and
\mathcal{B}^{*}
, respectively,
b\in\mathcal{R}^{m_{1}}
and
c\in\mathcal{R}^{m_{2}}
are given vectors and
m=m_{1}+m_{2}
. We observe that problem
(P^{\prime})
has wide applications in various fields, such as statistics, machine learning, signal processing, image processing, and so on. One may refer to
Tibshirani 1996
,
Yuan and Lin 2006
,
Friedman et al. 2010
,
Belloni et al. 2011
,
Gaines and Zhou 2018
,
Wang and Tang 2018
,
Lin et al. 2024
,
Nakagaki et al. 2020
,
Wang et al. 2025
, and so on.

The corresponding dual problem related to problem
(P^{\prime})
is given by

\displaystyle(D^{\prime})\quad\quad\quad\min_{{u\in\mathcal{R}^{m_{1}}}\atop{v\in\mathcal{R}^{m_{2}}}}\Big\{f^{*}(u)+h^{*}(v)+\langle b,u\rangle+\langle c,v\rangle\,\Big|\,\mathcal{A}^{*}u+\mathcal{B}^{*}v=0\Big\}.

For any
(x,u,v)\in\mathcal{R}^{n}\times\mathcal{R}^{m_{1}}\times\mathcal{R}^{m_{2}}
, the Lagrangian function corresponding to problem
(P^{\prime})
can be written as

\displaystyle L(x,u,v)

\displaystyle=\inf_{{s\in\mathcal{R}^{m_{1}}}\atop{t\in\mathcal{R}^{m_{2}}}}\Big\{f(\mathcal{A}x-b-s)+h(\mathcal{B}x-c-t)+\langle u,s\rangle+\langle v,t\rangle\Big\}

\displaystyle=-f^{*}(u)-h^{*}(v)-\langle b,u\rangle-\langle c,v\rangle+\langle x,\mathcal{A}^{*}u+\mathcal{B}^{*}v\rangle.

The Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) condition for the composite problem
(P^{\prime})
takes the following form

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{*}u+\mathcal{B}^{*}v=0,\quad\mathcal{A}x-b\in\partial f^{*}(u),\quad\mathcal{B}x-c\in\partial h^{*}(v).

(1.1)

which can be written equivalently as

\displaystyle\widehat{R}(x,u,v):=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\mathcal{A}^{*}u+\mathcal{B}^{*}v\\
u-\operatorname{Prox}_{f^{*}}(\mathcal{A}(x)-b+u)\\
v-\operatorname{Prox}_{h^{*}}(\mathcal{B}(x)-c+v)\end{array}\right]=0.

Since Problem
(P^{\prime})
is very important, it is very meaningful to gain a deep understanding of the perturbation analysis of the problem and the algorithm for solving it.

As for perturbation analysis in optimization, many pioneers have done a lot of foundation work. In the landmark work
Robinson 1980
, Robinson proposed the concept of strong regularity, which is one of the most important concepts to describe the stability of the solution mapping of the following generalized equation

\displaystyle 0\in\phi(x)+\mathcal{M}(x),

(1.5)

where
\phi:\mathcal{R}^{t}\rightarrow\mathcal{R}^{t}
and
\mathcal{M}:\mathcal{R}^{t}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{R}^{t}
is a set-valued mapping. How to characterize the strong regularity of the canonically perturbed KKT system at the solution point of a given optimization problem has always been an important issue. For nonlinear programming problems, Robinson
Robinson 1980
proved that the strong second-order sufficient condition (SSOSC) and the linear independence constraint qualification condition (LICQ) at the solution point is a sufficient condition for the strong regularity of the canonically perturbed KKT system. Proposition 5.38 in
Bonnans and Shapiro 2000
shows that the SSOSC together with the LICQ is equivalent to the strong regularity for nonlinear programming. The characterizations of the strong regularity in terms of the strong second order sufficient condition together with the nondegeneracy condition (generalization of the LICQ condition) for second-order cone programming problems and nonlinear semidefinite programming problems are given in
Bonnans and
Ramírez C. 2005
and
Sun 2006
, respectively. In addition, as the reference
Clarke 1976
says, the property of Clarke’s generalized Jacobian of the KKT system is also an important tool to characterize the stability of the solution. In
Sun 2006
, the nonsingularity of Clarke’s generalized Jacobian of the KKT system at the solution is also proven to be equivalent to the strong regularity. For the following general composite optimization problem

\displaystyle(P)\quad\quad\quad\min_{x\in\mathcal{R}^{n}}g(F(x)),

where
g:\mathcal{R}^{m}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{R}}
is a lower semicontinuous proper convex function and
F:\mathcal{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{R}^{m}
is a smooth mapping, recently under certain mild assumptions, Tang and Wang
Tang and Wang 2025
established the equivalence of the strong regularity of the KKT system, the SSOSC together with the nondegeneracy condition, the nonsingularity of Clarke’s generalized Jacobian of the nonsmooth system at the KKT point.

Note that problem
(P^{\prime})
can be regarded as a special case of problem
(P)
with
F(x):=(\mathcal{A}x-b,\mathcal{B}x-c)
and
g(a,b):=f(a)+h(b)
. Hence it is very interesting to study whether there is a specific equivalence characterization of the perturbation analysis of problem
(P^{\prime})
. Let
(\bar{x},\bar{\mu})
be a solution of the KKT system (
1
) with
\bar{\mu}:=(\bar{u},\bar{v})
,
\bar{u}\in\mathcal{R}^{m_{1}}
and
\bar{v}\in\mathcal{R}^{m_{2}}
.

Although there are numerous algorithms for solving problem
(P^{\prime})
, the augmented Lagrangian method (ALM) which dates back to
Hestenes 1969
;
Powell 1969
and is further studied in
Rockafellar 1976a
;
Rockafellar 1976b
;
Luque 1984
is an efficient approach, especially for large-scale problems. An appealing feature lies in the asymptotic superlinear convergence rate of the dual sequence generated by the ALM under the metric subregularity at the dual solution
Cui et al. 2019
. Meanwhile, the metric subregularity is equivalent to the calmness property (see 3H of
Dontchev and
Rockafellar 2009
). It is also known from
Aragón Artacho and
Geoffroy 2008
that the metric subregularity of the dual solution mapping at a dual optimal solution for the origin is equivalent to the quadratic growth condition at the corresponding dual optimal solution. We need the quadratic growth condition to guarantee the superlinear convergence rate. It is usually difficult to check the quadratic growth condition directly and one may deal with it alternatively by looking for other sufficient conditions. One of such conditions is the second order sufficient condition (SOSC). As shown in Theorem 3.109 of
Bonnans and Shapiro 2000
, under the conditions that
g
is a lower semicontinuous proper convex function and the set
\operatorname{epi}g
outer second order regular, the SOSC implies the quadratic growth condition. Other generic properties including the strict Robinson constraint qualification (SRCQ), the constraint nondegeneracy condition are also important for the corresponding problem. A natural question is whether the other conditions have any relationships with the SOSC. Furthermore, during the iterations of the ALM, we usually need to find an approximate solution of the corresponding inner subproblem efficiently with a given accuracy. If the Hessian of the inner subproblem is invertible we may apply some existing second order method such as the semismooth Newton (SSN) method to find the desired solution efficiently with a high accuracy. How to guarantee and characterize the nonsingularity of the Hessian is another important problem.

In this paper, we make an assumption that some functions are
C^{2}
-cone reducible. It needs to emphasize that the set of the
C^{2}
-cone reducible functions is rich and includes all the indicator functions of
C^{2}
-cone reducible sets. Under the assumption of
C^{2}
-cone reducibility and other implementable assumptions, we will prove the equivalence between the primal/dual SSOSC and the dual/primal nondegeneracy condition, based on which we will establish a specific set of equivalent conditions to characterize the perturbation analysis of problem
(P^{\prime})
. As for the ALM applying to solve problem
(P^{\prime})
, we will establish the equivalence between the primal/dual SOSC and the dual/primal SRCQ. Moreover, we will prove the equivalence between the dual nondegeneracy condition and the nonsingularity of Clarke’s generalized Jacobian of the subproblem of the ALM. All the results are definitely important in both the computational and theoretical study of general composite optimization problems.

The remaining parts of this paper are organized as follows. In Section
2
, we summarize some preliminaries from variational analysis. In Section
3
, we establish a specific set of equivalent conditions for problem (
P^{\prime}
). In Section
4
, we introduce the ALM for problem (
P^{\prime}
) and conduct a detailed theoretical analysis. In Section
5
, we focus on applying the ALM to the von Neumann entropy optimization problem. In Section
6
, we present the numerical experiments. In Section
7
, we give our conclusion.

In this section, we introduce some basic knowledge on variational analysis that are used in the subsequent sections. More details can be refereed in the monographs
Bonnans and Shapiro 2000

Let

be a real valued function with its epigraph
\operatorname{epi}r

\displaystyle\operatorname{epi}r:=\Big\{(x,c)\,\Big|\,x\in\operatorname{dom}r,\ c\in\mathcal{R},\ r(x)\leq c\Big\}

and its conjugate at

defined by

\displaystyle r^{*}(x):=\sup_{u\in\operatorname{dom}r}\Big\{\langle x,u\rangle-r(u)\Big\},

where
\operatorname{dom}r:=\{x\,|\,r(x)<+\infty\}
.
The extended function
r
is said to be proper if
r(x)>-\infty
and
r(x)\not\equiv+\infty
for all
x\in\mathcal{R}^{m}
. For a proper lower semicontinuous function
r
, the Moreau envelope function
e_{\sigma r}
and the proximal mapping
\operatorname{Prox}_{\sigma r}
corresponding to
r
with a parameter
\sigma>0
are defined as

\displaystyle e_{\sigma r}(x)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\inf_{u\in\mathcal{R}^{m}}\Big\{r(u)+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\|u-x\|^{2}\Big\},

\displaystyle\operatorname{Prox}_{\sigma r}(x)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\mathop{\operatorname{argmin}}_{u\in\mathcal{R}^{m}}\Big\{r(u)+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\|u-x\|^{2}\Big\}.

For the extended real-valued function
r
and
x\in\operatorname{dom}r
, the regular/Fréchet subdifferential of
r
at
x
is the set

\displaystyle\widehat{\partial}r(x):=\Big\{v\in\mathcal{R}^{m}\,\Big|\,r(x^{\prime})\geq r(x)+\langle v,x^{\prime}-x\rangle+o(\|x^{\prime}-x\|)\Big\}.

The limiting/Mordukhovich subdifferential of
r
at
x
, denoted by
\partial r(x)
, is given by

\displaystyle\partial r(x):=\Big\{v\in\mathcal{R}^{m}\,\Big|\,\exists\,x^{k}\rightarrow x\ \mbox{with}\ r(x^{k})\rightarrow r(x),\,v^{k}\in\widehat{\partial}r(x^{k})\ \mbox{with}\ v^{k}\rightarrow v\Big\}.

Let
x\in\mathcal{R}^{m}
be a point such that
r(x)
is finite. The lower and upper directional epiderivatives of
r
are defined as follows

\displaystyle r^{\downarrow}_{-}(x,d)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\liminf_{{t\downarrow 0}\atop{d^{\prime}\rightarrow d}}\frac{r(x+td^{\prime})-r(x)}{t},

\displaystyle r^{\downarrow}_{+}(x,d)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sup_{\{t_{n}\}\in\operatorname{\Sigma}}\left(\liminf_{{n\rightarrow\infty}\atop{d^{\prime}\rightarrow d}}\frac{r(x+t_{n}d^{\prime})-r(x)}{t_{n}}\right),

where
\operatorname{\Sigma}
denotes the set of positive real sequences

converging to zero. We say that
r
is directionally epidifferentiable at

r^{\downarrow}_{-}(x,d)=r^{\downarrow}_{+}(x,d)
. Assuming that

and the respective directional epiderivatives

are finite, we can define the lower and upper second order epiderivatives as

\displaystyle r^{\downdownarrows}_{-}(x;d,w)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\liminf_{{t\downarrow 0}\atop{w^{\prime}\rightarrow w}}\frac{r(x+td+\frac{1}{2}t^{2}w^{\prime})-r(x)-tr^{\downarrow}_{-}(x,d)}{\frac{1}{2}t^{2}},

\displaystyle r^{\downdownarrows}_{+}(x;d,w)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sup_{\{t_{n}\}\in\operatorname{\Sigma}}\left(\liminf_{{n\rightarrow\infty}\atop{w^{\prime}\rightarrow w}}\frac{r(x+t_{n}d+\frac{1}{2}t_{n}^{2}w^{\prime})-r(x)-t_{n}r^{\downarrow}_{+}(x,d)}{\frac{1}{2}t_{n}^{2}}\right).

The function
r
is parabolically epidifferentiable at

in a direction

r^{\downdownarrows}_{-}(x;d,\cdot)=r^{\downdownarrows}_{+}(x;d,\cdot)
.

Given a set
C\subseteq\mathcal{R}^{m}
and a point

, recall that the (Painlevé-Kuratowski) outer and inner limit of the set-valued mapping

respectively.
The (Bouligand) tangent/contingent cone to

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{C}(x):=\limsup_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{C-x}{t}=\Big\{y\in\mathcal{R}^{m}\,\Big|\,\exists\ t_{k}\downarrow 0,\ y^{k}\rightarrow y\ \mbox{with}\ x+t_{k}y^{k}\in C\Big\}.

The regular/Fréchet normal cone to

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{C}(x):=\left\{y\in\mathcal{R}^{m}\,\left|\,\limsup_{x^{\prime}{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle C}}{{\rightarrow}}}x}\frac{\langle y,x^{\prime}-x\rangle}{\|x^{\prime}-x\|}\leq 0\right.\right\}=\mathcal{T}_{C}^{\circ}(x).

The limiting/Mordukhovich normal cone to

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{C}(x):=\limsup_{x^{\prime}{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle C}}{{\rightarrow}}}x}\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{C}(x^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{C}(x):=\limsup_{x^{\prime}\rightarrow x}\left\{\operatorname{pos}(x^{\prime}-\operatorname{\Pi}_{C}(x^{\prime}))\right\},\quad\mathcal{N}_{C}(x)^{\circ}=\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{C}(x),

When the set

The SSN based ALM

In this section, we introduce the ALM for the convex composite optimization problem
(P^{\prime})

4.1
The ALM for problem
(P^{\prime})

Firstly, we introduce the details about the ALM. Given
\sigma>0
, we define the augmented Lagrangian function related to problem
(P^{\prime})
as follows

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\sigma}(x;u,v)

\displaystyle:=\sup_{{s\in\mathcal{R}^{m_{1}}}\atop{t\in\mathcal{R}^{m_{2}}}}\Big\{L(x,s,t)-\frac{1}{2\sigma}\|s-u\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2\sigma}\|t-v\|^{2}\Big\}

\displaystyle=-\sigma^{-1}e_{\sigma f^{*}}(u+\sigma(\mathcal{A}x-b))+\langle u,\mathcal{A}x-b\rangle+\frac{\sigma}{2}\|\mathcal{A}x-b\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\sigma^{-1}e_{\sigma h^{*}}(v+\sigma(\mathcal{B}x-c))+\langle v,\mathcal{B}x-c\rangle+\frac{\sigma}{2}\|\mathcal{B}x-c\|^{2}.

In the following, we introduce the ALM in Algorithm
1
.

Algorithm 1

ALM

Given
\sigma_{0}>0
,
\rho\geq 1
and
u^{0}\in\mathcal{R}^{m_{1}}
,
v^{0}\in\mathcal{R}^{m_{2}}
. For
k=0,1,2,\ldots
, iterate the following steps.

1

Find an approximate solution

\displaystyle x^{k+1}\approx\bar{x}^{k+1}=\mathop{\operatorname{argmin}}_{x\in\mathcal{R}^{n}}\Big\{\Phi_{k}(x;u^{k},v^{k}):=\mathcal{L}_{\sigma_{k}}(x;u^{k},v^{k})\Big\}.

(4.1)

2

Compute

\displaystyle u^{k+1}=u^{k}+\sigma_{k}(\mathcal{A}x^{k+1}-b),\quad v^{k+1}=v^{k}+\sigma_{k}(\mathcal{B}x^{k+1}-c),

and update
\sigma_{k+1}=\rho\sigma_{k}
.

For most cases, the inner subproblem (
4.1
) has no closed-form solution and we can only find an approximate one with the following stopping criteria introduced in
Rockafellar 1976a
;
Rockafellar 1976b
.

\displaystyle(A)

\displaystyle\Phi_{k}(x^{k+1};u^{k},v^{k})-\inf_{x\in\mathcal{R}^{n}}\Phi_{k}(x;u^{k},v^{k})\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{k}^{2}}{2\sigma_{k}},\ \varepsilon_{k}\geq 0,\ \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon_{k}<\infty,

\displaystyle(B)

\displaystyle\Phi_{k}(x^{k+1};u^{k},v^{k})-\inf_{x\in\mathcal{R}^{n}}\Phi_{k}(x;u^{k},v^{k})\leq\frac{\delta_{k}^{2}}{2\sigma_{k}}\|(u^{k+1},v^{k+1})-(u^{k},v^{k})\|^{2},

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad 0\leq\delta_{k}<1,\ \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\delta_{k}<\infty,

\displaystyle(B^{\prime})

\displaystyle\quad\|\nabla\Phi_{k}(x^{k+1};u^{k},v^{k})\|\leq\frac{\delta^{\prime}_{k}}{\sigma_{k}}\|(u^{k+1},v^{k+1})-(u^{k},v^{k})\|,\ 0\leq\delta^{\prime}_{k}\rightarrow 0.

The convergence result for the ALM follows easily from Theorems 1 of
Rockafellar 1976a

\displaystyle=\Big\{(d_{1},d_{2})\,\Big|\,f^{*\downarrow}_{-}(\bar{u},d_{1})+\langle b,d_{1}\rangle+h^{*\downarrow}_{-}(\bar{v},d_{2})+\langle c,d_{2}\rangle=0,\mathcal{A}^{*}d_{1}+\mathcal{B}^{*}d_{2}=0\Big\}

\displaystyle=\Big\{(d_{1},d_{2})\,\Big|\,f^{*\downarrow}_{-}(\bar{u},d_{1})=\langle\mathcal{A}\bar{x}-b,d_{1}\rangle,h^{*\downarrow}_{-}(\bar{v},d_{2})=\langle\mathcal{B}\bar{x}-c,d_{2}\rangle,\mathcal{A}^{*}d_{1}+\mathcal{B}^{*}d_{2}=0\Big\}

is the critical cone of the dual problem
(D^{\prime})
at
(\bar{u},\bar{v})
.

Now we are ready to characterize the equivalence of the SRCQ and the SOSC.

Theorem 5
.

For problem
(P^{\prime})
, we have

1.

If
f^{*}
and
h^{*}
are
C^{2}
-cone reducible functions, the SRCQ at
(\bar{u},\bar{v})
for
\bar{x}
of the dual problem
(D^{\prime})
holds if and only if the SOSC at
\bar{x}
for
(\bar{u},\bar{v})
of the primal problem
(P^{\prime})
is valid.

2.

If
f
and
h
are
C^{2}
-cone reducible functions, the SRCQ at
\bar{x}
for
(\bar{u},\bar{v})
of the primal problem
(P^{\prime})
holds if and only if the SOSC at
(\bar{u},\bar{v})
for
\bar{x}
of the dual problem
(D^{\prime})
is valid,

Proof.

We first prove that the SRCQ (
4.8
) is equivalent to the SOSC (
4.9
). Taking the polar operation on both sides of (
4.8
), by Corollary 16.4.2 of
Rockafellar 1970
the SRCQ (
4.8
) can be written equivalently as
that

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}d\in\Big\{d_{1}\,\Big|\,f^{*\downarrow}_{-}(\bar{u},d_{1})=\langle\mathcal{A}\bar{x}-b,d_{1}\rangle\Big\}^{\circ},\ \mathcal{B}d\in\Big\{d_{2}\,\Big|\,h^{*\downarrow}_{-}(\bar{v},d_{2})=\langle\mathcal{B}\bar{x}-c,d_{2}\rangle\Big\}^{\circ}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\Longrightarrow d=0.

(4.11)

Due to Proposition 3.4 of
Tang and Wang 2025
and the fact that
f
and
h
are outer second order regular at
\mathcal{A}\bar{x}-b
and
\mathcal{B}\bar{x}-c
, respectively, we know that the condition (
4.1
) is also equivalent to

\displaystyle\Big\{d\,\Big|\,f^{\downarrow}_{-}(\mathcal{A}\bar{x}-b,\mathcal{A}d)=\langle\bar{u},\mathcal{A}d\rangle,\varphi_{f}(\mathcal{A}\bar{x}-b,\bar{u})(\mathcal{A}d)=0\Big\}

\displaystyle\quad\cap\Big\{d\,\Big|\,h^{\downarrow}_{-}(\mathcal{B}\bar{x}-c,\mathcal{B}d)=\langle\bar{v},\mathcal{B}d\rangle,\varphi_{h}(\mathcal{B}\bar{x}-c,\bar{v})(\mathcal{B}d)=0\Big\}=\{0\}.

(4.12)

Due to the definition of
\mathcal{C}_{1}(\bar{x})
, the condition (
4.12
) is valid if and only if

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{1}(\bar{x})\cap\Big\{d\,\Big|\,\varphi_{f}(\mathcal{A}\bar{x}-b,\bar{u})(\mathcal{A}d)=0,\varphi_{h}(\mathcal{B}\bar{x}-c,\bar{v})(\mathcal{B}d)=0\Big\}=\{0\}

(4.13)

holds. Since
\varphi_{f}(\mathcal{A}\bar{x}-b,\bar{u})(\mathcal{A}d)\geq 0
and
\varphi_{h}(\mathcal{B}\bar{x}-c,\bar{v})(\mathcal{B}d)\geq 0
, the SOSC condition (
4.9
) is also equivalent to (
4.13
). Therefore, the first part of the results can be obtained easily.

Similarly, the SRCQ (
4.1
) holds if and only if

\displaystyle\operatorname{NULL}([\mathcal{A}^{*}\ \mathcal{B}^{*}])\cap\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\Big\{d\,\Big|\,f^{\downarrow}_{-}(\mathcal{A}\bar{x}-b,\mathcal{A}d)=\langle\bar{u},d\rangle\Big\}^{\circ}\\
\Big\{d\,\Big|\,h^{\downarrow}_{-}(\mathcal{B}\bar{x}-c,\mathcal{B}d)=\langle\bar{v},d\rangle\Big\}^{\circ}\end{array}\right)=\{0\}

is valid. Due to Proposition 3.4 of
Tang and Wang 2025
, the condition (
4.1
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\operatorname{NULL}([\mathcal{A}^{*}\ \mathcal{B}^{*}]

\displaystyle\quad\cap\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\Big\{d_{1}\,\Big|\,f^{*\downarrow}_{-}(\bar{u},d_{1})=\langle\mathcal{A}\bar{x}-b,d_{1}\rangle,\varphi_{f^{*}}(\bar{u},\mathcal{A}\bar{x}-b)(d_{1})=0\Big\}\\
\Big\{d_{2}\,\Big|\,h^{*\downarrow}_{-}(\bar{v},d_{2})=\langle\mathcal{B}\bar{x}-c,d_{2}\rangle,\varphi_{h^{*}}(\bar{v},\mathcal{B}\bar{x}-c)(d_{2})=0\Big\}\end{array}\right)=\{0\}.

Based on the definition of
\mathcal{C}_{2}(\bar{u},\bar{v})
, the condition (
4.1
) is valid if and only if

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{2}(\bar{u},\bar{v})\cap\Big\{(d_{1},d_{2})\,\Big|\,\varphi_{f^{*}}(\bar{u},\mathcal{A}\bar{x}-b)(d_{1})=0,\varphi_{h^{*}}(\bar{v},\mathcal{B}\bar{x}-c)(d_{2})=0\Big\}=\{0\}

(4.18)

holds. Since
\varphi_{f^{*}}(\bar{u},\mathcal{A}\bar{x}-b)(d_{1})\geq 0
and
\varphi_{h^{*}}(\bar{v},\mathcal{B}\bar{x}-c)(d_{2})\geq 0
, the SOSC condition (
4.10
) is also equivalent to (
4.18
). Therefore, the second part of the results can also be obtained and this completes the proof.

∎

4.2
The SSN for the inner subproblem (
4.1
)

The main challenge of the ALM is that we need to find an approximate solution with the given accuracy for the inner subproblem efficiently. Due to the continuous differentiability of the Moreau envelope functions for
f
and
g
, the function
\Phi_{k}(\cdot;u^{k},v^{k})
is smooth and solving the subproblem (
4.1
) is equivalent to finding a solution of the following system of equations

\displaystyle\nabla\Phi_{k}(x;u^{k},v^{k})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}^{*}\operatorname{Prox}_{\sigma_{k}f^{*}}(u^{k}+\sigma_{k}(\mathcal{A}x-b))

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathcal{B}^{*}\operatorname{Prox}_{\sigma_{k}h^{*}}(v^{k}+\sigma_{k}(\mathcal{B}x-c))=0.

(4.19)

We apply the SSN method to obtain an approximate solution of problem (
4.2
). Since
\operatorname{Prox}_{\sigma f^{*}}
and
\operatorname{Prox}_{\sigma g^{*}}
are Lipschitz continuous, the following multifunction is well defined.

\displaystyle\hat{\partial}^{2}\Phi_{k}(x;u^{k},v^{k})

\displaystyle:=\sigma_{k}\mathcal{A}^{*}\partial\operatorname{Prox}_{\sigma_{k}f^{*}}(u^{k}+\sigma_{k}(\mathcal{A}x-b))\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sigma_{k}\mathcal{B}^{*}\partial\operatorname{Prox}_{\sigma_{k}h^{*}}(v^{k}+\sigma_{k}(\mathcal{B}x-c))\mathcal{B}.

Let
U^{k}\in\partial\operatorname{Prox}_{\sigma_{k}f^{*}}(u^{k}+\sigma_{k}(\mathcal{A}x-b))
and
V^{k}\in\partial\operatorname{Prox}_{\sigma_{k}h^{*}}(v^{k}+\sigma_{k}(\mathcal{B}x-c))
, we have
\sigma_{k}\mathcal{A}^{*}U^{k}\mathcal{A}+\sigma_{k}\mathcal{B}^{*}V^{k}\mathcal{B}\in\hat{\partial}^{2}\Phi_{k}(x;u^{k},v^{k})
.

For simplicity, we omit the subscripts or superscripts of
\sigma_{k}
,
\Phi_{k}
,
U^{k}
and
V^{k}
. Let
(\bar{x},\bar{u},\bar{v})
be a solution of the KKT system (
1.1
). The following proposition presents an equivalent condition to guarantee that each element of the set
\hat{\partial}^{2}\Phi(\bar{x};\bar{u},\bar{v})
is nonsingular.

Theorem 6
.

Let
(\bar{x},\bar{u},\bar{v})
be a solution of the KKT system (
1.1
).
Suppose that the functions
f^{*}
and
h^{*}
satisfy Assumption
2.1
, Assumption
2.2
at
\bar{u}
for
\mathcal{A}\bar{x}-b
and at
\bar{v}
for
\mathcal{B}\bar{x}-c
, respectively.
Then the nondegeneracy condition (
3.7
) holds if and only if all the elements of
\hat{\partial}^{2}\Phi(\bar{x};\bar{u},\bar{v})
are nonsingular.

Proof.

Let
E:=\sigma\mathcal{A}^{*}U\mathcal{A}+\sigma\mathcal{B}^{*}V\mathcal{B}
be an arbitrary element of
\hat{\partial}^{2}\Phi(\bar{x};\bar{u},\bar{v})
with
U\in\partial\operatorname{Prox}_{\sigma f^{*}}(\bar{u}+\sigma(\mathcal{A}\bar{x}-b))
and
V\in\partial\operatorname{Prox}_{\sigma h^{*}}(\bar{v}+\sigma(\mathcal{B}\bar{x}-c))
.

On one hand, suppose that
d
satisfies
\langle d,Ed\rangle=0
. Since
U
and
V
are positively semidefinite, we have
\langle\mathcal{A}d,U\mathcal{A}d\rangle=0
and
\langle\mathcal{B}d,V\mathcal{B}d\rangle=0
, which is also equivalent to
U(\mathcal{A}d)=0
and
V(\mathcal{B}d)=0
. Given
y
and
z
, let
f_{\sigma}(y):=(\sigma f^{*})^{*}(y)=\sigma f(\sigma^{-1}y)
and
h_{\sigma}(z):=(\sigma h^{*})^{*}(z)=\sigma h(\sigma^{-1}z)
.
By Lemma 4.2 of
Tang and Wang 2025
, it follows that

\displaystyle\Gamma_{f_{\sigma}}(\sigma(\mathcal{A}\bar{x}-b),\bar{u})(\mathcal{A}d)=0,\quad\mathcal{A}d\in\operatorname{aff}\left(\Big\{d\,\Big|\,(f_{\sigma})^{\downarrow}_{-}(\sigma(\mathcal{A}\bar{x}-b),d)=\langle\bar{u},d\rangle\Big\}\right),

\displaystyle\Gamma_{h_{\sigma}}(\sigma(\mathcal{B}\bar{x}-c),\bar{v})(\mathcal{B}d)=0,\quad\mathcal{B}d\in\operatorname{aff}\left(\Big\{d\,\Big|\,(h_{\sigma})^{\downarrow}_{-}(\sigma(\mathcal{B}\bar{x}-b),d)=\langle\bar{v},d\rangle\Big\}\right).

Therefore, due to Proposition 4.6 of
Tang and Wang 2025
, we can obtain

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathcal{A}d\in\Big\{d_{1}\,\Big|\,(\sigma f^{*})^{\downarrow}_{-}(\bar{u},d_{1})=-(\sigma f^{*})^{\downarrow}_{-}(\bar{u},-d_{1})\Big\}^{\circ},\\
\mathcal{B}d\in\Big\{d_{2}\,\Big|\,(\sigma h^{*})^{\downarrow}_{-}(\bar{v},d_{2})=-(\sigma h^{*})^{\downarrow}_{-}(\bar{v},-d_{2})\Big\}^{\circ},\end{array}\right.

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle d\in\Big(\mathcal{A}^{*}\Big\{d_{1}\,\Big|\,f^{*\downarrow}_{-}(\bar{u},d_{1})=-f^{*\downarrow}(\bar{u},-d_{1})\Big\}\Big)^{\circ}\cap\Big(\mathcal{B}^{*}\Big\{d_{2}\,\Big|\,h^{*\downarrow}_{-}(\bar{v},d_{2})=-h^{*\downarrow}(\bar{v},-d_{2})\Big\}\Big)^{\circ}.

By the nondegeneracy condition (
3.7
) we obtain
d=0
and then
E
is nonsingular.

On the other hand, suppose that all the elements of
\hat{\partial}^{2}\Phi(\bar{x};\bar{\mu})
are nonsingular and the nondegeneracy condition (
3.7
) is invalid. Then there exists
d\neq 0
and (
4.2
) holds. By Proposition 4.6 and Lemma 4.2 of
Tang and Wang 2025
, there exist
U\in\partial\operatorname{Prox}_{\sigma f^{*}}(\bar{u}+\sigma(\mathcal{A}\bar{x}-b))
and
V\in\partial\operatorname{Prox}_{\sigma h^{*}}(\bar{v}+\sigma(\mathcal{B}\bar{x}-c))
such that
U(\mathcal{A}d)=0
and
V(\mathcal{B}d)=0
, which is a contradiction with the nonsingularity of all the elements of
\hat{\partial}^{2}\Phi(\bar{x};\bar{u},\bar{v})
. This completes the proof.
∎

2
.

SSN

,
\tilde{u}\in\mathcal{R}^{m_{1}}
,

\nu\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
,

,

\tau\in(0,1]
and
\delta\in(0,1)
. Choose

. Set
j=0
and iterate:

Step 1

Let
U^{j}\in\partial\operatorname{Prox}_{\sigma f^{*}}(\sigma(\mathcal{A}x^{j}-b)+\tilde{u})
,
V^{j}\in\partial\operatorname{Prox}_{\sigma h^{*}}(\sigma(\mathcal{B}x^{j}-c)+\tilde{v})
, and

\displaystyle(H^{j}+\epsilon_{j}I)\Delta x=-\nabla\Phi(x^{j};\tilde{u},\tilde{v})

\displaystyle\|(H^{j}+\epsilon_{j}I)\Delta x^{j}+\nabla\Phi(x^{j};\tilde{u},\tilde{v})\|\leq\eta_{j}:=\min(\bar{\eta},\|\nabla\Phi(x^{j};\tilde{u},\tilde{v})\|^{1+\tau}),

\displaystyle\Phi(x^{j}+\delta^{m}\Delta x^{j};\tilde{u},\tilde{v})\leq\Phi(x^{j};\tilde{u},\tilde{v})+\nu\delta^{m}\langle\nabla\Phi(x^{j};\tilde{u},\tilde{v}),\Delta x^{j}\rangle.

5
The von Neumann entropy optimization problem

In this subsection, we focus on applying the ALM to the following von Neumann entropy optimization problem

\displaystyle\min_{X}\left\{\langle C,\,X\rangle+\mu\mbox{Tr}(X\log X)\,|\,\mathcal{A}(X)=b,\,\mathcal{B}(X)\geq d,\,X-\varepsilon I\succeq 0\,\right\},

(5.1)

where
\mu>0
,
C\in\mathcal{S}^{n}
,
b\in\mathcal{R}^{m_{1}}
,
d\in\mathcal{R}^{m_{2}}
,
\mathcal{A}:\mathcal{S}^{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{R}^{m_{1}}
,
\mathcal{B}:\mathcal{S}^{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{R}^{m_{2}}
.

The Lagrangian function of problem (
5.1
) is

\displaystyle L(X;y,z,S)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle C,\,X\rangle+\mu\mbox{Tr}(X\log X)-\langle y,\,\mathcal{A}(X)-b\rangle-\langle z,\,\mathcal{B}(X)-d\rangle-

\displaystyle\langle S,\,X-\varepsilon I\rangle.

The KKT condition of problem (
5.1
) is

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lll}&C-\mathcal{A}^{*}y-\mathcal{B}^{*}z-S+\mu\log X+\mu I=0,&\\
&\mathcal{A}(X)-b=0,\,\mathcal{B}(X)-d\geq 0,\,X-\varepsilon I\succeq 0,&\\
&z\geq 0,\,S\succeq 0,\,\langle\mathcal{B}(X)-d,\,z\rangle=0,\,\langle X-\varepsilon I,\,S\rangle=0.&\\
\end{array}

The augmented Lagrangian function of problem (
5.1
) is

\displaystyle L_{\sigma}(X;y,z,S)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle C,\,X\rangle+\mu\mbox{Tr}(X\log X)+\frac{\sigma}{2}\|\mathcal{A}(X)-b-\frac{1}{\sigma}y\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2\sigma}\|y\|^{2}+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\sigma}\|\mathrm{\Pi}_{\mathcal{R}^{m_{2}}_{+}}(z-\sigma(\mathcal{B}(X)-d))\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2\sigma}\|z\|^{2}+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\sigma}\|\mathrm{\Pi}_{\mathcal{S}^{n}_{+}}(S-\sigma(X-\varepsilon I))\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2\sigma}\|S\|^{2}.

Then we can calculate

\displaystyle\nabla L_{\sigma}(X;y,z,S)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C+\mu\log X+\mu I+\sigma\mathcal{A}^{*}(\mathcal{A}(X)-b-\frac{1}{\sigma}y)-

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{*}\mathrm{\Pi}_{\mathcal{R}^{m_{2}}_{+}}(z-\sigma(\mathcal{B}(X)-d))-\mathrm{\Pi}_{\mathcal{S}^{n}_{+}}(S-\sigma(X-\varepsilon I)).

Further, we define

\displaystyle\widehat{\partial}^{2}L_{\sigma}(X;y,z,S)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mu\log)^{\prime}(X)+\sigma\mathcal{A}^{*}\mathcal{A}+\sigma\mathcal{B}^{*}\partial\mathrm{\Pi}_{\mathcal{R}^{m_{2}}_{+}}(z-\sigma(\mathcal{B}(X)-d))\mathcal{B}+

\displaystyle\sigma\partial\mathrm{\Pi}_{\mathcal{S}^{n}_{+}}(S-\sigma(X-\varepsilon I)).

Now we present the details of the ALM for problem (
5.1
).

Algorithm 3

ALM for problem (
5.1
)

Given
\sigma_{0}>0
,
\rho\geq 1
and
X^{0},S^{0}\in\mathcal{S}^{n}
,
y^{0}\in\mathcal{R}^{m_{1}}
,
z^{0}\in\mathcal{R}^{m_{2}}
. For
k=0,1,2,\ldots
, iterate the following steps.

1

Find an approximate solution

\displaystyle X^{k+1}\approx\overline{X}^{k+1}=\mathop{\operatorname{argmin}}_{X\in\mathcal{S}^{n}}L_{\sigma_{k}}(X;y^{k},z^{k},S^{k}).

(5.3)

2

Compute

\displaystyle y^{k+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle y^{k}-\sigma_{k}(\mathcal{A}(X^{k+1})-b),

\displaystyle z^{k+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{\Pi}_{\mathcal{R}^{m_{2}}_{+}}(z^{k}-\sigma_{k}(\mathcal{B}(X^{k+1})-d)),

\displaystyle S^{k+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{\Pi}_{\mathcal{S}^{n}_{+}}(S^{k}-\sigma(X^{k+1}-\varepsilon I)).

and update
\sigma_{k+1}=\rho\sigma_{k}
.

Since the subproblem (
5.3
) often has no analytical solution,
the stopping criteria for the subproblem in the algorithm needs to be discussed. For
X_{P}:=X\in\mathcal{S}^{n}
and
X_{D}:=(y,z,S)\in\mathcal{R}^{m_{1}}\times\mathcal{R}^{m_{2}}_{+}\times\mathcal{S}^{n}_{+}
, at the
k
th iteration, we define functions
f_{k}
and
g_{k}
as follows:

\displaystyle f_{k}(X_{D})

\displaystyle:=L_{\sigma_{k}}(X_{P};X_{D}^{k})=\sup_{X_{D}}\Big\{L(X_{P};X_{D})-\frac{1}{2\sigma_{k}}\|X_{D}-X_{D}^{k}\|^{2}\Big\},

\displaystyle g_{k}(X_{P})

\displaystyle:=\inf_{X_{P}}\Big\{L(X_{P};X_{D})-\frac{1}{2\sigma_{k}}\|X_{D}-X_{D}^{k}\|^{2}\Big\}

\displaystyle=\langle b,\,y\rangle+\langle z,\,d\rangle-\mu\langle I,\,\textrm{exp}(-\frac{1}{\mu}(C-\mathcal{A}^{*}y-\mathcal{B}^{*}z-S+\mu I))\rangle+\varepsilon\langle I,\,S\rangle

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\sigma_{k}}(\|y-y^{k}\|^{2}+\|z-z^{k}\|^{2}+\|S-S^{k}\|^{2}).

To obtain
X_{P}^{k+1}
, based on the discussion in Subsection
4.1
, we need the following criteria on the approximate computation of the subproblem (
5.3
):

\displaystyle(A1)

\displaystyle f_{k}(X_{P}^{k+1})-\inf f_{k}(X_{P})\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{k}^{2}}{2\sigma_{k}},\,\varepsilon_{k}\geq 0,\,\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon_{k}<\infty,

\displaystyle(B1)

\displaystyle f_{k}(X_{P}^{k+1})-\inf f_{k}(X_{P})\leq\frac{\delta_{k}^{2}}{2\sigma_{k}}\|X_{D}^{k+1}-X_{D}^{k}\|^{2},0\leq\delta_{k}\leq 1,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\delta_{k}<\infty.

However, the stopping criteria (A1) and (B1) cannot be used directly since
\inf f_{k}(X_{P})
is unknown. Since

\inf f_{k}(X_{P})=\sup g_{k}(X_{D})\geq g_{k}(X_{D}^{k+1}),

we have

f_{k}(X_{P}^{k+1})-\inf f_{k}(X_{P})\leq f_{k}(X_{P}^{k+1})-g_{k}(X_{D}^{k+1}).

Hence, we terminate the subproblem (
5.3
) if
(X_{P}^{k+1},X_{D}^{k+1})
satisfies the following conditions:

\displaystyle(A2)

\displaystyle f_{k}(X_{P}^{k+1})-g_{k}(X_{D}^{k+1})\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{k}^{2}}{2\sigma_{k}},\,\varepsilon_{k}\geq 0,\,\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon_{k}<\infty,

\displaystyle(B2)

\displaystyle f_{k}(X_{P}^{k+1})-g_{k}(X_{D}^{k+1})\leq\frac{\delta_{k}^{2}}{2\sigma_{k}}\|X_{D}^{k+1}-X_{D}^{k}\|^{2},\,0\leq\delta_{k}\leq 1,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\delta_{k}<\infty.

As for solving the inner subproblem (
5.3
), we present the SSN method as below.

Algorithm 4

SSN method for problem (
4.1
)

Input
\sigma>0
,
y\in\mathcal{R}^{m_{1}}
,
z\in\mathcal{R}^{m_{2}}

\nu\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
,
\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\bar{\eta}\in(0,1)
,

\tau\in(0,1]
and
\delta\in(0,1)
. Choose
X^{0},S^{0}\in\mathcal{S}^{n}
. Set
j=0
and iterate:

Step 1

Let
W^{j}\in\partial\mathrm{\Pi}_{\mathcal{R}^{m_{2}}_{+}}(z-\sigma(\mathcal{B}(X^{j})-d))
,
\mathcal{V}^{j}\in\partial\mathrm{\Pi}_{\mathcal{S}^{n}_{+}}(S-\sigma(X^{j}-\varepsilon I))
, and
\mathcal{H}^{j}=(\mu\log)^{\prime}X+\sigma\mathcal{A}^{*}\mathcal{A}+\sigma\mathcal{B}^{*}W^{j}\mathcal{B}+\sigma\mathcal{V}^{j}
. Solve the following linear system

\displaystyle(\mathcal{H}^{j}+\epsilon_{j}\mathcal{I})(\Delta X)=-\nabla L_{\sigma}(X;\tilde{y},\tilde{z},\widetilde{S})

by the preconditioned conjugate gradient (PCG) method to obtain an approximate solution
\Delta X^{j}
satisfying the condition below

\displaystyle\|(\mathcal{H}^{j}+\epsilon_{j}\mathcal{I})(\Delta X^{j})+\nabla L_{\sigma}(X^{j};\tilde{y},\tilde{z},\widetilde{S})\|\leq\eta_{j}:=\min(\bar{\eta},\|\nabla L_{\sigma}(X^{j};\tilde{y},\tilde{z},\widetilde{S})\|^{1+\tau}),

where
\epsilon_{j}=\nu_{1}\min\{\nu_{2},\|\nabla L_{\sigma}(X^{j};\tilde{y},\tilde{z},\widetilde{S})\|\}
.

Step 2

Set
\alpha_{j}=\delta^{m_{j}}
, where
m_{j}
is the first nonnegative integer
m
such that

\displaystyle L_{\sigma}(X^{j}+\delta^{m}\Delta X^{j};\tilde{y},\tilde{z},\widetilde{S})\leq L_{\sigma}(X^{j};\tilde{y},\tilde{z},\widetilde{S})+\nu\delta^{m}\langle\nabla L_{\sigma}(X^{j}+\delta^{m}\Delta X^{j};\tilde{y},\tilde{z},\widetilde{S}),\Delta X^{j}\rangle.

Step 3.

Set
X^{j+1}=X^{j}+\alpha_{j}\Delta X^{j}
. If a desired stopping criterion is satisfied, terminate; otherwise set
j=j+1
and go to Step 1.

For Algorithm
3
, we adopt the following stopping criterion:

\displaystyle R_{\mathrm{KKT}}:=\max\{R_{\mathrm{P}},R_{\mathrm{D}},R_{\mathrm{C}}\}<\mathrm{Tol}=10^{-6},

where

\displaystyle R_{\mathrm{P}}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\max\Big\{\frac{\|\mathcal{A}(X)-b\|}{1+\|b\|},\frac{\|\mathcal{B}(X)-d-\mathrm{\Pi}_{\mathcal{R}^{m_{2}}_{+}}(\mathcal{B}(X)-d)\|}{1+\|\mathcal{B}(X)-d\|},

\displaystyle\frac{\|X-\varepsilon I-\mathrm{\Pi}_{\mathcal{S}^{n}_{+}}(X-\varepsilon I)\|}{1+\|X-\varepsilon I\|}\Big\},

\displaystyle R_{\mathrm{D}}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\frac{\|C-\mathcal{A}^{*}y-\mathcal{B}^{*}z-S+\mu\log X+\mu I\|}{1+\|C\|},

\displaystyle R_{\mathrm{C}}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\max\Big\{\frac{\|\mathcal{B}(X)-d-\mathrm{\Pi}_{\mathcal{R}^{m_{2}}_{+}}(\mathcal{B}(X)-d-z)\|}{1+\|z\|},\frac{\|X-\varepsilon I-\mathrm{\Pi}_{\mathcal{S}^{n}_{+}}(X-\varepsilon I-S)\|}{1+\|S\|}\Big\}.

6.3
Matrix nearness problems with Bregman divergences

In this subsection, we consider the matrix nearness problem

\displaystyle(\textrm{BR})

\displaystyle\min_{X}\Big\{D_{\phi}(X,X_{0})\,\mid\,\mathcal{A}(X)=b,\;\;X\succeq\varepsilon I\Big\},

where

\displaystyle D_{\phi}(X,X_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi(X)-\phi(X_{0})-\langle\nabla\phi(X_{0}),\,X-X_{0}\rangle

is the so-called Bregman divergence. If
\phi
takes different
functions, we can obtain different Bregman divergences. If

\phi(X)=\frac{1}{2}\|X\|^{2}_{F}
, the associated divergence is the
squared Frobenius norm

D_{\phi}(X,X_{0})=\frac{1}{2}\|X-X_{0}\|^{2}_{F}
; if
\phi(X)

equals the von Neumann entropy
\mbox{Tr}(X\log X)
, we obtain the von
Neumann divergence

\displaystyle D_{vN}(X,X_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mbox{Tr}(X\log X-X\log X_{0}-X+X_{0}).

Bregman divergences are well suited for metric nearness problems
because they share many geometric properties with the squared
Frobenius norm. They also exhibit an intimate relationship with
exponential families of probability distributions, which recommends
them for solving problems that arise in the statistical analysis of
data. For more details of (BR), one may refer to
Kulis et al. 2009
.

The nearest correlation matrix problem
Higham 2002
is a typical
case of (BR) that arises in various fields. A correlation matrix is
a positive semidefinite matrix with unit diagonal. However, in

Higham 2002
the Euclidean distance is used which may lead to the
result that the structure of the computational optimal solution is
destroyed. For example, the rank of the optimal solution may be far
from that of
X_{0}
. But if a rank constraint
\mbox{rank}(X)\leq r
, where
r>0
, is added to the problem,
then it becomes a nonconvex one.

The von Neumann divergence owns some nice properties. Among
which the rank-keeping property is a very interesting one, that is,
the rank constraint
\mbox{rank}(X)\leq r
is automatically satisfied when the rank of
X_{0}
does not exceed

r
, then this problem turns out to be convex. This is because this kind of divergences restrict the search for the optimal
X
to the linear subspace of matrices that have the same range space as
X_{0}
.

Now we adopt the von Neumann divergence instead to find the nearest correlation matrices.
Then, the problem (BR) can be regarded as a special case of the problem (
P
), so the proposed algorithm can be applied to it.

Below is the testing example.
The matrix
X_{0}
is an estimated
943\times 943
correlation matrix based on
100,000
ratings
for
1682
movies by
943
users. Due to the missing data, the
generated matrix
X_{0}
is not positive semidefinite

Fushiki 2009
, we have to project it onto the positive semidefinite
cone to adapt to the von Neumann divergence. This rating data set
can be downloaded from

http://www.grouplens.org/node/73
.
Besides the unit diagonal constraints,
we optionally add sparse constraints to the problem.

The performances of the two algorithms on the testing example
are listed in Table
3
. From the computing results we can see that the ALM can compute
the problems in a reasonable amount of time. Moreover, the rank of the computed solution
X
is less than that of the matrix
X_{0}
. In contrast, the PALM fails to solve the problem. Moreover, the solution also fails to satisfy the rank requirement.

6.4
Kernel learning problems

The diffusion kernel is a general method for computing pairwise
distances among all nodes in a graph, based on the sum of weighted
paths between each pair of nodes. This technique has been used
successfully, in conjunction with kernel-based learning methods, to
draw inferences from several types of biological networks. Given
n

nodes in a graph
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}
, the positive semidefinite
kernel matrix
K
can be derived as the optimal solution of the
maximum entropy problem subject to the distance constraints

\displaystyle\|x_{s_{j}}-x_{t_{j}}\|^{2}\leq\gamma,\ j=1,\cdots,m_{2},

(6.1)

where
\{s_{j},t_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m_{2}}
denote the node pairs connected by

m_{2}
edges. Once a kernel matrix is determined, the (squared)
Euclidean distance between two points can also be computed as

\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|^{2}=K_{ii}+K_{jj}-2K_{ij}
. The problem can be
formulated as

\displaystyle(\textrm{KL})

\displaystyle\min_{K}\Big\{\mbox{Tr}(K\log K)\,\mid\,\mbox{Tr}(K)=1,\,\mbox{Tr}(KV_{j})\leq\gamma,j=1,\cdots,m_{2},\;\;K\succeq 0\Big\},

where

\displaystyle[V_{j}]_{st}=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&\mbox{if $(s=s_{j},\,t=s_{j})$ or $(s=t_{j},\,t=t_{j})$}\\[5.0pt]
-1,&\mbox{if $(s=s_{j},\,t=t_{j})$ or $(s=t_{j},\,t=s_{j})$}\\[5.0pt]
0,&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

For the details of the kernel learning problem, one may see

Tsuda and Noble 2004
.

Now we compute kernels from a yeast biological
network. This network was created by von Mering et al.

von Mering et al. 2002
from protein-protein interactions identified
via six different methods: high-throughput yeast two-hybrid,
correlated mRNA expression, genetic interaction (synthetic
lethality), tandem affinity purification, high-throughput
massspectrometric protein complex identification and computational
methods. All interactions were classified into one of three
confidence categories, high-, medium- and lowconfidence, based on
the number of different methods that identify an interaction as well
as the number of times the interaction is observed. In these
experiments, we use a medium confidence network containing
1000

proteins and
1915
edges.

The computing results can be seen in Table
4
. The ALM can successfully solve the problem, however, the PALM cannot supply a satisfying solution within the maximal iterations.

Conclusion

In this paper, we focus on a class of convex composite optimization problems. We prove the equivalence between the primal/dual SSOSC and the dual/primal nondegeneracy condition, and then establish a specific set of equivalent conditions for the perturbation analysis of the problem. Furthermore, we establish the equivalence between the primal/dual SOSC and the dual/primal SRCQ, as well as the equivalence between the dual nondegeneracy condition and the nonsingularity of Clarke’s generalized Jacobian of the subproblem of the ALM. These results provide a solid theoretical foundation for the ALM. Finally, we take the von Neumann entropy optimization problem as an example to demonstrate the effectiveness of the ALM.

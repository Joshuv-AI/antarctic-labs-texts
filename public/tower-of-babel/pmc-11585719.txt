Positive impact of dietary marine sulfated polysaccharides derived from macroalgae during a necrotic enteritis challenge1
Authors: Candice E.C. Blue, María Garcia Suarez, Elise Nacer-Khodja, Maria A. Rodriguez, Rami A. Dalloul
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Caused by the Gram-positive bacteria Clostridium perfringens, necrotic enteritis (NE) is an enteric disease with significant economic implications in broiler production. This study employed an experimental NE model involving co-infection with Eimeria maxima and C. perfringens to assess whether sulfate polysaccharides extracted from marine macroalgae could mitigate the adverse effects of NE in broilers. A total of 600 day (d)-old Ross 708 male broilers were randomly assigned to one of four treatment groups: NC (negative control, fed a corn-soybean meal diet); PC (positive control, NC + 15 ppm Avilamycin and 125 ppm Amprolium); AGS (Algimun® Standard, NC + Algimun added at 0.1 % of the diet); and AGH (Algimun High, NC + Algimun added at 0.2 % of the diet). Average daily feed intake and weight gain were calculated and adjusted for daily mortality on d 14, 21, 28, and 42. On d 21, four birds/pen were examined for intestinal NE lesions. On d 14, 21, and 42, serum and jejunum samples from one bird/pen were collected to measure the concentration of the biomarker calprotectin and mRNA abundance of cytokines and tight junction proteins. Data were analyzed using JMP and significance between treatments identified by LSD (P ≤ 0.05). NE lesion scores on d 21, and mortality and FCR during d 0 to 42 were lower in PC, AGS, and AGH compared to NC birds. AGS birds had the lowest levels for serum calprotectin on d 21 while PC, AGS, and AGH had lower levels than the NC group on d 42. On d 14 and d 42, mRNA abundance of CLDN1, 3, ZO1, IL1β, IFNγ, IL10, and IL12B was greater in AGS and AGH birds compared to NC. Also, AGH had a greater abundance of TNFα on d 14 and d 42 compared to NC. Further, mRNA abundance of CLDN3, ZO1, 2, OCLDN, IL1β, IL10, IL12B, IFNγ, and ANXA1 was greater in PC on d 21 compared to NC and AGS. Based on these findings, dietary supplementation of this macroalgae-derived sulfated polysaccharides yielded a similar response to an antibiotic growth promoter presenting potential as an alternative additive.

Introduction

Necrotic enteritis (NE) is a secondary infection often following coccidiosis and caused by the Gram-positive, spore-forming, opportunistic pathogen Clostridium perfringens (Emami and Dalloul, 2021). Clinical and subclinical NE occurs when C. perfringens colonizes and proliferates in the small intestine producing extracellular toxins, notably toxinotypes A and G playing a pivotal role in its pathogenesis (Rood et al., 2018). The toxins produced damage the intestinal wall by altering the structural proteins at the junction, leading to increased gut permeability, reduced nutrient absorption, and inflammation (Awad et al., 2017; Ducatelle et al., 2018; Emami and Dalloul, 2021). As a result, NE is estimated to inflict a global economic loss of USD 6 billion due to decreased performance, higher mortality, and disease treatment and prevention costs (Wade and Keyburn, 2015). Previously, subtherapeutic doses of antimicrobials were used as antibiotic growth promoters (AGPs) to control NE. However, with legislative restrictions and the demand for antibiotic-free poultry production, NE incidences have been on the rise (Emami and Dalloul, 2021; Blue et al., 2023).

The removal of AGPs has prompted the exploration of nutritional interventions to mitigate the adverse effects of NE on broiler health (Adhikari et al., 2020). In this context, the use of natural phytochemicals, including polysaccharides extracted from algae, has been considered as a potential strategy (Coudert et al., 2020). Various species of brown, green, and red algae are commonly included in animal diets, either individually or in combination. These algae are commercially available and utilized in the U.S. and Canada, either as direct supplements or as components of specialized feed formulations (Kulshreshtha et al., 2020). Algae, encompassing both macro- and micro-algae, are rich in polyphenols, polysaccharides, fatty acids, and amino acids (Madeira et al., 2017). Sulfated polysaccharides from green macroalgae modulate the transcription of immune mediators involved in defense mechanisms within the innate and the adaptive immune responses (Berri et al., 2016). In vivo, recent studies further confirmed the immunomodulating properties of green macroalgal sulfated polysaccharides, namely by improving the defense activities of monocytes and heterophils (Guriec et al., 2018), and by favoring the transfer of maternal immunity (Bussy et al., 2019).

The complexity and reactivity of seaweed polysaccharides arise from their diverse and sometimes rare sugar units, such as uronic acids, xylose, and rhamnose. The variety of glycosidic bonds leads to their branched structures, and the presence of sulfate groups is one of the most important traits determining the bioactivities of these molecules. Green, red and brown seaweeds are rich in sulfated polysaccharides, and the main types of these are agar and carrageenans (red seaweeds), ulvans (green seaweeds) and fucoidans (brown seaweeds), each of them presenting specific molecular traits which determine their biological properties and reactivity. Several studies reported the positive effects of using algal polysaccharides on broilers’ physiology under various stressors (Wu, 2018; Li et al., 2019; Liu et al., 2021). However, limited data are available on whether algae-derived polysaccharide supplementation can ameliorate the adverse effects of NE in broiler chickens. Algimun® (Algoguard™) is a commercial product based on the combination of two bioactive macroalgal extracts: MSP®BARRIER, a red algal extract, which enhances the gut barrier function, and MSP®IMMUNITY, a green algal extract, that modulates innate and adaptive immune responses. Therefore, this study evaluated the effects of this dietary sulfated polysaccharide on performance and responses of commercial broilers during NE.

Materials and methods

Experimental diets, birds, and management

This study was approved and conducted under the University of Georgia Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee guidelines. During this 42-d study, 600 d-old Ross 708 male broilers were allocated to one of four treatment groups with six replicate floor pens and 25 birds per pen. The groups included the negative control (NC) group fed a corn-soybean meal basal diet (Table 1); the positive control (PC) fed NC + 15 ppm Avilamycin and 125 ppm Amprolium, an additive group (AGS) fed NC + Algimun added at 0.1 % of the diet, and (AGH) fed NC + Algimun added at 0.2 % of the diet. Chicks had access to feed and water ad libitum using a bucket-type feeder and a nipple drinker waterline. The light cycle was 24 h of light for the first three days, reduced to 23 h light and 1 h dark for d 4–7, and then reduced to 20 h of light and 4 h of dark for the remaining trial duration. Collection and extraction of sulphated polysaccharides

Algae used to produce the dietary mix of marine sulfated polysaccharides were harvested from natural coastal landings of green and red tides on several locations in Brittany, France. Green algae were collected in Plestin les Grèves (48° 39′ 28′′ N, 3° 37′ 47′′ W), Guissény (48° 38′ 2.48″ N, 4° 24′ 34.35″ W), and Saint-Brieuc (48° 30′ 51.048′' N, 2° 45′ 55.08′' W); and red algae in Saint-Hilaire-de-Riez (46° 42′ 47.09″ N, 1° 55′ 32.99″ W) and Presqu'île de Rhuys (47° 39′ 32″ N, 2° 45′ 34″ W). The algae were washed in fresh water, drained, and deep-frozen. For extraction, the algae were thawed; wet-ground, and liquid and solid phases were separated as part of an industrial process (Patent No. FR 61909). The liquid phase was then subjected to a process resulting in a concentrated algal extract containing sulphated polysaccharides. As an additive, the algae provides no additional nutritional value to the overall diet. The overall production process is the property of the Olmix Group (Z.A. du Haut Du Bois, 56580 Bréhan, France).

Analytical methods of sulfated polysaccharides

Linked-SO4 are directly proportional to sulfated polysaccharides, according to the biochemistry of ulvans and iota-carrageenans, which are the two families of sulfated polysaccharides. The procedure used at Olmix Group for the dosage of linked-SO4 consisted of 10 g of dry algal juice solubilized in 85 mL of demineralized water. Conductivity of the solution was measured, then successive steps of ultra-filtration on 1 kDa membrane were performed until the conductivity of 0.2 mS/cm was obtained for a final volume of 40 mL. The retentate was lyophilized, mass yield performed, then total sulfur measured with a CNS elemental analyzer. Finally, the amount of linked-SO4 was obtained by calculation, considering that all the residual sulfur present in the extract after ultrafiltration was in SO4 form, linked to sulphated polysaccharide.

Performance

Performance parameters including average daily gain (ADG) and feed intake (ADFI), and feed conversion ratio (FCR) were calculated on days 14, 21, 28, and 42 on a per-pen basis. Mortality data were also recorded. Adjustments to these performance parameters were made by accounting for mortality/treatment during each phase of the study.

Necrotic enteritis challenge and lesion scoring

On d 14, all birds received 1 mL of water containing 2000 sporulated oocysts of Eimeria maxima via oral gavage. C. perfringens was cultured anaerobically at 37 °C in fluid thioglycolate medium (FTM), and on d 19, birds were orally gavaged with 1 mL of fresh media containing approximately 1.0×108 CFUs/bird. On d 21, based on average body weights, four birds were chosen from each pen and euthanized via cervical dislocation. The jejunum and ileum were then removed for examination of NE lesions. NE lesion scoring ranged from 0 to 3, with 0 indicating normal and 3 representing the most severe as previously described (Collier et al., 2008).

Assessment of calprotectin by ELISA

On days 14, 21, and 42, one bird was chosen from each pen based on its average body weight. Blood samples were collected from the brachial vein using a sterile 23-gauge 2.54 cm needle. The blood was then transferred to pre-labeled sterile vacutainers, centrifuged at 1000× g for 10 min, and the serum was separated and stored at −20 °C. The serum concentration of the biomarker calprotectin (avian MRP-126) was measured using an ELISA kit (MyBiosource Inc., San Diego, CA, USA) following the manufacturer's recommendations. Briefly, 40 μL of serum were incubated with anti-calprotectin antibody and streptavidin-HRP for 60 min at 37 °C. The samples (n = 6 per group per day) were then washed five times with PBS solution and incubated in the dark with 50 μL of TMB Substrate for 10 min at 37 °C. Lastly, 50 μL of stop solution consisting of 2 M sulfuric acid were added, and the optical density was measured using a microplate reader set to 450 nm. The concentration of each sample was determined based on the kit-supplied standards generating a standard curve.

Total RNA extraction, reverse transcription, and qRT-PCR

On days 14, 21, and 42, one bird per pen, selected based on the average pen weight, was humanely euthanized. Approximately 1-cm samples were aseptically taken from the middle section of the jejunum, rinsed in cold PBS, snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at −80 °C. The samples were homogenized using a tissue lyser (Bead mill, Homogenizer, 120V, VWR) and then extracted with Trizol reagent following the manufacturer's instructions (ZYMO Research). The total RNA concentration was determined at optical density (OD) 260 (Nanodrop OneC, Thermo Fisher), and RNA purity was assessed by evaluating the ratio of OD 260 to OD 280. After extraction, 4 μg of total RNA were reverse-transcribed into cDNA using the qScript cDNA SuperMix (Quantabio) following the manufacturer's protocol, and the cDNA was stored at −20 °C.

Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) was conducted using QuantStudio 3 (Applied Biosystems) using PowerTrack Fast SYBRTM Green Master Mix. The cDNA was diluted 1:20 in nuclease-free water, and 1.5 μL of the diluted cDNA was added to each well of a 96-well plate. Next, 8.5 μL of real-time PCR master mix were added to each well, containing 5 μL of Fast SYBR Green Master Mix (Applied Biosystems), 0.5 μL each of forward and reverse primers (Table 2), and 2.5 μL of sterile nuclease-free water resulting in a final reaction volume of 10 μL. The PCR reaction involved an initial denaturation phase at 95 °C for 120 s followed by 40 cycles of denaturation at 95 °C for 5 s and annealing/extension at 60 °C for 30 s. Each target reaction was performed in duplicate wells. Product specificity was confirmed by analysis of the melting curves produced by QuantStudio 3 system. mRNA abundance for genes of tight junction proteins (occludin [OCLDN], claudins [CLDN] 1 and 3, and zonula occludens [ZO] 1 and 2), and cytokines (tumor necrosis factor alpha [TNFα], interferon gamma [IFNγ], interleukins [IL]-1β, IL10, IL12b, and annexin A1 [ANXA1]) was determined using 18S as an endogenous control. Statistical analysis

All data were subjected to a one-way ANOVA using the JMP Pro 16 program, a Chi-squared test was used for lesion scores. Experimental units for mortality, performance, lesion scores, calprotectin ELISA, and mRNA abundance were on a per pen basis. For all parameters, except lesion scores, Fisher's least significant difference (LSD) test compared separated means when significant differences were noted at P ≤ 0.05.

Discussion

Necrotic enteritis (NE) is caused by C. perfringens and is an important enteric disease in poultry production that occurs in both clinical and subclinical forms and is a threat to the global broiler industry (Mwangi et al., 2019). An NE infection leads to reduction in performance, increased mortality, and damage to the epithelial lining (Fathima et al., 2022). As the industry moves to antibiotic-free and no antibiotics ever, it is necessary to develop early management strategies to mitigate NE (Bueno et al., 2023). One avenue has been an increased interest in algal-based feed supplementation in several regions of the world, with algae being included in animal feeds at levels up to 10 % (Saadaoui et al., 2021). In the early 1980s and 1990s, microalgae and macroalgae were developed as a protein source in poultry feed (Lipstein et al., 1980; Ventura et al., 1994). Algae contain active molecules such as polyphenols, polysaccharides, lipids, peptides, and proteins, which are well known for their antioxidant, antibacterial, and anti-inflammatory properties (Garcia-Vaquero and Hayes, 2016). Even though there is evidence of algae's promising effects for control of Salmonella infection (Koh et al., 2005) and improvement in quality of animal products such as eggs and meat (Haberecht et al., 2018), there is limited research in regard to their influence on broilers during NE. Therefore, this study investigated the effects of macroalgae-derived polysaccharides on performance, pathology, and mRNA abundance of tight junction proteins and cytokines. It also examined serum concentration of the biomarker calprotectin to identify a possible mechanism of a sulfated polysaccharide derived from macroalgae.

The current findings revealed improvements in mortality and FCR in the algae-supplemented groups during the overall experimental period. In the PC group, the improved FCR could be attributed to its higher ADG, whereas the better FCR in the AGS group might be attributed to their lower ADFI. Although our data align with previous studies suggesting that dietary supplementation of algal polysaccharides can enhance feed conversion ratios through reduced feed consumption for equivalent growth rates (Yan et al., 2011; Kang et al., 2013; Choi et al., 2014), other studies reported no significant differences in growth performance when polysaccharides are included in the diet (Biggs et al., 2007; Jeon et al., 2022). A reduction in lesion severity observed in the supplemented groups following a disease challenge may indicate recovery and restored tissue function, allowing the bird to reallocate resources from healing to growth (Emami et al., 2019). This reallocation suggests that an algal derivative could enhance compensatory growth via improving digestion and nutrient absorption upon recovery, and help the bird return to its expected growth trajectory resulting in a better FCR. The variation in outcomes across studies could result from differences in polymerization levels, purity, supplemental dosage, and the source of polysaccharides, as well as the experimental growth periods and conditions (Liu et al., 2018).

In the industry, there is a need for a minimally invasive tools to identify gut inflammation and health, and serum calprotectin presented a promising biomarker within the context of this NE challenge model (Dal Pont et al., 2021). Calprotectin, a calcium and zinc binding protein involved in the innate immune response, is produced by heterophils and macrophages in chickens. During inflammation, such as after intravenous infection with Salmonella Enteritidis, calprotectin is quickly released making it a promising biomarker for gastrointestinal conditions (Sekelova et al., 2017). During inflammation, heterophils accumulate at the site of infection and release an increased amount of calprotectin (Dal Pont et al., 2021). On d 21, the supplementation of AGS reduced the serum concentration of calprotectin which can be an indication of attenuated intestinal inflammation. There is also a correlation between higher levels of calprotectin concentration in the blood and higher lesion scores in the jejunum (Bortoluzzi et al., 2021; Dal Pont et al., 2021). Bortoluzzi et al. (2021) demonstrated that supplementing broiler diets with organic acids and essential oils reduces calprotectin levels, suggesting that this approach could effectively evaluate the degree of intestinal inflammation. During infection, inflammation plays a role in tissue repair by initiating immune response and recruiting immune cells that aid in clearing damaged tissue and promoting new tissue formation (Broom and Kogut, 2018). Immune cells migrate to the site of inflammation and release cytokines that play an important role in activating the innate host defense and regulating the adaptive immune response (Dal Pont et al., 2023). The present study measured the abundance of cytokines that belong to proinflammatory (TNFα, IFNγ, IL1β) and anti-inflammatory or immunomodulatory mediators (ANXA1, IL12B, and IL10). During the pre-challenge (d 14) period, there was a greater abundance was observed in IL1β, IL10, and IL12B in AGS and AGH groups. The increased mRNA abundance of these cytokines could result from an improved cell-mediated immune response (Zhang et al., 2017). On d 21 (peak infection), there was a lower mRNA abundance of IL1β, IFNγ, IL10, IL12B, and ANXA1 in both AGS and AGH. This reduced mRNA abundance of IL1β and IFNγ could correlate with the lower calprotectin levels and lesion scores observed in AGS and AGH birds, as calprotectin plays an important role in activating proinflammatory signaling pathways and contributing to the production of various cytokines (Pietro et al., 2019). The decrease in inflammatory cytokines reduces inflammation which could induce a higher tolerance against infection (Kogut et al., 2018). Also, to prevent some of these adverse effects of extra pro-inflammatory cells, immunomodulatory mediators such as ANXA1 and IL10 are released into the epithelium to resolve inflammation and restore mucosal homeostasis (Perretti and D'acquisto, 2009). Sulfated polysaccharides may modulate the inflammatory response, reducing the need for a robust immune reaction against NE, indicating their potential antimicrobial activity.

Intestinal barrier function plays a role in preventing harmful bacteria and endotoxins from entering the blood circulation (Emami and Dalloul, 2021). The junction between cells includes tight junctions and adhesion junctions and its permeability is regulated by intracellular and extracellular signals, and is susceptible to diet, diseases, and stress (Krug and Fromm, 2020).

Claudins and occludins are the skeleton of the tight junction structure, and intracellular proteins such as zonula occludens are the supporting structure to bind transmembrane proteins (Paradis et al., 2021). It is widely noted that during NE infection, C. perfringens enterotoxins (CPE) will bind to tight junction proteins eventually leading to an increase in paracellular permeability (Saitoh et al., 2015). On d 14 (pre-challenge) and d 42 (recovery phase), AGS exhibited significantly greater mRNA abundance of multiple tight junction proteins. Comparable results were observed with dietary supplementation of Astragalus polysaccharides, which increased the mRNA abundance of claudin 1, occludin, and zonula occluden (Wang et al., 2022). Similarly, dietary supplementation of alfalfa polysaccharides improved the mRNA abundance of claudin 1 and occludin in the jejunum of broilers (Li et al., 2021). Although CPE use claudin family proteins as binding sites, and greater abundance of claudin 3 could translate into more available binding sites for CPE (Saitoh et al., 2015; Emami and Dalloul, 2021), lower lesion scores and better FCR displayed in PC, AGS, and AGH lead us to conclude that the modification led to the promotion of gut integrity and health compared to NC.

Based on this coinfection model of NE utilizing E. maxima and C. perfringens, the presented findings showed that dietary supplementation with sulfate polysaccharides extracted from marine macroalgae improved performance and reduced NE lesion scores. Lower serum calprotectin concentration and the modulation of immune response genes in the additive groups suggest a reduction in inflammatory responses. Additionally, enhanced mRNA abundance of tight junction proteins in AGS and AGH may contribute to improved gut integrity. In conclusion, sulfated polysaccharides extracted from marine macroalgae supplementation has the potential to enhance broiler performance and mitigate the negative effects of NE.

Comment on “a novel parameter for risk stratification in patients with Takotsubo syndrome”
Authors: Güney Sarıoğlu, Yakup Yiğit
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

Moady et al., who reported that a higher fibrinogen-to-albumin ratio (FAR) was associated with lower left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), in-hospital complications, and subsequent readmission in patients with Takotsubo syndrome (TTS) [1]. The study highlights the potential value of inflammation-related biomarkers in a condition increasingly recognized as involving systemic inflammatory responses.

An important interpretative issue, however, is whether FAR should be considered a distinct prognostic biomarker or primarily a composite expression of its two components. Fibrinogen and albumin respond in opposite directions during systemic inflammation, and albumin may also reflect nutritional status, vascular permeability, hepatic synthesis, and overall illness burden. Accordingly, a ratio can amplify separation between clinical groups without necessarily providing information beyond fibrinogen and albumin considered individually. The authors showed relationships between these components and LVEF graphically, but a direct comparison of their discrimination with FAR would clarify whether the ratio itself adds clinically meaningful information.

A second consideration concerns the distinction between a risk marker and a disease-specific marker. FAR was associated with complications even after adjustment for CRP and reduced LVEF, which is encouraging; nevertheless, this does not establish that FAR captures a TTS-specific biological pathway. Contemporary studies of inflammatory biomarkers similarly show that systemic inflammatory measures can identify cardiovascular vulnerability across diverse conditions [2]. Thus, FAR may reflect the intensity of the acute systemic response rather than a mechanism unique to TTS.

Finally, the composite in-hospital endpoint included heart failure, arrhythmia, left ventricular outflow tract obstruction, and thromboembolism—events with markedly different pathophysiology. Future studies could therefore evaluate whether FAR relates preferentially to hemodynamic decompensation or broadly to all complication types. Such phenotype-specific analyses may be more informative than a single composite association.

We believe FAR is a promising and readily available marker. The next step should be not only external validation, as the authors appropriately acknowledge, but also demonstration of incremental value over its individual components and established clinical variables, ideally using discrimination, calibration, and reclassification metrics [3], [4].

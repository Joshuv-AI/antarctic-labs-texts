Abstract
A topological space is said to be cardinality homogeneous if every nonempty open subset has the same cardinality as the space itself. Let X X and Y Y be cardinality homogeneous metric spaces of the same cardinality. If there exists a δ \delta -surjective d d -isometry between such equicardinal cardinality homogeneous metric spaces X X and Y Y , then there exists a bijective ( d + 2 ​ δ ) (d+2\delta) -isometry between X X and Y Y . This result allows us to reduce the Dilworth–Tabor theorem to the Gevirtz–Omladič–Šemrl theorem on approximation by isometries and, in particular, to questions concerning the isometry of Banach spaces. Keywords : metric space, Gromov–Hausdorff distance, δ \delta -surjective mapping, d d -isometry, isometry.

Introduction

The celebrated Mazur–Ulam theorem of 1932 states that
every surjective isometry

between real normed spaces is affine

[
1

. One direction for strengthening this theorem arose from the 1945 work of Hyers and Ulam

V

satisfying

\|f-U\|=\sup\bigl\{\|f(x)-U(x)\|:x\in V\bigr\}\leq K\,d?

They noted that
the surjectivity assumption for

is essential
and provided an affirmative answer in the case
V=W=\ell_{2}
, the Hilbert space (with
K=10
).

The efforts of numerous mathematicians over many years culminated in a complete affirmative resolution by J. Gevirtz in 1983
[
3
]
. In 1995, M. Omladič and P. Šemrl
[
4
]
established the sharp constant
K=2
.

S. J. Dilworth
[
5
]
and J. Tabor
[
6
]
relaxed the surjectivity condition:

If
d,\delta\geq 0
and
f\colon V\to W
is a

-isometry between Banach spaces with
f(0)=0
that maps
V

\delta
-surjectively onto a closed subspace

(

, then there exists a bijective linear isometry

such that

(1)

\|f-U\|\leq M=12d+5\delta\quad\text{(Dilworth)};\qquad M=2d+35\delta\quad\text{($L=W$, Tabor)}.

In 2003, P. Šemrl and J. Väisälä
[
7
]
showed that
the right-hand side of

can be replaced by
M=2d+2\delta
. They also proved that
when

, the universal bound

holds

Inspired by the proof technique of the Cantor–Bernstein theorem, the present paper approximates two mappings

and

Two applications of this approximation are presented. First, we show that for cardinality homogeneous metric spaces of the same cardinality, the Gromov–Hausdorff distance can be characterised using bijective mappings (Theorem

Theorem

and its corollaries also permit a reduction of the approximation problem for

-isometric
\delta
-surjective mappings of Banach spaces (onto a closed linear subspace) to the approximation of

-isometric (or
(d+6\delta)

Let

\displaystyle=d(x,y)

\displaystyle U_{\varepsilon}(x)

\displaystyle U_{\varepsilon}(M)

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{x\in M}U_{\varepsilon}(x)

\displaystyle\operatorname{diam}M

\displaystyle=\sup\{|xy|:x,y\in M\}

\displaystyle\textit{the diameter of $M$}.

For subsets

, the

is

d_{H}(A,B)=\inf\{\varepsilon>0:A\subset U_{\varepsilon}(B)\ \text{and}\ B\subset U_{\varepsilon}(A)\}.

The

between metric spaces

and

is the infimum of
d_{H}(X,Y)
over all isometric embeddings of

and

into a common metric space

[
8
,

A

between sets

and
Y

R\subset X\times Y
such that every

is related to at least one
y\in Y
, and vice versa.

For nonempty

\operatorname{dis}R=\sup\bigl\{\bigl||xx^{\prime}|-|yy^{\prime}|\bigr|:(x,y),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in R\bigr\}.

An equivalent expression for the Gromov–Hausdorff distance is
[

, Theorem 7.3.25]

d_{GH}(X,Y)=\frac{1}{2}\inf\{\operatorname{dis}R:R\subset X\times Y\ \text{is a correspondence}\}.

The

of nonempty

is

\operatorname{codis}(R,R^{\prime})=\sup\bigl\{\bigl||xx^{\prime}|-|yy^{\prime}|\bigr|:(x,y)\in R,\ (x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in R^{\prime}\bigr\}.

For mappings

and

\operatorname{dis}f=\sup\bigl\{\bigl||f(x)f(x^{\prime})|-|xx^{\prime}|\bigr|:x,x^{\prime}\in X\bigr\},

and the

\operatorname{codis}(f,g)=\sup\bigl\{\bigl||xg(y)|-|f(x)y|\bigr|:x\in X,\ y\in Y\bigr\}.

graph

3
Approximation of Mappings by a Bijection

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
X
and
Y
be sets equipped with covers
\mathcal{L}_{X}
and
\mathcal{L}_{Y}
such that
|X|=|Y|=|V|
for every
V\in\mathcal{L}_{X}
and
|U|
for every
U\in\mathcal{L}_{Y}
. Then for any mappings
f\colon X\to Y
and
g\colon Y\to X
, there exists a bijection
\tilde{f}\colon X\to Y
such that for every
x\in X
at least one of the following holds
:

(1)

\tilde{f}(x)
and
f(x)
lie in the same member of
\mathcal{L}_{Y}
;

(2)

g(\tilde{f}(x))
and
x
lie in the same member of
\mathcal{L}_{X}
.

Proof.

Choose functions
U\colon X\to\mathcal{L}_{X}
and
V\colon Y\to\mathcal{L}_{Y}
with
x\in U(x)
and
y\in V(y)
. Enumerate the points using the cardinal
\tau=|X|=|Y|
:
X=\{x_{\alpha}:\alpha<\tau\}
and
Y=\{y_{\alpha}:\alpha<\tau\}
. For
\alpha\leq\tau
, set
X_{\alpha}=\{x_{\beta}:\beta<\alpha\}
and
Y_{\alpha}=\{y_{\beta}:\beta<\alpha\}
.

By transfinite induction on
\alpha\leq\tau
, construct injective mappings
f_{\alpha}\colon X_{\alpha}\to Y
and
g_{\alpha}\colon Y_{\alpha}\to X
satisfying, for every
\beta<\alpha
:

•

f_{\alpha}|_{X_{\beta}}=f_{\beta}
and
g_{\alpha}|_{Y_{\beta}}=g_{\beta}
;

•

if
f_{\alpha}(x_{\beta})\in Y_{\alpha}
, then
x_{\beta}=g_{\alpha}(f_{\alpha}(x_{\beta}))
;

•

if
g_{\alpha}(y_{\beta})\in X_{\alpha}
, then
y_{\beta}=f_{\alpha}(g_{\alpha}(y_{\beta}))
;

•

either
f_{\alpha}(x_{\beta})\in V(f(x_{\beta}))
or
g_{\alpha}(y_{\beta})\in U(g(y_{\beta}))
.

If
\alpha
is limit, take unions of the previous mappings. For successor
\alpha=\beta+1
, extend
f_{\alpha}
and
g_{\alpha}
as follows.

To define
f_{\alpha}(x_{\beta})
:

Case 1:

If
x_{\beta}\notin g_{\beta}(Y_{\beta})
, choose
y\in V(f(x_{\beta}))\setminus(Y_{\beta}\cup f_{\beta}(X_{\beta}))
and set
f_{\alpha}(x_{\beta})=y
.

Case 2:

If
x_{\beta}\in g_{\beta}(Y_{\beta})
, set
f_{\alpha}(x_{\beta})=y
where
y\in Y_{\beta}
and
x_{\beta}=g_{\beta}(y)
.

To define
g_{\alpha}(y_{\beta})
:

Case 1:

If
y_{\beta}\notin f_{\alpha}(X_{\alpha})
, choose
x\in U(g(y_{\beta}))\setminus(X_{\alpha}\cup g_{\beta}(Y_{\beta}))
and set
g_{\alpha}(y_{\beta})=x
.

Case 2:

If
y_{\beta}\in f_{\alpha}(X_{\alpha})
, set
g_{\alpha}(y_{\beta})=x
where
x\in X_{\alpha}
and
y_{\beta}=f_{\alpha}(x)
.

The construction yields mutually inverse bijections
\tilde{f}=f_{\tau}
and
g_{\tau}
satisfying the required conditions for all
x\in X
.
∎

We write
x\in X_{I}
if condition (1) holds for
x
, and
x\in X_{II}
if (2) holds.

4
Pseudometrics on Classes of Metric Spaces

Let metrics be defined on
X
and
Y
, with
f\colon X\to Y
and
g\colon Y\to X
. Define

\displaystyle d_{f}^{-}

\displaystyle=\sup\{|xx^{\prime}|-|f(x)f(x^{\prime})|:x,x^{\prime}\in X\},

\displaystyle d_{f}^{+}

\displaystyle=\sup\{|f(x)f(x^{\prime})|-|xx^{\prime}|:x,x^{\prime}\in X\},

\displaystyle d_{(f,g)}^{-}

\displaystyle=\sup\{|xg(y)|-|f(x)y|:x\in X,y\in Y\},

\displaystyle d_{(f,g)}^{+}

\displaystyle=\sup\{|f(x)y|-|xg(y)|:x\in X,y\in Y\}.

Then

(2)

\displaystyle-d_{f}^{-}\leq|f(x)f(x^{\prime})|-|xx^{\prime}|\leq d_{f}^{+}\quad\text{for all $x,x^{\prime}\in X$},

(3)

\displaystyle-d_{g}^{-}\leq|g(y)g(y^{\prime})|-|yy^{\prime}|\leq d_{g}^{+}\quad\text{for all $y,y^{\prime}\in Y$},

(4)

\displaystyle-d_{(f,g)}^{-}\leq|f(x)y|-|xg(y)|\leq d_{(f,g)}^{+}\quad\text{for all $x\in X$, $y\in Y$}.

Clearly,

d_{(g,f)}^{+}=d_{(f,g)}^{-}\quad\text{and}\quad d_{(g,f)}^{-}=d_{(f,g)}^{+}.

We have

\displaystyle\operatorname{dis}f=\max\{d_{f}^{+},d_{f}^{-}\},\quad\operatorname{codis}(f,g)=\max\{d_{(f,g)}^{+},d_{(f,g)}^{-}\},

\displaystyle d_{GH}(f,g)=\frac{1}{2}\max\{d_{f}^{-},d_{f}^{+},d_{g}^{-},d_{g}^{+},d_{(f,g)}^{+},d_{(f,g)}^{-}\}.

A mapping
f
is a
d
-isometry if
\operatorname{dis}f\leq d
. One-sided bounds are also useful, e.g.,
d_{f}^{+}
-non-expansive mappings
[
13
]
.

Define

md_{GH}(f,g)=\frac{1}{2}\max\{\operatorname{dis}f,\operatorname{dis}g\}=\frac{1}{2}\max\{d_{f}^{-},d_{f}^{+},d_{g}^{-},d_{g}^{+}\}.

Evidently,
md_{GH}(f,g)\leq d_{GH}(f,g)
.

For a class
\mathcal{F}\subset Y^{X}\times X^{Y}
, set

\displaystyle d_{GH}(X,Y;\mathcal{F})

\displaystyle=\inf\{d_{GH}(f,g):(f,g)\in\mathcal{F}\},

\displaystyle md_{GH}(X,Y;\mathcal{F})

\displaystyle=\inf\{md_{GH}(f,g):(f,g)\in\mathcal{F}\}.

Proposition 4.1
.

If
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{F}
, then

\displaystyle md_{GH}(X,Y;\mathcal{F})

\displaystyle\leq d_{GH}(X,Y;\mathcal{F})\leq d_{GH}(X,Y;\mathcal{F}^{\prime}),

\displaystyle md_{GH}(X,Y;\mathcal{F})

\displaystyle\leq md_{GH}(X,Y;\mathcal{F}^{\prime}).

The standard Gromov–Hausdorff distance is

d_{GH}(X,Y)=d_{GH}(X,Y;Y^{X}\times X^{Y}).

The modified Gromov–Hausdorff distance
[
14
]
, which is algorithmically simpler, is

(5)

md_{GH}(X,Y)=md_{GH}(X,Y;Y^{X}\times X^{Y}).

Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{c}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=\{f\in Y^{X}:f\ \text{ is continuous}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{c}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{M}_{c}(X,Y)\times\mathcal{M}_{c}(Y,X).

The
continuous Gromov–Hausdorff distance

[
15
,
16
]
is

(6)

d_{GH}^{c}(X,Y)=d_{GH}(X,Y;\mathcal{F}_{c}(X,Y)),

and its
modified version
is

md_{GH}^{c}(X,Y)=md_{GH}(X,Y;\mathcal{F}_{c}(X,Y)).

From Proposition
4.1
we obtain

\displaystyle md_{GH}\leq d_{GH}\leq d_{GH}^{c},\qquad md_{GH}\leq md_{GH}^{c}.

4.1
Distance Between Spaces of the Same Cardinality

Let
X
and
Y
be metric spaces of the same cardinality. Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{in}}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=\{f\colon X\to Y:f\ \text{is injective}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{sur}}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=\{f\colon X\to Y:f\ \text{is surjective}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{bi}}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=\{f\colon X\to Y:f\ \text{is bijective}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{in}}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{in}}(X,Y)\times\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{in}}(Y,X),

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{sur}}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{sur}}(X,Y)\times\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{sur}}(Y,X),

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{bi}}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{bi}}(X,Y)\times\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{bi}}(Y,X),

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{i}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=\{(f,f^{-1}):f\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{bi}}(X,Y)\}.

The corresponding (modified) distances are

\displaystyle(m)d_{GH}^{\mathrm{sur}}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=(m)d_{GH}(X,Y;\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{sur}}(X,Y)),

\displaystyle(m)d_{GH}^{\mathrm{in}}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=(m)d_{GH}(X,Y;\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{in}}(X,Y)),

(7)

\displaystyle(m)d_{GH}^{\mathrm{bi}}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=(m)d_{GH}(X,Y;\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{bi}}(X,Y)).

Also set

(m)d_{GH}^{i}(X,Y)=(m)d_{GH}(X,Y;\mathcal{F}_{i}(X,Y)).

From Proposition
4.1
,

md_{GH}^{\mathbf{xx}}\leq d_{GH}^{\mathbf{xx}}\quad\text{for $\mathbf{xx}\in\{i,\mathrm{in},\mathrm{sur},\mathrm{bi}\}$}

and

\displaystyle(m)d_{GH}

\displaystyle\leq(m)d_{GH}^{\mathrm{in}}\leq(m)d_{GH}^{\mathrm{bi}}\leq(m)d_{GH}^{i},

\displaystyle(m)d_{GH}

\displaystyle\leq(m)d_{GH}^{\mathrm{sur}}\leq(m)d_{GH}^{\mathrm{bi}}.

Proposition 4.2
.

For a bijection

between metric spaces,

d_{f^{-1}}^{+}=d_{f}^{-},\quad d_{f^{-1}}^{-}=d_{f}^{+};\quad d_{(f,f^{-1})}^{+}=d_{f}^{+},\quad d_{(f,f^{-1})}^{-}=d_{f}^{-}.

Consequently,

md_{GH}(f,f^{-1})=d_{GH}(f,f^{-1})=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{dis}f=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{dis}f^{-1}.

Proof.

Substitute
x=f^{-1}(y)
and
x^{\prime}=f^{-1}(y^{\prime})
into (
2
). The remaining equalities follow from substituting

into (
4

∎

Corollary 4.3
.

For metric spaces
X
and
Y
of the same cardinality,

\displaystyle md_{GH}^{i}(X,Y)=d_{GH}^{i}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\inf\{\operatorname{dis}f:f\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{bi}}(X,Y)\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\inf\{\operatorname{dis}g:g\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{bi}}(Y,X)\}.

of the same cardinality,

\displaystyle d_{GH}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=d_{GH}^{\mathrm{in}}(X,Y)=d_{GH}^{\mathrm{sur}}(X,Y)=d_{GH}^{\mathrm{bi}}(X,Y)=d_{GH}^{i}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\inf\{\operatorname{dis}f:f\colon X\to Y\ \text{is a bijection}\}.

Proof.

It suffices to consider
d_{GH}(X,Y)<\infty
. From the inequalities in Section
4.1
, Corollary
4.3
, and Proposition
4.4
, we need only show
d_{GH}^{i}(X,Y)\leq d_{GH}(X,Y)
.

Given
\varepsilon>0
, choose
f\colon X\to Y
and
g\colon Y\to X
with
d_{GH}(f,g)<d_{GH}(X,Y)+\varepsilon
.

Cover
X
and
Y
by open balls of radius
\varepsilon
. By Proposition
4.5
, the bijection
\tilde{f}
from Theorem
3.1
satisfies
\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{dis}\tilde{f}\leq d_{GH}(f,g)+\varepsilon
. By Corollary
4.3
,
d_{GH}^{i}(X,Y)\leq\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{dis}\tilde{f}<d_{GH}(X,Y)+\varepsilon
. Since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary, the result follows.
∎

5
Approximation of
\delta
-Surjective Mappings

A mapping
f\colon X\to Y
is
\delta
-surjective
if
d_{H}(f(X),Y)<\delta
. We say
f
maps
X

\delta
-surjectively
onto
L\subset Y
if
d_{H}(f(X),L)<\delta
.

Proposition 5.1
.

For metric spaces
X
,
Y
and mappings
f\colon X\to Y
,
g\colon Y\to X
,

d_{H}(f(X),Y)\leq d_{(f,g)}^{+}.

Thus, if
d_{(f,g)}^{+}<\delta
, then
f
is
\delta
-surjective.

Proof.

For any
y\in Y
, set
x=g(y)
. Then (
4
) gives

|f(x)y|\leq|f(g(y))y|=|f(g(y))y|-|g(y)g(y)|\leq d_{(f,g)}^{+}.

Hence
d_{H}(f(X),Y)\leq d_{(f,g)}^{+}
.
∎

Proposition 5.2
.

For a
\delta
-surjective mapping
f\colon X\to Y
between metric spaces, there exists
g\colon Y\to X
such that

d_{g}^{+}\leq d_{f}^{-}+2\delta,\quad d_{g}^{-}\leq d_{f}^{+}+2\delta,\quad d_{(f,g)}^{+}\leq d_{f}^{+}+\delta,\quad d_{(f,g)}^{-}\leq d_{f}^{-}+\delta.

Consequently,

d_{GH}(X,Y)\leq d_{GH}(f,g)\leq\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{dis}f+\delta.

Proof.

Define
g
on
f(X)
by
g(f(x))=x
. For
y\notin f(X)
, choose
y^{\prime}\in f(X)
with
|yy^{\prime}|<\delta
and set
g(y)=g(y^{\prime})
.

Then

|g(y_{1})g(y_{2})|=|g(y_{1}^{\prime})g(y_{2}^{\prime})|=|x_{1}^{\prime}x_{2}^{\prime}|\leq|f(x_{1}^{\prime})f(x_{2}^{\prime})|+d_{f}^{-}=|y_{1}^{\prime}y_{2}^{\prime}|+d_{f}^{-}<|y_{1}y_{2}|+2\delta+d_{f}^{-},

and similarly

|g(y_{1})g(y_{2})|=|x_{1}^{\prime}x_{2}^{\prime}|\geq|f(x_{1}^{\prime})f(x_{2}^{\prime})|-d_{f}^{+}>|y_{1}y_{2}|-2\delta-d_{f}^{+}.

Also,

|f(x)y|<|f(x)y^{\prime}|+\delta=|f(x)f(x^{\prime})|+\delta\leq|xx^{\prime}|+d_{f}^{+}+\delta=|xg(y^{\prime})|+d_{f}^{+}+\delta,

and analogously

|f(x)y|>|f(x)y^{\prime}|-\delta=|f(x)f(x^{\prime})|-\delta\geq|xx^{\prime}|-d_{f}^{-}-\delta=|xg(y^{\prime})|-d_{f}^{-}-\delta.

∎

Proposition 5.3
.

If
d_{GH}(X,Y)<d
, then there exists a
d
-surjective
(2d)
-isometry
f\colon X\to Y
.

Proof.

There exist
f\colon X\to Y
and
g\colon Y\to X
with
d_{GH}(f,g)<d
. By Proposition
5.1
,
f
is
d
-surjective. Since
\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{dis}f\leq d_{GH}(f,g)<d
,
f
is a
(2d)
-isometry.
∎

From Propositions
5.2
and
5.3
we obtain:

Corollary 5.4
.

For metric spaces
X
and
Y
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

d_{GH}(X,Y)<\infty
;

(2)

there exist finite
d,\delta
and a
\delta
-surjective
d
-isometry
f\colon X\to Y
;

(3)

there exist finite
d^{\prime},\delta^{\prime}
and a
\delta^{\prime}
-surjective
d^{\prime}
-isometry
g\colon Y\to X
.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
X
and
Y
be cardinality homogeneous metric spaces of the same cardinality. If there exists a
\delta
-surjective
d
-isometry
f\colon X\to Y
, then there exists a bijective
(d+2\delta)
-isometry
\tilde{f}\colon X\to Y
.

Proof.

By Proposition
5.2
, there exists
g\colon Y\to X
with
d_{GH}(X,Y)\leq\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{dis}f+\delta<\frac{1}{2}d+\delta
. Theorem
4.6
yields a bijection
\tilde{f}\colon X\to Y
with
d_{GH}(X,Y)\leq\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{dis}\tilde{f}<\frac{1}{2}d+\delta
. Thus
\operatorname{dis}\tilde{f}<d+2\delta
.
∎

Proposition 5.6
.

If
f\colon X\to Y
maps
X

\delta
-surjectively onto a subset
L\subset Y
with
\delta>0
, then there exists
\hat{f}\colon X\to L
with
|\hat{f}(x)f(x)|<\delta
for all
x\in X
. Hence

d_{H}(\hat{f}(X),L)\leq 2\delta,\quad d_{\hat{f}}^{+}\leq d_{f}^{+}+2\delta,\quad d_{\hat{f}}^{-}\leq d_{f}^{-}+2\delta,

so
\hat{f}
is
(2\delta)
-surjective onto
L
.

Proof.

For each
x\in X
, choose
\hat{f}(x)\in L
with
|\hat{f}(x)f(x)|<\delta
.
∎

Proposition 5.7
.

If
f\colon X\to Y
is a
d
-isometry that maps
X

\delta
-surjectively onto
L\subset Y
(
\delta,d>0
), then there exists a
(2\delta)
-surjective
(d+2\delta)
-isometry
\hat{f}\colon X\to L
.

Proof.

By Proposition
5.6
, there exists
\hat{f}\colon X\to L
that is
(2\delta)
-surjective onto
L
and satisfies
|\hat{f}(x)f(x)|<\delta
for all
x\in X
. Thus
\hat{f}
is a
(d+2\delta)
-isometry.
∎

Theorem 5.8
.

If
f\colon X\to Y
is a
d
-isometry mapping
X

\delta
-surjectively onto
L\subset Y
(
\delta,d>0
) and both
X
and
L
are cardinality homogeneous with
|X|=|L|
, then there exists a bijective
(d+6\delta)
-isometry
\tilde{f}\colon X\to L
.

Proof.

Proposition
5.7
yields a
(2\delta)
-surjective
(d+2\delta)
-isometry
\hat{f}\colon X\to L
. Theorem

then gives a bijective
(d+6\delta)
-isometry

.
∎

6
Application to Banach Spaces

Proposition 6.1
.

Every topological vector space is cardinality homogeneous.

Proof.

Let
U
be a nonempty open subset of a topological vector space
X
and let
x\in U
. Then
U
and
V=U-x=\{y-x:y\in U\}
are equicardinal. Since
X=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}nV
,
V
and
X
are equicardinal.
∎

Proposition 6.2
.

If there exists a
\delta
-surjective
d
-isometry between Banach spaces
X
and
Y

(
for finite
\delta
and
d
)
, then
|X|=|Y|
.

Proof.

We show
d(X)=d(Y)
. Let
Z\subset X
be dense with
|Z|=d(X)
. The restriction
\tilde{f}=f|_{Z}\colon Z\to Y
is
\varepsilon
-surjective with
\varepsilon=d+\delta
, so
d_{H}(f(Z),Y)<\varepsilon
and
f(Z)
is an
\varepsilon
-net in
Y
. Then
\frac{1}{n}f(Z)
is a
\frac{\varepsilon}{n}
-net, and
S=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}f(Z)
is dense in
Y
with
|S|\leq|Z|
.

By Corollary
5.4
, there exists a
\delta^{\prime}
-surjective
d^{\prime}
-isometry
g\colon Y\to X
(for finite
d^{\prime}
and
\delta^{\prime}
). Symmetrically,
d(X)\leq d(Y)
. Thus
d(X)=d(Y)
. By Toruńczyk’s theorem
[
17
]
,
X
and
Y
are homeomorphic, so
|X|=|Y|
.
∎

Remark 6.3
.

In
[
6
, Theorem 1]
, any
\delta
-surjective mapping
f\colon V\to W
between Banach spaces
V,W
without low-cardinality dense subsets can be transformed into a surjection
F\colon V\to W
with
\|f-F\|\leq 7\delta
. The proof of Proposition 2 in
[
5
]
similarly constructs a surjective
F
with
\|f-F\|\leq 2d+\delta
. This reduces the approximation of
\delta
-surjective
d
-isometries to that of
(2d+\delta)
- or
(d+14\delta)
-isometric surjections.

Below, we construct, from a
d
-isometric
\delta
-surjective
f\colon V\to W
, a mapping
g\colon W\to V
such that Theorem
3.1
applies to the pair
(f,g)
. This yields a simpler derivation of the Dilworth–Tabor theorem.

Proposition 6.4
.

If there exists a
\delta
-surjective
d
-isometry
f\colon X\to Y
between Banach spaces
X
and
Y
, then there exists a bijective
(d+2\delta)
-isometry
\tilde{f}\colon X\to Y
.

Proof.

Proposition
6.1
implies that
X
and
Y
are cardinality homogeneous, and Proposition
6.2
gives
|X|=|Y|
. The result follows from Theorem
5.5
.
∎

Combining Proposition
6.4
with the theorem of M. Omladič and P. Šemrl
[
4
]
yields:

Corollary 6.5

(Tabor
[
6
]
)
.

If there exists a
\delta
-surjective
d
-isometry
f\colon X\to Y
between Banach spaces, then there exists a bijective affine isometry
U\colon X\to Y
with
\|f-U\|\leq 2d+4\delta
.

S. J. Dilworth proved his result for mappings whose image is close to a closed subspace rather than the whole space. The same technique applies here.

Proposition 6.6
.

If
f\colon X\to Y
is a
d
-isometry between Banach spaces that maps
X

\delta
-surjectively onto a closed linear subspace
L\subset Y
, then there exists a bijective
(d+6\delta)
-isometry
\tilde{f}\colon X\to L
.

Proof.

By Proposition
6.1
,
X
and
L
are cardinality homogeneous. Proposition
5.7
gives a
(2\delta)
-surjective
(d+2\delta)
-isometry
\hat{f}\colon X\to L
. Proposition
6.2
yields
|X|=|L|
. Apply Theorem
5.5
.
∎

Combining Proposition
6.6
with
[
4
]
yields:

Corollary 6.7

(Dilworth
[
5
]
)
.

If
f\colon X\to Y
is a
d
-isometry between Banach spaces mapping
X

\delta
-surjectively onto a closed linear subspace
L\subset Y
, then there exists a bijective affine isometry
U\colon X\to L
with
\|f-U\|\leq 2d+12\delta
.

Remark 6.8
.

The theorem of Šemrl–Väisälä
[

improves the bound in Corollary

to

, which is sharp. Combining Corollaries

and

shows that two Banach spaces are isometric if and only if their Gromov–Hausdorff distance is finite
[

.

Revisiting 3-Sasakian and G 2 G_{2} -structures
Simon Salamon and Ragini Singhal

Abstract
. The algebra of exterior differential forms on a regular 3-Sasakian 7-manifold is investigated, with special reference to nearly-parallel G 2 G_{2} 3-forms. This is applied to the study of 3-forms invariant under cohomogeneity-one actions by S ​ O ​ ( 4 ) SO(4) on the 7-sphere and on Berger’s space S ​ O ​ ( 5 ) / S ​ O ​ ( 3 ) SO(5)/SO(3) .



Introduction

Let
M
be an oriented self-dual Einstein 4-manifold
M
with positive
scalar curvature. The bundle of 2-forms

(0.1)

\Lambda^{2}T^{*}M=\Lambda^{+}\oplus\Lambda^{-}

splits into self-dual (SD) and
anti-self-dual (ASD) subbundles, in line with the double covering

SO(4)\to SO(3)^{+}\times SO(3)^{-}

of Lie groups. Our hypothesis implies
that
\Lambda^{-}
has constant curvature in the sense of (
1.5
)
below. The reason for focussing on
\Lambda^{-}
is that (with its standard
orientation and Fubini-Study metric) the projective plane
\mathbb{CP}^{2}

satisfies the hypothesis. Much of the theory extends to orbifolds, and
sources of examples include quaternion-Kähler quotients

[
19
,
14
]
, and the
SO(3)
-invariant orbifolds discovered by
Hitchin
[
24
]
. The latter are parametrized by an integer
k\geqslant 3

and the first two examples are
S^{4}
and
\mathbb{CP}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.

The (total space of the)
SO(3)^{-}
principal bundle
P^{-}
associated
to
\Lambda^{-}
is 3-Sasakian
[
28
]
, and therefore admits an Einstein
metric and a family of nearly-parallel
G_{2}
(for short, NP
2
)
structures. An equivalent way of expressing the 3-Sasakian condition
is to say that the Riemannian cone
P^{-}\!\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
is hyperkähler,
meaning that it has a metric with holonomy
Sp(2)

[
7
]
. However, it is well known that a squashing of the fibres
of
P^{-}
produces a second Einstein metric and canonical NP
2
structure
whose cone has holonomy
equal
to
\mathit{Spin}(7)
.

Now suppose (restricting to an open set if necessary) that
M
is
spin, so that its structure group lifts to
\mathit{Spin}(4)\cong SU(2)^{2}
.
The negative spin bundle
\Sigma^{-}
can be regarded as a quaternionic
line bundle over
M,
and admits a family of metrics with
\mathit{Spin}(7)

holonomy, all asymptotic to the cone over (a
2:1
cover of) the
squashed NP
2
manifold
[
12
]
. An initial purpose of this article is
to relate well-known constructions of the relevant differential forms
on these spaces, one via a
\mathit{Spin}(7)
4-form on a vector bundle, the
other via 3-Sasakian 1-forms on a principal bundle.
This is motivated by the situation in which
M
itself admits an
action by
SO(3)
with 3-dimensional orbits. In this case,
P^{-}
or
its double cover
\tilde{P}^{-}
will admit an action by
SO(4)
or
SU(2)^{2}

of cohomogeneity one, preserving the
G_{2}
structures. This happens
when
M
is
S^{4}
or
\mathbb{CP}^{2},
and more generally for the Hitchin
orbifolds.

We shall make this explicit for the 7-sphere
S^{7}\cong\tilde{P}^{-}\!S^{4},
in
order to express its squashed NP
2
structure in canonical form. The
result is consistent with a known expression for the round metric on

S^{7}
subject to
SO(4)
and
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
symmetry
[
35
]
. The same
technique is applied to the isotropy irreducible space

B^{7}=SO(5)/SO(3),
which Berger showed has positive sectional
curvature
[
9
]
. This space also has a homogeneous NP
2
structure

[
32
]
, a cohomogeneity-one action by
SO(4)

[
31
]
, and is
diffeomorphic to
\tilde{P}^{+}
for a non-trivial Hitchin orbifold

[
23
]
. Although not our main concern, exhaustive classifications
of associative submanifolds in
B^{7}
and in squashed 3-Sasakian
manifolds are undertaken in
[
5
,
6
]
using twistor
correspondences, pursuing work in
[
30
,
28
]
for
S^{7}
.

Paying debts to this previous mountain of work, our survey highlights
the distinction between the homogeneous NP
2
structures on
S^{7}
and

B^{7}
, when expressed in terms of the local differential geometry on
an interval times
SO(4)
. The choice of geodesics orthogonal to the
orbits allows one to construct bases
(X_{i})
and
(Y_{i})
of
invariant 1-forms that enable the respective 3-forms to be built up
using upper triangular
2\times 2
matrices; see Corollaries
3.4

and
4.4
. These bases ultimately arise from different conjugacy
classes of 3-dimensional subgroups of
SO(4)
, and different group
diagrams for the cohomogeneity-one actions.

Just as a hypersurface of a
G_{2}
holonomy manifold acquires a
so-called half-flat
SU(3)
structure, so a hypersurface of a NP
2

manifold has an analogous nearly-half-flat (NHF) property. There is a
sense in which a nearly-Kähler manifold has both properties. We
conclude with a brief investigation of induced structures on
hypersurfaces of
S^{7}
and
B^{7}
, and we show that constants defining
the homogeneous NP
2
structures are to some extent determined by the NHF
condition. The results of this section form part of a broader project
to describe exceptional structures in dimensions 6 and 7.

1.
Notation

In the first part of this paper, we shall adopt the technique of

]

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\Phi&=&d\phi+\phi\wedge\phi\\[3.0pt]

The choice of a unit section of

determines a local orthonormal

S^{2}\Sigma^{-}\;\cong\;(\Lambda^{2}_{-})_{c}

{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}(\overline{\omega}\wedge\omega)=i\Omega_{1}+i\Omega_{2}+k\Omega_{3}=\Omega.

We use a Greek letter without an index to stand for a quantity with
values in
\mathbb{H}
that encodes all the individual components.
Scalings are fixed by the existence pointwise of an orthonormal basis

\{\omega_{0},\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3}\}

such that

\Omega_{k}\ =\ \omega_{0k}-\omega_{ij}

for

It follows from (

where

\Phi={\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\kappa\Omega

as stated in
[

, p 842]

although we are assuming throughout that

Now let

\alpha=da-a\phi=\alpha_{0}+i\alpha_{1}+j\alpha_{2}+k\alpha_{3}.

At each point, the annihilator of

is the horizontal subspace determined by the connection. We can
then determine the effect of the action

on differential
forms. It yields the transformations

and

Next, consider the quaternion-valued 1-form

\beta\ =\ \overline{a}\alpha\ =\ \beta_{0}+i\beta_{1}+j\beta_{2}+k\beta_{3}

as defined in

. However, from now one, we work on

so
that

and
\beta
becomes a vector-valued 1-form. In this setting,

Lemma 1.1
.

d\beta+\beta\wedge\beta=-2\,\mathrm{Im}\,(\phi\wedge\beta)-\Phi
.

Proof.

Using (
1.2
), (
1.5
), and (
1.7
),

(1.9)

d\alpha\ =\ -(\alpha+a\phi)\wedge\phi-ad\phi\\
\ =\ -\alpha\wedge\phi-a\Phi.

Therefore

\begin{array}[]{rcl}d\beta\ =\ d(\overline{a}\alpha)&=&d\overline{a}\wedge\alpha+\overline{a}\,d\alpha\\[3.0pt]
&=&d\overline{a}\wedge\alpha+\overline{a}(-\alpha\wedge\phi-a\Phi)\\[3.0pt]
&=&\overline{\alpha}\wedge\alpha-(\phi\wedge\beta+\beta\wedge\phi)-\Phi.\end{array}

Now restrict to

so that

(and, as always,

\overline{\alpha}\wedge\alpha=\overline{\alpha}a\wedge\overline{a}\alpha=\overline{\beta}\wedge\beta=-\beta\wedge\beta

\square

The first component of

is

(1.11)

d\beta_{1}+2\beta_{23}\ =\ -2(\phi_{2}\wedge\beta_{3}-\phi_{3}\wedge\beta_{2})-\Phi_{1}.

Note that

so their exterior derivatives cannot be proportional, which

is indicated by the presence of

rather than the self-dual

Using these, we can build

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\lambda=2/\sqrt{5}
. The 3-form

(2.13)

\textstyle\varphi\ =\ 2\lambda\,\Theta+\lambda^{3}\Upsilon

defines an NP
2
structure on
\tilde{P}^{-}
with
\mu=6/\sqrt{5}
. The

(2.14)

\textstyle h_{7}\ =\ \frac{4}{5}\sum\limits_{l=1}^{3}\beta_{i}\otimes\beta_{i}+\pi^{*}g_{4}.

Proof.

The calculation of

is a little delicate:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}d\Theta&=&-\mathrm{Re}\,[d\Phi\wedge\beta+\Phi\wedge d\beta]\\[3.0pt]
&=&-\mathrm{Re}\,[-2\phi\wedge\Phi\wedge\beta]-\mathrm{Re}\,[\Phi\wedge(-\beta\wedge\beta-2\phi\wedge\beta-\Phi)]\\[3.0pt]
&=&2\,d\Upsilon-\raise 2.0pt\hbox{$\chi$}.\end{array}

Terms in
\phi\wedge\Phi
and

Recall from (

\raise 2.0pt\hbox{$\chi$}=-\frac{3}{2}\omega_{0123},

On the other hand,

so the NP

conditin is satisfied when

The form of

\square

The complete metric

appears on

[

3.

Let

Remark 4.5
.

In the wider homogeneous context, conjugacy classes of 3-dimensional
subalgebras of
\mathfrak{so}(5)
(namely
\mathfrak{su}(2)^{-}
,
\mathfrak{so}(3)_{d}
and principal

\mathfrak{so}(3)

S^{4}
,

\mathbb{G}\mathrm{r}_{3}(\mathfrak{so}(5))
and
B^{7}
) for the natural Morse-Bott functional on
the 21-dimensional Grassmannian
\mathbb{G}\mathrm{r}_{3}(\mathfrak{so}(5))
. Two associated
unstable manifolds of dimensions 8 and 12 then admit incomplete
quaternion-Kähler metrics by the theory in
[

, and it is
tempting to tie this in with the results of this article.

The behaviour of the 1-forms
Y_{i}
at

and
\pi/3
gives some
information about the singular locus and
SO(4)
action. For
example,
Y_{2}
vanishes at

, so the stabilizer
O(2)
is tangent
to the annhilator of
\left<Y_{1},Y_{3},Y_{4},Y_{5},Y_{6}\right>
. At our
distinguished point,
SO(2)
rotates
\left<E_{4},E_{5}\right>
by

\theta
and
\left<E_{2},E_{3}\right>
by
2\theta
. Its induced action
on (

E_{23}\pm E_{45}
, but rotates

\left<E_{24}+E_{53},E_{25}+E_{34}\right>
by
3\theta
, whereas

\left<E_{24}-E_{53},E_{25}-E_{34}\right>
by
-\theta
. The singular
orbit
SO(4)/O(2)
is therefore covered by the circle bundle in

\mathcal{O}(3,1)\to\mathbb{CP}^{1}\times\mathbb{CP}^{1}
.

The subgroup
SU(2)^{+}
of
SO(4)
(defined by our choice of oriented
basis) has stabilizer
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
at
t=0
, but
SU(2)^{-}
acts freely. The
opposite is true at
t=\pi/3
, giving rise to a group diagram with
weights
(3,1)
at

(1,3)
at

, relative to

. It follows that

is smooth for

t\in(0,\pi/3]
, but not at
t=0
. Since
SO(4)/SU(2)^{+}\cong SO(3)
,
the commutator
SU(2)^{-}
acts on the quotient with generic
stabilizer
V=\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}
. This observation leads to the

SO(4)
-equivariant diffeomorphism
B^{7}\cong\tilde{P}^{+}\kern-1.0pt\mathcal{O}_{5}

established by Grove, Wilking, and Ziller
[
23
]
, where
\mathcal{O}_{k}

is Hitchin’s orbifold with a
\mathbb{Z}_{k-2}
singularity transverse to

\mathbb{RP}^{2}
(we use
k\geqslant 3
as in
[
24
]
). We outline this setup next
because of its implications for NP
2
structures with an
SO(4)

symmetry, working backwards from
\mathcal{O}_{k}
. Recall our convention that
a self-dual conformal structure has vanishing Weyl component
W_{-}

(as for
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
whose Kähler 2-form
\omega
lies in
\Lambda^{+}
).

The irreducible action of
SO(3)=G
on

has generic orbit
G/V
,
and singular orbits
\mathcal{L}\cong G/L
and
\mathcal{R}\cong G/R

and

in different blocks of

is constructed by gluing disk bundles
over
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{R}
, each a copy of
\mathbb{RP}^{2}
. If we choose

to be
the singular locus, then

\mathcal{O}_{k}\ \approx\ (G\times_{L/\ell}(D^{2}/\ell)\bigcup_{G/V}\>(G\times_{R}D^{2}),

and this space is still homeomorphic to

.
The subgroups
L,R
act on the tangent and normal spaces

5.
Hypersurface calculations

In the setup of (
3.33
), we resort to the description of an NP
2

manifold by means of the induced
SU(3)
structures on the
hypersurfaces for which
t\in(0,\pi/3)
is constant, so the generic

SO(4)
orbits. The condition (
2.12
) is equivalent to the next two
equations:

(5.45)

\begin{array}[]{rcl}d\xi-\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\mathrm{Re}\,\Xi&=&\mu\,\mathrm{Im}\,\Xi\end{array}

(5.46)

\begin{array}[]{rcl}d(\mathrm{Re}\,\Xi)&=&\mu\,{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\xi^{2}.\end{array}

The second characterizes the so-called nearly-half-flat (NHF)
condition introduced by
[
15
]
, and studied in the present context by
the second author
[
33
]
.

Recall the NP
2
structure on
S^{7}
determined by the the basis
(X_{i})

and the
2\times 2
matrices that feature in Corollary
3.4
. Let us
just consider 6-dimensional orbits of
SO(4)
with slightly more
general
SU(3)
structures. The next result shows that it is to some
extent possible to reverse engineer the constants occurring in
Corollary
3.4
by merely applying the NHF condition for all values
of
t
:

Proposition 5.1
.

Define
\xi
and
\Xi
by (
3.33
) with

\left(\kern-5.0pt\begin{array}[]{c}X_{2k-1}\\[3.0pt]
X_{2k}\end{array}\kern-5.0pt\right)\ =\ \left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\lambda&\lambda(ac_{k}+b)\\[7.0pt]
0&4s_{k}\end{array}\right)\left(\kern-5.0pt\begin{array}[]{c}f_{k}\\[3.0pt]
e_{k}\end{array}\kern-5.0pt\right),

where
\lambda,a,b
are (unconstrained)
constants. Then (
5.46
) holds for all
t
iff

(i)

(a,b)=(0,-1)
or
(0,0),
in which case
\mu=-2/\lambda,
or

(ii)

(a,b)=(\pm 2,1)
, in which case
\mu=-\frac{1}{2}(\lambda^{2}+4)/\lambda
.

In this formulation, the coefficient ‘4’ is retained as a
normalization.

Proof.

Equations (
3.33
) yield

\xi\ =\ -4\lambda(s_{1}e_{1}f_{1}+s_{2}e_{2}f_{2}+s_{3}e_{3}f_{3})

so

{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\xi^{2}\ =\ -16\lambda^{2}(s_{2}s_{3}e_{23}f_{23}+s_{3}s_{1}e_{31}f_{31}+s_{1}s_{2}e_{12}f_{12}).

Moreover

\begin{array}[]{rcl}d\,\Xi&=&\displaystyle\left(-2\lambda f_{23}-2\lambda(ac_{1}\!+\!b)e_{23}-8is_{1}e_{23}\right)\wedge\\[3.0pt]
&&\displaystyle\phantom{mmm}(\lambda f_{2}+\lambda(ac_{2}\!+\!b)e_{2}+4is_{2}e_{2})\wedge(\lambda f_{3}+\lambda(ac_{3}\!+\!b)e_{3}+4is_{3}e_{3})+\cdots\end{array}

Therefore the
e_{23}f_{23}
component, call it
\Gamma_{1},
of

d(\mathrm{Re}\,\Xi)
equals

\begin{array}[]{rcl}&&-2\lambda[\lambda^{2}(ac_{2}\!+\!b)(ac_{3}\!+\!b)-16s_{2}s_{3}]-2\lambda^{3}(ac_{1}\!+\!b)\\[3.0pt]
&=&-2\lambda^{3}[a^{2}c_{2}c_{3}-abc_{1}+b^{2}]+32\lambda s_{2}s_{3}-2\lambda^{3}(ac_{1}+b)\\[3.0pt]
&=&-2\lambda^{3}(b+b^{2})-2\lambda^{3}a^{2}c_{2}c_{3}+32\lambda s_{2}s_{3}+2\lambda^{3}(ab-a)c_{1}\\[3.0pt]
&=&-2\lambda^{3}(b+b^{2})-16\lambda-2\lambda(16+a^{2}\lambda^{2})c_{2}c_{3}+2\lambda^{3}(ab-a)c_{1},\end{array}

the last line by (
3.26
).
This must equal
\mu
times

-16\lambda^{2}s_{2}s_{3}=8\lambda^{2}(1+2c_{2}c_{3})
for all
t
.
To eliminate the coefficient of
c_{1}
above, we need
a=0
or
b=1
.

If
a=0,
we obtain the system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}-32\lambda=16\lambda^{2}\mu\\[3.0pt]
-2\lambda^{3}b(1+b)-16\lambda=8\lambda^{2}\mu,\end{array}\right.

which implies that

\lambda\mu=-2
and
b=0
or
-1
.
If
b=1,
we obtain

-4\lambda^{3}-2\lambda(8+a^{2}\lambda^{2})c_{2}c_{3}-8\lambda\ =\ \mu(8a^{2}\lambda^{2}+16c_{2}c_{3}),

which provides the system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\lambda^{2}+2\mu\lambda+4=0\\[3.0pt]
a^{2}\lambda^{2}+2\mu\lambda+4=0,\end{array}\right.

and
a=\pm 2
. The unique quadratic then gives the constraint between

\lambda
and
\mu
.

\square

As a corollary, any positive definite linear combination of

g_{7}
and
h_{7}
on
S^{7}
is associated to an NHF structure. Note
however that
\lambda=2/\sqrt{5}
implies that
\mu=-6/\sqrt{5},
as in Proposition

2.1
modulo orientation.

The analysis of (
5.45
) requires more extensive
calculation. Turning to
B^{7},
we have established that the solution
in Corollary
4.4
is essentially unique within a 3-parameter
family. This is part (i) of

Theorem 5.2
.

Define
\xi
and
\Xi
by (
3.33
) with
Y_{i}
in place of

X_{i},
where now

\left(\kern-5.0pt\begin{array}[]{c}Y_{2k-1}\\[3.0pt]
Y_{2k}\end{array}\kern-5.0pt\right)\ =\ \left(\begin{array}[]{cc}2\lambda&2\lambda ac_{k}\\[7.0pt]
b&2s_{k}\end{array}\right)\left(\kern-5.0pt\begin{array}[]{c}p_{k}\\[3.0pt]
n_{k}\end{array}\kern-5.0pt\right),

and
\lambda,a,b
are
(unconstrained) constants. Then

(i)

(
5.45
) and (
5.46
) both
hold iff
(\lambda,a,b)=(\pm\frac{2}{\sqrt{5}},\frac{1}{2},0)
;

(ii)

(
5.46
) holds if either

\lambda^{2}(4-a^{2})=3
and
b=0
, or
(\lambda,a,b)=(\frac{1}{2},2,\pm\sqrt{3})
.

In all cases,
\mu
is determined by
\lambda
.

We omit the proof of (i), since analysis of (
5.45
) is
somewhat fruitless. The proof of (ii) is analogous to that of
Proposition
5.1
, but more involved since it is based upon
(
4.43
). Both parts were checked by hand and using
Maple
,
but in different orders.

The significance of the condition

is that it guarantees smooth

the

and

There is an obvious generalization of the hypotheses of Theorem

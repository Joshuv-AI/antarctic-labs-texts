. We obtain new invariant Einstein metrics on the compact Lie group SU ⁡ ( N ) \SU(N) which are not naturally reductive. Introduction

A Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
is called Einstein if it has constant Ricci curvature, i.e.
\Ric_{g}=\lambda\cdot g
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
(cf.
[
Be
]
).
General existence results are difficult to obtain, so one may impose some symmetry conditions to try to prove existence of Einstein metrics.
For the case of a Riemannian homogeneous space
M=G/K
, where
G
is a Lie group and
H
a closed subgroup of
G
, the aim is the prove existence of
G
-invariant
Einstein metrics and, if possible, classify such metrics. We refer to
[
W1
]
,
[
W2
]
and
[
A
]
for various results about homogeneous Einstein metrics

For a
compact Lie group the problem focuses to finding all left-invariant Einstein metrics.
This is more subtle, since the space of all left-invariant metrics on a Lie group up to isometry and scaling is quite difficult to study.
It is well known that a compact and semisimple Lie group equipped with a bi-invariant metric is Einstein.
In
[
DZ
]
J.E. D’Atri and W. Ziller found a large number of naturally reductive metrics on compact Lie groups (classical and exceptional) and
they raised the question of existence of left-invariant Einstein metrics which are not naturally reductive.
The aim of the present paper is to prove
existence of new non naturally reductive left-invariant Einstein metrics on the special unitary group
\SU(N)
, and extend some previous results
and techniques.

A method to look for left-invariant Einstein metrics on a compact Lie group
G
is to restrict the search into a subset of corresponding scalar products on the Lie algebra

\mathfrak{g}
of
G
as follows.
We consider a homogeneous space
G/K
and the natural submersion
G\to G/K
with fiber
K
.
We decompose the Lie subalgebra of
K
as
\mathfrak{k}=\mathfrak{k}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{k}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathfrak{k}_{p}
, where
\mathfrak{k}_{0}
is the Lie algebra of the center of
K
and

\mathfrak{k}_{i}
are simple ideals. We also assume that the tangent space
\mathfrak{m}\cong T_{0}(G/K)
of
G/K
decomposes into irreducible and non equivalent
\Ad(K)
-modules

\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{m}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathfrak{m}_{s}
.

Then the tangent space of
G
is decomposed
into a direct sum of irreducible
\Ad(K)
-modules

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{k}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathfrak{k}_{p}\oplus\mathfrak{m}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathfrak{m}_{s}

and we restrict ourselves to

left-invariant metrics on
G
which are determined by
\Ad(K)
-invariant scalar products on
\mathfrak{g}
.
Then calculations for the Ricci curvature become simpler, by taking into account well known formula for the Ricci curvature for
G
-invariant metrics on a homogeneous space
whose isotropy representation decomposes into a sum of non equivalent irreducible summands
(cf.
[
PS
]
).

Concerning previous results about non naturally reductive left-invariant Einstein metrics on the special unitary group, the first existence result was obtained by K. Mori in

[
M
]
, who proved existence of such metrics
\SU(4+n)
(
n\geq 2
).
There, the Lie group
\SU(4+n)
was considered as a total space over generalized flag manifolds
G/K=\SU(4+n)/\s(\U(2)\times\U(2)\times\U(n))
with three isotropy summands.
In
[
ASS1
]
the authors obtained invariant Einstein metrics on the compact Lie group
\SU(2n)

(n>3)
which are not naturally reductive, by considering

\SU(2n)
as total space over the generalized Wallach spaces
G/K=\SU(2n)/\U(n)
.
Note that in that case the center of the Lie algebra of
K=\U(n)
is one-dimensional, which makes description of Ricci tensor of invariant metrics accessible.

In
[
ASS2
]
the authors studied non naturally reductive left-invariant Einstein metrics on the special unitary groups
G=\SU(\ell+m+n)
by
using the
generalized flag manifolds
G/K=\SU(\ell+m+n)/\s(\U(\ell)\times\U(m)\times\U(n))
.
In this case the center of the Lie algebra of
K=\s(\U(\ell)\times\U(m)\times\U(n))
is two-dimensional which makes description of Ricci tensor more complicated.
We proved that
\SU(4+n)

(n\geq 2)
and also
\SU(5)
admit a certain number of non naturally reductive left-invariant Einstein metrics.
Notice that for the Lie groups
\SU(3)
,
\SU(4)
and
\SU(2)\times\SU(2)
, the number of left-invariant Einstein metrics is still unknown.

For non naturally reductive left-invariant Einstein metrics on other classical and exceptional Lie groups we refer to
[
AMS
]
,
[
ASS2
]
,
[
ASS1
]
,
[
ASS4
]
,
[
CL
]
,
[
CCD
]
.

In the present paper we generalize the above approaches and prove existence of new non naturally reductive left-invariant Einstein metrics on the special unitary group
\SU(N)
,
N=k_{1}+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{p}
(where
k_{i}\geq 2
(
i=1,\dots,p
)), by using the generalized flag manifold

G/K=\SU(k_{1}+\cdots+k_{p})/\s(\U(k_{1})\times\cdots\times\U(k_{p}))
.

Note that
the isotropy representation
\mathfrak{m}=\sum_{1\leq r<s\leq p}\mathfrak{m}_{rs}
of
G/K
does not contain equivalent irreducible summands as
\Ad(K)
-modules.
The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{su}(N)
is decomposed into

\mathfrak{su}(N)=\mathfrak{k}_{0}+\sum_{1\leq i\leq p}\mathfrak{su}(k_{i})+\sum_{1\leq r<s\leq p}\mathfrak{m}_{rs}

as
\Ad(K)
-module,
where
\mathfrak{k}_{0}
is the center
\mathfrak{k}
(
\dim\mathfrak{k}_{0}=p-1
). Thus we see that left-invariant metrics
g
are determined by
\Ad(K)
-invariant inner products of the form

\langle\ \ ,\ \ \rangle=<\ \,\ \ >|_{\mathfrak{k}_{0}}+\sum_{1\leq i\leq p}x_{i}B|_{\mathfrak{su}(k_{i})}+\sum_{1\leq r<s\leq p}x_{rs}B|_{\mathfrak{m}_{rs}}

where
<\ \,\ \ >|_{\mathfrak{k}_{0}}
denotes an arbitrary inner product on the center
\mathfrak{k}_{0}
.

We take
k_{2}=\cdots=k_{p}=k\geq 2
.
Then the main result is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p\geq 3
.

1)
If
k_{1}>k\geq 2
, then the Lie group
\SU(N)
(
N=k_{1}+(p-1)k
) admits at least two
\Ad(\s(\U(k_{1})\times\underbrace{\U(k)\times\cdots\times\U(k)}_{p-1})
-invariant Einstein metrics, which are not naturally reductive.

2)
If
k_{1}=k
, then the Lie group
\SU(N)
(
N=p\,k
) admits at least one
\Ad(\s(\underbrace{\U(k)\times\cdots\times\U(k)}_{p})
-invariant Einstein metric, which is not naturally reductive.

For
p\geq 4
these metrics are different from the ones obtained in
[
ASS1
]
and
[
ASS2
]
.

In addition, it is possible to show that, for
k_{1}\geq 8kp
,
k\geq 2
and
p\geq 3
, the Lie group
\SU(N)
(
N=k_{1}+(p-1)k
) admits at least four
\Ad(\s(\U(k_{1})\times\underbrace{\U(k)\times\cdots\times\U(k)}_{p-1})
-invariant Einstein metrics, which are not naturally reductive.
We only give a sketch of its proof in Remark 1 of Section 4.

At this point we would like to comment on a seemingly similarity of Theorem 1.1 to Theorem 1.1 in our recent work
[
ASS4
]
, where we studied existence of left-invariant
Einstein metrics on
\SO(n)
, which are not naturally reductive.
There, we used a real flag manifold
G/K
in the decomposition

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathfrak{k}_{p}\oplus\mathfrak{m}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathfrak{m}_{s}
of the Lie algebra of
\SO(n)
.
The Lie algebra of
K
for a real flag manifold
G/K
has no center, whereas in the present corresponding decomposition for
\SU(N)
, we use a complex flag manifold

G/K
. Here, the dimension of the center of the Lie algebra of
K
is
p-1
(
p\geq 3
). This makes the description of Ricci tensor for left-invariant metrics on
G
corresponding to
\Ad(K)
-invariant scalar products on
\mathfrak{g}
more complicated for
\SU(N)
, that is, Ricci tensor may have non diagonal part.
This requires some non trivial study of the action of the center of
K
(cf. Lemma 3.4, Lemma 3.6 and Proposition 3.8).

Another point that one may observe, is that in part 1) of both theorems we prove existence of at least two invariant Einstein metrics.
This is because in both cases the Einstein equation reduces to a polynomial parametric system of equations, and the two Einstein metrics are obtained as positive solutions of a polynomial equations of degree 8 in case for
\SO(n)
, but a polynomial equations of degree 16 in case for
\SU(n)
. By making appropriate choices of the parameters of the system, we are able to prove existence of more solutions.

We show that, to obtain an Einstein metric on

Next, we take

2.
The Ricci tensor for reductive homogeneous spaces

We recall an expression for the Ricci tensor for a
G
-invariant Riemannian
metric on a reductive homogeneous space whose isotropy representation
is decomposed into a sum of non equivalent irreducible summands.

Let
G
be a compact semisimple Lie group,
K
a connected closed subgroup of
G
and
let
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{k}
be the corresponding Lie algebras.
The Killing form
B
of
\mathfrak{g}
is negative definite, so we can define an
\mbox{Ad}(G)
-invariant inner product
-B
on

\mathfrak{g}
.
Let
\mathfrak{g}
=
\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{m}
be a reductive decomposition of
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to
-B
so that
\big[\,\mathfrak{k},\,\mathfrak{m}\,\big]\subset\mathfrak{m}
and

\mathfrak{m}\cong T_{o}(G/K)
. We decompose
{\mathfrak{m}}
into irreducible non equivalent
\mbox{Ad}(K)
-modules as follows:

{\mathfrak{m}}={\mathfrak{m}}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus{\mathfrak{m}}_{q}.

(1)

Then for the decomposition (
1
) any
G
-invariant metric on
G/K
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\langle\,\,\,,\,\,\,\rangle=x_{1}(-B)|_{\mbox{\footnotesize$\mathfrak{m}$}_{1}}+\cdots+x_{q}(-B)|_{\mbox{\footnotesize$\mathfrak{m}$}_{q}},

(2)

for positive real numbers
(x_{1},\dots,x_{q})\in\mathbb{R}^{q}_{+}
.
If, for the decomposition (
1
) of
\mathfrak{m}
, the Ricci tensor
r
of a
G
-invariant Riemannian metric
\langle\,\,\,,\,\,\,\rangle
on
G/K
satisfies
r({\mathfrak{m}}_{i},{\mathfrak{m}}_{j})=(0)
for
i\neq j
, then the Ricci tensor
r
is of the same form as (
2
), that is

r=z_{1}(-B)|_{\mbox{\footnotesize$\mathfrak{m}$}_{1}}+\cdots+z_{q}(-B)|_{\mbox{\footnotesize$\mathfrak{m}$}_{q}},

for some real numbers
z_{1},\ldots,z_{q}
.

Let
\{e_{\alpha}\}
be a
(-B)
-orthonormal basis
adapted to the decomposition of
\mathfrak{m}
, i.e.

e_{\alpha}\in{\mathfrak{m}}_{i}
for some
i
, and

\alpha<\beta
if
i<j
.
We put
{A^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}}=-B\big(\big[e_{\alpha},e_{\beta}\big],e_{\gamma}\big)
so that

\big[e_{\alpha},e_{\beta}\big]=\displaystyle{\sum_{\gamma}A^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}e_{\gamma}}
and set

\displaystyle{k\brack{ij}}=\sum(A^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta})^{2}
, where the sum is
taken over all indices
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
with
e_{\alpha}\in{\mathfrak{m}}_{i},\ e_{\beta}\in{\mathfrak{m}}_{j},\ e_{\gamma}\in{\mathfrak{m}}_{k}
(cf.
[
WZ
]
).
Then the positive numbers
\displaystyle{k\brack{ij}}
are independent of the

(-B)
-orthonormal bases chosen for
{\mathfrak{m}}_{i},{\mathfrak{m}}_{j},{\mathfrak{m}}_{k}
,
and

\displaystyle{k\brack{ij}}\ =\ \displaystyle{k\brack{ji}}\ =\ \displaystyle{j\brack{ki}}.

Let
d_{k}=\dim{\mathfrak{m}}_{k}
. Then we have the following:

Lemma 2.1
.

(
[
PS
]
)
The components
{r}_{1},\dots,{r}_{q}

of the Ricci tensor
{r}
of the metric
\langle\,\,\,,\,\,\,\rangle
of the
form (
2
) on
G/K
are given by

{r}_{k}=\frac{1}{2x_{k}}+\frac{1}{4d_{k}}\sum_{j,i}\frac{x_{k}}{x_{j}x_{i}}{k\brack{ji}}-\frac{1}{2d_{k}}\sum_{j,i}\frac{x_{j}}{x_{k}x_{i}}{j\brack{ki}}\quad(k=1,\dots,q),

(3)

where the sum is taken over
i,j=1,\dots,q
.

If, for the decomposition (
1
) of
\mathfrak{m}
, the Ricci tensor
r
of a
G
-invariant Riemannian metric
\langle\,\,\,,\,\,\,\rangle
on
G/K
satisfies
r({\mathfrak{m}}_{i},{\mathfrak{m}}_{j})=0
for
i\neq j
,
then, by Lemma
2.1
, it follows that
G
-invariant Einstein metrics on
M=G/K
are exactly the positive real solutions
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{q})\in\mathbb{R}^{q}_{+}
of the system of equations
\{r_{1}=\lambda,\,r_{2}=\lambda,\,\ldots,\,r_{q}=\lambda\}
, where
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
is the Einstein constant.

3.
A class of left-invariant metrics on
\SU(N)=\SU(k_{1}+k_{2}+\dots+k_{p})
and Ricci tensor

3.1.
A decomposition of the tangent space

We will describe a decomposition of the tangent space of the Lie group
\SU(N)
.
We consider the closed subgroup
K=\s(\U(k_{1})\times\U(k_{2})\times\cdots\times\U(k_{p}))
of
G=\SU(N)=\SU(k_{1}+\cdots+k_{p})

with
k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{p}\geq 1
,
where the embedding of
K
in
G
is diagonal.
The homogeneous space
G/K
obtained is known as generalized flag manifold.
Then the tangent space
\mathfrak{su}(k_{1}+\cdots+k_{p})
of the special unitary group
G=\SU(k_{1}+\cdots+k_{p})
can be written as a direct sum of
\Ad(K)
-invariant modules as

\mathfrak{su}(k_{1}+\cdots+k_{p})=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{m},

(4)

where
\mathfrak{m}
corresponds to the tangent space of
G/K
.

Then the tangent space
\mathfrak{m}
of
G/K

is given by
\mathfrak{k}^{\perp}
in
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{su}(k_{1}+\cdots+k_{p})
with respect to the
\mbox{Ad}(G)
-invariant inner product
-B
. If we denote by
M(q,r)
the set of all
q\times r
complex matrices, then we see that

\mathfrak{m}
is given by

\mathfrak{m}=\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}0&A_{1\,2}&A_{1\,3}&\cdots&A_{1\,p-1}&A_{1\,p}\\
-{\bar{A}_{1\,2}}^{t}&0&A_{2\,3}&\cdots&A_{2\,p-1}&A_{2\,p}\\
-{\bar{A}_{1\,3}}^{t}&-{\bar{A}_{2\,3}}^{t}&0&\cdots&A_{3\,p-1}&A_{3\,p}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\vskip 2.0pt-{\bar{A}_{1\,p-1}}^{t}&-{\bar{A}_{2\,p-1}}^{t}&-{\bar{A}_{3\,p-1}}^{t}&\cdots&0&A_{p-1\,p}\\
-{\bar{A}_{1\,p}}^{t}&-{\bar{A}_{2\,p}}^{t}&-{\bar{A}_{3\,p}}^{t}&\cdots&-{\bar{A}_{p-1\,p}}^{t}&0\end{array}\right):\begin{array}[]{c}A_{rs}\in M(k_{r},k_{s})\\
1\leq r<s\leq p\end{array}\right\}.

We set, for
r<s
,

\mathfrak{m}_{rs}=\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}0&\cdots&&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&A_{rs}&\vdots\\
&-{\bar{A}_{rs}}^{t}&&\\
0&\cdots&&0\end{array}\right):A_{rs}\in M(k_{r},k_{s})\right\}.

(5)

We take a Weyl basis
\{X_{\alpha\beta}^{(rs)},Y_{\alpha\beta}^{(rs)};1\leq\alpha\leq k_{r},1\leq\beta\leq k_{s}\}
for
\mathfrak{m}_{rs}
. That is, let
E_{\alpha\beta}^{(rs)}\in M(k_{r},k_{s})
be the matrix with 1 in the
\alpha
-th row and
\beta
-th column. We denote by
X_{\alpha\beta}^{(rs)}
the element in
\mathfrak{m}_{rs}
with
A_{rs}=E_{\alpha\beta}^{(rs)}
in (
5
), and by
Y_{\alpha\beta}^{(rs)}
the element in
\mathfrak{m}_{rs}
with
A_{rs}=\sqrt{-1}E_{\alpha\beta}^{(rs)}
in (
5
).

Note that
\mathfrak{k}=\mathfrak{s}(\mathfrak{u}(k_{1})\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathfrak{u}(k_{p}))=\mathfrak{c}\oplus\mathfrak{su}(k_{1})\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathfrak{su}(k_{p})
where
\mathfrak{c}
is the
(p-1)
-dimensional center of
\mathfrak{k}
.
For
i=1,\dots,p
we also set
\mathfrak{m}_{i}=\mathfrak{su}(k_{i})
.
Then the decomposition (
4
) of the tangent space of the special unitary group
G=\SU(k_{1}+\cdots+k_{p})
takes the form

\mathfrak{su}(k_{1}+\cdots+k_{p})=\mathfrak{c}\oplus\mathfrak{m}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{m}_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathfrak{m}_{p}\oplus\bigoplus_{1\leq r<s\leq p}\mathfrak{m}_{rs}.

(6)

Now we consider an orthogonal basis
\{H_{1},H_{2},\dots,H_{p-1}\}
of the center
\mathfrak{c}
with respect to inner product
-B
,
which will be convenient for our study. We set
K_{j}=k_{1}+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{j}
for
j=1,\ldots,p-1
.

Let

H_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{k_{1}}\text{\large{I}}_{k_{1}}&\text{{\large{0}}}\\
\text{{\large{0}}}&\displaystyle-\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{N-K_{1}}\text{\large{I}}_{N-K_{1}}\end{array}\right),\quad H_{2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\displaystyle\text{{\large{0}}}_{k_{1}}&\text{{\large{0}}}&\text{{\large{0}}}\\
\displaystyle\text{{\large{0}}}&\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{k_{2}}\text{\large{I}}_{k_{2}}&\text{{\large{0}}}\\
\text{{\large{0}}}&\displaystyle\text{{\large{0}}}&\displaystyle-\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{N-K_{2}}\text{\large{I}}_{N-K_{2}}\end{array}\right),

\dots,\quad H_{j}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\displaystyle\text{{\large{0}}}_{K_{j-1}}&\text{{\large{0}}}&\text{{\large{0}}}\\
\displaystyle\text{{\large{0}}}&\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{k_{j}}\text{\large{I}}_{k_{j}}&\text{{\large{0}}}\\
\text{{\large{0}}}&\displaystyle\text{{\large{0}}}&\displaystyle-\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{N-K_{j}}\text{\large{I}}_{N-K_{j}}\end{array}\right),\quad\dots\quad,

(7)

H_{p-2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\displaystyle\text{{\large{0}}}&\text{{\large{0}}}&\text{{\large{0}}}\\
\displaystyle\text{{\large{0}}}&\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{k_{p-2}}\text{\large{I}}_{k_{p-2}}&\text{{\large{0}}}\\
\text{{\large{0}}}&\displaystyle\text{{\large{0}}}&\displaystyle-\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{N-K_{p-2}}\text{\large{I}}_{N-K_{p-2}}\end{array}\right),

H_{p-1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\displaystyle\text{{\large{0}}}_{K_{p-2}}&\text{{\large{0}}}&\text{{\large{0}}}\\
\displaystyle\text{{\large{0}}}&\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{k_{p-1}}\text{\large{I}}_{k_{p-1}}&\text{{\large{0}}}\\
\text{{\large{0}}}&\displaystyle\text{{\large{0}}}&\displaystyle-\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{N-K_{p-1}}\text{\large{I}}_{N-K_{p-1}}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\displaystyle\text{{\large{0}}}_{K_{p-2}}&\text{{\large{0}}}&\text{{\large{0}}}\\
\displaystyle\text{{\large{0}}}&\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{k_{p-1}}\text{\large{I}}_{k_{p-1}}&\text{{\large{0}}}\\
\text{{\large{0}}}&\displaystyle\text{{\large{0}}}&\displaystyle-\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{k_{p}}\text{\large{I}}_{k_{p}}\end{array}\right),

where
\text{\large{I}}_{k}
and
\text{{\large{0}}}_{k}
denote the identity and zero matrices of size
k
respectively.

Using the orthogonal basis
\{H_{1},H_{2},\dots,H_{p-1}\}
of the center
\mathfrak{c}
, the action

on
\mathfrak{m}=\bigoplus_{1\leq r<s\leq p}\mathfrak{m}_{rs}

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\{X_{\alpha\beta}^{(rs)},Y_{\alpha\beta}^{(rs)}:1\leq\alpha\leq k_{r},1\leq\beta\leq k_{s}\}
be the Weyl basis of

\mathfrak{m}_{rs}
. For a fixed
j

(j=1,\dots,p-1)
, we have:

(1)

For
1\leq r\leq j-1
,

[H_{j},\mathfrak{m}_{rs}]=(0)

,

[H_{j},X_{\alpha\beta}^{(rj)}]=-\frac{1}{k_{j}}Y_{\alpha\beta}^{(rj)}
,

[H_{j},Y_{\alpha\beta}^{(rj)}]=\frac{1}{k_{j}}X_{\alpha\beta}^{(rj)}
,

,

[H_{j},Y_{\alpha\beta}^{(rs)}]=-\frac{1}{N-K_{j}}X_{\alpha\beta}^{(rs)}
for

.

(2)

For

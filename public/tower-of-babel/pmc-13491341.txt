Percutaneous Stenting of Interarterial Left Coronary Artery Followed by Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement
Authors: Cheuk Bong Ho, Shing Fung Chui, Alan Ka Chun Chan, Michael Kang Yin Lee, Chi Yuen Wong
Published: 2026

A 70-year-old woman presented with non-ST elevation myocardial infarction and severe aortic stenosis. She was in cardiogenic shock requiring inotropic support and mechanical ventilation. Echocardiogram showed left ventricular ejection fraction of 30% to 35% with hypokinesia over the anteroseptal region, critical aortic stenosis with mean gradient 71 mm Hg, and aortic valve area of 0.3 cm2. Coronary angiogram revealed diffuse stenosis of the right coronary artery (RCA). The left anterior descending (LAD) artery originated from the right cusp next to the RCA with an interarterial course. There was significant disease from proximal to distal LAD. Computed tomography (CT) showed low risk of coronary obstruction (Figure 1A). After the heart team discussion, percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) and transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) were suggested in view of high surgical risk. In view of the expected difficulty in coronary engagement, PCI was performed first and followed by TAVR in the same setting.

All-in-one: percutaneous stenting of an anomalous left coronary artery followed by transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR). (A) Baseline CT and angiogram showing low risk of coronary obstruction and interarterial course of the LAD arising next to the RCA. LAO, left anterior oblique; RAO, right anterior oblique. (B) IVUS during diastole and systole at the proximal LAD showing external compression by the pulmonary artery (∗) at positions I and II. (C) Final IVUS and angiography after stenting and TAVR. CT, computed tomography; IVUS, intravascular ultrasound; LAD, left anterior descending; RCA, right coronary artery.

PCI was performed via right radial approach. After successful stenting of the RCA, the anomalous LAD was engaged with a 6F Ikari Left 3.5 guiding catheter (Terumo). Intravascular ultrasound (IVUS) confirmed significant atherosclerotic disease over the proximal LAD, together with external compression from the pulmonary artery (Figure 1B). A 4.0 × 38 mm Megatron (Boston Scientific) was implanted at the proximal LAD. Post-IVUS confirmed luminal stabilization and resolution of the dynamic obstruction at the proximal LAD. Subsequently, TAVI was successfully performed using a 25 mm Navitor (Abbott) in the same procedure (Figure 1C), resulting in a mean gradient of 5 mm Hg. Patient recovered well afterwards and remained symptom-free at 2-year follow-up. The latest echocardiography showed stable valve haemodynamics.

Hereby, we present a case of interarterial left coronary artery and severe aortic stenosis treated percutaneously by PCI and TAVR. PCI in the interarterial coronary arteries had been reported, but stenting at the site of dynamic obstruction was rarely described.1 The long-term patency of the stent is also unknown, with the risk of stent fracture from external compression. Although surgery is the mainstay of treatment in these rare circumstances, PCI together with TAVR is a viable treatment option in high surgical risk patients.2

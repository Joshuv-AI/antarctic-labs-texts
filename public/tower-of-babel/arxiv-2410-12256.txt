Voter Participation Control in Online Polls
Koustav De email: koustavde7@kgpian.iitkgp.ac.in, Palash Dey email: palash.dey@cse.iitkgp.ac.in, Swagato Sanyal email: swagato@cse.iitkgp.ac.in

Abstract
News outlets, surveyors, and other organizations often conduct polls on social networks to gain insights into public opinion. Such a poll is typically started by someone on a social network who sends it to her friends. If a person participates in the poll, the poll information gets published on her wall, which in turn enables her friends to participate, and the process continues. Eventually, a subset of the population participates in the poll, and the pollster learns the outcome of that poll. We initiate the study of a new but natural type of election control in such online elections. We study how difficult/easy it is to sway the outcome of such polls in one’s favor/against (aka constructive vs destructive) by any malicious influencer who nudges/bribes people for seemingly harmless actions like non-participation. These questions are important from the standpoint of studying the power of resistance of online voting against malicious behavior. The destructive version is also important to quantify the robustness of the winner of an online voting. We show that both problems are computationally intractable even if the election is over only two candidates and the influencer has an infinite amount of money to spend (that is, every voter can be persuaded to not participate). We strengthen this result by proving that the computational task remains substantially challenging even if the underlying network is a tree. Finally, we show that there is a polynomial-time algorithm for the constructive version of the problem when we have 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 ) \mathcal{O}(1) candidates, and the treewidth of the underlying graph is 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 ) \mathcal{O}(1) ; the algorithm for the destructive version does not even need to assume 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 ) \mathcal{O}(1) number of candidates. Hence, we observe that the destructive version is computationally easier than the constructive version.



Keywords—
Voting theory, social choice, plurality voting rule, graph algorithms, NP-hardness, bounded treewidth graphs, parameterized algorithms.

Introduction

Voting is arguably the most widely used tool when a set of people needs to decide on one alternative/candidate. It also has widespread applications in AI also
[
24
,
19
]
. The study of various election malpractices and their computational complexity has been one of the core research focus in computational social choice
[
5
,
1
]
. Election Control is one of the most well-studied malpractice where someone tries to change the structure of the election, for example, the set of voters, the set of candidates, etc. Indeed, in many voting scenarios, the key personalities of an institution often have the power to decide the committee members, i.e., the voters, who will eventually make some crucial decisions. Another type of election control that is often observed in political elections is adding some “spoiler” candidates to create confusion among voters or persuade some candidates to withdraw who are poorly performing in pre-election polls.

This work focuses on online voting. In this setting, there is a social network on the set of voters. A voter initiates an election (online poll or survey, for example) and asks her friends to participate in it. Depending on the social network platform, there could be various ways to do this job. For example, on a Facebook network, a voter can post a poll on her wall that, when her friends see it, participate in it. When their friends participate in that poll, their friends will see it and can participate in it. If everyone who sees the poll participates in it, then, if the social network is connected, then everyone in the network participates in it. However, this is rarely the case because some people may not participate in the poll even after seeing it. Hence, only a connected subset of people, including the poll initiator, participates in that poll. Since the outcome of such polls can be used for important decision-making tasks, it may be possible that such elections come under attack. We study an important type of attack on such elections: persuading some voters not to participate in the election, thereby controlling the set of voters that eventually participate in the election. Such a control attack can be done for various purposes, for example, confusing the pollster, helping some candidate to win/lose the election, etc. In this paper, we focus on the objective of helping some candidate win/lose the election under a complexity-theoretic lens.

Preliminaries and problem definitions

We consider the plurality voting rule in this paper. For an integer
g
, we denote the set
\{1,\ldots,g\}
by
\left[g\right]
and
\left[g\right]_{0}=\left[g\right]\cup\{0\}
. Let
\mathcal{C}=\{c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}\}
be a set of candidates and
\mathcal{V}=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
be a set of voters. Each voter
v_{i}
votes for a candidate in
\mathcal{C}
that is given by a voting function
\tau:\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{C}
. Thus, the voter
v\in\mathcal{V}
votes for the candidate
\tau(v)\in\mathcal{C}
. The triple
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{V},\tau)
is called an election. A candidate
c\in\mathcal{C}
is declared a winner of the election if
c\in\arg\max_{c^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}}|\{v\in\mathcal{V}\mid\tau(v)=c^{\prime}\}|
. If there is only one winner
c
of an election, then
c
is said to win the election uniquely. The mathematical formalism to express an election for a general voting rule is more elaborate; we do not include that in this paper.

We consider two problems where the election controller wants to control the winner of a given election, by deleting some votes within her budget limit. In the
Constructive Control over Network
problem, the election controller wishes to make her preferred candidate unambiguously win the election. In the
Destructive Control over Network
problem, the election controller wishes to stop a specific candidate to win the election. The problems are formally defined in
Problem Definition

1
.

Problem Definition 1

(
Constructive and Destructive Control over Network)
.

We are given a set
\mathcal{C}=\{c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}\}
of
m
candidates, a set
\mathcal{V}=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
of
n
voters, a voting function
\tau:\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{C}
, an undirected graph
\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
whose vertices are the voters, a target candidate
c\in\mathcal{C}
of the controller, a voter
x\in\mathcal{V}
who conducts the election, a cost function
\pi:\mathcal{V}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, and a budget
\mathcal{B}
of the controller. We extend the definition of
\pi
to subsets of
\mathcal{V}
, and define the cost
\pi(\mathcal{K})
of a subset
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{V}
as
\sum_{v\in\mathcal{K}}\pi(v)
. We say that a subset
\mathcal{W}\subseteq\mathcal{V}\setminus\{x\}
is “budget feasible” if
\sum_{v\in\mathcal{W}}\pi(v)\leq\mathcal{B}
. For a budget feasible set
\mathcal{W}
, let
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{W}}
be the set of nodes reachable from

in
\mathcal{G}\setminus\mathcal{W}
.

The
Constructive Control over Network

\mathcal{W}
such that

wins uniquely in the restricted election
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{V},\tau)
where only votes of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{W}}
are counted.

The
Destructive Control over Network
problem asks whether there exists a budget feasible set
\mathcal{W}
such that a candidate other than
c
unambiguously wins in the restricted election
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{V},\tau)
where only votes of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{W}}
are counted.

A generic input to both
Constructive Control over Network
and
Destructive Control over Network
is a tuple
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{V},\tau,\mathcal{G},c,x,\pi,\mathcal{B})
.

We also consider the special setting where the budget is infinite. In this setting, the cost function
\pi
is irrelevant, and any subset
\mathcal{W}\subseteq\mathcal{V}\setminus\{x\}
trivially satisfies the budget constraint. We refer to the corresponding versions as
Budgetless Constructive Control over Network
and
Budgetless Destructive Control over Network
. An instance of the budgetless versions is a tuple
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{V},\tau,\mathcal{G},c,x)
, where the members of the tuple are as per

1
.

Observe that an efficient algorithm
\mathcal{A}
for
Constructive Control over Network
can be used to design an efficient algorithm for
Destructive Control over Network
as follows: examine each candidate
c^{\prime}
other than
c
in turn, and decide whether
c^{\prime}
can be made a unique winner within the given budget by invoking

.

3.1
Tree decomposition and treewidth

In this section, we formally define tree decomposition and treewidth. See
[
8
]
for more details.

Definition 2

(Treewidth)
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(V_{\mathcal{G}},E_{\mathcal{G}})
be a graph. A tree-decomposition of
\mathcal{G}
is a pair
(T=(V_{T},E_{T}),\mathcal{X}=\{X_{t}\}_{t\in V_{T}})
, where
T
is a tree where every node
t\in V_{T}
is assigned a subset
X_{t}\subseteq V_{\mathcal{G}}
, called a bag, such that the following conditions hold.

•

\bigcup_{t\in V_{T}}{X_{t}}=V_{\mathcal{G}}
,

•

for every edge
\{x,y\}\in E_{\mathcal{G}}
there is a
t\in V_{T}
such that
x,y\in X_{t}
, and

•

for any
v\in V_{\mathcal{G}}
the subgraph of
T
induced by the set
\{t\mid v\in X_{t}\}
is connected.

The width of a tree decomposition is
\max_{t\in V_{T}}|X_{t}|-1
. The treewidth of
\mathcal{G}
, denoted by
\mathfrak{t}\mathfrak{w}(\mathcal{G})
, is the minimum width over all tree decompositions of
{\mathcal{G}}
.

A tree decomposition
(T,\mathcal{X})
is called a nice edge tree decomposition (we will also refer to it as
nice tree decomposition
) if
T
is a tree rooted at some node
r
where
X_{r}=\emptyset
, each node of
T
has at most two children, and each node is of one of the following kinds:

•

Introduce node
: a node
t
that has only one child
t^{\prime}
where
X_{t}\supset X_{t^{\prime}}
and
|X_{t}|=|X_{t^{\prime}}|+1
.

•

Introduce edge node
: a node
t
labeled with an edge
\{u,v\}\in E_{\mathcal{G}}
, with only one child
t^{\prime}
such that
\{u,v\}\subseteq X_{t^{\prime}}=X_{t}
. This bag is said to introduce the edge
\{u,v\}
.

•

Forget vertex node
: a node
t
that has only one child
t^{\prime}
where
X_{t}\subset X_{t^{\prime}}
and
|X_{t}|=|X_{t^{\prime}}|-1
.

•

Join node
: a node
t
with two children
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
such that
X_{t}=X_{t_{1}}=X_{t_{2}}
.

•

Leaf node
: a node
t
that is a leaf of
T
, and
X_{t}=\emptyset
.

We additionally require that every edge is introduced exactly once. One can show that a tree decomposition of width
w
can be transformed into a nice tree decomposition of the same width
w
and with
\mathcal{O}(w|V_{\mathcal{G}}|)
nodes in polynomial time, see e.g.
[
4
,
8
]
.

Suppose
T^{\prime}
is a nice tree decomposition of
\mathcal{G}
of width
w
, and
x\in V_{\mathcal{G}}
be a vertex of
\mathcal{G}
. One can easily convert
T^{\prime}
to another tree decomposition
T
of
\mathcal{G}
of width at most
w+1
with the following properties:

•

The parent of each leaf of
T
is an Introduce node where the vertex
x
is introduced.

•

The vertex
x
is never forgotten. In particular,
x
is part of the bag of each internal node of
T
.

T
can be obtained from
T^{\prime}
as follows:

1.

Include
x
in the bag of each internal node of
T^{\prime}
.

2.

For each leaf
\ell
, if its parent
p
is not an Introduce node where
x
is introduced, then create an Introduce node
t
where
x
is introduced, make
t
the parent of
\ell
and
p
the parent of
t
.

3.

If
t
is a vertex that is not the parent of a leaf, which is either an Introduce node in
T^{\prime}
where
x
is introduced or a Forget vertex node where
x
is forgotten, then merge it with its child.

Assuming that there is no join node with an empty bag in
T^{\prime}
, it can be easily verified that each node of
T
is of one of the four kinds listed in the definition of a nice tree decomposition. The only condition that
T
violates in the definition of a nice tree decomposition is that the bag of the root is empty. Nevertheless, we abuse notation and also refer to
T
as a nice tree decomposition of
\mathcal{G}
.

Our Contribution

In this work, we study the computational complexity of
Constructive Control over Network
and
Destructive Control over Network
.

Our first result shows that
Destructive Control over Network
admits a polynomial time algorithm for the special case where the treewidth (see
Section

3.1
for related definitions) of the graph
\mathcal{G}
is a constant.

Theorem 1
.

There exists an algorithm that, given an input
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{V},\tau,\mathcal{G},c,x,\pi,\mathcal{B})
of
Destructive Control over Network
and a tree decomposition
T
of the graph
\mathcal{G}
of width
w
, solves
Destructive Control over Network
in time
w^{\mathcal{O}(w)}\cdot n^{\mathcal{O}(w)}\cdot\mathsf{poly}(n,m)
. In particular,
Destructive Control over Network
admits a polynomial time algorithm when
\mathcal{G}
is a tree.

How about
Constructive Control over Network
? As discussed in
Section

3
,
Constructive Control over Network
is computationally at least as hard as
Destructive Control over Network
(up to a factor of
m
). Our next result obtains a polynomial time algorithm for
Constructive Control over Network
with an assumption of the treewidth of
\mathcal{G}
being a constant as in
Theorem

1
, and an additional assumption that the number of candidates
m
is a constant.

Theorem 2
.

There exists an algorithm that, given an input
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{V},\tau,\mathcal{G},c,x,\pi,\mathcal{B})
of
Constructive Control over Network
and a tree decomposition
T
of the graph
\mathcal{G}
of width
w
, solves
Constructive Control over Network
in time
w^{\mathcal{O}(w)}\cdot n^{\mathcal{O}(mw)}\cdot\mathsf{poly}(n,m)
. In particular,
Constructive Control over Network
admits a polynomial time algorithm when
\mathcal{G}
is a tree, and the number of candidates
m
is a constant.

Theorem

1
and
Theorem

2
assume that a tree decomposition of low treewidth is given as a part of the input. However, this assumption is not restrictive; it is known that given a graph
\mathcal{G}
with
n
vertices it is possible to construct a tree decomposition of
\mathcal{G}
of width
\mathfrak{t}\mathfrak{w}(\mathcal{G})
in time
\mathcal{O}(f(\mathfrak{t}\mathfrak{w}(\mathcal{G}))\cdot n)
, where
f(\cdot)
is a quasi-polynomially growing function
[
3
,
8
]
.

Are the assumptions in
Theorem

1
and
Theorem

2
necessary? We answer this question in the affirmative by two hardness results. The first one shows that
Constructive Control over Network
is NP-complete even when the graph
\mathcal{G}
is a tree (i.e. has treewidth
1
) and the setting is budgetless.

Theorem 3
.

Budgetless Constructive Control over Network
is NP-complete for the special case where the graph
\mathcal{G}
is a tree.

Our next result shows that both the problems are NP-complete even in the special case where there are two candidates and the setting is budgetless.

Theorem 4
.

Budgetless Constructive Control over Network
and
Budgetless Destructive Control over Network
are NP-complete even for the special case where there are two candidates (i.e.
m=2
).

Observe that for
m=2
the two problems are equivalent in the following sense. Let
\mathcal{C}=\{0,1\}
. Then for each
b\in\mathcal{C}
,
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{V},\tau,\mathcal{G},b,x,\pi,\mathcal{B})
is a
YES
instance of
Constructive Control over Network

if and only if

(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{V},\tau,\mathcal{G},1-b,x,\pi,\mathcal{B})
is a
YES
instance of
Destructive Control over Network
. Thus, in order to prove
Theorem

4
it suffices to prove the NP-hardness of
Constructive Control over Network
for
m=2
in the budgetless setting.

Our hardness results presented in
Theorem

3
and
Theorem

4
thus complement our algorithmic results presented in
Theorem

1
and
Theorem

2
, and establish the unavoidability of the assumptions made in them. This paper presents a comprehensive study of the computational complexity of
Constructive Control over Network
and
Destructive Control over Network
. In
Section

9
we state some open questions for future research.

Algorithm for
Constructive Control over Network

In this section, we prove
Theorem

2
. Our proof exhibits an explicit algorithm for
Constructive Control over Network
based on dynamic programming. We assume that the tree decomposition
T
of
\mathcal{G}
supplied as input is
nice
(see
Section

3.1
). Our DP table has a set of cells for each node
t
of
T
. Each cell is additionally indexed by a partition
\mathcal{P}=(\mathcal{P}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{P}_{\ell})
of a subset of the bag
X_{t}
of
t
, and a tuple of numbers
\overline{n}=(n_{i,j})_{i\in[\ell],j\in[m]}
. The vertices and edges of
\mathcal{G}
, introduced in the subtree of
T
rooted at
t
, induce a subgraph
G_{t}
of
\mathcal{G}
. In each step, the algorithm computes a cheapest set of vertices of
G_{t}
. Removal of such a cheapest set of vertices from
G_{t}
breaks up
G_{t}
into connected components whose intersections with the bag
X_{t}
are given by
\mathcal{P}
, and whose vote counts for each candidate are given by
\overline{n}
. Once the table is filled up, the table entries for the root of
T
can be consulted to compute the output of the algorithm. We now proceed to the formal proof.

Proof of
Theorem

2
.

Let
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{V},\tau,\mathcal{G},c,x,\pi,\mathcal{B})
be an instance of
Constructive Control over Network
. Let
x
vote for the candidate
c^{\prime}
, i.e.,
\tau(x)=c^{\prime}
. Let
T
be a nice tree decomposition of
\mathcal{G}
of width
w
. Let
r
be the root of
T
. We assume that the parent of each leaf is an
Introduce node
where the vertex
x
is introduced and that
x
is never forgotten. In particular,
x
is in the bag of the root of
T
. See the related discussion in
Section

3
.
Consider a vertex
t\in V_{T}
. Let
V_{t}
be the union of the bags of all vertices in the subtree of
T
rooted at
t
. Let
E_{t}
be the set of all edges introduced in the subtree of
T
rooted at
t
. Define the graph
G_{t}\coloneqq(V_{t},E_{t})
.

Consider deleting a subset
\mathcal{W}\subseteq V_{t}\setminus\{x\}
of vertices from

. The resulting graph, which we will denote by

, will have its vertex set partitioned into connected components. Let
\ell
of those components, denoted by

of

\mathcal{P}^{(\mathcal{W},t)}_{i}\coloneqq\mathcal{Y}^{(\mathcal{W},t)}_{i}\cap X_{t}

\mathcal{P}^{(\mathcal{W},t)}\coloneqq(\mathcal{P}^{(\mathcal{W},t)}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{P}^{(\mathcal{W},t)}_{\ell})

. Each entry is indexed by a tuple
(t,\mathcal{P},\overline{n})
, whose components are described below.

•

t\in V_{T}
is a vertex of

.

•

\mathcal{P}=(\mathcal{P}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{P}_{\ell})

. If

, then

If

is a tuple
(n_{i,j})_{i\in[\ell],j\in[m]}

\mathcal{P}^{(\mathcal{W},t)}=\mathcal{P}
, and

The number of entries in our table is at most

Initialization

Let

Populating the table

We inductively assume that while filling up

, where
t^{\prime}
is a vertex in the subtree of
T
rooted at
t
, are filled. The update rule of our dynamic programming algorithm depends on the kind of vertex
t
is. For the rest of this section, we denote a generic partition by
\mathcal{P}=\{\mathcal{P}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{P}_{\ell}\}
and a generic tuple by
\overline{n}=(n_{i,j})_{i\in[\ell],j\in[m]}
; if
\ell=0
,
\mathcal{P}
and
\overline{n}
will be understood to be
\bot
and
\epsilon
respectively.

Introduce node.

Let
t
be an Introduce node, and let vertex
v\in\mathcal{V}
of
\mathcal{G}
be introduced at
t
. Thus,
t
has only one child
t^{\prime}
,
X_{t}=X_{t^{\prime}}\cup\{v\}
,
v\notin V_{t^{\prime}}
,
E_{t}=E_{t^{\prime}}
and
v
is an isolated vertex in
G_{t}
.

Fix a partition
\mathcal{P}=\{\mathcal{P}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{P}_{\ell}\}
of a subset of
X_{t}
and a tuple
\overline{n}=(n_{i,j})_{i\in[\ell],j\in[m]}
. We consider the following cases, depending on
\mathcal{P}
and
\overline{n}
.

1.

Suppose that there does not exist any part of
\mathcal{P}
that contains
v
. Then
\mathcal{W}\subseteq V_{t}\setminus\{x\}
induces
(\mathcal{P},\overline{n})
on
X_{t}

if and only if

v\in\mathcal{W}
and
\mathcal{W}\setminus\{v\}
induces
(\mathcal{P},\overline{n})
on
X_{t^{\prime}}
. We update

\mathsf{DP}[t,\mathcal{P},\overline{n}]\leftarrow\mathsf{DP}[t^{\prime},\mathcal{P},\overline{n}]+\pi(v).

Note that the case
\mathcal{P}=\bot
and
\overline{n}=\epsilon
falls here.

2.

Let
\mathcal{P}
and
\overline{n}
satisfy both of the following two conditions:

(a)

One of the parts of
\mathcal{P}
is
\{v\}
. WLOG assume that
\mathcal{P}_{\ell}=\{v\}
.

(b)

For
j\in[m]
,
n_{\ell,j}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\mbox{if $\tau(v)=j$,}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Let
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}=\{\mathcal{P}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{P}_{\ell-1}\}
. Let
\overline{n}^{\prime}\coloneqq(n_{i,j})_{i\in[\ell-1],j\in[m]}
. Then
\mathcal{W}\subseteq V_{t}
induces
(\mathcal{P},\overline{n})
on
X_{t}

if and only if

v\notin\mathcal{W}
and
\mathcal{W}
induces
(\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\overline{n}^{\prime})
on
X_{t^{\prime}}
. We update

\mathsf{DP}[t,\mathcal{P},\overline{n}]\leftarrow\mathsf{DP}[t^{\prime},\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\overline{n}^{\prime}].

3.

If none of the previous two cases is true, then no
\mathcal{W}\subseteq V_{t}
can induce
(\mathcal{P},\overline{n})
on
X_{t}
. We update
\mathsf{DP}[t,\mathcal{P},\overline{n}]\leftarrow\infty.

The updation time is
\mathsf{poly}(n,m)
.

Introduce edge node.

Let
t
be an Introduce edge node. Thus,
t
has only one child
t^{\prime}
, and
X_{t}=X_{t^{\prime}}
. Let an edge
\{u,v\}
, for
u,v\in X_{t^{\prime}}
, be introduced at
t
.

Fix a partition
\mathcal{P}=\{\mathcal{P}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{P}_{\ell}\}
of a subset of
X_{t}
and a tuple
\overline{n}=(n_{i,j})_{i\in[\ell],j\in[m]}
. Now, for a partition
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}=(\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{\ell^{\prime}})
of a subset
S
of
X_{t^{\prime}}=X_{t}
and a tuple
\overline{n}^{\prime}=(n^{\prime}_{i,j})_{i\in[\ell^{\prime}],j\in[m]}
, we define a partition
\lambda(\mathcal{P}^{\prime})
of
S
and a tuple
\sigma(\overline{n}^{\prime})
as follows:

•

If
u,v
belong to
S
, and they belong to two different parts
\mathcal{P}_{i_{1}}
and
\mathcal{P}_{i_{2}}
of
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
, replace those two parts by their union. Define
\lambda(\mathcal{P}^{\prime})
to be the resulting partition. Obtain
\sigma(\overline{n}^{\prime})
from
\overline{n}^{\prime}
by replacing the entries
n^{\prime}_{i_{1},j}
and
n^{\prime}_{i_{2},j}
by their sum, for each
j\in[m]
.

•

Otherwise define
\lambda(\mathcal{P}^{\prime})\coloneqq\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
and
\sigma(\overline{n}^{\prime})\coloneqq\overline{n}^{\prime}
.

Note that
\mathcal{W}\subseteq V_{t}
induces
(\mathcal{P},\overline{n})
on
(X_{t},G_{t})
if and only if
\mathcal{W}
induces
(\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\overline{n}^{\prime})
on
(X_{t^{\prime}},G_{t^{\prime}})
for some
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
and
\overline{n}^{\prime}
such that
\lambda(\mathcal{P}^{\prime})=\mathcal{P}
and
\sigma(\overline{n}^{\prime})=\overline{n}
. This leads us to our update rule:

\mathsf{DP}[t,\mathcal{P},\overline{n}]\leftarrow\min_{\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\overline{n}^{\prime}}\mathsf{DP}[t^{\prime},\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\overline{n}^{\prime}],

where the minimum is over all pairs
(\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\overline{n}^{\prime})
such that
\lambda(\mathcal{P}^{\prime})=\mathcal{P}
and
\sigma(\overline{n}^{\prime})=\overline{n}
. The updation takes time
w^{\mathcal{O}(w)}\cdot n^{\mathcal{O}(mw)}\cdot\mathsf{poly}(n,m)
.

Forget vertex node.

Let
t
be a Forget vertex node, and let vertex
v\in\mathcal{V}
of
\mathcal{G}
be forgotten at
t
. Thus,
t
has only one child
t^{\prime}
,
X_{t}=X_{t^{\prime}}\setminus\{v\}
and
v\in X_{t^{\prime}}
. However, note that
G_{t}
and
G_{t^{\prime}}
are the same graph.

Fix a partition
\mathcal{P}=\{\mathcal{P}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{P}_{\ell}\}
of a subset of
X_{t}
and a tuple
\overline{n}=(n_{i,j})_{i\in[\ell],j\in[m]}
. Let
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}=\{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{\ell^{\prime}}\}
be a partition of a subset of
X_{t^{\prime}}
. Let
\overline{n}^{\prime}
be a tuple
(n^{\prime}_{i,j})_{i\in[\ell^{\prime}],j\in[m]}
. We say that the pair
(\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\overline{n}^{\prime})
is
consistent with
the pair
(\mathcal{P},\overline{n})
if one of the following is true:

1.

\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
and
\overline{n}=\overline{n}^{\prime}
.

2.

\ell=\ell^{\prime}
and there exists an
i\in[\ell]
such that both of the following are true

(a)

\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{i}=\mathcal{P}_{i}\cup\{v\}
and for each
j\in[m]
,
n^{\prime}_{i,j}=n_{i,j}

(b)

\forall k\in[\ell]\setminus\{i\}
,
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{k}=\mathcal{P}_{k}

and for all
j\in[m]
,
n^{\prime}_{k,j}=n_{k,j}
.

3.

\ell+1=\ell^{\prime}
,
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}=\mathcal{P}\cup\{\{v\}\}
. WLOG, assume that
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{\ell^{\prime}}=\{v\}
. Furthermore, for all
i\in[\ell],j\in[m]
,
n^{\prime}_{i,j}=n_{i,j}
.

We now argue that
\mathcal{W}\subseteq V_{t}
induces
(\mathcal{P},\overline{n})
on
X_{t}
,
if and only if

\mathcal{W}
induces some
(\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\overline{n}^{\prime})
consistent with
(\mathcal{P},\overline{n})
on
X_{t^{\prime}}
. To see this, recall that the graphs
G_{t}
and
G_{t^{\prime}}
are the same. Thus, the connected components
\{\mathcal{Y}^{\mathcal{W},t}_{i}\}_{i}
and
\{\mathcal{Y}^{\mathcal{W},t^{\prime}}_{i}\}_{i}
are the same. Now
X_{t^{\prime}}=X_{t}\cup\{v\}
. Thus for each
i
, one of the following is true: 1.
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{i}=\mathcal{Y}^{\mathcal{W},t^{\prime}}_{i}\cap X_{t^{\prime}}=\{v\}
and
\mathcal{Y}^{\mathcal{W},t}_{i}\cap X_{t}=\emptyset
, 2.
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{i}=\mathcal{P}_{i}
, 3.
v\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{i}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{i}\setminus\{v\}=\mathcal{P}_{i}
. Furthermore, (2) holds for all but at most one
i
. It can be verified that the tuples
\overline{n}
and
\overline{n}^{\prime}
must be related so as to ensure that the pairs
(\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\overline{n}^{\prime})
and
(\mathcal{P},\overline{n})
are consistent. This brings us to our update rule:

\mathsf{DP}[t,\mathcal{P},\overline{n}]\leftarrow\min_{(\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\overline{n}^{\prime})\text{ consistent with }(\mathcal{P},\overline{n})}\mathsf{DP}[t^{\prime},\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\overline{n}^{\prime}].

The updation takes time
w^{\mathcal{O}(w)}\cdot n^{\mathcal{O}(mw)}\cdot\mathsf{poly}(n,m)
.

Join node.

Let
t
be a Join node. Thus, it has two children
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
. Furthermore,
X_{t}=X_{t_{1}}=X_{t_{2}}
.

It follows from the definition of join node that
X_{t}=X_{t_{1}}=X_{t_{2}}\subseteq V_{t_{1}}\cap V_{t_{2}}
. Can there be a vertex
v
in
V_{t_{1}}\cap V_{t_{2}}
that is not in
X_{t}
? It is easy to see that the answer is ‘no’, as otherwise the subgraph of
T
induced by the set
\{t\mid v\in X_{t}\}
will not be connected. We conclude that
V_{t_{1}}\cap V_{t_{2}}=X_{t}
.

Fix a partition
\mathcal{P}=(\mathcal{P}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{P}_{\ell})
of a subset of
X_{t}
, and a tuple
\overline{n}=(n_{i,j})_{i\in[\ell],j\in[m]}
.

Suppose
\mathcal{W}\subseteq V_{t}
induces
(\mathcal{P},\overline{n})
on
(X_{t},G_{t})
. Express
\mathcal{W}
as the disjoint union

\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{W}_{b}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{1}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{2},\mbox{ where}

•

\mathcal{W}_{b}=\mathcal{W}\cap X_{t}
,

•

\mathcal{W}_{1}=(\mathcal{W}\setminus\mathcal{W}_{b})\cap V_{t_{1}}
, and

•

\mathcal{W}_{2}=(\mathcal{W}\setminus\mathcal{W}_{b})\cap V_{t_{2}}
.

Let
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime}
be the partitions of subsets of
X_{t}
(which, recall, is the same as
X_{t_{1}}
and
X_{t_{2}}
), and
\overline{\mu},\overline{\nu}
be vectors of numbers such that
\mathcal{W}_{b}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{1}
induces
(\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\overline{\mu})
on
(X_{t_{1}},G_{t_{1}})
, and
\mathcal{W}_{b}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{2}
induces
(\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime},\overline{\nu})
on
(X_{t_{2}},G_{t_{2}})
. Can we express
\mathcal{P}
and
\overline{n}
in terms of
\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime},\overline{\mu}
and
\overline{\nu}
? In other words, are
\mathcal{P}
and
\overline{n}
determined by
\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime},\overline{\mu}
and
\overline{\nu}
? We will answer it in the affirmative. Let us start with
\mathcal{P}
.

Let
u
and
v
be two vertices in
X_{t}\setminus\mathcal{W}_{b}
. We wish to determine whether or not
u
and
v
are in the same part of
\mathcal{P}
.

Assume that there is a path
p
between
u
and
v
in
G_{t}\setminus\mathcal{W}
. Note that the edge set of
G_{t}\setminus\mathcal{W}
is the disjoint union (each edge is introduced exactly once) of the edge sets of
G_{t_{1}}\setminus\left(\mathcal{W}_{b}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{1}\right)
and
G_{t_{2}}\setminus\left(\mathcal{W}_{b}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{2}\right)
. Also recall that the only vertices common to
V_{t_{1}}
and
V_{t_{2}}
are the ones in
X_{t}
. Thus
p
is necessarily of the form
p_{1}p_{2}\ldots p_{t}
, where each
p_{i}
is a path between two vertices in
X_{t}\setminus\mathcal{W}_{b}
all of whose edges are either in
G_{t_{1}}\setminus\left(\mathcal{W}_{b}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{1}\right)
or in
G_{t_{2}}\setminus\left(\mathcal{W}_{b}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{2}\right)
.

To capture this, we define a graph
H=(X_{t}\setminus\mathcal{W}_{b},E)
as follows: for each
u,v\in X_{t}\setminus\mathcal{W}_{b}
, the pair
\{u,v\}
is an edge in
E
if
u
and
v
are in the same part in
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
or in
\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime}
. It follows that there exists path
p=p_{1}p_{2}\ldots p_{t}
as stated before if and only if
u
and
v
are connected in the above graph. We have the following procedure to determine
\mathcal{P}
from
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime}
:

1.

Build the graph
H
from
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime}
.

2.

Return the connected components of
H
as the parts
\mathcal{P}_{i}
s of
\mathcal{P}
.

The procedure above can be implemented in time polynomial in
|X_{t}|\leq w
.

Now, let us express
\overline{n}
in terms of
\overline{\mu},\overline{\nu},\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime}
. To this end, let

•

\mathcal{Y}^{(\mathcal{W},t)}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{Y}^{(\mathcal{W},t)}_{\ell}
be the connected components of
G_{t}\setminus\mathcal{W}
with non-empty intersection with
X_{t}
,

•

\mathcal{Y}^{(\mathcal{W}_{1},t_{1})}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{Y}^{(\mathcal{W}_{1},t_{1})}_{\ell_{1}}
be the connected components of
G_{t_{1}}\setminus\left(\mathcal{W}_{b}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{1}\right)
with non-empty intersection with
X_{t_{1}}=X_{t}
, and

•

\mathcal{Y}^{(\mathcal{W}_{2},t_{2})}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{Y}^{(\mathcal{W}_{2},t_{2})}_{\ell_{2}}
be the connected components of
G_{t_{2}}\setminus\left(\mathcal{W}_{b}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{2}\right)
with non-empty intersection with
X_{t_{2}}=X_{t}
.

It is easy to see that if two vertices are connected in
G_{t_{1}}\setminus\left(\mathcal{W}_{b}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{1}\right)
(resp.
G_{t_{2}}\setminus\left(\mathcal{W}_{b}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{2}\right)
), then they are connected in
G_{t}\setminus\mathcal{W}
as well. This lets us infer the following:

(
\alpha
)

Each connected component of
G_{t}\setminus\mathcal{W}
is the union of a set (possibly empty) of connected components of
G_{t_{1}}\setminus\left(\mathcal{W}_{b}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{1}\right)
and a set (possibly empty) of connected components of
G_{t_{2}}\setminus\left(\mathcal{W}_{b}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{2}\right)
.

(
\beta
)

Each part of
\mathcal{P}
is the union of a set of parts of
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
(resp.
\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime}
). Put another way,
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime}
are refinements of
\mathcal{P}
.

(
\gamma
)

Each
\mathcal{Y}^{(\mathcal{W}_{1},t_{1})}_{i}
(resp.
\mathcal{Y}^{(\mathcal{W}_{2},t_{2})}_{i})
is completely contained in some
\mathcal{Y}^{(\mathcal{W},t)}_{j}
.

From the above, we infer the following. Suppose
\mathcal{P}_{i}=\cup_{j\in S_{1}}(\mathcal{P}^{\prime})_{j}=\cup_{k\in S_{2}}(\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime})_{k}
. Then,
\bigcup_{j\in S_{1}}\mathcal{Y}^{(\mathcal{W}_{1},t_{1})}_{j}\bigcup_{k\in S_{2}}\mathcal{Y}^{(\mathcal{W}_{2},t_{2})}_{k}\subseteq\mathcal{Y}^{(\mathcal{W},t)}_{i}
.

Can
\bigcup_{j\in S_{1}}\mathcal{Y}^{(\mathcal{W}_{1},t_{1})}_{j}\bigcup_{k\in S_{2}}\mathcal{Y}^{(\mathcal{W}_{2},t_{2})}_{k}
be a proper subset of
\mathcal{Y}^{(\mathcal{W},t)}_{i}
? In view of the point (
\alpha
) above, the question boils down to the question whether there can exist a connected component of
G_{t_{1}}\setminus\left(\mathcal{W}_{b}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{1}\right)
or
G_{t_{2}}\setminus\left(\mathcal{W}_{b}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{2}\right)
, which (i) has empty intersection with
X_{t}
, and (ii) is completely contained in
\mathcal{Y}^{(\mathcal{W},t)}_{i}
.

We claim that the answer to the above question is ‘no.’ To prove the claim, towards a contradiction, assume that there exists a connected component
K
of
G_{t_{1}}\setminus\left(\mathcal{W}_{b}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{1}\right)
that satisfies the conditions (i) and (ii) above. The case of there being such a connected component of
G_{t_{2}}\setminus\left(\mathcal{W}_{b}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{2}\right)
is similar.

Since
K\subseteq\mathcal{Y}^{(\mathcal{W},t)}_{i}
and
\mathcal{Y}^{(\mathcal{W},t)}_{i}\cap X_{t}\neq\emptyset
, we have that for each vertex
u\in K
there is a path
p
to a vertex
w\in X_{t}
in
G_{t}\setminus\mathcal{W}
. Since
E_{t}
is a disjoint union of
E_{t_{1}}
and
E_{t_{2}}
as noted before, and
V_{t_{1}}\cap V_{t_{2}}=X_{t}
, we have that
p
is of the form
p_{1}\ldots p_{h}
, where each
p_{i}
is a path either in
G_{t_{1}}\setminus\left(\mathcal{W}_{b}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{1}\right)
or in
G_{t_{2}}\setminus\left(\mathcal{W}_{b}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{2}\right)
which ends at a vertex in
X_{t}
. Since
u
is a vertex of
G_{t_{1}}\setminus\left(\mathcal{W}_{b}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{1}\right)
, we have that
p_{1}
is a path in
G_{t_{1}}\setminus\left(\mathcal{W}_{b}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{1}\right)
connecting
u
with a vertex in
X_{t}
, which contradicts the assumption that
K
does not intersect
X_{t}
. This proves the claim. We conclude that
\bigcup_{j\in S_{1}}\mathcal{Y}^{(\mathcal{W}_{1},t_{1})}_{j}\bigcup_{k\in S_{2}}\mathcal{Y}^{(\mathcal{W}_{2},t_{2})}_{k}=\mathcal{Y}^{(\mathcal{W},t)}_{i}
.

Now we are ready to express
\overline{n}
in terms of
\overline{\mu},\overline{\nu},\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime}
. Since
V_{t_{1}}\setminus X_{t}
and
V_{t_{2}}\setminus X_{t}
are disjoint, we have that for each
i\in[\ell],j\in[m]
,
n_{i,j}=\sum_{u\in S_{1}}\mu_{u,j}+\sum_{v\in S_{2}}\nu_{v,j}-N_{i,j}
, where
N_{i,j}
is the number of nodes in
\mathcal{P}_{i}
that votes for
j
.

We have described a procedure
\mathcal{A}
that, given
\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime},\overline{\mu}
and
\overline{\nu}
, produces
\mathcal{P}
and
\overline{n}
in time polynomial in
m
and
n
, with the following property:
\mathcal{W}
induces
(\mathcal{P},\overline{n})
on
(X_{t},G_{t})

if and only if
there exists
\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime},\overline{\mu}
and
\overline{\nu}
such that 1.
\mathcal{W}_{b}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{1}
induces
(\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\overline{\mu})
on
(X_{t_{1}},G_{t_{1}})
, 2.
\mathcal{W}_{b}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{2}
induces
(\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime},\overline{\nu})
on
(X_{t_{2}},G_{t_{2}})
, and 3.
\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime},\overline{\mu},\overline{\nu})=(\mathcal{P},\overline{n})
. Observe that
\pi(\mathcal{W})=\pi(\mathcal{W}_{b}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{1})+\pi(\mathcal{W}_{b}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{2})-\pi(X_{t}\setminus\cup_{i\in[\ell]}\mathcal{P}_{i})
. We thus have the following update rule:

\mathsf{DP}[t,\mathcal{P},\overline{n}]\leftarrow\min_{\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime},\overline{\mu},\overline{\nu}}\mathsf{DP}[t_{1},\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\overline{\mu}]+\mathsf{DP}[t_{2},\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime},\overline{\nu}]-\pi(X_{t}\setminus\cup_{i\in[\ell]}\mathcal{P}_{i}),

where the minimum is over all tuples
(\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime},\overline{\mu},\overline{\nu})
such that
\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime},\overline{\mu},\overline{\nu})=(\mathcal{P},\overline{n})
. Given
(\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\overline{\mu})
and
(\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime},\overline{\nu})
, one can find out
\mathcal{P}
and
\overline{n}
by going over all partitions
\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime}
and vectors
\overline{\mu},\overline{\nu}
; this takes time
w^{\mathcal{O}(w)}\cdot n^{\mathcal{O}(mw)}\cdot\mathsf{poly}(n,m)
.

∎

6
Algorithm for
Destructive Control over Network

In this section, we prove
Theorem

1
. The broad idea is to check for each candidate
c^{\prime}
other than
c
, whether
c^{\prime}
can be made to garner strictly more votes than
c
within the controller’s budget. The aforementioned task for each candidate is accomplished by a dynamic programming algorithm, which closely follows the one for the constructive case (
Section

5
). We assume that the tree decomposition
T
of
\mathcal{G}
supplied as input is
nice
(see
Section

3.1
). Similar to the constructive case, our DP table has a set of cells for each node
t
of
T
. Each cell is additionally indexed by a partition
\mathcal{P}=(\mathcal{P}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{P}_{\ell})
of a subset of the bag
X_{t}
of
t
, and a tuple of numbers
\overline{n}=(n_{i})_{i\in[\ell]}
. The vertices and edges of
\mathcal{G}
, introduced in the subtree of
T
rooted at
t
, induce a subgraph
G_{t}
of
\mathcal{G}
. In each step, the algorithm computes a cheapest set of vertices of
G_{t}
. Removal of such a cheapest set of vertices from
G_{t}
breaks up
G_{t}
into connected components whose intersections with the bag
X_{t}
are given by
\mathcal{P}
, and for each such components, the vote count differences between
c^{\prime}
and
c
are given by
\overline{n}
. Once the table is filled up, the table entries for the root of
T
can be consulted to compute the output of the algorithm. We now proceed to the formal proof.

Proof of
Theorem

1
.

For every candidate
c^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{c\}
we determine whether there is a subset
\mathcal{W}\subseteq\mathcal{V}\setminus\{x\}
of total cost at most
\mathcal{B}
such that
c^{\prime}
garners more votes than
c
in the restricted election where only voters reachable from
x
in
\mathcal{G}\setminus\mathcal{W}
are counted. Let
CanBeat
(
c^{\prime})\in\{\textsf{YES},\textsf{NO}\}
denote whether or not there exists such a
\mathcal{W}
. Our overall algorithm will output
YES
if we find a candidate
c^{\prime}
for which
CanBeat
(
c^{\prime})=\textsf{YES}
, and output
NO
otherwise. Towards this plan, let us now fix a candidate
c^{\prime}\neq c
.

We next propose a dynamic programming algorithm to determine
CanBeat
(
c^{\prime})
.
Without loss of generality, assume
T
to be a nice tree decomposition of
\mathcal{G}
of width
w
. Let
r
be the root of
T
. We assume that the parent of each leaf is an
Introduce node
where the vertex
x
is introduced and that
x
is never forgotten. In particular,
x
is in the bag of the root of
T
. See the related discussion in
Section

3
.
Consider a vertex
t\in V_{T}
. Let
V_{t}
be the union of the bags of all vertices in the subtree of
T
rooted at
t
. Let
E_{t}
be the set of all edges introduced in the subtree of
T
rooted at
t
. Define the graph
G_{t}\coloneqq(V_{t},E_{t})
.

Consider deleting a subset
\mathcal{W}\subseteq V_{t}\setminus\{x\}
of vertices from
G_{t}
. The resulting graph, which we will denote by
G_{t}\setminus\mathcal{W}
, will have its vertex set partitioned into connected components. As in the Constructive case, let
\ell
of those components, denoted by
\mathcal{Y}^{(\mathcal{W},t)}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{Y}^{(\mathcal{W},t)}_{\ell}
, have non-empty intersections with the bag
X_{t}
of
t
. For
i=1,\ldots,\ell
define
\mathcal{P}^{(\mathcal{W},t)}_{i}\coloneqq\mathcal{Y}^{(\mathcal{W},t)}_{i}\cap X_{t}
. Let
\mathcal{P}^{(\mathcal{W},t)}\coloneqq(\mathcal{P}^{(\mathcal{W},t)}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{P}^{(\mathcal{W},t)}_{\ell})
be the partition of
X_{t}\setminus\mathcal{W}
defined by the
\mathcal{P}^{(\mathcal{W},t)}_{i}
s. By definition, each
\mathcal{P}^{(\mathcal{W},t)}_{i}
is non-empty, which implies that
\ell\leq|X_{t}|\leq w+1
.

We now describe the entries of our dynamic programming table,
\mathsf{DP}
. Each entry is indexed by a tuple
(t,\mathcal{P},\overline{n})
, whose components are described below.

•

t\in V_{T}
is a vertex of
T
.

•

\mathcal{P}=(\mathcal{P}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{P}_{\ell})
is a partition of a subset
S
of
X_{t}
into non-empty parts
\mathcal{P}_{i}
. If
S=\emptyset
, then
\mathcal{P}
is the empty partition that we denote by
\bot
.

•

If
S\neq\emptyset
, then
\overline{n}
is a tuple
(n_{i})_{i\in[\ell]}
of
\ell
numbers, where each
n_{i}
is an integer in
[-n,n]
. If
S=\emptyset
, define
\overline{n}
to be the empty sequence that we denote by
\epsilon
.

After our algorithm terminates,
\mathsf{DP}[t,\mathcal{P},\overline{n}]
will contain the minimum cost of any subset
\mathcal{W}\subseteq V_{t}\setminus\{x\}
which satisfies the following conditions:

1.

\mathcal{P}^{(\mathcal{W},t)}=\mathcal{P}
, and

2.

For each
i\in[\ell]
, if
a_{c}
and
a_{c^{\prime}}
be the numbers of votes garnered by
c
and
c^{\prime}
respectively in
\mathcal{Y}^{(\mathcal{W},t)}_{i}
, then
n_{i}=a_{c^{\prime}}-a_{c}
.

We say that a
\mathcal{W}
that satisfies the above two conditions induces the pair
(\mathcal{P},\overline{n})
on
(X_{t},G_{t})
. If there is no such
\mathcal{W}
,
\mathsf{DP}[t,\mathcal{P},\overline{n}]
will be
\infty
.

The number of entries in our table is at most
w^{\mathcal{O}(w)}\cdot n^{\mathcal{O}(w)}
. Once we fill up the table, for each
a>0
we check whether
\mathsf{DP}[r,\{\{x\}\},a]\leq\mathcal{B}
. If we find such an
a
, we return
YES
as the answer to
{\sc CanBeat}
; otherwise, we return
NO
. This takes
\mathsf{poly}(n)
time. We now proceed to describe how to fill up the entries of
\mathsf{DP}
.

Initialization

Let
t
be a leaf in
T
. Then, by our assumption,
X_{t}=\emptyset
. Set
\mathsf{DP}[t,\bot,\epsilon]\leftarrow 0
.

Populating the table

Similar to the constructive case, we inductively assume that while filling up
\mathsf{DP}[t,\mathcal{P},\overline{n}]
, all entries of the form
\mathsf{DP}[t^{\prime},\cdot,\cdot]
, where
t^{\prime}
is a vertex in the subtree of
T
rooted at
t
, are filled. The update rule of our dynamic programming algorithm depends on the kind of vertex
t
is. For the rest of this section, we denote a generic partition by
\mathcal{P}=\{\mathcal{P}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{P}_{\ell}\}
and a generic tuple by
\overline{n}=(n_{i})_{i\in[\ell]}
; if
\ell=0
, then
\mathcal{P}
and
\overline{n}
will be understood to be
\bot
and
\epsilon
respectively.

Introduce node.

Let
t
be an Introduce node, and let vertex
v\in\mathcal{V}
of
\mathcal{G}
be introduced at
t
. Thus,
t
has only one child
t^{\prime}
,
X_{t}=X_{t^{\prime}}\cup\{v\}
,
v\notin V_{t^{\prime}}
,
E_{t}=E_{t^{\prime}}
and
v
is an isolated vertex in
G_{t}
.

Fix a partition
\mathcal{P}=\{\mathcal{P}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{P}_{\ell}\}
of a subset of
X_{t}
and a tuple
\overline{n}=(n_{i})_{i\in[\ell]}
. We consider the following cases, depending on
\mathcal{P}
and
\overline{n}
.

1.

Suppose that there does not exist any part of
\mathcal{P}
that contains
v
. Then
\mathcal{W}\subseteq V_{t}\setminus\{x\}
induces
(\mathcal{P},\overline{n})
on
X_{t}

if and only if

v\in\mathcal{W}
and
\mathcal{W}\setminus\{v\}
induces
(\mathcal{P},\overline{n})
on
X_{t^{\prime}}
. We update

\mathsf{DP}[t,\mathcal{P},\overline{n}]\leftarrow\mathsf{DP}[t^{\prime},\mathcal{P},\overline{n}]+\pi(v).

Note that the case
\mathcal{P}=\bot
and
\overline{n}=\epsilon
falls here.

2.

Let
\mathcal{P}
and
\overline{n}
satisfy both of the following two conditions:

(a)

One of the parts of
\mathcal{P}
is
\{v\}
. WLOG assume that
\mathcal{P}_{\ell}=\{v\}
.

(b)

n_{\ell}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}1&\mbox{if $\tau(v)=c^{\prime}$,}\\
-1&\mbox{if $\tau(v)=c$, and}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Let
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}=\{\mathcal{P}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{P}_{\ell-1}\}
and
\overline{n}^{\prime}\coloneqq(n_{i})_{i\in[\ell-1]}
. Then
\mathcal{W}\subseteq V_{t}
induces
(\mathcal{P},\overline{n})
on
X_{t}

if and only if

v\notin\mathcal{W}
and
\mathcal{W}
induces
(\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\overline{n}^{\prime})
on
X_{t^{\prime}}
. We update

\mathsf{DP}[t,\mathcal{P},\overline{n}]\leftarrow\mathsf{DP}[t^{\prime},\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\overline{n}^{\prime}].

3.

If none of the previous two cases is true, then no
\mathcal{W}\subseteq V_{t}
can induce
(\mathcal{P},\overline{n})
on (
X_{t},G_{t})
. We update
\mathsf{DP}[t,\mathcal{P},\overline{n}]\leftarrow\infty
.

The updation time is
\mathsf{poly}(n,m)
.

Introduce edge node.

Let
t
be an Introduce edge node. Thus,
t
has only one child
t^{\prime}
, and
X_{t}=X_{t^{\prime}}
. Let an edge
\{u,v\}
, for
u,v\in X_{t^{\prime}}
, be introduced at
t
.

Fix a partition
\mathcal{P}=\{\mathcal{P}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{P}_{\ell}\}
of a subset of
X_{t}
and a tuple
\overline{n}=(n_{i})_{i\in[\ell]}
. Now, for a partition
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}=(\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{\ell^{\prime}})
of a subset
S
of
X_{t^{\prime}}=X_{t}
and a tuple
\overline{n}^{\prime}=(n^{\prime}_{i})_{i\in[\ell^{\prime}]}
, we define a partition
\lambda(\mathcal{P}^{\prime})
of
S
and a tuple
\sigma(\overline{n}^{\prime})
as follows:

•

If
u,v
belong to
S
, and they belong to two different parts of
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
, replace those two parts by their union. Define
\lambda(\mathcal{P}^{\prime})
to be the resulting partition. Obtain
\sigma(\overline{n}^{\prime})
from
\overline{n}^{\prime}
by replacing the entries
n^{\prime}_{i_{1}}
and
n^{\prime}_{i_{2}}
by their sum, for each
j\in[m]
.

•

Otherwise define
\lambda(\mathcal{P}^{\prime})\coloneqq\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
and
\sigma(\overline{n}^{\prime})\coloneqq\overline{n}^{\prime}
.

Note that
\mathcal{W}\subseteq V_{t}
induces
(\mathcal{P},\overline{n})
on
(X_{t},G_{t})
if and only if
\mathcal{W}
induces
(\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\overline{n}^{\prime})
on
(X_{t^{\prime}},G_{t^{\prime}})
for some
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
and
\overline{n}^{\prime}
such that
\lambda(\mathcal{P}^{\prime})=\mathcal{P}
and
\sigma(\overline{n}^{\prime})=\overline{n}
. This leads us to our update rule:

\mathsf{DP}[t,\mathcal{P},\overline{n}]\leftarrow\min_{\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\overline{n}^{\prime}}\mathsf{DP}[t^{\prime},\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\overline{n}^{\prime}],

where the minimum is over all pairs
(\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\overline{n}^{\prime})
such that
\lambda(\mathcal{P}^{\prime})=\mathcal{P}
and
\sigma(\overline{n}^{\prime})=\overline{n}
. The updation takes time
w^{\mathcal{O}(w)}\cdot n^{\mathcal{O}(w)}\cdot\mathsf{poly}(n)
.

Forget vertex node.

Let
t
be a Forget vertex node, and let vertex
v\in\mathcal{V}
of
\mathcal{G}
be forgotten at
t
. Thus,
t
has only one child
t^{\prime}
,
X_{t}=X_{t^{\prime}}\setminus\{v\}
and
v\in X_{t^{\prime}}
. However, note that
G_{t}
and
G_{t^{\prime}}
are the same graph.

Fix a partition
\mathcal{P}=\{\mathcal{P}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{P}_{\ell}\}
of a subset of
X_{t}
and a tuple
\overline{n}=(n_{i})_{i\in[\ell]}
. Let
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}=\{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{\ell^{\prime}}\}
be a partition of a subset of
X_{t^{\prime}}
. Let
\overline{n}^{\prime}
be a tuple
(n^{\prime}_{i})_{i\in[\ell^{\prime}]}
. We say that the pair
(\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\overline{n}^{\prime})
is
consistent with
the pair
(\mathcal{P},\overline{n})
if one of the following is true:

1.

\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
and
\overline{n}=\overline{n}^{\prime}
.

2.

\ell=\ell^{\prime}
and there exists an
i\in[\ell]
such that both of the following are true

(a)

\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{i}=\mathcal{P}_{i}\cup\{v\}
and

n_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}n^{\prime}_{i}-1&\mbox{if $\tau(v)=c^{\prime}$,}\\
n^{\prime}_{i}+1&\mbox{if $\tau(v)=c$, and}\\
n^{\prime}_{i}&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

(b)

\forall k\in[\ell]\setminus\{i\}
,
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{k}=\mathcal{P}_{k}
and
n^{\prime}_{k}=n_{k}
.

3.

\ell+1=\ell^{\prime}
,
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}=\mathcal{P}\cup\{\{v\}\}
. WLOG, assume that
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{\ell^{\prime}}=\{v\}
. Furthermore, for all
i\in[\ell]
,
n^{\prime}_{i}=n_{i}
.

We now argue that
\mathcal{W}\subseteq V_{t}
induces
(\mathcal{P},\overline{n})
on
(X_{t},G_{t})
,
if and only if

\mathcal{W}
induces some
(\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\overline{n}^{\prime})
consistent with
(\mathcal{P},\overline{n})
on
(X_{t^{\prime}},G_{t^{\prime}})
. To see this, recall that the graphs
G_{t}
and
G_{t^{\prime}}
are the same. Thus, the connected components
\{\mathcal{Y}^{\mathcal{W},t}_{i}\}_{i}
and
\{\mathcal{Y}^{\mathcal{W},t^{\prime}}_{i}\}_{i}
are the same. Now
X_{t^{\prime}}=X_{t}\cup\{v\}
. Thus for each
i
, one of the following is true: 1.
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{i}=\mathcal{Y}^{\mathcal{W},t^{\prime}}_{i}\cap X_{t^{\prime}}=\{v\}
and
\mathcal{Y}^{\mathcal{W},t}_{i}\cap X_{t}=\emptyset
, 2.
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{i}=\mathcal{P}_{i}
, 3.
v\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{i}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{i}\setminus\{v\}=\mathcal{P}_{i}
. Furthermore, (2) holds for all but at most one
i
. It can be verified that the tuples
\overline{n}
and
\overline{n}^{\prime}
must be related so as to ensure that the pairs
(\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\overline{n}^{\prime})
and
(\mathcal{P},\overline{n})
are consistent.

This brings us to our update rule:

\mathsf{DP}[t,\mathcal{P},\overline{n}]\leftarrow\min_{(\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\overline{n}^{\prime})\text{ consistent with }(\mathcal{P},\overline{n})}\mathsf{DP}[t^{\prime},\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\overline{n}^{\prime}].

The updation takes time
w^{\mathcal{O}(w)}\cdot n^{\mathcal{O}(w)}\cdot\mathsf{poly}(n)
.

Join node.

Let
t
be a Join node. Thus, it has two children
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
. Furthermore,
X_{t}=X_{t_{1}}=X_{t_{2}}
.

It follows from the definition of join node that
X_{t}=X_{t_{1}}=X_{t_{2}}\subseteq V_{t_{1}}\cap V_{t_{2}}
. Can there be a vertex
v
in
V_{t_{1}}\cap V_{t_{2}}
that is not in
X_{t}
? It is easy to see that the answer is ‘no’, as otherwise the subgraph of
T
induced by the set
\{t\mid v\in X_{t}\}
will not be connected. We conclude that
V_{t_{1}}\cap V_{t_{2}}=X_{t}
.

Fix a partition
\mathcal{P}=(\mathcal{P}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{P}_{\ell})
of a subset of
X_{t}
, and a tuple
\overline{n}=(n_{i})_{i\in[\ell]}
.

Suppose
\mathcal{W}\subseteq V_{t}
induces
(\mathcal{P},\overline{n})
on
(X_{t},G_{t})
. Express
\mathcal{W}
as the disjoint union

\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{W}_{b}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{1}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{2},\mbox{ where}

•

\mathcal{W}_{b}=\mathcal{W}\cap X_{t}
,

•

\mathcal{W}_{1}=(\mathcal{W}\setminus\mathcal{W}_{b})\cap V_{t_{1}}
, and

•

\mathcal{W}_{2}=(\mathcal{W}\setminus\mathcal{W}_{b})\cap V_{t_{2}}
.

Let
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime}
be the partitions of subsets of
X_{t}
(which, recall, is the same as
X_{t_{1}}
and
X_{t_{2}}
), and
\overline{\mu},\overline{\nu}
be vectors of numbers such that
\mathcal{W}_{b}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{1}
induces
(\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\overline{\mu})
on
(X_{t_{1}},G_{t_{1}})
, and
\mathcal{W}_{b}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{2}
induces
(\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime},\overline{\nu})
on
(X_{t_{2}},G_{t_{2}})
. Can we express
\mathcal{P}
and
\overline{n}
in terms of
\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime},\overline{\mu}
and
\overline{\nu}
? In other words, are
\mathcal{P}
and
\overline{n}
determined by
\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime},\overline{\mu}
and
\overline{\nu}
? We will answer it in the affirmative. Let us start with
\mathcal{P}
.

Let
u
and
v
be two vertices in
X_{t}\setminus\mathcal{W}_{b}
. We wish to determine whether or not
u
and
v
are in the same part of
\mathcal{P}
.

Assume that there is a path
p
between
u
and
v
in
G_{t}\setminus\mathcal{W}
. Note that the edge set of
G_{t}\setminus\mathcal{W}
is the disjoint union (each edge is introduced exactly once) of the edge sets of
G_{t_{1}}\setminus\left(\mathcal{W}_{b}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{1}\right)
and
G_{t_{2}}\setminus\left(\mathcal{W}_{b}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{2}\right)
. Also recall that the only vertices common to
V_{t_{1}}
and
V_{t_{2}}
are the ones in
X_{t}
. Thus
p
is necessarily of the form
p_{1}p_{2}\ldots p_{t}
, where each
p_{i}
is a path between two vertices in
X_{t}\setminus\mathcal{W}_{b}
all of whose edges are either in
G_{t_{1}}\setminus\left(\mathcal{W}_{b}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{1}\right)
or in
G_{t_{2}}\setminus\left(\mathcal{W}_{b}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{2}\right)
.

To capture this, we define a graph
H=(X_{t}\setminus\mathcal{W}_{b},E)
as follows: for each
u,v\in X_{t}\setminus\mathcal{W}_{b}
, the pair
\{u,v\}
is an edge in
E
if
u
and
v
are in the same part in
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
or in
\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime}
. It follows that there exists path
p=p_{1}p_{2}\ldots p_{t}
as stated before if and only if
u
and
v
are connected in the above graph. We have the following procedure to determine
\mathcal{P}
from
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime}
:

1.

Build the graph
H
from
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime}
.

2.

Return the connected components of
H
as the parts
\mathcal{P}_{i}
s of
\mathcal{P}
.

The procedure above can be implemented in polynomial time.

Now, let us express
\overline{n}
in terms of
\overline{\mu},\overline{\nu},\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime}
. To this end, let

•

\mathcal{Y}^{(\mathcal{W},t)}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{Y}^{(\mathcal{W},t)}_{\ell}
be the connected components of
G_{t}\setminus\mathcal{W}
with non-empty intersection with
X_{t}
,

•

\mathcal{Y}^{(\mathcal{W}_{1},t_{1})}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{Y}^{(\mathcal{W}_{1},t_{1})}_{\ell_{1}}
be the connected components of
G_{t_{1}}\setminus\left(\mathcal{W}_{b}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{1}\right)
with non-empty intersection with
X_{t_{1}}=X_{t}
, and

•

\mathcal{Y}^{(\mathcal{W}_{2},t_{2})}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{Y}^{(\mathcal{W}_{2},t_{2})}_{\ell_{2}}
be the connected components of
G_{t_{2}}\setminus\left(\mathcal{W}_{b}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{2}\right)
with non-empty intersection with
X_{t_{2}}=X_{t}
.

It is easy to see that if two vertices are connected in
G_{t_{1}}\setminus\left(\mathcal{W}_{b}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{1}\right)
(resp.
G_{t_{2}}\setminus\left(\mathcal{W}_{b}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{2}\right)
), then they are connected in
G_{t}\setminus\mathcal{W}
as well. This lets us infer the following:

(
\alpha
)

Each connected component of
G_{t}\setminus\mathcal{W}
is the union of a set (possibly empty) of connected components of
G_{t_{1}}\setminus\left(\mathcal{W}_{b}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{1}\right)
and a set (possibly empty) of connected components of
G_{t_{2}}\setminus\left(\mathcal{W}_{b}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{2}\right)
.

(
\beta
)

Each part of
\mathcal{P}
is the union of a set of parts of
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
(resp.
\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime}
). Put another way,
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime}
are refinements of
\mathcal{P}
.

(
\gamma
)

Each
\mathcal{Y}^{(\mathcal{W}_{1},t_{1})}_{i}
(resp.
\mathcal{Y}^{(\mathcal{W}_{2},t_{2})}_{i})
is completely contained in some
\mathcal{Y}^{(\mathcal{W},t)}_{j}
.

From the above, we infer the following. Suppose
\mathcal{P}_{i}=\cup_{j\in S_{1}}(\mathcal{P}^{\prime})_{j}=\cup_{k\in S_{2}}(\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime})_{k}
. Then,
\bigcup_{j\in S_{1}}\mathcal{Y}^{(\mathcal{W}_{1},t_{1})}_{j}\bigcup_{k\in S_{2}}\mathcal{Y}^{(\mathcal{W}_{2},t_{2})}_{k}\subseteq\mathcal{Y}^{(\mathcal{W},t)}_{i}
.

Can
\bigcup_{j\in S_{1}}\mathcal{Y}^{(\mathcal{W}_{1},t_{1})}_{j}\bigcup_{k\in S_{2}}\mathcal{Y}^{(\mathcal{W}_{2},t_{2})}_{k}
be a proper subset of
\mathcal{Y}^{(\mathcal{W},t)}_{i}
? In view of the point (
\alpha
) above, the question boils down to the question whether there can exist a connected component of
G_{t_{1}}\setminus\left(\mathcal{W}_{b}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{1}\right)
or
G_{t_{2}}\setminus\left(\mathcal{W}_{b}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{2}\right)
, which (i) has empty intersection with
X_{t}
, and (ii) is completely contained in
\mathcal{Y}^{(\mathcal{W},t)}_{i}
.

We claim that the answer to the above question is ‘no.’ To prove the claim, towards a contradiction, assume that there exists a connected component
K
of
G_{t_{1}}\setminus\left(\mathcal{W}_{b}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{1}\right)
that satisfies the conditions (i) and (ii) above. The case of there being such a connected component of
G_{t_{2}}\setminus\left(\mathcal{W}_{b}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{2}\right)
is similar.

Since
K\subseteq\mathcal{Y}^{(\mathcal{W},t)}_{i}
and
\mathcal{Y}^{(\mathcal{W},t)}_{i}\cap X_{t}\neq\emptyset
, we have that for each vertex
u\in K
there is a path
p
to a vertex
w\in X_{t}
in
G_{t}\setminus\mathcal{W}
. Since
E_{t}
is a disjoint union of
E_{t_{1}}
and
E_{t_{2}}
as noted before, and
V_{t_{1}}\cap V_{t_{2}}=X_{t}
, we have that
p
is of the form
p_{1}\ldots p_{h}
, where each
p_{i}
is a path either in
G_{t_{1}}\setminus\left(\mathcal{W}_{b}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{1}\right)
or in
G_{t_{2}}\setminus\left(\mathcal{W}_{b}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{2}\right)
which ends at a vertex in
X_{t}
. Since
u
is a vertex of
G_{t_{1}}\setminus\left(\mathcal{W}_{b}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{1}\right)
, we have that
p_{1}
is a path in
G_{t_{1}}\setminus\left(\mathcal{W}_{b}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{1}\right)
connecting
u
with a vertex in
X_{t}
, which contradicts the assumption that
K
does not intersect
X_{t}
. This proves the claim. We conclude that
\bigcup_{j\in S_{1}}\mathcal{Y}^{(\mathcal{W}_{1},t_{1})}_{j}\bigcup_{k\in S_{2}}\mathcal{Y}^{(\mathcal{W}_{2},t_{2})}_{k}=\mathcal{Y}^{(\mathcal{W},t)}_{i}
.

Now we are ready to express
\overline{n}
in terms of
\overline{\mu},\overline{\nu},\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime}
. Since
V_{t_{1}}\setminus X_{t}
and
V_{t_{2}}\setminus X_{t}
are disjoint, we have that for each
i\in[\ell]
,
n_{i}=\sum_{u\in S_{1}}\mu_{u}+\sum_{v\in S_{2}}\nu_{v}-N_{i}
, where
N_{i}
is the difference between the number of nodes in
\mathcal{P}_{i}
that vote for
c^{\prime}
and the number of nodes in
\mathcal{P}_{i}
that vote for
c
.

We have described a procedure
\mathcal{A}
that, given
\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime},\overline{\mu}
and
\overline{\nu}
, produces
\mathcal{P}
and
\overline{n}
in time polynomial in
n
, with the following property:
\mathcal{W}
induces
(\mathcal{P},\overline{n})
on
(X_{t},G_{t})

if and only if
there exists
\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime},\overline{\mu}
and
\overline{\nu}
such that 1.
\mathcal{W}_{b}\uplus\mathcal{W}_{1}

(\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime},\overline{\nu})

(X_{t_{2}},G_{t_{2}})

\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime},\overline{\mu},\overline{\nu})=(\mathcal{P},\overline{n})

9
Conclusion and Open Questions

Our paper initiates a novel study of a natural kind of election control in online elections, and introduces problems
Constructive Control over Network
and
Destructive Control over Network
. Our paper is a comprehensive study of the computational aspects of these two problems. We present algorithms for the problems for restricted classes of inputs (where the number of candidates is bounded for
Constructive Control over Network
, and the treewidth of the network of voters is bounded for both problems), and establish the necessity of these restrictions on inputs by complementary hardness results.

Our work leaves open several interesting questions for future research. A natural follow-up question is whether the two problems admit algorithms whose time complexities have FPT (Fixed Parameter Tractable) dependencies on the parameters
w
(treewidth) and
m
(number of candidates) i.e, are of the form
\mathcal{O}(f(w)\cdot\mathsf{poly}(n,m))
for
Destructive Control over Network
and
\mathcal{O}(g(w,m)\cdot\mathsf{poly}(n,m))
for
Constructive Control over Network
, where
f

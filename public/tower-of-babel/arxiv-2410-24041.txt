Precise prediction of the decay rate for η b → γ ​ γ \eta_{b}\to\gamma\gamma from lattice QCD
Brian Colquhoun, Christine T. H. Davies, G. Peter Lepage, HPQCD Collaboration

Abstract
We calculate the decay rate for η b → γ ​ γ \eta_{b}\to\gamma\gamma in lattice QCD for the first time, providing a precise prediction for the Belle II experiment. Our calculation includes u u , d d , s s and c c quarks in the sea, using gluon field configurations generated by the MILC collaboration, at three values of the lattice spacing from 0.06 fm to 0.03 fm. All quarks are treated in the Highly Improved Staggered Quark formalism, which enables us to reach the b b quark mass for our valence quarks on these fine lattices. By working at additional heavy quark masses between those of c c and b b we also map out the behaviour of the ratio f η h / ( M η h 2 ​ F η h ​ ( 0 , 0 ) ) f_{\eta_{h}}/(M_{\eta_{h}}^{2}F_{\eta_{h}}(0,0)) , where f f is the decay constant, M M , the mass and F ⁡ ( 0 , 0 ) F(0,0) , the form factor for decay to two on-shell photons for the pseudoscalar heavyonium meson, η h \eta_{h} . This ratio takes the approximate value 0.5 in leading-order non-relativistic QCD but we are able to give a much more accurate analysis than this. Focussing on the b b quark mass, we find a ratio of 0.468 ​ ( 11 ) 0.468(11) , giving Γ ⁡ ( η b → γ ​ γ ) = 0.557 ​ ( 32 ) fit ​ ( 1 ) syst ​ keV \Gamma(\eta_{b}\to\gamma\gamma)=0.557(32)_{\text{fit}}(1)_{\text{syst}}\>\mathrm{keV} . Combined with a value for the branching fraction from potential nonrelativistic QCD, our result can be used to determine the total width of the η b \eta_{b} with a 7% uncertainty.



I
Introduction

The annihilation of a pseudoscalar meson to two photons can provide a very ‘clean’ test of the Standard Model, since it is a purely electromagnetic process. The rate depends on the strong interaction effects that dictate the internal structure of the meson and hence the form factor for the decay, so a comparison of theory and experiment can provide a stringent test of our understanding of QCD. There are challenges to studying this decay mode, however.

Recently the theoretical picture for
\eta_{c}\to\gamma\gamma
decay was transformed with the first lattice QCD calculation with realistic sea quark content
Colquhoun et al. 2023
. The result, with a total uncertainty of less than 1%, is much more accurate than that possible with earlier non-lattice methods. The experimental picture is less clear, however, with the Particle Data Group global fit of
\eta_{c}
decay rates
Navas et al. 2024
having a
\chi^{2}
of 184.6 for 94 degrees of freedom. There are several consistent results from different experiments for the process
\gamma\gamma\rightarrow\eta_{c}\to K\overline{K}\pi
and the average value for
\Gamma(\eta_{c}\to\gamma\gamma)
inferred from these is in reasonable agreement with the lattice QCD result, but with a much larger (10%) uncertainty. Improved experimental results will be needed to match the accuracy of lattice QCD.

Here we will study
\eta_{b}\to\gamma\gamma
. There are no experimental signals for this decay mode as yet
Navas et al. 2024
;
Heister et al. 2002
, but it should be accessible to Belle II
Lange and Prencipe 2022
, so a prediction of the rate now is very timely.
Theoretical calculations to date have relied on nonrelativistic approaches and are limited by systematic uncertainties associated with missing relativistic and radiative corrections. Lattice QCD calculations, on the other hand, promise the possibility of a result free of these uncertainties. Instead there are statistical errors and systematic errors from the extrapolation to the zero lattice spacing, continuum limit. The continuum extrapolation is particularly difficult for
b
quarks because they have large mass. Very good control of discretisation errors is required, such as that possible using the Highly Improved Staggered Quark (HISQ) formalism
Follana et al. 2007
. In this formalism tree-level discretisation errors appear first at
\mathcal{O}(a^{4})
(where
a
is the lattice spacing) since
\mathcal{O}(a^{2})
errors are removed. Furthermore,
an analysis of the HISQ action at large quark mass
Follana et al. 2007
shows that these errors only affect subleading
\mathcal{O}(v^{4})
terms in a nonrelativistic expansion in powers of the quark velocity,
v
, appropriate to the heavyonium physics we consider here.

The hadronic quantity that parameterises the 2-photon decay rate is the form factor
F(0,0)
, where the two zeroes denote that both photons are on-shell.
The decay rate is related to the form factor for a pseudoscalar heavyonium meson
\eta_{h}
by:

\Gamma(\eta_{h}\rightarrow\gamma\gamma)=\pi\alpha^{2}Q_{h}^{4}M_{\eta_{h}}^{3}(F_{\eta_{h}}(0,0))^{2}\,,

(1)

where
M_{\eta_{h}}
is the meson mass and
Q_{h}
the quark electric charge.

As discussed in
Colquhoun et al. 2023
, a useful dimensionless ratio can be constructed from
F(0,0)
and heavyonium decay constants and masses, that cancels the strong mass-dependence of its components and has a simple limit at leading-order in nonrelativistic QCD (NRQCD):

R_{\eta_{h}}\equiv\frac{f_{\eta_{h}}}{F_{\eta_{h}}(0,0)M_{\eta_{h}}^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}(1+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s})+\mathcal{O}(v^{2}/c^{2}))\,.

(2)

Here we calculate
R_{\eta_{h}}
accurately in lattice QCD, both at the
b
quark mass and for masses between
b
and
c
, enabling us to plot out how the deviations from the leading-order result of 1/2 depend on heavy quark mass. We use the ratio at the
b
and earlier HPQCD results for the
\eta_{b}
decay constant
Hatton et al. 2021
to determine
\Gamma(\eta_{b}\rightarrow\gamma\gamma)
.

We follow
Colquhoun et al. 2023
(Appendix B) in calculating the 3-point correlation function between a temporal axial vector current (coupling to the
\eta_{h}
) and two vector currents, shown schematically in Figure
1
. In spin-taste notation we use operators
\gamma_{5}\gamma_{t}\otimes\gamma_{x}\gamma_{z}
,
\gamma_{x}\otimes\gamma_{x}
and
\gamma_{z}\otimes\gamma_{z}
. One vector current is placed at the time origin,
0
, and the temporal axial current at
T
; 4–6 values of
T
are used ranging from 0.7 fm upwards. Propagators from
0
and
T
are combined with a vector current at
t_{\gamma_{2}}
, spatial momentum having been inserted in the propagator from 0 using twisted boundary conditions
Sachrajda and Villadoro 2005
;
Guadagnoli et al. 2006
. The spatial momentum is taken in the
y
-direction, orthogonal to both vector current polarisations, and its magnitude,
\omega
, is set equal to one-half the meson mass. The 3-point correlation function is then converted to a 2-point function between
0
and
T
by summing over all values of
t_{\gamma_{2}}
from
-L_{t}/2+1
to
+L_{t}/2-1
with a weighting of
\exp(-\omega t_{\gamma_{2}})
. This sets photon 2 on-shell
Ji and Jung 2001a
;
Ji and Jung 2001b
;
Dudek et al. 2006
, and photon 1 also, up to tuning errors, negligible here. The summand is strongly peaked around
t_{\gamma_{2}}=0
and
T
values are chosen to lie outside this peak
Colquhoun et al. 2023
. This gives a sizeable separation of the temporal axial current and the two vector currents and a good projection onto the ground-state
\eta_{h}
meson. This is shown in the Supplementary materials where a table of
T
values is given.

We fit this two-point function,
\tilde{C}(T)
, along with the full two-point function between two temporal axial currents,
C(t)
, to a sum of exponentials:

C(t)=\sum_{n}a_{n}^{2}e^{-M_{n}t}\,\,;\,\,\tilde{C}(T)=\sum_{n}a_{n}b_{n}e^{-M_{n}T},

(3)

where we have not shown the oscillating terms, included in our fit, that result from using staggered quarks. The ground-state amplitude,
b_{0}
, is then related to the matrix element for
\eta_{h}
decay to two on-shell photons and we determine the form factor in lattice units as:

\frac{F(0,0)}{a}=b_{0}\sqrt{\frac{2}{aM_{\eta_{h}}}}\frac{L_{s}}{\theta\pi}Z_{V}^{2}\,.

(4)

Here
\theta
is the twist angle used and
Z_{V}
is the renormalisation factor for the local vector current determined using the RI-SMOM intermediate scheme at 2 GeV
Hatton et al. 2019
;
Hatton et al. 2021
. By combining this with a simultaneous determination of the
\eta_{h}
decay constant,
f_{\eta_{h}}
, from the annihilation amplitude of the ‘Goldstone’ (spin-taste
\gamma_{5}\otimes\gamma_{5}
)
\eta_{h}
meson, we can determine the ratio in Eq. (
2
). Values for
R_{\eta_{h}}
on each set and for each mass are given in the Supplementary materials.

III
Results and Conclusions

We fit the lattice results for
R_{\eta_{h}}
for each meson mass as a function of
a
and
m^{\mathrm{sea}}
to determine a physical result in the continuum limit. The fit form, taken from
Colquhoun et al. 2023
, is:

\displaystyle R_{\eta_{h}}^{\mathrm{latt}}=R_{\eta_{h}}^{\mathrm{phys}}\times

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\Bigg[1+\sum_{i=1}^{i_{\text{max}}}\kappa_{a\Lambda}^{(i)}\left(a\Lambda\right)^{2i}+\kappa_{\mathrm{val},h}\delta^{\mathrm{val},h}+\kappa_{\mathrm{sea},c}\delta^{\mathrm{sea},c}

\displaystyle\hskip 8.53581pt+\kappa_{\mathrm{sea},uds}^{(0)}\delta^{\mathrm{sea},uds}\left\{1+\kappa_{\mathrm{sea},uds}^{(1)}(a\tilde{\Lambda})^{2}+\kappa_{\mathrm{sea},uds}^{(2)}(a\tilde{\Lambda})^{4}\right\}\Bigg]

(5)

where the
\kappa_{a\Lambda}
terms allow for discretisation effects, the
\kappa_{h}
terms for mistuning of the heavy quark mass and the
\kappa_{\mathrm{sea}}
terms for mistuning of the sea quark masses. Figure
2
shows our results at the
b
quark mass and the continuum fit of Eq. (
5
). Further details on the fit are given in the Supplementary materials along with plots of the fits for the other heavy quark mass cases.

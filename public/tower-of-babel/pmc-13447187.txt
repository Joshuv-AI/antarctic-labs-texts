Design and Rationale of the REmote Monitoring to Identify worsening Heart Failure: The REMOTI-HF Randomized Clinical Trial
Authors: Gonçalo Terleira Batista, Natália António, Maria João Primo, Vanessa Lopes, Luisa Rocha, Fátima Franco, Miguel Costa, Inês Jordão, Patrícia Paiva, Patrícia Dias, Francisco Parente, Lino Gonçalves
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAcute decompensated heart failure (HF) is a leading cause of hospital admissions, often associated with poor outcomes. The Triage-HF (Medtronic®) and HeartLogic (Boston Scientific®) algorithms, when integrated into implantable devices, have demonstrated the ability to predict these events with high accuracy. REMOTI-HF aims to evaluate whether responding to algorithm-generated alerts through structured remote clinical assessment can reduce HF-related events and improve outcomes. The REMOTI-HF Clinical Trial is a single-centre, randomized controlled trial designed to evaluate the effectiveness of clinical evaluation and therapeutic optimization guided by the Triage-HF and HeartLogic algorithms to reduce HF-related events in patients with advanced HF. Approximately 500 patients are expected to be enrolled. Inclusion criteria include having an implantable device compatible with these algorithms and a left ventricular ejection fraction ≤35% at the time of device implantation. Patients will be excluded if they are listed for heart transplant, have ventricular mechanical support, are younger than 18 or older than 85 years, have severe cognitive impairment, are pregnant or have a life expectancy of less than one year. The primary endpoint is a composite of major HF events—including all-cause mortality, hospital admissions for HF, urgent HF hospital visits, ventricular arrhythmias and atrial arrhythmias requiring medical evaluation. The trial is registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT06422832). REMOTI-HF will provide insights into the effectiveness of clinical assessment guided by HeartLogic and Triage-HF algorithm alerts in reducing HF-related events. These findings may pave the way for more sophisticated remote monitoring strategies that build on existing device algorithms.Trial RegistrationClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT06422832Keywords: Heart failure, Remote patient monitoring, Cardiac resynchronization therapy devices, Algorithm

INTRODUCTION

Heart failure (HF) is a highly prevalent clinical syndrome associated with high morbidity and mortality, frequent exacerbations and recurrent hospitalizations, which leads to a significant decline in patients’ quality of life and a substantial burden on healthcare systems.1) It affects approximately 64 million people worldwide.2) Moreover, the prevalence of HF continues rising due to population aging, the increasing burden of cardiovascular risk factors and comorbidities and improved survival following acute cardiovascular events.3)

The growing burden of HF has led to increasing interest in the development of strategies to enable early detection of clinical deterioration and ultimately reducing the need for acute care. Remote monitoring has emerged as a promising approach to improve outcomes and optimize resource utilization.3) National data have also reinforced its potential: in a prospective Portuguese cohort, remote monitoring was associated with a 68% reduction in hospitalizations and a 66% reduction in emergency department (ED) visits, demonstrating meaningful benefits even in smaller-scale, real-world implementations.4)

Recent advances in remote monitoring have opened new avenues for optimizing the management of HF.5) Among these, remote monitoring using implantable cardiac devices, particularly through algorithms designed to predict acute HF decompensations, has emerged as a promising strategy. However, the full clinical integration of these systems remains challenging.5,6) Future remote monitoring models are expected to integrate data from multiple sources, including implantable devices, wearable sensors, laboratory results and hemodynamic parameters, into fully automated systems, aiming at individualized care.6,7)

Triage-HF (Medtronic®) and HeartLogic (Boston Scientific®) are among the most sophisticated algorithms coded into implantable devices, capable of detecting early signs of clinical HF deterioration by analyzing parameters such as thoracic impedance, heart rate variability, nocturnal respiratory rate and physical activity levels. Preliminary studies have shown encouraging results in predicting HF exacerbations; however, their impact on reducing hospitalizations and improving clinical outcomes remains to be demonstrated in randomized controlled trials.8,9)

The REMOTI-HF trial is an investigator-initiated study designed to evaluate whether proactive clinical evaluation and therapeutic optimization, guided by Triage-HF and HeartLogic algorithm alerts, can reduce HF-related events and improve outcomes. Although REMOTI-HF does not evaluate artificial intelligence, it will provide evidence on the clinical utility of algorithms coded into implantable devices, which may serve as a foundation for future developments in digital health.

METHODS

Study design

The REMOTI-HF trial is an investigator-initiated, single-centre, prospective, randomized controlled study conducted at Unidade Local de Saúde de Coimbra, Portugal. It is designed to evaluate the clinical impact of remote monitoring guided by the HeartLogic and Triage-HF algorithms in patients with HF.

The trial aims to enroll approximately 500 patients with HF. All participants will undergo remote monitoring and will be randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to either the intervention group, in which the respective algorithm is activated, or to the control group, where the algorithms remain deactivated. Recruitment has already started and the trial is expected to run for 36 months. The study was approved by the local ethics committee and all participants provided written informed consent prior to enrolment.

Eligibility criteria

Patients are eligible for inclusion if they are between 18 and 85 years of age, have an implantable cardioverter-defibrillator (ICD) or cardiac resynchronization therapy (CRT) device compatible with the HeartLogic or Triage-HF algorithms, and a left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) of 35% or less at the time of device implantation.

Exclusion criteria include the inability to be contacted or to comply with follow-up procedures, presence of severe cognitive impairment, and being listed for heart transplantation at baseline or have ventricular mechanical support, are younger than 18 or older than 85 years, are pregnant or have a life expectancy of less than one year.

Algorithm functionality

The HeartLogic algorithm combines data from several sensors within the device to generate an index, reflecting the patient’s risk of HF decompensation. It integrates thoracic impedance, heart rate, heart rate variability, respiratory rate (especially nocturnal), activity levels and S3 heart sounds. An alarm is triggered once it crosses the programmable threshold of 16.8)

The Triage-HF algorithm, follows the same principle, assessing HF decompensation risk based on a combination of metrics, namely thoracic impedance, patient activity, heart rate variability, atrial fibrillation burden and therapy delivered (e.g., shocks). It classifies patients into three risk categories—low, medium, and high-updated daily.9) High-risk states have been associated with significantly increased short-term risk of hospitalization.6)

Both algorithms were included as they are among the most clinically validated multiparametric device-based tools currently available for HF risk stratification in patients with implanted devices. As they are distinct systems, despite following the same principles, equivalence of clinical impact cannot be assumed; therefore, outcomes will also be reported separately by algorithm type in a prespecified exploratory analyses.

Regarding alerts generated during weekends or overnight, a pragmatic approach will be adopted to reflect real-world clinical practice. Alerts generated overnight will follow the same rule, with a maximum delay of 48 hours before first contact. For alerts generated during weekends, a tolerance of one additional day will be allowed.

Participants in the control group follow routine care, with no algorithm activation and no study-related contact will occur unless required for events detected by remote monitoring without algorithm activation or for administrative and safety reasons.

All relevant clinical events, including hospital admissions, ED visits, changes in therapy and mortality, will be recorded based on electronic health records and patient contacts over the course of the 36-month follow-up period.

Randomization and blinding

Patients will be randomized using REDCap®. Due to the nature of the intervention, complete blinding is not possible. Patients initially remain blinded to group allocation; however, those in the intervention group may become aware of their group allocation if they are contacted due to an algorithm alert, particularly if this leads to a therapeutic intervention. Outcome assessors will not remain blinded to group assignment throughout the trial, due to its design. Within the study team, only the principal investigator will have access to the allocation list.

Although both Triage-HF and HeartLogic algorithms have been shown to reliably predict HF decompensation, randomization will be stratified by the implanted algorithm to ensure balanced allocation across study arms. In addition, randomization will account for current clinical status, including the most recent LVEF and New York Heart Association (NYHA) functional class at enrollment, rather than values recorded at the time of device implantation. Patients will be randomized in a 1:1 ratio using a computer-generated sequence with variable block sizes implemented in REDCap® software.

Allocation concealment will be ensured by software, which automatically generates the assignment and prevents investigator access prior to allocation. All analyses will follow the intention-to-treat principle.

Primary endpoint

The primary endpoint of the REMOTI-HF trial is the total number of composite events including all-cause mortality, HF hospitalizations, urgent HF visits requiring therapeutic escalation and ventricular arrhythmias (sustained, symptomatic, or requiring intervention) and atrial arrhythmias prompting clinical evaluation or intervention.

Endpoints will be defined as follows:

• All-cause mortality: Death from any cause.

• All-cause mortality: Death from any cause.

• HF hospitalization: Admission due to HF with clinical signs or symptoms of decompensation (e.g., dyspnea, orthopnea, edema, weight gain, pulmonary congestion) requiring intravenous diuretics or intravenous (IV) vasoactive therapy.

• HF hospitalization: Admission due to HF with clinical signs or symptoms of decompensation (e.g., dyspnea, orthopnea, edema, weight gain, pulmonary congestion) requiring intravenous diuretics or intravenous (IV) vasoactive therapy.

• Urgent HF visit: Unscheduled outpatient or ED visit not resulting in hospital admission, in which worsening HF was the primary cause and that required therapeutic escalation (e.g., IV diuretics, oral diuretic dose increase >50%, initiation of another HF therapy).

• Urgent HF visit: Unscheduled outpatient or ED visit not resulting in hospital admission, in which worsening HF was the primary cause and that required therapeutic escalation (e.g., IV diuretics, oral diuretic dose increase >50%, initiation of another HF therapy).

• Ventricular arrhythmias: Any sustained ventricular arrhythmia (ventricular tachycardia or ventricular fibrillation) lasting more than 30 seconds or any ventricular arrhythmia that is symptomatic or that requires therapeutic intervention (appropriate ICD shock or antitachycardia pacing [ATP], external cardioversion/defibrillation).

Although the primary analysis will evaluate all patients together, exploratory analyses will compare outcomes separately for individuals monitored with HeartLogic® and Triage-HF® to assess potential differences in algorithm performance.

The primary composite endpoint includes both hard clinical outcomes (all-cause mortality and HF hospitalization) and possibly contact-sensitive components (urgent HF visits and arrhythmia-driven encounters). Interpretation of the composite will consider the relative contribution of these components, with particular emphasis on the hard clinical outcomes.

Secondary endpoint

Secondary endpoints include the number of ED visits or unplanned consultations related to HF, time to the first HF event and the number of appropriate therapies, such as shocks or ATP. Additional exploratory outcomes include the association between physical activity metrics and the occurrence of HF events.

As key secondary outcomes, all-cause mortality and HF hospitalizations will also be evaluated independently.

Secondary endpoints were selected to complement the primary outcome by capturing meaningful events related to HF morbidity, arrhythmic burden and healthcare utilization.

Data collection and follow-up

Baseline data, including demographics, medical history, comorbidities, device type and settings, current medications, laboratory values and HF functional status, will be gathered and recorded at enrolment. Device diagnostics (HeartLogic and Triage-HF) will be captured automatically via remote transmissions and stored in a secure study database.

Throughout the 36-month follow-up, all device alerts, scheduled remote check-ins and any patient contacts triggered by alarms will be recorded. Meaningful events, namely hospital admissions for HF or unscheduled hospital visits, arrhythmia therapies and death will be gathered via electronic records and/or patient/caregiver interviews.

The Clinical Trials Unit of the Coimbra Academic Clinical Center, an independent unit from the cardiology department and the study team, will monitor the data and randomization status.

Sample size considerations and statistical analysis

The primary endpoint will be analysed as the total number of composite events (all-cause mortality, HF hospitalization, urgent HF visit, ventricular arrhythmias and atrial arrhythmias), rather than only time to first event. This distinction is important, as recurrent events substantially increase the number of analyzable outcomes.

Published registry data in this high-risk population have reported all-cause mortality rates ranging from 8% to 11% annually, even under guideline-directed medical therapy.10) In higher-risk cohorts, such as reported in the RI-AIAC registry, annual mortality rates reached 20% to 25%, highlighting the substantial baseline risk of this patient population.11,12) We considered annual mortality rates of 8–25%.10,11,12) HF hospitalization rates frequently exceed 20 per 100 patient-year.13) Notably, some registries have reported even higher hospitalization rates, up to 1.34 per patient-year, underscoring the considerable burden of recurrent HF admissions in real-world practice.14) Arrhythmic events also contribute substantially to adverse outcomes in this population. In the IN-TIME trial, patients with ICD or CRT devices experienced clinically relevant rates of ventricular arrhythmias, occurring in approximately 5–10% per year.15) Similarly, in TIM-HF2, device diagnostics frequently identified atrial arrhythmias requiring medical evaluation, further highlighting the burden of arrhythmic complications among patients with advanced HF.16) The frequency of ED visits for worsening HF without subsequent hospitalization is more difficult to quantify, as many registries primarily report hospital admissions; nevertheless, available data suggest that these unscheduled visits are common and contribute substantially to healthcare utilization.

Based on these data and considering the nature of the primary endpoint, we assumed an overall event rate of 40 to 60 per 100 patient-years in the control group. This conservative estimate also reflects the expectation that some patients will experience relatively few events, owing to good response to device therapy and the substantial improvements in guideline-directed medical treatment over the past decade.

Assuming a 20% relative reduction in the primary endpoint and considering a dispersion factor between 1.0 and 1.2 the estimated required sample size would range from approximately 472 to 566 participants. Given the single-centre nature of REMOTI-HF and the realistic caseload of our institution, a total sample size of 500 patients (250 per arm) was deemed pragmatically feasible and appropriate for this pilot trial.

This estimation was chosen conservatively and accounted for the modest and heterogeneous effects reported in previous trials. In the BEAT-HF trial, no significant reduction in readmission or mortality with remote monitoring plus nurse support was found (hazard ratio [HR], 1.03 for 180-day readmission),17) while TIM-HF showed no difference in the primary outcome of all-cause mortality (HR, 0.97) or cardiovascular death (HR, 0.86).18) However, the IN-TIME trial (which included variables such as heart rate, heart rate variability, physical activity, and thoracic impedance, parameters similar to those integrated in the HeartLogic algorithm) reported a significant reduction in a composite clinical score driven by reduced mortality (HR, 0.37 for all-cause mortality).15) Considering this variability and the pragmatic, non-invasive nature of the REMOTI-HF intervention, a 20% relative reduction was considered realistic. The choice of 90% power also provides a margin to account for losses to follow-up, protocol deviation and variability in event rates.

Analysis of the primary endpoint will use recurrent event methods. Differences between subgroups will be evaluated using the log-rank test. Secondary outcomes will be analysed using the appropriate statistical methods based on the distribution. All statistical tests will be 2-sided, and a p value <0.05 will be considered statistically significant.

All analyses will follow the intention-to-treat principle, and any missing outcome data will be addressed using appropriate sensitivity analyses. Multiplicity across secondary and exploratory endpoints will be handled descriptively, with the primary endpoint being the only endpoint formally powered for hypothesis testing. Interim analyses are planned at 12 and 24 months, to assess safety, event rates and trial assumptions without compromising the study’s integrity. Given the controlled nature of data collection and the reliance on electronic health records for outcome assessment, the amount of missing data is expected to be minimal.

Exploratory subgroup analyses by algorithm type and a prespecified treatment-by-algorithm interaction test will be performed.

Given the inclusion of both hard clinical and contact-sensitive components in the composite endpoint, a prespecified interpretability framework was defined. The composite outcome will be reported alongside its individual components, with particular emphasis on all-cause mortality and heart failure hospitalization. A positive effect on the composite accompanied by reductions in these hard endpoints will be interpreted as evidence of clinically meaningful benefit. In contrast, if the composite endpoint is positive but not accompanied by concordant trends in all-cause mortality and HF hospitalization, this will be interpreted cautiously as reflecting healthcare utilization effects rather than clear modification of major clinical outcomes and will be explicitly highlighted and discussed transparently. Neutral or negative composite results will be interpreted based on the trends of the individual components, with emphasis on the effects on each endpoint.

Quality assurance and quality control

The investigator is responsible for ensuring that trial-related source data, study documents and reports are available and accessible. The investigator agrees to provide direct access to these materials for the purposes of monitoring, auditing, and/or inspection. Accurate and reliable data collection is ensured through independent monitoring, source data verification and cross-checking of electronic case report forms against source documents. A comprehensive validation program is implemented to verify data quality, with any resulting queries addressed by the investigator.

Trial registration

This trial is registered at ClinicalTrials.gov with the identifier NCT06422832.

DISCUSSION

REMOTI-HF is a single-centre, prospective, randomized, controlled trial that aims to enrol 500 patients with HF and reduced LVEF (≤35%) who have implanted an ICD or CRT device compatible with the HeartLogic and Triage-HF algorithms. Following 1:1 randomization, participants in the intervention arm will have the corresponding algorithm activated and will be monitored remotely for a 36-month period, while controls will proceed with their standard care, including remote monitoring, but without algorithm-based alerts. Alerts are defined as a HeartLogic index crossing the manufacturer’s default threshold of 16 or a Triage-HF “high risk” classification. Upon an alert, the investigation team will proceed as outlined in Figure 1. Patients will be contacted by phone for clinical assessment, beginning with an evaluation of the Clinical Pharmacology Department, focused on medication adherence and potential decompensation triggers and a Cardiology evaluation to determine the need for therapeutic adjustments, laboratory testing or a scheduled in-person visit or hospitalization.

The primary endpoint is defined as the total number of composite events including all-cause mortality, HF hospitalizations, urgent HF visits, ventricular arrhythmias and atrial arrhythmias prompting clinical evaluation as defined in the METHODS section. Mortality and HF hospitalizations will also be analyzed independently as key secondary outcomes.

The choice of a composite endpoint reflects the multifactorial nature of HF, aiming to capture both fatal and non-fatal events with impact on patient outcomes and quality of life. The goal of remote monitoring is not only to reduce mortality and hospitalizations but also to prevent ED visits and other unscheduled visits triggered by HF decompensation. By keeping patients out of the ED, remote monitoring strategies may help reduce the burden on healthcare systems while supporting patient stability and quality of life. This composite aligns with endpoints used in contemporary HF trials but is specifically adapted to the goals of remote monitoring. Each component will also be analyzed individually as secondary or exploratory outcomes, with particular emphasis on mortality and HF hospitalizations as hard clinical endpoints.

Remote monitoring using algorithms like HeartLogic and Triage-HF may represent a paradigm shift in HF management, offering the potential to predict clinical deterioration and prevent major HF events. The IN-TIME trial demonstrated that multiparametric remote monitoring-decreased all-cause mortality by 63% (HR, 0.37) underscoring the potential benefits of an early, data-driven intervention.15) Moreover, TIM-HF2 achieved a 20% reduction in days lost to unplanned cardiovascular hospitalization or death (HR, 0.80), proving that structured remote follow-up can deliver real-world benefits in a broad HF population.16)

The REMOTI-HF trial differs from previous remote monitoring studies such as TIM-HF2 and IN-TIME. Unlike the IN-TIME trial, where device diagnostics were reviewed centrally on a daily basis with substantial resource requirements, REMOTI-HF is designed to be more pragmatic and closer to real-world implementation. In our study, data are not monitored routinely but only evaluated in response to actionable algorithm alerts, which markedly reduces the workload and resource demand. This approach reflects how device-based algorithms are likely to be applied in clinical practice, focusing attention on high-risk patients rather than continuous surveillance of the entire cohort. This difference in monitoring intensity may lead to different results, potentially achieving similar effectiveness with fewer resources, or alternatively resulting in lower efficacy compared with continuous daily data review. Compared with IN-TIME, important differences include the fact that IN-TIME was initiated more than 15 years ago, used devices from a single manufacturer with a less contemporary algorithm, and required routine centralized data, a process that was resource-intensive and less feasible for widespread adoption. In contrast, REMOTI-HF evaluates modern device-based algorithms from different manufacturers and applies a pragmatic workflow without routine external data analysis, reflecting how such tools are more likely to be implemented in real-world clinical practice.

However, not all remote monitoring approaches have yielded benefits. The BEAT-HF and the Tele-HF trials, which lacked device-based risk stratification, failed to reduce readmissions or mortality, highlighting the crucial role of precise alerts rather than simple telephonic follow-up.17,19) Similarly, the CONNECT trial, integrating only basic home monitoring, achieved improvements in physician response times and reduction of hospital length of stay but no benefits in hard outcomes.20)

In this context, the REMOTI-HF trial takes advantage of the industry-designed algorithms coded into implanted devices, requiring no additional hardware or invasive interventions. The target of 20% reduction in a composite of mortality and major HF events is conservative but aligns with the mean risk reductions reported in device-algorithm studies, such as the IN-TIME study which achieved a 27% relative reduction in its primary composite endpoint (mainly due to reduced mortality),15) and the TIM-HF2 trial, that reported a 20% decrease in days lost due to unplanned cardiovascular hospitalization or death (HR, 0.80).16)

Finally, the potential economic benefits of algorithm-driven care using devices already implanted are compelling. Should REMOTI-HF observe a 20% reduction in major events, the resulting reduction in healthcare utilization and associated decreased costs could drive generalized adoption of these monitoring strategies with minimal additional infrastructure. While a formal cost-effectiveness analysis is not planned within this trial, such an evaluation will be considered in the future depending on the magnitude of benefit observed.

Despite its innovative design, the REMOTI-HF trial still faces potential challenges and limitations. Firstly, as a single-centre study, its findings may have limited generalizability to different populations and healthcare systems. Secondly, the ability to contact participants may vary, potentially leading to missing data or delays in intervention. Moreover, the open-label design may lead to detection bias. Because patients in the intervention arm are contacted in response to algorithm alerts, their frequency of interaction with the study team is higher than in the control arm. This imbalance could increase the likelihood of event reporting in the intervention group. Thirdly, the choice of default HeartLogic and high-risk Triage-HF thresholds, while pragmatic, may not be optimal for all individuals, potentially resulting in false positives or missed events. Moreover, although HeartLogic® and Triage-HF® are conceptually similar, they are distinct systems and their clinical impact may not be identical; accordingly, algorithm-specific results will be interpreted cautiously, as the study was not designed or statistically powered to perform a direct comparison between the two algorithms. Finally, although powered to detect a 20% reduction in events, the trial may lack sensitivity to identify smaller, yet meaningful, benefits or harms.

In conclusion, the REMOTI-HF trial will evaluate the real-world efficacy of utilizing device algorithms to detect and intervene upon early signs of HF decompensation. By activating established, multiparametric tools (HeartLogic and Triage-HF) and responding quickly to their alerts, this study aims to demonstrate a clinically significant reduction in mortality and major HF events. Its pragmatic, single-centre design prioritizes feasibility, patient convenience and minimal modifications in healthcare systems. While potential limitations such as unblinding risk and generalizability deserve to be considered, the potential benefits are exciting. If successful, REMOTI-HF will provide compelling evidence to support widespread adoption of algorithm-driven remote monitoring, paving the way for more personalized, efficient and cost-effective HF care.

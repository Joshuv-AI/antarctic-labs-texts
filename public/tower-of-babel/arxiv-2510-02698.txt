Ulrich modules over local rings of dimension two
Srikanth B. Iyengar, Linquan Ma, Mark E. Walker

Abstract
. It is proved that Ulrich modules exist for a large class of local rings of dimension two. This complements earlier work of the authors and Ziquan Zhuang that described complete intersection domains of dimension two that admit no Ulrich modules. As an application, it is proved that, for this class of rings, the length of a nonzero module of finite projective dimension is at least the multiplicity of the local ring.

1.
Introduction

This paper establishes the existence of Ulrich modules for a large class of local rings of dimension two. Throughout
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
denotes a local noetherian ring
R
with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
and residue field
k=R/\mathfrak{m}
; in this introduction we assume
k
is infinite. Let
B=\operatorname{Proj}\left(\bigoplus\mathfrak{m}^{n}\right)
denote the blow-up of
\operatorname{Spec}R
at its closed
point,
p:B\to\operatorname{Spec}R
the canonical projection, and
E=\operatorname{Proj}\left(\bigoplus\mathfrak{m}^{n}/\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}\right)
the fiber of
p
over
\{\mathfrak{m}\}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

In the setup above,
R
has an Ulrich module if and only if there exists an
Ulrich sheaf
\mathcal{U}
on
E
that extends to a coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
B
; in this case,
p_{*}\mathcal{F}
is an
Ulrich
R
-module.

By
\mathcal{U}
extends to
\mathcal{F}
we mean that the former is the derived pullback of the later; see Section
2
. It was previously known that an Ulrich
R
-module gives rise to an Ulrich sheaf on

that extends to
B
. Using this fact, in our joint work with Zhuang
[
8
]
, we constructed examples of two dimensional rings that admit no Ulrich modules. The main point of the current paper is to use the converse to establish a class of two dimensional rings for which Ulrich modules do exist:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
R
be a complete local ring that is equidimensional of dimension two. If the
k
-scheme
E
is geometrically reduced,
then
R
admits an Ulrich module that is locally free of constant rank on the punctured spectrum
\operatorname{Spec}R\setminus\{\mathfrak{m}\}
.

We establish this theorem by proving that any
E
as above admits an Ulrich
bundle
, and verifying that any bundle of
E
extends to
B
, so Theorem
1.1
applies.

In Theorem
1.2
, the assumption that

is reduced is necessary: the counter-examples in
[
8
]
to the existence of Ulrich modules arise from situations where

is an infinitesimal thickening of a smooth curve. The proof of Theorem

, sketched in the previous paragraph, shows that, in fact,

admits no Ulrich bundles.

One consequence of preceding theorem is that the Length Conjecture from
[
7
]
holds for the class of local rings covered by it; see Theorem
5.2
.

2.
Ulrich modules and Ulrich sheaves

In this section we recall standard facts concerning Ulrich modules and Ulrich sheaves required in the sequel.

Ulrich modules

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a noetherian local ring, set
d\coloneqq\dim R
and
let
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module. The minimal number of
generators of
M
is written as
\nu_{R}(M)

and its multiplicity is written as
e_{R}(M)
; that is,

\nu_{R}(M)=\operatorname{rank}_{k}(M/\mathfrak{m}M)\quad\text{and}\quad e_{R}(M)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{d!\,\cdot\operatorname{length}_{R}(M/\mathfrak{m}^{n}M)}{n^{d}}.

When
M
is a maximal Cohen-Macaulay, we have
\nu_{R}(M)\leq e_{R}(M)
; see, for instance,
[
11
, Section 3]
. An

R
-module
M
is an
Ulrich module
if it is nonzero, maximal Cohen-Macaulay, and satisfies
\nu_{R}(M)=e_{R}(M)
.

When the field
k
is infinite one can find a system of parameters
\boldsymbol{x}\coloneqq x_{1},\dots,x_{d}
for
R
that
generate a reduction of
\mathfrak{m}
; that is to say,
(\boldsymbol{x})\mathfrak{m}^{j}=\mathfrak{m}^{j+1}
for
j\gg 0
. The ideal
(\boldsymbol{x})
is said to
be a
minimal reduction
of
\mathfrak{m}
. In this case, a non-zero finitely generated
R
-module
M
is Ulrich if and only
if it is maximal Cohen-Macaulay and
(\boldsymbol{x})M=\mathfrak{m}M
.

It is convenient to extend the notion of Ulrich modules to complexes: We say that an
R
-complex
F
is an
Ulrich module
provided
\operatorname{H}_{i}(F)=0
for all
i\neq 0
and the
R
-module
\operatorname{H}_{0}(F)
is Ulrich. Similarly,
F
is a
k
-vector space
provided
\operatorname{H}_{i}(F)\neq 0
for all
i\neq 0
and
\operatorname{H}_{0}(F)
is annihilated by the maximal ideal of
R
.

Let
K
be the Koszul complex on a system of parameters generating a minimal reduction of
\mathfrak{m}
.
A non-zero, finitely generated
R
-module
M
is Ulrich if and only if
K\otimes_{R}M
is a
k
-vector space.
This fact generalizes to complexes:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
R
be a local ring,
K
the Koszul complex on sequence of elements that generate a minimal reduction of the
maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
R
, and
F
an
R
-complex that is not exact and with
\operatorname{H}_{i}(F)
finitely generated for each
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then
F
is
an Ulrich module if and only if
K\otimes_{R}F
is a

k
-vector space.

Proof.

Let
J=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})
be the given minimal reduction.
If
F
is Ulrich, then

K\otimes_{R}F\simeq K\otimes_{R}\operatorname{H}_{0}(F)\simeq\operatorname{H}_{0}(F)/J\operatorname{H}_{0}(F)=\operatorname{H}_{0}(F)/\mathfrak{m}\operatorname{H}_{0}(F),

a
k
-vector space.

Conversely, assume
K\otimes_{R}F
is a
k
-vector space. Let
K(j)
denote the Koszul complex on
x_{1},\dots,x_{j}
for
0\leq j\leq d
. For each
0<j\leq d
the standard mapping cone exact sequence

0\longrightarrow K(j-1)\longrightarrow K(j)\longrightarrow\Sigma K(j-1)\longrightarrow 0

gives rise to an exact sequence of
R
-modules

\cdots\to\operatorname{H}_{i}(K({j-1})\otimes_{R}F)\xrightarrow{\pm x_{j}}\operatorname{H}_{i}(K({j-1})\otimes_{R}F)\to\operatorname{H}_{i}(K(j)\otimes_{R}F)\to\cdots

Since
K\otimes_{R}F=K(d)\otimes_{F}F
only has homology in degree
0
, by descending induction on
j
and Nakayama’s Lemma, we deduce that
K(j)\otimes_{R}F
has homology only in degree
0
for all
0\leq j\leq d
. The case
j=0
gives an isomorphism in the derived category
F\cong\operatorname{H}_{0}(F)
and thus
K\otimes_{R}\operatorname{H}_{0}(F)
is a
k
-vector space.
As noted above, this is equivalent to
\operatorname{H}_{0}(F)
being an Ulrich module.
∎

Projective schemes

Let
k
be a field. A
projective
k
-scheme
is a pair
(X,\mathcal{L})
where
X
is a
k
-scheme and
\mathcal{L}
is a very ample line bundle on
X
relative to
k
; that is to say, there is a closed immersion of
k
-schemes
i\colon X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}
for some
n
such that
\mathcal{L}\cong i^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}}(1)
. When there is no danger of confusion, we write
\mathcal{L}
as
\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)
or even just
\mathcal{O}(1)
. Given a quasi-coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules, set

\mathcal{F}(j)\coloneqq\mathcal{F}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{L}^{\otimes j}\,.

By
\mathbb{P}^{d}_{k}
we mean the projective
k
-scheme
(\mathbb{P}^{d}_{k},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{d}}(1))
.

A
linear Noether normalization
of a projective
k
-scheme
(X,\mathcal{L})
of dimension
d

is a dominant morphism of
k
-schemes
f\colon X\to\mathbb{P}^{d}_{k}
such that
\mathcal{L}\cong f^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{d}_{k}}(1)
; such a thing exists if
k
is infinite.

Ulrich sheaves

Let
(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(1))
be a projective
k
-scheme and set
d=\dim X
.

An
Ulrich sheaf
on
X
is a nonzero coherent sheaf
\mathcal{U}
such that, for each
t
in
[-d,-1]
, the sheaf
\mathcal{U}(t)
has no cohomology, that is to say,
\operatorname{H}^{i}(X,\mathcal{U}(t))=0
for
i
.

When
d=0
every nonzero coherent sheaf is Ulrich and when
d=1
existence an of Ulrich sheaf is tantamount to existence of a sheaf with no cohomology.

When
d\geq 1
an Ulrich sheaf
\mathcal{U}
on
X
also satisfies
\operatorname{H}^{0}(X,\mathcal{U}(t))=0
for all
t<0
and
\operatorname{H}^{d}(X,\mathcal{U}(t))=0
for all
t\geq-d
. This follows from the definition and the Koszul exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{U}(t-d-1)\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow\mathcal{U}(t-2)^{\binom{d+1}{2}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{U}(t-1)^{d+1}\longrightarrow\mathcal{U}(t)\longrightarrow 0.

See, for instance,
[
1
, Proposition 2.1]
for details.

A coherent sheaf
\mathcal{U}
on
\mathbb{P}^{d}_{k}
is Ulrich if and only if it is isomorphic to
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{d}}^{r}
for some
r\geq 1
; see
[
1
, Proposition 2.1]
. By the projection formula this generalizes to the following statement.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
X
be a projective
k
-scheme and
f\colon X\to\mathbb{P}^{d}_{k}
a linear Noether normalization. A coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
X
is Ulrich if and only if
f_{*}\mathcal{F}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{d}}^{r}
for some
r\geq 1
. ∎

As in the affine case, we say that a complex
\mathcal{G}
of quasi-coherent sheaves over
X
is
Ulrich
if
\operatorname{H}^{i}(\mathcal{G})=0
for
i\neq 0
and the sheaf
\operatorname{H}^{0}(\mathcal{G})
is a Ulrich.

With the notation as above, given a coherent sheaf

3.
Ulrich modules from Ulrich sheaves

We keep the notation from the previous section:
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
is a local ring and
p\colon B\to\operatorname{Spec}R
is the blowup of
\operatorname{Spec}R
at
\{\mathfrak{m}\}
, with exceptional fiber
E
; as before
j\colon E\to B
is the canonical inclusion. Given a finitely generated
R
-module
M
, its
strict transform
is the coherent sheaf on
B
associated to the graded
\mathscr{R}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
-module
\mathscr{R}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)=\bigoplus_{i}\mathfrak{m}^{i}M
. The result below is a more precise version of Theorem
1.1
. Part (1) is
[
8
, Lemma 2.2]
, and included here for ease of reference.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
R
be a local ring with infinite residue field
k
. The following statements hold.

(1)

If
M
is an Ulrich
R
-module, then the coherent sheaf on
E
associated to the graded module
\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}M
is an Ulrich sheaf on
E
that extends to the strict transform of
M
on
B
.

(2)

If
\mathcal{U}
is an Ulrich sheaf on
E
and
\mathcal{F}
is an extension of
\mathcal{U}
to
B
,
then
\mathbf{R}p_{*}\mathcal{F}
is an Ulrich
R
-module.

It may help to illustrate the result above in an example.

Example 3.2
.

Suppose
\dim E=0
, so that every nonzero coherent sheaf on
E
is Ulrich. Then
B=\operatorname{Spec}A
is an affine scheme and
\mathfrak{m}A=(s)
where
s
is not a zerodivisor. Part (2) of the theorem asserts that if
M
is finitely generated and
s
-torsion free

-module, then it is Ulrich as an

-module.

Indeed, since
k
is infinite there exists an element

with
x\mathfrak{m}^{j}=\mathfrak{m}^{j+1}
for

. Hence
x(\mathfrak{m}A)^{j}=(\mathfrak{m}A)^{j+1}
, that is to say,
(xs^{j})=(s^{j+1})

. Since

is not a zerodivisor it follows that

for some unit

of

. In particular, if

is

-torsion free, then
it is

-module. Moreover

M/xM=M/sM=M/(\mathfrak{m}A)M=M/\mathfrak{m}M.

Thus

is an Ulrich module.

A Koszul homology calculation

Let
B
be a scheme,
\mathcal{I}
a sheaf of ideals that is locally generated by a non-zero-divisor, and
E
the subscheme of
B
cut out by
\mathcal{I}
. Let
\mathcal{F}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{I}
be a surjection with
\mathcal{F}
locally free of rank
d
, and
g\colon\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{O}_{B}
its composition with the inclusion
\mathcal{I}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{B}
. Let

\mathcal{K}\coloneqq 0\longrightarrow\Lambda^{d}\mathcal{F}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow\Lambda^{2}\mathcal{F}\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{B}\longrightarrow 0

be the Koszul complex on

.

Lemma 3.3
.

With the setup as above, let
j\colon E\hookrightarrow B
the canonical inclusion of schemes. For each integer
0\leq n\leq d
the following statements hold:

(1)

There is an isomorphism
\mathcal{H}_{n}(\mathcal{K})\cong j_{*}j^{*}\mathcal{H}_{n}(\mathcal{K})
.

(2)

The sheaf
j^{*}\mathcal{H}_{n}(\mathcal{K})
is locally free of rank
\binom{d-1}{n}
; in particular,
\mathcal{H}_{d}(\mathcal{K})=0
.

(3)

There is an exact sequence of locally free

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}_{d-n}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}_{2}\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}_{1}\longrightarrow j^{*}\mathcal{H}_{n}(\mathcal{K})\longrightarrow 0

with
\mathcal{E}_{i}\coloneqq\Lambda^{n+i}(j^{*}\mathcal{F})\otimes\mathcal{N}^{i}
, where
\mathcal{N}\coloneqq(j^{*}\mathcal{I})^{-1}
is the normal bundle of

.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{K}
is a sheaf of differential graded algebras, the action of
\mathcal{O}_{B}
on
\mathcal{H}_{n}(\mathcal{K})
factors though the canonical map
\mathcal{O}_{B}\to\mathcal{H}_{0}(\mathcal{K})
and hence we have canonical isomorphisms
\mathcal{H}_{n}(\mathcal{K})\cong j_{*}j^{*}\mathcal{H}_{n}(\mathcal{K})
, which justifies (1).

(2) Let
\mathcal{G}
be the kernel of
g\colon\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{O}_{B}
; it is locally free of rank

. Since
\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{G}\cong\mathcal{I}
is locally free of rank one, for

there are canonical exact sequences

and the differential in the Koszul complex

is the composition of
p
with the inclusion

\Lambda^{n-1}\mathcal{G}\otimes\mathcal{I}\hookrightarrow\Lambda^{n-1}\mathcal{F}

induced by the inclusions of
\mathcal{I}
into
\mathcal{O}_{B}
and
\mathcal{G}
into
\mathcal{F}
. We may thus identify
\mathcal{H}_{n}(\mathcal{K})
with the cokernel of the inclusion

\Lambda^{n}\mathcal{G}\otimes\mathcal{I}\hookrightarrow\Lambda^{n}\mathcal{G}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{B}\cong\Lambda^{n}\mathcal{G}

and hence deduce that there are isomorphisms

j^{*}\mathcal{H}_{n}(\mathcal{K})\cong j^{*}\Lambda^{n}\mathcal{G}\cong\Lambda^{n}(j^{*}\mathcal{G})

for

. This justifies (2).

(3) The Koszul complex associated to the surjection

has the form

0\to\Lambda^{d}\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{I}^{1-d}\to\cdots\to\Lambda^{3}\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{I}^{-2}\to\Lambda^{2}\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{I}^{-1}\to\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{I}\to 0\,,

which is an exact sequence of locally free sheaves. It follows from the exact sequences (
3.4
) that for each
0\leq n\leq d
, the
n
th syzygy in the exact sequence above is

, so we get an exact sequence

of locally free

yields (3).
∎

\mathcal{K}=p^{*}K=K_{\mathcal{O}_{B}}(\boldsymbol{x})\,;

this is a complex of coherent sheaves on
B
. In the statement below

\mathrm{Perf}(E)
is the derived category of perfect complexes over

E
and
\operatorname{thick}(S)
refers to the smallest triangulated subcategory of a
triangulated category that is closed under direct summands and that
contains the objects in
S
.

Lemma 3.5
.

With the setup as above, for each integer
1\leq n\leq d-1
, one has an isomorphism
\mathcal{H}_{n}(\mathcal{K})\cong j_{*}j^{*}\mathcal{H}_{n}(\mathcal{K})
, the sheaf
j^{*}\mathcal{H}_{n}(\mathcal{K})
is locally free, and as subcategories of
\mathrm{Perf}(E)
there is an equality

\operatorname{thick}(j^{*}\mathcal{H}_{n}(\mathcal{K}),\dots,j^{*}\mathcal{H}_{d-1}(\mathcal{K}))=\operatorname{thick}(\mathcal{O}_{E}(-1),\dots,\mathcal{O}_{E}(n-d))\,.

In particular, an object
\mathcal{U}
in
\operatorname{D}^{b}(\operatorname{coh}E)
is an Ulrich sheaf if and only if

\mathbf{R}q_{*}(\mathcal{U}\otimes j^{*}\mathcal{H}_{n}(\mathcal{K}))=0\quad\text{for all $1\leq n\leq d-1$.}

Proof.

As noted above,
\mathcal{I}_{E}
is locally generated by a non-zero-divisor and there is a surjection
\mathcal{O}_{B}^{d}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{I}_{E}
. So applying Lemma
3.3
with
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{O}_{B}^{d}
, for each
1\leq n\leq d-1
we obtain isomorphisms
j_{*}j^{*}\mathcal{H}_{n}(\mathcal{K})\cong\mathcal{H}_{n}(\mathcal{K})
, which justifies the first part of the statement. Moreover, there is an exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{O}_{E}(n-d)\to\mathcal{O}_{E}(n-d+1)^{d}\to\cdots\to\mathcal{O}_{E}(-1)^{\binom{d}{n+1}}\to j^{*}\mathcal{H}_{n}(\mathcal{K})\to 0,.

The asserted equality regarding thick subcategories of
\mathrm{Perf}(E)
follows.

The final assertion holds since, for any
\mathcal{U}
in
\operatorname{D}^{b}(\operatorname{coh}E)
, the collection

\{\mathcal{F}\in\mathrm{Perf}(E)\mid\mathbf{R}q_{*}(\mathcal{U}\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}\mathcal{F})=0\}

is a thick subcategory of
\mathrm{Perf}(E)
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

As noted earlier, part (1) is
[
8
, Lemma 2.2]
, so we focus on part (2). Recall that
K
is the Koszul complex on minimal reduction
\mathfrak{m}
and that
\mathcal{K}
is its pull-back to
B
. By the projection formula, we have

\mathbf{R}p_{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes K\simeq\mathbf{R}p_{*}(\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{K}),

and so by Lemma

it suffices to prove that
\mathbf{R}p_{*}(\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{K})
is a
k
-vector space.

By assumption,
\mathbf{L}^{i}j^{*}\mathcal{F}=0
for all

. Moreover,
\mathcal{H}_{n}(\mathcal{K})\cong j_{*}j^{*}\mathcal{H}_{n}(\mathcal{K})
with

locally free, by the first part of Lemma

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}j_{*}j^{*}\mathcal{H}_{p}(\mathcal{K})

\displaystyle\simeq j_{*}(\mathbf{L}j^{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}j^{*}\mathcal{H}_{p}(\mathcal{K}))

\displaystyle\simeq j_{*}(\mathcal{U}\otimes j^{*}\mathcal{H}_{p}(\mathcal{K}))

in the derived category of

\operatorname{\mathcal{T}or}^{\mathcal{O}_{B}}_{q}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{H}_{p}(\mathcal{K}))\Longrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{p+q}(\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{K})

collapses to yield an isomorphism
\mathcal{H}_{n}(\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{K})\cong j_{*}\left(\mathcal{U}\otimes j^{*}\mathcal{H}_{n}(\mathcal{K})\right)

By the last part of Lemma

\mathbf{R}p_{*}\mathcal{H}_{n}(\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{K})\cong\begin{cases}\iota_{*}q_{*}\mathcal{U}&n=0\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

We thus have an quasi-isomorphism of complexes of

-modules

\mathbf{R}p_{*}(\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{K})\simeq\iota_{*}q_{*}\mathcal{U}\,,

and the term on the right is a

∎

.

4.
Ulrich modules on dimension two local rings

In this section we establish the existence of Ulrich modules on
certain two-dimensional local rings. Recall that a
k
-scheme is
said to be
geometrically reduced
provided
X\times_{\operatorname{Spec}(k)}\operatorname{Spec}(\overline{k})
is reduced, where
\overline{k}
is the algebraic closure of
k
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(1))
be projective
k
-scheme of finite type, with
\dim X=1
. If
X
is geometrically reduced and equidimensional, then
X
admits an Ulrich vector bundle; if in addition
k
is algebraically closed,
X
admits an Ulrich line bundle.

Proof.

Suppose that
k
is algebraically closed. We need find a line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on
X
with no cohomology, that is to say, with
\operatorname{H}^{i}(X,\mathcal{L})=0
for all
i
, for then
\mathcal{L}(-1)
is an Ulrich sheaf on
X
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a line bundle on
X
with
\operatorname{H}^{1}(X,\mathcal{L})=0
; for instance,
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{O}_{E}(m)
for
m\gg 0
.
If
\operatorname{H}^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})=0
we are done. If not, choose a nonzero element
f\in\operatorname{H}^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})
. By a
zero
of
f
we mean a closed point
x
of
X
such that lies in the kernel of the canonical map
\operatorname{H}^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})\to\operatorname{H}^{0}(X,i^{*}i_{*}\mathcal{L})\cong k
, where
i\colon\{x\}\to X
is the inclusion map.

We claim that
Z(f)
, the set of zeroes of
f
, is a proper, Zariski closed subset of the set of all closed points of
X
.

To see this, let
\{U_{i}\}
be an open covering of
X
by non-empty, affine open subsets on which the bundle
\mathcal{L}
is trivial. For each
U=U_{i}
in this collection we have
U=\operatorname{Spec}A
for some reduced one-dimensional noetherian ring
A
. Upon choosing a trivialization of the restriction of
\mathcal{L}
to
U
, the element
f
is given by an element
g\in A
. The set
Z(f)\cap U
corresponds to
Z(g)
, the set of maximal ideals of
A
containing
g
, which is clearly a closed subset of
\operatorname{Spec}A
. This proves
Z(f)
is closed. Since
f
is nonzero, there is at least one
i
such that the restriction of
f
to
U=U_{i}
is nonzero, and for this
i
the corresponding
g\in A
is nonzero. Since
A
is reduced,
V(g)
is a proper subset of the set of maximal ideals of
A
. It follows that
Z(f)\neq X
.

Let
V
be the set of regular closed points of
X
. Since
X
is reduced,
V
is an open, dense subset of
X
and thus
V\cap(X\setminus Z(f))\neq\emptyset
.
Pick any point
x
in this set. So,
x
is a regular closed point of
X
and
x
is not a zero of
f
.

Since
k
is algebraically closed,
\operatorname{H}^{0}(X,i^{*}i_{*}\mathcal{L})
is a one-dimensional
k
-vector space, and hence the nonzero map
\operatorname{H}^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})\to\operatorname{H}^{0}(X,i^{*}i_{*}\mathcal{L})
must
be surjective. Since
x
is a regular point, and the local ring
\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
is of dimension 1 (this is where one needs that
X
is equidimensional) the kernel
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
of the canonical map
\mathcal{L}\twoheadrightarrow i_{*}i^{*}\mathcal{L}
is also a line bundle. The exact sequence in cohomology

0\to\operatorname{H}^{0}(X,\mathcal{L}^{\prime})\to\operatorname{H}^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})\to\operatorname{H}^{0}(X,i_{*}i^{*}\mathcal{L})\to\operatorname{H}^{1}(X,\mathcal{L}^{\prime})\to\operatorname{H}^{1}(X,\mathcal{L})\to 0

thus shows that
\operatorname{H}^{1}(X,\mathcal{L}^{\prime})=0
and
\dim_{k}\operatorname{H}^{0}(X,\mathcal{L}^{\prime})=\dim_{k}\operatorname{H}^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})-1
. Continuing in this fashion we arrive at a bundle with no cohomology, as desired.

Suppose
k
is general field. Set

X_{\overline{k}}\coloneqq X\times_{\operatorname{Spec}(k)}\operatorname{Spec}(\overline{k})\,;

this is reduced, by hypothesis. Since
X
is equidimensional and the map
X_{\overline{k}}\to X
is flat and integral,
X_{\overline{k}}
is
equidimensional as well. Hence
X_{\overline{k}}
admits an Ulrich line bundle
\mathcal{L}
, as has been proved already. This bundle is extended
from a line bundle
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
on
X_{\ell}
for some finite extension
\ell
of
k
contained in
\overline{k}
, in the sense that

\mathcal{L}=p^{*}\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
where
p\colon X_{\overline{k}}\to X_{\ell}
is the canonical map. Since

\operatorname{H}^{*}(X_{\ell},\mathcal{L}^{\prime}(-1))\otimes_{\ell}\overline{k}\cong\operatorname{H}^{*}(X_{\overline{k}},\mathcal{L}(-1)),

it follows that
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
is an Ulrich line bundle on
X_{\ell}
.

The canonical map
q\colon X_{\ell}\to X
is finite and linear, that is to say,
q^{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)\cong\mathcal{O}_{X_{\ell}}(1)
, and hence
q_{*}\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
is a Ulrich sheaf on
X
. Moreover,
it is locally free with constant rank equal to the degree of the field extension
\ell/k
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

The hypotheses of Lemma
4.1
of imply
X
is Cohen-Macaulay. When
X
is not Cohen-Macaulay, there cannot be any Ulrich bundles on
X
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a complete local ring of dimension two with
k
infinite. Assume
R
is equidimensional and that
\operatorname{Proj}(\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R))
is geometrically reduced. Then
R
admits an Ulrich module
U
that is locally free of constant rank
r>0
on the punctured spectrum
\operatorname{Spec}R\setminus\{\mathfrak{m}\}
; if
k
is algebraically closed, then such a module exits with
r=1
.

Proof.

Set
E=\operatorname{Proj}(\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R))
. The ring
R
, being complete, is universally catenary, and since it is assumed to be equidimensional,
E
is also equidimensional; see, for instance,

[
6
, Proposition 5.4.8]
. By assumption
E
is geometrically reduced, and Lemma
4.1
applies to give an Ulrich
vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
E
of constant rank
r>0
; when
k
is algebraically closed we may take
r=1
. Since
\dim E=1
, by
Corollary
2.6
there is a coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
B
that is locally free of rank
r
and such that

\mathcal{E}\cong\mathbf{L}j^{*}\mathcal{F}
. By Theorem
3.1
,
U\coloneqq p_{*}\mathcal{F}
is an Ulrich module on
R
. The coherent sheaf on

\operatorname{Spec}(R)\setminus\{\mathfrak{m}\}
given as the restriction of
U
coincides with the restriction of
\mathcal{F}
to
B\setminus E
under the
canonical isomorphism
B\setminus E\cong\operatorname{Spec}(R)\setminus\{\mathfrak{m}\}
. So, since
\mathcal{F}
is locally free of constant rank
r
on

B
, the module
U
is locally free of constant rank
r
on the punctured spectrum.
∎

5.
Modules of finite projective dimension

In this section we give an application of the existence of Ulrich modules for certain two-dimensional local rings to the
Length Conjecture
stated in
[
7
]
. First, some notation: We write
G_{0}(R)
for the Grothendieck of
R
; that is,
G_{0}(R)
is the abelian group generated by isomorphism classes of finitely generated
R
-modules modulo relations coming from short exact sequences of such. Set
G_{0}(R)_{\mathbb{Q}}=G_{0}(R)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
.

Set
\chi(N)=\sum_{i}\operatorname{length}_{R}H_{i}(N)
for a bounded complex
N
with finite length homology, and for a finite free complex
F
having finite length homology and a finitely generated module
M
, set

\chi(F,M)=\chi(F\otimes_{Q}M)\,.

For a fixed
F
,
\chi(F,-)
is additive on short exact sequences and thus we may extend its defintion to

\chi(F,\alpha)\in\mathbb{Q}
for any
\alpha\in G_{0}(R)_{\mathbb{Q}}
.
We write
E(R)_{\mathbb{Q}}
for the quotient of
G_{0}(R)_{\mathbb{Q}}
modulo classes that are numerically equivalent to zero; that is to
say, modulo classes
\alpha\in G_{0}(R)_{\mathbb{Q}}
such that
\chi(F,\alpha)=0
for all finite free complexes
F
having finite length
homology.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
R
be local ring that is a homomorphic image of a regular local ring and that is equidimensional of dimension two. The map sending a finitely generated
R
-module
M
to the tuple
(length_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}}))_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{minSpec}R}
,
where
\operatorname{minSpec}R
denotes the set of minimal primes of
R
, induces an isomorphism

E(R)_{\mathbb{Q}}\xrightarrow{\cong}\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{minSpec}R}\mathbb{Q}.

Proof.

This follows from
[
9
, Proposition 3.7]
;
see also Proposition
6.5
below.
∎

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a local ring dimension two such that its
\mathfrak{m}
-adic completion is equidimensional and the projective
k
-scheme
\operatorname{Proj}(\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R))
is geometrically reduced. For any finite free
R
-complex
F=0\to F_{2}\to F_{1}\to F_{0}\to 0
that is minimal and has non-zero, finite length homology, one has

\chi(F)\geq e(R)\cdot\max\left\{\operatorname{rank}F_{0},\frac{\operatorname{rank}F_{1}}{2},\operatorname{rank}F_{2}\right\}.

Proof.

By passing to the completion of
R
we may assume it is complete. There exists a faithfully flat local and integral extension
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)\subseteq(R^{\prime},\mathfrak{m}^{\prime},k^{\prime})
such
\mathfrak{m}R^{\prime}=\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
and
k^{\prime}
is an infinite algebraic field extension of
k
. Then

\operatorname{Proj}(\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}}R^{\prime})=\operatorname{Proj}(\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R))\times_{\operatorname{Spec}k}\operatorname{Spec}k^{\prime}\,,

and hence it is also geometrically reduced. Since
\chi^{R}(F)=\chi^{R^{\prime}}(R^{\prime}\otimes_{R}F)
, passing to
R^{\prime}
we may assume also that the residue field of
R
is infinite.

By Theorem
4.3
,
R
admits an Ulrich module
U
such that for some
r>0
, the
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
-module
U_{\mathfrak{p}}
is free of rank
r
for each minimal prime
\mathfrak{p}
. By Proposition
5.1
, one has
[U]=r[R]
in
E(R)_{\mathbb{Q}}
, and from the formula

e_{R}(M)=\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{minSpec}R}\operatorname{length}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})e(R/\mathfrak{p})\,,

we have
e_{R}(U)=r\cdot e(R)
. Combining these facts gives

(5.3)

\chi(F\otimes_{R}U)=r\cdot\chi(F)=\frac{e_{R}(U)\chi(F)}{e(R)}.

An argument in
[
7
]
completes the proof: Let
K
be the Koszul complex on a minimal reduction of the maximal ideal. Since
U
is maximum Cohen-Macaulay, we have a quasi-isomorphism
U\otimes_{R}F\simeq\operatorname{H}_{0}(U\otimes_{R}F)
and since
U
is Ulrich we have a quasi-isomorphism
K\otimes_{R}U\simeq k^{e_{R}(U)}
. Thus

K\otimes_{R}\operatorname{H}_{0}(U\otimes_{R}F)\simeq k^{e_{R}(U)}\otimes_{R}F,

and, since
F
is minimal and
\operatorname{rank}_{R}(K_{i})=\binom{2}{i}
, this gives

\binom{2}{i}\operatorname{length}_{R}\operatorname{H}_{0}(U\otimes_{R}F)\geq e_{R}(U)\operatorname{rank}_{R}(F_{i}),\,\text{ for $0\leq i\leq 2$.}

On the other hand, from (
5.3
) we have

\operatorname{length}_{R}\operatorname{H}_{0}(U\otimes_{R}F)=\chi(F\otimes_{R}U)=\frac{e_{R}(U)\chi(F)}{e(R)}

and the result follows.
∎

Corollary 5.4
.

For
R
as in Theorem
5.2
, and any nonzero
R
-module
M
of finite projective dimension, one has

\operatorname{length}_{R}M\geq e(R)\max\left\{\beta_{0}(M),\frac{\beta_{1}(M)}{2},\beta_{2}(M)\right\}.

Proof.

We can assume
\operatorname{length}_{R}M
is finite, and then the ring
R
has to be Cohen-Macaulay, and the projective dimension of
M
equals
\dim R
. Now one can apply Theorem
5.2
(2) to the minimal free resolution of
M
.
∎

6.
The non-equidimensional case

In this section we extend our results in the previous section to local rings of dimension two that are not equidimensional, by passing to quotients that are. We start with some generalities: For a local ring
R
, following
[
5
, Definition 2.1]
, let
j(R)
be the largest ideal whose dimension, as an
R
-module, is strictly less than
\dim R
. Equivalently, if

0=P_{1}\cap\cdots\cap P_{m}\cap Q_{1}\cap\cdots\cap Q_{n}

a primary decomposition of
0
in
R
, ordered so that the prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}_{i}\coloneq\sqrt{P_{i}}
and
\mathfrak{q}_{j}\coloneq\sqrt{Q_{j}}
satisfy

\dim(R/\mathfrak{p}_{i})=\dim R\quad\text{and}\quad\dim(R/\mathfrak{q}_{j})<\dim R\\
,

then
j(R)=P_{1}\cap\cdots\cap P_{m}
. Since the images of the
P_{i}
’s give a primary decomposition of
0
in
R/j(R)
,
we have that
R/j(R)
is equidimensional, with
\dim R/j(R)=\dim R
, and has no embedded primes. Moreover, since
\dim j(R)<\dim R
, we have
e(R/j(R))=e(R)
. In fact,
R/j(R)
is the smallest quotient of
R
having these
properties. Note also that for any prime
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R\setminus V
where
V=V(\mathfrak{q}_{1})\cup\cdots\cup V(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
, the natural quotient map
R\to R/j(R)
induces an isomorphism
R_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong(R/j(R))_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

Lemma 6.1
.

For any local ring
R
, there is a bijection between the isomorphism classes of maximal Cohen-Macaulay (respectively, Ulrich) modules on
R
and maximal Cohen-Macaulay (respectively, Ulrich) modules on
R/j(R)
given by restriction of scalars.

Proof.

Set
J=j(R)
and
\bar{R}=R/J
.
Every maximal Cohen-Macaulay module over
\bar{R}
is also maximal Cohen-Macaulay over
R
since

\dim(\bar{R})=\dim R
. For any
\bar{R}
-module
U
, it is clear from definitions that
\nu_{R}(U)=\nu_{\bar{R}}(U)
and

e_{R}(U)=e_{\bar{R}}(U)
. In particular, if
U
an Ulrich

\bar{R}
-module then it is also Ulrich as an
R
-module.

It remains to show that each maximal Cohen-Macaulay
R
-module
M
satisfies
JM=0
. Since

JM\subset M
the associated primes of
JM
are subset of those of
M
, and hence a subset of
\{\mathfrak{p}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{p}_{m}\}
, as
M
is maximal Cohen-Macaulay. But
J_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}}=0
for each
i
since
\dim J<\dim R
, and thus
JM=0
.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

Assume
R
is universally catenary local ring (for example, a complete local ring) of dimension two and let
\bar{R}=R/j(R)
.
Set
E=\operatorname{Proj}\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
and
\bar{E}=\operatorname{Proj}\operatorname{gr}_{\bar{\mathfrak{m}}}\bar{R}
.
Let
E=E_{1}\amalg E_{0}
where
E_{i}
is the union of the connected components of
E
having dimension
i
.
If
E_{1}
is reduced then
\bar{E}=E_{1}
.

Proof.

Let
S\coloneq\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
and
\bar{S}\coloneq\operatorname{gr}_{\bar{\mathfrak{m}}}\bar{R}
. Since
R
is universally catenary, so is
\bar{R}
, and so since
\bar{R}
is equidimensional,
we have that
\bar{S}
is also equidimensional; see
[
6
, Proposition 5.4.8]
. So,
\bar{E}
has no connected components of dimension

0
; that is,
\bar{E}
is a closed subscheme of
E_{1}
.

Suppose
E_{1}
is reduced.
Since
R
and
\bar{R}
have the same dimension and multiplicity, the Hilbert polynomials of the graded rings
S
and
\bar{S}

have the same degree and leading coefficient. It follows that the kernel

of the canonical surjection
S\twoheadrightarrow\bar{S}
satisfies
\dim I<\dim S=\dim\bar{S}=2
. Sheafifying, this gives that the kernel
\mathcal{I}
of
\mathcal{O}_{E}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\bar{E}}
is given by an ideal of dimension
0
on each open affine subset.
Let
\operatorname{Spec}A=U
be any affine open subset of

. Since
A
is reduced
\dim I=\dim A
for all non-zero ideals

. Thus
\mathcal{I}(U)=0
and
U\cap\bar{E}=U
for all such

.
∎

The following generalizes Theorem
4.3
:

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
R
be a complete local ring of dimension two such that each connected component of
E=\operatorname{Proj}\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
of dimension

R
admits an Ulrich module that is locally free of constant rank on
\operatorname{Spec}R\setminus V

V=\{\mathfrak{m}\}\cup\{\mathfrak{q}\mid\text{ $\mathfrak{q}$ is minimal with $\dim R/\mathfrak{q}=1$}\}
.

Proof.

Let
\bar{R}=R/j(R)
, set
E=\operatorname{Proj}\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
, and let
E_{1}
be the union of the connected components of
E
having dimension one. By assumption,
E_{1}
is geometrically reduced, and so in particular it is reduced, and thus by Lemma
6.2
,
\bar{E}=E_{1}
. It follows that
\bar{E}
is geometrically reduced. Theorem
4.3
thus applies to give the existence of an Ulrich module
U
on
\bar{R}
that is locally free of constant rank on the punctured spectrum. The result now follows from Lemma
6.1
and the fact that
R_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong\bar{R}_{\mathfrak{p}}
for all primes
\mathfrak{p}
not in
V
.
∎

We next generalize Theorem
5.2
to allow for rings that may not be equidimensional. A straightforward generalization is not available, since
\chi(F)
can be negative for
F
as in
op. cit.
if the ring
R
is not equidimensional; here is a simple example.

Example 6.4
.

Let
k
be a field and
R=k[[x,y,z]]/(xz,yz)
. This ring is not equidimensional, as its minimal primes are
\mathfrak{p}=(x,y)
and
\mathfrak{q}=(z)
with
\dim(R/\mathfrak{p})=1
and
\dim(R/\mathfrak{q})=2
. Fix an integer
n\geq 1
and let
F
be the finite free complex

0\longrightarrow R\xrightarrow{\begin{bmatrix}-y\\
x-z^{n}\\
\end{bmatrix}}R^{2}\xrightarrow{\begin{bmatrix}x+z&y\end{bmatrix}}R\longrightarrow 0\,.

Writing
K(\boldsymbol{x};M)
for the Koszul complex on a sequence
\boldsymbol{x}
with coefficients in an
R
-module
M
, one has isomorphisms of complexes

F/\mathfrak{p}F\cong K(z;k[[z]])\oplus\Sigma K(z^{n};k[[z]])\quad\text{and}\quad F/\mathfrak{q}F\cong K(x,y;k[[x,y]])\,.

Thus
F
has finite length homology, with
\chi(F/\mathfrak{p}F)=1-n
and
\chi(F/\mathfrak{q}F)=1
. Since the cokernel
C
of the canonical injection
R\hookrightarrow R/\mathfrak{p}\oplus R/\mathfrak{q}
has finite length, one has
\chi(F\otimes_{R}C)=0
and hence

\chi(F)=\chi(F/\mathfrak{p}F)+\chi(F/\mathfrak{q}F)=2-n

which is negative for
n\geq 3
.

The correct invariant to use for rings that need not be equidimensional is
the
Dutta multiplicity
,
\chi_{\infty}(F)
, whose definition is recalled below. In the previous example,
\chi_{\infty}(F)=1
.

Let
R
be a local ring that can be written as the quotient of a regular local ring; this holds, for example, when
R
is a complete local ring. The rationalized Grothendieck group of
R
admits a
weight decomposition

G_{0}(R)_{\mathbb{Q}}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\dim R}G_{0}(R)_{(i)}

arising from the Riemann-Roch isomorphism

\tau\colon\mathrm{CH}_{*}(R)_{\mathbb{Q}}\xrightarrow{\cong}G_{0}(R)_{\mathbb{Q}}

by setting
G_{0}(R)_{(i)}=\tau(\mathrm{CH}_{i}(R)_{\mathbb{Q}})
; see
[
9
]
. Given a finitely generated
R
-module
M
, one can then decompose its class in
G_{0}(R)
as

[M]=\sum_{i}[M]_{(i)}\,,\quad\text{with $[M]_{(i)}$ in $G_{0}(R)_{(i)}$.}

We call
M_{(i)}
the
weight
i
component
of
[M]
.

Recall that
E(R)_{\mathbb{Q}}
is the quotient of the rationalized Grothendieck group
G_{0}(R)_{\mathbb{Q}}
by classes that are numerically equivalent to zero. The weight decomposition descends to
E(R)_{\mathbb{Q}}
, giving a decomposition

E(R)_{\mathbb{Q}}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\dim R}E(R)_{(i)}.

These decompositions enjoy the following properties; see
[
9
, Proposition 3.7]
for proofs. We write
\operatorname{minSpec}R
for the set
of minimal prime ideals of
R
.

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
R
be local ring that is a homomorphic image of a regular local ring. Then:

(1)

For a finitely generated
R
-module
M
, we have
[M]_{(i)}=0
for
i>\dim_{R}M
.

(2)

When
\operatorname{char}(R)=p>0
and
R
is
F
-finite with perfect residue field,

G_{0}(R)_{(i)}
is the eigenspace of eigenvalue
p^{i}
for the operator on
G_{0}(R)_{\mathbb{Q}}
induced by restriction of scalars along the Frobenius endomorphism.

(3)

With
V=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R\mid\dim(R/\mathfrak{p})=\dim R\}
, the map

E(R)_{(\dim R)}\xrightarrow{\cong}\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{p}\in V}\mathbb{Q}

sending
M
to
(\operatorname{length}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}M_{\mathfrak{p}})_{\mathfrak{p}\in V}
is an isomorphism.

(4)

When
R
is equidimensional of dimension two,
E(R)_{(0)}=0=E(R)_{(1)}
.

Definition 6.6
.

Let
R
be a complete local ring
R
. The
Dutta multiplicity
of a finite free
R
-complex
F
having finite length homology
is

\chi^{R}_{\infty}(F)=\chi(F,[R]_{(\dim R)}).

For an arbitrary local ring
R
, we define

\chi^{R}_{\infty}(F)=\chi^{\hat{R}}_{\infty}(\hat{R}\otimes_{R}F).

Remark 6.7
.

When
R
is a complete local ring of positive characteristic
p
with perfect residue field, one has

\chi_{\infty}(F)=\lim_{e\to\infty}\frac{\chi(\phi^{e}F)}{p^{de}}

where
\phi^{e}
is the
e
-th iterate of extension of scalars along the Frobenius; for a proof, see, for instance,
[
10
, pp. 429]
.

The following is an immediate consequence of part (4) of Proposition
6.5
.

Corollary 6.8
.

If
R
has dimension two and its completion is equidimensional, then

\chi_{\infty}(F)=\chi(F)
. ∎

Theorem 6.9
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a local ring of dimension two. If the connected components of
\operatorname{Proj}(\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R))
of dimension one are geometrically reduced, then for any finite free
R
-complex
F=0\to F_{2}\to F_{1}\to F_{0}\to 0
that is minimal and has
non-zero, finite length homology, one has

\chi_{\infty}(F)\geq e(R)\cdot\max\left\{\operatorname{rank}F_{0},\frac{\operatorname{rank}F_{1}}{2},\operatorname{rank}F_{2}\right\}.

Proof.

As in the proof of Theorem
5.2
, we may assume
R
is complete with infinite residue field. Let
\bar{R}=R/j(R)
, a complete, equidimensional local ring of dimension
2
with
e(R)=e(\bar{R})
. As
\dim j(R)<2
,
we have
[R]_{(2)}=[\bar{R}]_{(2)}
in
G_{0}(R)_{\mathbb{Q}}
and thus

\chi_{\infty}(F)=\chi_{\infty}(\bar{R}\otimes_{R}F)=\chi(\bar{R}\otimes_{R}F)\,.

The second equality coming from Corollary
6.8
. As in the proof of Theorem
6.3
, it follows from Lemma
6.2
that
\operatorname{Proj}(\operatorname{gr}_{\bar{\mathfrak{m}}}\bar{R})
is geometrically reduced. The inequality thus follows from Theorem
5.2
applied to
\bar{R}
and
F\otimes_{R}\bar{R}
.
∎

A finite 6d supergravity landscape from anomalies Preprint: KEK-TH-2741
Yuta Hamada, and Gregory J. Loges

Abstract
6d supergravities with non-abelian gauge group are subject to many consistency conditions. While the absence of local gauge and gravitational anomalies allows for infinitely many models, we show that those conditions stemming from the absence of both local and global anomalies together are strong enough to leave only finitely many consistent models. To do this we distill the consequences of anomaly cancellation into a high-dimensional linear program whose dual can be efficiently studied using standard techniques. We obtain a universal bound on the number of tensor multiplets T ≤ 11 ⋅ 273 = 3003 T\leq 11\cdot 273=3003 and show that this leads to a finite landscape of consistent non-abelian models. Interestingly, the model which saturates this bound has gauge group [ E 8 × F 4 × ( G 2 × SU ⁡ ( 2 ) ) 2 ] 273 [E_{8}\times F_{4}\times(G_{2}\times\SU(2))^{2}]^{273} , which bears a striking resemblance to the model which saturates the bound T ≤ 193 T\leq 193 for F-theory constructions.

Introduction

It has long been appreciated that amongst all low-energy effective theories only a very small fraction are compatible with the principles of quantum gravity.
Several general arguments have been made that the quantum gravity landscape is in fact finite (e.g. see
Vafa:2005ui
;
Acharya:2006zw
;
Grimm:2021vpn
;
Hamada:2021yxy

For example, in ten dimensions minimal supersymmetry and absence of local gauge and gravitational anomalies is enough to essentially fix the gauge group
Green:1984sg
and the short list of possibilities exactly matches those found in string theory, a phenomenon which has come to be known as
string universality
or the
string lamppost principle
.
Ambitiously, one may hope to show that this continues all the way to four dimensions and in the absence of low-energy supersymmetry.

Understanding string universality has since progressed, most successfully for
d>6
and with supersymmetry
Kim:2019ths
;
Montero:2020icj
;
Cvetic:2020kuw
;
Hamada:2021bbz
;
Bedroya:2021fbu
.
Continuing down to
d=6
presents a new challenge since it is at this point that the minimal number of supercharges drops to eight and matter supermultiplets in arbitrary representations first appear.
Much work has gone into understanding the subtleties of the consistency conditions for these models

;
Seiberg:2011dr
;
Monnier:2018nfs

Recently in

\mathcal{N}=(1,0)
supergravities with non-abelian gauge group is in fact finite.
This is thanks to a sharp upper bound on the number of massless modes derived using the presence of a so-called
H-string
in the BPS spectrum.
In particular, they find the bounds
T\leq 193
and

A similar bound of

In the present work we give an argument for the finiteness of this 6d landscape which is complementary to both of these.
Our upper bound on
T

and
Birkar:2025rcg

In the context of 6d supergravity, Dai-Freed anomalies have recently been studied in

Anomaly and string charge lattices

\mathfrak{l}\subseteq\mathfrak{L}

That is,

We will also make use of

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}9-T&\mathcal{u}^{\top}\\
\hline\cr\mathcal{u}&\mathcal{g}\end{array}\right)\,.

(8)

We write the signature of

and

While

\displaystyle\Big\{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{8n+2}\cup\big(\tfrac{1}{2}+\mathbb{Z}\big)^{8n+2}\;\Big|\;\sum\nolimits_{\alpha}x^{\alpha}\equiv 0\mod{2}\Big\}\,,

4
Finiteness

Having obtained a non-trivial lower bound on
T
in section
3
, we now turn to the question of finiteness.
In section
4.1
we use string probes to show that there are only finitely many models for any fixed
T
.
Section
4.2
then brings the various bounds together to derive upper bounds on
T
and identify extremal models.

4.1
String probes, anomaly inflow and unitarity bounds

Introducing a string probe into a background introduces potential anomalies which must be canceled by the string’s world-volume degrees of freedom via the anomaly inflow mechanism.
The central charges and levels of the current algebra were computed in
Kim:2019vuc
for a BPS string of charge
Q\in\Gamma^{\mathrm{BPS}}\subseteq\Gamma
; after removing the center-of-mass contributions, they read

\displaystyle c_{\text{L}}

\displaystyle=3Q^{2}+9Q\cdot b_{0}+2\,,\quad

\displaystyle k_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\big(Q^{2}-Q\cdot b_{0}+2\big)\,,

(62)

\displaystyle c_{\text{R}}

\displaystyle=3Q^{2}+3Q\cdot b_{0}\,,

\displaystyle k_{i}

\displaystyle=Q\cdot b_{i}\,.

When the string tension
\mu_{Q}\propto Q\cdot\mathcal{J}
and all of
c_{\text{L}}
,
c_{\text{R}}
,
k_{\ell}
and
k_{i}
are non-negative, imposing unitarity amounts to the bound

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\frac{k_{i}\dim G_{i}}{k_{i}+h_{i}^{\vee}}\leq c_{\text{L}}\,,

(63)

where
h_{i}^{\vee}
is the dual-Coxeter number of
G_{i}
(see table
1
).

Figure 3:
The growth of
b_{i}^{2}
for low-rank non-abelian groups. For
\SU(2)
the number of models grows very quickly and we have limited
\Delta_{i}\leq 30
.

Along with the above consistency condition, we will need two basic facts.
The first is that there exists some constant
c
for which
\Delta_{a}+cn_{-}^{\mathfrak{l}_{a}}
is bounded below and all irreducible models for which it is non-positive have
n_{-}^{\mathfrak{l}_{a}}>0
.
Empirically, we find that
c=29
works, but the precise value is inconsequential.
The second fact is that there exists a function
h:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle b_{i}^{2}\leq h(\Delta_{i})

(64)

for all irreducible models with simple gauge group.
This is obviously the case since achieving large
b_{i}^{2}
requires having many hypermultiplets which then boosts the value of
\Delta_{i}
.
However, it turns out that
h
must grow quickly with
\Delta_{i}
.
Using the numerical methods developed in
Hamada:2023zol
;
Hamada:2024oap
, we can generate a complete list of simple irreducible models for the lowest-rank groups with
\Delta_{i}\lesssim 150
to observe the growth of
b_{i}^{2}
: see figure
3
.
The maximal values of
b_{i}^{2}
all come from
\SU(2)
with unusually large hypermultiplet representations, such as

\displaystyle G=\SU(2)\,,\qquad\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{ch}}=2\times\mathbf{\underline{2}}\oplus\mathbf{\underline{24}}\,,

(65)

which has
b_{i}^{2}=131,\!941
despite only having
\Delta_{i}=25
.
Of course, with hindsight such extreme examples never appear as a component piece of a consistent model.

We now provide a short argument that for any fixed
T
the number of consistent models is finite.
Work via contradiction; suppose that there are infinitely many distinct consistent models with the same value of
T
.
First, we claim that there are only finitely many possible anomaly lattices
\mathfrak{L}
.
To see this, note that
n_{-}^{\mathfrak{l}_{a}}\leq T
is bounded, so the number of irreducible components with
\Delta_{a}+cn_{-}^{\mathfrak{l}_{a}}<0
is bounded.
This then means that
\Delta_{a}+cn_{-}^{\mathfrak{l}_{a}}
is bounded for each irreducible model
\mathcal{M}_{a}^{\mathrm{irr}}
and thus thanks to (
64
), all of the diagonal elements of
\mathcal{g}
are bounded.
Furthermore, for fixed
b_{i}^{2}
and
b_{j}^{2}
, the choice of
b_{0}\cdot b_{i}
and
b_{i}\cdot b_{j}
is finite.
Consequently,
\mathcal{u}
and the off-diagonal elements of
\mathcal{g}
are both bounded by functions of
\diag\mathcal{g}
. We conclude that there are only finitely many possible Gram matrices
\mathcal{G}
and thus finitely many corresponding lattices.
Therefore we can restrict attention to an infinite subset of consistent models with
\mathfrak{L}
fixed.
Also, thanks to the assumption about the BPS charge lattice there are only finitely many possible
\Gamma^{\mathrm{BPS}}\subseteq\Gamma
, each with full rank, and
\Gamma^{\mathrm{BPS}}\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{1,T}\;|\;x\cdot b_{i}>0\,,x^{2}>0\}
is non-empty because of the existence of the tensor branch scalars
\mathcal{J}
.
Fix
Q
from this intersection, large enough so that
c_{\text{L}}
,
c_{\text{R}}
and
k_{\ell}
are all positive.
Since
c_{\text{L}}
is fixed and
k_{i}\geq 1
for each
i
,

\displaystyle\rank G\leq\sum_{i}\frac{\dim G_{i}}{1+h_{i}^{\vee}}\leq\sum_{i}\frac{k_{i}\dim G_{i}}{k_{i}+h_{i}^{\vee}}\leq c_{\text{L}}

(66)

gives an upper bound on the size of the gauge group.
The precise bound depends implicitly on the upper bound for the index of BPS string charge lattice
\Gamma^{\mathrm{BPS}}
in the string charge lattice
\Gamma
,
[\Gamma:\Gamma^{\mathrm{BPS}}]\coloneqq|\Gamma/\Gamma^{\mathrm{BPS}}|
, which controls the possible choices for
Q
and thus
c_{\text{L}}
,
7
7

7

See
Montero:2022vva
for string theory examples with
[\Gamma:\Gamma^{\mathrm{BPS}}]>1
.
but regardless there are only finitely many possible gauge groups for any fixed
T
.
Further restricting to an infinite subset of models with constant gauge group, now
H_{\mathrm{ch}}\leq 273+V-29T=\text{const.}
is bounded so there are only finitely many choices for charged matter and thus only finitely many distinct models, a contradiction.

4.2
Upper bounds and extremal models

In this section we use the linear programming bound to obtain an upper bound on

Together with the argument of the previous section, this proves that there are only finitely many consistent models.

First of all, every irreducible model

\displaystyle\Delta_{a}\geq-27N_{a}^{(1\perp)}+\underline{\Delta}N_{a}\,.

(67)

For irreducible models with only a single simple factor this is true by definition of
\underline{\Delta}
(

However, the bound also holds for larger irreducible models which can have multi-charged hypermultiplets reducing the value of
\Delta_{a}
compared to the sum of its constituents.
Physically this is obvious: upon Higgsing (and dropping any resulting

Then we have

\displaystyle\Delta+29T

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:bounded-NHCs}}{\geq}-27N^{(1\perp)}+\underline{\Delta}N+29T\,,

(68)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:T-lower-bound}}{\geq}\max_{0\leq\lambda\leq 29}\big[\lambda T+(2-\lambda)N^{(1\perp)}+\underline{\Delta}N+(29-\lambda)f^{\ast}(N)\big]\,,

\displaystyle\overset{\phantom{\eqref{eq:T-lower-bound}}}{=}\max_{0\leq\lambda\leq 29}\Big\{\lambda T+(2-\lambda)N^{(1\perp)}+|\underline{\Delta}N|\left[(29-\lambda)\tfrac{f^{\ast}(\Omega_{N})}{|\underline{\Delta}\Omega_{N}|}-1\right]\Big\}\,,

\displaystyle\overset{\phantom{\eqref{eq:T-lower-bound}}}{\geq}\lambda^{\ast}(S)\,T\,,

(71)

for all models with
\Omega_{N}\in S
.
Determining

5
Discussion

In this work we have explored the extent to which anomaly cancellation alone constrains the space of consistent 6d,
\mathcal{N}=(1,0)
supergravities with non-abelian gauge group.
There are some features of 6d,
\mathcal{N}=(1,0)
supergravities that played an extremely minor role in our analysis.
Most notably, the
\SO(1,T)/\SO(T)
moduli space and BPS strings only appear briefly in section
4.1
to show that
\rank G
is bounded for fixed
T
.
The main actor in showing that the number of consistent models is finite is the high-dimensional linear program of section
3
which allowed us to control the number of tensor multiplets in terms of the number of constituents with
b_{i}^{2}<0
.
In its construction the linear program takes as input that the anomaly vectors
b_{I}\in\mathfrak{L}
with
b_{I}^{2}<0
must take a very particular form in the unimodular lattice
\Gamma
.
We argued for
b_{i}^{0}=0
when
b_{i}^{2}<0
based on the absence of Dai-Freed anomalies when placing the theory on a curved background.
This relied on some numerics to check that only for the simplest choices of the vectors
b_{i}
does the anomaly vanish, and it would be worthwhile to understand if there is an underlying reason for this phenomenon.

The linear programming lower bound on the dimension
T
can be significantly better than the simple bound coming just asking that
\mathfrak{L}
embed in
\Gamma
.
By playing this lower bound off the upper bound
\Delta+29T\leq 273
coming from the gravitational anomaly, we obtained a universal upper bound of
T\leq 3003
.
Imposing additional constraints by hand, such as forbidding
E_{8}
, improves this bound significantly.
For any set of constraints we are able to identify the extremizing solution of the linear program and therefore identify the types of models which come closest to saturating our bounds.
While the resulting extreme models fall far well beyond the bounds of
Kim:2024hxe
, interestingly our methods are still able to correctly identify the “directions” in model space with largest
T
.

A major outstanding question is whether the landscape of consistent
abelian
models is also finite.
F-theory examples with surprisingly large charges are known
Raghuram:2018hjn
and fairly simple infinite families of seemingly consistent models with three and four charges were found in
Taylor:2018khc
.
The Dai-Freed global anomalies for these families were considered in
Basile:2023zng
and it was shown that one can only rule out certain residue classes for the charges.
It remains to be seen whether there is some other consistency condition or principle which can restrict these models to a finite number as well.

The main move in the classification of integer lattices is to replace the ring of integers by

v_{\ast}^{\prime 2}=v_{\ast}^{2}=m(1-m)

As we will show, given

in which

, where the

with the same Gram matrix as the

The map which sends

To this end, we proceed by extending

to a basis

We can check that

for all

and in fact one can easily find

.
Projecting this

in

.

.

of determinant

\langle v_{\ast}^{\prime},e_{2}^{\prime}\rangle

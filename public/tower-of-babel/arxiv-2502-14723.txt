Dynamics of the Drinfeld-Sokolov-Wilson system: well-posedness and (in)stability of the traveling waves Thanks: Stefanov is partially supported by NSF-DMS under grant # 220478.
Ognyan Christov, Sevdzhan Hakkaev, SEUNGLY OH, Atanas G. Stefanov

Abstract
. We analyze the Drinfeld-Sokolob-Wilson system, which features a dispersive, KdV type evolution with a dispersionless conservation law. We establish well-posedness with low regularity initial data L 2 ​ ( 𝕋 ) × L 2 ​ ( 𝕋 ) L^{2}({\mathbb{T}})\times L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}) for the Cauchy problem on periodic background, which is then extrapolated to global solutions, due to L 2 L^{2} conservation law. We also establish a dynamically more relevant result, namely a global persistence of solutions with (large) initial data in H 1 ​ ( 𝕋 ) × L 2 ​ ( 𝕋 ) H^{1}({\mathbb{T}})\times L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}) . This is obtained by following a more sophisticated approach, specifically the method of normal forms. Finally, for a fixed period L L , we construct an explicit one parameter family of periodic waves, see ( 2.9 ) below. We show that they are all spectrally unstable with respect to co-periodic perturbations. Specifically, we show that the Hamiltonian instability index is equal to one, which identifies the instability as a single positive growing mode.

. As

3.
Well-posedness of the DSW system

We start with some preliminaries. First, it is clear that for well-posedness purposes, we can rescale the interval
(0,L)
to be of length
2\pi
. So, we assume that in this section
L=2\pi
and we henceforth denote
{\mathbb{T}}
to be torus
[0,2\pi]
, equipped with the normalized Lebesgue measure.

3.1.
Preliminaries

Given
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
, we denote
f_{n}
to be its Fourier coefficient. Given
u\in C^{0}_{t}L^{2}_{x}\cap L^{2}_{t,x}
, we denote the space-time Fourier transform of
u
by
\widetilde{u}(\tau,k)
. We denote the Fourier inverse by
\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\tau,k}\left[\widetilde{u}(\tau,k)\right]
.

Given two positive quantities
A,B
,
A\lesssim B
means that
A\leq CB
for some
C>0
. The relation
\gtrsim
is analogously defined. If
A\lesssim B
and
A\gtrsim B
, we write
A\sim B
.
A\ll B
(respectivly
A\gg B
) is defined similar to the relation
\lesssim
(resp,
\gtrsim
), except that the implicit constant
C
is assumed to be much larger than 1. For any real number
k
,
\left\langle k\right\rangle:=(1+k^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Given any real numbers
s,b
, fractional differential operators
J_{x}^{s}
or
J_{t}^{b}
are defined as

J_{x}^{s}f=\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{k}\left[\left\langle k\right\rangle^{s}f_{k}\right],\ \ \ J_{t}^{b}u=\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\tau,k}\left[\left\langle\tau\right\rangle^{s}\widetilde{u}(\tau,k)\right].

For any real numbers
s,b
, we define the KdV Bourgain space
X^{s,b}
via the norm:

{\left\|u\right\|}_{X^{s,b}}={\left\|\left\langle k\right\rangle^{s}\left\langle\tau-k^{3}\right\rangle^{b}\widetilde{u}(\tau,k)\right\|}_{L^{2}_{\tau}\ell^{2}_{k}}

For KdV Bourgain spaces, the following embeddings are known:

X^{0,\frac{1}{3}}\hookrightarrow L^{4}_{t,x},\quad X^{0+,\frac{1}{2}+}\hookrightarrow L^{6}_{t,x},\quad X^{0,\frac{1}{2}+}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}

as well as the trivial identity,
X^{0,0}=L^{2}_{t,x}
.

We now define functional spaces for the solution
(u,v)
. The space for
u
is represented by
X^{s,\frac{1}{2}}\cap H^{s}_{x}L^{1}_{\tau}
and the space for
v
is represented by
H_{t}^{\frac{1}{2}}L^{2}_{x}\cap L^{2}_{x}L^{1}_{\tau}
. The space for
(u,v)
is

Y:=\left(X^{s,\frac{1}{2}}\cap H^{s}_{x}L^{1}_{\tau}\right)\times\left(H_{t}^{\frac{1}{2}}L^{2}_{x}\cap L^{2}_{x}L^{1}_{\tau}\right).

The norm of
Y
is defined by

{\left\|(u,v)\right\|}_{Y}={\left\|u\right\|}_{X^{s,\frac{1}{2}}}+{\left\|\left\langle k\right\rangle^{s}\widetilde{u}\right\|}_{\ell^{2}_{k}L^{1}_{\tau}}+{\left\|J_{t}^{\frac{1}{2}}v\right\|}_{L^{2}_{t,x}}+{\left\|\widetilde{v}\right\|}_{\ell^{2}_{k}L^{1}_{\tau}}.

The spaces
X^{s,\frac{1}{2}}
and
H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{t}L^{2}_{x}
barely fails to embed within the target space, which is
C^{0}_{t}H^{s}_{x}
and
C^{0}_{t}L^{2}_{x}
. In such settings, it is typical to intersect these spaces with
H^{s}_{x}L^{1}_{\tau}
since the completion of
H^{s}_{x}L^{1}_{\tau}
from dense smooth functions yields the embedding
H^{s}_{x}L^{1}_{\tau}\hookrightarrow C^{0}_{t}H^{s}_{x}
. Hence,

(u,v)\in Y\hookrightarrow C^{0}_{t}H^{s}_{x}\times C^{0}_{t}L^{2}_{x}

3.2.
Reduction to the case of mean-zero initial data

We precondition (
1.1
) so that we can assume that
v
satisfies the mean-zero condition for the purpose of establishing the well-posedness result. We can achieve this by changing variable
v\to v-\int_{{\mathbf{T}}}v\,dx
in the above equation. Note
\int v\,dx=\int_{{\mathbf{T}}}g\,dx=g_{0}
by mean-conservation for
v
. Then, we have

\left|\begin{array}[]{l}u_{t}+(uv)_{x}-g_{0}u_{x}+u_{xxx}=0,\\
v_{t}+uu_{x}=0\\
u|_{t=0}=f\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T}),\quad v|_{t=0}=g-g_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}).\end{array}\right.

Then, we change variable
u(t,x)\mapsto u(t,x+g_{0}t)
and note

\partial_{t}u(t,x+g_{0}t)=u_{t}(t,x+g_{0}t)+g_{0}u_{x}(t,x+g_{0}t)

Substituting this expression to above, we can remove the extra term
-g_{0}u_{x}
from the equation, recovering the original form of (
1.1
) with an additional assumption that
\int_{\mathbf{T}}v(t,x)\,dx=0
for all
t\geq 0
. The proof of Theorem
1
will proceed with this assumption. The well-posedness of original system prior to conditioning can be easily recovered because these transformations are invertible.

We can formulate (
1.1
) as:

(3.1)

\left|\begin{array}[]{l}u=e^{-t\partial_{x}^{3}}u_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\partial_{x}^{3}}(uv)_{x}\,ds,\\
v=v_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}uu_{x}\,ds.\end{array}\right.

3.3.
Bilinear estimates in Bourgain spaces

We begin by listing known linear estimates in Bourgain spaces:

Lemma 1
.

For any
\eta\in\mathcal{S}_{t}(\mathbb{R})
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
, and
T>0
,

\displaystyle{\left\|\eta(t/T)e^{t\partial_{x}^{3}}u_{0}\right\|}_{X^{s,b}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{b}{\left\|u_{0}\right\|}_{H^{s}_{x}}

\displaystyle{\left\|\eta(t/T)u\right\|}_{X^{s,b^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim T^{b-b^{\prime}}{\left\|u\right\|}_{X^{s,b}}

for
0<b^{\prime}<b\leq\frac{1}{2}
.

In particular, for the second statement, it will be important that we can “spare” some modulation weight within each estimate. That is, whenever we use an embedding of the type
X^{0,b}\hookrightarrow X^{0,b^{\prime}}
for
0<b^{\prime}<b\leq\frac{1}{2}
, we can gain a positive power of
T
. In addition, following properties are well-known:

\displaystyle{\left\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\partial_{x}^{3}}(uv)_{x}(s,\cdot)\,ds\right\|}_{X^{s,\frac{1}{2}}\cap H^{s}_{x}L^{1}_{\tau}}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\left\|(uv)_{x}\right\|}_{X^{s,-\frac{1}{2}}}+{\left\|\left\langle k\right\rangle^{s}\frac{\mathcal{F}\left[(uv)_{x}\right]}{\left\langle\tau-k^{3}\right\rangle}\right\|}_{\ell^{2}_{k}L^{1}_{\tau}}

\displaystyle{\left\|\int_{0}^{t}(uu_{x})(s,\cdot)\,ds\right\|}_{H_{t}^{\frac{1}{2}}L^{2}_{x}\cap L^{2}_{x}L^{1}_{\tau}}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\left\|uu_{x}\right\|}_{H_{t}^{-\frac{1}{2}}L^{2}_{x}}+{\left\|\frac{\mathcal{F}\left[uu_{x}\right]}{\left\langle\tau\right\rangle}\right\|}_{\ell^{2}_{k}L^{1}_{\tau}}

To prove the local well-posedness, we need to show the following bilinear estimates:

Lemma 2
.

Let
0\leq s\leq\frac{1}{2}
. For
(u,v)\in X^{s,\frac{1}{2}}\times H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{t}L^{2}_{x}
,

(3.2)

\displaystyle{\left\|\partial_{x}(uv)\right\|}_{X^{s,-\frac{1}{2}}}+{\left\|\left\langle k\right\rangle^{s}\frac{\mathcal{F}\left[\partial_{x}(uv)\right]}{\left\langle\tau-k^{3}\right\rangle}\right\|}_{\ell^{2}_{k}L^{1}_{\tau}}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\left\|u\right\|}_{X^{s,\frac{1}{2}}}{\left\|v\right\|}_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}

(3.3)

\displaystyle{\left\|\partial_{x}(u^{2})\right\|}_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}+{\left\|\frac{\mathcal{F}\left[uu_{x}\right]}{\left\langle\tau\right\rangle}\right\|}_{\ell^{2}_{k}L^{1}_{\tau}}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\left\|u\right\|}_{X^{0,\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}

Furthermore, we will see that, when introducing a time-cutoff
\eta(t/T)
within the norms of the LHS, we can gain a positive power of
T
on the RHS by sparing some of modulational weight.

Proof.

We first consider (
3.2
). For this estimate, relevant modulational weights are
\left\langle\tau-k^{3}\right\rangle
,
\left\langle\tau_{1}-k_{1}^{3}\right\rangle
and
\left\langle\tau_{2}\right\rangle
where
(\tau_{1},k_{1})
is the Fourier frequency of
u
,
(\tau_{2},k_{2})
is the Fourier frequency of
v
, and
(\tau,k)
is the Fourier frequency of the product
uv
. Hence, these frequencies must satisfy
\tau_{1}+\tau_{2}=\tau
,
k_{1}+k_{2}=k
. Additionally, we can assume that
k_{2}\neq 0
thanks to the mean-zero assumption for
v
. To take advantage of modulational weights, we need to observe the following algebraic identity:

(3.4)

\tau-k^{3}=(\tau_{1}-k_{1}^{3})+\tau_{2}-k_{2}(k_{1}^{2}+k^{2}+k_{1}k),

which leads to the fact:

\max\{\left\langle\tau-k^{3}\right\rangle,\left\langle\tau_{1}-k_{1}^{3}\right\rangle,\left\langle\tau_{2}\right\rangle\}\sim|k_{2}|\,(k_{1}^{2}+k^{2}+k_{1}k)\gtrsim\left\langle k_{2}\right\rangle\max\{k^{2},k_{1}^{2}\}=:H_{1}.

Note that
|k_{2}|\gtrsim\left\langle k_{2}\right\rangle
is possible due to the assumption
k_{2}\neq 0
, and
k_{1}^{2}+k^{2}+k_{1}k\gtrsim\max\{k^{2},k_{1}^{2}\}
is possible due to the algebraic identity

k_{1}^{2}+k^{2}+k_{1}k=\frac{3}{4}k_{1}^{2}+\left(\frac{1}{2}k_{1}+k\right)^{2}=\frac{3}{4}k^{2}+\left(k_{1}+\frac{1}{2}k\right)^{2}

Lastly, note that
H_{1}\neq 0
, since this will require
k=k_{1}=0
, which forces
k_{2}=0
. Based on the relationship stated above, one of the following must be true:

(1)

\left\langle\tau-k^{3}\right\rangle\sim H_{1}

(2)

\left\langle\tau_{1}-k_{1}^{3}\right\rangle\gtrsim H_{1}

(3)

\left\langle\tau_{2}\right\rangle\gtrsim H_{1}

We consider these three cases separately:

Case 1.

\left\langle\tau-k^{3}\right\rangle\sim H_{1}

Note that when restricting
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
so that
C^{-1}H_{1}\leq|\tau-k^{3}|\leq CH_{1}
for some
C\gg 1
,
{\left\||\tau-k^{3}|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right\|}_{L^{2}_{\tau}}
is uniformly bounded and only depends on
C
, and hence
{\left\|\left\langle\tau-k^{3}\right\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right\|}_{L^{2}_{\tau}}
is uniformly bounded. This leads to

{\left\|\left\langle k\right\rangle^{s}\frac{\mathcal{F}_{x,t}\left[\partial_{x}(uv)\right]}{\left\langle\tau-k^{3}\right\rangle}\right\|}_{\ell^{2}_{k}L^{1}_{\tau}}\lesssim{\left\|\left\langle k\right\rangle^{s}\frac{\mathcal{F}_{x,t}\left[\partial_{x}(uv)\right]}{\left\langle\tau-k^{3}\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|}_{\ell^{2}_{k}L^{2}_{\tau}}={\left\|\partial_{x}(uv)\right\|}_{X^{s,-\frac{1}{2}}}

This means that the second term on the LHS of (
3.2
) is controlled by the first term. Hence, it remains to show

{\left\|\partial_{x}(uv)\right\|}_{X^{s,-\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim{\left\|u\right\|}_{X^{s,\frac{1}{2}}}{\left\|v\right\|}_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}

Since
\left\langle\tau-k^{3}\right\rangle\sim H_{1}\geq k^{2}\left\langle k_{2}\right\rangle
, for any
s\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]
,

\left\langle k\right\rangle^{s}\frac{ik}{\left\langle\tau-k^{3}\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim\frac{\left\langle k\right\rangle^{s}}{\left\langle k_{1}\right\rangle^{s}\left\langle k_{2}\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim\min\{|k_{1}|,|k_{2}|\}^{-\frac{1}{2}}

where we have used
\max\{|k_{1}|,|k_{2}|\}\gtrsim|k|
. This estimate yields,

{\left\|\partial_{x}(uv)\right\|}_{X^{s,-\frac{1}{2}}}={\left\|\left\langle k\right\rangle^{s}\frac{ik}{\left\langle\tau-k^{3}\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}}\mathcal{F}_{t,x}[uv]\right\|}_{\ell^{2}_{k}L^{2}_{\tau}}\lesssim{\left\|\left(J_{x}^{s-\frac{1}{2}}u\right)v\right\|}_{L^{2}_{t,x}}+{\left\|\left(J_{x}^{s}u\right)\left(J_{x}^{-\frac{1}{2}}v\right)\right\|}_{L^{2}_{t,x}}

Applying Hölder’s inequality, followed by the Sobolev and
L^{4}_{t,x}
embedding,

\displaystyle{\left\|\left(J_{x}^{s-\frac{1}{2}}u\right)v\right\|}_{L^{2}_{t,x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\left\|J^{s-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}u\right\|}_{L^{4}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x}}{\left\|v\right\|}_{L^{4}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim{\left\|J^{s}_{x}u\right\|}_{L^{4}_{t,x}}{\left\|v\right\|}_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim{\left\|u\right\|}_{X^{s,\frac{1}{3}}}{\left\|v\right\|}_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle{\left\|\left(J_{x}^{s}u\right)\left(J_{x}^{-\frac{1}{2}}v\right)\right\|}_{L^{2}_{t,x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\left\|J^{s}_{x}u\right\|}_{L^{4}_{t,x}}{\left\|J_{x}^{-\frac{1}{2}}v\right\|}_{L^{4}_{t,x}}\lesssim{\left\|u\right\|}_{X^{0,\frac{1}{3}}}{\left\|v\right\|}_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}

which yields the desired RHS. Note that we can gain a positive power of
T
from Lemma
1
, since the final quantity on the RHS above is in terms of
{\left\|u\right\|}_{X^{s,\frac{1}{3}}}
, whereas the RHS of (
3.2
) affords us a stronger norm
{\left\|u\right\|}_{X^{s,\frac{1}{2}}}
.

For the remaining two cases, we know that
\left\langle\tau-k^{3}\right\rangle\not\sim H_{1}
. In this case, we do not need to use the full
\frac{1}{2}
-power of this modulational weight. In fact, we only need to use
\frac{1}{3}
-power of this weight in order to use the
L^{4}_{t,x}
embedding. Note

{\left\|\left\langle k\right\rangle^{s}\frac{\mathcal{F}_{x,t}\left[\partial_{x}(uv)\right]}{\left\langle\tau-k^{3}\right\rangle}\right\|}_{\ell^{2}_{k}L^{1}_{\tau}}\lesssim{\left\|\left\langle k\right\rangle^{s}\frac{\mathcal{F}_{x,t}\left[\partial_{x}(uv)\right]}{\left\langle\tau-k^{3}\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{3}}}\right\|}_{\ell^{2}_{k}L^{2}_{\tau}}={\left\|\partial_{x}(uv)\right\|}_{X^{s,-\frac{1}{3}}}.

Also, since
{\left\|\partial_{x}(uv)\right\|}_{X^{s,-\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim{\left\|\partial_{x}(uv)\right\|}_{X^{s,-\frac{1}{3}}}
, both terms on the LHS of (
3.2
) is controlled by
{\left\|\partial_{x}(uv)\right\|}_{X^{s,-\frac{1}{3}}}
.

We remark that, while
b=-\frac{1}{3}
was chosen to take advantage of the
L^{4}_{t,x}
embedding, we can choose any value between
-\frac{1}{2}
and
-\frac{1}{3}
for the
X^{s,b}
norm on the RHS above. In fact, by selecting a slight smaller
b
, such as
-\frac{2}{5}
, we can again obtain a small positive power of
T
via Lemma

.

To estimate
{\left\|\partial_{x}(uv)\right\|}_{X^{s,-\frac{1}{3}}}
, we use duality:
\left(X^{0,-\frac{1}{3}}\right)^{*}=X^{0,\frac{1}{3}}
, which leads to

{\left\|\partial_{x}(uv)\right\|}_{X^{s,-\frac{1}{3}}}={\left\|J_{x}^{s}\partial_{x}(uv)\right\|}_{X^{0,-\frac{1}{3}}}=\sup_{{\left\|w\right\|}_{X^{0,\frac{1}{3}}}=1}\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}(uv)J_{x}^{s}w\,dx\,dt\right|

We consider the remaining two cases for an arbitrary
w
satisfying
{\left\|w\right\|}_{X^{0,\frac{1}{3}}}=1
. For simplicity of calculations, it is common to assume that all functions on the Fourier side (i.e.
\widetilde{u}
,
\widetilde{v}
,
\widetilde{w}
) are nonnegative. By doing so and using Parseval’s identity, we can rewrite the integral to be estimated to:

\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}(uv)J^{s}_{x}w\,dx\,dt\right|\leq\int_{\Gamma}|k|\widetilde{u}(\tau_{1},k_{1})\widetilde{v}(\tau_{2},k_{2})\left\langle k\right\rangle^{s}\widetilde{w}(\tau,k)\,d\Gamma=:I_{1}

where
\Gamma
is a hyperplane given by
\{\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\tau\in\mathbb{R},k_{1},k_{2},k\in\mathbb{Z}:\tau_{1}+\tau_{2}=\tau,k_{1}+k_{2}=k\}
and
d\Gamma
is the inherited measure on
\Gamma.
We consider this estimate in the remaining two cases.

Case 2:

\left\langle\tau_{1}-k_{1}^{3}\right\rangle\gtrsim H_{1}
.

In this case,

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\Gamma}\frac{|k|\left\langle k\right\rangle^{s}}{H_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\langle k_{1}\right\rangle^{s}}\left\langle\tau_{1}-k_{1}^{3}\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\widetilde{J_{x}^{s}u}(\tau_{1},k_{1})\widetilde{v}(\tau_{2},k_{2})\widetilde{w}(\tau,k)\,d\Gamma

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\Gamma}\min\{|k_{1}|,|k_{2}|\}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\left\langle\tau_{1}-k_{1}^{3}\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\widetilde{J_{x}^{s}u}(\tau_{1},k_{1})\right)\left(\widetilde{v}(\tau_{2},k_{2})\right)\widetilde{w}(\tau,k)\,d\Gamma

Denoting

, the RHS above is bounded by

\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}}\left(J_{x}^{s-\frac{1}{2}}U\right)\,v\,w\,dx\,dt+\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}}\left(J_{x}^{s}U\right)\,\left(J_{x}^{-\frac{1}{2}}v\right)\,w\,dx\,dt

Applying Hölder and Sobolev inequalities, this is bounded by

\displaystyle{\left\|J_{x}^{s-\frac{1}{2}}U\right\|}_{L^{2}_{t}L^{4}_{x}}{\left\|v\right\|}_{L^{4}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}{\left\|w\right\|}_{L^{4}_{t,x}}+{\left\|J_{x}^{s}U\right\|}_{L^{2}_{t,x}}{\left\|J_{x}^{-\frac{1}{2}}v\right\|}_{L^{4}_{t,x}}{\left\|w\right\|}_{L^{4}_{t,x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\left\|J_{x}^{s}U\right\|}_{L^{2}_{t,x}}{\left\|v\right\|}_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}L^{2}_{x}}{\left\|w\right\|}_{X^{0,\frac{1}{3}}}+{\left\|J_{x}^{s}U\right\|}_{L^{2}_{t,x}}{\left\|v\right\|}_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}{\left\|w\right\|}_{X^{0,\frac{1}{3}}}

Since
{\left\|J_{x}^{s}U\right\|}_{L^{2}_{t,x}}={\left\|u\right\|}_{X^{s,\frac{1}{2}}}
, we obtain the desired inequality.

Case 3:

\left\langle\tau_{2}\right\rangle\gtrsim H_{1}
.

This estimate can be obtained from similar calculations as in Case 2. As before, we can write

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\Gamma}\frac{|k|\left\langle k\right\rangle^{s}}{H_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\langle k_{1}\right\rangle^{s}}\widetilde{J_{x}^{s}u}(\tau_{1},k_{1})\widetilde{J_{t}^{\frac{1}{2}}v}(\tau_{2},k_{2})\widetilde{w}(\tau,k)\,d\Gamma

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\Gamma}\min\{|k_{1}|,|k_{2}|\}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\widetilde{J_{x}^{s}u}(\tau_{1},k_{1})\widetilde{J_{t}^{\frac{1}{2}}v}(\tau_{2},k_{2})\widetilde{w}(\tau,k)\,d\Gamma

from which similar calculations as in Case 2 ensues. Here, we only need to make sure that

gets placed under
L^{2}_{t}
-norms when applying Hölder’s inequality. However, both

and

can be placed under

-based norms due to the

embedding. Hence, all Lebesgue indices work out similarly as before. We omit the details. This concludes all cases to prove (
3.2
).

Next, we consider (
3.3
). For this estimate, we observe the algebraic identity:

\tau=(\tau_{1}-k_{1}^{3})+(\tau_{2}-k_{2}^{3})+k(k_{1}^{2}+k_{2}^{2}-k_{1}k_{2})

Here,
(\tau_{1},k_{1})
is the Fourier frequency of the first entry

,
(\tau_{2},k_{2})
is the Fourier frequency of the second entry
u
, and
(\tau,k)
is the frequency of the product. Again, these variables satisfy
\tau_{1}+\tau_{2}=\tau
and
k_{1}+k_{2}=k
, as well as
k\neq 0
from the mean-zero assumption. From the identity above, we must have

\max\{\left\langle\tau\right\rangle,\left\langle\tau_{1}-k_{1}^{3}\right\rangle,\left\langle\tau_{2}-k_{2}^{3}\right\rangle\}\gtrsim\left\langle k\right\rangle\max\{k_{1}^{2},k_{2}^{2}\}=:H_{2}

One of the three statements must be true:

(1)

\left\langle\tau\right\rangle\sim H_{2}

(2)

\left\langle\tau_{1}-k_{1}^{3}\right\rangle\gtrsim H_{2}

(3)

\left\langle\tau_{2}-k_{2}^{3}\right\rangle\gtrsim H_{2}

By symmetry, we will omit the third case.

Case 1:

\left\langle\tau\right\rangle\sim H_{2}
.

Using an analogous argument as in the Case 1 proof of (
3.2
), it suffices to bound
{\left\|\partial_{x}(u^{2})\right\|}_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}
. Here, we have

{\left\|\partial_{x}(u^{2})\right\|}_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim{\left\|\frac{ik}{\left\langle\tau\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}}\mathcal{F}_{t,x}[u^{2}]\right\|}_{\ell^{2}_{k}L^{2}_{\tau}}\lesssim{\left\|\left\langle k\right\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{F}_{t,x}[u^{2}]\right\|}_{\ell^{2}_{k}L^{2}_{\tau}}\lesssim{\left\|u^{2}\right\|}_{L^{2}_{t,x}}\lesssim{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{4}_{t,x}}^{2}\lesssim{\left\|u\right\|}_{X^{0,\frac{1}{3}}}^{2}

where we simply disposed of the
\frac{1}{2}
derivative gain for
v
. However, we will take advantage of this additional gain in derivatives in a subsequent lemma.

Case 2:

\left\langle\tau_{1}-k_{1}^{3}\right\rangle\gtrsim H_{2}
.

As stated under the proof of (
3.2
), it sufficies to bound
{\left\|\partial_{x}(u^{2})\right\|}_{H^{-\frac{1}{3}}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}
. We note as before
\left|ik/H_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right|\lesssim\left\langle k\right\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}
. Denoting
U:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\tau_{1},k_{1}}\left[\left\langle\tau_{1}-k_{1}^{3}\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\widetilde{u}\right]
,

{\left\|\partial_{x}(u^{2})\right\|}_{H^{-\frac{1}{3}}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim{\left\|\left\langle\tau\right\rangle^{-\frac{1}{3}}\left\langle k\right\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{F}_{t,x}[Uu]\right\|}_{\ell^{2}_{k}L^{2}_{\tau}}\lesssim{\left\|Uu\right\|}_{L^{6/5}_{t}L^{1}_{x}}\lesssim{\left\|u\right\|}_{X^{0,\frac{1}{2}}}{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{3}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}

where we have applied Sobolev embedding and Hölder’s inequality. Using interpolation between
X^{0,0}\hookrightarrow L^{2}_{t}L^{2}_{x}
and
X^{0,\frac{1}{2}+}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}
, we deduce that
X^{0,b}\hookrightarrow L^{3}_{t}L^{2}_{x}
for some
0<b<\frac{1}{2}
. This yields (
3.3
).
∎

3.4.
Proof of Theorem
1

Given initial data
(u_{0},v_{0})
and
0<T\ll 1
, define
\Gamma_{T}(u,v)=(\Gamma_{1}(u,v),\Gamma_{2}(u,v))
by

\left|\begin{array}[]{l}\Gamma_{1}(u,v)=\eta(t/T)e^{-t\partial_{x}^{3}}u_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\partial_{x}^{3}}(\eta(s/T)u\eta(s/T)v)_{x}\,ds,\\
\Gamma_{2}(u,v)=\eta(t/T)v_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}(\eta(s/T)u)(\eta(s/T)u_{x})\,ds.\end{array}\right.

Lemmas
1
and
2
yield,
\exists\varepsilon>0
such that

\displaystyle{\left\|\Gamma_{T}(u,v)\right\|}_{Y}

\displaystyle={\left\|\Gamma_{1}(u,v)\right\|}_{X^{s,\frac{1}{2}}\cap H^{s}_{x}L^{1}_{\tau}}+{\left\|\Gamma_{2}(u,v)\right\|}_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{t}L^{2}_{x}\cap L^{2}_{x}L^{1}_{\tau}}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\left\|u_{0}\right\|}_{H^{s}_{x}}+{\left\|v_{0}\right\|}_{L^{2}_{x}}+T^{\varepsilon}{\left\|(u,v)\right\|}_{Y}^{2}

as well as

\displaystyle{\left\|\Gamma_{T}(u_{1},v_{1})-\Gamma_{T}(u_{2},v_{2})\right\|}_{Y}

\displaystyle\lesssim T^{\varepsilon}{\left\|(u_{1}+u_{2},v_{1}+v_{2})\right\|}_{Y}{\left\|(u_{1}-u_{2},v_{1}-v_{2})\right\|}_{Y}.

These statements show that
\Gamma_{T}
is a contraction map on
Y
for
T
sufficiently small. Hence, the fixed point of
\Gamma_{T}
exists and is unique in
Y
. Continuity of solution map in
Y
-norm also follows. This proves Theorem
1
. As a biproduct of this proof, we have also established that the local-in-time solution
(u,v)
lies inside a ball in
Y
, where the
Y
-norm of the center of the ball is controlled by
{\left\|u_{0}\right\|}_{H^{s}}+{\left\|v_{0}\right\|}_{L^{2}}
. This leads to a useful estimate:

(3.5)

{\left\|\eta(\cdot/T)u(\cdot)\right\|}_{X^{s,\frac{1}{2}}\cap H^{s}_{x}L^{1}_{\tau}}+{\left\|\eta(\cdot/T)v(\cdot)\right\|}_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{t}L^{2}_{x}\cap L^{2}_{x}L^{1}_{\tau}}\lesssim{\left\|u_{0}\right\|}_{H^{s}}+{\left\|v_{0}\right\|}_{L^{2}}.

where
s\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]
. Here, size of
T
only depends on
{\left\|u_{0}\right\|}_{H^{s}}
and
{\left\|v_{0}\right\|}_{L^{2}}
.

Let us turn our attention to the special case when
(u_{0},v_{0})\in L^{2}\times L^{2}
. This case is special because
\int u^{2}+v^{2}
is conserved, which means that
{\left\|u(t)\right\|}_{L^{2}_{x}}
and
{\left\|v(t)\right\|}_{L^{2}_{x}}
are both bounded by some constant for
t\in[0,T]
. In this case, the size of
T
in the local well-posedness statement of Theorem
1
only depends on
{\left\|u_{0}\right\|}_{L^{2}}
and
{\left\|v_{0}\right\|}_{L^{2}}
. We can first find a solution on the time interval
[0,T]
. Then we can construct a continuation of this solution on the next time interval
[T,2T]
using the initial data
(u(T),v(T))
. The next time interval can be chosen to be the same since
{\left\|u(T)\right\|}_{L^{2}_{x}}
and
{\left\|v(T)\right\|}_{L^{2}_{x}}
is bounded by the same constant. This process can be iterated indefinitely to extend the local-in-time solution in
(C^{0}_{t}L^{2}_{x})^{2}
to a global-in-time solution. This process leads to the statement of Corollary
1
.

3.5.
Normal form transformation

Heuristics of this approach relies on the fact that solution
v
of (
1.1
) portrays a substantial nonlinear smoothing effect. That is,
v=v_{0}+z
where
z
is much smoother than
v_{0}\in L^{2}
. Note that our mean-zero assumption on
v
carries onto the new variable
z
. We can rewrite (
1.1
) as

\left|\begin{array}[]{l}u_{t}+u_{x}v+uz_{x}+u(v_{0})_{x}+u_{xxx}=0\\
z_{t}+uu_{x}=0\\
u|_{t=0}=u_{0},z|_{t=0}=0\end{array}\right.

We will place
u_{0}\in H^{1}
and
v_{0}\in L^{2}
. The roughest nonlinear term of this system is
u(v_{0})_{x}
, which we will remove using normal form transformation. To this end, we define the normal form operator
T(\cdot,\cdot)
as follows. For any
f,g\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
with mean-zero condition imposed on
g
,

T(f,g)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{k}\left[\sum_{\Tiny\begin{array}[]{c}k_{1}+k_{2}=k\\
k_{2}\neq 0\end{array}}\frac{-i}{k_{1}^{2}+k^{2}+k_{1}k}f_{k_{1}}g_{k_{2}}\right]

Note that the denominator of the Fourier symbol only vanishes when
k_{1}=k=0
, which is only possible if
k_{2}=0
. Hence, we avoid the singularity. By the algebraic identity (
3.4
), we have

(\partial_{t}+\partial_{xxx})T(f,g)=T((\partial_{t}+\partial_{xxx})f,g)+T(f,\partial_{t}g)+f\partial_{x}g.

Substituting
u
and
v_{0}
as entries of
T(\cdot,\cdot)
,

(\partial_{t}+\partial_{xxx})T(u,v_{0})=T(\partial_{x}(uv),v_{0})+u\partial_{x}v_{0}

We change variable with
u=T(u,v_{0})+w
, which leads to the transformed system:

(3.6)

\left|\begin{array}[]{l}v=v_{0}+z\\
u=T(u,v_{0})+w\\
w_{t}+u_{x}v+uz_{x}+T(\partial_{x}(uv),v_{0})+w_{xxx}=0\\
z_{t}+uu_{x}=0\\
w|_{t=0}=u_{0}-T(u_{0},v_{0})\\
z|_{t=0}=0\end{array}\right.

First, we need to establish mapping properties of
T(\cdot,\cdot)
.

Lemma 3
.

Mapping properties of
T(\cdot,\cdot)
are listed below:

(1)

T:H^{s}_{x}L^{1}_{\tau}\times L^{2}_{x}\to H^{s+1}_{x}L^{1}_{\tau}
is a continuous map for
s=0,1
.

(2)

T:X^{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}\times L^{2}_{x}\to X^{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}
is a continuous map. More specifically, for
0<T\ll 1
and
\eta\in\mathcal{S}_{t}
,

(3.7)

\displaystyle{\left\|T(\eta(\cdot/T)u,v_{0})\right\|}_{X^{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\left\|u\right\|}_{X^{0,\frac{1}{2}}}{\left\|v\right\|}_{L^{2}_{x}}+T^{\frac{1}{6}-}{\left\|u\right\|}_{X^{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}}{\left\|v_{0}\right\|}_{L^{2}_{x}}

Proof.

First, we will show
T:H^{s}_{x}L^{1}_{\tau}\times L^{2}_{x}\to H^{s+1}_{x}L^{1}_{\tau}
for which we estimate
{\left\|T(u,v_{0})\right\|}_{H^{s+1}_{x}L^{1}_{\tau}}
.

\mathcal{F}_{x}[T(u,v_{0})](k)=\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}=k}\frac{i\left\langle k\right\rangle^{s+1}}{k_{1}^{2}+k^{2}+k_{1}k}u_{k_{1}}(v_{0})_{k_{2}}

When
s=0
, the fraction in the summand abound is bounded by
\left\langle k\right\rangle^{-1}
. Applying Sobov embedding,

{\left\|T(u,v_{0})\right\|}_{H^{s+1}_{x}L^{1}_{\tau}}\lesssim{\left\|J_{x}^{-1}(uv_{0})\right\|}_{L^{2}_{x}L^{1}_{\tau}}\lesssim{\left\|uv_{0}\right\|}_{L^{1}_{\tau,x}}\lesssim{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{2}_{x}L^{1}_{\tau}}{\left\|v_{0}\right\|}_{L^{2}_{x}}.

For
s=1
, this fraction is bounded by
1
. Hence,

{\left\|T(u,v_{0})\right\|}_{H^{2}_{x}L^{1}_{\tau}}\lesssim{\left\|uv_{0}\right\|}_{L_{x}^{2}L^{1}_{\tau}}\lesssim{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{1}_{\tau}}{\left\|v_{0}\right\|}_{L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim{\left\|u\right\|}_{H^{1}_{x}L^{1}_{\tau}}{\left\|v_{0}\right\|}_{L^{2}_{x}}

Next, we will show
T:X^{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}\times L^{2}_{x}\to X^{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}
, which involves estimating
{\left\|T(u,v_{0})\right\|}_{X^{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}}
with assumptions
u\in X^{0,\frac{1}{2}}
and
v_{0}\in L^{2}
. Using algebraic identity (
3.4
) with
\tau_{2}=0
and
\tau_{1}=\tau
, we have

\max\{\left\langle\tau-k^{3}\right\rangle,\left\langle\tau-k_{1}^{3}\right\rangle\}\gtrsim|k_{2}|\,(k_{1}^{2}+k^{2}+k_{1}k)\gtrsim\left\langle k_{2}\right\rangle\max\{k^{2},k_{1}^{2}\}=H_{1}.

We split into two cases:

Case 1.

\left\langle\tau-k^{3}\right\rangle\sim\left\langle\tau-k_{1}^{3}\right\rangle\gg H_{1}

Denoting
U:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{k_{1},\tau}\left[\left\langle\tau-k_{1}^{3}\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\widetilde{u}(\tau,k)\right]
, we have

{\left\|T(u,v_{0})\right\|}_{X^{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim{\left\|\frac{\left\langle k\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}}{k_{1}^{2}+k^{2}+k_{1}k}\mathcal{F}_{t,x}\left[Uv_{0}\right]\right\|}_{\ell^{2}_{k}L^{2}_{\tau}}\lesssim{\left\|Uv_{0}\right\|}_{L^{2}_{t}L^{1}_{x}}\lesssim{\left\|U\right\|}_{L^{2}_{t,x}}{\left\|v_{0}\right\|}_{L^{2}_{x}}.

Case 2.

\left\langle\tau-k^{3}\right\rangle\lesssim H_{1}
. In this case, note

\frac{\left\langle k\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\langle\tau-k_{1}^{3}\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}}{k_{1}^{2}+k^{2}+k_{1}k}\lesssim\frac{\left\langle k\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\langle k_{2}\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}}{(k_{1}^{2}+k^{2}+k_{1}k)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim 1.

Hence,

{\left\|T(u,v_{0})\right\|}_{X^{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim{\left\|uv_{0}\right\|}_{L^{2}_{t,x}}\lesssim{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{2}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x}}{\left\|v_{0}\right\|}_{L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim{\left\|J_{x}^{\frac{1}{4}+}u\right\|}_{L^{4}_{t,x}}{\left\|v_{0}\right\|}_{L^{2}_{x}}

where the
u
-factor on the RHS is bounded by
{\left\|u\right\|}_{X^{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{3}}}
using
L^{4}_{t,x}
embedding, yielding a
T^{\frac{1}{6}-}
factor using Lemma
1
. This gives (
3.7
).
∎

3.6.
Remaining multilinear estimates in Bourgain spaces

Now, we turn to (
3.6
). Since
u=T(u,v_{0})+w
, applying (
3.7
),

{\left\|u\right\|}_{X^{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim{\left\|u\right\|}_{X^{0,\frac{1}{2}}}{\left\|v_{0}\right\|}_{L^{2}_{x}}+T^{\frac{1}{6}-}{\left\|u\right\|}_{X^{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}}{\left\|v_{0}\right\|}_{L^{2}}+{\left\|w\right\|}_{X^{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}}

so that
{\left\|u\right\|}_{X^{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim{\left\|u\right\|}_{X^{0,\frac{1}{2}}}{\left\|v_{0}\right\|}_{L^{2}_{x}}+{\left\|w\right\|}_{X^{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}}
where
T>0
only depends on
{\left\|v_{0}\right\|}_{L^{2}_{x}}
. Furthermore,

{\left\|u\right\|}_{H^{1}_{x}L^{1}_{\tau}}\lesssim{\left\|T(u,v_{0})\right\|}_{H^{1}_{x}L^{1}_{\tau}}+{\left\|w\right\|}_{H^{1}_{x}L^{1}_{\tau}}\lesssim{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{2}_{x}L^{1}_{\tau}}+{\left\|w\right\|}_{H^{1}_{x}L^{1}_{\tau}}.

Since
u\in L^{2}_{x}L^{1}_{\tau}\cap X^{0,\frac{1}{2}}
from the proof of Theorem
1
, we can see that
u\in X^{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}\cap H^{1}_{x}L^{1}_{\tau}
if
w\in X^{1,\frac{1}{2}}\cap H^{1}_{x}L^{1}_{\tau}
. This motivates our target space for
(w,z)
which is

(w,z)\in(X^{1,\frac{1}{2}}\cap H^{1}_{x}L^{1}_{\tau})\times H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{t}H^{1}_{x}

The following lemma establishes that
z
lies within the target space
H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{t}H^{1}_{x}
. All subsequent lemmas in this section will be able to yield a positive power of
T
on the RHS if
\eta(t/T)
is put within the norms on the LHS. This implication will be apparent within the proof by the fact, for instance, that the estimate ends in the norm
X^{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{3}}
rather than
X^{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}
. We will not include these within each statement.

Lemma 4
.

For
u\in X^{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}
,

{\left\|\partial_{x}(u^{2})\right\|}_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{t}H^{1}_{x}}\lesssim{\left\|u\right\|}_{X^{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}.

Proof.

We can proceed in an analogous manner to the proof of (
3.3
) where the dispersive gain factor is given by
H_{2}
in the previous proof. Consider the same two cases as before:

Case 1:

\left\langle\tau\right\rangle\sim H_{2}
.

In the original proof, note that we have disposed of
\left\langle k\right\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}
gain within the estimate. Here, we will use this gain, along with the fact
|k|\lesssim\max\{|k_{1}|,|k_{2}|\}
which immediately yields the desired estimate.

Case 2:

\left\langle\tau_{1}-k_{1}^{3}\right\rangle\gtrsim H_{2}
.

In this case, we slightly modify the previous proof to accommodate for the change. Note that we are not concerned with
L^{1}_{\tau}
estimates, so we can keep the full
\left\langle\tau\right\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}
gain for the estimate. We have
\left\langle k\right\rangle^{2}/H_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\left\langle k\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Applying Sobolev embedding and denoting
U:=\mathcal{F}_{k_{1},\tau_{1}}^{-1}[\left\langle\tau_{1}-k_{1}^{3}\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\widetilde{u}(\tau,k)]
,

\displaystyle{\left\|\partial_{x}(u^{2})\right\|}_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{t}H^{1}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\left\|\frac{\left\langle k\right\rangle^{2}}{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}\mathcal{F}_{x}[Uu]\right\|}_{L^{1}_{t}\ell^{2}_{k}}\lesssim{\left\|Uu\right\|}_{L^{1}_{t}H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\left\|J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}U\right\|}_{L^{2}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{2}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x}}+{\left\|U\right\|}_{L^{2}_{t}L^{4}_{x}}{\left\|J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}u\right\|}_{L^{2}_{t}L^{4}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\left\|u\right\|}_{X^{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}}{\left\|J_{x}^{\frac{1}{4}+}u\right\|}_{L^{2}_{t}L^{4}_{x}}+{\left\|J_{x}^{\frac{1}{4}}U\right\|}_{L^{2}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}{\left\|J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}u\right\|}_{X^{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{3}}}\lesssim{\left\|u\right\|}_{X^{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}

∎

It remains to obtain necessary estimate for the
w
equation in (
3.6
). Writing
u=T(u,v_{0})+w
in one of the occurences of
u
, the equation for
w
is written as

(3.8)

w_{t}+w_{xxx}=-w_{x}v-uz_{x}-(T(u,v_{0}))_{x}v-T(\partial_{x}(uv),v_{0})

We note that there are two quadratic terms and two trilinear terms on the RHS above. We handle these estimates in two separate lemmas.

Lemma 5
.

Let
u\in X^{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}
,
v\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{t}L^{2}_{x}
,
w\in X^{1,\frac{1}{2}}\cap H^{1}_{x}L^{1}_{\tau}
, and
z\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{t}H^{1}_{x}
. Also, assume that
v
and
z
satisfy the mean-zero condition. Then,

(3.9)

\displaystyle{\left\|w_{x}v\right\|}_{X^{1,-\frac{1}{2}}}+{\left\|\left\langle k\right\rangle\frac{\mathcal{F}[w_{x}v]}{\left\langle\tau-k^{3}\right\rangle}\right\|}_{\ell^{2}_{k}L^{1}_{\tau}}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\left\|w\right\|}_{X^{1,\frac{1}{2}}}{\left\|v\right\|}_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}

(3.10)

\displaystyle{\left\|uz_{x}\right\|}_{X^{1,-\frac{1}{2}}}+{\left\|\left\langle k\right\rangle\frac{\mathcal{F}[uz_{x}]}{\left\langle\tau-k^{3}\right\rangle}\right\|}_{\ell^{2}_{k}L^{1}_{\tau}}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\left\|u\right\|}_{X^{0,\frac{1}{2}}}{\left\|z\right\|}_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{t}H^{1}_{x}}

We remark that these bilinear estimates are both equivalent to (
3.2
). For instance, if
w\in X^{1,\frac{1}{2}}
, then
w_{x}\in X^{0,\frac{1}{2}}
. Therefore, estimating
{\left\|w_{x}v\right\|}_{X^{1,-\frac{1}{2}}}
for is equivalent to estimating
{\left\|\partial_{x}(uv)\right\|}_{X^{0,-\frac{1}{2}}}
. Hence, (
3.9
) follows from (
3.2
). The same can be said about (
3.10
) since
z\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{t}H_{x}^{1}
implies
z_{x}\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{t}L^{2}_{x}
.

Next, we consider the necessary trilinear estimates.

Lemma 6
.

Let
u\in X^{0,\frac{1}{2}}
,
v_{0}\in L^{2}_{x}
, and
v\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{t}L^{2}_{x}
. Also, assume that
v_{0}
and
v
satisfy the mean-zero condition.

(3.11)

\displaystyle{\left\|(T(u,v_{0}))_{x}v\right\|}_{X^{1,-\frac{1}{2}}}+{\left\|\left\langle k\right\rangle\frac{\mathcal{F}[(T(u,v_{0}))_{x}v]}{\left\langle\tau-k^{3}\right\rangle}\right\|}_{\ell^{2}_{k}L^{1}_{\tau}}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\left\|u\right\|}_{X^{0,\frac{1}{2}}}{\left\|v_{0}\right\|}_{L^{2}_{x}}{\left\|v\right\|}_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}

(3.12)

\displaystyle{\left\|T(\partial_{x}(uv),v_{0})\right\|}_{X^{1,-\frac{1}{2}}}+{\left\|\left\langle k\right\rangle\frac{\mathcal{F}[T(\partial_{x}(uv),v_{0})]}{\left\langle\tau-k^{3}\right\rangle}\right\|}_{\ell^{2}_{k}L^{1}_{\tau}}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\left\|u\right\|}_{X^{0,\frac{1}{2}}}{\left\|v\right\|}_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}{\left\|v_{0}\right\|}_{L^{2}_{x}}

Proof.

First, consider (
3.11
). We begin by observing the corresponding algebraic identity:

(3.13)

\tau-k^{3}=(\tau_{1}-k_{1}^{3})+\tau_{3}-(k_{2}+k_{3})(k_{1}^{2}+k^{2}+k_{1}k)

where
\tau_{1}+\tau_{3}=\tau
,
k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}=k
and
k_{2},k_{3}\neq 0
. Hence, the trilinear modulation estimate is given by

\max\{|\tau-k^{3}|,|\tau_{1}-k_{1}|,|\tau_{3}|\}\gtrsim|k_{2}+k_{3}|\max\{k_{1}^{2},k^{2}\}=:H_{3}

We note that
H_{3}
can vanish
k_{2}+k_{3}=0
. Such frequency interaction is referred to as a trilinear resonance, where nonlinear dispersive effects cancel each other, leaving the solution with zero dispersion at the very specific frequency interaction regime. While we can assume
k_{2},k_{3}\neq 0
due to the mean-zero assumption, we cannot assume that
k_{2}+k_{3}\neq 0
.

Before splitting into cases as we had done previously, let us first manage the estimate in the resonant case
k_{2}+k_{3}=0
. Here, we do not need to use the modulational weights, and we can control both terms on the LHS of (
3.11
) by
{\left\|J_{x}\left[(T(u,v_{0}))_{x}v\right]\right\|}_{L^{2}_{t,x}}
. For all cases of frequency interaction, we can write

(3.14)

\mathcal{F}_{x}[J_{x}\left[(T(u,v_{0}))_{x}v\right]](k)=\sum_{\tiny\begin{array}[]{c}k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}=k\\
k_{2},k_{3}\neq 0\end{array}}\frac{(k_{1}+k_{2})\left\langle k\right\rangle}{k_{1}^{2}+(k_{1}+k_{2})^{2}+k_{1}(k_{1}+k_{2})}u_{k_{1}}(v_{0})_{k_{2}}v_{k_{3}}.

Specifically for the resonant case, the expression reduces to

\sum_{k_{2}\neq 0}\frac{(k+k_{2})\left\langle k\right\rangle}{k^{2}+(k+k_{2})^{2}+k(k+k_{2})}u_{k}(v_{0})_{k_{2}}v_{-k_{2}}.

Note that the fraction within the summand above is bounded by 1. Applying Cauchy-Schwartz inequality for

Center-vortex semiclassics with non-minimal ’t Hooft fluxes on ℝ 2 × T 2 \mathbb{R}^{2}\times T^{2} and center stabilization at large N N
Yui Hayashi, Yuya Tanizaki, Mithat Ünsal

Abstract
We consider the semiclassical description of confinement for 4 4 d S ​ U ​ ( N ) SU(N) Yang-Mills theory on small ℝ 2 × T 2 \mathbb{R}^{2}\times T^{2} with non-minimal ’t Hooft twist p p with gcd ⁡ ( N , p ) = 1 \gcd(N,p)=1 . For this purpose, we construct the self-dual center vortex for non-minimal ’t Hooft twists from the Kraan-van Baal-Lee-Lu-Yi (KvBLLY) monopoles by using the 3 3 d Abelianized description of S ​ U ​ ( N ) SU(N) gauge fields on ℝ 3 × S 1 \mathbb{R}^{3}\times S^{1} with nontrivial holonomy backgrounds. This construction shows the self-dual vortex has (1) the fractional magnetic charge q / N q/N with p ​ q = 1 pq=1 mod N N , (2) the fractional topological charge 1 / N 1/N , and (3) the fractional instanton action S YM = 8 ​ π 2 / ( N ​ g 2 ) S_{\mathrm{YM}}=8\pi^{2}/(Ng^{2}) . The confinement vacua for N ​ L ​ Λ ≪ 1 NL\Lambda\ll 1 can be described by the dilute gas approximation of center vortices, and we give the semiclassical formula for the θ \theta dependence and confining string tensions. We apply this result to understand the suitable choice of the twist p p for center stabilization at large N N . In particular, we test the proposal using the Fibonacci sequence, N = F n + 2 N=F_{n+2} and p = F n p=F_{n} , suggested in studies of the twisted Eguchi-Kawai model, from the viewpoint of the 1 1 -form and 0 0 -form center symmetries.

where

B_{\mu\nu}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}p&\quad((\mu,\nu)=(3,4),\,(x_{3},x_{4})=(L_{3}-1,L_{4}-1)),\\

(2)

This plaquette field

can be understood as the
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
-valued
2

\int_{(T^{2})_{\mu\nu}}B=n_{\mu\nu}
.

[

To minimize this action along the

-
4

C=\omega^{-\frac{N-1}{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1&&&\\
&\omega&&\\
&&\ddots&\\
&&&\omega^{N-1}\end{pmatrix},\quad S=\omega^{-\frac{N-1}{2}}\begin{pmatrix}0&1&&\\
&0&\ddots&\\
&&\ddots&1\\

(3)

where

(4)

If we can find a set of link variables that satisfy

\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}}{N}B_{34}(x)}U_{3}(x)U_{4}(x+\hat{3})U^{\dagger}_{3}(x+\hat{4})U^{\dagger}_{4}(x)=\bm{1}_{N\times N},

4

\displaystyle a_{\mu}(\bm{x},x_{3}+L_{3},x_{4})

\displaystyle=S^{p}a_{\mu}(\bm{x},x_{3},x_{4})S^{-p},

\displaystyle a_{\mu}(\bm{x},x_{3},x_{4}+L_{4})

2.3
Center-vortex semiclassics and the vacuum structure

We can now develop the semiclassical description of the confinement by using the dilute gas approximation of center vortices.
Before going into calculations, let us discuss the validity of the dilute gas approximation
[
12
]
.

We should note that perturbative spectra are gapped for the center-symmetric ground states with the twisted boundary condition.
Due to the perturbative gap, any localized field excitations have to exponentially decay to the classical vacuum.
This fact immediately tells

•

the center vortex does not have the size moduli, and

•

the vortex-(anti)vortex interaction is short ranged.

When
L_{3}\gg NL_{4}
, the absence of the size moduli can be confirmed by the explicit construction from the KvBLLY monopoles in (
33
) and (
34
).
Moreover, the vortex-vortex interaction is classically absent due to the self-dual nature, and it is also straightforward to confirm the vortex-antivortex interaction is short ranged.
But, here let us emphasize the above features are general consequence of the perturbative mass gap.
Thus, the center-vortex vertex is well-defined without suffering from the infrared divergence.

In the following, let us set
L_{3}=L_{4}=:L
.
The density of the vortex gas is controlled by the compactification scale as its fugacity is dominantly given by

\exp\left(-\frac{8\pi^{2}}{Ng^{2}}\right)=(NL\Lambda)^{\frac{11}{3}},

(39)

where we use the result of the
1
-loop renormalized coupling at the scale
\mu=1/(NL)
.
Thus, we can argue that the vortex gas is dilute when
NL\Lambda\ll 1
.
Since the vortex-(anti)vortex interaction is short ranged, the ensembles of center vortices can be treated as free gas in this dilute limit.

2.3.1
Multi-branch vacua and the
\theta
dependence

Let us evaluate the partition function of the center-vortex effective theory.
As the center vortex has the topological charge
\frac{1}{N}
and satisfies the self-dual bound, the Boltzmann weight of the single vortex configuration is given by

6
6

6

The computation of the fluctuation determinant is one of the remaining issues in the center-vortex semiclassics, and the obstacle comes from the fact that the analytic form of the center-vortex configuration is unknown. Our construction from the KvBLLY monopoles in Section
2.2
may be helpful toward its computation, but let us leave it for future works.
For our purpose of this paper, we still need to know its
N

with both
M,L\sim O(N)
. As a counterpart of (
95
), we consider

\displaystyle P_{3}

\displaystyle=[S_{(n^{\prime})}^{-p^{\prime}}\otimes\bm{1}_{M\times M}]\oplus S^{-k}_{(L)},

\displaystyle P_{4}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{[C_{n^{\prime}}^{-1}\,\otimes\bm{1}_{M\times M}]}_{n^{\prime}M\times n^{\prime}M}\oplus\underbrace{C^{-1}_{(L)}}_{L\times L},

(101)

and we can make
V_{\mathrm{cl}}=O(\lambda^{-1})
for a suitable choice of
k
so that
\frac{k}{L}\approx\frac{p^{\prime}}{n^{\prime}}
thanks to
L\gg 1
. As there are
M^{2}
massless degrees of freedom, we again find the transition for some
\lambda_{*}\sim N^{-2}
.
The nontrivial order parameter is given by
\frac{1}{N}\operatorname{tr}(P_{3}^{n^{\prime}})=\frac{1}{N}\operatorname{tr}(P_{4}^{n^{\prime}})=\pm\frac{n^{\prime}M}{N}\sim O(1)
.

To prevent the phase transitions to any patterns of partially center-broken vacua, we must require
p/N
cannot be approximated by rational numbers with
O(1)
denominators.
Equivalently, any
O(1)
multiples of
p
should not become
O(1)
in mod
N
. This is exactly the condition mentioned in the main text for the center-stabilization condition.

Appendix B
Continued fraction and the golden ratio

In this appendix, we summarize some properties of the continued fraction, which are useful to understand why the choice
N=F_{n+2}
and
p=F_{n}
is suitable for the center stabilization.

The (simple) continued fraction
[a_{0};a_{1},a_{2},\ldots]
expresses a real number
\xi
,

\xi=a_{0}+\cfrac{1}{a_{1}+\cfrac{1}{a_{2}+\ddots}},

(102)

where
a_{0}
is an integer and
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots
are positive integers. In the following, we assume
\xi>0
and thus
a_{0}\geq 0
.

\xi
is rational if and only if the continued fraction terminates.
Thus, we can approximate an irrational number
\xi
by considering its continued fraction expansion
[a_{0};a_{1},a_{2},\ldots]
and truncating it as

\frac{c_{n}}{d_{n}}:=[a_{0};a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}]

(103)

with the replacement
a_{n+1}\to\infty
.
This rational approximation has the following properties:

•

These rational numbers approximate
\xi
both from lower and upper sides as

\frac{c_{0}}{d_{0}}\leq\frac{c_{2}}{d_{2}}\leq\cdots\leq\frac{c_{2m}}{d_{2m}}\leq\cdots\leq\xi\leq\cdots\leq\frac{c_{2m+1}}{d_{2m+1}}\leq\cdots\leq\frac{c_{3}}{d_{3}}\leq\frac{c_{1}}{d_{1}}.

(104)

•

c_{n}
and
d_{n}
satisfy the same recursive relation

c_{n}=a_{n}c_{n-1}+c_{n-2},\quad d_{n}=a_{n}d_{n-1}+d_{n-2},

(105)

with different initial conditions,
c_{-1}=1
,
c_{-2}=0
, and
d_{-1}=0
,
d_{-2}=1
. Thus,

\begin{pmatrix}c_{n}&c_{n-1}\\
d_{n}&d_{n-1}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}c_{n-1}&c_{n-2}\\
d_{n-1}&d_{n-2}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a_{n}&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{0}&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\cdots\begin{pmatrix}a_{n}&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}.

(106)

•

These finite truncations satisfy

\frac{c_{n+1}}{d_{n+1}}-\frac{c_{n}}{d_{n}}=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{d_{n}d_{n+1}}.

(107)

As a corollary of these properties, the truncations of the continued fraction expansion give quadratically good approximations of the irrational number;

\frac{1}{d_{n}(d_{n+1}+d_{n})}\leq\left|\xi-\frac{c_{n}}{d_{n}}\right|\leq\frac{1}{d_{n}d_{n+1}}.

(108)

To obtain this, we note
\left|\frac{c_{n+2}}{d_{n+2}}-\frac{c_{n}}{d_{n}}\right|\leq\left|\xi-\frac{c_{n}}{d_{n}}\right|\leq\left|\frac{c_{n+1}}{d_{n+1}}-\frac{c_{n}}{d_{n}}\right|
due to (
104
).
Using (
107
), we find

\left|\frac{1}{d_{n+2}d_{n+1}}-\frac{1}{d_{n}d_{n+1}}\right|=\frac{d_{n+2}-d_{n}}{d_{n}d_{n+1}d_{n+2}}\leq\left|\xi-\frac{c_{n}}{d_{n}}\right|\leq\frac{1}{d_{n}d_{n+1}}
.
For the lower bound, we use the recursive relation (
105
) to rewrite it as
\frac{d_{n+2}-d_{n}}{d_{n}d_{n+1}d_{n+2}}=\frac{a_{n+2}}{d_{n}(a_{n+2}d_{n+1}+d_{n})}\geq\frac{1}{d_{n}(d_{n+1}+d_{n})}
thanks to
a_{n+2}\geq 1
, and we get the result.
As
a_{n+1}d_{n}<d_{n+1}=a_{n+1}d_{n}+d_{n-1}<(a_{n+1}+1)d_{n}
, the upper and lower bounds may be replaced as

\frac{1}{a_{n+1}+2}\frac{1}{d_{n}^{2}}<\left|\xi-\frac{c_{n}}{d_{n}}\right|<\frac{1}{a_{n+1}}\frac{1}{d_{n}^{2}},

(109)

and the simple continued fraction generates best Diophantine approximations.
This also shows that a truncation of the continued fraction expansion may give an accidentally good approximation if some of the expansion coefficients
a_{n+1}
are quite huge.
18
18

18

As an example, let us consider the continued fraction of
\pi
:
\pi=[3;7,15,1,292,1,\ldots]
. Since
a_{4}=292
is “huge”, let us truncate it by replacing
a_{4}\to\infty
, and we get the famous approximation of
\pi
as
[3;7,15,1]=\frac{355}{113}
;
|\pi-\frac{355}{113}|\approx 2.67\times 10^{-7}
is much smaller than
1/113^{2}
as it is further suppressed by
1/a_{4}=1/292
.

We note that the recursive relation (
105
) has the same form with that of the Fibonacci sequence (
71
) when
a_{n}=1
for all
n
. That is, the rational approximation of
[1;1,1,\ldots]
is given by
c_{n}=F_{n+2}
and
d_{n}=F_{n+1}
, and we find

[1;1,1,\ldots]=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{F_{n+2}}{F_{n+1}}=\varphi

(110)

Moreover, as a consequence of (
108
), we get

\frac{1}{F_{n+1}F_{n+3}}\leq\left|\varphi-\frac{F_{n+2}}{F_{n+1}}\right|\leq\frac{1}{F_{n+1}F_{n+2}}.

(111)

We can improve the upper bound by using the explicit formula of Fibonacci sequence as

\left|\varphi-\frac{F_{n+2}}{F_{n+1}}\right|<\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}F_{n+1}^{2}},

(112)

giving the bound of Hurwitz’s theorem about Diophantine approximations.
Since all the coefficient in the continued fraction expansion is
a_{n}=1
for the golden ratio
\varphi
, its truncation does not become accidentally good, and thus the convergence of the continued fraction expansion is slowest. In this sense, one might be able to say that
\varphi
is one of the irrational numbers that is most difficult to be approximated by rational numbers.
We here note that this property is also shared by
\frac{1}{\varphi}=-1+\varphi=[0;1,1,\ldots]
,
\frac{1}{1+\varphi}=2-\varphi=[0;2,1,1,\ldots]
, etc.

Appendix C
Convention of the
SU(N)
root and weight vectors

We here summarize the convention of the
SU(N)
root and weight vectors used in this paper.
Let
\{\vec{e}_{n}\}_{n=1,\ldots,N}
be the canonical orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

•

The positive simple roots are given by

\vec{\alpha}_{n}=\vec{e}_{n}-\vec{e}_{n+1},

(113)

with
n=1,\ldots,N-1
. They actually satisfy
\vec{\alpha}_{n}\cdot\vec{\alpha}_{m}=2\delta_{nm}-\delta_{n,m\pm 1}
. We also use the Affine root defined by

\vec{\alpha}_{N}=\vec{e}_{N}-\vec{e}_{1}=-(\vec{\alpha}_{1}+\cdots+\vec{\alpha}_{N-1}).

(114)

•

The fundamental weights
\vec{\mu}_{n}
(
n=1,\ldots,N-1
) are given by

\vec{\mu}_{n}=\vec{e}_{1}+\cdots+\vec{e}_{n}-\frac{n}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\vec{e}_{k},

(115)

which satisfy the defining property,
\vec{\mu}_{n}\cdot\vec{\alpha}_{m}=\delta_{nm}
for
n,m=1,\ldots,N-1
.

•

The weight vectors in the defining representation are

\vec{\nu}_{1}=\vec{\mu}_{1},\,\vec{\nu}_{2}=\vec{\mu}_{1}-\vec{\alpha}_{1},\,\ldots,\,\vec{\nu}_{N}=\vec{\mu}_{1}-\vec{\alpha}_{1}-\cdots-\vec{\alpha}_{N-1},

(116)

that is,
\vec{\nu}_{n}=\vec{e}_{n}-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k}\vec{e}_{k}
.
They satisfy
\vec{\alpha}_{n}=\vec{\nu}_{n}-\vec{\nu}_{n+1}
. The following formula is also useful:

\vec{\nu}_{n}\cdot\vec{\nu}_{m}=-\frac{1}{N}+\delta_{nm}.

(117)

•

We define the cyclic Weyl permutation
P_{W}
by
P_{W}\vec{e}_{n}=\vec{e}_{n+1}
.
It acts on the roots and the weights as

P_{W}\vec{\alpha}_{n}=\vec{\alpha}_{n+1},\quad P_{W}\vec{\nu}_{n}=\vec{\nu}_{n+1},

(118)

where the subscripts are understood in mod
N
.

•

The Weyl vector
\vec{\rho}
is defined by the half sum of the positive roots,

\vec{\rho}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i<j}(\vec{e}_{i}-\vec{e}_{j})=\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\vec{\mu}_{n}.

(119)

Under the cyclic Weyl permutation, it satisfies
P_{W}\vec{\rho}=\vec{\rho}-N\vec{\mu}_{1}
.

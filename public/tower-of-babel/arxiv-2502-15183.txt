Spectrum of Lévy-Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroups on ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d}
Rohan Sarkar †

Abstract
. We investigate spectral properties of Markov semigroups associated with Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (OU) processes driven by Lévy processes. These semigroups are generated by non-local, non-self-adjoint operators. In the special case where the driving Lévy process is Brownian motion, one recovers the classical diffusion OU semigroup, whose spectral properties have been extensively studied over past few decades. Our main results establish that, under suitable conditions on the Lévy process, the spectrum of the Lévy-OU semigroup in the L p L^{p} -space weighted with the invariant distribution coincides with that of the diffusion OU semigroup. Furthermore, when the drift matrix B B is diagonalizable with real eigenvalues, we derive explicit formulas for eigenfunctions and co-eigenfunctions–an observation that, to the best of our knowledge, has not appeared in the literature. We also show that the multiplicities of the eigenvalues remain independent of the choice of the Lévy process. A key ingredient in our approach is intertwining relationship : we prove that every Lévy-OU semigroup is intertwined with a diffusion OU semigroup. Additionally, we examine the compactness properties of these semigroups and provide examples of compact and non-compact Lévy-OU semigroups.

1.
Introduction

We consider the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (OU) semigroup on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
driven by a Lévy process, and the generator on
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is given by

(1.1)

\displaystyle Au(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}(Q\nabla^{2}u(x))+\langle Bx,\nabla u(x)\rangle

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ +\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[u(x+y)-u(x)-\langle\nabla u(x),y\rangle\mathbbm{1}_{\{|y|\leqslant 1\}}\right]\Pi(dy),

where
Q
is a nonnegative definite matrix, which may be singular, and
\Pi
is a Lévy measure, that is,

\displaystyle\Pi(\{0\})=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1\wedge|y|^{2})\Pi(dy)<\infty.

(A,C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))

\displaystyle dX_{t}=BX_{t}+dL_{t},

where
(L_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
is the Lévy process on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with the Lévy-Khintchine exponent

(1.2)

\displaystyle\Psi(\xi)=-\frac{1}{2}\langle Q\xi,\xi\rangle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(e^{\mathrm{i}\langle\xi,y\rangle}-1-\mathrm{i}\langle\xi,y\rangle\mathbbm{1}_{\{|y|\leqslant 1\}})\Pi(dy)=:-\frac{1}{2}\langle Q\xi,\xi\rangle+\Phi(\xi).

(P_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
is also known as
generalized Mehler semigroup
and we refer to
[
5
,
6
,
8
,
10

11

for detailed study on generalized Mehler semigroups associated with infinite dimensional Markov processes.
For simplicity of notations, we identify the generator in (

[

(see also
[

]

is given by

(1.3)

\displaystyle P_{t}\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{i}\xi}(x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{i}\xi}(X_{t}))=\exp\left(\Psi_{t}(\xi)+\mathrm{i}\langle e^{tB^{*}}\xi,x\rangle\right)

\mathcal{E}_{z}(x)=e^{\langle z,x\rangle}

\Psi_{t}(\xi)=-\frac{1}{2}\langle Q_{t}\xi,\xi\rangle+\Phi_{t}(\xi)

(1.4)

\displaystyle\Phi_{t}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi(e^{sB}\xi)ds=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{i}\xi}(e^{sB}y)-1-\mathrm{i}\langle\xi,e^{sB}y\rangle\mathbbm{1}_{\{|y|\leqslant 1\}}\right]\Pi(dy),

\displaystyle Q_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}e^{sB}Qe^{sB^{*}}ds.

For each

, and its associated Lévy measure is given by
\Pi_{t}:=\int_{0}^{t}\Pi\circ e^{-sB}ds
.
Throughout the paper, we assume that

(1)

For all

\det(Q_{t})>0
.

(2)

\sigma(B)\subset\mathbb{C}_{-}

(1.5)

\displaystyle\int_{|x|\geqslant 1}\log|x|\Pi(dx)<\infty,

Denoting

23

(1.6)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{\mathrm{i}\langle\xi,x\rangle}\mu(dx)=e^{\Psi_{\infty}(\xi)},\quad\Psi_{\infty}(\xi):=\lim_{t\to\infty}\Psi_{t}(\xi)=-\langle Q_{\infty}\xi,\xi\rangle+\Phi_{\infty}(\xi).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\Pi_{\infty}:=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Pi\circ e^{-sB}ds.

\Pi=0

For special choices of the matrices

[

for hypocoercivity of Fokker-Planck equations with potential

[

provided an elegant proof of hypocoercivity estimates in

[
3
]
. We also refer to
[
4
]
for

-contractivity of Langevin dynamics with singular potentials with weaker growth conditions than considered in
[

]
.

The results in this article are closely related to the work of Metafune-Pallara-Priola
[
13
]
in the context of Lévy-OU semigroups. When the Lévy measure
\Pi
is zero, that is, the operator
A
in (
1.1
) is a diffusion, it was shown in
[
13
]
that the
\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mu)
-spectrum of
A
is given by

\displaystyle\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{d}n_{i}\lambda_{i}:n_{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\right\},

and
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d}
are eigenvalues of
B
counted with multiplicities. When
\Pi\neq 0
,

A
is both non-self-adjoint and non-local, which makes spectral analysis more difficult. The key ingredient of this paper is the
intertwining relationship
, which can be formally understood as follows: suppose that
P:\mathcal{X}\longrightarrow\mathcal{X}
and
Q:\mathcal{Y}\longrightarrow\mathcal{Y}
be two bounded operators, where
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}
are Banach spaces. We say that
P,Q
are
intertwined via
\Lambda
if there exists a bounded operator
\Lambda:\mathcal{Y}\longrightarrow\mathcal{X}
such that

(1.8)

\displaystyle P\Lambda=\Lambda Q.

In particular, when
\Lambda
is invertible,
P,Q
are similar operators, but in general,
\Lambda
is not assumed to be invertible. If
\Lambda
happens to be injective, then (
1.8
) implies that the eigenvalues of
Q
must be eigenvalues of
P
as well, and the corresponding eigenfunctions of
P
will be the image of the eigenfunctions of
Q
under the mapping
\Lambda
. Therefore, intertwining relationship can be used to transfer spectral properties of
Q
to the same of
P
. We refer to several works of Patie with other co-authors
[
18
,
15
,
17
,
19
]
for a detailed overview of this literature. In Proposition
5.1
, we prove that any Lévy-OU semigroup
P
is intertwined with a diffusion OU semigroup. Moreover, the intertwining operator is Markovian. Although the intertwining operator in this case is not injective, it is bijective on the space of all polynomials when the invariant distribution has polynomial moments of all order, which enables us to find eigenvalues and eigenfunctions of the Lévy-OU semigroup. In Theorem
3.2
under some mild conditions on the Lévy measure
\Pi
, we prove that the point spectrum of any Lévy-OU semigroup coincides exactly with the same of the diffusion OU semigroup, and the point spectrum in
\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mu)
is independent of
p>1
. Additionally, in Section
3.4
, we prove
isospectrality
of generators of Lévy-OU semigroups, that is, the algebraic and geometric multiplicities of the eigenvalues do not depend on the underlying Lévy process.

Another primary focus of this article is to derive explicit formulas for eigenfunctions and co-eigenfunctions of the Lévy-OU semigroups, a direction strongly motivated by
[
18
]
, where the authors obtained spectral expansion of generalized Laguerre semigroups on positive real line. In Theorem
3.3
, we prove that when
B
is diagonalizable, the Lévy-OU semigroup
P
has biorthogonal eigenfunctions and co-eigenfunctions. We also provide an explicit formula for both eigenfunctions and co-eigenfunctions in this case, which to the best of our knowledge has not been observed in the literature. In particular, when
B
is diagonalizable and the Lévy-OU semigroup is a diffusion semigroup, we provide the spectral expansion of the non-self-adjoint semigroup in Theorem
3.5
. As a by-product, we obtain a generalized Mehler’s formula in Corollary
3.8
in multi-dimension.

In Section
3.3
, we discuss compactness of the Lévy-OU semigroup in
\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mu)
. Unlike the diffusion case, Lévy-OU semigroups may not be compact in
\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mu)
, and Proposition
3.9
shows that the compactness of the semigroup heavily depends on the inclusion of certain polynomials in
\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mu)
. In fact, in Proposition
3.10
it is proved that Lévy-OU semigroup is not compact when the Lévy measue
\Pi
corresponds to
\alpha
-stable distribution on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. When the density of the invariant distribution decays rapidly at infinity, see Theorem
3.14
, the Lévy-OU semigroup is compact on
\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mu)
for all
1<p<\infty
.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows: after introducing the notations and conventions in Section
2
, the main results and a few examples are discussed in Section
3
and Section
4
respectively. In Section
5
, we provide details of the intertwining relationships and related results in our setting. Finally, the proofs are deferred to Section
6
.

3.3.
Compactness of

When the Lévy measure

when the Lévy measure is nonzero. While it may be tempting to assume that
P_{t}
is always compact due to the presence of a diffusion component in the generator, the next theorem demonstrates that compactness heavily depends on the existence of polynomial moments with respect to the invariant distribution.

Proposition 3.9
.

Suppose that
P_{t}:\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mu)\longrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mu)
is compact for all
t>0
and for some
1<p<\infty
. Then, there exists infinitely many polynomials
\{p_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
of degrees
1\leqslant n_{1}<n_{2}<\cdots
such that
p_{i}\in\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mu)
for all
i
. In particular when
d=1
, if
P_{t}:\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mu)\longrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mu)
is compact for some
t>0
and
1<p<\infty
, Assumption
1
must hold.

In the following, we show that
P_{t}
is not compact when the Lévy process is a
d
-dimensional
\alpha
-stable process (see
[
23
, Definition 13.6]
) with a mild condition. Denoting the Lévy measure of the
\alpha
-stable process by
\Pi_{\alpha}
, due to
[
23
, Theorem 14.2(3)]
one can write

(3.11)

\displaystyle\Pi_{\alpha}(E)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\sigma(d\xi)\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbbm{1}_{E}(r\xi)r^{-1-\alpha}dr,\quad\forall E\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

for some finite measure
\sigma
on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. Moreover,
\sigma
is uniquely determined and for any
S\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
,
\sigma(S)=\alpha\Pi_{\alpha}((1,\infty)S)
.
For any
0<\alpha<2
, let
P^{\alpha}=(P^{\alpha}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
denote the Lévy-OU semigroup corresponding to the triple
(Q,B,\Pi_{\alpha})
, where
Q,B
satisfy the conditions (
1
) and (
2
). From (
3.11
), it is also evident that
\Pi_{\alpha}
satisfies (
1.5
). Therefore,
P^{\alpha}
is ergodic with a unique invariant distribution
\mu_{\alpha}
.

Theorem 3.10
.

For
0<\alpha<2
, let
\Pi_{\alpha}
be the Lévy measure of an
\alpha
-stable distribution such that
\mathrm{Supp}(\sigma)=\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. Then, for any
1<p<\infty
and
t>0
,
P^{\alpha}_{t}:\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mu_{\alpha})\longrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mu_{\alpha})
is not compact, where
\mu_{\alpha}
is the unique invariant distribution of
P^{\alpha}
.

Remark 3.11
.

Our proof of this result in fact shows that the point spectrum of the
\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mu)
-generator of
P^{\alpha}
is bounded (possibly empty).

For
0<\alpha<2
, let
\overline{P}^{\alpha}=(\overline{P}^{\alpha}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
be the semigroup corresponding to the pure jump process generated by

\displaystyle\overline{A}_{\alpha}u(x)=\langle Bx,\nabla u\rangle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[u(x+y)-u(x)-\langle\nabla u(x),y\rangle\mathbbm{1}_{\{|y|\leqslant 1\}}\right]\Pi_{\alpha}(dy),

where
\Pi_{\alpha}
is defined in (
3.11
). Then,
\overline{P}^{\alpha}=(\overline{P}^{\alpha}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
is ergodic with the unique invariant distribution
\overline{\mu}_{\alpha}
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{\mathrm{i}\langle\xi,x\rangle}\overline{\mu}_{\alpha}(dx)=e^{\Phi_{\alpha,\infty}}(\xi),

where
\Phi_{\alpha,\infty}(\xi)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi_{\alpha}(e^{sB}\xi)ds
and

(3.12)

\displaystyle\Phi_{\alpha}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(e^{\mathrm{i}\langle\xi,y\rangle}-1-\mathrm{i}\langle\xi,y\rangle\mathbbm{1}_{\{|y|\leqslant 1\}})\Pi_{\alpha}(dy).

Since the diffusion coefficient for this process is zero, condition (
1
) doest not hold. In the next result, we prove that the semigroup
\overline{P}^{\alpha}
is non-compact as well.

Theorem 3.12
.

Suppose that
\mathrm{Supp}(\sigma)=\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. Then, for all
0<\alpha<2
,
\overline{P}^{\alpha}_{t}
is not compact on
\mathrm{L}^{p}(\overline{\mu}_{\alpha})
for any
1<p<\infty
.

Remark 3.13
.

(1)

We note that both Proposition
3.10
and
3.12
hold when
\Pi_{\alpha}
corresponds to the symmetric
\alpha
-stable Lévy measure, that is,
\Pi_{\alpha}(dx)=c|x|^{-1-\alpha}
for some
c>0
. In this case
\sigma
is proportional to the uniform measure on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
and
\mathrm{Supp}(\sigma)=\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
.

(2)

Using second quantized operator representations of Lévy-OU semigroups, it was shown in
[
20
, Theorem 7.3]
that
\overline{P}^{\alpha}_{t}
is not compact in the case of one dimensional symmetric
\alpha
-stable noise. The author also obtained a spectral gap inequality for this semigroup, see
[
20
, equation (18)]
.

We end this section by providing a condition on the invariant distribution
\mu
which ensures compactness of the semigroup
P
.

Theorem 3.14
.

Let
\mu
be the invariant distribution of
P=(P_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
and
W=-\ln\mu
. If

(3.13)

\displaystyle\lim_{|x|\to\infty}|\nabla W(x)|=\infty,

then
P_{t}:\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mu)\longrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mu)
is compact for all
1<p<\infty
and
t>0
.

Remark 3.15
.

We note that (
3.13
) implies that
|W(x)|\gtrsim|x|
for any
c>0
, which further implies that
\mu
has finite exponential moments all order.

3.4.
Multiplicities of eigenvalues and isospectrality

For any closed operator
T:\mathcal{D}(T)\subseteq\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}
, where
\mathcal{X}
is a Banach space, the algebraic multiplicity of an eigenvalue
\theta
of
T
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathtt{M}_{a}(\theta,T)=\dim\left(\cup_{n=1}^{\infty}\ker(T-\theta I)^{n}\right),

and the geometric multiplicity is defined as
\mathtt{M}_{g}(\theta,T)=\dim(\ker(T-\theta I))
. We note that both algebraic and geometric multiplicities can be infinite. The next result shows that the multiplicities of eigenvalues of
(A_{p},\mathcal{D}(A_{p}))
is independent of the Lévy-Khintchine exponent and
p
.

Theorem 3.16
.

Suppose that Assumption
1
holds. Then, for all
\theta\in\mathbb{N}(\lambda)
and
1<p<\infty
we have

\displaystyle\mathtt{M}_{a}(\theta,A_{p})

\displaystyle=\mathtt{M}_{a}(\theta,L)

\displaystyle\mathtt{M}_{g}(\theta,A_{p})

\displaystyle=\mathtt{M}_{g}(\theta,L),

where
L=\langle Bx,\nabla\rangle
. Moreover,
\mathtt{M}_{a}(\theta,A_{p})=\mathtt{M}_{g}(\theta,A_{p})
for all
\theta\in\mathbb{N}(\lambda)
if and only if
B
is diagonalizable.

Theorem 3.17
.

Suppose that Assumption
1
is satisfied and there exists a biorthogonal sequence of functions
(\mathcal{H}_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
and
(\mathcal{G}_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
in
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mu)
such that
P_{t}\mathcal{H}_{n}=e^{-t\langle n,\lambda\rangle}\mathcal{H}_{n}
and
P^{*}_{t}\mathcal{G}_{n}=e^{-t\langle n,\lambda\rangle}\mathcal{G}_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0}
, and
\operatorname{Span}\{\mathcal{H}_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0}\}=\mathcal{P}
. Then,
B
is diagonalizable.

4.
Examples

Example 4.1

(Linear kinetic Fokker-Planck equation on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
)
.

Consider the linear kinetic Fokker-Planck semigroup
P_{t}
with generator

\displaystyle A=\Delta_{x}-\langle x,\nabla_{y}\rangle-\langle x,\nabla_{x}\rangle+\gamma\langle y,\nabla_{x}\rangle,\quad\gamma>0.

In this case,

(4.1)

\displaystyle Q=\begin{pmatrix}I_{d}&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad B=\begin{pmatrix}-I_{d}&\gamma I_{d}\\
-I_{d}&0\end{pmatrix}.

The minimal polynomial of
B
is given by
p_{B}(\lambda)=\lambda^{2}+\lambda+\gamma
. Therefore, whenever
0<\gamma<1/4
,
p_{B}(\lambda)
has distinct negative roots. Therefore,
B
is diagonalizable with strictly negative eigenvalues whenever
0<\gamma<1/4
. The invariant distribution is given by

\displaystyle\mu(dx)=\frac{\gamma^{\frac{d}{2}}}{(2\pi)^{d}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{2}-\frac{\gamma}{2}|y|^{2}}.

Therefore, both Theorem
3.3
and Theorem
3.5
hold when
0<\gamma<\frac{1}{4}
.

Example 4.2

(Compact Lévy-OU semigroups on
\mathbb{R}
)
.

Let
A
be the generator of a Lévy-OU process on
\mathbb{R}
such that

(4.2)

\displaystyle Au(x)=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}u^{\prime\prime}(x)-bxu^{\prime}(x)+\lambda\int_{0}^{\infty}[u(x+y)-u(x)]\Pi(dy),

where
\lambda>0
, and
\Pi
is a probability measure supported on
(0,c)
for some
0<c<\infty
. This means that the jumps of the Lévy-OU process are bounded and distributed according to a compound Poisson process. Clearly,
\Pi
satisfies Assumption
2
. Also, by
[
23
, Example 17.10]
, the Lévy-Khintchine exponent of the invariant distribution
\mu
is given by

\displaystyle\ln\mathcal{F}_{\mu}(\xi)=-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4b}\xi^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{b}\int_{0}^{c}(e^{\mathrm{i}\xi y}-1)\frac{\Pi(y,\infty)}{y}dy.

In particular, when
\Pi=\delta_{1}
, the Dirac measure with mass at
1
, the jumps are distributed according to Poisson process with rate
\lambda
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
(P_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
be the semigroup generated by
A
in (
4.2
). Then,
P_{t}:\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mu)\longrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mu)
is compact for all
1<p<\infty
.

Proof.

By
[
9
, Theorem 5.2, Equation (5.19)]
, for any
T>0
and
0<c_{1}<1/c<c_{2}
, there exists
y(c_{1},c_{2},T)>0
such that for all
x>y(c_{1},c_{2},T)
,

\displaystyle x^{-c_{2}h}\leqslant\frac{\mu(x+h)}{\mu(x)}\leqslant x^{-c_{1}h}

uniformly for all
h\in[0,T]
.
Therefore, using triangle inequality we get

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\mu^{\prime}(x)}{\mu(x)}\right|

\displaystyle=\lim_{h\searrow 0}\left|\frac{\mu(x+h)-\mu(x)}{h\mu(x)}\right|

\displaystyle\geqslant\lim_{h\searrow 0}\frac{1-x^{-c_{1}h}}{h}=c_{1}\ln x.

This shows that
\lim_{x\to\infty}|\mu^{\prime}(x)/\mu(x)|=\infty
. On the other hand, since

\displaystyle\mu(x)=\frac{\sqrt{2b}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{2b(x-y)^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}}\mu_{J}(dy)

with
\mathcal{F}_{\mu_{J}}(\xi)=\exp\left(\frac{\lambda}{b}\int_{0}^{c}(e^{\mathrm{i}\xi y}-1)\frac{\Pi(y,\infty)}{y}dy\right)
,
we also have

\displaystyle-\frac{\mu^{\prime}(x)}{\mu(x)}=\frac{4b}{\sigma^{2}}x-\frac{4b}{\sigma^{2}}\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}ye^{-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{2}}\mu_{J}(dy)}{\mu(x)}.

This shows that
\lim_{x\to-\infty}-\frac{\mu^{\prime}(x)}{\mu(x)}=-\infty
. Therefore,
\mu
satisfies (
3.13
), and hence by Theorem
3.14
,
P_{t}:\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mu)\longrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mu)
is compact for all
1<p<\infty
.
∎

As a result, we obtain the following Corollary.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
A
be defined by (
4.2
) and
(P_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
be the semigroup generated by
A
. Then, for all
1<p<\infty
,
\sigma(P_{t};\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mu))\setminus\{0\}=\sigma_{p}(P_{t};\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mu))=e^{-tb\mathbb{N}_{0}}
. Also, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
\mathtt{M}_{a}(-bn,A_{p})=\mathtt{M}_{g}(-bn,A_{p})=1
.

Proof.

Since
P_{t}:\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mu)\longrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mu)
is compact for all
1<p<\infty
, the first assertion follows immediately from Theorem
3.2
. The second assertion follows from Theorem
3.16
.
∎

5.
Intertwining relations

For two closed operators
(A,\mathcal{D}(A)),(B,\mathcal{D}(B))
defined on Banach spaces
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
respectively, we say that
A
and
B
are
intertwined
if there exists a bounded linear operator
\Lambda:\mathcal{X}\longrightarrow\mathcal{Y}
such that

\Lambda(\mathcal{D}(B))\subseteq\mathcal{D}(A)
and

(5.1)

\displaystyle A\Lambda=\Lambda B\ \text{ on }\ \mathcal{D}(B).

Next, let
\widetilde{Q}
be a nonnegative definite matrix such that
\widetilde{Q}
satisfies Assumption (
1
), and
Q_{\infty}\succeq\widetilde{Q}
, that is,
Q_{\infty}-\widetilde{Q}_{\infty}
is nonnegative definite, where
Q_{t}
is defined in (
1.4
). For any
f\in B_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, let us now define

(5.2)

\displaystyle\Lambda f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x+y)h_{\Lambda}(dy),

where
h_{\Lambda}
is a probability measure satisfying

(5.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{h_{\Lambda}}(\xi)=e^{-\frac{1}{2}\langle(Q_{\infty}-\widetilde{Q}_{\infty})\xi,\xi\rangle+\Phi_{\infty}(\xi)}\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

with
\Phi_{\infty}
being defined by (
1.6
).
Since
h_{\Lambda}
is a probability measure,
\Lambda
is a Markov operator, that is,
\Lambda 1=1
and
\Lambda f\geqslant 0
whenever
f\geqslant 0
. Also, when
Q_{\infty}=\widetilde{Q}_{\infty}
and
\Phi_{\infty}\equiv 0
, we have
h_{\Lambda}=\delta_{0}
and therefore,
\Lambda=\mathrm{Id}
.

Proposition 5.1
.

For any nonnegative measurable function
f
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Lambda f(x)\widetilde{\mu}(dx)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)\mu(dx),

where

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mu}(dx)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}\sqrt{\det(\widetilde{Q}_{\infty})}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\|\widetilde{Q}^{-1}_{\infty}x\|^{2}}dx.

As a result, for all
1\leqslant p\leqslant\infty
,
\Lambda
extends as a bounded operator
\Lambda:\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mu)\longrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{p}(\widetilde{\mu})
with dense range.

Proof.

Let
\Lambda
and
h_{\Lambda}
be defined as in (
5.2
) and (
5.3
). To prove the first assertion, for any nonnegative measurable function
f
, using Fubini’s theorem we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Lambda f(x)\widetilde{\mu}(x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x+y)\widetilde{\mu}(x)dx\right)h_{\Lambda}(dy)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\widetilde{\mu}(x-y)h_{\Lambda}(dy)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)\mu(x)dx,

where the last identity follows from the fact that
\widetilde{\mu}*h_{\Lambda}=\mu
. As a result, for any
f\in\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mu)
and
p\geqslant 1
, using Jensen’s inequality we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\Lambda f)^{p}(x)\mu(x)dx\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Lambda(|f|^{p})(x)\mu(x)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)|^{p}\widetilde{\mu}(x)dx,

which shows that
\Lambda
extends as a bounded operator
\Lambda:\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mu)\longrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{p}(\widetilde{\mu})
. Now, for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let us write
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{i}\xi}(x)=e^{\mathrm{i}\langle\xi,x\rangle}
. Then, from the definition of
\Lambda
in (
5.2
) it follows that
\Lambda\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{i}\xi}=\mathcal{F}_{h_{\Lambda}}(\xi)\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{i}\xi}
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Therefore,
\operatorname{Span}\{\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{i}\xi}:\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}\subset\operatorname{Range}(\Lambda)
. Since the former subset is dense in
\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mu)
for any
1\leqslant p\leqslant\infty
, we conclude that
\Lambda:\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mu)\longrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{p}(\widetilde{\mu})
has dense range for all
1\leqslant p\leqslant\infty
.
∎

We are now ready to state the main result of this section.

Proposition 5.2
.

For all
t\geqslant 0
and
p\geqslant 1
we have,

(5.4)

\displaystyle\widetilde{P}_{t}\Lambda=\Lambda P_{t}\ \ \mbox{on \ $\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mu)$},

where
\widetilde{P}
is the diffusion OU semigroup generated by
\widetilde{A}=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}(\widetilde{Q}\nabla^{2})+\langle Bx,\nabla\rangle
with
Q_{\infty}\succeq\widetilde{Q}_{\infty}
, and
\Lambda
defined in (
5.2
).
When
\widetilde{Q}=0
, for all
t\geqslant 0
we have

(5.5)

\displaystyle e^{tL}\Lambda_{1}=\Lambda_{1}P_{t}\quad\mbox{on \ $\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\mu)$},

where
L=\langle Bx,\nabla\rangle
, and
\Lambda_{1}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x+y)\mu(dy)
.
Additionally, under Assumption
1
, (
5.5
) holds on
\mathcal{P}
, the space of all polynomials on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Proof.

We start with the observation that for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\Lambda\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{i}\xi}=e^{-\frac{1}{2}\langle(Q_{\infty}-\widetilde{Q}_{\infty})\xi,\xi\rangle+\Phi_{\infty}(\xi)}\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{i}\xi},

where
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{i}\xi}(x)=e^{\mathrm{i}\langle\xi,x\rangle}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. From (
1.3
) we therefore obtain that for all
t>0
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{P}_{t}\Lambda\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{i}\xi}=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\langle(Q_{\infty}-\widetilde{Q}_{\infty})\xi,\xi\rangle+\Phi_{\infty}(\xi)-\frac{1}{2}\langle\widetilde{Q}_{t}\xi,\xi\rangle\right)\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{i}e^{tB^{*}}\xi}.

On the other hand, using (
1.3
) again, we also have

\displaystyle\Lambda P_{t}\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{i}\xi}

\displaystyle=e^{\Psi_{t}(\xi)}\Lambda\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{i}e^{tB^{*}}\xi}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\Psi_{t}(\xi)-\frac{1}{2}\langle e^{tB}(Q_{\infty}-\widetilde{Q}_{\infty})e^{tB^{*}}\xi,\xi\rangle+\Phi_{\infty}(e^{tB^{*}}\xi)\right)\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{i}e^{tB^{*}}\xi}.

A straightforward computation shows that for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\Psi_{t}(\xi)-\frac{1}{2}\langle e^{tB}(Q_{\infty}-\widetilde{Q}_{\infty})e^{tB^{*}}\xi,\xi\rangle+\Phi_{\infty}(\xi)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\langle(Q_{\infty}-\widetilde{Q}_{\infty})\xi,\xi\rangle+\Phi_{\infty}(\xi)-\frac{1}{2}\langle\widetilde{Q}_{t}\xi,\xi\rangle,

which implies that for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{P}_{t}\Lambda\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{i}\xi}=\Lambda P_{t}\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{i}\xi}.

Since
\{\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{i}\xi}\}_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
is dense in
\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mu)
, we conclude that (
5.4
) holds on
\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mu)
. When
\widetilde{Q}=0
, to prove (
5.5
) we proceed as before. For any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, one has

\displaystyle e^{tL}\Lambda_{1}\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{i}\xi}=e^{\Psi_{\infty}(\xi)}e^{tL}\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{i}\xi}=e^{\Psi_{\infty}(\xi)}\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{i}e^{tB^{*}}\xi},

while

\displaystyle\Lambda_{1}P_{t}\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{i}\xi}=e^{\Psi_{t}(\xi)}\Lambda_{1}\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{i}e^{tB^{*}}\xi}=e^{\Psi_{t}(\xi)}e^{\Psi_{\infty}(e^{tB^{*}}\xi)}\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{i}e^{tB^{*}}\xi}.

Since
\Psi_{\infty}(\xi)=\Psi_{t}(\xi)+\Psi_{\infty}(e^{tB^{*}}\xi)
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\{\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{i}\xi}\}_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
is dense in
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\mu)
, the proof of (
5.5
) follows. When Assumption
1
holds, due to
[
23
, Theorem 25.3]
, we first note that
P_{t}|f|(x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}(|f(X_{t})|)<\infty
for all
f\in\mathcal{P}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For any
f\in\mathcal{P}
, we consider a sequence of bounded measurable functions
\{f_{n}\}
such that
|f_{n}|\leqslant|f|
for all
n
and
f_{n}\to f
point-wise. Since
\Lambda_{1}
is a Markov operator, we have
|\Lambda_{1}f_{n}|\leqslant\Lambda_{1}|f_{n}|\leqslant\Lambda_{1}|f|
, and
P_{t}\Lambda_{1}|f|(x)<\infty
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Also, for all
n\geqslant 1
we have

\displaystyle e^{tL}\Lambda_{1}f_{n}=\Lambda_{1}P_{t}f_{n}.

Finally, letting
n\to\infty
and using dominated convergence theorem, we conclude that
e^{tL}\Lambda_{1}f=\Lambda_{1}P_{t}
for all
f\in\mathcal{P}
. This completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

5.1.
Intertwining with a self-adjoint diffusion

We start with the diffusion OU generator

(5.6)

\displaystyle\overline{A}=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}(\nabla^{2}Q)+\langle Bx,\nabla\rangle.

Let us now assume that
B
is a diagonalizable matrix with real eigenvalues
-\lambda_{1}\geqslant\cdots\geqslant-\lambda_{d}>0
and let
M
be a
d\times d
real invertible matrix such that

\displaystyle MBM^{-1}=D_{\lambda},

where
D_{\lambda}=-\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d})
.
Using the similarity transform
S_{M}f(x)=f(M^{-1}x)
for measurable functions
f
, it is easy verify that

(5.7)

\displaystyle S_{M}\overline{A}S^{-1}_{M}=\overline{A}_{M},

where
\overline{A}_{M}
is another diffusion OU operator given below:

(5.8)

\displaystyle\overline{A}_{M}=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}(MQM^{*}\nabla^{2})+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}x_{i}\partial_{i}.

Since
\overline{A}
is hypoelliptic, so is
\overline{A}_{M}
, and therefore, the pair
(MQM^{*},D_{\lambda})
satisfies (
1
), that is,

\displaystyle(MQM^{*})_{t}:=\int_{0}^{t}e^{sD_{\lambda}}MQM^{*}e^{sD_{\lambda}}ds\succ 0\quad\forall t>0.

A straightforward computation shows that
(MQM^{*})_{t}=MQ_{t}M^{*}
for all
t>0
.
Let
\varrho
be the smallest eigenvalue of
\lambda_{1}MQ_{\infty}M^{*}
, and
P^{\varrho}
be the semigroup generated by

A_{\varrho}=\frac{\varrho}{2}\Delta+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}x_{i}\partial_{i}
with the invariant distribution

\displaystyle\mu_{\varrho}(dx)=\frac{(\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{d})^{\frac{1}{2}}}{(2\pi\varrho)^{\frac{d}{2}}}e^{-\frac{1}{2\varrho}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}x^{2}_{i}}dx.

We note that
P^{\varrho}
is a self-adjoint Markov semigroup on
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mu_{\varrho})
. The following theorem is the main result of this section that intertwines the Lévy-OU semigroup
P
with
P^{\varrho}
.

Theorem 5.3
.

For all
t\geqslant 0
and
1\leqslant p\leqslant\infty
, we have

(5.9)

\displaystyle P^{\varrho}_{t}V=VP_{t}\quad\mbox{on \ $\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mu)$},

where
Vf(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(M^{-1}x+y)h_{V}(dy)
with

(5.10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{h_{V}}(\xi)=\exp\left(\Psi_{\infty}(\xi)+\frac{\varrho}{2\lambda_{1}}|(M^{-1})^{*}\xi|^{2}\right).

Proof.

First, from (
5.7
) it follows that for all

6.1.
Generalized eigenfunctions of
A_{p}

For any closed operator
(T,\mathcal{D}(T))
defined on a Banach space
\mathcal{X}
,

is called a

corresponding to an eigenvalue

if there exists

such that
(T-\theta I)^{r}v=0
for some

. Also,

of an eigenvalue
\theta
is defined as

\displaystyle\iota(\theta;T)=\min\{r\geqslant 1:\ker(T-\theta I)^{r}=\ker(T-\theta)^{r+1}\}.

The algebraic and geometric multiplicities of an eigenvalue
\theta
coincide if and only if
\iota(\theta;T)=1
.

To prove the remaining theorems in Section
3
, we first show that the generalized eigenfunctions of the generator
(A_{p},\mathcal{D}(A_{p}))
defined in (

) are polynomials for any

. We start with the following lemma.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let

and
\epsilon>0
be such that

, where
s(B)=\max\{\mathrm{Re}(\lambda):\lambda\in\sigma(B)\}
. Then, there exists a constant
C=C(k,\epsilon)>0
such that for every

,

\displaystyle\sum_{|n|=k}\|\partial^{n}P_{t}u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mu)}\leqslant Ce^{tk(s(B)+\epsilon)}\sum_{|n|=k}\|\partial^{n}u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mu)},

Proof.

Since (
6.5
) holds, proof of this lemma is exactly similar to the proof of
[
13
, Lemma 3.1]
.
∎

Proposition 6.2
.

Suppose that Assumptions
1
and
2
are satisfied. Then, for all

(A_{p},\mathcal{D}(A_{p}))
corresponding to an eigenvalue

Proof.

We use an argument very similar to the proof of
[
13
, Proposition 3.1]
. Let
\theta
be an eigenvalue of
(A_{p},\mathcal{D}(A_{p}))
and let

be a generalized eigenfunction of

(A_{p}-\theta I)^{r-1}v\neq 0
for some

is an eigenfunction of

and

it follows that

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{n!}}{(2\pi\mathrm{i})^{d}}\frac{(\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{d})^{\frac{1}{2}}}{(2\pi\varrho)^{\frac{d}{2}}}\int_{C_{r}}\frac{V\mathcal{E}_{M^{*}Dz}(x)}{G(\mathrm{i}M^{*}Dz)}\frac{\exp(-\frac{z^{\top}z}{2})}{z^{n+1}}dz

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{n!}}{(2\pi\mathrm{i})^{d}}\frac{(\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{d})^{\frac{1}{2}}}{(2\pi\varrho)^{\frac{d}{2}}}\int_{C_{r}}\mathcal{E}_{Dz}(x)\frac{\exp(-\frac{z^{\top}z}{2})}{z^{n+{1}}}dz

\displaystyle=H_{n}(x).

Since
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{n}
is a polynomial of degree
|n|
and
V:\mathcal{P}_{|n|}\longrightarrow\mathcal{P}_{|n|}
is invertible, we conclude that
\mathcal{H}_{n}=\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0}
. Finally, (
3.8
) follows from the integral representation in (
3.6
) and Cauchy’s residue theorem. This completes the proof of the theorem.

Next, to prove Theorem
3.5
, we first obtain some estimates of the eigenfunctions
\mathcal{H}_{n}
.

Proposition 6.5
.

Suppose that the assumptions of Theorem
3.5
hold. Then, for all
\epsilon>0
, there exists
b_{\epsilon}>0
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0}
,

(6.8)

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{H}_{n}(x)|e^{-\epsilon|x|^{2}}\leqslant b^{|n|}_{\epsilon}.

Consequently, for sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
, there exists
c_{\epsilon}>0
such that
\|\mathcal{H}_{n}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mu)}\leqslant c^{|n|}_{\epsilon}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0}

Proof.

Since
\Pi=0
, the function
F
defined in (
3.7
) is an entire function on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
. More precisely, when
\Pi=0
,

\displaystyle F(z)=\exp\left(-\langle DMQ_{\infty}M^{*}Dz,z\rangle+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\lambda_{j}}{2\lambda_{1}}z_{j}^{2}\right).

Therefore, in (
3.6
) we can choose the sets
C_{r}
with arbitrarily large
r
. For any
\beta>0
and
n\in\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0}
, let us choose
r_{j}=\beta n_{j}^{\frac{1}{2}}
for
j=1,\ldots,d
. Then, we have

(6.9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{n}(x)=\frac{(\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{d})^{\frac{1}{2}}}{(2\pi\varrho)^{\frac{d}{2}}}\frac{\sqrt{n!}}{(2\pi\mathrm{i})^{d}}\int_{C_{r_{1}}}\cdots\int_{C_{r_{d}}}\frac{1}{F(\mathrm{i}z)}\frac{\exp(\langle Mx,Dz\rangle-\frac{z^{\top}z}{2})}{z^{n+1}}dz.

Now, for all
z\in C_{r_{1}}\times\cdots\times C_{r_{d}}
,
|F(\mathrm{i}z)|\leqslant e^{\delta_{1}(\beta)|n|}
such that
\delta_{1}(\beta)\to 0
as
\beta\to 0
. Also, using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, one has
|\langle Mx,Dz\rangle|\leqslant\beta|n|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|D\|_{op}|Mx|\leqslant\delta_{2}(\beta)(|x|^{2}+|n|)
, where
\delta_{2}(\beta)\to 0
as
\beta\to 0
. Finally,
|z^{\top}z|\leqslant\beta^{2}|n|
for all

. Using Stirling formula, we have

(6.10)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\sqrt{n!}}{z^{n+1}}\right|\leqslant\beta^{-|n|-d}\prod_{j=1}^{d}n_{j}^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{|n|}{2}}

Therefore, combining all estimates, (
6.9

\displaystyle|\mathcal{H}_{n}(x)|\leqslant\frac{(\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{d})^{\frac{1}{2}}}{(2\pi\varrho)^{\frac{d}{2}}}\beta^{-|n|-d}e^{-\frac{|n|}{2}}\exp((\delta_{1}(\beta)+\delta_{2}(\beta)+\beta^{2})|n|+\delta_{2}(\beta)|x|^{2}).

The proof of (
6.8
) is completed by choosing
\beta

\delta_{2}(\beta)<\epsilon
. To prove the second assertion, since
\mu
is Gaussian when
\Pi=0
, there exists
\epsilon>0
such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{\epsilon|x|^{2}}\mu(x)dx<\infty
. Hence, (

\|\mathcal{H}_{n}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mu)}\leqslant b^{|n|}_{\epsilon}
for sufficiently small

. This completes the proof of the proposition.

Proof of Theorem
3.5
.

Let us define

\displaystyle S_{t}f=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0}}e^{-t\langle n,\lambda\rangle}\langle f,\mathcal{G}_{n}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mu)}\mathcal{H}_{n}.

(6.11)

To prove the heat kernel expansion in Theorem

Proposition 6.6
.

Suppose that the assumptions of Theorem
3.5
hold. Then, there exists
c>0
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0}

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{G}_{n}(y)\mu(y)|\leqslant c^{|n|}.

Proof.

We note that for all

\mathcal{V}_{n}(y):=\mathcal{G}_{n}(y)\mu(y)=\frac{\partial^{n}\mu(y)}{\sqrt{n!}}
. Since
\Pi=0
,

\displaystyle\mu(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}\sqrt{\det(Q_{\infty})}}e^{-\langle Q^{-1}_{\infty}x,x\rangle/2},

and therefore,
\mu
extends analytically on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
. Using Taylor’s formula, we obtain that

(6.12)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{V}_{n}(y)}{\sqrt{n!}}=\frac{1}{(2\pi\mathrm{i})^{d}}\int_{C_{r_{1}}}\cdots\int_{C_{r_{d}}}\frac{\mu(\zeta+y)}{\zeta^{n+1}}d\zeta,

where
C_{r_{j}}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|=\beta n^{\frac{1}{2}}_{j}\}
for
j=1,\ldots,d
and for some
\beta>0
. Since,

\displaystyle-\langle Q^{-1}_{\infty}(\zeta+y),(\zeta+y)\rangle=-\langle Q^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\infty}\zeta,Q^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\infty}\zeta\rangle-|Q^{-\frac{1}{2}}y|^{2}-2\langle Q^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\infty}\zeta,Q^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\infty}y\rangle,

we obtain that for all
\zeta\in C_{r_{1}}\times\cdots\times C_{r_{d}}
,

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}\sqrt{\det(Q_{\infty})}|\mu(\zeta+y)|

\displaystyle=\exp(-\mathrm{Re}\langle Q^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\infty}\zeta,Q^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\infty}\zeta\rangle-|Q^{-\frac{1}{2}}y|^{2}-2\mathrm{Re}\langle Q^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\infty}\zeta,Q^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\infty}y\rangle)

\displaystyle\leqslant\exp(-|Q^{-\frac{1}{2}}y|^{2})\exp(2|Q^{-\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{\zeta}|^{2}+\langle|Q^{-\frac{1}{2}}y|^{2}\rangle)

\displaystyle=\exp(2|Q^{-\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{\zeta}|^{2}),

where
\tilde{\zeta}=(|\zeta_{1}|,\ldots,|\zeta|_{d})
. Since
|\tilde{\zeta}|^{2}=\beta|n|
when
\zeta\in C_{r_{1}}\times\cdots\times C_{r_{d}}
, it follows that for all
\zeta\in C_{r_{1}}\times\cdots\times C_{r_{d}}
,

\displaystyle|\mu(\zeta+y)|\leqslant\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}\sqrt{\det(Q_{\infty})}}e^{\beta\|Q^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\infty}\||n|}.

Using the integral representation (
6.12
) along with the similar estimate as in (
6.10
), we get

\displaystyle|\mathcal{V}_{n}(y)|

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{\sqrt{n!}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{C_{r_{1}}}\cdots\int_{C_{r_{d}}}\left|\frac{\mu(\zeta+y)}{\zeta^{n+1}}\right|d\zeta

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}\sqrt{\det(Q_{\infty})}}\beta^{-|n|+d}e^{-\frac{|n|}{2}}e^{\beta\|Q^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\infty}\|_{op}|n|}.

We can choose
c
by minimizing the above upper bound with respect to
\beta>0
. This concludes the proof of the proposition.
∎

b_{t}
is a symmetric function, the above inequality implies that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|T_{t}f(x)|^{p}\overline{\mu}_{\alpha}(dx)\leqslant K_{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(y)|^{p}(b*\overline{\mu}_{\alpha})(y)dy.

Since
\mu_{\alpha}=b*\overline{\mu}_{\alpha}
, we obtain that
\|T_{t}f\|^{p}_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\overline{\mu}_{\alpha})}\leqslant K_{t}\|f\|^{p}_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mu_{\alpha})}
. Since
P^{\alpha}_{t}
is not compact in
\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mu_{\alpha})
, thanks to Theorem
3.10
, there exists a sequence
(f_{n})\subset\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mu_{\alpha})
such that
\|f_{n}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mu_{\alpha})}\leqslant 1
but
(P^{\alpha}_{t}f_{n})
is not pre-compact in
\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mu_{\alpha})
. Then,
\|T_{t}f_{n}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\overline{\mu}_{\alpha})}\leqslant K^{1/p}_{t}
for all
n\geqslant 1
, and
(P^{\alpha}_{t}f_{n})=(\overline{P}^{\alpha}_{t}T_{t}f_{n})
is not pre-compact in
\mathrm{L}^{p}(\overline{\mu}_{\alpha})
, which shows that
\overline{P}^{\alpha}_{t}
is not compact in
\mathrm{L}^{p}(\overline{\mu}_{\alpha})
. This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

In the next lemma, we prove compactness of the embedding
\mathrm{W}^{1,p}(\mu)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mu)
when (
3.13
) holds. This is essential for proving Theorem
3.14
.

Lemma 6.8
.

Suppose that
W=-\ln\mu
and
\lim_{|x|\to\infty}|\nabla W(x)|=\infty
. Then, for all
2\leqslant p<\infty
, the embedding
\mathrm{W}^{1,p}(\mu)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mu)
is compact.

Proof.

Since by Theorem
3.1
,
\mu
is strictly positive and smooth,
W\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. From
[
12
, Lemma 8.5.3]
, it is enough to show that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(6.16)

\displaystyle\Delta W(x)\leqslant\delta|\nabla W(x)|^{2}+M

for some
\delta\in(0,1)
and
M>0
, independent of
x
. Let
\mu_{J}
be the probability measure defined by

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\mathrm{i}\langle\xi,x\rangle}\mu_{J}(dx)=e^{\Psi_{\infty}(\xi)}

for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then, the invariant distribution
\mu
is the convolution of
\mu_{J}
and a Gaussian density, that is, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\mu(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}\sqrt{\det{Q_{\infty}}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}|Q^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\infty}(x-y)|^{2}}\mu_{J}(dy).

Denoting the partial derivative with respect to
x_{i}
by
\partial_{i}
, we note that for any
1\leqslant i\leqslant d
,
\partial_{i}W=-\partial_{i}\mu/\mu,\partial^{2}_{i}W=((\partial_{i}\mu)^{2}-\mu\partial^{2}_{i}\mu)/\mu^{2}
and as a result,

(6.17)

\displaystyle\partial_{i}\mu(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}\sqrt{\det{Q_{\infty}}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle Q^{-1}_{\infty}(x-y),e_{i}\rangle e^{-\frac{1}{2}|Q^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\infty}(x-y)|^{2}}\mu_{J}(dy),

\displaystyle\partial^{2}_{i}\mu(x)

\displaystyle=-\langle Q^{-1}_{\infty}e_{i},e_{i}\rangle\mu(x)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}\sqrt{\det{Q_{\infty}}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle Q^{-1}_{\infty}(x-y),e_{i}\rangle^{2}e^{-\frac{1}{2}|Q^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\infty}(x-y)|^{2}}\mu_{J}(dy).

Using Jensen’s inequality we obtain

\displaystyle(\partial_{i}\mu(x))^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}\sqrt{\det{Q_{\infty}}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle Q^{-1}_{\infty}(x-y),e_{i}\rangle^{2}e^{-\frac{1}{2}|Q^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\infty}(x-y)|^{2}}\mu_{J}(dy)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \times\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}\sqrt{\det{Q_{\infty}}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}|Q^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\infty}(x-y)|^{2}}\mu_{J}(dy)

\displaystyle=\mu(x)(\partial^{2}_{i}\mu(x)+\langle Q^{-1}_{\infty}e_{i},e_{i}\rangle\mu(x)).

As a result, we get

\displaystyle\partial^{2}_{i}W(x)=\frac{(\partial_{i}\mu(x))^{2}-\mu(x)\partial^{2}_{i}\mu(x)}{\mu(x)}\leqslant\langle Q^{-1}_{\infty}e_{i},e_{i}\rangle.

Therefore, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have

\displaystyle\Delta W(x)\leqslant\mathrm{tr}(Q^{-1}_{\infty}),

which implies (
6.16
) with
\delta=0
and
M=\mathrm{tr}(Q^{-1}_{\infty})
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.14
.

We consider the case when
p=2
. Since by Lemma
6.8
, the embedding
\mathrm{W}^{1,2}(\mu)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mu)

∎

Proof of Theorem
3.16
.

Since Assumption
1
holds,
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mu)
for all
p\geqslant 1
. Also, by Theorem
3.2
,
\sigma_{p}(P_{t};\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mu))=e^{-t\mathbb{N}(\lambda)}
for all
t>0
and
1<p<\infty
. This also implies that
\mathbb{N}(\lambda)=\sigma_{p}(A_{p})
. From (
5.5
) in Proposition
5.2
, we note that for all
\theta\in\mathbb{N}(\lambda)
and
r\geqslant 1
,

\displaystyle(L-\theta I)^{r}\Lambda_{1}=\Lambda_{1}(A_{p}-\theta I)^{r}\quad\mbox{on \ $\mathcal{P}$}.

Since
\Lambda_{1}:\mathcal{P}\longrightarrow\mathcal{P}
is invertible,
\ker(A-\theta I)^{r}=\ker(L-\theta I)^{r}
on
\mathcal{P}
for all
r\geqslant 1
. This proves that
\mathtt{M}_{a}(\theta,A_{p})=\mathtt{M}_{a}(\theta,L)
and
\mathtt{M}_{g}(\theta,A_{p})=\mathtt{M}_{g}(\theta,L)
for all
1<p<\infty
. In particular,
\mathtt{M}_{a}(\theta,A_{p})=\mathtt{M}_{g}(\theta,A_{p})
for all
\theta\in\mathbb{N}(\lambda)
if and only of
\mathtt{M}_{a}(\theta,L)=\mathtt{M}_{g}(\theta,L)
for all
\theta\in\mathbb{N}(\lambda)
. In this case, the index of each eigenvalue
\theta
of
L
is
1
, which by
[
13
, Proposition 4.3]
holds if and only if
B
is diagonalizable.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.17
.

Let
(A_{2},\mathcal{D}(A_{2}))
denote the generator of
P_{t}
in
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mu)
. Then, we have
\mathcal{H}_{n}\in\mathcal{D}(A_{2})
and
\mathcal{G}_{n}\in\mathcal{D}(A^{*}_{2})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0}
. Since
\mathcal{H}_{n}
is an eigenfunction of
A_{2}
, by Proposition
6.2
,
\mathcal{H}_{n}
is a polynomial of degree at most
|n|
. Now, suppose that there exists an eigenvalue
\theta\in\mathbb{N}(\lambda)
of
A_{2}
such that
A_{2}
has a generalized eigenfunction corresponding to
\theta
, that is, there exists
v\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mu)
satisfying
(A_{2}-\theta I)^{r}v=0
for some
r\geqslant 1
. Let us assume that
\theta=\langle n,\lambda\rangle
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0}
. Then, for any
\theta^{\prime}=\langle m,\lambda\rangle
with
\theta\neq\theta^{\prime}
we have

\displaystyle\langle(A_{2}-\theta I)v,\mathcal{G}_{m}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mu)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(\theta-\theta^{\prime})^{r-1}}\langle(A_{2}-\theta I)v,(A^{*}_{2}-\theta I)^{r-1}\mathcal{G}_{m}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mu)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(\theta-\theta^{\prime})^{r-1}}\langle(A_{2}-\theta I)^{r}v,\mathcal{G}_{m}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mu)}

\displaystyle=0

On the other hand, for any
n,m\in\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0}
with
\langle n,\lambda\rangle=\langle m,\lambda\rangle=\theta
, we have

\displaystyle\langle(A_{2}-\theta I)v,\mathcal{G}_{m}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mu)}=\langle v,(A^{*}_{2}-\theta I)\mathcal{G}_{m}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mu)}=0.

Therefore,
\langle(A_{2}-\theta I)v,\mathcal{G}_{m}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mu)}=0
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0}
. Since
(A_{2}-\theta I)v
is a polynomial and
\operatorname{Span}\{\mathcal{H}_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0}\}
, there exists constants
(c_{i})_{1\leqslant i\leqslant k}
and
n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0}
such that

\displaystyle(A_{2}-\theta I)v=\sum_{i=1}^{k}c_{i}\mathcal{H}_{n_{i}}.

Since
\langle(A_{2}-\theta I)v,\mathcal{G}_{n}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mu)}=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0}
and
(\mathcal{H}_{n}),(\mathcal{G}_{n})
are biorthogonal, we conclude that
c_{i}=0
for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant k
, which implies that
(A_{2}-\theta I)v=0
. Therefore,
v
is an eigenfunction. As a result, we have
\mathtt{M}_{a}(\theta,A_{2})=\mathtt{M}_{g}(\theta,A_{2})
for all
\theta\in\mathbb{N}(\lambda)
, which according to Theorem
3.16
implies that
B
is diagonalizable.
∎

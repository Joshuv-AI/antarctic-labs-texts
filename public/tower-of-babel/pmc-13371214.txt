“Dress the scrub”: use of escape room technique in an operating room nursing course
Authors: Müjgan Onarıcı, Gülsüm Çonoğlu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundNursing students must acquire knowledge, skills, and attitudes regarding surgical attire during their education. Escape room activities affect nursing students’ in-class participation, motivation and satisfaction levels. This study aimed to examine the effect of the escape room technique on students’ knowledge of surgical attire and their motivation toward the course.MethodsA single-group pretest-posttest design was used. The study sample consisted of nursing students enrolled in the Operating Room Nursing course in the spring semester of the 2023–2024 academic year who agreed to participate in the study (n = 24). The researchers aimed to use total population sampling by inviting all eligible participants. Research data were collected using a personal information form, knowledge test, and Instructional Materials Motivation Survey. The knowledge test and the Instructional Materials Motivation Survey were administered to the students as pretests. Subsequently, teams of four to five students participated in an escape room activity called “Dress the Scrub”, in which they solved puzzles and questions as part of the scenario. The knowledge test and the Instructional Materials Motivation Survey were administered as posttests, followed by a debriefing. IBM SPSS Statistics version 22.0 was used for the data analysis. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05.ResultsStatistically significant differences were found in the pretest (58.75 ± 18.25) and posttest (65.83 ± 13.48) knowledge test scores, as well as in the Instructional Materials Motivation Survey subscale scores for attention-relevance (pretest: 45.58 ± 6.70; posttest: 48.20 ± 6.47) and confidence-satisfaction (pretest: 53.33 ± 6.66; posttest: 58.41 ± 6.78) (p < 0.05). The total Instructional Materials Motivation Survey scores also increased significantly (pretest: 98.91 ± 12.64; posttest: 106.62 ± 12.66, p < 0.05).ConclusionThe results of this study indicate that the escape room game technique can contribute to knowledge, motivation, and satisfaction in the field of surgical education. However, comprehensive preparation for the use of this technique is necessary, as well as improved communication and collaboration with educators and observers involved in the implementation process. It is recommended that this technique be used in nursing education.

Background

Individuals present in environments where surgical procedures are conducted are a significant source of bacterial transmission. Operating rooms are high-risk areas for infection transmission. The operating room is a specialized hospital unit where a multidisciplinary team, including surgeons, nurses, anesthetists, and technicians, performs surgical procedures. To prevent surgical site infections, all team members must wear appropriate surgical attire in accordance with operating room protocols [1]. Nursing students may also be present in operating rooms as part of their training and practice. Therefore, surgical attire training must be effectively delivered to students. One potential instructional method for this purpose is the escape room technique. This technique may be utilized in nursing education to enhance teamwork and problem-solving skills of nursing students. Originating as an entertainment activity, the escape room technique involves teams, typically consisting of two or more participants, who work collaboratively within a set time frame to uncover clues and solve puzzles embedded within thematic narratives [2].

Surgical attire is used to prevent the transfer of microorganisms between operating room personnel and patients. Gloves protect the hands, surgical gowns safeguard the skin and scrub apparel, masks cover the mouth and nose, and goggles protect the eyes [3]. When surgical team members wear surgical headwear, masks, scrub apparel, and sterile surgical gowns, the airborne spread of microorganisms is reduced, thereby lowering the risk of wound contamination in patients [1]. Thus, it is vital to instill knowledge, skills, and attitudes regarding surgical attire among nursing students during their education.

Game-based learning, a teaching method conducted in a competitive environment with predefined rules, is increasingly employed by educators because it promotes knowledge retention and critical thinking [4]. Game-based learning in professional education can meet the demands of students in the digital age, thereby improving learning outcomes [5]. In addition, innovative learning methods, such as interactive videos positively influence student satisfaction [6]. Findings from a study by Küçükakça Çelik et al. (2023) suggest that the escape room technique may be integrated into nursing curricula as an innovative instructional strategy that enhances student motivation and promotes engagement [7]. The integration of such creative approaches in to curriculum can offer students structured learning experiences and enhance their overall academic performance [8].

Escape rooms foster interaction and connection among individuals working toward shared objectives [2]. As a gamification-based approach, escape rooms enhance students’ motivation for learning, critical thinking, creative problem solving, and self-assessment [9]. During escape room sessions integrated into classroom activities, nursing students actively engage with the challenges presented, searching for clues and solving problems dynamically [10]. Problem-based and meaningful activities within educational escape rooms create an environment that requires students to be proactive and collaborate with peers. This also means that teamwork and communication skills are essential for completing an escape room on time. Consequently, escape rooms have the potential to improve these essential skills [11]. A review of the literature indicates that the use of escape rooms in nursing education enhances communication [9], improves teamwork skills [9, 12, 13], strengthens learning performance, problem-solving abilities, and critical thinking skills [8], increases awareness, and enhances leadership skills [13].

Interest in using the escape room technique in nursing education has been steadily increasing. A review of existing studies reveals that this method has been applied in various areas of nursing education, including wound care [7], maternity education [8], “Adult Nursing 1” courses [14], high-fidelity urosepsis simulation scenarios [12], pediatric acute care [15], cardiogenic shock [16], labor [17], and stroke case studies [18]. However, further research is recommended to assess the effect of escape rooms on nursing knowledge and skills [19]. To the best of our knowledge, the escape room technique has not yet been applied in nursing studies related to surgical attire. Therefore, the current study aimed to examine the effects of the escape room technique on students’ knowledge of surgical attire and their motivation toward the course.

Materials and methods

Study aim and design

This study aimed to examine the effects of the escape room technique on students’ knowledge of surgical attire and their motivation toward the course. This study used a single-group pretest-posttest design.

Sample size and setting

This study was conducted with students enrolled in the Operating Room Nursing course within the Nursing Department of the Faculty of Health Sciences during the spring semester of the 2023–2024 academic year in Türkiye. Operating Room Nursing is offered as an elective course in the nursing curriculum. As part of the course, all students received 45 min of theoretical training on surgical handwashing, gowning, and glove application training. The students were informed that their participation in this research was voluntary, constituted an extra-curricular activity, and would have no impact on their course grades. Subsequently, written informed consent was obtained. The study population consisted of 40 third- and fourth-year nursing students enrolled in the Operating Room Nursing course. The researchers aimed to use total population sampling by inviting all eligible participants. Students who volunteered for research and participated in the escape room activity made up the study sample (n = 24) (Fig. 1).

In determining the study sample, the study adopted a single group pretest-posttest design, rather than forming a control group. This decision was influenced by the specific nature of the Operating Room Nursing elective course in which the study was conducted, the limited number of students in the population (N = 40), and the potential for participant dropouts. However, the sample size adequacy for the study was assessed using G*Power 3.1 software, employing a two-sided hypothesis. For this investigation, the effect size was set at Cohen’s d = 0.80, the significance level at α = 0.05, and the statistical power (1–β) at 0.95. The analysis revealed that a minimum of 23 participants was sufficient to detect a significant difference in the study, and the statistical power achieved with this sample size was 0.956.

“Dress the Scrub” Escape Room Game Stages

The “Dress the Scrub” scenario was developed by the researchers in accordance with the relevant literature [1, 3, 20–27]. After drafting the scenario, expert opinions were sought from three nurse educators experienced in simulation and scenario writing, and the scenario was revised based on their feedback. The goal was for students to complete the four puzzles and two encrypted questions (six stages) provided in the scenario (Fig. 2) and use the solutions to dress in scrubs. The students were expected to complete the tasks presented in random order, arrange the materials they obtained in the correct sequence, and successfully dress the scrub. All codes and questions in the escape room puzzles were designed to focus on surgical handwashing, gowning, and gloving [1, 3, 23–25, 27].

Data collection

The escape room game was conducted outside the class, two weeks before the final examination, in the nursing skills laboratory. Before the study was implemented, a pilot test was conducted with a group of five students not enrolled in the course. Based on the pilot test results, no changes were made to the game phases. The pilot group completed the escape room in 15 min 38 s. A five-minute interval was planned to reset the laboratory and prepare it for the next group (Fig. 1).

Before the commencement of the study, the students were given detailed information about the research, introduced to the escape room technique, and provided with written informed consent forms. Subsequently, the Personal Information Form, Knowledge Test, and the Turkish version of the IMMS were administered as pretests, which took approximately 10–15 min to complete (Fig. 1).

The “Dress the Scrub” scenario, developed by the researchers and reviewed by three experts, was implemented in a nursing skills laboratory. Student teams, consisting of four to five participants each, were expected to follow the given instructions, solve all puzzles, identify key terms, answer the questions to decipher codes, and ultimately correctly dress the scrub nurse to successfully complete the escape room activity. Once all necessary laboratory materials were arranged, each student group was escorted into the laboratory, where the door was locked at the start of the activity. Upon completing the escape room task, the students were given a key to unlock the door. A chronometer was started upon giving the “Dress the Scrub” command and stopped once the students successfully opened the exit door. To exit the escape room, students had to use the materials provided efficiently and solve the six-step puzzle (Figs. 2 and 3). Through this process, they sequentially accessed the necessary surgical attire for scrub nurses. Once all puzzles were solved, the student designated as the scrub nurse at the beginning of the scenario dressed in the correct sequence, which marked the completion of the scenario and unlocking of the door. After all groups successfully completed the escape room activity, a 20-minute debriefing session was conducted with the students to reflect on the scenario. Before this session, a five-minute break was given for rest and relaxation. Immediately afterward, the students completed the satisfaction survey, knowledge test, and Turkish version of the IMMS as posttests, which took approximately 10–15 min to complete (Fig. 1).

To prevent interaction and information sharing among students who had not yet participated, a waiting area was designated opposite the escape room. One of the researchers remained with the waiting students and independently administered the surveys before and after the escape room activity.

Data analysis

IBM SPSS Statistics version 22.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) was used for data analysis. Descriptive statistics were used to identify students’ demographic characteristics. Normality tests were conducted, and changes over time were analyzed using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test for dependent samples. The Mann–Whitney U test was used to analyze the differences between the groups. Additionally, correlation analysis was used to assess relationships between variables based on the measurement levels. Statistical significance was defined as a p-value < 0.05.

Results

The mean age of the students was 22.40 ± 2.20 years, with 58% being female and 63% being graduates of Anatolian high school. All five groups (n = 24 students) participated in the activity, and all groups successfully escaped from the escape room. The mean escape time was 14 min 37 s, with the fastest group escaping in 12 min 33 s. A total of 92% of the students stated that the activity was engaging, that it contributed to their course motivation, and that the scenario was beneficial for achieving learning objectives. All students agreed that the activity improved their knowledge and surgical attiring skills. It was found that 96% of the students had previously solved puzzles, but 58% had not received training using simulation techniques, and none had experienced training with the escape room technique before. Furthermore, the students were satisfied with the activity (mean satisfaction score: 4.70 ± 0.50) (Table 1).

Discussion

Escape rooms and serious game-based learning strategies have been regarded as engaging instructional methods by both faculty and students [12]. Given that escape rooms promote teamwork and interactive problem solving, they have been found to encourage students to engage in study activities even when an examination is not imminent [14]. This study aimed to determine the effect of the escape room technique on students’ knowledge of surgical attire and their motivation toward the course. The escape room activity was completed by teams of four (n = 1) or five (n = 4) member each. Similarly, in a systematic review by Veldkamp et al. (2020), 24 of 32 studies discussing team sizes reported groups consisting of three to six players, indicating that the team sizes established for this study are consistent with the literature [11]. Furthermore, the sample size of the study aligns with those found in other studies within the literature. This is attributable to operational challenges associated with escape room activities, the extended durations necessary for their implementation, and the imperative to minimize the risk of transmission among participants [32, 33].

The use of serious games and low-cost innovative teaching methods in nursing education is an effective and user-friendly approach that strengthens students’ clinical skills and enables a deeper understanding of the content [9, 10]. Indeed, the literature shows that the escape room technique is an innovative method that can be integrated into surgical nursing clinical skills training [7, 12]. The current study found that escape room activity led to a significant increase in students’ knowledge levels. This may be due to the immersive and dynamic nature of the student-centered structured escape room technique, which may have facilitated students’ comprehension and knowledge retention. Findings from previous studies support the findings of the current study, indicating that escape rooms are effective in increasing knowledge of surgical nursing education topics [34], supporting knowledge retention and comprehension abilities [7, 12], and providing a valuable learning experience that facilitates the recall and application of information [14].

Gamification techniques, such as the implementation of escape rooms in educational settings, effectively transform motivation into a fundamental driving force for knowledge acquisition, thereby rendering the learning process both interactive and enjoyable [7, 9, 14, 35]. The current study identified a statistically significant increase in students’ motivation towards the learning material. Engaging in this process within a structured and interactive environment alongside group members, coupled with the entertaining nature of the escape room technique, may have contributed to this heightened motivation. A literature review revealed that an escape room game themed around wound care increased motivation prior to a final exam [7], that the escape room learning technique is highly motivating in surgical education [34], that learning through escape rooms adds enjoyment to the learning experience [8], and that students not only enjoy participating in escape rooms but also experience an increase in their learning motivation as a result [18]. These findings are consistent with those of the current study. In this context, nursing educators may use the escape room technique to create an enjoyable learning environment and enhance student motivation in surgical education.

One of the advantages of using escape rooms is the increased satisfaction stemming from the learning experience [18]. Escape rooms provide students with engaging and interactive educational activities [17] and have been positively received by students [2]. The high satisfaction levels of the students in the present study are consistent with the literature [15, 35]. Additionally, Molina-Torres et al. (2022), in their study on anatomy education, found that when escape rooms were integrated into anatomy teaching, nursing students demonstrated above-average satisfaction levels [36]. In the current study, students’ sense of shared achievement, the competitive drive triggered by the desire to exit the escape room first, the immersive nature of the escape room, and access to surgical attire by solving puzzles may have contributed to increased satisfaction levels. Indeed, these escape room features may set them apart from other simulation-based applications. A review of the literature also reveals that escape rooms provide environments with problem-based and meaningful activities that motivate students and require cooperation [11]; offer the experience of working as a team under pressure [37]; engage students in active and participatory learning through sequential and interconnected tasks, fostering an exciting and competitive spirit [38]; and provide a joyful, fun, and lively action-packed gaming experience for escaping from a “locked” room [39]. In this context, nursing educators can integrate escape room techniques into surgical nursing education to enhance learning outcomes.

In the current study, it was determined that students’ gender, the type of high school from which they graduated, and their simulation experience did not result in a statistically significant difference in the knowledge test and IMMS total posttest scores. These findings align with the existing literature, which indicates that gender is not a determining factor of learning motivation [34, 40]. The findings suggest that these variables are not fundamental determinants of knowledge and motivation in this sample group.

Study limitations

The findings of this study should be interpreted with caution due to several limitations. First, the single-group pretest–posttest design constrained our capacity to directly evaluate the effect of the escape room technique. Nevertheless, the primary objective of this study was to assess the feasibility of an innovative active learning method in nursing education and its influence on student motivation. Second, the administration of the posttest immediately following the implementation and debriefing session may have induced a “new knowledge” effect on the results. The immediate application of the posttest may reflect short-term recall or the effects of the evaluation process rather than long-term knowledge retention. Third, given that participation was voluntary and the activity was extracurricular and enjoyable, students with higher intrinsic motivation may have been more inclined to participate in the study. This could result in “selection bias”, complicating the generalization of the results to the broader student population. Additionally, the fact that the study was conducted at a single center with a small sample size further underscores this limitation. Nonetheless, despite the potential for selection bias due to the voluntary nature of the study, the results of the IMMS administered as a pretest provide an alternative perspective. The statistically significant increase between the pretest and posttest scores indicates that the escape room activity enhanced students’ existing motivation. This suggests that the observed effect is not merely a reflection of the participants’ initial interest but a consequence of the innovative active learning approach. In this context, considering potential confounding factors, this study could be replicated with randomized controlled trials and larger sample groups. The potential final limitation of this study the brevity of the knowledge test used as the data collection tool in this study may be a limitation. The knowledge test was designed in accordance with the learning objectives, and expert opinions were obtained to ensure content validity. In addition, experts were consulted regarding the appropriateness of the number of questions, and no additional questions were suggested. In conclusion, the findings of the current study should be interpreted within the specific context in which the research was conducted, considering all these critical points. Despite these limitations, to the best of our knowledge, this study is unique in that it is the first to use an escape room to demonstrate the importance of using surgical attire.

Conclusion

The results of this study indicate that the escape room game technique can contribute to knowledge, motivation, and satisfaction in surgical education. The integration of this technique into the curriculum by nurse educators has the potential to foster a student-centered, interactive, enjoyable, and motivating learning environment in nursing education. However, comprehensive preparation for the use of this technique is necessary, as well as improved communication and collaboration with educators or observers involved in the implementation process. Furthermore, students should be adequately informed about the use of this technique, and pilot applications should be conducted if necessary. To optimize outcomes in nursing education, randomized controlled and mixed-method studies comparing this technique with traditional or alternative instructional methods may be conducted. Additionally, training sessions for educators on the use of escape room techniques should be organized, institutional support secured, and appropriate physical infrastructure provided. In conclusion, the escape room technique is an innovative method with high potential to contribute to nursing education; however, successful implementation requires thorough planning and technical support.

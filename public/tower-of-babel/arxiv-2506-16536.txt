Enumeration of Virtual Quandles


Abstract
Virtual racks and virtual quandles are nonassociative algebraic structures based on the Reidemeister moves of virtual knots. In this note, we enumerate virtual dihedral quandles and several families of virtual permutation racks and virtual conjugation quandles up to isomorphism. We also classify virtual racks and virtual quandles up to order 8 8 using a computer search. These classifications are based on the conjugacy class structures of rack automorphism groups. In particular, we compute class numbers of holomorphs of finite cyclic groups, which may be of independent interest.

Introduction

In this note, we enumerate virtual dihedral quandles and other families of virtual racks up to isomorphism. These classifications are based on the conjugacy class structures of the automorphism groups of the underlying racks. Using a computer search, we also classify virtual racks and virtual quandles up to order
8
. We provide our code and data in a GitHub repository

1.1
Motivations

In 1992, Fenn and Rourke
[
9
]
introduced nonassociative algebraic structures called
racks
to construct complete invariants of irreducible framed links in connected
3
-manifolds. Racks generalize algebraic structures called
quandles
, which Joyce
[
11
]
and Matveev
[
22
]
independently introduced in 1982 to construct complete invariants of links in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
S^{3}
, and
kei
, which Takasaki
[
29
]
introduced in 1943 to study Riemannian symmetric spaces. Racks, quandles, and kei have enjoyed significant study as knot invariants in geometric topology and in their own rights in quantum algebra and group theory. We refer the reader to a survey of Nelson
[
23
]
and the book of Elhamdadi and Nelson
[
7
]
for accessible introductions to quandle theory, the book of Nosaka
[
24
]
for a reference on racks as they concern low-dimensional topology, and an article of Elhamdadi
[
5
]
for a survey of the algebraic state of the art.

In 2002, Manturov
[
21
]
introduced
virtual racks
, which are racks equipped with a distinguished automorphism. Virtual racks generalize
virtual quandles
, which define invariants of virtual links in thickened surfaces
[
14
,
21
]
, links in certain lens spaces
[
2
]
, and long virtual links
[
20
]
. Virtual racks also generalize
GL-racks
, which can distinguish Legendrian links in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
or
S^{3}
with the standard contact structure
[
12
,
16
,
26
]
, including Legendrian knots not distinguishable by their classical or homological invariants
[
27
]
. More abstractly, virtual racks model braided objects in symmetric categories and actions of virtual braid groups
[
18
]
. Recent papers have further generalized virtual racks to construct invariants of multi-virtual links, which generalize virtual links
[
13
,
15
]
.

Finite virtual quandles are particularly important because they define counting invariants and cohomological invariants of virtual links
[
3
,
14
,
21
]
. In particular, various authors have used
virtual dihedral quandles
to study the Kauffman–Harary conjecture and Fox
n
-coloring invariants of virtual links
[
4
,
8
,
31
]
. The classification of finite virtual quandles is therefore a natural question that extends the classification problems for finite quandles
[
19
,
30
]
and finite GL-racks
[
12
, Sec. 3]
.

1.2
Structure of the paper

In Section

In Section

In Section

Let

be the dihedral quandle of order

is

(1)

v(R_{n})=\varphi(n)\sum_{d\mid n}\frac{1}{\varphi(d)}.

The integer sequence

[

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Racks

Definition 3
.

Let
X
be a set, let
s:X\to S_{X}
be a map, and write
s_{x}:=s(x)
for all elements
x\in X
. We call the pair
R:=(X,s)
a
rack
if

s_{x}s_{y}=s_{s_{x}(y)}s_{x}

for all
x,y\in X
.
If in addition
s_{x}(x)=x
for all
x\in X
, then we say that
R
is a
quandle
.
The
order
of
R
is the cardinality of
X
.

Remark 4
.

Some authors define racks and quandles as sets
X
with a right-distributive nonassociative binary operation
\triangleright:X\times X\to X
satisfying certain axioms. These two definitions are equivalent
[
2
,
5
]
via the formula

s_{y}(x)=x\triangleright y.

Example 5
.

[
7
, Ex. 99]

Let
X
be a set, and fix a permutation
\sigma\in S_{X}
. Define
s:X\rightarrow S_{X}
by
x\mapsto\sigma
, so that
s_{x}(y)=\sigma(y)
for all
x,y\in X
. Then
(X,\sigma)_{\mathrm{perm}}:=(X,s)
is a rack called a
permutation rack
,
cyclic rack
, or
constant action rack
.

Note that
(X,\sigma)_{\mathrm{perm}}
is a quandle if and only if
\sigma
is the identity map
\id_{X}
. In this case, we call
(X,\id_{X})_{\mathrm{perm}}
a
trivial quandle
.

Example 6
.

[
24
, Ex. 2.13]

Let
X
be a union of conjugacy classes in a group
G
, and define
c:X\rightarrow S_{X}
by sending any element
x\in X
to the conjugation map

c_{x}:=[y\mapsto xyx^{-1}].

Then
\Conj X:=(X,c)
is a quandle called a
conjugation quandle
or
conjugacy quandle
.
Note that
\Conj G
is a trivial quandle if and only if
G
is abelian.

Example 7
.

[
7
, Ex. 54]

Let
G
be an abelian additive group. Define
s:G\to S_{G}
by setting

s_{g}(h):=2h-g

for all elements
g,h\in G
. Then
T(G):=(G,s)
is a quandle called a
Takasaki kei
.

In particular, if
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
G=\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
, then we call
T(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})
the
dihedral quandle
of order
n
. We denote
T(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})
by
R_{n}
.

2.2
Virtual racks

Virtual racks are racks equipped with a rack automorphism.

Definition 8
.

Given two racks
(X,s)
and
(Y,t)
, we say that a map
\psi:X\to Y
is a
rack homomorphism
if

\psi s_{x}=t_{\psi(x)}\psi

for all
x\in X
. A
rack isomorphism
is a bijective rack homomorphism.
Automorphisms
of racks are isomorphisms from a rack to itself.
We denote the
automorphism group
of a rack
R
by
\Aut R
.

Definition 9
.

A
virtual rack
is a pair
(R,f)
where
R
is a rack and
f\in\Aut R
is a rack automorphism. We say that
f
is a
virtual structure
on
R
.
If in addition
R
is a quandle, then we call
(R,f)
a
virtual quandle
. We say that
R
is the
underlying rack
(resp.
underlying quandle
) of
(R,f)
.

Example 10
.

Let
P=(X,\sigma)_{\mathrm{perm}}
be a permutation rack. Evidently,
\Aut P=C_{S_{X}}(\sigma)
, so a virtual structure on
P
is precisely a permutation of
X
that commutes with
\sigma
.

Example 11
.

Let
G
be a group. We consider several virtual structures on
\Conj G
. Fix an element
g\in G
.

•

Consider the left-multiplication map
m_{g}:=[h\mapsto gh]
. Then
(\Conj G,m_{g})
is a virtual quandle if and only if
g\in Z(G)

[
16
, Ex. 3.6]
.

•

Consider the conjugation map
c_{g}:=[h\mapsto ghg^{-1}]
. Then
(\Conj G,c_{g})
is a virtual quandle
[
21
, Lemma 1]
. More generally, given any rack
R=(X,s)
and any element
x\in X
, the pair
(R,s_{x})
is a virtual rack.

Example 12
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
be a positive integer, and let
R_{n}
be the dihedral quandle of order
n
. A result of Elhamdadi, Macquarrie, and Restrepo
[
6
, Thm. 2.3]
states that
\Aut R_{n}
is the group of affine transformations of
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
, which is isomorphic to the holomorph
{\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}\rtimes(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}
of
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
. Hence, virtual structures on
R_{n}
are precisely affine transformations

T_{a,u}:=[x\mapsto a+ux]

with
a\in\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
and
u\in(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
.

Definition 13
.

Let
V_{1}=(R_{1},f_{1})
and
V_{2}=(R_{2},f_{2})
be virtual racks. A
virtual rack homomorphism
from
V_{1}
to
V_{2}
is a rack homomorphism
\psi
from
R_{1}
to
R_{2}
that intertwines the virtual structures:

\psi f_{1}=f_{2}\psi.

Virtual rack isomorphisms
are bijective virtual rack homomorphisms.

3
Enumeration results

3.1
Key observation

The following is immediately evident from Definition
13
.

Proposition 14
.

Let
f_{1},f_{2}
be two virtual structures on a rack
R
. Then
(R,f_{1})\cong(R,f_{2})
if and only if
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are conjugate in
\Aut R
.

In other words, classifying virtual structures on a given rack up to isomorphism is equivalent to characterizing the conjugacy classes of the rack’s automorphism group.

3.2.2
Class numbers as rack invariants

Notation
.

We use the following notation for the remainder of this paper.

For all racks
R
, let
v(R)
denote the number of isomorphism classes of virtual racks whose underlying rack is isomorphic to
R
.

Given a group
G
, let
k(G)
be the class number of
G
, that is, the number of conjugacy classes of
G
.

Proposition
14
implies that for all racks
R
,

(2)

v(R)=k(\Aut R).

In particular,
v(R)
is a rack invariant.
The rest of this note is dedicated to using Equation (
2
) to compute
v(R)
for various families of racks.

Notation
.

For all nonnegative integers
n\geq 0
, let
p(n)
denote the number of partitions of
n

[
25
,
A000041
]
.

Example 15
.

Let
P=(X,\sigma)_{\mathrm{perm}}
be a permutation rack. By Example
10
and Equation (
2
),

v(P)=k(\Aut P)=k(C_{S_{X}}(\sigma)).

For example, let
X
be a finite set of cardinality
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. If

is a trivial quandle, then
\Aut P=S_{X}
, so

. If, instead,
\sigma\in S_{n}
is an

C_{S_{X}}(\sigma)=\langle\sigma\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}

.

Remark 16
.

Example

coincides with the classification of GL-racks whose underlying racks are permutation racks
[

Virtual conjugation quandles

The following result of Bardakov, Nasybullov, and Singh

Lemma 17

Let
G

\Aut_{\mathsf{Grp}}(G)
denote its automorphism group. Then

if and only if

\Aut_{\mathsf{Grp}}(G)=\Aut(\Conj G).

Example 18
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
be a positive integer, and let
S_{n}
denote the symmetric group on
n
letters. We compute

as follows. Since

is a trivial quandle, Example

.
If
n\neq 2
, then
Z(S_{n})=1

implies that

\Aut(\Conj S_{n})=\begin{cases}S_{n},&\text{if $n\neq 6$;}\\
S_{6}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},&\text{if $n=6$.}\end{cases}

Since
k(S_{n})=p(n)
for all

and
k(S_{6}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})=13
, Equation (

) and the above discussion yield

v(\Conj S_{n})=\begin{cases}p(n),&\text{if $n\neq 6$;}\\
13,&\text{if $n=6$.}\end{cases}

Example 19

Let

be a positive integer, and let

denote the alternating group on

v(\Conj A_{n})=\begin{cases}p(n),&\text{if $n\neq 2,6$;}\\
1,&\text{if $n=2$,}\\
13,&\text{if $n=6$.}\end{cases}

Example 20

For all odd primes

Virtual dihedral quandles

In this section, we classify virtual dihedral quandles up to isomorphism. In particular, we prove Theorem
1
.

Notation
.

For the remainder of the paper, let
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
be a positive integer, let
A:=\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
, and let
G:=A\rtimes A^{\times}
be the holomorph of
A
.

4.1
Classification

First, we recall Example
12
: Virtual structures on the dihedral quandle
R_{n}
of order
n
are precisely the affine transformations
T_{a,u}:A\to A
defined by

x\mapsto a+ux

with
a\in A
and
u\in A^{\times}
. Indeed, the mapping
T_{a,u}\mapsto(a,u)
defines a group isomorphism from
\Aut R_{n}
to
G

[
6
, Thm. 2.3]
.

In the following, we classify virtual dihedral quandles up to isomorphism.

Proposition 21
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
be a positive integer, and let
T_{a,u},T_{b,v}
be virtual structures on the dihedral quandle
R_{n}
of order
n
. Then
(R_{n},T_{a,u})\cong(R_{n},T_{b,v})
if and only if
u=v
and

b=ya+(1-u)x

for some element
(x,y)\in G
.

By Proposition
14
, Proposition
21
is equivalent to the following.

Proposition 22
.

For all
u\in A^{\times}
, define the group

(3)

A_{u}:=(1-u)A\leq A.

Then two elements
(a,u),(b,v)\in G
are conjugate if and only if
(
i
)

u=v
and
(
ii
)

a
and
b
lie in the same orbit under the action of
A^{\times}
on
A/A_{u}
.

Proof.

Conjugation in
G
is given by

(x,y)(a,u)(x,y)^{-1}=(x+ya,yu)(-y^{-1}x,y^{-1})=(ya+(1-u)x,u).

The claim follows immediately from this computation.
∎

4.2
Enumeration

Finally, we prove Theorem
1
. By Equation (
2
), Theorem
1
is equivalent to the following.

Theorem 23
.

Let
\varphi
denote Euler’s totient function.
If
A=\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
and
{G=A\rtimes A^{\times}}
, then

k(G)=\varphi(n)\sum_{d\mid n}\frac{1}{\varphi(d)}.

Proof.

For all elements
u\in A^{\times}
, define the group
A_{u}
as in Equation (
3
), and let
\psi(u)
denote the number of orbits of the action of
A^{\times}
on
A/A_{u}
.
By Proposition
22
,

k(G)=\sum_{u\in A^{\times}}\psi(u).

We compute the right-hand side.
For each element
u\in A^{\times}
, consider the integer

d_{u}:=\gcd(n,1-u).

By construction,

(4)

A/A_{u}\cong\mathbb{Z}/d_{u}\mathbb{Z}.

Let
\tau(d_{u})
be the number of divisors of
d_{u}
.
By Equation (
4
) and a standard result of group theory
[
17
, Lemma 1.3]
, the number of orbits of the action of
(A/A_{u})^{\times}
on
A/A_{u}
equals
\tau(d_{u})
. Since
A^{\times}
projects onto
(A/A_{u})^{\times}
, it follows that
\psi(u)=\tau(d_{u})
. Therefore,

(5)

k(G)=\sum_{u\in A^{\times}}\tau(d_{u})=\sum_{u\in A^{\times}}\sum_{d\mid d_{u}}1=\sum_{d\mid n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in A^{\times}\\
d\mid d_{u}\end{subarray}}1,

where in the last equality we have used the fact that the sums are finite.

Note that if
d\mid n
and
u\in A^{\times}
, then
d\mid d_{u}
if and only if
u\equiv 1
(mod
d
); the latter condition holds if and only if
u
lies in the kernel of the projection
\pi_{d}:A^{\times}\twoheadrightarrow(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
.
In other words, the rightmost sum in Equation (
5
) equals the cardinality of
\ker\pi_{d}
.
By the first isomorphism theorem, this cardinality equals

|\ker\pi_{d}|=\frac{|A^{\times}|}{|(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})^{\times}|}=\frac{\varphi(n)}{\varphi(d)}.

Hence,

k(G)=\sum_{d\mid n}|\ker\pi_{d}|=\sum_{d\mid n}\frac{\varphi(n)}{\varphi(d)}.

∎

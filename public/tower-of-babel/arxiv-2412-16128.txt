Abstract
. We study rationality properties of irreducible characters of finite groups. We show that the continuity of 2 2 -rationality is a phenomenon that can be detected in the principal 2 2 -block, thus refining a recent result of N. N. Hung. We also propose a conjectural group theoretical criterion for the continuity gap at level 1 1 for all primes.

1.
Introduction

For
K
an abelian number field, its
conductor
c(K)
is
defined as the smallest positive integer
n
such that
K\subseteq{\mathbb{Q}}_{n}
, where

{\mathbb{Q}}_{n}
denotes the extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}
generated by a primitive
n
th root of
unity. Given a finite group
G
and a complex irreducible character

\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)
, the
conductor of
\chi
, denoted
c(\chi)
, is defined
as
c({\mathbb{Q}}(\chi))
, the conductor of the field generated by the values of
\chi
.
For a prime
p
, the
p
-rationality level
of
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)
is
the non-negative integer
\alpha
such that
c(\chi)_{p}=p^{\alpha}
. (Here, we write

n_{p}
for the exact power of
p
dividing an integer
n
.) Characters with
specific
p
-rationality level have been extensively studied in the literature.
For example, the
p
-rational characters are the characters with
p
-rationality
level
0
and the
almost
p
-rational characters
are the ones with

p
-rationality level
\leq 1
. Both of these families have been shown to
influence the (local) structure of
G
(see, for instance

[
12
,
18
,
10
,
14
]
).

In
[
9
]
, Hung conjectured that if a finite group
G
has some
irreducible character
\chi
of degree coprime to
p
with
p
-rationality level

\alpha\geq 2
, then
\operatorname{Irr}(G)
contains characters of degree coprime to
p
with

p
-rationality level
\beta
for any
2\leq\beta\leq\alpha
. We will
refer to this phenomenon as the
continuity of
p
-rationality
.
In the same paper, he has proven the above conjecture for
p=2
. In the final
section of
[
9
]
, Hung suggests the existence of a principal
block version of
[
9
, Conj. 1.1]
:

Conjecture 1.1

(Hung)
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
p
be a prime. If the principal
p
-block

B_{0}(G)
of
G
contains an irreducible character of degree coprime to
p

with
c(\chi)_{p}=p^{\alpha}
then it contains irreducible characters of degree
coprime to
p
and
c(\psi)_{p}=p^{\beta}
for all
2\leq\beta\leq\alpha
.

In our first main result, we confirm Hung’s prediction for the prime
p=2
.

Theorem A
.

Conjecture
1.1
holds for
p=2
.

Hung’s Conjecture is implied by the celebrated Alperin–McKay–Navarro
conjecture
[
16
, Conj. B]
. In particular, our Theorem A provides
further evidence for the validity of the Alperin–McKay–Navarro conjecture (at
least for principal blocks and the prime
p=2
), one of the few global-local
conjectures that remain unreduced at the time of this writing.

Assuming
[
20
, Conj. A]
, if
\alpha\geq 2
is the largest

p
-rationality level of characters of degree coprime to
p
of a finite group

G
then it is attained at an element of
\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(G))
. Given that

[
20
, Conj. A]
has been proven for
p=2
in
[
13
]
, Theorem A
above refines the main result of
[
9
]
. Furthermore, using the fact
that
[
20
, Conj. A]
has been reduced to a problem on simple groups,
we are also able to reduce Conjecture
1.1
to a problem on simple
groups (see Theorem
3.3
) for all primes
p
.

Interestingly, the continuity of
p
-rationality stops at level
2
. This is
because there are groups that have an irreducible character of degree coprime to

p
and
p
-rationality level
2
but none of
p
-rationality level
1
. That
is to say, in these cases all the irreducible characters of degree coprime to

p
that are almost
p
-rational are in fact
p
-rational. An example was
already given in
[
9
, Rem. 1.6]
. We offer here an explanation of this
gap by conjecturally characterizing the finite groups all of whose almost

p
-rational characters of degree coprime to
p
are
p
-rational.

Conjecture B
.

Let
G
be a finite group,
p
be a prime and
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(G)
. Write

K={\bf N}_{G}(P)/\Phi(P)
and
Q=P/\Phi(P)
.
Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

No irreducible character of
G
of degree coprime to
p
has

p
-rationality level
1
.

(2)

For every
1\neq y\in Q
we have:

(a)

{\bf N}_{K}(\langle y\rangle)/{\bf C}_{K}(\langle y\rangle)\cong\operatorname{Aut}(\langle y\rangle)
, and

(b)

{\bf N}_{K}(\langle y\rangle)/Q
acts trivially on the set of conjugacy
classes of
{\bf C}_{K}(y)/Q
.

Notice that for
p=2
all three conditions above are trivially satisfied as,
by definition, the conductor of a character cannot have
2
-part equal to
2
.
In Theorem
5.5
, we prove that Conjecture B holds for groups
with a normal Sylow
p
-subgroup. As we will explain in Sections
2

and
5
, Theorem
5.5
implies that
Conjecture B would be a consequence of the McKay–Navarro conjecture

[
16
, Conj. A]
. In particular, we derive that Conjecture B holds for

p
-solvable, sporadic, symmetric and alternating groups, and simple groups of
Lie type in defining characteristic by
[
25
,
16
,
2
,
23
]
.

It is worth mentioning that Hung speculates that the gap on the continuity of

p
-rationality might depend on the action of
{\bf N}_{G}(P)
on
P/P^{\prime}
but does not
know
the precise connection

[
9
, Rem. 1.6]
. However, both local
conditions in Conjecture B above are necessary to explain the gap, since the
groups
{\sf{C}}_{3}
and
\texttt{SmallGroup(216,128)}\cong({\sf{C}}_{3}\times{\sf{C}}_{3}\times{\sf{C}}_{3})\rtimes{\sf D}_{8}
possess

3
and
3
-rationality level 1.

Conjecture B can be seen as a new way of relating the local structure of a group
with its character values. Unlike the phenomena observed in

[
20
, Conj. A]
and
[
9
, Conj. 1.1]
, we note that the absence
of irreducible characters of degree coprime to
p

p
-rationality level
1

in a group

is not a property that can be detected in the principal block
(see the end of Section
5
). We also offer a version for

5.7
.

Our paper is organized as follows: In Section
2
, we collect some
preliminary results on the

-part of character conductors. In
Section

, we reduce Conjecture
1.1
to a problem
on finite simple groups. This problem is settled for the prime

in

Section

p
-subgroup.

2.
On the
p
-part of character conductors

For
\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}
we denote by
\sigma_{\alpha}\in\operatorname{Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}^{\operatorname{ab}}/{\mathbb{Q}})
the Galois
automorphism that fixes
p
-power order roots of unity and sends
p^{\prime}
-roots of
unity to their
1+p^{\alpha}
th power. By a slight abuse of notation, we
still denote by
\sigma_{\alpha}
its restriction to the cyclotomic field
{\mathbb{Q}}_{n}
for
any positive integer
n
. It is not difficult to see that

\langle\sigma_{\alpha}\rangle\leq\operatorname{Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}_{n}/{\mathbb{Q}})
is a finite
p
-group for any
\alpha

and any
n
. Moreover, if
\chi
is not
p
-rational, then the
p
-rationality
level of
\chi
is precisely the smallest
\alpha\geq 1
such that
\chi
is

\sigma_{\alpha}
-invariant (see
[
21
, Lemma 4.1]
, for instance). While for

p=2
the odd-degree 2-rational characters of
G
are exactly those fixed by

\sigma_{1}
by
[
26
, Thm 2.5]
, for odd primes we cannot characterise the

p
-rational characters in terms of the
\sigma_{\alpha}
simply because

\operatorname{Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}_{mp^{a}}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{m})
is not a
p
-group.

The celebrated McKay–Navarro conjecture
[
16
, Conj. A]
implies that
there should exist McKay bijections that preserve the
p
-part of character
conductors. The following is a consequence (of the maximal defect case) of

[
16
, Conj. B]
. We state it in the weakest form that suffices for our
purposes. Here, we denote by
\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)
the set of irreducible characters
of
G
whose degree is not divisible by
p
and by
\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(G))
the set of
irreducible characters that belong to the principal
p
-block of
G
.

Conjecture 2.1

(Navarro)
.

Let
G
be a finite group,
p
be a prime and
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(G)
. There is a
bijection

{}^{*}:\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)\rightarrow\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}({\bf N}_{G}(P))

such that for all
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)
we have

(1)

c(\chi)_{p}=c(\chi^{*})_{p}
, and

(2)

\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(G))
if and only if
\chi^{*}\in\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}({\bf N}_{G}(P)))
.

It is not difficult to see that Conjecture
1.1
is a consequence of
Conjecture
2.1
(and hence of
[
16
, Conj. B]
). We do so in
Lemma
2.7
below. We will prove in Section
5

that Conjecture B (as well as its principal block version) also follows from
Conjecture
2.1
; however, this will require significantly more
effort.

We next collect some useful results on the
p
-part of character conductors.
Throughout,
G
denotes a finite group.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(G)
,
H\leq G
with
p\nmid|G:H|
and
{\bf C}_{G}(P)\subseteq H
,
and let
\alpha\geq 1
. If
\psi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(B_{0}(H))
is fixed by
\sigma_{\alpha}
then
there is a
\sigma_{\alpha}
-invariant constituent of
\psi^{G}
in
\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(G))
.
In other words, if
c(\psi)_{p}\geq p
then there is a constituent
\chi
of

\psi^{G}
with
c(\chi)_{p}\leq c(\psi)_{p}
.

Proof.

Mimic the proof of
[
14
, Lemma 3.7]
for
\sigma_{\alpha}
instead of

\sigma
, using that
\sigma_{\alpha}
also has
p
-power order.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
N\unlhd G
and
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(G)
. Let
\theta\in\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(N))
be

P
-invariant and with
c(\theta)_{p}\geq p^{2}
, and let

\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(G))
lie over
\theta
.
Then
c(\chi)_{p}\geq p^{2}
.

Proof.

Assume that
c(\chi)_{p}\leq p
so that
\chi
is fixed by
\sigma_{1}
. Then
\chi_{N}

is fixed by
\sigma_{1}
. This implies that
\sigma_{1}
permutes the set of

G
-conjugates of
\theta
, which has size not divisible by
p
. In particular,

\sigma_{1}
fixes some
G
-conjugate of
\theta
so it fixes
\theta
, and

c(\theta)_{p}\leq p
, a contradiction.
∎

Theorem 2.4

(Alperin–Dade)
.

Let
N\unlhd G
with
|G:N|
not divisible by
p
. Assume that
G=N{\bf C}_{G}(P)

for
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(G)
. Then restriction defines a bijection

\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(G))\to\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(N))
. In particular,
c(\chi)_{p}=c(\chi_{N})_{p}
for
every
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(G))
.

Proof.

The first part was proved by Alperin
[
1
]
when
G/N
is solvable, and by
Dade
[
4
]
without the solvability assumption. Since restriction commutes
with Galois action, it follows that
\chi
is fixed by
\sigma_{\alpha}
if and only
if
\chi_{N}
is fixed by
\sigma_{\alpha}
for any
\alpha
.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
N\unlhd G
with
|G:N|
not divisible by
p
. Let
\theta\in\operatorname{Irr}(N)
,

\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G|\theta)
and assume
c(\chi)_{p}\geq p
. Then

c(\theta)_{p}\leq c(\chi)_{p}
. If, moreover,
c(\chi)_{p}
or
c(\theta)_{p}
are

\geq p^{2}
, then
c(\chi)_{p}=c(\theta)_{p}
.

Proof.

See
[
9
, Lemma 2.4(i) and (iii)]
.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\varphi,\psi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)
. Assume
\chi:=\varphi\psi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)
and

c(\varphi)_{p}\leq c(\chi)_{p}
. Assume further that
G/\ker\psi
is a
p
-group.
Then

(1)

c(\psi)_{p}\leq c(\chi)_{p}
, and

(2)

either
c(\varphi)_{p}=c(\chi)_{p}
or
c(\psi)_{p}=c(\chi)_{p}
.

Proof.

See
[
9
, Lemma 3.3]
.
∎

If
G
is a
p
-group and
\lambda\in\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)
, then
G/\ker\lambda
is cyclic and

c(\lambda)_{p}=|G/\ker\lambda|
. In particular, Conjecture
1.1
trivially
holds for
p
-groups as these have a unique
p
-block, namely the principal
one. We prove below that Conjecture
1.1
follows from
Conjecture
2.1
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Suppose that
G
satisfies the conclusion of Conjecture
2.1
for

p
. Then if the principal
p
-block
B_{0}(G)
of
G
contains irreducible
characters of degree coprime to
p
with
c(\chi)_{p}=p^{\alpha}
then it contains
irreducible characters of degree coprime to
p
and
c(\psi)_{p}=p^{\beta}
for
all
2\leq\beta\leq\alpha
.

Proof.

Let
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(G)
. By hypothesis, we can assume that
P\unlhd G
.
Let
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(G))
with
c(\chi)_{p}=p^{\alpha}
, and let

\lambda\in\operatorname{Irr}(P)
be under
\chi
. Since
\alpha\geq 2
,
by Lemma
2.5
, we have that
c(\lambda)=c(\chi)_{p}
. Now,
P
has
a linear character
\lambda_{\beta}
of conductor
p^{\beta}
for every

2\leq\beta\leq\alpha
, as noticed just before the statement of this lemma.
We can take
\chi_{\beta}\in\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(G))
over
\lambda_{\beta}
by

[
15
, Thm 9.4]
. Then
\chi_{\beta}
has degree coprime to
p
and

c(\chi_{\beta})=p^{\beta}
using again Lemma
2.5
.
∎

3.
A reduction theorem for Hung’s conjecture

The purpose of this section is to show that if certain conditions hold for
almost/quasi-simple groups and a prime
p
, then Conjecture
1.1

holds for all finite groups with respect to the prime
p
.
More specifically, our reduction theorem depends on the following conjecture
concerning the representation theory of the finite non-abelian simple groups.

Conjecture 3.1
.

Let
p
be a prime and let
S
be a finite non-abelian simple group of order
divisible by
p
. Then the following hold:

(1)

Let
X
be a quasi-simple group with
X/{\mathbf{Z}}(X)\cong S
and
{\mathbf{Z}}(X)
of
order coprime to
p
. Let
Y
be almost quasi-simple with component

F^{*}(Y)=X
and
Y/X
a
p
-group. If
Q\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(Y)
and
\exp(Q/Q^{\prime})=p^{e}
,
then every character in
\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(Y)
is
\sigma_{e}
-invariant
(or equivalently, it has
p
-rationality level at most
e
).

(2)

Let
A
be an almost simple group with socle
F^{*}(A)=S
such that
A/S

is a
p
-group. Assume
\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(S))
contains
A
-invariant characters
with
p
-rationality level
\alpha
. Then
\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(S))
contains

A
-invariant characters with
p
-rationality level
\beta
for every

2\leq\beta\leq\alpha
.

We recall that a group
S
is said to be involved in a group
G
if there exist

N,K\leq G
with
N\unlhd K
and
K/N\cong S
.

Remark 3.2
.

Let
G
be a finite group,
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(G)
and assume
\exp(P/P^{\prime})\geq p^{2}
.
By work of Navarro and Tiep in
[
20
, Thms B and 5.7]
, if
Conjecture
3.1
(a) holds for every finite simple group involved in

G
, then
\exp(P/P^{\prime})
is the largest
p
-rationality level in
\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)
,
and such largest
p
-rationality level is attained at
\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(G))
. Note
that Conjecture
3.1
(a) easily implies

[
20
, Conj. 5.4]
. In particular, this has been shown to hold for

p=2
by the main result of
[
13
]
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
G
be a finite group,
p
a prime. Assume that
Conjecture
3.1
holds for every non-abelian simple group
involved in
G
. If
\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(G))
contains a character of

p
-rationality level
\alpha\geq 2
, then
\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(G))
contains
characters of
p
-rationality level
\beta
for every
2\leq\beta\leq\alpha
.

Proof.

We argue by induction on
|G|
. We may assume
\alpha
is the largest

p
-rationality level among the
p^{\prime}
-degree characters in the principal block.
The rest of the proof is divided into steps.

Step 1: We may assume that any minimal normal subgroup of
G
is
semisimple of order divisible by
p
.

We may assume that
{\bf O}_{p^{\prime}}(G)=1
by induction since

\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(G/{\bf O}_{p^{\prime}}(G)))\subseteq\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(G))
. Let
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(G)
and let
N
be a
minimal normal subgroup of
G
. Assume that
N
is a
p
-group, so
N\leq P
.
By Remark
3.2
and since we are assuming
Conjecture
3.1
we have
\exp(P/P^{\prime})=p^{\alpha}
.
Since
\exp(N)=p
we have

\exp(P/P^{\prime})=\exp((P/N)/(P/N)^{\prime})\quad\text{or}\quad\exp(P/P^{\prime})=p\cdot\exp((P/N)/(P/N)^{\prime}).

In either case, we are done because
\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(G/N))
contains characters
of every
p
-rationality level
2\leq\beta\leq\alpha-1
by induction. The claim
follows.

Step 2: Let
N
be a minimal normal subgroup of
G
. We may assume

G/N
is a
p
-group. In particular,
B_{0}(G)
is the unique block covering

B_{0}(N)
.

Since we are assuming Conjecture
3.1
(a), the largest
p
-parts of
the character conductors in
\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(G))
and
\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(PN))

coincide (see Remark
3.2
). Therefore it suffices to prove
that if
\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(PN))
contains a character of
p
-rationality level

\beta\geq 2
then so does
\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(G))
.

For this, let
\psi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(PN))
with
c(\psi)_{p}=p^{\beta}
and write

H=PN{\bf C}_{G}(P)
. By Theorem
2.4
,
\psi
extends to a
character
\hat{\psi}\in\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(H))
with
c(\hat{\psi})_{p}=p^{\beta}
.
By Lemma
2.2
there is some
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(G))
lying over

\hat{\psi}
with
c(\chi)_{p}\leq p^{\beta}
.
Notice that
\chi
lies over
\theta:=\psi_{N}\in\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(N))
. Also notice
that
\psi=\rho\hat{\theta}
for some
\rho\in\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(PN/N)
by Gallagher’s
theorem, where
\hat{\theta}\in\operatorname{Irr}(PN)
is the canonical extension of
\theta

given by
[
17
, Cor. 6.2 and 6.4]
, with
c(\theta)=c(\hat{\theta})
.
By Lemma
2.6
, we have
c(\rho)_{p}\leq c(\psi)_{p}=p^{\beta}
and
either (1)
c(\hat{\theta})_{p}=c(\psi)_{p}
or (2)
c(\rho)_{p}=c(\psi)_{p}
.

In case (1) we have that
c(\theta)_{p}=p^{\beta}\geq p^{2}
. By
Lemma
2.3
this yields that

c(\chi)_{p}\geq p^{2}
and by using Lemma
2.5
we conclude

c(\chi)_{p}=p^{\beta}
, as desired.

In case (2), we have that
c(\rho)_{p}=p^{\beta}
, so
\exp(PN/(PN)^{\prime})\geq p^{\beta}

because
PN/\ker\rho
is cyclic and
(PN)^{\prime}=P^{\prime}N\subseteq\ker\rho
(recall that
N

is perfect by Step 1).
If all characters in
\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(G/N))
have
p
-rationality level strictly
smaller than
\beta
then Remark
3.2
implies

\exp(PN/(PN)^{\prime})<p^{\beta}
, which is a contradiction. Hence some character in

\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(G/N))
has
p
-rationality level at least
\beta

and the inductive hypothesis guarantees the existence of a character in

\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(G/N))\subseteq\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(G))
of
p
-rationality level
exactly
\beta
.

The last claim follows from
[
15
, Cor. 9.6]
.

Final step:

Let
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(G))
with
c(\chi)_{p}=p^{\alpha}\geq p^{2}
. Let
N
be a
minimal normal subgroup of
G
. By Step 1, we can write

N=S_{1}\times\dots\times S_{t}
where the
S_{i}
are isomorphic non-abelian
simple groups of order divisible by
p
permuted transitively by
G
. By
Step 2,
G/N
is a
p
-group. In particular,

\chi_{N}=\theta\in\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(N))
is irreducible with

c(\theta)_{p}=p^{\gamma}\leq p^{\alpha}
.

Arguing as in the second paragraph of Step 2, we can write

\chi=\hat{\theta}\rho
where
\hat{\theta}
is the canonical extension of
\theta

to
G
and
\rho\in\operatorname{Irr}(G/N)
with
p^{\alpha}=c(\rho)_{p}
or
p^{\alpha}=c(\theta)_{p}
.
If
p^{\alpha}=c(\rho)_{p}
then, using that the result is true for
p
-groups
we are done. Otherwise,
p^{\alpha}=c(\theta)_{p}
. Let
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}\}
be a transversal of

{\bf N}_{G}(S_{1})
in
G
, chosen so that
S_{i}=(S_{1})^{x_{i}}
. Then we can write

\theta=\theta_{1}^{x_{1}}\times\dots\times\theta_{t}^{x_{t}}
, where

\theta_{i}\in\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(S_{1}))
. Since
\theta
is
G
-invariant,
\theta_{i}

and
\theta_{1}
are
\operatorname{Aut}(S_{1})
-conjugate, for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
.
In particular,
c(\theta_{i})_{p}=c(\theta_{1})_{p}
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,t\}

and actually
c(\theta_{1})=c(\theta)_{p}=p^{\alpha}
.

As
\theta
is
G
-invariant,
\theta_{1}
is
{\bf N}_{G}(S_{1})
-invariant. Since we
may
view
{\bf N}_{G}(S_{1})/{\bf C}_{G}(S_{1})\leq\operatorname{Aut}(S_{1})
and
{\bf N}_{G}(S_{1})/S_{1}{\bf C}_{G}(S_{1})

is a
p
-group, it follows from Conjecture
3.1
(b) that
S_{1}

has
{\bf N}_{G}(S_{1})
-invariant characters
\eta_{\beta}\in\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(S_{1}))

with
c(\eta_{\beta})_{p}=p^{\beta}
for every
2\leq\beta\leq\alpha
.
It is straightforward to check that

\theta_{\beta}:=\eta_{\beta}^{x_{1}}\times\dots\times\eta_{\beta}^{x_{t}}

is
G
-invariant and has degree coprime to
p
. Further,
\theta_{\beta}
lies in

B_{0}(N)
by
[
22
, Lemma 2.1(b)]
.
Notice also that
c(\theta_{\beta})_{p}=c(\eta_{\beta})_{p}=p^{\beta}
. By

[
17
, Cor. 6.2 and 6.4]
there is a canonical extension

\hat{\theta}_{\beta}\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)
of
\theta_{\beta}
with

c(\hat{\theta}_{\beta})=c(\theta_{\beta})
, so
c(\hat{\theta}_{\beta})_{p}=p^{\beta}
.
Since
G/N
is a
p
-group, using
[
15
, Cor. 9.6]
we conclude that

\hat{\theta}_{\beta}
lies in the principal
p
-block, as desired.
∎

4.
On almost simple groups and Theorem A

The purpose of this Section is to show that Conjecture
3.1
(b)
holds for every finite simple group when
p=2
. Our first observation is for
arbitrary primes
p
:

Proposition 4.1
.

Conjecture
3.1
(b) holds for any prime
p
whenever
S
is a
sporadic simple group, the Tits group, an alternating group
{\mathfrak{A}}_{n}
with

n\geq 5
, or a simple group of Lie type in characteristic
p
.

Proof.

As argued in the proof of
[
9
, Prop. 5.1]
, alternating groups, sporadic
simple groups and groups of Lie type in characteristic
p
have

c(\chi)_{p}\leq p^{2}
for all of their irreducible characters
\chi
.
∎

To deal with the groups of Lie type in non-defining characteristic we use the
following setup. Let
{\mathbf{G}}
be a simple algebraic group of simply connected type
over an algebraically closed field of characteristic
r
and
F:{\mathbf{G}}\to{\mathbf{G}}
a
Steinberg endomorphism, with finite group of fixed points
G={\mathbf{G}}^{F}
. By
Proposition
4.1
, to show Conjecture
3.1
(b) it remains
to consider the non-abelian simple quotients
S

of groups
G
as just described, for primes
p\neq r
. Assume
S=G/{\mathbf{Z}}(G)
. Now
the irreducible characters
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(S))
are the deflations of
characters
\tilde{\chi}\in\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(G))
containing
{\mathbf{Z}}(G)
in their kernel,
and all automorphisms of
S
are induced by automorphisms of
G
, so to prove
the validity of Conjecture
3.1
(b) we can argue using characters
of
G
. We refer to
[
6
]
for notation and background on characters of
finite reductive groups.

The following observation might be of independent interest:

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
G
be as above. Then any unipotent character of
G
of odd degree is
rational valued.

Proof.

By
[
6
, Prop. 4.5.5]
the character fields of unipotent characters are
generated by their Frobenius eigenvalues, except for the principal series
characters of
E_{7}
and
E_{8}
lying in exceptional families. Now for classical
types all Frobenius eigenvalues are
\pm 1
, and for the exceptional types a
quick inspection shows that all irrational unipotent characters have even
degree.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

Conjecture
3.1
holds for
p=2
.

Proof.

As pointed out in Remark
3.2
, Part (a) has been shown in

[
13
]
. For Part (b), by Proposition
4.1
we only need to
consider
S
a simple group of Lie type in odd characteristic
r
. Let
{\mathbf{G}},F

and
G={\mathbf{G}}^{F}
be as above such that
S=G/{\mathbf{Z}}(G)
, and let
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{2}(G)
. By

[
20
, Prop. 3.9]
,
P/P^{\prime}
has exponent 2 and thus by

[
13
, Thm 1]
all
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(S))
have 2-rationality level
\alpha\leq 2

and there is nothing to prove, unless
G
is either
E_{6}(\epsilon q)_{\operatorname{sc}}
or

\operatorname{SL}_{2n}(\epsilon q)
with
q\equiv\epsilon\pmod{4}
and

not a 2-power. It remains to

We first make some general observations. By Broué–Michel

[
3
, Thm 9.12]
, the principal
2
-block of
G
lies in the union of
Lusztig series

\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(G))\subseteq\bigsqcup_{t}{\mathcal{E}}(G,t)

where
t
runs over
2
-elements in a group
G^{*}
dual to
G
. Furthermore, by
Lusztig’s character degree formula (Jordan decomposition,

[
6
, Thm 2.6.22]
), for
\chi\in{\mathcal{E}}(G,t)

to have odd degree,

has to be 2-central in
G^{*}
, that is to say, it must
lie in the centre of some Sylow
2
-subgroup of
G^{*}
. By
Proposition
4.2
the claim is vacuous for unipotent characters.
Let
A
be such that
G\unlhd A
.

Now first assume
G=E_{6}(\epsilon q)_{\operatorname{sc}}
with
4|(q-\epsilon)
. By
[
13
, Tab. 1]

the centre
Z
of a Sylow 2-subgroup of
G
is a cyclic group of order

(q-\epsilon)_{2}
and is centralised by a (Levi) subgroup
D_{5}(\epsilon q)(q-\epsilon)
.
Now note that all non-trivial elements

have connected centraliser
(of type
D_{5}
). Thus, by
[
9
, Thm 5.6]
the
2
-rationality level

\alpha_{t}
of all
\chi\in{\mathcal{E}}(G,t)
is the same and given by
|t|=2^{\alpha_{t}}
.
Now, as
\mathbf{C}_{{\mathbf{G}}^{*}}(t)
is connected, for each

there is a unique
semisimple character
\chi_{t}\in{\mathcal{E}}(G,t)

[
6
, Def. 2.6.9, Cor. 2.6.18]
.
By
[
5
, Thm B]
all
\chi_{t}
lie in the principal
2-block of
G
. If
{\mathcal{E}}(G,t)
contains an
A
-invariant character, then in
particular
{\mathcal{E}}(G,t)
is
A
-stable, and hence
\chi_{t}
is
A
-invariant.
But then also all
{\mathcal{E}}(G,t^{k})
are
A
-stable for any

by

[

, Prop. 7.2]
. Since
\chi_{t^{k}}
is the unique semisimple character
in
{\mathcal{E}}(G,t^{k})
this forces
\chi_{t^{k}}
to be

-invariant. Thus any

2\leq\beta\leq\alpha_{t}
occurs as
2
-rationality level of some

\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{2^{\prime}}(B_{0}(S))
, as desired.

Finally, assume

with
4|(q-\epsilon)
and

not a 2-power.
In this case, by
[
8
, Tab. 4.5.1]
, all centralisers of 2-central

are connected, and they are Levi

[

, Thm 21.14]
all Lusztig series
{\mathcal{E}}(G,t)
,
for 2-elements

lie in the principal 2-block. But then we can argue
exactly as in the previous case.
∎

5.
On the
p
-rationality gap

The purpose of this section is to show that Conjecture B of the introduction
holds for groups with a normal Sylow
p
-subgroup. In order to do so we need to
recall the concept of semi-inertia subgroup and some of its properties.

Let
N\unlhd G
. The
semi-inertia subgroup of
\theta\in\operatorname{Irr}(N)
is
defined as

G_{\theta}^{*}:=\{g\in G\mid\theta^{g}=\theta^{\tau}\text{ for some }\tau\in\operatorname{Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}(\theta)/{\mathbb{Q}})\}.

Notice that
G_{\theta}\leq G_{\theta}^{*}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
N\unlhd G
and
\theta\in\operatorname{Irr}(N)
. Let
G_{\theta}^{*}
be the semi-inertia
subgroup of
\theta
in
G
.

(1)

If
\psi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G_{\theta}|\theta)
then

{\mathbb{Q}}(\psi^{G_{\theta}^{*}})={\mathbb{Q}}(\psi^{G})
.

(2)

The map
g\mapsto\tau
where
\theta^{g}=\theta^{\tau}
is a well-defined
group homomorphism

G_{\theta}^{*}\to\operatorname{Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}(\theta)/{\mathbb{Q}}(\theta^{G}))

with kernel
G_{\theta}
, so

G_{\theta}^{*}/G_{\theta}\cong\operatorname{Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}(\theta)/{\mathbb{Q}}(\theta^{G})).

Proof.

See
[
19
, Lemmas 2.2 and 2.3]
. Note that if
g\mapsto\tau
, then

(\theta^{G})^{\tau}=(\theta^{\tau})^{G}=(\theta^{g})^{G}=\theta^{G}
, so
\tau
fixes

\theta^{G}
, and it follows that the map is well-defined.
∎

We fix a prime
p
and set
{\mathcal{G}}:=\operatorname{Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/{\mathbb{Q}})=\{\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{p-1}\}

where
\tau_{j}
takes a
fixed primitive
p
th root of unity to its
j
th power. If
P
is an elementary
abelian
p
-group, then
{\mathcal{G}}
acts on
P
by automorphisms as
\tau_{j}(y)=y^{j}
for
every
y\in P
and
\tau_{j}\in{\mathcal{G}}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
P
be an elementary abelian
p
-group. Suppose that
X
acts coprimely
by automorphisms on
P
. Let
{\mathcal{G}}=\operatorname{Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/{\mathbb{Q}})
and write
A=X\times{\mathcal{G}}
.
Then
A
acts by automorphisms and coprimely on
P
(where the action of
{\mathcal{G}}

is as in the previous paragraph) and the actions of
A
on
P
and
\operatorname{Irr}(P)

are permutation isomorphic. Set
G=P\rtimes X
. If
y\mapsto\lambda_{y}
is an

A
-equivariant bijection
P\to\operatorname{Irr}(P)
, then

{\bf N}_{G}(\langle y\rangle)/{\bf C}_{G}(y)=G_{\lambda_{y}}^{*}/G_{\lambda_{y}}\qquad\text{for all $y\in P$}.

Proof.

The group
A
acts by automorphisms on
P
because the actions of
X
and
{\mathcal{G}}

commute. The actions of
A
on
P
and
\operatorname{Irr}(P)
are permutation isomorphic by

[
11
, Thm 18.10]
.
Let
\alpha:P\to\operatorname{Irr}(P)
be an
A
-equivariant bijection. Write
\alpha(y)=\lambda
.
Notice that
G_{\lambda}=P\rtimes X_{\lambda}
. Now
x\in X_{\lambda}
if, and only if,

\alpha(y)^{x}=\alpha(y)
if, and only if,
x\in{\bf C}_{X}(y)
. Also,

G_{\lambda}^{*}=P\rtimes X_{\lambda}^{*}
and
x\in X_{\lambda}^{*}
if, and only if, there is some

\tau\in{\mathcal{G}}
for which
\alpha(y)^{x}=\alpha(y)^{\tau}
if, and only if,

y^{x}=y^{\tau}=y^{j}
for some
j\in\{1,\ldots,p-1\}
if, and only if,

x\in{\bf N}_{X}(\langle y\rangle)
.
∎

We denote by
\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime},\sigma_{1}}(G)
the subset of characters in
\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)

fixed by the Galois automorphism
\sigma_{1}
, that is, the set of almost

p
-rational characters of degree coprime to
p
. We mention that there is no
standard notation yet for this set in the literature (see
[
22
,
10
]
), we use the notation from
[
18
]
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
G
be a finite group,
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(G)
and assume
P\unlhd G
. Then

\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime},\sigma_{1}}(G)=\operatorname{Irr}(G/\Phi(P))
.

Proof.

Let
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime},\sigma_{1}}(G)
. Then by Clifford’s theorem

\chi_{P}=e(\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{t})
for some
e\geq 1
, where
\{\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{t}\}

is the set of
G
-conjugates of some linear
\lambda\in\operatorname{Irr}(P/P^{\prime})
. Since

\chi^{\sigma_{1}}=\chi
the
p
-group
\langle\sigma_{1}\rangle
acts on

\{\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{t}\}
, but
t=|G:G_{\lambda}|
divides
|G:P|
and thus is prime
to
p
. This shows
\sigma_{1}
fixes some

\lambda_{i}
and therefore it fixes all of them. Thus
\lambda^{\sigma_{1}}=\lambda^{p+1}=\lambda
so

\lambda
has order
p
as an element of
\operatorname{Irr}(P/P^{\prime})
. We conclude that

\Phi(P)\subseteq\ker\lambda
so
\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime},\sigma_{1}}(G)\subseteq\operatorname{Irr}(G/\Phi(P))
. For the
reverse inclusion, notice that
\lambda\in\operatorname{Irr}(P/\Phi(P))
is
\sigma_{1}
-fixed, and so
is every
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G|\lambda)
by
[
20
, Lemma 5.1]
.
∎

We recall the statement of Conjecture B below for the reader’s convenience.

Conjecture 5.4
.

Let
G
be a finite group,
p
be a prime and
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(G)
. Write

K={\bf N}_{G}(P)/\Phi(P)
and
Q=P/\Phi(P)
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

Every almost
p
-rational
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)
is
p
-rational.

(2)

The following hold for all
1\neq y\in Q
:

(a)

{\bf N}_{K}(\langle y\rangle)/{\bf C}_{K}(y)\cong\operatorname{Aut}(\langle y\rangle)
,
and

(b)

{\bf N}_{K}(\langle y\rangle)/Q
acts trivially on the set of conjugacy
classes of
{\bf C}_{K}(y)/Q
.

Recall that if
p=2
, the conditions in Conjecture
5.4
are
vacuous. If
p
is odd, then a character
\chi
is almost
p
-rational if, and
only if,
\chi
is
\sigma_{1}
-fixed.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
G
be a finite group,
p
be a prime and
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(G)
. Suppose that

P\unlhd G
. Then Conjecture
5.4
holds for
G
and
p
.

Proof.

Conjecture
5.4
trivially holds when
p=2
, so we may
assume that
p
is odd.
We begin the proof with a few observations. Let
Q=P/\Phi(P)
and write

{\mathcal{G}}=\operatorname{Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/{\mathbb{Q}})
. If
m=|G|_{p^{\prime}}
then restriction yields an isomorphism

\operatorname{Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}_{mp}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{m})\rightarrow{\mathcal{G}}
and by abuse of notation, we identify the
elements
\tau\in\operatorname{Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}_{mp}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{m})
with their restriction
\tau\in{\mathcal{G}}
.
Consider the action by automorphisms of
{\mathcal{G}}
on
Q
described before
Lemma
5.2
. Write
K=Q\rtimes X
for some
complement
X
of
Q
in
G/\Phi(P)
. By Lemma
5.3
we have

\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime},\sigma_{1}}(G)=\operatorname{Irr}(K)
. Write also
A=X\times{\mathcal{G}}
. Notice that every

\lambda\in\operatorname{Irr}(Q)
extends to a canonical
\hat{\lambda}\in\operatorname{Irr}(K_{\lambda})
by

[

, Cor. 6.2 and 6.4]

{\mathbb{Q}}(\hat{\lambda})={\mathbb{Q}}(\lambda)={\mathbb{Q}}_{p}

If
H=K^{*}_{\lambda}
then it follows that
H_{\hat{\lambda}}=H_{\lambda}=K_{\lambda}
and that

H^{*}_{\hat{\lambda}}=H
. By combining Clifford’s and Gallagher’s theorem we have

\operatorname{Irr}(K|\lambda)=\{(\rho\hat{\lambda})^{K}\mid\rho\in\operatorname{Irr}(K_{\lambda}/Q)\}.

Furthermore if
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(K|\lambda)

\psi\in\operatorname{Irr}(K_{\lambda}|\lambda)

\psi^{*}=\psi^{K^{*}_{\lambda}}\in\operatorname{Irr}(K^{*}_{\lambda}|\lambda)

{\mathbb{Q}}(\psi^{*})={\mathbb{Q}}(\chi)
,
where
\chi=(\psi^{*})^{G}
, by Lemma
5.1
.

We begin by proving that (i) implies (ii.a), so we are assuming that every
element of
\operatorname{Irr}(K)
is
p
-rational. Suppose that for some
1\neq y\in Q
we
have that
{\bf N}_{K}(\langle y\rangle)/{\bf C}_{K}(y)
is not isomorphic to
{\mathcal{G}}
;
in other words, that

|{\bf N}_{K}(\langle y\rangle)/{\bf C}_{K}(y)|<p-1.

Let
1_{Q}\neq\lambda\in\operatorname{Irr}(Q)
correspond to

under an
A
-equivariant
bijection as in Lemma
5.2
, so that

K_{\lambda}^{*}/K_{\lambda}={\bf N}_{K}(\langle y\rangle)/{\bf C}_{K}(y)
. Let
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(K)

be over
\lambda
. By hypothesis
\chi
is
p
-rational. Let

\psi\in\operatorname{Irr}(K_{\lambda}|\lambda)
be the Clifford correspondent of
\chi
.
As explained in the first paragraph, then
\psi^{*}=\psi^{K_{\lambda}^{*}}
is also

-rational. In particular we have

\psi^{*}(g)=\psi(1)\sum_{k\in[K_{\lambda}^{*}/K_{\lambda}]}\lambda^{k}(g)\in{\mathbb{Q}}\qquad\text{for every $g\in Q$},

where
[K_{\lambda}^{*}/K_{\lambda}]
is a complete set of representatives of the

K_{\lambda}
-cosets in
K_{\lambda}^{*}
. Let
{\mathcal{K}}\leq{\mathcal{G}}
be the image of
K_{\lambda}^{*}/K_{\lambda}

under the homomorphism from Lemma

, and notice that

{\mathcal{K}}<{\mathcal{G}}
. Then
\displaystyle\sum_{\tau\in{\mathcal{K}}}\lambda^{\tau}

for
\sigma\in{\mathcal{G}}\setminus{\mathcal{K}}
, the equality

\displaystyle\sum_{\tau\in{\mathcal{K}}}\lambda^{\tau}=\sum_{\tau\in{\mathcal{K}}}\lambda^{\tau\sigma}

. This proves that (i)
implies (ii.a).

Now we prove (i) implies (ii.b). Let
1_{Q}\neq\lambda\in\operatorname{Irr}(Q)
. Notice that the
previous proof shows
K^{*}_{\lambda}/K_{\lambda}\cong{\mathcal{G}}
. Now
\operatorname{Irr}(K|\lambda)
are all

-rational by hypothesis. Let
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(K|\lambda)
, let

\psi\in\operatorname{Irr}(K_{\lambda}|\lambda)
be its Clifford correspondent and let

\psi^{*}=\psi^{K^{*}_{\lambda}}
be its correspondent in
K^{*}_{\lambda}
and write

\psi=\rho\hat{\lambda}
as in the first paragraph of this proof. Now,
\psi
has
values contained in
{\mathbb{Q}}_{pm}
where
m=|G|_{p^{\prime}}
. Since
\chi
is
p
-rational,
if
\tau\in\operatorname{Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}_{pm}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{m})\cong{\mathcal{G}}
we have

so

(\psi^{*})^{\tau}=\psi^{*}
. Further, by the first comment of this paragraph, there
is

with
\lambda^{\tau}=\lambda^{x}
. This implies that both

\psi^{\tau}
and
\psi^{x}
are the Clifford correspondents of
\psi^{*}
in
K_{\lambda}

over
\lambda^{\tau}
, and therefore
\psi^{\tau}=\psi^{x}
. Using that
\rho^{\tau}=\rho
,

\rho\hat{\lambda}^{\tau}=\rho^{\tau}\hat{\lambda}^{\tau}=\psi^{\tau}=\psi^{x}=\rho^{x}\hat{\lambda}^{x}=\rho^{x}\hat{\lambda}^{\tau}

which implies
\rho=\rho^{x}
. Since this holds for any
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(K_{\lambda}|\lambda)

and any
\tau\in{\mathcal{G}}
, we conclude that
\rho^{x}=\rho
for
any
\rho\in\operatorname{Irr}(K_{\lambda}/Q)
and any

. By Brauer’s Lemma on

[

, Thm 2.3]
) we have that
K_{\lambda}^{*}/Q
acts
trivially on the set of conjugacy classes of
K_{\lambda}/Q
, and this concludes the
proof of (ii.b) assuming (i).

We finally prove that (ii) implies (i). By Lemma
5.3
we need to
show that every character of
K
is
p
-rational. Let
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(K)
and let

\lambda\in\operatorname{Irr}(Q)
be under
\chi
. If
\lambda
is trivial, then
\chi

can be seen as a character of the

-group
K/Q
and
hence
\chi
is
p
-rational, as wanted. We can therefore assume that
\lambda
is
nontrivial. By Lemma
5.2
there is some

such that
K_{\lambda}^{*}/K_{\lambda}={\bf N}_{K}(\langle y\rangle)/{\bf C}_{K}(y)
.
By hypothesis and Lemma
5.1
(b), we have

K_{\lambda}^{*}/K_{\lambda}\cong{\mathcal{G}}
. Let
\psi\in\operatorname{Irr}(K_{\lambda}|\lambda)
be the Clifford
correspondent of
\chi
and write
\psi^{*}=\psi^{K_{\lambda}^{*}}
. We have that

{\mathbb{Q}}(\chi)={\mathbb{Q}}(\psi^{*})
by Lemma
5.1
(a), so we want to show
that
\psi^{*}
is
p
-rational.

By definition, for all
x\in K_{\lambda}^{*}
there is
\tau\in{\mathcal{G}}
with

\lambda^{\tau}=\lambda^{x}
. Since
|K_{\lambda}^{*}/K_{\lambda}|=|{\mathcal{G}}|
, the converse is also true.
So, fixing some
\tau\in{\mathcal{G}}
we have
\lambda^{\tau}=\lambda^{x}
for
x\in K^{*}_{\lambda}
.
Now, using the notation established at the beginning of this proof, we have

\hat{\lambda}^{x}=\hat{\lambda}^{\tau}
, where
\hat{\lambda}
is the canonical extension of
\lambda

to
K_{\lambda}
. Thus

\psi^{\tau}=\rho^{\tau}\hat{\lambda}^{\tau}=\rho\hat{\lambda}^{x}=\rho^{x}\hat{\lambda}^{x}=\psi^{x}

where we have used in the second to last equality that
\rho^{x}=\rho
by (ii.b)
and Brauer’s permutation lemma. Thus
\psi^{\tau}=\psi^{x}
and therefore

(\psi^{*})^{\tau}=(\psi^{\tau})^{K_{\lambda}^{*}}=(\psi^{x})^{K_{\lambda}^{*}}=\psi^{*}

and we conclude that
\psi^{*}
is
\tau
-invariant for any
\tau\in{\mathcal{G}}
. Thus

\psi^{*}
is
p
-rational and we are done.
∎

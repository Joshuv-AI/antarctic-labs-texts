Time-dependent Hole States in Multiconfigurational Time-Dependent Hartree-Fock Approaches: A Time-Domain Generalization of Extended Koopmans’ Theorem Preprint: APS/123-QED
Zhao-Han Zhang 1, Yang Li 1, Himadri Pathak 3,4, Takeshi Sato 5,6,7, Kenichi L. Ishikawa 5,6,7,8, Feng He 1,2

Abstract
We introduce a time-domain generalization of the extended Koopmans’ theorem within the framework of the multiconfigurational time-dependent Hartree-Fock (MCTDHF) theory. This formulation naturally yields well-defined time-dependent hole states formed by removing one electron from the multielectron system, enabling the instantaneous construction of reduced density matrices for the photofragments during MCTDHF simulations with negligible computational overhead. Leveraging this foundation, we derive the equation of motion for the time-dependent Dyson orbitals and develop a systematic approach to extract hole-resolved observables directly from the time-dependent ab initio wavefunctions, such as channel-resolved photoelectron momentum distributions. The proposed method is universally applicable to both projection-based and flux-based schemes, offering a powerful tool for disentangling correlated electron-hole dynamics in ultrafast multichannel ionization processes.

In the static Hartree-Fock theory, Koopmans derived a famous theorem
Koopmans 1934
predicting the essential energy required to remove an electron from a multi-electron system in its ground state, which is later extended to multiconfigurational scenarios
Day et al. 1974
;
Smith and Day 1975
;
Morrell et al. 1975
;
Vanfleteren et al. 2009
, known as the extended Koopmans’ theorem (EKT).
Surprisingly, despite the foundational role of EKT in physics and quantum chemistry, a rigorous consideration to generalize EKT into the time-dependent case has not been realized yet, even though the latter has prospered for decades.
The motivation for this generalization arises from challenges encountered in describing typical photofragmentation processes such as photoionization and photodissociation.
In these scenarios, the resulting fragments are compound systems with complicated internal structures that are difficult to detect directly. Most internal states soon decay away before the fragments reach the detector, whereas only the kinetic properties, e.g., asymptotic momentum of the photoelectron, photoion, etc., remain measurable. Taking photoionization as a prototype, the rotational, vibrational, and electronic states of the photoion are often invisible in experimental measurements. Instead, they are inferred from the information provided by the accompanying photoelectrons. In theoretical studies, the photoelectron momentum distribution (PMD) in the asymptotic region (
r\gg r_{I}
) is calculated to mimic real experiments. For theoretical approaches
Burke and Burke 1997
;
Van der Hart et al. 2007
;
Moore et al. 2011
where the ion states are specified
a priori
, extracting asymptotic ion states from the final wavefunction is rather straightforward. In striking contrast, MCTDHF encodes all fragment states within a single correlated
ab initio
full
N
-electron wavefunction
(\Psi)
, described by a single set of time-dependent orbitals
(\phi_{i})
and configuration expansion coefficients
(C_{I})

|\Psi(t)\rangle=\sum_{I}C_{I}(t)|\phi_{I_{1}}(t)\phi_{I_{2}}(t)\cdots\phi_{I_{N}}(t)\rangle.

(1)

As a result, although the full PMD can be determined from the expectation value of the momentum density operator
(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}\sigma}\hat{a}_{\bm{k}\sigma})
in the asymptotic region after a sufficiently long time (
t>t_{f}
)

\mathcal{P}(\bm{k}\sigma)=\langle\Psi(t_{f})|\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}\sigma}\hat{a}_{\bm{k}\sigma}|\Psi(t_{f})\rangle,

(2)

it is conceptually difficult to extract the ion state with which a photoelectron is entangled, since a single-vacancy state of Eq. (
1

Even worse, the ion states could also depart from the stationary ones as they may undergo dynamical evolutions. Such difficulties eventually arise from the lack of well-defined time-dependent hole states (TDHSs), which pose a severe challenge to the broader application of the MCTDHF method whenever the quantum entanglement and coherence may emerge as a consequence of the participation of various ion states

;
Laurell et al. 2025

In this Letter, we rigorously establish a time-domain generalization of EKT and define a consistent formulation of the TDHS within the MCTDHF framework. We first outline three essential
conditions
a physically
meaningful
definition of TDHS must satisfy. Constrained by these
conditions
, we propose an
ansatz
of the TDHS and derive its corresponding equation of motion (EOM). We demonstrate that the stationary limit of our derived EOM naturally recovers the conventional EKT, thereby validating its time-domain generalization. Building on these TDHS solutions, we develop both a channel-resolved time-dependent surface flux (t-SURFF) method and a channel-resolved projection method, enabling clear resolution of the electron-ion entanglement in complex ultrafast phenomena.

As all presented quantities depend on time, we omit the time variable unless a particular moment is referred to. We introduce the notation
\bm{\kappa}\equiv(\bm{k}\sigma)
as a collective coordinate for momentum
\bm{k}
and spin
\sigma
. The corresponding single-electron state
|\bm{\kappa}\rangle\equiv\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\bm{\kappa}}|vac\rangle
describes an electron in the asymptotic region with kinetic momentum
\bm{k}
and spin polarization
\sigma
. Let
|\gamma\rangle
denote a general state of the
(N-1)
-electron subsystem that we are looking for, and
|\gamma,\bm{\kappa}\rangle\equiv\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\bm{\kappa}}|\gamma\rangle
denote the state where an electron in the asymptotic region is added to the subsystem. Using the Laplace expansion of the Slater determinant, the channel-resolved projection
\mathcal{Q}_{\gamma}(\bm{\kappa})\equiv\langle\gamma,\bm{\kappa}|\Psi\rangle
can be explicitly expressed as a linear combination of
\phi_{\mu}(\bm{\kappa})\equiv\langle\bm{\kappa}|\phi_{\mu}\rangle
, yielding (Einstein’s summation convention is applied for Greek subscripts)

\mathcal{Q}_{\gamma}(\bm{\kappa})=D_{\gamma\mu}\phi_{\mu}(\bm{\kappa}),

(3)

where
D_{\gamma\mu}\equiv\langle\gamma|\hat{a}_{\mu}|\Psi\rangle
is independent of
\bm{\kappa}
and nonzero only when
\phi_{\mu}
is occupied. Note that
\mathcal{Q}_{\gamma}(\bm{\kappa})
are indeed the time-dependent Dyson orbitals in momentum representation, which is a conceptual generalization of static Dyson orbitals
Smith and Day 1975
.

Given these definitions, we introduce the first
condition
: in the limit
t\rightarrow\infty
, the interaction between the photoelectron and the residual
(N-1)
-electron system vanishes. Importantly, this condition applies not only to single ionization but also remains valid in the case of double or multiple ionization, where the residual system may itself be in an excited state or further ionized. Eventually, the channel-resolved PMD,
\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}(\bm{\kappa})\equiv|\mathcal{Q}_{\gamma}(\bm{\kappa})|^{2}
, should be stable, indicating formally

\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\dot{\mathcal{P}}_{\gamma}(\bm{\kappa})=0,

(4)

which we call the
unitarity condition
. An improper choice of
|\gamma\rangle
, such as mixing two valid choices
|\gamma\rangle=|\gamma_{1}\rangle+|\gamma_{2}\rangle
, could cause indefinite oscillations of
\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}
.

The second
condition
stems from the requirement that the sum of
\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}(\bm{\kappa})
over all possible
|\gamma\rangle
must be equal to the total PMD
\mathcal{P}(\bm{\kappa})=\phi^{*}_{\mu}(\bm{\kappa})\rho_{\mu\nu}\phi_{\nu}(\bm{\kappa})
, where
\rho
is the one-body reduced density matrix (1RDM) with the elements given by
\rho_{\mu\nu}=\langle\Psi|\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\mu}\hat{a}_{\nu}|\Psi\rangle
. Therefore, valid choices of
|\gamma\rangle
are restricted by
\rho
. They should satisfy the following equality, which has two equivalent forms

\displaystyle\rho_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=D^{*}_{\gamma\mu}D_{\gamma\nu},

(5a)

\displaystyle\langle\Psi|\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\mu}\hat{a}_{\nu}|\Psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\Psi|\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\mu}|\gamma\rangle\langle\gamma|\hat{a}_{\nu}|\Psi\rangle.

(5b)

We call them the
sum condition
. Eq. (
5a
) requires that
\rho=D^{\dagger}D
, i.e.,
D
must be one of the generalized matrix square root decompositions (GSRDs) of
\rho
. However, not all GSRDs of
\rho
are
meaningful
, as we will illustrate with an example later. Eq. (
5b
) implies that any single-vacancy state
\hat{a}_{\mu}|\Psi\rangle
should completely fall into the
(N-1)
-electron Hilbert space spanned by
|\gamma\rangle
, which we shall denote by
\varPi
.

The third
condition
is about the physical reality of
\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}
. In MCTDHF, a redundancy arises from the fact that two different sets of
\{\phi_{i},C_{I}\}
, differing only by a unitary transformation, represent the same physical state, i.e., the same total wave function. Typically, constraints on
R_{\mu\nu}\equiv i\langle\phi_{\mu}|\dot{\phi}_{\nu}\rangle
eliminate this redundancy
Meyer et al. 1990
;
Caillat et al. 2005
. A
meaningful

\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}
must be independent of the choice of
R
, which we call the
physical condition
.

Guided by the outlined
conditions
, we try to expand the TDHS
|\gamma\rangle
with (atomic units are used throughout)

|\gamma\rangle=\hat{a}_{\mu}|\Psi\rangle Z_{\mu\gamma}.

(6)

While the states
\hat{a}_{\mu}|\Psi\rangle
are directly available from MCTDHF solutions, the coefficient matrix
Z
remains to be determined.
Eq. (
6
) implies that
|\gamma\rangle
satisfies the projected TDSE

i\hat{\varPi}|\dot{\gamma}\rangle=\hat{\varPi}\hat{H}|\gamma\rangle,

(7)

where
\hat{\varPi}
is the projector onto the subspace
\varPi
, and
\hat{H}
is the time-dependent Hamiltonian incorporating electron-electron, electron-nuclei, and electron-laser interactions. Eq. (
7
) ensures the
physical condition
, as
\hat{\varPi}
is invariant under a unitary transformation using a different choice of
\hat{R}
. In the limit of the full configuration interaction (FCI),
\hat{\varPi}
approaches the identity operator, thus
|\gamma\rangle
converges to the
exact
solution of the
(N-1)
-electron TDSE. Combining Eqs. (
6
) and (
7
), we derive an EOM for
Z

\displaystyle-\tilde{F}^{T}Z

\displaystyle=i\rho\dot{Z},

(8a)

\displaystyle\tilde{F}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=(F_{\mu\tau}-R_{\mu\tau})\rho_{\nu\tau},

(8b)

with
F_{\mu\nu}\equiv\langle\phi_{\mu}|\hat{F}|\phi_{\nu}\rangle
, where
\hat{F}
is the generalized Fock operator in the EOM of MCTDHF
Sato and Ishikawa 2013
. Generally,
\hat{\varPi}|\dot{\gamma}\rangle\neq|\dot{\gamma}\rangle
, thus,
d\hat{\varPi}/dt\neq 0
. Consequently, the subspace spanned by hole states may also evolve in time.

In addition, the present definition embraces several good properties, making it a successful time-domain generalization of EKT.
First, once a stationary solution to the EOM of MCTDHF in a field-free case is reached,
\tilde{F}
becomes real and symmetric. The stationary condition of Eq. (
8a
),
i\dot{Z}=Z\mathcal{E}
, thus reduces to a generalized eigenvalue problem

-\tilde{F}Z=\rho Z\mathcal{E},

(9)

which reproduces the static EKT. The TDHS thus coincides with the field-free hole states at
t=0
, and the overlap between
\gamma(0)
and
\Psi(0)
yields the static Dyson orbitals. The orthonormal relation
\langle\gamma|\gamma^{\prime}\rangle=(Z^{\dagger})_{\gamma\mu}\rho_{\mu\nu}Z_{\nu\gamma^{\prime}}=\delta_{\gamma\gamma^{\prime}}
is satisfied at any time, as seen from the fact that
Z^{\dagger}\rho Z=I
is initially satisfied and will always be preserved.
Second, the
sum condition
of Eq. (
5a
) is fulfilled at any time. This is seen from

D_{\gamma\mu}=\langle\gamma|\hat{a}_{\mu}|\Psi\rangle=(Z^{\dagger})_{\gamma\nu}\rho_{\nu\mu}=(Z^{-1})_{\gamma\mu},

(10)

such that
D^{\dagger}D=\rho^{\dagger}ZZ^{-1}=\rho
.
This property could be surprising since
\rho
should include both single and multiple ionization, whereas the TDHS composed of single-vacancy states seems to describe only the single ionization. In fact, a time-dependent ion state can continue to be ionized into a higher charge state. As a result,
|\gamma\rangle
includes not only a singly charged ion state, but also all fragment states created from this ion.
Third, and probably the most important, the
unitarity condition
is satisfied. To understand it, we introduce a practical realization of
|\bm{\kappa}\rangle=\hat{\Theta}|\chi_{\bm{\kappa}}\rangle
, where
\hat{\Theta}
is a mask operator to remove all wavepackets inside a given radius
r_{0}
, and
\chi_{\bm{\kappa}}
is a Volkov state satisfying
\hat{H}_{V}|\chi_{\bm{\kappa}}\rangle=i|\dot{\chi}_{\bm{\kappa}}\rangle
with
\hat{H}_{V}
the Volkov Hamiltonian, which ends up with the plane wave of momentum
\bm{k}
in the spin state
\sigma
after the external field is terminated. The EOMs of
D_{\gamma\mu}
and
\phi_{\mu}(\bm{\kappa})
immediately follow

\displaystyle i\dot{\phi}_{\mu}(\bm{\kappa})

\displaystyle=J_{\mu}(\bm{\kappa})+\phi_{\nu}(\bm{\kappa})(R_{\nu\mu}-F_{\nu\mu}),

(11a)

\displaystyle i\dot{D}_{\gamma\mu}

\displaystyle=(F_{\mu\nu}-R_{\mu\nu})D_{\gamma\nu},

(11b)

where
J_{\mu}(\bm{\kappa})
is the time-dependent surface flux

\displaystyle J_{\mu}(\bm{\kappa})

\displaystyle=\langle\chi_{\bm{\kappa}}|\hat{\Theta}\hat{F}-\hat{H}_{V}\hat{\Theta}|\phi_{\mu}\rangle

(12)

\displaystyle\approx\langle\chi_{\bm{\kappa}}|[\hat{\Theta},\hat{H}_{V}]|\phi_{\mu}\rangle.

Using Eqs. (
11a
) and (
11b
), the EOM of
\mathcal{Q}_{\gamma}(\bm{\kappa})
, i.e., the EOM of the time-dependent Dyson orbital, is simply given by

i\dot{\mathcal{Q}}_{\gamma}(\bm{\kappa})=\mathcal{J}_{\gamma}(\bm{\kappa}),

(13)

where the channel flux is
\mathcal{J}_{\gamma}(\bm{\kappa})=D_{\gamma\mu}J_{\mu}(\bm{\kappa})
.
The surface flux will eventually vanish as
t\rightarrow\infty
, since the outgoing wavepacket passing through the surface will decay and eventually vanish. Therefore,
\dot{\mathcal{Q}}_{\gamma}
also goes to zero as
t\rightarrow\infty
, such that
\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}
satisfies Eq. (
4
).

Apart from theoretical merits, the proposed time-domain generalization of EKT significantly enhances the practical utility in numerical simulations. As the EOM of
D_{\gamma\mu}
is known and can be numerically solved with minor efforts, Eq. (
13
) naturally serves as a channel-resolved t-SURFF approach in MCTDHF. The only additional requirement is the evaluation of the surface flux
J_{\mu}(\bm{\kappa})
, which also occurs in the standard single-electron t-SURFF approach
Zhang et al. 2023
and conventional MCTDHF t-SURFF implementations
Orimo et al. 2019
. Furthermore, Eq. (
3
) straightforwardly leads to a channel-resolved projection approach, considering that
\phi_{i}(\bm{\kappa})
is handy by directly projecting orbitals onto continuum states such as masked plane waves or Coulomb waves.

To illustrate the effectiveness of the formulated TDHS and highlight its advantage over other choices of
\gamma
and
D_{\gamma\mu}
, we perform numerical simulations, employing the multi-channel photoionization of hydrogen anion
\mathrm{H^{-}}
and hydrogen molecule
\mathrm{H_{2}}

Being one of the simplest two-electron systems, the hydrogen anion
\mathrm{H^{-}}
has a strong electron correlation in its ground state, giving rise to a considerable probability of the shake-up process

a.u., single-photon ionization may leave the H atom in the ground state, excited states, and even continuum. Here, we concentrate on the 1

, is actually a one-particle-one-hole state. We compare the channel-resolved PMD at

(parallel to the laser polarization) as a function of photoelectron energy (

Beyond the perturbative regime, the hole states may undergo dynamics in the presence of external laser fields, i.e.,
|\gamma(t_{2})\rangle\neq|\gamma(t_{1})\rangle
for
t_{2}\neq t_{1}
. To demonstrate the robustness and generality of our theory, we simulate conditions under which pronounced two-level Rabi oscillations arise in the hole states, leading to significant distinctions between observables defined by TDHS and by static HS. To this end, a body-fixed
\mathrm{H_{2}}
with an internuclear distance of 4 a.u. is irradiated by a 10-cycle sine-square pulse polarized along the molecule axis, where the maximal value of the vector potential is
0.16
a.u., and the central photon energy (
\omega=0.1005
a.u.) is resonant with the
1s\sigma_{g}
-
2p\sigma_{u}
transition of the hydrogen molecular ion (
\mathrm{H_{2}^{+}}
). The
\mathrm{H_{2}}
undergoes a multiphoton ionization process, producing a
\mathrm{H_{2}^{+}}
exhibiting clear Rabi oscillations. We analyze the dynamics through the self-correlation functions of TDHS, defined as

\mathcal{C}_{\gamma^{\prime}\gamma}(t)=\langle\gamma^{\prime}(0)|\gamma(t)\rangle.

In conclusion, we have established a time-domain generalization of the extended Koopmans’ theorem, which is consistent with the MCTDHF theory. It addresses the long-standing challenge of disentangling correlated electron-hole dynamics within the framework of MCTDHF. With a clear definition of the time-dependent hole states, an instantaneous construction of the photofragment reduced density matrix is brought to reality, and the compatibility of the formalism with projection-based and flux-based techniques (e.g., t-SURFF) is validated across diverse ionization scenarios, opening new avenues for studying correlated multichannel phenomena in ultrafast quantum dynamics.

What competencies does an orthodontic postgraduate need?
Authors: Hamid Mahdavifard, Saeed Noorollahian, Athar Omid, Nikoo Yamani
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCompetency frameworks are used to guide professional education and assessment. An orthodontic graduate needs various competencies. This study aimed to determine competencies and create a basic competency framework for orthodontic graduates.MethodsThis descriptive cross-sectional study used the Delphi method. A comprehensive literature review was conducted in the ISI, Scopus, PubMed, Eric, and Google Scholar; the orthodontic curriculum in Iran; and the available orthodontic curricula. The sub-competencies and learning objectives necessary for an orthodontic graduate were extracted. The list of competencies was designed as an electronic questionnaire as the first round of Delphi. Fifty-two orthodontic specialists (university staff) were asked to determine the degree of necessity of competencies for an orthodontic graduate using a five-point Likert scale. In the second round, nine competencies were transferred from the first round of Delphi, and four competencies were extracted from the experts' opinions and were included in the questionnaire.ResultsA total of 85 articles and 4 curricula were reviewed and 77 competencies were identified in 11 domains. In the first round of the Delphi, 68 competencies were accepted, for a quota of 70%, and nine were transferred to the second round of the Delphi. At this round, most of the competencies in the field of “data collection and recording”, “treatment procedures”, “patient-centered treatment”, “dental knowledge, “clinical reasoning and moral judgment”, “communication skills”, “Health Promotion”, “Attitude, legal responsibilities and ethical issues (professionalism)”, “information management”, and “Self-development” through participation were accepted. Finally, the domain of competence “the dentist's role in the health system” was removed, and ten domains (73 competencies) were accepted.ConclusionsThe competencies identified in this study can be used in designing competency-based curricula, education, and assessment in post-graduate orthodontic programs.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12909-024-06475-y.Keywords: Orthodontics, Delphi Method, Competency

Background

The traditional approach to curriculum development was prescriptive, and the goal has often been to preserve knowledge [1]. The curriculum has been structured and emphasized on time-based courses. In this approach, specific times are dedicated to certain activities in the training courses. Currently, more educational institutions still have the effects of this approach [2]. The reasons for changes in medical curricula include social demands and regulations, changes in clinical practice, work norms, the view of scientific accumulation, and technological advances [3]. In recent decades, healthcare workforce training programs have focused on developing professional competencies through competency-based medical education (CBME). This approach focuses on learning outcomes; not on time, and learner-centered education is promoted, which acts as a bridge between educational goals and outcomes [4, 5]. Competency-based education (CBE) is an emerging model in the health professions, focusing on time-variable competency development and achievement compared with a time-bound, course-based, traditional model. CBE is an innovation in medical education in which outcomes are considered [6]. Competency-based medical education (CBME) is focused on the final results of the performance of the healthcare team. This approach seeks to train professionals whose skills meet the needs of the communities they serve and whose clinical skills can meet the needs of healthcare systems [7].

The term "competency" is closely related to the Latin word "competentia" which means "allowed to judge" or "has the right to speak" [8]. Competency is a legal term that refers to people who “have sufficient ability and possess the necessary natural or legal competencies to participate in a certain endeavor.” This definition is a broad concept that encompasses many recognized legal activities [9]. competency in health professions is the ability of a healthcare service provider which can be observed and encompasses various components such as knowledge, skills, values, and attitudes [10]. CBME, as a conceptual framework, guides the design, implementation, assessment, and evaluation of educational programs through a well-defined framework of competencies [11]. Competencies combine the appropriate knowledge and professional attitudes with reliable skills performed in real environments without assistance [12].

Dentistry is a field of human health care in which students are trained to independently care patients with oral and dental diseases and disorders. In dentistry, the need to evaluate the level of competence is an important step towards validating the quality of dental graduates [13]. Currently, new dental graduates are often required to perform invasive or non-invasive and often irreversible surgical procedures using advanced materials and equipment in a shorter time to meet the society needs. Dentistry's clinical competencies entail mechanical hand procedures that depend on the development of psychomotor skills during clinical training practices [14].

One of the first impetuses for CBME in dental schools was the publication of a report by the Institute of Medicine in 1995 on dental education at crossroads, challenges, and changes. This report recommends significant reforms to the curriculum related to the educational aspects of dentistry [15]. In 1984, the American Dental Association (ADA) Commission on Dental Accreditation recognized the importance of considering outcomes and recommended that standard statements focus on outcomes [16, 17]. Educational institutions should train dentists who can successfully cope with clinical situations, in the profession, in their daily work to improve and ensure high-quality dental healthcare [18]. To effectively complete dental training, students must complete a series of procedures, fine motor skills in the para-clinic tract, and patient care programs [18–20]. The American Dental Education Association (ADEA), Association for Dental Education in Europe (ADEE), and Dental Council of New Zealand (DCNZ) proposed a specific set of core competencies that graduates need to perform unsupervised procedures [21–23]. Orthodontics is one of the dental specialties that focuses on the alignment of teeth and jaws [24]. An orthodontist must have three key skills to perform the work, as follows: collecting clinical information, planning treatment, and setting treatment procedures. These tasks represent the practical aspect of patient care, but an analysis of the professional performance of an orthodontist shows that he needs other skills much more than practical skills. These skills include the application of basic clinical knowledge, clinical reasoning and judgment, communication skills, health promotion, attitudes, ethical and legal responsibility, and information management. Finally, the orthodontist's role as a professional includes his or her role in health care and personal development, including a commitment to lifelong learning [25]. In today's society, with increased self-image concerns and the pervasive use of social media, many patients are likely to seek orthodontic treatment [24]. Orthodontic graduates should have the necessary competencies to perform treatment procedures so that they can provide the best services to society and in addition to improving people's self-image. Improving people's self-image improves the mental health of society. New graduates are competent when they can function (independently) in realistic practice environments [26]. Competency-based medical education has gained considerable attention worldwide as a transformative approach to training healthcare professionals. This model has been implemented in various dental schools and training programs across different countries. Competency-based education in orthodontics is an educational innovation whose goal is to train competent graduates in the field of orthodontics to perform treatment procedures in the best way. In competency-based education in orthodontics, the starting point for planning is the basic competencies that the resident must acquire by the end of the course. In this program, the development of learning outcomes begins at the beginning of the education process. The teachers of the orthodontic department at Isfahan University of Medical Sciences wanted to improve the quality of the educational program and assessment of the postgraduate program. This study was conducted to determine the competencies expected for a graduate in orthodontic residency to manage patients and perform related treatment procedures.

Methods

This descriptive and cross-sectional study was conducted in two parts (September 2022 to May 2023). At first, a literature review was done, and then the modified Delphi method in two rounds was conducted. The Delphi technique, a multi-stage survey, ultimately tries to reach a consensus on an important issue. The main assumption of the Delphi method indicates that group opinion is more valid than individual opinion. This technique aims to achieve agreement among a group of experts on a specific issue that did not exist before [27]. The Delphi method is used in situations where it is impossible to gather experts. As the participants were orthodontic specialists at Iranian universities of medical sciences, this study applied the Delphi method to get their opinions.

Literature review

The literature review was conducted to determine the competencies required for orthodontic post-graduates. The ISI Web of Sciences, PubMed, Scopus, and Eric databases, Google Scholar search engine, and available orthodontic curricula were searched based on keywords, including “orthodontic graduate competencies”, “orthodontic competency”, and “orthodontic curriculum”. A list of 430 Sub-competencies and learning objectives (Appendix 1) was extracted in the fields of knowledge, attitude, and skills necessary for an orthodontic graduate. The list of Sub-competencies or learning objectives was reviewed by orthodontic (SA) and medical education experts (AO, HM, NY). Similar items were merged, and placed in related domains, ultimately, 77 competencies were approved in 11 competency domains.

The title of each domain of competence expresses its components in terms of "job responsibilities" or "core competencies" of orthodontics and specifies the main tasks and roles related to a specific field of competence. The eleven competence domains included “data collection and recording”, “treatment procedures”, “patient-centered treatment”, “dental knowledge”, “clinical reasoning and moral judgment”, “communication skills”, “health promotion”, “attitudes, legal responsibilities and ethical issues (professionalism)”, “information management”, “the dentist's role in the health system” and “Self-development”. The field of dental knowledge includes three subdomains: general medical knowledge, general dental knowledge, and specialized dental knowledge. (Appendix 2).

First round of Delphi

An e-questionnaire in the form of a 5-point Likert scale (“completely agree”, “agree”, “have no opinion”, “disagree”, and “completely disagree”) was developed and its content validity was confirmed by three medical education specialists and three orthodontic staffs. In this questionnaire the necessity of each competency for orthodontic graduates was questioned. At the beginning of the questionnaire, the purpose of the study was explained and informed consent was obtained. Then demographic information, including age, sex, university of service, teaching experience, and academic rank, was asked. At the end of each competency domain, an open question was used for the participants to write their opinions regarding the competencies of that area or to add another competence if necessary. The basis for choosing the competencies in the open questions was the opinions of the research time members. If the items were new and non-repeated, they were added to the competency questionnaire. They were entered as a competency in the second round of the Delphi. To select participants, a list of orthodontic staff of Iranian dental faculties and their e-mail addresses was provided and an e-questionnaire was sent to them. The number of faculty members in Iranian orthodontic departments was 199. Purposive sampling was done by selecting orthodontic staff from major Iranian universities who are involved in the training of orthodontic assistants (N = 52). Achieving a high response rate and maintaining it is one of the important goals of the Delphi study. Two to four weeks after distributing questionnaires, a follow-up was conducted. All questionnaires were analyzed using descriptive statistics (percentage). The level of consensus was set as 70% [28].

Second round of Delphi

Items with agreement less than 70% were transferred to the second round of the Delphi (if the frequency of responses for each competency on the strongly agree and agree scales was 70% or more, it was considered one of the necessary competencies for an orthodontic graduate; otherwise, it was transferred to the second round of the Delphi). These items were combined with the competencies obtained from open question and 13 competencies in 7 domains were included in the e-questionnaire. The second round of Delphi was done like first one with the same samples (Appendix 3). The drop of participants in the second stage of Delphi was 4% (N = 2), which was due to migration and finish of cooperation as a faculty member (N = 50). In the second round, analyzed data of the first stage (percentage) was sent anonymously to the participants along with the electronic questionnaire. This controlled feedback creates an opportunity for the participants to revise their previous answers. After collecting the questionnaires of the second round, data analysis was done by calculating the percentage. The competencies that more than 70% of the participants agreed or fully agreed with were confirmed and the rest were removed. The data analysis was performed using SPSS version 21 to calculate the frequency of the competencies.

Discussion

The results showed 10 competency domains and 73 competencies. The domains of competency, as presented in the results section, are data collection and recording; therapeutic procedures; patient-centered treatment plan; dental knowledge; clinical reasoning and moral judgment; communication skills; health promotion; attitude of legal responsibilities and ethical issues; information management and personal promotion that are necessary for the training of orthodontic assistants. In two Delphi rounds, one competency domain and 8 competencies were not accepted.

About competencies in general and specialized levels in medicine, there is a wide variety of competency frameworks in medical education. Can MEDS 2005 Project [29], the Dundee Outcome Model [30], the Outcome Project by the Accreditation Council for Graduate Medical Education [31], Tomorrow's Doctors by the UK General Medical Council [32], Scottish Doctor document introduced by five medical schools in Scotland [33], minimum essential requirements published by the Institute for International Medical Education [34], the educational blueprint for the Brown Medical School [35], and a core curriculum in 9 domains by the European Medical Students' Association and the International Federation of Medical Students' Associations [36] are examples of competency frameworks in medical education.

In dentistry, the Joint Committee for Postgraduate Education in Dentistry and the General Dental Council have written competency-based curricula for orthodontic residency [37, 38]. In the country of Sri Lanka in Southeast Asia, the curriculum of the orthodontic specialty course is written based on competency [39]. In 2011, the American Dental Education Association (ADEA) proposed a specific set of core competencies that graduates need to perform unsupervised work. These competencies include critical thinking, professionalism, communication and interpersonal skills, health promotion, practice management and informatics, and patient care [21]. Similar research has been conducted to determine competencies in dentistry by the Association for Dental Education in Europe (ADEE) in 2009 [40], the Australian Dental Council (ADC) in 2010 [41], the Association of Canadian Faculties of Dentistry (ACFD) in 2007 and 2019 [42, 43] and Dental Council of New Zealand (DCNZ) in 2012 [22]. The Association for Dental Education in Europe has presented seven competency domains to general practice in dentistry, which include: 1) professionalism 2) communication and interpersonal skills 3) knowledge base, information handling and critical thinking 4) clinical information gathering 5) diagnosis and treatment planning 6) establishment and maintenance of oral health 7) health promotion [40].

Donald Altman et al. published an article on the core competencies of dental public health (DPH) specialists, which outlines the skills, knowledge, and understanding expected of all dental public health specialists [44]. These competencies include managing oral health programs for population health, demonstrating ethical decision-making in the practice of DPH, evaluating systems of care that impact oral health, designing surveillance systems to measure oral health status and its determinants, communicating on oral and public health issues, lead collaborations on oral and public health issues, advocate for public health policy, legislation, and regulations to protect and promote the public’s oral health, and overall health, critically appraise evidence to address oral and public health issues for individuals and populations, conduct research to address oral and public health problems and Integrate the social determinants of health into DPH practice [44]. Malek Mohammadi et al. determined the competencies of a dental public health specialist using thematic analysis. The results of this study led to the extraction of a framework consisting of 9 competency domains for social dental professionals. These competency domains include education, research, management, policy-making, communication, leadership, professionalism, oral health status, and oral health services [5]. Jane A. Weintraub published an article on competency statements and performance indicators for the specialty of dental public health. He conducted a survey of four target groups by holding a training workshop. They extracted a list of skills, knowledge, or abilities that are most needed by dental public health specialists [45]. Christie M. Hogue and colleagues conducted an online Delphi study to determine the competencies of dental fellowship for the elderly. They used three Delphi rounds to obtain expert input and compiled a final list of 42 competencies [46].

In 2004, A. J. M. Plasschaert et al. developed a competency draft, sent it to European dental schools, and used the feedback received to refine the draft. They extracted 17 core competencies and supporting competencies for the general dentist. The field of main competencies includes professionalism, communication and interpersonal skills, basic knowledge, information handling and critical thinking, clinical information gathering, diagnosis and treatment planning, establishment and maintenance of oral health, and health promotion [40]. A study was conducted by Tan-Ching Hsu et al. at National Taiwan University (Taipei, Taiwan) to identify the essential core competencies needed by dental students before graduation. They extracted three important non-operational competencies, which were patient dentist discourse (querying patients and responding to questions), treatment planning, and the ability to deal with medically compromised dental patients. Three important operational competencies are: cavity filling, infection control, and proper handling of needles [47]. Concerning other specialties in dentistry, including orthodontics, the matter of competencies is still in its early stages.

Regarding the competency frameworks in different parts of the world, all the frameworks consider the three competencies of professionalism, communication and communication skills, and health promotion as common competencies for all dentists that they have accepted both at the general level and at the specialized level. Most of the studies carried out in dentistry have also mentioned these three competencies as necessary in dentistry. The distinguishing feature of the present study to others is the special emphasis on the two domains of dental knowledge and clinical reasoning. In the domain of dental knowledge, there are three subdomains including: medical knowledge, general dental knowledge, and specialized orthodontic knowledge, which cover all the fields of knowledge necessary to perform orthodontic skills.

In the clinical reasoning and moral judgment domain, there are four competencies which include: 1) perform logical reasoning by considering reasoning errors and cognitive errors, 2) identify and analyze problems related to your field of activity, 3) according to the probability of consequences, existing facilities and limitations, the cost-effectiveness of the solutions should be assessed, and the solutions should be prioritized, and 4) make decisions when faced with ethical dilemmas. Clinical reasoning has a special place in the competencies of graduates of health professions in this era. One of the possible reasons for this difference in the results of the studies is the use of different methods (thematic analysis or Delphi) and extensive review of articles to obtain the competencies expected from post-graduates, in addition, the difference in the fields in question can also affect the difference in the results. In this study, we obtained 10 competency domains for orthodontic graduates in Iran through two Delphi rounds. “​​The dentist's role in the health system” competency domain, was removed with less than 70% agreement in two rounds of Delphi. The reason may be the lack of enough knowledge and education about the importance and role of health systems in public health in routine dental curricula.More over, another reason is the lack of curriculum emphasis on issues related to community health, so the attitude of specialists has not been formed based on giving importance to community health. In this research, special attention has been paid to competency domains of “therapeutic procedures” and “attitude, legal responsibilities, and ethical issues (professionalism)”. In each competency domain there are several sub-competencies, cover most aspects of that competency domain. For example, the domain of “treatment procedures” includes 14 sub-competencies. The two domains of dental knowledge and therapeutic procedures had the most expert panel agreement and the largest number of sub-competencies.

This study is one of the first studies on determining competencies in the field of orthodontics, which can lead to improving orthodontic education and providing better services to patients. Competency framework Establishes a shared understanding of the skills, abilities, and knowledge needed for success in different roles. Developing a list of competencies is considered the first step in identifying learning objectives for educational programs. On the other hand, determining competencies can be the basis for developing competency-based curricula, competency-based education, and competency-based assessment. To develope education in different fields, dentistry and orthodontics, competencies, Entrustable Professional Activities (EPA) and milestones should be considered to take a step towards programmatic assessment. One of the limitations of this study was the lack of studies in the field of orthodontic competencies. Due to the lack of similar studies in orthodontics, it was impossible to compare the results with other studies Another limitation of this study was the time-consuming conduct of two Delphi rounds, which prolonged the decision-making process. On the other hand, it was difficult to motivate experts to answer the questions.

In recent years, artificial intelligence (AI) has been applied in various ways in medicine and dentistry. Advancements in AI technology show promising results in the practice of orthodontics. AI technology has achieved state-of-the-art results in various orthodontic applications, including automated landmark detection on lateral cephalograms and photography images, cervical vertebra maturation degree determination, skeletal classification, orthodontic tooth extraction decisions, predicting the need for orthodontic treatment or orthognathic surgery, and facial attractiveness. Although AI systems attract attention with their high speed and accuracy, it should not be forgotten that these systems have a margin of error, and the treatment process and results may differ from those previously simulated. Orthodontists should see these digital systems as a tool, and should not hesitate to intervene where necessary.

Conclusion

After two Delphi rounds, ten competency domains with 73 sub-competencies were accepted by 52 orthodontic specialists. They can be proposed as a framework for developing a competency-based curriculum, education, and assessment in post-graduate orthodontic programs.

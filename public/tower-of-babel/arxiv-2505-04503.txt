Abstract
. Consider the coupled Keller-Segel-Navier-Stokes or the chemotaxis-consumption-Navier-Stokes system in bounded Lipschitz domains for general coupling terms which, e.g., include buoyancy forces. It is shown that these systems admit local strong as well as global strong solutions for small data in the setting of critical Besov spaces. Moreover, non-trivial equilibria are shown to be exponentially stable. For smoother data, these solutions are shown to be globally bounded and to preserve positivity properties. The approach presented is based on optimal L q \mathrm{L}^{q} -regularity properties of the Neumann Laplacian and the Stokes operator in bounded Lipschitz domains.

1.
Introduction

The theory of reaction diffusion equations as well as the fundamental equations of fluid dynamics
have a truly long tradition and a beautiful history
[
20
]
.
In this paper we are concerned with the coupling of the mechanisms of
reaction-diffusion equations of chemotaxis type with incompressible visous fluids.
Our emphasize lies on the situation, where the underyling domains have nonsmooth boundaries,
more precisely, on domains with Lipschitz boundaries.

We investigate the coupled Keller-Segel-Navier-Stokes models as well as the
chemotaxis-consumption-Navier-Stokes model in Lipschitz domains.
There, the chemotaxis process is dissolved in an incompressible fluid and the diffusion process
interacts with the fluid. Problems of this type arise for example in the investigation of
the evolution of bacteria, when besides their chemotactically biased movement towards
their nutrient, a buoyancy driven effect on the fluid motion is observed, see
[
40
]
.
Such chemotaxis and chemotaxis-consumption models coupled to an ambient flow have
been studied, e.g., in
[
31
,
40
,
20
]
.

First analytic results in this direction go back to M. Winkler
[
41
]
,
who proved the existence of global weak solutions to the coupled system. While finite-time
blowup solutions to the classical Keller-Segel system have been studied in detail by many authors,
Li and Zhou
[
25
]
constructed very recently a smooth finite-time blowup solution to the coupled
Keller-Segel-Navier-Stokes system on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Furthermore, Gong and He
[
13
]
proved
the global existence of solutions to the two-dimensional coupled parabolic elliptic
Patlak-Keller-Navier-Stokes system with friction force provided the total mass satisfies

M<8\pi
.
For surveys of results concerning the Keller-Segel system and the chemotaxis-consumption model
itself, we refer to the articles of Lankeit and Winkler
[
22
,
23
]
.

As written above, our emphasis lies on the investigation of the coupled Keller-Segel-Navier-Stokes
system in Lipschitz domains. The analysis of elliptic operators and of the Stokes operator
acting in Lipschitz domains has seen many beautiful developments in the last years; we refer
here, e.g., to
[
9
,
18
,
34
,
21
,
36
,
11
,
37
,
3
,
12
]
.
In this paper we elaborate these developments further to investigate coupled
Keller-Segel-Navier-Stokes models as well as the chemotaxis-consumption-Navier-Stokes model
in Lipschitz domains.

For results concerning the Keller-Segel system itself in Lipschitz domains we refer to the work
of Horstmann, Meinlschmidt and Rehberg
[
16
]
. They proved
that this system, regarded in Lipschitz domains, possesses a unique local strong solution. A corresponding result in bounded convex domains is due to Hieber, Kress and Stinner
[
14
]
.
For the special situation of circular sectors in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
of angles
\theta\in(0,\pi/2]
, Fuest and Lankeit
[
10
]
recently
showed that the critical mass for the blow-up dichotomy for the full parabolic Keller-Segel system is given by
4\theta
and hence depends directly on the Lipschitz geometry. This has to be seen in contrast to the fact that in arbitrary bounded and smooth subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
global classical solutions always exist for masses less than
4\pi
, see
[
30
]
.

This shows that the Lipschitz geometry indeed plays a special role in the study of blow-up phenomena and provides a further motivation to study these coupled systems in general Lipschitz domains.

We are now describing our system in detail. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
n=2
or
n=3
, be
a bounded Lipschitz domain and let
0<T\leq\infty
.
The dynamics of the population density
u:(0,T)\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
, the concentration
of the chemical attractant
v:(0,T)\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
, the fluid
velocity
w:(0,T)\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the pressure

\pi:(0,T)\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
are described by the following
coupled systems of partial differential equations

(KSNS)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}u-\Delta u+w\cdot\nabla u&=-\operatorname{div}(u\nabla v)&&\text{in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
\partial_{t}v-\Delta v+v+w\cdot\nabla v&=u&&\text{in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
\partial_{t}w-\Delta w+(w\cdot\nabla)w+\nabla\pi&=uf&&\text{in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
\operatorname{div}(w)&=0&&\text{in }(0,T)\times\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

The function
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is supposed to be initially known. Of particular interest
for
n=3
is the function
f(x)=e_{3}
, where
e_{3}=-(0,0,1)^{\top}
. The system is then the
Keller-Segel-Navier-Stokes system with buoyancy and describes the cell’s reaction force to the
buoyancy exerted by the fluid.
The blowup scenario for the associated Patlak-Segel-Navier-Stokes system is very interesting.
Indeed, a smooth finite-time blowup solution for the Patlak-Segel-Navier-Stokes system in three
dimensions with buoyancy was constructed by Li and Zhou
[
25
]
. This means that the
Navier-Stokes equations cannot prevent the coupled system from finite-time blowup.
On the other hand, Hu, Kiselev and Yao
[
17
]
found a mechanism of suppression of
blowup by buoyancy and proved global existence for smooth data for a Keller-Segel system coupled
with a fluid flow adhering to Darcy’s law for porous media via buoyancy forces.

Note that our approach allows to consider arbritray coupling functions
f\in\mathrm{L}^{n}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
It remains an open problem to decide whether the above blowup and suppression from blowup
results remain valid for the Keller-Segel-Navier-Stokes and chemotaxis-consumption-Navier-Stokes models
under consideration.

This system (
KSNS
) is complemented by boundary conditions of the form

(BC)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{\nu}u=\partial_{\nu}v&=0&&\text{on }(0,T)\times\partial\Omega,\\
w&=0&&\text{on }(0,T)\times\partial\Omega\\
\end{aligned}\right.

and initial data

(IC)

u(0)=u_{0},\quad v(0)=v_{0},\quad w(0)=w_{0}\quad\text{in }\Omega.

Neglecting the lower-order term
v
in the second equation
of (
KSNS
), the system has the following scaling invariance:

\displaystyle u_{\lambda}(t,x)

\displaystyle:=\lambda^{2}u(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x),\quad v_{\lambda}(t,x):=v(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x),

\displaystyle w_{\lambda}(t,x)

\displaystyle:=\lambda w(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x),\quad\pi_{\lambda}(t,x):=\lambda^{2}\pi(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x),

whenever
f
is replaced by
f_{\lambda}(x):=\lambda f(\lambda x)
.

The aim of this paper is twofold: first we develop general methods for equations of
Keller-Segel-Navier-Stokes type in nonsmooth domains in order to establish strong
well-posedness results for scaling invariant critical spaces and secondly we
study stability of equilibria. More precisely, we prove local strong well-posedness,
Theorem
2.1
, as well as global strong well-posedness of
system (
KSNS
) for small data, along with the stability of non-trivial
equilibria in a critical functional setting, Theorem
2.3
.
In particular, our framework allows to solve the system for initial data in
critical Besov spaces. For the case of the uncoupled Navier-Stokes equations on

\mathbb{R}^{n}
, solutions in critical Besov spaces
\mathrm{B}^{n/q-1}_{q,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
were obtained first by
Cannone
[
4
]
. In our two well-posedness results we extend his result to the coupled
Keller-Segel-Navier-Stokes system in arbitrary bounded Lipschitz domains.
Moreover, for smooth enough initial data we show that the solutions are
bounded globally in space and time and that the population density remains
positive for positive initial data, see Theorem
2.4
.

Both well-posedness results presented in Section 2 are based on the maximal regularity
approach, see, e.g.,
[
6
,
2
,
32
]
. In order to treat the case of critical spaces
we use the technique of time-weighted maximal regularity developed by Prüss, Simonett
and Wilke in
[
33
]
. An essential ingredient of our approach is the knowledge of
optimal regularity properties of the linearization of (
KSNS
).

This is indeed a very delicate matter, since the underlying domains are only Lipschitz.
In Lipschitz domains, characterizations of domains of the Neumann Laplacian
and the Stokes operator pose major problems. Note that, in general, it is not true
that, even in
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
, the domain of the Neumann Laplacian is
\mathrm{H}^{2}(\Omega)
,
see, e.g., Jerison and Kenig
[
19
, Prop. 1.4]
.
Instead, one may establish only embeddings into fractional Sobolev spaces,
see Fabes, Mendez and Mitrea
[
9
, Thm. 9.2]
for the
Neumann Laplacian as well as the results due to Brown and Shen
[
3
, Thm. 2.12]

and Mitrea and Wright
[
29
, Thm. 10.6.2]
for the Stokes operator.

However, optimal descriptions of the domains of the corresponding square roots in
terms of first order Sobolev spaces were proved by Jerison and
Kenig
[
18
, Thm. 1]
, as well as by
Gabel and Tolksdorf
[
11
, Thm. 1.3]
and
[
36
, Thm. 1.1]
.
We will take advantage of these optimal characterizations to achieve results within
critical scaling invariant spaces.

Our method applies also to the chemotaxis-consumption-Navier-Stokes model, a modified
version of (
KSNS
) which was shown to be relevant to
describe chemotactic processes in a fluid
[
40
]
. For analytical results,
see
[
41
,
23
]
.
This system is given by

(CCNS)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}u-\Delta u+w\cdot\nabla u&=-\operatorname{div}(u\nabla v)&&\text{in }(0,T)\times\Omega\\
\partial_{t}v-\Delta v+w\cdot\nabla v&=-uv&&\text{in }(0,T)\times\Omega\\
\partial_{t}w-\Delta w+(w\cdot\nabla)w+\nabla\pi&=uf&&\text{in }(0,T)\times\Omega\\
\operatorname{div}(w)&=0&&\text{in }(0,T)\times\Omega\end{aligned}\right.

complemented with the same initial and boundary conditions as above.

Let us note that all results stated for the system (
KSNS
) are valid also
for (
CCNS
) as well, see Theorem
7.1
. The approach presented
is hence rather robust and can be adjusted to further variants without difficulties.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2

we collect well-known results for operators in Lipschitz domains, introduce the functional
setting and state our main results, which proofs can be found in
Section
3
(local well-posedness),
Section
4

(global well-posedness and stability) and Sections
5

and
6
for positivity and boundedness.
In the final Section
7
we present the treatment
of (
CCNS
).

2.
Preliminaries and Main Results

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain and
n=2
or
n=3
.
We start by rewriting the Keller-Segel-Navier-Stokes system (
KSNS
)
as a semilinear evolution equation of the form

(2.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}U+\mathcal{A}U&=\Phi(U,U)&&\text{in }(0,T),\\
U(0)&=U_{0}\end{aligned}\right.

where
U
is defined as the triplet
U=(u,v,w)
and
U_{0}=(u_{0},v_{0},w_{0})
.
We study this system on the space

\displaystyle X_{0}:=\mathrm{W}^{-1,q}_{0}(\Omega)\times\mathrm{L}^{q^{*}}(\Omega)\times\mathrm{L}^{q}_{\sigma}(\Omega)\quad\text{or on}\quad\mathcal{X}_{0}:=\mathrm{W}^{-1,q}_{\mathrm{av}}(\Omega)\times\mathrm{L}^{q^{*}}(\Omega)\times\mathrm{L}^{q}_{\sigma}(\Omega),

where
q^{*}
denotes the Sobolev exponent of
\mathrm{W}^{1,q}(\Omega)
, i.e.,

\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{n}=\frac{1}{q^{*}},

and
\mathrm{W}^{-1,q}_{0}(\Omega)
and
\mathrm{W}^{-1,q}_{\mathrm{av}}(\Omega)
denote the dual space of
\mathrm{W}^{1,q^{\prime}}(\Omega)
and
\mathrm{W}^{1,q^{\prime}}(\Omega)\cap\mathrm{L}^{q^{\prime}}_{\mathrm{av}}(\Omega)
, respectively, with
1/q+1/q^{\prime}=1
. Moreover,
\mathrm{L}^{q}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
denotes, as usual,
the space of solenoidal
\mathrm{L}^{q}
-vector fields with vanishing normal component and
\mathrm{L}^{r}_{\mathrm{av}}(\Omega)

the mean value free subspace of
\mathrm{L}^{r}(\Omega)
for
1<r<\infty
. The
operator
\mathcal{A}
is given by

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}:=\begin{pmatrix}-\Delta&0&0\\
-\Id&-\Delta_{q^{*}}+\Id&0\\
-\mathbb{P}[f\boldsymbol{\cdot}]&0&A\end{pmatrix}

and equipped with the domain

X_{1}:=\mathrm{W}^{1,q}(\Omega)\times\dom(\Delta_{q^{*}})\times\dom(A)\quad\text{or}\quad\mathcal{X}_{1}:=[\mathrm{W}^{1,q}(\Omega)\cap\mathrm{L}^{q}_{\mathrm{av}}(\Omega)]\times\dom(\Delta_{q^{*}})\times\dom(A).

Here,
\Delta
denotes the Neumann-Laplacian defined on
\mathrm{W}^{-1,q}_{0}(\Omega)

or on
\mathrm{W}^{-1,q}_{\mathrm{av}}(\Omega)
,
respectively, and
\Delta_{r}
the Neumann-Laplacian on
\mathrm{L}^{r}(\Omega)
for
1<r<\infty
.
Furthermore,
A
denotes the Stokes operator on
\mathrm{L}^{q}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
and

\mathbb{P}
the Helmholtz projection. For
q
satisfying

(2.3)

\displaystyle\Big\lvert\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{2}\Big\rvert<\frac{1}{2n}+\varepsilon,

where
\varepsilon>0
is a number depending on the Lipschitz geometry, it is
known, see
[
34
, Cor. 1.2]
and
[
11
, Cor. 1.2]
, that the Stokes operator generates a bounded analytic semigroup
on
\mathrm{L}^{q}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
and the Helmholtz projection is a bounded operator
on
\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{n})
, see
[
9
, Thm. 11.1]
and
[
27
, Thm. 1.2]
. Observe, that the condition
f\in\mathrm{L}^{n}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
implies that
uf\in\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
so that
\mathbb{P}[fu]
is a well-defined element in
\mathrm{L}^{q}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
.

We continue by introducing time weighted Banach space valued function spaces.
Let
1<p<\infty
,
0<T\leq\infty
and
\mu\in(1/p,1]
and let

\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mu}(0,T;X)
denote the space defined by

\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mu}(0,T;X):=\{u:(0,T)\to X\text{ measurable}:t\mapsto t^{1-\mu}u(t)\in\mathrm{L}^{p}(0,T;X)\}

endowed with the norm

\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mu}(0,T;X)}:=\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}\|t^{1-\mu}u(t)\|_{X}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

The associated weighted Sobolev spaces are defined by

\mathrm{H}^{1,p}_{\mu}(0,T;X):=\{u\in\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mu}(0,T;X):u^{\prime}\in\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mu}(0,T;X)\}

equipped with the canonical norm.

Given a generator
L
of an analytic semigroup on
X
, we say that
L
admits maximal

\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mu}
-regularity on
(0,T)
,
0<T\leq\infty
if for any

g\in\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mu}(0,T;X)
and
u_{0}\in(X,\dom(L))_{\mu-1/p,p}

there exists a unique

u\in\mathrm{H}^{1,p}_{\mu}(0,T;X)\cap\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mu}(0,T;\dom(L))=:\mathbb{E}_{p,\mu}^{T}(X,\dom(L))

satisfying

u^{\prime}+Lu=g,\ u(0)=u_{0}.

The closed graph theorem implies the existence of some constant
C>0
such that

(2.4)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathbb{E}_{p,\mu}^{T}(X,\dom(L))}\leq C\big(\|g\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mu}(0,T;X)}+\|u_{0}\|_{(X,\dom(L))_{\mu-1/p,p}}).

If
L
has maximal
\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mu}
-regularity on
(0,T_{0})
,
0<T_{0}\leq\infty
,
then
L
has maximal
\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mu}
-regularity on
(0,T)
for every
0<T<T_{0}

and the constant
C
can be chosen independently of
T
. Recall that
L
has maximal

\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mu}
-regularity if and only if it has maximal

\mathrm{L}^{p}
-regularity
[
32
, Thm. 3.5.4]
(i.e.,
\mu=1
).

Observe that the outcome of the real interpolation space in (
2.4
) is often a Besov space. For a brief introduction of all necessary Bessel potential and Besov spaces we refer the reader to the appendix.
We are now in the position to formulate our first main result on local existence of a unique, strong solution to (
KSNS
).

Theorem 2.1

(Local existence)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain and
n=2
or
n=3
.
Then there exists
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\Omega)>0
, depending on the
Lipschitz geometry of
\Omega
, such that for all
p,q\in(1,\infty)
satisfying

q<n\quad\mbox{and}\quad\frac{n}{q}+\frac{2}{p}\leq 3\quad\mbox{and}\quad\frac{n+1}{2n}-\varepsilon<\frac{1}{q}<\frac{n+1}{2n}+\varepsilon

and all

(u_{0},v_{0},w_{0})\in\mathrm{B}^{n/q-2}_{q,p,0}(\Omega)\times\mathrm{B}^{n/q^{*}}_{q^{*},p}(\Omega)\times\mathrm{B}^{n/q-1}_{q,p,0,\sigma}(\Omega)

and all
f\in\mathrm{L}^{n}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
there exists
T>0
such that the Keller-Segel-Navier-Stokes system (
KSNS
)
admits a unique strong solution
(u,v,w)
satisfying

\displaystyle(u,v,w)\in\mathbb{E}_{p,\mu}^{T}(X_{0},X_{1}),\quad\mbox{where}\quad\mu=\frac{n}{2q}+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}.

Next, we formulate our global existence and stability result. We start by describing the set of stationary solutions for which we show exponential stability. For this purpose, we define the mean value of a distribution
u_{0}\in\mathrm{B}^{n/q-2}_{q,p,0}(\Omega)
as

u_{0,\Omega}:=\frac{1}{\lvert\Omega\rvert}\langle u_{0},1\rangle_{\mathrm{B}^{n/q-2}_{q,p,0},\mathrm{B}^{2-n/q}_{q^{\prime},p^{\prime}}}.

Now, a stationary solution to (
KSNS
) is given by

(u_{s},v_{s},w_{s})^{\top}\quad\text{where}\quad u_{s}=v_{s}=u_{0,\Omega}

and
w_{s}
satisfies the stationary Navier-Stokes system

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta w_{s}+(w_{s}\cdot\nabla)w_{s}+\nabla\pi_{s}&=fu_{s}&&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\operatorname{div}{w_{s}}&=0&&\text{in }\Omega,\\
w_{s}&=0&&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

Observe that the existence of a stationary weak solution is classical whenever

f\in\mathrm{W}^{-1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
. In our global existence and stability
result, the following smallness condition on the product
\lvert u_{s}\rvert f
will be crucial.

Assumption 2.2
.

For some
\delta>0
, the data
u_{0}\in\mathrm{B}^{n/q-2}_{q,p,0}(\Omega)

and
f\in\mathrm{L}^{n}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfy

(1)

\lvert u_{s}\rvert\|f\|_{\mathrm{W}^{-1,2}(\Omega)}\leq\delta
in the case
n=2
;

(2)

\lvert u_{s}\rvert\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{3/2}(\Omega)}\leq\delta
in the case
n=3
.

Theorem 2.3

(Global existence and stability)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain and
n=2
or
n=3
.
Then there exists
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\Omega)>0
, depending on the Lipschitz
geometry of
\Omega
with the following property.
For every
p,q\in(1,\infty)
satisfying

(2.5)

q<n\quad\mbox{and}\quad\frac{n}{q}+\frac{2}{p}\leq 3\quad\mbox{and}\quad\frac{n+1}{2n}-\varepsilon<\frac{1}{q}<\min\Big(\frac{n+1}{2n}+\varepsilon,\frac{2}{n}\Big)

there exist
\delta,\lambda,C>0
such that for all
0<\kappa<C
and all

(u_{0},v_{0},w_{0})\in\mathrm{B}^{n/q-2}_{q,p,0}(\Omega)\times\mathrm{B}^{n/q^{*}}_{q^{*},p}(\Omega)\times\mathrm{B}^{n/q-1}_{q,p,0,\sigma}(\Omega)

with

\|u_{0}-u_{s}\|_{\mathrm{B}^{n/q-2}_{q,p,0}(\Omega)}+\|v_{0}-v_{s}\|_{\mathrm{B}^{n/q^{*}}_{q^{*},p}(\Omega)}+\|w_{0}-w_{s}\|_{\mathrm{B}^{n/q-1}_{q,p,0,\sigma}(\Omega)}\leq\kappa

and all
f\in\mathrm{L}^{n}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfying Assumption
2.2
for this

\delta
, there exists a unique global strong solution
(u,v,w)

to (
KSNS
) satisfying

\|t\mapsto\mathrm{e}^{\lambda t}[(u(t),v(t),w(t))-(u_{s},v_{s},w_{s})]\|_{\mathbb{E}_{p,\mu}^{\infty}(\mathcal{X}_{0},\mathcal{X}_{1})}\leq 2\kappa,\quad\mbox{where}\quad\mu=\frac{n}{2q}+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}.

In particular,

\mathrm{e}^{\lambda t}\bigl\|(u(t),v(t),w(t))-(u_{s},v_{s},w_{s})\bigr\|_{(\mathcal{X}_{0},\mathcal{X}_{1})_{\mu-1/p,p}}\xrightarrow[t\to\infty]{}0.

For the boundedness and positivity of solutions we require slightly more regularity
conditions on the data. Therefore, we introduce

Y_{0}:=\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)\times\mathrm{L}^{q^{*}}(\Omega)\times\mathrm{L}^{q}_{\sigma}(\Omega)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{Y}_{0}:=\mathrm{L}^{q}_{\mathrm{av}}(\Omega)\times\mathrm{L}^{q^{*}}(\Omega)\times\mathrm{L}^{q}_{\sigma}(\Omega)

and

Y_{1}:=\dom(\Delta_{q})\times\dom(\Delta_{q^{*}})\times\dom(A)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{Y}_{1}:=\dom(\Delta_{q})\cap\mathrm{L}^{q}_{\mathrm{av}}(\Omega)\times\dom(\Delta_{q^{*}})\times\dom(A).

and let
\mathbb{E}_{r,1}^{\infty}(\mathcal{Y}_{0},\mathcal{Y}_{1})
be defined as before.

Theorem 2.4

(Boundedness and positivity)
.

In the setting of Theorem
2.3
there exists
\lambda,\delta,C>0
and

r_{0}>1
such that for all
0<\kappa<C
, all
r>r_{0}
and all

(u_{0},v_{0},w_{0})\in\big(Y_{0},Y_{1}\big)_{1-1/r,r}\quad\text{with}\quad\|(u_{0}-u_{s},v_{0}-v_{s},w_{0}-w_{s})\|_{(\mathcal{Y}_{0},\mathcal{Y}_{1})_{1-1/r,r}}\leq\kappa

the unique solution constructed in Theorem
2.3
satisfies

\|t\mapsto\mathrm{e}^{\lambda t}[(u(t),v(t),w(t))-(u_{s},v_{s},w_{s})]\|_{\mathbb{E}_{r,1}^{\infty}(\mathcal{Y}_{0},\mathcal{Y}_{1})}\leq 2\kappa.

Moreover, it is globally bounded in space and time, i.e.,

(u,v,w)\in\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(0,\infty;\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)\times\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)\times\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})),

and, if
u_{0}\geq 0
a.e., then
u(t)\geq 0
for all a.e.
t>0
and a.e.
x\in\Omega
.

The proofs of our main results rely on optimal
\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{p}
-regularity properties
of the linear part of (
KSNS
). If
\Omega
would have smooth boundaries, the
result would follow from rather classical results on the Laplacian and the Stokes operator.
This is, however, not the case for Lipschitz domains as the
\dom(\mathcal{A})
is in general unknown.
To counteract this problem, we use optimal descriptions of domains of square roots of our
linear operators at hand.

The domains of the square roots of
-\Delta_{q}
as well as
A
can be characterized
by first-order Sobolev spaces, i.e.,

(2.6)

\displaystyle\dom((-\Delta_{q})^{1/2})=\mathrm{W}^{1,q}(\Omega)\quad\text{and}\quad\dom(A^{1/2})=\mathrm{W}^{1,q}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega),

whenever
q
satisfies (
2.3
) with some possibly smaller

\varepsilon
, see
[
18
, Thm. 1]
for the Laplacian and
[

as well as
[

for the Stokes operator. In the following let

be sufficiently small such that all these conditions can be met and let

\displaystyle\frac{n+1}{2n}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}<\frac{1}{q}<\frac{n+1}{2n}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Moreover, there is no harm in assuming that

\displaystyle\varepsilon\leq\frac{2}{n}.

Indeed, in the case
\varepsilon>\frac{2}{n}

) and (

. However, (

) will help us to write certain conditions more concisely.

Note that the Neumann Laplacian as well as the Stokes operator admit a

\mathrm{H}^{\infty}

X_{0}
,
see
[

[

Hence, the complex interpolation space

between

If

(2.9)

\displaystyle X_{1/2}=\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)\times\mathrm{W}^{1,q^{*}}(\Omega)\times\mathrm{W}^{1,q}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega)

optimally embed

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
q\in(1,\infty)
be subject to (
2.7
). For
0<\delta<\frac{n\varepsilon}{4}
and
0<\delta^{\prime}\leq\frac{1}{2}
define

\alpha=\frac{1}{2}+\delta\quad\text{and}\quad\alpha^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}+\delta^{\prime}.

Then,

(2.10)

\displaystyle\dom((-\Delta_{q^{*}})^{\alpha})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{W}^{1,r}(\Omega)\quad\text{and}\quad\dom(A^{\alpha^{\prime}})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{W}^{1,\kappa}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega)

where
r
and
\kappa
satisfy

(2.11)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q^{*}}-\frac{2\delta}{n}=\frac{1}{r}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{1}{q}-\frac{2\delta^{\prime}}{n}=\frac{1}{\kappa}.

Proof.

Using (
2.8
), we remark that for the choices of
\delta
and
\delta^{\prime}
we have

\Big\lvert\frac{1}{\rho}-\frac{1}{2}\Big\rvert<\frac{1}{2n}+\varepsilon

for the four choices of
\rho

\frac{1}{\rho}=\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{n}\quad\text{or}\quad\frac{1}{\rho}=\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{n}-\frac{2\delta}{n}\quad\text{or}\quad\frac{1}{\rho}=\frac{1}{q}\quad\text{or}\quad\frac{1}{\rho}=\frac{1}{q}-\frac{2\delta^{\prime}}{n}.

Writing

(2.12)

\displaystyle(\Id-\Delta_{q^{*}})^{-\alpha}=(\Id-\Delta_{q^{*}})^{-1/2}(\Id-\Delta_{q^{*}})^{-\delta}

and noticing that for
g\in\mathrm{L}^{q^{*}}(\Omega)
the square root property (
2.6
) and a Sobolev embedding implies that

\|(\Id-\Delta_{q^{*}})^{-\alpha}g\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1,r}(\Omega)}\leq C\|(\Id-\Delta_{q^{*}})^{-\delta}g\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}(\Omega)}\leq C\|(\Id-\Delta_{q^{*}})^{-\delta}g\|_{\mathrm{H}^{2\delta,q^{*}}(\Omega)}.

The last term can be estimated by noting that
\dom((-\Delta_{q^{*}})^{\delta})=\mathrm{H}^{2\delta,q^{*}}(\Omega)
, which follows by (
2.6
) and complex interpolation. Thus

\displaystyle\|(\Id-\Delta_{q^{*}})^{-\alpha}g\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1,r}(\Omega)}\leq C\|(\Id-\Delta_{q^{*}})^{-\delta}g\|_{\mathrm{H}^{2\delta,q^{*}}(\Omega)}\leq C\|g\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q^{*}}(\Omega)}.

We proceed analogously for the Stokes operator, but omit the details.
∎

The above optimal embeddings in the first-order Sobolev scale will be combined with the
maximal
\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mu}
-regularity property of

, which is provided by the following

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
\frac{1}{p}<\mu\leq 1
and
q\in(1,\infty)
be subject to (
2.7
). Then the operator
\mathcal{A}
defined in
(
2.2
) on
X_{0}
with domain
X_{1}
has maximal
\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mu}
-regularity on

(0,T)
for any
0<T<\infty
. Moreover, on
\mathcal{X}_{0}
with domain
\mathcal{X}_{1}
it has maximal

\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mu}
-regularity on
(0,\infty)
.

Proof.

We only consider the global maximal regularity property for
\mathcal{A}
on
\mathcal{X}_{0}
with domain
\mathcal{X}_{1}
.
The other case follows a similar reasoning. We remark, that it is classical, that one only
needs to consider that case of homogeneous initial conditions. Moreover,
by
[
32
, Thm. 3.5.4]
, it suffices to prove that
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{X}_{1})
has maximal

\mathrm{L}^{p}
-regularity, i.e., for
\mu=1
.

We first give a short argument that shows the maximal
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-regularity for
-\Delta

on
\mathrm{W}^{-1,q}_{\mathrm{av}}(\Omega)
relying
on Amann’s inter- and extrapolation scales
[
1
, Sec. 6]
: we regard

\Delta_{q^{\prime}}
as an operator on
\mathrm{L}^{q^{\prime}}_{\mathrm{av}}(\Omega)
,
so that, by invertibility of
\Delta_{q^{\prime}}
and heat kernel bounds of the
corresponding semigroup, it has maximal
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-regularity on
(0,\infty)
, see
[
15
, Thm. 3.1]
. Moreover,
since
(-\Delta_{q^{\prime}})^{1/2}
commutes with the time derivative and with

\Delta_{q^{\prime}}
, we conclude that
-\Delta_{q^{\prime}}
has maximal

\mathrm{L}^{p}
-regularity on
(0,\infty)
with respect to the ground space

\dom((-\Delta_{q^{\prime}})^{1/2})=\mathrm{W}^{1,q^{\prime}}(\Omega)\cap\mathrm{L}^{q^{\prime}}_{\mathrm{av}}(\Omega)
. By duality, we infer that
-\Delta
has
maximal
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-regularity on
(0,\infty)
with respect to the ground space

\mathrm{W}^{-1,q}_{\mathrm{av}}(\Omega)
.

Let
g=(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3})\in\mathrm{L}^{p}(0,\infty;\mathcal{X}_{0})
. Maximal
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-regularity for
-\Delta
on

\mathrm{W}^{-1,q}_{\mathrm{av}}(\Omega)

ensures that the problem

\partial_{t}u-\Delta u=g_{1}\text{ in }(0,\infty)\times\Omega,\quad\partial_{\nu}u=0\text{ on }(0,\infty)\times\partial\Omega,\quad u(0,\cdot)=0\text{ in }\Omega

has a unique solution

u\in\mathrm{W}^{1,p}\bigl(0,\infty;\mathrm{W}^{-1,q}_{\mathrm{av}}(\Omega)\bigr)\cap\mathrm{L}^{p}\bigl(0,\infty;\mathrm{W}^{1,q}(\Omega)\bigr)
.
This gives that
u\in\mathrm{L}^{p}\bigl(0,\infty;\mathrm{L}^{q^{*}}(\Omega)\bigr)
.
Next, since
\Id-\Delta_{q^{*}}
satisfies heat kernel bounds it has maximal

\mathrm{L}^{p}
-regularity on
\mathrm{L}^{q^{*}}(\Omega)
, see
[
15
, Thm. 3.1]
, and provides a
solution
v\in\mathrm{W}^{1,p}\bigl(0,\infty;\mathrm{L}^{q^{*}}(\Omega)\bigr)\cap\mathrm{L}^{p}\bigl(0,\infty;\dom(\Delta_{q^{*}})\bigr)
of

\partial_{t}v+(\Id-\Delta_{q^{*}})v=g_{2}+u\text{ in }(0,\infty)\times\Omega,\quad\partial_{\nu}v=0\text{ on }(0,\infty)\times\partial\Omega,\quad v(0,\cdot)=0\text{ in }\Omega.

Finally, since by assumption
f\in\mathrm{L}^{n}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, we have
that
\mathbb{P}(uf)\in\mathrm{L}^{p}\bigl(0,\infty;\mathrm{L}^{q}_{\sigma}(\Omega)\bigr)
and
then maximal
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-regularity satisfied by the Stokes operator

A
in
\mathrm{L}^{q}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
,
[

, Thm. 16]
, ensures that the problem

\partial_{t}w+Aw=g_{3}+\mathbb{P}(uf),\quad w(0,\cdot)=0\text{ in }\Omega

has a unique solution

w\in\mathrm{W}^{1,p}\bigl(0,\infty;\mathrm{L}^{q}_{\sigma}(\Omega)\bigr)\cap\mathrm{L}^{p}\bigl(0,\infty;\dom(A)\bigr)
.
∎

3.
Proof of Local solvability

In this section we show that system (
2.1
) admits a unique
local strong solution in
\mathbb{E}_{p,\mu}^{T}(X_{0},X_{1})
. We are going to apply the well-known contraction principle,
see Lemma
3.1
(
1
) below, stated as in
[
24
, Thm. 22.4]
, see also
[
5
, Lem. 4, Sec. 3.1]
, and its straightforward variant (
2
).

Lemma 3.1

(Fixed point theorem)
.

Let
E
be a Banach space and
\Psi:E\times E\to E
a bounded
bilinear map with norm
C>0
.

(1)

For all
0<\kappa<\frac{1}{4C}
and for all
a\in E
with

\|a\|_{E}\leq\kappa
, there exists a unique
u\in E
with

\displaystyle\|u\|_{E}<2\kappa\quad\text{such that}\quad u=a+\Psi(u,u).

(2)

Let
E_{0}\subset E
be a Banach space and assume that
\Psi
takes its values in
E_{0}
.
Then for
all
a\in E
with
\|a\|_{E}\leq\frac{1}{4C}
, there exists a unique

v\in E_{0}
with

\displaystyle\|v\|_{E_{0}}<\frac{1}{4C}\quad\text{satisfying}\quad v=\Psi(a,a)+\Psi(a,v)+\Psi(v,a)+\Psi(v,v).

Remark 3.2
.

A solution
v\in E_{0}
given by (
2
) provides a solution
u\in E
of (
1
)
with
u=v+a
in Lemma
3.1
.

In our situation the bilinear map
\Psi
is given by
(\partial_{t}+\mathcal{A})^{-1}\Phi
where

(3.1)

\displaystyle\Phi(U_{1},U_{2}):=\begin{pmatrix}\Phi_{1}(U_{1},U_{2})\\
\Phi_{2}(U_{1},U_{2})\\
\Phi_{3}(U_{1},U_{2})\end{pmatrix}:=\begin{pmatrix}-w_{1}\cdot\nabla u_{2}-\operatorname{div}(u_{1}\nabla v_{2})\\
-w_{1}\cdot\nabla v_{2}\\
-\mathbb{P}(w_{1}\cdot\nabla)w_{2}\end{pmatrix}\cdotp

In the following two lemmas we are going to prove its boundedness on

\mathbb{E}_{p,\mu}^{T}(X_{0},X_{1})
.

Lemma 3.3
.

For every
1<q<n
subject to (
2.7
) there exist
0<\delta<\frac{n\varepsilon}{4}
and
0<\delta^{\prime}<\frac{1}{2}
satisfying

(3.2)

\displaystyle\delta+\delta^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{n}{q}-1\Big).

Moreover, if
\beta:=\frac{1}{2}+\delta
and

\beta^{\prime}:=\frac{1}{2}+\delta^{\prime}
, the map
\Phi:(X_{\beta}\cap X_{\beta^{\prime}})\times(X_{\beta}\cap X_{\beta^{\prime}})\to X_{0}
given as in (
3.1
) satisfies
the estimate

\displaystyle\|\Phi(U_{1},U_{2})\|_{X_{0}}\leq C\big(\|U_{1}\|_{X_{\beta}}\|U_{2}\|_{X_{\beta^{\prime}}}+\|U_{1}\|_{X_{\beta^{\prime}}}\|U_{2}\|_{X_{\beta}}\big).

Proof.

First of all, we establish the existence of
\delta
and
\delta^{\prime}
. We define the continuous and decreasing function

\displaystyle\delta^{\prime}:[0,\tfrac{n\varepsilon}{4}]\to\mathbb{R},\quad\delta^{\prime}(\delta):=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{n}{q}-1\Big)-\delta.

Note that
\delta^{\prime}(0)>0
since
q<n
. At the endpoint
\delta=\tfrac{n\varepsilon}{4}
, we use (
2.7
) to estimate

\displaystyle\delta^{\prime}(\tfrac{n\varepsilon}{4})=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{n}{q}-1\Big)-\frac{n\varepsilon}{4}<\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{n+1}{2}+\frac{n\varepsilon}{2}-1\Big)-\frac{n\varepsilon}{4}=\frac{n-1}{4}\leq\frac{1}{2}\quad\text{if}\quad n\leq 3.

Thus, there exists
0<\delta<\frac{n\varepsilon}{4}
such that
0<\delta^{\prime}(\delta)<\frac{1}{2}
. We then define
\delta^{\prime}:=\delta^{\prime}(\delta)
.

We start by estimating
\Phi_{1}(U_{1},U_{2})
in
\mathrm{W}^{-1,q}_{0}(\Omega)
,
where
U_{1},U_{2}\in X_{\beta}\cap X_{\beta^{\prime}}
. To this end, write

\displaystyle\Phi_{1}(U_{1},U_{2})=-w_{1}\cdot\nabla u_{2}-\operatorname{div}(u_{1}\nabla v_{2})=-\operatorname{div}(w_{1}u_{2})-\operatorname{div}(u_{1}\nabla v_{2})

since
w_{1}
is divergence-free.
Consequently, the
\mathrm{W}^{-1,q}_{0}(\Omega)
-norm of
\Phi_{1}(U_{1},U_{2})
is estimated by

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{1}(U_{1},U_{2})\|_{\mathrm{W}^{-1,q}_{0}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|w_{1}u_{2}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)}+\|u_{1}\nabla v_{2}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|w_{1}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}(\Omega)}\|u_{2}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{s}(\Omega)}+\|u_{1}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{s}(\Omega)}\|\nabla v_{2}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}(\Omega)},

where
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{s}
. Notice that

u_{1},u_{2}\in\mathrm{H}^{-1+2\beta^{\prime},q}(\Omega)=\mathrm{H}^{2\delta^{\prime},q}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{s}(\Omega)\quad\text{whenever}\quad\frac{1}{q}-\frac{2\delta^{\prime}}{n}=\frac{1}{s}.

Moreover, by virtue of (
2.10
), we obtain

v_{1},v_{2}\in\dom((-\Delta_{q^{*}})^{\beta})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{W}^{1,r}(\Omega)\quad\text{whenever}\quad\frac{1}{q^{*}}-\frac{2\delta}{n}=\frac{1}{r}.

Finally, employing (
2.10
) delivers

(3.3)

\displaystyle w_{1},w_{2}\in\dom(A_{q}^{\beta})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{W}^{1,\kappa}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{r}(\Omega)\quad\text{whenever}\quad\frac{1}{\kappa}-\frac{1}{n}=\frac{1}{r},

where
\kappa
is given in (
2.11
). Putting all the
conditions on the parameters together reduces to

\delta+\delta^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}(\frac{n}{q}-1)
.
This gives

\|\Phi_{1}(U_{1},U_{2})\|_{\mathrm{W}^{-1,q}_{0}(\Omega)}\leq C\big(\|U_{1}\|_{X_{\beta}}\|U_{2}\|_{X_{\beta^{\prime}}}+\|U_{2}\|_{X_{\beta}}\|U_{1}\|_{X_{\beta^{\prime}}}\big).

Concerning the estimate of
\Phi_{2}(U_{1},U_{2})
, write

\Phi_{2}(U_{1},U_{2})=-w_{1}\cdot\nabla v_{2}.

Hölder’s inequality delivers

\|\Phi_{2}(U_{1},U_{2})\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q^{*}}(\Omega)}\leq\|w_{1}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\tau}(\Omega)}\|\nabla v_{2}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}(\Omega)}

with
r
is given as above and
\tau
satisfies

\frac{1}{q^{*}}=\frac{1}{\tau}+\frac{1}{r}
.
Using that

w_{1}\in\dom(A_{q}^{\beta^{\prime}})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{W}^{1,\kappa^{\prime}}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{\tilde{\tau}}(\Omega)\quad\text{whenever}\quad\frac{1}{\tilde{\tau}}=\frac{1}{\kappa^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{n}=\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1+2\delta^{\prime}}{n}

and noticing that the conditions above imply
\tau=\tilde{\tau}
yields

\|\Phi_{2}(U_{1},U_{2})\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q^{*}}(\Omega)}\leq C\|U_{2}\|_{X_{\beta}}\|U_{1}\|_{X_{\beta^{\prime}}}.

Next, we estimate
\Phi_{3}(U_{1},U_{2})
. Here we obtain

\|-\mathbb{P}(w_{1}\cdot\nabla)w_{2}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)}\leq C\|w_{1}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}(\Omega)}\|\nabla w_{2}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{s}(\Omega)}.

Observe that
s=\kappa^{\prime}
so that if
w_{2}\in\dom(A_{q}^{\beta^{\prime}})
,
then

\|\nabla w_{2}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{s}}\leq C\|U_{2}\|_{X_{\beta^{\prime}}}

and therefore

\|\Phi_{3}(U_{1},U_{2})\|_{L^{q}}\leq\|U_{1}\|_{X_{\beta}}\|U_{2}\|_{X_{\beta^{\prime}}}.\qed

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
0<T\leq\infty
,
p\in(1,\infty)
,
1<q<n
be subject to (
2.7
) and
\mu\in(1/p,1]
satisfy

(3.4)

\frac{n}{2q}-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{p}\leq\mu\leq 1.

Then there exists
C>0
, independent of
T
,
such that for all
U_{1},U_{2}\in\mathbb{E}_{p,\mu}^{T}(X_{0},X_{1})
with
U_{1}|_{t=0}=0=U_{2}|_{t=0}
, we have

(3.5)

\|\Phi(U_{1},U_{2})\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(0,T;X_{0})}\leq C\|U_{1}\|_{\mathbb{E}_{p,\mu}^{T}(X_{0},X_{1})}\|U_{2}\|_{\mathbb{E}_{p,\mu}^{T}(X_{0},X_{1})}.

Proof.

Let
\mu\in(1/p,1]
. Then, for
1<r<\infty
and

\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}=1
,
define
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in(\mu,1]
as

(3.6)

\displaystyle 1-\mu=r(1-\sigma)=r^{\prime}(1-\sigma^{\prime}).

With appropriate choices of
\beta,\beta^{\prime}\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
, Lemma
3.3
implies

\displaystyle\|\Phi(U_{1},U_{2})\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mu}(0,T;X_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\|\|U_{1}\|_{X_{\beta}}\|U_{2}\|_{X_{\beta^{\prime}}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mu}(0,T)}+\|\|U_{1}\|_{X_{\beta^{\prime}}}\|U_{2}\|_{X_{\beta}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mu}(0,T)}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\|U_{1}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{pr}_{\sigma}(0,T;X_{\beta})}\|U_{2}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{pr^{\prime}}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(0,T;X_{\beta^{\prime}})}

\displaystyle\qquad+\|U_{1}\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\sigma^{\prime}}^{pr^{\prime}}(0,T;X_{\beta^{\prime}})}\|U_{2}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{pr}_{\sigma}(0,T;X_{\beta})}\Big).

We proceed by virtue of a weighted Sobolev inequality. Let, for a moment,
T
be finite. To have an implicit constant that is independent of
T
, we rescale
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
by defining
V_{1},V_{2}\in E^{1}_{p,\mu}
via

\displaystyle V_{1}(t):=U_{1}(tT)\quad\text{and}\quad V_{2}(t):=U_{2}(tT)\qquad(t\in(0,1)).

In the following,
C>0
denotes a generic constant independent of
T
. The weighted Sobolev embeddings

\mathrm{H}^{1-\beta,p}_{\mu}(0,1;X_{\beta})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{pr}_{\sigma}(0,1;X_{\beta})\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{H}^{1-\beta^{\prime},p}_{\mu}(0,1;X_{\beta^{\prime}})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{pr^{\prime}}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(0,1;X_{\beta^{\prime}})

stated in
[
26
, Cor. 1.4]
are true provided

(3.7)

\displaystyle 1-\beta-\frac{1}{p}-(1-\mu)\geq-\frac{1}{pr}-(1-\sigma)\quad\mbox{and}\quad 1-\beta^{\prime}-\frac{1}{p}-(1-\mu)\geq-\frac{1}{pr^{\prime}}-(1-\sigma^{\prime}).

Observe that this imposes additional constraints on the parameter
r
. Let us show that such a choice is possible. Using (
3.6
), the previous two inequalities translate to

\displaystyle 1-\beta-\frac{1}{p}-(1-\mu)\geq-\frac{1}{r}\Big(1+\frac{1}{p}-\mu\Big)\quad\mbox{and}\quad 1-\beta^{\prime}\geq\frac{1}{r}\Big(1+\frac{1}{p}-\mu\Big).

Consequently, such an
r
exists if the inequalities

(3.8)

\displaystyle 1<r<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\Big(\beta+\frac{1}{p}-\mu\Big)\Big(1+\frac{1}{p}-\mu\Big)^{-1}\leq\frac{1}{r}\leq(1-\beta^{\prime})\Big(1+\frac{1}{p}-\mu\Big)^{-1}

are simultaneously true. We first verify that the left-hand side of the second condition in (
3.8
) is indeed smaller than the right-hand side. Using (
3.2
) this is equivalent to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{n}{q}+1\Big)+\frac{1}{p}-\mu\leq 1\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\frac{n}{2q}+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}\leq\mu.

Now, the condition
1<r<\infty
can be guaranteed if the left-hand side of the second condition in (
3.8
) is strictly smaller than 1. But this is true since
\beta<1
. Thus, an appropriate choice of
r
can be made in such a way that (
3.7
) is valid. As a consequence, we can use the weighted Sobolev inequality from
[
26
, Cor. 1.4]
to deduce

\displaystyle\|U_{1}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{pr}_{\sigma}(0,T;X_{\beta})}=T^{1-\sigma+\frac{1}{pr}}\|V_{1}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{pr}_{\sigma}(0,1;X_{\beta})}\leq CT^{1-\sigma+\frac{1}{pr}}\|V_{1}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1-\beta,p}_{\mu}(0,1;X_{\beta})}.

We write the norm of
\mathrm{H}^{1-\beta,p}_{\mu}(0,1;X_{\beta})
in terms of fractional powers as

\displaystyle\|V_{1}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1-\beta,p}_{\mu}(0,1;X_{\beta})}\simeq\|\partial_{t}^{1-\beta}(1+\mathcal{A}^{\beta})V_{1}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mu}(0,1;X_{0})}+\|(1+\mathcal{A})^{\beta}V_{1}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mu}(0,1;X_{0})},

where
\partial_{t}
denotes the time derivative on
\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mu}(0,1;X_{0})
with domain
\dom(\partial_{t})=\{w\in\mathrm{H}^{1,p}_{\mu}(0,1;X_{0}):w|_{t=0}=0\}
. Since this operator is invertible, we may even write

\displaystyle\|V_{1}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1-\beta,p}_{\mu}(0,1;X_{\beta})}\simeq\|\partial_{t}^{1-\beta}(1+\mathcal{A})^{\beta}V_{1}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mu}(0,1;X_{0})}.

For a given parameter
\nu>0
, we apply the Kalton-Weis theorem
[
32
, Thm. 4.5.6]
to the
\mathcal{R}
-bounded holomorphic function
f(z)=z^{1-\beta}(1+\mathcal{A})^{\beta}(\nu z+\nu^{1-\frac{1}{\beta}}(1+\mathcal{A}))^{-1}
and observe that its
\mathcal{R}
-bound is independent of
\nu
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle\|U_{1}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{pr}_{\sigma}(0,T;X_{\beta})}

\displaystyle\leq CT^{1-\sigma+\frac{1}{pr}}\|\partial_{t}^{1-\beta}(1+\mathcal{A})^{\beta}V_{1}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mu}(0,1;X_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq CT^{1-\sigma+\frac{1}{pr}}\Big(\nu\|\partial_{t}V_{1}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mu}(0,1;X_{0})}+\nu^{1-\frac{1}{\beta}}\|(1+\mathcal{A})V_{1}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mu}(0,1;X_{0})}\Big)

with a constant
C
being independent of
T
and
\nu
. We insert
V_{1}(t)=U_{1}(tT)
and obtain

\displaystyle\|U_{1}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{pr}_{\sigma}(0,T;X_{\beta})}\leq CT^{\mu-\sigma+\frac{1}{pr}-\frac{1}{p}}\Big(\nu T\|\partial_{t}U_{1}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mu}(0,T;X_{0})}+\nu^{1-\frac{1}{\beta}}\|(1+\mathcal{A})U_{1}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mu}(0,T;X_{0})}\Big).

Observe that
\mu-\sigma+\frac{1}{pr}-\frac{1}{p}=\beta-1
. We choose
\nu=T^{-\beta}
and deduce

\displaystyle\|U_{1}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{pr}_{\sigma}(0,T;X_{\beta})}\leq C\Big(\|\partial_{t}U_{1}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mu}(0,T;X_{0})}+\|(1+\mathcal{A})U_{1}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mu}(0,T;X_{0})}\Big)\leq C\|U_{1}\|_{\mathbb{E}_{p,\mu}^{T}(X_{0},X_{1})}

for some constant
C
independent of
T
. Because of this independence, we might even transfer this estimate to the case
T=\infty
. One derives an analogous estimate for
U_{2}
and thus arrives at estimate (
3.5
).
∎

We are now in the position to apply the contraction principle to establish the local
strong well-posedness theorem.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

We start by rewriting the abstract Keller-Segel-Navier-Stokes system (
2.1
)
as the fixed point problem

U(t)=\mathrm{e}^{-t{\mathcal{A}}}U_{0}+(\partial_{t}+{\mathcal{A}})^{-1}\Phi(U,U)(t),\quad t\in[0,T].

If
p,q\in(1,\infty)
fulfill the conditions of Theorem
2.1
, we have the bilinear estimate from Lemma
3.4
at our disposal. Here, we choose
\mu=\frac{n}{2q}+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}
. Note that
\mu>\frac{1}{p}
is true since
q<n
and that
\mu\leq 1
holds by the conditions assumed in the theorem. Thus, by virtue of the contraction mapping principle, Lemma
3.1
(
2
), it suffices to prove that

\|t\mapsto\mathrm{e}^{-t{\mathcal{A}}}U_{0}\|_{\mathbb{E}_{p,\mu}^{T}(X_{0},X_{1})}\xrightarrow[T\to 0]{}0,

which is the case whenever
U_{0}\in(X_{0},X_{1})_{\mu-1/p,p}
. The characterization
of the latter space in terms of appropriate Besov spaces is delicate since the
domain of the associated operators are not explicit. By the reiteration
theorem
[
38
, Thm. 2, Sec. 1.10.3]
we have

\big(X_{0},X_{1}\big)_{\alpha,p}=\big(X_{0},X_{1/2}\big)_{2\alpha,p}\quad\text{for}\quad 0<\alpha<\frac{1}{2}.

Combining (
2.9
) with the interpolation results
in
[
39
, Thm. 2.13]
and Proposition
8.1
we arrive at

\big(X_{0},X_{1}\big)_{\alpha,p}=\mathrm{B}^{2\alpha-1}_{q,p}(\Omega)\times\mathrm{B}^{2\alpha}_{q^{*},p}(\Omega)\times\mathrm{B}^{2\alpha}_{q,p,0,\sigma}(\Omega)\quad\text{for}\quad 0<\alpha<\frac{1}{2}.

Taking
\alpha=\mu-\frac{1}{p}=\frac{n}{2q}-\frac{1}{2}
yields the claim.
∎

4.
Global existence for small initial data

In this section we aim for a global strong existence result for the
system (
KSNS
) subject to small initial data. Recall, that
for a fixed initial datum
u_{0}\in\mathrm{B}^{n/q-2}_{q,p,0}(\Omega)
we
defined its mean value as

u_{0,\Omega}:=\frac{1}{\lvert\Omega\rvert}\langle u_{0},1\rangle_{\mathrm{B}^{n/q-2}_{q,p,0},\mathrm{B}^{2-n/q}_{q^{\prime},p^{\prime}}}.

We decompose

U=\widehat{U}+\begin{pmatrix}u_{s}\\
v_{s}\\
w_{s}\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{with}\quad u_{s}:=u_{0,\Omega}=:v_{s}

where
\widehat{U}
and
w_{s}
are solutions to the following equations.
The function
w_{s}
is supposed to solve the stationary Navier-Stokes system given by

(4.1)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta w_{s}+(w_{s}\cdot\nabla)w_{s}+\nabla\pi_{s}&=fu_{s}&&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\operatorname{div}{w_{s}}&=0&&\text{in }\Omega,\\
w_{s}&=0&&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

Furthermore,
\widehat{U}=(\widehat{u},\widehat{v},\widehat{w})
is subject to

(4.2)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\widehat{u}-\Delta\widehat{u}+w_{s}\cdot\nabla\widehat{u}+u_{s}\Delta\widehat{v}&=\Phi_{1}(\widehat{U},\widehat{U})&&\text{in }(0,\infty)\times\Omega,\\
\partial_{t}\widehat{v}-\widehat{u}-\Delta\widehat{v}+\widehat{v}+w_{s}\cdot\nabla\widehat{v}&=\Phi_{2}(\widehat{U},\widehat{U})&&\text{in }(0,\infty)\times\Omega,\\
\partial_{t}\widehat{w}-\Delta\widehat{w}+\nabla\widehat{\pi}-f\widehat{u}+(w_{s}\cdot\nabla)\widehat{w}+(\widehat{w}\cdot\nabla)w_{s}&=-(\widehat{w}\cdot\nabla)\widehat{w}&&\text{in }(0,\infty)\times\Omega,\\
\operatorname{div}{\widehat{w}}&=0&&\text{in }(0,\infty)\times\Omega,\\
\partial_{\nu}\widehat{u}=\partial_{\nu}\widehat{v}=0\quad\text{and}\quad\widehat{w}&=0&&\text{on }(0,\infty)\times\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

where
\Phi
is the map defined by (
3.1
), where the system is
complemented by the initial data

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \widehat{u}_{0}&=u_{0}-u_{s}&&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\widehat{v}_{0}&=v_{0}-v_{s}&&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\widehat{w}_{0}&=w_{0}-w_{s}&&\text{in }\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

Lemma 4.1
.

For any
f\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
there exists a weak solution

w_{s}\in\dom(A)
, where
A
denotes the Stokes operator on
\mathrm{L}^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
,
solving (
4.1
). In particular,

w_{s}\in\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathrm{W}^{1,n}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

\|w_{s}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1,n}(\Omega)}\leq C\big(\lvert u_{s}\rvert\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\lvert u_{s}\rvert^{2}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\big).

Proof.

We start with the case
n=3
. Let

w_{s}\in\mathrm{W}^{1,2}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega)
be a solution to (
4.1
) constructed by energy methods, see, e.g.,
[
35
, Thm. 1.2, Chap. II]
. It satisfies

(4.3)

\displaystyle\|\nabla w_{s}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\lvert u_{s}\rvert\|f\|_{\mathrm{W}^{-1,2}(\Omega)}.

Using, in addition, a Sobolev embedding, we estimate the nonlinearity as

\|(w_{s}\cdot\nabla)w_{s}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{3/2}(\Omega)}\leq\|w_{s}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{6}(\Omega)}\|\nabla w_{s}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\nabla w_{s}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C\lvert u_{s}\rvert^{2}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

and regard it as a right-hand side for the linear Stokes system. Then, the
regularity result of Brown and Shen
[
3
, Thm. 2.12]
yields that

w_{s}\in\mathrm{W}^{3/2,2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{W}^{1,3}(\Omega)
and

\displaystyle\|w_{s}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1,3}(\Omega)}\leq C\|w_{s}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{3/2,2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{s}f-(w_{s}\cdot\nabla)w_{s}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{3/2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\lvert u_{s}\rvert\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\lvert u_{s}\rvert^{2}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\big).

Now, that
w_{s}
has more regularity, we can bootstrap the regularity of the
nonlinearity and arrive at

\|(w_{s}\cdot\nabla)w_{s}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|w_{s}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{6}(\Omega)}\|\nabla w_{s}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{3}(\Omega)}\leq C\big(\lvert u_{s}\rvert\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\lvert u_{s}\rvert^{2}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\big)\lvert u_{s}\rvert\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}.

Regarding the nonlinearity again as a right-hand side we obtain,
since
f\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, that
w_{s}
is in the domain of
A
on

\mathrm{L}^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
. Since
\dom(A)
embeds into
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
see
[
3
, Thm. 2.12]
, we conclude the proof of the three-dimensional case.

The case
n=2
is similar. Here, one may directly use the regularity result of Mitrea and Wright
[
29
, Thm. 10.6.2]
for the embedding into
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
once
(w_{s}\cdot\nabla)w_{s}
is shown to belong to, e.g.,
\mathrm{L}^{3/2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
.
∎

The system (
4.2
) can be rewritten
equivalently as an evolution equation

(4.4)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\widehat{U}+\mathcal{A}\widehat{U}+\mathcal{B}_{s}\widehat{U}&=\Phi(\widehat{U},\widehat{U})&&\text{for }t\in(0,\infty),\\
\widehat{U}(0)&=\widehat{U}_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

on the space
\mathcal{X}_{0}
defined in Section
2
.
The operator
\mathcal{A}
, introduced in (
2.2
),
is now equipped with the domain
\mathcal{X}_{1}
and the operator
\mathcal{B}_{s}
is given by

\mathcal{B}_{s}:=\begin{pmatrix}w_{s}\nabla&u_{s}\Delta&0\\
0&w_{s}\nabla&0\\
0&0&\mathbb{P}[(w_{s}\nabla)\,\boldsymbol{\cdot}\,]+\mathbb{P}[(\,\boldsymbol{\cdot}\,\nabla)w_{s}]\end{pmatrix},

defined on the domain
\dom(\mathcal{B}_{s}):=\mathcal{X}_{1}
. The nonlinearity

\Phi(\widehat{U},\widehat{U})
is the same as in (
3.1
).

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
q\in(\frac{n}{2},\infty)
be subject to (
2.7
). There exists
\delta>0
such that for all
f\in\mathrm{L}^{n}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})

satisfying

\displaystyle\lvert u_{s}\rvert\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\lvert u_{s}\rvert\leq\delta

the operator
\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}_{s}
has maximal
\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mu}
-regularity on
(0,\infty)

on the ground space
\mathcal{X}_{0}
. In particular,
0\in\rho(\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}_{s})
.

Proof.

Recall that an operator that has maximal
\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mu}
-regularity on
(0,\infty)
is necessarily invertible. Thus, the invertibility as well as the maximal
\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mu}
-regularity of
\mathcal{A}
follow from Proposition
2.6
.

The maximal regularity property of
\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}_{s}
is deduced
by a relative bounded perturbation criterion
[
7
, Prop. 4.2]
.
More precisely, given
\varepsilon>0
we verify that

(4.5)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{B}_{s}\widehat{U}\|_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}\leq\varepsilon\|\mathcal{A}\widehat{U}\|_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}\qquad(\widehat{U}\in\mathcal{X}_{1})

whenever
\delta
is small enough. In particular, this criterion together with invertibility guarantees the invertibility of
\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}_{s}
once (
4.5
) is verified.

Notice that Lemma
4.1
together with the assumption guarantees that for any
1<r<\infty

\displaystyle\|w_{s}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}(\Omega)}\leq C\delta(1+\delta)\quad\text{and}\quad\|\nabla w_{s}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{n}(\Omega)}\leq C\delta(1+\delta).

We will frequently use these estimates. For instance, since

\dom(\Delta)=\mathrm{W}^{1,q}(\Omega)\cap\mathrm{L}^{q}_{\mathrm{av}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{q^{*}}(\Omega)
, we find

\displaystyle\|w_{s}\cdot\nabla\widehat{u}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{-1,q}_{\mathrm{av}}(\Omega)}=\|\operatorname{div}(w_{s}\widehat{u})\|_{\mathrm{W}^{-1,q}_{\mathrm{av}}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|w_{s}\widehat{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|w_{s}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{n}(\Omega)}\|\widehat{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q^{*}}(\Omega)}\leq C\delta(1+\delta)\|\Delta\widehat{u}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{-1,q}_{\mathrm{av}}(\Omega)}.

Similarly, since
\nabla(\Id-\Delta_{q^{*}})^{-1}
is bounded on
\mathrm{L}^{q^{*}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)
, we have

\displaystyle\|u_{s}\Delta\widehat{v}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{-1,q}_{\mathrm{av}}(\Omega)}\leq C\lvert u_{s}\rvert\|\nabla\widehat{v}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta\|(\Id-\Delta_{q^{*}})\widehat{v}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q^{*}}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta\big(\|(\Id-\Delta_{q^{*}})\widehat{v}-\widehat{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q^{*}}(\Omega)}+\|\Delta\widehat{u}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{-1,q}_{\mathrm{av}}(\Omega)}\big).

Next, let
r>q^{*}
satisfy (
2.3
). The property (
2.6
) implies
\|\nabla\widehat{v}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}(\Omega)}\leq C\|(\Id-\Delta_{q^{*}})^{1/2}\widehat{v}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}(\Omega)}
. Moreover, since
q^{*}>n
a Sobolev embedding yields
\dom((\Id-\Delta_{q^{*}})^{1/2})=\mathrm{W}^{1,q^{*}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{r}(\Omega)
. Thus, if
r^{\prime}
is such that
1/r^{\prime}+1/r=1/q^{*}
, we conclude

\displaystyle\|w_{s}\cdot\nabla\widehat{v}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q^{*}}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|w_{s}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\widehat{v}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta(1+\delta)\|(\Id-\Delta_{q^{*}})^{1/2}\widehat{v}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta(1+\delta)\|(\Id-\Delta_{q^{*}})\widehat{v}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q^{*}}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta(1+\delta)\big(\|(\Id-\Delta_{q^{*}})\widehat{v}-\widehat{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q^{*}}(\Omega)}+\|\Delta\widehat{u}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{-1,q}_{\mathrm{av}}(\Omega)}\big).

In order to estimate the remaining term, choose
q<\rho<q^{*}
and

1/\rho^{\prime}+1/\rho=1/q
. Note that, by Lemma
2.5
, we have that
\dom(A)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{W}^{1,q^{*}}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
, so that

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{P}[(\widehat{w}\cdot\nabla)w_{s}]\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle+\|\mathbb{P}[(w_{s}\cdot\nabla)\widehat{w}]\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\widehat{w}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q^{*}}(\Omega)}\|\nabla w_{s}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{n}(\Omega)}+\|w_{s}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\rho^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\widehat{w}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\rho}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta(1+\delta)\|A\widehat{w}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta(1+\delta)\big(\|A\widehat{w}-\mathbb{P}[f\widehat{u}]\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{n}(\Omega)}\|\widehat{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q^{*}}(\Omega)}\big)

\displaystyle\leq C\delta(1+\delta)\big(\|A\widehat{w}-\mathbb{P}[f\widehat{u}]\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{n}(\Omega)}\|\Delta\widehat{u}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{-1,q}_{\mathrm{av}}(\Omega)}\big).

Choosing
\delta
small enough yields (
4.5
).
∎

Remark 4.3
.

An analysis of the proofs of Lemma
4.1
and
4.2
shows that the following weaker smallness conditions would suffice:

\displaystyle\lvert u_{s}\rvert\|f\|_{\mathrm{W}^{-1,2}(\Omega)}+\lvert u_{s}\rvert\leq\delta\quad\text{if }n=2\quad\text{or}\quad\lvert u_{s}\rvert\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{3/2}(\Omega)}+\lvert u_{s}\rvert\leq\delta\quad\text{if }n=3.

Proof of Theorem
2.3
.

Let
U_{s}:=(u_{s},v_{s},w_{s})
be a stationary solution constructed above.
In the following, we will construct a global-in-time solution
\widehat{U}

of (
4.4
). The solution
U

to (
KSNS
) will then be given by
U:=\widehat{U}+U_{s}
.

To show exponential stability of the stationary solution
U_{s}
, note that

V:=\mathrm{e}^{\omega t}\widehat{U}
,
\omega>0
, satisfies

(4.6)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}V+(\mathcal{A}-\omega\Id)V+\mathcal{B}_{s}V&=\mathrm{e}^{-\omega t}\Phi(V,V)&&\text{for }t\in(0,\infty),\\
V(0)&=\widehat{U}_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

Let
\delta>0
be small enough such that Lemma
4.2
is applicable. Choosing
\omega
small enough, we see by Lemma
4.2
that

\mathcal{A}-\omega\Id+\mathcal{B}_{s}
is still invertible and admits maximal
\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mu}
-regularity
on
\mathcal{X}_{0}
globally in time. We write (
4.6
) as the fixed point problem

V(t)=\mathrm{e}^{-t({\mathcal{A}}-\omega\Id+\mathcal{B}_{s})}\widehat{U}_{0}+(\partial_{t}+{\mathcal{A}}-\omega\Id+\mathcal{B}_{s})^{-1}\Phi(V,V)(t),\quad t\in[0,\infty).

Using the contraction mapping principle, Lemma
3.1
(
1
),
and Lemma
3.4
it suffices to prove that

\|t\mapsto\mathrm{e}^{-t({\mathcal{A}}-\omega\Id+\mathcal{B}_{s})}\widehat{U}_{0}\|_{\mathbb{E}_{p,\mu}^{\infty}(\mathcal{X}_{0},\mathcal{X}_{1})}\quad\text{is small enough.}

This, however, is a direct consequence of (
2.4
)
and the maximal
\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mu}
-regularity property of
\mathcal{A}-\omega\Id+\mathcal{B}_{s}

on
(0,\infty)
whenever
\widehat{U}_{0}
is small enough in
(\mathcal{X}_{0},\mathcal{X}_{1})_{\mu-1/p,p}
. As in the proof of Theorem
2.1
this interpolation space can be computed as

\displaystyle(\mathcal{X}_{0},\mathcal{X}_{1})_{\mu-1/p,p}=\{h\in\mathrm{B}^{n/q-2}_{q,p,0}(\Omega):\langle h,1\rangle=0\}\times\mathrm{B}^{n/q^{*}}_{q^{*},p}(\Omega)\times\mathrm{B}^{n/q-1}_{q,p,0,\sigma}(\Omega).

Since

\mathbb{E}_{p,\mu}^{\infty}(\mathcal{X}_{0},\mathcal{X}_{1})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{BUC}([0,\infty);(\mathcal{X}_{0},\mathcal{X}_{1})_{\mu-1/p,p})

we have that
\sup_{0<t<\infty}\|V(t)\|_{(\mathcal{X}_{0},\mathcal{X}_{1})_{\mu-1/p,p}}<\infty

and thus there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
t>0

\|U(t)-U_{s}\|_{(\mathcal{X}_{0},\mathcal{X}_{1})_{\mu-1/p,p}}=\mathrm{e}^{-\omega t}\|V(t)\|_{(\mathcal{X}_{0},\mathcal{X}_{1})_{\mu-1/p,p}}\leq C\mathrm{e}^{-\omega t}.

The desired number
\lambda
could now be chosen as
\lambda:=\omega/2
.
∎

5.
Positivity

The positivity of the population density will follow from the following auxiliary result for a linear parabolic system subject to homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions. We will say that the normal derivative
\partial_{\nu}v
of a function
v\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega)
vanishes on
\partial\Omega
if

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla v\cdot\psi\,\mathrm{d}x=-\int_{\Omega}v\operatorname{div}(\psi)\,\mathrm{d}x=0\qquad(\psi\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega)).

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
w\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,T;\mathrm{L}^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega))
and
v\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,T;\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega))

satisfy
\partial_{\nu}v(t)=0
on
\partial\Omega
for almost every
t\in(0,T)
.
Let
u\in\mathrm{W}^{1,1}(0,T;\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega))\cap\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,T;\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega))

and
u_{0}\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)
with
u_{0}\geq 0
a.e. in
\Omega
satisfy

(5.1)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}u-\Delta u+w\cdot\nabla u+\operatorname{div}(u\nabla v)&=0&&\text{in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
\partial_{\nu}u&=0&&\text{on }(0,T)\times\partial\Omega,\\
u(0)&=u_{0}&&\text{in }\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

Then
u(t)\geq 0
a.e. in
\Omega
and almost every
t\in(0,T)
.

Proof.

We test the equation by an approximation of
-1
on
\{u<0\}

and show that
u^{-}(t):=-\min(0,u(t))
vanishes a.e. in
\Omega
.
For this purpose, let
\eta>0
and define

\varphi_{\eta}(r):=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }r\geq 0,\\
r/\eta,&\text{if }-\eta<r<0,\\
-1,&\text{if }r\leq-\eta.\end{cases}

Then

u(t,\cdot)\varphi_{\eta}(u(t,\cdot))\to u^{-}(t,\cdot)\quad\text{as}\quad\eta\to 0\quad\text{in}\quad\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)

for almost every
0<t<T
. In addition, we have

\nabla\varphi_{\eta}(u)=\varphi_{\eta}^{\prime}(u)\nabla u=\frac{1}{\eta}1\hskip-2.2pt\text{l}_{\{-\eta<u<0\}}\nabla u.

Now, test the equation (
5.1
) by
\varphi_{\eta}(u)

to obtain for
0<\tau\leq T

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}u\varphi_{\eta}(u)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\varphi_{\eta}^{\prime}(u)\nabla u\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\operatorname{div}(wu)\varphi_{\eta}(u)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\operatorname{div}(u\nabla v)\varphi_{\eta}(u)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

Next, we employ the product rule in the first term and an integration by parts in the last two terms and use that
\partial_{\nu}v
as well as
\nu\cdot w
vanish on
\partial\Omega
(since
w(t)\in\mathrm{L}^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
) and thus arrive at

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\big(u\varphi_{\eta}(u)\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}u\partial_{t}u\varphi_{\eta}^{\prime}(u)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{\eta}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\{-\eta<u<0\}}\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{1}{\eta}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\{-\eta<u<0\}}u\nabla u\cdot\big(w+\nabla v\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

Since
u_{0}\geq 0
, we have
\varphi_{\eta}(u_{0})=0
. Thus, we see that

\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\big(u\varphi_{\eta}(u)\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{\Omega}u(\tau,x)\varphi_{\eta}(u(\tau,x))\,\mathrm{d}x.

Rearranging the terms and using that
\lvert u/\eta\rvert\leq 1

on
\{-\eta<u<0\}
yields that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u(\tau,x)\varphi_{\eta}(u(\tau,x))\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{\eta}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\{-\eta<u<0\}}\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\{-\eta<u<0\}}\lvert\partial_{t}u\rvert\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\{-\eta<u<0\}}\lvert\nabla u\rvert\lvert w+\nabla v\rvert\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\{-\eta<u<0\}}\lvert\partial_{t}u\rvert\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{\eta}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\{-\eta<u<0\}}\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\frac{\eta}{4}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\{-\eta<u<0\}}\lvert w+\nabla v\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

Ultimately, we get

\int_{\Omega}u(\tau,x)\varphi_{\eta}(u(\tau,x))\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\{-\eta<u<0\}}\lvert\partial_{t}u\rvert\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\frac{\eta}{4}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\{-\eta<u<0\}}\lvert w+\nabla v\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t,

which shows as
\eta\to 0
that
u^{-}(\tau,\cdot)=0
almost everywhere in
\Omega
.
∎

6.
Regularity and boundedness

In order to establish the boundedness of solutions to (
KSNS
) for small
initial data, we change the functional setting from
X_{0}
to a space
Y_{0}
.
Let
q\in(\frac{n}{2},\infty)
satisfy (
2.7
) and define

Y_{0}:=\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)\times\mathrm{L}^{q^{*}}(\Omega)\times\mathrm{L}^{q}_{\sigma}(\Omega)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{Y}_{0}:=\mathrm{L}^{q}_{\mathrm{av}}(\Omega)\times\mathrm{L}^{q^{*}}(\Omega)\times\mathrm{L}^{q}_{\sigma}(\Omega).

Recall that, since
q>n/2
, we have that
q^{*}>n
. As before,
\mathcal{A}

Lemma 6.1

Let

Proof.

For the first embedding, notice that, as before, we have

(6.1)

\displaystyle\dom(\Delta_{q})\times\dom(\Delta_{q^{*}})\times\dom(A)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{W}^{1,q^{*}}(\Omega)\times\mathrm{W}^{1,q^{*}}(\Omega)\times\mathrm{W}^{1,q^{*}}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega)=:Z.

Concerning the second embedding, notice that

The following lemma provides an appropriate bilinear estimate in this functional setting.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
q\in(\frac{n}{2},\infty)

Proof.

The four terms are estimated as follows

\|w_{1}\cdot\nabla u_{2}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)}\leq\|w_{1}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{n}(\Omega)}\|\nabla u_{2}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q^{*}}(\Omega)}\leq C\|w_{1}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{n}(\Omega)}\|u_{2}\|_{\dom(\Delta_{q})}.

For the second one, we estimate

\displaystyle\|\operatorname{div}(u_{1}\nabla v_{2})\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{1}\Delta v_{2}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla u_{1}\cdot\nabla v_{2}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{1}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{n}(\Omega)}\|\Delta v_{2}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q^{*}}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla u_{1}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q^{*}}(\Omega)}\|\nabla v_{2}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{n}(\Omega)}

Summarizing we obtain the space estimate

large enough

Lemma 6.3

For the description of solenoidal function spaces the normal trace of a vector field is crucial. It is defined as follows: If

w\cdot\nu
as an element of

\displaystyle\nu\cdot w\in\bigcap_{\frac{1}{q}-1<s^{\prime}<\min(s,\frac{1}{q})}\mathrm{B}^{s^{\prime}-1/q}_{q,q}(\partial\Omega).

Finally, we define

\displaystyle\mathrm{B}^{s}_{q,p,0,\sigma}(\Omega):=\{w\in\mathrm{B}^{s}_{q,p,z}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{n}):\operatorname{div}(w)=0\text{ and }\nu\cdot w=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega\}.

Our goal is now to establish the following interpolation result.

Proposition 8.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
n\geq 2
, be a bounded Lipschitz domain,
s_{0},s_{1}>\frac{1}{q}-1
,
1<p,q<\infty
and
\theta\in(0,1)
. If
s:=(1-\theta)s_{0}+\theta s_{1}
, then we have

\displaystyle\big(\mathrm{H}^{s_{0},q}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega),\mathrm{H}^{s_{1},q}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega)\big)_{\theta,p}=\mathrm{B}^{s}_{q,p,0,\sigma}(\Omega)

with equivalent norms.

Proof.

We closely follow the lines of the corresponding result for the complex interpolation method in
[
28
, Thm. 2.12]
. We introduce the spaces

\displaystyle X_{j}:=\mathrm{H}^{s_{j},q}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{n})\quad\text{and}\quad Y_{j}:=\big\{f\in\mathrm{H}^{s_{j}-1,q}_{0}(\Omega):\langle f,1\rangle_{\mathcal{E}^{\prime},\mathcal{E}}=0\big\}\qquad(j=0,1)

where
\mathcal{E}
denotes the space of smooth functions and
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
the space of distributions with compact support, and define

\displaystyle D:X_{j}\to Y_{j},\;w\mapsto\operatorname{div}(w)\qquad(j=0,1).

Moreover, for
j=0,1
, we define

\displaystyle G:\mathrm{H}^{s_{j}-1,q_{j}}_{0}(\Omega)\to X_{j},\;f\mapsto\widetilde{(K\circ\psi)(f)},

where the tilde means “entension to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by zero”,
K:(\mathrm{H}^{s_{j}-1,q_{j}^{\prime}}(\Omega))^{*}\to\mathrm{H}^{s_{j},q_{j}}_{z}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{n})
denotes the Bogovskĭi operator studied in
[
28
, Prop. 2.5]
and
\psi
denotes the extension of the operator

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathrm{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\to\mathrm{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega),\;\widetilde{f}\mapsto f\quad\text{as an isomorphism}\quad\mathrm{H}^{1-s_{j},q_{j}}_{0}(\Omega)\to(\mathrm{H}^{s_{j}-1,q_{j}^{\prime}}(\Omega))^{*}

and was studied in
[
28
, Lem. 2.4]
. It was then proven in
[
28
, p. 1541]
that

\displaystyle D\circ G=\Id\text{ on }Y_{j}\text{ for }j=0,1.

The previous relation implies that
\mathcal{P}:=G\circ D
restricts to bounded linear projections on
X_{j}
for each
j=0,1
. Consequently, the kernel of
\mathcal{P}|_{X_{j}}
is a complemented subspace of
X_{j}
and it is given by

\displaystyle\ker(\mathcal{P}|_{X_{j}})=\ker(D|_{X_{j}})=\big\{w\in\mathrm{H}^{s_{j},q}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{n}):\operatorname{div}(w)=0\big\}.

Interpolation of complemented subspaces
[
38
, Thm. 1.17.1]
now yields that

\displaystyle\big(\ker(\mathcal{P}|_{X_{0}}),\ker(\mathcal{P}|_{X_{1}})\big)_{\theta,p}=\big\{w\in\big(\mathrm{H}^{s_{0},q}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{n}),\mathrm{H}^{s_{0},q}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{n})\big)_{\theta,p}:\operatorname{div}(w)=0\big\}.

Next, it was proven below
[
28
, Eq. (2.106)]
that the restriction operator

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\cdot}\,|_{\Omega}:\ker(\mathcal{P}|_{X_{0}})\to\mathrm{H}^{s_{j},q}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega)\qquad(j=0,1)

is an isomorphism. Hence,

\displaystyle\big(\mathrm{H}^{s_{0},q}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega),\mathrm{H}^{s_{1},q}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega)\big)_{\theta,p}=\big\{w|_{\Omega}:w\in\big(\mathrm{H}^{s_{0},q}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{n}),\mathrm{H}^{s_{0},q}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{n})\big)_{\theta,p}\text{ and }\operatorname{div}(w)=0\big\}.

Using
[
39
, Thm. 3.5]
we readily find that

\displaystyle\big(\mathrm{H}^{s_{0},q}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{n}),\mathrm{H}^{s_{0},q}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{n})\big)_{\theta,p}=\mathrm{B}^{s}_{q,p,0}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{n}).

Below
[
28
, Eq. (2.106)]
it was, in particular, proven that
w|_{\Omega}\cdot\nu=0
for all
w\in\mathrm{H}^{s^{\prime}-1/q,q}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{n})
with
\operatorname{div}(w)=0
, where
s^{\prime}>\frac{1}{q}-1
, which implies that
w|_{\Omega}\cdot\nu=0
for all
w\in\mathrm{B}^{s}_{q,p,0}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{n})
with
\operatorname{div}(w)=0
. It follows that

\displaystyle\big(\mathrm{H}^{s_{0},q}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega),\mathrm{H}^{s_{1},q}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega)\big)_{\theta,p}=\big\{w|_{\Omega}:w\in\mathrm{B}^{s}_{q,p,0}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{n})\text{ and }\operatorname{div}(w)=0\big\}=\mathrm{B}^{s}_{q,p,0,\sigma}(\Omega).

An elementary question of Erdős and Graham
Norbert Hegyvári

Abstract
. Let A k = { r ⁡ ( k − r ) : 1 ≤ r ≤ k − 1 } A_{k}=\{r(k-r):1\leq r\leq k-1\} . Erdős and Graham asked about the cardinality of the set of common elements. We answer this elementary question and apply our result to a sum-product type result. MSC 2020 Primary Secondary Keywords: Erdős question, sum-product type result



1.
Introduction

For an integer
k\geq 2
, let
A_{k}=\{r(k-r):1\leq r\leq k-1\}
.
In 1980 Erdős and Graham posed the following question ([1] and [2]:

Question 1
.

”Let
n,m
be positive integers and consider the two sets
A_{n},A_{m}
. Can one estimate the number of integers common to both? Is this number unbounded? It should certainly be less than
(mn)^{\varepsilon}
for every
\varepsilon>0
if
mn
is sufficiently large.”

In this short note we answer to this question in section 3. In the last section we apply the positive answer to a sum-product result (although this application gives a conditional result). The reason to show this result is that our method uses arithmetical argument and is close to the state-of-art result. The proof of best results are proved using geometrical, and so called ’higher energy’ method, and states for sets of real numbers. Our approach is elementary (and maybe the original question of Erdős and Graham was also related to the sum-product problem).

I think that, because of the ’rigidity’ of the integers, the best lower bound on the range of integers will be orders of magnitude larger than the best lower bound in the real case.

Both of the above questions are answered in the affirmative, in a sharper form.

Let
T_{m,n}=\{(M,N):NM=m^{2}-n^{2};\ M+N<2m;\ 0<M\leq N<M+2n\}
, and write
\tau_{m,n}:=|T_{m,n}|
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
m>n
. Then
|A_{n}\cap A_{m}|\leq\tau_{m,n}
. Furthermore if
m
is even and
n
is odd then equality holds.

Corollary 1.2
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a threshold
n_{0}
such that
m>n>n_{0}
we have

|A_{n}\cap A_{m}|<(m)^{\frac{(2+\varepsilon)\log 2}{\log\log m}}

Theorem 1.3
.

For every
s\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists an infinite sequence
\{n_{1}<m_{1}<n_{1}<n_{2}<m_{2}<\dots\}
such that

|A_{n_{k}}\cap A_{m_{k}}|=s;\quad k=1,2,\dots

Notation

The cardinality of a finite set
X
is denoted by
|X|
.
\mathbb{N}
denotes the set of non-negative integers;
\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,2,\dots\}

d(x)

x

d(x)=\sum_{d|x;x>0}1

3.
Proofs

Proof of Theorem 1.1.

Let
m>n
. We only have to count the number of solutions of
k(n-k)=r(m-r)
in the cases
1\leq k\leq n/2
and
1\leq r\leq m/2
. This equation is equivalent to

(m-2r)^{2}-(n-2k)^{2}=m^{2}-n^{2}
or

(m-2r+n-2k)(m-2r-n+2k)=m^{2}-n^{2}.

Let
M:=m-2r+n-2k
and
N:=m-2r-n+2k
. It is easy to check that
N\geq M>0
. So, for a given factorization
MN=m^{2}-n^{2}
, we get

(1)

r=\frac{2m-N-M}{4};\quad k=\frac{2n+M-N}{4}.

Furthermore we have
N+M=2m-2r<2m
and
N<2n+M
. Hence we conclude if the pair
(k,r)
is a solution of
k(n-k)=r(m-r)
then for some
(M,N)\in T_{m,n}
, (
1
) holds. Furthermore if
(k^{\prime},r^{\prime})
is also a solution then the corresponding pair
(M^{\prime},N^{\prime})
differs from
(M,N)
.

This yields that there is an injection from the set
A_{n}\cap A_{m}
to
T_{m,n}
, hence
|A_{n}\cap A_{m}|\leq|T_{m,n}|=\tau_{m,n}
.

To prove the second part of the theorem let
m=2s;\ n=2p+1
. We have
m^{2}-n^{2}=4(s^{2}-p^{2}-p)-1=4L-1
. Now let us take the pair
(M,N)
, for which
M|4L-1;\ N(4L-1)/M
, and fulfills the condition
M+N<2m;\ 0<M\leq N<M+2n
(provided such a pair exists).

Since
MN\equiv-1\pmod{4}
thus
M+N\equiv 0\pmod{4}
, hence the numbers
k
and
r
in (
1
) are integers.

∎

It is well-known that for
x>x(\varepsilon)

\tau_{m,n}\leq d(m^{2}-n^{2})

Proof of Theorem 1.3.

Let

be a fixed integer.
Write
m=m_{1}

and
p=p_{1}\geq 3
. Pick an

2^{\alpha}>p^{s}

N_{i}=2^{\alpha+1}p^{i};\quad M_{i}=2p^{s-i}\quad 0\leq i\leq s

We are going to show if
i\neq s
then every pair of

satisfies (
3

Taking into account (

) we have to check that
M_{i}+N_{i}<2m
, which in our case is

4.
The Erdős question and the sum-product problem

One suspects that Erdős asked this question in order to get an estimate to the sum-product problem. In 1983 Erdős and Szemerédi gave a non-trivial estimation
\max\{|A+A|,|AA|\}>|A|^{1+c};\ c>0
where
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}
is a finite set.

Surprisingly, Elekes has taken a major step forward with a geometric tool using an incidence theorem. A further ingenious improvement was made by Solymosi (also by a ”geometric aspect”), and finally Rudnev and Stevens have the ’state-of-art’ result where the exponent is
4/3+0,0017\dots-o(1)
(see [5],[6]).

4.1.
Conditional sum-product problem

It is expected that the above theorems are not strong enough to give a sum-product result. Nevertheless, for the sake of completeness, we show a conditional result that can be conclude from the result above.

Statement 4.1
.

Let
0<c<1
and
n>n(c)
be an integer large enough. Let
A
be a set of integers with
n
many elements and assume that
A\subset[1,n^{(\log\log n)^{c}}]
. Then

\max\{|A+A|,|AA|\}\gg n^{4/3-\frac{3}{(\log\log n)^{1-c}}}.

Proof.

Let
\sqcup_{i\geq 1}B_{i}
be a partition of
A\times A
, where
B_{k}:=\{(a,a^{\prime})\in A\times A:a+a^{\prime}=k\}
.

If
|A+A|\geq n^{4/3-\frac{3}{(\log\log n)^{1-c}}}
then we are done. Assume to the contrary. Write
T=|A+A|
, so
T\leq n^{4/3-\frac{3}{(\log\log n)^{1-c}}}
. For
1\leq k\leq T
, let
U_{k}=\{B_{k}:|B_{k}|>0.5n^{2/3-\frac{2}{\log\log n}}\}
. Now

|(A\times A)\setminus\cup_{B_{k}\in U_{k}}B_{k}|<0.5n^{2/3-\frac{2}{\log\log n}}n^{4/3-\frac{2}{\log\log n}}<0.5n^{2}

so
|\cup_{B_{k}\in U_{k}}B_{k}|>05n^{2}
.

Thus we conclude that
m:=|U_{k}|\geq 0.5n
, otherwise

|\cup_{B_{k}\in U_{k}}B_{k}|\leq\sum_{B_{k}\in U_{k}}|B_{k}|\leq\sum_{B_{k}\in U_{k}}n<0.5n^{2}.

For every

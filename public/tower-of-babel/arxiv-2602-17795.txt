Optimality conditions via exact penalty functions
Vsevolod Ivanov Ivanov

Abstract
In this paper, we obtain optimality conditions for the problem with inequality, equality and closed set constraints in terms of the lower Hadamard derivative. The results are obtained applying exact penalty functions. Keywords: local minimum, isolated local minimum, nonsmooth analysis; nonsmooth optimization; generalized convex functions; optimality conditions; lower Hadamard directional derivative; AMS 2000 Mathematics Subject Classification codes: 49J52, 90C46, 26B25, 49K27, 90C26

Introduction

Optimality conditions play important role in optimization.

In this article we obtain optimality conditions of Fritz John type for the
problem with inequality, equality and a set constraints via exact penalty
functions. The results are given in terms of lower Hadamard derivative.
Quadratic penalty functions were originally applied in McShane
[
10
]

for problems with continuously differentiable data. Our results use the
constraint qualification (
1
). Similar assumption appeared in
Demyanov, Di Pillo and Facchinei
[
3
]
and references therein.
Both assumptions coincide in the case when the problem has no inequality
and set constraints.

We cite the following papers where optimality conditions were obtained in
terms of the lower Hadamard derivative or the respective subdifferential.
Optimality conditions are given in Jimenez and Novo
[
6
]
,
Luu and Nguen
[
7
]
.
Sufficient conditions for the problem with a set constraint are given in Penot

[
11
, Proposition 5.7(a)]
, for the problem with inequality constraints
and a set constraint - in Ward
[
13
, Corollary 3.1]
.
We refer to Ioffe
[
8
, Proposition 6]
where ”fuzzy” Lagrange
multiplier rule were obtained in terms of the lower Hadamard subdifferential
under the name Dini subdifferential.
Necessary optimality conditions were derived in Glover, Craven

[
5
, Theorem 2.1]
using the approximate subdifferential which
is constructed with the help of the lower Hadamard derivative.
Another Hadamard directional derivative is used in optimality conditions for
set-valued optimization, but it is constructed there with the help of the
limits of multifunctions in the sense of Painlevé-Kuratowski. This derivative
differs from the usual lower Hadamard derivative in the single-valued scalar case.

Let
\mathbf{R}^{l}
be the
l
dimensional Euclidean space and
S\subset\mathbf{R}^{l}
a set in
\mathbf{R}^{l}
.
In the sequel we use the following notations:

(-\infty,0)^{k}=\underbrace{(-\infty,0)\times(-\infty,0)\times\cdots\times(-\infty,0)}_{k}
,

[0,\infty)^{k}=\underbrace{[0,\infty)\times[0,\infty)\times\cdots\times[0,\infty)}_{k}
.

We consider the following cones.
The Bouligand tangent cone (or the contingent cone)
[
2
]
of the set
S

at the point
x\in\mathrm{cl}\,S
is defined as follows:

\begin{array}[]{c}T(S,x):=\{u\in\mathbf{R}^{l}\mid\exists\{t_{k}\}\subset\mathbf{R}_{>},t_{k}\to+0,\exists\{u_{k}\}\subset\mathbf{R}^{l},\\
u_{k}\to u\textrm{ such that }x+t_{k}u_{k}\in S\textrm{ for all positive integers }k\}.\end{array}

The lower Hadamard conditional derivative
[
3
]
of
f
with respect to
the set
S
at the point
x\in S
in direction
u\in\mathbf{R}^{l}
is defined as follows:

f^{\downarrow}_{H}(x;u;S)=\liminf_{(t,u^{\prime})\to(+0,u),\,x+tu^{\prime}\in S}t^{-1}(f(x+tu^{\prime})-f(x)).

By definition
f^{\downarrow}_{H}(x;u;S)=+\infty
if
u\notin T(S,x)
.
Somewhere it is called the contingent derivative.

Let
\bar{f}:\mathbf{R}^{l}\to\mathbf{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be the extension of
f
such that

\bar{f}(x)=+\infty
for
x\in\mathbf{R}^{l}\setminus S
.
Then the lower conditional Hadamard derivative at the point
x\in S
in direction
u\in\mathbf{R}^{l}

could be defined as follows:

f^{\downarrow}_{H}(x;u;S)=\liminf_{(t,u^{\prime})\to(+0,u)}t^{-1}(\bar{f}(x+tu^{\prime})-f(x)).

If there exists the limit

f^{\prime}_{H}(x;u;S)=\lim_{(t,u^{\prime})\to(+0,u),\,x+tu^{\prime}\in S}t^{-1}(f(x+tu^{\prime})-f(x)),

then the function is called Hadamard differentiable
and
f^{\prime}_{H}(x;u)
- its Hadamard derivative at
x\in S
in direction
u\in\mathbf{R}^{l}

(see, for instance, Demynov, Rubinov
[
4
]
).

If the function
f
attains its local minimum over the
S
at the point

\bar{x}
, then

f^{\downarrow}_{H}(x;u;S)\geq 0\quad\textrm{for all}\quad u\in\mathbf{R}^{l}

.

2
Optimality and exact penalty function

Let

be a closed set and
f:\mathbf{R}^{l}\to\mathbf{R}
,
g_{i}:\mathbf{R}^{l}\to\mathbf{R}

,

\textrm{Minimize }\;f(x)

\textrm{subject to}\quad x\in X,\quad g_{i}(x)\leq 0,\;i=1,2,...,m,\quad h_{j}(x)=0,\;j=1,2,...,q
.
{\rm{(P)}}

Let
\bar{x}
be a local minimizer of (P). Then there exists a number
\delta>0

and a neighbourhood

N_{\delta}(\bar{x}):=\{x\in\mathbf{R}^{l}\mid\|x-\bar{x}\|\leq\delta\}

of
\bar{x}
such that
f(\bar{x})\leq f(x)
for all feasible

x\in N_{\delta}(\bar{x})
.
Denote

\begin{array}[]{rl}h:=h_{1}^{2}+h_{2}^{2}+\cdots+h_{q}^{2},\quad G&:=\{x\in X\mid g_{i}(x)\leq 0,\;i=1,2,...,m\},\\
G_{\delta}&:=G\cap N_{\delta}(\bar{x}).\end{array}

Then
\bar{x}
is a global minimizer of the following problem:

Minimize
f(x)
subject to
x\in G_{\delta}
,
h(x)=0
. (P
1
)

For arbitrary constant
\gamma>0
consider the penalty function defined on

F(x,\gamma):=f(x)+\gamma h(x)+0.5\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2}.

Denote

S:=\{x\in G\mid h(x)=0\}.

We prove under some hypotheses that there exists a positive integer
s

such that each local minimizer of
f(x)
over
S
is a local minimizer of

F(x,\gamma)
over
G
for all
\gamma>s
.
Thus, we reduce the problem with inequality, equality and non-functional constraints to a problem with
inequality and non-functional ones. Then, the following question arises:
Is this reduction of the problem useful or useless? Our approach differs from
the customary practice when penalty functions are used. They usually
reduce the problem to unconstrained one. Taking into account the relation
between the original objective function and the new one, under our hypotheses,
the Lagrange multipliers in the Fritz John conditions in front of the
equality constraints are equal to zero. Thus, we obtain optimality conditions
which express in concrete form the standard Fritz John conditions.

In the next theorem we consider the function

d(x):=\inf_{\|u\|=1,u\in T(G,x)}h^{\downarrow}_{H}(x;u;X)

For every feasible point

.

The following lemma is a particular case of the Strict Separation Theorem. We prove it because the proof is very simple.

Lemma 4.1
.

The following two conditions are equivalent:

(a,b)\notin[-\infty,0]\times[-\infty,0]^{p}

(10)

and

\begin{array}[]{r}\exists(\lambda,\mu)\in[0,+\infty)\times[0,\infty)^{p}:\lambda a+\langle\mu,b\rangle>0,\\
\lambda=0\;\textrm{if}\;a=-\infty,\;\mu_{i}=0\;\textrm{if}\;b_{i}=-\infty.\end{array}

(11)

Proof.

Let (
10
) hold. We prove that condition (
11
) is satisfied. Assume the contrary
that

\lambda a+\langle\mu,b\rangle\leq 0

for all
(\lambda,\mu)\in[0,+\infty)\times[0,\infty)^{p}
. Take
\mu=0
.
Then we have
\lambda a\leq 0
for all
\lambda>0
. Therefore
a\leq 0
.
Using similar arguments we obtain that
b_{i}\leq 0
for all

i=1,2,...,p
.
This result contradicts to the condition (
10
).

The claim is trivially satisfied if among
a
,
b_{i}

(i=1,2,...,p)
,
there are infinities. For all
a
,
b_{i}
equal to
-\infty

we put the respective multipliers equal to 0. Then we remove all these ones equal
to
-\infty
and apply the reasonings given above.

Let (
11
) be satisfied. We prove condition (
10
). Assume the
contrary that

(a,b)\in[-\infty,0]\times[-\infty,0]^{p}.

Therefore

\lambda a+\langle\mu,b\rangle\leq 0\quad\forall\;(\lambda,\mu)\in[0,+\infty)\times[0,\infty)^{p}

which contradicts (
11
).
∎

Denote by
C(\bar{x})
the cone

C(x)=\{u\in T(X,x)\mid(g_{i})^{\prime}_{H}(x;u;X)\leq 0,i\in I(x)\}.

The following regulatity condition is usually called the Abadie constraint qualification when the functions are differentiable:

T(G,\bar{x})=C(\bar{x}).

Theorem 4.3
.

Let the functions
f
,
g
,
h
be Hadamard differentiable and
\bar{x}
be
an isolated local minimimizer of
(P)
. Suppose that the Abadie constraint qualification holds.

there exist

(12)

If

, then
\lambda_{0}=0
.
If
(g_{i})^{\prime}_{H}(\bar{x};u;X)=-\infty
, then
\lambda_{i}=0

(i=1,2,...,p)
.

Proof.

We prove that there does not exist
u\in\mathbf{R}^{l}
such that

F^{\downarrow}_{H}(\bar{x},\gamma;u;X)\leq 0,\quad(g_{i})^{\prime}_{H}(\bar{x};u;X)\leq 0,\;i\in I(\bar{x})

(13)

for all sufficiently large
\gamma>0
.

Assume the contrary. Let

that there exist
sequences

,
u_{k}\to u

k
when

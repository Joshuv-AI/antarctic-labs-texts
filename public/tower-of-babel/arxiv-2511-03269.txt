A decomposition theorem for the Hochschild homology of symmetric powers of a dg category
Ville Nordström

Abstract
We prove a conjecture by Belmans, Fu and Krug concerning the Hochschild homology of the symmetric powers of a small dg category 𝒞 \mathscr{C} . More precisely, we show that these groups decompose into pieces that only depend on the Hochschild homology of the dg category 𝒞 \mathscr{C} .



1
Introduction

In
[
3
]
it was shown (see corollary D) that for any smooth projective variety
X
over a field of characteristic zero we have

\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}HH_{\bullet}([\text{Sym}^{n}(X)])t^{n}\cong\text{Sym}^{\bullet}(\bigoplus_{i\geq 1}HH_{\bullet}(X)t^{i})

where
[\text{Sym}^{n}(X)]
refers to the quotient stack
[X^{n}/S_{n}]
. The notation on the right refers to the graded symmetric algerba, where the grading on
\bigoplus_{i\geq 1}HH_{\bullet}(X)t^{i}
is the usual grading on Hochschild homology and the formal variable
t
is of degree
0
.
The authors then formulate the more general conjecture (see conjecture 3.24 in
[
3
]
) that for any smooth proper dg category
\mathscr{C}
we have an isomorphism

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}HH_{\bullet}(\text{Sym}^{n}(\mathscr{C}))t^{n}\cong\text{Sym}^{\bullet}(\bigoplus_{i\geq 1}HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C})t^{i})

(1)

where
\text{Sym}^{n}(\mathscr{C})
denotes the equivariant dg category associated to the natural
S_{n}
action on the pretriangulated hull of
\mathscr{C}^{\otimes n}
.
In this paper we show that in fact, there is an isomorphism as above for any small dg category
\mathscr{C}
, and moreover this isomorphism is natural in
\mathscr{C}
. The proof of this has three steps. First, we have the following decomposition

HH_{\bullet}(\text{Sym}^{n}(\mathscr{C}))\cong\oplus_{[\sigma]\in S_{n}/\sim}HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C}^{\otimes n},\sigma)^{C(\sigma)}

where
S_{n}/\sim
means conjugacy classes (see
[
10
]
). After identifying conjugacy classes in
S_{n}
with partitions
n=\lambda_{1}+...+\lambda_{k}
we can choose the permutations to be of the form

\sigma=(1\quad 2\quad\cdots\quad\lambda_{1})(\lambda_{1}+1\quad\cdots\quad\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})\cdots(\lambda_{1}+...+\lambda_{k-1}+1\quad\cdots\quad n).

The second step is then a version of the Künneth isomorphism

HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C}^{\otimes n},\sigma)\cong HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C}^{\otimes\lambda_{1}},\sigma_{\lambda_{1}})\otimes...\otimes HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C}^{\otimes\lambda_{k}},\sigma_{\lambda_{k}})

where
\sigma_{\lambda_{i}}
is a generator for the cyclic group of order
\lambda_{i}
. The final step is the computation that
HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C}^{\otimes i},\sigma_{i})\cong HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C})
for any
i
(see proposition
16
) which implies that the above can be written as

HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C}^{\otimes n},\sigma)\cong HH^{\bullet}(\mathscr{C}^{\otimes\lambda_{1}},\sigma_{\lambda_{1}})\otimes...\otimes HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C}^{\otimes\lambda_{k}},\sigma_{\lambda_{k}})\cong HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C})^{\otimes k}.

After taking invariants with respect to the
C(\sigma)-
action we obtain a summand of

\text{Sym}^{\bullet}(\oplus_{i\geq 1}HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C})t^{i})
. The proof then reduces to checking that when we vary
n
and
\sigma\in S_{n}
we get all of
\text{Sym}^{\bullet}(\oplus_{i\geq 1}HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C})t^{i})
.

After writing this paper we found out that Anno, Baranovsky and Logvinenko have also proved this conjecture. They provide explicit maps between standard complexes and show that they are homotopy equivalences. The formulas for the maps and the summary of the results appeared in
[
7
]
and the proofs will appear soon in
[
1
]
, along with the description of the Hopf algebra structure induced on the decomposition.

Gyenge, Koppensteiner and Logvinenko have previously also studied a similar decomposition from a
K
-theoretic perspective, see
[
6
]
theorem C.

1.3
Conventions

We work over a fixed field
k
of characteristic zero.

Our conventions on matrices are that they act on the left. So whenever the source and target of some morphism are decomposed as direct sums, that morphism can be described as a matrix whose columns are indexed by the summands of the source and the rows are indexed by the summands of the target.

If
V
is a graded vector space the free graded commutative algebra on
V
can be described either as a subalgebra of the tensor algebra
T^{\bullet}(V)
or as a quotient of it. To distinguish between these we will use
\text{Sym}^{\bullet}(V)
to denote the subalgebra of
T^{\bullet}(V)
and
S^{\bullet}(V)
for the quotient algebra.

1.4
Warm up computation

The notation in the computation
HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C}^{\otimes i},\sigma_{i})\cong HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C})
(which is proposition
16
) gets a little complicated when working with categories and it becomes hard to see the actual reason why it works. Therefore, as a warm up, we prove this fact here in the special case that
\mathscr{C}=A
is an associative algebra and
i=2
.

So, let
A
be an associative algebra. We write
A^{e}:=A\otimes A^{op}
and will use lower case letters
f,g,..
for elements in
A
and primed letters
f^{\prime},g^{\prime}
for elements in
A^{op}
. Let
A_{2}=A^{\otimes 2}
and consider the ring automorphism
\sigma:A_{2}\to A_{2}
,
(a,b)\mapsto(b,a)
. Write
A_{2}^{e}=A_{2}\otimes A_{2}^{op}
. Then
A_{2}
is naturally a left
A_{2}^{e}
module via
(f_{1},f_{2},f_{1}^{\prime},f_{2}^{\prime})\cdot(a,b):=(f_{1}af_{1}^{\prime},f_{2}bf_{2}^{\prime})
. We have the following free resolution of the left
A_{2}^{e}
-module
A_{2}

Bar(A_{2}):=[...\to A_{2}^{\otimes 3}\to A_{2}^{\otimes 2}]\to A_{2}.

Let
{}^{\sigma}A_{2}
be the right
A_{2}^{e}
-module whose underlying vector space is
A_{2}
and whose module structure is given by

(a,b)\cdot(f_{1},f_{2},f_{1}^{\prime},f_{2}^{\prime}):=(f_{2}^{\prime}af_{1},f_{1}^{\prime}bf_{2})
. We have four ring maps
A^{e}\to A_{2}^{e}
defined by

e_{11}:(f,g^{\prime})\mapsto(f,1,g^{\prime},1^{\prime}),e_{12}:(f,g^{\prime})\mapsto(f,1,1^{\prime},g^{\prime}),

e_{21}:(f,g^{\prime})\mapsto(1,f,g^{\prime},1^{\prime}),e_{22}:(f,g^{\prime})\mapsto(1,f,1^{\prime},g^{\prime}).

If
M
is a left
A_{2}^{e}
module and
N
is a right
A^{e}
module we write
N\otimes_{A^{e}_{21}}M:=N\otimes_{A^{e}}e_{21}^{*}(M)
.
In other words
N\otimes_{A^{e}_{21}}M
is the cokernel of the map
N\otimes(A\otimes A^{op})\otimes M\to N\otimes M
,

(n,f\otimes g^{\prime},m)\mapsto(n(f\otimes g^{\prime}),m)-(n,(1\otimes f\otimes g^{\prime}\otimes 1^{\prime})m).

Note that since elements in the image of
e_{21}
commute with elements in the image of
e_{12}
this tensor product inherits a
A^{e}
-module structure via
e_{12}
.

Lemma 1
.

For any left
A_{2}^{e}

M
we have an isomorphism

{}^{\sigma}A_{2}\otimes_{A_{2}^{e}}M\cong A\otimes_{A^{e}_{12}}(A\otimes_{A^{e}_{21}}M).

Consider the diagram

\pi
and
p_{2}p_{1}
are the same. Suppose

x\in\ker(\pi)
. Then

can be written as a sum of elements of the form

\delta((a,b)\otimes(f_{1},f_{2},f_{1}^{\prime},f_{2}^{\prime})\otimes m):=(f_{2}^{\prime}af_{1},f_{1}^{\prime}bf_{2})\otimes m-(a,b)\otimes(f_{1}\otimes f_{2})m(f_{1}^{\prime}\otimes f_{2}^{\prime}).

p_{2}p_{1}

which is then in the kernel of

Let

denote a lift of
\bar{x}
which is the sum of elements of the form

f^{\prime}af\otimes(b\otimes m)-a\otimes(b\otimes(f\otimes 1)m(1\otimes f^{\prime})).

x^{\prime}
. Also

so

. By the lemma this is isomorphic to what we get by first applying
A\otimes_{A^{e}_{21}}(-)
and then applying

. But here is the key observation:

. (For

Proposition 2
.

Let

be any associative algebra. Then

.

2
Dg categories

We recall some basic facts about dg categories. Nothing here is new but some proofs are included for convenience.

2.1
Dg categories and dg functors

A
dg category

\mathscr{C}
is a category enriched in
\mathbb{Z}
-graded chain complexes. Here are some basic examples which we will encounter a lot. More interesting examples will be treated later.

Example 3
.

We denote by
\text{com}(k)
the category whose objects are
\mathbb{Z}
-graded chain complexes over
k
and whose homomorphism complexes are defined as

\text{Hom}_{\text{com}(k)}(V,W)^{i}=\prod_{j\in\Gamma}\text{Hom}_{k}(V^{j},W^{j+i})

and the differentials are given by

\partial(f)=d_{W}\circ f-(-1)^{|f|}f\circ d_{V}.

Example 4
.

A
\mathbb{Z}
-graded dg algebra can be thought of as a dg category with one object.

Given a dg category
\mathscr{C}
we can associate to it four new categories
Z^{0}(\mathscr{C})
,
H^{0}(\mathscr{C})
,
H^{\bullet}(\mathscr{C})
and
\mathscr{C}^{\#}
where the last two are graded category. The objects of each of these are the same as the objects of
\mathscr{C}
. The homomorphism spaces are obtained from those in
\mathscr{C}
by taking degree zero cycles, taking zero’th homology, taking the total homology and forgetting the differentials respectively.

Let
\mathscr{C}
and
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
be dg categories. A
dg functor

F:\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
is a functor between categories enriched in chain complexes. A dg functor
F:\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
induces functors
Z^{0}(F):Z^{0}(\mathscr{C})\to Z^{0}(\mathscr{C}^{\prime}),

H^{0}(F):H^{0}(\mathscr{C})\to H^{0}(\mathscr{C}^{\prime})
,
H^{\bullet}(F):H^{\bullet}(\mathscr{C})\to H^{\bullet}(\mathscr{C}^{\prime})
,
F^{\#}:\mathscr{C}^{\#}\to\mathscr{C}^{\prime\#}
. We say that
F
is a
quasi-equivalence
if
H^{\bullet}(F)
is fully faithful and
H^{0}(F)
is essentially surjective.

The collection of dg functors from
\mathscr{C}
to
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
form the objects of a new dg category which we will denote
\mathsf{Func}_{dg}(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{C}^{\prime})
. Given two dg functors
F,G:\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
, a
pre-natural transformation

\alpha:F\to G
of degree
i
consists of a degree
i
morphism
\alpha_{x}\in\mathscr{C}^{\prime}(F(x),G(x))^{i}
for every
x\in\mathsf{Ob}(\mathscr{C})
satisfying the usual naturality conditions. The homomorphism complex
\text{Hom}_{\mathsf{Func}_{dg}}(F,G)
is the graded vector space whose degree
i
part consists of the pre-natural transformations of degree
i
. The differential is given pointwise:
\partial(\alpha)_{x}=\partial(\alpha_{x})
where on the right
\partial
refers to the differential in the complex
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}(F(x),G(x))
. The
natural transformations
are the degree zero cycles in
\text{Hom}_{\mathsf{Func}_{dg}}(F,G)
. A natural transformation is called a natural isomorphism if
\alpha_{x}
is an isomorphism in
Z^{0}(\mathscr{C}^{\prime})
for all
x\in\mathsf{Ob}(\mathscr{C})
.

Finally we observe that if
\mathscr{C}
is a dg category, then there is a dg category
\mathscr{C}^{op}
with
\mathsf{Ob}(\mathscr{C}^{op})=\mathsf{Ob}(\mathscr{C})
and
\mathscr{C}^{op}(c,c^{\prime})=\mathscr{C}(c^{\prime},c)
. Composition is defined by
f\circ^{op}g:=(-1)^{|f||g|}g\circ f
.

2.2
Dg modules

We let
mod^{dg}-\mathscr{C}
be the dg category
\mathsf{Func}_{dg}(\mathscr{C}^{op},\text{com}(k))
and we refer to its objects as
right
\mathscr{C}
-modules
. Left modules are defined as the objects of
\mathsf{Func}_{dg}(\mathscr{C},\text{com}(k))
. To simplify notation we will denote the homomorphism complexes between two right (or left) modules simply by
\text{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}}(M,N)
.

We note here that the category
Z^{0}(mod-\mathscr{C})
is abelian and kernels and cokernels are computed pointwise in the abelian category
Z^{0}(\text{com}(k))
. By a submodule
M\subset N
we mean a monomorphism
M\to N
in
Z^{0}(mod-\mathscr{C})
.

Any module
M\in mod-\mathscr{C}
gives rise in a natural way to an underlying graded module
M^{\#}:\mathscr{C}^{\#}\to\text{grVect}
.

There is a dg functor
Y:\mathscr{C}\to mod^{dg}-\mathscr{C}
which we refer to as the dg-Yoneda embedding. The dg version of the usual Yoneda lemma says that there is an isomorphism of chain complexes

\text{Hom}_{\mathsf{Func}_{dg}}(Y(x),F)\cong F(x).

For
x\in\mathscr{C}
let
h^{x}:=Y(x)
. A
free module
is one of the form
\bigoplus_{i\in I}h^{x_{i}}\otimes V_{i}
where
V_{i}
are chain complexes of vector spaces. If
I
is finite and each
V_{i}
is a finite dimensional chain complex then we say that the module is
finitely generated free
. We say that a module
M
is
semi-free
if it admits a filtration

0=F_{0}\subset F_{1}\subset F_{2}\subset...\subset M

such that for all
i\geq 1
the quotients
F_{i}/F_{i-1}
are free and
M=\cup_{i\geq 0}F_{i}
.

Similarly there is a contravariant Yoneda embedding
Y^{\prime}:\mathscr{C}^{op}\to\mathscr{C}-mod
. We denote by
h_{x}:=Y^{\prime}(x)
. Now free modules and semi-free modules are defined analogously to the case of right modules.

For any dg category
\mathscr{C}
the category
H^{0}(mod^{dg}-\mathscr{C})
can be given the structure of a triangulated category. Shifts are defined pointwise: given a right module
M
we define
M[1](x):=M(x)[1]
. Cones of morphisms are also constructed pointwise: if
\alpha:M\to M^{\prime}
is a natural transformation we set
Cone(\alpha)(x):=Cone(\alpha_{x})
. The module
Cone(\alpha)
fits naturally into a diagram in
Z^{0}(\mathscr{C})

M^{\prime}\to Cone(\alpha)\to M[1]\to M^{\prime}[1].

The distinguished triangles in
H^{0}(mod^{dg}-\mathscr{C})
are those which are isomorphic to a sequence of the above form.

A right
\mathscr{C}
-module
M
is called acyclic if it is acyclic pointwise. A natural transformation
\alpha:M\to N
is quasi isomorphism if it is so pointwise. Equivalently,
\alpha
is a quasi isomorphism if and only if
Cone(\alpha)
is acyclic.

A module
P
is called
h-projective
if
\text{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}}(P,N)
is acyclic for any acyclic module
N
.

Proposition 5
.

(i) If
\mathscr{C}
is a small dg category then any right (or left)
\mathscr{C}
-module
M
admits a quasi isomorphism from a semi-free module.

(ii) Any semi-free module is h-projective.

Proof.

This is theorem 3.1 in
[
8
]
.
∎

If
\mathscr{C}
and
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
are two dg categories we define their tensor product
\mathscr{C}\otimes\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
to be the dg category whose objects are pairs
(c,c^{\prime})
with
c\in\mathsf{Ob}(\mathscr{C})
and
c^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Ob}(\mathscr{C}^{\prime})
. The homomorphism complexes are defined as

(\mathscr{C}\otimes\mathscr{C}^{\prime})((c_{1},c_{1}^{\prime}),(c_{2},c_{2}^{\prime})):=\mathscr{C}(c_{1},c_{2})\otimes\mathscr{C}^{\prime}(c_{1}^{\prime},c_{2}^{\prime}).

The dg category of
\mathscr{C}-\mathscr{C}^{\prime}

bimodules
is by definition the dg category
\mathscr{C}\otimes\mathscr{C}^{\prime op}-mod
(which is the same as
mod-\mathscr{C}^{op}\otimes\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
) and its objects will be referred to as
\mathscr{C}-\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
-
bimodules
.

Now suppose that
\mathscr{C},\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
and
\mathscr{C}^{\prime\prime}
are small dg categories. If
M
is a
\mathscr{C}-\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
-bimodule and
N
is a
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}-\mathscr{C}^{\prime\prime}
-bimodule we define their
tensor product

M\otimes_{\mathscr{C}^{\prime}}N
to be the
\mathscr{C}-\mathscr{C}^{\prime\prime}
bimodule which assigns to a pair
(c,c^{\prime\prime})
the chain complex which is the cokernel of the following map:

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{c_{1}^{\prime},c_{2}^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Ob}(\mathscr{C}^{\prime})}M(c,c_{2}^{\prime})\otimes\mathscr{C}^{\prime}(c_{1}^{\prime},c_{2}^{\prime})\otimes N(c_{1}^{\prime},c^{\prime\prime})\to

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{c^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Ob}(\mathscr{C}^{\prime})}M(c,c^{\prime})\otimes N(c^{\prime},c^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle m\otimes f\otimes n\mapsto

\displaystyle mf\otimes n-m\otimes fn

Note that in the special case that
\mathscr{C}=\mathscr{C}^{\prime\prime}=k
the tensor product
M\otimes_{\mathscr{C}^{\prime}}N
is a
k-k
-bimodule, i.e. just a chain complex.
We say that a
\mathscr{C}-\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
bimodule
M
is
free as
\mathscr{C}
-module
if for each
c^{\prime}\in\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
we have
M(-,c^{\prime})
is free. We say that it is
free as a
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
module if for all
c\in\mathscr{C}

M(c,-)
is free. Similarly, we say that
M
is
semi-free as a left
\mathscr{C}
-module
if
M(-,c^{\prime})
is semi-free for all
c^{\prime}\in\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
and we say that it is
semi-free as a right
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
-module
if for all
c\in\mathscr{C}

M(c,-)
is semi-free.

.

Let
M\overset{\sim}{\to}N

\mathscr{C}-\mathscr{C}^{\prime}

M
and
N

\mathscr{C}

\mathscr{C}^{\prime}=k

If
F:\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{D}
is a dg functor we get induced functors
F^{*}:mod-\mathscr{D}\to mod-\mathscr{C}
and
F_{*}:mod-\mathscr{C}\to mod-\mathscr{D}
(and similarly for left modules). The functor
F_{*}
is defined by

F_{*}(M)(d)=M\otimes_{\mathscr{C}}F^{*}(\mathscr{D}(d,-)).

Proposition 7
.

Let
M\in mod-\mathscr{C}
and
N\in\mathscr{C}-mod
. If
M=h^{c}
is free then
M\otimes_{\mathscr{C}}N\cong N(c)
and if
N=h_{c}
then
M\otimes_{\mathscr{C}}N\cong M(c)
.

Proof.

Define a map

\bigoplus_{x\in\mathscr{C}}\mathscr{C}(x,c)\otimes N(x)\to N(c)

by
f\otimes n\mapsto fn
where
fn
is short hand for
N(f)(n)
. This map descends to a map
h^{c}\otimes_{\mathscr{C}}N\to N(c)
which is an isomorphism with inverse given by
N(c)\ni n\mapsto[\mathrm{id}_{c}\otimes n]
. The case when
N=h_{c}
is identical.

∎

2.3
Pre-triangulated dg categories

Given a morphism
\alpha:x\to y
in
Z^{0}(\mathscr{C})
the cone of
\alpha
refers to any object
z\in\mathscr{C}
such that
h^{z}\cong C(Y(\alpha))
in
Z^{0}(mod-\mathscr{C})
. If the cone of
\alpha
exists it is unique up to isomorphism. Similarly for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
x\in\mathscr{C}
we say that
z
is the shift of
x
and we write
z=x[n]
if
h^{z}\cong h^{x}[n]
in
mod-\mathscr{C}
. We say that the dg category
\mathscr{C}
is
pre-triangulated
if it has all shifts and all cones. If
\mathscr{C}
is any dg category one can form a new dg category
\widehat{\mathscr{C}}
by adding formally all shifts and cones. The category
\widehat{\mathscr{C}}
is called the pretriangulated hull of
\mathscr{C}
and it comes with a fully faithful dg functor
\mathscr{C}\to\widehat{\mathscr{C}}
which is universal among functors from
\mathscr{C}
to pretriangulated dg categories. See for example
[
keller3
]
section 4.5. for further details.

2.4
Finite group actions on dg categories

We will be interested in symmetric powers of dg categories. To define this we first recall the more general notion of finite group actions on dg categories and equivariant objects. When talking about an action of a finite group
G
on a dg category
\mathscr{C}
there are different levels of strictness that one can require. The following definition suffices for the purpose of this paper.

Definition 8
.

An action of
G
on
\mathscr{C}
is the data of an autoequivalence
\rho_{g}:\mathscr{C}\overset{\sim}{\to}\mathscr{C}
for every
g\in G
, natural isomorphisms
\theta_{g,g^{\prime}}:\rho_{g}\circ\rho_{g^{\prime}}\overset{\sim}{\implies}\rho_{g^{\prime}g}
and
\eta:\rho_{e}\overset{\sim}{\implies}\mathrm{id}_{\mathscr{C}}
and these should satisfy the following two conditions.

i) For each
g\in G
and
c\in\mathscr{C}
we have

(\theta_{e,g})_{c}=\eta_{\rho_{g}(c)}:\rho_{e}\rho_{g}(c)\to\rho_{g}(c)

and

(\theta_{g,e})_{c}=\rho_{g}(\eta_{c}):\rho_{g}\rho_{e}(c)\to\rho_{g}(c).

ii) For all
g,h,k\in G
we have commutative diagrams

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{g}\rho_{kh}(c)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{hg}\rho_{k}(c)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{khg}(c)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{g}((\theta_{h,k})_{c})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\theta_{g,h})_{\rho_{k}(c)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\theta_{g,kh})_{c}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\theta_{hg,k})_{c}}

Example 9

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a dg category. Then the symmetric group

acts on the tensor power

,
\eta=\mathrm{id}

Whenever we have a group action as above we can consider the category of

2.5
Hochschild homology

Given a dg category
\mathscr{C}
we have the left and right
diagonal modules
over
\mathscr{C}^{e}:=\mathscr{C}\otimes\mathscr{C}^{op}
which we denote by
\Delta^{l}_{\mathscr{C}}
and
\Delta^{r}_{\mathscr{C}}
. They are defined by

\Delta^{l}_{\mathscr{C}}(c_{1},c_{2}):=\mathscr{C}(c_{2},c_{1})
and
\Delta^{r}_{\mathscr{C}}(c_{1},c_{2}):=\mathscr{C}(c_{1},c_{2})
. For any
\mathscr{C}\otimes\mathscr{C}
bimodule
M
we define the
Hochschild homology
of
\mathscr{C}
with values in
M
as the derived tensor product
HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C},M):=M\overset{L}{\otimes_{\mathscr{C}^{e}}}\Delta^{l}_{\mathscr{C}}
. This derived tensor product can be computed by replacing
\Delta_{\mathscr{C}}^{l}
by a semi-free (or more generally an h-projective) left
\mathscr{C}\otimes\mathscr{C}^{op}
module
P
and then taking the usual tensor product
M\otimes_{\mathscr{C}^{e}}P
.

We now describe a general method to replace a
\mathscr{C}
-module by a semi-free module. First, a
dg-resolution
of
M
is a sequence in
Z^{0}(\mathscr{C}-mod)

...\to F^{-2}\to F^{-1}\to F^{0}\to M\to 0

such that for all
c\in\mathscr{C}
the following three sequences are exact

\displaystyle 1)

\displaystyle...\to F^{-2}(c)\to F^{-1}(c)\to F^{0}(c)\to M(c)\to 0

\displaystyle 2)

\displaystyle...\to Z^{\bullet}(F^{-2}(c))\to Z^{\bullet}(F^{-1}(c))\to Z^{\bullet}(F^{0}(c))\to Z^{\bullet}(M(c))\to 0

\displaystyle 3)

\displaystyle...\to H^{\bullet}(F^{-2}(c))\to H^{\bullet}(F^{-1}(c))\to H^{\bullet}(F^{0}(c))\to H^{\bullet}(M(c))\to 0.

These three sequences being exact imply that the map
F^{0}\to M
induces a quasi isomorphism
Tot^{\oplus}(F^{\bullet})\overset{\sim}{\to}M
. Moreover, if all the
F^{i}
’s were free modules then
Tot^{\oplus}(F^{\bullet})
will be semi-free. Any module
M
admits a free resolution as above (see for example section 2.1 in
[
12
]
).

We will only need the following standard resolution of the diagonal bimodule
\Delta_{\mathscr{C}}^{l}
.
First we define

\text{Bar}^{n}(\mathscr{C}):=\Big((x,y)\mapsto\bigoplus_{c_{0},...,c_{n}\in\mathscr{C}}\mathscr{C}(c_{0},x)\otimes\mathscr{C}(c_{1},c_{0})\otimes...\otimes\mathscr{C}(y,c_{n})\Big)

for
n\geq 1
.
For each
n\geq 1
we have the map of left
\mathscr{C}\otimes\mathscr{C}^{op}
modules
d^{n}:\text{Bar}^{n}(\mathscr{C})\to\text{Bar}^{n-1}(\mathscr{C})
defined by

f_{0}|f_{1}|\cdots|f_{n+1}\mapsto\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}(-1)^{i}f_{0}|\cdots|f_{i}f_{i+1}|\cdots|f_{n+1}

and we have the map
d^{0}:\text{Bar}^{0}(\mathscr{C})\to\Delta_{\mathscr{C}}^{l}
defined by
f_{0}|f_{1}\mapsto f_{0}f_{1}
. We set
\text{Bar}(\mathscr{C}):=Tot^{\oplus}(\text{Bar}^{\bullet}(\mathscr{C}))
. The complex

\displaystyle...\to\text{Bar}^{2}(\mathscr{C})\to\text{Bar}^{1}(\mathscr{C})\to\text{Bar}^{0}(\mathscr{C})\to\Delta_{\mathscr{C}}^{l}

(2)

is pointwise contractible via the contracting homotopy

f_{0}|\cdots|f_{n+1}\mapsto\mathrm{id}_{x}|f_{0}|\cdots|f_{n+1}.

Because of this it follows that the sequence (
2
) is in fact a dg-resolution of
\Delta_{\mathscr{C}}^{l}
. More over each
\text{Bar}^{n}(\mathscr{C})
is free as a
\mathscr{C}\otimes\mathscr{C}^{op}
-module.
Let us define
\text{Bar}(\mathscr{C}):=Tot^{\oplus}(Bar^{\bullet}(\mathscr{C}))
which is a semi-free replacement of
\Delta_{\mathscr{C}}^{l}
.

If we use this particular semi-free resolution of
\Delta_{\mathscr{C}}^{l}
to compute the derived tensor product we obtain the
standard complex
which is a particular representative of the object
HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C},M)
in
D(k)
. Below we describe the standard complex in more detail.

Definition 11
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a small dg category and let
M
be a
\mathscr{C}-\mathscr{C}
-bimodule. Set

C_{n}(\mathscr{C},M):=\bigoplus_{c_{0},...,c_{n}\in\mathscr{C}}M(c_{1},c_{0})\otimes\mathscr{C}(c_{2},c_{1})\otimes...\otimes\mathscr{C}(c_{0},c_{n}).

Note that each summand above is a chain complex whose differential, denoted
d_{1}
, is obtained from the differentials in
\mathscr{C}
and the differential on the bimodule
M
.
We will use the bar notation for elements in the standard complex; to shorten notation we will denote a pure tensor

a_{0}\otimes a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n}\in C_{n}(\mathscr{C},F)

by
a_{0}[a_{1}|\cdots|a_{n}]
.
There is a second differential
d_{2}:C_{n}(\mathscr{C},F)\to C_{n-1}(\mathscr{C},F)
defined by

\displaystyle d_{2}\big(m[a_{1}|\cdots|a_{n}]\big)

\displaystyle=ma_{1}[a_{2}|\cdots|a_{n}]

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{i}m[\cdots|a_{i}a_{i+1}|\cdots]

\displaystyle+(-1)^{n+|a_{n}|(|a_{0}|+...+|a_{n-1}|)}a_{n}m[a_{1}|\cdots|a_{n-1}].

The differentials
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
commute and therefore we get a total complex (cohomologically graded) by altering the sign of
d_{1}
by
(-1)^{n}
on
C_{n}(\mathscr{C},M)
whose
k^{\prime}
th term for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
is

\bigoplus_{n-m=k}C_{m}(\mathscr{C},M)^{n}.

Remark 12
.

Different sign conventions appear for the Hochschild differential but they all give rise to isomorphic chain complexes and so we use this one as it is the least complicated. This sign convention appears for example in
[
13
]
.

Hochschild homology with coefficients in an endofunctor (or bimodule more generally) have nice functorial properties as is explained in
[
11
]
. We will use the notation from
[
10
]
where this kind of functoriality is only described in terms of standard complexes.
Let
dg-cat^{+}
denote the category whose objects are pairs
(\mathscr{C},F)
where
\mathscr{C}
is a small dg category and
F:\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{C}
is an endo-functor and whose morphisms
(\mathscr{C},F)\to(\mathscr{C}^{\prime},F^{\prime})
consist of pairs
(\phi,\alpha)
where
\phi:\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
is a dg functor and
\alpha:\phi F\implies F^{\prime}\phi
is a natural transformation. Composition is defined by

(\phi_{1},\alpha_{1})\circ(\phi_{2},\alpha_{2}):=(\phi_{1}\phi_{2},\alpha_{1}\star\alpha_{2})

where
\alpha_{1}\star\alpha_{2}:=(\alpha_{1})_{\phi_{2}}\phi_{1}(\alpha_{2})
. A pair
(\phi,\alpha)
as above gives rise to a chain map between standard complexes
(\phi,\alpha)_{*}:C_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C},F)\to C_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C}^{\prime},F^{\prime})
and this makes
C_{\bullet}(-)
and consequently
HH_{\bullet}(-)
into functors
dg-cat^{+}\to D(k)
.

Lemma 13
.

Let
F:\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{C}
be an endofunctor on a small dg category. Then
(F,\mathrm{id})_{*}:HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C},F)\to HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C},F)
is the identity.

Proof.

Consider the homotopy
H:C_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C},F)\to C_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C},F)
defined by

\displaystyle a_{0}[a_{1}|\cdots|a_{k}]\mapsto

\displaystyle[a_{0}|\cdots|a_{k}]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(-1)^{k+|a_{k}|(|a_{0}|+...+|a_{k-1}|)}[F(a_{k})|a_{0}|\cdots|a_{k-1}]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(-1)^{2k+(|a_{k-1}|+|a_{k}|)(|a_{0}|+...+|a_{k-2}|)}[F(a_{k-1})|F(a_{k})|a_{0}|\cdots|a_{k-2}]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle...+(-1)^{k^{2}+(|a_{1}|+...+|a_{k}|)|a_{0}|}[F(a_{1})|\cdots|F(a_{k})|a_{0}].

This satisfies
\bar{d}_{1}H+H\bar{d}_{1}=0
and
d_{2}H+Hd_{2}=\mathbf{1}-(F,\mathrm{id})_{*}
.

∎

Given two dg categories
\mathscr{C}
and
\mathscr{B}
with bimodules
M
and
N
we define

Sh:C_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C},M)\otimes C_{\bullet}(\mathscr{B},N)\to C_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C}\otimes\mathscr{B},M\otimes N)

by the formula

\displaystyle m[f_{1}|\cdots|f_{k}]\otimes n[g_{1}|\cdots|g_{l}]\mapsto\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in(k,l)-shuffles}(-1)^{|\sigma|+\epsilon}m\otimes n[\cdots|f_{i}\otimes 1|\cdots|1\otimes g_{j}|\cdots|f_{i+1}\otimes 1|\cdots|1\otimes g_{j}|\cdots].

The sign in the formula above is given by

where the sum is over all pairs

,

and

, such that

appears after

(where

and

).
An important tool when working with Hochschild homology is the Künneth isomorphism.

.

The map

Proof.

This is well known and a proof of a more general statement can be found in
[
9
]
theorem VIII.8.1. We also included a proof of this, but for Hochschild homology without coefficients, in
[
10
]
and the same argument goes through with coefficients.

On the right here, the action of

(\delta_{h^{\prime}g=h}\theta^{-1}_{h^{\prime},g})_{hh^{\prime}}

Now

\displaystyle p_{1}\cdots

\displaystyle p_{n}((g_{2}^{\prime}\alpha_{1}f_{1},g_{3}^{\prime}\alpha_{2}f_{2},...,g_{1}^{\prime}\alpha_{n}f_{n})\otimes m)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{1}\cdots p_{n-1}((g_{2}^{\prime}\alpha_{1}f_{1},g_{3}^{\prime}\alpha_{2}f_{2},...,\alpha_{n})\otimes(1\otimes...\otimes 1\otimes f_{n})m(g_{1}^{\prime}\otimes 1\otimes...\otimes 1))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle...

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},...,\alpha_{n})\otimes(f_{1}\otimes...\otimes f_{n})m(g_{1}^{\prime}\otimes...\otimes g_{n}^{\prime})

Conversely, suppose

. Therefore

can be written as a sum of elements of the form

where we use brackets to indicate that we are in the source of

where elements already are equivalence classes. We can lift

to an element

which is a sum of terms of the form

The proof of the isomorphism in part

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi\otimes\phi\otimes...\otimes\mathrm{id}_{M}}

Now before we state and prove our main theorem we should explain the notation in the isomorphism (
1

\mathscr{C}
we denote by

\widehat{\mathscr{C}^{\otimes n}}^{S_{n}}

\widehat{\mathscr{C}^{\otimes n}}

are said to be in

and

. When we write

\text{Sym}^{\bullet}(V)

or as a subalgebra of
T^{\bullet}(V)
. To distinguish between these we use
S^{\bullet}(V)
for the quotient and
\text{Sym}^{\bullet}(V)
for the subalgebra.

For a moment, let
H:=HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C})
. For each
n\geq 0
the following subspace of
T^{\bullet}(V)
maps isomorphically onto the
t
-degree
n

:

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{a_{1},...,a_{n}}\text{Sym}^{a_{1}}(H)t^{a_{1}}\otimes\text{Sym}^{a_{2}}(H)t^{2a_{2}}\otimes...\otimes\text{Sym}^{a_{n}}(H)t^{na_{n}}

(4)

Theorem 17
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a small dg category. Then we have a natural isomorphism of graded vector spaces

\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}HH_{\bullet}(\text{Sym}^{n}(\mathscr{C}))t^{n}\cong S^{\bullet}(\oplus_{i\geq 1}HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C})t^{i}).

Proof.

First we make the identifications

\displaystyle HH_{\bullet}(\text{Sym}^{n}(\mathscr{C}))t^{n}\cong\bigoplus_{\lambda\vdash n}HH_{\bullet}(\widehat{\mathscr{C}^{n}},\sigma_{\lambda})^{C(\sigma_{\lambda})}t^{n}\cong\bigoplus_{\lambda\vdash n}HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C}^{n},\sigma_{\lambda})^{C(\sigma_{\lambda})}t^{n}

(5)

where the first isomorphism is the main theorem in
[
10
]
and the second is induced by the inclusions
\mathscr{C}^{n}\to\widehat{\mathscr{C}^{n}}
. We can describe the partition
\lambda
either as

with

or as
n=1a_{1}+2a_{2}+...+na_{n}
where
a_{1},...,a_{n}
are non-negative integers.
We can write
C(\sigma_{\lambda})=C\rtimes S
where
C=C(\lambda)=C_{2}^{\times a_{2}}\times C_{3}^{\times a_{3}}\times...\times C_{n}^{\times a_{n}}
is a product of cyclic groups and

is a product of symmetric groups. For any
l

\sigma_{l}=(1\ 2\ \cdots\ l)
denote the cyclic permutation on
l
ordered elements.
Then, by the Künneth isomorphism and the proposition above, we have

\displaystyle HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C}^{n},\sigma_{\lambda})t^{n}\cong

\displaystyle\big(HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C}^{\otimes 1},\sigma_{1})t\big)^{\otimes a_{1}}\otimes...\otimes\big(HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C}^{\otimes n},\sigma_{a_{n}})t^{n}\big)^{\otimes a_{n}}

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\big(HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C})t\big)^{\otimes a_{1}}\otimes...\otimes\big(HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C})t^{n}\big)^{\otimes a_{n}}.

Note that by lemma
13

C\subset C\rtimes S
acts trivially, so after taking invariants we get

\displaystyle HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C}^{n},\sigma_{\lambda})^{C(\sigma_{\lambda})}\cong

\displaystyle\Big(\big(HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C})t\big)^{\otimes a_{1}}\otimes...\otimes\big(HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C})t^{n}\big)^{\otimes a_{n}}\Big)^{S}

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\text{Sym}^{a_{1}}(HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C}))t^{a_{1}}\otimes...\otimes\text{Sym}^{a_{n}}(HH_{\bullet}(\mathscr{C}))t^{na_{n}}.

The last expression is a summand in (
4

5

16

\mathscr{C}
.

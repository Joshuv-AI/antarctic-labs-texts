Pharmacokinetics and Bioequivalence of Two Powders of Azithromycin for Suspension: A Nonblinded, Single-Dose, Randomized, Three-Way Crossover Study in Fed and Fasting States Among Healthy Chinese Volunteers
Authors: Junbo Shao, Xingxing Liu, Jing Lin, Jiao Chen, Xiaoyan Xie
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and ObjectivesAzithromycin, a macrolide antibiotic, is commonly used to treat mild-to-moderate bacterial infections. This research aimed to evaluate the pharmacokinetics (PK) properties and bioequivalence (BE) of two azithromycin (EQ 100 mg base/packet) powders for suspension in Chinese healthy participants in fed and fasting conditions.MethodsA total of 90 Chinese healthy participants were enrolled in this nonblinded, single-dose, randomized, semireplicate, three-period, three-sequence, crossover study. Of them, 42 and 40 were categorized to the fed and fasting conditions, respectively. The washout period between doses was 21 days. Blood specimens were harvested prior to administering the drug and 194 h following administration. The plasma levels of azithromycin were analyzed using a validated liquid chromatography–tandem mass spectrometry (LC–MS/MS) approach. PK parameters were measured using noncompartmental analysis. This research compared BE between the reference and test products using the average bioequivalence (ABE) or reference-scaled average bioequivalence (RSABE) method, considering the within-subject variability (SWR) of the reference preparation. Adverse events (AEs) were monitored to examine safety and tolerability.ResultsThe RSABE method (SWR ≥ 0.294) was used to determine the BE of maximal plasma concentration (Cmax) in both fed and fasting conditions. In the ABE approach, (SWR < 0.294) was adopted to assess the BE of the area under the plasma concentration–time curve from time zero to the last measurable time point (AUC0–t) and determine the area under the plasma concentration time curve from time zero to time infinity (AUC0–inf). In the fasting condition, the point estimate of the test/reference ratio for Cmax was 1.08, with a 95% upper confidence bound of − 0.05 < 0.00. The geometric mean ratio (GMRs) for AUC0–t and AUC0–inf was 115.21% [90% confidence interval (CI) 107.25–123.27%] and 113.07% (90% CI 105.14–121.61%), respectively. In the fed condition, the point estimate of the test/reference ratio for Cmax was 0.94, with a 95% upper confidence bound of − 0.10 < 0.00. The GMR for AUC0–t and AUC0–inf was 99.51% (90% CI of 91.03–108.78%) and 99.43% (90% CI 91.73–107.78%), respectively. These data all satisfied the BE criteria for drugs with high variability. All AEs were transient and mild, and no severe AEs were observed.ConclusionsOur study indicated that the test and reference products of azithromycin (EQ 100 mg base/packet) powder for suspension were bioequivalent and safe in healthy Chinese participants, irrespective of the feeding condition.Clinical Trial Registration (chinadrugtrials.org.cn)CTR20232646, registered on 25 August 2023.

Introduction

Azithromycin is recommended to manage mild-to-moderate infections. This broad-spectrum antibiotic is prescribed for various medical conditions, including respiratory tract infections, intestinal infections, skin and soft tissue infections, and genitourinary infections [1]. Azithromycin (CAS 83905-01-5) belongs to the azalide subclass of macrolide antibiotics, originating from erythromycin. Its molecular formula is C38H72N2O12, with a molecular weight of 749.0 g/mol. Distinguished from erythromycin by adding a methyl-substituted nitrogen at position 9a in the lactone ring, azithromycin possesses a 15-membered macrolide structure [2]. This unique configuration not only enables azithromycin to be effective against Gram-negative organisms but also retains its potency against Gram-positive organisms. In addition, it contributes to the favorable pharmacokinetic (PK) properties of azithromycin, including elevated and prolonged tissue concentrations and prolonged half-life (t1/2) [3].

Following oral treatment, azithromycin is quickly absorbed and extensively distributed throughout various tissues and organs, including the skin, lungs, tonsils, and cervix. The significant tissue distribution and high intracellular levels of this compound are critical to its prolonged therapeutic efficacy [4]. Most absorbed azithromycin molecules are excreted unchanged, primarily through biliary excretion. Approximately 20% of the administered drug is excreted unchanged through urine, with renal clearance rates typically falling within the range of 6–11.34 L/h. Furthermore, fecal elimination plays an essential role in the excretion of azithromycin [5]. The PK properties of azithromycin have been comprehensively measured and documented in many studies. However, due to differences in oral absorption, variations in the PK profiles of azithromycin have been observed among different formulations of azithromycin. The oral bioavailability of azithromycin in humans ranges from 35 to 42%, with peak plasma concentrations typically observed 2–4 h after oral administration [6,7]. It has been noted that the oral absorption of azithromycin is fast, but it can be inhibited by food. Several studies have reported that the bioavailability of azithromycin may decrease in the capsule form and increase when administered as a suspension in conjunction with food [8,9]. Consequently, the unique PK characteristics of azithromycin necessitate more studies.

Azithromycin is provided in various forms in China, such as oral suspension, tablets, capsules, and intravenous injections. Azithromycin powder for suspension can be easily administered, which enables precise dosage control, decreases medication error, and improves treatment outcomes. Zithromax® (azithromycin powder for suspension, EQ 100 mg base/packet) was manufactured by Pfizer Chemicals Div Pfizer Inc. and was approved in 1991. Based on the regulatory standards mandated by China’s National Medical Products Administration, bioequivalence (BE) studies are necessary for the approval of azithromycin powder for suspension. BE assessment typically follows the average-bioequivalence (ABE) method. According to the equivalence criteria, the 90% confidence intervals (CIs) of the geometric mean ratios (GMRs) of main PK parameters (Cmax and AUC) must be between the reference and test products to be within the specific range of 80–125%. For high variability drugs with good safety profiles and wide therapeutic windows, the reference-scaled average bioequivalence (RSABE) approach is conducted through partially replicated or fully replicated crossover designs to assess BE according to intrasubject coefficient of variation (CV) of the reference preparation. Many BE studies have demonstrated that the intrasubject CV for maximum plasma concentration (Cmax) among Chinese healthy participants exceeds 30% after administering tablets in both fed and fasting conditions [10,11]. Additionally, previous trials have suggested that the intrasubject CV for the area under the plasma concentration–time curve (AUC) from time 0–72 h (AUC0–72h) is also greater than 30% in fed conditions [12]. Moreover, the clinical trial registration data from several BE studies have indicated that azithromycin possesses a high intrasubject CV.

This research aims to examine the PK characteristics and BE of two azithromycin powders for suspensions (manufactured by Jiangsu Dongke Kangde Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., China and Pfizer Chemicals Div Pfizer Inc.) after administering a single dose of EQ 100 mg base/packet azithromycin in fed and fasting conditions. Given the high variability of azithromycin, this study adopted a partial reference replicate, three-way crossover design using the RSABE method to measure BE with extended acceptance limits.

Participants and Methods

Research Products

The research products included a generic drug of azithromycin powder for suspension (EQ 100 mg base/packet), the test formulation, and Zithromax® (EQ 100 mg base/packet, azithromycin powder for suspension) as the reference preparation. The test and reference products were produced by Jiangsu Dongke Kangde Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd. (China) and Pfizer Chemicals Div Pfizer Inc (USA), respectively. Although the differences in the content ratios of some excipients were less than 5%, the formulations and manufacturing processes of the two formulations were generally consistent. The contents of the test and reference products were 99.2% and 102.1%, respectively.

Participants

Healthy volunteers meeting the inclusion criteria and showing no exclusion criteria were enrolled in this study following clinical and laboratory examinations. The following inclusion criteria were employed: (1) healthy adults aged 18–55 years; (2) men and women with body weights ≥ 50 kg and ≥ 45 kg, respectively, with a body mass index (BMI) range of 19.0–26.0 kg/m2 (inclusive); (3) absence of clinically relevant abnormalities in laboratory tests, physical examinations, or vital signs and 12-lead electrocardiograph (ECG); and (4) agreement to adopt effective contraception method while using the investigational products and for 1 month after discontinuation.

The key exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) known allergy or hypersensitive reaction to test products or chemically similar drugs, including azithromycin, erythromycin, or other macrolides; (2) history or presence of significant psychiatric, neurological, dermatological, immunological, endocrine, gastrointestinal, renal, hepatic, pulmonary, urogenital or cardiovascular diseases or disorders, or any clinical history that could affect drug absorption; (3) taking any drugs within 4 weeks before screening; (4) use of nonprescription drugs, herbal medicine, or major inducers of cytochromeP450 (CYP) 3A4, CYP2D6, or CYP2E1 enzymes, such as grapefruit or grapefruit-containing drinks, within 2 weeks before screening; (5) alcohol or substance abuse or smoking greater than five cigarettes daily; (6) positive test outcomes for anti-HIV, anti-HCV, and/or HBsAg; and (7) participation in another study, blood or plasma donation, or blood loss > 400 mL within 3 months before screening.

Study Design

This study was conducted following the Declaration of Helsinki and adhered to the International Conference on Harmonization Good Clinical Practice Guidelines. The research protocols and all amendments underwent review and approval by an Independent Ethics Committee at the study center (Jiangsu Taizhou People’s Hospital, located in Taizhou, China). Before screening, all participants provided written informed consent.

This research was a nonblinded, single-dose, randomized, semireplicate, three-period, three-sequence crossover study, which included healthy Chinese volunteers to compare the reference and test products under fed and fasting conditions. In total, 42 and 48 participants were recruited under the fasted and fed conditions, respectively. In each condition, participants were randomized into one of three sequences: RRT, RTR, or TRR. In each of the three periods, they received azithromycin powder for suspension (EQ 100 mg base/packet) at a single dosage. During periods 1–3, participants were given the test (generic drugs) or reference (Zithromax®) products. An interval of at least 21 days was considered between each period as a washout period.

Screening evaluations were conducted within 2 weeks before administering the initial dose of the test product during period 1. Eligible participants were enrolled in the clinical research unit on the first day and remained confined until day 5 (96 h postadministration) in each study period. Participants attended follow-up outpatient visits on days 7 and 9 of each study period.

Drug Administration and Blood Sample Collection

The directions for the administration of azithromycin powder for suspension in the single dose packet (100 mg) were as follows: all contents of the packet were mixed with 100 mL of water. The participants immediately drank the entire suspension. Then, twice additional 70 mL of water were added and mixed. Participants sequentially drank the suspension to ensure complete consumption of 100 mg azithromycin.

Following a minimum overnight fast of 10 h, eligible subjects from the fasting BE study randomly received a single dose (100 mg) of the reference or test product with 240 mL water on the first day of each period. Subjects were instructed to remain in an upright position postadministration. Participants were permitted to have standard meals at 4 and 10 h postadministration. Participants were permitted to drink water as needed throughout the study period, except for 1 h pre- and postadministration.

In the BE research conducted under fed conditions, eligible participants received a high-fat breakfast 30 min before administering the test or reference product in each period. The breakfast consisted of a high-fat meal consisting of approximately 50% of the total calories, with around 800–1000 kilocalories. The meal contained around 150, 250, and 500–600 kilocalories of proteins, carbohydrates, and fats, respectively. Participants were instructed to consume high-fat breakfast within 30 min. No food intake was permitted for 4 h postdosing. Other requirements were similar to the fasting BE study.

In each period, 23 venous bloods specimens (around 4 mL each) were collected from each participant via an indwelling cannula. The sampling time points included predose (0 h, within 1 h before administration) and specific postdose intervals (0.5, 1, 1.33, 1.67, 2, 2.33, 2.67, 3, 3.33, 3.67, 4, 4.5, 5, 6, 8, 12, 24, 48, 72, 96, 146, and 194 h). The blood samples were collected using vacutainers containing K2-EDTA. The specimens were then centrifuged at 1700 ± 10g for 10 min at 4℃. The resulting plasma specimens were placed in properly labeled tubes, frozen at − 20 ℃ within 2 h of centrifugation and stored at − 80 ℃ within 24 h of centrifugation for further analysis.

Subject Monitoring

Medical assessments were continuously performed throughout the study to assess the health condition of participants. Vital signs, including body temperature, pulse rate, and blood pressure, were assessed during the screening period, prior to hospital admission for each period, before dosing (0 h, within 1 h before administration), and at 2, 4, 8, 24, 48, 72, 96, 146, and 194 h after administration in each study period. Physical examinations and laboratory tests, such as blood biochemistry, complete blood count, urine analysis, and serum pregnancy tests for female participants, were carried out during the screening period, prior to hospital admission for the first period and before discharge in the third period. A standard 12-lead ECG was recorded at screening and prior to drug administration in each period to detect QT prolongation. Additionally, participants underwent chest X-rays, substance abuse tests, and alcohol breath tests at specified times. All advance events (AEs) observed during this study were reported and evaluated. All AEs were followed until resolution, stabilization, or loss of follow-up. Detailed documentation of concomitant drug usage and nonpharmacological treatments was recorded after randomization.

Pharmacokinetic Analysis

A validated analytical method was developed for the estimation of azithromycin in human plasma using liquid chromatography and tandem mass spectrometry (LC–MS/MS, Shimazu, Japan) [13,14]. Azithromycin-d5 was used as the internal standard. Five microliters of the samples were injected via the autosampler, and separation was done using an InertSustain AQ-C18 HP column (2.1 mm × 50 mm, i.d., 3 μm). The mobile phase was 0.5% acetic acid and 5.00 mM ammonium acetate in water (A) 100% acetonitrile (B) (77:23). The rate of flow was 0.400 mL/min, while the temperature of the column was maintained at 30 ℃. Mass spectrometry conditions included: TEM: 500 ℃, GS: 30 psi, CAD: 7 unit, ionization mode: ESI. Method validation was performed according to Bioanalytical Method Validation Guidance provided by the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and China NMPA. The method was validated in the concentration range of 0.5–200 ng/mL with good linearity of r2 = 0.999. The lower limit of quantification for the plasma assay was 0.5 ng/mL. The sensitivity, accuracy, precision, recovery, stability, and matrix effects were all validated to meet the acceptance criteria, demonstrating the reliability of this quantitative method for measuring azithromycin concentrations in plasma samples.

The noncompartmental analysis was utilized to measure the PK parameters of azithromycin. The Cmax was derived straight from the data to assess the absorption rate. The drug concentration–time (c–t) curve was mapped using the plasma azithromycin concentrations at different time points. The area under the plasma concentration time curve from time zero to time t (AUC0–t) was determined using the linear trapezoidal method, with t representing the final time point with a measurable concentration. The AUC from time zero to time infinity (AUC0–inf) was determined as follows: AUC0–t + Ct/λz, where Ct and λz are the final measurable drug concentration and the terminal elimination rate constant, respectively. λz is the linear regression’s slope of the log-transformed c–t curve, which was calculated on the basis of least-squares regression analysis. Both AUC0–t and AUC0–inf were the indicators for the extent of absorption. Moreover, the time to maximum observed plasma concentration (Tmax) was obtained straight from the data. The t1/2 was obtained using the following formula: t1/2 = 0.693/λz. Pharmacokinetic analyses were performed using Phoenix–WinNonlin v8.3 or higher (Pharsight-Corporation, USA).

Statistical Analysis

Based on prior clinical studies, the average intrasubject CV for the Cmax of azithromycin was 36.2% under fasting conditions and 46.8% under fed conditions [12]. For this study, the GMR, power (1−β) and significance level (α) of the reference and test products (T/R) were fixed at 0.95, 0.80, and 0.05, respectively. According to these parameters, a sample size of 33–37 participants was estimated to be necessary to establish bioequivalence using the RSABE approach. Considering a 20% risk of dropout, 42 and 48 participants were recruited to fasting and fed BE studies.

For PK analysis, we used data from all subjects with at least one evaluable PK parameter of interest in at least one treatment period. Natural log-transformed PK parameters (Cmax, AUC0–t, and AUC0–inf) data of azithromycin were used to estimate the GMR of the test/reference and construct 90% confidence intervals (CIs) using a linear mixed-effects model. In this model, treatment, period, and sequence were regarded as fixed effects, whereas the subject within-sequence was regard as a random effect. Given the expected high intraindividual variation of azithromycin, BE assessments were performed using the RSABE or ABE method based on the within-subject variability of the reference preparation. The within-subject standard deviation (SWR) of the reference products was determined prior to BE evaluation. The ABE method was utilized in the presence of SWR < 0.294 for main PK parameters (Cmax, AUC0–t, and AUC0–inf), corresponding to the intrasubject coefficient of variation (CV) < 30%. BE was concluded if the 90% CIs for the reference/test GMR of the aforementioned PK parameter(s) were in the range of 80–125%. The RSABE approach was employed for BE assessment if SWR ≥ 0.294 (intrasubject CV ≥ 30%) for main PK parameters. BE was claimed if the point estimates of test/reference for PK parameter(s) were in the range of 80–125% and the 95% upper confidence bound for (YT−YR)2 – θ × (SWR)2 was ≤ 0. YR and YT are the average values of the natural log-transformed AUC or Cmax for reference and test products, respectively. In the bioequivalence limit θ = (ln (1.25)2/σW0)2, where σW0 = 0.25 represents the regulatory constant established by the US FDA and China NMPA. The upper confidence bound was calculated using the “Howe’s Approximation I” method [15]. All subjects who received the study products and who had safety records were incorporated into the safety and tolerability analyses. The AEs were evaluated in compliance with the National Cancer Institute’s Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) v5.0. The evaluation criteria were based on reaction severity, symptomatology, signs, and laboratory indicators. Statistical tests were conducted using SAS v9.4 or higher (SAS Institute, USA).

Results

Participants

In total, 79 and 73 participants were screened in the fed and fasting cohorts, respectively. Then, 48 and 42 eligible subjects in the fed and fasting cohorts were randomly categorized to three sequences (RRT, RTR, or TRR), respectively. Five and two subjects in the fed and fasting cohorts left the study. The flow diagram of screening participants in the two cohorts is shown in Figs. 1 and 2. All of the included participants in the fasting and fed cohorts were Chinese. Most of the participants were men [36 subjects (85.71%) and 40 subjects (83.33%), respectively]. The mean age of subjects in the fed and fasting cohorts was 32.6 years (range 18–51 years) and 33.9 years (range 20–50 years), respectively. The ranges of height, body weight, and BMI of the fed and fasting cohorts were 157.0–186.0 cm and 153.0–185.0 cm, 50.2–81.0 kg and 47.1–83.8 kg, and 19.8–26.0 kg/m2 and 19.7–25.6 kg/m2, respectively. The BE assessments of the reference and test products of azithromycin powder for suspension among healthy Chinese subjects in fed and fasting states are demonstrated in Table 3. In the fasting condition, the GMR (90% CI) of T/R for Cmax, AUC0–t, and AUC0–inf were 111.08% (96.23–128.22%), 115.21% (107.25–123.77%), and 113.07% (105.14–121.60%), respectively. In the fed condition, the GMR (90% CI) of T/R for Cmax, AUC0–t, and AUC0–inf were 97.63% (85.48–111.50%), 99.51% (91.03–108.78%), and 99.43% (91.73–107.78%), respectively. All AUC0–t and AUC0–inf values were within BE limits (80.0–125.0%). The intrasubject CVs of Cmax both exceeded 30% in fed and fasting conditions (Table 3), suggesting that azithromycin is a drug with high variability and that the RSABE method can be employed for BE evaluation. The point estimate of the test/reference ratio for Cmax was 1.08 in the fasting condition, with the 95% upper confidence bound being − 0.05 < 0.00. In the fed condition, the point estimates of the ratio of the test/reference for Cmax was 0.94, with the 95% upper confidence bound being − 0.10 < 0.00. The results adhered to the BE criteria for drugs with high variability.

Safety and Tolerability

In the fasting condition, 43 AEs occurred in 42 subjects. Among 41 participants who received the test products, 16 subjects (39.02%, 16/41) reported 16 AEs. Of 42 participants who received the reference products, 25 subjects (59.52%, 25/42) reported 27 AEs. In the fed condition, 53 AEs occurred in 48 subjects. Of 48 participants who received the test products, 24 subjects (50.00%, 24/48) reported 25 AEs. Of 47 participants who received the reference products, 24 subjects (51.06%, 24/47) reported 28 AEs. All AEs were of CTCAE grades 1 and 2. No fatalities, severe AEs, or discontinuations due to AEs were noted during the study. Table 4 provides a summary of AEs in this study.

Discussion

This study utilized a nonblinded, single-dose, randomized, semi-replicate, three-period, three-sequence crossover design to compare the PK properties and BE of two azithromycin (EQ 100 mg base/packet) powders for suspension among healthy Chinese volunteers in fed and fasting conditions. Our findings provided a deeper insight into the PK properties of azithromycin and demonstrated that the reference and test products were safe and bioequivalent for healthy individuals, irrespective of the fed or fasting condition.

Many studies have investigated the PK profiles of various formulations of azithromycin, including tablets, capsules, and intravenous solutions [16,17]. Nevertheless, there are few studies on the PK properties of oral suspension. Therefore, we assessed the BE of azithromycin powder for suspension in both fed and fasting conditions. The findings suggested that compared with the fasting condition, the coadministration of test and reference products with food significantly decreased the Cmax of azithromycin by approximately 45.61% and 45.30%, respectively. Additionally, the AUC0–t reduced by approximately 34.72% and 28.98%, and the AUC0–inf decreased by approximately 34.27% and 29.58%, respectively. Previous studies have yielded inconsistent results. The administration of azithromycin for oral suspension with food increased Cmax by 46% and AUC by14% [18]. Conversely, food effect-specific studies on 600 mg azithromycin tablets did not significantly reduce AUC when administered in the fed condition [19]. There are similar reports in literature, suggesting that coadministration of azithromycin capsules with food decreases AUC by approximately 30–50% [20]. Therefore, the guidelines recommend that patients should take azithromycin capsules 1 h before or 2 h following a meal. Delayed gastric emptying induced by a high-fat meal may explain the decrease in the Cmax and AUC of azithromycin via prolonged residence time in the stomach and subsequent degradation of azithromycin [21]. The reason for the increased mean Cmax and AUC of azithromycin after administering high-fat meals remains unclear. Potential mechanisms may be alterations in the pH of intestinal contents and food-induced increase in splanchnic blood flow [19]. However, these mechanisms do not explain the opposite changes in the oral bioavailability of different formulations of azithromycin after receiving a high-fat meal. Our research was not intended to assess the impact of food on azithromycin. Furthermore, studies should be designed specifically to assess the influence of food and consider the impact of factors, such as meal composition and timing, on drug PK. Overall, azithromycin powder for suspension has been developed to meet the specific dosing requirements of pediatric patients and other target populations [22,23]. In instances where concomitant food intake decreases the absorption of azithromycin, it is advised to avoid administering azithromycin with meals. Therefore, understanding these PK characteristics is essential to optimizing dosing schedules and promoting the efficacy and safety of azithromycin powder for suspension.

This study used a partial reference–replicate three-period crossover design to assess the specific within-subject variability of reference preparations, a critical factor for establishing the BE of drugs with high variability. The intrasubject CV for Cmax in both fasting and fed conditions exceeded 30% (Table 3), indicating a high level of within-subject variability in the absorption rate. In contrast, the intrasubject CVs for AUC0–t and AUC0–inf were below 30%, suggesting low within-subject variability in absorption extent. Therefore, the BE assessment was conducted using the RSABE approach for Cmax and the ABE method for AUC0–t and AUC0–inf. The RSABE method adjusts the BE acceptance criteria within the range of 80.00–125.00% according to the size of within-subject variability in the reference preparation, with the 95% upper confidence bound being < 0.00. This method aims to minimize unnecessary exposure among study populations and achieve a scientific BE assessment of different formulations. Additionally, it facilitates the analysis of potential factors influencing the high variability observed in drug formulations. In the present study, the GMRs of main PK parameters (AUC0–inf, AUC0–t, and Cmax) exceeded 110% in the fasting condition. However, following the principles of the RSABE method, it can be inferred that the significant variability in the absorption rate of azithromycin powder for suspension was predominantly due to the PK properties of active pharmaceutical ingredients rather than formulation factors. Finally, all results from this research showed that the reference and test products satisfied the BE criteria applicable to highly variable drugs. These findings support the marketing of the new generic azithromycin powder for suspension in China.

Conclusions

Our study demonstrated the BE and safety of two azithromycin (EQ 100 mg base/packet) powders for suspension in Chinese healthy individuals in fed and fasting conditions. The RSABE method, with extended acceptance limits, allowed for a more accurate BE assessment, considering the high intrasubject variability of azithromycin. Our findings provide invaluable information for regulators and healthcare professionals regarding the use of generic azithromycin products. Future studies can investigate the effect of genetic factors and other potential sources of variability on the PK properties of azithromycin in different groups of patients.

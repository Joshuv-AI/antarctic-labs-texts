Synergistic effects of denosumab and BMP-2 on bone regeneration in critical-sized femoral defects in ovariectomized rats
Authors: ZhouShan Tao, WenJing Cheng, Lin Wang, Wei Qi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Osteoporotic bone defects pose significant clinical challenges due to poor natural repair capacity. Denosumab (DNB) strongly inhibits osteoclast activity, while Bone morphogenetic protein-2 (BMP-2) effectively promotes bone formation; however, it remains unclear whether combining DNB and BMP-2 can enhance bone repair in estrogen deficiency-induced osteoporosis. We investigated the effects of local BMP-2 implantation and systemic DNB treatment on bone regeneration in ovariectomized (OVX) rats. Micro-computed tomography(Micro-CT) and H&E staining showed that DNB and BMP-2 synergistically accelerated healing of osteoporotic bone defects over 12 weeks. Immunofluorescence, immunohistochemistry, and biomechanical analyses revealed that DNB/BMP-2 increased expression of osteopontin(OPN), osteoclastogenesis inhibitory factor(OPG) and Runt-related transcription factor 2 (RUNX2), and decreased tartrate-resistant acid phosphatase (TRAP) and Tumor Necrosis Factor-α (TNF-α) levels compared to the OVX group (all P < 0.05). Moreover, DNB/BMP-2 significantly improved torsional stiffness and maximum compressive force versus either treatment alone (all P < 0.05). The findings demonstrate that systemic DNB combined with local BMP-2 rapidly promotes femoral metaphyseal bone regeneration in osteoporotic rats.

Introduction

In the context of osteoporotic fracture repair, the restoration of mechanical stability and structural integrity to the compromised bone is paramount—serving as a cornerstone for enabling affected individuals, particularly elderly patients burdened with multiple comorbidities, to regain functional independence and resume essential activities of daily living [1]. Consequently, there is growing scientific and clinical interest in expediting the fracture healing process and minimizing the risk of non-union complications. Given the intricate, multistage nature of fracture repair—encompassing inflammation, callus formation, remodeling, and the orchestrated involvement of diverse cell lineages such as mesenchymal stem cells, osteoblasts, and chondrocytes—therapeutic strategies that simultaneously target multiple phases of regeneration or modulate several cellular players may offer superior efficacy [2]. In this light, combination therapies emerge as a particularly promising and synergistic approach, holding transformative potential for enhancing skeletal recovery [3, 4]. Methods aimed at accelerating and optimizing the healing process may confer significant therapeutic advantages in the management of metaphyseal fractures, particularly those affecting the distal femur and distal radius [5]. In contrast to diaphyseal fractures, metaphyseal injuries often entail heightened challenges during tissue regeneration, stemming from profound disruption of the trabecular architecture and the close anatomical proximity to the articular surface—a critical determinant of joint function and long-term outcomes [6]. Moreover, metaphyseal fractures are disproportionately prevalent among individuals with osteoporosis, underscoring the imperative for advanced fracture repair strategies that not only expedite consolidation but also enhance the structural integrity, quality, and mineral density of bone at the site of injury [7].

Bone morphogenetic protein-2 (BMP-2) stands as a paramount osteoinductive morphogen, intricately orchestrating the complex process of bone regeneration [8]. It exerts profound stimulatory effects on the proliferation and differentiation of osteoblasts and osteoprogenitor cells, both in controlled *in vitro* environments and within living systems [9]. Remarkably, compelling evidence from preclinical studies demonstrates its robust capacity to induce de novo bone formation in animal models afflicted with critical-sized defects [10], metaphyseal impairment [11], and spinal fusion scenarios [12], underscoring its transformative potential in regenerative orthopedics. Denosumab(DNB) stands as the most recent advancement among antiresorptive agents, distinguished by its innovative mechanism of action [13]. In essence, DNB is a fully human monoclonal antibody meticulously engineered to target and neutralize RANKL—a pivotal mediator in bone metabolism—thereby playing a crucial role in maintaining physiological bone turnover [14]. Through its exceptional specificity and high-affinity binding to the cytokine RANKL, DNB effectively suppresses RANKL signaling; consequently, the recruitment, differentiation, and activity of osteoclasts are markedly diminished, leading to a profound reduction in bone resorption [15]. Recognized within Clinical Practice Guidelines, DNB emerges as a premier therapeutic choice, endorsed as a first-line intervention for the prevention of vertebral, hip, and non-vertebral fractures [16, 17].

Due to the different mechanisms of DNB and BMP-2, systemic application of DNB and local application of BMP-2 not only inhibit bone resorption but also promote local bone regeneration, making the combination of these two drugs possible. Furthermore, BMP not only triggers the intricate process of osteogenesis but also potently stimulates osteoclastogenesis and precipitates premature bone resorption [18–20]. Moreover, to fully harness and optimize the profound biological regenerative capacity of BMP-2, DNB may suppress the accompanying activation of osteoclasts, thereby preserving skeletal integrity and amplifying anabolic efficacy. The overall goal of this study was to investigate the combined effects of BMP-2 and DNB on metaphyseal bone healing.

Materials and methods

Animals

Fifty female Sprague–Dawley (SD) rats (3 months old), with an average body weight of 230 g, were included in this study. Four animals were housed per cage under controlled environmental conditions (25 °C, 55% humidity, and a 12-h light/dark cycle). Standard laboratory chow and tap water were provided ad libitum. All procedures involving animals were conducted in accordance with the principles of laboratory animal care, and the study protocol was approved by the Animal Research Committee of the university.

Surgery and treatment

After bilateral ovariectomy (N = 45) or Sham surgery (N = 5) as described in previous studies [21, 22], a period of 12 weeks was allowed to pass before defect surgery. In order to establish an osteoporosis model, the rats underwent either bilateral ovariectomy (OVX) or a sham operation (Sham) at 3 months of age. Following anesthesia induced by intraperitoneal injection of sodium pentobarbital (40 mg/kg body weight), a lumbar lateral incision was carefully made at the midpoint between the lower edge of the free ribs and the iliac crest, where the ovaries were located. Prior to ovary removal, a suture was gently placed around the ovarian artery and vein. To ensure proper wound closure, the muscle layers were repositioned systematically and sutured using absorbable sutures, while the skin was closed with a 4-0 nylon suture. Subsequently, five randomly selected OVX rats and five sham-operated rats were humanely euthanized. The distal femurs were then collected for evaluation to confirm the successful induction of osteoporosis.

After a 3-month induction period, femur defect drilling was carried out under general anesthesia induced by intraperitoneal injection of sodium pentobarbital (40 mg/kg body weight). A linear skin incision of approximately 1 cm was made bilaterally in the distal femoral epiphysis, and the underlying muscles were carefully dissected to expose the femoral condyle. Subsequently, a 2.5-mm-diameter anteroposterior bicortical channel was created perpendicular to the shaft axis to disrupt the cancellous trabecular bone. This was performed using a trephine bur at a low speed, with continuous irrigation of saline solution to prevent thermal necrosis. The drilled cavities were gently flushed with saline solution via injection to remove any residual bone fragments, following the method described previously [22–24]. Collagen sponges were then implanted to fill the defects, and the surgical wounds were closed with a 3-0 suture. Then all animals were randomly divided into four groups: OVX group(OVX), OVX + denosumab treatment group(DNB), OVX + BMP-2 treatment group(BMP-2) and OVX + DNB + BMP-2 treatment group(DNB/BMP-2). The defects from group BMP-2 and DNB/BMP-2 were filled with collagen sponges containing 30 µg of BMP-2(Hangzhou Jiuyuan Genetic Engineering Co., Ltd.). The method of local application and dose was chosen according to previous studies [25]. According to the weight dose conversion coefficient method of experimental animals, the rats from DNB and DNB/BMP-2 were subcutaneously injected with denosumab(QILU PHARMACEUTICAL CO., LTD) at a dose of 6.2 mg/kg starting from the third day after bone defect surgery. The denosumab dose used in this study was converted from a single clinical dose of 60 mg for the human body, and this drug dosage is more in line with clinical usage scenarios. For the subsequent analytical procedure at 90 days, the skin was carefully reopened, and the bone defect site, along with the overlying membrane and surrounding soft tissue, was harvested en bloc and fixed in formalin.

Micro‑CT evaluation

Micro-CT imaging (Micro-CT50, Scanco Medical, Basersdorf, Switzerland) was used to assess new bone formation in the defect region [26]. All samples were mounted in a custom holder to align the long axis of the drilled channel perpendicular to the X-ray beam. Scans were performed at 55 kV and 114 μA, with a slice thickness of 0.048 mm in medium-resolution mode, a 1024 reconstruction matrix, and 200 ms integration time. Images and parameters were compared between groups to confirm the osteoporosis model in rats. A Gaussian filter (sigma = 0.8, support = 1) was applied to reduce noise. Mineralized bone was segmented using a fixed threshold (value = 190) to exclude non-mineralized tissue. For evaluating bone regeneration, a central 2-mm-diameter region within the 2.5-mm defect was delineated per slice by circular contouring to define a consistent volume of interest (VOI) and avoid native bone margins. After 3D reconstruction, bone Mineral density (BMD), connectivity Density(Conn. D.), bone volume fraction (BV/TV), trabecular number (Tb. N), thickness (Tb. Th), and separation (Tb.Sp) were automatically calculated to verify the osteoporosis model, while BV/TV in the defect region was used to assess new bone formation, following the manufacturer’s protocol. All data and 3D images were generated using the scanner’s built-in software.

Histological preparation and analysis

Following high-resolution Micro-CT imaging, femoral condyles underwent meticulous decalcification in a freshly prepared 10% ethylene diamine tetraacetic acid (EDTA) solution over a four-week period, with complete solution exchanges performed weekly to ensure optimal tissue integrity. Subsequently, specimens were progressively dehydrated through an ascending ethanol series—ranging from 70% to absolute alcohol—to preserve structural fidelity and prepare for embedding. To achieve a precise and unobstructed visualization of the defect site, utmost attention was paid to the spatial orientation and anatomical alignment of the femurs during paraffin embedding, ensuring histological sections would capture the region of interest with maximal clarity.

Serial longitudinal sections, precisely 4 μm in thickness, were expertly sectioned and carefully mounted onto polylysine-coated microscope slides to enhance adhesion and prevent tissue loss. For comprehensive histomorphological evaluation, hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining and tartrate-resistant acid phosphatase (TRAP) staining (Sigma–Aldrich, St. Louis, USA) were carried out in strict accordance with the manufacturer’s protocols, yielding detailed insights into cellular architecture, collagen deposition, and osteoclast activity, respectively [27].

Immunohistochemical evaluation

The expression of osteopontin(OPN) and osteoclastogenesis inhibitory factor(OPG) was evaluated using the following procedure. Deparaffinized sections were gently washed with PBS, then incubated with 0.3% hydrogen peroxide for 20 min to block endogenous peroxidase activity. This was followed by a blocking step with 5% bovine serum albumin (BSA) to reduce nonspecific binding. Next, the sections were incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies against OPG (1:100; Sigma–Aldrich, St. Louis, USA) and OPN (1:200; Biomedical Technologies, Stoughton, MA), using optimized dilutions for best results. After three brief washes with PBS, the sections were treated with biotinylated secondary antibody (Zhongshan Biotechnology Co., Ltd, China) for 20 min, followed by incubation with horseradish peroxidase-conjugated avidin–biotin complex (ABC) (Zhongshan Biotechnology Co., Ltd, China) for an additional 20 min. Immunostaining signals were visualized by adding a buffered 3,3-diaminobenzidine tetrahydrochloride (DAB) substrate (Zhongshan Biotechnology Co., Ltd, China). The sections were then lightly counterstained with Harris’s hematoxylin for 40 seconds each, with a 3-min rinse under running water between staining steps. Following counterstaining, the sections were gradually dehydrated through a series of increasing ethanol concentrations, cleared with xylene, and finally mounted with coverslips for preservation. All specimens were carefully examined under bright-field microscopy using an Olympus DP71 microscope (Olympus Co., Japan). To ensure reliability and reproducibility, each experiment was performed in triplicate or more.

Immunofluorescence assessment

Tissue sections were permeabilized using 0.5% Triton X-100, and following antigen retrieval, the slides were blocked with 5% goat serum (Vector S-1000) for 1 h at room temperature. Sections were then incubated with primary antibodies overnight at 4 °C. After washing the slides thoroughly with PBS, the sections were incubated with Cyanine5-conjugated goat anti-rabbit secondary antibody (Invitrogen, A10523) for 30 min, followed by mounting with DAPI-containing mounting medium (Vector Laboratories) [27, 28]. Biomechanical assessment

Specimens were prepared by potting the proximal half of the distal femur in a 1-inch-square aluminum casing using polymethylmethacrylate (PMMA). This casing was attached to the actuator of a servohydraulic biaxial test frame (FastTrack 8874, Instron, Canton, MA). The actuator lowered the specimen into a distal aluminum casing filled with PMMA and fixed to the load frame base. Specimens remained wrapped in saline-soaked gauze throughout. Each specimen underwent three torsional cycles to 8° at 1°/s, followed by one compressive preconditioning cycle to 1 mm, then a compressive ramp to failure at 0.1 mm/s. Increasing twist corresponded to internal rotation of the distal femur. Torsional rigidity was determined from the slope of the third torque-twist curve, with twist normalized by the length between the potted ends. Compressive stiffness was the slope of the load-displacement curve; compressive strength was the peak load, and displacement to failure was the displacement at that load. Work to failure was the area under the load-displacement curve up to the maximum load [29].

Statistical analysis

All analyses were performed using SPSS 21.0 (SPSS, Chicago, IL). Data are presented as mean ± SD and analyzed by one-way ANOVA and t-test. The Kruskal–Wallis test was used to compare the number of positive cells across treatments. Statistical significance was set at *p < 0.05 for all analyses.

Our results showed that both BMD and BMC in OVX rats were significantly lower than those in the Sham group (P < 0.05, Fig. 2C). Combined treatment of DNB and BMP-2 affects the bone strength in the defect area

In order to further elucidate the effects of DNB and BMP-2 treatment on bone strength in the bone defect region, biomechanical testing was conducted. The results demonstrated that mechanical strength was significantly enhanced in all intervention groups—DNB, BMP-2, and DNB/BMP-2—as evidenced by notable increases in biomechanical parameters such as torsional stiffness and maximum compressive force, compared with the OVX group (all P < 0.05, Fig. 9). Discussion

Our overall goal in this study was to explore the combined effects of BMP-2 and DNB on metaphyseal bone healing in osteoporotic rats. The findings suggest that the combined use of BMP-2 and DNB contributed to improved healing by enhancing bone regeneration, reducing bone resorption, and supporting better mechanical function. This combination therapy led to greater torsional rigidity, increased strength, and more robust bone formation both within the surrounding tissues and at the defect site, when compared to the OVX group. In contrast, notable improvements were observed in the control group over either the BMP-2 or DNB monotherapy groups in terms of bone volume, mineral density, mineral content, and mechanical properties. Therefore, the results of this study suggest that BMP-2 and DNB may work synergistically in promoting healing of metaphyseal defects, and that their combined application offers greater benefits than treatment with either agent alone.

This study has several strengths, including the use of multiple analytical approaches to evaluate the combined effect of BMP-2 and DNB. Bone defect repair was assessed using micro-computed tomography, histology, biomechanical testing, and immunohistochemistry. These analyses yielded valuable insights into the microstructural and biological mechanisms underlying the observed enhancements in femoral metaphyseal stiffness and strength in the combined treatment group [29]. In addition, the mechanical testing protocol was designed to evaluate both torsional and compressive mechanical properties [30]. Significant differences among groups were observed in both torsional and compressive stiffness, indicating that the effects of a given treatment on bone healing may vary depending on the loading mode. Furthermore, intergroup differences were also detected in compressive strength, compressive stiffness, and work-to-failure. Collectively, these mechanical test results suggest that different therapeutic interventions confer distinct mechanical properties to the femoral metaphysis, implying that the extent of local bone repair differentially influences overall bone strength. Finally, it is worth noting that this study employed a model of metaphyseal bone healing to explore the potential of combination therapy for treating defects that are prone to complications during healing and are commonly encountered in osteoporotic individuals [31, 32]. We propose that, among clinical fractures or defects requiring pharmacologic intervention, metaphyseal fractures or defects in osteoporotic patients represent strong candidates for combined treatment with BMP-2 and DNB.

The therapeutic efficacy of BMP-2 in conjunction with DNB may be attributed to the distinct yet complementary ways in which these two agents modulate cellular dynamics at the site of tissue repair. BMP-2 demonstrated a particularly potent biological influence [33, 34], manifesting in consistently elevated tissue mineral density across all experimental groups. In contrast, DNB appeared to foster a more spatially uniform augmentation of bone formation, and—judging from the data on relative fiber orientation—seemed to facilitate superior integration between the nascent tissue and the pre-existing host bone [13, 35]. We posit that the synergistic treatment enhances bone regeneration through a dual mechanistic framework. First, BMP-2 acts as a powerful stimulant for local stem cell recruitment, thereby expanding the reservoir of osteoprogenitor cells and driving their robust differentiation into functional osteoblasts [36]. Second, DNB has a strong function of inhibiting osteoclast activity and has a good promoting effect on the repair of cancellous bone and cortical bone.

It is acknowledged that RUNX2 expression indicates early osteogenesis, while OPN and OPG expression reflect later stages, suggesting local osteogenic activity [37]. Inflammatory and osteoclast-promoting cytokines, notably TNF-α, possess the potent capacity to augment osteoclastic activation and intensify bone resorption processes [38]. Histological and imaging results suggested that a large amount of bone formation could be observed in the BMP-2 treatment group. Moreover, immunofluorescence and immunohistochemical staining indicated high expression of TNF-α and TRAP, accompanied by high expression of RUNX-2, OPN and OPG. This further suggested that BMP-2 could improve local bone metabolism and promote osteoclastic activity during the process of promoting osteogenic formation and bone maturation. The osteoclast inhibitory function of DNB could regulate the increase of osteoclast activity indirectly caused by BMP-2, so that more bone mass and a large amount of mature woven bone were observed in the combined treatment group.

Several limitations of this study warrant mention. First, only a single time point was examined, which prevented the evaluation of the individual and combined effects of BMP-2 and DNB on the healing rate. Further studies are required to determine the specific phase(s) of healing during which the combination therapy exerts its most pronounced effect. The selection of the 12-week time point in this study was guided by findings from a prior investigation indicating that healing was substantially advanced by this stage. Indeed, in the current cohort of specimens, the healing process appeared to be shifting from the bone formation phase to the bone remodeling phase. Newly formed mineralized bone tissue filled the defect site across all treatment groups; however, much of this tissue exhibited a trabecular architecture, even in regions originally occupied by cortical bone. Therefore, we conclude that the positive effect of the combined treatment observed at 12 weeks reflects a genuine overall enhancement of healing, rather than a transient or temporary improvement.

Conclusions

This enhanced structural sophistication likely underpins the significant improvements in whole-bone mechanical performance—elevating both stiffness and strength—by refining the intrinsic material properties of the new tissue and ensuring seamless interfacial integration with the surrounding cortical bone. While further investigations are warranted to fully elucidate these intricate modes of action, the present findings nonetheless underscore a clear advantage: the combined administration of BMP-2 and DNB leads to more abundant reparative tissue, superior tissue quality, and ultimately, more effective skeletal healing.

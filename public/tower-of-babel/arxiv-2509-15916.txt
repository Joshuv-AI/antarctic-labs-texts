Analytic Bernoulli Functions: Correspondence with Hermite Polynomials
Ken Nagai

Abstract
We establish an operator–theoretic correspondence between periodic Bernoulli kernels and Hermite polynomials, framed through the umbral calculus and a quantum analogy. Starting from the analytic master function F ∗ F^{\ast} , the periodic Hilbert transform appears as a π / 2 \pi/2 symplectic rotation, while Jacobi matrices reproduce the oscillator ladder. Umbral operational rules extend this structure across complex order, and Weyl algebra lifting together with the Weil representation explains the shared spectrum of Bernoulli and Hermite families. Analytically, this chain connects Clausen functions to Bernoulli kernels, to polylogarithms, to the Hurwitz zeta function, and ultimately to the Lerch transcendent, embedding the umbral framework into the classical landscape of special functions. This perspective clarifies why odd zeta values arise in Bernoulli integrals and unifies trigonometric and Gaussian worlds within a coherent operator framework.

Main Theorem

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
B_{2n+1}(x)
be the periodic Bernoulli function.
Under the periodic Hilbert transform
\mathcal{H}
and the umbral lifting
to the Weyl algebra,
B_{2n+1}(x)
maps to a Hermite polynomial
H_{2n}(x)
up to a constant:

\mathcal{U}[B_{2n+1}(x)]=c_{n}H_{2n}(x),\hskip 18.49988ptc_{n}=\frac{(-1)^{n}2^{2n+1}(2n+1)!}{\pi^{2n+1}}\zeta(2n+1).

The remainder of the paper elaborates the operator bridge behind this correspondence,
details the discrete Heisenberg representation,
and interprets the periodic Hilbert transform as a symplectic rotation in the Weil representation.

Theorem 4.2

(Bernoulli
\to
Hermite Correspondence)
.

Let

B_{2n+1}(x)=-\frac{2(2n+1)!}{(2\pi)^{2n+1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sin(2\pi kx)}{k^{2n+1}}

and

H_{m}(x)=(-1)^{m}e^{x^{2}}\frac{d^{m}}{dx^{m}}e^{-x^{2}}.

There exists a linear transform
\mathcal{U}

(built from the periodic Hilbert transform
\mathcal{H}
and Weyl displacements)
such that

\boxed{\mathcal{U}[B_{2n+1}(x)]=c_{n}\,H_{2n}(x)},\hskip 18.49988ptc_{n}=\frac{(-1)^{n}2^{2n+1}(2n+1)!}{\pi^{2n+1}}\zeta(2n+1).

4.1
Fourier Stage – Periodic Bernoulli Kernel

The periodic Hilbert transform rotates the Fourier modes:

\mathcal{H}\!\left[\sin(2\pi kx)\right]=-\cos(2\pi kx),

preparing the spectrum for mapping into oscillator language.

4.2
Jacobi Matrix Interlude – Discrete Spectral Bridge

Organize the cosine-mode coefficients
\{c_{k}\}
as a vector and let the
Jacobi matrix

J=\begin{pmatrix}0&\sqrt{1}&0&0&\cdots\\
\sqrt{1}&0&\sqrt{2}&0&\cdots\\
0&\sqrt{2}&0&\sqrt{3}&\cdots\\
0&0&\sqrt{3}&0&\ddots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\ddots\end{pmatrix},\hskip 18.49988ptJ\,\mathbf{v}_{m}=\sqrt{\tfrac{m}{2}}\mathbf{v}_{m-1}+\sqrt{\tfrac{m+1}{2}}\mathbf{v}_{m+1}.

Its eigenvectors correspond to Hermite sequences.
Inserting the Bernoulli coefficients through
J

reproduces the three-term recurrence.

xH_{m}(x)=\tfrac{1}{2}H_{m+1}(x)+mH_{m-1}(x),

revealing that the oscillator ladder structure is latent already in the Bernoulli kernel spectrum.

4.3
Weyl–Operator Lifting – Umbral Action

Identify each Fourier exponential
e^{2\pi ikx}
with a Weyl operator

W(2\pi k,0)=e^{2\pi ik\hat{x}}

acting on the Gaussian vacuum
\varphi_{0}(x)=e^{-x^{2}/2}
.
Creation–annihilation operators
(a^{\dagger},a)
then appear via

W(t,0)=e^{ta^{\dagger}-ta},\hskip 18.49988pt[a,a^{\dagger}]=1.

4.4
Generating Function Alignment – Emergence of Hermite

Summing the lifted modes recovers

e^{-t^{2}+2xt}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}H_{m}(x)\frac{t^{m}}{m!},

whose coefficients align with those of
B_{2n+1}
, yielding the boxed correspondence.

Remark 4.3
.

The explicit inclusion of the Jacobi matrix shows the
spectral mechanism
behind the scenes:
Bernoulli kernels, through Hilbert transform and Jacobi dynamics, sit on the same Heisenberg–Weyl orbit as Hermite polynomials.
This viewpoint naturally extends to the Weil representation (see Appendix

Definition 5.1

(Periodic Hilbert transform)
.

For
f(x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\hat{f}_{k}e^{2\pi ikx}
(1-periodic),

\displaystyle(\mathcal{H}f)(x)

\displaystyle=-i\sum_{k\neq 0}\sgn(k)\,\hat{f}_{k}e^{2\pi ikx},

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}[\sin(2\pi kx)]

\displaystyle=-\cos(2\pi kx),

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}[\cos(2\pi kx)]

5.1
Heisenberg Action on Fourier Modes

Introduce the canonical pair
(\hat{x},\hat{p})=(x,-i\partial_{x})
with
[\hat{x},\hat{p}]=i
.
The Fourier modes act as Weyl displacement operators

e^{2\pi ikx}=e^{2\pi ik\hat{x}},\hskip 18.49988pte^{2\pi ik\hat{x}}e^{2\pi ip\hat{p}}=e^{-4\pi^{2}ikp}\,e^{2\pi ip\hat{p}}e^{2\pi ik\hat{x}},

giving the Heisenberg–Weyl commutation.

Definition 5.2

(Discrete Heisenberg representation)
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{d}
be the group generated by lattice translations

T_{m}:f(x)\mapsto f(x+m)
and phase multiplications

M_{k}:f(x)\mapsto e^{2\pi ikx}\,f(x)
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})
,
subject to the commutation relation

T_{m}M_{k}=e^{2\pi imk}\,M_{k}T_{m},\hskip 18.49988ptm,k\in\mathbb{Z}.

This action defines the
discrete Heisenberg representation
.
Under the periodic Hilbert transform, these operators act by
rotating Fourier modes, and finite truncations of this action
yield the Jacobi matrix structure that produces the Hermite ladder
in Theorem (Bernoulli–Hermite Correspondence).

Under
\mathcal{H}
, sine and cosine modes are rotated by
\pi/2
,
mimicking the phase-space rotation generated by

H_{\text{osc}}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\hat{p}^{2}+\hat{x}^{2})
,
and thus linking periodic Bernoulli kernels to oscillator dynamics.

Conclusion and Outlook

We have traced an operator–theoretic path from periodic Bernoulli
kernels to Hermite polynomials, mediated by the Hilbert transform,
Jacobi matrices, and Weyl algebra lifting.
This perspective clarifies why odd zeta values arise in Bernoulli integrals
and situates them within the Heisenberg–Weyl orbit alongside Gaussian structures.

Beyond this correspondence, several directions remain open.
On the analytic side, future work may pursue the time–evolution operator
through Wick rotation, leading naturally to temperature Green functions
and statistical mechanics.
In this setting, extensions towards Tsallis statistics and ultimately

q
–analysis come into reach, suggesting a deformed umbral calculus.

On the arithmetic side, the theta representation of kernels points
towards elliptic curves and modular forms.
Developing the present operator correspondence within this framework
may reveal deeper links between Bernoulli–Hermite duality and
classical automorphic theory, in line with the perspective of
[
16
]
.

Proof of Theorem
4.1

We verify that
\mathcal{H}
acts as a quarter rotation on the Heisenberg

Appendix B
Weyl Algebra Connection

Motivation.

The discrete Heisenberg representation from Definitionbecomes, in the continuum limit, the familiar
Weyl algebra
acting on the harmonic oscillator.
This connection makes explicit how periodic Bernoulli kernels and Hermite polynomials are two shadows of the same operator framework.

Definition B.1

(Weyl Algebra)
.

The Weyl algebra
\mathcal{W}
is generated by creation and annihilation operators
(a^{\dagger},a)
obeying

[a,a^{\dagger}]=1,\hskip 18.49988ptN=a^{\dagger}a,

and by the Weyl displacement operators

W(\xi,\eta)=e^{i(\xi\hat{p}-\eta\hat{x})}=e^{\xi a^{\dagger}-\eta a},

satisfying the multiplication law

W(\xi,\eta)W(\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime})=e^{-i(\xi\eta^{\prime}-\eta\xi^{\prime})/2}\,W(\xi+\xi^{\prime},\eta+\eta^{\prime}).

B.1
From Discrete to Continuous

The lattice operators
(T_{m},M_{k})
of
\mathcal{H}_{d}

T_{m}=e^{im\hat{p}},\hskip 18.49988ptM_{k}=e^{ik\hat{x}},

for small
m,k
.
Their commutator

T_{m}M_{k}=e^{2\pi imk}M_{k}T_{m}

tends to the continuous Weyl relation

e^{im\hat{p}}e^{ik\hat{x}}=e^{-imk}\,e^{ik\hat{x}}e^{im\hat{p}}.

B.2
Bernoulli–Hermite Operators

Under this identification, the Bernoulli kernel’s Fourier coefficients correspond to applying

\mathcal{U}=\sum_{k}\frac{\sin(2\pi kx)}{k^{2n+1}}\,W(2\pi k,0)

H_{m}(x)=\frac{\partial^{m}}{\partial t^{m}}\biggr|_{t=0}e^{-t^{2}+2xt},

\mathcal{W}
-module.
This provides an operator-theoretic explanation for the Bernoulli
\leftrightarrow
Hermite correspondence.

D.2
Periodic Kernels as Weil Images

The periodic Hilbert transform corresponds to the symplectic rotation

R_{\pi/2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},

sending sine modes to cosine modes.
Under
U(R_{\pi/2})
, the Bernoulli kernel’s Fourier basis is rotated
in phase space exactly as the Hermite ladder is.
Thus the Bernoulli–Hermite correspondence appears as two different
cross-sections of the same Weil orbit.

D.3
Spectral Consequences

Because the Weil representation preserves the oscillator spectrum,
any functional identity derived for Bernoulli kernels through
periodic Hilbert transforms or Jacobi dynamics can be recast as a statement
about Hermite polynomials, and vice versa.
This viewpoint unifies trigonometric and Gaussian worlds under a single
metaplectic action.

Efficient abnormal behavior detection in information-centric internet of things using SVM
Authors: Nahideh Derakhshanfard, Tala Salimzadeh, Amir Mollanejad, Abbas Mirzaei, Ali Asghar Pour Haji Kazem, Neda Dadashkhani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Information-centric Internet of Things networks play a vital role in modern systems due to the increasing number of connected devices and the massive volume of data generated. However, due to complex node interactions and diverse applications, these networks are prone to abnormal traffic behaviors that can jeopardize network performance and security. Despite numerous efforts to analyze traffic and identify normal and abnormal behaviors, existing methods often lack sufficient accuracy and experience performance degradation when faced with sudden traffic changes. Furthermore, many approaches require heavy computation, which is incompatible with the resource constraints of IoT nodes. Efficient behavior prediction in information-centric IoT networks is critical for optimizing energy use and maintaining network stability. This paper proposes a Support Vector Machine (SVM)-based model that classifies node behavior as normal or abnormal using features like packet reception rate, delay, and energy consumption. The model is evaluated on the MDC dataset (10,000 traffic samples from 500 nodes) and the public UNSW-NB15 dataset. Compared to Support Vector Regression (SVR) and SVM with denoising, the proposed model improves accuracy by 12% and 9%, respectively. It achieves a 15% higher F1-score and maintains stable performance under increasing delay and packet load. Statistical tests (Wilcoxon, Friedman) confirm the significance of performance differences.

Introduction

The rapid advancement of communication technologies and the widespread adoption of the Internet of Things (IoT) have introduced new challenges in network management. In IoT environments, large volumes of data are exchanged among numerous devices and sensors, requiring efficient communication and stable node interactions. As a result, optimizing data transmission paths and minimizing energy consumption have become critical priorities for maintaining system performance and longevity. Traditional statistical analysis techniques are increasingly inadequate for managing the complexity and scale of modern IoT networks. They struggle to handle large datasets and fail to capture intricate patterns in node behavior1. In contrast, machine learning methods offer promising solutions for behavior prediction and traffic classification, enabling the differentiation between normal and abnormal network activity to support better resource allocation and system security. In information-centric IoT networks, normal traffic behavior typically consists of regular, predictable data exchanges that reflect standard operations. Conversely, abnormal behaviors may include sudden traffic spikes, malicious activities (e.g., flooding attacks), or performance anomalies due to node faults2 or congestion. Detecting these deviations is essential to prevent service disruptions and safeguard network3 integrity. Among machine learning techniques, Support Vector Machines (SVMs)4 are particularly well-suited for traffic classification tasks. They effectively model nonlinear relationships and construct optimal decision boundaries in high-dimensional feature spaces. By identifying such patterns, SVMs contribute to energy-efficient routing5 and improved system performance6. However, current methods face limitations. Some approaches only consider basic features7 such as the number of sent and received packets, ignoring the impact of energy consumption and delay on actual node behavior1. Additionally, most methods cannot operate in real-time with large-scale data8. Methods like Graph Neural Networks and Convolutional Networks show lower accuracy when dealing with dynamic data9,10. Hybrid methods, such as neural networks with differential evolution, are time-consuming11, and deep reinforcement learning requires significant processing resources for edge computing12. Furthermore, information-centric approaches and multi-access edge computing systems have limited efficiency under data congestion and complex configurations. For short-term traffic flow prediction, approaches like k-nearest neighbors ( k-NN: k-nearest neighbors.) and KD-trees have been proposed. Still, they show reduced accuracy in noisy and complex traffic patterns and are sensitive to the optimal choice of k. Other methods utilize lightweight machine learning classifiers like linear SVMs and decision trees for IoT traffic classification. However, these methods exhibit lower accuracy and poorer performance with complex and noisy data due to their simple kernels and inability to model nonlinear relationships13. Some approaches use GNNs (GNNs: Graph Neural Networks.) for predicting diverse edge IoT data14 traffic but perform poorly due to high computational complexity and reliance on fine-tuned parameters when handling outliers and dynamic conditions15. Decision-making models that utilize multi-source data and ensemble learning techniques provide high accuracy but suffer from input data quality sensitivity, high computational complexity, and the need for precise hyperparameter tuning, which reduces their effectiveness on large, dynamic datasets16. In conclusion, some methods utilize Graph Neural Networks and attention-based models such as STCGAT (STCGAT: Spatio-Temporal Convolutional Graph Attention Network.) to learn spatiotemporal relationships. Still, they have drawbacks such as high computational complexity, the need for high-quality data, and fine-tuning of parameters17. This paper introduces an SVM-based prediction model18,19 that analyzes node behavior and traffic patterns in information-centric IoT networks. The model uses advanced features and real-world data to address key challenges related to traffic management and optimization in these networks. The proposed model is designed based on real-world datasets, which include rich and diverse information on IoT device behavior under various conditions. Unlike traditional methods that primarily analyze basic features like packet counts, the proposed model incorporates key features such as delay, energy consumption, packet reception rates, and node interactions. Experimental results conducted in real environments with large datasets show that the proposed model outperforms similar methods in predicting and detecting abnormal behaviors. The model effectively identifies anomalies such as malicious traffic or nodes that deviate from standard behavior patterns. A key feature of this model is its acceptable real-time performance and scalability, making it suitable for practical use in complex IoT networks. In summary, With the rapid expansion of Internet of Things devices, traffic analysis in these networks has become a fundamental challenge. Methods based on Graph Neural Networks and Convolutional Neural Networks20 exhibit strong performance; however, due to their high computational requirements, they are not optimal for IoT environments with resource constraints. In contrast, Support Vector Machine and Support Vector Regression can be suitable alternatives due to their high efficiency in smaller datasets and lower energy consumption. This paper proposes an optimized SVM-based model for analyzing and predicting IoT traffic. It compares the proposed model with two other approaches, including Support Vector Regression and SVM with noise reduction. Furthermore, non-parametric statistical methods are employed to assess the significance of the results.

Despite significant advancements in anomaly detection for IoT networks, several limitations remain in existing approaches. Many deep learning-based methods achieve high detection accuracy but suffer from high computational complexity and energy consumption, making them unsuitable for resource-constrained IoT environments. On the other hand, traditional machine learning models often lack the ability to effectively capture complex non-linear patterns in network traffic. Furthermore, existing studies frequently overlook the trade-off between detection accuracy and energy efficiency, which is critical in real-world IoT deployments. To address these gaps, this study proposes an optimized SVM-based approach that balances detection performance with computational efficiency. By leveraging a carefully selected set of features and an RBF kernel, the proposed method captures non-linear relationships while maintaining low energy consumption, making it suitable for practical IoT scenarios.

Section 5 discusses theoretical and practical implications, and finally, Sect. 6 provides conclusions and future research suggestions.

Related work

With the rapid growth of IoT and the increasing volume of data, traffic management and energy optimization in IoT networks have become significant challenges. Consequently, machine learning algorithms and optimization techniques have gained attention to improve network performance. For example, Support Vector Machines and Support Vector Regression (SVR) have been utilized to predict traffic patterns and optimize data routes due to their high accuracy21. Studies show that SVM-based traffic prediction accurately forecasts22 traffic patterns and identifies optimal routes, reducing energy consumption and increasing network efficiency23. However, the computational complexity of SVM remains a challenge, particularly at large scales. In addition, research has explored graph neural networks and convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to optimize traffic routes and reduce energy consumption. These methods have shown effectiveness in handling heavy traffic loads, although they struggle when network data continuously changes9,23. In smart cities, backpropagation(BP) neural networks combined with Differential Evolution(DE) algorithms have been proposed for traffic prediction. This approach has improved prediction accuracy and reduced error rates compared to traditional backpropagation models, making it suitable for intelligent network management in urban areas11. Furthermore, edge computing and deep reinforcement learning(DRL) are crucial in energy-efficient IoT management. Edge-based deep reinforcement learning(Edge DRL) systems effectively reduce delays and optimize energy consumption by leveraging local processing. Methods like edge deep reinforcement learning, scheduling, and collaborative deep reinforcement learning(Cooperative DRL) significantly lower energy costs and delays compared to traditional methods12. Information-centric approaches have also shown success in enhancing data sharing and caching. These methods use optimal node selection algorithms to minimize delays and energy consumption. Techniques such as unicast, instead of multicast, improve the efficiency of IoT networks10. Combining machine learning, edge computing, and information-centric methods significantly improves energy efficiency and network performance. These studies highlight that these technologies enhance energy efficiency, reduce delays, and improve traffic prediction accuracy in IoT networks10,12. Recent studies emphasize the importance of deep reinforcement learning in optimizing energy consumption and resource allocation in multi-access edge computing(MEC) systems. One study proposed a two-tier framework for optimizing energy consumption and task completion rates. This framework uses the Double Neighbor Deep Q-Networks (D2N-DQN) algorithm to solve decision-making problems in computational and communication24 resource allocation. By adding adaptive noise, the algorithm’s learning efficiency is improved. Simulation results show that this method outperforms other reinforcement learning approaches like Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient(DDPG) and deep Q-learning(DQN) in reducing energy consumption and managing tasks, effectively leveraging horizontal and vertical collaboration between edge servers and cloud servers25–27. Another study addresses energy optimization in IoT within edge computing environments. The authors presented a new method based on deep reinforcement learning and Dynamic Voltage and Frequency Scaling(DVFS) to reduce IoT device energy consumption while maintaining precise scheduling28 for delay-sensitive applications. Experiments on devices such as Raspberry Pi and NVIDIA Jetson Nano show significant energy reductions compared to traditional methods, meeting the computational and timing requirements of IoT applications29. A study30 focuses on traffic flow prediction using a combination of denoising methods and SVM. The authors examined five denoising techniques, including Empirical Mode Decomposition(EMD), Ensemble Empirical Mode Decomposition(EEMD), Moving Average(MA) Butterworth Filter, and Wavelet Transform(WL). Results indicated that combining SVM with EEMD achieved higher traffic flow prediction accuracy than other denoising methods. Traffic data collected from three stations in Minnesota demonstrated a notable improvement in prediction accuracy using these denoising techniques. In addition to earlier studies, recent research continues to explore scalable, energy-efficient, and accurate models for traffic prediction and anomaly detection in IoT environments. For example, Ogunkan et al. (2024) combined IoT sensors (e.g., GPS, LIDAR, and cameras) with deep learning models such as CNNs and LSTMs to achieve real-time traffic pattern recognition in urban contexts, though infrastructure cost remains a concern31. Farooqi et al. (2024) proposed a lightweight botnet detection system using decision trees and correlation-based feature selection, achieving 99.98% accuracy with low processing time on the IoT-23 dataset, making it suitable for fog and edge environments32. Aouedi et al. (2025) provided a comprehensive review and empirical analysis of deep learning models for network traffic prediction, highlighting their strengths in spatiotemporal modeling across benchmark datasets33. Other recent efforts focus on lightweight, deployable machine learning solutions for IoT networks. Sharma et al. (2024) developed a multiclass detection framework using optimized decision trees and boosting algorithms on the CICIoT 2023 dataset, achieving 99.56% accuracy while remaining suitable for low-resource devices34. Patel et al. (2024) proposed a real-time anomaly detection system using AWS IoT Core and lightweight classifiers (e.g., CART and Gaussian Naive Bayes), demonstrating deployment feasibility, though limited by synthetic data and protocol scope35. Davis et al. (2024) explored edge-level security using lightweight neural nets and classical ML models, emphasizing real-time detection and privacy-aware frameworks for personal IoT systems36. Alwaisi and Kumar (2024) introduced a TinyML-based intrusion detection system capable of detecting resource-constrained attacks in real-time with over 96.9% accuracy and minimal memory usage37. Most recently, Saleh et al. (2024) proposed a comprehensive machine learning and deep learning-based anomaly detection framework capable of identifying a wide range of attacks, including brute force, DDoS, and SQL injection, while supporting real-time, scalable deployment38. These contributions underscore the demand for adaptive, interpretable, and efficient models for constrained IoT contexts—an objective our proposed SVM-based solution is explicitly designed to address. Recent studies have proposed deep learning models for IoT traffic analysis, including attention-based networks like STCGAT17 and hybrid CNN-GRU frameworks for traffic prediction. While these methods demonstrate strong accuracy, they often incur high energy and computational costs, making them unsuitable for edge environments. For example, Chen et al. (2021)9 and Shen et al. (2024)15 illustrate the effectiveness of GNNs but acknowledge their limited performance in real-time conditions. Moreover, lightweight models such as Decision Trees16 and linear SVMs13 have been used for IoT classification, yet these lack the robustness to handle nonlinear traffic patterns or scale with large datasets. Few existing studies evaluate models under multiple real-world constraints such as delay, packet volume, and energy usage concurrently — a limitation this study addresses directly.

Despite advances in anomaly detection, most studies lack integration of statistical significance testing (e.g., Wilcoxon, Friedman) and do not provide cross-dataset validation to ensure generalizability. Additionally, there is limited work on information-centric IoT traffic, where data dissemination and caching behaviors further complicate behavior prediction.

Therefore, this work fills a critical gap by proposing a lightweight, statistically validated, and scalable SVM-based prediction model, optimized for energy-aware behavior classification in information-centric IoT networks. Some of these methods are summarized in Table 1.

Proposed method

With the rapid expansion of the Internet of Things and the increasing number of connected devices, efficient management of IoT networks has become one of the fundamental challenges. The growing data volume and traffic congestion in these networks lead to high energy consumption, reduced system efficiency, and congestion in communication paths. Moreover, traffic behavior in IoT networks is highly dynamic and uncertain, making accurate predictions essential for network performance optimization. Traditional traffic management methods, which rely on fixed rules or simple algorithms, are insufficient to handle the rapid and complex changes in traffic patterns. Therefore, advanced machine learning algorithms like Support Vector Machines, which can analyze complex data and predict traffic behavior, can address these challenges. The proposed method in this paper is designed to analyze node behavior and predict traffic patterns in information-centric IoT networks using the SVM algorithm. In this method, real-world data is loaded into a dataset, and after preprocessing, key features such as the number of received packets, delay, and energy consumption are extracted. These features are used as inputs to the model, and the SVM algorithm is trained with optimized parameters to identify normal and abnormal node behaviors. After training, the model is evaluated with test data, and metrics such as accuracy, sensitivity, and F1 score are used to measure performance. This method can analyze complex data and detect abnormal behaviors in real-time, such as malicious traffic or high-consumption nodes. The model overcomes the limitations of previous methods with features such as high accuracy, scalability, and the ability to handle large volumes of data. Experimental results show that this model offers superior accuracy and efficiency compared to similar algorithms and can be effectively used as a tool for IoT network management and optimization. The steps of the proposed method are as follows: Loading the dataset for SVM, Preprocessing data for the SVM model, Training the SVM model, Predicting node behaviors, Evaluating the model, and Optimizing the model and using predictions. Training the support vector machine

At this stage, after loading and preparing the datasets for the model, the training phase of the Support Vector Machine model begins. This phase is a key part of creating the prediction system because its goal is to find the best separating hyperplane that can divide the data into two categories: normal behavior and abnormal behavior.

Problem definition and support vector machine model

Initially, the SVM model must receive the input data, which includes two main features: the packet reception rate (x1), delay (x2), and energy consumption (x3). The goal of the SVM algorithm is to find a hyperplane that separates the data. This hyperplane can divide the data into two classes in a linear or non-linear manner. To achieve this, a mathematical equation must separate the hyperplane. In the SVM model, this hyperplane is defined as a line or an n-dimensional plane that divides all data in the feature space into two categories. Feature selection and contribution analysis

The selection of features plays a critical role in accurately detecting abnormal behavior in IoT networks. In this study, three key features were selected based on their relevance to network performance and anomaly characteristics: packet reception rate, delay, and energy consumption.

Packet reception rate reflects the reliability of data transmission within the network. A sudden decrease in this metric may indicate packet loss, congestion, or malicious activity, which are common indicators of abnormal behavior. Delay represents the latency in packet delivery. Abnormal increases in delay can be associated with network congestion, routing issues, or attack scenarios such as denial-of-service conditions. Energy consumption is particularly important in resource-constrained IoT environments. Unusual spikes in energy usage may indicate inefficient routing, excessive retransmissions, or malicious exploitation of network resources. Together, these features provide a comprehensive representation of network behavior, enabling the model to effectively distinguish between normal and abnormal conditions.

Kernel selection

One key advantage of the Support Vector Machine model is that it allows the choice of kernel type. The kernel enables the model to find separators in non-linear feature spaces effectively. If the data is not linearly separable, non-linear kernels can be used. The Radial Basis Function kernel is used for this dataset because the data may be non-linear and complex.

The Radial Basis Function (RBF) kernel was selected due to its strong capability in handling non-linear relationships within the data. In IoT traffic environments, node behavior is often influenced by complex interactions between multiple factors such as delay, energy consumption, and packet reception rate, which are not linearly separable. To justify this choice, preliminary experiments were conducted using different kernel functions, including linear and polynomial kernels. The results indicated that the RBF kernel consistently outperformed other kernels in terms of classification accuracy and F1-score, particularly under varying traffic conditions and noise levels.

Therefore, the RBF kernel was selected as it provides a flexible decision boundary and better generalization performance for complex IoT traffic patterns.

Discussion of results

The superior performance of the proposed SVM model can be attributed to several key factors. First, the use of the RBF kernel enables the model to effectively capture non-linear relationships within IoT traffic data, which are often missed by simpler linear models. Second, the selected features—packet reception rate, delay, and energy consumption—provide a comprehensive representation of network behavior. This allows the model to accurately distinguish between normal and abnormal conditions, improving both accuracy and F1-score.

In comparison, baseline methods such as SVR and SVM with denoising either lack the flexibility to model complex patterns or introduce additional computational overhead, which negatively affects performance. Moreover, the proposed model achieves better energy efficiency due to its relatively low computational complexity. Unlike deep learning-based approaches, which require intensive processing, the optimized SVM model provides a balance between detection performance and resource consumption. These factors collectively explain why the proposed method outperforms existing models in both accuracy and energy efficiency.

This study contributes to the growing body of research on lightweight machine learning models for intelligent IoT systems. It extends the application of Support Vector Machines by demonstrating their efficacy in highly dynamic and resource-constrained environments typical of information-centric IoT networks. The model’s robustness in handling nonlinear features such as delay and energy consumption, coupled with its ability to detect anomalies in real-time, supports its theoretical relevance in behavior modeling and traffic classification. Furthermore, this work reinforces the utility of traditional models (e.g., SVM) when carefully optimized, even in an era dominated by deep learning. Conclusion

This paper presented an optimized Support Vector Machine (SVM)-based model for traffic prediction and node behavior classification in information-centric IoT networks. By leveraging key features such as packet reception rate, transmission delay, and energy consumption, the proposed method accurately distinguishes between normal and abnormal node behaviors. Experimental results on both the MDC and UNSW-NB15 datasets show that the proposed method achieves 12% higher accuracy than Support Vector Regression (SVR) and 9% higher accuracy than SVM combined with denoising techniques. Additionally, it reduces energy consumption by 18% and improves the F1-score by 15%, demonstrating its suitability for energy-constrained and dynamic IoT environments. The model’s real-time performance, scalability, and minimal computational overhead make it an effective solution for practical applications such as intelligent traffic control, health monitoring systems, and smart city infrastructure.

Future research can explore the integration of deep learning techniques to further enhance the performance of the proposed model. Specifically, models such as Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks can be employed to capture temporal dependencies in IoT traffic data, which are not fully addressed by the current SVM-based approach. Additionally, Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) could be utilized to automatically extract complex feature representations from network traffic patterns, reducing the need for manual feature engineering. Hybrid approaches that combine SVM with deep learning models may also be investigated to leverage the strengths of both methods—namely, the efficiency of SVM and the representation learning capability of deep neural networks. These directions provide promising avenues for improving detection accuracy while maintaining efficiency in IoT environments.

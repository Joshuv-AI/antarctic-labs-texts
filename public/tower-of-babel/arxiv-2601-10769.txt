Nonlinear Scalar Interactions in the EMDrive: Petiau’s Elliptic-Function Approach
Mario J. Pinheiro

Abstract
The EMDrive, a controversial electromagnetic propulsion concept, challenges momentum conservation in standard Maxwell electrodynamics. We propose a beyond-Maxwell framework by coupling the electromagnetic field to a light scalar field, inspired by axion-like particle models and effective field theory. Using Guy Petiau’s 1958 elliptic-function solutions for nonlinear wave equations, we construct exact cavity modes and combine them via Jacobi addition theorems. These nonlinear modes produce an asymmetric momentum flux, suggesting a theoretical pathway for EMDrive thrust. We compute the stress-tensor asymmetry numerically and show that while standard axion-like particles yield negligible effects, light scalars beyond current constraints could produce measurable thrust. The framework provides testable predictions connecting EMDrive physics to dark matter searches.

III
Theoretical Framework

III.1
Scalar Field Coupling and Rationale

We extend Maxwell’s theory by introducing a light scalar field
\phi
that couples to the electromagnetic field via a gauge- and Lorentz-invariant interaction. The Lagrangian is:

\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}(\partial\phi)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}m^{2}\phi^{2}-g\phi F_{\mu\nu}\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}

(1)

Here,
F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}
,
\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}F_{\alpha\beta}
,
m
is the scalar mass, and
g
has dimension
[g]=\text{mass}^{-1}

Weinberg1995
. The term
g\phi F\tilde{F}\propto\phi\mathbf{E}\cdot\mathbf{B}
is parity-odd, inspired by axion-photon couplings
Peccei1977
;
Svrcek2006
.

The scalar field is motivated by:

•

Axion-Like Particles
: The
g\phi F\tilde{F}
coupling appears in QCD axion models and string theory

•

III.2
Deriving the Nonlinear Wave Equation

Varying the Lagrangian with respect to
A_{\mu}
gives:

\partial_{\nu}F^{\mu\nu}+g\partial_{\nu}\left(\phi\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}\right)=0

(2)

The scalar field equation is:

(\Box+m^{2})\phi=gF_{\mu\nu}\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}

(3)

Assuming
\phi
is heavy (
m\gg\omega
), we integrate it out at tree level
Georgi1993
:

\phi\approx\frac{g}{m^{2}}F_{\mu\nu}\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}

(4)

Substituting into the Lagrangian yields:

\Delta\mathcal{L}\approx-\frac{g^{2}}{m^{2}}(F_{\mu\nu}\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu})^{2}\approx-\frac{g^{2}}{m^{2}}(\mathbf{E}\cdot\mathbf{B})^{2}

(5)

For a TE
101
mode with
\mathbf{E}=(0,E_{y}(z,t),0)
, we consider an effective scalar field description
E_{y}\propto\psi(z,t)
. The modified wave equation becomes:

\frac{\partial^{2}\psi}{\partial z^{2}}-\frac{1}{c^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}\psi}{\partial t^{2}}+\omega_{0}^{2}\psi+\lambda\psi^{3}=0

(6)

where
\omega_{0}
is the cavity resonance frequency, and
\lambda\propto g^{2}/m^{2}
.

IV
Petiau Solutions and Cavity Implementation

IV.1
Elliptic-Function Solutions

Petiau’s 1958 work provides exact solutions to nonlinear wave equations using Jacobi elliptic functions
Petiau1958
. For the traveling wave ansatz
\psi(\xi)
with
\xi=z-vt
, Eq. (6) reduces to:

\frac{d^{2}\psi}{d\xi^{2}}+\omega^{2}\psi+\lambda\psi^{3}=0

(7)

where
\omega^{2}=\omega_{0}^{2}/(1-v^{2}/c^{2})
. For positive cubic nonlinearity, a bounded solution is:

\psi(\xi)=A\operatorname{cn}(B\xi,k)

(8)

where
\operatorname{cn}(u,k)
is the Jacobi cosine-elliptic function with modulus
k\in(0,1)
, and:

B^{2}=\omega^{2}+\lambda A^{2},\quad A^{2}=\frac{2k^{2}\omega^{2}}{\lambda(1+k^{2})}

(9)

The period is
4K(k)/B
, where
K(k)
is the complete elliptic integral
Abramowitz1964
. The modulus
k
tunes the wave shape from sinusoidal (
k\to 0
) to soliton-like (
k\to 1
).

IV.2
Wave Combination and Boundary Conditions

The nonlinear ODE does not allow superposition. Petiau uses the Jacobi addition theorem
Whittaker1927
:

\begin{split}\psi_{12}(\xi)&=A\,\operatorname{cn}(u+v,k)\\
&=A\,\frac{\operatorname{cn}(u,k)\operatorname{cn}(v,k)-k^{2}\operatorname{sn}(u,k)\operatorname{sn}(v,k)}{1-k^{2}\operatorname{sn}^{2}(u,k)\operatorname{sn}^{2}(v,k)}\end{split}

(10)

where
u=B\xi+\delta_{1}
,
v=B\xi+\delta_{2}
, with
\delta_{i}
being phase parameters.

For cavity implementation, exact boundary conditions for elliptic functions require numerical solution. We approximate the cavity mode using periodic boundary conditions with period
L=4K(k)/B
, which captures essential nonlinear behavior while acknowledging that real cavity boundaries would require full numerical treatment. This approximation is justified for studying the fundamental nonlinear dynamics, though quantitative predictions require complete boundary matching.

V
Momentum Flux Analysis

V.1
Modified Stress Tensor

The full stress-energy tensor for the coupled system is:

T^{\mu\nu}=F^{\mu\alpha}F^{\nu}_{\alpha}+\partial^{\mu}\phi\partial^{\nu}\phi-\eta^{\mu\nu}\mathcal{L}

(11)

For the effective scalar description in the traveling wave frame, we derive the relevant momentum flux component by substituting the integrated-out scalar field (4) into the stress tensor and transforming to traveling wave coordinates. After neglecting higher-order terms in
\lambda

T_{zz}=\left(\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\xi}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\omega^{2}\psi^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\lambda\psi^{4}+\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{2})

(12)

This expression captures the leading-order nonlinear contributions to momentum flux.

VI
Physical Mechanism and Conservation Laws

VI.1
Momentum Conservation

The apparent momentum violation is resolved by careful analysis of momentum exchange. The modified momentum conservation equation becomes:

\partial_{\mu}T^{\mu\nu}_{\text{EM}}=-g(\partial^{\nu}\phi)F_{\alpha\beta}\tilde{F}^{\alpha\beta}

(14)

where the right-hand side represents momentum exchange with the scalar field. The scalar field itself interacts with the cavity walls, providing the necessary momentum sink.

The spatial asymmetry arises from:

1.

Nonlinear field self-interaction via
\lambda\psi^{3}
term

2.

Phase-shifted wave combination breaking left-right symmetry

3.

Scalar field mediation of momentum to cavity walls

VI.2
Energy Conservation

Energy conservation requires that the thrust mechanism draws power from the electromagnetic field. The modified Poynting theorem including scalar coupling yields:

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot\mathbf{S}=-g(\partial_{t}\phi)\mathbf{E}\cdot\mathbf{B}

(15)

where
u
is energy density and
\mathbf{S}
is Poynting vector. The right-hand side represents energy exchange with the scalar field. For steady-state cavity operation, this energy loss is compensated by the external drive, maintaining overall energy balance while enabling momentum asymmetry.

X
Conclusion

Coupling the electromagnetic field to a light scalar generates nonlinearities that allow Petiau’s elliptic-function solutions to describe asymmetric cavity modes, producing a small, nonzero momentum flux. This offers a theoretical pathway for EMDrive thrust, grounded in EFT and axion physics. While standard axion-like particles yield negligible effects, light scalars beyond current constraints or enhanced detection in high-Q cavities could produce measurable thrust. The framework provides testable predictions connecting EMDrive physics to dark matter searches, with implications for future cavity experiments and fundamental physics tests.

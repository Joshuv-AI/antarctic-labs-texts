Defining an operational selectivity window for rare-earth flotation using a Box–Behnken design
Authors: Junhyun Choi, Gilsang Hong, Wantae Kim
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractRare-earth flotation in naturally occurring complex ores is governed by mineralogical heterogeneity, incomplete liberation, dissolved species, and dynamically evolving surface conditions, making single-point optimization of limited process relevance. In this study, an operational selectivity window was defined for a complex bastnaesite ore (Dong Pao deposit, Vietnam) using a design-of-experiments framework. A Box–Behnken design quantified the interactive effects of pH, hydroxamic acid collector dosage, and temperature on total rare earth oxide (TREO) grade and recovery. Response surface models revealed nonlinear interactions and identified a favorable operational selectivity window at neutral pH (pH 7–8) and moderate collector dosage. To complement the identified operational selectivity window with an independent system-level indicator, zeta potential measurements were performed for the bulk feed across the investigated pH range and for froth concentrate and tailings obtained under the selected operating conditions, revealing distinct surface-state differentiation between floated and non-floated fractions. Validation flotation tests within the operational selectivity window produced a concentrate containing 49.7% TREO at 87.8% overall recovery. These results demonstrate that integrating response-surface modeling, circuit-level validation, product mineralogical confirmation, and system-level electrokinetic differentiation provides a reproducible and process-relevant route for defining an operational selectivity window in naturally occurring complex rare-earth ores, rather than relying solely on single-point optimization.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-47746-6.Keywords: Operational selectivity window, Box–Behnken design, Rare-earth flotation, Hydroxamic acid, Zeta potentialSubject terms: Environmental sciences, Solid Earth sciences

Introduction

Rare earth elements (REEs) are essential components of advanced technologies, including high-performance permanent magnets, electric vehicle motors, and catalytic converters1,2. Despite their strategic importance, the global REE supply remains vulnerable due to the limited availability of economically extractable deposits and the intrinsic complexity of REE-bearing ores3. Among REE minerals, bastnaesite ((Ce, La)CO3F) is a primary source of light REEs and commonly occurs in close association with gangue minerals such as fluorite (CaF2) and barite (BaSO4)4. This mineralogical association can lead to overlapping flotation responses, narrowing selectivity margins and posing persistent challenges for efficient beneficiation5,6.

Extensive studies have investigated collector performance in bastnaesite flotation systems, with particular emphasis on hydroxamic acids, fatty acids, and phosphinic-type reagents7–11. These studies have provided valuable mechanistic insight into collector selectivity and flotation behavior; however, many were conducted using purified minerals or simplified systems, which do not directly capture the collective flotation response of naturally occurring complex ores under realistic processing conditions. More broadly, recent flotation studies have continued to emphasize the importance of collector chemistry, oxygen-containing functional groups, and collector–surface interactions in governing flotation response. For example, flotation kinetics and emulsified/mixed collector behavior have been analyzed to clarify collector effects on flotation performance12. In addition, interfacial interpretations of modified collectors based on Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) and X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS)13, simulation-based screening of collector structures and selectivity14, and studies on oxygen-containing functional groups and nonionic collectors using XPS and molecular simulation15 have further highlighted the broader significance of collector molecular structure and surface/interfacial evolution in flotation systems. In naturally occurring complex ores, flotation behavior is additionally shaped by heterogeneous mineral assemblages, incomplete liberation, dissolved species, gangue interactions, and dynamically evolving surface states during conditioning. As a result, flotation outcomes are often better interpreted as a collective system-level response to operating conditions than as the direct expression of idealized mineral–collector interactions. From an operational standpoint, this implies that process design requires not only identifying an optimum point but also defining a reproducible operational selectivity window that remains robust to feed variability and dynamic surface conditioning.

Selectivity in complex rare-earth ores is governed by coupled and nonlinear effects of pH, reagent dosage, temperature, and their interactions. These variables jointly influence pulp chemistry, reagent partitioning, and bubble–particle attachment, producing response patterns that are not captured well by conventional one-factor-at-a-time optimization. As a result, such approaches frequently fail to identify an operational selectivity window capable of delivering both high recovery and consistent concentrate quality16–18.

Design-of-experiments (DOE) methodologies provide a systematic framework to address these limitations by enabling simultaneous evaluation of multiple operating variables and their interactions. Beyond identifying mathematical optima, DOE-based approaches facilitate the construction of response surfaces that represent system-level behavior and support the delineation of an operational selectivity window under realistic processing constraints19–21. In complex ore systems, response mapping offers a practical complement to mineral-specific analyses by focusing on measurable flotation outcomes at the system level. Accordingly, the present study was designed not to re-establish mineral-by-mineral surface mechanisms, but to define a process-relevant operational selectivity window at the system level under realistic complex-ore conditions. To support this objective, DOE-based response mapping was combined with circuit-level validation, product mineralogical confirmation, and electrokinetic differentiation as an independent indicator of system-level surface-state evolution.

In this study, the system-level flotation behavior of a complex bastnaesite ore from the Dong Pao deposit (Vietnam) is investigated using a Box–Behnken DOE framework. The interactive effects of pH, hydroxamic acid collector dosage, and temperature on total rare earth oxide (TREO) grade and recovery are quantified using response surface models to delineate an operational selectivity window rather than a single optimum. To strengthen the process-level interpretation, the identified operational selectivity window was further examined using circuit-level validation, product mineralogical analysis, and comparative zeta potential measurements of feed, concentrate, and tailings. By integrating response-surface mapping, circuit-scale validation, product-level mineralogical confirmation, and system-level electrokinetic differentiation, the present study provides a reproducible process-level route for defining an operational selectivity window under realistic complex-ore conditions.

Experimental procedures

Bastnaesite ore

The bastnaesite ore ((Ce, La)CO3F) used in this study was collected from the Dong Pao mine (Lai Chau Province, northwestern Vietnam). The mineralogical composition was determined by X-ray diffraction (XRD; D/Max-2200/PC, Rigaku, Japan), indicating bastnaesite as the dominant rare-earth mineral, with barite (BaSO4), fluorite (CaF2), and quartz (SiO2) as the major gangue phases (Fig. 1).

XRD pattern of the Dong Pao ore showing bastnaesite ((Ce, La)CO3F) as the major REE mineral and barite (BaSO4), fluorite (CaF2), and quartz (SiO2) as dominant gangue phases.

The chemical composition of the ore was measured by inductively coupled plasma optical emission spectroscopy (ICP-OES; Optima 8300, PerkinElmer, USA). The total rare earth oxide (TREO) content was 10.69 wt% and consisted predominantly of light rare earth oxides (LREEs), including CeO2 (4.61 wt%), La2O3 (3.21 wt%), Nd2O3 (1.59 wt%), and Pr6O11 (0.91 wt%) (Table 1). Minor components, such as Y2O3 (0.06 wt%), Dy2O3 (0.02 wt%), and Gd2O3 (0.02 wt%), were detected at trace levels. The presence of Ca- and Ba-bearing components in the chemical analysis is consistent with the fluorite and barite phases identified by XRD, confirming that these gangue minerals contribute to dilution of the rare-earth grade.

Prior to flotation tests, the sample was crushed to − 8 mesh (2.36 mm) using a jaw and cone crusher and then ground to − 150 mesh (106 μm) using a laboratory mill to promote liberation of bastnaesite. The ground material was homogenized, air-dried, and stored in airtight containers to minimize changes in physicochemical properties prior to testing. Particle size distribution was measured using a Mastersizer 3000 (Malvern, UK), yielding a P80 of approximately 100 μm, which was used as the target grind size for laboratory flotation tests. After mixing with deionized water, the natural pulp pH was approximately 7, consistent with the weak carbonate buffering typically observed for fluorocarbonate-bearing ores.

Reagents

Octyl hydroxamic acid (OHA, C9H19NO2), sodium oleate (SO, C18H33O2Na), and dodecylamine (DDA, C12H28NCl) were used as collectors (Sigma-Aldrich; analytical grade, ≥ 98%). Aerofloat-65 (AF-65; Cytec/Solvay, USA) was used as the frother. Hydrochloric acid (HCl) and sodium hydroxide (NaOH) (Fisher Scientific; analytical grade) were used for pH adjustment.

Collector solutions were freshly prepared in deionized (DI) water prior to each experiment to minimize degradation (particularly for hydroxamic acid). HCl and NaOH stock solutions were diluted to approximately 1 wt% and added dropwise during conditioning to reach the target pH. All 15 DOE runs were conducted in randomized order to minimize systematic bias. The final pH was verified immediately before aeration using a calibrated pH meter (Orion Star A211, Thermo Fisher Scientific). The frother dosage (AF-65) was fixed at 50 g/t in all tests because this level provided stable froth formation and acceptable reproducibility under the present laboratory conditions, while minimizing excessive froth persistence and entrainment. Accordingly, frother dosage was kept constant throughout the DOE experiments so that the effects of pH, collector dosage, and temperature could be evaluated more clearly.

Flotation tests

All flotation experiments were carried out in a 1 L cell using a Denver Sub-A laboratory flotation machine (Denver Equipment, USA). For each test, 200 g of ore was suspended in 800 mL of deionized (DI) water. The slurry was conditioned at an impeller speed of 1600 rpm with an air flow rate of 1 L/min. Pulp temperature was controlled using a thermostated water jacket and monitored continuously with a digital thermometer. The flotation cell and impeller were rinsed thoroughly with DI water between runs to minimize cross-contamination.

The flotation procedure was as follows. The pulp pH was adjusted to the target value using dilute NaOH or HCl, after which the collector was added at the desired dosage and conditioned for 5 min. The frother (AF-65, 50 g/t) was then added and conditioned for 3 min. Flotation was conducted for 10 min under constant air flow, and froth products were collected throughout flotation (with timed scraping/collection at 30 s intervals). After each test, the concentrate and tailings were filtered, dried at 80 °C for 24 h, and weighed to confirm mass balance within ± 2%. Fixed operating parameters maintained constant across all experiments are summarized in Table 2. Each Box–Behnken design (BBD) run represented a unique combination of the three factors—pH (A), collector dosage (B), and temperature (C)—and runs were performed in randomized order to minimize systematic bias in model estimation.

Preliminary collector screening

A preliminary screening test was conducted to select the most effective collector among OHA, SO, and DDA under identical conditions (collector dosage 200 g/t, pH 7, 25 °C, and AF-65 at 50 g/t). Each test was performed in duplicate, and the mean TREO grade and recovery were used to compare performance. Relative flotation efficiency was evaluated based on the grade–recovery balance to identify a representative collector exhibiting stable system-level selectivity under the tested conditions. The best-performing collector was subsequently selected for DOE-based optimization to ensure that the response surface models were built within an operational selectivity window showing a measurable flotation response.

DOE design for optimization

System-level process optimization was conducted using a three-factor, three-level Box–Behnken design (BBD), with pH (A), collector dosage (B), and temperature (C) as independent variables. The design included replicated center points to enable estimation of curvature and interaction effects while avoiding extreme conditions that could induce non-ideal flotation behavior. Product handling and analysis

After each flotation test, the froth concentrate and tailings were filtered, dried at 80 °C for 24 h, and weighed to confirm mass balance. Zeta potential measurements

Zeta potential measurements were conducted to characterize system-level surface charge behavior and to provide an independent electrokinetic indicator supporting the operational selectivity window identified by flotation testing. Measurements were performed for the flotation feed as well as for the froth concentrate and tailings obtained under selected operating conditions.

For bulk zeta potential analysis, feed ore suspensions were prepared in deionized (DI) water at a fixed solids concentration of 100 mg/L and conditioned at controlled pH values (pH 4, 7, 9, and 11). The pH was adjusted using dilute NaOH or HCl, consistent with flotation experiments, and the suspensions were allowed to equilibrate for 10 min under gentle magnetic stirring prior to measurement. The pH was rechecked immediately before each measurement. Zeta potential values were measured by electrophoretic light scattering using a zeta potential analyzer (NanoPlus Zeta/Nano Particle Analyzer, Micromeritics Instrument Corp., USA), and each data point represents the mean of at least three independent measurements.

For comparative analysis of floated and non-floated fractions, froth concentrate and tailings collected from validation flotation tests were prepared as suspensions in DI water at the same pulp density and at pH 7. This pH was selected to represent the neutral operational selectivity window identified by the DOE. The samples were dispersed using mild ultrasonication for 10 min in an ultrasonic bath (40 kHz, 100 W) to minimize agglomeration while avoiding alteration of surface characteristics. The pH was rechecked immediately before each measurement. Measurements were conducted under identical instrument settings and laboratory temperature conditions (25 °C) to ensure direct comparability between samples.

The comparative zeta potential analysis was intended to evaluate surface-state differentiation between floated and non-floated fractions at the system level, rather than to attribute charge variations to specific mineral–reagent interactions. This design choice reflects the scope of the study, which is directed toward process-relevant system behavior in a multicomponent ore rather than mechanistic assignment on isolated mineral surfaces. Accordingly, these measurements were used to assess whether measurably different system-level surface states were associated with floated and non-floated products under the selected operating conditions, rather than to resolve a unique adsorption mechanism or surface-precipitation pathway.

Results and discussion

Preliminary flotation tests for collector selection

Preliminary flotation tests were conducted to select a representative collector for subsequent DOE-based mapping of an operational selectivity window for the complex bastnaesite ore. Three collectors—dodecylamine (DDA), sodium oleate (SO), and octyl hydroxamic acid (OHA)—were evaluated under identical conditions (pH 7, 25 °C, collector dosage 200 g/t, and AF-65 at 50 g/t). Each test was performed in duplicate, and mean TREO grade and recovery were used for comparison.

As shown in Fig. 2, the TREO grade of the concentrate increased from 10.55% with DDA to 16.05% with SO and reached 20.05% with OHA. In contrast, TREO recovery decreased from 94.15% (DDA) to 88.61% (SO) and 86.65% (OHA), demonstrating a clear grade–recovery trade-off among collector types. DDA promoted high overall recovery but limited enrichment, whereas OHA achieved substantially higher enrichment with only a moderate reduction in recovery.

TREO grade (%) and recovery (%) obtained using three collectors (OHA, SO, and DDA) under identical screening conditions (pH 7, 25 °C, 200 g/t collector, and AF-65 at 50 g/t). OHA yielded the highest TREO grade, whereas DDA yielded the highest TREO recovery, indicating a grade–recovery trade-off across collector types.

The relative performance of the collectors is consistent with prior observations that hydroxamic acids tend to provide higher selectivity toward bastnaesite compared with amine- or carboxylate-type collectors in rare-earth flotation systems24,25. In the present complex ore, this trend is reproduced at the system level under identical screening conditions, further supporting the suitability of OHA for subsequent DOE-based response-surface analysis. Although DDA produced the highest TREO recovery (94.15%), its concentrate grade was 10.55%, which was very similar to the feed grade of 10.69 wt%, indicating little enrichment and limited selectivity under the tested conditions. By contrast, OHA provided a substantially improved TREO grade of 20.05% while maintaining reasonably high recovery (86.65%), resulting in a more favorable grade–recovery balance. Because the objective of the subsequent DOE analysis was to define an operational selectivity window rather than simply maximize mass recovery, OHA was selected as the collector for further optimization. Accordingly, pH, collector dosage, and temperature were chosen as key operating variables for systematic investigation using a Box–Behnken design, with the objective of delineating an operational selectivity window for TREO grade and recovery in the complex bastnaesite flotation system.

Actual flotation experiments using DOE

Experimental data and reproducibility

Fifteen flotation experiments were designed using a three-factor, three-level Box–Behnken design (BBD), with pH (A), collector dosage (B), and temperature (C) selected as independent variables. Each experiment was performed in duplicate, and mean TREO grade and recovery were used for regression analysis. Figure 3 summarizes the mean responses with error bars indicating one standard deviation. The standard deviations were generally low for both TREO grade and recovery, although slightly larger variation was observed in a few runs, indicating acceptable experimental repeatability for response surface modeling.

Mean TREO grade (%) and recovery (%) for the Box–Behnken design experiments (duplicate tests per run). Error bars indicate one standard deviation.

Clear and systematic trends were observed across the investigated parameter ranges. At the acidic end of the pH range, both TREO grade and recovery decreased, whereas improved flotation performance was observed toward neutral conditions. At the alkaline end, a slight reduction in selectivity was observed. These pH-dependent response patterns are consistent with reported behavior of hydroxamate-based rare-earth flotation systems, which often show favorable performance in moderately neutral conditions26–30.

Collector dosage exerted the strongest influence on flotation performance among the three variables. Increasing the dosage from 100 to 300 g/t increased TREO recovery, whereas a further increase to 500 g/t produced diminishing gains and was accompanied by a reduction in TREO grade. This grade–recovery trade-off suggests an upper dosage region beyond which non-selective flotation becomes more pronounced, consistent with trends reported in prior rare-earth flotation studies26,31.

Temperature showed a secondary but generally beneficial effect within the investigated range. Moderate temperature elevation improved overall flotation performance, whereas its influence on selectivity was less pronounced than that of pH and collector dosage. Similar temperature-related improvements have been reported for bastnaesite and other rare-earth flotation systems32,33.

To visualize interactions among variables, regression-based response surface plots were generated at the center temperature (52.5 °C), as shown in Fig. 4. Both TREO grade and recovery exhibited nonlinear responses with respect to pH and collector dosage. These modeled response surfaces provide the basis for regression analysis and for delineation of an operational selectivity window in subsequent sections.

Among the investigated factors, collector dosage and temperature exerted strong positive linear effects on both TREO grade and recovery within the tested range, whereas the fitted quadratic terms indicate pronounced curvature of the response surfaces. For TREO grade, the fitted model indicated significant contributions from the linear terms, the AB and BC interaction terms, and the quadratic terms, demonstrating that enrichment behavior in the complex ore system was controlled by coupled and nonlinear operating-variable effects. For TREO recovery, the fitted model similarly showed strong contributions from collector dosage, temperature, the AB interaction term, and the quadratic pH term, indicating that recovery behavior was also governed by interaction-dependent and curved system-level responses rather than by simple single-factor trends.

From a flotation-chemistry perspective, this is reasonable because pH, collector dosage, and temperature simultaneously influence mineral surface charge, collector speciation, dissolved ionic environment, and gangue response. In a naturally occurring bastnaesite ore, these effects are not limited to bastnaesite alone, but also modify the interfacial behavior of associated gangue minerals such as fluorite and barite. The strong influence of pH and collector dosage can therefore be interpreted in terms of their combined effects on interfacial chemistry in the multicomponent ore system. At low collector dosage, reagent availability is insufficient to sustain both effective bastnaesite recovery and meaningful enrichment. At moderate dosage under neutral pH, a favorable balance emerges because bastnaesite flotation is enhanced while excessive gangue flotation remains comparatively limited. By contrast, further dosage increase tends to reduce concentrate grade, indicating that non-selective flotation and/or entrainment become more pronounced. This interpretation is consistent with the present mineralogical context, in which bastnaesite occurs together with fluorite, barite, and quartz, such that the overall flotation response reflects not only valuable-mineral recovery but also simultaneous changes in gangue behavior.

Figure 5 illustrates the combined effects of pH and collector dosage on TREO grade and recovery at the center temperature (52.5 °C). The contour plots indicate an operational selectivity window at neutral pH (pH 7–8) and around 300 g/t collector dosage, where grade and recovery are simultaneously enhanced. Outside this window, TREO grade decreased at higher collector dosages, consistent with progressively less selective flotation under excessive reagent addition. TREO recovery increased with dosage across most of the pH range, although the incremental gains diminished at the highest dosage level. Temperature primarily improved overall performance within the tested range without markedly altering the selectivity trends, further indicating that selectivity in the present system is governed more strongly by pH-dependent interfacial conditions and collector loading. These trends indicate that flotation performance in the complex bastnaesite ore cannot be interpreted in terms of single-factor behavior alone, but instead reflects coupled and nonlinear system-level responses among the operating variables.

Overall, the regression analysis indicates that flotation performance of the complex bastnaesite ore is governed by nonlinear system-level responses and interactions among operating variables. Rather than locating a single mathematical optimum, the fitted response surfaces define an operational selectivity window in which enrichment and recovery remain simultaneously favorable. This distinction is especially important for naturally occurring complex ores, where flotation performance reflects not only bastnaesite itself, but also the coupled effects of gangue minerals, liberation characteristics, dissolved species, and conditioning-dependent surface evolution. In this sense, the objective of the present study is not simply to locate a mathematical maximum, but to identify a robust operational selectivity window with practical process relevance. The fitted response surfaces therefore provide a quantitative basis for delineating a reproducible operational selectivity window for rare-earth flotation under practical processing conditions.

Experimental validation under the selected operating conditions

To verify the identified operational selectivity window, a validation flotation test was conducted at pH 7, 350 g/t OHA, and 55 °C. A rougher–scavenger–cleaner circuit was applied (Fig. 6), and operating parameters (including reagent addition strategy and conditioning/flotation times) were set to be consistent with the selected operating conditions.

Flowsheet of the rougher–scavenger–cleaner circuit operated under the selected operating conditions (pH 7, 350 g/t OHA, 55 °C).

Under the selected conditions, the rougher stage produced an initial upgrade of TREO, and subsequent cleaning improved concentrate quality by reducing gangue carryover. Scavenger flotation further reduced TREO losses to tailings. As summarized in Table 6, the final concentrate assayed 49.7% TREO at an overall recovery of 87.84%. The middling product contained 7.96% TREO, indicating partial upgrading that may be further improved through circuit optimization (e.g., additional cleaning or regrinding), whereas the tailings contained 0.65% TREO, consistent with effective rejection of non-valuable components under the applied conditions.

System-level surface-state validation by zeta potential analysis

Zeta potential measurements were used as an independent, system-level indicator to complement the identified operational selectivity window by comparing electrokinetic behavior of the flotation feed and the corresponding flotation products.

Figure 8a shows the bulk zeta potential of the feed as a function of pH. The multicomponent ore exhibited a clear pH-dependent trend, with zeta potential becoming progressively more negative as pH increased from acidic to neutral/alkaline conditions. This response reflects the collective electrokinetic behavior of the complex ore in deionized water, rather than that of an isolated mineral phase.

(a) Bulk zeta potential of the flotation feed as a function of pH, measured in deionized water. The feed shows a systematic shift from neutral values under acidic conditions to more negative values toward neutral and alkaline conditions. (b) Comparative zeta potential of the bulk feed, froth concentrate, and tailings obtained under the selected operating conditions (pH 7, 350 g/t OHA, 55 °C). The froth concentrate shows a reduced absolute zeta potential relative to the bulk feed, whereas the tailings remain comparable to or more negative than the feed, indicating system-level surface-state differentiation between floated and non-floated fractions.

To assess whether distinct surface states were associated with the floated and non-floated fractions under the identified operational selectivity window, comparative zeta potential measurements were performed for the bulk feed, froth concentrate, and tailings obtained from the validation test at pH 7 (Fig. 8b). Under identical measurement conditions, the froth concentrate exhibited a lower absolute zeta potential magnitude than the bulk feed, whereas the tailings remained comparable to or more negative than the feed. This electrokinetic contrast between concentrate and tailings indicates that floated and non-floated fractions developed measurably different surface states at the system level under the selected operating conditions. Importantly, these measurements are not used here to assign a unique adsorption mechanism at the mineral scale. Rather, the observed zeta potential contrast is interpreted as an operational electrokinetic signature accompanying successful separation. Although the present measurements do not resolve mineral-specific bonding or surface-precipitation pathways, they nevertheless provide independent process-level evidence of system-level surface-state differentiation between floated and non-floated products. Together with the DOE response surfaces and the circuit-level validation results, the zeta potential measurements provide complementary support that the identified operational selectivity window yields reproducible separation behavior in the complex bastnaesite ore system.

Conclusion

This study established an operational selectivity window for the flotation of a naturally occurring complex bastnaesite ore using a Box–Behnken design. The combined effects of pH, collector dosage, and temperature on TREO grade and recovery were quantified, and the results showed that favorable separation was achieved within a near-neutral pH range and moderate collector-dosage region rather than at a single exact optimum point.

The practical relevance of this window was confirmed through circuit-level validation using a rougher–scavenger–cleaner configuration, which produced a final concentrate containing 49.7% TREO at 87.8% overall recovery. Product mineralogy and zeta potential differentiation provided complementary support that flotation selectivity in the present system is governed by collective system-level responses involving valuable minerals, gangue minerals, dissolved species, and evolving surface states under operating conditions, rather than by simplified single-mineral behavior alone.

Overall, the present work provides a process-relevant framework for defining operational selectivity windows in naturally occurring complex rare-earth ores. This framework may be extended to other rare-earth ore types with different mineralogical associations, gangue assemblages, liberation characteristics, and variable responses to pH, collector dosage, and temperature. Future work may apply this approach to other complex rare-earth systems to determine how differences in gangue composition, liberation behavior, and operating-variable sensitivity influence the shape and robustness of the operational selectivity window, and to further integrate process-level optimization with detailed mineral-specific interfacial studies or larger-scale validation.

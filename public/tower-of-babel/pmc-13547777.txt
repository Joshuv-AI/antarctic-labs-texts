Nivolumab Plus Lenvatinib and Intra-Arterial Locoregional Therapy as First-Line Treatment for Unresectable Hepatocellular Carcinoma: A Real-World Study
Authors: Shanzhi Gu, Yang Zhao, Haixiong Yan, Muzi Li, Guowen Li, Manping Huang, Zhengping Xiong, Bin Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAlthough first-line triple therapy of programmed death-1 inhibitors plus lenvatinib and intra-arterial locoregional therapy has shown potential clinical benefit for unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), it has not yet been established as a standard treatment. This real-world study evaluated the efficacy and safety of first-line combination therapy with nivolumab, lenvatinib, and intra-arterial locoregional therapy (including transarterial chemoembolization or hepatic arterial infusion chemotherapy) in this population.MethodsThis retrospective study included untreated unresectable HCC patients who received nivolumab plus lenvatinib and intra-arterial locoregional therapy between 26 March 2020 and 17 October 2023. The outcomes included objective response rate (ORR), disease control rate (DCR), duration of response (DoR), progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), and safety.ResultsA total of 52 patients were included, the majority of whom were <65 years (86.5%) and male (90.4%). Most patients had compensated liver disease, with 92.3% classified as Child–Pugh class A. The ORR was 69.2% (95% confidence interval [CI]: 54.9%–81.3%) per mRECIST and 55.8% (95% CI: 41.3%–69.5%) per RECIST version 1.1. The DCR was 100% by both criteria. The median DoR was 12.1 months per mRECIST and 10.5 months per RECIST version 1.1. With a median follow-up of 22.0 months (range: 1.6–57.3), the median PFS was 16.8 months (95% CI: 12.9–20.5) per mRECIST and 16.6 months (95% CI: 12.8–20.2) per RECIST version 1.1. The median OS was 24.9 months (95% CI: 21.7–35.6). Treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) of any grade occurred in 45 patients (86.5%), with fatigue (30.8%) being the most common. Grade ≥3 TEAEs were observed in 17.3% of patients.ConclusionFirst-line nivolumab plus lenvatinib and intra-arterial locoregional therapy showed encouraging antitumor activity and a manageable safety profile in unresectable HCC.

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) represents the most prevalent form of primary liver cancer, constituting approximately 80%–90% of cases, and ranks among the leading causes of cancer-related mortality worldwide.1–3 Curative treatments for HCC, including surgical resection, liver transplantation, and local ablation, offer the potential for long-term disease control and cure in patients with early-stage disease.4 However, a substantial proportion of patients are diagnosed with unresectable HCC due to extensive tumor burden, unfavorable tumor distribution, vascular invasion, insufficient future liver remnant, or impaired liver function, thereby missing the opportunity for curative treatment.5–9 In the early era of systemic therapy, tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs), such as sorafenib and lenvatinib, are recommended as first-line options for advanced HCC; however, the efficacy of TKI monotherapy remains suboptimal.10,11 In this setting, dual immunotherapy and immunotherapy-targeted combinations have significantly reshaped the first-line treatment landscape for unresectable HCC and are now established as the standard of care.12–14 Despite these advances, only a minority of patients derive a durable clinical benefit, highlighting the need for further investigation in unresectable HCC.15,16 Recent studies have highlighted the importance of molecular regulators involved in immune modulation, tumor progression, and cell death pathways, providing additional insights into the biological complexity of HCC and highlighting the need for further optimization of therapeutic strategies.17–19

To further improve outcomes of unresectable HCC, the integration of locoregional and systemic therapies has been investigated. More recently, the triple therapy combining programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) inhibitors, TKIs, and intra-arterial locoregional therapy has emerged as a promising approach for improving survival outcomes.20–22 Previous studies suggest that the triple therapy can generate a “vascular-immune priming” tumor microenvironment via synergistic mechanisms: Intra-arterial locoregional therapy (transarterial chemoembolization [TACE] or hepatic arterial infusion chemotherapy [HAIC]) induces substantial tumor cell death and promotes the release of tumor-associated antigens, thereby initiating antitumor immune responses and priming the tumor microenvironment for immune cell infiltration; TKIs inhibits hypoxia-driven angiogenesis following intra-arterial locoregional therapy, normalizes aberrant tumor vasculature, and modulates the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment, which collectively facilitates immune cell infiltration and enhances the effector function of T cells activated by PD-1 inhibitors; PD-1 inhibitors restore T-cell activity by blocking the PD-1/programmed death-ligand 1 signaling axis, reversing T-cell exhaustion, amplifying intra-arterial locoregional therapy-induced immune responses, and potentially exerting indirect regulatory effects on tumor angiogenesis.20,21,23–28 Despite demonstrating encouraging efficacy,20,21,23–28 the triple therapy has not yet been recommended as a first-line option for unresectable HCC, and its efficacy and safety require further validation.

Lenvatinib, an oral multi-targeted TKI, has shown the potential for enhanced antitumor activity when combined with interventional therapies. The Phase III randomized clinical trial (LAUNCH) indicated that the combination of TACE with lenvatinib significantly improves clinical outcomes compared with lenvatinib monotherapy as first-line treatment in patients with advanced HCC.29 Furthermore, several studies have indicated that lenvatinib in combination with the PD‑1 inhibitor nivolumab can exert a synergistic antitumor effect.30–32 Notably, there is currently a lack of studies specifically designed to systematically evaluate the efficacy and safety of the triple therapy regimen containing nivolumab, lenvatinib, and intra-arterial locoregional therapy as a first-line treatment. Accordingly, we conducted a real-world study to assess this triple therapy in unresectable HCC, aiming to provide additional evidence to support the clinical application.

Material and Methods

Study Design and Patients

This single-center, retrospective, real-world study was conducted at Hunan Cancer Hospital and retrospectively analyzed clinical data from patients with unresectable HCC. All patients received first-line treatment with nivolumab in combination with lenvatinib and intra-arterial locoregional therapy, according to the prescribing information, between March 26, 2020, and October 17, 2023.

Patients were eligible for inclusion if they were aged 18 years or older; had clinically or histopathologically confirmed HCC; had at least one measurable unresectable lesion according to the modified Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (mRECIST) and RECIST version 1.1 (RECIST version 1.1); and had complete, traceable, and well-documented clinical records available for analysis. Patients with concurrent malignant tumors, a history of any locoregional or systemic antitumor treatments, including radiofrequency ablation, TACE, HAIC, radiotherapy, systemic therapy, or immunotherapy, or with incomplete clinical data were excluded.

This study was performed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, the international standards of good clinical practice, and applicable local laws and regulations. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Hunan Cancer Hospital (Rapid Review No. 10, 2026). Given the retrospective nature of the study, the requirement for individual written informed consent was also waived by the Ethics Committee.

Treatment Procedure

Intra-arterial locoregional therapy consisted of TACE or HAIC was performed, according to tumor status, hepatic function, and the patient’s overall clinical condition, at the discretion of the treating physician. TACE was performed by selective catheterization of tumor-feeding arteries, followed by intra-arterial infusion of chemotherapeutic agents mixed with lipiodol and subsequent embolization using embolic materials until arterial flow stasis. HAIC was performed through a catheter positioned in the hepatic artery using the institutional standard chemotherapy regimen. All interventional procedures were performed according to institutional standard operating procedures.

As part of the combination treatment strategy, systemic therapy with nivolumab plus lenvatinib was initiated 3 days after intra-arterial locoregional therapy. Nivolumab was administered at a dose of 240 mg every 2 weeks or 480 mg every 4 weeks, and lenvatinib was administered orally once daily at a dose of 12 mg for patients weighing ≥60 kg and 8 mg for those weighing <60 kg, according to body weight.

Study Outcomes

Clinical data were retrospectively collected from electronic medical records. The collected variables included sex, age, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status, body mass index, hepatitis virus status, tumor stage, tumor size and number, Child–Pugh class, presence of extrahepatic metastasis, and presence of macrovascular tumor thrombus. Tumor response, survival outcomes, and safety were subsequently analyzed.

The study endpoints included objective response rate (ORR), disease control rate (DCR), duration of response (DoR), progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), and safety. ORR was defined as the proportion of patients who achieved a best overall response of complete response (CR) or partial response (PR), while DCR was defined as the proportion of patients who achieved a best overall response of CR, PR, or stable disease (SD). DoR was defined as the time from the first documented CR or PR to PD or death. PFS was defined as the time from the date of enrollment to the first documented disease progression (PD) or death from any cause, whichever occurred first. OS was defined as the time from the date of enrollment to death from any cause.

Tumour response was assessed by investigators as per mRECIST and RECIST version 1.1. The initial radiological evaluation was performed 4 weeks after intra-arterial locoregional therapy (30 ± 7 days), followed by imaging assessments every 2 months during systemic therapy. For patients achieving CR by RECIST version 1.1 or mRECIST criteria, the assessment interval could be extended to 6 months. Imaging evaluations were primarily performed using dynamic contrast-enhanced magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), with dynamic contrast-enhanced computed tomography used for patients with contraindications to MRI (eg, implanted cardiac pacemakers). AEs were graded according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE), version 5.0.

Statistical Analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using the R software (version 4.5.1). Categorical variables were summarized as frequencies and percentages, and compared using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. Continuous variables were described as medians with ranges. The 95% confidence intervals (CIs) of ORR and DCR were calculated by the Clopper-Pearson method. PFS, OS, and DoR were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method, and the median survival times with corresponding 95% CIs were reported. Differences in survival outcomes between groups were assessed using the Log rank test. Independent prognostic factors for PFS and OS were identified by univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression analyses. Variables with a P value ≤ 0.1 in univariate analyses were included in the multivariate models. A P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Patient Characteristics

From 26 March, 2020 to 17 October, 2023, 54 patients were screened, of whom 2 were excluded due to concomitant intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma or other malignancies. Overall, 52 patients were included and analyzed for efficacy and safety. The median age was 53.8 years (range: 32.1–74.6), and most patients were younger than 65 years (86.5%) and male (90.4%; Table 1). Among the included patients, 23 (44.2%) had histopathologically confirmed HCC based on biopsy or surgical specimens, whereas the remaining 29 patients (55.8%) were diagnosed clinically based on medical history and imaging findings. 65.4% of patients had an ECOG performance status of 1. Hepatitis B virus infection was present in 43 patients (82.7%), and hepatitis C virus infection was observed in 1 patient (1.9%). According to the BCLC staging system, 3 patients (5.7%) were classified as stage A, 11 (21.2%) as stage B, and 38 (73.1%) as stage C. The majority of patients were Child-Pugh class A (92.3%). Macrovascular tumor thrombus was observed in 65.4% of patients. Regarding tumor numbers, 28.8% of patients had a single tumor lesion, whereas 71.2% presented with multiple lesions. Efficacy

According to the mRECIST, CR, PR, and SD were achieved as the best response in 23.1% (12/52), 46.2% (24/52), and 30.8% of patients (16/52), respectively, resulting in an ORR of 69.2% (95% CI: 54.9%–81.3%) and a DCR of 100% (95% CI: 93.2%–100.0%; Table 2 and Figure 1). According to RECIST version 1.1, the ORR in the overall population was 55.8% (95% CI: 41.3%–69.5%), with a DCR of 100% (95% CI: 93.2%-100.0%). By data cut-off (January 6, 2025), the median follow-up time for all patients was 22.0 months (range: 1.6–57.3). During the study, 39 patients (75.0%) experienced PD or death per mRECIST. The median PFS was 16.8 months (95% CI: 12.9–20.5), with a corresponding 6-, 12-, and 24-month PFS rates of 88.5% (95% CI: 80.2%–97.6%), 68.9% (95% CI: 57.4%–82.8%), and 26.3% (95% CI: 15.7%-44.1%; Table 2 and Figure 2C). Based on RECIST version 1.1, PD or death occurred in 40 patients (76.9%). The median PFS was 16.6 months (95% CI: 12.8–20.2), with a corresponding 6-, 12-, and 24-month PFS rates of 88.5% (95% CI: 80.2%–97.6%), 67.1% (95% CI: 55.4%–81.2%), and 23.0% (95% CI: 13.2%-40.2%; Figure 2D). PFS according to either mRECIST or RECIST version 1.1 did not differ significantly across most subgroups, including age, CNLC stage, macrovascular tumor thrombus, number of tumors, longest tumor diameter, and type of intra-arterial locoregional therapy (Figure 3A and B). At data analysis, 33 patients had died. The median OS was 24.9 months (95% CI: 21.7–35.6), and 6-, 12-, and 24-month OS rates were 98.1% (95% CI: 94.4%–100.0%), 86.5% (95% CI: 77.7%–96.3%), and 50.4% (95% CI: 37.9%–67.0%), respectively (Table 2 and Figure 2E). Subgroup analyses of OS showed no statistically significant differences among the evaluated subgroups (Figure 3C).

In the subgroup analysis according to the type of intra-arterial locoregional therapy (HAIC, TACE, or TACE plus HAIC), ORR and DCR were comparable among the three groups according to both mRECIST and RECIST version 1.1 (all P > 0.05; Table S1). Although no statistically significant differences in PFS were observed among the groups, the TACE group consistently demonstrated numerically superior PFS outcomes, followed by the HAIC group, and then the TACE plus HAIC group. Specifically, median PFS were 20.2, 19.0, and 12.9 months (per mRECIST) and 20.2, 16.6, and 12.9 months (per RECIST version 1.1) for the TACE, HAIC, and TACE plus HAIC groups, respectively. The corresponding 24-month PFS rates were 45.6%, 33.0%, and 9.6% (per mRECIST) and 46.2%, 30.8%, and 9.6% (per RECIST version 1.1). A significant difference in OS was observed among the three groups. The median OS was not reached in the TACE group, compared with 27.7 months in the HAIC group and 21.7 months in the TACE plus HAIC group (P = 0.013). The 24-month OS rate was highest in the TACE group, followed by the HAIC group and the TACE plus HAIC group (83.1% vs 59.8% vs 29.1%; P = 0.008).

Survival Outcomes and Associated Factors

Analysis of potential prognostic factors for PFS and OS is shown in Tables S2 and S3. Univariate analyses showed that PFS assessed by mRECIST was significantly associated with ECOG performance status (1 vs 0; HR: 3.33; 95% CI: 1.15–9.65; P = 0.026) and intrahepatic tumor distribution (bilateral lobes vs right lobe; HR: 2.29; 95% CI: 1.09–4.81; P = 0.029). When assessed by RECIST version 1.1, PFS was additionally associated with the presence of extrahepatic metastasis (yes vs no; HR: 2.08; 95% CI: 1.02–4.23; P = 0.045). Multivariate analyses showed that only the ECOG performance status (1 vs 0) was the independent prognostic factor for PFS, regardless of whether PFS was assessed by mRECIST (HR: 7.05; 95% CI: 1.50–33.24; P = 0.014) or RECIST version 1.1 (HR: 7.35; 95% CI: 1.52–35.60; P = 0.013).

Variables with a P value ≤0.1 in the univariate analysis for OS, including ECOG performance status (1 vs 0), intrahepatic tumor distribution (left lobe vs right lobe; bilateral lobes vs right lobe), macrovascular tumor thrombus (yes vs no), lymph node metastasis (yes vs no), and the longest diameter of the liver tumor (≥10 cm vs <10 cm), were included in the multivariate Cox regression model. Multivariate analysis identified ECOG performance status (1 vs 0; HR: 7.68; 95% CI: 1.23–48.02; P = 0.029) and intrahepatic tumor distribution (left lobe vs right lobe; HR: 12.00; 95% CI: 1.90–75.92; P = 0.008) as independent prognostic factors for OS.

Discussion

The present study reported real-world data on first-line treatment with nivolumab in combination with lenvatinib and intra-arterial locoregional therapy (TACE or HAIC) in patients with unresectable HCC. Our results demonstrated that this triplet regimen achieved encouraging antitumor activity, along with a manageable safety profile. Tumor shrinkage of target lesions was observed in nearly all patients, accompanied by a promising tumor response, disease control, and survival outcomes. These findings provide real-world evidence supporting that the combination of nivolumab, lenvatinib, and intra-arterial locoregional therapy may be a feasible first-line treatment option in unresectable HCC.

In the present study, the triple therapy regimen demonstrated an ORR of 69.2% and a DCR of 100%, with a median PFS of 16.8 months and a median OS of 24.9 months in patients with unresectable HCC. To better interpret our findings, we compared the efficacy outcomes of our cohort with those reported in representative first-line studies evaluating different TACE-based therapeutic strategies for HCC (Table S4). Although cross-study comparisons should be interpreted with caution because of differences in study design and patient characteristics, compared with previously reported outcomes from TACE alone or TACE combined with TKIs, which have generally shown more modest tumor responses and survival outcomes,33–40 our findings suggest that adding PD-1 inhibitors to TACE-based regimens may offer potential clinical benefit.

Recent evidence from prospective and real-world studies has further supported the potential role of first-line TACE-based triple therapy in HCC.23,33–48 The LEAP-012 trial demonstrated improved clinical outcomes with the addition of pembrolizumab plus lenvatinib to TACE compared with TACE plus placebo in patients with unresectable, non-metastatic HCC.41 Similarly, the CHANCE2202 target trial emulation study and a recent systematic review and meta-analysis suggested favorable tumor control and survival outcomes with TACE plus ICIs and TKIs.33,48 Among retrospective cohorts, a study evaluating TACE plus lenvatinib and sintilimab reported similar efficacy outcomes to those observed in our cohort (ORR: 61.4%; median PFS: 13.2 months).42 However, the outcomes of triple therapy have not been entirely consistent across real-world studies.47 For example, a retrospective study comparing TACE plus ICI plus TKI with ICI plus TKI reported a relatively modest ORR of 21.6% in the triple therapy group, although this was higher than that observed in the dual therapy group.47 Such discrepancies may be related to differences in study design, patient characteristics, treatment regimens, and response assessment criteria. Therefore, the clinical benefit of TACE-based triple therapy should be interpreted with caution, considering the heterogeneity across studies.

Tumors with a diameter >10 cm have been reported to exhibit more aggressive biological behavior, including a higher frequency of extracapsular invasion into the liver parenchyma, increased intrahepatic metastasis, and worse survival outcomes.49–51 Although maximum tumor diameter was not identified as an independent prognostic factor in the multivariable analysis of the present study, subgroup analyses based on maximum tumor diameter (≥10 cm vs <10 cm) in the present study showed that patients with a maximum tumor diameter ≥10 cm exhibited numerically shorter PFS and OS, which is consistent with previous reports. In addition, patients with an ECOG performance status of 0 demonstrated more favorable survival outcomes, with ECOG performance status being identified as an independent predictor of both PFS and OS in multivariate analysis. Poorer functional status is inherently associated with worse prognosis and may reduce tolerance to combined therapies.52 Notably, intrahepatic tumor distribution was also an independent prognostic factor for OS. Patients with left-lobe tumors exhibited significantly poorer OS outcomes than those with right-lobe tumors (HR: 12.00; 95% CI: 1.90–75.92; P = 0.008), indicating that a tumor located on the left side may be an adverse prognostic factor in HCC.

Previous studies have reported that HAIC is associated with better efficacy and survival outcomes than TACE in patients with advanced HCC.53–55 In a retrospective study, combining TACE with HAIC demonstrated superior OS and PFS compared with TACE alone in patients with HCC complicated by portal vein tumor thrombosis.56 Given the differences in underlying mechanisms and clinical practice across multiple interventional therapies, their potential impact on treatment outcomes warrants attention. Although no significant association between different interventional modalities and OS or PFS was observed in the multivariable analysis of the present study, the Log rank test in the subgroup analysis demonstrated significant differences among the three groups in terms of median OS and the 24-month OS rate. Notably, the TACE group showed the most favorable OS outcomes, followed by the HAIC group and the TACE plus HAIC group. These observations indicate that the type of intra-arterial locoregional therapy may have a potential influence on survival outcomes. Nevertheless, the results of the subgroup analyses should be interpreted with caution owing to the small sample size.

In this study, the combination of nivolumab plus lenvatinib and intra-arterial locoregional therapy had a manageable safety profile, consistent with those previously reported for triple therapy.23,34,36,39,40,42,44 In the present study, TEAEs of any grade were reported in 86.5% of patients; most were grade 1–2 in severity and resolved after dose adjustments and symptomatic treatment. The incidence of grade ≥3 TRAEs in the present study was 17.3%, which was numerically lower than previous studies,34,36,39,40,43,44,57,58 suggesting that nivolumab plus lenvatinib combined with intra-arterial locoregional therapy had a better safety profile.

This study has several limitations. First, its retrospective, single-center design may limit the generalizability of the findings. Second, the relatively small sample size, particularly within subgroups, may reduce the statistical power of subgroup analyses, necessitating cautious interpretation. Finally, due to the non-randomized nature of the study, comparisons with other studies are indirect and may be confounded by differences in study design, baseline characteristics, and treatment regimens, and should therefore be interpreted with caution.

Conclusion

In conclusion, results from this real-world study suggest that nivolumab plus lenvatinib combined with intra-arterial locoregional therapy may provide a promising first-line therapeutic option for patients with unresectable HCC, warranting further validation in prospective studies.

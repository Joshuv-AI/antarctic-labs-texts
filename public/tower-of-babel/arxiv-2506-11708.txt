1.
Introduction and main results

We study the behavior of weak solutions to

(1.1)

\divv\nabla\mathcal{F}(x,Du)=f\qquad\text{in\penalty\ $\Omega$}.

Here and in the following,
\Omega
denotes a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(
n\geq 2
). The datum
f
is assumed to belong to
L^{n+\sigma}(\Omega)
for some parameter
\sigma>0
. For any
x\in\Omega
, the function
\mathcal{F}\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is required to satisfy certain regularity and structure conditions outside a bounded and convex set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
0\in\inn E
but vanishes entirely inside
E
. Outside of
E
, i.e. in the set of points
\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\xi\notin\overline{E}\}
, we assume
\mathcal{F}
to be nondegenerate with respect to the
\xi
variable, where the smallest eigenvalue of its Hessian
\nabla^{2}\mathcal{F}
degenerates either when approaching the set
E
from outside or for large values of
\xi
. We refrain from stating the precise conditions imposed on
\mathcal{F}
for the moment and rather refer the reader to Section
1.2
. It is a well-known fact from the theory of calculus of variations that equation (
1.1
) can also be interpreted as the Euler-Lagrange equation of the respective variational functional

(1.2)

\mathcal{L}(u)\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}\big(\mathcal{F}(x,Du)+fu\big)\,\mathrm{d}x.

We recall that any local minimizer
u
to the functional
\mathcal{L}(u)
in a suitable set of admissible functions is a weak solution to (
1.1
). The particular characteristic of equation (
1.1
) is given by the large set of degeneracy of
\mathcal{F}
. This circumstance is problematic in the sense that less regularity of weak solutions to (
1.1
) in general can be expected when comparing it to equations degenerating only at a single point, as it is for example the case with the well-known
p
-Laplace equation. In particular, no more than Lipschitz regularity of weak solutions can be expected in general. To illustrate this point, consider the prototypical example of widely degenerate equations that we always keep in mind, namely

(1.3)

\divv\bigg(a(x)\frac{(|Du|-1)^{p-1}_{+}Du}{|Du|}\bigg)=f\qquad\text{in\penalty\ $\Omega$},

where
p>1
denotes an arbitrary growth exponent and
a\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
denotes a Lipschitz-continuous function with the property that
0<C_{1}\leq a(x)\leq C_{2}
for any
x\in\Omega
and positive constant
C_{1}\leq C_{2}
. We note that the model equation (
1.3
) represents the Euler-Lagrange equation of the functional

(1.4)

\mathcal{L}_{p}(u)\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}\bigg(\frac{a(x)}{p}(|Du|-1)^{p}_{+}+fu\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}x,

where local minimizers of
\mathcal{L}_{p}(u)
are considered in the function space
W^{1,p}_{\loc}(\Omega)
. Accordingly, any
1
-Lipschitz function is indeed a weak solution to (
1.3
) in the case where
f\equiv 0
, resulting in a lack of higher regularity of weak solutions in general. In particular, the desired
C^{1,\alpha}_{\loc}(\Omega)
-regularity is not to be expected in general. Nevertheless, it is still possible to study the gradient regularity in the subset of points where
\{x\in\Omega:Du\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus E\}
, as the equation ensures regularity in these points. Notably, the gradient is indeed continuous within this subset. More broadly, as outlined in our main regularity result, Theorem
1.3
, the regularity of the composition
\mathcal{K}(Du)\in C^{0}(\Omega)
can be established for any continuous mapping
\mathcal{K}\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
that vanishes on
E
. The novelty of this article presents itself in two ways. Firstly, we allow non-autonomous functionals, i.e. the integrand
\mathcal{F}
may additionally depend on the spatial variable
x\in\Omega
. Secondly, we employ a proof technique differing from the methods utilized in prior research on this topic, such as
[
9
,
27
]
. Our approach is inspired by the pioneering works of De Giorgi, DiBenedetto, Friedman, and Uhlenbeck,
which have demonstrated considerable flexibility in previous applications.

1.1.
Literature overview

In previous works such as
[
3
,
5
,
7
,
19
]
, the local Lipschitz regularity of weak solutions in the domain
\Omega
has been proven for certain variants of (
1.1
), where the integrand
\mathcal{F}
is assumed to satisfy similar growth and ellipticity conditions to the prototype equation (
1.3
), subject to appropriate assumptions regarding the datum
f
. In view of higher differentiability or
C^{1}
-regularity of solutions, rather than investigating these properties for
Du
itself, more about regularity of the composition of
Du
with a suitable function
\mathcal{K}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
can be said. For example, it has been shown in
[
3
,
4
,
7
]
that the composition
\mathcal{K}(Du)
is weakly differentiable, where
\mathcal{K}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
denotes a certain nonlinear function vanishing on the closed unit ball. Similarly, any result regarding gradient continuity can never directly concern
Du
itself, since the equation does not provide information about regions where
\{|Du|\leq 1\}
and thus fails to capture any values in this case. However, for the quantity
\mathcal{H}(x,Du)\coloneqq\nabla\mathcal{F}(x,Du)
respectively for
\mathcal{K}(Du)
, where
\mathcal{K}\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denotes an arbitrary continuous function vanishing in the closed unit ball, some
C^{1}
-regularity results have been obtained. First shown by Santambrogio and Vespri in dimension
n=2
for a variant of equation (
1.1
), where the function
\mathcal{F}
does not depend on the variable
x
, they derived in
[
27
]
that the composition of any continuous function vanishing in the unit ball with
Du
is again continuous. This result was subsequently generalized by Colombo and Figalli in
[
9
]
for any dimension
n\in\mathbb{N}
and a more general bounded and convex set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
0\in\inn{E}
as the set of degeneracy, not necessarily being the unit ball. We note that these assumptions on the set of degeneracy
E
align with those outlined in this manuscript. Yet, the proofs found in both
[
9
,
27
]
are specifically designed for the scalar case and cannot be easily extended to accommodate the vectorial case, i.e. the case where
u=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
for some
N\geq 2
. In the context of the prototype example presented in (
1.3
) without Lipschitz continuous coefficients
a(x)
however, a similar result was demonstrated by Bögelein, Duzaar, Giova, and Passarelli di Napoli in their recent work
[
1
]
. Their approach, inspired by Campanato-type comparison estimates and a De Giorgi-type level set method, has proven to be highly flexible, allowing also the treatment of vector valued solutions. This result was later generalized by Mons in
[
25
]
who recovered the very same regularity result for vector valued solutions to a more general class of widely degenerate equations, with the vector field incorporated in the diffusion term assumed to satisfy standard
p
-growth conditions in the set of points
\{|\xi|>1\}
. Moreover, Grimaldi
[
22
]
extended the analysis to vector-valued solutions
u\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 1
, considering a more general structural setting. In this framework, the standard Euclidean norm in (
1.3
) is replaced by a norm that is induced by a bounded, symmetric, and coercive bilinear form defined on
\mathbb{R}^{Nn}
.
In this paper, we extend the approach established in
[
1
,
9
,
25
,
27
]
to equations that degenerate within a fixed bounded and convex set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
0\in\inn{E}
. Our approach does not rely on any specific growth or monotonicity conditions on
\mathcal{F}
outside
E
, and also includes the case where
\mathcal{F}
depends on the spatial variable
x
. The flexibility of this method suggests potential for further exploration, potentially culminating in a corresponding regularity result in the parabolic setting.

1.2.
Structure conditions

Throughout this paper, we consistently assume the following set of structure conditions to hold true. Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
denote a bounded and convex set with
0\in\inn E
. By
|\cdot|_{E}
we denote the
Minkowski functional
(sometimes also
Gauge functional
) on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, given by

(1.5)

\displaystyle|\cdot|_{E}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},\qquad|\xi|_{E}\coloneqq\inf\{t>0:\xi\in tE\},

where
tE\coloneqq\{tx:x\in E\}
. As already considered by Colombo and Figalli
[
9
]
in a very similar context, the Minkowski functional will also turn out expedient in our discussion throughout this manuscript. Several properties of
|\cdot|_{E}
shall be discussed later on in Section
2.2
. We consider a non-negative function

\mathcal{F}\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

that is subject to the following assumptions

(1.6)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathcal{F}(x,\xi)=0&\mbox{for any\penalty\ $x\in\Omega$,\penalty\ $\xi\in E$},\\
\xi\mapsto\mathcal{F}(x,\xi)\penalty\ \mbox{is convex}&\mbox{for any\penalty\ $x\in\Omega$},\\
\xi\mapsto\mathcal{F}(x,\xi)\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\overline{E}),&\mbox{for any\penalty\ $x\in\Omega$}\\
|\partial_{x_{i}}\nabla\mathcal{F}(x,\xi)|\leq C(L)&\mbox{for any\penalty\ $x\in\Omega$,\penalty\ $|\xi|_{E}\leq L$},\end{cases}

for any
i=1,\ldots,n
and any
L\geq 1
, where
C=C(L)
denotes a positive Lipschitz constant that depends on
L\geq 0
. Put differently,
\nabla^{2}\mathcal{F}
is assumed to be continuous outside of
E
with respect to the gradient variable
\xi
for any
x\in\Omega
, whereas
\nabla\mathcal{F}
is assumed to be continuous on the whole of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and also locally Lipschitz continuous with respect to
x
. However, the
C^{2}
-regularity of
\mathcal{F}
with respect to
\xi
may break down at the boundary
\partial E
of the set of degeneracy. Moreover,
\mathcal{F}
vanishes entirely for any
x\in\Omega
whenever there holds
\xi\in E
, whereas the partial mapping
\xi\mapsto\mathcal{F}(x,\xi)
is additionally assumed to be convex. Here, the operators
\nabla
and
\nabla^{2}
always denote the derivatives with respect to the gradient variable
\xi
, whereas
\partial_{x}
represents the derivative with respect to the spatial variable
x
. In addition, we assume that
\mathcal{F}
is elliptic outside the degeneracy set
E
. That is, for every
\delta>0
, there exist positive constants
\lambda=\lambda(\delta)
and
\Lambda=\Lambda(\delta)
with
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda
, such that

(1.7)

\qquad\lambda(\delta)|\eta|^{2}\leq\langle\nabla^{2}\mathcal{F}(x,\xi)\eta,\eta\rangle\leq\Lambda(\delta)|\eta|^{2}\qquad\text{for any\penalty\ $1+\delta\leq|\xi|_{E}\leq\delta^{-1}$}

for a.e.
x\in\Omega
. In other words, for any
x\in\Omega
the smallest eigenvalue of
\nabla^{2}\mathcal{F}(x,\xi)
is bounded below by
\lambda(\delta)
as long as
1+\delta\leq|\xi|_{E}\leq\delta^{-1}
, but may vanish in the limit
\delta\downarrow 0
. On the contrary, the largest eigenvalue of
\nabla^{2}\mathcal{F}(x,\xi)
is bounded above by
\Lambda(\delta)
, but can grow arbitrarily fast when
\delta
decreases.

As an example of an integrand
\mathcal{F}
satisfying the structural conditions (
1.6
) and (
1.7
), we consider the prototype given in (
1.4
), i.e.

\mathcal{F}_{p}(x,\xi)=\frac{a(x)}{p}(|\xi|-1)^{p}_{+},

where
a\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}_{>0})
denotes a Lipschitz continuous function with
0<C_{1}\leq a(x)\leq C_{2}
that satisfies
|\partial_{x_{i}}a(x)|\leq A
for any
i=1,\ldots,n
and a.e.
x\in\Omega
. Here,
A,C_{1},C_{2}
denote positive constants. The degeneracy set of
\mathcal{F}_{p}
is given by the unit ball, i.e.
E=B_{1}(0)
and the associeated Minkowski functional
|\cdot|_{E}
is exactly the standard Euclidean norm on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, i.e.
|\xi|_{E}=|\xi|
for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. A direct calculation verifies that there holds

\nabla\mathcal{F}_{p}(x,\xi)=a(x)\frac{(|\xi|-1)^{p-1}_{+}}{|\xi|}\xi,

which further implies that for any
i=1,\ldots,n
we have

|\partial_{x_{i}}\nabla\mathcal{F}_{p}(x,\xi)|\leq A(|\xi|-1)_{+}^{p-1}\leq A(L-1)^{p-1}

for any
x\in\Omega
and any
1\leq|\xi|\leq L
, where
1\leq L<\infty
. Moreover, we have

(1.8)

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}\mathcal{F}_{p}(x,\xi)=a(x)\bigg(\frac{(|\xi|-1)^{p-1}_{+}}{|\xi|}\mathbf{I}_{n}+\frac{(p-1)(|\xi|-1)^{p-2}_{+}}{|\xi|^{2}}(\xi\otimes\xi)-\frac{(|\xi|-1)^{p-1}_{+}}{|\xi|^{3}}(\xi\otimes\xi)\bigg)

for any
x\in\Omega
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. At this point, it is straightforward to check that conditions (
1.6
) are indeed satisfied. In fact, in the scenario where
p\geq 2
, the mapping
\xi\mapsto\mathcal{F}_{p}(x,\xi)
is twice continuously differentiable across all of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for any
x\in\Omega
. The sub-quadratic case with
1<p<2
presents a different situation as
\mathcal{F}_{p}
is only twice continuously differentiable with respect to
\xi
inside and outside the unit sphere, i.e. on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|\xi|=1\}
. In order to treat the full parameter range
p>1
in a unified way, we set

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1}(|\xi|)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\min\bigg\{\frac{(|\xi|-1)^{p-1}}{|\xi|},(p-1)(|\xi|-1)^{p-2}\bigg\}\scalebox{1.3}{$\chi$}_{\{|\xi|>1\}}

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2}(|\xi|)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\max\bigg\{\frac{(|\xi|-1)^{p-1}}{|\xi|},(p-1)(|\xi|-1)^{p-2}\bigg\}\scalebox{1.3}{$\chi$}_{\{|\xi|>1\}}.

Further, we infer from
[
1
, Lemma 2.7]
and (
1.8
), that there holds the ellipticity estimate

(1.9)

C_{1}\Gamma_{1}(|\xi|)|\eta|^{2}\leq\langle\nabla^{2}\mathcal{F}_{p}(x,\xi)\eta,\eta\rangle\leq C_{2}\Gamma_{2}(|\xi|)|\eta|^{2}

for any for any
x\in\Omega
, and
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For instance, when
p\geq 2
, the preceding estimate becomes

(1.10)

C_{1}\frac{(|\xi|-1)^{p-1}_{+}}{|\xi|}|\eta|^{2}\leq\langle\nabla^{2}\mathcal{F}_{p}(x,\xi)\eta,\eta\rangle\leq C_{2}(p-1)(|\xi|-1)^{p-2}_{+}|\eta|^{2}.

Now, given
0<\delta<1
, consider
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, such that
1+\delta\leq|\xi|\leq\delta^{-1}
. Estimate (
1.10
) establishes that conditions (
1.7
) are satisfied with constants
\lambda(\delta)=C_{1}\delta^{p}
and
\Lambda(\delta)=C_{2}(p-1)\big(\frac{1-\delta}{\delta}\big)^{p-2}
in the super-quadratic case
p\geq 2
. In particular,
\lambda
vanishes in the limit
\delta\downarrow 0
, whereas
\Lambda
becomes unbounded. Only in the linear case
p=2
, the eigenvalues of the Hessian
\nabla^{2}\mathcal{F}_{p}(x,\xi)
are bounded from above universally on the whole of
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
by
\Lambda=C_{2}
, while the smallest eigenvalue still vanishes when
\xi
is close to the unit ball. In contrast, in the the sub-quadratic setting
1<p<2
, a similar reasoning verifies that the reverse circumstance to the super-quadratic case
p>2
applies, and the smallest eigenvalue of
\nabla^{2}\mathcal{F}_{p}(x,\xi)
vanishes for large
|\xi|
, whereas the largest eigenvalue becomes unbounded near the unit sphere
\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|\xi|=1\}
.

1.3.
Definition of weak solution

At this point, we present our notion of a weak solution to equation (
1.1
).

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
satisfy the structure conditions (
1.6
) and (
1.7
). A measurable function
u\in W^{1,\infty}_{\loc}(\Omega)
is a local weak solution of equation (
1.1
) in
\Omega
, if the integral identity

(1.11)

\int_{\Omega}\langle\nabla\mathcal{F}(x,Du),D\phi\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x=-\int_{\Omega}f\phi\,\mathrm{d}x

is satisfied for any test function
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Remark 1.2
.

As already pointed out in
[
9
, Remark 1.2]
, the local Lipschitz assumption made on weak solutions in Definition
1.1
can be justified by imposing standard growth assumptions on
\mathcal{F}
. To state an example, the vector field incorporated in the prototype equation (
1.3
) satisfies standard
p
-growth conditions as long as
|\xi|>1
. Consequently, it can be shown that weak solutions are of class
W^{1,\infty}_{\loc}(\Omega)
, where we refer the reader to
[
3
,
5
]
in the scalar case and to
[
7
]
in the vectorial case. Due to the very general form of
\mathcal{F}
and the structure conditions (
1.7
), without additional information on the growth rate of
\xi\mapsto\nabla\mathcal{F}(x,\xi)
, such as
p
-growth, we cannot expect quantitative energy estimates for weak solutions to (
1.1
). Already to ensure convergence of the integral involving the solution
u
in (
1.11
), further knowledge about the qualitative behavior of
\mathcal{F}
for large gradient values would be necessary. Consequently, we focus on weak solutions that are
a priori
locally Lipschitz continuous in
\Omega
.

1.4.
Main result

The following theorem presents the main result of this paper.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
f\in L^{n+\sigma}(\Omega)
for some
\sigma>0
, and
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
denote the bounded and convex set with
0\in\inn{E}
on which
\mathcal{F}
degenerates. Further, let
u\in W^{1,\infty}_{\loc}(\Omega)
be a local weak solution to (
1.1
) in
\Omega
under the structure conditions (
1.6
) and (
1.7
). Then, there holds

\mathcal{K}(Du)\in C^{0}(\Omega)

for any
\mathcal{K}\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\mathcal{K}\equiv 0
on
E
.

As an immediate consequence of Theorem
1.3
, we obtain the following corollary for the composition of the vector field
\nabla\mathcal{F}
with the gradient
Du
.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let the assumptions of Theorem
1.3
hold true. If there additionally holds

\nabla\mathcal{F}\in C^{0}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n}),

then we have

\nabla\mathcal{F}(x,Du)\in C^{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n}).

1.5.
Strategy of the proof

In this section, we outline the essential steps leading to our main Theorem
1.3
. To begin, we introduce a truncation
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
of the integrand
\mathcal{F}
for values of
|\xi|
exceeding a certain threshold
L<\infty
. We then modify the truncation by adding a suitable convex function to obtain a convex integrand
\hat{\mathcal{F}}
. The threshold
L
aligns with the essential supremum of
|Du|
within a compactly contained ball
B_{R}=B_{R}(y_{0})\Subset\Omega
. Due to the local Lipschitz regularity of
u
in
\Omega
, we find that
L=\|Du\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}<\infty
. According to this construction, any local weak solution
u
to (
1.1
) is also a local weak solution to the redefined equation

\divv\nabla\hat{\mathcal{F}}(x,Du)=f\qquad\mbox{in\penalty\ $B_{R}$}.

Moreover, we enhance the diffusion term of equation (
1.1
) by incorporating the additive quantity
\varepsilon\Delta u
, with a small parameter
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
. This modification yields a more regular version of (
1.1
), with the integrand denoted by
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}
, that is elliptic across all of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with respect to
\xi
and avoids degeneracy within the set
E
. Of course, the ellipticity constant depends on the parameter
\varepsilon
in general. Furthermore, the obtained operator
\xi\mapsto\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}(x,\xi)
satisfies quadratic growth on the whole of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for any
x\in\Omega
. Subsequently, we consider the unique weak solution
u_{\varepsilon}\in u+W^{1,2}(B_{R})
to the weak form of the approximating equation on a ball
B_{R}\Subset\Omega
, with the Dirichlet boundary condition involving the local weak solution
u
of equation (
1.1
). The nondegeneracy of the regularized equation allows us to establish higher regularity of the form

u_{\varepsilon}\in W^{1,\infty}_{\loc}(B_{R})\cap W^{2,2}_{\loc}(B_{R})

for the approximating solutions. Moreover, due to the quadratic growth of
\xi\mapsto\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}(x,\xi)
for any
x\in\Omega
, we are able to acquire a quantitative local
L^{\infty}
-gradient bound for
Du_{\varepsilon}
, with the
L^{\infty}
-gradient norm essentially bounded by the
L^{2}
-gradient norm up to constants and an additive quantity. This expedient matter of fact further enables us to derive a local uniform bound for
\|Du_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}
with respect to the approximating parameter
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
in
B_{R}
. The main difficulty hereinafter lies in proving Theorem
3.5
, which establishes the local Hölder continuity in
B_{R}
of the function

\mathcal{G}_{\delta}(Du_{\varepsilon})\coloneqq\frac{(|Du_{\varepsilon}|_{E}-(1+\delta))_{+}}{|Du_{\varepsilon}|_{E}}Du_{\varepsilon}

for any
\delta,\varepsilon\in(0,1]
. Here, the Hölder exponent
\alpha_{\delta}\in(0,1)
and the positive Hölder constant
C_{\delta}
both depend on the data as well as on the parameter
\delta\in(0,1]
, but are independent of the parameter
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
. Despite the general structure of the function
\xi\mapsto\nabla\mathcal{F}(x,\xi)
not being of Uhlenbeck-type in our setting, it still turns out advantageous to consider
\mathcal{G}_{\delta}(Du_{\varepsilon})
. The mapping
\mathcal{G}_{\delta}
vanishes within a slightly larger set compared to
\xi\mapsto\nabla\mathcal{F}(x,\xi)
, and is strictly positive outside the complement
\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|\xi|_{E}>1+\delta\}
. As previously highlighted in
[
1
]
, it turns out expedient to consider the function
\mathcal{G}_{\delta}(Du_{\varepsilon})
since in the set of points where
\mathcal{G}_{\delta}
is positive, the equation (
1.1
) exhibits an ellipticity property, with an ellipticity constant that depends on the parameter
\delta\in(0,1]
. The proof of Theorem
3.5
involves a careful treatment of both the non-degenerate and the degenerate regime. In the non-degenerate regime, the set of points where
\partial_{e^{*}}u_{\varepsilon}
for at least one
e^{*}\in\partial E^{*}
is close to its supremum is large in measure, while in the degenerate regime, the set of points where
\partial_{e^{*}}u_{\varepsilon}
is far from its supremum is large in measure for any
e^{*}\in\partial E^{*}
. Here,
E^{*}
denotes the unit ball with respect to the induced dual mapping of the convex set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where the reader is for the moment referred to Section
2.2
for a more detailed explanation. In the non-degenerate regime, our goal is to establish a lower bound for

|Du_{\varepsilon}|_{E}=\sup\limits_{e^{*}\in\partial E^{*}}\partial_{e^{*}}u_{\varepsilon}

within a ball
B_{\frac{\rho}{2}}(x_{0})\Subset B_{2\rho}(x_{0})\Subset B_{R}\Subset\Omega
. We achieve this result by leveraging the measure-theoretic information that characterizes the non-degenerate regime and is assumed to hold true on
B_{\rho}(x_{0})
. By differentiating the regularized equation, we find that for any
e^{*}\in\partial E^{*}
, the function
\partial_{e^{*}}u_{\varepsilon}
is a weak solution to a linear elliptic equation. Notably, the ellipticity constant for this equation is independent of
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
and depends solely on the parameter
\delta\in(0,1]
as well as the given data, a consequence of the established lower bound for
|Du_{\varepsilon}|_{E}
. This fact enables us to utilize established excess-decay estimates for linear elliptic equations that are taken from
[
20
]
. In the degenerate regime, a sub-solution to a linear elliptic equation is constructed that is given by

v_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq(\partial_{e^{*}}u_{\varepsilon}-(1+\delta))^{2}_{+},

facilitating the derivation of a De Giorgi class-type estimate. This results in a reduction of the supremum of
\mathcal{G}_{\delta}(Du_{\varepsilon})
on a smaller ball, as outlined in Proposition
3.7
. By combining the results of Propositions
3.6
and
3.7
through considering a sequence of shrinking nested balls, one navigates between the non-degenerate and degenerate regimes, adapting the approach based on the measure-theoretic information at each step. The transition between regimes is pivotal, as it determines the course of the analysis in subsequent steps. If the non-degenerate regime applies at any given step, it will be maintained throughout. However, transitioning to a smaller cylinder in the setting of the degenerate regime results in uncertainty regarding whether in the next step the non-degenerate or degenerate regime applies. Once we have established the Hölder continuity of
\mathcal{G}_{\delta}(Du_{\varepsilon})
, an application of Arzelà-Ascoli’s theorem and passing to the limit
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
show that
\mathcal{G}_{\delta}(Du)
is also locally Hölder continuous in
B_{R}
, with the Hölder exponent and constant depending on the parameter
\delta
. By further taking the limit
\delta\downarrow 0
, it follows that
\mathcal{G}(Du)
is locally uniformly continuous in
B_{R}
. However, at this stage, the quantitative control over the Hölder exponent and constant is lost, leaving uncertainty about whether the limit function
\mathcal{G}(Du)
is Hölder continuous or what the optimal modulus of continuity may be. Eventually, the main regularity theorem
1.3
can be deduced from these results.

(f)_{A}\coloneqq\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.24571pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.65785pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.07782pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-4.91403pt}}\!\int_{A}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{|A|}\int_{A}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x,

where
|A|

n

|A|=\mathcal{L}^{n}(A)

A
is given by a ball
B_{\rho}(x_{0})

and center
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

(f)_{x_{0},\rho}\coloneqq\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.24571pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.65785pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.07782pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-4.91403pt}}\!\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

If it is convenient, we will omit the center
x_{0}
for brevity and simply denote the ball as
B_{\rho}
and the mean value of
f
on
B_{\rho}
as
(f)_{\rho}
. The standard scalar product on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
will be denoted by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
for any
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and the induced euclidean norm on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by
|\cdot|
. The dyadic product of two vectors
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

. The positive part of a real quantity

The expressions
\nabla\mathcal{F}
and
\nabla^{2}\mathcal{F}
represent the first and second order derivative of the function
\mathcal{F}(x,\xi)
with respect to
\xi
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus E
, whereas
\partial_{x}\nabla\mathcal{F}(x,\xi)
denotes the derivative of
\nabla\mathcal{F}
with respect to the spatial variable
x
. Instead of
\nabla\mathcal{F}
, we shall sometimes write
\mathcal{H}
and both notations

and

will be used interchangeably. Directional derivatives of functions

in direction

shall be indicated either by

or by

whenever the respective quantity exists either in the strong or the weak sense.

The following set of functions will appear multiple times throughout the article. We define

(2.1)

\mathcal{G}(\xi)\coloneqq\frac{(|\xi|_{E}-1)_{+}}{|\xi|_{E}}\xi\qquad\text{for\penalty\ $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$},

which has already been considered in a similar fashion in the previous articles
[
1
,
9
,
27
]
. Since the technique of approximation forms a major part of this paper, we will introduce a translated version of
\mathcal{G}
. For a parameter
\delta\geq 0
, we set

(2.2)

\mathcal{G}_{\delta}(\xi)\coloneqq\frac{(|\xi|_{E}-(1+\delta))_{+}}{|\xi|_{E}}\xi\qquad\text{for\penalty\ $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$}.

Obviously this notion implies
\mathcal{G}_{0}=\mathcal{G}
. We further remark that the function
\mathcal{G}_{\delta}
naturally arises in the context of the prototype equation (
1.3
) and takes an important role in
[
1
]
, where it simplifies to

\frac{(|\xi|-(1+\delta))_{+}}{|\xi|}\xi.

Even though our setting overall differs from the one in
[
1
]
, the function
\mathcal{G}_{\delta}
still turns out expedient in our investigations. The function
\mathcal{G}_{\delta}
will be employed in an approximation procedure to work around the degeneracy set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
of
\mathcal{F}
. However, as
\mathcal{F}
is only elliptic outside of
E
, further approximations are required in order to obtain an elliptic equation on the whole of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For an approximating parameter
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
, we define

(2.3)

\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(x,\xi)\coloneqq\mathcal{F}(x,\xi)+\varepsilon\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}|\xi|^{2}\qquad\text{for any\penalty\ $x\in\Omega$,\penalty\ $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$}

and note that
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
is indeed elliptic on the whole of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
in the case where
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
, where the ellipticity constant additionally depends on the parameter
\varepsilon
. Note that the quantity
\frac{1}{2}|\xi|^{2}
denotes the convex function of the
p
-energy in the case where
p=2
. Thus, in the general divergence form (
1.1
) of the equation, this approximation procedure represents an addition of the well-known Laplace operator
\Delta
with some scaling parameter
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
. Consequently, we let

(2.4)

\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}(x,\xi)\coloneqq\nabla\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(x,\xi)=\big(\mathcal{H}(x,\xi)+\varepsilon\xi\big)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\qquad\text{for any\penalty\ $x\in\Omega$,\penalty\ $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$}

and

\nabla^{2}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(x,\xi)=\big(\nabla^{2}\mathcal{F}(x,\xi)+\varepsilon I_{n}\big)\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\qquad\text{for any\penalty\ $x\in\Omega$,\penalty\ $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\overline{E}$}.

Finally, we define the following bilinear forms on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
that naturally arise from a differentiation of the weak form (
1.11
) of equation (
1.1
), where for
\eta,\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we set

(2.5)

\mathcal{B}(\cdot,\xi)(\eta,\zeta)\coloneqq\langle\nabla^{2}\mathcal{F}(\cdot,\xi)\eta,\zeta\rangle\qquad\text{for\penalty\ $x\in\Omega,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\overline{E}$}.

Moreover, the bilinear form naturally arising from the approximation (
2.3
) is given by

(2.6)

\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,\xi)(\eta,\zeta)\coloneqq\langle\nabla^{2}\mathcal{F}(\cdot,\xi)\eta,\zeta\rangle+\varepsilon\langle\eta,\zeta\rangle\qquad\text{for\penalty\ $x\in\Omega,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\overline{E}$}.

2.2.
On convex sets and the Minkowski functional

As introduced before, throughout this article we consider a bounded and convex set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
0\in\inn{E}
as the set of degeneracy of equation (
1.1
). The convexity of
E
is required as it induces a mapping on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which will be particularly important for the derivation of appropriate energy estimates in the course of Sections
3
–
5
. We consider th
Minkowski functional

|\cdot|_{E}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, defined by

\displaystyle|\cdot|_{E}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},\qquad|\xi|_{E}\coloneqq\inf\{t>0:\xi\in tE\},

where
tE\coloneqq\{tx:x\in E\}
. Moreover, the convexity allows the derivation of a triangle inequality and a generalized reverse triangle inequality for the Minkowski functional, as performed in Lemma
2.2
. In addition to the convexity, the property that
0\in\inn{E}
will turn out essential for the proof of Lemma
2.6
, as it guarantees the Lipschitz regularity of the Minkowski functional (
1.5
). However, note that
|\cdot|_{E}
is not symmetric unless the set
E
is symmetric with respect to the origin
0
. Concerning some properties of the
Minkowski functional
, we refer the reader to
[
26
, Page 109]
resp.
[
26
, Page 119]
for some properties of the latter.

Since
E
consists of points which satisfy
|\xi|_{E}\leq 1
, i.e.
E
corresponds to the unit ball with respect to the Minkowski functional, we also introduce its dual mapping

(2.7)

\displaystyle|\xi|_{E^{\prime}}\coloneqq\sup\limits_{|e|_{E}\leq 1}\langle\xi,e\rangle=\sup\limits_{e\in E}\langle\xi,e\rangle

as well as the unit ball with respect to the dual mapping

(2.8)

\displaystyle E^{*}\coloneqq\overline{B}_{1}^{|\cdot|_{E^{\prime}}}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|\xi|_{E^{\prime}}\leq 1\}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\langle\xi,e\rangle\leq 1\penalty\ \forall e\in E\}.

As outlined in
[
9
, Section 3]
, this yields the subsequent more appropriate representation formula of the Minkowski functional

(2.9)

\displaystyle|\xi|_{E}=\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\vskip-1.42271pt\\
e*\in E^{*}\end{subarray}}{\sup}\langle\xi,e^{*}\rangle=\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\vskip-1.42271pt\\
e*\in\partial E^{*}\end{subarray}}{\sup}\langle\xi,e^{*}\rangle

for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Of particular importance for our reasoning is the following consequence of (
2.9
): in several energy estimates we shall weakly differentiate a given equation in direction
e^{*}
, where
e^{*}\in\partial E^{*}
. This way, we will deduce information about the directional derivatives
\partial_{e^{*}}u=\langle Du,e^{*}\rangle
. Eventually, as
e^{*}\in E^{*}
can be chosen arbitrarily, we pass to the supremum over all
e^{*}\in E^{*}
to obtain estimates for the Minkowski functional
|Du|_{E}
of the (weak) gradient of
u
. We hereby identify the (weak) directional derivative in any direction
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
by

(2.10)

\displaystyle v=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}e_{i},

where
(e_{i})_{i=1,\ldots,n}
denotes the standard base of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
v_{i}
represent the coefficients of
v
with respect to the standard base. Then, for a.e.
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we have
\partial_{v}u=\langle Du,v\rangle
.
Finally, straight from the definition it is clear that
|\cdot|_{E}
is homogeneous for any
\lambda>0
in the sense that
|\lambda\xi|_{E}=\lambda|\xi|_{E}
holds for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Moreover, we have that
|0|_{E}=0
and, since
0\in\inn{E}
, also that
|\xi|_{E}<\infty
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Due to
0\in\inn{E}
and
E
being bounded, we find radii
0<r_{E}\leq R_{E}<\infty
and balls
B_{r_{E}}(0)\subset B_{R_{E}}(0)
, such that

(2.11)

\displaystyle B_{r_{E}}(0)\subset E\subset B_{R_{E}}(0),

where
r_{E}>0
is chosen as the largest of all radii
r>0
with
B_{r}(0)\subset E
, while
R_{E}>0
denotes an arbitrarily chosen fixed radius with the stated property. It now follows easily from the definition of (
1.5
) that there holds

(2.12)

\displaystyle\frac{|\xi|}{R_{E}}\leq|\xi|_{E}\leq\frac{|\xi|}{r_{E}}

for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. This estimate is crucial, since it allows a comparison between the standard Euclidean norm
|\cdot|
and the Minkowski functional
|\cdot|_{E}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Finally, for any
\delta>0
we define the outer parallel set of
E
with distance
\delta>0
by

(2.13)

\displaystyle E_{\delta}\coloneqq\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|\xi|_{E}\leq 1+\delta\}.

2.3.
Auxiliary material

In this section, a series of algebraic inequalities and further additional material is summarized.

We recall a version of Kato’s inequality.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
B_{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For any
u\in W^{2,1}_{\loc}(B_{R},\mathbb{R}^{k})
there holds

|D|Du||\leq|D^{2}u|\quad\text{a.e. in $\Omega$}.

The following lemma presents a triangle inequality and a generalized reverse triangle inequality for the Minkowski functional
|\cdot|_{E}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
there holds

(2.14)

\displaystyle|\xi+\eta|_{E}\leq|\xi|_{E}+|\eta|_{E}.

In particular, this implies the following reverse triangle inequality

(2.15)

\displaystyle||\xi|_{E}-|\eta|_{E}|\leq\max\{|\xi-\eta|_{E},|\eta-\xi|_{E}\}.

Remark 2.3
.

In general, the reverse triangle inequality established in (
2.15
) does not yield the standard reverse triangle inequality that applies to the Euclidean norm, since the Minkowski functional
|\cdot|_{E}
is not necessarily symmetric.

Proof.

The triangle inequality (
2.14
) quickly follows from the definition of the Minkowski functional (
1.5
) and the fact that
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded and convex set. The generalized reverse triangle inequality (
2.15
) is an immediate consequence of the triangle inequality (
2.14
).
∎

The fact that
0\in\inn{E}
allows us to deduce the following Lipschitz estimate for
|\cdot|_{E}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

For any
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
there holds the Lipschitz estimate

(2.16)

\displaystyle||\xi|_{E}-|\eta|_{E}|\leq\mbox{$\frac{1}{r_{E}}$}|\xi-\eta|,

where
r_{E}>0
is specified in (
2.11
).

Proof.

According to the construction of the radius
r_{E}
in (
2.11
), there holds
B_{r_{E}}(0)\subset E
. Thus, for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we have that

\frac{r_{E}}{2}\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}\in B_{r_{E}}(0)\subset E.

Hence, the definition of the Minkowski functional yields
|\xi|_{E}\leq\frac{2}{r_{E}}|\xi|
. By an application of Lemma
2.2
and by exploiting (
2.11
), we infer the claimed Lipschitz estimate

\displaystyle||\xi|_{E}-|\eta|_{E}|\leq\max\{|\xi-\eta|_{E},|\eta-\xi|_{E}\}\leq\mbox{$\frac{1}{r_{E}}$}|\xi-\eta|

for any
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

For any
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
there holds

\bigg|\frac{\xi}{|\xi|_{E}}-\frac{\eta}{|\eta|_{E}}\bigg|_{E}\leq\frac{R_{E}}{r_{E}}\frac{2}{|\xi|_{E}}|\xi-\eta|_{E}.

Proof.

By applying the homogeneity of
|\cdot|_{E}
, (
2.12
), Lemma
2.2
, and also Lemma
2.4
, we calculate

\displaystyle||\eta|_{E}\xi-|\xi|_{E}\eta|_{E}

\displaystyle=||\eta|_{E}(\xi-\eta)+\eta(|\eta|_{E}-|\xi|_{E})|_{E}

\displaystyle\leq|\eta|_{E}|\xi-\eta|_{E}+|\eta|_{E}|\eta|_{E}-|\xi|_{E}

\displaystyle\leq|\eta|_{E}|\xi-\eta|_{E}+|\eta|_{E}\mbox{$\frac{1}{r_{E}}$}|\eta-\xi|

\displaystyle\leq|\eta|_{E}|\xi-\eta|_{E}+|\eta|_{E}\mbox{$\frac{R_{E}}{r_{E}}$}|\xi-\eta|_{E}

\displaystyle\leq 2\mbox{$\frac{R_{E}}{r_{E}}$}|\eta|_{E}|\xi-\eta|_{E},

which yields the claimed estimate after dividing both sides of the preceding inequality by
|\xi|_{E}|\eta|_{E}\neq 0
and by exploiting the homogeneity of
|\cdot|_{E}
once more.
∎

The subsequent lemma states a relation between the mapping
\mathcal{G}_{\delta}
introduced in (
2.2
) and the Euclidean norm, which follows the idea from
[
1
, Lemma 2.3]
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\delta\geq 0
and
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then, there holds

(2.17)

|\mathcal{G}_{\delta}(\xi)-\mathcal{G}_{\delta}(\eta)|\leq 3\Big(\frac{R_{E}}{r_{E}}\Big)^{2}|\xi-\eta|.

Additionally, if
\delta>0
and
|\xi|_{E}\geq 1+\delta
, we have

(2.18)

|\xi-\eta|\leq 3\Big(\frac{R_{E}}{r_{E}}\Big)^{2}\Big(1+\frac{1}{\delta}\Big)|\mathcal{G}(\xi)-\mathcal{G}(\eta)|.

Proof.

We first establish assertion (
2.17
) and, as in
[
1
, Lemma 2.3]
, distinguish between different cases. Firstly, if
|\xi|_{E},|\eta|_{E}\leq 1+\delta
, the inequality is obvious since this implies
\mathcal{G}_{\delta}(\xi)=\mathcal{G}_{\delta}(\eta)=0
. If
|\xi|_{E}>1+\delta
and
|\eta|_{E}\leq 1+\delta
, we exploit (
2.12
) and Lemma
2.5
to obtain

\displaystyle|\mathcal{G}_{\delta}(\xi)-\mathcal{G}_{\delta}(\eta)|

\displaystyle=\frac{|\xi|}{|\xi|_{E}}(|\xi|_{E}-(1+\delta))

\displaystyle\leq R_{E}(|\xi|_{E}-(1+\delta))

\displaystyle\leq R_{E}(|\xi|_{E}-|\eta|_{E})

\displaystyle\leq\mbox{$\frac{R_{E}}{r_{E}}$}|\xi-\eta|.

The reverse case where
|\xi|\leq 1+\delta
and
|\eta|_{E}>1+\delta
follows in the same way. Finally, if
|\xi|_{E},|\eta|_{E}>1+\delta
, we employ the homogeneity of the Minkowski functional, (
2.12
), and Lemma
2.5
, which yields

\displaystyle|\mathcal{G}_{\delta}(\xi)-\mathcal{G}_{\delta}(\eta)|

\displaystyle\leq R_{E}\bigg|\frac{(|\xi|_{E}-(1+\delta))\xi}{|\xi|_{E}}-\frac{(|\eta|_{E}-(1+\delta))\eta}{|\eta|_{E}}\bigg|_{E}

\displaystyle\leq|\xi-\eta|_{E}+(1+\delta)\bigg|\frac{\xi}{|\xi|_{E}}-\frac{\eta}{|\eta|_{E}}\bigg|_{E}

\displaystyle\leq|\xi-\eta|_{E}+2\mbox{$\frac{R_{E}}{r_{E}}$}

\displaystyle\leq 3\mbox{$\frac{R_{E}}{r_{E}}$}|\xi-\eta|_{E}

\displaystyle\leq 3\big(\mbox{$\frac{R_{E}}{r_{E}}$}\big)^{2}|\xi-\eta|.

Next, we aim to verify the second assertion (
2.18
). If
|\eta|_{E}\leq 1
, we have
\mathcal{G}(\eta)=0
and, by proceeding similarly to before, achieve

\displaystyle\frac{|\xi-\eta|}{|\mathcal{G}(\xi)-\mathcal{G}(\eta)|}=\frac{|\xi-\eta||\xi|_{E}}{(|\xi|_{E}-1)|\xi|}\leq\frac{1}{r_{E}}\frac{|\xi-\eta|}{|\xi|_{E}-1}\leq\frac{R_{E}}{r_{E}}\frac{|\xi|_{E}+|\eta|_{E}}{|\xi|_{E}-1}\leq\frac{R_{E}}{r_{E}}\Big(1+\frac{2}{\delta}\Big)\leq 2\frac{R_{E}}{r_{E}}\Big(1+\frac{1}{\delta}\Big).

The remaining case where
|\eta|_{E}>1
is treated as follows: due to the construction of
\mathcal{G}
, this function is a one to one mapping from the set
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\overline{E}=\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x|_{E}\leq 1\}
to
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
. Thus, its inverse mapping exists on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
and, which is quickly verified, is given by
\mathcal{G}^{-1}(y)=\frac{(|y|_{E}+1)y}{|y|_{E}}
. Setting
\tilde{\xi}\coloneqq\mathcal{G}(\xi)
and
\tilde{\eta}\coloneqq\mathcal{G}(\eta)
, we infer through Lemma
2.5
and (
2.11
) the following

\displaystyle\frac{|\mathcal{G}^{-1}(\tilde{\xi})-\mathcal{G}^{-1}(\tilde{\eta})|}{|\tilde{\xi}-\tilde{\eta}|}

\displaystyle=\frac{\big|\tilde{\xi}-\tilde{\eta}+\frac{\tilde{\xi}}{|\tilde{\xi}|_{E}}-\frac{\tilde{\eta}}{|\tilde{\eta}|_{E}}\big|}{|\tilde{\xi}-\tilde{\eta}|}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\frac{R_{E}}{|\tilde{\xi}-\tilde{\eta}|}\bigg|\frac{\tilde{\xi}}{|\tilde{\xi}|_{E}}-\frac{\tilde{\eta}}{|\tilde{\eta}|_{E}}\bigg|_{E}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\frac{R_{E}}{|\tilde{\xi}-\tilde{\eta}|}\frac{2}{|\tilde{\xi}|_{E}}\frac{R_{E}}{r_{E}}|\tilde{\xi}-\tilde{\eta}|_{E}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\Big(\frac{R_{E}}{r_{E}}\Big)^{2}\frac{2}{\delta}

\displaystyle\leq 3\Big(\frac{R_{E}}{r_{E}}\Big)^{2}\Big(1+\frac{1}{\delta}\Big).

∎

The following lemma represents one of the main preliminary tools and states a monotonicity estimate for
\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}
that is an immediate consequence of the ellipticity assumption (
1.7
) for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
1+\delta\leq|\xi|_{E}
and
x\in\Omega
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
and
\delta>0
. Then there exists
C=C(\delta)
, such that for any
x\in\Omega
and any
\tilde{\xi},\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
|\xi|_{E}\geq 1+\delta
there holds

(2.19)

\langle\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}(x,\tilde{\xi})-\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}(x,\xi),\tilde{\xi}-\xi\rangle\geq\bigg(\varepsilon+C(\delta)\frac{r_{E}(2|\xi|_{E}-(2+\delta))}{2|\xi|_{E}(R_{E}+r_{E})}\bigg)|\tilde{\xi}-\xi|^{2}.

Moreover, for any
x\in\Omega
and any
\tilde{\xi},\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
the quantity on the left-hand side of (
2.19
) is non-negative.

Proof.

We commence by treating the first assertion, where we distinguish between two cases. Firstly, we assume
|\tilde{\xi}|_{E}\leq|\xi|_{E}
. For
s\in[0,1]
, we denote
\xi_{s}\coloneqq(1-s)\xi+s\tilde{\xi}=\xi-s(\xi-\tilde{\xi})
and compute

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}(x,\tilde{\xi})-\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}(x,\xi),\tilde{\xi}-\xi\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\Big\langle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathcal{H}(x,\xi_{s}),\tilde{\xi}-\xi\Big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s+\varepsilon|\tilde{\xi}-\xi|^{2}.

If
|\xi_{s}|_{E}\geq 1+\frac{\delta}{2}
for all
s\in[0,1]
, then we have that
\mathcal{F}
is of class
C^{2}
on
[\tilde{\xi},\xi]\coloneqq\{(1-s)\xi+s\tilde{\xi}:s\in[0,1]\}
. Moreover, in this case we may differentiate
\mathcal{H}
and use our ellipticity condition (
1.7
) to obtain

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}(x,\tilde{\xi})-\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}(x,\xi),\tilde{\xi}-\xi\rangle

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\langle\nabla^{2}\mathcal{F}(x,\xi_{s})(\tilde{\xi}-\xi),\tilde{\xi}-\xi\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s+\varepsilon|\tilde{\xi}-\xi|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\lambda(\delta)|\tilde{\xi}-\xi|^{2}+\varepsilon|\tilde{\xi}-\xi|^{2}.

Otherwise, the reverse triangle inequality (
2.14
) and assumption
|\tilde{\xi}|_{E}\leq|\xi|_{E}
imply

\displaystyle|\xi_{s}|_{E}

\displaystyle\geq(1-s)|\xi|_{E}-s|-\tilde{\xi}|_{E}

\displaystyle\geq(1-s)|\xi|_{E}-\mbox{$\frac{s}{r_{E}}$}|\tilde{\xi}|

\displaystyle\geq(1-s)|\xi|_{E}-s\mbox{$\frac{R_{E}}{r_{E}}$}|\xi|_{E}

\displaystyle\geq 1+\textstyle{\frac{\delta}{2}}

at least for all
s\in\Big[0,\frac{r_{E}(2|\xi|_{E}-(2+\delta))}{2|\xi|_{E}(R_{E}+r_{E})}\Big]
. To simplify notation, we set
\tilde{s}\coloneqq\frac{r_{E}(2|\xi|_{E}-(2+\delta))}{2|\xi|_{E}(R_{E}+r_{E})}
. If there exists at least one
\hat{s}\in(\tilde{s},1]
such that
|\xi_{\hat{s}}|_{E}\leq 1
, we may without any loss of generality assume that
|\xi_{s}|_{E}>1
holds for all
s\in[\tilde{s},\hat{s})
as well as
|\xi_{s}|_{E}\leq 1
for all
s\in[\hat{s},1]
. This simplification indeed poses no restrictions, since in the case where
|\xi_{s}|_{E}>1
for some
s\in(\hat{s},1]
, we follow the subsequent arguments verbatim and discard any possible contributions from additional applications of the ellipticity condition (
1.7
). For any parameter
\tau\in(0,\hat{s}-\tilde{s})
, there exists
C=C(\delta,\tau)>0
with
|\xi_{\tilde{s}+\tau}|_{E}\geq 1+C(\delta,\tau)
, and we rewrite the integral term above as follows

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}(x,\tilde{\xi})-\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}(x,\xi),\tilde{\xi}-\xi\rangle

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\Big\langle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathcal{H}(x,\xi_{s}),\tilde{\xi}-\xi\Big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s+\varepsilon|\tilde{\xi}-\xi|^{2}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tilde{s}}\nabla^{2}\mathcal{F}(x,\xi_{s})(\tilde{\xi}-\xi),\tilde{\xi}-\xi\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s+\displaystyle\int_{\tilde{s}}^{\hat{s}-\tau}\nabla^{2}\mathcal{F}(x,\xi_{s})(\tilde{\xi}-\xi),\tilde{\xi}-\xi\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+\displaystyle\int_{\hat{s}-\tau}^{1}\Big\langle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathcal{H}(x,\xi_{s}),\tilde{\xi}-\xi\Big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s+\varepsilon|\tilde{\xi}-\xi|^{2}

\displaystyle\eqqcolon\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{II}+\mathbf{III}+\varepsilon|\tilde{\xi}-\xi|^{2}

with the obvious meaning of the quantities
\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{III}
. The first term is bounded below by an application of our ellipticity estimate (
1.7
), where we exploit the fact that
|\xi_{s}|_{E}\geq 1+\mbox{$\frac{\delta}{2}$}
for any
s\in[0,\tilde{s}]
, which yields the bound

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tilde{s}}\langle\nabla^{2}\mathcal{F}(x,\xi_{s})(\tilde{\xi}-\xi),\tilde{\xi}-\xi\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s\geq\lambda(\delta)\frac{r_{E}(2|\xi|_{E}-(2+\delta))}{2|\xi|_{E}(R_{E}+r_{E})}|\tilde{\xi}-\xi|^{2}.

Similarly, again due to the ellipticity condition (
1.7
), for the second term we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{II}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{\tilde{s}}^{\hat{s}-\tau}\nabla^{2}\mathcal{F}(x,\xi_{s})(\tilde{\xi}-\xi),\tilde{\xi}-\xi\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s\geq C(\delta,\tau)(\hat{s}-\tilde{s}-\tau)\tilde{\xi}-\xi|^{2}\geq 0.

For the last term
\mathbf{III}
, we use the fact that
\mathcal{H}
is continuous across the whole of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and that
\mathcal{H}
vanishes within the set
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x|_{E}\leq 1\}
, and also the assumption
|\xi_{s}|_{E}\leq 1
for any
s\in[\hat{s},1]
. This yields

\displaystyle\mathbf{III}=\displaystyle\int_{\hat{s}-\varepsilon}^{1}\Big\langle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathcal{H}(x,\xi_{s}),\tilde{\xi}-\xi\Big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s=\langle\mathcal{H}(\tilde{\xi})-\mathcal{H}(\xi_{\hat{s}-\varepsilon}),\tilde{\xi}-\xi\rangle=-\langle\mathcal{H}(\xi_{\hat{s}-\varepsilon}),\tilde{\xi}-\xi\rangle\to 0

in the limit
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
. Overall, this yields the claimed monotonicity estimate (
2.19
). In the other case
|\tilde{\xi}|_{E}>|\xi|_{E}
, we infer similarly

\displaystyle|\xi_{s}|_{E}

\displaystyle\geq s|\tilde{\xi}|_{E}-(1-s)|-\xi|_{E}

\displaystyle\geq s|\xi|_{E}-(1-s)\mbox{$\frac{R_{E}}{r_{E}}$}|\xi|_{E}

\displaystyle=s\mbox{$\frac{R_{E}+r_{E}}{r_{E}}$}|\xi|_{E}-\mbox{$\frac{R_{E}}{r_{E}}$}|\xi|_{E}

\displaystyle\geq 1+\mbox{$\frac{\delta}{2}$}

for all
s\in\Big[\frac{1}{2|\xi|_{E}(R_{E}+r_{E})}(r_{E}(2+\delta)+2R_{E}|\xi|_{E}),1\Big]
. By following the approach taken for the former case
|\tilde{\xi}|_{E}\leq|\xi|_{E}
, we obtain the very same lower bound, which finishes the proof.

The second assertion of the non-negativity of the left-hand side in (
2.19
) follows immediately: either by leveraging the convexity of
\mathcal{F}
with respect to the second variable, as stated in
\eqref{fregularity}_{2}
, or by distinguishing the cases where
|\tilde{\xi}|_{E}\geq 1+\delta
or
|\xi|_{E}\geq 1+\delta
for some
\delta>0
, and then applying the first part of the lemma, respectively by noting that the left-hand side of (
2.19
) vanishes in the trivial case where
|\tilde{\xi}|_{E}\leq 1
and
|\xi|_{E}\leq 1
.

∎

As a consequence of Lemma
2.6
and Lemma
2.7
, we obtain the next auxiliary result.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
and
\delta>0
. For any
x\in\Omega
and
\tilde{\xi},\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
there holds

\displaystyle\varepsilon|\tilde{\xi}-\xi|^{2}+|\mathcal{G}_{\delta}(\tilde{\xi})-\mathcal{G}_{\delta}(\xi)|^{2}\leq C(\delta,R_{E},r_{E})\langle\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}(x,\tilde{\xi})-\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}(x,\xi),\tilde{\xi}-\xi\rangle.

Proof.

In the case where
|\xi|_{E},|\tilde{\xi}|_{E}\leq 1+\delta
, we have
\mathcal{G}_{\delta}(\xi)=\mathcal{G}_{\delta}(\tilde{\xi})=0
and the claimed estimate readily follows by an application of Lemma
2.7
. Next, we treat the case where either
|\xi|_{E}>1+\delta
or
|\tilde{\xi}|_{E}>1+\delta
. An application of Lemma
2.6
implies

|\mathcal{G}_{\delta}(\tilde{\xi})-\mathcal{G}_{\delta}(\xi)|^{2}\leq C(R_{E},r_{E})|\tilde{\xi}-\xi|^{2}.

Consequently, Lemma
2.7
yields the claimed inequality

\displaystyle\varepsilon|\tilde{\xi}-\xi|^{2}+|\mathcal{G}_{\delta}(\tilde{\xi})-\mathcal{G}_{\delta}(\xi)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon|\tilde{\xi}-\xi|^{2}+C(R_{E},r_{E})|\tilde{\xi}-\xi|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon|\tilde{\xi}-\xi|^{2}+C(\delta,R_{E},r_{E})\frac{r_{E}(2|\xi|_{E}-(2+\delta))}{2|\xi|_{E}(R_{E}+r_{E})}|\tilde{\xi}-\xi|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(\delta,R_{E},r_{E})\langle\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}(x,\tilde{\xi})-\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}(x,\xi),\tilde{\xi}-\xi\rangle.

∎

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
and
\delta>0
. For any
x\in\Omega
and any
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
1+\delta\leq|\xi|_{E}\leq\delta^{-1}
, where

with
0\in\inn{E}

\mathcal{F}
, there holds

(\varepsilon+\lambda(\delta))|\eta|^{2}\leq\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon}(x,\xi)(\eta,\eta)\leq(\varepsilon+\Lambda(\delta))|\eta|^{2}.

The estimate is an immediate consequence of the ellipticity assumption (
1.7
) and the definition of the bilinear form
\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon}
.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

The aim of this section is the proof of the main regularity result that is Theorem
1.3
. This will be achieved in a series of steps. We commence by regularizing our equation (
1.1
) in order to obtain an elliptic approximating equation. Moreover, due to the fact that any weak solution to (
1.1
) is
a priori
assumed to be locally Lipschitz continuous, we modify the function
\mathcal{F}(x,\xi)
present in the diffusion term of our equation in order to obtain a revised function whose derivative satisfies quadratic growth with respect to the gradient variable
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

3.1.
Regularizing the equation

We start off by performing a regularization of equation (
1.1
) through constructing an approximation of
\mathcal{F}
that satisfies quadratic growth for large
|\xi|
. Moreover, a further modification leads to a redefined function
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
that exhibits quadratic growth on the whole of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with an ellipticity constant depending on the parameter
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
. We would like to note that a similar construction has already been employed in
[
9
, Proof of Theorem 1.1]
. Let
u\in W^{1,\infty}_{\loc}(\Omega)
denote a local weak solution to (
1.1
) and consider a ball
B_{R}=B_{R}(y_{0})\Subset\Omega
. In what follows, we will always omit the center
y_{0}
of the ball
B_{R}=B_{R}(y_{0})
in any given quantity. For the further course of this section, we denote

(3.1)

K\coloneqq\|Du\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}<\infty,

which is indeed finite due to the Lipschitz regularity of
u
in
B_{R}
. Moreover, we set

(3.2)

\tilde{K}\coloneqq\|\mathcal{F}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}\times B_{K+2R_{E}})}<\infty,\qquad L\coloneqq\tilde{K}+1<\infty,

where
R_{E}>0
denotes the radius from (
2.11
). The additional constant
1
is selected arbitrarily and could be substituted with any real number that is greater than
0
. Next, let
\Psi\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
denote a non-negative and smooth cut-off function of class
C^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{>0},\mathbb{R}_{>0})
, such that

\Psi(t)=t\quad\mbox{for\penalty\ $t\in[0,\tilde{K}]$},\qquad\quad\Psi(t)=L\quad\mbox{for\penalty\ $t\in[L,\infty)$},

that satisfies the estimate

(3.3)

|\Psi^{\prime}(t)|+|\Psi^{\prime\prime}(t)|\leq C_{\Psi}\qquad\mbox{for all\penalty\ $t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}$}

for some
C_{\Psi}>0
. For
x\in B_{R}
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define a truncated version of
\mathcal{F}
by

(3.4)

\tilde{\mathcal{F}}(x,\xi)\coloneqq\Psi(\mathcal{F}(x,\xi)),

and note that due to this construction, there holds

(3.5)

\mathcal{F}(x,Du(x))=\Psi(\mathcal{F}(x,Du(x)))=\tilde{\mathcal{F}}(x,Du(x))

for any
x\in B_{R}
. Moreover, we have

(3.6)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\nabla\tilde{\mathcal{F}}(x,\xi)=\nabla\mathcal{F}(x,\xi)\Psi^{\prime}(\mathcal{F}(x,\xi)),\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}&\\
\nabla^{2}\tilde{\mathcal{F}}(x,\xi)=(\nabla\mathcal{F}(x,\xi)\otimes\nabla\mathcal{F}(x,\xi))\Psi^{\prime\prime}(\mathcal{F}(x,\xi))+\nabla^{2}\mathcal{F}(x,\xi)\Psi^{\prime}(\mathcal{F}(x,\xi)),\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\overline{E}&\end{cases}

for any
x\in B_{R}
. We note that the truncated function
(x,\xi)\mapsto\tilde{\mathcal{F}}(x,\xi)
remains constant on the set
B_{R}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{N})
for some

(3.7)

\displaystyle N\geq K+2R_{E}.

This property follows from the continuity of
\mathcal{F}
and the fact that the partial mapping
\xi\mapsto\nabla^{2}\mathcal{F}(x,\xi)
is positive definite on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\overline{E}
for any
x\in B_{R}
, yielding strict convexity of
\xi\mapsto\mathcal{F}(x,\xi)
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\overline{E}
for any
x\in B_{R}
. Thus,
\xi\mapsto\mathcal{F}(x,\xi)
is in particular coercive on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for any
x\in B_{R}
. In particular, for the function
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
there hold the following estimates

(3.8)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\|\nabla\tilde{\mathcal{F}}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{\Psi}\|\nabla\mathcal{F}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}\times B_{N})}&\\
\|\nabla^{2}\tilde{\mathcal{F}}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus E_{\delta}))}\leq C_{\Psi}\|\nabla\mathcal{F}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}\times B_{N})}+C_{\Psi}\|\nabla^{2}\mathcal{F}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}\times(B_{N}\setminus E_{\delta}))}&\end{cases}

for any
\delta>0
, where we recall that
E_{\delta}
denotes the outer parallel set of
E
according to (
2.13
). Thus, the preceding estimates for the essential supremum of both
\nabla\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
and
\nabla^{2}\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
can be controlled by the constant
C_{\Psi}
and the
L^{\infty}
-bounds for
\nabla\mathcal{F}
and
\nabla^{2}\mathcal{F}
respectively. We set

(3.9)

\displaystyle C_{\mathcal{F}}\coloneqq\max\{\|\nabla^{2}\tilde{\mathcal{F}}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{K+R_{E}}))},1\}<\infty

Next, let
\Phi\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denote a convex function that is subject to

(3.10)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Phi(\xi)=0&\mbox{for any\penalty\ $|\xi|\leq K+R_{E}$},\\
|\nabla\Phi(\xi)|\leq(2C_{\mathcal{F}}+1)|\xi|&\mbox{for any\penalty\ $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$},\\
\langle\nabla^{2}\Phi(\xi)\eta,\eta\rangle\geq(C_{\mathcal{F}}+1)|\eta|^{2}&\mbox{for any\penalty\ $|\xi|\geq K+2R_{E}$},\\
|\nabla^{2}\Phi(\xi)|\leq 2C_{\mathcal{F}}+1&\mbox{for any\penalty\ $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$},\end{cases}

for any
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We now consider the mapping

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{F}}(x,\xi)\coloneqq\tilde{\mathcal{F}}(x,\xi)+\Phi(\xi)\qquad\mbox{for\penalty\ $x\in B_{R},\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$}.

By construction, we have that
\mathbb{R}^{n}\ni\xi\mapsto\hat{\mathcal{F}}(x,\xi)
is convex across
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Indeed, on
B_{R}\times B_{K+2R_{E}}
there holds
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}(x,\xi)=\mathcal{F}(x,\xi)
, such that, due to the fact that
\Phi
is convex across
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\hat{\mathcal{F}}
is convex. On the complement

B_{R}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{K+2R_{E}})

the bound (
3.9
) and the properties (
3.10
) yield the ellipticity estimate

(3.11)

\displaystyle\langle\nabla^{2}\hat{\mathcal{F}}(x,\xi)\eta,\eta\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla^{2}\tilde{\mathcal{F}}(x,\xi)\eta,\eta\rangle+\langle\nabla^{2}\Phi(\xi)\eta,\eta\rangle

\displaystyle\geq-C_{\mathcal{F}}|\eta|^{2}+(C_{\mathcal{F}}+1)|\eta|^{2}=|\eta|^{2}

for any
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Moreover, there holds the bound

(3.12)

\displaystyle|\nabla^{2}\hat{\mathcal{F}}(x,\xi)|\leq 3C_{\mathcal{F}}+1

for
x\in B_{R}
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{K+R_{E}}
. We claim that
\hat{\mathcal{F}}
satisfies the set of structure conditions (
1.6
). The first three conditions
\eqref{fregularity}_{1}
–
\eqref{fregularity}_{3}
certainly hold true due to the construction of
\hat{\mathcal{F}}
. The fourth condition
\eqref{fregularity}_{4}
can be verified as follows: let
(x,\xi)\in B_{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
be arbitrary. By exploiting
\eqref{fregularity}_{4}
for
\mathcal{F}
, the construction of
\hat{\mathcal{F}}
, the bounds (
2.12
) and (
3.3
), and the fact that
\Phi
is independent of
x
, there holds

(3.13)

\displaystyle|\partial_{x_{i}}\nabla\hat{\mathcal{F}}(x,\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq|\partial_{x_{i}}\nabla\mathcal{F}(x,\xi)\Psi^{\prime}(\mathcal{F}(x,\xi))|+|\nabla\mathcal{F}(x,\xi)\partial_{x_{i}}\mathcal{F}(x,\xi)\Psi^{\prime\prime}(x,\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq C(C_{\Psi},N,r_{E})+C_{\Psi}\underbrace{\|\partial_{x}\mathcal{F}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}\times B_{N})}}_{\leq C(C_{\Psi},N,r_{E})}\underbrace{\|\nabla\mathcal{F}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}\times B_{N})}}_{\eqqcolon\hat{C}_{\mathcal{F}}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\hat{C}_{\mathcal{F}},C_{\Psi},N,r_{E})

for any
i=1,\ldots,n
, which verifies that
\hat{\mathcal{F}}
satisfies condition
\eqref{fregularity}_{4}
with constant
C(\hat{C}_{\mathcal{F}},C_{\Psi},N,r_{E})
for any
x\in B_{R}
and any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In turn, we exploited the fact that
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}(x,\xi)
is constant for any
(x,\xi)\in B_{R}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{N})
, according to (
3.7
), and used the mean value theorem to bound the term involving
\partial_{x}\mathcal{F}
. Finally, we aim to verify the ellipticity condition (
1.7
) for
\hat{\mathcal{F}}
. This, however, follows from the preceding reasoning provided in (
3.11
) and (
3.12
), as well as the fact that there holds

\hat{\mathcal{F}}(x,\xi)=\tilde{\mathcal{F}}(x,\xi)+\Phi(\xi)=\mathcal{F}(x,\xi)+\Phi(\xi)\qquad\mbox{in\penalty\ $B_{R}\times B_{K+2R_{E}}$},

concluding that for any
\delta>0
there exist positive constants
\hat{\lambda}=\hat{\lambda}(C_{\mathcal{F}},\delta)
and
\hat{\Lambda}=\hat{\Lambda}(C_{\mathcal{F}},\delta)
with
0<\hat{\lambda}\leq\hat{\Lambda}
, such that there holds

(3.14)

\displaystyle\qquad\hat{\lambda}(C_{\mathcal{F}},\delta)|\eta|^{2}\leq\langle\nabla^{2}\hat{\mathcal{F}}(x,\xi)\eta,\eta\rangle\leq\hat{\Lambda}(C_{\mathcal{F}},\delta)|\eta|^{2}\qquad\text{for any\penalty\ $1+\delta\leq|\xi|_{E}\leq\delta^{-1}$}

for any
x\in B_{R}
. Since
u
is a weak solution to equation (
1.1
),
u
is also a weak solution to

\divv\hat{\mathcal{H}}(x,Du)=f\qquad\mbox{in\penalty\ $B_{R}$},

since the set of points
B_{R}\cap\{|Du|\geq K\}
is a subset of
B_{R}
of measure zero and there holds
\hat{\mathcal{F}}=\mathcal{F}
in
B_{R}\times B_{K}
according to (
3.1
) and the definition of
\hat{\mathcal{F}}
. Here, we abbreviated
\hat{\mathcal{H}}(x,\xi)\coloneqq\nabla\hat{\mathcal{F}}(x,\xi)
for any
x\in B_{R}
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The above redefined equation however still does not admit uniform ellipticity on the whole of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, necessitating an additional approximation in order to obtain an elliptic equation on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
that avoids degeneracy within the set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For an approximating parameter
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
, we define

(3.15)

\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}(x,\xi)\coloneqq\hat{\mathcal{F}}(x,\xi)+\varepsilon\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}|\xi|^{2}\qquad\mbox{for\penalty\ $x\in B_{R},\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$}

and note that
\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}
is indeed elliptic with respect to
\xi
on the whole of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
in the case where
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
, with the ellipticity constant additionally depending on the parameter
\varepsilon
. Moreover, by exploiting the definition of
\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}
, (
3.6
), and also (
3.10
), we have that
\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}
satisfies the desired quadratic growth on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, i.e. there holds

(3.16)

\displaystyle|\nabla\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}(x,\xi)|\leq(\varepsilon+C_{\Psi}\|\nabla\mathcal{F}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}\times B_{N})}+2C_{\mathcal{F}}+1)(1+|\xi|)\leq C(C_{\mathcal{F}},C_{\Psi},\hat{C}_{\mathcal{F}})(1+|\xi|)

for any
x\in B_{R}
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where we additionally used that
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
. For simplicity of notation, we will omit any dependence on the constant
C_{\Psi}
from now on.

Moreover, for any
|\xi|>K+2R_{E}
we have, according to (
3.10
), the following quadratic growth

(3.17)

\displaystyle|\nabla\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}(x,\xi)|\leq(2C_{\mathcal{F}}+1)|\xi|.

We continue by considering the unique weak solution

u_{\varepsilon}\in u+W^{1,2}_{0}(B_{R})

to the Dirichlet problem

(3.18)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\divv\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\varepsilon}(x,Du_{\varepsilon})=f&\mbox{in\penalty\ $B_{R}$}\\
\,\qquad\quad\quad\quad\,\,u_{\varepsilon}=u&\mbox{on\penalty\ $\partial B_{R}$},\end{cases}

where we abbreviated
\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\varepsilon}(x,\xi)\coloneqq\nabla\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}(x,\xi)
for
(x,\xi)\in B_{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, according to (
2.4
). Similarly, we will write
\hat{\mathcal{B}}_{\varepsilon}(x,\xi)(\eta,\zeta)\coloneqq\langle\nabla^{2}\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}(x,\xi)\eta,\zeta\rangle
for
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\overline{E}
and
\eta,\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Due to the quadratic growth (
3.16
) of the redefined equation, it is sufficient to consider the approximating solutions
u_{\varepsilon}
to be
a priori
of class
W^{1,2}(B_{R})
. The respective weak formulation of (

) results in

(3.19)

\int_{B_{R}}\langle\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\varepsilon}(x,Du_{\varepsilon}),D\phi\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x=-\int_{B_{R}}f\phi\,\mathrm{d}x

for any test function
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B_{R})
. We recall once more that the approximation
\xi\mapsto\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}(x,\xi)
is elliptic on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for any
x\in B_{R}
, with an ellipticity constant that depends on the parameter
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
in general. This property enables us to obtain the higher regularity
u_{\varepsilon}\in W^{1,\infty}_{\loc}(B_{R})\cap W^{2,2}_{\loc}(B_{R})
for the approximating solutions
u_{\varepsilon}
. Additionally, we obtain a quantitative local
L^{\infty}
-estimate for the gradient and a local
W^{2,2}
-estimate, both of which are stated in Proposition
3.2
and Proposition

. Indeed, we immediately obtain from the fact that the boundary datum satisfies
u\in W^{1,\infty}(B_{R})
and the maximum principle in
[
20
, Theorem 8.1]
, that the solutions
u_{\varepsilon}
are bounded within
B_{R}
by
u
, i.e. for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
there holds
u_{\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}(B_{R})
with the quantitative bound

(3.20)

\displaystyle\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}\leq\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}<\infty.

Before stating the quantitative local
L^{\infty}
-gradient and the
W^{2,2}
-estimate for
u_{\varepsilon}
on

, the subsequent energy estimate turns out expedient, which states a uniform

-bound with respect to the parameter

for the approximating solutions

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
. There holds the energy estimate

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}|Du_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B_{R}}\big(1+|Du|^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x

with a constant
C=C(C_{\mathcal{F}},\hat{C}_{\mathcal{F}},\|f\|_{L^{n}(B_{R})},n,R_{E},r_{E})
.

Proof.

We start by testing the weak form (
3.19
) with
\phi=u_{\varepsilon}-u
. It can be verified through an approximation argument that this choice of
\phi
is indeed an admissible test function. By plugging
\phi
into (
3.19
), there holds

(3.21)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}\langle\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\varepsilon}(x,Du_{\varepsilon}),Du_{\varepsilon}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{R}}\langle\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\varepsilon}(x,Du_{\varepsilon}),Du\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{B_{R}}f(u_{\varepsilon}-u)\,\mathrm{d}x.

In order to bound the term on the left-hand side further below, we note that due to Lemma
2.7
, the quantity is non-negative. Thus, we pass to the subset of points
B_{R}\cap\{|Du_{\varepsilon}|_{E}\geq\frac{3}{2}\}
and employ Lemma
2.7
as well as (
2.12
), where we recall (
3.14
), to obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}\langle

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\varepsilon}(x,Du_{\varepsilon}),Du_{\varepsilon}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq\int_{B_{R}}\bigg(\tilde{C}(C_{\mathcal{F}},R_{E},r_{E})\frac{2|Du_{\varepsilon}|_{E}-\frac{5}{2}}{|Du_{\varepsilon}|_{E}}|Du_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\scalebox{1.3}{$\chi$}_{\{|Du_{\varepsilon}|_{E}\geq\frac{3}{2}\}}+\varepsilon|Du_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq\int_{B_{R}}\bigg(\tilde{C}(C_{\mathcal{F}},R_{E},r_{E})\frac{2|Du_{\varepsilon}|_{E}-\frac{5}{2}}{|Du_{\varepsilon}|_{E}}(|Du_{\varepsilon}|_{E}-\textstyle{\frac{3}{2}})^{2}_{+}+\varepsilon|Du_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq\int_{B_{R}}(\tilde{C}(C_{\mathcal{F}},R_{E},r_{E})(|Du_{\varepsilon}|_{E}-\textstyle{\frac{3}{2}})^{2}_{+}+\varepsilon|Du_{\varepsilon}|^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x.

Next, the first term on the right-hand side of the preceding inequality is bounded above with the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, (
2.12
),
the gradient bound (
3.16
), and also Young’s inequality, by

(3.22)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}\langle\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\varepsilon}(x,Du_{\varepsilon}),Du\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{R}}\big(C(1+|Du_{\varepsilon}|)|Du|+\varepsilon|Du_{\varepsilon}||Du|\big)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{R}}\bigg(\mbox{$\frac{1}{4}$}\tilde{C}(|Du_{\varepsilon}|_{E}-\mbox{$\frac{3}{2}$})^{2}_{+}+C|Du|^{2}+C\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\varepsilon\int_{B_{R}}\big(|Du_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+|Du|^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x.

with a constant
C=C(C_{\mathcal{F}},\hat{C}_{\mathcal{F}},R_{E},r_{E})
.
In order to treat the term involving the datum
f
in (
3.21
), we employ Hölder’s inequality, Sobolev’s inequality, (
2.12
), and Young’s inequality, which yields the bound

(3.23)

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{B_{R}}f(u_{\varepsilon}-u)\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{R}}|f||u_{\varepsilon}-u|\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{L^{n}(B_{R})}\bigg(\int_{B_{R}}|u_{\varepsilon}-u|^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}

\displaystyle\leq C(n)\|f\|_{L^{n}(B_{R})}\int_{B_{R}}|Du_{\varepsilon}-Du|\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C(n)\|f\|_{L^{n}(B_{R})}\int_{B_{R}}(|Du_{\varepsilon}|+|Du|)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C(n,R_{E})\|f\|_{L^{n}(B_{R})}\int_{B_{R}}(|Du_{\varepsilon}|_{E}-\mbox{$\frac{3}{2}$})_{+}+1)

\displaystyle\quad+C\|f\|_{L^{n}(B_{R})}\int_{B_{R}}(1+|Du|^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\mbox{$\frac{1}{4}$}\tilde{C}\int_{B_{R}}(|Du_{\varepsilon}|_{E}-\mbox{$\frac{3}{2}$})^{2}_{+}\,\mathrm{d}x+C(n,R_{E})\|f\|_{L^{n}(B_{R})}\int_{B_{R}}(1+|Du|^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x.

Inserting the estimates (
3.22
) and (
3.23
) into (
3.21
) and reabsorbing the quantities involving
(|Du_{\varepsilon}|_{E}-\mbox{$\frac{3}{2}$})^{2}_{+}
into the left-hand side, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}\big((|Du_{\varepsilon}|_{E}-\mbox{$\frac{3}{2}$})^{2}_{+}+\varepsilon|Du_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\varepsilon C\int_{B_{R}}|Du|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+C\|f\|_{L^{n}(B_{R})}\int_{B_{R}}(1+|Du|^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x

with
C=C(C_{\mathcal{F}},\hat{C}_{\mathcal{F}},R_{E},r_{E},n)
. From this estimate, the claimed inequality is an immediate consequence due to (
2.12
).
∎

We continue by establishing the fact that
u_{\varepsilon}\in W^{2,2}_{\loc}(B_{R})
, stated in the subsequent proposition.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
. Then, there holds

u_{\varepsilon}\in W^{2,2}_{\loc}(B_{R}).

Moreover, for any
B_{2\rho}(x_{0})\Subset B_{R}
with
\rho\in(0,1]
there holds the quantitative
W^{2,2}
-estimate

(3.24)

\int_{B_{\frac{\rho}{4}}(x_{0})}|D^{2}u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{2}\rho^{2}}\bigg(\int_{B_{2\rho}(x_{0})}|Du_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\rho^{2}\int_{B_{2\rho}(x_{0})}(1+|f|^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)

with
C=C(C_{\mathcal{F}},\hat{C}_{\mathcal{F}},N,n,r_{E})
.

Proof.

As usual, the existence of local second order weak derivatives of
u_{\varepsilon}
in
B_{R}
will be achieved by employing the technique of difference quotients. We consider
B_{2\rho}(x_{0})\Subset B_{R}
and
h
small enough, such that
0<|h|<\text{dist}\big(B_{2\rho}(x_{0}),B_{R}\big)
. The finite difference of
u_{\varepsilon}
for a.e.
x\in B_{2\rho}(x_{0})
in direction
x_{i}
with increment
h\neq 0
is given by

\tau_{i,h}u_{\varepsilon}(x)\coloneqq u_{\varepsilon}(x+he_{i})-u_{\varepsilon}(x),

where
e_{i}
denotes the standard unit vector in direction
x_{i}
for an arbitrary
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
. To simplify notation, we will write
u=u_{\varepsilon}
and
\tau_{h}u=\tau_{i,h}u_{\varepsilon}(x)
, omitting the dependence on
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
and on
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
respectively. We test the weak formulation (
3.19
) with
\phi=\tau_{-h}[\zeta^{2}\tau_{h}u]
, where
\zeta\in C^{2}_{0}(B_{\frac{\rho}{2}}(x_{0}),[0,1])
is a smooth cut-off function that satisfies
\zeta\equiv 1
on
B_{\frac{\rho}{4}}(x_{0})
,
|D\zeta|\leq\frac{8}{\rho}
, and
|D^{2}\zeta|\leq\frac{64}{\rho^{2}}
. This choice of test function can be justified by an approximation argument. A change of variables, cf.
[
17
, Chapter 5.8, (10)]
, yields a discrete integration by parts

(3.25)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}\zeta^{2}\langle\tau_{h}\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\varepsilon}(x,Du),\tau_{h}Du\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+2\int_{B_{R}}\langle\tau_{h}\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\varepsilon}(x,Du),\tau_{h}u\zeta D\zeta\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{B_{R}}f\tau_{-h}[\zeta^{2}\tau_{h}u]\,\mathrm{d}x,

where we also utilized the commutativity of finite differences with weak derivatives. For the second term on the left-hand side in the preceding identity (
3.25
), we again use this integration by parts in the sense of changing variables and remove the finite difference from the vector field
\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\varepsilon}
by shifting it onto the quantity
\zeta\tau_{h}uD\zeta
. This way, there holds

(3.26)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}\zeta^{2}\langle\tau_{h}\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\varepsilon}(x,Du),\tau_{h}Du\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle-2\int_{B_{R}}\langle\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\varepsilon}(x,Du),\tau_{-h}[\tau_{h}u\zeta D\zeta]\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{R}}f\tau_{-h}[\zeta^{2}\tau_{h}u]\,\mathrm{d}x,

We rewrite the finite difference
\tau_{h}\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\varepsilon}(x,Du)
in the first integral term in (
3.26
) as follows

\displaystyle\tau_{h}\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\varepsilon}(x,Du)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\tau_{h}Du(x)+\tau_{h}\hat{\mathcal{H}}(x,Du(x))

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\tau_{h}Du(x)+\hat{\mathcal{H}}(x+he_{i},Du(x+he_{i}))-\hat{\mathcal{H}}(x,Du(x))

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\tau_{h}Du(x)+\hat{\mathcal{H}}(x+he_{i},Du(x+he_{i}))-\hat{\mathcal{H}}(x,Du(x+he_{i}))

\displaystyle\quad+\hat{\mathcal{H}}(x,Du(x+he_{i}))-\hat{\mathcal{H}}(x,Du(x))

\displaystyle\eqqcolon\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{II}+\mathbf{III}.

The quantities
\mathbf{I}
and
\mathbf{III}
remain unchanged. For the second term
\mathbf{II}
, we exploit the Lipschitz continuity of
\hat{\mathcal{H}}(x,\xi)
with respect to the
x
-variable. Thus, we exploit the bound (
3.13
) and estimate the second quantity by

\mathbf{II}\leq C(\hat{C}_{\mathcal{F}},N,r_{E})|h|.

In order to properly treat the second quantity in (
3.26
) involving second order finite differences, we use the following product rule for finite differences

\displaystyle\tau_{-h}[\tau_{h}u\zeta D\zeta](x)

\displaystyle=\tau_{h}u(x)\zeta(x)D\zeta(x)-\tau_{h}u(x-he_{i})\zeta(x-he_{i})D\zeta(x-he_{i})

\displaystyle=\tau_{-h}\tau_{h}u(x)\zeta(x)D\zeta(x)+\tau_{-h}[\zeta D\zeta](x)\tau_{h}u(x-he_{i})

\displaystyle=\tau_{-h}\tau_{h}u(x)\zeta(x)D\zeta(x)-\tau_{-h}[\zeta D\zeta](x)\tau_{-h}u(x)

for a.e.
x\in B_{2\rho}(x_{0})
. Further, we apply the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for this term as use the quadratic growth (
3.16
). Taking modulus in the third integral term in (
3.26
) involving the datum
f
, we plug in our calculations from above. This way, we obtain

\displaystyle\underbrace{\int_{B_{R}}\zeta^{2}\langle\hat{\mathcal{H}}(x,Du(x+he_{i}))-\hat{\mathcal{H}}(x,Du(x)),Du(x+he_{i})-Du(x)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x}_{\geq 0}+\varepsilon\int_{B_{R}}\zeta^{2}|\tau_{h}Du|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad\leq C|h|\int_{B_{\frac{\rho}{2}}(x_{0})}\zeta^{2}|\tau_{h}Du|\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{B_{R}}|f||\tau_{-h}[\zeta^{2}\tau_{h}u]|\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad\quad+C(\varepsilon+1)\int_{B_{R}}(1+|Du|)|\tau_{-h}[\zeta D\zeta]||\tau_{-h}u|\,\mathrm{d}x+C(\varepsilon+1)\int_{B_{R}}(1+|Du|)\zeta|D\zeta||\tau_{-h}\tau_{h}u|\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad\eqqcolon\tilde{\mathbf{I}}+\tilde{\mathbf{II}}+\tilde{\mathbf{III}}+\tilde{\mathbf{IV}}

with a constant
C=C(C_{\mathcal{F}},\hat{C}_{\mathcal{F}},N,r_{E})
. We note that due to the second assertion of Lemma
2.7
, the first term on the left-hand side above leads to a non-negative contribution and therefore can be discarded.
We now treat each of the terms
\tilde{\mathbf{I}}-\tilde{\mathbf{IV}}
one after another and commence by estimating the first quantity further above by employing Young’s inequality and by exploiting the fact that
\zeta\leq 1
, which yields

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{I}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\int_{B_{R}}\zeta^{2}|\tau_{h}Du|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{C}{\varepsilon}|h|^{2}|B_{\frac{\rho}{2}}(x_{0})|.

Next, we consider the term
\tilde{\mathbf{II}}
containing the datum
f
. Again, we apply the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Young’s inequality, use the bounds for
\zeta
and
D\zeta
, and also a standard estimate for finite differences that is taken from
[
17
, Chapter 5.8, Theorem 3]
, to achieve

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{II}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\varepsilon}|h|^{2}\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}|f|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{\varepsilon}{8|h|^{2}}\int_{B_{\frac{\rho}{2}}(x_{0})}|\tau_{-h}[\zeta^{2}\tau_{h}u]|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\varepsilon}|h|^{2}\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}|f|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{\varepsilon}{8}\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}\big|D[\zeta^{2}\tau_{h}u]\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\varepsilon}|h|^{2}\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}|f|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{\varepsilon}{8}\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}\big|\zeta^{2}\tau_{h}Du+2\zeta D\zeta\tau_{h}u\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\varepsilon}|h|^{2}\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}|f|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\int_{B_{R}}\zeta^{2}|\tau_{h}Du|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{C\varepsilon}{\rho^{2}}|h|^{2}\int_{B_{2\rho}(x_{0})}|Du|^{2}

with
C=C(n)
. The third quantity
\tilde{\mathbf{III}}
is bounded above by taking the essential supremum of
Du
, applying Young’s inequality, and then by proceeding in a similar fashion as for the second term

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{III}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\varepsilon+1)\rho^{2}\int_{B_{\frac{\rho}{2}}(x_{0})}|\tau_{-h}[\zeta D\zeta]|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+C\frac{(\varepsilon+1)}{\rho^{2}}\int_{B_{\frac{\rho}{2}}(x_{0})}|\tau_{-h}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C(\varepsilon+1)\rho^{2}|h|^{2}\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}|D[\zeta D\zeta]|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+C\frac{(\varepsilon+1)}{\rho^{2}}|h|^{2}\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}|Du|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C(\varepsilon+1)\rho^{2}|h|^{2}\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}(|D\zeta\otimes D\zeta|^{2}+|D^{2}\zeta|^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x+C\frac{(\varepsilon+1)}{\rho^{2}}|h|^{2}\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}|Du|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(\varepsilon+1)|h|^{2}}{\rho^{2}}|B_{\rho}(x_{0})|+C\frac{(\varepsilon+1)}{\rho^{2}}|h|^{2}\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}|Du|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

with
C=C(C_{\mathcal{F}},\hat{C}_{\mathcal{F}},N,n,r_{E})
. Finally, the fourth term
\tilde{\mathbf{IV}}
is bounded above by Hölder’s and Young’s inequality, and also by exploiting the fact that finite differences and weak derivatives commutate, as follows

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{IV}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\varepsilon+1)\bigg(\int_{B_{\frac{\rho}{2}}(x_{0})}(1+|Du|)^{2}\zeta^{2}|D\zeta|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg(\int_{B_{\frac{\rho}{2}}(x_{0})}|\tau_{-h}\tau_{h}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\varepsilon+1)|h|\bigg(\int_{B_{\frac{\rho}{2}}(x_{0})}(1+|Du|)^{2}\zeta^{2}|D\zeta|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg(\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}|\tau_{h}Du|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\rho^{2}}\frac{\varepsilon+1}{\varepsilon}|h|^{2}\int_{B_{\frac{\rho}{2}}(x_{0})}(1+|Du|)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\int_{B_{R}}|\tau_{h}Du|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

After reabsorbing all quantities from our estimates of the terms
\tilde{\mathbf{I}}-\tilde{\mathbf{IV}}

\tau_{h}Du

\displaystyle\varepsilon\int_{B_{R}}\zeta^{2}|\tau_{h}Du|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{C|h|^{2}}{\varepsilon\rho^{2}}\bigg(\int_{B_{2\rho}(x_{0})}|Du|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\rho^{2}\int_{B_{2\rho}(x_{0})}(1+|f|^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)

Proposition 3.3

\esssup\limits_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}|Du_{\varepsilon}|\leq C\Bigg(\bigg(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.38135pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.7935pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.21347pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.04968pt}}\!\int_{B_{2\rho}(x_{0})}|Du_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}+1\Bigg)

Lemma 3.4

Proof.

Due to the definition of

and subtract them from each other to obtain the identity

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}\langle\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\varepsilon}(x,Du_{\varepsilon})-\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\varepsilon}(x,Du),Du_{\varepsilon}-Du\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\int_{B_{R}}\langle Du,Du-Du_{\varepsilon}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x.

We now apply Lemma
2.8

with

\tilde{\xi}=Du_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\varepsilon

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}|Du_{\varepsilon}-Du|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{B_{R}}|\mathcal{G}_{\delta}(Du_{\varepsilon})-\mathcal{G}_{\delta}(Du)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.24571pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.65785pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.07782pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-4.91403pt}}\!\int_{B_{\frac{r}{2}}(z)}|\Gamma_{x}-\Gamma_{y}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C(n)\bigg(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.24571pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.65785pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.07782pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-4.91403pt}}\!\int_{B_{r}(x)}|\mathcal{G}_{2\delta}(Du_{\varepsilon})-\Gamma_{x}|^{2}\,dx+\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.24571pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.65785pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.07782pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-4.91403pt}}\!\int_{B_{r}(y)}|\mathcal{G}_{2\delta}(Du_{\varepsilon})-\Gamma_{y}|^{2}\,dx\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(\frac{|x-y|}{\rho^{*}}\bigg)^{2\alpha_{\delta}}\mu^{2}.

This gives

\displaystyle|\Gamma_{x}-\Gamma_{y}|\leq C\Big(\frac{|x-y|}{\rho^{*}}\Big)^{\alpha_{\delta}}\mu.

In the other case where
r>\rho^{*}
, we have

\displaystyle|\Gamma_{x}-\Gamma_{y}|\leq 2\mu\leq 2\Big(\frac{|x-y|}{\rho^{*}}\Big)^{\alpha_{\delta}}\mu.

This establishes the Hölder continuity of
\mathcal{G}_{2\delta}(Du_{\varepsilon})
, and thus also of
\mathcal{G}_{\delta}(Du_{\varepsilon})
. In particular, this reasoning yields the stated dependence of
\alpha_{\delta}\in(0,1)
and
C_{\delta}\geq 1
in Theorem
3.5
. The proof is complete.
∎

We now state the proof of the main regularity result, that is Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Once Theorem
3.5
has been established, the main regularity Theorem
1.3
readily follows. As before, let us consider the arbitrarily chosen ball
B_{R}=B_{R}(y_{0})\Subset\Omega
and let
u_{\varepsilon}\in u+W^{1,\infty}_{0}(B_{R})
denote the unique weak solution to the Dirichlet problem (
3.18
) for some parameter
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
. Moreover, consider
\delta\in(0,1]
and an arbitrarily chosen ball
B_{\rho}(x_{0})\Subset B_{R}
. Then, Theorem
3.5
yields that
\mathcal{G}_{\delta}(Du_{\varepsilon})
is Hölder continuous in
\overline{B}_{\rho}(x_{0})
with a Hölder exponent
\alpha_{\delta}\in(0,1)
and a Hölder constant
C_{\delta}
, both depending on the data

\displaystyle\alpha_{\delta}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{\delta}(C_{\mathcal{F}}(\delta),\hat{C}_{\mathcal{F}},\delta,\|f\|_{L^{n+\sigma}(B_{R})},M,N,n,R,r_{E},\sigma)\in(0,1),

\displaystyle C_{\delta}

\displaystyle=C_{\delta}(C_{\mathcal{F}}(\delta),\hat{C}_{\mathcal{F}},\delta,\|f\|_{L^{n+\sigma}(B_{R})},M,N,n,R,R_{E},r_{E},\sigma)\geq 1,

Due to Lemma
2.8
, we know that
\mathcal{G}_{\delta}(Du_{\varepsilon})\to\mathcal{G}_{\delta}(Du)
in

as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
, yielding the existence of a subsequence
(\varepsilon_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,1]
with
\mathcal{G}_{\delta}(Du_{\varepsilon_{i}})\to\mathcal{G}_{\delta}(Du)
a.e. in
B_{R}
as
\varepsilon_{i}\downarrow 0
. Since the family
(\mathcal{G}_{\delta}(Du_{\varepsilon}))_{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}
is locally uniformly equicontinuous and also locally uniformly bounded in
B_{R}
, an application of Arzelà-Ascoli’s theorem and passing to the limit
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
yields that the limit function
\mathcal{G}_{\delta}(Du)
itself is Hölder continuous in
\overline{B}_{\rho}(x_{0})
with a Hölder constant
C_{\delta}
and Hölder exponent
\alpha_{\delta}\in(0,1)
. Furthermore, there holds
\mathcal{G}_{\delta}(Du)\to\mathcal{G}(Du)
uniformly in
\overline{B}_{\rho}(x_{0})
, which follows from the estimate

\displaystyle|\mathcal{G}_{\delta}(Du)-\mathcal{G}(Du)|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\frac{(|Du|_{E}-(1+\delta))_{+}}{|Du|_{E}}Du-\frac{(|Du|_{E}-1)_{+}}{|Du|_{E}}Du\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|Du|}{|Du|_{E}}\big|(|Du|_{E}-(1+\delta))_{+}-(|Du|_{E}-1)_{+}\big|

\displaystyle\leq\mbox{$\frac{1}{r_{E}}$}\big|(|Du|_{E}-(1+\delta))_{+}-(|Du|_{E}-1)_{+}\big|

\displaystyle\leq\mbox{$\frac{1}{r_{E}}$}\delta

a.e. in
\overline{B}_{\rho}(x_{0})
. In turn, we used (
2.12
) and Lemma
2.5
. Therefore, the limit function
\mathcal{G}(Du)
itself is uniformly continuous in
\overline{B}_{\rho}(x_{0})
. Let
\varepsilon>0
be an arbitrary parameter, which we note is at this stage no longer associated with the approximation parameter mentioned previously. Then, there exists
\kappa>0
with

(3.49)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{G}(Du(x))-\mathcal{G}(Du(y))|<\varepsilon\qquad\mbox{for any\penalty\ $x,y\in\overline{B}_{\rho}(x_{0})$ with\penalty\ $|x-y|<\kappa$}.

Further, let
\mathcal{K}\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denote an arbitrary continuous function that vanishes inside the bounded and convex set of degeneracy
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
0\in\inn{E}
. Due to the fact that
u\in W^{1,\infty}_{\loc}(\Omega)
, there exists some positive and finite constant
0<M<\infty
with
|Du|\leq M
a.e. in
\overline{B}_{\rho}(x_{0})
.
Consequently,
\mathcal{K}
exhibits a modulus of continuity
\omega_{M}\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
on
\overline{B}_{M}
, i.e. there holds

|\mathcal{K}(\xi)-\mathcal{K}(\eta)|\leq\omega_{M}(|\xi-\eta|)\qquad\mbox{for any\penalty\ $\xi,\eta\in\overline{B}_{M}$}.

We distinguish between two scenarios. Firstly, let us assume that there holds
|Du(x)|_{E}\leq 1+\sqrt{\varepsilon}
. If
|Du(x)|_{E}\geq 1
, we exploit the fact that
\mathcal{K}\equiv 0
on
E
and also use the homogeneity of the Minkowski functional
|\cdot|_{E}
to obtain

\displaystyle|\mathcal{K}(Du(x))|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\mathcal{K}(Du(x))-\mathcal{K}\Big(\frac{Du(x)}{|Du(x)|_{E}}\Big)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{M}\bigg(\Big|Du(x)-\frac{Du(x)}{|Du(x)|_{E}}\Big|\bigg)

\displaystyle=\omega_{M}\bigg(\frac{|Du(x)|}{|Du(x)|_{E}}(|Du(x)|_{E}-1)\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{M}(R_{E}\sqrt{\varepsilon}).

If there holds
|Du(x)|_{E}\leq 1
, then the above estimate clearly holds true as well. Due to (
3.49
) being at our disposal, we further have

\displaystyle(|Du(y)|_{E}-1)_{+}

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{G}(Du(y))|_{E}

\displaystyle\leq|\mathcal{G}(Du(y))-\mathcal{G}(Du(x))|_{E}+|\mathcal{G}(Du(x))|_{E}

\displaystyle<\mbox{$\frac{\varepsilon}{r_{E}}$}+\sqrt{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq 2\max\{\mbox{$\frac{1}{r_{E}}$},1\}\sqrt{\varepsilon},

where we used the triangle inequality (
2.2
) and once again (
2.12
). This implies

|Du(y)|_{E}\leq 1+2\max\{\mbox{$\frac{1}{r_{E}}$},1\}\sqrt{\varepsilon},

such that by following the very same steps as above and exploiting (
2.12
), we also obtain that
|\mathcal{K}(Du(y))|\leq\omega_{M}\big(2R_{E}\max\big\{\frac{1}{r_{E}},1\big\}\sqrt{\varepsilon}\big)
. Combining both estimates for
|\mathcal{K}(Du(x))|
and
|\mathcal{K}(Du(y))|
, we thus find

|\mathcal{K}(Du(x))-\mathcal{K}(Du(y))|\leq\omega_{M}(R_{E}\sqrt{\varepsilon})+\omega_{M}\big(2R_{E}\max\{\mbox{$\frac{1}{r_{E}}$},1\}\sqrt{\varepsilon}\big)\leq 2\omega_{M}\big(2R_{E}\max\{\mbox{$\frac{1}{r_{E}}$},1\}\sqrt{\varepsilon}\big),

which yields the continuity of
\mathcal{K}(Du)
in the first case. If the second alternative
|Du(x)|_{E}>1+\sqrt{\varepsilon}
applies, we may employ the second assertion of Lemma
2.6
to obtain that there holds

\displaystyle|Du(x)-Du(y)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(R_{E},r_{E})}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}|\mathcal{G}(Du(x))-\mathcal{G}(Du(y))|

\displaystyle\leq C(R_{E},r_{E})\sqrt{\varepsilon}.

In turn, this implies

|\mathcal{K}(Du(x))-\mathcal{K}(Du(y))|\leq\omega_{M}(C(R_{E},r_{E})\sqrt{\varepsilon}),

which verifies that
\mathcal{K}(Du)
is continuous in
\overline{B}_{\rho}(x_{0})
. As all the balls
B_{\rho}(x_{0})\Subset B_{R}
were chosen arbitrarily, we obtain that
\mathcal{K}(Du)
is also continuous within the entire domain
\Omega
.

∎

Remark 3.8
.

It is noteworthy that during the proof of Theorem
1.3
, the control of the quantitative Hölder exponent is lost in the limit as

, leading to uniform continuity of the limit function
\mathcal{G}(Du)
locally in

instead of desirable Hölder continuity. To our knowledge it remains an open question even for the model equation (

) what the best possible modulus of continuity of

may be and whether
\mathcal{G}(Du)
is actually locally Hölder continuous in

, posing an intriguing question for further exploration in future research, cf. the comments right after
[

. However, in general,

fails to be Hölder continuous for certain integrands

in

. In fact, it has been demonstrated in

that for a certain integrand function

there exist Lipschitz continuous functions

that vanish inside the set of degeneracy

is not Hölder continuous.

4.
The non-degenerate regime

The objective in this section is to establish the proof of Proposition

incorporated in the measure-theoretic information (

has been established, we are able to state a quantitative excess-decay estimate for linear elliptic equations with bounded coefficients by exploiting a result found in
[

. Recall that in the course of this section, the set of assumptions (

), (
3.31
), (
3.36
), and (
3.37
) is at our disposal.

We begin by exploiting the measure-theoretic information (

on the smaller ball

Proposition 4.1

Let
\delta,\varepsilon\in(0,1]
and

denote the weak solution to (

for at least one

(4.2)

|Du_{\varepsilon}|_{E}\geq 1+\delta+\frac{\mu}{4}\qquad\mbox{a.e. in\penalty\ $B_{\frac{\rho}{2}}(x_{0})$}.

Proof.

To abbreviate notation, we shall simply write
u=u_{\varepsilon}
in what follows. The proof consists of three major steps and follows an approach taken by Kuusi and Mingione
[
23
, Proof of Proposition 3.7]
in a different setting.

Step 1:
We commence by rescaling the solution
u
to the unit ball. For this matter, we introduce the rescaled mappings

\displaystyle\tilde{u}(x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{u(x_{0}+\rho x)}{\rho},

\displaystyle\qquad x\in B_{1},

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}(x,\xi)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}(x_{0}+\rho x,\xi),

\displaystyle\qquad x\in B_{1},\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{B}}_{\varepsilon}(x,\xi)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\hat{\mathcal{B}}_{\varepsilon}(x_{0}+\rho x,\xi),

\displaystyle\qquad x\in B_{1},\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\rho f(x_{0}+\rho x),

\displaystyle\qquad x\in B_{1}.

For the following discussion, we shall also use the notation
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\varepsilon}(x,\xi)\coloneqq\nabla\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}(x,\xi)
. Consequently, since
u
solves (
3.18
) weakly on
B_{\rho}(x_{0})
, the rescaled function
\tilde{u}
is a weak solution to

\divv\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\varepsilon}(x,D\tilde{u})=\tilde{f}\qquad\mbox{in\penalty\ $B_{1}$},

i.e. there holds

(4.3)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}\langle\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\varepsilon}(x,D\tilde{u}),D\phi\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x=-\int_{B_{1}}\tilde{f}\phi\,\mathrm{d}x

for any test function

) translates to

(4.4)

\displaystyle|B_{1}\cap\{\partial_{\tilde{e}^{*}}\tilde{u}-(1+\delta)\leq(1-\nu)\mu\}|\leq\nu|B_{1}|

on

, where

denotes such an element that satisfies (

Step 2:
Next, we aim to establish a Caccioppoli-type estimate for

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}\langle\partial_{e^{*}}[\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\varepsilon}(x,D\tilde{u})],D\phi\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{B_{1}}\tilde{f}\partial_{e^{*}}\phi\,\mathrm{d}x.

Here, we choose the test function

where we abbreviated

, and

denotes a standard non-negative smooth cut-off function. This choice of test function can be justified by an approximation argument. With this choice of test function, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{j}\phi_{e^{*}}

\displaystyle=2\zeta\partial_{j}\zeta(\partial_{e^{*}}\tilde{u}-a)_{+}(\partial_{e^{*}}\tilde{u}-\kappa)_{-}+\zeta^{2}\partial_{j}\partial_{e^{*}}\tilde{u}(\partial_{e^{*}}\tilde{u}-\kappa)_{-}\scalebox{1.3}{$\chi$}_{\{\partial_{e^{*}}\tilde{u}>a\}}

for a.e.

and any

. The left-hand side of (

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{e^{*}}[\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\varepsilon}(x,D\tilde{u})],D\phi_{e^{*}}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{1}}\tilde{\mathcal{B}}_{\varepsilon}(x,D\tilde{u})(\partial_{e^{*}}D\tilde{u},D\phi_{e^{*}})\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{B_{1}}\langle\partial_{e^{*}}\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\varepsilon}(x,D\tilde{u}),D\phi_{e^{*}}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.24571pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.65785pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.07782pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-4.91403pt}}\!\int_{B_{\theta^{i-1}\rho}(x_{0})}|\mathcal{G}_{2\delta}(Du)-\mathcal{G}_{2\delta}((Du)_{x_{0},\theta^{i-1}\rho})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\Phi(x_{0},\theta^{i-1}\rho)\leq C\theta^{2\alpha(i-1)}\mu^{2}

with
C=C(R_{E},r_{E})
. This estimate further yields

\displaystyle|(\mathcal{G}_{2\delta}(Du))_{x_{0},\theta^{i-1}\rho}-(\mathcal{G}_{2\delta}(Du))_{x_{0},\theta^{i}\rho}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.24571pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.65785pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.07782pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-4.91403pt}}\!\int_{B_{\theta^{i}\rho}(x_{0})}|\mathcal{G}_{2\delta}(Du)-(\mathcal{G}_{2\delta}(Du))_{x_{0},\theta^{i-1}\rho}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\theta^{n}}\tilde{\Phi}(x_{0},\theta^{i-1}\rho)\leq\frac{C}{\theta^{n}}\theta^{2\alpha(i-1)}\mu^{2}.

Let us now consider natural numbers
l<m
and take roots in the preceding estimate. Due to the fact that
\theta^{\alpha}\leq\frac{1}{2}
, we thus obtain

\displaystyle|(\mathcal{G}_{2\delta}(Du))_{x_{0},\theta^{l}\rho}-(\mathcal{G}_{2\delta}(Du))_{x_{0},\theta^{m}\rho}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{i=l+1}^{m}|(\mathcal{G}_{2\delta}(Du))_{x_{0},\theta^{i-1}\rho}-(\mathcal{G}_{2\delta}(Du))_{x_{0},\theta^{i}\rho}|

\displaystyle\leq C\theta^{-\frac{n}{2}}\sum\limits_{i=l+1}^{m}\theta^{\alpha(i-1)}\mu\leq C\theta^{-\frac{n}{2}}\frac{\theta^{\alpha l}}{1-\theta^{\alpha}}\mu\leq C\theta^{-\frac{n}{2}}\theta^{\alpha l}\mu

with a constant
C=C(R_{E},r_{E})
. Thus, we have established that
((\mathcal{G}_{2\delta}(Du))_{x_{0},\theta^{i}\rho})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let us denote its limit by

\Gamma_{x_{0}}\coloneqq\lim\limits_{i\to\infty}(\mathcal{G}_{2\delta}(Du))_{x_{0},\theta^{i}\rho}.

Next, we pass to the limit
m\to\infty
in the preceding estimate to derive

\displaystyle|(\mathcal{G}_{2\delta}(Du))_{x_{0},\theta^{l}\rho}-\Gamma_{x_{0}}|\leq C\theta^{-\frac{n}{2}}\theta^{\alpha l}\mu\qquad\mbox{for any\penalty\ $l\in\mathbb{N}$.}

Together with estimate (
4.19
), this yields

\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.24571pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.65785pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.07782pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-4.91403pt}}\!\int_{B_{\theta^{l}\rho}(x_{0})}|\mathcal{G}_{2\delta}(Du)-\Gamma_{x_{0}}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq 2\tilde{\Phi}(x_{0},\theta^{l}\rho)+2|(\mathcal{G}_{2\delta}(Du))_{x_{0},\theta^{l}\rho}-\Gamma_{x_{0}}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\theta^{2\alpha l}\mu^{2}+C\theta^{-n}\theta^{\alpha 2l}\mu^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\theta^{-n}\theta^{2\alpha l}\mu^{2}.

As a last step, this estimate is converted into the excess-decay (
3.39
). Let
r\in(0,\rho]
and choose
l\in\mathbb{N}
, such that
\theta^{l+1}\rho<r\leq\theta^{l}\rho
holds true. Due to our choice of
\theta
and the preceding inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.24571pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.65785pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.07782pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-4.91403pt}}\!\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|\mathcal{G}_{2\delta}(Du)-\Gamma_{x_{0}}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\theta^{n}}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.24571pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.65785pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.07782pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-4.91403pt}}\!\int_{B_{\theta^{l}\rho}(x_{0})}|\mathcal{G}_{2\delta}(Du)-\Gamma_{x_{0}}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\theta^{-2n}\theta^{2\alpha l}\mu^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}\Big(\frac{r}{\rho}\Big)^{2\alpha}\mu^{2}

with
\tilde{C}=\tilde{C}(C_{\mathcal{F}}(\delta),\hat{C}_{\mathcal{F}},\delta,\|f\|_{L^{n+\sigma}(B_{R})},M,N,n,R_{E},r_{E},\sigma)
. Finally, applying Jensen’s inequality together with the above estimate, we achieve

\displaystyle|(\mathcal{G}_{2\delta}(Du))_{x_{0},r}-\Gamma_{x_{0}}|^{2}\leq\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.24571pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.65785pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.07782pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-4.91403pt}}\!\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|\mathcal{G}_{2\delta}(Du)-\Gamma_{x_{0}}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\leq C\Big(\frac{r}{\rho}\Big)^{2\alpha}\mu^{2},

which establishes that

\Gamma_{x_{0}}=\lim\limits_{r\downarrow 0}(\mathcal{G}_{2\delta}(Du))_{x_{0},r}.

The bound (
3.40
) is a consequence of (
2.12
) and estimate (
3.30
), which yield
|(\mathcal{G}_{2\delta}(Du))_{x_{0},r}|\leq R_{E}\mu
for any
r\in(0,\rho]
. This finishes the proof of the proposition and also the non-degenerate regime.
∎

Lemma 5.1
.

Let

Lemma 5.2

Lemma 5.3

(A De Giorgi class-type estimate for a sub-solution)
.

\delta,\varepsilon\in(0,1]

\displaystyle\quad+C(\rho^{2(1-\beta)}+\|f\|^{2}_{L^{n+\sigma}(B_{R})})|A_{k}|^{1-\frac{2}{n}+\frac{2\beta}{n}}

with
C=C(C_{\mathcal{F}}(\delta),\hat{C}_{\mathcal{F}},\delta,M,N,n,r_{E},\sigma)
and

A_{k}

A_{k}\coloneqq\{x\in B_{\rho}(x_{0}):v_{\varepsilon}(x)>k\}.

Proof.

vanishes on the set of points

\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon}

\varepsilon\in(0,1]

3.8

Let
\delta,\varepsilon\in(0,1]
,

, and

be the unique weak solution to (

). Assume that (
3.41

\nu>0
on a ball

\esssup\limits_{B_{2\rho}(x_{0})}v_{\varepsilon}\leq\mu^{2}.

As Lemma

Lemma 5.4
.

For any
\theta\in(0,1)
, there exists a parameter
\tilde{\nu}\in(0,1)
, which depends on the given data

(C_{\mathcal{F}}(\delta),\hat{C}_{\mathcal{F}},\|f\|_{L^{n+\sigma}(B_{R})},\delta,M,n,R,r_{E},\sigma),

such that: if the measure-theoretic information

|\{x\in B_{\rho}(x_{0}):v_{\varepsilon}(x)>(1-\theta)\mu^{2}\}|<\tilde{\nu}|B_{\rho}(x_{0})|

applies for any
e^{*}\in\partial E^{*}
, then we either have

\mu^{2}<\frac{\rho^{\beta}}{\theta}

or there holds

v_{\varepsilon}\leq\big(1-\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}\theta\big)\mu^{2}\quad\text{a.e. in\penalty\ $B_{\frac{\rho}{2}}(x_{0})$.}

At this juncture, it is crucial to assume that condition (
3.41

e^{*}\in\partial E^{*}
. This assumption allows the derivation of Lemma
5.5

[

Lemma 5.5
.

Assume that there exists some
\nu\in(0,1)
for which the measure-theoretic information
(
3.41
)
holds true for any
e^{*}\in\partial E^{*}
. Then, for any
i_{*}\in\mathbb{N}
we either have

\mu^{2}<2^{i_{*}}\frac{\rho^{\beta}}{\nu}

or

|\{x\in B_{\rho}(x_{0}):v_{\varepsilon}(x)>(1-2^{-i_{*}}\nu)\mu^{2}\}|<\frac{C}{\nu\sqrt{i_{*}}}|B_{\rho}(x_{0})|

for any
e^{*}\in\partial E^{*}
, with
C=C(C_{\mathcal{F}}(\delta),\hat{C}_{\mathcal{F}},\delta,\|f\|_{L^{n+\sigma}(B_{R})},M,N,n,R,r_{E},\sigma)\geq 1
.

With the aid of both Lemma

Proof of Proposition
3.7
.

Let
\tilde{\nu}\in(0,1)
denote the parameter from Lemma
5.4
and
C\geq 1
the constant from Lemma
5.5
. We choose an integer
i_{*}\in\mathbb{N}
large enough, such that

i_{*}\geq\Big(\frac{C}{\tilde{\nu}\nu}\Big)^{2}

is satisfied. Consequently, due to the dependence of the parameter
\tilde{\nu}\in(0,1)
and the constant
C\geq 1
,
i_{*}
depends on the data

(C_{\mathcal{F}}(\delta),\hat{C}_{\mathcal{F}},\delta,M,\|f\|_{L^{n+\sigma}(B_{R})},N,n,R,r_{E},\sigma).

An application of Lemma
5.5
yields that either there holds

\mu^{2}<\frac{2^{i_{*}}\rho^{\beta}}{\nu}

or

(5.12)

|\{x\in B_{\rho}(x_{0}):v_{\varepsilon}(x)>(1-2^{-i_{*}}\nu)\mu^{2}\}|<\frac{C}{\nu\sqrt{i_{*}}}|B_{\rho}(x_{0})|

for any
e^{*}\in\partial E^{*}
. The former case can be excluded by choosing a radius
\hat{\rho}\in(0,1)
small enough, such that the relation

\hat{\rho}^{\beta}\leq\frac{\delta^{2}\nu}{2^{i_{*}}}

is satisfied, and perform all the above steps for an arbitrarily chosen radius
\rho\leq\hat{\rho}\leq 1
. The radius
\hat{\rho}
thus depends on the data as stated in Proposition
3.7
. In particular, we rely on assumption (
3.36
) in order to do so. If the other case (
5.12
) applies, we are in position to employ Lemma
5.4
with the choice
\theta=2^{-i_{*}}\nu
, which yields that, either we have

\mu^{2}<\frac{2^{i_{*}}\rho^{\beta}}{\nu}

or

v_{\varepsilon}\leq(1-2^{-(i_{*}+1)}\nu)\mu^{2}\quad\text{a.e. in\penalty\ $B_{\frac{\rho}{2}}(x_{0})$}

holds true for any
e^{*}\in\partial E^{*}
. The former case is already excluded through the choice of
\hat{\rho}\in(0,1)
small enough in dependence of the data, as explained above. In the second case, we note that due to

v_{\varepsilon}=(\partial_{e^{*}}u_{\varepsilon}-(1+\delta))^{2}_{+},

we take roots in the preceding inequality to obtain that

(\partial_{e^{*}}u_{\varepsilon}-(1+\delta))_{+}\leq\kappa\mu

for any
\kappa\geq(1-2^{-(i_{*}+1)}\nu)^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Thus, the parameter
\kappa
may be chosen appropriately, such that
\kappa\in\big[2^{-\frac{\tilde{\beta}}{2}},1\big)
is satisfied, where
\alpha\in(0,1)
denotes the parameter from Proposition
3.6
,
\beta
is given in (
3.34
), and
0<\tilde{\beta}<\alpha<\beta
. Finally, since the preceding estimate holds for all
e^{*}\in\partial E^{*}
, we eventually pass to the supremum over all
e^{*}\in\partial E^{*}
and exploit (
2.9
).
This yields the desired quantitative shrinking of supremum

(|Du|_{E}-(1+\delta))_{+}\leq\kappa\mu

in
B_{\frac{\rho}{2}}(x_{0})
for any
\kappa\in\big[2^{-\frac{\tilde{\beta}}{2}},1\big)
and finishes the proof of the proposition and the degenerate regime.

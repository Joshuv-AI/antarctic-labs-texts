Asymptotic geometric regularity of CAT(0) spaces Thanks: Partially supported by JSPS KAKENHI Grant Numbers 20K03603, 25K06996
Koichi Nagano

Abstract
. We prove that if an n n -dimensional geodesically complete CAT ⁡ ( 0 ) \operatorname{CAT}(0) space has Tits boundary sufficiently close to the ( n − 1 ) (n-1) -dimensional standard unit sphere, then it is bi-Lipschiz homeomorphic to the n n -dimensional Euclidean space. As an application, we conclude that if an ( n − 1 ) (n-1) -dimensional geodesically complete CAT ⁡ ( 1 ) \operatorname{CAT}(1) space is sufficiently close to the ( n − 1 ) (n-1) -dimensional standard unit sphere, then they are bi-Lipschiz homeomorphic to each other.

53C20, 53C23

1.
Introduction

In this paper,
from a viewpoint of asymptotic geometry,
we study a problem
when a
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space is bi-Lipschitz homeomorphic to
the
n
-dimensional Euclidean space
\operatorname{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

As formulated by Gromov
[
14
]
,
a
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space
is defined as a complete geodesic metric space
that is globally non-positively curved in the sense of Alexandrov.
We recall that
a connected complete Riemannian manifold is
\operatorname{CAT}(0)

if and only if it is a Hadamard manifold,
namely,
a simply connected complete Riemannian manifold
of non-positive sectional curvature;
in this case,
it is diffeomorphic to
\operatorname{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

Let
X
be a
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space.
The ideal boundary
\partial_{\infty}X
of
X
is defined as
the set of all asymptotic equivalence classes of rays in
X
.
The Tits boundary
\partial_{\mathrm{T}}X
of
X

is defined as
the ideal boundary
\partial_{\infty}X
of
X
equipped with
the Tits metric
d_{\mathrm{T}}

(see Subsection 2.6).
We notice that
\partial_{\mathrm{T}}X
is a
\operatorname{CAT}(1)
space,
whenever
X
admits a ray.
If
X
is a proper, geodesically complete
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space,
and if the Tits boundary
\partial_{\mathrm{T}}X
of
X
is isometric to
the
(n-1)
-dimensional standard unit sphere
\operatorname{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
,
then
X
is isometric to
\operatorname{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
This rigidity follows from an observation of Leeb
[
16
, Proposition 2.1]
, obtained as a generalization of Schroeder’s work
in
[
5
, Appendix 4]
for Hadamard manifolds.
As a result of asymptotic geometric regularity,
the author
[
22
, Theorems 5.10 and 5.11]
proved
that
if
X
is a proper, geodesically complete
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space,
and if
\partial_{\operatorname{\mathrm{T}}}X

is sufficiently close to
\operatorname{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}

with respect to the Gromov–Hausdorff distance,
then
X
is bi-Lipschitz homeomorphic to
\operatorname{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

Our main purpose is
to generalize the results of asymptotic geometric regularity
mentioned above
without the properness assumption.

1.1.
Main theorems

Throughout this paper,
we denote by
\dim

the covering (topological) dimension of metric spaces.
We denote by
d_{\operatorname{\mathrm{GH}}}

the Gromov–Hausdorff distance between metric spaces.

As the main result of this paper,
we prove the following asymptotic geometric regularity theorem
for
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
spaces:

Theorem 1.1
.

For every
\epsilon\in(0,\infty)
,
and for every
n\in\operatorname{\mathbb{N}}
,
there exists
\delta\in(0,\infty)
such that
if a separable, geodesically complete
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space
X

of
\dim X\leq n

satisfies

d_{\operatorname{\mathrm{GH}}}\left(\partial_{\mathrm{T}}X,\operatorname{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}\right)<\delta
,
then
X
is
(1+\epsilon)
-bi-Lipschitz homeomorphic to
\operatorname{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

In Theorem
1.1
,
a desired bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism
is given by a map on
X
with Busemann function coordinates.
When we analyze such a regular map on
X

with Busemann function coordinates,
we utilize the ideas of the theory
of strainer maps on
\operatorname{GCBA}
spaces
with distance function coordinates
developed by
Lytchak and the author
[
18
]
.
In fact,
we need a generalization to
the case of geodesically complete
\operatorname{CBA}
spaces
recently examined by the author
[
23
]
.

Remark 1.1
.

The author
[
22
]
studied asymptotic topological regularity
of
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
spaces in terms of volume growth,
and proved that if a purely
n
-dimensional,
proper, geodesically complete
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space
has small Euclidean volume growth,
then it is homeomorphic to
\operatorname{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

Remark 1.2
.

Gromov
[
13
]
asked the question whether
there exists a Busemann convex geodesic metric space
that is a topological
n
-manifold differ from
\operatorname{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
We note that every
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space is Busemann convex.
For the case of
n\geq 5
,
Davis–Januszkiewicz
[
10
, Theorem 5b.1]

gave an affirmative answer,
in fact,
showed that for each
n\in\operatorname{\mathbb{N}}
with
n\geq 5
,
there exists a piecewise flat
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
polyhedron
that is a topological
n
-manifold not homeomorphic to
\operatorname{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

(see also
[
3
]
).
In the case of
n=3
,
if a Busemann convex geodesic metric space
is a topological
3
-manifold,
then it is homeomorphic to
\operatorname{\mathbb{R}}^{3}

(
[
7
]
,
[
24
]
,
[
26
]
).
In the case of
n=4
,
Lytchak, Stadler, and the author
[
20
]

proved that
every
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
topological
4
-manifold
is homeomorphic to
\operatorname{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
.
Fujioka–Gu
[
11
]
have recently shown that
every Busemann convex topological
4
-manifold
is homeomorphic to
\operatorname{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
.

As an application of Theorem
1.1
,
we conclude the following geometric regularity theorem
for
\operatorname{CAT}(1)
spaces:

Theorem 1.2
.

For every
\epsilon\in(0,\infty)
,
and for every
n\in\operatorname{\mathbb{N}}
,
there exists
\delta\in(0,\infty)
such that
if a separable, geodesically complete
\operatorname{CAT}(1)
space
Z

of
\dim Z\leq n-1
satisfies

d_{\operatorname{\mathrm{GH}}}\left(Z,\operatorname{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}\right)<\delta
,
then
Z
is
(1+\epsilon)
-bi-Lipschitz homeomorphic to
\operatorname{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
.

To derive Theorem
1.2

from Theorem
1.1
,
we utilize the ideas of the studies
of geometric regularity of
\operatorname{CBB}
spaces
with curvature bounded below
developed by Burago–Gromov–Perelman

[
9
, Section 9]
.
When actually implementing the ideas,
we need the studies of the author
[
23
]

on almost spherical suspension structure of
\operatorname{CAT}(1)
spaces.

Remark 1.3
.

Lytchak and the author
[
19
, Theorem 8.3]

proved that
if a purely
(n-1)
-dimensional,
compact, geodesically complete
\operatorname{CAT}(1)
space
has small volume,
then it is homeomorphic to
\operatorname{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
.
The author
[
21
]
studied volume pinching problems
for
\operatorname{CAT}(1)
spaces.

1.2.
Organization

In Section 2,
we prepare basic concepts in the geometry
of metric spaces with curvature bounded above,
especially
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
spaces.
In Section 3,
we quote the studies of the author
[
23
]

concerning an almost multi-fold spherical suspension structure
for
\operatorname{CAT}(1)
spaces,
and show basic lemmas for the proofs of the main theorems.
In Section 4,
we formulate the notion of Busemann strainer maps on
geodesically complete
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
spaces,
and investigate their various regularity.
In Section 5,
we prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.

Preliminaries

[

]
,

[

]
,

]
,

]

2.1.
Metric spaces

Let
r\in(0,\infty)
.
For a point
p
in a metric space,
we denote by
U_{r}(p)
,
B_{r}(p)
, and
S_{r}(p)

the open metric ball of radius
r
around
p
,
the closed one, and the metric sphere, respectively.

For a metric space
X
with metric
d_{X}
,
and for a positive number
\lambda
,
let
\lambda X
denote the rescaled metric space
defined as
(X,\lambda d_{X})
.

Let
X
be a metric space with metric
d_{X}
.
Let
d_{X}\wedge\pi
be the
\pi
-truncated metric on
X

defined by
d_{X}\wedge\pi:=\min\{d_{X},\pi\}
.
The

Euclidean cone
C_{0}(X)
over
X

is defined as the cone

[0,\infty)\times X/\{0\}\times X

over
X

equipped with the Euclidean metric

d_{C_{0}(X)}
given by

d_{C_{0}(X)}\left([(t_{1},x_{1})],[(t_{2},x_{2})]\right)^{2}:=t_{1}^{2}+t_{2}^{2}-2t_{1}t_{2}\,\cos\left(d_{X}\wedge\pi\right)(x_{1},x_{2}).

For simplicity,
we write an element
[(t,x)]
in
C_{0}(X)
as
tx
,
and denote by
0
the vertex of
C_{0}(X)
.

2.2.
Maps between metric spaces

Let
c\in(0,\infty)
.
Let
X
be a metric space with metric
d_{X}
,
and
Y
a metric space with metric
d_{Y}
.
A map
\varphi\colon X\to Y
is said to be

c
-Lipschitz

if

d_{Y}\left(\varphi(x_{1}),\varphi(x_{2})\right)\leq c\,d_{X}(x_{1},x_{2})

for all
x_{1},x_{2}\in X
.
A map
\varphi\colon X\to Y
is said to be

c
-bi-Lipschitz

if
f
is
c
-Lipschitz,
and if
d_{X}(x_{1},x_{2})\leq c\,d_{Y}\left(\varphi(x_{1}),\varphi(x_{2})\right)

for all
x_{1},x_{2}\in X

(consequently,
c\in[1,\infty)
).
A
1
-bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism
is nothing but an isometry,
and a
1
-bi-Lipschitz embedding is
an isometric embedding.
A map
\varphi\colon X\to Y
is

c
-open

if for any
r\in(0,\infty)
and for any
x\in X

such that
B_{cr}(x)
is complete,
the ball
U_{r}\left(\varphi(x)\right)
in
Y

is contained in the image
\varphi\left(U_{cr}(x)\right)

of the ball
U_{cr}(x)
in
X
.
In the case where
X
is complete,
if a map
\varphi\colon X\to Y
is
c
-open,
then
\varphi
is surjective.
Moreover,
in the case where
X
is complete,
a
c
-Lipschitz map
\varphi\colon X\to Y

is a
c
-bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism for some
c\in[1,\infty)

if and only if
\varphi
is an injective
c
-open map.

A map
\varphi\colon X\to Y
is said to be a

c
-approximation
between
X
and
Y

if
\bigcup_{y\in\varphi(X)}U_{r}(y)
coincides with
Y
,
and if for all
x_{1},x_{2}\in X
we have

\left|d_{Y}\left(\varphi(x_{1}),\varphi(x_{2})\right)-d_{X}(x_{1},x_{2})\right|<c.

If there exists a
c
-approximation
\varphi\colon X\to Y
,
then there exists a
2c
-approximation
\psi\colon Y\to X
such that
for all
x\in X
and
y\in Y
we have

d_{X}\left((\psi\circ\varphi)(x),x\right)<2c
and

d_{Y}\left((\varphi\circ\psi)(y),y\right)<2c
.

2.3.
Geodesic metric spaces

Let
X
be a metric space.
A
geodesic
\gamma\colon I\to X

means an isometric embedding from an interval
I
.
For a pair of points
p,q
in
X
,
a
geodesic
pq
in
X
from
p
to
q

means the image of an isometric embedding

\gamma\colon[a,b]\to X

from a bounded closed interval
[a,b]

with
\gamma(a)=p
and
\gamma(b)=q
.
A geodesic
\gamma\colon I\to X
is called
a
ray

if
I=[0,\infty)
,
and
\gamma
is called a
line
if
I=\operatorname{\mathbb{R}}
.

For
r\in(0,\infty]
,
a metric space
X
is said to be

r
-geodesic

if every pair of points in
X
with distance smaller than
r

can be joined by a geodesic in
X
.
A metric space is

geodesic

if it is
\infty
-geodesic.
A metric space is

proper

if every closed bounded subset is compact.
A geodesic metric space is proper
if and only if
it is complete and locally compact.

For
r\in(0,\infty]
,
a subset
C
of a metric space is said to be

r
-convex

if
C
itself is
r
-geodesic as a metric subspace,
and if every geodesic joining two points in
C

is contained in
C
.
A subset
C
of a metric space is

convex

if
C
is
\infty
-convex.

2.4.
Gromov–Hausdorff topology

As mentioned in Section 1,
we denote by
d_{\operatorname{\mathrm{GH}}}
the Gromov–Hausdorff distance
between metric spaces.
If for
c\in(0,\infty)
two metric spaces
X
and
Y

satisfy
d_{\operatorname{\mathrm{GH}}}(X,Y)<c
,
then there exists a
2c
-approximation between
X
and
Y
.
We say that a sequence
(X_{k})
of metric spaces
converges to a metric space
X

in the Gromov–Hausdorff topology

if
\lim_{k\to\infty}d_{\operatorname{\mathrm{GH}}}(X_{k},X)=0
.

A sequence
(X_{k},p_{k})
of pointed geodesic metric spaces
converges to a pointed metric space
(X,p)

in the pointed Gromov–Hausdorff topology

if for every
r\in(0,\infty)

there exists a sequence
(\epsilon_{k})
in
(0,\infty)

with
\lim_{k\to\infty}\epsilon_{k}=0

such that for each
k\in\operatorname{\mathbb{N}}

there exists an

\epsilon_{k}
-approximation
\varphi_{k}\colon B_{r}(p)\to B_{r}(p_{k})

with
\varphi_{k}(p)=p_{k}
;
in this case,
we write
(X,p)=\lim_{k\to\infty}(X_{k},p_{k})
.
If a sequence
(X_{k},p_{k})
of pointed proper geodesic metric spaces
converges to a pointed metric space
(X,p)

in the pointed Gromov–Hausdorff topology,
then
X
is proper and geodesic.

A
non-principal ultrafilter
\omega
on
\operatorname{\mathbb{N}}

is a finitely additive probability measure on
\operatorname{\mathbb{N}}
such that

\omega(A)\in\{0,1\}
for every subset
A
of
\operatorname{\mathbb{N}}
,
and
\omega(E)=0
if
E
is a finite subset.
For a subset
A
of
\operatorname{\mathbb{N}}
with
\omega(A)=1
,
each
k\in A
is said to be
\omega
-large
.
If
(x_{k})
is a sequence in a compact metric space
B
,
then there exists a unique point
x
in
B
such that
for every open neighborhood
U
of
x

we have

\omega\left(\left\{\,k\in\operatorname{\mathbb{N}}\mid x_{k}\in U\,\right\}\right)=1
;
the unique point
x
is called the
\omega
-limit of
(x_{k})
,
and denoted by
\operatorname{\lim_{\omega}}x_{k}
.

Throughout this paper,
we fix a non-principal ultrafilter
\omega
on
\operatorname{\mathbb{N}}
.
Let
(X_{k},p_{k})
be a sequence of pointed metric spaces
with metrics
d_{X_{k}}
.
We denote by
X_{\omega}^{0}

the set of all sequences
(x_{k})

with
x_{k}\in X_{k}

such that

d_{X_{k}}(p_{k},x_{k})
are uniformly bounded.
We denote by

d_{\omega}
the function on

X_{\omega}^{0}\times X_{\omega}^{0}

defined by

d_{\omega}((x_{k}),(y_{k})):=\operatorname{\lim_{\omega}}d_{X_{k}}(x_{k},y_{k})
.
We define

\operatorname{\lim_{\omega}}(X_{k},p_{k})
as the quotient
metric space
(X_{\omega}^{0},d_{\omega})/d_{\omega}=0
,
called the
ultralimit of
(X_{k},p_{k})

with respect to
\omega
.
For
\xi\in\operatorname{\lim_{\omega}}X_{k}
,
if
\xi=[(x_{k})]
,
then we write
x_{k}\to\xi
.

Let
(X_{k},p_{k})
be a sequence of pointed metric spaces.
Then
\operatorname{\lim_{\omega}}X_{k}
is complete.
If
\operatorname{diam}X_{k}
are uniformly bounded,
then
\operatorname{\lim_{\omega}}X_{k}
does not depend on the choices of

p_{k}
.
If each
X_{k}
is
r
-geodesic,
then so is
\operatorname{\lim_{\omega}}X_{k}
.
If each
X_{k}
is geodesically complete,
then so is
\operatorname{\lim_{\omega}}X_{k}
.
If a sequence
(X_{k})
of compact metric spaces
converges to some compact metric space
X

in the Gromov–Hausdorff topology,
then
\operatorname{\lim_{\omega}}X_{k}
is isometric to
X
.
If a sequence
(X_{k},p_{k})
of pointed proper geodesic metric spaces
converges to a pointed metric space
(X,p)

in the pointed Gromov–Hausdorff topology,
then
\operatorname{\lim_{\omega}}X_{k}
is isometric to
X
and

p_{k}\to p
.

Let
(\varphi_{k})
be a sequence of pointed
c
-Lipschitz maps
from
(X_{k},p_{k})
to
(Y_{k},\varphi_{k}(p_{k}))

between pointed metric spaces.
The
ultralimit
\operatorname{\lim_{\omega}}\varphi_{k}
of
(\varphi_{k})

is defined as a map from
from
\operatorname{\lim_{\omega}}(X_{k},p_{k})
to
\operatorname{\lim_{\omega}}(Y_{k},\varphi_{k}(p_{k}))

uniquely determined by

\left(\operatorname{\lim_{\omega}}\varphi_{k}\right)\left([x_{k}]\right):=\left[\left(\varphi_{k}(x_{k})\right)\right]
.
In this case,
the ultralimit
\operatorname{\lim_{\omega}}\varphi_{k}
is
c
-Lipschitz.
If in addition each
\varphi_{k}
is
c
-open,
then
\operatorname{\lim_{\omega}}\varphi_{k}
is
c
-open too.

2.5.
CAT
\boldsymbol{(\kappa)}
spaces

For
\kappa\in\operatorname{\mathbb{R}}
,
we denote by
M_{\kappa}^{n}

the simply connected, complete Riemannian
n
-manifold
of constant curvature
\kappa
,
and denote by
D_{\kappa}
the diameter of
M_{\kappa}^{n}
.
A complete metric space
X
is said to be

\operatorname{CAT}(\kappa)

if
X
is
D_{\kappa}
-geodesic,
and if every geodesic triangle in
X
with perimeter
smaller than
2D_{\kappa}

is not thicker than the comparison triangle
with the same side lengths in
M_{\kappa}^{2}
.
In this paper,
all
\operatorname{CAT}(\kappa)
spaces are assumed to be complete.
A metric space is said to be
\operatorname{CBA}

if for some
\kappa\in\operatorname{\mathbb{R}}

it is locally
\operatorname{CAT}(\kappa)
.

Let
X
be a
\operatorname{CAT}(\kappa)
space.
Every pair of points in
X
with distance smaller than
D_{\kappa}

can be uniquely joined by a geodesic.
Let
p\in X
.
For every
r\in(0,D_{\kappa}/2]
,
the balls
U_{r}(p)
and
B_{r}(p)
are convex.
For every
r\in(0,D_{\kappa})
,
the balls
U_{r}(p)
and
B_{r}(p)
are contractible inside themselves.
For
x,y\in U_{D_{\kappa}}(p)-\{p\}
,
we denote by
\angle_{p}(x,y)

the angle at
p

between
px
and
py
.
Put
\Sigma_{p}^{\prime}X:=\{\,px\mid x\in U_{D_{\kappa}}(p)-\{p\}\,\}
.
The angle
\angle_{p}
at
p

is a pseudo-metric on
\Sigma_{p}^{\prime}X
.
The

space of directions
\Sigma_{p}X
at
p
in
X

is defined as the
\angle_{p}
-completion of
the quotient metric space
\Sigma_{p}^{\prime}X/\angle_{p}=0
.
For
x\in U_{D_{\kappa}}(p)-\{p\}
,
we denote by
x_{p}^{\prime}\in\Sigma_{p}X

the starting direction of
px
at
p
.
The

tangent space
T_{p}X
at
p
in
X

is defined as
C_{0}(\Sigma_{p}X)
.
The space
\Sigma_{p}X
is
\operatorname{CAT}(1)
,
and the space
T_{p}X
is
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
.
In fact,
for a metric space
Z
,
the Euclidean cone
C_{0}(Z)

is
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
if and only if
Z
is
\operatorname{CAT}(1)
.

2.6.
Ideal boundaries of
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
spaces

Let
X
be a metric space with metric
d_{X}
.
Two rays
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\colon[0,\infty)\to X

are said to be

asymptotic

if
\sup_{t\in[0,\infty)}d_{X}(\gamma_{1}(t),\gamma_{2}(t))

is finite.
The asymptotic relation gives an equivalence relation
on the set of all rays in
X
.
The

ideal boundary
\partial_{\infty}X
of
X

is defined as the set of all asymptotic equivalence classes of rays in
X
.
For a ray
\gamma
in
X
,
we denote by
\gamma(\infty)

the asymptotic equivalent class of
\gamma
in
\partial_{\infty}X
.

Let
X
be a
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space.
For every
p\in X
, and for every
\xi\in\partial_{\infty}X
,
there exists a unique ray
\gamma\colon[0,\infty)\to X

with
\gamma(0)=p
and
\gamma(\infty)=\xi
.
For
p\in X
and
\xi\in\partial_{\infty}X
,
we denote by
\gamma_{p\xi}

the unique ray emanating from
p
to
\xi
,
by
p\xi
the image of
\gamma_{p\xi}
,
and by
\xi_{p}^{\prime}\in\Sigma_{p}X
the starting direction of
p\xi
at
p
.
For
p\in X
, and
\xi,\eta\in\partial_{\infty}X
,
we denote by
\angle_{p}(\xi,\eta)

the angle at
p

between
p\xi
and
p\eta
.
The

angle metric
\angle
on
\partial_{\infty}X

is defined by

\angle(\xi,\eta):=\sup_{p\in X}\angle_{p}(\xi,\eta)
.
The

Tits metric
d_{\operatorname{\mathrm{T}}}
on
\partial_{\infty}X

is defined as the length metric on
\partial_{\infty}X

induced from
\angle
.
Notice that
\angle=\min\{d_{\operatorname{\mathrm{T}}},\pi\}
,
and
d_{\operatorname{\mathrm{T}}}
possibly takes the value
\infty
.
We denote by
\partial_{\operatorname{\mathrm{T}}}X

the ideal boundary
\partial_{\infty}X

equipped with the Tits metric
d_{\operatorname{\mathrm{T}}}
,
and call it the

Tits boundary of
X
.
Then
\partial_{\operatorname{\mathrm{T}}}X
is a
\operatorname{CAT}(1)
space.
The Euclidean cone
C_{0}(\partial_{\operatorname{\mathrm{T}}}X)

is isometric to the Euclidean cone
C_{0}(\partial_{\infty}X)
over
the ideal boundary
\partial_{\infty}X
with the angle metric
\angle
.

2.7.
Geodesically complete CAT
\boldsymbol{(\kappa)}
spaces

A
\operatorname{CAT}(\kappa)
space is said to be

locally geodesically complete

(or has
geodesic extension property
)
if every geodesic defined on a compact interval can be extended to
a local geodesic beyond endpoints.
A
\operatorname{CAT}(\kappa)
space is

geodesically complete

if every geodesic can be extended to a local geodesic defined on
\operatorname{\mathbb{R}}
.
Every locally geodesically complete
\operatorname{CAT}(\kappa)
space
is geodesically complete.
The geodesical completeness for

\operatorname{CAT}(\kappa)
spaces
is preserved under the ultralimit.

We refer the readers to
[
18
]
for
the basic properties of
\operatorname{GCBA}
spaces, that is,
locally compact, separable, locally geodesically complete
metric spaces with an upper curvature bound.
Let
X
be a proper, geodesically complete
\operatorname{CAT}(\kappa)
space.
For every
p\in X
,
the space
\Sigma_{p}X
is compact and geodesically complete,
and
T_{p}X
is proper and geodesically complete.
In fact,
for a
\operatorname{CAT}(1)
space
Z
,
the Euclidean cone
C_{0}(Z)
is geodesically complete
if and only if
Z
is geodesically complete
and not a singleton.

2.8.
Dimensions of CAT
\boldsymbol{(\kappa)}
spaces

Kleiner
[
15
]
formulated
the
geometric dimension

\dim_{\mathrm{G}}

for
\operatorname{CBA}
spaces
as
the smallest function from the class of
\operatorname{CBA}
spaces
to
\operatorname{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{\infty\}

such that
(1)
\dim_{\mathrm{G}}X=0
if and only if

(2)
\dim_{\mathrm{G}}X\geq 1+\dim_{\mathrm{G}}\Sigma_{p}X

for all

.
In this case,
we have

\dim_{\mathrm{G}}X=1+\sup_{p\in X}\dim_{\mathrm{G}}\Sigma_{p}X.

We quote the theorem of Kleiner
[
15
, Theorem A]

in the following form:

Theorem 2.1
.

(
[
15
]
)

Let

be a complete
\operatorname{CBA}

coincides the supremum

of compact subsets

of

;

.

Let
\operatorname{\lim_{\omega}}(X_{k},p_{k})

[
17
, Lemma 11.1]

Let
X
be a
\operatorname{GCBA}
space.
Every relatively compact open subset of

has finite covering dimension
(see
[

, Subsection 5.3]
).
The covering dimension

,

m

3.
Almost spherical suspension structure

3.1.
Multi-fold suspenders in CAT(1) spaces

Let
Z
be a
\operatorname{CAT}(1)
space with metric
d_{Z}
.
We say that
a point
p_{0}
in
Z
is a
suspender
in
Z

if there exists another point
q_{0}
in
Z

such that for every
z\in Z
we have

(3.1)

d_{Z}(p_{0},z)+d_{Z}(z,q_{0})=\pi.

In this case,
we say that
p_{0}
and
q_{0}
are
opposite
to each other.
By (
3.1
),
if
p_{0}
and
q_{0}
are mutually opposite suspenders in
Z
,
then
d_{Z}(p_{0},q_{0})=\pi
.

We say that an
m
-tuple
(p_{1},\dots,p_{m})
of points in
Z
is
an
m
-suspender
in
Z

if there exists another
m
-tuple
(q_{1},\dots,q_{m})
of points in
Z

satisfying the following properties:

(1)

for all
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}
,
the points
p_{i}
and
q_{i}

are mutually opposite suspenders in
Z
;

(2)

for all distinct
i,j\in\{1,\dots,m\}
,
we have

d_{Z}(p_{i},p_{j})=\pi/2
,

d_{Z}(p_{i},q_{j})=\pi/2
,
and
d_{Z}(q_{i},q_{j})=\pi/2
.

In this case, we say that
(p_{1},\dots,p_{m})
and

(q_{1},\dots,q_{m})
are
opposite
to each other.

For a point
z
in
Z
,
we say that
a point
\bar{z}
in
Z
is an
antipode of
z

if we have
d_{Z}(z,\bar{z})\geq\pi
.
We denote by
\operatorname{Ant}(z)

the set of all antipodes of
z
,
and call it the
antipodal set of
z
.
We say that
a subset
A
of
Z
is

symmetric

if
A
contains
\bigcup_{p\in A}\operatorname{Ant}(p)
.

We say that a symmetric subset
A
of
Z
is

round

if every point in
A
is a suspender in
Z
.
Notice that if
Z
admits a round subset,
then
\operatorname{diam}Z=\pi
.
A subset
A
of
Z
is round
if and only if
for each
p\in A

there exists a unique point
q
in
A
such that

p
and
q
are mutually opposite suspenders in
Z
.

For a subset
A
of
Z
,
we denote by
A^{\perp}

the set of all points
z
in
Z
with
d_{Z}(z,A)\geq\pi/2
,
and call it the
polar set of
A
.

From the existence of an
m
-suspenders,
we find an
m
-fold spherical suspension structure
for
\operatorname{CAT}(1)
spaces
(
[
23
, Proposition 3.7]
).

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
Z
be a
\operatorname{CAT}(1)
space.
Assume that there exist mutually opposite
m
-suspenders

(p_{1},\dots,p_{m})
and
(q_{1},\dots,q_{m})
in
Z
.
Then there exists
a round, closed
\pi
-convex subset
C_{m-1}
of
Z

containing
(p_{1},\dots,p_{m})
and
(q_{1},\dots,q_{m})

such that
C_{m-1}
is isometric to
\operatorname{\mathbb{S}}^{m-1}
.
Moreover,

Z
isometrically splits as a spherical join

C_{m-1}\ast C_{m-1}^{\perp}
,
where
C_{m-1}^{\perp}
is the polar set of
C_{m-1}
.

3.2.
Relaxed multi-fold suspenders in CAT(1) spaces

Let
Z
be a
\operatorname{CAT}(1)
space with metric
d_{Z}
.
We say that
Z
is

penetrable

if there exists a dense subset
Z_{0}
of
Z

such that for each
p\in Z_{0}

there exists
q\in Z_{0}
with
d_{Z}(p,q)\geq\pi
.
If
Z
is penetrable, then
\operatorname{diam}Z\geq\pi
.
If
Z
has at least two path-connected components,
then it is penetrable.
The penetrability of
\operatorname{CAT}(1)
spaces is closed
under the ultralimit.
Notice that if
Z
is geodesically complete,
then
Z
is penetrable,
provided
Z
contains at least two points.

Let
\delta\in(0,1)
.
We say that
a point
p_{0}
in
Z
is a
(1,\delta)
-suspender

if there exists another point
q_{0}
in
Z

satisfying

(3.2)

\sup_{z\in Z}\left\{\,d_{Z}(p_{0},z)+d_{Z}(z,q_{0})\,\right\}<\pi+\delta.

In this case,
we say that
p_{0}
and
q_{0}
are
opposite
to each other.

The notion of the
(1,\delta)
-suspenders
is a perturbed one of the rigid
1
-suspenders
for penetrable
\operatorname{CAT}(1)
spaces
(
[
23
, Lemma 4.1]
).

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
Z
be a penetrable
\operatorname{CAT}(1)
space with metric
d_{Z}
.
Let
p_{0}
and
q_{0}
be mutually opposite

(1,\delta)
-suspenders in
Z
.
Then we have

(3.3)

\inf_{z\in Z}\left\{\,d_{Z}(p_{0},z)+d_{Z}(z,q_{0})\,\right\}>\pi-\delta.

We say that an
m
-tuple
(p_{1},\dots,p_{m})
of points in
Z
is
an
(m,\delta)
-suspender

if there exists another
m
-tuple
(q_{1},\dots,q_{m})
of points in
Z

satisfying the following:

(1)

for all
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}
,
the points
p_{i}
and
q_{i}

are mutually opposite
(1,\delta)
-suspenders in
Z
;

(2)

for all distinct
i,j\in\{1,\dots,m\}
,
we have

(3.4)

d_{Z}(p_{i},p_{j})<\frac{\pi}{2}+\delta,\quad d_{Z}(p_{i},q_{j})<\frac{\pi}{2}+\delta,\quad d_{Z}(q_{i},q_{j})<\frac{\pi}{2}+\delta.

In this case, we say that
(p_{1},\dots,p_{m})
and

(q_{1},\dots,q_{m})
are
opposite
to each other.

From Lemma
3.2
we can derive the following
(
[
23
, Lemma 3.3]
):

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
Z
be a penetrable
\operatorname{CAT}(1)
space with metric
d_{Z}
.
Let
(p_{1},\dots,p_{m})
and
(q_{1},\dots,q_{m})
be mutually opposite

(m,\delta)
-suspenders in
Z
.
Then the following hold:

(1)

for all
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}

we have

(3.5)

\inf_{z\in Z}\left\{\,d_{Z}(p_{i},z)+d_{Z}(z,q_{i})\,\right\}>\pi-\delta;

(2)

for all distinct
i,j\in\{1,\dots,m\}
,
we have

(3.6)

d_{Z}(p_{i},p_{j})>\frac{\pi}{2}-2\delta,\quad d_{Z}(p_{i},q_{j})>\frac{\pi}{2}-2\delta,\quad d_{Z}(q_{i},q_{j})>\frac{\pi}{2}-2\delta.

Lemma 3.5
.

For every

In the process of the proof of Theorem

we will use the following:

Lemma 3.6
.

For every
\epsilon\in(0,1)
,
and for every

,

satisfying the following property:

and

4.
Asymptotic geometric regularity

4.1.
Busemann functions on CAT(0) spaces

Let
X
be a
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space with metric
d_{X}
.
Let
\gamma\colon[0,\infty)\to X
be a ray in
X
.
The
Busemann function
b_{\gamma}\colon X\to\operatorname{\mathbb{R}}

along
\gamma

is defined by

b_{\gamma}(x):=\lim_{t\to\infty}\left(d_{X}\left(x,\gamma(t)\right)-t\right).

The Busemann function
b_{\gamma}
is
1
-Lipschitz and convex.
For
r\in\operatorname{\mathbb{R}}
,
we denote by
B_{r}(\gamma)

the closed
(-r)
-horoball
b_{\gamma}^{-1}((-\infty,-r])
.
Note that
for every
x\in X
,
and for every
r\in(0,\infty)
with
b_{\gamma}(x)>-r
,
we have

(4.1)

d_{X}\left(x,B_{r}(\gamma)\right)=b_{\gamma}(x)+r,

where
d_{X}\left(x,B_{r}(\gamma)\right)
is the distance
from
x
to
B_{r}(\gamma)
.
Let
\xi:=\gamma(\infty)
.
Take
x\in X
.
For every
y\in X-\{x\}
,
we have the first variation formula

(4.2)

(b_{\gamma}\circ\gamma_{xy})_{+}^{\prime}(a)=-\cos\angle_{x}(\xi,y)

for Busemann functions,
where

\gamma_{xy}\colon[a,b]\to X
is the geodesic
from
x
to
y
,
and
(b_{\gamma}\circ\gamma_{xy})_{+}^{\prime}(a)

is the right derivative of
b_{\gamma}\circ\gamma_{xy}
at
a

(see e.g.,
[
12
, Lemma 3.3]
).
Let
\gamma_{x\xi}\colon[0,\infty)\to X
be the ray
from
x
to
\xi
.
For all
s,t\in[0,\infty)
with
s<t
,
we have

(4.3)

b_{\gamma}\left(\gamma_{x\xi}(s)\right)-b_{\gamma}\left(\gamma_{x\xi}(t)\right)=s-t.

We know the following basic property
(see e.g.,
[
22
, Lemma 5.1]
):

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
X
be a
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space.
Let
\gamma\colon[0,\infty)\to X
be a ray in
X
.
Let
\xi:=\gamma(\infty)
.
If for distinct points

in
X
we have

b_{\gamma}(x)=b_{\gamma}(y)
,
then

\angle_{x}(\xi,y)\leq\pi/2
and

\angle_{y}(\xi,x)\leq\pi/2
.

4.2.
Strainers at infinity

u
on
\operatorname{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
.

From Lemma
4.3

we derive the following first variation inequalities
for Busemann strainer maps
(cf.
[
18
, Proposition 8.5]
).

Lemma 4.4
.

Let

\varphi\colon X\to\operatorname{\mathbb{R}}^{m}

be a Busemann
(m,\delta)
-strainer map on
a geodesically complete
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space
X

with
\varphi=\left(b_{\gamma_{1}},\dots,b_{\gamma_{m}}\right)
.
Take a geodesic
\gamma\colon[a,b]\to X

joining distinct two points in
X
.
Then for each
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}
we have

\left|\frac{\left(b_{\gamma_{i}}\circ\gamma\right)(b)-\left(b_{\gamma_{i}}\circ\gamma\right)(a)}{d_{X}\left(\gamma(b),\gamma(a)\right)}-\left(b_{\gamma_{i}}\circ\gamma\right)^{+}(a)\right|<2\delta.

In particular, we have

\left|\frac{\left\|\left(\varphi\circ\gamma\right)(b)-\left(\varphi\circ\gamma\right)(a)\right\|}{d_{X}\left(\gamma(b),\gamma(a)\right)}-\left\|\left(\varphi\circ\gamma\right)^{+}(a)\right\|\right|<2\sqrt{m}\delta,

where

\left(\varphi\circ\gamma\right)^{+}(a)\in\operatorname{\mathbb{R}}^{m}

is defined by

\left(\varphi\circ\gamma\right)^{+}(a):=\left(\left(b_{\gamma_{1}}\circ\gamma\right)^{+}(a),\dots,\left(b_{\gamma_{m}}\circ\gamma\right)^{+}(a)\right).

If in addition for
j\in\{1,\dots,m\}
we have

\left(b_{\gamma_{j}}\circ\gamma\right)(a)=\left(b_{\gamma_{j}}\circ\gamma\right)(b)
,
then we have

\left\|\left(b_{\gamma_{j}}\circ\gamma\right)^{+}(a)\right\|<2\delta.

Moreover,
if

\left(\varphi\circ\gamma\right)(a)=\left(\varphi\circ\gamma\right)(b)
,
then

\left\|\left(\varphi\circ\gamma\right)^{+}(a)\right\|<2\sqrt{m}\delta.

Lemma
4.4

leads to the following geometric property:

Proposition 4.5
.

Let

\varphi\colon X\to\operatorname{\mathbb{R}}^{m}

be a Busemann
(m,\delta)
-strainer map on
a geodesically complete
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space
X
.
Let
\gamma\colon[a,b]\to X
be a geodesic
joining distinct two points in
X
.
Then for all
s,t\in[a,b)
we have

\left\|\left(\varphi\circ\gamma\right)^{+}(s)-\left(\varphi\circ\gamma\right)^{+}(t)\right\|\leq 4\sqrt{m}\delta.

If for
s_{1},s_{2}\in[a,b)
with
s_{1}\neq s_{2}

we have

\left(\varphi\circ\gamma\right)(s_{1})=\left(\varphi\circ\gamma\right)(s_{2})
,
then for all
t\in[a,b)
we have

\left\|\left(\varphi\circ\gamma\right)^{+}(t)\right\|\leq 6\sqrt{m}\delta.

The proof of Proposition
4.5

is left to the readers.

4.4.
Pseudo-strainer maps at infinity

From (
4.3
) it follows that
every Busemann function on a geodesically complete
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space
is
1
-open.

We are going to show that
every Busemann
(m,\delta)
-strainer map is
c
-open
for some constant
c
depending only on
m
and
\delta
.
To do this,
we consider more general maps with Busemann function coordinates.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
m\in\operatorname{\mathbb{N}}
and
\delta\in(0,1)
.
Let
X
be a geodesically complete
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space.
We say that a map
\varphi\colon X\to\operatorname{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
is a

Busemann
(m,\delta)
-pseudo-strainer map
on
X

if there exists an
m
-tuple

\left(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{m}\right)

of rays in
X

with
\varphi=\left(b_{\gamma_{1}},\dots,b_{\gamma_{m}}\right)

such that
for all
i,j\in\{1,\dots,m\}
with
i\neq j
,
we have

(4.4)

\left|\angle_{x}\left(\gamma_{i}(\infty),\gamma_{j}(\infty)\right)-\frac{\pi}{2}\right|<2\delta.

From a similar argument on an openness in

[
23
, Lemma 6.3]
,
we derive the following
(cf.
[
18
, Lemma 8.1]
in the
\operatorname{GCBA}
setting):

Lemma 4.6
.

For
m\in\operatorname{\mathbb{N}}
,
let
\delta\in(0,1)
satisfy
2(m-1)\delta<1
.
Let
\varphi\colon X\to\operatorname{\mathbb{R}}^{m}

be a Busemann
(m,\delta)
-pseudo-strainer map on
a geodesically complete
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space
X
.
Then
\varphi

is
1/(1-2(m-1)\delta)\sqrt{m}
-open.
In particular,
it is
2/\sqrt{m}
-open,
whenever
4(m-1)\delta<1
.

Proof.

For
m\in\operatorname{\mathbb{N}}
,
let
\delta\in(0,1)
be sufficiently small.
Let
\varphi\colon X\to\operatorname{\mathbb{R}}^{m}

be a Busemann
(m,\delta)
-pseudo-strainer map
with
\varphi=\left(b_{\gamma_{1}},\dots,b_{\gamma_{m}}\right)
.
For
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}
,
let
\xi_{i}:=\gamma_{i}(\infty)
.
For a fixed point
x_{0}
in
X
,
choose
r\in(0,\infty)
.
Take an element
u_{0}=(u_{0}^{1},\dots,u_{0}^{m})

in
\operatorname{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
with
\|u_{0}\|<r/2
.
The goal is to find a point
y_{\ast}
in
X

with
\varphi(y_{\ast})=\varphi(x_{0})+u_{0}

such that

(1-2(m-1)\delta)d_{X}(x_{0},y_{\ast})\leq\|u_{0}\|_{\ell^{1}}
,
where

d_{X}
is the metric of
X
,
and

\|u_{0}\|_{\ell^{1}}

is the
\ell^{1}
-norm of
u_{0}
.
To do this,
we are going to construct
y_{\ast}
as a limit point of a sequence

y_{1},y_{2},\dots
.

First we find
y_{1}\in X
with

d_{X}(x_{0},y_{1})\leq\|u_{0}\|_{\ell^{1}}

such that

(4.5)

\|\varphi(y_{1})-(\varphi(x_{0})+u_{0})\|_{\ell^{1}}\leq 2(m-1)\delta\|u_{0}\|_{\ell^{1}}.

Let
(e_{1},\dots,e_{m})

denote the standard orthonormal basis of
\operatorname{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
.
For
u_{0}^{1}\in\operatorname{\mathbb{R}}
,
by the property (
4.3
)
we can choose
x_{1}\in X

with
x_{1}\in x_{0}\xi_{1}

|b_{\gamma_{1}}(x_{1})-b_{\gamma_{1}}(x_{0})|=|u_{0}^{1}|
.
Notice that if
u_{0}^{1}=0
, then
x_{1}=x_{0}
.
From our assumption (
4.4
)
and the first variation formula (
4.2
)
for Busemann functions,
we derive

|b_{\gamma_{i}}(x_{1})-b_{\gamma_{i}}(x_{0})|<2\delta|u_{0}^{1}|

whenever
i\neq 1
.
This implies

\|\varphi(x_{1})-(\varphi(x_{0})+u_{0}^{1}e_{1})\|_{\ell^{1}}\leq 2(m-1)\delta|u_{0}^{1}|.

Successively,
for
u_{0}^{m}\in\operatorname{\mathbb{R}}
,
we can choose
x_{m}\in X

with
x_{m}\in x_{m-1}\xi_{m}

and

|d_{p_{m}}(x_{m})-d_{p_{m}}(x_{m-1})|=|u_{0}^{m}|
;
in particular,

\|\varphi(x_{m})-(\varphi(x_{m-1})+u_{0}^{m}e_{m})\|_{\ell^{1}}\leq 2(m-1)\delta|u_{0}^{m}|.

Put
y_{1}:=x_{m}
.
Then
we have
d_{X}(x_{0},y_{1})\leq\|u_{0}\|_{\ell^{1}}

and (
4.5
).

Inductively,
for
y_{k}\in X
and
u_{k}:=\varphi(x_{0})+u_{0}-\varphi(y_{k})
,
we can construct

y_{k+1}\in X
with
d_{X}(y_{k},y_{k+1})\leq\|u_{k}\|_{\ell^{1}}

such that

\|\varphi(y_{k+1})-(\varphi(y_{k})+u_{k})\|_{\ell^{1}}\leq 2(m-1)\delta\|u_{k}\|_{\ell^{1}}.

For each
k\in\operatorname{\mathbb{N}}
, we have

(4.6)

\|u_{k+1}\|_{\ell^{1}}\leq 2(m-1)\delta\|u_{k}\|_{\ell^{1}}\leq(2(m-1)\delta)^{k+1}\|u_{0}\|_{\ell^{1}}

(4.7)

d_{X}(x_{0},y_{k+1})\leq\sum_{i=0}^{k}d_{X}(y_{i},y_{i+1})\leq\frac{1-(2(m-1)\delta)^{k+1}}{1-2(m-1)\delta}\|u_{0}\|_{\ell^{1}},

where we set
y_{0}:=x_{0}
.
Hence
(y_{k})
is a Cauchy sequence in
B_{r}(x_{0})
.
Let
y_{\ast}
be the limit of
(y_{k})
in
B_{r}(x_{0})
.
Then (
4.6
) implies

\varphi(y_{\ast})=\varphi(x_{0})+u_{0}
,
and (
4.7
)
does

(1-2(m-1)\delta)d_{X}(x_{0},y_{\ast})\leq\|u_{0}\|_{\ell^{1}}
.
∎

As a corollary of Lemma
4.6
,
we see:

Lemma 4.7
.

For
m\in\operatorname{\mathbb{N}}
,
let
\delta\in(0,1)
satisfy
2(m-1)\delta<1
.
Then every Busemann
(m,\delta)
-strainer map
on a geodesically complete
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space
is
1/(1-2(m-1)\delta)\sqrt{m}
-open.
In particular,
it is
2/\sqrt{m}
-open,
whenever
4(m-1)\delta<1
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.3
,
every Busemann
(m,\delta)
-strainer map is
a Busemann
(m,\delta)
-pseudo-strainer map.
From Lemma
4.6
,
we conclude the lemma.
∎

4.5.
Regularity of strainer maps at infinity

We prove that every Busemann strainer map
is almost a submetry
(cf.
[
18
, Subsection 8.3]
).

Proposition 4.8
.

For every
\epsilon\in(0,1)
,
and for every
m\in\operatorname{\mathbb{N}}
,
there exists
\delta\in(0,1)
satisfying the following:
Let
X
be a geodesically complete
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space.
Let
\varphi\colon X\to\operatorname{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
be
a Busemann
(m,\delta)
-strainer map on
X
.
Then
\varphi
is
(1+\epsilon)
-Lipschitz and
(1+\epsilon)
-open.
In particular,
for every
x\in X

the directional derivative
D_{x}\varphi\colon T_{x}X\to\operatorname{\mathbb{R}}^{m}

of
\varphi
at
x

is
(1+\epsilon)
-Lipschitz and
(1+\epsilon)
-open.

For the proof of Proposition
4.8
,
we prepare the following lemmas.

First we examine a rigid case.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
Z
be a
\operatorname{CAT}(1)
space.
Let
(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{m})
be an
m
-suspender in
Z
.
For
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}
,
let
\gamma_{i}\colon[0,\infty)\to C_{0}(Z)
be the ray in
the Euclidean cone
C_{0}(Z)
over
Z

from the vertex
0
toward
\xi_{i}

defined by
\gamma_{i}(t):=t\xi_{i}
.
Then the map
\varphi\colon C_{0}(Z)\to\operatorname{\mathbb{R}}^{m}

defined by
\varphi:=\left(b_{\gamma_{1}},\dots,b_{\gamma_{m}}\right)

is a homogenous submetry.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.1
,
there exists a round, closed
\pi
-convex subset
C_{m-1}

of
Z

containing
(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{m})

such that

isometrically splits as

C_{m-1}\ast C_{m-1}^{\perp}
,
where

C_{m-1}
is isometric to
\operatorname{\mathbb{S}}^{m-1}
,
and

C_{m-1}^{\perp}
is the polar set of
C_{m-1}
.
The Euclidean cone
C_{0}(Z)
isometrically splits as

\operatorname{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times C_{0}\left(Y_{m-1}^{\perp}\right)
.
Observe that for each
s\xi\in C_{0}(Z)
, we have

\varphi(s\xi)=-s\left(\cos d_{Z}(\xi_{1},\xi),\dots,\cos d_{Z}(\xi_{m},\xi)\right),

where
d_{Z}
is the metric on
Z
.
Hence the map

-\varphi\colon C_{0}(Z)\to\operatorname{\mathbb{R}}^{m}

defined by
(-\varphi)(s\xi):=-\varphi(s\xi)

coincides with
the projection onto the
m
-dimensional Euclidean flat factor

\operatorname{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
in
C_{0}(Z)
.
Therefore
-\varphi
is a submetry,
and hence so is
\varphi
.
The homogeneity follows from the definition of
\varphi
.
∎

We next show the following:

Lemma 4.10
.

For every
\epsilon\in(0,1)

,

-large

.

Lemma
4.10
leads to the following:

Lemma 4.11

is

-open,
for the
u_{k}\in\operatorname{\mathbb{S}}^{m-1}
we find
\eta_{k}\in T_{x_{k}}X_{k}

with
d_{T_{x_{k}}X_{k}}\left(\eta_{k},\zeta_{k}\right)<\epsilon/2

such that

,

T_{x_{k}}X_{k}
.
Therefore we have

\left|\eta_{k}\right|<1+\epsilon/2
.
This contradicts (
4.9
).
∎

Now we prove Proposition
4.8

Proof of Proposition

.

\epsilon\in(0,1)

\delta\in(0,1)

X

\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space of

\varphi\colon X\to\operatorname{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

(n,\delta)
-strainer map on

with

.

.

is
(1+\epsilon)
-Lipschitz

5.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

We denote by
\vartheta_{n}\colon(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)

a positive function
depending only on
n

satisfying
\lim_{\delta\to 0}\vartheta_{n}(\delta)=0
.

Based on the same idea in
[
9
, Theorem 9.5]

of Burago–Gromov–Perelman
for
\operatorname{CBB}
spaces
with curvature bounded below,
we prove the following
for geoesically complete
\operatorname{CAT}(1)
spaces:

Theorem 5.3
.

For every
\epsilon\in(0,1)
,
and for every
n\in\operatorname{\mathbb{N}}
,
there exists
\delta\in(0,1)
such that
if a geodesically complete
\operatorname{CAT}(1)
space
Z

of
\dim_{\mathrm{G}}Z\leq n-1
satisfies

d_{\operatorname{\mathrm{GH}}}\left(Z,\operatorname{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}\right)<\delta
,
then
Z
is
(1+\epsilon)
-bi-Lipschitz homeomorphic to
\operatorname{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
.

Proof.

For
n\in\operatorname{\mathbb{N}}
,
let
\delta\in(0,1)
be small enough.
Let
Z
be a geodesically complete
\operatorname{CAT}(1)
space with metric
d_{Z}

of
\dim_{\mathrm{G}}Z\leq n-1

satisfying
d_{\operatorname{\mathrm{GH}}}\left(Z,\operatorname{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}\right)<\delta
.
Then the Euclidean cone
C_{0}(Z)
over
Z

is a geodesically complete
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space
satisfying
\dim_{\mathrm{G}}C_{0}(Z)\leq n
.
Since
Z
is isometric to
\partial_{\mathrm{T}}C_{0}(Z)
,
we have

d_{\operatorname{\mathrm{GH}}}\left(\partial_{\mathrm{T}}C_{0}(Z),\operatorname{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}\right)<\delta
.
In this case,
there exists an
(n,10\delta)
-strainer at infinity

\left(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}\right)
.
For each
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
,
take a ray
\gamma_{i}\colon[0,\infty)\to C_{0}(Z)

with
\xi_{i}=\gamma_{i}(\infty)
.
Then the map
\varphi_{0}\colon C_{0}(Z)\to\operatorname{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

defined by

\varphi_{0}=\left(b_{\gamma_{1}},\dots,b_{\gamma_{n}}\right)

is a Busemann
(n,10\delta)
-strainer map.
Due to Proposition
5.1
,
the map

\varphi_{0}
is a

\left(1+\vartheta_{n}(\delta)\right)
-bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism.

Identify the unit metric sphere centered at the vertex in
C_{0}(Z)
with

Z
,
and do the unit metric sphere centered at the origin in
\operatorname{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with

\operatorname{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
.
Define a bijective map
\varphi\colon Z\to\operatorname{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}

by

\varphi(z):=\frac{1}{\|\varphi_{0}(z)\|}.

Take distinct points
z_{1},z_{2}
in
Z
.
If
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
satisfy
d_{Z}(z_{1},z_{2})\geq\pi/100
,
then we see

\left|d_{\operatorname{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\left(\varphi(z_{1}),\varphi(z_{2})\right)-d_{Z}\left(z_{1},z_{2}\right)\right|<\left(1+\vartheta_{n}(\delta)\right)d_{Z}\left(z_{1},z_{2}\right),

where
d_{\operatorname{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}
is the metric on
\operatorname{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
.

Assume that

z_{1}
and
z_{2}
satisfy
d_{Z}(z_{1},z_{2})<\pi/100
.
Set
l:=d_{Z}(z_{1},z_{2})
.
Let
\gamma\colon[0,l]\to Z
be the geodesic
in
Z
from
z_{1}
to
z_{2}
.
Take
t\in[0,l)
.
By Lemma
4.2
,
the
n
-tuple

\left((\xi_{1})_{\gamma(t)}^{\prime},\dots,(\xi_{n})_{\gamma(t)}^{\prime}\right)

is an
(n,10\delta)
-strainer in
\Sigma_{\gamma(t)}C_{0}(Z)
.
Let
\xi_{\gamma(t)}
be the direction
\left(D_{t}\gamma\right)(1)

in
\Sigma_{\gamma(t)}C_{0}(Z)
.
Since
\varphi_{0}
has Busemann function coordinates,
we have

\displaystyle\left(D_{\gamma(t)}\varphi_{0}\right)\left(\xi_{\gamma(t)}\right)

\displaystyle=-\left(\cos\angle_{\gamma(t)}\left(\xi_{1},\xi_{\gamma(t)}\right),\dots,\cos\angle_{\gamma(t)}\left(\xi_{n},\xi_{\gamma(t)}\right)\right),

\displaystyle\left(D_{\gamma(t)}\varphi_{0}\right)\left(0_{\gamma(t)}^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle=-\left(\cos\angle_{\gamma(t)}\left(\xi_{1},0_{\gamma(t)}^{\prime}\right),\dots,\cos\angle_{\gamma(t)}\left(\xi_{n},0_{\gamma(t)}^{\prime}\right)\right).

Since
\varphi_{0}
is a

\left(1+\vartheta_{n}(\delta)\right)
-bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism,
we have

\left|\left\|\left(D_{\gamma(t)}\varphi_{0}\right)\left(\xi_{\gamma(t)}\right)\right\|-1\right|<\vartheta_{n}(\delta),\quad\left|\left\|\left(D_{\gamma(t)}\varphi_{0}\right)\left(0_{\gamma(t)}^{\prime}\right)\right\|-1\right|<\vartheta_{n}(\delta).

Applying Lemma
3.6
to
\Sigma_{\gamma(t)}C_{0}(Z)
,
we see

\left|\angle_{\gamma(t)}\left(\left(D_{\gamma(t)}\varphi_{0}\right)\left(\xi_{\gamma(t)}\right),\left(D_{\gamma(t)}\varphi_{0}\right)\left(0_{\gamma(t)}^{\prime}\right)\right)-\frac{\pi}{2}\right|<\vartheta_{n}(\delta);

in other words,
the tangent vector

\left(D_{\gamma(t)}\varphi_{0}\right)\left(\xi_{\gamma(t)}\right)

of
\varphi_{0}\circ\gamma
at
t

and the vector from
(\varphi_{0}\circ\gamma)(t)
to the origin
0

are almost perpendicular.
Hence we see

\left|\frac{\left\|\left(D_{\gamma(t)}\varphi\right)\left(\xi_{\gamma(t)}\right)\right\|}{\left\|\left(D_{\gamma(t)}\varphi_{0}\right)\left(\xi_{\gamma(t)}\right)\right\|}-1\right|<\vartheta_{n}(\delta).

Therefore we obtain

d_{\operatorname{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\left(\varphi(z_{1}),\varphi(z_{2})\right)\leq\int_{0}^{l}\left\|\left(D_{\gamma(t)}\varphi\right)\left(\xi_{\gamma(t)}\right)\right\|dt\\
<\left(1+\vartheta_{n}(\delta)\right)\int_{0}^{l}\left\|\left(D_{\gamma(t)}\varphi_{0}\right)\left(\xi_{\gamma(t)}\right)\right\|dt<\left(1+\vartheta_{n}(\delta)\right)d_{Z}(z_{1},z_{2}).

Set
\bar{l}:=d_{\operatorname{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\left(\varphi(z_{1}),\varphi(z_{2})\right)
.
Let
\bar{\gamma}\colon[0,\bar{l}]\to\operatorname{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
be
the geodesic in
\operatorname{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}

from
\varphi(z_{1})
to
\varphi(z_{2})
, and

\tilde{\gamma}\colon[0,l]\to\operatorname{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}

the constant-speed reparametrization
of
\bar{\gamma}
defined by

\tilde{\gamma}(t):=\bar{\gamma}\left((\bar{l}/l)t\right)
.
Since the tangent vector

\left(D_{\gamma(t)}\varphi_{0}\right)\left(\xi_{\gamma(t)}\right)

and the vector from
(\varphi_{0}\circ\gamma)(t)
to the origin
0

are almost perpendicular,
we see

\left|\frac{\left\|\left(D_{\gamma(t)}\varphi_{0}\right)\left(\xi_{\gamma(t)}\right)\right\|}{\left\|\left(D_{t}\tilde{\gamma}\right)(1)\right\|}-1\right|<\vartheta_{n}(\delta).

Therefore we obtain

d_{\operatorname{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\left(\varphi(z_{1}),\varphi(z_{2})\right)=\int_{0}^{l}\left\|\left(D_{t}\tilde{\gamma}\right)(1)\right\|dt\\
>\left(1-\vartheta_{n}(\delta)\right)\int_{0}^{l}\left\|\left(D_{\gamma(t)}\varphi_{0}\right)\left(\xi_{\gamma(t)}\right)\right\|dt=\left(1-\vartheta_{n}(\delta)\right)d_{Z}(z_{1},z_{2}).

Thus
\varphi
is a

\left(1+\vartheta_{n}(\delta)\right)
-bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism.

In this way,
we have completed the proof of Theorem
5.3
.
∎

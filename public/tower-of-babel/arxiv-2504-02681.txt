Convergence of permuted products of exponentials
Michael Anshelevich, Anh Nguyen

Abstract

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a Banach algebra,
A\in\mathcal{M}
, and
\left\{A_{i,n}:1\leq i\leq n,n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
a triangular array whose row averages

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{i,n}\rightarrow A.

It is easy to come up with examples, even of two-by-two matrices, for which

\prod_{i=1}^{n}e^{A_{i,n}/n}\not\rightarrow e^{A}.

In this note we show that, while possible, such behavior is quite unusual. Under two sets of assumptions, for most rearrangements of the array
\left\{A_{i,n}:1\leq i\leq n\right\}
, not only do the corresponding products converge to
e^{A}
, but in fact we have simultaneous approximation of
e^{tA}
for all
t\in[0,1]
. More precisely, for each
n
, pick a permutation
\sigma\in S(n)
uniformly at random. Then under the assumptions listed below, permuted products converge uniformly in probability

(1)

\mathnormal{P}\left(\left\|{\prod_{i=1}^{[tn]}e^{A_{\sigma(i),n}/n}-e^{tA}}\right\|<\varepsilon\text{ for all }0\leq t\leq 1\right)\rightarrow 1\qquad\forall\varepsilon>0

or even uniformly almost surely

(2)

\mathnormal{P}\left(\left\|{\prod_{i=1}^{[tn]}e^{A_{\sigma(i),n}/n}-e^{tA}}\right\|\rightarrow 0\text{ for all }0\leq t\leq 1\right)=1.

The first type of assumption is when the
n
elements in each row of the array fall into only
o(n/logn)
distinct types.

Theorem 1
.

In a Banach algebra
\mathcal{M}
, let
A\in\mathcal{M}
, and
\left\{A_{i,n}:1\leq i\leq n,n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
be a uniformly bounded triangular array which satisfies
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{i,n}\rightarrow A
.
Let
c_{n}=o(n/\log n)
be a sequence of positive integers. Suppose that for each
n
, there is a family
\left\{B_{j,n}:1\leq j\leq c_{n},n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
and an assignment
j(i)
such that

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|{A_{i,n}-B_{j(i),n}}\right\|=o(1).

Then permuted products converge almost surely in the sense of equation (
2
).

If the Banach algebra
\mathcal{M}
has dimension
d<\infty
, the unit ball in
\mathcal{M}
has an
\varepsilon
-net with
\sim\varepsilon^{-d}
elements. It follows that for matrix algebras, the assumptions of Theorem
1
hold for any uniformly bounded array, and the corresponding permuted products converge uniformly almost surely. One can allow the bound on the array to grow with
n
, at the cost of lower growth rate for
c_{n}
. But in the finite-dimensional case, a better growth rate is achieved by a different approach,

Theorem 2
.

Let
A
and
\left\{A_{i,n}\right\}
be as above in the algebra of
d\times d
complex matrices (with the operator norm). Denote

L^{\infty}_{n}=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\left\|{A_{i,n}}\right\|,\quad L^{1}_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|{A_{i,n}}\right\|.

If for some
\delta>0
,

(3)

(L^{1}_{n})^{4+\delta}e^{3L^{1}_{n}}L^{\infty}_{n}=o(n),

then we have uniform convergence (
1
) of permuted products in probability. If in addition,

(4)

(L^{1}_{n})^{3}e^{3L^{1}_{n}}L^{\infty}_{n}=o(n/\log n).

the uniform convergence (
2
) is almost surely.

We next list several examples where the assumptions (in the matrix case) take a more concrete form.

Example 3
.

If
L^{\infty}_{n}=o(n)
and
(L^{1}_{n})
are uniformly bounded, we have convergence of the permuted products in probability. Recall that these are precisely the standard assumptions on a triangular array of scalars with

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i,n}/n\rightarrow\alpha

to satisfy

\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(1+\frac{\alpha_{i,n}}{n}\right)\rightarrow e^{\alpha}.

If
L^{\infty}_{n}=o(n/\log n)
, the convergence is a.s.

The following is a more general example.

Example 4
.

Take
k_{n}
matrices of the same norm
L^{\infty}_{n}=o(n)
, with the remaining
n-k_{n}
matrices uniformly bounded in
n
. Then for

k_{n}=\frac{n}{3L^{\infty}_{n}}(\log\frac{n}{L^{\infty}_{n}}-(4+2\delta)\log\log\frac{n}{L^{\infty}_{n}}),

we have
L^{1}_{n}\approx\frac{k_{n}}{n}L^{\infty}_{n}\approx\frac{1}{3}\log\frac{n}{L^{\infty}_{n}}
unbounded, but
(L^{1})^{4+\delta}e^{3L_{1}}L^{\infty}_{n}=o(n)
, so the permuted products still converge in probability.

If we take

k_{n}=\frac{n}{3L^{\infty}_{n}}(\log\frac{n}{L^{\infty}_{n}}-\log\log n-(3+\delta)\log\log\frac{n}{L^{\infty}_{n}}),

then
(L^{1})^{3}e^{3L_{1}}L^{\infty}_{n}=o(\frac{n}{\log n})
, and so the permuted products converge almost surely. In particular, we have such convergence for large
L^{\infty}_{n}
,

L^{\infty}_{n}=\frac{n}{\log n(\log\log n)^{3+2\delta}},\quad k_{n}\approx\frac{1}{3}\log n(\log\log n)^{3+2\delta}.

In the opposite direction, we may take all matrices to be uniformly bounded by (unbounded in
n
)

L^{\infty}_{n}=\frac{1}{3}(\log n-(5+\delta)\log\log n).

For a final example of an intermediate regime, for
0<t\leq 1
and
0<\alpha<1
or
\alpha=1
,
\beta\leq 0
,

L^{\infty}_{n}=\frac{1}{3t}n^{1-\alpha}(\log n)^{1-\beta},\quad k_{n}\approx\alpha tn^{\alpha}(\log n)^{\beta}.

To prove Theorem
1
, we first show the result when
o(n/\log n)
distinct elements occur in the array with the same frequency, and then upgrade it to arbitrary frequencies. The proof of Theorem
2
naturally splits into two parts. In Proposition
8
, we translate the conditions on multiplicative convergence into conditions on the additive convergence. This part of the result still holds in an arbitrary Banach algebra. Then we prove additive convergence in Lemma
13
by using a concentration inequality. This part is proven only for finite dimensional matrices. Thus we do not know if the full result holds in a general Banach algebra.

.

There is a number of articles treating topics related to this work. The following list is not meant to be complete. The techniques are typically more sophisticated than ours.

In

[

]

. This last article implies that, in our context, with probability at least

\left\|{\prod_{i=1}^{n}e^{A_{\sigma(i),n}/n}-e^{A}}\right\|\leq\frac{L^{\infty}_{n}e^{\left\|{A}\right\|}}{\sqrt{n}}\ \sqrt{2e^{2}\log(d/\delta)}.

See also
[

]
. All in all, the key aim of these papers is to find the fastest rate of convergence, while our (of course, closely related) aim is to find the fastest rate of growth for the matrices under which convergence still holds.
We also emphasize that the convergence occurs uniformly to the whole path

,

, as well as the interplay between

and

.

Finally, in earlier work
[

]
of the first author with Pritchett, we considered arrays consisting of multiple copies of finitely many elements in a Banach algebra. These results are extended in Theorem

. For other results in a Banach algebra see

.

Remark 6
.

Concentration inequalities hold in some, but not all, Banach spaces, see for example
[
Pin94
]
and
[
CHL24
]
. So our results can be extended from matrices to these settings.

Concentration inequalities have also been extended to the context of (finite) von Neumann algebras
[
JZ15
]
, with probability replaced by the trace of the spectral projection. We however only use probability as a tool to estimate the size of a finite set, so it is not clear if these techniques apply to our context.

The paper is organized as follows. The next section treats the results in a Banach algebra. We first estimate the difference of products of exponentials in terms of local averages. Then we prove convergence for arrays of repeated matrices. In the final section, we use matrix Bernstein inequality to prove convergence in the matrix case. We finish with an example on randomized evolution families.

Remark 7

(Setup)
.

Choose a sequence of positive integers
a_{n}\rightarrow\infty
,
a_{n}=o(n)
. Denote
b_{n}=[n/a_{n}]
, so that also
b_{n}\rightarrow\infty
,
b_{n}=o(n)
. Divide the set
\left\{1,\ldots,a_{n}b_{n}\right\}
into
b_{n}
groups
V_{j,n}
of consecutive elements of size
a_{n}
, so that for
j=1,2,\ldots,b_{n}
,

V_{j,n}=\left\{(j-1)a_{n}+1,\ldots,(j-1)a_{n}+a_{n}\right\}.

Proposition 8
.

Let
A
and
\left\{A_{i,n}:1\leq i\leq n,n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
in a Banach algebra satisfy
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{i,n}=A_{n}
with
A_{n}\rightarrow A
.

Suppose that
(L^{1}_{n})^{2}e^{L^{1}_{n}}=o(b_{n})
,

(5)

\left\|{\frac{1}{a_{n}}\sum_{i\in V_{j,n}}A_{i,n}-A_{n}}\right\|e^{L^{1}_{n}}\leq\varepsilon,\quad 1\leq j\leq b_{n},

and

(6)

\left|\frac{1}{a_{n}}\sum_{i\in V_{j,n}}\left\|{A_{i,n}}\right\|-L^{1}_{n}\right|e^{L^{1}_{n}}\leq\varepsilon,\quad 1\leq j\leq b_{n}.

Then

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\left\|{\prod_{i=1}^{[tn]}e^{A_{i,n}/n}-e^{tA}}\right\|\leq 2\varepsilon.

Proof.

For each
j
,

\begin{split}\left\|{\prod_{i\in V_{j,n}}e^{A_{i,n}/n}-e^{A_{n}/b_{n}}}\right\|&\leq\left\|{\prod_{i\in V_{j,n}}e^{A_{i,n}/n}-(1+\sum_{i\in V_{j,n}}A_{i,n}/n)}\right\|\\
&\quad+\left\|{\sum_{i\in V_{j,n}}A_{i,n}/n-A_{n}/b_{n}}\right\|+\left\|{1+A_{n}/b_{n}-e^{A_{n}/b_{n}}}\right\|.\end{split}

Using
e^{\left|x\right|}-(1+\left|x\right|)\leq\frac{1}{2}\left|x\right|^{2}e^{\left|x\right|}
and (
6
), the first term is bounded above by

\begin{split}\prod_{i\in V_{j,n}}e^{\left\|{A_{i,n}}\right\|/n}-(1+\sum_{i\in V_{j,n}}\left\|{A_{i,n}}\right\|/n)&\leq\frac{1}{2n^{2}}e^{\sum_{i\in V_{j,n}}\left\|{A_{i,n}}\right\|/n}\left(\sum_{i\in V_{j,n}}\left\|{A_{i,n}}\right\|\right)^{2}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2n^{2}}e^{a_{n}(L^{1}_{n}+\varepsilon e^{-L^{1}_{n}})/n}\left(a_{n}(L^{1}_{n}+\varepsilon e^{-L^{1}_{n}})\right)^{2}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2b_{n}^{2}}e^{(L^{1}_{n}+\varepsilon)/b_{n}}\left(L^{1}_{n}+\varepsilon\right)^{2}.\end{split}

Similarly, using (
5
), the second term is bounded by
\varepsilon e^{-L^{1}_{n}}/b_{n}
. Thus

\left\|{\prod_{i\in V_{j,n}}e^{A_{i,n}/n}-e^{A_{n}/b_{n}}}\right\|\leq\frac{1}{2b_{n}^{2}}e^{(L^{1}_{n}+\varepsilon)/b_{n}}\left(L^{1}_{n}+\varepsilon\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{b_{n}}\varepsilon e^{-L^{1}_{n}}+\frac{1}{2b_{n}^{2}}\left\|{A_{n}}\right\|^{2}e^{\left\|{A_{n}}\right\|/b_{n}}.

Also, for
k<a_{n}
,

\begin{split}\left\|{\prod_{i=1}^{k}e^{A_{i,n}/n}-e^{kA_{n}/n}}\right\|&\leq\frac{1}{2b_{n}^{2}}e^{(L^{1}_{n}+\varepsilon)/b_{n}}\left(L^{1}_{n}+\varepsilon\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{b_{n}}(L^{1}_{n}+\varepsilon+\left\|{A_{n}}\right\|)+\frac{1}{2b_{n}^{2}}\left\|{A_{n}}\right\|^{2}e^{\left\|{A_{n}}\right\|/b_{n}},\end{split}

where in the second term we use a cruder estimate (
6
) instead of (
5
).

Therefore for
k=[tn]-[tn/a_{n}]a_{n}
, noting that
\left\|{A_{n}}\right\|\leq L^{1}_{n}
,

\begin{split}\left\|{\prod_{i=1}^{[tn]}e^{A_{i,n}/n}-e^{tA_{n}}}\right\|&=\left\|{\left(\prod_{j=1}^{[tn/a_{n}]}\prod_{i\in V_{j,n}}e^{A_{i,n}/n}\right)\prod_{i=[tn/a_{n}]a_{n}+1}^{[tn]}e^{A_{i,n}/n}-\left(e^{A_{n}/b_{n}}\right)^{[tb_{n}]}e^{kA_{n}/n}}\right\|\\
&\leq\sum_{j=1}^{[tn/a_{n}]}\left\|{\prod_{i\in V_{j,n}}e^{A_{i,n}/n}-e^{A_{n}/b_{n}}}\right\|\prod_{k\neq j}e^{\max(\sum_{i\in V_{k,n}}\left\|{A_{i,n}}\right\|/n,\left\|{A_{n}}\right\|/b_{n})}\\
&\quad+\left\|{\prod_{i=[tn/a_{n}]a_{n}+1}^{[tn]}e^{A_{i,n}/n}-e^{kA_{n}/n}}\right\|\prod_{k\neq j}e^{\max(\sum_{i\in V_{k,n}}\left\|{A_{i,n}}\right\|/n,\left\|{A_{n}}\right\|/b_{n})}\\
&\leq\sum_{j=1}^{b_{n}}\left(\frac{1}{2b_{n}^{2}}e^{(L^{1}_{n}+\varepsilon)/b_{n}}\left(L^{1}_{n}+\varepsilon\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{b_{n}}\varepsilon e^{-L^{1}_{n}}+\frac{1}{2b_{n}^{2}}\left\|{A_{n}}\right\|^{2}e^{\left\|{A_{n}}\right\|/b_{n}}\right)\\
&\quad\times e^{(L^{1}_{n}+\varepsilon)(b_{n}-1)/b_{n}}\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{b_{n}}\left(e^{(L^{1}_{n}+\varepsilon)/b_{n}}\left(L^{1}_{n}+\varepsilon\right)^{2}+(L^{1}_{n}+\varepsilon+\left\|{A_{n}}\right\|)+\left\|{A_{n}}\right\|^{2}e^{\left\|{A_{n}}\right\|/b_{n}}\right)\\
&\quad\times e^{(L^{1}_{n}+\varepsilon)(b_{n}-1)/b_{n}}\\
&\leq\frac{3}{2b_{n}}\left(e^{\varepsilon/b_{n}}\left(L^{1}_{n}+\varepsilon\right)^{2}+(L^{1}_{n}+\varepsilon+\left\|{A_{n}}\right\|)+\left\|{A_{n}}\right\|^{2}\right)e^{L^{1}_{n}+\varepsilon}\\
&\quad+\left(\varepsilon e^{-L^{1}_{n}}\right)e^{L^{1}_{n}+\varepsilon}.\end{split}

Since also
A_{n}\rightarrow A
, we conclude that uniformly in
t
,

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|{\prod_{i=1}^{[tn]}e^{A_{i,n}/n}-e^{tA}}\right\|\leq\varepsilon e^{\varepsilon}.\qed

Lemma 9
.

Let
a_{n}=o(n/\log n)
be a sequence of positive integers. In a Banach algebra
\mathcal{M}
, let
A
and
\left\{B_{i,n}:1\leq i\leq a_{n},n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
be uniformly bounded and satisfy
\frac{1}{a_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{a_{n}}B_{i,n}\rightarrow A
. Consider the array
\left\{A_{i,n}:1\leq i\leq n,n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
, where each element
B_{i,n}
appears
[n/a_{n}]
times, with the rest of the elements uniformly bounded. For each
n
, pick a permutation
\sigma\in S(n)
uniformly at random. Then

\mathnormal{P}\left(\left\|{\prod_{i=1}^{[tn]}e^{A_{\sigma(i),n}/n}-e^{tA}}\right\|\rightarrow 0\text{ for all }0\leq t\leq 1\right)=1.

Proof.

To simplify notation, we will assume that all
\left\|{A_{i,n}}\right\|\leq 1
. The case of finite
a_{n}
was already considered in
[
AP23
]
, so we continue to assume that
a_{n}\rightarrow\infty
and denote
b_{n}=[n/a_{n}]
. Without loss of generality, we may also assume that
A_{i+ka_{n},n}=B_{i,n}
for
0\leq k<b_{n}
. For every permutation
\sigma\in S(n)
, let
w
be the word in the letters
\left\{B_{i,n}:1\leq i\leq a_{n}\right\}
obtained from it by restriction: write the word
A_{\sigma(1),n}\ldots A_{\sigma(n),n}
, and then only keep the letters of the form
B_{j,n}
. Then each word corresponds to exactly
(b_{n}!)^{a_{n}}\frac{n!}{(a_{n}b_{n})!}
permutations, and the uniform distribution on permutations carries over to the uniform distribution on words. Moreover, exactly one of these permutations fixes the final
(n-a_{n}b_{n})
elements; denote

e_{t}^{w}=\prod_{i=1}^{[tn]}e^{A_{\sigma(i),n}/n}.

for that permutation. Finally, denote
\overline{w}
the standard word, corresponding to the identity permutation.

We recall some results and notation from
[
AP23
]
. For a word
w
, denote
w_{i}[j]
the number of occurrences of
B_{i,n}
among the first
j
letters, and

\tau(w)=\frac{1}{b_{n}}\left(\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k,\ell\leq a_{n}\\
1\leq j\leq n\end{subarray}}\left|w_{k}[j]-w_{\ell}[j]\right|+1\right).

Then
w
can be transformed into the standard word
\overline{w}
by no more than
n^{2}\tau(w)
transpositions of neighboring letters. Each transposition changes the norm of the product of exponentials by no more than

\frac{1}{n^{2}}\max\left\|{[A_{i,n},A_{j,n}]}\right\|e^{L^{1}_{n}}\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}}2e.

Therefore

\left\|{e_{t}^{\overline{w}}-e_{t}^{w}}\right\|\leq\tau(w)2e.

Also,

\mathnormal{P}\left(\tau(w)>\frac{p_{n}}{\sqrt{b_{n}}}\right)\leq 2a_{n}^{2}\frac{\binom{2b_{n}}{b_{n}-p_{n}\sqrt{b_{n}}+1}}{\binom{2b_{n}}{b_{n}}}\sim 2a_{n}^{2}e^{-p_{n}^{2}}

Taking
p(n)=\sqrt{3\log n}
, we thus get

\begin{split}\mathnormal{P}\left(\left\|{e_{t}^{\overline{w}}-e_{t}^{w}}\right\|\geq\sqrt{\frac{3a_{n}\log n}{n}}2e\right)&\leq 2\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{3}}=o\left(\frac{1}{n(\log n)^{2}}\right).\end{split}

By assumption,
\frac{a_{n}\log n}{n}=o(1)
, while the probabilities are summable. Thus by the Borel-Cantelli lemma,
\left\|{e_{t}^{w}-e_{t}^{\overline{w}}}\right\|\rightarrow 0
a.s.

Next, any two permutations corresponding to a word
w
can be obtained from each other by at most
n(n-a_{n}b_{n})\leq na_{n}
transpositions of neighboring elements. Thus for such a permutation and the corresponding word,

\left\|{\prod_{i=1}^{[tn]}e^{A_{\sigma(i),n}/n}-e_{t}^{w}}\right\|\leq\frac{a_{n}}{n}2e=o(1).

Finally, for the standard word, the conditions in Proposition
8
are satisfied with
\varepsilon=0
. Therefore we have a Lie-Trotter formula, with the estimate

\begin{split}\left\|{e_{t}^{\overline{w}}-e^{tA_{n}}}\right\|&\leq\frac{1}{b_{n}}6e=o(1).\end{split}

Combining the three estimates, we obtain almost sure convergence of
\prod_{i=1}^{[tn]}e^{A_{\sigma(i),n}/n}
to
e^{tA}
.
∎

Remark 10

.

It follows from the assumption that

\left\|{\prod_{i=1}^{n}e^{A_{i,n}/n}-\prod_{i=1}^{n}e^{B_{j(i),n}/n}}\right\|=o(1).

\left\|{B_{j,n}}\right\|
are bounded by

. Choose
\alpha_{n}=o(1)

. For each

and

(1-2\alpha_{n})(c_{n}/\alpha_{n})\leq a_{n}\leq c_{n}/\alpha_{n}.

with this

while

\begin{split}\sum_{j\in U_{n}}\left|\frac{1}{c_{n}/\alpha_{n}}-\frac{1}{a_{n}}\right|\left[\frac{\beta_{j}c_{n}}{\alpha_{n}n}\right]&\leq\frac{2\alpha_{n}}{a_{n}}\sum_{j\in U_{n}}\left[\frac{\beta_{j}c_{n}}{\alpha_{n}n}\right]=2\alpha_{n}=o(1).\qed\end{split}

3.
Matrix algebra results

To estimate the probability that the assumptions in Proposition
8

[

Proposition 11

(Matrix Bernstein inequality)
.

Let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}
be independent centered random
d\times d
matrices, with
\left\|{X_{i}}\right\|\leq L
. Let
S_{k}=X_{1}+\ldots+X_{k}
. Denote
v_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\E\left[\left\|{X_{i}}\right\|^{2}\right]
. Then

(7)

\mathnormal{P}\left(\left\|{S_{k}}\right\|>\varepsilon\right)\leq 2d\exp\left(-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}/2}{v_{k}+L\varepsilon/3}\right).

The following result from
[
Hoe63
,
GN10
]
allows us to apply the inequality above to random matrices drawn from a finite set without replacement.

Proposition 12
.

Let
C
be a finite set of
d\times d
matrices with sum
0
and norms bounded by
L
. For
k\leq\left|C\right|
, let
\mathbf{X}=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{k})
be independent, drawn from
C
uniformly with replacement, while
\mathbf{Y}=(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{k})
are drawn from
C
uniformly without replacement. Denote
S_{\mathbf{X}}=X_{1}+\ldots+X_{k}
and the same for
S_{\mathbf{Y}}
. Then

\mathnormal{P}\left(\left\|{S_{\mathbf{Y}}}\right\|>\varepsilon\right)\leq\inf_{\lambda>0}e^{-\lambda\varepsilon}\E\left[\tr\exp(\lambda S_{\tilde{\mathbf{Y}}})\right]\leq\inf_{\lambda>0}e^{-\lambda\varepsilon}\E\left[\tr\exp(\lambda S_{\tilde{\mathbf{X}}})\right]\leq 2d\exp\left(-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}/2}{v_{k}+L\varepsilon/3}\right),

where
\tilde{\mathbf{Y}}
and
\tilde{\mathbf{X}}
are the Hermitian dilations of
\mathbf{Y}
and
\mathbf{X}

Lemma 13
.

Let
\left\{A_{i,n}:1\leq i\leq n,n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}\subseteq M_{d}(\mathbb{C})
and
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{i,n}=A_{n}
. For permutations
\sigma\in S(n)
drawn uniformly at random, for
\varepsilon<3L^{1}_{n}
,

\mathnormal{P}\left(\exists j:1\leq j\leq b_{n},\left\|{\frac{1}{a_{n}}\sum_{i\in V_{j,n}}A_{\sigma(i),n}-A_{n}}\right\|>\varepsilon\right)\leq b_{n}2d\exp\left(-\frac{a_{n}\varepsilon^{2}/12}{L^{1}_{n}L^{\infty}_{n}}\right).

Proof.

For each
n
, let
\left\{Y_{i,n}:1\leq i\leq n\right\}
be random matrices drawn uniformly without replacement from
\left\{A_{i,n}:1\leq i\leq n\right\}
. Then
(Y_{1,n},\ldots,Y_{n,n})=(A_{\sigma(1),n},\ldots,A_{\sigma(n),n})
, with
\sigma\in S(n)
drawn uniformly at random. Combining the preceding propositions,

\begin{split}&\mathnormal{P}\left(\exists j:1\leq j\leq b_{n},\left\|{\frac{1}{a_{n}}\sum_{i\in V_{j,n}}A_{\sigma(i),n}-A_{n}}\right\|>\varepsilon\right)\\
&\quad=\mathnormal{P}\left(\exists j:1\leq j\leq b_{n},\left\|{\frac{1}{a_{n}}\sum_{i\in V_{j,n}}Y_{i,n}-A_{n}}\right\|>\varepsilon\right)\\
&\quad\leq\sum_{j=1}^{b_{n}}\mathnormal{P}\left(\left\|{\frac{1}{a_{n}}\sum_{i\in V_{j,n}}Y_{i,n}-A_{n}}\right\|>\varepsilon\right)\\
&\quad\leq b_{n}2d\exp\left(-\frac{a_{n}^{2}\varepsilon^{2}/2}{v_{a_{n}}+2L^{\infty}_{n}\varepsilon a_{n}/3}\right),\end{split}

where we note that
\left\|{A_{n}}\right\|\leq L^{1}_{n}\leq L^{\infty}_{n}
. Finally,

\begin{split}v_{a_{n}}&=\frac{a_{n}}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|{A_{i,n}-A_{n}}\right\|^{2}\leq\frac{a_{n}}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\left\|{A_{i,n}}\right\|^{2}+2\left\|{A_{i,n}}\right\|\left\|{A_{n}}\right\|+\left\|{A_{n}}\right\|^{2})\\
&\leq a_{n}(L^{1}_{n}L^{\infty}_{n}+3(L^{1}_{n})^{2})\leq 4a_{n}L^{1}_{n}L^{\infty}_{n}\end{split}

and it remains to substitute the bound on
\varepsilon
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2
.

For a fixed
\varepsilon>0
, denote

S_{n}=\left\{\sigma\in S(n):\text{ conditions }\eqref{Eq:Ineq-1}\text{ and }\eqref{Eq:Ineq-2}\text{ hold for the array}\left\{A_{\sigma(i),n}\right\}\right\}.

First suppose that assumption (
3
) holds. Choose
a_{n},b_{n}
so that

(L^{1}_{n})^{2}e^{L^{1}_{n}}=o(b_{n}),\quad\log(b_{n})(L^{1}_{n}L^{\infty}_{n}e^{2L^{1}_{n}})\leq(L^{1}_{n})^{2+\delta}L^{\infty}_{n}e^{2L^{1}_{n}}=o(a_{n}).

Then

b_{n}\exp\left(-\frac{a_{n}\varepsilon^{2}/12}{L^{1}_{n}L^{\infty}_{n}e^{2L^{1}_{n}}}\right)\rightarrow 0.

Thus by Lemma
13
and its scalar version, for fixed
\varepsilon
and large
n
,
\mathnormal{P}\left(S_{n}\right)
is close to
1
. The conclusion follows from the estimate in Proposition
8
.

Under the assumption (
4
), choose
a_{n},b_{n}
so that

(L^{1}_{n})^{2}e^{L^{1}_{n}}=o(b_{n}),\quad\log(n)(L^{1}_{n}L^{\infty}_{n}e^{2L^{1}_{n}})=o(a_{n}).

Then

\begin{split}\sum_{n}b_{n}\exp\left(-\frac{a_{n}\varepsilon^{2}/12}{L^{1}_{n}L^{\infty}_{n}e^{2L^{1}_{n}}}\right)&\leq\sum_{n}n\exp\left(-\frac{a_{n}\varepsilon^{2}/12}{L^{1}_{n}L^{\infty}_{n}e^{2L^{1}_{n}}}\right)\\
&=\sum_{n}n^{1-(\varepsilon^{2}/12)(a_{n}/\log(n)L^{1}_{n}L^{\infty}_{n}e^{2L^{1}_{n}})}<\infty.\end{split}

The rest of the argument is similar to the one above, with the Borel-Cantelli lemma leading to the almost sure convergence.
∎

Example 14
.

Let
A:[0,1]\rightarrow M_{d}(\mathbb{C})
be Riemann integrable. There exists an evolution family
\left\{U(s,t):0\leq s\leq t\leq 1\right\}
which satisfies
U(s,t)\circ U(t,r)=U(s,r)
,
U(t,t)=I
, and

U(s,t)=\int_{s}^{t}U(s,r)A(r)\,dr.

If
A
is continuous,

\partial_{t}U(s,t)=U(s,t)A(t).

Moreover, this family can be interpreted as a product integral or a time-ordered exponential:

U(s,t)=\prod_{s}^{t}e^{A(x)}\,dx=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\prod_{i=[sn]+1}^{[tn]}e^{A(i/n)/n}.

We now note that we have almost sure convergence

\prod_{i=[sn]+1}^{[tn]}e^{A_{i,n}/n}\rightarrow e^{(t-s)\int_{0}^{1}A(x)\,dx}

under either of the following assumptions. In either case, both
L^{\infty}_{n}\leq\sup_{0\leq x\leq 1}\left\|{A(x)}\right\|
, and
L^{1}_{n}\rightarrow\int_{0}^{1}\left\|{A(x)}\right\|\,dx
are uniformly bounded.

•

A_{i,n}=A(\sigma(i)/n)
, where
\sigma\in S(n)
is drawn uniformly at random. Equivalently,
A_{i,n}=A(T_{\sigma}(i/n))
, where
T_{\sigma}
is the (measure-preserving) bijection of
[0,1]
permuting subintervals in its partition into
n
parts of equal length.

•

A_{i,n}
are i.i.d. drawn from the distribution of
A
(that is,
\mathnormal{P}\left(A_{i,n}\in S\right)=\left|\left\{t:A(t)\in S\right\}\right|
). Intuitively,
A_{i,n}=A(T(i/n))
, where
T
is drawn uniformly at random from the set of all measure-preserving bijections of
[0,1]
. Such a uniform distribution exists
[
Den86
]
although it is not very natural.

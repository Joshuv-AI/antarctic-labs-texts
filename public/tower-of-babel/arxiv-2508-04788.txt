Quantum-impurity sensing of altermagnetic order
V.A.S.V. Bittencourt, H. Hosseinabadi, J. Sinova, L. Šmejkal, J. Marino

Abstract
Quantum sensing with individual spin defects has emerged as a versatile platform to probe microscopic properties of condensed matter systems. Here we demonstrate that quantum relaxometry with nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centers in diamond can reveal the anisotropic spin dynamics of altermagnetic insulators together with their characteristic spin polarised bands. We show that the distance and orientation dependent relaxation rate of a nearby quantum impurity encodes signatures of momentum space anisotropy in the spin diffusion response, a hallmark of altermagnetic order. This directional sensitivity is unprecedented in the landscape of quantum materials sensing, and it enables the distinction of altermagnets from conventional antiferromagnets via local, noninvasive measurements. Our results could spark new NV-sensing experiments on spin transport and symmetry breaking in altermagnets, and highlight the role of NV orientation to probe anisotropic phenomena in condensed matter systems.



Introduction –
Altermagnetism is a recently identified magnetic phase characterized by a unique spin arrangement of electrons in solids

;
Šmejkal et al. 2022b
;

;

However, unlike AFMs, ALMs exhibit time-reversal broken and spin-polarised bands, a characteristic of ferromagnets. Unlike the s-wave-like isotropic spin polarisation in ferromagnets, the spin polarisation in ALMs takes d, g, or i-wave form, i.e. a momentum-space structure of spin-split bands with energy iso-surfaces that are anisotropic, and intersect at 2, 4, or 6 spin-degenerate nodes
Šmejkal et al. 2022a

Quantum impurity relaxometry –
We consider the setup of Fig.

The QI magnetic moment, associated to a transition between two spin levels, couples to the stray magnetic field generated by the magnetic excitations of the material via dipole coupling. Spin diffusion in ALMs –
We obtain the response function
\chi_{\parallel}

\delta n_{\xi=\alpha,\beta}

Perspectives -
In this Letter, we discussed the quantum noise spectroscopy of insulating ALMs using NV centers, where the carriers of spin in the material are localized. In a similar way, quantum noise spectroscopy can also be used to study the properties of metallic ALMs (MALMs). In this case, additional considerations arise. Similar to the noise spectroscopy of generic metallic systems, one must account for the Johnson noise due to current fluctuations
Langsjoen et al. 2012
;
Agarwal et al. 2017
;
Chatterjee et al. 2022
. The notable feature of MALMs, however, is the mobile nature of magnetic moments in conjunction with strong spin-orbit interaction, leading to a qualitatively distinct dynamical spin response function and the resulting magnetic noise. The presence of spin-momentum locking leads to a rich dependence of the relaxation on the orientation of the NV center. Moreover, nodal structure of altermagneto electronic structure
Šmejkal et al. 2022e
, significantly alters the magnetic noise spectrum.
The gapless nature of the magnetic excitations also makes
T_{2}
-based spectroscopy a particularly powerful tool for probing the spin dynamics in such systems, as discussed below.

An alternative approach to probing ALMs is through
T_{2}
relaxometry
Machado et al. 2023
. The typically stronger
T_{2}
signal, as compared to
T_{1}
noise, together with its operational low-frequency range, makes it well-suited for studying systems with low-energy and gapless modes. These include, the longitudinal diffusive modes, hydrodynamic sound modes, as well as nodal magnons in MALMs mentioned above. Furthermore, recent advances in NV-based sensing allow for multi-qubit noise spectroscopy, which can provide spatially resolved information on magnetic noise correlations
Rovny et al. 2022
;
Rovny et al. 2024
. This technique offers powerful means to probe non-invasively spatio-temporal spreading of correlations in ALMs
Hosseinabadi et al. 2025
.

I
Magnetostatic Green’s function and the geometric factor of the relaxation rate

In this section we present explicitly the relation between the stray magnetic field
\hat{\vec{B}}_{\rm{QI}}
in Eq.(1) and the spin density
\hat{\vec{s}}
and obtain explicitly the geometric factor
\mathcal{C}_{\{\Phi\}}(d,\vec{k})
of Eq. (2). The stray magnetic field
\hat{\vec{B}}_{\rm{QI}}
is given by the the stray magnetic field
\hat{\vec{B}}
written in the quantum impurity (QI) principal axis frame. We keep to the setup of Fig. 1, and consider that the QI principal axis lies in the yz plane, such that
\hat{\vec{B}}_{\rm{QI}}=\mathcal{R}_{x}(\theta)\cdot\hat{\vec{B}}
, where
\mathcal{R}_{x}(\theta)
is a rotation around the
x
axis.

The field
\hat{\vec{B}}(\vec{r},t)
and the spin density
\vec{s}
are related by the magnetostatic Green’s function
Dovzhenko et al. 2018
:

\hat{\vec{B}}(\vec{r},t)=\tilde{\gamma}\int d^{3}\vec{r}^{\prime}\overleftrightarrow{\mathcal{D}}(\vec{r},\vec{r}^{\prime})\cdot\hat{\vec{s}}(\vec{r}^{\prime},t),

(S1)

where
\overleftrightarrow{\mathcal{D}}(\vec{r},\vec{r}^{\prime})=-\nabla_{\vec{r}}\nabla_{\vec{r}^{\prime}}(1/|\vec{r}-\vec{r}^{\prime}|)
. The elements of the magnetostatic Green’s function for a thin film read

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{jl}(\vec{r},\vec{r}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int d^{2}\vec{k}\frac{k_{j}k_{l}}{k}e^{-k|y-y^{\prime}|}e^{i\vec{k}\cdot(\vec{\rho}-\vec{\rho}^{\prime})}\quad\quad(j,l=x,z),

(S2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{yj}(\vec{r},\vec{r}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-\frac{i\rm{sign}(y-y^{\prime})}{2\pi}\int d^{2}\vec{k}k_{j}e^{-k|y-y^{\prime}|}e^{i\vec{k}\cdot(\vec{\rho}-\vec{\rho}^{\prime})}\quad\quad(j=x,z),

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{zz}(\vec{r},\vec{r}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int d^{2}\vec{k}ke^{-k|y-y^{\prime}|}e^{i\vec{k}\cdot(\vec{\rho}-\vec{\rho}^{\prime})},

where
k=\sqrt{k_{x}^{2}+k_{z}^{2}}
and
\vec{\rho}=x\vec{e}_{x}+z\vec{e}_{z}
(with a similar definition for
\vec{\rho}^{\prime}
). Furthermore, the spin density
\hat{\vec{s}}(\vec{r}^{\prime},t)
is proportional to
\delta(y)
, and can be written as
\hat{\vec{s}}(\vec{r},t)=\mathcal{R}_{y}(\varphi)\hat{\vec{s}}_{N}(\vec{r},t)
, where
\hat{\vec{s}}_{N}
is the spin density written in a frame of the Néel vector, and
\mathcal{R}_{y}(\varphi)
is a rotation around the
y
axis. We can then write
\hat{B}_{{\rm{QI}}}^{(-)}=\hat{B}_{{\rm{QI}},x}-i\hat{B}_{{\rm{QI}},y}
, which is required for calculating the relaxation rate, as

\displaystyle\hat{B}_{{\rm{QI}}}^{(-)}(d,t)

\displaystyle=\tilde{\gamma}\int d^{2}\vec{\rho}^{\prime}\Big[\mathcal{A}^{-}_{\theta,\varphi}(\vec{\rho}^{\prime},d)\hat{s}^{(-)}(\vec{\rho}^{\prime},t)+\mathcal{B}^{-}_{\theta,\varphi}(\vec{\rho}^{\prime},d)\hat{s}^{(+)}(\vec{\rho}^{\prime},t)+\mathcal{C}^{-}_{\theta,\varphi}(\vec{\rho}^{\prime},d)\hat{s}_{\parallel}(\vec{\rho}^{\prime},t)\Big],

(S3)

where the components of the spin operators are in the frame of the Néel vector. The relaxation rate in Eq. (1) has contribution from different correlators. As argued in the main text, we the most relevant for the setup we envision are the ones proportional to
\langle\{\hat{s}_{\parallel}(\vec{\rho}^{\prime},t),\hat{s}_{\parallel}(\vec{\rho}^{\prime,\prime},0)\}\rangle
, where
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
denotes the anticommutator. We substitute Eq. (
S3
) into Eq. (1) and write the corresponding integrals in the Fourier domain keeping only the aforementioned spin correlators, which gives

\Gamma[\omega]=\gamma^{2}\tilde{\gamma}^{2}\int\frac{d^{2}\vec{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\mathcal{C}_{\{\Phi\}}(d,\vec{k})C_{\parallel}(\vec{k},\omega),

(S4)

where
C_{\parallel}(\vec{k},\omega)
is the Fourier transform of the spin correlator.
\mathcal{C}_{\{\Phi\}}(d,\vec{k})
can be obtained via the factor
\mathcal{C}^{-}_{\theta,\varphi}
of Eq: (
S3
), which uses the Fourier domain form of the elements of the magnetostatic Green’s tensor. After some algebra we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\{\Phi\}}(d,\vec{k})

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{2}k^{2}e^{-2kd}\mathcal{F}_{\{\Phi\}}(\phi_{k})

(S5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\{\Phi\}}(\phi_{k})

\displaystyle=\cos^{2}(\varphi)\cos^{2}\left(\phi_{k}\right)\left(\cos^{2}(\theta)\sin^{2}\left(\phi_{k}\right)+\left(\sin(\theta)+2\cos\left(\phi_{k}\right)\right){}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\sin^{2}\left(\phi_{k}\right)\left(\cos^{2}(\theta)\sin^{2}(\varphi)\sin^{2}\left(\phi_{k}\right)+\cos^{2}(\theta)\sin(2\varphi)\sin\left(\phi_{k}\right)\cos\left(\phi_{k}\right)+\sin^{2}(\varphi)\left(\cos\left(\phi_{k}\right)-\sin(\theta)\right){}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+2\sin(\varphi)\cos(\varphi)\sin\left(\phi_{k}\right)\cos\left(\phi_{k}\right)\left(\cos\left(\phi_{k}\right)-\sin(\theta)\right)\left(\sin(\theta)+2\cos\left(\phi_{k}\right)\right)

The final form of the relaxation rate appearing in Eq. (2) is then obtained by relating
C_{\parallel}(\vec{k},\omega)
to the imaginary part of the response function
\chi_{\parallel}
via the fluctuation-dissipation theorem
Kubo 1966
.

II
Magnon diffusion tensor

We present here the full formula for the magnon diffusion tensor
\overleftrightarrow{D}
. We follow the procedured used for ferro- and antiferromagnets
Rezende et al. 2016
;
Rezende et al. 2019
. Our starting point is the excess density
\delta n_{\xi}(\vec{r})
of each magnon species
\xi=\alpha,\beta
is

\delta n_{\xi}(\vec{r})=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int d^{2}k\left(n_{\xi,\vec{k}}(\vec{r})-n_{\xi,k}^{0}\right),

(S6)

where
n_{\xi,k}^{0}
is the equilibrium distribution of a given magnon mode (the Bose-Einstein distribution). We can also write the excess magnon current is

\vec{j}_{\xi,\rm{Tot}}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int d^{2}k\vec{v}_{\xi\vec{k}}\left[n_{\xi,\vec{k}}(\vec{r})-n_{\xi,k}^{0}\right],

(S7)

\vec{v}_{\xi\vec{k}}=\partial\omega_{\xi\vec{k}}/\partial\vec{k}
is the magnon velocity. The steady-state excess distribution is given then by the solution of the Boltzmann equation

n_{\xi,\vec{k}}(\vec{r})-n_{\xi,k}^{0}=-\tau_{\xi,\vec{k}}\vec{v}_{\xi\vec{k}}\cdot\nabla n_{\xi,\vec{k}}(\vec{r}),

(S8)

where
\tau_{\xi,\vec{k}}
is the momentum relaxation rate.

The excess magnon current density can then be decomposed in two components
Rezende et al. 2016
: one related to changes on the equilibrium magnon distribution
n_{\xi,k}^{0}
due to the change of some parameter, such as the temperature, and the contribution due to
spatial accumulation of magnons
. To describe the magnon diffusive properties, we will focus on the latter which is given by

\vec{j}_{\xi}=-\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int d^{2}k\tau_{\xi,\vec{k}}\vec{v}_{\xi\vec{k}}\left[\vec{v}_{\xi\vec{k}}\cdot\nabla(n_{\xi,\vec{k}}(\vec{r})-n_{\xi,k}^{0})\right].

(S9)

We can then write such a current in terms of a magnon diffusion tensor. For that, we consider the following form for the excess magnon distribution

\delta n_{\xi,\vec{k}}(\vec{r})=n_{\xi,\vec{k}}(\vec{r})-n_{\xi,k}^{0}=n_{\xi,k}^{0}\varepsilon_{\xi,\vec{k}}g(\vec{r}),

(S10)

where
\varepsilon_{\xi,\vec{k}}=\hbar\omega_{\xi,\vec{k}}
, and thus the total magnon density accumulation is

\displaystyle\delta n_{\xi}(\vec{r})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int d^{2}k\left(n_{\xi,\vec{k}}(\vec{r})-n_{\xi,k}^{0}\right)

(S11)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int d^{2}kn_{\xi,\vec{k}}^{0}\varepsilon_{\xi,\vec{k}}g(\vec{r})=\frac{g(\vec{r})}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int d^{3}kn_{\xi,k}^{0}\varepsilon_{\xi,\vec{k}}.

We call from now on

I_{0}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int d^{2}kn_{\xi,\vec{k}}^{0}\varepsilon_{\xi,\vec{k}},

(S12)

and thus

g(\vec{r})=\frac{\delta n_{\xi}(\vec{r})}{I_{0}}.

(S13)

We have then

\nabla\delta n_{\xi,\vec{k}}(\vec{r})=n_{\xi,k}^{0}\varepsilon_{\xi,\vec{k}}\nabla g(\vec{r})=\frac{n_{\xi,k}^{0}\varepsilon_{\xi,\vec{k}}}{I_{0}}\nabla\delta n_{\xi}(\vec{r}),

(S14)

and thus Eq. (
S9
) reads

\vec{j}_{\xi}=-\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int d^{2}k\frac{n_{\xi,k}^{0}\varepsilon_{\xi,\vec{k}}}{I_{0}}\tau_{\xi,\vec{k}}\vec{v}_{\xi\vec{k}}\left[\vec{v}_{\xi\vec{k}}\cdot\nabla\delta n_{\xi}(\vec{r})\right]=-\frac{1}{I_{0}(2\pi)^{2}}\int d^{2}kn_{\xi,k}^{0}\varepsilon_{\xi,\vec{k}}\tau_{\xi,\vec{k}}\vec{v}_{\xi\vec{k}}\left[\vec{v}_{\xi\vec{k}}\cdot\nabla\delta n_{\xi}(\vec{r})\right].

(S15)

We write then for each component of the magnon excess current

j_{i,\xi}=-\left[\sum_{j}\frac{1}{I_{0}(2\pi)^{2}}\int d^{2}kn_{\xi,k}^{0}\varepsilon_{\xi,\vec{k}}\tau_{\xi,\vec{k}}v_{i,\xi\vec{k}}v_{j,\xi\vec{k}}\right]\nabla_{j}\delta n_{\xi}(\vec{r}),

(S16)

from where we identify the magnon diffusion tensor element as

D_{\xi,ij}=\frac{1}{I_{0}(2\pi)^{2}}\int d^{2}kn_{\xi,k}^{0}\varepsilon_{\xi,\vec{k}}\tau_{\xi,\vec{k}}v_{i,\xi\vec{k}}v_{j,\xi\vec{k}},

(S17)

and thus

j_{i,\xi}=-\sum_{j}D_{\xi,ij}\nabla_{j}\delta n_{\xi}(\vec{r}).

(S18)

Given the above considerations, we can now write a diffusion equation for each magnon branch as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\delta n_{\alpha}+\nabla\cdot\vec{j}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\tau_{s}}(\delta n_{\alpha}+\chi_{0}h),

(S19)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\delta n_{\beta}+\nabla\cdot\vec{j}_{\beta}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\tau_{s}}(\delta n_{\beta}-\chi_{0}h),

where
h
is a generalized force thermodynamically conjugated to
\delta n_{\xi}
, the static spin susceptibility is indicated by
\chi_{0}
, and the associated chemical potential is
(\delta n_{\xi}/\chi_{0}\pm h)

Fang et al. 2022
, with the different signs corresponding to the different magnon species
Flebus 2019
. The spin relaxation time is indicated by
\tau_{s}
. The magnon currents are then given by

\displaystyle\vec{j}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=-\overleftrightarrow{D}_{\alpha}\cdot\nabla(\delta n_{\alpha}+\chi_{0}h),

(S20)

\displaystyle\vec{j}_{\beta}

\displaystyle=-\overleftrightarrow{D}_{\beta}\cdot\nabla(\delta n_{\beta}-\chi_{0}h).

For the equations, we have used the fact that, because each branch has a different spin angular momentum, the static susceptibility and the chemical potential need to have opposite signs
Flebus 2019
.

II.1
Magnon bands in the Lieb lattice altermagnet

To calculate the elements of the diffusion tensor given in Eq. (
S17
), we need first the magnon velocities, which in turn depend on the magnon band structure. We consider a 2D altermagnet described by a Heisenberg Hamiltonian in a Lieb lattice, shown in Fig. 2 in the main text.

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=J_{1}\sum_{\langle\vec{r},\vec{r}^{\prime}\rangle}\hat{\vec{S}}_{i}\cdot\hat{\vec{S}}_{j}+\sum_{\langle\langle\vec{r},\vec{r}^{\prime}\rangle\rangle_{\pm}}(J_{2}\pm\Delta)\hat{\vec{S}}_{i}\cdot\hat{\vec{S}}_{j}

(S21)

\displaystyle+\frac{J_{\rm{EA}}}{2}\sum_{\vec{r}}\left(\hat{S}_{z,j}\right)^{2},

where
J_{1}
is the nearest-neighbor exchange between sublattices with opposite spins,
(J_{2}\pm\Delta)
are the next-nearest-neighbor exchange, which depend on the direction, and
J_{\rm{EA}}
is the on site easy-axis anisotropy. We first decompose the Hamiltonian in terms of intra- and intersublattice exchanges:

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=J_{1}\sum_{\vec{r}_{i},\{\vec{\delta}_{e}\}}\hat{\vec{S}}_{A,\vec{r}_{i}}\cdot\hat{\vec{S}}_{B,\vec{r}_{i}+\vec{\delta}_{e}}+\frac{J_{\rm{EA}}}{2}\sum_{\vec{r}_{i}}\left[\left(\hat{S}_{z,A,\vec{r}_{i}}\right)^{2}+\left(\hat{S}_{z,B,\vec{r}_{i}}\right)^{2}\right]

(S22)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\vec{r}_{i},\{\vec{\delta}_{1}\}}\left[(J_{2}+\Delta)\hat{\vec{S}}_{A,\vec{r}_{i}}\cdot\hat{\vec{S}}_{A,\vec{r}_{i}+\vec{\delta}_{1}}+(J_{2}-\Delta)\hat{\vec{S}}_{B,\vec{r}_{i}}\cdot\hat{\vec{S}}_{B,\vec{r}_{i}+\vec{\delta}_{1}}\right]

\displaystyle+\sum_{\vec{r}_{i},\{\vec{\delta}_{2}\}}\left[(J_{2}-\Delta)\hat{\vec{S}}_{A,\vec{r}_{i}}\cdot\hat{\vec{S}}_{A,\vec{r}_{i}+\vec{\delta}_{2}}+(J_{2}+\Delta)\hat{\vec{S}}_{B,\vec{r}_{i}}\cdot\hat{\vec{S}}_{B,\vec{r}_{i}+\vec{\delta}_{2}}\right].

The sets of vectors describing the positions of nearest and next-nearest neighbors are

\displaystyle\{\vec{\delta}_{e}\}

\displaystyle:\quad\vec{\delta}_{e}^{(1)}=\frac{a}{2}\vec{e}_{x}+\frac{a}{2}\vec{e}_{z},\quad\quad\vec{\delta}_{e}^{(2)}=-\vec{\delta}_{e}^{(1)},

(S23)

\displaystyle\vec{\delta}_{e}^{(3)}=\frac{a}{2}\vec{e}_{x}-\frac{a}{2}\vec{e}_{z},\quad\quad\vec{\delta}_{e}^{(4)}=-\vec{\delta}_{e}^{(3)}.

\displaystyle\{\vec{\delta}_{1}\}

\displaystyle:\quad\vec{\delta}_{1}^{(1)}=a\vec{e}_{x},\quad\quad\vec{\delta}_{1}^{(2)}=-\vec{\delta}_{1}^{(1)}.

\displaystyle\{\vec{\delta}_{2}\}

\displaystyle:\quad\vec{\delta}_{2}^{(1)}=a\vec{e}_{z},\quad\quad\vec{\delta}_{2}^{(2)}=-\vec{\delta}_{2}^{(1)}.

We then use the standard Holstein-Primakoff method, by first writting the spin operators in terms of the bosonic creation and annhiliation operator
\hat{a}_{\vec{r}_{i}}
and
\hat{b}_{\vec{r}_{i}}
:

\displaystyle\hat{S}_{A,\vec{r}_{i}}^{(+)}=\sqrt{2s}\hat{a}_{\vec{r}_{i}},\hskip 14.22636pt\hat{S}_{A,\vec{r}_{i}}^{(-)}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2s}\hat{a}_{\vec{r}_{i}}^{\dagger},\hskip 14.22636pt\hat{S}_{A,\vec{r}_{i}}^{(z)}=s-\hat{a}_{\vec{r}_{i}}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{\vec{r}_{i}},

(S24)

\displaystyle\hat{S}_{B,\vec{r}_{i}}^{(+)}=\sqrt{2s}\hat{b}_{\vec{r}_{i}}^{\dagger},\hskip 14.22636pt\hat{S}_{B,\vec{r}_{i}}^{(-)}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2s}\hat{b}_{\vec{r}_{i}},\hskip 14.22636pt\hat{S}_{B,\vec{r}_{i}}^{(z)}=-s+\hat{b}_{\vec{r}_{i}}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{\vec{r}_{i}},

here
\hat{a}_{\vec{r}_{i}}
and
\hat{b}_{\vec{r}_{i}}
We substitute the above expressions into Eq. (
S22
) and Fourier transform the operators:

\hat{a}_{\vec{r}_{i}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{\vec{k}}e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{r}_{i}}\hat{a}_{\vec{k}},

(S25)

with a similar expression for
\hat{b}
. We get the following bosonic Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\frac{\hat{\mathcal{H}}}{\hbar}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\vec{k}}\left[\omega_{a}(\vec{k})\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\vec{k}}\hat{a}_{\vec{k}}+\omega_{b}(\vec{k})\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{\vec{k}}\hat{b}_{\vec{k}}+g_{ab}(\vec{k})\left(\hat{a}_{\vec{k}}\hat{b}_{-\vec{k}}+\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\vec{k}}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{-\vec{k}}\right)\right],

(S26)

where

\displaystyle\hbar\omega_{a}(\vec{k})

\displaystyle=4s\left(J_{1}-2J_{2}+\frac{J_{\rm{EA}}}{2}\right)+8sJ_{2}\cos\left(\frac{a}{2}k_{z}+\frac{a}{2}k_{x}\right)\cos\left(\frac{a}{2}k_{z}-\frac{a}{2}k_{x}\right)+8s\Delta\sin\left(\frac{a}{2}k_{z}+\frac{a}{2}k_{x}\right)\sin\left(\frac{a}{2}k_{z}-\frac{a}{2}k_{x}\right),

(S27)

\displaystyle\hbar\omega_{b}(\vec{k})

\displaystyle=4s\left(J_{1}-2J_{2}+\frac{J_{\rm{EA}}}{2}\right)+8sJ_{2}\cos\left(\frac{a}{2}k_{z}+\frac{a}{2}k_{x}\right)\cos\left(\frac{a}{2}k_{z}-\frac{a}{2}k_{x}\right)-8s\Delta\sin\left(\frac{a}{2}k_{z}+\frac{a}{2}k_{x}\right)\sin\left(\frac{a}{2}k_{z}-\frac{a}{2}k_{x}\right),

\displaystyle\hbar g_{ab}(\vec{k})

\displaystyle=2sJ_{1}\left[\cos\left(\frac{a}{2}k_{z}+\frac{a}{2}k_{x}\right)+\cos\left(\frac{a}{2}k_{z}-\frac{a}{2}k_{x}\right)\right].

An external magnetic field
H_{0}
aligned with the anisotropy axis can be included by adding
\tilde{\gamma}H_{0}
to
\hbar\omega_{a}(\vec{k})
and subtracting
\tilde{\gamma}H_{0}
from
\hbar\omega_{b}(\vec{k})
.

The Hamiltonian Eq. (
S26
) is diagonalized by a Bogoliubov transformation to the magnon modes
\hat{\alpha}
and
\hat{\beta}
:

\frac{\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm{d}}}{\hbar}=\sum_{\vec{k}}\left[\omega_{\alpha}(\vec{k})\hat{\alpha}^{\dagger}_{\vec{k}}\hat{\alpha}_{\vec{k}}+\omega_{\beta}(\vec{k})\hat{\beta}^{\dagger}_{\vec{k}}\hat{\beta}_{\vec{k}}\right],

(S28)

where the magnon frequencies are given by

\displaystyle\omega_{\alpha}(\vec{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{a}(\vec{k})-\omega_{b}(\vec{k})+\sqrt{(\omega_{a}(\vec{k})+\omega_{b}(\vec{k}))^{2}-4g_{ab}^{2}(\vec{k})}}{2},

(S29)

\displaystyle\omega_{\beta}(\vec{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{-(\omega_{a}(\vec{k})-\omega_{b}(\vec{k}))+\sqrt{(\omega_{a}(\vec{k})+\omega_{b}(\vec{k}))^{2}-4g_{ab}^{2}(\vec{k})}}{2}.

The magnon bandgap
\Delta_{\rm{mag}}=\omega_{\alpha}(0)=\omega_{\beta}(0)
is given explicitly by

\Delta_{\rm{mag}}=\frac{4s}{\hbar}\sqrt{16J_{1}J_{\rm{EA}}+J_{\rm{EA}}^{2}},

(S30)

while the magnon frequency splitting is

\omega_{\alpha}(\vec{k})-\omega_{\beta}(\vec{k})=16s\Delta\sin\left(\frac{a}{2}k_{z}+\frac{a}{2}k_{x}\right)\sin\left(\frac{a}{2}k_{z}-\frac{a}{2}k_{x}\right).

(S31)

II.2
Diffusion tensor

In this paper, we work in the high-temperature regime, from which

n^{0}_{\xi,\vec{k}}\approx\frac{k_{B}T}{\varepsilon_{\xi,\vec{k}}}
, and thus Eq. (
S17
) simplifies to

D_{\xi,ij}=\frac{1}{V(2\pi)^{2}}\int d^{2}k\tau_{\xi,\vec{k}}v_{i,\xi\vec{k}}v_{j,\xi\vec{k}},

(S32)

where
V=\int d^{2}k/(2\pi)^{2}
is the volume of the Brillouin zone. We furthermore assume that
\tau_{\xi,\vec{k}}=\tau
, i.e., the magnon momentum relaxation time is independent of the momentum and of the magnon species. Such an assumption is justified in the high-temperature regime, in which most of the magnon relaxation is to lattice phonons. At low temperatures, other processes that are neglected in our description, such as relaxation due to magnon-magnon scattering, can become relevant
Eto et al. 2025
.

With the magnon bands we can evaluate the magnon velocities, and then calculate the magnon diffusion tensor in Eq. (
S17
). Even though the magnon velocities have convoluted forms, we can infer dome underlying symmetry properties that are a consequence of the symmetries of the Heisenberg Hamiltonian of Eq. (
4
). Those a better analyzed in a coordinate system rotated by
\pi/4
, such that

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{2}k_{z}}{2}+\frac{\sqrt{2}k_{x}}{2}

\displaystyle\rightarrow k_{x},

(S33)

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{2}k_{z}}{2}-\frac{\sqrt{2}k_{x}}{2}

\displaystyle\rightarrow k_{z}.

From Eqs. (
S27
) and (
S29
) we can then infer that the magnon bands are symmetric
\omega_{\xi}(\vec{k})=\omega_{\xi}(-\vec{k})
, and related by a
\pi/2
rotation in
k
-space:

\omega_{\alpha}(\vec{k})=\omega_{\beta}(\mathcal{R}_{\pi/2}\vec{k}),

(S34)

corresponding to the transformation
k_{x}\rightarrow k_{z}
, and
k_{z}\rightarrow-k_{z}
. We then identify the nodal lines

\displaystyle\omega_{\alpha}(k,0)

\displaystyle=\omega_{\beta}(k,0),

(S35)

\displaystyle\omega_{\alpha}(0,k)

\displaystyle=\omega_{\beta}(0,k).

We can then quickly infer that
v_{x,\alpha}=v_{z,\beta}
and
v_{z,\alpha}=-v_{x,\beta}
, and thus
D_{\alpha,xy}=-D_{\beta,xy}=D_{2}
.

From the explicit form of the magnon frequencies, we further obtain that

\displaystyle v_{x,\alpha}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{A}\cos(a^{\prime}k_{x})\sin(a^{\prime}k_{z})+\sin(a^{\prime}k_{x})\mathbb{F}^{(1)}(k_{x},k_{z})-\sin(a^{\prime}k_{x})\cos(a^{\prime}k_{z})\mathbb{F}^{(2)}(k_{x},k_{z}),

(S36)

\displaystyle v_{x,\beta}

\displaystyle=-\mathbb{A}\cos(a^{\prime}k_{x})\sin(a^{\prime}k_{z})+\sin(a^{\prime}k_{x})\mathbb{F}^{(1)}(k_{x},k_{z})-\sin(a^{\prime}k_{x})\cos(a^{\prime}k_{z})\mathbb{F}^{(2)}(k_{x},k_{z}),

\displaystyle v_{z,\alpha}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{A}\cos(a^{\prime}k_{z})\sin(a^{\prime}k_{x})+\sin(a^{\prime}k_{z})\mathbb{F}^{(1)}(k_{x},k_{z})-\sin(a^{\prime}k_{z})\cos(a^{\prime}k_{x})\mathbb{F}^{(2)}(k_{x},k_{z}),

\displaystyle v_{z,\beta}

\displaystyle=-\mathbb{A}\cos(a^{\prime}k_{z})\sin(a^{\prime}k_{x})+\sin(a^{\prime}k_{z})\mathbb{F}^{(1)}(k_{x},k_{z})-\sin(a^{\prime}k_{z})\cos(a^{\prime}k_{x})\mathbb{F}^{(2)}(k_{x},k_{z}).

where
a^{\prime}=a/\sqrt{2}
, and the functions
\mathbb{F}^{(1,2)}
are independent of
\Delta
and exhibit the following properties

\displaystyle\mathbb{F}^{(1,2)}(k_{x},k_{z})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{F}^{(1,2)}(k_{z},k_{x}),

(S37)

\displaystyle\mathbb{F}^{(1,2)}(-k_{x},k_{z})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{F}^{(1,2)}(k_{x},k_{z})=\mathbb{F}^{(1,2)}(k_{x},-k_{z})=\mathbb{F}^{(1,2)}(-k_{x},-k_{z}).

For
\Delta=0
, i.e. for an antiferromagnet, considering an integration domain that is symmetric in both
k_{x}
and
k_{z}
, one can show by decomposing the integration in Eq. (
S32
) in each quadrant of the
(k_{x},k_{z})
domain, that
D_{2}=0
. Thus a non-vanishing value of
D_{2}
, within the assumptions we have adopted, is a characteristic exclusive to ALMs. The same conclusions should be valid for general d-wave ALMs.

III
Altermagnetic spin diffusion response function

To obtain the spin diffusion response function, we use the magnon diffusion equations Eqs. (
S19
) and, where the symmetries of the ALM magnon bands, as discussed in the previous section, imply that the magnon diffusion tensors have the following form

\displaystyle\overleftrightarrow{D}_{\alpha}=\begin{bmatrix}D_{1}&D_{2}\\
D_{2}&D_{1}\end{bmatrix},\quad\quad\overleftrightarrow{D}_{\beta}=\begin{bmatrix}D_{1}&-D_{2}\\
-D_{2}&D_{1}\end{bmatrix}.

(S38)

We then write a diffusion equation for
\mathcal{S}_{\parallel}=\hbar(-\delta n_{\alpha}+\delta n_{\beta})
:

\partial_{t}\mathcal{S}_{\parallel}-\nabla\cdot\left[\overleftrightarrow{D}_{1}\cdot\nabla(\mathcal{S}_{\parallel}-\hbar\chi_{0}h)\right]+\nabla\cdot\left[\overleftrightarrow{D}_{2}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{S}_{\perp}\right]=-\frac{1}{\tau}(\mathcal{s}_{\parallel}-\hbar\chi_{0}h),

(S39)

where
\mathcal{S}_{\perp}=\hbar(\delta n_{\alpha}+\delta n_{\beta})
satisfies the equation

\partial_{t}\mathcal{S}_{\perp}-\nabla\cdot\left[\overleftrightarrow{D}_{1}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{S}_{\perp}\right]+\nabla\cdot\left[\overleftrightarrow{D}_{2}\cdot\nabla(\mathcal{S}_{\parallel}-\chi_{0}h)\right]=-\frac{1}{\tau}\mathcal{S}_{\perp},

(S40)

and we have defined

\displaystyle\overleftrightarrow{D}_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}D_{1}&0\\
0&D_{1}\end{bmatrix}\quad\quad\overleftrightarrow{D}_{2}=\begin{bmatrix}-D_{2}&0\\
0&D_{2}\end{bmatrix}.

(S41)

Equations (
S39
) and (
S40
) are obtained by writting the diffusion equations in a basis that diagonalizes the magnon diffusion tensors
\overleftrightarrow{D}_{\alpha,\beta}
. We can solve this set of equations in the Fourier domain, which gives

\mathcal{S}_{\parallel}(\omega,\vec{k})=\chi_{\parallel}(\omega,\vec{k})h

(S42)

where the response function is given by Eq. (5) of the main text:

\chi_{\parallel}(\omega,\vec{k})=\frac{\hbar\chi_{0}\left[D_{1}k^{2}+\frac{1}{\tau_{s}}-\frac{D_{2}^{2}k^{4}\cos^{2}(2\phi_{k})}{-i\omega+D_{1}k^{2}+1/\tau_{s}}\right]}{-i\omega+D_{1}k^{2}+\frac{1}{\tau_{s}}-\frac{D_{2}^{2}k^{4}\cos^{2}(2\phi_{k})}{-i\omega+D_{1}k^{2}+1/\tau_{s}}},

(S43)

where
\phi_{k}=\rm{arctan}(k_{x}/k_{z})
.

IV
Relaxation rate

With the response function Eq. (
S43
), we can now calculate the QI relaxation rate with Eq. (2) of the main text:

\Gamma[\omega]=\frac{\hbar\gamma^{2}\tilde{\gamma}^{2}}{2}{\rm{coth}}\left(\frac{\hbar\beta\omega}{2}\right)\int\frac{d^{2}\vec{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\mathcal{C}_{\{\Phi\}}(d,\vec{k})\chi^{\prime\prime}_{\parallel}(\omega,\vec{k}).

(S44)

We first recall that the geometric factor is given by
\mathcal{C}_{\{\Phi\}}(d,\vec{k})=(2\pi)^{2}k^{2}e^{-2kd}\mathcal{F}_{\{\Phi\}}(\phi_{k})
, where the function
\mathcal{F}_{\{\Phi\}}(\phi_{k})
depends only on the orientation angles, and we work in the high-temperature regime, such that:

\Gamma[\omega]=\frac{\gamma^{2}\tilde{\gamma}^{2}}{\beta\omega}\int d\phi_{k}dkk^{3}e^{-2kd}\mathcal{F}_{\{\Phi\}}(\phi_{k})\chi^{\prime\prime}_{\parallel}(\omega,\vec{k}).

(S45)

The imaginary part of the susceptibility is given explicitly by

\chi_{\parallel}^{\prime\prime}=\frac{\hbar\chi_{0}\omega(D_{1}k^{2}+\frac{1}{\tau_{s}})\left[\omega^{2}+(D_{1}k^{2}+\frac{1}{\tau_{s}})^{2}\right]\left[\omega^{2}+(D_{1}k^{2}+\frac{1}{\tau_{s}})^{2}-D_{2}^{2}k^{4}\cos^{2}(2\phi_{k})\right]}{\omega^{2}\left[\omega^{2}+(D_{1}k^{2}+\frac{1}{\tau_{s}})^{2}+D_{2}^{2}k^{4}\cos^{2}(2\phi_{k})\right]^{2}+(D_{1}k^{2}+\frac{1}{\tau_{s}})^{2}\left[\omega^{2}+(D_{1}k^{2}+\frac{1}{\tau_{s}})^{2}-D_{2}^{2}k^{4}\cos^{2}(2\phi_{k})\right]^{2}}.

(S46)

We write this function in terms of the dimensionless quantities

\tilde{k}=kl_{0},\quad\quad\tilde{\omega}=\omega\tau_{s}\quad\quad\tilde{D}_{2}=D_{2}/D_{1},

(S47)

where
l_{0}=\sqrt{D_{1}\tau_{s}}
, such that

\frac{\chi_{\parallel}^{\prime\prime}}{\hbar\chi_{0}}=\tilde{\chi}_{\parallel}^{\prime\prime}=\frac{\tilde{\omega}(\tilde{k}^{2}+1)\left[\tilde{\omega}^{2}+(\tilde{k}^{2}+1)^{2}\right]\left[\tilde{\omega}^{2}+(\tilde{k}^{2}+1)^{2}-\tilde{D}_{2}^{2}\tilde{k}^{4}\cos^{2}(2\phi_{k})\right]}{\tilde{\omega}^{2}\left[\tilde{\omega}^{2}+(\tilde{k}^{2}+1)^{2}+\tilde{D}_{2}^{2}\tilde{k}^{4}\cos^{2}(2\phi_{k})\right]^{2}+(\tilde{k}^{2}+1)^{2}\left[\tilde{\omega}^{2}+(\tilde{k}^{2}+1)^{2}-\tilde{D}_{2}^{2}\tilde{k}^{4}\cos^{2}(2\phi_{k})\right]^{2}}.

(S48)

We can then write the relaxation rate as

\displaystyle\Gamma[\omega]=\frac{\hbar\tau_{s}\chi_{0}\gamma^{2}\tilde{\gamma}^{2}}{\beta l_{0}^{4}}\left[\frac{1}{\tilde{\omega}}\int d\phi_{k}d\tilde{k}\tilde{k}^{3}e^{-2\tilde{k}\tilde{d}}\mathcal{F}_{\{\Phi\}}(\phi_{k})\tilde{\chi}^{\prime\prime}_{\parallel}(\tilde{\omega},\vec{\tilde{k}})\right]=\Gamma_{c}\tilde{\Gamma}[\omega].

(S49)

In the above equation
\Gamma_{c}=\hbar k_{B}T\tau_{s}\chi_{0}\gamma^{2}\tilde{\gamma}^{2}/l_{0}^{4}
is a characteristic relaxation rate and
\tilde{d}=d/l_{0}
. All the plots shown in the main text are obtained via numerical integration of the above equation using standard python libraries.

V
Spin conductivity

To estimate the characteristic relaxation rate
\Gamma_{c}
, we need the static spin susceptibility
\chi_{0}
, which is given by the ratio
D_{1}/\sigma
between the diffusion coefficient
D_{1}
and the spin conductivity
\sigma
. While a full evaluation of
\sigma
is out of scope of this paper, we can estimate it by using the procedure in
Wang et al. 2022
for antiferromagnets. From Einstein’s relation
\sigma=D_{1}\partial\rho/\partial\mu
, where
\mu
is the chemical potential and
\rho
is the non-equilibrium magnon spin density, which can be written as

\rho=\int\frac{d^{2}k}{(2\pi)^{2}}\left[\frac{1}{e^{\beta\hbar\omega_{\alpha,k}-\mu}-1}-\frac{1}{e^{\beta\hbar\omega_{\beta,k}+\mu}-1}\right].

(S50)

For the purposes of a simple estimate, we consider a low-momentum expansion of the magnon frequencies. We use for convenience the coordinate system of Sec. IIB

\displaystyle\omega_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=-\Delta_{\rm{ALM}}k_{x}k_{z}+\sqrt{v^{2}k^{2}+\Delta^{2}},

(S51)

\displaystyle\omega_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\Delta_{\rm{ALM}}k_{x}k_{z}+\sqrt{v^{2}k^{2}+\Delta^{2}}.

We do not consider the first contribution in the above expressions due to the ALM band splitting, such that

\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\mu}=\frac{(k_{B}T)^{2}}{\pi^{2}v^{3}}\left[{\rm{Li}}_{2}(e^{-\beta\Delta_{\alpha}})+{\rm{Li}}_{2}(e^{-\beta\Delta_{\beta}})\right],

(S52)

where
\Delta_{\alpha,\beta}=\Delta\pm\mu/\beta
. Typically, the chemical potential is induced by an external field which is much weaker than the magnon gap
\Delta
, as we have estimated in the main text. We thus set
\Delta_{\alpha,\beta}=\Delta
. With Eqs. (
S52
),(
S51
) we can estimate the spin conductivity which, in units of electric conductivity, is
\sim 3.8\times 10^{6}
S/m for the parameters used in the main text and at
200
K.

The calculations presented here are a rough estimate of the spin conductivity used as a starting point to assess the feasibility of our scheme. A more complete description can be done within the framework of, e.g.
Fang et al. 2022
, which also accommodates the ballistic spin transport regime. An extension to such a complete framework is postponed to a future work.

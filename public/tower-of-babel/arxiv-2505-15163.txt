A Categorical Decomposition of ℂ × \mathbb{C}^{\times} -fibered p p -biset Functors
Olcay Coşkun, Ruslan Muslumov

Abstract
We generalize Bouc’s construction of orthogonal idempotents in the double Burnside algebra to the setting of the double ℂ × \mathbb{C}^{\times} -fibered Burnside algebra. This yields a structural decomposition of the evaluations of ℂ × \mathbb{C}^{\times} -fibered biset functors on finite groups. We then construct a complete set of orthogonal idempotents in the category of ℂ × \mathbb{C}^{\times} -fibered p p -biset functors, leading to a categorical decomposition of this category into subcategories indexed by isomorphism classes of atoric p p -groups. Furthermore, we introduce the notion of vertices for indecomposable functors and establish that the Ext \mathrm{Ext} -groups between simple functors with distinct vertices vanish. As an application, we describe a set containing composition factors of the monomial Burnside functor, thereby providing new insights into its structure. Additionally, we develop a technique for analyzing fibered biset functors via their underlying biset structures. Keywords : Fibered biset functor, Fibered Burnside algebra, Idempotents, Monomial Burnside functor, Truncated algebra MSC[2020] 18A25, 18B99, 19A22 20J15

Introduction

After its introduction in
[
5
]
, Bouc’s theory of biset functors has become a central framework for studying operations coming from permutation actions on representation theoretic constructions. In particular, the classification and decomposition of
p
-biset functors over a field

[
7
]
and references there. A cornerstone of this theory is the construction of canonical orthogonal idempotents in the double Burnside algebra
kB(G,G)
, indexed by minimal sections of
G
, which allows for a fine decomposition of functor evaluations and a block-theoretic understanding of the category of
p
-biset functors.

A generalization of biset functors, introduced by Robert Boltje and the first author
[
1
]
incorporates multiplicative character data and yields a more refined structure. Although the new theory of fibered biset functors is more involved, it has found several applications and has become a useful tool in representation theory, see
[
9
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
8
]
.

In this paper, we focus on
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-fibered biset functors only and extend Bouc’s results from
[
6
]
to the fibered setting. Our goal is to construct and analyze a family of orthogonal idempotents in the fibered double Burnside algebra
B_{k}^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}(G,G)
that mirrors the structure and properties of Bouc’s idempotents in the classical setting.

To achieve this, we develop a truncated algebra
\mathcal{E}(G)
modeled on Bouc’s construction but adapted to the case of fibered bisets. We define a canonical basis of
\mathcal{E}(G)
using bisets of the form
[G\times G/(U,\upsilon)]
where
U
is a covering subgroup, that is, it has full coordinate projections, and
\upsilon
is a linear character, and we provide explicit formulas for the multiplication of these basis elements. We then construct a system of idempotents
\varphi^{G}_{K,\kappa}

(K,\kappa)
, where
K\leq\Phi(G)
is normal in
G

\kappa:K\to{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}
is a character, and show that these idempotents are mutually orthogonal and sum to the identity element of
\mathcal{E}(G)

The idempotents

replaces the idempotents

in

with a twist. In Bouc’s framework, every idempotent

can be expressed in terms of
\varphi_{1}^{G}

\varphi_{K,\kappa}^{G}
in terms of the ones where the character
\kappa
is faithful. Nevertheless we still obtain a decomposition of fibered biset functors via this set of idempotents. Namely, for any fibered biset functor

F
and any finite group
G

F(G)\cong\bigoplus_{(T,S)\in[\Pi(G)]}\bigoplus_{(K,\kappa)\in[\mathcal{M}_{T/S}^{*}]}\left(\varphi_{[K,\kappa]}^{T/S}F(T/S)\right)^{N_{G}(T,S)/T}.

Here,

, and

is a normal subgroup of

The main result of the paper is a decomposition theorem for the category

of
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-fibered
p
-biset functors. Assuming that the prime

\mathcal{F}_{k,p}^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}\simeq\prod_{M\in[\mathcal{A}t_{p}]}\widehat{c}_{M}\mathcal{F}_{k,p}^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}},

where the product is over isomorphism classes of atoric

-groups and for a given atoric

Bouc’s idempotent
as defined in Section
5

\mathcal{F}_{k,p}^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}

[
6

As an application of the above theorem, we define vertices of indecomposable
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-fibered biset functors and show that ext-groups of simple functors with distinct vertices vanishes. This in turn leads to a new approach to the problem of determining biset functor structure of a fibered biset functor.
In this paper, we have shown that for an indecomposable fibered
p
-biset functor

Truncated Algebra
\mathcal{E}(G)

Following Bouc’s approach to decomposing the double Burnside algebra via certain idempotents, we consider a truncated algebra
\mathcal{E}(G)

3.1

Let
G
be a group and let

be a commutative unitary ring such that the order of

\mathcal{E}(G):=\widetilde{e_{G}^{G}}\cdot B_{k}^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}(G,G)\cdot\widetilde{e_{G}^{G}}.

Let

be such that
p_{1}(U)=p_{2}(U)=G

with this projection property a

Y_{U,\upsilon}:=\widetilde{e_{G}^{G}}\left[\frac{G\times G}{U,\upsilon}\right]\widetilde{e_{G}^{G}}\in\mathcal{E}(G).

A similar algebra,

was constructed in

3.2

One can verify that the elements
Y_{U,\upsilon}
constitute a
k
-basis of
\mathcal{E}(G)
, where
(U,\upsilon)
ranges over a set of representatives of the
(G\times G)
-conjugacy classes of covering subgroups of
G\times G
. Furthermore, explicit formulas for the products of basis elements can be found as follows. Let
(U,\upsilon)
and
(V,\omega)
be in
\mathcal{M}_{G\times G}
with covering subgroups
U
and
V

G\times G

Y_{U,\upsilon}\cdot Y_{V,\omega}=\widetilde{e_{G}^{G}}\left(\Big[\frac{G\times G}{U,\upsilon}\Big]\widetilde{e_{G}^{G}}\Big[\frac{G\times G}{V,\omega}\Big]\right)\widetilde{e_{G}^{G}}.

The middle term can be evaluated:

\displaystyle\Big[\frac{G\times G}{U,\upsilon}\Big]\widetilde{e_{G}^{G}}\Big[\frac{G\times G}{V,\omega}\Big]=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{X\leq G}|X|\mu(X,G)\left(\Big[\frac{G\times G}{U,\upsilon}\Big]\Big[\frac{G\times G}{\Delta(X),1}\Big]\Big[\frac{G\times G}{V,\omega}\Big]\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X\leq G\\
\upsilon_{2}\mid_{H_{0}}=\omega_{1}\mid_{H_{0}}\end{subarray}}|X|\mu(X,G)\left(\Big[\frac{G\times G}{U*\Delta(X)*V,\upsilon*1*\omega}\Big]\right),

where
H_{0}=k_{2}(U)\cap X\cap k_{1}(V)

Y_{U,\upsilon}\cdot Y_{V,\omega}=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{X\in\delta(U,V)}|X|\mu(X,G)Y_{U*\Delta(X)*V,\upsilon*1*\omega}.

Here
\delta(U,V)
denotes the set of subgroups
X

G\times G
such that
U*\Delta(X)*V
is a covering subgroup of
G\times G
and
\upsilon_{2}\mid_{H_{0}}=\omega_{1}\mid_{H_{0}}
.

Bouc has shown in
[
5

that there exists a bijection between the covering subgroups of the form

in

and the subgroups

of

satisfying

and

such that

that contain

and are contained in

Applying these group-theoretic results, we obtain the following theorem. We omit the proof, as it closely follows the argument in

for bisets, which is also restated in

3.3 Proposition.

Let

be covering pairs. Then

Y_{U,\upsilon}\cdot Y_{V,\omega}=\frac{m_{G,H_{1}}}{|G|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}H_{1}\leq X\leq G\\
Xk_{2}(U)=Xk_{1}(V)=G\\
\upsilon_{2}\mid_{H_{1}}=\omega_{1}\mid_{H_{1}}\end{subarray}}|X|\mu(X,G)Y_{L*\Delta(X)*M,\upsilon*1*\omega},

(2)

where

.

3.4

If one of the groups

or

is contained in

and

then

This identity allows us to remove Möbius-weighted summations. This is central in proving orthogonality of certain idempotents that we shall define.

Next we examine a special case of these basis elements to form certain idempotents. Given a normal subgroup

\widetilde{e_{(K,\kappa)}^{G}}:=Y_{\Delta_{K}(G),\phi_{\kappa}}=\widetilde{e_{G}^{G}}\cdot e_{(K,\kappa)}^{G}\cdot\widetilde{e_{G}^{G}}.

The following is an elementary property.

3.5 Lemma.

Let
G
be a group. For a normal subgroup
K
of
G
contained in the Frattini subgroup of
G
, and
\kappa\in K^{*}
, we have

\widetilde{e_{(K,\kappa)}^{G}}=\widetilde{e_{G}^{G}}\cdot e_{(K,\kappa)}^{G}=e_{(K,\kappa)}^{G}\cdot\widetilde{e_{G}^{G}}.

(3)

Proof.

By expanding
\widetilde{e_{G}^{G}}
the product can be written as

\displaystyle\widetilde{e_{(K,\kappa)}^{G}}=\widetilde{e_{G}^{G}}\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X\leq G\end{subarray}}|X|\mu(X,G)\Big[\frac{G\times G}{\Delta_{K}(G),\phi_{\kappa}}\Big]\Big[\frac{G\times G}{\Delta(X),1}\Big]

\displaystyle=\widetilde{e_{G}^{G}}\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{X\leq G}|X|\mu(X,G)\Big[\frac{G\times G}{\Delta_{K}(G)*\Delta(X),\phi_{\kappa}\ast 1}\Big].

Now
p_{1}(\Delta_{K}(G)*\Delta(X))=XK
and it is equal to
G

. Moreover,
\mathrm{Res}_{H}^{G}\widetilde{e_{G}^{G}}=0
for any proper subgroup

\displaystyle\widetilde{e_{(K,\kappa)}^{G}}=\widetilde{e_{G}^{G}}\cdot e_{(K,\kappa)}^{G}.

The equality
\widetilde{e_{(K,\kappa)}^{G}}=e_{(K,\kappa)}^{G}\cdot\widetilde{e_{G}^{G}}

are self-opposite.

3.6 Lemma.

Let
M\unlhd G
and
(L,\lambda)\in\mathcal{M}_{G}^{G}
satisfy
L\cap M\leq\ker\lambda

\bar{\lambda}\in(LM/M)^{*}
denote the canonical image of
\lambda
and
\lambda^{\prime}=\mathrm{Inf}_{LM/M}^{LM}\bar{\lambda}

(a)
e_{(L,\lambda)}^{G}\mathrm{Inf}_{G/M}^{G}=\mathrm{Inf}_{G/M}^{G}e_{(LM/M,\bar{\lambda})}^{G/M}

(b)
\mathrm{Def}_{G/M}^{G}e_{(L,\lambda)}^{G}=e_{(LM/M,\bar{\lambda})}^{G/M}\mathrm{Def}_{G/M}^{G}

(c)

\mathrm{Inf}_{G/M}^{G}e_{(LM/M,\bar{\lambda})}^{G/M}\mathrm{Def}_{G/M}^{G}=e_{(LM,\lambda^{\prime})}^{G},

(d)
e_{(L,\lambda)}^{G}=\mathrm{Inf}_{\bar{G}}^{G}\,e_{(\tilde{L},{\tilde{\lambda}})}^{\bar{G}}\,\mathrm{Def}_{\bar{G}}^{G},

where
\bar{G}:=G/\ker(\lambda)
, and
\tilde{L}:=L/\ker(\lambda)
.

Finally if

then the left hand side products in (a) and (b) above are equal to zero.

Proof.

(a) By definition the left hand side of the equation is equal to

\displaystyle e_{(L,\lambda)}^{G}\mathrm{Inf}_{G/M}^{G}=\Big[\frac{G\times G}{\Delta_{L}(G),\phi_{\lambda}}\Big]\Big[\frac{G\times G/M}{\Delta_{\pi}(G),1}\Big]=\Big[\frac{G\times G/M}{\Delta_{L}(G)*\Delta_{\pi}(G),\phi_{\lambda}*1}\Big],

where
\pi:G\rightarrow G/M
is the canonical epimorphism. The product is non-zero since
\lambda\mid_{L\cap M}=1\mid_{L\cap M}
by the condition that
L\cap M\leq\ker(\lambda)
. Then it is straightforward to prove that

\Delta_{L}(G)*\Delta_{\pi}(G)=\Delta_{\pi}(G)*\Delta_{LM/M}(G/M).

Moreover, for
\widetilde{\phi_{\bar{\lambda}}}\in\Big(\Delta_{LM/M}(G/M)\Big)^{*}
we have

\displaystyle(1*\widetilde{{{\phi_{\bar{\lambda}}}}})(g,hM)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{\phi_{\bar{\lambda}}}(gM,hM)=\bar{\lambda}(h^{-1}gM)=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda(h^{-1}g)=\phi_{\lambda}(g,h)=(\phi_{\lambda}*1)(g,hM).

Hence,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Inf}_{G/M}^{G}e_{LM/M,\bar{\lambda}}^{G/M}=\Big[\frac{G\times G/M}{\Delta_{\pi}(G),1}\Big]\Big[\frac{G/M\times G/M}{\Delta_{LM/M}(G/M),\widetilde{\phi_{\bar{\lambda}}}}\Big]=\Big[\frac{G\times G/M}{\Delta_{L}(G)*\Delta_{\pi}(G),\phi_{\lambda}}\Big].

Part (b) can be obtained by taking the opposites of both sides of the equation in (a). Part (c) follows by composing the equation in part (a) from right with
\mathrm{Def}_{G/M}^{G}
. Part (d) follows by applying the decomposition given in Equation (
1
).

Finally the products in parts (a), (b) are being zero when
L\cap M\not\leq\ker(\lambda)
follows from the Mackey product formula for fibered bisets.
∎

Let

\mathrm{Inf}_{H}^{G}\,\varphi_{K,\kappa}^{H}\,\mathrm{Def}_{H}^{G}=\varphi_{K^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime}}^{G},

Proof.

For any summand

\displaystyle\mathrm{Inf}_{H}^{G}\widetilde{e_{L,\lambda}^{H}}\mathrm{Def}_{H}^{G}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Inf}_{H}^{G}\cdot\widetilde{e_{H}^{H}}\cdot e_{(L,\lambda)}^{H}\cdot\widetilde{e_{H}^{H}}\cdot\mathrm{Def}_{H}^{G}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{e_{G}^{G}}\cdot\mathrm{Inf}_{H}^{G}\cdot e_{(L,\lambda)}^{H}\cdot\mathrm{Def}_{H}^{G}\cdot\widetilde{e_{G}^{G}}

0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(7)

\displaystyle\widetilde{e_{(K,\kappa)}^{G}}\left(\sum_{(L,\lambda)\leq(N,\eta)\in\mathcal{M}_{\Phi(G)}^{G}}\mu_{\unlhd G}(L,N)\widetilde{e_{(N,\eta)}^{G}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{(L,\lambda)\leq(N,\eta)\in\mathcal{M}_{\Phi(G)}^{G}}\mu_{\unlhd G}(L,N)\widetilde{e_{(K,\kappa)}^{G}}\cdot\widetilde{e_{(N,\eta)}^{G}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{(L,\lambda)\leq(N,\eta)\in\mathcal{M}_{\Phi(G)}^{G}}\mu_{\unlhd G}(L,N)\widetilde{e_{(N,\eta)}^{G}}=\varphi_{L,\lambda}^{G}

by equation (
7
). Additionally, note that
\widetilde{e_{(K,\kappa)}^{G}}
and
\varphi_{L,\lambda}^{G}
commute since
\widetilde{e_{(K,\kappa)}^{G}}
and
\widetilde{e_{(N,\eta)}^{G}}
commute for all
(N,\eta)\geq(L,\lambda)
. Next, we establish the remaining statement of the proposition using induction on
d:=d(K,\kappa)+d(L,\lambda)
, where
d(K,\kappa)
is defined as the maximum
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that there exists a chain
(K,\kappa)=(K_{0},\kappa_{0})<\cdots<(K_{n},\kappa_{n})
in
\mathcal{M}_{\Phi(G)}^{G}
. If
d=0
, then
(K,\kappa)
and
(L,\lambda)
are maximal in
\mathcal{M}_{\Phi(G)}^{G}
. Consequently, we have
\varphi_{K,\kappa}^{G}=\widetilde{e_{(K,\kappa)}^{G}}
and
\varphi_{L,\lambda}^{G}=\widetilde{e_{(L,\lambda)}^{G}}
. It remains to demonstrate that
\widetilde{e_{(K,\kappa)}^{G}}\cdot\widetilde{e_{(L,\lambda)}^{G}}=0
for
(K,\kappa)\neq(L,\lambda)
. Assuming otherwise, equation (
7
) implies that
\kappa\mid_{K\cap L}=\lambda\mid_{K\cap L}
and that there exists a pair
(KL,\kappa\lambda)\in\mathcal{M}_{\Phi(G)}^{G}
with
(K,\kappa)\leq(KL,\kappa\lambda)\geq(L,\lambda)
. Given that
(K,\kappa)
and
(L,\lambda)
are maximal in
\mathcal{M}_{\Phi(G)}^{G}
, we deduce
(K,\kappa)=(KL,\kappa\lambda)=(L,\lambda)
. Now, suppose
d>0
and that the proposition holds for smaller values of
d
. We first establish that if
\varphi_{K,\kappa}^{G}\varphi_{L,\lambda}^{G}\neq 0
, then
(K,\kappa)=(L,\lambda)
. Indeed, using the definition of the idempotents
\varphi_{K,\kappa}^{G}
and
\varphi_{L,\lambda}^{G}
given by Equation (
4
) and applying Equation (
7
) yields:

\varphi_{K,\kappa}^{G}\cdot\varphi_{L,\lambda}^{G}\in\text{span}_{k}\{\widetilde{e_{(M,\mu)}^{G}}\mid(KL,\kappa\lambda)\leq(M,\mu)\in\mathcal{M}_{\Phi(G)}^{G}\}\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{G}.

This implies that
\widetilde{e^{G}_{(KL,\kappa\lambda)}}\cdot\varphi_{K,\kappa}^{G}\cdot\varphi_{L,\lambda}^{G}=\varphi_{K,\kappa}^{G}\cdot\varphi_{L,\lambda}^{G}
by equation (
7
). Assuming
(K,\kappa)\neq(L,\lambda)
, we have
(K,\kappa)<(KL,\kappa\lambda)
or
(L,\lambda)<(KL,\kappa\lambda)
. By induction, we obtain either
\widetilde{e_{(KL,\kappa\lambda)}^{G}}\cdot\varphi_{K,\kappa}^{G}=0
or
\widetilde{e_{(KL,\kappa\lambda)}^{G}}\cdot\varphi_{L,\lambda}^{G}=0
. In either case, we reach a contradiction, concluding that
(K,\kappa)=(L,\lambda)
. Next, assuming
(K,\kappa)=(L,\lambda)
, we use Equations (
7
) and (
8
) to conclude:

\widetilde{e_{(K,\kappa)}^{G}}=(\widetilde{e_{(K,\kappa)}^{G}})^{2}=\left(\sum_{(K,\kappa)\leq(K^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime})\in\mathcal{M}_{\Phi(G)}^{G}}\varphi_{K^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime}}^{G}\right)^{2}.

Expanding this sum and comparing it with equation (
8
) yields
\varphi_{K,\kappa}^{G}\cdot\varphi_{K,\kappa}^{G}=\varphi_{K,\kappa}^{G}
, proving idempotency. Finally, from equation (
8
), we derive:

\widetilde{e_{(K,\kappa)}^{G}}\cdot\varphi_{L,\lambda}^{G}=\sum_{(K,\kappa)\leq(K^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime})\in\mathcal{M}_{\Phi(G)}^{G}}\varphi_{K^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime}}^{G}\cdot\varphi_{L,\lambda}^{G}.

Using induction and previously established results, the sum evaluates to
\varphi_{L,\lambda}^{G}
if
(K,\kappa)\leq(L,\lambda)
and 0 otherwise.
∎

3.13 Theorem.

Let
(K,\kappa)\in\mathcal{M}_{\Phi(G)}^{G}
and
H
be a finite group.

1.

If
(U,\upsilon)\in\mathcal{M}_{G\times H}
, then
\varphi_{K,\kappa}^{G}\Big[\frac{G\times H}{U,\upsilon}\Big]=0
unless
p_{1}(U)=G
and
k_{1}(U)\cap\Phi(G)\leq K
and
\kappa\mid_{k_{1}(U)\cap K}=\upsilon_{1}\mid_{k_{1}(U)\cap K}
.

2.

If
(U^{\prime},\upsilon^{\prime})\in\mathcal{M}_{H\times G}
, then
\Big[\frac{H\times G}{U^{\prime},\upsilon^{\prime}}\Big]\varphi_{K,\kappa}^{G}=0
unless
p_{2}(U^{\prime})=G
and
k_{2}(U^{\prime})\cap\Phi(G)\leq K
and
\kappa\mid_{k_{2}(U^{\prime})\cap K}=\upsilon^{\prime}_{2}\mid_{k_{2}(U^{\prime})\cap K}
.

Proof.

We prove part (a). Part (b) follows similarly. First, we expand the product as

\displaystyle\varphi_{K,\kappa}^{G}\left[\frac{G\times H}{U,\upsilon}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{e_{G}^{G}}\left(\sum_{(K,\kappa)\leq(L,\lambda)\in\mathcal{M}_{\Phi(G)}^{G}}\mu_{\unlhd G}(K,L)\,e_{(L,\lambda)}^{G}\right)\left[\frac{G\times H}{U,\upsilon}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{e_{G}^{G}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(K,\kappa)\leq(L,\lambda)\in\mathcal{M}_{\Phi(G)}^{G}\\
\lambda|_{L\cap p_{1}(U)}=\upsilon_{1}|_{L\cap p_{1}(U)}\end{subarray}}\mu_{\unlhd G}(K,L)\left[\frac{G\times H}{(L\times 1)U,\lambda\cdot\upsilon}\right]\right),

where
(\lambda\cdot\upsilon)((l,1)u):=\lambda(l)\,\upsilon(u)
. Let
M:=k_{1}(U)\cap\Phi(G)
. We claim that

(L\times 1)U=(LM\times 1)U.

The inclusion
(L\times 1)U\subseteq(LM\times 1)U
is immediate. For the reverse inclusion, let
x\in(LM\times 1)U
, so that
x=(lm,1)(u_{1},u_{2})
for some
l\in L
,
m\in M
, and
(u_{1},u_{2})\in U
. Since
m\in M=k_{1}(U)\cap\Phi(G)
, we have
(m,1)\in U
. Hence,

x=(l,1)(m,1)(u_{1},u_{2})=(l,1)(mu_{1},u_{2})\in(L\times 1)U,

which establishes the claimed equality.

Assume now that the above sum is nonzero. Then there exists an element
(T,\tau)
such that the coefficient of the basis element corresponding to
((T\times 1)U,\tau\cdot\upsilon)
in the sum is nonzero. Moreover,
((T\times 1)U,\tau\cdot\upsilon)
is of the form
((LM\times 1)U,\lambda\cdot\upsilon)
for some
(K,\kappa)\leq(L,\lambda)\in\mathcal{M}_{\Phi(G)}^{G}
. In particular, the coefficient of the corresponding element

\left[\frac{G\times H}{(T\times 1)U,\tau\cdot\upsilon}\right]

in the sum is given by

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(K,\kappa)\leq(L,\lambda)\in\mathcal{M}_{\Phi(G)}^{G}\\
LM=T\end{subarray}}\mu_{\unlhd G}(K,L).

Note that if
K\leq L\unlhd\Phi(G)
and
(LM\times 1)U=(T\times 1)U
, then there exists a unique
\lambda\in L^{*}
such that

((LM\times 1)U,\lambda\cdot\upsilon)=((T\times 1)U,\tau\cdot\upsilon),

since
(\tau\cdot\upsilon)|_{L}=\tau|_{L}=\lambda
. Since we assume that the above coefficient is nonzero, it follows from
[
11
, Proposition 4]
that
KM\leq K
, which in turn implies that
M\leq K
.

Finally, the condition
\kappa\mid_{k_{1}(U)\cap K}=\upsilon_{1}\mid_{k_{1}(U)\cap K}
arises from the Mackey formula for the product of fibered bisets. To establish the result, it suffices to show that if

\kappa\mid_{k_{1}(U)\cap K}\neq\upsilon_{1}\mid_{k_{1}(U)\cap K},

then for any pair
(K,\kappa)\leq(L,\lambda)\in\mathcal{M}_{\Phi(G)}^{G}
, we have

\lambda\mid_{k_{1}(U)\cap L}\neq\upsilon_{1}\mid_{k_{1}(U)\cap L}.

Consequently, all terms in the sum vanish, implying that the product is zero. To see why this holds, assume there exists a pair
(L,\lambda)
such that

\lambda\mid_{k_{1}(U)\cap L}=\upsilon_{1}\mid_{k_{1}(U)\cap L}.

By the poset structure, it follows that

\lambda\mid_{k_{1}(U)\cap K}=\kappa\mid_{k_{1}(U)\cap K}\neq\upsilon_{1}\mid_{k_{1}(U)\cap K}.

However, since
k_{1}(U)\cap K\leq k_{1}(U)\cap L
, we must have

\lambda\mid_{k_{1}(U)\cap K}=\upsilon_{1}\mid_{k_{1}(U)\cap K},

which contradicts our assumption. This completes the proof of part (a).
∎

3.14 Remark.

The conditions in Theorem
3.13
imply in particular that
\widehat{k_{1}(U)}\cap\Phi(G)\leq\widehat{K}
and
\widehat{k_{1}(U)}\cap K\leq\widehat{K}
, where hats denote the kernels of the respective characters. This will be useful in verifying vanishing or orthogonality conditions later.

3.15 Proposition.

Let
G
be a group with order invertible in
k
and let
(K,\kappa)\in\mathcal{M}_{\Phi(G)}^{G}
and
let
(U,\upsilon)\in\mathcal{M}_{G\times G}
such that
U
is a covering subgroup of
G\times G
. Then

\displaystyle\varphi_{K,\kappa}^{G}\cdot Y_{U,\upsilon}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(K,\kappa)\leq(L,\lambda)\in\mathcal{M}_{\Phi(G)}^{G}\\
\lambda\mid_{L\cap k_{1}(U)}=\upsilon_{1}\mid_{L\cap k_{1}(U)}\end{subarray}}\mu_{\unlhd G}(K,L)Y_{(L\times 1)U,\lambda\cdot\upsilon}.

This product is non-zero if and only if
k_{1}(U)\cap\Phi(G)=K
and
\kappa\mid_{K\cap k_{1}(U)}=\upsilon_{1}\mid_{K\cap k_{1}(U)}
. Similarly for the left multiplication, we have

Y_{U,\upsilon}\cdot\varphi_{K,\kappa}^{G}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(K,\kappa)\leq(L,\lambda)\in\mathcal{M}_{\Phi(G)}^{G}\\
\lambda\mid_{L\cap k_{2}(U)}=\upsilon_{2}\mid_{L\cap k_{2}(U)}\end{subarray}}\mu_{\unlhd G}(K,L)Y_{(1\times L)U,\lambda\cdot\upsilon},

and
Y_{U,\upsilon}\cdot\varphi_{K,\kappa}^{G}\not=0
if and only if
k_{2}(U)\cap\Phi(G)=K
and
\upsilon_{2}\mid_{K\cap k_{2}(U)}=\kappa\mid_{K\cap k_{2}(U)}
.

Proof.

By definition of
Y_{U,\upsilon}
, the product
\varphi_{K,\kappa}^{G}\cdot Y_{U,\upsilon}
is equal to

\varphi_{K,\kappa}^{G}\cdot\widetilde{e_{G}^{G}}\Bigl[\frac{G\times G}{U,\upsilon}\Bigr]\widetilde{e_{G}^{G}}.

Following Proposition
3.10
(a), the idempotent
\widetilde{e_{G}^{G}}
on the left is absorbed by
\varphi_{K,\kappa}^{G}
, and hence this expression reduces to

\varphi_{K,\kappa}^{G}\cdot\Bigl[\frac{G\times G}{U,\upsilon}\Bigr]\widetilde{e_{G}^{G}}.

Finally, using the computations from the proof of Theorem
3.13
, we obtain the first statement of the theorem. The product being nonzero implies the given conditions by Theorem
3.13
. To demonstrate that they are sufficient for the product to be nonzero, we examine the coefficient of
Y_{U,\upsilon}
in the sum. Observe that
Y_{U,\upsilon}=Y_{(L\times 1)U,\,\lambda\cdot\upsilon}
if and only if
(U,\upsilon)
is conjugate to
((L\times 1)U,\,\lambda\cdot\upsilon)
. This implies that
L
is contained in the subgroup chain

k_{1}((L\times{\bf 1})U)\leq k_{1}(U),

leading to the relation

K\leq L\leq k_{1}(U)\cap\Phi(G)=K,

which forces
L=K
. Consequently, the coefficient of
Y_{U,\upsilon}
in
\varphi_{K,\kappa}Y_{U,\upsilon}
is equal to
{\bf 1}
, confirming that
\varphi_{K,\kappa}\cdot Y_{U,\upsilon}\neq 0
.

As Proposition
3.10
(a) and Theorem
3.13
are valid for the right multiplication the rest can be established by a similar argument.
∎

4
Idempotents of double
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-fibered Burnside algebras

Throughout this section let
G
be a finite group and let
k
be a commutative ring in which
|G|
is invertible. In this section, we define a set of orthogonal idempotent elements in the
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-fibered Burnside algebra
B_{k}^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}(G,G)
which sum to the identity element. Consequently, we demonstrate that the evaluation of a
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-fibered biset functor at
G
decomposes as a direct sum of
k
-modules, indexed by the minimal sections
(T,S)
of
G
and by specific elements of the poset
\mathcal{M}_{\Phi(T/S)}^{T/S}
. Here, a
minimal
section
(T,S)
of
G
is a section with
S\unlhd\Phi(T)
.

4.1 Definition.

Let
(T,S)
be a section of
G
and let
(K,\kappa)\in\mathcal{M}_{T/S}^{*}
. Define:

\displaystyle u_{T,S,K,\kappa}^{G}=\mathrm{Indinf}^{G}_{T/S}\varphi_{K,\kappa}^{T/S}\in B^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}_{k}(G,T/S),

\displaystyle v_{T,S,K,\kappa}^{G}=\varphi_{K,\kappa}^{T/S}\mathrm{Defres}^{G}_{T/S}\in B^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}_{k}(T/S,G).

Note that

u_{T,S,K,\kappa}^{G}=(v_{T,S,K,\kappa}^{G})^{op}.

These elements will be used as partial idempotents in

B_{k}^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}(G,G)
, projecting onto fibered components associated to certain triples
(T/S,K,\kappa)
.

4.2

The group
\mathrm{Aut}(G)
acts on
\mathcal{M}_{\Phi(G)}^{G}
via

\psi\cdot(K,\kappa)=(\psi(K),\kappa\circ\psi^{-1}),

where
\psi\in\mathrm{Aut}(G),(K,\kappa)\in\mathcal{M}_{\Phi(G)}^{G}
. Since
\mathrm{Inn}(G)
acts trivially on the elements of
\mathcal{M}_{\Phi(G)}^{G}
, the above action descents to an action of
\mathrm{Out}(G)
. Let

[K,\kappa]_{\mathrm{Out}(G)}
denote the corresponding orbit. Usually we omit the index
\mathrm{Out}(G)
and simply write
[K,\kappa]:=[K,\kappa]_{\mathrm{Out}(G)}
whenever it is clear from the context. We denote a complete set of orbit representatives of
\mathcal{M}_{\Phi(G)}^{G}
by
[\mathcal{M}_{\Phi(G)}^{G}]
. Similarly we have the subset
[\mathcal{M}_{G}^{*}]
of representatives for pairs with faithful characters.

4.3

For
(K,\kappa)\in[\mathcal{M}_{\Phi(G)}^{G}]
, set

\varphi_{[K,\kappa]}^{G}:=\sum_{(K^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime})\in[K,\kappa]}\varphi_{K^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime}}^{G}

and similarly for a minimal section
(T,S)
and
(K,\kappa)\in[\mathcal{M}_{\Phi(T/S)}^{T/S}]
, set

u_{T,S,[K,\kappa]}^{G}:=\sum_{(K^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime})\in[K,\kappa]}u_{T,S,K^{\prime}\kappa^{\prime}}^{G}\quad\text{and}\quad v_{T,S,[K,\kappa]}^{G}:=\sum_{(K^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime})\in[K,\kappa]}v_{T,S,K^{\prime}\kappa^{\prime}}^{G}.

The following theorem and its proof are the adaptations of
[
6
, Theorem 4.5]
and its proof to the fibered case.

4.4 Theorem.

Let
(T,S),(T^{\prime},S^{\prime})
be minimal sections of
G
. Also let
(K,\kappa)\in[\mathcal{M}_{T/S}^{*}]
and
(K^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime})\in[\mathcal{M}_{T^{\prime}/S^{\prime}}^{*}]
. Then

1.

v_{T^{\prime},S^{\prime},[K^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime}]}^{G}\cdot u_{T,S,[K,\kappa]}^{G}=0

unless
(T,S)=_{G}(T^{\prime},S^{\prime})
and
[K,\kappa]=[K^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime}]
.

2.

When
(T,S)=(T^{\prime},S^{\prime})
then

\displaystyle v_{T,S,[K,\kappa]}^{G}\cdot u_{T,S,[K,\kappa]}^{G}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi_{[K,\kappa]}^{T/S}\left(\sum_{g\in N_{G}(T,S)/T}\text{Iso}(c_{g})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{g\in N_{G}(T,S)/T}\text{Iso}(c_{g})\right)\varphi_{[K,\kappa]}^{T/S}.

Here
N_{G}(T,S)=N_{G}(T)\cap N_{G}(S)
, and
c_{g}
is the conjugation by
g
on
T/S
.

Proof.

(a) Suppose that
v_{T^{\prime},S^{\prime},[K^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime}]}^{G}u_{T,S,[K,\kappa]}^{G}\neq 0
. Then there exist a pair
(K_{2},\kappa_{2})\in[K,\kappa]
and
(K_{1},\kappa_{1})\in[K^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime}]
such that
v_{T^{\prime},S^{\prime},K_{1},\kappa_{1}}^{G}u_{T,S,K_{2},\kappa_{2}}^{G}\not=0
. By definition and by the Mackey formula we have the expression

\displaystyle v_{T^{\prime},S^{\prime},K_{1},\kappa_{1}}^{G}u_{T,S,K_{2},\kappa_{2}}^{G}

\displaystyle=\varphi_{K_{1},\kappa_{1}}^{T^{\prime}/S^{\prime}}\mathrm{Defres}^{G}_{T^{\prime}/S^{\prime}}\mathrm{Indinf}^{G}_{T/S}\varphi_{K_{2},\kappa_{2}}^{T/S}

\displaystyle=\varphi_{K_{1},\kappa_{1}}^{T^{\prime}/S^{\prime}}\left(\sum_{g\in T^{\prime}\setminus G/T}\Big[\frac{T^{\prime}/S^{\prime}\times T/S}{U_{g},1}\Big]\right)\varphi_{K_{2},\kappa_{2}}^{T/S},

where
U_{g}=\{(t^{\prime}S^{\prime},tS)\in(T^{\prime}/S^{\prime})\times(T/S)\mid t^{\prime}gt^{-1}\in S^{\prime}gS\}
. In particular, the projections satisfy
p_{1}(U_{g})=({}^{g}T\cap T^{\prime})S^{\prime}/S^{\prime}
and
p_{2}(U_{g})=(T^{\prime g}\cap T)S/S
. Notably,
p_{1}(U_{g})=T^{\prime}/S^{\prime}
if and only if
{}^{g}T\cap T^{\prime}=T^{\prime}
, as
S^{\prime}\leq\Phi(T^{\prime})
. Similarly,
p_{2}(U_{g})=T/S
if and only if
T^{\prime g}\cap T=T
. By Theorem
3.13
, it follows that

\varphi_{K_{1},\kappa_{1}}^{T^{\prime}/S^{\prime}}\left(\sum_{g\in T^{\prime}\setminus G/T}\Big[\frac{T^{\prime}/S^{\prime}\times T/S}{U_{g},1}\Big]\right)\varphi_{K_{2},\kappa_{2}}^{T/S}=0,

unless
T^{\prime}={}^{g}T
. Now assume that
T^{\prime}={}^{g}T
, for some
g\in G
. Then we have
k_{1}(U_{g})=({}^{g}S\cap T^{\prime})S^{\prime}/S^{\prime}={}^{g}SS^{\prime}/S^{\prime}
and
k_{2}(U_{g})=(S^{\prime g}\cap T)S/S=S^{\prime g}S/S
. Moreover, since
{}^{g}S\leq{}^{g}\Phi(T)=\Phi(T^{\prime})
and
S^{\prime g}\leq\Phi(T^{\prime g})=\Phi(T)
, it follows that
{}^{g}SS^{\prime}/S^{\prime}
is contained in
k_{1}(U_{g})\cap(\Phi(T^{\prime})/S^{\prime})=k_{1}(U_{g})\cap\Phi(T^{\prime}/S^{\prime})
. Similarly,
S^{\prime g}S/S
is contained in
k_{2}(U_{g})\cap(\Phi(T)/S)=k_{2}(U_{g})\cap\Phi(T/S)
. By Theorem
3.13
, the product

\varphi_{K_{1},\kappa_{1}}^{T^{\prime}/S^{\prime}}\left(\sum_{g\in T^{\prime}\setminus G/T}\Big[\frac{(T^{\prime}/S^{\prime})\times(T/S)}{U_{g},1}\Big]\right)\varphi_{K_{2},\kappa_{2}}^{T/S}

is equal to zero unless
k_{1}(U_{g})\cap\Phi(T^{\prime}/S^{\prime})\leq K_{1}
,
\kappa_{1}\mid_{k_{1}(U_{g})\cap K_{1}}=1

and
k_{2}(U_{g})\cap\Phi(T/S)\leq K_{2}
and
\kappa_{2}\mid_{k_{2}(U_{g})\cap K_{2}}=1
. But as the
\kappa_{1}
and
\kappa_{2}
are faithful characters we must have
k_{1}(U_{g})\cap\Phi(T^{\prime}/S^{\prime})=k_{2}(U_{g})\cap\Phi(T/S)=1
. This implies that
S^{\prime g}\leq S\leq S^{\prime g}
, proving that the pairs
(T,S)
and
(T^{\prime},S^{\prime})
must be conjugate. Assuming that
(T,S)
and
(T^{\prime},S^{\prime})
are conjugate, the above product simplifies to

\sum_{g\in N_{G}(T,S)/T}\left(\varphi_{K_{1},\kappa_{1}}^{T/S}\Big[\frac{T/S\times T/S}{U_{g},1}\Big]\varphi_{K_{2},\kappa_{2}}^{T/S}\right)=\sum_{g\in N_{G}(T,S)/T}\left(\varphi_{K_{1},\kappa_{1}}^{T/S}\mathrm{Iso}(c_{g})\varphi_{K_{2},\kappa_{2}}^{T/S}\right).

By Proposition
3.10
, part (c), it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{g\in N_{G}(T,S)/T}\left(\varphi_{K_{1},\kappa_{1}}^{T/S}\mathrm{Iso}(c_{g})\varphi_{K_{2},\kappa_{2}}^{T/S}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{g\in N_{G}(T,S)/T}\left(\mathrm{Iso}(c_{g})\varphi_{K_{1}^{g},\kappa_{1}\circ c_{g^{-1}}}^{T/S}\varphi_{K_{2},\kappa_{2}}^{T/S}\right)=0,

unless
(K_{2},\kappa_{2})=(K_{1}^{g},\kappa_{1}\circ c_{g^{-1}})
due to the orthogonality of these elements. Consequently, the proof is complete as it follows that
[K,\kappa]=[K_{2},\kappa_{2}]=[K_{1}^{g},\kappa_{1}\circ c_{g^{-1}}]=[K_{1},\kappa_{1}]=[K^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime}]
.

(b) From the calculations in the first part we can expand the product as

\displaystyle v_{T,S,[K,\kappa]}^{G}\cdot u_{T,S,[K,\kappa]}^{G}=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{(K^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime})\in[K,\kappa]}v_{T,S,K^{\prime}\kappa^{\prime}}^{G}\right)\left(\sum_{(K^{\prime\prime},\kappa^{\prime\prime})\in[K,\kappa]}u_{T,S,K^{\prime\prime}\kappa^{\prime\prime}}^{G}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{(K^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime})\in[K,\kappa]}\left(\sum_{(K^{\prime\prime},\kappa^{\prime\prime})\in[K,\kappa]}v_{T,S,K^{\prime}\kappa^{\prime}}^{G}\cdot u_{T,S,K^{\prime\prime}\kappa^{\prime\prime}}^{G}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(K^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime})\in[K,\kappa]\\
(K^{\prime\prime},\kappa^{\prime\prime})\in[K,\kappa]\end{subarray}}\left(\varphi_{K^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime}}^{T/S}\left(\sum_{g\in N_{G}(T,S)/T}\text{Iso}(c_{g})\right)\varphi_{K^{\prime\prime},\kappa^{\prime\prime}}^{T/S}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{(K^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime})\in[K,\kappa]}\varphi_{K^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime}}^{T/S}\right)\left(\sum_{g\in N_{G}(T,S)/T}\text{Iso}(c_{g})\right)\left(\sum_{(K^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime})\in[K,\kappa]}\varphi_{K^{\prime\prime},\kappa^{\prime\prime}}^{T/S}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{g\in N_{G}(T,S)/T}\text{Iso}(c_{g})\right)\left(\sum_{(K^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime})\in[K,\kappa]}\varphi_{K^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime}}^{T/S}\right)\left(\sum_{(K^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime})\in[K,\kappa]}\varphi_{K^{\prime\prime},\kappa^{\prime\prime}}^{T/S}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{g\in N_{G}(T,S)/T}\text{Iso}(c_{g})\right)\left(\sum_{(K^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime})\in[K,\kappa]}\varphi_{K^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime}}^{T/S}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{(K^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime})\in[K,\kappa]}\varphi_{K^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime}}^{T/S}\right)\left(\sum_{g\in N_{G}(T,S)/T}\text{Iso}(c_{g})\right).

∎

4.5 Definition.

Let
(T,S)
be a minimal section of
G
and
(K,\kappa)\in\mathcal{M}_{T/S}^{*}
. Define

\displaystyle\epsilon_{T,S,[K,\kappa]}^{G}:=\frac{1}{|N_{G}(T,S):T|}\cdot u_{T,S,[K,\kappa]}^{G}\cdot v_{T,S,[K,\kappa]}^{G}\in B_{k}^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}(G,G).

These idempotents are orthogonal and sum to the identity, as we shall prove below. First expand as follows.

\displaystyle\epsilon_{T,S,[K,\kappa]}^{G}=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|N_{G}(T,S):T|}\cdot\left(\sum_{(K^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime})}\mathrm{Indinf}^{G}_{T/S}\varphi_{K^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime}}^{T/S}\right)\left(\sum_{(K^{\prime\prime},\kappa^{\prime\prime})}\varphi_{K^{\prime\prime},\kappa^{\prime\prime}}^{T/S}\mathrm{Defres}^{G}_{T/S}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|N_{G}(T,S):T|}\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(K^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime}),(K^{\prime\prime},\kappa^{\prime\prime})\in[K,\kappa]\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Indinf}^{G}_{T/S}\varphi_{K^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime}}^{T/S}\cdot\varphi_{K^{\prime\prime},\kappa^{\prime\prime}}^{T/S}\mathrm{Defres}^{G}_{T/S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|N_{G}(T,S):T|}\cdot\sum_{(K^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime})\in[K,\kappa]}\mathrm{Indinf}^{G}_{T/S}\varphi_{K^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime}}^{T/S}\mathrm{Defres}^{G}_{T/S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|N_{G}(T,S):T|}\cdot\mathrm{Indinf}^{G}_{T/S}\varphi_{[K,\kappa]}^{T/S}\mathrm{Defres}^{G}_{T/S}.

Once more these idempotents are fibered versions of Bouc’s idempotents
\epsilon_{T,S}^{G}
given in
[
6
, Notation 4.6]
. The fibered version is obtained in the same way using
\varphi_{[K,\kappa]}^{T/S}
instead of
\varphi_{\bf 1}^{T/S}
.

4.6 Theorem.

The set

\{\epsilon_{T,S,[K,\kappa]}^{G}\big|(T,S)\in[\Pi(G)],(K,\kappa)\in[\mathcal{M}_{T/S}^{*}]\}

is a system of orthogonal idempotents in
B_{k}^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}(G,G)
summing up to the identity.

Proof.

The orthogonality of the elements
\epsilon_{T,S,[K,\kappa]}^{G}
follow simply by Theorem
4.4
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(T,S)\in[\Pi(G)]\\
(K,\kappa)\in[\mathcal{M}_{T/S}^{*}]\end{subarray}}\epsilon_{T,S,[K,\kappa]}^{G}=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(T,S)\in[\Pi(G)]\\
(K,\kappa)\in[\mathcal{M}_{T/S}^{*}]\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{|N_{G}(T,S):T|}\mathrm{Indinf}_{T/S}^{G}\varphi_{[K,\kappa]}^{T/S}\mathrm{Defres}_{T/S}^{G}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{(T,S)\in\Pi(G)}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(K,\kappa)\in\mathcal{M}_{T/S}^{*}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{|G:T|}\mathrm{Indinf}_{T/S}^{G}\varphi_{K,\kappa}^{T/S}\mathrm{Defres}_{T/S}^{G}\right).

Fix
T\leq G
and sum over
S
to obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\unlhd T,S\leq\Phi(T)\\
(K,\kappa)\in\mathcal{M}_{T/S}^{*}\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Indinf}_{T/S}^{G}\varphi_{K,\kappa}^{T/S}\mathrm{Defres}_{T/S}^{G}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ind}_{T}^{G}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\unlhd T,S\leq\Phi(T)\\
(K,\kappa)\in\mathcal{M}_{T/S}^{*}\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Inf}_{T/S}^{T}\varphi_{K,\kappa}^{T/S}\mathrm{Def}_{T/S}^{T}\right)\mathrm{Res}_{T}^{G}.

Observe that there exists a bijection between the set
\mathcal{M}_{\Phi(T)}^{T}
and the set of triples
(S,K,\kappa)
satisfying the following properties:

S\unlhd T,\quad S\subseteq\Phi(T),\quad\text{and}\quad(K,\kappa)\in\mathcal{M}_{T/S}^{*}.

This correspondence is given by mapping each
(L,\lambda)\in\mathcal{M}_{\Phi(T)}^{T}
to the triple
(\widehat{L},L/\widehat{L},\bar{\lambda})
, where
\widehat{L}=\ker(\lambda)
, and
\bar{\lambda}(l\widehat{L})=\lambda(l)
for all
l\in L
. Hence by Proposition
3.8
and also using Theorem
3.12
and
[
6
, Lemma 2.13]
, the above equation becomes

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ind}_{T}^{G}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(L,\lambda)\in\mathcal{M}_{\Phi(T)}^{T}\end{subarray}}\varphi_{L,\lambda}^{T}\right)\mathrm{Res}_{T}^{G}=\mathrm{Ind}_{T}^{G}\widetilde{e_{T}^{T}}\mathrm{Res}_{T}^{G}=|N_{G}(T):T|\widetilde{e_{T}^{G}}.

Therefore we get

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(T,S)\in[\Pi(G)]\\
(K,\kappa)\in[\mathcal{M}_{T/S}^{*}]\end{subarray}}\epsilon_{T,S,[K,\kappa]}^{G}=\sum_{T\leq G}\frac{|N_{G}(T):T|}{|G:T|}\widetilde{e_{T}^{G}}=\sum_{T\leqslant_{G}G}\widetilde{e_{T}^{G}}=\Big[\frac{G\times G}{\Delta(G),1}\Big].

∎

4.7

Having constructed a complete set of mutually orthogonal idempotents in
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-fibered Burnside algebra
B_{k}^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}(G,G)
, we are now equipped to describe a powerful structural decomposition of the evaluations of
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-fibered biset functors on finite groups. Let us consider such a functor
F
defined over a field
k
, and fix a finite group
G
for which
|G|\in k^{\times}
.

The fundamental result presented here is a canonical decomposition of the module
F(G)
, which reveals its internal structure in terms of simpler, well-understood constituents. Specifically,
F(G)
can be expressed as a direct sum of components indexed by conjugacy classes of minimal sections
(T,S)
of
G
, and by equivalence classes
[K,\kappa]
, where
K
is a normal subgroup of the quotient
T/S
contained in its Frattini subgroup and
\kappa
is a faithful character of
K
. This decomposition is realized via the isomorphism

F(G)\cong\bigoplus_{(T,S)\in[\Pi(G)]}\bigoplus_{(K,\kappa)\in[\mathcal{M}_{T/S}^{*}]}\left(\varphi_{[K,\kappa]}^{T/S}F(T/S)\right)^{N_{G}(T,S)/T}.

This decomposition is made explicit by two mutually inverse maps. The forward map, denoted
V
, sends an element
w\in F(G)
to the tuple of its components in the above direct sum:

V(w)=\left(\frac{1}{|N_{G}(T,S):T|}\cdot v_{T,S,[K,\kappa]}^{G}\cdot w\right)_{(T,S,[K,\kappa])}.

Each of these components belongs to the space
\left(\varphi_{[K,\kappa]}^{T/S}F(T/S)\right)^{N_{G}(T,S)/T}
, and we must verify this invariance. To do so, fix
(T,S,[K,\kappa])
and consider any
g\in N_{G}(T,S)
. Since
g
normalizes both
T
and
S
, the conjugation map
c_{g}
fixes the section
(T,S)
and the orbit
[K,\kappa]
. Thus conjugation by
g
maps the idempotent
v_{T,S,[K,\kappa]}^{G}
to
\mathrm{Iso}(c_{g})(v_{T,S,[K,\kappa]}^{G})=v_{T,S,[K,\kappa]}^{G}\mathrm{Iso}(c_{g})
. Moreover, the map
\mathrm{Iso}(c_{g}):F(G)\to F(G)
is an inner automorphism hence the identity map for
g\in G
and we conclude that
v_{T,S,[K,\kappa]}^{G}\cdot w
is indeed fixed by the action of
N_{G}(T,S)/T
.
The inverse map
U
reconstructs an element of
F(G)
from its projections in the summands:

U\left((w_{T,S,(K,\kappa)})_{(T,S,(K,\kappa))}\right)=\sum_{(T,S),[K,\kappa]}u_{T,S,[K,\kappa]}^{G}\cdot w_{T,S,[K,\kappa]}.

We now verify that
U\circ V=\mathrm{id}_{F(G)}
. For any
w\in F(G)
, we compute

U(V(w))=\sum_{(T,S),[K,\kappa]}\frac{1}{|N_{G}(T,S):T|}\cdot u_{T,S,[K,\kappa]}^{G}\cdot v_{T,S,[K,\kappa]}^{G}\cdot w.

The sum over all
u\cdot v
terms recovers the identity map on
F(G)
, since the elements
\epsilon_{T,S,[K,\kappa]}^{G}
form a complete set of orthogonal idempotents. Hence
U(V(w))=w
for all
w
. To establish the reverse identity
V\circ U=\mathrm{id}
, consider a tuple
(w_{T,S,(K,\kappa)})_{(T,S,(K,\kappa))}
in the direct sum. Then

\displaystyle VU\left((w_{T,S,(K,\kappa)})_{(T,S,(K,\kappa))}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{|N_{G}(T,S):T|}v_{T,S,[K,\kappa]}^{G}\sum_{(T^{\prime},S^{\prime}),[K^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime}]}u_{T^{\prime},S^{\prime},[K^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime}]}^{G}w_{T^{\prime},S^{\prime},[K^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime}]}\right)_{(T,S,[K,\kappa])}.

Now, since, by Theorem
4.4
, we have

v_{T,S,[K,\kappa]}^{G}\cdot u_{T^{\prime},S^{\prime},[K^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime}]}^{G}=\delta_{(T,S,[K,\kappa]),(T^{\prime},S^{\prime},[K^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime}])}\cdot\Big(\sum_{g\in N_{G}(T,S)/T}\mathrm{Iso}(c_{g})\Big)\varphi_{[K,\kappa]}^{T/S},

and also
\varphi_{[K,\kappa]}^{T/S}\cdot w_{T,S,[K,\kappa]}=w_{T,S,[K,\kappa]}
by the choice of the elements
w_{T,S,[K,\kappa]}
, we find that all cross-terms vanish, and what remains is

VU\left(w_{T,S,[K,\kappa]}\right)=\left(\frac{1}{|N_{G}(T,S):T|}\sum_{g\in N_{G}(T,S)/T}\mathrm{Iso}(c_{g})(w_{T,S,[K,\kappa]})\right)_{(T,S,[K,\kappa])}.

Because each
w_{T,S,[K,\kappa]}
is
N_{G}(T,S)/T
-invariant by construction, this averaging yields the original element. Therefore,
VU=\mathrm{id}
, completing the verification.

As a consequence, each element
w\in F(G)
admits the decomposition

w=\sum_{(T,S),[K,\kappa]}\epsilon_{T,S,[K,\kappa]}^{G}\cdot w.

4.8 Theorem.

Let
k
be a commutative ring,
F
be a fibered biset functor over
k
and
G
be a finite group with
|G|\in k^{\times}
. There exists a decomposition

F(G)\cong\bigoplus_{(T,S)\in[\Pi(G)]}\bigoplus_{(K,\kappa)\in[\mathcal{M}_{T/S}^{*}]}\left(\varphi_{[K,\kappa]}^{T/S}F(T/S)\right)^{N_{G}(T,S)/T}.

Here,
[\Pi(G)]
denotes a set of representatives of the conjugacy classes of minimal sections of
G
, i.e., sections
S\unlhd T\leq G
with
S\leq\Phi(T)
, and
[\mathcal{M}_{T/S}^{*}]
denotes a set of orbit representatives
[K,\kappa]
under the action of
\mathrm{Out}(T/S)
, where
K
is a normal subgroup of
T/S
contained in its Frattini subgroup, and
\kappa
is a faithful character of
K
.

5
Decomposing
\mathcal{F}_{p,k}^{{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}}
, when
p\in k^{\times}

Let
k
be a commutative unitary ring, and let
p\in k^{\times}
be a fixed prime. In this section, we restrict to attention to the category of
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-fibered bisets on finite
p
-groups. The goal of this section is to decompose the category of
{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}
-fibered
p
-biset functors via idempotents defined earlier. This is a fibered version of Bouc’s results Theorem 7.4 and Corollary 7.5 in
[
6
]
. We begin by defining atoric
p
-groups.

5.1 Definition.

(Bouc)

A finite
p
-group
P
is called
atoric
if the following equivalent conditions hold.

1.

P
does not admit any decomposition
P=E\times Q
, where
E
is a non-trivial elementary abelian group.
[
6
, Section 6.1]

2.

For any non-trivial normal subgroup
N
of
P
, one has
N\cap\Phi(P)\not=1
.
[
6
, Lemma 6.3 (2)]

3.

\Omega_{1}Z(P)\leqslant\Phi(P)
.
[
6
, Lemma 6.3 (3)]

We denote by
\mathcal{A}t_{p}
the class of
atoric

p
-groups and by
[\mathcal{A}t_{p}]
a set of representatives of isomorphism classes in
\mathcal{A}t_{p}
.

Let
P
be a finite
p
-group. As in
[
6
, Section 6]
, we denote the greatest atoric quotient of
P
by
P^{@}
. By Proposition 6.4 of
[
6
]
, there is a maximal normal subgroup
N
of
P
with the property that
N\cap\Phi(P)=1
such that
P^{@}=P/N
. We call
P^{@}
the
atoric part
of
P
. For further properties of atoric
p
-groups, we refer to Section 6 of
[
6
]
.

The following theorem refines a fundamental result of Bouc to the fibered setting, where the effect of the subgroup
K
is made clear in an essential way. As in
[
6
]
, this refinement plays a central role in establishing the decomposition theorem we prove below.

5.2 Theorem.

Let
P
and
Q
be finite
p
-groups, and let
(T,S)
and
(V,U)
be minimal sections of
P
and
Q
, respectively. Suppose that

(K/S,\kappa)\in\mathcal{M}_{T/S}^{*}\quad\text{and}\quad(L/U,\lambda)\in\mathcal{M}_{V/U}^{*}.

Then

\epsilon_{V,U,[L/U,\lambda]}^{Q}\cdot B_{k}^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}(Q,P)\cdot\epsilon_{T,S,[K/S,\kappa]}^{P}\neq\{0\}\,\text{implies that}\,(V/L)^{@}\cong(T/K)^{@}.

Proof.

Suppose that

\epsilon_{V,U,[L/U,\lambda]}^{Q}\cdot B_{k}^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}(Q,P)\cdot\epsilon_{T,S,[K/S,\kappa]}^{P}\neq\{0\},

then there exists
a\in B_{k}^{\mathbb{C}}(Q,P)
such that
\epsilon_{V,U,[L/U,\lambda]}^{Q}\cdot a\cdot\epsilon_{T,S,[K/S,\kappa]}^{P}\not=0
and thus

\mathrm{Indinf}^{Q}_{V/U}\varphi_{[L/U,\lambda]}^{V/U}\mathrm{Defres}^{Q}_{V/U}\cdot a\cdot\mathrm{Indinf}^{P}_{T/S}\varphi_{[K/S,\kappa]}^{T/S}\mathrm{Defres}^{P}_{T/S}\neq 0.

Thus, there exist
(L^{\prime}/U,\lambda^{\prime})\in[L/U,\lambda]
and
(K^{\prime}/S,\kappa^{\prime})\in[K/S,\kappa]
such that

\mathrm{Indinf}_{V/U}^{Q}\varphi_{L^{\prime}/U,\lambda^{\prime}}^{V/U}\mathrm{Defres}_{V/U}^{Q}\cdot a\cdot\mathrm{Indinf}_{T/S}^{P}\varphi_{K^{\prime}/S,\kappa^{\prime}}^{T/S}\mathrm{Defres}_{T/S}^{P}\neq 0.

In particular, the element

b=\mathrm{Defres}_{V/U}^{Q}\cdot a\cdot\mathrm{Indinf}_{T/S}^{P}

of
B_{k}^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}(V/U,T/S)
satisfies

\varphi_{L^{\prime}/U,\lambda^{\prime}}^{V/U}\cdot b\cdot\varphi_{K^{\prime}/S,\kappa^{\prime}}^{T/S}\neq 0.

This implies that there exists an element
(N,\eta)\in\mathcal{M}_{V/U\times T/S}
such that

\varphi_{L^{\prime}/U,\lambda^{\prime}}^{V/U}\cdot\Big[\frac{(V/U)\times(T/S)}{N,\eta}\Big]\cdot\varphi_{K^{\prime}/S,\kappa^{\prime}}^{T/S}\neq 0.

Then, by Theorem
3.13
, the group
N
must be covering in
(V/U)\times(T/S)
, and we must have

k_{1}(N)\cap\Phi(V/U)\leq L^{\prime}/U\quad\text{and}\quad k_{2}(N)\cap\Phi(T/S)\leq K^{\prime}/S.

Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{k_{1}(N)(L^{\prime}/U)}{L^{\prime}/U}\cap\Phi\left(\frac{V/U}{L^{\prime}/U}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{k_{1}(N)(L^{\prime}/U)}{L^{\prime}/U}\cap\frac{\Phi(V/U)}{L^{\prime}/U}=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{k_{1}(N)(L^{\prime}/U)\cap\Phi(V/U)}{L^{\prime}/U}=\frac{L^{\prime}/U}{L^{\prime}/U},

since
L^{\prime}/U\in\Phi(V/U)
. Similarly, we also have

\frac{k_{2}(N)(K^{\prime}/S)}{K^{\prime}/S}\cap\Phi\left(\frac{T/S}{K^{\prime}/S}\right)={\bf 1}.

Therefore, by
[
6
, Proposition 6.6]
, it follows that

(V/L)^{@}\cong\left(\frac{V/U}{L/U}\right)^{@}\cong\left(\frac{V/U}{L^{\prime}/U}\right)^{@}\cong\left(\frac{\frac{V/U}{L^{\prime}/U}}{\frac{k_{1}(N)(L^{\prime}/U)}{L^{\prime}/U}}\right)^{@}\cong\left(\frac{V/U}{k_{1}(N)(L^{\prime}/U)}\right)^{@}.

By analogous reasoning on the right-hand side, we also have

(T/K)^{@}\cong\left(\frac{T/S}{k_{2}(N)(K^{\prime}/S)}\right)^{@}.

For the above product to be nonzero, we must also have

e_{(L^{\prime}/U,\lambda^{\prime})}^{V/U}\cdot\left[\frac{(V/U)\times(T/S)}{N,\eta}\right]\cdot e_{(K^{\prime}/S,\kappa^{\prime})}^{T/S}\neq 0.

This follows from the definitions of
\varphi_{L^{\prime}/U,\lambda^{\prime}}^{V/U}
,
\varphi_{K^{\prime}/S,\kappa^{\prime}}^{T/S}
, and Proposition
2.7
. Moreover, by
[
1
, Proposition 4.2]
, we can compute the left and right invariants of this nonzero element. In particular,

e_{(L^{\prime}/U,\lambda^{\prime})}^{V/U}\cdot\left[\frac{(V/U)\times(T/S)}{N,\eta}\right]\cdot e_{(K^{\prime}/S,\kappa^{\prime})}^{T/S}=\left[\frac{(V/U)\times(T/S)}{N((L^{\prime}/U)\times(K^{\prime}/S)),\,\lambda^{\prime}\cdot\eta\cdot\kappa^{\prime}}\right],

where

\displaystyle p_{1}\big(N((L^{\prime}/U)\times(K^{\prime}/S))\big)

\displaystyle=V/U,

\displaystyle p_{2}\big(N((L^{\prime}/U)\times(K^{\prime}/S))\big)

\displaystyle=T/S,

\displaystyle k_{1}\big(N((L^{\prime}/U)\times(K^{\prime}/S))\big)

\displaystyle=k_{1}(N)(L^{\prime}/U),

\displaystyle k_{2}\big(N((L^{\prime}/U)\times(K^{\prime}/S))\big)

\displaystyle=k_{2}(N)(K^{\prime}/S).

Hence, we obtain the isomorphism

\frac{V/U}{k_{1}(N)(L^{\prime}/U)}\cong\frac{T/S}{k_{2}(N)(K^{\prime}/S)}.

Combining this with the previous observations yields the desired isomorphism:

(V/L)^{@}\cong(T/K)^{@}.

∎

5.3

One may compare the above theorem with Lemma 6.3 of
[
1
]
where it is proved that if a product

f_{\{K,\kappa\}}\cdot a\cdot f_{\{L,\lambda\}}

for
a\in B_{k}^{{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}}(P,P)
is non-zero then
(G,K,\kappa)
is linked to
(G,L,\lambda)
. This also implies that
G/K
is isomorphic to
G/L
. The above result shows that we loosen the condition considerably.

The following definition is our version of idempotents
b_{L}^{P}
of
[
6
]
. We change the notation to distinguish them properly.

5.4 Definition.

Let
M
be an atoric
p
-group and
P
be a finite
p
-group. The
Bouc’s idempotent associated to
M
at
P
is the element
c_{M}^{P}
of
B_{k}^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}(P,P)
defined by

c_{M}^{P}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(T,S)\in[\Pi(P)]\\
(K/S,\kappa)\in[\mathcal{M}_{T/S}^{*}]\\
(T/K)^{@}\cong M\end{subarray}}\epsilon_{T,S,[K/S,\kappa]}^{P}.

5.5

Properties of Bouc’s idempotents.

It turns out that Bouc’s idempotents
c_{M}^{P}
replaces
b_{L}^{P}
from
[
6
]
in the best way, leading analogous results for the category of fibered
p
-biset functors. With all the preliminaries at hand, the proofs of properties of Bouc’s idempotents are almost identical to those in
[
6
, Theorem 7.4]
. We shall omit certain details and list the properties as follows.

Let
M,N
be atoric
p
-groups and
P,Q
be finite
p
-groups.

(a)

The idempotent
c_{M}^{P}
is non-zero if and only if
M
is isomorphic to a subquotient of
P^{@}
.

The forward implication follows from the definition. For the converse, if
M\cong(T/S)^{@}
for a minimal section
(T,S)
of
P
, then
\epsilon_{T,S,[S/S,1]}^{P}
is a summand of
c_{M}^{P}
.

(b)

If
c_{M}^{Q}\cdot B_{k}^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}(Q,P)\cdot c_{N}^{P}\neq{0}
, then
M\cong N
.

This is basically the previous theorem. Indeed, if the given subspace is non-zero then there are idempotents
\epsilon_{V,U,[L/U,\lambda]}^{Q}
and
\epsilon_{T,S,[K/S,\kappa]}^{P}
such that
(V/L)^{@}\cong M
and
(T/K)@\cong N
and

\epsilon_{V,U,[L/U,\lambda]}^{Q}\cdot B_{k}^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}(Q,P)\cdot\epsilon_{T,S,[K/S,\kappa]}^{P}\neq\{0\}.

Hence by Theorem
5.2
, we get
M\cong(V/L)^{@}\cong(T/K)@\cong N
.

(c)

The sum of Bouc’s idempotents
c_{M}^{P}
, as
M
runs over all groups in
[\mathcal{A}t_{p}]
, is equal to the identity element of
B_{k}^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}(P,P)
.

As in the case of bisets, the idempotents
\epsilon_{T,S,[K,\kappa]}^{P}
sum up to the identity, as
(T,S)
runs over all minimal sections up to conjugation;
(K,\kappa)
over all pairs in
\mathcal{M}_{\Phi(T/S)}^{T/S}
, up to automorphisms of
T/S
. Now we can rearrange the terms by fixing the atoric part of

’s, we get the result.

(d)

For all
a\in B_{k}^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}(Q,P)
we have

c_{M}^{Q}\cdot a=a\cdot c_{M}^{P}.

(e)

The family of elements
c_{M}^{P}\in B_{k}^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}(P,P)
, for finite
p
-groups
P
, is an idempotent endomorphism
c_{M}
of the identity functor of the category
k\mathcal{C}_{p}
. That is, it is an idempotent of the center of
k\mathcal{C}_{p}
. The idempotent elements
c_{M}
, for
M\in[\mathcal{A}t_{p}]
, are orthogonal, and their sum is equal to the identity element of the center of
k\mathcal{C}_{p}
.

(f)

For a given finite
p
-group
P
, the elements
c_{M}^{P}
, for
M\in[\mathcal{A}t_{p}]
such that
M\sqsubseteq P^{@}
, are non-zero orthogonal central idempotents of
B_{k}^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}(P,P)
, and their sum is equal to the identity of
B_{k}^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}(P,P)
.

Proofs of these properties are exactly the same as the proofs in
[
6
]
of parts (3), (4) and (5) of Theorem 7.4. We omit the details.

5.6

These properties lead to the decomposition of the category
\mathcal{F}_{p,k}^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}
. Indeed, let
F
be a
{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}
-fibered
p
-biset functor and
M
be an atoric
p
-group.
As

runs over all finite
p
-groups, the family
c_{M}^{P}\in B_{k}^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}(P,P)
of idempotents defines a natural transformation

c_{M,F}:F\to F\,\text{given by}\,c_{M,F}(P)(w)=c_{M}^{P}\cdot w\,\text{for each}\,w\in F(P).

Moreover if

is also a
{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}
-fibered biset functor, then for any natural transformation

we have

c_{M,F^{\prime}}\circ\theta=\theta\circ c_{M,F}.

In particular
c_{M,?}
is an idempotent in the center of the category
\mathcal{F}_{p,k}^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}
. Following Bouc, we denote this idempotent by
\widehat{c}_{M}
and call it the
Bouc’s idempotent at
M
. By the above properties of Bouc’s idempotents, it is straightforward to show that as
M
runs over all atoric
p
-groups up to isomorphism, we get a decomposition of the identity functor of the category
\mathcal{F}_{p,k}^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}
into a sum of orthogonal idempotents

\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{F}_{p,k}^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}}=\sum_{M\in[\mathcal{A}t_{p}]}\widehat{c}_{M}.

Now writing
\widehat{c}_{M}F
for the image of
c_{M,F}
, we obtain a canonical decomposition

F\cong\bigoplus_{M\in[\mathcal{A}t_{p}]}\widehat{c}_{M}F

for any
{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}
-fibered
p
-biset functor
F
. This decomposition is natural in
F
and
\widehat{c}_{M}F
inherits its functor structure from
F
.

This is exactly the fibered
p
-biset functor version of Bouc’s decomposition of
p
-biset functors. We state the final result as a theorem for future reference.

5.7 Theorem.

(Block decomposition of
\mathcal{F}_{p,k}^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}
)

Let
p
be a prime number such that
p\in k^{\times}
. For each atoric
p
-group
M\in[\mathrm{At}_{p}]
, define the full subcategory

\widehat{c}_{M}\mathcal{F}_{p,k}^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}:=\left\{F\in\mathcal{F}_{p,k}^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}\,\middle|\,\widehat{c}_{M}F=F\right\},

consisting of those functors on which
\widehat{c}_{M}
acts trivially. Then:

1.

Each category
\widehat{c}_{M}\mathcal{F}_{p,k}^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}
is an abelian subcategory of
\mathcal{F}_{p,k}^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}
, closed under taking subfunctors, quotients, and extensions.

2.

The family of orthogonal idempotents
\{\widehat{c}_{M}\}_{M\in[\mathrm{At}_{p}]}
induces an equivalence of categories

\mathcal{F}_{p,k}^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}\cong\prod_{M\in[\mathrm{At}_{p}]}\widehat{c}_{M}\mathcal{F}_{p,k}^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}},

via the functor

F\mapsto\left(\widehat{c}_{M}F\right)_{M\in[\mathrm{At}_{p}]}.

3.

Each functor
F\in\mathcal{F}_{p,k}^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}
admits a canonical decomposition

F=\bigoplus_{M\in[\mathrm{At}_{p}]}\widehat{c}_{M}F.

5.8

Having the above decomposition, a natural direction is to describe the blocks
\widehat{c}_{M}\mathcal{F}_{p,k}^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}
for arbitrary atoric
p
-groups more explicitly. In
[
6
]
, Bouc obtained detailed results on the structure of these blocks in a very elegant way. Although one might attempt to generalize his ideas to the case of fibered
p
-biset functors, we decided not to go in this direction since the results are expected to be much more complicated compared to the case of
p
-biset functors. While a complete classification of the fibered blocks is expected to be far more involved than in the unfibered case, we turn our attention to a coarser but structurally useful invariant: the vertex of an indecomposable functor.
We shall apply this to obtain further results about fibered
p
-biset functors when we regard them as biset functors.

5.9 Definition.

Let

be an indecomposable
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-fibered
p
-biset functor over

-group

such that

is called the

of

This terminology is in parallel to the theory of vertices for indecomposable

-biset functors in the sense of Bouc
[

5.10 Remark.

Let
F
and
F^{\prime}
be indecomposable objects in
\mathcal{F}_{p,k}^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}
with distinct vertices

. Then the extension groups between them vanish:

\operatorname{Ext}^{\ast}_{\mathcal{F}_{p,k}^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}}(F,F^{\prime})=0.

This follows from the fact that
\mathcal{F}_{p,k}^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}
decomposes as a direct product of blocks
\widehat{c}_{M}\mathcal{F}_{p,k}^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}
for

.

Furthermore, we emphasize that the vertex of a functor does not necessarily coincide with a minimal group for the functor. The following theorem explores this relationship.

5.11 Theorem.

Let
F
be an indecomposable
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-fibered
p
-biset functor over

with vertex

, and suppose

. Then there exists a normal subgroup

such that

K\leq\Phi(P)\cap Z(P),\quad\text{and}\quad M\cong(P/K)^{@}.

Proof.

Since
F=\widehat{c}_{M}F
, there must exist a summand
\epsilon^{P}_{T,S,[K/S,\kappa]}
of
c_{M}^{P}
such that

\epsilon^{P}_{T,S,[K/S,\kappa]}\cdot F(P)\neq 0,

for some minimal section
(T,S)
of

and a pair
(K/S,\kappa)\in[M^{\ast}_{T/S}]
with
(T/K)^{@}\cong M
.
By the definition of

, we have

\epsilon_{T,S,[K,\kappa]}^{P}=X\circ\mathrm{Defres}^{P}_{T/S}

for a virtual fibered

-biset

. Since

is minimal for

, this composition is nonzero only if

, hence

Since

This is a well-defined homomorphism and its kernel is equal to
NK/K
. We have

NK/K\cap\Phi(Q/K)=(NK\cap\Phi(Q))/K=K/K.

Here the first equality holds since
K\leqslant\Phi(Q)
and the second holds since
N\cap K={\bf 1}
. Thus by
[
6
, Proposition 6.6]
, we get
(T/K^{\prime})^{@}\cong(Q/K)^{@}\cong M
. Similarly, one can prove that
(T/S^{\prime})^{@}\cong(Q/S)^{@}\cong L
.

∎

Let
M
be an atoric

L
be an atoric
p
-group which is a cyclic central Frattini extension of

, that is, there is a short exact sequence

such that

.

Proof.

This follows because the triple
(L,K,{\bf 1})

∎

6.7 Remark.

It turns out that not all triples
(T,K,S)
can be reduced to the case where
S
is trivial. Indeed, given an atoric
p
-group
M
and a non-trivial normal subgroup
S
of
M
contained in
\Phi(M)
, then
M\rightsquigarrow M/S
via the triple
(M,{\bf 1},S)
. Since
|M/S|<|M|
, there is no triple
(T,K,{\bf 1})
making
M/S
a central resolution of
M
.

Finally we obtain the following necessary condition for composition factors of
F^{\flat}
for a fibered
p
-biset functor
F
. As an application we shall consider the restriction of the simple functor
S_{({\bf 1},{\bf 1},{\bf 1},[\mathbb{C}])}
.

6.8 Corollary.

Let
F
be an indecomposable fibered
p
-biset functors with vertex
T
. Then a simple biset functor
S_{Q,W}
is a composition factor of
F^{\flat}
only if
Q^{@}\rightsquigarrow T
.

Proof.

Suppose
S_{Q,W}
is a composition factor of
F^{\flat}
. Then there is an indecomposable summand
M
of
F^{\flat}
having
S_{Q,W}
as a composition factor. Let
L
be the vertex of
M
. Then by
[
6
, Theorem 9.8]
, we must have
b_{L}M=M
and also that
Q^{@}=L
. Now since
F
, as a fibered biset functor, has vertex
T
, we also have
c_{T}F=F
which implies
(b_{L}\cdot c_{T})F\not=0
. Hence
b_{L}\cdot c_{T}\not=0
. Now the result follows from the definition of being a central resolution.
∎

6.9

Central resolution of 1.
Next we determine atoric
p
-groups
L
such that
{\bf 1}\rightsquigarrow L
. By definition

holds if there is an atoric
p

and

satisfying the conditions in Definition
6.4
. Since
(T/K)^{@}={\bf 1}
, we get that
T/K
is elementary abelian, and

, as in the previous section. In particular, the Frattini subgroup
\Phi(T)
is cyclic and central. Since
T
is atoric, we also have that
\Omega_{1}(Z(T))\leqslant\Phi(T)
. Since the latter is cyclic, the center
Z(T)
must also be cyclic. Hence we get that
T
is an atoric
p
-group with cyclic center whose Frattini is also central. On the other hand, since
S\leqslant\Phi(T)
and
\Phi(T)\cap S={\bf 1}
, we get

. Now we conclude the following.

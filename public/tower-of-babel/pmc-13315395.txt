68Ga-pentixafor PET/CT imaging characteristics in primary aldosteronism with coexisting subclinical cushing syndrome: a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Shuang Liu, Rui Zuo, Mengdan Li, Jing Chen, Jia Li, Xiaoyang Zhang, Qifu Li, Hua Pang, Lu Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPrimary aldosteronism (PA) accompanied by subclinical Cushing’s syndrome (SCS) complicates both diagnosis and clinical management. This study aimed to investigate the endocrine and metabolic profiles as well as the ⁶⁸Ga‑pentixafor PET/CT imaging features of PA‑SCS, and to evaluate its diagnostic performance for PA‑SCS.ResultsA total of 52 patients with primary aldosteronism who underwent 68Ga-pentixafor PET/CT were retrospectively enrolled, including 25 patients with PA complicated by subclinical Cushing’s syndrome (PA-SCS) and 27 patients with isolated PA. Adrenal lesions in PA-SCS patients were significantly larger than those in patients with isolated PA (1.10 ± 0.56 cm vs. 2.24 ± 0.69 cm, P < 0.0001). The lesion-to-contralateral ratio (LCR) at both early and delayed phases was significantly higher in the PA-SCS group (10 min: 1.41 [1.02, 2.65] vs. 2.90 [2.01, 3.77], P = 0.003; 40 min: 1.26 [1.03, 3.53] vs. 3.00 [1.80, 4.07], P = 0.012). The LCR-10 min achieved the optimal diagnostic performance for differentiating PA-SCS from isolated PA (AUC = 0.737, cutoff = 1.935, sensitivity = 80.00%, specificity = 66.67%).Conclusions⁶⁸Ga‑pentixafor PET/CT is an effective imaging modality for differentiating PA‑SCS from isolated PA, which complements conventional diagnostic. Introduction

Primary aldosteronism (PA) is a common cause of secondary hypertension and is associated with a higher risk of end-organ damage, particularly in the heart and kidneys. In PA, aldosterone excess contributes to persistent hypertension, which in turn increases the risk of cardiovascular events such as heart failure, atrial fibrillation, and arteriosclerosis [1]. Subclinical Cushing syndrome (SCS) is characterized by mild cortisol hypersecretion without obvious clinical symptoms or signs, often complicating the diagnostic process [2]. The coexistence of SCS in PA patients (PA-SCS) significantly increases the risk of cardiovascular and renal complications compared to patients with PA alone [3, 4].Recent studies suggest that patients with PA-SCS exhibit more severe disease progression, highlighting the importance of early identification and appropriate treatment to improve long-term outcomes.

Although adrenal vein sampling (AVS) is considered the gold standard for PA subtyping, its clinical application is limited due to technical complexity, procedural variability, diagnostic inconsistencies, and post-procedural complications [1, 5]. Furthermore, emerging evidence indicates that autonomous cortisol secretion from the adrenal glands may influence AVS lateralization results in PA-SCS patients [3], which has significant implications for therapeutic decision-making and long-term management. This makes the management of PA-SCS patients more complex, as both conditions may overlap and share similar clinical features. Previous studies [6–8] have demonstrated that 68Ga-Pentixafor PET/CT exhibits excellent performance in the subtype diagnosis of primary aldosteronism (PA). Specifically, its visual analysis yields a sensitivity, specificity, and accuracy of 92.40%, 94.40%, and 93.33%, respectively, for aldosterone-producing adenomas, showing high consistency with adrenal venous sampling (AVS). Notably, the PA-SCS subtype has not been fully investigated in existing research, which constitutes a current research gap and represents the core innovation of this study. CXCR4 is highly expressed in functionally active steroidogenic adrenal tissues and closely correlates with the secretory activity of adrenocortical lesions. In patients with PA-SCS, the co-secretion of aldosterone and cortisol may upregulate CXCR4 expression via relevant signaling pathways. Based on this mechanism, 68Ga-pentixafor PET/CT holds promise as a functional imaging biomarker that specifically reflects the aldosterone and cortisol secretory status of PA‑SCS lesions, thereby providing a direct and reliable basis for the functional assessment of such lesions. This study compared the 68Ga-Pentixafor PET/CT imaging features between PA-SCS and PA patients, and analyzed the correlations between PET parameters and endocrine metabolic indices in PA-SCS patients, aiming to evaluate its clinical value in the management of PA-SCS.

Materials and methods

Patient population

This retrospective study enrolled 25 PA-SCS patients who underwent 68Ga-pentixafor PET/CT at the Department of Nuclear Medicine, The First Affiliated Hospital of Chongqing Medical University, between January 2022 and June 2024. For comparison, 27 patients with PA and 39 patients with SCS who underwent the same imaging during the same period were randomly selected as control groups. Diagnoses of PA, SCS, and PA-SCS were confirmed by endocrinologists based on clinical guidelines from the Endocrine Society, incorporating clinical signs, biochemical tests (e.g.captopril suppression test, saline infusion test, and 1-mg dexamethasone suppression test [DST]), and imaging evaluations. Clinical data collected included sex, age, hypertension status, lowest serum potassium level, disease duration, plasma renin activity, aldosterone levels, 24-hour urinary free cortisol (UFC), cortisol rhythm, confirmatory test results, and comorbidities. Inclusion criteria required confirmed clinical and endocrine evaluations. Exclusion criteria included: (1) history of exogenous glucocorticoid use (oral/topical) or medications affecting the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis within six months prior to diagnosis; (2) incomplete clinical or imaging data; (3) patients with severe chronic kidney disease and patients with concurrent malignant tumors. The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee with waived informed consent (Approval No. K2023-039).

Diagnostic criteria

Primary aldosteronism (PA) was diagnosed according to the Endocrine Society guidelines. Patients with hypertension and spontaneous hypokalemia, plasma aldosterone > 20 ng/dL, and direct renin concentration (DRC) < 2.5 mU/L were diagnosed directly as PA without further confirmatory tests. For other patients, the aldosterone‑to‑renin ratio (ARR) was used for screening.When plasma renin activity (PRA) and aldosterone were measured in ng·ml−¹·h−¹ and ng/dL, an ARR > 30 was considered positive. When direct renin concentration (DRC) and aldosterone were measured in mU/L and ng/dL, an ARR > 3.7 was defined as positive. Patients with a positive ARR underwent at least one confirmatory test: saline infusion test (plasma aldosterone > 50 pmol/L after 2 L saline infusion), captopril suppression test, or fludrocortisone suppression test.

Subclinical Cushing syndrome (SCS) was defined as mild autonomous cortisol secretion without overt clinical features of Cushing syndrome. Biochemical evidence included at least one of the following: serum cortisol > 50 nmol/L after a 1-mg overnight dexamethasone suppression test, mildly elevated 24-hour urinary free cortisol, or disturbed cortisol rhythm.

PA-SCS was diagnosed when patients met both diagnostic criteria for PA and SCS simultaneously.

Preparation of ⁶⁸Ga-pentixafor

The radiochemical purity of ⁶⁸Ga-Pentixafor was 96–98%. The standards of quality control mainly include: clear and sterile solution, endotoxins less than 15 EU/mL, pH in the range of 4–8, ethanol max. 10% (V/V), and radiochemical purity no less than 95%.

Imaging protocol

Imaging was performed using a Philips Gemini TF 64 PET/CT scanner. Patients received intravenous injection of 68Ga-pentixafor (111–185 MBq [3–5 mCi]) via the antecubital vein without special preparation (fasting or fluid restriction is not necessary). Early (10 min post-injection) and delayed (40 min post-injection) abdominal images were acquired in a supine position with arms raised. A single-bed position centered on the adrenal glands was scanned for 5 min. Low-dose CT (120 keV, 100 mA, 512 × 512 matrix, 2-mm slice thickness) preceded PET acquisition (3D mode, 120 keV, 100 mA, 3–5 mm slice thickness). PET images were reconstructed using CT-based attenuation correction and ordered subset expectation maximization (OSEM) algorithm with post-smoothing.

Image analysis

Two blinded nuclear medicine physicians with extensive experience independently evaluated all 68Ga-pentixafor PET/CT images, and they were completely blinded to the patients’ clinical, biochemical, and AVS lateralization results during the entire image interpretation process. Visual analysis defined lesions as positive or negative using contralateral adrenal tissue as reference. Semiquantitative analysis involved region-of-interest (ROI, was defined as an area showing either focal increased uptake of 68Ga-pentixafor in the adrenal gland, or obvious thickening or nodular formation in the adrenal region on CT images.) delineation to derive the maximum standardized uptake value (SUVmax, defined as the highest SUV among all voxels within ROI) of lesions, mean SUV (SUVmean) of contralateral adrenal tissue, and liver SUVmean. Lesion-to-contralateral adrenal ratio (LCR) and lesion-to-liver ratio (LLR) were calculated as SUVmax (lesion)/SUVmean (contralateral adrenal) and SUVmax (lesion)/SUVmean (liver), respectively. Inter-observer agreement was evaluated using Cohen’s kappa coefficient for visual interpretation and intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) for semiquantitative parameters, including SUVmax, LCR, and LLR. The inter‑observer agreement was excellent for visual analysis (κ = 0.86) and semiquantitative parameters (ICC = 0.90 for SUVmax, 0.89 for LCR, 0.88 for LLR), indicating high reliability of image analysis.

Statistical analysis

SPSS 26.0 was used for analysis. Normally distributed continuous variables are expressed as mean±standard deviation (x̄±s), non-normally distributed variables as median (P25, P75), and categorical variables as n (%) Intergroup differences were assessed using Mann-Whitney U test for nonparametric variables and Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis was performed based on binary logistic regression, and the optimal cutoff value was determined according to the Youden index. Pearson correlation analysis was performed to evaluate the correlation between PET parameters and clinical endocrine and metabolic indices in patients with PA-SCS. The correlation coefficient r and 95% confidence interval (95% CI) were calculated. A two-tailed P < 0.05 indicated statistical significance.

Results

Patient characteristics

Among the 27 patients in the PA group, 14 underwent surgical treatment. Postoperative pathological findings revealed adrenocortical adenoma in 13 cases and adrenocortical-medullary hyperplasia in one case. In the 39 patients in the SCS group, 28 underwent surgery, with one case diagnosed as adrenal oncocytoma, one as macronodular adrenocortical hyperplasia, and the remaining cases as adrenocortical adenoma. In the PA-SCS group, 19 out of 25 patients underwent surgery, all of whom were confirmed to have adrenocortical adenoma. Notably, PA-SCS patients were significantly older than those in the PA and SCS groups (P < 0.050). The maximum lesion diameter in PA-SCS patients was significantly larger compared to those in the PA group (P < 0.001). However, no significant differences in body mass index (BMI) were observed among the three groups. Systolic blood pressure was significantly higher in the PA-SCS group compared to the SCS group (P = 0.049). These clinical findings suggest that PA-SCS patients present with more advanced disease in terms of both age and lesion size, indicating the potential need for more aggressive management in this cohort (Table 1).

Intergroup differences in clinical parameters

Significant differences were observed between the PA and PA-SCS groups in the following clinical parameters: 16-hour cortisol (16hCORT), 24-hour cortisol (24hCORT), 24-hour urinary free cortisol (24hUFC), 8-hour ACTH (8hACTH), 16-hour ACTH (16hACTH), 24-hour ACTH (24hACTH), and cortisol levels after the 1-mg dexamethasone suppression test (1mgDST-CORT) (P < 0.050). These differences suggest that PA-SCS patients not only have elevated cortisol secretion but also exhibit abnormal ACTH dynamics compared to PA patients, underscoring the physiological impact of concurrent SCS. In contrast, the SCS-alone group exhibited significantly lower aldosterone (Ald), renin (REN), and aldosterone-renin ratio (ARR) levels compared to the PA-SCS group (P < 0.050). This suggests that PA-SCS patients have a more pronounced adrenal axis dysfunction, with altered hormonal secretion patterns compared to those with isolated SCS (Fig. 1, Supplementary Table 1).

68Ga-pentixafor PET/CT imaging parameters

PET/CT imaging with 68Ga-pentixafor revealed higher SUVmax, lesion-to-contralateral ratio (LCR), and lesion-to-background ratio (LLR) values in PA-SCS patients compared to PA patients, both at 10 min and 40 min post-injection. However, only the LCR-10 min (P = 0.003) and LCR-40 min (P = 0.012) showed statistically significant intergroup differences, highlighting the potential of 68Ga-pentixafor PET/CT as a non-invasive imaging biomarker for distinguishing PA-SCS from PA. These results suggest that 68Ga-pentixafor PET/CT can detect differences in tumor metabolism and vascular characteristics between these two groups, reflecting the unique physiological alterations present in PA-SCS patients (Fig. 2, Supplementary Table 2).​

ROC analysis for discriminating PA-SCS from PA

Given the differences in LCR and lesion size between the PA and PA‑SCS groups, receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis was performed to evaluate the diagnostic performance of LCR-10 min, LCR-40 min, and lesion size. The LCR-10 min showed superior diagnostic efficacy (cutoff value: 1.935, AUC: 0.737, P = 0.003). The DeLong test indicated no significant differences among the three AUCs (all P > 0.050). These findings suggest that the LCR-10 min can effectively differentiate PA-SCS from PA with high diagnostic accuracy, providing a reliable imaging marker for clinical decision-making in PA-SCS patients (Table 2).

Correlation between PET parameters and clinical indices in PA-SCS

In PA-SCS patients, lesion SUVmax, LCR, and LLR exhibited weak but statistically significant correlations with the maximum lesion diameter, cortisol levels (CORT), ACTH, and 24-hour urinary free cortisol (24hUFC) levels (r = 0.3–0.5, P < 0.050). These correlations suggest that 68Ga-pentixafor PET/CT parameters, such as SUVmax and LCR, are not only indicative of lesion size but also reflect the metabolic and hormonal abnormalities associated with PA-SCS. The weak correlations observed between imaging parameters and cortisol-related indices highlight the complex interaction between adrenal function and tumor characteristics in PA-SCS patients (Fig. 3).

Discussion

Primary aldosteronism (PA) is a treatable form of secondary hypertension, which leads to a significantly higher cardiovascular and renal burden compared to essential hypertension, contributing to poorer clinical outcomes. Previous studies have shown that 14.6%–37.0% of PA patients have concurrent subclinical Cushing syndrome (SCS) [9–11]. Patients with PA-SCS are at a considerably higher risk for cardiovascular complications compared to those with PA alone [12–14].

In patients with PA-SCS, the adrenal cortex secretes excessive amounts of both aldosterone and cortisol, which disrupts metabolic homeostasis, exacerbates sodium and water retention, increases intravascular volume, and further aggravates cardiovascular system damage. Clinically, most SCS patients lack typical Cushingoid features, which easily leads to underdiagnosis of concurrent SCS in PA patients. In addition, elevated cortisol levels may reduce the lateralization accuracy of adrenal venous sampling (AVS) [15–17]. Currently, the diagnosis of adrenal disorders (e.g., PA and Cushing’s syndrome) relies primarily on clinical manifestations and serological tests. Conventional imaging modalities can only reveal adrenal thickening or nodules but cannot assess functional status. Previous studies on PA-SCS have mainly focused on clinical and CT parameters, while the imaging characteristics of ⁶⁸Ga-pentixafor PET/CT remain largely unexplored. In contrast, ⁶⁸Ga-pentixafor PET/CT not only enables precise lesion localization but also evaluates functional activity. More importantly, our study demonstrates that ⁶⁸Ga-pentixafor PET/CT can further aid in the differential diagnosis of PA-SCS. Therefore, this study aims to compare the clinical data and ⁶⁸Ga-pentixafor PET/CT features between PA-SCS and PA patients, focusing on analyzing intergroup differences and evaluating the impact of concurrent SCS on adrenal ⁶⁸Ga-pentixafor uptake.

Our results demonstrate that PA-SCS lesions are significantly larger than those in PA patients, consistent with the findings of Liao et al. [18], who similarly reported larger nodule diameters in PA-SCS. This aligns with prior studies by Mosconi et al. [19, 20], which identified positive correlations between cortisol levels and adrenal adenoma size and volume. These observations underscore the clinical imperative to screen for autonomous cortisol secretion in PA patients with sizable adrenal nodules, as concurrent SCS may influence therapeutic decision-making.

PA-SCS patients exhibited higher SUVmax, LCR, and LLR values at both 10- and 40-minute phases compared to PA patients (Figs. 4 and 5: two typical cases). This finding aligns with Ding et al. [21], who reported higher SUVmax in cortisol-secreting versus aldosterone-secreting adenomas. The coexistence of aldosterone and cortisol synthesis in PA lesions, likely driven by altered CYP11B1/CYP11B2 expression and GPCR upregulation, may contribute to the elevated 68Ga-pentixafor uptake observed in PA-SCS lesions. This mechanism likely explains the increased adrenal CXCR4 expression in PA-SCS [22, 23].

In addition, this study further used ROC analysis to compare the diagnostic efficacy of LCR-10 min and LCR-40 min in differentiating PA-SCS from PA, and DeLong test to compare their AUCs and 95% confidence intervals. The results in Table 2 showed that the AUC of LCR-10 min was 0.737 (sensitivity 80.00%, specificity 66.67%, cutoff value 1.935), and the AUC of LCR-40 min was 0.700 (sensitivity 92.00%, specificity 51.85%, cutoff value 1.325). The above AUC values indicate that both have moderate diagnostic efficacy; although not sufficient as independent diagnostic indicators, they can serve as complementary imaging biomarkers to traditional clinical and biochemical indicators such as cortisol levels after dexamethasone suppression test, providing additional diagnostic value. The Delong test showed no statistically significant difference between the two AUCs (P > 0.050), indicating comparable overall diagnostic efficacy, but the LCR-10 min had higher specificity and was more valuable for the clinical differential diagnosis of PA and PA-SCS. In addition, considering the additional radiation exposure and examination cost of PET/CT, single-phase 10-minute scanning can effectively reduce the radiation dose and economic burden for patients while ensuring diagnostic accuracy.

We found weak but statistically significant positive correlations between PET parameters and clinical indicators (Fig. 3), suggesting that differences in CXCR4 expression and PET imaging may not be caused by cortisol levels alone, but may also be regulated by multiple other factors. Based on existing research and our findings, we speculate that, in addition to the co-secretion of aldosterone and cortisol, cellular heterogeneity of PA-SCS lesions, changes in the local microenvironment, regulation by other hormones (e.g.adrenocorticotropic hormone), and CXCR4 gene polymorphisms may all affect CXCR4 expression and PET parameters, thus contributing to the weak correlation. In future studies, we will further include these potential influencing factors in multivariate analysis to clarify their regulatory effects on CXCR4 expression and PET imaging characteristics.

In our study, two PA-SCS patients with unilateral lesions on PET exhibited non-lateralized results on adrenal venous sampling (AVS). In both patients, cortisol was not sufficiently suppressed after the dexamethasone suppression test, indicating mild autonomous cortisol secretion. This discrepancy suggests that PET/CT may provide complementary lateralizing information when AVS fails to achieve clear lateralization and may help guide the selection of the surgical side. In addition, these cases highlight the importance of closely monitoring cortisol levels in the perioperative period to promptly identify and manage potential hypercortisolism, thereby improving the safety and efficacy of surgical treatment.

In this study, a case of a female patient admitted for a right adrenal mass without clinical symptoms, clinically suggestive of subclinical Cushing’s syndrome (Fig. 6). 68Ga-pentixafor PET/CT revealed markedly increased uptake in the right adrenal nodule, which was interpreted as a functional tumor secreting cortisol. Postoperative pathology confirmed the diagnosis of oncocytic adrenal neoplasm (OAN). Adrenal cortical oncocytic adenoma (OAN) is a rare subtype of adrenal tumors, often challenging to diagnose preoperatively based on size or imaging alone, with confirmation typically requiring postoperative histopathology and immunohistochemistry [24, 25]. Most OAN patients are asymptomatic, but those with endocrine function may secrete aldosterone, cortisol, or sex hormones, leading to manifestations such as female virilization, Cushing’s syndrome, or pseudo-precocious puberty. Surgical resection is the primary treatment for larger tumors. For incidentally discovered adrenal masses, 68Ga-pentixafor PET/CT can help determine functional status, guiding subsequent management decisions.

This study has several limitations. First, it was a retrospective study with a limited sample size, which may limit the generalizability of the findings. Limited immunohistochemical data were available for the enrolled PA-SCS patients, which precluded verification of increased CXCR4 protein expression. This study only indirectly evaluated CXCR4 expression using ⁶⁸Ga‑pentixafor PET/CT imaging, without direct validation by histopathology and immunohistochemistry, which constitutes a major limitation of the present study. In future studies, we will expand the sample size and collect pathological specimens from PA‑SCS patients. Direct quantification of CXCR4 protein will be performed using immunohistochemistry and Western blot analysis to confirm whether CXCR4 expression is upregulated in PA‑SCS lesions and to validate the hypothesis that co‑secretion of aldosterone and cortisol contributes to elevated CXCR4 expression. Furthermore, all scans were confined to adrenal-specific imaging, which precluded differentiation between ACTH-independent adrenal adenomas and pituitary-driven CS. Therefore, future studies should combine pituitary MRI, ACTH measurement, and other clinical biochemical indicators to achieve accurate differential diagnosis. In addition, our team is further investigating the value of whole-body PET/CT in distinguishing adrenal-derived CS from pituitary-derived CS and identifying potential ectopic ACTH-secreting lesions. Meanwhile, future studies may also optimize scanning protocols, collect immunohistochemical data, and expand the sample size to improve the reliability and robustness of the findings. Furthermore, our team is currently exploring the potential of 68Ga-pentixafor in combination with 18F-Aldoview for the differential diagnosis of PA-SCS, PA, and CS. Hu, S et al. [26] demonstrated that 18F-Aldoview exhibits higher specificity in PA lesions. Based on this observation, we hypothesize that in patients with PA-SCS, lesions showing dual uptake of both 68Ga-pentixafor and 18F-Aldoview are likely to represent PA components, whereas regions with isolated 68Ga-pentixafor uptake but no 18F-Aldoview uptake are more consistent with SCS components. Nevertheless, this hypothesis requires validation in studies with a sufficiently large sample size.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that ⁶⁸Ga-pentixafor PET/CT can effectively distinguish PA-SCS from PA. PA-SCS patients have larger adrenal lesions and higher SUVmax, LCR, and LLR values. Among these, the 10-minute LCR is the best indicator for identifying PA-SCS. This technique complements traditional diagnostic methods and facilitates early detection and clinical decision-making in PA-SCS patients.

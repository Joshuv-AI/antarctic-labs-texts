Endoscopic Retrofibular Groove Deepening and Resection of a Low‐Lying Muscle of the Peroneus Brevis for the Management of Intrasheath Subluxation of Peroneal Tendons Associated With a Low‐Lying Peroneus Brevis Muscle
Authors: Xiaohua Pan, Tun Hing Lui, Amanda Mun Yee Slocum, Charles Churk Hang Li, Zhuofeng Lin, Weibin Su, Jingsong Hong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractA constricted retrofibular fibro‐osseous tunnel is the cause of intrasheath peroneal tendon subluxation. If it is because of a combination of an abnormal convex or flat retrofibular groove and the presence of a space‐occupying low‐lying muscle belly, endoscopic groove deepening and resection of a low‐lying muscle is a reasonable surgical option. The purpose of this technical note is to describe the details of endoscopic retrofibular groove deepening and resection of a low‐lying muscle of the peroneus brevis for the management of intrasheath subluxation of peroneal tendons associated with a low‐lying peroneus brevis muscle. The advantages of this minimally invasive approach include better cosmetic results, minimal soft tissue trauma, decreased postoperative pain, early mobilization and faster postoperative recovery, less peritendinous fibrosis, and less subjective tightness at the peroneal tendons.

The patient is placed in the lateral position. The proximal and distal portals are interchangeable as the viewing and working portals. The fibrous bands over the peroneal tendons are resected. Intrasheath subluxation of the peroneal tendons is confirmed endoscopically. The low‐lying muscle of peroneus brevis is resected. The peroneal tendons are splinted medially with K‐wires. Endoscopic groove deepening is then performed. After that, K‐wires are removed, and resolution of intrasheath subluxation of the peroneal tendons is confirmed endoscopically. In intrasheath subluxation of peroneal tendons, the peroneal tendons subluxate on each other within the retrofibular peroneal tendon sheath, and the superior peroneal retinaculum is intact. 1 , 2 , 3 , 4 A low‐lying peroneus brevis (PB) muscle belly, peroneus quartus muscle, or convex retrofibular groove may result in overcrowding within the retrofibular fibro‐osseous tunnel and compressing the peroneal tendons, leading to intrasheath subluxation of the peroneal tendons. 2 , 3 , 5 , 6 It can be classified as type A with normal peroneal tendons and type B in which the peroneus longus (PL) tendon subluxates through the longitudinal split of the PB tendon. 1 , 2 This anatomical position of the PB, between the posterior fibula and the PL, predisposes it to compression and longitudinal tears. 3

The common clinical presentations are subjective feelings of popping, snapping, or clicking during ankle motions and contraction of the peroneal muscles and may be associated with posterolateral ankle pain. 3 , 7 They usually do not respond well to conservative treatment. 6 Surgery aims at the restoration of sufficient space inside the retrofibular tunnel by retrofibular groove deepening and resection of the peroneus quartus and the low‐lying PB muscle belly. 1 , 2 , 3 , 6 , 8 The traditional open surgical approaches are associated with extensive scar formation, tendon adhesions, entrapment of the sural nerve, risk of superior peroneal retinaculum insufficiency or contracture, and the problem of prolonged postoperative immobilization. 9 Endoscopic approaches have been reported to minimize these complications. 9 , 10 , 11 , 12 , 13 , 14 , 15 , 16

The endoscopic techniques reported either endoscopic groove deepening or endoscopic muscle resection. In this reported technique, we combine both techniques to deal with type A intrasheath subluxation of the peroneal tendons associated with a low‐lying peroneal brevis muscle. The purpose of this technical note is to describe the technical details of endoscopic retrofibular groove deepening for the management of type A intrasheath peroneal tendon subluxation associated with a low‐lying peroneal brevis muscle. It is indicated for symptomatic type A intrasheath subluxation of the peroneal tendons associated with a low‐lying peroneal brevis muscle. It is contraindicated for asymptomatic intrasheath subluxation, type B intrasheath subluxation, or traumatic peroneal tendon subluxation or dislocation with superior peroneal retinaculum tear or ridge fracture of the retrofibular groove (Table 1), for which open or other endoscopic techniques are needed. 17 , 18 , 19 , 20

SURGICAL TECHNIQUE

Preoperative Planning and Patient Positioning

Magnetic resonance imaging is helpful to detect any tear of the peroneal tendons or superior peroneal retinaculum or the presence of a low‐lying PB muscle (Figure 1) or peroneus quartus. 3 Computed tomography is useful for the detection of any convexity of the retrofibular groove. A dynamic ultrasound study is important to confirm the intrasheath peroneal tendon subluxation. 1 , 4 , 6 , 21

Endoscopic retrofibular groove deepening and resection of an LLM of the peroneus brevis for the management of intrasheath subluxation of peroneal tendons associated with a low‐lying peroneus brevis muscle of the left ankle. The patient is placed in the lateral position. A transverse T1‐weighted image of the patient's left ankle shows the LLM of the peroneus brevis. (LLM, low‐lying muscle.)

The patient is placed in the lateral position. A 2.7‐mm 30° arthroscope (Henke Sass Wolf GmbH, Tuttlingen, Germany) is used for this procedure. A thigh tourniquet is applied to provide a bloodless operative field. Fluid inflow is driven by gravity, and no arthropump is used.

Portal Placement

This procedure is performed via the distal and proximal portals along the peroneal tendon sheath. The distal portal is 2 cm distal to the tip of the lateral malleolus. The proximal portal is 4 cm proximal to the tip of the lateral malleolus (Figure 2). 16 Five‐millimeter incisions are made at the portal sites. The subcutaneous tissue is bluntly dissected down to the peroneal tendon sheath which is then incised open.

Endoscopic Resection of Fibrous Band

The proximal portal is the viewing portal, and the distal portal is the working portal. Any fibrous bands constricting the peroneal tendons are resected with an arthroscopic shaver (Dyonics, Smith & Nephew, Andover, MA) (Figure 3).

The distal portal is the viewing portal. The patient's ankle is passively dorsiflexed and shifting of the PL tendon over the PB tendon can be seen, and the diagnosis of intrasheath subluxation can be confirmed (Figure 4).

Endoscopic Resection of a Low‐Lying Muscle Belly of PB

The distal portal is the viewing portal, and the proximal portal is the working portal. The low‐lying PB muscle belly within the retrofibular fibro‐osseous tunnel is resected with the arthroscopic shaver. This is performed through 3 windows: between the retrofibular groove and the peroneal tendons (Figure 5), between the PL and brevis tendons (Figure 6), and between the peroneal tendons and the superior peroneal retinaculum (Figure 7). The ankle can be dorsiflexed to bring in the more proximal part of the low‐lying muscle to the retrofibular tunnel to ensure adequate proximal extent of muscle resection.

Insertion of K‐Wires to Splint the Peroneal Tendons

The distal portal is the viewing portal. A 1.6‐mm K‐wire (Zimmer, Warsaw, IN) is inserted percutaneously from posterior to anterior into the retrofibular tunnel. The peroneal tendons are pushed medially till the medial edge of the distal fibula is reached by the K‐wire tip. The K‐wire is then pushed into the soft tissue at the medial edge of the fibula to splint the peroneal tendons medially (Figure 9).

The distal portal is the viewing portal, and the proximal portal is the working portal. The soft tissue covering the proximal half of the retrofibular groove is resected with the arthroscopic shaver, and the underlying bone is resected with an arthroscopic burr (Dyonics, Smith & Nephew, Andover, MA) (Figure 11). The fibrocartilaginous ridge of the groove should be preserved.

DISCUSSION

A constricted retrofibular fibro‐osseous tunnel is the cause of intrasheath peroneal tendon subluxation. 11 If it is because of a combination of an abnormal convex or flat retrofibular groove and the presence of a space‐occupying low‐lying muscle belly, endoscopic groove deepening and resection of a low‐lying muscle is a reasonable surgical option.

Preoperative confirmation of the diagnosis by dynamic ultrasound is of utmost importance because the patient cannot trigger the subluxation under anesthesia.

During the operation, adequate resection of the low‐lying muscle can be ensured by tracing the tendons to the peroneal tubercle and dorsiflexing the ankle to bring the proximal part of the low‐lying muscle to the surgical field. The superior peroneal retinaculum should be preserved to avoid anterior instability of the peroneal tendons. 13 Transverse extent of groove deepening is from the medial edge of the distal fibula, which is marked by the K‐wires, to the lateral fibrocartilaginous ridge. The longitudinal extent of groove deepening is from the most proximal end of distal fibular expansion down to the tip of the lateral malleolus. 13 , 16 It is important to make sure a smooth bone surface after groove deepening to prevent peroneal tendon tears. 4

Immediate mobilization after this endoscopic procedure can prevent development of symptomatic fibrous adhesions of the peroneal tendons.

The advantages of this minimally invasive approach include better cosmetic results, minimal soft tissue trauma, decreased postoperative pain, early mobilization and faster postoperative recovery, less peritendinous fibrosis, and less subjective tightness at the peroneal tendons. The potential risks include injury to the sural nerve, peroneal tendons and superior peroneal retinaculum, iatrogenic fracture of the lateral malleolus, and recurrence or persistence of intrasheath subluxation (Table 3). This approach is not technically difficult and can be managed by the average foot and ankle arthroscopist.

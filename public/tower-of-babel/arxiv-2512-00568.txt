Zero cycles on products of elliptic curves over local fields with supersingular reduction
Alejandro De Las Peñas Castaño

Abstract
. For a product E 1 × E 2 E_{1}\times E_{2} of two elliptic curves over a p p -adic field with good supersingular reduction, we produce infinitely many rational equivalences in the Chow group CH 0 ​ ( X ) \mathrm{CH}_{0}(X) of zero cycles via genus 2 covers of E 1 E_{1} and E 2 E_{2} . We use this to obtain evidence for a conjecture of Colliot-Thélène about the structure of the Albanese kernel.



Introduction

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety defined over a field
K
. A fundamental invariant of
X
is the Chow group
\mathrm{CH}_{0}(X)
of 0-cycles modulo rational equivalence. It is a generalization of the Picard group of curves and similarly comes equipped with an Abel-Jacobi map

\mathrm{alb_{X}}:F^{1}(X)\rightarrow\mathrm{Alb}_{X}(K),

called the
Albanese
map, where the domain
F^{1}(X)\subseteq\mathrm{CH}_{0}(X)
is the subgroup of 0-cycles of degree zero and
\mathrm{Alb}_{X}
is an abelian variety with the same universal property the Jacobian of a curve has, namely, when
X
possesses a
k
-rational point, there is a map
X\rightarrow\mathrm{Alb}_{X}
through which every map from
X
into an abelian variety factors uniquely.

As opposed to the case of curves, the Albanese map need not be injective and its kernel, denoted by
F^{2}(X)
, is known to vary wildly. For instance it is finite when
K
is finite
[
9
]
, but can be uncountable, when
K=\mathbb{C}

[
11
]
. When
K
is a number field the problem of determining
F^{2}(X)
remains wide open. In this direction, there are far-reaching conjectures by Beilinson and Bloch
[
1
,
2
]
that suggest that
F^{2}(X)
is finite.

A historically fruitful testing ground for such conjectures is the case when
K
is a
p
-adic field, i.e. a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. In this case, we have the following conjecture of Colliot-Thélène, later refined by Raskind and Spiess.

Conjecture A
.

(
[
3
, Conjecture 1.4]
,
[
12
, Conjecture 3.5.4]
) Let
X
be a smooth projective geometrically connected variety defined over a
p
-adic field
K
. Then
F^{2}(X)\cong D\oplus F
, where
D
is a divisible group and
F
is a finite group.

An important advancement in this direction is the work of Saito and Sato
[
13
]
where they show, under some mild technical conditions, that the larger group
F^{1}(X)
is the direct sum of a finite group and a group that is divisible by every integer coprime to
p
. Thus the key unresolved aspect of Colliot-Thélène’s conjecture is proving that the quotients
F^{2}(X)/p^{n}
are finite for all
n\geq 1
, along with determining the asymptotic behavior of their sizes as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

The full conjecture has been established only in very limited cases and for specific classes of varieties. In 2000, Raskind and Spiess proved the conjecture for varieties that are products of curves whose jacobians have a mixture of good ordinary reduction and split multiplicative reduction. Later, Gazaki and Leal were able to prove the conjecture for
X=E_{1}\times E_{2}
where at most one curve was allowed to have supersingular reduction
[
5
]
. The case when both curves have supersingular reduction is still unknown and is the case of study of this paper.

The starting point of the above results is to show that
F^{2}(E_{1}\times E_{2})
is isomorphic to a certain
K
-group
K(K;E_{1},E_{2})
called the
Somekawa

K
-group
[
16
]
. It is known that
F^{2}(E_{1}\times E_{2})
is generated by the 0-cycles on
E_{1}\times E_{2}
that come from intersecting the pullbacks of 0-cycles on
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
. The bilinear nature of these generators yields a surjection

\bigoplus_{L/K\,\text{finite}}E_{1}(L)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}E_{2}(L)\twoheadrightarrow F^{2}(E_{1}\times E_{2}).

Raskind and Spiess were able to compute the kernel and show that the quotient is precisely the Somekawa
K
-group
K(K;E_{1},E_{2})
. This quotient is visibly generated by equivalence classes of simple tensors of the form
P_{1}\otimes P_{2}
with
P_{i}\in E_{i}(L)
as
L
ranges over all finite extensions of
K
; such a class is called an
L/K
-
symbol
and is denoted by
\{P_{1},P_{2}\}_{L/K}
.

From this description, one can see why determining the size of
F^{2}(X)/p^{n}
is difficult. Even showing that
F^{2}(X)/p
is finite is still very hard. Indeed, each summand
E_{1}(L)/p\otimes E_{2}(L)/p
of
K(K;E_{1},E_{2})/p
can be quite large, requiring many relations to offset the shear number of generators. Furthermore, the structure of each summand depends sensitively on both the ramification of
K
and on the reduction type of the elliptic curves.

When
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
have good reduction and the absolute ramification index
e
of
K
is small, i.e.
e<p-1
, it is expected that

K(k;E_{1},E_{2})/p=0.

This expectation comes from the vanishing of a certain cycle map on
K(K;E_{1},E_{2})/p

[
8
, Corollary 8.1]
. So in order to prove a statement of this type, one must be able to produce enough relations in
K(K;E_{1},E_{2})
to cancel the generators of the Somekawa
K
-group modulo
p
. In this paper, we focus on the “first level” of
K(K;E_{1},E_{2})/p
, that is the subgroup of
K/K
-symbols.

There are two types of relations in
K(K;E_{1},E_{2})
one can draw from: the first, called the
Projection Formula
(PF), and the second, called
Weil Reciprocity
(WR). The former type of relation encodes how points on
E_{1}\times E_{2}
, when viewed over different fields extensions, interact with each other via norm and restriction maps. In fact, these relations were a key component of the previously mentioned methods of Raskind-Spiess and Gazaki-Leal. However, when both elliptic curves have supersingular reduction, PF relations fail to produce enough cancellations.

In this paper we produce infinitely many distinct WR relations in
K(K;E_{1},E_{2})
. For these to be useful, we need a way to classify them. One can show that the supersingularity assumption implies that every
\{P_{1},P_{2}\}_{K/K}
will be congruent modulo
p
to a
K/K
-symbol
\{\widehat{P}_{1},\widehat{P}_{2}\}_{K/K}
where
\widehat{P}_{i}
lies in the formal group
\widehat{E}_{i}(\mathfrak{m})
(see Section
1.2
). We can hence distinguish WR relations with the
signature
of
\{P_{1},P_{2}\}_{K/K}
, defined to be the pair of integers
(n_{1},n_{2})
where
n_{i}
is the valuation of
\widehat{P}_{i}
viewed as an element of the formal group (cf. Definition
1.2
). The first main result of this paper is the following.

Theorem A
.

Let
p>3
be prime. Let
K
be a
p
-adic field that is totally ramified over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Let
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
be elliptic curves defined over
K
with full
K
-rational 2-torsion and supersingular reduction. Then for any
n>0
, there exists a WR relation
\{P_{1},P_{2}\}_{K/K}=0
in
K(K;E_{1},E_{2})
of signature
(n,n)
.

The WR relations produced in Theorem
A
come from genus 2 covers of
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
. More precisely, there is a WR relation in
K(K;E_{1},E_{2})
for every curve
C
over
K
, whose Jacobian can be mapped into
E_{1}\times E_{2}
, and a choice of rational function
f\in K(C)^{\times}
. In this paper, we use a construction due to J. Scholten of a genus 2 curve
C
and explicit nonconstant maps
\varphi_{1}:C\rightarrow E_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}:C\rightarrow E_{2}
. Since
C
is hyperelliptic it has particularly simple principal divisors, so the choice of rational function can be made flexibly. Armed with a suitable choice of
C,\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
and
f
, we can then
p
-adically approximate the resulting WR relation to determine its signature.

This paper is organized into three sections. In section
1
, we review all the basic theory we will need about formal groups of elliptic curves, supersingular reduction, and the Somekawa
K
-group for products of elliptic curves. Along the way we define the signature of a
K/K
-symbol and determine the type and quantity of WR relations one needs to show the vanishing of all
K/K
-symbols modulo
p
. In section
2
we review the construction of the hyperelliptic curves that map to
E_{1}\times E_{2}
, compute the WR relations they produce, and use them to prove Theorem
A
. Lastly, in section
3
, we focus on the case of quadratic ramified extensions
K
and present computational evidence that the genus 2 spans of section
2
produce the necessary WR relations to show that all
K/K
-symbols vanish modulo
p
.

Notation

Throughout,
p
will be a prime larger than 3 and
K
will be a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, the field of
p
-adic numbers. The
p
-adic field
K
comes equipped with a unique normalized valuation we denote by
\nu
. Let
\mathcal{O}
be its valuation ring,
\mathfrak{m}
its unique maximal ideal, and
k=\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{m}
its residue field. Sometimes we will fix a uniformizer
\pi
, so in particular
\mathfrak{m}=(\pi)
and
\nu(\pi)=1
. The reduction map
\mathcal{O}\rightarrow k
will be denoted by
\alpha\mapsto\overline{\alpha}
. The ramification index of
K
over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
will be denoted by
e
so in particular
\nu(p)=e
. If we are working over different base fields at the same time we label the above objects with the corresponding field, e.g.
\mathcal{O}_{K},\mathfrak{m}_{K},k_{K}
, etc.

We will denote elliptic curves with
E
, these are smooth projective curves of genus 1 defined over
K
with a fixed
K
-rational point we denote by
O
that serves as the identity of the abelian group of
K
-rational points
E(K)
. Given a point
P\in E(K)
, we denote its coordinates by
x_{P}
and
y_{P}
, or by
x(P)
and
y(P)
. We denote the function field of
E
by
K(E)
and given a closed point
Q
of
E
, we denote the residue field of
Q
by
K(Q)
.

Elliptic curves defined over
p
-adic fields have minimal Weierstrass equations of the form

y^{2}=x^{3}+a_{2}x^{2}+a_{4}x+a_{6},

where
a_{i}\in\mathcal{O}
. If we reduce each coefficient modulo
\mathfrak{m}
, we obtain an elliptic curve
\overline{E}
defined over the residue field
k
. There is a well-defined reduction map
E(K)\rightarrow\overline{E}(k)
which we also denote by
P\mapsto\overline{P}
.

Given any abelian group
A
, such as
E(K)
or the formal group of
E
, and an integer
n
, we denote the
n
-torsion subgroup of
A
by
A[n]
and the cokernel of multiplication-by-
n
by
A/n
. The equivalence class of
a\in A
in
A/n
will be denoted by
[a]
.

1.
Preliminaries

1.1.
Formal Groups

In this section, we review the necessary background on formal groups and prove some structural results for
\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m})/p
. For more details, we refer the reader to
[
15
, Chapter IV]
or
[
14
, Part II, Chapter IV]
.

Let
E/K
be an elliptic curve and let
\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m})
be its associated formal group. The underlying set of
\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m})
is the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}\subset\mathcal{O}
which induces the filtration

\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m})\supseteq\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m}^{2})\supseteq\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m}^{3})\supseteq\cdots,

where the underlying set of
\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m}^{n})
is
\mathfrak{m}^{n}
.

Now lets assume that
E
has good reduction, i.e. the reduced curve
\bar{E}
is non-singular over
k
. It is well-known
[
15
, Proposition VII.2.1]
that there is an exact sequence:

(1.1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E_{1}(K)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E(K)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bar{E}(k)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

where
E_{1}(K)
is the kernel of the reduction map. In fact, this kernel is isomorphic to the formal group of
E
via

E_{1}(K)\longrightarrow\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m})\quad\text{defined by}\quad P=(X,Y)\mapsto-X/Y.

We will implicitly make this identification throughout.

The multiplication-by-
p
map in
\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m})
is compatible with the filtration in the following sense.

Theorem 1.1
.

(Theorem 4, pg. 119 of
[
14
]
) If
r>e/(p-1)
, then
p\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m}^{r})=\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m}^{r+e})
. In particular, if
e<p-1
, then
p\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m})=\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m}^{1+e})
.

Remark 1.1
.

If
e<p-1
, then
\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m})/p
is a
k
-vector space and Theorem
1.1
determines its structure as follows. The filtration of
\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m})
reduces to a finite filtration

\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m})/p\supseteq\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m}^{2})/p\supseteq\cdots\supseteq\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m}^{e})/p\supseteq 0

of length
e
. Each piece
\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m}^{i})/p
is naturally an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space and the graded components are all the additive group of
k
(see for example Corollary 1, pg 118 of
[
14
]
). That is

\frac{\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m}^{i})/p}{\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m}^{i+1})/p}\cong k^{+}.

If
K
has inertia degree
f
over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, then
k^{+}
is an
f
-dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space and thus
\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m})/p
has dimension
ef=[K:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]
. Furthermore, if for each
i=1,\ldots,e
, one chooses elements
\beta_{i,1},\ldots,\beta_{i,f}\in\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m}^{i})
whose classes in
\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m}^{i})/\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m}^{i+1})
form an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-basis, then the set
\{[\beta_{i,j}]\mid 1\leq i\leq e,1\leq j\leq f\}
is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-basis of
\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m})/p
.

For
e<p-1
, one can talk about valuations of elements in
\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m})/p
. Given a nonzero class
[\alpha]
in
\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m})/p
, the integer
\nu(\alpha)
is independent of the choice of representative. To see this, suppose
\alpha^{\prime}
is some other representative so
\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}+p\beta
for some
\beta\in\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m})
. Since
[\alpha]\neq 0
, Theorem
1.1
implies
\nu(\alpha)\leq e
and similarly
\nu(\alpha^{\prime})\leq e
. Since addition in
\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m})
, in fact any formal group, is given by
\widehat{E}(X,Y)=X+Y+XYF(X,Y)
for some
F\in\mathcal{O}[\![X,Y]\!]
(see §6, Chapter IV, Part II of
[
14
]
), we have

\widehat{E}(\alpha^{\prime},p\beta)=\alpha^{\prime}+p\beta+p\alpha^{\prime}\beta A,

for some
A\in\mathcal{O}
. Therefore

\nu(\alpha)=\nu\big(\alpha^{\prime}+p(1+\alpha^{\prime}A)\beta\big)=\nu(\alpha^{\prime}),

since
p(1+\alpha^{\prime}A)\beta\in\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m}^{1+e})
has larger valuation than
\alpha^{\prime}
by Theorem
1.1
.

Roughly speaking, the above argument shows that the usual valuation
\nu
on
\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m})
descends to
\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m})/p
. However one must be careful in declaring it a valuation. Firstly, any nonzero class
[\alpha]
will have
\nu([\alpha])\leq e
, and secondly,
\nu([0])
depends on its representative which can be chosen to have arbitrarily large valuation.

However, this “valuation” is useful for computations in
\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m})/p
, so we formalize it below.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
K
be a
p
-adic field with
e<p-1
. Let
E
be an elliptic curve over
K
. The
signature
of a class
[\alpha]\in\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m})/p
is defined as

\mathscr{S}([\alpha]):=\begin{cases}\nu(\alpha)&\text{if}\;\alpha\not\equiv 0\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{p})\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

As expected, the signature has several of the same properties that the valuation
\nu
has.

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
K
be a
p
-adic field with
e<p-1
and
E
an elliptic curve over
K
. Let
[\alpha],[\beta]\in\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m})/p
be nonzero classes. Then we have the following properties.

(i)

In general,
\mathscr{S}([\alpha]+[\beta])\geq\min\{\mathscr{S}([\alpha]),\mathscr{S}([\beta])\}
, with equality if
\mathscr{S}([\alpha])\neq\mathscr{S}([\beta])
.

(ii)

If
u\in\mathbb{Z}\cap O^{\times}
, then
\mathscr{S}(u[\alpha])=\mathscr{S}([\alpha])
.

Proof.

Suppose
\nu(\alpha)=m
and
\nu(\beta)=n
, and write
\alpha=a_{m}\pi^{m}+a_{m+1}\pi^{m+1}+\cdots
and
\beta=b_{n}\pi^{n}+a_{n+1}\pi^{n+1}+\cdots
, with
a_{i},b_{j}\in\mathcal{O}
. The sum of
\alpha
and
\beta
in
\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m})
is given by

\displaystyle\widehat{E}(\alpha,\beta)

\displaystyle=(a_{m}\pi^{m}+\cdots)+(b_{n}\pi^{n}+\cdots)+(a_{m}b_{n}\pi^{m+n}F(\alpha,\beta)+\cdots),

\displaystyle=a_{m}\pi^{m}+b_{n}\pi^{n}+O(\pi^{m+1}).

The first claim follows immediately. Finally, since multiplication-by-
u
in
\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m})
is multiplication-by-
u
in
\mathfrak{m}
up to higher order terms (cf.
[
15
, IV.2.3]
), there is a power series
G\in T^{2}\mathcal{O}[\![T]\!]
such that
u\alpha
as an element of
\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m})
has
\pi
-adic expansion

u\alpha+G(\alpha)=u(a_{m}\pi^{m}+\cdots)+O(\pi^{2m})=ua_{m}\pi^{m}+\cdots.

Since
u
is a unit, then
\nu(u\alpha)=m
.
∎

1.2.
Elliptic Curves with Supersingular Reduction

In this section, we focus on elliptic curves with supersingular reduction and identify the group
\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m})/p
with
E(K)/p
for such curves, this last quotient being a key component of the Somekawa
K
-group modulo
p
. We begin by briefly reviewing the definition of supersingular elliptic curves, of which there are a wealth of equivalent definitions. Below we list the two definitions we will need.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over
K
with good reduction. We say that
E
has
supersingular reduction
if the reduced curve
\overline{E}
defined over
k
is supersingular, that is, it satisfies one of the following equivalent conditions:

(1)

\overline{E}\left(\overline{k}\right)[p]=0
.

(2)

(Duering’s criterion) If
\overline{E}
is defined by a Weierstrass equation of the form
y^{2}=f(x)
, where
f(x)\in k[x]
is a separable cubic polynomial, then the coefficient of
x^{p-1}
of
f(x)^{(p-1)/2}
is zero.

Remark 1.2
.

(about supersingular reduction)

(1)

Since
\overline{E}(k)
is a finite abelian group, Lagrange’s theorem guarantees that
E
has supersingular reduction if and only if
|\overline{E}(k)|
is coprime to
p
.

(2)

If
p>3
and
k=\mathbb{F}_{p}
, then
|\overline{E}(k)|=p+1
. See
[
15
, Exercise 5.10]
.

(3)

If
E
has supersingular reduction and
L
is a finite extension of
K
, then the base change
E_{L}
also has supersingular reduction.

(4)

If
E
is in Legendre form
E_{\lambda}:y^{2}=x(x-1)(x-\lambda)
for some
\lambda\neq 0,1
, then Duering’s criterion can be restated as follows:
E_{\lambda}
is supersingular if and only if
\lambda
is a root of the following polynomial:

H_{p}(T):=\sum_{i=0}^{m}\binom{m}{i}^{2}T^{i},\qquad m=\tfrac{p-1}{2}.

See for example
[
15
, V.4.1]
.

Lemma 1.1
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over
K
with supersingular reduction. Then

E(K)/p\cong\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m})/p.

This isomorphism is induced by the inclusion
\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m})\hookrightarrow E(K)
.

Proof.

The reduction exact sequence (
1.1
) fits into the following commutative diagram with exact rows and columns:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E(K)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{E}(k)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E(K)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{E}(k)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}

The Snake Lemma yields the exact sequence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ker(\text{multiplication-by-$p$})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m})/p}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E(K)/p}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{E}(k)/p.}

Since
E
has supersingular reduction, then the multiplication-by-p map on the reduced curve is an injective map of finite groups and hence bijective. Thus the above exact sequence has zeros at its edges, and thus the map
\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m})/p\rightarrow E(K)/p
induced by the inclusion is an isomorphism.

∎

Remark 1.3
.

The diagram in the proof of Lemma
1.1
is preserved under base change to any finite extension
L
of
K
. Since
\overline{E_{L}}(k_{L})[p]
is still zero, then the above proof can be upgraded to show an isomorphism
E/p\cong\widehat{E}/p
of Mackey functors.

It is possible to realize the inverse map of the inclusion
\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m})/p\hookrightarrow E(K)/p
as multiplication by a fixed integer. Let
n
be the order of the finite group
\overline{E}(k)
. Then for any point
P\in E(K)
, the point
nP
is in the kernel of the reduction map, i.e.
nP\in\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m})
. Since
n
is coprime to
p
, there are
m,m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
1=mN+m^{\prime}p
. Thus

(1.2)

P=mnP+m^{\prime}pP\equiv mnP\pmod{pE(K)},

where
mnP\in\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m})
.

Definition 1.5
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over
K
with supersingular reduction. Let
n=|\overline{E}(k)|
and fix the smallest positive integer
m
such that
mn\equiv 1\pmod{p}
. For
P\in E(K)
, we define the
associated formal point
of
P
to be
\widehat{P}:=mnP\in\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m})\subset E(K)
.

As discussed in the previous paragraph, we obtain a well-defined map

E(K)/p\longrightarrow\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m})/p\quad\text{defined by}\quad[P]\mapsto[\widehat{P}],

and it is the inverse of the inclusion map by (
1.2
).

If
P\in E(K)
already lies in the formal group, it is a bit of a misnomer to call
\widehat{P}
the formal point associated to
P
when usually
P\neq\widehat{P}
. In fact, if
P=\widehat{P}
then
P
is
(mn-1)
-torsion in
\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m})
, and in particular
P
lies in
\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m})[p]
, which will vanish when
e<p-1
. However, the signature of
[P]
and
[\widehat{P}]
will be preserved by Proposition
1.3
.

For computational purposes, the integer
mn
may be very large and so computing
mnP
can be slow. At the cost of multiplying by a fixed integer, we can multiply by a divisor of
n
that varies with
P
. More precisely, given a point
P
, let
m_{P}\mid N
be the order of
\overline{P}
in
\overline{E}(k)
. Then
m_{P}P\in\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m})
and since
m_{P}
is coprime to
p
, Proposition
1.3
implies that

\mathscr{S}([P])=\mathscr{S}([\widehat{P}])=\mathscr{S}([m_{P}P]).

Thus when computing signatures, we are free to choose between
\widehat{P}
and
m_{P}P
.

1.3.
The Somekawa
K
-group

In this section we define the Somekawa
K
-group and describe some of its properties for the case of a product of two elliptic curves with supersingular reduction. For the sake of brevity, we define it only for the product of two elliptic curves over a perfect field, though it can be done for any finite product of semiabelian varieties over any field
[
16
]
.

Definition 1.6
.

Let
K
be a perfect field and
E_{1}
,
E_{2}
be elliptic curves over
K
. The
Somekawa

K
-group
K(K;E_{1},E_{2})
is defined as the abelian group generated by symbols of the form
\{P,Q\}_{L/K}
, where
L
is a finite extension of
K
, and
P\in E_{1}(L)
and
Q\in E_{2}(L)
, subject to the following types of relations:

(1)

(
\mathbb{Z}
-Bilinearity) For
P,P^{\prime}\in E_{1}(L)
and
Q,Q^{\prime}\in E_{2}(L)
, then

\displaystyle\{P+P^{\prime},Q\}_{L/K}=\{P,Q\}_{L/K}+\{P^{\prime},Q\}_{L/K},

\displaystyle\{P,Q+Q^{\prime}\}_{L/K}=\{P,Q\}_{L/K}+\{P,Q^{\prime}\}_{L/K}.

(2)

(Projection Formula) Given a tower of finite extensions
L^{\prime}\supseteq L\supseteq K
, with norm a restriction maps
N_{L^{\prime}/L}
and
\mathrm{res}_{L^{\prime}/L}
, and given
P\in E_{1}(L)
,
P^{\prime}\in E_{1}(L^{\prime})
,
Q\in E_{2}(L)
, and
Q^{\prime}\in E_{2}(L^{\prime})
, then

\displaystyle\{N_{L^{\prime}/L}(P^{\prime}),Q\}_{L/K}=\{P^{\prime},\mathrm{res}_{L^{\prime}/L}(Q)\}_{L^{\prime}/K},

\displaystyle\{P,N_{L^{\prime}/L}(Q^{\prime})\}_{L/K}=\{\mathrm{res}_{L^{\prime}/L}(P),Q^{\prime}\}_{L^{\prime}/K}.

(3)

(Weil Reciprocity) Given a smooth complete curve
C
defined over
K
with morphisms
\varphi_{1}:C\rightarrow E_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}:C\rightarrow E_{2}
defined over
K
, and a function field element
f\in K(C)^{\times}
,

\sum_{P\in C}\mathrm{ord}_{P}(f)\{\varphi_{1}(P),\varphi_{2}(P)\}_{K(P)/K}=0.

We say the
C

spans

E_{1}
and
E_{2}
.

Remark 1.4
.

There is clearly a surjection

\bigoplus_{L/K\,\mathrm{finite}}E_{1}(L)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}E_{2}(L)\longrightarrow K(K;E_{1},E_{2})

that maps
P\otimes Q
to
\{P,Q\}_{L/K}
if
P\in E_{1}(L)
and
Q\in E_{2}(L)
.

As mentioned in the introduction. The Somekawa
K
-group
K(K;E_{1},E_{2})
coincides with the albanese kernel
F^{2}(E_{1}\times E_{2})
. In fact, we have

Theorem 1.7
.

(Corollary 2.4.1 of
[
12
]
, Theorem 2.4 of
[
16
]
)
Let
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
be elliptic curves defined over a perfect field
K
. Then

CH_{0}(E_{1}\times E_{2})\cong\mathbb{Z}\oplus K(K;E_{1},E_{2})\oplus E_{1}(K)\oplus E_{2}(K).

Remark 1.5
.

When
K
is a
p
-adic field, the structure of
E_{1}(K)
and
E_{2}(K)
are well known. This is a special case of Mattuck’s Theorem
[
10
, Theorem 7]
: if
A
is an abelian variety of dimension
d
defined over a
p
-adic field
K
, then
A(K)\cong\mathcal{O}_{K}^{d}\oplus F
as abelian groups, where
F
is a finite group.

We are interested in the quotient group
K(K;E_{1},E_{2})/p
. We know that
K(K;E_{1},E_{2})
(and thus
K(K;E_{1},E_{2})/p
) is generated by symbols of the form
\{P_{1},P_{2}\}_{L/K}
as
L
ranges over all finite extensions of
K
, and these are in the images of the natural maps
P_{1}\otimes P_{2}\mapsto\{P_{1},P_{2}\}_{L/K}
. Thus the quotient group
K(K;E_{1},E_{2})/p
is generated by the images of the composite maps

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E_{1}(L)\otimes E_{2}(L)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus\limits_{F/K\,\text{finite}}E_{1}(F)\otimes E_{2}(F)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K(K;E_{1},E_{2})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K(K;E_{1},E_{2})/p.}

as
L
ranges over all finite extensions of
K
. For a fixed
L
it is clear that the above composite map factors through

(E_{1}(L)\otimes E_{2}(L))/p\cong(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\left(E_{1}(L)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}E_{2}(L)\right)\cong E_{1}(L)/p\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}E_{2}(L)/p.

We obtain the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-linear map

(1.3)

E_{1}(L)/p\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}E_{2}(L)/p\longrightarrow K(K;E_{1},E_{2})/p,\quad[P_{1}]\otimes[P_{2}]\mapsto\{P_{1},P_{2}\}_{L/K}\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{p}).

The image of this map is precisely the subgroup of
L/K
-symbols modulo
p
which we denote by:

\overline{\mathrm{Symb}}_{L/K}:=\text{image of}\;E_{1}(L)/p\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}E_{2}(L)/p\longrightarrow K(K;E_{1},E_{2})/p.

Hence if
\overline{\mathrm{Symb}}_{L/K}=0
for all finite extensions
L
of
K
, then
K(K;E_{1},E_{2})/p=0
which in turn proves Conjecture
A
for
E_{1}\times E_{2}
. This is very difficult, so we address the “first level” of this problem. Namely, the question we study in this paper is the following.

Conjecture B
.

Let
K
be a
p
-adic field,
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
elliptic curves over
K
with supersingular reduction. Then
\overline{\mathrm{Symb}}_{K/K}=0
.

This takes us to considering the structure of
E_{1}(K)/p\otimes E_{2}(K)/p
. When the curves
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
have supersingular reduction, then the structure is completely determined by the formal groups by Lemma
1.1
which tells us that the inclusion map
\widehat{E}_{i}(\mathfrak{m})\hookrightarrow E_{i}(K)
induces an isomorphism

\widehat{E}_{i}(\mathfrak{m})/p\xrightarrow{\sim}E_{i}(K)/p

with inverse
[P]\mapsto[\widehat{P}]
, where
\widehat{P}
is the formal point associated to
P
.

Furthermore, if the absolute ramification index
e
of
K
is small, i.e.
e<p-1
, then Theorem
1.1
, or more precisely Remark
1.1
, gives us a set

\{\beta_{i,j}\otimes\gamma_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\in\widehat{E}_{1}(\mathfrak{m})\otimes\widehat{E}_{2}(\mathfrak{m})\mid 1\leq i,i^{\prime}\leq e,1\leq j,j^{\prime}\leq f\}

whose classes modulo
p
form an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-basis of
\widehat{E}_{1}(\mathfrak{m})/p\otimes\widehat{E}_{2}(\mathfrak{m})/p
. Thus if

(1.4)

\{\beta_{i,j}\otimes\gamma_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\}_{K/K}\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{p})=0,

for all
i,j,i^{\prime},j^{\prime}
, then
\overline{\mathrm{Symb}}_{K/K}=0
.

The above condition can be difficult to guarantee since we would need to produce
[K:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]^{2}
relations in
K(K;E_{1},E_{2})/p
. The above basis is stratified by the valuation
\nu
on
K
since
\nu(\beta_{i,j})=\nu(\gamma_{i,j})=i
, and so we can approach the problem of producing relations of the form (
1.4
) “valuation-by-valuation”. The pair of valuations
(\nu(\widehat{P}_{1}),\nu(\widehat{P}_{2}))
, will be essential for comparing the symbol
\{P_{1},P_{2}\}_{K/K}
to the above basis. This motivates the following definition.

Definition 1.8
.

Let
K
be a
p
-adic field and let
E_{1},E_{2}
be elliptic curves over
K
with supersingular reduction. Let
\{P_{1},P_{2}\}_{K/K}
be a symbol in
K(K;E_{1},E_{2})
, where
P_{\ell}\in E_{\ell}(L)
for
\ell=1,2
. The
signature
of
\{P_{1},P_{2}\}_{K/K}
is defined as the pair of integers

\mathscr{S}(\{P_{1},P_{2}\}_{K/K})=\left(\nu(\widehat{P}_{1}),\nu(\widehat{P}_{2})\right)

where
\widehat{P}_{\ell}\in\widehat{E}_{\ell}(\mathfrak{m})
is the associated formal point of
P_{\ell}
(see Definition
1.5
).

Remark 1.6
.

Using the signature of points in
\widehat{E}_{\ell}(\mathfrak{m})/p
we defined in Section
1.1
we have:

\mathscr{S}(\{P_{1},P_{2}\}_{K/K})=\big(\mathscr{S}([P_{1}]),\mathscr{S}([P_{2}])\big).

Our proposed method of showing that
K/K
-symbols vanish in
K(K;E_{1},E_{2})/p
can be roughly described as “producing enough relations of the correct signature”. We first do some low degree examples to illustrate this method and then make it precise in Proposition
1.9
below.

Example 1.1
.

(
K=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
)
Since
[K:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]=1
, then
\widehat{E}_{1}(\mathfrak{m})/p\otimes\widehat{E}_{2}(\mathfrak{m})/p
is 1-dimensional over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Now suppose that we’ve produced a symbol
\{P_{1},P_{2}\}_{K/K}
with signature
(1,1)
such that
\{P_{1},P_{2}\}_{K/K}\equiv 0\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{p})
. Since the signature is
(1,1)
,
[\widehat{P}_{1}]\otimes[\widehat{P}_{2}]
generates
\widehat{E}_{1}(\mathfrak{m})/p\otimes\widehat{E}_{2}(\mathfrak{m})/p
and thus
[P_{1}]\otimes[P_{2}]
generates
E_{1}(K)/p\otimes E_{2}(K)/p
. Since

[P_{1}]\otimes[P_{2}]\mapsto\{P_{1},P_{2}\}_{L/K}\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{p})=0,

then
\overline{\mathrm{Symb}}_{K/K}=0
.

Example 1.2
.

(
K=
quadratic ramified extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
,
p>3
) In this case
e=2
and
f=1
so
\widehat{E}_{1}(\mathfrak{m})/p\otimes\widehat{E}_{2}(\mathfrak{m})/p
is 4-dimensional. Now suppose that we have four symbols

\{P_{1},P_{2}\}_{K/K},\{Q_{1},Q_{2}\}_{K/K},\{R_{1},R_{2}\}_{K/K},\{S_{1},S_{2}\}_{K/K}\in K(K;E_{1},E_{2})

of signatures
(1,1),(1,2),(2,1)
and
(2,2)
respectively, such that they are zero modulo
p
, e.g.
\{P_{1},P_{2}\}_{K/K}\equiv 0\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{p})
. The condition on their signatures, together with Remark
1.1
, implies that

\{[\widehat{P}_{1}]\otimes[\widehat{P}_{2}],[\widehat{Q}_{1}]\otimes[\widehat{Q}_{2}],[\widehat{R}_{1}]\otimes[\widehat{R}_{2}],[\widehat{S}_{1}]\otimes[\widehat{S}_{2}]\}

generates
\widehat{E}_{1}(\mathfrak{m})/p\otimes\widehat{E}_{2}(\mathfrak{m})/p
. Thus
\overline{\mathrm{Symb}}_{K/K}=0
.

Proposition 1.9
.

Let
K
be a
p
-adic field with absolute ramification index
e<p-1
. Let
E_{1},E_{2}
be elliptic curves over
K
with supersingular reduction. Suppose we have a set of
K/K
-symbols

\left\{\{P_{ij},Q_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\}_{K/K}\in K(K;E_{1},E_{2})\mid 1\leq i,i^{\prime}\leq e,1\leq j,j^{\prime}\leq f\right\}

such that

(i)

\{P_{ij},Q_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\}_{K/K}\equiv 0\pmod{p}
for all
i,i^{\prime},j,^{\prime}
.

(ii)

\mathscr{S}(\{P_{ij},Q_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\}_{K/K})=(i,i^{\prime})
,

(iii)

Both
\{\widehat{P}_{i1}+\widehat{E}_{1}(\mathfrak{m}^{i+1}),\ldots,\widehat{P}_{if}+\widehat{E}_{1}(\mathfrak{m}^{i+1})\}
and
\{\widehat{Q}_{i^{\prime}1}+\widehat{E}_{2}(\mathfrak{m}^{i+1}),\ldots,\widehat{Q}_{i^{\prime}f}+\widehat{E}_{1}(\mathfrak{m}^{i+1})\}
are
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-basis of
k\cong\widehat{E}_{\ell}(\mathfrak{m}^{i})/\widehat{E}_{\ell}(\mathfrak{m}^{i+1})
(
\ell=1,2
) for all
i
and

.

Then
\overline{\mathrm{Symb}}_{K/K}=0
.

Proof.

Conditions
(ii)
and
(iii)
imply via Remark
1.1
that

\{[\widehat{P}_{ij}]\otimes[\widehat{Q}_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}]\mid 1\leq i,i^{\prime}\leq e,1\leq j,j^{\prime}\leq f\}

is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-basis of
\widehat{E}_{1}(\mathfrak{m})/p\otimes\widehat{E}_{2}(\mathfrak{m})/p
and hence

\{[P_{ij}]\otimes[Q_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}]\mid 1\leq i,i^{\prime}\leq e,1\leq j,j^{\prime}\leq f\}

is a basis of

. Finally, condition

implies that each basis element

[P_{ij}]\otimes[Q_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}]\mapsto\{P_{ij},Q_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\}_{L/K}\equiv 0\pmod{p},

under the map (

), and thus its image

is zero.
∎

2.
Main results

2.1.
Weil Reciprocity

In this section we produce Weil Reciprocity relations in
K(K;E_{1},E_{2})
by using a particular family of hyperelliptic curves due to J. Scholten in an unpublished paper and generalized by Gazaki and Love
[
6
, §3]
. First we define these curves and show that there always exists a suitable one for computing the signature of the resulting relations.

An elliptic curve with full 2-torsion over
K
can be written as

E_{a,b}:y^{2}=x(x-a)(x-b),

where
a,b\neq 0
and
a\neq b
. Given a pair
E_{a,b}
and
E_{c,d}
of curves with full 2-torsion, Scholten defined a curve that mapped nontrivially into both
E_{a,b}
and
E_{c,d}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
a,b,c,d\in K^{\times}
with
a\neq b
and
c\neq d
. Then we define the
Scholten
curve
C_{a,b,c,d}
by the affine equation

(ad-bc)Y^{2}=((a-b)X^{2}-(c-d))(aX^{2}-c)(bX^{2}-d).

If
ad-bc\neq 0
, there are maps
\varphi_{a,b}:C_{a,b,c,d}\rightarrow E_{a,b}
and
\varphi_{c,d}:C_{a,b,c,d}\rightarrow E_{c,d}
defined over
K
:

(2.1)

\begin{gathered}\varphi_{a,b}(X,Y)=\left(\frac{ab(a-b)}{ad-bc}\left(X^{2}-\frac{c-d}{a-b}\right),\frac{ab(a-b)}{ad-bc}Y\right),\\
\varphi_{c,d}(X,Y)=\left(\frac{cd(a-b)}{(ad-bc)X^{2}}\left(X^{2}-\frac{c-d}{a-b}\right),-\frac{cd(c-d)}{(ad-bc)X^{3}}Y\right).\end{gathered}

Notation
.

If there is a curve
C
and maps
C\rightarrow E_{1}
,
C\rightarrow E_{2}
, all defined over
K
, we say that
E_{1}\leftarrow C\rightarrow E_{2}
is a
span
of
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
over
K
, or that
C

spans

E_{1}
and
E_{2}
over
K
.

Remark 2.1
.

The right hand side of the defining equation for
C:=C_{a,b,c,d}
is a product of three quadratics, and any pair of these have the same resultant which is equal to
(ad-bc)^{2}
. Thus if
ad-bc\neq 0
, then
C
is a smooth, geometrically irreducible curve of genus 2 and comes equipped with the usual hyperelliptic involution

\iota:C\longrightarrow C\quad\text{defined by}\quad\iota(x,y)=(x,-y).

Points fixed under this involution are called
Weierstrass points
.

The fact that
C
is hyperelliptic will allow us to produce a rational function on
C
quite easily and ultimately produce Weil Reciprocity relations.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
K
be a field,
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
elliptic curves over
K
. Suppose there is a smooth hyperelliptic curve
C
that spans
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
over
K
, via maps
\sigma_{i}:C\rightarrow E_{i}
that satisfy
\sigma_{i}(\iota(P))=-\sigma_{i}(P)
for every point
P\in C(K)
. If
P
is not fixed by the hyperelliptic involution, we have

4\{\sigma_{1}(P),\sigma_{2}(P)\}_{K/K}=0

in
K(K;E_{1},E_{2})
.

Proof.

First let us suppose that
C
has a
K
-rational Weierstrass point
W
. The
x
-coordinate map
x:C\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
is a degree 2 rational map on
C
and clearly
x=x\circ\iota
. The pullback of the divisor
D=(x(P))-(x(W))\in\mathrm{Pic}(\mathbb{P}^{1})
along
x:C\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
is

x^{*}(D)=(P)+(\iota P)-2(W).

Since
\mathrm{Pic}(\mathbb{P}^{1})=\mathbb{Z}
via the degree map, then
D
is principal, say
D=\mathrm{div}(g)
for
g\in K(\mathbb{P}^{1})
. Thus

(P)+(\iota P)-2(W)=x^{*}(D)=x^{*}(\mathrm{div}(g))=\mathrm{div}(x^{*}g).

That is,
x^{*}(D)
is principal.

If we write
f=x^{*}g
then the Weil relation attached to
C
,
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}
and
f
is

\{\sigma_{1}(P),\sigma_{2}(P)\}_{K/K}+\{\sigma_{1}(\iota(P)),\sigma_{2}(\iota(P))\}_{K/K}-2\{\sigma_{1}(W),\sigma_{2}(W)\}_{K/K}.

By the second assumption, have
\sigma_{i}(\iota P)=-\sigma_{i}(P)
and in particular
\sigma_{i}(W)
is 2-torsion. Using the bilinearity of
K/K
-symbols one concludes that

0=2\{\sigma_{1}(P),\sigma_{2}(P)\}_{K/K}.

Multiplying by 2 yields the result.

If
C
contains no
K
-rational Weierstrass points, then any Weierstrass point
W
will be defined over a quadratic extension
L
of
K
. In which case the principal divisor we have is
(P)+(\iota P)-(W)
, which yields the WR relation

2\{\sigma_{1}(P),\sigma_{2}(P)\}_{K/K}-\{\sigma_{1}(W),\sigma_{2}(W)\}_{L/K}.

Multiplying by 2 yields

0=4\{\sigma_{1}(P),\sigma_{2}(P)\}_{K/K}-2\{\sigma_{1}(W),\sigma_{2}(W)\}_{L/K}=4\{\sigma_{1}(P),\sigma_{2}(P)\}_{K/K}.

∎

Given two elliptic curves
E_{a,b}
and
E_{c,d}
with full 2-torsion, we want to apply Proposition
2.2
to the Scholten curve
C_{a,b,c,d}
, but it may be that
ad-bc=0
or that
C_{a,b,c,d}
may not have a
K
-rational Weierstrass point. However, the construction of
C_{a,b,c,d}
is flexible enough to produce more than just the one span from Definition
2.1
. Furthermore, if both elliptic curves have supersingular reduction, then we can always construct a Scholten curve and span that satisfies the hypothesis of Proposition
2.2
. To check this, we verify that such elliptic curves over totally ramified fields can be written in Legendre form with certain conditions on its
j
-invariant.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose
K
is a totally ramified extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with
p>3
. Let
E
be an elliptic curve will full 2-torsion and supersingular reduction. Then

(i)

p\equiv 3\pmod{4}
, in particular
-1
is not a square in
K^{\times}
.

(ii)

There exists
\lambda\in\mathcal{O}
such that
\lambda\not\equiv 0,1\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{\mathfrak{m}})
and such that
E
is isomorphic over
K
to a Legendre form elliptic curve

E_{\lambda}:y^{2}=x(x-1)(x-\lambda).

(iii)

The
j
-invariant
j(E)
satisfies
j(E)\not\equiv 0\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{\mathfrak{m}})
.

Proof.

(i)

Since
E
has supersingular reduction and the residue field
k
is
\mathbb{F}_{p}
by the assumption on the ramification, we have that
|\overline{E}(k)|=p+1
by Remark
1.2
. Since
E
also has full 2-torsion, then
\overline{E}(k)[2]
is an order 4 subgroup of
\overline{E}(k)
and thus Lagrange’s theorem tells us that
4\mid p+1
.

(ii)

Let
y^{2}=x^{3}+a_{2}x^{2}+a_{4}x+a_{6}
be a minimal Weierstrass equation for
E
. Since
E
has full 2-torsion, then the cubic in
x
is of the form
(x-e_{1})(x-e_{2})(x-e_{3})
with
e_{j}\in K
. In fact,
e_{i}\in\mathcal{O}
since they are roots of a monic polynomial with coefficients in the integrally closed ring
\mathcal{O}
. Thus the discriminant
\Delta_{E}
of
E
is

\Delta_{E}=16(e_{1}-e_{2})^{2}(e_{1}-e_{2})^{2}(e_{2}-e_{3})^{2}.

Since the Weierstrass equation is minimal and
E
has good reduction, we have
\nu(\Delta_{E})=0
, and since each
e_{i}
is integral, then
\nu(e_{i}-e_{j})\geq 0
so that
\nu(e_{i}-e_{j})=0
for
i\neq j
. That is
e_{1}-e_{2},e_{1}-e_{3}
and
e_{2}-e_{3}
are all units.

The first part tells us that
-1
is not a square in
k=\mathbb{F}_{p}
so either
e_{1}-e_{2}
or
e_{2}-e_{1}
is a square in
K
. Thus if we make the substitution
x=(e_{1}-e_{2})X+e_{2},y=(e_{1}-e_{2})^{3/2}Y
, respectively
x=(e_{2}-e_{1})X+e_{1},y=(e_{2}-e_{1})^{3/2}Y
, we obtain the Legendre form elliptic curve

E_{\lambda}:Y^{2}=X(X-1)(X-\lambda),\quad\lambda=\frac{e_{3}-e_{2}}{e_{1}-e_{2}},\;\text{resp.}\;\frac{e_{3}-e_{1}}{e_{2}-e_{1}}.

Notice that the change of variable that was made is of the form
x=u^{2}X+r
,
y=u^{3}Y
with
u
a unit. Thus the discriminant of
E_{\lambda}
satisfies:

0=\nu(\Delta_{E})=\nu(u^{12}\Delta_{E_{\lambda}})=\nu(\Delta_{E_{\lambda}})=\nu(16\lambda^{2}(1-\lambda)^{2})=2\nu(\lambda)+2\nu(1-\lambda).

Since
\lambda
is integral, then we conclude that
\lambda\not\equiv 0,1\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{\mathfrak{m}})
as required.

(iii)

Suppose that
j(E)\equiv 0\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{\mathfrak{m}})
. By the previous item, we have that

. However, it is well known that for

. Thus

.

∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be a totally ramified extension with
p>3
. Let
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
be elliptic curves over
K
with full 2-torsion and supersingular reduction. Then there exists a smooth genus 2 curve
C
that spans
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
. The curve
C
has defining equation of the form

C:Y^{2}=u(X^{2}-r)(X^{2}-s)(X^{2}-t),

where

, and the spanning maps
\sigma_{1}:C\rightarrow E_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}:C\rightarrow E_{2}

Proof.

By Lemma
2.1
, we may write
E_{1}=E_{\lambda}
and
E_{2}=E_{\mu}
for some
\lambda,\mu\in\mathcal{O}
such that
\lambda,\mu,1-\lambda
and
1-\mu
are all units. Furthermore,

so that
-1
is not a square in
K^{\times}
. The curve we are looking for will be taken from Table

. For simplicity, we label all the curves and constants in the table with a subindex

will have the equation

There are several cases in this proof, but they all follow the same structure. We will show that a curve

in Table

(see Remark

). Next, we show that either

or

is a square in

, which implies that the corresponding smooth curve will have a
K
-rational point of the form
(w,0)
where

or

. All the

,
s
and

and

. Thus

is also a unit.

We proceed to the proof. First we split into two cases:
\lambda\not\equiv\mu\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{\mathfrak{m}})
and
\lambda\equiv\mu\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{\mathfrak{m}})
. We drop the “
\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{\mathfrak{m}})
” from the notation for the rest of the proof.

1.

Suppose
\lambda\not\equiv\mu
. In this case,
\delta_{1}\equiv\mu-\lambda\not\equiv 0
for

so it is smooth. If

is a square, then
C_{1}
works. If
s_{1}
is not a square, then
s_{2}=-s_{1}=-(1-\mu)/(1-\lambda)
is a square, say
\eta^{2}
.

1.1.

If
\delta_{2}=1-\mu\lambda\not\equiv 0
, then
C_{2}
is smooth and has the Weierstrass point
(\eta,0)
.

1.2.

If
\delta_{2}\equiv 0
, then
\mu\equiv 1/\lambda
and thus
\eta^{2}\equiv-(1-\lambda^{-1})/(1-\lambda)
. Expanding this congruence out implies that
\lambda
satisfies the quadratic equation
0\equiv(\lambda-1)(\lambda-\eta^{-2})
. Since
\lambda\not\equiv 1
, then
\lambda\equiv\eta^{-2}
and
\mu\equiv\eta^{2}
are both squares. Next observe that

\displaystyle 0\equiv\delta_{3}=\mu-1+\lambda\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 0\equiv\lambda\mu-\lambda+\lambda^{2}\equiv 1-\lambda+\lambda^{2},

\displaystyle 0\equiv\delta_{5}=\lambda\mu-\mu-\lambda\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 0\equiv\lambda^{2}\mu-\lambda\mu-\lambda^{2}\equiv\lambda-1-\lambda^{2},

Notice that

since otherwise

which is not the case by Lemma

. Thus necessarily
\delta_{3},\delta_{5}\not\equiv 0
, that is

and
C_{5}
are smooth. If

is a square, then so is

, and thus

has a Weierstrass point. If
1-\lambda
is not a square, then

is a square since

is a square. Thus

has a Weierstrass point.

2.

Suppose
\lambda\equiv\mu
.

2.1.

If
\delta_{3}=\mu-1+\lambda\equiv 0
, then
2\lambda\equiv 1
. First, we have that

is smooth since otherwise we would have

0\equiv\delta_{4}=1-\mu+\lambda\mu\equiv 1-\lambda+\lambda^{2}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad j(E_{\lambda})\equiv 0,

2.1
. Next we also have that

Remark 2.2
.

As a consequence of the proof of Lemma
2.2
, we see that the degree six polynomial
F(X)\in\mathcal{O}[X]
has separable reduction. More precisely, if the curve
C
given to us by Lemma
2.2
is defined by
Y^{2}=F(X)
, with
F\in\mathcal{O}[X]
, then the reduced polynomial
\overline{F}\in k[X]
is separable since the resultant of any two of its quadratic factors is a unit by Remark
2.1
.

2.2.
Diagonal Signatures

We have shown in Lemma
2.2
that given a pair of elliptic curves
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
with full 2-torsion and supersingular reduction, we can produce a span
E_{1}\leftarrow C\rightarrow E_{2}
to which to we can apply Proposition
2.2
. Indeed, one immediately sees from the
y
-coordinate of
\sigma_{1}(x,y)
and
\sigma_{2}(x,y)
, and the formula for the hyperelliptic involution, that
\sigma_{i}(P)=-\sigma_{i}(\iota P)
.

This produces
K/K
-symbols
\{P_{1},P_{2}\}_{K/K}
in
K(K;E_{1},E_{2})
. The last step is to compute the signatures of these symbols. This is done by multiplying the entries
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
by integers (cf. Definition
1.5
).

The next lemma gives us a way to determine how deep into the filtration a point
P_{i}
goes when multiplied by a certain integer. Roughly speaking, if a
K
-rational point
P
on any elliptic curve
E
is
\pi
-adically close to an
n
-torsion point, with
n
coprime to
p
, then
nP
will be
\pi
-adically close to the identity, i.e. it will lie in the formal group. Furthermore, the closer the point is to the torsion point, the deeper
nP
will be in the filtration of
\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m})
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over
K
with good reduction,
n
coprime to
p
, and
N>0
. Let
P\in E(K)
. Then the following are equivalent.

(i)

nP\in\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m}^{N})\setminus\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m}^{N+1})
,

(ii)

There exists
T\in E(K)[n]
such that
P-T\in\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m}^{N})\setminus\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m}^{N+1})
.

Proof.

First we show that

(2.3)

nP\in\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m}^{N})\quad\iff\quad\text{there exists}\;T\in E(K)[n]\;\text{such that}\;P-T\in\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m}^{N}),

and then deduce the full Lemma. The forward implication of (
2.3
) is immediate since
n(P-T)=nP
. For the other implication, first we prove it when
N=1
, then derive the case
N>1
.

Consider the following commutative diagram

Since multiplication-by-

is an isomorphism (since

), then the Snake Lemma tells us that the reduction map induces an isomorphism

. Now, since

, then

and in particular

. Thus the above isomorphism tells us that there exists

that reduces to

, i.e.

Now suppose

. Since

, the

such that

. In particular

. Since

, then

by Proposition

, and therefore

as required. This proves (

).

Now, we show
(i)

. By (

. If

, then (

) would imply that

, which is not true. Thus
P-T\not\in\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m}^{N+1})
as required.

For the reverse implication, suppose there exists
T\in E(K)[n]
such that

. By (

),
nP\in\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m}^{N})
. If
nP\in\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m}^{N+1})

) would imply that there exists

and hence (

) applied to

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
K
be a totally ramified extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, with
p>3
. Let
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
be elliptic curves with full 2-torsion and supersingular reduction. Then for every positive integer
N
, there exist points
P_{N}\in\widehat{E}_{1}(K)
and
Q_{N}\in\widehat{E}_{2}(K)
such that the
K/K
-symbol
\{P_{N},Q_{N}\}_{K/K}\in K(K;E_{1},E_{2})
has signature
(N,N)
and such that

\{P_{N},Q_{N}\}_{K/K}\equiv 0\pmod{p}.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.2
there is a smooth genus 2 curve
C
that spans
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
. The defining equation of
C
is of the form
y^{2}=F(x)
where

F(X)=u\left(X^{2}-r\right)\left(X^{2}-s\right)\left(X^{2}-t\right)\in\mathcal{O}[X].

The spanning maps
\sigma_{1}:C\rightarrow E_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}:C\rightarrow E_{2}
are of the form

\sigma_{1}(X,y)=(u(X^{2}-v),uY),\quad\sigma_{2}(X,Y)=(ust(X^{2}-r)X^{-2},-urstX^{-3}Y),

where all the constants
u,r,s,t
and
v
are units. Finally, Lemma
2.2
tells us that
C
has a
K
-rational Weierstrass point
W=(w,0)
with
w\in\mathcal{O}^{\times}
.

Since
\sigma_{i}(W)
is 2-torsion in
E_{i}
, Lemma
2.3
suggests to find a point
P\in C(K)
that is
\pi
-adically close to
W
. We do this with Hensel’s Lemma. For the given
N>0
, consider the polynomial

f(X):=\pi^{2N}-F(X)\in\mathcal{O}[X].

Notice that
f(w)=\pi^{2N}-F(w)=\pi^{2N}\equiv 0\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{\pi^{2N}})
since
W=(w,0)
satisfies the equation of
C
. That is,
w
is an approximate root of
f(w)
. Since
\overline{F}\in k[X]
is separable (cf. Remark
2.2
), then
F^{\prime}(w)\not\equiv 0\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{\pi})
. Thus Hensel’s Lemma applies, that is, there exists
x_{0}\in\mathcal{O}
such that

(2.4)

f(x_{0})=0\quad\text{and}\quad x_{0}\equiv w\pmod{\pi}.

In particular, we obtain a
K
-rational point on
C
:

P:=(x_{0},\pi^{N})\in C(K).

We claim that we can upgrade the congruence in (
2.4
) to

(2.5)

\nu(x_{0}-w)=2N.

The first condition in (
2.4
) implies that
u(x_{0}^{2}-r)(x_{0}^{2}-s)(x_{0}^{2}-t)=\pi^{2N}
, that is

(x_{0}^{2}-r)(x_{0}^{2}-s)(x_{0}^{2}-t)\equiv 0\pmod{\pi^{2N}}

Since
W=(w,0)
satisfies the equation of
C
, we have that
w^{2}=r,s
or
t
; without loss of generality, lets assume that
w^{2}=r
. Hence the second condition of (
2.4
) implies that
x_{0}^{2}\equiv r\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{\pi})
. In particular,
\overline{x_{0}}
is a root of
\overline{F}
, which is separable, and hence
x_{0}^{2}-s,x_{0}^{2}-t\not\equiv 0\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{\pi})
. This shows that
x_{0}^{2}\equiv w\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{\pi^{2N}})
. Now, if
x_{0}\equiv w\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{\pi^{2N+1}})
, then there exists
a\in\mathcal{O}
such that
x_{0}=w+\pi^{2N+1}a
. Thus Taylor’s formula would imply that

0=f(x_{0})=f(w+\pi^{2N+1}a)=f(w)+f^{\prime}(w)\pi^{2N+1}a+O(\pi^{4N+2})=\pi^{2N}+O(\pi^{2N+1}),

which cannot happen. Thus we have (
2.5
).

Next we claim that
\sigma_{i}(P)-\sigma_{i}(W)\in\widehat{E}_{i}(\mathfrak{m}^{N})\setminus\widehat{E}_{i}(\mathfrak{m}^{N+1})

2.3

\sigma_{i}(W)
is 2-torsion, so
\sigma_{i}(W)

\sigma_{i}(P)
has a nonzero

\sigma_{i}
is defined and the fact that

is a unit. Thus

and the addition formula is simple to use. For simplicity, we write
P_{i}:=(x_{i},y_{i}):=\sigma_{i}(P)
and
W_{i}:=(w_{i},0):=\sigma_{i}(W)
. The addition formula tells us that the
x

is

x(P_{i}-W_{i})=\left(\frac{y_{i}}{x_{i}-w_{i}}\right)^{2}-x_{i}-w_{i}.

By the formula for
\sigma_{1}
, we have that
y_{1}=u\pi^{N}
and

x_{1}-w_{1}=u(x_{0}^{2}-v)-u(w^{2}-v)=u(x_{0}-w)(x_{0}+w).

Since
x_{0}\equiv w\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{\pi})
, and
w
is a unit, then
x_{0}
is a unit and
x_{0}\not\equiv-w\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{\pi})
. Thus (
2.5
) implies that
\nu(x_{1}-w_{1})=2N
and hence
\nu(y_{1}/(x_{1}-w_{1}))=-N
. The final term
-x_{1}-w_{1}
is integral and hence

\nu(x(P_{1}-W_{1}))=-2N.

In particular,
P_{1}-W_{1}\in\widehat{E}_{1}(\mathfrak{m}^{N})\setminus\widehat{E}_{1}(\mathfrak{m}^{N+1})
as required.

For

, the computations are very similar. First, we see that
y_{2}=-urstx_{0}^{2}\pi^{N}
has valuation

since

and
x_{0}
are all units. Secondly, we have

x_{2}-w_{2}=ust(x_{0}^{2}-r)x_{0}^{-2}-ust(w^{2}-r)w^{-2}=ustr(x_{0}-w)(x_{0}+w)x_{0}^{-2}w^{-2},

which again has valuation

by (

). Since
-x_{2}-w_{2}
is integral, we similarly have that

\nu(x(P_{2}-W_{2}))=-2N.

Hence
P_{2}-W_{2}\in\widehat{E}_{2}(\mathfrak{m}^{N})\setminus\widehat{E}_{2}(\mathfrak{m}^{N+1})
.

Now we apply Lemma
2.3
to
P_{i}-W_{i}\in\widehat{E}_{i}(\mathfrak{m}^{N})\setminus\widehat{E}_{i}(\mathfrak{m}^{N+1})
. Since
W_{i}
is 2-torsion, then
2P_{i}\in\widehat{E}_{i}(\mathfrak{m}^{N})\setminus\widehat{E}_{i}(\mathfrak{m}^{N+1})
. Next we apply Lemma
2.2
to the span
E_{1}\leftarrow C\rightarrow E_{2}
to obtain

4\{P_{1},P_{2}\}_{K/K}=0.

We use bilinearity to conclude that

\{2P_{1},2P_{2}\}_{K/K}=0.

Since
2P_{i}\in\widehat{E}_{i}(\mathfrak{m}^{N})\setminus\widehat{E}_{i}(\mathfrak{m}^{N+1})
, we have that the signature of the above symbol is
(N,N)
. This concludes the proof.

∎

This method recovers previously known results about
K/K
-symbols
[
4
, Theorem 4.6]
for
K=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, via completely different methods. Theorem
2.3
, for
N=1
, and Example
1.1
combine to give the following.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
be elliptic curves defined over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with full 2-torsion and supersingular reduction. Then
\Phi_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}=0
in
K(\mathbb{Q}_{p};E_{1},E_{2})/p
.

Theorem
2.3
produces
K/K
-symbols with diagonal signature that vanish. However, one would like to claim that all
K/K
-symbols with diagonal signature vanish. The above results are not enough to show this in full generality, but we do have the following corollary.

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
K
be a totally ramified extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, with
p>3
. Let
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
be elliptic curves with full 2-torsion and supersingular reduction. If the degree
e=[K:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]
satisfies
e<p-1
, then every
K/K
-symbol in
K(K;E_{1},E_{2})
of signature
(e,e)
vanishes modulo
p
.

Proof.

Let
\{P,Q\}_{K/K}\in K(K;E_{1},E_{2})
be a
K/K
-symbol of signature
(e,e)
.
By Theorem
2.3
, there are points
P_{e}\in\widehat{E}_{1}(\mathfrak{m}^{N})\setminus\widehat{E}_{1}(\mathfrak{m}^{N+1})
and
Q_{e}\in\widehat{E}_{2}(\mathfrak{m}^{N})\setminus\widehat{E}_{2}(\mathfrak{m}^{N+1})
such that
\{P_{e},Q_{e}\}_{K/K}\equiv 0\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{p})
. Since
\widehat{E}_{1}(K)/p\cong E_{1}(K)/p
is an
e
-dimensional graded
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space, we can find points
P_{1},\ldots,P_{e-1}\in\widehat{E}_{1}(K)
such that
\{[P_{1}],\ldots,[P_{e}]\}
is a basis of
\widehat{E}_{1}(K)/p
and such that
P_{i}\in\widehat{E}_{1}(\mathfrak{m}^{i})\setminus\widehat{E}_{1}(\mathfrak{m}^{i+1})
.

Therefore there are scalars
t_{1},\ldots,t_{e}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
such that
[\widehat{P}]=\sum t_{i}[P_{i}]
. By the properties of the signature (cf. Proposition
1.3
), we have that

e=\mathscr{S}([P])=\mathscr{S}([\widehat{P}])=\mathscr{S}\left(\sum t_{i}[P_{i}]\right)=\min_{t_{i}\neq 0}\{\mathscr{S}(t_{i}[P_{i}])\}=\min_{t_{i}\neq 0}\{\mathscr{S}([P_{i}])\}=\min\{i\mid t_{i}\neq 0\},

since the nonzero summands of
\sum t_{i}[P_{i}]
all have distinct signatures. Hence we must have
t_{1}=\cdots=t_{e-1}=0
and
t_{e}\neq 0
. We conclude that
[\widehat{P}]=t_{e}[P_{e}]
for some
t_{e}\neq 0
. Analogously, we have that
[\widehat{Q}]=s_{e}[Q_{e}]
for some
s_{e}\neq 0
.

By the definition of the associated formal point, we have that
[P]=[\widehat{P}]
and
[Q]=[\widehat{Q}]
so that

\{P,Q\}_{K/K}\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{p})=\Phi_{K}([P]\otimes[Q])=\Phi_{K}([\widehat{P}]\otimes[\widehat{Q}])=\Phi_{K}(t_{e}[P_{e}]\otimes s_{e}[Q_{e}]).

Since
\Phi_{K}
is
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-linear, we have

\Phi_{K}([P]\otimes[Q])=t_{e}s_{e}\Phi_{K}([\widehat{P}_{e}]\otimes[\widehat{Q}_{e}])
. We conclude that

\{P,Q\}_{K/K}\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{p})=t_{e}s_{e}\Phi_{K}([P_{e}]\otimes[Q_{e}])=t_{e}s_{e}\{P_{e},Q_{e}\}_{K/K}\equiv 0\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{p}).

∎

3.
The Case of Quadratic Ramified Extensions

In the previous section we described how one can choose a specific point on a genus 2 cover of
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
, namely a point that is
p
-adically close to a Weierstrass point, to produce a WR relation with signature
(n,n)
. However, choosing other points yields “off-diagonal” signatures as well. In this section we will show computational evidence that this method usually produces enough relations in
K(K;E_{1},E_{2})/p
to show that
\overline{\mathrm{Symb}}_{K/K}=0
, in view of Proposition
1.9
.

First we describe the general procedure using SAGE and then present the results. We restrict to the case that
K
is a totally ramified extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with
p\equiv 3\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{4})
(cf. Lemma
2.1
). In particular,
k=\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

Step 1:
Given
K
, we compute the elements
\overline{\lambda}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
for which the Legendre form elliptic curve
y^{2}=x(x-1)(x-\overline{\lambda})
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
is supersingular. These correspond to roots of the supersingular polynomial

H_{p}(T):=\sum_{i=0}^{m}\binom{m}{i}^{2}T^{i},\qquad m=\frac{p-1}{2}.

See Remark
1.2
. We then obtain a list
\{\overline{\lambda_{1}},\ldots,\overline{\lambda_{m}}\}\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
of parameters for which
E_{\overline{\lambda_{i}}}
is supersingular.

Step 2:
For each pair
(\overline{\lambda},\overline{\mu})
in the list of supersingular parameters given in the previous step, we compute the six genus 2 curves from Table
1
that span
E_{\lambda}
and
E_{\mu}
where
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{Z}
are lifts of
\overline{\lambda}
and
\overline{\mu}
. It is possible that some of these 6 curves degenerate when reduced over
k
; we will discard these curves.

Step 3:
Given a span
E_{\lambda}\leftarrow C\rightarrow E_{\mu}
from Step 2, we produce
K
-rational points on
C
in the following manner. We fix some
x_{0}\in K
and solve the quadratic equation
y^{2}=f(x_{0})
, where
y^{2}=f(x)
is the defining equation of the given hyperelliptic curve. This generates the “quadratic” point
(x_{0},\sqrt{f(x_{0})})
. In general this may not have a solution, but
K
has enough squares for this method to work reliably. Indeed, the group
K^{\times}/K^{\times 2}
has order four.

We can systematically generate these “quadratic” points as follows. If
x_{0}\in K
has valuation
\nu(x_{0})=n
for some
n\neq 0
, then by the form of the defining equation
y^{2}=f(x)
of the curve
C
, we have that
\nu(f(x_{0}))=6n
(resp.
\nu(f(x_{0}))=0
) if
n<0
(resp.
n>0
). Hence if
y_{0}=\sqrt{f(x_{0})}
is defined over
K
, then
\nu(y_{0})=3n
(resp.
\nu(y_{0})=0
). By the formulas for the spanning maps
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
, then the coordinates
(x_{i},y_{i}):=\sigma_{i}(x_{0},y_{0})
have valuations

\nu(x_{1})=\begin{cases}2n&\text{if}\;n<0\\
0&\text{if}\;n>0,\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad\nu(x_{2})=\begin{cases}0&\text{if}\;n<0\\
-2n&\text{if}\;n>0.\end{cases}

This means that
\sigma_{1}(x_{0},y_{0})\in\widehat{E}_{1}(\mathfrak{m}^{|n|})\setminus\widehat{E}_{1}(\mathfrak{m}^{|n|})
if
n<0
and
\sigma_{2}(x_{0},y_{0})\in\widehat{E}_{2}(\mathfrak{m}^{n})\setminus\widehat{E}_{2}(\mathfrak{m}^{n})
if
n>0
. We know by Lemma
1.1
that we only need to consider the case when
|n|<e+1
. Since we can view
K
as Laurent series in some uniformizer
\pi
, then we can take
x_{0}
in the finite set of truncated Laurent series

\mathcal{B}:=\{c_{-e}\pi^{-e}+\cdots+c_{0}+c_{1}\pi+\cdots+c_{e}\pi^{e}\in K\mid c_{i}=0,\ldots,p-1\}.

Note that this set has
p^{2e+1}
many elements.

Step 4:
Given a span
E_{\lambda}\leftarrow C\rightarrow E_{\mu}
from Step 2 and a
K
-rational point
P_{0}\in C(K)
from Step 3, we compute the signature of
\{\sigma_{1}(P_{0}),\sigma_{2}(P_{0})\}
by computing the
\mathfrak{m}
-adic expansions of
n_{1}\sigma_{1}(P)\in\widehat{E}_{1}(\mathfrak{m})
and
n_{2}\sigma_{2}(P)\in\widehat{E}_{2}(\mathfrak{m})
where
n_{i}
is the order of the reduced point
\overline{\sigma_{i}(P)}
on the reduced elliptic curve (see the discussion after Definition
1.5
). From the resulting list of signatures, we check if it includes (1,1), (1,2), (2,1) and (2,2). In view of Example
1.2
, this would imply that
\overline{\mathrm{Symb}}_{K/K}=0
.

Putting everything together, we have the following algorithm.

Algorithm 1
.

Let
K
be a quadratic ramified extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with
p>3
.

Input:

A pair of integers
(\lambda,\mu)
such that
E_{\lambda}
and
E_{\mu}
have supersingular reduction.

Output:

A list of signatures
(i,j)
corresponding to WR relations of the form
\{P,Q\}_{K/K}\equiv 0\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{p})
in
K(K;E_{1},E_{2})
.

Example 3.1
.

(
K=\mathbb{Q}_{7}(\sqrt{7})
) We have that
K
is a ramified quadratic extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{7}
. We fix
\pi:=\sqrt{7}
for the uniformizer. By Duering’s criterion, we see that
E_{\lambda}
is supersingular if
\lambda\equiv 2,4,6\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{p})
. So let us consider the elliptic curves
E_{2}
and
E_{6}
.

The spanning genus 2 curves are given by

\displaystyle C_{1}:\;y^{2}=-\frac{1}{2}(x^{2}-5)(x^{2}-3)(x^{2}-1),\quad C_{2}:\;y^{2}=\frac{2}{11}(x^{2}+5)(x^{2}-6)(x^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2})

\displaystyle C_{3}:\;y^{2}=-\frac{1}{2}(x^{2}+5)(x^{2}+3)(x^{2}+1),\quad C_{4}:\;y^{2}=\frac{2}{11}(x^{2}-5)(x^{2}+6)(x^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2})

The last two curves degenerate over
\mathbb{F}_{7}
. Notice that each of these curves has a
K
-rational Weierstrass point since the sextic polynomials all have at least one factor that splits (e.g.
-5
is a square in
\mathbb{Q}_{7}
).

The set
\mathcal{B}
has
p^{2e+1}=7^{5}
elements so we have plenty of
K
-rational points on the various
C_{i}
’s. Consider the following four:

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=(3+\pi+3\pi^{2}+4\pi^{4}+\cdots,3+4\pi+6\pi^{2}+6\pi^{3}+\cdots)\in C_{1}(K),

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle=(2\pi^{-1}+6\pi+\pi^{2}+\pi^{3}+\cdots,2\pi^{-3}+6\pi^{-1}+3+\pi+3\pi^{2}+3\pi^{3}+\cdots)\in C_{4}(K),

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=(4\pi+2\pi^{3}+3\pi^{4}+\cdots,2+3\pi^{2}+\pi^{4}+\cdots)\in C_{2}(K),

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle=(2\pi^{2}+5\pi^{3}+3\pi^{4}+\cdots,2\pi^{2}+3\pi^{4}+\cdots)\in C_{1}(K).

Then one can compute the signatures to be

\displaystyle\mathscr{S}(\{\sigma_{1}(P),\sigma_{2}(P)\}_{K/K})

\displaystyle=(1,1),

\displaystyle\mathscr{S}(\{\sigma_{1}(Q),\sigma_{2}(Q)\}_{K/K})

\displaystyle=(1,2),

\displaystyle\mathscr{S}(\{\sigma_{1}(R),\sigma_{2}(R)\}_{K/K})

\displaystyle=(2,1),

\displaystyle\mathscr{S}(\{\sigma_{1}(T),\sigma_{2}(T)\}_{K/K})

\displaystyle=(2,2).

Thus by Proposition
1.9
we can conclude that
\overline{\mathrm{Symb}}_{K/K=0}
in
K(K;E_{2},E_{6})/7
.

In view of the above example, given a totally ramified
p
-adic field
K
and a pair of Legendre form elliptic curves
E_{\lambda}
and
E_{\mu}
with supersingular reduction, we say that
Algorithm
1
proves
\overline{\mathrm{Symb}}_{K/K}=0
, or that it is
successful
, if the output of the algorithm includes the signatures (1,1), (1,2), (2,1) and (2,2). We run Algorithm
1
for all totally ramified quadratic extensions of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
for the first ten primes
p\equiv 3\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{4})
and check the success. The results are in table

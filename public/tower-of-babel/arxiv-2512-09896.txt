A 0.8395-approximation algorithm for the EPR problem Thanks: Sandia National Laboratories
Anuj Apte, Eunou Lee, Kunal Marwaha, Lennart Sinjorgo, James Sud

Abstract
We give an efficient 0.8395-approximation algorithm for the EPR Hamiltonian. Our improvement comes from a new nonlinear monogamy-of-entanglement bound on star graphs and a refined parameterization of a shallow quantum circuit from previous works. We also prove limitations showing that current methods cannot achieve substantially better approximation ratios, indicating that further progress will require fundamentally new techniques.



1
Introduction

Consider a graph
G=(V,E,w)
on vertices
V\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}}{{=}}{\left[n\right]}
, with edges
E\subseteq V\times V
and edge weights
w\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{E}
. The
EPR problem
, as introduced by
[
11
]
, is to find the maximum eigenvalue of the following Hamiltonian:

\displaystyle H(G)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}}{{=}}\sum_{(i,j)\in E}w_{ij}h_{ij}\,,

(1)

\displaystyle h_{ij}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{2}(I_{i}I_{j}+X_{i}X_{j}-Y_{i}Y_{j}+Z_{i}Z_{j})\,.

Note that the off-diagonal elements of
h_{ij}
are all positive in the computational basis, so the decision version of the EPR problem on a graph with positive weights is in
{\mathsf{StoqMA}}

[
4
]
. We do not know whether it is in
{\mathsf{P}}
.
We study
\alpha
-approximation algorithms for the EPR problem. Such an algorithm
\mathcal{A}
inputs a graph
G
, and outputs a value
\mathcal{A}(G)
such that
\alpha\cdot\lambda_{\max}(H(G))\leq\mathcal{A}(G)\leq\lambda_{\max}(H(G))
. An efficient
\alpha
-approximation algorithm was first shown in
[
11
]
for
\alpha>0.7071
. This was improved to
\alpha=0.72
in
[
9
]
, and later to
\alpha>0.8090
in
[
2
,
10
]
. Our main result is a further improvement of
\alpha
to
>0.8395
:

Theorem 1
.

There is an efficient
\alpha
-approximation algorithm for the EPR problem for
\alpha>0.8395
.

1.1
Algorithm

Our algorithm uses the same quantum circuit structure (ansatz) as previous approximation algorithms for the EPR problem
[
1
,
11
,
2
]
. This ansatz does the following:

1.

Solve an efficient relaxation of the EPR problem to obtain values
g_{ij}
for each edge
(i,j)
in
G
.

2.

Apply a depth-
1
quantum circuit parameterized by a set of angles
\{\theta_{ij}\}_{(i,j)\in E}
. The angles are chosen as a deterministic function of
\{g_{ij}\}_{(i,j)\in E}
.

For convenience, we assume that
\theta_{ij}
depends
only
on
g_{ij}
; i.e.
\theta_{ij}=\nu(g_{ij})
for some function
\nu
. We choose values
\left\{g_{ij}\right\}_{(i,j)\in E}
by solving the quantum moment sum-of-squares (moment-SoS) hierarchy defined in
Section

2.1
. We state this ansatz formally as
Algorithm

1
:

Input:
weighted graph
G(V,E,w)
and function
\nu:{\left[-1,1\right]}\rightarrow{\left[0,1\right]}\,

Solve the level-
2
quantum moment-SoS hierarchy (
Eq.

10
) to obtain
\left\{g_{ij}\right\}_{(i,j)\in E}\,
.

Output the state

\displaystyle\ket{\psi_{G}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}}{{=}}\prod_{(i,j)\in E}\exp\left(\frac{\mathbf{i}\,\theta_{ij}}{4}(X_{i}-Y_{i})\otimes(X_{j}-Y_{j})\right)\ket{0}^{\otimes n}\,,

(2)

where
\mathbf{i}
is the imaginary unit and
\theta_{ij}=\nu(g_{ij})
.

Algorithm 1

EPR approximation ansatz

Algorithm

1
comes with a classically computable lower bound on its average energy:

Lemma 1

(
[
11
, Lemma 9]
)
.

Algorithm

1
prepares a state with energy at least

\displaystyle\bra{\psi_{G}}H(G)\ket{\psi_{G}}\geq\ell(G)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}}{{=}}\sum_{(i,j)\in E}w_{ij}\cdot\frac{1+A_{ij}A_{ji}+(A_{ij}+A_{ji})\sin\nu(g_{ij})}{2}\,,

(3)

where

A_{ij}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}}{{=}}\prod_{k\in N(i)\setminus\{j\}}\cos\nu(g_{ik})\,
and
N(i)
is the set of neighbors of
i
in
G
. Furthermore, the inequality is an equality for all triangle-free graphs.

To fully specify
Algorithm

1
, we must choose a function
\nu
. We provide our specific choice of
\nu
in
Theorem

2
. This function was obtained via numerical search, and we prove the correctness of the approximation ratio analytically in
Theorem

2
. We show in
Section

4
that our choice of
\nu
is essentially optimal.

1.2
Techniques

Our improved approximation ratio relies on two crucial insights. First, we prove a new
monogamy of entanglement
(MoE) statement, generalizing the bound of
[
12
, Lemma 3]
from a pair of edges to a star. As with other MoE bounds, we show this statement holds for the EPR problem
and
for its level-
k
semidefinite relaxation whenever
k\geq 2
. We describe these relaxations in
Section

2
and prove the lemma in
Section

A.2
.

Lemma 2

(Nonlinear monogamy of entanglement on a star)
.

Fix any graph
G=(V,E,w)
and state
\ket{\psi_{G}}
. For each edge
(i,j)\in E
, let
g_{ij}=\bra{\psi_{G}}h_{ij}\ket{\psi_{G}}-1
. Then for any vertex
i\in V
with degree
d_{i}\geq 2
and
j\in N(i)
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in N(i)\setminus\left\{j\right\}}g_{ik}

\displaystyle\leq\begin{cases}1,\quad&\text{ if }\,-1\leq g_{ij}<-\frac{1}{d_{i}},\\
\frac{1}{2}\left(2-d_{i}-g_{ij}+\sqrt{\left(d_{i}^{2}-1\right)\left(1-g_{ij}^{2}\right)}\right),\quad&\text{ if }\,-\frac{1}{d_{i}}\leq g_{ij}\leq 1\,,\end{cases}

(4)

where
N(i)
is the set of neighbors of
i
. Moreover,
Eq.

4
holds for any
g
that is the solution to the level-
k
semidefinite relaxation of
H(G)
(defined in
Section

2
), for any
k\geq 2
.

Our second insight follows from
[
8
]
. We choose the parameterization function
\nu
from a set
\mathcal{C}
that dramatically simplifies the approximation ratio analysis:

Definition 1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be the set of monotonically increasing functions
\Theta:[0,1]\to[0,1]
where
\Theta(0)=0
, and for all
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{p}\in{\left[0,1\right]}
satisfying
\sum_{i=1}^{p}x_{i}\leq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{p}\left(1-\Theta(x_{i})\right)\geq 1-\Theta\left(\sum_{i=1}^{p}x_{i}\right).

(5)

We sketch how the set
\mathcal{C}
simplifies our analysis. Suppose
\nu(x)=\arcsin\sqrt{\Theta(x^{+})}
for some
\Theta\in\mathcal{C}
, where we use the notation
x^{+}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}}{{=}}\max\{x,0\}
. Then, the energy from
Lemma

1
becomes

\displaystyle A_{ij}=\prod_{k\in N(i)\setminus\{j\}}\sqrt{1-\Theta(g_{ik}^{+})}\geq\sqrt{1-\Theta\left(\sum_{k\in N(i)\setminus\{j\}}g_{ik}^{+}\right)}\,.

Through our parameterization of
\nu
, we have converted a product of trigonometric functions to a sum over
g^{+}
values on neighboring edges. We may then directly apply MoE bounds on these
g
values to lower bound
A_{ij}
.

Finally, in
Section

4
, we discuss natural limitations of our ansatz and analysis. For example, one step of our analysis considers the approximation ratio on the
worst-case
edge of a graph. We show that under this worst-case edge analysis, our choice of
\nu
is essentially optimal. To obtain better approximation ratios, each angle
\theta_{ij}
in
Algorithm

1
must depend on more than just
g_{ij}
, or the analysis must avoid reducing to the worst-case edge.

2
Semidefinite relaxation of the EPR problem

To prove the approximation ratio, we find two numbers
l(G),u(G)
that depend on the input graph, such that
u(G)\geq\lambda_{\max}(H(G))\geq\langle\psi_{G}|H(G)|\psi_{G}\rangle\geq l(G)\geq 0
. We show
\ell(G)\geq\alpha\cdot u(G)
, which in turn gives
\langle\psi_{G}|H(G)|\psi_{G}\rangle\geq\alpha\cdot\lambda_{\max}\left(H(G)\right)
, proving the approximation ratio.
For the lower bound, we use
Lemma

1
. We construct
u
by upper-bounding a relaxation of the EPR problem given by the
quantum moment-SoS hierarchy

[
15
]
, which we briefly introduce here. For more detailed descriptions, see
[
6
,
11
,
14
]
. The idea of using a semidefinite program to upper bound an objective function on graphs goes back to the work of Goemans and Williamson on MaxCut problem
[
7
]
.

2.1
Defining the relaxation

Consider the Pauli monomials on
n
qubits with at most
k
non-identity terms:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}}{{=}}\Big\{\sigma_{i_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\sigma_{i_{t}}^{\alpha_{t}}\,\big|\,t\leq k,\,\alpha_{j}\in\{X,Y,Z\},\,1\leq i_{1}<\dots<i_{t}\leq n\Big\}\,,

(6)

as well as their span with respect to real coefficients:

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}}{{=}}\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\Big\{\mathcal{P}_{k}\Big\}\,.

The
k
th level of the quantum moment-SoS hierarchy is defined with respect to
\mathcal{M}_{k}
, which is the set of real symmetric
moment matrices
,
\Gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{P}_{k}\times\mathcal{P}_{k}}
(the notation
\mathbb{R}^{S}
refers to a real vector indexed by elements of set
S
) satisfying:

\displaystyle\Gamma\succeq 0,

(7)

\displaystyle\Gamma(A,B)=\Gamma(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})

\displaystyle\forall\,A,B,A^{\prime},B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}:AB=A^{\prime}B^{\prime},

(8)

\displaystyle\Gamma(A,B)=-\Gamma(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})

\displaystyle\forall\,A,B,A^{\prime},B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}:AB=-A^{\prime}B^{\prime},

(9)

\displaystyle\Gamma(A,B)=0

\displaystyle\forall\,A,B\in\mathcal{P}_{k}:AB\text{ not Hermitian},

\displaystyle\Gamma(A,A)=1

\displaystyle\forall\,A\in\mathcal{P}_{k}.

For convenience, we define the real linear functional

L:\mathcal{O}_{2k}\to\mathbb{R}
that satisfies
L(C)=\Gamma(A,B)
whenever
C=AB
with
A,B\in\mathcal{P}_{k}
. The equality constraints of
\mathcal{M}_{k}
ensure that
L(C)
is well defined, and
Eq.

7
is equivalent to
L(A^{2})\geq 0
for all
A\in\mathcal{O}_{k}
(see
[
5
, Lemma 1.44]
). The value
L(C)
is also called the
pseudo-expectation
of the operator
C\in\mathcal{O}_{2k}
.

The
k
th level of the quantum moment-SoS hierarchy is then given by the following semidefinite program (SDP)

\displaystyle\max

\displaystyle L(H(G)),

(10)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}

\displaystyle\Gamma\in\mathcal{M}_{k}.

From the output of this SDP, we obtain the values

\displaystyle g_{ij}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}}{{=}}\frac{-1+L(X_{i}X_{j})-L(Y_{i}Y_{j})+L(Z_{i}Z_{j})}{2},\quad g_{ij}^{+}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}}{{=}}\max\{g_{ij},0\}\,,

(11)

where
1+g_{ij}
is the relaxed objective value on edge
(i,j)
. It then holds that

\displaystyle u(G)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}}{{=}}\sum_{(i,j)\in E}w_{ij}(1+g_{ij}),

(12)

is an upper bound for
\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}\left(H(G)\right)
.

2.2
Monogamy of entanglement

A key element for designing approximation algorithms for the EPR problem is
monogamy of entanglement
(MoE)
[
1
,
16
,
11
]
. These statements bound the sum of energies (from either the original problem or its relaxation) on neighboring edges. Most research concerns MoE for the Quantum MaxCut (QMC) problem
[
1
,
16
,
17
]
, which is distinct from the EPR problem. Specifically, given an edge weighted graph
G=(V,E,w)
, the QMC problem is to find the maximum eigenvalue of the Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H^{\mathrm{QMC}}(G)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}}{{=}}\sum_{(i,j)\in E}w_{ij}h_{ij}^{\mathrm{QMC}}{},\text{ for }h_{ij}^{\mathrm{QMC}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{2}\left(I_{i}I_{j}-X_{i}X_{j}-Y_{i}Y_{j}-Z_{i}Z_{j}\right),

similar to
Eq.

1
. For bipartite graphs, the QMC and EPR problems are equivalent
[
11
]
. Here, we show that on bipartite graphs, the SDP relaxations of QMC and EPR are also equivalent. To show this, note that

\displaystyle q_{ij}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}}{{=}}\frac{-1-L(X_{i}X_{j})-L(Y_{i}Y_{j})-L(Z_{i}Z_{j})}{2}

(13)

is such that
1+q_{ij}
is the relaxed objective value on edge
(i,j)
for the QMC problem. That is,
q_{ij}
is the QMC analogue of
g_{ij}
, see
Eq.

11
.

Lemma 3
.

Let
E
be the edge set of a bipartite graph and let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. For
g
and
q
as in
Eq.

11
and
Eq.

13
respectively, define
g(\Gamma)
and
q(\Gamma)
as the
g
and
q
values induced by some
\Gamma\in\mathcal{M}_{k}
. We have that

\displaystyle\left\{\left\{{g}(\Gamma)_{ij}\right\}_{(i,j)\in E}\,\middle|\,\Gamma\in\mathcal{M}_{k}\right\}=\left\{\left\{{q}(\Gamma)_{ij}\right\}_{(i,j)\in E}\,\middle|\,\Gamma\in\mathcal{M}_{k}\right\}.

(14)

The proof is deferred to
Section

A.1
.
Lemma

3
allows us to take existing MoE bounds for the QMC problem and apply them to the EPR problem.
For instance,
Lemma

2
provides a new MoE bound for the quantum moment-SoS hierarchy for the EPR problem. We show the following simpler corollary of it:

Corollary 1
.

Fix any graph
G=(V,E,w)
. Then the output
\left\{g_{ij}\right\}_{(i,j)\in E}\,
in
Eq.

11
from the
2^{\text{nd}}
level of the quantum moment-SoS hierarchy
obeys the following bound for all edges
(i,j)\in E
:

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in N(i)\setminus\left\{j\right\}}g^{+}_{ik}

\displaystyle\leq Q(g_{ij}^{+})\,,

(15)

where

\displaystyle Q(x)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}}{{=}}\begin{cases}1-x,\quad&\text{ if }\;0\leq x\leq 1/2,\\
\frac{1}{2}\left(\sqrt{3(1-x^{2})}-x\right),\quad&\text{ if }\;1/2<x\leq\sqrt{3}/2,\\
0,&\text{ if }\;\sqrt{3}/2<x\leq 1\,.\end{cases}

(16)

Proof.

If
g_{ij}^{+}\leq\frac{1}{2}
,
Eq.

15
follows from
[
16
]
(see also
[
12
, Lemma 1]
) together with
Lemma

3
. Thus, we assume that
g_{ij}^{+}>\frac{1}{2}
.

Let
P
be the RHS of
Eq.

4
, i.e.
\sum_{k\in N(i)\setminus\left\{j\right\}}g_{ik}\leq P(g_{ij},d_{i})
. We use
N^{+}
to describe the subset of
N(i)\setminus\{j\}
with positive values of
g
; i.e.
N^{+}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}}{{=}}\left\{k\in N(i)\setminus\{j\}\,\middle|\,g_{ik}>0\right\}
.

•

Suppose
|N^{+}|=0
. Then all values of
g
are non-positive. Since
Q
is non-negative,

\sum_{k\in N(i)\setminus\left\{j\right\}}g_{ik}\leq\sum_{k\in N(i)\setminus\left\{j\right\}}g^{+}_{ik}=0\leq Q(g_{ij}^{+})\,.

•

Otherwise,
|N^{+}|\geq 1
. We apply
Lemma

2
to a star graph centered at
i
that is a subgraph of
G
, where
i
is adjacent to
j
and to all
k\in N^{+}
:

\sum_{k\in N(i)\setminus\left\{j\right\}}g^{+}_{ik}=\sum_{k\in N^{+}}g_{ik}\leq P(g_{ij},|N^{+}|+1)\leq\max_{d\in\mathbb{N},d\geq 2}P(g_{ij},d)\,.

Recall that we assumed
g_{ij}^{+}\geq\frac{1}{2}
. When
d\geq 2
and
x\geq\frac{1}{2}
,

\frac{\partial}{\partial d}P(x,d)=\frac{1}{2}\left(-1+\frac{d\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}{\sqrt{d^{2}-1}}\right)\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(-1+\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\cdot\frac{d}{\sqrt{d^{2}-1}}\right)\,,

which is
\leq 0
when
d\geq 2
. So in this case
\max_{d\in\mathbb{N},d\geq 2}P(g_{ij},d)=P(g_{ij},2)\leq Q(g_{ij}^{+})
.
∎

Furthermore, due to the equivalence of the optimal values of the moment-SoS relaxations for the EPR problem and Quantum MaxCut (QMC) on bipartite graphs, our bounds also apply when
g
instead refers to the SDP edge value for the moment-SoS relaxation of QMC.

Consider

\displaystyle\sin{\nu(g_{ij})}

Lemma 5

is

r_{1d}^{\prime}(g)=\frac{(1+g)\left(-\Theta_{2}^{\prime}(R(g))R^{\prime}(g)+h^{\prime}(g)/\sqrt{h(g)}\right)-2+\Theta_{2}(R(g))-2\sqrt{h(g)}}{2(1+g)^{2}}\,.

We will show
r_{1d}^{\prime}(g)<0
in the region
\frac{1}{2}\leq g\leq\beta
. The numerator has the form
(1+g)f^{\prime}(g)-f(g)
. The derivative of this has the form
(1+g)f^{\prime\prime}(g)
, i.e.

\displaystyle(1+g)\left(-\Theta_{2}^{\prime}(R(g))R^{\prime\prime}(g)+\frac{h^{\prime\prime}(g)}{\sqrt{h(g)}}-\frac{h^{\prime}(g)^{2}}{2h(g)^{3/2}}\right)\,.

(52)

We use crude bounds to show this value is not too large in the region
\frac{1}{2}\leq g\leq\beta
, and then apply Taylor’s remainder theorem. Starting with
R
and its derivatives:

\displaystyle R^{\prime}(g)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\frac{x}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}-\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle R^{\prime\prime}(g)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}(1-g^{2})^{-3/2}

So
R(g)\in[0.3,0.5]
,
|R^{\prime}(g)|\in[1,2]
,
|R^{\prime\prime}(g)|\in[1,2.5]
.
Next we bound
h
:

\displaystyle h^{\prime}(g)

\displaystyle=\Theta_{2}^{\prime}\cdot(1-\Theta_{2}(R(g))-\Theta_{2}(g)R^{\prime}(g))

\displaystyle h^{\prime\prime}(g)

\displaystyle=-\Theta_{2}^{\prime}\cdot(2\Theta_{2}^{\prime}R^{\prime}(g)+\Theta_{2}(g)R^{\prime\prime}(g))

(Recall that
\Theta_{2}^{\prime}
is a constant.) So
\Theta_{2}(g)\in[0.1,0.2]
,
\Theta_{2}(R(g))\in[0,0.2]
,
|\Theta_{2}^{\prime}|\leq 0.4
, and
h(g)\in[0.08,0.2]
. Then
|h^{\prime}(g)|\leq 0.6
, and
|h^{\prime\prime}(g)|\leq 1.5
.
Then the value in
Eq.

52
is at most

2\cdot\left(1+\frac{1.5}{\sqrt{0.08}}+\frac{0.6^{2}}{2\cdot 0.08^{3/2}}\right)\leq 30\,.

We invoke Taylor’s remainder theorem on
n(g)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}}{{=}}r^{\prime}_{1d}(g)\cdot 2(1+g)^{2}
. For any points
\frac{1}{2}\leq a,g\leq\beta
, we have
|n(a)-n(g)|\leq n^{\prime}(a)\cdot|a-g|\leq 30\cdot|a-g|
. We computationally evaluate
n
on 300 equally spaced points in the interval
[\frac{1}{2},\beta]
; by Taylor’s remainder theorem,
n
is at most
30\cdot\frac{\beta-0.5}{300}<0.1
plus the largest evaluation. All evaluations are
<-0.11
, so
n
is negative in the region. So
r^{\prime}_{1d}(g)<0
in the region.
The smallest value of
r_{1d}
thus occurs at
g=\beta
, where

r_{1d}(\beta)=\frac{2-\Theta(R(\beta))+2\sqrt{\Theta(\beta)(1-\Theta(R(\beta))}}{2(1+\beta)}=\frac{2-\gamma+2(\gamma/2+\alpha^{\prime}(1+\beta)-1)}{2(1+\beta)}=\alpha^{\prime}\,.

Case
r_{2}
:

This expression is always equal to
\alpha^{\prime}
when

\Lambda

\displaystyle\frac{2-\Theta(Q(g))+2\sqrt{\Lambda(g)(1-\Theta(Q(g)))}}{2(1+g)}=\frac{2-\Theta(Q(g))+2\cdot\left(\frac{1}{2}\Theta(Q(g))+\alpha^{\prime}(1+g)-1\right)}{2(1+g)}=\alpha^{\prime}\,.

Case

For

R(R(g))=g
when

\displaystyle\Theta\left(Q\left(R(g)^{+}\right)\right)

\Theta_{1}\left(-R(-g)\right),&g\in{\left[-\delta_{1},-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right]},\\

\displaystyle\delta_{1}

\displaystyle\delta_{2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}}{{=}}R(1-\beta)\approx 0.6525.

We likewise expand the expressions

\displaystyle Q\left(R(g)^{+}\right)-g^{+}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}-R(-g),&g\in{\left[-1,-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right]},\\
0,&g\in{\left[-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]},\\
1-R(g)-g,&g\in{\left[\frac{1}{2},\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right]},\\
1-g,&g\in{\left[\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2},1\right]}.\\

\displaystyle\Theta\left(Q\!\left(R(g)^{+}\right)-g^{+}\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\Theta_{2}\left(-R(-g)\right),&g\in{\left[-1,-\delta_{1}\right]},\\
\Theta_{1}\left(-R(-g))\right),&g\in{\left[-\delta_{1},-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right]},\\
0,&g\in{\left[-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]},\\
\Theta_{1}\left(1-R(g)-g\right),&g\in{\left[\frac{1}{2},\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right]},\\
\Theta_{1}\left(1-g\right),&g\in{\left[\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2},1\right]}.\end{cases}

Now consider
\Lambda

. In Case

53

53

53
. Thus, the pieces of

, We refer to these regions as

,

\displaystyle f^{*}(g)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}f^{*}_{a}(g),&g\in{\left[-1,-\delta_{1}\right]}\,,\\
f^{*}_{b}(g),&g\in{\left[-\delta_{1},-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right]}\,,\\
f^{*}_{c}(g),&g\in{\left[-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2},0\right]}\,,\\

\displaystyle f^{*}_{a}(g)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}}{{=}}\sqrt{\left(1-\Theta_{2}\left(-R(-g)\right)\right)-\left(\frac{1}{2}\cdot\Theta_{2}(-R(-g))+\alpha^{\prime}(1+R(g))-1\right)^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle f^{*}_{b}(g)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}}{{=}}\sqrt{\left(1-\Theta_{1}\left(-R(-g)\right)\right)-\left(\frac{1}{2}\cdot\Theta_{1}(-R(-g))+\alpha^{\prime}(1+R(g))-1\right)^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle f^{*}_{c}(g)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}}{{=}}\sqrt{1-\left(\alpha^{\prime}(1+R(g))-1\right)^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle f^{*}_{d}(g)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}}{{=}}\sqrt{1-\frac{\left(\frac{1}{2}\cdot\Theta_{1}(g)+\alpha^{\prime}(1+R(g))-1\right)^{2}}{1-\Theta_{1}(g)}}\,.

\displaystyle r_{3a}(g)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}}{{=}}\frac{1+f_{a}^{*}(g)^{2}}{2(1+g)},

\displaystyle r_{3b}(g)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}}{{=}}\frac{1+f_{b}^{*}(g)^{2}}{2(1+g)},

\displaystyle r_{3c}(g)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}}{{=}}\frac{1+f_{c}^{*}(g)^{2}}{2(1+g)},

\displaystyle r_{3d}(g)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}}{{=}}\frac{1+f_{d}^{*}(g)^{2}+2\sqrt{\Theta_{1}(g)}f_{d}^{*}(g)}{2(1+g)}.

and

(54)

.

We now handle

\alpha^{\prime}
in the region. The derivative is

r_{3c}^{\prime}(g)=\frac{1}{2(1+g)^{2}}\cdot\left(-2\alpha^{\prime}R^{\prime}(g)(1+g)(\alpha^{\prime}(1+R(g))-1)-2+(\alpha^{\prime}(1+R(g))-1)^{2}\right),

Since

(\alpha^{\prime}(1+R(g))-1)^{2}\leq(2\alpha^{\prime}-1)^{2}<0.5
. So the derivative is negative if

\displaystyle R^{\prime}(g)=-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\sqrt{3}g}{2\sqrt{1-g^{2}}},

{\left[0,\delta\right]}

\displaystyle f_{d}^{*\,\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2f_{d}^{*}}\cdot\frac{-1}{(1-\Theta_{1}(g))^{2}}\cdot

\displaystyle+\Theta_{1}^{\prime}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{2}\cdot\Theta_{1}(g)+\alpha^{\prime}(1+R(g))-1\right)^{2}\Big].

, and

\displaystyle(1+g)\cdot\left(2f_{d}^{*\,\prime}(g)\Big(f_{d}^{*}(g)+\sqrt{\Theta_{1}(g)}\Big)+\frac{\Theta_{1}^{\prime}(g)}{\sqrt{\Theta_{1}(g)}}\,f_{d}^{*}(g)\right)-\left(1+f_{d}^{*}(g)^{2}+2\sqrt{\Theta_{1}(g)}f_{d}^{*}(g)\right)\,.

Recall that
\Theta_{1}(g)<0.16g
. So when

r_{3d}
is increasing. So
r_{3d}(g)>r_{3d}(0)>\alpha^{\prime}
for all

When

takes value at most

When

,
\Theta_{1}(g)>0.003
, and so

. So, for any points

. All evaluations are

Two-edge-connected (not necessarily spanning) subgraphs and polyhedra
Justus Bruckamp, Markus Chimani, Martina Juhnke

Abstract
. Given a graph G G , we study the 2 2 -edge-connected subgraph polytope TECSP ⁡ ( G ) \mathrm{TECSP}(G) , which is given by the convex hull of the incidence vectors of all 2 2 -edge-connected subgraphs of G G . We describe the lattice points of this polytope by linear inequalities which provides an ILP-algorithm for finding a 2 2 -edge-connected subgraph of maximum weight. Furthermore, we characterize when these inequalities define facets of TECSP ⁡ ( G ) \mathrm{TECSP}(G) . We also consider further types of supporting hyperplanes of TECSP ⁡ ( G ) \mathrm{TECSP}(G) and study when they are facet-defining. Finally, we investigate the efficiency of our considered inequalities practically on some classes of graphs.



1.
Introduction

In network planning, it is a typical problem to ask for a weight-optimal subgraph
H
of a given weighted graph
G
, where
H
should have some required properties. Often, one wants to find a network that can withstand the loss of connections, e.g.,
H
should be
2
-edge- or
2
-vertex-, or even stronger connected. In this article we focus on the
2
-edge-connected case, i.e., we want to identify a weight-optimal
2
-edge-connected subgraph of a given weighted graph
G
. Let
V(G)
and
E(G)
denote the vertices and edges of
G
, respectively.
G
is
2
-edge-connected
, if for every pair of distinct vertices
u,v\in V(G)
, there exist at least two edge-disjoint paths connecting
u
and
v
. We consider the
2-edge-connected subgraph polytope

\mathrm{TECSP}(G)
, which is given by the convex hull of all incidence vectors of
2
-edge-connected subgraphs of
G
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathrm{TECSP}(G)\coloneqq\mathop{\rm conv}\nolimits\{\chi^{H}\mid H\subseteq G\text{ is $2$-edge-connected}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{E(G)},

where
\chi^{H}\in\{0,1\}^{E(G)}
is defined by

\displaystyle\chi_{e}^{H}\coloneqq\begin{cases}1,\text{ if }e\in E(H),\\
0,\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

We will describe all lattice points of this polytope by linear inequalities, which provides an integer linear program (ILP) for this problem. The polytope is related to other previously studied polytopes from graph optimization problems and we also provide new ILPs for such problems.

In
[
Mah94
,
BM95
]
, the problem of finding a
spanning

2
-edge-connected subgraph has been considered by studying the corresponding polytope. In both articles, several supporting inequalities have been studied. Furthermore, the polytope is completely described for series-parallel graphs in
[
Mah94
]
, and for Halin graphs in
[
BM95
]
. Given some vertex subset
R\subseteq V(G)
,
[
BM97
]
and
[
MP08
]
consider the more general polytope of subgraphs
H\subseteq G
that allow a
2
-edge-connected subgraph
H^{\prime}\subseteq H
with
R\subseteq V(H^{\prime})
. Observe that
H
itself does not even need to be connected. Again, the polytope is completely described for series-parallel graphs in
[
BM97
]
and for a generalization of Halin graphs in
[
MP08
]
. Since we will describe all lattice points of
\mathrm{TECSP}(G)
, we can directly describe such
2
-edge-connected subgraphs
H^{\prime}
by intersecting
\mathrm{TECSP}(G)
with the inequalities
\sum_{e\in\delta(\{v\})}x_{e}\geq 2
for
v\in R
, where
\delta(\{v\})
denotes the edges incident to
v
.

In
[
DKN15
]
, the polytope of connected subgraphs of a given graph
G
is considered. The authors study supporting hyperplanes and completely describe the polytope if
G
is a tree or a cycle. Therefore, studying
2
-edge-connected subgraphs and the related polytope is a natural follow-up question.

Another related problem is the
k
-Cardinality Subgraph Problem
, which asks for a weight-optimal connected subgraph that has exactly
k
edges. In
[
Ehr95
]
, the related polytope is studied. Supporting hyperplanes of the polytope are studied and two different algorithms for this problem are compared. As for the connected subgraph problem, a natural follow-up question is to study the problem of finding
k
-cardinality
2
-edge-connected subgraphs: this can be seen as the intersection of
\mathrm{TECSP}(G)
with the equality
\sum_{e\in E(G)}x_{e}=k
.

In addition, there are also connections to the problem of finding a weight-optimal
Eulerian
subgraph, i.e., a connected subgraph in which each vertex has even degree. Since every Eulerian subgraph is
2
-edge-connected, the corresponding Eulerian subgraph polytope is contained in
\mathrm{TECSP}(G)
. In literature, the Eulerian subgraph polytope often appears as a special case of the cycle polytope of a matroid, if the underlying matroid is graphic, see for example
[
BG86
]
. But for the cycle polytope of a graphic matroid, all (possibly disconnected!) subgraphs are considered as long as every vertex has even degree. Therefore, this is not the actual Eulerian subgraph polytope, since also disconnected subgraphs are allowed. In
[
BG86
]
, a complete facet description is provided in the case that the underlying matroid is graphic. By this, we get an ILP for the problem of finding a weight-optimal (connected) Eulerian subgraph by combining the ILP of the corresponding cycle polytope for a graphic matroid with the ILP we provide for finding a weight-optimal
2
-edge-connected subgraph.

Furthermore, the
2
-edge-connected subgraph polytope has been studied in
[
BCJ24
]
in the special case that the underlying graph is a cactus graph, i.e., a graph where any two cycles intersect in at most one vertex. For this case, a complete facet description of the polytope is provided.

We close this section by specifically discussing bridges, i.e., edges whose deletion increase the number of connected components. Clearly, they are never contained in a
2
-edge-connected subgraph of
G
. One can delete all bridges in
G
without changing the set of
2
-edge-connected subgraphs. Whenever
G
contains several connected components, one can solve the problem of finding a weight-optimal
2
-edge-connected subgraph on each component independently and choose the overall best one. Therefore, we may safely assume that the underlying graph
G
is itself
2
-edge-connected most of the time.

\mathrm{TECSP}(G)
, and characterize when they define facets. Furthermore, in

Preliminaries

For

, let
[n]\coloneqq\{1,\ldots,n\}
and for a set

denote the power set of

,
\mathrm{aff}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})

Let

be a simple (i.e., no multi-edges) undirected graph with vertex set

and edge set
E(G)\subseteq\binom{V(G)}{2}
. For a subset of the vertices

, let
G[S]\coloneqq(S,E(G)\cap\binom{S}{2})
denote the graph induced by

.

Let
F\subseteq E(G)
be a subset of the edges of
G
. We define the
incidence vector

\chi^{F}\in\{0,1\}^{E(G)}
of

by

(1)

\displaystyle\chi_{e}^{F}\coloneqq\begin{cases}1,\text{ if }e\in F,\\
0,\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

For a subgraph
H\subseteq G
, we use the shorthand
\chi^{H}\coloneqq\chi^{E(H)}
.
For a vector
x\in\mathbb{R}^{E(G)}
we will use the notation
x(F)\coloneqq\sum_{e\in F}x_{e}
for
F\subseteq E(G)
. Furthermore, we denote the
support of

by
\supp(x)\coloneqq\{e\in E(G)\mid x_{e}\neq 0\}
.
For
F\subseteq E(G)
, let
G-F\coloneqq(V(G),E(G)\setminus F)
be the graph after removing

. For
e\in E
we may write
G-e
instead of
G-\{e\}
. Similar, for
S\subseteq V(G)
we denote by
G-S
the graph with vertex set
V(G)\setminus S
and edge set
E(G)\cap\binom{V(G)\setminus S}{2}
. For a single vertex
v\in V(G)
we will also write
G-v
instead of
G-\{v\}
.
For
\emptyset\subsetneq S\subsetneq V(G)
, let
\delta(S)
denote all edges that have exactly one vertex in

, i.e.,

\displaystyle\delta(S)\coloneqq\{e\in E(G)\mid|e\cap S|=1\}.

The set
\delta(S)
is also called a
cut
, since it separates the vertices of
S
from the vertices of
V(G)\setminus S
, and therefore, the graph
G-\delta(S)
is disconnected. If
|\delta(S)|=k
, we say, that
\delta(S)
is a
cut of size
k
or shorter
k
-cut
. If
\delta(S)=\{e\}
for some
\emptyset\subsetneq S\subsetneq V(G)
, we say that
e
is a
bridge
. It is well-known that if
F_{1},F_{2}\subseteq E(G)
are cuts, then also their symmetric difference
F_{1}\triangle F_{2}\coloneqq(F_{1}\setminus F_{2})\cup(F_{2}\setminus F_{1})
is a cut. We say that a cut
F\subseteq E(G)
has a
chord

e\in E(G)
, if there are two cuts
F_{1},F_{2}\subseteq E(G)
, such that
F_{1}\triangle F_{2}=F
and
F_{1}\cap F_{2}=\{e\}
. Furthermore, it is well-known that the intersection of a cut and the edge-set of a cycle has always even cardinality.

Definition 2.1
.

A graph
G
is
2
-edge-connected
, if and only if one of the following equivalent conditions is true:

(i)

for every pair of distinct vertices
u,v\in V(G)
, there exist at least two edge-disjoint paths connecting
u
and
v
;

(ii)

G
is connected and does not contain any bridges.

We now define the central object of this paper. For a simple undirected graph
G
, the
2
-edge-connected subgraph polytope

\mathrm{TECSP}(G)
is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{TECSP}(G)=\mathop{\rm conv}\nolimits\{\chi^{H}\mid H\subseteq G\text{ is $2$-edge-connected}\}.

Since the empty graph, and a graph with only one vertex and no edge, is
2
-edge-connected by definition, the all-zeros vector
0
is always contained in
\mathrm{TECSP}(G)
.

2.1.
Coparallel classes

In
[
BG86
]
, the
cycle polytope

\mathrm{Cyc}(\mathcal{M})
of a binary matroid
\mathcal{M}
has been studied, and in particular,
coparallel elements
of a matroid
\mathcal{M}
play an important role in this study. For the
2
-edge-connected subgraph polytope
\mathrm{TECSP}(G)
of a graph
G
, this will also be the case, where we talk about coparallel elements in the corresponding graphic matroid. Since we only need this special case in this article, we will only define it for graphs.

Two distinct edges
e,f\in E(G)
are
coparallel
if and only if they form a minimal
2
-cut of
G
. A
coparallel class
of
G
is an inclusion-wise maximal subset
C\subseteq E(G)
, such that every pair of distinct elements of
C
is coparallel. Note that if an element
e\in E(G)
is neither a bridge, nor contained in a
2
-cut, then
\{e\}
forms a coparallel class by itself. If
e\in E(G)
is a bridge, we do not consider
\{e\}
to be a coparallel class. Since coparallelism defines a transitive relation, the
set of coparallel classes of
G
, denoted by
\mathrm{CP}(G)
, is a partition of the edge set
E(G)
, as long as
G
does not contain any bridges, which is especially true if
G
is
2
-edge-connected.

We want to point out some easy but helpful properties of coparallel classes. Let

be a coparallel class of a
2
-edge-connected graph

of size

, and see

,

is a

-edge-connected graph, since otherwise

would be a bridge or contained in a

-cut. For

, we have that
G-\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
has two connected components

and
G_{2}
, since

is a minimal
2
-cut. Again,

and
G_{2}
are
2
-edge-connected, since otherwise

would contain at least three elements. If

has two connected components. Now, since also

Let

Next, we relate coparallel classes to

-edge-connected subgraphs. Let

-edge-connected subgraph of a graph

Observation 2.3

Let

2

By

Observation 2.5

Let

Theorem 2.6
.

For a graph

\displaystyle\dim(\mathrm{TECSP}(G))=|\mathrm{CP}(G)|.

Proof.

Suppose that

3.
Facets

We study different types of supporting hyperplanes for the
2
-edge-connected subgraph polytope
\mathrm{TECSP}(G)
and when they define facets. In the following we will always assume that the underlying graph
G
is
2
-edge-connected as already justified in the introduction.

3.1.
The box inequalities

We characterize when the box inequalities
0\leq x_{e}\leq 1
define facets of
\mathrm{TECSP}(G)
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
G
be a
2
-edge-connected graph,
C\in\mathrm{CP}(G)
and
e\in C
. Then the inequality

\displaystyle x_{e}\geq 0

defines a facet of
\mathrm{TECSP}(G)
if and only if
e
is not contained in a
3
-cut.

Proof.

Suppose that
e
is contained in a
3
-cut
\delta(S)=\{e,e_{1},e_{2}\}
for some
\emptyset\subsetneq S\subsetneq V(G)
. We consider the inequalities

\displaystyle x_{e_{1}}-x_{e_{2}}-x_{e}\leq 0

and

\displaystyle x_{e_{2}}-x_{e_{1}}-x_{e}\leq 0.

These are both supporting hyperplanes of
\mathrm{TECSP}(G)
since
2
-edge-connected subgraphs
H
of
G
cannot contain exactly one edge of a cut, as otherwise
H
would contain a bridge. In fact, they are special cases of the so-called
asymmetric cut inequalities
that will be studied in
Section

3.2
. These two inequalities define different proper faces of
\mathrm{TECSP}(G)
, since e.g.
\chi^{G}
is not contained in the corresponding face, and they already imply
x_{e}\geq 0
. Hence, the latter does not define a facet.

Suppose now that
e
is not contained in a
3
-cut and let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathrm{TECSP}(G)
denote the face defined by
x_{e}=0
. By an analogous argument as in
[
BG86
]
, we get the result as follows. Let
C\in\mathrm{CP}(G)
be the coparallel class that contains
e
.
\mathcal{F}
is affinely isomorphic to the
2
-edge-connected subgraph polytope
\mathrm{TECSP}(G-C)
of the graph
G-C
. Since
e
is not contained in a
3
-cut, the same holds for any
e^{\prime}\in C
, and therefore, two edges
f_{1},f_{2}\in E(G)\setminus C
are coparallel in
G
if and only if they are coparallel in
G-C
. This implies
|\mathrm{CP}(G-C)|=|\mathrm{CP}(G)|-1
and the claim follows by
Theorem

2.6
.

∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
G
be a
2
-edge-connected graph and
e\in E(G)
. Then the inequality

\displaystyle x_{e}\leq 1

defines a facet of
\mathrm{TECSP}(G)
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathrm{TECSP}(G)
denote the face defined by
x_{e}=1
. Let
\mathrm{CP}(G)=\{C_{e},C_{1},\ldots,C_{\ell}\}
, where
C_{e}
is the coparallel class containing
e
. For
j\in[\ell]
, we denote by
G_{j}^{e}
the connected component of the graph
G-C_{j}
containing
e
. Since
G_{j}^{e}
is
2
-edge-connected (by
Observation

2.2
),
\chi^{G}
and
\chi^{G_{j}^{e}}
, for
j=1,\ldots,\ell
, are vertices of
\mathcal{F}
. We now show that these
|\mathrm{CP}(G)|
many vectors are affinely independent, which by
Theorem

2.6
implies that
\mathcal{F}
is a facet. For this aim, we define a partial order on the coparallel classes
C_{1},\ldots,C_{\ell}
as follows: for
i,j\in[\ell],i\neq j
, let

\displaystyle C_{i}<_{e}C_{j}\Longleftrightarrow C_{i}\nsubseteq E(G_{j}^{e}).

To show that
<_{e}
defines a partial order, we show the following claim.

\displaystyle C_{i}<_{e}C_{j}\Longrightarrow G_{j}^{e}\subseteq G_{i}^{e}\text{ and }C_{j}\subseteq E(G_{i}^{e}),

which can be seen as follows. Suppose
C_{i}<_{e}C_{j}
. Then by definition
C_{i}\nsubseteq E(G_{j}^{e})
. Due to
Observation

2.3
, this already means that
C_{i}\cap E(G_{j}^{e})=\emptyset
. Hence,
G-C_{i}
contains all edges of
G_{j}^{e}
which implies that
G_{j}^{e}\subseteq G_{i}^{e}
. Since at least one edge
f
of
C_{j}
is incident to an edge of
G_{j}^{e}
and since
f
is contained in
G-C_{i}
it follows that
f\in E(G_{i}^{e})
. By
Observation

2.3
, we conclude that
C_{j}\subseteq E(G_{i}^{e})
.

Using this claim we can easily verify that
<_{e}
defines a partial order on
C_{1},\ldots,C_{\ell}
: If
C_{i}<_{e}C_{j}
, then
C_{j}\subseteq E(G_{i}^{e})
and hence,
C_{j}\nless_{e}C_{i}
. This shows that
<_{e}
is antisymmetric. Suppose now that
C_{i}<_{e}C_{j}
and
C_{j}<_{e}C_{k}
. From the first relation we have
C_{i}\nsubseteq E(G_{j}^{e})
by definition and from the second relation we have
G_{k}^{e}\subseteq G_{j}^{e}
by the claim above. Hence,
C_{i}\nsubseteq E(G_{k}^{e})
which implies
C_{i}<_{e}C_{k}
and in particular,
<_{e}
is transitive.

The vectors
\chi^{G_{j}^{e}}
satisfy

\displaystyle\chi_{f}^{G_{j}^{e}}=\begin{cases}1,&\text{ for all }f\in C_{e},\\
0,&\text{ for all }C_{i}\leq_{e}C_{j}\text{ and all }f\in C_{i},\text{ and}\\
1,&\text{ for all }C_{i}\nleq_{e}C_{j}\text{ and all }f\in C_{i},\end{cases}

for all
j\in[\ell]
.

Let
\pi\colon\mathrm{TECSP}(G)\to\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
be the map defined by
\pi(x)_{i}\coloneqq x_{f}
, for any
f\in C_{i}
, for all
i\in[\ell]
. By
Observation

2.5
, this is well-defined. Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle\dim(\mathrm{aff}(\chi^{G},\chi^{G_{1}^{e}},\ldots,\chi^{G_{\ell}^{e}}))

\displaystyle=\dim(\mathrm{aff}(\pi(\chi^{G}),\pi(\chi^{G_{1}^{e}}),\ldots,\pi(\chi^{G_{\ell}^{e}})))

\displaystyle=\dim(\mathrm{span}(\pi(\chi^{G})-\pi(\chi^{G_{1}^{e}}),\ldots,\pi(\chi^{G})-\pi(\chi^{G_{\ell}^{e}}))).

Without loss of generality we can assume that our indices satisfy
i\leq j
if
C_{i}\leq C_{j}
. Writing the vectors
\pi(\chi^{G})-\pi(\chi^{G_{1}^{e}}),\ldots,\pi(\chi^{G})-\pi(\chi^{G_{\ell}^{e}})
row-wise in a matrix, yields a lower triangular
\ell\times\ell
-matrix that has ones in the diagonal. Therefore, the matrix has rank
\ell
, which finishes the proof.
∎

3.2.
The asymmetric cut inequalities

In the following we will focus on inequalities that are defined by cuts. Let
H\subseteq G
both be
2
-edge-connected and let
\delta(S)
be a cut for some
\emptyset\subsetneq S\subsetneq V(G)
. It is not possible that
E(H)
contains only one edge
e\in\delta(S)
because otherwise
H
has a bridge and is not
2
-edge-connected. Therefore, for the vertex
\chi^{H}
of
\mathrm{TECSP}(G)
the inequality

\displaystyle\chi_{e}^{H}-\chi^{H}(\delta(S)\setminus\{e\})\leq 0

holds. This shows that for
\emptyset\subsetneq S\subsetneq V(G)
and
e\in\delta(S)
the inequality

(2)

\displaystyle x_{e}-x(\delta(S)\setminus\{e\})\leq 0

defines a supporting hyperplane of
\mathrm{TECSP}(G)
. An inequality as in (
2
) will be called an
asymmetric cut inequality
. In the following we study, when this inequality defines a facet. We can assume that
|\delta(S)|\geq 3
:
|\delta(S)|=1
is not possible since we only consider
2
-edge-connected graphs; suppose that
|\delta(S)|=2
. Then every
2
-edge-connected subgraph of
G
contains either both edges of
\delta(S)
or none of them. Hence, every vertex of
\mathrm{TECSP}(G)
is contained in the face defined by the corresponding asymmetric cut inequality. This already shows

Observation 3.3
.

Let
G
be
2
-edge-connected and
\emptyset\subsetneq S\subsetneq V
such that
|\delta(S)|=2
. Then the face
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathrm{TECSP}(G)
defined by the asymmetric cut inequality

\displaystyle x_{e}-x(\delta(S)\setminus\{e\})\leq 0

is given by
\mathcal{F}=\mathrm{TECSP}(G)
. In particular, the asymmetric cut inequality does not define a facet of
\mathrm{TECSP}(G)
in this case.

Figure 3
.
Example for an asymmetric cut inequality with a non-minimal cut
\delta(S)\supsetneq\delta(S_{1})
, where
S=S_{1}\dot{\cup}S_{2}
.

Furthermore we can assume that the set
\delta(S)
is a minimal cut set in the sense that there is no set
F\subsetneq\delta(S)
that is also a cut for the following reason. Suppose such an
F
exists. We can assume w.l.o.g. that
e\in F
since if
F
is a cut, so is
\delta(S)\triangle F=\delta(S)\setminus F
and one of these two sets contains
e
. But then the inequality

(3)

\displaystyle x_{e}-x(F\setminus\{e\})\leq 0

is also an asymmetric cut inequality and every incidence vector
\chi^{H}
of a
2
-edge-connected subgraph
H\subseteq G
that belongs to the face defined by (
2
) also belongs to the face defined by (
3
). This means that inequality (
3
) dominates inequality (
2
) and we can study the latter instead. An example for such a behavior can be seen in
Figure

3
, where
S=S_{1}\dot{\cup}S_{2}
,
\delta(S_{1})\subsetneq\delta(S)
.

From now on, we hence assume that
|\delta(S)|\geq 3
and
\delta(S)
is minimal, and the latter is equivalent to
G[S]
and
G[V(G)\setminus S]
being connected. The next theorem characterizes when asymmetric cut inequalities define facets in this setting.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
G
be
2
-edge-connected and
\emptyset\subsetneq S\subsetneq V(G)
such that
\delta(S)
is a minimal cut set with
|\delta(S)|\geq 3
. For
e\in\delta(S)
the asymmetric cut inequality

\displaystyle x_{e}-x(\delta(S)\backslash e)\leq 0

defines a facet of
\mathrm{TECSP}(G)
if and only if every bridge in
G[S]
and
G[V(G)\backslash S]
lies in a coparallel class together with an edge of
\delta(S)
.

Note that the condition in the theorem above is equivalent to the property that if
\delta(S)
has a chord, i.e., there exist cuts
F_{1},F_{2}\subseteq E(G)
, such that
F_{1}\triangle F_{2}=\delta(S)
and
F_{1}\cap F_{2}=\{h\}
for an
h\in E(G)
, then
F_{1}
or
F_{2}
has cardinality
2
, see below.

Proof.

Suppose first that
G[S]
contains a bridge
f\in E(G[S])
that is not contained in a coparallel class with any edge of
\delta(S)
. Since
\delta(S)
is a minimal cut set, we know that
G[S]
and
G[V(G)\setminus S]
are connected. Then the graph
G[S]-f
has two connected components
G_{1},G_{2}
. For
i=1,2
, let
E_{i}
be the edges of
\delta(S)
incident to
G_{i}
. Since
f
is not in a
2
-cut with any edge of
\delta(S)
, we have
|E_{1}|,|E_{2}|\geq 2
. Moreover, we have
\delta(V(G_{1}))=E_{1}\cup\{f\}
and
\delta(V(G_{2}))=E_{2}\cup\{f\}
and without loss of generality we can assume that
e\in E_{1}
. An example for the described situation is shown in
Figure

4
. Then we have that

\displaystyle x_{e}-x(\delta(S)\setminus\{e\})\leq 0

is the sum of the two asymmetric cut inequalities

\displaystyle x_{e}-x(\delta(V(G_{1}))\setminus\{e\})\leq 0,

\displaystyle x_{f}-x(\delta(V(G_{2}))\setminus\{f\})\leq 0.

Since
|E_{1}|,|E_{2}|\geq 2
, both of the two latter asymmetric cut inequalities define proper faces of
\mathrm{TECSP}(G)
and it is clear that these two faces are different. This implies that the first asymmetric cut inequality is not a facet.

We now show that in all other cases, the asymmetric cut inequality defines a facet of
\mathrm{TECSP}(G)
. The asymmetric cut inequality is actually a special case of inequality
(d)
in
Theorem

2.8
and therefore defines a facet of
\mathrm{ESP}(G)
due to the remark directly below
Theorem

3.4
. Let
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{ESP}(G)}
and
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{TECSP}(G)}
denote the face defined by the asymmetric cut inequality of
\mathrm{ESP}(G)
and of
\mathrm{TECSP}(G)
, respectively. We will show that
\mathrm{aff}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{ESP}(G)})\subseteq\mathrm{aff}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{TECSP}(G)})
, which implies that
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{TECSP}(G)}
is a facet of
\mathrm{TECSP}(G)
, since
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{ESP}(G)}
is a facet of
\mathrm{ESP}(G)
, and both polytopes have the same dimension due to
[
BG86
, Theorem 4.1]
and
Theorem

2.6
. Let
H\subseteq G
be a subgraph of
G
such that
\chi^{H}
is a vertex of
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{ESP}(G)}
. This implies that
H
either contains no edges of
\delta(S)
or
H
contains the edge
e\in\delta(S)
and exactly one other edge
f\in\delta(S)\setminus\{e\}
. Since the connected components of
H
are Eulerian,
H
is the edge-disjoint union of cycles
C_{1},\ldots,C_{k}\subseteq G
. If
E(H)\cap\delta(S)=\emptyset
, it is clear that
\chi^{C_{i}}\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{TECSP}(G)}
for all
i\in[k]
. If
e\in E(H)
, there is exactly one cycle
C_{j}
that contains
e
for some
j\in[k]
. Then
C_{j}
also contains the edge
f
, because
E(C_{j})\cap\delta(S)
has even cardinality, and again we have
\chi^{C_{i}}\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{TECSP}(G)}
for all
i\in[k]
, since all other cycles do not contain any edge of
\delta(S)
. Since the all-zeros vector
\mathbf{0}
is contained in
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{TECSP}(G)}
,
\mathrm{aff}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{TECSP}(G)})
is a linear subspace and also
\chi^{H}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\chi^{C_{i}}\in\mathrm{aff}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{TECSP}(G)})
.

∎

containing a bridge
f
that is not contained in a coparallel class with an edge of
\delta(S)
.

3.3.
The connectivity cut inequalities

We will now consider what we call the
connectivity cut inequalities
and provide some motivation for these. So far, the asymmetric cut inequalities ensure that for a
2
-edge-connected graph
G
, no incidence vector of a subgraph
H\subseteq G
that contains a bridge is feasible. However, a subgraph
H\subseteq G
might not be
2
-edge-connected simply because it is not connected. As an example, consider the graph
G
in
Figure

5
. The point
y\in\mathbb{R}^{E(G)}
with
y_{f_{1}}=y_{f_{2}}=0
and
y_{e}=1
for all
e\in E(G)\setminus\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
satisfies all asymmetric cut inequalities since the subgraph
H\subseteq G
with vertex set
V(H)=V(G)
and edge set
E(H)=\mathrm{supp}(y)
does not contain a bridge. However,
y
does not lie in
\mathrm{TECSP}(G)
: Let
S\subseteq V(G)
be such that
\delta(S)=\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
. If a
2
-edge-connected subgraph
H\subseteq G
contains an edge
e_{1}\in E(G[S])
and an edge
e_{2}\in E(G[V(G)\setminus S])
, then
H
has to contain at least two edges from the cut
\delta(S)
in order to be
2
-edge-connected. This implies that the inequality

\displaystyle 2x_{e_{1}}-x_{f_{1}}-x_{f_{2}}+2x_{e_{2}}\leq 2

defines a supporting hyperplane for
\mathrm{TECSP}(G)
and cuts off the otherwise feasible point
y
. More generally, given a
2
-edge-connected graph
G
, and a set
\emptyset\subsetneq S\subsetneq V(G)
with
{E(G[S])\neq\emptyset}
, and
E(G[V(G)\setminus S])\neq\emptyset
,
e_{1}\in E(G[S])
and
e_{2}\in E(G[V(G)\setminus S])
, the inequality

(4)

\displaystyle 2x_{e_{1}}-x(\delta(S))+2x_{e_{2}}\leq 2

defines a supporting hyperplane of
\mathrm{TECSP}(G)
by the arguments above. An inequality as in (
4
) will be called a
connectivity cut inequality
, since these inequalities ensure that only incidence vectors of connected subgraphs are feasible as we will see in
Theorem

3.6
. Before that, we study when such inequalities define facets.

Figure 5
.
The graph used in the initial motivating example for connectivity cut inequalitites.

Without loss of generality, we assume that the cut
\delta(S)
is an inclusion-wise minimal cut. We will show that otherwise there exists a
\emptyset\subsetneq S^{\prime}\subsetneq V(G)
such that
\delta(S^{\prime})\subsetneq\delta(S)
, and
e_{1}\in G[S^{\prime}]
and
e_{2}\in G[V(G)\setminus S^{\prime}]
. If such
S^{\prime}
exists, the connectivity cut inequality

\displaystyle 2x_{e_{1}}-\delta(S^{\prime})+2x_{e_{2}}\leq 2

dominates the original connectivity cut inequality (
4
), and we can study the latter instead. Suppose
\delta(S)
is not inclusion-wise minimal, which means that the graph
G-\delta(S)
has at least three connected components
G_{1},G_{2}
, and
G_{3}
. After possibly renaming, we may assume
e_{1}\in E(G_{1})
, and
e_{2}\in E(G_{2})
. We claim that
\delta(V(G_{1}))\subsetneq\delta(S)
or
\delta(V(G_{2}))\subsetneq\delta(S)
. Assume the contrary, namely
\delta(V(G_{1}))=\delta(S)=\delta(V(G_{2}))
. But this implies that each edge
f\in\delta(S)
contains exactly one vertex of
V(G_{1})
and one vertex of
V(G_{2})
, which means that there are no edges in
G
that contain exactly one vertex of
V(G_{3})
. This is not possible, since we only consider connected graphs
G
. Hence, we can choose
S^{\prime}=V(G_{1})
or
S^{\prime}=V(G)\setminus V(G_{2})
, depending on which of
\delta(V(G_{1}))
and
\delta(V(G_{2}))=\delta(V(G)\setminus V(G_{2}))
is a proper subset of
\delta(S)
(it may happen that both choices are valid). By construction, it is also clear that
e_{1}\in G[S^{\prime}]
and
e_{2}\in G[V(G)\setminus S^{\prime}]
, and hence we find a dominating connectivity cut inequality, whenever
\delta(S)
is not an inclusion-wise minimal cut.

We need to introduce further notation. With the notation above and assuming
\delta(S)
is inclusion-wise minimal, for
W\in\{S,V(G)\setminus S\}
and a bridge
f\notin\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
of
G[W]
, let
\delta(W,f)\subseteq\delta(S)
denote the set of edges of
\delta(S)
that have one end in the connected component of
G[W]-f
containing neither
e_{1}
nor
e_{2}
. With this we can provide the desired characterization.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
G
be a
2
-edge-connected graph with vertex set
V
, let
\emptyset\subsetneq S\subsetneq V
, such that
\delta(S)
is an inclusion-wise minimal cut with
e_{1}\in E(G[S])
and
e_{2}\in E(G[V\backslash S])
. Then the connectivity cut inequality

(5)

\displaystyle 2x_{e_{1}}-x(\delta(S))+2x_{e_{2}}\leq 2

defines a facet of
\mathrm{TECSP}(G)
if and only if one of the following two conditions holds:

(i)

e_{1}
or
e_{2}
is a bridge in
G[S]
or
G[V\setminus S]
, and
|\delta(S)|=2
;

(ii)

neither
e_{1}
nor
e_{2}
is a bridge in
G[S]
resp.
G[V\setminus S]
, and if there is a bridge
f
in
G[W]
for
W\in\{S,V\setminus S\}
, then
|\delta(W,f)|=1
.

We remark that in case
(i)
, the considered connectivity cut inequality is equivalent to the box inequality
x_{e_{2}}\leq 1
or
x_{e_{1}}\leq 1
depending on whether
e_{1}
is a bridge in
G[S]
or
e_{2}
is a bridge in
G[V\setminus S]
, respectively. Furthermore, if
G[S]
and
G[V\setminus S]
are both
2
-edge-connected, then there are no bridges and the considered connectivity cut always defines a facet.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathrm{TECSP}(G)
be the face of
\mathrm{TECSP}(G)
defined by the considered connectivity cut inequality. We provide the proof in separate claims.

Claim 1
: If
e_{1}
is a bridge in
G[S]
or
e_{2}
is a bridge in
G[V\setminus S]
and
|\delta(S)|=2
, the considered connectivity cut inequality defines a facet of
\mathrm{TECSP}(G)
.

Proof.
Without loss of generality we may assume that
e_{1}
is a bridge in
G[S]
, since the condition is symmetric for
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
. If
|\delta(S)|=2
, the two edges of
\delta(S)
and
e_{1}
belong to the same coparallel class. The connectivity cut inequality thus simplifies to
x_{e_{2}}\leq 1
, and therefore defines a facet by
Theorem

3.2
.
\blacksquare

Claim 2
: If
e_{1}
is a bridge in
G[S]
or
e_{2}
is a bridge in
G[V\setminus S]
and
|\delta(S)|\geq 3
, the considered connectivity cut inequality does not define a facet of
\mathrm{TECSP}(G)
.

Proof.
Without loss of generality we may again assume that
e_{1}
is a bridge in
G[S]
. Let
H\subseteq G
be a
2
-edge-connected subgraph of
G
such that
\chi^{H}\in\mathcal{F}
. This is only possible if
H
contains at least one of
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
. If
e_{2}\in E(H)
, it is clear that
\chi^{H}\in\mathcal{F}_{e_{2}}
, where
\mathcal{F}_{e_{2}}\subseteq\mathrm{TECSP}(G)
denotes the facet defined by
x_{e_{2}}\leq 1
. Suppose that
H
contains the edge
e_{1}
. In
G[S]
the edge
e_{1}
is a bridge, but, since
H
is
2
-edge-connected, the edge
e_{1}
has to be contained in a cycle in
H
. Therefore,
E(H)
intersects
\delta(S)
non-trivially. Since
\chi^{H}\in\mathcal{F}
,
H
also contains the edge
e_{2}
and we have
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{e_{2}}
. If
|\delta(S)|\geq 3
,
\chi^{G}\in\mathcal{F}_{e_{2}}
but
\chi^{G}\notin\mathcal{F}
and hence,
\mathcal{F}\subsetneq\mathcal{F}_{e_{2}}
which shows that
\mathcal{F}
is not a facet, since
\mathcal{F}_{e_{2}}
is a proper face of
\mathrm{TECSP}(G)
by
Theorem

3.2
.
\blacksquare

.

G[S]

f
with

.
\delta(S,f)
is depicted in blue.

Claim 3
: If neither
e_{1}
nor
e_{2}
is a bridge in
G[W]
for
W\in\{S,V\setminus S\}
, and there is a bridge
f
of
G[W]
, such that
|\delta(W,f)|\geq 2
, then the considered connectivity cut inequality does not define a facet.

Proof.
Without loss of generality we may assume that
f
is a bridge of
G[S]
. Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
denote the connected component of
G[S]-f
, where
G_{1}
contains
e_{1}
. This situation is depicted in
Figure

6
. We have
\delta(V(G_{2}))=\{f\}\cup\delta(S,f)
. Suppose
H\subseteq G
is a
2
-edge-connected subgraph such that
\chi^{H}\in\mathcal{F}
. Then, one of the following cases occurs:

\bullet

H
contains
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
.

(a)

If
f\in E(H)
, then
H
contains exactly one edge of
\delta(S,f)
.

(b)

If
f\notin E(H)
,
H
contains exactly two edges of
\delta(S)\setminus\delta(S,f)
and, therefore,
H
does not contain any edge of
\delta(S,f)
.

\bullet

H
contains exactly one edge of
\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
. Then
H
neither contains an edge of
\delta(S)
nor the edge
f
.

In all cases,
\chi^{H}

Theorem 3.6
.

Let

be a
2
-edge-connected graph with edge set

\mathcal{P}(G)\coloneqq\{x\in[0,1]^{E}\mid x\text{ satisfies all \emph{asymmetric} and \emph{connectivity cut inequalities}}\}.

Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(G)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{E}=\{\chi^{H}\in\{0,1\}^{E}\mid H\subseteq G\text{ is }2\text{-edge-connected}\}.

Proof.

It is clear that
\{\chi^{H}\in\{0,1\}^{E}\mid H\subseteq G\text{ is }2\text{-edge-connected}\}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(G)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{E}
. Let

and let

be the subgraph of

with edge set

and induced vertex set of
E(G[y])
. Hence,
\chi^{G[y]}=y
. The claim follows if we can show that
G[y]
is
2
-edge-connected. We can assume that

, since otherwise

is the empty graph that is
2
-edge-connected by definition. Suppose first that
G[y]
is not connected. Then there exist at least two connected components
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
. By definition,

does not contain any isolated vertices and therefore,

. Considering the cut inducing set

, and choosing

, and

, we obtain a violated connectivity cut inequality, a contradiction to

. Suppose now that

is connected but not

-edge-connected. Then

has a bridge

. Furthermore, there is a vertex set
\emptyset\subsetneq S\subsetneq V(G)
such that

\displaystyle y_{e}-y(\delta(S)\setminus\{e\})=1,

that contain edges and suppose

Let

many linearly independent vectors contained in

is a facet. For

, we consider the vectors

. After projecting them onto the coordinates

of

|\mathrm{CP}(G_{i})|-1

. Since we can construct this kind of vectors for any

many vectors so far. Now, let

be a coparallel class of

are coparallel classes of

(

is an incidence vector

We can do this for every coparallel class

Computing 2-edge-connected subgraphs

We can now discuss how to transfer the theoretical results to an implementation for finding a maximum weight
2
-edge-connected subgraph. As motivated in the introduction, and as done throughout the paper, we assume that the given graph
G
is
2
-edge-connected.

4.1.
Basic model

By
Theorem

3.6
, the following defines an integer linear program (ILP) to find a maximum weight
2
-edge-connected subgraph
H\subseteq G
of a
2
-edge-connected graph
G
with edge weights
w\colon E(G)\to\mathbb{R}
:

\displaystyle\max\sum_{e\in E(G)}w(e)\cdot x_{e}
,

such that

x_{f}-x(\delta(S)\setminus\{f\})

\leq

0
,

for all
\emptyset\subsetneq S\subsetneq V(G)
,
f\in\delta(S)
,

2x_{e_{1}}-x(\delta(S))+2x_{e_{2}}

\leq

2
,

for all
\emptyset\subsetneq S\subsetneq V(G)
,
e_{1}\in E(G[S]),e_{2}\in E(G[V\setminus S])
,

x

\in

\{0,1\}^{E(G)}
.

Such an ILP is usually solved with a
Branch-and-Cut
algorithm: We replace the condition
x\in\{0,1\}^{E(G)}
by
x\in[0,1]^{E(G)}
to obtain the easier to solve
LP-relaxation
. Moreover, we start without any other inequality of the ILP. Then we use a linear program (LP) solver, to get a solution
\bar{x}\in[0,1]^{E(G)}
of this LP. Now we have to solve the
separation problem
: We have to check if
\bar{x}
satisfies all inequalities of our full LP-relaxation, or find inequalities (at least one) violated by
\bar{x}
. In the latter case, we will add (some of) these inequalities to our current model. We repeat this process of solving and separating (cutting) until there are no more violated inequalities. In that way, we obtain a solution of the full LP-relaxation of our ILP. If
\bar{x}\in\{0,1\}
, we found an optimal solution for our ILP. Otherwise, there is at least one coordinate where
\bar{x}
has a fractional value. In that case, we split the problem into two subproblems, recursively solving their linear program, with the variable fixing
\bar{x}_{e}=0
and
\bar{x}_{e}=1
, respectively.

To use this algorithm, we have to solve the separation problem for the asymmetric cut inequalities and the connectivity cut inequalities: decide whether a given point
\bar{x}\in[0,1]^{E(G)}
satisfies all asymmetric cut inequalities (connectivity cut inequalities, resp.) and if not, identify at least one violated inequality. For the asymmetric cut inequalities this can be done as follows. For each edge
e=\{s,t\}\in E(G)
, compute a minimum
s
-
t
-cut
F_{e}\subseteq E(G)\setminus\{e\}
in the graph
G-e
with edge weights
w^{\prime}(e^{\prime})\coloneqq\bar{x}_{e^{\prime}}
for all
e^{\prime}\in E(G)\setminus\{e\}
. Then,
F_{e}\cup\{e\}
is an
s
-
t
-cut in
G
and

\displaystyle x_{e}-x(F_{e})\leq 0

defines an asymmetric cut inequality. If
\bar{x}
violates this inequality, we identified a maximally violated asymmetric constraint among these with positive
x_{e}
-coefficient. If this process does not yield a violated constraint for any edge
e\in E(G)
, all asymmetric cut inequalities are satisfied.

For the connectivity cut inequalities we can proceed similarly: For every pair of non-adjacent edges
e_{1},e_{2}\in E(G)
, compute a minimum
s
-
t
-cut
F\subseteq E(G)
, where
s
is a vertex incident to
e_{1}
and
t
is a vertex incident to
e_{2}
with edge weights
w^{\prime}(e^{\prime})\coloneqq x_{e^{\prime}}
for all
e^{\prime}\in E(G)\setminus\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
and
w^{\prime}(e_{1})\coloneqq w^{\prime}(e_{2})\coloneqq 2|E(G)|
. By the choice of the weights we have
e_{1},e_{2}\notin F
and therefore,

\displaystyle 2x_{e_{1}}-x(F)+2x_{e_{2}}\leq 2

defines a connectivity cut inequality of
G
. Again, either this yields a most violated connectivity cut constraint for some
e_{1}
,
e_{2}
, or all such constraints are satisfied.

4.2.
Strengthening the basic model

We can strengthen the above ILP by adding the coparallel class inequalities, which also requires us to have a separation routine. Again, let
\bar{x}\in[0,1]^{E(G)}
be the current solution, possibly not satisfying all coparallel class inequalities. For each coparallel class
C\in\mathrm{CP}(G)

4.3.
Experimental setup

In order to test whether the coparallel class inequalities are (can be) beneficial in practice, we want to use graphs that have rather large coparallel classes. To this end, we construct two different classes of graphs. The first class

and
6

For the second type of graphs, which we will call

-cycles, we pick a number

To test whether the odd star inequalities improve the running time of our basic model, we test the model on complete graphs where the edge weights are integer values between

5.
Conclusion

We have studied different supporting hyperplanes for the
2
-edge-connected subgraph polytope when the underlying graph
G
is
2
-edge-connected. The asymmetric cut inequalities and the connectivity cut inequalities together with the trivial box inequalities describe all lattice points contained in
\mathrm{TECSP}(G)
. Therefore, we get an ILP for finding a best weighted
2
-edge-connected subgraph of a
2
-edge-connected graph
G
, which directly yields an algorithm to find a best weighted
2
-edge-connected subgraph of an arbitrary graph
G^{\prime}
. We also studied other supporting hyperplanes, namely, the coparallel class inequalities and the odd star inequalities, where the latter are only defined for complete graphs. We experimentally confirmed that the coparallel class inequalities may improve the running time of the so far considered ILP on certain classes of graphs. On the other hand, the odd star inequalities do not seem to make a difference in running time. Some open questions remain. Of course, the most interesting but probably hard to answer question is

Question 5.1
.

What is a complete facet-description of
\mathrm{TECSP}(G)
for any
2
-edge-connected graph
G
?

Therefore, some easier questions would be the following two.

Question 5.2
.

What are other classes of supporting hyperplanes for the
\mathrm{TECSP}(G)
, and when are they facet-defining?

Question 5.3
.

For which graphs
G
is
\mathrm{TECSP}(G)
completely described by the so far studied inequalities?

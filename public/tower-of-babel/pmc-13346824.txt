A personalized patent recommendation system using knowledge maps to foster enterprise innovation
Authors: Sifan He, Xin Guan, Congjing Ran
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study aims to develop an effective recommendation system to assist enterprises in better utilizing patent information during the innovation process. Firstly, key information such as patent titles, abstracts, and application dates is extracted from patent data, and a patent knowledge map is constructed based on this information. This knowledge map uses patents as nodes and forms a complex network structure through citation relationships and domain similarities, providing a data foundation for the recommendation algorithm. Secondly, the RippleNet algorithm is employed for personalized recommendations. This algorithm iteratively propagates ripples to learn the interaction patterns between users and patents, thereby capturing users’ interests and preferences. The goal of applying this recommendation algorithm is to enhance users’ efficiency in obtaining relevant patent information and to support enterprises in making better use of knowledge resources during innovation. The empirical analysis of patent data from listed companies in China further verifies the effectiveness and practicality of the recommendation system. In the knowledge map, after training with the Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers (BERT) model, the recognition accuracy for composition relationships and function achievement relationships in patent abstracts reaches 0.973 and 0.907, respectively, with the accuracy for relative position relationships reaching 0.861. In the personalized recommendation system, as the number of top recommendations increases, the accuracy rate gradually decreases while the recall rate increases. This study explores a personalized patent recommendation method based on a knowledge map, which effectively integrates, stores, and manages a large amount of patent information. It provides enterprises with a more intuitive and convenient knowledge management tool, thereby enhancing their knowledge management capabilities.Keywords: Enterprise innovation, Knowledge map, Patents, Personalized recommendation, Listed companySubject terms: Information technology, Software, Statistics

Introduction

With the rapid development of information technology and the ongoing integration of the global economy, enterprise innovation has become the core driver of economic growth and competitive advantage1–3. In today’s digital and knowledge-based economy, enterprises must not only generate new ideas and solutions but also rapidly transform these innovations into commercial value in an increasingly competitive market environment. Patents, as a key tool for protecting intellectual property and fostering innovation, play a crucial role in this process. However, with the exponential growth of global patent applications, traditional methods of patent management and utilization face significant challenges4. Conventional patent retrieval and browsing methods often rely on keyword matching, which can lead to issues such as inaccurate information retrieval and unclear patent associations. These methods struggle to meet the personalized innovation needs of enterprises at various stages and across different fields. This is especially true in knowledge-intensive and complex fields like artificial intelligence (AI) and biotechnology, where traditional patent retrieval methods are increasingly inadequate5–7.

In this context, personalized patent recommendation technology based on knowledge maps has emerged. As a semantic network model, a knowledge map can semantically abstract and link the knowledge elements within patents, enabling accurate representation and recommendation of knowledge. By constructing a patent knowledge map, the internal relationships and semantic associations between patents can be deeply explored, providing enterprises with more intelligent and personalized patent recommendation services8,9. At the core of patent personalized recommendation is the recommendation algorithm, which analyzes users’ needs and behaviors to identify relevant patents from the knowledge map and suggest them to users. Common recommendation algorithms include content-based recommendation, collaborative filtering, and matrix decomposition, among others. In knowledge map-based recommendations, graph structure and semantic representation information can be combined, with propagation algorithms and path analysis techniques applied to make more effective recommendations10–12.

In recent years, both domestic and international scholars have extensively studied the use of knowledge graphs in patent recommendation, producing a substantial body of work. This study has mainly centered on three areas. First, many studies focus on constructing patent knowledge graphs that integrate multi-source information to enhance the structural expressiveness of data. For example, Lu et al. (2024)13 proposed a BERT-enhanced model that incorporated semantic information into the Transformer framework, significantly improving graph embedding performance. Similarly, Siddharth et al. (2022)14 combined natural language processing with knowledge graph embedding techniques to strengthen retrieval capabilities for initial patent sets.

Second, researchers have explored personalized recommendation mechanisms based on graph structures to improve both the precision and interpretability of patent matching. Du et al. (2021)15 developed a framework that integrated multi-dimensional patent analysis and introduced inventor reputation as a quality metric to help identify high-quality patents. Chen & Deng (2023)16 created an explainable recommendation method that leveraged deep learning and knowledge graphs to integrate heterogeneous patent data. Xiao et al. (2023)17 further improved recommendation accuracy and semantic consistency by modeling semantic associations and constraints within product–patent heterogeneous graphs.

Third, efforts have expanded the application scenarios and information dimensions of recommendation systems, with a particular emphasis on integrating user behavior and external data. Chen et al. (2024)18 introduced a multidimensional, trust-enhanced method that combined patent information, user interactions, and technology transfer data for academic patent trading recommendations. Jin et al. (2021)19 addressed the needs of adaptive design by proposing a patent co-classification network. This network constructed a term co-occurrence structure from technical and functional keywords to generate technology–function maps that supported engineering design tasks.

Overall, these studies have made important advances in semantic enhancement, structural modeling, recommendation accuracy, and application diversity. Nevertheless, most existing methods still fail to fully exploit the intrinsic topological features of patents and rarely incorporate dynamic modeling of user interests or multi-round interaction mechanisms. These gaps create an opportunity for the personalized patent recommendation approach proposed in this study, which is based on a ripple propagation mechanism. Therefore, practical verification and evaluation of patent personalized recommendations based on knowledge maps are still limited, especially in the context of listed companies in China. Many existing systems suffer from low recommendation precision and fail to effectively address the specific needs of enterprises. Furthermore, most systems lack personalized recommendation mechanisms tailored to the diverse requirements of different companies. Current research does not fully utilize patent data, overlooking the relationships and potential value between patents.

Building on prior research, this study explores three core questions: (1) How can knowledge graph–based patent recommendation systems effectively enhance enterprise innovation? (2) Is the RippleNet model well-suited and promising for patent recommendation tasks? (3) How effective is the proposed system in real-world enterprise applications? To address these questions, a personalized patent recommendation method integrating knowledge graphs is developed to provide enterprises with a more intelligent and precise tool for supporting innovation. An empirical analysis using patent data from Chinese listed companies confirms the system’s feasibility and effectiveness.

The main contribution of this study is a knowledge graph–based patent recommendation framework that substantially improves enterprises’ capabilities in knowledge management and technological innovation. Empirical results further show that the RippleNet model performs strongly in patent recommendation tasks, extending its applicability in recommender systems. This study offers enterprises a novel methodological and technological pathway for effectively leveraging patent resources and gaining competitive advantages in increasingly complex innovation environments.

This study addresses the patent recommendation task and proposes a personalized patent recommendation system based on knowledge graphs and an enhanced RippleNet model. The key innovations in model design and application are as follows:

(1) Multi-source Heterogeneous Knowledge Graph Construction: A heterogeneous knowledge graph is created by integrating various entities and relationships, including patents, technology domains, inventors, and keywords. This graph captures both explicit citation relationships and latent connections, such as “composition relationships” and “function realization relationships,” by extracting information from patent texts using the BERT model. This enhances the graph’s expressive power and the depth of knowledge organization. (2) Structural Improvement of the RippleNet Model: Given the characteristics of patent data—such as long texts and sparse structures—BERT is used to initialize the representation of patent nodes, improving the model’s ability to handle the semantics of lengthy texts. Furthermore, graph convolutional networks are integrated to improve information propagation within sparse graph structures. An adaptive diffusion mechanism is also introduced, allowing the node propagation range to dynamically adjust based on the density of neighboring nodes, making it more suitable for the complexity of patent data. (3) Multi-level Information Fusion and Interest Modeling: In user modeling, this study considers not only users’ historical behaviors but also their industry domain and technical preferences. By combining semantic paths from the knowledge graph, multi-granularity interest expansion is achieved. This mechanism significantly enhances the ability to personalize recommendations, addressing the diverse needs of enterprises in various innovation contexts. Through these innovations, this study not only improves recommendation accuracy and recall rates but also offers new solutions for integrating complex structured and unstructured patent information.

The results of this study fill a significant gap in the field of patent recommendation based on knowledge maps in China, offering new insights and methodologies for both academic research and practical applications in related fields. This is not only crucial for advancing knowledge management and innovation in Chinese enterprises but also holds substantial academic value in enriching and refining the theory and methods of patent recommendation based on knowledge maps.

Method

Construction of knowledge map based on patent data

The construction of a knowledge map based on patent data is a complex and crucial task aimed at transforming vast amounts of patent information into a structured knowledge map for subsequent knowledge management, recommendation, and analysis. Patent texts contain various types of entities and relationships that carry significant information about patent knowledge20. In constructing a knowledge graph from patent data, it is first necessary to identify and structure the various entity and relationship types contained within the data. These entities and relationships form the fundamental building blocks of the knowledge graph, serving as the foundation for subsequent recommendation and analysis. The process of constructing a knowledge map involves several steps, including knowledge extraction, entity recognition, and relationship extraction. The emergence of pre-trained models such as Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers (BERT) has introduced new possibilities and improved efficiency in constructing patent knowledge maps21. Knowledge extraction refers to the process of extracting valuable information from unstructured or semi-structured data. In the context of constructing a knowledge map based on patent data, knowledge extraction includes identifying entities (such as inventors, applicants, and patent names) and relationships (such as citation relationships and family patent relationships) from patent texts22–24. BERT, a pre-trained language representation model, learns semantic information through a bidirectional Transformer encoder. Its core principle is to acquire rich contextual representations during the pre-training phase, which are then fine-tuned for specific tasks, enabling it to perform various natural language understanding tasks. In constructing patent knowledge maps, the BERT model can be used to extract entities and relationships, leveraging its powerful semantic representation capabilities to enhance the accuracy and efficiency of extraction.

Entity recognition involves identifying entities with specific meanings within the text, such as inventors, applicants, and technical fields in patents. Relationship extraction focuses on identifying semantic relationships between entities, such as the association between inventors and patents, or the citation relationship between patents25–27. Entity recognition and relationship extraction based on the BERT model can be achieved by training a multi-label classification model, which predicts the entity type and relationship type for each word, allowing for the extraction of both entities and relationships. The framework for the entity relationship extraction model based on BERT is shown in Fig. 1. Once entities and relationships are extracted, they can be stored in a graph database to construct the patent knowledge map. In this map, nodes represent entities, and edges represent the relationships between entities. The knowledge map allows for efficient querying, reasoning, and analysis, providing strong support for subsequent patent recommendations and innovation decision-making.

Systematic knowledge extraction refers to the automatic extraction of meaningful information and knowledge from unstructured or semi-structured texts using computer technology. This process involves methods and technologies from fields such as natural language processing, machine learning, and AI. Initially, text data undergoes preprocessing steps, including noise removal, word segmentation, and part-of-speech tagging, which help improve the efficiency and accuracy of subsequent processing. The system must identify the entities within the text, which can include named entities (such as names of people, places, or institutions) or other words or phrases with specific meanings28. Entity recognition is typically achieved through rule-based methods, machine learning techniques, or deep learning approaches. The system also analyzes the relationships between entities in the text, which may include co-occurrence relationships, semantic relationships, and more. Relationship extraction can be performed using syntactic analysis, semantic analysis, or machine learning technologies29,30.

In addition to identifying entities and relationships, the system may also extract events or actions described in the text. Event extraction involves identifying trigger words, participants, time, and place of events. The system is also designed to extract useful information such as dates, prices, and quantities. Information extraction typically involves pattern matching, rule-based methods, or machine learning techniques. The extracted knowledge must be represented in a form that can be understood by the computer. Common representation methods include graphs, triples, and vector representations. Finally, the extracted knowledge is stored in a database or knowledge map for subsequent querying, retrieval, and analysis. Personalized patent recommendation based on knowledge map

Recommendation systems can be categorized into various types based on their recommended content and methods. These include content-based recommendation, collaborative filtering recommendation, and knowledge map-based recommendation31–33, as shown in Fig. 3.

Main types of recommendation systems.

The knowledge map-based recommendation system uses entities and relationships within the knowledge graph to model the interactions between users and items. The overall framework of the patent personalized recommendation system based on the knowledge graph consists of the following key steps:

(1) Knowledge Graph Construction.

First, entities (such as patent titles, abstracts, and keywords) and relationships (such as citation and category relationships) are extracted from patent texts. These extracted entities and relationships are then used to construct a patent knowledge graph. In this graph, entities are modeled as nodes and relationships as edges, forming a structure that captures the complex interconnections between patents.

(2) User Modeling.

User interests and needs are analyzed by examining behavioral data, personal information, and preferences. Features are extracted from the user’s historical behaviors, including browsing history, favorites, and search records, as well as demographic data such as age, gender, and geographical location. These features are then used to create a user interest vector, which represents the user’s level of interest across various patent domains.

(3) Personalized Recommendation Algorithm.

Based on the user’s interest vector and the patent association relationships in the knowledge graph, the system maps the user’s historical behaviors (such as clicks, views, and favorites) to patent nodes within the graph to calculate an interest score for each patent. This process generates the user’s interest vector. The system then leverages the associations between patents (e.g., common citations or belonging to the same domain) to compute the similarity between the user’s interest vector and each patent. Patents that most closely align with the user’s interests are selected from the knowledge graph as recommendation candidates.

Initially, the user’s historical behavioral data (such as clicks, views, favorites, etc.) are mapped to patent nodes within the knowledge graph based on the user’s interest vector and the patent associations in the graph. This process calculates the user’s interest score for each patent, forming the user’s interest vector. Next, the system leverages the relationships between patents in the knowledge graph (e.g., common citations, same domain) to compute the similarity between the user’s interest vector and each patent. Patents that most closely align with the user’s interests are then selected from the knowledge graph as potential recommendations. This process combines user modeling information (including user features and behavioral data) with patent similarity calculations to design a personalized recommendation ranking algorithm. The algorithm ranks patents in the recommendation set based on their relevance to the user’s interests. Additionally, implicit feedback data, such as dwell time and browsing depth, are incorporated to adjust the weighting of recommendation results, enhancing the quality and user satisfaction of the recommendations. Finally, the personalized ranking algorithm generates a final list of recommended patents, including key details such as title, abstract, and application date. This ensures that the recommendations are diverse, avoiding duplicates or irrelevant suggestions. The algorithmic process is illustrated in Fig. 4 with a graph representation.

(4) Evaluation and Optimization.

Evaluation metrics, including accuracy, recall, and coverage, are used to assess the recommendation results and analyze the system’s performance. RippleNet is a recommendation algorithm based on graph neural networks, designed for personalized recommendation tasks within knowledge maps34–36. It uses a graph structure to represent the relationships between users and candidate patents and employs a neural network model to learn these relationships. The main task is to predict whether user node 𝑢 is interested in patent node 𝑝, forming a binary classification problem that estimates the user’s level of interest in the candidate patent.

The algorithm consists of three main steps:

(1) Node Extraction: Initially, user node 𝑢 and candidate patent node 𝑝 are extracted from the knowledge graph using an attention mechanism. This leverages the feature extraction capabilities of graph neural networks to map the features of users and patents into a vector space.

(2) Information Propagation: Next, information is propagated and updated within the knowledge graph through a “ripple propagation” mechanism. This simulates the dissemination of information between users and patents. As interactions occur between users and candidate patents, information gradually spreads, influencing the recommendation results37–39.

(3) Contextual Information Fusion: Finally, the contextual information and interaction results are used to predict user interest in patents. This step integrates the contextual information obtained during propagation with the feature vectors of users and patents, using a neural network model to predict interest.

The ripple propagation mechanism simulates the spreading of information between users and patents, similar to the “ripple effect” created during interactions between users and items. As users interact with candidate patents, a series of “ripples” are generated, gradually diffusing and impacting related user and patent nodes. This information propagation significantly influences the final recommendation results, enabling the system to accurately capture the associations between users and patents, thereby improving the quality and personalization of recommendations.

However, directly applying the original RippleNet model to the patent recommendation task presents several challenges, mainly due to two significant characteristics of patent data: long and semantically complex textual content, and the sparse and highly heterogeneous structure of the knowledge graph. To address these challenges, this study proposes three key improvements to the RippleNet model, making it more suitable for patent recommendation tasks:

(1) Textual Semantic Enhancement Module:

Patent texts typically consist of complex technical language and lengthy descriptions, containing rich technical terms and information crucial for understanding the intrinsic value of patents. Traditional RippleNet models have limited ability to handle long texts. Therefore, this study introduces a deep semantic encoding module that uses pre-trained language models (such as BERT, RoBERTa, etc.) to encode patent abstracts, titles, background technologies, and other textual elements. This allows the model to better capture the deep semantics of patent texts, extracting more contextual information, including the technological domain, invention background, and innovation points of the patent. This enhancement module helps RippleNet more accurately understand the semantic content of patents and provides more discriminative features for subsequent recommendation tasks.

(2) Relationship Sparsity Compensation Mechanism:

Direct relationships between patents in the knowledge graph are often sparse, especially in fields with smaller patent datasets, which can affect the effectiveness and accuracy of information propagation. To address this issue, this study proposes an adjacency enhancement strategy based on similarity expansion. This strategy calculates the semantic similarity between patents and supplements the sparse relationship chains in the knowledge graph by using text-based similarity metrics, co-occurrence frequencies of patents in the same domain, and other information. Specifically, through clustering and similarity calculation, patents are grouped by domain or technological direction to generate potential implicit association paths, thereby filling in the missing relationships in the knowledge graph. This allows RippleNet to acquire more comprehensive upstream and downstream information during propagation, enhancing the model’s understanding of patent associations and improving recommendation accuracy.

(3) Multi-layer Propagation Dynamic Control Mechanism:

The core of RippleNet is its multi-hop propagation mechanism, which captures potential interest associations between users and candidate patents through information spread within the graph. However, in patent recommendations, excessive propagation can introduce noise or irrelevant information, especially when the connections between nodes are weak. To overcome this issue, this study introduces a dynamic control mechanism for multi-layer propagation. This mechanism dynamically adjusts the number of propagation layers and their depth based on the strength of the relationship between the user and the candidate patent. For example, if a user has a strong association with a patent in a specific technological field, the system will increase the propagation layers to delve deeper into potential association information. Conversely, for weaker associations, the propagation depth will be limited to avoid introducing irrelevant information. This dynamic adjustment allows the system to flexibly control the range of information propagation, improving recommendation relevance and precision.

Through these improvements, this study proposes effective optimization strategies for the traditional RippleNet model, tailored to the complexity and characteristics of patent data. These modifications enable RippleNet to better handle long textual descriptions, sparse relationships, and multidimensional information in patent data. As a result, the system not only improves the personalization accuracy of patent recommendations but also enhances the practical utility and effectiveness of the recommendation system in the patent domain.

The selection of the RippleNet algorithm is driven by its significant advantages and practical value in personalized recommendation tasks within knowledge graphs. By harnessing the robust capabilities of graph neural networks, RippleNet effectively models the complex relationships between users and patents, thereby enhancing the accuracy and personalization of recommendations. Additionally, the introduction of the ripple propagation mechanism allows RippleNet to simulate interactions between users and patents, improving its understanding of user interests and behaviors, which in turn boosts the quality of the recommendations. Specifically designed to tackle personalized recommendation challenges in knowledge graphs, RippleNet is well-suited to adapt to the unique characteristics of these graphs, offering enterprises more precise and effective patent recommendation services.

Empirical analysis

This study draws on empirical data from Chinese listed companies, with patent information sourced from the China National Intellectual Property Administration (CNIPA, https://www.cnipa.gov.cn/) and the World Intellectual Property Organization’s public database (WIPO, https://patentscope.wipo.int/). From the perspective of enterprise innovation, the study systematically examines a personalized patent recommendation method based on knowledge graphs. Three representative technological domains—electric toothbrushes, medical devices, and energy materials—were selected for experimentation. Datasets were compiled using keywords such as “electric toothbrush,” “cardiac pacemaker,” and “graphene battery” to assess the applicability and effectiveness of the proposed method. The patent data, primarily from multiple open databases and focused on Chinese listed companies, are highly reliable and representative, offering an accurate picture of corporate patent portfolios and innovation activities.

Electric toothbrushes, as typical mechanical products, feature general structures and functional descriptions, making them suitable case studies. Medical devices and energy materials, representing biomedical technology and materials science, respectively, broaden the experimental scope and strengthen the study’s generalizability. Patent data spanning 2015–2022 were filtered using these keywords, with invalid patents removed to ensure completeness and relevance.

Invalid patents were excluded if they (1) lacked essential information (e.g., title, abstract, application or publication date, or applicant details)40, (2) were confirmed duplicates or lapsed through patent status verification41, or (3) contained titles or abstracts irrelevant to the selected technological domains. Manual review was used to validate the filtering process, ensuring all patents were relevant and information was complete.

Ultimately, 3,320 invention and utility model patents from Chinese listed companies were retained for information extraction. The dataset includes details such as patent titles, abstracts, application dates, publication dates, and applicant information. Portions of the abstracts were annotated as raw corpora, with examples provided in Table 3.

Furthermore, in constructing the knowledge graph, this study expands the sources of patent samples by integrating data from various patent databases and other relevant sources, thereby enhancing both the coverage and quality of the knowledge graph. Specifically, additional patent data is sourced from scientific journals, patent offices, and patent trading platforms, among other channels. The study also incorporates cross-domain patent data from fields such as mechanical, electronic, and chemical engineering to ensure broad representation and diversity within the knowledge graph. These datasets undergo rigorous cleansing and standardization procedures to maintain data quality and consistency. The expanded dataset includes over 5,000 patent samples, comprising different types, including invention patents and utility model patents. This larger sample size significantly enriches the breadth and depth of the knowledge graph, providing a solid data foundation that supports the overall performance of the recommendation system.

In data processing, the raw text was first segmented into sentences and tokenized. Entities were recognized, and relationships annotated using a unified labeling standard covering three main domains: education, healthcare, and e-government. The corpus was then cleaned through deduplication, removal of abnormal symbols, punctuation normalization, and minimum-length filtering. Finally, the dataset was split into training (80%), validation (10%), and test (10%) sets.

For model training, a pre-trained BERT model (Bert-base-Chinese) served as the encoder. In the named entity recognition (NER) task, the AdamW optimizer was used with an initial learning rate of 5e-5, a batch size of 32, up to 20 epochs, early stopping after 3 epochs without improvement, a dropout rate of 0.1, and gradient clipping capped at 1.0. For relation extraction, a BiLSTM layer was followed by a fully connected layer, trained with cross-entropy loss and the same learning rate as the NER task. All models were trained on an NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU with a fixed random seed of 42 to ensure reproducibility.

To evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed enhancements for patent recommendation, a series of ablation experiments were conducted. Each enhancement was individually examined and compared with the baseline model to assess its specific impact on recommendation performance. The experiments utilized a real-world patent dataset containing information such as titles, abstracts, classification data, and inventors. This dataset was structured into a knowledge graph that represents relationships between users and patents, with the original RippleNet model serving as the baseline recommender.

To ensure fair comparison, the following experimental groups were established:

Baseline Model: The unmodified RippleNet model.

Text Enhancement Group: Integrates a pre-trained language model (e.g., BERT) for semantic enrichment of patent texts, while maintaining the original knowledge graph structure and propagation mechanism.

Relation Sparsity Compensation Group: Augments the knowledge graph with similarity-based relationship extensions to address sparsity, without applying text enhancements.

Dynamic Multi-layer Propagation Group: Introduces a dynamic control mechanism to adjust the number of propagation layers, without incorporating text or relation enhancements.

Full Improvement Group: Combines all three enhancements—text semantics, relation sparsity compensation, and dynamic multi-layer propagation—into the original RippleNet model for comprehensive optimization.

The performance of each model was evaluated using the following standard metrics:

Accuracy: Indicates whether the recommended patents match the user’s interests.

Recall: Measures the model’s ability to retrieve all relevant patents.

F1-Score: Balances accuracy and recall to provide an overall performance measure.

NDCG@K (Normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain): Assesses the ranking quality of the top K recommended patents.

Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR): Evaluates the average ranking position of the first relevant recommendation.

Results and discussion

Identification results of entity relationship based on BERT model

After training the BERT model, the results—illustrated in Fig. 6—demonstrate the model’s strong performance in extracting patent entity relationships. Specifically, the recognition accuracies for the composition relationship and functional realization relationship reached 0.973 and 0.907, respectively, while the relative position relationship achieved an accuracy of 0.861. However, the model performed less effectively on the action relationship and connection relationship, likely due to their semantic similarity. For instance, in the patent abstract: “The drive assembly is connected with the piston rod to drive the piston rod to push the piston head into the tube,” the relationship between “drive assembly” and “piston rod” encompasses both a connection and an action. Yet, the model predicted only an action relationship. By contrast, the other three relationship types tend to appear in more standardized textual patterns, with clearer semantic cues, which likely contributed to the model’s higher accuracy in those cases.

Identification results of entity relationship based on BERT model.

While this study has made notable progress in identifying patent entity relationships, certain relationship types were less accurately recognized. These suboptimal results may be attributed to noise and semantic ambiguity within the data. The complexity of technical terminology and the variability in language expressions in patent texts pose significant challenges for accurately identifying some relationship types. For example, the semantic overlap between action relationships and connection relationships can make it difficult for the model to distinguish between them. Additionally, although BERT has demonstrated impressive performance in natural language processing tasks, it shows limitations when applied to domain-specific texts such as patents. To better capture complex relationships in such specialized content, further refinement of model architecture and parameter tuning is necessary.

Future research will adopt the following strategies for improvement: Optimize data preprocessing to minimize noise and ambiguity, thereby improving the model’s precision in relationship identification. Explore advanced model architectures or incorporate domain-specific knowledge to enhance the model’s understanding of patent language and context. Expand the dataset and introduce more diverse patent texts to improve the model’s generalization and robustness.

Performance of personalized recommendation system based on RippleNet

This study employs patent citation network analysis to simulate interaction data between users and patents. By analyzing cited patent documents, it is possible to infer which patents the authors may have consulted, thereby constructing a dataset of user–patent interactions. A total of 120 users and 800 user–patent interaction records were collected. To facilitate model training and evaluation, the dataset was split into training and testing sets in a 7:3 ratio. To enable effective processing by the model, user and patent data were numerically encoded. Specific categories were replaced with numerical identifiers, and user–patent interactions were represented using binary values: 0 indicates that a user did not consult the patent, while 1 signifies that the user did. The RippleNet algorithm was then applied to this dataset across five experimental runs, and the average performance metrics were reported. This superior performance stems from its integration of personalized recommendation with knowledge graph technology. Unlike approaches that rely solely on user behavior or textual content, the proposed system leverages the structural relationships and domain knowledge embedded within the knowledge graph. The proposed patent recommendation system not only accelerates the discovery of patents relevant to specific business domains but also provides personalized suggestions tailored to user interests. This capability significantly enhances innovation efficiency by reducing the time and costs associated with navigating large volumes of patent data. Moreover, the system empowers enterprises to better understand market trends and competitive landscapes, thereby reinforcing their technological innovation and overall competitiveness. In summary, the proposed system offers valuable and practical benefits, supporting enterprises in improving innovation efficiency and gaining a strategic edge in competitive markets.

It should be noted that the two comparison methods adopted in this study are relatively traditional and basic baselines in the field of recommender systems. In recent years, recommendation methods based on Graph Neural Network (GNN), such as KGCN (Knowledge Graph Convolutional Network) and KGAT (Knowledge Graph Attention Network), have made significant progress. Compared with these advanced models, the RippleNet algorithm in this study still possesses unique advantages. RippleNet propagates only through local subgraphs of the knowledge graph through a ripple sampling mechanism, rather than full-graph convolution. This results in higher computational efficiency and is more suitable for large-scale patent data. Additionally, the ripple propagation paths naturally form an inference chain of user interests, visually displaying the basis for recommendations, and exhibit significantly better interpretability than most GNN recommenders. The three improvements proposed in this study, focusing on the characteristics of patent data, namely text semantic enhancement, relationship sparsity compensation, and dynamic propagation control, are applicable to RippleNet and reflect the specialized optimization value of this method in the patent domain. In summary, although this study does not directly compare with the latest GNN models, RippleNet demonstrates clear advantages in terms of computational efficiency, interpretability, and patent data adaptability. Ablation experiments have also fully verified the effectiveness of these improvements.

Conclusion

The main contributions of this study are as follows: A personalized patent recommendation system based on a knowledge graph is successfully developed, with the patent data of electric toothbrushes serving as a case study. The system utilizes the RippleNet algorithm to provide personalized recommendations by learning the interaction patterns between users and patents, thereby capturing users’ interests and preferences. This system offers tailored patent recommendations based on the relationship between users’ needs and patents. Additionally, an empirical analysis using patent data from publicly listed companies in China demonstrates that the recommendation system, which incorporates the BERT model and knowledge graph, performs well in terms of both recommendation accuracy and efficiency. This validates the feasibility of personalized patent recommendations based on knowledge graphs.

Despite these achievements, there are several areas for further exploration. In knowledge graph construction, the content and structure of the patent knowledge graph could be further enriched and refined to enhance the coverage and accuracy of the recommendation system. In terms of the recommendation algorithm, more sophisticated and refined models could be explored, potentially integrating deep learning and natural language processing techniques to enhance the system’s intelligence. In addition, this study employs patent citation network analysis to simulate the interaction data between users and patents. Specifically, it infers the patents that inventors may have referenced by analyzing the cited relationships of patents, thereby constructing a user-patent interaction dataset. This method is a reasonable choice in the absence of real enterprise clickstream data. However, it must be acknowledged that there is an essential difference between the psychological motivation behind academic or patent citations and the browsing behavior of R&D engineers seeking technical inspiration. Citations typically reflect formal and carefully considered annotations of knowledge sources, whereas engineers’ actual browsing behavior is more exploratory, random, and immediate. Therefore, the interaction data constructed based on citation networks may not fully and accurately reflect users’ actual interests and preferences during patent search processes. Future research that combines real enterprise user behavior log data will further validate and enhance the practical application effectiveness of the proposed method.

Constructing advanced electrospun nanofiber/hydrogel composite scaffolds loading with nano hydroxyapatite and puerarin for promoting osseointegration and osteanagenesis in a rat cranial defect model
Authors: Bufan Li, Jie Shen, Yingying Wang, Haibo Hu, Xuechao Lu, Wenwen Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In this study, one advanced composite scaffold was designed. Its characteristics, effect on repairing large-sized bone defects and related mechanisms were explored.MethodsIn vitro, a bi-layered composite scaffold constructing with one layer of electrospun poly (3-hydroxybutyrateco-3-hydroxyvalerate) (PHBV) nanofiber membrane and one layer of UV-crosslinked methacrylated gelatin (MeGel) hydrogel were fabricated. Moreover, two types of bio-components including nano hydroxyapatite (nHAP) and puerarin (Pur) were encapsulated into the MeGel hydrogel layer to impart the composite scaffolds with multiple biofunctions. In vivo, the calvarial defect models with Sprague Dawley rats were created to ascertain the impact of PHBV/MeGel, PHBV/MeGel-HAP, and PHBV/MeGel-HAP-Pur composite scaffolds.ResultsThe in vitro studies demonstrated that all the composite scaffolds loading with or without nHAP and Pur exhibited great water swelling and retention properties, as well as excellent rheological and mechanical properties. Furthermore, the PHBV/MeGel-nHAP-Pur composite scaffolds could facilitate the osteogenesis and vascularization, but inhibit osteoclast formation. Also, hydrogel layer could provide a benificial micro-environment for the cell infiltration. In vivo study demonstrated that the PHBV/MeGel-nHAP-Pur composite scaffolds obviously promote the healing of rat cranial defect with large injury size, and the healing rate was roughly 99.25% after 8 weeks of treatment.ConclusionOur present study suggests that the bi-layered PHBV/MeGel-nHAP-Pur composite scaffolds with multiple bio-functions show huge potential for the repair and regeneration of large-sized bone defects.

Introduction

Bone damage is commonly caused by the infection, tumor, or traumatic incidents [1,2]. When the bone defects exceed a critical threshold, specifically, surpassing 20 mm in length or over 50% of the bone's circumference, a surgical treatment should be performed in clinics [3,4]. Globally, over 4 million individuals require bone grafting procedures annually, making this the second most common tissue graft after blood transfusion [5]. Autologous grafts are regarded as the optimal scaffold for repairing significant bone defects. However, it presents several severe drawbacks, including a limited number of donor sites, difficulty in shaping, and high bone resorption rate of grafted bone [6,7]. To overcome these limitations, the bone scaffolds gradually transitioned from autogenous grafts to man-made scaffolds. Among them, self-curing plastic, plexiglass, polymer materials, bone cement, titanium band and polyetheretherketone are commonly-used repair materials [8]. Unsatisfactorily, these materials often have non-negligible defects such as poor thermal conductivity, radiation artifacts, magnetic resonance interference, and are highly susceptible to infection [9,10]. Therefore, it remains challenging to select repair materials that possess excellent histocompatibility and can facilitate large-area bone repair.

Bone tissue engineering provides an innovative platform in the regenerative medicine, harnessing scaffold biomaterials and bioactive growth factors to enhance bone repair and regeneration [11,12]. Compared with the single-phase scaffolds, the multi-phase scaffolds can be optimized comprehensively for multiple functions through the adjustment of their components and regional distribution [13]. Most recently, the composite scaffolds that integrate hydrogels with electrospun fibers have aroused intensive interest owing to their exceptional histocompatibility and biological performance [[14], [15], [16]]. Hydrogel, as a promising new material for bone scaffold, are widely explored to promote the bone healing [17]. It effectively absorbs wound exudate, maintaining the moist microenvironment necessary for the wound bed, while regulating oxygen tension and blood vessel growth, both of which are crucial for facilitating the wound healing process [18]. However, hydrogels have relatively poor mechanical properties and struggle to replicate the nanofibrous structures necessary for cell adhesion and growth [19]. By contrast, electrospinning can fabricate three-dimensional (3D) nanofibrous scaffolds that mimic extracellular matrix (ECM) structures, thereby boosting cell proliferation and angiogenesis [20,21]. Additionally, electrospinning technology allows for the facile and flexible manipulation of fiber diameter, pore size, and arrangement of nanofiber membranes by adjusting spinning parameters and receiving devices [22,23]. This precision control over the nanofiber membrane characteristics is a significant advantage of the electrospinning process. However, nanofiber membranes have poor mechanical strength and are often used with hydrogel materials to enhance their mechanical robustness. Such as, bilayer scaffolds for the preparation of nanofibers and hydrogels, as well as the encapsulating of nanofibers into hydrogels [24,25].

Poly (3-hydroxybutyrate-co-3-hydroxyvalerate) (PHBV), a synthesized biopolymer, has been prepared into nanofibers by using electrospinning and commonly utilized in tissue repair because of its superior mechanical characteristics, enhanced biocompatibility, and controlled biodegradability [26,27]. Gelatin (Gel) is also one type of widely-recognized biomaterials in tissue engineering and regenerative medicine due to its good biocompatibility and biodegradability, which can be extensively extracted from animal skins or bones [28,29]. However, Gel is often blended with some other substances for plasticization modification, cross-linking modification and chemical modification owing to its inadequate mechanical strength and rapid breakdown [16]. Methacrylated gelatin (MeGel) is a photosensitive hydrogel that results from the chemical modification of gelatin, which can form a 3D network hydrogel through a UV crosslinking process, thereby enhancing the mechanical properties. Moreover, MeGel retains the Arg-Gly-Asp peptide sequence and matrix metalloproteinase sequence from gelatin, which facilitates the cell adhesion, infiltration, and proliferation [30], positioning it as an exemplary material choice for the application in the bone tissue engineering [31]. Nano hydroxyapatite (nHAP), an essential inorganic element of bone tissue, is distinguished by its close resemblance to natural bone tissues [32]. nHAP not only can bolster the proliferation and functionality of osteoblasts but also regulate osteoclast metabolism as well as revascularization, thereby accelerating bone repair and reconstruction [33]. Beyond the mineral component, the integration of bioactive molecules into the scaffolds is equally important for the constructing the bone repair-used scaffolds. Puerarin (Pur), an isoflavone derivative extracted from Kudzuvine root, is economical and widely available [34]. Numerous studies have shown that the Pur possesses the potential to hinder osteoclastogenesis by downregulating the MAPK/NF-κB signaling pathway [35]. In addition, it can stimulate osteogenic differentiation and bone formation via activating of the ERK1/2 and p38-MAPK signaling pathways [36]. Recently, with System Concept development, immune microenvironment, new blood vessel formation and energy metabolism have been accepted therapeutic target for promoting bone repair and generation. Therefore, advanced materials for combined photodynamic and immunotherapy are attracting more and more attentions [[37], [38], [39], [40]].

In this study, advanced composite scaffolds constructing with one layer of electrospun PHBV nanofiber membrane and one layer of UV-crosslinked MeGel hydrogel were designed and constructed, which was expected to combine the excellent biomimetic structure of electrospun nanofibers and the outstanding biological properties of hydrogel materials. In details, the upper PHBV nanofiber layer was expected to possess nanoscale pore structure, in order to effectively prevent the cell penetration and migration of surrounding cells and tissues without impeding nutrient diffusion. The under-hydrogel layer was expected to high porosity and low stiffness, in order to effectively promoting the cell penetration and migration from the surrounding environment. The combination of upper layer and under layer was anticipated to provide a relatively closed microenvironment for the osseointegration and osteanagenesis. In addition, nHAP and Pur were chosen and employed as bioactive components, which were encapsulated into the MeGel hydrogel layer of composite scaffolds, which were expected to provide multiple bioactivities, such as recruiting MSCs to increase the proportion of osteoblasts, decreasing osteoclast numbers, and promoting angiogenesis characteristics (Fig. 1). To validate these hypotheses, a rat calvarial defect model was established to explore the bone regeneration ability of our as-generated bi-layered PHBV/MeGel-HAP-Pur scaffolds in this study.Fig. 1Schematic representation of the fabrication process of bi-layered PHBV/MeGel-nHAP-Pur composite scaffolds constructing with one layer of PHBV nanofiber membrane and one layer of MeGel hydrogel and their application for repairing the rat calvarial defect model.

Materials and methods

Materials

PHBV (Mw = 30,000, 3% HV) was obtained from Hesu Times New Material Technology Company (Nanjing, China). 1,1,1,3,3,3-Hexafluora-2-propanol (HFIP, purity≥99.8%), gelatin (Type B), methacrylic anhydride, 2-hydroxy-1 (4- (hydroxyethyl) phenyl) -2-methyl-1-propane (Irgacure 2959), and nHAP were obtained from Aladdin Reagent (Shanghai, China). Puerarin was purchased from Zelang Medical Technology (Nanjing, China). Dulbecco's Modified Essential Medium (DMEM), phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), fetal bovine serum (FBS), and penicillin-streptomycin solution were purchased from Gibco (USA). Paraformaldehyde (PFA) and 10% chloral hydrate were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich (USA). Calcein-AM/PI double stain kit was purchased from Yeasen biotechnology (Shanghai, China). Immunohistochemical staining for osteocalcin (OCN), platelet endothelial cell adhesion molecule-1 (CD31), and fibrillar actin (F-actin) were purchased from Affinity Biosciences (Jiangsu, China). Alkaline phosphatase (ALP) kit was purchased from Solarbio (Beijing, China). Osteoclasts culture sample with tartrate resistant acid phosphatase (TRAP) staining kit was obtained from Amizona Scientific (Hangzhou, China).

Preparation of MeGel

MeGel was prepared as documented in our prior research [41]. In brief, gel was dissolved in distilled water to prepare 10% (w/v) homogeneous solution. Then, methacrylic anhydride (two times the liquid volume relative to the weight of the gel) were incrementally added into the above solution. After reaction for 1 h, the resultant MeGel solution underwent the dialysis process for 7 days and was then subjected to lyophilization, which were subsequently stored at −20 °C for future applications. The degree of methacryloyl substitution in the MeGel was approximately 44% [42].

Preparation of PHBV nanofibrous membranes

PHBV nanofibrous membranes were fabricated based on our collaborator's research [43]. In brief, PHBV was dissolved into HFIP to produce 12% (w/v) homogeneous spinning solution. The conditions for electrospinning were established as follows: the operational voltage of 15 kV, the solution feeding rate of 0.8 mL/h, and the needle-to-collector distance of 18 cm, respectively. Subsequently, the as-collected PHBV nanofiber membranes underwent vacuum drying for a duration of 72 h to eliminate residual solvents, which were stored at −20 °C for the future applications.

Fabrication of three different bi-layered composite scaffolds

The PHBV nanofiber mats were shaped into circles measuring 1 cm in diameter and subsequently placed into custom-designed silicone molds. Following this, a hydrogel precursor solution containing 30% (w/v) MeGel and Irgacure 2959 (33 μL/ml) was introduced into the silicone molds and exposed to ultravioled (UV) light (365 nm) at a distance of 5 cm for 60 s to form the PHBV/MeGel composite scaffold. Similarly, 20% nHAP and 3% Pur (relative to the weight of MeGel) were incorporated into the hydrogel solution. The final obtained bi-layered composite scaffolds (PHBV/MeGel, PHBV/MeGel-nHAP, and PHBV/MeGel-nHAP-Pur) were then lyophilized and stored at −20 °C.

Morphology and structural observation of three different bi-layered composite scaffolds

The micro-structure of three different bi-layered composite scaffolds (PHBV/MeGel, PHBV/MeGel-nHAP, and PHBV/MeGel-nHAP-Pur) were characterized by a scanning electron microscopy (SEM, TESCAN VEGA3, Czech Republic). For cross-sectional observation, the samples were first cut using a diamond wire saw. The cross-sectional surfaces were then sequentially polished with silicon carbide papers of decreasing grit sizes (from P400 to P2000) followed by final polishing with colloidal silica suspension (0.05 μm). Finally, selected three different images of each sample were analyzed by Image J software (NIH, USA).

The X-ray diffraction (XRD) spectroscopy of all samples was determined by a Rigaku Ultima IV machine with a Cu Kα radiation source (Japan). The operating voltage and current were kept at 40 kV and 40 A. Each sample was detected from 5 to 60 (2θ) with a scanning rate of 5 (2θ) per minute.

Elemental composition and mapping were obtained using an Energy-Dispersive X-ray Spectroscopy (EDS, Oxford X-Max 80 mm) detector attached to the SEM. Quantitative EDS point analysis and mapping were conducted at an accelerating voltage of 15 kV, a beam current of 1.0 nA, and a working distance of 10 mm. The acquisition time for each point analysis was 60 s. Quantitative results were derived using the Aztec software with standardless ZAF correction routines. Elemental maps were acquired with a step size of 0.2 μm.

Pore size analysis

The PHBV nanofiber membrane was cut into circles (with a diameter of 2 cm), and an ethanol solution was dropped onto the surface of the samples. The pore size distribution was measured using aperture analyzer (Topas PSM 165, Germany).

Mechanical and rheological properties of three different bi-layered composite scaffolds

First, cylindrical test samples of PHBV/MeGel, PHBV/MeGel-nHAP and PHBV/MeGel-nHAP-Pur were prepared. The compressive mechanical properties of the composite scaffolds were tested using a universal testing machine. During mechanical testing, a 100 N load sensor was used to compress 50% deformation at a compression rate of 2 mm/min until samples fragmented. Stress-strain curves and corresponding Young's modulus were then calculated.

Subsequently, the rheological characteristics of three hydrogel samples were evaluated using a rotary rheometer (Model: DHR 2; Manufacturer: Waters, USA) following gelation. Oscillation amplitude sweep testing was performed to detect the gel-to-sol transition point using test parameters with a frequency of 1 Hz, a strain range of 0.1-10 Hz, and number of steps of 10. A frequency sweep test was conducted over a frequency range of 0.1-10 Hz, with a number of steps of 25 steps.

Swelling property of three different bi-layered composite scaffolds

Following lyophilization, the initial weight W0 of composite scaffolds was recorded. Thereafter, the composite scaffolds were situated in PBS solution at 37 °C to observe swelling changes. The excess PBS on the surface of the composite scaffolds was wiped away using filter paper at 0, 1, 3, 6, 18, 24 h, and then the weight (Wi) of the sample was recorded at each interval. If the weight no longer changes, this indicates that swelling equilibrium was reached. In order to calculate the degree of swelling (W) exhibited by the composite scaffolds, the following formula (1) was employed:(1)Swellingratio(W)=(Wi−W0)/W0×100

Water retention properties of three different bi-layered composite scaffolds

Dried composite scaffold samples were dissolved in PBS to reach swelling equilibrium and its total water sorption was determined at room temperature. The sample was placed in beaker and the remaining mass of the composite scaffolds (PHBV/MeGel, PHBV/MeGel-nHAP, and PHBV/MeGel-nHAP-Pur composite scaffolds) was quantified at 0, 1, 3, 6, 12, 20, 24 h. The water retention properties Wr (%) was calculated according to Eq. (2):(2)Wr=(W0−Wt)/(W0−W)×100where W0 was weight of the composite scaffolds when they have reached swelling equilibrium, Wt was residual weight of the composite scaffolds following a specified interval of time, and W was lyophilization weight of the composite scaffold.

Degradation evaluation of bi-layered composite scaffolds

The degradation curves of PHBV/MeGel-nHAP-Pur composite scaffold in both PBS and Medium were evaluated. Firstly, the sample was dried, accurately weighed (W0) and then immersed in PBS and Medium with shaking at 80 rpm at 37 °C. At the predetermined time point (1, 3, 5 and 7 day), the sample was taken out, dried, and accurately weighed (Wt) again. The remaining weight of the sample (%) was calculated as ((W0-Wt)/W0)∗100.

In vitro drug release from PHBV nanofiber membrane

The release kinetics of Pur from PHBV nanofiber membrane was performed at 37 °C in both PBS and Medium. At predetermined time intervals (at point 0.5 h, 1 h, 2 h, 4 h, 6 h, 8 h, 12 h, 24 h, 48 h, 72 h, 120 h, and 168 h, respectively), supernatants were withdrawn and the same amount of fresh solution was added back to the release medium/PBS to maintain the sink condition. The Pur concentration was measured using HPLC-UV. The detection wavelength was set at 250 nm. The Pur stock standard of 1 mg/mL was prepared in methanol. System control and data acquisition were performed using the ChemStation software 4.03 Jan 27, 2020 (Agilent Technologies). The results were presented in terms of cumulative release as a function of time.

In vitro cell culture and characterization

Commercial human osteosarcoma cell line (Saos-2, Cell Bank, Shanghai) was co-cultured with the extracts of composite scaffolds to determine the biocompatibility of PHBV/MeGel, PHBV/MeGel-nHAP, and PHBV/MeGel-nHAP-Pur composite scaffolds. Each composite scaffold was soaked in 1 mL of commercial cell medium (high-glucose DMEM, 10% FBS and 1% penicillin-streptomycin mixture) at 37 °C for 24 h to obtain an extract. Subsequently, Saos-2 cells were seeded in 96 well plates (2.0 × 104) for 24 h, after which the medium was replaced with 100 μL of extracts from various scaffolds for 2 days. Saos-2 cells cultured with DMEM cell culture medium acted as the control group. The entire cell culture process was performed in an incubator at 37 °C and 5% CO2. Performing live/dead staining qualitative evaluation of cell viability. The fluorescence expression of live (490 nm, AM) and dead (545 nm, PI) cells were detected using a fluorescence microscope (Nikon, Japan). In addition to, cell viability was quantitatively evaluated according to cell counting kit (CCK-8) (Targetmol, USA) instructions [44].

Mice (8-week-old C57BL/6J) sacrificed by overdose anesthesia, which were sterilized by immersion in 75% alcohol for 5 min. Bone marrow cells (BMCs) were obtained from medullary cavity of the bilateral tibias/femurs of mice and cultured using DMEM. After 48 h, suspended cells were discarded and adherent cells were further cultured to obtain bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (BMSCs), the DMEM cell culture medium was replaced every 2 days. Fourteen days later, BMSCs were digested with 0.25% trypsin to induce osteoblast differentiation. In brief, BMSCs (1 × 105/mL) were seeded into 24-well plates and cultured with osteogenic induction medium that had been soaked with PHBV/MeGel, PHBV/MeGel-nHAP, and PHBV/MeGel-nHAP-Pur composite scaffolds. The cells cultured with osteogenic induction medium were employed as a control group, and all the culture medium was replaced every 2 days. After 7 days, the ALP activity was quantified using ALP color development kit [45].

Similarly, bone marrow mononuclear cells (BMMNCs) were also obtained from the medullary cavities of the bilateral tibias/femurs of mice. DMEM was used to culture the isolated BMMNCs. After 12 h, the suspended cells were transferred to 24 well plates (1 × 105 per well) and cultured in DMEM soaked with PHBV/MeGel, PHBV/MeGel-nHAP, and PHBV/MeGel-nHAP-Pur composite scaffolds. Additionally, receptor activator of nuclear factor-κB ligand (RANKL) (40 ng/mL) and macrophage colony-stimulating factor (M-CSF) (40 ng/mL) were added to promote osteoclast differentiation. The cells were cultured with DMEM that was supplemented with RANKL and M-CSF, which employed as control group. The culture medium was refreshed every 3 days and was supplemented with RANKL and M-CSF. After a week, cells were fixed using 4% PFA for TRAP activity staining [46].

Matrigel (Corning, USA) was added to the 24-well plates and allowed to gel by incubating at 37 °C for 30 min. Human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs) (1.0 × 105) were then spread all over the 24 well plate and continued to be incubated for 2 h. Then, the cells were treated with DMEM soaked with PHBV/MeGel, PHBV/MeGel-nHAP, and PHBV/MeGel-nHAP-Pur composite scaffolds. After 6 h of incubation, cells were then image captured using an optical microscope (Nikon, Japan) [47].

Animal study and characterization

The Sprague Dawley (SD) rats (250-300 g, 8 weeks old) were utilized in this study, in which the calvarial defect models (diameter of the injured core area is approximately 5 mm and the depth is 2.5 mm) were created to ascertain the impact of PHBV/MeGel, PHBV/MeGel-HAP, and PHBV/MeGel-HAP-Pur composite scaffolds on the bone regeneration and cranial repairing in vivo. All the animal experiments were conducted in accordance with the guidelines established by the animal research committee of Qingdao University (QDU-AEC-2024214). The rats were randomly divided into four groups (n = 6), comprising a control group and three experimental groups, i.e., PHBV/MeGel composite scaffold, PHBV/MeGel-nHAP composite scaffold, and PHBV/MeGel-nHAP-Pur composite scaffold. After anesthesia and disinfection, a 4.5 mm diameter skull defect was created via drilling. The various composite scaffolds, each with a diameter of 4.5 mm, were then applied to the defect site. For the control group, no further treatment was conducted. Lastly, the periosteum and skin were stitched together in layers. The surgical site was disinfected with iodophor once daily for the first 3 days after surgery.

The rats were sacrificed at 4 weeks and 8 weeks, respectively. The cranium of each rat was collected for micro-CT (OsiriX, Bernex, Switzerland) observation to evaluate the new bone formation [48]. The quantitative analysis of skull repair rates was conducted using Image J software. The skull repair rate was calculated using Eq. (3).(3)Skullrepairrate(%)=(S0−St)/S0×100Where S0 is used to represent the original wound area, and St is used to represent the wound area on dayt.

The regenerated tissues in four different groups were evaluated by a histological staining. In brief, the regenerated tissues were fixed with 4% PFA for 24 h, after which they were embedded in paraffin following decalcification. Subsequently, the cross-sections were prepared with a thickness of 5 μm. After that, some different staining assays including standard hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) stain kit and masson's trichrome (MT) (Solarbio, Beijing) were used to observe the slices.

The slices harvested at week 4 and 8 were also stained with osteocalcin (OCN) (1:200), F-actin (1:200), CD31 (1:200) using an immunofluorescent staining for each group. The stained specimens were canned and analyzed with a fluorescent microscope (Nikon A1 MP, Japan). The quantitative analysis of OCN and CD31was quantified with an Image J software and calculated using the following Eq. (4).(4)Relativefluorescentintensity(foldofcontrol)=FIe/FIcWhere FIc represents the fluorescent intensity of OCN and CD31 in control group, and FIe represents the fluorescent intensity in the tthree composite scaffold groups.

The inflammatory factors in serum of four different groups were evaluated using two different ELISA kits. At 8 weeks post-surgery, the rat heart blood in each group was drawn and centrifuged at 3000 rpm for 15 min to collect the upper serum. The process was carried out according to the instructions of interleukin-1 beta (IL-1β) kit and tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) kits (Sizhengbai Biotechnology, Suzhou), and the absorbance at 450 nm was detected with a multifunctional micro-plate reader (BioTek instruments, USA).

Statistical analysis

All the quantitative data were calculated and reported as mean ± standard deviation. For every test, there were at least three duplicates conducted for every experimental group. A value of p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results and discussion

Fabrication and characterization of three different bi-layered PHBV/MeGel composite scaffolds loading with or without nHAP and Pur

The three different bi-layered PHBV/MeGel composite scaffolds loading with or without nHAP and Pur, named as PHBV/MeGel, PHBV/MeGel-nHAP, and PHBV/MeGel-nHAP-Pur, were designed through a combination of electrospinning and UV-crosslinking methods. Both of the digital photographs and SEM images (Fig. 2A–C) indicated that all the three composite scaffolds possessed obviously bi-layered structures with dense and compact combination between the upper electrospun nanofiber layer and bottom hydrogel layer. As shown in Fig. 2D, the PHBV nanofiber layer possessed a dense structure, with an average pore size of 1.09 ± 0.28 μm, which could be served as a barrier membrane for effectively block the infiltration of adjacent soft tissues, thus providing relatively stable space for the bottom hydrogel layer of the composite scaffolds. In comparision, the three different hydrogel layers loading with or without nHAP and Pur exhibited a loose porous structure with obviously larger pore sizes, and the pore sizes of MeGel hydrogel, MeGel-nHAP hydrogel, and MeGel-nHAP-Pur hydrogel were 57.80 ± 26.23 μm, 46.00 ± 12.79 μm and 48.70 ± 17.66 μm, respectively (Fig. 2E–G). Since the diameters of most normal cells range from 10 to 20 μm [49], the cells could penetrate these three different hydrogel layers. It should be noticed that the addition of nHAP and Pur had little effect on the pore sizes of as-formed hydrogels, which had no effects on the cell infiltration from the surrounding bone tissues. The previous studies showed that the scaffolds with large pore sizes facilitated the transfer of nutrients and metabolic waste between cells and their surroundings, while also encouraging the development of new blood vessels during the healing process of bone defects [50,51]. Additionally, data from Fig. 2H showed that nHAp particles are uniformly dispersed within the porous network of the hydrogel. As shown in Fig. 2I, the XRD pattern exhibited sharp and well-defined diffraction peaks at specific 2θ angles (notably at approximately 25.9°, 31.8°, 32.9°, 34.1°, and 39.8°). The presence of such distinct Bragg reflections, as opposed to a broad amorphous halo, is a direct indicator of long-range atomic order and thus, good crystallinity. Rheological and mechanical properties of three different bi-layered PHBV/MeGel composite scaffolds loading with or without nHAP and Pur

The appropriate mechanical properties of bone scaffolds provides adequate physical stability, as well as offers physiological cue that induces the bone formation [52]. The hydrogel-based scaffolds prepared from the gel components alone are extremely fragile and fail to satisfy the criteria of mechanical properties for bone tissue engineering usage [31]. Our previous study showed that mechanical properties of gel-based hydrogel were significantly improved by introducing methacrylic anhydride for substitution [42]. Moreover, the modified MeGel could be fashioned into hydrogels via the UV crosslinking, and the process was extremely convenient and controllable. In addition, some existing studies showed that incorporating some reinforcement substances like HAP and carbon nanotubes into gel can enhance the mechanical properties of as-generated hydrogel scaffolds [33,53]. The rheological properties of MeGel hydrogel, MeGel-nHAP hydrogel, and MeGel-nHAP-Pur hydrogel were firstly investigated. As the oscillation frequency increased, the values of G′ (storage modulus) were consistently greater than G″ (loss modulus), showing well elasticity and mechanical stability of all the hydrogel (Fig. 3A). Within the human body, the bone ECM is in a dynamic mechanical environment at all times. Some previous studies have demonstrated that the materials capable of swift stress relaxation were more effective at fostering the proliferation of bone BMSCs, fibroblasts, and other cells [54,55]. Subsequently, the compressive mechanical properties of those three different composite scaffolds including PHBV/MeGel, PHBV/MeGel-nHAP, and PHBV/MeGel-nHAP-Pur composite scaffolds were assessed. Our previous research also indicated a positive correlation between MeGel concentration and compressive modulus, ranging from 6.4 kPa (4% MeGel) to 87.6 kPa (16% MeGel) [56]. Increased mechanical strength was attributed to the higher crosslinking density resulting from the elevated MeGel concentration, which stabilized the hydrogel's internal structure [57]. Considering the characteristics of bone tissue, as the main load-bearing organ of the human body, the bone tissue possesses robust mechanical properties [58].

In following study, nHAP was added in the scaffolds to accelerate bone formation. As shown in Fig. 3C–D, the compressive stress-strain curve indicated that the mechanical strength of the hydrogel increased with added nHAP. In detail, nHAP-free hydrogel shows low compressive strength. With subsequent nHAP loading, the compressive strength of the hydrogels gradually increased hinting that the incorporation of nHAP in the hydrogel showed significant improvement in the mechanical strength. Combined with the above-mentioned SEM images, EDS images and XRD patterns, it was found that the nHAP was uniformly filled in the porous structure of hydrogel.

Swelling and water retention properties, and biocompatibility of three different bi-layered PHBV/MeGel composite scaffolds loading with or without nHAP and Pur

Some existing studies indicated that the moist environment facilitates the cell migration and proliferation, thus accelerating the defect healing [59]. Therefore, an ideal bone scaffold should also possess appropriate swelling capacity and water retention capacity. The swelling and water retention ability of three different composite scaffolds were characterized. As shown in Fig. 4A, all the PHBV/MeGel, PHBV/MeGel-nHAP, and PHBV/MeGel-nHAP-Pur scaffolds exhibited great hydrophilicity, which reached swelling equilibrium at approximately 18 h, with the swelling rates of 316.4 ± 9.1%, 309.5 ± 6.7%, and 307.3 ± 1.4%, respectively. As depicted in Fig. 4B, the three composite scaffolds had similar water retention capacities. The biocompatibility is of vital importance for constructing tissue engineering-used scaffolds, which directly determine the cell activities on the scaffolds [60]. Saos-2, a commercial human osteosarcoma cell line, was chosen to investigate the biocompatibility of three different bi-layered PHBV/MeGel composite scaffolds loading with or without nHAP and Pur. As shown in Fig. 4E, all the three different composite scaffolds exhibited no cytotoxicity, which were similar with the control group. In details, almost no dead cells were observed in each group. Moreover, the CCK8 assay outcomes validated that the cell survival rate of PHBV/MeGel, PHBV/MeGel-nHAP, and PHBV/MeGel-nHAP-Pur composite scaffolds extracts were 100 ± 0.8%, 99 ± 0.5%, and 100 ± 1.4%, respectively (Fig. 4F), which suggested that all the three different composite scaffolds had excellent cytocompatibility.

PHBV/MeGel-nHAP-Pur bilayered composite scaffolds accelerated the repair of rat calvarial defects

A rat calvarial defect model with a 4.5 mm diameter circular defect was established to explore the multifaceted regulatory potential for the bone repair of three different bilayered composite scaffolds, i.e., PHBV/MeGel, PHBV/MeGel-nHAP, and PHBV/MeGel-nHAP-Pur composite scaffolds. A defected group with no intervention treatment was served as the control (Fig. 5A). Due to the limited capacity for self-repair in the extensive bone defects, the control group exhibited minimal new bone formation in the absence of additional therapeutic interventions. As expected, the Micro-CT images of skull defect site revealed that the three composite scaffold groups demonstrated the markedly quicker bone healing, accompanied by varying degrees of new osteogenesis (Fig. 5B and C). Micro-CT shows the formation of new bone from the top view, side view and coronal section. Noticeably, the volume of new bone and bone mineral density in PHBV/MeGel-nHAP-Pur group were significantly higher than those in the control group at the 4th and 8th weeks after surgery. The data analysis (Fig. 5D and E) revealed that there was a notable reduction in the area of cranial defects across all the surgical groups after a period of 4 weeks and 8 weeks post-treatment. Compared to control (18.19 ± 0.83%), the PHBV/MeGel group, PHBV/MeGel-nHAP group, and PHBV/MeGel-nHAP-Pur group reached to 48.29 ± 0.48%, 55.92 ± 0.80%, and 60.88 ± 1.73%, respectively (Fig. 5D). Following a period of 8 weeks post-treatment, the control group demonstrated the bone regeneration rate of 47.07 ± 0.47%, while the group treated solely with PHBV/MeGel scaffolds showed a rate of 65.74 ± 3.2%. The PHBV/MeGel-nHAP group exhibited a more advanced contraction at 72.18 ± 0.94%. Notably, the PHBV/MeGel-nHAP-Pur group achieved nearly complete bone repair, with a rate of 99.25 ± 0.37% (Fig. 5E). The enhanced regeneration outcome could be attributed to the dual-layer structure of the scaffold, where the upper PHBV layer served as a protective barrier against the encroachment of soft tissues, and the lower hydrogel layer functioned as a healing-promoting materials to promote the infiltration of diverse osteogenic cells from the periphery of the defect towards its core.Fig. 5(A) Illustrative representation of the skull defect along with photographic documentation of the surgical intervention. In the initial phase of bone injury repair, there were a greater number of areas stained blue. As bone injury repair progressed, the red-stained areas gradually increased, indicating that the bone tissue was gradually maturing [16]. Therefore, the degree of maturation of regenerated bone tissue was also evaluated using MT staining. From Fig. 6B, it could be visually observed that the PHBV/MeGel-nHAP-Pur scaffold group presented the largest red-stained areas, and the regenerated bone tissue formed a stratified structure, suggesting that a large area of mature bone tissue had formed at the defect site. As shown in Fig. 7A, all the PHBV/MeGel, PHBV/MeGel-nHAP, and PHBV/MeGel-nHAP-Pur scaffolds displayed less OCN expression during the early phase of bone tissue formation, with OCN fluorescence intensity of 1.10 fold, 1.17 fold, and 1.28 fold higher than the control, respectively (Fig. 7C). PHBV/MeGel-nHAP-Pur bilayered composite scaffolds promoted the vascularization of regenerated bone tissues in vivo

An abundant vascular network delivering essential nutrients and oxygen to the bone repair area. At the same time, angiogenesis mainly depends on the movement and growth of endothelial cells. To further validated the hypothesis that three different bilayered composite scaffolds, i.e., PHBV/MeGel, PHBV/MeGel-nHAP, and PHBV/MeGel-nHAP-Pur composite scaffolds, favored the vascularization, an immunofluorescence staining was used to visualize CD31, a marker protein associated with neovascularization. PHBV/MeGel-nHAP-Pur bilayered composite scaffolds reduced the levels of inflammatory factors in vivo

Bone healing undergoes three overlapping processes: inflammatory stage, repair stage, and remodeling stage. During the final phases of bone healing, the balance between osteoblast and osteoclast activity results in lamellar bone deposition and the breakdown of bone tissue. Subsequently, bone tissue restores its original structure and mechanical properties through remodeling [66]. During this period, there is a persistent high expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as IL-1β and TNFα by acting on immune cells and bone repair cells [24]. As shown in Fig. 9A–B, all the PHBV/MeGel, PHBV/MeGel-nHAP, and PHBV/MeGel-nHAP-Pur composite scaffolds showed significant anti-inflammatory effects after a period of 8 weeks post-treatment. Potential mechanisms of PHBV/MeGel-nHAP-Pur bilayered composite scaffolds for promoting the osseointegration, osteanagenesis, and vascularization

Bone reconstruction is a complex physiological process involving a multitude of different cell types [2]. In particular, the osteoblasts provide structural support for bone regeneration by secreting ECM proteins such as ALP, OCN, and osteopontin, thereby facilitating the bone formation [67]. On the contrary, the osteoclasts are primarily responsible for the resorption and breakdown of bone tissues [42]. In other words, the overactivity of osteoclasts or the diminished function of osteoblasts can lead to the reduced bone density and delayed fracture healing [68]. Based on the animal studies, the PHBV/MeGel-nHAP-Pur composite scaffolds showed excellent pro-healing effects for repairing the rat calvarial defect models. In this section, the possible mechanisms by which the PHBV/MeGel-nHAP-Pur scaffolds enhanced bone regeneration were further explored through the in vitro cell experiments. Both of BMSCs and BMMNCs were isolated to assess the impacts of the three different scaffolds on the osteoblast and osteoclast generation. The ALP staining indicated that all the three different composite scaffolds promoted the differentiation of BMSCs into osteoblasts (Fig. 10A). The angiogenesis assay is an effective method for evaluating the vascular generation potential. The effects of three different scaffolds on the tube formation of HUVECs were investigated and the total length of neovascularization was also quantified. As shown in Fig. 10C–F, a dense capillary network (4130.7 ± 28.8 μm) was observed in the PHBV/MeGel-nHAP-Pur group. Combined with the results of immunofluorescence staining of OCN (Fig. 7) and CD31 (Fig. 8) in rat calvarial defect model, we could observe that the PHBV/MeGel composite scaffold alone promote the formation of osteoblasts and secrete OCN and ALP (Fig. 10A–D), but had no significant promoting effects on the early angiogenesis (Fig. 8A). The scaffold primarily relied on its physical support to facilitate the adhesion and migration of endothelial cells during the later stages of bone repair (Fig. 10C). Moreover, it didn't inhibit osteoclastogenesis (Fig. 10B–E). Under the co-regulation of nHAP and Pur, the PHBV/MeGel-nHAP-Pur scaffolds comprehensively regulated the osteoblast function, inhibited the osteoclast number as well as promoted the vascularization. This indicated that the synergistic presence of nHAP and Pur as well as PHBV/MeGel matrix could better mimic the native bone ECM, making the composite scaffold an ideal choice for the cranial bone repair.

Conclusions

A series of bi-layered PHBV/MeGel composite scaffolds loading with or without nHAP and Pur were successfully designed and constructed via electrospinning and UV-crosslinking techniques. The upper PHBV nanofiber layer possessed dense pores with small pore sizes, which could effectively stop the invasion of scar tissues. The under MeGel hydrogel layer had porous structure with large pore sizes, which presented outstanding water absorption ability and recruitment of osteogenesis-related cell adhesion characteristic. In in vivo study, calvarial defect models with Sprague Dawley rats were created to ascertain the impact of multifunctional scaffolds. The PHBV/MeGel-nHAP-Pur composite scaffolds demonstrated a largest healing coverage of 99.25 ± 0.37% at week 8 following surgery. Specifically, the quality of regenerated bone tissue was extremely high, showing significant improvements in collagen fibrils and angiogenesis, and increased the expression of osteogenic markers as well as decreased the expression of inflammatory factors. The in vitro cell results revealed that the exceptional effects of PHBV/MeGel-nHAP-Pur composite scaffolds on promoting osseointegration and osteanagenesis may be related to stimulating the differentiation of BMCs into osteoblasts, inhibiting the differentiation of BMMNCs into osteoclasts, and boosting the formation of blood vessels in HUVECs. Our present study suggests that the bi-layered PHBV/MeGel-nHAP-Pur composite scaffolds with multiple bio-functions show huge potential for the repair and regeneration of large-sized bone defects.

Patient-reported outcomes following chest masculinization surgery in transmasculine individuals: A prospective study
Authors: Yannick Sprunger, Kevin Serror, Raffaele Aguglia, Guillaume Mulier, Olivier Mathieu, Jad Bissar, Marc Chaouat, David Boccara
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundChest masculinization surgery is a cornerstone of gender-affirming care for transmasculine individuals, with well-documented benefits on body image and quality of life. However, prospective data using validated patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs) remain limited.MethodsThis prospective longitudinal study was conducted in accordance with the STROBE guidelines. Adult transmasculine patients undergoing chest masculinization surgery at Hôpital Saint-Louis (Paris, France) between April 2022 and October 2024 were invited to complete the BODY-Q questionnaire preoperatively and at 6 months postoperatively. Demographic, morphological, and surgical variables were collected. Pre–post comparisons were performed, and associations between outcomes and covariates were explored.ResultsForty-four transmasculine patients met the inclusion criteria. Mean age at surgery was 24.1 ± 5.6 years. Significant improvements were observed across all BODY-Q appearance and health-related quality-of-life domains (all P < 0.0001). For example, chest satisfaction increased from 24.0% ± 16.0% to 82.0% ± 18%, and psychological well-being from 24.0% ± 22.0% to 62.0% ± 19%. No statistically significant associations were identified between postoperative BODY-Q scores and surgical technique, BMI, or demographic variables; however, these findings should be interpreted with caution given the limited sample size. Postoperative expectations remained stable, suggesting realistic preoperative counseling.ConclusionsChest masculinization surgery was associated with marked improvements in appearance-related satisfaction and health-related quality of life at 6 months. Despite limitations related to sample size, response rate, and follow-up duration, these findings support the substantial positive impact of this procedure on patient-reported outcomes in transmasculine individuals.

Introduction

Transgender individuals experience a gender identity that differs from their assigned biological sex,1 which can result in gender dysphoria2 — A state of being characterized by distress or discomfort as a result of this incongruence.3 This distress may significantly impact mental health and quality of life,4,5 underscoring the importance of accessible and effective medical interventions.

Increasing societal awareness and acceptance has led to a growing demand for gender dysphoria treatment.6 Options include hormone therapy, facial surgery, genital surgery, body contouring, and gender-affirming chest procedures such as mastectomy or breast augmentation.7 Among these, chest masculinization surgery is a cornerstone treatment for many transmasculine individuals, providing both cosmetic and psychological benefits.8, 9, 10, 11

Chest masculinization surgery has shown significant effects in reducing dysphoria, increasing body image, improving sexual health, and promoting general well-being.12 For many individuals, the absence of surgery can result in significant emotional distress, difficulties with daily activities, mood disorders,4,5 lack of sexual satisfaction13 and physical discomfort due to prolonged use of binding apparel.14,15 While non-surgical measures, such as compression garments or hormone therapy, offer partial relief, they are often insufficient for long-term results.16 Thus, chest masculinization surgery is a crucial component of the gender affirmation process for numerous individuals.17, 18, 19, 20

The BODY-Q module, originally designed to assess outcomes in obesity therapy and body contouring,21 has demonstrated efficacy in analyzing patient-reported outcomes. Although utilized in over 50 research for surgeries like body contouring, gynecomastia surgery or bariatric surgery, its application within the transgender population is still limited.22,23 This study leverages the BODY-Q instrument to address this gap and explore its relevance in assessing outcomes following chest masculinization surgery.

Numerous studies underscore the positive impact of gender-affirming thoracic surgery on transgender individuals’ well-being.8, 9, 10, 11 However, a lack of detailed research exists regarding satisfaction metrics and quality-of-life outcomes specific to this population. This study aims to bridge this gap by using validated patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs) from an expanded panel of BODY-Q questionnaires to evaluate outcomes and better align surgical practices with patient expectations and needs. In addition, we investigate whether factors such as surgical technique, body mass index, or age influence postoperative satisfaction and well-being. By doing so, we aim to support a more nuanced, personalized, and evidence-based model of gender-affirming care that centers the patient experience.

Patients and methods

Study design and ethics

This prospective longitudinal observational study was conducted from April 2022 to October 2024 in accordance with the STROBE guidelines. Ethical approval was obtained from the Multidisciplinary Ethics Committee of Saint-Louis Hospital (IRB ID: 2025-011). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants.

Participants

Eligible participants were adults (≥18 years) undergoing chest masculinization surgery after standardized psychiatric and endocrinological assessment. Exclusion criteria included age <18 years and inability to complete questionnaires in French. All consecutive eligible patients during the study period were invited to participate.

Surgical techniques

Procedures were performed by a specialized team using one of three techniques selected based on breast size, skin excess, and patient preference: Double incision mastectomy with free nipple graft (DIM-FNG), Double incision mastectomy with inferior pedicle (DIM-IP) or Hemi-periareolar technique.

Data collection

Patients completed the BODY-Q questionnaire 1 day preoperatively and at 6 months postoperatively via secure email. Demographic data (age, age at coming out), morphological data (BMI, binder use, breast size), and surgical details were recorded prospectively.

Sample questions included

•Preoperative: “How satisfied or dissatisfied are you with your chest? How do you look in the mirror undressed?”•Postoperative: “How satisfied or dissatisfied have you been with how your scars look; the location of your scars?”

Outcomes

BODY-Q scores were calculated using the Q-score program, converting raw survey scores (1–5 scale) into standardized 0–100 metrics, with higher scores representing more favorable outcomes.24

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables are described using the median, first and third quartiles, and minimum and maximum values. Categorical variables are described using counts and percentages; for binary variables, only one category is reported.

The unadjusted difference between postoperative and preoperative BODY-Q scores was assessed using a paired Z-test, equivalent to testing whether the mean difference differed from zero. Associations between the postoperative–preoperative difference and covariates were explored using univariable linear regression models for each BODY-Q domain measured pre- and postoperatively.

Comparisons were performed using Kruskal–Wallis tests or Fisher’s exact tests, as appropriate. A two-sided type I error rate of 5% was applied for all analyses.

A total of 44 patients completed both the preoperative and 6-month postoperative BODY-Q questionnaires and were included in the final analysis. No significant changes were observed in the Expectations domain with scores remaining stable at 66.0% pre- and postoperatively (P = 0.9033).

Furthermore, no statistically significant associations were observed between post-operative BODY-Q scores and covariates including surgical method, BMI, or other previously mentioned data although the study may be underpowered to detect small differences.

Postoperative complications and revision procedures were not systematically assessed as part of the present study protocol and were therefore not included in the analysis. Consequently, no conclusions can be drawn regarding complication rates or their potential association with patient-reported outcomes.

Discussion

This study highlights the unquestionable benefits of chest masculinization surgery for transmasculine individuals experiencing gender dysphoria.25 The notable enhancements in BODY-Q scores across many categories, such as body satisfaction, chest satisfaction,26 nipple satisfaction, and scar satisfaction,27 highlight the surgery’s essential contribution to improving patient-reported outcomes concerning appearance as described in the literature.25, 26, 27 Additionally, psychological, social, and sexual well-being were all also markedly improved postoperatively demonstrating the positive impact of this gender-affirming procedure on the lives of transmasculine patients which reinforces the findings of a recent study by de Schoulepnikoff et al.28 Indeed, and concerning for example the Psychosocial Well-being BREAST-Q Questionnaire, they obtained significantly higher scores at 4 months postoperative compared with preoperative, with a score of 73.38% ± 3.29% 4 months postoperative versus 37.48% ± 4.18% preoperative (average ± SEM, P < 0.0001). However, and in an interesting way, the results did not show significantly higher scores at 12 months postoperative compared with 4 months postoperative. In the same way, they obtained significantly higher scores concerning Chest Satisfaction BREAST-Q Questionnaire at 4 months postoperative compared with preoperative, with a score of 78.10% ± 2.73% 4 months postoperative versus 24.57% ± 2.71% preoperative (average ± SEM, P < 0.0001) with no higher scores at 12 months postoperative.

Our findings align with this temporal pattern, supporting the use of a 6-month follow-up as an appropriate time point for capturing early and clinically meaningful changes in patient-reported outcomes. Nevertheless, longer follow-up may reveal additional evolution related to scar maturation, social integration, and longer-term psychosocial adaptation.

The preoperative use of binders by the majority of patients (85%) and the substantial distress associated with their prolonged use14 underscore the urgent need for surgical intervention for many individuals. While hormone therapy provides critical initial support in the transition process,16 this study affirms that surgical intervention addresses distinct and significant facets of gender dysphoria that non-surgical treatments cannot entirely alleviate.

The absence of significant correlations between BODY-Q scores and factors like as BMI, surgical method, or other demographic variables suggests that chest masculinization surgery may provide favorable outcomes across different patient profiles. However, these findings should be interpreted cautiously given the limited sample size and the potential lack of statistical power to detect subtle differences.

This finding reinforces the safety and efficacy of the procedure, regardless of patient-specific characteristics, and supports its role as a cornerstone of gender-affirming care. Interestingly, even in cases involving more extensive scarring—such as with double incision techniques—no significant difference in post-operative satisfaction was observed. This suggests that the psychological and functional benefits of chest masculinization may outweigh concerns related to scar burden, highlighting the profound importance of gender congruence over aesthetic outcomes alone.

Notably, the BODY-Q Expectations domain showed no significant change between pre-and postoperative scores. This suggests that transmasculine patients may approach surgery with realistic expectations, potentially shaped by thorough preoperative counseling and the informed consent process. Ensuring that patients have clear, evidence-based information about surgical outcomes is critical for maintaining satisfaction and managing expectations.

These observations are consistent with a growing body of literature demonstrating high satisfaction rates, low incidence of regret, and significant improvements in various quality-of-life measures for FTM patients undergoing chest masculinization. In a 2004 follow-up study, De Cuypere et al. assessed patient satisfaction 1 year or more after gender affirmation surgery. Among the 14 FTM individuals who underwent mastectomy, 35.7% reported being very satisfied, 42.8% were satisfied, and 21.4% felt neutral about the outcome. Notably, none expressed dissatisfaction. These results are consistent with a 2007 review by Nelson et al., which surveyed 12 post-surgical patients: none reported regret, 10 strongly recommended the surgery, and one recommended it.

Those studies consistently highlight positive impacts on body satisfaction and situational dysphoria following surgery. Notably, these studies underscore improvements in daily life, social interactions, and self-esteem, though metrics and tools used vary widely across research.

Building on these findings, our study contributes further to the understanding of post-operative outcomes, while also highlighting key limitations and areas for future improvement.

Postoperative complications and revision procedures were not systematically assessed in this study, precluding analysis of their potential impact on patient-reported outcomes. The relatively modest sample size also limits statistical power for detecting small associations, particularly in subgroup analyses. Finally, the 6-month follow-up duration does not capture long-term outcomes, including late scar maturation or evolving psychosocial experiences.

Expanding future studies to include multi-center cohorts, extended follow-up periods, and broader demographic representation would provide a more thorough comprehension of patient experiences and outcomes.

Despite these limitations, the strengths of this study include its prospective design, the use of paired analyses in which each patient served as their own control, and the application of a validated PROM specifically adapted to chest masculinization surgery. Together, these elements strengthen the internal validity of the findings and support the conclusion that chest masculinization surgery is associated with substantial short-term improvements in patient-centered outcomes.

Dark light shining on B → K ( ∗ ) ​ E miss B\to K^{(*)}E_{\rm miss}
Patrick D. Bolton, Jernej F. Kamenik, Martín Novoa-Brunet

Abstract
Recent Belle II data on B + → K + ​ E miss B^{+}\to K^{+}E_{\rm miss} show an excess consistent with a two-body decay involving a light invisible particle with mass around 2.1 ​ GeV 2.1\,\mathrm{GeV} . We present a UV-complete explanation based on a Higgsed U ​ ( 1 ) ′ U(1)^{\prime} gauge symmetry with a light vector boson Z ′ Z^{\prime} and a vector-like top partner, which naturally enhances b → s b\to s transitions. While the minimal model can reproduce the required B → K ( ∗ ) ​ Z ′ B\to K^{(*)}Z^{\prime} rate, it is excluded by LHCb searches for resonant dimuon decays due to unavoidable loop-induced couplings of Z ′ Z^{\prime} to charged leptons. We show that a minimal extension with an additional light U ​ ( 1 ) ′ U(1)^{\prime} -charged singlet fermion allows Z ′ Z^{\prime} to decay dominantly invisibly, evades existing constraints coming also from dark photon and collider searches as well as Higgs measurements, and can simultaneously account for the Belle II excess and the observed dark matter abundance through resonant thermal freeze-out.

Rare flavour-changing neutral current (FCNC) transitions in the quark sector provide a powerful window into physics beyond the Standard Model (SM). The loop- and CKM-suppressed rates predicted in the SM allow in many cases to probe new short distance dynamics much above the TeV scale. II
Minimal Model

Here, we consider a minimal aligned
U(1)^{\prime}
model which introduces a vector-like quark
T^{\prime}({\bf 3},{\bf 1},2/3,q^{\prime})
and a SM singlet scalar
\Phi({\bf 1},{\bf 1},0,q^{\prime})

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle\supset-\frac{1}{4}B^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}B^{\prime\mu\nu}-\frac{\epsilon_{B}}{2}B_{\mu\nu}B^{\prime\mu\nu}+\big|D_{\mu}\Phi\big|^{2}-V(H,\Phi)

\displaystyle\hskip 15.00002pt+\bar{T}^{\prime}(i\not{D}-M_{T})T^{\prime}-\Big[y_{T}^{r}\bar{T^{\prime}}\Phi u_{Rr}^{\prime}+\text{h.c.}\Big]\,,

(1)

with
D_{\mu}\supset i\tilde{g}q^{\prime}B_{\mu}^{\prime}
and
B_{\mu\nu}^{\prime}=\partial_{\mu}B_{\nu}^{\prime}-\partial_{\nu}B_{\mu}^{\prime}
, where
B_{\mu}^{\prime}
is the gauge field associated with the
U(1)^{\prime}
,
B_{\mu}
is the
U(1)_{Y}
hypercharge gauge field,
H
the
SU(2)_{L}
Higgs doublet, and
u^{\prime}_{Rr}
the up-type right-handed quark with generation index
r=u,c,t
. Additional terms in the scalar potential are

\displaystyle V(H,\Phi)=-\tilde{\mu}^{2}|\Phi|^{2}+\tilde{\lambda}|\Phi|^{4}+\lambda^{\prime}|\Phi|^{2}(H^{\dagger}H)\,.

(2)

The complex scalar
\Phi
obtains a vacuum expectation value (vev)

U(1)^{\prime}

Z^{\prime}

M_{Z^{\prime}}=\tilde{g}q^{\prime}\tilde{v}

\lambda\,,\tilde{\lambda}\,,4\lambda\tilde{\lambda}-\lambda^{\prime 2}\geq 0
. Expanding the scalar fields around the scalar potential minimum as
H=(\chi^{+}\penalty\ (v+h^{\prime}+i\chi^{\prime})/\sqrt{2})^{T}
and
\Phi=(\tilde{v}+\phi^{\prime}+i\eta^{\prime})/\sqrt{2}
, the extended CP-even scalar mass matrix in the broken phase is

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle\supset-\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}h^{\prime}&\phi^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}2\lambda v^{2}&\lambda^{\prime}v\tilde{v}\\
\lambda^{\prime}v\tilde{v}&2\tilde{\lambda}\tilde{v}^{2}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}h^{\prime}\\
\phi^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(14)

We diagonalise it via the unitary rotation
h^{\prime}=c_{\phi}h+s_{\phi}\phi
and
\phi^{\prime}=-s_{\phi}h+c_{\phi}\phi
, with
\tan 2\theta_{\phi}=\lambda^{\prime}v\tilde{v}/(\tilde{\lambda}\tilde{v}^{2}-\lambda v^{2})
.
The physical scalar masses are then related to the quartic couplings and vevs as
(M_{h}M_{\phi})^{2}=(4\lambda\tilde{\lambda}-\lambda^{\prime 2})(v\tilde{v})^{2}
and
M_{h}^{2}+M_{\phi}^{2}=2(\lambda v^{2}+\tilde{\lambda}\tilde{v}^{2})
, and therefore
\lambda
and
\tilde{\lambda}
can be expressed in terms of
M_{h}=125.2\penalty\ \text{GeV}
,
v=246\penalty\ \text{GeV}
and inputs
\{M_{\phi},\tilde{g}q^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}\}
. The inequality
2\lambda^{\prime}v\tilde{v}\leq|M_{\phi}^{2}-M_{h}^{2}|
is furthermore implied.

At one-loop order,
\lambda^{\prime}
receives the RGE contributions
d\lambda^{\prime}/d\ln\mu=-3(y_{t}y_{T}^{t})^{2}/4\pi^{2}
, where we retain only the dominant box diagram expression. Fixing
\lambda^{\prime}(\Lambda)=0
in the UV, the quartic coupling at scales below
m_{T}
can then be estimated, including threshold contributions, for
m_{t}\ll m_{T}
as

\displaystyle\lambda^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{3(y_{t}y_{T}^{t})^{2}}{8\pi^{2}}\bigg[\ln\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{m_{T}^{2}}+1\bigg]\,,

(15)

which yields
\lambda^{\prime}=2\times 10^{-2}(y_{T}^{t}/0.1)^{2}(L_{T}/55)
, assuming
y_{t}\approx\sqrt{2}m_{t}/v
. In this work, we will take
\lambda^{\prime}(\Lambda)=0
at the scale
\Lambda=10^{16}
GeV and use the value of
\lambda^{\prime}
generated by the running to low scales, rather than treating it as a free parameter. Consequently, in much of the relevant parameter space, it is not possible to satisfy the condition
2\lambda^{\prime}v\tilde{v}\leq|M_{\phi}^{2}-M_{h}^{2}|
in the limit
M_{\phi}\ll M_{h}
. For simplicity, in the following we will therefore only consider the
M_{\phi}>M_{h}
scenario.

3

Note that in the minimal model,
Z^{\prime}\to\phi\phi

Another constraint on the scalar sector of the model arises from perturbative unitarity. The amplitudes for scattering processes
a\to b
involving CP-even initial and final states

, can be decomposed into partial waves to build a coupled-channel matrix of
J=0
s-wave amplitudes,

\displaystyle a_{0}=-\frac{1}{16\pi}\begin{pmatrix}4\lambda&\sqrt{2}\lambda&\sqrt{2}\lambda&0&0\\
\sqrt{2}\lambda&3\lambda&\lambda&0&0\\
\sqrt{2}\lambda&\lambda&3\lambda&\lambda^{\prime}/2&0\\
0&0&\lambda^{\prime}/2&3\tilde{\lambda}&\tilde{\lambda}\\
0&0&0&\tilde{\lambda}&3\tilde{\lambda}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(16)

where initial/final state masses have been neglected and the limit of large

is taken. The condition
|\text{Re}\,a_{0}|\leq 1/2
for perturbative unitarity

;
88
is then applied to the largest eigenvalue of Eq. (
16

If
\lambda^{\prime}
is sufficiently small, the SM and

sectors decouple and the subsequent bounds are
\lambda<4\pi/3
and
\tilde{\lambda}<2\pi
. These upper bounds can be combined with the relations below Eq. (
14

\phi
masses,

\displaystyle\frac{8\pi v^{2}}{3}+\frac{3(\lambda^{\prime}\tilde{v})^{2}}{8\pi A}<M_{\phi}^{2}<4\pi\tilde{v}^{2}+\frac{(\lambda^{\prime}v)^{2}}{4\pi B}\,,

(17)

where

,

M_{\phi}
can satisfy the condition
|\text{Re}\,a_{0}|\leq 1/2

\lambda^{\prime}
then indicates that
\lambda^{\prime}<4\pi\,\text{min}[\tilde{v}B/v,\sqrt{2AB/3}]\approx 4\pi\,\text{min}[\tilde{v}/v,\sqrt{2/3}]

At scales relevant for

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\text{eff}}

\displaystyle\supset C_{ij}^{V}\mathcal{O}_{ij}^{V}+C_{ij}^{\prime V}\mathcal{O}_{ij}^{\prime V}+\Big[C_{ij}^{T}\mathcal{O}_{ij}^{T}+\text{h.c.}\Big]\,,

(18)

where the vector and dipole-like operators are

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{ij}^{(\prime)V}

\displaystyle=(\bar{d}_{i}\gamma_{\mu}P_{L(R)}d_{j})Z^{\prime\mu}\,,

(19)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{ij}^{T}

\displaystyle=(\bar{d}_{i}\sigma_{\mu\nu}P_{L}d_{j})Z^{\prime\mu\nu}\,,

(20)

respectively. The coefficients
C(\mu)
can be matched to the full model at the electroweak scale,
\mu\sim M_{W}
, and then run down to
\mu\sim m_{b}
under
SU(3)_{c}\times U(1)_{\text{em}}

C(M_{W})
which we use in our numerical studies are provided in App.
A

It is instructive to understand important properties and limits of
C(\mu)
. First, the right-handed vector coupling vanishes identically,
C_{ij}^{\prime V}(M_{W})=0
. For
m_{t}\ll m_{T}
, the left-handed vector coupling at
\mu=M_{W}
can be approximated as

\displaystyle C_{ij}^{V}(M_{W})

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{C}_{ij}m_{t}^{2}\bigg[\tilde{g}q^{\prime}s_{R}^{2}X_{t}-\frac{g}{c_{w}}\Delta_{Z^{\prime}}Y_{t}\bigg]\,,

(21)

with
X_{t}=\ln m_{T}^{2}/M_{W}^{2}-1
,
Y_{t}\equiv Y(m_{t}^{2}/M_{W}^{2})=1.47
using the function
Y(x)
defined in App.
A
and we define for convenience
\mathcal{C}_{ij}=4G_{F}V_{ti}^{*}V_{tj}/16\sqrt{2}\pi^{2}
. The first term in Eq. (
21
) arises from the direct coupling of
Z^{\prime}
to
t/T
in Eq. (
11
), while the second term is induced by the mixing of
Z^{\prime}
into
Z
. The mixing of
Z^{\prime}
into
\gamma
does not contribute, because the photon coupling to the FCNC down-type quark vector current vanishes due to gauge invariance. For
i=j
, the
Z^{\prime}
also couples to down-type quarks according to Eq. (
11
). The expression in Eq. (
21
) only holds for
\tilde{g}q^{\prime}
sufficiently above the consistency bound in Eq. (
5
). To fit the Belle II excess in some of the parameter space, we find that
\tilde{g}q^{\prime}
is required to be close to the consistency bound. In this limit, the left-handed vector coupling is instead given by

\displaystyle C_{ij}^{V}(M_{W})

\displaystyle\approx\mathcal{C}_{ij}m_{t}m_{T}\tilde{g}q^{\prime}\,.

(22)

The vector operators do not run under QED or QCD, so the expressions for
C_{ij}^{V}
and
C_{ij}^{\prime V}
above and in App.

are also valid at
\mu\sim m_{b}
.

In the limit
m_{t}\ll m_{T}
, the dipole-like operator is given approximately at the matching scale
\mu=M_{W}
by

\displaystyle\hskip-1.99997ptC_{ij}^{T}(M_{W})

\displaystyle\approx m_{i}\mathcal{C}_{ij}\Big[\tilde{g}q^{\prime}s_{R}^{2}A_{t}-e\epsilon_{A}B_{t}-\frac{g}{c_{w}}\Delta_{Z^{\prime}}C_{t}\Big]\,,

(23)

where
A_{t}=0.0323
,
B_{t}=0.194
and
C_{t}=0.150
are the numerical outputs of loop functions, given in App.
A
. Like the vector coupling
C_{ij}^{V}
, the first term in Eq. (

) is induced by
Z^{\prime}
coupling directly to
t/T
. The second and third terms in Eq. (
23
) are generated by the mixing of
Z^{\prime}
into
\gamma
and
Z
, respectively. The running of
C_{ij}^{T}
down to
\mu\sim m_{b}
is dominated by QCD corrections. However, a detailed treatment of the running is not necessary for the following analysis, as we will demonstrate shortly.

Through these vector and dipole-like interactions, the

can be produced on-shell in the process

for

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(B^{+}\to K^{+}Z^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\tau_{B^{+}}\frac{|\vec{p}_{K}|^{3}}{8\pi M_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}\Big|C_{bs}^{V}f_{+}+C_{bs}^{T}\tilde{f}_{T}\Big|^{2}\,,

where

is the

is the three-momentum of

in the
B^{+}
rest-frame, and

. Here,

and

are the vector and tensor

with respect to

and is therefore neglected. The

can also be produced on-shell in the process

for

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(B\to K^{*}Z^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\tau_{B}\frac{|\vec{p}_{K^{*}}|}{4\pi}\bigg\{\Big|C_{bs}^{V}\tilde{V}-C_{bs}^{T}\tilde{T}_{1}\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip-45.00006pt+\Big|C_{bs}^{V}\tilde{A}_{1}-C_{bs}^{T}\tilde{T}_{2}\Big|^{2}+8\Big|C_{bs}^{V}\tilde{A}_{12}-C_{bs}^{T}\tilde{T}_{23}\Big|^{2}\bigg\}\,,

\displaystyle\tilde{T}_{2}=\frac{m_{B}^{2}-m_{K^{*}}^{2}}{m_{B}}T_{2}\,,

\displaystyle\tilde{A}_{12}=\frac{m_{K^{*}}}{M_{Z^{\prime}}}A_{12}\,,

\displaystyle\tilde{T}_{23}=\frac{M_{Z^{\prime}}m_{K^{*}}}{m_{B}+m_{K^{*}}}T_{23}\,.

III.2.5
Other Flavour Probes

Here we briefly review other potential flavour probes of the model and discuss why they are currently less sensitive than the bounds shown in Fig.
8
. In principle,
Z^{\prime}
can feature as a real or virtual state in other rare processes, since it couples to FCNC down-type quark currents in Eq. (
19
). However, for
M_{Z^{\prime}}=2.1\penalty\ \text{GeV}
,
Z^{\prime}
cannot be produced on-shell from kaon decays. While
K\to\pi\psi\bar{\psi}
via an off-shell
Z^{\prime}
is kinematically possible for
2m_{\psi}<m_{K}-m_{\pi}
, we show in the next section that the viable region for
\psi
is outside this range. In particular being stable and weakly coupled,
\psi
will necessarily decouple from the primordial plasma in the early universe and form a thermal relic. In order not to overclose the universe, its annihilation cross-section should thus be sizeable enough. As we show in the next section, for
m_{\psi}<M_{Z^{\prime}}/2
as required by Belle II and LHCb, this is only possible close to the resonant condition
0.9\lesssim m_{\psi}/\text{GeV}\lesssim 1
.
At the boundary of the allowed region,
\psi
can actually constitute all of the observed DM. Thus, we do not consider much lower
m_{\psi}
values.

The processes
B\to\pi Z^{\prime}/\rho Z^{\prime}
are kinematically allowed, but suppressed by
V_{td}/V_{ts}
with respect to
B\to K^{(*)}Z^{\prime}
, and subject to similar upper bounds as
B\to K^{*}E_{\text{miss}}

73
.
Finally, the heavy top partner
T
contributes at the one-loop level to rare SM processes via the charged- and neutral-current interactions in Eq. (
II.1.1
). For example, the
b\to s\gamma
transitions as well as
B_{d,s}
meson oscillations are modified in the model. However, since the contributions of
T
are always proportional to
s_{L}\ll s_{R}
, the current sensitivities are insufficient to constrain the relevant parameter space of the model in Fig.
8
. In particular, EWPO are always more constraining, see Ref.
65
for a more complete discussion.

III.3
Dark Matter

The precise CMB measurements by the Planck collaboration constitute an important constraint on the model, due to the presence of a stable
\psi
in the early universe. Incidently, the measured relic abundance of DM
\Omega_{\text{DM}}h^{2}=0.120\pm 0.001

17

\psi
constituting (part or) all of the DM (
\Omega_{\psi}\leq\Omega_{\text{DM}}
). Thus the measured DM relic abundance can be considered both as an opportunity and a constraint on the model.

The freeze-out of
\psi
in the early universe is governed by the Boltzmann equation,

\displaystyle\frac{dY_{\psi}}{dx}=-\frac{s\braket{\sigma v}}{HxZ}\Big(Y_{\psi}^{2}-Y_{\psi,\text{eq}}^{2}\Big)\,,

(47)

where
x=m_{\psi}/T
, with
T
being the temperature of the thermal bath. Furthermore,
Y_{\psi}=n_{\psi}/s
is the number density of
\psi

s=2\pi^{2}h_{\text{eff}}T^{3}/45
(with
Y_{\psi,\text{eq}}
the equilibrium value),
H=\sqrt{8\pi\rho/3M_{\text{Pl}}^{2}}
is the Hubble expansion rate with a radiation-dominated energy density
\rho=\pi^{2}g_{\text{eff}}T^{4}/30
,
\braket{\sigma v}
is the thermally averaged annihilation cross section of
\psi
into species still in thermal equilibrium, and
Z=(1+\frac{1}{3}d\ln h_{\text{eff}}/d\ln T)^{-1}
accounts for the rate of change of
T
. Above,
M_{\text{Pl}}=1.22\times 10^{19}
GeV is the Planck mass and
g_{\text{eff}}
(
h_{\text{eff}}
) is the effective number of degrees of freedom for the energy (entropy) density.

We may solve Eq. (
47

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Y_{\psi}(x)}-\frac{1}{Y_{f}}=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{45}}M_{\text{Pl}}m_{\psi}\int_{x_{f}}^{x}dx\frac{\sqrt{g_{*}}\braket{\sigma v}}{x^{2}}\,,

(48)

where
Y_{f}\equiv Y_{\psi,\text{eq}}(x_{f})

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Omega_{\psi}h^{2}}=\int_{x_{f}}^{x}dx\frac{\sqrt{g_{*}}}{x^{2}}\frac{\braket{\sigma v}}{2.0\times 10^{-27}\penalty\ \text{cm}^{3}\penalty\ \text{s}^{-1}}\,,

, which is sensitive to energy injection from
e^{\pm}
and
\gamma
produced by DM annihilation final states. We can estimate

\displaystyle p_{\text{ann}}=\sum_{i}\frac{f_{i}^{\text{eff}}\braket{\sigma v}_{i}}{m_{\psi}}\bigg(\frac{\Omega_{\psi}}{\Omega_{\text{DM}}}\bigg)^{2}\,,

(56)

where, for the annihilation channel
i
,
f_{i}^{\text{eff}}
is the fraction of energy transferred to the plasma
103
;
87
,
\braket{\sigma v}_{i}
is the thermally averaged cross section, and we re-scale by the fractional contribution of
\psi
to the total DM relic density. The scattering cross section is well approximated by the zero velocity limit expression,

\displaystyle\braket{\sigma v}_{i}=\frac{6\pi\gamma_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}\mathcal{B}_{\psi\bar{\psi}}\mathcal{B}_{i}}{m_{\psi}^{2}\sqrt{\epsilon_{\psi}}(\gamma_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}+\epsilon_{\psi}^{2})}\,.

(57)

Using
micrOMEGAs
, the
f_{i}^{\text{eff}}
and
\braket{\sigma v}_{i}
are computed and the parameter space excluded by the Planck upper limit is shown as a light blue shaded region in Fig.
12
. For
\epsilon_{\psi}
sufficiently large, and thus below the resonance, the
\braket{\sigma v}_{i}
are suppressed enough for the CMB bound to be evaded. Note that for masses in this range, the
e^{+}
and
\gamma
spectra calculated in
micrOMEGAs
, which act as input to
f_{i}^{\text{eff}}
, carry significant uncertainties. Hadronisation of the quark final-states is calculated using
Pythia
and extrapolated to lower masses, where hadronic resonances make the
Pythia
calculations less reliable. Nevertheless, we do not expect this to make up for the large suppression of
\braket{\sigma v}_{i}
below the resonance at recombination. The late time annihilation of DM to SM particles is similarly suppressed, and therefore indirect detection bounds, e.g. from Super-Kamiokande
13
or
X
-ray data
55
;
31
, are evaded.

To conclude, the resonant thermal annihilation of
\psi
in the early universe for
0.9\lesssim m_{\psi}/\text{GeV}\lesssim 1.0\penalty\ \text{GeV}
, where
\psi
simultaneously appear as missing energy at Belle II, offers a compelling DM scenario which is not yet experimentally excluded by direct or indirect detection. The model presented offers the minimal field content to fit both the Belle II data and generate the correct relic abundance with a light
Z^{\prime}
. Of course, the presence of additional fields in an extended dark sector charged under
U(1)^{\prime}
would modify the DM phenomenology, as we discuss briefly in the next section.

IV
Discussion and Conclusions

In previous work
45
;
46
, we demonstrated within a
model–independent EFT framework that the Belle II
excess in
B^{+}\to K^{+}E_{\rm miss}
can be well described by the
emission of a single light vector boson, with a mass of about

M_{Z^{\prime}}\simeq 2.1
GeV, in
b\to s
transitions.
We have now extended this to a
simple UV-complete construction, based on a
Higgsed
U(1)^{\prime}
gauge symmetry with a light vector boson
Z^{\prime}
, a complex scalar
\Phi
whose vacuum
expectation value gives mass to
Z^{\prime}
and a

U(1)^{\prime}
charged vector-like top partner
T
realising a rank-1 pattern of flavour breaking, aligned to the third generation, that naturally enhances
b\to s
processes.

In this minimal construction, loop-induced
b\to sZ^{\prime}
transitions generate the desired
Belle II signal through a left handed current operator, but the same loops unavoidably induce kinetic and mass
mixing between
Z^{\prime}
and the electroweak gauge bosons, leading to
irreducible couplings of
Z^{\prime}
to charged leptons that cannot even be fine-tuned away.
The resulting branching ratios in the minimal model,
displayed in Fig.
5
, show that
Z^{\prime}
decays dominantly into
visible SM final states, with a sizeable
\mu^{+}\mu^{-}

branching fraction in the parameter region favoured by Belle II.
The corresponding contributions to
B\to K^{(*)}\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
are excluded
by existing LHCb searches for prompt and displaced dimuon resonances
(Fig.
6
).
We therefore conclude that the minimal aligned
U(1)^{\prime}
model cannot
account for the Belle II anomaly; its failure is directly tied to the
absence of sufficiently large invisible
Z^{\prime}
decay channels as the
Z^{\prime}
effectively always decays inside the detector.

Motivated by this, we extended the
U(1)^{\prime}
charged sector by adding a light
SM singlet fermion
\psi
, which opens a dominant invisible decay mode
Z^{\prime}\to\psi\bar{\psi}
and sufficiently suppresses the
visible branching ratios (provided the
\psi

U(1)^{\prime}
charge is an order of magnitude larger than that of
T
and
\Phi
), as seen in the modified decay patterns in
Fig.
7
.
The same interaction controls the
\psi
annihilation rate in the early universe, and a
combination of flavour, collider, electroweak, and cosmological constraints
selects a narrow
\psi
mass regime, just below half
of the
Z^{\prime}
mass, illustrated in
Fig.
12
, where
\psi
can constitute part or all of DM.
Outside this resonant region, the model violates at least one of these constraints. Future improved measurements and searches, planned at existing and upcoming experimental facilities like
Belle II
22
and FCC
12
, will be able to further constrain the viable parameter space. The model thus represents a highly constrained and predictive scenario relating missing energy signatures in rare
B
decays to a comprehensive collider and DM phenomenology.

Extensions of the model field content could potentially relax some of the constraints found in the minimal model. For instance, the large
U(1)^{\prime}
charge ratio (
r_{\psi}
) between
\psi
and
T,\Phi
could be avoided by considering multiple light dark fermions
\psi_{i}
with
r_{\psi_{i}}\sim 1
instead of one
\psi
with
r_{\psi}\gg 1
. Secondly, the CMB and direct detection constraints could be further relaxed for dark fermions with axial-vector couplings to the
Z^{\prime}
. Such a scenario would be obtained if different
U(1)^{\prime}
charges are assigned to the left- and right-handed components
\psi_{L}\equiv\psi
and
\psi_{R}\equiv\psi^{\prime}
. For example, with
q^{\prime}_{L}\equiv q^{\prime}_{\psi}\neq q^{\prime}_{R}=q^{\prime}_{\psi^{\prime}}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\supset\bar{\psi}i\not{D}\psi+\bar{\psi^{\prime}}i\not{D}\psi^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}\Big[Y_{\psi}\bar{\psi^{\prime}}^{c}\psi^{\prime}\tilde{\Phi}+\text{h.c.}\Big]\,,

(58)

where
\psi^{c}=C\bar{\psi}^{T}
, with
C
the charge conjugation matrix, and we also introduce the scalar
\tilde{\Phi}=(\tilde{v}_{\psi}+\varphi+ia)/\sqrt{2}
with the
U(1)^{\prime}
charge
-2q^{\prime}_{R}
. In the broken
U(1)^{\prime}
phase, the Majorana mass
m_{\psi^{\prime}}=\tilde{v}_{\psi}Y_{\psi}/\sqrt{2}
is generated for
\psi^{\prime}
. The state
\psi
may also be a Majorana fermion if the charges obey
q^{\prime}=q^{\prime}_{L}-q^{\prime}_{R}
, thus permitting the Yukawa coupling

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\supset-y_{\psi}\bar{\psi}\psi^{\prime}\Phi+\text{h.c.}\,.

(59)

For
\tilde{v}y_{\psi}\ll\tilde{v}_{\psi}Y_{\psi}
, a seesaw-like mechanism generates a Majorana mass for
\psi
of size
m_{\psi}\approx(\tilde{v}y_{\psi})^{2}/(\sqrt{2}\tilde{v}_{\psi}Y_{\psi})
.
In this setup, both
\psi
and
\psi^{\prime}
have purely axial-vector couplings to
Z^{\prime}
and
their annihilation would be p-wave (
v^{2}
-suppressed), reducing
\braket{\sigma v}
at recombination by
\sim(v_{\rm CMB}/v_{\text{fo}})^{2}\sim 10^{-6}
, while maintaining efficient freeze-out. The annihilation then proceeds either through the
Z^{\prime}
resonance for
m_{\psi^{(\prime)}}\lesssim M_{Z^{\prime}}/2
or alternatively for
m_{\psi^{(\prime)}}\gtrsim M_{Z^{\prime}}
through the process
\psi^{(\prime)}\psi^{(\prime)}\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}
shown in Fig.
11
(right). In such an extended scenario, the missing energy at Belle II can either be provided by the
\psi^{(\prime)}
fields,
Z^{\prime}\to\psi^{(\prime)}{\psi}^{(\prime)}
, or alternatively the physical CP-even and -odd components of
\Phi
and
\tilde{\Phi}
(mass eigenstates
\varphi^{\prime}
and
a^{\prime}
),
Z^{\prime}\to\varphi^{\prime}a^{\prime}
, if they are sufficiently light. We leave such considerations for future work.

Multi-scale residual convolutional block-based compressive sensing image reconstruction for vehicular communication
Authors: Jingtao Guan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractVehicular communication, as the core connection hub between the perception layer, decision layer, and execution layer of intelligent driving, faces an explosive demand for real-time image transmission and processing. The accuracy and real-time performance of image reconstruction directly determine the reliability of safety functions such as lane detection, pedestrian detection, and collision warning. However, conventional compressive sensing image reconstruction algorithms suffer from detail loss, structural distortion, and high computational complexity in vehicular scenarios, making them difficult to adapt to limited bandwidth and computing resources. To address this, a compressive sensing image reconstruction algorithm for vehicular communication is proposed. The algorithm first integrates Multi-Scale Residual Convolution, Coordinate Spatial Attention mechanism, and Depth wise Separable Convolution to construct an image feature extraction algorithm. It accurately captures key details in vehicular images while reducing computational cost. Then, it combines a Generative Adversarial Network optimized by a variational autoencoder with a Vision Transformer to model the correlation between low-sampling features and complete images, ensuring global coherence of the reconstruction results. Experimental results show that in dense pedestrian areas, the structural similarity index reaches 0.935, the peak signal-to-noise ratio is 33.64 dB, the maximum memory usage is 413.6 MB, and the response time is 162.4 ms with 2000 data samples. The proposed algorithm outperforms comparative methods in multiple metrics, balancing reconstruction accuracy, anti-interference capability, and real-time performance, and it adapts well to dynamic vehicular communication environments, providing efficient and reliable image support for intelligent driving visual systems.Keywords: Vehicular communication, Multi-scale residual convolution block, Generative adversarial network, Compressive sensing, Image reconstructionSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Research background

With the rapid development of intelligent driving technology, vehicular communication has become the core hub connecting the perception layer, decision layer, and execution layer. The demand for real-time image transmission and processing increases rapidly. Image data in vehicular scenarios support key safety functions such as lane detection, pedestrian detection, and collision warning, and their reliability directly depends on the accuracy and real-time performance of image reconstruction1. However, vehicular communication faces challenges including channel fluctuations caused by high-speed movement, limited bandwidth resources, and noise interference from electromagnetic disturbances. Traditional image transmission methods require large bandwidth and cannot easily adapt to dynamic vehicular communication environments. Conventional compressive sensing image reconstruction algorithms are prone to detail loss and structural distortion under low sampling rates, complex noise, or extreme lighting, failing to meet the strict image quality requirements of intelligent driving2. Therefore, developing a high-accuracy, high-robustness, and high-efficiency compressive sensing image reconstruction algorithm suitable for vehicular communication is crucial for overcoming image transmission bottlenecks and ensuring driving safety.

Research status

With the integration of compressive sensing theory and deep learning technology, image reconstruction algorithms have seen progress in vehicular communication applications. M. S. Kang et al. optimized local image features through a staged incremental update reconstruction model and gradually integrated global information to iteratively improve reconstruction accuracy. The Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) of the reconstructed images reaches 28.5 dB, but the core parameters rely on manual settings, leading to performance degradation in dense multi-object scenarios3. Gan et al. proposed a two-stage progressive modeling high-accuracy reconstruction algorithm to address detail loss and insufficient global spatial correlation in conventional compressive images. The PSNR reaches 32.8 dB, but performance decreases under low-light conditions4. Zha et al. optimized non-local sparse and low-rank constraint modules within a deep learning framework, achieving cooperative reconstruction of local details and global structure. The Structural Similarity (SSIM) reaches 0.91, but the method captures sparse features poorly when sampling resources are limited5. Shen used dual-branch collaboration and feature interaction mechanisms to achieve high-accuracy reconstruction, with PSNR reaching 33.2 dB, but SSIM drops from 0.93 to 0.79 under complex noise, showing limited robustness6. Reddy proposed a near-lossless predictive measurement coding scheme to improve image quality through redundancy reduction and compressive sensing optimization. At 20% undersampling, PSNR reaches 30.8 dB, but reconstruction efficiency decreases for large-size images7. Overall, existing compressive sensing image reconstruction algorithms depend on manual parameters, exhibit weak robustness in low-light or noisy environments, and lack an integrated solution balancing vehicular communication adaptability, robustness in complex scenarios, and real-time reconstruction, which cannot fully meet intelligent driving requirements.

Main research methods

The core of compressive sensing image reconstruction algorithms is to achieve efficient image transmission and high-quality recovery in vehicular scenarios through low-sampling compression and accurate reconstruction, providing reliable image support for intelligent driving functions. Multi-Scale Residual Convolution (MSRC) excels at capturing hierarchical features and performs well in multi-dimensional information extraction8. Depthwise Separable Convolution (DSC) decomposes standard convolution into depthwise and pointwise convolutions, reducing computational complexity while maintaining feature extraction capability9. Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) is a powerful generative model that learns the distribution of real data through adversarial training between the generator and discriminator, producing realistic images10. Vision Transformer (ViT) splits images into patches and captures global dependencies among visual elements using self-attention, improving performance in image classification, object detection, and other visual tasks11. Therefore, in response to the problems of low accuracy, high latency, and weak robustness of the existing compressed sensing image reconstruction methods, a compressed sensing image reconstruction algorithm for vehicle communication is proposed. The research combines MSRC and DSC to construct an image feature extraction algorithm, and on this basis, uses GAN and ViT to build a compressed sensing image reconstruction algorithm for vehicle communication. It is expected that this algorithm can effectively improve the performance of the vehicle vision system, provide more reliable image information support for the realization of functions such as autonomous driving and assisted driving, and promote the safe and efficient operation of the intelligent transportation system. The innovation points of the research lie in proposing a feature extraction strategy that integrates MSRC and DSC, which can accurately capture key details such as lane lines and pedestrian contours in vehicle images to alleviate the loss of details under low sampling rates, and also reduce the computational complexity by leveraging DSC to adapt to the limited bandwidth of vehicles. At the same time, a collaborative reconstruction scheme using GAN and ViT is introduced, which utilizes GAN to learn the real distribution of vehicle images to improve the fidelity of reconstruction in complex environments, and through ViT, captures global pixel dependencies, using multi-scale convolution kernels to construct residual blocks, which can accurately capture different scale target features in vehicle scenes, solving the problem of image distortion caused by the collapse of the adversarial training mode in traditional algorithms. Finally, by integrating multiple modules and autonomously learning to adjust the sampling rate, the algorithm achieves collaborative optimization throughout the process, adapting to the dual requirements of image real-time performance and accuracy for intelligent driving. The algorithm proposed by the research institute, when compared with existing composite structures such as Transformer, utilizes multi-scale feature fusion and anti-interference mechanisms. It can effectively distinguish between interference and valid features through the complementary verification of different convolution kernels, thereby improving the image accuracy of the algorithm in various scenarios. In addition, lightweight and training stability modules were also designed to enhance the real-time deployment performance and scene adaptability of the algorithm. The core contributions of the research are reflected in three aspects: First, through efficient feature extraction of multi-scale residual blocks, achieving ultra-high compression ratios, enabling the limited bandwidth to carry more image transmission volumes compared to traditional solutions. Second, the CMD-VGV reconstruction algorithm is constructed, through the collaborative design of VAE-GAN-ViT, breaking through the technical bottleneck of insufficient reconstruction accuracy under low sampling rates and disordered global structures in complex vehicle scenes. For interference in vehicle scenes such as rain, fog, and strong light, the multi-scale feature fusion mechanism of the algorithm can effectively distinguish interference from effective features through complementary verification of different convolution kernels. Third, the algorithm design meets the requirements of vehicle communication, with the reconstruction process directly outputting perception-level features, without the need for secondary extraction by the perception module, reducing the overall processing delay of the system and the computational power requirements for the vehicle chip.

Materials and methods

Image feature extraction algorithm based on MSRC

In vehicular scenarios, image feature extraction is a core pre-processing step for compressive sensing image reconstruction. It captures key structural information and detailed features under low sampling rate, noise interference, and multi-object conditions, providing reliable support for high-quality reconstruction12. MSRC, with its multi-branch convolution kernels and residual connections, effectively extracts features at different scales and meets the requirements for global structural feature extraction13. To extract image features more efficiently, a dual-output MSRC is applied, combined with a residual network framework to enhance feature extraction efficiency. The structure of the dual-output MSRC is shown in Fig. 1.

(1) Deep and shallow feature extraction: the features processed by multiple layers of multi-scale residual blocks are passed through dual-output convolution layers to generate two feature tensors. The shallow feature tensor retains more spatial details, while the deep feature tensor contains stronger semantic information. (2) Feature fusion: The two features are optimized independently through parallel convolution branches and enhanced via cross-branch residual connections to strengthen feature complementarity. The dual-output features are integrated through hierarchical feature fusion to adaptively extract key information. Convolution layers reduce computational complexity while preserving multi-scale feature correlations. The final fused feature map is combined with the input feature of the initial convolution layer via residual connections, forming a closed-loop enhancement path that improves edge clarity and structural coherence of the panoramic image. The dual-output MSRC, with its adaptive learning of hierarchical features, captures information at different image scales effectively. However, it has limitations in jointly expressing multi-scale features and cannot fully perceive spatial dependencies between pixels. Coordinate Spatial Attention (CSA) improves the perception of spatial dependencies by normalizing pixel coordinates as additional channels and generating attention weights, preventing the loss of useful features14. Therefore, CSA is applied to optimize MSRC, resulting in the CSA-MSRC algorithm. The process is shown in Fig. 2.

Operation process of the CSA-MSRC algorithm.

As shown in Fig. 2, the CSA-MSRC algorithm is mainly divided into two parts. (1) Extract the initial feature map: after the input of the original image, a preliminary feature map is generated through the initial convolution layer. Subsequently, multiple residual blocks are used to capture small-scale details through small convolution kernels and extract large-scale structures through large convolution kernels. The cross-branch residual connection splits the features into shallow detail and deep semantic branches, achieving interaction through element-wise addition and adaptive weight adjustment. Then, it is back-propagated to the initial feature map through residual connections, which not only enhances feature complementarity but also avoids gradient disappearance. (2) Feature fusion: The CSA coordinate information embedding layer normalizes pixel coordinates and concatenates features, injecting spatial position information. The attention weight generation module models the spatial position-dependent relationship of the fused feature map, strengthening the feature weights of key regions and avoiding the loss of useful features during the transmission process. CSA encodes the horizontal and vertical pixel coordinates as position features and concatenates them with image features, enhancing spatial perception. The coordinate embedding is expressed in Eq. By combining CSA, dual-output MSRC effectively captures both local details and global structures at different scales while enhancing the perception of spatial dependencies. However, in vehicular communication, it still has high computational complexity and cannot efficiently reduce the cost of feature extraction, limiting its adaptation to bandwidth-constrained, real-time image transmission scenarios. Depthwise Separable Convolution (DSC) decomposes standard convolution into depthwise and pointwise convolutions, reducing complexity while preserving feature extraction capability15. Therefore, CSA-MSRC is combined with DSC to construct the image feature extraction algorithm named CMD. The detailed process is shown in Fig. 3.

Operation flow chart of CMD algorithm.

As shown in Fig. 3, the CMD image feature extraction algorithm is mainly divided into two parts: (1) Feature fusion of the original image: After the original image is input, it is first generated a preliminary feature map through the dual-output MSRN, and then different-scale local texture details and global structural features are captured layer by layer through multiple residual blocks. After residual connection fusion, a multi-scale feature tensor is output, providing multi-dimensional feature support. Subsequently, the pixel coordinate information is normalized and integrated into the feature map by using CSA, and the spatial position dependency relationship is modeled through the attention weight generation module to strengthen the feature weights of key areas such as edges and textures. (2) Lightweight processing: The output feature map is used as the input of DSC. DSC first performs convolution operations independently on the feature channels output by CSA, only retaining the high-weight key feature spatial structures marked by CSA, avoiding the waste of resources caused by traditional convolution computing all channels indiscriminately. Then, the single-channel features output by deep convolution are integrated across channels, replacing the high-complexity calculation of traditional standard convolution, and achieving efficient cross-channel fusion of key features. Through the weight filtering of CSA, the calculation input size of DSC is reduced, and DSC decomposes the standard convolution to reduce the computational load of the algorithm while maintaining the feature extraction capability. This not only ensures the accurate extraction of key features of vehicle images, but also significantly reduces resource consumption through the structural integration of CSA and DSC, achieving a balance between feature extraction accuracy and lightweighting.

Compressive image reconstruction algorithm combining CMD and improved GAN

The constructed CMD algorithm integrates dual-output MSRC for multi-scale feature extraction, CSA for spatial dependency perception, and DSC for lightweight processing. It precisely extracts key details in vehicular images while reducing computational complexity, providing high-quality and lightweight feature support for compressive sensing reconstruction. However, vehicular compressive sensing reconstruction also needs to address low reconstruction accuracy under low sampling rates and insufficient realism in complex scenarios. Feature extraction alone cannot map low-sampling data to high-quality complete images. GAN learns the distribution of real vehicular images through adversarial training between the generator and discriminator, mapping low-sampling features to complete and realistic images, meeting the requirements of detail fidelity and visual coherence in vehicular scenarios16. GAN training can suffer from gradient vanishing or mode collapse. VAE improves training stability and enhances GAN’s standard learning and feature extraction ability through variational inference and reconstruction loss17. Therefore, VAE is applied to optimize GAN, forming the VAE-GAN algorithm. The process is shown in Fig. 4.

Results and analysis

Performance analysis of CMD image feature extraction algorithm

To evaluate the performance of the proposed CMD image feature extraction algorithm, it was compared with Hybrid Attention Transformer (HAT), Feature Pyramid Network-Spatial Attention (FPN-SA), and Laplacian Pyramid Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory Network (Lap-BiLSTM) through experiments. The HAT algorithm achieves the collaborative optimization of global dependency modeling and local feature extraction by integrating the multi-head self-attention mechanism with convolution operations. It performs exceptionally well in the task of capturing target correlation features in complex scenarios and is a typical application of Transformer-type models in the field of vehicle-mounted vision in recent years. The FPN-SA algorithm can accurately locate the hierarchical features of core targets such as lane lines and traffic signs in vehicle images, meeting the core requirements of extracting key target features in vehicle scenarios. It is the mainstream representative of multi-scale and attention fusion schemes. The Lap-BiLSTM algorithm can achieve image multi-scale decomposition. When combined with BiLSTM for modeling the feature dependencies in the temporal dimension, it can effectively address issues such as image blurring and target deformation caused by high-speed movement in vehicle scenarios, and is a classic solution combining sequence modeling and multi-scale analysis. The experimental system ran on Windows 10 with an AMD Ryzen 9 7950X3D@4.7 GHz CPU, NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU (24GB), and 32GB memory. Experiments were conducted on the Google Street View (GSV) and DIV2K datasets. PSNR and SSIM were measured for feature extraction performance. The results are shown in Fig. 8.

Comparison of PSNR and SSIM experimental results.

As shown in Fig. 8a, the SSIM of CMD eventually stabilized at 0.953, which improved by 0.031 and 0.025 compared with FPN-SA and LapSRN, respectively. Figure 8b shows that when the number of iterations reached 180, the PSNR of CMD reached 36.68 dB, representing a 9.73% increase over FPN-SA. These results indicated that CMD extracted vehicular image features more accurately than the compared algorithms, providing high-quality feature support for subsequent compressive sensing image reconstruction. Reconstruction analysis of CMD-VGV compressive sensing images

After evaluating CMD performance, the CMD-VGV compressive sensing image reconstruction algorithm, constructed based on CMD and improved GAN, was further analyzed. It was compared with Fast Fourier Convolution and Transformer-based GAN (FFC-Trans-GAN), Gated Convolution and Hierarchical Transformer Wasserstein GAN (GC-HT-WGAN), and Residual Gated Connection Generative Adversarial Network (ReGGAN) through experiments. The CMD-VGV algorithm adopts a three-stage phased training and joint fine-tuning training process. VAE, GAN, and ViT share the same Adam optimizer (β1 = 0.5, β2 = 0.999), where the initial learning rate of VAE is 0.0001 and the batch size is 32. Subsequently, based on the latent space trained by VAE, the generator and discriminator of GAN are trained with an initial learning rate of 0.0001 and a batch size of 32. An alternating training mode is adopted to avoid the generator from overfitting the discriminator. Then, ViT is introduced to capture long-range dependencies across all image pixels, correct the global structural deviation of the reconstructed image by VAE-GAN, and achieve end-to-end collaborative optimization. The initial learning rate is 0.00005 and the batch size is 16. The experiments used the DIV2K and SUN360 datasets. DIV2K contained 800 training samples and 100 testing samples, while SUN360 contained 1000 training samples and 200 testing samples. These datasets covered different road conditions and pedestrian densities, simulating common vehicular scenarios. Loss curves during image reconstruction for the four algorithms were first tested, as shown in Fig. 11.

Comparison of loss curves in the image reconstruction process.

As shown in Fig. 11a, when the number of iterations reached 50, the loss of CMD-VGV was 0.029, significantly lower than the other algorithms. Afterward, the loss curve gradually stabilized. At 80 iterations, CMD-VGV’s loss reached 0.026, while ReGGAN’s loss was 0.057. Figure 11b shows that at 90 iterations, CMD-VGV’s loss reached 0.023, 0.038 lower than ReGGAN. CMD-VGV’s loss curve converged fastest when iterations were below 20. These results indicated that CMD-VGV achieved faster convergence and lower loss compared with the other algorithms. Subsequently, Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curves and Precision-Recall (PR) curves were measured for the four algorithms, as shown in Fig. 12.

Comparison of ROC curve and PR curve.

Figure 12a shows that CMD-VGV’s ROC curve was closest to the top-left corner, with an AUC of 0.923, the highest among the compared algorithms (0.899, 0.846, 0.813), demonstrating superior image reconstruction performance. Figure 12b shows that CMD-VGV’s PR curve was closest to the top-right corner, significantly outperforming the other algorithms. This indicated that CMD-VGV maintained high precision while achieving high recall, further validating its superior reconstruction performance. Overall, CMD-VGV outperformed the compared algorithms in both recall and precision, showing better robustness. In order to analyze the running efficiency and memory usage of the four algorithms, the research conducted tests on the system memory usage during the image reconstruction process of the four algorithms, as well as the end-to-end full-process response time including encoding, transmission and decoding. The test results are shown in Fig. 13.

System memory usage and end-to-end response time test results.

From Fig. 13a, it can be seen that the memory usage curve of the CMD-VGV algorithm is relatively gentle with little fluctuation, and the maximum memory usage is 413.6 M. When the data volume is 2000, the end-to-end response time of the entire process including encoding, transmission, and decoding is 162.4 ms, which can meet the real-time requirements of vehicle communication. Figure 13b shows that GC-HT-WGAN had a maximum memory usage of 492.5 MB and a response time of 229.7 ms. Figure 13c and d show that FFC-Trans-GAN and ReGGAN had maximum memory usage of 594.8 MB and 516.7 MB, and response times of 247.5 ms and 228.9 ms, respectively. In conclusion, the CMD-VGV algorithm can meet the real-time requirements of vehicle-to-vehicle communication, providing timely image data support for the intelligent driving decision-making layer, and avoiding the problem of delayed safety function responses caused by excessive delay. Overall, the CMD-VGV algorithm can maintain high-precision reconstruction effects in various complex vehicle-mounted scenarios. Its multi-scale feature fusion and anti-interference mechanism effectively cope with common interferences in practical applications such as rain and fog occlusion and high-speed movement, providing data support for the deployment of the algorithm in diverse vehicle environments. Discussion

The proposed CMD image feature extraction algorithm demonstrated significant performance advantages in the preprocessing feature extraction stage of vehicular compressive sensing image reconstruction. On the GSV dataset, CMD achieved an SSIM of 0.953, 0.031 higher than FPN-SA, and a PSNR of 36.68 dB, 9.73% higher than FPN-SA. On the DIV2K dataset, its GMSD reached 0.038 and VIF reached 0.924, significantly better than Lap-BiLSTM, which achieved 0.055 and 0.872. These performances were attributed to the dual-output MSRC design of CMD. By using multiple residual blocks and cross-branch connections, CMD captured both shallow texture details and deep semantic information hierarchically, avoiding insufficient representation of complex vehicular targets by single-scale feature extraction. Meanwhile, the CSA mechanism, through pixel coordinate embedding and attention weight modeling, enhanced the perception of spatial position dependencies in dynamic vehicular scenes, effectively preventing the loss of target edge features under high-speed motion. This design outperformed the cross-scale feature aggregation module proposed by Cui26. Additionally, CMD decomposed standard convolution into depth-wise convolution and point-wise convolution via DSC, greatly reducing computational cost while maintaining feature extraction capability. When the data volume reached 1800, memory usage was only 357.2 MB, 48.6 MB lower than FPN-SA, and the floating-point operations were 0.753 G, 0.186 G less than HAT, which perfectly adapted to limited vehicular communication bandwidth and computing resources, overcoming the real-time deployment limitations of high-complexity super-resolution methods reported by Ran et al.27.

The CMD-VGV compressive sensing image reconstruction algorithm, constructed based on CMD, further integrated CMD’s feature advantages with the generation capability of the improved GAN, showing comprehensive superiority in full reconstruction tasks under vehicular scenarios. The algorithm achieved rapid convergence within 20 iterations and a loss of 0.023 at 90 iterations, 0.038 lower than ReGGAN. This was attributed to VAE, which constrained reconstruction through variational inference and reconstruction loss, effectively mitigating gradient vanishing and mode collapse in traditional GANs under low sampling rates, allowing CMD-VGV to generate more accurate and realistic vehicular images. Compared with the visual adaptive framework proposed by J. Zhang, CMD-VGV showed better convergence28. In terms of reconstruction accuracy and robustness, CMD-VGV’s ROC curve was closest to the top-left corner with an AUC of 0.923, significantly higher than the compared algorithms (0.899–0.813). Its PR curve was closer to the top-right corner, indicating that high recall was maintained while precision remained high. Even in dense pedestrian areas, CMD-VGV achieved an SSIM of 0.935 and a PSNR of 33.64 dB, 0.023 and 2.57 dB higher than GC-HT-WGAN. In sparse pedestrian areas, the reconstructed SSIM further increased to 0.941, demonstrating strong adaptability to different vehicular scenarios. These advantages were attributed to ViT, which captured long-range dependencies among all image pixels, addressing the global structure distortion caused by the limited receptive field of VAE-GAN, resulting in better continuity of lane markings and intact distant vehicle contours. This overcame the limitation of the perception-driven U-Net proposed by Zhou et al.29, which struggled with global modeling for large-scale vehicular images. Regarding vehicular scenario adaptability, CMD-VGV had a maximum memory usage of 413.6 MB, and a response time of 162.4 ms when the data volume was 2000, significantly lower than FFC-Trans-GAN (247.5 ms) and ReGGAN (228.9 ms), fully meeting the requirements of real-time image transmission and reconstruction in intelligent driving. This addressed the trade-off between robustness and efficiency in complex noisy environments observed by Yue when using hierarchical variational inference modules30. In the actual deployment of intelligent driving, the adaptability to extreme scenarios is the core value manifestation of the algorithm. In rainy weather conditions, the raindrops blocking the view cause image blurring. CMD-VGV distinguishes interference from effective features through multi-scale convolution kernels, combined with CSA weights filtering, and reconstructs SSIM to 0.905, meeting the requirements of vehicle-mounted images. In foggy low visibility scenarios, VAE-GAN repairs the occluded features, ViT enhances global coherence, and SSIM remains at 0.892. In high-speed moving scenarios, the lightweight design of DSC enables the algorithm to respond end-to-end in only 162.4 ms, meeting the requirements of high-speed frame rates, and ViT can correct the structural offset caused by motion blur, with SSIM reaching 0.901. In terms of engineering deployment, the algorithm’s maximum memory usage is 413.6 MB, which can reduce the deployment cost of vehicle manufacturers and has the foundation for large-scale promotion.

Summary

Traditional compressive sensing image reconstruction methods often suffered from detail loss and high computational complexity in vehicular communication scenarios, making them difficult to adapt to limited bandwidth. Existing solutions also struggled to balance reconstruction accuracy, real-time performance, and adaptability to vehicular environments, failing to meet the reliability requirements of image data for intelligent driving. This study proposed a compressive sensing image reconstruction algorithm designed for vehicular communication. By integrating MSRC, CSA, and DSC, the CMD feature extraction algorithm was constructed, achieving precise extraction of key vehicular image features and lightweight processing. On this basis, a CMD-VGV reconstruction algorithm was built by combining VAE-optimized GAN and ViT, addressing low-sampling data recovery and global structure continuity. The proposed algorithm effectively mitigated structural distortion and detail loss seen in traditional methods, while balancing reconstruction accuracy, anti-interference capability, and computational efficiency. It adapted to dynamic vehicular communication environments and provided reliable image support for the perception layer of intelligent driving. However, the performance of the proposed algorithm in extremely complex vehicular scenarios was not fully verified. Future work focuses on improving cross-scenario generalization and optimizing real-time processing efficiency. By integrating multi-modal vehicular data and adjusting algorithm feature weights, the study aims to further enhance the applicability and reliability of the algorithm in diverse vehicular scenarios.

432. Imaging lithium in Alzheimer’s disease (ILiAD)
Authors: V C Wing, N E Woollam, S Clews, G Hall, P Thelwall, D A Cousins
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundLithium is a well-established and effective medication in bipolar disorder which due to its neuroprotective effects, is now under investigation as a treatment in Alzheimer’s disease (AD). Lithium magnetic resonance imaging (7Li-MRI) can directly measure brain lithium concentrations in vivo, and in bipolar disorder this has shown preferential uptake to regions including the hippocampus.Aims & ObjectivesThe ILiAD study was designed to determine the feasibility of using 7Li-MRI to measure lithium signal intensity and regional brain distribution following the lower doses of lithium used in AD.MethodIn phase I patients aged 50-80 with mild-to-moderate AD were treated with lithium carbonate MR (Priadel) at 100mg/day for 1 week and 200mg/day for 1 week with assessments at days 0, 7 and 14. Structural and 3D 7Li-MRI images were acquired on a 3T Philips scanner with a dual-tuned H/Li-radiofrequency coil and analysed using an in-house python-based pipeline which co-localised the anatomical and lithium images without upscaling.ResultsInterim analysis of phase I (n=3) demonstrated that the scanning protocol and low dose lithium was well tolerated, with average steady-state serum lithium levels of 0.2mmol/L and 0.3mmol/L at the 100mg/day and 200mg/day doses respectively. There was inter-subject variability in the brain lithium signal intensity which was detectable in some but not all subjects at the 200mg/day dose. These phase I results informed the decision to employ the same dosing regimen in phase II, which aims to enrol a further n=8 patients with AD alongside n=8 age-matched healthy controls.Discussion & ConclusionsThe ILiAD study has demonstrated for the first time that 3D Li-MRI signal can be detected at such low doses and blood levels of lithium. It also showed the feasibility of using this technique in a dementia population. Completion and analysis of phase II will allow comparison of brain lithium distribution in AD compared to controls and inform future clinical trials and imaging studies of lithium in dementia.

P. Jameson Graber, Elizabeth Matter, Jesus Ruiz Bolanos

Abstract
. We analyze a fractional mean field game of controls system, showing existence of solutions when the order of the fractional Laplacian is s ∈ ( 1 2 , 1 ) s\in\mathinner{\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right)} . Here the running cost depends on the distribution μ \mu of not only the states but also optimal strategies. The coupling is assumed to satisfy the Lasry-Lions monotonicity condition. We derive three types of a priori estimates on solutions. First, we use the monotonicity condition to derive moment estimates on μ \mu . Second, we derive abstract estimates on fractional parabolic equations and apply them to the mean field game. Third, we derive new estimates on the time regularity of the distribution μ \mu by analyzing the associated Lévy process. We apply these estimates and the Leray-Schauder fixed point theorem to establish existence of solutions.

1.
Introduction

The purpose of this article is to analyze a system of partial differential equations having the form

(1.1)

\begin{cases}(i)&-\partial_{t}u+(-\Delta)^{s}u+H(x,Du,\mu)=0,\quad{\text{in}}\ {\mathbb{T}}^{d}\times(0,T),\\
(ii)&\partial_{t}m+(-\Delta)^{s}m-\Div(D_{p}H(x,Du,\mu)m)=0,\quad{\text{in}}\ {\mathbb{T}}^{d}\times(0,T),\\
(iii)&\mu(t)=\mathinner{\left(I_{d},-D_{p}H(x,Du,\mu)\right)}\sharp m(t),\quad\text{in}\ [0,T],\\
(iv)&m(x,0)=m_{0}(x),\ \ u(x,T)=u_{T}(x),\quad{\rm{in}}\ {\mathbb{T}}^{d}.\end{cases}

System (
1.1
) corresponds to a type of mean field game, or MFG.
Briefly, this means there exists a continuum of agents whose individually solve an optimal control problem whose value function is given by
u
, while the population density is modeled by
m
.
We recall that mean field games were introduced in
[
LL07
,
HMC06
,
HCM07
]
, and the interested reader may refer to several monographs and lecture notes for an overview
[
CD17a
,
CD17b
,
BFY13
,
G+14
,
Car10
,
ACD+21
]
.
In the case of System (
1.1
), the coupling is through the probability distribution
\mu(t)
, which is the distribution of both states and controls of all agents.
For that reason, this system is referred to as a mean field game of controls, or MFGC
[
CL18
,
Kob21
,
Kob22
]
, though in previous literature such systems have also been called “extended mean field games”
[
GPV14
,
GV16
]
.
What distinguishes MFGC from other MFG is seen in Equation (
1.1
)
(iii)
: the optimal control problem for each individual depends on the distribution not only of states but also of all optimal strategies.
All MFG involve a fixed point problem: for every distribution there is an optimal strategy, for every strategy there is a distribution, and there is equilibrium precisely when these coincide.
For MFGC, the distribution is given not only by
m(t)
but also by
\mu(t)
defined in Equation (
1.1
)
(iii)
, which can be seen as an additional fixed point problem that must be solved before resolving the larger problem of equilibrium.

Compared to the extensive literature on mean field games systems, there are relatively few general results on mean field games of controls.
Kobeissi proved the existence and uniqueness of classical solutions to MFGC in the uniformly parabolic setting (that is, with a Laplacian in place of the fractional Laplacian seen in (
1.1
) above). See
[
Kob21
,
Kob22
]
, where the first study makes use of “smallness” conditions to prove well-posedness, while the latter makes extensive use of the Lasry-Lions monotonicity condition for mean field games of controls; in this direction see also
[
CL18
, Section 5]
.
We also refer to
[
GPV14
,
GV16
]
for some early results in this direction.
More recent works have treated the uniformly parabolic case on bounded domains with Dirichlet or Neumann boundary conditions
[
BS24
,
GR25
]
.
Mean field games of controls also have natural applications to economics
[
GLL11
,
ABL+14
]
.
Several works, including by the first author, have been written on a mean field games model for Cournot competition, which is a natural example of a mean field game of controls
[
CS15
,
CS17
,
GB18
,
GM20
,
GM18
,
GIN21
,
GL22
,
GS23
,
CLT25
]
.

Most of these references deal with second-order parabolic models, modeling stochastic games with Brownian motion, or else first-order models modeling deterministic games (except for
[
GIN21
]
, on a Cournot model with both Brownian motion and jumps).
In this article, we are interested in studying a mean field game of controls with a nonlocal diffusion, which models a game where the state of players can jump; see e.g.
[
CS17
]
.
We have chosen the fractional Laplacian as a benchmark case; more general nonlocal diffusion generators would be interesting for applications.
Mean field games with a fractional Laplacian have been studied by a few authors.
Cesaroni et al. proved the existence of classical solutions to a stationary model
[
CCD+17
]
, while Cirant and Goffi proved a similar result for time-dependent problems in
[
CG19
]
; both results are set on the torus, as in our case.
Ersland and Jakobsen proved the existence of classical solutions to mean field games with nonlocal diffusion on the whole space
[
EJ21
]
, and Chowdhury et al. have proved similar results for fully nonlinear systems
[
CJK24
]
.
See also some related results on the master equation
[
JR23
]
, long-time behavior
[
EJP25
]
, and numerics
[
CEJ23
]
.

As far as we know, this is the first article on a general class of mean field games of controls with a nonlocal diffusion term.
Our goal is to prove existence of classical solutions to (
1.1
), in the case where
\frac{1}{2}<s<1
.
To prove this, we will apply the Leray-Schauder fixed point theorem, which relies mainly on first proving a sequence of a a priori estimates.
We draw inspiration from the results of Kobeissi on MFGC with monotone couplings
[
Kob22
]
, but certain regularity estimates are not feasible when only the fractional Laplacian is present.
To adapt to this setting, we have found many techniques found in
[
CG19
]
very helpful.

The remainder of this article is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we introduce various basic notions and abstract results on fractional parabolic equations that will be useful in what follows.
Section
3
is the heart of our study, where we prove a priori estimates on classical solutions of (
1.1
).
In Section
4
we apply the Leray-Schauder fixed point theorem to prove existence of solutions.
Finally, we prove uniqueness employing standard arguments based on the Lasry-Lions monotonicity condition.

,

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\tilde{q}}(\mu)

.
For

2.2.
Function spaces

For any measurable subset
E

{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, the space

On any metric space

, which has the norm
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert f\right\rVert}_{0}=\sup_{x\in X}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert f(x)\right\rvert}
.

For any closed set

[D^{i}u]_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\beta}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\sup_{x\neq y\in E}\dfrac{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert D^{i}u(x)-D^{i}u(y)\right\rvert}}{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert}^{\beta}}<\infty

for each multiindex

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert i\right\rvert}=k
.

We can define for
\alpha,\beta\in(0,1)
the Hölder space

for any closed set

and any

as the space of continuous functions

[u]_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha,\beta}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=[u]_{{\mathcal{C}}_{x}^{\alpha}}+[u]_{{\mathcal{C}}_{t}^{\beta}}<\infty

[u]_{{\mathcal{C}}_{x}^{\alpha}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\sup_{t\in[a,b]}[u(\cdot,t)]_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha}},\qquad[u]_{{\mathcal{C}}_{t}^{\beta}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\sup_{x\in E}[u(x,\cdot)]_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\beta}}.

We also use the standard definitions of Sobolev spaces.
W^{k,p}(E)
are functions in

with weak derivatives up to degree

in

. This space has as norm:

(2.5)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u\right\rVert}_{W^{k,p}}=\begin{cases}\sum_{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert}\leq k}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert D^{\alpha}u\right\rVert}_{L^{p}},&1\leq p<\infty\\
\sum_{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert}\leq k}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert D^{\alpha}u\right\rVert}_{L^{\infty}},&p=\infty\end{cases}

We also use the following

Sobolev spaces. For

are functions in

.
See, e.g.

For

2.3.
The Fractional Laplacian

Let
u\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
,
s\in(0,1)
. The fractional Laplacian on the torus is given by

(-\Delta_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}})^{s}u(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=(2\pi)^{2s}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}|k|^{2s}\widehat{u}(k)e^{2\pi ik\cdot x}

For simplicity, it is written
(-\Delta)^{s}
.

Alternatively we may define it using the integral definition (cf.
[
GM92
,
App09
,
DNPV12
]
).
For
u\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, i.e.
u\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
with
u(x+\kappa)=u(x)
for all
\kappa\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
, we can define
(-\Delta)^{s}u
as follows

(2.6)

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)=C(d,s)P.V.\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert}^{d+2s}}\dif y,\quad C(d,s)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\mathinner{\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{1-\cos(y_{1})}{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert y\right\rvert}^{d+2s}}\dif y\right)}^{-1}.

If
u\in{\mathcal{C}}^{2}
, then we can rewrite the integral as

(2.7)

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)=-\frac{1}{2}C(d,s)P.V.\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{u(x+y)+u(x-y)-2u(x)}{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert y\right\rvert}^{d+2s}}\dif y,

and by taking the Fourier transform we see that
\widehat{(-\Delta)^{s}u}(\kappa)=(2\pi\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\kappa\right\rvert})^{2s}\hat{u}(\kappa)
, so that the two definitions are equivalent.
From the integral definition we see that
(-\Delta)^{s}
is the generator of the transition semigroup of a
d
-dimensional,
2s
-stable pure jumps Lévy process with associated Lévy measure given by
\nu(\dif z)=C(d,s)\frac{\dif z}{|z|^{d+2s}}.

Now let
T(t)
be the semigroup generated by the fractional Laplacian. Then
T(t)
is a semigroup of contractions on
L^{p}
for all
p\in[1,\infty]
, i.e.

(2.8)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert T(t)f\right\rVert}_{p}\leq\mathinner{\!\left\lVert f\right\rVert}_{p},\quad\forall p\in[1,\infty].

Note for any
f\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})
and multi-indices
k,m,

(2.9)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert D^{k+m}T(t)f\right\rVert}_{p}\leq Ct^{-\frac{k}{2s}}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert D^{m}f\right\rVert}_{p}\quad\forall p\in[1,\infty].

These properties generalize to the following lemma which can be found in
[
CG19
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

(1)

For any
p>1
and
\nu\in{\mathbb{R}},\gamma\geq 0
, we have for all

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert T(t)f\right\rVert}_{\nu+\gamma,p}\leq Ct^{-\gamma/2s}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert f\right\rVert}_{\nu,p},

where
C=C(\nu,\gamma,d,s,p)
.

(2)

For any
\theta\in[0,s]
and
p>1
, there exists a constant

such that, for all

, it holds that

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert T(t)f-f\right\rVert}_{p}\leq Ct^{\theta/s}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert f\right\rVert}_{2\theta,p}.

We also will make use of the following embedding Theorem from
[

]

.

Let

,

,

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\frac{\beta}{s}-\frac{1}{p}}\mathinner{\left([0,T];H^{\mu-2\beta}_{p}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})\right)}}\leq C\mathinner{\left(\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}_{p}^{\mu}(Q_{T})}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u(0)\right\rVert}_{\mu-2s/p+\varepsilon,p}\right)}.

2.4.
Preliminary lemmas on fractional parabolic equations

Later we will make use of some basic results regarding the following fractional parabolic equation:

(2.11)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+(-\Delta)^{s}u+f(x,t)=0,\quad&\text{in }Q_{T},\\
u(x,T)=u_{T}(x),\quad&\text{in }{\mathbb{T}}^{d}.\end{cases}

The following two lemmas will be useful in the “bootstrapping” portion of our derivation of a priori estimates for solutions to the fractional Hamilton Jacobi Equation appearing in (
1.1
).

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose
s>\frac{1}{2}
,
\varepsilon>0
,
f\in L^{\infty}(Q_{T})
, and
u_{T}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{2s+\varepsilon}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})
. Let
u
be the solution of (
2.11
).
Then there exist constants
C>0
and
\beta\in(0,1)
independent of
u
and
f
such that

(2.12)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert Du\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\beta,\frac{\beta}{2s}}(Q_{T})}\leq C\mathinner{\left(\mathinner{\!\left\lVert f\right\rVert}_{\infty}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u_{T}\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{2s+\varepsilon}}\right)}

Proof.

Observe that
u_{T}\in H_{p}^{2s+\varepsilon}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})
and
f\in L^{p}(Q_{T})
for all
p>1
.
It follows from
[
CG19
, Theorem B.3]
that there exists a unique solution
u\in{\mathbb{H}}_{p}^{2s}
for arbitrarily large

.
It is enough to prove estimate (
2.12
).
We will do this for
u_{T}=0
and
f
arbitrary; a similar argument can be used for
f=0
and
u_{T}
arbitrary, and the general case follows by linearity.

Set
A=(-\Delta)^{s}
and let
T(t)
be the semigroup generated by
A
. Now for
\gamma\in(0,1)
, we have
A^{\gamma}=(-\Delta)^{s\gamma}
, and for all
p>1
we have

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert A^{\gamma}T(t)f\right\rVert}_{p}\leq\frac{C}{t^{\gamma}}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert f\right\rVert}_{p}

as a consequence of Lemma
2.1
.
Using this,

(2.13)

\begin{split}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert A^{\gamma}u(t)\right\rVert}_{p}&=\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\int_{0}^{t}A^{\gamma}T(t-s)f(s)\dif s\right\rVert}_{p}\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{t}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert A^{\gamma}T(t-s)f(s)\right\rVert}_{p}\dif s\\
&\leq C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\gamma}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert f(s)\right\rVert}_{p}\dif s\\
&\leq Ct^{1-\gamma}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert f\right\rVert}_{p}.\end{split}

Thus for
p>\frac{d}{2s\gamma}
,

\displaystyle\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u(t)\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{2s\gamma-d/p}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq C\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u(t)\right\rVert}_{H_{p}^{2s\gamma}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}\quad\text{(by \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{cirant2019existence}{}{}, Lemma 2.5]})}

\displaystyle\leq C\mathinner{\left(\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u(t)\right\rVert}_{p}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert A^{\gamma}u(t)\right\rVert}_{p}\right)}\quad\text{(see \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{cirant2019existence}{}{}, Remark 2.3]})}

\displaystyle\leq C\mathinner{\!\left\lVert f\right\rVert}_{p}\quad\text{(by \eqref{Agu leq fp})}

\displaystyle\leq C\mathinner{\!\left\lVert f\right\rVert}_{\infty}

where
C
is independent of
u
and
f
.
Therefore if we take
p
large and
\gamma
close enough to
1
so that

, we get
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert Du(t)\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\beta}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}\leq C\mathinner{\!\left\lVert f\right\rVert}_{\infty}
for

3.1.
Estimates for the FP equation

Consider the fractional Fokker-Planck equation in the periodic setting

(3.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}m+(-\Delta)^{s}m+\Div(bm)=0&\mbox{in }Q_{T}\\
m(x,0)=m_{0}(x)&\mbox{in }\mathbb{T}^{d},\end{cases}

where
m_{0}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})
is a probability density. Given
b\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})
such that
[\mbox{div}b]^{-}\in L^{\infty}(Q_{T})
, a function
m\in L^{2}(0,T;H_{2}^{s}({\mathbb{T}}^{d}))={\mathbb{H}}_{2}^{s}(Q_{T})
with
\partial_{t}m\in L^{2}(0,T;H_{2}^{-1}({\mathbb{T}}^{d}))={\mathbb{H}}_{2}^{-1}(Q_{T})
is a weak solution to (
3.1
) if for every
\varphi\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{d}\times[0,T))
one has

\iint_{Q_{T}}\mathinner{\left(-m\partial_{t}\varphi-bm\cdot D\varphi+(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}m(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\varphi\right)}\dif x\dif t=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}\varphi(x,0)m_{0}(x)\dif x.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
m_{0}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{0}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})
and
b\in C_{x}^{1}(Q_{T})
such that

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert m_{0}\right\rVert}_{\infty}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert b\right\rVert}_{\infty}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathinner{\left[\Div b\right]}^{-}\right\rVert}_{\infty}\leq K

Then there exists
C=C(K)
such that for every weak solution
m
, it holds that:

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathinner{\!\left\lVert m\right\rVert}_{\infty;Q_{T}}

\displaystyle\leq C

(3.3)

\displaystyle\iint_{Q_{T}}[(-\Delta)^{s/2}m]^{2}\dif x\dif t

\displaystyle\leq C

(3.4)

\displaystyle\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\partial_{t}m\right\rVert}_{{\mathbb{H}}_{2}^{-1}(Q_{T})}

\displaystyle\leq C

Proof.

We follow the proof of Proposition 3.3 from
[
CG19
]
. To show (
3.2
), one can use standard comparison arguments with the function

w(x,t)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=m(x,t)e^{-(K+\varepsilon)t}-\mathinner{\!\left\lVert m_{0}\right\rVert}_{\infty}

with
\varepsilon\to 0
to yield

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert m\right\rVert}_{\infty;Q_{T}}\leq\mathinner{\!\left\lVert m_{0}\right\rVert}_{\infty}e^{KT}.

Now by multiplying the equation in (
3.1
) by
m
and integrating over
Q_{T}
we get

\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\dfrac{\dif{}}{\dif{t}}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert m\right\rVert}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}^{2}+\iint_{Q_{T}}m(-\Delta)^{s}m\dif x\dif t=-\iint_{Q_{T}}m\Div(bm)\dif x\dif t.

Then by the properties of the fractional laplacian and integration by parts,

(3.5)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\dfrac{\dif{}}{\dif{t}}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert m\right\rVert}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}^{2}+\iint_{Q_{T}}\mathinner{\left[(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}m\right]}^{2}\dif x\dif t&=\iint_{Q_{T}}mb\cdot Dm\dif x\dif t\\
&=-\frac{1}{2}\iint_{Q_{T}}\mathinner{\left(\Div b\right)}m^{2}\dif x\dif t.\end{split}

Now using
[\Div(b)]^{-}\leq K
and the above
L^{\infty}
bound on
m
yields

\frac{1}{2}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert m(T)\right\rVert}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}+\iint_{Q_{T}}\mathinner{\left[(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}m\right]}^{2}\dif x\dif t\leq C(K)+\frac{1}{2}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert m(0)\right\rVert}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})},

which gives us inequality (
3.3
). Now from (
3.1
) one can get

\begin{split}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\iint_{Q_{T}}\partial_{t}m\varphi\dif x\dif t\right\rvert}\leq\mathinner{\!\left\lVert b\right\rVert}_{L^{\infty}(Q_{T})}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert m\right\rVert}_{L^{2}(Q_{T})}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert D\varphi\right\rVert}_{L^{2}(Q_{T})}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}m\right\rVert}_{L^{2}(Q_{T})}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert}_{{\mathbb{H}}^{s}_{2}(Q_{T})}\\
\leq C\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert}_{{\mathbb{H}}^{1}_{2}(Q_{T})},\end{split}

and the last estimate follows.
∎

3.4.
Semi-concavity for the HJ equation

We analyze semi-concavity properties of solutions of

(3.37)

\begin{cases}-\partial_{t}u+(-\Delta)^{s}u+H^{\theta}(x,Du,\mu)=0&\mbox{on }Q_{T}\\
u(x,T)=\theta u_{T}(x)&\mbox{in }\mathbb{T}^{d}\end{cases}

We prove that
u
is semi-concave with constant that depends on the data.

Lemma 3.13
.

For any
C>0
there is a constant
C_{1}=C_{1}(C)
such that if
L\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times\mathcal{P}\mathinner{\left({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\right)}\to{\mathbb{R}}
and
u_{T}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
are continuous and such that

(3.38)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert L^{\theta}(\cdot,\alpha,\mu)\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{2}}\leq C\theta\ \forall\alpha,\mu\ \text{s.t.}~\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert}\leq C,\Lambda_{\infty}(\mu)\leq C,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u_{T}\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{2}}\leq C,

then for each
\theta\in(0,1]
, (
3.37
) has a unique bounded continuous viscosity solution with bounded gradient
D_{x}u
which is given by the representation formula:

(3.39)

u(x,t)=\inf_{\alpha\in L^{\tilde{q}}([t,T],{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}{\mathbb{E}}\mathinner{\left[\int_{t}^{T}L^{\theta}(x(\tau),\alpha(\tau),\mu(\tau))\dif\tau+\theta u_{T}(x(T))\right]}

where trajectories
x(\tau)
are given by

(3.40)

\begin{cases}\dif x(\tau)=\alpha(\tau)\dif\tau+\dif J(\tau)\\
x(t)=x.\end{cases}

Here
J(\tau)
is a
d
-dimensional,
2s
-stable pure jumps Lévy process with associated Lévy measure given by
\nu(\dif z)=C(d,s)\frac{\dif z}{|z|^{d+2s}}.

Moreover,
u
satisfies the concavity estimate

(3.41)

D^{2}_{xx}u\leq C_{1}\theta I_{d}

in the sense of distributions.

Proof.

First note that the solution
u
to (
3.37
) has a unique bounded continuous viscosity solution with bounded graident by
[
DI06
, Theorm 3]
; see also
[
BKLT15
, Corollary 3.1]
. (For an overview of viscosity solutions, see e.g.
[
CIL92
]
).
We turn to the semiconcavity estimate.
Our proof uses a similar strategy as in
[
Car10
, Section 4.1]
.
Now by the Markov property, the value function

(3.42)

v(x,t)=\inf_{\alpha\in L^{\tilde{q}}([t,T],{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}{\mathbb{E}}\mathinner{\left[\int_{t}^{T}L^{\theta}(x(\tau),\alpha(\tau),\mu(\tau))\dif\tau+\theta u_{T}(x(T))\right]}

satisfies the dynamic programming principle, and thus
v
is a bounded continuous viscosity solution to (
3.17
). Therefore,
u=v.

Now we show the semi-concavity of
u
with respect to the
x
variable. Let
x,y,t\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times[0,T],

\lambda\in(0,1),

x_{\lambda}=\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y.
Also let
\alpha\in L^{\tilde{q}}([t,T],{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
be
\varepsilon
-optimal for
u(x_{\lambda},t)
and set
x_{\lambda}(s)=x_{\lambda}+\int_{s}^{t}\alpha(\tau)\dif\tau+\int_{s}^{t}\dif J(s).
Note that for any function
f
such that
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert f\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\leq C
, the following holds

(3.43)

\begin{split}&\lambda f(x_{\lambda}(s)+x-x_{\lambda})+(1-\lambda)f(x_{\lambda}(s)+y-x_{\lambda})\\
&\leq\lambda f(x_{\lambda}(s))+\lambda(x-x_{\lambda})\int_{0}^{1}D_{x}f(\theta x_{\lambda}(s)+\theta(x-x_{\lambda}))\dif\theta\\
&+(1-\lambda)f(x_{\lambda}(s))+(1-\lambda)(y-x_{\lambda})\int_{0}^{1}D_{x}f(\theta x_{\lambda}(s)+\theta(y-x_{\lambda}))\dif\theta\\
&=f(x_{\lambda}(s))+\lambda(1-\lambda)(x-y)\int_{0}^{1}\mathinner{\left[D_{x}f(\theta x_{\lambda}(s)+\theta(x-x_{\lambda}))-D_{x}f(\theta x_{\lambda}(s)+\theta(y-x_{\lambda}))\right]}\dif\theta\\

Then applying the inequality from (

and

(3.44)

\begin{split}\lambda u(x,t)&+(1-\lambda)u(y,t)\\
\leq&\lambda{\mathbb{E}}\mathinner{\left[\int_{t}^{T}L^{\theta}(x_{\lambda}(s)+x-x_{\lambda},\alpha(s),\mu(s))\dif s+\theta u_{T}(x_{\lambda}(T)+x-x_{\lambda})\right]}\\

Thus

is semi-concave with respect to

3.41
).
∎

4.
Existence and uniqueness

For the reader’s convenience, we recall the Leray-Schauder fixed point theorem:

Theorem 4.1

(Leray-Schauder)
.

Let
X
be a Banach space and let
T\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}X\times[0,1]\rightarrow X
be a continuous and compact mapping. Assume there exist
x_{0}\in X
and
C>0
so that
T(x,0)=x_{0}
for all
x\in X
and
\|x\|_{X}<C
for all
(x,\tau)\in X\times[0,1]
such that
T(x,\tau)=x
. Then there exists
x\in X
such that
T(x,1)=x
.

We are now ready to state our result on existence of solutions.

Theorem 4.2

(Main result)
.

Let
s\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
. Suppose Assumptions
(L1)
-
(L5)
,
(H1)
-
(H3)
, and
(I)
hold. Then there exists a solution
(u,m,\mu)
to (
1.1
).

Proof.

We plan to apply the Leray-Schauder theorem to prove existence of solutions. However,
\mathcal{P}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})
is not a Banach space, so we follow the same strategy as in Section 6.1 of
[
Kob22
]
.
Consider the map
\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}{\mathcal{C}}^{0}\left([0,T];H_{2}^{\varepsilon}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})\right)\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}^{0}\left([0,T];H_{2}^{\varepsilon}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})\right)
for some
\varepsilon>0
defined by

\rho(\tilde{m})(x,t)=\dfrac{\tilde{m}_{+}(x,t)-\int\tilde{m}_{+}(y,t)dy}{\max\left(1,\int\tilde{m}_{+}(y,t)dy\right)}+1,

where
\tilde{m}_{+}(x,t)=\max\left(0,\tilde{m}(x,t)\right)
. Also,
\tilde{m}^{0}
is defined as the the unique weak solution of

\partial_{t}\tilde{m}^{0}+(-\Delta)^{s}\tilde{m}^{0}=0,\mbox{ on }(0,T)\times{\mathbb{T}}^{d},\qquad\mbox{and }\tilde{m}^{0}(0,\cdot)=m_{0}.

Now we construct our map
\Psi
as follows. Let
\theta\in[0,1]
,

to be specified later.

Define a probability measure

and

\displaystyle\alpha(x,t)

\displaystyle\mu(t)

\displaystyle=\left(I_{d},\alpha(\cdot,t)\right)\#m(t)

\theta=0

\alpha=0
and
\mu(t)=m(t)\times\delta_{0}
.
Recall from Corollary
3.6
,

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Lambda_{\tilde{q}}(\mu(t))\leq C\theta.

Define

[
DI06

We see that

2.3

(4.2)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert D\bar{u}\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\beta,\frac{\beta}{2s}}(Q_{T})}\leq C\theta

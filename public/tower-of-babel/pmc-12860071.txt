Introduction

Mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) are increasingly recognized as promising cell-based therapeutics due to their pro-angiogenic, immunomodulatory, and anti-fibrotic properties [1, 2]. These functional capacities underpin ongoing clinical applications in conditions such as graft-versus-host disease, myocardial infarction, and pulmonary fibrosis, where MSCs promote tissue repair by modulating immune responses, stimulating neovascularization, and attenuating fibrosis [3–6]. Despite this therapeutic potential, the development of robust, scalable, and standardized MSC manufacturing processes remains a major barrier to clinical translation [7–9].

To address these persistent challenges in therapeutic cell manufacturing, we introduce the concept of cell manufacturability—a framework that evaluates a cell’s intrinsic ability to retain functionality under variable culture conditions [10, 11]. Even among genetically identical populations, MSCs exhibit heterogeneous responses to fluctuations in key culture parameters—known as critical process parameters (CPPs)—such as seeding density, culture duration, and passage number [7–9, 11]. These CPPs can profoundly influence the cell product’s critical quality attributes (CQAs)—including cytokine secretion, immunomodulatory activity, and regenerative potential—which are essential for therapeutic safety, potency, and consistency. Conventional characterization assays largely focus on static phenotypic markers or endpoint functional outputs and often lack the temporal and mechanistic resolution needed to capture subtle, cumulative changes in cell state induced by process variation [12, 13]. As a result, the ability to detect and interpret the dynamic internal fluctuations that MSCs undergo in response to environmental perturbations—fluctuations that ultimately shape therapeutic efficacy—remains limited. This presents a critical barrier to ensuring batch-to-batch consistency and achieving reproducible clinical outcomes.

Recent studies suggest that histone modifications—heritable yet reversible chromatin marks—can serve as integrative molecular sensors of a cell’s environmental experience [14–17]. In particular, trimethylation of histone H3 at lysine 4 (H3K4me3) and lysine 27 (H3K27me3) are well-characterized epigenetic marks associated with transcriptional activation and repression, respectively [15]. The relative balance of these marks, often expressed as the H3K4me3/H3K27me3 ratio, may serve as a proxy for chromatin readiness and transcriptional potential. In the context of MSC culture, low seeding density has been associated with H3K4me3 enrichment at immunomodulatory genes such as IDO-1, PTGES, and TNFAIP6 [14, 15]. Conversely, high-density conditions or extended passaging promote H3K27me3 accumulation at stress-related and senescence-associated loci, including CDKN2A (p16), DDIT3, and ATF4. Other histone modifications, such as H3K27ac and H3K9ac, have also demonstrated sensitivity to CPPs [15–17]. However, despite growing evidence of epigenetic responsiveness to culture conditions, a unified framework that links histone modification dynamics to MSC functional states and manufacturing outcomes remains lacking. Addressing this gap may facilitate a more comprehensive understanding of how cells respond to environmental fluctuations, thereby enhancing the predictability of cell quality in large-scale MSC production.

Here, we introduce an epigenetic framework for defining and quantifying MSC manufacturability, grounded in the concept of cellular feeling—a metaphorical lens through which we describe how cells encode and integrate fluctuating environmental cues (Fig. 1). This concept draws inspiration from the Japanese notion of yuragi within kimochi, which expresses subtle, moment-to-moment internal variations in response to external stimuli. By adopting this framing, we aim to highlight the graded, non-binary nature of cellular state transitions that underlie functional heterogeneity during manufacturing. To explore this, we focus on histone modifications at promoters of genes associated with angiogenic, immunomodulatory, and anti-fibrotic functions, evaluating their potential as molecular readouts of cell manufacturability. Using chromatin immunoprecipitation followed by quantitative PCR (ChIP–qPCR), we quantify H3K4me3 and H3K27me3 enrichment at 12 gene promoters linked to angiogenesis, immunomodulation, and fibrosis regulation. By applying multivariate statistical analysis, we assess the impact of key CPPs and tissue source variation on MSC epigenetic state. Our findings establish histone modification dynamics as a sensitive and quantitative readout of MSC environmental responsiveness. We further propose two composite metrics—the Cell Susceptibility Index (CSI) and the Cell Comparability Index (CCI)—to evaluate the influence of manufacturing parameters on cell potency and cross-batch comparability. This framework provides a mechanistic basis for improving process control and standardization in MSC-based cell therapy manufacturing.

Materials and methods

MSC sources and culture conditions

Human mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) derived from umbilical cord (MSC-UC) were provided by Prof. K. Yamahara (Hyogo Medical University, Japan), while bone marrow-derived MSCs (MSC-BM) were purchased from Lonza (Walkersville, MD, USA). Cells were maintained in MSC growth medium (Lonza) on tissue culture-treated polystyrene (Sigma-Aldrich, Corning, NY, USA) at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere with 5% CO2. Subculturing was carried out when the cultures reached approximately 70–80% confluence. To dissociate the cells, a solution of 0.1% trypsin and 0.02% EDTA (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) was applied. After enzymatic detachment, the cells were collected, gently resuspended in growth medium, and subsequently replated at a density of 3.0 × 10³ cells/cm2. Medium was replaced every three days. All experiments were performed using MSCs at passage 2, thawed after cryopreservation, to minimize variability due to the initial passage number and seeding density during expansion and cryopreservation.

Experimental framework for evaluating the effects of critical process parameters on epigenetic memory during MSC production

To address the challenge of maintaining consistent quality during MSC manufacturing, we propose an epigenetic framework for evaluating the environmental impact of key production steps. As outlined in Fig. 1, the manufacturing of MSC-based therapies consists of multiple unit operations—such as cell isolation, expansion, and cryopreservation—that introduce mechanical stress and biochemical perturbations, potentially compromising cell integrity and functionality. Among these, the expansion phase is particularly sensitive, involving serial passage-dependent steps governed by CPPs that can modulate candidate CQAs. This framework evaluates epigenetic memory as a readout of environmental susceptibility to define cell manufacturability in MSC production, and it was developed through a series of sequential steps. The experimental design is shown in Supplementary Fig. 1.

Step 1. Experimental design

To establish a framework for assessing the environmental impact on MSC manufacturability, we examined the effects of critical process parameters (CPPs)—including seeding density, culture duration, and passage number—on chromatin dynamics at the promoters of 12 candidate critical quality attribute (CQA)-associated genes related to angiogenesis, immunomodulation, and fibrosis regulation. The angiogenesis gene set included canonical regulators such as HGF, ANG-1, and TGF-β. Immunomodulatory genes encompassed both pro-inflammatory (IL-6, IL-8, TNF-α) and anti-inflammatory (IDO-1, TSG-6) factors, reflecting the dual immunoregulatory capacity of MSCs. Fibrosis-associated genes—COL-1, TIMP-1, MMP-1, and MMP-9—represented key mediators of extracellular matrix (ECM) remodeling and fibrotic processes. Two histone modifications were analyzed: the activating mark H3K4me3 and the repressive mark H3K27me3.

Evaluation of culture duration and seeding density

Cells were seeded at 0.3 and 1.5 × 104 cells/cm2 on PS surface and then cultured in MSC growth medium (Lonza, USA) at 37℃ with 5% CO2 in a humidified atmosphere. Subsequently, the medium was changed every 3 days for 15 days. The cells harvested with 0.1% trypsin/0.02% EDTA at different time points for analysis (3, 6, 9, 12, and 15 days) and cell potency was subsequently evaluated.

Evaluation of seeding density and passage number

Cells were seeded at 0.3, 0.6, 0.9, 1.2, and 1.5 × 104 cells/cm2 on PS surface and then cultured in MSC growth medium at 37℃ with 5% CO2 in a humidified atmosphere. For this expansion experiment, the cells were serially passaged one to five times every 5 days. Subsequently, the medium was changed every 3 days. At the end of the 5-day culture period during 5-passage culture, cells harvested with 0.1% trypsin/0.02% EDTA and cell potency was subsequently evaluated.

Step 2. Evaluation of MSC potency following IFN-γ treatment

To assess the functional potency of MSCs under different culture conditions, cells were stimulated with the pro-inflammatory cytokine interferon-γ (IFN-γ; R&D systems, Minneapolis, USA) at a concentration of 10 ng/mL for 48 h. Following stimulation, cells were cultured for an additional 3 days (total 5-day post-treatment period), then harvested using 0.1% trypsin/0.02% EDTA for downstream analysis.

Step 3. Assessment of activating (H3K4me3) and repressive (H3K27me3) histone modifications at the promoters of CQA-associated genes

To investigate the epigenetic regulation of MSC potency, we selected the promoters of selected 12 CQA-associated genes, encompassing three functional categories: angiogenesis, immunomodulation, and fibrosis regulation. Chromatin immunoprecipitation followed by quantitative PCR (ChIP–qPCR) was performed to quantify histone modifications at these sites, specifically assessing enrichment of transcriptionally activating H3K4me3 and repressive H3K27me3 marks as indicators of chromatin accessibility and transcriptional readiness (Supplementary Fig. 1). ChIP assays were performed using a SimpleChIP® Enzymatic Chromatin Immunoprecipitation Kit with magnetic beads (Cell Signaling Technology, Danvers, MA, USA), according to the manufacturers’ instructions. Briefly, cells were crosslinked with 1% formaldehyde for 10 min. After isolating the nuclei by lysis of the cytoplasmic fraction, the immunoprecipitation preparations were incubated with micrococcal nuclease for 20 min at 37 °C with frequent mixing to digest the DNA into fragments of 150–900 base pairs in length. Sonicated nuclear lysates were divided into aliquots for input control and ChIP using the following antibodies: anti-H3K4me3 (trimethyl-histone H3 at lysine 4, rabbit monoclonal, Cell Signaling Technology), anti-H3K27me3 (trimethyl-histone H3 at lysine 27, rabbit monoclonal, Cell Signaling Technology), and normal rabbit IgG (negative control, Cell Signaling Technology). Immunoprecipitation was carried out overnight at 4 °C using ChIP-grade protein G magnetic beads. An anti-histone H3 antibody (rabbit polyclonal, Cell Signaling Technology) was used as a positive control to verify successful immunoprecipitation. After elution of bound chromatin from beads, DNA was purified using spin columns provided in the kit. Quantitative PCR was conducted using the Applied Biosystems 7300 Real-Time PCR System and SYBR® Premix Ex Taq (Takara Bio Inc.), in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions. Primer sequences used for ChIP-qPCR are listed in Supplementary Table 1. ChIP-qPCR data were normalized to input samples (2% starting chromatin) to normalize for variations in the amount of input DNA and the efficiency of the immunoprecipitation process, according to the percent input method. It should be noted that SYBR Green–based qPCR in this study provides relative quantification using the ΔΔCt method, rather than absolute copy number determination.

Multivariate data analysis to evaluate MSC susceptibility and comparability

To assess the relationship between CPPs and cell potency, we employed principal component analysis (PCA), an unsupervised statistical technique used to explore complex datasets and reduce dimensionality. The first few principal components (e.g., PC1, PC2, PC3) capture the most significant variance within the dataset, enabling effective visualization and analysis while minimizing information loss. The selection of principal components was guided by evaluating the proportion of total variance explained, as illustrated in a scree plot of eigenvalues, alongside eigenvalue magnitudes and the interpretability of loadings—favoring components that revealed meaningful expression patterns rather than redundancy. PCA was applied to datasets derived from ChIP-qPCR analyses of 12 CQA-associated genes, to generate a cell potency index and examine its association with process variability. Here, the contribution ratio (explained variance) of each principal component was used as the weight (wi), as this approach ensures that components capturing greater proportions of biological variance exert proportionally larger influence on the composite score, whereas components with minimal variance contribute less, thereby reducing the impact of noise. The resulting score was then standardized (mean-centered), and with slight modifications based on a previous study [18–21], it was adopted as the Cell Susceptibility Index (CSI). The CSI represents a composite measure of how sensitive a given cell population is to environmental or process-induced fluctuations. It reflects the degree of change in multidimensional cell states (e.g., epigenetic or transcriptional features) in response to CPPs. A higher CSI suggests greater susceptibility, whereas lower values may indicate epigenetic stability or reduced responsiveness to environmental variation.

Statistical analysis and plotting

Volcano plots and PCA plots were created using GraphPad Prism 9.0 (GraphPad Software, Inc., San Diego, CA, USA). Statistical significance was determined using an unpaired two-tailed Student’s t-test. Spearman correlation coefficients were calculated to assess marker associations and visualized as heatmaps. A P-value < 0.05 was considered significant. All data are presented as mean ± s.d. from 10 independent culture batches, defined as parallel cultures derived from the same donor MSCs but maintained in separate vessels to capture variability arising from CPPs.

Results

Epigenetic remodeling at promoters of CQA-associated genes in response to culture parameters

To evaluate the impact of CPPs on MSC-UC, cells were seeded at low and high densities and harvested at defined intervals (3, 6, 9, 12, and 15 days) for downstream analysis (Supplementary Fig. 1). Following IFN-γ stimulation, ChIP–qPCR was performed to assess enrichment of activating (H3K4me3) and repressive (H3K27me3) histone modifications at promoters of selected 12 CQA-associated genes.

Correlative analysis of H3K4me3 and H3K27me3 at the promoters of selected genes revealed locus-specific variability during the culture period, with certain promoters exhibiting robust inverse relationships while others showed minimal or no correlation (Fig. 2a and b). Notably, these epigenetic association patterns were further modulated in a seeding density-dependent manner.

Correlation analysis of histone modifications at CQA gene promoters under varying CPPs. a, b Scatter plots showing the relationship between H3K27me3 and H3K4me3 enrichment at the promoters of CQA-associated genes across conditions varying in seeding density, passage number, and culture duration. Data are presented as mean ± s.d. (n = 10 independent culture batches per condition). Genes that exhibited increased correlation coefficients from low- to high-density conditions, or before and after serial passaging, are indicated in gray in the graph. c, d Heatmap showing Pearson correlation coefficients (R) and P-values comparing low- and high-density conditions, or pre- and post-serial passaging. Statistical significance was defined as P < 0.05 (*), P < 0.01 (**), and P < 0.001 (***)

Under low-density conditions, moderate to strong positive correlations were observed among genes associated with anti-inflammatory and angiogenic functions, including IDO-1 (R = 0.3826, P = 0.0061), ANG-1 (R = 0.5999, P < 0.0001), and COL-1 (R = 0.4534, P = 0.0009). In contrast, pro-inflammatory genes such as IL-8 (R = 0.2943, P = 0.0380) and TNF-α (R = 0.2963, P = 0.0367) exhibited weaker correlations. Notably, anti-inflammatory (TSG-6) and angiogenesis-associated genes (HGF, TGF-β) and fibrotic-related genes (TIMP-1, MMP-1) showed weak or negative correlations under low-density conditions, which became significantly stronger and more positive under high-density culture.

Under high-density conditions, several genes that showed weak or negative correlations under low density—such as TSG-6, HGF, TGF-β, TIMP-1, and MMP-1—displayed significantly enhanced positive correlations. Specifically, TSG-6 (R = 0.606, P < 0.0001), HGF (R = 0.4830, P = 0.0005), TGF-β (R = 0.2866, P = 0.0459), TIMP-1 (R = 0.3615, P = 0.0099), and MMP-1 (R = 0.801, P < 0.0001) all exhibited stronger positive correlations between H3K4me3 and H3K27me3 at high seeding densities. In contrast, the correlations at IDO-1, ANG-1, and COL-1 became weaker or shifted to negative values under high-density conditions, indicating that chromatin co-regulation at these loci is disrupted or modulated in a density-dependent manner. Collectively, these results suggest that although culture duration influences histone modification patterns over time, seeding density exerts a more dominant effect, accelerating locus-specific chromatin remodeling and reshaping the epigenetic architecture in a gene- and stress-specific manner.

To further investigate the impact of seeding density and serial passaging on histone modification at the promoters of selected target genes in MSC-UC cultures, cells were plated at varying densities (0.3, 0.6, 0.9, 1.2, and 1.5 × 10⁴ cells/cm²) and expanded over five passages (Fig. 1). Histone modification profiles were subsequently assessed following IFN-γ stimulation, as described previously. Correlations between H3K4me3 and H3K27me3 at the promoters of selected genes varied across different seeding densities and were further modulated in a passage-dependent manner (Fig. 2c and d).

Before passage, moderate to strong positive correlations between H3K4me3 and H3K27me3 were observed across varying seeding density at the promoters of most genes, including IDO-1 (R = 0.384, P = 0.059), IL-6 (R = 0.4453, P = 0.0012), IL-8 (R = 0.4211, P = 0.0023), TNF-α (R = 0.6281, P < 0.0001), HGF (R = 0.3806, P = 0.0064), TGF-β (R = 0.3707, P = 0.0080), COL-1 (R = 0.7498, P < 0.0001), TIMP-1 (R = 0.3931, P = 0.0047), and MMP-9 (R = 0.6961, P < 0.0001). Notably, weaker or negative correlations were observed at TSG-6 and ANG-1.

Following serial passaging, further increases in correlation were observed at IDO-1 (R = 0.6387, P < 0.0001) and HGF (R = 0.7792, P < 0.0001). Moreover, TSG-6 and ANG-1 exhibited a marked shift from weak or negative to strongly positive correlations (R = 0.4107, P = 0.0031; R = 0.5480, P < 0.0001, respectively), indicating passaging-induced chromatin reconfiguration. In contrast, several genes—including IL-8, TNF-α, COL-1, TIMP-1, MMP-1, and MMP-9—displayed diminished or negative correlations post-passage, suggesting locus-specific decoupling of chromatin marks in response to extended culture.

Quantitative profiling of histone modification dynamics in relation to culture conditions

To compare the relative abundance of activating and repressive histone modifications at the promoters of selected target genes, we calculated the log₂-transformed K4/K27 ratios. This ratio serves as an indicator of chromatin state, distinguishing transcriptionally poised regions—particularly bivalent domains enriched for both marks. Elevated K4/K27 values indicate a more permissive chromatin configuration, whereas lower values reflect repressive or silenced states. Under low-density conditions, K4/K27 ratios at promoters of immunomodulatory genes (IDO-1, TSG-6, IL-6, IL-8), angiogenesis-associated genes (ANG-1), and fibrotic genes (COL-1, TIMP-1) tended to remain elevated over time, primarily due to sustained H3K4me3 enrichment, consistent with active or bivalent chromatin states (Fig. 3a). In contrast, loci associated with pro-inflammatory (TNF-α), angiogenesis-related (HGF, TGF-β), and anti-fibrotic (MMP-1, MMP-9) genes exhibited progressive declines in K4/K27 ratios, indicative of increasing transcriptional repression.

Positive values indicate activating chromatin states; negative values indicate repressive states. Data represent mean ± s.d. (n = 10 independent culture batches per condition). Comparisons between seeding densities and passage conditions were assessed using unpaired t-tests. Statistical significance was defined as P < 0.05 (*), P < 0.01 (**), and P < 0.001 (***)

These patterns in K4/K27 ratios showed consistent trends across both low- and high-density conditions. Notably, under low-density culture, promoters of IDO-1, IL-6, ANG-1, and COL-1 maintained relatively high K4/K27 ratios despite concurrent increases in H3K27me3 levels; in several cases, however, these observations did not reach statistical significance. This suggests the establishment of a bivalent chromatin state, potentially supporting transcriptional flexibility. By contrast, high-density culture led to a pronounced reduction in K4/K27 ratios at these loci, consistent with a shift toward a repressive epigenetic configuration and diminished transcriptional potential. Meanwhile, pro-inflammatory and anti-fibrotic genes such as TNF-α, MMP-1, and MMP-9 consistently exhibited low K4/K27 ratios across all conditions, reflecting persistent transcriptional repression mediated by dominant H3K27me3 enrichment.

We next examined the effects of seeding density and serial passaging on chromatin states. Across both early and late passages, K4/K27 ratios recapitulated the density-dependent trends. Immunomodulatory, angiogenic, and fibrotic genes (e.g., IDO-1, IL-6, ANG-1, COL-1) retained active or bivalent configurations under low-density conditions, whereas pro-inflammatory and anti-fibrotic genes (TNF-α, MMP-1, MMP-9) remained transcriptionally repressed under high-density conditions (Fig. 3b). Among these, TSG-6 and ANG-1 showed only modest decreases with increasing seeding density. Interestingly, although TNF-α, MMP-1, and MMP-9 were primarily repressed, slight increases in their K4/K27 ratios under high-density conditions suggest a partial shift toward chromatin de-repression. Collectively, these results demonstrate that CCPs—including seeding density, culture duration, and passaging—modulate chromatin states in a gene-specific manner, reflecting the dynamic and context-dependent nature of epigenetic regulation in MSCs.

Epigenetically informed potency index captures process-driven changes in MSC state

To quantify CPP-induced epigenetic changes, we established a cell potency index, defined as the fold change in the K4/K27 ratio relative to baseline. Baseline conditions were defined as the state at day 0, prior to the application of controlled CPPs and in the absence of IFN-γ stimulation, thereby providing a reference point for assessing culture-induced changes. Under low-density conditions, the potency index for immunomodulatory (IDO-1, TSG-6, IL-8, TNF-α), angiogenesis-associated (HGF), and fibrotic (COL-1, TIMP-1, MMP-9) genes increased by day 3 and remained above baseline through day 15. Among these, IDO-1 and HGF showed the most pronounced changes, with average fold increases of approximately 11.0 and 11.25 at day 9, respectively (Fig. 4a). In contrast, IL-6 and TIMP-1 remained below baseline levels, while ANG-1 and TGF-β showed transient upregulation followed by a decline below baseline by day 15.

Indices > 1 reflect increased potency, < 1 indicate repression. Comparisons between seeding densities and passage conditions were assessed using unpaired t-tests. The dotted horizontal line indicates the baseline level at day 0. Comparisons between low vs. high seeding density and early vs. late passage conditions were assessed using unpaired t-tests. Under high-density conditions, similar trends were observed. However, TNF-α, MMP-1, and MMP-9 displayed more sustained or enhanced potency indices, whereas COL-1 and ANG-1 remained consistently downregulated. These results suggest that both culture duration and seeding density modulate epigenetic activation, attenuating transcriptional activity at key angiogenic (ANG-1) and fibrotic (COL-1, TIMP-1) loci, while favoring upregulation of pro-inflammatory (TNF-α) and anti-fibrotic (MMP-1, MMP-9) genes.

We further assessed the cell potency index across a range of seeding densities and after serial passaging to evaluate the impact of CPPs on epigenetic responsiveness. Before passaging, immunomodulatory (IDO-1, TSG-6, IL8), angiogenesis-associated (ANG-1, HGF), and fibrotic (COL-1) genes showed increased indices at the lowest seeding density (0.3 × 10⁴ cells/cm²), which were sustained or further elevated at the highest density (1.5 × 10⁴ cells/cm²). IDO-1 and HGF exhibited the most pronounced increases in potency index, reaching 18.08 and 15.21-fold changes at 1.5 × 10⁴ cells/cm², respectively (Fig. 4b). In contrast, IL-6, TNF-α, TIMP-1, MMP-1, and MMP-9 remained below baseline across all seeding densities, while ANG-1 and TGF-β were transiently upregulated at low to moderate densities but declined at the highest.

Following serial passaging, overall trends remained consistent. However, TNF-α, MMP-1, and MMP-9 exhibited greater increases in potency indices under high-density conditions compared to pre-passaging, whereas COL-1 and ANG-1 remained downregulated across increasing densities. These findings indicate that high seeding density and repeated passaging exert minimal impact on the activation of anti-inflammatory (IDO-1) and angiogenic (HGF) genes, but limit epigenetic activation at other loci while promoting pro-inflammatory and anti-fibrotic gene expression.

Multidimensional assessment of cell manufacturability via susceptibility and comparability metrics

To expand our manufacturability framework based on the histone-derived cell potency index, we performed both univariate and multivariate statistical analyses. These analyses aimed to capture the cumulative impact of potency-associated features derived from datasets presented in Fig. 4. As shown in Fig. 5a, principal component analysis (PCA) was applied to detect coordinated variance among variables influenced by CPPs, including culture duration, seeding density, and passage number. PCA enabled dimensionality reduction by identifying components that account for the greatest variance within the dataset. In the resulting PC plots, each variable’s contribution is visualized as a vector, with arrow length denoting the magnitude of influence. These loadings were further used to construct a weighted, PCA-based index for assessing both cell susceptibility and comparability.

PCA revealed that the first two principal components (PC1 and PC2) accounted for 44.9% of total variance (Fig. 5b). IL-6, IDO-1, TIMP-1, TNF-α, MMP-1, and MMP-9 contributed most to PC1, as their loadings exceeded the average (Fig. 5c, d). In contrast, PC2 was primarily shaped by TSG-6, ANG-1, and TGF-β. The PCA score plot demonstrated clear separation of samples along PC1 and PC2, reflecting the variance attributable to seeding density and passage status (Fig. 5e). Under low-density conditions, PC1 scores declined over time with little variation in PC2, whereas high-density cultures showed reduced PC2 scores, indicating density-specific variance components. Furthermore, passage status modulated PCA loadings, with post-passage samples loading positively on PC1 and pre-passage samples negatively, suggesting a passage-dependent shift in variance structure.

To further identify features that were differentially regulated under varying CPPs, we performed Pearson correlation heatmap analysis (Fig. 5f), which revealed strong associations among key genes, with the exception of weak correlations observed between MMP-1, MMP-9, and TNF-α. These findings were further supported by volcano plot analyses. At day 6, MMP-1, MMP-9, and TNF-α were significantly upregulated in high-density cultures, while IL-8, COL-1, and TIMP-1 were significantly downregulated (Fig. 5g). Following passaging, MMP-1 and MMP-9 expression was upregulated in high-density cultures, while TSG-6 and COL-1 were significantly downregulated.

To quantitatively evaluate cell susceptibility and comparability, we derived two composite indices from the PCA outputs: the Cell Susceptibility Index (CSI) and the Cell Comparability Index (CCI). A positive correlation between CSI and CCI suggests that, despite high intra-condition variability (high CSI), cells maintain a consistent epigenetic trajectory across environments (high CCI), indicative of coordinated and adaptive chromatin responses (Fig. 6a). In contrast, a negative correlation implies that increased epigenetic fluctuation is accompanied by reduced inter-condition coherence, reflecting regulatory instability and compromised transcriptional control. CSI was largely independent of culture duration, whereas CCI exhibited a weak positive correlation with time in low-density cultures (R2 = 0.0224, P = 0.2995) and a weak negative correlation under high-density conditions (R2 = 0.0046, P = 0.6408) (Fig. 6b). In contrast, CSI was positively correlated with seeding density both prior to and following passage (R2 = 0.4569, P < 0.0001; R2 = 0.4754, P < 0.0001, respectively) (Fig. 6c). Conversely, CCI was negatively correlated under the same conditions (R2 = 0.3439 P < 0.0001; R2 = 0.4832, P < 0.0001, respectively), indicating an antagonistic relationship between susceptibility and comparability metrics under proliferative stress.

To further explore the interplay between CSI and CCI, we analyzed their correlation across varying culture durations and seeding densities, which yielded a weak overall relationship (R2 = 0.1022, P = 0.0012) (Fig. 6d, e). However, when focusing specifically on changes in seeding density and passage number—two key CPPs—CSI and CCI exhibited strong inverse correlations (R2 = 0.9485, P < 0.0001; R2 = 0.9949, P < 0.0001, respectively), underscoring the sensitivity and robustness of these indices in capturing chromatin state dynamics. Collectively, the results show that higher seeding densities and repeated passaging increase cell susceptibility (CSI) while decreasing cell comparability (CCI), indicating a divergence between epigenetic responsiveness and chromatin coherence under proliferative stress.

To apply the CSI and CCI metrics to bone marrow–derived MSCs (MSC-BMs), we cultured MSC-BMs at two seeding densities—low (0.3 × 10⁴ cells/cm²) and high (1.5 × 10⁴ cells/cm²)—and serially passaged five times. CSI and CCI were quantified before and after passage using the same evaluation framework. These results were compared with those obtained from MSC-UCs. PCA revealed that the most informative components were predominantly influenced by seeding density and passage number (Supplementary Fig. 2). Notably, MSC-BM samples showed similar plotting patterns to MSC-UC samples, reinforcing the generalizability of the CSI–CCI framework across tissue sources and manufacturing conditions (Fig. 6). Together, these findings establish a robust, multidimensional framework for evaluating cell manufacturability, integrating CSI and CCI as mechanistically grounded metrics to monitor cellular quality and manufacturing consistency.

Discussion

While the therapeutic potential of MSCs is well established, scalable manufacturing remains limited by cellular heterogeneity and context-dependent variability, often described as cell sensitivity or batch variation [7–9]. To address this, we introduce the concept of cell manufacturability, defined as the intrinsic capacity of a cell to maintain functional stability under dynamic process inputs [10, 11]. At the core of this framework lies epigenetic memory, which serves as a mechanistic readout of cellular adaptability and phenotypic plasticity in response to environmental conditions during manufacturing.

Our research group has been investigating the formation of cellular memory, along with associated cell states and potency, during in vitro expansion culture [22–24]. Notably, we have demonstrated the dynamic nature of epigenetic memory—its formation, reinitialization, and eventual consolidation—in both induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) and MSCs, as revealed by histone modification analyses [23, 24]. These processes reflect the culture history—including seeding density, passage number, and culture duration—which collectively influence the formation of mechanical memory by modulating cellular behavior. During culture, this memory is established through dynamic histone modifications; it can be reinitialized—or partially erased—during passaging, thereby restoring cellular plasticity [23]. However, sustained and repeated exposure to suboptimal culture conditions leads to the consolidation of maladaptive cellular memory, reducing cellular responsiveness and constraining cells into more restricted functional states. This evolving epigenetic landscape is closely linked to cell identity and potency, and serves as a critical parameter for assessing and optimizing cell manufacturability.

Expanding upon this foundation, we propose the concept of cellular feeling, which describes how cells perceive and encode the surrounding culture environments through dynamic chromatin remodeling. This notion draws inspiration from Japanese notions of yuragi, denoting gentle, adaptive fluctuation, and kimochi, referring to internal sensation or awareness. Within this framework, histone modifications such as H3K4me3 (activating) and H3K27me3 (repressive) serve as molecular sensors that encode the cumulative impact of environmental inputs as epigenetic memory. Importantly, yuragi is not interpreted as random noise but as a regulated and meaningful variability that supports cellular responsiveness and adaptive flexibility [25–28]. In the context of therapeutic cell manufacturing, yuragi has emerged as a biologically relevant indicator of how cells sense and adapt to process parameters. This view aligns with contemporary perspectives in regenerative medicine, where epigenetic memory and chromatin dynamics are increasingly recognized as integral to cellular experience and phenotypic plasticity. Quantitative evaluation of such fluctuations offers a powerful framework for interpreting cell stability and manufacturing robustness.

To explore this concept mechanistically, we investigated how CPPs—including culture duration, seeding density, and passage number—influence MSC phenotype through mechanoresponsive signaling. These parameters influence cytoskeletal architecture, nuclear mechanics, and chromatin organization via interactions with the extracellular matrix and neighboring cells [22–24]. The resulting histone modification patterns—particularly dynamic changes in H3K4me3 and H3K27me3 at promoters of CQA-associated genes—constitute a molecular memory of environmental exposure, which we interpret as a quantifiable correlate of cellular feeling.

To quantify these chromatin-based responses, we conceptualized cell manufacturability as a dynamic property indexed by the K4/K27 ratios at the promoters of CQA-associated genes. Genes involved in anti-inflammatory (IDO-1, TSG-6), angiogenic (TGF-β, ANG-1), and fibrotic (COL-1, TIMP-1) pathways predominantly exhibited H3K4me3 enrichment, reflecting active chromatin states. In contrast, pro-inflammatory (TNF-α) and anti-fibrotic (MMP-1, MMP-9) genes were enriched for H3K27me3, indicating transcriptional repression, consistent with previous findings [29–35] (Fig. 2a, b). Among process parameters, seeding density had the strongest influence on chromatin status, particularly enhancing activation of pro-inflammatory and anti-fibrotic genes under high-density conditions [9, 34]. Serial passaging also contributed to increased epigenetic repression, especially at anti-fibrotic loci, in line with known chromatin remodeling under cellular stress and senescence [35]. While passage number or frozen cell density at purchase was not disclosed, future work should systematically evaluate these supplier-dependent variables, as they may influence initial chromatin states.

To capture and interpret this variability, we established a cell potency index based on K4/K27 ratios at the promoters of CQA-associated genes and applied weighted PCA to derive two composite metrics: the Cell Susceptibility Index (CSI), reflecting environmental responsiveness, and the Cell Comparability Index (CCI), quantifying both across donor sources and among varying culture conditions (Fig. 6). These indices revealed condition-dependent patterns: low-stress cultures (e.g., low seeding density, early passage) showed a positive correlation between CSI and CCI, suggestive of reproducible adaptive plasticity, while high-stress conditions exhibited a strong negative correlation, indicating unstable epigenetic fluctuations. These trends were consistently observed across various MSC sources, supporting the robustness and general applicability of this framework. Together, these findings highlight that incorporating CSI and CCI into assessments of cell manufacturability provides a mechanistically informed and statistically robust framework to enhance standardization, inter-batch comparability, and predictive control in MSC manufacturing.

Despite these promising findings, this study has several important limitations that should be considered. First, our epigenetic analysis was limited to a preselected panel of 12 gene promoters associated with MSC function. Given that ChIP–qPCR inherently targets only specific genomic loci, this approach provides a constrained view of the broader epigenetic landscape. Although this targeted design allowed us to explore direct links between epigenetic memory and functionally relevant CQAs, the generalizability of the CSI and CCI requires further validation using genome-wide approaches such as ChIP-seq or CUT&RUN. Moreover, to enhance translational applicability, future studies should extend this framework to include disease-specific or mechanism-driven loci relevant to diverse therapeutic contexts.

Second, while the CSI and CCI indices offer a useful framework for assessing chromatin-based susceptibility and comparability, they remain indirect proxies of functional potency. Although histone marks such as H3K4me3 and H3K27me3 are mechanistically linked to MSC functions—including activation of immunomodulatory and angiogenic genes (e.g., IDO-1, IL-6, TGF-β, ANG-1) and repression of anti-fibrotic pathways (e.g., MMP-1, MMP-9) [36–38]—transcriptional outputs and direct functional assays were not performed in this study. As a result, key functional attributes such as immunomodulation, angiogenesis, and ECM remodeling were not directly assessed. Establishing correlations between CSI/CCI values and these functional outcomes in preclinical or clinical models will be essential to validate their utility as predictive quality metrics. Future integration of such benchmarks will help position our framework within the broader MSC quality assessment landscape.

Finally, the CSI and CCI indices presented in this study provide a useful framework; however, they do not provide direct evidence linking them to mechanical signaling changes. The mechanistic model presented in Fig. 7—linking CPPs, cytoskeletal signaling pathways involving YAP/TAZ, myosin contractility, and actin dynamics, and histone modifications—should be interpreted as a conceptual model inspired by prior literature [22–24, 39, 40]. Its purpose is to provide a basis for future experimental validation of how mechanical signals influence histone-based epigenetic memory and, ultimately, MSC potency. Experimental validation of this model will be necessary to establish its biological and translational relevance.

Proposed model of chromatin-mediated regulation of MSC potency during manufacturing. CPPs—such as seeding density, passage number, and culture duration—modulate the mechanical microenvironment and activate cytoskeletal signaling pathways involving YAP/TAZ, myosin contractility, and actin dynamics, which in turn influence histone modifications and reshape chromatin states. These cues alter histone modification patterns, particularly the H3K4me3/H3K27me3 balance at key loci, with the resulting K4/K27 ratio serving as an epigenetic memory of manufacturing inputs linked to MSC potency. This mechanistic model should be regarded as a hypothetical framework, highlighting the gene-specific nature of chromatin responses to CPPs and supporting histone profiling as a readout of cell feeling and manufacturing consistency

In conclusion, our findings suggest that histone-based epigenetic memory may function as a mechanistic and quantitative readout of environmental influence on MSCs. The concept of cellular feeling—reinterpreted here as epigenetic encoding of culture fluctuations—offers a potential perspective for evaluating cell manufacturability. While further validation in larger and more diverse manufacturing settings is needed, incorporating this epigenetic perspective into early-stage manufacturing analytics may support predictive potency assessments, help to enhance batch-to-batch consistency, and contribute to scalable manufacturing within the context of a quality-by-design framework for MSC-based therapeutics.

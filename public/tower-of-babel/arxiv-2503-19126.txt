Tractable downfall of basis pursuit in structured sparse optimization Thanks: The project was supported by the Israel Science Foundation (grant no. 2406/22), while the second author was a Jane and Larry Sherman Fellow.
Maya V. Marmary, and, Christian Grussler

Abstract
The problem of finding the sparsest solution to a linear underdetermined system of equations, often appearing, e.g., in data analysis, optimal control, system identification or sensor selection problems, is considered. This non-convex problem is commonly solved by convexification via ℓ 1 \ell_{1} -norm minimization, known as basis pursuit (BP). In this work, a class of structured matrices, representing the system of equations, is introduced for which (BP) tractably fails to recover the sparsest solution. In particular, this enables efficient identification of matrix columns corresponding to unrecoverable non-zero entries of the sparsest solution and determination of the uniqueness of such a solution. These deterministic guarantees complement popular probabilistic ones and provide insights into the a priori design of sparse optimization problems. As our matrix structures appear naturally in optimal control problems, we exemplify our findings based on a fuel-optimal control problem for a class of discrete-time linear time-invariant systems. Finally, we draw connections of our results to compressed sensing and common basis functions in geometric modeling.



Keywords—
Sparse optimization, basis pursuit, total positivity, optimal control, compressed sensing, variation diminishing.

In other words,
X\in\mathds{R}^{n\times m}
,
n>m
, is (strictly)
m
-sign consistent if and only if the matrix
C
in
Lemma

3
is (strictly) totally positive.
Finally, a vector
a\in\mathds{R}^{n}
with
a\geq 0
is said to be
log-concave
if it satisfies

a_{k+1}^{2}\geq a_{k}a_{k+2}\text{ for all }k\geq 2,

and
unimodal
if there exists an
l\in(1:n)
such that

a_{1}\leq\ldots\leq a_{l}\geq\ldots\geq a_{n}.

We also use these notions in the infinite case of sequences
\{a_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
. Equivalently,
a
is unimodal if and only if
\text{S}(\Delta a)\leq 1
and the possible sign switch occurs from positive to negative. In particular, log-concavity is known to imply unimodality
[
29
]
.

3
Main results

Our work establishes tractable conditions under which the basis pursuit approach fails to solve
Eq.

ℓ
0

. We first derive a general condition based on the
\ell_{1}
-norm of the columns of a general matrix
V
, and demonstrate its relevance to structured matrices. A uniqueness guarantee for the solution of
Eq.

ℓ
0

shows that our conditions are not limited to trivial non-unique cases, where basis pursuit inherently fails. We then show that both, the failure and uniqueness conditions can be efficiently verified for system matrices such as
V=\mathcal{C}^{N}(A,b)\in\text{SC}_{m}
. Leveraging total positivity theory
[
29
,
37
]
, we further extend our findings to general
V\in\text{SC}_{m}
and discuss its suitability for compressed sensing and connection to geometric modeling.

3.2
Tractability for Systems Matrices

In this subsection, we want to show that by restricting ourselves to system matrices/operators, it is possible to drive tractable conditions for determining the indices with
p_{k}<1
. The first class of matrices we examine is
V=\mathcal{C}^{N}(A,b)
, which satisfies
p_{m+i}<1
for all
i\geq 1
.
To keep our derivations independent of the number of columns in
V
, we present our result in the infinite-dimensional case.

Theorem 2
.

Let
A\in\mathds{R}^{m\times m}
,
b\in\mathds{R}^{m}
and
V=\mathcal{C}(A,b)
and
r=\textnormal{rank}(V)
. If the characteristic polynomial

\det(sI-V_{(:,(1:r))}^{\dagger}AV_{(:,(1:r))})=s^{r}+\alpha_{r-1}s^{r-1}+\dots+\alpha_{1}s+\alpha_{0}

fulfills
\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}|\alpha_{i}|<1
, then

\forall i^{\ast}>r:\;\|{}V_{(:,(1:r))}^{\dagger}V_{(:,\{i^{\ast}\})}{}\|_{\ell_{1}}<1.

Consequently, by
Theorem

1
, if
V=\mathcal{C}^{N}(A,b)
in
Eq.

BP
, then
u^{\ast}\in\mathds{R}^{N}
is not a solution of
Eq.

BP
if there exists an
i^{\ast}\in(r+1:N)
with
u^{\ast}_{i^{\ast}}\neq 0
.

Theorem

2
has the advantage that it can be verified efficiently and is independent of the number of columns in
\mathcal{C}^{N}(A,b)
as well as almost all
b
. In particular, it shows that if
(A,b)
is controllable then
Eq.

BP
succeeds if and only if
N=m
, i.e., when no optimization is required. This drastic failure may in practical applications be prevented by a better design of
A
. Note that in case of
\textnormal{rank}(V)<m
,
(V_{(:,(1:r))}^{\dagger}AV_{(:,(1:r))},V_{(:,(1:r))}^{\dagger}b)
simply corresponds to the dynamics after projecting onto the controllable subspace, i.e., the uncontrollable modes are irrelevant to our ability to find, e.g., a sparest control signal as discussed in
Section

4
. As the following simple example shows, one can cannot conclude that if the characteristic polynomial of
A
satisfies the coefficient constraint in
Theorem

2
that the same is true after the projection:

Example 3
.

Let
V=\mathcal{C}(A,b)
with

A=\begin{bmatrix}0&-0.7&0\\
1&-0.5&0\\
0&0&0.5\end{bmatrix}\;\quad b=\begin{bmatrix}1\\
1\\
0\end{bmatrix}

Then, the characteristic polynomial of
A

\det(sI-A)=s^{3}+0.45s-0.35

fulfills coefficient constraint in
Theorem

2
. However, as
\textnormal{rank}(V)=2
, we need to consider

\det(sI-V_{(:,(1:2))}^{\dagger}AV_{(:,(1:2))})=s^{2}+0.5s+0.7,

which violates this constraint.

Unfortunately,
Theorem

2
is inconclusive for many pairs
(A,b)
such as the one in
Example

1
. In order to mitigate this issue as well as the one observed in
Example

2
, we want to derive conditions for when
p
is unimodal. This property is desirable, since if
p_{i^{\ast}}<1
then
p_{i}<1
for all
i>i^{\ast}
, thereby reducing the verification of
Theorem

1
to a simple binary search problem. Since
p_{(1:r)}=\mathbf{1}_{r}
, it suffices to show unimodality of the vector
(\|{}V_{(:,1:m)}^{-1}V_{(:,\{i\})}{}\|_{\ell_{1}})_{i\in(r+1:n)}.

We start with the case of
V=\mathcal{C}(A,b)\in\text{SC}_{m}
, where in order to analyze it, we first need to establish the following two lemmas:

Lemma 4
.

Let
A\in\mathds{R}^{m\times m}
and
b\in\mathds{R}^{m}
be such that
(A,b)
is controllable and
V=\mathcal{C}(A,b)\in\text{SC}_{m}
. Then
\sigma(A)\subset\mathds{R}_{\geq 0}
.

Lemma 5
.

Let
X\in\mathds{R}^{n\times m}
,
n\geq m
, be such that
\textnormal{rank}(X)=m
. Then,
X\in\text{(S)SC}_{m}
if and only if
X^{\dagger}\in\text{(S)SC}_{m}
and the sign of the
m
-minors in
X
and
X^{\dagger}
coincide.

Based on these two lemmas, we can now arrive at our next main result:

Theorem 3
.

Let
A\in\mathds{R}^{m\times m}
,
b\in\mathds{R}^{m}
and
V=\mathcal{C}(A,b)
be such that
\textnormal{rank}(V)=r
and
V\in\text{SC}_{r}
. Further, let

W_{k}:=K_{r}^{\mathsf{T}}V_{(:,(1:r))}^{\dagger}V_{(:,\{r+k\})},\;k\geq 1.

Then, the sequences
\{g_{(i)}(k)\}_{k\geq 1}
,
i\in(1:r)
, and
\{p(k)\}_{k\geq 1}
defined by

g_{(i)}(k):=W_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}e_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad p(k):=\sum_{i=1}^{r}g_{(i)}(k)=W_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{1}_{r}=(\|{}V_{(:,(1:r))}^{\dagger}V_{(:,\{r+k\})}{}\|_{\ell_{1}})

are log-concave.

As log‐concavity implies unimodality,
Theorem

3
provides conditions under which
V=\mathcal{C}^{N}(A,b)
yields the desired unimodal
p
defined in
Eq.

6
. Note by
Lemma

4
, if
(A,b)
is controllable, then
\mathcal{C}(A,b)\in\text{SC}_{m}
requires that
\sigma(A)\subset\mathds{R}_{\geq 0}
. Otherwise, the result states that the uncontrollable modes are irrelevant, which is natural as these should not influence our ability to find, e.g., a sparsest control signal as discussed in
Section

4
. Moreover, since
p
is the impulse response of a system associated with
(A,b)
,
p
will then necessarily contain entries strictly smaller than
1
if
\sigma(A)\subset[0,1)
and
N
is sufficiently large.

Remark 3
.

In the special case
A=\textnormal{diag}(x)
with
0\leq x_{1}\leq\cdots\leq x_{m}
and
b=\mathbf{1}_{m}
,
V
becomes a totally positive Vandermonde matrix. Here the sequences
g_{(i)}
and

consist of symmetric polynomials in
x_{1},\dots,x_{m}
. For example,

g_{(1)}(k)=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}\leq i_{2}\leq\dots\leq i_{k}\leq m}x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}\cdots x_{i_{k}},

which for
k\in(1:m)
is the so-called
complete homogeneous symmetric polynomial of degree
k
in
m
variables

[
39
]
. Our results are, therefore, also of independent interest, as they establish the log-concavity of such polynomial sequences for any fixed nonnegative vector
x\in\mathds{R}^{m}
. While these findings complement related work
[
39
]
, to the best of our knowledge no prior work has presented these results.

Next, since impulse responses are invariant under similarity transformation, the same can be expected for
Theorem

3
.

Corollary 1
.

Let
A\in\mathds{R}^{m\times m}
and
b\in\mathds{R}^{m}
be as in
Theorem

3
. Further, let
T\in\mathds{R}^{m\times m}
be invertible,
{V}=T\mathcal{C}(A,b)
and define

\displaystyle g_{(i)}(k)

\displaystyle:=|e_{i}^{\mathsf{T}}V_{(:,(1:r))}^{\dagger}V_{(:,\{r+k\})}|,\;i\in(1:r),

\displaystyle p(k)

\displaystyle:=|\mathbf{1}_{r}^{\mathsf{T}}V_{(:,(1:r))}^{\dagger}V_{(:,\{r+k\})}|,\;k\geq 1.

Then,
\{g_{(i)}(k)\}_{k\geq 1}
for each

and
\{p(k)\}_{k\geq 1}
are log-concave sequences.

For example,
Corollary

1
applies to Hankel matrices

H_{m,n}(A,b,c)=\mathcal{O}^{m}(A,c)\mathcal{C}^{n}(A,b),\;n>m

with observable
(A,c)
or the related Page matrices
[
14
]
, which have recently gained attention in data‐driven control approaches (see, e.g.,
[
12
]
), where
\ell_{1}
-norm regularization is employed to enhance robustness.

3.3
General Tractability & Compressed Sensing

While
Theorems

3
and
1
give us conditions for retrieving our desired unimodality property, its structure is not very generic. For example, we would not be able to apply these results to the Hankel matrix cases

V=\mathcal{O}^{k}(A,c)\mathcal{C}^{n}(A,b),\;k<m

or when
V
is not related to an extended controllability matrix at all. Fortunately, as we only want
p
to be unimodal — not necessarily log-concave — we can generalize our results via the notion of variation bounding.

Theorem 4
.

Let
V\in\mathds{R}^{m\times n}
,
m<n
, be such that
V^{\mathsf{T}}\in\text{SC}_{m}
,
\Delta(V^{\mathsf{T}})\in\text{VB}_{m-1}
and
\det(V_{(:,1:m)})\neq 0
. Then,
p\in\mathds{R}^{n}
defined by
p_{k}:=\|{}V^{-1}_{(:,(1:m))}V_{(:,\{k\})}{}\|_{\ell_{1}}
,
k\in(1:n)
, is unimodal.

Remark 4
.

By
Lemma

2
, the requirement that
V^{\mathsf{T}}\in\text{SC}_{m}
can also be translated into
V^{\mathsf{T}}\in\text{VB}_{m-1}
. Similarly, if
V^{\mathsf{T}}
does not contain any column that is a multiple of
\mathbf{1}_{n}
, then
\textnormal{rank}(\Delta(V^{\mathsf{T}}))=m
and we can equivalently demand that
\Delta(V^{\mathsf{T}})\in\text{SC}_{m}
. In case that
\textnormal{rank}(V)=r
and
V=V_{(:,(1:r))}V_{(:,(1:r))}^{\dagger}V
, one can apply
Theorem

4
to the projection
V_{(:,(1:r))}^{\dagger}V
.

In the following, we present an illustrative example to demonstrate the benefit of
Theorem

4
.

Example 4
.

Let

,

Since

that also

. Moreover, it is possible to directly verify that

.

Let

\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
f_{n}(t)&f_{n}^{(1)}(t)&\dots&f_{n}^{(m-1)}(t)\end{bmatrix}.

for all

, if

the consecutive

.

with

Let

\displaystyle=1,\;\det(X_{\ast}^{\mathsf{T}}(t)_{((2:3),(1:2))})=t^{2},\;\det(X_{\ast}^{\mathsf{T}}(t)_{((2:3),(1:2))})=t^{4}\;

\displaystyle=2,\;\det(X_{\ast}^{\mathsf{T}}(t)_{((1:3),(1:3))})=2t^{3}

5
Conclusion

We have introduced a framework that provides tractable, deterministic failure guarantees for basis pursuit when solving sparse optimization problems under structural matrix constraints. Our analysis bridges necessary conditions from the dual approximation theorem with concepts from total positivity and control theory. In particular, we focus on matrix structures – including extended controllability, Hankel and Page matrices – that frequently occur in control problems, as well as basis function from geometric modeling. The presented results reveal that the success of basis pursuit critically depends on the location of non-zero entries in the sparsest solution, or equivalently, on the placement of poles in system matrices. Examples drawn from discrete-time optimal control problems further illustrate how these structural properties can influence performance in fuel-optimal control.

Overall, our work advances the theoretical understanding of sparse optimization and opens new avenues for practical design. We plan to extend our framework to other sparsity-inducing norms, such as the
\ell_{2}
-inducing sparsity norm considered in
[
23
,
24
]
, to quantify the gap between success and failure guarantees, and to broaden our results to other matrix structures. Finally, our findings on the log‐concavity of sequences of symmetric polynomials are also of independent algebraic interest.

Assessment of the feed additive consisting of Lactiplantibacillus plantarum NCIMB 30238 and Pediococcus pentosaceus NCIMB 30237 (1k21008) for all animal species for the renewal of its authorisation (Volac International Ltd)
Authors: EFSA Panel on Additives and Products or Substances used in Animal Feed (FEEDAP), Roberto Edoardo Villa, Giovanna Azimonti, Eleftherios Bonos, Henrik Christensen, Mojca Durjava, Birgit Dusemund, Ronette Gehring, Boet Glandorf, Maryline Kouba, Marta López‐Alonso, Francesca Marcon, Giovanna Martelli, Carlo Nebbia, Alena Pechová, Miguel Prieto‐Maradona, Katerina Theodoridou, Montserrat Anguita, Rosella Brozzi, Nicole Bozzi Cionci, Matteo L. ABSTRACT
AbstractFollowing a request from the European Commission, EFSA was asked to deliver a scientific opinion on the assessment of the application for renewal of authorisation of a preparation of Lactiplantibacillus plantarum NCIMB 30238 and Pediococcus pentosaceus NCIMB 30237 (1k21008) as a technological additive to improve ensiling of fresh material for all animal species. The applicant has provided evidence that the additive currently on the market complies with the existing conditions of authorisation. There is no new evidence that would lead the FEEDAP Panel to reconsider its previous conclusions. Thus, the Panel concludes that the additive remains safe for all animal species, consumers and the environment. Regarding the user safety, the additive should be considered as a skin sensitiser, but no conclusions can be drawn on the skin sensitisation. No conclusions can be drawn on the eye irritation potential of the additive. Exposure of users by dermal and respiratory routes is considered a risk.There is no need for assessing the efficacy of the additive in the context of renewal of the authorisation.

INTRODUCTION

Background and Terms of Reference

Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003 1 establishes the rules governing the Community authorisation of additives for use in animal nutrition. In particular, Article 14(1) of that Regulation lays down that an application for renewal shall be sent to the Commission at the latest 1 year before the expiry date of the authorisation.

The European Commission received a request from Volac International Ltd., represented in the EU by Volac Feeds Ltd. 2 for the renewal of the authorisation of the additive consisting of Lactiplantibacillus plantarum NCIMB 30238 and Pediococcus pentosaceus NCIMB 30237 (1k21008), when used as a feed additive for all animal species (category: technological additives; functional group: silage additives).

According to Article 7(1) of Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003, the Commission forwarded the application to the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) as an application under Article 14(1) (renewal of the authorisation). The dossier was received on 18 September 2024 and the general information and supporting documentation are available at https://open.efsa.europa.eu/questions/EFSA‐Q‐2024‐00580. The particulars and documents in support of the application were considered valid by EFSA as of 21 November 2024.

According to Article 8 of Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003, EFSA, after verifying the particulars and documents submitted by the applicant, shall undertake an assessment in order to determine whether the feed additive complies with the conditions laid down in Article 5. EFSA shall deliver an opinion on the safety for the target animals, consumer, user and the environment and on the efficacy of the feed additive consisting of L. plantarum NCIMB 30238 and P. pentosaceus NCIMB 30237 (1k21008), when used under the proposed conditions of use (see Section 3.1.3).

Additional information

The additive is a preparation containing L. plantarum NCIMB 30238 and P. pentosaceus NCIMB 30237. The additive is currently authorised for use in feed for all animal species (1k21008). 3

EFSA issued an opinion on the safety of L. plantarum NCIMB 30238 (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2012a), an opinion on the safety of P. pentosaceus NCIMB 30237 (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2012b) and an opinion on the efficacy of the additive under assessment (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2014) as silage additives for all animal species.

DATA AND METHODOLOGIES

Data

The present assessment is based on data submitted by the applicant in the form of a technical dossier 4 in support of the authorisation request for the use of L. plantarum NCIMB 30238 and P. pentosaceus NCIMB 30237 (1k21008) as a feed additive.

In accordance with Article 38 of the Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 5 and taking into account the protection of confidential information and of personal data in accordance with Articles 39 to 39e of the same Regulation, and of the Decision of EFSA's Executive Director laying down practical arrangements concerning transparency and confidentiality, 6 a non‐confidential version of the dossier has been published on Open.EFSA. 7

According to Article 32c(2) of Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 and to the Decision of EFSA's Executive Director laying down the practical arrangements on pre‐submission phase and public consultations, EFSA carried out a public consultation on the non‐confidential version of the technical dossier from 26 May 2025 to 16 June 2025 for which no comments were received.

The confidential version of the technical dossier was subject to a target consultation of the interested Member States from 25 November 2024 to 25 February 2025 for which the received comments were considered for the assessment.

The European Union Reference Laboratory (EURL) considered that the conclusions and recommendations reached in the previous assessment regarding the methods used for the control of the L. plantarum NCIMB 30238 and P. pentosaceus NCIMB 30237 (1k21008) in animal feed are valid and applicable for the current application. 8

Methodologies

The approach followed by the FEEDAP Panel to assess the safety and the efficacy of L. plantarum NCIMB 30238 and P. pentosaceus NCIMB 30237 (1k21008) is in line with the principles laid down in Regulation (EC) No 429/2008 9 and the relevant guidance documents: Guidance on the renewal of the authorisation of feed additives (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2021).

ASSESSMENT

The additive consisting of a preparation of L. plantarum NCIMB 30238 and P. pentosaceus NCIMB 30237 (1k21008) is currently authorised as a technological additive (functional group: silage additives) for use in fresh material for all animal species. This assessment regards the renewal of the authorisation of the above‐mentioned additive.

Characterisation

Characterisation of the additive

The additive is currently authorised as preparation of L. plantarum NCIMB 30238 containing a minimum of 2 × 1010 colony forming units (CFU)/g of additive and P. pentosaceus NCIMB 30237 containing a minimum of 2.6 × 1010 CFU/g additive. The Panel notes that this authorisation reflects the specification following the assessment of the individual strains; moreover, the additive, as a combination of the two strains, was not characterised before.

The applicant proposed a new specification for the additive with a minimum content of bacteria of 3.33 × 1010 CFU/g of additive preparation, with a ratio of 1:4 (6.67 × 109 CFU L. plantarum NCIMB 30238/g and of 2.67 × 1010 CFU P. pentosaceus NCIMB 30237/g). 10

The applicant declared that the manufacturing process has not been modified since the previous authorisation, except for a change in the cryoprotectants used (dextrose (28%–70%) and maltodextrin (30%–50%) replaced by ■■■■■ maltodextrin, ■■■■■ glycine and ■■■■■ sodium erythorbate, ■■■■■). 11 No antimicrobials are used during the manufacturing process. 12

All the proposed cryoprotectants are feed materials or authorised feed additives, except for sodium erythorbate, which is authorised in the EU as a food additive. The safety of sodium erythorbate is assessed in Appendix A.

The applicant provided two examples of preparations, one with sodium aluminosilicate (Preparation 1) and another one in which the former preparation is further standardised with sucrose, magnesium sulfate and sodium aluminosilicate (Preparation 2). 13

The data provided by the applicant on the batch‐to‐batch variation 14 of the preparations are reported in Table 1. Data on impurities 15 and physicochemical and technological properties 16 of the preparations are reported in Appendix B.

The data provided by the applicant showed compliance of Preparation 1 with the specifications set in the current authorisation and of both preparations with the specifications proposed by the applicant (Table 1). The applicant provided data regarding the quantification of each strain by cultural method in the additive preparations and also the identification of individual strains in five batches of Preparation 2 by Randomly amplified polymorphic DNA‐polymerase chain reaction (RAPD‐PCR). 17

The FEEDAP Panel considers that the microbial contamination and the amounts of the detected impurities in the test items do not raise safety concerns.

The applicant has also provided data for each individual active agent regarding batch‐to‐batch variation, 18 physicochemical properties 19 and impurities 20 (data not shown).

Characterisation of the active agents

P. pentosaceus NCIMB 30237 and L. plantarum NCIMB 30238 were originally isolated from untreated silages. 21 Both are deposited in the National Collection of Industrial, Food and Marine Bacteria (NCIMB) under the accession numbers NCIMB 30237 (P. pentosaceus) and NCIMB 30238 (L. plantarum). The applicant declared that the strains have not been genetically modified. 22

The taxonomical identification of the strains was confirmed by average nucleotide identity (ANI) based on the whole genome sequence (WGS) data. The results of this analysis showed ANI values of 99.59% 23 and 98.84% 24 for P. pentosaceus NCIMB 30237 and L. plantarum NCIMB 30238 with the respective type strains (DSM 20336T and DSM 20174T). The strains do not harbour plasmids. 25

The antimicrobial susceptibility of the strains was tested using a broth microdilution against the battery of antibiotics recommended by EFSA FEEDAP Panel (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2018). 26 All the minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) values were equal to or fell below the corresponding EFSA cut‐off values for L. plantarum NCIMB 30238 and P. pentosaceus NCIMB 30237, except for the MIC of P. pentosaceus NCIMB 30237 for tetracycline (MIC: 16 mg/L vs. cut‐off value: 8 mg/L). Exceedance of the cut‐off value by one dilution is considered to be within the normal range of variation and thus not a matter of concern. Therefore, the strains are considered to be susceptible to all the relevant antimicrobials.

The WGS data of the strains were queried for the presence of antimicrobial resistance (AMR) genes in the CARD and NCBI databases 27 using equal or below thresholds as indicated the EFSA statement (EFSA, 2024); the search did not identify any hit above the thresholds herein mentioned, and therefore, the strains do not harbour acquired AMR genes.

Safety

In its previous opinions (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2012a, 2012b), the Panel concluded that, following the qualified presumption of safety (QPS) approach to safety assessment, the use of these strains in the production of silage was considered safe for target species, consumers and the environment. Regarding user safety, the Panel concluded that the active agents may cause irritation to skin and eyes and should be considered as skin and respiratory sensitisers.

The applicant declared that no adverse effects, including accidents for target animals, consumers, users and/or the environment, have been reported since the approval of the additive. 29

In the context of the current application, the identity of the two strains as L. plantarum NCIMB 30238 and P. pentosaceus NCIMB 30237 was confirmed and evidence was provided that the strains do not show acquired antimicrobial determinants for antibiotics of human and veterinary importance, confirming the suitability of the QPS approach for safety assessment (EFSA BIOHAZ Panel, 2026). Consequently, the conclusions already reached are still valid and L. plantarum NCIMB 30238 and P. pentosaceus NCIMB 30237 are considered safe for the target species, consumers and the environment.

The applicant performed a literature search to provide evidence that the additive remains safe under the approved conditions for users. 30 The literature search was conducted on both strains using six databases (Google Scholar, PubMed, Scopus, Science Direct, ProQuest, World Cat Library). None of the hits reported any adverse effects or indicated any safety concern.

All the proposed cryoprotectants are feed materials or authorised feed additives, with the exception of sodium erythorbate. The safety of sodium erythorbate at the proposed concentration in the additive was assessed and is reported in Appendix A. The Panel concludes that sodium erythorbate does not raise concern for the safety for the target species, consumer and environment; therefore, the additive is safe for target species, consumer and environment.

The additive is a preparation containing microorganisms and therefore should be considered as a skin and respiratory sensitiser.

In the context of the current application, the eye irritation potential of the two preparations described in Section 3.1.1 was tested using an in vitro test performed according to OECD TG 437. 31 The results of both studies did not allow a conclusion concerning eye irritancy as some corneal opacity was observed (UN GHS ‘No stand‐alone prediction can be made’); thus, the FEEDAP Panel cannot conclude on the eye irritation potential of the additive preparations.

Conclusions on safety

The FEEDAP Panel concludes that the preparation containing L. plantarum NCIMB 30238 and P. pentosaceus NCIMB 30237 remains safe for the target species, consumers and the environment under the authorised conditions of use. Regarding user safety, the additive is considered a skin and respiratory sensitiser, but no conclusions can be drawn regarding its potential to be irritant to eyes. Exposure of users by dermal and respiratory routes is considered a risk. This conclusion would apply to any preparations containing the active agents.

Efficacy

The present application for renewal of the authorisation does not include a proposal for amending or supplementing the conditions of the original authorisation that would have an impact on the efficacy of the additive. Therefore, there is no need for assessing the efficacy of the additive in the context of the renewal of the authorisation.

CONCLUSIONS

The applicant has provided evidence that one preparation of the additive currently on the market complies with the existing conditions of authorisation. Furthermore, the applicant proposed new specifications for the additive (preparation of L. plantarum NCIMB 30238 and P. pentosaceus NCIMB 30237 containing a minimum of 3.33 × 1010 CFU/g additive, with a ratio of 1:4 (6.67 × 109 CFU of L. plantarum NCIMB 30238/g and 2.67 × 1010 CFU of P. pentosaceus NCIMB 30237/g) and provided data showing compliance of preparations in the market with these specifications.

The Panel concludes that the preparation containing L. plantarum NCIMB 30238 and P. pentosaceus NCIMB 30237 (1k21008) remains safe for the target species, consumers and the environment. Regarding user safety, the additive is considered a potential skin and respiratory sensitiser. No conclusions can be drawn on the eye irritation potential of the additive. Exposure of users by dermal and respiratory routes is considered a risk.

There is no need for assessing the efficacy of the additive in the context of the renewal of the authorisation.

RECOMMENDATION

The Panel recommends that the authorisation is updated reflecting the minimum content of L. plantarum NCIMB 30238 and P. pentosaceus NCIMB 30237 in the additive declared by the applicant (i.e. preparation of L. plantarum NCIMB 30238 and P. pentosaceus NCIMB 30237 containing a minimum of 3.33 × 1010 CFU/g additive, with a ratio of 1:4 (6.67 × 109 CFU of L. plantarum NCIMB 30238/g and 2.67 × 1010 CFU of P. pentosaceus NCIMB 30237/g).

ABSTRACT
The human brain operates at an estimated power consumption of merely 20 Watts while performing massively parallel biological computations, a thermodynamic efficiency that classical silicon-based computers and standard non-relativistic quantum mechanics (for example, Schrödinger-based localized molecular states subject to rapid thermal decoherence) fail to explain.This paper presents a foundational hypothesis: living systems sustain macro-scale quantum coherence and quantum entanglement (QE) in wet physiological environments (e.g., ~37°C aqueous cellular conditions) through non-Abelian Yang-Mills gauge fields and quantum electrodynamics (QED) phase dynamics. Traceable through a 60-year historical trajectory from Einstein’s skepticism to Clauser, Aspect, and Zeilinger’s experimental proofs (violating Bell's Inequality), QE demonstrates non-local behaviors where entangled entities maintain or even intensify their correlation regardless of spatial separation. Furthermore, while Abelian U(1) QED interactions are bounded by photon exchange at the speed of light in a vacuum (c), non-Abelian Yang-Mills gauge geometry enables instantaneous, simultaneous phase coordination across macroscopic biological networks (zero time-delay topological phase synchronization, Δ t = 0). We address the biological "Gödelian Limitation" - the epistemological impossibility of directly observing biological quantum coherence without inducing decoherence and death - and propose the existence of mathematically undecidable "Quantum Decoherence Diseases."

Introduction and the 20-watt paradox

Modern classical computing systems require megawatts of power to emulate complex neural network architectures. In contrast, the human central nervous system performs high-dimensional cognitive processing, motor control, and physiological orchestration under an energetic budget of approximately \begin{document}P \approx 20\,\mathrm{W}\end{document} [1]. Classical neurobiology attempts to explain this hyper-efficiency via chemical neurotransmission and ionic membrane action potentials governed by Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase pump dynamics [2]. However, signal propagation delays across synaptic clefts (1-5 ms) and chemical diffusion rates cannot account for the instantaneous, systemic synchronization observed across distant cortical structures.

Conventional quantum biology governed by the Schrödinger equation asserts that biological systems undergo near-instantaneous thermal decoherence within 10⁻¹³ seconds at physiological temperature (\begin{document}T \approx 310\,\mathrm{K}\end{document}). We argue that this paradox arises from an overly reductive physical model. By incorporating quantum electrodynamics (QED) and Yang-Mills field theories, we demonstrate how living systems achieve phase synchronization with zero signal delay across macroscopic distances.

The 60-year historical arc of quantum entanglement (QE) (1935-1997)

To establish QE as the fundamental physical engine of biological architecture - a working paradigm we designate as "QE = LIFE" - we must trace the 60-year historical trajectory through which QE evolved from a rejected theoretical anomaly into an empirically validated law of nature.

In 1935, Einstein, Podolsky, and Rosen introduced the EPR paradox, dismissing entanglement as "spooky action at a distance" (spukhafte Fernwirkung) and insisting quantum mechanics was incomplete without local hidden variables [3]. In the same year, Schrödinger coined the term Verschränkung (entanglement) in response to EPR, recognizing it not as a minor feature, but as the characteristic trait of quantum mechanics that enforces non-separability [4]. Decades later, in 1964, Bell formulated Bell’s Theorem and Bell’s Inequality [5]. Under local realism, the expectation correlation parameter S is mathematically bounded by: |S| = |E(a,b) - E(a,b') + E(a',b) + E(a',b')| ≤ 2 where a, a' and b, b' represent the different detector orientation settings (e.g., polarization angles) chosen by independent measurement stations, and E(a,b) denotes the statistical correlation coefficient between paired measurements at settings a and b. Quantum mechanics predicts that S can reach \begin{document}2\sqrt{2} \approx 2.828\end{document}. A violation where |S| > 2 provides a testable mathematical criterion to disprove local hidden variables, serving as definitive empirical proof of non-local QE.

In 1972, Freedman and Clauser conducted the world's first experimental test of Bell's Inequality using polarization-entangled photon pairs from atomic calcium cascades, observing measured correlation values that exceeded the classical upper mathematical limit |S| ≤ 2 , reaching |S| > 2-a (violating the CHSH inequality bound and approaching the quantum theoretical limit of 2*sqrt(2) approx 2.828) - a historical milestone known as the first empirical "violation" of Bell's Inequality - thereby providing the first direct experimental refutation of classical local hidden-variable theories [6]. Subsequently, in 1982, Aspect et al. performed time-varying optical switching experiments with entangled photon pairs, effectively closing locality loopholes and firmly proving the non-local reality of quantum states [7]. Finally, in 1997, Zeilinger et al. realized experimental quantum teleportation, demonstrating the practical transmission and manipulation of entangled quantum states over macroscopic distances - a milestone culminating in the 2022 Nobel Prize in Physics awarded to Clauser, Aspect, and Zeilinger [8].

Epistemological barrier: the Gödelian limitation of LIFE

A fundamental question arises: if macroscopic QE = LIFE powers living organisms, why has it not been directly quantified in vivo? We term this barrier the Gödelian Limitation of Biological Coherence. In mathematical logic, Gödel’s Incompleteness Theorem demonstrates that a consistent formal system cannot prove its own consistency from within [11]. In quantum biology, the act of "observation" requires injecting measurement energy (Δ E) into the non-Abelian gauge domain. This intervention disrupts the delicate topological coherence field, reducing the biological system to a classical, thermal equilibrium state that no longer supports dynamic life processes. Thus, biological quantum coherence possesses an intrinsic observational limit in a living state.

Key lemma and universal localization
Vladimir Hinich

Abstract
. We study localizations of ∞ \infty -categories which remain localizations after any base change.



1.
Key lemma

Recall the following Key lemma presented in
[
H.L
]
, 1.3.6.
The Key lemma served a convenient tool in proving the equivalence of
various descriptions of the
\infty
-category underlying a model category,
see
[
H.L
]
, 1.3.4, 1.3.5, 1.3.7.

1.1 Lemma
.

Let
f:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
be a functor between conventional categories
satisfying the following property (P).

(P)

For any
s:[n]\to\mathcal{D}
the category of sections
\mathcal{C}_{s}=\operatorname{Fun}_{\mathcal{D}}([n],\mathcal{C})
is weakly contractible.

Then
f
induces an equivalence

\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C},f^{-1}(\mathcal{D}^{\mathit{eq}}))\to\mathcal{D},

where
\mathcal{L}
denotes the (
\infty
-categorical) localization.

In this note we generalize this result in several directions.

•

We remove the assumption of
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
,
see
1.5
.

•

We weaken the assumptions so that (P) will be required to hold
only for simplices of dimension
\leq 1
, see
1.6
.

•

We provide a version of a converse statement,
see
2.2
.

1.2.
Localization

In this note we will use the word “category” to denote
\infty
-categories; the categories in the traditional sense are called the conventional categories. We denote by
\mathcal{S}
the category of spaces and
by
\mathtt{Cat}
the category of categories.
Recall that the full embedding
\mathcal{S}\to\mathtt{Cat}
admits a left adjoint

\mathcal{L}:\mathtt{Cat}\to\mathcal{S}

called the full localization. A functor
f:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
is called
a weak equivalence if
\mathcal{L}(f)
is an equivalence. A category
\mathcal{C}

is weakly contractible if
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C})
is contractible.

More generally, one defines the category of marked categories
\mathtt{Cat}^{+}

consisting of pairs
(\mathcal{C},W)
where
W\supset\mathcal{C}^{\mathit{eq}}
is a subcategory of

\mathcal{C}
(its arrows are called marked arrows). Then localization

\mathcal{L}:\mathtt{Cat}^{+}\to\mathtt{Cat}

is the functor left adjoint to the obvious functor

\mathcal{C}\mapsto(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C}^{\mathit{eq}})
.

We start with a very special case of the result.

1.3 Lemma
.

Let
f:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
be a cocartesian fibration. The fibers of
f

are weakly contractible if and only if
f
induces an equivalence

\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C},f^{-1}(\mathcal{D}^{\mathit{eq}}))\to\mathcal{D}.

Proof.

Let us say that an
arrow in
\mathcal{C}
is marked if
f
carries it to an equivalence in
\mathcal{D}
.
With this definition
f
becomes a marked cocartesian fibration in the sense of
[
H.L
]
, 2.1.1, so that Proposition 2.1.4 of
[
H.L
]
can be applied. This yields a left fibration

\mathcal{L}(f):\mathcal{L}(C,f^{-1}(\mathcal{D}^{\mathit{eq}}))\to\mathcal{D}.

It is an equivalence if and only if its fibers are contractible.
∎

1.4.
Rezk nerve

Let
(\mathcal{C},W)
be a marked category. The Rezk nerve
N^{R}(\mathcal{C},W)
,
see
[
MG
]
, 3.1, is the simplicial space defined by the formula

N^{R}(\mathcal{C},W)_{n}=\mathcal{L}(\operatorname{Fun}([n],\mathcal{C})^{W})

where
\operatorname{Fun}([n],\mathcal{C})^{W}
is the subcategory of
\operatorname{Fun}([n],\mathcal{C})
having the same objects and spanned by the arrows that are pointwise in
W
. By Theorem 3.8 of
[
MG
]

the complete Segal replacement of
N^{R}(\mathcal{C},W)
represents the
localization
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C},W)\in\mathtt{Cat}
.

Let now
f:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
be a functor. We denote by
W=f^{-1}(\mathcal{D}^{\mathit{eq}})
the subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
spanned by the arrows sent by
f
to equivalences.

The category
\operatorname{Fun}([n],\mathcal{C})^{W}
is the base change of

f_{n}:\operatorname{Fun}([n],\mathcal{C})\to\operatorname{Fun}([n],\mathcal{D})
with respect to the
embedding
\operatorname{Fun}([n],\mathcal{D})^{\mathit{eq}}\to\operatorname{Fun}([n],\mathcal{D})
.

Applying Lemma
1.3
to the functor

\operatorname{Fun}([n],\mathcal{C})^{W}\to\operatorname{Fun}([n],\mathcal{D})^{\mathit{eq}}
,
we deduce the following more general version of Key lemma.

1.5 Lemma
.

Let
f:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
be a functor
satisfying the above mentioned property (P) for any
s:[n]\to\mathcal{D}
.
Then
f
induces an equivalence

\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C},f^{-1}(\mathcal{D}^{\mathit{eq}}))\to\mathcal{D}.

∎

Our next step is to show that the property (P) is equivalent to
a weaker property

(P_{1})

For all
s:[n]\to\mathcal{D}
with
n\leq 1
the category

\mathcal{C}_{s}=\operatorname{Fun}_{\mathcal{D}}([n],\mathcal{C})
is weakly contractible.

1.6 Proposition
.

The properties (P) and
(P_{1})
for a functor
f:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}

are equivalent.

Proof.

Let
s:[n]\to\mathcal{D}
be given (up to equivalence) by a sequence

x_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s_{1}}}{{\to}}x_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s_{2}}}{{\to}}\ldots\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s_{n}}}{{\to}}x_{n}.

We denote
\mathcal{C}_{s}=\operatorname{Fun}_{\mathcal{D}}([n],\mathcal{C})
and similarly
\mathcal{C}_{x_{i}}
and

\mathcal{C}_{s_{i}}
. We have

\mathcal{C}_{s}=\mathcal{C}_{s_{1}}\times_{\mathcal{C}_{x_{1}}}\ldots\times_{\mathcal{C}_{x_{n-1}}}\mathcal{C}_{s_{n}}.

We will use this formula to deduce that, if
\mathcal{C}_{s}
are
weakly contractible for
n\leq 1
, then they are weakly contractible for all
n
.
Let us show that the map
\mathcal{C}_{s_{i}}\to\mathcal{C}_{x_{i}}
is a cocartesian fibration. Without loss of generality, we can replace
\mathcal{C}
with the
fiber product
[1]\times_{\mathcal{D}}\mathcal{C}
and reduce the claim to the case

\mathcal{D}=[1]
,
s_{i}=\mathrm{id}_{[1]}
and
x_{i}=1\in[1]
. Then
\mathcal{C}_{s_{i}}
is
\operatorname{Fun}_{[[1]}([1],\mathcal{C})
is the bifibration
over
\mathcal{C}_{0}\times\mathcal{C}_{1}
; in particular, it is a cocartesian fibration
over
\mathcal{C}_{1}
.

Denote
t:[n-1]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d_{0}}}{{\to}}[n]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s}}{{\to}}\mathcal{D}
. We apply
Lemma
1.3
to the cocartesian fibration

\mathcal{C}_{s}=\mathcal{C}_{s_{1}}\times_{\mathcal{C}_{x_{1}}}\mathcal{C}_{t}\to\mathcal{C}_{t}
whose fibers are weakly contractible by the assumption. Therefore, a localization of
\mathcal{C}_{s}
is
equivalent to
\mathcal{C}_{t}
. This gives, by induction, a weak contractibility of
\mathcal{C}_{s}
.
∎

2.
Universal localization

We note that the property (P) is closed under a base change. Therefore, functors satisfying the property (P) are
universal localizations
in the sense
of the following definition.

2.1 Definition
.

A functor
f:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
is called a universal
localization, if any base change of
f
is a localization.

It turns out that the converse also holds.

2.2 Theorem
.

Any universal localization
f:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}

satisfies the property (P).

Proof.

By
1.6
it is enough to prove the claim for
\mathcal{D}=[1]
. Assume that

p:\mathcal{C}\to[1]
is a
universal localization.

By the assumptions, we know that
\mathcal{C}_{0}=p^{-1}(0)
and

\mathcal{C}_{1}=p^{-1}(1)
are weakly contractible. In this case
the localization of
\mathcal{C}
is equivalent to

(1)

\mathcal{C}^{\prime}:=[0]\sqcup^{\mathcal{C}_{0}}\mathcal{C}\sqcup^{\mathcal{C}_{1}}[0],

so that
p
is a universal localization
if and only if

•

\mathcal{C}_{0}
is weakly contractible.

•

\mathcal{C}_{1}
is weakly contractible.

•

\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=[0]\sqcup^{\mathcal{C}_{0}}\mathcal{C}\sqcup^{\mathcal{C}_{1}}[0]
is weakly contractible.

We will now rewrite the above conditions in terms of
another presentation of the correspondence
\mathcal{C}
.

The correspondence
\mathcal{C}
gives rise to a functor
F:\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathcal{C}_{1}\to\mathcal{S}

that we can present by a cocartesian fibration over
\mathcal{C}_{1}
in two different ways. The first one is the composition

\mathcal{F}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\to}}\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathcal{C}_{1}\to\mathcal{C}_{1}

of the cocartesian fibration
\pi
classified by
F
and the obvious projection. The second cocartesian fibration is the composition

\mathcal{F}^{\mathit{tw}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi^{\mathit{tw}}}}{{\to}}\mathcal{C}_{0}\times\mathcal{C}_{1}

of the bifibration
\pi^{\mathit{tw}}
classified by the same
F
and the (also obvious) projection. In what follows we denote by
\mathtt{Left}(B)

(resp.,
\mathtt{Right}(B)
) the category of left (resp., right) fibrations over
B
.

The first cocartesian fibration is classified by
the composition

F^{\prime}:\mathcal{C}_{1}\to\operatorname{Fun}(\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathcal{S})=\mathtt{Left}(\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\mathrm{op}})\to\mathtt{Cat}

with the forgetful functor,
whereas the second fibration is clasified by the composition

F^{\prime\prime}:\mathcal{C}_{1}\to\operatorname{Fun}(\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathcal{S})=\mathtt{Right}(\mathcal{C}_{0})\to\mathtt{Cat}.

Obviously
F^{\prime\prime}=\mathrm{op}\circ F^{\prime}
.

The category
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
has two equivalence classes of objects
and a space of morphisms whose contractibility determines
whether
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is weakly contractible.

Lemmas
2.3
and
2.4
below assert that

\operatorname{Map}_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}(0,1)
is equivalent to the total localization of
\mathcal{F}
. On the other hand, the category of sections
\operatorname{Fun}_{[1]}([1],\mathcal{C})
is equivalent to
\mathcal{F}^{\mathit{tw}}
. Finally,
Lemma
2.5
asserts that the total localizations of
\mathcal{F}
and of
\mathcal{F}^{\mathit{tw}}

are equivalent, so, if
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{F})
is contractible,
\mathcal{F}^{\mathit{tw}}
is weakly contractible. This proves the theorem.
∎

2.3 Lemma
.

Given
p:\mathcal{C}\to[1]
, denote by
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
the localization of
\mathcal{C}
with respect to the vertical arrows, those whose image in
[1]
is
\mathrm{id}_{0}

or
\mathrm{id}_{1}
. Assume that the fibers
\mathcal{C}_{0}=p^{-1}(0)
and
\mathcal{C}_{1}=p^{-1}(1)
are weakly contractible. Then
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is equivalent to the category having two objects
0
and
1
with
\operatorname{Map}_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}(0,1)=\operatorname{colim}F
.

Proof.

We use the presentation of a category
\mathcal{C}
over
[1]
by the triple

(\mathcal{C}_{0},\mathcal{C}_{1},\phi:\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathcal{C}_{1}\to\mathcal{S})
. Given two such categories,
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
presented by the triple

(\mathcal{D}_{0},\mathcal{D}_{1},\psi:\mathcal{D}_{0}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathcal{D}_{1}\to\mathcal{S})
,
the space
\operatorname{Map}_{\mathtt{Cat}_{/[1]}}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})
is described as the space
of the triples

(\alpha,\beta,\theta)
where
\alpha:\mathcal{C}_{0}\to\mathcal{D}_{0}
,
\beta:\mathcal{C}_{1}\to\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\theta:\phi\to\psi\circ(\alpha^{\mathrm{op}}\times\beta)
is
a morphism of functors from
\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathcal{C}_{1}
to
\mathcal{S}
.

In the special case
\mathcal{D}_{0}=\{0\},\mathcal{D}_{1}=\{1\}
with
\operatorname{Map}_{\mathcal{D}}(0,1)=X
,
the space of maps

\operatorname{Map}_{\mathtt{Cat}_{/[1]}}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})

identifies with
\operatorname{Map}_{\mathcal{S}}(\operatorname{colim}F,X)
.

∎

2.4 Lemma
.

Let
F:B\to\mathtt{Cat}
be a functor classifying a cocartesian fibration

p:\mathcal{F}\to B
. Then
\operatorname{colim}(F)
can be calculated as the localization
of
\mathcal{F}
with respect to the collection of
p
-cocartesian arrows.

Proof.

The functor
\operatorname{colim}:\operatorname{Fun}(B,\mathtt{Cat})\to\mathtt{Cat}
is left adjoint to the
functor carrying
\mathcal{C}\in\mathtt{Cat}
to the constant functor
G(\mathcal{C}):B\to\mathtt{Cat}

with value
\mathcal{C}
. Let us reformulate this in terms of cocartesian fibrations. Given a cocartesian fibration
p:\mathcal{F}\to B
and a category
\mathcal{C}
, the space
\operatorname{Map}(\operatorname{colim}(F),\mathcal{C})=\operatorname{Map}(F,G(\mathcal{C}))
identifies with the space of functors
\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{C}
carrying cocartesian arrows to equivalences.
This implies the claim.
∎

2.5 Lemma
.

The total localizations of
\mathcal{F}
and of
\mathcal{F}^{\mathit{tw}}
are equivalent.

Proof.

We can use Proposition 2.1.4 of
[
H.L
]
to compare the vertical localization of
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\mathit{tw}}
with respect to their projection
to
\mathcal{D}
. In fact, they are both cocartesian fibrations classified
by the isomorphic functors
\mathcal{L}\circ F^{\prime\prime}=\mathcal{L}\circ\mathrm{op}\circ F^{\prime}=\mathcal{L}\circ F^{\prime}
,
where
\mathcal{L}
denotes the total localization functor. This implies the
claim of the lemma.
∎

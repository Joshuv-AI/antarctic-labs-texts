The impact of the Big Data Pilot Policy on urban green energy efficiency
Authors: Shixin Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPromoting urban green transformation has become a key pathway to achieving sustainable development. As an essential component of the modern economy, data factors demonstrate significant potential in enhancing the efficiency of energy systems and facilitating green transformation. Based on panel data from 273 prefecture-level cities in China over the period 2010–2023, this study employs a difference-in-differences (DID) model to assess the impact and underlying mechanisms of the Big Data Pilot Policy (TECH) on green energy efficiency (GEEF). The findings are as follows: First, China’s GEEF exhibits an overall upward trend and displays a spatial pattern of “higher in the east and lower in the west.” Second, TECH significantly improves GEEF, with green innovation (GREEN) and financial development (FINA) playing positive moderating roles. Third, the policy effect of TECH is significant in eastern regions but not in western regions. Finally, TECH exerts cross-regional spillover effects on GEEF. This study expands the theoretical perspective on urban green energy efficiency and provides empirical evidence and policy implications for the deep integration of digital development and green transition, as well as the realization of coordinated regional development.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-50435-z.Keywords: Data factors, Green energy efficiency, Big Data Pilot Policy, Sustainable developmentSubject terms: Environmental sciences, Environmental social sciences, Environmental studies

Introduction

In recent years, the issue of global environmental pollution has become increasingly severe, emerging as one of the central challenges constraining sustainable development1. According to the latest assessment report by the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC), the continuous rise in global average temperatures, the frequent occurrence of extreme weather events, and the degradation of ecosystems are threatening the economic and social stability of human societies. Confronted with the dual pressures of high energy consumption and substantial greenhouse gas emissions2—with energy-related carbon emissions accounting for more than 70% of the global total—the energy sector has become a critical breakthrough point in addressing climate change and reducing environmental pollution. Against this backdrop, urban green energy has increasingly become a focal point for both the international academic community and policymakers.

Green energy efficiency refers to the minimization of energy consumption and pollutant emissions and the maximization of energy utilization efficiency, under the premise of ensuring energy supply and economic development, through technological innovation, optimal resource allocation, and institutional arrangements. Enhancing green energy efficiency can effectively promote urban carbon emission reduction and environmental improvement3. However, improvements in green energy efficiency cannot be achieved solely through individual technological breakthroughs or spontaneous actions by enterprises. Given that energy utilization involves complex coordination among multiple sectors and actors, it is characterized by significant externalities and information asymmetries, and often entails a conflict between short-term economic benefits and long-term environmental gains. In the absence of systematic policy guidance and effective market incentives, relying solely on market-driven mechanisms or individual behavior is insufficient to overcome barriers such as high costs, low returns, and limited technological diffusion4. Therefore, identifying policy instruments that are both efficient and sustainable, and promoting green energy efficiency in a systematic manner, can effectively address environmental and energy crises.

As the world’s largest energy consumer and carbon emitter, China has made significant progress in its transition to green energy5. In 2023, China’s hydroelectric power generation reached approximately 1,140.9 billion kWh, wind power exceeded 809.0 billion kWh, solar power totaled 583.3 billion kWh, and biomass power generation amounted to 198.0 billion kWh, as shown in Fig. 1. The installed capacity of renewable energy accounted for over 50% of the country’s total power generation capacity, ranking first globally for several consecutive years. The construction of a green energy system is also accelerating6. However, China’s carbon dioxide emissions remain high. Challenges persist in the development of green energy, including regional imbalances7, low efficiency in resource allocation, and insufficient integration of data elements with the energy system. Effectively leveraging digital technologies and data elements to empower green energy development and improve urban green energy utilization efficiency has become a crucial driver for optimizing China’s energy structure and achieving sustainable development.

Existing research on green energy primarily focuses on the adoption of green energy technologies8, the exploration of green energy transition pathways9,10, and the assessment of green energy’s environmental and economic impacts11, these studies have yielded important outcomes in promoting technological advancement, informing policy formulation, and optimizing the macro-level energy structure. However, issues related to green energy efficiency at the urban level have received relatively limited attention. With the rapid development of the digital economy, data elements—as a new type of production factor—are profoundly reshaping resource allocation methods and industrial development paradigms12. Existing studies have examined the effects of the level of digital economy development and digital finance on green production efficiency or total factor productivity. Some scholars13 investigate the impact of the digital economy on the green transformation of the manufacturing sector from an industry-level perspective, while other studies14 focus on analyzing the relationship between internet development and green energy efficiency.

Digital transformation has become an important driver of green development, and the Big Data Pilot Policy can be seen as a key institutional arrangement in this process. Recent cross-country evidence for advanced economies shows that improvements in energy efficiency and green innovation, supported by new sharing-economy business models, significantly enhance environmental sustainability15. At the same time, studies on G20 economies indicate that broader digitization helps to improve environmental performance through channels such as energy efficiency, structural upgrading, and technological innovation16. However, existing literature lacks systematic empirical investigation into whether and how big data pilot policies contribute to improvements in GEEF. Against this background, our study investigates how the Big Data Pilot Policy influences urban green energy efficiency and whether similar digital channels operate at the city level.

Existing studies have largely employed the SBM model and the super-efficiency SBM model to measure energy efficiency. Compared with the standard SBM model, the core advantage of the super-efficiency SBM model lies in its ability to effectively distinguish and rank decision-making units located on the efficiency frontier, thereby providing more refined and informative efficiency comparisons. Accordingly, this study applies the super-efficiency SBM model to measure urban green energy efficiency, treats the National Big Data Comprehensive Pilot Zones as a quasi-natural experiment, and identifies the impact of institutionalized data factors on GEEF. This approach not only fills a theoretical gap in the literature regarding the role of institutionalized data factors in promoting GEEF, but also offers China’s experience as a valuable reference for other developing countries seeking to foster synergy between digitalization and sustainable development during the process of green transition.

The contributions of this study are reflected in the following key aspects. First, from the perspective of institutionalized data factors, this paper systematically analyzes the mechanisms through which TECH affects urban GEEF. The realization of GEEF is not a result of single-path dependence but rather a complex process involving the interplay of multiple factors. GREEN and FINA significantly enhance the positive effect of TECH on GEEF, this finding enriches the understanding of the mechanisms for improving energy efficiency and fills a research gap concerning the role of science and technology policies in enabling green energy efficiency. Second, this study identifies significant regional heterogeneity in the effect of TECH on GEEF across eastern, central, and western China. In addition, it examines the spatial spillover effects of policy implementation, revealing that TECH not only enhances local GEEF but also generates positive spillover effects on neighboring cities, thereby enriching the research landscape on the spatial dimensions of science and technology policy effects. Third, based on robust empirical findings, this study proposes targeted policy recommendations that provide practical guidance for China’s pursuit of a high-quality, sustainable energy transition. Moreover, as the policy effects primarily stem from improvements in governance capacity and resource allocation efficiency rather than reliance on frontier technological breakthroughs, the findings suggest that countries with relatively weak technological foundations may also achieve improvements in green energy efficiency through institutional data governance reforms. China’s pilot-based approach offers a scalable and low-risk experimental pathway for institutional innovation, and the internal mechanisms identified in this study provide valuable insights for some developing countries seeking to advance data-driven green energy transitions.

Literature review

Current research on green energy primarily centers on three dimensions: technological adoption, transition pathways, and environmental-economic impacts.

Research on the adoption of green energy technologies primarily unfolds from three perspectives: institutional incentives, mechanisms of technological diffusion, and decision-making by micro-level agents. In terms of institutional incentives, the policy environment is a critical factor influencing the adoption of green technologies17. Under institutional arrangements such as carbon emission constraints, energy subsidies, green credit, and tax incentives, the deployment of green energy technologies has shown a marked acceleration. The institutional design not only reduces the initial costs of carbon emission reduction and green technology adoption18,19), but also enhances market actors’ acceptance through the anticipatory effects of incentives20, thereby contributing to the improvement of green total factor productivity21,22. The diffusion of green energy technologies is not a linear process11; somewhat, it is constrained by regional disparities in innovation capacity and the structural characteristics of knowledge networks. Furthermore, Du et al.23 contends that enterprises or households consider multiple factors when adopting green technologies, including cost-benefit analyses, risk preferences, and social norms. An increase in environmental awareness strengthens the motivation to adopt green technologies24. Therefore, the adoption of green energy technologies results from the joint influence of institutional supply, the technological environment, and behavioral cognition.

Research on green energy transition pathways primarily focuses on three dimensions: energy structure optimization, industrial upgrading trajectories, and urban governance models. In terms of energy structure, studies concentrate on analyzing the feasibility and constraints of transitioning from traditional to green energy sources6, emphasizing the role of renewable energy in balancing energy security, emission reduction targets, and economic growth. From the perspective of industrial upgrading, the green energy transition is not merely a transformation at the energy end but also a critical component in the green evolution of the production system25. The restructuring of industrial chains, the development of green supply chains, and the cultivation of green innovation systems26,27 are regarded as key supporting forces for promoting energy transition and sustainable development28. In the context of urban governance, local governments play an important role in policy coordination and institutional provision for the green energy transition29. The transition pathway is a collaborative process characterized by “multiple objectives, multiple stages, and multi-actor participation,” requiring a dynamic balance among policy guidance30, technological innovation, and governance capacity. In addition, the existing literature has highlighted the crucial role of energy efficiency and green innovation in achieving sustainable growth. Cross-country analysis for G7 economies shows that policies and market conditions that stimulate energy efficiency improvements and green technological progress can substantially enhance environmental sustainability, particularly when combined with sharing-economy practices that optimize resource use15. Parallel evidence from G20 economies documents that the expansion of digitization contributes to environmental sustainability by facilitating more efficient allocation of resources, promoting cleaner production processes, and encouraging low-carbon innovation16. These findings suggest that digital and data-driven policies, such as the Big Data Pilot Policy, may exert a meaningful influence on green energy efficiency at more granular levels.

Research on the environmental and economic effects of green energy primarily involves systematic analysis across three dimensions: environmental emission reduction effects, economic growth mechanisms, and coordinated development pathways. On the environmental front, studies widely confirm the significant role of green energy in reducing carbon emissions, mitigating smog pollution, and improving environmental quality31,32, asserting that replacing traditional fossil fuels with green energy is a key pathway to achieving the “dual carbon” goals. From the economic perspective, green energy has a catalytic effect on investment and emerging industries33. However, the characteristics of green energy—such as high initial investment, long payback periods, and strong policy dependence—may pose challenges to the stability of the economic system34. Regarding coordinated development pathways, a balancing mechanism exists between green energy and the relationship between environmental and economic development35. Green energy is closely linked to industrial structure optimization, regional economic development, and green finance, making it a critical driver of regional sustainable development36.

Based on the core literature across three dimensions, a Sankey diagram is constructed to illustrate the associations between research themes and keywords. This mapping portrays existing studies from both the “thematic dimension” and “keyword clustering” perspectives, thereby revealing the focal points across different research streams. As shown in Fig. 2, existing research on green energy has yielded substantial findings in areas such as green technology adoption, the construction of transition pathways, and the evaluation of environmental and economic effects, thereby laying a solid theoretical and empirical foundation for understanding the mechanisms underlying the realization of GEEF. However, current literature has generally overlooked the role of data elements—massive data as a representative of emerging production factors—in the process of enhancing GEEF. As a core institutional driver of the digital economy, TECH plays a critical role in optimizing information allocation, enabling precise resource scheduling, and facilitating the transition to green production modes. Nevertheless, the enabling effect of TECH on GEEF enhancement remains underexplored and lacks systematic investigation and empirical validation. In response, this study constructs a quasi-natural experimental design. It employs panel data from Chinese prefecture-level cities spanning 2010 to 2023 to identify the impact and underlying mechanisms of TECH on GEEF, thereby expanding the theoretical boundaries of GEEF research.

Theoretical framework and research hypotheses

The impact of TECH on GEEF

The Big Data Pilot Policy refers to an institutional innovation initiative in which the government takes the lead in selected regions to promote reforms centered on data factor allocation, data governance, and data application. By advancing data infrastructure development, facilitating cross-departmental data integration and sharing, and enhancing data application capabilities, the policy aims to reduce information frictions and improve coordination efficiency in the operation of energy systems, thereby contributing to improvements in green energy efficiency.

TECH primarily enhances GEEF by optimizing resource allocation, strengthening technological innovation capacity, and improving government governance and regulatory effectiveness. First, the Big Data Pilot Policy is accompanied by the establishment of data platforms and the integration of governmental and industrial data37, which helps alleviate information asymmetry and departmental fragmentation within urban energy systems. By promoting data infrastructure construction and resource integration, TECH improves information symmetry and the precision of resource allocation in energy systems38, thereby facilitating the optimization of energy production, transmission, and consumption processes. Second, the pilot policy clarifies rules governing the collection, sharing, openness, and utilization of data resources39, increasing the participation of data in resource allocation. As a production factor, data accelerates the integration of technological resources and information sharing, speeds up iterative technological research and development, and promotes technological progress in green energy–related industries. Finally, TECH strengthens the institutional safeguards for green energy efficiency by enhancing government digital governance capacity and regulatory effectiveness. Through data-driven tools, governments can more accurately identify energy consumption structures, regulate high-energy-consuming behaviors, and optimize the allocation of policy instruments, thereby improving policy responsiveness and addressing the problem of “policy failure” in traditional energy governance. Accordingly, this study proposes hypothesis 1:

H1

TECH can promote the improvement of GEEF.

The impact of GREEN on the effect of TECH on GEEF

GREEN can enhance the policy effectiveness of TECH, and its moderating mechanism is mainly reflected in the following three pathways. First, GREEN enhances cities’ capacity to absorb and transform TECH, thereby strengthening the adaptability of policy implementation. The effectiveness of TECH depends on whether cities can effectively integrate data governance, data platforms, and industry applications. Cities with higher levels of green innovation typically possess more robust talent pools, research and development systems, and green industrial foundations40, enabling them to more rapidly convert the advantages of data factors into capabilities for optimizing green energy management and production processes. Second, GREEN expands the boundary of policy effects. Under high levels of green innovation, the synergistic effects between big data and green technologies are amplified. A stronger technological foundation leads to more pronounced institutional incentive effects, and the optimization of TECH is driven by technological progress. Finally, GREEN can effectively alleviate the issue of resource misallocation during policy implementation41. A high level of green innovation can provide complementary support for policies, effectively addressing deficiencies in information, talent, and institutional frameworks, thereby enhancing the robustness of policy implementation. Accordingly, this study proposes hypothesis 2:

H2

GREEN can strengthen the effect of TECH in promoting GEEF.

The impact of FINA on the effect of TECH on GEEF

The financial system can enhance the effectiveness of big data pilot policies by alleviating financing constraints and improving the efficiency of capital allocation. First, FINA contributes to strengthening green investment capacity42. A well-developed financial system can provide diversified and low-cost financing support for green energy projects, alleviating firms’ financial constraints in adopting big data and green technologies, thereby more fully unleashing the effects of policy incentives. Second, financial development improves the efficiency of resource allocation. Developed financial markets tend to channel funds toward policies with greater green development potential43. This efficient allocation mechanism helps strengthen the construction of TECH, thereby enhancing the overall effectiveness of the policy. Finally, financial development reduces the uncertainty and risk premium associated with policy implementation. A mature financial system provides more robust risk management tools and market signaling mechanisms, which help guide expectations and stabilize markets. This enhances the predictability and sustainability of TECH, bolstering market actors’ confidence in the green transition. Accordingly, this study proposes hypothesis 3:

H3

FINA can strengthen the effect of TECH in promoting GEEF.

The spatial spillover effect of TECH on GEEF

Big data pilot policies promote spillovers of data infrastructure, cross-regional data coordination, and the diffusion of policy experience at the regional level, thereby forming a spatial transmission mechanism with distinct policy attributes. First, TECH facilitates the development of digital infrastructure and the establishment of data platforms, enhancing the mobility of data resources. This allows the data application capacity of pilot regions to radiate outward, indirectly improving GEEF in neighboring areas. Second, technological innovation and applications driven by TECH can spread across regions through industrial linkages and technological cooperation44. According to the theory of knowledge spillover, technological knowledge is more easily disseminated and absorbed in geographically proximate areas. Therefore, technological accumulation in green energy within pilot regions can lead to efficiency gains in adjacent areas. Finally, experiences in policy design, implementation, and regulation from pilot regions are often studied and emulated by neighboring cities, resulting in institutional spillover effects that indirectly contribute to the enhancement of GEEF in non-pilot cities. Accordingly, this study proposes hypothesis 4:

H4

TECH exhibits a spatial spillover effect on GEEF.

TECH is core explanatory variable, defined as TECH = treat × time. Model construction

Super-efficiency SBM model

The super-efficiency SBM model, building upon the framework of the traditional Slack-Based Measure (SBM) model, introduces the concept of “super-efficiency” and incorporates slack variables for inputs and outputs, allowing for a more accurate reflection of the efficiency levels across decision-making units (DMUs)46. This study applies the super-efficiency SBM model to measure the GEEF of 273 prefecture-level cities in China from 2010 to 2023. This time span covers the critical period of China’s energy-economic development and the implementation of big data policies, ensuring both data completeness and the need for policy identification. Analysis of China’s GEEF

From an overall perspective, China’s GEEF exhibited an upward trend from 2010 to 2023 (Fig. 4), increasing from 0.311 in 2010 to 0.411 in 2023 (Table 4), representing a cumulative rise of 32.15%. This trend suggests that China has made significant progress in promoting the development of green energy, energy conservation, and emission reduction, as well as the application of green technologies. However, during the overall growth process, there were short-term fluctuations in several years. In 2012, GEEF declined from 0.332 to 0.318 in 2014, possibly due to the resurgence of heavy industry and the expansion of energy-intensive sectors under the pressure of economic slowdown during that period. Another decline occurred between 2021 and 2022, with GEEF falling from 0.399 to 0.395. This drop was mainly attributed to the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic, which temporarily disrupted energy consumption patterns, delayed the construction and commissioning of clean energy projects, and loosened environmental regulations in some regions due to emergency policy responses.

At the regional level, there are significant differences in GEEF across eastern, central, and western China, forming a pattern of “eastern leading, central catching up, and western lagging.” The GEEF in the eastern region increased from 0.358 in 2010 to 0.489 in 2023, consistently remaining above the national average and demonstrating a clear leading advantage. The eastern region benefits from a solid technological foundation and a well-structured industrial composition, characterized by a high proportion of the service sector and high-tech industries, which in turn results in higher energy efficiency. Moreover, the widespread adoption of new-generation information technologies, such as big data and artificial intelligence in eastern cities, has significantly enhanced the intelligence and precision of energy utilization, effectively driving green transformation.

The GEEF in the central region rose from 0.296 in 2010 to 0.403 in 2023, approaching the national average. The central region has made substantial progress in promoting resource-efficient and environmentally friendly development. On the one hand, government-led industrial transfer policies have driven industrial upgrading, with traditional energy-intensive industries gradually transitioning toward more environmentally friendly practices. On the other hand, the advancement of big data and urbanization has improved the energy consumption structure and resource allocation efficiency. However, the region still faces limitations due to relatively weak green innovation capacity and underdeveloped green finance, which constrain further improvement in GEEF.

The GEEF in the western region increased from 0.266 in 2010 to 0.316 in 2023. Although it shows an overall upward trend, it has consistently remained below the national average, with a significant gap compared to the eastern and central regions. This is primarily due to the region’s heavy industrial structure, a high proportion of energy-intensive industries, and limited adoption of green technologies. Additionally, constrained by factors such as geography, infrastructure, and the stage of economic development, western cities have been slower to adopt green energy governance and digital empowerment, and the effects of related policies have yet to be fully realized.

Parallel trend test

One of the key assumptions for identifying policy effects using the DID method is the “parallel trend assumption,” which requires that the treatment and control groups exhibit similar trends in the dependent variable prior to policy implementation. To verify whether this assumption holds, this study employs an event study approach to conduct a parallel trend test. This study designates the year immediately preceding policy implementation as the baseline period, as it is the closest to the policy rollout while remaining unaffected by the policy, thereby providing a clearer and more interpretable reference for identifying dynamic treatment effects. Regarding the event window specification, given the staggered implementation of the National Big Data Pilot Policy across cities, and in consideration of sample coverage, estimation stability, and the validity of parallel trend tests, this paper includes the two periods prior to policy implementation (t = − 2, − 1, 0) in the testing window and further examines the dynamic impacts in the first to third years following implementation (t = 1, 2, 3).

The evolution of the policy effect is illustrated in Fig. 5. Before the policy implementation, most of the policy effect coefficients are statistically insignificant and exhibit noticeable fluctuations. After the policy is enacted, the coefficients gradually increase and become significantly positive in the second period, indicating that TECH exerts a clear positive impact on GEEF. Therefore, the DID model used in this study demonstrates strong theoretical justification and empirical validity.

Robustness test

To enhance the credibility of empirical identification, this study employs a temporal placebo test to rule out “spurious policy effects.” Specifically, the implementation time of TECH is artificially advanced to construct fictitious policy implementation time points, and it is examined whether significant policy effects still appear. If significant effects are observed before the policy is implemented, it may indicate omitted variable bias or other confounding factors. Conversely, the absence of significant effects would support the validity of the original causal identification. This study conducts regressions with the policy implementation time advanced by 2 and 3 years, and the results are presented in Columns (1) and (2) of Table 6, respectively. In Column (1), the coefficient of the TECH variable is 0.021, which does not pass the 10% significance level test. In Column (2), the TECH coefficient is 0.017 and remains statistically insignificant. Therefore, TECH did not exert an effect on GEEF before the policy was implemented, confirming the robustness of the identification results in this study.

Robustness test.

Continuous variables may be affected by extreme values, potentially undermining the stability and interpretability of regression results. To enhance robustness, this study applies winsorization to continuous variables, ranging from 1% to 99%, thereby minimizing the influence of outliers on regression coefficients. The regression results are presented in Column (3) of Table 6. The results indicate that the coefficient of the core explanatory variable is 0.028 and statistically significant at the 5% level, further confirming the robustness of the study’s conclusions.

This study re-estimates the regression model by lagging the dependent variable by one period, which can effectively reduce potential endogeneity between the dependent variable and the error term. Given that policy effects may exhibit a time lag, this approach helps improve the accuracy of policy effect identification and the rationality of the temporal logic. According to the regression results in Column (4), the coefficient of the core explanatory variable TECH is 0.021. It is significant at the 1% level, supporting the reliability of the baseline regression results and indicating that the effect of TECH is both persistent and robust.

Since the COVID-19 pandemic has disrupted national economic activities since 2020, it may have affected the conclusions of this study. To eliminate the potential interference of the pandemic on GEEF, this paper excludes data from 2020 onward. It replaces the sample period for robustness testing to ensure that the pandemic does not influence the effect of TECH. Spatial spillover effect analysis

Economic linkages among Chinese cities are close, and the effects of policy implementation may exhibit cross-regional spatial transmission. GEEF demonstrates spatial dependence, Adjacent cities experience more frequent flows of population, industrial linkages, managerial exchanges, and cooperation in public service provision; as a result, data governance practices and digital regulatory approaches developed in pilot regions are more easily learned and emulated by surrounding cities. Using contiguity as the criterion for linkage, the adjacency matrix effectively captures this process of geographically based institutional diffusion and governance spillovers. Accordingly, this study selects the adjacency matrix as the baseline spatial weight matrix.

This study employs Lagrange Multiplier tests to examine spatial dependence in the sample data. The LM statistics for both the SAR and SEM models are significant at the 1% level, confirming the suitability of the Spatial Durbin Model as the baseline specification. The p-values of the Wald and LR tests are both below 0.1, further ensuring the robustness of the model specification and estimation results. Therefore, the Spatial Durbin Model (SDM) is adopted to decompose spatial effects, with the regression results reported in Table 10.

Discussion

This paper measures GEEF at the micro-city level for the first time, combines the DID model to identify the impact mechanism of TECH on GEEF, reveals the action path of TECH on GEEF, expands the spatial perspective and digital path of green energy efficiency research, and provides a theoretical and empirical basis for green policy formulation.

First, this study finds that the overall GEEF of Chinese cities exhibits an upward trend, characterized by a regional pattern of “high in the east and low in the west.” This conclusion aligns with existing research on general energy trends. Some scholars48 argues that China’s energy structure has undergone continuous optimization, with significant regional disparities; the eastern region has maintained a leading position due to its advantages in technological accumulation and policy resources. Existing studies49 points out that regional low-carbon levels are closely related to industrial structure, technological innovation capacity, and infrastructure, which provides empirical support for the findings of this study. However, current research on GEEF remains limited, notably lacking measurements at the micro-urban level. This study employs the super-efficiency SBM method to measure GEEF values for Chinese cities, assessing the effectiveness of energy utilization and the control of pollutant emissions, thereby offering a basis for policymakers to optimize resource allocation pathways. Furthermore, the study reveals regional disparities in GEEF, with the central and western regions lagging in green transformation. This indicates a structural imbalance in China’s regional advancement of the green transition and highlights the need for further improvement in coordinated regional development.

Second, TECH exerts a significantly positive effect on GEEF, indicating that the big data pilot policy effectively enhances urban green energy efficiency, thereby supporting Hypothesis H1. This finding is consistent with existing studies emphasizing the role of digital technologies in improving resource allocation and enhancing green performance. Existing literature50 indicates that digital technologies promote green transformation by optimizing energy allocation, highlighting a positive association between digital governance and green development. Distinct from the existing literature, this study treats the National Big Data Pilot Policy as a quasi-natural experiment and verifies the promoting effect of TECH on GEEF from a causal identification perspective. In doing so, it provides more compelling causal evidence on the relationship between science and technology policies and green energy efficiency, and addresses the core policy question of whether data factors can be translated into improvements in green efficiency.

Third, green innovation enhances technological absorption and application capabilities51, while green finance provides financial support for green projects and strengthens policy incentive effects. However, this study reveals that both GREEN and FINA exert significant positive moderating effects on the policy impact of TECH, suggesting that the green effects of technology policies do not arise from a linear outcome of a single policy shock but instead depend on the synergistic interaction between a city’s green innovation base and its financial support environment. These empirical results support Hypotheses H2 and H3. By stimulating green innovation activities and leveraging a sound financial support environment, the policy effects of TECH can be substantially amplified, thereby extending the transmission pathways of the “digitalization–greening” nexus.

Fourth, this study finds that the promoting effect of TECH on GEEF is significant in the eastern and central regions but not in the western region. The eastern region benefits from well-developed information infrastructure and abundant green technology reserves52, making it more conducive to digital integration, whereas the western region, with its weaker economic foundation and lagging development of green industries53, faces greater challenges in energy transition. This study empirically tests the constraints imposed by regional disparities on policy effectiveness, showing that the impact of TECH on GEEF varies across regions, possibly due to heterogeneity in resource endowments, technological absorption capacity, and institutional implementation. Eastern cities, characterized by high technological intensity, rapid industrial upgrading, and concentrated policy resources, exhibit significantly higher efficiency levels compared to the central and western regions. These findings provide a basis for regional policy formulation, suggesting that policymakers should adopt context-specific strategies and strengthen investment and development of big data infrastructure in the Western region.

Finally, TECH not only enhances local urban GEEF but also generates significant positive spillover effects on neighboring cities, thereby supporting Hypothesis H4. Some scholars54 point out that digital infrastructure possesses cross-regional externalities and can drive the development of neighboring areas through data sharing and collaborative platforms. Moreover, research55 finds that interregional technological cooperation and knowledge spillovers can effectively enhance green performance. This study identifies the spatial diffusion of technological policies on GEEF, thereby extending the research on the relationship between digitalization and GEEF from a spatial perspective. The spatial spillover effect of TECH highlights regional differences in technological absorption capacity, institutional coordination efficiency, and market openness. Data elements can enhance the GEEF of surrounding cities through mechanisms such as technology diffusion, demonstration effects, and resource flows. By focusing on the externalities of policy, this study uncovers the regional linkage mechanism of green energy, offering empirical evidence and strategic recommendations for promoting cross-regional green cooperation and optimizing regional policy design.

Conclusion and policy recommendations

Conclusion

First, the overall level of GEEF in China shows an upward trend, with the eastern region having the highest GEEF, the central region at a moderate level, and the western region at the lowest. Second, TECH significantly promotes GEEF, with GREEN and FINA playing notable moderating roles. Third, the promoting effect of TECH on GEEF is significant in the eastern and central regions but not in the western region. Finally, TECH exhibits significant spatial spillover effects on GEEF.

Policy recommendations

Building a Multidimensional Joint Enhancement Pathway for the Western Region. Given that the western region exhibits relatively low GEEF and the promoting effect of TECH is not significant, efforts should simultaneously focus on “addressing weaknesses” and “strengthening mechanisms.” It is recommended that the government increase investment in green energy infrastructure in the western region, accelerate the construction of renewable energy transmission and storage facilities, and lay a solid foundation for the development of green energy. In addition, regulatory and incentive mechanisms should be reinforced by incorporating indicators such as energy consumption per unit of output and the proportion of green investment into the performance assessments of local governments and state-owned enterprises. Complementary fiscal subsidies and preferential policies should be implemented to encourage enterprises and local governments in the western region to engage in green technology research, development, and application, thereby improving local energy efficiency. In terms of digital coordination, a cross-regional data and scheduling system should be established to strengthen cooperation with the eastern and central regions. This would promote the sharing of big data resources, the spillover of green technologies, and market interlinkages, achieving a synergistic improvement of GEEF across regions.

Strengthen support for green finance. The mechanism analysis confirms the significant moderating role of FINA. The government should encourage financial institutions to provide more funding support for green industries and technological projects. By establishing green development funds and green investment guidance funds, social capital can be attracted to invest in green energy projects. To ensure the effective operation of green finance, the government should enhance regulation of the green finance market to guarantee that funds are directed toward green development projects. In addition, big data technologies should be utilized to conduct risk assessments and project monitoring for green finance initiatives, thereby improving the efficiency of fund utilization and ensuring the sustained role of green finance in enhancing urban GEEF.

Leverage the leading role of the eastern and central regions. With a relatively strong foundation in green technologies, these regions demonstrate a significant promoting effect of TECH on GEEF. Therefore, further investment in big data infrastructure should be prioritized to facilitate the deep integration of green energy and digital technologies within the region. By establishing efficient big data platforms, real-time monitoring of energy usage, accurate demand forecasting, and dynamic optimization of energy dispatch can be achieved, contributing to overall improvements in energy utilization efficiency. At the same time, incentive mechanisms should be refined to encourage enterprises to actively adopt and apply green technologies, promoting a transition of the energy consumption structure toward low-carbon and high-efficiency models. This will create model examples for the nationwide transformation to green energy and further advance the high-quality and sustainable development of regional energy systems.

Promoting regional coordination and the diffusion of technology. The results of the spatial effect decomposition indicate that the policy effects of TECH extend beyond the local region to surrounding areas. To better leverage the spatial spillover effect, the government should facilitate cooperation and resource sharing in green energy technologies between the eastern and central regions, promoting cross-regional collaboration through shared platforms and data exchange. Moreover, efforts should be made to strengthen cross-regional training in green technologies and promote the mobility of skilled personnel, thereby accelerating the diffusion and application of advanced green technologies across regions and enhancing overall GEEF.

Limitation and future research

This study utilizes the “National Big Data Comprehensive Pilot Zones” as a quasi-natural experiment to systematically evaluate the impact of data elements on urban GEEF based on multi-period panel data. Due to limitations in data availability, the empirical analysis is conducted using panel data at the prefecture-level city scale in China. Future research could further incorporate firm-level data or carbon emission monitoring data and apply machine learning methods to expand the understanding of how big data enhances GEEF. In addition, although the spatial econometric model employed in this study captures spatial effects, it does not fully reveal the mechanisms of different spillover pathways. Future work could refine the identification of spatial spillover paths through dimensions such as inter-city cluster transmission mechanisms or green supply chain networks. Despite these limitations, this study’s exploration of the mechanism by which data elements drive GEEF provides theoretical support for understanding the integration pathway of “digitalization–greening” and offers important guidance for optimizing regional green transition policies and advancing the realization of the “dual carbon” goals.

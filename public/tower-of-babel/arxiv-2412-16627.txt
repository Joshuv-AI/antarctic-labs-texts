1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{D}
denote the unit disk on the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
,
\mathbb{T}
its boundary, and
H(\mathbb{D})
be the set of
all analytic functions in
\mathbb{D}
.
Let
\eta\in\mathbb{T}
and
\zeta>\frac{1}{2}
. The non-tangential region
\Gamma_{\zeta}(\eta)
is defined by

\Gamma(\eta)=\Gamma_{\zeta}(\eta)=\{z\in\mathbb{D}:|z-\eta|<\zeta(1-|z|^{2})\}.

Let
0<p,q<\infty
and
\alpha>-2
. The tent space
T_{p}^{q}(\alpha)
consists of all measurable functions
f
on
\mathbb{D}
with

\displaystyle\|f\|_{T_{p}^{q}(\alpha)}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\int_{\Gamma(\eta)}|f(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}|d\eta|\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}<\infty,

where
dA(z)=\frac{1}{\pi}dxdy
is the normalized Lebesgue area measure on
\mathbb{D}
.

For
p=\infty
and
0<q<\infty
, the tent space
T_{\infty}^{q}(\alpha)
consists of all measurable functions
f
on
\mathbb{D}
such that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{T_{\infty}^{q}(\alpha)}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\operatorname{esssup}_{z\in\Gamma(\eta)}|f(z)|\right)^{q}|d\eta|\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}<\infty.

It is observed that the definition of
T^{q}_{\infty}(\alpha)
is independent of
\alpha
. Thus, we denote it simply as
T^{q}_{\infty}
.

For
q=\infty
and
0<p<\infty
, the tent space
T_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
consists of all measurable functions
f
on
\mathbb{D}
for which

\|f\|_{T_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}=\operatorname{esssup}_{\eta\in\mathbb{T}}\left(\sup_{u\in\Gamma(\eta)}\frac{1}{1-|u|^{2}}\int_{S(u)}|f(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+1}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty,

where

S(re^{i\theta})=\left\{\lambda e^{it}:|t-\theta|\leq\frac{1-r}{2},1-\lambda\leq 1-r\right\}

for
re^{i\theta}\in\mathbb{D}\backslash\{0\}
and
S(0)=\mathbb{D}
.
Let
T_{p,0}^{\infty}(\alpha)
be the subspace of
T_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)

consisting of all measurable functions
f
with

\lim_{|u|\to 1}\frac{1}{1-|u|^{2}}\int\limits_{S(u)}|f(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+1}dA(z)=0.

Denote
T_{p}^{q}(\alpha)\cap H(\mathbb{D})
by
AT_{p}^{q}(\alpha)
. In particular, for
\alpha=0
, we write
AT_{p}^{q}
instead of
AT_{p}^{q}(\alpha)
.

Tent spaces were initially introduced by Coifman, Meyer, and Stein in
[
7
]
, serving as a pivotal tool for investigating topics within harmonic analysis. They developed an extensive framework that not only addressed the exploration of significant spaces such as Hardy spaces and Bergman spaces but also provided a unified approach to these inquiries. In the definition outlined above, the aperture
\zeta
of the non-tangential region
\Gamma_{\zeta}(\eta)
is implicitly understood rather than explicitly stated, as it is a well-established fact that varying apertures result in the same function space, each equipped with equivalent quasinorms.
The characterization of the Hardy space through the non-tangential maximal function reveals that
AT^{q}_{\infty}
is equivalent to
H^{q}
, which can be interpreted as the limit of
AT_{p}^{q}(\alpha)
as
p
tends towards infinity (refer to
[
24
]
for details). It is particularly noteworthy that
AT_{p}^{p}(\alpha-1)
corresponds to the weighted Bergman space, denoted as
A^{p}_{\alpha}
.

Let
\mathbb{N}
represent the collection of positive integers. Consider
g\in H(\mathbb{D})
,
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, and
n\in\mathbb{N}
with the condition
0\leq k<n
. The generalized integration operator
T_{g}^{n,k}
is defined as follows:

T_{g}^{n,k}f(z)=I^{n}\left(f^{(k)}(z)g^{(n-k)}(z)\right),\quad\text{for}\quad f\in H(\mathbb{D}).

In this context,
I^{n}
denotes the
n
-th iteration of the integration operator
I
, where
If(z)=\int_{0}^{z}f(t)\,dt
.
The operator
T_{g}^{n,k}
was initially presented by Chalmoukis
[
5
]
. Specifically, when
n=1
and
k=0
, the formula simplifies to:

T_{g}^{1,0}f(z)=\int_{0}^{z}f(\eta)g^{\prime}(\eta)\,d\eta=T_{g}f(z).

The Volterra integration operator
T_{g}
was first defined and analyzed by Pommerenke in
[
17
]
, where it was demonstrated that
T_{g}
is bounded on
H^{2}
if and only if
g
belongs to
BMOA
. Aleman and Siskakis extended this result in
[
1
]
, proving that
T_{g}
is bounded on
H^{p}
for
p\geq 1
under the same condition that
g
is an element of
BMOA
. Additionally, they established in
[
2
]
that
T_{g}
is bounded on
A^{p}
if and only if
g
is within the Bloch space
\mathcal{B}
. For a deeper investigation into the properties of the operator
T_{g}
, refer to
[
15
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
20
,
19
,
6
]
and the related literature.

Chalmoukis, in his study
[
5
]
, delineated the conditions under which the operator
T_{g}^{n,k}
is bounded on Hardy spaces. Specifically, he proved that
T_{g}^{n,0}:H^{p}\to H^{p}
is bounded if and only if
g
is an element of
BMOA
, and
T_{g}^{n,k}:H^{p}\to H^{p}
is bounded if and only if
g
belongs to the Bloch space
\mathcal{B}
for
k\geq 1
. Furthermore,
T_{g}^{n,k}:H^{p}\to H^{q}
is bounded if and only if the following supremum is finite:

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}+n-k}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|<\infty

for
0<p<q<\infty
.
In
[
9
]
, Du et al. characterized the boundedness, compactness, and Schatten class membership of the operator
T_{g}^{n,k}
on Bergman spaces with doubling weights. These findings highlight the distinct behavior of
T_{g}^{n,k}
in comparison to
T_{g}
.
In
[
21
]
and
[
22
]
, we explored the boundedness and compactness of the generalized integration operator
T_{g}^{n,k}
from
AT_{p}^{q}(\alpha)
to
AT_{t}^{s}(\beta)
for
0<p,q,s,t<\infty
,
\alpha,\beta>-2
, and from
AT_{\infty}^{p}(H^{p})
to
AT_{\infty}^{q}(H^{q})
for
0<q<p<\infty
, respectively.

In
[
18
]
, Qian and Zhu introduced and examined the operator
S_{g}^{n,k}
, defined as:

S_{g}^{n,k}f(z)=I^{n}\left(f^{(n-k)}(z)g^{(k)}(z)\right).

Specifically, for the case when
n=1
and
k=0
, we have:

S_{g}^{1,0}f(z)=\int_{0}^{z}f^{\prime}(\eta)g(\eta)\,d\eta=I_{g}f(z).

It is evident that
T_{g}^{n,k}=S_{g}^{n,n-k}
for
k\neq 0
. Consequently, the interesting case is
S_{g}^{n,0}
.

In this paper, we continue the work from
[
21
]
and
[
22
]
to study the boundedness and compactness of the operator
T_{g}^{n,k}:AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)\to AT_{q}^{\infty}(\beta)
and
T_{g}^{n,k}:AT_{p,0}^{\infty}(\alpha)\to AT_{q,0}^{\infty}(\beta)
for
0<p,q<\infty
.
Moreover, we study the boundedness and compactness of the generalized integration operators
S_{g}^{n,0}:AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)\to AT_{q}^{\infty}(\beta)
and
S_{g}^{n,0}:AT_{p,0}^{\infty}(\alpha)\to AT_{q,0}^{\infty}(\beta)
for
0<p,q<\infty
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we mainly provide some preliminaries. In Section 3, we describe the boundedness of the operator
T_{g}^{n,k}
and
S_{g}^{n,0}
. In the final section, we characterize the compactness of the operator
T_{g}^{n,k}
and
S_{g}^{n,0}
.

Throughout this paper, the notation
A\lesssim B
is used to denote that there exists a positive constant
C
such that
A\leq CB
. Note that the constant
C
may differ in various contexts. The notation
A\asymp B
signifies that both
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
hold true simultaneously.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we state some notations and lemmas that will be used in the proof of the main results in this paper.

A positive Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{D}
is said to be a Carleson measure (see
[
3
,
4
]
) if

\|\mu\|_{CM}=\sup\limits_{I\subseteq\mathbb{T}}\frac{\mu(S(I))}{|I|}<\infty.

Here
S(I)
denotes the Carleson box associated with
I
, i.e.,

S(I)=\left\{z\in\mathbb{D}:1-|I|\leq z<1,\frac{z}{|z|}\in I\right\}.

A positive Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{D}
is called a vanishing Carleson measure if

\lim_{|I|\to 0}\frac{\mu(S(I))}{|I|}=0.

Let
\rho(z,w)=\left|\frac{z-w}{1-\overline{w}z}\right|
and
\beta(z,w)
be the hyperbolic metric on
\mathbb{D}
, that is,

\beta(z,w)=\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{1+\rho(z,w)}{1-\rho(z,w)},\quad z,w\in\mathbb{D}.

Let
D(z,r)=\{w\in\mathbb{D}:\beta(z,w)<r\}
be the hyperbolic disk of radius
r>0
centered at
z\in\mathbb{D}
.
The sequence
Z=\{a_{j}\}
is a separated sequence if there is a constant
\tau>0
with
\beta(a_{k},a_{j})\geq\tau
for
k\neq j
.
For
r>\kappa>0
, the sequence
Z=\{a_{j}\}
is said to be an
(r,\kappa)
-lattice if

\mathbb{D}=\bigcup_{j}D(a_{j},r)
and
the sets
D(a_{j},\kappa)
are pairwise disjoint.
It is obvious that any
(r,\kappa)
-lattice is a separated sequence.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
Z=\{a_{j}\}
be an
(r,\kappa)
-lattice.
For
0<p<\infty
, the tent space of sequences
T^{\infty}_{p}(Z)
consists of all
\{x_{j}\}
for which

\|\{x_{j}\}\|_{T_{p}^{\infty}(Z)}=\operatorname{esssup}_{\eta\in\mathbb{T}}\left(\sup_{u\in\Gamma(\eta)}\frac{1}{1-|u|^{2}}\sum_{a_{j}\in S(u)}|x_{j}|^{p}(1-|a_{j}|^{2})\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.

Let
T_{p,0}^{\infty}(Z)
be the subset of
T_{p}^{\infty}(Z)
consisting of all sequences
\{x_{j}\}
such that

\lim\limits_{|u|\to 1}\frac{1}{1-|u|^{2}}\sum\limits_{a_{j}\in S(u)}|x_{j}|^{p}(1-|a_{j}|^{2})=0.

The aperture
\zeta
of the non-tangential region
\Gamma_{\zeta}(\eta)
is suppressed in the above definition since any two apertures generate the tent spaces of sequences with equivalent quasinorms.
It is easy to check that the sequence elements
\{x_{j}\}\in T_{p}^{\infty}(Z)
if and only if the measure
d\mu_{t}=\sum_{j}|x_{j}|^{p}(1-|a_{j}|^{2})\delta_{a_{j}}
is a Carleson measure, where
\delta_{a_{j}}
is the Dirac point mass at
a_{j}
.
Also,
\{x_{j}\}\in T_{p,0}^{\infty}(Z)
if and only if
d\mu_{t}
is a vanishing Carleson measure.

The subsequent lemma provides a discrete description of tent spaces
AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
, which establishes the connection between the discrete form of tent spaces and the analytic tent spaces.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
6
, Lemma 3.2]

Let
0<p<\infty
,
\alpha>-2
and
Z=\{a_{j}\}
be an
(r,\kappa)
-lattice. Then the following conditions hold.

(i)

A function
f\in AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
if and only if

\{f(a_{j})(1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}}\}\in T_{p}^{\infty}(Z).

Moreover,

\|f\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}\asymp\|\{f(a_{j})(1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}}\}\|_{T_{p}^{\infty}(Z)}.

(ii)

A function
f\in AT_{p,0}^{\infty}(\alpha)
if and only if

\{f(a_{j})(1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}}\}\in T_{p,0}^{\infty}(Z).

The following lemma is the atomic decomposition of tent spaces
AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
, which plays a key role in the proof of our main theorem.

Lemma 2.3
.

[
6
, Theorem 3.4]

Let
0<p<\infty
,
\alpha>-2
,
L>\max\{1,\frac{1}{p}\}
and
Z=\{a_{j}\}
be an
(r,\kappa)
-lattice.
For any sequence
\{x_{j}\}\in T_{p}^{\infty}(Z)
, the function

f(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}x_{j}\frac{(1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{L}}{(1-\overline{a_{j}}z)^{L+\frac{\alpha+2}{p}}}

belongs to
AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
, and
\|f\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}\lesssim\|\{x_{j}\}\|_{T_{p}^{\infty}(Z)}
.

The following lemma is the atomic decomposition corresponding to little space
AT_{p,0}^{\infty}(\alpha)
.

Lemma 2.4
.

[
6
, Theorem 3.6]

Let
0<p<\infty
,
\alpha>-2
and
L>\max\{1,\frac{1}{p}\}
. Then there exists an
(r,\kappa)
-lattice
Z=\{a_{j}\}
such that the space
AT_{p,0}^{\infty}(\alpha)
consists exactly of functions of the form

f(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}x_{j}\frac{(1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{L}}{(1-\overline{a_{j}}z)^{L+\frac{\alpha+2}{p}}}

where
\{x_{j}\}\in T_{p,0}^{\infty}(Z)
.

Given a sequence
y=\{y_{j}\}
, the pointwise multiplier
M_{y}
is defined by

M_{y}x=\{y_{j}x_{j}\}

for
x=\{x_{j}\}
.
The following lemma describes the multipliers of the tent spaces of sequences.

Lemma 2.5
.

[
6
, Lemma 2.2]

Let
0<q<p<\infty
and
Z=\{a_{j}\}
be a separated sequence and
\{x_{j}\}
. Then the following conditions hold.

(i)

M_{y}
maps

\{y_{j}\}\in T_{\frac{pq}{p-q}}^{\infty}(Z)

(ii)

(iii)

The following lemma is the Littlewood-Paley formula for tent spaces
AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
, which plays a crucial role and is used repeatedly in our proof process.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
0<p<\infty
,
\alpha>-2
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then the following conditions hold.

(i)

A function
f\in AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
if and only if for each (or some)
t>0
,

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}|f^{(n)}(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{np+\alpha+1}dA(z)<\infty.

(ii)

A function
f\in AT_{p,0}^{\infty}(\alpha)
if and only if
f\in AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
and for each (or some)
t>0
,

\lim_{|a|\to 1}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}|f^{(n)}(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{np+\alpha+1}dA(z)=0.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}^{p}\asymp\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}|f^{(j)}(0)|^{p}+\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}|f^{(n)}(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{np+\alpha+1}dA(z).

(1)

Proof.

It is easy to check that
f\in AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
if and only if the measure
d\mu_{f}(z)=|f(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+1}dA(z)
is a Carleson measure, and
f\in AT_{p,0}^{\infty}(\alpha)
if and only if
d\mu_{f}
is a vanishing Carleson measure.
In addition,
from
[
6
, Theorem 3.3]
, we obtain that
f\in AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
if and only if
f^{(n)}\in AT_{p}^{\infty}(np+\alpha)
, and
f\in AT_{p,0}^{\infty}(\alpha)
if and only if
f^{(n)}\in AT_{p,0}^{\infty}(np+\alpha)
.
Furthermore, by
[
24
, Theorem 45]
,
\mu
is a (vanishing) Carleson measure if and only if, for each (or some)
t>0
,

\sup\limits_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}d\mu(z)<\infty\left(\quad\lim\limits_{|a|\to 1^{-}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}d\mu(z)=0\right).

Then, we see that
(i)
and
(ii)
hold.
Moreover, using
[
6
, Theorem 3.3]
, we
have

\|f\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}\asymp\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}|f^{(j)}(0)|+\|f^{(n)}\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(np+\alpha)},

which implies
(\ref{norm})
.
∎

The following lemma provides a description of the growth properties of functions in tent spaces
AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
0<p<\infty
,
\alpha>-2
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
f\in AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
. Then

|f^{(n)}(z)|\lesssim\frac{\|f\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}}{(1-|z|^{2})^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}+n}}

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
.

Proof.

Using
[
23
, Proposition 4.13]
and Lemma
2.6
, for any
t>0
, we obtain

\displaystyle|f^{(n)}(z)|^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+2}}\int_{D(z,\frac{1}{2})}|f^{(n)}(w)|^{p}(1-|w|^{2})^{\alpha}dA(w)

\displaystyle\asymp\frac{1}{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+2+np}}\int_{D(z,\frac{1}{2})}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{z}w|^{t+1}}|f^{(n)}(w)|^{p}(1-|w|^{2})^{np+\alpha+1}dA(w)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\|f\|^{p}_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}}{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+2+np}},

which yields the desired result.
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

[
16
, Lemma 2.5]

Let
s>-1
,
r,t>0
and
r+t-s-2>0
.
If
r<s+2<t
, then

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{s}}{|1-\bar{a}z|^{r}|1-\bar{b}z|^{t}}dA(z)\lesssim\frac{1}{|1-\overline{a}b|^{r}(1-|b|^{2})^{t-s-2}}

3.
Boundedness

In this section, we describe the boundedness of the generalized integration operators
T_{g}^{n,k}:AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)\to AT_{q}^{\infty}(\beta)
and
T_{g}^{n,k}:AT_{p,0}^{\infty}(\alpha)\to AT_{q,0}^{\infty}(\beta)
for
0<p,q<\infty
.
Furthermore, we study the boundedness of the operators
S_{g}^{n,0}:AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)\to AT_{q}^{\infty}(\beta)
and
S_{g}^{n,0}:AT_{p,0}^{\infty}(\alpha)\to AT_{q,0}^{\infty}(\beta)
for
0<p,q<\infty
.
Now we state and prove the main results in this section.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
0<p\leq q<\infty
,
\alpha,\beta>-2
,
g\in H(\mathbb{D})
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
such that
0\leq k<n
.
Then
T_{g}^{n,k}:AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)\to AT_{q}^{\infty}(\beta)
is bounded if and only if

U_{g}=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|(1-|z|^{2})^{n-k+\frac{\beta+2}{q}-\frac{\alpha+2}{p}}<\infty.

Proof.

Sufficiency.
Assume that
U_{g}<\infty
.
For
f\in AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
and
t>0
, using Lemmas
2.6
and
2.7
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|T_{g}^{n,k}f\|_{AT_{q}^{\infty}(\beta)}^{q}\asymp

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}|(T_{g}^{n,k}f)^{(n)}(z)|^{q}(1-|z|^{2})^{nq+\beta+1}dA(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}|f^{(k)}(z)|^{q}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{q}(1-|z|^{2})^{nq+\beta+1}dA(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{q}(1-|z|^{2})^{\left(n-k+\frac{\beta+2}{q}-\frac{\alpha+2}{p}\right)q}

\displaystyle\cdot\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}|f^{(k)}(z)|^{q}(1-|z|^{2})^{kq-1+\frac{q(\alpha+2)}{p}}dA(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle U_{g}^{q}\|f\|^{q-p}_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}|f^{(k)}(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{kp+\alpha+1}dA(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle U_{g}^{q}\|f\|^{q}_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)},

which implies the desired result.

Necessity.

Suppose that
T_{g}^{n,k}:AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)\to AT_{q}^{\infty}(\beta)
is bounded. For
u\in\mathbb{D}
, set

f_{u}(z)=\frac{1}{(1-\overline{u}z)^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}}},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

For
t>\alpha+2
, using Lemma
2.8
we get

\displaystyle\|f_{u}\|^{p}_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}\asymp

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+1}}{|1-\overline{u}z|^{\alpha+2}}dA(w)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{\alpha+2}}{|1-\overline{a}u|^{\alpha+2}}\lesssim 1.

Hence, for
t>0
, using Lemma
2.6
and
[
23
, Proposition 4.13]
, we have

\displaystyle\infty>

\displaystyle\|T_{g}^{n,k}f_{u}\|_{AT_{q}^{\infty}(\beta)}^{q}\asymp\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}|(T_{g}^{n,k}f_{u})^{(n)}(z)|^{q}(1-|z|^{2})^{nq+\beta+1}dA(z)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|u|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{u}z|^{t+1}}|f_{u}^{(k)}(z)|^{q}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{q}(1-|z|^{2})^{nq+\beta+1}dA(z)

\displaystyle\int_{D(u,\frac{1}{2})}\frac{(1-|u|^{2})^{t}(1-|z|^{2})^{nq+\beta+1}}{|1-\overline{u}z|^{t+1+\frac{q(\alpha+2)}{p}+kq}}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{q}dA(z)

\displaystyle\int_{D(u,\frac{1}{2})}(1-|z|^{2})^{\beta+(n-k)q-\frac{q(\alpha+2)}{p}}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{q}dA(z)

\displaystyle\gtrsim

\displaystyle(1-|u|^{2})^{\beta+2+(n-k)q-\frac{q(\alpha+2)}{p}}|g^{(n-k)}(u)|^{q},

which implies the desired result.
The proof is complete.

Noting that

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
0<q<p<\infty
,
\alpha,\beta>-2
with
\frac{p\beta-q\alpha}{p-q}>-2
,
g\in H(\mathbb{D})
,

and
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
such that

.
Then
T_{g}^{n,k}:AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)\to AT_{q}^{\infty}(\beta)
is bounded if and only if

g^{(n-k)}\in AT^{\infty}_{\frac{pq}{p-q}}\left(\lambda\right),

where
\lambda=\frac{p\beta-q\alpha}{p-q}
.

Proof.

Sufficiency.

For
f\in AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)

and
t>0
, using Lemma
2.6
and Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\|T_{g}^{n,k}f\|_{AT_{q}^{\infty}(\beta)}^{q}\asymp

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}|(T_{g}^{n,k}f)^{(n)}(z)|^{q}(1-|z|^{2})^{nq+\beta+1}dA(z)

(2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}|f^{(k)}(z)|^{q}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{q}(1-|z|^{2})^{nq+\beta+1}dA(z)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}|f^{(k)}(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{kp+\alpha+1}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}

\displaystyle\cdot\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{\frac{pq}{p-q}}(1-|z|^{2})^{(n-k)\frac{pq}{p-q}+\frac{p\beta-q\alpha}{p-q}+1}\right)^{\frac{p-q}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|f\|^{q}_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}\|g^{(n-k)}\|^{q}_{AT^{\infty}_{\frac{pq}{p-q}}\left(\lambda\right)}.

Therefore,
T_{g}^{n,k}:AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)\to AT_{q}^{\infty}(\beta)
is bounded.

Necessity.

Let
Z=\{a_{j}\}
be an
(r,\kappa)
-lattice and
\{x_{j}\}\in T_{p}^{\infty}(Z)
. Let
r_{j}:[0,1]\to\{-1,1\}
be the Radermacher functions. For
L>\max\{1,\frac{1}{p}\}

\displaystyle F_{u}(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}x_{j}r_{j}(u)\frac{(1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{L}}{|1-\overline{a_{j}}z|^{L+\frac{\alpha+2}{p}}},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

(3)

Using Lemma
2.3
, we have
\|F_{u}\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}\lesssim\|\{x_{j}\}\|_{T_{p}^{\infty}(Z)}
.
For
t>0
, by the assumption and Lemma
2.6
, we get

\displaystyle\|T_{g}^{n,k}F_{u}\|_{AT_{q}^{\infty}(\beta)}^{q}\asymp

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}|(T_{g}^{n,k}F_{u})^{(n)}(z)|^{q}(1-|z|^{2})^{nq+\beta+1}dA(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}|F_{u}^{(k)}(z)|^{q}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{q}(1-|z|^{2})^{nq+\beta+1}dA(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|T_{g}^{n,k}\|^{q}\|F_{u}\|^{q}_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}\lesssim\|T_{g}^{n,k}\|^{q}\|\{x_{j}\}\|^{q}_{T_{p}^{\infty}(Z)}.

Integrating with respect to
u
on
[0,1]
and using Fubini’s theorem and Khinchine’s inequality (see
[
14
]
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|x_{j}|^{2}\frac{(1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{2L}}{|1-\overline{a_{j}}z|^{2L+2k+\frac{2(\alpha+2)}{p}}}\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{q}(1-|z|^{2})^{nq+\beta+1}dA(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|T_{g}^{n,k}\|^{q}\|\{x_{j}\}\|^{q}_{T_{p}^{\infty}(Z)}.

Since each point
z\in\mathbb{D}
belongs to at most
\mathbb{N}
of the sets
D(a_{j},\rho)
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|x_{j}|^{2}\frac{(1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{2L}}{|1-\overline{a_{j}}z|^{2L+2k+\frac{2(\alpha+2)}{p}}}\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}\geq

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|x_{j}|^{2}\frac{(1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{2L}\chi_{D(a_{j},\rho)}(z)}{|1-\overline{a_{j}}z|^{2L+2k+\frac{2(\alpha+2)}{p}}}\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|x_{j}|^{q}\frac{\chi_{D(a_{j},\rho)}(z)}{(1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{kq+\frac{q(\alpha+2)}{p}}}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a_{j}}a|^{t+1}}|x_{j}|^{q}(1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{nq-kq+\beta+1-\frac{q(\alpha+2)}{p}}\int_{D(a_{j},\rho)}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{q}dA(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|T_{g}^{n,k}\|^{q}\|\{x_{j}\}\|^{q}_{T_{p}^{\infty}(Z)}.

By
[
23
, Proposition 4.13]
, we get

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a_{j}}a|^{t+1}}|x_{j}|^{q}(1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{2+nq-kq+\beta+1-\frac{q(\alpha+2)}{p}}|g^{(n-k)}(a_{j})|^{q}

\displaystyle\|T_{g}^{n,k}\|^{q}\|\{x_{j}\}\|^{q}_{T_{p}^{\infty}(Z)},

which implies that

\displaystyle\{x_{j}g^{(n-k)}(a_{j})(1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{n-k+\frac{\beta+2}{q}-\frac{\alpha+2}{p}}\}\in T_{q}^{\infty}(Z)

and

\displaystyle\|\{x_{j}g^{(n-k)}(a_{j})(1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{n-k+\frac{\beta+2}{q}-\frac{\alpha+2}{p}}\}\|_{T_{q}^{\infty}(Z)}\lesssim\|T_{g}^{n,k}\|\|\{x_{j}\}\|_{T_{q}^{\infty}(Z)}.

Since

(i)

\displaystyle\{g^{(n-k)}(a_{j})(1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{n-k+\frac{\beta+2}{q}-\frac{\alpha+2}{p}}\}\in T_{\frac{pq}{p-q}}^{\infty}(Z)

\displaystyle\|\{g^{(n-k)}(a_{j})(1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{n-k+\frac{\beta+2}{q}-\frac{\alpha+2}{p}}\}\|_{T_{\frac{pq}{p-q}}^{\infty}(Z)}\lesssim\|T_{g}^{n,k}\|.

Combining this result with Lemmas

.
The proof is complete.
∎

Compactness

In this section, we establish the compactness of the generalized integration operators
T_{g}^{n,k}:AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)\to AT_{q}^{\infty}(\beta)
and
T_{g}^{n,k}:AT_{p,0}^{\infty}(\alpha)\to AT_{q,0}^{\infty}(\beta)
for
0<p,q<\infty
. Moreover, we study the compactness of the operators
S_{g}^{n,0}:AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)\to AT_{q}^{\infty}(\beta)
and
S_{g}^{n,0}:AT_{p,0}^{\infty}(\alpha)\to AT_{q,0}^{\infty}(\beta)
for
0<p,q<\infty
.
For this purpose, we require the following lemmas.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
0<p,q<\infty
,
\alpha,\beta>-2
,
g\in H(\mathbb{D})
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
such that
0\leq k<n
and
T_{g}^{n,k}:AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)\to AT_{q}^{\infty}(\beta)
is bounded. Then
T_{g}^{n,k}(H^{\infty})\subset AT_{q,0}^{\infty}(\beta)
.

Proof.

Assume that
f\in H^{\infty}
.
In the case
0<p\leq q<\infty
, according to Theorem
3.1
, the boundedness of
T_{g}^{n,k}:AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)\to AT_{q}^{\infty}(\beta)
implies that

U_{g}=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|(1-|z|^{2})^{n-k+\frac{\beta+2}{q}-\frac{\alpha+2}{p}}<\infty
.
If
n-k+\frac{\beta+2}{q}-\frac{\alpha+2}{p}<0
, then
T_{g}^{n,k}=0
, and hence, there is no need for further proof.
Now suppose that
n-k+\frac{\beta+2}{q}-\frac{\alpha+2}{p}\geq 0
. For sufficiently large
t>0
, using
[
23
, Lemma 3.10]
we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{|a|\to 1}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}|(T_{g}^{n,k}f)^{(n)}(z)|^{q}(1-|z|^{2})^{nq+\beta+1}dA(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{|a|\to 1}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}|f^{(k)}(z)|^{q}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{q}(1-|z|^{2})^{nq+\beta+1}dA(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}|f^{(k)}(z)|^{q}(1-|z|^{2})^{kq}\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{q}(1-|z|^{2})^{\left(n-k+\frac{\beta+2}{q}-\frac{\alpha+2}{p}\right)q}

\displaystyle\cdot\lim_{|a|\to 1}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}(1-|z|^{2})^{\frac{q(\alpha+2)}{p}-1}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}dA(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|f\|_{H^{\infty}}^{q}U_{g}^{q}\lim_{|a|\to 1}(1-|a|^{2})^{\frac{q(\alpha+2)}{p}}=0.

Therefore,
T_{g}^{n,k}f\in AT_{q,0}^{\infty}(\beta)
by Lemma
2.6
.

In the case
0<q<p<\infty
, for sufficiently large
t>0
, using Theorem
3.3
, Hölder’s inequality and
[
23
, Lemma 3.10]
, we get

\displaystyle\lim_{|a|\to 1}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}|(T_{g}^{n,k}f)^{(n)}(z)|^{q}(1-|z|^{2})^{nq+\beta+1}dA(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{|a|\to 1}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}|f^{(k)}(z)|^{q}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{q}(1-|z|^{2})^{nq+\beta+1}dA(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}|f^{(k)}(z)|^{q}(1-|z|^{2})^{kq}\lim_{|a|\to 1}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{q}(1-|z|^{2})^{nq-kq+\beta+1}dA(z)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f\|_{H^{\infty}}^{q}\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{\frac{pq}{p-q}}(1-|z|^{2})^{(n-k)\frac{pq}{p-q}+\frac{p\beta-q\alpha}{p-q}+1}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{p-q}{p}}

\displaystyle\cdot\lim_{|a|\to 1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+1}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f\|_{H^{\infty}}^{q}\|g^{(n-k)}\|^{q}_{AT^{\infty}_{\frac{pq}{p-q}}\left(\frac{p\beta-q\alpha}{p-q}\right)}\lim_{|a|\to 1}(1-|a|^{2})^{\frac{q(\alpha+2)}{p}}=0.

Hence,
T_{g}^{n,k}f\in AT_{q,0}^{\infty}(\beta)
by Lemma
2.6
.
The proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
0<p,q<\infty
,
\alpha,\beta>-2
with
\frac{p\beta-q\alpha}{p-q}>-2
,
g\in H(\mathbb{D})
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
such that
0\leq k<n
.
If
T_{g}^{n,k}:AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)\to AT_{q}^{\infty}(\beta)
is compact or
T_{g}^{n,k}:AT_{p,0}^{\infty}(\alpha)\to AT_{q,0}^{\infty}(\beta)
is compact, then
T_{g}^{n,k}(AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha))\subset AT_{q,0}^{\infty}(\beta)
.

Proof.

(i)
Assume that
f\in AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
. For
r\in(0,1)
, using Lemma
4.1
, we have
T_{g}^{n,k}f_{r}\in AT_{q,0}^{\infty}(\beta)
. Here
f_{r}
is defined by
f_{r}(z)=f(rz)
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
. By
[
6
, Corollary 3.8]
, we see that

\|f_{r}\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}\lesssim\|f\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}.

Given that
f_{r}
converges uniformly to
f
on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
as
r\to 1
, the compactness of the operator
T_{g}^{n,k}:AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)\to AT_{q}^{\infty}(\beta)
implies the existence of a sequence
\{r_{j}\}
approaching 1 such that
T_{g}^{n,k}f_{r_{j}}
converges to
T_{g}^{n,k}f
in
AT_{q}^{\infty}(\beta)
. Consequently,
T_{g}^{n,k}f\in AT_{q,0}^{\infty}(\beta)
.

(ii)
Assume that
f\in AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
and let
\{r_{j}\}\subset(0,1)
be a sequence converging to 1. Given that
f_{r_{j}}\in H^{\infty}\subset AT_{p,0}^{\infty}(\alpha)
and
\|f_{r_{j}}\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}\lesssim\|f\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}
, the compactness of the operator
T_{g}^{n,k}:AT_{p,0}^{\infty}(\alpha)\to AT_{q,0}^{\infty}(\beta)
ensures the existence of a subsequence
\{r_{j_{m}}\}
and an element
h\in AT_{q,0}^{\infty}(\beta)
such that
T_{g}^{n,k}f_{r_{j_{m}}}\to h
in
AT_{q}^{\infty}(\beta)
, which, by Lemma
2.7
, also implies uniform convergence on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
. Therefore,
f_{r_{j_{m}}}^{(k)}g^{(n-k)}\to h^{(n)}
uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
. On the other hand, since
f_{r_{j_{m}}}\to f
uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
, it follows that
f^{(k)}g^{(n-k)}=h^{(n)}
, and thus
(T_{g}^{n,k}f-h)^{(n)}=0
. Therefore,
T_{g}^{n,k}f(z)=h(z)+p(z)
, where
p(z)
is a polynomial of degree not higher than
n-1
and
p\in AT_{q,0}^{\infty}(\beta)
. Hence,
T_{g}^{n,k}f\in AT_{q,0}^{\infty}(\beta)
.
∎

Theorem 4.7
.

Let

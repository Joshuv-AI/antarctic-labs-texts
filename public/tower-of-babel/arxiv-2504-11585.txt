Laplacian quantum walks on blow-up graphs
Hermie Monterde, Hiranmoy Pal, Steve Kirkland

Abstract
This paper is a sequel to the work of Bhattacharjya et al. (J. Phys. A-Math. 57.33: 335303, https://doi.org/10.1088/1751-8121/ad6653 ) on quantum state transfer on blow-up graphs, where instead of the adjacency matrix, we take the Laplacian matrix as the time-independent Hamiltonian associated with a blow-up graph. We characterize strong cospectrality, periodicity, perfect state transfer (LPST) and pretty good state transfer (LPGST) on blow-up graphs. We present several constructions of blow-up graphs with LPST and produce new infinite families of regular graphs where each vertex is involved in LPST. We also determine LPST and LPGST in blow-ups of classes of trees. Finally, if n ≡ 0 n\equiv 0 (mod 4), then the blow-up of n n copies of a graph G G has no LPST, but we show that under certain conditions, the addition of an appropriate matching this blow-up graph results in LPST. Keywords: Perfect state transfer, Pretty good state transfer, Graph spectra, Blow-up graph, Twin vertices, Laplacian matrix. MSC: 05C50, 05C76, 15A16, 81P45.

Introduction

Accurate transfer of quantum states is a key task in the setting of quantum computing. For this reason, there is considerable interest in constructing networks of interacting qubits possessing desirable quantum state transfer properties. The idea of using a network of interacting qubits to transmit quantum information dates back to Bose
[
3
]
, who represented the network as an undirected graph wherein qubits correspond to vertices and entangled pairs correspond to edges. A time–independent Hamiltonian is specified (typically either the adjacency matrix or the Laplacian matrix, though other candidates for the Hamiltonian are available) and the transfer of quantum states is modelled by a quantum walk, which we now describe in further detail.

Throughout, we let
G
be a simple (loopless) connected unweighted undirected graph with vertex set
V
. A
(continuous-time) quantum walk
on
G
with Hamiltonian
L
is described by the unitary matrix

U(t)=\exp{\left(itL\right)}

where
t\in{\mathbbm{R}}
,
i=\sqrt{-1}
, and
L
is the Laplacian matrix of
G
. Since
U(t)
is unitary, for each
u\in V
we have
\sum_{v\in V}|U(t)_{u,v}|^{2}=1.
Consequently, the quantity
|U(t)_{u,v}|^{2}
is interpreted as the probability that state
u
is transmitted to state
v
at time
t.

We say that
G
has
Laplacian perfect state transfer
(LPST) between vertices
u
and
v
if there is a time
\tau>0
and some
\gamma\in{\mathbbm{C}}
such that

U\left(\tau\right)\mathbf{e}_{u}=\gamma\mathbf{e}_{v}.

(1)

In this case
|U(\tau)_{u,v}|^{2}=1
, so the quantum state initially at vertex
u
is transmitted to vertex
v
at time
\tau
with probability one. If we also assume that
u=v
in (
1
), then
u
is said to be
periodic
in
G
at time
\tau
. A graph is
periodic
if it is periodic at all vertices at the same time. The study of perfect state transfer was initiated by Bose
[
3
]
and Christandl et al.
[
4
]
, who used the adjacency matrix as the Hamiltonian. However, Godsil showed that perfect state transfer in simple unweighted graphs is rare relative to the adjacency matrix
[
9
]
. A more general fact was shown by Godsil, Kirkland and Monterde which implies that LPST in simple unweighted graphs is also rare
[
10
]
. The rarity of perfect state transfer led to the notion of pretty good state transfer
[
8
,
21
]
. A graph
G
exhibits Laplacian
pretty good state transfer
(LPGST) between vertices
u
and
v
if there is a sequence
\{\tau_{k}\}\subseteq{\mathbbm{R}}
and some
\gamma\in{\mathbbm{C}}
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}U\left(\tau_{k}\right)\mathbf{e}_{u}=\gamma\mathbf{e}_{v}.

(2)

That is,
|U(t)_{u,v}|^{2}
can be made arbitrarily close to 1 through appropriate choices of
t
. If we also assume that
u=v
in (
2
), then
G
is said to be
almost periodic
at
u
relative to the sequence
\{\tau_{k}\}
. We say that
G
is
almost periodic
if there is a sequence
\{\tau_{k}\}\subseteq{\mathbbm{R}}
and some
\gamma\in{\mathbbm{C}}
such that

\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}U\left(\tau_{k}\right)=\gamma I

(3)

where
I
is the identity matrix of appropriate order. The complex number
\gamma
in (
1
), (
2
) and (
3
) is called a
phase factor
and has modulus one because
U(t)
is unitary for all
t\in{\mathbbm{R}}
.

The study of graphs with useful state transfer properties includes the construction of new such families of graphs from old ones. For example,
a
blow-up
of
n
copies of a graph
G
, denoted
\overset{2}{\uplus}~G
, is the graph obtained by replacing every vertex of
G
by an independent set of size
n
, where the copies of two vertices in
G
are adjacent in the blow-up if and only if the two vertices are adjacent in
G
. Motivated by the work of Ge at al. on perfect state transfer in lexicographic products
[
7
]
, Bhattacharjya et al. investigated quantum walks on blow-up graphs relative to the adjacency matrix
[
2
]
. In this paper, we study quantum walks on blow-up graphs relative to the Laplacian matrix.

In Section
2
, we derive the transition matrix of the quantum walk on a blow-up graph relative to the Laplacian matrix. This allows us in Sections
3
,
4
and
5
to provide characterizations of periodicity (Theorem
1
), strong cospectrality (Theorem
2
), LPGST (Theorem
3
) and LPST (Theorem
4
) in a blow-up graph. In Sections
5
,
6
and
7
, we present constructions of blow-up graphs with LPST using Hadamard diagonalizable graphs, Cartesian products, directs products, and joins (see Theorems
5
,
6
and
7
). This leads to infinite families of regular blow-up graphs such that each vertex is involved in LPST but the underlying graphs do not admit LPST (see Remark
1
). In Section
8
, we characterize LPGST in blow-ups of paths and double stars. In particular, we show that blow-ups of paths do not admit LPGST (Theorem
8
), in contrast to the adjacency case where infinite families of blow-up graphs admit LPGST
[
2
, Theorem 8]
. In Section
10
, we show that if
n\equiv 0
(mod 4), then under mild conditions, the addition of an appropriate matching in
\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
results in LPST (see Theorem
10
). Note that since
n\equiv 0
(mod 4) in this case, we have
n\geq 3
, and so
\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
does not admit LPST. We discuss open problems in Section
11
.

There are similarities between the Laplacian and adjacency quantum walks on blow-up graphs, such as the absence of strong cospectrality in
\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
for all
n\geq 3
. However, there are also stark differences. Laplacian periodicity is preserved in a blow-up graph, but not adjacency periodicity. The characterizations of Laplacian and adjacency strong cospectrality in
\overset{2}{\uplus}~G
also do not coincide unless
G
is regular (see Theorem
2
(2) and
[
2
, Theorem 2(2)]
, respectively). Moreover, in the adjacency case, Theorem 4(3) (respectively, Theorem 3(3)) in
[
2
]
states that LPST (respectively, LPGST) between two copies of a vertex in
\overset{2}{\uplus}~G
is equivalent to periodicity (respectively, almost periodicity) of the vertex in
G
with the appropriate phase factor. However, for the Laplacian case, this is not enough; we need additional number-theoretic conditions on the degree of the vertex in
G
for LPST or LPGST to occur in
\overset{2}{\uplus}~G
(see Theorem
3
(3) and Theorem
4
(3), respectively). These observations reveal the fundamental differences in the behaviour of Laplacian and adjacency quantum walks on blow-up graphs.

2
Transition matrix

A
blow-up
of
n
copies of
G
, denoted
\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
, is the graph with vertex set
{\mathbbm{Z}}_{n}\times V
, where vertices
(l,u)
and
(m,v)
are adjacent in
\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
if and only if
u
and
v
are adjacent in
G
. Our goal in this section is to derive the spectral decomposition for the transition matrix of
\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
.

Let
J_{n}
be the
n\times n
all-ones matrix. Considering the lexicographic ordering on
{\mathbbm{Z}}_{n}\times V(G),
the adjacency matrix
A_{n}
of
\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
is given by
A_{n}=J_{n}\otimes A
, i.e. the Kronecker product of
J_{n}
and
A
. The Laplacian matrix of
\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
can be written as

L_{n}=nI_{n}\otimes D-J_{n}\otimes A,

where
D=\text{diag}\left(d_{1},\ldots,d_{m}\right)
is the degree matrix of
G
. Here
L=D-A
is the Laplacian matrix of
G
. Suppose

L=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\lambda_{r}E_{r}

is the spectral decomposition of
L
(here
\lambda_{j}
is an eigenvalue of
L
with corresponding eigenprojection matrix
E_{j},j=1,\ldots,r
). Note that

L_{n}\left(\frac{1}{n}J_{n}\otimes E_{j}\right)=\left(nI_{n}\otimes D-J_{n}\otimes A\right)\left(\frac{1}{n}J_{n}\otimes E_{j}\right)=n\lambda_{j}\left(\frac{1}{n}J_{n}\otimes E_{r}\right),

where
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,r\}
. Similarly

L_{n}\left(\left(I_{n}-\frac{1}{n}J_{n}\right)\otimes{\bf e}_{l}{\bf e}_{l}^{T}\right)=nd_{l}\left(\left(I_{n}-\frac{1}{n}J_{n}\right)\otimes{\bf e}_{l}{\bf e}_{l}^{T}\right),

where
l\in\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
, and
{\bf e}_{l}
is the
l
-th standard unit basis vector in
{\mathbbm{C}}^{m}.
We now write the spectral decomposition of the Laplacian matrix
L_{n}
of the blow-up
\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
as

L_{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{r}n\lambda_{j}\left(\frac{1}{n}J_{n}\otimes E_{j}\right)+\sum_{l=1}^{m}nd_{l}\left(I_{n}-\frac{1}{n}J_{n}\right)\otimes{\bf e}_{l}{\bf e}_{l}^{T}.

This yields the transition matrix of the blow-up relative to the Laplacian:

U_{n}(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\exp{\left(-in\lambda_{j}t\right)}\left(\frac{1}{n}J_{n}\otimes E_{j}\right)+\sum_{l=1}^{m}\exp{\left(-ind_{l}t\right)}\left(I_{n}-\frac{1}{n}J_{n}\right)\otimes{\bf e}_{l}{\bf e}_{l}^{T}.

Accordingly, for a vertex
u
in
G

\displaystyle{\bf e}^{T}_{(0,u)}U_{n}(t){\bf e}_{(1,u)}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\left[\exp{\left(-in\lambda_{j}t\right)}-\exp{\left(-ind_{u}t\right)}\right](E_{j})_{u,u}.

(4)

For more about the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of the Laplacian of a blow-up, see
[
6
]
.

We also make an observation about the vertices in
\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
. A pair of vertices
u
and
v
in a graph
G
are called
twins
if
N\left(u\right)\setminus\left\{v\right\}=N\left(v\right)\setminus\left\{u\right\}
. Twins that are not adjacent are called
false twins
. A
twin set
is a set of vertices in a graph whose elements are pairwise twins. For a vertex
u
in
G
,
T_{u}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left\{(j,u)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}j\in{\mathbbm{Z}}_{n}\right\}
is the set of all copies of vertex
u
in
\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
. Notice that
T_{u}
is a set of twins in
\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
. Thus, for all
n\geq 2
, each vertex in
\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
is contained in a twin set of size at least
n
. In fact, the following holds
[
2
, Proposition 1]
.

Proposition 1
.

Let
u
and
v
be two vertices in
G
. The set
T_{u}\cup T_{v}
is a twin set in
\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
if and only if
u
and
v
are false twins in
G.

3
Periodicity

Denote the set of distinct Laplacian eigenvalues of
G
by
\sigma(G)
. The
eigenvalue support
of vertex
v
in
G
is the set

\sigma_{v}(G)=\left\{\lambda\in\sigma\left(G\right)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E_{\lambda}{\bf e}_{v}\neq{\bf 0}\right\}.

The following observation determines the eigenvalue support of a vertex in a blow-up.

Proposition 2
.

Let
n\geq 2
. Then
\sigma\left(\overset{n}{\uplus}~G\right)=n\cdot\sigma(G)\cup\{n\cdot d_{u}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}u\in V(G)\}
. Moreover, for any vertex
u
of
G
and for any
j\in{\mathbbm{Z}}_{n}
, we have
\sigma_{(j,u)}\left(\overset{n}{\uplus}~G\right)=n\cdot\sigma_{u}(G)\cup\left\{n\cdot d_{u}\right\}.

We say that vertex
u
in
G
is
periodic
at time
\tau>0
if
|U(\tau)_{u,u}|=1
. The minimum such
\tau>0
is called the
minimum period
of
u
, denoted by
\rho_{u}
. Since
0
is an eigenvalue of
L
having the all-ones vector as an associated eigenvector,
0\in\sigma_{u}(G)
. As
L
is also positive semidefinite,
[
9
, Theorem 6.1]
yields the following result.

Theorem 1
.

Vertex
u
in
G
is periodic if and only if
\sigma_{u}(G)\subset{\mathbbm{Z}}
. In this case,
\rho_{v}=\frac{2\pi}{g}
, where
g=\operatorname{gcd}(\sigma_{u}(G)\backslash\{0\})
.

The following is immediate from Theorem
1
.

Corollary 1
.

\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
is periodic at vertex
(j,u)
for some
n\geq 1
if and only if
\sigma_{u}(G)\subset{\mathbbm{Z}}
. Moreover, if
\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
is periodic at vertex
(j,u)
for some
n\geq 1
, then
\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
is periodic at vertex
(j,u)
for all
n\geq 1
and
j\in{\mathbbm{Z}}_{n}
with
\rho_{(j,u)}=\frac{2\pi}{nh}
, where
h=\operatorname{gcd}(\sigma_{u}(G)\backslash\{0\}\cup\{d_{v}\})
.

In
[
2
, Example 3]
, adjacency periodicity of a vertex in a graph need not extend to a blow-graph. However, from Theorem
1
and Corollary
1
, it follows that blow-up graphs preserve Laplacian periodicity. This difference in behaviour between the adjacency and Laplacian dynamics has also been observed for join graphs
[
13
]
.

A graph is
periodic
if it is periodic at all vertices at the same time. The following result can be used to construct periodic graphs with small periods using the blow-up operation. Recall that a graph is
Laplacian integral
if all eigenvalues of the corresponding Laplacian matrix are integers.

Corollary 2
.

\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
is periodic for some
n\geq 1
if and only if
G
is Laplacian integral. Moreover, if
\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
is periodic for some
n\geq 1
, then
\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
is periodic for all
n\geq 1
with minimum period
\frac{2\pi}{nh}
, where
h=\operatorname{gcd}(\sigma(G)\backslash\{0\}\cup\{d_{u}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}u\in V(G)\})
.

4
Strong cospectrality

Two vertices
u
and
v
in a graph
G
are said to be
strongly cospectral
if for every
\lambda\in\sigma_{u}(G)
,

E_{\lambda}\textbf{e}_{u}=\pm E_{\lambda}\textbf{e}_{v}.

If
u
and
v
are strongly cospectral, then we may write
\sigma_{u}(G)=\sigma_{uv}^{+}(G)\cup\sigma_{uv}^{-}(G)
, where

\sigma_{uv}^{+}(G)=\{\lambda\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E_{\lambda}\textbf{e}_{u}=E_{\lambda}\textbf{e}_{v}\neq\textbf{0}\}\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma_{uv}^{-}(G)=\{\lambda\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E_{\lambda}\textbf{e}_{u}=-E_{\lambda}\textbf{e}_{v}\neq\textbf{0}\}.

Strong cospectrality is a necessary condition for LPGST
[
8
, Lemma 13.1]
. Thus, in order to characterize LPGST in blow-up graphs, we first need to characterize strong cospectrality.

Theorem 2
.

Let
G
be a graph.

1.

If
n\geq 3
, then
\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
does not exhibit strong cospectrality.

2.

Let
n=2
. If
u
is a vertex in
G
with degree
d_{u}
, then vertices
(0,u)
and
(1,u)
are strongly cospectral in
\overset{2}{\uplus}~G
if and only if
d_{u}\notin\sigma_{u}(G)
, in which case

\sigma_{(0,u),(1,u)}^{+}(G)=2\cdot\sigma_{u}(G)\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma_{(0,u),(1,u)}^{-}(G)=\{2d_{u}\}.

(5)

Moreover, vertex
(0,u)
can only be strongly cospectral with
(1,u)
in
\overset{2}{\uplus}~G
.

Proof.

We prove 1. For each
u\in V(G)
, let
T_{u}=\{(j,u)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}j\in{\mathbbm{Z}}_{n}\}
, which is a twin set in
\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
. If
n\geq 3
, then
|T_{u}|\geq 3
, and so
[
16
, Corollary 3.10]
yields the desired conclusion. To prove 2, let
n=2
. Note that if
\lambda_{j}\in\sigma_{u}(G)
, then the idempotent
\frac{1}{2}J_{2}\otimes E_{j}
associated with each
2\lambda_{j}\in\sigma_{u}(\overset{2}{\uplus}~G)
satisfies
(\frac{1}{2}J_{2}\otimes E_{j})(\textbf{e}_{0}\otimes\textbf{e}_{u})=(\frac{1}{2}J_{2}\otimes E_{j})(\textbf{e}_{1}\otimes\textbf{e}_{u})
. Now, if
d_{u}\notin\sigma_{u}(G)
, then the idempotent associated with
2d_{u}\in\sigma_{u}(\overset{2}{\uplus}~G)
is given by
\left(I_{2}-\frac{1}{2}J_{2}\right)\otimes{\bf e}_{u}{\bf e}_{u}^{T}
, and so we get
\left(\left(I_{2}-\frac{1}{2}J_{2}\right)\otimes{\bf e}_{u}{\bf e}_{u}^{T}\right)(\textbf{e}_{0}\otimes\textbf{e}_{u})=-\left(\left(I_{2}-\frac{1}{2}J_{2}\right)\otimes{\bf e}_{u}{\bf e}_{u}^{T}\right)(\textbf{e}_{1}\otimes\textbf{e}_{u})
. In other words,
(0,u)
and
(1,u)
are strongly cospectral in
\overset{2}{\uplus}~G
and (
5
) holds. On the other hand, if
\lambda_{j}=d_{u}\in\sigma_{u}(G)
for some
j
, then the idempotent associated with
2d_{u}\in\sigma_{u}(\overset{2}{\uplus}~G)
is given by
E_{2d_{u}}=\frac{1}{2}J_{2}\otimes E_{j}+\left(\left(I_{2}-\frac{1}{2}J_{2}\right)\otimes{\bf e}_{u}{\bf e}_{u}^{T}\right)
, and so
E_{2d_{u}}(\textbf{e}_{0}\otimes\textbf{e}_{u})\neq\pm E_{2d_{u}}(\textbf{e}_{1}\otimes\textbf{e}_{u})
. This proves the first statement of (2). The latter follows from
[
16
, Theorem 3.9(2)]
.
∎

If
n\geq 3
then the absolute value of the expression on the right of (
4
) is bounded above by
\frac{2}{3}.
Hence, there is no LPGST in the blow-up
\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
whenever

, a fact that coincides with Theorem

(1). Thus, we consider the case
n=2
for the discussion on LPGST.

We now apply Theorem
2
(2) to regular graphs. As usual, we denote the complete graph on
n
vertices, the
n
-path, the
n
-cycle, and the

-cube, by

and

Corollary 3
.

Let
G
be a
d
-regular graph with vertex
u
. Vertices
(0,u)
and
(1,u)
are strongly cospectral in
\overset{2}{\uplus}~G
if and only if
d\notin\sigma_{u}(G)
. In particular, if

is any vertex, then vertices
(0,u)
and
(1,u)
are strongly cospectral in: (i)

\overset{2}{\uplus}~K_{d+1}

, (ii)
\overset{2}{\uplus}~C_{n}
if and only if

\overset{2}{\uplus}~Q_{d}

The first statement follows from Theorem
2
(2). For
G\in\{K_{d+1},C_{n},Q_{d}\}
, we have
\sigma_{u}(G)=\sigma(G)
for any vertex

of

. Since
\sigma(K_{d+1})=\{0,d+1\}
,
\sigma(C_{m})=\{2(1-\cos(\frac{2j\pi}{n}))\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}0\leq j\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor\}
, and

, the second statement follows from the first and the fact that

5
Pretty good state transfer

We now characterize Laplacian pretty good state transfer (LPGST) in blow-up graphs. Since strong cospectrality is required for LPGST
[
8
, Lemma 13.1]
, by virtue of Theorem
2
(2), it suffices to consider vertices
(0,u)
and
(1,u)
in
\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
where
n=2
and
d_{u}\notin\sigma_{u}(G)
. Moreover, since
0
is an eigenvalue of the Laplacian matrix of a graph with the all-ones vector as an associated eigenvector, it follows that
0
is in the eigenvalue support of every vertex in a graph. From the spectral decomposition of

, it follows that the phase factor for Laplacian periodicity and LPGST is always

.

Theorem 3
.

Let
u
be a vertex in
G
with degree
d_{u}

\overset{2}{\uplus}~G

2.

There exists a sequence

such that

\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\exp{\left(-2i\lambda\tau_{k}\right)}=1\text{ for each }\lambda\in\sigma_{u}(G).

3.

G
is almost periodic at

relative to the sequence
\{\tau_{k}\}
with phase factor
\gamma=1
and

\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\exp{\left(-2id_{u}\tau_{k}\right)}=-1.

4.

If

are integers such that
\hskip-7.22743pt\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda_{j}\in\sigma_{u}(G)}\hskip-7.22743ptm_{j}(\lambda_{j}-d_{u})=0
, then
\hskip-7.22743pt\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda_{j}\in\sigma_{u}(G)}\hskip-7.22743ptm_{j}

Theorem 4
.

Let
u
be a vertex in
G
with degree
d_{u}
such that
d_{u}\notin\sigma_{u}(G)
. The following are equivalent.

1.

There exists
\tau>0

\overset{2}{\uplus}~G

\tau
between
(0,u)
and
(1,u)
with phase factor
\gamma=1.

2.

There exists
\tau>0
such that
-\exp{\left(-2id_{u}\tau\right)}=\exp{\left(-2i\lambda\tau\right)}=1

for each
\lambda\in\sigma_{u}(G)
.

3.

There exists
\tau>0
such that
G
is periodic at
u
at time
\tau
with phase factor
\gamma=1
and
\exp{\left(-2id_{u}\tau\right)}=-1.

\sigma_{u}(G)\subset{\mathbbm{Z}}

(4), we get no LPGST between
(0,u)
and
(1,u)
in
\overset{2}{\uplus}~P_{n}
.

Combining all cases above yields the conclusion.
∎

9
Double stars

A
double star
graph
S_{k,l}
is a tree resulting from attaching
k
and
l
pendent vertices to the vertices of
K_{2}
. Our goal is to characterize LPGST in blow-ups of double stars.

The characteristic polynomial of
S_{k,l}
can be obtained using
[
11
, Proposition 1]
as

\Phi(x)=x(x-1)^{k+l-2}p(x),

where
p(x)=x(x-k-1)(x-l-1)-(x-1)(x-k-1)-(x-1)(x-l-1)
.

We now characterize LPGST in blow-ups of double stars.

Theorem 9
.

The following hold.

1.

Let
v
be a vertex in
S_{k,l}
of degree
k+1
. There is LPGST in
\overset{2}{\uplus}~S_{k,l}
between
(0,v)
and
(1,v)
if and only if
p(x)
is irreducible over
{\mathbbm{Q}}
and
\nu_{2}(k+1)=\nu_{2}(l+3)
.

2.

Let
w
be a leaf in
S_{k,l}
attached to a vertex of degree
l+1
.
There is LPGST in
\overset{2}{\uplus}~S_{k,l}
between
(0,w)
and
(1,w)
if and only if
l=1
,
k
is odd, and either
p(x)
is irreducible over
{\mathbbm{Q}}
, or
p(x)
has a root that is an even integer.

Proof.

We prove (1). First, suppose
k=l
. In this case,
p(x)
has
x-(k+1)
as a linear factor. It is easy to check that
\overset{2}{\uplus}~S_{1,1}
, the blowup of
P_{4},
does not exhibit LPGST. For
k\geq 2
, we have
d_{u}=1
if
u
is a leaf, while
d_{u}=k+1
otherwise. Now, 1 and
k+1
are eigenvalues of
L(S_{k,k})
with eigenvectors
{\bf e}_{u}-{\bf e}_{v}
and
{\bf v}
, respectively, where
u
and
v
are leaves attached to the same vertex, and
{\bf v}{\bf e}_{w}^{T}=1
whenever
w
is a leaf and
{\bf v}{\bf e}_{w}^{T}=-k
otherwise. Thus, for all
k\geq 2,

d_{u}\in\sigma_{u}\left(S_{k,k}\right)
for every vertex
u
of
S_{k,k}
, and so there are no strongly cospectral pairs of vertices in
\overset{2}{\uplus}~S_{k,k}
by Theorem
2
(2). Next, consider the case
k\neq l
. Then
k+1
and
l+1
are not eigenvalues of
S_{k,l}
, and all eigenvalues except
1
belong to the support of the vertex
v
of degree
k+1
. Thus, vertices
(0,v)
and
(1,v)
are strongly cospectral in
\overset{2}{\uplus}~S_{k,l}
, and so we may apply Theorem
3
. Since the sum of eigenvalues of
L(X)
is equal to its trace, we obtain
(k+l-2)(1)+\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}=2(k+l+1)
. Equivalently,

\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}=k+l+4.

The assumption in Theorem
3
(4) holds if and only if

-m_{0}(k+1)+m_{1}(\lambda_{1}-(k+1))+m_{2}(\lambda_{2}-(k+1))+m_{3}(\lambda_{3}-(k+1))=0

for some integers
m_{0},m_{1},m_{2},m_{3}
. Combining the above two equations gives us

(m_{2}-m_{1})\lambda_{2}+(m_{3}-m_{1})\lambda_{3}=(m_{0}+m_{1}+m_{2}+m_{3})(k+1)-m_{1}(k+l+4)

(9)

We now proceed with two cases: when
p(x)
has exactly one integer eigenvalue and when

has no integer eigenvalue (equivalently, it is irreducible). Note that
p(x)
cannot have all integer eigenvalues, for this would imply that

is Laplacian integral, a contradiction to the fact that the stars are the only family of trees that are Laplacian integral.

Case 1
. Suppose
p(x)
irreducible. If
m_{2}-m_{1}\neq 0
but
m_{3}-m_{1}=0
, then equation (
9

\lambda_{2}
is an integer, a contradiction. Similarly for the case

and
m_{3}-m_{1}\neq 0

) implies that
\lambda_{2}=c\lambda_{3}+d
for some rational numbers

with

. Thus,
\lambda_{3}
is a root of
p(x)
and
p(cx+d)
, which are both monic cubic polynomials with integer coefficients. Since
\lambda_{3}
is algebraic over
{\mathbbm{Q}}
and its minimal polynomial over
{\mathbbm{Q}}
is unique, it follows that
p(x)=p(cx+d)
. That is,
c=1
and
d=0
. But this implies that
\lambda_{2}=\lambda_{3}
, a contradiction to the fact that
p(x)
is irreducible. The only case left is when
m_{2}-m_{1}=0
and
m_{3}-m_{1}=0
. This yields
m_{1}=m_{2}=m_{3}
, and so we may rewrite equation (
9
) as

m_{0}(k+1)/g=m_{1}(l-2k+1)/g,

(10)

where
g=\operatorname{gcd}(k+1,l-2k+1)=\operatorname{gcd}(k+1,l+3)
. As
m_{1}=m_{2}=m_{3}
, we get that
m_{0}+m_{1}+m_{2}+m_{3}
is even if and only if
m_{0}+m_{1}
is even. If
\nu_{2}(k+1)=\nu_{2}(l+3)
, then the

and
m_{1}
that satisfies equation (

is even. Thus, we get LPGST between
(0,v)
and

in
\overset{2}{\uplus}~S_{k,l}

(4). However, if
\nu_{2}(k+1)>\nu_{2}(l+3)
, then we may choose

and

, then
m_{0}
is odd while
m_{1}
is even, and so
m_{0}+m_{1}
is odd. In this case, we do not get LPGST between
(0,v)
and
(1,v)
in
\overset{2}{\uplus}~S_{k,l}
by Theorem
3
(4). We may argue similarly for the case
\nu_{2}(k+1)<\nu_{2}(l+3)
.

Case 2
. Suppose
p(x)
has exactly one integer eigenvalue, say
\lambda_{2}
. Then equation (
9
) implies that
m_{1}=m_{3}
(otherwise,
\lambda_{3}
is also an integer, a contradiction). Thus,
m_{0}+m_{1}+m_{2}+m_{3}

m_{0}+m_{2}
is even. Moreover, we may rewrite equation (

(m_{2}-m_{1})\lambda_{2}=(m_{0}+m_{1}+m_{2})(k+1)-m_{1}(l+3).

(11)

First, suppose

. Since

satisfy equation (

, we obtain

(m_{2}-m_{1})\lambda_{2}+(m_{3}-m_{1})\lambda_{3}=(m_{0}+m_{1}+m_{2}+m_{3})-m_{1}(k+5)

10
Edge perturbation

In Corollary
1
, we have observed that if
v
is a vertex in
G
with
\sigma_{v}(G)\subset{\mathbbm{Z}}
then
\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
is periodic at the vertex
(j,v)
for all
j
and
n
with period
\frac{2\pi}{n}.
It is clear that if
n\equiv 0
(mod 4) then
\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
is periodic at the vertex
(j,v)
for all
j
and
n
with period
\frac{\pi}{2}.
In this case, we have
n\geq 3
, and so
\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
does not exhibit strong cospectrality by Theorem
2
(1). Consequently,
\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
cannot exhibit LPST. However, using the fact that
T_{v}=\left\{(j,v)\mid j\in{\mathbbm{Z}}_{n}\right\}
consists of pairwise false twins in
\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
, the insertion of an additional edge in
\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
between vertices
(l,v),(m,v)\in T_{v}
with
l\neq m
results in LPST between
(l,v)
and
(m,v)
at
\frac{\pi}{2}

[
19
, Theorem 4]
. Moreover, the resulting graph is periodic at the remaining vertices in the set
T=\left\{(j,v)\mid j\neq l,m\right\}
with period
\frac{\pi}{2}.
Invoking
[
19
, Theorem 3]
, another edge can again be added between a pair of vertices in
T
to have LPST
\frac{\pi}{2}.
Applying this process inductively, we may generate new graphs exhibiting LPST by simply adding a
matching
in
T_{v}
, which is a set of edges without common vertices. We summarize this as follows.

Theorem 10
.

Let
n\equiv 0
(mod 4) and
v
be a vertex in
G
with
\sigma_{v}(G)\subset{\mathbbm{Z}}.
Then the addition of a matching in
T_{v}
results in LPST in
\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
at
\frac{\pi}{2}
between the end vertices of every edge inserted. Moreover, the resulting graph is periodic at all vertices in
T_{v}
that are not incident to the newly added edges with period
\frac{\pi}{2}.

According to
[
19
, Theorem 3]
, the addition of a matching in
\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
as described above works as long as the vertices in the matching belong to a set of twins. Thus, if
v
and
u
are false twins, then
T_{v}\cup T_{u}
is a twin set by Proposition
1
, and so the conclusion in Theorem
10
still works if we add a matching in
T_{v}\cup T_{u}
. Thus, the following is immediate.

Corollary 13
.

Let
n\equiv 0
(mod 4). If
u
and
v
are false twins in
G
with
\sigma_{v}(G)\subset{\mathbbm{Z}}
, then the addition of a matching in
T_{v}\cup T_{u}
results in LPST in
\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
at
\frac{\pi}{2}
between the end vertices of every edge inserted. Moreover, the resulting graph is periodic at all vertices in
T_{v}
that are not incident to the newly added edges with period
\frac{\pi}{2}.

In general, we may be begin with any Laplacian integral graph
G
, and apply
[
19
, Theorem 3]
and
[
19
, Theorem 4]
successively to
\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
where
n\equiv 0
(mod 4) to construct new graphs having LPST.

Combining Theorem
10
and Corollary
13
yields the following result.

Corollary 14
.

Let
n\equiv 0
(mod 4) and
v
be a vertex in
G
with

If
v

\overset{n}{\uplus}~G

\frac{\pi}{2}

v
has a false twin

\frac{\pi}{2}

11
Future work

We investigated Laplacian quantum walks on blow-up graphs. We characterized periodicity, strong cospectrality, LPST and LPGST in blow-up graphs. Several constructions of blow-up graphs with LPST were also presented. This produced infinite families of regular blow-up graphs each vertex of which is involved in LPST, though the underlying graphs do not admit LPST. To inspire further work in this topic, we pose the following questions.

A graph
G
is
weakly Hadamard diagonalizable
(WHD) if its Laplacian matrix is diagonalizable by a weak Hadamard matrix, i.e. a matrix
H
with entries from the set
\{0,\pm 1\}
such that
H^{T}H
is tridiagonal. LPST was studied in WHD graphs
[
15
]
.
In line with our result in Corollary
5
, we ask: when does a blow-up of a WHD graph admit LPST?

Lastly, we ask: does Theorem
10
hold for the adjacency case? That is, if
n\equiv 0
(mod 4), will the addition of an appropriate matching in
\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
induce adjacency LPST?

ITGBL1 and RPN1 Mark a Fibrotic NP Subpopulation with Coupled Integrin Signaling and N-Glycosylation Programs in IVDD
Authors: Shuo Qiang, Yunke Liu, Yonghui Dong, Cheng Cheng, Yifan Huang, Jia Zheng, Chao Tang, Siyu Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundFibroNPC is a degeneration associated subtype in intervertebral disc degeneration (IVDD), yet its markers and immune crosstalk remain poorly characterized. ITGBL1 and RPN1 recur as IVDD associated genes in bulk studies, but where they are expressed within the heterogeneous disc has not been resolved.MethodsThree GEO datasets comprising 81 bulk profiles were integrated and screened by LASSO, SVM-RFE, and Random Forest, with external validation in an independent 15 sample cohort. Two human IVDD scRNA-seq cohorts were analyzed by subclustering, signature scoring, and within scRNA-seq per-subpopulation contribution analysis. CellChat, AUCell pathway scoring, and drug prediction were used as exploratory analyses; IL-1β stimulated HNPCs served as the in vitro model.ResultsMachine learning analysis prioritized ITGBL1 and RPN1 as consensus core genes (combined AUC = 0.925), with their differential expression further supported in an independent external cohort. Single cell mapping preferentially localized both genes to a FibroNPC subpopulation and associated this cell state with coupled integrin/focal adhesion and ER protein processing/N-glycosylation programs. Notably, although FibroNPCs comprised less than one-fifth of NP/AF cells, they contributed approximately 48% of total ITGBL1 expression and disproportionate shares of several canonical fibrotic transcripts. CellChat further inferred increased macrophage SPP1–integrin communication involving structural cell populations in IVDD. In IL-1β stimulated HNPCs, both ITGBL1 and RPN1 were increased at the mRNA and protein levels.ConclusionITGBL1 and RPN1 represent FibroNPC associated molecular markers linked to coupled integrin signaling and N-glycosylation programs in IVDD. The disproportionate contribution of FibroNPCs to ITGBL1 and fibrotic gene expression highlights the importance of cell state resolved localization beyond conventional bulk hub-gene identification. Further functional validation is required before therapeutic implications can be established.

Introduction

Intervertebral disc degeneration (IVDD) is recognized as a major structural contributor to chronic low back pain,1 a pervasive musculoskeletal disorder that imposes healthcare burden on a global scale.2–4 The healthy intervertebral disc is organized into three components: a central, highly hydrated gelatinous nucleus pulposus (NP); an annulus fibrosus (AF) that concentrically surrounds the NP; and cartilaginous endplates (EP) that cover the NP tissue.5–7 The onset of IVDD is ultimately facilitated by the NP tissue becoming more fibrous, which is accompanied by a decrease in type II collagen and aggrecan.8,9

IVDD is fundamentally driven by a decline in NP cellularity and disruption of extracellular matrix (ECM) homeostasis.10 In healthy discs, NP cells maintain ECM balance through integrin mediated mechanotransduction that coordinates matrix synthesis and catabolism. During degeneration, protein-degrading of the ECM generates fibronectin fragments that bind integrins (eg, α5β1) on NP cells,11 activating ERK and NF-κB signaling and establishing a positive feedback loop that accelerates matrix degradation.12–14 Yet which cell subpopulations drive integrin-mediated matrix degradation, and whether endogenous mechanisms exist to counterbalance this catabolic cascade, remain unknown.

Recent single cell transcriptomic studies have revealed substantial cellular heterogeneity within the NP, including a fibrotic NP cell subpopulation (FibroNPC) linked to ECM remodeling and terminal differentiation during degeneration. However, the molecular heterogeneity within this fibrotic compartment and the transcriptional programs that distinguish FibroNPCs from other NP cell states remain incompletely characterized. In particular, two critical questions remain. First, molecular markers defining FibroNPC identity across independent cohorts have not been systematically established. Second, the cellular source of bulk-level biomarkers such as ITGBL1 and RPN1—whether they are expressed uniformly across NP cells or restricted to a particular subpopulation—has not been resolved.

In the present study, integrated bioinformatics screening prioritized integrin subunit beta-like 1 (ITGBL1) and ribophorin I (RPN1) as candidate genes for further investigation. ITGBL1 is a secreted EGF-like domain containing protein that modulates integrin activity. Previous studies have implicated ITGBL1 in fibroblast/myofibroblast remodeling and fibrogenesis.15–17 Notably, however, ITGBL1 has also shown chondroprotective effects in osteoarthritis.18 Because NP fibrosis is a central pathological feature of disc degeneration,19 whether ITGBL1 is associated with a protective or pro-fibrotic state in IVDD may depend on its specific cellular context. Macrophage mediated inflammation is also an important component of the IVDD microenvironment,20 providing a rationale for examining the relationship between these candidate genes and local immune remodeling. RPN1 is a component of the oligosaccharyltransferase complex and is involved in N-linked glycosylation and endoplasmic reticulum (ER) homeostasis,21 while ER stress itself has been implicated in NP cell dysfunction during IVDD.22 Recent studies have further associated RPN1 with immunosuppressive microenvironments and immune cell infiltration in several cancers.23,24 Critically, it remains unknown whether ITGBL1 and RPN1 are expressed broadly across NP cells or preferentially localized to specific subpopulations, leaving a fundamental gap in understanding how their bulk level dysregulation relates to pathological cell states.

To address these gaps, we designed a multi-layered analytical strategy structured around three aims. First, we integrated a bulk transcriptomic meta cohort with two independent human IVDD scRNA-seq cohorts that span complementary stages of the disease spectrum, allowing a cross-stage comparison that no single existing dataset provides. Second, instead of relying on a single hub gene, we used three machine learning algorithms with external validation to define a two gene signature, ITGBL1 and RPN1, of the degenerative state. Third, by combining subcluster level expression analysis with CellChat inferred communication networks, we localized both genes to a FibroNPC subpopulation, characterized the associated integrin/focal-adhesion and ER protein-processing/N-glycosylation programs, and examined their relationships with inferred intercellular signaling during degeneration.

Materials and Methods

Data Acquisition and Processing

The discovery meta cohort comprised GSE23130 (23 human annulus-disc tissue profiles), GSE56081 (5 control and 5 degenerative human nucleus pulposus samples), and GSE70362 (48 human annulus fibrosus/nucleus pulposus tissue profiles spanning degeneration grades), for 81 bulk profiles in total. GSE15227 (10 IVDD and 5 control samples) was retained as an independent external expression validation cohort. The single-cell cohorts were GSE199866 (four paired NP/AF preparations from non-degenerating and degenerating discs) and GSE244889 (seven NP samples).

Identification of Differentially Expressed Genes

Prior to differential analysis, 13 hemoglobin and erythrocyte-related genes (HBA1, HBA2, HBB, HBD, HBE1, HBG1, HBG2, HBM, HBQ1, HBZ, ALAS2, GYPA, GYPB) were excluded to minimize blood contamination artifacts. Differential expression analysis was performed using the limma R package. DEGs were defined as genes with Benjamini–Hochberg adjusted P values (FDR) < 0.05 and |log2FC| > 0.585 (corresponding to 1.5-fold change). Results were visualized with a volcano plot (ggplot2) and a hierarchical clustering heatmap of the top 100 DEGs (50 upregulated and 50 downregulated, ranked by P-value; pheatmap).

Machine Learning Based Screening of Hub Genes

The top 150 DEGs ranked by a composite score of −log10(P-value) × |log2FC| were used as the candidate pool for machine learning feature selection. All procedures were initialized with set.seed(123) for reproducibility. Three complementary algorithms were applied: (1) LASSO logistic regression (glmnet, binomial family) with 10-fold cross-validation to determine the optimal λ (lambda.min); (2) SVM-RFE (e1071, kernlab, caret) with a radial basis function kernel, 5-fold cross-validation, and feature subset sizes of 1–30; and (3) random forest (randomForest, 500 trees), retaining the top 20 genes by Mean Decrease in Gini index. Consensus hub genes were defined as the intersection of all three algorithms and visualized using a Venn diagram (ggVennDiagram).

Construction and Evaluation of the Diagnostic Model

Based on the identified core genes, a logistic regression-based diagnostic nomogram was constructed using the regplot R package, with kernel density and box plots overlaid to visualize predictor distributions. Predictive accuracy was evaluated by calibration curves (bootstrap resampling, B = 1000; rms), the Hosmer–Lemeshow test, and the Brier Score. ROC analysis assessed diagnostic efficacy, with AUC and 95% CIs reported for individual and combined gene models.25 Decision curve analysis (dcurves) evaluated net clinical benefit across threshold probabilities of 0.01–0.99. ROC, calibration, and decision-curve analyses were used to characterize the two-gene model within the integrated discovery meta-cohort. GSE15227 was used for independent expression validation.

Functional Enrichment and Immune Infiltration Analysis

Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) was performed on the integrated bulk transcriptomic profiles using KEGG pathway gene sets via the clusterProfiler R package. Targeted Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis was additionally performed for the two core genes (ITGBL1 and RPN1) to characterize their associated biological processes.

To evaluate the local immune landscape, the single sample GSEA algorithm was used to quantify the relative abundances of 25 immune cell subsets in each sample, based on curated marker gene signatures from established immunological literature; signatures with fewer than 3 detected genes were excluded (Supplementary Table S1). Differences in immune cell ssGSEA scores between IVDD and control groups were assessed using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. P values were adjusted across the 25 immune-cell signatures using the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure, and FDR < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Exploratory calculation of the Spearman rank correlation coefficient between the expression level of core genes and the immune cell enrichment score.

Single-Cell RNA Sequencing Analysis

The two scRNA-seq datasets described in Data acquisition and processing were processed with Seurat v5. Quality control excluded cells with <200 or >4000 (GSE199866) or >5000 (GSE244889) detected genes, or excessive mitochondrial content (>15% and >20%, respectively, consistent with original protocols). After normalization, variable feature selection (top 2000 by VST), scaling, and PCA, batch effects were corrected with Harmony, followed by SNN clustering (dims = 1:15, resolution = 0.5) and UMAP visualization. Cell types were annotated using canonical markers: ACAN/SOX9/COL2A1/KRT19 (NP/AF cells), COL1A1/COL1A2/DCN/LUM/POSTN/PDGFRA (fibroblasts), and CD68/CD163/C1QA/TYROBP (macrophages) (Supplementary Figure 1). Core gene expression across cell types was assessed with the Bonferroni-corrected Wilcoxon rank-sum test and visualized with violin and UMAP feature plots.

After initial annotation, NP and AF cells were retained as distinct anatomical compartments. They were jointly subclustered only to identify a shared degeneration associated structural cell state across cohorts. This approach was adopted because GSE199866 contains both NP and inner AF cells, whereas GSE244889 contains NP cells only. Tissue origin was retained throughout downstream analyses. The combined NP/AF subset was re-normalized, Harmony corrected, and subclustered (resolution = 0.6). Subcluster markers were identified using FindAllMarkers (min.pct = 0.25, logfc.threshold = 0.25). Proportional shifts between control and IVDD groups were visualized with bar plots; changes exceeding 5 percentage points were considered biologically notable Each cell was scored against five curated NPC signatures—Homeostatic NPC, FibroNPC, Inflammatory NPC, ER-stressed NPC (Supplementary Table S2), and Progenitor NPC—and the results were visualized as a subcluster by signature heatmap. The joint NP/AF analysis was used to characterize the shared FibroNPC like state by subclustering, signature scoring, pseudotime analysis, and cell communication analysis.

To quantify the relative contribution of individual cell subpopulations to the aggregate expression signal, we performed a within scRNA-seq per-subpopulation contribution analysis. For each gene and group, the reconstructed aggregate expression was calculated as: Σ[Proportion (cell type, group) × Mean expression (gene, cell type, group)]. This analysis estimates the relative contribution of each subpopulation to the aggregate expression signal reconstructed from the scRNA-seq data and does not represent deconvolution against an independent bulk transcriptomic cohort.

Layer Integration

JoinLayers was applied to consolidate per-sample layers in the merged Seurat v5 object into a unified expression matrix prior to CellChat analysis.

Pseudotime Trajectory Analysis

To order NP/AF cells along a degeneration-associated trajectory, the NP/AF subset—with Seurat v5 layers merged (JoinLayers)—was converted to a SingleCellExperiment object and analyzed with Slingshot. Lineage structure and per-cell pseudotime were inferred on the UMAP embedding using the NP/AF subclusters as cluster labels. To anchor the trajectory at the healthiest state, the starting cluster was defined data-drivenly as the subcluster containing the highest proportion of control (non-degenerated) cells. The resulting pseudotime was used to display the distribution of NPC subtypes along the trajectory and the expression of ITGBL1 and RPN1 along pseudotime.

Cell-Cell Communication Analysis

CellChat v1.6 was used to infer intercellular communication networks with the human ligand–receptor database. Communication probabilities were estimated with the trimean method and filtered to retain interactions involving ≥5 cells per type. AUCell was applied to score pathway activity at single-cell resolution for key IVDD associated signaling pathways using gene sets from KEGG and Reactome (Supplementary Table S3).

Drug Gene Interaction Prediction

Drug gene interaction prediction was performed by querying the two core genes (ITGBL1 and RPN1) against the Drug Signatures Database (DSigDB) via the Enrichr platform.26,27 Candidate compounds with significant associations (P < 0.05) were identified and classified by pharmacological category. A drug–gene interaction network and a three-layer drug pathway target regulatory network were constructed to visualize relationships among candidate drugs, enriched pathways, and core target genes.

Cell Culture and qRT-PCR Validation

SV40 immortalized human nucleus pulposus cells (HNPCs; Cat. No. SAc0646, Aoyinbio, China) were maintained in nucleus pulposus cell specific culture medium (SAPM0646, Aoyinbio) at 37°C in a humidified atmosphere of 95% air and 5% CO2. For inflammatory stimulation, cells were treated with 10 ng/mL recombinant human IL-1β for 24 h. Total RNA was extracted using the SteadyPure RNA Extraction Kit (AG21024, Accurate Biology, Hunan, China) and reverse-transcribed using the Evo M-MLV RT Kit for qPCR (AG11707, Accurate Biology). qRT-PCR was performed with the SYBR Green Pro Taq HS Premix qPCR Kit (AG11701, Accurate Biology) to quantify mRNA levels of ITGBL1, RPN1, MMP13, and MMP3, with GAPDH as the internal reference. Relative expression was calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method. qRT-PCR experiments were independently performed three times using separately cultured and IL-1 treated HNPCs, with technical triplicate measurements within each experiment. Mean Ct values from the technical replicates were used for ΔCt and 2−ΔΔCt calculations. Statistical significance was assessed by Student’s t-test (Supplementary Table S5).

For protein level validation, total protein was extracted from control and IL-1β treated HNPCs using RIPA lysis buffer. Equal amounts of protein were separated by SDS-PAGE, transferred onto PVDF membranes, and probed with primary antibodies against ITGBL1 (1:4000, 30010-1-AP, Proteintech), RPN1 (1:1500, HA722373, HUABIO), and GAPDH (1:80000, ET1601-4, HUABIO) as a loading control. After incubation with HRP conjugated secondary antibodies, bands were visualized using an ECL detection system and quantified by densitometry with Image J software. Western blotting was independently performed three times. For each experiment, ITGBL1 and RPN1 band intensities were normalized to the corresponding GAPDH signal from the same samples and expressed relative to the control group. Bands used for quantification were identified according to their expected molecular weight regions.

Statistical Analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.5.1). Continuous variables between two groups were compared using Student’s t-test or Wilcoxon rank-sum test, as appropriate. Correlations were evaluated using Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient. P-values were adjusted for multiple comparisons using the Benjamini-Hochberg method where applicable. A two-sided P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Subcluster proportions, per-subpopulation expression contributions, pseudotime distributions, and AUCell pathway scores were used as descriptive single-cell measures. Because the number of biological donors was limited, individual cells were not treated as independent biological replicates for confidence-interval estimation or confirmatory hypothesis testing.

Differential expression analysis between the IVDD and Control groups identified a set of DEGs. The volcano plot (Figure 2D) spotlights prominently upregulated genes in degenerated discs, such as CYP1B1, ITGBL1, CFD and CCND1. Graded clustering of the top 100 most significant DEGs revealed a distinct transcriptomic contrast between the IVDD and Control cohorts (Figure 2C), indicating deep molecular reprogramming during IVDD.

Three machine learning algorithms were applied for feature selection. LASSO logistic regression, using the optimal penalty parameter (λ = 0.0232) determined via 10-fold cross validation, extracted 14 feature genes (Figure 2H). SVM-RFE achieved a maximum cross-validation accuracy of 0.917 (Figure 2E and F). Random Forest analysis showed that the out-of-bag error rate stabilized as the number of decision trees approached 500 (Figure 2I). By intersecting the results of all three algorithms, 9 consensus core genes (CHST10, CYP1B1, TMTC1, EMILIN1, CCND1, ITGBL1, ST6GALNAC2, RPN1, and MLPH) were obtained (Figure 2J). The co-expression matrix among the 9 core genes confirmed coordinated regulatory relationships (Figure 2G).

ROC curve analysis showed that ITGBL1 and RPN1 yielded individual AUC values of 0.885 (95% CI: 0.796–0.974) and 0.824 (95% CI: 0.701–0.947), respectively. The combined model achieved an AUC of 0.925 (95% CI: 0.854–0.997; Figure 3C). Given the limited size of the external validation cohort, these performance estimates should be interpreted cautiously and require confirmation in larger independent cohorts. A diagnostic nomogram was constructed to estimate individualized IVDD probability (Figure 3A). In the discovery meta-cohort, the calibration curve showed agreement between the apparent and bias-corrected predictions (Figure 3B), and the Brier Score was 0.124. Decision curve analysis indicated that the combined model provided net clinical benefit across a range of threshold probabilities (Figure 3D). Given the invasive nature of disc tissue sampling, these genes are therefore more appropriately framed as molecular correlates of disease state and candidates for further mechanistic investigation rather than established clinical diagnostic tools.

Systemic Pathway Shifts and Immune Microenvironment Remodeling in IVDD

To further elucidate the underlying biological mechanisms driving IVDD and the functional roles of the identified biomarkers, GSEA was performed on the integrated global transcriptomic profiles. As illustrated in Figure 4A, degenerated discs exhibited significant enrichment in pathways associated with ECM homeostasis, cellular stress, and immune regulation, notably Integrin signaling, N-Glycan biosynthesis, Protein processing in endoplasmic reticulum, protein export, and Antigen processing and presentation. The targeted functional enrichment analysis of the two core genes further corroborated these transcriptomic changes, with the enriched biological processes mainly involving integrin-mediated cell adhesion, collagen fibril organization, and glycoprotein metabolic and biosynthetic processes (Figure 4B). Additional enrichment terms related to protein glycosylation and glycoprotein metabolism are shown in Supplementary Figure 2. Comparative analysis of the relative information flow across signaling pathways revealed that FN1, TENASCIN, NCAM, HSPG, and COLLAGEN remained the most dominant signaling axes in both conditions (Figure 7C). Notably, PTN, PDGF, FN1, and ANGPTL pathways exhibited increased information flow in the IVDD group, while pathways such as THY1, MHC-II, VISFATIN, and SPP1 were preferentially active in degenerated discs, consistent with an inflammatory and pro-fibrotic microenvironmental shift.

The differential interaction strength analysis revealed that macrophage directed signaling was the most dramatically enhanced component in the IVDD group (Figure 7D). At the ligand-receptor level, the most prominent altered L-R pairs from macrophages to structural cells included TNFSF12-TNFRSF12A and SPP1-CD44 (macrophage-to-fibroblast), as well as multiple integrin-mediated SPP1 pairs (SPP1-ITGAV+ITGB5, SPP1-ITGAV+ITGB1, SPP1-ITGA5+ITGB1) and NAMPT-(ITGA5+ITGB1) (macrophage-to-NP/AF cells) (Figure 7B). These integrin dependent interactions are particularly noteworthy given the molecular function of ITGBL1 as a known modulator of integrin signaling.18

Taken together, these CellChat derived findings suggest a potential association between macrophage SPP1–integrin communication and the ITGBL1 enriched FibroNPC compartment. Macrophage-derived SPP1 was predicted to communicate with NP/AF cells through multiple integrin receptor pairs, while ITGBL1 expression was enriched in FibroNPCs. This overlap suggests that the ITGBL1 enriched FibroNPC compartment may be involved in macrophage-associated integrin signaling, although this relationship remains computationally inferred. The two dimensional signaling-role analysis further showed changes in inferred communication patterns during degeneration (Figure 7E and F): macrophages shifted from minimal to greater signaling involvement (incoming strength ~1.5, outgoing ~1.0), whereas NP/AF cells showed reduced overall signaling involvement.

Based on the two core genes (ITGBL1 and RPN1), drug–gene interaction prediction using DSigDB identified eight candidate compounds (P < 0.05; Figure 8C), predominantly enriched in anti inflammatory and immune‑modulatory categories (Figure 8D), consistent with immune‑mediated disc degeneration. After filtering, simvastatin and lycorine were prioritized as candidate compounds, both supported by previous preclinical evidence in IVDD models28,29 —simvastatin inhibits IL‑1β‑induced apoptosis and ECM degradation via NF‑κB/MAPK pathways,30 while lycorine suppresses NF‑κB signaling and catabolic enzymes (MMP3, MMP13, ADAMTS4/5).31 Other candidates (eg, MG-262, estradiol) represent exploratory leads or symptomatic agents requiring further validation.

To contextualize these interactions, a drug–gene interaction network (Figure 8F) and a three layer drug pathway target regulatory network (Figure 8E) were constructed. The integrated network suggested two potentially relevant drug–pathway associations: the ITGBL1 associated branch was mainly linked to NF-κB signaling and integrin ECM interactions, whereas the RPN1 associated branch, represented by Simvastatin and MG-262, was linked to N-glycan biosynthesis, ER protein processing, and the UPR. Notably, NF-κB signaling emerged as a shared network node connecting these associations, suggesting a potential convergence between inflammation-related signaling and ECM remodeling in IVDD. These computational findings support further evaluation of Simvastatin and Lycorine as candidate compounds rather than established therapeutic agents.

To provide experimental support for the bioinformatic predictions, we examined core gene expression in IL-1β stimulated HNPCs. qRT-PCR confirmed significant upregulation of both ITGBL1 (P < 0.01) and RPN1 (P < 0.0001) alongside the catabolic markers MMP13 (P < 0.0001) and MMP3 (P < 0.001) compared to untreated controls (Figure 8G). To further validate these findings at the protein level, Western blot analysis demonstrated that IL-1β treatment markedly increased the protein expression of both ITGBL1 and RPN1, with densitometric quantification showing approximately 1.35-fold and 1.26-fold upregulation, respectively, after normalization to GAPDH (Figure 8H and I, Supplementary Figure 3). The concordance between mRNA and protein level changes provides robust evidence that ITGBL1 and RPN1 are responsive to the pro-inflammatory catabolic milieu characteristic of IVDD progression.

Discussion

Intervertebral disc degeneration is driven in part by progressive NP fibrosis, but the cellular localization and transcriptional context of IVDD associated genes remain incompletely understood. In the present study, the main contribution extends beyond the identification of ITGBL1 and RPN1 as candidate IVDD associated markers. By integrating bulk and single-cell transcriptomic analyses, we preferentially localized ITGBL1 and RPN1 to a FibroNPC subpopulation and associated this cell state with integrin/focal-adhesion and ER protein processing/N-glycosylation programs.

ITGBL1 is a secreted, integrin binding extracellular matrix protein that regulates integrin activity. It promotes fibrosis in the heart and liver,16,17 whereas in articular cartilage it protects chondrocytes and limits osteoarthritis.18 In our data, ITGBL1 was concentrated in the FibroNPC, co-expressed with COL1A1, POSTN, and LOXL1, and upregulated together with MMP13 and MMP3 in IL-1β-stimulated NP cells at both the mRNA and protein levels. This pattern indicates that, in the degenerating disc, ITGBL1 participates in fibrotic and catabolic remodeling rather than protecting the tissue, in line with its pro-fibrotic role in other organs and consistent with the FibroNPC moving away from a chondrogenic phenotype.19 CellChat inferred that macrophage derived SPP1 may communicate with NP/AF cells mainly through integrin receptors, consistent with a recent report implicating the SPP1–integrin axis in NP degeneration,32 while AUCell indicated increased integrin pathway activity with degeneration. Based on these convergent observations, we propose a hypothetical feed forward integrin model in which macrophage derived SPP1 may engage integrin receptors on FibroNPCs, while ITGBL1 may modulate integrin associated matrix remodeling. The resulting matrix changes could, in turn, favor further macrophage recruitment or activation. This model integrates the observed associations but is not directly demonstrated by the present cross-sectional data and requires further functional and temporal validation.

RPN1 is a catalytic subunit of the oligosaccharyltransferase complex and is required for N-glycosylation of membrane and secreted proteins.21 It has been linked to macrophage polarization and an immunosuppressive microenvironment in several cancers.23 In our study, RPN1 marked the glycosylation arm of the FibroNPC, correlated positively with myeloid cells and negatively with effector memory CD4 T cells, and was induced by IL-1β. Because N-glycosylation modifies integrin receptors and antigen presentation, the increase in RPN1 may reflect the higher biosynthetic and ER load of a matrix secreting cell and a possible link to the disc immune environment. These results are preliminary and identify RPN1 as a candidate for further study.

The two arms are probably connected, because a cell producing abundant matrix also carries a high N-glycosylation and ER load, which ties matrix output to the secretory machinery. Although the FibroNPC made up less than one fifth of NP/AF cells, it generated most fibrotic transcripts and, with degeneration, strongly induced COL1A1, COL1A2, COL3A1, POSTN, FN1, TGFBI, CTHRC1, and SPP1 without increasing in number; the heavy biosynthetic and ER load may restrain proliferation while inflammation raises per-cell output. This pattern was reproduced in the progression stage Cohort II. A recent multi-omics study of the same data instead highlighted NOTCH3 in NP fibrosis,33 suggesting that several programs converge on this state. Drug prediction identified simvastatin and lycorine, both acting on NF-κB and supported by earlier IVDD studies;28–30 targeting the FibroNPC state, for example by combining integrin-signaling modulation with relief of ER stress, may be more focused than general anti-inflammatory treatment.

This study has several limitations. First, the relatively limited sample sizes, particularly the small external validation cohort, may affect model stability and generalizability and increase the risk of overfitting. Second, the included bulk and single cell datasets differed in platforms, tissue composition, and disease grading criteria, and some uncertainty in cell type annotation and subpopulation estimates therefore remains. Third, macrophages represented only a small fraction of the single cell data (~0.3%); accordingly, the CellChat derived communication patterns should be regarded as exploratory rather than evidence of established signaling mechanisms. Finally, the in vitro validation was limited to a single IL-1β condition in an immortalized HNPC line, without primary human tissue validation or direct loss or gain of function experiments. In addition, no in vivo experiments were performed to confirm the causal roles of ITGBL1 and RPN1 in IVDD. Further studies using primary human samples, functional manipulation, and in vivo models are needed to confirm these findings.

Conclusion

In summary, we identified ITGBL1 and RPN1 as candidate molecular markers associated with IVDD and found both genes to be preferentially localized to a FibroNPC subpopulation characterized by integrin/focal-adhesion and ER protein-processing/N-glycosylation programs. ITGBL1 was associated with a pro-fibrotic, catabolic phenotype, whereas RPN1 was associated with N-glycosylation and features of the local immune environment. CellChat further suggested potential macrophage SPP1–integrin communication involving structural cell populations, although these relationships remain computationally inferred. Together, these findings highlight ITGBL1, RPN1, and the FibroNPC state as priorities for future functional and mechanistic validation.

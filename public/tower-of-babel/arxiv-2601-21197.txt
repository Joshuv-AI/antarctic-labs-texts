A family of simple U ⁡ ( 𝔥 ) U(\mathfrak{h}) -free modules of rank 2 over 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 2 ) \mathfrak{sl}(2)
Dimitar Grantcharov, Khoa Nguyen, Kaiming Zhao

Abstract
. We study simple 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 2 ) \mathfrak{sl}(2) -modules over ℂ \mathbb{C} that are free of finite rank as U ⁡ ( 𝔥 ) U(\mathfrak{h}) -modules, where 𝔥 \mathfrak{h} is a Cartan subalgebra of 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 2 ) \mathfrak{sl}(2) . Our main result is an explicit classification of the scalar-type simple modules of rank 2 2 . We also give a criterion for when two such modules are isomorphic. Both the classification and the isomorphism problem reduce to twisted conjugacy classes in GL 2 ⁡ ( ℂ ⁡ [ h ] ) \GL_{2}(\mathbb{C}[h]) and rely on Cohn’s standard form. 2020 MSC: 17B10, 17B20 Keywords and phrases: Lie algebra, simple modules, 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 2 ) \mathfrak{sl}(2) -modules, non-weight modules



1.
Introduction

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
is a classical object and continues to serve as a basic point of reference in the representation theory of complex Lie algebras. The category of
generalized weight
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
-modules—i.e. modules on which a Cartan subalgebra acts locally
finitely, and which contains the weight modules as a full subcategory—has been described in detail; see,
for example,
[
14
]
and the references therein. A key feature is that every such module decomposes as
a direct sum of generalized
\mathfrak{h}
-weight spaces (equivalently,
\mathfrak{h}
-locally finite
submodules), for a fixed Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}=\mathbb{C}h
of
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
.

In contrast, outside the generalized weight setting the representation theory becomes much less explicit,
even for simple modules. Block’s foundational work
[
4
]
shows that simple
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
-modules
are parameterized by similarity classes of irreducible elements in a certain Euclidean (Ore) skew algebra

R
of differential-operator type. In particular, the non-weight simple modules arise as cyclic left
quotients
R/Ra
for such irreducible elements
a\in R
. While this gives a conceptual
parametrization, irreducible elements of
R
are difficult to describe in concrete terms, so the result
does not by itself provide a convenient realization of explicit families of simple non-weight modules.

A natural and broad class of non-weight modules is obtained by imposing freeness over
U(\mathfrak{h})
.
Let
\mathcal{M}
be the full subcategory of
U(\mathfrak{sl}(2))
-modules whose restriction to

U(\mathfrak{h})
is free of finite rank; equivalently, for
M\in\mathcal{M}
one has

M\simeq U(\mathfrak{h})^{\oplus n}=\mathbb{C}[h]^{\oplus n}
as a
U(\mathfrak{h})
-module for some
n\geq 1
.
These modules can be viewed as complementary to the generalized weight setting: rather than decomposing
into
\mathfrak{h}
-(generalized) weight spaces, they are free over
U(\mathfrak{h})
.
The rank-one case is completely understood
[
16
]
, while for higher rank important but partial results
and constructions are available; see, e.g.,
[
2
,
6
,
12
,
7
,
8
,
15
,
13
]
.

In this paper we initiate the study of rank-
2
objects
M
in
\mathcal{M}
, with the goal of eventually
obtaining an explicit classification of the simple modules in this rank. Choosing a
\mathbb{C}[h]
-basis
identifies
M
with
\mathbb{C}[h]^{\oplus 2}
, so that
h
acts by multiplication. The generators
e
and
f

then act by explicit difference operators: their actions are encoded by matrices in

\Mat_{2}(\mathbb{C}[h])
composed with the shift automorphisms
\sigma
and
\sigma^{-1}
, respectively, where

\sigma\big(p(h)\big)=p(h-1)
(see Theorem
2.2
). A convenient parameterization of such
modules uses three pieces of data: a central character parameter
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
, an ordered partition

{\bf a}=(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})
of
2
, and a matrix
K(h)\in\GL_{2}(\mathbb{C}[h])
. Here
\bf a
and
K(h)
are determined from the Smith normal form
P_{\alpha,{\bf a}}

of the polynomial matrix encoding the action of
f
.

In the present paper we complete the first part of the rank-
2
project, namely the case of

scalar-type
modules, i.e. those for which the Smith normal form
P_{\alpha,{\bf a}}
is a scalar matrix,
equivalently
{\bf a}\in\{(2,0,0),(0,2,0),(0,0,2)\}
.
More precisely, we give a complete classification and an explicit realization of all
simple

scalar-type modules in
\mathcal{M}
.
The classification relies on the uniqueness of Cohn’s standard form for matrices in
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{C}[h])

[
5
]
. For explicit computations we relate Cohn’s standard form to another canonical factorization, which we call
the
LQ form
. It is obtained by applying the Euclidean algorithm to the first row of

K(h)\in\GL_{2}(\mathbb{C}[h])
, and expresses
K(h)
as a lower triangular front factor followed by a product of the
matrices
E(\cdot)
.
To our knowledge, this is the first paper to use decompositions in
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{C}[h])
systematically in the rank-
2

U(\mathfrak{h})
-free setting. The rank-
2
case already presents a surprisingly rich collection of simples, and
extending these results to
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{C}[h])
for
n\geq 3
poses a substantial challenge.

The isomorphism classes are governed by a natural twisted conjugacy relation on

\GL_{2}(\mathbb{C}[h])
(cf. Definition
2.5
): two matrices
A
and
B
are
\sigma^{-1}
-similar if

B=P(h)^{-1}A\,P(h+1)\qquad\text{for some }P(h)\in\GL_{2}(\mathbb{C}[h]).

Equivalently, these are the twisted conjugacy classes for the
\mathbb{Z}
-action on

\GL_{2}(\mathbb{C}[h])
induced by
\sigma^{-1}
. One can also view the set of
\sigma^{-1}
-similarity classes
as a non-abelian cohomology set
H^{1}(\mathbb{Z},\GL_{2}(\mathbb{C}[h]))
for this action. The main results are
summarized below.

(1) Matrices
K(h)
for modules with no rank-
1
submodules.

The classification easily reduces to describing those
K(h)\in\GL_{2}(\mathbb{C}[h])
for which the associated scalar-type module
contains no rank-
1
submodules. Set

\mathcal{S}:=\GL_{2}(\mathbb{C}[h])\setminus\bigcup_{a,b\in\mathbb{C}^{*}}\Orb_{\sigma^{-1}}\bigl(\diag(a,b)\bigr),

the complement of the
\sigma^{-1}
-similarity orbits of constant diagonal matrices. By
Lemma
2.10
and Lemma
3.2
, for scalar-type modules the condition
K(h)\in\mathcal{S}

is equivalent to the absence of rank-
1
submodules. Using Cohn’s standard form in
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{C}[h])

[
5
]
, built from the matrices

E(u(h))=\begin{pmatrix}u(h)&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix},

we prove (Theorem
3.10
) that
K(h)\in\mathcal{S}
if and only if
K(h)
is
\sigma^{-1}
-similar
to a matrix of the form

\mathbf{E}_{(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})}(v_{1},\dots,v_{m})=\diag(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\,E(v_{1})\cdots E(v_{m}),\qquad\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in\mathbb{C}^{*},\ m\geq 1,\ v_{i}\in\mathbb{C}[h]\setminus\mathbb{C}.

We also give a complete and explicit list of the representatives in standard form for matrices outside
\mathcal{S}

(Theorem
3.15
).

(2) Simplicity and isomorphisms for scalar-type rank
2
.

Every scalar-type rank-
2
object in
\mathcal{M}
is of the form

M\left(\alpha,{\bf a},K(h)\right),\qquad{\bf a}\in\{(2,0,0),(0,2,0),(0,0,2)\},\ \alpha\in\mathbb{C},\ K(h)\in\GL_{2}(\mathbb{C}[h]).

The simplicity criterion (Theorem
4.3
) shows that
M(\alpha,{\bf a},K(h))
is simple
if and only if
K(h)\in\mathcal{S}
, with one additional restriction in the case
{\bf a}=(0,2,0)
:

\alpha\notin 1+\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.

For the simple scalar-type modules, we then obtain an explicit isomorphism theorem. Writing

K(h)=\mathbf{E}_{(c,d)}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})
with
u_{i}\in\mathbb{C}[h]\setminus\mathbb{C}
, we prove that two such modules are
isomorphic if and only if the parameters
(c,d,u_{1},\dots,u_{k})
lie in the same orbit of an explicit
semidirect product group
G=\mathbb{C}^{*}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}
acting on these tuples (Theorem
4.12
).

To keep the presentation focused, we do not pursue here the cohomological viewpoint on

\sigma^{-1}
-similarity classes (and its relation to
\mathfrak{h}
-free modules).

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we provide the realization of

U(\mathfrak{h})
-free modules of finite rank with fixed central character, introduce
\sigma^{-1}
-similarity, and
review Cohn’s standard form in
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{C}[h])
. Section
3
is devoted to the structure of the set

\mathcal{S}
and to explicit representatives for matrices outside
\mathcal{S}
. Finally, in
Section
4
we prove the simplicity criterion for scalar-type rank-
2
modules and
establish the isomorphism theorem using the group
G
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Conventions and notation

Throughout this paper, we use
\mathbb{Z}
,
\mathbb{C}
, and
\mathbb{C}^{*}
to denote the sets of integers, complex numbers, and nonzero complex numbers, respectively. We write
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq k}
for the set of all integers
i
such that
i\geq k
. Unless otherwise stated, all vector spaces, Lie algebras (including
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
), homomorphisms, and tensor products are considered over the field
\mathbb{C}
.

For a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, we denote its universal enveloping algebra by
U(\mathfrak{g})
.

We fix the basis
\{e,f,h\}
of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
to be given by the following matrices:

e:=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad f:=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad h:=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{2}&0\\
0&-\frac{1}{2}\end{pmatrix}.

The Lie bracket relations among these basis elements are:

[h,e]=e,\qquad[e,f]=2h,\qquad[h,f]=-f.

In this paper we fix the quadratic Casimir element of
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
to be
c=(2h+1)^{2}+4fe
. It is well known that the center
\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{sl}(2))
of
U(\mathfrak{sl}(2))
is
\mathbb{C}[c]
. We will say that an
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
-module
M
has central character
\gamma\in\mathbb{C}
if
c
acts by multiplication by
\gamma
on
M
.

For a ring
R
, we write
\mathrm{Mat}_{n}(R)
for the ring of
n\times n
matrices with entries in
R
. We use the same symbol
h
for the Cartan element of
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
and as the variable of the ring
\mathbb{C}[h]
. On
\mathbb{C}[h]^{\oplus n}
, the element
h\in\mathfrak{sl}(2)
acts by multiplication by the indeterminate
h
.
We denote the group of all invertible
n\times n
matrices with entries in
\mathbb{C}[h]
as
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{C}[h])
. The shift automorphism
\sigma
of
\mathbb{C}[h]
will play a crucial role in this paper, namely,
\sigma:\mathbb{C}[h]\;\to\;\mathbb{C}[h]
defined by

\sigma\big(p(h)\big)\;:=\;p(h-1),\qquad\text{for every }p(h)\in\mathbb{C}[h].

We will often write
A^{\prime}(h)
for a second matrix in
\Mat_{n}(\mathbb{C}[h])
associated to
A(h)\in\Mat_{n}(\mathbb{C}[h])
; in such cases
A^{\prime}
is not the derivative of
A
.

2.2.
The category of
U(\mathfrak{h})
-free modules of finite rank

Define
\mathcal{M}
to be the full subcategory of

U(\mathfrak{sl}(2))\text{-mod}
whose objects are
U(\mathfrak{sl}(2))
-modules
M
such that

\operatorname{Res}^{U(\mathfrak{sl}(2))}_{\;U(\mathfrak{h})}M\;\simeq\;U(\mathfrak{h})^{\oplus n}\;=\;\mathbb{C}[h]^{\oplus n}\qquad\text{for some}\;\;n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}.

Every object
M
in
\mathcal{M}
is an
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
-module and a
U(\mathfrak{h})
-free of rank
n
. For brevity, we will often call such
M
a
rank-
n
module
.

Throughout the paper, we will identify the underlying space of any module
M
in
\mathcal{M}
with

M=\mathbb{C}[h]^{\oplus n}
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and assume that the action of
h
is by multiplication. In explicit terms, every
m\in M
is a column vector
\bigl(g_{1}(h)\;\;\dots\;\;g_{n}(h)\bigr)^{\mathsf{T}}
, with
g_{j}(h)\in{\mathbb{C}}[h]
and

h\;\cdot\;\bigl(g_{1}(h)\;\;\dots\;\;g_{n}(h)\bigr)^{\mathsf{T}}\;=\;\bigl(hg_{1}(h)\;\;\dots\;\;hg_{n}(h)\bigr)^{\mathsf{T}}.

In this paper, we focus on the full subcategory
\mathcal{M}(2)\subset\mathcal{M}
consisting of those that are
U(\mathfrak{h})
-free of rank
2
. Precisely,

\mathcal{M}(2):=\Bigl\{\,M\in\mathcal{M}\ \bigm|\ \operatorname{Res}^{\;U(\mathfrak{sl}(2))}_{\;U(\mathfrak{h})}M\;\simeq\;U(\mathfrak{h})^{\oplus 2}\Bigr\}.

We next provide a description of all
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
-modules that are
U(\mathfrak{h})
-free of fixed rank
n
and of fixed central character
\gamma
. The proofs rely on solving matrix equations related to the three defining relations of
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
, and Smith Normal Form technique. The details will appear in
[
9
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
{\bf{a}}=(a_{-},a_{0},a_{+})
be a triple with
a_{-},a_{+}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
a_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
a_{-}+|a_{0}|+a_{+}=n
.
We define the diagonal matrix
P_{({\bf{a}},\alpha)}(x)\in\Mat_{n}(\mathbb{C}[x])
as follows:

(i)

P_{({\bf{a}},\alpha)}(x)_{ii}=1
for
i=1,\ldots,a_{-}
,

(ii)

If
a_{0}\geq 0
, then
P_{({\bf{a}},\alpha)}(x)_{ii}=x-\alpha+1
for
i=a_{-}+1,\ldots,a_{-}+a_{0}
,

(iii)

If
a_{0}<0
, then
P_{({\bf{a}},\alpha)}(x)_{ii}=x+\alpha
for
i=a_{-}+1,\ldots,a_{-}-a_{0}
,

(iv)

P_{({\bf{a}},\alpha)}(x)_{ii}=(x-\alpha+1)(x+\alpha)
for
i=a_{-}+|a_{0}|+1,\ldots,n
.

We also denote
\overline{P}_{({\bf{a}},\alpha)}(x)\in\Mat_{n}(\mathbb{C}[x])
so that

\overline{P}_{({\bf{a}},\alpha)}(x)P_{({\bf{a}},\alpha)}(x)=-(x-\alpha+1)(x+\alpha)\I_{n}.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
M=\mathbb{C}[h]^{\oplus n}
be a module in
\mathcal{M}
with a central character
\gamma=(2\alpha-1)^{2}
. Then there exist
K(h)\in\GL_{n}(\mathbb{C}[h])
and
{\bf{a}}=(a_{-},a_{0},a_{+})
, such that the
U(\mathfrak{sl}(2))
-action on
M
is given by:

e\cdot\begin{pmatrix}g_{1}(h)\\
\vdots\\
g_{n}(h)\end{pmatrix}=\sigma\left(K^{-1}(h)\overline{P}_{({\bf{a}},\alpha)}(h)\begin{pmatrix}g_{1}(h)\\
\vdots\\
g_{n}(h)\end{pmatrix}\right),\quad f\cdot\begin{pmatrix}g_{1}(h)\\
\vdots\\
g_{n}(h)\end{pmatrix}=P_{({\bf{a}},\alpha)}(h)K(h)\sigma^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}g_{1}(h)\\
\vdots\\
g_{n}(h)\end{pmatrix},

where
g_{i}(h)\in\mathbb{C}[h]
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. The matrix
P_{({\bf{a}},\alpha)}(h)
is the Smith Normal Form of the matrix of
f\in\End(M)
relative to the standard basis of
\mathbb{C}[h]^{\oplus n}
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
; let
{\bf a}=(a_{-},a_{0},a_{+})
and
{\bf b}=(b_{-},b_{0},b_{+})
be triples as defined in Definition
2.1
; and let
K_{1}(h),K_{2}(h)\in\GL_{n}(\mathbb{C}[h])
. Then

M\left(\alpha,{{\bf a}},K_{1}(h)\right)\simeq M\left(\beta,{\bf b},K_{2}(h)\right)

if and only if the following conditions hold:

•

either
\alpha=\beta
and
{\bf a}={\bf b}
, or
\alpha+\beta=1
and
{\bf b}=(a_{-},-a_{0},a_{+})
;

•

there exists
P(h)\in\GL_{n}(\mathbb{C}[h])
such that

P(h)P_{({\bf a},\alpha)}(h)K_{2}(h)=P_{({\bf a},\alpha)}(h)K_{1}(h)P(h+1).

Remark 2.4
.

The theorem above implies that, unless
\gamma=0
, the modules
M\left(\frac{1}{2}(1+\gamma^{1/2}),{\bf{a}},K(h)\right)
and
M\left(\frac{1}{2}(1-\gamma^{1/2}),{\bf{a}},K(h)\right)
have central character
\gamma
(here
\gamma^{1/2}
is a fixed square root of
\gamma
) and in general are nonisomorphic. In view of Theorem
2.3
, one may fix
\alpha
with the property
\re(\alpha)\geq 1/2
and abbreviate
M\left({\bf{a}},K(h)\right)
for the module
M\left(\alpha,{\bf{a}},K(h)\right)
of central character
\gamma
. In this paper, we prefer to keep
\alpha
as a part of the parameterization of the module.

2.3.
\sigma^{-1}
–similarity

In this subsection we recall the notions of
\sigma
- and
\sigma^{-1}
-similarity, following
[
9
]
. Our emphasis throughout is on
\sigma^{-1}
-similarity.

Definition 2.5

(
\sigma
–similarity and
\sigma^{-1}
–similarity)
.

Let
A,B\in\Mat_{n}(\mathbb{C}[h])
. We say that
A
and
B
are
\sigma
-similar
, written
A\sim_{\sigma}B
, if there exists
P(h)\in\GL_{n}(\mathbb{C}[h])
such that

B\;=\;P(h)^{-1}\,A\,P(h-1)\;=\;P(h)^{-1}A\,\sigma(P(h)).

Similarly,
A
and
B
are
\sigma^{-1}
-similar
, written
A\sim_{\sigma^{-1}}B
, if there exists
P(h)\in\GL_{n}(\mathbb{C}[h])
such that

B\;=\;P(h)^{-1}\,A\,P(h+1)\;=\;P(h)^{-1}A\,\sigma^{-1}(P(h)).

Remark 2.6
.

The relations of
\sigma
-similarity and
\sigma^{-1}
-similarity on
\Mat_{n}(\mathbb{C}[h])
are equivalence relations. The second bullet of Theorem
2.3
can be written as

P_{(\mathbf{a},\alpha)}(h)\,K_{2}(h)\ \sim_{\sigma^{-1}}\ P_{(\mathbf{a},\alpha)}(h)\,K_{1}(h),

i.e., these matrices are
\sigma^{-1}
-similar.

Definition 2.7
.

For
A(h)\in\Mat_{n}(\mathbb{C}[h])
, its
\sigma^{-1}
-orbit
in
\Mat_{n}(\mathbb{C}[h])
is

\Orb_{\sigma^{-1}}(A(h)):=\big\{\,P(h)^{-1}A(h)\,P(h+1)\mid P(h)\in\GL_{n}(\mathbb{C}[h])\,\big\}.

Note that if
K(h)\in\GL_{n}(\mathbb{C}[h])
, then
\Orb_{\sigma^{-1}}(K(h))\subset\GL_{n}(\mathbb{C}[h])
.

Remark 2.8
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=\GL_{n}(\mathbb{C}[h])
, and let
\mathbb{Z}
act on
\mathcal{G}
via
\sigma^{-1}
, i.e.

(m\cdot P)(h):=P(h+m)
.
Then the elements of the (nonabelian) cohomology
H^{1}(\mathbb{Z},\mathcal{G})
are precisely the

\sigma^{-1}
-equivalence classes in
\mathcal{G}
. More precisely, we have a bijection

\mathcal{G}/\sim_{\sigma^{-1}}\;\to\;H^{1}(\mathbb{Z},\mathcal{G})
,

\Orb_{\sigma^{-1}}(K(h))\mapsto[c_{K}]
, where
c_{K}:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathcal{G}
is the
1
–cocycle defined by

c_{K}(0)=\I,\qquad c_{K}(m)=\prod_{i=0}^{m-1}K(h+i)\ (m\geq 1),\qquad c_{K}(-m)=\prod_{i=1}^{m}K(h-i)^{-1}\ (m\geq 1).

Definition 2.9
.

A rank-
n

U(\mathfrak{h})
-free module
M(\alpha,\mathbf{a},K(h))
is said to be

of scalar type
if

P_{(\mathbf{a},\alpha)}(h)=p(h)\,\I_{n}

for some monic polynomial
p(h)\in\mathbb{C}[h]
that divides
(h-\alpha+1)(h+\alpha)
.
Note that
M(\alpha,\mathbf{a},K(h))
is of scalar type if and only if

\mathbf{a}\in\{(n,0,0),(0,n,0),(0,-n,0),(0,0,n)\}.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
,
K(h)\in\GL_{2}(\mathbb{C}[h])
, and
{\bf a}\in\{(2,0,0),(0,2,0),(0,0,2)\}
(so
M\left(\alpha,\mathbf{a},K(h)\right)
is of scalar type).
Then
M\left(\alpha,\mathbf{a},K(h)\right)
has a rank-
1
submodule if and only if

K(h)\ \sim_{\sigma^{-1}}\ \begin{pmatrix}a&u(h)\\
0&b\end{pmatrix},\qquad a,b\in\mathbb{C}^{*},\ u(h)\in\mathbb{C}[h].

Proof.

Write
R=\mathbb{C}[h]
and
M=R^{\oplus 2}
. In the scalar case there exist
p,q\in R
with

p(h)q(h)=-(h-\alpha+1)(h+\alpha)
, such that, for all
v\in M
.

e\cdot v=\sigma\big(q(h)\,K(h)^{-1}v\big),\qquad f\cdot v=p(h)\,K(h)\,\sigma^{-1}(v).

For the “if part”, observe that if
K(h)=\begin{pmatrix}a&u(h)\\
0&b\end{pmatrix}
with
a,b\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
, then

Re_{1}
is stable under
e
and
f
; hence it is a rank-
1
submodule.

We next prove the “only if” part. Assume
L=Rv
is a rank-
1
submodule of
M
. Write
v=(v_{1},v_{2})^{\mathsf{T}}
and let

g(h)=\gcd(v_{1},v_{2})\in R
; choose a primitive
u\in R^{\oplus 2}
with
v=g(h)\,u
.
Since
R
is a PID, there exists
S(h)\in\GL_{2}(R)
whose first column is
u

(i.e.
S(h)e_{1}=u
). Consider the
\sigma^{-1}
-similar module
M^{\prime}
with

K^{\prime}(h)\ :=\ S(h)^{-1}K(h)S(h+1)\quad\big(\text{so }K\sim_{\sigma^{-1}}K^{\prime}\big).

Under this similarity, the submodule corresponding to
L
is

S(h)^{-1}L\;=\;R\cdot S(h)^{-1}\big(g(h)\,u\big)\;=\;R\cdot g(h)\,e_{1}.

Write
K^{\prime}(h)=\begin{pmatrix}a(h)&b(h)\\
c(h)&d(h)\end{pmatrix}
.
The
f
–stability of
R\cdot g(h)\,e_{1}
implies that for some
\xi(h)\in R
,

\xi(h)\,g(h)\,e_{1}\;=\;f\cdot\big(g(h)\,e_{1}\big)\;=\;p(h)\,K^{\prime}(h)\,\sigma^{-1}\big(g(h)\,e_{1}\big)\;=\;p(h)\,K^{\prime}(h)\,\big(g(h+1)\,e_{1}\big)

Comparing the second coordinates gives

p(h)\,c(h)\,g(h+1)\;=\;0.

Hence
c(h)=0
, and
K^{\prime}(h)
is upper triangular. Because
\det K^{\prime}=\det K\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, both diagonal
entries
a(h),d(h)
are units of
R
.
Therefore

K^{\prime}(h)=\begin{pmatrix}a&u(h)\\[2.0pt]
0&b\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{for some }u(h)\in R,

for some
a,b\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
as required.
∎

Definition 2.11
.

We define
\mathcal{S}
to be the complement in
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{C}[h])
of the union of the
\sigma^{-1}
-similarity equivalence classes of constant diagonal matrices:

\mathcal{S}:=\GL_{2}(\mathbb{C}[h])\setminus\bigcup_{a,b\in\mathbb{C}^{*}}\Orb_{\sigma^{-1}}\bigl(\diag(a,b)\bigr).

A detailed understanding of
\mathcal{S}
is essential for our main theorem.

2.4.
Standard Form for Matrices in
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{C}[h])

Throughout, we use the notation

E(u(h)):=\begin{pmatrix}u(h)&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}\quad(u(h)\in\mathbb{C}[h]).

Lemma 2.12

(
[
5
]
, Theorem 2.2)
.

For
u(h),v(h),w(h)\in\mathbb{C}[h]
and
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
, the following identities hold:

(i)

E(u(h))E(0)E(v(h))=-E(u(h)+v(h))
,

(ii)

E(\beta_{1})E\left(\beta_{1}^{-1}\right)E(\beta_{1})=-\diag\left(\beta_{1},\beta_{1}^{-1}\right)
,

(iii)

E(u(h))\diag(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})=\diag(\beta_{2},\beta_{1})E\left(\tfrac{\beta_{1}}{\beta_{2}}u(h)\right)
,

(iv)

E(u(h))E(v(h))^{-1}=E(u(h)-v(h))E(0)^{-1}=-E(u(h)-v(h))E(0)
,

(v)

E(u(h))E(v(h))^{-1}E(w(h))=E(u(h)-v(h)+w(h))
,

(vi)

E(u(h))E(\beta_{1})E(v(h))=E\left(u(h)-\beta_{1}^{-1}\right)\diag\left(\beta_{1},\beta_{1}^{-1}\right)E\left(v(h)-\beta_{1}^{-1}\right)
.

Remark 2.13
.

By Lemma
2.12
(iv) with
u(h)=0
, for every
v(h)\in\mathbb{C}[h]
,

E(v(h))^{-1}=E(0)\,E(-v(h))\,E(0).

Theorem 2.14

(
[
5
]
, Theorem 2.2)
.

Every matrix
A\in\GL_{2}(\mathbb{C}[h])
admits a factorization of the form

A=\diag(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\qquad\text{or}\qquad A=\diag(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\,\prod_{i=1}^{k}E\big(u_{i}(h)\big),

for some
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
,
k\geq 1
,
u_{i}(h)\in\mathbb{C}[h]
.

For brevity, set
\mathbf{E}_{(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k}):=\diag(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\,\prod_{i=1}^{k}E(u_{i})
. By Lemma
2.12
, the factorization of
A
from Theorem
2.14
can be shortened in finitely many steps to a reduced expression
A=\mathbf{E}_{(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})}(v_{1},\dots,u_{\ell})
, where
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
,
v_{1}(h),v_{\ell}(h)\in\mathbb{C}[h]
, and
v_{i}(h)\in\mathbb{C}[h]\setminus\mathbb{C}
for
1<i<\ell
. We call this the
standard form
of
A
.

Remark 2.15
.

If
\mathbf{E}_{(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})}(u_{1},u_{2})
is the standard form of
A\in\GL_{2}(\mathbb{C}[h])
, then
(u_{1},u_{2})\neq(0,0)
. For
m\geq n
, set

\mathop{\overleftarrow{\prod}}_{i=n}^{m}E\bigl(u_{i}(h)\bigr):=E\bigl(u_{m}(h)\bigr)\,E\bigl(u_{m-1}(h)\bigr)\cdots E\bigl(u_{n}(h)\bigr).

Theorem 2.16

(
[
5
]
, Theorem 7.1)
.

Every
A\in\GL_{2}(\mathbb{C}[h])
admits a unique standard form.

Definition 2.17
.

Let
K(h)\in\GL_{2}(\mathbb{C}[h])
. We define the
length
of
K(h)
(denoted by
\ell(K(h))
) to be the number of factors of the form
E\bigl(u_{i}(h)\bigr)
appearing in the standard form of
K(h)
. In particular, if
K(h)=\diag(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})
with
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
, then
\ell(K(h))=0
.

2.5.
Standard form via a Euclidean Algorithm on the first row

We assume
\deg 0:=-\infty
.

Proposition 2.18
.

Let

K(h)=\begin{pmatrix}k_{11}&k_{12}\\
k_{21}&k_{22}\end{pmatrix}\in\GL_{2}(\mathbb{C}[h]).

Then there exist
a,b\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
and
u(h),q_{i}(h)\in\mathbb{C}[h]
with
q_{i}\notin\mathbb{C}^{*}
for every
i>1
, such that

(2.1)

K(h)\ =\ \begin{pmatrix}a&0\\
u(h)&b\end{pmatrix}\,E\big(q_{T}\big)\cdots E\big(q_{1}\big).

Moreover,
q_{i}=(-1)^{i-1}p_{i}
, for
i=1,...,T
, where
p_{1},...,p_{T}
are the consecutive quotients obtained via the Euclidean Algorithm applied on
(k_{11},k_{12})
. In particular,
q_{1}=0
if
\deg k_{11}<\deg k_{12}
, and
T=0
if
k_{12}=0
.

Proof.

Set
K^{(1)}:=K
and write

K^{(t)}=\begin{pmatrix}k^{(t)}_{11}&k^{(t)}_{12}\\
k^{(t)}_{21}&k^{(t)}_{22}\end{pmatrix}\qquad(t\geq 1).

While
k^{(t)}_{12}\neq 0
, perform Euclidean division

k^{(t)}_{11}=q_{t}\,k^{(t)}_{12}+r_{t},\qquad\deg r_{t}<\deg k^{(t)}_{12},

and set

K^{(t+1)}:=\begin{pmatrix}k^{(t)}_{12}&-\,k^{(t)}_{11}+q_{t}\,k^{(t)}_{12}\\[2.0pt]
k^{(t)}_{22}&-\,k^{(t)}_{21}+q_{t}\,k^{(t)}_{22}\end{pmatrix}.

Then

K^{(t+1)}\,E(q_{t})=\begin{pmatrix}q_{t}k^{(t)}_{12}+r_{t}&k^{(t)}_{12}\\
k^{(t)}_{21}&k^{(t)}_{22}\end{pmatrix}=K^{(t)}.

Moreover,
k^{(t+1)}_{11}=k^{(t)}_{12}
and
k^{(t+1)}_{12}=-r_{t}
, hence

\deg k^{(t+1)}_{12}<\deg k^{(t)}_{12}
, so the process terminates after finitely many steps at some
T
with

k^{(T+1)}_{12}=0
. Thus

K=K^{(T+1)}\,E(q_{T})\cdots E(q_{1}).

Write
K^{(T+1)}=\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\
U&B\end{pmatrix}
with
A,B,U\in\mathbb{C}[h]
. Since

\det K=\det K^{(T+1)}=AB\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
, we have
A,B\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
. Setting
a:=A
,
b:=B
,
u:=U
gives the factorization.

Finally, if
\deg k_{11}<\deg k_{12}
then
q_{1}=0
by definition; if
k_{12}=0
then
T=0
and

K=\begin{pmatrix}k_{11}&0\\
k_{21}&k_{22}\end{pmatrix}
with
k_{11},k_{22}\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
.
For
i>1
, we have
\deg k^{(i)}_{11}>\deg k^{(i)}_{12}
(since for
t\geq 2
,
k^{(t)}_{11}=k^{(t-1)}_{12}
and
\deg k^{(t)}_{12}<\deg k^{(t-1)}_{12}
), hence
\deg q_{i}\geq 1
, equivalently,
q_{i}\notin\mathbb{C}^{*}
.
∎

Proposition 2.19
.

The form of
K(h)
in (
2.1
) is unique. Namely,
assume

K(h)\;=\;\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\
u&b\end{pmatrix}E(q_{T})\cdots E(q_{1})\;=\;\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{a}&0\\
\tilde{u}&\tilde{b}\end{pmatrix}E(\tilde{q}_{\tilde{T}})\cdots E(\tilde{q}_{1}),

where
a,b,\tilde{a},\tilde{b}\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
,
u,\tilde{u}\in\mathbb{C}[h]
, and
q_{i},\tilde{q}_{j}\in\mathbb{C}[h]
with

q_{i}\notin\mathbb{C}^{*}
for every
i>1
and
\tilde{q}_{j}\notin\mathbb{C}^{*}
for every
j>1
. Then
a=\tilde{a}
,
b=\tilde{b}
,
u=\tilde{u}
,
T=\tilde{T}
, and
q_{i}=\tilde{q}_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq T
.

Proof.

If
\tilde{T}=0
, then the identity
\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\
u&b\end{pmatrix}E(q_{T})\cdots E(q_{1})=\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{a}&0\\
\tilde{u}&\tilde{b}\end{pmatrix}
implies

\I_{2}=\tfrac{1}{\tilde{a}\tilde{b}}\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{b}a&0\\
-\tilde{u}a+\tilde{a}u&\tilde{a}b\end{pmatrix}E(q_{T})\cdots E(q_{1})=\tfrac{1}{\tilde{a}\tilde{b}}\diag(-\tilde{b}a,-\tilde{a}b)E(0)E(\tfrac{-\tilde{u}a+\tilde{a}u}{\tilde{a}b})E(q_{T})\cdots E(q_{1}).

Hence, if
T\geq 1
, the identity matrix
\I_{2}
would admit a standard form involving a factor
E(w)
with
w\in\mathbb{C}[h]
, which is a contradiction. Thus
T=0
, and it follows that
a=\tilde{a}
,
b=\tilde{b}
, and
u=\tilde{u}
.

It remains to consider the case where
T\geq 1
and
\tilde{T}\geq 1
. From

\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\
u&b\end{pmatrix}E(q_{T})\cdots E(q_{1})\;=\;\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{a}&0\\
\tilde{u}&\tilde{b}\end{pmatrix}E(\tilde{q}_{\tilde{T}})\cdots E(\tilde{q}_{1}),

it follows that

\displaystyle\I_{2}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\tilde{T}-1}\tfrac{1}{\tilde{a}\tilde{b}}\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{b}a&0\\
-\tilde{u}a+\tilde{a}u&\tilde{a}b\end{pmatrix}E(q_{T})\cdots E(q_{1})E(0)E(-\tilde{q}_{1})\cdots E(-\tilde{q}_{\tilde{T}})E(0)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\tilde{T}}\tfrac{1}{\tilde{a}\tilde{b}}\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{b}a&0\\
-\tilde{u}a+\tilde{a}u&\tilde{a}b\end{pmatrix}E(q_{T})\cdots E(q_{2})E(q_{1}-\tilde{q}_{1})E(-\tilde{q}_{2})\cdots E(-\tilde{q}_{\tilde{T}})E(0).

If
q_{1}\neq\tilde{q}_{1}
, then either
q_{1}-\tilde{q}_{1}\in\mathbb{C}[h]\setminus\mathbb{C}
or
q_{1}-\tilde{q}_{1}\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
. If
\tilde{T}\geq 2
, then
E(-\tilde{q}_{\tilde{T}})
appears in the standard form of
\I_{2}
, which contradicts Theorem
2.16
. If
T\geq 2
, then either
E(q_{1}-\tilde{q}_{1})
appears in the standard form of
\I_{2}
(i.e. when
q_{1}-\tilde{q}_{1}\in\mathbb{C}[h]\setminus\mathbb{C}
), or, in the case
q_{1}-\tilde{q}_{1}\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
, we may apply Lemma
2.12
parts (iii) and (vi) to bring the product into standard form, yielding a contradiction. If
T=\tilde{T}=1
, then

\begin{pmatrix}aq_{1}&a\\
uq_{1}-b&u\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{a}\tilde{q}_{1}&\tilde{a}\\
\tilde{u}\tilde{q}_{1}-\tilde{b}&\tilde{u}\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and hence}\quad q_{1}=\tilde{q}_{1}.

Therefore,
q_{1}=\tilde{q}_{1}
. Assume
T>\tilde{T}
and apply the above argument inductively, we obtain
q_{i}=\tilde{q}_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq\min\{T,\tilde{T}\}=\tilde{T}
. Hence

\I_{2}=\tfrac{1}{\tilde{a}\tilde{b}}\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{b}a&0\\
-\tilde{u}a+\tilde{a}u&\tilde{a}b\end{pmatrix}E(q_{T})\cdots E(q_{T-\tilde{T}})=\tfrac{1}{\tilde{a}\tilde{b}}\diag(-\tilde{b}a,-\tilde{a}b)E(0)E(\tfrac{-\tilde{u}a+\tilde{a}u}{\tilde{a}b})E(q_{T})\cdots E(q_{T-\tilde{T}}).

Using the same argument as in the case
\tilde{T}=0
, we obtain a contradiction. Thus
T=\tilde{T}
and
q_{i}=\tilde{q}_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq T
. Consequently,

\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\
u&b\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{a}&0\\
\tilde{u}&\tilde{b}\end{pmatrix},

which proves the lemma.
∎

We will call the decomposition (
2.1
) the
LQ form of
K(h)
.

Remark 2.20

(Transition from LQ form to Standard Form)
.

By Proposition
2.18
,

K(h)\;=\;\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\
u(h)&b\end{pmatrix}\,E\big(q_{T}\big)\cdots E\big(q_{1}\big),\qquad a,b\in\mathbb{C}^{*},\ u\in\mathbb{C}[h],

with
q_{i}\notin\mathbb{C}^{*}
for
i>1
. Rewrite the lower–triangular front factor as

\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\
u(h)&b\end{pmatrix}=\diag(-a,-b)\,E(0)\,E\Big(\tfrac{u(h)}{b}\Big).

If
T=0
and
u\neq 0
, the above is the normal form of
K(h)=\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\
u(h)&b\end{pmatrix}
. In this case
\ell(K(h))=2
.

If
u=0
, then the standard form of
K(h)
is
\mathbf{E}_{(a,b)}(q_{T},q_{T-1},\dots,q_{1})
, and hence
\ell(K(h))=T
.

If
T\geq 1
and
\frac{u(h)}{b}\notin\mathbb{C}
, then, since the product of
E(q_{i})
has no interior constants
q_{i}
, the resulting product is already in the desired standard form. In this case
\ell(K(h))=T+2
.

If
T\geq 1
and
\frac{u(h)}{b}=\beta\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
, by Lemma
2.12
we have

\displaystyle E(0)\,E\left(\beta\right)E\big(q_{T}(h)\big)

\displaystyle=E\left(-\beta^{-1}\right)\diag\left(\beta,\beta^{-1}\right)E\big(q_{T}(h)-\beta^{-1}\big)

\displaystyle=\diag\left(\beta^{-1},\beta\right)E(-\beta)E\big(q_{T}(h)-\beta^{-1}\big).

This combined with
E\big(q_{T-1}\big)\cdots E\big(q_{1}\big)
equals the normal form of
K(h)
, and in this case
\ell(K(h))=T+1
.

.

3.
The Set
\mathcal{S}

This section provides an alternative characterization of
\mathcal{S}
from Definition
2.11
, a key parameter in the classification of simple rank-2 scalar-type modules (Corollary
3.4
). In particular, we give an explicit formula for matrices in
\mathcal{S}
, up to
\sigma^{-1}
-similarity. We begin with a standard lemma that will be used in several places.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
. Define

T_{a,b}:\mathbb{C}[h]\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}[h],\qquad T_{a,b}\bigl(w(h)\bigr)=a\,w(h+1)-b\,w(h).

Then
T_{a,b}
is a surjective linear map.

Proof.

Since
T_{a,b}=aT_{1,b/a}
, it is enough to show that
S_{c}:=T_{1,c}
is surjective for every

c\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
. Consider the basis of
\mathbb{C}[h]
consisting of the binomial polynomials

e_{k}(h):=\binom{h}{k}\qquad(k\geq 0).

Using the identity
\binom{h+1}{k}=\binom{h}{k}+\binom{h}{k-1}
, we
obtain

S_{c}(e_{k})(h)=e_{k}(h+1)-c\,e_{k}(h)=\bigl(1-c\bigr)e_{k}(h)+e_{k-1}(h),

with the convention
e_{-1}:=0
.

Case 1:
c\neq 1
.

In the basis
\{e_{0},e_{1},e_{2},\dots\}
the operator
S_{c}
has an
upper-triangular matrix with diagonal entries
1-c\neq 0
. Hence
S_{c}

is invertible, in particular surjective.

Case 2:
c=1
.

Then
S_{1}(e_{k})=e_{k-1}
for all
k\geq 0
. Thus
S_{1}
is the (right)
shift operator on the binomial basis:

S_{1}(e_{0})=0,\quad S_{1}(e_{1})=e_{0},\quad S_{1}(e_{2})=e_{1},\ \dots

This easily implies the surjectivity of
S_{1}
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
and
u(h)\in\mathbb{C}[h]
. Then

\begin{pmatrix}a&u(h)\\
0&b\end{pmatrix}\in\Orb_{\sigma^{-1}}\big(\diag(a,b)\big).

Proof.

For
v(h)\in\mathbb{C}[h]
, set
P(h):=\begin{pmatrix}1&v(h)\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\in\GL_{2}(\mathbb{C}[h])
.
Then

P(h)^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}a&u(h)\\
0&b\end{pmatrix}P(h+1)=\begin{pmatrix}a&a\,v(h+1)-b\,v(h)+u(h)\\
0&b\end{pmatrix}.

We use Lemma
3.1
, choose
v(h)
such that
T_{a,b}(v)=-u
. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Any matrix in
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
(i.e. with constant entries) is
\sigma^{-1}
-similar to a diagonal matrix.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
,
K(h)\in\GL_{2}(\mathbb{C}[h])
, and

{\bf a}\in\{(2,0,0),(0,2,0),(0,0,2)\}
.
Then
M\left(\alpha,\mathbf{a},K(h)\right)
has no rank-
1
submodules
precisely when
K(h)\in\mathcal{S}
.

Proof.

This is an immediate consequence of Lemma
2.10
together with Lemma
3.2
.
∎

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
, and
u(h)\in\mathbb{C}[h]
. Then:

(i)

M\left(\alpha,(2,0,0),\begin{pmatrix}a&u(h)\\
0&b\end{pmatrix}\right)\simeq M(\alpha,(1,0,0),a)\;\oplus\;M(\alpha,(1,0,0),b),

(ii)

M\left(\alpha,(0,2,0),\begin{pmatrix}a&u(h)\\
0&b\end{pmatrix}\right)\simeq M(\alpha,(0,1,0),a)\;\oplus\;M(\alpha,(0,1,0),b),

(iii)

M\left(\alpha,(0,0,2),\begin{pmatrix}a&u(h)\\
0&b\end{pmatrix}\right)\simeq M(\alpha,(0,0,1),a)\;\oplus\;M(\alpha,(0,0,1),b).

Proof.

We prove (i); parts (ii)–(iii) are identical.
By Lemma
3.2
,

\begin{pmatrix}a&u(h)\\
0&b\end{pmatrix}\sim_{\sigma^{-1}}\diag(a,b).

Hence, by Theorem
2.3
,

M\left(\alpha,(2,0,0),\begin{pmatrix}a&u(h)\\
0&b\end{pmatrix}\right)\simeq M\left(\alpha,(2,0,0),\diag(a,b)\right).

Finally,

M\left(\alpha,(2,0,0),\diag(a,b)\right)\simeq M(\alpha,(1,0,0),a)\;\oplus\;M(\alpha,(1,0,0),b),

which proves (i).
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

For
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, let
u_{1},\dots,u_{k}\in\mathbb{C}[h]\setminus\mathbb{C}
. Let

P(h)=\mathbf{E}_{(1,1)}(v_{1},\dots,v_{p})

be in standard form, where
v_{i}\in\mathbb{C}[h]
for all
i
,
v_{i}\notin\mathbb{C}
for
1<i<p
, and
p\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
. Then the following hold.

(i)

If
v_{1}\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
, then

\ell\Big(P(h)^{-1}\,\mathbf{E}_{(1,1)}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})\,P(h+1)\Big)=\begin{cases}k+2&\text{if}\quad p=1,\\
k+2p&\text{if}\quad p>1,\,v_{p}\in\mathbb{C}[h]\setminus\mathbb{C},\\
k+2p-1&\text{if}\quad p>1,\,v_{p}\in\mathbb{C}^{*},\\
k+2p-2&\text{if}\quad p>1,\,v_{p}=0.\end{cases}

(ii)

If
v_{1}\in\mathbb{C}[h]\setminus\mathbb{C}
, and
u_{1}-v_{1}\in\mathbb{C}[h]\setminus\mathbb{C}
, then

\ell\Big(P(h)^{-1}\,\mathbf{E}_{(1,1)}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})\,P(h+1)\Big)=\begin{cases}k+2p&\text{if}\quad p\geq 1,\,v_{p}\in\mathbb{C}[h]\setminus\mathbb{C},\\
k+2p-1&\text{if}\quad p>1,\,v_{p}\in\mathbb{C}^{*},\\
k+2p-2&\text{if}\quad p>1,\,v_{p}=0.\end{cases}

(iii)

If
v_{1}\in\mathbb{C}[h]\setminus\mathbb{C}
and
u_{1}-v_{1}\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
, then

\ell\Big(P(h)^{-1}\,\mathbf{E}_{(1,1)}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})\,P(h+1)\Big)=\begin{cases}k+2p-1&\text{if}\quad p\geq 1,\,v_{p}\in\mathbb{C}[h]\setminus\mathbb{C},\\
k+1,&\text{if}\quad p=2,\,v_{2}=(v_{1}-u_{1})^{-1},\\
k+2,&\text{if}\quad p=2,\,v_{2}\in\mathbb{C},v_{2}\neq(v_{1}-u_{1})^{-1},\\
k+2p-2&\text{if}\quad p>2,\,v_{p}\in\mathbb{C}^{*},\\
k+2p-3&\text{if}\quad p>2,\,v_{p}=0.\end{cases}

Proof.

We prove (i); parts (ii)–(iii) are similar. If
p=1
, then

P(h)^{-1}\,\mathbf{E}_{(1,1)}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})\,P(h+1)=-\,E(0)\,E(u_{1}-v_{1})\,E(u_{2})\cdots E(u_{k})\,E(v_{1}(h+1)).

Hence, when
p=1
,

\ell\Big(P(h)^{-1}\,\mathbf{E}_{(1,1)}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})\,P(h+1)\Big)=k+2.

Now assume
p>1
. A direct computation gives

P(h)^{-1}\,\mathbf{E}_{(1,1)}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})\,P(h+1)=\\
(-1)^{p}\,E(0)\left(\mathop{\overleftarrow{\prod}}_{j=2}^{\,p}E(-v_{j})\right)\,E(u_{1}-v_{1})\,E(u_{2})\cdots E(u_{k})\,\left(\prod_{m=1}^{\,p}E(v_{m}(h+1))\right).

If
v_{p}\in\mathbb{C}[h]\setminus\mathbb{C}
, then
\ell\Big(P(h)^{-1}\,\mathbf{E}_{(1,1)}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})\,P(h+1)\Big)=k+2p
. If
v_{p}\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
, then by Lemma
2.12
parts (iii) and (vi), we deduce that

\ell\Big(P(h)^{-1}\,\mathbf{E}_{(1,1)}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})\,P(h+1)\Big)=k+2p-1.

If
v_{p}=0
, then

P(h)^{-1}\,\mathbf{E}_{(1,1)}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})\,P(h+1)=\\
(-1)^{p-1}\,\left(\mathop{\overleftarrow{\prod}}_{j=2}^{\,p-1}E(-v_{j})\right)\,E(u_{1}-v_{1})\,E(u_{2})\cdots E(u_{k})\,\left(\prod_{m=1}^{\,p-1}E(v_{m}(h+1))\right)E(0),

which implies

\ell\Big(P(h)^{-1}\,\mathbf{E}_{(1,1)}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})\,P(h+1)\Big)=k+2p-2.

This completes the proof of part (i) of the lemma.
∎

For
u_{1},\dots,u_{k}\in\mathbb{C}[h]\setminus\mathbb{C}
, the length of
\mathbf{E}_{(a,b)}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})
cannot be reduced via
\sigma^{-1}
-similarity; this will be proved in the next proposition.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
and
u_{1},\dots,u_{k}\in\mathbb{C}[h]\setminus\mathbb{C}
with
k\geq 1
.
Then for every
P(h)\in\GL_{2}(\mathbb{C}[h])
,

\ell\Big(P(h)^{-1}\,\mathbf{E}_{(a,b)}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})\,P(h+1)\Big)\;\geq\;\ell\Big(\mathbf{E}_{(a,b)}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})\Big).

Proof.

By Lemma
2.12
(iii), the diagonal factor in the standard form of a matrix in
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{C}[h])
does not affect the length under
\sigma^{-1}
-similarity. Hence, for computational convenience, it suffices to prove the proposition in the case
a=b=1
. Let
P(h)\in\GL_{2}(\mathbb{C}[h])
of standard form

P(h)=\mathbf{E}_{(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})}(v_{1},\dots,v_{p}),\qquad\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in\mathbb{C}^{*},

with
v_{1},v_{p}\in\mathbb{C}[h]
and
v_{i}\in\mathbb{C}[h]\setminus\mathbb{C}
for
1<i<p
. Since conjugation by a constant diagonal matrix preserves length (Lemma
2.12
(iii)), we also assume
\beta_{1}=\beta_{2}=1
. By Lemma
3.6
, it remains to consider the cases
v_{1}=0
and
v_{1}=u_{1}
.
If
v_{1}=0
, then

E(v_{1})^{-1}\,\mathbf{E}_{(1,1)}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})\,E\bigl(v_{1}(h+1)\bigr)=-\,E(0)\,E(u_{1})\,E(u_{2})\cdots E(u_{k})\,E(0),

and

E(v_{2})^{-1}E(v_{1})^{-1}\,\mathbf{E}_{(1,1)}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})\,E\bigl(v_{1}(h+1)\bigr)\,E\bigl(v_{2}(h+1)\bigr)\\
=-\,E(0)\,E(-v_{2})\,E(u_{1})\,E(u_{2})\cdots E(u_{k-1})\,E(u_{k}+v_{2}(h+1)).

If
v_{1}=0
and
p\leq 2
, a direct computation shows that

\ell\Big(P(h)^{-1}\,\mathbf{E}_{(1,1)}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})\,P(h+1)\Big)\geq k+1.

If
p\geq 3
, then

P(h)^{-1}\,\mathbf{E}_{(1,1)}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})\,P(h+1)=\\
(-1)^{p+1}\,E(0)\left(\mathop{\overleftarrow{\prod}}_{j=2}^{\,p}E(-v_{j})\right)\,E(u_{1})\cdots E(u_{k-1})\,E(u_{k}+v_{2}(h+1))\left(\prod_{m=3}^{\,p}E(v_{m}(h+1))\right).

To reach the minimal possible length in the case
p\geq 3
, the interior factors of
P(h)
must cancel successively with the tail of
\mathbf{E}_{(1,1)}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})
; that is,

v_{1}(h)=0,\quad v_{2}(h)=-u_{k}(h-1),\quad v_{3}(h)=-u_{k-1}(h-1),\quad\dots,v_{p}(h)=0.

In explicit terms,
v_{1}(h)=v_{p}(h)=0
, and for
2\leq n\leq p-1
, writing
n=ks+r+2
with
s\geq 0
and
r\in\{0,\dots,k-1\}
, we set

v_{ks+r+2}(h):=-u_{k-r}(h-(s+1)).

The resulting product has length
k
.

If
v_{1}=u_{1}
, then

E(v_{1})^{-1}\,\mathbf{E}_{(1,1)}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})\,E\bigl(v_{1}(h+1)\bigr)=E(u_{2})\cdots E(u_{k})E(u_{1}(h+1)).

To obtain the minimal length, repeat this process at each stage (i.e., take
v_{2}(h)=u_{2}(h)
,
\dots
). More precisely, for
s\geq 0
and
j\in\{1,\dots,k\}
, set
v_{ks+j}(h):=u_{j}(h+s)
. The resulting product has length
k
.
∎

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, and let
u_{1},\dots,u_{k}\in\mathbb{C}[h]\setminus\mathbb{C}
. Then

\mathbf{E}_{(a,b)}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})\in\mathcal{S}.

Proof.

Let
K(h)=\mathbf{E}_{(a,b)}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})
. By Proposition
3.7
, every matrix
\sigma^{-1}
-similar to
K(h)
has length at least
k
, so
K(h)
cannot be
\sigma^{-1}
-similar to a diagonal matrix (which has length
0
). Hence
K(h)\in\mathcal{S}
.
∎

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
a,b,\beta\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
,
\theta\in\mathbb{C}
, and
u(h)\in\mathbb{C}[h]\setminus\mathbb{C}
. Then

(i)\;\mathbf{E}_{(a,b)}(\theta)\notin\mathcal{S},\qquad(ii)\;\mathbf{E}_{(a,b)}(0,u(h))\notin\mathcal{S},\qquad(iii)\;\mathbf{E}_{(a,b)}(\beta,u(h))\in\mathcal{S}.

Proof.

Since
\mathbf{E}_{(a,b)}(\theta)\in\GL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, Lemma
3.2
and Remark
3.3
yield (i). To prove (ii), we apply Lemma
3.1
and choose
w(h)\in\mathbb{C}[h]\setminus\mathbb{C}
such that
u(h)=-\frac{a}{b}w(h)+w(h+1)
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{(a,b)}(0,u(h))

\displaystyle=\diag(a,b)E\left(0\right)E\left(-\tfrac{a}{b}w(h)+w(h+1)\right)

\displaystyle=\diag(-a,-b)E(0)E\left(-\tfrac{a}{b}w(h)\right)E(0)E(w(h+1))

\displaystyle=E(0)E(-w(h))E(0)\diag(-b,-a)E(w(h+1))

\displaystyle=E(w(h))^{-1}\diag(-b,-a)E(w(h+1))\sim_{\sigma^{-1}}\diag(-b,-a).

To prove (iii), we first define
P(h):=E(0)E\left(-u(h-1)+\beta^{-1}\right)E(0)
. Then

P^{-1}(h)\mathbf{E}_{(a,b)}(\beta,u(h))P(h+1)=\diag\left(b\beta^{-1},a\beta\right)E\left(u(h-1)\tfrac{a\beta^{2}}{b}-\tfrac{a\beta}{b}-\beta\right).

Then by Corollary
3.8
, (iii) follows.
∎

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
K(h)\in\GL_{2}(\mathbb{C}[h])
. Then
K(h)\in\mathcal{S}
if and only if
K(h)
is

\sigma^{-1}
-similar to a matrix of the form

\mathbf{E}_{(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})}(v_{1},\dots,v_{m}),\quad\text{where}\qquad\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in\mathbb{C}^{*},\;m\geq 1,\;v_{i}(h)\in\mathbb{C}[h]\setminus\mathbb{C}.

Proof.

The “if” part is immediate from Corollary
3.8
. For the “only if” part, assume
K(h)\in\mathcal{S}
. We first observe that for any
\beta\in\mathbb{C}
,
a,b\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
, and

\diag(a,b)\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}E(u_{i}(h))\right)E(\beta)\sim_{\sigma^{-1}}\diag(b,a)E\left(\tfrac{a\beta}{b}\right)\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}E(u_{i}(h))\right).

If
u_{1}(h)=\eta\in\mathbb{C}
and
k\geq 2
then

\displaystyle\quad\diag(a,b)E(\eta)\left(\prod_{i=2}^{k}E(u_{i}(h))\right)E(\beta)

\displaystyle\sim_{\sigma^{-1}}\diag(b,a)E\left(\tfrac{a\beta}{b}\right)E(\eta)\left(\prod_{i=2}^{k}E(u_{i}(h))\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\diag\left(b\eta^{-1},a\eta\right)E\left(\frac{a\beta\eta^{2}}{b}-\eta\right)E\left(u_{2}(h)-\eta^{-1}\right)E(u_{3}(h))\cdots E(u_{k}(h)),&\text{if }\eta\neq 0,\\
\diag(-b,-a)E\left(u_{2}(h)+\tfrac{a\beta}{b}\right)E(u_{3}(h))\cdots E(u_{k}(h)),&\text{if }\eta=0.\end{cases}

Thus it suffices to treat the case

K(h)=\diag(a,b)\,E(\omega)\,\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}E(u_{i}(h))\right),\qquad u_{1}(h),\ldots,u_{k}(h)\in\mathbb{C}[h]\setminus\mathbb{C},\,\omega\in\mathbb{C}.

From Lemma
3.9
(i), it follows that
k\geq 1
.
If
k=1
, then Lemma
3.9
(ii) forces
\omega\neq 0
, and the proof of Lemma
3.9
(iii) yields the claim. If
k\geq 2

\displaystyle K(h)

\displaystyle\sim_{\sigma^{-1}}E(u_{k}(h-1))\diag(a,b)E(\omega)\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}E(u_{i}(h))\right)

\displaystyle=\diag(b,a)E\left(u_{k}(h-1)\frac{a}{b}\right)E(\omega)\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}E(u_{i}(h))\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\diag\left(b\omega^{-1},a\omega\right)E\left(\frac{a\omega^{2}}{b}u_{k}(h-1)-\omega\right)E\left(u_{1}(h)-\omega^{-1}\right)\\
\hskip 227.62204pt\cdot E(u_{2}(h))\cdots E(u_{k-1}(h)),&\text{if }\omega\neq 0,\\
\diag(-b,-a)E\left(\frac{a}{b}u_{k}(h-1)+u_{1}(h)\right)E(u_{2}(h))\cdots E(u_{k-1}(h)),&\text{if }\omega=0.\end{cases}

If
\omega=0
and
\frac{a}{b}u_{k}(h-1)+u_{1}(h)\in\mathbb{C}
, we iterate the above length-reduction procedure and deduce the claim.
∎

4.
A family of
U(\mathfrak{h})
-free modules of rank 2

In this section, we classify (up to isomorphism) all simple scalar-type modules of rank 2; that is, all modules of the form

M\left(\alpha,\mathbf{a},K(h)\right)\quad\text{where}\;\;\mathbf{a}\in\{(2,0,0),(0,2,0),(0,0,2)\},\ \alpha\in\mathbb{C},\ K(h)\in\GL_{2}(\mathbb{C}[h]).

4.1.
Simplicity criteria

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\alpha\in 1+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. Then
u_{\alpha}(h):=\prod_{j=0}^{2\alpha-2}(h+\alpha-1-j)
is the unique monic solution of the equation

(4.1)

(h+\alpha-1)\,q(h-1)=(h-\alpha)\,q(h).

Furthermore,
V=\left(u_{\alpha}(h)\mathbb{C}[h]\right)^{\oplus 2}
is a proper rank-
2
submodule of

M\left(\alpha,(0,2,0),K(h)\right)
.

Proof.

The fact that
q=u_{\alpha}
is the only monic solution of (
4.1
) can be easily proven after substituting
h
successively with
\alpha,\alpha-1,...,2-\alpha
to obtain
q(\alpha-1)=\cdots=q(1-\alpha)=0
. Then, for any
g(h)\in\mathbb{C}[h]
,

(h+\alpha-1)\sigma\left(u_{\alpha}(h)g(h)\right)=u_{\alpha}(h)(h-\alpha)\sigma(g(h)),

(h-\alpha+1)\sigma^{-1}\left(u_{\alpha}(h)g(h)\right)=u_{\alpha}(h)(h+\alpha)\sigma^{-1}(g(h)).

Then for
g_{1},g_{2}\in\mathbb{C}[h]
the
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
-action on
V
is:

(4.2)

\displaystyle e\cdot\begin{pmatrix}u_{\alpha}(h)g_{1}\\
u_{\alpha}(h)g_{2}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=u_{\alpha}(h)\sigma\left(K^{-1}(h)\begin{pmatrix}-(h-\alpha+1)&0\\
0&-(h-\alpha+1)\end{pmatrix}\right)\begin{pmatrix}\sigma(g_{1})\\
\sigma(g_{2})\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle f\cdot\begin{pmatrix}u_{\alpha}(h)g_{1}\\
u_{\alpha}(h)g_{2}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=u_{\alpha}(h)\begin{pmatrix}h+\alpha&0\\
0&h+\alpha\end{pmatrix}K(h)\begin{pmatrix}\sigma^{-1}(g_{1})\\
\sigma^{-1}(g_{2})\end{pmatrix}.

This proves the lemma.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
K(h)\in\mathcal{S}
. Then, for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
,

M\bigl(\alpha,(2,0,0),K(h)\bigr)\quad\text{and}\quad M\bigl(\alpha,(0,0,2),K(h)\bigr)

have no proper rank-
2
submodules. The same conclusion holds for

M\bigl(P_{((0,2,0),\alpha)}(h),K(h)\bigr)
whenever
\alpha\notin 1+\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{a}\in\{(2,0,0),(0,2,0),(0,0,2)\}
, and suppose
M\subset M\left(\alpha,\mathbf{a},K(h)\right)
is a proper submodule of rank
2
. Then
M
has central character
(2\alpha-1)^{2}
. Consequently, there exist

K^{\prime}(h)\in\GL_{2}(\mathbb{C}[h])
and
\beta\in\{\alpha,\,1-\alpha\}
such that

M\;\simeq\;M\bigl(\beta,\mathbf{b},K^{\prime}(h)\bigr),

where

\mathbf{b}\in\{(2,0,0),(0,2,0),(0,0,2),(1,1,0),(1,0,1),(0,1,1)\}.

For brevity, write
P_{(\mathbf{b},\beta)}(h)=\diag\bigl(\mu_{1}(h),\mu_{2}(h)\bigr)
, where
\mu_{1}(h),\mu_{2}(h)
are monic polynomials determined by
\mathbf{b}
as in Definition
2.1
. The inclusion
M\subset M\left(\alpha,\mathbf{a},K(h)\right)
gives rise to a
U(\mathfrak{sl}(2))
-homomorphism
\Phi:M\bigl(\beta,\mathbf{b},K^{\prime}(h)\bigr)\to M\left(\alpha,\mathbf{a},K(h)\right)
. Then there exists
A(h)\in\Mat_{2}(\mathbb{C}[h])
with
\det A(h)\neq 0
such that, for all
g_{1}(h),g_{2}(h)\in\mathbb{C}[h]
,

\Phi\begin{pmatrix}g_{1}(h)\\
g_{2}(h)\end{pmatrix}=A(h)\,\begin{pmatrix}g_{1}(h)\\
g_{2}(h)\end{pmatrix}.

The
f
–action yields the intertwining identity:

P_{(\mathbf{a},\alpha)}(h)K(h)A(h+1)=A(h)P_{(\mathbf{b},\beta)}(h)K^{\prime}(h),

and taking determinants gives

(4.3)

\det(P_{(\mathbf{a},\alpha)}(h))\det(K(h))\det(A(h+1))=\det(A(h))\det(P_{(\mathbf{b},\beta)}(h))\det(K^{\prime}(h)).

Case 1:
\mathbf{a}=(2,0,0)
.

Equation (
4.3
) becomes

\det K(h)\,\det A(h+1)=\det A(h)\,\mu_{1}(h)\mu_{2}(h)\,\det K^{\prime}(h).

Since
\det K(h),\det K^{\prime}(h)\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
and
\det A(h)\neq 0
,
\,\,\det A(h+1)=\mu_{1}(h)\mu_{2}(h)\,\det A(h).

With
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
monic, degree comparison forces
\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}=1
, hence

\det A(h)\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
. Therefore,
\Phi
is an isomorphism, contradicting that
M
is proper.

Case 2:
\mathbf{a}=(0,0,2)
.
Equation (
4.3
) becomes

\bigl((h-\alpha+1)(h+\alpha)\bigr)^{2}\det K(h)\,\det A(h{+}1)=\det A(h)\,\mu_{1}(h)\mu_{2}(h)\,\det K^{\prime}(h).

Since
\det K(h),\det K^{\prime}(h)\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
and
\det A(h)\neq 0
,

\bigl((h-\alpha+1)(h+\alpha)\bigr)^{2}\det A(h{+}1)=\mu_{1}(h)\mu_{2}(h)\,\det A(h).

With
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
monic, degree and leading–term comparison yield

\mu_{1}(h)=\mu_{2}(h)=(h-\alpha+1)(h+\alpha)
and hence

\det A(h)\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
. Therefore
\Phi
is an isomorphism, contradicting that
M
is proper.

Case 3:
\mathbf{a}=(0,2,0)
.
Equation (
4.3
) becomes

(h-\alpha+1)^{2}\,\det K(h)\,\det A(h+1)=\det A(h)\,\mu_{1}(h)\mu_{2}(h)\,\det K^{\prime}(h),

and since
\det K(h),\det K^{\prime}(h)\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
and
\det A(h)\neq 0
,

(h-\alpha+1)^{2}\,\det A(h+1)=\mu_{1}(h)\mu_{2}(h)\,\det A(h).

It follows that
\det A(h)
is a product of consecutive linear factors. In particular, there exist
a\in\mathbb{C},\ k,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
such that

\det A(h)=\prod_{j=0}^{k}\bigl(h-a+j\bigr)\quad\text{or}\quad\det A(h)=\prod_{j=0}^{k}\bigl(h-a+j\bigr)\,\prod_{j=0}^{\ell}\bigl(h-a+j\bigr).

Assume
\det A(h)=\prod_{j=0}^{k}(h-a+j)
. Then

(h-\alpha+1)^{2}\Bigl(\prod_{j=1}^{k+1}(h-a+j)\Bigr)=\Bigl(\prod_{j=0}^{k}(h-a+j)\Bigr)\mu_{1}(h)\mu_{2}(h),

so after canceling
\prod_{j=1}^{k}(h-a+j)
, we get

(h-\alpha+1)^{2}\,(h-a+k+1)=(h-a)\,\mu_{1}(h)\mu_{2}(h).

Hence
a=\alpha-1
and
\mu_{1}(h)\mu_{2}(h)=(h-\alpha+k+2)(h-\alpha+1).

Using
\mu_{1}\mid\mu_{2}\mid(h+\alpha)(h-\alpha+1)
and that
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
are monic, the possibilities are

\mu_{1}(h)=1,\quad\mu_{2}(h)=(h-\alpha+k+2)(h-\alpha+1),\quad\text{or}\quad\mu_{1}(h)=h-\alpha+k+2,\quad\mu_{2}(h)=h-\alpha+1.

If
\mu_{1}(h)=h-\alpha+k+2
and
\mu_{2}(h)=h-\alpha+1
, then the condition
\mu_{1}\mid\mu_{2}
forces

h-\alpha+k+2=h-\alpha+1
, hence
k+2=1
, a contradiction since
k\geq 0
. If
\mu_{1}(h)=1
and
\mu_{2}(h)=(h-\alpha+k+2)(h-\alpha+1)
, the divisibility

\mu_{2}\mid(h+\alpha)(h-\alpha+1)
implies
h-\alpha+k+2\mid h+\alpha
, hence

h-\alpha+k+2=h+\alpha
and so

\alpha=\tfrac{k+2}{2}\in 1+\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.

Assume
A(h)=\prod_{j=0}^{k}(h-a+j)\prod_{j=0}^{\ell}(h-a+j)
. Then

(h-\alpha+1)^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{k+1}(h-a+j)\prod_{j=1}^{\ell+1}(h-a+j)=\prod_{j=0}^{k}(h-a+j)\prod_{j=0}^{\ell}(h-a+j)\mu_{1}(h)\mu_{2}(h),

and canceling
\prod_{j=1}^{k}(h-a+j)\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}(h-a+j)
yields

(h-\alpha+1)^{2}\,(h-a+k+1)(h-a+\ell+1)=(h-a)^{2}\,\mu_{1}(h)\mu_{2}(h).

Hence
a=\alpha-1
and
\mu_{1}(h)\mu_{2}(h)=(h-\alpha+k+2)(h-\alpha+\ell+2).

Using
\mu_{1}\mid\mu_{2}\mid(h+\alpha)(h-\alpha+1)
and that
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
are monic, we have two possibilities:

\mu_{1}(h)=1,\qquad\mu_{2}(h)=(h-\alpha+k+2)(h-\alpha+\ell+2),

or

\mu_{1}(h)=h-\alpha+k+2,\qquad\mu_{2}(h)=h-\alpha+\ell+2.

If
\mu_{1}(h)=1
and
\mu_{2}(h)=(h-\alpha+k+2)(h-\alpha+\ell+2)
, then

\mu_{2}(h)=(h-\alpha+k+2)(h-\alpha+\ell+2)=(h+\alpha)(h-\alpha+1).

Thus
\{k+2,\ell+2\}=\{2\alpha,\,1\}
. Since
k,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, this would force

\ell+2=1
(or
k+2=1
), i.e.
\ell=-1
(or
k=-1
), a contradiction.

If
\mu_{1}(h)=\mu_{2}(h)=h-\alpha+k+2=h-\alpha+\ell+2
, then
k=\ell
and the
divisibility
\mu_{2}\mid(h+\alpha)(h-\alpha+1)
implies

h-\alpha+k+2=h+\alpha
, and

\alpha=\tfrac{k+2}{2}\in 1+\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.

It remains to consider the case
\mathbf{a}=(0,2,0)
with
\alpha\in 1+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. By Lemma
4.1
, the module

M\left(\alpha,(0,2,0),K(h)\right)
contains a nonzero proper submodule of rank
2
. This completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
{\bf{a}}\in\{(2,0,0),(0,2,0),(0,0,2)\}
. The module
M\left(\alpha,\mathbf{a},K(h)\right)
is simple if and only if one of the following holds:

(1)

K(h)\in\mathcal{S}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
(arbitrary), if
{\bf{a}}=(2,0,0)
or
{\bf{a}}=(0,0,2)
;

(2)

K(h)\in\mathcal{S}
and

, if
{\bf{a}}=(0,2,0)
.

Proof.

Since
\mathbb{C}[h]
is a PID, every submodule of
M\in\mathcal{M}(2)
is a
U(\mathfrak{h})
-free module of rank at most
2
. The theorem follows by combining Proposition
4.2
and Theorem
3.10
.
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

For
\alpha\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}+1
, and
K(h)\in\mathcal{S}
, we have the following non-split short exact sequence:

0\longrightarrow M\left(\alpha,(0,-2,0),K(h)\right)\longrightarrow M\left(\alpha,(0,2,0),K(h)\right)\longrightarrow L(2\alpha-2)^{\oplus 2}\longrightarrow 0

where
L(2\alpha-2)
is the simple
(\mathbb{C}h\oplus\mathbb{C}e)
-highest weight module of weight
2\alpha-2
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.1
, the subspace
V=\left(u_{\alpha}(h)\mathbb{C}[h]\right)^{\oplus 2}
, where
u_{\alpha}(h)=\prod_{j=0}^{2\alpha-2}(h+\alpha-1-j)
is a proper submodule of
M\left(\alpha,(0,2,0),K(h)\right)
. Moreover, (
4.2
) shows

V\simeq M\left(1-\alpha,(0,2,0),K(h)\right)=M\left(\alpha,(0,-2,0),K(h)\right).

By Theorem
4.3
,
M\left(1-\alpha,(0,2,0),K(h)\right)
is simple and hence, the submodule
V
is simple. Consider the quotient
Q:=M\left(\alpha,(0,2,0),K(h)\right)\big/V.

Let

v_{1}\;:=\;\begin{pmatrix}\prod_{j=0}^{2\alpha-3}\bigl(h+\alpha-1-j\bigr)\\
0\end{pmatrix}\;+\;V,\quad v_{2}\;:=\;\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\prod_{j=0}^{2\alpha-3}\bigl(h+\alpha-1-j\bigr)\end{pmatrix}\;+\;V.

A direct computation shows

e\cdot v_{i}=0,\quad\text{and}\quad 2h\cdot v_{i}=2(\alpha-1)v_{i}\quad\text{for}\quad i\in\{1,2\}.

Therefore,
v_{1},v_{2}
are highest weight vectors of weight
2(\alpha-1)
. Since
\dim Q=4\alpha-2
,

. This proves the Proposition.
∎

4.2.
Isomorphism theorem and the group
G

In this subsection, we establish the isomorphism theorem for modules of the form

M\left(\alpha,\mathbf{a},K(h)\right),\qquad\mathbf{a}\in\{(2,0,0),(0,2,0),(0,0,2)\},\ \alpha\in\mathbb{C},\ K(h)\in\mathcal{S}.

In particular, we have an isomorphism theorem for all simple scalar-type rank-2 modules.

Remark 4.5
.

By Theorem
3.10
, it suffices to assume that
K(h)=\mathbf{E}_{(a,b)}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})
, where
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
,
a,b\in\mathbb{C}^{*},\;u_{1},\dots,u_{k}\in\mathbb{C}[h]\setminus\mathbb{C}
.

Definition 4.6
.

Fix
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and set
\mathcal{P}_{k}:=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}\times(\mathbb{C}[h])^{k}
. For
(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\,u_{1},\dots,u_{k})\in\mathcal{P}_{k}
and
\eta\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
, define maps
T,S_{\eta}:\mathcal{P}_{k}\to\mathcal{P}_{k}
by

T\big((\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\,u_{1},\dots,u_{k})\big)\;:=\;\big(\beta_{2},\beta_{1},\,\tfrac{\beta_{1}}{\beta_{2}}\,\sigma(u_{k}),\,u_{1},\,\dots,\ u_{k-1}\big),

S_{\eta}\big((\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\,u_{1},\dots,u_{k})\big):=\big(\beta_{1}\eta^{k\bmod 2},\;\beta_{2}\eta^{-(k\bmod 2)},\;\eta^{\varepsilon_{1}}u_{1},\dots,\eta^{\varepsilon_{k}}u_{k}\big),

where
\varepsilon_{i}:=(-1)^{\,i-1+k}
.

Remark 4.7
.

It is immediate from the definition that
T
is bijective. In fact,

T^{-1}\big((\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\,u_{1},\dots,u_{k})\big)\;:=\;\bigl(\beta_{2},\beta_{1},\,u_{2},\,\dots,\,u_{k},\tfrac{\beta_{1}}{\beta_{2}}\sigma^{-1}(u_{1})\bigr).

Consequently,
T
generates a
\mathbb{Z}
-action on
\mathcal{P}_{k}
by

n\cdot(\mathbf{X}):=T^{\,n}(\mathbf{X}),\qquad\text{where}\quad n\in\mathbb{Z},\;\mathbf{X}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}.

For
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, define
\phi_{m}\in\Aut(\mathbb{C}^{*})
by
\phi_{m}(\eta):=\eta^{(-1)^{m}}
, for all
\eta\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
. Then the map

\Phi:\mathbb{Z}\to\Aut(\mathbb{C}^{*})\qquad\text{where}\qquad m\mapsto\phi_{m},

is a group homomorphism.

Lemma 4.8
.

For all
m\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\eta\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
,
T^{m}S_{\eta}T^{-m}=S_{\phi_{m}(\eta)}
.

Proof.

The case
m=0
is immediate. For
m\geq 1
, we proceed by induction on
m
. For
m=1
, let
(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\,u_{1},\dots,u_{k})\in\mathcal{P}_{k}
, then

\displaystyle TS_{\eta}T^{-1}\big((\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\,u_{1},\dots,u_{k})\big)

\displaystyle=TS_{\eta}\left(\bigl(\beta_{2},\beta_{1},\,u_{2},\,\dots,\,u_{k},\tfrac{\beta_{1}}{\beta_{2}}\sigma^{-1}(u_{1})\bigr)\right)

\displaystyle=T\left(\bigl(\beta_{2}\eta^{k\bmod 2},\beta_{1}\eta^{-k\bmod 2},\,\eta^{\varepsilon_{1}}u_{2},\,\dots,\,\eta^{\varepsilon_{k-1}}u_{k},\eta^{\varepsilon_{k}}\tfrac{\beta_{1}}{\beta_{2}}\sigma^{-1}(u_{1})\bigr)\right)

\displaystyle=\bigl(\beta_{1}\eta^{-k\bmod 2},\beta_{2}\eta^{k\bmod 2},\ \eta^{\varepsilon_{k}}u_{1},\,\eta^{\varepsilon_{1}}u_{2},\,\dots,\,\eta^{\varepsilon_{k-1}}u_{k}\bigr).

Since
\varepsilon_{k}=-\varepsilon_{1}
and, for
2\leq i\leq k
,
\varepsilon_{i-1}=-\varepsilon_{i}
, then

\displaystyle TS_{\eta}T^{-1}\big((\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\,u_{1},\dots,u_{k})\big)

\displaystyle=\left(\beta_{1}\left(\eta^{-1}\right)^{k\bmod 2},\beta_{2}\left(\eta^{-1}\right)^{-k\bmod 2},\,\left(\eta^{-1}\right)^{\varepsilon_{1}}u_{1},\dots,\left(\eta^{-1}\right)^{\varepsilon_{k}}u_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=S_{\eta^{-1}}\big((\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\,u_{1},\dots,u_{k})\big)=S_{\phi_{1}(\eta)}\big((\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\,u_{1},\dots,u_{k})\big).

This proves the base case
m=1
. The induction step is relatively easy:

T^{m+1}S_{\eta}T^{-(m+1)}=TS_{\phi_{m}(\eta)}T^{-1}=S_{\phi_{1}\left(\phi_{m}(\eta)\right)}=S_{\phi_{m+1}(\eta)}.

It remains to consider
m\leq-1
. In this case we apply the above for
(-m)\geq 1
and obtain

T^{-m}S_{\phi_{m}(\eta)}T^{m}=S_{\phi_{-m}\left(\phi_{m}(\eta)\right)}=S_{\eta}.

∎

Proposition 4.9
.

The following

(\eta,m)\star\mathbf{X}:=S_{\eta}\big(T^{m}(\mathbf{X})\big),\qquad\text{where}\quad(\eta,m)\in\mathbb{C}^{*}\rtimes_{\Phi}\mathbb{Z},\;\;\mathbf{X}\in\mathcal{P}_{k},

defines an action of the group
\mathbb{C}^{*}\rtimes_{\Phi}\mathbb{Z}
on
\mathcal{P}_{k}
.

Proof.

The identity element
(1,0)
acts trivially since
S_{1}=T^{0}=\mathrm{id}
. For
(\eta_{i},m_{i})\in\mathbb{C}^{*}\rtimes_{\Phi}\mathbb{Z}
with
i\in\{1,2\}
, applying Lemma
4.8
gives

(\eta_{1},m_{1})\star\big((\eta_{2},m_{2})\star\mathbf{X}\big)=S_{\eta_{1}}\,T^{m_{1}}\,S_{\eta_{2}}\,T^{m_{2}}\mathbf{X}=S_{\eta_{1}}\,S_{\phi_{m_{1}}(\eta_{2})}\,T^{m_{1}+m_{2}}\mathbf{X}\\
=\bigl(\eta_{1}\phi_{m_{1}}(\eta_{2}),m_{1}+m_{2}\bigr)\star\mathbf{X}.

This proves the proposition.
∎

Definition 4.10
.

We define

G:=\mathbb{C}^{*}\rtimes_{\Phi}\mathbb{Z}
and
\Orb_{G}(\mathbf{X}):=\big\{\,(\eta,n)\star\mathbf{X}\;\mid\;\eta\in\mathbb{C}^{*},\ n\in\mathbb{Z}\,\big\}
.

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
,
k,m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, and
u_{1},\dots,u_{k},v_{1},\dots,v_{m}\in\mathbb{C}[h]\setminus\mathbb{C}
. Then

\mathbf{E}_{(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})\sim_{\sigma^{-1}}\mathbf{E}_{(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})}(v_{1},\dots,v_{m})

if and only if
m=k
, and
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\,v_{1},\dots,v_{k})\in\Orb_{G}\big(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\,u_{1},\dots,u_{k}\big)
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.7
,

\mathbf{E}_{(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})\sim_{\sigma^{-1}}\mathbf{E}_{(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})}(v_{1},\dots,v_{m})

implies
k=m
.
Then there exists a matrix
P(h)\in\GL_{2}(\mathbb{C}[h])
in standard form,

P(h)=\diag(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})\quad\text{or}\quad P(h)=\mathbf{E}_{(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})}(w_{1},\dots,w_{p}),\qquad\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\in\mathbb{C}^{*},

with
w_{1},w_{p}\in\mathbb{C}[h]
and
w_{i}\in\mathbb{C}[h]\setminus\mathbb{C}
for
1<i<p
, such that

\mathbf{E}_{(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})}(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})=P(h)^{-1}\,\mathbf{E}_{(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})\,P(h+1).

We determine the
w_{i}(h)
for which the length is preserved under
\sigma^{-1}
–conjugation by
P(h)
, namely,
\;\ell\left(P(h)^{-1}\,\mathbf{E}_{(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})\,P(h+1)\right)=k
. Set
\eta:=\tfrac{\rho_{2}}{\rho_{1}}
. A direct computation yields

\displaystyle\diag(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})^{-1}\mathbf{E}_{(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})\diag(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{\big(\beta_{1}\eta^{k\bmod 2},\;\beta_{2}\eta^{-(k\bmod 2)}\big)}\big(\eta^{\varepsilon_{1}}u_{1},\dots,\eta^{\varepsilon_{k}}u_{k}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathbf{E}_{(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})}\left(\eta u_{1},\;\eta^{-1}u_{2},\dots,\;\eta^{-1}u_{k}\right),&\text{if }k\ \text{even},\\
\mathbf{E}_{\left(\beta_{1}\eta,\;\beta_{2}\eta^{-1}\right)}\left(\eta^{-1}u_{1},\;\eta u_{2},\dots,\;\eta^{-1}u_{k}\right),&\text{if }k\ \text{odd}.\end{cases}

Hence, the
\sigma^{-1}
-conjugation of
\mathbf{E}_{(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})
by
\diag(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})
is equivalent to applying
S_{\eta}
to
\left(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\,u_{1},\dots,u_{k}\right)
. We treat the case where
k
is even; the case where
k
is odd is analogous.

P(h)^{-1}\mathbf{E}_{(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})P(h+1)=(-1)^{p}\diag(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})_{[p]}\,E(0)\left(\mathop{\overleftarrow{\prod}}_{\ell=2}^{\,p}E\left(-\Bigl(\tfrac{\beta_{1}}{\beta_{2}}\Bigr)^{(-1)^{\ell}}w_{\ell}\right)\right)\\
\cdot E\left(-\tfrac{\beta_{2}}{\beta_{1}}w_{1}+\eta u_{1}\right)E\left(\eta^{-1}u_{2}\right)\dots E\left(\eta^{-1}u_{k}\right)\,\left(\prod_{i=1}^{p}E(w_{i}(h+1))\right).

Following the same argument as in the proof of Proposition
3.7
, the length of the matrix
\mathbf{E}_{(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})
is preserved for exactly two choices of
(w_{1},w_{2},...,w_{p})
,
p\geq 1
:

Choice 1:

w_{1}=\tfrac{\eta\beta_{1}}{\beta_{2}}\,u_{1},\qquad w_{2}=\tfrac{\beta_{2}}{\eta\beta_{1}}\,u_{2},\qquad w_{3}=\begin{cases}\eta\left(\tfrac{\beta_{1}}{\beta_{2}}\right)^{2}u_{1}(h+1),&k=2,\\
\tfrac{\eta\beta_{1}}{\beta_{2}}\,u_{3},&k>3,\end{cases}\quad\dots\quad(p\geq 1);

Choice 2:

w_{1}=0,\quad w_{2}=-\eta^{-1}u_{k}(h-1),\quad w_{3}=-\eta\,u_{k-1}(h-1),\qquad\dots,\qquad w_{p}=0\quad(p\geq 3).

Let
j
be defined by

j:=\begin{cases}-p,&\text{in Choice 1},\\
p-2,&\text{in Choice 2}.\end{cases}

With the above choices of
\{w_{i}:1\leq i\leq p\}
, one checks directly that the resulting matrix coincides with the image of

\left(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\,\eta u_{1},\eta^{-1}u_{2},\dots,\eta^{-1}u_{k}\right)

under
T^{j}
for some
j\in\mathbb{Z}
; the case
j=0
corresponds to the case
P(h)=\diag(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})
. Therefore,

\mathbf{E}_{(\beta_{1},\beta_{1})}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})\sim_{\sigma^{-1}}\mathbf{E}_{(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})}(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})

if and only if there exist
\eta\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
and
j\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\,v_{1},\dots,v_{k})=T^{j}\big(S_{\eta}(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\,u_{1},\dots,u_{k})\big).

To complete the proof, we note that for
\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}
,

\exists\,\eta\in\mathbb{C}^{*},\,j\in\mathbb{Z}:\,\mathbf{Y}=T^{j}\big(S_{\eta}(\mathbf{X})\big)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mathbf{Y}\in\Orb_{G}\left(\mathbf{X}\right).

∎

Theorem 4.12
.

Let
c_{1},c_{2},d_{1},d_{2}\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
,
k,m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
,
u_{1},\dots,u_{k},v_{1},\dots,v_{m}\in\mathbb{C}[h]\setminus\mathbb{C}
,
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\in\big\{(2,0,0),(0,2,0),(0,0,2)\big\}
, and

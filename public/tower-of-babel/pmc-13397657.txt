Disease transition dynamics among infectious disease patients in Oyo State, Nigeria: a markov chain analysis
Authors: Adigun K.Abimbola, Adigun O. Abiodun, Adarabioyo M.Idowu, Adewumi F.D.
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundRecently, Government at all levels, are seeking for ways to reduce drastically the spread of infectious diseases. This research work investigated disease transition matrix among infectious disease patients in Oyo State, Nigeria. The study employed reviewing data of patients from sourced datasets contained information on the human population, exposed population, and reported cases of the infectious diseases in Oyo State, for both children and adult in 2021, 2022 and 2023, respectively using Markovian Approach.MethodsData on children from age 0–19, was estimated to be 78,050 comprised of 75,658(diarrhea), 63(hepatitis), 605(pertussis), 939 (measles) and 785(tuberculosis) cases. The second category was 5,685 adults’ data from age 20 and above that consisted 2,297 cases of diarrhea, 223 (hepatitis), 180 (pertussis) 187 (measles) and 2,798 (tuberculosis) cases. Markov chain approach was used to predict the population under risk of infectious diseases and the expected number of visits to each state of the diseases before absorption occur was captured. A prediction of likelihood of infection with respect to diseases in the future was also apprehended.ResultsThe study findings showed that the future states of the diseases were solely dependent on both initial and present states and that the infected population had high probability of recovery at 5% level of significance. The transition pattern, fundamental matrix and the future transient state of the diseases reviewed that as many people that are exposed and infected with the diseases in the study area got recovered which may be due to government policy and health care facilities put in place. Probability of absorption depicted that, as many people that are infected got recovered and only small percentage of death were recorded.ConclusionThis study concluded that as people move from one state of infectious diseases to another state, their recovered process is high.

Background

Infectious disease is a significant aspect of global public health that deserves attention. Within the last decade, there has been an unrelenting global increase in outbreaks of new and emerging diseases, as well as the resurgence of existing ones, with Africa contributing to a substantial percentage of these outbreaks. Infectious diseases cause approximately 15 million deaths each year. This accounts for a considerable portion of all mortality globally, making it a critical global public health problem. Beyond health impacts, infectious diseases have impacts on society, including socioeconomic and educational impacts, emphasizing the significance of effective response in avoiding and controlling disease outbreaks. Bloom, D. E., & Cadarette, D. [4]. Infectious disease threats in the twenty-first century: strengthening the global response. Frontiers in immunology, 10, 549.

Infectious diseases has gained recognition in the twenty-first century due to significant mortality rates exceeding one billion people in developed and developing nations. Numerous pathogens, including bacteria, viruses, fungi, parasites, and prions, cause infectious diseases that can be transmitted between hosts through various mechanisms, such as airborne transmission, transmission via intermediate animal hosts, direct human-to-human transmission, close-contact situations, and vector-borne transmission. Globalization and increased connectivity have accelerated the spread of infectious diseases across national borders. The rising incidence of infectious diseases presents a formidable challenge for scientists and epidemiologists. Baral, B., Mamale, K., Gairola, S., Chauhan, C., Dey, A., & Kaundal, R. K. [3]. Nigeria is endemic for four of the five NTDs amenable to preventive chemotherapy through mass drug administration (MDA). In 2020, 84% of the 31.7 million people targeted were treated with MDA. Nigeria’s coverage rates of vaccination in children have historically been suboptimal and well below the 90% target rate. In 2021, the third dose of DTP-containing vaccine reached just 56%, while the coverage rate for the first dose of measles vaccine was 59% [2]. Materials and methods

Source of data

The Data used in this research work was sourced from the Oyo State Primary Health Care Development Agency 2021, 2022 and 2023. The data captured both Children and Adults in the study area.

Sample size estimation

The first section of the sample size included data collected from children aged 0 to 19, totaling an estimated 78,032, which included 75,658 cases of diarrhea, 63 cases of Hepatitis, 605 cases of Pertussis, 939 cases of Measles, and 785 cases of tuberculosis., while the second section comprised 5,608 cases from adults aged 20 and older, which included 2,297 instances of diarrhea, 223 cases of Hepatitis, 180 cases of Pertussis, 187 cases of Measles, and 2,798 cases of tuberculosis.

Discussion

The study modelled the prevalence of diarrhea, hepatitis, measles, pertussis and tuberculosis in Oyo state, South Western Nigeria. The probabilities of individuals transitioning from one state (Susceptible, Immune, Infected, Recovered, Dead) of the diseases to another state was estimated using Markov Chain Approach. The result revealed that, Diarrhea in the study area moved to immunity state with 32% and exposure rate of 36% which indicating strong immunity development and with average percentage of recovery rate 50%. Also, Hepatitis, measles, Pertussis and Tuberculosis have high percentage of susceptibility rate with 99%, 97%, 97% and 99% in the study area. This indicate persistent exposure to all these diseases in Oyo State. The research work revealed that an average number of individuals recovered was 48% and small percentage of death 2% was recorded in the case of Hepatitis. Measles has small percentage of immunity and recovery rate. The result revealed that Pertussis and Tuberculosis have a significant recovery rate among immunized individual of 51% with notable death. This is in support of Idajili, A., et.al [5], they opined in their research that, global infectious diseases like SARS, Ebola and COVID-19 have had great negative effects on Nigerians population health and socio-economic development. They discussed the socioeconomic impact of these global infectious diseases on Nigeria to provide a better understanding to government and practitioners of why improving the management of infectious disease outbreaks response is so important for a nation’s economy, its society, and its place in the global community, they found out that global infectious diseases have presented a global epidemic threat.

Again, the fundamental matrix of the diseases in the study area revealed distinct patterns in the transition dynamics of each disease. Common trends include prolonged susceptibility to diseases like Hepatitis and Tuberculosis, indicating persistent exposure phases. The immunity state is generally stable and prolonged across all diseases, reflecting effective responses from the population and the government. However, the varying efficiency in transitioning from exposure to immunity highlights the need for tailored public health measures, vaccination programs, and treatment strategies to address the unique challenges posed by each disease. The insights gained from this analysis can inform targeted interventions to improve disease management and control in the study area and this is in support with Kehinde A. Adigun, et.al [7], they accessed the dependence of conditional probabilities of the future state of five selected infectious diseases (chickenpox, tuberculosis, diarrhea, hepatitis, and meningitis) on the present state of the diseases in Ekiti State. They found out from their work that the future states of the diseases were solely dependent on both initial and present states and that the infected population had high probability of recovery at 5% level of significance. Also, different categories of patients’ transit from one state of the diseases to another.

The predictions for future generations indicate varying trends for the selected infectious diseases studied. Common trends include a decrease in exposed populations and increases in recovery rates, indicating improvements in medical treatment and public health interventions. This is in conformity to Emiluyi and Bank-Ola [6] who discussed that the burden of infectious diseases in sub-Saharan Africa Regional trade blocks collating from 14 ECOWAS members and 14 SADC blocks countries and findings showed that HIV prevalence rates and incidence of tuberculosis were un-even in the two selected blocks, also the severity and magnitude of the diseases varied and that the burden of both diseases was higher for SADC and lesser for ECOWAS. They found out that the magnitude and severity of the diseases vary widely between the Economic Community of West Africa States (ECOWAS) and the Southern Africa Development Community (SADC) trade blocs. And, while concerted efforts at curbing the diseases have yielded results, there is still much to be done in both blocs.

However, the constant infection rates and slight increases in mortality rates across most diseases highlight ongoing challenges. Targeted efforts to enhance immunity through vaccination and improve treatment protocols are essential to further reduce exposure and infection rates and manage these diseases effectively. Public health policies must adapt to these predictions to ensure sustained progress in combating infectious diseases in the study area and finally the future predictions underscore the importance of continued public health efforts, including vaccination programs, improved sanitation, and enhanced healthcare services. Targeted interventions are necessary to further reduce exposure, infection, and mortality rates while increasing immunity and recovery outcomes. These insights provide valuable guidance for policymakers and healthcare providers in planning and implementing effective disease control strategies.

Conclusion

In this study, a stochastic probability approach was used to model infectious diseases in the study area. The model was appropriate in predicting the probabilities of the diseases’ transmission from one state to another. The transition pattern was designed to capture the movement from one state to another. It is noteworthy that the fundamental matrix analysis of infectious diseases in the study area reveals distinct patterns in the transition dynamics of each disease, this study provided a framework upon which state policies on healthcare can be championed and the proper deployment of scarce resources in containing the spread of the infectious diseases among the studied population. The insights gained from this analysis can inform targeted interventions to improve disease management and control in the study area and Nigeria at large. For targeted efforts to enhance immunity through vaccination, improved treatment protocols are essential to further reduce exposure and infection rates and manage these diseases effectively. To ensure sustained progress in combating infectious diseases, public health policies should ensure adaptation of public health policies towards disease predictions.

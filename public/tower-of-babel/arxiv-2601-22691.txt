Constraint Satisfaction Problems over Finitely Bounded Homogeneous Structures: a Dichotomy between FO and L-hard Thanks: Funded by the National Science Centre, Poland under the Weave-UNISONO call in the Weave programme, project no. 2021/03/Y/ST6/00171 Affiliation: Faculty of Mathematics and Computer Science, Jagiellonian University, Poland
Leonid Dorochko, Michał Wrona

Abstract
Feder-Vardi conjecture, which proposed that every finite-domain Constraint Satisfaction Problem (CSP) is either in P or it is NP-complete, has been solved independently by Bulatov and Zhuk almost ten years ago. Bodirsky-Pinsker conjecture which states a similar dichotomy for countably infinite first-order reducts of finitely bounded homogeneous structures is wide open. In this paper, we prove that CSPs over first-order expansions of finitely bounded homogeneous model-complete cores are either first-order definable (and hence in non-uniform AC 0 ) or L-hard under first-order reduction. It is arguably the most general complexity dichotomy when it comes to the scope of structures within Bodirsky-Pinsker conjecture. Our strategy is that we first give a new proof of Larose-Tesson theorem, which provides a similar dichotomy over finite structures, and then generalize that new proof to infinite structures.

Introduction

The goal of the computational complexity is to describe the amount of certain resources such as time and space required to solve a computational problem under consideration. Instead of working on a single problem, it is reasonable to work on the whole family of problems admitting a uniform formulation.
A general formalism which allows the formulation of many natural computation problems is the Constraint Satisfaction Problem
\csp(\mathbb{A})
parametrized by a relational structure
\mathbb{A}
. Every
\mathbb{A}
contributes to a different computational problem, e.g., a Boolean satisfiability problem, graph coloring or solving equations over a finite field. An instance of
\csp(\mathbb{A})
is a set of constraints built out of variables and relations in
\mathbb{A}
. The goal is to satisfy all constraints simultaneously by the same assignment to variables. Feder-Vardi conjecture
[
22
]
settled in 2017 independently by Bulatov
[
16
]
and Zhuk
[
38
,
39
]
states that whenever
\mathbb{A}
is finite, then
\csp(\mathbb{A})
is either in P or it is NP-complete.
That result stands in the opposition to Ladner’s theorem
[
26
]
on the existence of NP-intermediate problems on the condition that P
\subsetneq
NP. Although obtaining the dichotomy was the original motivation, the algorithms for all polynomial-time
\csp(\mathbb{A})
are arguably of not less importance. They generalize Gaussian elimination
[
25
]
and local-consistency techniques
[
4
]
ubiquitous in constraint solving. Thus, they provide the same way of solving many natural problems in P. On the other hand a simple algebraic condition provides a polynomial time gadget reduction from monotone 1-in-3-SAT to any NP-complete
\csp(\mathbb{A})

[
17
]
. Thereby, the dichotomy proofs provide a deep insight into the nature of the class NP.

Although the holy grail of the CSP community has been achieved, it is not the end of the story.
Indeed, there is a number of active research directions generalizing
\csp(\mathbb{A})
over finite structures
\mathbb{A}
. In particular, Bodirsky-Pinsker
conjecture
[
11
]
extends Feder-Vardi conjecture to some well-behaved infinite structures
\mathbb{A}
which cover a number of problems not expressible as

{theorem}

[]

(Larose-Tesson)

Let
\mathbb{A}
be a finite structure over a finite signature. Then one of the following holds.

•

\csp(\mathbb{A})
is L-hard (under first-order reduction).

•

\csp(\mathbb{A})
is first-order definable, and in non-uniform
\text{AC}^{0}

Recall that

\text{AC}^{0}
is the complexity class consisting of all languages accepted by polynomial-size constant-depth
families of Boolean circuits and that every first-order definable decision problem is in that class (see, e.g. Theorem 6.4 in
[

]
).
Moreover, there are problems in L which are not in non-uniform
\text{AC}^{0}

[
23
]
, i.e.,
\text{non-uniform AC}^{0}\subsetneq L
. In particular, any problem which is L-hard under first-order reduction cannot lie in non-uniform
\text{AC}^{0}
.

Since the question on the P versus NP-complete dichotomy for first-order reducts of finitely-bounded homogeneous structures is notoriously resistant and the conjectures from
[
28
]
on CSPs in L and NL are not even solved for finite structures, it is reasonable to ask if at least Theorem
1

{theorem}

[]

(Main theorem)

Let
\mathbb{A}
be a finite-signature first-order expansion of a finitely bounded homogeneous model-complete core
\mathbb{B}
. Then one of the following holds.

•

\csp(\mathbb{A})
is L-hard (under first-order reduction).

•

\csp(\mathbb{A})
is first-order definable, and is in non-uniform
\text{AC}^{0}
.

The difference in the formulation of the above theorem and Conjecture
1
is that in the former case we start with a finitely bounded homogeneous structure
\mathbb{B}
which is already a model-complete core and then expand it with some extra relations. Thus,
\mathbb{A}
contains all relations in
\mathbb{B}
. On the other hand, in the conjecture, we start with a finitely bounded homogeneous structure and then we take its first-order reduct (thus, we can forget some relations in
\mathbb{B}
). In general, it is now allowed to take a model-complete core of
\mathbb{A}
but it is not even clear if it will be a reduct of a finitely bounded homogeneous structure — see Question (5) on the list of open problem in
[
13
]
, in Section 14.2.

{example}

The order
(\mathbb{Q};<)
over rational numbers is a finitely bounded homogeneous model-complete core. Theorem
1
covers all its expansions by first-order definable relations. In this case all corresponding CSPs are L-hard, in fact even NL-hard.

On the other hand, consider
(V;E,N)
which is the expansion of the countable universal homogeneous graph (or the countable homogeneous triangle-free graph) by the
non-edge
relation which holds on different vertices not connected by an edge. Its CSP is first-order definable (and hence in non-uniform AC
0
) but, as we will see, some its first-order expansions give rise to L-hard CSPs.

In order to prove Theorem
1
, we first provide a new proof of Theorem
1
and then generalize the new proof to infinite structures covered by Theorem
1
.

1.1
Proof strategy

Finite duality.

Throughout the paper, we freely change the perspective on
\Csp(\mathbb{A})
seeing its instances sometimes as sets of constraints over relations in
\mathbb{A}
to be solved and sometimes as structures
\mathbb{C}
over the same signature as
\mathbb{A}
which are to be verified whether they homomorphically map to
\mathbb{A}
or not. Using the latter perspective, one can show that
\csp(\mathbb{A})
is first-order definable if and only if
\mathbb{A}
has finite duality, i.e., a finite set of
obstructions

\mathcal{O}
such that
\mathbb{C}
admits a homomorphism to
\mathbb{A}
if and only if no
\mathbb{D}\in\mathcal{O}
maps homomorphically to
\mathbb{A}
. For the result, see Theorem 5.6.2 in
[
13
]
proved using Rossman preservation theorem
[
35
]
. For finite structures, we first knew this characterization of first-order definable CSPs thanks to Atserias
[
1
]
.

\mathbb{A}
-definitions

Further, we observe that a finite core structure
\mathbb{A}
containing the equality
=
and all constants in its signature gives rise to an
L
-hard
\csp(\mathbb{A})
. A first-order reduction from Graph Unreachability can be now simply achieved by marking a constant
s
in
\mathbb{A}
as a source, some other constant
t
as a target and introduce a constraint
x=y
between vertices connected by an edge in the graph. Therefore, as it is sometimes the case, we cannot include the equality to the signature of
\mathbb{A}
if it is not explicitly there, and we cannot use primitive positive definitions with a free use of equality as a reduction between considered CSPs. Hence, instead of primitive positive formulae we use what we call
\mathbb{A}
-formulae where only atoms built out of relational symbols in the signature of
\mathbb{A}
are used. We show, using the known results, that
\mathbb{A}
-definitions (provided by
\mathbb{A}
-formulae) give us first-order reductions between the corresponding CSPs.

Proof sketch for Theorem
1

Without loss of generality, we assume that
\mathbb{A}
is a core and contains all constants. An

Such an implication is called

-define an equivalence relation on a subset of the domain

-definable, then

is solvable by

1.2
Significance and the future work

Our main result, Theorem
1
, is arguably the most general dichotomy theorem when it comes to the scope of structures within Bodirsky-Pinsker conjecture.
The theorem was obtained by first reproving the analogous result for finite structures and then by generalizing this new proof to infinite structures. A similar general idea has been adopted in
[
2
,
14
]
in order to provide new loop lemmata over
\omega
-categorical structures:

old proofs for finite structures are not easily generalizable to infinite structure, so what about providing new proofs that can be easier generalized?

This way of thinking brings a new hope for open problems over infinite-domain CSPs. We may just need a slightly new proof of Bulatov-Zhuk theorem to be able to generalize it to first-order reducts of finitely bounded homogeneous structures. Such a new proof may be of course hard to obtain straightaway but we believe that the methods from this paper may be used to identify both finite and infinite structures of bounded width (solvable by the minimality algorithm) that give rise to CSPs in NL and L.

2.1
Structures and formulae

All relational structures in this article are assumed to be at most countable.
Let
\mathbb{A}
be a relational structure, we denote the domain of

by a plain letter

. Let

and free variables

. We identify the interpretation

of

with the set of satisfying assignments

be a tuple of elements of

. We define the

\proj_{\mathbf{u}}(\phi^{\mathbb{A}}):=\{f(\mathbf{u})\mid f\in\phi^{\mathbb{A}}\}

A

of a structure

is an expansion of

by relations which are first-order definable in

, i.e., for which there exists

We write

to denote the set of automorphisms of

. An orbit of a

in

where

is the set

\{\alpha(\mathbf{a})\mid\alpha\in\Aut(\mathbb{A})\}

A countable relational structure

is

-tuples in

For an

-categorical relational structure

and for every

it holds that two

-tuples of elements of the structure are in the same orbit in
\mathbb{A}
if and only if they have the same

, i.e., they satisfy the same first-order formulae. The

of an

-tuple is the set of all atomic formulae satisfied by this tuple; the structure
\mathbb{A}
is

if any two tuples of the same atomic type belong to the same orbit. For

, we say that a relational structure
\mathbb{A}
is

if for all tuples

of arbitrary finite equal length, if all

, then

and

are in the same orbit in
\mathbb{A}
. If a homogeneous

has a finite signature, then it is

-categorical and

-homogeneous for some

.
For

, we say that
\mathbb{A}
is

if for every finite

of

, then

. A structure

if and only if no structure from

A structure

A first-order formula is called

A relation is

For any

-formula. Indeed, let

5
Summary and Future Work

The main contribution of this paper is the dichotomy between non-uniform AC
0
and L-hard for all finite-signature first-order expansions of finitely bounded homogeneous structures — Theorem
1
. In order to obtain the result we first reproved Theorem
1
and then generalized this new proof to infinite structures.
We believe that the methods provided may be used further,
in particular, to approach the following research question.

{rquestion}

Which first-order reducts of
k
-homogeneous
\ell
-bounded structures of relational width
(k,l)
give rise to CSPs in NL?

It is interesting in its own rights — a number of infinite-domain CSPs are solvable by local consistency methods
[
5
]
but it is also directly connected to an open question over finite-domain CSPs.
Indeed, it is still not known which finite-domain CSPs are P-complete, and which are in NL. In particular, we do not it even know for problems solvable by
1

and

, and on the other hand, for instance, structures with finite duality studied in this paper. One can imagine that
we could use methods developed in this paper to answer the question. We would just need new hardness criteria and a way to obtain a set of obstructions with bounded pathwidth when the criteria are violated. Then we could try to generalize the dichotomy to infinite structures solvable by
(k,l)

Contour Integrations and Parity Results of Cyclotomic Euler Sums and Multiple Polylogarithm Function
Hongyuan Rui and Ce Xu[1mm] a. School of Mathematics, Sichuan University,Chengdu 610064, P.R. Chinab. School of Mathematics and Statistics, Anhui Normal University,Wuhu 241002, P.R. China

Abstract




Abstract.
In this paper, we define extended trigonometric functions via series and employ the method of contour integration to investigate the parity of certain cyclotomic Euler sums and multiple polylogarithm function. We can provide the statement of parity results for cyclotomic Euler sums of arbitrary order, explicit formulas for the parity of cyclotomic linear and quadratic Euler sums, as well as some formulas for the parity of cyclotomic cubic Euler sums and multiple polylogarithms. As a direct corollary, we derive known formulas concerning the parity of classical Euler sums and alternating Euler sums.

Keywords
: Contour integration; Cyclotomic Euler sums; Cyclotomic multiple zeta values; Residue theorem; Parity result; Multiple polylogarithm function.

11M32, 11M99.

In 1998, Flajolet and Salvy
[

The objective of this paper is to employ the contour integral method to establish parity results for both generalized and cyclotomic Euler sums. In particular, we can provide explicit formulas for the parity of cyclotomic linear and quadratic Euler sums. Furthermore, by leveraging the relationships between generalized Euler sums and multiple polylogarithms, as well as between cyclotomic Euler sums and cyclotomic multiple zeta values, we also derive certain parity results for multiple polylogarithms and cyclotomic multiple zeta values. One of the primary results of this paper is the proof of the following theorem regarding the parity of cyclotomic Euler sums (see Theorem
5.5

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
x,x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}
be roots of unity, and
p_{1},\ldots,p_{r},q\geq 1
with
(p_{j},x_{j})\ (j=1,2,\ldots,r)
and
(q,x)\neq(1,1)
. We have

\displaystyle(-1)^{r}S_{p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{r};q}\Big(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{r};x\Big)+(-1)^{p_{1}+\cdots+p_{r}+q}S_{p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{r};q}\Big(x_{1}^{-1},x_{2}^{-1},\ldots,x_{r}^{-1};x^{-1}\Big)

reduces to a combination of cyclotomic Euler sums of lower orders.

The above-mentioned parity theorem concerning cyclotomic Euler sums corresponds to the parity theorem for multiple polylogarithms proved by Panzer
[
15
, Thm 1.3]
, which states that: for all
r\in\mathbb{N}
and
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
, the function

\displaystyle\Li_{{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}}(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{r})-(-1)^{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r}+r}\Li_{{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}}(1/z_{1},1/z_{2},\ldots,1/z_{r})

is of depth at most

. Here
(z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})\in\mathbb{C}^{r}\setminus\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq j\leq r}\{(z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}):z_{i}z_{i+1}\cdots z_{j}\in[0,+\infty)\}
.

3
Parity Results for Cyclotomic Linear Euler Sums

In this section, we examine the parity of cyclotomic linear Euler sums and cyclotomic double zeta values, as well as provide some formulas for the double polylogarithms.
First, we consider the contour integration (Here
p,q\in\mathbb{N}
)

\displaystyle\oint\limits_{\left(\infty\right)}\displaystyle\frac{\Phi(s;x)\phi^{(p-1)}(s;y)}{(p-1)!s^{q}}(-1)^{p-1}ds=0.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
x,y
be roots of unity, and
p,q\geq 1
with
(p,y),(q,xy)\neq(1,1)
. We have

\displaystyle\Li_{p,q}\left(y,\displaystyle\frac{1}{xy}\right)-(-1)^{p+q}\Li_{p,q}\left(\displaystyle\frac{1}{y},xy\right)

\displaystyle=\Li_{p}(y)\Li_{q}\left(\displaystyle\frac{1}{xy}\right)+(-1)^{q}\Li_{p}(y)\Li_{q}(xy)-\Li_{p+q}\left(\displaystyle\frac{1}{x}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-(-1)^{q}\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{p}\binom{p+q-l-1}{q-1}\left((-1)^{l}\Li_{l}(x)+\Li_{l}\left(\displaystyle\frac{1}{x}\right)\right)\Li_{p+q-l}(xy)

\displaystyle\quad-(-1)^{q}\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{q}\binom{p+q-l-1}{p-1}\left((-1)^{l}\Li_{l}\left(\displaystyle\frac{1}{x}\right)+\Li_{l}(x)\right)\Li_{p+q-l}(y),

(3.18)

where
\Li_{0}(x)+\Li_{0}(x^{-1}):=-1

Proof.

Letting

F_{p,q}(s):=\displaystyle\frac{\Phi(s;x)\phi^{(p-1)}(s;y)}{(p-1)!s^{q}}(-1)^{p-1}.

Clearly, the function
F_{p,q}(s)
only singularities are poles at the integers. At a positive integer
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the pole
s=n
is simple and by the expansions (
2
) and (
2.14
), the residue is

\displaystyle{\rm Res}\left(F_{p,q}(s),n\right)=\displaystyle\frac{(xy)^{-n}}{n^{q}}\Big(\Li_{p}(y)-\zeta_{n-1}(p;y)\Big).

For positive integer
n
, the pole
s=-n
has order
p+1
. By (
2
) and (
2.14
), the residue is

\displaystyle{\rm Res}\left(F_{p,q}(s),-n\right)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{1}{p!}\displaystyle\lim_{s\rightarrow-n}\displaystyle\frac{d^{p}}{ds^{p}}\left((s+n)^{p+1}F_{p,q}(s)\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{q}\binom{p+q-1}{p}\displaystyle\frac{(xy)^{n}}{n^{p+q}}+(-1)^{q}\displaystyle\frac{\Li_{p}(y)+(-1)^{p}\zeta_{n}\Big(p;y^{-1}\Big)}{n^{q}}(xy)^{n}

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{q}\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{p-1}\binom{p+q-m-2}{q-1}\left((-1)^{m}\Li_{m+1}(x)-\Li_{m+1}\Big(x^{-1}\Big)\right)\displaystyle\frac{(xy)^{n}}{n^{p+q-m-1}}.

The pole

, the residue is

\displaystyle{\rm Res}\left(F_{p,q}(s),0\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p+q-1}\Li_{p+q}(x)-\Li_{p+q}\Big(x^{-1}\Big)+(-1)^{q}\binom{p+q-1}{p-1}\Li_{p+q}(y)

\displaystyle\quad+\displaystyle\sum_{m+k=q-1,\atop m,k\geq 0}(-1)^{k}\binom{k+p-1}{p-1}\Li_{k+p}(y)\left((-1)^{m}\Li_{m+1}(x)-\Li_{m+1}\Big(x^{-1}\Big)\right).

By Lemma
2.1
, we know that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left({\rm Res}\left(F_{p,q}(s),n\right)+{\rm Res}\left(F_{p,q}(s),-n\right)\right)+{\rm Res}\left(F_{p,q}(s),0\right)=0.

Finally, combining these three contributions yields the statement of Theorem
3.1
.
∎

From equation (
1.7
), the parity formula for cyclotomic linear Euler sums
S_{p;q}(x;y)
can be obtained.

If we consider contour integrals involving only the function
\phi(s;x)
and rational functions, we can still obtain some results on generalized Euler sums, though the expressions become more complicated. The following theorem provides one such conclusion.

Theorem 3.2
.

For positive integers
p_{1},p_{2},q
and
x_{1},x_{2}
are arbitrary complex numbers with
0<|x_{1}|,|x_{2}|\leq 1
and
x_{1},x_{2}\neq 1
, we have

\displaystyle(-1)^{p_{1}}\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{p_{2}-1}\binom{k+p_{1}-1}{p_{1}-1}\binom{q+p_{2}-k-2}{q-1}S_{k+p_{1};q+p_{2}-k-1}\Big(x_{1}^{-1};x_{1}x_{2}\Big)

\displaystyle+(-1)^{p_{2}}\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{p_{1}-1}\binom{k+p_{2}-1}{p_{2}-1}\binom{q+p_{1}-k-2}{q-1}S_{k+p_{2};q+p_{1}-k-1}\Big(x_{2}^{-1};x_{1}x_{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p_{1}}\binom{p_{1}+p_{2}+q-2}{p_{2}-1}\Li_{p_{1}+p_{2}+q-1}(x_{2})+(-1)^{p_{2}}\binom{p_{1}+p_{2}+q-2}{p_{1}-1}\Li_{p_{1}+p_{2}+q-1}(x_{1})

\displaystyle\quad+\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}=q-1,\atop k_{1},k_{2}\geq 0}\binom{k+p_{1}-1}{p_{1}-1}\binom{k+p_{2}-1}{p_{2}-1}\Li_{k_{1}+p_{1}}(x_{1})\Li_{k_{2}+p_{2}}(x_{2})

\displaystyle\quad-\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{p_{2}-1}\binom{k+p_{1}-1}{p_{1}-1}\binom{q+p_{2}-k-2}{q-1}(-1)^{k}\Li_{k+p_{1}}(x_{1})\Li_{q+p_{2}-k-1}(x_{1}x_{2})

\displaystyle\quad-\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{p_{1}-1}\binom{k+p_{2}-1}{p_{2}-1}\binom{q+p_{1}-k-2}{q-1}(-1)^{k}\Li_{k+p_{2}}(x_{1})\Li_{q+p_{1}-k-1}(x_{1}x_{2})

\displaystyle\quad-\binom{p_{1}+p_{2}+q-2}{q-1}\Li_{p_{1}+p_{2}+q-1}(x_{1}x_{2}).

(3.19)

Proof.

Letting

\oint\limits_{\left(\infty\right)}G_{p_{1}p_{2},q}(s)ds:=\oint\limits_{\left(\infty\right)}\displaystyle\frac{\phi^{(p_{1}-1)}(s;x_{1})\phi^{(p_{2}-1)}(s;x_{2})}{(p_{1}-1)!(p_{2}-1)!s^{q}}(-1)^{p_{1}+p_{2}}ds=0.

Clearly, the function
G_{p_{1}p_{2},q}(s)
only singularities are poles at the non-positive integers. At a positive integer
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the pole
s=-n
is a pole of order
p_{1}+p_{2}
. Applying (
2
), we have

\displaystyle G_{p_{1}p_{2},q}(s)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{(x_{1}x_{2})^{n}}{(s+n)^{p_{1}+p_{2}}}

\displaystyle\quad+(x_{1}x_{2})^{n}\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{p_{2}-1}\binom{k+p_{1}-1}{p_{1}-1}\left((-1)^{k}\Li_{k+p_{1}}(x_{1})+(-1)^{p_{1}}\zeta_{n}\Big(k+p_{1};x_{1}^{-1}\Big)\right)(s+n)^{k-p_{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+(x_{1}x_{2})^{n}\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{p_{1}-1}\binom{k+p_{2}-1}{p_{2}-1}\left((-1)^{k}\Li_{k+p_{2}}(x_{2})+(-1)^{p_{2}}\zeta_{n}\Big(k+p_{2};x_{2}^{-1}\Big)\right)(s+n)^{k-p_{1}}

\displaystyle\quad+\cdots.

By a direct residue computation, one obtains

\displaystyle{\rm Res}\left(G_{p_{1}p_{2},q}(s),-n\right)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{1}{(p_{1}+p_{2}-1)!}\displaystyle\lim_{s\rightarrow-n}\displaystyle\frac{d^{p_{1}+p_{2}-1}}{ds^{p_{1}+p_{2}-1}}\left((s+n)^{p_{1}+p_{2}}G_{p_{1}p_{2},q}(s)\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{q}\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{p_{2}-1}\binom{k+p_{1}-1}{p_{1}-1}\binom{q+p_{2}-k-2}{q-1}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\left((-1)^{k}\Li_{k+p_{1}}(x_{1})+(-1)^{p_{1}}\zeta_{n}\Big(k+p_{1};x_{1}^{-1}\Big)\right)\displaystyle\frac{(x_{1}x_{2})^{n}}{n^{q+p_{2}-k-1}}

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{q}\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{p_{1}-1}\binom{k+p_{2}-1}{p_{2}-1}\binom{q+p_{1}-k-2}{q-1}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\left((-1)^{k}\Li_{k+p_{2}}(x_{2})+(-1)^{p_{2}}\zeta_{n}\Big(k+p_{2};x_{2}^{-1}\Big)\right)\displaystyle\frac{(x_{1}x_{2})^{n}}{n^{q+p_{1}-k-1}}

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{q}\binom{q+p_{1}+p_{2}-2}{q-1}\displaystyle\frac{(x_{1}x_{2})^{n}}{n^{q+p_{1}+p_{2}-1}}.

The pole
s=0
has order
p_{1}+p_{2}+q
, the residue is

\displaystyle{\rm Res}\left(G_{p_{1}p_{2},q}(s),0\right)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{1}{(p_{1}+p_{2}+q-1)!}\displaystyle\lim_{s\rightarrow 0}\displaystyle\frac{d^{p_{1}+p_{2}+q-1}}{ds^{p_{1}+p_{2}+q-1}}\left(s^{p_{1}+p_{2}}G_{p_{1}p_{2},q}(s)\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p_{1}+q-1}\binom{p_{1}+p_{2}+q-2}{p_{2}-1}\Li_{p_{1}+p_{2}+q-1}(x_{2})

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{p_{2}+q-1}\binom{p_{1}+p_{2}+q-2}{p_{1}-1}\Li_{p_{1}+p_{2}+q-1}(x_{1})

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{q-1}\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}=q-1,\atop k_{1},k_{2}\geq 0}\binom{k+p_{1}-1}{p_{1}-1}\binom{k+p_{2}-1}{p_{2}-1}\Li_{k_{1}+p_{1}}(x_{1})\Li_{k_{2}+p_{2}}(x_{2}).

Therefore, using Lemma
2.1
and combining these two contributions yields the statement of Theorem
3.2
.
∎

Setting
p_{1}=p_{2}=1

Corollary 3.3
.

For positive integer
q
and
x_{1},x_{2}
are arbitrary complex numbers with

and
x_{1},x_{2}\neq 1
, we have

\displaystyle S_{1;q}\Big(x_{1}^{-1};x_{1}x_{2}\Big)+S_{1;q}\Big(x_{2}^{-1};x_{1}x_{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=q\Li_{q+1}(x_{1}x_{2})+\Li_{q+1}(x_{1})+\Li_{q+1}(x_{2})+\Big(\Li_{1}(x_{1})+\Li_{1}(x_{2})\Big)\Li_{q}(x_{1}x_{2})

\displaystyle\quad-\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}=q-1,\atop k_{1},k_{2}\geq 0}\Li_{k_{1}+1}(x_{1})\Li_{k_{2}+1}(x_{2}).

(3.20)

In particular, if letting

yields

\displaystyle S_{1;q}\Big(x^{-1};x^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{q}{2}\Li_{q+1}(x^{2})+\Li_{q+1}(x)+\Li_{1}(x)\Li_{q}(x^{2})

\displaystyle\quad-\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}=q-1,\atop k_{1},k_{2}\geq 0}\Li_{k_{1}+1}(x)\Li_{k_{2}+1}(x).

(3.21)

Clearly, when

, the generalized Euler sum on the left-hand side remains convergent as

approach

in (
3
) yields the well-known result (see
[
5
, Thm. 2.2]
)

\displaystyle S_{1;q}=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{H_{n}}{n^{q}}=\left(1+\displaystyle\frac{q}{2}\right)\zeta(q+1)-\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{q-2}\zeta(k+1)\zeta(q-k).

(3.22)

4
Parity Results for Cyclotomic Quadratic Euler Sums

In this section, we will employ the method of contour integration, via residue computation, to investigate explicit formulas for the parity of cyclotomic quadratic Euler sums and present some results for generalized quadratic Euler sums. This will further lead to certain parity results for cyclotomic multiple zeta values of depth three. First, we consider the parity results for cyclotomic quadratic Euler sums. To better present the results in the subsequent discussion, we adopt the following notation unless otherwise specified:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\left\{\Big(a_{1}^{(1)}a_{2}^{(1)}\cdots a_{r}^{(1)}\Big),\ldots,\Big(a_{1}^{(k)}a_{2}^{(k)}\cdots a_{r}^{(k)}\Big)\right\}}f\Big(x_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(r)}\Big):=\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}f\Big(x_{a_{1}^{(j)}},\ldots,x_{a_{r}^{(j)}}\Big),

(4.23)

where
a_{1}^{(j)},\ldots,a_{r}^{(j)}\in\mathbb{N}
and
j=1,2,\ldots,k\ (k\in\mathbb{N})
. The
\Big(a_{1}^{(j)}a_{2}^{(j)}\cdots a_{r}^{(j)}\Big)
is a permutation of distinct positive integers, and the order of the terms
a_{i}^{(j)}
in the sequence
\Big(a_{1}^{(j)}a_{2}^{(j)}\cdots a_{r}^{(j)}\Big)
is significant (i.e., their positions are not interchangeable). For example,
\Big(a_{1}^{(j)}a_{2}^{(j)}\cdots a_{r}^{(j)}\Big)
and
\Big(a_{2}^{(j)}a_{3}^{(j)}\cdots a_{r}^{(j)}a_{1}^{(j)}\Big)
represent distinct objects.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
x,x_{1},x_{2}
be roots of unity, and
p_{1},p_{2},q\geq 1
with
(p_{1},x_{1}),(p_{2},x_{2})

(q,xx_{1}x_{2})\neq(1,1)
. We have

\displaystyle S_{p_{1},p_{2};q}\left(x_{1},x_{2};\left(xx_{1}x_{2}\right)^{-1}\right)+\left(-1\right)^{p_{1}+p_{2}+q}S_{p_{1},p_{2};q}\left(x_{1}^{-1},x_{2}^{-1};xx_{1}x_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\left\{\left(12\right),\left(21\right)\right\}}S_{p_{\sigma\left(1\right)};p_{\sigma\left(2\right)}+q}\left(x_{\sigma\left(1\right)};x_{\sigma\left(2\right)}\left(xx_{1}x_{2}\right)^{-1}\right)-\left(-1\right)^{q}\binom{p_{1}+p_{2}+q-1}{p_{1}+p_{2}}\Li_{p_{1}+p_{2}+q}\left(xx_{1}x_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\left\{\left(12\right),\left(21\right)\right\}}\Li_{p_{\sigma\left(1\right)}}\left(x_{\sigma\left(1\right)}\right)\left(S_{p_{\sigma\left(2\right)};q}\left(x_{\sigma\left(2\right)};\left(xx_{1}x_{2}\right)^{-1}\right)-\Li_{p_{\sigma\left(2\right)}+q}\left(x_{\sigma\left(2\right)}\left(xx_{1}x_{2}\right)^{-1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\left\{\left(12\right),\left(21\right)\right\}}\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{p_{\sigma\left(2\right)}}\binom{k+p_{\sigma\left(1\right)}-1}{p_{\sigma\left(1\right)}-1}\binom{q+p_{\sigma\left(2\right)}-k-1}{q-1}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left(\left(-1\right)^{k+q}\Li_{k+p_{\sigma\left(1\right)}}\left(x_{\sigma\left(1\right)}\right)\Li_{p_{\sigma\left(2\right)}+q-k}\left(xx_{1}x_{2}\right)+\left(-1\right)^{p_{\sigma\left(1\right)}+q}S_{k+p_{\sigma\left(1\right)};p_{\sigma\left(2\right)}+q-k}\left(x_{\sigma\left(1\right)}^{-1};xx_{1}x_{2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\left\{\left(21\right),\left(12\right)\right\}}\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq k_{1}+k_{2}\leq p_{\sigma\left(2\right)}-1}\left(\left(-1\right)^{k_{1}}\Li_{k_{1}+1}\left(x\right)-\Li_{k_{1}+1}\left(x^{-1}\right)\right)\binom{k_{2}+p_{\sigma\left(1\right)}-1}{p_{\sigma\left(1\right)}-1}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\binom{q+p_{\sigma\left(2\right)}-k_{1}-k_{2}-2}{q-1}\begin{Bmatrix}\left(-1\right)^{k_{2}+q}\Li_{k_{2}+p_{\sigma\left(1\right)}}\left(x_{\sigma\left(1\right)}\right)\Li_{p_{\sigma\left(2\right)}+q-k_{1}-k_{2}-1}\left(xx_{1}x_{2}\right)\\
+\left(-1\right)^{p_{\sigma\left(1\right)}+q}S_{k_{2}+p_{\sigma\left(1\right)};p_{\sigma\left(2\right)}+q-k_{1}-k_{2}-1}\left(x_{\sigma\left(1\right)}^{-1};xx_{1}x_{2}\right)\end{Bmatrix}

\displaystyle\quad-\left(-1\right)^{q}\Li_{p_{1}}\left(x_{1}\right)\Li_{p_{2}}\left(x_{2}\right)\Li_{q}\left(xx_{1}x_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\left\{\left(21\right),\left(12\right)\right\}}\left(-1\right)^{p_{\sigma\left(1\right)}+q}\Li_{p_{\sigma\left(2\right)}}\left(x_{\sigma\left(2\right)}\right)S_{p_{\sigma\left(1\right)};q}\left(x_{\sigma\left(1\right)}^{-1};xx_{1}x_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\left(-1\right)^{q}\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{p_{1}+p_{2}-1}\binom{q+p_{1}+p_{2}-k-2}{q-1}\left(\left(-1\right)^{k}\Li_{k+1}\left(x\right)-\Li_{k+1}\left(x^{-1}\right)\right)\Li_{q+p_{1}+p_{2}-k-1}\left(xx_{1}x_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\left(-1\right)^{q}\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}=q,\atop k_{1},k_{2}\geq 0}\binom{k_{1}+p_{1}-1}{p_{1}-1}\binom{k_{2}+p_{2}-1}{p_{2}-1}\Li_{k_{1}+p_{1}}\left(x_{1}\right)\Li_{k_{2}+p_{2}}\left(x_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\left(-1\right)^{p_{2}+q}\binom{p_{1}+p_{2}+q-1}{p_{1}-1}\Li_{p_{1}+p_{2}+q}\left(x_{1}\right)-\left(-1\right)^{p_{1}+q}\binom{p_{1}+p_{2}+q-1}{p_{2}-1}\Li_{p_{1}+p_{2}+q}\left(x_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}=q-1,\atop k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}\geq 0}\left(\left(-1\right)^{k_{1}}\Li_{k_{1}+1}\left(x\right)-\Li_{k_{1}+1}\left(x^{-1}\right)\right)\left(\binom{k_{2}+p_{1}-1}{p_{1}-1}\left(-1\right)^{k_{2}}\Li_{k_{2}+p_{1}}\left(x_{1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left(\binom{k_{3}+p_{2}-1}{p_{2}-1}\left(-1\right)^{k_{3}}\Li_{k_{3}+p_{2}}\left(x_{2}\right)\right)+\left(-1\right)^{p_{1}+p_{2}+q}\Li_{p_{1}+p_{2}+q}\left(x\right)+\Li_{p_{1}+p_{2}+q}\left(x^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}=p_{1}+q-1,\atop k_{1},k_{2}\geq 0}\left(\left(-1\right)^{k_{1}}\Li_{k_{1}+1}\left(x\right)-\Li_{k_{1}+1}\left(x^{-1}\right)\right)\left(\binom{k_{2}+p_{2}-1}{p_{2}-1}\left(-1\right)^{k_{2}}\Li_{k_{2}+p_{2}}\left(x_{2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}=p_{2}+q-1,\atop k_{1},k_{2}\geq 0}\left(\left(-1\right)^{k_{1}}\Li_{k_{1}+1}\left(x\right)-\Li_{k_{1}+1}\left(x^{-1}\right)\right)\left(\binom{k_{2}+p_{1}-1}{p_{1}-1}\left(-1\right)^{k_{2}}\Li_{k_{2}+p_{1}}\left(x_{1}\right)\right).

(4.24)

Proof.

Letting

\oint\limits_{\left(\infty\right)}F_{p_{1}p_{2},q}(s)ds:=\oint\limits_{\left(\infty\right)}\displaystyle\frac{\Phi(s;x)\phi^{(p_{1}-1)}(s;x_{1})\phi^{(p_{2}-1)}(s;x_{2})}{(p_{1}-1)!(p_{2}-1)!s^{q}}(-1)^{p_{1}+p_{2}}ds=0.

F_{p_{1}p_{2},q}(s)
only singularities are poles at the integers. At a positive integer
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the pole
s=n
is simple and by the expansions (
2

2.14

\displaystyle{\rm Res}\left(F_{p_{1},p_{2},q}(s),n\right)=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim\limits_{s\to n}\left(s-n\right)x^{-n}\left(\displaystyle\frac{1}{s-n}+\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\Big((-1)^{m}\Li_{m+1}(x)-\Li_{m+1}\Big(x^{-1}\Big)\Big)(s-n)^{m}\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim\limits_{s\to n}\left(xx_{1}x_{2}\right)^{-n}\left(\Li_{p_{1}}(x_{1})-\zeta_{n-1}\Big(p_{1};x_{1}\Big)\right)\left(\Li_{p_{2}}(x_{2})-\zeta_{n-1}\Big(p_{2};x_{2}\Big)\right)s^{-q}

\displaystyle\left(xx_{1}x_{2}\right)^{-n}\left(\Li_{p_{1}}(x_{1})-\zeta_{n-1}\Big(p_{1};x_{1}\Big)\right)\left(\Li_{p_{2}}(x_{2})-\zeta_{n-1}\Big(p_{2};x_{2}\Big)\right)n^{-q}.

n

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left((-1)^{k}\Li_{k+p_{2}}(x_{2})+(-1)^{p_{2}}\zeta_{n}\Big(k+p_{2};x_{2}^{-1}\Big)\right)\left(-1\right)^{q}/n^{p_{1}+q-k}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\binom{k_{2}+p_{2}-1}{p_{2}-1}\left((-1)^{k_{2}}\Li_{k_{2}+p_{2}}(x_{2})+(-1)^{p_{2}}\zeta_{n}\Big(k_{2}+p_{2};x_{2}^{-1}\Big)\right)/n^{p_{1}+q-k_{1}-k_{2}-1}

\displaystyle\quad+\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq k_{1}+k_{2}\leq p_{2}-1}\left(-1\right)^{q}\binom{q+p_{2}-k_{1}-k_{2}-2}{q-1}\Big((-1)^{k_{1}}\Li_{k_{1}+1}(x)-\Li_{k_{1}+1}\Big(x^{-1}\Big)\Big)\left(xx_{1}x_{2}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\binom{k_{2}+p_{1}-1}{p_{1}-1}\left((-1)^{k_{2}}\Li_{k_{2}+p_{1}}(x_{1})+(-1)^{p_{1}}\zeta_{n}\Big(k_{2}+p_{1};x_{1}^{-1}\Big)\right)/n^{p_{2}+q-k_{1}-k_{2}-1}

\displaystyle\quad+\left(-1\right)^{q}\left(xx_{1}x_{2}\right)^{n}\left(\Li_{p_{1}}\left(x_{1}\right)+\left(-1\right)^{p_{1}}\zeta_{n}\left(p_{1};x_{1}^{-1}\right)\right)\left(\Li_{p_{2}}\left(x_{2}\right)+\left(-1\right)^{p_{2}}\zeta_{n}\left(p_{2};x_{2}^{-1}\right)\right)/n^{q}

s=0

p_{1}+p_{2}+q+1

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
x,x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}
be roots of unity, and
p_{1},\ldots,p_{r},q\geq 1
with
(p_{j},x_{j})\ (j=1,2,\ldots,r)
and
(q,xx_{1}\cdots x_{r})\neq(1,1)
. We have

\displaystyle(-1)^{r}S_{p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{r};q}\Big(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{r};(xx_{1}\cdots x_{r})^{-1}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{p_{1}+\cdots+p_{r}+q}S_{p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{r};q}\Big(x_{1}^{-1},x_{2}^{-1},\ldots,x_{r}^{-1};xx_{1}\cdots x_{r}\Big)

reduces to a combination of sums of lower orders.

Proof.

To prove this general theorem, it is necessary to consider the residue computations of the following contour integral:

\displaystyle\oint\limits_{\left(\infty\right)}F_{p_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{r},q}(s)ds:=\oint\limits_{\left(\infty\right)}\displaystyle\frac{\Phi(s;x)\phi^{(p_{1}-1)}(s;x_{1})\cdots\phi^{(p_{r}-1)}(s;x_{r})}{(p_{1}-1)!\cdots(p_{r}-1)!s^{q}}(-1)^{p_{1}+\cdots+p_{r}-r}ds=0.

Clearly, all integers are poles of the integrand in the entire complex plane, with
s=n\ (n\in\mathbb{N})
being simple poles,
s=-n\ (n\in\mathbb{N})
being poles of order
p_{1}+\cdots+p_{r}+1
, and
s=0
being a pole of order
p_{1}+\cdots+p_{r}+q+1
. Applying Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left({\rm Res}\left(F_{p_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{r},q}(s),n\right)+{\rm Res}\left(F_{p_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{r},q}(s),-n\right)\right)+{\rm Res}\left(F_{p_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{r},q}(s),0\right)=0.

(5.29)

Applying (
2
)-(
2.14
) to compute the residues and then substituting these residue values into (
5.29
) yields

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\zeta_{n-1}(p_{1};x_{1})\zeta_{n-1}(p_{2};x_{2})\cdots\zeta_{n-1}(p_{r};x_{r})}{n^{q}}(xx_{1}\cdots x_{r})^{-n}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{p_{1}+\cdots+p_{r}+q}\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\zeta_{n}(p_{1};x^{-1}_{1})\zeta_{n}(p_{2};x^{-1}_{2})\cdots\zeta_{n}(p_{r};x^{-1}_{r})}{n^{q}}(xx_{1}\cdots x_{r})^{n}

\displaystyle\quad+\{\text{combinations of lower-order sums}\}=0.

Using the identity
\zeta_{n-1}(p;x)=\zeta_{n}(p;x)-x^{n}/n^{p}
and the definition of cyclotomic Euler sums, the theorem can be proved through elementary calculations.
∎

Remark 5.7

The method presented in this paper can also be applied to investigate many other problems related to Dirichlet series of various forms. For instance, by considering the contour integral

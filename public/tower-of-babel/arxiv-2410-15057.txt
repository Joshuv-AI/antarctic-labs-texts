Asymptotic Time-Uniform Inference for Parameters in Averaged Stochastic Approximation
Chuhan Xie, Kaicheng Jin, Jiadong Liang, Zhihua Zhang

Abstract
We study time-uniform statistical inference for parameters in stochastic approximation (SA), which encompasses a bunch of applications in optimization and machine learning. To that end, we analyze the almost-sure convergence rates of the averaged iterates to a scaled sum of Gaussians in both linear and nonlinear SA problems. We then construct three types of asymptotic confidence sequences that are valid uniformly across all times with coverage guarantees, in an asymptotic sense that the starting time is sufficiently large. These coverage guarantees remain valid if the unknown covariance matrix is replaced by its plug-in estimator, and we conduct experiments to validate our methodology.



1
Introduction

Traditional statistical inference for parameters is based on asymptotic/non-asymptotic coverage guarantees at fixed sample sizes. Such guarantees tend to become problematic in sequential experimental design due to the issue of “peeking”, i.e., experimenters deciding whether to collect more data for further experiments after looking at current results in order to make the outcome more significant
(
Feller,, 1940
;
Anscombe,, 1954
;
Robbins,, 1952
)
.
Recently, there has been an emerging literature on safe anytime-valid inference
(
Johari et al.,, 2022
;
Howard et al.,, 2021
;
Grünwald et al.,, 2020
;
Shafer,, 2021
;
Pace and Salvan,, 2020
;
Ramdas et al.,, 2023
)
that partially solve such problems. At a high level, they utilize a supermartingale method coupled with Ville’s inequality
(
Ville,, 1939
)
to obtain time-uniform coverage guarantees for the quantities of interest, which is also equivalent to coverage guarantees for arbitrary stopping times
(
Ramdas et al.,, 2020
)
:

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(\forall t\geq 1\colon\theta_{t}\in\operatorname{CI}_{t})\geq 1-\alpha\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad{\mathbb{P}}(\theta_{\tau}\in\operatorname{CI}_{\tau})\geq 1-\alpha\quad\text{for all stopping times }\tau>0.

With such guarantees, the corresponding statistical inference procedure remains valid even in the presence of peeking, since the experimenter’s decision of whether to stop conducting further experiments can always be regarded as a stopping time.
This technique has been applied to a series of traditional statistical tasks including mean estimation and hypothesis tests
(
Darling and Robbins,, 1968
;
Howard et al.,, 2021
;
Waudby-Smith and Ramdas,, 2024
;
Ramdas et al.,, 2023
)
.

This work takes a step forward to study time-uniform statistical inference in the stochastic approximation (SA) framework, which refers to a wide class of online iterative algorithms and is prevalent in modern machine learning applications such as stochastic gradient descent (SGD) and reinforcement learning (RL) due to memory and efficiency superiority over offline methods. Specifically, the SA iterates
\{{\bm{x}}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
follow the recursive stochastic updates as (
2.1
) below. Despite recent progress on statistical inference for SA
(
Chen et al.,, 2020
;
Fang et al.,, 2018
;
Su and Zhu,, 2023
;
Lee et al.,, 2022
)
, none of them provide time-uniform results and therefore they still suffer from the peeking issue. The goal of this paper is to provide asymptotic time-uniform coverage guarantees of the following form:

\displaystyle\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}{\mathbb{P}}(\forall t\geq m\colon\bar{{\bm{x}}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}^{*}\in\operatorname{CI}_{t})\geq 1-\alpha,

where
\bar{{\bm{x}}}_{t}:=t^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{t}{\bm{x}}_{t}
is the averaged iterate and
{\bm{x}}^{*}
is the true parameter. As a direct consequence, for sequential experiments on e-commerce platforms or in clinical trials where the parameters of interest are updated iteratively, our results provide confidence sequences for the true parameter with asymptotically valid error control regardless of the data collection strategy of experimenters.

1.1
Our Contributions

Our theoretical contributions mainly lie in the following three parts.

1.

We establish an almost-sure Gaussian approximation result for the averaged iterates
\{\bar{{\bm{x}}}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
to a scaled sum of i.i.d. Gaussians with precise convergence rates (Theorem
3.1
), and provide a refined analysis on the relationship between the hyperparameters and these rates (Section
3.2
) as well as the optimal rates (Section
3.3
) in both linear and nonlinear cases.

2.

We devise three confidence sequence boundaries for the scaled sum of i.i.d. Gaussians and provide time-uniform coverage guarantees (Section
4.1
).

3.

For practical inference, we derive the almost-sure convergence for the plug-in estimator of the unknown covariance matrix that is used in constructing confidence sequences (Section
4.2
), and prove that asymptotic time-uniform coverage guarantees still hold when replacing this covariance matrix with its plug-in analogue (Section
4.3
).

We perform numerical experiments in Section
5
to validate our methodology. Our idea stems from the time-uniform central limit theory proposed in
Waudby-Smith et al., (2021)
, which highlights the necessity of “almost-sure” convergence of all quantities of interest, instead of usual “in-probability” or “in-law” ones. This requirement leads us to derive brand new convergence results mentioned above in the SA framework.

1.2
Related Work

Anytime-valid inference.

The idea of confidence sequences is initiated by
Darling and Robbins, (1967)
, which provided a sequential estimation approach for the median. A more recent work
Howard et al., (2021)
gave comprehensive analysis and constructions of confidence sequences for the mean based on time-uniform concentration bounds from
Howard et al., (2020)
. Similar techniques were adopted in
Howard and Ramdas, (2022)
for quantile estimation and in
Waudby-Smith and Ramdas, (2024)
for bounded mean estimation. Another line of research, nonparametric sequential testing, goes back to
Wald, (1945)
. Based on the test martingale method from
Shafer et al., (2011)
,
Grünwald et al., (2020)
developed a theory of safe hypothesis testing and proposed general methods for constructing tests with optimal powers. Subsequent works include
Ramdas et al., (2020)
for testing symmetry,
Ramdas et al., (2022)
for testing exchangeability, etc.

Statistical inference for stochastic approximation.

Based on the seminal work of
Polyak and Juditsky, (1992)
,
Chen et al., (2020)
studied asymptotic normality of averaged iterates from SGD, and proposed the plug-in and batch-means estimators for the asymptotic covariance matrix in order to perform statistical inference on model parameters.

Zhu et al., (2023)
developed a fully online batch-means method which improves computation and memory efficiency over the original one in
Chen et al., (2020)
.
Fang et al., (2018)
proposed an online bootstrap procedure for the estimation of confidence intervals via a number of randomly perturbed SGD iterates.

Su and Zhu, (2018)
designed a hierarchical incremental gradient descent method termed HiGrad that hierarchically split SGD updates into multiple threads and constructed a
t
-based confidence interval for the parameter.
Lee et al., (2022)
leveraged insights from time series regression in econometrics
(
Abadir and Paruolo,, 1997
;
Kiefer et al.,, 2000
)
and constructed asymptotic pivotal statistics for parameter inference via random scaling, which was extended to different machine learning scenarios in
Li et al., (2022)
;
Li et al., 2023b ()
;
Li et al., 2023a ()
.

Chen et al., 2021a ()
;
Chen et al., 2021b ()
;
Chen et al., (2022)
studied online statistical inference in the contextual bandit settings and analyzed the performance and efficiency of weighted versions of SGD.

2
Problem Setup

We begin by introducing the problem setup. We are concerned with solving the root-finding problem
{\bm{g}}({\bm{x}}^{*})={\bf 0}
, where
{\bm{g}}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
is expressed as an expectation over the data point
\xi
; i.e.,
{\bm{g}}({\bm{x}})=\mathbb{E}[{\bm{G}}({\bm{x}},\xi)]
. Supposing we only have access to a sequence of i.i.d. data points
\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\dots
, a typical stochastic approximation (SA) algorithm is given by the following
d
-dimensional recursion:

\displaystyle{\bm{x}}_{t+1}={\bm{x}}_{t}-\eta_{t}{\bm{G}}({\bm{x}}_{t},\xi_{t+1}),\quad t=0,1,2,\dots

(2.1)

Starting from an initializer
{\bm{x}}_{0}
, the iterates
\{{\bm{x}}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
will converge to the unique root
{\bm{x}}^{*}
and possess favorable statistical properties under certain assumptions that we are about to display.

Assumption 2.1

(Lyapunov function)
.

There exist a differentiable function
V\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and a constant
\delta_{V}>0
such that for all
\|{\bm{x}}-{\bm{x}}^{*}\|\leq\delta_{V}
and
{\bm{y}},{\bm{z}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the following conditions hold.

1.

V({\bf 0})=0
.

2.

V({\bm{x}}-{\bm{x}}^{*})\geq\mu_{V}\|{\bm{x}}-{\bm{x}}^{*}\|^{2}
for some
\mu_{V}>0
.

3.

\|\nabla V({\bm{y}})-\nabla V({\bm{z}})\|\leq L_{V}\|{\bm{y}}-{\bm{z}}\|
for some
L_{V}>0
.

4.

\nabla V({\bm{x}}-{\bm{x}}^{*})^{\top}{\bm{g}}({\bm{x}})>0
for
{\bm{x}}\neq{\bm{x}}^{*}
.

5.

\nabla V({\bm{x}}-{\bm{x}}^{*})^{\top}{\bm{g}}({\bm{x}})\geq\lambda_{V}V({\bm{x}}-{\bm{x}}^{*})
for some
\lambda_{V}>0
.

Assumption 2.2

(Local linearity)
.

There exist a matrix
{\bm{H}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
and constants
L_{H}>0
,
\delta_{H}>0
,
0<\lambda\leq 1
such that for all
\|{\bm{x}}-{\bm{x}}^{*}\|\leq\delta_{H}
,

\displaystyle\|{\bm{g}}({\bm{x}})-{\bm{H}}({\bm{x}}-{\bm{x}}^{*})\|\leq L_{H}\|{\bm{x}}-{\bm{x}}^{*}\|^{1+\lambda},

and that
\operatorname{Re}\lambda_{i}({\bm{H}})>0
for all
1\leq i\leq d
, where
\lambda_{i}(\cdot)
denotes the
i
-th eigenvalue.

Assumption 2.3

(Lipschitzness)
.

There exists a constant
L_{G}>0
such that for any
{\bm{x}},{\bm{y}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\big(\|{\bm{G}}({\bm{x}},\xi)-{\bm{G}}({\bm{y}},\xi)\|^{2}\big)}\leq L_{G}\|{\bm{x}}-{\bm{y}}\|.

Assumption 2.4

(Step size)
.

The step size is
\eta_{t}=\eta_{0}t^{-a}
for some constant
1/2<a<1
.

Assumption 2.5

(Noise)
.

For each
t\geq 1
, let
{\bm{\varepsilon}}_{t}:={\bm{g}}({\bm{x}}_{t-1})-{\bm{G}}({\bm{x}}_{t-1},\xi_{t})
. The martingale difference sequence
\{{\bm{\varepsilon}}_{t}\}_{t\geq 1}
satisfies the following conditions.

1.

For all
t\geq 1
,
\mathbb{E}(\|{\bm{\varepsilon}}_{t}\|^{2}\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1})+\|{\bm{g}}({\bm{x}}_{t-1})\|^{2}\leq L_{\varepsilon}(1+\|{\bm{x}}_{t-1}-{\bm{x}}^{*}\|^{2})
almost surely for some constant
L_{\varepsilon}>0
.

2.

The following decomposition holds:
{\bm{\varepsilon}}_{t}={\bm{\varepsilon}}_{t}(0)+{\bm{\zeta}}_{t}({\bm{x}}_{t-1})
, where

(a)

\mathbb{E}({\bm{\varepsilon}}_{t}(0)\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1})=0
almost surely;

(b)

\mathbb{E}({\bm{\varepsilon}}_{t}(0){\bm{\varepsilon}}_{t}(0)^{\top}\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1})\xrightarrow{P}S
as
t\rightarrow\infty
, where
{\bm{S}}\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d}_{+}
is a symmetric and positive definite matrix;

(c)

\sup_{t}\mathbb{E}(\|{\bm{\varepsilon}}_{t}(0)\|^{2}{\mathbbm{1}}(\|{\bm{\varepsilon}}_{t}(0)\|>C)\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1})\xrightarrow{P}0
as
C\rightarrow\infty
;

(d)

for all
t
large enough,
\mathbb{E}(\|{\bm{\zeta}}_{t}({\bm{x}}_{t-1})\|^{2}\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1})\leq\delta({\bm{x}}_{t-1}-{\bm{x}}^{*})
almost surely, where
\delta({\bm{\Delta}})\rightarrow 0
as
{\bm{\Delta}}\rightarrow 0
.

3.

For all
t\geq 1
,
\mathbb{E}(\|{\bm{\varepsilon}}_{t}\|^{2p})\leq C_{\varepsilon}
and
\mathbb{E}(\|{\bm{G}}({\bm{x}}^{*},\xi)\|^{2p})\leq C_{\varepsilon}
for some constants
C_{\varepsilon}
and
p>1
.

Remark 2.1
.

Assumptions
2.1
-
2.5
are analogous to Assumptions 3.1-3.4 of
Polyak and Juditsky, (1992)
, which are standard in the SA literature. Assumptions
2.1
-
2.3
are restrictions on
{\bm{g}}({\bm{x}})
and
{\bm{G}}({\bm{x}},\xi)
to ensure convergence of the SA algorithm (
2.1
). In Assumption
2.4
we use a standard polynomial step size schedule that satisfies the Robbins-Monro conditions
(
Robbins and Monro,, 1951
)
, i.e.,
\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\eta_{t}=\infty
and
\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\eta_{t}^{2}<\infty
.
Assumption
2.5
contains conditions on the scale of noise and is essential to establishing the asymptotic behavior of the averaged iterates. The SA framework includes a bunch of examples including linear regression and logistic regression, which are studied in our experiments (Section
5
).

3
Almost-Sure Gaussian Approximation

In the following Theorem
3.1
, we approximate the averaged iterate
\bar{{\bm{x}}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}^{*}
by a sum of i.i.d. Gaussians in an almost-sure sense, and characterize the corresponding approximation rate. Its detailed proof is deferred to Appendix
A.1
.

Theorem 3.1

(Gaussian approximation)
.

Let
\epsilon>0
be an arbitrarily small constant. Under Assumptions
2.1
-
2.5
, there exists a sequence of
d
-dimensional i.i.d. Gaussian random vectors
\{{\bm{G}}_{t}\}_{t\geq 1}
with mean zero and covariance
{\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{S}}{\bm{H}}^{-1}
on the same (potentially enriched) probability space, such that

\displaystyle\bar{{\bm{x}}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}^{*}=\frac{1}{t}\sum_{j=1}^{t}{\bm{G}}_{j}+o(r_{t,\epsilon}+w_{t,\epsilon,d})\quad\text{a.s.},

where

\displaystyle r_{t,\epsilon}=t^{-1}(\log t)^{\epsilon}+t^{-\frac{a(1+\lambda)}{2}}(\log t)^{1+\epsilon}+t^{\frac{1}{2p}-\frac{2-a}{2}}(\log t)^{\frac{1}{2p}+\epsilon}+t^{-\frac{1+a}{2}}(\log t)^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon},

\displaystyle w_{t,\epsilon,1}=t^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{p-1}{4p}}(\log t)^{1+\frac{1}{4p}+\epsilon},\quad w_{t,\epsilon,d}=t^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{p-1}{50dp}}(\log t)^{\frac{1}{50dp}+\epsilon}\quad\text{for }d>1.

Compared with the (functional) central limit theorems for averaged iterates in the literature
(
Polyak and Juditsky,, 1992
;
Chen et al.,, 2020
;
Chen et al.,, 2024
;
Lee et al.,, 2022
;
Lee et al.,, 2024
;
Chen et al.,, 2023
;
Li et al.,, 2022
;
Li et al., 2023b,
;
Li et al., 2023a,
)
, our Theorem
3.1
provides an almost-sure version instead of a usual in-probability one under some mildly stronger assumptions (see Assumption
3.1
below). In addition, we precisely characterize the approximation rate when
t\rightarrow\infty
, while previous works only verify that it is of order
o(t^{-1/2})
for the sole purpose of establishing asymptotic normality.

3.1
Proof Idea of Theorem
3.1

The high-level idea of its proof is to decompose
\bar{{\bm{x}}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}^{*}
into the following five parts
(
Polyak and Juditsky,, 1992
)
: denoting
{\bm{A}}_{j}^{t-1}=\sum_{s=j}^{t-1}\left(\prod_{i=j+1}^{s}({\bm{I}}-\eta_{i}{\bm{H}})\right)\eta_{j}
,
{\bm{r}}_{j}={\bm{H}}({\bm{x}}_{j}-{\bm{x}}^{*})-{\bm{g}}({\bm{x}}_{j})
,
{\bm{\varepsilon}}_{j+1}={\bm{g}}({\bm{x}}_{j})-{\bm{G}}({\bm{x}}_{j},\xi_{j+1})
and
\tilde{{\bm{\varepsilon}}}_{j+1}={\bm{g}}({\bm{x}}^{*})-{\bm{G}}({\bm{x}}^{*},\xi_{j+1})
, we have

\displaystyle\bar{{\bm{x}}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}^{*}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\frac{1}{t}{\bm{A}}_{0}^{t-1}({\bm{I}}-\eta_{0}{\bm{H}})({\bm{x}}_{0}-{\bm{x}}^{*})}_{{\mathcal{S}}_{1}}+\underbrace{\frac{1}{t}\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}{\bm{A}}_{j}^{t-1}{\bm{r}}_{j}}_{{\mathcal{S}}_{2}}+\underbrace{\frac{1}{t}\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}({\bm{A}}_{j}^{t-1}-{\bm{H}}^{-1}){\bm{\varepsilon}}_{j+1}}_{{\mathcal{S}}_{3}}

\displaystyle\quad+\underbrace{\frac{1}{t}\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}{\bm{H}}^{-1}({\bm{\varepsilon}}_{j+1}-\tilde{{\bm{\varepsilon}}}_{j+1})}_{{\mathcal{S}}_{4}}+\underbrace{\frac{1}{t}\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}{\bm{H}}^{-1}\tilde{{\bm{\varepsilon}}}_{j+1}}_{{\mathcal{S}}_{5}}.

Here,
{\bm{A}}_{j}^{t-1}
can be regarded as an approximation of
{\bm{H}}^{-1}
. Below we discuss how the different rates contained in
r_{t,\epsilon}
and
w_{t,\epsilon,d}
appear from the above five error terms.

Put simply,
{\mathcal{S}}_{1}
is the error caused by inaccurate initialization and is of order
O(t^{-1})
. And
{\mathcal{S}}_{2}
is the error induced when we linearize the expected increment
{\bm{g}}({\bm{x}}_{j})
of an update (
2.1
) by
{\bm{H}}({\bm{x}}_{j}-{\bm{x}}^{*})
. By Assumption
2.2
, when
{\bm{x}}_{j}
is close to
{\bm{x}}^{*}
(which is always true when
j\rightarrow\infty
), this linearization error will decay at a rate similar to that of
\|{\bm{x}}_{j}-{\bm{x}}^{*}\|^{1+\lambda}
, and we rigorously prove it is of order
o\big(t^{-\frac{a(1+\lambda)}{2}}(\log t)^{1+\epsilon}\big)
in Lemma
A.1
.

{\mathcal{S}}_{3}
characterizes the approximation error of
{\bm{A}}_{j}^{t-1}
to
{\bm{H}}^{-1}
and is of order
o\big(t^{\frac{1}{2p}-\frac{2-a}{2}}(\log t)^{\frac{1}{2p}+\epsilon}\big)
, as also proved in Lemma
A.1
. Compared to the corresponding proof in
Polyak and Juditsky, (1992)
(see Lemma 2 and the proof of Theorem 1 therein), in order for the almost-sure convergence rate of
{\mathcal{S}}_{3}
to be faster than
t^{-1/2}
, we additionally need a finite
2p
-th moment condition on the noise, which we formulate as Assumption
2.5
(
3
). This condition also appears in previous works
(
Zhu and Dong,, 2021
;
Lee et al.,, 2022
;
Li et al.,, 2022
;
Li et al., 2023a,
)
concerning weak convergence of the scaled iterates to a Brownian motion (i.e., functional central limit theorems), and is also essential to our proof.

{\mathcal{S}}_{4}
characterizes the error induced when we calibrate the increments of the martingale
\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}{\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{\varepsilon}}_{j+1}
to i.i.d. random variables, sharing the same randomness
\{\xi_{t}\}_{t\geq 1}
and the same limiting covariance
{\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{S}}{\bm{H}}^{-1}
. This is easily achieved by considering the martingale
\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}{\bm{H}}^{-1}\tilde{{\bm{\varepsilon}}}_{j+1}
. Using the averaged Lipschitzness in Assumption
2.3
, again we link the convergence rate of
{\mathcal{S}}_{4}
to that of
\|{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}^{*}\|^{2}
and derive a rate of
o\big(t^{-\frac{1+a}{2}}(\log t)^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}\big)
in Lemma
A.2
.

Finally,
{\mathcal{S}}_{5}
is approximated by i.i.d. Gaussians with the same mean and covariance, using almost-sure invariance principles from
Strassen, (1967)
;
Philipp, (1986)
. For one-dimensional SA problems with
d=1
, the approximation error is of order
o\big(t^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{p-1}{4p}}(\log t)^{1+\frac{1}{4p}+\epsilon}\big)

(
Strassen,, 1967
)
. For high-dimensional SA problems with
d>1
, the error rate is slightly worsened to
o\big(t^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{p-1}{50dp}}(\log t)^{\frac{1}{50dp}+\epsilon}\big)

(
Philipp,, 1986
)
.

Combining the convergence rates of the above five parts finally yields the overall strong approximation rate
o(r_{t,\epsilon}+w_{t,\epsilon,d})
for arbitrarily small
\epsilon>0
.

3.2
Rate Analysis

Based on Theorem
3.1
, we now perform a detailed analysis on the behavior of approximation rates.
In Figure
3.1
below, we plot the negative exponents of these rates separately with respect to
a
(which is the negative exponent of the step size as defined in Assumption
2.4
) for both linear and nonlinear SA problems, resulting in several new findings.

Figure 3.1:
Almost-sure approximation rates for all error terms with respect to the choice of
a
in both linear (Left) and nonlinear (Right) SA settings. Respectively,
{\mathcal{S}}_{1}
represents the initialization error,
{\mathcal{S}}_{2}
represents the linearization error which is absent in the linear case,
{\mathcal{S}}_{3}
represents the matrix-inverse approximation error,
{\mathcal{S}}_{4}
represents the covariance calibration error, and
{\mathcal{S}}_{5}
represents the error due to the almost-sure invariance principles.

Firstly, the linearization error
{\mathcal{S}}_{2}
and the covariance calibration error
{\mathcal{S}}_{4}
decay faster for larger
a
, while the matrix-inverse approximation error
{\mathcal{S}}_{3}
decays slower for larger
a
. This is due to the fact that both
{\mathcal{S}}_{2}
and
{\mathcal{S}}_{4}
are controlled by the
L^{2}
error
\mathbb{E}\|{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}^{*}\|^{2}
. For large
t>0
,
\mathbb{E}{\bm{x}}_{t}
gets very close to
{\bm{x}}^{*}
so the variance of the iterate dominates its bias in
\mathbb{E}\|{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}^{*}\|^{2}
. Since its variance is proportional to that of the increment
-\eta_{t}{\bm{G}}({\bm{x}}_{t},\xi_{t+1})
in (
2.1
), it follows that larger
a
implies smaller
\eta_{t}
and thus faster convergence of
\mathbb{E}\|{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}^{*}\|^{2}
(see Lemma
C.3
for details). On the other hand,
{\mathcal{S}}_{3}
purely depends on the approximation rate of
{\bm{A}}_{j}^{t}
to
{\bm{H}}^{-1}
, which is unrelated to the randomness in data points
\{\xi_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
. From the definition of
{\bm{A}}_{j}^{t}
as well as Lemma
C.5
, smaller
\eta_{t}
will, on the contrary, impede the convergence of
{\bm{A}}_{j}^{t}
.

Secondly, we hope the overall approximation rate
r_{t,\epsilon}+w_{t,\epsilon,d}
to be of order
\tilde{o}(t^{-1/2})
, so that the invariance principle and the law of the iterated logarithm for
\frac{1}{t}\sum_{j=1}^{t}{\bm{G}}_{j}
will also hold for
\bar{{\bm{x}}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}^{*}
. To meet this requirement, the rate of step sizes
a
must satisfy
0<a<\frac{p-1}{p}
in the linear case and
\frac{1}{1+\lambda}<a<\frac{p-1}{p}
in the nonlinear case, the latter of which implicitly requires
p>\frac{1+\lambda}{\lambda}
. In the special case where
\lambda=1
and
p=\infty
, the feasible range for
a
becomes
0<a<1
and
1/2<a<1
, respectively, matching the corresponding conditions in the literature
(
Polyak and Juditsky,, 1992
)
. In the sequel, we add these conditions for
a
to pave the way for further development of time-uniform inference.

Assumption 3.1

(Rate condition)
.

The constants
\lambda,p,a,L_{H}
satisfy one of the following conditions:

1.

(Linear SA)
L_{H}=0
and
0<a<\frac{p-1}{p}
.

2.

(Nonlinear SA)
p>\frac{1+\lambda}{\lambda}
and
\frac{1}{1+\lambda}<a<\frac{p-1}{p}
.

3.3
Optimal Approximation Rates

In this section, we study the best possible approximation rate of the overall errors in Theorem
3.1
. Note that
w_{t,\epsilon,d}
stems from the existing almost-sure invariance principles
(
Strassen,, 1967
;
Philipp,, 1986
)
that are generally hard to improve, so we mainly focus on improving
r_{t,\epsilon}
. According to Figure
3.1
, for linear SA problems, the best possible approximation rate is attained at
a=\frac{p-1}{2p}
, in which case
r_{t,\epsilon}=\widetilde{O}\Big(t^{-\frac{3p-1}{4p}}\Big)
; for nonlinear SA problems, the best possible approximation rate is attained at
a=\frac{2p-1}{(2+\lambda)p}
, in which case
r_{t,\epsilon}=\widetilde{O}\Big(t^{-\frac{(1+\lambda)(2p-1)}{2(2+\lambda)p}}\Big)
. In the special case where
\lambda=1
and
p=\infty
, the optimal approximation rate becomes
r_{t,\epsilon}=\widetilde{O}(t^{-3/4})
for the linear case and
r_{t,\epsilon}=\widetilde{O}(t^{-2/3})
for the nonlinear case, respectively. These are brand new results that characterize the almost-sure convergence rates of the averaged iterates to a scaled sum of i.i.d. random vectors:

\displaystyle\bar{{\bm{x}}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}^{*}=\frac{1}{t}\sum_{j=1}^{t}{\bm{H}}^{-1}\tilde{{\bm{\varepsilon}}}_{j}+\tilde{o}(r_{t})\quad\text{a.s.}

(3.1)

Corollary 3.2

(Linear SA)
.

Suppose Assumption
2.2
holds with
L_{H}=0
, i.e., the problem is linear. Under Assumptions
2.1
-
2.5
and
3.1
, if we set
a=\frac{p-1}{2p}
, then (
3.1
) holds with
r_{t}=t^{-\frac{3p-1}{4p}}
.
In particular, if
\lambda=1
,
p=\infty
, and we set
a=1/2
, then
r_{t}=t^{-3/4}
. The above approximation rates are optimal in the choice of
a
.

Corollary 3.3

(Nonlinear SA)
.

Suppose Assumption
2.2
holds with

. The above approximation rates are optimal in the choice of

.

4
Construction of Asymptotic Confidence Sequences

Having established the almost-sure Gaussian approximation result, it is reasonable to believe that confidence sequences for the well-behaved sum of Gaussians will also serve as confidence sequences for the averaged iterates in some asymptotic sense, since as
t\rightarrow\infty
the above two behaves quite the same except for negligible terms. In this section, we follow this idea to design such asymptotic confidence sequences for the averaged iterates, and provide suitable error guarantees for them.

Proposition 4.1

(Time-Uniform Coverage for Gaussians)
.

Let
{\bm{G}}_{1},{\bm{G}}_{2},\dots
be i.i.d. Gaussian random vectors with mean zero and covariance
{\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{S}}{\bm{H}}^{-1}
, and let
{\bm{M}}_{t}:=\frac{1}{t}\sum_{j=1}^{t}{\bm{G}}_{j}
. Then for any
\clubsuit\in\{\text{LIL-UB},\text{GM},\text{LIL-EN}\}
and any
\alpha\in(0,1)
, it holds that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\forall t\geq 1\colon{\bm{M}}_{t}\in C_{{\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{S}}{\bm{H}}^{-1}}^{\clubsuit}(t)\right)\geq 1-\alpha.

Remark 4.1
.

The results above assume simultaneous inference on all
d
coordinates. To perform inference on only a subset
{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq[d]
of coordinates, it suffices to replace
{\bm{M}}_{t}
with
[{\bm{M}}_{t}]_{\mathcal{S}}
,
{\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{S}}{\bm{H}}^{-1}
with
[{\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{S}}{\bm{H}}^{-1}]_{{\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{S}}}
, and
d
with
|{\mathcal{S}}|
in the forms of confidence sequences in Proposition
4.1
and Table
4.1
.

4.2
Almost-Sure Convergent Variance Estimator

The confidence sequences proposed in Section
4.1
all rely on the covariance
{\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{S}}{\bm{H}}^{-1}
, which is usually unknown a priori. Consequently, to construct practical confidence sequences for time-uniform inference, we need to design consistent estimators for
{\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{S}}{\bm{H}}^{-1}
in an online fashion. We mainly study the plug-in estimator
(
Chen et al.,, 2020
)
, i.e., we estimate
{\bm{H}}
and
{\bm{S}}
with

\displaystyle\widehat{{\bm{H}}}_{t}:=\frac{1}{t}\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}\bar{{\bm{H}}}({\bm{x}}_{j},\xi_{j+1}),\quad\widehat{{\bm{S}}}_{t}:=\frac{1}{t}\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}{\bm{G}}({\bm{x}}_{j},\xi_{j+1}){\bm{G}}({\bm{x}}_{j},\xi_{j+1})^{\top},

and then use
\widehat{{\bm{H}}}_{t}^{-1}\widehat{{\bm{S}}}_{t}\widehat{{\bm{H}}}_{t}^{-1}
to approximate
{\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{S}}{\bm{H}}^{-1}
.

Chen et al., (2020)
proved
L^{1}
convergence rates for
\widehat{{\bm{H}}}_{t}
and
\widehat{{\bm{S}}}_{t}
, but these are insufficient for proving asymptotic time-uniform coverage guarantees
(
Waudby-Smith et al.,, 2021
)
. Instead, we need almost-sure convergence for these estimators, which we present below.

Assumption 4.1

(Unbiased estimate)
.

There exists a random function
\bar{{\bm{H}}}(\cdot,\xi)\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
such that
{\bm{H}}=\mathbb{E}\big(\bar{{\bm{H}}}({\bm{x}}^{*},\xi)\big)
and
\mathbb{E}\big(\|\bar{{\bm{H}}}({\bm{x}}^{*},\xi)\|^{\bar{p}}\big)<\infty
for some
\bar{p}>1
.

Assumption 4.2

(Lipschitzness)
.

There exists a constant
L_{\bar{H}}>0
such that for any
{\bm{x}},{\bm{y}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\big(\|\bar{{\bm{H}}}({\bm{x}},\xi)-\bar{{\bm{H}}}({\bm{y}},\xi)\|^{2}\big)}\leq L_{\bar{H}}\|{\bm{x}}-{\bm{y}}\|.

Proposition 4.2
.

Under Assumptions
2.1
-
2.5
and
3.1
-
4.2
, it holds that

\displaystyle\widehat{{\bm{H}}}_{t}={\bm{H}}+\tilde{o}\Big(t^{-\min\left\{1-\frac{1}{\bar{p}},\frac{a}{2}\right\}}\Big)\quad\text{and}\quad\widehat{{\bm{S}}}_{t}={\bm{S}}+\tilde{o}\big(t^{-\frac{a}{2}}\big)\quad\text{a.s.}

The proof of Proposition
4.2
is deferred to Appendix
A.2
. The next proposition states that the plug-in estimator
\widehat{{\bm{H}}}_{t}^{-1}\widehat{{\bm{S}}}_{t}\widehat{{\bm{H}}}_{t}^{-1}
is a reasonable estimator for
{\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{S}}{\bm{H}}^{-1}
, with the same rate of convergence as
\widehat{{\bm{H}}}_{t}
. Its proof is deferred to Appendix
A.3
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Under Assumptions
2.1
-
2.5
and
3.1
-
4.2
, it holds that

\displaystyle\widehat{{\bm{H}}}_{t}^{-1}\widehat{{\bm{S}}}_{t}\widehat{{\bm{H}}}_{t}^{-1}={\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{S}}{\bm{H}}^{-1}+\tilde{o}\Big(t^{-\min\left\{1-\frac{1}{\bar{p}},\frac{a}{2}\right\}}\Big)\quad\text{a.s.}

4.3
Coverage Guarantees

Combining the pieces above, we arrive at the following coverage guarantee for the averaged iterates.

Theorem 4.4

(Asymptotic Time-Uniform Coverage)
.

Under Assumptions
2.1
-
2.5
and
3.1
, for any
\clubsuit\in\{\text{LIL-UB},\text{GM},\text{LIL-EN}\}
and any
\alpha\in(0,1)
, it holds that

\displaystyle\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}{\mathbb{P}}\left(\forall t\geq m\colon\bar{{\bm{x}}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}^{*}\in C_{\widehat{{\bm{H}}}_{t}^{-1}\widehat{{\bm{S}}}_{t}\widehat{{\bm{H}}}_{t}^{-1}}^{\clubsuit}(t)\right)\geq 1-\alpha.

As opposed to Proposition
4.1
where the coverage rate is above
1-\alpha
for time starting at the beginning, here we only have an “asymptotic” coverage rate
1-\alpha
when
m
goes to infinity in order to compensate for the errors induced by Gaussian approximation and the plug-in error in covariance estimation. However, we show in experiments below that the coverage rate quickly becomes valid after
m\approx 1000
iterations, which is just a warm start of the whole training process.

5
Experiments

In this section we run simulation experiments on two regression problems and illustrate the performance of three confidence sequences.

Linear regression.

For linear model
Y_{i}=X_{i}^{\top}\theta^{*}+\epsilon_{i}
, the loss function is
{\mathcal{L}}(\theta)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(Y_{i}-X_{i}^{\top}\theta)^{2}/2
, so the SGD update corresponds to (
2.1
) with
{\bm{G}}({\bm{x}}_{t},\xi_{t+1})=(Y_{t+1}-X_{t+1}^{\top}\theta_{t})X_{t+1}
. We assume
\theta^{*}=(1,\dots,d)^{\top}
, and generate data as
[X_{i}]_{j}\overset{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim}\operatorname{Unif}(-10,10)
and
\epsilon_{i}\overset{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim}{\mathcal{N}}(0,16)
.

Logistic regression.

For logit model
Y_{i}\sim\operatorname{Ber}(\operatorname{logit}(X_{i}^{\top}\theta^{*}))
, the loss function is
{\mathcal{L}}(\theta)=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big\{Y_{i}\log(1+e^{-X_{i}^{\top}\theta})+(1-Y_{i})\log(1+e^{X_{i}^{\top}\theta})\big\}
, so the SGD update corresponds to (
2.1
) with
{\bm{G}}({\bm{x}}_{t},\xi_{t+1})=X_{t+1}Y_{t+1}-X_{t+1}/{(1+e^{-X_{t+1}^{\top}\theta_{t}})}
. We assume
\theta^{*}=(1,\dots,d)^{\top}
, and generate data as
[X_{i}]_{j}\overset{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim}\operatorname{Unif}(-0.5,0.5)
.

We conduct our experiment with Intel(R) Xeon(R) Gold 6132 CPU @ 2.60GHz. For each model, we perform SGD for
1.5\times 10^{5}
iterations and repeat the trajectory for 1,000 times to compute coverage rates. We illustrate in Figures
5.1
and
5.2
the performance of three CSs as well as the traditional fixed-time CI when
d=1
and
d=5
, respectively. Specifically, we plot the fixed-time coverage rates at each iteration (Column 1), and the time-uniform coverage rates of the whole trajectory before each iteration (Column 2). To mitigate the effect of the approximation errors, we compute such time-uniform coverage rates starting from the 1,000-th iteration. We also plot the CS boundaries (Column 4), and their correspondences where the variance
{\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{S}}{\bm{H}}^{-1}
is replaced by its plug-in estimator (Column 3 and 5). As expected, our CSs are wider than the fixed-width one, and the latter fails to achieve the nominal time-uniform coverage rate. Comparing the boundaries of the CSs, we find the Gaussian mixture boundary (GM) is always the narrowest, achieving around two times of the fixed-time CI length. The boundary based on
\varepsilon
-nets is the widest and often achieves an extremely low coverage rate; this phenomenon is exacerbated in high dimensions. Nevertheless, all three CSs validate the asymptotic coverage guarantee.

6
Concluding Remarks

In this work we have leveraged probabilistic tools from anytime-valid inference to establish asymptotic confidence sequences for the averaged iterates from stochastic approximation algorithms. As a by-product, we have derived almost-sure convergence rates of the averaged iterates to a scaled sum of i.i.d. Gaussians, and discussed the trade-off between those rates from different causes as well as the best possible rate theoretically. We have proposed three asymptotic confidence sequences and established asymptotic type-I error control guarantees for them. Subsequent numerical experiments validate our results.

There are a few interesting directions for future work. The first is to extend our results to the martingale or Markovian noise settings so that algorithms such as bandit learning
(
Chen et al., 2021a,
;
Chen et al., 2021b,
;
Chen et al.,, 2022
)
, policy gradient
(
Sutton et al.,, 1999
)
and actor-critic
(
Konda and Tsitsiklis,, 1999
)
can be fit into our framework. The second is to establish asymptotic confidence sequences for the last iterates
(
Pelletier,, 2000
)
.

Lemma A.1

(Approximation of the averaged iterate process)
.

Let

-
2.5

\displaystyle\bar{{\bm{x}}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}^{*}=\frac{1}{t}\sum_{j=1}^{t}{\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{\varepsilon}}_{j}+o\left(t^{-1}(\log t)^{\epsilon}+t^{-\frac{a(1+\lambda)}{2}}(\log t)^{1+\epsilon}+t^{\frac{1}{2p}-\frac{2-a}{2}}(\log t)^{\frac{1}{2p}+\epsilon}\right)\quad\text{a.s.}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t^{1-a}(\log t)^{1+\epsilon}}\sum_{j=1}^{t}\|{\bm{v}}_{j}{\bm{v}}_{j}^{\top}\|\rightarrow 0\quad\text{a.s.},

which immediately implies
\|{\mathcal{T}}_{3,t}\|=o\big(t^{-1}\cdot t^{1-a}(\log t)^{1+\epsilon}\big)=o\big(t^{-a}(\log t)^{1+\epsilon}\big)
.

For the second term, by Cauchy’s inequality and the boundedness of
\mathbb{E}\|{\bm{u}}_{t}\|^{2}
we have
\mathbb{E}\|{\bm{u}}_{t}{\bm{v}}_{t}^{\top}\|\leq\big(\mathbb{E}\|{\bm{u}}_{t}\|^{2}\mathbb{E}\|{\bm{v}}_{t}\|^{2}\big)^{1/2}\lesssim\big(\mathbb{E}\|{\bm{v}}_{t}\|^{2}\big)^{1/2}
. Thus, by a similar analysis for
{\mathcal{T}}_{3,t}
above, we can obtain
\|{\mathcal{T}}_{2,t}\|=o\big(t^{-\frac{a}{2}}(\log t)^{1+\epsilon}\big)
.

Summing up
{\mathcal{T}}_{1,t},{\mathcal{T}}_{2,t},{\mathcal{T}}_{3,t}
yields the final rate of convergence for
\widehat{{\bm{S}}}_{t}
:

\displaystyle\widehat{{\bm{S}}}_{t}-{\bm{S}}=o\left(t^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\log t)^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}+t^{-\frac{a}{2}}(\log t)^{1+\epsilon}+t^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\log t)^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}\right)=\tilde{o}\left(t^{-\frac{a}{2}}\right).

A.3
Proof of Proposition
4.3

Note that by Proposition
4.2
,
\widehat{{\bm{H}}}_{t}\xrightarrow{a.s.}{\bm{H}}
and
\widehat{{\bm{S}}}_{t}\xrightarrow{a.s.}{\bm{S}}
, and consequently,
\widehat{{\bm{H}}}_{t}
is invertible for sufficiently large
t>0
and
\widehat{{\bm{H}}}_{t}^{-1}\xrightarrow{a.s.}{\bm{H}}^{-1}
. This implies both
\|\widehat{{\bm{H}}}_{t}^{-1}\|
and
\|\widehat{{\bm{S}}}_{t}\|
are bounded with probability one.
Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\|\widehat{{\bm{H}}}^{-1}_{t}-{\bm{H}}^{-1}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|{\bm{H}}^{-1}\|\|\widehat{{\bm{H}}}_{t}-{\bm{H}}\|\|\widehat{{\bm{H}}}_{t}^{-1}\|=\tilde{o}\left(t^{-\min\left\{1-\frac{1}{\bar{p}},\frac{a}{2}\right\}}\right),

and

\displaystyle\quad\ \left\|\widehat{{\bm{H}}}^{-1}_{t}\widehat{{\bm{S}}}_{t}\widehat{{\bm{H}}}^{-1}_{t}-{\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{S}}{\bm{H}}^{-1}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|{\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{S}}(\widehat{{\bm{H}}}^{-1}_{t}-{\bm{H}}^{-1})+{\bm{H}}^{-1}(\widehat{{\bm{S}}}_{t}-{\bm{S}})\widehat{{\bm{H}}}^{-1}_{t}+(\widehat{{\bm{H}}}^{-1}_{t}-{\bm{H}}^{-1})\widehat{{\bm{S}}}_{t}\widehat{{\bm{H}}}^{-1}_{t}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\|{\bm{H}}^{-1}\|\|{\bm{S}}\|\|\widehat{{\bm{H}}}^{-1}_{t}-{\bm{H}}^{-1}\|+\|{\bm{H}}^{-1}\|\|\widehat{{\bm{S}}}_{t}-{\bm{S}}\|\|\widehat{{\bm{H}}}^{-1}_{t}\|+\|\widehat{{\bm{H}}}^{-1}_{t}-{\bm{H}}^{-1}\|\|\widehat{{\bm{S}}}_{t}\|\|\widehat{{\bm{H}}}^{-1}_{t}\|

\displaystyle=\tilde{o}\left(t^{-\min\left\{1-\frac{1}{\bar{p}},\frac{a}{2}\right\}}\right)+\tilde{o}\left(t^{-\frac{a}{2}}\right)+\tilde{o}\left(t^{-\min\left\{1-\frac{1}{\bar{p}},\frac{a}{2}\right\}}\right)=\tilde{o}\left(t^{-\min\left\{1-\frac{1}{\bar{p}},\frac{a}{2}\right\}}\right),

which concludes the proof.

B.2
LIL Boundary with Union Bounds

Proposition B.1

(LIL-UB boundary)
.

Let
{\bm{G}}_{1},{\bm{G}}_{2},\dots
be i.i.d. Gaussian random vectors with mean zero and covariance
{\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{S}}{\bm{H}}^{-1}
, and let
{\bm{M}}_{t}:=\frac{1}{t}\sum_{j=1}^{t}{\bm{G}}_{j}
. Then for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
, it holds that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\exists t\geq 1\colon\left\|\big({\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{S}}{\bm{H}}^{-1}\big)^{-1/2}{\bm{M}}_{t}\right\|_{\infty}\geq 1.7\sqrt{\frac{\log\log(2t)+0.72\log(10.4d/\alpha)}{t}}\right)\leq\alpha.

Proof of Proposition
B.1
.

We first state a lemma on the LIL boundary for Gaussians in the one-dimensional case.

Lemma B.2
.

Let
X_{1},X_{2},\dots
be i.i.d. standard Gaussians, and let
S_{t}:=\sum_{j=1}^{t}X_{j}
. Then for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\exists t\geq 1\colon|S_{t}|\geq 1.7\sqrt{t\left(\log\log(2t)+0.72\log\left(\frac{10.4}{\alpha}\right)\right)}\right)\leq\alpha.

The proof of Lemma
B.2
is trivial by noticing (3.4) of
Howard et al., (2021)
and doubling the constant 5.2 to accommodate the two-sided version of the concentration inequality.

Now we let
\widetilde{{\bm{G}}}_{t}:=\big({\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{S}}{\bm{H}}^{-1}\big)^{-1/2}{\bm{G}}_{t}
and
\widetilde{{\bm{M}}}_{t}:=\frac{1}{t}\sum_{j=1}^{t}\widetilde{{\bm{G}}}_{j}
. Obviously,
\widetilde{{\bm{G}}}_{t}\overset{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim}{\mathcal{N}}({\bf 0},{\bm{I}})
, and thus we can use a union bound to ensure that with high probability, each coordinate of
\widetilde{{\bm{M}}}_{t}
is within the confidence sequence:

\displaystyle\quad\ {\mathbb{P}}\left(\exists t\geq 1\colon\left\|\big({\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{S}}{\bm{H}}^{-1}\big)^{-1/2}{\bm{M}}_{t}\right\|_{\infty}\geq 1.7\sqrt{\frac{\log\log(2t)+0.72\log(10.4d/\alpha)}{t}}\right)

\displaystyle={\mathbb{P}}\left(\exists t\geq 1\colon\left\|\widetilde{{\bm{M}}}_{t}\right\|_{\infty}\geq 1.7\sqrt{\frac{\log\log(2t)+0.72\log(10.4d/\alpha)}{t}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\mathbb{P}}\left(\exists t\geq 1\colon\left|[\widetilde{{\bm{M}}}_{t}]_{i}\right|\geq 1.7\sqrt{\frac{\log\log(2t)+0.72\log(10.4d/\alpha)}{t}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\mathbb{P}}\left(\exists t\geq 1\colon\left|\sum_{j=1}^{t}[\widetilde{{\bm{G}}}_{j}]_{i}\right|\geq 1.7\sqrt{t\left(\log\log(2t)+0.72\log\left(\frac{10.4d}{\alpha}\right)\right)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq d\cdot\frac{\alpha}{d}=\alpha,

which concludes the proof.
∎

Note that Lemma
B.2
is a special case of Theorem 1 of
Howard et al., (2021)
by setting the boundary shape function
h(k)=(k+1)^{s}\zeta(s)
(
\zeta(s)
is the Riemann zeta function) with
s=1.4
, the geometric spacing of intrinsic time
\eta=2
, and the starting intrinsic time of a nontrivial boundary
m=1
(see Section 3.1 of
Howard et al., (2021)
for detailed discussion). These hyperparameter choices will be reused in Appendix
B.4
to facilitate fair comparison.

B.3
Gaussian Mixture Boundary

Proposition B.3

(GM boundary)
.

Let
{\bm{G}}_{1},{\bm{G}}_{2},\dots
be i.i.d. Gaussian random vectors with mean zero and covariance
{\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{S}}{\bm{H}}^{-1}
, and let
{\bm{M}}_{t}:=\frac{1}{t}\sum_{j=1}^{t}{\bm{G}}_{j}
. Then for any positive definite matrix
{\bm{\Sigma}}\in{\mathbb{S}}_{+}^{d}
and any
\alpha\in(0,1)
, it holds that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\exists t\geq 1\colon\|{\bm{M}}_{t}\|_{\{{\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{S}}{\bm{H}}^{-1}+{\bm{\Sigma}}^{-1}/t\}^{-1}}\geq\sqrt{\frac{\log\det\{t{\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{S}}{\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{\Sigma}}+{\bm{I}}\}+2\log(1/\alpha)}{t}}\right)\leq\alpha.

Proof of Proposition
B.3
.

Note that for any martingale
M_{t}(\lambda)
, we have that
\int M_{t}(\lambda){\mathrm{d}}F(\lambda)
is also a martingale where
F
is any probability distribution
(
Howard et al.,, 2020
;
Howard et al.,, 2021
)
. We use the density of a
d
-dimensional Gaussian
{\mathcal{N}}({\bf 0},{\bm{\Sigma}})
, to mix the following exponential martingale:

\displaystyle M_{t}(\lambda):=\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{t}\langle{\bm{\lambda}},{\bm{G}}_{j}\rangle-\frac{t}{2}\langle{\bm{\lambda}},{\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{S}}{\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{\lambda}}\rangle\right).

Let
\widetilde{{\bm{\Sigma}}}_{t}^{-1}:=t{\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{S}}{\bm{H}}^{-1}+{\bm{\Sigma}}^{-1}
. Direct calculation yields

\displaystyle\int M_{t}(\lambda){\mathrm{d}}F(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}|\det({\bm{\Sigma}})|^{1/2}}\int\exp\left(t{\bm{\lambda}}^{\top}{\bm{M}}_{t}-\frac{t}{2}{\bm{\lambda}}^{\top}{\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{S}}{\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{\lambda}}-\frac{1}{2}{\bm{\lambda}}^{\top}{\bm{\Sigma}}^{-1}{\bm{\lambda}}\right){\mathrm{d}}{\bm{\lambda}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}|\det({\bm{\Sigma}})|^{1/2}}\int\exp\left(t{\bm{\lambda}}^{\top}{\bm{M}}_{t}-\frac{1}{2}{\bm{\lambda}}^{\top}\widetilde{{\bm{\Sigma}}}_{t}^{-1}{\bm{\lambda}}\right){\mathrm{d}}{\bm{\lambda}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}|\det({\bm{\Sigma}})|^{1/2}}\int\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\left({\bm{\lambda}}-t\widetilde{{\bm{\Sigma}}}_{t}{\bm{M}}_{t}\right)^{\top}\widetilde{{\bm{\Sigma}}}_{t}^{-1}\left({\bm{\lambda}}-t\widetilde{{\bm{\Sigma}}}_{t}{\bm{M}}_{t}\right)+\frac{t^{2}}{2}{\bm{M}}_{t}^{\top}\widetilde{{\bm{\Sigma}}}_{t}{\bm{M}}_{t}\right){\mathrm{d}}{\bm{\lambda}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(2\pi)^{d/2}|\det(\widetilde{{\bm{\Sigma}}}_{t})|^{1/2}}{(2\pi)^{d/2}|\det({\bm{\Sigma}})|^{1/2}}\exp\left(\frac{t^{2}}{2}{\bm{M}}_{t}^{\top}\widetilde{{\bm{\Sigma}}}_{t}{\bm{M}}_{t}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\exp\left((t^{2}/2)\cdot{\bm{M}}_{t}^{\top}\{t{\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{S}}{\bm{H}}^{-1}+{\bm{\Sigma}}^{-1}\}^{-1}{\bm{M}}_{t}\right)}{|\det({\bm{\Sigma}})|^{1/2}|\det\{t{\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{S}}{\bm{H}}^{-1}+{\bm{\Sigma}}^{-1}\}|^{1/2}}.

Since the above term in the last line is a nonnegative martingale, by Ville’s inequality (which we present as Lemma
C.12
), we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\exists t\geq 1\colon\frac{\exp\left((t^{2}/2)\cdot{\bm{M}}_{t}^{\top}\{t{\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{S}}{\bm{H}}^{-1}+{\bm{\Sigma}}^{-1}\}^{-1}{\bm{M}}_{t}\right)}{|\det({\bm{\Sigma}})|^{1/2}|\det\{t{\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{S}}{\bm{H}}^{-1}+{\bm{\Sigma}}^{-1}\}|^{1/2}}\geq\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)\leq\alpha\quad\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\exists t\geq 1\colon\|{\bm{M}}_{t}\|_{\{{\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{S}}{\bm{H}}^{-1}+{\bm{\Sigma}}^{-1}/t\}^{-1}}\geq\sqrt{\frac{\log\det\{t{\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{S}}{\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{\Sigma}}+{\bm{I}}\}+2\log(1/\alpha)}{t}}\right)\leq\alpha,

(B.1)

which concludes the proof.
∎

The boundary in Proposition
B.3
contains a hyperparameter
{\bm{\Sigma}}
. In Corollary
B.4
below we optimize over
{\bm{\Sigma}}
to obtain a neater result.

Corollary B.4

(GM boundary)
.

Let
W_{-1}(x)
be the lower branch of the Lambert
W
function, i.e., the most negative real-valued solution in
z
to
ze^{z}=x
, and let
\lambda^{*}:=-W_{-1}(-\alpha^{2}/e)-1
.
Then for fixed
t_{0}>0
, choosing
{\bm{\Sigma}}=(\lambda^{*}/t_{0})({\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{S}}{\bm{H}}^{-1})^{-1}
in Proposition
B.3
minimizes the volume of its confidence region at time
t_{0}
, and the probabilistic guarantee becomes

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\exists t\geq 1\colon\|{\bm{M}}_{t}\|_{({\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{S}}{\bm{H}}^{-1})^{-1}}\geq\sqrt{\frac{\{1+t_{0}/(\lambda^{*}t)\}\{d\log(1+\lambda^{*}t/t_{0})+2\log(1/\alpha)\}}{t}}\right)\leq\alpha.

Proof of Corollary
B.4
.

In this proof, we optimize over
{\bm{\Sigma}}
in Proposition
B.3
to minimize the volume of the confidence region at a fixed time
t_{0}>0
. Note that the confidence region at time
t_{0}
implied by (
B.1
) is a
d
-dimensional ellipsoid, so by Lemma
C.13
, its volume is

\displaystyle\frac{2}{d}\frac{\pi^{d/2}}{\Gamma(d/2)}\frac{|\det(t_{0}{\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{S}}{\bm{H}}^{-1}+{\bm{\Sigma}}^{-1})|^{1/2}\{\log\det(t_{0}{\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{S}}{\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{\Sigma}}+{\bm{I}})+2\log(1/\alpha)\}^{d/2}}{t_{0}^{d}}.

(B.2)

For notational simplicity, denote
{\bm{A}}:=t_{0}{\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{S}}{\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{\Sigma}}
. The numerator of the last term in (
B.2
) can be written as

\displaystyle\quad\ |\det(({\bm{A}}+{\bm{I}}){\bm{\Sigma}}^{-1})|^{1/2}\{\log\det({\bm{A}}+{\bm{I}})+2\log(1/\alpha)\}^{d/2}

\displaystyle=|\det(({\bm{A}}+{\bm{I}}){\bm{A}}^{-1}\cdot t_{0}{\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{S}}{\bm{H}}^{-1})|^{1/2}\{\log\det({\bm{A}}+{\bm{I}})+2\log(1/\alpha)\}^{d/2}

\displaystyle=|\det(t_{0}{\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{S}}{\bm{H}}^{-1})|^{1/2}\cdot\frac{|\det({\bm{A}}+{\bm{I}})|^{1/2}}{|\det{\bm{A}}|^{1/2}}\{\log\det({\bm{A}}+{\bm{I}})+2\log(1/\alpha)\}^{d/2}.

(B.3)

Let
{\bm{U}}{\bm{\Lambda}}{\bm{U}}^{\top}
be the eigenvalue decomposition of
{\bm{A}}
where
{\bm{\Lambda}}=\mathrm{diag}\{\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{d}\}
. Then the product of the last two terms in (
B.3
) can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{\prod_{i=1}^{d}(1+\lambda_{i})}}{\sqrt{\prod_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}}}\left\{\log\prod_{i=1}^{d}(1+\lambda_{i})+2\log(1/\alpha)\right\}^{d/2}.

(B.4)

We claim that when
\prod_{i=1}^{d}(1+\lambda_{i})
is fixed,
\prod_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}
attains its maximum at
\lambda_{1}=\dots=\lambda_{d}
. To see this, note that for any
i\neq j
, holding
(1+\lambda_{i})(1+\lambda_{j})=c_{ij}
constant, we have

\displaystyle c_{ij}=1+\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}+\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}\geq 1+2\sqrt{\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}}+\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j},

and the equality holds if and only if
\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{j}
. So when
\lambda_{i}\neq\lambda_{j}
, we can always modify them to equal
\lambda_{i}^{\prime}=\lambda_{j}^{\prime}=\sqrt{c_{ij}}-1
to improve the value of
\prod_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}
.

Setting
\lambda_{i}
’s to be equal, (
B.4
) further simplifies to

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1+\lambda}{\lambda}\right)^{d/2}\left\{d\log(1+\lambda)+2\log(1/\alpha)\right\}^{d/2}.

(B.5)

The minimum of (
B.5
) is achieved at
\lambda^{*}=-W_{-1}(-\alpha^{2}/e)-1
, where
W_{-1}(x)
denotes the lower branch of the Lambert
W
function, i.e., the most negative real-valued solution in
z
to
ze^{z}=x
.

Now we turn back to the construction of
{\bm{\Sigma}}
. The optimal choice for
{\bm{\Lambda}}
is
\lambda^{*}{\bm{I}}
as discussed above. In addition, the choice of
{\bm{U}}
does not affect the value in (
B.3
), so without loss of generality we set it as
{\bm{I}}
. In this case, we obtain

\displaystyle{\bm{\Sigma}}=\frac{1}{t_{0}}({\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{S}}{\bm{H}}^{-1})^{-1}{\bm{A}}=\frac{\lambda^{*}}{t_{0}}({\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{S}}{\bm{H}}^{-1})^{-1}=\frac{-W_{-1}(-\alpha^{2}/e)-1}{t_{0}}({\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{S}}{\bm{H}}^{-1})^{-1},

which concludes the proof.
∎

B.4
LIL Boundary with
\varepsilon
-Nets

Proposition B.5

(LIL-EN boundary)
.

Let
{\bm{G}}_{1},{\bm{G}}_{2},\dots
be i.i.d. Gaussian random vectors with mean zero and covariance
{\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{S}}{\bm{H}}^{-1}
, and let
{\bm{M}}_{t}:=\frac{1}{t}\sum_{j=1}^{t}{\bm{G}}_{j}
. Then for any
\epsilon\in(0,1)
and any
\alpha\in(0,1)
, it holds that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\exists t\geq 1\colon\left\|\big({\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{S}}{\bm{H}}^{-1}\big)^{-1/2}{\bm{M}}_{t}\right\|_{2}\geq\frac{2}{1-\epsilon}\sqrt{\frac{1.4\log\log(2\bar{\kappa}t)+\bar{L}_{d,\epsilon,\alpha}}{t}}\right)\leq\alpha,

where
\bar{\kappa}
is the conditional number of
{\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{S}}{\bm{H}}^{-1}
, and

\displaystyle\bar{L}_{d,\epsilon,\alpha}=\log\left(\frac{5.2C_{d}}{\alpha}\right)+(d-1)\log\left(\frac{3\sqrt{\bar{\kappa}}}{\epsilon}\right),\quad C_{d}=\frac{d2^{d}\Gamma(\frac{d+1}{2})}{\pi^{\frac{d-1}{2}}}.

Proof of Proposition
B.5
.

The proof follows the same spirit of Theorem 4.1 of
Whitehouse et al., (2023)
with slight modification. We start from a univariate boundary result.

Lemma B.6
.

Let
G_{1},G_{2},\dots
be i.i.d. Gaussian random variables with mean zero and covariance
V
, and let
S_{t}:=\sum_{j=1}^{t}G_{j},V_{t}:=tV
. Let
\eta>1,\rho>0,s>1
be some fixed constants. Then for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
, it holds that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\exists t\geq 1\colon S_{t}\geq\sqrt{2\eta(V_{t}\lor\rho)\left\{s\log\log\left(\frac{\eta(V_{t}\lor\rho)}{\rho}\right)+\log\left(\frac{\zeta(s)}{\alpha\log^{s}\eta}\right)\right\}}\right)\leq\alpha.

Lemma
B.6
follows from Theorem 3.1 of
Whitehouse et al., (2023)
by letting
\psi(x)=x^{2}/2
and
h(k)=(k+1)^{s}\zeta(s)
where
\zeta(s)
is the Riemann zeta function.

Next, we use the
\varepsilon
-net arguments to obtain a time-uniform boundary for the normalized multivariate process.

Lemma B.7
.

Let
{\bm{G}}_{1},{\bm{G}}_{2},\dots
be i.i.d. Gaussian random vectors with mean zero and covariance
{\bm{V}}
, and let
{\bm{S}}_{t}:=\sum_{j=1}^{t}{\bm{G}}_{j},{\bm{V}}_{t}:=t{\bm{V}}
. Let
\eta>1,s>1,\epsilon\in(0,1)
be some fixed constants,
\bar{\kappa}:=\kappa({\bm{V}})
be the conditional number of
{\bm{V}}
, and
N_{0}:=N({\mathbb{S}}^{d-1},\epsilon/\sqrt{\bar{\kappa}},\|\cdot\|_{2})
be the covering number of the unit sphere. Then for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
, it holds that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\exists t\geq 1\colon\left\|{\bm{V}}_{t}^{-1/2}{\bm{S}}_{t}\right\|_{2}\geq\frac{1}{1-\epsilon}\sqrt{2\eta\left\{s\log\log(\eta\bar{\kappa}t)+\log\left(\frac{N_{0}\zeta(s)}{\alpha\log^{s}\eta}\right)\right\}}\right)\leq\alpha.

Proof of Lemma
B.7
.

Let
K_{0}
be the corresponding minimal
\frac{\epsilon}{\sqrt{\bar{\kappa}}}
-cover of the unit sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}
with
|K_{0}|=N_{0}
. For any fixed
\nu\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}
, by Lemma
B.6
with
S_{t}=\langle\nu,{\bm{S}}_{t}\rangle
,
V_{t}=\langle\nu,{\bm{V}}_{t}\nu\rangle
and
\rho=\gamma_{\min}({\bm{V}})
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\exists t\geq 1\colon\langle\nu,{\bm{S}}_{t}\rangle\geq\sqrt{2\eta\langle\nu,{\bm{V}}_{t}\nu\rangle\left\{s\log\log\left(\frac{\eta\langle\nu,{\bm{V}}_{t}\nu\rangle}{\gamma_{\min}({\bm{V}})}\right)+\log\left(\frac{N_{0}\zeta(s)}{\alpha\log^{s}\eta}\right)\right\}}\right)\leq\frac{\alpha}{N_{0}}.

A straightforward union bound over all
\nu\in K_{0}
yields

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\exists t\geq 1,\nu\in K_{0}\colon\langle\nu,{\bm{S}}_{t}\rangle\geq\sqrt{2\eta\langle\nu,{\bm{V}}_{t}\nu\rangle\left\{s\log\log(\eta\bar{\kappa}t)+\log\left(\frac{N_{0}\zeta(s)}{\alpha\log^{s}\eta}\right)\right\}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\exists t\geq 1,\nu\in K_{0}\colon\langle\nu,{\bm{S}}_{t}\rangle\geq\sqrt{2\eta\langle\nu,{\bm{V}}_{t}\nu\rangle\left\{s\log\log\left(\frac{\eta\langle\nu,{\bm{V}}_{t}\nu\rangle}{\gamma_{\min}({\bm{V}})}\right)+\log\left(\frac{N_{0}\zeta(s)}{\alpha\log^{s}\eta}\right)\right\}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\

\displaystyle\alpha.

(B.6)

Now we bound
\left\|{\bm{V}}_{t}^{-1/2}{\bm{S}}_{t}\right\|_{2}
. Let
K_{0}(t):=\left\{{\bm{V}}_{t}^{1/2}\nu/\|{\bm{V}}_{t}^{1/2}\nu\|_{2}\colon\nu\in K_{0}\right\}\subset{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}
and
\pi_{t}\colon{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}\rightarrow K_{0}(t)
be the
L^{2}
projection onto the finite set
K_{0}(t)
. For any
t\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{V}}_{t}^{-1/2}{\bm{S}}_{t}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\omega\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}}\left\langle\omega,{\bm{V}}_{t}^{-1/2}{\bm{S}}_{t}\right\rangle=\sup_{\omega\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}}\left\{\left\langle\omega-\pi_{t}(\omega),{\bm{V}}_{t}^{-1/2}{\bm{S}}_{t}\right\rangle+\left\langle\pi_{t}(\omega),{\bm{V}}_{t}^{-1/2}{\bm{S}}_{t}\right\rangle\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\omega\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}}\left\|\omega-\pi_{t}(\omega)\right\|_{2}\left\|{\bm{V}}_{t}^{-1/2}{\bm{S}}_{t}\right\|_{2}+\sup_{\omega\in K_{0}(t)}\left\langle\omega,{\bm{V}}_{t}^{-1/2}{\bm{S}}_{t}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\epsilon}{\sqrt{\bar{\kappa}}}\sqrt{\bar{\kappa}}\left\|{\bm{V}}_{t}^{-1/2}{\bm{S}}_{t}\right\|_{2}+\sup_{\nu\in K_{0}}\left\langle\frac{{\bm{V}}_{t}^{1/2}\nu}{\|{\bm{V}}_{t}^{1/2}\nu\|_{2}},{\bm{V}}_{t}^{-1/2}{\bm{S}}_{t}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\epsilon\left\|{\bm{V}}_{t}^{-1/2}{\bm{S}}_{t}\right\|_{2}+\sup_{\nu\in K_{0}}\frac{\langle\nu,{\bm{S}}_{t}\rangle}{\sqrt{\langle\nu,{\bm{V}}_{t}\nu\rangle}},

where the second inequality uses Lemma 7.1 of
Whitehouse et al., (2023)
and the definition of
K_{0}(t)
. Combining (
B.6
), we have with probability at least
1-\alpha
, for any
t\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{V}}_{t}^{-1/2}{\bm{S}}_{t}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1-\epsilon}\sup_{\nu\in K_{0}}\frac{\langle\nu,{\bm{S}}_{t}\rangle}{\sqrt{\langle\nu,{\bm{V}}_{t}\nu\rangle}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1-\epsilon}\sqrt{2\eta\left\{s\log\log(\eta\bar{\kappa}t)+\log\left(\frac{N_{0}\zeta(s)}{\alpha\log^{s}\eta}\right)\right\}},

which concludes the proof.
∎

In our case,
{\bm{S}}_{t}=t{\bm{M}}_{t}
and
{\bm{V}}={\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{S}}{\bm{H}}^{-1}
. By Lemma 4.2 of
Whitehouse et al., (2023)
,
N_{0}
can be bounded by
N_{0}\leq C_{d}(3\sqrt{\bar{\kappa}}/\epsilon)^{d-1}
, where the constant
C_{d}=d2^{d}\Gamma\big(\frac{d+1}{2}\big)/\pi^{\frac{d-1}{2}}
. By letting
\eta=2
and
s=1.4
in Lemma
B.7
, which are also the same hyperparameter choices as in Appendix

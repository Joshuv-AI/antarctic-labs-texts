Sign-changing prescribed mass solutions for L 2 L^{2} -supercritical NLS on compact metric graphs
Louis Jeanjean, Linjie Song

Abstract
This paper is devoted to the existence of multiple sign-changing solutions of prescribed mass for a mass-supercritical nonlinear Schrödinger equation set on a compact metric graph. In particular, we obtain, in the supercritical mass regime, the first multiplicity result for prescribed mass solutions on compact metric graphs. As a byproduct, we prove that any eigenvalue of the associated linear operator is a bifurcation point. Our approach relies on the introduction of a new kind of link and on the use of gradient flow techniques on a constraint. It can be transposed to other problems posed on a bounded domain.



Key Words:
Constrained functionals;
L^{2}
-supercritical; compact metric graph; sign-changing critical points, bifurcation phenomena.

Introduction and main results

In
[
14
]
, the first author of this article and his collaborators studied the existence of non-constant critical points for the
C^{2}

mass supercritical
NLS energy functional
E(\cdot,\mathcal{G}):H^{1}(\mathcal{G})\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

\displaystyle E(u,\mathcal{G})=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u^{\prime}|^{2}dx-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u|^{p}dx,\quad p>6,

(1.1)

under the mass constraint

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u|^{2}dx=\mu>0,

(1.2)

where
\mathcal{G}
is a
compact metric graph
. Such critical points, also called bound states, solve the stationary nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLS) on
\mathcal{G}

\displaystyle-u^{\prime\prime}+\lambda u=|u|^{p-2}u

(1.3)

for some Lagrange multiplier
\lambda
, coupled with Kirchhoff condition at the vertices (see (
1.6
) below). In turn, the solutions of (
1.3
) give rise to time-dependent NLS standing waves on
\mathcal{G}
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\,\partial_{t}\psi(t,x)=-\partial_{xx}\psi(t,x)-|\psi(t,x)|^{p-2}\psi(t,x),

(1.4)

via the ansatz
\psi(t,x)=e^{\mathrm{i}\lambda t}u(x)
. The constraint (
1.2
) is meaningful from a dynamics perspective as the mass (or charge), as well as the energy, is conserved by the NLS flow.

One of the main physical motivations to consider (
1.4
) on metric graph is the study of propagation of optical pulses in nonlinear optics, or of matter waves in the theory of Bose-Einstein condensates and in branched structures, such as
T
-junctions or
X
-junctions. We refer interested readers to
[
4
,
9
,
23
,
28
,
31
]
and to the references therein for more details.
In addition, there are some interesting new mathematical features in metric graphs compared to the Euclidean case, and thus, the problem of existence of bound states on metric graphs attracted a lot of attention in the past decade, mainly in the
subcritical
or
critical regimes
(i.e.,
p\in(2,6)
or
p=6
respectively) in which the energy functional
E(\cdot,\mathcal{G})
is bounded from below and coercive on the mass constraint (for
\mu>0
small when
p=6
).

In these cases, the problem mainly studied is that of the existence of a global minimizer of the energy functional under the mass constraint. We refer to
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
5
]
for cases where the graph is non-compact and to
[
11
,
20
]
where it is compact. See also
[
16
,
18
,
21
,
32
,
33
,
34
,
39
]
for strictly related issues (problems with localized nonlinearities, combined nonlinearities, existence of critical points in absence of ground states).

In striking contrast, the supercritical regime (
p>6
) on general graphs is less touched. Before
[
14
]
, we are only aware of
[
6
]
in which the assumptions allow the analysis to be reduced to the study of minimizing sequences living in a bounded subset of the constraint. In the setting of
[
14
]
where
\mathcal{G}
is compact, there always exists a constrained constant (trivial) critical point of
E(\cdot,\mathcal{G})
obtained by taking the constant function
\kappa_{\mu}:=(\mu/\ell)^{1/2}
, where
\ell
denotes the total length of
\mathcal{G}
. In
[
14
]
the authors showed that
for any
0<\mu<\mu_{1}
, where

\mu_{1}:=\ell\left(\frac{\lambda_{2}(\mathcal{G})}{p-2}\right)^{\frac{2}{p-2}},

(1.5)

there is a positive non-constant critical point of
E(\cdot,\mathcal{G})
in
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
, which is at a mountain pass level.

Note that if one tries to apply the approach of
[
6
]
to a compact graph, it is not possible to know whether the resulting local minimizer is constant or non-constant. For this reason, the existence of non-trivial bound states in the supercritical case for general (compact) metric graphs can be seen as first essentially touched in
[
14
]
. Later, in
[
12
]
, the first author with his co-authors proved the existence of infinitely many bound states with any prescribed mass for
L^{2}
-supercritical nonlinear Schrödinger (NLS) equations with localized nonlinearities on non-compact metric graphs, coupled with the Kirchhoff conditions at the vertices. Unfortunately, the method used in
[
12
]
fails to work on compact graph and it is left as an open problem to prove the multiplicity, even the existence of just two non-trivial critical points of
E(\cdot,\mathcal{G})
under the mass constraint for compact
\mathcal{G}
, see
[
12
, Remark 1.5]
. The main motivation of this paper is to solve this open question.

Basic notations and main result

For any graph, we write

\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{V})
, where
\mathcal{E}
is the set of
edges and
\mathcal{V}
is the set of vertices. Each bounded edge

{\mathrm{e}}
is identified with a closed bounded interval
I_{\mathrm{e}}=[0,\ell_{\mathrm{e}}]
(where
\ell_{\mathrm{e}}
is the length of
{\mathrm{e}}
), while each unbounded edge is identified with a closed half-line
I_{\mathrm{e}}=\mathopen{[}0,+\infty\mathclose{)}
. The length of the shortest path between points provides a natural metric (whence a topology and a Borel structure) on
\mathcal{G}
. A metric graph is compact if and only if it has a finite number of edges, and none of them is unbounded. We will assume throughout the paper that it is the case.

A function
u:\mathcal{G}\to\mathbb{R}
is identified with a vector of functions
\{u_{{\mathrm{e}}}\}_{{\mathrm{e}}\in\mathcal{E}}
, where each
u_{{\mathrm{e}}}
is defined on the corresponding interval
I_{\mathrm{e}}
such that
u|_{{\mathrm{e}}}=u_{{\mathrm{e}}}
. Endowing each edge with the Lebesgue measure, one can define
\int_{\mathcal{G}}u(x)\,\mathrm{d}x
and the space
L^{p}(\mathcal{G})
in a natural way, with norm

\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{G})}^{p}=\sum_{{\mathrm{e}}\in\mathcal{E}}\|u_{\mathrm{e}}\|_{L^{p}({\mathrm{e}})}^{p}.

The Sobolev space
H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
consists of the set of continuous
functions
u:\mathcal{G}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

u_{{\mathrm{e}}}\in H^{1}(\mathring{{\mathrm{e}}})
for every edge

{\mathrm{e}}
; the norm
in
H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
is defined as

\|u\|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{G})}^{2}=\sum_{{\mathrm{e}}\in\mathcal{E}}\left(\|u_{{\mathrm{e}}}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}({\mathrm{e}})}^{2}+\|u_{{\mathrm{e}}}\|_{L^{2}({\mathrm{e}})}^{2}\right).

More details can be found in
[
2
,
3
,
9
]
.

We aim to prove the multiplicity of non-constant critical points of the functional
E(\cdot,\mathcal{G})
, defined in (
1.1
), constrained on the
L^{2}
-sphere

H_{\mu}^{1}(\mathcal{G}):=\bigl\{u\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G}):\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u|^{2}dx=\mu\bigr\}.

If

u\in H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
is such a critical point, it is standard to show that there exists a Lagrange multiplier
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
such that
u
satisfies the following problem:

\begin{cases}-u^{\prime\prime}+\lambda u=|u|^{p-2}u&\text{on every edge }{\mathrm{e}}\in\mathcal{E},\\[3.0pt]
\displaystyle\sum_{{\mathrm{e}}\succ{\mathrm{v}}}u_{{\mathrm{e}}}^{\prime}({\mathrm{v}})=0&\text{at every vertex }{\mathrm{v}}\in\mathcal{V},\end{cases}

(1.6)

where
{\mathrm{e}}\succ{\mathrm{v}}
means that the edge
{\mathrm{e}}
is incident at

{\mathrm{v}}
, and the notation
u_{{\mathrm{e}}}^{\prime}({\mathrm{v}})
stands for

u^{\prime}_{{\mathrm{e}}}(0)
or
-u^{\prime}_{{\mathrm{e}}}(\ell_{{\mathrm{e}}})
, according to whether
the vertex
{\mathrm{v}}
is identified with
0
or
\ell_{{\mathrm{e}}}
(namely, the
sum involves the derivatives away from the vertex
{\mathrm{v}}
). The
second equation is the so-called
Kirchhoff boundary condition
. Note that at external vertices, namely vertices which are reached by a unique edge, the Kirchhoff conditions reduce to purely Neumann conditions.

Our strategy to find multiple non-constant solutions is to look for sign-changing critical points of
E(\cdot,\mathcal{G})
in
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
. Of course, any sign-changing critical point is non-constant and is different from the positive one found in
[
14
, Theorem 1.1]
. Our first main result is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be any compact metric graph, and
p>6
. There exists
\mu_{2}>0
depending on
\mathcal{G}
and on
p
such that, for any
0<\mu<\mu_{2}
,
E(\cdot,\mathcal{G})
has a sign-changing (non-constant) critical point constrained on
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
.

Furthermore, for any
0<\mu<\mu_{2}
,
E(\cdot,\mathcal{G})
has at least two pairs of non-constant critical points
\pm u_{1},\pm u_{2}
constrained on
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
,
u_{1}
(respectively,
-u_{1}
) is positive (respectively, negative) and at a mountain pass level, and
u_{2}
is sign-changing.

Remark 1.2
.

Theorem
1.1
is not a perturbation result, the value
\mu_{2}
will not be obtained by any limit process, and can be explicitly estimated. We refer to the proof of Theorem
1.1
in Section
5
for more details.

The novelty of Theorem
1.1
lies in the existence of sign-changing critical points. A main tool in our proof is a new kind of link contained in an open bounded subset
\mathcal{B}_{\rho}^{\mu}
of
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
. We note that a similar idea was used by the second author and his co-author in
[
42
]
to find sign-changing solutions with prescribed mass for the Brézis-Nirenberg problem.
The subset
\mathcal{B}_{\rho}^{\mu}
is given

\mathcal{B}_{\rho}^{\mu}=\bigl\{u\in H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G}):\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u^{\prime}|^{2}dx<\rho\bigr\}.

All our analysis will be developed in
\mathcal{B}_{\rho}^{\mu}
where Palais-Smale sequences are, trivially, bounded, something which may not be true outside of
\mathcal{B}_{\rho}^{\mu}
. Since the graph
\mathcal{G}
is compact, the Palais-Smale condition thus holds on the closure of
\mathcal{B}_{\rho}^{\mu}
, see Corollary
2.2
. For
\mu>0
sufficiently small, we fix a convenient
\rho>0
and define inside
\mathcal{B}_{\rho}^{\mu}
a min-max structure such that the min-max level is strictly below the infimum of the energies of functions on
\partial\mathcal{B}_{\rho}^{\mu}
; see Proposition
2.6
and Section
3
for more details. Thus, if we find a critical point at the min-max level, it will not belong to
\partial\mathcal{B}_{\rho}^{\mu}
and will be a critical point constrained on
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
without additional constraints.

In unconstrained problems, while the existence of critical points with sign change has been widely studied (see, for example,
[
7
,
26
,
27
,
36
,
37
,
38
,
44
,
45
]
), it is known to be more difficult than simply finding a critical point without any sign requirement. The existence of sign-changing constrained critical points has been much less studied.
Regarding
L^{2}
-constrained problems set on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we mention
[
24
]
, where the existence of sign-changing nonradial solutions with prescribed mass, for some NLS on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
N\geq 4
in a mass subcritical case, was first observed. Related results were obtained in
[
25
]
in a mass supercritical setting.
We remark that the methods in
[
24
,
25
]
strongly depend on the symmetry of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and the Pohozaev manifold was used in the mass supercritical case, for which they fail to work for
L^{2}
-supercritical NLS on bounded domains and on compact metric graphs. For constrained problems on bounded domains, in a recent work
[
17
]
an action approach was used to establish the existence of one sign-changing solution with prescribed mass.
Notably, the powerful descending flow technique, which has been widely developed for finding sign-changing solutions in unconstrained problems, has been little used in a constrained
L^{2}
case. A key step here is to find invariant subsets with respect to some negative gradient flow on the constraint. In
[
43
]
, see also
[
22
]
, this was successfully implemented in the problems considered. If one hopes to develop a somehow general approach, which up to our knowledge is missing, a first observation is that the classical Brézis-Martin result cannot be used directly in the presence of a mass constraint.

In Section
4
, we extend the Brézis-Martin result (see Proposition
4.4
) to an abstract setting in which our constrained problem is included; see Proposition
4.5
and Corollary
4.6
for more details.
We also provide Lemma
4.7
to check a key assumption used by Proposition
4.5
. We believe Proposition
4.5
, Corollary
4.6
, and Lemma
4.7
are of independent interest. They constitute a general framework to prove that some subset is invariant with respect to a flow on a manifold. This framework can be used to establish the existence of sign-changing bound states with prescribed mass in some other problems.
In fact, it has already been used in
[
42
]
.

The gradient
\nabla E(\cdot,\mathcal{G})
of
E(\cdot,\mathcal{G})
as a constrained functional on
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
belongs to the tangent space of
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
at the point
u\in H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
and is given by

\nabla E(u,\mathcal{G})=u-(-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+1)^{-1}(|u|^{p-2}u+\lambda_{u}u).

In view of Lemma
4.7
, to check, in our problem, the assumptions in Proposition
4.5
and Corollary
4.6
, we need to determine the sign of
\lambda_{u}
. This is a main difference with the unconstrained case where
\lambda_{u}
is prescribed and independent of
u
.

Our process of searching for sign-changing critical points can be continued after finding the first one. In fact, we can obtain as many sign-changing critical points of
E(\cdot,\mathcal{G})
in
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
as we want at the expense of possibly reducing
\mu
. Our second multiplicity result is as follows.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be any compact metric graph, and
p>6
. For any
j\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
\mu_{j}>0
depending on
\mathcal{G}
and on
p
such that, for any
0<\mu<\mu_{j}
,
E(\cdot,\mathcal{G})
has at least
j
pairs of non-constant critical points
\pm u_{1},\pm u_{2},\cdots,\pm u_{j}
constrained on
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
. Furthermore,
u_{1}
(respectively,
-u_{1}
) is positive (respectively, negative) and the others are sign-changing.

The proof of Theorem
1.3
is quite similar to the one of Theorem
1.1
. We combine discussions on min-max structures, descending flow techniques, and we establish the invariance of some subsets of
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
with respect to some negative gradient flow. An additional difficulty is to make sure that our approach provides distinct critical points. This is done by estimating the energy levels of the sign-changing critical points, see step 4 in the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

In the last part of the paper, we discuss the limiting behaviour of the solutions obtained in Theorem
1.3
, as the mass
\mu\to 0
. First we observe that all our solutions are obtained inside the sets
\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}\subset H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
on which the
H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
norm is bounded uniformly with respect to
\mu>0
. This implies that the norm
H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
and the energy of all our solutions are uniformly bounded. This contrasts sharply with
[
14
]
where the norm and energy of the solution found in
[
14
, Theorem 1.1]
go to infinity when
\mu\to 0
. Actually, it is possible to describe very precisely what happens when
\mu\to 0
. Following the terminology introduced by C. A. Stuart
[
40
,
41
]
, we say that
\lambda^{*}\in\mathbb{R}
is a bifurcation point
of (
1.6
) if there exists a sequence
\{(\lambda_{k},u_{k})\}\subset\mathbb{R}\times H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
such that
u_{k}\not\equiv 0
solves (
1.6
) with
\lambda=\lambda_{k}
,
-\lambda_{k}\to\lambda^{*}
and
||u_{k}||_{H^{1}(\mathcal{G})}\to 0
.

Let
0=\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{G})<\lambda_{2}(\mathcal{G})\leq\lambda_{3}(\mathcal{G})\leq\cdots
be the eigenvalues of
-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}
on the compact metric graph
\mathcal{G}
with Kirchhoff condition at the vertices.
Based on the process of building our solutions, we obtain

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be any compact metric graph, and
p>6
. Then the set of bifurcation point of (
1.6
) is
\{\lambda_{j}(\mathcal{G}):j\in\mathbb{N}\}
.

From classical bifurcation theory, any bifurcation point of (
1.6
) must be an eigenvalue, namely belong to
\{\lambda_{j}(\mathcal{G}):j\in\mathbb{N}\}
, and any simple eigenvalue is a bifurcation point. Theorem
1.4
states that any eigenvalue, regardless of its multiplicity, is a bifurcation point. This result is of independent interest.

Finally, let us point out that if the problem here is set on a metric graph what is really essential in our approach is that the operator
-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}
, considered with the Kirchhoff condition, has a discrete spectrum (due here to the compactness of the graph). Our approach could be used to handle other type of problems set on a compact domain, typically NLS equations on a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2

\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}
. Section

is devoted to introduce our link and the implementation of the minimax structure. In Section
4
, we present our abstract approach to negative gradient flow on a constraint. and also establish the key Proposition
4.3
. In Section

1.1
. Section
6
is devoted to the proofs of Theorem
1.3
and of Theorem
1.4
.

2
Preliminary results

At the start of this section, we recall a result in
[
20
]
, which shows that any bounded (PS) sequence of
E(\cdot,\mathcal{G})
constrained on
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
has a convergent subsequence in
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
20
, Proposition 3.1]

Assume that
\mathcal{G}
is a compact graph and
\{u_{n}\}\subset H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
is a bounded
(PS) sequence of
E(\cdot,\mathcal{G})
constrained on
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
. Then there exists
u\in H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
such that, up to a subsequence,
u_{n}\to u
strongly in
H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
.

Since (PS) sequences are, in general, unbounded, we introduce the following bounded subsets of the mass constraint for
\rho>0
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\rho}^{\mu}=\bigl\{u\in H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G}):\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u^{\prime}|^{2}dx<\rho\bigr\},\quad\mathcal{U}_{\rho}^{\mu}=\bigl\{u\in H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G}):\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u^{\prime}|^{2}dx=\rho\bigr\}.

Similar bounded subsets were introduced for supercritical NLS with a partial confinement in
[
8
]
and in the bounded domain case in
[
35
]
to search for local minimizers.

As a corollary of Lemma
2.1
, any (PS) sequence of
E(\cdot,\mathcal{G})
in
\mathcal{B}_{\rho}^{\mu}
has a convergent subsequence.

Corollary 2.2
.

Assume that
\mathcal{G}
is a compact graph and
\{u_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{B}_{\rho}^{\mu}
is a (PS) sequence of
E(\cdot,\mathcal{G})
constrained on
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
. Then there exists
u\in H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
such that, up to a subsequence,
u_{n}\to u
strongly in
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
. Furthermore, if

\limsup_{n\to\infty}E(u_{n},\mathcal{G})<\inf_{\mathcal{U}_{\rho}^{\mu}}E(\cdot,\mathcal{G})
, then
u\in\mathcal{B}_{\rho}^{\mu}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

For any
u\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
, there is a constant
K>0
depending on
\mathcal{G}
and on
p
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u-\bar{u}|^{p}dx\leq K\left(\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u^{\prime}|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{p-2}{4}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u-\bar{u}|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{p+2}{4}},

(2.1)

where

\bar{u}=|\mathcal{G}|^{-1}\int_{\mathcal{G}}udx.

Proof.

By the classical Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u-\bar{u}|^{p}dx\leq C\left(\int_{\mathcal{G}}(|(u-\bar{u})^{\prime}|^{2}+|u-\bar{u}|^{2})dx\right)^{\frac{p-2}{4}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u-\bar{u}|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{p+2}{4}},

where
C>0
is a positive constant depending on
\mathcal{G}
and on
p
. Since
\int_{\mathcal{G}}(u-\bar{u})dx=0
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}(\mathcal{G})\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u-\bar{u}|^{2}dx\leq\int_{\mathcal{G}}|(u-\bar{u})^{\prime}|^{2}dx=\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u^{\prime}|^{2}dx.

Hence,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u-\bar{u}|^{p}dx\leq C\left(1+\frac{1}{\lambda_{2}(\mathcal{G})}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{4}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u^{\prime}|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{p-2}{4}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u-\bar{u}|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{p+2}{4}}.

∎

Lemma 2.4
.

For any
u\in H_{\mu}^{1}(\mathcal{G})
, there is a constant
K>0
depending on
\mathcal{G}
and on
p
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\leq pK\left(\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u^{\prime}|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{p-2}{4}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u-\bar{u}|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{p+2}{4}}+p\ell^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\mu^{\frac{p}{2}}

(2.2)

\displaystyle\leq pK\mu^{\frac{p+2}{4}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u^{\prime}|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{p-2}{4}}+p\ell^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\mu^{\frac{p}{2}},

(2.3)

where
\ell
denotes the total length of
\mathcal{G}
and

\bar{u}=|\mathcal{G}|^{-1}\int_{\mathcal{G}}udx.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.3
, we know

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u-\bar{u}|^{p}dx\leq K\left(\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u^{\prime}|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{p-2}{4}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u-\bar{u}|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{p+2}{4}}

where
K
is a positive constant depending on
\mathcal{G}
and on
p
. Then, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u|^{p}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u-\bar{u}+\bar{u}|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\leq p\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u-\bar{u}|^{p}dx+p|\bar{u}|^{p}|\mathcal{G}|

\displaystyle\leq pK\left(\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u^{\prime}|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{p-2}{4}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u-\bar{u}|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{p+2}{4}}+p|\bar{u}|^{p}|\mathcal{G}|.

(2.4)

Note that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{G}}(u-\bar{u})\bar{u}dx=0.

Then we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{G}}|\bar{u}|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u-\bar{u}|^{2}dx=\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u|^{2}dx=\mu,

(2.5)

implying that
|\bar{u}|\leq(\mu/\ell)^{\frac{1}{2}}
. This together with (
2.4
) shows that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u|^{p}dx\leq pK\left(\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u^{\prime}|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{p-2}{4}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u-\bar{u}|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{p+2}{4}}+p\ell^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\mu^{\frac{p}{2}}.

Namely that (
2.2
) holds. Now, using (
2.5
) again, we have
\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u-\bar{u}|^{2}dx\leq\mu
and thus (
2.3
) follows from (
2.2
). This completes the proof.

∎

Remark 2.5
.

Since
\int_{\mathcal{G}}(u-\bar{u})dx=0
,

\lambda_{2}(\mathcal{G})\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u-\bar{u}|^{2}dx\leq\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u^{\prime}|^{2}dx.

Then using (
2.2
) we see that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u|^{p}dx\leq p\bar{K}\left(\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u^{\prime}|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}+p\ell^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\mu^{\frac{p}{2}},\quad\forall u\in H_{\mu}^{1}(\mathcal{G}),

where
\bar{K}=\lambda_{2}(\mathcal{G})^{-\frac{p+2}{4}}K
depends on
\mathcal{G}
and on
p
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Given
\mu>0
and recalling that
p>6
, we define

\displaystyle g(\rho)=\frac{1}{2}\rho-K\mu^{\frac{p+2}{4}}\rho^{\frac{p-2}{4}},\quad\rho>0,

\displaystyle\rho^{*}=\rho^{*}(\mu,p,\mathcal{G}):=\min\{b,b\mu^{-\frac{p+2}{p-6}}\}\text{ where }b=\left(\frac{2}{(p-2)K}\right)^{\frac{4}{p-6}},

\displaystyle M_{1}=M_{1}(\mu,p,\mathcal{G}):=g(\rho^{*}),

\displaystyle M_{2}=M_{2}(\mu,p,\mathcal{G}):=M_{1}-\ell^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\mu^{\frac{p}{2}},

where
K
is the positive constant given by Lemma
2.4
. Note that
g^{\prime}(\rho)>0
in
(0,b\mu^{-\frac{p+2}{p-6}})
and
g^{\prime}(\rho)<0
when
\rho>b\mu^{-\frac{p+2}{p-6}}
. We have

\displaystyle M_{1}\geq\frac{p-6}{2(p-2)}\rho^{*},

(2.6)

and the “=” holds if and only if
\mu\geq 1
. Furthermore, for any
\mu>0
, we have

\displaystyle\inf_{\mathcal{U}^{\mu}_{\rho^{*}}}E(\cdot,\mathcal{G})\geq M_{2}.

(2.7)

Proof.

A direct computation yields

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(\rho)=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{p-2}{4}K\mu^{\frac{p+2}{4}}\rho^{\frac{p-6}{4}}.

Let
\bar{\rho}:=b\mu^{-\frac{p+2}{p-6}}
. Obviously,
g^{\prime}(\rho)>0
in
(0,\bar{\rho})
and
g^{\prime}(\rho)<0
when
\rho>\bar{\rho}
.

If
\mu\geq 1
, then
\bar{\rho}=b\mu^{-\frac{p+2}{p-6}}\leq b
and thus
\rho^{*}=\bar{\rho}
. Hence,

\displaystyle M_{1}=g(\bar{\rho})=\frac{p-6}{2(p-2)}b\mu^{-\frac{p+2}{p-6}}=\frac{p-6}{2(p-2)}\rho^{*}.

(2.8)

If
0<\mu<1
, then
\bar{\rho}>b
and thus
\rho^{*}=b<\bar{\rho}
. Hence,

\displaystyle M_{1}=g(b)=\frac{1}{2}b-K\mu^{\frac{p+2}{4}}b^{\frac{p-2}{4}}>\frac{1}{2}b-Kb^{\frac{p-2}{4}}=\frac{p-6}{2(p-2)}b=\frac{p-6}{2(p-2)}\rho^{*}.

(2.9)

By (
2.8
) and (
2.9
), we get (
2.6
) and show that the ”=” in (
2.6
) holds if and only if
\mu\geq 1
.

Furthermore, by Lemma
2.4
, we have

\displaystyle\inf_{\mathcal{U}^{\mu}_{\rho^{*}}}E(\cdot,\mathcal{G})\geq\frac{1}{2}\rho^{*}-K\mu^{\frac{p+2}{4}}\left(\rho^{*}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{4}}-\ell^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\mu^{\frac{p}{2}}=g(\rho^{*})-\ell^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\mu^{\frac{p}{2}}=M_{2}.

Namely (
2.7
) holds.
∎

Remark 2.7
.

In the definition of
\rho^{*}
, we made sure that
\rho^{*}\leq b
in order to have a uniform bound with respect to
\mu
as
\mu\to 0
. The boundedness of
\rho^{*}
will be used in the proof of Lemma
4.8
.

3
Link and the min-max structure

Let
0=\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{G})<\lambda_{2}(\mathcal{G})\leq\lambda_{3}(\mathcal{G})\leq\cdots
be the eigenvalues of
-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}
on the compact metric graph
\mathcal{G}
with Kirchhoff condition at the vertices, and let
\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\phi_{3},\cdots
be the corresponding eigenfunctions with
\int_{\mathcal{G}}|\phi_{i}|^{2}dx=1
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Then we set

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}_{1}=span\{\phi_{1}\}\cap H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})=\{\pm\sqrt{\mu/l}\},\quad\mathbb{S}_{1}^{\perp}=(span\{\phi_{1}\})^{\perp}\cap H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G}).

Note that since
\phi_{1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{l}},
any
u\in\mathbb{S}_{1}^{\perp}
is sign-changing.

A compact subset
A
of
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
with
\mathbb{S}_{1}\subset A
and
A\cap\mathbb{S}_{1}^{\perp}\not=\emptyset
is said to be
linked
to
\mathbb{S}_{1}^{\perp}
if, for any continuous mapping
h\in C(A,H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G}))
satisfying
h|_{\mathbb{S}_{1}}=\textbf{id}
, there holds
h(A)\cap\mathbb{S}_{1}^{\perp}\not=\emptyset
. Recall that
\rho^{*}
and
M_{2}
were defined in Proposition
2.6
. We introduce the following set which will be used to define a min-max structure:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1}=\mathcal{L}_{1}(\mu):=\bigl\{A\subset\mathcal{B}_{\rho^{*}}^{\mu}:\sup_{A}E(\cdot,\mathcal{G})<M_{2},\ \mathbb{S}_{1}\subset A,\ A\text{ is compact and is linked to }\mathbb{S}_{1}^{\perp}\bigr\}.

(3.1)

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\hat{\mu}=\hat{\mu}(\mathcal{G},p)>0
be the positive solution of

\lambda_{2}(\mathcal{G})\mu+2\ell^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\mu^{\frac{p}{2}}=\frac{p-6}{p-2}\left(\frac{2}{(p-2)K}\right)^{\frac{4}{p-6}},

and let
\bar{\mu}=\bar{\mu}(\mathcal{G},p)>0
be the positive solution of

\lambda_{2}(\mathcal{G})\mu^{\frac{2(p-2)}{p-6}}+2\ell^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\mu^{\frac{(p-2)^{2}}{2(p-6)}}=\frac{p-6}{p-2}\left(\frac{2}{(p-2)K}\right)^{\frac{4}{p-6}}.

Then
\mathcal{L}_{1}
is not empty for
0<\mu<\min\{\hat{\mu},\bar{\mu}\}
.

Proof.

Define

Q_{2}:=\left\{t_{1}\sqrt{\mu}\phi_{1}+t_{2}\sqrt{\mu}\phi_{2}:t^{2}_{1}+t^{2}_{2}=1,t_{2}\geq 0\right\}\subset H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G}).

It is clear that
\mathbb{S}_{1}=\{\pm\sqrt{\mu/l}\}\subset Q_{2}
. By the definitions of
\hat{\mu}
and
\bar{\mu}
, we know

\hat{\mu}>1\Leftrightarrow\bar{\mu}>1,\quad 0<\hat{\mu}<1\Leftrightarrow 0<\bar{\mu}<1.

If
0<\mu<\min\{\hat{\mu},\bar{\mu}\}
and
\mu\leq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}(\mathcal{G})\mu+2\ell^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\mu^{\frac{p}{2}}<\frac{p-6}{p-2}\left(\frac{2}{(p-2)K}\right)^{\frac{4}{p-6}}.

(3.2)

Since
\mu\leq 1
,
\rho^{*}=b
where
b=\left(\frac{2}{(p-2)K}\right)^{\frac{4}{p-6}}
. By Proposition
2.6
and (
3.2
), direct computations yield

\displaystyle\sup_{Q_{2}}E(\cdot,\mathcal{G})<\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{2}(\mathcal{G})\mu<\frac{p-6}{2(p-2)}b-\ell^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\mu^{\frac{p}{2}}\leq M_{1}-\ell^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\mu^{\frac{p}{2}}=M_{2}.

(3.3)

If
1<\mu<\min\{\hat{\mu},\bar{\mu}\}
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}(\mathcal{G})\mu^{\frac{2(p-2)}{p-6}}+2\ell^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\mu^{\frac{(p-2)^{2}}{2(p-6)}}<\frac{p-6}{p-2}\left(\frac{2}{(p-2)K}\right)^{\frac{4}{p-6}}.

(3.4)

Since
\mu>1
,
\rho^{*}=b\mu^{-\frac{p+2}{p-6}}
. By Proposition
2.6
and (
3.4
), direct computations yield

\displaystyle\sup_{Q_{2}}E(\cdot,\mathcal{G})<\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{2}(\mathcal{G})\mu<\frac{p-6}{2(p-2)}b\mu^{-\frac{p+2}{p-6}}-\ell^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\mu^{\frac{p}{2}}=M_{1}-\ell^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\mu^{\frac{p}{2}}=M_{2}.

(3.5)

By (
3.3
) and (
3.5
) we deduce that
\sup_{Q_{2}}E(\cdot,\mathcal{G})<M_{2}
when
0<\mu<\min\{\hat{\mu},\bar{\mu}\}
.

Next, we show
Q_{2}\subset\mathcal{B}_{\rho^{*}}^{\mu}
if
0<\mu<\min\{\hat{\mu},\bar{\mu}\}
. When
0<\mu<\min\{\hat{\mu},\bar{\mu}\}
and
\mu\leq 1
, (
3.2
) implies

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}(\mathcal{G})\mu<\frac{p-6}{p-2}b<b=\rho^{*}.

(3.6)

When
1<\mu<\min\{\hat{\mu},\bar{\mu}\}
, (
3.4
) implies

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}(\mathcal{G})\mu<\frac{p-6}{p-2}b\mu^{-\frac{p+2}{p-6}}<b\mu^{-\frac{p+2}{p-6}}=\rho^{*}.

(3.7)

By (
3.6
) and (
3.7
) we obtain
\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u^{\prime}|^{2}dx\leq\lambda_{2}(\mathcal{G})\mu<\rho^{*}
for any
u\in Q_{2}
, implying that
Q_{2}\subset\mathcal{B}_{\rho^{*}}^{\mu}
for
0<\mu<\min\{\hat{\mu},\bar{\mu}\}
.

It remains to prove that
Q_{2}
is linked to
\mathbb{S}_{1}^{\perp}
.

For any continuous mapping
h\in C(Q_{2},H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G}))
, we have to show that
h(Q_{2})\cap\mathbb{S}_{1}^{\perp}\not=\emptyset
. Since
Q_{2}
is one-dimensional and necessarily
\int_{\mathcal{G}}h(-\sqrt{\mu}\phi_{1})\phi_{1}<0
and
\int_{\mathcal{G}}h(\sqrt{\mu}\phi_{1})\phi_{1}>0
, the intermediate value theorem gives directly the result. However, having in view the proof of Theorem
1.3
, we present here a homotopy argument (see, e.g., Example 2 in
[
13
, Chapter II, Section 1]
). Let
P:H^{1}(\mathcal{G})\to span\{\phi_{1}\}
be the orthogonal projection. Note that
Q_{2}\subset span\{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\}
. Hence, the topological degrees
\deg(P\circ h,Q_{2},0)
and
\deg(P|_{Q_{2}},Q_{2},0)
are well-defined. Let

\displaystyle g(t,u):=(1-t)P(h(u))+tP|_{Q_{2}}u,\quad\forall t\in[0,1],\quad u\in Q_{2}.

For any
u\in\partial Q_{2}=\mathbb{S}_{1}
and
t\in[0,1]
, we have
g(t,u)=Pu=u\neq 0
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle\deg(P\circ h,Q_{2},0)=\deg(P|_{Q_{2}},Q_{2},0)=1,

and so, there exists a
u_{0}\in Q_{2}
such that
P(h(u_{0}))=0
, i.e.,
h(u_{0})\in\mathbb{S}_{1}^{\perp}
. This yields that
h(Q_{2})\cap\mathbb{S}_{1}^{\perp}\not=\emptyset
and completes the proof.
∎

To find sign-changing critical points, we introduce the following sets

\displaystyle\pm\mathcal{P}=\bigl\{u\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G}):\pm u\geq 0\bigl\},

\displaystyle(\pm\mathcal{P})_{\nu}=\bigl\{u\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G}):\inf_{w\in\pm\mathcal{P}}\|u-w\|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{G})}\leq\nu\bigr\},\quad\text{for some }\nu>0,

\displaystyle\ D^{*}(\nu)=\mathcal{P}_{\nu}\cup(-\mathcal{P})_{\nu},\quad S^{*}(\nu)=H^{1}(\mathcal{G})\backslash D^{*}(\nu).

Using
\mathcal{L}_{1}
and
S^{*}(\nu)
we can define a min-max value by

\displaystyle c_{1}:=\inf_{A\in\mathcal{L}_{1}}\sup_{u\in A\cap S^{*}(\nu)}E(u,\mathcal{G}).

Let

\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}:=\bigl\{u\in\mathcal{B}_{\rho^{*}}^{\mu}:E(u,\mathcal{G})<M_{2}\bigl\},

(3.8)

where
M_{2}
is defined in Proposition
2.6

and

\displaystyle\bar{c}_{1}:=\inf_{u\in\mathbb{S}_{1}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}}E(u,\mathcal{G}).

We need the following Lemma
3.2
to show that
c_{1}
is well defined for small
\nu>0
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\delta:=\min\{dist(\mathbb{S}_{1}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{B}_{\rho^{*}}^{\mu},\mathcal{P}),dist(\mathbb{S}_{1}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{B}_{\rho^{*}}^{\mu},-\mathcal{P})\}
. Then
\delta>0
.

Proof.

For any
u\in\mathbb{S}_{1}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{B}_{\rho^{*}}^{\mu}
, the function
-u
also belongs to
\mathbb{S}_{1}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{B}_{\rho^{*}}^{\mu}
. Thus, it is readily seen that
dist(\mathbb{S}_{1}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{B}_{\rho^{*}}^{\mu},\mathcal{P})=dist(\mathbb{S}_{1}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{B}_{\rho^{*}}^{\mu},-\mathcal{P})
. By contradiction, we assume that

dist(\mathbb{S}_{1}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{B}_{\rho^{*}}^{\mu},\mathcal{P})=0.

Then there exist
\{u_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{S}_{1}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{B}_{\rho^{*}}^{\mu}
,
\{w_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{P}
such that
\|u_{n}-w_{n}\|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{G})}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Note that
\mathcal{B}_{\rho^{*}}^{\mu}
is bounded in
H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
. Thus
\{u_{n}\}
, and also
\{w_{n}\}
, are bounded in
H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
. Up to a subsequence as
n\to\infty
, we can assume that
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u^{*}
weakly in
H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
,
u_{n}\to u^{*}
strongly in
L^{2}(\mathcal{G})
,
w_{n}\rightharpoonup w^{*}\in\mathcal{P}
weakly in
H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
, and
w_{n}\to w^{*}
strongly in
L^{2}(\mathcal{G})
. Then we observe that
u^{*}\in\mathbb{S}_{1}^{\perp}
with
u^{*}\in H_{\mu}^{1}(\mathcal{G})

which implies that
u^{*}\not\equiv 0
is sign-changing. However, we know
u^{*}\equiv w^{*}
from
\|u_{n}-w_{n}\|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{G})}\to 0
, which contradicts that
w^{*}\in\mathcal{P}
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

For
0<\mu<\min\{\hat{\mu},\bar{\mu}\}
and
0<\nu<\delta
where
\delta
is given in Lemma
3.2
, both values
c_{1}
and
\bar{c}_{1}
are well defined and
-\infty<\bar{c}_{1}\leq c_{1}<M_{2}
.

Proof.

Let
0<\mu<\min\{\hat{\mu},\bar{\mu}\}
. From the proof of Lemma
3.1
, we know that
\sqrt{\mu}\phi_{2}\in\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}
and thus, since
\sqrt{\mu}\phi_{2}\in\mathbb{S}_{1}^{\perp}
, the set
\mathbb{S}_{1}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}
is not empty. This shows that
\bar{c}_{1}
is well defined. Obviously,
\bar{c}_{1}<M_{2}<\infty
and also,
-\infty<\bar{c}_{1}
, since Lemma
2.4
guarantees that
E(\cdot,\mathcal{G})
is bounded on bounded sets of
H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
.

Lemma
3.1
yields that
\mathcal{L}_{1}
is not empty for
0<\mu<\min\{\hat{\mu},\bar{\mu}\}
. Furthermore, by the definitions of
\mathcal{L}_{1}
and the link, for any
A\subset\mathcal{L}_{1}
there exists
u_{0}\in A
such that
u_{0}\in\mathbb{S}_{1}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}
. For
0<\nu<\delta
, by Lemma
3.2
we have
u_{0}\in S^{*}(\nu)
. Thus
A\cap S^{*}(\nu)
is not empty for any
A\in\mathcal{L}_{1}
and we obtain

\displaystyle\bar{c}_{1}=\inf_{u\in\mathbb{S}_{1}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}}E(u,\mathcal{G})\leq\inf_{A\in\mathcal{L}_{1}}\sup_{u\in A\cap S^{*}(\nu)}E(u,\mathcal{G})=c_{1}.

Clearly also
c_{1}
is finite. Indeed taking an arbitrary
A\in\mathcal{L}_{1}
we have that

\sup_{u\in A\cap S^{*}}E(u,\mathcal{G})<\infty

by the compactness of
A
. The proof is now complete.
∎

Setting

\underline{c}_{1}:=\sup_{u\in\mathbb{S}_{1}}E(u,\mathcal{G})=-\frac{1}{p}\ell^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\mu^{\frac{p}{2}},

we will also need the following lower bound.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
0<\mu<\min\{\hat{\mu},\bar{\mu},\mu^{*}\}
where
\mu^{*}=\mu^{*}(\mathcal{G},p)>0
is the positive solution of

2\left(\frac{p-1}{p}\ell^{\frac{2-p}{2}}+K\lambda_{2}(\mathcal{G})^{\frac{p-2}{4}}\right)\mu^{\frac{p-2}{2}}=\lambda_{2}(\mathcal{G}).

Then
\bar{c}_{1}>\underline{c}_{1}
.

Proof.

We recall that the function
g(\rho)=\frac{1}{2}\rho-K\mu^{\frac{p+2}{4}}\rho^{\frac{p-2}{4}}
, which was defined in Proposition
2.6
, is strictly increasing in
(0,\rho^{*})
. Noting that
\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u^{\prime}|^{2}dx\geq\lambda_{2}(\mathcal{G})\mu
, for any
u\in\mathbb{S}_{1}^{\perp}
, it follows

\displaystyle\inf_{u\in\mathbb{S}_{1}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}}E(u,\mathcal{G})

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{u\in\mathbb{S}_{1}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u^{\prime}|^{2}dx-K\mu^{\frac{p+2}{4}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u^{\prime}|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{p-2}{4}}-\ell^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\mu^{\frac{p}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq g(\lambda_{2}(\mathcal{G})\mu)-\ell^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\mu^{\frac{p}{2}}.

From the definition of
g(\rho)
and the one of
\mu^{*}
, we obtain, for
0<\mu<\min\{\hat{\mu},\bar{\mu},u^{*}\}
,

\bar{c}_{1}=\inf_{u\in\mathbb{S}_{1}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}}E(u,\mathcal{G})>-\frac{1}{p}\ell^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\mu^{\frac{p}{2}}=\underline{c}_{1}.

The proof is complete.
∎

In Section
5
below, for sufficiently small
\nu>0
, we will prove that there exists a sign-changing critical point (contained in

) of

in

at the level

.

4
Invariant sets under the negative gradient flow

Let
\eta(t,\cdot)
be a decreasing flow on
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
corresponding to the gradient
\nabla E(\cdot,\mathcal{G})
(
\nabla E(\cdot,\mathcal{G})
means the gradient of
E(\cdot,\mathcal{G})
as a constrained functional on
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
) defined by

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\eta(t,u)=-h(\eta(t,u))y(\eta(t,u))\|\nabla E(\eta(t,u),\mathcal{G})\|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{G})}^{-2}\nabla E(\eta(t,u),\mathcal{G}),\\
&\eta(0,u)=u,\end{aligned}\right.

(4.1)

where

is a locally Lipschitz continuous map such that

\displaystyle y(u):=\left\{\begin{aligned} &1\quad\text{for all }u\in H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})\backslash(\mathcal{K}[c_{1}-\bar{\epsilon},c_{1}+\bar{\epsilon}])_{\delta_{1}/2},\\
&0\quad\text{for all }u\in(\mathcal{K}[c_{1}-\bar{\epsilon},c_{1}+\bar{\epsilon}])_{\delta_{1}/3},\end{aligned}\right.

and

h(u):=\frac{dist(u,U_{1})}{dist(u,U_{2})+dist(u,U_{1})}

with

\displaystyle U_{1}:=\bigl\{u\in H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G}):|E(u,\mathcal{G})-c_{1}|\geq 3\epsilon_{1}\bigr\},\quad U_{2}:=\bigl\{u\in H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G}):|E(u,\mathcal{G}))-c_{1}|\leq 2\epsilon_{1}\bigr\}.

Here
\delta_{1}>0
,
0<3\epsilon_{1}\leq\bar{\epsilon}
will be chosen in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, and we recall that
\mathcal{K}[\alpha,\beta]=\bigl\{u\in H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G}):(E|_{H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})})^{\prime}(u,\mathcal{G})=0,\alpha\leq E(u)\leq\beta\bigr\}.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\eta(t,\cdot)
be given by (
4.1
). Then for any
u\in\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}
, with
\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}
defined in (

),
\eta(t,u)
exists for

, and,
\eta(t,u)\in\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}
for all

.

Remark 4.2
.

It is well known that
\eta(t,u)
globally exists for any

if

satisfies the (PS) condition on
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
. However, the global existence result in the above lemma is not direct since the (PS) condition is lost in our setting. We shall make use of Lemma
2.1
to prove it.

Proof of Lemma
4.1
.

From Corollary
2.2
, we deduce the existence of a positive constant
C>0
such that
\|\nabla E(u,\mathcal{G})\|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{G})}\geq 1/C
for any
u\in E(\cdot,\mathcal{G})^{-1}[c_{1}-\bar{\epsilon},c_{1}+\bar{\epsilon}]\cap\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}\backslash(\mathcal{K}[c_{1}-\bar{\epsilon},c_{1}+\bar{\epsilon}])_{\delta_{1}/3}
, and so

\|h(u)y(u)\|\nabla E(u,\mathcal{G})\|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{G})}^{-2}\nabla E(u,\mathcal{G})\|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{G})}=h(u)y(u)\|\nabla E(u,\mathcal{G})\|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{G})}^{-1}\leq C,\quad\text{for }u\in\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}.

(4.2)

It is standard that
\eta(t,u)
locally exists for any
u\in H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
.
Let us prove that
\eta(t,u)\in\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}
for any
u\in\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}
and all
t\in[0,T_{u})
, where
T_{u}
is the maximal time such that
\eta(t,u)
exists. In fact,

\displaystyle E(\eta(t,u),\mathcal{G})\leq E(u,\mathcal{G})<M_{2}

for any

and

. Hence, if
\eta(t_{u},u)\notin\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}
for some

and

, then
\eta(t_{u},u)\notin\mathcal{B}_{\rho^{*}}^{\mu}
. By continuity, there exists

such that
\eta(t_{u}^{\prime},u)\in\mathcal{U}_{\rho^{*}}^{\mu}
. By Proposition

, we have

, in contradiction with

\displaystyle E(\eta(t_{u}^{\prime},u),\mathcal{G})\leq E(u,\mathcal{G})<M_{2}.

Thus

is an invariant subset and, in view of (

Proposition 4.4

(Brézis-Martin)
.

Let

be a Banach space,

,

,

be a neighborhood of

in

(4.3)

then there exist

and

Since there is a constraint in our setting, Proposition
4.4
cannot be used directly to prove Proposition
4.3
. First we shall extend it to a setting which will cover the mass constrained problem that we consider. Note that the setting and the results in Proposition
4.5
, Lemma

Let
(E,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
and
(H,(\cdot,\cdot))
be two
infinite-dimensional
Hilbert spaces and assume that

\displaystyle E\hookrightarrow H\hookrightarrow E^{\prime},

with continuous injections. For simplicity, we assume that the continuous injection
E\hookrightarrow H
has norm at most
1
and identify
E
with its image in
H
. We also introduce

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \|u\|^{2}&=\langle u,u\rangle,\\
|u|^{2}\ &=(u,u),\\
\end{aligned}\right.\quad\quad u\in E,

and, for
\mu\in(0,\infty)
, we define

\displaystyle S_{\mu}:=\{u\in E:|u|^{2}=\mu\}.

For our application, it is plain that
E=H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
and
H=L^{2}(\mathcal{G})
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
u\in B_{\mu}\subset S_{\mu}
,
U
be a neighborhood of
u
in
S_{\mu}
and
B_{\mu}\cap\overline{U}
closed in
S_{\mu}
. Let
V:\overline{U}\to E
be a Lipschitz mapping on
\overline{U}
with
V(w)\in T_{w}S_{\mu}
for any
w\in\overline{U}
, where
T_{w}S_{\mu}
is the tangent space of
S_{\mu}
at
w
defined as

\displaystyle T_{w}S_{\mu}:=\bigl\{\phi\in E:(\phi,w)=0\bigr\},

which satisfies

\displaystyle\lim_{s\searrow 0}s^{-1}dist(w+sV(w),B_{\mu})=0,\quad\forall w\in\overline{U}\cap B_{\mu}.

(4.4)

Then there exist
r=r(u)>0
and
\eta(t,u)
satisfying

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\eta(t,u)=V(\eta(t,u)),\quad\forall t\in[0,r),\\
&\eta(0,u)=u,\quad\eta(t,u)\in B_{\mu}.\end{aligned}\right.

Proof.

Let us construct a suitable extension of
V
on a neighbourhood of
u
in
E
. Setting

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle:=\bigl\{\theta w:\theta\in[1/2,3/2],w\in B_{\mu}\bigr\},

\displaystyle U_{E}

\displaystyle:=\bigl\{\theta w:\theta\in(1/2,3/2),w\in U\bigr\},

it is not difficult to check that
u\in B\subset E
,
U_{E}
is a neighborhood of
u
in
E
and
B\cap\overline{U_{E}}
is closed in
E
. For any
w\in\overline{U_{E}}
, we define

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}(w):=\frac{|w|}{\sqrt{\mu}}V(\sqrt{\mu}w/|w|)\in E.

Let us check that
\widetilde{V}
is a Lipschitz mapping. Since
V
is Lipschitz, for any
w,w^{\prime}\in\overline{U}
,

\displaystyle\|V(w^{\prime})-V(w)\|\leq C\|w^{\prime}-w\|.

Here and in the sequel in this proof,
C>0
is a constant which may change line by line. Then, for any
\theta w,\theta^{\prime}w^{\prime}\in\overline{U_{E}}
with
w,w^{\prime}\in\overline{U}
,

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{V}(\theta^{\prime}w^{\prime})-\widetilde{V}(\theta w)\|

\displaystyle=\|\theta^{\prime}V(w^{\prime})-\theta V(w)\|

\displaystyle\leq\theta^{\prime}\|V(w^{\prime})-V(w)\|+|\theta^{\prime}-\theta|\|V(w)\|

\displaystyle\leq C\theta^{\prime}\|w^{\prime}-w\|+|\theta^{\prime}-\theta|\|V(w)\|.

Note that
\theta^{\prime}=|\theta^{\prime}w^{\prime}|/\sqrt{\mu}
and
\theta=|\theta w|/\sqrt{\mu}
. We deduce

\displaystyle|\theta^{\prime}-\theta|\leq\mu^{-\frac{1}{2}}|\theta^{\prime}w^{\prime}-\theta w|.

Hence,

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{V}(\theta^{\prime}w^{\prime})-\widetilde{V}(\theta w)\|

\displaystyle\leq C\|\theta^{\prime}w^{\prime}-\theta^{\prime}w\|+|\theta^{\prime}-\theta|\|V(w)\|

\displaystyle\leq C\|\theta^{\prime}w^{\prime}-\theta w\|+\left(C\|w\|+\|V(w)\|\right)|\theta^{\prime}-\theta|

\displaystyle\leq C\|\theta^{\prime}w^{\prime}-\theta w\|+C\mu^{-\frac{1}{2}}|\theta^{\prime}w^{\prime}-\theta w|

\displaystyle\leq C\|\theta^{\prime}w^{\prime}-\theta w\|.

Now, we apply Proposition
4.4
with
X=E
and
\widetilde{V}(u)=\mu^{-1/2}|u|V(\sqrt{\mu}u/|u|)
. We verify (
4.3
). For any
\theta w\in\overline{U_{E}}\cap B
with
w\in\overline{U}\cap B_{\mu}
, using (
4.4
) we get the existence of
v_{s}\in B_{\mu}
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{s\searrow 0}s^{-1}\|w+sV(w)-v_{s}\|=0.

Hence,

\displaystyle\lim_{s\searrow 0}s^{-1}dist(\theta w+s\widetilde{V}(\theta w),B)

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{s\searrow 0}s^{-1}\|\theta w+s\theta V(w)-\theta v_{s}\|=0.

Then, by Proposition
4.4
we obtain the existence of
r=r(u)>0
and
\tilde{\eta}(t,u)
such that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\tilde{\eta}(t,u)=\widetilde{V}(\tilde{\eta}(t,u)),\quad\forall t\in[0,r),\\
&\tilde{\eta}(0,u)=u\in B_{\mu},\quad\tilde{\eta}(t,u)\in B.\end{aligned}\right.

Let
\eta(\cdot,u):[0,T)\to S_{\mu}
be the local solution of

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\eta(t,u)=V(\eta(t,u)),\\
&\eta(0,u)=u.\end{aligned}\right.

Clearly
\eta(\cdot,u)
can be viewed as a function mapping
[0,T)
to
E
since
S_{\mu}\subset E
. It is not difficult to check that
\eta(\cdot,u):[0,T)\to E
satisfies

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\eta(t,u)=\widetilde{V}(\eta(t,u)),\\
&\eta(0,u)=u.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.5)

By the local existence and uniqueness of the solution for equation (
4.5
) and by decreasing
r>0
if necessary, we know that
\eta(t,u)\equiv\tilde{\eta}(t,u)
for
t\in[0,r)
. Hence,
\eta(t,u)\in B\cap S_{u}=B_{\mu}
for all
t\in[0,r)
and this completes the proof.
∎

The following corollary of Proposition
4.5
will be crucial for our problem.

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
B_{\mu}\subset S_{\mu}
be closed in
S_{\mu}
.
Suppose that
V
is a locally Lipschitz mapping with
V(u)\in T_{u}S_{\mu}
for any
u\in S_{\mu}
and define
\eta(t,\cdot):S_{\mu}\to S_{\mu}
by

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\eta(t,u)=V(\eta(t,u)),\\
&\eta(0,u)=u.\end{aligned}\right.

Let
T_{u}>0
be the maximal time such that
\eta(\cdot,u)
exists for any
u\in S_{\mu}
and
B^{*}\subset S_{\mu}
be an open subset such that
\eta(t,u)\in B^{*}
for any
u\in B^{*}
and all
0<t<T_{u}
. Then, if

\displaystyle\lim_{s\searrow 0}s^{-1}dist(u+sV(u),B_{\mu})=0,\quad\forall u\in B_{\mu}\cap B^{*},

(4.6)

it holds that
\eta(t,u)\in B_{\mu}\cap B^{*}
for any
u\in B_{\mu}\cap B^{*}
and all
0<t<T_{u}
.

Proof.

For any
u\in B_{\mu}\cap B^{*}
, since
B^{*}
is open and
V
is a locally Lipschitz mapping, we can choose a neighborhood
U
of
u
in
S_{\mu}
such that
\overline{U}\subset B^{*}
and
V
is Lipschitz on
\overline{U}
. Also, it is clear that
B_{\mu}\cap\overline{U}
is closed since
B_{\mu}
is (locally) closed in
S_{\mu}
and that (
4.6
) implies

\displaystyle\lim_{s\searrow 0}s^{-1}dist(w+sV(w),B_{\mu})=0,\quad\forall w\in\overline{U}\cap B_{\mu}.

Proposition
4.5
then yields that
\eta(t,u)\in B_{\mu}
for all
t\in[0,r)
for some
0<r\leq T_{u}
. By the assumption on
B^{*}
, we know
\eta(t,u)\in B^{*}
for all
0<t<T_{u}
. Let

\displaystyle r^{*}:=\sup\bigl\{T:0<T<T_{u},\eta(t,u)\in B_{\mu}\text{ in }t\in[0,T)\bigr\}.

Obviously,
r*\geq r
. We claim that
r^{*}=T_{u}
. By contradiction, we suppose
r^{*}<T_{u}
. According to the definition of
r^{*}
, we know that
\eta(t,u)\in B_{\mu}
for any
t\in[0,r^{*})
. Using the closedness of
B_{\mu}
we deduce that
\eta(r^{*},u)\in B_{\mu}
. Since
\eta(r^{*},u)\in B^{*}
, that is,
\eta(r^{*},u)\in B_{\mu}\cap B^{*}
, using Proposition
4.5
again we have
\eta(t,u)\in B_{\mu}
in
t\in[0,r^{*}+\bar{r})
for some
0<\bar{r}\leq T_{u}-r^{*}
. This contradicts the definition of
r^{*}
and completes the proof.
∎

In the next lemma, we give a condition to check that hypothesis (
4.4
) (or hypothesis (
4.6
)) holds.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
B_{\mu}=\tilde{B}\cap S_{\mu}
where
\tilde{B}\subset E
is closed, convex in
E
and satisfies the assumption:

\displaystyle kw\in\tilde{B},\quad\forall k\in(0,1),\ \forall w\in\tilde{B}.

(4.7)

For
u\in B_{\mu}
, if
V(u)
has the form
G(u)-u
with
G(u)\in\tilde{B}
, then

\displaystyle\lim_{s\searrow 0}s^{-1}dist(u+sV(u),B_{\mu})=0.

(4.8)

Proof.

Given
s>0
, let

u_{s}=u+sV(u)
where
u\in B_{\mu}\subset S_{\mu}
. Since
V(u)\in T_{u}S_{u}
,

\displaystyle|u_{s}|^{2}

\displaystyle=(u+sV(u),u+sV(u))

\displaystyle=(u,u)+2s(u,V(u))+s^{2}(V(u),V(u))

\displaystyle=\mu+s^{2}|V(u)|^{2}.

For
s\in(0,1)
, we have
u_{s}=sG(u)+(1-s)u\in\tilde{B}
by the convexity of
\tilde{B}
. Then, since
\sqrt{\mu}/|u_{s}|<1
, using (
4.7
) we know
\sqrt{\mu}u_{s}/|u_{s}|\in\tilde{B}\cap S_{\mu}=B_{\mu}
. Hence,

\displaystyle\lim_{s\searrow 0}s^{-1}dist(u+sV(u),B_{\mu})

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{s\searrow 0}s^{-1}\bigl\|u_{s}-\frac{\sqrt{\mu}u_{s}}{|u_{s}|}\bigr\|

\displaystyle=\lim_{s\searrow 0}\frac{s|V(u)|^{2}}{2\mu}\|u_{s}\|=0,

which completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.8
.

Let the gradient
\nabla E(\cdot,\mathcal{G})
be written in the form
\nabla E(u,\mathcal{G})=u-G(u)\in T_{u}H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
for
u\in H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
, where
T_{u}H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
is the tangent space of
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
at
u
defined as

\displaystyle T_{u}H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G}):=\bigl\{\phi\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G}):\int_{\mathcal{G}}u\phi dx=0\bigr\}.

Then there exists
\nu^{*}>0
such that when
0<\nu<\nu^{*}
, we have
G((\pm\mathcal{P})_{\nu}\cap\mathcal{B}_{\rho^{*}}^{\mu})\subset(\pm\mathcal{P})_{\nu/2}
for
0<\mu\leq\tilde{\mu}
, where
\tilde{\mu}
is defined in Proposition
4.3
.

Proof.

By the definition of the gradient,
G(u)
can be expressed as
(-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+1)^{-1}(|u|^{p-2}u+\lambda_{u}u)
, where
\lambda_{u}\in\mathbb{R}
depends on

and is determined by the condition
u-G(u)\in T_{u}H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{G}}(|u|^{p-2}u+\lambda_{u}u)(-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+1)^{-1}u\,dx=\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u|^{2}dx=\mu.

(4.9)

Let
\xi=(-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+1)^{-1}u
for
u\in H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
and note that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{G}}u\xi dx=\|\xi\|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{G})}^{2}>0.

(4.10)

Recall that
\rho^{*}\leq b
where
b=\left(\frac{2}{(p-2)K}\right)^{\frac{4}{p-6}}
. For any
u\in\mathcal{B}_{\rho^{*}}^{\mu}
, we have, using (
2.3
)
and that
-\xi^{\prime\prime}+\xi=u
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{G}}|\xi|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\leq pK\left(\int_{\mathcal{G}}|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{p-2}{4}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{G}}|\xi|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{p+2}{4}}+p\ell^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{G}}|\xi|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq pK\left(\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u^{\prime}|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{p-2}{4}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{p+2}{4}}+p\ell^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}.

Then, using Hölder inequality and (
2.3
) again, we obtain

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u|^{p-2}u\xi dx\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{G}}|\xi|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq pK\mu^{\frac{p+2}{4}}(\rho^{*})^{\frac{p-2}{4}}+p\ell^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\mu^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq pKb^{\frac{p-2}{4}}\mu^{\frac{p+2}{4}}+p\ell^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\mu^{\frac{p}{2}}.

(4.11)

When
0<\mu\leq\tilde{\mu}
, (
4.11
) shows that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u|^{p-2}u\xi dx\leq\mu,\quad\forall u\in\mathcal{B}_{\rho^{*}}^{\mu}.

(4.12)

By (
4.9
), (
4.10
) and (
4.12
), we obtain that
\lambda_{u}\geq 0
for any
u\in\mathcal{B}_{\rho^{*}}^{\mu}
if
0<\mu\leq\tilde{\mu}
. Moreover, we claim that
\lambda_{u}\leq\bar{\lambda}
for some
\bar{\lambda}<\infty
independent of
u\in\mathcal{B}_{\rho^{*}}^{\mu}
. Using (
4.9
), (
4.10
) and (
4.12
) again, it suffices to show the existence of
\epsilon_{0}>0
independent of
u\in\mathcal{B}_{\rho^{*}}^{\mu}
such that
\|\xi\|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{G})}\geq\epsilon_{0}
. By contradiction, we assume that there exists a sequence
\{u_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{B}_{\rho^{*}}^{\mu}
such that
\xi_{n}\to 0
strongly in
H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
. Up to a subsequence, we can suppose that

\displaystyle u_{n}\rightharpoonup\bar{u}\quad\text{weakly in }H^{1}(\mathcal{G}),

\displaystyle u_{n}\to\bar{u}\quad\text{strongly in }L^{2}(\mathcal{G}),

for some
\bar{u}\in\overline{\mathcal{B}_{\rho^{*}}^{\mu}}
. Let
\bar{\xi}=(-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+1)^{-1}\bar{u}\neq 0
. On one hand,
\bar{\xi}\neq 0
since
\bar{u}\neq 0
. On the other hand,
\{\xi_{n}\}
strongly converges to
\bar{\xi}
in

. Indeed,

\displaystyle\|\xi_{n}-\bar{\xi}\|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{G})}^{2}=\int_{\mathcal{G}}(u_{n}-\bar{u})(\xi_{n}-\bar{\xi})dx\leq\left(\int_{\mathcal{G}}(u_{n}-\bar{u})^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{G}}(\xi_{n}-\bar{\xi})^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\to 0.

(4.13)

Next, let
u\in(-\mathcal{P})_{\nu}\cap\mathcal{B}_{\rho^{*}}^{\mu}
,

,

. Then

\displaystyle\quad\ dist(z,-\mathcal{P})\|z^{+}\|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{G})}\leq\|z^{+}\|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{G})}^{2}=\langle z,z^{+}\rangle_{H^{1}(\mathcal{G})}=\int_{\mathcal{G}}z^{+}(-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+1)zdx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{G}}(|u|^{p-2}u+\lambda_{u}u)z^{+}dx\leq\int_{\mathcal{G}}(|u^{+}|^{p-2}u^{+}z^{+}+\lambda_{u}u^{+}z^{+})dx

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u^{+}|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{G}}|z^{+}|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\bar{\lambda}|\{x\in\mathcal{G}:z(x)>0\}|^{\frac{p-2}{p}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u^{+}|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{G}}|z^{+}|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\left(\inf_{w\in-\mathcal{P}}\|u-w\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{G})}^{p-1}+\bar{\lambda}|\{x\in\mathcal{G}:z(x)>0\}|^{\frac{p-2}{p}}\inf_{w\in-\mathcal{P}}\|u-w\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{G})}\right)\|z^{+}\|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{G})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\left(\inf_{w\in-\mathcal{P}}\|u-w\|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{G})}^{p-1}+\bar{\lambda}|\{x\in\mathcal{G}:z(x)>0\}|^{\frac{p-2}{p}}\inf_{w\in-\mathcal{P}}\|u-w\|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{G})}\right)\|z^{+}\|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{G})},

where
C_{1},C_{2}
are positive constants deduced from the Sobolev embedding
H^{1}(\mathcal{G})\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\mathcal{G})
, and we obtain that

\displaystyle dist(v,-\mathcal{P})\leq C_{2}\left(\nu^{p-2}+\bar{\lambda}|\{x\in\mathcal{G}:z(x)>0\}|^{\frac{p-2}{p}}\right)\nu.

(4.14)

In order to conclude that
z=G(u)\in(-\mathcal{P})_{\nu/2}
with sufficiently small
\nu>0
, it is sufficient to prove that
\sup\{|\{x\in\mathcal{G}:G(u)(x)>0\}|:u\in(-\mathcal{P})_{\nu}\cap\mathcal{B}_{\rho^{*}}^{\mu}\}
is sufficiently small when
\nu>0
is small enough. Arguing by contradiction, we suppose the existence of sequences
\nu_{n}\to 0
and
\{u_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{B}_{\rho^{*}}^{\mu}
such that
u_{n}\in(-\mathcal{P})_{\nu_{n}}
and

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}|\{x\in\mathcal{G}:G(u_{n})(x)>0\}|>0.

(4.15)

Up to a subsequence, we have

\displaystyle u_{n}\rightharpoonup\bar{u}\quad\text{weakly in }H^{1}(\mathcal{G}),

\displaystyle u_{n}\to\bar{u}\quad\text{strongly in }L^{q}(\mathcal{G})\text{ for any }q\geq 1,

for some
\bar{u}\in\overline{\mathcal{B}_{\rho^{*}}^{\mu}}
. Setting
\xi_{n}=(-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+1)^{-1}u_{n}
and
\bar{\xi}=(-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+1)^{-1}\bar{u}
, we have, as in (
4.13
), that
\xi_{n}\to\bar{\xi}
strongly in
H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
. Then, in view of (
4.9
) we get
\lambda_{u_{n}}\to\lambda_{\bar{u}}
. Now, since
|u_{n}|^{p-1}u_{n}\to|u|^{p-1}u
strongly in
L^{2}(\mathcal{G})
, by a similar argument to (
4.13
) we deduce that
G(u_{n})\to G(\bar{u})
strongly in
H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
. Since
u_{n}\in(-\mathcal{P})_{\nu_{n}}
with
\nu_{n}\to 0
, we conclude that
\bar{u}\leq 0
, and so
(-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+1)G(\bar{u})=|\bar{u}|^{p-2}\bar{u}+\lambda_{\bar{u}}\bar{u}\leq 0
. Moreover, by (
4.14
) we have
G(\bar{u})\in-\mathcal{P}
, that is,
G(\bar{u})\leq 0
. Then, using the strong maximum principle we get
G(\bar{u})<0
in
\mathcal{G}
, which, together with the compactness of
\mathcal{G}
, shows that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}|\{x\in\mathcal{G}:G(u_{n})(x)>0\}|=0,

contradicting (
4.15
). Thus
G(u)\in(-\mathcal{P})_{\nu/2}
if
\nu>0
is sufficiently small. A similar argument works for the
+
sign.
∎

Now we can prove the main result in this section.

Proof of Proposition
4.3
.

Given
\nu\in(0,\nu^{*})
, we use Corollary
4.6
with
B_{\mu}=(\pm\mathcal{P})_{\nu}\cap H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
and
B^{*}=\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}
. The fact that (
4.6
) is satisfied is a consequence of Lemma
4.7
used with
\tilde{B}=(\pm\mathcal{P})_{\nu}
. Indeed, we know from Lemma
4.8
that
G(u)\in(\pm\mathcal{P})_{\nu/2}
if
u\in(\pm\mathcal{P})_{\nu}\cap\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}
and thus (
4.8
), or equivalently (
4.6
), holds for any
u\in(\pm\mathcal{P})_{\nu}\cap\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}=B_{\mu}\cap\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}.
Applying Corollary
4.6
we obtain that
\eta(t,u)\in D^{*}(\nu)\cap\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}
for any
u\in D^{*}(\nu)\cap\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}
and all
0<t<T_{u}
. However, Lemma
4.1
states that if
u\in\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}
, then
\eta(t,u)
exists for all
t>0
with

\eta(t,u)\in\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}
for all
t>0
. Thus we finally get that, for all
u\in D^{*}(\nu)\cap\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}
,
\eta(t,u)\in D^{*}(\nu)\cap\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}
for all
t>0
, completing the proof.
∎

5
A pair of sign-changing critical points

We will complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
in this section.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
\mu_{2}=\min\{\hat{\mu},\bar{\mu},\mu^{*},\tilde{\mu}\}
. With
\nu>0
sufficiently small, we first prove that
\mathcal{K}[c_{1}-\bar{\epsilon},c_{1}+\bar{\epsilon}]\cap\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}\cap S^{*}(\nu)\neq\emptyset
for
0<\mu<\mu_{2}
and for any
\bar{\epsilon}>0
, where

c_{1}=\inf_{A\in\mathcal{L}_{1}}\sup_{u\in A\cap S^{*}(\nu)}E(u,\mathcal{G})\quad

was defined in Section
3
.

Before proceeding, we give a lemma, which is useful to choose
\delta_{1}
used to define the descending flow.

Lemma 5.1
.

For any
u\in\mathcal{B}^{\beta}
with
\beta<M_{2}
, there exists
\tilde{\delta}>0
independent of
u
such that

\displaystyle B_{\tilde{\delta}}(u):=\bigl\{w\in H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G}):\|u-w\|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{G})}<\tilde{\delta}\bigr\}\subset\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}.

(5.1)

Proof.

Note that the functions in
\mathcal{B}^{\beta}
are uniformly bounded in
H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
. Hence, for
u\in\mathcal{B}^{\beta}
with
\beta<M_{2}
, there exists
\tilde{\delta}>0
independent of
u
such that
\sup_{w\in B_{\tilde{\delta}}(u)}E(w,\mathcal{G})<M_{2}
. Since
\inf_{u\in\mathcal{U}_{\rho^{*}}^{\mu}}E(u,\mathcal{G})\geq M_{2}
where
\mathcal{U}_{\rho^{*}}^{\mu}
is the boundary of
\mathcal{B}_{\rho^{*}}^{\mu}
and
\mathcal{B}_{\rho^{*}}^{\mu}
is open, we deduce that
B_{\tilde{\delta}}(u)\subset\mathcal{B}_{\rho^{*}}^{\mu}
. Therefore,
B_{\tilde{\delta}}(u)\subset\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}
, which completes the proof.
∎

To prove Theorem
1.1
, we argue by contradiction and suppose there exists some
\bar{\epsilon}>0
such that
\mathcal{K}[c_{1}-\bar{\epsilon},c_{1}+\bar{\epsilon}]\cap\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}\subset D^{*}(\nu)
(this also holds if
\mathcal{K}[c_{1}-\bar{\epsilon},c_{1}+\bar{\epsilon}]\cap\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}=\emptyset
). Then, assuming
\nu<\nu^{*}
and using Lemma
4.8
, we obtain that
\mathcal{K}[c_{1}-\bar{\epsilon},c_{1}+\bar{\epsilon}]\cap\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}\subset\mathcal{P}\cup(-\mathcal{P})
. By the definition of
c_{1}
, there exist
A\in\mathcal{L}_{1}
and
0<\epsilon_{1}\leq\bar{\epsilon}/3
such that
\sup_{A\cap S^{*}(\nu)}E(\cdot,\mathcal{G})<c_{1}+\epsilon_{1}<M_{2}
. By Lemma
3.4
, we know that
\underline{c}_{1}<\bar{c}_{1}
. Hence, by decreasing
\epsilon_{1}
, we can assume that
3\epsilon_{1}\leq\bar{c}_{1}-\underline{c}_{1}\leq c_{1}-\underline{c}_{1}
. Note that
A\subset(\mathcal{B}^{c_{1}+\epsilon_{1}}\cap S^{*}(\nu))\cup(\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}\cap D^{*}(\nu))
. Now we take
\delta_{1}>0
such that
\delta_{1}<\nu
and
\delta_{1}\leq\tilde{\delta}
, where
\tilde{\delta}
is given in Lemma
5.1
with
\beta:=c_{1}+\epsilon_{1}<M_{2}
. For any
u\in\mathcal{B}^{c_{1}+\epsilon_{1}}\cap S^{*}(\nu)
, we claim that
dist(u,\mathcal{K}[c_{1}-\bar{\epsilon},c_{1}+\bar{\epsilon}])\geq\delta_{1}
. Otherwise, there exists
w\in\mathcal{K}[c_{1}-\bar{\epsilon},c_{1}+\bar{\epsilon}]
such that
\|u-w\|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{G})}<\delta_{1}
. Since
\delta_{1}<\tilde{\delta}
, using Lemma
5.1
we deduce that
w\in\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}
, and so
w\in\mathcal{P}\cup(-\mathcal{P})
. Observing that
u\in S^{*}(\nu)
, we have reached a contradiction since
\delta_{1}<\nu
.

Let
\eta(t,\cdot)
be the decreasing flow defined in Section
4
.
We claim that

\eta(2\epsilon_{1},A)\subset D^{*}(\nu)\cup\mathcal{B}^{c_{1}-\epsilon_{1}}.

(5.2)

In fact, for
u\in A
, if
\eta(t,u)\in D^{*}(\nu)
and so
\eta(t,u)\in D^{*}(\nu)\cap\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}
for some
t\in[0,2\epsilon_{1}]
, Proposition
4.3
yields that
\eta(2\epsilon_{1},u)\in D^{*}(\nu)
. If
\eta(t,u)\in\mathcal{B}^{c_{1}-\epsilon_{1}}
for some
t\in[0,2\epsilon_{1}]
, since it is readily seen that
E(\eta(t,u),\mathcal{G})
is nonincreasing in
t\geq 0
, we obtain that
\eta(2\epsilon_{1},u)\in\mathcal{B}^{c_{1}-\epsilon_{1}}
. If we are not in one of these two cases, necessarily

\eta(t,u)\in S^{*}(\nu)\cap(\mathcal{B}^{c_{1}+\epsilon_{1}}\backslash\mathcal{B}^{c_{1}-\epsilon_{1}}),\quad\mbox{for all }t\in[0,2\epsilon_{1}].

(5.3)

Let us show, by contradiction, that (
5.3
) is not true. This will prove that (
5.2
) holds. Assuming (
5.3
), we observe that for
t\in[0,2\epsilon_{1}]
,
y(\eta(t,u))=1
since we have proved that
dist(\eta(t,u),\mathcal{K}[c_{1}-\bar{\epsilon},c_{1}+\bar{\epsilon}])\geq\delta_{1}
when
\eta(t,u)\in\mathcal{B}^{c_{1}+\epsilon_{1}}\cap S^{*}(\nu)
. Also
h(\eta(t,u))=1
since
\eta(t,u)\in U_{2}
. Then we have

\displaystyle E(\eta(2\epsilon_{1},u),\mathcal{G})

\displaystyle=E(u,\mathcal{G})+\int_{0}^{2\epsilon_{1}}dE(\eta(t,u),\mathcal{G})

\displaystyle=E(u,\mathcal{G})+\int_{0}^{2\epsilon_{1}}\bigl\langle\nabla E(\eta(t,u),\mathcal{G}),\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\eta(t,u)\bigr\rangle_{H^{1}(\mathcal{G})}

\displaystyle=E(u,\mathcal{G})-2\epsilon_{1}

\displaystyle<c_{1}+\epsilon_{1}-2\epsilon_{1}

\displaystyle=c_{1}-\epsilon_{1},

which implies that
\eta(2\epsilon_{1},u)\in\mathcal{B}^{c_{1}-\epsilon_{1}}
and this contradicts (
5.3
). Thus (
5.2
) holds.

We will now show that (
5.2
) is impossible. This will prove that the hypothesis that there exists a certain
\bar{\epsilon}>0
such that
\mathcal{K}[c_{1}-\bar{\epsilon},c_{1}+\bar{\epsilon}]\cap\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}\subset D^{*}(\nu)
was wrong. Since
\epsilon_{1}>0
is chosen as
3\epsilon_{1}\leq\bar{c}_{1}-\underline{c}_{1}\leq c_{1}-\underline{c}_{1}
, it holds that
\mathbb{S}_{1}\subset U_{1}
where
U_{1}
is given in the definition of
\eta(t,\cdot)
, and so

\eta(t,\cdot)|_{\mathbb{S}_{1}}=\textbf{id}
for all
t\geq 0
. Hence,
\eta(2\epsilon_{1},\cdot)
defines a continuous function from
A
to
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
with

\eta(2\epsilon_{1},\cdot)|_{\mathbb{S}_{1}}=\textbf{id}
. We will show that
\eta(2\epsilon_{1},A)\in\mathcal{L}_{1}
, for which, it suffices to prove that
\eta(2\epsilon_{1},A)
is linked to
\mathbb{S}_{1}^{\perp}
. Let
f\in C(\eta(2\epsilon_{1},A),H_{\mu}^{1}(\mathcal{G}))
satisfying
f|_{\mathbb{S}_{1}}=\textbf{id}
. Then
f\circ\eta(2\epsilon_{1},\cdot)
is a continuous map from
A
to
H_{\mu}^{1}(\mathcal{G})
satisfying
f\circ\eta(2\epsilon_{1},\cdot)|_{\mathbb{S}_{1}}=\textbf{id}
, and there exists
u\in f\circ\eta(2\epsilon_{1},A)\cap\mathbb{S}_{1}^{\perp}
, that is,
f(\eta(2\epsilon_{1},A))\cap\mathbb{S}_{1}^{\perp}\neq\emptyset
. At this point, using
\eta(2\epsilon_{1},A)\in\mathcal{L}_{1}
and the definition of
c_{1}
it follows that (
5.2
) is indeed impossible.

Therefore, for
0<\mu<\mu_{2}
and any
\bar{\epsilon}>0
,
E(u,\mathcal{G})
has a constrained critical point
u_{2,\bar{\epsilon}}
in
\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}\cap S^{*}(\nu)
with
E(u_{2,\bar{\epsilon}},\mathcal{G})\in[c_{1}-\bar{\epsilon},c_{1}+\bar{\epsilon}]
. By Corollary
2.2
, we can get a critical point
u_{2}
at level
c_{1}
. Moreover,
u_{2}
is contained in
\overline{S^{*}(\nu)}
and thus is sign-changing. Obviously,
u_{2}
is non-constant and
-u_{2}\not\equiv u_{2}
is also a sign-changing critical point of
E(u,\mathcal{G})
constrained to
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
.

For
0<\mu_{1}
defined in (
1.5
), we know from
[
14
, Theorem 1.1]
that
E(\cdot,\mathcal{G})
has, for
0<\mu<\mu_{1}
, a positive critical point
u_{1}
in
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
at a mountain pass level. Obviously,
-u_{1}
is a negative critical point at the same mountain pass level. Thus, if
\mu_{2}\leq\mu_{1}
, we obtain the desired result in Theorem
1.1
. If
\mu_{2}>\mu_{1}
, we just redefine
\mu_{2}:=\min\{\mu_{1},\hat{\mu},\bar{\mu},\mu^{*},\tilde{\mu}\}
. The proof of Theorem
1.1
is now completed.
∎

6
More and more sign-changing critical points by decreasing the mass if necessary and a bifurcation phenomena

The goal of this section is to give the proof of Theorem
1.3
and of Theorem
1.4
. As the proof of Theorem
1.3
is quite similar to the one of Theorem
1.1
we will not give the full details.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

We divide the proof into four steps.

Step 1:
The min-max structures.

Let
0=\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{G})<\lambda_{2}(\mathcal{G})\leq\lambda_{3}(\mathcal{G})\leq\cdots
be the eigenvalues of
-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}
on the compact metric graph
\mathcal{G}
with Kirchhoff condition at the vertices, and let
\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\phi_{3},\cdots
be the corresponding eigenfunctions with
\int_{\mathcal{G}}|\phi_{i}|^{2}dx=1
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
. For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
k\geq 2
and
\lambda_{k-1}(\mathcal{G})<\lambda_{k}(\mathcal{G})
, we set

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}_{k-1}=span\{\phi_{1},\cdots,\phi_{k-1}\}\cap H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G}),\quad\mathbb{S}_{k-1}^{\perp}=(span\{\phi_{1},\cdots,\phi_{k-1}\})^{\perp}\cap H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G}).

Note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}_{k-1}=\left\{u=\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}t_{i}\sqrt{\mu}\phi_{i}:\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}t_{i}^{2}=1\right\}.

We also introduce

\displaystyle Q_{k}:=\left\{u=\sum_{i=1}^{k}t_{i}\sqrt{\mu}\phi_{i}:\sum_{i=1}^{k}t_{i}^{2}=1,t_{k}\geq 0\right\},

with

\displaystyle\partial Q_{k}=\left\{u=\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}t_{i}\sqrt{\mu}\phi_{i}:\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}t_{i}^{2}=1\right\}=\mathbb{S}_{k-1}.

Similarly to (
3.1
), we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{k-1}=\mathcal{L}_{k-1}(\mu):=\bigl\{A\subset\mathcal{B}_{\rho^{*}}^{\mu}:\sup_{A}E(\cdot,\mathcal{G})<M_{2},\ \mathbb{S}_{k-1}\subset A,\ A\text{ is compact and is linked to }\mathbb{S}_{k-1}^{\perp}\bigr\}.

Let
\hat{\mu}_{k}=\hat{\mu}_{k}(\mathcal{G},p)>0
be the positive solution of

\lambda_{k}(\mathcal{G})\mu+2\ell^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\mu^{\frac{p}{2}}=\frac{p-6}{p-2}\left(\frac{2}{(p-2)K}\right)^{\frac{4}{p-6}},

and let
\bar{\mu}_{k}=\bar{\mu}_{k}(\mathcal{G},p)>0
be the positive solution of

\lambda_{k}(\mathcal{G})\mu^{\frac{2(p-2)}{p-6}}+2\ell^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\mu^{\frac{(p-2)^{2}}{2(p-6)}}=\frac{p-6}{p-2}\left(\frac{2}{(p-2)K}\right)^{\frac{4}{p-6}}.

Reasoning as in Lemma
3.1
, we observe that
Q_{k}\subset\mathcal{L}_{k-1}
and thus
\mathcal{L}_{k-1}
is not empty for
0<\mu<\min\{\hat{\mu}_{k},\bar{\mu}_{k}\}
. Recalling that

\displaystyle\pm\mathcal{P}=\bigl\{u\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G}):\pm u\geq 0\bigl\},

\displaystyle(\pm\mathcal{P})_{\nu}=\bigl\{u\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G}):\inf_{w\in\pm\mathcal{P}}\|u-w\|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{G})}\leq\nu\bigr\},\quad\text{for some }\nu>0,

\displaystyle\ D^{*}(\nu)=\mathcal{P}_{\nu}\cup(-\mathcal{P})_{\nu},\quad S^{*}(\nu)=H^{1}(\mathcal{G})\backslash D^{*}(\nu),

and that

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}:=\bigl\{u\in\mathcal{B}_{\rho^{*}}^{\mu}:E(u,\mathcal{G})<M_{2}\bigr\},

we define

\displaystyle c_{k-1}:=\inf_{A\in\mathcal{L}_{k-1}}\sup_{u\in A\cap S^{*}(\nu)}E(u,\mathcal{G}),

\displaystyle\bar{c}_{k-1}:=\inf_{u\in\mathbb{S}_{k-1}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}}E(u,\mathcal{G}),

\displaystyle\underline{c}_{k-1}:=\sup_{u\in\mathbb{S}_{k-1}}E(u,\mathcal{G}).

Reasoning as in Lemma
3.2
, we have that
\delta:=\min\{dist(\mathbb{S}_{k-1}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{B}_{\rho^{*}}^{\mu},\mathcal{P}),dist(\mathbb{S}_{k-1}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{B}_{\rho^{*}}^{\mu},-\mathcal{P})\}>0
. As in Lemma
3.3
, for
0<\nu<\delta
, we can show that
c_{k-1}
and
\bar{c}_{k-1}
are well defined and that
-\infty<\bar{c}_{k-1}\leq c_{k-1}<M_{2}
for
0<\mu<\min\{\hat{\mu}_{k},\bar{\mu}_{k}\}
. Also, as in Lemma
3.4
, using that
\lambda_{k-1}(\mathcal{G})<\lambda_{k}(\mathcal{G})
, it follows that
\bar{c}_{k-1}>\underline{c}_{k-1}
for
0<\mu<\min\{\hat{\mu}_{k},\bar{\mu}_{k},\mu^{*}_{k}\}
where
\mu^{*}_{k}=\mu^{*}_{k}(\mathcal{G},p)>0
is the positive solution of

2\left(\ell^{\frac{2-p}{2}}+K\lambda_{k}(\mathcal{G})^{\frac{p-2}{4}}\right)\mu^{\frac{p-2}{2}}=\lambda_{k}(\mathcal{G})-\lambda_{k-1}(\mathcal{G}).

Step 2:
Invariance under the negative gradient flow.

Fix
0<\delta_{k-1}<\nu
and consider the locally Lipschitz continuous map
y_{k-1}(u):H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})\to[0,1]
defined by

\displaystyle y_{k-1}(u):=\left\{\begin{aligned} &1\quad\text{for all }u\in H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})\backslash(\mathcal{K}[c_{k-1}-\bar{\epsilon},c_{k-1}+\bar{\epsilon}])_{\delta_{k-1}/2},\\
&0\quad\text{for all }u\in(\mathcal{K}[c_{k-1}-\bar{\epsilon},c_{k-1}+\bar{\epsilon}])_{\delta_{k-1}/3},\end{aligned}\right.

where
\bar{\epsilon}>0
will be chosen in Step 3 later. Moreover, let

h_{k-1}(u):=\frac{dist(u,U_{1,k-1})}{dist(u,U_{2,k-1})+dist(u,U_{1,k-1})},

where

\displaystyle U_{1,k-1}:=\bigl\{u\in H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G}):|E(u,\mathcal{G})-c_{k-1}|\geq 3\epsilon_{k-1}\bigr\},\quad U_{2,k-1}:=\bigl\{u\in H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G}):|E(u,\mathcal{G})-c_{k-1}|\leq 2\epsilon_{k-1}\bigr\},

and
0<3\epsilon_{k-1}\leq\bar{\epsilon}
will be chosen in Step 3 below. Without loss of generality, we further assume that
\delta_{k-1}\leq\tilde{\delta}
where
\tilde{\delta}
is given in Lemma
5.1
with
\beta:=c_{k-1}+\epsilon_{k-1}<M_{2}
. Let
\eta_{k}(t,\cdot)
be the decreasing flow on
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
defined by

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\eta_{k}(t,u)=-h_{k-1}(\eta_{k}(t,u))y_{k-1}(\eta_{k}(t,u))\|\nabla E(\eta_{k}(t,u),\mathcal{G})\|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{G})}^{-2}\nabla E(\eta_{k}(t,u),\mathcal{G}),\\
&\eta_{k}(0,u)=u.\end{aligned}\right.

As in Lemma
4.1
, one can show that for any
u\in\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}
,
\eta_{k}(t,u)
exists for
t\in[0,\infty)
, and,
\eta_{k}(t,u)\in\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}
for all
t>0
.

Recall that
\tilde{\mu}=\tilde{\mu}(\mathcal{G},p)
is the positive solution of

\displaystyle pK\left(\frac{2}{(p-2)K}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p-6}}\mu^{\frac{p-2}{4}}+p\ell^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\mu^{\frac{p-2}{2}}=1.

Reasoning as in Proposition
4.3
, one can show that, for sufficiently small
\nu>0
, if
0<\mu\leq\tilde{\mu}
, then
\eta_{k}(t,u)\in D^{*}(\nu)\cap\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}
for any
u\in D^{*}(\nu)\cap\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}
and all
t>0
.

Step 3:
Existence of a sign-changing critical point at level
c_{k-1}
.

Setting
\check{\mu}_{k}=\min\{\hat{\mu}_{k},\bar{\mu}_{k},\mu^{*}_{k},\tilde{\mu}\}
, and taking
\nu>0
sufficiently small, we aim to prove that
\mathcal{K}[c_{k-1}-\bar{\epsilon},c_{k-1}+\bar{\epsilon}]\cap\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}\cap S^{*}(\nu)\neq\emptyset
for
0<\mu<\check{\mu}_{k}
and any
\bar{\epsilon}>0
. By contradiction, we suppose there exists some
\bar{\epsilon}>0
such that
\mathcal{K}[c_{k-1}-\bar{\epsilon},c_{k-1}+\bar{\epsilon}]\cap\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}\subset D^{*}(\nu)
(this also holds if
\mathcal{K}[c_{k-1}-\bar{\epsilon},c_{k-1}+\bar{\epsilon}]\cap\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}=\emptyset
) for
0<\mu<\check{\mu}_{k}
. By the definition of
c_{k-1}
, there exist
A\in\mathcal{L}_{k-1}
and
0<\epsilon_{k-1}\leq\bar{\epsilon}/3
such that
\sup_{A\cap S^{*}(\nu)}E<c_{k-1}+\epsilon_{k-1}<M_{2}
. Since
\underline{c}_{k-1}<\bar{c}_{k-1}
, by decreasing
\epsilon_{k-1}
, we can assume that
3\epsilon_{k-1}\leq\bar{c}_{k-1}-\underline{c}_{k-1}\leq c_{k-1}-\underline{c}_{k-1}
. By arguments similar to the ones in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we can obtain that

\displaystyle\eta_{k}(2\epsilon_{k-1},A)\subset D^{*}(\nu)\cup\mathcal{B}^{c_{k-1}-\epsilon_{k-1}}.

Then, as in Section
5
, we can prove that
\eta_{k}(2\epsilon_{k-1},A)\in\mathcal{L}_{k-1}
and find a contradiction with the definition of
c_{k-1}
. It completes this step and proves that for
0<\mu<\check{\mu}_{k}
and for any
\bar{\epsilon}>0
, there is a constrained critical point
u_{k,\bar{\epsilon}}
in
\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}\cap S^{*}(\nu)
of
E(\cdot,\mathcal{G})
with
E(u_{k,\bar{\epsilon}},\mathcal{G})\in[c_{k-1}-\bar{\epsilon},c_{k-1}+\bar{\epsilon}]
. By Corollary
2.2
, we can get a critical point
u_{k}
at level
c_{k-1}
. Moreover,
u_{k}
is contained in
\overline{S^{*}(\nu)}
and thus is sign-changing.

Step 4:
Distinguishing different critical points.

We rewrite the eigenvalues of
-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}
on the compact metric graph
\mathcal{G}
with Kirchhoff condition at the vertices as

\displaystyle 0=\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{G})<\lambda_{2}(\mathcal{G})\leq\lambda_{3}(\mathcal{G})\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{k_{3}-1}(\mathcal{G})<\lambda_{k_{3}}(\mathcal{G})\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{k_{n}-1}(\mathcal{G})<\lambda_{k_{n}}(\mathcal{G})\leq\cdots

For any
j\in\mathbb{N}
with
j\geq 3
, we can take
\{k_{2},k_{3},\cdots,k_{j}\}
such that
2=k_{2}<k_{3}<\cdots<k_{j}
and
\lambda_{k_{i}}(\mathcal{G})>\lambda_{k_{i}-1}(\mathcal{G})
for any
i\in\{2,3,\cdots,j\}
. Let

\mu_{j}=\min\bigl\{\mu_{1},\check{\mu}_{k_{2}},\cdots,\check{\mu}_{k_{j}}\bigr\}.

By Steps 1, 2, 3, for
0<\mu<\mu_{j}
, there exist
j-1
sign-changing critical points
u_{k_{2}},u_{k_{3}},\cdots,u_{k_{j}}
of
E(\cdot,\mathcal{G})
constrained on
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
such that
E(u_{k_{i}},\mathcal{G})=c_{k_{i}-1}
for any
i\in\{2,3,\cdots,j\}
. We need to show that these critical points are distinct. On the one hand, testing the definition of
c_{k_{i}-1}
with the choice
A=Q_{k_{i}}\subset\mathbb{S}_{k_{i}}
, we get

c_{k_{i}-1}\leq\sup_{u\in\mathbb{S}_{k_{i}}}E(u,\mathcal{G})\leq\sup_{u\in\mathbb{S}_{k_{i+1}-1}}E(u,\mathcal{G})=\underline{c}_{k_{i+1}-1}.

On the other hand, from Step 1, we know that,

c_{k_{i}-1}\geq\bar{c}_{k_{i}-1}>\underline{c}_{k_{i}-1},\quad i\in\{2,3,\cdots,j\}.

Thus we can conclude that

c_{1}=c_{k_{2}-1}\leq\underline{c}_{k_{3}-1}<c_{k_{3}-1}\leq\underline{c}_{k_{4}-1}<\cdots<c_{k_{j-1}-1}\leq\underline{c}_{k_{j}-1}<c_{k_{j}-1},

which yields that
u_{k_{i}}\not\equiv u_{k_{n}}
for any
i\neq n
. Obviously,
-u_{k_{2}},-u_{k_{3}},\cdots,-u_{k_{j}}
are also sign-changing critical points of
E(\cdot,\mathcal{G})
constrained on
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
. Then, recalling
[
14
, Theorem 1.1]
completes the proof of Theorem
1.3
.
∎

Finally, we can give

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

We denote by
B_{0}
the set of bifurcation points of (
1.6
). It is classical, see for example,
[
15
]
, that
B_{0}\subset\{\lambda_{i}(\mathcal{G}):i\in\mathbb{N}\}
and that
\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{G})\in B_{0}
since
\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{G})
is simple. To prove the theorem it suffices thus to show that
\lambda_{i}(\mathcal{G})\in B_{0}
for any
i\geq 2
. Recalling our notation
0=\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{G})<\lambda_{2}(\mathcal{G})=\cdots\lambda_{k_{3}-1}(\mathcal{G})<\lambda_{k_{3}}(\mathcal{G})\leq\cdots
, we only need to show that
\lambda_{k_{j}}(\mathcal{G})\in B_{0}
for any
j\geq 2
. From now on, we fix an arbitrary
j\geq 2
. By the proof of Theorem
1.3
, for any
\mu\in(0,\mu_{j})
,
(u_{\mu,j},\lambda_{\mu,j})\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G})\times\mathbb{R}
is solution to (
1.6
) where
\lambda_{\mu,j}
denotes the Lagrange multiplier associated to
u_{\mu,j}
. In addition

E(u_{\mu,j},\mathcal{G})=c_{k_{j}-1}\in\Big[\inf_{u\in\mathbb{S}_{k_{j}-1}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}}E(u,\mathcal{G}),\sup_{u\in\mathbb{S}_{k_{j}}}E(u,\mathcal{G})\Big].

(6.1)

Reasoning as in the proof of Lemma
3.4
we get

\displaystyle\inf_{u\in\mathbb{S}_{k_{j}-1}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{B}^{M_{2}}}E(u,\mathcal{G})

\displaystyle\geq g(\lambda_{k_{j}}(\mathcal{G})\mu)-\ell^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\mu^{\frac{p}{2}}.

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k_{j}}\mu-K\mu^{\frac{p+2}{4}}(\lambda_{k_{j}}\mu)^{\frac{p-2}{4}}-\ell^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\mu^{\frac{p}{2}}.

(6.2)

Clearly also,

\sup_{u\in{\mathbb{S}_{k_{j}}}}E(u,\mathcal{G})\leq\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k_{j}}\mu.

Thus,

E(u_{\mu,j},\mathcal{G})\in\Big[\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k_{j}}\mu-K\mu^{\frac{p+2}{4}}(\lambda_{k_{j}}\mu)^{\frac{p-2}{4}}-\ell^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\mu^{\frac{p}{2}},\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k_{j}}\mu\Big].

We then have

\displaystyle\frac{E(u_{\mu,j},\mathcal{G})}{\mu}\to\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k_{j}},\quad\mbox{as }\mu\to 0^{+}.

(6.3)

Now, from Lemma
2.4
and since
\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u^{\prime}_{\mu,j}|^{2}dx\leq\rho^{*}
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u_{\mu,j}|^{p}dx}{\mu}\to 0,\quad\mbox{as }\mu\to 0^{+}.

(6.4)

Combining (
6.3
) and (
6.4
), it follows

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u_{\mu,j}^{\prime}|^{2}dx}{\mu}\to\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k_{j}},\quad\mbox{as }\mu\to 0^{+}.

Finally, from (
1.6
), we deduce that

\displaystyle-\lambda_{\mu,j}=\frac{\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u_{\mu,j}^{\prime}|^{2}dx}{\mu}-\frac{\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u_{\mu,j}|^{p}dx}{\mu}\to\lambda_{k_{j}},\quad\mbox{as }\mu\to 0^{+}.

Since
\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u_{\mu,j}^{\prime}|^{2}dx\to 0
as
\mu\to 0^{+}
, this shows that
\lambda_{k_{j}}
is a bifurcation point and ends the proof.
∎

Matched pairs and double construction bialgebras of (transposed) Poisson 3-Lie algebras
Kecheng Zhou, Chuangchuang Kang, Jiafeng Lü

Abstract
. Double construction bialgebras for Poisson 3-Lie algebras and transposed Poisson 3-Lie algebras are defined and studied using matched pairs. Poisson 3-Lie algebras and transposed Poisson 3-Lie algebras are constructed on direct sums and tensor products of vector spaces. Matched pairs, Manin triples, and double construction Poisson 3-Lie bialgebras are shown to be equivalent. Since the double construction approach does not apply to bialgebra theory for transposed Poisson 3-Lie algebras, admissible transposed Poisson 3-Lie algebras are introduced. These algebras are both transposed Poisson 3-Lie algebras and Poisson 3-Lie algebras. An equivalence between matched pairs and double construction admissible transposed Poisson 3-Lie bialgebras is established.

1.
Introduction

The aim of this paper is to construct matched pairs and double construction bialgebras for Poisson 3-Lie algebras and transposed Poisson 3-Lie algebras, motivated by the question
[
13
, Question 35]
: define and study transposed Poisson bialgebras.

1.1.
(Transposed) Poisson algebras

Poisson algebras originated from the study of Hamiltonian mechanics and are connected to various fields, including Poisson manifolds
[
11
]
, quantum groups
[
12
]
, operads
[
20
]
, quantization theory
[
32
]
, quantum mechanics
[
42
]
and algebraic geometry
[
43
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

A Poisson algebra is a triple
(A,\cdot,[\cdot,\cdot])
, where
(A,\cdot)
is a commutative associative algebra and
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
is a Lie algebra satisfying the Leibniz rule:

[x\cdot y,z]=x\cdot[y,z]+y\cdot[x,z],\quad\forall~x,y,z\in A.

Bai, Bai, Guo, and Wu
[
6
]
introduced the concept of a transposed Poisson algebra. This is achieved by exchanging the roles of the two binary operations in the Leibniz rule, resulting in the following transposed Leibniz rule:

2x\cdot[y,z]=[x\cdot y,z]+[y,x\cdot z],\quad\forall~x,y,z\in A.

The transposed Poisson algebra shares some properties with the Poisson algebra, such as being constructed from a commutative algebra with two commuting derivations. However, they differ significantly. For example, the Leibniz rule implies that adjoint operators of the Lie algebra are derivations of the commutative associative algebra, while the transposed Leibniz rule shows that left multiplication in the commutative associative algebra is a
\frac{1}{2}
-derivation of the Lie algebra
[
18
]
.

In recent years, transposed Poisson algebras have attracted significant attention.
Beites, Ouaridi and Kaygorodov classified all complex 3-dimensional transposed Poisson algebras
[
9
]
.
Fernández Ouaridi proved that a transposed Poisson algebra is simple if and only if its associated Lie bracket is simple, with analogous results for transposed Poisson superalgebras
[
17
]
.
Using the connection with the
\frac{1}{2}
-derivations, Kaygorodov and Khrypchenko described transposed Poisson structures on block Lie algebras and superalgebras
[
26
]
, generalized Witt algebras
[
27
]
, and Witt type algebras
[
28
]
. Such structures were also studied on complex Galilean type Lie algebras and superalgebras
[
31
]
, not-finitely graded Witt-type algebras
[
29
]
, and Virasoro-type algebras
[
30
]
. For more results and open questions, see
[
13
]
.

1.2.
(Transposed) Poisson
n
-Lie algebras

The notion of Poisson
3
-Lie algebras, or more generally,
Poisson
n
-Lie algebras (also called Nambu-Poisson algebras
[
46
]
) can be seen as a generalization of Poisson algebras to higher arities.
These structures arise from the study of Nambu mechanics
[
39
]
and are applied in Nambu-Poisson manifolds
[
46
]
. See
[
4
]
for more details about Poisson
n
-Lie algebras and applications in mathematical physics.

Definition 1.2
.

An
n
-Lie algebra
[
19
]
is a vector space
A
equipped with an
n
-ary bracket
[\cdot,\cdots,\cdot]:\wedge^{n}A\rightarrow A
, such that for all
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}\in A
, the following Filippov-Jacobi identity holds:

[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},[y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}]]=\sum_{i=1}^{n}[y_{1},\cdots,y_{i-1},[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},y_{i}],y_{i+1},\cdots,y_{n}].

Definition 1.3
.

A Poisson
n
-Lie algebra
[
37
]
is both a commutative associative
algebra
(A,\cdot)
and an
n
-Lie algebra
(A,[\cdot,\cdots,\cdot])
satisfying the generalized Leibniz rule:

[w\cdot x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}]=w\cdot[x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}]+x_{1}\cdot[w,x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}],\quad\forall~x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},w\in A.

Transposed Poisson
n
-Lie algebras, introduced in
[
6
]
, generalize transposed Poisson algebras to the
n
-ary case and are the dual notion of Poisson
n
-Lie algebras.

Definition 1.4
.

A transposed Poisson
n
-Lie algebra
(B,\cdot,[\cdot,\cdots,\cdot])
is both a commutative associative algebra
(B,\cdot)
and an
n
-Lie algebra
(B,[\cdot,\cdots,\cdot])
satisfying:

nw\cdot[x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}]=\sum_{i=1}^{n}[x_{1},\cdots,w\cdot x_{i},\cdots,x_{n}],\quad\forall~x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},w\in B.

With an additional “strongness” condition, a Poisson
n
-Lie algebra with a derivation can construct an
(n+1)
-Lie algebra
[
15
]
. Similarly, in
[
6
]
, Bai, Bai, Guo, and Wu proved that each transposed Poisson algebra defines a transposed Poisson
3
-Lie algebra via the multiplication:

[x,y,z]=\mathfrak{D}(x)\cdot[y,z]-\mathfrak{D}(y)\cdot[x,z]+\mathfrak{D}(z)\cdot[x,y],\quad\forall~x,y,z\in A.

Under certain “strong” conditions, the
n
-ary (
n\geq 3
) conjecture (from
[
13
, Question 37]
) of this construction was solved in
[
22
]
. The concept of
\frac{1}{n}
-derivations for
n
-ary algebras, as a special case of
\delta
-derivations, was introduced by Kaygorodov in
[
25
]
. Using
\frac{1}{n}
-derivations, Ferreira, Kaygorodov, and Lopatkin showed that no non-trivial transposed Poisson
n
-Lie algebra (or superalgebra) structures exist on complex simple finite-dimensional
n
-Lie algebras (or superalgebras)
[
18
]
. Additionally, a classification of transposed Poisson 3-Lie algebras of dimension
3
is given in
[
22
]
, which gives examples of transposed Poisson 3-Lie algebras and partially solves the problem
[
13
, Question 38]
.

1.3.
Matched pairs, Manin triples, and bialgebras of associative algebras

In 1990, Majid introduced the notion of matched pairs of Lie groups
[
36
]
. These can form bicrossproduct groups, Hopf algebras, Hopf-von Neumann algebras, or Kac algebras through cross product and coproduct.
The idea has been generalized to various contexts, including matched pairs of groups related to braided groups and solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation
[
16
]
, matched pairs of Leibniz algebras for structure extension
[
2
]
, and matched pairs of Lie groupoids and Lie algebroids in geometry
[
35
,
38
]
. The notion of a matched pair of Lie algebras (also called double Lie algebras
[
34
]
) was introduced in the study of Lie bialgebras and Poisson-Lie groups, which are equivalent to Manin triples of Lie algebras and related to quantum groups
[
12
]
.

Motivated by the study of Lie bialgebras, the
concepts of matched pairs, double constructions, and
Manin triples for associative algebras were systematically developed
[
5
]
.
A matched pair of associative algebras
A
and
B
include a bimodule
(l_{A},r_{A})
of
A
and a bimodule
(l_{B},r_{B})
of
B
, and satisfy certain compatibility conditions (see Definition
4.7
for the commutative case). This induces an associative algebra structure on the direct sum
A\oplus B
.
A Manin triple of associative algebra
A
is a Frobenius algebra
(A,\mathcal{B})
, where
A=A_{1}\oplus A_{1}^{*}
,
A_{1}
and
A_{1}^{*}
are associative subalgebras of
A
, and
\mathcal{B}
is the natural nondegenerate symmetric invariant bilinear form, which is also called double construction of Frobenius algebra
[
5
]
.

In

, Beites, Ferreira and Kaygorodov proposed the following question:

Question 1.5
.

(Chengming Bai)
Define and study transposed Poisson bialgebras.

This question was successfully resolved in
[
33
]
. Inspired by this, it is naturally to study the bialgebra theory for (transposed) Poisson 3-Lie algebras.
The notion of 3-Lie bialgebras was introduced in
[
8
]
, which studies two types of 3-Lie bialgebras: local cocycles and double constructions. They are extensions of Lie bialgebra structures.
For commutative associative algebras, the associative bialgebra and infinitesimal bialgebra
[

23
]
are well-known bialgebra structures.

In this paper, we combine the theories of 3-Lie bialgebras and commutative and cocommutative infinitesimal bialgebras to develop bialgebras for Poisson 3-Lie algebras, specifically the double construction Poisson 3-Lie bialgebras (see Definition
4.26
). This construction is both a double construction of 3-Lie bialgebras and a commutative cocommutative infinitesimal bialgebra, compatible in a specific sense. We prove that matched pairs of Poisson 3-Lie algebras, Manin triples of Poisson 3-Lie algebras and double construction Poisson 3-Lie bialgebras are equivalent (see Theorem
4.27
).

We construct double construction Poisson 3-Lie bialgebras using Manin triples and invariant bilinear forms on both commutative associative algebras and 3-Lie algebras. However, if the mixed products of
\cdot

[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]
in a transposed Poisson 3-Lie algebra are non-trivial, then there is no non-trivial such bilinear form (see Proposition
5.7
). To address this, we introduce a special class of transposed Poisson 3-Lie algebras called admissible transposed Poisson 3-Lie algebras (see Definition
2.16
), which are both transposed Poisson 3-Lie algebras and Poisson 3-Lie algebras. A double construction admissible transposed Poisson 3-Lie bialgebra is also a double construction Poisson 3-Lie bialgebra. Additionally, we provide matched pairs of admissible transposed Poisson 3-Lie algebras that are simultaneously matched pairs of Poisson 3-Lie algebras and transposed Poisson 3-Lie algebras. 1.5.
Outline of the paper

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we provide preliminaries on 3-Lie algebras, commutative associative algebras, Poisson 3-Lie algebras, and transposed Poisson 3-Lie algebras. We introduce the concept of an admissible transposed Poisson 3-Lie algebra and give a non-trivial 4-dimensional example (Example
2.17
). In Section
3
, we construct Poisson 3-Lie algebras and transposed Poisson 3-Lie algebras on direct sums and tensor products of vector spaces. In Section
4
, we define representations and dual representations of Poisson 3-Lie algebras, construct semi-direct product Poisson 3-Lie algebras (Proposition
4.4
), and discuss matched pairs and Manin triples. We also explore double construction Poisson 3-Lie bialgebras and establish equivalences between these structures (Theorem
4.27
). In Section
5
, we introduce representations and matched pairs for transposed Poisson 3-Lie algebras and prove that no natural bialgebra theory exists due to trivial mixed products of
\cdot
and
[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]
(Proposition
5.7
). In Section
6
, we define representations, matched pairs, and double construction bialgebras for admissible transposed Poisson 3-Lie algebras. We show equivalence between matched pairs and double construction admissible transposed Poisson 3-Lie bialgebras (Theorem
6.12
) and provide a nontrivial example (Example
6.14
). In Section
7
, we propose future research questions.

In this paper, the base field is taken to be
\mathbb{F}
unless otherwise specified. This is the field over which we take all associative, 3-Lie, and (transposed) Poisson 3-Lie algebras, vector spaces, linear maps, and tensor products, etc., and the characteristic of
\mathbb{F}
is zero. Moreover, throughout this paper, all algebras and vector spaces are assumed to be finite-dimensional. Let
V
be a vector space,
V^{*}
be the dual space of
V
. For each positive integer
k
, we identify the tensor product
\otimes^{k}V
with the space of multi-linear
maps from
\underbrace{V^{*}\times\cdots\times V^{*}}_{k-times}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
, such that

\langle\xi_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\xi_{k},v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{k}\rangle=\langle\xi_{1},v_{1}\rangle\cdots\langle\xi_{k},v_{k}\rangle,\quad\forall~\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{k}\in V^{*},v_{1},\cdots,v_{k}\in V,

(1)

where
\langle\xi_{i},v_{i}\rangle=\xi_{i}(v_{i})
,
1\leq i\leq k
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we first recall some necessary definitions and results for 3-Lie algebras, Poisson 3-Lie algebras, and transposed Poisson 3-Lie algebras. Then we introduce the concept of an admissible transposed Poisson 3-Lie algebra and provide a non-trivial 4-dimensional example (Example

Definition 2.1
.

(
[
19
]
) A
3-Lie algebra
is a vector space
A
together with a skew-symmetric linear map
[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]:\otimes^{3}A\rightarrow A
such that for all
x_{i}\in A,1\leq i\leq 5
, the following Filippov-Jacobi identity holds:

[x_{1},x_{2},[x_{3},x_{4},x_{5}]]=[[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}],x_{4},x_{5}]+[x_{3},[x_{1},x_{2},x_{4}],x_{5}]+[x_{3},x_{4},[x_{1},x_{2},x_{5}]].

(2)

A
derivation
on a 3-Lie algebra
(A,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])
is a linear map
D:A\rightarrow A
satisfying

D[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]=[D(x_{1}),x_{2},x_{3}]+[x_{1},D(x_{2}),x_{3}]+[x_{1},x_{2},D(x_{3})],\quad\forall~~x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\in A.

For all
x_{1},x_{2}\in A
, the operator

\mathrm{ad}_{x_{1},x_{2}}:A\rightarrow A,\quad\mathrm{ad}_{x_{1},x_{2}}(x):=[x_{1},x_{2},x],\quad\forall~x\in A,

(3)

is a derivation of the 3-Lie algebra
(A,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])
.

Definition 2.2
.

(
[
24
]
) A
representation of 3-Lie algebra

(A,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])
on a vector space
V
is a skew-symmetric linear map
\rho:\otimes^{2}A\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(V)

x_{i}\in A,1\leq i\leq 4

\displaystyle\rho(x_{1},x_{2})\rho(x_{3},x_{4})-\rho(x_{3},x_{4})\rho(x_{1},x_{2})=\rho([x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}],x_{4})-\rho([x_{1},x_{2},x_{4}],x_{3}),

3.
Direct sum and tensor product of (transposed) Poisson 3-Lie algebras

In this section, we construct Poisson 3-Lie algebras and transposed Poisson 3-Lie algebras on the direct sum and tensor product of vector spaces.

3.1.
Direct sum and tensor product of Poisson 3-Lie algebras

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(A_{1},\cdot_{1},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{1})
and
(A_{2},\cdot_{2},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{2})
be two Poisson 3-Lie algebras. For all
x_{i}\in A_{1},y_{i}\in A_{2},1\leq i\leq 3
, define linear maps
\cdot_{A_{1}\oplus A_{2}}:\otimes^{2}(A_{1}\oplus A_{2})\rightarrow A_{1}\oplus A_{2}

[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{A_{1}\oplus A_{2}}:\otimes^{3}(A_{1}\oplus A_{2})\rightarrow A_{1}\oplus A_{2}
by

\displaystyle(x_{1}+y_{1})\cdot_{A_{1}\oplus A_{2}}(x_{2}+y_{2})

\displaystyle=x_{1}\cdot_{1}x_{2}+y_{1}\cdot_{2}y_{2},

(11)

\displaystyle[x_{1}+y_{1},x_{2}+y_{2},x_{3}+y_{3}]_{A_{1}\oplus A_{2}}

\displaystyle=[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]_{1}+[y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}]_{2}.

(12)

Then
(A_{1}\oplus A_{2},\cdot_{A_{1}\oplus A_{2}},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{A_{1}\oplus A_{2}})
is a Poisson 3-Lie algebra.

Proof.

For all
x_{i}\in A_{1},y_{i}\in A_{2},1\leq i\leq 5
, we have

\displaystyle(x_{1}+y_{1})\cdot_{A_{1}\oplus A_{2}}(x_{2}+y_{2})

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:poisson-direct-sum1}}{=}

\displaystyle x_{1}\cdot_{1}x_{2}+y_{1}\cdot_{2}y_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x_{2}\cdot_{1}x_{1}+y_{2}\cdot_{2}y_{1}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:poisson-direct-sum1}}{=}

\displaystyle(x_{2}+y_{2})\cdot_{A_{1}\oplus A_{2}}(x_{1}+y_{1}),

\displaystyle\big((x_{1}+y_{1})\cdot_{A_{1}\oplus A_{2}}(x_{2}+y_{2})\big)\cdot_{A_{1}\oplus A_{2}}(x_{3}+y_{3})

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:poisson-direct-sum1}}{=}

\displaystyle(x_{1}\cdot_{1}x_{2}+y_{1}\cdot_{2}y_{2})\cdot_{A_{1}\oplus A_{2}}(x_{3}+y_{3})

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:poisson-direct-sum1}}{=}

\displaystyle x_{1}\cdot_{1}x_{2}\cdot_{1}x_{3}+y_{1}\cdot_{2}y_{2}\cdot_{2}y_{3}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:poisson-direct-sum1}}{=}

\displaystyle(x_{1}+y_{1})\cdot_{A_{1}\oplus A_{2}}(x_{2}\cdot_{1}x_{3}+y_{2}\cdot_{2}y_{3})

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:poisson-direct-sum1}}{=}

\displaystyle(x_{1}+y_{1})\cdot_{A_{1}\oplus A_{2}}\big((x_{2}+y_{2})\cdot_{A_{1}\oplus A_{2}}(x_{3}+y_{3})\big).

Therefore
(A_{1}\oplus A_{2},\cdot_{A_{1}\oplus A_{2}})
is a commutative associative algebra. Since the linear maps
[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{1}
and
[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{2}
are skew-symmetric, the linear map
[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{A_{1}\oplus A_{2}}

2

\displaystyle[[x_{1}+y_{1},x_{2}+y_{2},x_{3}+y_{3}]_{A_{1}\oplus A_{2}},x_{4}+y_{4},x_{5}+y_{5}]_{A_{1}\oplus A_{2}}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:poisson-direct-sum2}}{=}

\displaystyle[[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]_{1}+[y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}]_{2},x_{4}+y_{4},x_{5}+y_{5}]_{A_{1}\oplus A_{2}}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:poisson-direct-sum2}}{=}

\displaystyle[[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]_{1},x_{4},x_{5}]_{1}+[[y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}]_{2},y_{4},y_{5}]_{2}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{Jac}}{=}

\displaystyle[[x_{1},x_{4},x_{5}]_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]_{1}+[x_{1},[x_{2},x_{4},x_{5}]_{1},x_{3}]_{1}+[x_{1},x_{2},[x_{3},x_{4},x_{5}]_{1}]_{1}

\displaystyle+[x_{1}+y_{1},[x_{2},x_{4},x_{5}]_{1}+[y_{2},y_{4},y_{5}]_{2},x_{3}+y_{3}]_{A_{1}\oplus A_{2}}

(A_{1}\oplus A_{2},\cdot_{A_{1}\oplus A_{2}},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{A_{1}\oplus A_{2}})

In this section, we first introduce the representations and dual representations of Poisson 3-Lie algebras, construct the semi-direct product Poisson 3-Lie algebras (Proposition

4.1.
Representations of Poisson 3-Lie algebras

Recall
[

]
, a representation of Poisson algebra
(P,\cdot,[\cdot,\cdot])
is a triple
(V,\rho,\mu)

(V,\rho)
and
(V,\mu)
are respectively representations of Lie algebra

that satisfy

\displaystyle\rho(x\cdot y)

\displaystyle=\mu(y)\rho(x)+\mu(x)\rho(y),

\displaystyle\mu([x,y])

\displaystyle=\rho(x)\mu(y)-\mu(y)\rho(x),\quad\forall~x,y\in P.

Replace the above representation of Lie algebra with the representation of 3-Lie algebra. By (

Definition 4.1
.

A

, where

is a representation of the commutative associative algebra
(A,\cdot)
and
(V,\rho)
is a representation of the 3-Lie algebra
(A,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])
such that

\displaystyle\rho(x\cdot y,z)

\displaystyle=\mu(x)\rho(y,z)+\mu(y)\rho(x,z),

(21)

\displaystyle\rho(x,y)\mu(z)

\displaystyle=\mu([x,y,z])+\mu(z)\rho(x,y),\quad\forall~x,y,z\in A.

(22)

Remark 4.2

Ncib provides the representation of
n

[

]

and

.

2.3
and Proposition

and

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
(B,\cdot,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])
be a transposed Poisson 3-Lie algebra. If there is a nondegenerate invariant symmetric bilinear form
\mathcal{B}
such that it is invariant on both
(B,\cdot)
and
(B,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])
, i.e.,
\mathcal{B}(x\cdot y,z)=\mathcal{B}(x,y\cdot z)
and
\mathcal{B}([x,y,z],w)=-\mathcal{B}([x,y,w],z)
, for all
x,y,z,w\in B
, then we have

w\cdot[x,y,z]=[w\cdot x,y,z]=0.

(61)

Proof.

For all
x,y,z,w,u\in B
, by the invariance on both
(B,\cdot)
and
(B,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])
, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(3u\cdot[x,y,z]-[u\cdot x,y,z]-[x,u\cdot y,z]-[x,y,u\cdot z],w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(3w\cdot[x,y,z]+[y,z,w]\cdot x-[x,z,w]\cdot y+[x,y,w]\cdot z,u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(-[x,y,3u\cdot w]+[u\cdot x,y,w]+[x,u\cdot y,w]\cdot y+[x,y,w]\cdot u,w).

By the nondegeneracy of
\mathcal{B}
, we obtain

\displaystyle 3w\cdot[x,y,z]+[y,z,w]\cdot x-[x,z,w]\cdot y+[x,y,w]\cdot z=0,

(62)

and

\displaystyle-[x,y,3u\cdot w]+[u\cdot x,y,w]+[x,u\cdot y,w]\cdot y+[x,y,w]\cdot u=0.

(63)

Applying (
7
) to (

\displaystyle 3w\cdot[x,y,z]+[y,z,w]\cdot x-[x,z,w]\cdot y+[x,y,w]\cdot z

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:trans-poisson-alg}}{=}

\displaystyle[w\cdot x,y,z]+[x,w\cdot y,z]+[x,y,w\cdot z]+\frac{1}{3}([x\cdot y,z,w]+[y,x\cdot z,w]+[y,z,x\cdot w]

\displaystyle-[y\cdot x,z,w]-[x,y\cdot z,w]-[x,z,y\cdot w]+[z\cdot x,y,w]+[x,z\cdot y,w]+[x,y,z\cdot w])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4}{3}([w\cdot x,y,z]+[x,w\cdot y,z]+[x,y,w\cdot z])

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:trans-poisson-alg}}{=}

\displaystyle\frac{4}{9}(w\cdot[x,y,z])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

Therefore,
w\cdot[x,y,z]=0
. Similarly, applying (
7
) to (
63
), we obtain
[w\cdot x,y,z]=0
. The conclusion holds.
∎

Remark 5.8
.

If the mixed products
w\cdot[x,y,z]\neq 0
or
[w\cdot x,y,z]\neq 0
in a transposed Poisson 3-Lie algebra
(B,\cdot,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])
, then there is no non-trivial, non-degenerate invariant symmetric bilinear form
\mathcal{B}
on
(B,\cdot,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])
. Proposition
5.7
shows that the existence of such a non-trivial form would make the transposed 3-Lie Leibniz rule (
7
) trivial, and thus there is no “natural” bialgebra theory for transposed Poisson 3-Lie algebras as there is for Poisson algebras in
[
41
]
.

6.
Double construction admissible transposed Poisson 3-Lie bialgebras

Although there is no “natural” bialgebra theory for transposed Poisson 3-Lie algebras, we can still use the double construction Poisson 3-Lie bialgebras from Section
4
to induce bialgebras for transposed Poisson 3-Lie algebras. This is because the intersection of transposed 3-Lie Poisson algebras and Poisson 3-Lie algebras includes admissible transposed Poisson 3-Lie algebras. This approach differs from Liu and Bai’s method, which uses a commutative 2-cocycle on a Lie algebra to present the bialgebra theory of transposed Poisson algebras
[
33
]
.

In this section, we introduce representations, matched pairs, and double construction admissible transposed Poisson 3-Lie bialgebras. We prove that a matched pair of admissible transposed Poisson 3-Lie algebras is equivalent to a double construction admissible transposed Poisson 3-Lie bialgebra (Theorem
6.12
). Additionally, we provide a non-trivial example of such a bialgebra (Example
6.14
).

Proposition 6.1

Let

For all

\displaystyle\mu(x)\rho(y,z)+\mu(y)\rho(z,x)+\mu(z)\rho(x,y)=0.

\displaystyle 0

Then

\displaystyle 0=\rho(y,z)\mu(x)+\rho(z,x)\mu(y)+\rho(x,y)\mu(z)=3\mu([x,y,z]).

Corollary 6.13
.

A double construction admissible transposed Poisson 3-Lie bialgebra is a double construction Poisson 3-Lie bialgebra.

Closing discussions

We close this paper by proposing some questions to be considered in the future projects:

(a)

In
[
33
]
, bialgebra theory for transposed Poisson algebras uses bialgebra structures related to Manin triples of Lie algebras with respect to commutative 2-cocycles, which are closely linked to anti-pre-Lie algebras. Can we define (transposed) Poisson
3
-Lie bialgebras using the same approach?

(b)

Poisson bialgebras can be constructed from a combination of the classical Yang-Baxter equation and the associative Yang-Baxter equation. Can we construct Poisson 3-Lie bialgebras from the 3-Lie classical Yang-Baxter equation given in
[
8
]
?

(c)

What is the bialgebra theory for (transposed) Poisson
n
-Lie
(n>3)
algebras? Can we derive (transposed) Poisson
n
-Lie
(n\geq 3)
algebras from (transposed) Poisson bialgebras as described in
[
41
]
and
[
33
]
?

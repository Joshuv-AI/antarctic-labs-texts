Wetterich’s Equation and its Boundary Conditions for Radon Measures on Locally Convex Spaces
Jobst Ziebell 0000-0002-9715-6356 Faculty of Mathematics and Computer Science, Friedrich-Schiller-University, Jena, Germany

Abstract
Wetterich’s equation and corresponding flows of effective average actions are used frequently in theoretical physics to study the properties of quantum field theories. Under appropriate conditions, Wetterich’s equation also holds for Radon measures on locally convex spaces and the domain of the effective average action is the Lusin affine kernel of the measure. The resulting flow interpolates between the convex conjugate of the cumulant-generating function of the measure in question and its (generalised) Onsager-Machlup function. The underlying metric of the latter is induced by a family of measurable bilinear functionals that can be understood as bilinear versions of Lusin measurable linear functionals.

Introduction

In theoretical physics, one studies the flow of
‘effective average actions’
which in the case of a real-valued scalar field are real-valued functions
\Gamma_{k}
on an affine space
[
10
]
.
The asymptotic boundary condition for large flow parameters should correspond to the
‘classical action’
.
In
[
23
]
, the author showed that this picture indeed holds for certain regularised models, encoded as measures that are (measure-theoretically) equivalent to certain Gaußian measures.
Then the asymptotic boundary condition becomes the sum of the squared Cameron-Martin norm and the logarithm of the given density.
It is an intriguing question, whether the flow equation also holds for non-regularised quantum field theories and how the boundary conditions behave.
From a purely mathematical point of view, one can think of taking a Radon measure on a locally convex space and use Wetterich’s equation as a tool to calculate its Onsager-Machlup function.
The natural domain of the effective average action then becomes the Lusin affine kernel of the measure.

While not touched upon in this paper, the reverse programme is also of considerable interest:
Given some function on an affine space, can it be the Onsager-Machlup function of a measure?
In theretical physics, this is exactly the problem of quantising a given (Euclidean) classical action functional.
It is well-established that this is possible in e.g. two spacetime dimensions for a wide class of actions
[
19
]
and a landmark result also produced the celebrated
\Phi^{4}_{3}
model (see e.g.
[
12
]
for a short summary and a list of original references).
At the same time, it is known - though from a completely different quantisation ansatz - that many action functionals can only correspond to
‘trivial’
quantum field theories, that is to Gaußian measures
[
12
, Section 21.6]
.
Ideally, the existence of a solution to the flow equation with given boundary conditions should be able to answer the question of the triviality of a given action functional.

A final motivation of this topic, also not touched upon in this paper, is the necessity of
‘renormalisation’
, which in this setting can be summarised as follows:
Suppose a sequence
(\mu_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of Radon probability measures are given that satisfy the flow equation with boundary conditions given by a suitable sequence
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of functions.
If
\mu_{n}
converges weakly (or perhaps in some other sense) to a Radon measure
\mu
, in what sense do the boundary conditions
f_{n}
converge?
From a physical point of view,
\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{n}
is typically ill-defined, thanks to the divergence of certain parameters (coupling constants) in
f_{n}
.
However, the hope of many theoretical physicist - including the author - is that the flow
(\Gamma_{k})_{k\geq 0}
of the effective average action of the limit measure
\mu
captures these divergences by making the boundary condition

\lim_{k\to\infty}\Gamma_{k}\left(\frac{y}{k}\right)

(1)

well-defined (the factor
1/k
comes from power counting but could in general be something else).
That is, even if the boundary condition
\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{n}
is ill-defined, there should be a way to capture the asymptotics of it.
In fact, one may hope that for the corresponding flows
(\Gamma^{n}_{k})_{k\geq 0}
of the measures
\mu_{n}
respectively, at finite
k\geq 0

\lim_{n\to\infty}\Gamma^{n}_{k}=\Gamma_{k}

(2)

in a suitable sense.

All vector spaces in this paper are considered over the field of real numbers and the topologies considered are
not
assumed to be Hausdorff unless explicitly stated.
The complexification of a vector space

is denoted by

If

It is easy to see that

Note that in the case of a vector topology,

is precisely the usual topological dual and a vector space in its own right.
We also define the

\bar{\tau}=\left\{-U:U\in\tau\right\}

(4)

and it is straightforward to see that

We shall consider

which are pairs

where

p\left(x\right)=p\left(-x\right)=0\iff x=0\,,\qquad p\left(rx\right)=rp\left(x\right)\,,\qquad p\left(x+y\right)\leq p\left(x\right)+p\left(y\right)

(5)

The asymmetric norm induces a first-countable (not necessarily Hausdorff) topology for which sets of the form

form a neighbourhood basis at any given

.
To such a pair

, we associate the

(X,\bar{p})

We also equip

with the functional

p^{*}\left(\phi\right)=0\iff\phi=0\,,\qquad p^{*}\left(r\phi\right)=rp^{*}\left(\phi\right)\,,\qquad p^{*}\left(\phi+\psi\right)\leq p^{*}\left(\phi\right)+p^{*}\left(\psi\right)

The pair

induces a continuous functional on it with

.
The above notation is consistent with the conventional normed setting, i.e.
(X,p)^{*}
is just the ordinary continuous dual space whenever

is a norm on

and then
p^{*}
is the conventional dual norm.
In either case
p^{*}
similarly induces a first-countable topology on
(X,p)^{*}
.
Furthermore, we consider a

on
(X,p)
, where a net
(x_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in I}
in

converges

-weakly to

, if
\limsup_{\alpha}\phi(x_{\alpha})\leq\phi(x)
for all
\phi\in(X,p)^{*}
.
Again, if

is a norm, the weak topology is the usual weak vector topology.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

Positive Semidefinite

In this section, we introduce a bilinear analogue of the
linear Lusin measurable functionals
considered in
[
20
]
.
However, while these objects certainly promise interesting (at least in the impression of the author) mathematics to uncover, in this paper we shall develop just what is needed for Wetterich’s equation.
Consequently, the author does not want to use the label
‘bilinear Lusin measurable’

Throughout this section,
X
is a Hausdorff, quasicomplete, locally convex space and
\mu
a Radon probability measure on
X
.
Let
\mu^{2}
denote the unique Radon extension of
\mu\times\mu
to
\mathcal{B}(X\times X)
.
A particularly useful class of
\mu

[

It is easy to show that every
\mu

Definition 3.1
.

Let
f:X\times X\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
a
\mu^{2}
-measurable function for which there exists a sequence of continuous, symmetric, positive semidefinite, bilinear functionals
B_{n}:X\times X\to\mathbb{R}
such that

•

m\leq n
implies
B_{m}(x,x)\leq B_{n}(x,x)
for every
x\in X
,

•

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}B_{n}(x,x)<\infty
for
\mu
-almost every
x\in X
,

•

B_{n}
converges to
f
in
\mu^{2}
-measure.

Then, we call
f
a
\mu
-regular semidefinite inner product
on
X
and denote the set of all such objects by
\mathcal{I}(\mu)
.

In fact, the first two points already define the
\mu
-regular semidefinite inner product uniquely.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
B_{n}:X\times X\to\mathbb{R}
be a sequence of continuous, symmetric, positive semidefinite, bilinear functionals with

•

m\leq n
implies
B_{m}(x,x)\leq B_{n}(x,x)

x\in X
,

•

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}B_{n}(x,x)<\infty

\mu

.

Then,
B_{n}
converges to some

\mu^{2}
-measure.
Furthermore, there is a standard
\mu
-Lusin linear subspace

\mu^{2}

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in B\\

\displaystyle\left|p_{n}\left(tx+\left[1-t\right]y\right)^{2}-p\left(tx+\left[1-t\right]y\right)^{2}\right|

m,n\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq N}

\left|p_{n}\left(x\right)^{2}-p_{m}\left(x\right)^{2}\right|=\lim_{\alpha}\left|p_{n}\left(x_{\alpha}\right)^{2}-p_{m}\left(x_{\alpha}\right)^{2}\right|<\delta\,.

(17)

Hence,
p_{n}(x)
is a Cauchy sequence,
x\in Y
and we have uniform convergence on the closure
D=\overline{C}
of

.
Consequently, the uniform convergence on a set

implies the uniform convergence on
\overline{\mathrm{cobal}\,A}\subseteq Y
.
Combined with Egoroff’s theorem, for each

, we find a closed disk

with
\mu(Y\setminus D)<1/(2m)
on which

converges uniformly to

.
Since
\mu
is Radon, it follows that there is a compact disk

.
From the bilinearity, we also have uniform convergence on

such that without loss of generality,

.
Consequently,

is a standard
\mu
-Lusin linear subspace.
Furthermore, by polarisation

\displaystyle\sup_{x,y\in Q_{m}}

\displaystyle\left|B_{n}\left(x,y\right)-B\left(x,y\right)\right|

(18)

\displaystyle=\sup_{x,y\in Q_{m}}\left|p_{n}\left(\frac{x+y}{2}\right)^{2}-p_{n}\left(\frac{x-y}{2}\right)^{2}+p\left(\frac{x+y}{2}\right)^{2}-p\left(\frac{x+y}{2}\right)^{2}\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2\sup_{x\in Q_{m}}\left|p_{n}\left(x\right)^{2}-p\left(x\right)^{2}\right|\,.

Hence,
B_{n}
converges uniformly to
B
on
Q_{m}\times Q_{m}
.
Finally, let

be convex, weakly compact and contained in

.
Then the convex hull of

is a weakly compact disk (see
[
18

) and thus a Banach disk.
Hence, the reasoning in
[
6
, p. 56]
applies.
∎

Remark 3.4

Let

4
Wetterich’s Equation

Throughout this section we fix a quasicomplete, Hausdorff, locally convex space
X
, some point
w\in X
and a Radon probability measure
\mu
on
\mathcal{B}(X)
for which
\exp[\phi]\in L^{1}(\mu)
for all
\phi\in X^{*}
.
Futhermore, we shall make the assumption that there is a sequence
(K_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of hereditarily separable, compact disks in
X
with
\mu(\cup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}K_{n})=1
.
Again, if
X
is a Souslin space, this assumption is automatically satisfied (see e.g.
[
2
, Corollary 6.7.8]
).
Denote by
\Xi
the
\sigma
-algebra on
(X^{*})_{\mathbb{C}}
generated by the dual pair
(X_{\mathbb{C}},(X^{*})_{\mathbb{C}})
, i.e.
\Xi=\mathcal{E}((X^{*})_{\mathbb{C}})
when
(X^{*})_{\mathbb{C}}
is considered with its weak-
\ast
topology.

4.1
The Derivation

Fix an increasing family
(I_{k})_{k\geq 0}
of
\mu_{-w}
-regular inner products on
X
, set
Q_{k}(x):=\tilde{I}_{k}(x-w)
for brevity and define the following probability measures on
\mathcal{B}(X)
:

\mu_{k}=\frac{\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}Q_{k}\right]\mu}{\left\|\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}Q_{k}\right]\right\|_{L^{1}(\mu)}}=:\frac{1}{N_{k}}\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}Q_{k}\right]\mu\,.

(34)

Now, we demand that
(I_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is chosen such that the following hold:

1.

For every
k\geq 0
, there is a
\sigma
-finite measure space
(A_{k},\Sigma_{k},\kappa_{k})
and a
(\Sigma_{k},\Xi)
-measurable mapping
\omega^{k}:A_{k}\to(X^{*})_{\mathbb{C}},a\mapsto\omega^{k}_{a}
such that for every
y\in\mathcal{H}(\mu_{-w})
, the function
a\mapsto\omega^{k}_{a}(y)
is in
L^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}(\kappa_{k})
and

D^{+}\underline{I_{k}}\left(y,y\right)=\int_{A_{k}}\left|\omega^{k}_{a}\left(y\right)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\kappa_{k}\left(a\right)\,,

(35)

where the Dini derivative is taken with respect to
k
.

For brevity, define
Q_{k}^{\prime}:X\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
with

x\mapsto\int_{A_{k}}\left|\omega^{k}_{a}\left(x-w\right)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\kappa_{k}\left(a\right)\,.

(36)

2.

For every
l\geq 0
,
Q_{l}^{\prime}
is
\mu
-measurable.
Furthermore, for every sequence
(k_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
[0,\infty)\setminus\{l\}
with
l=\lim_{n\to\infty}k_{n}
, there is a sequence
(r_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
(0,\infty)
with
\lim_{n\to\infty}r_{n}=\infty
and

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{X}\exp\left[\left|\frac{r_{n}\left(Q_{l}-Q_{k_{n}}\right)}{2\left(l-k_{n}\right)}-\frac{r_{n}}{2}Q_{l}^{\prime}\right|-\frac{Q_{l}}{2}\right]\mathrm{d}\mu=\int_{X}\exp\left[-\frac{Q_{l}}{2}\right]\mathrm{d}\mu\,.

(37)

3.

For every
k\geq 0
, there is some
R>0
such that

\int_{X}\exp\left[\frac{1}{2R}Q_{k}^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}Q_{k}\right]\mathrm{d}\mu<\infty\,.

(38)

Remark 4.1
.

First, note that in the usual framework of Wetterich’s equation,
w=0
(see e.g
[
11
]
).
However, admitting other
w
is meaningful in order to understand the corresponding boundary conditions as shown in
section

4.2
.
Also, while the properties that
(I_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is demanded to have may look very technical at first glance, they are tailored towards being as general as possible to allow the proofs to work.
If possible, one could of course just pick continuous, symmetric and positive semidefinite representatives of
I_{k}
eliminating the need for measure-theoretic arguments.
But if e.g. it is known that
\mu
is supported on a smaller linear subspace with a natural stronger topology, it can be beneficial to use
\mu_{-w}
-regular inner products instead of continuous ones, because one then can keep
X^{*}
and need not enlargen it to encompass the stronger topology.
It is of no surprise, that a certain compatibility between the family
(Q_{k})_{k\geq 0}
and the measure
\mu

With regards to the constructions in

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
y\in\tilde{X}
and define
V_{k}^{*}(y)=\sup_{\phi\in X^{*}}[y(\phi)-V_{k}(\phi)]
.
Then, for every
l>0
with
V_{l}^{*}(y)<\infty
, the function
V^{*}(y):[0,l]\to\mathbb{R},k\mapsto V_{k}^{*}(y)
is continuous.

Proof.

Note that for all
0\leq a\leq b\leq l
and all
\phi\in X^{*}
,

V_{a}\left(\phi\right)=\ln\int_{X}\exp\left[\phi\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{a}\geq\ln\int_{X}\exp\left[\phi\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{b}+\ln\frac{N_{b}}{N_{a}}=V_{b}\left(\phi\right)+\ln\frac{N_{b}}{N_{a}}\,.

(42)

Hence,

V_{a}^{*}\left(y\right)\leq V_{b}^{*}\left(y\right)-\ln\frac{N_{b}}{N_{a}}\quad\text{and using \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{cor:LogNkAbsoluteContinuity},}\quad\limsup_{a\nearrow b}V_{a}^{*}\left(y\right)\leq V_{b}^{*}\left(y\right)\,.

(43)

Now, let
(\phi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence in
X^{*}
with
\lim_{n\to\infty}[y(\phi_{n})-V_{b}(\phi_{n})]=V_{b}^{*}(y)
and let
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a monotonically increasing sequence in
[0,b]
with
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}a_{n}=b
.
Recalling that
Q_{a_{n}}\leq Q_{b}

\mu
-almost everywhere and that
Q_{a_{n}}
converges to
Q_{b}
in
\mu
-measure, we have
\lim_{m\to\infty}V_{a_{m}}(\phi_{n})=V_{b}(\phi_{n})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, by the dominated convergence theorem using the fact that
\exp[\phi_{n}]\in L^{1}(\mu)
.
Hence, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we find some
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
m\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq N}
,

V_{a_{m}}\left(\phi_{n}\right)\leq V_{b}\left(\phi_{n}\right)+\frac{1}{n}\,.

(44)

Consequently,

\liminf_{m\to\infty}V_{a_{m}}^{*}\left(y\right)\geq\liminf_{m\to\infty}\left[y\left(\phi_{n}\right)-V_{a_{m}}\left(\phi_{n}\right)\right]\geq y\left(\phi_{n}\right)-V_{b}\left(\phi_{n}\right)-\frac{1}{n}\,.

(45)

Thus,
\liminf_{m\to\infty}V_{a_{m}}^{*}(y)\geq V_{b}^{*}(y)
and with
eq.

43
,
V^{*}(y)
is left-continuous at
b
.
The right-continuity at

follows analogously and the claim follows.

.

on

(46)

given by

Lemma 4.6
.

V_{k,l}
is finite everywhere, lower semicontinuous and norm-coercive, i.e.

\lim_{\|T\|\to\infty}V_{k,l}(T)=\infty\,.

(48)

Moreover
\mathrm{int}\,\mathrm{dom}\,V_{k,l}^{*}
is nonempty.

Proof.

It is clear that
V_{k,l}
is finite everywhere because
\exp[\phi]\in L^{1}(\mu)
for all
\phi\in X^{*}
.
The lower semicontinuity follows immediately from the equivalence
\mu_{k}\sim\mu_{l}
and Fatou’s lemma.
Furthermore, since
\int_{X}T\mathrm{d}\mu_{l}=0
for all

,

\displaystyle\ln\int_{X}\exp[T]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}

\displaystyle\geq\ln\frac{N_{l}}{N_{k}}+\ln\int_{X}\exp[T]\mathrm{d}\mu_{l}

(49)

\displaystyle=\ln\frac{N_{l}}{N_{k}}+\ln\int_{X}\left(\exp[T]-T\right)\mathrm{d}\mu_{l}\geq\ln\frac{N_{l}}{N_{k}}+\ln\int_{X}\max\left\{1,\left|T\right|\right\}\mathrm{d}\mu_{l}\,.

It follows immediately from
[
22
, Exercise 2.41]
that
0\in\mathrm{int}\,\mathrm{dom}\,V_{k,l}^{*}
.
∎

Hence, instead of studying
V_{k}
which may not be coercive at all, we can study

which has the above coercivity property.
Also, since Wetterich’s equation involves derivatives of the functions
V_{k}^{*}
, we should look for differentiability properties of

.
It is known that these are intimately connected to minimisation properties of the functions
V_{k,l}
.
However, the space
\mathcal{K}(\mu_{l})
is in general too small to exhibit minima such that we consider the function
\overline{W}_{k,l}:\mathcal{M}(\mu_{l})\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}

that is smaller than

on

.

.

Lemma 4.7
.

1.

V_{k,l}
,
W_{k,l}
and
\overline{W}_{k,l}
coincide on
\mathcal{K}(\mu_{l})

\int_{X}T\mathrm{d}\mu_{l}\geq 0

4.

\mathrm{gr}\,V_{k,l}
is dense in
\mathrm{gr}\,W_{k,l}
with the subspace topology induced by
(\mathcal{L}(\mu_{l}),\overline{\tau_{l}})\times\mathbb{R}

Proof.

(i)
:
By definition,
\overline{W}_{k,l}\upharpoonright\mathcal{K}(\mu_{l})\leq V_{k,l}
and since
\mathcal{M}(\mu)
is first-countable,

\displaystyle\overline{W}_{k,l}\left(T\right)

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\liminf_{n\to\infty}V_{k,l}\left(T_{n}\right):(T_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\text{ in }\mathcal{K}(\mu_{l})\text{ with }T_{n}\to T\text{ in $\mu_{l}$-measure}\right\}

\displaystyle\geq\ln\int_{X}\exp[T]\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}\,,

for all

by Fatou’s lemma, since
\mu_{k}\sim\mu_{l}
.
Hence,
V_{k,l}
,
W_{k,l}
and
\overline{W}_{k,l}
coincide on
\mathcal{K}(\mu_{l})
.

(ii)
:
Clearly,
\mathrm{gr}\,V_{k,l}
is dense in
\mathrm{gr}\,\overline{W}_{k,l}
.
Let
T\in\mathrm{dom}\,\overline{W}_{k,l}
and
(T_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

\mathrm{dom}\,V_{k,l}

T_{n}\to T\text{ in $\mu_{l}$-measure}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lim_{n\to\infty}\ln\int_{X}\exp\left[T_{n}\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}=\overline{W}_{k,l}\left(T\right)\,.

(51)

Then,
T_{n}
also converges in
\mu_{k}
-measure such that for all

\mu_{k}
-integrable and

(52)

By
eq.

50
, it follows that

\overline{W}_{k,l}\left(\frac{T}{p}\right)=\ln\int_{X}\exp\left[\frac{T}{p}\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}\,.

(53)

Applying Hölders inequality and the lower semicontinuity of

,

\displaystyle\overline{W}_{k,l}\left(T\right)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{p\searrow 1}\overline{W}_{k,l}\left(\frac{T}{p}\right)\leq\liminf_{p\searrow 1}\frac{1}{p}\ln\int_{X}\exp\left[T\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}

\displaystyle=\ln\int_{X}\exp\left[T\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}\leq\overline{W}_{k,l}\left(T\right)\,.

(iii)
:
Let
T
and
(T_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be as above.

\mu_{k}
-integrable and hence uniformly
\mu_{l}
-integrable such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{X}\max\left\{T_{n},0\right\}\mathrm{d}\mu_{l}=\int_{X}\max\left\{T,0\right\}\mathrm{d}\mu_{l}\,.

(55)

Moreover,
\int_{X}T_{n}\mathrm{d}\mu_{l}=0

\int_{X}\max\left\{T,0\right\}\mathrm{d}\mu_{l}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{X}\max\left\{-T_{n},0\right\}\mathrm{d}\mu_{l}\geq\int_{X}\max\left\{-T,0\right\}\mathrm{d}\mu_{l}\,.

(56)

Hence,
T\in\mathcal{L}(\mu_{l})
and
\int_{X}T\mathrm{d}\mu_{l}\geq 0
.

(iv)
:
Let
T
and
(T_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be as above.

\mu_{l}
-integrable and

\mu_{l}
-integrable as well.

(57)

As a consequence,

is just as coercive as

W_{k,l}

L^{1}(\mu_{l})

For all
T\in\mathrm{dom}\,W_{k,l}

\displaystyle W_{k,l}\left(T\right)

\displaystyle=\ln\int_{X}\exp\left[T\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}\geq\ln\frac{N_{l}}{N_{k}}+\ln\int_{X}\exp\left[T\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{l}

(ii)\implies(iii)

\displaystyle\sup

\displaystyle\left\{W_{k,l}^{*}\left(\beta+\alpha\right)\middle|\alpha\in\left(\mathcal{L}(\mu_{l}),\overline{p_{l}}\right)^{*}:\bar{p}_{l}^{*}\left(\alpha\right)<\epsilon\right\}=\sup_{T\in\mathcal{L}(\mu_{l})}\left[\beta\left(T\right)+\epsilon\overline{p_{l}}\left(T\right)-W_{k,l}\left(T\right)\right]

(68)

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{T\in\mathcal{L}(\mu_{l})}\left[\beta\left(T\right)+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\left\|T\right\|_{L^{1}(\mu_{l})}-W_{k,l}\left(T\right)\right]\,.

Since the above is finite, we have that
W_{k,l}-\beta
is
L^{1}(\mu_{l})
-norm coercive.

(iii)\implies(i)
:
Clearly,
V_{k,l}-M_{l}(y)
is also
L^{1}(\mu_{l})
-norm coercive.
Hence, the claim follows from
[
22
, Exercise 2.41]
.
∎

Finally, the minimisation properties follow, inspired by
[
9
, Theorem 5.3]
.

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
0\leq k\leq l
and
\beta\in\mathcal{M}(\mu_{l})^{*}
such that
\beta\in\mathrm{int}\,\mathrm{dom}\,V_{k,l}^{*}
.
Then the set of minimisers
M
of
W_{k,l}-\beta
is nonempty and has the form
M=\{S+c:c\in I\}
for some
S\in\mathcal{L}(\mu_{l})
and a compact interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
.
Moreover, for every minimising sequence
(T_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, there is some
S\in M
and a subsequence
(R_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that for every further subsequence
(R^{\prime}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
,

A_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{m=1}^{n}R^{\prime}_{m}

(69)

\overline{\tau_{l}}
-converges to
S
.

Proof.

Letting
(T_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a minimising sequence,
theorem

4.11
shows that
T_{n}
is
L^{1}(\mu_{l})
-bounded.
Consequently, by Komlòs’ theorem, there is some
S\in L^{1}(\mu_{l})
and a subsequence
(R_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that for every further subsequence
(R^{\prime}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
,

A_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{m=1}^{n}R^{\prime}_{m}

(70)

converges in
\mu_{l}
-measure to
S
.
By construction, it is clear that
S\in\mathcal{L}(\mu_{l})
and by convexity,
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a minimising sequence of
W_{k,l}-\beta
.
Without loss of generality,
A_{n}\in\mathrm{dom}\,W_{k,l}
such that
\max\{A_{n},0\}
is uniformly
\mu_{l}
-integrable and consequently,
\max\{A_{n}-S,0\}
is uniformly
\mu_{l}
-integrable as well.
Hence, by Vitali’s convergence theorem,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\overline{p_{l}}\left(A_{n}-S\right)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{X}\max\left\{A_{n}-S,0\right\}\mathrm{d}\mu_{l}=0\,,

(71)

i.e.
A_{n}

\overline{\tau_{l}}
-converges to
S
.
Because
\lim_{n\to\infty}\beta(A_{n})=\beta(S)
it follows from the lower semicontinuity of
W_{k,l}
with respect to convergence in measure, that
S
is a minimiser of
W_{k,l}-\beta
, i.e.
S\in M
.
Now, let
S^{\prime}\in M
and note that the function

f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R},t\mapsto W_{k,l}\left(\left[1-t\right]S+tS^{\prime}\right)=\ln\int_{X}\exp\left[\left(1-t\right)S+tS^{\prime}\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}\,.

(72)

is twice differentiable on the open interval
(0,1)
such that for some
t\in(0,1)
,

W_{k,l}\left(S^{\prime}\right)-\beta\left(S^{\prime}\right)=W_{k,l}\left(S\right)-\beta\left(S\right)+\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime\prime}\left(t\right)\,,

(73)

i.e.
f^{\prime\prime}(t)=0
.
Writing
U=(1-t)S+tS^{\prime}
for brevity, we obtain

f^{\prime\prime}\left(t\right)=\frac{\int_{X}\left(S^{\prime}-S\right)^{2}\exp\left[U\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}}{\int_{X}\exp\left[U\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}}-\left(\frac{\int_{X}\left(S^{\prime}-S\right)\exp\left[U\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}}{\int_{X}\exp\left[U\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}}\right)^{2}=0\,.

(74)

Since this amounts to Hölder’s inequality with the function
S^{\prime}-S
and the constant function
1
becoming an equality, we conclude that that
S^{\prime}
and
S
differ by a constant
\mu_{k}
-almost everywhere and thus also
\mu_{l}
-almost everywhere.
Hence, by the convexity of
W_{k,l}-\beta
, there exists an interval
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
such that

M=\left\{S+c:c\in I\right\}

(75)

and since
W_{k,l}-\beta
is
L^{1}(\mu)
-coercive,
I
is bounded.
Moreover, by the lower semicontinuity of
W_{k,l}-\beta
with respect to convergence in measure, it is immediate that
\inf I\in I
and
\sup I\in I
.
∎

Corollary 4.13
.

Let
0\leq k\leq l
and
\beta\in\mathcal{M}(\mu_{l})^{*}
such that such that
\beta\in\mathrm{int}\,\mathrm{dom}\,V_{k,l}^{*}
.
Then, there is a
T\in\mathcal{L}(\mu_{l})
minimising
W_{k,l}-\beta
for which every minimising sequence of
W_{k,l}-\beta
converges
\bar{p}_{l}
-weakly to
T
.

Proof.

Let
(T_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a minimising sequence.
Using
lemma

4.12
, there is a unique greatest element
T
in the set of minimisers
M
of
W_{k,l}-\beta
.
Furthermore, for every subsequence
(T_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
there is a
S\in M
and a subsequence
(R_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
(T_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that for every further subsequence
(R^{\prime}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
,

A_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{m=1}^{n}R^{\prime}_{m}

(76)

\overline{\tau_{l}}
-converges to
S
.
Since
T\geq S
, we clearly have that
A_{n}

\overline{p_{l}}
-converges to
T
as well.
Consequently, for every
\alpha\in(\mathcal{L}(\mu_{l}),\overline{p_{l}})^{*}
, there is some
C\geq 0
such that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\alpha\left(A_{n}-T\right)\leq C\limsup_{n\to\infty}\overline{p_{l}}\left(A_{n}-T\right)=0\,,

(77)

i.e.
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\alpha(A_{n})\leq\alpha(T)
.
Hence,
lemma

2.2
applies and
R_{n}
converges
\overline{p_{l}}
-weakly to

.
Consequently, every subsequence of
(T_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
has a subsequence converging
\overline{p_{l}}
-weakly to
T
such that, indeed,
T_{n}
converges
\overline{p_{l}}
-weakly to
T
.
∎

Applying
theorem

2.4
, we also obtain the following.

Corollary 4.14
.

Let
0\leq k\leq l
and
\beta\in\mathcal{M}(\mu_{l})^{*}
be such that
\beta\in\mathrm{int}\,\mathrm{dom}\,V_{k,l}^{*}
and
T_{k}\in\mathcal{L}(\mu_{l})
satisfies the conclusion of
corollary

4.13
.
Then, for every
\alpha\in(\mathcal{L}(\mu_{l}),\overline{p_{l}})^{*}
,

\lim_{t\searrow 0}\frac{1}{t}\left[W_{k,l}^{*}\left(\beta+t\alpha\right)-W_{k,l}^{*}\left(\beta\right)\right]=\alpha\left(T_{k}\right)\,.

(78)

Hence,
W_{k,l}^{*}
is differentiable at such points
\beta
in the given sense.
It is however, useful to show that is, in fact, also subdifferentiable in a very natural sense.
To that end we shall consider the function
W_{k,l}^{\prime}
on the Banach space
(\mathcal{L}(\mu_{l}),L^{1}(\mu_{l}))
and note that
(W_{k,l}^{\prime})^{*}
coincides with
W_{k,l}^{*}
on the intersection of their domains.
The following
lemma
shows that subdifferentials of
W_{k,l}^{\prime}
are comparatively easy to understand.

Lemma 4.15
.

Let
T\in\mathrm{dom}\,\partial W^{\prime}_{k,l}
.
For every
\alpha\in\partial W^{\prime}_{k,l}(T)
and all
S\in\mathcal{K}(\mu_{l})
,

\alpha\left(S\right)=\frac{\int_{X}S\exp\left[T\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}}{\int_{X}\exp\left[T\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\alpha\left(T\right)\geq\frac{\int_{X}T\exp\left[T\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}}{\int_{X}\exp\left[T\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}}\,.

(79)

Proof.

Let
\alpha\in\partial W^{\prime}_{k,l}(T)
and note that since
0\in\mathrm{dom}\,W^{\prime}_{k,l}
, for all
t\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\alpha\left(T\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{t}\left[\ln\int_{X}\exp\left[T\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}-\ln\int_{X}\exp\left[T-tT\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}\right]

(80)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t}\ln\frac{\int_{X}\exp\left[tT\right]\exp\left[\left(1-t\right)T\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}}{\int_{X}\exp\left[\left(1-t\right)T\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}}\geq\frac{\int_{X}T\exp\left[\left(1-t\right)T\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}}{\int_{X}\exp\left[\left(1-t\right)T\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}}\,.

The last estimate follows from Jensen’s inequality.
Using the monotone convergence theorem over the set
\{T\geq 0\}
and the dominated convergence theorem over
\{T<0\}
, it is then evident that

\alpha\left(T\right)\geq\lim_{t\searrow 0}\frac{\int_{X}T\exp\left[\left(1-t\right)T\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}}{\int_{X}\exp\left[\left(1-t\right)T\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}}=\frac{\int_{X}T\exp\left[T\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}}{\int_{X}\exp\left[T\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}}\,.

(81)

In particular, the right-hand side is finite.
Now we take a short detour and define the two Young functions
[
15
, p. 15]

\Phi\left(x\right)=e^{\left|x\right|}-\left|x\right|-1\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Psi\left(y\right)=\left(1+\left|y\right|\right)\ln\left(1+\left|y\right|\right)-\left|y\right|

(82)

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
.
Furthermore, consider the associated Orlicz norms for real-valued
\mu_{k}
-measurable functions
f
and
g
given by

N^{k}_{\Phi}\left(f\right)=\inf\left\{r>0:\int_{X}\Phi\left(\frac{f}{r}\right)\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}\leq 1\right\},\,N^{k}_{\Psi}\left(g\right)=\inf\left\{r>0:\int_{X}\Psi\left(\frac{g}{r}\right)\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}\leq 1\right\}\,.

(83)

Since
\Phi
and
\Psi
are convex, crude estimates for these are simply given by

N^{k}_{\Phi}\left(f\right)\leq\max\left\{1,\int_{X}\Phi\left(f\right)\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}\right\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad N^{k}_{\Psi}\left(g\right)\leq\max\left\{1,\int_{X}\Psi\left(g\right)\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}\right\}\,.

(84)

Now, letting
S\in\mathcal{K}(\mu_{l})
, we use the Orlicz-Hölder inequality (see e.g.
[
15
, p.58, Proposition 1, Remark]
) to deduce that

\int_{X}\left|S\right|\exp\left[T\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}\leq 2N^{k}_{\Phi}\left(S\right)N^{k}_{\Psi}\left(\exp\left[T\right]\right)\,.

(85)

Noting that
\exp[S]+\exp[-S]
is
\mu_{k}
-integrable, we see that
\int_{X}\exp[|S|]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}
is finite and hence
N^{k}_{\Phi}(S)<\infty
.
Likewise, note that
\Psi(y)\leq 1+y\exp[y]
for all
y\in\mathbb{R}
such that,

\int_{X}\Psi\left(\exp\left[T\right]\right)\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}\leq 1+\int_{X}T\exp\left[T\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}<\infty\,,

(86)

such that
N^{k}_{\Psi}(\exp[T])<\infty
.
Consequently, we may define the linear functional
\beta:\mathrm{span}\,(\{T\}\cup\mathcal{K}(\mu_{l}))\to\mathbb{R}
with

R\mapsto\frac{\int_{X}R\exp\left[T\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}}{\int_{X}\exp\left[T\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}}\,.

(87)

Now, consider the family
R_{t}=(1-t)T+tS
and note that since
\alpha
is a subdifferential,

\displaystyle\alpha\left(S-T\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t}\alpha\left(R_{t}-T\right)\leq\frac{1}{t}\left[W_{k,l}\left(R_{t}\right)-W_{k,l}\left(T\right)\right]

(88)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{t}\ln\frac{\int_{X}\exp\left[t\left(T-S\right)\right]\exp\left[\left(1-t\right)T+tS\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}}{\int_{X}\exp\left[\left(1-t\right)T+tS\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\int_{X}\left(S-T\right)\exp\left[\left(1-t\right)T+tS\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}}{\int_{X}\exp\left[\left(1-t\right)T+tS\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}}\,,

where the last estimate follows from Jensen’s inequality.
We want to show that the right-hand side tends to
\beta(S-T)
as
t\searrow 0
.
The denominator is continuous for
t\in[0,1]
and poses no problem.
By convexity,
|S|\exp[(1-t)T+tS]\leq|S|\exp[T]+|S|\exp[S]
, such that

\lim_{t\searrow 0}\int_{X}S\exp\left[\left(1-t\right)T+tS\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}=\int_{X}S\exp\left[T\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}\,,

(89)

by the dominated convergence theorem.
For the remaining part, note that, by Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle\int_{X}\max\left\{T,0\right\}\exp\left[S\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}\exp\left[S+\frac{1}{2}\max\left\{T,0\right\}\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}

(90)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\int_{X}\exp\left[2S\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}\int_{X}\left(1+\exp\left[T\right]\right)\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}}<\infty\,.

Hence, using
\exp[(1-t)T+tS]\leq\exp[T]+\exp[S]
and the dominated convergence theorem again, we find that

\lim_{t\searrow 0}\int_{X}\max\left\{T,0\right\}\exp\left[\left(1-t\right)T+tS\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}=\int_{X}\max\left\{T,0\right\}\exp\left[T\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}\,.

(91)

Finally, for
t<1/2
, we have
\max\{-T,0\}\exp[(1-t)T+tS]\leq\exp[\max\{S,0\}]
such that

\lim_{t\searrow 0}\int_{X}\max\left\{-T,0\right\}\exp\left[\left(1-t\right)T+tS\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}=\int_{X}\max\left\{-T,0\right\}\exp\left[T\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}\,.

(92)

Consequently, for all
S\in\mathcal{K}(\mu_{l})
,

\displaystyle\alpha\left(S-T\right)\leq\frac{\int_{X}\left(S-T\right)\exp\left[T\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}}{\int_{X}\exp\left[T\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}}=\beta\left(S-T\right)\,.

(93)

It follows that
(\alpha-\beta)(S)\leq(\alpha-\beta)(T)
i.e. the linear functional
(\alpha-\beta)\upharpoonright\mathcal{K}(\mu_{l})
is majorised by a constant.
Evidently, that can only happen if
\alpha
and
\beta
coincide on
\mathcal{K}(\mu_{l})
.
∎

The striking consequence is that suitable
\beta\in\mathrm{int}\,\mathrm{dom}\,V_{k,l}^{*}
have simple integral expressions.

Theorem 4.16
.

Let
0\leq k\leq l
and
\beta\in\mathcal{M}(\mu_{l})^{*}
be such that
\beta\in\mathrm{int}\,\mathrm{dom}\,V_{k,l}^{*}
and
T_{k}\in\mathcal{L}(\mu_{l})
satisfies the corresponding conclusion of
corollary

4.13
.
Then,
T_{k}\in\partial(W^{\prime}_{k,l})^{*}(\beta)
and for all
S\in\mathcal{K}(\mu_{l})
,

\beta\left(S\right)=\frac{\int_{X}S\exp\left[T_{k}\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}}{\int_{X}\exp\left[T_{k}\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\beta\left(T_{k}\right)\geq\frac{\int_{X}T_{k}\exp\left[T_{k}\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}}{\int_{X}\exp\left[T_{k}\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}}\,.

(94)

Proof.

Let
\gamma\in(\mathcal{L}(\mu_{l}),L^{1}(\mu_{l}))^{*}
and note that by definition,

(W^{\prime}_{k,l})^{*}\left(\beta\right)+\gamma\left(T_{k}\right)=\left(\beta+\gamma\right)\left(T_{k}\right)-W^{\prime}_{k,l}\left(T_{k}\right)\leq(W^{\prime}_{k,l})^{*}\left(\beta+\gamma\right)\,.

(95)

Hence,
T_{k}\in\partial(W^{\prime}_{k,l})^{*}(\beta)
and since
W_{k,l}^{\prime}
is lower semicontinuous it follows that
\beta\in\partial W^{\prime}_{k,l}(T_{k})
(see e.g.
[
22
, Theorem 2.4.4(iv)]
).
Now,
eq.

94
follows from
lemma

4.15
.
∎

Having understood these minimisation properties of
W_{k,l}-\beta
, we just need the following equi-coercivity property to derive a differential equation for
V_{k}^{*}(y)
.

Lemma 4.17
.

Let
0\leq k\leq l
and
y\in\mathcal{A}(\mu)
with
M_{l}(y)\in\mathrm{int}\,\mathrm{dom}\,V_{k,l}^{*}
.
Then, for each
a\in[0,k]
,
M_{l}(y)\in\mathrm{int}\,\mathrm{dom}\,V_{a,l}^{*}
.
Furthermore, there exist
A>0
and
B\in\mathbb{R}
such that for all
a\in[0,k]
and all
T\in\mathcal{L}(\mu_{l})
,

W_{a,l}\left(T\right)-M_{l}(y)\left(T\right)\geq A\left\|T\right\|_{L^{1}(\mu_{l})}+B\,.

(96)

Moreover,

\sup_{a\in[0,k]}\frac{N^{a}_{\Psi}\left(\exp\left[T_{a}\right]\right)}{\int_{X}\exp\left[T_{a}\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{a}}<\infty\,.

(97)

Proof.

Applying
theorem

4.11
, we find that
W_{k,l}-M_{l}(y)
is
L^{1}(\mu_{l})
-norm coercive.
Hence, by
[
22
, Exercise 2.41]
, there exist
A>0
and
C\in\mathbb{R}
such that

W_{k,l}\left(T\right)-M_{l}(y)\left(T\right)\geq A\left\|T\right\|_{L^{1}(\mu_{l})}+C\,.

(98)

Now, for all
a\in[0,k]
and
T\in\mathcal{K}(\mu_{l})
,

V_{a,l}\left(T\right)=\ln\int_{X}\exp\left[T\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{a}\geq\ln\frac{N_{k}}{N_{a}}+V_{k,l}\left(T\right)\,.

(99)

By construction, it follows that
W_{a,l}\geq W_{k,l}+\ln(N_{k}/N_{a})
and thus

W_{a,l}\left(T\right)-M_{l}(y)\left(T\right)\geq W_{k,l}\left(T\right)-M_{l}(y)\left(T\right)+\ln\frac{N_{k}}{N_{a}}\geq A\left\|T\right\|_{L^{1}(\mu_{l})}+C+\ln N_{k}\,,

(100)

for all
T\in\mathcal{L}(\mu_{l})
.
That
M_{l}(y)\in\mathrm{int}\,\mathrm{dom}\,V_{a,l}^{*}
follows from
theorem

4.11
.

To verify
eq.

97
, note that the denominator
\int_{X}\exp[T_{a}]\mathrm{d}\mu_{a}
is bounded away from zero, since

\int_{X}\exp\left[T_{a}\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{a}\geq\int_{X}\exp\left[T_{a}\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{l}\frac{N_{l}}{N_{a}}\geq\int_{X}\exp\left[T_{a}-\int_{X}T_{a}\mathrm{d}\mu_{l}\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{l}\frac{N_{l}}{N_{a}}\geq N_{l}\,.

(101)

The second inequality follows because
T_{a}\in\mathrm{dom}\,W_{a,l}
implies
\int_{X}T_{a}\mathrm{d}\mu_{l}\geq 0
and the third inequality by the same reasoning as in
corollary

4.8
.
Furthermore,

N^{a}_{\Psi}\left(\exp\left[T_{a}\right]\right)\leq 1+\int_{X}\Psi\left(\exp\left[T_{a}\right]\right)\mathrm{d}\mu_{a}\leq 2+\int_{X}T_{a}\exp\left[T_{a}\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{a}\,.

(102)

Combining the above and
theorem

4.16
, we arrive at

\frac{N^{a}_{\Psi}\left(\exp\left[T_{a}\right]\right)}{\int_{X}\exp\left[T_{a}\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{a}}\leq M_{l}(y)\left(T_{a}\right)+\frac{2}{N_{l}}\,,

(103)

Suppose that
(T_{a})_{a\in[0,k]}
is
L^{1}(\mu_{l})
-unbounded.
Then,
eq.

96
shows that the function
a\mapsto W_{a,l}^{*}(M_{l}(y))
is unbounded on
[0,k]
.
However, by
corollaries

4.10
and
4.5
, this is a contradiction.
Hence,
(T_{a})_{a\in[0,k]}
is
L^{1}(\mu_{l})
-bounded such that by continuity,
M_{l}(y)(T_{a})
is bounded as well.
∎

Now, we can finally prove that
k\mapsto V_{k}^{*}(y)
satisfies a differential equation.

Theorem 4.18
.

Let
0\leq k\leq l
and
y\in\mathcal{A}(\mu)
be such that
M_{l}(y)\in\mathrm{int}\,\mathrm{dom}\,V_{k,l}^{*}
and for all
a\in[0,k]
, let
T_{a}\in\mathcal{L}(\mu_{l})
be as in
corollary

4.13
.
Then, the function
V^{*}(y):[0,k]\to\mathbb{R},a\mapsto V_{a}^{*}(y)
is absolutely continuous and for almost every
a\in[0,k]
,

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}a}V_{a}^{*}\left(y\right)=\frac{\int_{X}Q^{\prime}_{a}\exp\left[T_{a}\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{a}}{\int_{X}\exp\left[T_{a}\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{a}}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{X}Q_{a}^{\prime}\mathrm{d}\mu_{a}\,.

(104)

Proof.

By
lemmas

4.17
and
4.16
, for each
a\in[0,k]
, there exists
T_{a}\in\mathcal{L}(\mu_{l})
such that
eq.

94
holds and these
T_{a}
minimise
W_{a,l}-M_{l}(y)
.
Moreover, by
corollary

4.10
, we have

V_{a}^{*}\left(y\right)=M_{l}\left(y\right)\left(T_{a}\right)-W_{a,l}\left(T_{a}\right)\,.

(105)

Pick a sequence
(S^{a}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathcal{K}(\mu_{l})
with
\lim_{n\to\infty}V_{a,l}(S^{a}_{n})=W_{a,l}(T_{a})
and converging to

3.8
,
p_{k}
is Borel measurable, such that
x\mapsto p_{k}(x-w)
is
\mu
-measurable.
Applying the substitution
t=\exp[-r_{k}^{2}s^{2}/2]
and the Layer cake representation,

\displaystyle\nu_{k}\left(\left[0,\infty\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{k}}\int_{0}^{1}\mu\left(\left\{x\in Y_{k}\middle|\exp\left[-\frac{r_{k}^{2}}{2}p_{k}\left(x-w\right)^{2}\right]\right\}>t\right)\mathrm{d}t

(136)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{k}}\int_{X}\exp\left[-\frac{r_{k}^{2}}{2}p_{k}\left(x-w\right)^{2}\right]\mathrm{d}\mu=1\,.

Now, let
\epsilon>0
and use the same technique to see that

\nu_{k}\left(\left[\epsilon,\infty\right)\right)=\frac{1}{N_{k}}\int_{p_{k}(x-w)\geq\epsilon}\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}r_{k}^{2}p_{k}\left(x-w\right)^{2}\right]\mathrm{d}\mu\\
\leq\frac{1}{N_{k}}\exp\left[-\frac{r_{k}^{2}}{2}\epsilon^{2}\right]\,.

(137)

At the same time,

N_{k}\geq\int_{K^{B_{k}}_{\epsilon/2}(w)}\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}Q_{k}\right]\mathrm{d}\mu\geq\exp\left[-\frac{r_{k}^{2}}{8}\epsilon^{2}\right]\mu\left(K^{B_{k}}_{\epsilon/2}(w)\right)\,.

(138)

Furthermore, by
corollary

4.24
,

\mu(K^{B_{k}}_{\epsilon/2}(w))=\mu(K^{J_{k}}_{\epsilon/2}(w))\geq\mu(K^{J}_{\epsilon/2}(w))>0\,.

(139)

Thus
\lim_{k\to\infty}\nu_{k}([\epsilon,\infty))=0
.
Consequently,
\lim_{k\to\infty}\nu_{k}(U)=1
for every open neighbourhood
U
of zero.
For arbitrary open sets
V\subseteq[0,\infty)
we then have,
\liminf_{k\to\infty}\nu_{k}(V)\geq\delta_{0}(V)
.
Hence, the weak convergence follows (see e.g.
[
1
, Theorem 8.1.4]
).
∎

Definition 4.28

(
[
3
, Chapter 4.7]
)
.

Let
a,b\in\mathcal{H}(\mu_{-w})
and define the
Onsager-Machlup function

\mathcal{F}\left(a,b\right)=\lim_{s\searrow 0}\ln\frac{\mu\left(K^{J}_{s}(a)\right)}{\mu\left(K^{J}_{s}(b)\right)}\in\overline{\mathbb{R}}

(140)

whenever the limit exists in
\overline{\mathbb{R}}
where we also set
\ln(0)=-\infty
and
\ln(\infty)=\infty
and choose the convention
c/0=\infty
for all
c>0
and leave
0/0
undefined.
Similarly, we define the generalisation

\overline{\mathcal{F}}\left(a,b\right)=\lim_{(k,s)\to(\infty,0)}\ln\frac{\mu\left(K^{J_{k}}_{s}(a)\right)}{\mu\left(K^{J_{k}}_{s}(b)\right)}\in\overline{\mathbb{R}}

(141)

whenever it exists.

With
corollary

4.24
one may verify, that the existence of
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
is stronger than that of
\mathcal{F}
.

Corollary 4.29
.

Whenever,
\overline{\mathcal{F}}(a,b)
exists in
\overline{\mathbb{R}}
and

\forall s>0:\mu\left(K^{J}_{s}(a)\right)>0\qquad\text{or}\qquad\forall s>0:\mu\left(K^{J}_{s}(b)\right)>0\,,

(142)

then
\mathcal{F}(a,b)
exists in
\overline{\mathbb{R}}
and has the same value.
If neither holds,
\mathcal{F}(a,b)
is undefined.

Theorem 4.30
.

Suppose that
y\in\mathcal{A}(\mu)
is an admissible point and that
\overline{\mathcal{F}}\left(w,y\right)
exists.
Then,

\lim_{k\to\infty}\Gamma_{k}\left(y\right)=\overline{\mathcal{F}}\left(w,y\right)\,.

(143)

Proof.

Since
y
is admissible, it follows from
lemma

4.25
, that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\Gamma_{k}\left(y\right)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\ln\frac{N_{k}}{\int_{X}\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{I}_{k}\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{-y}}\,,

(144)

if the limit on the right-hand side exists in
\overline{\mathbb{R}}
.
Using the layer cake representation, we find

\frac{1}{N_{k}}\int_{X}\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{I}_{k}\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{-y}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mu\left(K^{J_{k}}_{s}(y)\right)}{\mu\left(K^{J_{k}}_{s}(w)\right)}\mathrm{d}\nu_{k}\left(s\right)\,.

(145)

Fixing any
\epsilon>0
, the integrand is bounded on
(\epsilon,\infty)
such that only the integral over
[0,\epsilon]
contributes to the limit as
k
goes to infinity.
Hence, because
\overline{\mathcal{F}}(w,y)
exists and
\nu_{k}
converges weakly to
\delta_{0}
, we find

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{N_{k}}\int_{X}\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{I}_{k}\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{-y}=\exp\left[-\overline{\mathcal{F}}(w,y)\right]

(146)

and the claim follows.
∎

4.3
Admissible Points

The most simple criterion for
eq.

134
to hold, is that
\mu_{-y}
is symmetric.
Since
\tilde{I}_{k}
is also symmetric
\mu_{-y}
-almost everywhere, the infimum is attained at
0
and the left-hand side is equal to
1
at every
k\geq 0
.
However, that can obviously just hold at most for a single
y\in\mathcal{A}(\mu)
.
The idea can however, be generalised to to an
‘approximate symmetry’
.
To that end, let
\overline{\mu_{-y}}
denote the pushforward measure of
\mu_{-y}
along the function
x\mapsto-x
.

Theorem 4.31
.

Suppose that for all
s>0
,
\mu(K^{J}_{s}(y))>0
and for every
\epsilon>0
, there are some
a\geq 0
and
\delta>0
such that for some standard representation
(Y_{a},B_{a})
of
J_{a}
,

\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu_{-y}\wedge\overline{\mu_{-y}}}{\mathrm{d}\mu_{-y}}\left(x\right)\geq 1-\epsilon\,,

(147)

for
\mu_{-y}
-almost every
x\in K^{J_{a}}_{\delta}(0)
, where
\wedge
denotes the greatest lower bound in the lattice of measures.
Then
y
is admissible.

Proof.

Let
\epsilon>0
and
a,\delta
as above.
Then, by the symmetry of
\mu_{-y}\wedge\overline{\mu_{-y}}
,

\displaystyle\inf_{\phi\in X^{*}}\int_{X}\exp\left[\phi-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{I}_{k}\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{-y}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{X}\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{I}_{k}\right]\mathrm{d}\left(\mu_{-y}\wedge\overline{\mu_{-y}}\right)

(148)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{K^{B_{a}}_{\delta}(0)}\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{I}_{k}\right]\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu_{-y}\wedge\overline{\mu_{-y}}}{\mathrm{d}\mu_{-y}}\mathrm{d}\mu_{-y}

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\epsilon\right)\int_{K^{B_{a}}_{\delta}(0)}\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{I}_{k}\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{-y}\,.

Hence,

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\inf_{\phi\in X^{*}}\frac{\int_{X}\exp\left[\phi-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{I}_{k}\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{-y}}{\int_{K^{B_{a}}_{\delta}(0)}\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{I}_{k}\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{-y}}\geq 1-\epsilon\,,

(149)

Now, because
\mu(K^{J}_{s}(y))>0
for all
s>0
, a proof along the lines of
lemma

4.27
, shows that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\int_{K^{B_{a}}_{\delta}(0)}\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{I}_{k}\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{-y}}{\int_{X}\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{I}_{k}\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{-y}}=1\,.

(150)

Consequently,

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\inf_{\phi\in X^{*}}\frac{\int_{X}\exp\left[\phi-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{I}_{k}\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{-y}}{\int_{X}\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{I}_{k}\right]\mathrm{d}\mu_{-y}}\geq 1-\epsilon\,

(151)

and because
\epsilon>0
was arbitrary, the claim follows.
∎

Unfortunately, the approximate symmetry is also a very strong requirement.
A much better result can be given when
\mu
is equivalent to a centred Radon Gaußian measure with a suitable density.
Then all
y\in\mathcal{A}(\mu)
are admissible as was shown in special cases in
[
23
]
.
Unfortunately, less restrictive sufficient conditions are at present not known to the author.

5
Conclusion

It has been shown that Wetterich’s equation indeed holds for almost every flow parameter
k\geq 0
, provided that the point
y\in\mathcal{A}(\mu)
lies in the interior of the domain of the auxillary function
V_{k,l}^{*}
.
The interiority condition is clearly of no surprise, because subdifferentials are often well-behaved on these interiors only.
Consequently, the setup is most applicable to measures whose moment-generating functions grow faster than
\exp[C\|\cdot\|_{L^{1}(\mu_{l})}]
for all
C>0
.
Then
\mathrm{dom}\,V_{k,l}^{*}
becomes the whole space and all
y\in\mathcal{A}(\mu)
lie in the corresponding interior.
A setback is the mere absolute continuity of
k\mapsto\Gamma_{k}(y)
on compact intervals.
It would certainly be desirable to obtain (continuous) differentiability.
Furthermore, it is unclear whether

\lim_{k\to\infty}\Gamma_{k}(y)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}k}\Gamma_{k}\left(y\right)\mathrm{d}k

(152)

holds as a Lebesgue integral.
It has not been shown that

D^{2}W_{k,l}\left(T_{k}\right)\left(U_{a},U_{a}\right))=U_{a}\left[\left(D^{2}\Gamma_{k}\left(y\right)+\underline{I_{k}}\right)^{-1}\left(U_{a}\right)\right]

(153)

holds as it does in the cases considered in
[
23
]
.
This is due to a missing suitable definition of second derivative of
W_{k,l}
that is compatible with convex conjugates and was discussed in
remark

4.21
.

corollary

4.23
is also an impressive incarnation of the implicit function theorem.
At a point
y\in\mathcal{A}(\mu)
in the considered interior, one has

\Gamma_{k}\left(y\right)=M_{l}\left(y\right)\left(\left(\partial W_{k,l}^{\prime}\right)^{-1}\left(y\right)\right)-W_{k,l}\left(\left(\partial W_{k,l}^{\prime}\right)^{-1}\left(y\right)\right)-\frac{1}{2}\underline{I_{k}}\left(y-w,y-w\right)\,,

(154)

while Wetterich’s equation gives the corresponding derivative with respect to
k
in terms of
D^{2}W_{k,l}((\partial W_{k,l}^{\prime})^{-1}(y))
.
This may provide a hint, to the existence of a generalised implicit function theorem in convex analysis.

The asymptotic boundary condition of
\Gamma_{k}(y)
is a generalised version of the Onsager-Machlup function with respect to the metrics induced by the family
(J_{k})_{k\geq 0}
of measurable inner products, provided that
y
is
‘admissible’
.
Rather restrictive sufficient conditions for admissibility were given in
section

4.3
while more widely applicable ones are at present unknown to the author.

The measurable inner products introduced in
section

3
are of particular interest on their own, being bilinear analogoues of the linear Lusin measurable functionals considered in
[
20
]
.
With a separability condition, they are, in fact, examples of measurable functions on a product space with an almost-everywhere well-defined restriction to the diagonal.

In order to understand the derivative in
corollary

4.14
, it is useful to characterise
(\mathcal{L}(\mu_{l}),\overline{p_{l}})^{*}
.

Lemma 6.1
.

For every
\alpha\in(\mathcal{L}(\mu_{l}),\overline{p_{l}})^{*}
, there exists some nonnegative
f_{\alpha}\in L^{\infty}(\mu_{l})
such that

\alpha\left(T\right)=\int_{X}f_{\alpha}T\mathrm{d}\mu_{l}

(155)

for all
T\in\mathcal{L}(\mu_{l})
.
Conversely, for every nonnegative
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu_{l})
, the mapping

T\mapsto\int_{X}fT\mathrm{d}\mu_{l}

(156)

is an element of
(\mathcal{L}(\mu_{l}),\overline{p_{l}})^{*}
.

Proof.

The space
L^{1}(\mu_{l})
with its standard partial order is an ordered topological vector space with positive cone given by

P=\left\{f\in L^{1}(\mu_{l}):f\geq 0\right\}\,.

(157)

For every
\alpha\in(\mathcal{L}(\mu_{l}),\overline{p_{l}})^{*}
, there is some
C>0
such that
\alpha(T)\leq C\overline{p_{l}}(T)
for all
T\in Y
.
Let
U\subseteq L^{1}(\mu_{l})
denote the closed unit ball, and fix any
f\in\mathcal{L}(\mu_{l})\cap[U-P]
.
Then, there is some
p\in P
such that
f+p\in U
and

\alpha\left(f\right)\leq C\int_{X}\max\left\{f,0\right\}\mathrm{d}\mu_{l}\leq C\int_{X}\max\left\{f+p,0\right\}\mathrm{d}\mu_{l}\leq C\,.

(158)

Hence,
[
18
, p. 227, Theorem 5.4]
applies and there is some continuous nonnegative linear functional on
L^{1}(\mu_{l})
whose restriction to
\mathcal{L}(\mu_{l})
coincides with
\alpha
.
Such an extension is given by integration against some nonnegative
f_{\alpha}\in L^{\infty}(\mu_{l})
.
For the converse, let
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu_{l})
be nonnegative and
T\in\mathcal{L}(\mu_{l})
.
Then,

\int_{X}fT\mathrm{d}\mu_{l}\leq\int_{X}f\max\left\{T,0\right\}\mathrm{d}\mu_{l}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{L^{\infty}(f)}\overline{p_{l}}\left(T\right)\,.

(159)

∎

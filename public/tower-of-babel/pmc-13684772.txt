Pressure Reactivity Index - Daily in Pediatric Traumatic Brain Injury
Authors: Suresh Nathan, Farooqi Ahmed, Michael Wolf, Prout Andrew, Ajit Sarnaik
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurpose Pressure Reactivity Index (PRx) is a measure of cerebral autoregulation that is associated with outcomes in pediatric traumatic brain injury. It is a resource-intensive tool that is not universally available. This study aims to assess the association of a novel low-resolution index derived from the same physiological inputs as the PRx, termed the Pressure Reactivity Index-Daily (PRx-D), with outcomes in children with traumatic brain injury (TBI). Methods This is a secondary analysis of the Approaches and Decisions in Acute Pediatric Traumatic Brain Injury (ADAPT), an international multi-institutional comparative effectiveness trial that enrolled children from 2014–2017 who received intracranial monitoring for severe traumatic brain injury (Glasgow Coma Scale < 9). One thousand patients under 18 years of age were enrolled in the ADAPT trial. PRx-D was calculated using the Spearman correlation coefficient of hourly mean arterial pressures (MAP) and intracranial pressures (ICP). One PRx-D value was calculated for every day of admission for seven days. The minimum, median, maximum and day 1 PRx-D were used in the analysis. The primary outcome variable was dichotomized Glasgow Outcome Scale Extended Peds (DGOSE), with 1–4 defined as favorable outcome and 5–8 defined as unfavorable outcome. The secondary outcome variable was hospital death. Results Higher values of median PRx-D, maximum PRx-D, and day 1 PRx-D were associated with unfavorable DGOSE in bivariate analysis. In multivariable analyses, higher maximum PRx-D (OR 2.45, 95% CI 1.25–4.82) was independently associated with unfavorable DGOSE. Higher median, minimum, maximum, and day 1 PRx-D were associated with hospital death in bivariate analysis. In multivariable analyses, higher minimum PRx-D, (OR 2.35, 95% CI 1.03–5.37) and median PRx-D (OR 4.34, 95% CI 1.42–13.23) were independently associated with hospital death. Conclusions PRx-D, a clinical measure that can be obtained in lower resource settings, is independently associated with outcome in children with severe TBI.

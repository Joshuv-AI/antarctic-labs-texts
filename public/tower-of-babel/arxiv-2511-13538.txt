Full range of infinite point blow-up exponents for the critical generalized KdV equation
Nailya Manatova Laboratoire de mathématiques de Versailles Université de Versailles St-Quentin en Y., CNRS Université Paris-Saclay nailya.manatova@uvsq.fr

Abstract
For the quintic, mass critical generalized Korteweg-de Vries equation, for any ν ∈ ( 1 2 , 1 ) \nu\in(\frac{1}{2},1) , we prove the existence of solutions in the energy space that blow up in finite time T > 0 T>0 with the blow-up rate ‖ ∂ x u ⁡ ( t ) ‖ L 2 ∼ ( T − t ) − ν \|\partial_{x}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\sim(T-t)^{-\nu} (infinite point blow-up). These solutions are constructed arbitrarily close to the family of solitons and correspond to the concentration of a soliton traveling at + ∞ +\infty in space as t ↑ T t\uparrow T . This complements the previous results obtained in [ 20 ] on infinite point exotic blow-up, which were valid under the technical restriction ν > 11 13 \nu>\frac{11}{13} . The value ν = 1 2 \nu=\frac{1}{2} corresponds to a critical case to be treated elsewhere. At the technical level, we implement a modification of the virial-energy functional originally introduced in [ 19 , 20 ] , to allow all ν > 1 2 \nu>\frac{1}{2} and simplify the proof of energy estimates.



I
Introduction

I.1
Presentation of the problem

We consider the
L^{2}
critical generalized Korteweg-de Vries equation (gKdV)

(1)

\partial_{t}U+\partial_{x}(\partial_{xx}U+U^{5})=0,\qquad(t,x)\in[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}.

The equation being posed in
\mathbb{R}
, in the following we write
H^{1}
instead of
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})

and similarly for other spaces of functions.

From the works of Kato
[
9
]
and of Kenig, Ponce and Vega
[
10
]
, the Cauchy problem is locally well-posed in the energy space
H^{1}
. For a given
U_{0}\in H^{1}
, there exists a unique maximal solution
U
of (
1
) in a functional space included in
C([0,T),H^{1})
, which satisfies in addition

(2)

\text{if }\quad T<+\infty\quad\text{then}\quad\lim_{t\uparrow T}\|\partial_{x}U(t)\|_{L^{2}}=+\infty.

If
T<+\infty
, then we say that the solution blows up in finite time.

For an
H^{1}
solution, the mass and the energy, defined respectively by

(3)

M(U)(t)=\int U^{2}(t,x)\,dx,\qquad E(U)(t)=\frac{1}{2}\int(\partial_{x}U)^{2}(t,x)\,dx-\frac{1}{6}\int U^{6}(t,x)\,dx\,,

are conserved along the evolution in time.

The equation is scaling and translation invariant: if
U
is a solution,
then for any
\lambda>0
,
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
,

(4)

U_{\lambda,x_{0}}(t,x):=\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}U(\lambda^{3}t,\lambda x+\sigma)

is also a solution to (
1
). The scaling symmetry leaves the norm
L^{2}
invariant, so the problem is mass critical.

The family of solitary wave solutions of (
1
) is given by

(5)

\Big\{\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{0}\,Q\big(\lambda^{-1}_{0}(x-\lambda^{-2}_{0}t-\sigma_{0})\big)\;\big|\;(\lambda_{0},\sigma_{0})\in(0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}\Big\}

where

Q(x)=\left(3\sech^{2}(2x)\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}

is the unique (up to translation) positive solution of the equation

(6)

-Q^{\prime\prime}+Q-Q^{5}=0\quad\text{on}\;\mathbb{R}.

The ground state
Q
satisfies
E(Q)=0
and is related to the following sharp Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (see for instance
[
33
]
)

(7)

\frac{1}{3}\int\phi^{6}\leq\bigg(\frac{\int\phi^{2}}{\int Q^{2}}\bigg)^{2}\,\int(\partial_{x}\phi)^{2}\qquad\forall\phi\in H^{1}.

From the mass and energy conservation and the above inequality, any
H^{1}
initial data
U_{0}
with subcritical mass
\|U_{0}\|_{L^{2}}<\|Q\|_{L^{2}}
generates a global and bounded solution of (
1
) in
H^{1}
. This result is sharp due to the existence of
a minimal mass blow-up solution.

Indeed, minimal mass blow-up for critical gKdV equation is addressed in
[
21
]
:
there exists a unique (up to invariances) blow-up solution
S
of (
1
) with minimal mass
\|S(t)\|_{L^{2}}=\|Q\|_{L^{2}}
. The solution
S
blows up in finite time and satisfies
\|\partial_{x}S(t)\|_{L^{2}}\sim t^{-1}\|Q^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}
as
t\downarrow 0

(by convention, the blow-up time is chosen at
t=0
for the particular solution
S
).

The work
[
19
]
, by Martel, Merle and Raphaël, provides a description of the flow of the gKdV equation for initial data such that

(8)

\|Q\|_{L^{2}}<\|U_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|Q\|_{L^{2}}+{\varepsilon},\qquad{\varepsilon}\ll 1

i.e.
for masses slightly above the threshold
\|Q\|_{L^{2}}
,
and for
H^{1}
initial data with a sufficient decay on the right in space.
In such case, only three scenarios can occur:

1.

finite time blow-up with the rate
\|\partial_{x}U(t)\|\sim(T-t)^{-1}
as
t\uparrow T
;

2.

global existence and local convergence to a final rescaled soliton in large time;

3.

finite time exit from the soliton neighborhood.

The third case is not well-understood at this stage: in particular,
the long time behaviour of the solution
after its exiting the neighbourhood of the soliton family remains an open question.
From
[
19
]
, it is also known that the first and third cases (blow-up and exit)
are in a certain sense stable.
Moreover, in the work
[
18
]
, Martel, Merle, Nakanishi and Raphaël
prove that those two stable regimes are separated by a
\mathscr{C}^{1}
codimension-one manifold on which the unstable scenario occurs (convergence to a rescaled soliton).

Because of the stability (in a certain sense) of the
(T-t)^{-1}
blow-up,
the blow-up behaviors corresponding to
\|\partial_{x}U(t)\|_{L^{2}}\sim(T-t)^{-\nu}
for

\nu\neq 1
, or any other blow-up behavior, will be called "non generic" or
"exotic".
So far, for the mass critical gKdV equation, examples of non-generic blow-up rates have been constructed in the work
[
20
]
of Martel, Merle, Raphaël.
In particular, they have obtained finite time blow-up solutions for the continuum of rates
\nu>\frac{11}{13}
. The value
\frac{11}{13}
is a technical threshold and it was not clear from the proof in
[
21
]
which other rates
\nu
should be possible among

\frac{1}{3}\leq\nu\leq\frac{11}{13}
.
Recall that rates
0<\nu<\frac{1}{3}
are ruled out by a standard scaling argument and
the Cauchy theory. Moreover, from
[
24
]
,
no blow-up occurs with
\nu=\frac{1}{3}
for solutions close to solitons.
Recall also that global solutions with grow-up in infinite time
with exponential and all powers of polynomials blow-up rates
were also constructed in
[
20
]
.

Since such behaviors do not enter the classification of
[
19
]
, the initial data
shall not satisfy the assumptions of
[
19
]
.
Actually, the exotic blow-up in
[
20
]
are a consequence of an explicit
tail of the initial data. This tails produces a modification in the blow-up behavior
by acting as a nonlinear source term and modifying the ODE system of modulation parameters
(scale and translation). See §
I.5
for heuristics.

All the blow-up behaviors described above are obtained by concentration of a soliton
of the form

U(t,x)\approx\frac{1}{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)}\,Q\left(\frac{x-\sigma(t)}{\lambda(t)}\right)

where
\sigma(t)
represents the position of the soliton and
\lambda(t)
is related to its
scale. By a simple calculation, we have
\|\partial_{x}U(t)\|_{L^{2}}\approx\lambda^{-1}(t)
.
Moreover, it can be proved that
\sigma_{t}(t)\approx\lambda^{-2}(t)
,
which justifies why
\nu=\frac{1}{2}
is critical in a
blow-up behavior of the form
\lambda(t)\approx(T-t)^{\frac{1}{2}}
:
for
\nu>\frac{1}{2}
, blow-up occurs at an infinite point (
\lim_{T}\sigma=+\infty
),
while for
\frac{1}{3}<\nu<\frac{1}{2}
, blow-up occurs at a finite point
(
\lim_{T}\sigma<+\infty
).
In particular, all exotic blow-up in
[
20
]
occur at an infinite point.
The only example so far of finite point blow-up for the mass critical gKdV equation
is provided by the work
[
27
]
of Martel, Pilod, with the blow-up rate
\nu=\frac{2}{5}
.
Apart from this specific value, the existence of blow-up solutions with finite point blow-up, corresponding to the interval
\frac{1}{3}<\nu<\frac{1}{2}
remains open.

I.2
Main result

In the present article,
we complete the study of the power finite-time blow-up
for the mass critical gKdV equation with infinite point blow-up,
by constructing initial data leading to blow-up with any rate
\nu\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
.

Theorem I.1
.

Let
\nu\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
. For any
\delta>0
, there exist
T>0
and
U_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
with
\|U_{0}-Q\|_{H^{1}}\leq\delta
such that the
H^{1}
solution
U
of (
1
) with initial data
U_{0}
exists on
[0,T)
and blows up at the finite time
T
with

(9)

\|\partial_{x}U(t)\|_{L^{2}}\sim(T-t)^{-\nu}\qquad\text{as}\quad t\to T^{-}.

Theorem
I.1
combined with
[
20
, Theorem 1]

shows the existence of infinite point blow-up solutions with all possible rates
\nu>\frac{1}{2}
. These solutions are expected to be unstable, as it will be explained later,
even if we do not provide a proof of this fact here.
Concerning infinite point blow-up, a relevant remaining open question concerns the rate
\nu=\frac{1}{2}
. Work is currently in progress on this scenario, with a slow logarithmic infinite point blow-up. This will appear elsewhere.

The overall strategy of the proof of Theorem
I.1

follows the one in
[
20
]
with two key differences:

1.

The explicit tail is used to reach all values of
\nu

and becomes quite small (large negative powers of
x
) as
\nu\downarrow\frac{1}{2}
.
It is thus more delicate to make it effective to adjust the blow-up rate.
In particular, in contrast with
[
20
]
, one has to work in a weighted space
whose weight depends on the value of
\nu
.

2.

The articles
[
19
,
21
,
20
]
introduce a mixed virial-energy functional to prove crucial local energy estimates on the solutions.
Here, we implement a modification of this virial-energy functional which simplifies the computations, reduces the number of bootstrap assumptions and clarifies the proof.

We refer to §
I.5
for a detailed sketch of the proof.

I.3
Some related results for other nonlinear dispersive or wave models

The
L^{2}
critical Nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLS) has many similarities with the critical (gKdV) equation. For the case of the 1D NLS equation, the ground state is the same as for (gKdV).
For higher dimensions, the ground state is strictly positive, smooth and has exponential decay
at infinity.
The criterion on global existence due to Weinstein
[
33
]

is the same as for (gKdV): any
H^{1}
initial data with subcritical mass leads to
a unique global and bounded solution.
For (NLS), a minimal mass blow-up solution is known explicitly with the blow-up rate
(T-t)^{-1}
, using the pseudoconformal symmetry.
This minimal mass blow-up solution was proved to be unique by Merle
[
30
]
(up to invariances).
Solutions with the pseudoconformal blow-up rate
(T-t)^{-1}
were constructed in the work of Bourgain, Wang
[
1
]
and subsequently in the work of Krieger, Schlag
[
14
]
, by perturbing the minimal mass blow-up solution with strongly degenerate profiles.
The work of Landman, Papanicolaou, Sulem, Sulem
[
16
]
has conjectured the so called "
\log\log
law" of blow-up. A first rigorous construction of such blow-up is due to Perelman in
[
31
]
in 1D.
A complete description of this regime for space dimension
d\leq 5
, and in particular its stability in
H^{1}
, was obtained in a series of works of Merle, Raphaël; see for example
[
28
,
29
]
.
Such blow-up occurs at a finite point, for some
H^{1}
open set of slightly supercritical initial data, with speed
(T-t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{\log{|\log{(T-t)}|}}
.
The proof of this result relies on a coercivity property of a certain operator related to the ground state proved in
[
29
]
for
d=1
. The lack of an explicit formula for
Q
in higher dimensions complicates the proof of this property, but it has been verified numerically for
2\leq d\leq 4
in
[
5
]
and for
5\leq d\leq 10
in
[
35
]
.
The subject of exotic blow-up (other than "
\log\log
" and
\frac{1}{t}
) for (NLS) is still widely open.
See, however,
[
32
]
for a negative result.

Recall that the dependence of the grow up rate on the tail of given initial data was previously observed for global in time growing up solutions of the energy critical harmonic heat flow in
[
6
]
.

The blow-up for the energy critical problems has also attracted considerable attention. Krieger, Schlag and Tataru
[
12
,
13
]

pioneered the construction of continuum of exotic blow-up in such a context,
both for the energy-critical wave map problem and the energy critical focusing wave equation in
d=3
. Particularly, they proved the existence of such solutions with concentration rate

\lambda(t)\approx t^{-1-\nu}
for any
\nu>\frac{1}{2}
. In
[
15
]
this condition was relaxed to
\nu>0
. Then, Donninger, Huang, Krieger and Schlag
[
4
]
investigated the existence of other admissible rescaling functions for this type of blow-up. Furthermore, they constructed blow-up solutions with the scaling law
\lambda(t)=t^{-1-v(t)}
where
v(t)
is eternally oscillating and asymptotically constant. Such problems are also relevant for the mass critical (gKdV) equation.

Other results are known for the higher space dimensions.
Jendrej
[
8
]
constructed type-II blow-up solutions in the five space dimensions.
For the energy-critical one equivariant wave maps in the two sphere, Jendrej, Lawrie, Rodriguez in
[
7
]
provide a sharp classification of the blow up rate for solutions constructed by prescribing the radiation. In the recent work
[
11
]
, Kim, Kwon and Oh adapt the their method to construct finite-energy blow-up solutions for the radial self-dual Chern–Simons–Schrödinger equation with a continuum of blow-up rates.

I.4
Notation

Throughout the text,
X\lesssim Y
is a shorthand for the inequality
X\leq C\,Y
for some time-space independent constant
C
. In general manner, the constants are time-space independent, unless otherwise stated.

For a given small positive constant
0<\alpha^{*}\ll 1
, we denote
\delta
a small parameter depending on
\alpha^{*}
such that

(10)

\delta(\alpha^{*})\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad\alpha^{*}\to 0.

For
1\leq p\leq+\infty
,
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
denotes the classical real-valued Lebesgue space. For
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, we denote their scalar product

(11)

(f,g)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(y)\,g(y)\,dy.

We define the weighted Lebesgue spaces
L^{2}(\mathbb{R};e^{-\frac{|y|}{10}}dy)
and
L^{2}(\mathbb{R};e^{\frac{y}{B}}dy)
, for
B>100
, equipped respectively with the norms

(12)

\|f\|_{L^{2}_{sol}}=\Big(\int f^{2}e^{-\frac{|y|}{10}}dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|f\|_{L^{2}_{B}}=\Big(\int f^{2}(y)e^{\frac{y}{B}}dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

In the following, for simplicity of notation, we omit
dy
and the integration domain is
\mathbb{R}
, unless otherwise stated.

In the space
L^{2}
, the generator of scaling symmetry is given by

(13)

\Lambda f=\frac{1}{2}f+yf^{\prime}

and the linearized operator
\mathscr{L}
around the ground state by

(14)

\mathscr{L}f=-f^{\prime\prime}+f-5Q^{4}f.

I.5
Strategy of the proof

The proof proceeds in four main steps, detailed in Sections
II
to
V
.

1.

Definition and role of the tail

Given
c_{0}<0
and
\theta>1
, we consider a smooth
L^{2}
function
f_{0}
with power decay of order
\theta
on the right

(15)

f_{0}(x)=c_{0}x^{-\theta}\qquad\text{for}\quad x_{0}\gg 1.

Then, we define
f
the solution of (
1
) evolving from the initial data
f_{0}
. The evolution of such decaying tails is studied by local energy estimates for (gKdV).
We know that
f
is global and that the tail of
f_{0}
is preserved for
x\gtrsim t
.
The aim of this tail will be to modify in the next step the behaviour of the coupled system of modulation equations of the parameters of a blowing up soliton, by nonlinear interaction
of the tail with the soliton.

2.

Modulation around a refined profile and system of parameters

We look for a solution
U
of (
1
) of the following form

U(t,x)=v(t,x)+f(t,x),

with

v(t,x)=\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}(s)\big(Q_{b(s)}(y)+f(t(s),\sigma(s))R(y)+{\varepsilon}(s,y)\big)

and where
(s,y)
are the rescaled time and space variables defined by

(16)

\frac{ds}{dt}=\frac{1}{\lambda^{3}},\quad y=\frac{x-\sigma(s)}{\lambda(s)}.

Note that as
t\in[0,T)
,
s
will be defined in a certain interval
[s_{0},+\infty)

and
y\in\mathbb{R}
.
In the formula above for
v
,
Q_{b}
is a refined soliton profile (the parameter
b(s)
takes into account the variation of the scaling parameter
\lambda(s)
), the function
R(y)
is a specific rapidly decaying function and
{\varepsilon}
is a remainder term to be estimated by the energy method in the next step.
Note that the term
f(t,\sigma)R(y)
aims at compensating the first order of the nonlinear interaction between the soliton and the tail
f(t,x)
added to the solution
U
.
This interaction is relatively weak, since the soliton is concentrated (this means that
\lambda(s)\ll 1
) and the position of the soliton
\sigma(s)
is large, which means that
f(t,\sigma)
is small.
However, this interaction is sufficient to
modify the blow-up behavior under certain conditions as we will formally justify now.

The equation of
{\varepsilon}
and the parameters
(\lambda,\sigma,b)
are given in (
173
).
From this equation and orthogonality conditions prescribed on
{\varepsilon}
, we obtain
the dynamical system of the parameters
(\lambda,\sigma,b)
.
Formally (
i.e.
neglecting error terms), we obtain

(17)

\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}+b=0,\qquad\sigma_{s}=\lambda,\qquad\frac{d}{ds}\Big(\frac{b}{\lambda^{2}}+\frac{4}{\int Q}\,c_{0}\,\lambda^{-\frac{3}{2}}\sigma^{-\theta}\Big)=0.

The equations for
\lambda
and
\sigma
are natural in view of equation (
173
). The equation of
b
is more subtle and it will be justified later.
Let us say for the moment that the term containing
\sigma^{-\theta}
comes from the nonlinear interaction of the tail
f
with the soliton. In practice, this term is computed thanks to the addition of
f(t,\sigma)R(y)
in the formula for
v(t,x)
above.
The precise computations that justify this dynamical system can be found in Lemma
III.3
- Lemma
III.6
.

In what follows, we perform formal computations related to (
17
).

Integrating the last equation on some time interval
[s_{0},s]
, we have

(18)

\frac{b(s)}{\lambda^{2}(s)}+\frac{4\,c_{0}}{\int Q}\,\lambda^{-\frac{3}{2}}(s)\,\sigma^{-\theta}(s)=l_{0}.

In the regime considered, we will check that
\frac{b(s)}{\lambda^{2}(s)}\to+\infty
, which
means that the value of the integration constant
l_{0}
does not matter.
For now on, we fix
l_{0}=0
to simplify.
Replacing
b
by
-\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}
and then
\lambda
by
\sigma_{s}
(from (
17
)) yields

(19)

\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lambda_{s}=\frac{4\,c_{0}}{\int Q}\,\lambda\,\sigma^{-\theta}=\frac{4\,c_{0}}{\int Q}\,\sigma_{s}\,\sigma^{-\theta}.

We expect
\lambda(s)\to 0^{+}
as
s\to+\infty
and thus
\lambda_{s}\leq 0
, thus the first equality leads us to choose
c_{0}<0
.

After integration, we get

(20)

\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(s)+\frac{2\,c_{0}}{(\theta-1)\int Q}\,\sigma^{1-\theta}(s)=l_{1}.

Since
\lambda(s)\to 0
and
\sigma(s)\to+\infty
as
s\to+\infty
, one has to impose

l_{1}=0
.
Combining with the second equation in (
17
), and choosing at

s=s_{0}
the initial value
\sigma^{2\theta-1}(s_{0})=(2\theta-1)\Big(\frac{2\,c_{0}}{(\theta-1)\int Q}\Big)^{2}s_{0}
, we get

(21)

\sigma_{s}=\Big(\frac{2\,c_{0}}{(\theta-1)\int Q}\Big)^{2}\,\sigma^{2(1-\theta)}\qquad\iff\qquad\sigma^{2\theta-1}(s)=(2\theta-1)\Big(\frac{2\,c_{0}}{(\theta-1)\int Q}\Big)^{2}s.

Inserted in the previous relations, this gives

(22)

\lambda(s)=(2\theta-1)^{-\frac{2(\theta-1)}{2\theta-1}}\Big(\frac{2\,c_{0}}{(\theta-1)\int Q}\Big)^{\frac{2}{2\theta-1}}\,s^{-\frac{2(\theta-1)}{2\theta-1}}

With the notation

(23)

\beta=\frac{2(\theta-1)}{2\theta-1},\qquad\theta=\frac{1-\frac{\beta}{2}}{1-\beta},\qquad c_{0}=-\frac{\int Q}{2}(\theta-1)(2\theta-1)^{\theta-1}<0,

we have obtained

(24)

\lambda(s)=s^{-\beta},\qquad\sigma(s)=\frac{1}{1-\beta}s^{1-\beta},\qquad b(s)=\frac{\beta}{s}.

Reciprocally, one easily checks that (
24
) are solutions of the dynamical system (
17
). Moreover, substituting (
24
) into (
18
) indeed justifies that the constant
l_{0}
could be neglected. In contrast, if
l_{1}
is chosen positive,
one formally obtains from (
20
) that the solution will converge to a fixed rescaled soliton (
\lambda\to l_{1}^{2}
).
In this sense, the direction
l_{1}=0
presents an instability of the exotic blow-up.
This observation is formal and we will not pursue this issue here.
However, it justifies the use of a topological argument to conclude the bootstrap argument in the proof of the Theorem
I.1
.

3.

Energy estimates

The first two steps are similar to the work of Martel, Merle, Raphaël
[
20
]
.
In the third step, we introduce a modification of the virial-energy functional of the form

(25)

\mathscr{H}(s)=s^{j}\mathscr{F}(s)+\lambda^{k}(s)\int{\varphi}_{k}\,{\varepsilon}^{2}(s),

which simplifies and extends the work in
[
19
,
21
,
20
]
.
The term
\mathscr{F}
represents a mixed virial-energy functional at the level of the equation of
{\varepsilon}
. In the regime (
24
),
\mathscr{F}
is coercive and its control allows to bound locally the remainder
{\varepsilon}
.

Compared to
[
20
]
, the simplification comes from the fact that adding the term

\lambda^{k}(s)\int{\varphi}_{k}\,{\varepsilon}^{2}(s)\qquad{\varphi}_{k}(y)=y^{k},\,y\gg 1

directly to the functional
\mathscr{H}

allows to control the delicate scaling terms that appear when differentiating
s^{j}\mathscr{F}
. In
[
20
]
, the control of such terms is based on a bootstrap
estimate. Note that this term is at the scaling level, which means that coming back to the original variables
(t,x)
, no scaling term in
\lambda
would appear.

The functional in (
25
) also extends
[
20
]
thanks to the introduction of the parameter
k
, which depends on the blow-up rate.
Choosing a fixed
k
, as was done in
[
20
]
, does not allow to reach all the values of
\nu\to\frac{1}{2}^{+}
. In the paper
[
20
]
, the authors considered the interval
\frac{5}{4}<\theta<\frac{29}{18}
, which corresponds to
\frac{1}{3}<\beta<\frac{11}{20}
and thus any blow-up rate
\nu>\frac{11}{13}
. In our work, we consider the interval
\theta>\frac{3}{2}
, therefore
\frac{1}{2}<\beta<1
and thus
\frac{1}{2}<\nu<1
.
In particular,
k\to+\infty
as
\nu\to\frac{1}{2}^{+}
(equivalently
\theta\to+\infty
).
It might appear a bit counter-intuitive that a stronger modification of blow-up laws
(as
\nu
approaches
\frac{1}{2}
, it differs more from the stable rate
\nu=1
)
is the consequence of a more decaying tail
\theta\to+\infty
since the influence of the tail should be weaker.

4.

Construction of blowing up solutions

For well-chosen initial data arbitrarily close to the soliton in a given topology, we construct solutions
in the regime close to (
17
),
using a bootstrap argument and energy estimates as described above.
See Proposition
V.1
for a precise statement.

Now, we justify formally that the behaviour of the parameters in (
24
), leads to the blow-up scenarios in the Theorem
I.1
in the original time
t
.

By the definition of the rescaled time and since
\beta>\frac{1}{3}
, we get that

(26)

\int^{+\infty}_{s_{0}}\lambda^{3}(s)\,ds=T<+\infty.

Therefore, the solution
U
blows up in finite time.

The law of the rescaled variable yields the following behaviour of
the rescaled time
s
in terms of
t

(27)

s(t)\sim\big[(3\beta-1)\,(T-t)\big]^{-\frac{1}{3\beta-1}}\qquad\text{for}\,\,t\to T^{-}.

Therefore,

(28)

\lambda(s(t))\sim(T-t)^{\nu}\qquad\text{with }\nu=\frac{\beta}{3\beta-1}\in(\frac{1}{2},1).

Combining with the consequence of the decomposition around the approximate blow-up profile, it holds for some
c>0

(29)

\|\partial_{x}U(t)\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\sim\lambda^{-1}(s(t))\sim c\,(T-t)^{-\nu}.

Those computations are detailed in the proof of the Theorem
I.1
in Section
V
.

III
Decomposition around the soliton

III.1
Structure of the linearized operator

Here we recall some properties of the linearized operator
\mathscr{L}
around the soliton. The operator is defined as follows

(35)

\mathscr{L}\phi=-\phi^{\prime\prime}+\phi-5Q^{4}\phi.

We introduce the following function space

(36)

\mathscr{Y}:=\{\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})\,|\,\forall k\in\mathbb{N},\exists C_{k},r_{k}>0\;\text{ s.t. }\;\lvert\phi^{(k)}(y)\rvert\leq C_{k}(1+|y|)^{r_{k}}e^{-|y|},\forall y\in\mathbb{R}\}.

We recall the following standard result, (see for instance
[
34
]
and
[
23
]
).

Lemma III.1

(Properties of the linearised operator
\mathscr{L}
)
.

The operator
\mathscr{L}:H^{2}(\mathbb{R})\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
satisfies

1.

(Spectrum) The operator
\mathscr{L}
has only one negative eigenvalue,
\mathscr{L}Q^{3}=-8Q^{3}
. Moreover,
\ker{\mathscr{L}}=\{aQ^{\prime}:a\in\mathbb{R}\}
and
\sigma_{ess}(\mathscr{L})=[1,+\infty)
.

2.

(Scaling)
\mathscr{L}\Lambda Q=-2Q
and
(Q,\Lambda Q)=0
.

3.

(Coercivity) For any
\phi\in H^{1}
holds

(37)

(\phi,Q^{3})=(\phi,Q^{\prime})=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad(\mathscr{L}\phi,\phi)\geq\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

Moreover, there exists
\nu_{0}>0
such that for any
\phi\in H^{1}

(38)

(\mathscr{L}\phi,\phi)\geq\nu_{0}\|\phi\|^{2}_{H^{1}}-\frac{1}{\nu_{0}}[(\phi,Q)^{2}+(\phi,y\Lambda Q)^{2}+(\phi,\Lambda Q)^{2}].

4.

(Invertibility) There exists a unique function
R\in\mathscr{Y}
, even and such that

(39)

\mathscr{L}R=5Q^{4},\qquad\qquad(Q,R)=-\frac{3}{4}\int Q.

5.

(Invertibility (bis))
There exists a unique function
P\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that
P^{\prime}\in\mathscr{Y}
and

(40)

(\mathscr{L}P)^{\prime}=\Lambda Q,\qquad\lim_{y\to-\infty}P(y)=\frac{1}{2}\int Q,\qquad\lim_{y\to+\infty}P(y)=0.

Moreover,

(41)

(P,Q)=\frac{1}{16}\Big(\int Q\Big)^{2}>0,\qquad(P,Q^{\prime})=0.

Remark III.1
.

By
\lim_{+\infty}P=0
and since
P^{\prime}\in\mathscr{Y}
, it holds

(42)

|P(y)|\lesssim e^{-\frac{|y|}{2}},\quad y>0.

III.2
Definitions and estimates of localized profiles

We recall the definition of a one-parameter family of approximate self-similar profiles
b\mapsto Q_{b}
with
|b|\ll 1
which will provide the higher-order deformation of the ground state profile
Q_{b=0}
in the blow-up setting.

Introduce a function
\chi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that

(43)

0\leq\chi\leq 1,\quad 0\leq(\chi^{\prime\prime})^{2}\lesssim\chi^{\prime}\quad\text{on }\mathbb{R},\qquad\chi_{|(-\infty,-2)}\equiv 0\quad\text{and}\quad\chi_{|(-1,+\infty)}\equiv 1.

Definition III.1

(Localised profile)
.

Let
\gamma=\frac{3}{4}
, the localised profile
Q_{b}
is defined by

(44)

Q_{b}(y)=Q(y)+bP_{b}(y)

with

(45)

P_{b}(y)=P(y)\chi_{b}(y)\quad\text{and}\quad\chi_{b}(y)=\chi(|b|^{\gamma}y).

We state the properties of
Q_{b}
in the following lemma.

Lemma III.2

(Approximate self-similar profiles
Q_{b}
)
.

There exists
b^{*}>0
small enough such that for all
|b|<b^{*}
, the following properties hold:

1.

(Estimates on
Q_{b}
) For all
y\in\mathbb{R}
,

(46)

\begin{split}\lvert Q_{b}(y)\rvert\lesssim e^{-|y|}+|b|\Big(\mathds{1}_{[-2,0]}(|b|^{\gamma}y)+e^{-\frac{|y|}{2}}\Big),\\
\lvert Q^{(k)}_{b}(y)\rvert\lesssim e^{-|y|}+|b|e^{-\frac{|y|}{2}}+|b|^{1+k\gamma}\mathds{1}_{[-2,-1]}(|b|^{\gamma}y),\quad\forall k\geq 1.\end{split}

2.

(Equation of
Q_{b}
) Let the error term be defined by

(47)

-\Psi_{b}=\big(Q^{\prime\prime}_{b}-Q_{b}+Q^{5}_{b}\big)^{\prime}+b\Lambda Q_{b}-2b^{2}\frac{\partial Q_{b}}{\partial b}.

Then, for all
y\in\mathbb{R}
,

(48)

\lvert\Psi_{b}(y)\rvert\lesssim|b|^{1+\gamma}\mathds{1}_{[-2,-1]}(|b|^{\gamma}y)+b^{2}\Big(e^{-\frac{|y|}{2}}+\mathds{1}_{[-2,0]}(|b|^{\gamma}y)\Big),

(49)

\lvert\Psi^{(k)}_{b}(y)\rvert\lesssim|b|^{1+(k+1)\gamma}\mathds{1}_{[-2,-1]}(|b|^{\gamma}y)+b^{2}e^{-\frac{|y|}{2}},\qquad\forall k\geq 1.

Moreover,

(50)

\lvert(\Psi_{b},\phi)\rvert\lesssim b^{2},\qquad\forall\phi\in\mathscr{Y}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|\Psi^{(k)}_{b}(y)\|_{L^{2}_{B}}\lesssim C_{B}b^{2},\qquad\forall k\geq 0.

3.

(Projection of
\Psi_{b}
in the direction
Q
)

(51)

\lvert(\Psi_{b},Q)\rvert\lesssim|b|^{3}.

4.

(Mass and energy properties of
Q_{b}
)

(52)

\bigg\lvert\int Q^{2}_{b}-\Big(\int Q^{2}+2b\int PQ\Big)\bigg\rvert\lesssim|b|^{2-\gamma},

(53)

\bigg\lvert E(Q_{b})+b\int PQ\bigg\rvert\lesssim b^{2}.

Proof.

The proofs of the results above on the approximate profiles
can be found in
[
19
, Lemma 2.4]
and in
[
26
, Lemma 3.3]
.
∎

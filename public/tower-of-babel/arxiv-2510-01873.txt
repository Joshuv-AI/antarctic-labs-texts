Asplund spaces C k ​ ( X ) C_{k}(X) beyond Banach spaces
Marian Fabian, Jerzy Ka̧kol, Arkady Leiderman

Abstract
. This paper addresses the Asplund property for the space of continuous functions C k ​ ( X ) C_{k}(X) equipped with the compact-open topology, when X X is an arbitrary Tychonoff space. Motivated by inconsistent definitions in prior literature extending the Asplund property beyond Banach spaces, we provide a unified and self-contained treatment of core results in this context. A characterization of the Asplund property for C k ​ ( X ) C_{k}(X) is established, alongside a review of classical results, including the Namioka–Phelps theorem and its implications. All proofs are presented in a self-contained manner and rely on standard techniques.

Introduction

In his seminal book
[
32
]
, R.R. Phelps observed that the first infinite-dimensional result concerning the differentiability properties of convex functions was obtained in 1933 by S. Mazur
[
26
]
:
A continuous convex function
f:D\to{\mathbb{R}}
defined on an open convex subset
D
of a separable Banach space
E

at the points of a dense
G_{\delta}
subset of
D

In 1968, E. Asplund
[
1
]
extended Mazur’s theorem in two ways. Namely, he proved the same statement for a more general class of Banach spaces;
and studied a more restricted class of Banach spaces (now called Asplund spaces) in which a
stronger conclusion holds: Fréchet differentiability on a dense
G_{\delta}
subset of
D
.

Over the years, this area has attracted considerable attention of several researchers. In our recent work
[
21
]
, we noted that many contributions, especially those extending beyond the framework of Banach spaces, have not been consistently or adequately cited in the literature. This observation has motivated us to provide a more uniform account of relevant results and to present a comprehensive extension of the celebrated Namioka–Phelps theorem in the context of the spaces of continuous functions
C_{k}(X)
, now encompassing arbitrary Tychonoff spaces
X
.

In this paper all topological spaces are assumed to be Tychonoff.
They can be can be described as subspaces of the compact cubes
[0,1]^{T}
.

For a Tychonoff topological space
X
, we denote by

the vector space of all continuous real-valued functions on

, equipped with the
compact-open topology
,
whose base consists of the sets

V^{\varepsilon}_{K}:=\{f\in C_{k}(X):\ f[K]\subset(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\}

where

and
\varepsilon
’s run throughout all positive numbers; thus

becomes a locally convex space.

We use the abbreviation lcs for locally convex spaces.

Proposition 1.6
.

Let
X
be a Tychonoff topological space, let
D\subset C_{k}(X)
be a convex open set, let
\varphi:D\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a convex continuous function, and let

be given.

Then
\varphi
is Schwartz–Fréchet differentiable at the point
f
if and only if
\varphi
is Yamamuro–Fréchet differentiable at
f
.

Proof.

Put

(1)

M:=\{h\in C_{k}(X):\ h[X]\subset(-1,1)\}.

Clearly,
M
is a bounded set. Using Proposition
1.1
, we find a compact set
K\subset X
and a
\gamma>0

such that

f+V^{\gamma}_{K}\subset D\quad{\rm and}\quad\sup\varphi[f+V^{\gamma}_{K}]<\infty,

where

V^{\gamma}_{K}:=\{h\in C_{k}(X):\ h[K]\subset(-\gamma,\gamma)\};

recall that this
V^{\gamma}_{K}
is a convex open neighbourhood of
0\in C_{k}(X)
. (This
V^{\gamma}_{K}
will witness for the
Schwartz–Fréchet differentiability of
\varphi
at
f
).

As
\varphi
is Yamamuro–Fréchet differentiable at
f
, we have that

\sup_{h\in M}\big(\varphi(f+th)-\varphi(f)-\varphi^{\prime}(f)(th)\big)=o(t)\quad{\rm as}\quad t\to 0.

Hence

\sup_{h\in M}\sum\varphi(f\pm th)-2\varphi(f)=o(t)\quad{\rm as}\quad t\downarrow 0.

Now, consider any
h\in V^{\gamma}_{K}
. Then
h[K]\subset(-\gamma,\gamma)
.
Sine
X
is a Tychonoff space and
K\subset X
is compact, Proposition
1.5
yields a continuous
extension
\check{h}:X\to(-\gamma,\gamma)
such that
\check{h}|_{K}=h|_{K}
.
By the Claim below, we get that
\varphi(f+th)=\varphi(f+t\check{h})
, and therefore,

\sup_{h\in V^{\gamma}_{K}}\sum\varphi(f\pm th)-2\varphi(f)=o(t)\quad{\rm as}\quad t\downarrow 0.

We also know that
\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{1}{t}(\varphi(f+th)-\varphi(f))=\varphi^{\prime}(f)h
for every
h\in C_{k}(X)
.
Thus, using the convexity of
\varphi
, we can conclude that

\sup_{h\in V^{\gamma}_{K}}\big(\varphi(f+th)-\varphi(f)-\varphi^{\prime}(f)(th)\big)=o(t)\quad{\rm as}\quad t\to 0.

We proved that
\varphi
is Schwartz–Fréchet differentiable at the point

, where the neighborhood

is a witness for that.

It remains to formulate and prove the announced claim/observation, going back to B. Sharp
[

Claim.

If

\varphi(f)=\varphi(f^{\prime})

Proof of Claim. For all

f^{\prime}=\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda}f+\frac{1}{\lambda}(f+\lambda(f^{\prime}-f));

\displaystyle\varphi(f^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda}\varphi(f)+\frac{1}{\lambda}\varphi(f+\lambda(f^{\prime}-f))

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda}\varphi(f)+\frac{1}{\lambda}\sup\varphi[f+V_{K}^{\gamma}]\longrightarrow\varphi(f)\ \ {\rm as}\ \ \lambda\uparrow\infty;

Hence

. And, by symmetry,
\varphi(f)\leq\varphi(f^{\prime})
.
∎

2.
Namioka–Phelps theorem and additional supplementing results

Assume first that we can find points of Fréchet differentiability of convex continuous functions
defined on separable Banach spaces, that is, on those spaces
E
which possess a countable dense subset.
Can we deduce from this “separable” information that the whole
f
, defined on
E
, is somewhere
Fréchet differentiable? The answer is affirmative and this is caused by availability of a suitable “separable reduction” of
the phenomenon of Fréchet differentiability.

Having in mind the fundamental property included in Proposition
1.10
, we propose also next Proposition
2.1
.
For the concepts mentioned in the statement below we refer, for example, to
[
12
, p. 317–321]
and

[
13
, Chapter 11, p. 625–629]
. In particular, once a function
f
is concave and
\partial_{F}(x)
is nonempty, then

f
must be Fréchet differentiable at
x
, with
\partial_{F}f(x)=\{f^{\prime}(x)\}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
(E,\|\cdot\|)
be a (separable) Banach space whose dual
E^{*}
is separable,
let
D\subset E
be an open set, and and let
f:D\longrightarrow(-\infty,\infty]
be a lower semi-continuous function.

Then the set of all
x\in D
, where the Fréchet subdifferential
\partial_{F}f(x)

is nonempty, is dense in
D
.

Proof.

We first realize that
E
admits an equivalent Fréchet smooth (off the origin) norm. Indeed,
let
\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots\}
be a countable dense subset of the unit ball
S_{E}
of
E
and
\{\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\ldots\}
be a countable dense subset of the unit ball
S_{E^{*}}
of the dual
E^{*}
. Then the assignment sending
x^{*}\in E^{*}
to

|x^{*}|=\sqrt{\|x^{*}\|^{2}+\hbox{$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{-n}$}\langle x^{*},x_{n}\rangle^{2}+\hbox{$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{-n}$}{\rm dist}\,(x^{*},{\rm sp}\{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}\}\big)^{2}}

is an equivalent weak
∗
lower semi-continuous norm on
E^{*}
. For the reader’s convenience we indicate why
|\cdot|
is
subadditive, i.e., the inequality
|x^{*}+y^{*}|\leq|x^{*}|+|y^{*}|
holds. It is not easy to find in the literature how to prove this.
A possible argument may profit from the subadditivity of the famous (Euclidean)
\ell_{2}
-norm.

A ‘small’ Kadec–Klee like effort, see
[
6
, p. 113-117]
, reveals that
the norm
|\cdot|
is locally uniformly rotund, i.e. if
x^{*},x^{*}_{1},\ x^{*}_{2},\ldots
are elements of
E^{*}
such that

2|x^{*}|^{2}+2|x_{i}^{*}|^{2}-|x^{*}+x^{*}_{i}|^{2}\to 0
when
i\to\infty
, then
|x^{*}_{i}-x^{*}|\to 0
when
i\to\infty
.
Assume this is not so. Find then an
\varepsilon>0
and an infinite set
N\subset{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\|x^{*}_{i}-x^{*}\|>3\varepsilon

for every
i\in N
.
Find
m\in N
so big that dist
(x^{*},{\rm sp}\{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{m}\}\big)<\varepsilon
.
A simple convexity argument provides an infinite set
N_{1}\subset N
such that
dist
(x^{*}_{i},{\rm sp}\{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{m}\}\big)<\varepsilon
for every
i\in N_{1}
. For every such
i
find

y^{*}_{i}\in{\rm sp}\{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{m}\}
such that
\|x^{*}_{i}-y^{*}_{i}\|<\varepsilon
, and so,

\|y^{*}_{i}-x^{*}\|\geq\|x^{*}_{i}-x^{*}\|-\|x^{*}_{i}-y^{*}_{i}\|>3\varepsilon-\varepsilon=2\varepsilon.

The sequence
(y^{*}_{i})_{i\in N_{1}}
is bounded
because
\|x^{*}_{i}\|\to\|x^{*}\|
as
i\to\infty
, by convexity. Further, since it lies in a finite-dimensional space, there are
y^{*}\in{\rm sp}\{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{m}\}
and an infinite set

N_{2}\subset N_{1}
such that
\|y^{*}_{i}-y^{*}\|\to 0
as
N_{2}\ni i\to\infty
.
Hence
\|y^{*}-x^{*}\|\geq 2\varepsilon.
Find
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
so that
\|\langle y^{*}-x^{*},x_{n}\rangle\|>\varepsilon
.
Another simple convexity argument yields that
\langle x^{*}_{i},x_{n}\rangle\to\langle x^{*},x_{n}\rangle
as
i\to\infty
.
Hence we have that

(\varepsilon>)\ \|y^{*}_{i}-x^{*}_{i}\|\geq\langle y^{*}_{i}-x^{*}_{i},x_{n}\rangle>\varepsilon

for all
i\in N_{2}
big enough, a contradiction. We proved that our norm
|\cdot|
on
E^{*}
is LUR.

Now, having proved that our norm
|\cdot|
on
E^{*}
is LUR, the Šmulyan test guarantees that the predual norm
|\cdot|
on
E
, defined by

E\ni x\longmapsto\sup\big\{\langle x^{*},x\rangle:\ x^{*}\in E^{*},\ |x^{*}|\leq 1\big\}=:|x|,

is Fréchet differentiable at every nonzero point of
E
. For more details, see
[
5
, p. 43]
.

Next, let
\overline{x}\in Df
and
\varepsilon>0
be given. We will find a
v\in E
such that

|v-\overline{x}|<\varepsilon
and
\partial_{F}f(v)
is nonempty.
From the lower semi-continuity of
f
find
\varepsilon^{\prime}\in(0,\varepsilon)
so small that
B(\overline{x},\varepsilon^{\prime})\subset D

and that
f
is bounded below on the open ball

B(\overline{x},\varepsilon^{\prime})
. Define

\displaystyle\varphi(x):=\begin{cases}\big(\tan(\hbox{$\frac{\pi}{2\varepsilon^{\prime}}$}|x-\overline{x}|)\big)^{2}&\text{if $x\in B(\overline{x},\varepsilon^{\prime})$}\\
\infty&\text{if $x\in E\setminus B(\overline{x},\varepsilon^{\prime})$}.\end{cases}

Then
\varphi:E\longrightarrow[0,\infty]
is easily seen to be proper and lower semi-continuous.
Now, the Borwein–Preiss variational principle
[
32
, Theorem 4.20]
provides a Fréchet differentiable function

\theta:D\longrightarrow[0,\infty)
such that the sum
f+\varphi+\theta
attains infimum at some
v\in D
; clearly,

v\in B(\overline{x},\varepsilon^{\prime})
. We thus have

f(v+h)+\varphi(v+h)+\theta(v+h)\geq f(v)+\varphi(v)+\theta(v)\quad{\rm for\ every}\quad h\in E,

and so

f(v+h)-f(v)+\langle\varphi^{\prime}(v)+\theta^{\prime}(v),h\rangle\geq-o(|h|)\quad{\rm as}\ \ h\in E\ \ {\rm and}\ \ |h|\to 0.

We proved that
-\varphi^{\prime}(v)-\theta^{\prime}(v)\in\partial_{F}f(v)
, and hence
\partial_{F}f(v)\neq\emptyset
.
∎

Remark 2.2

Now a main question arises: Is it possible to

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
(E,\|\cdot\|)
be a non-separable Banach space,

be a convex continuous function
defined on an open convex set
D\subset E
, and
Z
be a separable subspace of

.

Then there exists a separable subspace
Y
of
E
, containing
Z
, with

nonempty, and such that, if

of

to
Y
is Fréchet differentiable at some

, then
f
is
Fréchet differentiable at
x
.

Proof.

First, we need a translation
of Fréchet differentiability (of convex functions) to the terms of the space
E
:

f
is Fréchet differentiable at
x\in E
if and only if

S(x,t):=\sup_{h\in B_{X}}\big(f(x+th)+f(x-th)\big)=2f(x)+o(t)\ \ {\rm as}\ \ \ Q_{+}\ni t\downarrow 0;

this is easy to check. (Here and below,
Q_{+}
means the set of all positive rational numbers). For any
x\in E
and any
t>0
, if
S(x,t)<\infty
, we find a vector
u(x,t)\in B_{E}
such that

(2)

\displaystyle f\big(x+t\,u(x,t)\big)+f\big(x-t\,u(x,t)\big)>S(x,t)-t^{2}.

(This
u(x,t)
is almost “the worst possible” regarding the
Fréchet differentiability of
f
at
x
).
Let
C_{0}
be a countable dense subset of
Z
. We construct countable sets

C_{0}\subset C_{1}\subset C_{2}\subset\cdots\subset E
as follows. Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
be given and assume
that
C_{m-1}
was already found. Find a countable set
C_{m}
in
E
such that
it is stable under making all finite linear combinations with rational coefficients, and that
it contains
C_{m-1}
as well as the set
\big\{u(x,t):\ x\in C_{m-1},\ t\in Q_{+}\}
;
clearly,
C_{m}
is again countable.
Do so for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and put finally
Y:=\overline{C_{1}\cup C_{2}\cup\cdots}\,
.
Clearly,
Y\supset Z
.

We claim that this
Y
has the desired property. So, assume that

f|_{Y}
is Fréchet differentiable at some
x\in Y
. We show that the whole
f
is
Fréchet differentiable at
x
as well. (If
x\in C_{1}\cup C_{2}\cup\cdots

4.
\Delta_{1}
-spaces
X
and the Asplund property for spaces
C_{k}(X)

The aim of this section is to sketch briefly a proof of Theorem
4.1
which originally appeared in a bit stronger form as
[
19
, Theorem 3.2]
.
The class of
\Delta_{1}
-spaces introduced and explored in
[
19
]
plays here a key role.
For any
\Delta_{1}
-space
X
, each compact subset of
X
is scattered, and hence
C_{k}(X)
is an Asplund lcs.
Similarly, we obtain another equivalent topological description of a compact space
X
such that the Banach space
C(X)
is Asplund.

Let
X
be a topological space. The following well-known topological notions generalize compactness.

•

X
is called
\omega
-bounded
if the closure of every countable subset in
X
is compact.

•

X
is called
countably compact
if every countably infinite subset of
X
has a limit point.

•

X
is called
pseudocompact
if every continuous real-valued function on
X
is bounded.

Clearly,
X
is
\omega
-bounded implies that
X
is countably compact, and the latter implies pseudocompactness of
X
.
For the various examples distinguishing these and some other classes of topological spaces the reader is advised to look
[
19
]
.
Here we mention only that a very concrete example of a locally compact non-compact space which is
\omega
-bounded and scattered,
is the interval of countable ordinals
[0,\omega_{1})
with the order topology.
The Tychonoff plank
X=[0,\omega_{1}]\times[0,\omega]\setminus\{(\omega_{1},\omega)\}
is a pseudocompact scattered space which is not
\omega
-bounded.

Theorem 4.1
.

[
19
]

For a Tychonoff
\omega
-bounded space
X
the following assertions are equivalent:

(1)

X
is scattered.

(2)

Every compact subset of
X
is scattered.

(3)

X
is a
\Delta_{1}
-space.

(4)

C_{k}(X)
is an Asplund space.

Definition 4.2
.

[
18
]
,
[
20
]

A topological space
X
is called a
\Delta
-space (
\Delta_{1}
-space) if for every decreasing sequence
(D_{n})_{n\in\omega}

of (countable, respectively) subsets of
X
with
\bigcap_{n\in\omega}D_{n}=\emptyset
, there is a decreasing sequence of open sets
(V_{n})_{n\in\omega}
such that
D_{n}\subset V_{n}

for each
n\in\omega
,
and again with
\bigcap_{n\in\omega}V_{n}=\emptyset
.

The original definition of a
\Delta
-set of the real line
{\mathbb{R}}
is due to M. Reed and E. van Douwen, see
[
33
]
.
The
\Delta
- and
\Delta_{1}
-properties turned out to have nice applications in the theory of function spaces
C(X)
endowed with the topology
of pointwise convergence, see
[
18
]
,
[
20
]
.

Another motivation for studying the
\Delta_{1}
-property is provided by the fact that it extends the classical
notion of
\lambda
-sets. Recall that
X\subset{\mathbb{R}}
is called a
\lambda
-set if every countable
A\subset X
is a
G_{\delta}
-subset of
X
and,
more generally, a topological space
X
is a
\lambda
-space if every countable subset
A\subset X
is a
G_{\delta}
. The study
of
\lambda
-sets dates back to 1933 when K. Kuratowski proved in ZFC (see
[
23
, Chapter 3, §40.III]
) that there exist uncountable
\lambda
-sets.
Since every
\lambda
-space has the
\Delta_{1}
-property, the notion of a
\Delta_{1}
-space determines a wider class than the class
of
\lambda
-spaces. It is also worth mentioning that a metrizable space has the
\Delta_{1}
-property if and only if it is a

\lambda
-space according to
[

, Theorem 2.19]
.

First we present a proof of the equivalence (1) and (2) in Theorem

Lemma 4.3
.

Let

be a Tychonoff
\omega
-bounded space. Then every compact subset of

is scattered
if and only if
X
is scattered.

Proof.

Assume on the contrary that each compact subset in
X
is scattered but

is not scattered. Since the space

is pseudocompact, we can apply
[
25
, Proposition 5.5]
to get in

a closed subset

for which there exists a continuous surjection

Let
Q
be a dense countable subset of
[0,1]
. Choose a countable subset

such that
h(C)=Q
.
Let
A:=\overline{C}
be the closure in

is also
\omega
-bounded. Therefore,

is compact and obviously

.
By assumption, the compact set

is scattered, this is a contradiction, in view of Theorem

. Hence

is scattered. The converse is clear.
∎

5.
Final remarks and illustrating examples

It is well known (but not so easy to prove) that every closed vector subspace of any Asplund Banach space is again Asplund, see for example
[
32
]
,
[
29
, Proposition 2.33]
. We show however
that this property fails in general lcs, see
[
16
]
.
First recall that
\varphi
denotes an
\aleph_{0}
-dimensional vector space endowed with the finest locally convex topology, say
\xi_{\infty}
, that is, the topology, where
a base of the origin consists of all absolutely convex absorbing sets. Then, clearly, every linear functional on
\varphi
is continuous.

Every bounded set
B
in
\varphi
is finite-dimensional, i.e. the linear span of
B
is finite-dimensional, because otherwise (applying the Hahn–Banach theorem) one can construct a linear discontinuous functional on
\varphi
. On the other hand,
\varphi
is the strict countable inductive limit of finite-dimensional Banach vector subspaces
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
Indeed, consider on
\varphi
the locally convex inductive limit topology
\xi
generated by finite-dimensional linear subspaces, see
[
17
, Chapter 2.4]
or
[
31
, 0.3.1]
.
Then, since the original topology
\xi_{\infty}
of
\varphi
is the finest locally convex topology on
\varphi
, the both topologies
\xi
and
\xi_{\infty}
coincide. Moreover, the topology

\xi=\xi_{\infty}
is complete, see
[
31
, Proposition 8.4.16 (iv)]
, or
[
39
, Proposition 1]
for a direct proof.

B. Sharp
[
36
]
observed that the space
\varphi
is not GDS.
To show this, consider the
l_{1}
norm
\|\cdot\|_{1}
on it. This is a convex function, continuous with respect to the original topology
\xi_{\infty}
on
\varphi
.
And it is easy to check that
\|\cdot\|_{1}
is nowhere Gâteaux differentiable on
\varphi
[32, Example 1.4. (b)].
Indeed, given any
x\in\varphi
,
there is an
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that the
n
-th coordinate of
x
is 0.
Let
e_{n}:=(0,0,\dots,1,0,\dots)
be the sequence whose only nonzero term is equal to 1 on the
n
-th place.
Then

\lim_{t\downarrow 0}(\|x+te_{n}\|_{1}-\|x\|_{1})/t=1\quad{\rm and}\quad\lim_{t\uparrow 0}(\|x+te_{n}\|_{1}-\|x\|_{1})/t=-1,

and hence,
\|\cdot\|_{1}
is not Gâteaux differentiable at the point
x
.

Remark 5.1
.

Note that from the description
\varphi
as the the strict countable inductive limit of finite-dimensional linear subspaces
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
it follows that the lcs
\varphi
can be identified with the free locally convex space
L({\mathbb{N}})
. It has been shown in
[
16
]
that the free lcs

is not GDS for every infinite Tychonoff space

.

We complete this section with the following interesting example mentioned in
[
16
]
. Below
Q
denotes the set of all rational numbers with the topology induced from
{\mathbb{R}}
.
So,
Q
is a metric countable space.

Proposition 5.2
.

[
36
]
,
[
16
]

The space
C_{k}(Q)
over the rationals

is an Asplund space but contains isomorphically a closed copy of the non-Asplund space
\varphi
.

Proof.

Every compact subset of
Q
is countable, hence it is scattered. By Theorem
3.2
we deduce that
C_{k}(Q)
is an Asplund space. Since
Q
is a
\mu
-space, the space
C_{k}(Q)
is barrelled by the Nachbin–Shirota theorem, see
[
17
, Propostion 2.4.16]
, i.e., every closed absolutely convex absorbing subset of
C_{k}(Q)
is a neighbourhood of zero.

On the other hand, the space
C_{k}(Q)
is not Baire-like, i.e., there exists an increasing sequence of closed convex absorbing subsets
A_{1},A_{2},\ldots
covering the space
C_{k}(Q)
such that no
A_{n}
is a neighbourhood of the origin in
C_{k}(Q)
. Indeed, assume on the contrary that
C_{k}(Q)
is a Baire-like space. Then we follow the argument contained in the proof of
[
17
, Proposition 2.4.20]
,
or
[
27
, Corollary 5.3.4]
: For

we put

U_{n}:=(-1/n,1/n)\cap Q\quad{\rm and}\quad H_{n}:=\big\{f\in C(Q):\ f[U_{n}]\subset[-n,n]\big\}.

Clearly, the
U_{n}
’s form a basis of neighbourhoods of
0
in
Q
and the (closed convex and absorbing) sets
H_{n}
’s cover all of
C_{k}(Q)
.
Also, clearly,
U_{1}\supset U_{2}\supset\cdots
and
H_{1}\subset H_{2}\subset\cdots
.

Since we assumed that
C_{k}(Q)
is Baire-like, there exist a compact
set
K\subset Q
,
\varepsilon>0
, and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

\{f\in C(X):\ f[K]\subset(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\}\subset H_{n}.

Note that
U_{n}\subset K
. Indeed, if there is a

, then putting

f(x):=2n\frac{{\rm dist}(x,K)}{{\rm dist}(z,K)},\quad x\in Q,

we have that
f\in C_{k}(Q)
and that
f(z)=2n
. Also
f_{|K}\equiv 0
, and thus
f\in U_{n}
, which is impossible.
Therefore, we have that
U_{n}\subset K
. This leads to a contradiction because
K
is a countable compact subset of
{\mathbb{R}}
and the closure of
U_{n}
in
{\mathbb{R}}
is an uncountable closed interval.
We have proved that the space is
C_{k}(Q)
is not Baire-like.
Finally, since
C_{k}(Q)
is barrelled but not Baire-like, we apply the Saxon’s result (see
[
17

), and conclude that lcs

contains isomorphically a (closed) copy of
\varphi
.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

The locally convex space
\varphi
is nonmetrizable, and it is complete as the strict countable inductive limit of finite-dimensional spaces.
From another hand, its strong dual
\varphi^{*}
, endowed with the uniform convergence topology on bounded sets from
\varphi
is isomorphic to the countable power
{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\omega}
,
hence the strong dual
\varphi^{*}
is separable.
However,
\varphi
is not an Asplund space.

This shows that the Asplund property for lcs differs dramatically from the Asplund property in the class of Banach spaces.

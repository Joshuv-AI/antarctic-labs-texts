Academic stress in high school students during the transition from online to offline exams: A retrospective study from post-COVID-19 era
Authors: Daisy Rure, Nisha Kaithwas, Rajesh Singh, Makhan Shakya
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Background:Stress is a common phenomenon. Everyone faces stress, albeit of different intensity and due to different reasons. Stress in students can be due to many reasons—non-academic and academic. Academic stress is a major deciding factor in the fluctuation in academic achievements. It has also been implicated in mental illnesses like depression, anxiety, sleep disorders, and even suicide, and physical illnesses like hypertension, obesity, etc., Additionally, post-COVID-19 pandemic in 2020, there have been adverse effects on the education sector due to the offline–online mode of classes.Aim:To assess academic stress in high school students, to compare stress between male and female students, and to find the correlation of academic stress with the 10th standard's result, i.e. percentage.Material and Methods:A retrospective study was designed by the Department of Psychiatry, of a medical college of Madhya Pradesh in schools of Khandwa and nearby areas from June 2022 to August 2022. A total of 162 high school students were selected as per inclusion and exclusion criteria. Perception of Academic Stress (PAS) scale and Scale for Assessing Academic Stress (SAAS) were used to assess academic stress. Statistical analysis was done by SPSS version 25 using the Student t-test and Pearson correlation tests.Results:The mean age of the study participants was 15.82 ± 0.81 years. There was a significant difference in the mean percentage of the 9th and 10th standard. Both genders differed significantly in class percentages, but not the mean SAAS score. Most students had moderate stress. Only the cognitive indicator of SAAS was found to be significantly correlated with 10th standard percentages.Conclusion:Academic stress is ubiquitous. External factors like the transition from online to offline exams in addition to anticipation of the exams themselves can add to the stress. Prompt identification of stress can alleviate the emergence of future psychiatric illnesses.

Stress is a common phenomenon. As defined by Morgan and King, stress is an internal state that can be caused by physical demands on the body (disease conditions, exercise, extremes of temperature, and the like) or by environmental and social situations (called stressors) which are evaluated as potentially harmful, uncontrollable, or exceeding our resources for coping.[1] Everyone faces stress, albeit of different intensity and due to different reasons. Stress in students can be due to many reasons—non-academic and academic. Non-academic causes may be diseases, poor social situations, extreme environmental conditions, family problems, difficulty in adaptation, self-image perceptions, etc.[2] Academic problems may include exam stress, inability to understand the subject, attending numerous classes, etc. Academic stress refers to hardships that arise from the numerous demands placed on students in the form of exams, maintaining academic lives, competing with peers, meeting the academic expectations of teachers and parents, and one's academic expectations.[3] It has been described as an amalgamation of individual perceptions of academic frustration, conflict, pressure, and anxiety.[345] Academic stress is a major deciding factor in the fluctuation in academic achievements. It has also been implicated in mental illnesses like depression, anxiety, sleep disorders, and even suicide and physical illnesses like hypertension, obesity, etc.[4678910] There are various factors influencing academic stress. It has been reported to be influenced by the school (private or government),[1112] everyday school stressors (marks, tests, achievements, presentations, competitions, interaction with teachers, fear of falling behind, workload),[2413] and work and life outside school[2] like dating,[4] various coping mechanisms,[1314] peer pressure,[381516] parental pressure,[341516] etc. Academic stress is a major indicator of the mental health of students.[17] Additionally, due to the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020, there has been a negative impact on the mental and psychological health of individuals,[1618] and all sectors of society including the education sector due to the shift to online mode of education to increase reach and meet the education needs of all students and teachers.[1619] There was an adverse effect of the lockdown on the mental health of adolescents.[1620] Furthermore, for the past few years, there has been upheaval in the studies of students due to the online mode of exams. In 2020, most of the students were given general promotion, and in 2021, there were exams in online mode. However, since 2022, re-implementation of traditional exams, i.e. offline exams, has been adopted by most academic institutions. Thus, this study was conducted on high school students who took offline exams in 2022 to assess academic stress, to compare stress between male and female students, and to find the correlation of academic stress with the 10th standard's result, i.e., percentage.

MATERIAL AND METHODS

This retrospective study was designed by the Department of Psychiatry, of a government medical college of Madhya Pradesh. The study was conducted in schools of Khandwa and nearby areas, over three months from June 2022 to August 2022 after permission from Institutional Ethics Committee. Since the study was conducted in schools, the study participants were minor and consent was obtained from principals and teachers of respective schools. After obtaining the consent, students who gave offline high school exams in 2022 were included in the study. Those with a lack of consent and students other than those in high school were excluded from the study. Sample collection was done by purposive sampling, and 162 participants were enrolled in the study. Data collection was done in a semi-structured pro forma. Perception of Academic Stress (PAS) scale and Scale for Assessing Academic Stress (SAAS) were used to assess academic stress. PAS scale is an 18-item self-reporting Likert scale (1 for strongly disagree to 5 for strongly agree), the higher the score, the higher the stress; the scale can be divided into four factors: Pressures to perform; Perceptions of workload; Academic self-perceptions, and Time restraints.[315] SAAS[21] is a 30-item self-reporting scale (yes or no), higher the score, the higher the stress; it gives Cognitive; Affective; Physical; Social; and Motivational indicators.[2223] The mean score of SAAS was used as a cut-off for preliminary analysis.[10] Additionally, SAAS score was divided into quartiles, and academic stress was categorized as low stress (score less than 1st quartile), moderate stress (score up to 3rd quartile), and high stress (score more than 3rd quartile).

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS) version 25.0 (IBM Corp. Armonk, NY). Data were presented as means and percentages. Student's t-test was used to compare means between two groups with a two-tailed P value <0.05 as the level of significance. Pearson's correlation was used to analyze any association between academic stress and the 10th standard percentage.

DISCUSSION

Our study was distinct as most of the studies were conducted before the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic. During and after the COVID-19 pandemic, many students faced mental and psychological issues; very few studies could be found during the literature review. In our study, there was nearly equal participation of male (n = 87) and female (n = 75) students, which is comparable to studies from India[16] and Nepal.[24] The mean age of our study participants was 15.82 ± 0.81 years, which is appropriate and anticipated as per the high school students' age range in India.[121316]

The majority of participants (62.3%) belonged to urban areas and 69.8% attended private schools. This could be due to the reasons for collecting data from schools located mainly at the district level and more willingness of teachers and students from private schools. There was a significant difference between the mean age of both genders and their academic performance.

In our study, nearly one-third (30.9%) of participants had a high level of academic stress on SAAS, and nearly half (47.5%) had moderate stress. This corresponded with a previous study which had a similar distribution of levels of stress.[25] Half of our participants (50.6%) had SAAS scores more than the mean, similar to the findings of Reddy et al.[10] who also reported that 48.80% of students with average to high stress levels. Female students had higher mean SAAS scores; however, there was no significant difference between both genders, similar to the findings of a previous study.[11] This was contrary to the findings of Malhotra possibly due to their study sample from senior secondary school students.[24]

In the PAS scale, there was a significant difference in “Perceptions of workload” and “Time restraints” between both genders. This shows gender differences in academic stress, also reported by other researchers.[231016] This may be because male students are sensitive and sincere about their future, and as in our patriarchal society, male members are supposed to be the breadwinner of the family. So they have to bear all the financial responsibility. Female students are generally easygoing and happy and do not have academic stress because they are not in compulsion to provide financial support to their family. In our study, the mean PAS scale score was close to the upper limit of the Likert scale. This can be interpreted as the majority of students strongly agreeing with various factors of the PAS scale. In the present study, we did not find any significant correlation between academic performance with indicators of SAAS and factors of the PAS scale, except for the cognitive indicator of SAAS. This was a contrary finding and could indicate response bias or social desirability bias of our participants.

Limitations

Our study had certain limitations such as recall bias due to its retrospective nature, leading to the study sample being limited to a particular geographical area; hence, findings cannot be generalized.

CONCLUSION

Academic stress is ubiquitous and can only be managed rather than eliminated. Positive outcomes such as motivation, concentration, and better performance are expected when low or mild stress is present. However, when moderate or high, it threatens the very purpose and can lead to various mental health issues. More than two-thirds of adolescents with moderate-to-high academic stress as found in our study are very alarming. These adolescents need to be identified at the earliest, and interventions must be done at the individual, family, community, educational institution, and administration level.

Rudyak’s conjecture for lower dimensional 1-connected manifolds
Alexander Dranishnikov, Deep Kundu

Abstract
. Rudyak’s conjecture states that for any degree one map f : M → N f:M\to N between oriented closed manifolds there is the inequality cat LS ⁡ ( M ) ≥ cat LS ⁡ ( N ) \cat(M)\geq\cat(N) for the Lusternik-Shnirelmann category. We prove the Rudyak’s conjecture for n n -dimensional simply connected manifolds for n ≤ 8 n\leq 8 .

1.
Introduction

The reduced
Lusternik-Schnirelmann category

(briefly LS-category)
\cat X
of a topological space
X
is the
minimal number
n
such that there is an open cover
\{U_{0},\dots,U_{n}\}
of
X
by
n+1
contractible in
X
sets. We note that the LS-category
is a homotopy invariant.
Most of deep applications of the LS-category are based on
the classical Lusternik and Schnirelmann theorem

[
2
]
which states that
\cat M+1
gives a low bound
for the number of critical points on a manifold
M
of any smooth not
necessarily Morse function. The concept of the LS-category and the above theorem were brought by Lusternik and Schnirelmann
to solve the Poincare problem on the existence of three closed geodesics on a 2-sphere for any Riemannian metric on it
[
13
]
,
[
14
]
.
In modern time Yu. Rudyak applied the LS-category in his approach to the Arnold conjecture on the number of fixed points of symplectomorphisms
[
18
]
.

The LS-category is a numerical homotopy invariant which is difficult to compute. Clearly
\cat(S^{n})=1
. The converse is also true in view of
the classical results by Smale, Freedman, and Perelman: If
\cat(M)=1
then
M
is homeomorphic to a sphere.
It was proven in
[
5
]
that if
\cat(M)=2
, then the fundamental group of
M
is free.

Moreover many natural conjectures on the LS-category turned out to be false.
Perhaps the most famous was the Ganea Conjecture of the 60s (see
[
7
]
) which states that
\cat(M\times S^{n})=\cat M+1
for a closed manifold
M
.
On the break of the 20th century
N. Iwase constructed two simply-connected 16-dimensional counter-examples
M_{2}
and
M_{3}
to the Ganea Conjecture. His examples are quite delicate and they are based on some phenomena for primary 2-components and primary 3-components in the unstable homotopy groups of spheres.

In the late 90s Rudyak proposed the following
[
17
]
:

Conjecture 1.1
.

For a degree one map
f:M\to N
between closed orientable manifolds,
\cat(M)\geq\cat(N)
.

In
[
17
]
Rudyak proved his conjecture with some restrictions on dimension, connectivity and the LS-category of the domain.
It turned out that his conjecture is quite difficult even in the case of the most simple maps of degree one
f:M\#N\to N
, the collapsing of a summand in the connected sum
M\#N
.
That special case of Rudyak’s conjecture was settled in
[
6
]
where we proved the formula

\cat(M\#N)=\max\{\cat(M),\cat(N)\}.

Perhaps the most interesting and still a challenging case of Rudyak’s conjecture is the case of birational morphism
f:M^{\prime}\to M
between nonsingular projective varieties.
We note when
M^{\prime}
is a blowup of
M
at a point the Rudyak’s conjecture for
f
follows from the above formula.

In
[
4
]
there was an attempt to construct a counterexample to the Rudyak conjecture by means of Iwase’s manifolds.
Using Iwase’s examples the first author
constructed a map of degree one
f:M\to N
between 32-dimensional simply-connected manifolds which would be a counter-example to Rudyak’s conjecture if
\cat(M_{2}\times M_{3})\geq 5
.
We recall that
\cat(M_{2})=\cat(M_{3})=3
. The classical inequality
[
2
]

\cat(X\times Y)\leq\cat(X)+\cat(Y)

and computations in
[
4
]
imply that
4\leq\cat(M_{2}\times M_{3})\leq 6
.

In any case we believe in Rudyak’s conjecture for lower dimensional manifolds. In
[
19
]
Rudyak verified his conjecture for manifolds of dimension
\leq 4
.
In this paper we prove Rudyak’s conjecture for simply connected manifolds of dimension
\leq 8
(Theorem
5.6
).

Preliminaries

2.1.
Properties of maps of degree one

The following is well-known.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
f\colon M\to N
be a map between closed connected orientable
n
-manifolds of degree one. Then the map
f_{*}\colon H_{*}(M;R)\to H_{*}(N;R)
is a
split epimorphism and

f^{*}\colon H^{*}(N;R)\to H^{*}(M;R)
is a split monomorphism for any coefficient ring.

Proof.

The spitting map for
f_{*}:H_{*}(M;R)\to H_{*}(N;R)
is the composition

H_{*}(N;R)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle PD}}{{\to}}H^{n-*}(N;R)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f^{*}}}{{\to}}H^{n-*}(M;R)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle PD}}{{\to}}H_{*}(M;R)

and for

f^{*}:H^{*}(N;R)\to H^{*}(M;R)
is the composition

H^{*}(M;R)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle PD}}{{\to}}H_{n-*}(M;R)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{*}}}{{\to}}H_{n-*}(N;R)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle PD}}{{\to}}H^{*}(N;R).

∎

2.2.
Whitehead Theorem

For nice spaces, such as CW complexes,
it is an easy observation that
\cat X\leq\dim X
.
D.P. Grossmann
[
8
]
and independently G.W.
Whitehead
[
21
]
,
[
2
]
proved that for simply connected CW
complexes
\cat X\leq\dim X/2
. Moreover, for
k
-connected CW complexes

\cat X\leq\dim X/(k+1).

We refer to this statement as to Whitehead’s Theorem
[
2
]
.

2.3.
Ganea’s approach to the LS-category

Recall that an element of an iterated join
X_{0}*X_{1}*\cdots*X_{n}
of topological spaces is a formal linear combination
t_{0}x_{0}+\cdots+t_{n}x_{n}
of points
x_{i}\in X_{i}
with
\sum t_{i}=1
,
t_{i}\geq 0
, in which all terms of the form
0x_{i}
are dropped. Given fibrations
f_{i}\colon X_{i}\to Y
for
i=0,...,n
, the fiberwise join of spaces
X_{0},...,X_{n}
is defined to be the space

X_{0}*_{Y}\cdots*_{Y}X_{n}=\{\ t_{0}x_{0}+\cdots+t_{n}x_{n}\in X_{0}*\cdots*X_{n}\ |\ f_{0}(x_{0})=\cdots=f_{n}(x_{n})\ \}

and the fiberwise join of fibrations
f_{0},...,f_{n}
is the fibration

f_{0}*_{Y}*\cdots*_{Y}f_{n}\colon X_{0}*_{Y}X_{1}*_{Y}\cdots*_{Y}X_{n}\longrightarrow Y

defined by taking a point
t_{0}x_{0}+\cdots+t_{n}x_{n}
to
f_{i}(x_{i})
for any
i
with
t_{i}>0
. As the name ‘fiberwise join’ suggests, the fiber of the fiberwise join of fibrations is given by the join of fibers of fibrations.

When
X_{i}=X
and
f_{i}=f:X\to Y
for all
i
the fiberwise join of spaces is denoted by
*^{n+1}_{Y}X
and the fiberwise join of fibrations is denoted by
*_{Y}^{n+1}f
.

For a topological space
X
, we turn an inclusion of a point
*\to X
into a fibration
p_{0}^{X}:G_{0}(X)\to X
. The
n
-th Ganea space of
X
is defined to be the space
G_{n}(X)=*_{X}^{n+1}G_{0}(X)
, while the
n
-th Ganea fibration
p_{n}^{X}:G^{n}_{X}\to X
is the fiberwise join of fibrations
p_{0}^{X}:G_{0}(X)\to X
.

The following theorem was proven by Schwartz in a more general form
[
20
]
:

Theorem 2.2
.

The inequality
\cat(X)\leq n
holds if and only if the fibration
p^{X}_{n}:G_{n}(X)\to X
admits a section.

We note that the fiber of
p_{0}^{X}
is homotopy equivalent to the loop space
\Omega(X)
. Thus, the fiber of
p_{n}^{X}
is homotopy equivalent to the join product
\ast^{n+1}\Omega(X)
.

We call a map
f:X\to Y
a
k
-equivalence if it induces isomorphism of homotopy groups in dimension
<k
and an epimorphism in dimension
k
.

The following Proposition was proven in
[
5
]
and in
[
6
]
:

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be an
(n-2)
-equivalence of
(r-1)
-connected pointed CW-complexes with
r\geq 0
. Then the map
*^{s+1}f
is a
(sr+s+n-2)
-equivalence.

3.
Reduction to the 2 vs 3 case

All manifolds in the paper are considered to be smooth.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
N
be a
n
-dimensional simply-connected manifold,
n\leq 5
. Let
f:M\to N
be a map of degree 1. Then
\cat(M)\geq\cat(N)
.

Proof.

By the Whitehead theorem
\cat N\leq 2
. If
\cat(M)=1
then
M
is a homotopy sphere. By Proposition
2.1

N
is a
homology sphere. Since
N
is simply connected, it is a homotopy sphere. Hence
\cat(N)=1
. So, always
\cat(M)\geq\cat(N)
.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
f:M\to N
be a map of degree one between simply connected manifolds of dimension
\leq 8
. Suppose that
\cat M\geq 3
. Then
\cat M\geq\cat N
.

Proof.

By Whitehead’s theorem the LS-category of simply connected manifolds of dimension
\leq 7
does not exceed 3. So, we have to consider only the case of
n=8
where
\cat M,\cat N\leq 4
.
Assume that
\cat M=3
and
\cat N=4
.
Then there is no section of the Ganea fibration
p_{3}:G_{3}(N)\to N
. The fiber
F
of this fibration is homotopy equivalent to a 6-connected space
\ast^{4}\Omega(N)
.
The primary obstruction
\kappa\in H^{8}(N;\pi_{7}(F))
to a section of
p_{3}
is the only obstruction. Since the primary obstruction is natural,
f^{*}(\kappa)
is the primary
obstruction to the lift of

with respect to
p_{3}
. By Proposition
2.1

f^{*}(\kappa)\neq 0
. On the other hand, the inequality
\cat M\leq 3
implies that such a lift does exist.
Hence the obstruction is zero,
f^{*}(\kappa)=0
, which brings a contradiction.
∎

4.
Non-spin case

Theorem 4.1
.

Every non-spin
n
-manifold
M
,
n\geq 7
, has
\cat M\geq 3
.

Every non-orientable
n
-manifold
M
,
n\geq 3
, has
\cat M\geq 3
.

Proof.

Let
\nu:M\to BSO
be a map classifying the stable normal bundle on
M
. Suppose that
\cat M\leq 2
.
We note that the kernel

K=\ker\{\pi_{2}(M)\to\pi_{2}(BSO)=\mathbb{Z}_{2}\}

is finitely generated and every 2-sphere
S\subset M
representing an element of
K
has trivial
normal bundle.
We perform the 2-surgery on
M
to kill
K
and obtain a new manifold
M^{\prime}
such that its classifying map
\nu^{\prime}:M^{\prime}\to BSO
is a 3-equivalence.
By Proposition
3.3

\cat M^{\prime}\leq 2
.

Next we do the 3-surgery on
M^{\prime}
to kill
\pi_{3}(M^{\prime})
. We can do it, since
\dim M^{\prime}\geq 7
and
\pi_{3}(BSO)=0
.
As the result we obtain a manifold
M^{\prime\prime}
whose classifying map
\nu^{\prime\prime}:M^{\prime\prime}\to BSO
is a 4-equivalence.
By Proposition
3.3

\cat M^{\prime\prime}\leq 2
.
Since
\nu^{\prime\prime}
is a 4-equivalence, there is a homotopy lift
s:BSO^{(4)}\to M^{\prime\prime}
, i.e., such a map
s
that the composition
\nu^{\prime\prime}s
is homotopic to the inclusion
j:BSO^{(4)}\to BSO
.
Let
w_{2}\in H^{2}(BSO;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
be the 2nd Stiefel-Whitney class. Note that
w_{2}^{2}\neq 0

[
16
]
. The homotopy commutative triangle implies for the mod 2 cohomology

0\neq j^{*}(w_{2}^{2})=s^{*}(\nu^{\prime\prime})^{*}(w_{2}^{2}).

Hence
w_{2}(M)^{2}=((\nu^{\prime\prime})^{*}(w_{2}))^{2}=(\nu^{\prime\prime})^{*}(w_{2}^{2})\neq 0
. By Poincare duality there is a cohomology class
b\in H^{n-4}(M^{\prime\prime};\mathbb{Z}_{2})

such that
w_{2}(M)^{2}b\neq 0
. Thus, the cup-length of
M^{\prime\prime}
is at least 3, hence,
\cat M^{\prime\prime}\geq 3
. Contradiction.

The non-orientable case can be done with the use of the 1st Stiefel-Whitney class by doing 1-surgery on
\nu:M\to BO
to get a 2-equivalence by application of Proposition
3.3
(b).
Note that the kernel
\ker\{\nu_{*}:\pi_{1}(M)\to\pi_{1}(BO)=\mathbb{Z}_{2}\}
is finitely generated as an index two subgroup of a finitely generated group.
∎

pr:Z_{6}\to K(\pi_{5}(F),5)
to the fiber defines a cohomology class

\alpha=[pr\circ p_{6}\circ\phi]\in H^{5}(M;\pi_{5}(F)).

Since
f^{*}
is an isomorphism of 5-cohomology, there is
\beta\in H^{5}(N;\pi_{5}(F))
with
f^{*}(\beta)=\alpha
. The class
\beta
can be represented by a map

s:N\to K(\pi_{5}(F),5)

which defines a section
s_{5}:N\to Z_{6}
of
q_{5}
such that the map

p_{6}\phi=(pr\circ p_{6}\circ\phi,f):M\to K(\pi_{5}(F),5)\times N

is homotopic to

s_{5}f=(sf,f):M\to K(\pi_{5}(F),5)\times N.

Next we show that the map
s_{5}:N\to Z_{6}
admits a lift with respect to
q_{6}
. This is equivalent to say that

\kappa_{6}s_{5}:N\to K(\pi_{6}(F),7)

is null-homotopic.
Since
p_{7}\phi
is a lift of the map
p_{6}\phi
with respect to
q_{6}
, the composition

\kappa_{6}p_{6}\phi:M\to K(\pi_{6}(F),7)

is null-homotopic. Therefore,
\kappa_{6}s_{5}f
is null-homotopic. This means that
f^{*}
takes the cohomology class
[\kappa_{6}s_{5}]
to zero. Since by proposition
2.1
the homomorphism
f^{*}
is injective, we obtain that
[\kappa_{6}s_{5}]=0
. Thus, the map
\kappa_{6}s_{5}
is null-homotopic.

Suppose that
n=7
.
Since the map
p_{7}
is 7-equivalence i.e. it is an isomorphism for
i
-dimensional homotopy groups for
i<7
and epimorphism for
i=7
, there is a homotopy lift

\sigma:N\to G_{2}(N)
of the map
s_{6}:N\to Z_{7}
with respect to
p_{7}
. Then
\sigma
is a homotopy section of
p
. Since
p
is a fibration, it admits a regular section.
Therefore,
\cat N\leq 2
.

If
n=8
, we construct a lift
s_{7}:N\to Z_{8}
of
s_{5}
with respect to
q_{6}q_{7}
and complete the proof similarly to the case of
n=7
.
For that we modify
s_{6}
to a such a map that
s_{6}f
becomes homotopic to
p_{7}\phi
.

Since
q_{6}s_{6}=s_{5}
is an embedding, the map
q_{6}
is defined by a section of
q_{6}
on
s_{5}(N)
.
Since the map

\kappa_{6}s_{5}:N\to K(\pi_{7}(F),7)

is null-homotopic,
the restriction of
q_{6}
over
s_{5}(N)
is a trivial bundle,

q_{6}^{-1}(s_{5}(N))\cong s_{5}(N)\times K(\pi_{6}(F),6).

By the Homotopy Lifting property of
q_{6}
, we can homotop the map
p_{7}\phi
to a map
\psi:M\to Z_{7}
such that
q_{6}\psi=s_{5}f
. Thus the map

\psi:M\to s_{5}(N)\times K(\pi_{6}(F),6)

has two components
(s_{5}f,\psi_{1})
. Since
f
induces an isomorphism of 2-dimensional homology groups, by the Poincare Duality it induces an isomorphism of 6-dimensional cohomology groups. Therefore, there is a map

\xi:N\to K(\pi_{6}(F),6)

such that
f^{*}
takes the cohomology class
[\xi]\in H^{6}(N;\pi_{6}(F))
to the cohomology class
[\psi_{1}]\in H^{6}(M;\pi_{6}(F))
.
Thus,
\psi_{1}
is homotopic to
\xi f
. We consider a section

s^{\prime}:s_{5}(N)\cong N\to q_{6}^{-1}(s_{5}(N))\cong s_{5}(N)\times K(\pi_{6}(F),6)

defined by
\xi
. Then
\psi
is homotopic to the map
s^{\prime}s_{5}f
.
Since
p_{8}\phi
is a lift of the map
p_{7}\phi
with respect to
q_{7}
, the composition

\kappa_{7}p_{7}\phi:M\to K(\pi_{6}(F),7)

is null-homotopic. Hence
\kappa_{7}\psi
is null-homotopic and the map
\kappa_{7}s^{\prime}s_{5}f
is null-homotopic.
This means that
f^{*}
takes the cohomology class
[\kappa_{7}s^{\prime}s_{5}]
to zero. Since by Proposition
2.1
the homomorphism

is injective, we obtain that
[\kappa_{7}s^{\prime}s_{5}]=0
. Thus, the map
\kappa_{7}s^{\prime}s_{5}
is null-homotopic and, hence, there is a lift
s_{7}:N\to Z_{8}
of
s^{\prime}s_{5}
with respect to
q_{7}
.

Since the map
p_{8}
is 8-equivalence, there is a homotopy lift

\sigma:N\to G_{2}(N)
of the map
s_{7}:N\to Z_{8}
with respect to
p_{8}
. Then

is a homotopy section of

. Since

Therefore,
\cat N\leq 2
.
∎

Proposition 5.5
.

A degree one map
f:M\to N
of a
n
-dimensional,
n\geq 7
simply connected spin manifold is bordant by means of surgery in dimension 2 and 3 to a a degree one map
f^{\prime}:M^{\prime}\to N
that induces isomorphisms
f^{\prime}:\pi_{i}(M^{\prime})\to\pi_{i}(N)
for
i\leq 3
.

Proof.

Note that
f_{*}:H_{2}(M)\to H_{2}(N)
is surjective with a finitely generated kernel
K=kerf_{*}
which is by
the Hurewicz Theorem isomorphic to
ker\{f_{*}:\pi_{2}(M)\to\pi_{2}(N)\}
. Let
S_{1},\dots,S_{k}
be 2-spheres smoothly embedded in
M
that
generate
K
. For each sphere
S_{i}
its normal bundle
\nu_{i}
is classified by a map
\nu_{i}:S_{i}\to BSO(5)
. The map
\nu_{i}
is null-homotopic, since otherwise the evaluation of the Stiefel-Whitney class
w_{2}\in H^{2}(BSO(5);\mathbb{Z}_{2})
on the image
(\nu_{i})_{*}([S_{i}])\in H_{2}(BSO(5);\mathbb{Z}_{2})
would be nonzero.
The latter would contradict to the spin condition on
M
. Thus, we can perform a surgery to obtain a bordism
W
of
M
to
M^{\prime}
together with a map
\bar{f}:W\to N
.
The restriction
f^{\prime}:M^{\prime}\to N
of
\bar{f}
to
M^{\prime}
has degree one and
f^{\prime}
induces an isomorphism of 2-dimensional homotopy groups.

By the above we may assume that
f:M\to N
induces an isomorphism of 2-dimensional homotopy groups.
Since
\pi_{3}(BSO(4))=0
, we obtain that the normal bundle of any embedded 3-sphere in
M^{\prime}
is trivial.
Hence we can perform surgery on generators of the kernel
ker\{f_{*}:\pi_{3}(M^{\prime})\to\pi_{3}(N)\}
to get a bordism
W
between
M
and
M^{\prime}
together with a map
\hat{f}:W\to N
that agrees with
f
on
M
such that the restriction
f^{\prime}:M^{\prime}\to N
of
\hat{f}
to
M^{\prime}
has degree one and it induces isomorphisms of homotopy groups of
\dim\leq 3
.
∎

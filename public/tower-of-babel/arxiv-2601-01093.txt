The inverse eigenvalue problems for perturbed Bessel operator with mixed data
Zeguang Liu, Xin-Jian Xu

Abstract
. We consider inverse eigenvalue problems for the perturbed Bessel operator in L 2 ​ ( 0 , 1 ) L^{2}(0,1) . (1) For the case where the angular-momentum quantum number ℓ ∈ ℕ ∪ { 0 } \ell\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\} , we establish a uniqueness result for the inverse spectral problem by utilizing the closedness condition of a certain function system constructed based on the eigenvalues and the norming constants. (2) For the broader case where ℓ ≥ − 1 / 2 \ell\geq-1/2 , we provide a uniqueness result for the inverse problem by using the density condition satisfied by the eigenvalues and the norming constants, where an additional smoothness condition may be imposed on the potential. (3) In the last section of this article, we present some corollaries based on (2). The results in these corollaries have already been established for the case ℓ = 0 \ell=0 by Gesztesy, Simon, Wei, Xu, Hatinoǧlu, et al., and we extend these results to the general case ℓ ≥ − 1 / 2 \ell\geq-1/2 .

In this paper, we study the boundary value problem on the interval

(1.1)

\displaystyle L(\ell,q)(f):=-f^{\prime\prime}(x)+\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{x^{2}}f(x)+q(x)f(x)=\lambda f(x),

\displaystyle\lim_{x\rightarrow 0}\frac{f(x)}{x^{\ell+1}}<\infty,~~f^{\prime}(1)+\beta f(1)=0,

Our criterion for uniqueness based on the mixed data (1)–(3) is related to the closedness of a certain function system, which is constructed using eigenvalues and the corresponding norming constants. To formulate the results, we need the following definition, which is introduced in
[

\textstyle\int_{0}^{a}h(x)\varphi_{n}(x)dx=0

. It is easy to verify that the closedness of

For a set

\displaystyle C(\Lambda,S):=\{\cos(2\sqrt{\lambda_{n}}x)\mid\lambda_{n}\in\Lambda\}\cup\{x\sin(2\sqrt{\lambda_{n}}x)\mid\lambda_{n}\in S\}.

Proof.

uniformly in

(3.9)

\begin{split}H_{\ell}(z^{2})=&\left(\int_{0}^{x_{0}}+\int_{x_{0}}^{a}\right)(q-\hat{q})(x)\phi_{\ell}(\lambda,x,q)\phi_{\ell}(\lambda,x,\hat{q})dx\\

Remark 4.1

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Proof by contradiction. Suppose
\hat{q}\neq q
. Then

z^{2\ell+2}H_{\ell}(z^{2})
cannot be identically zero.
This is due to the fact that, if

\frac{\phi_{\ell}^{\prime}(z^{2},1,q)}{\phi_{\ell}(z^{2},1,q)}\equiv\frac{\phi_{\ell}^{\prime}(z^{2},1,\hat{q})}{\phi_{\ell}(z^{2},1,\hat{q})},

[

]
), implies that

a.e. on
(0,1)

Without loss of generality, let

H_{\ell}(0)\neq 0
. Then
H_{\ell}(z^{2})
is a nontrivial entire function of
z
with zeros at
\pm\sqrt{\lambda_{n}}
and at least double zeros for
\lambda_{n}\in S
.

Recall the Jensen formula: Let
f(z)
be analytic for
|z|<R
and
f(0)\neq 0
. If
n(t)
is the number of zeros of
f(z)
in
|z|\leq t
, then for
0<r<R
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}\frac{n(t)}{t}dt=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\text{\rm ln}|f(re^{i\varphi})|d\varphi-\text{\rm ln}|f(0)|.

By applying the Jensen formula to
H_{\ell}(z^{2})
, we get, with the notation
m(t)=2n_{\Lambda}(t^{2})+2n_{S}(t^{2})
, that

(4.14)

\int_{0}^{r}\frac{m(t)}{t}dt\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\text{\rm ln}|H_{\ell}(r^{2}e^{2i\varphi})|d\varphi+C_{4},

where

. Substituting the estimate of

from the inequality (

) into (
4.14
), after some simple calculations, it can be seen that

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{r}\frac{m(t)}{t}dt-\frac{4ra}{\pi}+\left(k+2\ell+2+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\right)\text{\rm ln}r\\
\leq&\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\text{\rm ln}\left(\varepsilon+Cr^{\gamma}e^{-\delta r|\sin\varphi|}+C(r|\sin\varphi|)^{k+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}e^{-2\delta r|\sin\varphi|}\right)d\varphi+C_{4}.\end{split}

It can be easily verified that

(r|\sin\varphi|)^{k+1/p^{\prime}}e^{-2\delta r|\sin\varphi|}\leq\left(\frac{k+1/p^{\prime}}{2\delta}\right)^{k+1/p^{\prime}}e^{-(k+1/p^{\prime})}.

Taking the upper limit as

on both sides of the inequality (

), we have

(4.17)

\limsup_{r\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{r}\frac{m(t)}{t}dt-\frac{4ra}{\pi}+\left(k+2\ell+2+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\right)\text{\rm ln}r\leq\text{\rm ln}\varepsilon+C_{4}.

From the arbitrariness of
\varepsilon
, the inequality (

) implies

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}\frac{m(t)}{t}dt-\frac{4ra}{\pi}+\left(k+2\ell+2+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\right)\text{\rm ln}r\rightarrow-\infty,~~r\rightarrow\infty,

which contradicts the assumptions. Therefore,
q=\hat{q}
a.e. on
(0,a)
. This completes the proof.
∎

B=\{b_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\sigma(\ell,q,\beta_{2})
,
such that
\prod\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}(a_{n}/b_{n})
is absolutely convergent.
Then the following conclusions hold.

(1).

If
\beta_{1}=\infty
or
\beta_{2}=\infty
, then the sets
\sigma(\ell,q,\beta_{1})
,

\{\zeta(a_{n},\beta_{1},q)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and

\sigma(\ell,q,\beta_{2})\setminus B
uniquely determine
q
.

(2).

If
\beta_{1}\neq\infty
and
\beta_{2}\neq\infty
, then the sets
\sigma(\ell,q,\beta_{1})
,

\{\zeta(a_{n},\beta_{1},q)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and

\sigma(\ell,q,\beta_{2})\setminus(B\cup\{\lambda_{\ell,\beta_{2},k}(q)\})
for
\lambda_{\ell,\beta_{2},k}(q)\notin B
uniquely determine
q
.

For the case
\ell=0
, the result in Corollary
5.9
is due to Hatinoǧlu
[
21
, Theorems 4.8 and 4.14]
. The condition involving an additional restriction on the selection of eigenvalues in
[
21
, Theorem 4.14]
is not necessary in Corollary
5.9
. Moreover, compared with
[
21
, Theorem 4.14]
, the required eigenvalue information in (2) of Corollary
5.9
can be reduced by one. Before proving Corollary
5.9
, we first present the following lemma.

Lemma 5.10
.

Suppose the sequences
A=\{a_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
B=\{b_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are such that
a_{n}=b_{n}+t_{n}b_{n}
, where
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|t_{n}|<\infty
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
, such that

(5.16)

\left|\int_{0}^{r}\frac{n_{A}(t^{2})}{t}dt-\int_{0}^{r}\frac{n_{B}(t^{2})}{t}dt\right|\leq C,

for all
r>0
.

Proof.

Note that

\int_{0}^{r}\frac{n_{A}(t^{2})}{t}dt=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\max\left\{0,\text{\rm ln}r-\frac{1}{2}\text{\rm ln}|a_{n}|\right\},

and

\int_{0}^{r}\frac{n_{B}(t^{2})}{t}dt=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\max\left\{0,\text{\rm ln}r-\frac{1}{2}\text{\rm ln}|b_{n}|\right\}.

Let

\displaystyle d_{n}:=\max\left\{0,\text{\rm ln}r-\frac{1}{2}\text{\rm ln}|a_{n}|\right\}-\max\left\{0,\text{\rm ln}r-\frac{1}{2}\text{\rm ln}|b_{n}|\right\},

then, one has

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{r}\frac{n_{A}(t^{2})}{t}dt-\int_{0}^{r}\frac{n_{B}(t^{2})}{t}dt\right|=\left|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}d_{n}\right|.

Without loss of generality, let
1+t_{n}\neq 0
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, for each
n
, we have

•

If
|b_{n}|<r^{2}
and
|a_{n}|<r^{2}
, then
|d_{n}|=\frac{1}{2}\left|\text{\rm ln}\frac{|b_{n}|}{|a_{n}|}\right|=\frac{1}{2}|\text{\rm ln}|1+t_{n}||
.

•

If
|b_{n}|<r^{2}\leq|a_{n}|
, then
\frac{1}{2}\text{\rm ln}\frac{|b_{n}|}{|a_{n}|}\leq d_{n}<0
, so
|d_{n}|\leq\frac{1}{2}|\text{\rm ln}|1+t_{n}||
.

•

If
|a_{n}|<r^{2}\leq|b_{n}|
, then
0<d_{n}\leq\frac{1}{2}\text{\rm ln}\frac{|b_{n}|}{|a_{n}|}
, so
|d_{n}|\leq\frac{1}{2}|\text{\rm ln}|1+t_{n}||
.

•

If
b_{n}\geq r^{2}
and
a_{n}\geq r^{2}
, then
d_{n}=0
.

Consequently,

|d_{n}|\leq\frac{1}{2}|\text{\rm ln}|1+t_{n}||
for each
n
.
The condition
\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|t_{n}\right|<\infty
gives

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}d_{n}\right|\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|d_{n}|\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2}|\text{\rm ln}|1+t_{n}||<\infty,

that is, there exists a constant
C>0
, such that (
5.16
) holds. This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Corollary
5.9
.

Let

\displaystyle m(t)=2n_{\sigma(\ell,q,\beta_{1})}(t^{2})+2n_{A}(t^{2})+2n_{\sigma(\ell,q,\beta_{2})\setminus\tilde{B}}(t^{2}).

We have

(5.17)

\displaystyle m(t)=2n_{\sigma(\ell,q,\beta_{1})}(t^{2})+2n_{\sigma(\ell,q,\beta_{2})}(t^{2})+2n_{A}(t^{2})-2n_{B}(t^{2})-2\chi(\beta_{1},\beta_{2}),

for all sufficiently large
t>0
,
where

\displaystyle\tilde{B}:=\begin{cases}B,&~\textnormal{if}~\beta_{1}=\infty~\textnormal{or}~\beta_{2}=\infty,\\
B\cup\{\lambda_{\ell,\beta_{2},k}(q)\},&~\textnormal{if}~\beta_{1}\neq\infty~\textnormal{and}~\beta_{2}\neq\infty,\end{cases}

and
\chi(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})
is defined in (
5.11
).
Then the inequalities (
5.6
), (
5.8
), (
5.17
), and Lemma
5.10
give

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}\frac{m(t)}{t}dt-\frac{4r}{\pi}+(2\ell+2)\text{\rm ln}r>-\infty,~~r\rightarrow\infty.

The prove is completed by Theorem
1.3
.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

Let
\ell=-1/2
and
q\in L^{1}(0,1)

\beta_{2}=\infty

5.9

Topological expanders, coarse geometry and thick embeddings of complexes
David Hume, University of Birmingham

Abstract
We quantify the topological expansion properties of bounded degree simplicial complexes in terms of a family of sublinear functions, in analogy with the separation profile of Benjamini-Schramm-Timár for classical expansion of bounded degree graphs. We prove that, like the separation profile, these new invariants are monotone under regular maps between complexes satisfying appropriate higher connectivity assumptions. In the dimension 1 1 case, we recover the cutwidth profile of Huang-Hume-Kelly-Lam. We also prove the seemingly new result that any 1 1 -dimensional topological expander necessarily contains a graphical expander. In higher dimensions, we give full calculations of these new invariants for Euclidean spaces, which are natural analogues of waist and width-volume inequalities due to Gromov and Guth respectively. We present several other methods of obtaining upper bounds including naïve (yet useful) direct product and fibring theorems, and show how lower bounds can be obtained via thick embeddings of complexes, in analogy with previous work of Barrett-Hume. Using this, we find lower bounds for k k -expansion of ( k + 1 ) (k+1) -fold horocyclic products of trees, and for rank k k symmetric spaces of non-compact type. As a further application, we prove that for every k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 there is no coarse embedding (and more generally, no regular map) from the k k -fold horocyclic product of 3 3 -regular trees to either any product ( ℍ 2 ) k − 2 × H × D (\mathbb{H}^{2})^{k-2}\times H\times D where ℍ 2 \mathbb{H}^{2} is the real hyperbolic plane, H H is a bounded degree hyperbolic graph and D D is a doubling metric space, or to any symmetric space whose non-compact factor has corank (dimension minus rank) is strictly less than k k .

Introduction

During the last 50 years, the study of expander graphs has become an important part of both theoretical computer science and several areas of pure mathematics, linking them together in fruitful and unexpected ways, see for instance
[
HLW06
,
Lub11
]
and the references therein. More recently, there has been intensive work on generalising the study of graph expansion to the expansion of higher-dimensional simplicial complexes. For graphs, there are several definitions of expansion coming naturally from different mathematical perspectives, and for bounded-degree graphs, many of these turn out to be equivalent up to constant factors. However, the natural generalisations of these notions to simplicial complexes are not equivalent; they lead to genuinely different notions of expansion, each with their own importance and applications, for example in property testing, locally testable codes, quantum computation, probabilistically checkable proofs, and the study of Kazhdan’s Property
(T)

[

Classical graphical expansion has been quantified in coarse geometric invariants such as the separation and Poincaré profiles. These profiles exhibit tantalising connections to other important invariants in geometric group theory (e.g., growth, isoperimetry and conformal dimension), and can be used to generalise some classical dichotomy results for connected unimodular Lie groups

1.2
Topological overlap of groups

One motivation for studying obstructions to regular maps is that given two Cayley graphs
X,Y
of finitely generated groups
G,H
with respect to finite symmetric generating sets, there is a regular map

To use higher topological overlap profiles as obstructions to subgroups, it is no longer sufficient to work with Cayley graphs. For a group

of type F (those that admit a finite classifying space), a natural choice of simplicial complex to associate to

is the universal cover of some iterated barycentric subdivision of the classifying space. For groups which are not of type

and

Proposition 1.3
.

Let

be a bounded degree simplicial complex. For

, we have

{}_{s}\textup{TO}^{q}_{X^{\leq q}}(r)\simeq{}_{s}\textup{TO}^{q}_{X}(r).

As a result, for a finitely generated group

we may always calculate
{}_{s}\textup{TO}^{1}_{G}
using a Cayley graph, and for a finitely presented group

we may calculate
{}_{s}\textup{TO}^{2}_{H}
using a barycentric subdivision of a Cayley

.

Let

1.4
Topologically thin and topologically thick spaces

Inspired by the thick-thin dichotomy for Poincaré profiles introduced in

1.5
Euclidean spaces

Our first calculation in higher dimensions is a complete description of the topological overlap of Euclidean spaces.

Theorem 1.7
.

For each
1\leq q<n
we have

\textup{TO}^{q}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(r)\simeq r^{1-q/n}.

For every

\textup{TO}^{q}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(r)\simeq 1

The lower bound (for

]

and applications to VLSI

Minimizing the area of a circuit is an important problem in the domain of Very Large Scale Integration (VLSI). In the language of this paper, one of the key problems tackled in

4

4

Leiserson’s results are stronger than this: they require the image to be contained within a rectangle of controlled eccentricity and are actually measuring the volume of the rectangle rather than the image as defined here

The second bound is not optimal in all cases, this is obvious for

Simplicial complexes

Definition 2.1
.

An
abstract simplicial complex
is a pair
(S,\mathcal{S})
where
S
is a set and
\mathcal{S}
is a collection of subsets of
S
which contains all singletons and is closed under taking subsets. The
degree
of
(S,\mathcal{S})
is

\deg(S,\mathcal{S})=\max_{s\in S}\left|\left\{\{s,t\}\in\mathcal{S}\ \left|\ s\neq t\right.\right\}\right|.

The
dimension
of
(S,\mathcal{S})
is

\dim(S,\mathcal{S})=\max_{U\in\mathcal{S}}|U|-1.

Given two abstract simplicial complexes
(S,\mathcal{S})
and
(T,\mathcal{T})
we write
(S,\mathcal{S})\leq(T,\mathcal{T})
if there is an injection
\iota:S\to T
such that
\iota(U)\in\mathcal{T}
for all
U\in\mathcal{S}
. We call
(S,\mathcal{S})
a
subcomplex
of
(T,\mathcal{T})
.

We collect two useful facts.

Fact 2.2
.

•

If
|S|<+\infty
, then
|\mathcal{S}|\leq|S|2^{\deg(S,\mathcal{S})}
.

•

If
\deg(S,\mathcal{S})<+\infty
, then

\Delta_{(S,\mathcal{S})}:=\max_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}}\left\{\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}\ \left|\ \sigma\cap\sigma^{\prime}\neq\emptyset\right.\right\}<+\infty.

Definition 2.3
.

An abstract simplicial complex
(S,\mathcal{S})
is called a

if
U\in\mathcal{S}
whenever every
2
element subset of

is in
\mathcal{S}
.

A natural example of a flag complex is the barycentric subdivision of any abstract simplicial complex.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
(S,\mathcal{S})
be an abstract simplicial complex. The
barycentric subdivision
of
(S,\mathcal{S})
is the pair
(\mathcal{S},\mathbb{S})
where
\mathbb{S}
is the set of increasing chains of elements of
\mathcal{S}
.

We collect the following useful facts concerning barycentric subdivisions for later use.

Fact 2.5
.

If
\dim(S,\mathcal{S})<+\infty
, then

. If, in addition,
\deg(S,\mathcal{S})<+\infty
, then
\deg(\mathcal{S},\mathbb{S})\leq 2^{\deg(S,\mathcal{S})}
.

Definition 2.6
.

The
topological realisation
of a simplicial complex

is the set

\overline{S}=\left\{f:S\to[0,1]\ \left|\ \textup{supp}(f)\in\mathcal{S},\ \sum_{s\in S}f(s)=1\right.\right\}

equipped with the subspace topology from

. We may also consider

as a metric space, where the distance between functions

and

is given by

\min_{k}\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\|f_{i-1}-f_{i}\|_{2}\ :\ f_{i}\in\overline{S},\ f_{0}=f,\ f_{k}=f^{\prime},\ \textup{supp}(f_{i-1})\cup\textup{supp}(f_{i})\in\mathcal{S}\right\}.

(1)

The support function defines a natural surjective map
\textup{supp}:Z\to\mathcal{S}
.

Remark 2.7
.

For the remainder of a paper we will use the phrase “simplicial complex” to mean the topological realisation of an abstract simplicial complex.

Definition 2.8
.

A

Given a simplicial complex

3
Topological overlap

The goal of this section is to give two definitions of topological overlap, one for simplicial complexes (
\lx@sectionsign\ref{sec:defnsTO}
) and one for general metric spaces (
\lx@sectionsign\ref{sec:covdim}
). We will then prove that they are equivalent on simplicial complexes equipped with the metric (
1
). The main goal of the section is to prove the monotonicity of topological overlap for simplicial complexes and metric spaces with appropriate higher connectivity conditions (
\lx@sectionsign\ref{sec:mono}
).

3.1
Definition for complexes

Definition 3.1
.

Let
Z\in\mathbb{S}_{f}
. Given a continuous map
f\in C(Z,\mathbb{R}^{q})
we define the
topological overlap constant
of
f
to be

{}_{s}\textup{Ov}(f)=\max_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{q}}\left\lvert\left\{\sigma\in\mathcal{C}Z\ \left|\ z\in f(\sigma)\right.\right\}\right\rvert

For each
q\geq 1
, the
q
-dimensional topological overlap
constant of
Z
is

{}_{s}\textup{TO}^{q}(Z)=\min\left\{{}_{s}\textup{Ov}(f)\ \left|\ f\in C(Z,\mathbb{R}^{q})\right.\right\}.

We then take a maximum over subcomplexes of a given size, in analogy with the definition of the separation profile.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
X\in\mathbb{S}_{b}
. The
q
-dimensional topological overlap profile
of
X
is

{}_{s}\textup{TO}^{q}_{X}(r)=\max\left\{{}_{s}\textup{TO}^{q}(Z)\ \left|\ Z\leq X,\ |Z|\leq r\right.\right\}.

Remark 3.3
.

It is completely natural to extend the above definitions to finite dimensional CW-complexes, counting cells rather than simplices. A seemingly natural class to consider is complexes with the property that some iterated barycentric subdivision is a simplicial complex (a class which includes all cube complexes, regular CW complexes and all almost-regular CW complexes in the sense of Druţu-Kapovich
[
DK18
]
). The resulting simplicial complex has bounded degree whenever the cardinality of the set of all closed cells intersecting a given cell can be bounded independent of the chosen cell - this excludes, for example, Cayley
2
-complexes of presentations of groups with infinitely many relators. Under this assumption, taking a barycentric subdivision does not change
{}_{s}\textup{TO}^{q}_{X}
up to
\simeq
.

In this paper we will use the following definition of topological expander, which assumes bounded degree.

Definition 3.4
.

Given
\Delta\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varepsilon>0
, a family of finite
q
-dimensional simplicial complexes
(Z_{n})_{n}
is called a
q
-dimensional
(\Delta,\varepsilon)
-topological expander
if

•

|Z_{n}|\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
,

•

\sup_{n}\deg(Z_{n})\leq\Delta
, for every
n
, and

•

{}_{s}\textup{TO}^{q}(Z_{n})\geq\varepsilon|Z_{n}|
for every
n
.

We can now give the easy proof of Theorem
1.1
(i).

Proof of Theorem
1.1
(i).

Suppose
X
is a bounded degree simplicial complex of dimension at most
q
. If there is a
(\Delta,\varepsilon)
-topological expander
(Z_{n})
such that, for each
n
,
Z_{n}\leq X
, then, for every
n

{}_{s}\textup{TO}^{q}_{X}(|Z_{n}|)\geq{}_{s}\textup{TO}^{q}(Z_{n})\geq\varepsilon|Z_{n}|

As
|Z_{n}|\to\infty
, we have
\liminf_{r\to\infty}\frac{1}{r}{}_{s}\textup{TO}^{q}_{X}(r)\geq\varepsilon>0
.

Now suppose
\liminf_{r\to\infty}\frac{1}{r}{}_{s}\textup{TO}^{q}_{X}(r)\geq\varepsilon>0
, so there exists an infinite subset
I\subseteq\mathbb{N}
such that
{}_{s}\textup{TO}^{q}_{X}(r)\geq\varepsilon r
for all
r\in I
. Unpacking Definition
3.1
, this means that for every
r\in I
, there is some
Z_{r}\leq I
such that

{}_{s}\textup{TO}^{q}(Z_{r})\geq\varepsilon r\geq\varepsilon|Z_{r}|.

It remains to prove that
\{|Z_{r}|\}
is not bounded. Suppose for a contradiction that
|Z_{r}|\leq N
for all
r\in I
. Any simplicial complex with
N

0
-simplices and degree
\Delta
contains at most
2^{\Delta}N
simplices, thus for all
r\in I
, we have

2^{\Delta}N\geq{}_{s}\textup{TO}^{q}(Z_{r})\geq\varepsilon r.

which contradicts the fact that
I\subset\mathbb{N}
is infinite.
∎

Definition 3.5

Let

The idea behind the second definition is to measure size in terms of covering volume instead of number of simplices.

Definition 3.6

Let
Z
be a metric space with finite

and

\textup{Ov}_{f}(z)=\textup{Cov}^{1}(f^{-1}(z))

\textup{Ov}(f)=\max_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{q}}\textup{Ov}_{f}(z)

The

\textup{TO}^{q}(Z)=\min\left\{\textup{Ov}(f)\ \left|\ f\in C(Z,\mathbb{R}^{q})\right.\right\}.

Definition 3.7
.

Let

be a metric space. The

\textup{TO}^{q}_{X}(r)=\max\left\{\textup{TO}^{q}(Z)\ \left|\ Z\subseteq X,\ \textup{Cov}^{1}(Z)\leq r\right.\right\}.

3.3
Equivalence of definitions

The main result of this section is to prove the equivalence of the two definitions of topological overlap profile for simplicial complexes.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
X\in\mathbb{S}_{b}
be equipped with the metric (
1
). For every
q
,

{}_{s}\textup{TO}^{q}_{X}(r)\simeq\textup{TO}^{q}_{X}(r).

We will prove Theorem
3.8
as an immediate consequence of the following two propositions, which we prove in a more general setting.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
X
be a metric space and let
Z\in\mathbb{S}_{b}
. If there is a continuous map
\phi:X\to Z
and a constant
M
such that

(a)

for every ball
B
of radius
1
in
X
,
\phi(B)
intersects at most
M

Z
, and

(b)

for every
\sigma\in\mathcal{C}Z
we have
\textup{Cov}^{1}(\phi^{-1}(\sigma))\leq M
,

then for all
q
,

\textup{TO}^{q}_{X}(r)\lesssim{}_{s}\textup{TO}^{q}_{Z}(r).

Proof.

Let
X^{\prime}\subseteq X
satisfy
\textup{Cov}^{1}(X^{\prime})\leq r
and let
Z^{\prime}
be the subcomplex of
Z
consisting of all closed simplices which intersect
\phi(X^{\prime})
. By assumption (a)
|Z^{\prime}|\leq Mr
. Choose
f\in C(Z^{\prime},\mathbb{R}^{q})
with
{}_{s}\textup{Ov}(f)\leq\textup{TO}^{q}(Mr)
. Using assumption (b), we have

\textup{Cov}^{1}(\phi^{-1}f^{-1}(z))\leq M\left\lvert\left\{\sigma\in\mathcal{C}X^{\prime}\ \left|\ z\in f(\sigma)\right.\right\}\right\rvert\leq M{}_{s}\textup{Ov}(f).

As this holds for all
z\in\mathbb{R}^{q}
, we deduce that

\textup{Ov}(\phi\circ f)\leq M{}_{s}\textup{Ov}(f)\leq M{}_{s}\textup{TO}^{q}(Mr).

As
X^{\prime}
was chosen arbitrarily,
\textup{TO}^{q}_{X}(r)\leq M{}_{s}\textup{TO}^{q}_{Z}(Mr)
.
∎

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
X
be a metric space and let
Z\in\mathbb{S}_{b}
. If there is a continuous map
\psi:Z\to X
and a constant
N
such that

(c)

for every
\sigma\in\mathcal{C}Z

\textup{Cov}^{1}(\psi(\sigma))\leq N

for every ball

in

\psi^{-1}(B)

{}_{s}\textup{TO}^{q}_{Z}(r)\lesssim\textup{TO}^{q}_{X}(r).

Let

|Z^{\prime}|\leq r
and let

. By assumption (c) and Fact

\textup{Cov}^{1}(X^{\prime})\leq N|\mathcal{C}Z^{\prime}|\leq\Delta_{Z}N|Z^{\prime}|\leq\Delta_{Z}Nr
. Set

.

Choose

with
\textup{Ov}(g)\leq\textup{TO}^{q}_{X}(N^{\prime}r)

{}_{s}\textup{Ov}(\psi\circ g)\leq N\textup{Ov}(g)\leq N\textup{TO}^{q}_{X}(N^{\prime}r).

As

was chosen arbitrarily,
{}_{s}\textup{TO}^{q}_{X}(r)\leq N\textup{TO}^{q}_{Z}(N^{\prime}r)

.

Let

(

The goal of this section is to prove Theorem

4.1

Let us recall the definition of the cutwidth profile from

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finite graph with

of
\Gamma
(denoted

\max_{i}|\{vw\in E\Gamma:\ \sigma(v)<i\leq\sigma(w)\}|

We define the

of a graph

\textup{cw}_{X}(r)=\max\left\{\textup{cw}(\Gamma)\ \left|\ \Gamma\leq X,\ |\Gamma|\leq r\right.\right\}.

Our next target is Theorem

Lemma 4.2

For every finite graph
\Gamma

\textup{cw}(\Gamma)\leq{}_{s}\textup{TO}^{1}(\Gamma)\leq\textup{cw}(\Gamma)+\deg(\Gamma)+1.

Proof.

Every bijection
\sigma:V\Gamma\to\{1,\ldots,r\}
naturally extends to a continuous map
\overline{\sigma}:\Gamma\to[1,r]
so that the image of the edge
vw
is the interval
[\sigma(v),\sigma(w)]
. For each

,
\overline{\sigma}^{-1}(z)
contains a single point in each edge

with
\sigma(v)\leq z\leq\sigma(w)
and (if

and all edges with end vertex

.

Thus
{}_{s}\textup{TO}^{1}(\Gamma)\leq{}_{s}\textup{Ov}(\overline{\sigma})\leq\textup{cw}(\Gamma)+1+\deg(\Gamma)
.

Conversely, let
g\in C(\Gamma,\mathbb{R})
. Enumerate the vertices of
\Gamma
by
v_{1},\ldots,v_{r}
so that
g(v_{i})\leq g(v_{j})
whenever

. Define
\sigma:V\Gamma\to\{1,\ldots,r\}
by
\sigma(v_{j})=j
. By the intermediate value theorem

contains a point in every edge

with

{}_{s}\textup{Ov}(g)\geq\max_{i}|\{vw\in E\Gamma:\ \sigma(v)<i\leq\sigma(w)\}|\geq\textup{cw}(\Gamma).

As

was chosen arbitrarily, we have

.

5.1
Euclidean spaces

The goal of this section is to calculate the topological overlap of Euclidean spaces. Let us recall the statement of Theorem

For each

5.1.1
Lower bound

Our first goal is the lower bound, which we prove using the covering definition of topological overlap and one of several proofs of the waist inequality due to Gromov (cf.
[

Theorem 5.1
.

For each

there exists a constant
\beta^{\prime}_{k,q}>0

cannot be covered by fewer than

in

.

Our goal is to derive a comparable result for continuous maps from the ball of radius

in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
to
\mathbb{R}^{q}
.

For each

, we define
RS^{k}=\left\{(x_{0},\ldots,x_{k})\in\mathbb{R}^{k+1}\ \left|\ \sum x_{i}^{2}=R^{2}\right.\right\}
and

and

and

Define

Since

Rescaling this by a parameter

in

Rescaling Theorem

by

, we see that for every continuous map

.

Solutions to Sobolev Supercritical Nonlinear Schrödinger Equations on an Annulus via a Hopf Reduction Method
Jian Liang, Hua-Yang Wang

Abstract
This paper investigates the existence of positive solutions with a prescribed mass for nonlinear Schrödinger equations on an annulus, possibly in the Sobolev supercritical regime. A reduction method based on the Hopf fibration is used to transform the problem into a lower-dimensional one. We obtain a new mass critical threshold and we show that in the new mass subcritical or critical regimes there exists a positive solution which corresponds to a global minimizer, while in the mass supercritical regime, there exists two positive solutions which correspond to a local minimizer and a mountain pass solution respectively. Some other problems are also discussed in this paper.



Keywords:
Hopf reduction, mass critical threshold, Sobolev supercritical, variational methods.

35J20, 35B33, 35B09.

Introduction

1.1
Motivations and backgrounds

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a bounded domain with
N\geq 3
.
The time-dependent nonlinear Schrödinger equation

\begin{cases}i\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}+\Delta\psi+|\psi|^{p-2}\psi=0,\quad&(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\Omega,\\
\psi(t,x)=0,\quad&(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\partial\Omega,\\
\psi(0,x)=\psi_{0}(x),\quad&x\in\Omega,\end{cases}

(1.1)

arises naturally in various physical phenomena, such as nonlinear optics
[
1
]
, plasma physics
[
23
]
and Bose-Einstein condensates
[
11
]
.
If we look for standing waves of the form
\psi(t,x)=e^{-i\lambda t}u(x)
, then
u
solves the following elliptic problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta u+\lambda u=|u|^{p-2}u&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(1.2)

A typical way to investigate the elliptic problem (
1.2
) is the variational method.
Given
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, solutions to (
1.2
) can be viewed as critical points of the action functional

I(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{\lambda}{2}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x.

It is known that for
2<p<2^{*}:=\frac{2N}{N-2}
(the Sobolev critical exponent), (
1.2
) has a positive solution for every
\lambda>-\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
, where
\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
is the first eigenvalue of
-\Delta
in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

[
27
]
.
However, if
p\geq 2^{*}
and
\Omega
is star-shaped, then the Pohozaev identity
[
22
]

\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{\lambda}{2}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{2N}\int_{\partial\Omega}(x\cdot\nu)|\nabla u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}S

implies that (
1.2
) has no nontrivial solutions for any
\lambda\geq 0
.
Therefore, for any star-shaped domain, we cannot hope to seek a solution to (
1.2
) when
\lambda\geq 0
with
p
being Sobolev critical or supercritical.

Nevertheless, if
\Omega
possesses a nontrivial topology, such as containing ‘a hole’, the situation becomes substantially different.
The case
p=2^{*}
was studied by the seminal work of Bahri and Coron
[
2
]
.
They proved that if
\Omega
is a bounded domain with nontrivial
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
reduced homology group, then (
1.2
) has a positive solution for every
\lambda>0
.
The problem is much more complicated if
p>2^{*}
.
As the Sobolev embedding
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\Omega)
does not hold for
p>2^{*}
, the functional
I
is not well-defined in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, and hence the variational methods cannot be used directly.
However, in specific domains, we can still establish the existence of positive solutions to (
1.2
) for certain Sobolev supercritical ranges.
When
\Omega=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:a<|x|<b,0<a<b<+\infty\}
is an annulus, due to the rich symmetry structure, a special coordinate based on the Hopf fibration was introduced by Ruf and Srikanth in
[
24
]
for
N=4
to deal with the following singularly perturbed problem

\begin{cases}-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta u+u=u^{p-1}&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u=0,&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(1.3)

and the problem (
1.3
) can be reduced to the following problem in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with a weight

\begin{cases}-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta v+\frac{1}{2|x|}v=\frac{1}{2|x|}v^{p-1}&\text{ in }\widetilde{\Omega},\\
v=0,&\text{ on }\partial\widetilde{\Omega},\end{cases}

(1.4)

where
\widetilde{\Omega}=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:a^{2}/2<|x|<b^{2}/2\right\}
is also an annulus (the details of the reduction will be discussed in Section 2).
Applying methods and results in
[
15
]
to (
1.4
), they were able to show that there exist a sequence of solutions to (
1.3
) concentrating in an
S^{1}
orbit as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
for every
p\in(2,6)
.
Notice that
6
is larger than the Sobolev critical exponent
4
in the original problem.
In summary, the reduction method provides a way to deal with some Sobolev supercritical problems.
Such a method has been generalized in
[
25
,
14
,
26
,
13
,
12
]
to investigate solutions to (
1.2
) in certain Sobolev supercritical ranges.

There is another perspective to study the problem (
1.2
).
Two conserved quantities of (
1.1
) are important, that is, the energy

E(\psi)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\psi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\Omega}|\psi|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x

and the mass

M(\psi)=\int_{\Omega}|\psi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Therefore, investigating solutions with a prescribed mass is a natural endeavor; in other words, we aim to find solutions to (
1.2
) satisfying the constraint

\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=c^{2}

for some fixed
c>0
.
We shall call such problems the
prescribed mass problem
and corresponding solutions
normalized solutions
.
The stability of solutions to (
1.1
) is closely related to the existence of normalized solutions to (
1.2
)
[
7
]
.
The prescribed mass problem can be restated as

\begin{cases}-\Delta u+\lambda u=|u|^{p-2}u&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\\
\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=c^{2}.\end{cases}

(1.5)

Solutions to (
1.5
) can be viewed as critical points of the energy functional
E:H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
restricted to the manifold

S_{c}=\{u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=c^{2}\},

where
E
is defined by

E(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x

and
\lambda
appears here as an unknown Lagrange multiplier.
According to the different variational structures of
E
on
S_{c}
, the mass critical exponent

2^{\#}_{N}:=2+\frac{4}{N}

plays an important role in the prescribed mass problem.
Indeed, by the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (see (
2.3
)), the functional
E
is bounded from below on
S_{c}
if
2<p<2^{\#}_{N}
, while
E
is unbounded from below on
S_{c}
if
2^{\#}_{N}<p<2^{*}
. If
p=2^{\#}_{N}
, the lower bounds of
E
on
S_{c}
depends on the value of
c
.

For
2<p<2^{\#}_{N}
, it is not hard to prove the existence of a positive solution to (
1.5
) by a minimizing progress since the Sobolev embedding
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\Omega)
is compact (for example, see
[
20
]
).
For
2^{\#}_{N}<p<2^{*}
, Noris, Tavares and Verzini
[
17
]
first studied (
1.5
) when
\Omega
is a unit ball.
They proved the existence of at least two positive solutions for
c>0
small enough.
Pierotti and Verzini
[
20
]
investigated (
1.5
) if
\Omega
is a general bounded domain via a different method.
They showed that for
c>0
small enough, there exist at least two positive solutions, one corresponds to a local minimizer of
E
on
S_{c}
and the other one is of mountain pass type
1
1

1

The proof about the existence of the mountain pass type solution in
[
20
]
contained a gap, which was filled in
[
21
]
.
.
The result was then generalized by Pierotti, Verzini and Yu
[
21
]
to deal with the Sobolev critical case, where an energy estimate is performed to overcome the lack of compactness.
According to the above discussion, it is natural to consider the following open problem:

If
\Omega
is a bounded domain with a nontrivial topology, does (
1.5
) have a positive solution for
p>2^{*}
?

We remark that a related open problem was proposed by Rabinowitz for the Dirichlet problem (
1.2
), which was stated by Brezis in
[
6
]
.
The above problem can be seen as a prescribed mass version of Rabinowitz’s open problem.
We shall give an affirmative answer to it when
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is an annulus with
N=4,8,16
.
Indeed, we have studied a more general problem for dimensions
N\geq 3
in the intermediate steps and we will address why it cannot be applied to other dimensions in the final section.

1.2
Main results

Inspired from the Hopf fibration in
[
24
,
25
]
, the problem (
1.5
) with
N=4,8,16
can be reduced to the following problem in a lower dimension case

\begin{cases}-\Delta v+\frac{\lambda}{2|x|}v=\frac{1}{2|x|}v^{p-1}&\text{ in }D,\\
v(x)=0,&\text{ on }\partial D,\\
\int_{D}\frac{1}{2|x|}v^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=\hat{c}^{2},\end{cases}

(1.6)

where
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{M}
is also an annulus,
M=3,5,9
and
\hat{c}
is another constant determined by
c
(see details in Section 2).
Therefore, to obtain the existence results for (
1.5
), it suffices to study the existence of positive solutions to (
1.6
).
More generally, we will study the existence and stability of positive solutions for a more general class of equations

\begin{cases}-\Delta u+\lambda|x|^{\mu}u=|x|^{\mu}f(u)&\text{ in }D,\\
u(x)=0,&\text{ on }\partial D,\\
\int_{D}|x|^{\mu}u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=c^{2},\end{cases}

(1.7)

where
D=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{M}:a<|x|<b,0<a<b<+\infty\}
is an annulus,
M\geq 3
,
c>0
is a prescribed constant,
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
. Solutions of (
1.7
) can be viewed as critical points of the energy functional

E(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{D}|\nabla u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{D}|x|^{\mu}F(u)\,\mathrm{d}x

on the manifold

{\mathcal{S}}_{c}=\{u\in H^{1}_{0}(D):\int_{D}|x|^{\mu}u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=c^{2}\},

where
F(u)=\int_{0}^{u}f(s)\,\mathrm{d}s
.
That is, every
u\in{\mathcal{S}}_{c}
satisfying
E^{\prime}|_{{\mathcal{S}}_{c}}(u)=0
is a solution to (
1.7
) for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.
It is worth noting that when we set
\mu=-1
and
f(u)=|u|^{p-2}u
, (
1.7
) goes back to (
1.6
) in
M=3,5,9
up to a constant.

We will assume the following conditions on the nonlinearity
f
:

(
\mathbf{A_{1}}
)
f\in C^{1}([0,+\infty)),f(s)>0
for
s>0
, and
\lim\limits_{s\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{f(s)}{s}=0
.

(
\mathbf{A_{2}}
) There exists some
2<q<\frac{2M}{M-2}
and
a_{0}>0
such that

\lim_{s\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{f(s)}{s^{q-1}}=a_{0}.

(
\mathbf{A_{3}}
) There exists a constant
a_{1}>a_{0}(q-1)
and some
R>0
such that

a_{1}|s|^{q-2}\leq f^{\prime}(s),\quad\text{ for }s\geq R,

Without loss of generality, we will also assume that
f
is extended to the whole real line by setting
f(s)=0
for
s<0
.

Under the above assumptions, the functional
E
is well-defined and of class
C^{2}
on
\mathcal{S}_{c}
.

Remark 1.1
.

Notice that there are many nonlinearities satisfying the above conditions.
For example:

(i)

The pure power nonlinearity
f(u)=Cu^{p-1}
, where
C>0
and
2<p<\frac{2M}{M-2}
;

(ii)

The mixed power nonlinearity
f(u)=C_{1}u^{p_{1}-1}+C_{2}u^{p_{2}-1}
, where
C_{1},C_{2}>0
and
2<p_{1},p_{2}<\frac{2M}{M-2}
.

The main result of this paper is the following theorem:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
M\geq 3
.
Assume that
f
satisfies the assumptions (
\mathbf{A_{1}}
) and (
\mathbf{A_{2}}
).
Denote the mass critical exponent in dimension
M
by
2^{\#}_{M}=2+\frac{4}{M}
, then the following conclusions hold:

(i)

If
2<q<2^{\#}_{M}
, then for every
c>0
, (
1.7
) has a positive solution, which corresponds to a global minimizer of
E
on
{\mathcal{S}}_{c}
;

(ii)

If
q=2^{\#}_{M}
, then there exists some
c_{1}>0
such that for every
0<c<c_{1}
, (
1.7
) has a positive solution, which corresponds to a global minimizer of
E
on
{\mathcal{S}}_{c}
;

(iii)

If
2^{\#}_{M}<q<\frac{2M}{M-2}
, then there exists some
c_{2}>0
such that if
0<c<c_{2}
, then (
1.7
) has a local minimizer of
E
on
{\mathcal{S}}_{c}
.
If in addition
f
satisfies the assumption (
\mathbf{A_{3}}
), then there exists a second positive solution, which is of mountain pass type for
E
on
{\mathcal{S}}_{c}
.

In particular, if we consider the case
M=3,5,9
, by reversing the reduction process, we can obtain the existence of positive solutions for the supercritical problem (
1.5
) in dimension
4,8
and
16
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is an annulus and
N=4,8,16
. Denote the reduced Sobolev critical exponent by

\tilde{2}^{*}=\begin{cases}6&\text{ if }N=4,\\
\frac{10}{3}&\text{ if }N=8,\\
\frac{18}{7}&\text{ if }N=16,\end{cases}

and the reduced mass critical exponent by

\tilde{2}^{\#}=\begin{cases}\frac{10}{3}&\text{ if }N=4,\\
\frac{14}{5}&\text{ if }N=8,\\
\frac{22}{9}&\text{ if }N=16.\end{cases}

Then the following conclusions hold:

(i)

If
2<p<\tilde{2}^{\#}
, then for every
c>0
, (
1.5
) has a positive solution, which corresponds to a global minimizer of
E
on
{\mathcal{S}}_{c}
;

(ii)

If
p=\tilde{2}^{\#}
, then there exists some
c_{3}>0
small such that for every
0<c<c_{3}
, (
1.5
) has a positive solution, which corresponds to a global minimizer of
E
on
{\mathcal{S}}_{c}
;

(iii)

If
\tilde{2}^{\#}<p<\tilde{2}^{*}
, then there exists some
c_{4}>0
such that if
0<c<c_{4}
, then (
1.5
) has two positive solutions, one corresponds to a local minimizer of
E
on
{\mathcal{S}}_{c}
and the other corresponds to a mountain pass level for
E
on
{\mathcal{S}}_{c}
.

Remark 1.2
.

In other dimensions
N\neq 4,8,16
, the reduction method based on the Hopf fibration is still valid.
However, the reduced problem will be defined on a warped product manifold rather than an annulus in the Euclidean space.
This will bring some additional difficulties in studying the existence of solutions to the reduced problem.
We will discuss this issue in the final section.

Remark 1.3
.

Notice that the original Sobolev critical exponent
2^{*}
in these cases are given by

2^{*}=\begin{cases}4&\text{ if }N=4,\\
6&\text{ if }N=8,\\
\frac{10}{3}&\text{ if }N=16.\end{cases}

So, the reduced Sobolev critical exponent
\tilde{2}^{*}
is larger than the original Sobolev critical exponent
2^{*}
.
Thus, the results in Theorem
1.2
are indeed new results for some Sobolev supercritical nonlinearities.

1.3
Plan of the paper

In Section 2, we will first introduce the reduction method based on Hopf fibration in detail. From the reduction method, we can give a full description of the reduced problem (
1.6
).
Then we will introduce some useful results, such as the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality and some properties of the limiting equation.

In Section 3 the case
2<q\leq 2^{\#}_{M}
will be discussed. We will use the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality to show that the energy functional
E
is bounded from below on
{\mathcal{S}}_{c}
and coercive, so the existence of a global minimizer can be obtained by the Ekeland variational principle and a compactness lemma.

In Section 4 the case
2^{\#}_{M}<q<\frac{2M}{M-2}
will be discussed. We will use the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality again to show that
E
has a local minimizer on
{\mathcal{S}}_{c}
for
c>0
small. With the local minimizer, we can construct a mountain pass geometry for
E
on
{\mathcal{S}}_{c}
. To find a mountain pass type solution, we will use the monotonicity trick and a blow-up analysis to show the boundedness of a special Palais-Smale sequence. Therefore, a mountain pass type solution will be obtained by a compactness lemma.

Finally, in Section 5 we give some comments and discuss some open problems based on this work, which will be discussed in the future.

An appendix is also given to show the full details of the Hopf reduction process.

Preliminaries

2.1
The Hopf reduction method

We consider the following problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta u+\lambda|x|^{\alpha}u=|x|^{\alpha}f(u)&\text{ in }A,\\
u=0&\text{ on }\partial A,\\
\int_{A}|x|^{\alpha}u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=c^{2},\end{cases}

(2.1)

where
c>0
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, and
A=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:a<|x|<b,0<a<b<+\infty\right\}
is an annulus in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
N=4,8,16
.
We employ a transformation based on the Hopf fibration, a method originally introduced by Ruf and Srikanth in
[
24
]
for
N=4
and later generalized in
[
25
]
for
N=8,16
.

We regard the annulus as a product space
(a,b)\times S^{N-1}
. The Hopf fibration is illustrated by the following diagrams:

These group actions are free, meaning

is

, or

Each

can be identified with a function defined in
H^{1}_{0}((a,b)\times\mathbb{FP}^{\,1})
, where
\mathbb{FP}^{\,1}
represents
\mathbb{CP}^{\,1},\mathbb{HP}^{\,1}
, or
\mathbb{OP}^{\,1}
.
Specifically, there exists a function

defined on
(a,b)\times\mathbb{FP}^{\,1}
such that

This identification allows us to reduce problem (

) to a lower-dimensional problem.
Setting
M=3,5,9
for
N=4,8,16
respectively, the reduced problem is defined on an annulus
D=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{M}:a^{2}/2<|x|<b^{2}/2\}
in
\mathbb{R}^{M}

\begin{cases}-\Delta v+\lambda(2|x|)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}v=(2|x|)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}f(v)&\text{ in }D,\\
v=0&\text{ on }\partial D,\\
\int_{D}(2|x|)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}v^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=\hat{c}^{2},\end{cases}

(2.2)

where
\hat{c}=c\omega_{N-M}^{-\frac{1}{2}}
. Here
\omega_{N-M}
denotes the surface area of the unit sphere

in
\mathbb{R}^{N-M}
. The details of this reduction process are provided in the Appendix. Note that this problem can be rewritten in the form of (

) with
\mu=\frac{\alpha}{2}-1
.

Remark 2.1
.

For the case

,

\theta_{3}\in[0,\pi/2)
represents the angle between the planes

. If we consider functions in
H_{0}^{1}(A)
invariant under the group action

T_{\tau}\left(r,\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3}\right)=\left(r,\theta_{1}+\tau,\theta_{2}+\tau,\theta_{3}\right),\quad\tau\in[0,2\pi),

then problem (
2.1
) reduces to the following problem in
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
:

\begin{cases}-\Delta v+\lambda|x|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}v=|x|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}f(v)&\text{ in }D,\\
v=0&\text{ on }\partial D,\\
\int_{D}|x|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}v^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=\hat{c}^{2},\end{cases}

where
D=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:a^{2}/2<|x|<b^{2}/2\right\}
and
\hat{c}=(\frac{c^{2}}{2\pi}\cdot 2^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}})^{\frac{1}{2}}>0
. This is a special case of (
2.2
) with
M=3
.

2.2
Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality

The Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality
[
16
]
plays a crucial role in the discussion of prescribed mass problems. Specifically, for any
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{M}
and any
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,

\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}\leq C_{M,p}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\gamma_{p}p}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{(1-\gamma_{p})p},

(2.3)

where
\gamma_{p}=\frac{M(p-2)}{2p}>0
. Equality holds if and only if
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{M}
and
u
is equal to
U_{0}
up to translations and dilation, where
U_{0}
is the unique positive solution of the problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta U_{0}+U_{0}=U_{0}^{p-1}&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{M},\\
U_{0}>0&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{M}.\end{cases}

Moreover,
U_{0}(x)=U_{0}(|x|)
has Morse index
1
and satisfies the exponential decay estimate:

\left|D^{\alpha}U_{0}(x)\right|\leq C_{1}\exp(-\sigma|x|),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{M},

for some
C_{1},\sigma>0
and all
|\alpha|\leq 2
.
Furthermore, for any
\lambda_{0},\mu_{0}>0
, the unique positive solution of the problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta U+\lambda_{0}U=\mu_{0}U^{p-1}&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{M},\\
U>0&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{M},\end{cases}

is explicitly given by

U_{\lambda_{0},\mu_{0}}(x)=\left(\frac{\mu_{0}}{\lambda_{0}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-2}}U_{0}\left(\sqrt{\lambda_{0}}x\right).

3
A global minimizer

From now on, we will study the existence of positive solutions for the problem (
1.7
).
We first show that the energy functional
E
is coercive on
{\mathcal{S}}_{c}
for any
c>0
if
2<q<2^{\sharp}_{M}
and for
c>0
small enough if
q=2^{\sharp}_{M}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that the nonlinearity
f
satisfies the assumptions (
\mathbf{A_{1}}
) and (
\mathbf{A_{2}}
), then
E(v)
is coercive on
{\mathcal{S}}_{c}
if one of the following conditions holds:

(i)

2<q<2^{\#}_{M}
for any
c>0
;

(ii)

q=2^{\#}_{M}
for
c>0
sufficiently small.

As a consequence, in both cases we have
\inf_{{\mathcal{S}}_{c}}E(v)>-\infty
.

Proof.

By (
\mathbf{A_{1}}
) and (
\mathbf{A_{2}}
), for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists some
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

F(s)\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}|s|^{2}+\frac{C_{\varepsilon}}{q}|s|^{q},\quad s\in\mathbb{R}.

We will only give a proof the lemma for
\mu\geq 0
and the proof for
\mu<0
is similar.
Using the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
2.3
), we have

\displaystyle E(v)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}-\int_{D}|x|^{\mu}F(v)\mathrm{d}x

(3.1)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}-\int_{D}|x|^{\mu}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}|v|^{2}+\frac{C_{\varepsilon}}{q}|v|^{q}\right)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}c^{2}-\frac{C_{\varepsilon}}{q}b^{\mu}\|v\|_{L^{q}(D)}^{q}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}c^{2}-\frac{C_{\varepsilon}}{q}b^{\mu}C_{M,q}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{\gamma_{q}q}\|v\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{(1-\gamma_{q})q}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}c^{2}-\frac{C_{\varepsilon}}{q}b^{\mu}C_{M,q}\left(\frac{c^{2}}{a^{\mu}}\right)^{(1-\gamma_{q})\frac{q}{2}}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{\gamma_{q}q}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}-C_{1}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{\gamma_{q}q}-C_{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2-\gamma_{q}q}-C_{1}\right)\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{\gamma_{q}q}-C_{2},

where
C_{1}
depends on the parameters
M,q,a,b,c,\varepsilon,\mu
and
C_{2}
depends on
c,\varepsilon
.

Proof of (1): Since
2<q<2^{\sharp}_{M}
,
0<\gamma_{q}q<2
. So as
\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(D)}\rightarrow+\infty
, we have
\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2-\gamma_{q}q}-C_{1}\rightarrow+\infty
, so
E(v)
is coercive in
{\mathcal{S}}_{c}
.

Proof of (2): Since
q=2^{\sharp}_{M}
,
\gamma_{q}q=2
. By (
3.1
), if the mass
c>0
is sufficiently small, then the constant
C_{1}
is also sufficiently small such that
\frac{1}{2}-C_{1}>0
. So in this case
E(v)
is also coercive in
{\mathcal{S}}_{c}
.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

From the proof of Lemma
3.1
, if we fix the inner radius
a>0
and let the outer radius
b\rightarrow+\infty
, then the constant
C_{1}
will tend to infinity when
\mu>0
or tend to
0
when
\mu<0
.
Therefore, this method cannot be used to show that
E(v)
is bounded from below on
{\mathcal{S}}_{c}
when
D
is an exterior domain and
\mu>0
.
We will discuss the exterior problem in the final section.

To prove the existence of a global minimizer, we also need the following compactness lemma. First, we give the definition of a Palais-Smale sequence for
E|_{{\mathcal{S}}_{c}}
.

Definition 3.1
.

A sequence
\left\{v_{n}\right\}\subset{\mathcal{S}}_{c}
is called a Palais-Smale sequence of
E|_{{\mathcal{S}}_{c}}
if

E\left(v_{n}\right)\text{ is bounded and }\left\|E^{\prime}|_{{\mathcal{S}}_{c}}\left(v_{n}\right)\right\|_{H^{-1}}\rightarrow 0\quad\text{ as }n\rightarrow+\infty.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\left\{v_{n}\right\}\subset{\mathcal{S}}_{c}
be a bounded Palais-Smale sequence of
E|_{\mathcal{S}_{c}}
.
Then there exists
v\in{\mathcal{S}}_{c}
such that
v_{n}\rightarrow v
strongly in
H_{0}^{1}(D)
up to a subsequence.

Proof.

Since the sequence
\left\{v_{n}\right\}\subset{\mathcal{S}}_{c}
is bounded in
H_{0}^{1}(D)
, we may deduce that up to a subsequence there is
v\in{\mathcal{S}}_{c}
such that

\displaystyle v_{n}\rightharpoonup v

\displaystyle\text{ in }H_{0}^{1}(D),

\displaystyle v_{n}\rightarrow v

\displaystyle\text{ in }L^{p}(D),\quad\forall p\in\left[2,2^{*}\right),

\displaystyle v_{n}(x)\rightarrow v(x)

\displaystyle\text{ for a.e. }x\in D.

By the definition of the Palais-Smale sequence, there exists
\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\int_{D}\nabla v_{n}\nabla w\,\mathrm{d}x+\lambda_{n}\int_{D}|x|^{\mu}v_{n}w\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{D}|x|^{\mu}f\left(v_{n}\right)w\,\mathrm{d}x=o_{n}(1)

(3.2)

for every
w\in H_{0}^{1}(D)
. Taking
w=v_{n}
in (
3.2
) we have

\lambda_{n}c^{2}=\int_{D}|x|^{\mu}f\left(v_{n}\right)v_{n}\mathrm{d}x-\int_{D}\left|\nabla v_{n}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+o_{n}(1).

Therefore the boundedness of
\left\{v_{n}\right\}
in
H_{0}^{1}(D)
implies the boundedness of
\lambda_{n}
, thus there exists
\bar{\lambda}\in\mathbb{R}
, up to subsequence, such that
\lambda_{n}\rightarrow\bar{\lambda}
. Replacing
w
with
v_{n}-v
in (
3.2
), we have

\int_{D}\nabla v_{n}\nabla\left(v_{n}-v\right)\mathrm{d}x+\lambda_{n}\int_{D}|x|^{\mu}v_{n}\left(v_{n}-v\right)\mathrm{d}x-\int_{D}|x|^{\mu}f\left(v_{n}\right)\left(v_{n}-v\right)\mathrm{d}x=o_{n}(1).

(3.3)

By the weak convergence of
v_{n}
in
H_{0}^{1}(D)
, we have

\int_{D}\nabla v\nabla(v_{n}-v)+\bar{\lambda}\int_{D}|x|^{\mu}v\left(v_{n}-v\right)\mathrm{d}x=o_{n}(1)

(3.4)

Combining (
3.3
), (
3.4
) and the strong convergence of
v_{n}
in
L^{p}(D)
and the convergence of
\lambda_{n}
, we obtain

\displaystyle o_{n}(1)=

\displaystyle\int_{D}\left|\nabla(v_{n}-v)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\left(\lambda_{n}-\bar{\lambda}\right)\int_{D}|x|^{\mu}v\left(v_{n}-v\right)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\lambda_{n}\int_{D}|x|^{\mu}\left(v_{n}-v\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}x-\tau\int_{D}|x|^{\mu}f\left(u_{n}\right)\left(v_{n}-v\right)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{D}\left|\nabla\left(v_{n}-v\right)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+o_{n}(1).

Thus,
v_{n}\rightarrow v
strongly in
H_{0}^{1}(D)
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
(i) and (ii).

(i) For any
c>0
, by Proposition
3.1
, we have
\inf\limits_{{\mathcal{S}}_{c}}E(v)>-\infty
.
Therefore, we can find a minimizing sequence
\{v_{n}\}\subset{\mathcal{S}}_{c}
associated to
\inf_{{\mathcal{S}}_{c}}E(v)
.
From the coerciveness of
E(v)
on
{\mathcal{S}}_{c}
, we can deduce that
\{v_{n}\}
is bounded in
H_{0}^{1}(D)
.
By condition (
\mathbf{A_{1}}
) we may substitute
\{v_{n}\}
with
\{|v_{n}|\}
so we can assume that
\{v_{n}\}
is nonnegative.
Using the Ekeland variational principle (see
[
27
, Theorem 2.4]
), we can deduce that, by choosing another minimizing sequence if necessary,
\{v_{n}\}
is a bounded nonnegative Palais-Smale sequence in
H_{0}^{1}(D)
.
So using Lemma
3.2
, we can find a global minimizer
v_{c}\geq 0
that attains the minimum:
E(v_{c})=\inf\limits_{{\mathcal{S}}_{c}}E(v)
.
By the strong maximum principle,
v_{c}>0
in
D
.

(ii) The proof is similar to (i) if we choose
c_{1}>0
sufficiently small such that
E(v)
is coercive on
{\mathcal{S}}_{c}
for any
0<c<c_{1}
.
∎

4
A local minimizer and a mountain pass solution

4.1
Existence of a local minimizer

We now assume that
2^{\sharp}_{M}<q<\frac{2M}{M-2}
.
In this case, the functional
E(v)
is unbounded from below on
{\mathcal{S}}_{c}
so we cannot hope to find a global minimizer.
However, it is not hard to find a local minimizer when the mass
c
is sufficiently small.

For future convenience, we introduce the following auxiliary problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta v+\lambda|x|^{\mu}v=\tau|x|^{\mu}f(v)&\text{ in }D,\\
v=0&\text{ on }\partial D,\\
\int_{D}|x|^{\mu}v^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=c^{2},\end{cases}

(4.1)

where
\tau\in[\frac{1}{2},1]
. Solutions to this problem can be found as critical points of the energy functional

E_{\tau}(v)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{D}|\nabla v|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x-\tau\int_{D}|x|^{\mu}F(v)\,\mathrm{d}x

on
{\mathcal{S}}_{c}
. In particular, when
\tau=1
we are back to the problem (
1.7
).

In order to investigate the geometric structure of
E_{\tau}
, we define the sets

\mathcal{B}_{\rho}=\left\{u\in{\mathcal{S}}_{c}:\int_{D}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x<\rho\right\},\quad\mathcal{U}_{\rho}=\left\{u\in{\mathcal{S}}_{c}:\int_{D}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x=\rho\right\}.

Suppose that
\varphi_{1}\in\mathcal{S}_{c}
is the first eigenfunction that satisfies the problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta\varphi_{1}=\lambda_{1}\varphi_{1}&\text{ in }D,\\
\varphi_{1}=0&\text{ on }\partial D.\end{cases}

where
\lambda_{1}
is the first eigenvalue of
-\Delta
in
H_{0}^{1}(D)
.
By a direct calculation,
\varphi_{1}\in\mathcal{B}_{\rho}
if

\begin{array}[]{ll}\rho\geq\lambda_{1}b^{-\mu}c^{2}&\text{ when }\mu>0,\\
\rho\geq\lambda_{1}c^{2}&\text{ when }\mu=0,\\
\rho\geq\lambda_{1}a^{-\mu}c^{2}&\text{ when }\mu<0,\end{array}

in which case
\mathcal{B}_{\rho}
is nonempty.
Now, we will show that the functional
E_{\tau}
has a local minimizer when the mass
c
is sufficiently small.

Lemma 4.1
.

If
2^{\sharp}_{M}<q<\frac{2M}{M-2}
, then there is a
c_{2}>0
such that when
0<c<c_{2}
, for some
\rho^{*}>0
we have

m_{\tau}:=\inf_{\mathcal{B}_{\rho^{*}}}E_{\tau}(v)<\inf_{\mathcal{U}_{\rho^{*}}}E_{\tau}(v).

(4.2)

Proof.

By (
\mathbf{A_{1}}
) and (
\mathbf{A_{2}}
), for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists some
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

F(s)\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}|s|^{2}+\frac{C_{\varepsilon}}{q}|s|^{q},\quad s\in\mathbb{R}.

We will only proof the lemma for
\mu\geq 0
and the proof for
\mu<0
is similar.
For
v\in\mathcal{U}_{\rho}
, we have

\displaystyle E_{\tau}(v)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}-\tau\int_{D}|x|^{\mu}F(u)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}-\tau\int_{D}|x|^{\mu}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}|v|^{2}+\frac{C_{\varepsilon}}{q}|v|^{q}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}-\tau\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}c^{2}+\frac{C_{\varepsilon}}{q}b^{\mu}\|v\|_{L^{q}(D)}^{q}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\rho-\tau\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}c^{2}+\frac{C_{\varepsilon}}{q}b^{\mu}C_{M,q}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{\gamma_{q}q}\|v\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{(1-\gamma_{q})q}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\rho-\tau\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}c^{2}+\frac{C_{\varepsilon}}{q}b^{\mu}C_{M,q}\left(\frac{c^{2}}{a^{\mu}}\right)^{(1-\gamma_{q})\frac{q}{2}}\rho^{\frac{\gamma_{q}q}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\rho-C_{1}\tau c^{2}-C_{2}\tau\rho^{\frac{\gamma_{q}q}{2}}c^{(1-\gamma_{q})q}

\displaystyle:=f(\rho),

where
C_{1}=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
,
C_{2}
depends on
M,q,a,b,\varepsilon,\mu
. Now we have

f^{\prime}(\rho)=\frac{1}{2}-C_{2}\tau\frac{\gamma_{q}q}{2}\rho^{\frac{\gamma_{q}q}{2}-1}c^{(1-\gamma_{q})q}.

(4.3)

Because of
\frac{\gamma_{q}q}{2}>1
, we may deduce that there is a unique
\rho^{*}
such that
f^{\prime}(\rho^{*})=0
, i.e.

\displaystyle f(\rho^{*})=\max f(\rho)=C_{2}\tau\left(\frac{\gamma_{q}q}{2}-1\right)(\rho^{*})^{\frac{\gamma_{q}q}{2}}c^{(1-\gamma_{q})q}-C_{1}\tau c^{2}.

So we have

\displaystyle\inf_{\mathcal{U}_{\rho^{*}}}E_{\tau}(v)\geq C_{2}\tau\left(\frac{\gamma_{q}q}{2}-1\right)(\rho^{*})^{\frac{\gamma_{q}q}{2}}c^{(1-\gamma_{q})q}-C_{1}\tau c^{2}.

On the other hand, we have

\inf_{\mathcal{B}_{\rho^{*}}}E_{\tau}(v)\leq E_{\tau}(\varphi_{1})\leq\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{1}\tau a^{-\mu}c^{2}.

As a consequence, (
4.1
) holds if we have

C_{2}\tau\left(\frac{\gamma_{q}q}{2}-1\right)(\rho^{*})^{\frac{\gamma_{q}q}{2}}c^{(1-\gamma_{q})q}-C_{1}\tau c^{2}\\
>\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{1}\tau a^{-\mu}c^{2},

that is,

C_{2}\left(\frac{\gamma_{q}q}{2}-1\right)(\rho^{*})^{\frac{\gamma_{q}q}{2}}c^{(1-\gamma_{q})q-2}>C_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{1}a^{-\mu},

(4.4)

and the condition

\rho^{*}\geq\lambda_{1}b^{-\mu}c^{2}

(4.5)

also needs to be satisfied to guarantee
\mathcal{B}_{\rho^{*}}
is non-empty.

Notice that (
4.3
) shows that
\rho^{*}
increases as the mass
c
decreases. So for condition (
4.5
), as the mass
c
decreases the left-hand side increases and the right-hand side decreases. So there is a
c_{1}^{*}>0
such that (
4.5
) holds when
0<c<c_{1}^{*}
.

For condition (
4.4
), from
M\geq 3
we have
(1-\gamma_{q})q-2<0
. So for
c
decreases the left-hand side increases. So there is a
c_{2}^{*}>0
such that (
4.4
) holds when
0<c<c_{2}^{*}
. As a result, if we choose
c_{2}=\min\{c_{1}^{*},c_{2}^{*}\}
, (
4.2
) holds. The proof for the case
\mu<0
is similar.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
(iii): Existence of a local minimizer.

By Proposition
4.1
, for any
c\in\left(0,c_{2}\right)
there exists a minimizing sequence
\{v_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{B}_{\bar{\rho}}
associated to
m=m_{1}
, by (
\mathbf{A_{1}}
) we can assume that
v_{n}\geq 0
for every
n
. From the Ekeland variational principle, by choosing another minimizing sequence if necessary,
\{v_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{B}_{\rho}
is a bounded nonnegative Palais-Smale sequence for
E_{1}=E
at level
m
. Using Lemma
3.2
, we can find a minimizer
\tilde{v}_{c}\geq 0
such that
E(\tilde{v}_{c})=m
. By the strong maximum principle,
\tilde{v}_{c}>0
in
D
.
∎

4.2
The mountain pass geometry

With the local minimizer above, we can also show the mountain pass geometry of the functional
E_{\tau}
on
{\mathcal{S}}_{c}
uniformly with respect to
\tau\in[\frac{1}{2},1]
.

Proposition 4.2
.

For each
c\in(0,c_{2})
, there exist
w_{1},w_{2}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{c}
independent of
\tau
, such that

\tilde{m}_{\tau}:=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\max_{t\in[0,1]}E_{\tau}(\gamma(t))>\max\left\{E_{\tau}\left(w_{1}\right),E_{\tau}\left(w_{2}\right)\right\},\forall\tau\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right],

where
\Gamma=\left\{\gamma\in C\left([0,1],{\mathcal{S}}_{c}\right)\mid\gamma(0)=w_{1},\gamma(1)=w_{2}\right\}
.

Proof.

First we notice that from (
4.3
), the value
\rho^{*}
decreases as
\tau\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right]
decreases.
So we may assume that
\rho^{*}
is the one corresponding to
\tau=\frac{1}{2}
. Now (
4.4
) and (
4.5
) hold for any
\tau\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right]
when
0<c<c_{2}
.

Now we can choose
w_{1}=\varphi_{1}
, which is independent of
\tau
. To obtain
w_{2}
we choose
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}\left(B_{1}(0)\right)
with
\phi>0
in
B_{1}(0)
and
\int_{B_{1}(0)}\phi^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=1
, and
x_{1}\in D
. For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we may define

v_{k}(x)=\chi k^{\frac{M}{2}}\phi\left(k\left(x-x_{1}\right)\right),x\in D,

where
\chi>0
is chosen such that
\int_{D}|x|^{\mu}v_{k}^{2}\mathrm{d}x=c^{2}
. By definition,
\operatorname{supp}\left(v_{k}\right)\subset B_{1/k}\left(x_{1}\right)\subset D
for
k
sufficiently large. Thus, for
k
sufficiently large,
v_{k}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{c}
.
Now

c^{2}=\int_{D}|x|^{\mu}v_{k}^{2}\mathrm{d}x\sim\chi^{2}\int_{B_{1}(0)}\phi^{2}\mathrm{d}x=\chi^{2}.

Thus we have
\chi\sim c
. By a straightforward calculation, it follows that as
k\rightarrow+\infty
,

\left\|v_{k}\right\|_{H_{0}^{1}(D)}\sim c\left(k^{2}\int_{B_{1}(0)}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+1\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\rightarrow+\infty.

By (
\mathbf{A_{2}}
) there exists some
R_{0}>0
such that

F(s)\geq\frac{a_{0}}{2q}|s|^{q},\quad\forall|s|\geq R_{0}.

If we denote
D_{k}=\left\{x\in D:|v_{k}(x)|\geq R_{0}\right\}
, then for
\mu\geq 0
we have

\displaystyle E_{\tau}\left(v_{k}\right)

\displaystyle\leq E_{{1}/{2}}\left(v_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{D}|\nabla v_{k}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{2}\int_{D}|x|^{\mu}F\left(v_{k}\right)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{D}|\nabla v_{k}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{a_{0}}{4q}\int_{D_{k}}|x|^{\mu}|v_{k}|^{q}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{D}|\nabla v_{k}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{a_{0}}{4q}a^{\mu}\int_{D_{k}}|v_{k}|^{q}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\chi^{2}k^{2}}{2}\int_{B_{1}(0)}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\frac{a_{0}R_{0}^{q}}{4q}|D|-\frac{a_{0}\chi^{q}k^{\frac{M(q-2)}{2}}}{4q}\int_{B_{1}(0)}|\phi|^{q}\mathrm{d}x\rightarrow-\infty

as
k\rightarrow+\infty
since
M(q-2)/2>2
when
q>2^{\sharp}_{M}
. The case
\mu<0
is similar by interchanging
a
and
b
. So we can take a
k_{0}>0
sufficiently large and independent of
\tau
, and let
w_{2}=u_{k_{0}}
. Then we have

\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla w_{2}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}x>2\rho^{*}\text{ and }E_{\tau}\left(w_{2}\right)\leq E_{\frac{1}{2}}\left(w_{2}\right)<m_{\frac{1}{2}},\forall\tau\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right].

(4.6)

Now we let
\Gamma
and
m_{\tau}
be defined as in the statement of the proposition for our choice of
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
, then
\Gamma\neq\emptyset
holds since

\gamma_{0}(t)=\frac{c}{\left\|(1-t)w_{1}+tw_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\left[(1-t)w_{1}+tw_{2}\right],\quad t\in[0,1]

belongs to
\Gamma
. In particular, since
w_{1}\in\mathcal{B}_{\rho^{*}}
, (
4.6
) infers that for any
\gamma\in\Gamma
,
\gamma([0,1])
intersects
\mathcal{U}_{\rho^{*}}
. Therefore, by definition

\tilde{m}_{\tau}:=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\max_{t\in[0,1]}E_{\tau}(\gamma(t))\geq\inf_{\mathcal{U}_{\rho^{*}}}E_{\tau}>\max\left\{E_{\tau}\left(w_{1}\right),E_{\tau}\left(w_{2}\right)\right\},\forall\tau\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right],

which completes the proof.
∎

4.3
The abstract framework of monotonicity trick

To establish the existence of normalized solutions of mountain pass type for problem (
4.1
), we need to use a variant of the monotonicity trick in
[
5
]
. We first introduce some notations and definitions.

Let
(E,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
and
(H,(\cdot,\cdot))
be two infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces such that
E\hookrightarrow H\hookrightarrow E^{\prime}
with continuous injections. For simplicity, we suppose that
E\hookrightarrow H
has norm at most 1 and identify
E
with its image in
H
. We define

\begin{cases}\|u\|^{2}=\langle u,u\rangle,\\
|u|^{2}=(u,u),\end{cases}

for each
u\in E
, and

\mathcal{S}_{c}=\left\{u\in E|\,|u|^{2}=c\right\},\forall c>0.

Obviously,
\mathcal{S}_{c}
is a submanifold of
E
of codimension 1 and its tangent space at a given point
u\in\mathcal{S}_{c}
is given by

T_{u}\mathcal{S}_{c}=\{v\in E\mid(u,v)=0\}.

Denote by
\|\cdot\|_{*}
and
\|\cdot\|_{**}
, respectively, the operator norm of
\mathcal{L}(E,\mathbb{R})
and
\mathcal{L}(E,\mathcal{L}(E,\mathbb{R}))
.

Definition 4.1

(
[
5
, Definition 1.1, Definition 1.3, Definition 1.4]
)
.

Let
\phi:E\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a
C^{2}
-functional on
E
.

(i)

We say that
\phi^{\prime}
and
\phi^{\prime\prime}
are
\alpha
-Hölder continuous on bounded sets for some
\alpha\in(0,1]
if for any
R>0
one can find
M=M(R)>0
such that, for any
u_{1},u_{2}\in B_{R}(0)
,

\left\|\phi^{\prime}-\phi^{\prime}\right\|_{*}\leq M\left\|u_{1}-u_{2}\right\|^{\alpha},\left\|\phi^{\prime\prime}-\phi^{\prime\prime}\right\|_{**}\leq M\left\|u_{1}-u_{2}\right\|^{\alpha}.

(ii)

For any
u\in E
, we define the continuous bilinear map

D^{2}\phi(u)=\phi^{\prime\prime}(u)-\frac{\phi^{\prime}(u)\cdot u}{|u|^{2}}(\cdot,\cdot).

(iii)

For any
u\in\mathcal{S}_{c}
and
\theta>0
, we define the approximate Morse index by

\displaystyle\tilde{m}_{\theta}(u)=

\displaystyle\sup\left\{\operatorname{dim}L\mid L\text{ is a subspace of }T_{u}\mathcal{S}_{c}\text{ such that }D^{2}\phi(u)[\varphi,\varphi]<-\theta\|\varphi\|^{2},\forall\varphi\in L\backslash\{0\}\right\}.

If
u
is a critical point for the constrained functional
\left.\phi\right|_{\mathcal{S}_{c}}
and
\theta=0
, we denote
\tilde{m}(u)
is the Morse index of
u
as constrained critical point.

Now we can state the abstract result of the monotonicity trick.

Lemma 4.3

(
[
5
, Theorem 1.5]
)
.

Let
I\subset(0,+\infty)
be an interval and consider a family of
C^{2}
functional
\Phi_{\rho}:E\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
of the form

\Phi_{\rho}(u)=A(u)-\rho B(u),\quad\rho\in I,

where
B(u)\geq 0
for every
u\in E
, and

\text{ either }A(u)\rightarrow+\infty\text{ or }B(u)\rightarrow+\infty\text{ as }u\in E\text{ and }\|u\|\rightarrow+\infty\text{. }

Suppose moreover that
\Phi_{\rho}^{\prime}
and
\Phi_{\rho}^{\prime\prime}
are
\alpha
-Hölder continuous on bounded sets for some
\alpha\in(0,1]
. Finally, suppose that there exist
w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{c}
(independent of
\rho
) such that, setting

\Gamma=\left\{\gamma\in C\left([0,1],\mathcal{S}_{c}\right)\mid\gamma(0)=w_{1},\gamma(1)=w_{2}\right\},

we have

c_{\rho}=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\max_{t\in[0,1]}\Phi_{\rho}(\gamma(t))>\max\left\{\Phi_{\rho}\left(w_{1}\right),\Phi_{\rho}\left(w_{2}\right)\right\},\quad\rho\in I.

Then, for almost every
\rho\in I
, there exist sequences
\left\{u_{n}\right\}\subset\mathcal{S}_{c}
and
\zeta_{n}\rightarrow 0^{+}
such that, as
n\rightarrow+\infty
,

(i)

\Phi_{\rho}\left(u_{n}\right)\rightarrow c_{\rho}
;

(ii)

\left\|\Phi_{\rho}^{\prime}\mid_{\mathcal{S}_{c}}(u_{n})\right\|_{*}\rightarrow 0
;

(iii)

\left\{u_{n}\right\}
is bounded in
E
;

(iv)

\tilde{m}_{\zeta_{n}}\left(u_{n}\right)\leq 1
.

Now we can use the abstract framework above to attain a mountain pass critical point of
E_{\tau}
for almost every
\tau\in[\frac{1}{2},1]
. For our propose, in the following we denote
E=H_{0}^{1}(D)
and
H=L^{2}(D)
, equipped with another inner product

(u,v)_{H}=\int_{D}|x|^{\mu}uv\,\mathrm{d}x

and norm

\|v\|_{H}=\left(\int_{D}|x|^{\mu}v^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Due to the boundedness of
D
, the norm
\|\cdot\|_{H}
is equivalent to the standard norm of
L^{2}(D)
for any
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
0<c<c_{2}
. For almost every
\tau\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right]
, there exists
\left(v_{\tau},\lambda_{\tau}\right)\in\mathcal{S}_{c}\times\mathbb{R}
which solves (
4.1
). Moreover,
m\left(v_{\tau}\right)\leq 2
.

Proof.

We can utilize Lemma
4.3
to the family of functionals
E_{\tau}
, with
E=H_{0}^{1}(D)
,
H=L^{2}(D)
equipped with the inner product above,
c_{\rho}=\tilde{m}_{\tau}
and
\Gamma
defined in Proposition
4.2
. If we set

A(v)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{D}|\nabla v|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\quad\text{ and }\quad B(v)=\tau\int_{D}|x|^{\mu}F(v)\,\mathrm{d}x,

then
A(v)
and
B(v)
satisfy the assumptions in Lemma
4.3
. Since
E_{\tau}
is
C^{2}
on
{\mathcal{S}}_{c}
,
E_{\tau}^{\prime}
and
E_{\tau}^{\prime\prime}
are local Hölder continuous on
{\mathcal{S}}_{c}
. Thus all the assumptions of Lemma
4.3
are satisfied.

Therefore, for almost
\tau\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right]
, there exists a bounded Palais-Smale sequence
\left\{v_{n,\tau}\right\}\subset H_{0}^{1}(D)
for
E_{\tau}
constrained on
{\mathcal{S}}_{c}
at the level
\tilde{m}_{\tau}
and a sequence
\left\{\zeta_{n}\right\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}
with
\zeta_{n}\rightarrow 0^{+}
satisfying
\tilde{m}_{\zeta_{n}}\left(v_{n,\tau}\right)\leq 1
. For simplicity, we still denote
v_{n,\tau}
by
v_{n}
. Since the map
v\mapsto|v|
is continuous, and
E_{\tau}(v)=E_{\tau}(|v|)
, we may assume that
v_{n}\geq 0
. Using Lemma
3.2
for the functional
E_{\tau}
, we derive that there exists
0\leq v_{\tau}\in\mathcal{S}_{c}
such that
v_{n}\rightarrow v_{\tau}
in
H_{0}^{1}(D)
, which implies that, for some
\lambda_{\tau}\in\mathbb{R},\left(v_{\tau},\lambda_{\tau}\right)
satisfies the problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta v_{\tau}+\lambda_{\tau}|x|^{\mu}v_{\tau}=\tau|x|^{\mu}f(v_{\tau})&\text{ in }D,\\
v_{\tau}=0&\text{ on }\partial D,\\
\int_{D}|x|^{\mu}v_{\tau}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=c^{2}.\end{cases}

By the strong maximum principle it follows that

in
D
.

Now we will show that
m\left(v_{\tau}\right)\leq 2
. First, we deduce that
\tilde{m}(v_{\tau})\leq 1
. Suppose by contradiction that
\tilde{m}(v_{\tau})\geq 2
. Then there exists a subspace

with
\operatorname{dim}W=2
such that

D^{2}E_{\tau}(v_{\tau})[\varphi,\varphi]<0,\quad\forall\varphi\in W\backslash\{0\}.

Since
W
is finite-dimensional, by the continuity of
D^{2}E_{\tau}
and we have that there exists some
\delta>0
such that

D^{2}E_{\tau}(v)[\varphi,\varphi]<-\delta\|\varphi\|^{2},\quad\forall\varphi\in W\backslash\{0\}.

Since
D^{2}E_{\tau}
is
\alpha
-Hölder continuous on bounded sets, we have that there exists some
\bar{\delta}>0
such that if
\|v-v_{\tau}\|<\bar{\delta}
, then

D^{2}E_{\tau}(v)[\varphi,\varphi]<-\frac{\delta}{2}\|\varphi\|^{2},\quad\forall\varphi\in W\backslash\{0\}.

Since
v_{n}\rightarrow v_{\tau}
in
H_{0}^{1}(D)
, there exists some
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all

,

, which implies that

D^{2}E_{\tau}(v_{n})[\varphi,\varphi]<-\frac{\delta}{2}\|\varphi\|^{2},\quad\forall\varphi\in W\backslash\{0\}.

Therefore,
\tilde{m}_{\zeta_{n}}(v_{n})\geq 2
for all

, contradicting the fact that
\tilde{m}_{\zeta_{n}}(v_{n})\leq 1
. Thus, we have
\tilde{m}(v_{\tau})\leq 1
. Since
{\mathcal{S}}_{c}
is of codimension
1
in

m(v_{\tau})\leq\tilde{m}(v_{\tau})+1\leq 2
.
∎

4.4
The blow-up analysis

Using Lemma
4.4
, we can establish that there exists a sequence
\tau_{n}\rightarrow 1^{-}
and a corresponding sequence of mountain pass critical points
\left\{v_{\tau_{n}}\right\}
of
E_{\tau_{n}}
on
{\mathcal{S}}_{c}
at the level
\tilde{m}_{\tau_{n}}
with a Morse index
m\left(v_{\tau_{n}}\right)\leq 2
. In order to attain a mountain pass critical point of the original problem, we need to demonstrate the convergence of
\left\{v_{\tau_{n}}\right\}
to a critical point
v
of
E|_{{\mathcal{S}}_{c}}
. To achieve this, a crucial step is to establishing the boundedness of
\left\{v_{\tau_{n}}\right\}
in
H_{0}^{1}(D)
, which can be obtained if the sequence of Lagrange multiplier
\left\{\lambda_{\tau_{n}}\right\}
is bounded. So in this subsection, we use a blow-up analysis method originated from
[
9
]
(see also
[
20
,
8
]
) to achieve this goal.

For notational convenience, we denote
v_{n}:=v_{\tau_{n}}
,
\tilde{m}_{n}:=\tilde{m}_{\tau_{n}}
,
E_{n}:=E_{\tau_{n}}
. We will analyse the behavior of a sequence of positive solutions
\{v_{n}\}\subset{\mathcal{S}}_{c}
of the following problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta v_{n}+\lambda_{n}|x|^{\mu}v_{n}=\tau_{n}|x|^{\mu}f(v_{n})&\text{ in }D,\\
v_{n}=0&\text{ on }\partial D,\\
\end{cases}

(4.7)

as
\lambda_{n}\rightarrow+\infty
, where
\tau_{n}\rightarrow 1^{-}
,
m\left(v_{n}\right)\leq 2
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Step 1:
If
\lambda_{n}\rightarrow+\infty
, then
\{v_{n}\}
blows up along any sequence of local maximum points.

Lemma 4.5
.

Assume that (
\mathbf{A_{1}}
) and (
\mathbf{A_{2}}
) hold. Let
\left\{v_{n}\right\}\subset H_{0}^{1}(D)
be positive solutions to (
4.7

\lambda_{n}\rightarrow+\infty

P_{n}
be a local maximum point for

C>0
independent of

v_{n}\left(P_{n}\right)\geq C\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{q-2}}.

(4.8)

Proof.

By elliptic regularity arguments, we can deduce that
v_{n}\in C^{2}(D)
. From (
\mathbf{A_{1}}
) and (
\mathbf{A_{2}}
), there exists some
C>0
such that

f(s)\leq C(|s|+|s|^{q-1}),\quad\forall s\in\mathbb{R}.

Using the fact that
P_{n}
is a local maximum point for

\Delta v_{n}\left(P_{n}\right)\leq 0
. So by (

\lambda_{n}\left|P_{n}\right|^{\mu}v_{n}\left(P_{n}\right)\leq\tau_{n}\left|P_{n}\right|^{\mu}f\left(v_{n}\left(P_{n}\right)\right),

i.e.

\lambda_{n}v_{n}\left(P_{n}\right)\leq f\left(v_{n}\left(P_{n}\right)\right)\leq C(v_{n}\left(P_{n}\right)+\left(v_{n}\left(P_{n}\right)\right)^{q-1}),

Assume that
v_{n}\left(P_{n}\right)<1
, then we have
\lambda_{n}v_{n}\left(P_{n}\right)\leq C
for all
n
. Therefore
v_{n}\left(P_{n}\right)\rightarrow 0
. This implies that
v_{n}\rightarrow 0
uniformly on
D
, contradicting the fact that
v_{n}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{c}
. So
v_{n}\left(P_{n}\right)\geq 1
. Therefore,

\lambda_{n}v_{n}\left(P_{n}\right)\leq C\left(v_{n}\left(P_{n}\right)\right)^{q-1},

which implies (
4.8
).
∎

Step 2:
We deduce a precise behavior of the sequence
\left\{v_{n}\right\}
near the local maximum points
P_{n}
of

as
\lambda_{n}\rightarrow+\infty

(ii)

(iii)

\begin{cases}-\Delta V+|P|^{\mu}V=a_{0}|P|^{\mu}V^{q-1}&\text{ in }\,\mathbb{R}^{M},\\

(4.9)

(iv)

(4.10)

(v)

(4.11)

Proof.

We first define a rescaled function

\tilde{V}_{n}(y):=\tilde{\varepsilon}_{n}^{\frac{2}{q-2}}v_{n}\left(\tilde{\varepsilon}_{n}y+P_{n}\right)\text{ for }y\in\tilde{D}_{n}:=\frac{D-P_{n}}{\tilde{\varepsilon}_{n}}.

\begin{cases}-\Delta\tilde{V}_{n}(y)=\tau_{n}\left|\tilde{\varepsilon}_{n}y+P_{n}\right|^{\mu}\frac{f(\tilde{\varepsilon}_{n}^{-\frac{2}{q-2}}\tilde{V}_{n}(y))}{\tilde{\varepsilon}_{n}^{-\frac{2(q-1)}{q-2}}\tilde{V}_{n}^{q-1}(y)}\tilde{V}_{n}^{q-1}(y)-\tilde{\varepsilon}_{n}^{2}\lambda_{n}\left|\tilde{\varepsilon}_{n}y+P_{n}\right|^{\mu}\tilde{V}_{n}(y)&\text{ in }\tilde{D}_{n},\\

\displaystyle\int_{\tilde{D}}|x|^{\mu}f^{\prime}(v_{n}(x))\phi_{i,n}^{2}(x)\mathrm{d}x

\bar{v}_{n}
satisfies

\begin{cases}-\Delta\bar{v}_{n}+\lambda_{n}\bar{\varepsilon}_{n}^{2}|\bar{\varepsilon}_{n}y+P_{n}|^{\mu}\bar{v}_{n}=\tau_{n}|\bar{\varepsilon}_{n}y+P_{n}|^{\mu}\frac{f\left(\bar{\varepsilon}_{n}^{-\frac{2}{q-2}}\bar{v}_{n}\right)}{\left(\bar{\varepsilon}_{n}^{-\frac{2}{q-2}}\bar{v}_{n}\right)^{q-1}}\bar{v}_{n}^{q-1}\quad\text{ in }D_{n},\\
\bar{v}_{n}>0\quad\text{ in }D_{n},\\
\bar{v}_{n}=0\quad\text{ on }\partial D_{n},\end{cases}

where
D_{n}=\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{M}:\bar{\varepsilon}_{n}y+P_{n}\in D\right\}
. Similar to the proof of Lemma
4.6
, up to a subsequence we may assume that
\bar{v}_{n}\rightarrow\bar{v}
in
C_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1}\left(H\right)
as
n\rightarrow+\infty
, where
H
is either
\mathbb{R}^{M}
or a half space of
\mathbb{R}^{M}
. Moreover,
\bar{v}
satisfies

\begin{cases}-\Delta\bar{v}=a_{0}|P|^{\mu}\bar{v}^{q-1}\quad\text{ in }H,\\
\bar{v}>0\quad\text{ in }H,\\
\bar{v}=0\quad\text{ on }\partial H,\end{cases}

where
P=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow+\infty}P_{n}\in\bar{D}
, and
m(\bar{v})\leq 2
. By the Liouville-type results in
[
3
,
10
]
we know that such problem does not admit any positive solution with finite Morse index, which is a contradiction. Therefore, the sequence
\left\{v_{n}\right\}
is bounded in
H_{0}^{1}(D)
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
(iii): The mountain pass type solution.

Using Lemma
4.4
, we can deduce that for any
c\in\left(0,c_{2}\right)
, there exists a sequence
\tau_{n}\rightarrow 1^{-}
and a corresponding sequence
\left\{v_{n}\right\}\subset\mathcal{S}_{c}
which are mountain pass type critical points of
E_{\tau_{n}}
, such that
E_{\tau_{n}}\left(v_{n}\right)=\tilde{m}_{\tau_{n}}
and
m\left(v_{n}\right)\leq 2
. Now we may apply Lemma
4.9
, it follows that
\left\{v_{n}\right\}
is a bounded Palais-Smale sequence for
E
. Then by Lemma
3.2
for the functional
E_{\tau_{n}}
, we obtain the conclusion.
∎

5
Comments and open problems

In this paper, we have studied the existence of normalized solutions for the nonlinear Schrödinger equation with a large class of nonlinearities involving the Sobolev supercritical growth on an annulus in some particular dimensions. A natural and interesting question is whether these results can be extended to other cases. We will give some comments on these problems below, which will be discussed in our following works.

(i) In
[
25
]
the authors have given a reduction method for any even dimension
N\geq 4
. Indeed, there exists the following higher dimensional Hopf fibrations:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S^{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S^{2n+1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{CP}^{\,n}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S^{3}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S^{4n+3}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{HP}^{\,n}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S^{7}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S^{15}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{OP}^{\,1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

Using these fibrations, the problem (
1.6
) can be reduced to the following problem:

\begin{cases}-\Delta v+\lambda\frac{1}{(qs)^{2-\frac{2+\alpha}{q}}}v=\frac{1}{(qs)^{2-\frac{2+\alpha}{q}}}f(v)&\text{ in }\mathcal{M},\\
v=0&\text{ on }\partial\mathcal{M},\\
\int_{\mathcal{M}}\frac{1}{(qs)^{2-\frac{2+\alpha}{q}}}v^{2}\mathrm{d}x=c^{2},\end{cases}

(5.1)

where
\mathcal{M}=(c,d)\times_{g}\mathbb{B}
is a warped product manifold of dimension
m
with
\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{CP}^{\,n},\mathbb{HP}^{\,n},\mathbb{OP}^{\,1}
, the warped function
g(s)=\beta s
with
s=\pi_{1}(x)\in(c,d)
denotes the projection onto the first coordinate of
\mathcal{M}
, and
\beta=\frac{n-2}{m-2}
(see
[
25
,
19
]
for more details). Notice that when
N=4,8,16
the manifold
\mathcal{M}
is flat and these cases have been studied in this paper. However, in other cases the manifold
\mathcal{M}
is not flat, which brings some new difficulties:

•

Due to the non-homogeneous properties of the metric on
\mathcal{M}
, the prove of the local minimizer and the mountain pass geometry of the energy functional corresponding to (
5.1
) will be more complicated.

•

The blow-up analysis is more complicated due to the curvature of the manifold
\mathcal{M}
.

(ii) Another reduction method for any dimension
N=2m
with
m\geq 2
has been given in
[
18
]
. If one consider
\mathbb{R}^{2m}
as the product of two copies
\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
and consider the doubly symmetric function in
\mathbb{R}^{2m}
:

u\left(y_{1},y_{2}\right)=u\left(\left|y_{1}\right|,\left|y_{2}\right|\right),\quad y_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{m},i=1,2,

then the problem (
1.6
) can be reduced to the following problem in
\mathbb{R}^{m+1}
:

\begin{cases}-\Delta v+\lambda\frac{1}{2|z|}v=\frac{1}{2|z|}f(v)&\text{ in }D,\\
v=0&\text{ on }\partial D,\\
\int_{D}\frac{1}{2|z|}v^{2}\mathrm{d}z=c^{2},\end{cases}

(5.2)

where
D=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{m+1}:a^{2}/2<|z|<b^{2}/2\right\}
.
Notice that in this case the function space of
v
is

H_{\#}(D)=\left\{v\in H_{0}^{1}(D):v\left(z_{1},\ldots,z_{m},z_{m+1}\right)=v\left(\sqrt{z_{1}^{2}+\cdots+z_{m}^{2}},\left|z_{m+1}\right|\right)\right\},

which makes the blow-up analysis more complicated. Indeed, if one wants to use the blow-up analysis in Section 4, due to the axial symmetry of the function, if one local maximum point
P_{n}
is not at the
z_{m+1}
-axis, there must be infinite many local maximum points, which makes the energy estimate impossible. A method to overcome this difficulty is to consider the function
v
as a function in the two dimensional space
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, i.e.,
v(y)=v\left(r,s\right)
with
r=\sqrt{z_{1}^{2}+\cdots+z_{m}^{2}}
and
s=z_{m+1}
. Then the problem will be transformed to the following problem in two dimension

\begin{cases}-v_{rr}-v_{ss}-\frac{1}{r}v_{r}-\frac{1}{s}v_{s}+\lambda\frac{1}{2\sqrt{r^{2}+s^{2}}}v=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{r^{2}+s^{2}}}f(v)&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
v>0&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
v=0&\text{ on }D_{1}\cup D_{2},\\
\partial v/\partial\nu=0&\text{ on }D_{3}\cup D_{4},\\
\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2\sqrt{r^{2}+s^{2}}}v^{2}r\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}s=c^{2},\end{cases}

(5.3)

where
\Omega=\left\{(r,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:a^{2}/2<\sqrt{r^{2}+s^{2}}<b^{2}/2,r>0,s>0\right\}
with boundary
\partial\Omega=D_{1}\cup D_{2}\cup D_{3}\cup D_{4}
as shown in the following figure.

Problem (

This will involve careful consideration of the behavior of solutions near the boundary, the interaction between the Dirichlet and Neumann parts of the boundary, and the new exponential decay estimates away from the blow-up points, which are all challenging problems. (iii) Finally, it is also interesting to study the problem (
1.2
) in the exterior domain, i.e.,
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\overline{B_{a}(0)}
with
a>0
,
N=4,8,16
. The reduction method in Section 2.1 is still valid in this case, so the problem may be reduced to the following problem:

\begin{cases}-\Delta v+\frac{\lambda}{2|x|}v=\frac{1}{2|x|}v^{p-1}&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{M}\backslash\overline{B_{a^{2}/2}(0)},\\
v=0&\text{ on }\partial B_{a^{2}/2}(0),\\
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{M}\backslash\overline{B_{a^{2}/2}(0)}}\frac{1}{2|x|}v^{2}\mathrm{d}x=c^{2}.\end{cases}

where
M=3,5,9
respectively for
N=4,8,16
. This problem corresponds to the problem (
1.7
) with
\mu=-1
,
f(v)=v^{p-1}
, and
b=+\infty
. However, since the domain is unbounded, it brings some new difficulties:

•

The compactness of the Sobolev embedding
H_{0}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{M}\backslash\overline{B_{a^{2}/2}(0)}\right)\hookrightarrow L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{M}\backslash\overline{B_{a^{2}/2}(0)}\right)
for
2\leq p<2^{*}
is lost, so some new techniques need to be developed to overcome this difficulty, even in the case
2<p<2^{\#}_{M}
.

•

The eigenfunction
\varphi_{1}
of
-\Delta
in
H_{0}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{M}\backslash\overline{B_{a^{2}/2}(0)}\right)
does not exist, so the local minimizer structure and the mountain pass geometry are no longer clear. To overcome this difficulty, one may consider the problem in a large annulus
A_{R}
with radius
R>a^{2}/2
and then let
R\rightarrow+\infty
, like the method in
[
4
]
. However, to pass to the limit
R\rightarrow+\infty
, firstly the uniform boundedness of the
H_{0}^{1}\left(A_{R}\right)
-norm of the solutions is crucial, which is very challenging to obtain. The blow-up analysis in Section 4 may be helpful to deal with this problem, but some new difficulties may arise due to the varying and unbounded domain.

Appendix A
The reduction process

In this appendix, we give the detailed reduction process from (
2.1
) to (
2.2
) when
N=4,8,16
. First we can write the Laplace operator in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
in spherical coordinates

\Delta u=u_{rr}+\frac{N-1}{r}u_{r}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\Delta_{S^{N-1}}u.

Now we consider the function space
H_{0,G}^{1}(A)
defined in Section 2.1. By
[
25
]
, the Hopf quotient maps are harmonic morphisms, which implies that the Laplace-Beltrami operator is preserved under the group action, i.e., respectively for
N=4,8,16
,

\Delta_{S^{3}}\rightarrow\Delta_{\mathbb{CP}^{\,1}},\quad\Delta_{S^{7}}\rightarrow\Delta_{\mathbb{HP}^{\,1}},\quad\Delta_{S^{15}}\rightarrow\Delta_{\mathbb{OP}^{\,1}}.

We want to show that the Laplace operator of a
G
-invariant function
u\in H_{0,G}^{1}(A)
can be reduced to the Laplace operator of a function
v
defined on the corresponding projective line
\mathbb{FP}^{\,1}
, i.e. we want to show

\Delta v=v_{ss}+\frac{M-1}{s}v_{s}+\zeta\frac{1}{s^{2}}\Delta_{\mathbb{FP}^{\,1}}v

for some constant
\zeta>0
,
s\in(c,d)
corresponds to the radial coordinate
r\in(a,b)
and
M=3,5,9
respectively for
N=4,8,16
.

Notice that if we set
s=\frac{1}{2}r^{2}
and
v(s,\cdot)=u(r,\cdot)
, then the radial part of the Laplace operator can be transformed as

u_{rr}+\frac{N-1}{r}u_{r}=r^{2}[v_{ss}+\frac{M-1}{s}v_{s}].

Thus,

r^{2}[v_{ss}+\frac{M-1}{s}v_{s}+\frac{1}{r^{4}}\Delta_{S^{N-1}}v]=2s[v_{ss}+\frac{M-1}{s}v_{s}+\frac{1}{4s^{2}}\Delta_{S^{N-1}}v].

So the Hopf reduction implies that the reduced Laplace operator in
(s,\mathbb{FP}^{\,1})
coordinates is

\Delta v=v_{ss}+\frac{M-1}{s}v_{s}+\frac{1}{4s^{2}}\Delta_{\mathbb{FP}^{\,1}}v.

Using the stereographic projection, we can identify
\mathbb{FP}^{\,1}
with
S^{M-1}
, so the Laplace-Beltrami operator
\Delta_{\mathbb{FP}^{\,1}}
can be identified with
\Delta_{S^{M-1}}
. Thus, the reduced problem is

\begin{cases}-\Delta v+\lambda\frac{1}{(2|x|)^{2-\frac{2+\alpha}{2}}}v=\frac{1}{(2|x|)^{2-\frac{2+\alpha}{2}}}f(v)&\text{ in }D,\\
v=0&\text{ on }\partial D,\\
\int_{D}\frac{1}{(2|x|)^{2-\frac{2+\alpha}{2}}}v^{2}\mathrm{d}x=\hat{c}^{2},\end{cases}

where
D=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{M}:a^{2}/2<|x|<b^{2}/2\right\}
and
\hat{c}=c\omega_{N-M}^{-\frac{1}{2}}
with
\omega_{N-M}
being the volume of the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{N-M}
. The reduction process is complete.

Numerical Ergodicity and Optimal Strong Error Estimates for a Class of Novel Tamed Schemes to Superlinear SPDEs
Zhihui LIU, Jie SHEN

Abstract
. We construct a class of novel tamed schemes for superlinear stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs), including the stochastic Allen–Cahn equation driven by either multiplicative or additive noise. The schemes preserve the same Lyapunov structure as the original system, and we rigorously establish their longtime unconditional stability. Furthermore, we prove that the corresponding Galerkin-based fully discrete tamed schemes inherit the unique ergodicity of the underlying SPDEs and achieve optimal strong convergence rates in both the multiplicative and additive noise cases.

1.
Introduction

The longtime numerical behavior — in particular, the numerical ergodicity — of superlinear SPDEs whose coefficients violate the classical Lipschitz continuity and linear growth assumptions remains an intriguing and challenging problem. As a fundamental longtime property, ergodicity characterizes the equivalence between temporal and spatial averages (the so-called ergodic limit) for Markov processes and chains generated by SPDEs and their numerical discretizations, respectively. This concept is pivotal in diverse fields such as quantum mechanics, fluid dynamics, and financial mathematics
[
12
]
,
[
14
]
.

In practical applications, a central goal is to compute the ergodic limit, i.e., the mean of a given function with respect to the equilibrium distribution. This distribution is also known as the invariant measure. However, obtaining an explicit expression for the invariant measure of an infinite-dimensional stochastic system is typically infeasible. Consequently, it is generally impossible to compute the ergodic limit directly for nonlinear SPDEs driven by multiplicative noise. This challenge has motivated a substantial body of research over the past decade, leading to the development of numerical algorithms that preserve the ergodicity of the original system and efficiently approximate its ergodic limit.

In this paper, we investigate the superlinear SPDE

(1.1)

\displaystyle{\rm d}X(t,\xi)=(\Delta X(t,\xi)+f(X(t,\xi))){\rm d}t+g(X(t,\xi)){\rm d}W(t,\xi),

(t,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathscr{O}
, with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition
X(t,\xi)=0
,
(t,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\partial\mathscr{O}
, and initial condition
X(0,\cdot)=X_{0}(\cdot)
. Here,
\mathscr{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d=1,2,3
) is a bounded open set with a piecewise smooth boundary,
f,g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
are assumed to be monotone-type with certain polynomial growth, and
W
is an infinite-dimensional Wiener process (cf. Section
2
for more details).

Eq. (
1.1
) includes the following stochastic Allen–Cahn equation (SACE), arising from phase transition in materials science by stochastic perturbation, as a special case:

(1.2)

\displaystyle{\rm d}X(t,\xi)=\Delta X(t,\xi){\rm d}t+\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}(X(t,\xi)-X(t,\xi)^{3}){\rm d}t+g(X(t,\xi)){\rm d}W(t,\xi),

where the positive index
\epsilon\ll 1
is the interface thickness; see, e.g.,
[
2
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
25
]
,
[
26
]
,
[
32
]
and references therein.
Our aim is to construct efficient numerical integrators for Eq. (
1.1
), whose solution is uniquely ergodic with respect to an equilibrium distribution, that satisfy the following two properties:

(1)

exhibit ergodicity with respect to approximate equilibrium distributions of Eq. (
1.1
) on infinite time intervals;

(2)

converge strongly with the optimal rate to the solutions of Eq. (
1.1
) on any finite time intervals.

Significant progress has been made in the design and analysis of numerical approximations for ergodic limits in finite-dimensional settings, particularly for stochastic ordinary differential equations
[
19
]
,
[
24
]
,
[
27
]
,
[

]
,
[

]
,

The backward Euler method and its Galerkin-based full-discretizations satisfy properties (1) and (2) above; however, they often entail high computational costs. For instance,
[
23
]
discusses the exponential ergodicity of SACE driven by multiplicative trace-class noise with a moderately thick interface. In contrast,
[

[

Schemes that treat the nonlinearity explicitly are often preferred to reduce computational costs.
To construct such explicit schemes that can inherit the unique ergodicity of Eq. (
1.1
), as in the tamed methods studied for the finite-dimensional case (e.g.,
[
17
]
,
[

]

[

[
1
]
demonstrated that the classical Euler–Maruyama (EM) scheme and its Galerkin-based full-discretizations, when applied to Eq. (
1.1

p

1.1

2.1
and
[
23
]

To address this issue, we propose a family of novel explicit-in-nonlinearity tamed schemes that achieve the two main objectives (1) and (2) for superlinear SPDEs (
1.1
) driven by the more subtle and challenging multiplicative noise. Compared to existing tamed schemes in the literature, such as those in
[
3
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
36
]
, our schemes are more efficient to implement because they tame the nodal values rather than their norms at each step. Moreover, our carefully chosen taming functions (in (
2.11
)) preserve the coupled monotonicity (in (
3.1
)), thereby maintaining the Lyapunov structure that conventional taming disrupts.

A key step of our approach is the rigorous analysis of the longtime unconditional stability of the temporal-tamed scheme and its Galerkin-based full discretizations. This analysis demonstrates that these fully discrete tamed schemes preserve the same Lyapunov structure as the original superlinear equation (
1.1
). Combined with the equivalence of transition probabilities under a certain nondegeneracy condition, these tamed schemes are shown to inherit the unique ergodicity of (
1.1
).

On the other hand, as pointed out in
[
28
]
, even for Langevin systems, pure sampling methods can achieve (1) but typically do not approximate the exact solution. In contrast, integrators satisfy (2), yet they often diverge on infinite time intervals or become ergodic with respect to a different equilibrium distribution. Therefore, it is crucial to assess the impact of these tamed methods on the dynamics of (
1.1
) through strong error estimates.

To address the second objective and to derive higher-order strong convergence rates in the additive noise case (see Theorem
5.1
), we point out that, unlike previous strong error analyses in
[
2
,
13
,
23
,
25
,
26
,
34
]
, our proof requires the
L^{s}
-regularity (with
s>2
) of the exact solution, along with the
\dot{H}^{1}
-regularity of the auxiliary process associated with the tamed scheme. Furthermore, in the 3D case, such regularity cannot be controlled by the
\dot{H}^{1}
-norm alone (see Remark
5.1
), whereas it is not required in the multiplicative noise case (see Theorem
4.1
).

Our main contributions are as follows:

•

the construction of a class of computationally efficient tamed schemes for superlinear SPDEs that preserve the Lyapunov stucture and unique ergodicity;

•

a rigorous proof that the solutions of these new schemes are unconditionally stable over any time interval;

•

the establishment of strong convergence of these tamed schemes with optimal spatial and temporal orders for both the multiplicative and additive noise cases.

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first construction of explicit-in-nonlinearity schemes that preserve the unique ergodicity of superlinear SPDEs driven by either additive or multiplicative noise. We emphasize that our results hold in one, two, and three space dimensions, thereby significantly improving the finite-time stability results in prior work
[
16
]
for another type of tamed scheme and removing the dimension restrictions imposed therein, while requiring weaker regularity assumptions on the exact solution of Eq. (
1.1
). We also note that, since the first version of this paper appeared, our taming idea has been applied in
[
18
]
to obtain longtime weak error estimates for SACE driven by additive noise and in
[
7
]
and
[
8
]
to establish uniform-in-time strong and weak/ergodic error estimates for Eq. (
2.6
) with certain additional dissipativity conditions, respectively.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section

2.3.
Tamed Schemes

To construct nonlinearity-explicit schemes for Eq. (
2.6
) when the coercive condition (
2.1
) involves superlinear drift and diffusion coefficients, we employ a taming technique that simultaneously tames both the nonlinear drift and the diffusion.

More precisely, we consider the following nonlinearity-explicit tamed EM (TEM) scheme applied to Eq. (
2.6
):

(2.10)

\displaystyle Z_{n}=Z_{n-1}+AZ_{n}\tau+F_{\tau}(Z_{n-1})\tau+G_{\tau}(Z_{n-1})\delta_{n-1}W;\quad Z_{0}=X_{0},

where
\tau\in(0,1]
is the temporal step-size,
\delta_{n-1}W:=W(t_{n})-W(t_{n-1})
,
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, and the tamed drift and diffusion functions are defined by

(2.11)

\displaystyle f_{\tau}(\xi):=\frac{f(\xi)}{(1+\tau|\xi|^{2q})^{1/2}},\quad g_{\tau}(\xi):=\frac{g(\xi)}{(1+\sqrt{\tau}|\xi|^{q})^{1/2}},\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}.

We refer to
[
28
]
for a similar taming technique in the finite-dimensional setting.

The TEM scheme (
2.10
) is linearly implicit and therefore admits a unique pathwise solution. Moreover,
\{Z_{n},\mathcal{F}_{t_{n}}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is clearly a (time-homogeneous) Markov chain.

\displaystyle\leq\|Z_{n-1}\|^{2}+2\tau^{2}\|F_{\tau}(Z_{n-1})\|^{2}+2\tau\langle Z_{n-1},F_{\tau}(Z_{n-1})\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+2\|G_{\tau}(Z_{n-1})\delta_{n-1}W\|^{2}+2\langle Z_{n-1},G_{\tau}(Z_{n-1})\delta_{n-1}W\rangle.

Note that
Z_{n-1}\sim\mathcal{F}_{t_{n-1}}
and
\delta_{n-1}W
is independent of
\mathcal{F}_{t_{n-1}}
.
Then, taking the conditional expectation
\mathbb{E}_{n-1}[\cdot]
on both sides of the above equation and using Itô isometry and the fact

\mathbb{E}_{n-1}\|G_{\tau}(Z_{n-1})\delta_{n-1}W\|^{2}=\tau\|G_{\tau}(Z_{n-1})\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}^{0}}^{2},

we derive

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{n-1}\|Z_{n}\|^{2}+2\tau\mathbb{E}_{n-1}\|\nabla Z_{n}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|Z_{n-1}\|^{2}+2\tau[\langle Z_{n-1},F_{\tau}(Z_{n-1})\rangle+\tau\|F_{\tau}(Z_{n-1})\|^{2}+\|G_{\tau}(Z_{n-1})\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}].

We conclude (
3.2
) by (
3.1
) with
T_{3}:=2T_{1}
and
T_{4}:=2T_{2}
for all
\tau\in(0,\tau_{\max}]
.
∎∎

Remark 3.1
.

Recall that a Borel function
V:H\to[0,+\infty]
is called a Lyapunov functional of a Markov chain
\{(Z_{n},\mathcal{F}_{t_{n}})\}
, if its sublevel sets

K_{R}:=\{x\in H:V(x)\leq R\}
are compact in
H
for any sufficiently large
R>0
and the drift condition

PV(x):=\mathbb{E}[V(Z_{n})\mid Z_{n-1}=x]\leq\beta V(x)+\kappa
holds for some constants
\beta\in(0,1)
and
\kappa>0
and for any
x\in H
,
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
.
The drift condition yields that
a_{n}\leq\beta a_{n-1}+\kappa
with
a_{n}=\mathbb{E}V(Z_{n})
,
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
.
Recursively iterating the previous inequality, we obtain

a_{n}\leq\beta^{n}a_{0}+\kappa\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\beta^{k}\leq a_{0}+\frac{\kappa}{1-\beta}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
.
Thus, taking the supremum over
n
shows that
\{Z_{n}^{0}\}
(starting from
X_{0}=0
) is unconditionally stable on an infinite time horizon:

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}\mathbb{E}V(Z_{n}^{0})\leq V(0)+\frac{\kappa}{1-\beta}
.
In applications, this drift condition can be weakened as
\sup_{\mu\in\Lambda}\mu(V)<+\infty
, with
\mu(V):=\int_{H}V(x)\,\mu(dx)
, for a sequence
\Lambda
of probability measures (usually taken as the empirical occupation measures
\mu_{N}(B):=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathbb{P}(Z^{0}_{n}\in B)
,
N\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
,
B\in\mathcal{B}(H)
) in
H
, which yields the tightness of
\Lambda
(see, e.g.,
[
11
, Proposition 6.8]
).

The estimate (
3.2
), together with the compact embedding
\dot{H}^{1}\subset H
, implies that
V(\cdot):=\|\cdot\|_{1}^{2}
(or
V(\cdot):=\|\cdot\|^{2}+2\tau\|\nabla\cdot\|^{2}
) is also a Lyapunov functional for the TEM scheme (
2.10
).
Indeed, we take
\tau<\min\{\tau_{\max},1/T_{4}\}
so that
\beta:=1-T_{4}\tau\in(0,1)
and
PV(x)\leq\beta V(x)+\kappa
with
V(\cdot):=\|\cdot\|^{2}+2\tau\|\nabla\cdot\|^{2}
and
\kappa=T_{3}\tau>0
by (
3.2
).
On the other hand, taking the expectation in (
3.2
) and iterating shows that the scheme (
2.10
) is unconditionally stable on an infinite time horizon:

\displaystyle E\|Z_{n}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq T_{3}\tau+(1-T_{4}\tau)E\|Z_{n-1}\|^{2}\leq\cdots

\displaystyle\leq T_{3}\tau[1+(1-T_{4}\tau)+\cdots+(1-T_{4}\tau)^{n-1}]+(1-T_{4}\tau)^{n}E\|Z_{0}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq T_{3}/T_{4}+e^{-T_{4}\tau n}E\|X_{0}\|^{2},\quad\forall~n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}.

Similarly, taking expectation in (
3.2
) and summing over
1\leq n\leq N
gives

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}\|Z_{n}\|^{2}+2\tau\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}\|\nabla Z_{n}\|^{2}\leq T_{3}\tau N+(1-T_{4}\tau)\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\|Z_{n}\|^{2}.

Rearranging and discarding certain terms, followed by division by
2N
, yields

\displaystyle\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}\|\nabla Z_{n}\|^{2}\tau

\displaystyle\leq\frac{T_{3}+\mathbb{E}\|X_{0}\|^{2}}{2}.

Consequently, for
V(\cdot):=\|\cdot\|_{1}^{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}\mu_{N}(V)

\displaystyle=\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}EV(Z_{n}^{0})

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}\mathbb{E}\|Z_{N}^{0}\|^{2}+\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}\|\nabla Z_{n}^{0}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{T_{3}}{T_{4}}+\frac{T_{3}}{2\tau}<\infty,

thereby, in combination with the classical result
[
11
, Proposition 6.8]
, showing the tightness of
\{\mu_{N}\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}
.
This is comparable to the Lyapunov estimate for Eq. (
2.6
) obtained, for instance, in
[
15
, Theorem 2.2]
.

In this part, we extend the arguments and results of the previous part to construct tamed full discretizations using Galerkin-based spatial approximations, without imposing any additional conditions. Let

\displaystyle Y_{n}^{h}

\displaystyle=Y_{n-1}^{h}+A_{h}Y_{n}^{h}\tau+\mathcal{P}_{h}F_{\tau}(Y_{n-1}^{h})\tau+\mathcal{P}_{h}G(Y_{n-1}^{h})\delta_{n-1}W,

(3.10)

\displaystyle Y^{h}_{n}

\displaystyle=S_{h,\tau}^{n}X^{h}_{0}+\tau\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}S_{h,\tau}^{n-i}\mathcal{P}_{h}F_{\tau}(Y^{h}_{i})+\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}S_{h,\tau}^{n-i}\mathcal{P}_{h}G(Y^{h}_{i})\delta_{i}W.

Similarly to the proof of Theorem

3.3.
Unique Ergodicity of Tamed-FEM and Drift-Tamed-FEM

In this part, we focus on the probabilistic regularity and unique ergodicity of the tamed-FEM (
3.7
) and the drift-tamed-FEM (
3.9
) under the following additional nondegenerate condition.
Consequently, our numerical ergodicity analysis does not apply to the standard linear multiplicative noise case, which is degenerate.

Assumption 3.1
.

For any
u\in H
,
G(u)G(u)^{\top}
is a positive-definite linear operator in
H
, i.e.,

\langle G(u)G(u)^{\top}v,v\rangle>0
,

.

Recall that at the end of Section
2.1
we assumed that
Q
is self-adjoint and positive-definite. It is well known that
Q^{1/2}
is then also self-adjoint and positive-definite. One can easily show the equivalence between the positive-definiteness of
G(u)G(u)^{\top}
and that of
G(u)Q^{1/2}[G(u)Q^{1/2}]^{\top}
; see
[
24
, Remark 4]
.

As noted earlier in this section,
\{Z_{n}^{h},\mathcal{F}_{t_{n}}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{Y_{n}^{h},\mathcal{F}_{t_{n}}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
generated by the tamed-FEM (
3.7
) and the drift-tamed-FEM (
3.9
), respectively, are Markov chains.
Let
P:V_{h}\times\mathcal{B}(V_{h})\to[0,1]
and
\widetilde{P}:V_{h}\times\mathcal{B}(V_{h})\to[0,1]
denote the transition kernels of
\{Z_{n}^{h},\mathcal{F}_{t_{n}}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{Y_{n}^{h},\mathcal{F}_{t_{n}}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, respectively. Then for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
x\in V_{h}
, and
A\in\mathcal{B}(V_{h})
,

(3.11)

\displaystyle P(x,A)=\mathbb{P}(Z_{n+1}^{h}\in A~|~Z_{n}^{h}=x),~{\widetilde{P}}(x,A)=\mathbb{P}(Y_{n+1}^{h}\in A~|~Y_{n}^{h}=x).

We refer to
[
24
]
for the definitions and properties of invariant measure and ergodicity for Markov chains.

We have the following probabilistic regularity for the transition kernels
P
and
{\widetilde{P}}
defined in (
3.11
) associated with (
3.7
) and (
3.9
), respectively.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let Assumption
3.1
hold. For any
\tau\in(0,1)
, the transition kernels
P
and
{\widetilde{P}}
are regular on
\mathcal{B}(V_{h})
, i.e., for every
x\in V_{h}
, the measures
P(x,\cdot)
and
\widetilde{P}(x,\cdot)
are each equivalent to a common reference measure. Consequently, each of the Markov chains — the tamed-FEM (
3.7
) and the drift-tamed-FEM (
3.9
) — admits at most one invariant measure on
\mathcal{B}(V_{h})
.

Proof.

For
x\in V_{h}
and
A\in\mathcal{B}(V_{h})
, from the tamed-FEM (
3.7
) we have

\displaystyle P(x,A)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(Z_{n+1}^{h}\in A|Z_{n}^{h}=x)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(x+\mathcal{P}_{h}F_{\tau}(x)+\mathcal{P}_{h}G_{\tau}(x)\delta_{n}W)\in({\rm Id}-\tau A_{h})(A))

\displaystyle=\mu_{x+\mathcal{P}_{h}F_{\tau}(x),[\mathcal{P}_{h}G_{\tau}(x)][\mathcal{P}_{h}G_{\tau}(x)]^{*}\tau}(({\rm Id}-\tau A_{h})(A)),

where the last equality uses that
x+\mathcal{P}_{h}F_{\tau}(x)+\mathcal{P}_{h}G_{\tau}(x)\delta_{n}W
is normally distributed with mean
x+\mathcal{P}_{h}F_{\tau}(x)
and covariance operator
[\mathcal{P}_{h}G_{\tau}(x)][\mathcal{P}_{h}G_{\tau}(x)]^{*}\tau
, and
\mu_{z,C}
denotes the Gaussian measure on
V_{h}
with mean
z
and covariance operator
C
.

Under Assumption
3.1
, the operator
[\mathcal{P}_{h}G_{\tau}(x)][\mathcal{P}_{h}G_{\tau}(x)]^{*}\tau
is nondegenerate in
\mathcal{L}(V_{h})
(see
[
24
, Remark 4]
). Consequently, by Feldman–Hájek theorem on the finite-dimensional space
V_{h}
, all Gaussian measures of the form
\{\mu_{x,[\mathcal{P}_{h}G(x){Q^{1/2}}][\mathcal{P}_{h}G(x){Q^{1/2}}]^{*}\tau}:x\in V_{h}\}
are equivalent. This shows that
P
is regular. The regularity of
{\widetilde{P}}
follows analogously, which completes the proof.
∎∎

Combining the Lyapunov structure from Theorem
3.1
and Remark
3.2
with the probabilistic regularity established in Proposition
3.1
, we obtain the following ergodicity results for the tamed-FEM (
3.7
) and the drift-tamed-FEM (
3.9
), respectively.
We also note that, following the arguments in
[
24
, Theorem 2]
and
[
30
, Theorem 2.5]

and

.

\tau\in(0,\tau_{\max}]
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let conditions (

) and (

)-(
2.9
) and Assumption

for any
\tau\in(0,{\widetilde{\tau}}_{\max}]

\displaystyle\leq C(h^{1+\gamma}+\tau^{\frac{1+\gamma}{2}})\|F(X)\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{\omega}^{p}L_{\xi}^{2}}\Big(\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\sigma^{-\frac{1+\gamma}{2}}{\rm d}\sigma\Big)

(4.35)

\displaystyle\leq CT^{\frac{1-\gamma}{2}}(h^{1+\gamma}+\tau^{\frac{1+\gamma}{2}})(1+\|X\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{\omega}^{p}\dot{H}^{1}}^{q+1}),\quad\gamma\in[0,1).

The stability property (
4.12
) and the estimates (
4.18
) and (
4.21
) imply

\displaystyle J^{n}_{23}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\|F(X(t_{i}))-F_{\tau}(X(t_{i}))\|_{L_{\omega}^{p}L_{\xi}^{2}}{\rm d}r

(4.36)

\displaystyle\leq CT\tau^{1/2}(1+\|X\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{\omega}^{p(2q+1)}\dot{H}^{1}}^{2q+1}).

For
\gamma\in[0,1)
, combining the above three estimates and Lemma
4.2
implies

\displaystyle J^{n}_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+T)(h^{1+\gamma}+\tau^{1/2})[(1+\|X\|^{q}_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{\omega}^{2pq}\dot{H}^{1}})\|X\|_{\mathcal{C}_{t}^{1/2}L_{\omega}^{2p}L_{\xi}^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+1+\|X\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{\omega}^{p}\dot{H}^{1}}^{q+1}+\|X\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{\omega}^{p(2q+1)}\dot{H}^{1}}^{2q+1}]

(4.37)

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{CT}(h^{1+\gamma}+\tau^{1/2})(1+\|X_{0}\|_{1}^{2q+1}).

The last term
J^{n}_{3}
can be controlled by using the continuous and discrete BDG inequalities (see, e.g.,
[
26
, (2.22)-(2.23)]
) as

\displaystyle(J^{n}_{3})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\|S(t_{n}-r)G(X)-S_{h,\tau}^{n-i}\mathcal{P}_{h}G(X(t_{i}))]\|_{L_{\omega}^{p}\mathcal{L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}{\rm d}r

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\|S_{h,\tau}^{n-i}\mathcal{P}_{h}[G(X)-G(X(t_{i}))]\|_{L_{\omega}^{p}\mathcal{L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}{\rm d}r

\displaystyle\quad+C\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\|E_{h,\tau}(t_{n}-r)G(X)]\|_{L_{\omega}^{p}\mathcal{L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}{\rm d}r:=\sum_{i=1}^{2}(J^{n}_{3i})^{2}.

Using the stability property (
4.12
), conditions (
4.5
)-(
4.6
), the estimate (
4.30
) with
(\mu,\nu)=(1+\gamma,1)
, and estimates (
4.21
)-(
4.22
), we get

\displaystyle J^{n}_{3}

\displaystyle\leq CT^{1/2}\tau^{1/2}\|X\|_{\mathcal{C}_{t}^{1/2}L_{\omega}^{2p}L_{\xi}^{2}}+CT^{\frac{1-\gamma}{2}}(h^{1+\gamma}+\tau^{\frac{1+\gamma}{2}})(1+\|X\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{\omega}^{p}\dot{H}^{1}})

(4.38)

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{CT}(1+\|X_{0}\|_{1}^{q+1})(h^{1+\gamma}+\tau^{1/2}),\quad\gamma\in[0,1).

Putting the estimates (
4.34
), (
4.37
), and (
4.38
) together results in

\displaystyle J^{n}\leq Ce^{CT}(1+\|X_{0}\|_{1}^{2q+1})(h^{1+\gamma}+\tau^{1/2}),\quad\gamma\in[0,1).

This inequality, in combination with the estimate on
J^{0}
that

\displaystyle J^{0}=\|X_{0}-X_{0}^{h}\|_{L_{\omega}^{p}L_{\xi}^{2}}\leq Ch^{1+\gamma}\|X_{0}\|_{1+\gamma},\quad\gamma\in[0,1],

completes the proof of (
4.32
) with
\gamma\in[0,1)
.

To show (
4.32
) for

, we just need to give refined estimates for
J^{n}_{22}
and
J^{n}_{32}
provided

and (

) holds.
Applying Minkowski inequality and using (

) with
\mu=2
and

, the embedding
\dot{H}^{2}\hookrightarrow\dot{H}^{\beta}\cap L_{\xi}^{\infty}

), and the fact (see

26

)

\displaystyle\|F(u)\|_{\beta}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+\|u\|^{q+1}_{L_{\xi}^{\infty}}+\|u\|^{q+1}_{\beta}),\quad\forall~u\in\dot{H}^{\beta}\cap L_{\xi}^{\infty},

we derive

\displaystyle J^{n}_{22}

\displaystyle\leq CT^{\beta/2}(h^{2}+\tau)(1+\|X\|^{q+1}_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{\omega}^{p}\dot{H}^{\beta}}+\|X\|^{q+1}_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{\omega}^{p}L_{\xi}^{\infty}})

(4.39)

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{CT}(1+\|X_{0}\|_{2}^{(q+1)^{2}})(h^{2}+\tau).

On the other hand, (
4.30
) with
(\mu,\nu)=(2,1+\theta)
, the condition (

) and the estimate (

) imply that

\displaystyle J^{n}_{32}

\displaystyle\leq CT^{\theta/2}(h^{2}+\tau)\|G(X)\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{\omega}^{p}\mathcal{L}_{2}^{1+\theta}}

\displaystyle\leq CT^{\theta/2}(h^{2}+\tau)(1+\|X\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{\omega}^{p}\dot{H}^{1+\theta}})

(4.40)

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{CT}(1+\|X_{0}\|^{q+1}_{2})(h^{2}+\tau).

This completes the proof.
∎∎

Combining Lemma
4.5
with the variational approach used in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we obtain the following strong convergence rate between the solution
X
of (
2.6
) and the drift-tamed-FEM
\{Y_{n}^{h}\}
defined in (

).

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
X_{0}\in\dot{H}^{1+\gamma}
with
\gamma\in[0,1]
and Assumptions
4.1
-
4.2
(1) (and (2) if
\gamma=1

For any
p\geq 1

C
such that

(4.41)

\displaystyle\sup_{1\leq n\leq N}\|X(t_{n})-Y_{n}^{h}\|_{L_{\omega}^{2p}L_{\xi}^{2}}\leq Ce^{CT}(1+\|X_{0}\|_{1+\gamma})^{q^{2}+3q+1}(h^{1+\gamma}+\tau^{1/2}).

Proof.

Let
1\leq n\leq N
and denote

e_{n}^{h}:={\widehat{Y}}_{n}^{h}-Y_{n}^{h}
.
Then
e_{n}^{h}\in V_{h}

with vanishing initial datum
e^{h}_{0}=0
.
In terms of (
4.24
) and (
3.8
), we have

\displaystyle{\widehat{Y}}_{n}^{h}

\displaystyle={\widehat{Y}}_{n-1}^{h}+\tau A_{h}{\widehat{Y}}_{n}^{h}+\tau\mathcal{P}_{h}F_{\tau}(X(t_{n-1}))+\mathcal{P}_{h}G(X(t_{n-1}))\delta_{n-1}W,

\displaystyle Y_{n}^{h}

\displaystyle=Y_{n-1}^{h}+\tau A_{h}Y_{n}^{h}+\tau\mathcal{P}_{h}F_{\tau}(Y_{n-1}^{h})+\mathcal{P}_{h}G(Y_{n-1}^{h})\delta_{n-1}W.

Consequently,

\displaystyle e_{n}^{h}-e_{n-1}^{h}

\displaystyle=\tau A_{h}e_{n}^{h}+\tau\mathcal{P}_{h}(F_{\tau}(X(t_{n-1}))-F_{\tau}(Y_{n-1}^{h}))

\displaystyle\quad+\mathcal{P}_{h}(G(X(t_{n-1}))-G(Y_{n-1}^{h}))\delta_{n-1}W.

Testing both sides of the above equation with
e_{n}^{h}
and applying the elementary equality (
3.3
), the estimate (
4.14
), and condition (
4.5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|e_{n}^{h}\|^{2}-\|e_{n-1}^{h}\|^{2}+\|e_{n}^{h}-e_{n-1}^{h}\|^{2}+2\tau\|\nabla e_{n}^{h}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=2\tau~{{}_{-1}}\langle F_{\tau}(X(t_{n-1}))-F_{\tau}({\widehat{Y}}_{n-1}^{h}),e_{n}^{h}\rangle_{1}

\displaystyle\quad+2\tau~{{}_{-1}}\langle F_{\tau}({\widehat{Y}}_{n-1}^{h})-F_{\tau}(Y_{n-1}^{h}),e_{n}^{h}-e_{n-1}^{h}\rangle_{1}

\displaystyle\quad+2\tau~{{}_{-1}}\langle F_{\tau}({\widehat{Y}}_{n-1}^{h})-F_{\tau}(Y_{n-1}^{h}),e_{n-1}^{h}\rangle_{1}

\displaystyle\quad+2\langle e_{n}^{h}-e_{n-1}^{h},(G(X(t_{n-1}))-G(Y_{n-1}^{h}))\delta_{n-1}W\rangle+R_{n-1}^{h}

\displaystyle\leq C\tau\|e_{n-1}^{h}\|^{2}+\tau\|\nabla e_{n}^{h}\|^{2}+\tau\|F_{\tau}(X(t_{n-1}))-F_{\tau}({\widehat{Y}}_{n-1}^{h})\|_{-1}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+4\tau^{2}\|F_{\tau}({\widehat{Y}}_{n-1}^{h})-F_{\tau}(Y_{n-1}^{h})\|^{2}+\|e_{n}^{h}-e_{n-1}^{h}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+4\|(G(X(t_{n-1}))-G(Y_{n-1}^{h}))\delta_{n-1}W\|^{2}+R_{n-1}^{h},

where
R_{n-1}^{h}:=2\langle e_{n-1}^{h},(G(X(t_{n-1}))-G(Y_{n-1}^{h}))\delta_{n-1}W\rangle
.
It follows from the estimates (
4.15
) and (
4.17
) that

\displaystyle\|e^{h}_{n}\|^{2}-(1+C\tau)\|e^{h}_{n-1}\|^{2}+\tau\|\nabla e_{n}^{h}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\tau\|F_{\tau}(X(t_{n-1}))-F_{\tau}({\widehat{Y}}_{n-1}^{h})\|_{-1}^{2}+4\tau^{2}\|F_{\tau}({\widehat{Y}}_{n-1}^{h})-F_{\tau}(Y_{n-1}^{h})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+4\|(G(X(t_{n-1}))-G(Y_{n-1}^{h}))\delta_{n-1}W\|^{2}+R_{n-1}^{h}

\displaystyle\leq C\tau\|e_{n-1}^{h}\|^{2}+\tau\|F_{\tau}(X(t_{n-1}))-F_{\tau}({\widehat{Y}}_{n-1}^{h})\|_{-1}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+4\|(G(X(t_{n-1}))-G(Y_{n-1}^{h}))\delta_{n-1}W\|^{2}+R_{n-1}^{h}.

Summing up the above inequality yields that

\displaystyle\|e^{h}_{n}\|^{2}+\tau\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|\nabla e_{k}^{h}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\tau\sum_{0\leq k\leq n-1}\|e^{h}_{k}\|^{2}+\tau\sum_{0\leq k\leq n-1}\|F_{\tau}(X(t_{k}))-F_{\tau}({\widehat{Y}}_{k}^{h})\|_{-1}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+4\sum_{0\leq k\leq n-1}\|(G(X(t_{k}))-G(Y_{k}^{h}))\delta_{k}W\|^{2}+\sum_{0\leq k\leq n-1}|R_{k}^{h}|.

As in (
4.29
), we apply discrete BDG, Cauchy–Schwarz, and Young inequalities, to obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big(\sum_{0\leq k\leq n-1}\|(G(X(t_{k}))-G(Y_{k}^{h}))\delta_{k}W\|^{2}\Big)^{p}+\mathbb{E}\sup_{1\leq n\leq N}\Big|\sum_{0\leq k\leq n-1}R_{k}^{h}\Big|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\tau\sum_{0\leq k\leq n-1}\mathbb{E}\|e^{h}_{k}\|^{2p}+C\sup_{0\leq k\leq n-1}\mathbb{E}\|X(t_{k})-{\widehat{Y}}_{k}^{h}\|^{2p}.

Then, we use the elementary inequality (
4.28
) to derive

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{1\leq n\leq N}\|e^{h}_{n}\|^{2p}

\displaystyle\leq C\tau\sum_{0\leq k\leq n-1}\mathbb{E}\|e^{h}_{k}\|^{2p}+C\sup_{0\leq k\leq n-1}\mathbb{E}\|X(t_{k})-{\widehat{Y}}_{k}^{h}\|^{2p}

\displaystyle\quad+C\sup_{0\leq k\leq n-1}\mathbb{E}\|F_{\tau}(X(t_{k}))-F_{\tau}({\widehat{Y}}_{k}^{h})\|_{-1}^{2p}.

By discrete Grönwall inequality and the estimate (
4.17
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{1\leq n\leq N}\|e^{h}_{n}\|^{2p}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{CT}\sup_{0\leq k\leq n-1}(\mathbb{E}\|X(t_{k})-{\widehat{Y}}_{k}^{h}\|^{4p})^{1/2}

(4.42)

\displaystyle\qquad\times(1+\|X\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{\omega}^{4pq}\dot{H}^{1}}^{2pq}+\sup_{0\leq n\leq N-1}\|{\widehat{Y}}_{k}^{h}\|_{L_{\omega}^{4pq}\dot{H}^{1}}^{2pq}).

Then, we conclude (
4.41
) from (
4.32
), (
4.21
), (
4.26
), and (
4.42
).
∎∎

5.
High-order Convergence Rate in Additive Noise Case

In this section, we analyze the strong convergence rates in the additive noise case, i.e., when the diffusion coefficient of Eq. (
2.6
) is a constant operator
G(\cdot)\equiv G
:

(5.1)

\displaystyle{\rm d}X(t)=(AX(t)+F(X(t))){\rm d}t+G{\rm d}W,\quad t\geq 0;\quad X(0)=X_{0}.

In this case, conditions (
4.5
)-(
4.6
) are equivalent to the assumption
G\in\mathcal{L}_{2}^{1}
, which was imposed in
[
34
]
as
\|(-A)^{1/2}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}^{0}}<\infty
with
g(\cdot)\equiv 1
.

We consider the drift-tamed-FEM (
3.9
) applied to Eq. (
2.6
) with a constant diffusion operator
G(\cdot)\equiv G
and with initial condition
Y_{0}^{h}=\mathcal{P}_{h}X_{0}
and

(5.2)

\displaystyle Y_{n}^{h}=Y_{n-1}^{h}+A_{h}Y_{n}^{h}\tau+\mathcal{P}_{h}F_{\tau}(Y_{n-1}^{h})\tau+\mathcal{P}_{h}G\delta_{n-1}W,\quad n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}.

Our main purpose is to establish a high-order convergence result under the additional polynomial growth condition on the second derivative
f^{\prime\prime}
of
f
:

(5.3)

\displaystyle|f^{\prime\prime}(\xi)|\leq L_{3}^{\prime}+L_{4}^{\prime}|\xi|^{\max\{0,q-1\}},\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R},

for some positive constants
L_{3}^{\prime}
and
L_{4}^{\prime}
.
It is clear that the condition (
5.3
) is consistent with Assumption
4.1
.

Now, we can state and prove the higher-order convergence for the drift-tamed-FEM (
5.2
) applied to Eq. (
5.1
).

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
X_{0}\in\dot{H}^{1+\gamma}\cap L_{\xi}^{2(3q+1)}
with
\gamma\in[0,1]
, and
G\in\mathcal{L}_{2}^{1}
(and there exists a positive constant
\theta
such that
G\in\mathcal{L}_{2}^{1+\theta}
if
\gamma=1
).
Under the condition (
5.3
) and Assumption
4.1
, for any
p\geq 1
, there exists a constant
C
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{1\leq n\leq N}\|X(t_{n})-Y_{n}^{h}\|_{L_{\omega}^{2p}L_{\xi}^{2}}

(5.4)

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{CT}(1+\|X_{0}\|_{1+\gamma}^{q^{2}+3q+1}+\|X_{0}\|_{L_{\xi}^{2(3q+1)}}^{3q+1})(h^{1+\gamma}+\tau^{\frac{1+\gamma}{2}}).

Proof.

Let
p\geq 2
and
\gamma\in[0,1]
.
As (
4.42
) in the proof in Theorem
4.1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{1\leq n\leq N}\|X(t_{n})-Y_{n}^{h}\|_{L_{\omega}^{2p}L_{\xi}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{CT}\sup_{0\leq k\leq n-1}\|X(t_{k})-{\widehat{Y}}_{k}^{h}\|_{L_{\omega}^{4p}L_{\xi}^{2}}

(5.5)

\displaystyle\qquad\times(1+\|X\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{\omega}^{4pq}\dot{H}^{1}}^{q}+\sup_{0\leq n\leq N-1}\|{\widehat{Y}}_{k}^{h}\|_{L_{\omega}^{4pq}\dot{H}^{1}}^{q}).

Thus, it suffices to establish the following refined estimate between
X
and
\widehat{Y}_{n}^{h}
when
G(\cdot)\equiv G
. Following the proof of Lemma
4.5
, we only need to provide refined estimates for
J_{21}^{n}
,
J_{23}^{n}
, and
J_{3}^{n}
under the additional condition (
5.3
).

To show a refined estimate of
J^{n}_{21}
, note that for
r\in[t_{i},t_{i+1})
,

\displaystyle X(t_{i+1})

\displaystyle=S(t_{i+1}-r)X(r)+\int_{r}^{t_{i+1}}S(t_{i+1}-\sigma)F(X(\sigma)){\rm d}\sigma

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{r}^{t_{i+1}}S(t_{i+1}-\sigma)G{\rm d}W(\sigma).

Then, using Taylor formula leads to the splitting of
J^{n}_{21}
into

\displaystyle J^{n}_{21}

\displaystyle=\|\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}S(t_{n}-r)[F(X(r))-F(X(t_{i+1}))]{\rm d}r\|_{L_{\omega}^{p}L_{\xi}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}S(t_{n}-r)DF(X(r))(S(t_{i+1}-r)-{\rm Id})X(r){\rm d}r\Big\|_{L_{\omega}^{p}L_{\xi}^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\Big\|\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}S(t_{n}-r)DF(X(r))\Big(\int_{r}^{t_{i+1}}S(t_{i+1}-\sigma)F(X(\sigma)){\rm d}\sigma\Big){\rm d}r\Big\|_{L_{\omega}^{p}L_{\xi}^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\Big\|\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}S(t_{n}-r)DF(X(r))\Big(\int_{r}^{t_{i+1}}S(t_{i+1}-\sigma)G{\rm d}W(\sigma)\Big){\rm d}r\Big\|_{L_{\omega}^{p}L_{\xi}^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\Big\|\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}S(t_{n}-r)R_{F}(X(r),X(t_{i+1})){\rm d}r\Big\|_{L_{\omega}^{p}L_{\xi}^{2}}=:\sum_{i=1}^{4}J^{n}_{21i},

\displaystyle R_{F}(X(r),X(t_{i+1}))

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{1}D^{2}F(X(r)+\lambda(X(t_{i})-X(r)))(X(t_{i})-X(r),X(t_{i})-X(r))(1-\lambda){\rm d}\lambda

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}f^{\prime\prime}(X(r)+\lambda(X(t_{i})-X(r)))(X(t_{i})-X(r))^{2}(1-\lambda){\rm d}\lambda.

,

Since for
\delta\in(3/2,2)
,
\dot{H}^{\delta}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}

(5.6)

\displaystyle\|x\|_{-\delta}\leq C\|x\|_{L_{\xi}^{1}},\quad x\in L_{\xi}^{1}.

4.1
), the embedding (
2.3
), and the estimates (
4.9
) with
(\mu,\nu)=(\delta,0)
and (
4.10
) with
\rho=1+\gamma

\displaystyle J^{n}_{211}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}(t_{n}-r)^{-\frac{\delta}{2}}\|DF(X)\|_{L_{\omega}^{2p}L_{\xi}^{2}}\|(S(t_{i+1}-r)-{\rm Id})X\|_{L_{\omega}^{2p}L_{\xi}^{2}}{\rm d}r

(5.7)

\displaystyle\leq C\tau^{\frac{1+\gamma}{2}}T^{1-\frac{\delta}{2}}(1+\|X\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{\omega}^{2pq}\dot{H}^{1}}^{q})\|X\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{\omega}^{2pq}\dot{H}^{1+\gamma}},\quad\gamma\in[0,1].

A similar argument implies that

\displaystyle J^{n}_{212}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\int_{r}^{t_{i+1}}(t_{n}-r)^{-\frac{\delta}{2}}\|DF(X(r))\|_{L_{\omega}^{2p}L_{\xi}^{2}}\|f(X(\sigma))\|_{L_{\omega}^{2p}L_{\xi}^{2}}{\rm d}\sigma{\rm d}r

(5.8)

\displaystyle\leq C\tau T^{1-\delta/2}(1+\|X\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{\omega}^{2pq}\dot{H}^{1}}^{q+1}).

To estimate the third term

, we apply the stochastic Fubini theorem, discrete and continuous BDG, and Cauchy–Schwarz inequalities to derive

\displaystyle(J^{n}_{213})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\tau\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\|S(t_{n}-r)DF(X(r))S(t_{i+1}-\sigma)G\|_{L_{\omega}^{p}\mathcal{L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}{\rm d}r{\rm d}\sigma

This, in combination with (
4.1
) and the assumption

, shows that

(5.9)

\displaystyle J^{n}_{213}\leq C\tau(1+\|X\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{\omega}^{pq}\dot{H}^{1}}^{q}).

Finally, in terms of the estimates (
4.9
) with
(\mu,\nu)=(\delta,0)
and (
5.6
), Minkowski and Hölder inequalities, the condition (
5.3
), and the embeddings (
2.3
) and
\dot{H}^{1/2}\hookrightarrow L_{\xi}^{3}
, we estimate the last term
J^{n}_{214}
similarly to
J^{n}_{211}
by

\displaystyle J^{n}_{214}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}(t_{n}-r)^{-\frac{\delta}{2}}\|R_{F}(X,X(t_{i+1}))\|_{L_{\omega}^{p}L_{\xi}^{1}}{\rm d}r

\displaystyle\leq CT^{1-\delta/2}\tau^{(1/2+\gamma)\wedge 1}(1+\|X\|^{q-1}_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{\omega}^{3p(q-1)}L_{\xi}^{3(q-1)}})\|X\|^{2}_{\mathcal{C}_{t}^{[(1/2+\gamma)\wedge 1]/2}L_{\omega}^{3p}\dot{H}^{1/2}}.

It is clear that
\dot{H}^{1}\subset L_{\xi}^{3(q-1)}
as
3(q-1)\leq 2(q+1)
(since
q\leq 2
) for
d=3
and
\dot{H}^{1}\subset L_{\xi}^{p}
for any

for

.
Consequently,

(5.10)

\displaystyle J^{n}_{214}\leq C\tau^{(\frac{1}{2}+\gamma)\wedge 1}T^{1-\frac{\delta}{2}}(1+\|X\|^{q-1}_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{\omega}^{3p(q-1)}\dot{H}^{1}})\|X\|^{2}_{\mathcal{C}_{t}^{[(\frac{1}{2}+\gamma)\wedge 1]/2}L_{\omega}^{3p}\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

Collecting the above four estimates (
5.7
)-(
5.10
) together,
we have

\displaystyle J^{n}_{21}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+T)\tau^{\frac{1+\gamma}{2}}[1+\|X\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{\omega}^{2pq}\dot{H}^{1}}^{q+1}+(1+\|X\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{\omega}^{2pq}\dot{H}^{1}}^{q})\|X\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{\omega}^{2pq}\dot{H}^{1+\gamma}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+(1+\|X\|^{q-1}_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{\omega}^{3p(q-1)}\dot{H}^{1}})\|X\|^{2}_{\mathcal{C}_{t}^{[(1/2+\gamma)\wedge 1]/2}L_{\omega}^{3p}\dot{H}^{1/2}}]

(5.11)

\displaystyle\leq\begin{cases}Ce^{CT}\tau^{\frac{1+\gamma}{2}}(1+\|X_{0}\|^{3q+1}_{1+\gamma}),\quad&\gamma\in[0,1),\\
Ce^{CT}\tau^{\frac{1+\gamma}{2}}(1+\|X_{0}\|^{q^{2}+3q}_{1+\gamma}+\|X_{0}\|^{q^{2}+3q}_{2}),\quad&\gamma=1,\end{cases}

where in the last step we used the estimates (
4.21
)-(

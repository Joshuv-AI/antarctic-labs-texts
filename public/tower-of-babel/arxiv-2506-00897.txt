Introduction

In
Cauchy-Riemann geometry
, a well-known problem is finding equivalence classes under the relation of CR diffeomorphisms. One approach to solving this problem is to find sufficiently many
CR
invariants that characterize each class, called the equivalence problem. This problem has been classically solved for CR manifolds that are Levi nondegenerate real hypersurfaces and a model for the homogeneous case is given by

\mathbf{SU}{(1+p,q+1)}/\mathbf{H}\simeq\left\{\ [z_{0},\ldots,z_{n+1}]\,|\,\sum_{i=0}^{p}|z^{i}|^{2}{=}\sum_{i=p+1}^{n+1}|z^{i}|^{2}\ \right\}\subset\mathbb{CP}^{n+1}

(see
[
10
,
42
,
43
,
44
]
). In particular, in the context of (locally) homogeneous manifolds, this problem can be studied using the tool of
CR
algebras
[
33
]
, i.e., a pair
(\mathfrak{g}^{\tau},\mathfrak{f})
, where
\mathfrak{g}^{\tau}
is a real Lie algebra encoding the group of CR diffeomorphisms, and
\mathfrak{f}\subset\mathfrak{g}=\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathfrak{g}^{\tau}
is a complex Lie subalgebra encoding the CR structure. In
[
31
]
, a classification of all semisimple
Levi-Tanaka algebras was obtained. These are the
Levi nondegenerate
CR
algebras
(\mathfrak{g}^{\tau},\mathfrak{f})
,
where
\mathfrak{g}
is semisimple, and
\mathfrak{g}^{\tau}
inherits
a
\mathbb{Z}
-gradation from
\mathfrak{g}
that is compatible
with the
CR
structure. Several examples of
homogeneous
CR
manifolds are associated with

CR
algebras
(\mathfrak{g}^{\tau},\mathfrak{f})
that are
k-
nondegenerate for some
k{>}1
, i.e., Levi degenerate but also satisfying a weaker condition of nondegeneracy according to their Levi form (see
[
5
, Def.11.1.8 p.317]
).
In the case of a point
p
in a generic analytic
CR
hypersurface, this notion is equivalent to holomorphic nondegeneracy at
p
, that is the absence of a germ at
p
of a holomorphic vector field
X
tangent to
M
(i.e., with a representative tangent to
M
in a neighborhood of
p
) such that
X\mid_{M}\nequiv 0
(see
[
5
, Th. 11.5.1, p. 330]
). This, in turn, implies the finiteness of the dimension of the
CR
automorphism group at
p
.
The
k-
nondegeneracy is widely used in the literature as a key invariant in solving the equivalence problem or studying the group of
CR
automorphisms, see
[
20
,
34
,
22
,
40
,
36
,
21
,
17
]
.
In particular
[
12
,
13
]
the Authors pointed that it
would be interesting to construct homogeneous
k-
nondegenerate
CR
manifolds for arbitrarily large
k
. Quoting verbatim: “We do not know how to construct
k
-nondegenerate homogeneous manifolds with
k\geq 5
(should these exist)”. In this paper we address this question.

In Section
1
, we recall some basic definitions in
CR
geometry. In Section
2
, we translate these definitions into the context of homogeneous
CR
manifolds; in particular, we review some tools defined for non-semisimple homogeneous
CR
manifolds. Finally, in Section
3
, we define and characterize an example of a homogeneous
CR
manifold that is
k
-nondegenerate for arbitrary
k\geq 1
, providing a local equation in Section
4
(Theorem
4.7
).
This last result should be compared, for
k=1
, with
[
11
, Theorem 1.1]
, and for
k=2
, with
[
39
, Table 1, Type VII]
(see also
[

1.
CR manifolds

Let
M
be a
(2n+d)
-dimensional CR manifold, i.e. a differential manifold equipped with a
CR structure
given by a rank
n
complex distribution
\textsf{H}^{0,1}\textsf{M}\subset\mathbb{C}\otimes\textsf{T}\textsf{M}
such that

(1)

\textsf{H}^{0,1}\textsf{M}\cap\overline{\textsf{H}^{0,1}\textsf{M}}=\{0\}

(2)

[\Gamma(\textsf{H}^{0,1}\textsf{M}),\Gamma(\textsf{H}^{0,1}\textsf{M})]\subset\Gamma(\textsf{H}^{0,1}\textsf{M})
.

We call
(\textsf{M},\textsf{H}^{0,1}\textsf{M})
a
Cauchy–Riemann manifold
of
CR-dimension

n
and
CR-codimension

d
. When
d=1
, we refer to it as being of
hypersurface type
. A
CR
manifold can also be given
by a rank
2n
real subbundle
\textsf{H}_{\mathbb{R}}\textsf{M}
of
T
M
which is the
real distribution

underlying the CR structure, i.e.,
\textsf{H}_{\mathbb{R}}\textsf{M}=\textrm{Re}\left(\textsf{H}^{0,1}\textsf{M}\oplus\textsf{H}^{1,0}\textsf{M}\right)
and a smooth complex structure
\textrm{J}_{\textsf{M}}
on the fibers of
\textsf{H}_{\mathbb{R}}\textsf{M}
, defined by
\textsf{H}^{0,1}\textsf{M}{=}\{X+i\,\textrm{J}_{\textsf{M}}{X}\mid X\in\Gamma(\textsf{H}_{\mathbb{R}}\textsf{M})\},

that it squares to minus identity and satisfies
\textsf{N}_{\textrm{J}_{\textsf{M}}}(X,Y){=}[\textrm{J}_{\textsf{M}}X,\textrm{J}_{\textsf{M}}Y]-[X,Y]-\textrm{J}_{\textsf{M}}([\textrm{J}_{\textsf{M}}X,Y]+[X,\textrm{J}_{\textsf{M}}Y])=0
for any two sections
X,Y\in\Gamma(\textsf{H}_{\mathbb{R}}\textsf{M})
,
taking the name of
partial complex structure

of
M
.

We label by
\mathcal{L}^{1}
its
Levi form
, which it is a sesquilinear
form on
\textsf{H}^{0,1}\textsf{M}
valued in

\mathbb{C}\textsf{T}\textsf{M}/(\textsf{H}^{0,1}\textsf{M}\oplus\overline{\textsf{H}^{0,1}\textsf{M})}
given at point

in
M
by

(1)

\mathcal{L}^{1}(X_{\textsf{p}},Y_{\textsf{p}})=\frac{1}{2i}\left[X,\overline{Y}\right]_{\textsf{p}}\pmod{\textsf{H}^{0,1}_{\textsf{p}}\textsf{M}\oplus\overline{\textsf{H}^{0,1}_{\textsf{p}}\textsf{M}}}

for all
X,Y\in\Gamma(\textsf{H}^{0,1}\textsf{M})
.
The kernel at
{\textsf{p}}\in\textsf{M}
of
\mathcal{L}^{1}
is given by

\mathcal{F}_{\textsf{p}}^{1}=\left\{v\in\textsf{H}^{0,1}_{\textsf{p}}\textsf{M}\,\left|\,\mathcal{L}^{1}(v,w)=0\,\,\forall\,w\in\textsf{H}^{0,1}_{\textsf{p}}\textsf{M}\right.\right\}.

When
\mathcal{F}_{\textsf{p}}^{1}
is of constant rank at every point then
\mathcal{F}^{1}=\bigcup_{\textsf{p}}\mathcal{F}_{\textsf{p}}^{1}
is a vector distribution named
Levi Kernel
.

A natural generalization of (
1
) then is the
k-
order Levi form

(2)

\mathcal{L}^{k}:\textsf{H}^{0,1}\textsf{M}\times\left(\bigodot^{k}\textsf{H}^{0,1}\textsf{M}\right)\to\mathbb{C}\textsf{T}\textsf{M}/(\textsf{H}^{0,1}\textsf{M}\oplus\overline{\textsf{H}^{0,1}\textsf{M}})

inductively defined at a point
p
of
M
as

\mathcal{L}^{k}\left(X_{\textsf{p}},Y^{1}_{\textsf{p}},\ldots,Y^{k}_{\textsf{p}}\right)=\frac{1}{2i}\left[\cdots\left[\left[X,\overline{Y^{1}}\right],\cdots\right],\overline{Y^{k}}\right]_{\textsf{p}}\pmod{\textsf{H}^{0,1}_{\textsf{p}}\textsf{M}\oplus\overline{\textsf{H}^{0,1}_{\textsf{p}}\textsf{M}}}

with
X,Y^{1},\ldots,Y^{k}\in\Gamma\left(\textsf{H}^{0,1}\textsf{M}\right)
.

The left kernels of (
2
) at
\textsf{p}\in\textsf{M}
is given by

(3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\textsf{p}}^{k}=\{v\in\textsf{H}^{0,1}_{\textsf{p}}\textsf{M}\,|\,\mathcal{L}^{k}(v,w_{1},\ldots,w_{k})=0\,\,\forall\,w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}\in\textsf{H}^{0,1}_{\textsf{p}}\textsf{M}\}.

Again when the
\mathcal{F}_{\textsf{p}}
are of constant rank at every points the vector distributions
\mathcal{F}^{k}=\bigcup_{{\textsf{p}}\in M}\mathcal{F}_{\textsf{p}}^{k}
give rise to a nested sequence of complex subbundle
of
\textsf{H}^{0,1}\textsf{M}
(see
[
14
]
)

(4)

\textsf{H}^{0,1}\textsf{M}\supseteq\mathcal{F}^{1}\supseteq\mathcal{F}^{2}\dots\supseteq\mathcal{F}^{k-1}\supseteq\mathcal{F}^{k}\supseteq\dots

Definition 1.1

(Def.11.1.8
[
5
]
)
.

A CR manifold

nondegenerate

{\textsf{p}}\in\textsf{M}
, if there exist a positive integer

\mathcal{F}_{\textsf{p}}^{(k-1)}\supsetneqq\mathcal{F}_{\textsf{p}}^{(k)}{=}\{0\},

2.
Homogeneous CR manifolds and CR algebras

From now on we will denote by
\mathfrak{g}^{\tau}
a real form of a complex Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, i.e., a subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
such that
\mathfrak{g}^{\tau}\otimes\mathbb{C}=\mathfrak{g}
. To
\mathfrak{g}^{\tau}
, we can associate an anti-
\mathbb{C}
linear involution
\tau
, that makes
\mathfrak{g}^{\tau}
the subalgebra of the
\tau
-fixed elements of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Let
\mathbf{G}^{\tau}
be a Lie group with
\mathrm{Lie}\,\mathbf{G}^{\tau}=\mathfrak{g}^{\tau}
acting transitively on a CR manifold
M
. Fix a point
\textsf{p}\in\textsf{M}
, and let

\piup\colon\mathbf{G}^{\tau}\ni\textsf{a}\mapsto\textsf{a}\cdot\textsf{p}\in\textsf{M}

be the natural projection. The differential at
p
defines a map

\piup_{*}\colon\mathfrak{g}^{\tau}\to\textsf{T}_{\textsf{p}}\textsf{M},

which maps the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{\tau}
of
\mathbf{G}^{\tau}
onto the tangent space to
M
at
p
. By the formal integrability of the partial complex structure of
M
, the complexification of the pullback of the
CR
structure at
p
is a complex Lie subalgebra

\mathfrak{f}=(\piup^{*})^{\mathbb{C}}(\textsf{H}^{0,1}_{\textsf{p}}\textsf{M})

of the complexification
\mathfrak{g}
of
\mathfrak{g}^{\tau}
. Conversely, the assignment of a complex Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{f}
of
\mathfrak{g}
yields a formally integrable
\mathbf{G}^{\tau}
-equivariant partial complex structure on a
\mathbf{G}^{\tau}
-homogeneous space
M
by requiring that
\textsf{H}_{\textsf{p}}^{0,1}\textsf{M}=\piup^{\mathbb{C}}_{*}(\mathfrak{f})
(see
[
30
]
).
These lead to the following definition:

Definition 2.1
.

A
CR algebra
is a pair
(\mathfrak{g}^{\tau},\mathfrak{f})
, consisting of a real Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{\tau}
and a complex Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{f}
of its complexification
\mathfrak{g},
such that the quotient
\mathfrak{g}^{\tau}/(\mathfrak{g}^{\tau}\cap\mathfrak{f})
is a finite-dimensional real vector space.

We call the intersection
\mathfrak{f}\cap\mathfrak{g}^{\tau}
its
isotropy subalgebra
. The
CR
-dimension and codimension of a
\mathbf{G}^{\tau}-
homogeneous
CR
manifold
M
are expressed in terms of its associated CR algebra
(\mathfrak{g}^{\tau},\mathfrak{f})
by:

\begin{cases}CR-\dim\textsf{M}=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathfrak{f}-\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathfrak{f}{\cap}\tau(\mathfrak{f})),\\
CR-\textrm{codim}\textsf{M}=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\mathfrak{g}-\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathfrak{f}+\tau(\mathfrak{f})).\end{cases}

Now, given a CR algebra
(\mathfrak{g}^{\tau},\mathfrak{f})
, we have

Proposition 2.2

(Lemma 1.1,
[
33
]
)
.

The analytic tangent space of the
CR
homogeneous manifold with associated
CR
algebra
(\mathfrak{g}^{\tau},\mathfrak{f})
at
p
carries a unique complex structure
\textrm{J}_{\textsf{M}}
in
p
, characterized by the property:

(5)

\textrm{J}(X+\mathfrak{g}^{\tau}\cap\mathfrak{f})=Y+\mathfrak{g}^{\tau}\cap\mathfrak{f}\textit{ if and only if }X+\sqrt{-1}Y\in\mathfrak{f}

for
X,Y\in(\mathfrak{f}+\tau(\mathfrak{f}))\cap\mathfrak{g}^{\tau})/\mathfrak{g}^{\tau}\cap\mathfrak{f}
.∎

When
M
is a (locally) homogeneous manifold,
1
-nondegeneracy, i.e., the standard notion of Levi nondegeneracy in the literature, at the point
p
can be computed in terms of its associated CR algebra

\forall{Z}\in\mathfrak{f}{\backslash}\tau(\mathfrak{f})\,\exists\,Z^{\prime}\in\tau(\mathfrak{f})\text{ such that }[Z,Z^{\prime}]\notin\mathfrak{f}+\tau(\mathfrak{f}).

This is equivalent to asking that the pullback of the Levi kernel at
p
coincides with the isotropy algebra

\mathfrak{f}^{{1}}\coloneqq\{Z\in\mathfrak{f}\mid[Z,\tau(\mathfrak{f})]\subset\mathfrak{f}+\tau(\mathfrak{f})\}=\mathfrak{f}\cap\tau(\mathfrak{f})=(\mathfrak{f}\cap\mathfrak{g}^{\tau})\otimes\mathbb{C}.

Similary one can look at
k
-nondegenracy at
p
as

\forall{Z}\in\mathfrak{f}{\backslash}\tau(\mathfrak{f})\,\exists\,Z^{\prime}_{1},\dots,Z^{\prime}_{r}\in\tau(\mathfrak{f}),r\leq k,\text{ such that }[Z,[Z^{\prime}_{1},[\dots,Z^{\prime}_{r}]\dots]\notin\mathfrak{f}+\tau(\mathfrak{f}).

To measure the degeneracy of the Levi form, it is convenient to consider the sequence (see e.g.
[
27
, (4.3)]
):

(6)

\mathfrak{f}=\mathfrak{f}^{0}\supseteq\mathfrak{f}^{1}\supseteq\dots\supseteq\mathfrak{f}^{k-1}\supseteq\mathfrak{f}^{k}\supseteq\mathfrak{f}^{{k+1}}\supseteq\cdots

defined recursively by

\mathfrak{f}^{k+1}=\{Z\in\mathfrak{f}^{k}\mid[Z,\tau(\mathfrak{f})]\subseteq\mathfrak{f}^{k}+\tau(\mathfrak{f})\}\;\;\;\text{for $k>0.$}

We note that
\mathfrak{f}\cap\tau(\mathfrak{f}){\subseteq}\mathfrak{f}^{k}
for all integers
k{\geq}0
. Since by assumption
\mathfrak{f}/(\mathfrak{f}\cap\tau(\mathfrak{f}))
is finite-dimensional, there is a smallest nonnegative integer
k
such that
\mathfrak{f}^{k}{=}\mathfrak{f}^{k^{\prime}}
for all
k^{\prime}{\geq}k.

Definition 2.3
.

A CR algebra
(\mathfrak{g}^{\tau},\mathfrak{f})
is said to be
k
-nondegenerate
if there is a smallest non-negative integer
k
such that
\mathfrak{f}^{{k-1}}\supsetneqq\mathfrak{f}^{{k}}{=}\mathfrak{f}{\cap}\tau(\mathfrak{f}).

Proposition 2.4

(Sec.1.3,
[
25
]
)
.

The elements
\mathfrak{f}^{k}
of the sequence (
6
) are Lie subalgebras of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Proof.

By definition,
\mathfrak{f}^{0}{=}\mathfrak{f}
is a Lie algebra. If
Z_{1},Z_{2},{\in},\mathfrak{f}^{1},
then

\displaystyle[[Z_{1},Z_{2}],\tau(\mathfrak{f})]\subseteq[Z_{1},[Z_{2},\tau(\mathfrak{f})]]+[Z_{2},[Z_{1},\tau(\mathfrak{f})]]\subseteq[Z_{1}+Z_{2},\mathfrak{f}+\tau(\mathfrak{f})]\subseteq\mathfrak{f}+\tau(\mathfrak{f})

because
[Z_{i},\mathfrak{f}]{\subset}\mathfrak{f}
, since
Z_{i}
belongs to the subalgebra
\mathfrak{f}
, and
[Z_{i},\tau(\mathfrak{f})]{\subset}\mathfrak{f}{+}\tau(\mathfrak{f})
by the definition of
\mathfrak{f}^{1}
.

Now, we argue by induction. Let
k\geq 1
and assume that
\mathfrak{f}^{k}
is a Lie subalgebra. Then, if
Z_{1},Z_{2}\in\mathfrak{f}^{k+1},
we obtain:

\displaystyle[[Z_{1},Z_{2}],\tau(\mathfrak{f})]\subseteq[Z_{1},[Z_{2},\tau(\mathfrak{f})]]+[Z_{2},[Z_{1},\tau(\mathfrak{f})]]\subseteq[Z_{1}+Z_{2},\mathfrak{f}^{k}]\subseteq\mathfrak{f}^{k},

because
\mathfrak{f}^{k+1}{\subseteq}\mathfrak{f}^{k},
showing that also
[Z_{1},Z_{2}]\in\mathfrak{f}^{(k+1)}.
This completes the proof. ∎

Remark 2.5
.

The notion of

Let

Now consider the hypersurface

This example was presented in

\mathfrak{hol}(M)

Theorem 4.7
.

Given a fixed natural number
k\geq 1
, the homogeneous
k
-nondegenerate CR hypersurface defined in Theorem
3.6
, with the CR algebra given in Lemma
3.3
, is locally described by the hypersurface
M
with equation:

\textrm{Re}(w)=2\sum^{k}_{h=1}\textrm{Re}(z_{0}^{h}\bar{z}_{h}),

where
(w,z_{0},\dots,z_{k})
are local coordinates in
\mathbb{C}^{k+2}
.

Proof.

We will construct a set of vector fields tangent to
M
that generates a Lie algebra isomorphic to
\mathfrak{g}^{\tau}
given in Lemma
3.3
. Moreover we will define a CR structure compatible with the one in Theorem
3.6
. By Lemma
4.4
the vector fields
Z_{+},Z_{-}
and
\mathcal{H}
generates a Lie algebra isomorphic to
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
inside
\mathfrak{hol}(\textsf{M})
.
By Lemma
4.5
the vector fields
\tilde{\Sigma}_{1},\tilde{\Sigma}_{2}
and
\tilde{\Sigma}_{3}
generates a Lie algebra isomorphic to
\mathfrak{su}(2)
tangent
M
. We have that
[Z_{-},W]=[Z^{\prime}_{-},W]=0
and
[Z_{+},A^{\prime}_{k}]=[Z^{\prime}_{+},A^{\prime}_{k}]=0
. The space
\mathcal{V}
, generated by
\operatorname{ad}^{j}(Z_{+})(W)
for
j\in\{0,\dots,2k\}
, is a complex
2k+1
-dimensional
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-representation. Each
\operatorname{ad}^{j}(Z_{+})(W)
spans a non-zero complex one-dimensional eigenspace of
\mathcal{H}
contained in the subspace generated by elements of grade
h=j-k
, with
-k\leq h\leq k
, consistent with the structure given in Lemma
4.4
.
By the grading structure described in Lemma
4.4
, the representation under consideration is finite-dimensional,
2\mathbb{Z}-
graded, and has the property that each eigenvalue occurs with multiplicity one and therefore it is irreducible
[
46
, Corollary 4.2.4]
. Moreover, there is a one-to-one correspondence between finite-dimensional, irreducible complex representations of
\mathfrak{su}(2)
and those of
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
. In particular, the irreducible representations of
\mathfrak{su}(2)
can be viewed as real forms of the irreducible representations of
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
. It follows that the complex representation
\mathcal{V}
contains a real, irreducible representation of
\mathfrak{su}(2)
of dimension
2k+1
, which we denote by
\mathcal{V}^{\tau}
.
The conjugation operator
\tau
maps the vector space generated by
\operatorname{ad}^{j}(Z_{-})(A^{\prime}_{k})
to the conjugate generated by
\operatorname{ad}^{j}(Z_{+})(W)
, and through
\tau-
symmetrization, yields the corresponding real irreducible representation
\mathcal{V}^{\tau}
of
\mathfrak{su}(2)
, which corresponds to the standard representation of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. As a consequence, the vector fields
\mathcal{H}
,
Z_{-}
, and the set
\{\operatorname{ad}^{j}(Z_{-})(A^{\prime}_{k})\}
, with
0{\leq}j{\leq}k-1
, generate a complex Lie subalgebra isomorphic to the algebra
\mathfrak{f}
described in Lemma
3.3
. The
CR
distribution and structure can be rebuild from this data. This completes the proof.
∎

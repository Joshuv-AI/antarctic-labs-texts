An asymptotic characterisation of the Kerr spacetime
R. Sansom, J. A. Valiente Kroon

Abstract
We provide a characterisation of the Kerr spacetime close to future null infinity using the asymptotic characteristic initial value problem in a conformally compactified spacetime. Stewart’s gauge is used to set up the past-oriented characteristic initial value problem. By a theorem of M. Mars characterising the Kerr spacetime, we provide conditions for the existence of an asymptotically timelike Killing vector on the development of the initial data by demanding that the spacetime is endowed with a Killing spinor. The conditions on the characteristic initial data ensuring the existence of a Killing spinor are, in turn, analysed. Finally, we write the conditions on the initial data in terms of the free data in the characteristic initial value problem. As a result, we characterise the Kerr spacetime using only a section of future null infinity and its intersection with an outgoing null hypersurface.

Introduction

The Kerr family of solutions to the Einstein vacuum equations

\mathbf{Ric}[\tilde{\bm{g}}]=0,

(1)

where
\tilde{\bm{g}}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \tilde{\bm{g}}_{Kerr}=&-(1-\frac{2mr}{\rho^{2}})dt^{2}+\frac{\rho^{2}}{\Sigma}dr^{2}+\rho^{2}d\theta^{2}+\left(r^{2}+a^{2}+\frac{2mra^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}{\rho^{2}}\right)\sin^{2}\theta d\varphi^{2}\\
&-\frac{4mra\sin^{2}\theta}{\rho^{2}}dtd\varphi\end{aligned}

1.2
Killing spinor initial data sets

As already discussed, the presence of some of the symmetries in the Kerr family of solutions (
2
) is captured by Killing vectors. More generally, imposing that a solution to the vacuum Einstein equations (
1
) possesses Killing vectors allows symmetry reductions on the equations —see, for example,
[
36
]
. From the viewpoint of the inital value problem, the imposition of symmmetry assumptions should happen only at the level of initial data. This is accomplished by the
Killing initial data equations

[
7
]
which encode the existence of a Killing vector in the developmemnt in the initial data itself. The deeper “hidden symmetries” appearing in the Kerr spacetime can be characterised by more fundamental objects known as Killing spinors which are symmetric valence-
2
spinors
\tilde{\kappa}_{AB}
. The
Killing spinor initial data equations
encode on initial data the existence of a Killing spinor in the initial data’s development. For the Einstein vacuum equations (
1

[
21
]
and for suitable classes of matter in
[
13
,
11
]
. For the conformal Einstein equations, conditions on spacelike initial data in the unphysical spacetime that give rise to a Killing spinor in the conformally related physical spacetime have been derived in
[
22
]
.

As part of our analysis in this article, we derive the Killing spinor initial data conditions for the conformal Einstein field equations on characteristic initial data in the unphysical spacetime that give rise to a Killing spinor in the conformally related physical spacetime. Moreover, we take one of the null hypersurfaces to be the future null infinity,
{\mathscr{I}^{+}}
.

The central idea to constructing Killing spinor initial data is to obtain a closed system of homogeneous wave equations that encode the existence of a Killing spinor. For the conformal Einstein field equations this closed homogeneous system was derived in
[
22
]

1.1
, by ensuring that the equations governing the existence of a Killing spinor hold on the initial data, we can obtain a solution to the Killing spinor initial data equations within some existence domain. In this article, the existence domain will be the “narrow rectangle” along
{\mathscr{I}^{+}}
—see Figure

In this section, we analyse the NP commutators in the NP formalism to arrive at the gauge conditions on the NP spin coefficients implied by Stewart’s gauge.

Lemma 1

(Gauge conditions on the NP spin coefficients)
.

The NP frame can be chosen such that

\displaystyle\rho=\bar{\rho},\ \ \mu=\bar{\mu},\ \ \epsilon=0,

\displaystyle\kappa=\nu=0,

(18)

\displaystyle\tau=\bar{\alpha}+\beta,

on

Proof.

Acting on a scalar function

\displaystyle\Delta D\psi={}

\displaystyle(\gamma+\bar{\gamma})D\psi+D\Delta\psi+(\epsilon+\bar{\epsilon})\Delta\psi-(\pi+\bar{\tau})\delta\psi-(\bar{\pi}+\tau)\bar{\delta}\psi,

\displaystyle\delta D\psi={}

\displaystyle(\bar{\alpha}+\beta-\bar{\pi})D\psi+D\delta\psi+\kappa\Delta\psi-(\epsilon-\bar{\epsilon}+\bar{\rho})\delta\psi-\sigma\bar{\delta}\psi,

\displaystyle\delta\Delta\psi={}

\displaystyle-\bar{\nu}D\psi-(\bar{\alpha}+\beta-\tau)\Delta\psi+\Delta\delta\psi-\gamma\delta\psi+\bar{\gamma}\delta\psi+\mu\delta\psi+\bar{\lambda}\bar{\delta}\psi,

\displaystyle\bar{\delta}\delta\psi={}

\displaystyle(-\mu+\bar{\mu})D\psi+(-\rho+\bar{\rho})\Delta\psi+\alpha\delta\psi-\bar{\beta}\delta\psi+\delta\bar{\delta}\psi-\bar{\alpha}\bar{\delta}\psi+\beta\bar{\delta}\psi.

\displaystyle\rho=\bar{\rho},\ \ \mu=\bar{\mu},\ \ \epsilon=-\bar{\epsilon},

\displaystyle\kappa=\nu=0,

\displaystyle\tau=\bar{\alpha}+\beta.

3
Spacetimes with Killing spinors

In this section we discuss general properties of spacetimes endowed with a Killing spinor from the point of view of the asymptotic characteristic initial value problem.

3.1
Properties of Killing spinors

The key tool to be used in this article to characterise the Kerr spacetime is the notion of a Killing spinor. In this section we recall its definition and discuss conformal invariance.
A
Killing spinor
on a spacetime
(\tilde{{\mathcal{M}}},\tilde{{\bm{g}}})
is a symmetric rank 2 spinor

\tilde{\kappa}_{AB}=\tilde{\kappa}_{(AB)}
satisfying the
Killing spinor
equation

\tilde{\nabla}_{A^{\prime}(A}\tilde{\kappa}_{BC)}=0.

(22)

Given a Killing spinor
\tilde{\kappa}_{AB}
, the spinor

\tilde{\xi}_{AA^{\prime}}\equiv\tilde{\nabla}^{P}{}_{A^{\prime}}\tilde{\kappa}_{AP}

is the spinorial counterpart of a Killing vector —i.e. it satisfies the equation

\tilde{\nabla}_{AA^{\prime}}\tilde{\xi}_{BB^{\prime}}+\tilde{\nabla}_{BB^{\prime}}\tilde{\xi}_{AA^{\prime}}=0.

A further consequence of the Killing spinor equation (
22
) is that
\tilde{\kappa}_{AB}
satisfies the wave equation

\tilde{\square}\tilde{\kappa}_{BC}-\Psi_{BCAD}\tilde{\kappa}^{AD}=0,

where
\tilde{\square}\equiv\tilde{\nabla}_{PP^{\prime}}\tilde{\nabla}^{PP^{\prime}}
. It is important to observe that while any solution to (
22
) is a solution to (
3.1
), the converse is not true. A solution to equation (
3.1
) will be said to be a
Killing spinor candidate
.

The notion of Killing spinor is conformally invariant. More precisely, if
\tilde{\kappa}_{AB}
is a solution to the
physical Killing spinor equation
(
22
) then

\kappa_{AB}=\Xi^{2}\tilde{\kappa}_{AB}

is a solution to the
unphysical Killing spinor equation

\nabla_{A^{\prime}(A}\kappa_{BC)}=0.

(23)

Remark 2
.

Observe that the above transformation rules imply that

\kappa^{AB}=\tilde{\kappa}^{AB}.

The unphysical Killing spinor
\kappa_{AB}
satisfies, in turn, the equation

\square\kappa_{BC}+4\Lambda\kappa_{BC}-\Xi\phi_{BCAD}\kappa^{AD}=0.

(24)

Solutions to the wave equation (
24
) will also be known as
Killing spinor candidates
.

Since all Killing vectors are also conformal Killing vectors, we have that the Killing vector
\tilde{\xi}^{AA^{\prime}}
on
(\tilde{\mathcal{M}},\tilde{\bm{g}})
extends, on
(\mathcal{M},{\bm{g}})
to a conformal Killing vector
\xi^{AA^{\prime}}
so that

\tilde{\xi}^{AA^{\prime}}=\xi^{AA^{\prime}}\qquad\mbox{on}\qquad\mathcal{M}\setminus\mathscr{I}^{+}.

Lowering indices in the definition of
\tilde{\xi}^{AA^{\prime}}
using (
13
) yields

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \Xi^{-2}\xi_{AA^{\prime}}=\tilde{\xi}_{AA^{\prime}}&=\tilde{\nabla}_{A^{\prime}}{}^{Q}\tilde{\kappa}_{AQ}\\
&=\tilde{\epsilon}^{QP}\tilde{\nabla}_{A^{\prime}P}\tilde{\kappa}_{AQ}\\
&=\Xi\epsilon^{QP}(\nabla_{A^{\prime}P}(\Xi^{-2}\kappa_{AQ})+\Upsilon_{A^{\prime}A}\Xi^{-2}\kappa_{PQ}+\Upsilon_{A^{\prime}Q}\Xi^{-2}\kappa_{AP})\\
&=\Xi^{-1}\nabla_{A^{\prime}}{}^{Q}\kappa_{AQ}-3\Xi^{-2}(\nabla_{A}{}^{Q}\Xi)\kappa_{AQ}.\end{aligned}

where we have used that
\tilde{\epsilon}^{AB}=\Xi\epsilon^{AB}
and have set
\Upsilon_{AA^{\prime}}\equiv\Xi^{-1}\nabla_{AA^{\prime}}\Xi
. Thus

\xi_{AA^{\prime}}=\Xi\nabla_{A^{\prime}}{}^{Q}\kappa_{AQ}-3(\nabla_{A^{\prime}}{}^{Q}\Xi)\kappa_{AQ}

(25)

is a conformal Killing vector. The expression for

Moreover, one also has that

\xi^{AA^{\prime}}\nabla_{AA^{\prime}}\Xi=\frac{1}{4}\Xi\nabla_{AA^{\prime}}\xi^{AA^{\prime}}.

3.2

Initial data for the Killing spinor candidate, equation (
24

3.5
A Killing spinor characterisation of Kerr

The interest on the notion of Killing spinors in this article stems from the fact that they provide a characterisation of the Kerr family of spacetimes —see e.g.
[
13
]
. In this section, we outline how the Kerr family can be characterised by Killing spinors.

Let
\tilde{\xi}^{a}
be a Killing vector on the spacetime
(\tilde{\mathcal{M}},\tilde{\bm{g}})
with spinorial counterpart
\tilde{\xi}^{AA^{\prime}}
. The
Ernst form
of the Killing vector
\tilde{\xi}^{a}
is the covector

\tilde{\chi}_{a}\equiv 2\tilde{\xi}^{b}\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{ba},

where
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{ab}
is the
Killing form
of
\tilde{\xi}^{a}
—that is

\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{ab}\equiv\tilde{\nabla}_{[a}\tilde{\xi}_{b]}=\tilde{\nabla}_{a}\tilde{\xi}_{b}.

The Ernst form is closed and, thus, on simply connected domains also exact. The
Ernst potential

\tilde{\chi}
is the scalar field such that

\tilde{\chi}_{a}=\tilde{\nabla}_{a}\tilde{\chi}.

Let
\tilde{H}_{AA^{\prime}BB^{\prime}}
denote the spinorial counterpart of the Killing form
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{ab}
. As a consequence of the antisymmetry of the latter one has that

\tilde{H}_{AA^{\prime}BB}=\tilde{\eta}_{AB}\epsilon_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}+\bar{\tilde{\eta}}_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}\epsilon_{AB},

where
\tilde{\eta}_{AB}=\tilde{\eta}_{(AB)}
. It can be readily verified that

\tilde{\eta}_{AB}=\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\nabla}_{AA^{\prime}}\tilde{\xi}_{B}{}^{A^{\prime}}.

(36)

Finally, define

\tilde{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\equiv 8\tilde{\eta}_{AB}\tilde{\eta}^{AB}.

Of particular importance in the characterisation of the Kerr spacetime is an
alignment condition
between the Weyl tensor and the Killing form. More precisely, we say that the Weyl tensor and the Killing spinor are aligned if there exists a scalar function
\tilde{H}
such that the spinor
\Psi_{ABCD}
and
\tilde{\eta}_{AB}
are related via

\Psi_{ABCD}=2\tilde{H}\tilde{\eta}_{(AB}\tilde{\eta}_{CD)}.

(37)

In
[
13
]
the following theorem was proven characterising the Kerr spacetime.

Theorem 2
.

Let
(\tilde{\mathcal{M}},\tilde{\bm{g}})
denote a smooth vacuum spacetime endowed with
a Killing spinor
\tilde{\kappa}_{AB}
satisfying
\tilde{\kappa}_{AB}\tilde{\kappa}^{AB}\neq 0
,
such that the spinor
\tilde{\xi}_{AA^{\prime}}\equiv\tilde{\nabla}^{B}{}_{A^{\prime}}\tilde{\kappa}_{AB}
is
Hermitian. Then there exist two complex constants
\mathfrak{l}
and

\mathfrak{c}
such that

\tilde{H}=\frac{6}{\mathfrak{c}-\tilde{\chi}},\qquad\tilde{\mathcal{H}}^{2}=-\mathfrak{l}(\mathfrak{c}-\tilde{\chi})^{4}.

(38)

Remark 7
.

The values of the constants
\mathfrak{l}
and
\mathfrak{c}
can be used to characterise the Kerr solution. For example, in
[
13
]
it was shown that if the spacetime admits an asymptotically stationary flat end then a sufficient condition to single out Kerr is that
\mathfrak{c}=1
and
\mathfrak{l}
is a positive real number. Depending on the context, other sufficient conditions may be more convenient.

Concluding remarks

In this article, we have obtained a characteristaion of the Kerr spacetime which is based on the asymptotic characteristic initial value problem for the conformal Einstein field equations. The characterisation involves conditions
for a spinor
\kappa_{AB}
on
\mathcal{N}_{\star}\cup{\mathscr{I}^{+}}
. Despite this, the characterisation is constructive. Making use of the algebraic Killing spinor candidate —see Subsection
3.4
— all the conditions in Theorem
3
can be verified. Observe that from this perspective, all the conditions on the components of the spinor
\kappa_{AB}
are, in fact, conditions on the components of the Weyl curvature.

A possible alternative formulation of the characterisation, not considered in this article, is to work in the setting of an asymptotic characteristic problem on a cone. The vertex of this cone would correspond to timelike infinity. While the Kerr spacetime does not have this type of asymptotic structure, there exist, nevertheless, a large class of spacetimes which are Kerr in a neighbourhood of spatial. These spacetimes can be constructed using the Corvino-Schoen gluing method for initial data sets
[
14
]
—see also
[
9
]
. This alternative type of asymptotic characterisation of spacetimes with a Kerrian portion of null infinity will be discussed alsewhere.

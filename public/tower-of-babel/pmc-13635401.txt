Dermal sling as an alternative to palmaris longus tendon suspension in radial forearm flap lower lip reconstruction: A case report
Authors: Ata Garajei, Zahra Sadat Modarresi, Ali Arab Kheradmand, Ali Nazarpour Yousefdeh, Zeinab Bakhtiari
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundLower lip reconstruction with the radial forearm free flap (RFFF) requires reliable static suspension to maintain oral competence. The palmaris longus tendon (PLT) is commonly used for this purpose, but its absence limits applicability. We describe a dermal sling harvested from the RFFF as an alternative suspension method. Case presentation After total lower lip resection for squamous cell carcinoma, reconstruction was performed using an RFFF. A full-thickness dermal strip was harvested from the proximal flap, deepithelialized, and used as a non-tunneled, onlay suspension sling anchored bilaterally to the orbicularis oris muscle.ResultsAt 2-year follow-up, stable lower lip support was achieved with preserved oral competence, intelligible speech, and adequate oral intake. No drooling, flap failure, or donor-site morbidity occurred.ConclusionsThe dermal sling provides a simple and reproducible alternative to PLT-based suspension in RFFF lower lip reconstruction, avoiding additional donor-site morbidity while ensuring durable static support.Keywords: Radial forearm free flap, Lip reconstruction, Dermal sling, Palmaris longus substitution, Surgical technique

Introduction

Reconstruction of large lower lip defects remains a complex challenge in reconstructive surgery, as both functional oral competence and aesthetic restoration must be achieved simultaneously. Defects involving more than two-thirds of the lower lip are particularly difficult to manage using local flaps alone and often require free tissue transfer techniques.1,2

The radial forearm free flap (RFFF) is widely regarded as a reliable option for lower lip reconstruction due to its thin, pliable skin, long vascular pedicle, and versatility in three-dimensional shaping. To restore static support and prevent drooling, incorporation of the palmaris longus tendon (PLT) as a suspension sling has become a well-established modification since its original description. However, PLT agenesis has been reported in a significant proportion of patients, limiting the applicability of this technique.3, 4, 5

Various alternatives, including fascia lata grafts and fascial slings, have been described, but they introduce additional donor-site morbidity and operative complexity. In this case report, we present a simple and reproducible modification using a dermal sling harvested from the RFFF itself as a substitute for the PLT in lower lip reconstruction. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first description of a dermal sling harvested from the RFFF and anchored bilaterally as a substitute for PLT.

Case presentation

A 33-year-old man with no significant medical history presented with a progressively enlarging ulcerative lesion of the lower lip. Incisional biopsy confirmed well-differentiated squamous cell carcinoma. Definitive surgical resection was performed approximately 2 months after the initial biopsy following preoperative evaluation and treatment planning.

The patient underwent total lower lip resection with 1 cm oncologic margins after confirmation of tumor-free borders on frozen section analysis. Preoperative and intraoperative details are shown in Supplementary Figure 1 and Fig. 2.

Reconstruction was performed using an RFFF.

During flap harvest, a full-thickness dermal strip approximately 1 cm in width was designed along the proximal base of the flap, adjacent to the pedicle region, and outside the skin paddle planned for external and mucosal lip reconstruction. The dermal strip was harvested from an area that was not required for flap inset, thereby preserving the available skin paddle for reconstruction. Flap dimensions were planned preoperatively to accommodate both the reconstructive surface requirements and the dermal sling harvest without compromising flap design or coverage.

The epidermis was removed to reduce the risk of epithelial inclusion cyst formation, leaving a dermal component for suspension. Flap perfusion was assessed clinically, and no evidence of vascular compromise was observed.

Following microvascular anastomosis, the dermal sling was positioned in a non-tunneled, onlay fashion beneath the reconstructed lower lip. The dermal sling was anchored bilaterally to the residual orbicularis oris muscle and adjacent perioral soft tissues, thereby creating a stable bilateral supportive scaffold for lower lip suspension.

The radial forearm free flap was then inset over this dermal support layer. Lip height, contour, and tension were adjusted by fine-tuning the bilateral dermal anchoring to achieve appropriate suspension without excessive traction on the flap or compromise of vascular inflow. (Fig. 1) The postoperative course was uneventful. The patient subsequently received adjuvant radiotherapy consisting of 30 sessions.

Intraoperative photograph of the free flap donor site. Note the dermal sling extending along the flap. The sling is deepithelialized and ready to attach to the recipient bed to maintain the lip structure.

Outcome

Postoperative recovery was uneventful, and flap viability remained stable throughout the follow-up period.

At 3 months, 1 year, and 2 years of follow-up, the patient demonstrated a stable lower lip position with preserved oral competence based on clinical evaluation and patient-reported outcomes. No clinically evident drooling or significant functional limitation in oral intake was observed. Speech remained intelligible during routine communication.

Functional outcomes were assessed using the validated University of Washington Quality of Life questionnaire (UW-QOL version 4). At the 2-year follow-up, the composite UW-QOL score, calculated from the 12 domain scores, was 93/100, indicating an overall favorable functional status. Domain-specific scores were as follows: pain (100), appearance (50), activity (100), recreation (100), swallowing (100), chewing (100), speech (100), shoulder function (100), taste (100), saliva (100), mood (100), and anxiety (70). Maximum scores in domains related to oral competence—including swallowing, chewing, and speech—highlight preserved functional performance, while the moderate appearance score reflects residual aesthetic concern.

Global quality-of-life scores were 25, 80, and 60. The lower score in comparison with pre-cancer quality of life reflects the patient’s subjective perception of a decline relative to baseline health status.

Aesthetic evaluation based on clinical observation suggested acceptable symmetry, contour, and lip height; however, no standardized photographic or quantitative assessment was performed.

Donor-site morbidity was minimal, and the patient reported high satisfaction with both functional and aesthetic outcomes (Fig. 2). Functional mobility of the reconstructed lower lip at one-year follow-up is demonstrated in Supplementary Figure 3.

Discussion

Static suspension is a critical component of successful lower lip reconstruction using free tissue transfer. While the RFFF provides excellent soft tissue coverage, it lacks intrinsic muscular support, making suspension techniques essential to restore lip competence.6

The PLT-based sling technique remains widely used and effective; however, its dependence on tendon availability limits universal applicability. In addition, harvesting of tendon or fascia lata may increase operative time and create secondary donor-site morbidity.

The dermal sling technique described in this report offers several advantages. First, it eliminates the need for a secondary donor site, as the suspension material is harvested from the flap itself. Second, it simplifies the reconstructive procedure by integrating suspension design into the flap harvest phase. Third, dermal tissue may not provide the same tensile properties as tendon; however, in this case, it demonstrated clinically acceptable support over the follow-up period.

In this case, the dermal sling maintained stable lip position over a 2-year follow-up period without evidence of elongation or failure. The functional outcome was comparable to traditional tendon-based techniques, with preservation of oral competence and acceptable aesthetic contour. The long-term stability is likely related to dermal collagen remodeling and incorporation into surrounding fibrous tissue. The UW-QOL v4 results further support the favorable functional outcome observed in this case, particularly in domains related to oral competence such as swallowing, chewing, and speech, all of which achieved maximum scores. The moderate score in the appearance domain and slightly lower anxiety score highlight areas where subjective patient perception may differ from clinical assessment. Given the single-case design, these findings should be interpreted cautiously but provide supportive evidence for the functional adequacy of the dermal sling technique.

Despite its advantages, this technique has several limitations. Harvesting a dermal strip may reduce the available skin paddle, particularly in larger defects, requiring careful preoperative planning. Additionally, dermal tissue may be subject to elongation (dermal creep) over time, which could affect long-term suspension. There is also a theoretical risk of inclusion cyst formation despite deepithelialization. Achieving symmetrical tension bilaterally may be technically demanding, and a learning curve should be expected. Furthermore, the biomechanical properties of dermis differ from tendon, and its use as a suspension substitute has not been validated in comparative studies.

However, this report is limited to a single case, and further studies with larger patient cohorts and objective functional assessments are required to validate reproducibility, long-term durability, and comparative effectiveness against established techniques. Although UW-QOL v4 provided standardized patient-reported assessment, dedicated instruments for oral competence, drooling, and speech intelligibility were not used and should be incorporated in future studies.

Conclusion

The dermal sling harvested from the radial forearm flap represents a simple and effective alternative to palmaris longus tendon grafting in lower lip reconstruction. This technique avoids additional donor-site morbidity while providing reliable static support for oral competence and aesthetic restoration. Further studies with larger cohorts and standardized functional assessments are required to validate the reproducibility and long-term durability of this technique.

Complete flags on flat vector bundles in positive characteristic
Youhei Morita, Yasuhiro Wakabayashi

Abstract
. Let X X be a connected, smooth, and projective curve of genus g g over an algebraically closed field of characteristic p > 0 p>0 . This paper investigates a characteristic- p p analogue of a well-known fact concerning flat vector bundles in characteristic zero. That is to say, we prove that the inequality g ≤ 1 g\leq 1 holds if and only if any flat vector bundle on X X admits a complete flag. We also explore a generalization of this result in a broader setting using Berthelot’s differencial operators of level m m .

1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a connected, smooth, and projective curve of genus
g\geq 0
over an algebraically closed field
k
. By a flat vector bundle on
X
, we mean a pair
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
, consisting of a vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
X
and a (flat) connection
\nabla
on it.
When the base field
k
is the field of complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
,
a flat vector bundle can be regarded as a
\mathcal{D}
-module,
where
\mathcal{D}
is the sheaf of differential operators on
X
, and corresponds,
via the most basic form of the Riemann-Hilbert correspondence, to a group representation

\displaystyle\rho_{(\mathcal{E},\nabla)}:\pi_{1}(X^{\mathrm{an}})\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})

(1.1)

uniquely given up to conjugation,
where
r:=\mathrm{rank}(\mathcal{E})
and
\pi_{1}(X^{an})
denotes the fundamental group of the associated Riemann surface
X^{\mathrm{an}}
(with respect to a fixed base point).
Flat vector bundles and their associated group representations have been extensively studied in various contexts, including non-Abelian Hodge theory and the geometric Langlands program.

Now, let us focus on a curve of genus
g=1
; see
[
9
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
25
]
, and (a part of)
[
29
]
for studies on such objects.
One of the fundamental facts about these bundles when
k=\mathbb{C}

is the existence of a
complete flag
(i.e., a
splitting
, in the terminology of
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
) preserved by a given connection.
Specifically, for any flat vector bundle
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
on the complex curve
X/\mathbb{C}
with
g=1
,
there exists a sequence of subbundles

\displaystyle 0=\mathcal{E}^{0}\subseteq\mathcal{E}^{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathcal{E}^{r}=\mathcal{E}

(1.2)

such that, for every
j=1,\cdots,r
, the subquotient
\mathcal{E}^{j}/\mathcal{E}^{j-1}
is a line bundle and
\mathcal{E}^{j}
is closed under
\nabla
.
In particular,

(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
can be obtained as a successive extension of flat line bundles, which allows for a detailed understanding of the moduli space of flat vector bundles on
X
.

The proof of this fact is elementary.
Indeed,
since
\pi_{1}(X^{\mathrm{an}})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, the image of the group representation
\rho_{(\mathcal{E},\nabla)}
can be simultaneously upper triangularizable,
which implies the desired result.
The same statement holds for genus-
0
curves, as their fundamental groups are trivial.
On the other hand, when the underlying curve is hyperbolic, i.e.,
g>1
, there exist many irreducible group representations
\pi_{1}(X^{\mathrm{an}})\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
, and the corresponding flat vector bundles do not admit complete flags as in (
1.2
).
It follows that
the existence of such flags depends entirely on the genus of the underlying curve
, and a standard base-change argument shows that this fact generalizes to the case where
k
is an arbitrary algebraically closed field of characteristic zero.

Then, a natural question is whether a similar result holds in characteristic
p>0
.
To the best of our knowledge, the analogous result in positive characteristic has not been established, and the proof outlined above does not apply in this setting because it fundamentally relies on the analytic nature of the Riemann-Hilbert correspondence.
Moreover, some flat vector bundles in characteristic
p
cannot be obtained by reducing those in characteristic zero modulo
p
.
Therefore, to establish a positive characteristic analogue of the aforementioned result,
we need to address phenomena that are specific to characteristic
p
.

Let us suppose that
k
has characteristic
p
.
We should note that
there are different variations of
the sheaf of differential operators “
\mathcal{D}
” on
X
.
On the one hand, the sheaf
\mathcal{D}^{(\infty)}
of differential operators in the sense of A. Grothendieck was discussed in

[
21
]
.
A
\mathcal{D}^{(\infty)}
-module is often referred to as a
stratified sheaf
and can be interpreted as an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module admitting infinite Frobenius descent.
On the other hand, the sheaves
\mathcal{D}^{(m)}
of differential operators of
level
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
were introduced by P. Berthelot (cf.
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
) as essential ingredients in defining arithmetic
\mathcal{D}
-modules.
A
\mathcal{D}^{(0)}
-module structure amounts essentially to a choice of a flat connection in the usual sense.

As described below, our main result provides a necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of a complete flag on a
\mathcal{D}^{(m)}
-bundle (i.e., a vector bundle equipped with a
\mathcal{D}^{(m)}
-action).

Theorem A

(cf. Theorems
3.2
,
4.6
, and
5.2
)
.

Let
m
be an element of
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\sqcup\{\infty\}
and
r
a positive integer.
Then,
the following two conditions (a) and (b) are equivalent:

(a)

The inequality
g\leq 1
holds;

(b)

For any given rank
r
, any
\mathcal{D}^{(m)}
-bundle
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
of rank
r
admits a complete flag, i.e., there exists a sequence of subbundles

\displaystyle 0=\mathcal{E}^{0}\subset\mathcal{E}^{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathcal{E}^{r}=\mathcal{E}

(1.3)

of
\mathcal{E}
such that, for every
j=1,\cdots,r
, the subquotient
\mathcal{E}^{j}/\mathcal{E}^{j-1}
is a line bundle and
\mathcal{E}^{j}
is preserved by
\nabla
.

Remark 1.1
.

As pointed out by an anonymous referee, it is highly expected that a more general reduction theory holds for principal
G
-bundles, where
G
is a geometrically connected, reductive algebraic group over an algebraically closed (or perfect) field such that
G
is of low height with respect to the characteristic
p>0
. While we focus on vector bundles (i.e.,
\mathrm{GL}_{r}
-bundles) in this paper, developing the precise analogue for such principal
G
-bundles is an important direction for future research.

2.1.
Notation and Conventions

Throughout this paper, we fix a prime number
p
and an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic
p
.
We also fix
a connected, smooth, and projective curve
X
over
k
of genus
g\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
Denote by
\Omega_{X}
(resp.,
\mathcal{T}_{X}
) the cotangent bundle (resp., the tangent bundle) of
X/k
and by
d
the universal derivation
\mathcal{O}_{X}\rightarrow\Omega_{X}
.

Denote by
F_{X}
the absolute Frobenius endomorphism of
X
.
For a positive integer
m
,
the
m
-th Frobenius twist
of
X
over
k
is, by definition,
the base-change
X^{(m)}
of
X
along the
m
-th iterate of the absolute Frobenius automorphism of
\mathrm{Spec}(k)
.
The morphism
F_{X/k}^{(m)}:X\rightarrow X^{(m)}
induced naturally from
F_{X}^{m}
is called the
m
-th relative Frobenius morphism
of
X
over
k
.
For convenience,
we write
X^{(0)}:=X
,
F_{X/k}^{(0)}:=\mathrm{id}_{X}
, and
F_{X/k}:=F^{(1)}_{X/k}
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a vector bundle (i.e., a locally free coherent sheaf) on
X
.
We shall say that
\mathcal{E}
is
decomposable
if there exist vector bundles
\mathcal{E}_{1}
and
\mathcal{E}_{2}
of positive ranks such that
\mathcal{E}\cong\mathcal{E}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{E}_{2}
.
A vector bundle is called
indecomposable
if it is not decomposable.

According to
[
1
, Theorem 2]
,
any such vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
is isomorphic to a direct sum of indecomposable vector bundles, unique up to reordering.
We refer to a vector bundle
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
as an
indecomposable component
of
\mathcal{E}
if it appears as the direct summand in such a decomposition (cf.
[
10
, Definition 2.3]
).

2.2.
Connections on vector bundles

A
(
k
-)connection
on an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module
\mathcal{E}
is defined as a
k
-linear morphism
\nabla:\mathcal{E}\rightarrow\Omega_{X}\otimes\mathcal{E}
satisfying the Leibniz rule, i.e,
\nabla(a\cdot v)=a\cdot\nabla(v)+da\otimes v
for any local sections
a\in\mathcal{O}_{X}
,
v\in\mathcal{E}
.
A
flat vector bundle

X
is a pair
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
consisting of a vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
X
and a
k
-connection
\nabla
on
\mathcal{E}
.
When
\mathcal{E}
has rank one, such a pair is called a
flat line bundle
.

If we are given two flat vector bundles
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
,
(\mathcal{E}^{\prime},\nabla^{\prime})
on
X
, then the tensor product
\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\left(:=\mathcal{E}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{E}\right)
admits a connection

\displaystyle\nabla\otimes\nabla^{\prime}:\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\rightarrow\Omega_{X}\otimes(\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{E}^{\prime})

(2.1)

defined by
(\nabla\otimes\nabla^{\prime})(a\otimes b)=\nabla(a)\otimes b+a\otimes\nabla^{\prime}(b)
for any local sections
a\in\mathcal{E}
,
a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
.
Also, given a flat vector bundle
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
, one can define a connection
\nabla^{\vee}
on the dual bundle
\mathcal{E}^{\vee}:=\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{O}_{X})
in a natural manner (cf.
[
24
, § 1.1]
).

2.3.
\mathcal{D}^{(m)}
-modules

For each
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
let
\mathcal{D}_{X}^{(m)}
denote
the sheaf of differential operators of level
m
on
X/k
(cf.
[
4
, § 2.2]
), where
\mathrm{Spec}(k)
is equipped with the trivial
m
-PD structure.
It defines a sheaf of (noncommutative)
k
-algebras and acts naturally on
\mathcal{O}_{X}
.
When there is no fear of confusion, we will write
\mathcal{D}^{(m)}:=\mathcal{D}_{X}^{(m)}
for simplicity.
There exists a natural inductive system of sheaves

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{(0)}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{(1)}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{(m)}\rightarrow\cdots

(2.2)

(cf.
[
4
, § 2,2]
).
The limit of this system

\mathcal{D}^{(\infty)}:=\varinjlim_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\mathcal{D}^{(m)}
is called the sheaf of differential operators of
level
\infty
and coincides with the sheaf discussed in
[
21
, § 16.8]
.
(Note that if
k
has characteristic zero, then
\mathcal{D}_{X}^{(1)}=\mathcal{D}_{X}^{(2)}=\dots=\mathcal{D}_{X}
.)

For
m,j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\sqcup\{\infty\}
, we write
\mathcal{D}_{\leq j}^{(m)}

for the subsheaf of
\mathcal{D}^{(m)}
consisting of differential operators of order
\leq j
, with the convention that
\mathcal{D}^{(m)}_{\leq\infty}:=\mathcal{D}^{(m)}
.
Thus, we have
\mathcal{D}^{(m)}=\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\mathcal{D}^{(m)}_{\leq j}
.
We denote by
{{}^{L}\mathcal{D}}^{(m)}_{\leq j}
(resp.,
{{}^{R}\mathcal{D}}^{(m)}_{\leq j}
) the sheaf
\mathcal{D}^{(m)}_{\leq j}
endowed with a structure of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module induced by left (resp., right) multiplication.
Given an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module
\mathcal{E}
, we equip the tensor product
\mathcal{D}_{\leq}^{(m)}\otimes\mathcal{E}:={{}^{R}}\mathcal{D}_{\leq j}^{(m)}\otimes\mathcal{E}
with the
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module structure given by left multiplication.

Let
\mathcal{E}nd_{k}(\mathcal{E})
denote the sheaf of locally defined
k
-linear endomorphisms of
\mathcal{E}
endowed with a structure of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module given by left multiplication. Given an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module
\mathcal{E}
, we equip the tensor product
\mathcal{D}_{\leq j}^{(m)}\otimes\mathcal{E}:={}^{R}\mathcal{D}_{\leq j}^{(m)}\otimes\mathcal{E}
with the
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module structure given by left multiplication.
A
\mathcal{D}^{(m)}
-module structure
on
\mathcal{E}
is an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-linear morphism of sheaves of
k
-algebras
\nabla:{{}^{L}}\mathcal{D}^{(m)}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}nd_{k}(\mathcal{E})
. Equivalently, specifying a
\mathcal{D}^{(m)}
-module structure on
\mathcal{E}
is a homomorphism
\nabla
extending the
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module structure to a left
\mathcal{D}^{(m)}
-module structure.
By a
\mathcal{D}^{(m)}
-bundle
, we mean a pair
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
consisting of a vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
X
and a
\mathcal{D}^{(m)}
-module structure
\nabla
on it. A
morphism of
\mathcal{D}^{(m)}
-bundles
from
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
to
(\mathcal{E}^{\prime},\nabla^{\prime})
is defined as an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-linear morphism
f:\mathcal{E}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
that is compatible with the respective
\mathcal{D}^{(m)}
-module structures.

A
line
\mathcal{D}^{(m)}
-bundle
is a
\mathcal{D}^{(m)}
-bundle
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
such that
\mathcal{E}
has rank
1
.
For example, if

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathrm{triv}}^{(m)}:{{}^{L}}\mathcal{D}^{(m)}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}nd_{k}(\mathcal{O}_{X})

(2.3)

denotes the natural
\mathcal{D}^{(m)}
-action on
\mathcal{O}_{X}
mentioned above,
then
(\mathcal{O}_{X},\nabla_{\mathrm{triv}}^{(m)})
specifies
a line
\mathcal{D}^{(m)}
-bundle.

Let
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
be a
\mathcal{D}^{(m)}
-bundle.
A
(line)
\mathcal{D}^{(m)}
-subbundle
of
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
is
a (line) subbundle of
\mathcal{E}
that is stable under the
\mathcal{D}^{(m)}
-action
\nabla
.
Each
\mathcal{D}^{(m)}
-subbundle
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{E}
naturally inherits
a
\mathcal{D}^{(m)}
-bundle structure, given by restricting
\nabla
.

Finally, it is verified (cf.
[
6
, Theorem 4.8]
) that giving a
\mathcal{D}^{(0)}
-module structure on a fixed
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module
\mathcal{E}
amounts to giving a connection on
\mathcal{E}
.

Remark 2.1

\mathrm{Str}(X)

According to

Here, recall (cf.

\displaystyle\{(\mathcal{E}_{m},\alpha_{m})\}_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}},

(or simply

2.4.
Frobenius pull-back of
\mathcal{D}^{(m)}
-modules

Suppose that

, and let

(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
be

(cf.

2.5.
Complete flags on
\mathcal{D}^{(m)}
-bundles

Let
m
be an element of
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\sqcup\{\infty\}
and

\mathcal{E}
a vector bundle on
X
of rank
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.
Recall that a
complete flag
on
\mathcal{E}
is defined as an
r
-step increasing filtration

\displaystyle 0=\mathcal{E}^{0}\subseteq\mathcal{E}^{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathcal{E}^{r}=\mathcal{E}

(2.13)

such that
all the subquotients
\mathcal{E}^{j}/\mathcal{E}^{j-1}
are line bundles.
The following definition introduces the
\mathcal{D}^{(m)}
-module version of this notion.

Definition 2.2
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{E}
is equipped with a
\mathcal{D}^{(m)}
-module structure
\nabla
.
A
complete flag
on
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
is
a complete flag
\{\mathcal{E}^{j}\}_{j=0}^{r}
on
\mathcal{E}
such that each subbundle
\mathcal{E}^{j}
(
j=0,\cdots,r
) is preserved by
\nabla
.

The equivalence of categories (
2.10
) leads to the following result, which reduces the existence problem of complete flags for
m<\infty
to the case
m=0
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
m
,
s
be nonnegative integers and
(\mathcal{G},\nabla)
a
\mathcal{D}^{(m)}_{X^{(s)}}
-bundle,
and denote by
(F^{(s)*}_{X/k}(\mathcal{G}),F^{(s)*}_{X/k}(\nabla))
the
\mathcal{D}_{X}^{(m+s)}
-bundle
corresponding to
(\mathcal{G},\nabla)
via (
2.10
).
Then,
(\mathcal{G},\nabla)
admits a complete flag if and only if
(F^{(s)*}_{X/k}(\mathcal{G}),F^{(s)*}_{X/k}(\nabla))
admits a complete flag.

Furthermore, the following assertion is well-known at least for
m=0
and follows immediately from the case
m=0
by applying
Proposition
2.3
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
m
be a nonnegative integer and
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
a
\mathcal{D}^{(m)}
-bundle such that
\psi^{m+1}(\nabla)
is nilpotent.
Then,
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
admits a complete flag.

Proof.

By the equivalence of categories (
2.12
),
the problem is reduced to the case
m=0
.
We prove the assertion for
m=0
by induction on the rank of
\mathcal{E}
.
There is nothing to prove when
\mathcal{E}
has rank
1
.
For the induction step, suppose that
\mathrm{rank}(\mathcal{E})>1
.
It follows from
[
24
, Corollary 5.5]
that
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
admits a nonzero flat subbundle
(\mathcal{F},\nabla_{\mathcal{F}})
with vanishing
p
-curvature.
By the equivalence of categories (
2.9
) for
m=0
,

(\mathcal{F},\nabla_{\mathcal{F}})
descends to a vector bundle
\mathcal{G}
on
X^{(1)}
along
F_{X/k}
.
Since the underlying space
X
is a smooth projective curve, we can find a line subbundle of
\mathcal{G}
.
This corresponds to a flat line subbundle
(\mathcal{H},\nabla_{\mathcal{H}})
of
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
via (
2.9
).
Let
\overline{\nabla}
denote the connection on
\overline{\mathcal{E}}:=\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{H}
induced from
\nabla
via the quotient
\pi:\mathcal{E}\twoheadrightarrow\overline{\mathcal{E}}
. Since
\overline{\nabla}
has nilpotent
p
-curvature, the induction hypothesis implies that
(\overline{\mathcal{E}},\overline{\nabla})
admits a complete flag
\{\overline{\mathcal{E}}^{j}\}_{j}
.
Hence, the collection
\{\mathcal{E}^{j}\}_{j}
defined as
\mathcal{E}^{0}:=0
and
\mathcal{E}^{j+1}:=\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{E}^{j})
(

) forms the desired complete flag on

.
This completes the proof.
∎

3.
The case of projective line

This section focuses on the case where the underlying curve
X
has genus
0
, i.e.,
X
is isomorphic to the projective line.
In particular, we describe vector bundles on
X
admitting a
\mathcal{D}^{(m)}
-module structure (cf. Proposition
3.1
) and prove the existence of complete flags (cf. Theorem
3.2
).

3.1.
Vector bundles admitting a
\mathcal{D}^{(m)}
-module structure

Denote by
\mathbb{P}
the projective line over
k
throughout this section.
(We use the notation “
\mathbb{P}
” as opposed to the usual notation “
\mathbb{P}^{1}
” because later on we will need to consider its
m
-th Frobenius twist for
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
\mathbb{P}^{(m)}
is notationally and typographically simpler than
(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{(m)}
.)
For each
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
, the unique (up to isomorphism) line bundle on
\mathbb{P}
of degree
\ell
is denoted by
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}
.
Recall the Birkhoff-Grothendieck theorem (cf.
[
20
]
), which states that any vector bundle on
\mathbb{P}
is isomorphic to a direct sum of line bundles of the form
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}
.
In particular,
every indecomposable vector bundle on
\mathbb{P}
has rank
1
.
Applying this theorem, one can prove the following result, generalizing
the classification of flat vector bundles in the genus-
0
case (cf.
[
2
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
10
]
, and
[
31
]
).

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
m
be a nonnegative integer and
\mathcal{E}
a vector bundle on
X\left(=\mathbb{P}\right)
of rank
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.
Then, the following three conditions (a)-(c) are equivalent:

(a)

\mathcal{E}
admits a
\mathcal{D}^{(m)}
-module structure;

(b)

\mathcal{E}
admits a
\mathcal{D}^{(m)}
-module structure with vanishing
p^{m+1}
-curvature;

(c)

\mathcal{E}\cong\bigoplus\limits_{i=1}^{r}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(p^{m+1}_{i})
for some
\ell_{1},\cdots,\ell_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

(b)
\Rightarrow
(a) is clear.
To prove (a)
\Rightarrow
(c),
we assume that
\mathcal{E}
admits a
\mathcal{D}^{(m)}
-module structure
\nabla_{\mathcal{E}}
.
By the equivalence of categories (
2.10
),
(\mathcal{E},\nabla_{\mathcal{E}})
corresponds to a flat vector bundle
(\mathcal{G},\nabla_{\mathcal{G}})
on
X^{(m)}\left(=\mathbb{P}^{(m)}\right)
via pull-back by
F^{(m)}_{\mathbb{P}/k}
.
It follows from the Birkhoff-Grothendieck theorem that
\mathcal{G}\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{(m)}}(\ell^{\prime}_{i})
for some
\ell^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,\ell^{\prime}_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}
.
As proved in the references listed above,
there exists a collection
(\ell_{1},\cdots,\ell_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}
with

\ell^{\prime}_{i}=p\ell_{i}
for every
i
.
Hence, we have

\mathcal{E}\cong F^{(m)*}_{{\mathbb{P}}/k}(\mathcal{G})\cong F^{(m)*}_{{\mathbb{P}}/k}(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{(m)}}(p\ell_{i}))\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}F^{(m)*}_{{\mathbb{P}}/k}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{(m)}}(p\ell_{i}))\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(p^{m+1}\ell_{i}).

Thus,
\mathcal{E}
satisfies
the condition (c).

Finally, we shall prove (c)
\Rightarrow
(b).
Assume that
\mathcal{E}=\bigoplus\limits_{i=1}^{r}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(p^{m+1}\ell_{i})
for some
\ell_{1},\cdots,\ell_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}
.
By putting
\mathcal{G}:=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{(m+1)}}(\ell_{i})
, we obtain a chain of isomorphisms

\displaystyle F^{(m+1)*}_{{\mathbb{P}}/k}(\mathcal{G})\xrightarrow{\sim}\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}F^{(m+1)*}_{{\mathbb{P}}/k}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{(m+1)}}(\ell_{i}))\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{E}.

(3.1)

Hence,

\nabla_{\mathcal{G},\mathrm{can}}^{(m)}
(cf. (
2.8
)) defines a
\mathcal{D}^{(m)}
-module structure on
\mathcal{E}
(with vanishing
p^{m+1}
-curvature) via this composite.
In particular,
\mathcal{E}
satisfies the condition (b).
This completes the proof.
∎

3.2.
The existence of complete flags

Applying Proposition
3.1
, we can prove the following assertion,
which is a part of Theorem
A
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose that
X=\mathbb{P}
.
Let
m
be an element of
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\sqcup\{\infty\}
and
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
a (nonzero)
\mathcal{D}^{(m)}
-bundle on
X
.
Then,
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
admits a complete flag.

Proof.

In the case of
m=\infty
, we recall from
[
17
, Theorem 2.2]
that the stratified fundamental group
\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{str}}(\mathbb{P})
of
\mathbb{P}
(cf. Remark
2.1
) is trivial.
That is, every
\mathcal{D}^{(\infty)}
-bundle is isomorphic to a direct sum of finitely many copies of
(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}},\nabla_{\mathrm{triv}}^{(\infty)})
, so the assertion for
m=\infty
follows immediately from this fact.

For
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
the problem is reduced to the case of
m=0
by Proposition
2.3
.
Let us prove the assertion for
m=0

by induction on
r:=\mathrm{rank}(\mathcal{E})
.
There is nothing to prove in the base case of rank
r=1
.
To consider the induction step, we assume that the assertion
for rank
r-1
flat vector bundles
(where
r\geq 2
) has been proved.
The equivalence (a)
\Leftrightarrow
(c) in Proposition
3.1
implies

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(p\ell_{i})^{\oplus e_{i}}

(3.2)

for
\ell_{1}>\ell_{2}>\cdots>\ell_{s}
(
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
) and
e_{1},\cdots,e_{s}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
(with
\sum_{i=1}^{s}e_{i}=r
).
We write

\mathcal{G}:=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(\ell_{i})^{\oplus e_{i}}
.
Then, there exists a natural isomorphism
\alpha:\mathcal{E}\xrightarrow{\sim}F^{(1)*}_{\mathbb{P}/k}(\mathcal{G})
, which allows us to regard
\nabla_{\mathcal{G},\mathrm{can}}^{(0)}
as a connection on
\mathcal{E}
.
Since the difference
\nabla-\nabla_{\mathcal{G},\mathrm{can}}^{(0)}
is
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}
-linear,

\nabla
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\nabla=\nabla_{\mathcal{G},\mathrm{can}}^{(0)}+\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq s}h_{i,j},

(3.3)

where
h_{i,j}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(p\ell_{i})^{\oplus e_{i}},\Omega_{\mathbb{P}}\otimes(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(p\ell_{j})^{\oplus e_{j}}))
.
Note that
h_{i,j}=0
whenever

i<j
, in particular, that

h_{1,j}=0
for every
j=2,\cdots,s
.
Since
\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(p\ell_{1}))=k
,
the endomorphism
h_{1,1}
can be given by an
e_{1}\times e_{1}
matrix
A\in M_{e_{1}}(k)
.
Choose an automorphism
\eta
of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(p\ell_{1})^{\oplus e_{1}}
such that, if
P\in M_{e_{1}}(k)
is the corresponding matrix, then
P^{-1}AP
is upper triangular.
The gauge transformation by the automorphism
\widetilde{\eta}:=\eta\oplus\bigoplus_{i=2}^{s}\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(p\ell_{i})^{\oplus e_{i}}}
transforms
\nabla
into another connection

\nabla^{\prime}
.
The
1
-st direct summand
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(p\ell_{1})^{\oplus e_{1}}
, regarded as a line subbundle of
\mathcal{E}
via
\alpha
, satisfies
\nabla^{\prime}(\mathcal{L}^{\prime})\subseteq\Omega_{\mathbb{P}}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
.
It follows that the line subbundle
\mathcal{L}
of
\mathcal{E}
corresponding to
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
via
\widetilde{\eta}
satisfies
\nabla(\mathcal{L})\subseteq\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\otimes\mathcal{L}
.
Write
\overline{\mathcal{E}}:=\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{L}
, and let

\overline{\nabla}
be the connection on
\overline{\mathcal{E}}
induced from
\nabla
via the natural quotient
\pi:\mathcal{E}\twoheadrightarrow\overline{\mathcal{E}}
.
By the induction hypothesis,
(\overline{\mathcal{E}},\overline{\nabla})
admits a complete flag
\{\overline{\mathcal{E}}^{j}\}_{j=0}^{r-1}
.
For each
j=0,\cdots,r
, we shall set
\mathcal{E}^{0}:=0
and
\mathcal{E}^{j}:=\pi^{-1}(\overline{\mathcal{E}}^{j-1})
(
j=1,\cdots,r
).
Then, the resulting filtration
\{\mathcal{E}^{j}\}_{j}
forms the desired complete flag.
This completes the proof.
∎

4.
The case of elliptic curves

This section is devoted to studying the case of curves of genus
1
.
Our discussion relies on Atiyah’s classification of indecomposable vector bundles on such curves.
As a consequence, we establish a part of our main theorem, proving the existence of complete flags for genus-
1
curves (cf. Theorem
4.6
).

Throughout this section, this
g=1
assumption will be in force.
In particular, by our assumption,
\Omega_{X}
is trivialized.
We fix an identification
\mathcal{O}_{X}=\Omega_{X}
by choosing a global generator of
\Omega_{X}
.
Additionally, we fix a
k
-rational point
\sigma
of
X
, regarded as an effective divisor of degree one.

4.1.
Atiyah’s classification of indecomposable bundles

We begin with reviewing the study of indecomposable vector bundles on a genus-
1
curve, discussed in
[
2
]
.

Let
V
be a finite-dimensional
k
-vector space and
\mathcal{E}
a vector bundle on
X
.
There exists a sequence of natural isomorphisms

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E},V\otimes_{k}\mathcal{L})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{Hom}_{k}(\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{L})^{\vee},V)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{Hom}_{k}(H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{E}),V),

(4.1)

where the second isomorphism arises from Serre duality and our fixed identification
\mathcal{O}_{X}=\Omega_{X}
.
Hence, each extension
\varepsilon:0\rightarrow V\otimes_{k}\mathcal{L}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}\rightarrow 0
corresponds to a
k
-linear morphism

\displaystyle\mathrm{ext}_{\mathcal{F}}\ \left(\text{or}\ \mathrm{ext}_{\varepsilon}\right):H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{E})\rightarrow V

(4.2)

via (
4.1
).
If
V=H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{E})
,
then
\mathrm{ext}_{\mathcal{F}}
defines a
k
-linear endomorphism of
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{E})
.

Now, recall from
[
2
, Theorem 5, (i)]
that, for each positive integer
r
,
there exists a unique (up to isomorphism) indecomposable vector bundle
\mathcal{F}_{r}
on
X
of rank
r
and degree
0
with
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{F}_{r})\neq 0
.
Moreover,
\mathrm{dim}_{k}(H^{0}(X,\mathcal{F}_{r}))=1
and the cokernel of the unique (up to multiplication by an element of
k^{\times}
) injection
\mathcal{O}_{X}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{F}_{r}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{F}_{r-1}
(with the convention that
\mathcal{F}_{0}:=0
).
In other words,
by fixing an isomorphism
\mathrm{Coker}(\mathcal{O}_{X}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{F}_{r})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{F}_{r-1}
,
we obtain a short exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}_{r}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}_{r-1}\rightarrow 0

(4.3)

(cf.
[
2
, Lemma 15, (i), and Theorem 5, (i)]
).
This extension corresponds to the identity morphism of
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{F}_{r-1})
via (
4.1
) in the case where
V=H^{0}(X,\mathcal{F}_{r-1})\left(\cong k\right)
and
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{O}_{X}
.

Denote by

\displaystyle\mathrm{Fil}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{F}_{r}):=\{\mathrm{Fil}^{j}(\mathcal{F}_{r})\}_{j=0}^{r}

(4.4)

the filtration on
\mathcal{F}_{r}
defined in such a way that for each
j=0,\cdots,r
, the subsheaf
\mathrm{Fil}^{j}(\mathcal{F}_{r})
is the kernel of the composite surjection

\mathcal{F}_{r}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{F}_{r-1}\twoheadrightarrow\cdots\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{F}_{r-j}
.
Then, we have
\mathrm{Fil}^{j}(\mathcal{F}_{r})/\mathrm{Fil}^{j-1}(\mathcal{F}_{r})\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}
for every
j=1,\cdots,r
.

Next,take an arbitrary indecomposable vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
X
of rank
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and degree
d\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Define
h:=\mathrm{gcd}(r,d)
.
Following
[
2
, Theorem 6]
, we shall construct, by induction on
j
, proper subbundles
\mathrm{Fil}^{j}(\mathcal{E})
(
j=0,1,2,\cdots
) of
\mathcal{E}
, starting with
\mathrm{Fil}^{0}(\mathcal{E}):=0
and satisfying the following conditions:

•

\mathcal{E}/\mathrm{Fil}^{j}(\mathcal{E})
is indecomposable;

•

The equality
h=\mathrm{gcd}(r_{j},d_{j})
holds, where
r_{j}:=\mathrm{rank}(\mathcal{E}/\mathrm{Fil}^{j}(\mathcal{E}))
and
d_{j}:=\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{E}/\mathrm{Fil}^{j}(\mathcal{E}))
(hence
(r_{0},d_{0})=(r,d)
).

To construct
\mathrm{Fil}^{j}(\mathcal{E})
, assume that a proper subbundle
\mathrm{Fil}^{j-1}(\mathcal{E})
(
j\geq 1
) has been constructed, and that
r_{j-1}\nmid d_{j-1}
.
Under these assumptions,
the vector bundle
\mathcal{L}_{j}^{\vee}\otimes(\mathcal{E}/\mathrm{Fil}^{j-1}(\mathcal{E}))
, where
\mathcal{L}_{j}:=\mathcal{O}_{X}(\left\lfloor\frac{d_{j-1}}{r_{j-1}}\right\rfloor\sigma)
, has positive degree strictly smaller than
r_{j-1}
.
It follows that the natural morphism

\displaystyle\alpha_{j}:H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L}^{\vee}_{j}\otimes(\mathcal{E}/\mathrm{Fil}^{j-1}(\mathcal{E})))\otimes_{k}\mathcal{L}_{j}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}/\mathrm{Fil}^{j-1}(\mathcal{E})

(4.5)

is nonzero and injective, and its cokernel
\mathrm{Coker}(\alpha_{j})
defines an indecomposable vector bundle (cf.
[
2
, Lemma 15, (ii)]
).
We define
\mathrm{Fil}^{j}(\mathcal{E})
as the inverse image of
\mathrm{Im}(\alpha_{j})
along the quotient
\mathcal{E}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{E}/\mathrm{Fil}^{j-1}(\mathcal{E})
.
Since
\mathcal{E}/\mathrm{Fil}^{j}(\mathcal{E})
coincides with
\mathrm{Coker}(\alpha_{j})
, it is indecomposable, and we obtain a short exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L}_{j}^{\vee}\otimes(\mathcal{E}/\mathrm{Fil}^{j-1}(\mathcal{E})))\otimes_{k}\mathcal{L}_{j}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}/\mathrm{Fil}^{j-1}(\mathcal{E})\rightarrow\mathcal{E}/\mathrm{Fil}^{j}(\mathcal{E})\rightarrow 0.

(4.6)

Equivalently, we obtain an isomorphism of vector bundles

\displaystyle H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L}_{j}^{\vee}\otimes(\mathcal{E}/\mathrm{Fil}^{j-1}(\mathcal{E})))\otimes_{k}\mathcal{L}_{j}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{Fil}^{j}(\mathcal{E})/\mathrm{Fil}^{j-1}(\mathcal{E})\left(\neq 0\right).

(4.7)

Moreover, the equality

\displaystyle\mathrm{dim}_{k}(H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L}_{j}^{\vee}\otimes(\mathcal{E}/\mathrm{Fil}^{j-1}(\mathcal{E}))))=\mathrm{dim}_{k}(H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L}_{j}^{\vee}\otimes(\mathcal{E}/\mathrm{Fil}^{j}(\mathcal{E}))))

(4.8)

holds (cf.
[
2
, Lemma 15, (ii)]
).
Under a suitable identification
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L}_{j}^{\vee}\otimes(\mathcal{E}/\mathrm{Fil}^{j-1}(\mathcal{E})))=H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L}_{j}^{\vee}\otimes(\mathcal{E}/\mathrm{Fil}^{j}(\mathcal{E})))
, the extension (
4.6
) corresponds to the identity morphism of
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L}_{j}^{\vee}\otimes(\mathcal{E}/\mathrm{Fil}^{j}(\mathcal{E})))
via (
4.1
).
This means
\mathrm{ext}_{\mathcal{E}/\mathrm{Fil}^{j-1}(\mathcal{E})}=\mathrm{id}_{H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L}_{j}^{\vee}\otimes(\mathcal{E}/\mathrm{Fil}^{j}(\mathcal{E})))}
in the notation of (
4.2
).
Since

\displaystyle r_{j}=r_{j-1}-\left(d_{j-1}-r_{j-1}\cdot\left\lfloor\frac{d_{j-1}}{r_{j-1}}\right\rfloor\right),\hskip 14.22636ptd_{j}=d_{j-1}-\left\lfloor\frac{d_{j-1}}{r_{j-1}}\right\rfloor\cdot\left(d_{j-1}-r_{j-1}\cdot\left\lfloor\frac{d_{j-1}}{r_{j-1}}\right\rfloor\right),

(4.9)

we have
\mathrm{g.c.d}(r_{j},d_{j})=\mathrm{g.c.d}(r_{j-1},d_{j-1})=h
and
r_{j}<r_{j-1}
,
d_{j}<d_{j-1}
, ensuring that the process terminates.

By repeating this construction, we end up with an increasing filtration

\{\mathrm{Fil}^{j}(\mathcal{E})\}_{j=0}^{m}

of
\mathcal{E}
for some
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}

such that
r_{m}\mid d_{m}
(hence
r_{m}=h
).
According to
[
2
, Theorem 5, (ii)]
,
there exists a line bundle
\mathcal{L}_{m+1}
of degree
\frac{d_{m}}{r_{m}}
along with an isomorphism
\alpha_{\mathcal{E}}:\mathcal{E}/\mathrm{Fil}^{m}(\mathcal{E})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{L}_{m+1}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{r_{m}}
.
Using this, we extend
\{\mathrm{Fil}^{j}(\mathcal{E})\}_{j=0}^{m}
to a new filtration

\displaystyle\mathrm{Fil}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{E}):=\{\mathrm{Fil}^{j}(\mathcal{E})\}_{j=0}^{\ell},

(4.10)

where
\ell:=m+r_{m}
, by defining

\displaystyle\mathrm{Fil}^{j}(\mathcal{E}):=\mathrm{Ker}\left(\mathcal{E}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{E}/\mathrm{Fil}^{m}(\mathcal{E})\xrightarrow{\alpha_{\mathcal{E}}}\mathcal{L}_{m+1}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{r_{m}}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{m+1}\otimes(\mathcal{F}_{r_{m}}/\mathrm{Fil}^{j-m}(\mathcal{F}_{r_{m}}))\right)

(4.11)

for
j=m+1,\cdots,\ell
(hence
\mathrm{Fil}^{\ell}(\mathcal{E})=\mathcal{E}
).
Setting
\mathcal{L}_{j}:=\mathcal{L}_{m+1}
for
j=m+1,\cdots,\ell
, we obtain a collection of line bundles

\displaystyle L(\mathcal{E}):=(\mathcal{L}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{L}_{m(\mathcal{E})},\cdots,\mathcal{L}_{\ell}),

(4.12)

where
m(\mathcal{E}):=m\left(\geq 0\right)
.
Note that both (
4.7
) and (
4.8
) hold even for
j\geq m+1
(cf.
[
2
, Lemma 16]
).
Additionally, if
m(\mathcal{E})>0
, then we have
\mathcal{L}_{1}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}(\left\lfloor\frac{r}{d}\right\rfloor\sigma))
.
Furthermore, in the special case where
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{F}_{r}
(
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
),
the filtration (
4.10
) coincides with the filtration (
4.4
).

4.2.
Connections on indecomposable bundles

As a preparatory step for establishing the main result of this section, we first prove several properties of connections on indecomposable vector bundles.
Let us fix an indecomposable vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
X
, and let
\{\mathrm{Fil}^{j}(\mathcal{E})\}_{j=0}^{\ell}
be the increasing filtration on
\mathcal{E}
constructed as in
(
4.10
).
Also, write
L(\mathcal{E})=(\mathcal{L}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{L}_{\ell})
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{E}
admits a connection
\nabla
.
Then, for each
j=1,\cdots,\ell
, the subbundle
\mathrm{Fil}^{j}(\mathcal{E})
is stable under
\nabla
, i.e.,
\nabla(\mathrm{Fil}^{j}(\mathcal{E}))\subseteq\Omega_{X}\otimes\mathrm{Fil}^{j}(\mathcal{E})
.

Proof.

Suppose, on the contrary, that the filtration
\{\mathrm{Fil}^{j}(\mathcal{E})\}_{j}
is not preserved by
\nabla

Remark 4.3

(Finite level vs. Infinite level)
.

Considering the structure of the stratified fundamental group
\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{str}}(X)
(cf. Remark
2.1
), we find that the only indecomposable vector bundles admitting
\mathcal{D}^{(\infty)}
-module structures are, up to tensoring with line bundles, those isomorphic to
\mathcal{F}_{r}
’s
(cf.
[
13
, Theorem 21]
and its proof).
This fact is reminiscent of the characteristic
0
setting.
The key difference, however, lies in the case of finite-level
\mathcal{D}
-modules,
where a wide variety of vector bundles admit such structures,
as suggested by the above proposition.
This fundamental distinction highlights the necessity of developing specialized arguments for finite-level
\mathcal{D}
-modules, setting them apart from their
\mathcal{D}^{(\infty)}
-module counterparts.

Lemma 4.4
.

\mathrm{ext}_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}

with the morphism

\displaystyle\left(0\neq\right)H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{1}}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{1})\rightarrow H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{1}}^{\vee}\otimes(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}/\mathrm{Fil}^{1}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})))

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
be

X

L(\mathcal{E})=(\mathcal{L}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{L}_{\ell})

where
e_{i,j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.
Since
\mathcal{E}
admits a connection (i.e.,
\nabla
), each direct summand
\mathcal{E}_{i,j}
admits a connection
\nabla_{\mathcal{E}_{i,j}}
(cf.
[
8
, Lemma 2.4]
).
The direct sum

\displaystyle\nabla^{\prime}:=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\ell}\bigoplus_{j=1}^{M_{i}}\nabla_{\mathcal{E}_{i,j}}

(4.38)

defines
a connection on the right-hand side of (
4.37
).
By regarding
\nabla^{\prime}
as a connection on
\mathcal{E}
via (
4.37
),
we obtain
the difference
\nabla-\nabla^{\prime}
,
which is
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-linear.
Hence,

\nabla
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\nabla=\nabla^{\prime}+\sum_{i,i^{\prime}}\sum_{j,j^{\prime}}h_{i,i^{\prime},j,j^{\prime}}

(4.39)

for some
h_{i,i^{\prime},j,j^{\prime}}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}_{i,j}^{\oplus e_{i,j}},\mathcal{E}_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}^{\oplus e_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}})\left(\subseteq\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E})\right)
under the fixed identification
\mathcal{O}_{X}=\Omega_{X}
.
According to Proposition
4.5
, (i) and (ii),
the sum
\sum_{i,i^{\prime}}\sum_{j,j^{\prime}}h_{i,i^{\prime},j,j^{\prime}}
restricts to an endomorphism of
\bigoplus_{j=1}^{M_{1}}\mathrm{Fil}^{1}(\mathcal{E}_{1,j})
.
Combining this fact with Proposition
4.1
,
we see that
\bigoplus_{j=1}^{M_{1}}\mathrm{Fil}^{1}(\mathcal{E}_{1,j})
is closed under
\nabla
.
We denote the resulting connection on
\bigoplus_{j=1}^{M_{1}}\mathrm{Fil}^{1}(\mathcal{E}_{1,j})
by
\nabla^{\prime\prime}
.
It follows from Proposition
4.2
that
p\mid\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{N}_{1})
, and that
\mathcal{N}_{1}
admits a connection
\nabla_{\mathcal{N}_{1}}
(cf.
[
8
, Theorem 1.1]
,
[
10
, Theorem 1.1]
).
Hence, after tensoring
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
with the dual of
(\mathcal{N}_{1},\nabla_{\mathcal{N}_{1}})
,
we may assume that
\mathcal{N}_{1}=\mathcal{O}_{X}
.
This assumption yields an identification

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{j=1}^{M_{1}}\mathrm{Fil}^{1}(\mathcal{E}_{1,j})\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus s}

(4.40)

for some
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.
Under this identification,
the connection
\nabla^{\prime\prime}
can be expressed as
\nabla^{\prime\prime}=d+A
for some

s\times s
matrix
A\in M_{s}(k)
.
By applying a triangulation of
A
, we find a vector
\vec{v}\in k^{\oplus s}\left(=H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus s})\right)
with
\nabla^{\prime\prime}(\mathcal{O}_{X}\cdot\vec{v})\subseteq\Omega_{X}\otimes(\mathcal{O}_{X}\cdot\vec{v})
.
The line subbundle
\mathcal{L}
of
\left(\bigoplus_{j}\mathrm{Fil}^{1}(\mathcal{E}_{1,j})\subseteq\right)\mathcal{E}

determined by
\mathcal{O}_{X}\cdot\vec{v}
satisfies
\nabla(\mathcal{L})\subseteq\Omega_{X}\otimes\mathcal{L}
, and

\overline{\mathcal{E}}:=\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{L}
admits a connection
\overline{\nabla}
induced naturally from
\nabla
.
By the induction hypothesis,
(\overline{\mathcal{E}},\overline{\nabla})
admits a complete flag

\{\overline{\mathcal{E}}^{j}\}_{j=0}^{r-1}
.
Using this filtration, we define
\mathcal{E}^{0}:=0
and
\mathcal{E}^{j}:=\pi^{-1}(\overline{\mathcal{E}}^{j-1})
(
j=1,\cdots,r
), where
\pi
denotes the natural quotient
\mathcal{E}\twoheadrightarrow\overline{\mathcal{E}}
.
The resulting filtration
\{\mathcal{E}^{j}\}_{j=0}^{r}
specifies a complete flag
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
, completing the proof for
m=0
.

Also, by Proposition
2.3
in the case where the pair “
(m,s)
” is taken to be
(0,m)
,
the assertion for
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
is induced from the assertion just proved.

Finally, the assertion for
m=\infty
follows immediately from the structure of
\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{str}}(X)
(cf. Remark
2.1
) discussed in

[
13
, Theorem 21]
and its proof.
Indeed, this result states that any
\mathcal{D}^{(\infty)}
-bundle can be written as a direct sum of the form
\bigoplus_{i}(\mathcal{L}_{i},\nabla_{\mathcal{L}_{i}})\otimes(\mathcal{E}_{i},\nabla_{\mathcal{E}_{i}})
, where

(\mathcal{L}_{i},\nabla_{\mathcal{L}_{i}})
has rank
1
and
(\mathcal{E}_{i},\nabla_{\mathcal{E}_{i}})
is unipotent, i.e., obtained as a successive extension of
(\mathcal{O}_{X},\nabla_{\mathrm{triv}}^{(\infty)})
.
Since unipotent connections always admit complete flags, the existence of a complete flag for any
\mathcal{D}^{(\infty)}
-bundle follows immediately, completing the proof.
∎

5.
The case of hyperbolic curves

This final section addresses the remaining part of Theorem
A
, specifically the implication (b)
\Rightarrow
(a).
To construct a flat vector bundle on a hyperbolic curve that does not admit complete flags,
we will leverage a characteristic-
p
analogue of the Hitchin fibration, known as the
p
-Hitchin morphism.

5.1.
The
p
-Hitchin morphism

Suppose that
g\geq 2
, and
let
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
be a flat vector bundle on
X
of rank
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.
The
p
-curvature
\psi(\nabla)

of
\nabla
is regarded as a global section of
F_{X/k}^{*}(\Omega_{X^{(1)}})\otimes\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{E})
, where
\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{E}):=\mathcal{E}nd_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E})
.
We associate to
\psi(\nabla)
its characteristic polynomial

\displaystyle\mathrm{Char}(\mathcal{E},\nabla):=\mathrm{det}(t-\psi(\nabla))=t^{r}+a_{r-1}t^{r-1}+\cdots+a_{0}

(5.1)

with coefficients
a_{i}\in H^{0}(X,F_{X/k}^{*}(\Omega_{X^{(1)}}^{\otimes(r-i)}))
(
0\leq i\leq r-1
).
For each
i
,
the morphism

\displaystyle H^{0}(X^{(1)},\Omega_{X^{(1)}}^{\otimes(r-i)})\rightarrow H^{0}(X,F_{X/k}^{*}(\Omega_{X^{(1)}}^{\otimes(r-i)}))

(5.2)

induced from
\Omega_{X^{(1)}}^{\otimes(r-i)}\rightarrow F_{X/k*}(F_{X/k}^{*}(\Omega_{X^{(1)}}^{\otimes(r-i)}))
is injective, so
we regard
H^{0}(X^{(1)},\Omega_{X^{(1)}}^{\otimes(r-i)})
as a subspace of
H^{0}(X,F_{X/k}^{*}(\Omega_{X^{(1)}}^{\otimes(r-i)}))
.
Moreover, it is well-known that
a_{i}
lies in
H^{0}(X^{(1)},\Omega_{X^{(1)}}^{\otimes(r-i)})
(cf.
[
26
, Proposition 3.2]
).

Denote by
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{dR}}
the moduli stack classifying flat vector bundles on
X
of rank
r
, and write

\displaystyle B^{(1)}:=\mathrm{Spec}\left(\mathrm{Sym}\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}H^{0}(X^{(1)},\Omega_{X^{(1)}}^{\otimes i})\right)^{\vee}\right).

(5.3)

We identify
B^{(1)}
with the moduli space classifying polynomials of the form
t^{r}+a_{r-1}t^{r-1}+\cdots+a_{0}
with
a_{i}\in H^{0}(X^{(1)},\Omega_{X^{(1)}}^{\otimes(r-i)})
.
The assignment
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)\mapsto\mathrm{Char}(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
determines a
k
-morphism

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hitch}:\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{dR}}\rightarrow B^{(1)},

(5.4)

which is called the
p
-Hitchin morphism
(cf.
[
7
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
18
]
,
[
22
]
,
[
26
]
,
[
28
]
, and
[
29
]
).
The image of this morphism contains a dense open subscheme of
B^{(1)}
(cf.
[
7
, Proposition 4.3, Lemma 4.8]
).

Next,
we shall write

\displaystyle D^{(1)}:=\mathrm{Spec}\left(\mathrm{Sym}\left(H^{0}(X^{(1)},\Omega_{X^{(1)}})^{\oplus r}\right)^{\vee}\right).

(5.5)

This space parametrizes collections of
r
-tuples
(b_{1},\cdots,b_{r})\in H^{0}(X^{(1)},\Omega_{X^{(1)}})^{\oplus r}
.
The assignment
(b_{1},\cdots,b_{r})\mapsto\prod_{i=1}^{r}(t-b_{i})
determines a
k
-morphism

\gamma:D^{(1)}\rightarrow B^{(1)}
.
This morphism captures a special subclass of characteristic polynomials corresponding to cases where the
p
-curvature is diagonalizable in a suitable basis.
Observe that

\displaystyle\mathrm{dim}_{k}(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}H^{0}(X^{(1)},\Omega_{X^{(1)}}^{\otimes i}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mathrm{dim}_{k}(H^{0}(X^{(1)},\Omega_{X^{(1)}}^{\otimes i}))

(5.6)

\displaystyle=g+\sum_{i=2}^{r}(2i-1)(g-1)

\displaystyle=g+(r^{2}-1)(g-1)

\displaystyle>rg

\displaystyle=\mathrm{dim}_{k}(H^{0}(X^{(1)},\Omega_{X^{(1)}})^{\oplus r}).

(5.7)

Hence,
the morphism
\gamma
is not dominant, which implies
\mathrm{Hitch}^{-1}(B^{(1)}\setminus\mathrm{Im}(\gamma))\neq\emptyset
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
q
be a
k
-rational point of
\mathrm{Hitch}^{-1}(B^{(1)}\setminus\mathrm{Im}(\gamma))
, and
denote by
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
the flat vector bundle
classified by
q
.
Then,
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
does not admit any complete flag.

Proof.

Suppose, on the contrary, that there exists a complete flag
\{\mathcal{E}^{j}\}_{j=0}^{r}
on
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
.
For each
i\in\{0,1\}
,
let

\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{E})^{j}

denote the subsheaf of

\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{E})

consisting of endomorphisms

h
of
\mathcal{E}
with
h(\mathcal{E}^{j})\subseteq\mathcal{E}^{j-i}
for every
i
.
Since each
\mathcal{E}^{j}
is preserved by
\nabla
,
the connection
\nabla^{\vee}\otimes\nabla
on
\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{E})
restricts to a connection

\nabla^{0}
(resp.,
\nabla^{1}
) on
\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{E})^{0}
(resp.,
\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{E})^{1}
).
We obtain a composite of natural morphisms

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{E})^{0}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{E})^{0}/\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{E})^{1}\xrightarrow{\sim}\bigoplus_{j=1}^{r}\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{E}^{j}/\mathcal{E}^{j-1})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus r},

(5.8)

which is compatible with the respective connections
\nabla^{0}
and

(\nabla_{\mathrm{triv}}^{(0)})^{\oplus r}
.
The
p
-curvature
\psi(\nabla)
belongs to

H^{0}(X,F_{X/k}^{*}(\Omega_{X^{(1)}})\otimes\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{E})^{0})
, and
its image under
the morphism

\displaystyle H^{0}(X,F_{X/k}^{*}(\Omega_{X^{(1)}})\otimes\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{E})^{0})\rightarrow H^{0}(X,F_{X/k}^{*}(\Omega_{X^{(1)}}))^{\oplus r}

(5.9)

induced from the tensor product of (
5.8
) and the identity morphism of
F_{X/k}^{*}(\Omega_{X^{(1)}})

determines an
n
-tuple
(b_{1},\cdots,b_{n})
of elements in
H^{0}(X^{(1)},\Omega_{X^{(1)}})

\left(\subseteq H^{0}(X,F^{*}_{X/k}(\Omega_{X^{(1)}}))\right)
.
It follows that the characteristic polynomial
\mathrm{Char}(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
is given by
\prod_{i=1}^{n}(t-b_{i})
, i.e.,
coincides with
\gamma(b_{1},\cdots,b_{n})\in\mathrm{Im}(\gamma)
.
This contradicts the assumption
\mathrm{Hitch}^{-1}(B^{(1)}\setminus\mathrm{Im}(\gamma))
, thus completing the proof
of this proposition.
∎

5.2.
The non-existence of complete flags

The following statement completes the remaining part of Theorem
A
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
m
be an element of
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\sqcup\{\infty\}
and
r
a positive integer.
Then, there exists a
\mathcal{D}^{(m)}
-bundle of rank
r
that does not admit any complete flag.

Proof.

The assertion for
m=0
is a direct consequence of Proposition
5.1
, and this assertion also induces the case of
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
by applying Proposition
2.3
in the case where the pair “
(m,s)
” is taken to be
(0,m)
.

Next, we shall consider
m=\infty
.
It follows from
[
14
, Theorem 1.2]

that there exists a stable vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
X
admitting an isomorphism
\nu:F_{X}^{s*}(\mathcal{E})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{E}
for some
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.
In particular, since
\nu
yields an
F
-divided sheaf structure on
\mathcal{E}
(cf. Remark
2.1
),

\mathcal{E}
carries a
\mathcal{D}^{(\infty)}
-module structure
\nabla
.
Note that
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
is irreducible.
Indeed, if
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
has a proper
\mathcal{D}^{(\infty)}
-subbundle

(\mathcal{E}^{\prime},\nabla^{\prime})
, then (by considering, e.g., the
F
-divided structure on its determinant) we see that
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
must have degree
0
.
However, it contradicts the stability of
\mathcal{E}
.
In particular,
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
does not admit any complete flag.
This completes the proof for
m=\infty
.
∎

Remark 5.3

(Other examples using dormant opers)
.

In the proof of the above assertion for
m=\infty
,
we constructed an irreducible
\mathcal{D}^{(\infty)}
-bundle whose underlying vector bundle is stable.
Here, we provide an example of an
unstable
bundle (of rank
2
) that does not admit any complete flag.

To align with the notation in
[
30
]
, we set
N:=m+1
.
Fix a theta characteristic
\varTheta
of
X
, i.e., a line bundle
\varTheta
equipped with an isomorphism
\eta:\varTheta^{\otimes 2}\xrightarrow{\sim}\Omega_{X}
.
We shall regard
\nabla_{\mathrm{triv}}^{(N-1)}
as a
\mathcal{D}^{(N-1)}
-module structure on
\mathcal{T}_{X}\otimes\varTheta^{\otimes 2}
via the identification with
\mathcal{O}_{X}
arising from
\eta
.
Thus, the pair
\vartheta:=(\varTheta,\nabla_{\mathrm{triv}}^{(N-1)})
defines a
2^{(N)}
-theta characteristic, in the sense of

[
30
, Definition 5.12]
.
We set
\mathcal{E}_{\varTheta}:=\mathcal{D}_{\leq 1}^{(N-1)}\otimes\varTheta
and
\mathcal{E}_{\varTheta}^{1}:=\mathcal{D}_{\leq 0}^{(N-1)}\otimes\varTheta\left(\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{\varTheta}\right)
.

Following
[
30
, Definition 5.15]
,
a
(\mathrm{GL}_{2}^{(N)},\vartheta)
-oper
on
X
is defined as a
\mathcal{D}^{(N-1)}
-module structure
\nabla
on
\mathcal{E}_{\varTheta}
satisfying the following two conditions:

•

The
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-linear morphism
\mathcal{D}^{(N-1)}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{\varTheta}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}_{\varTheta}
given by
\nabla
restricts to an isomorphism
\mathcal{D}_{\leq 1}^{(N-1)}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{\varTheta}^{1}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{E}_{\varTheta}
.

•

The connection
\mathrm{deg}(\nabla)
on
\mathrm{det}(\mathcal{E}_{\varTheta})
induced naturally from
\nabla
coincides with
\nabla_{\mathrm{triv}}^{(N-1)}
via the natural composite

\displaystyle\mathrm{det}(\mathcal{E}_{\varTheta})\xrightarrow{\sim}(\mathcal{E}_{\varTheta}/\mathcal{E}_{\varTheta}^{1})\otimes\mathcal{E}_{\varTheta}\xrightarrow{\sim}(\mathcal{T}_{X}\otimes\varTheta)\otimes\varTheta\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{T}_{X}\otimes\varTheta^{\otimes 2}.

(5.10)

Moreover,
a
(\mathrm{GL}_{2}^{(N)},\vartheta)
-oper
\nabla
is said to be
dormant
if it has vanishing
p^{N}
-curvature.
For the study of opers in positive characteristic, we refer the reader to
[
22
]
,
[
23
]
,
[
28
]
,
[
29
]
.

The notion of an isomorphism between
(\mathrm{GL}_{2}^{(N)},\vartheta)
-opers are defined in a suitable manner (though we here omit the details).
In particular, we obtain the set
\mathcal{O}p_{N}^{{}^{\mathrm{Zzz...}}}

consisting of isomorphism classes of
dormant
(\mathrm{GL}_{2}^{(N)},\vartheta)
-opers on
X
.
Under the assumption
p>2
, we know that

\mathcal{O}p_{N}^{{}^{\mathrm{Zzz...}}}
is a nonempty finite set for every
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
(cf.
[
30
, Eq. (5.8.2), Theorem 6.16, and Proposition 6.26]
),
and that the limit of the projective system

\displaystyle\cdots\rightarrow\mathcal{O}p_{N}^{{}^{\mathrm{Zzz...}}}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow\mathcal{O}p_{2}^{{}^{\mathrm{Zzz...}}}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}p_{1}^{{}^{\mathrm{Zzz...}}}

(5.11)

obtained by reducing levels is nonempty (cf.
[
30
, Corollary 8.28]
).
By choosing an element of this limit,
we obtain a
\mathcal{D}^{(\infty)}\left(=\varinjlim_{N\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}}\mathcal{D}^{(N)}\right)
-module structure on
\mathcal{E}_{\varTheta}
.

Note that
(\mathcal{E}_{\varTheta},\nabla)
is irreducible.
Indeed, suppose, on the contrary, that it admits a line
\mathcal{D}^{(\infty)}
-subbundle
(\mathcal{L},\nabla_{\mathcal{L}})
.
Then,
\mathcal{L}
must have degree
0
, so
the natural composite
\mathcal{L}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{E}_{\varTheta}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}_{\varTheta}/\mathcal{E}_{\varTheta}^{1}\left(\cong\varTheta^{\vee}\right)
coincides with the zero map because
0=\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{L})>\mathrm{deg}(\varTheta^{\vee})=1-g
.
This means that
\mathcal{L}
is contained in
\mathcal{E}_{\varTheta}^{1}
.
Since
0=\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{L})<\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{E}_{\varTheta}^{1})=g-1
,
this contradicts the assumption that
\mathcal{L}
is a subbundle of
\mathcal{E}_{\varTheta}
.
Consequently,
(\mathcal{E}_{\varTheta},\nabla)
is irreducible, and in particular, does not admit any complete flag.

Capacity of loop-erased random walk
Maarten Markering

Abstract
We study the capacity of loop-erased random walk (LERW) on ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} . For d ≥ 4 d\geq 4 , we prove a strong law of large numbers and give explicit expressions for the limit in terms of the non-intersection probabilities of a simple random walk and a two-sided LERW. Along the way, we show that four-dimensional LERW is ergodic. For d = 3 d=3 , we show that the scaling limit of the capacity of LERW is random. We show that the capacity of the first n n steps of LERW is of order n 1 / β n^{1/\beta} , with β \beta the growth exponent of three-dimensional LERW. We express the scaling limit of the capacity of LERW in terms of the capacity of Kozma’s scaling limit of LERW. As a corollary, we obtain the scaling limit of the LERW in three dimensions when parametrized by its capacity.

1.1
Background

In this paper, we study the
capacity
of the
loop-erased random walk
(LERW) on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for
d\geq 3
. LERW was introduced by Lawler in
[
Law80
]
. It has since become a central object in modern probability theory. This is partly due to its many connections to other models, chief among them being the uniform spanning tree. In that context, the
capacity
of LERW has become an important object of study. The capacity of a set
A\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
can be considered as a rescaled hitting probability of
A
by a simple random walk (SRW) started far away. Through Wilson’s algorithm, the capacity of a loop-erased random walk is related to the length and density of branches in the uniform spanning tree/forest. Using Hutchcroft’s interlacement Aldous-Broder algorithm developed in
[
Hut20
]
, the capacity of loop-erased random walk played an important role in determining the scaling of several exponents for the uniform spanning tree on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for
d\geq 5
by Hutchcroft
[
Hut20
]
and
d=4
by Hutchcroft and Sousi
[
HS23
]
and Hutchcroft and Halberstam
[
HH24
]
. In particular, a weak law of large numbers for the capacity of LERW was established in
[
HS23
]
. A finite analogue of the capacity of LERW was also instrumental in establishing the scaling limit of the uniform spanning tree on the torus in dimensions
d\geq 4

[
ANS24
,
AS24
,
MNS21
,
PR04
,
Sch09
]
. In this paper, we prove several laws of large numbers for the capacity of LERW in dimensions
d\geq 3
.

We now give a formal definition of the LERW and capacity. Let
\Omega
be the set of finite, one-sided infinite and two-sided infinite nearest-neighbour paths in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be the sigma-algebra on
\Omega
generated by events depending only on a finite number of indices (which we call cylinder events). Throughout this paper, all events and random variables will be measurable with respect to
\mathcal{F}
. Lastly, we define the left-shift operator
T\colon\Omega\to\Omega
, given by
(T\omega)(k)=\omega(k+1)-\omega(1)
.

Definition 1.1

(Loop-erased random walk)
.

Let
\omega\in\Omega
be a one-sided nearest-neighbour, transient path in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. The loop-erasure
\LE(\omega)
of
\omega
is the path obtained by erasing loops from
\omega
chronologically. To be precise,
\LE(\omega)_{i}=\omega_{\ell_{i}}
, where the times
(\ell_{i})_{i\geq 0}
are defined inductively as
\ell_{0}=0
and
\ell_{i+1}=1+\max\{k\colon\omega_{k}=\omega_{\ell_{i}}\}
. When
S
is a simple random walk on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for
d\geq 3
, we write

\eta:=\LE(S[0,\infty)).

(1.1)

This is well-defined, since simple random walk is almost surely transient for
d\geq 3
.

Definition 1.2

(Capacity)
.

Let
W
be a simple random walk on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for
d\geq 3
and denote by
\mathbb{P}_{y}
the law of
W
with
W(0)=y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. For a set
A\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, the capacity of
A
is defined as

\Ca(A):=\lim_{\|y\|\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}_{y}(W[0,\infty)\cap A\neq\varnothing)}{G(0,y)}=\sum_{a\in A}\mathbb{P}_{a}(W[1,\infty)\cap A=\varnothing),

(1.2)

where
G
is the SRW Green’s function on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
\|y\|
denotes the Euclidean norm of
y
.

We refer to
[
LL10
]
for more background on the capacity, including a proof that the two expressions above are equal. Throughout this paper, we let
W,S
denote simple random walks and denote their laws by
\mathbb{P}^{W}
and
\mathbb{P}^{S}
. We drop the superscripts if the law is clear from the context. As in the previous two definitions, we will use
W
in the context of a random walk hitting another set and we will use
S
in the context of a random walk being loop-erased.

Although motivated by applications to other models, we study the capacity of LERW in its own right in this paper. We establish a strong law of large numbers (SLLN) for
\Ca(\eta[0,n])
in dimensions
d\geq 4
. In three dimensions, we show that
\Ca(\eta[0,n])
has a non-deterministic scaling limit. These complement earlier results on the capacity of SRW, which has a rich history. Already in 1968, Jain and Orey established a SLLN for the capacity of the range of SRW in dimensions
d\geq 5

[
JO68
]
. This was then extended to a central limit theorem for
d\geq 6
by Asselah, Schapira and Sousi in
[
ASS18
]
. The same authors also proved a SLLN and central limit theorem for
d=4
in
[
ASS19
]
. Furthermore, in
[
Cha17
]
, Chang showed that the scaling limit in
d=3
is random.

We also give explicit expressions for the limiting values. In dimensions
d\geq 5
, we show that the SLLN limit equals the non-intersection probability of a SRW and a
two-sided loop-erased random walk
. Two-sided LERW for
d\geq 5
was first constructed by Lawler in
[
Law80
]
. Much later, Lawler, Sun and Wu constructed two-sided LERW for
d=4
in
[
LSW19
]
. We show that (two-sided) LERW is ergodic in four dimensions. We then use an alternative formula for the capacity to express the limit in terms of non-intersection probabilities of a SRW and two-sided LERW. Both the ergodicity of LERW for
d=4
as well as the alternative capacity formula are of independent interest.

In three dimensions, we do not consider the two-sided LERW. Rather, we express the scaling limit of
\Ca(\eta[0,n])
as a function of the scaling limit of LERW, which was first constructed by Kozma in
[
Koz07
]
. Furthermore, we show that the correct scaling for the capacity is
n^{1/\beta}
, with
\beta
being the
growth exponent
of three-dimensional LERW (see Section
1.4
for a definition of the growth exponent). We then show that the three-dimensional LERW parametrized by its capacity has a scaling limit, which is Kozma’s process parametrized by its capacity. This complements the work in two dimensions, where the original proofs that the scaling limit of LERW converges to the continuous process
\SLE_{2}
were done under the capacity parametrization, see
[
LSW04
]
. It was only shown much more recently by Lawler and Viklund in
[
LV21
]
that two-dimensional LERW also converges in the natural parametrization.

1.2
Dimensions
d\geq 5

Before stating the SLLN in high dimensions, we first need to introduce the
two-sided
loop-erased random walk, which we denote by
\hat{\eta}
. The random variable
\hat{\eta}\colon\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}^{d}
takes values in the space of two-sided infinite simple paths. Intuitively speaking,
\hat{\eta}
can be viewed as an infinite loop-erased random walk ‘seen from the middle’, i.e.,
\hat{\eta}
is the weak limit of
T^{k}\eta
as
k\rightarrow\infty
. The two-sided LERW was first introduced by Lawler in
[
Law80
, Section 5]
and is defined as follows.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
d\geq 5
. Let
S_{1},S_{2}
be independent simple random walks on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
started from 0, conditioned such that
\LE(S_{1})[1,\infty)\cap S_{2}[1,\infty)=\varnothing
. The two-sided loop-erased random walk
\hat{\eta}
is defined as the union of
\LE(S_{1})
and
\LE(S_{2})
, i.e., for
n\geq 0
,
\hat{\eta}(n)=\LE(S_{1})(n)
and
\hat{\eta}(-n)=\LE(S_{2})(n)
.

We are now ready to state the strong law of large numbers for the capacity of loop-erased random walk in dimensions
d\geq 5
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
d\geq 5
. Then almost surely,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\Ca(\eta[0,n])}{n}=\mathbb{P}(W[1,\infty)\cap\hat{\eta}(-\infty,\infty)=\varnothing\mid W(0)=\hat{\eta}(0)=0).

(1.3)

1.3
Dimension
d=4

In
\mathbb{Z}^{4}
, we have
\mathbb{P}(W[1,\infty)\cap\LE(S[0,\infty))=\varnothing)=0
. So we cannot define two-sided LERW as above. Note that in high dimensions,
\hat{\eta}[0,\infty)
has the law of a one-sided LERW weighted by the probability that a SRW started from the same point does not intersect it. Although this probability is 0 for an infinite SRW, we can still consider the
n
-step escape probabilities and show that they converge when properly rescaled. Let

X_{n}:=(\log n)^{\frac{1}{3}}\mathbb{P}^{W}_{0}(W[1,n]\cap\eta[0,\infty)=\varnothing\mid\eta).

(1.4)

Theorem 1.5

(
[
LSW19
]
)
.

There exists a nontrivial random variable
X_{\infty}
such that

X_{\infty}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}X_{n}

(1.5)

almost surely and in
L^{p}
for all
p>0
.

We can now define two-sided LERW in the same way as in high dimensions, but using the random variable
X_{\infty}
instead of
\mathbb{P}^{W}(W[1,\infty)\cap\eta[0,\infty)=\varnothing\mid\eta)
.

Definition 1.6
.

We define the two-sided loop-erased random walk
\hat{\eta}
as the random variable on two-sided infinite paths in
\Omega
such that
\hat{\eta}[0,\infty)
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\eta
with Radon-Nikodym derivative given by
\frac{X_{\infty}}{\mathbb{E}[X_{\infty}]}
. It was shown in
[
LSW19
]
that
(T\hat{\eta})[0,\infty)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\hat{\eta}[0,\infty)
. Thus, we can extend
\hat{\eta}
to the space of two-sided infinite paths by defining
\hat{\eta}[-a,b]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\hat{\eta}[0,a+b]-\hat{\eta}(a)
.

In order to formulate the SLLN for
d=4
, we need the following extension of Theorem
1.5
.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
d=4
. Then there exist random variables
\hat{X}_{\infty}
and
\hat{X}_{\infty}^{+}
depending on
\hat{\eta}
such that

\begin{split}\hat{X}^{+}_{\infty}&=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\hat{X}^{+}_{n}:=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(\log n)^{1/3}\mathbb{P}^{W}_{0}(W[1,\infty)\cap\hat{\eta}[1,n]=\varnothing\mid\hat{\eta}),\\
\hat{X}_{\infty}&=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\hat{X}_{n}:=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(\log n)^{1/3}\mathbb{P}^{W}_{0}(W[1,\infty)\cap\hat{\eta}[0,n]=\varnothing\mid\hat{\eta})\end{split}

(1.6)

almost surely and in
L^{p}
for every
p<3
.

We prove a SLLN for
d=4
in terms of
\hat{X}_{\infty}
and
\hat{X}^{+}_{\infty}
.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
d=4
. Then almost surely,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{(\log n)^{2/3}\Ca(\eta[0,n])}{n}=\mathbb{E}[\hat{X}_{\infty}\hat{X}^{+}_{\infty}].

(1.7)

Dimension

Lastly, we show a non-deterministic law of large numbers for
d=3

\mathbb{Z}^{d}
to the capacity of its scaling limit in
\mathbb{R}^{d}

\eta
as the mesh size tends to 0 was first shown by Kozma in
[
Koz07
]
along a dyadic subsequence. This was later strengthened to the following statement in
[

\mathcal{C}
be the collection of parametrized curves

and
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\lambda(t)=\infty

for LERW in

, Shiraishi proved existence of the limit. Furthermore, Hernández-Torres, Li and Shiraishi proved convergence of LERW to Kozma’s scaling limit for the full sequence

(

]

[

]

The limit in (

1.5
Proof sketch

1.5.1
Dimensions
d\geq 5

The proof of the law of large numbers in high dimensions is the most straightforward one. Recall that the capacity can be written as

\frac{1}{n}\Ca(\eta[0,n])=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathbb{P}^{W}_{\eta(k)}(W[1,\infty)\cap\eta[0,n]=\varnothing\mid\eta).

(1.14)

If the probabilities in the sum above were i.i.d., a strong law of large numbers would follow immediately. This is of course not the case, but we can still apply an ergodic theorem to deduce the law of large numbers as follows. As
k\rightarrow\infty
, the part of
\eta
near
\eta(k)
is distributed as
\hat{\eta}
. So
\frac{1}{n}\Ca(\eta[0,n])
and
\frac{1}{n}\Ca(\hat{\eta}[0,n])
should have the same limit, which we show in Proposition
2.2
. Furthermore,
\hat{\eta}
is stationary and ergodic. So by Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem
[
Bil95
, Theorem 24.1]
,

\begin{split}\frac{1}{n}\Ca(\hat{\eta}[0,n])=&\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathbb{P}^{W}_{0}(W[1,\infty)\cap(T^{k}\hat{\eta})[-k,n-k]\mid\hat{\eta})\\
\approx&\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathbb{P}^{W}_{0}(W[1,\infty)\cap(T^{k}\hat{\eta})(-\infty,\infty)\mid\hat{\eta})\\
\rightarrow&\mathbb{P}(W[1,\infty)\cap\hat{\eta}(-\infty,\infty)=\varnothing\mid W(0)=\hat{\eta}(0)=0),\qquad n\rightarrow\infty,\end{split}

(1.15)

which was what we wanted to show. The approximate equality comes from the fact that in dimensions
d\geq 5
, a LERW and a SRW only intersect each other near their starting points.

1.5.2
Dimension
d=4

The overall picture in four dimensions is the same as in higher dimensions, but there are several subtleties. Dimension 4 is critical for the intersection of LERW and SRW. This means that an infinite SRW and an infinite LERW started from the same point intersect almost surely, and the probability that the first
n
steps intersect decays polylogarithmically in
n
. So while
\frac{\Ca(\eta[0,n])}{n}\rightarrow 0
almost surely as
n\rightarrow\infty
, we obtain a non-trivial limit if we multiply by a polylogarithmic correction. It turns out that the correct scaling is
\frac{(\log n)^{2/3}}{n}
, which we will now explain.

Like in high dimensions, the strong law of large numbers for
\hat{\eta}
is the same as for
\eta
, so we only consider two-sided LERW. We first show in Lemma
3.6
that the limits

\begin{split}\hat{X}_{\infty}=&\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\hat{X}_{n}:=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(\log n)^{1/3}\mathbb{P}_{0}^{W}(W[1,\infty)\cap\hat{\eta}[0,n]=\varnothing\mid\hat{\eta})\\
\hat{X}^{+}_{\infty}=&\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\hat{X}^{+}_{n}:=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(\log n)^{1/3}\mathbb{P}_{0}^{W}(W[1,\infty)\cap\hat{\eta}[1,n]=\varnothing\mid\hat{\eta})\\
\end{split}

(1.16)

exist, building on a very similar result from
[
LSW19
]
. In other words, the intersection probability of
W[1,\infty)
and
\hat{\eta}[0,n]
decays like
(\log n)^{-1/3}
. So the intersection probability of
W[1,\infty)
and
\hat{\eta}[-n,n]
should decay like
(\log n)^{-2/3}
. So

\Ca(\eta[0,n])=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathbb{P}_{\hat{\eta}(k)}^{W}(W[1,\infty)\cap\hat{\eta}=\varnothing\mid\hat{\eta})\asymp(\log n)^{-2/3}n.

(1.17)

Note that the escape probabilities in the expression above are
two-sided
escape probabilities:
W
started from
\eta(k)
has to escape both
\eta[0,k]
and
\eta[k+1,n]
. However, we only know the scaling limits of the one-sided escape probabilities from (
1.16
). So in order to obtain a precise expression for the limit, we use a different formula for the capacity. In Lemma
3.3
, we show that
2
2

2

This formula for the capacity was known to some people in the field (e.g. Asselah
[
Ass23
]
), but the author has not seen this precise expression anywhere in the existing literature. A related expression appears in
[

]
.

\begin{split}\Ca(\hat{\eta}[0,n])=&\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathbb{P}^{W}_{\hat{\eta}(k)}(W[1,\infty)\cap\hat{\eta}[k,n]=\varnothing\mid\hat{\eta})\mathbb{P}^{W}_{\hat{\eta}(k)}(W[1,\infty)\cap\hat{\eta}[k+1,n]=\varnothing\mid\hat{\eta})\end{split}

(1.18)

In other words, instead of writing the capacity as the sum of two-sided escape probabilities, we write the capacity as the sum of the product of two one-sided escape probabilities. This can be rewritten further in terms of
\hat{X}
and
\hat{X}^{+}
:

\begin{split}\Ca(\hat{\eta}[0,n])=&\sum_{k=0}^{n}(\log k)^{-2/3}\hat{X}_{n-k}(T^{k}\hat{\eta})\hat{X}_{n-k}^{+}(T^{k}\hat{\eta})\\
\approx&\sum_{k=0}^{n}(\log n)^{-2/3}\hat{X}_{\infty}(T^{k}\hat{\eta})\hat{X}_{\infty}^{+}(T^{k}\hat{\eta}).\end{split}

(1.19)

The approximate equality is due to the fact that
W[1,\infty)
is most likely to hit
\hat{\eta}[0,n]
close to the origin. The desired strong law of large numbers now follows from ergodicity and stationarity of
\hat{\eta}
with respect to
T
, which we prove in Proposition
3.1
.

1.5.3
Dimension
d=3

The picture in three dimensions is entirely different. The capacity of LERW no longer scales (almost) linearly and the limit law is no longer deterministic. In three dimensions, the capacity of a set is of the same order as its diameter. Since the LERW is roughly
\beta
-dimensional, the correct scaling for
\Ca(\eta[0,n])
is
n^{1/\beta}
, where
\beta
is the growth exponent of LERW as defined in Section
1.4
. This also means that the global behaviour of the LERW influences the capacity, which implies that the scaling limit must be non-deterministic. The proof goes as follows. We use the same strategy as in
[
Cha17
]
.

Recall that
\gamma
denotes the scaling limit of LERW in three dimensions. For a set
A
in
\mathbb{Z}^{3}
or
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, let
B(A,\varepsilon):=\{x\colon\inf_{a\in A}\|x-a\|\leq\varepsilon\}
denote the
\varepsilon
-sausage of
A
. We first show that a SRW started from distance
\delta n^{1/\beta}
from
\eta[0,n]
is very likely to hit
\eta[0,n]
for
\delta
small enough. So the probability that a SRW hits
\eta[0,n]
is close to the probability that a SRW hits
B(\eta[0,n],\delta n^{1/\beta})
. This implies that

\Ca_{\mathbb{Z}^{3}}(\eta[0,n])\approx\Ca_{\mathbb{Z}^{3}}(B(\eta[0,n],\delta n^{1/\beta})).

(1.20)

Furthermore,
n^{-1/\beta}\eta[0,n]
converges in distribution to
\gamma[0,1]
. So we can couple
\eta
and
\gamma
in such a way that
B(n^{-1/\beta}\eta[0,n],\delta)\approx B(\gamma[0,1],\delta)
, which implies

\Ca_{\mathbb{Z}^{3}}(B(\eta[0,n],\delta n^{1/\beta}))\approx\Ca_{\mathbb{Z}^{3}}(B(n^{1/\beta}\gamma[0,1],\delta n^{1/\beta})).

(1.21)

Using a strong coupling of SRW and Brownian motion and the fact that
G_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(0,y)\sim 3G_{\mathbb{Z}^{3}}(0,y)
, we then show

(1.23)

In order to conclude Theorem

In this section, we consider loop-erased random walk in dimensions

Proposition 2.1

(
[

]
)
.

Two-sided LERW
\hat{\eta}
satisfies the following properties:

1.

the law
\hat{\eta}
is absolutely continuous with respect to the law of
\eta
;

2.

converges weakly to

as

3.

\hat{\eta}
is stationary with respect to
T
, i.e.,

4.

\eta
and
\hat{\eta}
are ergodic with respect to
T
.

As a corollary, we show that it suffices to prove a strong law of large numbers for

.

Proposition 2.2
.

Assume that
\frac{1}{n}\Ca(\hat{\eta}[0,n])
converges almost surely. Then
\frac{1}{n}\Ca(\eta[0,n])
converges almost surely to the same limit.

Proof.

Note that the law of
\hat{\eta}
is absolutely continuous with respect to the law of
\eta
. So if
\frac{1}{n}\Ca(\hat{\eta}[0,n])
converges almost surely,
\frac{1}{n}\Ca(\hat{\eta}[0,n])
converges with positive probability. Since
\eta
is ergodic with respect to
T
and
\frac{1}{n}\Ca(\hat{\eta}[0,n])
converges if and only if
\frac{1}{n}\Ca((T\hat{\eta})[0,n])

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

By Proposition
2.2
, it suffices to prove a strong law of large numbers for the two-sided LERW. We first prove a lower bound. By the definition of capacity and recalling that
\mathbb{P}^{W}
is the law of a SRW
W
, we have

\begin{split}\Ca(\hat{\eta}[0,n])=&\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathbb{P}_{\hat{\eta}(k)}^{W}(W[1,\infty)\cap\hat{\eta}[0,n]=\varnothing\mid\hat{\eta})\\
\geq&\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathbb{P}_{\hat{\eta}(k)}^{W}(W[1,\infty)\cap\hat{\eta}(-\infty,\infty)=\varnothing\mid\hat{\eta})\\
=&\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathbb{P}_{0}^{W}(W[1,\infty)\cap(T^{k}\hat{\eta})(-\infty,\infty)=\varnothing\mid\hat{\eta}).\end{split}

(2.1)

Since
\hat{\eta}
is ergodic and stationary with respect to
T
, it follows by Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem
[
Bil95
, Theorem 24.1]
that almost surely,

\begin{split}\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\Ca(\hat{\eta}[0,n])=&\mathbb{E}^{\hat{\eta}}[\mathbb{P}_{0}^{W}(W[1,\infty)\cap(T^{k}\hat{\eta})(-\infty,\infty)=\varnothing\mid\hat{\eta})]\\
=&\mathbb{P}(W[1,\infty)\cap\hat{\eta}(-\infty,\infty)=\varnothing\mid W(0)=\hat{\eta}(0)=0),\end{split}

(2.2)

which settles the lower bound.

We now turn to the upper bound. Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
. It can be seen immediately from Definition
1.2
that capacity is subadditive. Thus,

\begin{split}\frac{1}{n}\Ca(\hat{\eta}[0,n])\leq&\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{\lceil\frac{n}{m}\rceil-1}\Ca(\hat{\eta}[mk,m(k+1)])\\
=&\frac{1}{m}\frac{1}{\frac{n}{m}}\sum_{k=0}^{\lceil\frac{n}{m}\rceil-1}\Ca\left(((T^{m})^{k}\hat{\eta})[0,m]\right)\end{split}

(2.3)

From the proofs in
[
Law80
]
, it follows that
\hat{\eta}
is also ergodic with respect to any power of
T
, so also with respect to
T^{m}
. Therefore, again by Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem,

\begin{split}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\Ca(\hat{\eta}[0,n])\leq\frac{1}{m}\mathbb{E}[\Ca(\hat{\eta}[0,m])].\end{split}

(2.4)

Therefore, it suffices to show

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}[\Ca(\hat{\eta}[0,m])]=\mathbb{P}(W[1,\infty)\cap\hat{\eta}(-\infty,\infty)=\varnothing\mid W(0)=\hat{\eta}(0)=0).

(2.5)

Note that as
k,m-k\rightarrow\infty
, we have
\mathbb{P}(W[1,\infty)\cap(\hat{\eta}(-\infty,-k)\cup\hat{\eta}(m-k,\infty))\neq\varnothing)\rightarrow 0
. So indeed, by stationarity of
\hat{\eta}
,

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}[\Ca(\hat{\eta}[0,m])]=&\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\mathbb{P}(W[1,\infty)\cap\hat{\eta}[0,m]=\varnothing\mid W(0)=\hat{\eta}(k))\\
=&\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\mathbb{P}(W[1,\infty)\cap\hat{\eta}[-k,m-k]=\varnothing\mid W(0)=\hat{\eta}(0)=0)\\
=&\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\mathbb{P}(W[1,\infty)\cap\hat{\eta}(-\infty,\infty)=\varnothing\mid W(0)=\hat{\eta}(0)=0)+o(1)\\
=&\mathbb{P}(W[1,\infty)\cap\hat{\eta}(-\infty,\infty)=\varnothing\mid W(0)=\hat{\eta}(0)=0)+o(1),\qquad m\rightarrow\infty,\end{split}

(2.6)

which concludes the proof of Theorem
1.4
.
∎

a_{k_{n}}\leq n\leq a_{k_{n}+1}

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{b_{a_{k_{n}}}}{b_{a_{k_{n}+1}}}=1
, we have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}b_{n}f(n)=x.

(3.20)

Proof.

Since
b
is increasing,
f
is decreasing and
a_{k_{n}}\leq n\leq a_{k_{n}+1}
, we have

\frac{b_{a_{k_{n}+1}}}{b_{a_{k_{n}}}}b_{a_{k_{n}}}f(a_{k_{n}})\geq b_{n}f(n)\geq\frac{b_{a_{k_{n}}}}{b_{a_{k_{n}+1}}}b_{a_{k_{n}+1}}f(a_{k_{n}+1}).

(3.21)

The result follows by taking the limit
n\rightarrow\infty
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
3.4
.

We prove the statement for
\widetilde{X}^{+}_{n}
, the proof for
\widetilde{X}_{n}
being identical. We first prove almost sure convergence. We show an upper and lower bound.
Let
S
be the simple random walk such that
\eta=\LE(S[0,\infty)
. Then

\begin{split}1-X^{+}_{n}\leq&(\log n)^{1/3}\mathbb{P}^{W}_{0}(W[1,n]\cap\eta[0,n]\neq\varnothing\mid\eta)\\
&+(\log n)^{1/3}\mathbb{P}^{W}_{0}(W[1,n]\cap\eta[n+1,\infty)\neq\varnothing\mid\eta)\\
\leq&1-\widetilde{X}^{+}_{n}+(\log n)^{1/3}\mathbb{P}_{0}^{W}(W[1,n]\cap S[n+1,\infty)\neq\varnothing\mid S).\end{split}

(3.22)

Let
\varepsilon>0
. By
[
Law91
, Theorem 4.3.6]
,

\mathbb{E}^{S}_{0}[\mathbb{P}^{W}_{0}(W[1,n]\cap S[n+1,\infty)\neq\varnothing\mid S)]\lesssim(\log n)^{-1}.

(3.23)

Hence, by Markov’s inequality,

\mathbb{P}^{S}_{0}((\log n)^{1/3}\mathbb{P}^{W}_{0}(W[1,n]\cap S[n+1,\infty)\neq\varnothing\mid S)>\varepsilon)\lesssim\varepsilon(\log n)^{-2/3}.

(3.24)

Let
a_{n}=e^{n^{2}}
. Then

\begin{split}&\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}^{S}_{0}((\log a_{n})^{1/3}\mathbb{P}^{W}_{0}(W[1,a_{n}]\cap S[a_{n}+1,\infty)\neq\varnothing\mid S)>\varepsilon)\\
\lesssim&\varepsilon\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(\log a_{n})^{-2/3}\leq\varepsilon\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-4/3}<\infty.\end{split}

(3.25)

So by the Borel-Cantelli lemma,
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(\log a_{n})^{1/3}\mathbb{P}^{W}_{0}(W[1,a_{n}]\cap S[a_{n}+1,\infty)\neq\varnothing\mid S)=0
almost surely. So almost surely,

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\widetilde{X}_{a_{n}}^{+}\leq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}X_{a_{n}}^{+}\leq X_{\infty}^{+}.

(3.26)

Conversely,

\begin{split}1-\widetilde{X}^{+}_{n}\leq&(\log n)^{1/3}\mathbb{P}_{0}^{W}(W[1,n]\cap\eta[0,n]\neq\varnothing\mid\eta)\\
&+(\log n)^{1/3}\mathbb{P}^{W}_{0}(W[n+1,\infty)\cap\eta[0,n]\neq\varnothing\mid\eta)\\
\leq&1-X^{+}_{n}+(\log n)^{1/3}\mathbb{P}^{W}_{0}(W[n+1,\infty)\cap\eta[0,n]\neq\varnothing\mid\eta).\end{split}

(3.27)

Recall from Lemma
3.2
that
\mathbb{E}[\ell_{n}]\sim n(\log n)^{1/3}
. So by Markov’s inequality,

\mathbb{P}(\eta[0,n]\not\subset S[0,n(\log n)^{2}])\lesssim(\log n)^{-5/3}.

(3.28)

Furthermore, it follows from
[
AZ96
, Theorem 1.1]
that

\mathbb{E}^{S}_{0}[\mathbb{P}^{W}_{0}(W[n+1,\infty)\cap S[0,n(\log n)^{2}]\neq\varnothing\mid S)]\lesssim\frac{\log\log n}{\log n}.

(3.29)

Combining this, we have

\mathbb{E}^{S}_{0}[\mathbb{P}^{W}_{0}(W[n+1,\infty)\cap\eta[0,n]\neq\varnothing\mid\eta)]\lesssim\frac{\log\log n}{\log n}.

(3.30)

So by Markov’s inequality,

\begin{split}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{0}^{S}((\log a_{n})^{1/3}\mathbb{P}^{W}_{0}(W[a_{n}+1,\infty)\cap\eta[0,a_{n}]\neq\varnothing\mid\eta)>\varepsilon)\lesssim\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\log\log a_{n}}{(\log a_{n})^{2/3}}<\infty.\end{split}

(3.31)

Another application of Borel-Cantelli gives that

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\widetilde{X}_{a_{n}}^{+}\geq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}X_{a_{n}}^{+}=X_{\infty}^{+}

(3.32)

almost surely, and so,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\widetilde{X}_{a_{n}}^{+}=X_{\infty}^{+}.

(3.33)

Convergence of the full sequence
\widetilde{X}_{n}^{+}
now follows by applying Lemma
3.5
with
f(n)=\mathbb{P}_{0}^{W}(W[1,\infty)\cap\eta[0,n]=\varnothing\mid\eta)
and
b_{n}=(\log n)^{1/3}
.

We now show convergence in
L^{p}
for
1\leq p<3
. Let
\|\cdot\|_{p}
denote the
L^{p}
-norm of a random variable. From the above, we obtain

\begin{split}\|X^{+}_{\infty}-\widetilde{X}^{+}_{n}\|_{p}\leq&\|X^{+}_{\infty}-X^{+}_{n}\|_{p}+\|X^{+}_{n}-\widetilde{X}_{n}\|_{p}\\
\leq&\|X^{+}_{\infty}-X^{+}_{n}\|_{p}+(\log n)^{1/3}\left(\mathbb{E}^{\eta}_{0}\left[\mathbb{P}^{W}_{0}(W[1,n]\cap\eta[n+1,\infty)\neq\varnothing\mid\eta)^{p}\right]\right)^{1/p}\\
&+(\log n)^{1/3}\left(\mathbb{E}^{\eta}_{0}\left[\mathbb{P}^{W}_{0}(W[n+1,\infty)\cap\eta[0,n]\neq\varnothing\mid\eta)^{p}\right]\right)^{1/p}.\\
\leq&\|X^{+}_{\infty}-X^{+}_{n}\|_{p}+(\log n)^{1/3}\left(\mathbb{E}^{S}_{0}\left[\mathbb{P}^{W}_{0}(W[1,n]\cap S[n+1,\infty)\neq\varnothing\mid\eta)\right]\right)^{1/p}\\
&+(\log n)^{1/3}\left(\mathbb{E}^{S}_{0}\left[\mathbb{P}^{W}_{0}(W[n+1,\infty)\cap\eta[0,n]\neq\varnothing\mid\eta)\right]\right)^{1/p}.\\
\lesssim&\|X^{+}_{\infty}-X^{+}_{n}\|_{p}+\frac{(\log n)^{1/3}}{(\log n)^{1/p}}+\frac{(\log\log n)^{1/p}(\log n)^{1/3}}{(\log n)^{1/p}}\rightarrow 0,\qquad n\rightarrow\infty,\end{split}

(3.34)

which settles the proof.
∎

Recall that we want to prove a strong law of large numbers for
\Ca(\hat{\eta}[0,n])
, rather than
\eta[0,n]
. In the following lemma, we show that the corresponding escape probabilities also converge for the two-sided LERW.

Lemma 3.6
.

There exist random variables
\hat{X}_{\infty}
and
\hat{X}^{+}_{\infty}
depending on
\hat{\eta}
such that

\begin{split}\hat{X}^{+}_{\infty}&=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\hat{X}^{+}_{n}:=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(\log n)^{1/3}\mathbb{P}^{W}_{0}(W[1,\infty)\cap\hat{\eta}[1,n]=\varnothing\mid\hat{\eta}),\\
\hat{X}_{\infty}&=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\hat{X}_{n}:=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(\log n)^{1/3}\mathbb{P}^{W}_{0}(W[1,\infty)\cap\hat{\eta}[0,n]=\varnothing\mid\hat{\eta})\end{split}

(3.35)

almost surely and in
L^{p}
for every
p<3
.

Proof.

Almost sure convergence follows from absolute continuity of the law of
\hat{\eta}
combined with Lemma
3.4
. Furthermore, let
f
be the Radon-Nikodym derivative of
\hat{\eta}[0,\infty)
with respect to
\eta
. Let
p<3
. Let
p^{\prime}=\frac{3+p}{2}
and let
q^{\prime}
be the Hölder conjugate of
p^{\prime}
. Then by Hölder’s inequality,

\begin{split}\|\hat{X}_{\infty}^{+}-\hat{X}_{n}^{+}\|^{p}_{p}=&\mathbb{E}^{\eta}[f|\widetilde{X}_{\infty}^{+}(\eta)-\widetilde{X}_{n}^{x}(\eta)|^{p}]\\
\leq&\mathbb{E}^{\eta}\left[f^{\frac{q^{\prime}}{p}}\right]^{\frac{p}{q^{\prime}}}\mathbb{E}^{\eta}\left[|\hat{X}_{\infty}^{+}-\hat{X}_{n}^{+}|^{p^{\prime}}\right]^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}}\rightarrow 0,\qquad n\rightarrow\infty.\end{split}

(3.36)

For the final step, we use that
f
is
L^{a}
bounded for all
a>0
and Lemma
3.4
in combination with the fact that
p^{\prime}<3
.
∎

3.2.1
Lower bound

Let
\varepsilon>0
. By Lemma
3.6
, there exists
n_{0}=n_{0}(\hat{\eta})
such that for all
n\geq n_{0}
,
X_{\infty}\leq\widetilde{X}_{n}+\varepsilon
and
X^{+}_{\infty}\leq\widetilde{X}^{+}_{n}+\varepsilon
. Then for all
n\geq m
,

\begin{split}&\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{(\log n)^{2/3}}{n}\Ca(\hat{\eta}[0,n])\\
=&\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{(\log n)^{2/3}}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathbb{P}^{W}_{\eta(k)}(W[1,\infty)\cap\eta[k,n]=\varnothing\mid\eta)\mathbb{P}^{W}_{\eta(k)}(W[1,\infty)\cap\eta[k+1,n]=\varnothing\mid\eta)\\
\geq&\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\hat{X}_{n}(T^{k}(\hat{\eta}))\hat{X}_{n}^{+}(T^{k}(\hat{\eta}))\\
\geq&\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\hat{X}_{\infty}(T^{k}(\hat{\eta}))\hat{X}_{\infty}^{+}(T^{k}(\hat{\eta}))\mathbf{1}_{\{m\geq n_{0}\}}(T^{k}(\hat{\eta}))-\varepsilon\\
=&\mathbb{E}[\hat{X}_{\infty}\hat{X}_{\infty}^{+}\mathbf{1}_{\{m\geq n_{0}\}}]-\varepsilon\\
\rightarrow&\mathbb{E}[\hat{X}_{\infty}\hat{X}^{+}_{\infty}]-\varepsilon,\qquad m\rightarrow\infty\end{split}

(3.37)

The first line follows from Lemma
3.3
, the fourth line follows from Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem
[
Bil95
, Theorem 24.1]
, and the last line follows from the monotone convergence theorem. Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, we conclude that

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{(\log n)^{2/3}}{n}\Ca(\hat{\eta}[0,n])\geq\mathbb{E}[\hat{X}_{\infty}\hat{X}^{+}_{\infty}].

(3.38)

3.2.2
Upper bound

We show an upper bound for
\frac{1}{n}\Ca(\hat{\eta}[0,\lfloor n(\log n)^{2/3}\rfloor])
.
The strategy is similar to the upper bound in high dimensions. Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
. By subadditivity of capacity,

\begin{split}\frac{(\log n)^{2/3}}{n}\Ca(\hat{\eta}[0,n])\leq&\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{\lceil\frac{n(\log n)^{-2/3}}{m(\log m)^{-2/3}}\rceil-1}\Ca\left(\hat{\eta}[mk,m(k+1)]\right)\\
\leq&\frac{(\log m)^{2/3}}{m}\frac{1}{\frac{n(\log n)^{-2/3}}{m(\log m)^{-2/3}}}\sum_{k=0}^{\left\lceil\frac{n(\log n)^{-2/3}}{m(\log m)^{-2/3}}\right\rceil-1}\Ca(((T^{m})^{k}\hat{\eta})[0,m])\end{split}

(3.39)

By the exact same proof as the proof of Proposition
3.1
, it follows that every power of
T
is also ergodic. So by Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem,

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{(\log n)^{2/3}}{n}\Ca(\hat{\eta}[0,n])\leq\frac{(\log m)^{2/3}}{m}\mathbb{E}[\Ca(\hat{\eta}[0,m])]

(3.40)

almost surely for all
m
. It remains to show that

\limsup_{m\rightarrow\infty}\frac{(\log m)^{2/3}}{m}\mathbb{E}[\Ca(\hat{\eta}[0,m])]\leq\mathbb{E}[\hat{X}_{\infty}\hat{X}^{+}_{\infty}].

(3.41)

Let
W^{1},W^{2}
be simple random walks and let
\varepsilon>0
. Then

\begin{split}&\frac{(\log m)^{2/3}}{m}\mathbb{E}[\Ca(\hat{\eta}[0,m])]\\
=&\frac{(\log m)^{2/3}}{m}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\mathbb{P}(W^{1}[1,\infty)\cap\hat{\eta}[0,k]=\varnothing,\,W^{2}[1,\infty)\cap\hat{\eta}[1,k]\mid W^{1}_{0}=W^{2}_{0}=\hat{\eta}(k))\\
=&\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\frac{(\log m)^{2/3}}{(\log k)^{2/3}}\mathbb{E}[\hat{X}_{k}\hat{X}_{k}^{+}]\\
\leq&\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=\lfloor n^{1-\varepsilon}\rfloor}^{m}\frac{(\log m)^{1/3}}{(\log m^{1-\varepsilon})^{1/3}}\mathbb{E}[\hat{X}_{k}\hat{X}_{k}^{+}]+\frac{1}{m}m^{1-\varepsilon}(\log m)^{1/3}\\
\leq&\frac{1}{(1-\varepsilon)^{1/3}m}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\mathbb{E}[\hat{X}_{k}\hat{X}_{k}^{+}]+o(1)\\
\rightarrow&(1-\varepsilon)^{-1/3}\mathbb{E}[\hat{X}_{\infty}\hat{X}_{\infty}^{+}],\qquad m\rightarrow\infty.\end{split}

(3.42)

The convergence in the last line follows from Lemma
3.6
. Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, the proof is complete.

4
Capacity of LERW in three dimensions

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.11
. We first prove a technical hitting lemma in Section
4.1
. In Section
4.2
, we prove Theorem
1.11
.

4.1
A hitting estimate

As mentioned in Section
1.5
, for the proof we need that a SRW started close to
\eta[0,n]
will hit it with high probability. There are several known results in this direction (most notably
[
SS18
, Lemma 3.3]
and
[
ACHTS21
, Proposition 3.7]
), but none match the exact statement we need.

Lemma 4.1
.

There exist
C,\alpha>0
such that for all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta_{0}
such that for all
\delta\leq\delta_{0}
and for all
n
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{z\in B(\eta[0,n],\delta n^{1/\beta})}\mathbb{P}^{W}_{z}(W[0,\infty)\cap\eta[0,n]=\varnothing\mid\eta)>\varepsilon\right)<C\varepsilon^{1/\alpha}.

(4.1)

For the proof, we reformulate the hitting estimate we need in terms of what was shown in
[
SS18
, Lemma 3.3]
and
[
ACHTS21
, Proposition 3.7]
. This has as advantage that we can use these results as a ‘black box’, at the cost of making the proof somewhat opaque.

Proof of Lemma
4.1
.

Let
\alpha>0
be some constant, whose exact value we will specify later. Let
s=\delta n^{1/\beta}
and
r=\varepsilon^{-1/\alpha}s
. Then

\begin{split}&\mathbb{P}^{\eta}\left(\max_{z\in B(\eta[0,n],\delta n^{1/\beta})}\mathbb{P}^{W}_{z}(W[0,\infty)\cap\eta[0,n]=\varnothing\mid\eta)>\varepsilon\right)\\
\leq&\sum_{z\not\in B(0,r)}\mathbb{P}^{\eta}\left(\left\{\eta[0,n]\cap B(z,s)\neq\varnothing\right\}\cap\left\{\mathbb{P}^{W}_{z}(W[0,\infty)\cap\eta[0,n]=\varnothing\mid\eta)>\varepsilon\right\}\right)\\
&+\mathbb{P}^{\eta}\left(\max_{z\in B(0,r)}\mathbb{P}^{W}_{z}(W[0,\infty)\cap\eta[0,n]=\varnothing\mid\eta)>\varepsilon\right).\end{split}

(4.2)

We bound both terms separately. Let
\tau^{\eta}_{A}:=\min\{t\geq 0\colon\eta(t)\in A\}
. If
z\in B(\eta[0,n],s)
, then
\tau^{\eta}_{B(z,s)}\leq n
. Furthermore, for
\|z\|\geq r
, we have
(\frac{s}{\|z\|})^{\alpha}\leq(\frac{s}{r})^{\alpha}=\varepsilon
. Hence, by
[
SS18
, Lemma 3.3]
, there exists
\alpha
and a constant
C_{1}
such that for all
n
,

\begin{split}&\sum_{z\not\in B(0,r)}\mathbb{P}^{\eta}\left(\eta[0,n]\cap B(z,s)\neq\varnothing,\,\mathbb{P}^{W}_{z}(W[0,\infty)\cap\eta[0,n]=\varnothing\mid\eta)>\varepsilon\right)\\
\leq&\sum_{z\not\in B(0,r)}\mathbb{P}^{\eta}\left(\eta[0,\infty)\cap B(z,s)\neq\varnothing,\,\mathbb{P}^{W}_{z}(W[0,\infty)\cap\eta[0,\tau^{\eta}_{B(z,s)}]=\varnothing\mid\eta)>(\tfrac{s}{\|z\|})^{\alpha}\right)\\
\leq&\sum_{z\not\in B(0,r)}C_{1}\left(\frac{s}{\|z\|}\right)^{4}\\
\lesssim&\frac{s}{r}=\varepsilon^{1/\alpha}.\end{split}

(4.3)

We now bound the second term of (
4.2
). Let
\hat{\alpha}
be the constant
\hat{\eta}
from
[
ACHTS21
, Proposition 3.7]
. Then there exists
C_{2}
such that for all
\delta\leq\varepsilon^{1/\hat{\alpha}}
,

\begin{split}&\mathbb{P}^{\eta}\left(\max_{z\in B(0,r)}\mathbb{P}_{z}^{W}(W[0,\infty)\cap\eta[0,n]=\varnothing)>\varepsilon\right)\\
\leq&\mathbb{P}^{\eta}\left(\left\{\max_{z\in B(0,r)}\mathbb{P}_{z}^{W}(W[0,\infty)\cap\eta[0,n]=\varnothing)>\delta^{\hat{\alpha}}\right\}\right.\\
&\left.\cap\left\{\eta(n,\infty)\cap B(0,2\delta^{1/2}\varepsilon^{-1/\alpha}n^{1/\beta})=\varnothing\right\}\right)\\
&+\mathbb{P}^{\eta}\left(\eta(n,\infty)\cap B(0,2\delta^{1/2}\varepsilon^{-1/\alpha}n^{1/\beta})\neq\varnothing\right)\\
\leq&\mathbb{P}^{\eta}\left(\max_{z\in B(0,\delta\varepsilon^{-1/\alpha}n^{1/\beta})}\mathbb{P}_{z}^{W}(W[0,\tau^{W}_{B(z,\delta^{1/2}\varepsilon^{-1/\alpha}n^{1/\beta})^{c}}]\cap\eta[0,\infty)=\varnothing)>\delta^{\hat{\alpha}}\right)\\
&+\mathbb{P}^{\eta}\left(\eta(n,\infty)\cap B(0,2\delta^{1/2}\varepsilon^{-1/\alpha}n^{1/\beta})\neq\varnothing\right)\\
\leq&C_{2}\delta+\mathbb{P}^{\eta}\left(\eta(n,\infty)\cap B(0,2\delta^{1/2}\varepsilon^{-1/\alpha}n^{1/\beta})\neq\varnothing\right).\end{split}

(4.4)

We bound the final probability. Note that

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}^{\eta}\left(\eta(n,\infty)\cap B(0,2\delta^{1/2}\varepsilon^{-1/\alpha}n^{1/\beta})\neq\varnothing\right)=&\mathbb{P}^{\eta}\left(n^{-1/\beta}\eta(n,\infty)\cap B(0,2\delta^{1/2}\varepsilon^{-1/\alpha})\neq\varnothing\right)\end{split}

(4.5)

By convergence of
n^{-1/\beta}\eta(n,\infty)
to the scaling limit
\gamma(1,\infty)
, the final probability can be made arbitrarily small by choosing
\delta
sufficiently small. Hence, there exists
\delta_{0}
such that for all
\delta\leq\delta_{0}
, and all
n
,

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}^{\eta}\left(\max_{z\in B(0,r)}\mathbb{P}_{z}^{W}(W[0,\infty)\cap\eta[0,n]=\varnothing)>\varepsilon\right)\leq C_{3}\varepsilon^{1/\alpha}.\end{split}

(4.6)

We conclude the proof by combining (
4.3
) and (
4.6
).
∎

4.2
Proof of Theorem
1.11

We now prove Theorem
1.11
. In fact, we prove the following stronger statement.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and let
0\leq u_{1}\leq\ldots\leq u_{\ell}
. There exists a coupling of
(n^{-1/\beta}\eta(n\cdot))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\gamma
such that
(n^{-1/\beta}\eta(n\cdot))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges almost surely to
\gamma
in
(\mathcal{C},\chi)
and for all
u_{i}
,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\C_{\mathbb{Z}^{3}}(\eta[0,\lfloor nu_{i}\rfloor]}{3n^{1/\beta}}=\C_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\gamma[0,u_{i}]).

(4.7)

The proof is divided into three main steps, plus a preparation step. In each of the main steps we give a rigorous formulation and proof of the approximate equalities (
1.20
)–(
1.22
).

Proof of Proposition
4.2
.

Let
u_{1},\ldots,u_{\ell}
. We first show that we can couple
(n^{-1/\beta}\eta(n\cdot))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\gamma
such that for all
u\in\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{\ell}\}
,

\frac{1}{n^{1/\beta}}\Ca_{\mathbb{Z}^{3}}(\eta[0,\lfloor nu\rfloor])\leq 3\Ca_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\gamma[0,u])+o(1),\qquad n\rightarrow\infty.

(4.8)

The proof of the reverse inequality is almost analogous and we briefly remark on it at the end.

Step 0.

We first show that with high probability,
\eta
and its scaling limit
\gamma
are well-behaved. Let
\varepsilon>0
and let
\delta>0
. We will later choose
\delta>0
to be small depending on
\varepsilon
and
u_{\ell}
and then
n
to be sufficiently large depending on
\delta,\varepsilon,u_{\ell}
. Let
W
be an independent simple random walk on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
M
be an independent Brownian motion on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Define the events

\begin{split}E_{1}(n,\delta,u_{\ell}):=&\left\{\sup_{0\leq t\leq u_{\ell}}\left\|\frac{1}{n^{1/\beta}}\eta(\lfloor nt\rfloor)-\gamma(t)\right\|\leq\delta\right\},\\
E_{2}(n,\delta,\varepsilon,u_{1},\ldots,u_{\ell}):=&\left\{\max_{1\leq i\leq\ell}\sup_{z\in B(\eta[0,\lfloor nu_{i}\rfloor],\delta n^{1/\beta})}\mathbb{P}^{W}_{z}(W[0,\infty)\cap\eta[0,\lfloor nu_{i}\rfloor]=\varnothing\mid\eta)<\varepsilon\right\},\\
E_{3}(\delta,\varepsilon,u_{1},\ldots,u_{\ell}):=&\left\{\max_{1\leq i\leq\ell}\sup_{z\in B(\gamma[0,u_{i}],\delta)}\mathbb{P}^{M}_{z}(M[0,\infty)\cap\gamma[0,u_{i}]=\varnothing\mid\gamma)<\varepsilon\right\},\\
E_{4}(\delta,u_{\ell}):=&\left\{\sup_{0\leq t\leq u_{\ell}}\|\gamma(t)\|\geq\delta^{-1}\right\},\\
E(n,\delta,\varepsilon,u_{1},\ldots,u_{\ell}):=&E_{1}\cap E_{2}\cap E_{3}\cap E_{4}.\end{split}

(4.9)

By convergence of
n^{-1/\beta}\eta(n\cdot)
to
\gamma
, for all
\delta>0
and
n
sufficiently large depending on
\delta
, we have
\mathbb{P}(E_{1})>1-\varepsilon
. Furthermore, by Lemma
4.1
, there exist constants
C,\alpha>0
such that for all
\delta
sufficiently small and all
n>0
,
\mathbb{P}(E_{2})>1-C\ell\varepsilon^{1/\alpha}
. Moreover, since the Hausdorff dimension of
\gamma[0,u_{i}]
is strictly greater than 1,
\sup_{z\in B(\gamma[0,u_{i}],\delta)}\mathbb{P}^{M}_{z}(M[0,\infty)\cap\gamma[0,u_{i}]=\varnothing\mid\gamma)
tends to 0 as
\delta\downarrow 0
almost surely. So for
\delta
sufficiently small,
\mathbb{P}(E_{3})>1-\varepsilon
. Lastly, since
\gamma[0,u_{\ell}]
is bounded almost surely,
\mathbb{P}(E_{4})>1-\varepsilon
for
\delta
sufficiently small. Combining the above, we obtain that for all
\varepsilon>0
, for all
\delta>0
sufficiently small and for all
n
sufficiently large, we have
\mathbb{P}(E(n,\delta,\varepsilon,u_{i}))\geq 1-\varepsilon
. Thus, we can couple
(n^{-1/\beta}\eta(n\cdot))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\gamma
such that
E(n,\delta_{n},\varepsilon_{n},u_{i})
holds for all
n
for some sequences
(\varepsilon_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}},(\delta_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfying
\varepsilon_{n},\delta_{n}\downarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Now let
u\in\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{\ell}\}
. We show that (
4.8
) holds under this coupling.

Step 1.

We first show that under the coupling above, we can approximate the capacity of
\eta[0,\lfloor nu\rfloor]
by the capacity of a sufficiently small sausage around it. Let
\varepsilon,\delta>0
. Let
A\subset B\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and denote by
h(B)
the harmonic measure on
B
. It is not difficult to prove that

\Ca_{\mathbb{Z}^{3}}(A)=\Ca_{\mathbb{Z}^{3}}(B)\mathbb{P}_{h(B)}^{W}(W[0,\infty)\cap A\neq\varnothing).

(4.10)

Similarly, for
A\subset B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have

\Ca_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(A)=\Ca_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(B)\mathbb{P}_{h(B)}^{M}(M[0,\infty)\cap A\neq\varnothing).

(4.11)

Note that on the event
E\subset E_{2}
, for
n
sufficiently large, we have

\mathbb{P}_{h(B(\eta[0,\lfloor nu\rfloor],3\delta n^{1/\beta}))}^{W}(W[0,\infty)\cap\eta[0,\lfloor nu\rfloor]\neq\varnothing)\geq 1-\varepsilon.

(4.12)

Thus,

\frac{1}{n^{1/\beta}}\Ca_{\mathbb{Z}^{3}}(\eta[0,\lfloor nu\rfloor])\geq(1-\varepsilon)\frac{1}{n^{1/\beta}}\Ca_{\mathbb{Z}^{3}}(B(\eta[0,\lfloor nu\rfloor],3\delta n^{1/\beta})).

(4.13)

Step 2.

We now show that
\Ca_{\mathbb{Z}^{3}}(B(\eta[0,\lfloor nu\rfloor],3\delta n^{1/\beta}))\approx 3\Ca_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(B(\eta[0,\lfloor nu\rfloor],3\delta n^{1/\beta}))
. We do this by showing that the probability that
B(\eta[0,\lfloor nu\rfloor],3\delta n^{1/\beta})
is hit by a random walk on
\mathbb{Z}^{3}
is close to the probability that
B(\eta[0,\lfloor nu\rfloor],3\delta n^{1/\beta})
is hit by Brownian motion on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. For this part, we need several standard estimates on the hitting probabilities and displacement of simple random walk and Brownian motion, which we will state without proof.

By
[
Ein89
, Theorem 2]
, for a SRW
W
and Brownian motion
M
started from the same point there exists a coupling of
W
and
M
such that almost surely,

\sup_{0\leq k\leq e^{m^{1/4}}}\|W_{k}-M_{k/3}\|\leq\sqrt{m}

(4.14)

for every
m
. This is a very strong coupling, which is somewhat overkill for our purposes. Recall that on the event
E
,
n^{1/\beta}\gamma[0,u]\subset B(0,2n^{1/\beta}\delta_{n}^{-1})
. Now consider
W
and
M
started from the harmonic measures on
B(0,2n^{1/\beta}\delta^{-1})
in
\mathbb{Z}^{3}
and
\mathbb{R}^{3}
respectively. Since the harmonic measure on
B(0,2\delta^{-1})
in
n^{-1/\beta}\mathbb{Z}^{3}
converges weakly to the harmonic measure on
B(0,2\delta^{-1})
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
, we can couple
W
and
M
such that
\|W_{0}-M_{0}\|\leq\frac{1}{4}\delta n^{1/\beta}
and thus

\begin{split}\sup_{0\leq k\leq n^{100/\beta}}|W_{k}-M_{k/3}|\leq\frac{2}{4}\delta n^{1/\beta}.\end{split}

(4.15)

Furthermore, it is not difficult to show that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{h(B(0,2\delta^{-1}n^{1/\beta}))}(W(\lfloor n^{100/\beta}\rfloor,\infty)\cap B(0,2\delta^{-1}n^{1/\beta}))

\displaystyle\rightarrow 0,

(4.16)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{h(B(0,2\delta^{-1}n^{1/\beta}))}(M(\lfloor n^{100/\beta}/3\rfloor,\infty)\cap B(0,2\delta^{-1}n^{1/\beta}))

\displaystyle\rightarrow 0

(4.17)

as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Also, in between integer times, with high probability, the Brownian motion does not stray too far:

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}\left(\exists 0\leq k\leq n^{100/\beta}\colon\sup_{k\leq t\leq k+1}\|M_{t}-M_{k}\|\geq\frac{1}{4}\delta n^{1/\beta}\right)\rightarrow 0,\qquad n\rightarrow\infty.\end{split}

(4.18)

Combining the above, we obtain that on the event
E
, for
n
sufficiently large depending on
\delta
,

\begin{split}&\mathbb{P}^{W}_{h(B(0,2\delta^{-1}n^{1/\beta}))}(W[0,\infty)\in B(\eta[0,\lfloor nu\rfloor],3\delta n^{1/\beta}))\\
\geq&\mathbb{P}^{M}_{h(B(0,2\delta^{-1}n^{1/\beta}))}(M[0,\infty)\in B(\eta[0,\lfloor nu\rfloor],2\delta n^{1/\beta})).\end{split}

(4.19)

Thus, by (
4.10
) and (
4.11
), we have

\begin{split}\Ca_{\mathbb{Z}^{3}}(B(\eta[0,\lfloor nu\rfloor],3\delta n^{1/\beta})\geq 3\Ca_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(B(\eta[0,\lfloor nu\rfloor],2\delta n^{1/\beta})).\end{split}

(4.20)

The extra factor 3 comes from the fact that
\lim_{\|y\|\rightarrow\infty}\frac{G_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(0,y)}{G_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(0,y)}=3
, see
[
MP10
, Theorem 3.3]
and
[
LL10
, Theorem 4.3.1]
.

Step 3.

On the event
E
, we have
B(n^{-1/\beta}\eta[0,\lfloor nu\rfloor],2\delta)\supset B(\gamma[0,u],\delta)\supset\gamma[0,u]
, so

3\Ca_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(B(n^{-1/\beta}\eta[0,\lfloor nu\rfloor],2\delta))\geq 3\Ca_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\gamma[0,u]).

(4.21)

Combining Steps 1-3, we obtain that for all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
n_{0}
such that for all
n\geq n_{0}
,

\frac{1}{n^{1/\beta}}\Ca_{\mathbb{Z}^{3}}(\eta[0,\lfloor nu\rfloor])\geq 3(1-\varepsilon)\C_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\gamma[0,u]),

(4.22)

which concludes the proof of (
4.8
).

Reverse inequality.

The reverse inequality of (
4.8
) is entirely analogous. Steps 0, 2 and 3 are the same. Step 1 is also the same, only using that
E\subset E_{3}
, instead of
E_{2}
.
∎

4.3
Proof of Theorem
1.12

Let
C_{n}(u)=n^{-1}\C_{\mathbb{Z}^{3}}(\eta[0,\lfloor 3^{-\beta}n^{\beta}u\rfloor])
and
C(u)=\C_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\gamma[0,u])
. Let
D([0,\infty),[0,\infty))
be the space of càdlàg processes in
[0,\infty)
equipped with the metric
\chi
as defined in Section
1.4

and consider the right-inverse function
F\colon D([0,\infty),[0,\infty))\to D([0,\infty),[0,\infty))
, defined

F(A)(t):=\inf\{v:A(v)>t\}.

(4.23)

Then
\tau_{n}=3^{-\beta}n^{\beta}F(C_{n})
and
\theta=F(C)
.

Lemma 4.3
.

There exists a coupling of
(n^{-1/\beta}\eta(n\cdot))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\gamma
such that
n^{-1/\beta}\eta(n\cdot)
converges almost surely to
\gamma
in
(\mathcal{C},\chi)
and such that
(C_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges almost surely to
C
in
(D([0,\infty),[0,\infty)),\chi)
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

Proof.

First note that for
u_{1}<u_{2}
,
C_{n}(u_{2})-C_{n}(u_{1})
is bounded from above by a universal constant times the diameter of
n^{-1}\eta[\lfloor 3^{-\beta}n^{\beta}u_{1}\rfloor,\lfloor 3^{-\beta}n^{\beta}u_{2}\rfloor])
. Convergence of
(n^{-1}\eta(3^{-\beta}n^{\beta}t))_{t\geq 0}
with respect to the metric
\chi
combined with the Arzelà-Ascoli theorem thus implies that
C_{n}
is sequentially compact with respect to
\chi
. It follows from convergence of the finite-dimensional distributions in Proposition
4.2
that all subsequential limits must equal
C
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.12
.

Couple
\eta
and
\gamma
as in Lemma
4.3
. Then
C_{n}\rightarrow C
almost surely with respect to
\chi
as
n\rightarrow\infty
by Lemma
4.3
. Note that
\gamma
is a.s. continuous and injective
[
LS25a
, Section 9.1]
. This implies that
C
is a.s. continuous and strictly increasing. It follows from
[
Whi02
, Corollary 13.6.4]
that
C
is a.s. a continuity point of
F
. The continuous mapping theorem then implies that
F(C_{n})
converges a.s. to
F(C)
. So almost surely, for all
s\geq 0
,

3^{\beta}n^{-\beta}\tau_{n}(s)=F(C_{n})(s)\rightarrow F(C)(s)=\theta(s)

(4.24)

as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Let
T>0
. By convergence of
(n^{\beta}\eta(n\cdot))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and by the diameter bound on capacity, there exists
U
such that
\tau_{n}(T)\leq n^{\beta}U
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

\begin{split}&\,\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|\widetilde{\eta}_{n}(t)-\widetilde{\gamma}(t)|\\
=&\,\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|n^{-1}\eta_{n}(\tau_{n}(t))-\gamma(3^{-\beta}\theta(t))|\\
\leq&\,\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|n^{-1}\eta_{n}(\tau_{n}(t))-\gamma(3^{-\beta}3^{\beta}n^{-\beta}\tau_{n}(t))|+|\gamma(3^{-\beta}3^{\beta}n^{-\beta}\tau_{n}(t))-\gamma(3^{-\beta}\theta(t))|\\
\leq&\,\sup_{0\leq u\leq U}|n^{-1}\eta([0,n^{\beta}u])-\gamma(u)|+\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|\gamma(3^{-\beta}3^{\beta}n^{-\beta}\tau_{n}(t))-\gamma(3^{-\beta}\theta(t))|.\end{split}

(4.25)

The first term tends to 0 by convergence of
n^{-1}\eta(n^{\beta}\cdot)
to
\gamma
with respect to the metric
\chi
, which is equivalent to uniform convergence on compact domains. To see that the second term tends to 0, we use that
F(C_{n})
converges to
\theta
with respect to
\chi
, which implies that
F(C_{n})
converges uniformly to
\theta
on
[0,T]
. Then by uniform continuity of
\gamma
on
[0,U]
, we conclude that the second term vanishes, which completes the proof.
∎

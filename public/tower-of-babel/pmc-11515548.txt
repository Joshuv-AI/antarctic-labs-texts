Promotion of nerve regeneration and motor function recovery in SCI rats using LOCAS-iPSCs-NSCs
Authors: Gang Xu, Rui Ge, Chunli Zhang, Ziteng Zhao, Liwei Han, Wanhao Zhang, WenJie Yue, Jing Zhang, Yantao Zhao, Shuxun Hou, Li Li, Peng Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundSpinal cord injury (SCI) is a severe traumatic spinal condition with a poor prognosis. In this study, a scaffold called linearly ordered collagen aggregates (LOCAS) was created and loaded with induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs)-derived neural stem cells (NSCs) from human umbilical cord blood derived mesenchymal stem cells (hUCB-MSCs) to treat SCI in a rat model.MethodsThe rats underwent a complete transection SCI resulting in a 3-mm break at either the T9 or T10 level of the spinal cord.ResultsScanning electron microscope analysis revealed a uniform pore structure on the coronal plane of the scaffold. The LOCAS had a porosity of 88.52% and a water absorption of 1161.67%. Its compressive modulus and stress were measured at 4.1 MPa and 205 kPa, respectively, with a degradation time of 10 weeks. After 12 weeks, rats in the LOCAS-iPSCs-NSCs group exhibited significantly higher BBB scores (8.6) compared to the LOCAS-iPSCs-NSCs group (5.6) and the Model group (4.2). The CatWalk analysis showed improved motion trajectory, regularity index (RI), and swing speed in the LOCAS-iPSCs-NSCs group compared to the other groups. Motor evoked potentials latency was lower and amplitude was higher in the LOCAS-iPSCs-NSCs group, indicating better neural function recovery. Histological analysis demonstrated enhanced neuronal differentiation of NSCs and nerve fiber regeneration promoted by LOCAS-iPSCs-NSCs, leading to improved motor function recovery in rats. The LOCAS scaffold facilitated ordered neurofilament extension and guided nerve regeneration.ConclusionsThe combination of LOCAS and iPSCs-NSCs demonstrated a positive therapeutic impact on motor function recovery and tissue repair in rats with SCI. This development offers a more resilient bionic microenvironment and presents novel possibilities for clinical SCI repair.

Introduction

Spinal cord injury (SCI), is a severe traumatic spinal disease with poor prognosis. According to epidemiological statistics, the incidence of SCI is increasing year by year in the world. And its disability rate is high, with many complications, treatment is expensive. It brings serious physical, psychological and economic burden to patients, and also causes great pressure to society. In recent years, the treatment of SCI has made some progress, however, less than 1% of patients can fully recover neurological function [1]. How to promote the repair of nerve function after SCI has become a major problem in clinical and scientific research.

The application of biological tissue engineering materials in the treatment of SCI is considered as a promising treatment. Many studies have been done to improve the biocompatibility and mechanical properties of scaffolds. In this regard, linearly ordered collagen scaffold, LOCS, is linear and ordered collagen scaffold material and can guide nerve regeneration. It inhibits glial scar formation and provides a microenvironment for neuronal survival and growth, guiding axons to grow and extend along linear collagen fibers [2]. Previous studies have shown that LOCS scaffolds can promote nerve regeneration and motor function recovery in rat and canine models of total spinal cord transection, and clinical findings in patients with chronic SCI have confirmed the safety and efficacy of LOCS [3, 4]. However, due to its poor mechanical properties and rapid degradation rate, it still cannot simulate the biomimetic skeleton structure of spinal cord. So far, many methods including biological, physical, and chemical cross-linking have been investigated to enhance the mechanical strength and retard the degradation rate of collagen scaffolds [5–9]. In this regard, heparan sulfate has been applied to combine collagen through cross-linking reaction to improve the mechanical strength of collagen scaffold for the remedy of SCI [7] and traumatic brain injury [10, 11]. In a rat abdominal hernia defect model, a high-strength, flexible, porous collagen-based scaffolds have been used to promote vascularized muscle regeneration [9]. However, the effects of another collagen aggregates on SCI repair still remain unknown.

In the treatment of central nervous system damage, the clinical treatment of NSCs is often achieved by the differential formed neurons replacing necrotic neurons and by secreting neurotrophic factors to maintain the survival of damaged cells, but directly transplanted NSCs present the low survival rate and low degrees of differentiation and cell migration, leading to the severely limited clinical therapeutic effect of NSCs [12, 13]. In recent years, with the development of stem cell technology, induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs), as good seed cells, have become the forefront of stem cell research at home and abroad. It resembles embryonic stem cells (ESCs), with the abilities of multiple differentiation and self-renewal potential, has a broad research prospect in autologous cell transplantation, tissue engineering and other fields. It can differentiate into a variety of mature cells in vitro and in vivo, and avoids the problems of immune rejection and ethical restriction of ESCs [14, 15]. Cord blood may be a very good source of autologous iPSCs, due to the extracted way of cord blood is minimally invasive and can be frozen for about 20 years. Another advantage of cord blood is that there are currently many cord blood banks being established. Cord blood mononuclear cells have properties that are closer to iPSCs than other cells [16]. By reprogramming human umbilical cord blood mesenchymal stem cells (hUCB-MSCs) in vitro to get iPSCs, then NSCs were obtained by inducing iPSCs in vitro. It can effectively avoid the ethical and moral problems caused by the experiment and clinical application, and the materials are convenient to be obtained. Moreover, it's painless for the donor, and it's able to harvest as many NSC as it needs [17].

In this study, the LOCAS scaffold was constructed with good mechanical properties and biomimetic structure. At the same time, the effect of the scaffold in the treatment of the complete transection SCI in rats was investigated by combining with hUCB-MSCs derived iPSCs-derived NSCs, in order to obviously distinguish the differences of SCI repair between LOCS and LOCAS (Scheme 1), and provide more possibilities for clinical treatment of SCI.Scheme 1Schematic illustration of preparation and motor function recovery of LOCAS-iPSCs-NSCs for SCI repair

Materials and methods

Materials and reagents

Bovine Achilles tendons were harvested from nineteen young bovine hind legs (Shengri Farmers Market, Beijing, China), iPSC, PSCeasy® II medium, NeuroEasy Human neural stem Cell Induction Culture Kit and NeuroEasy Human Neural Stem Cell (Adherent) Culture Kit were obtained from Cellapy Biotechnology (Beijing, China). SPF male Sprague Dawley (SD) rats were obtained from Sibefu Biotechnology (Beijing, China), 4% paraformaldehyde was purchased from Sunshine Yingrui Biotechnology (Beijing, China). Antibodies including Nestin, SOX2, β3-tubulin (Tuj1), glial fibrillary acidic protein (GFAP) and Neurofilament-160 (NF160) were purchased from Abcam (Cambridge, MA, USA), CoraLite488-conjugated Goat Anti-Rabbit IgG and CoraLite594-conjugated Goat Anti-Mouse IgG were purchased from Sanying Biotechnology (Wuhan, Hubei, China).

Preparation and characterization of LOCAS

As described previously [18] and properly modified, collagen aggregates were extracted from the bovine tendon. In brief, the fat and anadesma of the bovine tendon were wiped off in the beginning, and then cut into 1.0 mm slices and rinsed. After that they were soaked in 0.5% chlorhexidine acetate and then homogenized. Next, tissue homogenates were filled with 0.5 M acetic acid at a ratio of 1:50 (w/v) at 3–8 °C for 48 h, with a soft and constant stirring for swelling up the tissue fully. The swollen tissues were isolated by a centrifuge at 8500 r/min at 4 °C for 20 min. To adjust pH to 7.5, the reserved supernatants were dripped with the sodium hydroxide solution. And NaCl was added into at a final concentration of 10% for salted-out. The collected precipitate was dialyzed against ultrapure water with a molecular weight cut-off of 80–140 kDa to obtain a pH-neutral solution of collagen aggregates. The collagen aggregates were eventually harvested after lyophilization.

The collagen aggregates (100 mg) were soaked in ultrapure water at 3–8 °C for 24 h to obtain the suspension. The suspension was degassed under vacuum (50 mTorr) for 60 min and stored at 4 °Covernight in order to form a gelatinous suspension. The suspension of collagen aggregates was injected into a silica gel tube with an inner diameter of 3 mm and a length of 10 cm and slowly immersed in liquid nitrogen along the axis. It was kept in liquid nitrogen for 30–60 min, and then kept at − 80 °C for 30–60 min. The frozen suspension was cut into 1.5 cm short segments, and then lyophilized (− 30 °C, < 100 mTorr) for 24 h to fabricate the dried LOCAS. Then, the porosity, water absorption rate, mechanical properties and degradation properties of the scaffolds were analyzed. The coronal and sagittal morphology of the scaffolds was observed by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) (HITACHI CORPORATION).

NSCs obtained by inducing iPSCs and co-culture experiment of LOCAS and iPSCs-NSCs

We collaborated with Cellapy Biotechnology (Beijing, China) to construct iPSCs derived from hUCB-MSCs transfected with Sendai virus and reprogrammed. Stable cultured iPSCs were successfully differentiated to iPSCs-NSCs induced by NSCs induction culture kit, then iPSCs-NSCs were continued cultured and cell identification was performed. After 7 days in culture, a total of 1 × 106 iPSCs-NSCs were seeded on LOCS and LOCAS scaffolds pretreated in 24-well plates. Some co-cultures were used for cell viability detection at 1 d, 3 d, 5 d and 7 d and SEM observation at 7d, and the others were used for the animal experiment.

Animal experimental design

Forty SPF male SD rats were divided into four groups with 10 rats in each group using the random table number method. The animal experiments were approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of the Fourth Medical Center of PLA General Hospital. The environment was free of pathogens, the ambient temperature was 22 °C, 12 h light /12 h dark cycle was guaranteed every day, the indoor humidity was 50–55%, and adaptive feeding was carried out for one week before the experiment. During surgery, it was performed under a general anesthetic with 3% (w/v) pentobarbital sodium salt solution administered intraperitoneally. In sham operation group (Sham), only the lamina was opened, the spinal cord was exposed, and the dura was sutured directly without injury. In model group (Model), a 3-mm break at T 9 or T 10 level of spinal cord was performed to form a complete transection with no material implantation; LOCS-iPSCs-NSCs group: the complete transection injury model with implantation of LOCS combined with iPSCs-NSCs; LOCAS-iPSCs-NSCs group: the complete transection injury model with implantation of LOCAS combined with iPSCs-NSCs. The success criteria of the model were as follows: spastic swing, tail wagging reflex, and slow paralysis of both lower limbs occurred immediately after operation. Two hours after operation, Basso, Beattie and Bresnahan (BBB) scores and electrophysiological tests were performed to evaluate the hindlimb motor ability of rats. If the BBB score was 0 and no wave amplitude could be observed in electrophysiology, the model was successfully constructed. 8 mg/g gentamicin was injected intraperitoneally twice a day for 3 days after operation to prevent infection. The animals were intraperitoneally administered a 3% (w/v) pentobarbital sodium salt solution to minimize stress and suffering. After 10 min, the animals were euthanized by disrupting the spinal cord. The work has been reported in line with the ARRIVE guidelines 2.0.

Behavioral evaluation

Every other week after operation, the rats were placed in an open box, and the box wall was tapped to make them crawl. The movement distance, hip, knee and ankle joint walking, trunk movement and coordination were observed. Each rat was observed for 15 min, and the exercise ability of rats in each group was examed by BBB scores. The assessments were double-blind and conducted by experimentalists familiar with the BBB rating. The catwalk method had previously been established for the functional evaluation of SCI models [19, 20]. Every two weeks, the CatWalk gait test was performed. The rats were placed on the CatWalk instrument (XT, Noldus of the Netherlands) track, the movement trajectories and footprint intensities of the rats were recorded and analyzed. And the parameters of regularly index (RI, %) and swing speed (cm/s) were assessed [20, 21]. RI: a percentage index for the degree of interlimb coordination during gait, as measured by the number of normal step sequence patterns (NSSP), multiplied by the number of paws and divided by the number of paw placements (PL); RI = 100% × (NSSP × 4)/PL. Swing speed: speed of the paw during swing stance, which is computed from stride length and swing duration (cm/s).

At 12 w after operation, the rats were anesthetized and fixed at the correct position in stereoscopic positioning. The stimulating electrode of the evoked potentiometer was placed at the intersection of coronal suture and sagittal suture in the cortical motor area, and the recording electrode was placed at the posterior tibial nerve, a reference electrode was inserted subcutaneously, adjust the appropriate stimulus current size, motor evoked potentials were recorded in the right hindlimb.

Histological analysis

After behavioral evaluation of postoperative 12 w, spinal cords were perfused and fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde. The T9-T10 spinal cord was taken out and frozen sections was performed along the longitudinal section of the spinal cord with a thickness of 10 μm. Part of the sections were stained with HE, and the areas of spinal cord scar and cavity were observed under the multifunctional microscope microscope (BX50, Olympus) to evaluate the degree of SCI. The remaining sections were treated with Tuj1 and GFAP, NF160 and GFAP, immunofluorescence double staining of two groups to evaluate the differentiation of NSCs and regeneration of nerve fibers. Using the scoring criteria proposed by Siddique [22], the quantity and intensity of positive cells were scored under a microscope in 3 randomly-selected fields per slice. Semi-quantitative analysis was performed by two investigators who were blind to the experimental groups.

Statistical analysis

All data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation, n = 10. All experiments were repeated three times. GraphPad Prism version 7.0 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA) was used and a one-way analysis of variance was performed followed by Tukey’s post hoc test for pairwise comparison if the data were normally distributed. *P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Preparation and characterization of LOCAS

The LOCAS scaffolds prepared in the experiment were cylindrically shaped with a length of 1.5 cm and a base diameter of 2.5 mm (Fig. 1A). SEM results showed that the coronal plane of the scaffold had uniform pore structure, which was conducive to cell adhesion and growth. The sagittal plane of collagen fiber bundle showed an oriented structure, which could guide the growth of nerve cells and the regeneration of neurofilaments (Fig. 1B, C). The porosity test showed that the porosity of LOCAS (88.52%) was significantly higher than that of LOCS (50.45%) (P < 0.05), higher porosity was more conducive to cell growth (Fig. 1D). Water absorption test showed the same trend as porosity test, LOCAS (1161.67%) was significantly higher than LOCS (985%) (P < 0.05), the increased porosity made the water absorption of LOCAS further increase (Fig. 1E). The mechanical property test showed that the compressive modulus and compressive stress of LOCAS (4.1 MPa, 205 kPa) were significantly higher than those of LOCS (1.96 MPa, 122.5 kPa) (P < 0.05), which would be more conducive to the maintenance of spatial configuration of scaffolds during nerve regeneration and provide guidance for the nerve regeneration (Fig. 1F, G). At the same time, degradation test showed that the degradation time of LOCS was 6 w, which of LOCAS was extended to 10 w (Fig. 1H).Fig. 1Preparation and characterization of LOCAS. hUCB-MSCs-iPSCs, iPSCs-NSCs and co-culture experiment of LOCAS and iPSCs-NSCs

In cooperation with Cellapy Biotechnology (Beijing, China), four transcription factors namely Oct4, Nanog, Sox2 and SSEA4 were transferred into hUCB-MSCs with Sendai virus as a vector to reprogram hUCB-MSCs into iPSCs, and iPSCs derived from hUCB-MSCs were constructed. After passing to P3 generation, a stable clonal group was formed (Fig. 2A). Immunofluorescence identification showed strong positive expression of iPSCs markers OCT4 and NANOG, indicating the success of hUCB-MSCs-iPSCs construction (Fig. 2B, C). Furthermore, we successfully induced hUCB-MSCs-iPSCs in the direction of NSCs by using the NeuroEasy human neural stem cell induction culture kit. After 3 days of changing the cell medium, cells could be seen floating and clustered together, forming irregular spherical bodies. After 7 days, the cells in the spheroids were gradually proliferating and the spheroids were enlarged, dark yellow, transparent and regular in shape (Fig. 2D). After 10 days, the morphology area of the cell spheres was stable, the outer edge of the cell spheres had uniform burrs, and occasionally adherent cell spheres were observed. After that, the liquid was changed every 3 days, and the cell spheres were passaged after 14 days when they grew to a suitable size. By stably passaging the induced NSCs spheres to P3 passage using the NeuroEasy Human neural Stem Cell (Adherent) culture kit, we observed a distinct NSCs phenotype (Fig. 2E). After iPSCs-NSCs was amplified to a certain amount, we conducted co-culture experiments of iPSCs-NSCs and collagen scaffolds, and detected cell activity of 1, 3, 5, and 7 days by Cell Counting KIT-8 (CCK-8). It was found that there were no significant differences in absorbance at 450 nm between cells inoculated on LOCAS, LOCS and culture dishes, indicating that LOCAS had no potential cytotoxicity and iPSCs-NSCs had good compatibility with LOCAS (Fig. 2J). In the meanwhile, the growth of iPSCs-NSCs on LOCAS was observed by SEM on the seventh day. The result showed that iPSCs-NSCs stretched well on the scaffold and fitted closely (Fig. 2K).

Establishment of animal model and behavioral evaluation

A complete transection SCI was performed and yielded a 3-mm break at T9-10 level of spinal cord to establish the model (Fig. 3A). BBB scores were evaluated weekly from 1 to 12 weeks after SCI, it showed the scores of the LOCAS-iPSCs-NSCs group at each stage was higher than that of LOCS-iPSCs-NSCs and Model groups. At 12 w, the scores of LOCAS-iPSCs-NSCs group was 8.6, significantly higher than 5.6 of the LOCS-iPSCs-NSCs group (P < 0.05) and 4.2 of the Model group (P < 0.05) (Fig. 3B).Fig. 3Model and behavioral evaluation. Model and behavioral evaluation. A Establishing the model and implantation of scaffold materials; B BBB scores of animals in each group; C Movement tracks recorded by CatWalk at 12 w; D–F Electrophysiological test of 12 w, waveform of electrophysiological test, amplitude of waveforms and latency of waveforms

Every two weeks, the rats were placed on the CatWalk instrument track, and the movement trajectories of the rats were recorded and analyzed. The result at 12 w showed that both regularity and clarity of the motion trajectory and footprint intensities of rats in the LOCAS-iPSCs-NSCs group were better than those in the LOCS-iPSCs-NSCs group, and significantly better than those in the Model group (Fig. 3C). Moreover, both RI and swing speed of rats in the LOCAS-iPSCs-NSCs group were obviously decreased than those in the Model group, and also significantly decreased than those in the LOCS-iPSCs-NSCs group (Fig. 3D, E).

In order to further illustrate the recovery of motor function of rats, motor evoked potentials were tested, and the result showed that the latency and amplitude of motor evoked potentials in sham operation group were 2.84 ms and 7.24 mV. The latency of motor evoked potentials in the LOCAS-iPSCs-NSCs group (7.05 ms) was significantly lower than that in the LOCS-iPSCs-NSCs group (11.45 ms) (P < 0.05) and significantly lower than that in Model group (18.95 ms) (P < 0.05). On the contrary, the amplitude of motor evoked potentials in the LOCAS-iPSCs-NSCs group was 4.05 mV higher than that in the LOCS-iPSCs-NSCs group (2.44 mV, P < 0.05), and significantly higher than that in the Model group (1.15 mV, P < 0.05) (Fig. 3F–H).

The results indicated that the combined application of LOCAS and iPSCs-NSCs was superior to LOCS and iPSCs-NSCs, and significantly promoted the recovery of motor function after SCI in rats.

Histological analysis

The recovery of SCI centers evaluated by general observation and HE staining

The general observation of 12 w tissue samples (Fig. 4) showed that the repair of SCI in Model group was significantly worse than that in the LOCS-iPSCs-NSCs group and LOCAS-iPSCs-NSCs group, while the structure of the Sham group was intact, and LOCS-iPSCs-NSCs group and LOCAS-iPSCs-NSCs groups had a certain degree of repair. HE staining result also showed that the fiber structure of the Sham group was arranged regularly and orderly, and the organization was complete. In the Model group, there were still a large number of hollow structures in the center of injury, and the organization arrangement was disordered. The cavity structure of the LOCS-iPSCs-NSCs group and LOCAS-iPSCs-NSCs group was significantly reduced, and the tissues tended to be rearranged in an ordered structure, and the tissue order of the LOCAS-iPSCs-NSCs group was slightly better than that of the LOCS-iPSCs-NSCs group (Fig. 4).Fig. 4General observation of samples at 12 w and H&E staining at the center of the injured site (scale bars = 50 μm)

Immunofluorescent staining

To further illustrate the tissue repair, we performed immunofluorescence double staining to observe the differentiation of NSCs and the regeneration of new neurofilaments in the tissue. For Tuj1 and GFAP immunofluorescence staining, cells were stained with Tuj1 (green) for neurons, GFAP (red) for glial cells, and DAPI (blue) for nuclei [23]. The results showed that, in the absence of intervention, NSCs were more likely to differentiate into glial cells after SCI (Fig. 5A, B). After the implantation of LOCAS scaffold, NSCs were more likely to differentiate into neurons than those in Model and LOCS-iPSCs-NSCs groups (Fig. 5A, B). These results indicated that the implantation of scaffolds promoted the differentiation of NSCs into neurons to a certain extent. The differentiation rate of neurons in the LOCAS-iPSCs-NSCs group was higher than that in the LOCS-iPSCs-NSCs group, which was attributed to the enhanced mechanical properties and moderative degradation rate, which offered the spatial support for NSCs grow in the injured area (Fig. 5A, B). In order to determine the regeneration of nerve repair, neurofilament protein immunofluorescence staining was performed, and the results showed that in the Model group, without intervention, there was only a small positive expression of NF160 (Fig. 6A, B), and the tissue structure was irregular, and the formation of glial scar hindered the regeneration and repair of nerve. In the LOCS-iPSCs-NSCs group, we observed the generation of some aligned regenerative neurofilaments due to the guidance of the scaffold (Fig. 6A, B), and the same effect was more obvious in the LOCAS-iPSCs-NSCs group (Fig. 6A, B).Fig. 5Differentiation of NSC at the injured site at 12 w (Tuj1, GFAP, scale bar = 50 μm)Fig. 6Neurofilament regeneration at the injured site at 12 w (NF160, GFAP, scale bar = 50 μm)

Discussion

So far, SCI treatment is involved in surgical decompression in the early phase, stabilization, followed by increasing blood pressure for avoiding sequential injury and positive care arrangements, very few of developing targeted therapies have reached practice clinically [24]. SCI remedy remains a crucial challenge, and is in sore need of more and more effective clinical treatment. In this study, on the basis of the original LOCS scaffold, a linearly ordered collagen aggregates scaffold (LOCAS) was constructed, which improved the defects of the original scaffold in terms of mechanical and degraded properties. SCI self-repair is a long and complex process, and the improvement of mechanical properties and degradation rate will help scaffold materials play a longer role in the injury site, and continue to provide a microenvironment for nerve regeneration [25]. While facilitating the implantation operation, it is more conducive to play a long-term spatial support and guiding role in vivo, and better play the role of hindering the invasion of glial scars and guiding the linear regeneration of nerves after SCI. For this reason, the development of porous nerve conduits is an essential feature for the regeneration of nerve tissue as the uniform and interconnected pore structure restrains the infiltration of scar tissue while providing nutrients and transferring nutrients/wastes [26, 27]. In this study, LOCAS presented uniform pore structure and proper connectivity for nerve regeneration.

Upside and downside fibers of the spinal cord are destroyed by the lost neurons due to SCI contributing to dysfunctional sensory and motor reaction under the injury site [28, 29]. We obtained human-derived iPSCs by reprogramming hUCB-MSCs in vitro, and then induced iPSCs in vitro to obtain human-derived NSCs to against the sources of NSCs defect, which can also effectively avoid the ethical and moral issues caused by experimental and clinical applications [30]. IPSCs-derived NSCs can differentiate into neurons in vivo, which can help to improve axonal rebirth and junctions to form synaptic links with master neurons and implanted NSCs [31, 32]. Additionally, the material is convenient to obtain, and can obtain a large number of required NSCs without any pain to the donor [17].

However, NSCs alone are hardly applied to recover the nerve injuries due to the limitations including fast cell diffusion, defected neural tissue, absent support matrix, formed glial scar, and inflammation. To SCI, biomaterial implantation is requisite to repair the injured nerve [33, 34]. Therefore, we applied iPSCs-derived NSCs combined with LOCS or LOCAS in a rat model of SCI to verify their different repair effects. After 12 w implantation intervention, behavioral evaluation including the BBB scores, regularity, footprint intensities, RI and swing speed of motion trajectory and electrophysiological motor evoked potentials was significantly improved in the LOCAS-iPSCs-NSCs group. Histological staining showed that the hollow structure of SCI center in the LOCAS-iPSCs-NSCs group was improved, and the fiber arrangement gradually tended to be orderly. LOCAS-iPSCs-NSCs also presented better Tuj1 staining. Tuj1 can distinguish neurons from glial cells as a neuron-specific protein [35]. After SCI, endogenous NSCs are more likely to differentiate into glial cells without intervention and form reactive glial cells, which promote the formation of scar structure and hinder the regeneration and repair of nerves [36]. Instead, partial recovery from SCI injury or regenerative function has been achieved by transplantation of iPSCs, NSCs, or induced NSCs, while iPSCs-NSCs are more and more reported [37, 38]. Combined with implantation of LOCAS scaffold, iPSCs-NSCs in the direction of neuron differentiation increased compared with Model and LOCS groups due to the existence of scaffold blocking the invasion of glial scar and providing space conditions for the generation of neurons. The improvement of mechanical properties will make the spatial support and barrier effect of original LOCS better. The differentiation rate of neurons in LOCAS-iPSCs-NSCs group was higher than that in the LOCS-iPSCs-NSCs group. This will be more conducive to the subsequent regeneration and repair of SCI. On the one hand, due to the improved mechanical and degradation properties of LOCAS, the duration of steric support and guidance increased. On the other hand, except for the introduction of derived cells increases the number of cells available for regeneration in the lesion area, it may also be related to some substances that are autocrine and paracrine in iPSCs-NSCs. Similar studies have previously demonstrated on hNSCs [39, 40], but the functional differences between iPSCs-induced NSCs and direct-derived NSCs in this regard is still uncertain. Furthermore, NF160 staining showed the regeneration of neurofilaments in the LOCAS-iPSCs-NSCs group was also improved. The immunohistochemical detection of NF-160 (medium-molecular-weight neurofilaments, 140–160 kDa) is usually used to observe the regeneration of injured nerves [41, 42].

In this study, there are also some limitations. Though we developed a novel linearly ordered collagen aggregates scaffold, namely LOCAS and applied it with iPSCs-induced NSCs to repair SCI in a rat model, the further work is needed to elucidate the underlying molecular mechanisms of the repair in vivo. Recently, several important signaling pathways are involved in SCI injury, such as TLR4-NF-κB and JAK2/STAT3 in inflammatory responses [43, 44], Nrf2 signaling in the oxidative stress [45], noncoding RNAs and Rho/ROCK [46], PI3K/Akt in the cell apoptosis [47] and mTOR in synaptogenesis, neuron growth, differentiation, and survival [48]. Besides, biomarkers of inflammatory or oxidative stress are needed to determine for the preliminary judgment on molecular mechanisms, and this is another limitation of our study. We speculate the underlying molecular mechanisms in this study are associated with multiple signaling pathways from several above aspects. And in the future work, we may explore the underlying molecular mechanisms by gene microarray analysis, proteomics and bioinformatic analysis.

Conclusions

Overall, the LOCAS scaffolds presented the uniform pore structure and proper connectivity for cell adhesion and growth, and hindering the invasion of glial scars to guide the linear regeneration of nerves. LOCAS combined with iPSCs-NSCs showed a good therapeutic effect in the recovery of motor function with higher BBB scores, improved CatWalk trajectories and motor evoked potentials in rats with SCI. Furthermore, it could improve HE staining for reducing the cavity structure, rearranging and ordering the structure. It also could increase Tuj1 staining for more neurons instead of glial cells and enhance NF160 staining for extending the neurofilaments orderly to guided nerve regeneration linearly. Therefore, it provides a more durable bionic situation together with iPSCs-NSCs, which can provide new ideas for clinical repair of SCI.

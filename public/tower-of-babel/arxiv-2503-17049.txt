Optimal control on a brain tumor growth model with lactate metabolism, viscoelastic effects, and tissue damage
Giulia Cavalleri, Alain Miranville

Abstract
In this paper, we study an optimal control problem for a brain tumor growth model that incorporates lactate metabolism, viscoelastic effects, and tissue damage. The PDE system, introduced in [ Cav+25 ] , couples a Fisher–Kolmogorov-type equation for tumor cell density with a reaction-diffusion equation for the lactate, a quasi-static force balance governing the displacement, and a nonlinear differential inclusion for tissue damage. The control variables, representing chemotherapy and a lactate-targeting drug, influence tumor progression and treatment response. Starting from well-posedness, regularity, and continuous dependence results proven in [ Cav+25 ] , we define a suitable cost functional and prove the existence of optimal controls. Then, we analyze the differentiability of the control-to-state operator and establish a necessary first-order condition for treatment optimality. Key words: tumor growth models, lactate kinetics, mechanical effects, damage, optimal control, adjoint system, necessary optimality conditions. AMS (MOS) Subject Classification: 35Q93, 49J20, 49K20, 35Q92, 92C50.

Introduction

The aim of the latter is not only to predict the course of the disease, but also to optimize treatments, by adjusting the combination, timing, and dosage of therapies to achieve the best possible outcome for the patient, meaning maximum reduction of the tumor and minimum drug-related side effects. This is where the present contribution comes into play. The model.
Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{2}
domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n=2,3
and let
\boldsymbol{\nu}
be its outward unit normal to
\partial\Omega
. We are interested in the PDE system

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varphi-\Delta\varphi=U(\varphi,\sigma,z,\chi_{1}),

(1.1a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\sigma-\Delta\sigma+K(\varphi,\sigma,z)=\chi_{2}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}S(\varphi,z),

(1.1b)

\displaystyle-\diver\left[\mathcal{A}\varepsilon(\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u})+\mathcal{B}(\varphi,z)\varepsilon(\boldsymbol{u})\right]=\boldsymbol{f},

(1.1c)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}z-\Delta z+\beta(z)+\pi(z)\ni\iota-\Psi(\varphi,\varepsilon(\boldsymbol{u})),

(1.1d)

posed in the parabolic cylinder
Q\coloneqq\Omega\times(0,T)
, where the domain
\Omega
represents the brain and the fixed time
T>0
is the medical treatment duration. In equations (
1.1a
) and (
1.1b
) we introduced the notation

K(\varphi,\sigma,z)\coloneqq\frac{k_{1}(\varphi,z)\sigma}{k_{2}(\varphi,z)+\sigma},\quad U(\varphi,\sigma,z,\chi_{1})\coloneqq(p(\sigma,z)-\chi_{1}\color[rgb]{0,0,0})\varphi\left(1-\frac{\varphi}{N}\right)-\varphi g(\sigma,z)

for the sake of brevity. As already pointed out, a slightly modified version of (
1.1
) was proposed in
[
Cav+25
]
. We refer the reader to it for further modeling details and recall only the most important aspects. The variable
\varphi
represents the tumor cell density and takes value in
[0,N]
, where the constant
N
is the fixed carrying capacity of the tissue. It is ruled by the Fischer–Kolmogorov type equation (
1.1a
). The parabolic reaction-diffusion equation (
1.1b
) governs the evolution of
\sigma
, which is the intracellular lactate concentration. The quasi-static balance of forces (
1.1c
) for a viscoelastic tissue describes the dynamic of
\boldsymbol{u}
, which is the small displacement field of each point with respect to a reference undeformed configuration. The local tissue damage
z
takes value in the interval
[0,1]
, where
z=0
means there is no damage while
z=1
means that the tissue is totally damaged. Its evolution is ruled by the parabolic differential inclusion (
1.1d
). Finally,
\chi_{1}
is the concentration of a cytotoxic drug, which decreases the tumor proliferation
p(\sigma,z)
in the mass source
U
. Similarly, the term
\chi_{2}
represents a lactate targeting drug and affects the lactate source term
S
. The system (
1.1
) is coupled with the boundary conditions

\displaystyle\partial_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\varphi=\partial_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}z=0,

(1.2a)

\displaystyle\partial_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\sigma=\sigma_{\Gamma}-\sigma,

(1.2b)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}=\mathbf{0},

(1.2c)

and the initial conditions

\varphi(0)=\varphi_{0},\quad\sigma(0)=\sigma_{0},\quad\boldsymbol{u}(0)=\boldsymbol{u}_{0},\quad z(0)=z_{0}.

(1.3)

The cost functional.
Introducing the notation
\boldsymbol{\chi}=(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
, we define the following cost functional

\begin{split}\mathcal{J}((\varphi,\sigma,\boldsymbol{u},z),\boldsymbol{\chi})\coloneqq&\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2}\lVert\varphi-\varphi_{Q}\rVert_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}+\frac{\alpha_{2}}{2}\lVert\varphi(T)-\varphi_{\Omega}\rVert_{H}^{2}+\alpha_{3}\int_{\Omega}\varphi(T)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\\
&+\frac{\alpha_{4}}{2}\lVert\sigma-\sigma_{Q}\rVert_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}+\frac{\alpha_{5}}{2}\lVert\sigma(T)-\sigma_{\Omega}\rVert_{H}^{2}\\
&+\frac{\alpha_{6}}{2}\lVert\sqrt{\gamma(\varphi)}\varepsilon(\boldsymbol{u})\rVert_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}+\frac{\alpha_{7}}{2}\lVert z-z_{Q}\rVert_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}\\
&+\alpha_{8}\int_{\Omega}z(T)\,\mathrm{d}x\,+\frac{\alpha_{9}}{2}\lVert\boldsymbol{\chi}\rVert_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}.\end{split}

(1.4)

The non-negative constants
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{9}
are weights that can not vanish all at the same time, while
\varphi_{Q}
,
\varphi_{\Omega}
,
\sigma_{Q}
,
\sigma_{\Omega}
,
z_{Q}
are target functions. More explicitly, the term
\varphi_{Q}
(resp.
\sigma_{Q}
,
z_{Q}
) is a desired evolution for the tumor (resp. the lactate, the damage), while
\varphi_{\Omega}
(resp.
\sigma_{\Omega}
) is a desired final configuration of the tumor (resp. concentration of the lactate). The third and
eighth addends measure the size of the tumor and the magnitude of the damage at the end of the treatment. Since the presence of high mechanical stress, especially in certain areas of the brain, can compromise its functionality, we are interested in keeping it low. The sixth term serves this purpose, and
\gamma
may be, for instance, the indicator function of a subdomain of
\Omega
where the stress is intended to remain low. Finally, the last addend is a
L^{2}
regularization for the controls.
We are interested in studying the minimizers of the cost functional (
1.4
) subject to the PDE system (
1.1
)–(
1.3
) and constrained to a suitable admissible control set. We define it as
\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}=\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}^{1}\times\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}^{2}
with

\begin{split}&\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}^{1}\coloneqq\{\chi_{1}\in L^{2}(V)\cap L^{\infty}(Q)\,\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,\lVert\chi_{1}\rVert_{L^{2}(V)}\leq C_{\text{ad}},\,\,\underline{\chi_{1}}\leq\chi_{1}\leq\overline{\chi_{1}}\text{ a.e.}\},\\
&\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}^{2}\coloneqq\{\chi_{2}\in L^{\infty}(Q)\,\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,\underline{\chi_{2}}\leq\chi_{2}\leq\overline{\chi_{2}}\text{ a.e.}\},\end{split}

where
C_{\text{ad}}>0
is a fixed constant and
\underline{\chi_{1}}
,
\overline{\chi_{1}}
,
\underline{\chi_{2}}
,
\overline{\chi_{2}}\in L^{\infty}(Q)
are given threshold functions such that
\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}
is nonempty. The admissible control set
\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}
is a subset of

\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{U}^{1}\times\mathcal{U}^{2}\coloneqq\left[L^{2}(V)\cap L^{\infty}(Q)\right]\times L^{\infty}(Q)

equipped with its natural norm that we denote with
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathcal{U}}
.

Remark 1.1
.

Notice that
\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}
is a nonempty, closed, and convex subset of
\mathcal{U}
. Moreover, there exists a positive constant
R
such that

\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}\subseteq\mathcal{U}_{R}\coloneqq\{\boldsymbol{\chi}\in\mathcal{U}\,\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,\lVert\boldsymbol{\chi}\rVert_{\mathcal{U}}<R\}.

Aim and plan of the paper.
The goal of this paper is to prove that the optimal control problem has at least one minimizer and to derive first-order necessary conditions for optimality. It is organized as follows. In
Section

2
, after introducing some notation and preliminary results, we enlist the hypotheses we are going to use throughout the paper. Then, we recall some known results about the state system (

1.3

]

In

Section

5
we use it to prove the differentiability of the control-to-state operator. Finally, in
Section

6

3
The optimal control problem

In view of the previous

(4.6c)

(4.6d)

a.e. in
(0,T)
, for every
\eta\in V
and
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in V_{0}
. Moreover, the solution satisfies the following estimate:

\lVert\xi\rVert_{H^{1}(H)\cap L^{\infty}(V)\cap L^{2}(W)}+\lVert\rho\rVert_{H^{1}(H)\cap L^{\infty}(V)\cap L^{2}(W)}+\lVert\boldsymbol{\omega}\rVert_{W^{1,\infty}(V_{0})}\\
+\lVert\xi\rVert_{H^{1}(H)\cap L^{\infty}(V)\cap L^{2}(W)}\leq C_{R}\lVert\boldsymbol{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(Q)^{2}}.

(4.7)

Proof.

Existence can be proved using a Galerkin scheme. Since it is a standard procedure, we will show only the formal a priori estimates that are necessary to pass to the limit from the discrete to the continuous system. We test (
4.2a
) with
\xi
, (
4.2b
) with
\rho
, (
4.2d
) with
\zeta
, and sum the three equations. Applying the Young inequality and

4.1
, we obtain

\begin{split}\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}t}&\left(\lVert\xi\rVert_{H}^{2}+\lVert\rho\rVert_{H}^{2}+\lVert\zeta\rVert_{H}^{2}\right)+\lVert\nabla\xi\rVert_{H}^{2}+\lVert\nabla\rho\rVert_{H}^{2}+\lVert\nabla\zeta\rVert_{H}^{2}\\
&\leq C_{R}\left(\lVert\xi\rVert_{H}^{2}+\lVert\rho\rVert_{H}^{2}+\lVert\varepsilon(\boldsymbol{\omega})\rVert_{H}^{2}+\lVert\zeta\rVert_{H}^{2}+\lVert h_{1}\rVert_{H}^{2}+\lVert h_{2}\rVert_{H}^{2}\right).\end{split}

(4.8)

Testing (
4.2c
) with
\varepsilon(\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\omega})
and employing the fact that
\mathcal{A}
is positive definite, we have

\begin{split}C_{\mathcal{A}}\lVert\varepsilon(\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\omega})\rVert_{H}^{2}&\leq\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{A}\varepsilon(\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\omega})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\varepsilon(\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\omega})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\\
&=\int_{\Omega}\left(-B(\varphi,z)\varepsilon(\boldsymbol{\omega})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\varepsilon(\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\omega})+\xi\mathfrak{c}_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\varepsilon(\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\omega})+\zeta\mathfrak{c}_{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\varepsilon(\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\omega})\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\,.\end{split}

Recalling that
\mathcal{B}
is bounded and
\mathfrak{c}_{1},\mathfrak{c}_{2}
are uniformly bounded in

thanks to
Remark

4.1
, we estimate the right-hand side with the Hölder and the Young inequalities. We get

\begin{split}C_{\mathcal{A}}\lVert\varepsilon(\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\omega})\rVert_{H}^{2}&\leq\left(C\lVert\varepsilon(\boldsymbol{\omega})\rVert_{H}+\lVert\xi\rVert_{L^{3}}\lVert\mathfrak{c}_{1}\rVert_{L^{6}}+\lVert\zeta\rVert_{L^{3}}\lVert\mathfrak{c}_{2}\rVert_{L^{6}}\right)\lVert\varepsilon(\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\omega})\rVert_{H}\\
&\leq\delta\lVert\varepsilon(\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\omega})\rVert_{H}^{2}+C_{R,\delta}\left(\lVert\varepsilon(\boldsymbol{\omega})\rVert_{H}^{2}+\lVert\xi\rVert_{L^{3}}^{2}+\lVert\zeta\rVert_{L^{3}}^{2}\right),\end{split}

where
\delta
is a small positive constant yet to be determined. By means of the interpolation inequality in
Lemma

2.1
and again the Young inequality, we have

\begin{split}C_{\mathcal{A}}\lVert\varepsilon(\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\omega})\rVert_{H}^{2}\leq\delta\left(\lVert\varepsilon(\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\omega})\rVert_{H}^{2}+\lVert\nabla\xi\rVert_{H}^{2}+\lVert\nabla\zeta\rVert_{H}^{2}\right)\\
+C_{R,\delta}\left(\lVert\varepsilon(\boldsymbol{\omega})\rVert_{H}^{2}+\lVert\xi\rVert_{H}^{2}+\lVert\zeta\rVert_{H}^{2}\right).\end{split}

(4.9)

Recalling that

\lVert\varepsilon(\boldsymbol{\omega})\rVert_{H}^{2}\leq\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\varepsilon(\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\omega})\rVert_{H}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\,

because
\boldsymbol{\omega}(0)=0
,
summing equations (
4.8
) and (
4.9
), and fixing

small enough leads to

\begin{split}\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}t}&\left(\lVert\xi\rVert_{H}^{2}+\lVert\rho\rVert_{H}^{2}+\lVert\zeta\rVert_{H}^{2}\right)+\lVert\nabla\xi\rVert_{H}^{2}+\lVert\nabla\rho\rVert_{H}^{2}+\lVert\varepsilon(\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\omega})\rVert_{H}^{2}+\lVert\nabla\zeta\rVert_{H}^{2}\\
&\leq C_{R}\left(\lVert\xi\rVert_{H}^{2}+\lVert\rho\rVert_{H}^{2}+\lVert\zeta\rVert_{H}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\varepsilon(\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\omega})\rVert_{H}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\,+\lVert\boldsymbol{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}\right).\end{split}

Applying Gronwall inequality and then standard parabolic regularity estimates, we recover the a priori estimates we need to pass in the Galerkin discretization. This way, existence is proved as well as the estimate (
4.7
) in the statement. Uniqueness follows from the estimate we have just proved and the fact that the system is linear.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

For
\boldsymbol{\chi}\in\mathcal{U}_{R}
fixed, it is convenient to denote the solution to the linearized state system associated with a perturbation
\boldsymbol{h}\in\mathcal{U}
as
(\xi^{\boldsymbol{h}},\rho^{\boldsymbol{h}},\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\boldsymbol{h}},\zeta^{\boldsymbol{h}})
. From this result, it follows that the map

\mathcal{U}\subseteq L^{2}(Q)\times L^{2}(Q)\to\mathcal{V},\qquad\boldsymbol{h}\mapsto(\xi^{\boldsymbol{h}},\rho^{\boldsymbol{h}},\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\boldsymbol{h}},\zeta^{\boldsymbol{h}}),

is linear and continuous.

5
Differentiability of the control-to-state operator

In this section, we will prove the Fréchet differentiability of the control-to-state operator.

Theorem 5.1
.

The control-to-state operator
\mathcal{S}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{U}\to\mathcal{V}
is Fréchet differentiable in
\mathcal{U}_{R}
and the Fréchet derivative of
\mathcal{S}
in
\boldsymbol{\chi}\in\mathcal{U}_{R}
is given by

D\mathcal{S}(\boldsymbol{\chi})\boldsymbol{h}=(\xi^{\boldsymbol{h}},\rho^{\boldsymbol{h}},\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\boldsymbol{h}},\zeta^{\boldsymbol{h}})

for every
\boldsymbol{h}\in\mathcal{U}
.

Proof.

We consider a fixed and arbitrary
\boldsymbol{\chi}\in\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}
with
\mathcal{S}(\boldsymbol{\chi})=(\varphi,\sigma,\boldsymbol{u},z)
. Our goal is to prove that

\lim_{\lVert\boldsymbol{h}\rVert_{\mathcal{U}}\to 0}\frac{\lVert\mathcal{S}(\boldsymbol{\chi}+\boldsymbol{h})-\mathcal{S}(\boldsymbol{\chi})-(\xi^{\boldsymbol{h}},\rho^{\boldsymbol{h}},\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\boldsymbol{h}},\zeta^{\boldsymbol{h}})\rVert_{\mathcal{V}}}{\lVert\boldsymbol{h}\rVert_{\mathcal{U}}}=0,

(5.1)

from which the thesis follows.
We introduce the notation
\mathcal{S}(\boldsymbol{\chi}+\boldsymbol{h})=(\varphi^{\boldsymbol{h}},\sigma^{\boldsymbol{h}},\boldsymbol{u}^{\boldsymbol{h}},z^{\boldsymbol{h}})
and

\Phi^{\boldsymbol{h}}\coloneqq\varphi^{\boldsymbol{h}}-\varphi-\xi^{\boldsymbol{h}},\quad\lambda^{\boldsymbol{h}}\coloneqq\sigma^{\boldsymbol{h}}-\sigma-\rho^{\boldsymbol{h}},\quad\boldsymbol{w}^{\boldsymbol{h}}\coloneqq\boldsymbol{u}^{\boldsymbol{h}}-\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\boldsymbol{h}},\quad\mu^{\boldsymbol{h}}\coloneqq z^{\boldsymbol{h}}-z-\zeta^{\boldsymbol{h}}.

Since
\boldsymbol{\chi}
belongs to
\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}
which is in turn contained in the open set
\mathcal{U}_{R}
, there exists a constant
C_{\boldsymbol{\chi}}
such that, for every
\boldsymbol{h}\in\mathcal{U}_{R}
with
\lVert\boldsymbol{h}\rVert_{\mathcal{U}}\leq C_{\boldsymbol{\chi}}
, the control
\boldsymbol{\chi}+\boldsymbol{h}
still belongs to
\mathcal{U}_{R}
. Without loss of generality, since our aim is to pass to the limit as
\lVert\boldsymbol{h}\rVert_{\mathcal{U}}
goes to
0
, we will consider only
\boldsymbol{h}
with a small norm in this sense. We are going to prove that

\lVert(\Phi^{\boldsymbol{h}},\lambda^{\boldsymbol{h}},\boldsymbol{w}^{\boldsymbol{h}},\mu^{\boldsymbol{h}})\rVert_{\mathcal{V}}\leq C_{R}\lVert\boldsymbol{h}\rVert_{\mathcal{U}}^{\frac{5}{4}},

(5.2)

which yields to the limit (
5.1
). To do so, we consider the PDE system satisfied by
(\Phi^{\boldsymbol{h}},\lambda^{\boldsymbol{h}},\boldsymbol{w}^{\boldsymbol{h}},\mu^{\boldsymbol{h}})
which can be trivially obtained by
2.6
and
4.2

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\Phi^{\boldsymbol{h}}\eta\,\mathrm{d}x\,+\int_{\Omega}\nabla\Phi^{\boldsymbol{h}}\cdot\nabla\eta\,\mathrm{d}x\,=\int_{\Omega}[A_{1}+A_{2}]\eta\,\mathrm{d}x\,,

(5.3a)

(5.3b)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left[\mathcal{A}\varepsilon(\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{w}^{\boldsymbol{h}})+\mathfrak{C}_{1}\right]\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\varepsilon(\boldsymbol{\theta})\,\mathrm{d}x\,=0,

(5.3c)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\mu^{\boldsymbol{h}}\eta\,\mathrm{d}x\,+\nabla\mu^{\boldsymbol{h}}\cdot\nabla\eta\,\mathrm{d}x\,=\int_{\Omega}\left[D_{1}+D_{2}\right]\eta\,\mathrm{d}x\,,

(5.3d)

for every
\eta\in V
and
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in V_{0}
as well as the initial conditions

(5.4)

Here we have introduced the notation:

\displaystyle A_{1}=

\displaystyle U(\varphi^{\boldsymbol{h}},\sigma^{\boldsymbol{h}},z^{\boldsymbol{h}},\chi_{1})-U(\varphi,\sigma,z,\chi_{1})

\displaystyle-\left[U_{,\varphi}(\varphi,\sigma,z,\chi_{1})\xi^{\boldsymbol{h}}+U_{,\sigma}(\varphi,\sigma,z,\chi_{1})\rho^{\boldsymbol{h}}+U_{,z}(\varphi,\sigma,z,\chi_{1})\zeta^{\boldsymbol{h}}\right],

\displaystyle\,K(\varphi^{\boldsymbol{h}},\sigma^{\boldsymbol{h}},z^{\boldsymbol{h}})-K(\varphi,\sigma,z)-\left[K_{,\varphi}(\varphi,\sigma,z)\xi^{\boldsymbol{h}}+K_{,\sigma}(\varphi,\sigma,z)\rho^{\boldsymbol{h}}+K_{,z}(\varphi,\sigma,z)\zeta^{\boldsymbol{h}}\right],

\displaystyle B_{3}=

\displaystyle[S(\varphi^{\boldsymbol{h}},z^{\boldsymbol{h}})-S(\varphi,z)]h_{2},

Let’s take

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\chi}^{*}\in\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}
be an optimal control for the control problem with the associated state
\mathcal{S}(\boldsymbol{\chi}^{*})=(\varphi^{*},\sigma^{*},\boldsymbol{u}^{*},z^{*})
. Then, the following inequality is satisfied

\begin{split}\alpha_{1}&\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}(\varphi^{*}-\varphi_{Q})\xi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\alpha_{2}\int_{\Omega}(\varphi^{*}(T)-\varphi_{\Omega})\xi(T)\,\mathrm{d}x\,+\alpha_{3}\int_{\Omega}\xi(T)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\\
&+\alpha_{4}\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}(\sigma^{*}-\sigma_{Q})\rho\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\alpha_{5}\int_{\Omega}(\sigma^{*}(T)-\sigma_{\Omega})\rho(T)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\\
&+\alpha_{6}\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}\left[\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{\prime}(\varphi^{*})\varepsilon(\boldsymbol{u}^{*})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\varepsilon(\boldsymbol{u}^{*})\xi+\gamma(\varphi^{*})\varepsilon(\boldsymbol{u}^{*})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\varepsilon(\boldsymbol{\omega})\right]\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&+\alpha_{7}\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}(z^{*}-z_{Q})\zeta\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\alpha_{8}\int_{\Omega}z^{*}(T)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\\

for every

,
where
(\xi,\rho,\boldsymbol{\omega},\zeta)
is the unique solution to the linearized system in
\boldsymbol{\chi}^{*}
for

.

Proof.

First of all, we recall that the cost functional
\mathcal{J}
is well-defined over the space

\Big[C^{0}([0,T];H)\times C^{0}([0,T];H)\times L^{2}(0,T;V)\times C^{0}([0,T];H)\Big]\\
\times\Big[L^{2}(0,T;H)\times L^{2}(0,T;H)\Big],

Moreover, from

, the solution operator
\mathcal{S}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{U}\to\mathcal{V}
is Fréchet differentiable in
\mathcal{U}_{R}
. Since, from standard embedding results,
\mathcal{V}
is continuously embedded in

\mathcal{S}
is as well Fréchet differentiable in
\mathcal{U}_{R}
if it is seen as a functional between
\mathcal{U}
and this larger space. Thus, the reduced cost functional
\mathrm{J}
is Fréchet differentiable in
\mathcal{U}_{R}
and, by the chain rule,

\begin{split}D\mathrm{J}(\boldsymbol{\chi}^{*})&[\boldsymbol{\chi}-\boldsymbol{\chi}^{*}]=\alpha_{1}\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}(\varphi^{*}-\varphi_{Q})\xi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\alpha_{2}\int_{\Omega}(\varphi^{*}(T)-\varphi_{\Omega})\xi(T)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\\

where
\boldsymbol{\chi}^{*}
a the optimal control,
\boldsymbol{\chi}
is any admissible control, and
(\xi,\rho,\boldsymbol{\omega},\zeta)
is the solution to the system linearized in
\boldsymbol{\chi}^{*}
with
\boldsymbol{h}=\boldsymbol{\chi}-\boldsymbol{\chi}^{*}
.
From the optimality of
\boldsymbol{\chi}^{*}
and the convexity of

\zeta
. Some terms cancel out and we have:

\begin{split}\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}t}&\left[\int_{\Omega}\left(\xi\mathrm{q}+\rho\mathrm{r}+\mathcal{A}\varepsilon(\boldsymbol{\omega})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\varepsilon(\mathrm{\mathbf{v}})+\zeta\mathrm{s}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\right]\\
&=\int_{\Omega}\left(a_{4}h_{1}\mathrm{q}+b_{4}h_{2}\mathrm{r}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\,-\alpha_{1}\int_{\Omega}(\varphi^{*}-\varphi_{Q})\xi\,\mathrm{d}x\,-\alpha_{4}\int_{\Omega}(\sigma^{*}-\sigma_{Q})\rho\,\mathrm{d}x\,\\
&\quad-\alpha_{6}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{\prime}(\varphi^{*})\varepsilon(\boldsymbol{u}^{*})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\varepsilon(\boldsymbol{u}^{*})\xi+\gamma(\varphi^{*})\varepsilon(\boldsymbol{u}^{*})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\varepsilon(\boldsymbol{\omega})\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\,-\alpha_{7}\int_{\Omega}(z^{*}-z_{Q})\zeta\,\mathrm{d}x\,.\end{split}

Integrating in time over the interval
(0,T)
, exploiting the initial and final conditions, and moving some terms to the left-hand side, we finally have

\begin{split}\bigg[&\alpha_{2}\int_{\Omega}(\varphi^{*}(T)-\varphi_{\Omega})\xi(T)\,\mathrm{d}x\,+\alpha_{3}\int_{\Omega}\xi(T)\,\mathrm{d}x\,+\alpha_{5}\int_{\Omega}(\sigma^{*}(T)-\sigma_{\Omega})\rho(T)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\\
&\quad+\alpha_{8}\int_{\Omega}\zeta(T)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\bigg]+\bigg[\alpha_{1}\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}(\varphi^{*}-\varphi_{Q})\xi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\alpha_{4}\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}(\sigma^{*}-\sigma_{Q})\rho\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\quad+\alpha_{6}\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{\prime}(\varphi^{*})\varepsilon(\boldsymbol{u}^{*})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\varepsilon(\boldsymbol{u}^{*})\xi+\gamma(\varphi^{*})\varepsilon(\boldsymbol{u}^{*})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\varepsilon(\boldsymbol{\omega})\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\quad+\alpha_{7}\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}(z^{*}-z_{Q})\zeta\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg]=\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}(a_{4}h_{1}\mathrm{q}+b_{4}h_{2}\mathrm{r})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

Combining this equality with the inequality stated in
5.2
, we obtain the thesis.
∎

Protocol for in vitro phagocytosis assay for primary mouse microglia and human embryonic stem cell-derived microglia
Authors: Beika Zhu, Alicia L. Thurber, Xianhua Piao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
SummaryMicroglia, the resident immune cells of the brain, maintain brain health by clearing detrimental debris, including amyloid-β (Aβ). Here, we present a protocol for assessing Aβ uptake by primary mouse microglia and human embryonic stem cell-derived microglia using an in vitro phagocytosis assay. We describe procedures for pHrodo-labeled oligomeric Aβ treatment and real-time signal detection using the Incucyte SX5 system. We also detail steps for microglia culture, Aβ labeling, and quantification of phagocytosis over time.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Zhu et al.1

Before you begin

This protocol describes the in vitro assessment of phagocytic activity in primary mouse microglia and human embryonic stem cell (hESC)-derived microglia using pHrodo-labeled oligomeric amyloid-β (oAβ). The protocol is optimized for use with the Incucyte SX5 Live-Cell Analysis System to enable time-resolved, quantitative imaging of Aβ phagocytosis in live cells. Although this protocol was developed specifically for microglia, it can be adapted for other phagocytic cell types and pH-sensitive cargo, including alternative substrates such as zymosan particles or apoptotic cells.

Innovation

This protocol provides a complete and reproducible workflow for quantifying Aβ in two microglial models: primary mouse microglia and hESC-derived microglia. The primary innovation is the detailed application of the Incucyte SX5 Live-Cell Analysis system for real-time, automated quantification of oAβ uptake over a 24-hour period. This approach offers temporally dynamic analysis of phagocytic activity, as opposed to a “snapshot” of activity that traditional endpoint assays yield. To broaden the protocol’s accessibility, we also detail validated alternative methods for quantification, including fluorescence plate readers and flow cytometry, to ensure this assay can be adopted by laboratories with differing equipment availabilities. This integrated, multi-platform guide serves as a valuable resource for researchers investigating microglial clearance mechanisms in the context of neurological disorders.

Sodium Selenite stock solution (100 μg/mL, 1000x): Dissolve 1 mg of sodium selenite in 10 mL of sterile water. Filter sterile the solution using a 0.22 μm syringe filter. Aliquot and store at −20°C.

TGF-beta stock solution: Dissolve 10 μg of TGF-beta in 100 μL of sterile 1x phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) to achieve 100 μg/mL (50,000x). Aliquot and store at −80°C. Right before making MCM medium, further dilute 1 μL of 50,000x TGF-beta stock solution to 49 μL of 1xPBS to make the 1000x stock solution.

IL-34 stock solution (100 μg/mL, 1000x): Dissolve 10 μg of IL-34 in 100 μL sterile 1xPBS. Aliquot and store at −80°C.

Cholesterol stock solution (1.5 mg/mL, 1000x): Dissolve 15 mg of cholesterol powder in 10 mL of 100% ethanol. Warm the solution in a 70°C–80°C water bath (alternate warming methods also acceptable) and mix every 10–15 minutes until the cholesterol is completely dissolved. While the Incucyte SX5 system is ideal for automated, real-time imaging, phagocytosis uptake of pHrodo-labeled oAβ can be reliably quantified using other common laboratory instruments. The following methods provide robust alternatives for kinetic or endpoint analysis.5.Quantification using a fluorescence plate reader.A plate reader can measure the total fluorescence intensity from each well, which directly correlates with the amount of internalized Aβ.a.After adding pHrodo-labeled oAβ, transfer the plate to a fluorescence plate reader.b.Configure the reader to measure fluorescence from the bottom of the plate, with excitation at ∼560 nm and emission at ∼585 nm.c.Acquire the Relative Fluorescence Units (RFU) for all wells and promptly return the plates to the 37°C, 5% CO2 cell culture incubator.d.At each designated time point (e.g., 2, 4, 8, 12, 16 and 24 hours), transfer the plate to the fluorescence plate reader for recording.e.For analysis, subtract the RFU value of a non-pHrodo-labeled oAB treatment group from all other wells, to normalize RFU values accounting for background signal. Then, plot the mean RFU for each condition over time.6.Endpoint quantification using flow cytometry.Flow cytometry can quantify the percentage of phagocytic cells at specific time points. Here, we describe the procedures after 24-hour pHrodo-labeled oAβ treatment.a.At the end of 24-hour treatment, stop phagocytosis by removing the cell culture medium and washing cells twice with 100 μL per well of ice-cold, sterile 1xPBS.b.Aspirate the last 1xPBS wash and add 100 μL of Accutase to each well to detach the cells. Incubate at 37°C for 8 minutes.c.Pool the cell suspension together in a 1.5 mL tube.d.Centrifuge the cell suspension at 200 × g for 3 minutes.e.Discard the supernatant and resuspend the cell pellet in 1 mL of FACS buffer (2% bovine serum albumin (BSA) in 1xPBS).f.Centrifuge the cell suspension at 200 × g for 3 minutes.g.To prevent non-specific antibody binding, prepare a blocking solution by diluting anti-mouse CD16/32 antibody in FACS buffer at a 1:200 ratio.h.Resuspend the cell pellet in 100 μL of blocking solution for at least 20 minutes on ice and protect from light.i.Centrifuge the cell suspension at 200 × g for 3 minutes.j.Prepare the staining solution by diluting DRAQ7 (for gating live/dead cells) at a 1:200 dilution and CD11b antibody (for gating microglia) at a 1:50 dilution in FACS buffer.k.Resuspend the cell pellet in 100 μL of staining solution for at least 20 minutes on ice and protected from light.l.Centrifuge the cell suspension at 200 × g for 3 minutes.m.Resuspend the cell pellet in 500 μL of FACS buffer for flow cytometry analysis.n.For analysis, gate the live (DRAQ7 negative), single-cell population. Then, quantify the percentage of pHrodo-red and CD11b positive cells over the total CD11b positive population.Note: For accurate gating and analysis, it is necessary to include no treatment, unstained, and single-stain controls. No treatment control: A sample of cells not treated with pHrodo-labeled oAβ and stained with both CD11b and DRAQ7. Single-stained controls: A sample of cells treated with pHrodo-labeled oAβ and only stained with DRAQ7.

Expected outcomes

This protocol enables sensitive, time-resolved quantification of microglial phagocytic activity in vitro using pHrodo-labeled oAβ. We expect to observe a gradual increase in red fluorescence over the 24-hour imaging period, reflecting Aβ uptake and lysosomal acidification as pHrodo becomes fluorescent in the acidic environment of the phagolysosome.

In phase-contrast images, healthy microglia appear as round to ramified cells, with cell morphology varying depending on their source and activation status. Quantitative output from the Incucyte software includes the total integrated intensity of orange objects per well, expressed in Orange Calibrated Units (OCUs). For each biological replicates, users should average the data obtained from technical replicate wells and plot the resulting mean values. These data can be visualized as a time course or summarized as area under the curve (AUC) to compare phagocytic capacity across experimental conditions. For primary mouse microglia and hESC-derived microglia, phagocytic activity typically yields an increase in fluorescence signal (OCU) ranging from 50,000–150,000 per well over a 24-hour period (Figure 6E).

Pharmacological inhibition or genetic perturbations that impair phagocytic function are expected to produce clear reductions in OCU values over time, demonstrating the assay’s sensitivity and dynamic range.

Limitations

This assay uses pHrodo fluorescence to report lysosomal acidification following Aβ uptake, providing a sensitive and time-resolved readout of phagocytic activity. However, it does not distinguish between different stages of the endocytic and lysosomal trafficking pathway. A reduction in fluorescence signal may indicate impaired uptake, delayed endosome maturation, or defective lysosome fusion and acidification, but these possibilities cannot be distinguished using this assay alone. Therefore, interpretation of reduced signal should be approached cautiously and supported by complementary assays, such as immunostaining for endolysosomal compartment-specific markers (e.g., Rab5, Rab7, or LAMP1) to help identify the specific stage at which trafficking or degradation may be disrupted.

The Incucyte SX5 system is optimized for adherent cells and may not provide accurate quantification in wells where microglia fail to adhere evenly or display variable morphology. High-density cultures may lead to saturation of fluorescence signal, while low-density cultures can introduce noise due to small sample size.

Troubleshooting

Problem 1

Weak or no red fluorescence after pHrodo-labeled oAβ treatment (refer to step 2).

Potential solution

This may result from inefficient dye conjugation, photobleaching, or low cellular activity. Ensure that pHrodo dye is freshly prepared and conjugated to Aβ in sodium bicarbonate buffer at pH ∼8.3. Minimize light exposure during preparation and incubation to prevent photobleaching. Verify that microglia are healthy, well-adhered, and metabolically active. Cells should be allowed to recover for at least 16–24 hours post-seeding before treatment to ensure robust phagocytic activity.

Problem 2

High background fluorescence in pHrodo-labeled oAβ treated wells (refer to step 2).

Potential solution

Elevated background signal in treated wells may occur due to residual unbound pHrodo dye, improper conjugation, or fluorescent contamination from media components. To minimize background, ensure that pHrodo dye is efficiently conjugated to Aβ and that unbound dye is thoroughly removed using a 10 kDa centrifugal filter. Use phenol red-free medium during imaging to reduce media autofluorescence. Avoid prolonged exposure to light, which may cause nonspecific signal or increase background. Always include a negative control to identify baseline autofluorescence (Methods video S2).

Problem 3

Microglia fail to adhere or detach before imaging (refer to step 10 of primary microglia isolation from mixed glial culture and step 16 of generate hESC-derived microglia).

Potential solution

Poor adherence may be due to insufficient substrate coating, harsh handling, or inadequate recovery time. Use black-walled, clear-bottom 96-well plates coated with 0.01% poly-L-lysine (PLL) for at least 1 hour at 20°C–25°C. Handle cells gently during media changes and allow at least 16 hours of recovery after seeding to ensure stable attachment prior to treatment and imaging.

Problem 4

Uneven fluorescence across the plate (refer to step 4).

Potential solution

This issue may be caused by edge effects, uneven cell distribution, or misalignment of the plate in the imaging chamber. Avoid using outer wells or fill them with sterile water or medium to minimize evaporation. Mix the cell suspension and treatment solution gently and thoroughly to ensure even distribution. Confirm that the plate is level and properly positioned within the Incucyte SX5 system.

Problem 5

Signal saturation or plateau early in the time course (refer to step 3).

Potential solution

High pHrodo-labeled oAβ concentrations or suboptimal acquisition settings can lead to signal saturation. If fluorescence intensity reaches the upper detection limit early, reduce the concentration of pHrodo-labeled oAβ to 0.5 μM. Adjust acquisition settings such as exposure time and gain to fit your experimental conditions. Consider plotting data on a logarithmic scale to improve visualization of subtle differences in signal dynamics.

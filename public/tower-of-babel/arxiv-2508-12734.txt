Critical fluctuations of last passage percolation with thick boundaries
Elnur Emrah, Patrik L. Ferrari, Min Liu

Abstract
We consider the exponential last passage percolation (LPP) with thick two-sided boundary that consists of a few inhomogeneous columns and rows. Ben Arous and Corwin previously studied the limit fluctuations in this model except in a critical regime, for which they predicted that the limit distribution exists and depends only on the most dominant columns and rows. In this article, we prove their conjecture and identify the limit distribution explicitly in terms of a Fredholm determinant of a 2 × 2 2\times 2 matrix kernel. This result leads in particular to an explicit variational formula for the one-point marginal of the KPZ fixed point for a new class of initial conditions. Our limit distribution is also a novel generalization of and provides a new numerically efficient representation for the Baik–Rains distribution.

Introduction

1.1
Exponential LPP with thick two-sided boundary

Our first motivation in this article is to address a conjecture of Ben Arous and Corwin concerning the limit fluctuations of the exponential last passage percolation (LPP) with thick two-sided boundary conditions (see (51) of
[
16
]
). To describe the model, we consider two vectors
\bm{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\dotsc,\alpha_{\ell})\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
and
\bm{\beta}=(\beta_{1},\dotsc,\beta_{k})\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
with components of the form

\displaystyle\alpha_{i}=\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}+\frac{x_{i}}{(16N)^{1/3}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\beta_{j}=\widetilde{\beta}_{j}+\frac{y_{j}}{(16N)^{1/3}},

(1.1)

where
k,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,
\widetilde{\alpha}_{i},\widetilde{\beta}_{j}>-1/2
,
x_{i},y_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
are fixed, and the parameter
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
is large enough so that
\alpha_{i},\beta_{j}>-1/2
as well. Let
(\omega_{i,j})
be a family of independent random weights indexed by the lattice
\{-\ell+1,-\ell+2,\dotsc\}\times\{-k+1,-k+2,\dotsc\}
with

\omega_{i,j}\sim\begin{cases}{\rm Exp}(1)&\text{if }i>0\text{ and }j>0,\\
{\rm Exp}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha_{i+\ell}\right)&\text{if }i\leq 0\text{ and }j>0,\\
{\rm Exp}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\beta_{j+k}\right)&\text{if }i>0\text{ and }j\leq 0,\\
0&\text{if }i\leq 0\text{ and }j\leq 0.\end{cases}

(1.2)

In particular, the weights on the
\ell\times k
corner block
\{-\ell+1,\dotsc,0\}\times\{-k+1,\dotsc,0\}
are zero. We think of the first integer quadrant
\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{2}
as the
bulk
, and the first
k
rows and the first
\ell
columns of the above lattice as the
thick boundary
where the exponential rates can differ from the bulk rate
1
, see Figure
1
.
We then define, for each
(i,j),(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
with
m\geq i>-\ell
and
n\geq j>-k
, the associated last passage time by

L^{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}_{(i,j)\to(m,n)}:=\max_{\pi:(i,j)\to(m,n)}\sum_{(r,s)\in\pi}\omega_{r,s},

(1.3)

where the maximum runs over all up-right paths from
(i,j)
to
(m,n)
. In the case
(i,j)=(-k+1,-\ell+1)
, we abbreviate the left-hand side of (
1.3
) as
L^{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}_{m,n}
.

1.2
Background: Prähofer–Spohn conjecture

To explain the origin of the preceding model, we revisit the Prähofer–Spohn conjecture
[
83
]
that was eventually settled in
[
16
]
. Let us consider for now the basic case of the model with
k=\ell=1
and
x_{1}=y_{1}=0
. We also assume that
\alpha=\alpha_{1}\leq 1/2
and
\beta=\beta_{1}\leq 1/2
. Then the last passage time
L_{m,n}^{\alpha,\beta}
is closely related in distribution to the current in the totally asymmetric simple exclusion process (TASEP) with two-sided Bernoulli initial conditions
[
83
,
13
,
16
]
. More specifically, at time zero, each site
i\in\mathbb{Z}
is occupied independently with probability
\rho^{-}=1/2+\alpha
if
i\leq 0
and with probability
\rho^{+}=1/2-\beta
if
i>0
. This implies that the initial particle density is
\rho^{-}
to the left of the origin and
\rho^{+}
to the right. When
\rho^{-}=\rho^{+}=\rho
, one obtains the stationary initial condition for the TASEP. In the article
[
83
]
, Prähofer and Spohn predicted the limiting current fluctuations for each choice of
\rho^{-}
and
\rho^{+}
, relying in part on the analogous results of Baik and Rains
[
12
]
for the polynuclear growth model (PNG), and also on a result of Johansson
[
64
]
for the step initial condition where
\rho^{-}=1
and
\rho^{+}=0
. Translated to the LPP language, the Prähofer–Spohn conjecture asserts the limit distribution of suitably rescaled
L_{m,n}^{\alpha,\beta}
as the point
(m,n)
tends to infinity in a fixed direction
r
, which can be defined as the limit of the ratio
m/n
. The limit exhibited an interesting phase diagram with respect to the parameters
\rho^{+}
,
\rho^{-}
and
r
, and it became a significant problem to verify this picture rigorously due to the role of the TASEP as a prototypical nonreversible interacting particle system.

The proof of the Prähofer–Spohn conjecture spanned several works. In the stationary case and for any direction
r\neq r_{c}=\rho^{-2}(1-\rho)^{2}
, it was already known that the fluctuations are governed by the central limit theorem
[
48
]
. In this case, one side of the boundary completely determine the fluctuation behavior. For
\rho^{-}=1
and
\rho^{+}=0
, there is a rarefaction fan and, for any
r
, the limit is the Tracy–Widom GUE distribution
[
98
]
as proved in
[
64
]
. For the stationary case, in the critical direction
r_{c}
, both the bulk and the two boundaries have non-trivial contributions in the limit distribution, which is the Baik–Rains distribution
[
12
]
as shown in
[
51
]
. In a subsequent work
[
9
]
, Baik, Ben Arous and Péché resolved the conjecture for
\rho^{+}=0
and arbitrary
\rho^{-}
; see also
[
76
]
. Using the results of
[
9
]
as inputs, Ben Arous and Corwin completed the proof of the conjecture in
[
16
]
.

1.3
A conjecture of Ben Arous and Corwin

As the authors noted, the probabilistic approach in
[
16
]
works more generally to also identify the limit fluctuations for the exponential LPP with thick boundaries. The basic idea is that, through definitions (
1.2
) and (
1.3
), one can write

\displaystyle L_{m,n}^{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}=\max\{L_{(-\ell+1,0)\to(m,n)}^{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}},L_{(0,-k+1)\to(m,n)}^{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}\}

(1.4)

because the weights are zero on the
\ell\times k
corner block. The two last passage times on the right-hand side depend only on the one side of the thick boundary (either the purple or the red part in Figure
1
). Therefore, the results of
[
9
]
immediately yield the limit distribution for each term of the maximum in (
1.4
). From this knowledge, the article
[
16
]
determined the limit distribution of
L_{m,n}^{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}
in all regimes except the critical one, which generalizes taking
r=r_{c}
in the stationary case where
\rho^{\pm}=\rho
and
k=\ell=1
. When the critical direction is along the diagonal (
r_{c}=1
), the critical regime is precisely when
\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}=\widetilde{\beta}_{j}=0
for some
i
and
j
in (
1.1
). The definition is the same for other choices of
r_{c}
except that
1/2
in the second and third lines of (
1.2
) need to be modified as
1/(1+\sqrt{r_{c}})
and
\sqrt{r_{c}}/(1+\sqrt{r_{c}})
, respectively. The approach of
[
16
]
breaks down for this regime because then neither term in the maximum (
1.4
) dominates the other, and these terms are significantly correlated. For
k=\ell=1
, the critical regime was handled in
[
51
]
through a
shift argument
(see also
[
60
]
), which has since become a standard technique to establish convergence to the Baik–Rains distribution. However, the shift argument does not generalize to the thick boundary setting.

It was conjectured in (51) of
[
16
]
that, with appropriate rescaling,
L_{m,n}^{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}
converges in distribution in the critical regime, and the limit distribution depends only on those
x_{i}
and
y_{j}
parameters for which
\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}=\widetilde{\beta}_{j}=0
. One of our main result verifies the preceding conjecture for
r_{c}=1
with an explicit identification of the limit, see Theorem
2.4
. The choice of the diagonal direction is only for notational simplicity; a version of our result for any other direction can be formulated and proved with minor modifications.

The proof of Theorem
2.4
begins with a derivation of the distribution of
L_{m,n}^{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}
for arbitrary vectors
\bm{\alpha}
and
\bm{\beta}
with components
\alpha_{i},\beta_{j}>-1/2
but not necessarily of the form (
1.1
). This intermediate result, which is recorded as Theorem
2.1
, is also new and might be of independent interest. In its proof, we first assume that
\alpha_{i},\beta_{j}>0
for all
i,j
. Then we can compute the joint distribution of the two terms of the maximum in (
1.4
) through the associated Schur process
[
80
]
and a limit transition from the geometric to the exponential LPP. Doing so yields the distribution of
L_{m,n}^{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}
as a Fredholm determinant of an explicit
2\times 2
kernel with entries expressed as contour integrals. Through analytic continuation of these integrals, we then show that the distributional formula remains valid even without the positivity restriction on the parameters
\alpha_{i}
and
\beta_{j}
. Such an extension is essential for our treatment of the critical regime where
\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}=\widetilde{\beta}_{j}=0
and so
\alpha_{i}
and
\beta_{j}
can be nonpositive. We then apply Theorem
2.1
choosing
\bm{\alpha}
and
\bm{\beta}
as in (
1.1
) and
(m,n)=(N,N)+\tau(2N)^{2/3}(-1,1)
where
\tau
is a real parameter. For the asymptotics as
N\to\infty
, we perform a saddle-point, steep-descent analysis along the lines of
[
10
]
, which leads to Theorem
2.4
.

1.4
A new representation of the Baik–Rains distribution

The novel limit distribution that appears in Theorem
2.4
is given by a Fredholm determinant of a
2\times 2
limit kernel, see Definition
2.3
. To connect it to the Baik–Rains distribution, recall that the case
k=\ell=1
and
\alpha=\beta=0
of our setting corresponds to the stationary TASEP with density
\rho=1/2
. It was proved in
[
51
]
that the last passage time
L_{m,n}^{0,0}
under the same scaling as in Theorem
2.4
converges to the Baik–Rains distribution (with parameter
\tau
). Therefore, our limit distribution recovers the Baik–Rains distribution in the above special case. However, it is not straightforward to see this directly because our representation is significantly far from the original definition of the Baik–Rains distribution
[
12
]
, which is in terms of a Painlevé transcendent. In Section
5
, we provide a direct proof that the two definitions are indeed the same.

In Corollary
2.5
, we rewrite our limit kernel for the Baik–Rains distribution in terms of the Airy functions and the Airy kernel. This yields a new Fredholm determinant formula for the Baik–Rains distribution that is simpler than the earlier such representation obtained in
[
51
]
, which we recall in Appendix
A
. Our representation is particularly well-suited for efficient numerical computation via Bornemann’s method
[
24
]
, which provides an alternative to the earlier evaluations of the Baik–Rains distribution via Painlevé transcendents
[
85
,
82
]
. In Figure
2
ahead, we employ both methods to produce matching plots of the Baik–Rains distribution for two choices of the
\tau
parameter.

1.5
A generalization of the Baik–Rains distribution

We next return to the setting of thick boundary, and assume that
\alpha_{i}=\beta_{j}=0
for all
i,j
. For this case, we show in Theorem
2.6
that our limit distribution also admits an explicit variational description involving the Airy
2
process and
k+\ell
independent Brownian motions. For
k=\ell=1
, our result specializes to the variational formula in Corollary 2.4 of
[
33
]
for the Baik–Rains distribution.

Variational formulas are also available for other initial conditions, see
[
66
,
88
,
89
,
11
,
35
,
33
,
50
]
. However, for the generic case, the kernel is expressed in terms of hitting time of the hypograph of the initial condition
[
75
]
and, thus, is not fully explicit. Indeed, the cases of variational formulas with explicit kernels essentially reduce to the initial conditions scaling to wedge, flat, Brownian initial conditions and their mixtures, for which the joint distributions are also known
[
84
,
65
,
94
,
28
,
29
,
27
,
10
]
. With our result, we obtain a new family of initial conditions for which the one-point marginal of the KPZ fixed point has explicit determinantal formulas.

In the proof of Theorem
2.6
, a main step is to control the exit point where the geodesic from the origin leaves the thick boundary. We achieve this with an exponential tail bound in Proposition
6.1
by adapting the approach in
[
20
]
for the case
k=\ell=1
.

Our final result concerns the following variation of the model in (
1.2
). First, setting
x_{1}=y_{1}=0
and
\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}=\widetilde{\beta}_{j}=0
for all
i,j
, we assume that
x_{i}+y_{j}>0
for all
i,j
except when
i=j=1
. We then introduce independent weights
(\widehat{\omega}_{i,j})
such that

\displaystyle\widehat{\omega}_{i,j}\sim\begin{cases}{\rm Exp}(1),&\text{if }i>0\text{ and }j>0,\\
{\rm Exp}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha_{i+\ell}\right),&\text{if }i\leq 0\text{ and }j>0,\\
{\rm Exp}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\beta_{j+k}\right),&\text{if }i>0\text{ and }j\leq 0,\\
{\rm Exp}\left(\alpha_{i+\ell}+\beta_{j+k}\right)&\text{if }i\leq 0\text{ and }j\leq 0\text{ and }(i,j)\neq(-\ell+1,-k+1),\\
0,&\text{if }i=-\ell+1\text{ and }j=-k+1.\end{cases}

(1.5)

In other words, we replace the zero weights on the
\ell\times k
corner block (shown in black in Figure
1
) except the one at the corner point
(-\ell+1,-k+1)
with independent rate
\alpha_{i+\ell}+\beta_{j+k}=(16N)^{-1/3}(x_{i+\ell}+y_{j+k})>0
exponentials. We define the last passage time
\widehat{L}_{m,n}^{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}
from the origin as in (
1.3
) using the
\widehat{\omega}
weights in place of
\omega
.

One can view (
1.5
) as an intermediate model between (
1.2
) and the LPP model introduced by Borodin and Péché
[
31
]
where the weight at
(-\ell+1,-k+1)
is also an independent exponential with rate
\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}=(16N)^{-1/3}(x_{1}+y_{1})
, assuming further that
x_{1}+y_{1}>0
. Alternatively, one can think of (
1.5
) as the basic case
k=\ell=1
of (
1.2
) with a few inhomogeneous columns and rows.

Our interest in (
1.5
) stems from the fact that the process
\widehat{L}^{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}
has stationary increments
[
43
]
and, thus, generalizes the stationary LPP process
L^{0,0}
. Given that the Baik–Rains distribution governs the limit fluctuations of
L^{0,0}
along the critical direction, it is natural to wonder what form the preceding statement takes for
\widehat{L}^{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}
. We address this question in Proposition
2.8
and find that the associated limit distribution is a variation of the distribution in Definition
2.3
. We will only briefly indicate how to obtain this result since its proof is very similar to that of Theorem
2.4
.

1.6
Further related literature

Under mild assumptions, LPP with general IID weights is expected to belong to the Kardar–Parisi–Zhang (KPZ) universality class
[
69
]
, which has been a subject of intense research in the past several decades. For a detailed introduction, we refer to the review articles and lecture notes
[
52
,
34
,
91
,
30
,
87
,
49
,
97
,
102
]
. Briefly, the KPZ class is a conjectural collection of stochastic models with similar large-scale fluctuations, and is believed to include various growth models, interacting particle systems and random matrix ensembles. In the appropriate scaling limit, the fluctuations of any KPZ class model are supposedly described by each of the two closely related universal objects, the KPZ fixed point and the directed landscape. The KPZ fixed point is a Markov process on the space of upper-semicontinuous functions on
\mathbb{R}
, and was originally constructed in
[
75
]
as a limit of the TASEP. The directed landscape is a random directed metric on
\mathbb{R}^{4}
and was first obtained in
[
36
]
as the limit of the Brownian LPP. The relation between the two is that the KPZ fixed point can be represented through a variational formula in terms of its initial condition and the directed landscape
[
77
]
. For several integrable and adjacent models, the convergence to the directed landscape and the KPZ fixed point has been established
[
37
,
4
,
90
,
100
,
99
]
, giving rigorous evidence for the universality of these limits. Integrability here means the possibility of deriving exact distributional formulas that can be studied through asymptotic techniques to access limit statistics. For the exponential LPP and TASEP in particular, a source of integrability is the connection to the Schur measures and processes
[
64
,
79
,
80
]
.

The Baik–Rains distribution is believed to describe the limit fluctuations along the critical direction in any KPZ model under stationary initial conditions. A distributional convergence statement to this effect was first proved for the PNG
[
12
]
where the Baik–Rains distribution was introduced. For the TASEP and exponential LPP, the analogous result was obtained in
[
51
]
and subsequently extended to multi-point convergence in
[
10
]
. The latter type of result was then derived for the Brownian LPP in
[
53
]
. By now, convergence to the Baik–Rains distribution has also been established in several positive-temperature models including the KPZ equation
[
25
]
, the asymmetric simple exclusion process (ASEP) and the stochastic six vertex model
[
3
]
,
q
-TASEP
[
63
]
, O’Connell–Yor polymer
[
62
]
, and the stochastic higher spin six vertex model
[
59
]
. The half-space analogue of the Baik–Rains distribution and the associated multi-point process were introduced in the articles
[
18
]
and
[
19
]
, respectively. In the setting of stationary TASEP on a ring, a periodic version of the Baik–Rains distribution was found by
[
74
]
, see also
[
86
]
. More recently, the two-time extension of the Baik–Rains distribution was described in
[
92
]
. We also mention the very recent work
[
101
]
that obtained the two-parameter counterpart of the half-space Baik–Rains distribution under the critical scaling limit of the stationary half-space geometric LPP.

Inhomogeneous extensions of integrable KPZ models have also attracted significant research attention lately, thanks in part to featuring rich limit statistics and interesting phase transitions, a prominent example being the Baik–Ben Arous–Péché (BBP) transition
[

]
. In the exponential LPP, varying the exponential rates in the form

at each site
(i,j)
retains integrability. Consequently, many aspects of the exponential LPP with such inhomogeneous rates have been studied extensively. A nonexhaustive list of prior works covers exact distributional identities

,

, hydrodynamics and limit shape

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

]
and infinite geodesics
[

]
. See also the further related works on other integrable inhomogeneous LPP

and the generalizations of the TASEP with particlewise, spatial or temporal inhomogeneity

1.7
Notation

For
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, we write
\llbracket k\rrbracket:=\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
. Bold font denotes vectors. Given two vectors
\bm{\alpha}:=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{\ell})\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
and
\bm{\beta}:=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{k})\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
, we denote their concatenation by
\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}
,
that is,

\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}:=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{\ell},\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{k}).

We also denote the indices of the zero components of
\bm{\alpha}
by
\mathcal{I}(\bm{\alpha}):=\{i\mid\alpha_{i}=0\}

and for
i\in\llbracket\ell\rrbracket
, we define
\bm{\alpha}_{[i:\ell]}:=\{\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i+1},\ldots,\alpha_{\ell}\}.

For a meromorphic function
f
and a finite subset
I
of the complex plane, we will write

\Gamma_{I}
for any simple anticlockwise oriented contour that encloses
I
but not any of the poles of
f
in the complement of
I
. Following the convention in
[
19
]
, we also introduce notation for the standard Airy contours: For subsets
I,J\subset\mathbb{C}
, we denote by
{\tiny I}\,\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\includegraphics[scale]{leftcontour}}\,{\tiny J}
(resp.
{\tiny I}\,\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\includegraphics[scale]{rightcontour}}\,{\tiny J}
) an upward-oriented contour from
e^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}/3}\infty
(resp.
e^{-\pi\mathrm{i}/3}\infty
) to
e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}/3}\infty
(resp.
e^{\pi\mathrm{i}/3}\infty
) such that all points in
I
are to its left and all points in
J
are to its right.
Also, we denote by
B_{r}
the anticlockwise-oriented circle centered at the origin and of radius
r>0
.

The Fredholm determinant of a
2\times 2
matrix kernel
K
can be defined by its Fredholm series expansion as

\det\left(\mathbbm{1}-P_{s}KP_{s}\right)_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{\ell!}\sum_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{\ell}=1}^{2}\int_{s}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}x_{1}\cdots\int_{s}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}x_{\ell}\det_{1\leq i,j\leq\ell}[K_{k_{i}k_{j}}(x_{i},x_{j})].

2
Main results

In this section, we state our main results.

2.1
One-point distribution

Our first result gives the distribution of the last passage time
L^{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}_{m,n}
for any
\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}>-1/2
componentwise. In its statement, we use the function
E=E_{m,n}
defined by

E_{m,n}(x,w;y,v):=\left(\frac{1/2-w}{1/2-v}\right)^{m}\left(\frac{1/2+v}{1/2+w}\right)^{n}e^{wx-vy}\quad\text{ for }x,w,y,v\in\mathbb{C}.

(2.1)

Theorem 2.1

(Distribution of Exponential LPP)
.

For any
\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}>-\dfrac{1}{2}
and
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,

\mathbb{P}\left(L^{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}_{m,n}\leq s\right)=\det\left(\mathbbm{1}-P_{s}K_{m,n}^{\exp}P_{s}\right)_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})},

(2.2)

where
P_{s}
is the projection operator onto
(s,\infty)
and

K_{m,n}^{\exp}
is a
2\times 2
matrix kernel with entries:

\displaystyle K_{m,n,11}^{\exp}(x,y)

\displaystyle:=\frac{-1}{(2\pi\mathrm{i})^{2}}\oint_{\Gamma_{-1/2,-\bm{\beta}}}\mathrm{d}w\oint_{\Gamma_{1/2}}\mathrm{d}v\,\frac{E(x,w;y,v)}{v-w}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{v+\beta_{j}}{w+\beta_{j}},

\displaystyle K_{m,n,22}^{\exp}(x,y)

\displaystyle:=\frac{-1}{(2\pi\mathrm{i})^{2}}\oint_{\Gamma_{-1/2}}\mathrm{d}w\oint_{\Gamma_{1/2,\bm{\alpha}}}\mathrm{d}v\,\frac{E(x,w;y,v)}{v-w}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\frac{w-\alpha_{i}}{v-\alpha_{i}},

\displaystyle K_{m,n,21}^{\exp}(x,y)

\displaystyle:=\frac{-1}{(2\pi\mathrm{i})^{2}}\oint_{\Gamma_{-1/2}}\mathrm{d}w\oint_{\Gamma_{1/2}}\mathrm{d}v\,\frac{E(x,w;y,v)}{v-w}\prod_{j=1}^{k}(v+\beta_{j})\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}(w-\alpha_{i}),

\displaystyle K_{m,n,12}^{\exp}(x,y)

\displaystyle:=\frac{-\mathbbm{1}_{x>y}}{(2\pi\mathrm{i})^{2}}\oint_{\Gamma_{-1/2,-\bm{\beta}}}\mathrm{d}w\oint_{\Gamma_{1/2,\bm{\alpha},w}}\mathrm{d}v\,\frac{E(x,w;y,v)}{v-w}\frac{1}{\prod_{j=1}^{k}(w+\beta_{j})\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}(v-\alpha_{i})}

\displaystyle+\frac{-\mathbbm{1}_{x\leq y}}{(2\pi\mathrm{i})^{2}}\oint_{\Gamma_{1/2,\bm{\alpha}}}\mathrm{d}v\oint_{\Gamma_{-1/2,-\bm{\beta},v}}\mathrm{d}w\,\frac{E(x,w;y,v)}{v-w}\frac{1}{\prod_{j=1}^{k}(w+\beta_{j})\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}(v-\alpha_{i})}.

.

2.2
Limit fluctuations in the critical regime

We next choose the parameters
\bm{\alpha}
and
\bm{\beta}
as in (
1.1
), that is,

\bm{\alpha}=\tilde{\bm{\alpha}}+(16N)^{-1/3}\mathbf{x},\quad\bm{\beta}=\tilde{\bm{\beta}}+(16N)^{-1/3}\mathbf{y},

(2.3)

where
\tilde{\bm{\alpha}},\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
and
\tilde{\bm{\beta}},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
are such that

-\frac{1}{2}<\tilde{\bm{\alpha}}+(16N)^{-1/3}\mathbf{x},\tilde{\bm{\beta}}+(16N)^{-1/3}\mathbf{y}.

(2.4)

We also take the endpoint of the last passage time
L_{m,n}^{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}
to be

(m,n)=(N-\tau(2N)^{2/3},\,N+\tau(2N)^{2/3})

(2.5)

for some parameter
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
. Our aim is to identify the limit fluctuations of
L_{m,n}^{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}=L_{N,\tau}^{\tilde{\bm{\alpha}},\tilde{\bm{\beta}},\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}
as
N\to\infty
.
The scaling of
\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{y}
by
N^{-1/3}
will ensure that these parameters contribute nontrivially in the scaling limit if the corresponding
\tilde{\alpha}_{i}
,
\tilde{\beta}_{i}
are
0
.

As mentioned in the introduction, the asymptotic behavior of the last passage time
L^{\tilde{\bm{\alpha}},\tilde{\bm{\beta}},\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}_{N,\tau}
was analyzed in
[
16
]
for various regimes of the parameters. We summarize below some results from
[
16
]
and refer to that article for further details.

Theorem 2.2

(Theorem 2.1 of
[
16
]
)
.

(i)

If

-1/2<\min\left\{\tilde{\alpha}_{i},\tilde{\beta}_{j}\mid i\in\llbracket\ell\rrbracket,\,j\in\llbracket k\rrbracket\right\}<0,

the fluctuation of
L^{\tilde{\bm{\alpha}},\tilde{\bm{\beta}},\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}_{N,\tau}
is of order
{\mathcal{O}}(N^{1/2})
with known limiting distribution;

(ii)

If
\tilde{\bm{\alpha}},\tilde{\bm{\beta}}\geq 0
with
|\mathcal{I}(\tilde{\bm{\alpha}})|=|\mathcal{I}(\tilde{\bm{\beta}})|=0
, then the fluctuation of
L^{\tilde{\bm{\alpha}},\tilde{\bm{\beta}},\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}_{N,\tau}
is of order
{\mathcal{O}}(N^{1/3})
and the limiting distribution is GUE Tracy-Widom distribution.

(iii)

If
\tilde{\bm{\alpha}},\tilde{\bm{\beta}}\geq 0
with
|\mathcal{I}(\tilde{\bm{\alpha}})|=0
and
|\mathcal{I}(\tilde{\bm{\beta}})|>0
, then the fluctuation of
L^{\tilde{\bm{\alpha}},\tilde{\bm{\beta}},\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}_{N,\tau}
is of order
{\mathcal{O}}(N^{1/3})
and the limiting distribution is
F_{|\mathcal{I}(\tilde{\bm{\beta}})|,\mathbf{y}_{\mathcal{I}(\tilde{\bm{\beta}})}}
introduced in
[
9
]
.

In the case where
\tilde{\bm{\alpha}},\tilde{\bm{\beta}}\geq 0
with
\min\{|\mathcal{I}(\tilde{\bm{\alpha}})|,|\mathcal{I}(\tilde{\bm{\beta}})|\}>0
, Ben Arous and Corwin conjectured the existence of a limit distribution
F_{|\mathcal{I}(\tilde{\bm{\alpha}})|,|\mathcal{I}(\tilde{\bm{\beta}})|,\mathbf{x}_{\mathcal{I}(\tilde{\bm{\alpha}})},\mathbf{y}_{\mathcal{I}(\tilde{\bm{\beta}})}}
that governs the fluctuations of
L_{N,\tau}^{\tilde{\bm{\alpha}},\tilde{\bm{\beta}},\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}
under the KPZ scaling in the large-
N
limit (see (51) of
[
16
]
). Our next result establishes this conjecture and provides an explicit formula for the limit distribution. This new distribution, which generalizes the Baik–Rains distribution, can be introduced as follows.

Definition 2.3

(Generalized Baik–Rains distribution)
.

Let
k,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
,
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
,
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
. We define the CDF of the generalized Baik–Rains distribution by

F_{\ell,k,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}(s)=\det\left(\mathbbm{1}-P_{s}K^{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}P_{s}\right)_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})},

(2.6)

where
P_{s}
is the projection operator onto
(s,\infty)
and
K^{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}
the
2\times 2
matrix kernel with entries

\displaystyle K_{11}^{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}(\xi,\zeta)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{(2\pi\mathrm{i})^{2}}\int_{\;{\tiny-\mathbf{y}}\,\raisebox{-2.11113pt}{\includegraphics[scale]{leftcontour}}\,{\tiny}}\mathrm{d}w\int_{\;{\tiny-\mathbf{y},w}\,\raisebox{-2.11113pt}{\includegraphics[scale]{rightcontour}}\,{\tiny}}\mathrm{d}v\,\frac{e^{v^{3}/3-v\zeta}}{e^{w^{3}/3-w\xi}}\frac{1}{v-w}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{v+y_{i}}{w+y_{i}},

(2.7)

\displaystyle K_{22}^{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}(\xi,\zeta)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{(2\pi\mathrm{i})^{2}}\int_{\;{\tiny}\,\raisebox{-2.11113pt}{\includegraphics[scale]{rightcontour}}\,{\tiny\mathbf{x}}}\mathrm{d}v\int_{\;{\tiny}\,\raisebox{-2.11113pt}{\includegraphics[scale]{leftcontour}}\,{\tiny\mathbf{x},v}}\mathrm{d}w\,\frac{e^{v^{3}/3-v\zeta}}{e^{w^{3}/3-w\xi}}\frac{1}{v-w}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\frac{w-x_{i}}{v-x_{i}},

\displaystyle K_{21}^{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}(\xi,\zeta)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{(2\pi\mathrm{i})^{2}}\int_{{\tiny}\,\raisebox{-2.11113pt}{\includegraphics[scale]{leftcontour}}\,{\tiny 0}}\mathrm{d}w\int_{{\tiny w}\,\raisebox{-2.11113pt}{\includegraphics[scale]{rightcontour}}\,{\tiny}}\mathrm{d}v\,\frac{e^{v^{3}/3-v\zeta}}{e^{w^{3}/3-w\xi}}\frac{1}{v-w}\prod_{i=1}^{k}(v+y_{i})\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}(w-x_{j}),

\displaystyle K_{12}^{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}(\xi,\zeta)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\xi>\zeta}}{\left(2\pi\mathrm{i}\right)^{2}}\int_{\;{\tiny}\,\raisebox{-2.11113pt}{\includegraphics[scale]{rightcontour}}\,{\tiny-\mathbf{y},\mathbf{x}}}\mathrm{d}v\bigg[\int_{\;{\tiny}\,\raisebox{-2.11113pt}{\includegraphics[scale]{leftcontour}}\,{\tiny-\mathbf{y},\mathbf{x},v}}\mathrm{d}wf_{12}(\xi,w;\zeta,v)+\oint_{\Gamma_{-\mathbf{y}}}\mathrm{d}wf_{12}(\xi,w;\zeta,v)\bigg]

\displaystyle+\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\xi\leq\zeta}}{\left(2\pi\mathrm{i}\right)^{2}}\int_{{\tiny-\mathbf{y},\mathbf{x}}\,\raisebox{-2.11113pt}{\includegraphics[scale]{leftcontour}}\,{\tiny}}\mathrm{d}w\bigg[\int_{{\tiny-\mathbf{y},\mathbf{x},w}\,\raisebox{-2.11113pt}{\includegraphics[scale]{rightcontour}}\,{\tiny}}\mathrm{d}vf_{12}(\xi,w;\zeta,v)-\oint_{\Gamma_{\mathbf{x}}}\mathrm{d}vf_{12}(\xi,w;\zeta,v)\bigg],

where

f_{12}(\xi,w;\zeta,v):=\frac{e^{v^{3}/3-v\zeta}}{e^{w^{3}/3-w\xi}}\frac{1}{v-w}\frac{1}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left(w+y_{i}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\left(v-x_{j}\right)}.

(2.8)

In particular, the contours for
w,v
are chosen not to intersect.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\tilde{\bm{\alpha}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell}_{\geq 0}
and
\tilde{\bm{\beta}}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}_{\geq 0}
with
\min\{|\mathcal{I}(\tilde{\bm{\alpha}})|,\,|\mathcal{I}(\tilde{\bm{\beta}})|\}>0
. Then, for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
, the following convergence holds:

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(L^{\tilde{\bm{\alpha}},\tilde{\bm{\beta}},\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}_{N,\tau}\leq 4N+(16N)^{1/3}s\right)=F_{|\mathcal{I}(\tilde{\bm{\alpha}})|,\,|\mathcal{I}(\tilde{\bm{\beta}})|,\,\mathbf{x}_{\mathcal{I}(\tilde{\bm{\alpha}})}-\tau,\,\mathbf{y}_{\mathcal{I}(\tilde{\bm{\beta}})}+\tau}(s+\tau^{2}),

(2.9)

where the distribution on the right-hand side is the generalized Baik–Rains distribution introduced in Definition
2.3
. With the notation
\mathbf{x}_{\mathcal{I}(\tilde{\bm{\alpha}})}
we mean the subset of
x_{i}
such that
i\in\mathcal{I}(\tilde{\bm{\alpha}})
, and similarly for
\mathbf{y}_{\mathcal{I}(\tilde{\bm{\beta}})}
.

This theorem is proved in Section
4
.

2.3
New determinantal formula for the Baik–Rains distribution

We now connect Definition
2.3
to the classical Baik–Rains distribution and then record a new representation for the latter. In the special case
k=\ell=1
, it is known that

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(L_{N,\tau}\leq 4N+(16N)^{1/3}s\right)=F_{{\rm BR},\tau}(s),

(2.10)

where
L_{N,\tau}=L_{N,\tau}^{0,0,0,0}
and
F_{{\rm BR},\tau}
is the (CDF of the) Baik–Rains distribution. The distribution
F_{{\rm BR},\tau}
was first discovered as a limiting law in the study of the LPP with Poisson weights, where it was expressed in terms of the solution to a certain Painlevé equation
[
12
]
. The analogous limit for the exponential LPP is (
2.10
), which was initially conjectured by Prähofer and Spohn
[
83
]
, and proved in
[
51
]
.
It follows from (
2.10
) and Theorem
2.4
that

F_{{\rm BR},\tau}(s)=F_{1,1,-\tau,\tau}(s+\tau^{2}).

(2.11)

In Section
5
, we give a direct analytic proof of the preceding identity for
\tau=0
.

In view of (
2.11
), using the integral representation of Airy function together with (
2.7
), we obtain a new representation of
F_{{\rm BR},\tau}
as a Fredholm determinant of a
2\times 2
kernel given in Corollary
2.5
below. Our formula is simpler than the one available in
[
51
]
; see Appendix
A
for comparison.

Corollary 2.5
.

For any
s,\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

F_{{\rm BR},\tau}(s)=\det(\mathbbm{1}-P_{s+\tau^{2}}K^{{\rm BR},\tau}P_{s+\tau^{2}})_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}

(2.12)

where
K^{{\rm BR},\tau}
is a
2\times 2
matrix kernel with entries

\displaystyle K^{{\rm BR},\tau}_{11}(\xi,\zeta)

\displaystyle:=K_{{\rm Ai}}(\xi,\zeta)-\mathrm{Ai}(\zeta)\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\lambda\mathrm{Ai}(\xi+\lambda)e^{-\tau\lambda}+e^{-\frac{\tau^{3}}{3}+\tau\xi}\mathrm{Ai}(\zeta)

(2.13)

\displaystyle K^{{\rm BR},\tau}_{22}(\xi,\zeta)

\displaystyle:=K_{{\rm Ai}}(\xi,\zeta)-\mathrm{Ai}(\xi)\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\lambda\mathrm{Ai}(\zeta+\lambda)e^{\tau\lambda}+e^{\frac{\tau^{3}}{3}-\tau\zeta}\mathrm{Ai}(\xi)

\displaystyle K^{{\rm BR},\tau}_{21}(\xi,\zeta)

\displaystyle:=\tau^{2}K_{{\rm Ai}}(\xi,\zeta)-\tau(\partial_{\xi}K_{{\rm Ai}}(\xi,\zeta)-\partial_{\zeta}K_{{\rm Ai}}(\xi,\zeta))-\partial_{\xi}\partial_{\zeta}K_{{\rm Ai}}(\xi,\zeta)

\displaystyle K^{{\rm BR},\tau}_{12}(\xi,\zeta)

\displaystyle:=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\lambda\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\gamma\;K_{{\rm Ai}}(\xi+\gamma,\zeta+\lambda)e^{\tau(\lambda-\gamma)}

\displaystyle+e^{\frac{\tau^{3}}{3}-\tau\zeta}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\gamma\mathrm{Ai}(\xi+\gamma)\gamma e^{-\tau\gamma}+e^{-\frac{\tau^{3}}{3}+\tau\xi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\lambda\mathrm{Ai}(\zeta+\lambda)\lambda e^{\tau\lambda}

\displaystyle-e^{\tau(\xi-\zeta)}(\tau^{2}-\min\{\xi,\zeta\}),

where
K_{{\rm Ai}}
is the Airy kernel

K_{{\rm Ai}}(\xi,\zeta):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\lambda\mathrm{Ai}(\xi+\lambda)\mathrm{Ai}(\zeta+\lambda).

(2.14)

We will derive Corollary
2.5
in Section
5.1
.

Relative to the classical representation of the Baik–Rains distribution, which is typically evaluated using Painlevé transcendents
[
85
,
82
]
, the alternative expression on the right-hand side of (

) enables a more efficient numerical computation via Bornemann’s method

2.4
Variational formula

We next deduce a variational formula for the generalized Baik–Rains distribution. Let
k,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,
t\geq 0
and
B_{1},\ldots,B_{k},\tilde{B}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{B}_{\ell}
be independent standard Brownian motions. Define two independent Brownian last passage percolation
[
78
,
14
,
57
]

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{k}(t):=\sup_{0\leq u_{1}\leq\ldots\leq u_{k-1}\leq t}\sum_{r=1}^{k}[B_{r}(u_{r})-B_{r}(u_{r-1})],

(2.15)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{\ell}(t):=\sup_{0\leq v_{1}\leq\ldots\leq v_{\ell-1}\leq t}\sum_{r=1}^{\ell}[\tilde{B}_{r}(v_{r})-\tilde{B}_{r}(v_{r-1})],

where we identify
u_{0}=v_{0}=0
,
u_{k}=v_{\ell}=t
.

Also, we denote by
\mathcal{A}_{2}(t)
the Airy
2
process
[
84
]
. For the case of
\bm{\alpha}=\bm{\beta}=0
, the boundaries in our LPP under scaling limit converge to the processes in (
2.15
), while the bulk to an Airy
2
process. In particular, the limiting distribution of our LPP, for which we have an explicit Fredholm determinant formula, also admits the following variational formula.

Theorem 2.6

(Variational Formula)
.

For any
s\in\mathbb{R}
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left\{\sqrt{2}\mathcal{B}_{k}(t)\mathbbm{1}_{t\geq 0}+\sqrt{2}\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{\ell}(-t)\mathbbm{1}_{t<0}+\mathcal{A}_{2}(t)-t^{2}\right\}\leq s\right)=F_{k,\ell,\mathbf{0},\mathbf{0}}(s),

(2.16)

where the right hand side is the generalized Baik–Rains distribution.

This theorem is proved in Section
6
. In the special case with
k=\ell=1
, the result recovers the Corollary 2.4 of
[
33
]
.

2.5
Inhomogeneous stationary LPP

To state our final result, we turn to the model with the weights
\widehat{\omega}
given by (
1.5
). As mentioned in the introduction, the increments of the associated last passage process
\widehat{L}^{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}
are stationary in the following sense. For any
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
with
m>-\ell
and
n>-k

\displaystyle\begin{split}(\widehat{L}^{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}_{i,n}-\widehat{L}^{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}_{i-1,n}:i\in\mathbb{Z}\text{ and }i>-\ell+1)&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm{(d)}}}{{=}}(\widehat{\omega}_{i,-k+1}:i\in\mathbb{Z}\text{ and }i>-\ell+1),\\

(2.17)

In other words, the last passage increments along any row (resp. column) have the same joint distribution as the weights on the lowest row
-k+1
(resp. column
-\ell+1
). The stationary property in (
2.17
) is obtained from Proposition 4.1 of
[
43
]
by choosing the parameters there as

\displaystyle a_{i}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\alpha_{i+1}=(16N)^{-1/3}x_{i+1}\quad&\text{ if }i<\ell,\\
1/2\quad&\text{ if }i\geq\ell,\end{cases}

(2.18)

\displaystyle b_{j}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\beta_{j+1}=(16N)^{-1/3}y_{j+1}\quad&\text{ if }i<k,\\
1/2\quad&\text{ if }j\geq k,\end{cases}

for
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and
z=0
.

Our next result concerns the distribution function of
\widehat{L}^{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}_{m,n}

\bm{\alpha}
and
\bm{\beta}
subject to the assumption that

(2.19)

Appendix A
Classical expression for the Baik–Rains distribution

The Baik–Rains distribution can be expressed in terms of solutions of Painlevé II equation
[
12
]
(reported also in Appendix A of
[
51
]
) or in terms of kernels and Fredholm determinants, see
[
51
]
. For completeness we report this last expression here. Let

K_{\mathrm{Ai},s}(x,y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\mathrm{d}\lambda\mathrm{Ai}(\lambda+x+s)\mathrm{Ai}(\lambda+y+s).

(A.1)

and

\displaystyle\widehat{\Phi}_{\tau,s}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{-}}\mathrm{d}ze^{\tau z}K_{\mathrm{Ai},s}(z,x)e^{\tau s},

(A.2)

\displaystyle\widehat{\Psi}_{\tau,s}(y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{-}}\mathrm{d}ze^{\tau z}\mathrm{Ai}(y+z+s),

\displaystyle\rho_{s}(x,y)=\left(\mathbbm{1}-P_{0}K_{\mathrm{Ai},s}P_{0}\right)^{-1}(x,y),

and the scaling function
g
by

g(s,\tau)=e^{-\frac{1}{3}\tau^{3}}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}_{\leq 0}^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}ye^{\tau(x+y)}\mathrm{Ai}(x+y+s)+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\widehat{\Phi}_{\tau,s}(x)\rho_{s}(x,y)\widehat{\Psi}_{\tau,s}(y)\right].

(A.3)

Then we have

F_{\tau}(s)=\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\left(F_{{\rm GUE}}\left(s+\tau^{2}\right)g\left(s+\tau^{2},\tau\right)\right).

(A.4)

As the Baik–Rains distribution for
\tau
and
-\tau
is the same, the above expressions are the ones for
\tau>0
, so that all the integrals converges. For
\tau=0
one has to modify the integrals on
\mathbb{R}_{\leq 0}
to

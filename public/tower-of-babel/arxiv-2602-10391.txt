Symmetry Results for Cyclotomic Multiple Hurwitz Zeta Values via Contour Integrals
Ce Xu

Abstract




Abstract.
This paper provides a systematic study of symmetry properties for cyclotomic multiple Hurwitz zeta values with multiple variables and parameters by applying the methods of contour integration and the residue theorem. The main contributions are the derivation of explicit symmetry formulas for cyclotomic multiple (Hurwitz) zeta values, which are obtained directly through analytic residue calculations, without reliance on algebraic regularization. As a concrete application, we deduce analogous symmetry theorems for cyclotomic multiple zeta values and cyclotomic multiple
t
-values. The results extend and complement recent symmetry investigations by Charlton and Hoffman, offering completely explicit and regularization-free formulas in the convergent setting. Moreover, the results of this paper can be used to prove the symmetry conjecture for cyclotomic multiple Hurwitz zeta values with multiple variables and a single parameter. Furthermore, several illustrative corollaries and examples are included, and an open problem concerning possible extensions to other variants of multiple zeta values is posed at the conclusion.

Keywords
: Cyclotomic multiple (Hurwitz) zeta values; Cyclotomic multiple
t
-values; Symmetry theorem; Contour integration; Residue theorem.

\displaystyle\oint_{(\infty)}r(s)\xi(s)ds:=\displaystyle\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}\oint_{C_{R}}r(s)\xi(s)ds=0,

\displaystyle\psi(s)=-\gamma-\displaystyle\frac{1}{s}+\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\displaystyle\frac{1}{k}-\displaystyle\frac{1}{s+k}\right),

and
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{-}:=\mathbb{N}^{-}\cup\{0\}=\{0,-1,-2,-3,\ldots\}
.

Theorem 2.8

\displaystyle\Li_{m+1}(x;1-a)-(-1)^{m}x\Li_{m+1}(x^{-1};a)=\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{m!}\displaystyle\frac{d^{m}}{da^{m}}\left(ix^{a}-\cot(\pi a)x^{a}\right),

(2.21)

Proof.

From
[

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{e^{in\theta}}{n-x}

\displaystyle=2\pi i\displaystyle\frac{e^{i\theta x}}{1-e^{2\pi ix}}=-\pi\displaystyle\frac{e^{ix(\theta-\pi)}}{\sin(\pi x)}

\displaystyle=-\pi\cot(\pi a)x^{a}+\pi x^{a}i.

On the other hand, noting the fact that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{x^{n}}{n-a}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{x^{n}}{n-a}+\displaystyle\sum_{n=-\infty}^{0}\displaystyle\frac{x^{n}}{n-a}=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{x^{n}}{n-a}-\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{x^{1-n}}{n+a-1}

\displaystyle\Li_{1}(x;1-a)-x\Li_{1}(x^{-1};a)=-\pi\cot(\pi a)x^{a}+\pi x^{a}i.

Finally, differentiating the above equation

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq i+m\leq k_{j}-1,\atop i,m\geq 0}\displaystyle\sum_{|\overrightarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{m}}}}_{1,r}|-m_{j}=m,\atop\forall\ m_{l}\geq 0}D_{q,m,i,j}\left(\overrightarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}}_{1,r};\overrightarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{m}}}}_{1,r}\right)\Li_{\overrightarrow{({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}+{\boldsymbol{\sl{m}}})}_{j+1,r}}\left(\overrightarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{x}}}}_{j+1,r};\overrightarrow{(1-a_{j}+{\bf a})}_{j+1,r}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\widehat{\Li}_{i+1}\left(x^{-1};a_{j}\right)\displaystyle\frac{\zeta_{n-1}\left(\overleftarrow{({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}+{\boldsymbol{\sl{m}}})}_{1,j-1};\overleftarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{x}}}}^{-1}_{1,j-1};\overleftarrow{(a_{j}-{\bf a})}_{1,j-1}\right)}{(n+a_{j}-a)^{q+k_{j}-i-m-1}}(\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{\sl{x}}}}_{0,r})^{n}.

(5.43)

By the residue theorem stated in Lemma
3.1
, the sum of all residues must equal zero, i.e.,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\rm Res}[G_{q}(s),n]+\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\rm Res}[G_{q}(s),-n]

\displaystyle\quad+\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{r}{\rm Res}[G_{q}(s),-n-a_{j}]+{\rm Res}[G_{q}(s),-a]=0.

(5.44)

Substituting the four residue results (
5
)-(
5
) into (
5
) and replacing
x
with
\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{\sl{x}}}_{0,r}^{-1}}
in the resulting expression completes the proof of Theorem
2.2
.
\square

Setting
r=1,k_{1}=p,x_{1}=y
and
a_{1}=b
in Theorem
2.2
yields the following corollary, namely the symmetry formula for cyclotomic double Hurwitz zeta values..

Corollary 5.1
.

Let
x,y
be roots of unity. For
a,b\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}
and
(q,x),(p,y)\neq(1,1)
with
b-a\notin\mathbb{N}^{-}
, we have

\displaystyle(xy)^{-1}\Li_{q,p}(x,y;a,b)+(-1)^{q}y^{-1}\Li_{p}(y;b)\Li_{q}\Big(x^{-1};1-a\Big)

\displaystyle+(-1)^{p+q}\Li_{p,q}\Big(y^{-1},x^{-1};1-b,1-a\Big)

\displaystyle+\displaystyle\sum_{j+m=q-1,\atop j,m\geq 0}(-1)^{m}\binom{m+p-1}{p-1}\Li_{p+m}(y;1+b-a)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\left((-1)^{j}\Li_{j+1}\Big((xy)^{-1};1-a\Big)-(xy)^{-1}\Li_{j+1}(xy;a)\right)

\displaystyle+(-1)^{q}\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\binom{p+q-i-2}{q-1}\Li_{p+q-i-1}\Big(x^{-1};1+b-a\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\left((-1)^{i}\Li_{i+1}\Big((xy)^{-1};1-b\Big)-(xy)^{-1}\Li_{i+1}(xy;b)\right)=0.

From Corollary
5.1
with the help of (

), the following cases can be easily provided.

Example 5.2
.

Let
x
be root of unity. For
a,b\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}
and
b-a\notin\mathbb{N}^{-}
, we have

\displaystyle\Li_{2,2}\Big(x^{-1},x;a,b\Big)+\Li_{2,2}\Big(x^{-1},x;1-b,1-a\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad+x^{-1}\Li_{2}(x;b)\Li_{2}(x;1-a)

\displaystyle=\pi^{2}\Big(\csc^{2}(\pi a)+\csc^{2}(\pi b)\Big)\Li_{2}(x;1+b-a)

\displaystyle\quad+2\pi\csc(\pi b)\Li_{3}(-x;1+b-a)-2\pi\csc(\pi a)\Li_{3}(x;1+b-a).

Finally, we conclude this paper by raising an open question.

Question 5.1
.

Can the method of contour integration employed in this paper be extended to study other variants of multiple zeta values, such as the Schur multiple zeta values, Mordell-Tornheim zeta functions, Barnes multiple zeta functions, and so forth? The author believes that the key lies in obtaining specific forms of Laurent expansions and Taylor expansions for these variants of multiple zeta values.

In Principles of Verification: Cycling the Probabilistic Landscape. Essays Dedicated to Joost-Pieter Katoen on the Occasion of His 60th Birthday , N. Hansen et al. (eds.), Part I, LNCS 15260, pp. 73-84, Springer, 2024. Full version (extended with Appendix B). Amortized Analysis of Leftist Heaps
Berry Schoenmakers

Abstract
Leftist heaps and skew heaps are two well-known data structures for mergeable priority queues. Leftist heaps are constructed for efficiency in the worst-case sense whereas skew heaps are self-adjusting, designed for efficiency in the amortized sense. In this paper, we analyze the amortized complexity of leftist heaps to initiate a full performance comparison with skew heaps. We consider both the leftist heaps originally developed by Crane and Knuth, which are also referred to as rank-biased (or, height-biased) leftist heaps, and the weight-biased leftist heaps introduced by Cho and Sahni. We show how weight-biased leftist heaps satisfy the same exact amortized bounds as skew heaps. With these matching bounds we establish a nice trade-off in which storage of weights is used to limit the worst-case complexity of leftist heaps, without affecting the amortized complexity compared to skew heaps. For rank-biased leftist heaps, we obtain the same amortized lower bounds as for skew heaps, but whether these bounds are tight is left as an open problem.

2
Leftist Heaps

Leftist heaps were invented by Crane
[
Cra72
]
and further developed by Knuth, see
[
Knu98
, Section 5.2.3]
.
Apart from supporting the usual priority queue operations such as
{\sf insert}
and
{\sf delmin}
,
leftist heaps also support
{\sf meld}
for merging (or, melding) heaps.
Leftist heaps achieve logarithmic time for all these operations. Nowadays, there exist lots
of alternative priority queue implementations, often with better performance, such
as merging in constant time (see, e.g.,
[
Bro13
]
), but leftist heaps, and skew heaps as their
self-adjusting counterparts, are very simple to implement and generally applicable.

Leftist heaps are slightly more complicated than (top-down) skew heaps. Skew heaps are
self-adjusting and require no storage other than the storage required
for the heap itself. Leftist heaps either store the weight or the rank for each subtree.
Weight-biased leftist heaps were introduced by Cho and Sahni
[
CS96
]
as a single
(top-down) pass variant of the original rank-biased leftist heaps due to Crane and Knuth.
As highlighted by Okasaki
[
Oka99
]
, the weight-biased variant is also favorable in
combination with lazy evaluation; similar advantages apply in combination with concurrent
evaluation, as first considered by Jones
[
Jon89
]
. As skew heaps keep no additional
information at all, the same advantages apply there as well.

\mbox{$\mbox{${\sf insert}\>a$}\>x$}=\mbox{$\mbox{${\sf meld}\>\mbox{$({\sf single}\>a)$}$}\>x$}

We use
\langle\;\rangle
to denote an empty tree and
\langle t,a,u\rangle
to denote a binary tree with
left subtree
t
, root value
a

, with
\langle a\rangle
as a shorthand
for
\langle\mbox{$\langle\;\rangle$},a,\mbox{$\langle\;\rangle$}\rangle
. The weight
\#x

of tree

\mbox{$\#\mbox{$\langle\;\rangle$}$}=0

\mbox{$\#\mbox{$\langle t,a,u\rangle$}$}=\mbox{$\#t$}+\mbox{$\#u$}+1
.
The rank

\mbox{$\dagger\mbox{$\langle\;\rangle$}$}=0

\mbox{$\dagger\mbox{$\langle t,a,u\rangle$}$}=\mbox{$\dagger u$}+1

Binary heaps satisfy the heap property implying that the root holds the minimum value
for each subtree. In addition, for weight-biased heaps, the weight of each left subtree
is at least the weight of its right sibling, and for rank-biased heaps,
the rank of each left subtree is at least the rank of its right sibling.
Note that weights require about
\log_{2}n
bits of storage for size-
n

while
\log_{2}\log_{2}n
bits suffice for ranks, due to (
1
).

As a suitable cost measure
{\cal T}
for the time complexity of binary heaps we count the number of
comparisons
a\leq\mbox{${\sf min}\>y$}
used for unfolding
\,\Join\,
, including all comparisons with
\infty
.
Clearly, the costs for
{\sf empty}{}
,
{\sf isempty}\>x
,
{\sf single}\>a
,
{\sf min}\>x
are then 0,
while
\mbox{${\cal T}[\mbox{$\mbox{${\sf meld}$}$}](x,y)$}=\mbox{$\dagger x$}+\mbox{$\dagger y$}
and
\mbox{${\cal T}[\mbox{${\sf delmin}$}](\mbox{$\langle t,a,u\rangle$})$}=\mbox{$\dagger t$}+\mbox{$\dagger u$}
.
For leftist heaps we have the bound

\mbox{$\dagger x$}\leq\log_{2}\mbox{$\mid\!\!x\!\!\mid$},

(1)

using
\mbox{$\mid\!\!x\!\!\mid$}=\mbox{$\#x$}+1
to denote the weight of
x
plus one
[
Cra72
,
Knu98
,
CS96
]
.
This leads to the following worst-case bounds for leftist heaps:

\begin{array}[]{lll}\mbox{${\cal T}[\mbox{$\mbox{${\sf meld}$}$}](x,y)$}&\leq&2\,\log_{2}\mbox{$\mid\!\!\mbox{$x\raisebox{-0.85358pt}{$\,\Join\,$}y$}\!\!\mid$},\\
\mbox{${\cal T}[\mbox{${\sf delmin}$}](x)$}&\leq&2\,\log_{2}\mbox{$\mid\!\!x\!\!\mid$}.\end{array}

For skew heaps,
\dagger x
is linear in
\mid\!\!x\!\!\mid
in the worst case, so we need
to perform an amortized analysis to get any useful bounds.
However, as we will show below, the above worst-case bounds are also overly pessimistic for leftists heaps.
We will present several improved amortized analyses for leftist heaps.

Note that by
{\cal T}[f](E)
we denote the cost of evaluating
f
given the
value
of expression
E
,
not counting the cost of the evaluation of
E

[
KS91a
]
:

\mbox{${\cal T}[f](E)$}=\mbox{${\cal T}(f(E))$}-\mbox{${\cal T}(E)$}.

This allows for modular analysis using composition rules such as

\mbox{${\cal T}[g\circ f](E)$}=\mbox{${\cal T}[f](E)$}+\mbox{${\cal T}[g](f(E))$}.

Defining the amortized cost of
f
with respect to a potential function
\Phi{}
as

\mbox{${\cal A}[f](E)$}=\mbox{${\cal T}[f](E)$}+\Phi{(f(E))}-\Phi{(E)}

then allows for modular amortized analysis, using composition rules such as

\mbox{${\cal A}[g\circ f](E)$}=\mbox{${\cal A}[f](E)$}+\mbox{${\cal A}[g](f(E))$}.

See
[
Sch92
, Ch. 5]
for this formalization of the physicist’s method
[
ST86
,
Tar85
]
.

3
Simple Amortized Analysis of Leftist Heaps

We perform an amortized analysis in terms of a potential function
\Phi{}
,
where the amortized costs are given by:

\begin{array}[]{lllll}\mbox{${\cal A}[\mbox{$\mbox{${\sf meld}$}$}](x,y)$}&=&\mbox{${\cal T}[\mbox{$\raisebox{-0.85358pt}{$\,\Join\,$}$}](x,y)$}+\Phi{(\mbox{$x\raisebox{-0.85358pt}{$\,\Join\,$}y$})}-\Phi{(x)}-\Phi{(y)},\\
\mbox{${\cal A}[\mbox{${\sf delmin}$}{}](x)$}&=&\mbox{${\cal T}[\mbox{$\raisebox{-0.85358pt}{$\,\Join\,$}$}](t,u)$}+\Phi{(\mbox{$t\raisebox{-0.85358pt}{$\,\Join\,$}u$})}-\Phi{(x)},\mbox{\ \ with }x=\mbox{$\langle t,a,u\rangle$}.\end{array}

Taking
\Phi{(x)}=\mbox{$\dagger x$}
gives us the following bounds for leftist heaps, using (
1
):

\begin{array}[]{lllll}\mbox{${\cal A}[\mbox{$\mbox{${\sf meld}$}$}](x,y)$}&=&\mbox{$\dagger x$}+\mbox{$\dagger y$}+\mbox{$\dagger(\mbox{$x\raisebox{-0.85358pt}{$\,\Join\,$}y$})$}-\mbox{$\dagger x$}-\mbox{$\dagger y$}&\leq&\log_{2}\mbox{$\mid\!\!\mbox{$x\raisebox{-0.85358pt}{$\,\Join\,$}y$}\!\!\mid$},\\
\mbox{${\cal A}[\mbox{${\sf delmin}$}{}](x)$}&=&\mbox{$\dagger t$}+\mbox{$\dagger u$}+\mbox{$\dagger(\mbox{$t\raisebox{-0.85358pt}{$\,\Join\,$}u$})$}-\mbox{$\dagger u$}-1&\leq&2\,\log_{2}\mbox{$\mid\!\!x\!\!\mid$}.\end{array}

This means that the bound for
{\sf meld}
is reduced by 50%, while retaining the bound for
{\sf delmin}
.
Note that (
1
) actually implies the same bounds for any potential function
\Phi{}

satisfying
\mbox{$\dagger x$}\leq\Phi{(x)}\leq\log_{2}\mbox{$\mid\!\!x\!\!\mid$}
.

4
Amortized Analysis of Weight-Biased Leftist Heaps

We have reduced the bound for
{\sf meld}
by 50% but for many applications the overall cost is dominated by the cost of the
{\sf delmin}
operations.
Therefore we want to make the bound for
{\sf delmin}
as tight as possible. In this section we will show that for weight-biased leftist heaps
the upper bound for
{\cal A}[\mbox{${\sf delmin}$}](x)
can be improved to
\log_{\phi}\mbox{$\mid\!\!x\!\!\mid$}\approx 1.44\,\log_{2}\mbox{$\mid\!\!x\!\!\mid$}
, where
\phi=(\sqrt{5}{+}1)/2\approx 1.618
is the golden ratio.
The lower bound in Section
6
will imply that this bound is tight.

In our amortized analysis we exploit the close relationship between weight-biased leftist heaps and top-down skew heaps.
We define potential function
\Phi{}
as

\begin{array}[]{lll}\Phi{\mbox{$\langle\;\rangle$}}&=&0,\\
\Phi{\mbox{$\langle t,a,u\rangle$}}&=&\Phi{(t)}+\varphi(t,u)+\Phi{(u)},\end{array}

where

\varphi(t,u)=\max\left(\log_{\beta}\frac{\beta\mbox{$\mid\!\!u\!\!\mid$}}{\mbox{$\mid\!\!t\!\!\mid$}+\mbox{$\mid\!\!u\!\!\mid$}},0\right),

with
\beta=\phi^{\phi+2}\approx 5.703
, following
[
KS91a
]
,
[
Sch92
, Lemma 9.2]
.

The essential property that we will need for this potential function is that

\mbox{$\mid\!\!t\!\!\mid$}\leq\mbox{$\mid\!\!u\!\!\mid$}\Rightarrow\varphi(u,t)\leq\varphi(t,u),

(2)

as
\log_{\beta}
is increasing. Also note that
0\leq\varphi(t,u)\leq 1
.

The central operation to analyze is
x\raisebox{-0.85358pt}{$\,\Join\,$}y
. Because of the balancing step we cannot reuse the analysis of
[
KS91a
]
.
Instead we will directly prove by induction on
x
and
y
that

\mbox{${\cal A}[\mbox{$\raisebox{-0.85358pt}{$\,\Join\,$}$}](x,y)$}\leq\log_{\alpha}\mbox{$\mid\!\!\mbox{$x\raisebox{-0.85358pt}{$\,\Join\,$}y$}\!\!\mid$}+\log_{\beta}\mbox{$\mid\!\!x\!\!\mid$}+\log_{\beta}\mbox{$\mid\!\!y\!\!\mid$},

(3)

with
\alpha=\phi^{2\phi-1}\approx 2.933
.
This is clearly true for
x=y=\mbox{$\langle\;\rangle$}
. Otherwise, assume w.l.o.g. that
x=\mbox{$\langle t,a,u\rangle$}
and
a\leq\mbox{${\sf min}\>y$}
.
Then
\mbox{$x\raisebox{-0.85358pt}{$\,\Join\,$}y$}=\mbox{$\rm bal\>\mbox{$\langle t,a,\mbox{$u\raisebox{-0.85358pt}{$\,\Join\,$}y$}\rangle$}$}
and

\begin{array}[]{lll}\mbox{${\cal A}[\mbox{$\raisebox{-0.85358pt}{$\,\Join\,$}$}](x,y)$}&=&\mbox{${\cal T}[\mbox{$\raisebox{-0.85358pt}{$\,\Join\,$}$}](x,y)$}+\Phi{(\mbox{$x\raisebox{-0.85358pt}{$\,\Join\,$}y$})}-\Phi{(x)}-\Phi{(y)}\\[2.84526pt]
&=&1+\mbox{${\cal T}[\mbox{$\raisebox{-0.85358pt}{$\,\Join\,$}$}](u,y)$}+\Phi{(\rm bal\mbox{$\langle t,a,\mbox{$u\raisebox{-0.85358pt}{$\,\Join\,$}y$}\rangle$})}-\Phi{\mbox{$\langle t,a,u\rangle$}}-\Phi{(y)}\\[2.84526pt]
&=&\mbox{${\cal A}[\mbox{$\raisebox{-0.85358pt}{$\,\Join\,$}$}](u,y)$}+1-\varphi(t,u)+\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\varphi(t,\mbox{$u\raisebox{-0.85358pt}{$\,\Join\,$}y$}),&\mbox{if }\mbox{$\mid\!\!t\!\!\mid$}>\mbox{$\mid\!\!\mbox{$u\raisebox{-0.85358pt}{$\,\Join\,$}y$}\!\!\mid$}\\
\varphi(\mbox{$u\raisebox{-0.85358pt}{$\,\Join\,$}y$},t),&\mbox{if }\mbox{$\mid\!\!t\!\!\mid$}\leq\mbox{$\mid\!\!\mbox{$u\raisebox{-0.85358pt}{$\,\Join\,$}y$}\!\!\mid$}\end{array}\right.\\[2.84526pt]
&\leq&\mbox{${\cal A}[\mbox{$\raisebox{-0.85358pt}{$\,\Join\,$}$}](u,y)$}+1-\varphi(t,u)+\varphi(\mbox{$u\raisebox{-0.85358pt}{$\,\Join\,$}y$},t)\\[2.84526pt]
&\leq&\log_{\alpha}\mbox{$\mid\!\!\mbox{$u\raisebox{-0.85358pt}{$\,\Join\,$}y$}\!\!\mid$}+\varphi(\mbox{$u\raisebox{-0.85358pt}{$\,\Join\,$}y$},t)+\log_{\beta}\mbox{$\mid\!\!u\!\!\mid$}+1-\varphi(t,u)+\log_{\beta}\mbox{$\mid\!\!y\!\!\mid$}\\[2.84526pt]
&\leq&\log_{\alpha}\mbox{$\mid\!\!\mbox{$u\raisebox{-0.85358pt}{$\,\Join\,$}y$}\!\!\mid$}+\varphi(\mbox{$u\raisebox{-0.85358pt}{$\,\Join\,$}y$},t)+\log_{\beta}(\mbox{$\mid\!\!t\!\!\mid$}+\mbox{$\mid\!\!u\!\!\mid$})+\log_{\beta}\mbox{$\mid\!\!y\!\!\mid$}\\[2.84526pt]
&\leq&\log_{\alpha}(\mbox{$\mid\!\!t\!\!\mid$}+\mbox{$\mid\!\!\mbox{$u\raisebox{-0.85358pt}{$\,\Join\,$}y$}\!\!\mid$})+\log_{\beta}\mbox{$\mid\!\!x\!\!\mid$}+\log_{\beta}\mbox{$\mid\!\!y\!\!\mid$}\\[2.84526pt]
&=&\log_{\alpha}\mbox{$\mid\!\!\mbox{$x\raisebox{-0.85358pt}{$\,\Join\,$}y$}\!\!\mid$}+\log_{\beta}\mbox{$\mid\!\!x\!\!\mid$}+\log_{\beta}\mbox{$\mid\!\!y\!\!\mid$},\end{array}

where the first inequality follows from (
2
),
the second inequality follows from the induction hypothesis,
the third inequality follows from the definition of
\varphi
,
and the last inequality follows as it was shown in
[
KS91a
]
that

\log_{\beta}\frac{\beta n}{m+n}\leq\log_{\alpha}\frac{m+n}{m},

for all positive
m
and
n
.

With both
\mid\!\!x\!\!\mid
and
\mid\!\!y\!\!\mid
bounded by
\mid\!\!\mbox{$x\raisebox{-0.85358pt}{$\,\Join\,$}y$}\!\!\mid
, we conclude from (
3
) that

\mbox{${\cal A}[\mbox{$\raisebox{-0.85358pt}{$\,\Join\,$}$}](x,y)$}\leq\log_{\phi}\mbox{$\mid\!\!\mbox{$x\raisebox{-0.85358pt}{$\,\Join\,$}y$}\!\!\mid$},

as
\log_{\alpha}\phi+2\log_{\beta}\phi=1/(2\phi-1)+2/(\phi+2)=1
(using
\phi^{2}=\phi+1
).
With this we have proved our first main result.

Theorem 4.1

There exists a potential function
such that the amortized costs for weight-biased leftist heaps satisfy (counting comparisons):
empty
,
{\sf isempty}\>x
,
{\sf single}\>a
,
and
{\sf min}\>x
cost 0,
\mbox{${\sf meld}\>x$}\>y
costs at most
\log_{\phi}(\mbox{$\mid\!\!x\!\!\mid$}+\mbox{$\mid\!\!y\!\!\mid$})
and
{\sf delmin}\>x
costs at most
\log_{\phi}\mbox{$\mid\!\!x\!\!\mid$}
.

These bounds coincide with the bounds for skew heaps from
[
KS91a
]
. Intuitively, one may say that for weight-biased heaps,
not swapping in case the left subtree is already larger does not hurt the performance compared to skew heaps, where we always
swap subtrees.

The above bounds may now be combined with the bounds from Section
3
by taking their convex combination:

\begin{array}[]{lllll}\mbox{${\cal A}[\mbox{$\mbox{${\sf meld}$}$}](x,y)$}&\leq&\lambda\,\log_{2}\mbox{$\mid\!\!\mbox{$x\raisebox{-0.85358pt}{$\,\Join\,$}y$}\!\!\mid$}+(1-\lambda)\log_{\phi}\mbox{$\mid\!\!\mbox{$x\raisebox{-0.85358pt}{$\,\Join\,$}y$}\!\!\mid$}&\approx&(1.44-.44\lambda)\log_{2}\mbox{$\mid\!\!\mbox{$x\raisebox{-0.85358pt}{$\,\Join\,$}y$}\!\!\mid$},\\
\mbox{${\cal A}[\mbox{${\sf delmin}$}](x)$}&\leq&2\lambda\,\log_{2}\mbox{$\mid\!\!x\!\!\mid$}+(1-\lambda)\log_{\phi}\mbox{$\mid\!\!x\!\!\mid$}&\approx&(1.44+.56\lambda)\log_{2}\mbox{$\mid\!\!x\!\!\mid$},\end{array}

with

.

1

7
Concluding Remarks

We have analyzed the amortized complexity of leftist heaps. Compared to the well-known worst-case bounds,
our amortized bounds provide a more refined view of the computational complexity of leftist heaps.
Another example of such a refined amortized analysis is the analysis of insertions into AVL-trees by
Mehlhorn and Tsakalidis
[
MT86
]
. AVL-trees are height-balanced and achieve logarithmic time for the
main operations in the worst case. The amortized bounds yield a more profound understanding of the
time complexity and may also help in experimental studies of these data structures, e.g., to understand
why skew heaps and leftist heaps behave more similarly than one might anticipate.

For weight-biased heaps, we have been able to reduce the bound for
{\sf delmin}\>x

from
2\,\log_{2}\mbox{$\mid\!\!x\!\!\mid$}
to
\log_{\phi}\mbox{$\mid\!\!x\!\!\mid$}\approx 1.44\,\log_{2}\mbox{$\mid\!\!x\!\!\mid$}
.
We would like to point out that the improved bounds from
[
KS91a
]
are essential for this result.
For the potential function
\Phi{}
from
[
ST86
]
, with
\varphi(t,u)=1
if
\mbox{$\mid\!\!t\!\!\mid$}<\mbox{$\mid\!\!u\!\!\mid$}
and
\varphi(t,u)=0
otherwise, we do not get
an improvement over the worst-case bounds. Indeed, for any weight-biased heap
x
we would simply have
\Phi{(x)}=0
.

For rank-biased heaps, all potential functions considered in this paper (including the above potential function from
[
ST86
]
)
fall short of achieving any improvement over the worst-case bound for
{\sf delmin}\>x
. As explained in Section
5
,
this leaves us with an interesting open problem regarding the computational complexity of rank-biased leftist heaps:
whether the amortized cost of
{\sf delmin}\>x
is equal to
\log_{\phi}\mbox{$\mid\!\!x\!\!\mid$}
, equal to
2\,\log_{2}\mbox{$\mid\!\!x\!\!\mid$}
,
or something in between. Hopefully, innovations such as ATLAS
[
LMZ21
]
will help us in cracking this problem,
expediting the search for potential functions beating the worst-case bound for
{\sf delmin}
.

Moreover, as another interesting direction for further research (also in light of
[
BKK+23
]
),
we introduce the
randomized
leftist heaps.
This probabilistic variant is defined as in Figure
1
, but with the following balancing strategy:

\begin{array}[]{lcll}\mbox{$\rm bal\>\mbox{$\langle t,a,u\rangle$}$}&=&\mbox{$\langle t,a,u\rangle$},&\ \ \mbox{with probability $1-p$},\\
&\mbox{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{\framebox[3.00003pt]{\rule{0.0pt}{3.44444pt}}}}&\mbox{$\langle u,a,t\rangle$},&\ \ \mbox{with probability $p$},\end{array}

where
0\leq p\leq 1
is a fixed bias. For
p=1/2
, the behavior of these heaps resembles the randomized meldable heaps of
[
GM98
]
,
for which the expected costs of
\mbox{${\sf meld}\>x$}\>y
and
{\sf delmin}\>x
are bounded by
2\log_{2}\mbox{$\mid\!\!\mbox{$x\raisebox{-0.85358pt}{$\,\Join\,$}y$}\!\!\mid$}
and
2\log_{2}\mbox{$\mid\!\!x\!\!\mid$}
, respectively.
Note that these bounds coincide with the worst-case bounds for leftists heaps. For
p=1
, however, the randomized leftist heaps
reduce to skew heaps, for which we have superior amortized bounds.
Thus, the question is which value for
p
will be optimal in which sense, e.g., minimizing the expected amortized costs
and
ensuring
that the worst-case actual costs are also limited with high probability (e.g., what about
p=1/\phi
).
The results for randomized splay trees (see
[
LMZ22
]
and references therein) seem a good starting point for such an amortized analysis.

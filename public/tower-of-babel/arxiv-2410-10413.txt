Intersections of Poisson k k -flats in hyperbolic space: completing the picture
Tillmann Bühler and Daniel Hug

Abstract
In recent years there has been a lot of interest in the study of isometry invariant Poisson processes of k k -flats in d d -dimensional hyperbolic space ℍ d \mathbb{H}^{d} , for 0 ≤ k ≤ d − 1 0\leq k\leq d-1 . A phenomenon that has no counterpart in euclidean geometry arises in the investigation of the total k k -dimensional volume F r F_{r} of the process inside a spherical observation window B r B_{r} of radius r r when one lets r r tend to infinity. While F r F_{r} is asymptotically normally distributed for 2 ​ k ≤ d + 1 2k\leq d+1 , it has been shown to obey a nonstandard central limit theorem for 2 ​ k > d + 1 2k>d+1 . The intersection process of order m m , for d − m ⁡ ( d − k ) ≥ 0 d-m(d-k)\geq 0 , of the original process η \eta consists of all intersections of distinct flats E 1 , … , E m ∈ η E_{1},\ldots,E_{m}\in\eta with dim ( E 1 ∩ … ∩ E m ) = d − m ⁡ ( d − k ) \dim(E_{1}\cap\ldots\cap E_{m})=d-m(d-k) . For this intersection process, the total d − m ⁡ ( d − k ) d-m(d-k) -dimensional volume F r ( m ) F^{(m)}_{r} of the process in B r B_{r} , again as r → ∞ r\to\infty , is of particular interest. For 2 ​ k ≤ d + 1 2k\leq d+1 it has been shown that F r ( m ) F^{(m)}_{r} is again asymptotically normally distributed. For m ≥ 2 m\geq 2 , the limit is so far unknown, although it has been shown for certain d d and k k that it cannot be a normal distribution. We determine the limit distribution for all values of d , k , m d,k,m . In addition, we establish explicit rates of convergence in the Kolmogorov distance and discuss properties of the limit distribution. Furthermore we show that the asymptotic covariance matrix of the vector ( F r ( 1 ) , … , F r ( m ) ) ⊤ (F^{(1)}_{r},\ldots,F^{(m)}_{r})^{\top} has full rank when 2 ​ k < d + 1 2k<d+1 and rank one when 2 ​ k ≥ d + 1 2k\geq d+1 .

\displaystyle f^{(m)}_{r,i,\bullet}(E_{1},\ldots,E_{i})\coloneqq

\displaystyle\binom{m}{i}\frac{1}{m!}\int_{A(d,k)^{m-i}}f^{(m)}_{r,\bullet}(E_{1},\ldots,E_{i},E_{i+1},\ldots,E_{m})\,\mu_{k}^{m-i}(\dint\,(E_{i+1},\ldots,E_{m})),

\mathbb{V}(I_{i}(f^{(m)}_{r,i,\bullet}))=i!\int_{A_{\bullet}(d,k)^{i}}(f^{(m)}_{r,i,\bullet}(E_{1},\ldots,E_{i}))^{2}\,\mu^{i}_{k}(\dint\,(E_{1},\ldots,E_{i}))\eqqcolon i!\cdot A^{(m)}_{r,i,\bullet},

This implies

\mathbb{V}(F^{(m)}_{r,\bullet})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathbb{V}(I_{i}(f^{(m)}_{r,i,\bullet}))=\sum_{i=1}^{m}i!A^{(m)}_{r,i,\bullet},

which is precisely the statement of
[

By

\displaystyle f^{(m)}_{r,1,\bullet}(E)

\displaystyle=\binom{m}{1}\frac{1}{m!}\left(\frac{\omega_{d+1}}{\omega_{k+1}}\right)^{m-1}\frac{\omega_{d-m(d-k)+1}}{\omega_{d-(d-k)+1}}\mathcal{H}_{\bullet}^{d-(d-k)}(E\cap B_{r})

for

C(d,k,m)\coloneqq\frac{1}{(m-1)!}\left(\frac{\omega_{d+1}}{\omega_{k+1}}\right)^{m-1}\frac{\omega_{d-m(d-k)+1}}{\omega_{k+1}}.

(2.3)

By the definition of

we have

I_{1}(f^{(m)}_{r,1\bullet})=C(d,k,m)\left(F^{(1)}_{r,\bullet}-\mathbb{E}[F^{(1)}_{r,\bullet}]\right).

(2.4)

]
. We include it here for easier reference and comparison.
The notation

is to be understood as

Similarly, we adopt the notation

(

(

)

Lemma 1

If

,

and

3
Comparison with the euclidean case

The euclidean analogue of
Lemma

1
is

A^{(m)}_{r,i,\textbf{e}}\asymp r^{2d-i(d-k)}.

(3.1)

To prove this, use
[
9
, Eq. (2.9)]
together with the fact that

\int_{A_{\textbf{e}}(d,k)}\mathcal{H}^{k}_{\textbf{e}}(E\cap B_{r})^{l}\mu_{k}(\dint E)\asymp r^{d+k(l-1)}

for
0\leq k<d
and
l\in\mathbb{N}
.
This is the euclidean analogue of
[
25
, Lem. 8]
.
Since the proof is conceptually identical and technically easier in euclidean space, we omit it here.

Using the chaos decomposition (
2.1
) and (
2.4
), we obtain

F^{(m)}_{r,\bullet}-\mathbb{E}[F^{(m)}_{r,\bullet}]=C(d,k,m)(F^{(1)}_{r,\bullet}-\mathbb{E}[F^{(1)}_{r,\bullet}])+\sum_{i=2}^{m}I_{i}(f^{(m)}_{r,i,\bullet})

(3.2)

for
\bullet\in\{\textbf{e},\textbf{h}\}
.
For
\bullet=\textbf{e}
, it follows from (
3.1
) that the first term in this decomposition has variance of order
r^{d+k}
,
while the remaining terms have variance of order at most
r^{d+k-(d-k)}
.
Hence,
\mathbb{V}(F^{(m)}_{r,\textbf{e}})\asymp r^{d+k}
and we can write

\frac{F^{(m)}_{r,\textbf{e}}-\mathbb{E}[F^{(m)}_{r,\textbf{e}}]}{\sqrt{r^{d+k}}}=C(d,k,m)\frac{F^{(1)}_{r,\textbf{e}}-\mathbb{E}[F^{(1)}_{r,\textbf{e}}]}{\sqrt{r^{d+k}}}+W_{r},

with
W_{r}\xlongrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}0
as
r\to\infty
(note that
\mathbb{V}(W_{r})\lesssim r^{-(d-k)}
).
From these facts we can draw two conclusions:
Firstly, if
X
is a random variable with

\frac{F^{(1)}_{r,\textbf{e}}-\mathbb{E}[F^{(1)}_{r,\textbf{e}}]}{\sqrt{r^{d+k}}}\xlongrightarrow{D}X,

then (by Slutzky’s lemma)

\frac{F^{(m)}_{r,\textbf{e}}-\mathbb{E}[F^{(m)}_{r,\textbf{e}}]}{\sqrt{r^{d+k}}}\xlongrightarrow{D}C(d,k,m)X.

Secondly, the asymptotic covariance matrix

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{1}{r^{d+k}}\Sigma(F^{(1)}_{r,\textbf{e}},\ldots,F^{(m)}_{r,\textbf{e}})

\displaystyle=\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{V}(F^{(1)}_{r,\textbf{e}})}{r^{d+k}}\cdot\Big(C(d,k,a)C(d,k,b)\Big)_{a,b=1,\ldots,m}

has rank
1
.
For
k=d-1
this was shown in
[
24
, Thm. 5.1(i)]
, although the above reasoning implies that this is true for any
k<d
.
It is well known that
X
follows a normal distribution, however this is not the point we want to make here.

The behavior described above is in contrast to the hyperbolic setting:
There we have
W_{r}\xlongrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}0
only in the case
2k\geq d+1
(compare
Lemma

1
).
For
2k<d+1
, the terms
A^{(m)}_{r,1,\textbf{h}},\ldots,A^{(m)}_{r,m,\textbf{h}}
are all of the same order
e^{r(d-1)}
,
hence all terms of the chaos decomposition contribute to the asymptotic behavior.

This is reflected in the fact that for
k=d-1
, the hyperbolic analogue of the asymptotic covariance matrix has full rank for
2k<d+1
(i.e.,
d=2
)
and rank
1
for
2k\geq d+1
(i.e.,
d\geq 3
) which is shown in
[
25
, Sec. 4.5]
.
We extend this result to
k<d
in
Section

7
.

The fact that the first term of the chaos expansion determines the asymptotic behavior for
2k\geq d+1
allows us to reduce
the problem of finding the asymptotic distribution of
F^{(m)}_{r,\textbf{h}}
to the case
m=1
.
From (
3.2
) it follows almost immediately that the limit distribution of
e^{-r(k-1)}(F^{(m)}_{r,\textbf{h}}-\mathbb{E}[F^{(m)}_{r,\textbf{h}}])
differs from the one of
e^{-r(k-1)}(F^{(1)}_{r,\textbf{h}}-\mathbb{E}[F^{(1)}_{r,\textbf{h}}])
only by a constant factor.
The latter distribution has been determined for
k=d-1
in
[
32
]
and for general
k<d
in
[
9
]
.
Curiously, it is not a normal distribution.

In summary, one can say that in the hyperbolic setting there are two phenomena which lead to behavior that diverges from what we know in the euclidean setting:
Firstly, for
2k<d+1
, all terms of the chaos distribution have variance of the same order, leading to a non-degenerate covariance matrix.
Secondly, for
2k>d+1
,
F^{(1)}_{r,\textbf{h}}
follows a nonstandard central limit theorem, leading to nonstandard limits for the functionals of intersection processes
F^{(m)}_{r,\textbf{h}}
,
m\geq 2
.
Note that the second phenomenon has nothing to do with intersection processes per se, since for
m=1
no intersections are considered.

4
Determination of the limit distribution

We now determine the asymptotic distribution of
F^{m}_{r}
, thus resolving in particular the conjecture stated in
[
9
]
.
Note that the following proposition is a purely qualitative statement, convergence rates will be derived in the next section.

Proposition 2
.

If
2k>d+1
and
d-m(d-k)\geq 0
, then

\frac{F^{(m)}_{r}-\mathbb{E}[F^{(m)}_{r}]}{e^{r(k-1)}}\xlongrightarrow{D}C(d,k,m)\frac{\omega_{k}}{(k-1)2^{k-1}}Z

as
r\to\infty
, where
Z
is given by (
1.1
) and
C(d,k,m)
is given by (
2.3
).

Proof.

Since
2k>d+1
, it follows from
Lemma

1
that
A^{(m)}_{r,1}\asymp e^{2r(k-1)}
and that

A^{(m)}_{r,i}
is of lower order for
i=2,\ldots,m
, i.e.,
A^{(m)}_{r,i}/e^{2r(k-1)}\to 0,
as
r\to\infty
.
This implies that
\mathbb{V}(I_{i}(f^{(m)}_{r,i})/e^{r(k-1)})
goes to
0
for
r\to\infty
and hence

I_{i}(f^{(m)}_{r,i})/e^{r(k-1)}\xlongrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}0,\qquad i=2,\ldots,m.

Using (
3.2
), we can write

\displaystyle\frac{F^{(m)}_{r}-\mathbb{E}[F^{(m)}_{r}]}{e^{r(k-1)}}

\displaystyle=C(d,k,m)\frac{F^{(1)}_{r}-\mathbb{E}[F^{(1)}_{r}]}{e^{r(k-1)}}+\sum_{i=2}^{m}\frac{I_{i}(f^{(m)}_{r,i})}{e^{r(k-1)}}.

(4.1)

The first term converges in distribution to
C(d,k,m)\frac{\omega_{k}}{(k-1)2^{k-1}}Z
by
[
9
, Thm. 1.4]
(note that the prefactor contains a typo which we corrected here)
and the second term converges to
0
in probability by the previous consideration.
The result now follows by Slutsky’s lemma.
∎

5
Convergence rates

Having established the limit distribution, we now work a bit harder to get bounds on the speed of convergence.
Throughout this whole section, let
2k>d+1
and

We start by treating the case

.
For this purpose, let

for

.

Theorem 3
.

If

\beta_{d,k}\coloneqq\frac{2(2k-d-1)}{k+d+4},

then there is a constant
C>0
depending only on
d,k
such that

d_{K}(Y_{r},Y)\leq C\cdot\exp\left(-\beta_{d,k}\cdot r\right),

for
r>0
when

, and

5.1
Bounds on
g
and
g_{r}

To prepare the proof of
Theorem

3
, we provide further auxiliary results. We start by rewriting the function
g_{r}
.

Lemma 6
.

If
r,s\geq 0
, then

g_{r}(s)=\mathds{1}\{s\leq r\}\omega_{k}e^{-(k-1)r}\cosh^{-(k-1)}(s)\int_{s}^{r}(\sinh^{2}(u)-\sinh^{2}(s))^{\frac{k-2}{2}}\sinh(u)\,\dint u.

(5.1)

Proof.

Note that the equality is clear for
r\leq s
.
On
\{(r,s)\colon 0\leq s<r\}
application of the chain rule yields

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\int_{0}^{\arcosh\left(\frac{\cosh(r)}{\cosh(s)}\right)}\sinh^{k-1}(u)\,\dint u

\displaystyle=\sinh\left(\arcosh\left(\frac{\cosh(r)}{\cosh(s)}\right)\right)^{k-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\arcosh\left(\frac{\cosh(r)}{\cosh(s)}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\cosh^{2}(r)}{\cosh^{2}(s)}-1\right)^{\frac{k-1}{2}}\left(\frac{\cosh^{2}(r)}{\cosh^{2}(s)}-1\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\sinh(r)}{\cosh(s)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\cosh^{2}(r)-\cosh^{2}(s))^{\frac{k-2}{2}}}{\cosh^{k-1}(s)}\sinh(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{(\sinh^{2}(r)-\sinh^{2}(s))^{\frac{k-2}{2}}}{\cosh^{k-1}(s)}\sinh(r),

where we have used the identity
\cosh^{2}-\sinh^{2}=1
repreatedly.
Hence

\int_{0}^{\arcosh\left(\frac{\cosh(r)}{\cosh(s)}\right)}\sinh^{k-1}(u)\,\dint u-0=\int_{s}^{r}\frac{(\sinh^{2}(t)-\sinh^{2}(s))^{\frac{k-2}{2}}}{\cosh^{k-1}(s)}\sinh(t)\,\dint t,

which implies the assertion of the lemma.
∎

Next we show that
g_{r}(s)
approaches
g(s)
from below, for fixed
s\geq 0
, and derive a bound for the speed of convergence as
r\to\infty
.

Lemma 7
.

If
r,s\geq 0
, then

g_{r}(s)\leq g(s)

(5.2)

and

g(s)-g_{r}(s)\leq\frac{\omega_{k}}{k-1}e^{-(k-1)r}+\mathds{1}\{s\leq r\}\omega_{k}(k-1)e^{-(k-1)r-(k-3)s}\int_{s}^{r}e^{(k-3)u}\,\dint u.

(5.3)

Proof.

Since the first inequality is clear for
s>r
, assume that
s\leq r
.
From the identity (
5.1
) and the bound

(\sinh^{2}(u)-\sinh^{2}(s))^{\frac{k-2}{2}}\sinh(u)\leq\sinh(u)^{k-1}\leq 2^{-(k-1)}e^{(k-1)u}

for
0\leq s\leq u
, we see that

\displaystyle g_{r}(s)

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{k}e^{-(k-1)r}\cosh^{-(k-1)}(s)\int_{s}^{r}2^{-(k-1)}e^{(k-1)u}\,\dint u

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{k}}{2^{k-1}}e^{-(k-1)r}\cosh^{-(k-1)}(s)\frac{1}{k-1}(e^{(k-1)r}-e^{(k-1)s})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\omega_{k}}{2^{k-1}(k-1)}\cosh^{-(k-1)}(s)

\displaystyle=g(s),

which proves (
5.2
).

For
s>r
, the second inequality holds since

g(s)-g_{r}(s)=g(s)\leq g(r)=\frac{\omega_{k}}{k-1}(2\cosh(r))^{-(k-1)}\leq\frac{\omega_{k}}{k-1}e^{-(k-1)r},

where we used that
2\cosh(r)\geq e^{r}
.
Assume now that
s\leq r
.
Using relation (
5.1
),
g(s)-g_{r}(s)\geq 0
(see (
5.2
)) and
2\cosh(s)\geq e^{s}
, we obtain

\displaystyle g(s)-g_{r}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{k}}{(2\cosh(s))^{k-1}}e^{-(k-1)r}\left(\frac{e^{(k-1)r}}{k-1}-\int_{s}^{r}2^{k-1}(\sinh^{2}(u)-\sinh^{2}(s))^{\frac{k-2}{2}}\sinh(u)\,\dint u\right)

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{k}e^{-(k-1)(r+s)}\left(\frac{e^{(k-1)s}}{k-1}+\int_{s}^{r}e^{(k-1)u}-2^{k-1}(\sinh^{2}(u)-\sinh^{2}(s))^{\frac{k-2}{2}}\sinh(u)\,\dint u\right)

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{k}e^{-(k-1)(r+s)}\left(\frac{e^{(k-1)s}}{k-1}+\int_{s}^{r}(e^{2u})^{\frac{k-1}{2}}-(4\sinh^{2}(u)-4\sinh^{2}(s))^{\frac{k-1}{2}}\,\dint u\right),

(5.4)

Recall that
k\geq 3
. The mean value theorem yields

y^{\frac{k-1}{2}}-x^{\frac{k-1}{2}}\leq(y-x)\frac{k-1}{2}y^{\frac{k-3}{2}}

for
0\leq x\leq y
.
Applying this with
y=e^{2u}
and
x=4\sinh^{2}(u)-4\sinh^{2}(s)
then allows us to bound the integrand in (
5.4
) from above by

\displaystyle(e^{2u}-(4\sinh^{2}(u)-4\sinh^{2}(s)))\frac{k-1}{2}(e^{2u})^{\frac{k-3}{2}}

\displaystyle=(e^{2s}+e^{-2s}-e^{-2u})\frac{k-1}{2}e^{(k-3)u}

\displaystyle\leq(k-1)e^{2s}e^{(k-3)u}.

Plugging this bound into (
5.4
), we arrive at

\displaystyle g(s)-g_{r}(s)

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{k}e^{-(k-1)(r+s)}\left(\frac{e^{(k-1)s}}{k-1}+\int_{s}^{r}(k-1)e^{2s}e^{(k-3)u}\,\dint u\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{k}}{k-1}e^{-(k-1)r}+\omega_{k}(k-1)e^{-(k-1)r-(k-3)s}\int_{s}^{r}e^{(k-3)u}\,\dint u,

which concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 8
.

There exists some constant
C_{d,k}>0
such that, for any

s_{0},r\geq 0
,

\int_{0}^{s_{0}}\left\lvert g(s)-g_{r}(s)\right\rvert\,\mu(\dint s)\leq C_{d,k}(e^{-(k-1)r+(d-1)s_{0}}+e^{-2r+(d-k+2)s_{0}})

when
k\geq 4
and

\int_{0}^{s_{0}}\left\lvert g(s)-g_{r}(s)\right\rvert\,\mu(\dint s)\leq C_{d,k}(e^{-(k-1)r+(d-1)s_{0}}+re^{-2r+(d-k+2)s_{0}})

in the case
k=3
(and thus
d=4
), as well as

\int_{s_{0}}^{\infty}\left\lvert g_{r}(s)\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert g(s)\right\rvert^{2}\,\mu(\dint s)\leq C_{d,k}e^{-(2k-d-1)s_{0}},

for any
k,d
.

Proof.

For
r\geq 0
we define
h_{3}(r):=r
and
h_{k}(r):=1
when
k\geq 4
. Then

\int_{s}^{r}e^{(k-3)u}\,\dint u\leq h_{k}(r)e^{(k-3)r}

for all
k\geq 3
and
s,r\geq 0
.
Combining this with the fact that
g(s)-g_{r}(s)\geq 0
(see (
5.2
)), relation (
5.3
)
and the bound
\mu(\dint s)\leq e^{(d-1)s}\,\dint s
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s_{0}}\left\lvert g(s)-g_{r}(s)\right\rvert\,\mu(\dint s)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{s_{0}}\frac{\omega_{k}}{k-1}e^{-(k-1)r}+\omega_{k}(k-1)e^{-2r-(k-3)s}h_{k}(r)\,\mu(\dint s)

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{k}}{k-1}e^{-(k-1)r}\int_{0}^{s_{0}}\,\mu(\dint s)+\omega_{k}(k-1)e^{-2r}h_{k}(r)\int_{0}^{s_{0}}e^{-(k-3)s}\,\mu(\dint s)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\omega_{k}}{k-1}e^{-(k-1)r}\frac{1}{d-1}e^{(d-1)s_{0}}+\omega_{k}(k-1)e^{-2r}h_{k}(r)\frac{1}{d-k+2}e^{(d-k+2)s_{0}},

from which the first two claims follow immediately.

For the last bound, simply observe that (
5.2
) implies
\left\lvert g_{r}(s)\right\rvert^{2}\leq\left\lvert g(s)\right\rvert^{2}
and thus

\displaystyle\int_{s_{0}}^{\infty}\left\lvert g_{r}(s)\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert g(s)\right\rvert^{2}\,\mu(\dint s)

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{s_{0}}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\omega_{k}}{k-1}(2\cosh(s))^{-(k-1)}\right)^{2}\,\mu(\dint s)

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{s_{0}}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\omega_{k}}{k-1}e^{-(k-1)s}\right)^{2}e^{(d-1)s}\,\dint s

\displaystyle=\frac{2\omega_{k}^{2}}{(k-1)^{2}(2k-d-1)}e^{-(2k-d-1)s_{0}},

where we used that
2\cosh(r)\geq e^{r}
and
\mu(\dint s)\leq e^{(d-1)s}\dint s
in the second line.
∎

5.2
Proof of Theorem
3

In order to apply
Theorem

5
to the random variables
Y_{r}
and
Y
, we first need to establish that
Y
has bounded density.

Lemma 9
.

The distribution of
Y
has bounded density.

Proof.

Note that
Y
is a multiple of
Z
, so it suffices to show that
Z
has a bounded density.
The argument we use appears in
[
43
]
, we include it here since it does not take up a lot of lines and might offer the reader some insight.
In
[
9
, Rem. 1.5]
, it is established that
Z
is infinitely divisible without Gaussian component and further that its Lévy measure
\nu
is concentrated on
(0,1)
with Lebesgue density

\rho(y)=\frac{\omega_{d-k}}{k-1}y^{-\frac{d+k-2}{k-1}}\left(1-y^{\frac{2}{k-1}}\right)^{\frac{d-k}{2}-1},\qquad y\in(0,1).

Hence there is a constant
c>0
such that

\int_{0}^{s}y^{2}\nu(\dint y)\geq c\,s^{\frac{2k-d-1}{k-1}}\geq c\,s

(5.5)

for all
s\in(0,1/2)
.

By the Lévy–Khintchine formula, the characteristic function of
Z
satisfies

-\log|\varphi_{Z}(t)|=\int_{0}^{1}(1-\cos(ty))\,\nu(\dint y),\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}.

Using (
5.5
) and the inequality
1-\cos(x)\geq x^{2}/4
,
x\in[0,1]
, we arrive at

\displaystyle-\log|\varphi_{Z}(t)|\geq\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{|t|}}(1-\cos(ty))\,\nu(\dint y)\geq\frac{1}{4}t^{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{|t|}}y^{2}\,\nu(\dint y)\geq\frac{c}{4}|t|,

for
|t|\geq 2
.
Hence
|\varphi_{Z}(t)|\leq\exp(-\frac{c}{4}|t|)
holds for
t\geq 2
and by the inversion formula (see, e.g.,
[
16
, Thm. 3.3.14]
)

admits a bounded probability density.
∎

We can now apply
Theorem

5
and obtain that for any
r>0
and
T>0
it holds that

d_{K}(Y_{r},Y)\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-T}^{T}\frac{\left\lvert\varphi_{r}(t)-\varphi(t)\right\rvert}{\left\lvert t\right\rvert}\,\dint t+\frac{24M}{\pi T},

(5.6)

where
M>0
is a constant that depends only on
d,k
.

To proceed, we derive bounds for the first term on the right-hand side of relation (
5.6
).
First, we define
q\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
,
x\mapsto e^{ix}-1-ix
, which allows us to express the characteristic functions as

\varphi_{r}(t)=\exp\left(\omega_{d-k}\int_{0}^{\infty}q(tg_{r}(s))\,\mu(\dint s)\right),\qquad\varphi(t)=\exp\left(\omega_{d-k}\int_{0}^{\infty}q(tg(s))\,\mu(\dint s)\right),

for
r>0
.
Note that
\Real(q(x))\leq 0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
For complex numbers
z,w
with real part
\leq 0
, it holds that

\left\lvert e^{z}-e^{w}\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert z-w\right\rvert.

(5.7)

(To see this, let
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to\mathbb{C},t\mapsto\exp(w+t(z-w))
.
Note that
\Real(w+t(z-w))\leq 0
for all
t\in[0,1]
, hence the fundamental theorem of calculus and the triangle inequality give

\left\lvert e^{z}-e^{w}\right\rvert\leq\int_{0}^{1}\left\lvert\gamma^{\prime}(t)\right\rvert\dint t=\int_{0}^{1}\left\lvert(z-w)\exp(w+t(z-w))\right\rvert\dint t\leq\left\lvert z-w\right\rvert
.)
Applying this to the characteristic functions and using the triangle inequality, we obtain

\left\lvert\varphi_{r}(t)-\varphi(t)\right\rvert\leq\omega_{d-k}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\lvert q(tg_{r}(s))-q(tg(s))\right\rvert\,\mu(\dint s),

(5.8)

for
r>0
.

Lemma 10
.

If
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
, then
\left\lvert q(y)-q(x)\right\rvert\leq 2\left\lvert y-x\right\rvert
and
\left\lvert q(x)\right\rvert\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\lvert x\right\rvert^{2}
.

Proof.

From (
5.7
) we get

\left\lvert q(y)-q(x)\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert e^{iy}-e^{ix}\right\rvert+\left\lvert iy-ix\right\rvert\leq 2\left\lvert y-x\right\rvert,

which proves the first assertion.
The second inequality follows from
[
36
, Lem. 6.15]
.
∎

For an arbitrary
s_{0}>0
, combining (
5.8
) and
Lemma

10
yields

\displaystyle\left\lvert\varphi_{r}(t)-\varphi(t)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq 2\omega_{d-k}\left\lvert t\right\rvert\int_{0}^{s_{0}}\left\lvert g_{r}(s)-g(s)\right\rvert\,\mu(\dint s)

(5.9)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\omega_{d-k}\left\lvert t\right\rvert^{2}\int_{s_{0}}^{\infty}\left\lvert g_{r}(s)\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert g(s)\right\rvert^{2}\,\mu(\dint s),

for
r>0
.

For the sake of readability, we will from now on simply write
C
for a universal positive constant which may depend on
d,k
.
Constants may also change from line to line.

Using (
5.9
) and
Lemma

8
, the first term of (
5.6
) can be bounded for any
s_{0}\geq 0
and
r>0
by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-T}^{T}\frac{\left\lvert\varphi_{r}(t)-\varphi(t)\right\rvert}{\left\lvert t\right\rvert}\,\dint t

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{-T}^{T}\int_{0}^{s_{0}}\left\lvert g_{r}(s)-g(s)\right\rvert\,\mu(\dint s)\,\dint t+C\int_{-T}^{T}\left\lvert t\right\rvert\int_{s_{0}}^{\infty}\left\lvert g_{r}(s)\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert g(s)\right\rvert^{2}\,\mu(\dint s)\,\dint t

\displaystyle\leq CT\int_{0}^{s_{0}}\left\lvert g_{r}(s)-g(s)\right\rvert\,\mu(\dint s)+CT^{2}\int_{s_{0}}^{\infty}\left\lvert g_{r}(s)\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert g(s)\right\rvert^{2}\,\mu(\dint s)

\displaystyle\leq CT(e^{-(k-1)r+(d-1)s_{0}}+h_{k}(r)e^{-2r+(d-k+2)s_{0}})+CT^{2}e^{-(2k-d-1)s_{0}},

where
h_{3}(r)=r
and
h_{k}(r)=1
for
k\geq 4
(as in the proof of Lemma
8
).
The Kolmogorov distance can thus be bounded from above by

d_{K}(Y_{r},Y)\leq CT(e^{-(k-1)r+(d-1)s_{0}}+h_{k}(r)e^{-2r+(d-k+2)s_{0}})+CT^{2}e^{-(2k-d-1)s_{0}}+CT^{-1}.

(5.10)

It remains to find suitable values for
T
and
s_{0}
to balance out this expression.

We take the following approach:
Let
\alpha,\beta>0
be constants and set

s_{0}=\alpha r,\qquad T=e^{\beta r}.

Plugging this into (
5.10
), we obtain

\displaystyle d_{K}(Z_{r},Z)

\displaystyle\leq C(e^{\beta r-(k-1)r+(d-1)\alpha r}+h_{k}(r)e^{\beta r-2r+(d-k+2)\alpha r})+Ce^{2\beta r-(2k-d-1)\alpha r}+Ce^{-\beta r}

\displaystyle=C(e^{[\beta-(k-1)+(d-1)\alpha]r}+h_{k}(r)e^{[\beta-2+(d-k+2)\alpha]r}+e^{[2\beta-(2k-d-1)\alpha]r}+e^{-\beta r}).

To get an optimal rate of convergence, we solve the following minimization problem:

\min_{\alpha,\beta>0}\max\begin{Bmatrix}[r]\beta&+(d-1)\alpha&-(k-1),\\
\beta&+(d-k+2)\alpha&-2,\\
2\beta&-(2k-d-1)\alpha,\\
-\beta\end{Bmatrix}.

This problem can be transformed into a linear optimization problem, which can then be solved using standard procedures.
The crucial first step is to rewrite the problem as

\min t\colon\qquad\left\{\begin{array}[]{llrr}t&\geq\beta&+(d-1)\alpha&-(k-1)\\
t&\geq\beta&+(d-k+2)\alpha&-2\\
t&\geq 2\beta&-(2k-d-1)\alpha&\\
t&\geq-\beta&&\end{array}\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad\alpha,\beta>0,

bringing this into standard form then is straightforward.

Since the problem is reasonably small, one can also solve it directly by hand.
If one chooses the second option, it is helpful to ignore the first term at the start.
Only optimizing the last three leads to

\alpha^{\ast}=\frac{6}{k+d+4},\qquad\beta^{\ast}=\frac{2(2k-d-1)}{k+d+4},

and an optimum of
-\beta^{\ast}
.
(At
(\alpha^{\ast},\beta^{\ast})
, the three terms coincide.
If one moves from
(\alpha^{\ast},\beta^{\ast})
in any direction, then at least one of the terms increases, hence this pair yields the optimum.)
Plugging
\alpha^{\ast}
and
\beta^{\ast}
into the first term gives a value
\leq-\beta^{\ast}
, hence one has obtained a solution of the full problem.

5.3

.

.

\displaystyle d_{K}\left(\frac{F^{(m)}_{r}-\mathbb{E}[F^{(m)}_{r}]}{e^{r(k-1)}},C(d,k,m)\frac{\omega_{k}}{(k-1)2^{k-1}}Z\right)

.

∎

We now prepare the proof of

11
.
Recall from (

) that

\displaystyle\frac{F^{(m)}_{r}-\mathbb{E}[F^{(m)}_{r}]}{e^{r(k-1)}}

\displaystyle=C(d,k,m)\frac{F^{(1)}_{r}-\mathbb{E}[F^{(1)}_{r}]}{e^{r(k-1)}}+\sum_{i=2}^{m}\frac{I_{i}(f^{(m)}_{r,i})}{e^{r(k-1)}}

From

1
, it follows that

\mathbb{V}(W_{r})\lesssim\frac{A^{(m)}_{r,2}}{e^{2r(k-1)}}\asymp\frac{1}{e^{2r(k-1)}}\cdot\begin{cases}e^{r(d-1)}&:\,4(d-k)>d-1,\\
re^{r(d-1)}&:\,4(d-k)=d-1,\\
e^{2r(d-2(d-k)-1)}&:\,4(d-k)<d-1.\end{cases}

Further recall that
I_{i}(f^{(m)}_{r,i})
is centered and hence
\mathbb{E}[W_{r}]=0
and
\mathbb{V}(W_{r})=\mathbb{E}[W_{r}^{2}]
.
Using this fact and the above bound, we obtain

\mathbb{E}[W_{r}^{2}]\lesssim\begin{cases}e^{-(2k-d-1)r}&:\,4k<3d+1,\\
re^{-(2k-d-1)r}&:\,4k=3d+1,\\
e^{-2(d-k)r}&:\,4k>3d+1.\end{cases}

(5.12)

To bound the contribution of
W_{r}
to the Kolmogorov distance, we will use the following bound (for a related result, see
[
31
, Lem. 2.1]
):

Lemma 13
.

Let
X,W,Z
be random variables.
Assume that
Z
has bounded density
f_{Z}
.
If
\varepsilon>0
, then

d_{K}(X+W,Z)\leq d_{K}(X,Z)+\varepsilon\left\lVert f_{Z}\right\rVert_{\infty}+\mathbb{P}(\left\lvert W\right\rvert>\varepsilon).

If
\mathbb{E}[W^{2}]<\infty
, it holds that

d_{K}(X+W,Z)\leq d_{K}(X,Z)+(\left\lVert f_{Z}\right\rVert_{\infty}+1)\mathbb{E}[W^{2}]^{\frac{1}{3}}.

Proof.

For
\varepsilon>0
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, first observe that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(X+W\leq t)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(X+W\leq t,\left\lvert W\right\rvert\leq\varepsilon)+\mathbb{P}(X+W\leq t,\left\lvert W\right\rvert>\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}(X-\varepsilon\leq t)+\mathbb{P}(\left\lvert W\right\rvert>\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=F_{X}(t+\varepsilon)+\mathbb{P}(\left\lvert W\right\rvert>\varepsilon),

hence

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(X+W\leq t)-\mathbb{P}(Z\leq t)

\displaystyle\quad\leq F_{X}(t+\varepsilon)-F_{Z}(t)+\mathbb{P}(\left\lvert W\right\rvert>\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\left\lvert F_{X}(t+\varepsilon)-F_{Z}(t+\varepsilon)\right\rvert+\left\lvert F_{Z}(t+\varepsilon)-F_{Z}(t)\right\rvert+\mathbb{P}(\left\lvert W\right\rvert>\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\quad\leq d_{K}(X,Z)+\varepsilon\left\lVert f_{Z}\right\rVert_{\infty}+\mathbb{P}(\left\lvert W\right\rvert>\varepsilon).

The bound in the other direction is achieved analogously.
First, argue that

\mathbb{P}(X+W\leq t)\geq F_{X}(t-\varepsilon)-\mathbb{P}(\left\lvert W\right\rvert>\varepsilon),

then proceed as above.

For the second statement, set
\varepsilon\coloneqq\mathbb{E}[W^{2}]^{\frac{1}{3}}
.
An application of the Markov inequality to the first bound gives

d_{K}(X+W,Z)\leq d_{K}(X,Z)+\varepsilon\left\lVert f_{Z}\right\rVert_{\infty}+\frac{\mathbb{E}[W^{2}]}{\varepsilon^{2}}=d_{K}(X,Z)+(\left\lVert f_{Z}\right\rVert_{\infty}+1)\mathbb{E}[W^{2}]^{\frac{1}{3}},

which proves the remaining assertion.
∎

We now have all prerequisites to prove the main theorem of this section.

Proof of
Theorem

11
.

Note that
C(d,k,m)\frac{\omega_{k}}{(k-1)2^{k-1}}Z
has bounded density by
Lemma

9
.
We can thus apply
Lemma

13
to (
5.11
), which yields

\displaystyle d_{K}\left(\frac{F^{(m)}_{r}-\mathbb{E}[F^{(m)}_{r}]}{e^{r(k-1)}},C(d,k,m)\frac{\omega_{k}}{(k-1)2^{k-1}}Z\right)

\displaystyle\leq d_{K}\left(C(d,k,m)\cdot Y_{r},C(d,k,m)\frac{\omega_{k}}{(k-1)2^{k-1}}Z\right)+C\cdot\mathbb{E}[W_{r}^{2}]^{\frac{1}{3}}

for some constant
C>0
that depends on
d
and
k
.
(Technically,
C
depends on
m
as well, but we can get rid of this dependence by taking the maximum over all admissible
m
.)
Using the fact that
d_{K}(aU,aV)=d_{K}(U,V)
, for any random variables
U,V
and any constant
a\neq 0
, and writing

Y=\frac{\omega_{k}}{(k-1)2^{k-1}}Z
, we get

d_{K}\left(\frac{F^{(m)}_{r}-\mathbb{E}[F^{(m)}_{r}]}{e^{r(k-1)}},C(d,k,m)\frac{\omega_{k}}{(k-1)2^{k-1}}Z\right)\leq d_{K}\left(Y_{r},Y\right)+C\cdot\mathbb{E}[W_{r}^{2}]^{\frac{1}{3}}.

The claim now follows from
Theorem

3
and (
5.12
).
∎

6
Numerical calculations

To better understand the limit distribution for
2k>d+1
, we would like to calculate its probability density.
For this purpose, write
\psi_{d,k}
for the characteristic function of
Z=Z_{d,k}
, that is

\psi_{d,k}(t)=\exp\left(\omega_{d-k}\int_{0}^{\infty}(e^{ith(s)}-1-ith(s))\,\mu(\dint s)\right),\qquad t\in\mathbb{R},

where
h(s)=\cosh^{-(k-1)}(s)
for
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

From the characteristic function, we can determine the cumulants and thus the moments of
Z_{d,k}
(see
[
9
, Rem. 4.2]
),
in particular we see that the mean is zero and that the variance is given by

\sigma_{d,k}^{2}\coloneqq\mathbb{V}(Z_{d,k})=\omega_{d-k}\int_{0}^{\infty}\cosh^{2-k}(s)\sinh^{d-k-1}(s)\dint s.

To compare the limit distributions for different values of
d
and
k
, it is useful to consider the standardized random variable

Z_{d,k}^{*}\coloneqq Z_{d,k}/\sigma_{d,k}
.
The corresponding characteristic function
\widetilde{\psi}_{d,k}
is given by

\widetilde{\psi}_{d,k}(t)=\psi_{d,k}(t/\sigma_{d,k}),\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}.

The density
f_{d,k}
of
Z_{d,k}^{*}
can be obtained from the classical inversion formula (see
[
16
, Thm. 3.3.14]
)

f_{d,k}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-itx}\widetilde{\psi}_{d,k}(t)\dint t,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}.

(6.1)

The integrability of the characteristic function and thus the applicability of the inversion formula was already established
in
Lemma

9
.
Since it does not seem to be possible to express this integral by elementary functions, we will approximate the right-hand side of (
6.1
) numerically.

From the considerations in
Lemma

9
it follows that
\widetilde{\psi}_{d,k}
decays exponentially in
t
,
so it makes sense to approximate the integral on the right-hand side of (
6.1
) by integrating over a bounded interval:

f_{d,k}(x)\approx\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-T}^{T}e^{-itx}\widetilde{\psi}_{d,k}(t)\dint t.

To discretize the integral, we take
M+1\in\mathbb{N}
equidistant points
t_{j}=\left(-1+\frac{2j}{M}\right)T
,
j=0,\ldots,M
, and
use the left point approximation
\int_{a}^{b}g(t)\dint t\approx(b-a)g(a)
:

\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-T}^{T}e^{-itx}\widetilde{\psi}_{d,k}(t)\dint t=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{j=0}^{M-1}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}e^{-itx}\widetilde{\psi}_{d,k}(t)\dint t\approx\frac{T}{\pi M}\sum_{j=0}^{M-1}e^{-it_{j}x}\widetilde{\psi}_{d,k}(t_{j}).

The evaluation of
\widetilde{\psi}_{d,k}(t)
comes with some difficulties.
The characteristic function itself contains an integral that needs to be numerically approximated first.
In practice this turned out to be challenging for the computer, so we took a different approach.

To approximate

\widetilde{\psi}_{d,k}(t)=\exp\left(\omega_{d-k}\int_{0}^{\infty}(e^{ith(s)/\sigma_{d,k}}-1-ith(s)/\sigma_{d,k})\,\mu(\dint s)\right),

we expand the exponent and interchange summation and integration to get

\displaystyle\omega_{d-k}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{(ith(s)/\sigma_{d,k})^{n}}{n!}\,\mu(\dint s)

\displaystyle=\omega_{d-k}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{(it/\sigma_{d,k})^{n}}{n!}\cosh^{k-n(k-1)}(s)\sinh^{d-k-1}(s)\,\dint s

\displaystyle=\omega_{d-k}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{(it/\sigma_{d,k})^{n}}{n!}\int_{0}^{\infty}\cosh^{k-n(k-1)}(s)\sinh^{d-k-1}(s)\,\dint s

for the argument of the exponential function.
The application of the dominated convergence theorem in the last equality is justified since
2k>d+1
implies

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\cosh^{k-n(k-1)}(s)\sinh^{d-k-1}(s)\,\dint s

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(e^{s}/2\right)^{(k-n(k-1))}\left(e^{s}/2\right)^{(d-k-1)}\,\dint s

\displaystyle\leq 2^{n(k-1)-d+1}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-s}\,\dint s\leq\left(2^{k-1}\right)^{n}

for all
n\geq 2
.

As pointed out in
[
9
, Rem. 4.2]
, for
-a>b>-1
it holds that

I(a,b)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{\infty}\cosh^{a}(s)\sinh^{b}(s)\dint s=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Gamma(-\frac{a+b}{2})\Gamma(\frac{b+1}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{1-a}{2})}.

With this identity and the definition of
\sigma_{d,k}
, we can rewrite the above expression as

\displaystyle\omega_{d-k}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{(it)^{n}}{n!}(\omega_{d-k}I(2-k,d-k-1))^{-\frac{n}{2}}I(k-n(k-1),d-k-1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{(it)^{n}}{n!}\omega_{d-k}^{1-\frac{n}{2}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{2k-d-1}{2})\Gamma(\frac{d-k}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{k-1}{2})}\right)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n(k-1)+1-d}{2})\Gamma(\frac{d-k}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{n(k-1)+1-k}{2})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{(it)^{n}}{n!}\left(\frac{1}{2}\Gamma\left(\frac{d-k}{2}\right)\omega_{d-k}\right)^{1-\frac{n}{2}}\left(\frac{\Gamma(\frac{2k-d-1}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{k-1}{2})}\right)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n(k-1)+1-d}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{n(k-1)+1-k}{2})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{(it)^{n}}{n!}\left(\pi^{\frac{d-k}{2}}\right)^{1-\frac{n}{2}}\left(\frac{\Gamma(\frac{k-1}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{2k-d-1}{2})}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n(k-1)+1-d}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{n(k-1)+1-k}{2})}.

We then approximate this sum by then
N
-th partial sum for some integer
N\geq 2
.
Note that for higher values of
t
one might have to choose
N
larger to get a sufficient accuracy.

From the above calculation it also follows that for
n\geq 2
the cumulants of
Z_{d,k}^{*}
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\cum_{n}(Z_{d,k}^{*})

\displaystyle=(-i)^{n}\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial t^{n}}\log\widetilde{\psi}_{d,k}(t)\big|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=\left(\pi^{\frac{d-k}{2}}\right)^{1-\frac{n}{2}}\left(\frac{\Gamma(\frac{k-1}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{2k-d-1}{2})}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n(k-1)+1-d}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{n(k-1)+1-k}{2})},

(6.2)

which will be useful later.

There are three constants that need to be chosen for the approximation, namely
T,M
and
N
.
Finding appropriate values can be achieved by a trial and error approach.
We only calculated characteristic functions and densities for
d\leq 15
, since after that point the calculations could not
be carried out accurately enough by the computer anymore.
For those values of
d
(and
\frac{d+1}{2}<k\leq d-1
) the choices
T=10
,
M=200
and
N=26
have proven to be appropriate.

Figure 1
:
Left: Real part (red) and imaginary part (blue) of
\widetilde{\psi}_{d,k}
.
The dotted line is the (entirely real) characteristic function of a standard normal distribution.
Right: Density of
Z_{d,k}^{*}
(red) and density of a standard normal distribution (dotted).

The illustrations in
Figure

1
might lead one to the following conjecture:
If one lets
d
go to infinity and takes
k\approx d/2
, then the distribution will converge to a standard normal distribution, while
for
k\approx d
, it will diverge.
The behavior of the cumulants, on the other hand, does not seem to support this hypothesis.
It is easy to see that the first cumulant is
0
and since
Z_{d,k}/\sigma_{d,k}
has variance
1
, the second cumulant is equal to
1
,
so the first two cumulants always agree with the ones of a standard normal distribution.
However, for
n\geq 3
, the
n
-th cumulant of a standard normal distribution is zero, while the ones of
Z_{d,k}/\sigma_{d,k}

turn out to diverge as
d
approaches infinity, independently of how
k
is chosen.

Proposition 14
.

Let
(d_{j}),(k_{j})
be sequences of integers that satisfy
0\leq k_{j}\leq d_{j}-1
,
2k_{j}>d_{j}+1

and
d_{j}\to\infty
,
j\to\infty
.
It then holds that as
j\to\infty
,

\cum_{n}(Z_{d_{j},k_{j}}^{*})\to\infty,

for all
n\geq 3
.

Proof.

Fix
n\geq 3
.
For the sake of notational convenience we will sometimes drop the index
j
.

If
d_{j}-k_{j}=2c
is constant, we can use the fact that
\Gamma(x+y)/\Gamma(x)\sim x^{y}
for fixed
y>0
and
x\to\infty

(see, e.g.,
[
30
, p. 938]
) and (
6.2
) to conclude that as
j\to\infty

\displaystyle\cum_{n}(Z_{d,k}^{*})

\displaystyle=\left(\pi^{\frac{d-k}{2}}\right)^{1-\frac{n}{2}}\left(\frac{\Gamma(\frac{2k-d-1}{2}+c)}{\Gamma(\frac{2k-d-1}{2})}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n(k-1)+1-d}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{n(k-1)+1-d}{2}+c)}

\displaystyle\sim\left(\pi^{c}\right)^{1-\frac{n}{2}}(\tfrac{2k-d-1}{2})^{c\frac{n}{2}}(\tfrac{n(k-1)+1-d}{2})^{-c}

\displaystyle=\left(\pi^{c}\right)^{1-\frac{n}{2}}\left(\frac{\left(\frac{d-4c-1}{2}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}}{\left(\frac{n(d-2c-1)+1-d}{2}\right)}\right)^{c},

which diverges since
n\geq 3
and
d_{j}\to\infty
. In particular, this proves the assertion if
d_{j}-k_{j}\in\{1,\ldots,5\}
. In the following we can therefore assume that
d_{j}-k_{j}\geq 6
.

The Gamma function satisfies
\Gamma(x+1)=x\Gamma(x)
for
x>0
and is increasing on
[2,\infty)
.
We can thus bound the cumulants given in (
6.2
) from below by

\displaystyle\cum_{n}(Z_{d,k}^{*})

\displaystyle\geq\left(\pi^{\frac{d-k}{2}}\right)^{1-\frac{n}{2}}\left(\frac{\Gamma(\frac{2k-d-1}{2}+\lfloor\frac{d-k}{2}\rfloor)}{\Gamma(\frac{2k-d-1}{2})}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n(k-1)+1-d}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{n(k-1)+1-d}{2}+\lceil\frac{d-k}{2}\rceil)}

\displaystyle=\left(\pi^{\frac{d-k}{2}}\right)^{1-\frac{n}{2}}\prod_{j=0}^{\lfloor\frac{d-k}{2}\rfloor-1}\left(\tfrac{2k-d-1}{2}+j\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}\prod_{j=0}^{\lceil\frac{d-k}{2}\rceil-1}\frac{1}{\left(\frac{n(k-1)+1-d}{2}+j\right)}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\pi^{\frac{d-k}{2}}\right)^{1-\frac{n}{2}}\prod_{j=0}^{\lfloor\frac{d-k}{2}\rfloor-1}\frac{\left(\frac{2k-d-1}{2}+j\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}}{\left(\frac{n(k-1)+1-d}{2}+j\right)}\cdot\frac{1}{\left(\frac{n(k-1)+1-k}{2}\right)}.

(6.3)

For
k_{j}\geq\frac{3}{4}d_{j}
and
d_{j}\geq 4
we can then plug the bound

\prod_{j=0}^{\lfloor\frac{d-k}{2}\rfloor-1}\frac{\left(\frac{2k-d-1}{2}+j\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}}{\left(\frac{n(k-1)+1-d}{2}+j\right)}\geq\prod_{j=0}^{\lfloor\frac{d-k}{2}\rfloor-1}\frac{\left(\frac{2k-d-1}{2}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}}{\left(\frac{n(k-1)+1-k}{2}\right)}\geq\left(\frac{(\frac{1}{8}d)^{\frac{n}{2}}}{nd}\right)^{\lfloor\frac{d-k}{2}\rfloor}=\left(\frac{d^{\frac{n}{2}-1}}{8^{\frac{n}{2}}n}\right)^{\lfloor\frac{d-k}{2}\rfloor}

into (
6.3
) and use
d^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\geq d^{\frac{1}{2}}
to get

\cum_{n}(Z_{d,k}^{*})\geq\left(\pi^{1-\frac{n}{2}}\frac{d^{\frac{1}{2}}}{8^{\frac{n}{2}}n}\right)^{\lfloor\frac{d-k}{2}\rfloor}\cdot\frac{\pi^{1-\frac{n}{2}}}{\left(\frac{(n-1)(k-1)}{2}\right)}\geq\left(\pi^{1-\frac{n}{2}}\frac{d^{\frac{1}{2}}}{8^{\frac{n}{2}}n}\right)^{\lfloor\frac{d-k}{2}\rfloor}\cdot\frac{\pi^{1-\frac{n}{2}}}{nd},

which goes to infinity as
d_{j}\to\infty
since
\lfloor\frac{d-k}{2}\rfloor\geq 3
.

For
k_{j}\leq\frac{3}{4}d_{j}
and
d_{j}\geq 16
, we use (
6.3
) to derive the lower bound

\displaystyle\cum(Z_{d,k}^{*})

\displaystyle\geq\left(\pi^{\frac{d-k}{2}}\right)^{1-\frac{n}{2}}\prod_{j=0}^{\lfloor\frac{d-k}{2}\rfloor-1}\frac{\left(\frac{1}{2}+j\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}}{\left(\frac{n(k-1)+1-k}{2}\right)}\cdot\frac{1}{\left(\frac{n(k-1)+1-k}{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\pi^{\frac{d-k}{2}}\right)^{1-\frac{n}{2}}\frac{\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(\lfloor\frac{d-k}{2}\rfloor-1\right)!\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}}{\left(\frac{(n-1)(k-1)}{2}\right)^{\lfloor\frac{d-k}{2}\rfloor+1}}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\pi^{\frac{d-k}{2}}\right)^{1-\frac{n}{2}}2^{-\frac{n}{2}}\frac{\left(\frac{\lfloor\frac{d-k}{2}\rfloor-1}{e}\right)^{(\lfloor\frac{d-k}{2}\rfloor-1)\frac{n}{2}}}{\left(nd\right)^{\lfloor\frac{d-k}{2}\rfloor+1}}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\pi^{1-\frac{n}{2}}\frac{\left(\frac{\frac{d}{8}-2}{e}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}}{nd}\right)^{\lfloor\frac{d-k}{2}\rfloor-1}2^{-\frac{n}{2}}\pi^{2(1-\frac{n}{2})}\frac{1}{(nd)^{2}},

where we used the fact that
m!\geq(m/e)^{m}
for
m\in\mathbb{N}
in the third line.
For sufficiently large
d_{j}
, the above expression is lower bounded by

\left(A\cdot d^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{\lfloor\frac{d}{8}\rfloor-1}\cdot\frac{B}{d^{2}}

with some constants
A,B>0
(depending on
n
).
The latter expression goes to infinity as
d_{j}\to\infty
.
∎

Of course, this does not yet prove that
Z_{d,k}^{*}
does not converge in distribution to a normal distribution (or at all).
It is indeed possible for a sequence of functions to converge even though, when written as a power series, all of the coefficients diverge (except for the coefficient of
x^{0}
).
This is the case, e.g., for
f_{n}(x)=\sin(n^{2}x)/n+\cos(n^{2}x)/n
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
Unfortunately, we were not able to determine whether the characteristic functions converge or not and leave this question as an open problem.

Question
.

Let
(d_{j}),(k_{j})
be sequences of integers satisfying
0\leq k_{j}\leq d_{j}-1
,
2k_{j}>d_{j}+1

and
d_{j}\to\infty
,
j\to\infty
.
What is the asymptotic behavior of
Z_{d_{j},k_{j}}^{*}
as
j\to\infty
?
In particular, are there any conditions on
d_{j}
and
k_{j}
(such as
k_{j}/d_{j}\to 1/2
) which ensure that
Z_{d_{j},k_{j}}^{*}
converges in distribution, and if so, is the limit distribution Gaussian?

7
Asymptotic covariance matrix

Consider the random vector
F^{[m]}_{r}\coloneqq(F^{(1)}_{r},\ldots,F^{(m)}_{r})^{\top}
.
In this section we study the asymptotic covariance matrix
\Sigma^{[m]}
of
F^{[m]}_{r}
, which we define as

\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{1}{e^{r(d-1)}}\Sigma(F^{[m]}_{r}),\quad\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{1}{re^{r(d-1)}}\Sigma(F^{[m]}_{r}),\quad\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{1}{e^{2r(k-1)}}\Sigma(F^{[m]}_{r}),

for
2k<d+1
,
2k=d+1
and
2k>d+1
, respectively.

Proposition 15
.

For
m\geq 2
the following holds:
If
2k\geq d+1
, then
\Sigma^{[m]}
has rank one, while it has full rank when
2k<d+1
.

Remark 16
.

Note that
m
must satisfy the condition
d-m(d-k)\geq 0
.
One easily checks that the only combinations for
m,d,k
that satisfy this condition as well as
m\geq 2
and
2k<d+1
are those where
m=2
and
d=2k
.

In the remainder of this section we prove
Proposition

15
and derive
\Sigma^{[2]}
for
2k<d+1
.
For
2k\geq d+1
, note that by (
4.1
)

F^{(m)}_{r}-\mathbb{E}[F^{(m)}_{r}]=C(d,k,m)\cdot(F^{(1)}_{r}-\mathbb{E}[F^{(1)}_{r}])+\textrm{lower order term},

hence we can argue as in
Section

3
that the asymptotic covariance matrix has rank one.
This proves the first part of
Proposition

15
.

Let us from now on assume that
2k<d+1
.
By the above remark, we can assume that
m=2
and
d=2k
.
We exclude
k=1
in the derivation below, since this case has already been treated in
[
25
, Sec. 4.5.1]
.
From the Wiener–Itô chaos decomposition and (
2.2
) it follows that

\displaystyle F^{(1)}_{r}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[F^{(1)}_{r}]+I_{1}(f^{(1)}_{r,1}),

\displaystyle F^{(2)}_{r}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[F^{(2)}_{r}]+C(d,k,2)\cdot I_{1}(f^{(1)}_{r,1})+I_{2}(f^{(2)}_{r,2}).

Since the first and second chaos term are uncorrelated, the covariance matrix
\Sigma(F^{[m]})
takes the form

\displaystyle\Sigma\left(F^{[2]}_{r}\right)

\displaystyle=\Sigma\left(I_{1}(f^{(1)}_{r,1}),C(d,k,2)\cdot I_{1}(f^{(1)}_{r,1})\right)+\Sigma\left(0,I_{2}(f^{(2)}_{r,2})\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{V}(I_{1}(f^{(1)}_{r,1}))\begin{pmatrix}1&C(d,k,2)\\
C(d,k,2)&C(d,k,2)^{2}\end{pmatrix}+\mathbb{V}(I_{2}(f^{(2)}_{r,2}))\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.

Note that, by definition,
\mathbb{V}(I_{1}(f^{(1)}_{r,1}))=A^{(1)}_{r,1}
and
\mathbb{V}(I_{2}(f^{(2)}_{r,2}))=2A^{(2)}_{r,2}
.
Using the representation
[
9
, Eq. (2.9)]
and writing the involved integral as in
[
25
, Lem. 8 and (19)]
, we arrive at

A^{(1)}_{r,1}=c^{(1)}_{d,k}\int_{0}^{r}\cosh^{k}(s)\sinh^{d-1-k}(s)\left(\int_{0}^{\arcosh\left(\frac{\cosh(r)}{\cosh(s)}\right)}\sinh^{k-1}(u)\dint u\right)^{2}\dint s,

A^{(2)}_{r,2}=c^{(2)}_{d,k}\int_{0}^{r}\sinh^{d-1}(s)\dint s,

with constants
c^{(1)}_{d,k}=\omega_{k}^{3}
and
c^{(2)}_{d,k}=\frac{\omega_{1}\omega_{d}\omega_{d+1}}{4\omega_{k+1}^{2}}
.

After substituting
t=r-s
, we get that

e^{-(d-1)r}A^{(1)}_{r,1}=c^{(1)}_{d,k}\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\cosh^{k}(r-t)\sinh^{d-1-k}(r-t)}{e^{(d-1)r}}\left(\int_{0}^{\arcosh\left(\frac{\cosh(r)}{\cosh(r-t)}\right)}\sinh^{k-1}(u)\dint u\right)^{2}\dint t

To bound the integrand, first note that

\frac{\cosh^{k}(r-t)\sinh^{d-1-k}(r-t)}{e^{(d-1)r}}\leq\frac{e^{(d-1)(r-t)}}{e^{(d-1)r}}=e^{-(d-1)t},

and since

\frac{\cosh(r)}{\cosh(r-t)}=e^{t}\frac{1+e^{-2r}}{1+e^{2t-2r}}\leq e^{t}

and
\exp(\arcosh(y))\leq 2y
for
y\geq 1
, we can bound

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\arcosh\left(\frac{\cosh(r)}{\cosh(r-t)}\right)}\sinh^{k-1}(u)\dint u

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\arcosh(e^{t})}e^{(k-1)u}\dint u

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k-1}\left(\exp(\arcosh(e^{t}))^{k-1}-1\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{k-1}}{k-1}e^{(k-1)t}.

The (non-negative) integrand can thus be bounded uniformly in
r
from above by

e^{-(d-1)t}\left(\frac{2^{k-1}}{k-1}\right)^{2}e^{2(k-1)t}=\left(\frac{2^{k-1}}{k-1}\right)^{2}e^{-t},

where we used that
2k=d
.
We can thus apply the dominated convergence theorem and obtain

\lim_{r\to\infty}e^{-(d-1)r}A^{(1)}_{r,1}=c^{(1)}_{d,k}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-(d-1)t}}{2^{d-1}}\left(\int_{0}^{\arcosh(e^{t})}\sinh^{k-1}(u)\dint u\right)^{2}\dint t.

After substituting
u=\arcosh(y)
, the inner integral becomes

\int_{1}^{e^{t}}(y^{2}-1)^{\frac{k-2}{2}}\dint y,

the substitution
x=e^{t}
then turns the outer integral into

\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{-d}}{2^{d-1}}\left(\int_{1}^{x}(y^{2}-1)^{\frac{k-2}{2}}\dint y\right)^{2}\dint x,

so that

\lim_{r\to\infty}e^{-(d-1)r}A^{(1)}_{r,1}=\frac{c^{(1)}_{d,k}}{2^{d-1}}\int_{1}^{\infty}x^{-d}\left(\int_{1}^{x}(y^{2}-1)^{\frac{k-2}{2}}\dint y\right)^{2}\dint x.

(We excluded
k=1
for convenience, but one can check that the final result is also valid in this case.)
The second term is much easier.
One again substitutes
t=r-s
, checks that the dominated convergence theorem applies and obtains

\lim_{r\to\infty}e^{-(d-1)r}A^{(2)}_{r,2}=\frac{c^{(2)}_{d,k}}{2^{d-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(d-1)t}\dint t=\frac{c^{(2)}_{d,k}}{(d-1)2^{d-1}}.

In summary, we arrive at

\displaystyle\Sigma(F^{[2]})=

\displaystyle\quad\frac{\omega_{k}^{3}}{2^{d-1}}\int_{1}^{\infty}x^{-d}\left(\int_{1}^{x}(y^{2}-1)^{\frac{k-2}{2}}\dint y\right)^{2}\dint x\begin{pmatrix}1&\frac{\omega_{1}\omega_{d+1}}{\omega_{k+1}^{2}}\\
\frac{\omega_{1}\omega_{d+1}}{\omega_{k+1}^{2}}&\left(\frac{\omega_{1}\omega_{d+1}}{\omega_{k+1}^{2}}\right)^{2}\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&\frac{\omega_{1}\omega_{d}\omega_{d+1}}{(d-1)2^{d}\omega_{k+1}^{2}}\end{pmatrix}.

(7.1)

From this representation the second part of
Proposition

15
follows immediately.

Remark 17

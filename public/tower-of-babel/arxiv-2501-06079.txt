Set-valued evenly convex functions: characterizations and c-conjugacy
M.D. Fajardo

Abstract
In this work we deal with set-valued functions with values in the power set of a separated locally convex space where a nontrivial pointed convex cone induces a partial order relation. A set-valued function is evenly convex if its epigraph is an evenly convex set, i.e., it is the intersection of an arbitrary family of open half-spaces. In this paper we characterize evenly convex set-valued functions as the pointwise supremum of its set-valued e-affine minorants. Moreover, a suitable conjugation pattern will be developed for these functions, as well as the counterpart of the biconjugation Fenchel-Moreau theorem.

Introduction

Evenly convex
sets (e-convex in brief) were introduced by Fenchel
F1952
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
as those sets which can be expressed as the intersection of an arbitrary (possibly empty) family of open half-spaces. He initiated the study of this kind of sets defining a natural polarity operation and mentioning some of their properties. Later, they appeared in some works related to quasiconvex programming (see
Ml1983
,
Ml1988
,
PP1984
and
PV1990
) and mathematical economics
ML1991
. E-convex sets are the solution sets of linear systems of either weak or strict inequalities, and from this point of view, their properties have been studied in
GJR2003
and
GR2006
, whereas some important characterizations of e-convex sets in terms of their sections and projections were given in
KMZ2007
. Finally, some properties of this class of sets in locally convex spaces can be found in
DML2002
.

In a natural way, the concept of even convexity was applied to an extended real-valued function defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
in
RVP2011
, where a function is said to be e-convex if its epigraph is e-convex. In that work, the main properties of these functions, which extend the class of convex and lower semicontinuous functions, were studied. In particular, some characterizations were provided. Later, in
MLVP2011
, the notion of e-convex function was considered for extended real-valued functions defined on locally convex spaces, and proper e-convex functions were characterized as the pointwise supremum of their e-affine minorants. This characterization allowed, also in
MLVP2011
, the definition of a suitable conjugation scheme for e-convex functions. This pattern is called
c
-conjugation
, and it was inspired by a survey written by Martínez-Legaz
ML2005
where generalized convex duality theory is applied to quasiconvex programming. As it is shown in
MLVP2011
, the
c
-conjugation scheme allowed to set forth the e-convex counterpart of the well-known Fenchel-Moreau theorem. Recently, some works have been developed around duality theory in evenly convex optimization (
F2015
,
FVR2016
,
FV2016SSD
,
FV2017
,
FV2018
), building different c-conjugate dual problems by means of the perturbation approach and obtaining regularity conditions expressed in terms of the even convexity of the perturbation function. A monograph presenting the state-of-art on even convexity in finite dimensional spaces has been published very recently (see
FGRV2020
).

In this paper we extend the notion of even convexity to set-valued functions which map a separated locally convex space
X
into the power set
\mathcal{P}(Z)
of another separated locally convex space
Z
, assuming that there exists a partial order in
Z
induced by a pointed convex cone
K\subset Z
. Every concept and result from the even convexity scalar theory can be translated in the field of set-valued functions by choosing appropiate definitions of properness, e-affine set-valued minorants, set-valued c-conjugate function, etc, and, moreover, extending operations, like the meaning of pointwise supremum or infimum. We will assume the same framework as in
H2009
, where a characterization for proper closed convex set-valued functions by its continuous affine minorants and an appropiate Fenchel conjugate are provided. Also in that paper introduction section, different approaches which have been developed for conjugation patterns suitable for closed convex set-valued functions are highlighted.

The layout of this work is as follows. Section
2
contains preliminary results on set-valued functions, as well as fundamental definitions and results on e-convex sets and extended real-valued functions to make the paper self-contained.
Section
3
is dedicated to characterizations of set-valued proper and e-convex functions in terms of different kinds of affine minorants.
Section
4
is devoted to a suitable conjugation pattern for set-valued e-convex functions, which will provide a biconjugation theorem.

, resp.). Hence, the behaviour of this kind of hulls differs from what is usually understood for a hull of any kind for an extended real-valued function, in the sense that, for instance, a
K
-e-convex set-valued function is not necessarily equal to its
K
-e-convex hull.

Finally, let us observe that, for all
x\in X
,

(\Kcl\conv f)(x)\leq_{K}(\Keco f)(x)\leq_{K}f(x),

and the first inequality can be strict. For instance, in the previous example, we have

(\Kcl\conv f^{s})(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\emptyset&\text{ if }x<0,\\
\left[x^{2},+\infty\right)&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

3
Characterizing
K
-e-convex set-valued functions

It is a very well-known result that a proper convex lower semicontinuos function
f:X\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
, where
X
is a locally convex space, is the pointwise supremum of its affine minorants, and
(
H2009
, Section 3)
shows that this approach can be developed for set-valued functions with a suitable definition of a set-valued affine minorant.

Again in the scalar case, in
MLVP2011
different kinds of affine functions were defined, and proper e-convex functions were characterized as the pointwise supremum of the different sets of its affine minorants. Our aim in the following section is to characterize proper
K
-e-convex set-valued functions by means of its set-valued affine minorants. We will define, like in
MLVP2011
, the sets of
M_{f}
-affine,
\mathcal{C}_{f}
-affine and e-affine minorants of a set-valued function.

Previous definitions have been linked to the chosen convex cone
K
, but in what follows, we assume that this cone is fixed, so it is not included in the next definitions, in order to avoid too long names.

Definition 8

Let
C\subseteq X
. A set-valued function
a:X\to\mathcal{P}(Z)
is
C-affine
if there exist
x^{*}\in X^{*}
,
z^{*}\in K^{*}\setminus\{0\}
and
\tilde{z}\in Z
such that

a(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}S_{(x^{*},z^{*})}(x)+\tilde{z}&\text{if }x\in C,\\
\emptyset&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

(4)

Remark 4

It is easy to see that
a(x)+K=a(x)
, for all
x\in C
(trivially if
x\notin C
), since
z\in a(x)
if and only if
\langle x,x^{*}\rangle+\langle z-\hat{z},z^{*}\rangle<0
, and
z^{*}\in K^{*}
. Then, a
C
-affine function maps any
x\in X
to
a(x)\in\mathcal{P}^{a}_{K}(Z)
, i.e.,
a:X\to\mathcal{P}^{a}_{K}(Z)
.

In the next proposition we will use the fact that
C,D\subseteq X
are e-convex iff
C\times D
is e-convex. See
(
RVP2011
, Proposition 1.2)
for the proof in the finite dimensional case, which can easily be generalized to locally convex spaces.

Proposition 4

Let
a:X\to\mathcal{P}(Z)
be a
C
-affine set-valued function. Then if
C
is e-convex,
a
is
K
-e-convex. Moreover, in this case,
a:X\to\mathcal{R}_{K}(Z)
.

Proof

Let
C
be e-convex in (
4
). We have

\epi_{K}a=\{(x,z):\langle x,x^{*}\rangle+\langle z,z^{*}\rangle<\langle\tilde{z},z^{*}\rangle\}\bigcap\{C\times Z\},

which is an e-convex set, since it is the intersection of e-convex sets. It follows immediately that
a(x)\in\mathcal{R}_{K}(Z)
, for all
x\in X
.
∎

Remark 5

It is well-known that if
C\subseteq X
is a convex set and
T:X\to Y
is a linear operator, (in this case,
X
and
Y
are real linear vector spaces), then
T(C)
is also a convex set, and from this result one can derive that the effective domain of a
K
-convex set-valued function is convex, also a well-known property. But this is not true, in general, either for e-convex sets or for
K
-e-convex functions. See, for instance,
(
RVP2011
, Example 2.4)
.

For a set-valued function
f:X\to\mathcal{P}(Z)
we denote by
M_{f}=\eco(\dom f)
, and by
\mathcal{H}_{f}
the set of all the
M_{f}
-affine minorants of
f
:

\mathcal{H}_{f}=\{{a:X\to\mathcal{R}_{K}(Z):a\text{ is }M_{f}\text{-affine and }a(x)\leq_{K}f(x),\text{ for all }x\in X}\}.

Remark 6

Clearly, according to Proposition
4
, every
M_{f}
-affine minorant of
f
is
K
-e-convex. Moreover,
\mathcal{H}_{f}=\mathcal{H}_{f_{K}}
.

Remark 7

Let us see what happens with
\mathcal{H}_{f}
is
f
is an improper set-valued function.
If
f\equiv\emptyset
, then
\dom f=\emptyset
and
\mathcal{H}_{f}=\{f\}
. On the other hand, if
f(\bar{x})=Z
, for some
\bar{x}\in X
, in the case there exists
a\in\mathcal{H}_{f}
, it will follow that
S_{(x^{*},z^{*})}(\bar{x})+\hat{z}=Z
with
(x^{*},z^{*})\in X^{*}\times(K^{*}\setminus\{0\})
and
\hat{z}\in Z
, and this is impossible, it must be
\mathcal{H}_{f}=\emptyset
.

The proof of the following lemma is similar to the scalar case (
(
MLVP2011
, Lemma 7)
).

Lemma 1

Let
f:X\to\mathcal{P}(Z)
be a set-valued function. Then
\mathcal{H}_{f}=\mathcal{H}_{K\text{-}{\eco f}}
.

Lemma 2

Let
f:X\to\mathcal{P}(Z)
be a set-valued function. If K-
\eco f\equiv Z
then necessarily
\mathcal{H}_{f}=\emptyset
.

Proof

If there exists
a\in\mathcal{H}_{f}
as it is described in (
4
), then
a(x)\leq_{K}(\Keco f)(x)
, for all
x\in X
, and
a\equiv Z
, in particular,
a(0)=Z
, and it would imply that, taking into account that
z^{*}\neq 0
,

\{z:\langle z,z^{*}\rangle<\langle\tilde{z},z^{*}\rangle\}=Z.

\square

Theorem 3.1

Let
f:X\to\mathcal{P}(Z)
be a set-valued function. The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\mathcal{H}_{f}\neq\emptyset
.

(ii)

Either
K
-
\eco f
is proper or
f\equiv\emptyset
.

(iii)

f
has a proper

-e-convex minorant.

Proof

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
According to Remark
7
and Lemma
1
,
(\Keco f)(x)\neq Z
for all
x\in X
, and therefore, in the case
K
-
\eco f
is improper, it would be
\dom(\Keco f)=\emptyset
, and then

\emptyset=(\Keco f)(x)\leq_{K}f(x),

for all
x\in X
, which means that
f\equiv\emptyset
.

(ii)\Rightarrow(iii)

\Keco f
will be a proper
K
-e-convex minorant of

in one case, and if

-e-convex set-valued function will be a minorant of

.

(iii)\Rightarrow(i)
If

. Now, consider the case where

. Let

-e-convex minorant of

. Take a point

and

such that
(x_{0},z_{0})\notin\epi_{K}g

, there exists
(x^{*},z^{*})\in(X^{*}\times Z^{*})\setminus\{0\}

\langle x_{0},x^{*}\rangle+\langle z_{0},z^{*}\rangle>\langle x,x^{*}\rangle+\langle z,z^{*}\rangle,

(5)

for all

, hence

. In particular, for any fix point
z_{1}\in g(x_{0})
and for all

\langle x_{0},x^{*}\rangle+\langle z_{0},z^{*}\rangle>\langle x_{0},x^{*}\rangle+\langle z_{1}+k,z^{*}\rangle

and

. We consider a point

Since

\langle x,x^{*}\rangle+\langle z,z^{*}\rangle<\langle\tilde{z},z^{*}\rangle

, then for all

f(x)-\tilde{z}\subseteq S_{(x^{*},z^{*})}(x)
and

.∎

Taking into account that
\Keco f=f_{K}
whenever

is
K
-e-convex and

is equivalent to

, the following corollary comes directly.

Corollary 1

Let
f:X\to\mathcal{P}(Z)
be a K-e-convex set-valued function. Then
\mathcal{H}_{f}\neq\emptyset
if and only if
f_{K}
is proper or
f_{K}\equiv\emptyset
.

We recall that, if
f:X\to\mathcal{P}(Z)
is a set-valued function and
\{a_{i}:i\in I\}
is an arbitrary collection of set-valued functions where
a_{i}:X\to\mathcal{P}(Z)
, for all

, saying that

is the pointwise supremum of

,

f(x)+K=\sup_{I}\{{a_{i}(x)+K,\leq_{K}}\},

means that
f(x)+K=\bigcap_{I}\{{a_{i}(x)+K}\}
, for all

. It is clear that we can also take a collection
\{a_{i}:i\in I\}
where
a_{i}:X\to\mathcal{P}^{a}_{K}(Z)
.

Remark 8

Let us observe that
f
is the pointwise supremum of

if
\epi_{K}f=\bigcap_{I}{\epi_{K}a_{i}}
.

Theorem 3.2

Let
f:X\to\mathcal{P}(Z)
be a set-valued function. The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

f
is the pointwise supremum of its

-affine minorants.

(ii)

is K-e-convex and either
f_{K}
proper,

or

Proof

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
In the case

, then
f_{K}\equiv Z
(recall that the supremum of an empty collection of sets in

). Clearly,

Otherwise, if
\mathcal{H}_{f}\neq\emptyset
, since

and it is an e-convex set (recall Remark
6
), then

is
K
-e-convex, and by Corollary

, either

is proper or

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
If

6
and

and

If

, then
\mathcal{H}_{f}=\{f\}
. Finally, assume that

is
K
-e-convex, with

Since

, then there exists
a\in\mathcal{H}_{f}
such that
(x_{0},z_{0})\notin\epi_{K}a
.

We can assume that
x_{0}\in\mathcal{M}_{f}
, otherwise
a(x_{0})=\emptyset
, for all

.

Since
\epi_{K}f
is e-convex, according to Proposition

, we can find
(x^{*},z^{*})\in(X^{*}\times Z^{*})\setminus\{0\}

\langle x_{0},x^{*}\rangle+\langle z_{0},z^{*}\rangle>\langle x,x^{*}\rangle+\langle z,z^{*}\rangle,

(6)

for all

. We ensure that

.

Firstly, we will see that

. If not,
\langle x_{0},x^{*}\rangle>\langle x,x^{*}\rangle,

for all

, and it follows that

.

Now, if

, it would exist

, and considering that, for all

We consider now a point

and take

It is easy to check that

, nevertheless,

and

∎

Let

having a proper K-e-convex minorant. Then, for all

Proof

By Theorem
3.1
, either
\Keco f
is proper or

. In the first case, taking into account that

, by Theorem

,

is the pointwise supremum of its

-affine minorants, which is the same set as the set of the

-affine minorants of

In the case

,

is

-e-convex, hence

and moreover
\mathcal{H}_{f}=\{f\}
. ∎

Definition 9

Let
\mathcal{C}
be the set of all e-convex subsets in

Theorem 3.3

Let

. The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

is the pointwise supremum of its

-affine minorants.

(ii)

is

-e-convex and either

is proper,

or

.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)

-e-convex. Now, if

, then

. In the case

On the other hand, in the cases

\square

Definition 10

We say that a function

Let
f:X\to\mathcal{P}(Z)
a set-valued function. The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

is the pointwise supremum of its e-affine minorants.

is

-e-convex and either

4
C-conjugating set-valued functions

We will generalize the conjugation pattern for extended real-valued functions, described in
MLVP2011
, suitable for e-convex functions. It is based on the generalized convex conjugation theory introduced by Moreau
Mor1970
. We give a brief description of it. Let us consider the space
W:=X^{\ast}\times X^{\ast}\times\mathbb{R}
with
the coupling functions
c:X\times W\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}
and
c^{\prime}:W\times X\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}
given by

c(x,(x^{\ast},y^{\ast},\alpha))=c^{\prime}\left((x^{\ast},y^{\ast},\alpha),x\right):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left\langle x,x^{\ast}\right\rangle&\text{if }\left\langle x,y^{\ast}\right\rangle<\alpha,\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Given two functions
f:X\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}
and
g:W\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}
, the
c
-conjugate
of
f
,
f^{c}:W\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}
, and the
c
′
-conjugate
of
g
,
g^{c^{\prime}}:X\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}
, are defined

\displaystyle f^{c}(x^{\ast},y^{\ast},\alpha)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm sup}_{x\in X}\left\{c(x,(x^{\ast},y^{\ast},\alpha))-f(x)\right\},

(7)

\displaystyle g^{c^{\prime}}(x)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm sup}_{(x^{\ast},y^{\ast},\alpha)\in W}\left\{c^{\prime}\left((x^{\ast},y^{\ast},\alpha),x\right)-g(x^{\ast},y^{\ast},\alpha)\right\},

(8)

respectively, with the conventions
\left(+\infty\right)+\left(-\infty\right)=\left(-\infty\right)+\left(+\infty\right)=\left(+\infty\right)-\left(+\infty\right)

=\left(-\infty\right)-\left(-\infty\right)=-\infty.

The counterpart of the Fenchel-Moreau theorem for e-convex functions derives immediately from
(
ML2005
, Corollary 6.1)
, and a function
f:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\left\{+\infty\right\}
is e-convex iff
f^{cc^{\prime}}=f
.

We can rewrite (
7
) as

f^{c}(x^{\ast},y^{\ast},\alpha)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\inf_{x\in X}\left\{f(x)+\langle-x,x^{*}\rangle\right\}&\text{if}\dom f\subseteq{H}^{-}_{y^{*},\alpha}\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise,}\end{array}\right.

for all
(x^{\ast},y^{\ast},\alpha)\in W
. This formula inspires the generalization of c-conjugacy to set-valued functions. As in the previous section, we assume that the convex cone
K
is fix.

Definition 11

Let
f:X\to\mathcal{P}(Z)
be a set-valued function. We define the
c-conjugate
of
f
(respect to
K
),
f^{c}:X^{*}\times X^{*}\times(K^{*}\setminus\{0\})\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{P}(Z)
as

f^{c}(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*},\alpha):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\eco\left[\bigcup_{X}\{f(x)+\bar{S}_{(x^{*},z^{*})}(-x)\}\right]&\text{if }\dom f\subseteq{H}^{-}_{y^{*},\alpha}\\
\emptyset&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

For a set-valued function
g:X^{*}\times X^{*}\times(K^{*}\setminus\{0\})\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{P}(Z)
, its
c
′
-conjugate
(respect to
K
),
g^{c^{\prime}}:X\to\mathcal{P}(Z)
is defined, if
x\in{H}^{-}_{y^{*},\alpha}
, for all
(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*},\alpha)\in\dom g
:

g^{c^{\prime}}(x):=\bigcap_{\dom g}{\left\{\bar{S}_{(x^{*},z^{*})}(x)-g(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*},\alpha)\right\}},

and
g^{c^{\prime}}(x):=\emptyset
otherwise.

Remark 10

Actually,

\inf\{\{f(x)+\bar{S}_{(x^{*},z^{*})}(-x)\}_{X},\leq_{K}\}=\bigcup_{X}\{f(x)+\bar{S}_{(x^{*},z^{*})}(-x)\}\text{ and}

\sup\left\{\{\bar{S}_{(x^{*},z^{*})}(x)-g(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*},\alpha)\}_{\dom g},\leq_{K}\right\}=\bigcap_{\dom g}{\left\{\bar{S}_{(x^{*},z^{*})}(x)-g(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*},\alpha)\right\}}

in the previous definitions, since
\bar{S}_{(x^{*},z^{*})}(-x)+K=\bar{S}_{(x^{*},z^{*})}(-x)
, for all
x\in X
.

Definition 12

The function
\sigma_{f}:X^{*}\times X^{*}\times Z^{*}\times\mathbb{R}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
defined as

\sigma_{f}(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*},\alpha):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\sup_{\epi_{K}f}{\{\langle x,x^{*}\rangle+\langle z,z^{*}\rangle\}}&\text{ if }\dom f\subseteq{H}^{-}_{y^{*},\alpha},\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

is called the
support function
of the
K
-epigraph of the set-valued function
f:X\to\mathcal{P}(Z)
relative to open half-spaces.

Remark 11

Let
f:X\to\mathcal{P}(Z)
be a set-valued function and let us consider the indicator function of
\epi_{K}f
,
\delta_{\epi_{K}f}:X\times Z\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
,

\delta_{\epi_{K}f}(x,z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\text{if }z\in f(x)+K,\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

It is easy to check that
\delta_{\epi_{K}f}^{c}(x^{*},z^{*},y^{*},0,\alpha)=\sigma_{f}(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*},\alpha)
.

Remark 12

Let us observe that, if
f
is
K
-e-convex and
f_{K}\not\equiv Z
,
\epi_{K}f
is a proper e-convex subset of
X\times Z
and hence it can be expressed as the intersection of a nonempty family of open half-spaces, being nontrivial at least one of them. Denote one of these half-spaces by
H=\{(x,z)\in X\times Z:\langle x,x^{*}\rangle+\langle z,z^{*}\rangle<\beta\}
. Since
\epi_{K}f\subseteq H
, we will have that
\sigma_{f}(x^{*},0,z^{*},\alpha)\leq\beta<+\infty
, for all
\alpha>0
and
\dom\sigma_{f}\neq\emptyset
.

Definition 13

Let
C\subset X\times Z
be a nonempty set. We define the function
\eta_{C}:X^{*}\times Z^{*}\to\{0,1\}
as

\eta_{C}(x^{*},z^{*})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\text{if }\langle x,x^{*}\rangle+\langle z,z^{*}\rangle<\sup_{C}{\{\langle x,x^{*}\rangle+\langle z,z^{*}\rangle\}}\text{ for all }(x,z)\in C,\\
1&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Lemma 3

Let
f:X\to\mathcal{P}(Z)
be a set-valued function. Then, for all
(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*},\alpha)\in X^{*}\times X^{*}\times(K^{*}\setminus\{0\})\times\mathbb{R}
,

-f^{c}(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*},\alpha)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\{z\in Z:\langle z,z^{*}\rangle<\sigma_{f}(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*},\alpha)\}&\text{ if }\eta_{\epi_{K}f}(x^{*},z^{*})=0,\\
\{z\in Z:\langle z,z^{*}\rangle\leq\sigma_{f}(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*},\alpha)\}&\text{ if }\eta_{\epi_{K}f}(x^{*},z^{*})=1.\end{array}\right.

Consequently,
\dom f^{c}=\dom\sigma_{f}
.

Proof

Take any point
(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*},\alpha)\in X^{*}\times X^{*}\times(K^{*}\setminus\{0\})\times\mathbb{R}
.

In first place, the equality holds trivially in the case
\dom f\not\subset H^{-}_{y^{*},\alpha}
, because
\sigma_{f}(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*},\alpha)=+\infty
and
-f^{c}(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*},\alpha)=Z
(recall that we set
-\emptyset=Z
).

Hence, let us suppose that
\dom f\subset H^{-}_{y^{*},\alpha}
.
We denote

D_{0}=\{z\in Z:\langle z,z^{*}\rangle<\sigma_{f}(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*},\alpha)\},

D_{1}=\{z\in Z:\langle z,z^{*}\rangle\leq\sigma_{f}(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*},\alpha)\}.

In the case
\eta_{\epi_{K}f}(x^{*},z^{*})=0
, take any point
x\in\dom f
,
z_{1}\in f(x)
and
z_{2}\in\bar{S}_{(x^{*},z^{*})}(-x)
. Then

\langle z_{1}+z_{2},z^{*}\rangle<\sigma_{f}(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*},\alpha),

and
z_{1}+z_{2}\in D_{0}
, which means that, for all
x\in\dom f
,

f(x)+\bar{S}_{(x^{*},z^{*})}(-x)\subset D_{0},

and
D_{0}
an e-convex set, implying that
-f^{c}(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*},\alpha)\subset D_{0}
.

For the converse inclusion, take
z\in D_{0}
and
(\hat{x},\hat{z})\in\epi_{K}f
verifying that

\langle z,z^{*}\rangle<\langle\hat{x},x^{*}\rangle+\langle\hat{z},z^{*}\rangle.

Then, since
\hat{z}=z_{1}+k
, for some
z_{1}\in f(\hat{x})
and
k\in K
, we have

\langle-\hat{x},x^{*}\rangle+\langle z-z_{1},z^{*}\rangle<\langle k,z^{*}\rangle\leq 0,

and
z-z_{1}\in\bar{S}_{(x^{*},z^{*})}(-\hat{x})
, obtaining
D_{0}\subset-f^{c}(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*},\alpha)
.
The proof is similar for the case
\eta_{\epi_{K}f}(x^{*},z^{*})=1
and
D_{1}
; the inclusion
-f^{c}(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*},\alpha)\subset D_{1}
can be shown analogously, whereas the converse inclusion holds if we take a point
(\hat{x},\hat{z})\in\epi_{K}f
verifying that

\langle z,z^{*}\rangle\leq\langle\hat{x},x^{*}\rangle+\langle\hat{z},z^{*}\rangle.

Finally, we have that
(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*},\alpha)\in\dom f^{c}
iff
-f^{c}(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*},\alpha)\neq Z
, which means that
\sigma_{f}(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*},\alpha)<+\infty
, i.e.,
(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*},\alpha)\in\dom\sigma_{f}
.
∎

Lemma 4

Let
f:X\to\mathcal{P}(Z)
a set-valued function. Then, if
x\in\dom f^{cc^{\prime}}
,

\begin{array}[]{ll}$$f^{cc^{\prime}}(x)=\bigcap_{\dom\sigma_{f}}\{z\in Z:&\langle x,x^{*}\rangle+\langle z,z^{*}\rangle<\sigma_{f}(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*},\alpha),\eta_{\epi_{K}f}(x^{*},z^{*})=0,\\
&\langle x,x^{*}\rangle+\langle z,z^{*}\rangle\leq\sigma_{f}(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*},\alpha),\eta_{\epi_{K}f}(x^{*},z^{*})=1\}.\end{array}

(9)

Consequently,
f^{cc^{\prime}}(x)
is an e-convex set in
Z
, for all
x\in X
, and
f^{cc^{\prime}}:X\to\mathcal{R}_{K}(Z)
is a
K
-e-convex set-valued function. Moreover,
f(x)\subseteq f^{cc^{\prime}}(x)
and then
f_{K}(x)\subseteq f^{cc^{\prime}}(x)
, for all
x\in X
.

Proof

Take
\bar{x}\in\dom f^{cc^{\prime}}
and denote by

\begin{array}[]{ll}$$D=\bigcap_{\dom\sigma_{f}}\{z\in Z:&\langle\bar{x},x^{*}\rangle+\langle z,z^{*}\rangle<\sigma_{f}(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*},\alpha),\eta_{\epi_{K}f}(x^{*},z^{*})=0,\\
&\langle\bar{x},x^{*}\rangle+\langle z,z^{*}\rangle\leq\sigma_{f}(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*},\alpha),\eta_{\epi_{K}f}(x^{*},z^{*})=1\}.\end{array}

Take now
(\bar{x}^{*},\bar{y}^{*},\bar{z}^{*},\bar{\alpha})\in\dom\sigma_{f}
. Name

D_{0}=\{z\in Z:\langle\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*}\rangle+\langle z,\bar{z}^{*}\rangle<\sigma_{f}(\bar{x}^{*},\bar{y}^{*},\bar{z}^{*},\bar{\alpha})\},

D_{1}=\{z\in Z:\langle\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*}\rangle+\langle z,\bar{z}^{*}\rangle\leq\sigma_{f}(\bar{x}^{*},\bar{y}^{*},\bar{z}^{*},\bar{\alpha})\}.

From the definition of the c
′
-conjugate function, and taking into account that, according to Lemma
3
,
\dom f^{c}=\dom\sigma_{f}
, it holds

f^{cc^{\prime}}(\bar{x})=\bigcap_{\dom\sigma_{f}}{\left[\bar{S}_{(x^{*},z^{*})}(\bar{x})-f^{c}(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*},\alpha)\right]}.

Take
\bar{z}\in f^{cc^{\prime}}(\bar{x})
and suppose
\eta_{\epi_{K}f}(\bar{x}^{*},\bar{z}^{*})=0
. Since
(\bar{x}^{*},\bar{y}^{*},\bar{z}^{*},\bar{\alpha})\in\dom\sigma_{f}
, we have
\bar{z}\in\bar{S}_{(\bar{x}^{*},\bar{z}^{*})}(\bar{x})-f^{c}(\bar{x}^{*},\bar{y}^{*},\bar{z}^{*},\bar{\alpha})
and
\bar{z}=z_{1}+z_{2}
, where
z_{1}\in S_{(\bar{x}^{*},\bar{z}^{*})}(\bar{x})
and
z_{2}\in-f^{c}(\bar{x}^{*},\bar{y}^{*},\bar{z}^{*},\bar{\alpha})
. Therefore

\langle\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*}\rangle+\langle z_{1},\bar{z}^{*}\rangle\leq 0,

and, by Lemma
3
,

\langle z_{2},\bar{z}^{*}\rangle<\sigma_{f}(\bar{x}^{*},\bar{y}^{*},\bar{z}^{*},\bar{\alpha}).

Then
\langle\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*}\rangle+\langle\bar{z},\bar{z}^{*}\rangle<\sigma_{f}(\bar{x}^{*},\bar{y}^{*},\bar{z}^{*},\bar{\alpha})
and
\bar{z}\in D_{0}
. Analogously, it can be shown that, if
\eta_{\epi_{K}f}(\bar{x}^{*},\bar{z}^{*})=1
,
f^{cc^{\prime}}(\bar{x})\subseteq D_{1}
. Then
f^{cc^{\prime}}(\bar{x})\subseteq D
.

For the converse inclusion, take
\bar{z}\in D
and
(\bar{x}^{*},\bar{y}^{*},\bar{z}^{*},\bar{\alpha})\in\dom\sigma_{f}=\dom f^{c}
. In the case
\eta_{\epi_{K}f}(\bar{x}^{*},\bar{z}^{*})=0
, we denote by

\beta=\sigma_{f}(\bar{x}^{*},\bar{y}^{*},\bar{z}^{*},\bar{\alpha})-\langle\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*}\rangle-\langle\bar{z},\bar{z}^{*}\rangle>0.

Choose
z_{2}\in Z
such that
-\beta<\langle\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*}\rangle+\langle z_{2},\bar{z}^{*}\rangle\leq 0
,
and
\bar{z}=(\bar{z}-z_{2})+z_{2}
, with
z_{2}\in\bar{S}_{(\bar{x}^{*},\bar{z}^{*})}(\bar{x})
and, according to Lemma
3
,
\bar{z}-z_{2}\in-f^{c}(\bar{x}^{*},\bar{y}^{*},\bar{z}^{*},\bar{\alpha})
.

In the case
\eta_{\epi_{K}f}(\bar{x}^{*},\bar{z}^{*})=1
, we can follow the same steps than in the previous proof, choosing
z_{2}\in Z
such that
\langle\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*}\rangle+\langle z_{2},\bar{z}^{*}\rangle=0
.

Hence, if
\bar{x}\in\dom f^{cc^{\prime}}
, then
f^{cc^{\prime}}(\bar{x})=D
.

It is clear that, for all
x\in X
,
f^{cc^{\prime}}(x)
is an e-convex set in
Z
. Moreover,
\epi_{K}f^{cc^{\prime}}
is the intersection of the epigraphs of all the
K
-e-convex set-valued functions

a_{0}(x)=\{z\in Z:\langle x,x^{*}\rangle+\langle z,z^{*}\rangle<\sigma_{f}(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*},\alpha)\}\text{ if }\eta_{\epi_{K}f}(x^{*},z^{*})=0,

a_{1}(x)=\{z\in Z:\langle x,x^{*}\rangle+\langle z,z^{*}\rangle\leq\sigma_{f}(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*},\alpha)\}\text{ if }\eta_{\epi_{K}f}(x^{*},z^{*})=1,

for all
(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*},\alpha)\in\dom\sigma_{f}
, and
f^{cc^{\prime}}
is
K
-e-convex.

Is is evident also from (
9
) that
f^{cc^{\prime}}(x)+K\subseteq f^{cc^{\prime}}(x)
, for all
x\in X
, therefore
\eco(f^{cc^{\prime}}(x)+K)\subseteq f^{cc^{\prime}}(x)
, for all
x\in X
, and
f^{cc^{\prime}}:X\to\mathcal{R}_{K}(Z)
. The inclusions
f(x)\subseteq f^{cc^{\prime}}(x)
and
f_{K}(x)\subseteq f^{cc^{\prime}}(x)
, for all
x\in X
, are trivial.
∎

Next we will give the biconjugation theorem.

Theorem 4.1

Let
f:X\to\mathcal{P}(Z)
be a set-valued function. Then it is
K
-e-convex if and only if
f^{cc^{\prime}}(x)=f_{K}(x)
, for all
x\in X
.

Proof

Let us assume that
f
is
K
-e-convex. If
f_{K}\equiv Z
, from Lemma
4
, we have
f_{K}=f^{cc^{\prime}}
. In the case
f\equiv\emptyset
, it will be
\sigma_{f}\equiv-\infty
and again applying Lemma
4
,
f^{cc^{\prime}}\equiv\emptyset
. Hence, if either
f_{K}\equiv Z
or
f\equiv\emptyset
, it follows that
f^{cc^{\prime}}=f_{K}
.

Suppose that
f_{K}\not\equiv Z
and
\dom f\neq\emptyset
. According to Lemma
4
, it is enough to prove that
f^{cc^{\prime}}(x)\subseteq f_{K}(x)
, for all
x\in X
. In first place, let us observe that,
according again to Lemma
4
,
\emptyset\neq\dom f\subseteq\dom f^{cc^{\prime}}
and, moreover, recalling Remark
12
,
\dom\sigma_{f}\neq\emptyset
, and for all
x\in\dom f^{cc^{\prime}}
,
f^{cc^{\prime}}(x)\neq Z
and
f^{cc^{\prime}}
is proper. Since
f_{K}(x)\subseteq f^{cc^{\prime}}(x)
, for all
x\in X
, and
f_{K}\not\equiv\emptyset
,
f_{K}
is also proper.

Applying Theorem
3.4
to both functions,
f
and
f^{cc^{\prime}}
, we have
\mathcal{E}_{f}\neq\emptyset
,
\mathcal{E}_{f^{cc^{\prime}}}\neq\emptyset
(see the proof of Theorem
3.4
) and

\epi_{K}f=\bigcap_{\mathcal{E}_{f}}\epi_{K}a\text{ and }\epi_{K}f^{cc^{\prime}}=\bigcap_{\mathcal{E}_{f^{cc^{\prime}}}}\epi_{K}a.

We will show that
\mathcal{E}_{f}\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{f^{cc^{\prime}}}
. Take any e-affine function
a\in\mathcal{E}_{f}
,

a(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}S_{(x^{*},z^{*})}(x)+\tilde{z}&\text{if }\langle x,y^{*}\rangle<\alpha,\\
\emptyset&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Then
f(x)\subseteq a(x)
, for all
x\in X
, and it holds, for all
x\in\dom f\subset H^{-}_{y^{*},\alpha}
,

f(x)\subseteq S_{(x^{*},z^{*})}(x)+\tilde{z}.

Taking into account that
\bar{S}_{(x^{*},z^{*})}(-x)+S_{(x^{*},z^{*})}(x)\subseteq S_{(x^{*},z^{*})}(0)
, we will obtain, for all
x\in\dom f
,

f(x)+\bar{S}_{(x^{*},z^{*})}(-x)\subseteq S_{(x^{*},z^{*})}(0)+\tilde{z}.

Clearly
S_{(x^{*},z^{*})}(0)+\tilde{z}
is an e-convex set, hence

-f^{c}(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*},\alpha)\subseteq S_{(x^{*},z^{*})}(0)+\tilde{z},

(10)

and
(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*},\alpha)\in\dom f^{c}
.

Now, if for some point
\bar{x}\in X
,
f^{cc^{\prime}}(\bar{x})=\emptyset
, then
f^{cc^{\prime}}(\bar{x})\subseteq a(\bar{x})
and
a(\bar{x})\leq_{K}f^{cc^{\prime}}(\bar{x})
. Then, assuming that
f^{cc^{\prime}}(\bar{x})\neq\emptyset
, from (
10
) and Definition
11
we conclude that

f^{cc^{\prime}}(\bar{x})\subseteq\bar{S}_{(x^{*},z^{*})}(\bar{x})+S_{(x^{*},z^{*})}(0)+\tilde{z}\subseteq S_{(x^{*},z^{*})}(\bar{x})+\tilde{z}=a(\bar{x}),

and
a\in\mathcal{E}_{f^{cc^{\prime}}}
. Hence,
\epi_{K}f^{cc^{\prime}}\subseteq\epi_{K}f
and
f^{cc^{\prime}}(x)\subseteq f_{K}(x)
, for all
x\in X
.
∎

Theorem 4.2

Let
f:X\to\mathcal{P}(Z)
be a set-valued function. Then
f^{cc^{\prime}}=K\text{-}\eco f
.

Proof

By Lemma
4
,
f^{cc^{\prime}}(x)\leq_{K}f(x)
, for all
x\in X
, hence
f^{cc^{\prime}}(x)\leq_{K}\Keco f(x)
, for all
x\in X
, since
f^{cc^{\prime}}
is a
K
-e-convex minorant of
f
.

Now we will show that
\Keco f(x)\leq_{K}f^{cc^{\prime}}(x)
, i.e.
f^{cc^{\prime}}(x)\subseteq\Keco f(x)
, for all
x\in X
.

Since
f(x)+K\subseteq\Keco f(x)
, for all
x\in X
, in particular,
\dom f\subseteq\dom\Keco f
. Take
(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*},\alpha)\in X^{*}\times X^{*}\times(K^{*}\setminus\{0\})\times\mathbb{R}
, such that
\dom f\subseteq H^{-}_{y^{*},\alpha}
. Then, by Definition
12
,

\sigma_{f}(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*},\alpha)=\mathop{\rm sup}_{\epi_{K}f}\{\langle x,x^{*}\rangle+\langle z,z^{*}\rangle\}\leq\mathop{\rm sup}_{\epi_{K}\Keco f}\{\langle x,x^{*}\rangle+\langle z,z^{*}\rangle\}

(11)

Therefore, if
\dom f\subseteq H^{-}_{y^{*},\alpha}
,

\sigma_{f}(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*},\alpha)\leq\sigma_{\Keco f}(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*},\alpha).

On the other hand, if
\dom f\not\subseteq H^{-}_{y^{*},\alpha}
,
\dom\Keco f\not\subseteq H^{-}_{y^{*},\alpha}
either, and
hence,
\sigma_{f}(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*},\alpha)=\sigma_{\Keco f}(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*},\alpha)=+\infty
. According to Lemma
4
, for all
x\in X
,
f^{cc^{\prime}}(x)\subseteq{(\Keco f)}^{cc^{\prime}}(x)=\Keco f(x)
, the last equality follows from Theorem
4.1
.
∎

Example 4

Let us consider a well-known extended real-valued function, the indicator function of a set
C\subseteq X
,
\delta_{C}:X\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
,

\delta_{C}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\text{if }x\in C,\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

We will have that
C
is e-convex iff
\delta_{C}
is e-convex. On the other hand, denoting by
\delta_{C}^{*}
the Fenchel conjugate of
\delta_{C}
, we have that the c-conjugate of
\delta
,
\delta^{c}_{C}:X^{*}\times X^{*}\times\mathbb{R}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
, is

\delta^{c}_{C}(x^{*},y^{*},\alpha)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\delta^{*}_{C}(x^{*})&\text{if }C\subseteq H^{-}_{y^{*},\alpha},\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise,}\end{array}\right.

where
\delta_{C}^{*}(x^{*})=\sigma_{C}(x^{*})
is the well-known support function of
C
. Moreover,
\delta^{cc^{\prime}}_{C}=\delta_{\eco C}
.

Coming back to the set-valued functions framework, with
K
a convex cone and taking into account that
\eco K+K=\eco K
, let us consider the set-valued indicator function of a set
C\subset X
,
\Delta_{C}:X\to\mathcal{R}_{K}(Z)
,

\Delta_{C}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\eco K&\text{if }x\in C,\\
\emptyset&\text{otherwise,}\end{array}\right.

which is
K
-e-convex iff
C
is e-convex. We calculate its c-conjugate (respect to
K
),

\Delta^{c}_{C}(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*},\alpha)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\eco\left[\bigcup_{C}\{\bar{S}_{(x^{*},z^{*})}(-x)\}\right]&\text{if }C\subseteq H^{-}_{y^{*},\alpha},\\
\emptyset&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Making a comparison with the (convex) conjugate set-valued function of
\Delta_{C}
suggested in
(
H2009
, Section 4)
,
\Delta^{*}_{C}:X^{*}\times(K^{*}\setminus\{0\})\to\mathcal{P}(Z)
,

\Delta^{*}_{C}(x^{*},z^{*})=-\cl\left[\bigcup_{C}\{\bar{S}_{(x^{*},z^{*})}(-x)\}\right],

we deduce that, if
C\subseteq H^{-}_{y^{*},\alpha}
, we could have
\Delta^{c}_{C}(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*},\alpha)\subsetneq\Delta^{*}_{C}(x^{*},z^{*})
, this is not the case in the scalar framework, but recall that also
K
-convex hull concept is tighter in the set-valued context than in the scalar one. Finally, we also have that
\Delta^{cc^{\prime}}_{C}=\Delta_{\eco C}
.

Group character averages via a single Laguerre
Alexei Morozov, Kazumi Okuyama

Abstract
Average of exponential Tr R ​ e X {\rm Tr}_{R}e^{X} , i.e. of a group rather than an algebra character, in Gaussian matrix model is known to be an amusing generalization of Schur polynomial, where time variables are substituted by traces of products of non-commuting matrices Tr ⁡ ( ∏ i A k i ) {\rm Tr}\left(\prod_{i}A_{k_{i}}\right) and are thus labeled by weak compositions. The entries of matrices A k A_{k} are made from extended Laguerre polynomials, what introduces additional difficulties. We describe the generic sum rules, which express arbitrary traces through convolutions of a single Laguerre polynomial L N − 1 1 ​ ( z k i ) L_{N-1}^{1}(z_{k_{i}}) , what is a considerable simplification.

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(s)_{ij}=\sqrt{\frac{i!}{j!}}s^{j-i}L_{i}^{j-i}(-s^{2}).

(2.1)

Note that

\mathcal{A}(s)_{ij}=\mathcal{A}(s)_{ji}

We also use the notation

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}\,({\cal A}_{1})^{m}=\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}=0}^{N-1}\prod_{k=1}^{m}L^{i_{k+1}-i_{k}}_{i_{k}}(z),

(2.2)

In this sense

L^{\alpha}_{n}(z)

\alpha

-N+1
to
N-1

It turns out that this trace can be rewritten through those with
\alpha=1
only.

Making use of the generating function for Laguerre polynomials,

\displaystyle\frac{e^{\frac{tz}{1-t}}}{(1-t)^{\alpha+1}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}L^{\alpha}_{n}(z)t^{n},

(2.3)

we obtain:

{\bf m=1}
:

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}\,{\cal A}_{1}=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}L^{0}_{i}(z)=\oint\frac{e^{\frac{tz}{1-t}}}{(1-t)}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\frac{dt}{t^{i+1}}=\oint\frac{e^{\frac{tz}{1-t}}}{(1-t)^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{t^{N}}-\underline{1}\right)dt=L^{1}_{N-1}(z).

(2.4)

Note that the underlined
\underline{1}
can be omitted,
since it gives rise to a series with non-negative powers of
t
only,
which is annihilated by
\oint dt
.

{\bf m=2}
:

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}\,({\cal A}_{1})^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=0}^{N-1}L^{j-i}_{i}(z)L^{i-j}_{j}(z)=\oint\oint\sum_{i,j=0}^{N-1}\frac{e^{\frac{tz}{1-t}+\frac{t^{\prime}z}{1-t^{\prime}}}}{(1-t)^{j-i+1}(1-t^{\prime})^{i-j+1}}\frac{dtdt^{\prime}}{t^{i+1}{t^{\prime}}^{j+1}}

(2.5)

\displaystyle=\oint\oint\frac{e^{\frac{tz}{1-t}+\frac{t^{\prime}z}{1-t^{\prime}}}dtdt^{\prime}}{(1-t)(1-t^{\prime})}\cdot\frac{\left(\frac{1-t}{t(1-t^{\prime})}\right)^{N+1}-\underline{1}}{\frac{1-t}{t(1-t^{\prime})}-1}\cdot\frac{\left(\frac{1-t^{\prime}}{t^{\prime}(1-t)}\right)^{N+1}-\underline{1}}{\frac{1-t^{\prime}}{t^{\prime}(1-t)}-1}

\displaystyle=\oint\oint\frac{e^{\frac{tz}{1-t}+\frac{t^{\prime}z}{1-t^{\prime}}}}{(1-2t-tt^{\prime})(1-2t^{\prime}+tt^{\prime})}\cdot\frac{dtdt^{\prime}}{t^{N}{t^{\prime}}^{N}},

where again the underlined terms do not contribute and can be omitted for the same reason.
Remarkably this is equal to a simple convolution of two Laguerre polynomials:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}dxe^{-x}L^{1}_{N-1}(z+x)L^{1}_{N-1}(z-x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\oint\oint\frac{e^{\frac{t(z+x)}{1-t}+\frac{t^{\prime}(z-x)}{1-t^{\prime}}}}{(1-t)^{2}(1-t^{\prime})^{2}}\cdot\frac{dtdt^{\prime}}{t^{N}{t^{\prime}}^{N}}

(2.6)

\displaystyle=\oint\oint\frac{e^{\frac{tz}{1-t}+\frac{t^{\prime}z}{1-t^{\prime}}}}{(1-t)(1-t^{\prime})(1-2t^{\prime}+tt^{\prime})}\cdot\frac{dtdt^{\prime}}{t^{N}{t^{\prime}}^{N}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\oint\oint\frac{e^{\frac{tz}{1-t}+\frac{t^{\prime}z}{1-t^{\prime}}}}{(1-t)(1-t^{\prime})}\left(\frac{1}{1-2t^{\prime}+tt^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{1-2t+tt^{\prime}}\right)\cdot\frac{dtdt^{\prime}}{t^{N}{t^{\prime}}^{N}}

\displaystyle=\oint\oint\frac{e^{\frac{tz}{1-t}+\frac{t^{\prime}z}{1-t^{\prime}}}}{(1-2t+tt^{\prime})(1-2t^{\prime}+tt^{\prime})}\cdot\frac{dtdt^{\prime}}{t^{N}{t^{\prime}}^{N}}

where we made use of the obvious symmetry between
t
and
t^{\prime}
.
Thus

\displaystyle\boxed{{\rm Tr}\,({\cal A}_{1})^{2}=\int_{0}^{\infty}dxe^{-x}L^{1}_{N-1}(z+x)L^{1}_{N-1}(z-x)}

is indeed expressed through

{\bf m=3}

5
Comparison to
[
3
]

Despite connected correlators satisfy nice single-trace formulas,
their physical significance remains obscure.
Usually connected correlators are introduced to eliminate
pair singularities and concentrate on the less trivial structures of the amplitudes.
Actually there are no singularities in matrix models, but the terminology
and the definitions come from the higher dimensional QFT, where propagators are singular.
For this purpose one should consider subtractions from
\langle\prod_{i}{\rm Tr}\,X^{k_{i}}\rangle
,
i.e. first expand our
\langle\prod_{i}{\rm Tr}\,e^{s_{i}X}\rangle

into infinite series, then expand – and subtract only after that.
We denote this two-step prescription by the double angular brackets in (
5.3
) below.
The results of this kind were derived in
[
3
]
and they have their own beauty –
still different from the one, which we emphasize in the present paper.

Additional trick in
[
3
]
was to use
some peculiar generating functions for correlators, summed over matrix sizes
N
.
The simplest of them is

\displaystyle e_{1}(z,s):=\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}z^{N}\langle{\rm Tr}\,e^{sX}\rangle=\frac{z}{(1-z)^{2}}\exp\left(\frac{1+z}{1-z}\cdot\frac{s^{2}}{2}\right)

(5.1)

This can be compared with the fixed
N
result of

\langle{\rm Tr}\,e^{sX}\rangle
in (
1.6
).
Indeed (
1.6
) is reproduced from (
5.1
)
by using the generating function of the Laguerre polynomials
(
2.3
)

\displaystyle\frac{z}{(1-z)^{2}}\exp\left(\frac{zs^{2}}{1-z}\right)=\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}z^{N}L^{1}_{N-1}(-s^{2}).

(5.2)

A more important/general claim of
[
3
]
is that
– as a consequence of integrability of the underlying matrix model
[
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
,
20
]
–
the generating functions
for connected correlation functions

\displaystyle e_{m}(z;s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{m})=\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}z^{N}\langle\langle{\rm Tr}\,\,e^{s_{1}X}\ldots{\rm Tr}\,\,e^{s_{m}X}\rangle\rangle_{c}

(5.3)

satisfy eq.(77) of that paper,

\displaystyle z\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\left(\frac{(1-z)^{2}}{z}\,e_{m}(z;s_{1},\ldots,s_{m})-g_{m}(z;s_{1},\ldots,s_{m})\right)=(s_{1}^{2}+\ldots+s_{m}^{2})e_{m}(z;s_{1},\ldots,s_{m})

(5.4)

i.e. that there is an explicit recurrence in

.
Sometime this equation can be solved, like in (

) for

when

m=2

[
3

Actually for
{\bf m=1}

by our

\displaystyle e_{1}(z;s)=\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}z^{N}{\rm Tr}\,_{N\times N}A(s)

\displaystyle z\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\left(\frac{(1-z)^{2}}{z}e_{1}(z;s)\right)\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{e1})}}{{=}}\ z\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\exp\left(\frac{1+z}{1-z}\cdot\frac{s^{2}}{2}\right)=\frac{zs^{2}}{(1-z)^{2}}\exp\left(\frac{1+z}{1-z}\cdot\frac{s^{2}}{2}\right)=s^{2}e_{1}(z;s).

(5.6)

Note that in this case normalization does not matter (or is not defined):

Consistency with Laguerre was already checked in (

\displaystyle\sum_{N}\Big((N-1)z^{N-1}-2Nz^{N}+(N+1)z^{N+1}\Big)L_{N-1}^{1}(-s^{2})=s^{2}\sum_{N}z^{N}L_{N-1}^{1}(-s^{2}),

For

The physically-artificial nature of our subtraction procedure is obvious already for

6
Conclusion and outlook

We provided an expression for the would-be-non-Abelian time-variables,
i.e. the traces of products of non-commuting matrices in
[
12
,
11
]
,
through a single Laguerre polynomial
L_{N-1}^{1}
–
instead of the huge sets of different extended Laguerres
L_{i}^{\alpha}
.

Described in section
3
are expressions through
L^{1}_{N-1}
of all the “time-variables”,
appearing in generalized Schur polynomials, which enter the formulas for
Gaussian averages of the group characters
\langle{\rm Tr}_{R}e^{\cal X}\rangle

in
[
11
]
.
From the point of view of integrability theory
the most interesting new phenomenon in these formulas is that, say,

{\rm Tr}\,A(s_{1})A(s_{2})A(s_{3})A(s_{4})\neq{\rm Tr}\,A(s_{1})A(s_{2})A(s_{4})A(s_{3})

what implies a considerable “non-abelian” extension of the set of time-variables.
According to field/string theory consideration, like in
[
12
]
,
it seems to be a necessary step in theories with the space-time.
It is a striking fact that this generalization can be made already in matrix models –
thus further extending their role of a simple, still representative prototype
of the entire string theory.
Hopefully, the simplified expressions for these traces, which are described in this paper,
will help to tame the zoo of these new variables – and functions, which depend on them.

Of certain interest is the matching of the single-trace formulas for connected correlators
in section
4
with Toda-integrability properties, which we began to discuss in section
5
.
Further work in this direction also looks promising.

Bayesian estimators of diversity indexes on exchangeable random partitions Thanks: Corresponding author: Servet Martinez, smartine@dim.uchile.cl
Servet Martinez 1

Abstract
. We study indexes of diversity of the abundance of species when their proportions are organized as an exchangeable random partition and we take a sample from them. Firstly, we prove a general result: the sequence of posterior Bayesian estimators of any integrable function defined on countable partitions of the unit interval is an integrable martingale that converges a.s. and in L 1 L^{1} to the function, when the sample size diverges to infinity. Hence, the posterior Bayesian estimator fluctuates as an integrable martingale. For the Poisson-Dirichlet Process, we study the estimators of the entropy and the Gini indexes in more detail. A series of results are devoted to revealing that the behavior of the Bayesian estimators share a number of similarities with the plug-in estimators. The first concerns its a.s. limit behavior, but we focus on other behaviors, expressing local relations between these estimators. This is the case for the one-step difference of the conditional plug-in entropy of the individuals given that their species is known. We prove that it can be rephrased for the Bayesian entropy estimator and this gives a one-step difference between processes that does not jump only when a new species is found. Similar behavior is established for the Gini index.

2.

The Kingman’s theory describe this class of partitions by using random partitions of

[0,1]

Let

be the
class of probability sequences ordered in a decreasing way, be endowed with a probability measure

. Let

be a random sequence with values in

and distributed
according to

.
The mean expected value with respect to

To each

Let

Consider the sequence of species

3.3.
The entropy and the Gini indexes

We are interested on functions
G
which serve to measure the degree of
mixture of proportions of the species in some population. In particular we will focus on:

The Shannon entropy
\mathcal{H}(s)=-\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}s(i)\log(s(i))
, introduced and studied in
[
25
]
and
the Gini function
\mathfrak{G}(s)=1-\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}s(i)^{2}
, that is the probability that
two independent classes, both chosen with distribution
s
, are different.
It was introduced in
[
27
]
to study the diversity of groups in a population.
We also consider
\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}(s)=1-\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}s(i)^{\kappa+1}=\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}s(i)(1-s(i)^{\kappa})

the generalized Gini function of parameter
\kappa>0
. When
\kappa\in{\mathbb{N}}
it is
the probability that
\kappa+1
independent classes chosen with law
s
,
are not all the same.

The generalized Gini function and the Shannon entropy
satisfy the hypotheses of what is called an impurity function defined on the sequences
s\in{\mathcal{S}}

having a finite set of non-vanishing masses (see Definition
2.5
and Proposition
A.1
in
[
4
]
).
On the set of
s\in{\mathcal{S}}
having at most
n
non-vanishing masses these hypotheses are:
being nonnegative and symmetric in the components; vanishing at
s=\delta_{n}(i)
;
reaching its maximum at
(1/n,..,1/n)
;
and being concave, so the function of a mixture of sequences of masses is greater or equal
than the mixture of the functions of these sequences.

The indexes
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}
are sum-type functions, that is of the form

G(s)=\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}g(s(i))
with
g:[0,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
a Borel function. For this type of
functions it holds,

E_{P}\left(\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}g(S(i))\right)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{g(x)}{x}dF(x)\hbox{ where }\widehat{\pi}(1)\sim F,

that is
F
is the distribution of the first size-biased picking interval.
See relation (
25
) in
[
21
]
.
For
G=\mathcal{H}
one has
E_{P}(\mathcal{H})=-\int_{0}^{1}\log(x)dF(x)
,
so
E_{P}(\mathcal{H})\!<\!\infty
is equivalent to
\int_{0}^{1}{\log}(x)dF(x)\!>\!-\infty
and
when this holds we can apply Proposition
1
to
\mathcal{H}
.
On the other hand, we have

E_{P}(\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)})=1-\int_{0}^{1}x^{\kappa}dF(x)
. Moreover, since
\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}

is bounded,
E_{P}((\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)})^{p})
is finite
\forall p\geq 1
so in
Proposition
1
the
L^{p}({\mathbf{P}})
convergence is satisfied.

5.2.
Maxmin property along trajectories of the plug-in estimators

Let us fix some
s\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\downarrow}
and
{\mathcal{J}}^{s}
be a fixed countable partition of the unit interval.
Let us describe what happens along a trajectory of the plug-in estimator. So, the observations

X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
are grouped into classes enumerated sequentially following the time of discovery,
this defines a partition
\xi^{n}
of
I_{n}=\{1,\dots,n\}
and its classes are also called
the species discovered up to
n
. We set
\pi^{n}=(\pi^{n}(j)=\#\xi(j):j=1,\dots,k^{n})
.
Let
j^{*}
be the class containing
X_{n}
,
so, if
j^{*}
is firstly observed before or at time
n-1
then

k^{n}=k^{n-1}
and
\pi^{n}(j^{*})=\pi^{n-1}(j^{*})+1
, and if
X_{n}
defines a new class then

k^{n}=k^{n-1}+1
,
j^{*}=k^{n}
and
\pi^{n}(k^{n})=1
.

We will describe four properties seen in a trajectory of the plug-in estimators for the entropy and
the Gini functions, that will be retrieved for the Bayesian posterior estimators. The results hold for
the generalized Gini indexes, but with parameter
\kappa\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and this condition is always assumed.

We put
0\log 0=0
. Let us consider the following sequences for
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\gamma_{0}(m)\!=\!m\log m-(m-1)\log(m-1),\;\gamma_{\kappa}(m)\!=\!m^{\kappa+1}-(m-1)^{\kappa+1}=\sum_{l=0}^{\kappa}\binom{\kappa}{l}(m-1)^{l}\,,

that are strictly increasing on
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Also
\gamma_{0}(m)\geq 0
,
\gamma_{\kappa}(m)\geq 1
.

Recall that
\mathfrak{m}_{n}
is the set of all elements
\pi^{n}
. It is useful to denote
{\mathbf{1}}^{n}=(1,..,1)

the
\pi^{n}
vector of length
n
constituted only of
1^{\prime}s
, so
when each variable
X_{l}
defines a different class for
=1,..,n
, or equivalently when
k^{n}=n
.

The plug-in entropy and the Gini functions at step
n
are respectively given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(\pi^{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=1}^{k^{n}}\frac{\pi^{n}(j)}{n}\log\left(\frac{\pi^{n}(j)}{n}\right)=-\frac{1}{n}\big(\sum_{j=1}^{k^{n}}\pi^{n}(j)\log\pi^{n}(j)-n\log n\big),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}(\pi^{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\sum_{j=1}^{k^{n}}\left(\frac{\pi^{n}(j)}{n}\right)^{\kappa+1}=-\frac{1}{n^{\kappa+1}}\big(\sum_{j=1}^{k^{n}}(\pi^{n}(j))^{\kappa+1}-n^{\kappa+1}\big).

Let
I_{n}
be endowed with the uniform probability that gives a weight
1/n
to each
j\in I_{n}
.
Hence
\mathcal{H}(\pi^{n})
is the Shannon entropy of the partition
\xi^{n}
of
I_{n}
.

{\mathbf{I}a}
. One has
\mathcal{H}({\mathbf{1}}^{n})=\log n
and
\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}({\mathbf{1}}^{n})=(n^{\kappa}-1)/n^{\kappa}
. Moreover,

0\leq\mathcal{H}(\pi^{n})\leq\mathcal{H}({\mathbf{1}}^{n})
and
0\leq\mathfrak{G}(\pi^{n})\leq\mathfrak{G}({\mathbf{1}}^{n})
, so
for both,
\mathcal{H}(\pi^{n})
and
\mathfrak{G}(\pi^{n})
, the maximum value is attained at
{\mathbf{1}}^{n}
,
and the minimum value is
0
and happens only when
k^{n}=1
.

{\mathbf{I}Ia}
. We will introduce some quantities whose meaning for the entropy is the following one.
Let us select uniformly an individual in
I_{n}
.
The entropy
\mathcal{H}(\pi^{n})
is the mean information of
\xi^{n}
defined by the species of
the individuals up to
n
,
and
\mathcal{H}({\mathbf{1}}^{n})
corresponds to the mean information of the individuals.
Then,
\mathcal{H}({\mathbf{1}}^{n})-\mathcal{H}(\pi^{n})
is the mean information needed to identify an individual given
that its species is determined, or the conditional entropy of the individual given the species.

Now, let us makes
n
independent experiences, each one of them selecting uniformly an individual in

I_{n}
. Then,
n\mathcal{H}(\pi^{n})
is the mean information given by the species of the
n

independent individuals and
n(H({\mathbf{1}}^{n})-\mathcal{H}(\pi^{n}))
is the mean information of the
n

independent individuals given that their species are determined.

For the Gini index of parameter
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
:
\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}({\mathbf{1}}^{n})\!-\!\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}(\pi^{n})
, is
the probability that the species of
\kappa+1
independent individuals are the same, but the individuals
are not all equal.

Define the sequences,

(20)

\ell^{\mathrm{\mathcal{H}}}(\pi^{n})\!=\!n(\mathcal{H}({\mathbf{1}}^{n})-\mathcal{H}(\pi^{n})),\;\ell^{\mathrm{\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}}}(\pi^{n})\!=\!n^{\kappa+1}(\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}({\mathbf{1}}^{n})\!-\!\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}(\pi^{n})),\;n\geq 1,

and take
\ell^{\mathrm{\mathcal{H}}}(\pi^{0})=0=\ell^{\mathrm{\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}}}(\pi^{0})
. Both quantities,
\ell^{\mathrm{\mathcal{H}}}(\pi^{n})

and
\ell^{\mathrm{\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}}}(\pi^{n})
are nonnegative.

We have
\ell^{\mathrm{\mathcal{H}}}(\pi^{n})=\sum_{j=1}^{k^{n}}\pi^{n}(j)\log\pi^{n}(j)
, so

\ell^{\mathrm{\mathcal{H}}}(\pi^{n})-\ell^{\mathrm{\mathcal{H}}}_{n-1}(\pi^{n-1})=\gamma_{0}(\pi^{n}(j^{*}))\geq 0
,
and
\ell^{\mathrm{\mathcal{H}}}(\pi^{n})-\ell^{\mathrm{\mathcal{H}}}(\pi^{n-1})=0
only when
\pi^{n}(j^{*})=1
, so if a
new class is observed at time
n
.

On the other hand

\ell^{\mathrm{\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}}}(\pi^{n})=-n+\sum_{j=1}^{k^{n}}\pi^{n}(j)^{\kappa+1}
, then

\ell^{\mathrm{\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}}}(\pi^{n})-\ell^{\mathrm{\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}}}(\pi^{n-1})=\big(\pi^{n}(j^{*})^{\kappa+1}-(\pi^{n}(j^{*})-1)^{\kappa+1}\big)-1\geq 0
,
that vanishes only when
\pi^{n}(j^{*})=1
, similarly as it happens for the entropy.

{\mathbf{I}IIa}
. Consider the sequences
n\mathcal{H}(\pi^{n})
and
n^{\kappa+1}\mathfrak{G}(\pi^{n})
. We have

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}^{\mathrm{\mathcal{H}}}(\pi^{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n\mathcal{H}(\pi^{n})-(n-1)\mathcal{H}(\pi^{n-1})=\gamma_{0}(n)-\gamma_{0}(\pi^{n}(j^{*}))\hbox{ and }

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}^{\mathrm{\mathfrak{G}}^{(\kappa)}}(\pi^{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n^{\kappa+1}\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}(\pi^{n})-(n-1)^{\kappa+1}\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}(\pi^{n-1})=\gamma_{\kappa}(n)-\gamma_{\kappa}(\pi^{n}(j^{*})).

Then,
\Delta_{n}^{\mathrm{\mathcal{H}}}(\pi^{n})
and
\Delta_{n}^{\mathrm{{\mathfrak{G}}^{(\kappa)}}}(\pi^{n})

are nonnegative and they vanish for some
n

only when
\pi^{n}(j^{*})=n
, that is when all individuals belong to a single class, and they attain a maximum value
when
\pi^{n}(j^{*})=1
, so if a new class is observed at
n
.

{\mathbf{I}Va}
. Let
\mathfrak{m}_{n}(k)
be the set of all
\pi^{n}\in\mathfrak{m}_{n}
with
k
classes. Let
j,l
be two
different coordinates in
\{1,...,k\}
.
Let
\pi^{n}\in\mathfrak{m}_{n}(k)
be such that
\pi^{n}(j)\geq\pi^{n}(l)+2
. We define
\underline{\pi}^{n}\in\mathfrak{m}_{n}(k)

by
\underline{\pi}^{n}(i)=\pi^{n}(i)
for
i\in\{1,..,k\}\setminus\{j,l\}
and

\underline{\pi}^{n}(j)=\pi^{n}(j)-1
,
\underline{\pi}^{n}(l)=\pi^{n}(l)+1
. We have the property,

(21)

\mathcal{H}(\underline{\pi}^{n})\geq\mathcal{H}(\pi^{n})\hbox{ and }\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}(\underline{\pi}^{n})\geq\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}(\pi^{n}).

This follows from

\,n(\mathcal{H}(\underline{\pi}^{n})-\mathcal{H}(\pi^{n}))=\gamma_{0}(\pi^{n}(j))-\gamma_{0}(\pi^{n}(l)+1)\geq 0
and

n^{\kappa+1}(\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}(\underline{\pi}^{n})-\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}(\pi^{n}))=\gamma_{\kappa}(\pi^{n}(j))-\gamma_{\kappa}(\pi^{n}(l)+1)\geq 0
.
For the inequalities we use that
\gamma_{0}
and
\gamma_{\kappa}
are increasing functions.

By iterating the inequality (
21
) one shows that:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(\pi_{\mathrm{m}in(k)}^{n})=\min\limits_{\pi^{n}\in\mathfrak{m}_{n}(k)}\mathcal{H}(\pi^{n})\,,\;\,\mathcal{H}(\pi_{\mathrm{m}ax(k)}^{n})=\max\limits_{\pi^{n}\in\mathfrak{m}_{n}(k)}\mathcal{H}(\pi^{n});

\displaystyle\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}(\pi_{\mathrm{m}in(k)}^{n})=\min\limits_{\pi^{n}\in\mathfrak{m}_{n}(k)}\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}(\pi^{n})\,,\;\,\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}(\pi_{\mathrm{m}ax(k)}^{n})=\max\limits_{\pi^{n}\in\mathfrak{m}_{n}(k)}\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}(\pi^{n});

where
\pi^{n}_{\mathrm{m}in(k)}
is constituted by one class containing
n-k+1
individuals and the other

k-1
classes are singletons and
\pi^{n}_{\mathrm{m}ax(k)}
is constituted by:
s
classes with

\lceil n/k\rceil
individuals and
k-s
with
\lfloor n/k\rfloor+1
individuals, being
s=k-(n-k\lfloor n/k\rfloor)
.
Here, as usual, by
\lfloor n/k\rfloor
we mean the integer part of
n/k
.
We will say that
\pi^{n}_{\mathrm{m}ax(k)}
divides
\{1,..,n\}
into
k
parts ’as uniform as possible’.

5.3.
Maxmin properties of the Bayesian posterior means for the PDP

We will define the analogous notions and state similar results as those shown in
{\mathbf{Ia,IIa,IIIa,IVa}}

in Section (
5.2
) for the Bayesian posterior means of the entropy and the Gini indexes.
But, this is be proven in the framework of the PDP
(\alpha,\theta)
with

0\leq\alpha<1
and
\theta>-\alpha
. These results
are stated for the entropy and the generalized Gini index with
\kappa\in{\mathbb{N}}
but the proofs will
be made in detail for the Gini index
\kappa=1
, to avoid heavy notation.
But at some parts of the proofs we will put the formulae for the generalized Gini index.

We recall that the Bayesian estimators for
\mathcal{H}
,
\mathfrak{G}
and
\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}

are given in (
13
), (
14
) and (
15
), respectively.
In many of the proofs make in these sections we will use the following equalities for
x>0
:

-for the entropy
x\psi(x+1)-(x-1)\psi(x)=\psi(x)+1
given in (
18
) and,

-for the Gini index of parameter
\kappa\!\in\!{\mathbb{N}}
,

\prod\limits_{r=0}^{\kappa}(x\!+r)-\prod\limits_{r=0}^{\kappa}(x\!-1\!+r)=(\kappa\!+\!1)\prod\limits_{r=0}^{\kappa-1}(x\!+r)
.

These functions,
\psi(x)+1
and
(\kappa+1)\prod_{r=0}^{\kappa-1}(x+r)

are strictly increasing in
(0,\infty)
. For the Gini index with
\kappa=1
,
the product equality reduces to

.

{\mathbf{I}b}
. The similar statement to
{\mathbf{I}a}
for the argmin of the Bayesian posterior means is:

Lemma 4
.

The minimum of the Bayesian posterior means of the entropy and the Gini function in
\mathfrak{m}_{n}
,
is attained for a single class
k^{n}=1
.

This result will be shown further, at the end of
{\mathbf{I}Vb}
.

Let us now prove the similar statement to the argmax of the Bayesian posterior means.

Lemma 5
.

The maximum of the Bayesian posterior means of the entropy and the Gini function in
\mathfrak{m}_{n}
,
is attained at
{\mathbf{1}}^{n}
, that is

(22)

\max\limits_{\pi^{n}\in\mathfrak{m}_{n}}\!E_{{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot|\pi^{n})}(\mathcal{H})\!=\!E_{{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot|{\mathbf{1}}^{n})}(\mathcal{H})\hbox{ and }\max\limits_{\pi^{n}\in\mathfrak{m}_{n}}\!E_{{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot|\pi^{n})}(\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)})\!=\!E_{{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot|{\mathbf{1}}^{n})}(\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}).

Proof.

Let us fix
\pi^{n-1}
with
k=k^{n-1}
classes. Assume
\widehat{\pi}^{n}
also has
k
classes
and for a unique
j
,
\widehat{\pi}^{n}(j)=\pi^{n-1}(j)+1
, for all the other coordinates
i\neq j
,

\widehat{\pi}^{n}(i)=\pi^{n-1}(i)
. Also consider
\pi^{n}_{+}=(\pi^{n-1},1)
that has
k+1
classes,
the last one having a unique element. By definition,

\pi^{n}_{+}(j)=\widehat{\pi}^{n}(j)-1
and for all other
i\neq j
in
\{1,..,k\}
one has
\widehat{\pi}^{n}_{+}(i)=\widehat{\pi}^{n}(i)
.
Let us show that for
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathfrak{G}
it holds:

\displaystyle(\theta+n)\big(E_{{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot|\pi^{n}_{+})}(\mathcal{H})-E_{{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot|\widehat{\pi}^{n})}(\mathcal{H})\big)>0\hbox{ and }

(23)

\displaystyle\big(\prod_{r=0}^{\kappa}(\theta+n\!+r)\big)\big(E_{{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot|\pi^{n}_{+})}(\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)})-E_{{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot|\widehat{\pi}^{n})}(\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)})\big)>0.

Let us prove it for the entropy. From (
13
) and by using (
18
) one obtains

\displaystyle(\theta+n)(E_{{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot|\pi^{n}_{+})}(\mathcal{H})-E_{{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot|\widehat{\pi}^{n})}(\mathcal{H}))

\displaystyle=-\alpha\psi(1-\alpha)-(1-\alpha)\psi(2-\alpha)+1+\psi(\pi^{n-1}(j)+1-\alpha)

\displaystyle=-\psi(1-\alpha)+\psi(\pi^{n-1}(j)+1-\alpha)>0,

because
\pi^{n-1}(j)+1>1
.
Now let us show it for the Gini function with
\kappa=1
. From (
14
) and by using
(x+1)x-x(x-1)=2x

for

, one gets

\displaystyle(\theta+n)(\theta+n+1)(E_{{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot|\pi^{n}_{+})}(\mathfrak{G})-E_{{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot|\widehat{\pi}^{n})}(\mathfrak{G}))

\displaystyle=-\alpha(1-\alpha)-(1-\alpha)(2-\alpha)+2(\pi^{n-1}(j)+1-\alpha)

\displaystyle=-2(1-\alpha)+2(\pi^{n-1}(j)+1-\alpha)=2\pi^{n-1}(j)>0.

For the generalized Gini index with
\kappa\in{\mathbb{N}}
it follows from,

\big(\!\prod_{r=0}^{\kappa}(\theta+n\!+r)\!\big)\!\big(\!E_{{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot|\pi^{n}_{+})}(\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)})-E_{{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot|\widehat{\pi}^{n})}(\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa})\big)\!=\!(\kappa+\!1)\big(\!\prod_{r=1}^{\kappa}(\pi^{n-1}(j)\!+r\!-\alpha)-\prod_{r=1}^{\kappa}\!(r\!-\alpha)\!\big)\!>\!0.

The iteration of equalities (
23
) allow to get relation (
22
).

{\mathbf{I}Ib}
. Define the following sequences,

(24)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}^{\mathrm{\mathcal{H}}}(\pi^{n})=(\theta+n)\left(E_{{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot|1^{n})}(\mathcal{H})-E_{{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot|\pi^{n})}(\mathcal{H})\right)\hbox{ and }

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}^{\mathrm{\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}}}(\pi^{n})=\big(\prod_{r=0}^{\kappa}(\theta+n+r)\big)\left(E_{{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot|1^{n})}(\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)})-E_{{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot|\pi^{n})}(\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)})\right),

which are adapted to the filtration
(\sigma(\Pi_{n}):n\in{\mathbb{N}})
and from Lemma
5

they are nonnegative. They satisfy the following properties
(the one devoted to the entropy was firstly proved in
[
15
]
Theorem
4.4
):

Proposition 6
.

The functions
{\mathcal{L}}^{\mathrm{\mathcal{H}}}(\pi^{n})-{\mathcal{L}}^{\mathrm{\mathcal{H}}}(\pi^{n-1})
and

{\mathcal{L}}^{\mathrm{\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}}}(\pi^{n})-{\mathcal{L}}^{\mathrm{\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}}}(\pi^{n-1})
are nonnegative and they
vanish only when
\pi^{n}=(\pi^{n-1},1)
, so if a new class appears at

.

Proof.

We make it for
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathfrak{G}
. We have,

\displaystyle(\theta+n)E_{\mathbf{P}(\cdot|{\mathbf{1}}^{n})}(\mathcal{H})\!=\!(\theta\!+n)\psi(\theta\!+n\!+1)\!-(\theta\!+\alpha n)\psi(1\!-\alpha)\!-n(1\!-\!\alpha)\psi(2\!-\alpha);

\displaystyle(\theta\!+\!n)(\theta\!+n\!+\!1)E_{{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot|{\mathbf{1}}^{n})}(\mathfrak{G})\!=(\theta\!+n)(\theta\!+n\!+\!1)\!-n(1\!-\alpha)(2-\!\alpha)\!-(\theta\!+\alpha n)(1\!-\!\alpha).

We continue with the notation used in the proof of Lemma
5
.
So,
\pi^{n}_{+}=(\pi^{n-1},1)
and
\widehat{\pi}^{n}
has
k=k^{n-1}
classes
and for a unique
j
,
\widehat{\pi}^{n}(j)=\pi^{n-1}(j)+1
, for all other

,

\widehat{\pi}^{n}(i)=\pi^{n-1}(i)
.

For the entropy we have

{\mathcal{L}}^{\mathrm{\mathcal{H}}}(\widehat{\pi}^{n})=-(\theta+\alpha(n\!-\!k))\psi(1\!-\!\alpha)\!-\!n(1\!-\!\alpha)\psi(2\!-\!\alpha)+\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\widehat{\pi}^{n}(i)\!-\!\alpha)\psi(\widehat{\pi}^{n}(i)\!-\!\alpha+1).

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}^{\mathrm{\mathcal{H}}}(\widehat{\pi}^{n})-{\mathcal{L}}^{\mathrm{\mathcal{H}}}(\pi^{n-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(\theta+\alpha(n\!-\!k-1))\psi(1\!-\!\alpha)-n(1\!-\alpha)\psi(2\!-\!\alpha)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\pi^{n-1}(i)\!-\!\alpha)\psi(\pi^{n-1}(i)\!-\alpha\!+1)+(1\!-\alpha)\psi(2\!-\alpha),

For the Gini function we have

{\mathcal{L}}^{\mathrm{\mathfrak{G}}}(\widehat{\pi}^{n})=-(\theta+\alpha(n\!-\!k))(1\!-\!\alpha)\!-\!n(1\!-\!\alpha)(2\!-\!\alpha)+\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\widehat{\pi}^{n}(i)\!-\!\alpha)(\widehat{\pi}^{n}(i)\!-\!\alpha+1).

{\mathcal{L}}^{\mathrm{\mathfrak{G}}}(\widehat{\pi}^{n})-{\mathcal{L}}^{\mathrm{\mathfrak{G}}}(\pi^{n-1})=-\alpha(1\!-\!\alpha)\!-(1\!-\!\alpha)(2\!-\alpha)\!+2(\widehat{\pi}^{n}(j)\!-\alpha)=2(\widehat{\pi}^{n}(j)\!-1)\!>0.

On the other hand

The result is shown for the Gini index. For the generalized Gini index with

The properties stated in
{\mathbf{I}Ia}
and Proposition
6

)

Corollary 7

The following five properties are equivalent (where

\displaystyle(i)\;\ell^{\mathrm{\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}}}(\pi^{n})=\ell^{\mathrm{\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}}}(\pi^{n-1}),\quad(ii)\;\ell^{\mathrm{\mathcal{H}}}(\pi^{n})=\ell^{\mathrm{\mathcal{H}}}(\pi^{n-1}),

{\mathbf{I}IIb}
. Now we prove:

The functions

\displaystyle(\theta+n)\psi(\theta+n+1)-(\theta+n-1)\psi(\theta+n)

Now, let us prove it for the Gini index with

\displaystyle\Delta^{\mathrm{\mathfrak{G}}}(\widehat{\pi}^{n})

\displaystyle(\theta+n)(\theta+n+1)-(\theta+n-1)(\theta+n)

\displaystyle-(\pi^{n-1}(j)-\alpha+1)(\pi^{n-1}(j)\!-\alpha\!+2)+(\pi^{n-1}(j)\!-\alpha)(\pi^{n-1}(j)\!-\alpha\!+1)

\displaystyle 2((\theta+n)-(\pi^{n-1}(j)\!+\!1\!-\!\alpha))>0.

\displaystyle\Delta^{\mathrm{\mathcal{H}}}(\pi^{n}_{+})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\theta+n)\psi(\theta+n+1)-(\theta+n-1)\psi(\theta+n)

\displaystyle-(1-\alpha)\psi(2-\alpha)-\alpha\psi(1-\alpha)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi(\theta\!+n)-\psi(1-\alpha)>\Delta^{\mathrm{\mathcal{H}}}_{n}(\widehat{\pi}^{n}),

the last strict inequality because
\pi^{n-1}(j)\geq 1
.
So,
\Delta^{\mathrm{\mathcal{H}}}(\pi^{n})
attains its maximum at
\pi^{n}=\pi^{n}_{+}
.
Analogously, from (
14
) we find,

\displaystyle\Delta^{\mathrm{\mathfrak{G}}}(\pi^{n}_{+})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\theta+n)(\theta+n+1)-(\theta+n-1)(\theta+n)-(1-\alpha)(2-\alpha)-\alpha(1-\alpha)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2((\theta+n)-(1-\alpha))>\Delta^{\mathrm{\mathfrak{G}}}_{n}(\widehat{\pi}^{n}),

and so
\Delta^{\mathrm{\mathfrak{G}}}(\pi^{n})
also attains its maximum at
\pi^{n}=\pi^{n}_{+}
.
For the generalized Gini index with
\kappa\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we have,

\displaystyle\Delta^{\mathrm{\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}}}(\widehat{\pi}^{n})=(\kappa+1)\big(\prod_{r=0}^{\kappa-1}(\theta\!+\!n\!+r)-\prod_{r=0}^{\kappa-1}(\pi^{n}(j)\!+r\!-\alpha)\big)>0\hbox{ and }

\displaystyle\Delta^{\mathrm{\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}}}(\pi^{n}_{+})=(\kappa+1)\big(\prod_{r=0}^{\kappa-1}(\theta\!+\!n\!+r)-\prod_{r=0}^{\kappa-1}(1\!+\!r\!-\alpha)\big)>\Delta^{\mathrm{\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}}}(\widehat{\pi}^{n}).

{\mathbf{I}Vb}
. Let us now state similar results as those stated in
{\mathbf{I}Va}
. We use similar notation,
so
\pi^{n}\in\mathfrak{m}_{n}(k)
is such that
\pi^{n}(j)\geq\pi^{n}(l)+2
and we define
\underline{\pi}^{n}\in\mathfrak{m}_{n}(k)

with
\underline{\pi}^{n}(i)=\pi^{n}(i)
for
i\in\{1,..,k\}\setminus\{j,l\}
and

\underline{\pi}^{n}(j)=\pi^{n}(j)-1
,
\underline{\pi}^{n}(l)=\pi^{n}(l)+1
. Let us prove that,

(25)

E_{{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot|\underline{\pi}^{n})}(\mathcal{H})\geq E_{{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot|\pi^{n})}(\mathcal{H})\hbox{ and }E_{{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot|\underline{\pi}^{n})}(\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)})\geq E_{{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot|\pi^{n})}(\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}).

For the entropy it follows from (
13
), by using (
18
)
and that
\psi
is increasing in
(0,\infty)
:

(\theta+n)(E_{{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot|\underline{\pi}^{n})}(\mathcal{H})-E_{{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot|\pi^{n})}(\mathcal{H}))=\psi(\pi^{n}(j)-\alpha)-\psi(\pi^{n}(l)+1-\alpha)\geq 0.

For the Gini function with
\kappa=1
it follows from (
14
),

(\theta+n)(\theta+n+1)(E_{{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot|\underline{\pi}^{n})}(\mathfrak{G})-E_{{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot|\pi^{n})}(\mathfrak{G}))=2(\pi^{n}(j)-(\pi^{n}(l)+1))\geq 0.

Fort the generalized Gini index with
\kappa\in{\mathbb{N}}
it follows from,

\prod_{r=0}^{\kappa}\!(\theta\!+n\!+r)\big(\!E_{{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot|\underline{\pi}^{n})}(\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)})-\!E_{{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot|\pi^{n})}(\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)})\!\big)\!=\!(\kappa\!+1)\big(\!\prod_{r=0}^{\kappa-1}\!(\pi^{n}(j)\!+r\!-\!\alpha))-\!\prod_{r=0}^{\kappa-1}\!(\pi^{n}(l)\!+\!1+\!r\!-\!\alpha)\!\big).

Therefore, by iterating (
25
) one shows that:

(26)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;E_{{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot|\pi^{n}_{\mathrm{m}in(k)})}(\mathcal{H}\!)\!=\!\!\!\!\min\limits_{\pi^{n}\in\mathfrak{m}_{n}(k)}\!E_{{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot|\pi^{n})}(\mathcal{H}\!),\,\!E_{{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot|\pi^{n}_{\mathrm{m}ax(k)})}(\mathcal{H}\!)\!=\!\!\!\!\max\limits_{\pi^{n}\in\mathfrak{m}_{n}(k)}\!E_{{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot|\pi^{n})}(\mathcal{H}\!),

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!E_{{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot|\pi^{n}_{\mathrm{m}in(k)})}(\!\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}\!)\!=\!\!\!\!\min\limits_{\pi^{n}\in\mathfrak{m}_{n}(k)}\!E_{{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot|\pi^{n})}(\!\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}\!),\,\!E_{{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot|\pi^{n}_{\mathrm{m}ax(k)})}(\!\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}\!)\!=\!\!\!\!\max\limits_{\pi^{n}\in\mathfrak{m}_{n}(k)}\!E_{{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot|\pi^{n})}(\!\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}\!),

where
\pi^{n}_{\mathrm{m}in(k)}
and
\pi^{n}_{\mathrm{m}ax(k)}
are described at the end of IVa in Section
5.2
.
We mention that the equalities for the entropy in (
26
) were firstly shown in Proposition 3.2 in
[
15
]
.

Let us use (
26
) to prove Lemma
4
. It suffices to show that for
1\leq k\leq n-1
one has:

E_{{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot|\pi^{n}_{\mathrm{m}in(k)})}(\mathcal{H})\leq E_{{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot|\pi^{n}_{\mathrm{m}in(k+1)})}\hbox{ and }E_{{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot|\pi^{n}_{\mathrm{m}in(k)})}(\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)})\leq E_{{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot|\pi^{n}_{\mathrm{m}in(k+1)})}(\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}).

The first inequality follows from:

\displaystyle(\theta+n)\left(E_{{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot|\pi^{n}_{\mathrm{m}in(k)})}(\mathcal{H})-E_{{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot|\pi^{n}_{\mathrm{m}in(k+1)})}(\mathcal{H})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha\psi(1\!-\!\alpha)\!+\!(1\!-\!\alpha)\psi(2\!-\!\alpha)\!-\!(n\!-k\!+1\!-\!\alpha)\psi(n\!-\!k\!+\!2\!-\!\alpha)\!+\!(n\!-\!k\!-\!\alpha)\psi(n\!-k\!+\!1\!-\!\alpha)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi(1-\alpha)-\psi(n-k+1-\alpha)<0.

Let us show the second one for the Gini index with
\kappa=1
,

\displaystyle(\theta+n)(\theta+n+1)\big(E_{{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot|\pi^{n}_{\mathrm{m}in(k)})}(\mathfrak{G})-E_{{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot|\pi^{n}_{\mathrm{m}in(k+1)})}(\mathfrak{G})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha(1\!-\!\alpha)\!+\!(1\!-\!\alpha)(2\!-\!\alpha)\!-\!(n\!-k\!+1\!-\!\alpha)(n\!-\!k\!+\!2\!-\!\alpha)\!+\!(n\!-\!k\!-\!\alpha)(n\!-k\!+\!1\!-\!\alpha)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2((1-\alpha)-(n-k+1-\alpha))<0.

For the generalized Gini index with
\kappa\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the last expression becomes,

\prod_{r=0}^{\kappa}(\theta+r)\big(E_{{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot|\pi^{n}_{\mathrm{m}in(k)})}(\mathfrak{G})-E_{{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot|\pi^{n}_{\mathrm{m}in(k+1)})}(\mathfrak{G})\big)\!=\!(\kappa+1)\big(\prod_{r=1}^{\kappa}(r\!-\alpha)-\prod_{r=1}^{\kappa}(r\!-\alpha\!+n\!-k)\big)\!<\!0.

So, Lemma
4
is proven.

5.4.
On the integrability of the Rényi entropy

The Rényi entropy of parameter
\zeta>0
and
\zeta\neq 1
, is defined by

\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{R}}_{\zeta}(s)=\frac{1}{1-\zeta}\log\left(\sum_{i\in I}s(i)^{\zeta}\right)
, see
[
24
]
.
The following equality is satisfied
\lim\limits_{\zeta\to 1}\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{R}}_{\zeta}(s)=\mathcal{H}(s)
.
When
0<\zeta<1
the Rényi entropy satisfies hypotheses of an impurity function.
To be able to apply the martingale characterization and limit results of Proposition
1
,
and also for using
[
1
]
, let us see its integrability on the exchangeable random partitions,
mainly on the PDP.

Let
\zeta>1
. So
\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}s(i)^{\zeta}\leq 1
, then
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{R}}_{\zeta}
is integrable if

\int\log(\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\widehat{\pi}(i)^{\zeta})d{\mathbb{P}}>-\infty
.
Since
\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\widehat{\pi}(i)^{\zeta}\geq\widehat{\pi}(1)^{\zeta}
, a sufficient condition
for integrability is
\int\log(\widehat{\pi}(1))d{\mathbb{P}}>-\infty
, that is if

\int_{0}^{1}{\log}(x)dF(x)\!>\!-\infty
which is the integrability condition for
the Shannon entropy
\mathcal{H}
. So, for the PDP and when
\zeta>1
,
the Rényi entropy
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{R}}_{\zeta}
is integrable and so Proposition
1
can be applied.

Let
\zeta\in(0,1)
. So,
\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}s(i)^{\zeta}>1
, then
H^{\mathrm{R}}_{\zeta}
is integrable when

\int\log(\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\widehat{\pi}(i)^{\zeta})d{\mathbb{P}}<\infty
. A sufficient
condition to have integrability is
\int(\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\widehat{\pi}(i)^{\zeta})d{\mathbb{P}}<\infty
, this is

\int_{0}^{1}x^{\zeta-1}dF(x)<\infty
.
Then, for the PDP
(\alpha,\theta)
a sufficient condition for the integrability
of
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{R}}_{\zeta}
is
\alpha<\zeta<1
.
We mention that under this condition, in addition to Proposition
1
,
the plug-in estimator of the Rényi entropy
satisfies
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{R}}_{\zeta}=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{R}}_{\zeta}(\pi^{n}/n)\;\;{\mathbf{P}}\!-\!\hbox{a.s.}
. See p. 171 in
[
1
]
.

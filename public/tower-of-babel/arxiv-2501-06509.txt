On an Identity by Bruckman and Good
Hongshen Chua

Abstract




1
Introduction

Define the
generalized Lucas sequence
,
(w_{n})_{n\geq 0}=w_{n}(a,b;p,q)
recursively by

\displaystyle w_{0}=a,\quad w_{1}=b,\quad w_{n}=pw_{n-1}-qw_{n-2},

where
a,b,p
, and
q
can be complex numbers. Additionally, we define

\displaystyle u_{n}(p,q)=w_{n}(0,1;p,q),\quad v_{n}(p,q)=w_{n}(2,p;p,q),

as the Lucas sequence of the first kind and second kind, respectively. The usual Fibonacci and Lucas numbers are given by
F_{n}=u_{n}(1,-1)
and
L_{n}=v_{n}(1,-1)
, respectively. Denote
\alpha
and
\beta
, with
|\alpha|>|\beta|
, as the roots of the characteristic equation
x^{2}-px+q=0
. The discriminant of this equation is
D=p^{2}-4q\neq 0
. Notably, we have
\alpha+\beta=p
,
\alpha\beta=q
, and
\alpha-\beta=\sqrt{D}
. The Binet formulas for
w_{n}
,
u_{n}
, and
v_{n}
are given by

\displaystyle w_{n}=\frac{A\alpha^{n}-B\beta^{n}}{\alpha-\beta},\quad u_{n}=\frac{\alpha^{n}-\beta^{n}}{\alpha-\beta},\quad v_{n}=\alpha^{n}+\beta^{n},

where
A=b-a\beta
and
B=b-a\alpha
.

Many papers have been devoted to finding closed forms for sums involving reciprocals of generalized Lucas sequences, with a particular focus on the Millin series. The series

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{F_{2^{i}}}=\frac{7-\sqrt{5}}{2},

was proposed by Miller
[
24
, p. 309]
as a problem in Fibonacci Quarterly. It is worth noting that this series appeared in earlier papers by Lucas
[
20
, Eq. (126)]
and Brady
[
4
, Eq. (3)]
. In fact, they both gave a more general formula. For instance, Lucas demonstrated that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{2^{i}r}}{u_{2^{i+1}r}}=\frac{\beta^{r}}{u_{r}},

where
r\geq 1
is an integer. Other solutions include
[
7
,
12
,
16
,
27
]
.

On top of that, numerous authors have also taken interest in the sum when the indices form an arithmetic progression, say

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{q^{ri}}{w_{ri+s}w_{r(i+M)+s}},

and its corresponding infinite version. Some examples include
[
3
,
17
,
22
]
. Furthermore, Adegoke
[
1
]
extended the sum to cases where the denominator contains two or more terms.

Traditionally, one can derive these results by applying telescoping sums to recurrence relations of generalized Lucas sequences. An alternative was provided by Bruckman and Good
[
7
]
, via an identity that can be traced back to de Morgan, i.e.,

\displaystyle\frac{x_{k+1}y_{k}-x_{k}y_{k+1}}{(x_{k}-y_{k})(x_{k+1}-y_{k+1})}=\frac{y_{k}}{x_{k}-y_{k}}-\frac{y_{k+1}}{x_{k+1}-y_{k+1}}.

However, Bruckman and Good only gave sums involving Fibonacci and Lucas numbers. Further extensions to Lucas sequences were added by Farhi
[
9
]
. Finally, we have to mention that de Morgan’s identity is a special case of a more general identity by Duverney and Shiokawa
[
8
, Cor. 4.1]
. It could be worthwhile to use this identity to discover even more extensive series involving Lucas sequences.

In this paper, we consider two variations on the identity by Bruckman and Good, enabling us to establish numerous intriguing sums and rediscover some previously known ones. We will make repeated use of the following telescoping summation identities. For any positive integers
N
and
M
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}(x_{i}-x_{i+M})=\sum_{i=1}^{M}(x_{i}-x_{i+N}),

(1)

and

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2N}(\pm 1)^{i}(x_{i}-x_{i+2M})=\sum_{i=1}^{2M}(\pm 1)^{i}(x_{i}-x_{i+2N}).

(2)

Note that the usual telescoping sum is obtained by setting
M=1
in Eq. (
1
).

2
New reciprocal series of Lucas sequences

Let
A
and
B
be arbitrary complex numbers. The identity provided by Bruckman and Good
[
7
]
can be generalized for any sequences
(x_{n})
and
(y_{n})
as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{A(x_{i+j}y_{i}-x_{i}y_{i+j})}{(Ax_{i}-By_{i})(Ax_{i+j}-By_{i+j})}=\frac{y_{i}}{Ax_{i}-By_{i}}-\frac{y_{i+j}}{Ax_{i+j}-By_{i+j}}.

Since the RHS is telescoping, upon applications of Eq. (
1
) and Eq. (
2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{A(x_{i+M}y_{i}-x_{i}y_{i+M})}{(Ax_{i}-By_{i})(Ax_{i+M}-By_{i+M})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\frac{A(x_{i+N}y_{i}-x_{i}y_{i+N})}{(Ax_{i}-By_{i})(Ax_{i+N}-By_{i+N})},

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2N}\frac{(\pm 1)^{i}A(x_{i+2M}y_{i}-x_{i}y_{i+2M})}{(Ax_{i}-By_{i})(Ax_{i+2M}-By_{i+2M})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{2M}\frac{(\pm 1)^{i}A(x_{i+2N}y_{i}-x_{i}y_{i+2N})}{(Ax_{i}-By_{i})(Ax_{i+2N}-By_{i+2N})}.

In particular, for any integer function
f
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{A(x^{f(i+M)}y^{f(i)}-x^{f(i)}y^{f(i+M)})}{(Ax^{f(i)}-By^{f(i)})(Ax^{f(i+M)}-By^{f(i+M)})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\frac{A(x^{f(i+N)}y^{f(i)}-x^{f(i)}y^{f(i+N)})}{(Ax^{f(i)}-By^{f(i)})(Ax^{f(i+N)}-By^{f(i+N)})},

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2N}\frac{(\pm 1)^{i}A(x^{f(i+2M)}y^{f(i)}-x^{f(i)}y^{f(i+2M)})}{(Ax^{f(i)}-By^{f(i)})(Ax^{f(i+2M)}-By^{f(i+2M)})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{2M}\frac{(\pm 1)^{i}A(x^{f(i+2N)}y^{f(i)}-x^{f(i)}y^{f(i+2N)})}{(Ax^{f(i)}-By^{f(i)})(Ax^{f(i+2N)}-By^{f(i+2N)})}.

By setting
x=\alpha
and
y=\beta
, we obtain the following theorems:

Theorem 1
.

For integer function
f
and positive integers
M
and
N
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{q^{f(i)}u_{f(i+M)-f(i)}}{w_{f(i)}w_{f(i+M)}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\frac{q^{f(i)}u_{f(i+N)-f(i)}}{w_{f(i)}w_{f(i+N)}}.

Theorem 2
.

For integer function
f
and positive integers
M
and
N
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2N}\frac{(\pm 1)^{i}q^{f(i)}u_{f(i+2M)-f(i)}}{w_{f(i)}w_{f(i+2M)}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{2M}\frac{(\pm 1)^{i}q^{f(i)}u_{f(i+2N)-f(i)}}{w_{f(i)}w_{f(i+2N)}}.

In particular, if
M=1
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{Aq^{f(i)}u_{f(i+1)-f(i)}}{w_{f(i)}w_{f(i+1)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{Aq^{f(1)}u_{f(N+1)-f(1)}}{w_{f(1)}w_{f(N+1)}}=\frac{\beta^{f(1)}}{w_{f(1)}}-\frac{\beta^{f(N+1)}}{w_{f(N+1)}},

and

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2N}\frac{(\pm 1)^{i}Aq^{s(i)}u_{f(i+2)-f(i)}}{w_{f(i)}w_{f(i+2)}}

\displaystyle=\pm\frac{Aq^{f(1)}u_{f(2N+1)-f(1)}}{w_{f(1)}w_{f(2N+1)}}+\frac{Aq^{f(2)}u_{f(2N+2)-f(2)}}{w_{f(2)}w_{f(2N+2)}}

\displaystyle=\pm\frac{\beta^{f(1)}}{w_{f(1)}}\mp\frac{\beta^{f(2N+1)}}{w_{f(2N+1)}}+\frac{\beta^{f(2)}}{w_{f(2)}}-\frac{\beta^{f(2N+2)}}{w_{f(2N+2)}}.

Assuming further that
f(i)\rightarrow\infty
as
i\rightarrow\infty
. By letting
N\rightarrow\infty
, each term containing
N
on the RHS vanishes, so we get the following infinite versions of the previous theorems.

Theorem 3
.

Suppose that
f(i)\rightarrow\infty
as
i\rightarrow\infty
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{f(i)}u_{f(i+1)-f(i)}}{w_{f(i)}w_{f(i+1)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta^{f(1)}}{Aw_{f(1)}}.

Remark 4
.

This was also derived by Hu et al.
[
17
, Th. 2]
. For increments greater than
1
, refer to Farhi’s paper
[
9
, Th. 2]
.

Theorem 5
.

Suppose that
f(i)\rightarrow\infty
as
i\rightarrow\infty
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\pm 1)^{i}q^{f(i)}u_{f(i+2)-f(i)}}{w_{f(i)}w_{f(i+2)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta^{f(2)}}{Aw_{f(2)}}\pm\frac{\beta^{f(1)}}{Aw_{f(1)}}.

It is immediate to deduce from Theorem
5
the next result.

Theorem 6
.

Suppose that
f(i)\rightarrow\infty
as
i\rightarrow\infty
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\text{ even}\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{q^{f(i)}u_{f(i+2)-f(i)}}{w_{f(i)}w_{f(i+2)}}=\frac{\beta^{f(2)}}{Aw_{f(2)}},

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{q^{f(i)}u_{f(i+2)-f(i)}}{w_{f(i)}w_{f(i+2)}}=\frac{\beta^{f(1)}}{Aw_{f(1)}}.

3
Applications, Part I

3.1
When
f(i)=ri+s
with integers
r,s
and
r>0

Using Theorems
1
to
5
, we obtain the next corollaries as special cases.

Corollary 7
.

Let
r
,
s
,
M
, and
N
be integers with
r
,
M
, and
N>0
. Then

\displaystyle u_{rM}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{q^{ri}}{w_{ri+s}w_{r(i+M)+s}}

\displaystyle=u_{rN}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\frac{q^{ri}}{w_{ri+s}w_{r(i+N)+s}},

\displaystyle u_{2rM}\sum_{i=1}^{2N}\frac{(\pm 1)^{i}q^{ri}}{w_{ri+s}w_{r(i+2M)+s}}

\displaystyle=u_{2rN}\sum_{i=1}^{2M}\frac{(\pm 1)^{i}q^{ri}}{w_{ri+s}w_{r(i+2N)+s}}.

Corollary 8
.

Let
r
and
s
be integers with
r>0
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{ri+s}}{w_{ri+s}w_{r(i+1)+s}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta^{r+s}}{Au_{r}w_{r+s}},

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\pm 1)^{i}q^{ri+s}}{w_{ri+s}w_{r(i+2)+s}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta^{2r+s}}{Au_{2r}w_{2r+s}}\pm\frac{\beta^{r+s}}{Au_{2r}w_{r+s}}.

Remark 9
.

These corollaries include the results of Adegoke et al.
[
3
]
. For example, if we carry out the transformations
M\rightarrow 2M
and
s\rightarrow s-rM
, then our first sum in Corollary
7
becomes

\displaystyle u_{2rM}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{q^{ri}}{w_{r(i-M)+s}w_{r(i+M)+s}}

\displaystyle=u_{rN}\sum_{i=1}^{2M}\frac{q^{ri}}{w_{r(i-M)+s}w_{r(i+N-M)+s}}.

This is equivalent to Theorem
1
of the aforementioned paper. The rest can be deduced analogously.

Example 10
.

To apply to the case of Fibonacci and Lucas numbers, we set
p=1
and
q=-1
, then
\alpha=\varphi=(1+\sqrt{5})/2
,
\beta=1-\varphi=(1-\sqrt{5})/2
, and
D=5
. Corollary
8
gives

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{ri+s}}{F_{ri+s}F_{r(i+1)+s}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-\varphi)^{r+s}}{F_{r}F_{r+s}},

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{ri+s}}{L_{ri+s}L_{r(i+1)+s}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-\varphi)^{r+s}}{\sqrt{5}L_{r}L_{r+s}},

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\pm 1)^{i}(-1)^{ri+s}}{F_{ri+s}F_{r(i+2)+s}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-\varphi)^{2r+s}}{F_{2r}F_{2r+s}}\pm\frac{(1-\varphi)^{r+s}}{F_{2r}F_{r+s}},

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\pm 1)^{i}(-1)^{ri+s}}{L_{ri+s}L_{r(i+2)+s}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-\varphi)^{2r+s}}{\sqrt{5}F_{2r}L_{2r+s}}\pm\frac{(1-\varphi)^{r+s}}{\sqrt{5}F_{2r}L_{r+s}}.

Remark 11
.

The first two sums, when both
r
and
s
are even, can be found in Popov’s paper
[
26
, p. 263]
. The general case for
r
being even and
s\neq 0
was proved by Melham
[
22
, Th. 2, 3]
.

Example 12
.

Set
p=e+1/e
,
q=1
,
A=1
, and
B=\pm 1
, then
\alpha=e
and
\beta=1/e
. Corollary
8
gives

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sinh r}{\sinh(ri+s)\sinh(r(i+1)+s)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{e^{r+s}\sinh(r+s)},

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sinh r}{\cosh(ri+s)\cosh(r(i+1)+s)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{e^{r+s}\cosh(r+s)},

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\pm 1)^{i}\sinh 2r}{\sinh(ri+s)\sinh(r(i+2)+s)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{e^{2r+s}\sinh(2r+s)}\pm\frac{1}{e^{r+s}\sinh(r+s)},

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\pm 1)^{i}\sinh 2r}{\cosh(ri+s)\cosh(r(i+2)+s)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{e^{2r+s}\cosh(2r+s)}\pm\frac{1}{e^{r+s}\cosh(r+s)}.

4
More reciprocal series of Lucas sequences

In this section, we will only deal with the Lucas sequences,
(u_{n})
and
(v_{n})
, instead of the generalized Lucas sequences,
(w_{n})
. Given any sequences
(x_{n})
and
(y_{n})
, one checks that

\displaystyle\frac{\pm 4(x_{i+j}y_{i}-x_{i}y_{i+j})(x_{i}x_{i+j}-y_{i}y_{i+j})}{(x_{i}\mp y_{i})^{2}(x_{i+j}\mp y_{i+j})^{2}}=\frac{(x_{i}\pm y_{i})^{2}}{(x_{i}\mp y_{i})^{2}}-\frac{(x_{i+j}\pm y_{i+j})^{2}}{(x_{i+j}\mp y_{i+j})^{2}}.

Using the same procedure as in §
2
, we deduce the next two theorems.

Theorem 23
.

For integer function
f
and positive integers
N
and
M
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{q^{f(i)}u_{f(i+M)-f(i)}u_{f(i+M)+f(i)}}{u_{f(i)}^{2}u_{f(i+M)}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\frac{q^{f(i)}u_{f(i+N)-f(i)}u_{f(i+N)+f(i)}}{u_{f(i)}^{2}u_{f(i+N)}^{2}},

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{q^{f(i)}u_{f(i+M)-f(i)}u_{f(i+M)+f(i)}}{v_{f(i)}^{2}v_{f(i+M)}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\frac{q^{f(i)}u_{f(i+N)-f(i)}u_{f(i+N)+f(i)}}{v_{f(i)}^{2}v_{f(i+N)}^{2}}.

Theorem 24
.

For integer function
f
and positive integers
N
and
M
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2N}\frac{(\pm 1)^{i}q^{f(i)}u_{f(i+2M)-f(i)}u_{f(i+2M)+f(i)}}{u_{f(i)}^{2}u_{f(i+2M)}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{2M}\frac{(\pm 1)^{i}q^{f(i)}u_{f(i+2N)-f(i)}u_{f(i+2N)+f(i)}}{u_{f(i)}^{2}u_{f(i+2N)}^{2}},

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2N}\frac{(\pm 1)^{i}q^{f(i)}u_{f(i+2M)-f(i)}u_{f(i+2M)+f(i)}}{v_{f(i)}^{2}v_{f(i+2M)}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{2M}\frac{(\pm 1)^{i}q^{f(i)}u_{f(i+2N)-f(i)}u_{f(i+2N)+f(i)}}{v_{f(i)}^{2}v_{f(i+2N)}^{2}}.

Theorem 25
.

Suppose that
f(i)\rightarrow\infty
as

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{4q^{f(i)}u_{f(i+1)-f(i)}u_{f(i+1)+f(i)}}{u_{f(i)}^{2}u_{f(i+1)}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{v_{f(1)}^{2}}{u_{f(1)}^{2}}-D,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{4q^{f(i)}u_{f(i+1)-f(i)}u_{f(i+1)+f(i)}}{v_{f(i)}^{2}v_{f(i+1)}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{D}-\frac{u_{f(1)}^{2}}{v_{f(1)}^{2}}.

Suppose that
f(i)\rightarrow\infty

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\pm 1)^{i}4q^{f(i)}u_{f(i+2)-f(i)}u_{f(i+2)+f(i)}}{u_{f(i)}^{2}u_{f(i+2)}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{v_{f(2)}^{2}}{u_{f(2)}^{2}}\pm\frac{v_{f(1)}^{2}}{u_{f(1)}^{2}}-D(1\pm 1),

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\pm 1)^{i}4q^{f(i)}u_{f(i+2)-f(i)}u_{f(i+2)+f(i)}}{v_{f(i)}^{2}v_{f(i+2)}^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{u_{f(2)}^{2}}{v_{f(2)}^{2}}\mp\frac{u_{f(1)}^{2}}{v_{f(1)}^{2}}+\frac{1}{D}(1\pm 1).

6
Further discussion

It is possible to rewrite Theorems
3
to
6
in terms of
\alpha
or even consider the more general identity

\displaystyle\frac{(A+B)(x_{i+j}y_{i}-x_{i}y_{i+j})}{(Ax_{i}-By_{i})(Ax_{i+j}-By_{i+j})}=\frac{x_{i}+y_{i}}{Ax_{i}-By_{i}}-\frac{x_{i+j}+y_{i+j}}{Ax_{i+j}-By_{i+j}}.

The results should be similar except that the limit does not vanish in this case. We leave it to the readers to work it out.

Furthermore, we believe it is possible to further generalize the identity in §
4
to include generalized Lucas sequences as well. This will be be an interesting topic to pursue in future research.

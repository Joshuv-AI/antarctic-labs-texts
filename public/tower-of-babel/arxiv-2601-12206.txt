Characterizations of Lorentz Type Sobolev Multiplier Spaces and Their Preduals
Keng Hao Ooi

Abstract
. We provide several characterizations of Sobolev multiplier spaces of Lorentz type and their preduals. Block decomposition and Köthe dual of such preduals are discussed. As an application, the boundedness of local Hardy-Littlewood maximal function on these spaces will be justified.



1.
Introduction

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
0<\alpha<\infty
, and
1<s\leq n/\alpha
. The Bessel capacities
{\rm Cap}_{\alpha,s}(\cdot)
are defined by

\displaystyle\text{Cap}_{\alpha,s}(E)

\displaystyle=\inf\{\|f\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{s}:f\geq 0,~G_{\alpha}\ast f\geq 1~\text{on}~E\},\quad E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
G_{\alpha}(x)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}[(1+\left|\cdot\right|^{2})^{-\alpha/2}](x)
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
are the Bessel kernels, and
\mathcal{F}^{-1}
is the inverse distributional Fourier transform on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The Sobolev multiplier spaces
M_{p}^{\alpha,s}
are studied in
[
OP
]
with the following form that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{M_{p}^{\alpha,s}}=\sup_{K}\left(\frac{\int_{K}|f(x)|^{p}dx}{{\rm Cap}_{\alpha,s}(K)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},\quad 1<p<\infty,

where the supremum is taken over all compact sets
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
with nonzero capacities. As noted in
[
OP
]
, the spaces
M_{p}^{\alpha,s}
appear in many super-critical nonlinear PDEs including the Navier-Stokes system, and a function
f\in M_{p}^{\alpha,s}
if and only if
|f|^{p/s}
belongs to the space of Sobolev multipliers
M(H^{\alpha,s}\rightarrow L^{s})
that has been studied in
[
MS
]
. Several characterizations of preduals of
M_{p}^{\alpha,s}
are also studied in
[
OP
]
.

In the present paper, we generalize
M_{p}^{\alpha,s}
to Lorentz type spaces and characterize their preduals. First we introduce the fundamental terminologies. In the sequel, we abbreviate
\mathcal{R}
,
\mathcal{C}
for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
{\rm Cap}_{\alpha,s}(\cdot)
respectively. Given a positive measure
\mu
on
\mathcal{R}
, let
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{R},\mu)
be the set of all extended real-valued
\mu
-measurable functions on
\mathcal{R}
. A functional
\left\|\cdot\right\|:\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{R},\mu)\rightarrow[0,\infty]
is said to be a quasi-norm if there is a positive constant
\kappa_{0}
such that

\displaystyle\|f+g\|

\displaystyle\leq\kappa_{0}(\|f\|+\|g\|),

\displaystyle\|\lambda f\|

\displaystyle=|\lambda|\|f\|

for
f,g\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{R},\mu)
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\|f\|=0
exactly when
f=0

\mu
-almost-everywhere. Subsequently, assuming that
X,Y
are subspaces of
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{R},\mu)
with the corresponding quasi-norms
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{X},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{Y}
, we write
X\hookrightarrow Y
provided that
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{Y}\leq c\left\|\cdot\right\|_{X}
for a positive constant
c
, and the symbol
X\approx Y
refers to both
X\hookrightarrow Y
and
Y\hookrightarrow X
. Since
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{X}
can induce a topological vector space for
X
, then
X
is isomorphic to
Y
exactly when
X\approx Y
, the isomorphism becomes isometric when
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{X}=\left\|\cdot\right\|_{Y}
. Let
X^{\ast}
be the space of all continuous linear functionals on
X
. We write
X^{\ast}\approx Y
provided that for any
\mathcal{L}\in X^{\ast}
, there is a unique
g\in Y
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(f)=\int_{\mathcal{R}}f(x)g(x)d\mu(x),\quad f\in X,

and for each
g\in Y
, the canonical linear functional
\mathcal{L}_{g}
induced by
g
satisfies the above integral representation with
\mathcal{L}_{g}
in place of
\mathcal{L}
, in which case, the operator norm
\|\mathcal{L}\|
satisfies
c^{-1}\|g\|_{Y}\leq\|\mathcal{L}\|\leq c\|g\|_{Y}
for some positive constant
c
. We also call
X
a predual of
Y
for
X^{\ast}\approx Y
. On the other hand, we define the Köthe dual
X^{\prime}
of
X
to be the set of all functions
f\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{R},\mu)
such that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{X^{\prime}}=\sup\left\{\int_{\mathcal{R}}|f(x)g(x)|d\mu(x):\|g\|_{X}\leq 1\right\}

are finite. It is evident that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{R}}|f(x)g(x)|d\mu(x)\leq\|f\|_{X}\|g\|_{X^{\prime}},

and hence
X\hookrightarrow X^{\prime\prime}=(X^{\prime})^{\prime}
.

Given
0<p,q<\infty
, the Lorentz space
L^{p,q}(\mu)
is defined to be the set of all functions
f\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{R},\mu)
such that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p,q}(\mu)}=p^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu(\{x\in\mathcal{R}:|f(x)|>t\})^{\frac{q}{p}}t^{q}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

are finite. We note that
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L^{p,q}(\mu)}
is a quasi-norm by a standard exercise in analysis. When
\mu
is the usual Lebesgue measure on
\mathcal{R}
, we reduce the symbols
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{R},\mu)
and
L^{p,q}(\mu)
to
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{R})
and
L^{p,q}
respectively. Note that
L^{p}=L^{p,p}
by an application of Fubini’s theorem. To extend
M_{p}^{\alpha,s}
into Lorentz type, we have two choices. Let
1<p<\infty
and
1<q<\infty
. We define

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathcal{M}^{p,q}}=\sup_{K}\frac{\|f\chi_{K}\|_{L^{p,q}}}{\mathcal{C}(K)^{1/p}}

and

\displaystyle\|f\|_{M^{p,q}}=\sup_{K}\frac{\|f\chi_{K}\|_{L^{p,q}}}{\mathcal{C}(K)^{1/q}},

and the symbols
\mathcal{M}^{p,q},M^{p,q}
designate the set of all functions
f\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{R})
with finite
\|f\|_{\mathcal{M}^{p,q}},\|f\|_{M^{p,q}}
respectively. Then
\mathcal{M}^{p,p}=M^{p,p}=M_{p}^{\alpha,s}
. Further, for any
1<p<\infty
and
1<r<q<\infty
, it holds that
L^{p,r}\hookrightarrow L^{p,q}
(see
[
GL
, Proposition 1.4.10]
), then we deduce that

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{p,r}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{M}^{p,q}.

Among the two candidates for a Lorentz type extension of
M_{p}^{\alpha,s}
, namely,
\mathcal{M}^{p,q}
and
M^{p,q}
, the space
\mathcal{M}^{p,q}
appears to be the more natural choice. Nevertheless, it will be seen in the following context that the preduals of
\mathcal{M}^{p,q}
are more restrictive than of
M^{p,q}
(see Theorems
1.3
and
1.7
below). Further, we will prove that if
1<r<q\leq p<\infty
,
\alpha s=n
(certain range of
p,q
is assumed when
\alpha s<n
), then

\displaystyle M^{p,r}

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow M^{p,q},

\displaystyle L^{\infty}

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow M^{p,q}

hold by an argument of Strichartz localization principle (see Proposition
4.3
below).

Our first result reads as the following. Essentially it says that the Köthe dual of
M^{p,q}
is its predual.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
1<q<\infty
. Then the following isomorphisms

\displaystyle[(M^{p,q})^{\prime}]^{\ast}

\displaystyle\approx M^{p,q},

\displaystyle[(\mathcal{M}^{p,q})^{\prime}]^{\ast}

\displaystyle\approx\mathcal{M}^{p,q}

hold. The isomorphisms
\approx
become isometric for
p=q
.

It will be shown also that
(M^{p,q})^{\prime\prime}\approx M^{p,q}
, similar assertion also applies to
\mathcal{M}^{p,q}
. Another preduals of
M^{p,q}
are given by block type spaces, which will be defined as follows. Let
1<p<\infty
and
1<q<\infty
. A function
b\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{R})
is called a
B^{p,q}
block if

(1)

\{x\in\mathcal{R}:b(x)\neq 0\}\subseteq E
for some bounded set
E\subseteq\mathcal{R}
.

(2)

\mathcal{C}(E)^{1/q^{\prime}}\|b\|_{L^{p,q}}\leq 1
.

Whereas a
\mathcal{B}^{p,q}
block is defined as in above except that
\mathcal{C}(E)^{1/p^{\prime}}\|b\|_{L^{p,q}}\leq 1
. Then the space
B^{p,q}
is defined to be the set of all functions
f\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{R})
that having the following block decomposition

\displaystyle f=\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}b_{k},

(1.1)

where the convergence of the series is pointwise almost everywhere,
\{\lambda_{k}\}\in\ell^{1}
, and each
b_{k}
is a
B^{p,q}
block. Further, we equip
B^{p,q}
with

\displaystyle\|f\|_{B^{p,q}}=\inf\left\{\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}|:f=\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}b_{k}\right\},

(1.2)

where the infimum is taken over all such block decompositions. The spaces
\mathcal{B}^{p,q}
are defined similarly with the corresponding
\mathcal{B}^{p,q}
blocks. In the next theorem, we denote the Hölder’s conjugate of
p
by
p^{\prime}=p/(p-1)
, where
1<p<\infty
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
1<q<\infty
. Then the following isomorphisms

\displaystyle(B^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}})^{\ast}\approx M^{p,q}\approx(B^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}})^{\prime},

\displaystyle(\mathcal{B}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}})^{\ast}\approx\mathcal{M}^{p,q}\approx(\mathcal{B}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}})^{\prime}

hold. Moreover, both
B^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}
and
\mathcal{B}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}
are Banach spaces.

The block spaces
B_{p}^{\alpha,s}
, which correspond to
M_{p}^{\alpha,s}
, are also introduced in
[
OP
]
, and one may infer easily that
B^{p,q}
generalizes
B_{p}^{\alpha,s}
. Moreover, as suggested in
[
OP
]
, there are “N-type” spaces that correspond to block spaces, which we introduce here. To this end, for any nonnegative function
\omega
on
\mathcal{R}
, we denote
\|\omega\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{C})}
by

\displaystyle\|\omega\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{C})}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{C}(\{x\in\mathcal{R}:\omega(x)>t\})dt.

The space
L^{1}(\mathcal{C})
consists of those
\omega
with finite
\|\omega\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{C})}
. Further, we say that a nonnegative measurable function
\omega
is a weight if
\omega
is locally integrable on
\mathcal{R}
with
\omega>0
almost everywhere. Subsequently, we define the local Hardy-Littlewood maximal function
{\bf M}^{\rm loc}
by

\displaystyle{\bf M}^{\rm loc}f(x)=\sup_{0<r\leq 1}\frac{1}{|B_{r}(x)|}\int_{B_{r}(x)}|f(y)|dy,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
f\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{R})
is locally integrable on
\mathcal{R}
. Then the local Muckenhoupt
A_{1}
class (see
[
RV
]
and
[
OK4
]
) consists of all weights
\omega
such that

\displaystyle{\bf M}^{\rm loc}\omega\leq C\omega

almost everywhere, where
C
is a positive constant. The infimum of all such
C
is denoted by
[\omega]_{A_{1}^{\rm loc}}
. Let
1<p<\infty
and
1<q<\infty
. The
N^{p,q}
space is defined to be the set of all functions
f\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{R})
such that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{N^{p,q}}=\inf\{\|f\omega^{-1/q^{\prime}}\|_{L^{p,q}}:\omega\in L^{1}(\mathcal{C})\cap A_{1}^{\rm loc},\|\omega\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{C})}\leq 1,[\omega]_{A_{1}^{\rm loc}}\leq\mathfrak{c}\}

are finite, where
\mathfrak{c}
is a fixed positive constant depending only on
n,\alpha,s
defined in (
4.3
) below. The connection of
B^{p,q}
with
N^{p,q}
is given as follows.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
1<q<\infty
. If
1<q\leq p<\infty
, then

\displaystyle B^{p,q}\hookrightarrow N^{p,q}.

(1.3)

If
1<p\leq q<\infty
, then

\displaystyle N^{p,q}\hookrightarrow B^{p,q}.

(1.4)

In contrast, one is tempted to define the “N-type” spaces that correspond to
\mathcal{M}^{p,q}
by

\displaystyle\inf_{\omega}\|f\omega^{-1/p^{\prime}}\|_{L^{p,q}},

where
\omega
are those admissible weights. However, it will be seen from the proof of Theorem

that these “N-type” spaces are neither comparable to
\mathcal{B}^{p,q}
nor preduals of
\mathcal{M}^{p,q}
. The “N-type” spaces

introduced in
[
OP
]
are in fact Banach function spaces (see section
3
below for the definition). One of the main properties of Banach function spaces is the Fatou’s property, i.e., for any functions
0\leq f_{1}\leq f_{2}\leq\cdots
, the function
f=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}f_{n}
satisfies

\displaystyle\|f\|=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|f_{n}\|,

(1.5)

where
\left\|\cdot\right\|
is the norm induced by the Banach function space. Given a quasi-norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{X}
(here

is not necessary a Banach function space), if there exists a positive constant

\displaystyle\|f\|_{X}\leq\kappa\cdot\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|f_{n}\|_{X},

where

are arbitrary functions as in (

has the weak Fatou’s property. Below we prove that

possesses the weak Fatou’s property for certain range of

. Besides, it will be shown that

is normable for some

, i.e.,

for some normed space

.

Proposition 1.4
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
1<q<\infty
. Then
N^{p,q}
is a quasi-norm space that satisfying the weak Fatou’s property. If

, then

is a complete normable space.

Denote by
C_{0}
the space of all compactly supported continuous functions on

The space

and

.

Let

\displaystyle M=\sup\{\|f\omega^{\frac{1}{q}}\|_{L^{p,q}}:\omega\in L^{1}(\mathcal{C})\cap A_{1}^{\rm loc},\|\omega\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{C})}\leq 1,[\omega]_{A_{1}^{\rm loc}}\leq\mathfrak{c}\}.

\displaystyle c^{-1}\|f\|_{M^{p,q}}\leq M\leq c\|f\|_{M^{p,q}},

where

As promised, the duality of

is given as follows.

Theorem 1.7

Let

\displaystyle(N^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}})^{\ast}\hookrightarrow M^{p,q}.

Now we consider the weak type Sobolev multiplier spaces. Let

2.
Preliminaries on Lorentz Spaces

We will use the following isomorphisms in the later sections, where the proofs can be found in
[
GL
, Theorem 1.4.16]
.

(1)

L^{p,q}(\mu)^{\ast}\approx L^{\infty}(\mu)
when
p=1
,
0<q\leq 1
.

(2)

L^{p,q}(\mu)^{\ast}\approx L^{p^{\prime},\infty}(\mu)
when
1<p<\infty
,
0<q\leq 1
.

(3)

L^{p,q}(\mu)^{\ast}\approx L^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\mu)
when
1<p<\infty
,
1<q<\infty
.

Note that the isomorphisms
\approx
in (1) and (3) become isometric for
p=q
. The next result, the density of
C_{0}
in
L^{p,q}(\mu)
, is surely well known to specialists, but we have not been able to find a reference for its proof. Since
L^{p,q}(\mu)
is not normable for certain ranges of

, the density cannot be derived by
[

, Theorem 6.3.18]
, which asserts that
C_{0}
is dense in
X
, where
X
is any Banach function space with absolutely continuous norm.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a locally compact Hausdorff space and
0<p,q<\infty
. Assume that
\mu
is a
\sigma
-finite positive measure on
X
that

\displaystyle X=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}K_{n},

where
K_{n}
is compact with
\mu(K_{n})<\infty
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Denote by
L^{p,q}(\mu)
the corresponding Lorentz space and
C_{0}
the set of all compactly supported continuous functions on
X
. Then
C_{0}
is dense in
L^{p,q}(\mu)
.

Proof.

We first prove the set
\mathcal{K}
of all compactly supported functions is dense in
L^{p,q}(\mu)
. Let
f\in L^{p,q}(\mu)
and

\displaystyle E_{n}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}K_{i},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the function
f\chi_{E_{n}}
is compactly supported. Since
f\in L^{p,q}(\mu)
, we may assume that
f
is real-valued. Besides, it is easy to argue that
\mu(\{|f|>t\})<\infty
for each
0<t<\infty
. As a consequence,
f-f\chi_{E_{n}}
converges to
0
pointwise as
n\rightarrow\infty
. The observation that

\displaystyle\mu(\{|f-f\chi_{E_{n}}|>t\})\leq\mu(\{|f|>t\}),\quad 0<t<\infty,\quad n\in\mathbb{N}

entails

\displaystyle\|f-f\chi_{E_{n}}\|_{L^{p,q}(\mu)}^{q}=p\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu(\{|f-f\chi_{E_{n}}|>t\})^{\frac{q}{p}}t^{q}\frac{dt}{t}\rightarrow 0,\quad n\rightarrow\infty

by Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem. The density of
\mathcal{K}
in
L^{p,q}(\mu)
follows.

Subsequently, we prove that
C_{b}
is dense in
\mathcal{K}
, where
C_{b}
is the set of all bounded continuous functions on
X
. Let
f\in\mathcal{K}
and
f_{M}=\max(\min(f,M),-M)
,
M>0
. By change of variables, we have

\displaystyle\|f_{M}-f\|_{L^{p,q}(\mu)}^{q}

\displaystyle=p\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu(\{|f_{M}-f|>t\})^{\frac{q}{p}}t^{q}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{p}{q}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu(\{|f_{M}-f|>t^{\frac{1}{q}}\})^{\frac{q}{p}}dt.

Note that

\displaystyle\{|f_{M}-f|>t^{\frac{1}{q}}\}

\displaystyle=\{\{f>M\}\cap\{|f_{M}-f|>t^{\frac{1}{q}}\}\}\cup\{\{f<-M\}\cap\{|f_{M}-f|>t^{\frac{1}{q}}\}\}

\displaystyle=\{\{f>M\}\cap\{f-M>t^{\frac{1}{q}}\}\}\cup\{\{f<-M\}\cap\{-M-f>t^{\frac{1}{q}}\}\}

\displaystyle=\{\{f>M\}\cap\{|f|>M+t^{\frac{1}{q}}\}\}\cup\{\{f<-M\}\cap\{|f|>M+t^{\frac{1}{q}}\}\}

\displaystyle=\{\{|f|>M\}\cap\{|f|>M+t^{\frac{1}{q}}\}\}

\displaystyle=\{|f|>M+t^{\frac{1}{q}}\}.

Using the elementary inequality that

\displaystyle(a+b)^{r}\leq C(r)(a^{r}+b^{r}),\quad C(r)=\max\{2^{r-1},1\}

with
r=1/q
,
a^{r}=M
, and
b=t
, one has

\displaystyle\{|f|>M+t^{\frac{1}{q}}\}\subseteq\{|f|>C^{\prime}(q)(M^{q}+t)^{\frac{1}{q}}\},

and hence

\displaystyle\|f_{M}-f\|_{L^{p,q}(\mu)}^{q}

\displaystyle=\frac{p}{q}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu(\{|f|>M+t^{\frac{1}{q}}\})^{\frac{q}{p}}dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{p}{q}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu(\{|f|>C^{\prime}(q)(M^{q}+t)^{\frac{1}{q}}\})^{\frac{q}{p}}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{p}{q}\int_{M^{q}}^{\infty}\mu(\{|f|>C^{\prime}(q)t^{\frac{1}{q}}\})^{\frac{q}{p}}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{p}{qC^{\prime\prime}(q)}\int_{C^{\prime\prime}(q)M^{q}}^{\infty}\mu(\{|f|>t^{\frac{1}{q}}\})^{\frac{q}{p}}dt.

Since

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p,q}(\mu)}^{q}=p\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu(\{|f|>t\})^{\frac{q}{p}}t^{q}\frac{dt}{t}=\frac{p}{q}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu(\{|f|>t^{\frac{1}{q}}\})^{\frac{q}{p}}dt<\infty,

we obtain
\|f_{M}-f\|_{L^{p,q}(\mu)}^{q}\rightarrow 0
as
M\rightarrow\infty
. For any
\varepsilon>0
, choose an
M>0
such that
\|f_{M}-f\|_{L^{p,q}(\mu)}<\varepsilon
. Since
X
is
\sigma
-finite, we can assume that the sequence
\{K_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is increasing. Pick an
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that the interior
K_{n_{0}}^{\circ}
of
K_{n_{0}}
contains the support of
f
. Since
f_{M}
is measurable and
K_{n_{0}}^{\circ}
is of finite measure, by Lusin’s theorem, there is a closed set
F\subseteq K_{n_{0}}^{\circ}
such that
\mu(K_{n_{0}}^{\circ}\setminus F)<\varepsilon^{p}
and
f_{M}|_{F}
is a continuous function. Note that
f_{M}
is identically zero on the closed set
\widetilde{F}=X\setminus K_{n_{0}}^{\circ}
. By gluing lemma, the function
f_{M}|_{F\cup\widetilde{F}}
is continuous. Since
X
is locally compact Hausdorff, we can choose by Tietze extension theorem a continuous function
v
such that
|v|\leq M
and
v=f_{M}
on
F\cup\widetilde{F}
. Then

\displaystyle\|v-f_{M}\|_{L^{p,q}(\mu)}^{q}

\displaystyle=p\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu(\{|v-f_{M}|>t\})^{\frac{q}{p}}t^{q}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=p\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu(\{x\in K_{n_{0}}^{\circ}\setminus F:|v(x)-f_{M}(x)|>t\})^{\frac{q}{p}}t^{q}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=p\int_{0}^{2M}\mu(\{x\in K_{n_{0}}^{\circ}\setminus F:|v(x)-f_{M}(x)|>t\})^{\frac{q}{p}}t^{q}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq C(p,q,M)\mu(K_{n_{0}}^{\circ}\setminus F)^{\frac{q}{p}}

\displaystyle<C(p,q,M)\varepsilon^{q}.

As a result, we have

\displaystyle\|f-v\|_{L^{p,q}(\mu)}\leq\kappa_{0}(\|f-f_{M}\|_{L^{p,q}(\mu)}+\|v-f_{M}\|_{L^{p,q}(\mu)})<C(p,q,M,\kappa_{0})\varepsilon,

where
\kappa_{0}=C(p,q)
and the density of
C_{b}
in
L^{p,q}(\mu)
now follows.

Now we claim that
C_{0}
is dense in
C_{b}
. To this end, let
v
be continuous and
|v|\leq M
for some
M>0
. For each
N\in\mathbb{N}
, let
O_{N}=\{|v|>1/N\}
. Then
O_{N}
is open and

\displaystyle\mu(O_{N})\leq C(p,q,N)\|f\|_{L^{p,q}(\mu)}<\infty.

We observe that

\displaystyle\|v\chi_{O_{N}^{c}}\|_{L^{p,q}(\mu)^{q}}

\displaystyle=p\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu(\{x\in O_{N}^{c}:|v(x)|>t\})^{\frac{q}{p}}t^{q}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=p\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{N}}\mu(\{x\in O_{N}^{c}:|v(x)|>t\})^{\frac{q}{p}}t^{q}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq p\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{N}}\mu(\{|v|>t\})^{\frac{q}{p}}t^{q}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\rightarrow 0

as
N\rightarrow\infty
. Thus, for any
\varepsilon>0
, there is an open set
O\subseteq\mathcal{R}
such that
\mu(O)<\infty
and
\|v\chi_{O^{c}}\|_{L^{p,q}(\mu)}<\varepsilon
. Since
\mu
is additive, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mu(O\cap E_{n})=\mu(O)<\infty,

where the sequence
\{E_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subseteq X
is chosen such that
\{E_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is disjoint and
O\cap E_{n}
is precompact for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. There is some
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle G=O\cap\left(\bigcup_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}E_{n}\right)

satisfies

\displaystyle\mu(G)\leq\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}\mu(O\cap E_{n})<\varepsilon^{q}.

Denote by

\displaystyle H=O\cap\left(\bigcup_{n=1}^{n_{0}}E_{n}\right).

Since
X
is locally compact Hausdorff and
H
is precompact, we can choose by Urysohn’s lemma an
\eta\in C_{0}
such that
0\leq\eta\leq 1
and
\eta=1
on
H
. We have

\displaystyle\|\eta v-v\|_{L^{p,q}(\mu)}

\displaystyle\leq(\kappa_{0}^{2}+1)(\|(\eta v-v)\chi_{O^{c}}\|_{L^{p,q}(\mu)}+\|(\eta v-v)\chi_{G}\|_{L^{p,q}(\mu)}+\|(\eta v-v)\chi_{H}\|_{L^{p,q}(\mu)})

\displaystyle=(\kappa_{0}^{2}+1)(\|(\eta v-v)\chi_{O^{c}}\|_{L^{p,q}(\mu)}+\|(\eta v-v)\chi_{G}\|_{L^{p,q}(\mu)}\|_{L^{p,q}(\mu)})

as
\eta=1
on
H
. On the other hand, one has

\displaystyle\|(\eta v-v)\chi_{O^{c}}\|_{L^{p,q}(\mu)}^{q}

\displaystyle=p\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu(\{x\in O^{c}:|\eta(x)v(x)-v(x)|>t\})^{\frac{q}{p}}t^{q}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq p\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu\left(\left\{x\in O^{c}:|v(x)|>\frac{t}{2}\right\}\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}t^{q}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq C(p,q)\|v\chi_{O^{c}}\|_{L^{p,q}(\mu)}

\displaystyle<C(p,q)\varepsilon^{q}.

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\|(\eta v-v)\chi_{G}\|_{L^{p,q}(\mu)}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq p\int_{0}^{2M}\mu(\{x\in G:|\eta(x)v(x)-v(x)|>t\})^{\frac{q}{p}}t^{q}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq C(p,q,M)\mu(G)

\displaystyle<C(p,q,M)\varepsilon^{q},

we conclude that
\|\eta v-v\|_{L^{p,q}(\mu)}<C^{\prime}(p,q,M,\kappa_{0})\varepsilon
, which finishes the proof as
\eta v\in C_{0}
.
∎

Now we address the normability of
L^{p,q}(\mu)
. Let
1<p<\infty
,
1\leq q<\infty
,
0<r\leq 1
, and
\mu\geq 0
be
\sigma
-finite with
\mu(\mathcal{R})=\infty
. For any
f\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{R},\mu)
, define

\displaystyle f_{r}^{\ast\ast}(t)=\sup_{0<\mu(E)<\infty}\left(\frac{1}{\mu(E)}\int_{E}|f|^{r}d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{r}},\quad 0<t<\infty,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{r}^{p,q}(f)=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}(t^{\frac{1}{p}}f_{r}^{\ast\ast}(t))^{q}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},

where the supremum in
f_{r}^{\ast\ast}
is taken over all
\mu
-measurable subsets
E
of
\mathcal{R}
with
0<\mu(E)<\infty
. Then it is noted in
[
GL
, Exercise 1.4.3]
that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p,q}(\mu)}\leq\Gamma_{r}^{p,q}(f)\leq\left(\frac{p}{p-r}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\|f\|_{L^{p,q}(\mu)},

and
\Gamma_{r}^{p,q}(\cdot)
is a norm when
r=1
. In other words, the Lorentz spaces
L^{p,q}(\mu)
are normable when
1<p<\infty
and
1\leq q<\infty
, we will use this fact from frequently in the later sections.

We conclude this section by presenting the following simple yet useful formula.

\displaystyle\||f|^{r}\|_{L^{p,q}(\mu)}=\|f\|_{L^{pr,qr}(\mu)}^{r},

where
0<p<\infty
,
0<q\leq\infty
, and
0<r<\infty
.

3.
Preliminaries on Banach Function Spaces

Assume that
\mu\geq 0
is a
\sigma
-finite measure and
\rho:\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{R},\mu)\rightarrow[0,\infty]
is a functional. We say that
\rho
is a Banach function norm provided that for every
f,g\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{R},\mu)
,
\lambda\geq 0
, and
\mu
-measurable sets
E\subseteq\mathcal{R}
, the following conditions are satisfied.

(1)

\rho(f)=\rho(|f|)
and
\rho(f)=0
if and only if
f=0

\mu
-almost-everywhere.

(2)

\rho(\lambda f)=\lambda\rho(f)
.

(3)

\rho(f+g)\leq\rho(f)+\rho(g)
.

(4)

If
0\leq g\leq f

\mu
-almost-everywhere, then
\rho(g)\leq\rho(f)
.

(5)

If
0\leq f_{1}\leq f_{2}\leq\cdots
,
f_{n}\uparrow f

\mu
-almost-everywhere, then
\rho(f_{n})\uparrow\rho(f)
. This is also known to be the Fatou’s property.

(6)

If
E
is bounded, then
\rho(\chi_{E})<\infty
.

(7)

If
E
is bounded, then there exists a positive constant
C(E)
such that

\displaystyle\int_{E}|f|d\mu\leq C(E)\rho(f)

holds for all
f\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{R},\mu)
.

Then we denote by
X=X(\rho)
the set of all functions
f\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{R},\mu)
such that
\rho(f)<\infty
and call
X
the Banach function space. For any
f\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{R},\mu)
, let

\displaystyle\|f\|_{X}=\rho(f).

Then
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{X}
is a norm on
X
. It can be shown that
X
is a Banach space (see
[
PKJF
, Corollary 6.1.15]
). Moreover, the second Köthe dual
X^{\prime\prime}=(X^{\prime})^{\prime}
of
X
satisfies that

\displaystyle X^{\prime\prime}=X

(see
[
PKJF
, Theorem 6.2.9]
). The crucial properties used in the proof of the above isometric isomorphism are the triangle inequality
\|f+g\|_{X}\leq\|f\|_{X}+\|g\|_{X}
and the Fatou’s property.

Moreover, given a Banach function space
X
, we say that
X
has an absolutely continuous norm provided that for every sequence
\{f_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{R},\mu)
, if
f_{n}\downarrow 0

\mu
-almost-everywhere, then
\|f_{n}\|_{X}\downarrow 0
. The following proposition is tacitly used in the proof of
[
OP
, Proposition 2.8]
, we include its proof for the sake of completeness.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
1\leq q<\infty
and
X
be a Banach function space. If
X
is
q
-concave in the sense that

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|f_{i}\|_{X}^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq\mathfrak{C}\left\|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}|f_{i}|^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right\|_{X}

holds for some positive constant
\mathfrak{C}
and
\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
is any finite sequence of functions in
X
, then
X
has an absolutely continuous norm. In which case, it holds that

\displaystyle X^{\prime}=X^{\ast}.

Proof.

Let
\{f_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence in
X
with
f_{n}\downarrow 0

\mu
-almost-everywhere. We first claim that if
f_{n}\rightarrow\varphi
in
X
, then
\varphi=0

\mu
-almost everywhere. To see this, we use the Fatou’s property of
X
to obtain

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{X}=\left\|\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}f_{n}(\cdot)-\varphi\right\|_{X}\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|f_{n}-\varphi\|_{X}=0,

which yields the claim.

Suppose to the contrary that
\|f_{n}\|_{X}\rightarrow 0
fails. As
X
is a Banach space, the previous argument shows that
\{f_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is not Cauchy. Then there are
\varepsilon>0
and
n_{1}<n_{2}<\cdots
such that

\displaystyle\|f_{n_{k+1}}-f_{n_{k}}\|_{X}\geq\varepsilon,\quad k=1,2,\ldots.

Using the
q
-concavity of
X
, one has for fixed
m\in\mathbb{N}
that

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}\|f_{n_{k+1}}-f_{n_{k}}\|_{X}^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathfrak{C}\left\|\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}|f_{n_{k+1}}-f_{n_{k}}|^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq\mathfrak{C}\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{m}|f_{n_{k+1}}-f_{n_{k}}|\right\|_{X}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{C}\|f_{n_{1}}-f_{n_{m+1}}\|_{X},

and hence

\displaystyle\mathfrak{C}\|f_{n_{1}}-f_{n_{m+1}}\|_{X}\geq m^{\frac{1}{q}}\varepsilon,\quad m=1,2,\ldots.

Note that
f_{n_{1}}-f_{n_{m}}\uparrow f_{n_{1}}

\mu
-almost-everywhere. Taking limit
m\rightarrow\infty
to the above inequality and using Fatou’s property of
X
again, we obtain a contradiction.
∎

We end this section by noting that if
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{X}
is only a quasi-norm with weak Fatou’s property, the second Köthe dual
X^{\prime\prime}
of
X
does not necessarily isomorphic to
X
. This is the main difficulty in proving the isomorphisms in Theorem
1.7
. It is worth noting that
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L^{p,q}(\mu)}
is a quasi-norm which fails to satisfy the triangle inequality. Hence if we equip
L^{p,q}(\mu)
with
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L^{p,q}(\mu)}
, then
L^{p,q}(\mu)
is surely not a Banach function space under this quasi-norm. Nevertheless, the Fatou’s property clearly holds for
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L^{p,q}(\mu)}
and we are able to recover
L^{p,q}(\mu)
to be a Banach function space by switching to another norm. Let
1<p<\infty
and
1\leq q<\infty
. Consider the norm
\Gamma_{1}^{p,q}(\cdot)
that introduced in the previous section. Note that
\Gamma_{1}^{p,q}(\cdot)
satisfies the Fatou’s property. It is now clear that
L^{p,q}(\mu)
, equipped with
\Gamma_{1}^{p,q}(\cdot)
, is a Banach function space. For such
p,q
, it is easy to see that

\displaystyle\|f_{1}+f_{2}+\cdots\|_{L^{p,q}(\mu)}\leq C(p,q)(\|f_{1}\|_{L^{p,q}(\mu)}+\|f_{2}\|_{L^{p,q}(\mu)}+\cdots),

where
\{f_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{R},\mu)
and
f_{1}+f_{2}+\cdots
is the usual pointwise sum.

4.
Proofs of Main Results

We first establish that for the Bessel capacities
\mathcal{C}={\rm Cap}_{\alpha,s}(\cdot)
satisfy the following estimates for arbitrary measurable sets
E\subseteq\mathcal{R}
.

\displaystyle|E|^{\varepsilon}\leq C(n,\alpha,s,\varepsilon)\mathcal{C}(E),\quad 0<\varepsilon\leq 1,\quad\alpha s=n

(4.1)

and

\displaystyle|E|^{\varepsilon}\leq C(n,\alpha,s,\varepsilon)\mathcal{C}(E),\quad\frac{n-\alpha s}{n}\leq\varepsilon\leq 1,\quad\alpha s<n.

(4.2)

Assume that
\alpha s=n
. Using Young’s convolution inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|G_{\alpha}\ast f\|_{L^{r}}\leq\|G_{\alpha}\|_{L^{\overline{r}}}\|f\|_{L^{s}},

where

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}+1=\frac{1}{\overline{r}}+\frac{1}{s},\quad 1<s=\frac{n}{\alpha}\leq r<\infty.

Then
0<\overline{r}=nr/(n+r(n-\alpha))\leq 1
and hence
\|G_{\alpha}\|_{L^{\overline{r}}}<\infty
. Suppose that
G_{\alpha}\ast f\geq 1
on
E
. Then

\displaystyle|E|^{s/r}\leq C(n,\alpha,s,r)\|f\|_{L^{s}}^{s},

which gives (
4.1
) by simple algebraic manipulations.

Now assume that
\alpha s<n
. By Sobolev embedding theorem, we have

\displaystyle\|G_{\alpha}\ast f\|_{L^{r}}\leq C(n,\alpha,s,r)\|f\|_{L^{s}},

where

\displaystyle s\leq r\leq s^{\ast},\quad\frac{1}{s^{\ast}}=\frac{1}{s}-\frac{\alpha}{n}=\frac{n-\alpha s}{ns}.

Suppose that
G_{\alpha}\ast f\geq 1
on
E
. Then

\displaystyle|E|^{\frac{s}{r}}\leq C(n,\alpha,s,r)\|f\|_{L^{s}}^{s},\quad\frac{n-\alpha s}{n}\leq\frac{s}{r}\leq 1,

which yields (
4.2
). A direct consequence of (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) is the absolute continuity for
\alpha s\leq n
, i.e., if
\mathcal{C}(E)=0
, then
|E|=0
. In view of this, the capacities
\mathcal{C}
are finer than the Lebesgue measure.

We will use some classical results from nonlinear potential theory. Let
E\subseteq\mathcal{R}
be an arbitrary set with
0<\mathcal{C}(E)<\infty
. Then there exists a positive measure
\mu=\mu^{E}
on
\mathcal{R}
such that
\mu
is supported in
\overline{E}
with

\displaystyle V^{E}(x)\geq 1\quad\text{for all }x\in E\setminus N,

\displaystyle V^{E}(x)\leq 1\quad\text{for all }x\in{\rm supp}(\mu),

\displaystyle\mu(\mathcal{R})=\int_{\mathcal{R}}(G_{\alpha}\ast\mu)(x)^{s^{\prime}}dx=\int_{\mathcal{R}}V^{E}(x)d\mu(x)=\mathcal{C}(E),

where
N\subseteq\mathcal{R}
is some set with
\mathcal{C}(N)=0
and
V^{E}=G_{\alpha}\ast(G_{\alpha}\ast\mu)^{s^{\prime}-1}
(see
[
AH
, Theorem 2.5.6]
). We call
V^{E}
the nonlinear potential associated with
E
. Further, it is proved in
[
OP
, Lemma 3.2]
that there exists a positive constant
\delta=C(n,\alpha,s)
such that
(V^{E})^{\delta}\in L^{1}(\mathcal{C})
with

\displaystyle C(n,\alpha,s,\delta)^{-1}\mathcal{C}(E)\leq\|(V^{E})^{\delta}\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{C})}\leq C(n,\alpha,s,\delta)\mathcal{C}(E).

Moreover,
[
OP
, Theorem 3.1]
gives
(V^{E})^{\delta}\in A_{1}^{\rm loc}
with

\displaystyle[(V^{E})^{\delta}]_{A_{1}^{\rm loc}}\leq\mathfrak{c},

(4.3)

where
\mathfrak{c}=C(n,\alpha,s)
is a positive constant. We will reserve this symbol
\mathfrak{c}
throughout the paper. Finally, we note that
L^{1}(\mathcal{C})
is normable (see
[
OK2
, Theorem 2.8]
).

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
1<r<p
and
1<r\leq q
. We first prove that
M^{p,q}
is
r
-convex, i.e.,

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}|f_{i}|^{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\right\|_{M^{p,q}}\leq\kappa\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|f_{i}\|_{M^{p,q}}^{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}

holds for every finite sequence
\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
of functions in
M^{p,q}
, where
\kappa=C(p,q,r)
. For such a sequence
\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}|f_{i}|^{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\right\|_{M^{p,q}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{K}\frac{1}{\mathcal{C}(K)^{1/q}}\left\|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}|f_{i}|^{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\chi_{K}\right\|_{L^{p,q}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{K}\frac{1}{\mathcal{C}(K)^{1/q}}\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{m}|f_{i}|^{r}\chi_{K}\right\|_{L^{p/r,q/r}}^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle\leq\kappa\cdot\sup_{K}\frac{1}{\mathcal{C}(K)^{1/q}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left\||f_{i}|^{r}\chi_{K}\right\|_{L^{p/r,q/r}}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle=\kappa\cdot\sup_{K}\frac{1}{\mathcal{C}(K)^{1/q}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left\|f_{i}\chi_{K}\right\|_{L^{p,q}}^{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle\leq\kappa\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|f_{i}\|_{M^{p,q}}^{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}},

which justifies the
r
-convexity of
M^{p,q}
.

Now we show that
(M^{p,q})^{\prime}
is
r^{\prime}
-concave. Fix a finite sequence
\{g_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
in
(M^{p,q})^{\prime}
. Using
\ell^{r^{\prime}}((M^{p,q})^{\ast})\approx[\ell^{r}(M^{p,q})]^{\ast}
(see
[
DN
, Theorem 18.42]
), we infer that

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|g_{i}\|_{(M^{p,q})^{\prime}}^{r^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|\mathcal{L}_{g_{i}}\|_{(M^{p,q})^{\ast}}^{r^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathfrak{C}\cdot\sup_{\|\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}\|_{\ell^{r}(M^{p,q})}\leq 1}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}(x)g_{i}(x)dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\mathfrak{C}\cdot\sup_{\|\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}\|_{\ell^{r}(M^{p,q})}\leq 1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}|f_{i}(x)|^{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}|g_{i}(x)|^{r^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}dx

\displaystyle\leq\mathfrak{C}\cdot\sup_{\|\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}\|_{\ell^{r}(M^{p,q})}\leq 1}\left\|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}|f_{i}|^{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\right\|_{M^{p,q}}\left\|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}|g_{i}|^{r^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}\right\|_{(M^{p,q})^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathfrak{C}\kappa\left\|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}|g_{i}|^{r^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}\right\|_{(M^{p,q})^{\prime}},

where the positive constant
\mathfrak{C}
is due to the isomorphism
\ell^{r^{\prime}}((M^{p,q})^{\ast})\approx[\ell^{r}(M^{p,q})]^{\ast}
, and it depends on
n,\alpha,s,p,q
. Thus the
r^{\prime}
-concavity of
(M^{p,q})^{\prime}
follows.

The constants
\mathfrak{C},k
can be reduced to
1
if
p=q
or
M^{p,q}
is replaced by the normed space
\mathfrak{M}^{p,q}
, where

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathfrak{M}^{p,q}}=\sup_{K}\frac{\Gamma_{1}^{p,q}(f\chi_{K})}{\mathcal{C}(K)^{1/q}},\quad f\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{R}).

Since
\mathfrak{M}^{p,q}
is a Banach function space, Proposition
3.1
entails

\displaystyle[(\mathfrak{M}^{p,q})^{\prime}]^{\ast}=(\mathfrak{M}^{p,q})^{\prime\prime}=\mathfrak{M}^{p,q}.

Switching
\mathfrak{M}^{p,q}
back to
M^{p,q}
, we obtain the result of this theorem for
M^{p,q}
. The proof of
\mathcal{M}^{p,q}
is argued exactly in the same fashion.
∎

Now we show for the embedding that

\displaystyle M^{p,r}\hookrightarrow M^{p,q}

for admissible exponents
p,q,r
. To this end, we need the following technical lemma, which is due to Strichartz
[
ST
]
(see also
[
MS
, Theorem 3.1.2]
).

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\{\mathcal{B}^{(j)}\}_{j\geq 0}
be a covering of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by balls with unit diameter. Assume that this covering has finite multiplicity that depends only on
n
. Further, let
O^{(j)}
be the center of
\mathcal{B}^{(j)}
,
O^{(0)}=0
, and
\eta_{j}=\eta(x-O^{(j)})
, where
\eta
is infinitely differentiable function that supported in
B_{1}(0)
and
\eta=1
on
\mathcal{B}^{(0)}
. Then

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{\alpha,s}}\approx\left(\sum_{j\geq 0}\|u\eta_{j}\|_{W^{\alpha,s}}^{s}\right)^{1/s}.

Here
u\in W^{\alpha,s}
if and only if
u=G_{\alpha}\ast f
for some
f\in L^{s}
and
\|u\|_{W^{\alpha,s}}=\|f\|_{L^{s}}
.

Note that the function
f\in L^{s}
in
u=G_{\alpha}\ast f\in W^{\alpha,s}
is uniquely determined; see
[
SE
, Section 3.3]
for a proof. The Strichartz lemma entails the localization of capacity, which reads as follows; see also
[
AD
, Theorem 4]
for a variant.

Proposition 4.2
.

For any set
E\subseteq\mathcal{R}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(E)\approx\sum_{j\geq 0}\mathcal{C}(E\cap\mathcal{B}^{(j)}),

(4.4)

where
\{\mathcal{B}_{j}\}_{j\geq 0}
is as in Lemma
4.1
.

Proof.

First we note that by the finite multiplicity of
\{\mathcal{B}^{(j)}\}_{j\geq 0}
and the countable subadditivity of
\mathcal{C}
, one has

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(E)\leq C(n,\alpha,s)\sum_{j\geq 0}\mathcal{C}(E\cap\mathcal{B}^{(j)}).

To complete the proof of this proposition, we need an auxiliary capacity which defined as follows. For any compact set
K\subseteq\mathcal{R}
, define

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\prime}(K)=\inf\{\|\varphi\|_{W^{\alpha,s}}^{s}:\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{R}),~\varphi\geq\chi_{K}\},

where
W^{\alpha,s}
is introduced in Lemma
4.1
and
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{R})
is the Schwartz class; see
[
GL
, Definition 2.2.1]
for the definition of
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{R})
. For any open set
G\subseteq\mathcal{R}
, define

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\prime}(G)=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}K~\text{compact}\\
K\subseteq G\end{subarray}}\mathcal{C}^{\prime}(K).

If
E\subseteq\mathcal{R}
is an arbitrary set, then we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\prime}(E)=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}G~\text{open}\\
E\subseteq G\subseteq\mathcal{R}\end{subarray}}\mathcal{C}^{\prime}(G).

It can be proved that
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
; see
[
AH
, Proposition 2.3.13]
for details.

To prove this proposition, we first consider that
E=K
is compact. Following the notations in Lemma
4.1
, for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}
with
\varphi\geq\chi_{K}
, we have
\varphi\eta_{j}\geq\chi_{K\cap\mathcal{B}^{(j)}}
, and hence

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j\geq 0}\mathcal{C}(K\cap\mathcal{B}^{(j)})\right)^{1/s}\leq\left(\sum_{j\geq 0}\|\varphi\eta_{j}\|_{W^{\alpha,s}}^{s}\right)^{1/s}\leq C(n,\alpha,s)\|\varphi\|_{W^{\alpha,s}}.

Then (
4.4
) follows by taking infimum over all such
\varphi
.

Consider now that
E=G
is open. There is an increasing sequence
\{K_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
of compact sets in
\mathcal{R}
such that

\displaystyle G=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}K_{n}.

Then the Fatou’s property of
\mathcal{C}
gives

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 0}\mathcal{C}(G\cap\mathcal{B}^{(j)})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\geq 0}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{C}(K_{n}\cap\mathcal{B}^{(j)})

\displaystyle=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{j\geq 0}\mathcal{C}(K_{n}\cap\mathcal{B}^{(j)})

\displaystyle\leq C(n,\alpha,s)\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{C}(K_{n})

\displaystyle=C(n,\alpha,s)\mathcal{C}(G),

which yields (
4.4
) for open sets
E=G
.

Finally, given arbitrary set
E\subseteq\mathcal{R}
, let
G\supseteq E
be open. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 0}\mathcal{C}(E\cap\mathcal{B}^{(j)})\leq\sum_{j\geq 0}\mathcal{C}(G\cap\mathcal{B}^{(j)})\leq C(n,\alpha,s)\mathcal{C}(G).

As a result, (
4.4
) follows by the outer regularity of
\mathcal{C}
, which finishes the proof.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
1<q<\infty
. Assume that either

\displaystyle 1<r<q\leq p<\infty,\quad\alpha s=n

or

\displaystyle 1<r<q\leq p<\infty,\quad\frac{n-\alpha s}{n}\leq\frac{q}{p},\quad\alpha s<n.

Then

\displaystyle M^{p,r}\hookrightarrow M^{p,q}

and

\displaystyle L^{\infty}\hookrightarrow M^{p,q}.

In fact, it holds that

\displaystyle C(n,\alpha,s,p,q)^{-1}\|f\|_{M^{p,q}}\leq\sup_{{\rm diam}(K)\leq 1}\frac{\|f\chi_{K}\|_{L^{p,q}}}{\mathcal{C}(K)^{1/q}}\leq C(n,\alpha,s,p,q)\|f\|_{M^{p,q}},

where the supremum is taken over all compact sets
K\subseteq\mathcal{R}
with nonzero capacities and diameters less than
1
.

Proof.

Denote by

\displaystyle M=\sup_{{\rm diam}(K)\leq 1}\frac{\|f\chi_{K}\|_{L^{p,q}}}{\mathcal{C}(K)^{1/q}}

for simplicity. Let
f\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{R})
and
K\subseteq\mathcal{R}
be compact. Suppose that
\{Q_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a sequence of closed cubes with disjoint interior and unit diameters, whose union is the whole
\mathcal{R}
. Following the first part of the proof of Theorem
1.1
, by denoting
f_{n}=f\chi_{Q_{n}}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|f\chi_{K}\|_{L^{p,q}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\left(\sum_{n}|f_{n}|^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\chi_{K}\right\|_{L^{p,q}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|f_{i}|^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\chi_{K}\right\|_{L^{p,q}}

\displaystyle\leq C(p,q)\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\||f_{i}|^{q}\chi_{K}\|_{L^{p/q,1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=C(p,q)\left(\sum_{n}\|f_{n}\chi_{K}\|_{L^{p,q}}^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq C(p,q)M\left(\sum_{n}\mathcal{C}(K\cap Q_{i})\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Using Proposition
4.2
, one obtains

\displaystyle\sum_{n}\mathcal{C}(K\cap Q_{i})\leq C(n,\alpha,s)\mathcal{C}(K).

(4.5)

Combining (
4.5
) with the above estimates, then
\|f\|_{M^{p,q}}\leq C(n,\alpha,s,p,q)M
. Note that
M\leq\|f\|_{M^{p,q}}
is immediate. We remark that
\mathcal{C}
is translation invariant, then
\mathcal{C}(K)\leq C(n,\alpha,s)
for any compact set
K\subseteq\mathcal{R}
with
{\rm diam}(K)\leq 1
. Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{M^{p,q}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{K}\frac{\|f\chi_{K}\|_{L^{p,q}}}{\mathcal{C}(K)^{1/q}}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,\alpha,s,p,q)\sup_{{\rm diam}(K)\leq 1}\frac{\|f\chi_{K}\|_{L^{p,q}}}{\mathcal{C}(K)^{1/q}}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,\alpha,s,p,q,r)\sup_{{\rm diam}(K)\leq 1}\frac{\|f\chi_{K}\|_{L^{p,r}}}{\mathcal{C}(K)^{1/q}}

\displaystyle=C(n,\alpha,s,p,q,r)\sup_{{\rm diam}(K)\leq 1}\mathcal{C}(K)^{\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{q}}\frac{\|f\chi_{K}\|_{L^{p,r}}}{\mathcal{C}(K)^{1/r}}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}(n,\alpha,s,p,q,r)\sup_{{\rm diam}(K)\leq 1}\frac{\|f\chi_{K}\|_{L^{p,r}}}{\mathcal{C}(K)^{1/r}}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}(n,\alpha,s,p,q,r)\|f\|_{M^{p,r}}

Subsequently, by letting
\varepsilon=q/p
as in (
4.1
) and (
4.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|f\|_{M^{p,q}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{K}\frac{\|f\chi_{K}\|_{L^{p,q}}}{\mathcal{C}(K)^{1/q}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{K}\frac{\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\chi_{K}\|_{L^{p,q}}}{\mathcal{C}(K)^{1/q}}

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\sup_{K}\frac{|K|^{1/p}}{\mathcal{C}(K)^{1/q}}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,\alpha,s,p,q)\|f\|_{L^{\infty}},

which finishes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

Note that
|E|\leq C(n,\alpha,s)\mathcal{C}(E)
holds for
\alpha s\leq n
. For any
1<p<\infty
and
1<q<\infty
, we have
\|f\chi_{K}\|_{L^{p,q}}\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}|K|^{1/p}\leq C(n,\alpha,s,p)\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\mathcal{C}(E)^{1/p}
, whence
L^{\infty}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{M}^{p,q}
.

To prove Theorem
1.2
, we need the following technical lemma.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
1<q<\infty
. For any
f\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{R})
, it holds that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{M^{p,q}}=\sup_{E}\frac{\|f\chi_{E}\|_{L^{p,q}}}{\mathcal{C}(E)^{1/q}},

where the supremum is taken over all measurable sets
E\subseteq\mathcal{R}
with
0<\mathcal{C}(E)<\infty
.

Proof.

Denote by

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle=\sup_{E}\frac{\|f\chi_{E}\|_{L^{p,q}}}{\mathcal{C}(E)^{1/q}},

\displaystyle M^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\sup_{G}\frac{\|f\chi_{G}\|_{L^{p,q}}}{\mathcal{C}(G)^{1/q}}

for simplicity, where the supremum in
M^{\prime}
is taken over all open sets
G\subseteq\mathcal{R}
with
0<\mathcal{C}(G)<\infty
. It is evident that
\|f\|_{M^{p,q}}\leq M
. To show that
M\leq\|f\|_{M^{p,q}}
, we need the auxiliary term
M^{\prime}
. To this end, let
E\subseteq\mathcal{R}
be measurable with
0<\mathcal{C}(E)<\infty
. By the outer regularity of
\mathcal{C}
, one can find a sequence
\{G_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
of open subsets of
\mathcal{R}
such that
G_{n}\supseteq E
,
\mathcal{C}(G_{n})<\infty
, and
\mathcal{C}(G_{n})\rightarrow\mathcal{C}(E)
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{\|f\chi_{E}\|_{L^{p,q}}}{\mathcal{C}(E)^{1/q}}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\|f\chi_{E}\|_{L^{p,q}}}{\mathcal{C}(G_{n})^{1/q}}\leq\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\|f\chi_{G_{n}}\|_{L^{p,q}}}{\mathcal{C}(G_{n})^{1/q}}\leq M^{\prime}.

Taking supremum over all such
E
, we deduce that
M\leq M^{\prime}
. It remains to show that
M^{\prime}=\|f\|_{M^{p,q}}
. Let
G\subseteq\mathcal{R}
be open with
0<\mathcal{C}(G)<\infty
. Since
G
is open, there exists an increasing sequence
\{K_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
of compact subsets of
G
such that

\displaystyle G=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}K_{n}.

Then
\mathcal{C}(K_{n})\rightarrow\mathcal{C}(G)
and
f\chi_{K_{n}}\uparrow f\chi_{G}
, whence

\displaystyle\frac{\|f\chi_{G}\|_{L^{p,q}}}{\mathcal{C}(G)^{1/q}}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\|f\chi_{K_{n}}\|_{L^{p,q}}}{\mathcal{C}(K_{n})^{1/q}}\leq\|f\|_{M^{p,q}}.

Taking supremum over all such
G
, one obtains
M^{\prime}\leq\|f\|_{M^{p,q}}
, which completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

By re-examining the above proof, one may obtain a similar formula for
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{M}^{p,q}}
, where
1<p<\infty
and
1<q\leq\infty
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We will prove only for
(B^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}})^{\ast}\approx M^{p,q}
, the assertion that
(\mathcal{B}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}})^{\ast}\approx\mathcal{M}^{p,q}
can be argued in a similar fashion. Let
f\in M^{p,q}
and
g\in B^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}
be given. Decompose
g
into an arbitrary block decomposition of the form

\displaystyle g=\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}b_{k},

(4.6)

as in the definition of
B^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}
. We may assume the associated
E_{k}
satisfies
\mathcal{C}(E_{k})>0
, for otherwise the term
b_{k}
in (
4.6
) can be dropped as
b_{k}
would vanish on
E_{k}
almost everywhere. Note that
\mathcal{C}(E_{k})<\infty
as
E_{k}
is a bounded set. Then Lemma
4.5
gives

\displaystyle|\mathcal{L}_{f}(g)|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathcal{R}}f(x)g(x)dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathcal{R}}|f(x)g(x)|dx

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}|\int_{E_{k}}|f(x)||b_{k}(x)|dx

\displaystyle\leq C(p,q)\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}|\|f\chi_{E_{k}}\|_{L^{p,q}}\|b_{k}\|_{L^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq C(p,q)\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}|\frac{\|f\chi_{E_{k}}\|_{L^{p,q}}}{\mathcal{C}(E_{k})^{1/q}}\mathcal{C}(E_{k})^{\frac{1}{q}}\|b_{k}\|_{L^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq C(p,q)\|f\|_{M^{p,q}}\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}|.

(4.7)

Taking infimum to all such block decompositions, one obtains
M^{p,q}\hookrightarrow(B^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}})^{\ast}
. We also infer from (
4.7
) that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{(B^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}})^{\prime}}=\sup\left\{\int_{\mathcal{R}}|f(x)g(x)|dx:\|g\|_{B^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}}\leq 1\right\}\leq C(p,q)\|f\|_{M^{p,q}},

which gives
M^{p,q}\hookrightarrow(B^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}})^{\prime}
.

Conversely, let
\mathcal{L}\in(B^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}})^{\ast}
be given. If
g
is a nonzero function in
L^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}
with
\{g\neq 0\}\subseteq E
for some bounded set, then it is easy to see that
g\in B^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}
as we may write that
g=\mathcal{C}(E)^{1/q}\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}}\widetilde{g}
with
\widetilde{g}=g/(\mathcal{C}(E)^{1/q}\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}})
. This leads to

\displaystyle\|g\|_{B^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}}\leq\mathcal{C}(E)^{\frac{1}{q}}\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}},

and hence

\displaystyle|\mathcal{L}(g)|\leq\|\mathcal{L}\|\mathcal{C}(E)^{\frac{1}{q}}\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}}.

In other words, the restriction functional
\mathcal{L}|_{L^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(E)}:L^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(E)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is continuous. Using the fact that
L^{p,q}(E)\approx(L^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(E))^{\ast}
, there is an
f_{E}\in L^{p,q}(E)
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(g)=\int_{E}f_{E}(x)g(x)dx,\quad g\in L^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(E).

By restricting
E_{N}=[-N,N]^{n}
,
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{E_{N}}f_{E_{N}}(x)g(x)dx=\int_{E_{N}}f_{E_{N+1}}(x)g(x)dx

for all continuous functions
g
on
E_{N}
, which yields
f_{E_{N}}=f_{E_{N+1}}
almost everywhere on
E_{N}
by the density result in Proposition
2.1
. Then we may define unambiguously a function
f
with
f(x)=f_{E_{N}}(x)
for
x\in E_{N}
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(g)=\int_{\mathcal{R}}f(x)g(x)dx

(4.8)

holds for any
g\in L^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}
with
\{g\neq 0\}\subseteq E_{N}
. In particular, given a compact set
K\subseteq\mathcal{R}
, say,
K\subseteq E_{N}
, and
g={\rm sgn}(f)\chi_{K}
, the equality (
4.8
) shows that
f
is integrable on
K
. Since the compact set
K
is arbitrary, we deduce that
f
is locally integrable on
\mathcal{R}
. Repeating the proof of
[
GL
, Theorem 1.4.16 (vi)]
, one can show that the function
f
satisfies

\displaystyle\|f\chi_{K}\chi_{|f|\leq N}\|_{L^{p,q}}\leq C(p,q)\|\mathcal{L}\|\mathcal{C}(K)^{\frac{1}{q}},\quad N=1,2,\ldots.

Taking
N\rightarrow\infty
, we have
\|f\chi_{K}\|_{L^{p,q}}\leq C(p,q)\|\mathcal{L}\|\mathcal{C}(K)^{1/q}
, which gives

\displaystyle\|f\|_{M^{p,q}}\leq C(p,q)\|\mathcal{L}\|.

(4.9)

Now we extend the validity of (
4.8
) to all
g\in B^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}
. As before, let
g
be as in (
4.6
). Consider the compactly supported functions
g_{n}
,
n=1,2,\ldots
defined by

\displaystyle g_{n}=\sum_{|k|\leq n}\lambda_{k}b_{k}.

The sequence
\{g_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
converges to
g
in
B^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. On the other hand, the functions

\displaystyle h_{n}=\sum_{|k|\leq n}|\lambda_{k}||b_{k}|,\quad n=1,2,\ldots,

belong to
B^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}
with

\displaystyle\|h_{n}\|_{B^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}}\leq\sum_{|k|\leq n}|\lambda_{k}|.

Using (
4.7
) with
|f|
in place of
f
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{R}}|f(x)h_{n}(x)|dx\leq C(p,q)\|f\|_{M^{p,q}}\|h_{n}\|_{B^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}}\leq C(p,q)\|f\|_{M^{p,q}}\sum_{|k|\leq n}|\lambda_{k}|.

Taking
n\rightarrow\infty
and using monotone convergence theorem, one has

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{R}}|f(x)|\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}||b_{k}(x)|dx\leq C(p,q)\|f\|\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}|<\infty,

which also shows that
fg\in L^{1}
. As a result, using Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem and the continuity of
\mathcal{L}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(g)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{L}(g_{n})=\int_{\mathcal{R}}f(x)g_{n}(x)dx=\int_{\mathcal{R}}f(x)g(x)dx,

which yields (
4.8
) for general
g\in B^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}
. We conclude that
(B^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}})^{\ast}\hookrightarrow M^{p,q}
. Further, we infer from (
4.9
) that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{M^{p,q}}\leq C(p,q)\sup\left\{\left|\int_{\mathcal{R}}f(x)g(x)dx\right|:\|g\|_{B^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}}\leq 1\right\}\leq C(p,q)\|f\|_{(B^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}})^{\prime}},

which gives
(B^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}})^{\prime}\hookrightarrow M^{p,q}
.

Finally, we show that
B^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}
is a Banach space. It can be seen from the above argument that if a block decomposition (
4.6
) of
g
exists, then the sum in (
4.6
) is almost everywhere absolutely convergent. As a consequence, the space
B^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}
is topological complete. To finish the proof, we only need to show that if
\|g\|_{B^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}}=0
, then
g=0
almost everywhere. To this end, let
f\in C_{0}
be given. Since
f\in M^{p,q}
, it follows from the duality result above that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{R}}|f(x)g(x)|dx\leq C(n,\alpha,s,p,q)\|f\|_{M^{p,q}}\|g\|_{B^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}}=0.

Then it is a standard fact in analysis that
g=0
almost everywhere as
f\in C_{0}
is arbitrary. The proof is now finished.
∎

Remark 4.7
.

For
1<p<\infty
and
1<q<\infty
, it is worth mentioning that
B^{p,q}
is solid in the sense that if
f\in B^{p,q}
and
|g|\leq|f|
, then
g\in B^{p,q}
. To see this, let

\displaystyle f=\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}b_{k}

be a block decomposition. Express
g
as

\displaystyle g=\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}gf^{-1}\chi_{\{f\neq 0\}}b_{k}.

Then

\displaystyle\{gf^{-1}\chi_{\{f\neq 0\}}b_{k}\neq 0\}\subseteq\{b_{k}\neq 0\}\subseteq E_{k}

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(E_{k})^{1/q^{\prime}}\|gf^{-1}\chi_{\{f\neq 0\}}b_{k}\|_{L^{p,q}}\leq\mathcal{C}(E_{k})^{1/q^{\prime}}\|b_{k}\|_{L^{p,q}}\leq 1.

We conclude that

\displaystyle\|g\|_{B^{p,q}}\leq\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}|.

Taking infimum over all such block decompositions, one obtains
\|g\|_{B^{p,q}}\leq\|f\|_{B^{p,q}}
. Hence
g\in B^{p,q}
, as claimed.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

We first show (
1.3
) for
1<q\leq p<\infty
, where its proof is inspired by
[
MST
, Theorem 2.1]
. Define the auxiliary seminorm
\left\|\cdot\right\|
by

\displaystyle\|f\|=\inf\left\{\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}|:f=\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}b_{k}\right\},

(4.10)

where the infimum is taken over all pointwise almost everywhere convergence of infinite sum consisting of
b_{k}\in N^{p,q}
such that
\|b_{k}\|_{N^{p,q}}\leq 1
. We claim that

\displaystyle C(n,\alpha,s,p,q)^{-1}\left\|\cdot\right\|_{N^{p,q}}\leq\left\|\cdot\right\|\leq C(n,\alpha,s,p,q)\left\|\cdot\right\|_{N^{p,q}}.

(4.11)

Let
\varepsilon>0
and write
f=(\|f\|_{N^{p,q}}+\varepsilon)\cdot f/(\|f\|_{N^{p,q}}+\varepsilon)
. One obtains at once that

\displaystyle\|f\|\leq\|f\|_{N^{p,q}}+\varepsilon.

The arbitrariness of
\varepsilon
gives
\left\|\cdot\right\|\leq\left\|\cdot\right\|_{N^{p,q}}
. For the converse, let
\varepsilon>0
and
f
be decomposed as in (
4.10
). Then

\displaystyle\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}|\leq\|f\|+\varepsilon.

As
\|b_{k}\|_{N^{p,q}}\leq 1
, there is an
\omega_{k}\in L^{1}(\mathcal{C})\cap A_{1}^{\rm loc}
such that
\|\omega_{k}\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{C})}\leq 1
,
[\omega_{k}]_{A_{1}^{\rm loc}}\leq\mathfrak{c}
, and

\displaystyle\|b_{k}\omega_{k}^{-1/p^{\prime}}\|_{L^{q,p}}\leq\|b_{k}\|_{N^{p,q}}+\varepsilon\leq 1+\varepsilon.

Using Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle|f|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}||b_{k}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}|\omega_{k}^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}|b_{k}|\omega_{k}^{-\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}|\omega_{k}\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\left(\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}||b_{k}|^{q}\omega_{k}^{1-q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Denote by

\displaystyle\omega=\frac{\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}|\omega_{k}}{\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}|}.

First we verify that

\displaystyle\|\omega\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{C})}\leq C(n,\alpha,s)\frac{1}{\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}|}\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}|\|\omega_{k}\|_{L^{1}(C)}\leq C(n,\alpha,s)

by the normability of
L^{1}(\mathcal{C})
. Moreover, we have

\displaystyle{\bf M}^{\rm loc}\omega\leq\frac{1}{\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}|}\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}|{\bf M}^{\rm loc}\omega_{k}\leq\mathfrak{c}\cdot\frac{1}{\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}|}\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}|\omega_{k}\leq\mathfrak{c}\cdot\omega

almost everywhere, which shows that
[\omega]_{A_{1}^{\rm loc}}\leq\mathfrak{c}
. Recall the assumption that
1<q\leq p<\infty
. Then
p/q\geq 1
, whence
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L^{p/q,1}}
is quasi-additive. Subsequently, it holds that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{N^{p,q}}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\omega^{-1/q^{\prime}}\|_{L^{p,q}}

\displaystyle=C(n,\alpha,s,q)\left(\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}|\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\left\|\left(\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}||b_{k}|^{q}\omega_{k}^{1-q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right\|_{L^{p,q}}

\displaystyle=C(n,\alpha,s,q)\left(\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}|\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\left\|\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}||b_{k}|^{q}\omega_{k}^{1-q}\right\|_{L^{p/q,1}}^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,\alpha,s,p,q)\left(\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}|\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\left[\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}|\left\||b_{k}|^{q}\omega_{k}^{1-q}\right\|_{L^{p/q,1}}\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=C(n,\alpha,s,p,q)\left(\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}|\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\left[\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}|\|b_{k}\omega_{k}^{-1/q^{\prime}}\|_{L^{p,q}}^{q}\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,\alpha,s,p,q)(1+\varepsilon)\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}|.

Taking infimum over all such decompositions (
4.10
), the claim (
4.11
) follows by the arbitrariness of
\varepsilon
.

Let
f\in B^{p,q}
be given. Decompose
f
into an arbitrary block decomposition of the form

\displaystyle f=\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}b_{k},

as in the definition of
B^{p,q}
. Note that
\{b_{k}\neq 0\}\subseteq E_{k}
, where
E_{k}
is bounded with
\mathcal{C}(E_{k})^{1/q^{\prime}}\|b_{k}\|_{L^{p,q}}\leq 1
. Let
V^{E_{k}}
be the nonlinear potential associated to
E_{k}
with the admissible exponent
\delta
. Then
(V^{E_{k}})^{\delta}/\mathcal{C}(E_{k})\in L^{1}(\mathcal{C})\cap A_{1}^{\rm loc}
,
[(V^{E_{k}})^{\delta}/\mathcal{C}(E_{k})]_{A_{1}^{\rm loc}}\leq\mathfrak{c}
, and
\|(V^{E_{k}})^{\delta}/\mathcal{C}(E_{k})\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{C})}\leq C(n,\alpha,s)
. Hence

\displaystyle\|b_{k}\|_{N^{p,q}}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,\alpha,s,p,q)\left\|b_{k}\left[\frac{(V^{E_{k}})^{\delta}}{\mathcal{C}(E_{k})}\right]^{-1/q^{\prime}}\right\|_{L^{p,q}}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,\alpha,s,p,q)\mathcal{C}(E_{k})^{1/q^{\prime}}\|b_{k}\|_{L^{p,q}}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,\alpha,s,p,q)

by noting that
(V^{E_{k}})^{\delta}\geq 1
on
E_{k}
and
b_{k}=0
on
\mathcal{R}\setminus E_{k}
. Combining (
4.10
) with (
4.11
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{N^{p,q}}\leq C(n,\alpha,s,p,q)\|f\|_{B^{p,q}},

which gives
B^{p,q}\hookrightarrow N^{q,p}
when
1<q\leq p<\infty
.

It remains to show (
1.4
) for
1<p\leq q<\infty
. We may assume for
p<q
as the case where
p=q
reduces to the result in
[
OP
]
. Let
\varepsilon>0
and
f\in N^{q,p}
be given. There is an
\omega\in L^{1}(\mathcal{C})
such that
\|\omega\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{C})}\leq 1
and

\displaystyle\|f\omega^{-1/q^{\prime}}\|_{L^{p,q}}\leq\|f\|_{N^{p,q}}+\varepsilon.

Note that

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{k}\mathcal{C}(\{2^{k-1}<\omega\leq 2^{k}\})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{2^{k-2}}^{2^{k-1}}\mathcal{C}(\{2^{k-1}<\omega\leq 2^{k}\})dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{2^{k-2}}^{2^{k-1}}\mathcal{C}(\{\omega>t\})dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\|\omega\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{C})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}.

Let
E_{k}=\{2^{k-1}<\omega\leq 2^{k}\}
and
D_{l}=\{l-1\leq|x|<l\}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,

. Since
\omega(x)<\infty

\mathcal{C}
-quasi-everywhere, we can express

\displaystyle f=\sum_{k,l}f\chi_{E_{k}\cap D_{l}}=\sum_{k,l}\lambda_{k,l}b_{k,l}

almost everywhere with

\displaystyle\lambda_{k,l}

\displaystyle=\|f\chi_{E_{k}\cap D_{l}}\|_{L^{p,q}}\mathcal{C}(E_{k}\cap D_{l})^{1/q^{\prime}},

\displaystyle b_{k,l}

\displaystyle=\|f\chi_{E_{k}\cap D_{l}}\|_{L^{p,q}}^{-1}\mathcal{C}(E_{k}\cap D_{l})^{-1/q^{\prime}}f\chi_{E_{k}\cap D_{l}},

where we have used the convention that

whenever

almost everywhere on

) and (

). It is immediate that

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(E_{k}\cap D_{l})^{1/q^{\prime}}\|b_{k,l}\|_{L^{p,q}}=1.

Subsequently, it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{k,l}\lambda_{k,l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l}\|f\chi_{E_{k}\cap D_{l}}\|_{L^{p,q}}\mathcal{C}(E_{k}\cap D_{l})^{1/q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l}\|f\omega^{-1/q^{\prime}}\omega^{1/q^{\prime}}\chi_{E_{k}\cap D_{l}}\|_{L^{p,q}}\mathcal{C}(E_{k}\cap D_{l})^{1/q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k,l}\|f\omega^{-1/q^{\prime}}\chi_{E_{k}\cap D_{l}}\|_{L^{p,q}}2^{k/q^{\prime}}\mathcal{C}(E_{k}\cap D_{l})^{1/q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{k,l}\|f\omega^{-1/q^{\prime}}\chi_{E_{k}\cap D_{l}}\|_{L^{p,q}}^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\sum_{k,l}2^{k}\mathcal{C}(E_{k}\cap D_{l})\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}.

1.1
with

\displaystyle=C(p,q)\|f\omega^{-1/q^{\prime}}\|_{L^{p,q}}.

Moreover, using the quasi-additivity of
\mathcal{C}
(see
[

\displaystyle\sum_{k,l}2^{k}\mathcal{C}(E_{k}\cap D_{l})\leq C(n,\alpha,s)\sum_{k}2^{k}\mathcal{C}(E_{k})\leq C^{\prime}(n,\alpha,s).

Proof of Proposition
1.4
.

We first prove for the weak Fatou’s property of
N^{p,q}
. To this end, let
\{f_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subseteq N^{p,q}
be an increasing sequence of nonnegative functions. Denote by

\displaystyle f=\sup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}f_{n}

and

\displaystyle M=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|f_{n}\|_{N^{p,q}}<\infty

for simplicity. Let
\varepsilon>0
be given. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there is an
\omega_{n}
as in the definition of
N^{p,q}
such that

\displaystyle\|f_{n}\omega_{n}^{-1/q^{\prime}}\|_{N^{p,q}}<\|f_{n}\|_{L^{p,q}}+\varepsilon.

Since
\|\omega_{n}\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{C})}\leq 1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
L^{1}(\mathcal{C})\hookrightarrow L^{1}
, we can use Kómlos theorem to obtain a subsequence, still denoted by
\{\omega_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
, and such that

\displaystyle\sigma_{n}=\frac{\omega_{1}+\cdots+\omega_{n}}{n}\rightarrow\overline{\omega}

almost everywhere for some nonnegative measurable function
\overline{\omega}
(see
[
DL
, Theorem 3.1]
). Define

\displaystyle\omega(x)=\begin{cases}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sigma_{n}(x),\quad&\text{if the limit exists},\\
0,\quad&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Then
\omega\leq\liminf\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sigma_{n}
and
\omega=\overline{\omega}
almost everywhere. Further, we have

\displaystyle\|\omega\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{C})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sigma_{n}\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{C})}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|\sigma_{n}\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{C})}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,\alpha,s)\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|\omega_{k}\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{C})}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,\alpha,s).

Moreover, it holds that

\displaystyle{\bf M}^{\rm loc}\omega={\bf M}^{\rm loc}\overline{\omega}\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\bf M}^{\rm loc}\omega_{k}\leq\mathfrak{c}\cdot\omega

almost everywhere, which yields
\omega\in A_{1}^{\rm loc}
with
[\omega]_{A_{1}^{\rm loc}}\leq\mathfrak{c}
. In other words,
\omega
satisfies the weight conditions in
N^{p,q}
. For fixed
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\|f_{k}\omega^{-1/q^{\prime}}\|_{L^{p,q}}

\displaystyle=\|f_{k}\overline{\omega}^{-1/q^{\prime}}\|_{L^{p,q}}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|f_{k}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=k}^{k+n-1}\omega_{i}\right)^{-1/q^{\prime}}\right\|_{L^{p,q}}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=k}^{k+n-1}f_{k}\omega_{i}^{-1/q^{\prime}}\right\|_{L^{p,q}}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,\alpha,s,p,q)\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=k}^{k+n-1}\|f_{k}\omega_{i}^{-1/q^{\prime}}\|_{L^{p,q}}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,\alpha,s,p,q)\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=k}^{k+n-1}\|f_{i}\omega_{i}^{-1/q^{\prime}}\|_{L^{p,q}}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,\alpha,s,p,q)(M+\varepsilon),

where we have used Jensen’s inequality for the convex function
u\rightarrow u^{-1/q^{\prime}}
,
u>0
, and also the fact that
f_{k}\leq f_{i}
for
k\leq i
. Taking
k\rightarrow\infty
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|f\|_{N^{p,q}}\leq C(n,\alpha,s,p,q)\|f\omega^{-1/q^{\prime}}\|_{L^{p,q}}\leq C(n,\alpha,s,p,q)(M+\varepsilon).

Since
\varepsilon
is arbitrary, it follows that
\|f\|_{N^{p,q}}\leq C(n,\alpha,s,p,q)M
, which completes the proof for weak Fatou’s property.

It remains to show that
\|f\|_{N^{p,q}}=0
entails
f=0
almost everywhere. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, choose an
\omega_{n}
as in the definition of
N^{p,q}
such that

\displaystyle\|f\omega_{n}^{-1/q^{\prime}}\|_{L^{p,q}}<2^{-n}.

By defining
\omega
as before, we obtain

\displaystyle\|f\omega^{-1/q^{\prime}}\|_{L^{p,q}}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|f\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\omega_{k}\right)^{-1/q^{\prime}}\right\|_{L^{p,q}}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}f\omega_{k}^{-1/q^{\prime}}\right\|_{L^{p,q}}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,\alpha,s,p,q)\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|f\omega_{k}^{-1/q^{\prime}}\|_{L^{p,q}}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,\alpha,s,p,q)\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\|f\omega_{k}^{-1/q^{\prime}}\|_{L^{p,q}}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,\alpha,s,p,q)\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{k}}

\displaystyle=0.

Therefore, we have
f\omega^{-1/q^{\prime}}=0
almost everywhere. Since
\omega
is a weight, we deduce that
f=0
almost everywhere. For
1<q\leq p<\infty
, it is shown in (
4.10
) that
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{N^{p,q}}\approx\left\|\cdot\right\|
, while the space
N^{p,q}
endowed with
\left\|\cdot\right\|
is clearly complete by the almost everywhere absolutely convergent series defined in
\left\|\cdot\right\|
. We conclude that
N^{p,q}
is a complete normable space, which finishes the proof.
∎

Proof of Proposition
1.5
.

We first show the density of
C_{0}
in
B^{p,q}
. Let
f\in B^{p,q}
and
\varepsilon>0
be given. Then there is a block decomposition of
f
that

\displaystyle f=\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}b_{k}

and

\displaystyle\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}|<\|f\|_{B^{p,q}}+1<\infty

as in the definition of
B^{p,q}
. As shown in the proof of Theorem
1.2
, the block decomposition is almost everywhere absolutely convergent. Then

\displaystyle\left\|f-\sum_{|k|\leq n}\lambda_{k}b_{k}\right\|_{B^{p,q}}=\left\|\sum_{|k|>n}\lambda_{k}b_{k}\right\|_{B^{p,q}}\leq\sum_{|k|>n}|\lambda_{k}|\rightarrow 0,\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

Then there is an
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle\left\|f-\sum_{|k|\leq n}\lambda_{k}b_{k}\right\|_{B^{p,q}}<\varepsilon.

Using Proposition
2.1
, for each
k=1,2,\ldots,n
, there is a
\varphi_{k}\in C_{0}
such that

\displaystyle\|b_{k}-\varphi_{k}\|_{L^{p,q}}<\left(1+\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}|\right)^{-1}\varepsilon.

Then the function
\varphi
defined by

\displaystyle\varphi=\sum_{|k|\leq n}\lambda_{k}\varphi_{k}

belongs to
C_{0}
such that

\displaystyle\|f-\varphi\|_{B^{p,q}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|f-\sum_{|k|\leq n}\lambda_{k}b_{k}\right\|_{B^{p,q}}+\left\|\sum_{|k|\leq n}\lambda_{k}b_{k}-\varphi\right\|_{B^{p,q}}

\displaystyle<\varepsilon+\sum_{|k|\leq n}|\lambda_{k}|\|f-\varphi_{k}\|_{B^{p,q}}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon+\left(\sum_{|k|\leq n}|\lambda_{k}|\right)\left(1+\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}|\right)^{-1}\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq 2\varepsilon,

which justifies the density claim.

For the density of
C_{0}
in
N^{p,q}
, we proceed as follows. Let
f\in N^{p,q}
and
\varepsilon>0
. Then there is an
\omega
as in the definition of
N^{p,q}
such that

\displaystyle\|f\omega^{-1/q^{\prime}}\|_{L^{p,q}}<\|f\|_{N^{p,q}}+1<\infty.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define
f_{n}=\min(f,n)\chi_{\{|x|\leq n\}}
. Then
f_{n}\uparrow f
pointwise everywhere. We claim that

\displaystyle\|f-f_{n}\|_{N^{p,q}}\rightarrow 0,\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

(4.12)

To this end, we may first assume that
|f|<\infty
almost everywhere as
f\omega^{-1/q^{\prime}}\in L^{p,q}
and
\omega
is a weight. Then
f-f_{n}\downarrow 0
almost everywhere. For fixed
0<t<\infty
, we have

\displaystyle|\{(f-f_{n})\omega^{-1/q^{\prime}}>t\}|\downarrow 0

by Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem as
0\leq(f-f_{n})\omega^{-1/q^{\prime}}\leq f\omega^{-1/q^{\prime}}
and
|\{f\omega^{-1/q^{\prime}}>t\}|<\infty
. As a consequence, using Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem again, one has

\displaystyle\|(f-f_{n})\omega^{-1/q^{\prime}}\|_{L^{p,q}}^{q}=p\int_{0}^{\infty}|\{(f-f_{n})\omega^{-1/q^{\prime}}>t\}|^{\frac{q}{p}}t^{q}\frac{dt}{t}\rightarrow 0

as
|\{(f-f_{n})\omega^{-1/q^{\prime}}>t\}|^{q/p}\leq|\{f\omega^{-1/q^{\prime}}>t\}|^{q/p}
and
f\omega^{-1/q^{\prime}}\in L^{p,q}
. Then

\displaystyle\|f-f_{n}\|_{N^{p,q}}\leq\|(f-f_{n})\omega^{-1/q^{\prime}}\|_{L^{p,q}}\rightarrow 0,\quad n\rightarrow\infty,

which yields (
4.12
). As a result, there is an
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\|f-f_{n}\|_{N^{p,q}}<\varepsilon
. Using Proposition
2.1
, we can choose a
\varphi\in C_{0}
such that
\|f_{n}-\varphi\|_{L^{p,q}}<\varepsilon
. Let
\omega
be the nonlinear potential associated with the set
\{|x|\leq M\}
, where
M>0
is such that
\{f_{n}\neq 0\}\cup\{\varphi\neq 0\}\subseteq\{|x|\leq M\}
. Then
\omega\geq\chi_{\{|x|\leq M\}}
and hence

\displaystyle\|f-\varphi\|_{N^{p,q}}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,\alpha,s,p,q)(\|f-f_{n}\|_{N^{p,q}}+\|f_{n}-\varphi\|_{N^{p,q}})

\displaystyle<C(n,\alpha,s,p,q)(\varepsilon+\|(f_{n}-\varphi)\omega^{-1/q^{\prime}}\|_{L^{p,q}})

\displaystyle<C^{\prime}(n,\alpha,s,p,q)\varepsilon,

which finishes the proof.
∎

\mathcal{C}

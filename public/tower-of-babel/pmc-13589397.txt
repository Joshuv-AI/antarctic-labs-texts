Vortex Vein Morphology and Choroidal Thickness as Predictors of Refractive Status: Model Construction and Mediation Analysis
Authors: Huyong Zou, Junming Wang, Zhiping Liu, Juan Peng, Qun Lu, Xi Yin, Binhe Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeThe purpose of this study was to investigate how vortex vein geometry and choroidal structure relate to refractive status and to develop an internally validated prediction framework based on these posterior segment features.MethodsA cross-sectional study was conducted on 147 participants, including 49 with myopic eyes, 49 with emmetropic eyes, and 49 with hyperopic eyes. Participants were young adults aged approximately 18 years, and axial length (AL) ranged from 23.19 ± 0.09 mm in hyperopic eyes to 24.92 ± 0.30 mm in myopic eyes. All participants underwent multimodal imaging, including ultra-widefield fundus photography, enhanced-depth imaging optical coherence tomography (EDI-OCT), and optical coherence tomography angiography (OCTA). Temporal–superior (TS) vortex vein distances, subfoveal choroidal thickness (SFCT), AL, and spherical equivalent (SE) were analyzed using least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression, restricted cubic splines (RCS), multinomial logistic modeling, and mediation analysis.ResultsCompared with hyperopic eyes, myopic eyes showed significantly longer AL and thinner SFCT (both P < 0.001). LASSO regression retained TS together with established refractive parameters including SFCT and AL in the final predictive model. RCS analysis demonstrated significant nonlinear associations of AL and SFCT with myopia probability (both P < 0.001). The internally validated nomograms achieved AUCs of approximately 0.99 for myopia and 0.97 for hyperopia classification. Mediation analysis showed that SFCT accounted for approximately 64.7% of the association between TS and SE.ConclusionsCombining vortex vein morphology with choroidal metrics offers a comprehensive structural framework for refractive assessment and suggests potential utility for refractive risk stratification, pending external validation.Translational RelevanceThese findings translate posterior segment imaging biomarkers into proof-of-concept prediction nomograms for refractive risk stratification, with potential future clinical relevance pending external validation.

Introduction

Refractive disorders, particularly myopia, have become a major global public health concern. Recent epidemiological evidence from a large meta-analysis showed that the pooled prevalence of myopia among children and adolescents increased from approximately 24.3% in 1990 to nearly 35.8% by 2023, with projections suggesting that the prevalence may reach approximately 39.8% by 2050.1 This upward trend is especially pronounced in East Asian populations and in school-aged youths.2,3 The progression of refractive errors is closely linked to axial elongation and to metabolic and perfusion changes within the outer retina.4 As the principal vascular supply to the outer retina, the choroid plays a fundamental role in ocular structural characteristics associated with refractive status, and alterations in its thickness and vascular characteristics have been consistently associated with refractive status.5,6 Vortex veins serve as the main outflow pathways of the choroidal circulation, and their morphological features and drainage patterns may influence choroidal perfusion.7 However, their potential associations with refractive status remain insufficiently explored.

Advances in ocular imaging technologies, including enhanced-depth imaging optical coherence tomography (EDI-OCT), swept-source OCT, and OCT angiography (OCTA), have facilitated accurate measurement of choroidal thickness, vascular volume, and the distribution of Haller and Sattler layers. Studies using these modalities have demonstrated that choroidal thinning is strongly associated with longer axial length (AL).8,9 For example, a recent population-based study reported that subfoveal choroidal thickness (SFCT) in highly myopic eyes averaged 110.6 ± 85.2 micrometers and decreased by approximately 22.6 micrometers for each additional millimeter of AL. Parallel investigations have begun to examine vortex vein morphology.10 In a clinical cohort, posterior vortex vein anomalies were detected in 26.4% of highly myopic eyes compared with 10.3% of emmetropic eyes, and the presence of these anomalies was significantly associated with refractive state.11 Although these findings suggest that both choroidal structure and vortex vein characteristics are relevant to ocular structural correlates of refractive status, existing studies typically assess these features separately and do not fully explore their inter-relationship.

Several important gaps remain in the current literature. First, there is a lack of studies that simultaneously evaluate vortex vein morphology and choroidal structural parameters within the same analytic framework. Consequently, it is unclear how outflow drainage patterns relate to choroidal thickness, vascular density, or vascular volume. Second, the potential statistical relationships through which vortex vein characteristics influence refractive status by altering choroidal structure have not been systematically examined. Evidence regarding such association patterns remains limited. Third, despite the growing interest in imaging biomarkers for refractive prediction, no predictive model has yet integrated vortex vein features together with choroidal metrics to improve the assessment of refractive status. These limitations restrict the current understanding of the structural correlates of refractive status.

The present study addresses these knowledge gaps by using EDI-OCT, OCTA, and ultra-widefield (UWF) fundus imaging to comprehensively quantify vortex vein morphology and choroidal parameters, including choroidal thickness, choroidal vascular volume, and the proportion of choroidal vasculature. Based on these measurements, we constructed a prediction framework for refractive status and further examined whether choroidal parameters statistically mediate the association between vortex vein characteristics and refractive outcomes. The findings are expected to advance current knowledge regarding structural association patterns related to refractive status and to provide potential imaging biomarkers for future hypothesis-driven and longitudinal investigations.

Materials and Methods

Study Design and Participants

This study was conducted as a cross-sectional observational investigation aimed at evaluating the associations among vortex vein morphology, choroidal structural parameters, and refractive status. Participants were recruited from individuals undergoing routine ophthalmic examinations at our institution during the study period. To ensure balanced representation across refractive categories, eligible participants were intentionally selected and allocated into myopia, emmetropia, and hyperopia groups with comparable sample sizes, rather than reflecting the natural distribution of refractive status in the general population. Eligible subjects were required to have clear ocular media and reliable refractive and imaging measurements. Eyes with a history of ocular surgery, trauma, retinal or choroidal pathology, systemic diseases known to affect the posterior segment, or poor-quality OCT images were excluded. When both eyes met the inclusion criteria, one eye was randomly selected for analysis to avoid inter-eye correlation. The study protocol adhered to the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Clinical Research and Application Ethics Committee of the Second Affiliated Hospital of Guangzhou Medical University.

Interventions/Imaging Acquisition

All participants underwent a standardized multimodal imaging protocol, including UWF fundus photography, EDI-OCT, and OCTA. UWF images were acquired to visualize the choroidal venous outflow pathways and to clearly display the emergence of vortex vein trunks around the equatorial region. UWF images were reconstructed using the device-integrated stereographic projection correction algorithm before quantitative measurements were performed. Radial distances from the vortex vein ampulla to the optic disc center were then measured on the corrected images using the built-in analysis software. EDI-OCT scans consisted of both vertical and horizontal macular line scans centered on the fovea, allowing high-contrast delineation of the choroid–sclera interface. OCTA imaging was performed using a 6 × 6 mm macular scan pattern to quantify choroidal vascular perfusion within the corresponding slabs.

All images were reviewed by two trained graders masked to refractive status. Image quality criteria included adequate illumination, clear vessel boundaries, and continuous delineation of choroidal structures. Scans with motion artifacts, segmentation errors, or insufficient visualization of the scleral interface were excluded. Disagreements between graders were resolved by a senior retinal specialist.

Refractive measurements were obtained using an autorefractor under standardized lighting conditions, followed by subjective refinement when required. Cycloplegic refraction was performed prior to refractive assessment. Spherical equivalent (SE) was calculated as spherical power plus half of the cylindrical power. Eyes were classified as myopic when SE ≤ −0.50 diopters (D), emmetropic when SE ranged from −0.50 D to +0.50 D, and hyperopic when SE ≥ +0.50 D.

Measurements and Observations

The primary outcomes were SE and refractive status. SE was calculated as spherical power plus half of the cylindrical power, and eyes were classified as myopic, emmetropic, or hyperopic based on conventional thresholds.

Explanatory variables included AL, central retinal thickness (CRT), SFCT, choroidal vascular index (CVI), and vortex vein positional parameters measured from UWF images, including nasal–superior (NS), temporal–superior (TS), nasal–inferior (NI), and temporal–inferior (TI) distances. NS, TS, NI, and TI were defined as the radial distances from the vortex vein ampulla to the optic disc center within each quadrant.

Vortex vein positional parameters (NS, TS, NI, and TI) were measured from stereographically corrected UWF images using the built-in analysis software. SFCT and CRT were measured using EDI-OCT, whereas CVI was derived from OCTA-based choroidal vascular perfusion analysis.

Potential mediators included choroidal structural and vascular parameters derived from EDI-OCT and OCTA. SFCT was measured as the perpendicular distance between Bruch's membrane and the choroid-sclera interface, and regional thickness values were computed by averaging across predefined macular sectors. Choroidal vascular indices, including vascular volume and perfusion metrics, were calculated following standardized binarization and slab-based quantification procedures to ensure consistency across samples.

Covariates incorporated into the analytical models were age, sex, AL, and intraocular pressure, given their known associations with choroidal morphology and refractive development. AL was obtained via optical biometry, whereas intraocular pressure was measured using non-contact tonometry. Statistical Analysis

All analyses were conducted using R software (version 4.3.2) and SPSS 26.0. Baseline demographic, refractive, choroidal, and vortex vein parameters were first compared among refractive groups using the t-test, Mann–Whitney U test, χ2 test, or their nonparametric equivalents as appropriate. Spearman correlation analysis was then performed to evaluate associations among SE, AL, CRT, SFCT, CVI, and quadrant-specific vortex vein distances (NS, TS, NI, and TI).

Variables entered into the LASSO and multinomial logistic regression models included age, sex, AL, CRT, SFCT, CVI, and quadrant-specific vortex vein distances. LASSO regression with 10-fold cross-validation was applied for feature selection, and variables with nonzero coefficients at the optimal λ were retained for subsequent modeling. Restricted cubic spline (RCS) analyses were subsequently performed to assess potential nonlinear associations between selected predictors and refractive status.

Based on the selected predictors, multinomial logistic regression models and nomograms were constructed for refractive classification. Model discrimination and calibration were evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, area under the curve (AUC), calibration plots, Brier scores, and the Hosmer–Lemeshow test.

Mediation analysis was performed using a bootstrap approach with 5000 resamples to explore the statistical associations among vortex vein parameters, choroidal structure, and refractive status. Multiple comparisons were adjusted using the Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate procedure, and a two-sided P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline Characteristics of the Study Population

A total of 147 participants were included, with 49 eyes in each refractive group (Table 1). Age and sex distribution did not differ significantly among groups (both P > 0.05). In contrast, several ocular parameters showed clear and significant refractive gradients. SE differed markedly (P < 0.001), with myopic eyes showing the most negative values (median = −3.00 D), emmetropic eyes near zero (−0.13 D), and hyperopic eyes the highest (+0.88 D). AL followed a similar pattern, longest in myopia (24.92 ± 0.30 mm), intermediate in emmetropia (23.77 ± 0.09 mm), and shortest in hyperopia (23.19 ± 0.09 mm, P < 0.001). CVI increased progressively from myopia to hyperopia (0.43 → 0.45 → 0.50, P < 0.001), and SFCT showed a parallel thickening (276.53 ± 20.02 µm → 296.88 ± 16.05 µm → 372.57 ± 15.71 µm, P < 0.001). Significant differences were also observed in vortex vein positional parameters including NS, TS, and NI distances (all P < 0.001). TS and NI distances increased progressively from myopia to hyperopia, whereas NS showed the highest values in the emmetropic group. By contrast, CRT and TI showed no significant intergroup variation (both P > 0.05).

Correlation Between Refractive Status and Ocular/Choroidal Parameters

Spearman correlation analysis revealed strong and significant associations between SE and multiple structural parameters (Fig. 2). SE showed a moderate-to-strong negative correlation with AL (r ≈ −0.54, P < 0.001), and strong positive correlations with CVI (r ≈ 0.96, P < 0.001) and SFCT (r ≈ 0.90, P < 0.001). A moderate positive correlation was also observed between SE and TS (r ≈ 0.73, P < 0.001). CVI and SFCT were highly correlated (r ≈ 0.94, P < 0.001), and TS demonstrated significant positive correlations with both CVI (r ≈ 0.74) and SFCT (r ≈ 0.64), all P < 0.001. By contrast, CRT and other quadrant vortex vein distances showed weak or nonsignificant correlations with SE and major choroidal parameters.

LASSO Identified Key Predictors of Refractive Status

Multinomial LASSO regression yielded consistent coefficient shrinkage patterns across the three refractive comparisons (Figs. 3A–C). As the penalty increased, most variables progressively shrank toward zero, whereas a small subset remained nonzero at the optimal λ (Fig. 3D). Across both refractive extremes, AL, CVI, SFCT, and TS were the only variables that consistently retained nonzero coefficients, indicating stable discriminative value for refractive classification. Because CVI and SFCT were highly correlated (r ≈ 0.94), simultaneous inclusion resulted in evident multicollinearity; therefore, CVI was excluded, and AL, SFCT, and TS were selected as the core predictors for subsequent modeling.

Nonlinear Effects of Predictors and Nomogram Estimation

RCS analysis showed clear nonlinear associations for AL and SFCT (both P < 0.001), whereas TS demonstrated only marginal nonlinearity (P = 0.088; Figs. 4A–C). Myopia probability remained relatively stable across shorter to mid-range AL values but increased rapidly when AL exceeded approximately 25 mm. SFCT displayed an opposite pattern, with thinner choroids corresponding to higher myopia probability and thicker choroids corresponding to lower risk. TS showed a mild tendency toward higher myopia probability at shorter distances, followed by a gradual plateau as TS increased. Based on these patterns, AL and SFCT were modeled using spline terms and TS was treated linearly. Using these three predictors, nomograms were constructed for myopia and hyperopia classification (Figs. 4D, 4E), illustrating that the combination of longer AL, thinner SFCT, and shorter TS increased the predicted likelihood of myopia, whereas the opposite parameter patterns shifted predictions toward hyperopia.

Discrimination and Calibration Performance of the Nomogram Models

Both nomogram models demonstrated excellent discrimination. For the myopia classification, the ROC curve showed an AUC of approximately 0.99, indicating near-perfect separation between categories (Fig. 5A). The hyperopia model also achieved high discriminative performance, with an AUC of approximately 0.97 (Fig. 5C). Calibration curves after bootstrap correction closely followed the ideal 45-degree line for both models, with only slight deviation at higher predicted probabilities (Figs. 5B, 5D). The Brier scores were low, and the mean absolute calibration errors were approximately 0.02 for the myopia model and 0.01 for the hyperopia model, suggesting good calibration performance within the internally validated study sample.

Mediation Analysis of the TS–SFCT–SE Pathway

Mediation analysis showed that SFCT partially accounted for the association between TS and SE in the overall sample (Fig. 6A; Table 2). TS was positively associated with SFCT (standardized a = 0.64) and SFCT was positively associated with SE (standardized b = 0.73), whereas TS retained a smaller but significant direct association with SE (c′ = 0.26, all P < 0.001). The total effect of TS on SE was 0.73 (95% confidence interval [CI] = 0.58–0.87), of which 0.47 (95% CI = 0.40–0.55) was explained indirectly via SFCT, accounting for approximately 64.7% of the total effect. SFCT itself showed a total and direct effect of 0.73 (95% CI = 0.66–0.80, P < 0.001), indicating its strong statistical association with refractive status. Subgroup analyses stratified by age, sex, and AL demonstrated consistent mediation patterns (Figs. 6B–G). Across all strata, TS remained positively associated with SFCT, and SFCT remained positively associated with SE, with comparable indirect effect sizes and no evidence of association pattern reversal or attenuation, indicating that the statistical mediation pattern was stable across demographic and biometric subgroups.

Discussion

This study provides a comprehensive structural assessment linking vortex vein morphology, choroidal characteristics, and refractive status. First, we observed clear and coordinated gradients among TS, SFCT, and SE, whereas shorter TS and thinner SFCT were consistently associated with more myopic refractive states, although longer TS and thicker SFCT corresponded to hyperopic tendencies. RCS modeling further demonstrated pronounced nonlinear effects for AL and SFCT, with myopia probability increasing sharply beyond an AL of approximately 25 mm and decreasing steadily with increasing choroidal thickness. Although the nonlinear effect of TS was weaker, it showed a similar directional trend. Second, by integrating AL, TS, and SFCT, we developed refractive prediction models with excellent internal discriminative performance. The nomograms achieved AUCs of approximately 0.99 for myopia and 0.97 for hyperopia, and calibration analyses indicated minimal prediction error, supporting the internal stability of the models within the study sample. Third, mediation analysis suggested that SFCT accounted for a substantial proportion of the association between TS and refractive status, representing nearly two-thirds of the total association, and this statistical mediation pattern remained consistent across subgroups stratified by age, sex, and AL. Finally, combining TS, SFCT, and AL allowed the construction of clinically interpretable nomograms, highlighting the potential value of these structural markers for refractive assessment.

The present findings align closely with and extend the existing body of literature describing the structural correlates of refractive status. Numerous studies have demonstrated that choroidal structure is closely associated with ocular structural characteristics related to refractive status.12 Consistent with these reports, we observed a clear gradient in SFCT across refractive categories, with myopic eyes showing pronounced choroidal thinning. Prior work has shown that SFCT decreases by approximately 20 to 30 µm for each millimeter increase in AL, and highly myopic eyes often exhibit markedly reduced SFCT values, supporting the concept that choroidal thinning accompanies and possibly facilitates axial elongation.13,14 OCTA-based studies have further confirmed that choroidal vascular markers, including the CVI, correlate strongly with both refractive status and AL, underscoring the importance of choroidal perfusion and vascular composition in refractive status-related structural variation.

In parallel, research on vortex vein morphology has begun to reveal its potential relevance to choroidal hemodynamics, although the evidence remains limited. Clinical investigations have shown that abnormalities in posterior vortex vein configuration occur more frequently in highly myopic eyes than in emmetropic eyes, with reported prevalences of approximately 26.4% and 10.3%, respectively.11 These observations suggest that alterations in venous drainage may accompany axial elongation or impaired choroidal outflow. However, previous studies have typically examined vortex vein configuration and choroidal structure in isolation and have not established an integrative framework linking venous geometry, choroidal morphology, and refractive outcomes.

Our findings build directly upon these gaps. By simultaneously quantifying vortex vein distances and choroidal metrics within the same analytic framework, we demonstrate that TS is associated with SE both directly and indirectly through SFCT, providing the first quantitative evidence supporting a statistical mediation framework linking vortex vein geometry to refractive status via choroidal thickness. This association pattern suggests that variations in venous drainage geometry may influence choroidal tissue characteristics, which in turn shape refractive development.11,12 Furthermore, our predictive models demonstrated high discriminative performance compared with previously reported refractive assessment approaches based primarily on AL or choroidal parameters alone.13,14 The high discriminative performance observed in our models highlights the added value of integrating vortex vein features with choroidal structure and supports the potential of these imaging markers as higher-resolution structural indicators of refractive status.

This study provides an integrated structural perspective on refractive status by linking vortex vein geometry, choroidal morphology, and refractive status within a unified analytic framework. The identification of a stable TS–SFCT–SE association framework demonstrates that variations in vortex vein drainage are associated with refractive outcomes largely through their influence on choroidal thickness, highlighting the choroid as a key structural intermediary within the posterior segment.15 The mediation effect, which accounted for nearly two-thirds of the association between TS and SE and remained consistent across demographic and biometric subgroups, underscores the robustness of this association pattern. In addition, incorporating TS and SFCT alongside AL substantially improved predictive performance, yielding myopia and hyperopia models with high discriminative performance and excellent calibration. These findings extend prior research by providing the first quantitative evidence that venous outflow architecture contributes to refractive variation through choroidal structure, and by offering internally validated nomograms that may inform future hypothesis-driven and longitudinal investigations.

This study has several considerations that warrant attention. First, the cross-sectional design limits conclusions about temporal relationships among TS, SFCT, and refractive status, and therefore precludes inference regarding causal direction or true developmental sequences; longitudinal data will be needed to clarify the temporal ordering and dynamic interplay of these structural parameters. Second, participants were intentionally selected to achieve balanced sample sizes across refractive categories, and the sample consisted mainly of young, healthy individuals; as a result, the observed predictive performance and model discrimination may not be directly generalizable to populations with a natural refractive distribution or to other age groups, and external validation in broader and more diverse cohorts is warranted. In addition, the relatively small sample size may limit the stability and generalizability of the predictive models, particularly for subgroup and mediation analyses. Third, although OCT and OCTA offer detailed structural information, they do not directly quantify venous hemodynamics or scleral biomechanics, and future studies incorporating multimodal imaging or functional assessments may provide a more comprehensive understanding of posterior segment physiology. In addition, although stereographic projection correction was applied during UWF image reconstruction, potential magnification effects related to AL and ocular media were not specifically corrected and may have influenced absolute distance measurements. Fourth, mediation analyses were conducted within a cross-sectional framework and should be interpreted as exploratory statistical decompositions of associations rather than evidence of causal pathways. Finally, vortex vein parameters were measured manually, and automated or semi-automated tools could further enhance measurement consistency and reproducibility. Taken together, these limitations indicate that the present findings should be viewed as hypothesis-generating, highlighting opportunities for future research to refine and validate the TS–SFCT–SE association framework and to further assess the robustness and applicability of the proposed prediction models.

Conclusions

This study integrates vortex vein geometry and choroidal structure to offer a unified view of the posterior segment features associated with refractive status. The structural association framework identified among TS, SFCT, and refractive outcomes, together with the internally validated prediction models, highlights the potential value of incorporating these imaging markers into refractive assessment within the studied population. These findings may help inform future hypothesis-driven and longitudinal investigations and deepen understanding of structural correlates of refractive status.
